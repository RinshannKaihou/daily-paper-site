Why this matters

Turns “in-loop reward rising while external truth drifts” into a measurable audit — a reference method for self-evolving loops, automated reward design, and agent-reliability monitoring.

01Methodology, distilled

The loop under study is Dr. Zero: a proposer writes questions and pseudo-labels from source documents, the solver answers, and agreement is the reward — three rounds of 18 proposer + 25 solver updates on Qwen3.5-4B/9B, eight H200s per run.

Diagnosis. After training, every saved step is audited post hoc: an LLM judge rebuilds an evidence-backed reference from the source document and scores the pseudo-label plus five solver responses, decomposing the signal into label truth T P, solver truth T S, agreement A, false-agreement mass F (pairs sharing the same wrong answer), and lost credit L. The audit never touches admission or reward.

Two interventions, compared. MSV re-verifies each label with six same-model samples (attacks label quality). CrossFit instead changes only who supplies proposer feedback: source documents are split once into two folds at the document level, and an auxiliary solver trained strictly out-of-fold scores each fold’s questions, roles swapping every round — reward shape and the main solver’s update rule untouched.

Isolation & evaluation. A fixed 3,000-question replay bank varies evaluator provenance alone; downstream means seven QA benchmarks (NQ, TriviaQA, PopQA, HotpotQA, 2WikiMQA, MuSiQue, Bamboogle), 1,325 fixed questions, Cover-EM.

02CrossFit in one picture

Roles swap each round; reward stays Dr. Zero’s f(k) = (5−k)/4. Borrowed from cross-fitting in double/debiased ML — explicitly without its guarantees.

07Strengths

  • Falsifiable diagnosisAgreement decomposed into T P, T S, A, F, L; all 129 training steps retained, unsmoothed.
  • Exemplary mechanism isolationOne fixed 3,000-question replay bank where only the evaluator’s provenance changes.
  • Drop-in minimal patchMain solver’s update rule and reward shape untouched; portable to any proposer–solver loop.
  • Candid cost accountingH200-hours, judge counts, and the 2.6–2.7× combined price reported, not hidden.
  • Ports beyond search agentsAny learned evaluator scoring outputs drawn from its own training data shares the ancestry hazard.

08Limitations

  • Narrow scopeOne loop (Dr. Zero), one family (Qwen3.5), two scales, fixed k=2 folds, no fold-count ablation.
  • Judge-dependent auditSingle LLM judge, ~14% of steps unresolved, no human verification sample; F=0.004/0.001 sits within judge noise.
  • Fragile headline statsSingle runs, one greedy trajectory, no confidence intervals; PopQA +0.125 survives only a few question flips.
  • Residual failure channelsShared pretraining errors and overlapping web evidence can still correlate the two folds’ mistakes.
  • Weak MSV baselineSame-model verifier is known to share errors; no cross-family verifier was run.

09Questions for further exploration

  1. Does CrossFit survive semantically connected corpora (e.g., one wiki) where both folds share evidence and pretraining errors?
  2. Would a different-family or larger verifier rescue MSV — or is per-candidate verification capped by label noise itself?
  3. With k > 2 folds, is there a point where each auxiliary solver becomes too weak to be a useful rewarder?
  4. Does the A↑ F↑ L↓ signature appear in Absolute Zero-style reasoning or corpus-based self-play — and does the patch transfer?
  5. Could cheap held-out probe items replace the expensive LLM judge for at-scale monitoring during training?
  6. Is the downstream gain a healthier curriculum rather than better feedback? The authors’ own Appendix D flags this — a curriculum-difficulty analysis would settle it.
