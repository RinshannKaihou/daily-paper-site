# Recursive Harness Self-Improvement for Frontier Reasoning Data Synthesis

> **一句话总结：** 提出 task-harness co-evolution 框架：在冻结模型权重与验证逻辑的前提下，用"在线技能更新（求解失败即时转化为可复用技能）+ 批后 harness 修订（候选评估 + 回滚）"双机制递归进化数据合成 harness，14 轮进化后固定求解器 DeepSeek-V4-Pro 在数学/代码/科学任务上的准确率从 100.0% 降到 54.8%（组合机制比固定 harness 递归难 23.5 个百分点）；10K 合成数学数据 GRPO 训练 Qwen3.5-4B Thinking 在四基准平均达 62.7%，超过最强递归基线 IQC 的 59.5% 和检查点的 58.4%。

> **TL;DR:** This paper argues that recursive data synthesis should evolve not just the tasks but the *construction harness* (skills, prompts, workflow) that generates them. It combines online self-improvement (solver failures converted into reusable skills mid-batch) with post-task self-improvement (a meta-agent proposes harness revisions accepted only if they produce harder valid tasks within a bounded cost allowance, with rollback after 3 rejections), while model weights and verification logic stay fixed. Over 14 evolution rounds, fixed solver DeepSeek-V4-Pro accuracy on the evolved tasks falls from 100.0% to 54.8%, and the dual schedule is 23.5 points harder than fixed-harness recursion (Full 50.0% vs Task-only 73.5% at round 14). Downstream, GRPO on 10K examples lifts Qwen3.5-4B Thinking to 62.7% overall mean-16 (APEX Shortlist/HMMMT/LCB/SciBench average) vs 59.5% for the strongest recursive baseline IQC (+3.2) and 58.4% for the untrained checkpoint (+4.3); SFT brings a 35B model to 55.7% vs 51.1%, and a 27B model SFT'd on 10K math examples reaches 62.5% on APEX 2025 (+6.2), between DeepSeek-v4-pro (61.5) and Kimi K3 Think (65.6).

## 核心贡献

- **任务-harness 协同进化框架**：将递归数据合成从"只复用任务做种子"（RST、IQC 等任务级递归）扩展到同时进化生成程序本身。harness 形式化为 H = (L, P, W, ρ_V, ρ_R)：可复用构造技能、角色提示、工作流、验证/修订的放置位置；模型权重、验证器实现与最终有效性标准全程冻结，适应只发生在生成过程中。
- **双更新调度机制**：
  - *在线自改进（Stage 2）*：Director 控制任务生成（Extend/Revise/Verify），当求解器在中间 QA 上失败且归因于推理弱点时，Meta Agent 立即将该失败转化为可复用构造技能（ADMIT 准入），当轮未完成任务即可使用新技能——失败反馈不必等批结束。
  - *批后自改进（Stage 3）*：批结束后基于执行日志、指标与人工目标提出 harness 候选修订（可增删技能、改提示与工作流、移动验证/修订位置），候选与在线基线从同一批当前任务出发各合成一轮，只有通过"有效性不降 + 难度严格上升 + 成本增幅 ≤ min{1+1.5(r_D−1), 1.5}（即 20% 难度增益最多允许 30% 成本增长，上限 50%）"门槛才采纳；三次拒绝则整体回滚到在线快照。
- **难度持续增长 + 下游训练价值的实证**：150 条种子链（数学/代码/科学各 50）进化 14 轮，求解器准确率 100.0% → 54.8%；消融显示两种调度互补（组合比任一单独都难）；下游 GRPO 与 SFT 全面优于 OpenMathInstruct-2、OpenCodeReasoning、WildSci、MetaMath 等静态数据集和 IQC 递归基线；冻结 harness 迁移实验（H14 在 50 条 held-out 数学种子上 52.0% vs H0 的 70.0%）把收益归因到"可复用的生成程序"而非特定任务链。

## Key Contributions

- **Task-harness co-evolution**: extends task-only recursive synthesis (RST, IQC) so that the reusable construction procedure — skills, prompts, workflow, verification/revision placement — evolves alongside the task distribution, with model weights and verifier logic frozen throughout.
- **Two complementary update schedules**: (1) *Online self-improvement* admits failure-derived skills into the library mid-generation via an ADMIT gate, so unfinished tasks in the same batch immediately benefit; (2) *Post-task self-improvement* runs an outer optimization loop where candidate harness revisions are evaluated head-to-head against the frozen online snapshot on a shared cohort, adopted only under a difficulty-first empirical gate (strictly lower solver accuracy, no validity decrease, cost growth ≤ min{1 + 1.5(r_D − 1), 1.5}), with full rollback to the snapshot after three rejections.
- **Evidence for difficulty growth and training utility**: 45.2-point solver-accuracy decline over 14 rounds across math/coding/science; a 4-way update ablation on shared seeds showing the combined schedule dominates; downstream gains under both GRPO and SFT against static datasets (OpenMathInstruct-2, OpenCodeReasoning, WildSci, MetaMath) and the task-level recursive baseline IQC; and a frozen-harness transfer experiment (evolved H14: 52.0% vs initial H0: 70.0% on 50 held-out math seeds) tying the gains to a reusable procedure rather than particular task chains.

## Methodology

**Harness formulation.** A harness is H = (L, P, W, ρ_V, ρ_R): reusable construction skills L, role prompts P, workflow W, and the placements of verification/revision steps. Weights, the verifier implementation, and final validity criteria are fixed; online adaptation edits only the skill component, while batch-level optimization may revise all components (including when verification and revision fire).

**Difficulty accounting.** Each round retains one valid task per original seed chain (failed attempts stay in logs and cost accounting; the hardest ancestor is never selected retrospectively). For a matched cohort, mean accuracy Ā(H) is the solver's fraction of correct answers averaged over four attempts per task under a fixed solver configuration; relative difficulty r_D = Ā(H_o)/Ā(H') is a ratio of cohort means (r_D > 1 = harder). Generation cost C̄_gen includes Director, generation, verification, revision, and online-update calls, including failed attempts.

**Stage 1 — Initialization.** Candidate seeds and source pages from coding, math, and science are screened for duplicates and benchmark overlap; an acquisition model extracts intent/method/difficulty/tool hints into reusable task transformations. Skill examples and training exports are re-checked for contamination, since input screening alone does not exclude contamination introduced during synthesis.

**Stage 2 — Online self-improvement.** A Director agent selects skills and drives Extend/Revise/Verify until Finish or budget exhaustion. During verification, the generating model attempts an intermediate QA; a failure attributable to a reasoning weakness on a valid QA yields a trace that the Meta Agent converts into a skill candidate, admitted only with applicability, reusability, and supporting evidence (invalid QAs, wrong reference answers, and tool failures trigger repair instead). At batch boundary the resulting harness freezes as H_n^online — the comparison baseline and fallback.

**Stage 3 — Post-task self-improvement.** In-loop tests of retained tasks produce scores and failure traces; the Meta Agent first updates skills in a copy of H_n^online, then proposes harness edits from execution logs, metrics, and human-specified goals/constraints. A frozen shared cohort S_n of current retained tasks is the evaluation input: candidate and baseline each independently synthesize one further round from S_n under identical model/tool/resource limits, compared on new outputs (not previously recorded scores). Q(H) is the fraction of new final candidates passing validity before fallback (missing candidates invalid). The Critic accepts the first candidate satisfying: candidate in the edit space; Q(H') ≥ Q(H_o); Ā(H') < Ā(H_o); and C̄_gen(H') ≤ min{1 + 1.5(r_D − 1), 1.5}·C̄_gen(H_o) — e.g., a 20% difficulty gain permits at most 30% higher cost, capped at +50%. After three rejections (initial proposal + two retries), H_{n+1} = H_n^online and all post-task edits are discarded (online skills and rejection logs remain).

**Experiments.** Fixed solver: DeepSeek-V4-Pro. Downstream: GRPO on Qwen3.5-4B Thinking (10K examples per config); SFT on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B (12K per config; Ours = 10K math + 2K coding) and Qwen3.8-27B. Evaluation via MathArena: APEX (mean-16 accuracy, 16 attempts per problem), HMMT Feb 2025/2026, LiveCodeBench v6, SciBench.

## Results & Highlights

- **Difficulty progression**: DeepSeek-V4-Pro mean accuracy over the 150 seed chains falls from 100.0% (round 0) to 54.8% (round 14) — a 45.2-point decline — with per-domain round-14 accuracies of 50.0% (math), 58.5% (coding), 56.0% (science); the decline occurs in every domain under a fixed cohort, not via domain-composition drift.
- **Update-schedule ablation** (50 shared seeds, round 14): Full 50.0% vs Task-only (fixed harness) 73.5% — 23.5 points harder; vs Post-task-only 59.0% (+9.0) and Online-only 67.0% (+17.0). Online-only actually regresses (accuracy rises from 64.5% at round 10 to 67.0% at round 14), while from rounds 10→14 Full still deepens difficulty (57.0% → 50.0%).
- **GRPO, Qwen3.5-4B Thinking (Table 1, 10K examples)**: Ours reaches 62.7% overall mean-16 (APEX Shortlist 36.4 / HMMT Feb 2025 78.3 / LCB v6 59.2 / SciBench 76.9) vs 59.5% for the strongest recursive baseline IQC (+3.2) and 58.4% for the untrained checkpoint (+4.3); static baselines trail at OpenMathInstruct-2 58.1%, OpenCodeReasoning 57.2%, WildSci 55.6%, MetaMath 54.5%. Ours exceeds the checkpoint on all four benchmarks (+4.0/+4.8/+3.4/+5.1).
- **SFT, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B (Table 2)**: Ours averages 55.7% (APEX 7.3 / APEX Shortlist 49.7 / HMMT Feb 2026 86.7 / LCB v6 79.1) vs 51.1% for both the checkpoint and the best baseline mix MMIQC/IQC (+4.6); OpenMathInstruct-2 (50.7) and OpenCodeReasoning (49.0) actually fall below the checkpoint.
- **APEX with a 27B student**: Qwen3.8-27B SFT'd on 10K synthesized math examples improves from 56.3% to 62.5% mean-16 on APEX 2025 (+6.2), placing it between DeepSeek-v4-pro (61.5) and Kimi K3 Think (65.6), ahead of Gemini 3.1 Pro (60.9) and GPT-5.4 xhigh (54.2) among the selected references.
- **Frozen-harness transfer**: with both harnesses frozen on 50 held-out math seeds, evolved H14 yields 52.0% solver accuracy after ten recursive rounds vs 70.0% for initial H0 — an 18.0-point gap that cannot be attributed to H14 starting from harder terminal tasks (both restart from the same seeds).
- **Operator cost**: per-round Extend/Revise/Verify cost per seed at round 14 is $1.065 (Full) vs $1.332 (Task-only) — a 20.0% reduction — though this measure excludes Director, skill-update, and search overhead.

## Authors & Credibility

The 8-author team spans EPIC Lab at Shanghai Jiao Tong University (corresponding author Linfeng Zhang, whose group is known for work on knowledge distillation and efficient LLM training/inference), plus USTC, Shanghai University of Finance and Economics, Imperial College London, Cambridge, NTU, Fudan, and UESTC; the first three authors contributed equally. The paper follows current frontier-lab conventions: contamination screening is documented in a dedicated appendix, evaluation uses the external MathArena platform (16 attempts per problem) rather than self-reported harnesses, and an explicit AI-use statement discloses that generative AI assisted data generation, code development, log analysis, and manuscript drafting, with the authors taking responsibility for verification. Notably, the paper includes an unusually candid "Scope of the evidence" paragraph and future-work list that pre-registers its own limitations — a positive credibility signal.

## Critical Assessment

- **Difficulty is solver-relative and single-solver.** All difficulty claims are measured by one fixed solver (DeepSeek-V4-Pro, 4 attempts/task). The authors themselves concede the results "do not establish transfer to independent solvers" — a task that is hard for DeepSeek-V4-Pro may be routine for, say, Kimi K3 Think, so "frontier reasoning data" is asserted, not demonstrated, beyond the indirect APEX evidence.
- **Single runs, no variance.** The paper explicitly notes "repeated solver attempts are not independent evolution runs" and that "independent-run uncertainty remains unreported." Every headline number (100.0→54.8, 62.7 vs 59.5) is a point estimate from one trajectory; the 14-round decay curves could be sensitive to seed or sampler choices.
- **The ablation has a declared confound.** Post-task-only bundles skill revision *and* candidate evaluation/rollback; the paper admits the comparison "does not isolate the contribution of candidate screening within post-task optimization." The paper's cleanest mechanistic claim — that the gate matters — is therefore not fully supported by its own experiments.
- **The cost win is narrow.** The 20.0% per-seed reduction ($1.065 vs $1.332) covers only Extend/Revise/Verify fees and explicitly excludes Director, skill-update, and search overhead; total evolution cost and candidate validity/fallback rates "remain unreported." The economic claim in the abstract ("bounded cost increase") is enforced by the gate but never audited end-to-end.
- **SFT comparison has a composition mismatch.** In Table 2, baselines receive 12K examples of one domain while Ours receives 10K math + 2K coding — a domain-composition confound the paper waves at by framing comparisons as "characterizing the evaluated data mixtures" rather than isolating the synthesis method.
- **Presentation choices flatter the result.** Figure 2 truncates both y-axes (50–100% for difficulty, 20–75% for APEX), and Figure 4/5 start at 45% — the headline visuals amplify gaps that are more modest on a full axis. The 35B APEX column starts from a near-floor 4.2%, making the +3.1 gain there weakly informative.
- **Rigor positives worth crediting**: the adoption gate is fully specified, rollback is deterministic, held-out-seed transfer is a genuinely well-designed control, and the honest limitations section is above community norm.

## Strengths

- Clean conceptual move: separating *what is generated* (tasks) from *how it is generated* (harness) and freezing weights/verifier isolates procedural adaptation from model adaptation — a crisp framing absent from task-only recursion work (RST, IQC).
- The dual-schedule design is mechanistically motivated and ablated on shared seeds with a shared harness, and late-round behavior (Full keeps hardening while Online-only regresses) is a discriminating pattern, not just an endpoint comparison.
- Frozen-harness transfer on held-out seeds (52.0% vs 70.0%) is the right experiment for the "reusable procedure" claim, controlling for starting-task hardness.
- Downstream utility is shown under two training paradigms (GRPO and SFT) and three student sizes (4B/27B/35B-A3B) against both static and recursive baselines, with external MathArena evaluation at mean-16.
- Unusually transparent about scope: bounded-cost gate, explicit fallback accounting, contamination re-checks, and a self-critical evidence-scope section.

## Limitations

- Difficulty gains are validated against a single solver and within the evolution domains; cross-solver and cross-domain difficulty transfer are untested.
- One run per configuration; no error bars, no repeated evolution trajectories, no convergence analysis beyond 14 rounds (the authors note finite trajectories "do not establish eventual convergence").
- Downstream attribution lacks matched-token training on fixed-harness vs evolved-harness data — the comparison across data mixtures cannot cleanly isolate the contribution of harness evolution.
- Total generation cost, candidate validity rates, and fallback frequencies are unreported; the operator-cost claim excludes Director and skill-update overhead.
- No human or independent quality audit of synthesized tasks beyond automated validity — "harder" (lower solver accuracy) is not shown to mean "better" (non-degenerate, novel, well-posed).
- Contamination screening is heuristic; the authors acknowledge input screening "does not exclude contamination introduced during synthesis."

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the measured difficulty transfer to solvers outside DeepSeek-V4-Pro's family — both weaker students and stronger frontier models — or does the harness merely overfit one solver's weaknesses?
- With matched training tokens on fixed-harness vs evolved-harness data, how much downstream gain actually survives attribution to harness evolution rather than data mixture?
- Can the post-task arm be unbundled — how much of its +9.0 over Task-only comes from candidate screening/rollback versus plain skill revision?
- Does the skill library grow without bound, and is there a measurable notion of skill quality or redundancy beyond the ADMIT gate?
- Including Director, skill-update, and search overhead, does the full pipeline remain cheaper per unit of difficulty than fixed-harness recursion end-to-end?
- What happens beyond round 14 — do validity rates, task well-posedness, or verifier pass rates degrade as difficulty climbs, and is there a difficulty ceiling where synthesis collapses?
