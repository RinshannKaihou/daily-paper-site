01Methodology

Boosting theory, three-way evidence, and one honest gap.

Theory

Model the refinement loop as boosting: a weak coding agent’s patch is a hypothesis over the residual (git diff) between draft and target; the loop composes patches sequentially. Under an idealized Agentic Boosting Protocol, gains are monotone and saturate (Thm 2). Drop the protocol and Prop 3 survives alone: a fixed reachable edit class forces ηt → 0; sustained improvement demands expanding the class — new scaffold capability or new weights. Best-of-k attains the best worker’s ceiling exactly (Prop 5); majority vote collapses at maximal failure overlap (Prop 6); a reused pool has a computable re-consultation horizon (Prop 7). All proofs are Lean 4 machine-checked — Lean even refuted the authors’ own conjectured strict inequality in Prop 5.

Evidence

Part A validates the machinery on synthetic decision stumps. Part B runs mini-swe-agent as the weak learner through T=4 rounds on 55 SWE-bench Lite tasks × 5 seeds — 1,650 trajectories, a 2×3 design (Haiku vs Sonnet × three feedback modes), with a custom τ=0.6 pytest evaluator audited against gold patches. Part C reads 401 production sessions (1,211 commits, 23 repos) as structural git metrics against a 9,395-session pre-AI human baseline.

What deployment lacks

  • ×Retention of all T proposals
  • ×Explicit reweighting distribution Dt
  • ×Vote-ready worker diversity

All three AdaBoost preconditions fail in real loops — their absence becomes the paper’s buildable harness spec.

03Strengths

What this preprint gets unusually right.

  • A clarifying reframing: separating test-time search, training, and scaffold rewriting dissolves a recurring RSI confusion — a weights-frozen audit catches test-time training and misses scaffold expansion entirely.
  • Machine-checked theory (Lean 4, Mathlib, no axioms, no sorry) with precise scoping of what remains assumption.
  • Triangulated evidence: synthetic + controlled benchmark loop + production data, with a pre-AI human baseline that kills the tempting “churn decay = recursive improvement” story.
  • Exceptional error accounting: the 23/50 evaluator-failure audit and the underpowered-null power analysis model the honesty the field needs.
  • Cheap, concrete open problems (retention, explicit Dt, cross-family vote, post-first-pass refinement) defining a buildable harness rather than vague future work.

04Limitations

Where the evidence bends.

  • The key quantity γ cannot be cleanly estimated — the harness stops querying after first pass (acknowledged critical caveat).
  • Diversity claims rest on a single-vendor pool with effective diversity near two; no cross-model-family vote is tested.
  • Part B absolute rates are harness-specific (custom τ=0.6 evaluator, non-standard pool weighting); even τ=1.0 figures are not official resolve rates.
  • T=4 rounds and k=55 tasks are small; the saturation trajectory is short.
  • Part C is observational: tier assignment is confounded (Opus planned, Sonnet executed), and only structural git metadata is released.
  • The (M1)/(M2) assumptions are unverified in any deployed system; checkability rests on a scaffold-immutability proxy.

05Questions for further exploration

  1. Would a genuinely cross-family pool (Claude + GPT + Gemini) clear Prop 6’s m* < k/2 bar, or do correlated tool/environment failures keep overlap near k even across vendors?
  2. If a loop actually implemented the Agentic Boosting Protocol (retention + explicit Dt), does the transferred product bound keep descending past T=4?
  3. Can the per-round edge γ be identified with a keep-querying-after-first-pass design — and at what compute cost?
  4. Is there a structural-git-metrics statistic that separates regime-(i) saturation from ordinary human-like editing economics, given the human baseline matches the AI decay shape?
  5. When scaffolds self-expand (self-written verifiers, test harnesses), can the proposed audit detect reward-backing exploitation of L(X, Y, γ) before it masquerades as capability gain?
