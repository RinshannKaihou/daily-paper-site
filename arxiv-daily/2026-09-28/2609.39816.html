Key metric

772.4×

perplexity spread across the 512 exactly-equivalent sign variants of two-level Strassen — every stability criterion scored all of them with one identical value.

Why this matters

A textbook case of aggressive optimization silently corrupting outputs — algorithm-level silent data corruption that accuracy alone will never catch.

Critical edge

Certification exists only for int8; FP8 — the format actually deployed — has none. And the first Triton kernels run 2.04–27.9× slower than classical int8. No wall-clock win today.

VerdictOnce certified, realization choice becomes a pure cost decision: any certified implementation is bitwise equal to classical int8, so no fast realization can ever change a scored likelihood again.

01

Methodology

distilled — the full arc in four moves
STEP 1

Formalize

A realization = a fast algorithm’s coefficients + its quantization spec. Two-level Strassen uses 49 block products instead of 64; sign matrices yield 512 variants, exactly equivalent in real arithmetic, free to diverge once quantized. Prefix safety = row-locality: output row t reads only row t. FP8 block sums over token rows are not.

STEP 2

Audit

Kim et al.’s protocol: score each answer token in one pass, then replace the token and all later text with the bf16 greedy continuation. Three regimes — prefix replacement (240 OpenBookQA items), window scoring (five models), packed two-request batches (768 pairs).

STEP 3

Show blindness

Every analyzed stability criterion reads coefficients only through absolute values or nonzero patterns — sign changes are invisible to all of them. Realized error is measured as teacher-forced WikiText-2 perplexity, ratio to bf16.

STEP 4

Prove, then build

Theorem 1: prefix-safe form-(1) algorithms need R ≥ mkn block multiplications — the classical count. Proposition 1 runs Strassen on bounded integer codes instead: rows quantized independently, integer sums never round, scales copied from classical int8. Three coefficient-only conditions guarantee bitwise equality, checked once before any input.

Beyond the audit — the violation generalizes

two further regimes where row-mixing leaks across requests

  • Window scoring · 5 models Replacing the last three quarters of a 1,024-token window changes 18.2–40.9% of next-token predictions under the unrepaired realization, 7.8–15.2% under the repaired ones, none under controls.
  • Packed batches · 768 pairs The first request’s next-token distribution changes in every pair under the unrepaired realization and repaired #2 (greedy token: 11.9–13.0% of pairs), never under controls. On Qwen2.5-3B the first request’s K/V projections already differ at the first layer.
03

Strengths vs Limitations

contrasting panel

Strengths

  • Full arc: motivated property → impossibility theorem → constructive fix → verification.
  • Meticulous design: row-local controls, 95% bootstrap CIs, distinct-output census across all 512 variants.
  • Bitwise verification — the strongest evidence class available for an invariance claim.
  • Honest cost accounting: reports its own kernels’ slowdowns and the correction trade-off instead of hiding them.
  • Realization-agnostic certification: conditions depend only on coefficients, tiling, and the quantization spec — checked once, before any input; all 512 variants provably interchangeable.

Limitations

  • int8 only: no FP8 certification, and FP8 is the low-precision format actually deployed in LLM inference.
  • No speed benefit today: Triton kernels 2.04–3.17× (one level) and 27.9× (two levels) slower on H20; in-model runs emulate int8 in fp32.
  • Quality cost at bound 31: excess NLL worse than the deployed FP8 kernel on 3 of 4 models (raw); the corrected variant’s saving erodes to 0.79–0.82× (worst case 1.43–1.71×).
  • Narrow audits: 240 OpenBookQA items, ARC-Easy, WikiText-2 perplexity — no generative, MMLU-scale, or long-context evaluation.
  • Theory scope: Theorem 1 constrains only fixed form-(1) algorithms; learned (AlphaTensor-style) or runtime-reordered schemes are not ruled out.
04

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Can a certified prefix-invariant fast realization be built directly in FP8 — exploiting its exact integer structure or hybrid int8-product / FP8-scale schemes?
  2. How much of the 49/64 saving can survive engineering: fused tensor-core int8 pipelines, CPU/ASIC integer paths, better call shapes — can the 2.04–27.9× wall-clock gap close?
  3. Do these likelihood flips distort full-benchmark rankings — how large is the realized ranking distortion when W8A8/FP8-served models score MMLU-scale suites, beyond 240 OpenBookQA items?
  4. How does the overflow-correction cost behave on real activation distributions across layers and models — is the code-bound-127 variant end-to-end multiplication-saving at all?
  5. Where else does prefix invariance silently matter — speculative decoding, KV-cache reuse, batched RL rollouts — and can cross-request contamination compound over long horizons?
