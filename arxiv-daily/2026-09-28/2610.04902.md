> **一句话总结：** 提出 SERA，在同一个共享策略内联合训练递归智能体的三种耦合能力——分解、求解、评估：父节点在委派前承诺一份加权的自然语言 rubric，对子智能体轨迹打连续执行分；rubric 生成器用「经验证结果上的 margin 排序损失」单独训练（评估奖励不直接回传任务奖励，防止共适应刷分），并用叶节点覆盖率奖励直接为「分解质量」提供监督。在 TextCraft-Synth 和 TextWorld-Sync 上分别以 74.31% 和 65.07% 平均成功率超过最强递归基线 RAO 5.38 和 13.14 个点，外部验证/裁判调用量降至 1/2.1–1/5.1，且训练出的 rubric 在推理时做树搜索选择器还能再涨 2.43 个点。

> **TL;DR:** SERA trains a single shared policy (Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507) to decompose, solve, and evaluate its own subtasks in recursive delegation: before delegating, the parent freezes a weighted rubric; the rubric score becomes a dense per-node execution reward; rubric generation is trained by a margin-ranking objective against verifier/judge-verified outcomes (external supervision is used only for rubric training, never as the reward), plus a leaf-coverage reward that credits decomposition. On TextCraft-Synth it reaches 74.31% average success vs 68.93% for the strongest recursive baseline RAO (+5.38 pts), and on TextWorld-Sync 65.07% vs 51.93% (+13.14 pts), while cutting external judge/verifier calls by 2.1x (TextCraft) and 4.1x (TextWorld); at inference, rubric-guided bottom-up tree search adds a further +2.43 pts on TextWorld-Sync (65.07% -> 67.50%), matching an external frontier Kimi judge (67.29%).

## 核心贡献

- **框架贡献**：把递归智能体训练显式建模为单一共享策略中三种耦合能力（分解 decompose / 求解 solve / 评估 evaluate）的联合学习，并论证每种能力需要各自的训练信号——RAO 类二值 per-node 奖励「对分解质量沉默」，且裁判在每个节点上全程调用既贵又粗。
- **学习式自评估机制**：委派时生成「事前承诺」的加权 rubric（生成器只看委派时信息，绝不看子轨迹），执行期用冻结 rubric 打分作为连续执行奖励；rubric 生成通过对照反事实 rollout 的、以经验证成败为锚的 margin 排序目标训练，而非直接用任务奖励——避免 rubric 生成与求解共适应、分数膨胀。
- **分解奖励**：叶节点覆盖率奖励按「该委派事件的子树覆盖了根任务多少叶级工作」给分解动作记分（失败树为零），在不给执行 token 增加直接信号的前提下补充结构化负载的激励。
- **监督成本大幅下降**：外部裁判只在训练 rubric 生成器时被咨询；训练后评估完全在策略内完成——TextCraft 验证器调用 550.9K -> 257.9K（2.1x），TextWorld 外部裁判调用 147.80K -> 35.79K（4.1x）、输入 token 633.80M -> 125.32M（5.1x）。
- **推理时二次扩展**：学到的 rubric 兼作树搜索的自底向上节点选择器，TextWorld-Sync 平均成功率 65.07% -> 67.50%（+2.43），与外部 Kimi 裁判（67.29%）持平，Extreme 档 22.86% -> 28.95%。

## Key Contributions

- A framing of recursive-agent training as learning three coupled capabilities (decomposing, solving, evaluating) in one shared policy, with ablation evidence that each requires its own training signal.
- A learned self-evaluation mechanism: policy-generated, ex-ante-frozen weighted rubrics scored by the same policy; rubric generation trained by a ranking objective anchored on verified success/failure outcomes from counterfactual rollouts — the judge is consulted only during rubric training, never as a direct reward, which reduces external supervision while outperforming direct judge use.
- A leaf-coverage decomposition reward that credits delegation proportionally to the verified leaf-level work in its subtree, yielding gains concentrated on interdependent-subtask tasks (TextWorld-Sync Hard +8.58, Extreme +17.42 vs SERA w/o D).
- State-of-the-art results on TextCraft-Synth and TextWorld-Sync over prompting, single-agent RL (GRPO/CISPO), and recursive multi-agent RL (RAO and variants), plus the learned rubric as an inference-time tree-search selector for further test-time gains without additional training.

## Methodology

SERA trains one policy π_θ that either solves a task directly or emits a delegation action creating a child instance of itself, recursively forming a tree (max depth 3, 20 environment steps per node, K=8 independent trees per root task for variance reduction). Three rewards are defined and optimized by alternating CISPO updates (execution : decomposition : rubric-training ratio 16 : 2 : 2) with leave-one-out advantage estimation and inverse-frequency depth weighting:

1. **Self-evaluated execution reward (§3.2).** At delegation, the parent generates a rubric ρ_u = {(k_j, w_j)}, a set of natural-language success criteria with weights summing to 1 — a frozen ex-ante commitment conditioned only on delegation-time information. After the child finishes, a separate gradient-free invocation of the same policy scores the frozen rubric against the child's trajectory and final state, producing a binary success gate g_v ∈ {0,1} (did the child complete the delegated subtask) and per-criterion scores c_j; the node score s_v = g_v · Σ w_j c_j. The root receives environment-verified outcome R_i; every non-root node receives its rubric score.
2. **Leaf-coverage decomposition reward (§3.3).** For delegation event e in tree T_i, φ = |L_v(e)| / |L(T_i)| — the fraction of the root task's leaf nodes (nodes that solve atomic subproblems and feed the final answer) covered by e's subtree; reward r = R_i · φ. Delegations in failed trees get zero; neither execution nor rubric-generation tokens receive this reward, so it isolates a direct optimization signal for decomposition quality.
3. **Rubric generation trained from verified outcomes (§3.4).** Optimizing rubrics through the task reward would let the generator and solver (same weights) co-adapt and inflate scores. Instead, at each delegation point the environment state is cloned and B−1 counterfactual rollouts of the subtask are sampled; each gets a verified outcome y_b ∈ {0,1} from a program verifier (TextCraft-Synth) or external LLM judge (TextWorld-Sync). A margin ranking loss L = mean max(0, δ − s_i + s_j) over verified success/failure pairs trains the generator to score successes above failures on the *same* subtask — isolating rubric quality from absolute task difficulty.

All three advantages use LOO baselines over independently sampled trees (root-group baseline for execution; per-tree mean for decomposition; batch mean over valid ranking groups for rubric generation), optimized with the shared CISPO clipped importance-sampling objective (Chen et al. 2025). Training: Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 fully fine-tuned, Adam 1e-6, batch 8 root tasks, on 2x8 H200 GPUs; 250 updates on TextCraft-Synth, 400 on TextWorld-Sync. TextCraft-Synth (Gandhi et al. 2026): procedural crafting with a deterministic per-subtask program verifier, 852 Medium training tasks, evaluated on 632 tasks across four recipe-depth levels. TextWorld-Sync (new, built on TextWorldExpress CookingWorld): 7 coordination-structured task families (dependency chains, resource conflicts, load balancing, hierarchical decomposition) over a shared world, 1,500 train / 1,400 test tasks; subtask outcomes are not independently verifiable, so an external LLM judge supplies binary labels only for rubric training. Baselines share the identical CISPO/LOO optimization pipeline: prompting (single- and multi-agent), single-agent GRPO/CISPO, and RAO / RAO+D / RAO+RT (RAO = recursive multi-agent RL with binary verifier/judge per-node rewards), plus SERA w/o D&RT and SERA w/o D ablations.

## Results & Highlights

- **Main results (Table 1, 3 seeds):** SERA attains 74.31% average success on TextCraft-Synth vs 68.93% for RAO (+5.38 pts) and 65.07% on TextWorld-Sync vs 51.93% (+13.14 pts) — the highest or second-highest average on both. Single-agent CISPO manages only 39.82% on TextWorld-Sync and multi-agent prompting 16.78%; merely exposing the model to a delegation interface (prompting nodes) is insufficient — RAO beats single-agent RL by double digits, and SERA's specialization on top of RAO is what the ablations attribute.
- **Rubric training is the largest single factor:** replacing binary per-node rewards with rubric scores from an *untrained* generator (SERA w/o D&RT) reaches 72.26% / 51.10% — only +3.33 over RAO on TextCraft and 0.83 *below* RAO on TextWorld. Training rubric generation (SERA w/o D) lifts this by +2.58 (TextCraft) and +9.16 (TextWorld); adding the decomposition reward (full SERA) adds another +4.81 on TextWorld, concentrated on Hard (+8.58) and Extreme (+17.42), though it slightly *hurts* TextCraft (−0.53 vs SERA w/o D). Conversely RAO+RT (rubric training with binary execution rewards) collapses to 45.62% on TextWorld vs RAO's 51.93% — the two components are coupled.
- **In-policy self-evaluation rivals a frontier judge (Table 3):** a trained policy writing and scoring its own per-subtask rubrics hits 74.84% average on TextCraft-Synth, beating Kimi-as-generator+scorer (73.10%) and Kimi-generator/policy-scorer (71.41%); gains concentrate on Hard (79.90% vs 77.45%) and Extreme (11.82% vs 4.17%). A single global rubric reused across subtasks drops to 69.77% (−4.49), and adding the label-free Rank+Disc discriminative reward (Ding et al. 2024) collapses performance to 65.77% (−9.07 pts) — score variance in a tree reflects subtask difficulty more than rubric quality, so label-free discrimination misleads when evaluation and solving share a policy.
- **External supervision drops sharply (Table 2):** verifier/judge calls fall from 550.9K to 257.9K on TextCraft-Synth (2.1x); on TextWorld-Sync external judge calls fall from 147.80K to 35.79K (4.1x) and judge input tokens from 633.80M to 125.32M (5.1x). SERA w/o D&RT uses zero external calls but plateaus at 51.10% on TextWorld vs SERA's 65.07% — the useful trade-off is selective external grounding during rubric training plus learned self-evaluation afterward.
- **Test-time scaling (Table 4):** with N=2 candidate branches selected bottom-up by rubric score, the SERA-trained policy judge raises TextWorld-Sync average from 65.07% to 67.50% (+2.43 pts; Extreme 22.86% -> 28.95%), statistically on par with the external Kimi-K3 judge (67.29%, Extreme 27.62%). Without SERA training, the policy judge yields −0.22 pts on average (16.57% vs 16.79% no-selection) and fails entirely on Hard/Extreme (0.00%) — SERA training is what turns the policy into a usable self-evaluator.
- **Training cost:** SERA is the fastest multi-agent method on TextCraft (24h06m vs RAO 29h36m on 2x8 H200) but ~2x slower than RAO on TextWorld (24h14m vs 13h08m), traded for the +13.14-point gain.

## Critical Assessment

- **Narrow evaluation scope, acknowledged by the authors.** Everything trains and evaluates on Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 and two procedurally generated *synthetic* text-game benchmarks (crafting recipes; cooking-world coordination). The central claim — learned rubric generation drives recursive-agent training — is untested on real-world agent tasks (web, code repositories, research workflows) and on larger models, where the judge-gap between a 4B scorer and a frontier judge might behave differently.
- **TextWorld-Sync is partially in-distribution for difficulty.** TextCraft-Synth trains on Medium only and transfers across recipe depths (a genuine generalization test), but the TextWorld-Sync training split already contains 900 Medium / 150 Hard / 450 Extreme tasks — so the headline +13.14 over RAO on TextWorld includes difficulty levels the policy was explicitly trained on, weakening the "generalization" reading on that benchmark.
- **Self-preference risk is mitigated, not eliminated.** The policy writes and scores its own rubrics; the safety net is the ranking objective anchored on verified outcomes. But this means performance is tethered to an external verifier/judge at training time: on TextWorld-Sync the anchor is itself an LLM judge whose labels are noisy, and in domains with no verifiable outcome at all (the authors' own stated future work), SERA has no grounding signal. The Rank+Disc ablation (−9.07 pts) is good evidence the anchoring is load-bearing, but robustness under sparse or noisy verification remains unmeasured — no experiment varies judge label quality.
- **Honest but mixed decomposition story.** The leaf-coverage reward helps only where subtasks are interdependent (+4.81 TextWorld) and slightly hurts TextCraft (−0.53); it treats all leaves equally, so a delegation tree that pads leaf count is not penalized by design — importance weighting is left as future work.
- **Compute and seeds.** Results average 3 seeds with std reported (good), but several TextCraft Extreme cells have large variance (e.g., SERA-level results in Table 1 fluctuate by several points across methods), and the 4B/16-GPU budget means the 2x TextWorld training wall-clock vs RAO (24h14m vs 13h08m) is a real cost not folded into the headline comparison.

## Strengths

- Clean conceptual contribution: separating *what* reward (continuous rubric score), *who* supervises (verifier only during rubric training), and *which tokens* each signal updates (alternating 16:2:2 with disjoint token supervision) is a crisp design that maps directly onto the ablation table.
- The rubric-as-ex-ante-commitment detail (frozen before the child runs, generator blind to the child's trajectory) is a principled defense against rubric-trajectory co-adaptation, and the gating mechanism (g_v = 0 forces score 0 on incomplete attempts) prevents noise from unearned partial credit.
- The most important scientific finding is isolated and falsifiable: rubric *training* (not rubric *use*) is the driver — SERA w/o D&RT vs SERA w/o D is a one-change comparison worth +9.16 pts on TextWorld-Sync, and the Rank+Disc degradation (−9.07) demonstrates *why* verified anchoring matters when evaluator and solver share weights.
- Strong experimental hygiene: baselines share the exact same CISPO/LOO optimization pipeline and update budgets; deterministic environment verification for all reported success rates; supervision-cost and wall-clock tables make the external-savings claim auditable; the test-time section honestly shows the untrained policy judge gives −0.22 pts, i.e., the capability is trained, not free.

## Limitations

- Only Qwen3-4B and two synthetic benchmarks; no real-world agent tasks, no larger models, no cross-model evaluation (a 4B policy scoring its own outputs vs a frontier judge may not transfer upward).
- Requires *some* verified outcome signal at training time (program verifier or LLM judge) for the ranking anchor; fully unverifiable domains are out of scope by construction.
- The learned rubric can still drift from genuine success if verification is sparse or noisy — the authors flag this as open, and no stress test of anchor noise is provided.
- Leaf-coverage decomposition reward is importance-blind (all leaves weigh equally) and empirically helps only on interdependent-subtask benchmarks while slightly hurting TextCraft-Synth.
- TextWorld-Sync training includes Hard and Extreme tasks, so difficulty generalization is only cleanly demonstrated on TextCraft-Synth; code and benchmark are promised but not confirmed released at v2; TextWorld training runs ~2x slower than RAO (24h14m vs 13h08m).

## Relevance

与你最高优先级的「Self-Evolving Agents & Automated Research」方向直接相关：SERA 展示了让智能体在共享权重内自学习「评估自身产物」的能力，并用经验证结果锚定的排序目标抵御自我评估漂移——这正是自动化 reward/rubric 设计与长程自主智能体可靠性问题的核心机制；其「学习式裁判还能在推理时做树搜索选择器」的结果对自我改进型 agent 系统很有参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does rubric-trained self-evaluation hold when the training-time anchor is a *noisy* judge rather than a deterministic verifier? A controlled injection of judge label noise would directly probe the failure mode the authors acknowledge.
- Would the gains transfer to a larger base model (e.g., 32B+) or to real-world delegation tasks (code repositories, web research) where subtask success is not programmatically checkable?
- Can subtask-level importance weights replace uniform leaf-coverage in the decomposition reward, removing the pad-the-leaf-count loophole without hurting TextCraft?
- The Rank+Disc collapse (−9.07 pts) is shown for one alternative objective — how do other label-free rubric-reward variants (e.g., adversarial rubric co-evolution) fare in the tree setting where score spread conflates difficulty and quality?
- Can the learned rubric generalize *across tasks* (write rubrics for unseen task families), enabling self-evaluation in settings where even the root outcome is only fuzzily verifiable?
