01Methodology — the experience loop

Distilled from the source; notation follows the paper.

EEM intervenes at the experimentation stage of an autonomous research run — the point where an agent must decide which experiments deserve compute. Every informative trajectory segment is compressed into an experience item e = (record, lesson): the record preserves context, direction, action and outcome; the lesson states applicability conditions, an evidence-bounded empirical finding, and a decision implication — filed under semantic tags (mechanism (in)effectiveness, error diagnoses, settings, comparisons).

Extraction runs in two stages: evidence grounding separates what the observation actually supports from what the agent expected, then hindsight abstraction converts grounded observations into reusable decision knowledge — blocking lessons invented from priors. At decision time the agent retrieves relevant lessons and assesses sufficiency h(d): if sufficient it decides directly (pursue / revise / reject); if not, it names the missing evidence g(d) and runs a bounded pilot — capped at 120 seconds — probing only that gap. Pilot and full-scale results are distilled back; the library starts empty per task, and cross-task warm-start is deliberately deferred over negative-transfer risk.

The pilot is explicitly not meant to approximate the full experiment — it only supplies the missing decision-critical evidence. Its cost is audited in 02; what remains unvalidated is the gate itself — see 03.

03aStrengths

  • Sufficiency as a first-class quantity Deciding which experiments deserve investment becomes an explicit, assessable gate — not an implicit side effect of reflection.
  • Evidence-grounded lessons The record + lesson representation, grounded in observations before abstraction, counters the hallucinated-lesson failure mode common in memory agents.
  • Quality and cost move together +13.1% overall on −11.4% tokens; experience reuse pays for its own distillation and pilot overhead (experimentation stage −18.4%).
  • Ablations match the claims Three components, three ablations, all matter: −24.9% without the library supports the thesis, and distillation beats raw record storage.
  • Fully automatic and portable No human-in-the-loop unlike AutoResearchClaw; works across coding-oriented and general-purpose backbones — and the paper honestly volunteers its own deficits.

03bLimitations

  • One benchmark, home team All results on the authors' own ARC-Bench vs their own prior system; no external benchmark (CORE-Bench, ScienceAgentBench, MLAgentBench) or third-party baseline — external validity unestablished.
  • LLM-judged where it matters most No human audit of the judge; the largest gain (+31.7%) sits in Result Analysis — the 50%-weighted, hardest-to-score dimension.
  • Variance ≈ margin Per-task spread (~0.09 on ML10) is the same order as the 25-topic headline margin (0.078), with no significance tests.
  • The core gate is unvalidated No error rates for h(d), no ablation of retrieval size K, no library-growth analysis; the 120 s pilot budget is fixed and unexamined.
  • Accumulation is within-task only The library resets per topic; cross-task transfer is deferred — a per-task experience loop, not the persistent memory the framing suggests.

04Questions for further exploration

  1. How reliable is the sufficiency gate in practice — false-sufficient vs false-insufficient rates, and the downstream cost of each error?
  2. Can the library warm-start across tasks (E0 ≠ ∅) without the flagged negative transfer — e.g., by conditioning transfer on applicability-condition matching?
  3. Do the gains survive external, non-home-team benchmarks (CORE-Bench, ScienceAgentBench) with human-audited or ensemble judging — especially on the heavily weighted Result Analysis metric?
  4. Is the benefit from storing experience or from the sufficiency-triggered pilot policy? A factorial design over storage × acquisition would beat the existing hybrid ablation.
  5. How does EEM compare head-to-head with Research Preference Models (Foster et al., 2026), which also run small pilots for candidate ranking but via learned preference rather than explicit lessons?
