Why this matters

Self-improving multi-agent systems need to know if evolved skills survive deployment on a different team — this gives a rare, honest, trace-level answer.

Critical edge

The 20/28 headline hides a split: CF is 13/14 positive, but AgentsNet is exactly 7/14 — half fail. Every number rests on a single recorded run: no repeated seeds, no error bars, and the acceptance rule tolerates one-task regressions on 15-task sets.

— Critical Assessment / Limitations

Methodology — distilled

Two evolved skill types, two task families, two model ladders, 28 transfer directions.

A source evolution→ B freeze & map→ I selection→execution→ II execution→benefit→ III confirm & held-out test

Evo2Team studies routing rules (which of small/medium/large model each agent calls) and communication rules (group size, message budgets, failure recovery) across hierarchical Count-Frequency aggregation and decentralized AgentsNet graph coordination, on GPT and Qwen model ladders spanning 4–32 agents — 14 bidirectional/scaling directions per family, 28 domain-direction cells.

Stage A evolves a source team's bank via trial-call evidence with quality regression plus price-ceiling acceptance, then freezes bank and policy. Stage B maps the frozen bank to a target team (relative model tier preserved, agent-count conditions adjusted); six frozen selectors — Direct, KNN, KMM, CORAL, Options, SF-GPI — rank eligible skills before adaptation.

Three analysis boundaries follow: I compares full node traces (not aggregate scores) to detect when different selections yield identical executions; II accounts per-task loss deltas vs an all-large baseline; III runs 3× fixed-graph confirmations, then held-out tests.

Positive-transfer bar: adaptation+confirmation must cost less than evolving a fresh target bank and the held-out outcome must beat all-large on quality and cost (or match quality at lower cost, ε = 0.05 RMSE).

Strengths

  • A sharper question than prior work: not "do skills help?" but when does a bank help a different team — operationalized via the three-boundary decomposition.
  • Execution-trace evidence — node traces, call counts, reroutes, large-model share — making claims like "different selectors, byte-identical executions" verifiable.
  • Honest cost accounting: exploration vs deployment separated, break-even batches published, diagnostic tests labeled as non-deployments.
  • Valuable negative results rarely reported: 22/22 confirmation passes vs 18/22 new-graph success; coverage (8 tasks) not implying benefit (−13.33 pp for G16→G32).
  • Complete appendix matrices — every direction × 8 baselines × RMSE/success/cost — enabling exactly the re-analysis the paper advocates.

Limitations

  • AgentsNet transfer is a coin flip: 7/14 positive, acceptance tolerates one-task regressions on a 15-task set, and only 45 of 150 direction-task records use transferred skills.
  • Single-run evaluations — no seeds, no confidence intervals; 4.1%-class RMSE gaps (e.g. Q16→Q32) could plausibly flip under model-response variance.
  • Synthetic scope only: counting + classical graph problems; no tool use, code, or long-horizon tasks where skill transfer matters most.
  • No external baselines: only its own six frozen selectors, KNN/CORAL feedback controls, and a DoRA-style scaling rule.
  • Costs ride one pricing snapshot (June 2026 API aliases); some directions never break even, and the "No composition" ablation that beats full Evo2Team on CF-Qwen is reported but not explained.

Questions for further exploration

Q1Would the three-boundary decomposition change its conclusions on longer-horizon, tool-using tasks where execution traces are far more diverse than counting or graph coordination?

Q2Can the four confirmation-to-test regressions be predicted from observable features (graph statistics, response entropy) before deployment — or is fresh-model variance irreducible without on-instance evaluation?

Q3Is "different banks, identical executions" a property of condition-ordered rule banks specifically, or would it persist for parametric or learned skill representations?

Q4The "No composition" ablation beats full Evo2Team RMSE in all three Qwen CF directions — is whole-recipe composition actually harming quality there?

Q5At what price ratio between small and large models does cross-ladder transfer stop being worth its exploration cost?
