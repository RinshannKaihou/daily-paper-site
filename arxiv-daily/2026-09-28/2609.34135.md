> **一句话总结：** 弗吉尼亚理工的 Evo2Team 框架首次把"技能库在多智能体系统间的迁移"拆成 选择→执行→收益 三个边界逐级追踪：在 Count-Frequency 聚合与 AgentsNet 图协同两类任务、4–32 agents、GPT 与 Qwen 两套模型阶梯共 28 个迁移方向上，源团队演化出的路由/通信技能使 20/28 个 held-out 迁移达到正迁移标准（如 CF G16→G32 将 RMSE 从 2.854 降到 2.161，大模型调用占比从 89.5% 降到 56.9%，部署成本 $2.763→$1.577），且每个方向的边际探索成本均低于在目标团队上从零演化（CF 为目标演化的 9.0–91.6%，AgentsNet 为 49.7–71.9%）——但 AgentsNet 上仅 7/14 方向为正，且固定图确认通过的任务在新图上有 4/22 回归失败。

> **TL;DR:** This paper asks whether a skill bank (routing rules for choosing small/medium/large LLMs and communication rules for grouping, message budgets, and recovery) evolved on one multi-agent system still helps a *different* target team. On Count-Frequency (CF) aggregation and AgentsNet graph coordination across team sizes 4–32 with GPT and Qwen model ladders (28 transfer directions), Evo2Team maps and adapts the frozen source bank and beats the strongest frozen selector baseline KNN in flagship cases: CF G16→G32 cuts mean RMSE from 2.854 (KNN) to 2.161 (+28 of 32 tasks improved) while dropping deployment cost from $2.763 to $1.577, and G8→Q8 / Q8→G8 reduce RMSE ~51% vs KNN (winning 31/30 of 32 tasks). Exploration costs 9.0–91.6% (CF) and 49.7–71.9% (AgentsNet) of evolving a fresh target bank, and 20 of 28 held-out outcomes are positive — but only 7 of 14 on AgentsNet, and 4 of 22 confirmation-passing task records regress on new graphs, showing that fixed-graph confirmation cannot certify transfer.

## 核心贡献

- **提出"选择→执行→收益"三边界分析框架**：首次把技能迁移的有效性逐级分解为（I）不同选择器选出的技能库是否产生不同执行、（II）执行变化惠及多少任务、（III）固定图上的三轮确认能否预示新实例上的表现——并用完整执行轨迹（node traces）而非聚合分数来回答。
- **Evo2Team 端到端流水线**：源团队演化（S/M/L 三档模型探测 + 质量与成本反馈修订路由/通信规则）→ 验证冻结 → 映射到目标团队（相对模型层级保持、按 agent 数调整适用条件）→ 六种冻结选择器选库 + 组合适配 + 执行 + 三次确认 + held-out 测试。
- **迁移经济学**：证明复用源技能库的边际探索成本在每个方向都低于目标团队从零演化（CF 9.0–91.6%、AgentsNet 49.7–71.9%，含复用参考运行一次计费后为 11.7–98.8% / 59.3–84.6%），并给出 break-even 批次数（如 Q4→Q4 需 209 个 test-sized batch 才回本）。
- **关键负结果与诊断**：KNN 与 CORAL 在两个 AgentsNet 方向选出不同技能库却产生逐字节相同的执行轨迹；CF 全部 14 个方向两者选出完全相同的有序技能；确认阶段 36 条迁移任务记录经三轮检查剩 22 条，全部通过固定图确认，但在新图上只有 18/22 成功——"确认通过 ≠ 新实例成功"。
- **组件消融**：task-group guard 一致地限制支出但对质量的影响依赖目标团队（Qwen 目标去掉后质量不变、成本升 15–17%；GPT 目标去掉后成功率反升，如 Q4→G4 从 1/15 升到 8/15）。

## Key Contributions

- **A selection→execution→benefit decomposition of skill transfer.** The paper traces evolved routing and communication skills through executed node traces rather than aggregate scores, separating (I) whether different selectors produce different executions, (II) how many tasks the executed changes reach, and (III) whether three fixed-graph confirmation runs predict new-instance outcomes.
- **Evo2Team, an end-to-end evolution-to-deployment pipeline**: source MAS probes small/medium/large models to revise routing rules and coordination/message/recovery rules; the bank is validated, frozen, mapped to the target team (relative model-tier preserved, agent-count conditions adjusted), then selected, adapted, executed, confirmed 3×, and held-out tested across 28 transfer directions.
- **Transfer economics**: marginal exploration cost is below fresh target evolution in every one of the 28 directions — adaptation+confirmation is 9.0–91.6% (CF) and 49.7–71.9% (AgentsNet) of target-evolution cost, or 11.7–98.8% / 59.3–84.6% when reused reference evaluations are charged once — with explicit break-even batch counts (e.g., 209 test-sized batches for AgentsNet Q4→Q4).
- **Diagnostic negative results**: KNN and CORAL select different banks in two AgentsNet directions yet produce identical recorded executions; in all 14 CF directions they select the same ordered skills. Of 36 task records using transferred recipes, three rounds of fixed-graph confirmation retain 22, all of which pass on confirmation graphs but only 18 of 22 succeed on new test graphs — four are regressions against both all-large and the mapped source policy.
- **Component ablations** showing the task-group guard buys lower cost with target-dependent quality effects (removing it raises GPT-target success, e.g., Q4→G4 from 1/15 to 8/15 at cost $0.152→$0.472).

## Methodology

**Skills and settings.** Two kinds of evolved skills are studied: *routing rules* (which of small/medium/large model each agent uses) and *communication rules* (group size, message budgets, failure recovery). Two task families are used: hierarchical Count-Frequency (CF) aggregation, where 4–32 agents count element frequencies in disjoint shards and merge/verify results, and decentralized AgentsNet graph coordination (coloring, matching, vertex cover, leader election, consensus on Watts–Strogatz / Barabási–Albert / Delaunay graphs). Two model ladders: GPT (gpt-4.1-nano / gpt-5.4-mini / gpt-5.4) and Qwen (qwen3-8b / qwen3.5-35b-a3b / qwen3.7-max), yielding 14 bidirectional/scaling directions per family and 28 domain-direction cells.

**Stage A — Source evolution and validation.** Source agents try S/M/L models under the same upstream context; trial-call evidence (quality and cost) drives revision of routing and communication rules via quality regression plus price-ceiling acceptance (epsilon = 0.05, eta = 0.02). Separate source tasks choose and confirm the deployed policy; the bank and source policy are then frozen.

**Stage B — Mapping to the target team.** A recipe r = (I, theta) selects skill IDs plus numeric parameter overrides. Routing rules keep their relative model tier under the new ladder; conditions depending on agent count are adjusted. Frozen selectors rank eligible source skills: Direct (reliability), KNN (12 nearest source contexts), KMM (import weighting), CORAL (feature alignment), Options (coverage/reliability/condition specificity), SF-GPI (probe outcomes with success/cost/latency/failure scoring), plus a DoRA-style numeric scaling control.

**Stage I (selection-to-execution boundary).** Because the planner reads banks in source order and executes a rule only when its condition applies, if K covers the eligible pool every selector includes all skills. The paper compares *full node traces* on shared tasks (method labels excluded, numeric formatting normalized) to detect when different selections yield identical executions.

**Stage II (execution-to-benefit boundary).** Adaptation proposes discrete edits (add/remove/replace skills, move routing interval boundaries to midpoints of observed values, adjust integer communication parameters, recombine recipes); proposals repeating evaluated plans are skipped, others screened on a subset of adaptation tasks. Coverage accounting: with T tasks using transferred recipes and per-task loss delta_i = m_i(L) − m_i(r) vs all-large, the average gain is bar-Delta = (T/N) * bar-Delta_T — so gains on a few tasks can look small overall even when per-task gains are large.

**Stage III — Confirmation and held-out testing.** Each recipe is compared to all-large L and the mapped source policy F via quality gap g(r,b) (CF: RMSE difference; AgentsNet: min of success and partial-correctness differences). Strict quality improvement is searched first, then equal-quality-at-lower-cost (c(r) ≤ (1+eta)c(F)). Shortlisted recipes run 3× on confirmation tasks (CF combines repeated RMSE; AgentsNet applies task-group guards with all-large fallback). Held-out transfer is positive if adaptation costs less than target evolution, the test outcome is better on both quality and cost (or similar quality at lower cost, epsilon = 0.05 in RMSE; AgentsNet allows one fewer success among 15 tasks at epsilon partial-correctness gap). Tests of unselected policies are marked diagnostic, not accepted deployments.

## Results & Highlights

- **Source evolution is strong but not sufficient**: the joint quality/price/model-tier/confirmation goal is met in **14 of 16** source settings; e.g., at GPT/16, source policy RMSE falls **2.203 → 1.525** vs all-large as cost falls **$1.006 → $0.933**, and Qwen/4 raises AgentsNet whole-network success **0.867 → 0.933** at cost **$0.418 → $0.385**.
- **Headline transfer win (CF G16→G32)**: Evo2Team cuts mean RMSE from **2.854 (KNN) to 2.161**, improving **28 of 32 tasks** over KNN; execution records show 4 groups vs KNN's 11, calls falling **57 → 42** per task, reroutes **608 → 0**, large-model call share **89.5% → 56.9%**, and deployment cost **$2.763 → $1.577** (−43%).
- **Cross-ladder transfers are the strongest**: **G8→Q8 and Q8→G8 each reduce mean RMSE by ~51% relative to KNN, winning 31 and 30 of 32 tasks**; Q4→Q8 cuts RMSE by **9–11%** and cost by **22–54%** against all six frozen selectors.
- **Overall outcome matrix**: **20 of 28** held-out outcomes meet the positive-transfer criterion (CF **13/14**, AgentsNet **7/14**), including three saved diagnostic tests; the 7 positive AgentsNet directions save **6.10–14.61%** deployment cost against all-large. 17 of 25 confirmation-selected policies yield positive held-out outcomes.
- **Selection alone explains little**: KNN and CORAL choose *different* banks in two AgentsNet directions (Q4→Q8, G4→G8) yet produce **identical recorded node traces**; in all 14 CF directions they select the same ordered skills. Reach also varies wildly: G8→Q8 gains are broad (31/32 tasks) while Q16→Q32 reduces mean RMSE only **4.1%** vs KNN and improves just **18 of 32** tasks.
- **Quality and cost can decouple**: G4→G8 lowers CF RMSE **1.201 → 0.832** but *raises* the 32-task test cost **$0.449 → $1.005**; Q4→Q8 lowers RMSE 1.007 → 0.964 at *lower* cost $0.345 → $0.335 — only the latter is a positive deployment outcome.
- **Confirmation does not certify new instances**: across five earlier-accepted AgentsNet directions, guard checks shrink transferred-recipe records **36 → 30 → 25 → 22**; all 22 succeed on fixed confirmation graphs, but only **18/22 succeed on new test graphs**, with 4 regressions against both all-large and the mapped source policy.
- **Ablations (6 AgentsNet directions + 3 CF Qwen directions)**: removing the task-group guard leaves Qwen-target quality unchanged while raising cost **15–17%**, but *raises* GPT-target success — Q4→G4 from **1/15 to 8/15** (cost $0.152→$0.472) and Q8→G8 from **5/15 to 10/15** (cost $1.195→$2.210). On all three Qwen targets, Evo2Team deploys cheaper than KNN/CORAL feedback controls and matches or exceeds KNN success; on the three GPT targets KNN succeeds on more tasks but costs more per deployment.
- **Exploration economics**: adaptation+confirmation costs **9.0–91.6%** (CF) and **49.7–71.9%** (AgentsNet) of evolving a fresh bank on the target; even charging reused reference runs once, every direction stays below 1.0 (CF **11.7–98.8%**, AgentsNet **59.3–84.6%**). Break-even against all-large deployment requires e.g. **75, 97, 209** test-sized batches (AgentsNet G4→Q4, Q4→Q8, Q4→G4).

## Authors & Credibility

- **Renxiang Wang and Jiaming Cui, Virginia Tech (Blacksburg, VA)** — a two-author academic group; the paper is a Virginia Tech preprint (September 2026). No prior-publication list is given in the main text, but the work sits squarely in the LLM-agent skill-evolution literature (Voyager, Agent Workflow Memory, SkillRL, SkillGrain, GEPA, EvoPrompt are all cited and positioned).
- Credibility signals are unusually strong for a systems-behavior paper: an explicit **AI-use statement**, **ethics statement** (reporting failure cases alongside cost benefits), and a **reproducibility statement** with an anonymous code archive, recorded source-skill mappings, full execution trajectories, per-direction cost matrices (Appendix A–D), exact API model IDs including dated snapshots (Table 18), and separated source/adaptation/reference cost accounting (Appendix E). The authors repeatedly resist overclaiming — e.g., explicitly labeling tests of unselected policies as "diagnostic, not accepted deployments" and noting that break-even counts are arithmetic scenarios, not provider invoices.

## Critical Assessment

- **Genuine novelty, but in framing more than mechanism.** The selection→execution→benefit decomposition and trace-level comparison (finding that KNN and CORAL pick different banks yet produce byte-identical executions) is a real contribution — most skill-transfer papers report only aggregate scores. The evolution/adaptation machinery itself, however, is assembled from known parts (quality-regression + price-ceiling acceptance, midpoint boundary proposals, K-skill quotas), and the paper does not compare against any *external* transfer method — the only controls are its own six frozen selectors, a KNN/CORAL feedback variant, and a DoRA-style scaling rule.
- **The headline "20/28 positive" hides a split**: CF is 13/14 positive while AgentsNet is exactly **7/14 — half the AgentsNet transfers fail**. The abstract leads with the aggregate; a reader must consult Table 9 to learn that cross-domain generalization of the framework is uneven. On AgentsNet, 15 test tasks mean one task = 6.67 percentage points, and the acceptance rule explicitly tolerates "one fewer success among fifteen," so positive/negative labels near the boundary are fragile.
- **No statistical treatment.** Every variant has "one recorded evaluation" on its task set (stated in Table 13/14 captions); there are no repeated seeds, no confidence intervals, and no significance tests for the 4.1%-RMSE-class differences (e.g., Q16→Q32's 5.070 vs KNN's 5.570 could plausibly flip under model-response variance the paper itself demonstrates via its fresh-resampling regressions).
- **Both benchmarks are synthetic and small.** CF is a counting aggregation task; AgentsNet is five classical graph problems. Neither involves long-horizon tool use, retrieval, or code — the authors concede "the next step is to evaluate the same workflow on longer-horizon tasks." The claimed $ savings are also sensitive to a single pricing snapshot (gpt-5.4 vs qwen3.7-max, June 2026 aliases), and Table 16–17 show some directions (e.g., CF G4→G8, several AgentsNet cells) never break even.
- **Missing ablation the paper itself motivates**: Stage II edits routing boundaries and communication parameters, but there is no ablation isolating *mapped routing rules* vs *adapted communication rules* — only coarse component removals (composition, plan filter, group guard, feedback controls). The "No composition" variant actually beats full Evo2Team RMSE in all three CF Qwen directions (e.g., Q4→Q4: 0.584 vs 0.621 at similar cost), which the paper reports but does not explain away.
- **Reproducibility is good on paper**: anonymous code archive, model IDs, task IDs, and full matrices are promised; the epsilon/eta constants (0.05/0.02), selection quotas, and acceptance rules are all specified. The main reproducibility risk is API non-determinism, which the paper acknowledges but does not quantify.

## Strengths

- **A sharper question than prior work**: not "do skills help?" but "when does a skill bank help a *different* team, which tasks benefit, and does it hold on new instances?" — with the three-boundary decomposition giving an operational answer.
- **Execution-trace-level evidence** (node traces, call counts, reroute counts, large-model call share) rather than aggregate scores, making claims like "different selections, same execution" verifiable.
- **Honest cost accounting**: exploration vs deployment separated, reused references charged once as a sensitivity, break-even batch counts published, and diagnostic (non-accepted) tests labeled as such.
- **Valuable negative results** rarely reported: confirmation on fixed graphs passing 22/22 while new-graph success is 18/22; coverage (8 tasks) not implying benefit (−13.33 pp success for G16→G32); the group guard helping cost on Qwen but suppressing success on GPT.
- **Complete matrices in the appendix** (Tables 4–8: every direction × 8 baselines × RMSE/success/partial/cost), enabling exactly the kind of re-analysis the paper advocates.

## Limitations

- **AgentsNet transfer is a coin flip**: 7/14 positive outcomes, and the acceptance rule tolerates one-task regressions on a 15-task test set, so per-direction conclusions rest on very few task records (45 of 150 direction-task records use transferred skills across all AgentsNet directions).
- **Single-run evaluations** with no error bars; the paper demonstrates fresh-model-response variance causes the 4 confirmation-to-test regressions, yet does not propagate that variance into the main comparisons.
- **Synthetic task scope only** (CF counting + AgentsNet graph coordination); no tool-use, code, or long-horizon agent tasks where skill transfer matters most in practice.
- **No external baseline methods**: comparisons are limited to the paper's own six frozen selectors, KNN/CORAL feedback controls under the same ceilings, and a DoRA-style scaling rule — not to published transfer/skill-selection systems.
- **Cost claims are snapshot-dependent** (proprietary API prices, alias-mapped model IDs) and some directions never break even; savings on small-ladder pairs (Q4→Q4) require 209 batches to amortize.
- **Ablation coverage is thin relative to the pipeline**: 6 AgentsNet directions + 3 CF directions out of 28, and the component that drives the CF gains (composition) is the one whose removal sometimes helps.

## Relevance

这篇论文直接命中你最关心的 Self-Evolving Agents 方向：它研究的正是"自演化智能体的技能库在部署时是否可靠迁移"这一可信性问题，并且用执行轨迹与成本经济学给出了少见的诚实现证答案，对设计自改进多智能体系统的验证/确认环节很有参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the selection→execution→benefit decomposition change its conclusions on longer-horizon, tool-using agent tasks where execution traces are far more diverse than CF counting or graph coordination?
- Can the four confirmation-to-test regressions be predicted from observable features (graph statistics, model response entropy) before deployment, or is fresh-model-response variance fundamentally irreducible without on-instance evaluation?
- Is the KNN-vs-CORAL "different banks, identical executions" phenomenon a property of condition-ordered rule banks specifically, or would it persist for parametric or learned skill representations?
- The "No composition" ablation beats full Evo2Team RMSE in all three Qwen CF directions — is whole-recipe composition actually harming quality there, and would per-task composition scores explain the gap?
- How does the break-even analysis generalize when model prices shift (e.g., small models improving faster than large ones), and at what price ratio does cross-ladder transfer stop being worth its exploration cost?
