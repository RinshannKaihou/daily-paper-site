§1

Methodology

Per-rubric histories · incremental judging · GRPO fusion

Setting A ReAct-style agent with two tools — Search and Visit — answers an open-ended question over T turns, ending in a report scored by a weighted rubric set (RaR reward). GRPO spreads that single outcome advantage across every turn, so a high-scoring rollout reinforces ineffective intermediate decisions. Standard credit-assignment fixes need a ground-truth answer — which open-ended tasks lack.

Rubric-grounded credit For each rubric k, Dr.Credit keeps a support history H(t,k) of evidence already banked. At every non-final turn, an LLM judge (Qwen3-35B-A3B) scores the new observation for incremental support only, relative to that history — repeated evidence earns zero. Per-rubric scores sum to the turn's credit.

Search vs Visit Visit turns get 0 / 0.2 / 0.5 per rubric (no / partial / high-value evidence). Search turns add two branches: navigation credit — a later Visit's credit flows back to the Search that surfaced the page via URL matching — plus snippet credit (η = 0.1 per accepted rubric match on never-visited results), summed and capped at 1.

Optimization Turn credits are group-normalized into a process advantage A^ry, added to GRPO's outcome advantage on non-final turns only — final-report supervision stays intact. Training: Qwen3-8B after 2-epoch SFT, then 200 RL steps on 7,149 query–rubric pairs (batch 32, G = 8 rollouts).

Credit pipeline, per turn

1Per-rubric history H(t,k) — evidence banked so far
↓
2LLM judge scores only new support vs history
↓
3Turn credit c(t) = Σk g(t,k); histories update by union
↓
4Group-normalize → process advantage A^ry
↓
5A(t) = A^O + A^ry on non-final turns; final turn keeps A^O alone

Visit: judge tiers 0 / 0.2 / 0.5 per rubric.
Search: nav attribution (URL match-back) ⊕ snippet judge (η = 0.1), capped at 1.

§3

Strengths vs Limitations

Strengths

  • Removes the canonical-answer dependency that blocks most credit assignment on open-ended rubric tasks — precise formalism: per-rubric histories, union updates, score-before-update.
  • Dual-branch Search credit cleanly decomposes "source discovery" (navigation) from "directly usable evidence" (snippet).
  • Unusually thorough main-text ablations: credit design, supervision source, and evidence history each isolated with quantitative deltas.
  • Analyses beyond leaderboards: evidence-acquisition curves, quality under turn budgets, honest wall-clock accounting including its own early overhead.
  • Strong credibility markers: 8B model competitive with frontier systems, gains on all four benchmarks (three unseen in training), training-time parity or better.

Limitations

  • Every Visit turn and snippet needs the 35B judge — scoring cost dominates early training and couples quality to judge capability and bias.
  • Evaluation confined to rubric-satisfaction metrics: no human study, no factuality/citation-accuracy audit, no non-rubric transfer benchmark.
  • Single scale and family (8B policy / 35B judge); the ratio may not transfer to larger policies or weaker judges.
  • Hyperparameter-heavy (0/0.2/0.5 tiers, η, caps, rejected discount) with only coarse main-text ablation; URL matching is brittle to redirect/tracking noise.
  • Search/Visit-only toolset: extension to code execution or other tools is asserted by the framework but never measured.
§4

Open questions & provenance

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does the +3.5 gain survive a judge from a different model family — and how does credit quality degrade with smaller judges?
  2. How does the method behave when rubrics are noisy or partially wrong, given the entire process signal is rubric-relative?
  3. Can exact URL-matching navigation be replaced with learned or embedding-based attribution where matching is unreliable?
  4. Does rubric-grounded process credit transfer to non-search tools — code execution, file editing — in broader agentic settings?
  5. At larger scale, does process supervision alone close the −2.3 outcome gap as rubric coverage grows?

UCAS School of AI + CAS State Key Lab of Multimodal AI Systems + Alibaba Token Hub + Peking University. First author Yingjian Zhu interned at Alibaba; corresponding authors Kun Ding (UCAS) and Zhenyi Wang (Alibaba).
