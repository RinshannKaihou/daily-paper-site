> **一句话总结：** 提出"边缘注意力"（把 post-softmax 注意力按 query 位置加权平均）这一新观测量，并证明它有两个方向的涌现结构：按 token 汇总的 per-token 边缘注意力是跨模型共享的文本属性（67 个模型与共识的 Spearman ρ=0.87，换一篇文档骤降至 0.02），按 head 汇总的 per-head 边缘注意力是跨文档稳定的模型私有属性（同模型跨文档 0.99，跨架构仅 0.37）；作者用输入-输出 Jacobian 的近可辨识性定理解释前者，并把后者做成一次性的 KV cache 逐 head 预算（Head Mass），在 RULER-4096 上 16× 压缩时 Qwen3-8B 达 87.3 分，远超同打分器的均匀预算基线 AdaKV+EA（46.9），且无需 KVzip 那样的每文档二次前向。

> **TL;DR:** The authors marginalize attention over query positions and find two emergent structures: the per-token reduction is shared across models (Spearman ρ=0.87 with a 67-model consensus on 850 Pile documents, dropping to 0.02 on a different document), while the per-head reduction is a stable, model-private signature (0.99 within a model across documents, 0.37 across architectures). A near-identifiability theorem on the input-output Jacobian explains the per-token sharing, and the per-head profile becomes a static, per-model KV-cache head budget ("Head Mass") that, paired with the training-free Expected Attention scorer, lifts Qwen3-8B RULER-4096 from 46.9 (AdaKV+EA) to 87.3 at 16× compression — approaching KVzip's 59.4–95.0 range with no per-document second forward pass — though KVzip still wins on phi-4 and Mistral-7B.

## 核心贡献

- **提出边缘注意力（marginal attention）**：把每个 head 的注意力按输出位置加权平均（权重 ω 为该位置经 value 投影的范数 ‖W_O v_s‖），得到一个 head×token 矩阵 M；沿两个方向约简得到两个新量——per-token（对 head 求和归一化）与 per-head（对 token 求和）边缘注意力。
- **两个对偶的涌现结构（实证）**：per-token 边缘注意力与模型无关、是文本的属性（67 模型共识一致性 ρ=0.87；同一模型在另一文档上仅 0.02）；per-head 边缘注意力与文档无关、是模型私有属性（同模型跨文档 0.99；不同架构 0.37；打乱 head 顺序 0.01）。实验覆盖 38 个家族、28 种架构、0.27B–117B 的 67 个开源模型（含 MoE 与 MHA），基于 The Pile 的 17 个领域 850 篇文档。
- **理论解释（Theorem 1）**：定义 per-token 边缘影响 j(X)（输入-输出 Jacobian 各块的 Frobenius 范数分布），在 Jacobian 光滑性、前序 token 依赖、对数衰减三条假设下证明：next-token logits 越接近的两个模型，其 per-token 边缘影响越接近（近可辨识性）；实证上边缘注意力是该影响的一阶逐 head 近似（Qwen2.5-0.5B 上平均 Spearman 0.635；16 模型 861 对的标量投影近似 0.861），甚至 Mamba、RWKV-4 这类无注意力模型的边缘影响也与共识一致。
- **应用：静态 KV cache 逐 head 预算**：用 per-head 边缘注意力（Head Mass, HM）在 200 篇预训练文档上离线计算一次预算（分钟级），在线只需一个 training-free 的 per-token 打分（Expected Attention, EA）；在 RULER-4096（13 任务、6500 文档、4×/8×/16× 压缩）上与逐文档重算预算的 KVzip、逐目标训练的 LU-KV 竞争力相当，同时把"预算分配"与"token 打分"解耦。
- **代码与可复现性**：估计器、投影与分解的库 + 端到端可复现 notebook 开源于 github.com/Flegyas/marginal-attention；67 个模型 checkpoint 全部公开并列明来源。

## Key Contributions

- **Marginal attention as a new observable.** Post-softmax attention is marginalized (ω-weighted, ω_s = ‖W_O v_s‖) over query positions, yielding a heads×tokens matrix M; two reductions — per-token (sum over heads, normalized) and per-head (sum over tokens) — are proposed as objects worth studying in their own right.
- **Two dual emergent structures (empirical).** Across 67 open models (38 families, 28 architectures, 0.27B–117B, incl. MoE and MHA) on 850 Pile documents (17 domains), the per-token profile agrees with a cross-model consensus at Spearman ρ = 0.87 (and 0.02 on a different document → property of the text), while the per-head profile is document-agnostic and model-private (0.99 within model across documents, 0.37 across architectures, 0.01 permuted).
- **Identifiability theory.** Defining per-token marginal influence j(X) from input–output Jacobian block norms, Theorem 1 shows that models with near-equal next-token logits under small perturbations have near-equal marginal influences, under three assumptions (Jacobian smoothness, preceding-token dependence, log decay). Empirically, marginal attention is the first-order per-head approximation of this influence (avg Spearman 0.635 on Qwen2.5-0.5B; 0.861 weighted vs 0.772 unweighted scalar projection over 861 pairs from 16 models); attention-free Mamba and RWKV-4 influences also match the consensus.
- **Static KV-cache head budget (Head Mass).** The per-head profile, computed once offline on 200 pretraining documents (minutes, no target-model forward), replaces per-document or per-target-trained budgets; paired with the training-free Expected Attention scorer it is competitive with KVzip (per-document reconstruction pass) and LU-KV (per-target training) on RULER-4096 at 4×/8×/16× compression across five 7B–32B models.
- **First formal result in the Platonic-representation space for mechanisms.** The authors position Theorem 1 as the first formal result of the kind discussed by the Platonic Representation hypothesis, applied to mechanisms rather than representations — while noting it does not imply semantic convergence.

## Methodology

**Definitions.** For an n-token sequence X and head h, marginal attention is m_s^(h)(X) = (1/n) Σ_t ω_s^(h)(X) A_{s,t}^(h)(X), with ω_s^(h) = ‖W_O^(h) v_s(X)‖ (the norm of what position s sends through head h; both factors computable in a single forward pass). The per-token profile a(X) = (1/Z) Σ_h m^(h) is a distribution over positions; the per-head profile p_h = E_X[p_h(X)] with p_h(X) = W_h(X)/Z(X) is one vector per model.

**Cross-model/cross-document protocol.** Per-token profiles are broadcast: each token's value is compared across models by Spearman correlation on the token's value after removing the average over 30 relative-position bins; each model is compared to the consensus of all others (2211 pairs total), with a "seed ceiling" from 10 pythia-410m pretraining seeds. Heads of different models have no correspondence, so per-head profiles are binned into 10 relative-depth bins; within-model stability is measured half-vs-half over documents (170 documents, 10 per domain, seeded). Controls include deleting punctuation/whitespace, varying head counts, and permuting heads.

**Jacobian link.** The per-token marginal influence j_s(X) is the distribution of Frobenius norms of the Jacobian blocks J^(s,t). For a single attention layer with fixed weights, head h contributes A_{s,t}^(h) W_OV^(h) x_s to the block — exactly the summand of marginal attention — so a ∝ Σ‖J-block‖ when head contributions do not cancel; verified directly: along Pythia-410M training the head-summed block stays within 5% of the sum of orthogonal heads' values (≤17% below at final checkpoints). The full Jacobian is estimated with Hutchinson on 85 documents (block-of-blocks projection to characters, ranks, 50 relative-position bins); a cheaper scalar projection of each block's gradient of log-probability of the observed token approximates influence across models. Theory assumes Jacobian smoothness, preceding-token dependence, and log decay, with diagnostics in the appendix.

**Eviction.** Retention score S_{h,s}(X) = β_h · γ_h(X): a per-head budget β_h times a per-entry score γ. Head Mass sets β_h = H·B·p̄_h (clipped at floor 0.01, rescaled so heads spend exactly B on average) from the per-head profile on 200 pretraining documents; the score is Expected Attention (EA). Baselines: AdaKV+EA (uniform budget), AdaKV+KVzip, KVzip (both budget and score per-document), LU-KV+EA (per-target offline calibration), HM+Guide (budget tilted by a linear map predicting KVzip's score from Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct — a probe, not a practical method). Evaluation: RULER (13 tasks, 6500 documents, context 4096, compression 4×/8×/16×) on Qwen3-8B, Llama-3.1-8B, phi-4, Qwen3-32B, Mistral-7B; LongBench v2 and per-task/cost tables in the appendix.

## Results & Highlights

- **Per-token sharing is real and text-locked.** Pooled over 67 models and 2211 pairs, each model's per-token profile agrees with the 67-model consensus at Spearman ρ = 0.87 (comparable to the pythia-410m 10-seed ceiling), but only 0.02 with the consensus on a different document — the signal is a property of the text, not a generic attention prior. Agreement is highest for a model vs its own instruct version, and decays with family/data distance.
- **Per-head profile is model-private.** Within one model, the profile computed on one half of the documents agrees at ρ = 0.99 with the profile on the other half; across models of different architectures on the same depth bins it drops to 0.37 (different labs and data: 0.20; permuted heads: 0.01).
- **Attention ≈ first-order Jacobian part.** On Qwen2.5-0.5B over 85 documents, per-token marginal attention vs per-token marginal influence agrees at average Spearman ρ = 0.635 across domains (lowest on EuroPar; near-zero sanity check 0.022 on a distinct document); uniform weights ω ≡ 1 reduce this to 0.534. Across models, the cheaper scalar-projection influence agrees with the consensus at 0.861 (weighted) vs 0.772 (unweighted) over 861 pairs from 16 models; Mamba and RWKV-4 — with no attention mechanism — still show consensus-agreeing marginal influence.
- **KV eviction: static budget ≈ per-document budget.** On RULER-4096 (13 tasks) at 16× compression, HM+EA scores 87.3 on Qwen3-8B vs 46.9 for AdaKV+EA (same scorer, uniform budget) and 49.9 for AdaKV+KVzip, against 95.4 uncompressed; KVzip reaches only 59.4 there. HM+EA beats AdaKV+KVzip in all but one cell and closes most of the EA→KVzip gap without KVzip's double target forward; the HM budget is built offline in minutes whereas a KVzip surrogate trains for hours. But KVzip remains best on phi-4 (95.6/95.6/91.8 at 4×/8×/16×) and Mistral-7B 8×/16× (89.2/72.2), and HM+EA degrades on both Qwen3 targets at 16× (Qwen3-32B: 79.1 vs 95.7 uncompressed).
- **What the shared profile tracks.** Regressing word properties against the consensus profile attributes the explainable signal overwhelmingly to grammatical role (0.99) rather than surface form (0.35), local predictability (0.27), word frequency (0.24), or position (0.02); the shared profile separates documents and emerges over training only after models beat a bigram baseline (cross-family agreement rises as loss falls).

## Critical Assessment

The core empirical dichotomy — per-token shared across models, per-head model-private — is demonstrated with unusually broad coverage (67 models incl. MoE, MHA, and attention-free Mamba/RWKV cross-checks, seed ceilings, permuted-head controls), and the eviction application is a genuinely useful decoupling rather than a bolted-on demo. That said, several gaps deserve scrutiny. First, the theory does not close: Theorem 1 is about marginal *influence*, and the link from influence back to marginal *attention* is explicitly empirical — and only moderately strong at that (average Spearman 0.635, falling to 0.534 with uniform weights). The headline "we explain the per-token emergence theoretically" therefore rests on a two-link chain whose second link is an approximation the authors cannot yet prove, and the ω = ‖W_O v_s‖ weighting is a degree of freedom doing real work (0.861 vs 0.772; 0.635 vs 0.534), even though the unweighted variant is honestly reported. Second, the corpus bound matters: consensus is computed on mostly-English documents capped at 2048 tokens (the context limit of older models in the pool), so the "property of the text" claim is untested at the long contexts where KV eviction actually matters, and RULER evaluation runs at context 4096 (16× → effective 256 retained tokens), not the 32k–128k regimes of production serving. Third, on the application the method is competitive but not dominant: KVzip still wins outright on phi-4 and Mistral-7B, and HM+EA's large per-token variance on the two Qwen3 targets (e.g., 79.1 vs 95.7 uncompressed on Qwen3-32B at 16×) suggests averaging budgets over generic pretraining text loses task-specific allocation; the HM+Guide variant that would address this needs an extra small-model forward per document and, by the authors' own admission, is not servable. Fourth, the theorem's three assumptions (smoothness, preceding-token dependence, log decay) are validated with empirical diagnostics rather than proven to hold for real models, making the result a "one logit near-identifiability" statement with constants that give qualitative, not quantitative, control. These are honest limitations the paper itself flags, which increases rather than decreases confidence in the parts that are demonstrated.

## Strengths

- A crisp conceptual contribution: one observable (marginal attention) with two reductions that cleanly split into a text property and a model property, each verified against the right null (different document for the former, permuted heads for the latter).
- Exceptional empirical breadth: 67 models, 38 families, 28 architectures, 0.27B–117B, MoE and MHA, plus attention-free architectures corroborating the Jacobian story — far beyond the 2–3 model comparisons typical in this literature.
- Theory and practice genuinely connected: the same quantity that the identifiability theorem protects (per-token) is what allows the token score to be supplied by a 15× smaller model, and the quantity that is model-private (per-head) is what makes a static budget meaningful.
- Practical payoff with a cost argument: offline minutes + training-free scoring vs KVzip's per-document double forward and surrogate training (hours), with most of the quality recovered.
- Reproducibility as a first-class concern: released library, end-to-end notebooks, public checkpoints, unchanged third-party harnesses, and a transparent AI-use statement.

## Limitations

- The influence→attention link is empirical only (ρ = 0.635 average; 0.534 unweighted); the theory covers per-token marginal influence, not marginal attention itself.
- Consensus corpus is mostly English and capped at 2048 tokens per document; long-context and multilingual generality of both findings is unverified.
- Eviction is evaluated at context 4096; HM+EA falls behind on both Qwen3 targets at 16× compression and never beats KVzip on phi-4 or Mistral-7B at higher compression.
- HM+Guide (the stronger score tilt) requires an extra small-model forward per document, unsupported by standard serving stacks — reported as a probe only.
- Theorem assumptions are validated empirically, not established for real trained models; the guarantee is near-identifiability with constants of unknown tightness.

## Relevance

该文属于 LLM 隐藏状态与可解释性/表示几何的核心议题：用 Jacobian 可辨识性理论解释跨模型注意力结构的涌现，与用户关注的表示收敛、内部表征分析高度契合；其 KV cache 逐 head 静态预算应用也轻度触及"极限性能优化下 correctness 如何保持"这一兴趣点（16× 压缩下不同模型退化差异很大）。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the per-token consensus survive at 32k–128k token contexts and in non-English text, where position-0/sink tokens and long-range structure dominate marginal attention?
- Can the influence→attention link be proven (i.e., does ω-weighted marginal attention directly satisfy the three assumptions), closing the gap between Theorem 1 and the measured quantity?
- Is the shared per-token profile behaviorally consequential — does it predict representation stitching, transfer, or distillation success — or is it an epiphenomenon of shared next-token objectives?
- Why does the pretraining-text-averaged Head Mass budget visibly underperform on the two Qwen3 targets at 16× — is it the hybrid attention pattern, or does task-adaptive budget allocation fundamentally require per-document signals at high compression?
- Could the Guide tilt be replaced by a servable approximation (e.g., a cached per-position-type correction) to get KVzip-level quality without an extra forward pass?
