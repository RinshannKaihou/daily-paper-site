> **一句话总结：** 把"LLM judge 有多准"改写成"在错误预算 α 下，judge 可证明接管多少 agent 评测工作量"：针对 agent 语料按任务强聚类（组内相关 ρ 高达 0.81）导致 i.i.d. 证书虚高的问题（朴素 Clopper-Pearson 证书在 WEB-M 上声称 98% 自动化，实际错误率却在 17.5% 的任务重采样中超预算，是承诺值 5% 的 3.5 倍），提出按任务级自助重采样 + Bonferroni 校正的 TaskBoot 证书；在该证书下，一个 4B logprob 判官（SFT + 拒绝加权 GRPO）在 α=0.1 时可证明接管 TOOL 上 29.7%、WEB-A 上 58.5% 的评测量，是强引导前沿模型中唯一在两个主语料上均获非零证书的判官，且证书还能为自训练伪标签给出污染上界（六次实测 0.000–0.041）。

> **TL;DR:** The paper reframes LLM-judge quality as a deployment question — what fraction of agent-evaluation work can a judge provably absorb at error budget α with confidence 1−δ. Because trajectories from the same task arrive in correlated clusters (intra-task ρ up to 0.81), i.i.d. selective-prediction certificates overstate safety: on WEB-M (MiniWoB++, 48 agents) the standard Clopper-Pearson certificate claims 98% automation while its realized error exceeds the budget in 17.5% of task resamples vs the promised 5%. The proposed TaskBoot (task-level bootstrap over a 40-quantile threshold grid, Bonferroni-corrected) is the only construction certifying non-trivial coverage (0.30–0.84) on all seven agent corpora, where per-task CP / Hoeffding / design-effect-corrected baselines certify exactly 0. Under it, a 4B logprob judge (SFT then GRPO) certifies 29.7% of TOOL and 58.5% of WEB-A trajectories auto-decidable at α=0.1 (base judge: 0.000/0.510; RL over SFT adds +0.004/+0.025), versus 0.000 on TOOL for every strongly elicited frontier judge — the one frontier config that beats it on WEB-A (0.704 vs 0.585) costs ~100× more per decision.

## 核心贡献

- **问题重构（certificate, not agreement）：** 不再问"judge 与人类一致率多少"，而是问运维方真正需要的问题——给定错误预算 α（{0.1, 0.2}）与置信 1−δ（δ=0.05），判官可证明接管的评测工作量最大比例是多少；reject（自动判失败）与 release（自动判成功）两侧按各自错误泛函分别认证。
- **指出并修复 i.i.d. 证书的失效：** 同一任务的多条轨迹强相关（7 个语料 ρ∈[0.25, 0.81]，设计效应最高 17.6），逐任务 Clopper-Pearson、任务均值 Hoeffding、DEFF 校正 CP 等有限样本聚类方法在全部 7 个真实语料（G=7–382）上证书为 0；朴素 i.i.d. CP 证书在 WEB-M 上的实际错误率在 17.5% 的任务重采样中超预算。
- **TaskBoot 证书：** 在预声明的 40 个分数分位阈值网格上，按"任务"（而非轨迹）做自助重采样计算聚类错误分位数，Bonferroni 校正后认证最大覆盖阈值；模拟实验（1.5×10⁷ 轨迹、每格 300 次、G 低至 20）显示违反保证率 ≤1%，且与朴素 CP 覆盖率差距在 1 个点以内——即"均匀地安全，且不牺牲覆盖"。
- **4B logprob 判官 + 非对称 GRPO：** 单 token verdict 的归一化概率打分（一次前向、禁用推理链），先 SFT 再用 μ>λ 的非对称奖励 GRPO（加重惩罚"误杀成功"）；在 α=0.1 认证 TOOL 0.297 / WEB-A 0.585，是强引导前沿判官中唯一双主语料非零者；Proposition 1 证明证书对分数的严格递增变换不变——Platt/等温回归/温度缩放都救不了任何判官，只有换排序（换判官）才行。
- **可训练前预测证书覆盖率：** 两参数关系 cōv* = 1.08·(1−π)(2A₀−1) − 0.05（π 为语料成功率、A₀ 为未训练判官 AUROC）在留一语料下解释 R²=0.96（MAE 0.033），并给出 RL 何时在 SFT 之上有效的三个可测门槛（A₀≫0.5、SFT 未饱和、n_eff≥≈29）。
- **CertHarvest：证书兼作自训练滤波器：** 只从 certified 区域收割伪标签，污染由构造被 α 上界约束（六次收割实测 0.000–0.041，池 914–2,200 条）；零目标域标签下 WEB-A 达 0.586（≈域内 GRPO 上限 0.585）、WEB-M 达 0.830，TOOL 域内两轮 0.293→0.347 超过最好的监督 GRPO 臂 0.321；无证书时拒绝收割。

## Key Contributions

- **Reframe:** turns "how good is a judge" into "how much evaluation work can it provably absorb" — a certified-coverage operating point (largest auto-decided fraction with error ≤ α at probability 1−δ), certified separately on the reject and release sides.
- **Diagnosis:** task clustering in agent corpora (ρ 0.25–0.81, design effects up to 17.6) makes standard i.i.d. selective-prediction certificates invalid; every finite-sample cluster-valid alternative (per-task CP, Hoeffding on task means, DEF-corrected CP) certifies zero coverage on all seven real corpora (G=7–382).
- **TaskBoot:** a task-level bootstrap test over a pre-declared 40-quantile threshold grid with Bonferroni accounting; simulation (1.5×10⁷ trajectories, 300 trials/cell, down to G=20) shows guarantee violations in ≤1% of trials while matching naive CP's coverage within a point — uniformly safe at no cost in coverage.
- **A 4B logprob judge** (renormalized single-token SUCCESS probability, SFT then GRPO with an asymmetric reward penalizing discarded successes) certifies 0.297 (TOOL) / 0.585 (WEB-A) at α=0.1 — the only judge among strongly elicited frontier configurations certifying on both headline corpora; Proposition 1 (rank invariance) shows no monotone recalibration can rescue the others.
- **Predictability ex ante:** a two-parameter law on corpus success rate π and untrained-judge AUROC (A₀) predicts certified coverage with leave-one-corpus-out R²=0.96 (MAE 0.033), plus three measurable gates for when RL helps over SFT.
- **CertHarvest:** the certificate doubles as a self-training filter — pseudo-labels harvested inside certified regions have contamination bounded by α by construction (realized 0.000–0.041 across six harvests), enabling zero-target-label transfer to the in-domain GRPO ceiling on WEB-A/WEB-M and beating supervised GRPO on TOOL.

## Methodology

**Setup.** A corpus is trajectories x_gj grouped by task g with binary outcomes y_gj ∈ {0,1} from expert annotation or a trusted oracle. A judge maps a rendered trajectory to a score s(x) ∈ [0,1]. A selective automation rule is a pair of thresholds (θ_rej, θ_rel): trajectories with s(x) ≥ θ_rel are auto-released as successes, s(x) ≤ θ_rej auto-rejected as failures, the rest go to a human. The two sides carry different error functionals (discarded successes vs shipped failures) and are certified separately: ccv*(α,δ) = max_θ cov(θ̂) s.t. Pr(err(θ̂) > α) ≤ δ.

**Corpora and protocol.** Seven corpora spanning tool-use dialogues (TOOL, from τ²-bench), terminal sessions (TERM), three web corpora (WEB-A = 1,302 expert-annotated ARB trajectories; WEB-M/WEB-F = MiniWoB++ with weak/frontier agents), and two code-repair corpora (CODE-O from OpenHands; CODE-S multilingual). Every corpus is split by task into SFT/RL/CAL/TEST with one declared seed; every hyperparameter uses CAL only; TEST is read once per configuration. The protocol is pre-registered and failure corpora stay in the main text. Trajectories are rendered as text with no reward or oracle signal — the judge must infer the outcome from what the agent saw and did (making CODE-O judge-blind by design: its outcome is a held-out test suite never shown in the trajectory).

**TaskBoot certificate.** Fix a side, a grid Θ of 40 empirical score quantiles (linspace 0.02–0.98), and task-grouped calibration data. For each θ, draw B bootstrap resamples of the G tasks with replacement and recompute the selective error on each resample (Eq. 3); θ is certified if the upper (1−δ/|Θ|)-quantile of the clustered error ratio is ≤ α (Bonferroni). Return the maximum-coverage certified θ, or certify nothing. The bootstrap quantile consistently estimates the clustered error ratio's sampling distribution as G grows (Field & Welsh 2007); validity is checked by simulation under a task-effect model (ρ ∈ [0.1, 0.8], G ∈ {20,50,100,500}) and by out-of-sample audits (θ̂ calibrated on CAL, realized error measured once on held-out TEST) on every real corpus.

**Judge.** A 4B-parameter instruction model prompted as a skeptical auditor answering a single verdict token; the score is the renormalized next-token probability s(x) = p(SUCCESS|x)/(p(SUCCESS|x)+p(FAIL|x)) — continuous, one forward pass, reasoning disabled, every trajectory parses by construction. Training: SFT on verdict labels from the task-disjoint SFT split, then GRPO with an asymmetric reward r(v,y) = 1[v=y] − λ·1[success rejected] − μ·1[failure released], where μ > λ (reject-weighted) penalizes discarding successes more than shipping failures; strength swept over {1,3,5} plus the accuracy arm, and the arm is selected on CAL by certified coverage (the test-optimal arm is reported once, labeled an oracle).

**CertHarvest (certificate-gated self-training).** Certify a reject region on one half of CAL tasks, pseudo-label the other half, retain failures, re-certify — always on human-labeled CAL/TEST tasks that pseudo-labels never touch, so the loop cannot erode its own guarantee; contamination is bounded by α with probability 1−δ and refused entirely when no certificate exists.

## Results & Highlights

- **i.i.d. certificates overstate safety (the motivating failure):** on WEB-M (ρ=0.80, G=32, 8 rollouts/task), the standard Clopper-Pearson-style i.i.d. certificate selects a threshold covering 98% of trajectories at α=0.2, but its realized error exceeds the budget in 17.5% of task resamples — 3.5× the promised δ=5% (6.9% at α=0.1); on mildly clustered corpora the same audit passes (≤3.2%).
- **All finite-sample cluster-valid alternatives certify nothing:** per-task CP and task-mean Hoeffding certify exactly 0 on all seven corpora (G=7–382), and DEF-corrected CP certifies 0 everywhere at α=0.1, while TaskBoot certifies 0.30–0.84 wherever a usable judge exists; in simulation TaskBoot violates its guarantee in ≤1% of trials in every cell including G=20 (1.5×10⁷ trajectories, 300 trials/cell).
- **Main judge result (Table 2, certified reject coverage @ α=0.1, δ=0.05):** the 4B judge goes 0.000 → 0.293 (SFT) → 0.297 (GRPO) on TOOL and 0.510 → 0.569 → 0.585 on WEB-A (Δ_RL +0.004 / +0.025); WEB-M saturates at 0.832 → 0.836 and WEB-F at 0.758, CODE-O is judge-blind (0.000 everywhere), and TERM/CODE-S fail for lack of tasks (G=13/7).
- **Frontier judges certify inconsistently (Table 3):** gpt-5.6-sol (CoT + verbalized probability) certifies 0.704 on WEB-A vs the trained 4B's 0.585 — at ~100× the inference cost per decision — but certifies 0.000 on TOOL (AUROC 0.794 vs the 4B's 0.903); claude-sonnet-5, gemini-2.5-pro (CoT and self-consistency), and gpt-4o (logprob) all certify 0.000 on WEB-A as well, making the 4B the only judge certifying on both headline corpora.
- **Out-of-sample audits hold:** realized reject error at α=0.1 is 0.027–0.037 on WEB-A and 0.011–0.012 on TOOL — never above budget.
- **Coverage is predictable before training:** cōv* = 1.08·(1−π)(2A₀−1) − 0.05 fits the seven corpora with leave-one-corpus-out R²=0.96 and MAE 0.033 (in-sample 0.98); dropping (1−π) collapses LOOCV R² to 0.38 and AUROC alone to 0.47.
- **Self-training with bounded contamination:** across six CertHarvest harvests (pools of 914–2,200 trajectories), realized contamination was 0.000–0.041, never above the α=0.10 budget; a leave-one-corpus-out judge with zero WEB-A training labels certifies ~0.575 and one harvest round closes to the in-domain GRPO ceiling (0.586 vs 0.585); on WEB-M the transfer judge already certifies 0.830, matching the ceiling; on TOOL, in-domain SFT (0.293) + two harvest rounds reaches 0.347, past the best supervised GRPO arm (0.321) — but a second round on WEB-A degrades 0.585 → 0.465 despite contamination staying in budget (0.024).
- **What the coverage buys:** at six minutes of human review per trajectory, the certified operating points save roughly 30 reviewer-hours per 1,000 trajectories on TOOL and 59 on WEB-A, with error ≤ 0.1 guaranteed on the auto-decided portion; the judge runs on one local, on-premise GPU.

## Critical Assessment

- **The statistics is deliberately simple, and that is both the point and the ceiling.** TaskBoot is a task-level percentile bootstrap with Bonferroni over a 40-point grid — no new theory. The real contribution is the negative result: every "proper" finite-sample cluster-valid construction (per-task CP, Hoeffding on task means, DEF-corrected CP) collapses to zero coverage at realistic G, so an asymptotic-in-G bootstrap approximation validated by simulation is the only non-vacuous option. The authors are upfront that this is not a finite-sample theorem, but buyers of the guarantee should understand it is simulation-audited, not proven at finite G.
- **The headline indictment of i.i.d. certificates rests on one corpus.** The 17.5%-violation is demonstrated on WEB-M (ρ=0.80, G=32) alone; the paper's own three adversarial designs (size–outcome correlation, heavy-tailed cluster sizes, matched to WEB-M) failed to break the naive certificate at population level because its Bonferroni slack absorbs the abuse. So "i.i.d. certificates overstate safety" is empirically a one-corpus, one-regime (high-ρ, many-rollout) claim — flagged in Limitations, but the abstract's framing is broader than the evidence.
- **The RL story is weaker than the abstract implies.** "Reject-weighted GRPO" headlines the judge, yet on TOOL the CAL certificate cannot rank the three GRPO arms (0.150/0.149/0.145) and the CAL-selected GRPO adds only +0.004 over SFT (0.297 vs 0.293); the 0.321 reject-weighted figure is a test-oracle arm reported once, labeled as an oracle. On WEB-A all arms sit on the same 0.585 plateau. Most of the certified coverage comes from the SFT'd base plus corpus structure, not the asymmetric reward.
- **Small corpora carry the headline numbers.** WEB-A has n=200 trajectories (G=68), split four ways — so "transfer matches the in-domain ceiling (0.586 vs 0.585)" and WEB-A certificate differences generally are within the resolution of a few hundred held-out trajectories; TOOL's n=1,280 is more comfortable but its certified coverage is only ~0.30.
- **Modest, mostly one-sided automation.** The release side rarely certifies at practical budgets, so the deployed guarantee is mostly "auto-reject failures, humans still confirm successes." That is honest and correctly stated, but "certified selective automation" could be over-read as full-pipeline automation by skimmers.
- **Reproducibility depends entirely on appendices.** The base 4B model family is not named in the main text (training details in Appendix H), frontier elicitation is only sketched in Table 3, and the protocol's single-seed design means run-to-run variance is unquantified (the authors say so). The predictability "law" is a 7-point, one-seed fit — LOOCV R²=0.96 on seven points is fragile, and the three RL gates are stated post-hoc.
- **Red flags: none serious.** The candor about failures (judge-blind CODE-O, uncertifiable TERM/CODE-S, the degrading second self-training round on WEB-A) offsets the mild abstract-vs-body inflation around the GRPO weighting.

## Strengths

- Asks the operationally correct question (how much work can the judge provably absorb) and answers it with an auditable, pre-registered protocol rather than an agreement rate.
- The rank-invariance proposition is crisp and immediately actionable: Platt scaling, isotonic regression, and temperature scaling provably leave every certificate unchanged — recalibration cannot rescue a judge, only a different ranking (a different judge) can.
- TaskBoot is uniformly safe at no cost in coverage in every tested regime (≤1% violation in simulation; realized errors 0.011–0.037 vs α=0.1 in out-of-sample audits), while all cluster-valid finite-sample alternatives certify zero.
- The certificate-gated self-training loop is a rare concrete safety interlock for self-improvement: contamination bounded by construction, verified at 0.000–0.041 across six harvests, and the system refuses to harvest when no certificate exists.
- Failure regimes are diagnosed, not hidden: judge-blind corpora (CODE-O), saturated corpora (WEB-M/F), and too-few-task corpora (TERM/CODE-S) all stay in the main text with mechanistic explanations.
- Practical economics are quantified: ~30–59 reviewer-hours saved per 1,000 trajectories, on a single local GPU, versus a frontier configuration costing ~100× more per decision that still certifies 0 on TOOL.

## Limitations

- TaskBoot's guarantee is asymptotic in the number of task clusters, validated by simulation down to G=20 plus out-of-sample audits — not a finite-sample theorem; the anti-conservativeness of the naive i.i.d. certificate is demonstrated on one real corpus, not universally.
- Everything comes from a single pre-registered run: one seed for the task-level splits, hyperparameters on calibration data, one test read; the cluster bootstrap quantifies sampling uncertainty over tasks but not training randomness.
- The deployed guarantee mostly removes review of failures — release-side coverage rarely certifies at practical budgets, so successes still require human confirmation.
- The certifiability model is an empirical trend fit on seven corpora with one seed; the three RL gates are stated post-hoc.
- Certified self-training is a one-round recommendation: a second round on WEB-A degraded coverage from 0.585 to 0.465 even though harvest contamination stayed within budget (0.024) — the α-bound caps how many pseudo-labels are wrong, not how skewed the harvested pool is.
- Small corpora bind: WEB-A's n=200, TERM's G=13, and CODE-S's G=7 sit at or below the regime where the certificate can resolve anything, and CAL-based arm selection could not rank GRPO arms on TOOL (0.150/0.149/0.145).

## Relevance

该论文正中用户最高优先级方向"自进化智能体与自动化研究"：它为长时程自主 agent 系统的可靠性评测提供了统计证书工具（judge 可证明接管的评测比例），且 CertHarvest 用同一证书为伪标签自训练回路给出污染上界和安全阀门——这正是自改进/自动化研究闭环中"何时可以信任自产标签"的核心机制，对自动评测与奖励设计也极具参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Can a genuinely finite-sample cluster-valid construction (e.g., tighter cluster-aware conformal bounds) ever be non-vacuous at G≈30–100, or is a bootstrap approximation the only workable point between the invalid naive certificate and the vacuous conservative ones?
2. The 17.5% failure of the i.i.d. certificate is shown on a single ρ=0.80 corpus — can the (ρ, G, m_g) boundary where i.i.d. certification starts failing be characterized theoretically rather than audited corpus by corpus?
3. CertHarvest's α-bound caps contamination but not selection skew: is there a companion statistic (e.g., an acceptance-rate or coverage-decay bound) that would make multi-round self-training safe instead of the current one-round recommendation?
4. Release-side coverage rarely certifies — what judge architectures or additional evidence (environment-state access, post-hoc tool replay) could unlock certified auto-acceptance of successes rather than only auto-rejection of failures?
5. Does the two-parameter predictability law (π and base AUROC) transfer beyond these seven corpora to other agent-evaluation domains such as SWE-bench-style repositories, GUI agents, or long-horizon research tasks?
