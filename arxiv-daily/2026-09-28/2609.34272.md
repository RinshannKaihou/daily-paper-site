> **一句话总结：** 作者发现 BF16 FlashAttention-3 的反向传播破坏了 softmax 梯度"每行和为零"的守恒律——BF16 舍入留下的非零行质量 ρ 会沿注意力加权平均键 μₖ 向 query 梯度泄漏一个误差项，当训练后期注意力坍缩到单键(one-hot)时该泄漏超过真实信号数个量级，导致 450M/50B token 预训练在 25B token 后梯度范数暴涨千倍、loss 比 FP32 attention 高 0.2 nats；提出的 GProj(gauge projection)用每行两次 rank-one 修正恢复零行和，把中位 query/key 梯度误差从 FA3 的 773%/85.6%（前向修复后 219%/13.3%）降到 0.34%/0.37%（与 FP32 attention 持平），每步仅多花 4.7% 时间。

> **TL;DR:** While pretraining a 450M-parameter transformer on 50B tokens, the authors found FlashAttention-3's BF16 backward silently corrupts gradients: after ~25B tokens the gradient norm grew a thousandfold and loss ended 0.2 nats above an identical FP32-attention run, with no NaN or overflow. The root cause is a broken conservation law — the exact softmax score gradient sums to zero per row, but FA3 rounds it to BF16 before contracting with keys, and the residual row mass leaks an error along the attention-weighted mean key that dominates when attention becomes one-hot late in training. Their fix, GProj (gauge projection), restores the zero row sum with two rank-one corrections per row: on 8 captured attention inputs from the failing layers, median relative dQ/dK error drops from FA3's 773%/85.6% (219%/13.3% after the known FA3-SBS forward repair) to 0.34%/0.37% — matching FP32 attention (0.38%/0.37%) — for +4.73% step time (vs +33.43% for fused FP32 attention), and GProj trains the full 50B-token schedule to the same loss as FP32 attention while FA3 destabilizes.

## 核心贡献

- **定位一起静默的训练后期失败**：450M 模型在 50B token 预训练中前 25B token 一切正常，随后梯度范数增长约千倍、loss 收在比 FP32 attention 高 0.2 nats 的位置——全程无 overflow、无 NaN。通过位级冻结前向、仅在 FP32/FP64 中重算第 5、11 两层的 attention backward，证明多余梯度几乎全部来自 kernel 的反向数值误差。
- **提出守恒律解释（理论）**：精确 softmax score 梯度满足沿每行求和为零（对键的平移不变性/gauge symmetry）。FA3 把 score 梯度舍入为 BF16 操作数 t 后再与 keys 收缩，舍入残差 ρ=Σⱼ tⱼ 产生沿均值键 μₖ 的泄漏项 αρμₖ；注意力越集中（one-hot），真实梯度越接近零而泄漏不变，误差相对大小无界。文中给出一个四键数值构造（row 质量恰为 −η，query 梯度本应为 0 的坐标算出 −24），以及"前向修复无法消除该误差"的定理（Theorem 4.2）。
- **GProj 修复**：在 BF16 操作数上用 r 加权投影 t−λr（λ=ρ/m，m 为 kernel 实际乘的 BF16 概率质量）恢复零行和，每行只需两次 rank-one 修正——query 修正放进现有 backward pass，key 修正需要一个额外 pass；不改前向、不留额外前向状态、不纠正 dV。
- **实证充分**：8 个从失败 run 捕获的真实 attention 输入 + 60 例合成测试套件 + 5 个 50B token 从头训练对照（FA3 / FA3-SBS / GProj / key smoothing / FP32 attention，同一 seed、同一数据顺序、16×H200），外加 3 个 102M plain-transformer 对照。
- **顺带发现**：已发表的动态平移修复（QY-shift, Qu & Yao ICLR 2026）在相同设定下从最初几十亿 token 起就停滞（13.2B token 处终止，8.4–8.9B 时落后约 0.31 nats），根因是其 shift 规则在 BF16 下下溢，而非其 attention backward 本身；SageBwd 式 key smoothing 只把爆发推迟约 3B token，不能阻止。

## Key Contributions

- **Identification of a silent late-training failure**: a 450M transformer pretrained on 50B tokens with BF16 FlashAttention-3 runs healthy for 25B tokens, then the gradient norm grows ~1000x and ends 0.2 nats above an FP32-attention control — with no overflow, no NaN, and a bit-identical forward. Recomputing only the attention backward of layers 5 and 11 in FP32 removes almost all of the excess gradient, proving the fault is in the kernel's backward.
- **A conservation-law explanation**: the exact attention score gradient sums to zero along every row (translation invariance over keys). FA3 rounds the score gradient to a BF16 operand before contracting with keys, leaving a nonzero row mass ρ that leaks an error term ρμ_k along the attention-weighted mean key. When late-training attention concentrates on a single key, the true gradient is near zero, so the leak outweighs the signal by orders of magnitude and the relative error is provably unbounded (Theorem 4.2) — no forward-side repair can remove it. A four-key construction (exact zero gradient coordinate becomes −24 after the cast) witnesses the leak exactly.
- **GProj (gauge projection)**: a backward-only fix that restores the zero row sum on the operands the kernel actually multiplies, using two rank-one corrections per row (one folded into the existing backward pass for dQ, one extra pass for dK). Median query/key gradient error drops from 219%/13.3% (after the known FA3-SBS forward repair) to 0.34%/0.37% — parity with FP32 attention — at 4.7% extra training-step time.
- **Matched from-scratch training evidence**: five 50B-token runs (FA3, FA3-SBS, GProj, key smoothing, FP32 attention) on 16 H200s with identical seed and data order — GProj and FP32 attention finish at the same loss (1.665) with small gradients; FA3 ends at 1.866 with gradient norms exceeding 10 from 29.9B tokens onward.
- **A negative result on an existing remedy**: the released dynamic-shift repair (QY-shift) stalls from the first few billion tokens due to an underflow in its shift rule under BF16, and SageBwd-style key smoothing only delays FA3's blow-up by ~3B tokens without preventing it.

## Methodology

**Failure localization.** The case study is a 450M-parameter Full-AttentionResidual transformer (24 layers, hidden 1024, 16 query heads / 4 KV heads, head dim 64, seq len 4096, Muon optimizer) trained on 50B tokens with native BF16 FlashAttention-3, QK-normalization and softcapping disabled. At 33.6B tokens the authors froze every forward activation bitwise and recomputed only the attention backward of the two affected layers (5 and 11) in FP32; the full-model gradient norm fell by more than two orders of magnitude, isolating the error to the attention backward. A four-step ablation ladder (native → correct saved output → exact VJP → FP32 backward) then separated two error channels: (channel one) the saved-output/FMA error in the forward softmax — the known FA3-SBS hazard, fixed by subtracting the unscaled row maximum before scaling; and (channel two) the BF16 cast of the score gradient itself, which no forward repair reaches.

**Theory.** For one query row, they define the exact VJP (G_Q = dQ = α Σⱼ gⱼ kⱼ with gⱼ = pⱼ(aⱼ−μₐ)) and prove Proposition 3.1: common translations of keys or values preserve the VJP, i.e. Σⱼ gⱼ = 0. The BF16 cast t = g+ε breaks this; Equation 4 decomposes the contraction into a harmless rounding term plus the leak αρμ_k. A two-key analysis gives a leak bound of 2ω₀ relative to the true gradient, with ω₀ = 2⁻⁸ the BF16 unit roundoff — negligible when keys are spread, dominant when the row is one-hot. Theorem 4.2 shows any backward that contracts the cast operand directly (FA3-SBS, FP32 saved outputs, dynamic shifting) has unbounded worst-case relative error under key translation, while the r-weighted projection error is bounded by α·ω₀·σ_k·σ_a independent of μ_k.

**GProj kernel.** After FA3-SBS's subtract-before-scale forward, the backward forms the BF16 operand t (round of reconstructed probabilities times value product), measures its row mass ρ and the actual mass m = Σⱼ rⱼ of the BF16 probabilities it multiplies, and projects t ↦ t − λr with λ = ρ/m (dividing by m, not 1, is required for exact cancellation). dQ ← BF16(α(tK − λr⊗…)) fits in FA3's existing pass; dK needs a second pass because λ is known only after the whole row is seen. All accumulators and row statistics stay FP32; no extra forward state; dV is left to the native kernel. Hopper SASS target, head dim 64, GQA, dense and packed-causal layouts, query tile 96 (vs 128 in FA3).

**Evaluation protocol.** (i) Gradient accuracy on eight attention inputs captured from the failing run (layers 5 & 11 at 23.1B and 33.6B tokens, 4096 queries × 4096 keys, full-tensor relative L2 error against an FP64 reference computed on the same BF16 inputs); (ii) a 60-case synthetic suite (tile boundaries, singleton rows, common-key shifts, long sequences) plus analytic calibrations; (iii) five matched 50B-token from-scratch runs differing only in attention computation (same seed 42, same data order, 11,930 updates, 16×H200); (iv) complete training-step timing on one H200 at 4096 tokens; (v) a 102M plain-transformer control trained from scratch to test whether the discrepancy appears without the residual architecture.

## Results & Highlights

- **Gradient accuracy vs FP64 reference (Table 1; median full-tensor relative L2 error over the 8 captured inputs from the 450M model's layers 5/11):** FA3 has dQ/dK/dV error of 773%/85.6%/0.369%; the known forward repair FA3-SBS reduces this to 219%/13.3%/0.334%; GProj reaches 0.342%/0.371%/0.334% — on par with FP32 attention (0.384%/0.371%/0.165%). The QY-shift dynamic-shifting baseline is *less* accurate than unmodified FA3 (1307%/264%/80.5%). The key pass alone lowers the median dK error from 13.2651% to 0.3712% while leaving O, dQ, dV byte-identical (Appendix E).
- **From-scratch training (Figure 3, Table 8; 450M model, 50B tokens, 16×H200, seed 42):** FA3's gradient norm grows a thousandfold after ~25B tokens (norm > 10 from 29.9B onward) and finishes at mean loss 1.866 over the final 0.84B tokens — 0.2 nats above FP32 attention's 1.665. GProj ends at 1.665, matching FP32 attention exactly, with the gradient norm never exceeding 10; key smoothing finishes at 1.903 with norm > 10 at 33.3B (it only delays FA3's onset by ~3B tokens); FA3-SBS finishes stably (1.649) but still drives layers 4/5/11 into logit scales of ~39,000–49,000, a regime GProj and FP32 avoid entirely (their max logit scale is 26–27, vs 51,057 for FA3 at 33.6B — Table 3).
- **Cost (Table 4; complete training step at 4096 tokens, single H200):** FA3 = 227.257 ms/update; GProj = 238.120 ms (+4.73%); fused FP32 attention = 308.276 ms (+33.43%); eager FP32 attention = 719.420 ms (+216.45%). Peak memory is identical (9.191 GiB) and GProj saves no extra forward state.
- **Synthetic suite (Table 2):** across 58 nonzero-target test cases, GProj's median/max relative errors are 0.2238%/2.49% (dQ) vs FA3-SBS's 0.2601%/4794% — the maximum drops by ~200x; under random common-key translations the zero-target-coordinate error is four orders of magnitude below FA3-SBS, and GProj passes all 60 cases without ever increasing dK/dV error.
- **Failure localization precision:** replacing only the saved output O with an FP64-recomputed value reduces the absolute dQ error by a median factor of 72.02x at checkpoint 8000/layer 11, with a predicted-vs-observed error cosine of 0.9999 — the one-line saved-output model explains essentially the entire local error (Appendix I).

Notably, the authors port the competing QY-shift method byte-for-byte from the authors' released BF16 source and verify bitwise equality before benchmarking it — unusually careful treatment of a competing baseline. ## Critical Assessment

This is careful, honest systems-numerics work, but several scope choices deserve scrutiny. **(1) The failure regime is partly self-selected:** the runs disable QK-normalization and softcapping ("normalization controls: query-key normalization and softcapping disabled", Table 9), and the blow-up emerges only in two layers whose queries put nearly all attention on a single key with logit scales reaching ~51,000 — a regime that QK-norm is specifically designed to suppress in many modern architectures. The claim that the error "is easily blamed on hyperparameters or data" is fair, but the paper does not run (and cannot run at this scale) a QK-norm-enabled arm to show the failure survives it; the 102M plain-transformer control (Appendix K) shows the *gradient error* exists broadly (0.26–0.27% at LR1, becoming instability at LR4) but that control also lacks QK-norm. **(2) Narrow hardware/shape coverage, admitted but limiting:** everything is BF16 FA3 on Hopper with head dim 64, dense causal; no FP8, no MLA/sliding-window, no AMD, and the value gradient dV is explicitly left unrepaired. **(3) The 4.7% overhead is a best case:** it is measured at 4096 tokens where attention is a modest share of a 450M step; the paper itself notes overhead grows with attention's share of the step, so the cost at 100B+-scale models with long context would be larger — though still far below the +33.43% of fused FP32 attention. **(4) Baselines are handled well but the comparison set is small:** the strong result against QY-shift (which stalls due to a shift-rule underflow, a defect of *that implementation's* BF16 arithmetic rather than of dynamic shifting in principle) is well-evidenced via the byte-identical port and checkpoint diagnosis, yet the paper acknowledges the repaired FA3-SBS output still finishes with slightly better loss (1.649) than GProj (1.665) — a nuance easy to miss: GProj's headline win is stability and gradient fidelity, not final-loss superiority over FA3-SBS. **(5) Theory-reality gap is small but present:** Theorem 4.2 assumes a perfect forward and exact arithmetic in the contractions; the implemented kernel carries extra accumulation/ratio/cast terms (Equation 15), so the 0.34% residual is consistent with, but not derived from, the bound. Reproducibility is above average — the eight captures alone reproduce the accuracy table, and the synthetic suite runs in 65 seconds on one H200 — but no explicit public code/kernel release URL is stated in the main text I reviewed, which would be the deciding factor for practitioners.

## Strengths

- A genuine mystery told cleanly: the failure has no smoking gun (no NaN, no overflow, normal-looking loss for 25B tokens), and the paper's localization ladder (bitwise-frozen forward → layer-wise FP32 recompute → saved-output channel → cast channel) is a model of how to diagnose silent numerical faults.
- The conservation-law framing is a real conceptual contribution: it converts "kernel has rounding error" into a statement about *which* error matters (row mass) and *when* (one-hot attention), and yields a theorem explaining why all forward-side repairs provably fail.
- GProj is almost free (+4.73% step time, no extra forward state, no memory growth) and achieves FP32-parity gradient accuracy; the two-pass design with FP32 accumulators shows real kernel-engineering craft (SASS-level detail in Appendix B).
- Baseline treatment is unusually fair: FA3-SBS, key smoothing, QY-shift, eager and fused FP32 attention are all defined in one arithmetic-contract table, and the QY-shift port is verified bitwise against the authors' own code before being criticized.
- Multiple independent evidence types agree: captured-input accuracy, a 60-case synthetic suite, five matched 50B-token training runs, regime audits, and a plain-transformer control — the conclusion does not rest on one benchmark.

## Limitations

- Scale and architecture: all headline results use a single 450M model (102M for the control); no evidence at multi-billion parameter scale, where attention/step share and logit regimes differ. The 4.7% overhead is quoted at 4096 tokens and grows with attention's share.
- Hardware/precision coverage: BF16 FlashAttention-3 on Hopper GPUs, head dimension 64, dense causal only; dV is left to the native kernel; no FP8, no other backends beyond a fixed-operand sweep (cuDNN and PyTorch SDPA are shown to share the backward defect, but GProj is only built for FA3).
- The QK-normalization/softcapping-disabled configuration leaves open whether production models that use these controls ever enter the one-hot high-logit regime where the leak dominates; the paper argues logit growth is partly *caused* by the kernel error (which GProj repairs), but a QK-norm arm would settle it directly.
- GProj's key correction requires a second backward pass and per-row statistics known only after the full row is seen; adaptation to other kernel schedules (e.g., FlashAttention-2-style tiling or non-Hopper kernels) is nontrivial.
- Key smoothing and FP32-attention comparisons are at 450M/50B only; the training-side conclusions (0.2-nat gap, delayed blow-up) are single-seed in architecture terms despite the shared seed-42 protocol (the plain-transformer control uses 3 seeds, but only at 102M/8000 updates).

## Relevance

这篇文章正命中你关心的两个方向：LLM 训练稳定性与可观测性（无声的后期梯度爆炸、loss 恶化、无 NaN 的静默失败及其定位方法论），以及极端性能优化下的可靠性（BF16 融合 kernel 的浮点精度错误与灾难性消除）。它给出的"冻结前向 + 分层重算反向"诊断流程和守恒律审计思路，对监控大规模预训练中的 kernel 级静默数据损坏很有参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the one-hot high-logit regime (logit scale > 10⁴) still arise in models trained with QK-normalization or softcapping enabled, or does GProj's benefit reduce to the bounded 0.1–0.3% error floor there?
- How does the 4.7% overhead translate to production-scale models (tens of billions of parameters, 32k+ context) where attention dominates the training step — and does the second key pass remain necessary under FlashAttention-4/FP8 schedules?
- The paper shows cuDNN and PyTorch SDPA backends share the backward defect at fixed operands: does a GProj-style projection generalize to those backends' tiling schemes, and would a generic "conservation-law audit" (checking Σⱼ tⱼ = 0 on operands the kernel multiplies) catch analogous broken invariants in other fused kernels (e.g., RoPE, cross-entropy)?
- Could the row-mass leak interact with quantized-attention training (SageBwd, MXFP4) differently than with BF16, given those methods normalize by quantized masses by design?
- Since FA3-SBS alone finished with slightly lower loss (1.649 vs 1.665) than GProj despite worse gradient fidelity, is there a measurable downstream capability difference between the arms, or is final loss alone the right yardstick?
