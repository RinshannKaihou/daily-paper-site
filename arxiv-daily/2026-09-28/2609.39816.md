> **一句话总结：** 论文证明快矩阵乘法（两层 Strassen，49 次块乘法 vs 经典 64 次）在低精度 FP8 舍入下会静默破坏 LLM 的前缀不变性（prefix invariance）：在 Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct 上，两个"修复后"表面精度正常（35.42%/32.92% vs bf16 基线 34.58%）的 fast FP8 实现，在把允许前缀之后的文本替换为 bf16 贪心续写时，仍改变 240 题 OpenBookQA 中 5.83% 和 10.00% 的 likelihood 选择答案，而行局部的 bf16 模型与部署版 FP8 kernel 改变为 0；现有稳定性判据对 512 个精确等价的符号变体给出同一个数值，实际 teacher-forced WikiText-2 困惑度却相差 772.4 倍。作者证明逐积前缀安全必然退回经典乘法数（Theorem 1），进而在逐行独立量化的边界整数编码上构造出与经典 int8 逐位一致（bitwise）的认证两层 Strassen 实现（0.7656× 经典乘法量），从构造上免费继承前缀不变性——在 4 个模型上替换测试 0/240 改变，直接扰动测试 0/496 vs FP8 调度的 48/496。

> **TL;DR:** Fast matrix multiplication (two-level Strassen: 49 vs 64 block multiplications) silently breaks prefix invariance under FP8 rounding. On Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, two fast FP8 realizations with normal-looking OpenBookQA accuracy (35.42% and 32.92% vs 34.58% for the bf16 baseline) still flip 5.83% and 10.00% of likelihood-scored answers (out of 240 items) when text after the allowed prefix is replaced — while the two row-local controls (the bf16 model and the deployed FP8 kernel) flip 0%. Numerical-stability criteria give all 512 exact-equivalent sign variants one identical value, yet their teacher-forced WikiText-2 perplexity spans 772.4×. The authors prove prefix-safe products force the classical multiplication count (Theorem 1), then build certified two-level Strassen realizations on bounded integer codes that match the classical int8 operator bit-for-bit (0.7656× multiplications; 0/496 row-perturbation violations vs 48/496 for FP8) and change none of 240 answers on four models — making realization choice a pure cost decision, though their first Triton kernels run slower than classical int8.

## 核心贡献

- **问题识别：精度 ≠ 不变性。** 首次系统证明 fast 矩阵乘法在低精度下的块和舍入会让后面的 token 通过线性层"泄漏"进更早 token 的输出，破坏 likelihood 评分和打包 batch 服务所依赖的前缀不变性——把精度修回正常并不能恢复不变性（repaired #1 的 14 个改变答案中 9 个前后皆错，accuracy 对此视而不见）。
- **证明两类现有检验全部失效。** Accuracy 检不出违例；数值分析的稳定性判据（非零系数个数、最大系数幅值、Ballard 等误差界的 Q/E 参数、Xie 等的 Φ）都只通过绝对值或非零模式读系数，符号翻转不改它们，512 个符号变体只得一个值——而 FP8 调度下实际困惑度比值从 1.367 到 1056.0（772.4×），仅差一个符号的两个变体相差 761.0×，Strassen 原始符号也只排第 3.11。
- **不可能性结果（Theorem 1）。** (1) 型算法（块积的线性组合）若要逐积前缀安全，需要 R ≥ mkn 次块乘法，即经典计数——省乘法必然混行，混行的贡献必须在输出行形成的那一刻精确抵消。
- **认证实现构造（Proposition 1）。** 在逐行独立量化的边界整数编码上运行两层 Strassen：整数和天然不舍入，scale 事后精确照搬经典 int8；三个只依赖系数、tiling 与量化规范的确定性条件保证与经典 int8 算子逐位一致，行局部性 ⇒ 免费继承前缀不变性。码界 31 下乘法量为 0.7656×；溢出校正（Â = A₀ + 256R_A 分解）可扩到全码界 127（校正计算 0.79–0.82× 经典乘法量，逐溢出行列全乘则 1.43–1.71×）。
- **范式转变：认证把"选哪个实现"变成纯成本决策。** 质量由量化规范单独决定，任何通过认证的实现都算出相同的输出位，再也不会因为选了某个 fast 实现而改变一个 scored likelihood。

## Key Contributions

- Demonstrates that **accuracy does not certify prefix invariance**: two fast FP8 realizations of two-level Strassen, repaired to ordinary-looking accuracy on Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct (35.42%/32.92% vs 34.58% bf16 on OpenBookQA), still change 5.83%/10.00% of likelihood-chosen answers under prefix-replacement testing, while row-local controls change none.
- Shows **stability criteria are structurally blind**: every criterion analyzed (nonzero-coefficient count, largest coefficient magnitude, Ballard et al.'s rounding-error bound parameters Q/E, Xie et al.'s Φ) is invariant under sign changes, assigning all 512 sign variants one value — while realized teacher-forced perplexity on WikiText-2 spans 772.4× under the FP8 schedule (two variants differing in a single sign: 761.0× apart; Strassen's original signs rank 3.11).
- Proves **Theorem 1**: an algorithm of form (1) that is prefix-safe product-by-product uses R ≥ mkn block multiplications — the classical count. Keeping the multiplication saving forces products that mix rows, whose contributions must cancel exactly by the time each output row is formed.
- Constructs **certified realizations**: two-level Strassen on bounded integer codes that quantize token rows independently, mix and cancel the codes exactly, and rescale as classical int8 does. Proposition 1's three coefficient-only conditions guarantee bitwise equality to the classical int8 operator, so every certified realization inherits prefix invariance by construction — at 49 of 64 block multiplications (0.7656×) for code bound 31, extendable to full code bound 127 via an overflow correction.
- Validates empirically across four models (Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, OLMo-2-7B-Instruct, Qwen3-8B): the certified realization changes none of 240 OpenBookQA answers under the replacement test, moves 0 of 496 earlier/later-row pairs on direct Gaussian-input tests (vs 48/496 for the FP8 schedule), and matches classical int8 bit-for-bit — turning realization choice into a pure cost decision.

## Methodology

**1. Formalization.** A "realization" = a fast algorithm's coefficients together with its quantization specification c (code bounds, group scaling, rounding, summation order, output format). Fast algorithms of form (1) compute R block products M_r = (Σᵢ u_{ri} Aᵢ)(Σⱼ v_{rj} Bⱼ) and combine them. Two-level Strassen (m=k=n=4) uses R=49 instead of 64. Diagonal sign matrices D, E, F flip block rows/columns without changing the exact result (AB = D·DAE · EBF·F), yielding 512 "sign variants" — exactly equivalent in real arithmetic, but free to diverge once quantized. A linear layer is prefix-safe when its matrix multiplication is row-local (output row t reads only row t of A), like the classical int8 reference operator; FP8 block sums over token rows are not row-local.

**2. Prefix-invariance audit** (following Kim et al.'s design): each candidate-answer token is scored in one forward pass; a second input keeps the allowed prefix and replaces the token itself and all later text (with the bf16 model's greedy continuation, or with unrelated filler). Answer flips or per-position score changes indicate violations. Two fast FP8 realizations are built on DeepGEMM's deployed FP8 schedule with repaired rounding (one-level and two-level Strassen variants, rescaling token rows by powers of two and rounding weight block sums jointly, adapting Ning et al.'s rules); controls are the bf16 model and the deployed FP8 kernel (both row-local). Three regimes are tested: prefix replacement (OpenBookQA/ARC-Easy, first 240 items), window scoring (five models from four families, 1,024-token windows), and packed two-request batches with shared forward pass (768 pairs).

**3. Stability-criteria analysis.** The criteria (nonzero count, largest coefficient, Ballard et al.'s Q and E, Xie et al.'s Φ) are shown to read coefficients only via absolute values or nonzero patterns, so all 512 variants score identically. Realized error is measured as teacher-forced perplexity on WikiText-2 (ratio to bf16) for every variant under the FP8 schedule, plus a replacement-robustness check with bootstrap CIs.

**4. Theory.** Theorem 1: prefix-safe form-(1) algorithms need R ≥ mkn block multiplications, so any saving-preserving fast algorithm must mix rows and cancel exactly.

**5. Certified construction.** Quantize each row of A and each column-pair group of B to bounded integer codes independently; run the fast algorithm on the codes where integer sums do not round (int32 accumulation: condition (ii) L_W·L_A·L_B·b_A·b_B ≤ 2³¹, exact for inner length ≤ 8729 with L_W=16; groups of g=128); apply scales exactly as classical int8 afterwards. Proposition 1's conditions (i)–(iii) involve only coefficients, tiling, and c, so they are checked once, before any input: two-level Strassen admits code bound 31. An overflow correction splits overflowing codes Â = A₀ + 256R_A and adds correction terms (Eq. 2) to lift the bound to the full 127.

**6. Verification.** Bitwise comparison against classical int8 on 200 random tiles; distinct-output census across all 512 variants (integer: 1 distinct product; FP8-rounded: 0 exact with a 3.17-fold largest-error spread); direct row-perturbation test on Gaussian inputs (0/496 vs 48/496); in-model runs in every linear layer except the output of four models, emulating int8 arithmetic exactly in fp32 (intermediate sums < 2³¹), with the replacement audit and excess NLL over bf16 on eight 2048-token WikiText-2 chunks (raw and temperature-adjusted) against the deployed FP8 kernel; Triton kernel timings at 4096×4096 on an NVIDIA H20 GPU.

## Results & Highlights

- **Accuracy hides the violation.** On OpenBookQA (Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, first 240 items): repaired #1 and #2 fast FP8 realizations score 35.42% and 32.92% accuracy vs 34.58% for the bf16 model, yet change the likelihood-chosen answer on 5.83% (95% CI 2.92–8.75) and 10.00% (6.25–13.75) of items when the suffix is replaced by the bf16 greedy continuation; the row-local controls (bf16 model, deployed FP8 kernel) change 0.00%; the unrepaired realization scores only 18.75% accuracy and flips 54.58%. Target-token log-probabilities change at 80.14% and 89.00% of scored positions (repaired #1/#2). Changes persist under filler replacement (7.08%/7.92%) and on ARC-Easy (6.67%/12.08% vs 0.00% for controls).
- **Stability criteria cannot separate realizations.** All 512 sign variants of two-level Strassen receive identical values on every analyzed criterion, but their teacher-forced WikiText-2 perplexity ratios to bf16 span 1.367–1056.0 (a 772.4× spread) under the FP8 schedule; two variants differing in a single sign differ by 761.0×; Strassen's original signs rank only 3.11. The spread survives prefix replacement at 0.985× its teacher-forced value (95% CI [0.863, 1.141]).
- **The certified realization is exact and prefix-invariant.** At code bound 31, two-level Strassen uses 49 of 64 block multiplications (0.7656× the classical count), matches classical int8 bit-for-bit on 200 random tiles, all 512 sign variants yield exactly 1 distinct integer product (vs 0 exact and a 3.17-fold error spread under FP8 rounding), and moves 0 of 496 earlier/later-row pairs on Gaussian inputs (FP8 schedule: 48/496). Run in four models, it changes none of the 240 OpenBookQA answers, like the row-local controls.
- **Violations extend beyond prefix replacement.** Window scoring on five models from four families: replacing the last three quarters of a 1,024-token window changes 18.2–40.9% of next-token predictions in the scored quarter under the unrepaired realization and 7.8–15.2% under the repaired ones, none under controls. Packed two-request batches (768 pairs on three models): the first request's next-token distribution changes in every pair under the unrepaired realization and repaired #2 (greedy token: 11.9–13.0% of pairs), never under controls; on Qwen2.5-3B the first request's K/V projections already differ at the first layer.
- **Quality and cost of certification.** Excess NLL over bf16 on eight 2048-token WikiText-2 chunks at code bound 31 exceeds the deployed FP8 kernel's on three of four models (raw; all four after temperature adjustment); with the overflow correction at code bound 127 it is at or below the kernel's on all four models, raw and adjusted. The correction computes 0.79–0.82× classical int8's multiplications on sampled layer slices (1.43–1.71× if every overflowing row/column pair is multiplied). First Triton kernels are slower than classical int8 on H20: one Strassen level takes 2.04–3.17× the classical time across three group sizes, two levels 27.9×.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: high and genuine.** The central observation — that accuracy repair and stability criteria are both orthogonal to prefix invariance — is crisp, falsifiable, and demonstrated rather than asserted. Theorem 1 is a clean impossibility result, and the int8-exact fast realization is an elegant constructive counterpart. Framing realization choice as "a pure cost decision once certified" is a genuinely useful conceptual contribution to inference infrastructure.
- **Rigor: strong.** Two row-local controls cleanly isolate the mechanism (block-sum rounding over token rows); violations are shown in three independent regimes (replacement, window, packed batch) across five models from four families; the certified path is verified bitwise rather than statistically — the strongest possible evidence class for this claim.
- **Red flag 1 — the fix does not cover the deployed format.** All violations are demonstrated in FP8 (DeepGEMM schedules), yet certification exists only for the int8 path. FP8 — the format LLM inference actually deploys — has no certified realization here, so the paper diagnoses a live FP8 problem but treats an int8 one.
- **Red flag 2 — no wall-clock win, and in-model results are emulation.** The certified realization is 2.04–3.17× (one level) to 27.9× (two levels) slower than classical int8 in their own Triton kernels, and the in-model experiments (Section 4.2) emulate int8 arithmetic in fp32 rather than running integer tensor-core hardware — there is no end-to-end hardware evidence that a certified fast realization can serve a model. The multiplication saving is currently theoretical.
- **Red flag 3 — quality cost at the certified bound.** At code bound 31 the excess NLL is worse than the deployed FP8 kernel on three of four models (raw), so certification at the cheap bound is not a drop-in quality win either; only the overflow-corrected code-127 variant matches the kernel, and its cost (0.79–0.82×, worst case 1.43–1.71×) erodes the 0.7656× saving.
- **Evaluation breadth.** The likelihood audits use only the first 240 OpenBookQA items and ARC-Easy, plus WikiText-2 perplexity — no generative tasks, no MMLU-scale suites, no long-context benchmarks; the headline 5.83%/10.00% figures rest on a synthetic replacement protocol, though the filler and packed-request variants corroborate the underlying mechanism.
- **Scope of theory.** Theorem 1 constrains only algorithms of form (1) (fixed linear combinations of block products); it does not rule out prefix-invariant fast schemes outside this class (e.g., runtime-reordering or learned algorithms), so "prefix safety forces the classical count" should not be over-read as a universal claim.

## Strengths

- A clear, practically motivated property (prefix invariance under likelihood scoring and packed-batch serving) with a matching impossibility theorem and a constructive fix — the full problem→theory→construction→verification arc.
- Meticulous experimental design: row-local controls, 95% bootstrap intervals, distinct-output censuses across all 512 sign variants, and direct input-level perturbation tests.
- Bitwise verification of the certified realization (1 distinct output across 512 variants, 0/496 perturbations) — the strongest evidence class available for an invariance claim.
- Honest cost accounting: the paper reports its own kernels' slowdowns and quantifies the overflow-correction trade-off instead of hiding them.
- Realization-agnostic certification: conditions depend only on coefficients, tiling, and the quantization spec, checked once before any input, and any certified variant (all 512) is provably interchangeable.

## Limitations

- Certified realizations exist only for int8 with bounded codes (31, or 127 with overflow correction); no FP8 certification, and FP8 is the low-precision format actually deployed in LLM inference.
- No speed benefit today: reference Triton kernels are 2.04–3.17× (one Strassen level) and 27.9× (two levels) slower than classical int8 on an NVIDIA H20; in-model experiments emulate int8 in fp32 rather than executing on integer hardware.
- Code bound 31 measurably degrades quality relative to the deployed FP8 kernel (excess NLL on WikiText-2) on three of four models without the correction; the corrected full-range variant's multiplication saving (0.79–0.82×) depends on overflow density and can reach 1.43–1.71×.
- Audits are limited to multiple-choice likelihood scoring (240 OpenBookQA items, ARC-Easy) and WikiText-2 perplexity; no generative or long-context evaluation.
- Certification covers only fixed form-(1) bilinear algorithms; other fast-MM families (e.g., learned/AlphaTensor-style algorithms) are discussed but not certified.

## Relevance

与用户的核心方向高度相关：这是"激进性能优化引入正确性问题"的范例研究——fast 算子在精度指标完全正常的情况下静默污染模型输出分布（一种算法层面的 silent data corruption），直接命中 Inference Reliability & SDC Detection 与 Reliability under Extreme Performance Optimization；其"认证 bitwise 等价 + 可证明不变性"的思路对量化部署的可靠性验证也很有参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can a certified prefix-invariant fast realization be constructed directly in FP8 — e.g., exploiting FP8's exact integer structure or hybrid int8-product/FP8-scale schemes — given FP8 is what deployed kernels actually use?
- How much of the 49/64 multiplication saving can survive engineering: would fused tensor-core int8 pipelines, CPU/ASIC integer paths, or better call shapes close the 2.04–27.9× wall-clock gap reported for the first Triton kernels?
- Do these likelihood flips distort full-benchmark rankings in practice — i.e., when W8A8/FP8-served models are used to score MMLU-scale suites, how large is the realized ranking distortion beyond 240 OpenBookQA items?
- How does the overflow-correction cost behave on real activation distributions across layers and models — is the full code-bound-127 variant end-to-end multiplication-saving at all?
- Beyond likelihood scoring, where else does prefix invariance silently matter — speculative decoding, KV-cache reuse, or batched RL rollouts — and can cross-request contamination there compound over long horizons?
