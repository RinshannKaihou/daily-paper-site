> **一句话总结：** 提出 EEM（Experimental Experience Modeling）框架，把自主科研 agent 的历史实验轨迹蒸馏为"源记录 + 经验教训"（适用条件、实证发现、决策启示）的可复用经验条目；新决策时先检索并评估历史经验是否充分，不足时才触发定向低成本 pilot 实验（上限 120 秒），实验产物再回流经验库——在 ARC-Bench 的 25 个科研主题上总体得分 0.674，超过最强基线 AutoResearchClaw 的 0.596（相对提升 13.1%），同时每主题总 token 消耗下降 11.4%（843,384 → 746,857）。

> **TL;DR:** EEM is an experience-driven decision framework for autonomous research agents. It distills past experimental trajectories into reusable "record + lesson" items (applicability conditions, evidence-bounded empirical finding, decision implication), retrieves them to judge whether a candidate experimental direction warrants further investment, and runs a targeted low-cost pilot experiment only when stored experience is insufficient; pilot and full-scale results are distilled back into the library. On ARC-Bench (25 ML research topics, LLM-judged, overall = 0.25·CodeDev + 0.25·CodeExec + 0.50·ResultAnalysis) with a shared GPT-5.3-Codex backbone, EEM reaches 0.674 overall vs 0.596 for the strongest baseline AutoResearchClaw (+13.1%) and 0.511/0.419 for AIDE-ML / AI Scientist v2, while cutting per-topic total tokens by 11.4% (843,384 → 746,857). Ablations attribute the largest share to the accumulated experience library: removing it drops overall to 0.506 (−24.9%), vs 0.565 without on-demand pilots and 0.649 without distillation.

## 核心贡献

- **提出 EEM 框架**：首个把"实验经验"作为一等公民来支持自主科研实验决策的框架，包含历史经验获取、经验引导的实验决策、经验迭代积累三个组件。
- **经验的形式化定义**：每条经验 e_i = (r_i, ℓ_i) 由源记录 r_i（保留原始证据的上下文/方向/动作/结果）和蒸馏出的教训 ℓ_i 组成；教训包含三要素——适用条件、有证据边界的实证发现、对未来资源投入的决策启示，并附机制有效/无效、错误诊断、实验设置、实验对比等语义标签。
- **两阶段经验提取**：先做证据锚定（evidence grounding，把观察与 agent 当时的预期分离，经验只陈述记录支持的内容），再做后见之明抽象（hindsight abstraction，回答"适用条件是什么 / 支持什么关系 / 对后续投入意味着什么"），保证经验来自真实观察而非 agent 的先验想象。
- **充分性评估 + 按需 pilot**：对每个候选方向先检索相关经验并判断充分性 h_t(d)；充分则直接决定推进/修改/放弃，不充分时识别缺失的决策关键信息 g_t(d)，只针对该缺口设计有界的低成本 pilot 实验（实验中上限 120 秒），再合并新旧经验做最终决策。
- **实验验证**：在 ARC-Bench 上总体得分与 token 效率同时优于三个全自动科研基线，消融实验证明三个组件各有贡献，且框架在通用模型（GPT-5.5）骨干上仍可运行。

## Key Contributions

- **Experimental Experience Modeling (EEM)**: a framework that treats experimental experience as a first-class asset for autonomous research, with three components — historical experience acquisition, experience-guided experimental decision making, and iterative experience accumulation.
- **A principled experience representation**: each experience item pairs a source record (preserving the original evidence) with a distilled lesson containing applicability conditions, an evidence-bounded empirical finding, and a decision implication, organized by semantic tags (mechanism effectiveness/ineffectiveness, error and failure diagnoses, experimental settings, comparisons).
- **Two-stage experience extraction**: evidence grounding separates what the record supports from the agent's original expectation, and hindsight abstraction converts grounded observations into reusable decision knowledge — lessons are guaranteed to derive from observations, not the agent's priors.
- **Sufficiency assessment with on-demand pilots**: for each candidate direction EEM retrieves applicable experience, assesses sufficiency h_t(d); if insufficient it identifies the decision-relevant gap g_t(d) and runs a bounded, targeted low-cost pilot (capped at 120 s in experiments) probing only the unresolved uncertainty, then decides with historical + newly acquired experience.
- **Empirical validation**: simultaneous improvements in research quality and token efficiency on ARC-Bench against three fully autonomous baselines, component-wise ablations, backbone-swap applicability (GPT-5.3-Codex vs general-purpose GPT-5.5), and run-stability analysis.

## Methodology

**Setting.** End-to-end autonomous research: scientific question in, research paper out, via initial planning (literature review → hypotheses → candidate directions), experimentation (implement, evaluate, iterate), and writing. EEM intervenes in the experimentation stage, where an agent must decide *which* experiments are worth running and whether a direction deserves further investment.

**Experience definition and representation.** Trajectories contain many uninformative events, so EEM keeps only records connecting a research context + attempted direction to an observed consequence. Each item is e_i = (r_i, ℓ_i): the source record r_i preserves context, direction, action, and outcome (so evidence is grounded in observations, not the agent's priors); the distilled lesson ℓ_i states (i) *applicability conditions* under which the finding holds, (ii) the *empirical finding* bounded by the record's evidence, and (iii) the *decision implication* for future resource investment. Items carry non-exclusive semantic tags: mechanism effectiveness, mechanism ineffectiveness and adverse effects, errors and failure diagnoses, experimental settings, and experimental comparisons.

**Experience extraction.** From a trajectory segment τ, informative records are first extracted ({r_i}), then each is distilled: ΔE(τ) = {Distill_θ(r_i)}. Distillation runs two stages: (i) evidence grounding interprets the observed consequence relative to the attempted direction, separating empirical support from the agent's expectation; (ii) hindsight abstraction answers three questions — under what conditions the observation applies, what empirical relationship the record supports, and how it should affect future investment — turning "this configuration improved a metric in this run" into reusable guidance. Both pilot and full-scale trajectories go through the same extraction.

**Experience-guided decision making.** For candidate direction d in research state s_t: (1) *Retrieve* applicable experience R_e(d) from library E_t (K relevant items with model knowledge K). (2) *Assess* sufficiency: (h_t(d), g_t(d)) = Assess_θ(s_t, d, K, R_e(d)), where h_t(d) ∈ {0,1} flags whether historical experience suffices and g_t(d) names the missing decision-relevant experience (e.g., mismatched conditions, inconsistent findings, unresolved uncertainty). (3) If h_t(d) = 1, *decide directly*: pursue to full-scale evaluation, revise, or reject — positive experience justifies investment, negative experience prevents repeating ineffective experiments. (4) If h_t(d) = 0, *acquire on demand*: design a bounded pilot that probes only the gap g_t(d) (reduced scale, duration, coverage; 120-second cap in the experiments), extract experience from the pilot trajectory, and decide with D_t(d) = R_e(d) ∪ ΔE_t^p(d). Crucially, the pilot is *not* meant to approximate the full experiment — it only supplies the missing decision-critical evidence.

**Iterative accumulation.** After each round the library updates as E_{t+1} = U(E_t, ΔE_t^p ⊔ ΔE_t^f), where U incorporates new record-lesson pairs and deduplicates entries from the same underlying decision record; unfunded candidates may still contribute pilot experience. The library is initialized empty per research task (per-task history); warm-starting across tasks is left as future work due to negative-transfer risk. The loop terminates when evidence supports the intended claims or the iteration limit is hit.

## Results & Highlights

- **Overall performance on ARC-Bench (25 ML research topics, LLM-judged, overall = 0.25·CD + 0.25·CE + 0.50·RA, shared GPT-5.3-Codex backbone):** EEM scores **0.674 overall** vs **0.596 for AutoResearchClaw (Full-Auto)** — a **+13.1% relative improvement** over the strongest evaluated baseline — and far above AIDE-ML (0.511) and AI Scientist v2 (0.419).
- **Result Analysis (RA, the heaviest-weighted dimension at 50% of overall):** EEM reaches **0.582 vs 0.442 for AutoResearchClaw (+31.7% relative)** and 0.336/0.261 for AIDE-ML / AI Scientist v2; Code Execution also improves to **0.619 vs 0.562**. Notably, EEM's Code Development score (0.911) is actually *below* AutoResearchClaw (0.938) and AIDE-ML (0.958) — the win comes from evidence-grounded analysis, not code generation.
- **Token efficiency (Table 2, per-topic averages):** EEM cuts total consumption from **843,384 to 746,857 tokens (−11.4%)** vs AutoResearchClaw (input −8.7%, output −16.5%), with the largest saving in the experimentation stage: **474,330 → 387,224 (−18.4%)** — achieved despite the extra cost of distillation and pilot acquisition.
- **Ablations (Table 3):** removing the accumulated historical experience library causes the largest collapse, **0.674 → 0.506 (−24.9% relative)**; removing on-demand pilot acquisition drops overall to **0.565**; keeping raw source records without distillation yields **0.649** — each component contributes, and distillation adds value beyond raw history.
- **Backbone and stability (single task ML10):** swapping to general-purpose GPT-5.5 completes the full pipeline but scores lower than GPT-5.3-Codex, with the largest drop in code development; across four independent runs overall scores stay within **~0.68–0.77**, with result analysis stable and code dimensions more variable.

## Authors & Credibility

The authors are from the State Key Laboratory of AI Safety and the Institute of Computing Technology, Chinese Academy of Sciences (Wenda Wei, Yingchen Zhang, Ruiqing Zhang, Jiafeng Guo, Xueqi Cheng), with Daiting Shi from Baidu Inc. Jiafeng Guo and Xueqi Cheng are senior, well-established researchers in information retrieval and large-scale ML at ICT CAS, and the group has a strong recent track record in LLM-based search and research agents — the cited baseline AutoResearchClaw (Liu et al., 2026) comes from the same ecosystem, so the team knows this evaluation setting deeply. That insider position cuts both ways: benchmark familiarity boosts credibility of the setup but also means the benchmark and the strongest baseline are the group's own artifacts (see Critical Assessment).

## Critical Assessment

- **Self-referential evaluation is the biggest concern.** Every main result is on ARC-Bench (Liu et al., 2026), and the strongest baseline, AutoResearchClaw, is the same group's own prior system; no external benchmark (e.g., CORE-Bench, ScienceAgentBench, MLAgentBench) or third-party baseline is tested. The 13.1% headline gain could partly reflect co-design of EEM with the benchmark's rubrics.
- **The judge is an LLM and the biggest gains sit in the most subjective metric.** All scores come from an LLM evaluator following benchmark rubrics, no human audit of judge reliability is reported, and EEM's largest improvement (+31.7%) is precisely in Result Analysis — the dimension weighted at 50% of overall and the hardest to score objectively. Code Development, where numbers are most verifiable, is the one metric where EEM *trails* both AutoResearchClaw (0.911 vs 0.938) and AIDE-ML (0.958).
- **Run-variance is of the same order as the headline margin.** On the one task where stability is measured (ML10), four runs span roughly 0.68–0.77 overall (~0.09 spread), while the headline margin over AutoResearchClaw averaged over 25 topics is 0.078. No significance tests are reported; the mean-over-25 design helps, but per-task variance this large warrants it.
- **The sufficiency gate h_t(d) is never directly validated.** There is no measurement of how often the assessment wrongly declares "sufficient" (risking premature full-scale investment) or "insufficient" (wasting pilots), no retrieval ablation on K, and no analysis of library growth, redundancy, or retrieval degradation over long runs — the pilot budget (120 s) is also arbitrary and never ablated.
- **Accumulation is within-task only.** Despite the "continuously growing library" framing, E_0 resets to empty for every topic; the cross-task transfer that would make this a true memory system is explicitly deferred, so the evaluated system is a per-task experience loop rather than persistent agent memory.
- **Honest reporting is a point in its favor:** the paper volunteers the Code Development deficit, the GPT-5.5 quality drop, and run variability rather than hiding them — the efficiency claim (−11.4% tokens) is also fully consistent with the reported per-stage tables.

## Strengths

- Clear, well-motivated problem framing: deciding *which* experiments are worth running, and treating experience sufficiency as an explicit, assessable quantity rather than an implicit side effect of reflection.
- The record + lesson representation with evidence grounding is a genuinely useful design — lessons are provably derived from observations, which addresses the hallucinated-lesson failure mode common in experience/memory agents.
- Quality and cost improve together: +13.1% overall while using 11.4% fewer tokens, meaning experience reuse actually pays for the overhead of distillation and pilots (experimentation-stage tokens −18.4%).
- Ablations are aligned one-to-one with the three claimed components and all three matter, with the largest effect (−24.9% without the library) supporting the central thesis; distillation is shown to beat raw record storage (0.674 vs 0.649).
- Backward-compatibility shown across backbone types (coding-oriented vs general-purpose), and the framework is fully automatic, requiring no human-AI collaboration loop unlike AutoResearchClaw.

## Limitations

- Single-benchmark evaluation on the authors' own ARC-Bench, against a strongest baseline from the same group; external validity is unestablished.
- Experience library is per-task and cold-start; no cross-task or cross-domain reuse, which limits the "accumulation" story to within one research run.
- Backbone-swap and stability analyses cover only one task (ML10) out of 25; GPT-5.5 results are not reported across the full benchmark.
- No validation of the sufficiency-assessment module itself (error rates of h_t(d), pilot cost-benefit tradeoff, sensitivity to retrieval size K, library growth behavior).
- Scoring depends entirely on LLM judges following rubrics, with no human verification reported; pilot budget of 120 seconds is a fixed, unexamined choice.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How reliable is the sufficiency gate h_t(d) in practice — what are its false-sufficient and false-insufficient rates, and what do the downstream costs of each error look like?
- Can the experience library be warm-started across tasks (E_0 ≠ ∅) without the negative transfer the authors flag, e.g., by conditioning transfer on applicability-condition matching?
- Do the gains survive on external, non-home-team benchmarks (CORE-Bench, ScienceAgentBench) with human-audited or ensemble judging, especially for the heavily weighted Result Analysis metric?
- Is EEM's benefit mainly from *storing* experience or from the *sufficiency-triggered pilot policy* — a hybrid ablation (raw records + pilots, distilled lessons without pilots) partially answers this, but a factorial design over storage × acquisition would be cleaner?
- How does EEM compare head-to-head with AI Research Preference Models (RPMs, Foster et al. 2026), which also use small-scale pilots for candidate ranking but via learned preference rather than explicit lessons?
