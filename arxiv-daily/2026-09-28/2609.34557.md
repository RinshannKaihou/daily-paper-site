> **一句话总结：** SkillRubric 把每条可复用技能表示为"双视角"结构——面向执行者的 guidance（怎么做事）+ 面向评估者的 rubric（怎么算做好）——用截图/工具输出作为证据，由多模态验证器把 rubric 兑现成 turn 级过程奖励，并通过"冻结策略配对 rollout 验证 guidance、固定 guidance 离线对齐验证 rubric"的交替协同进化（每 H=10 步更新一次）持续刷新技能库；在 8 个多模态 agent 基准上，Qwen3-VL-8B 平均 pass@1 从最强基线 SkillRL 的 13.12% 提升到 17.88%（+4.76 pts），Qwen3.5-9B 从 25.24% 提升到 28.79%（+3.55 pts），同时把平均交互轮数从 5.86/9.51 降到 4.49/4.55。

> **TL;DR:** SkillRubric (HKU × Tencent WeChat) turns each reusable skill into two aligned views — actor-facing guidance and an evaluator-facing rubric — and converts the rubric into turn-level process rewards via a coarse-to-fine multimodal verifier over screenshots/tool outputs, with guidance and rubric co-evolving in gated alternating updates (guidance accepted only if frozen-policy paired rollouts improve outcomes; rubrics accepted only if offline bootstrap alignment with outcome verdicts improves without pass-rate inflation). Across 8 multimodal agent benchmarks (AgentVista, BrowseComp-VL, VDR-Bench, VisualToolBench IID; VisBrowse-Bench, MMSearch-Plus, MM-SearchExam, TIR-Bench OOD), it beats the strongest skill-based baseline SkillRL by +4.76 pts on Qwen3-VL-8B (17.88% vs 13.12% avg pass@1) and +3.55 pts on Qwen3.5-9B (28.79% vs 25.24%), with the component ablation showing co-evolution as the largest single gain (+4.50 pts over GRPO+Rubric on Qwen3-VL-8B) and mean interaction turns dropping from 5.86→4.49 and 9.51→4.55.

## 核心贡献

- **双视角技能表示（核心洞察）**：一条构造良好的技能天然同时规定"如何行动"和"成功执行应满足什么"。SkillRubric 将每条技能表示为 S = (d, g, C)：适用性描述 d、面向 actor 的 guidance g（3-5 个动作阶段 + 常见错误警告）、面向 evaluator 的 rubric C（3-5 个方法无关、状态式的可验证 checkpoint）。两个视角来自同一程序性抽象，但对齐由构造保证，且 rubric 对 policy 严格隐藏。
- **证据落地的 turn 级过程奖励**：粗阶段（GPT-5-mini）做上下文感知的准则-轮次匹配，细阶段（Gemini 3.5 Flash）基于原始截图/工具输出给出二元满足度 s 和责任分 α，并把"证据状态"（missing/hypothesis/supported/verified/contradicted → 0/0.25/0.6/1/0）的变化量作为增量进度；过程优势 = 责任加权的满足度 + 增量进度项（clip 到 [-δ,δ]），与结果优势 A^out 相加后按 turn 共享给该 turn 内所有 policy 生成的 token。
- **门控交替协同进化**：guidance 更新只看"冻结策略下同条件配对 rollout"（≥4 对有效，接受条件：平均结果提升且无任何配对回归 >0.05）；rubric 更新延迟 H=10 步后在 128 条缓存轨迹上离线做 1,000 次配对 bootstrap 对齐检验（单侧 90% LCB ≥ -0.02）并通过 Δp ≤ 0.10 约束防止准则放水。顺序 (d,g,C)→(d,g',C)→(d,g',C') 保证归因干净：先隔离 guidance 对轨迹分布的影响，再让 rubric 适应新分布。
- **两级多教师技能蒸馏**：GPT-5.5 / Claude Opus 4.8 / Gemini 3.1 Pro 三教师各跑 3 条 rollout，先做 intra-teacher 聚合（对比成功/失败轨迹），再做 cross-teacher 合并，配 no-leakage 约束（准则中禁止任务答案、工具名、实体），最后用 Qwen3-Embedding-4B 多视角嵌入做 complete-linkage 层次聚类合并成能力级技能。
- **实验验证**：8 基准 × 2 骨干全部取得训练方法第一；组件消融、进化间隔消融、交互轮数分析和 SkillBank 跨模型迁移实验（Qwen3.5-35B-A3B、Qwen3.5-122B-A10B、GPT-5 nano/mini 等仅靠推理时注入技能即获益）共同支撑方法有效性。

## Key Contributions

- **Dual-view skill representation**: each reusable skill S = (d, g, C) carries an actor-facing guidance g (how to act) and an evaluator-facing rubric C (what counts as progress), both derived from the same procedural abstraction — aligning execution and evaluation "by construction" instead of authoring rubrics independently. The rubric is never exposed to the policy.
- **Evidence-grounded turn-level process rewards**: a two-stage verifier (GPT-5-mini coarse selector + Gemini 3.5 Flash fine-grained multimodal verifier) judges each rubric criterion against raw screenshots/tool outputs, assigns binary satisfaction plus non-negative responsibility scores over candidate turns, and measures incremental progress via an ordinal evidence-state scale; the resulting process advantage is added to the group-relative outcome advantage in a clipped GRPO objective.
- **Gated alternating co-evolution**: guidance revisions are validated by paired rollouts under a frozen policy (accept only if mean outcome improves with no paired regression >0.05); rubric revisions are validated offline on 128 cached outcome-labeled trajectories with a 1,000-sample bootstrap alignment test (one-sided 90% LCB ≥ -0.02) plus a pass-rate-inflation constraint (Δp ≤ 0.10). Updates alternate every H = 10 policy steps so behavior changes and reward-specification changes are never conflated.
- **Two-level multi-teacher distillation**: skills are distilled from GPT-5.5 / Claude Opus 4.8 / Gemini 3.1 Pro trajectories (both successes and failures) via intra-teacher aggregation then cross-teacher consolidation, with strict no-leakage constraints (no answers, tool names, or task-specific entities in criteria), followed by deterministic complete-linkage clustering into capability-level SkillBank entries.
- **Consistent empirical gains**: best trained method on all 8 benchmarks and both backbones; component, interval, and transfer ablations attribute the largest marginal gain to co-evolution and show evolved SkillBanks transfer inference-only to larger and closed-source models.

## Methodology

**Stage 1 — Dual-view SkillBank construction.** For each of 400 IID training tasks (100 per benchmark), three frontier teachers each generate 3 rollouts. A frozen skill synthesizer M_skill (GPT-5.5) first produces one teacher-specific candidate skill per task by contrasting successful and failed rollouts (intra-teacher aggregation), then merges the three candidates into a unified, model-agnostic skill (cross-teacher consolidation), and finally restructures it into the dual view: guidance with 3-5 action stages plus pitfall warnings, and 3-5 state-based, method-agnostic rubric checkpoints that must pass a counterfactual test (same score regardless of tool used) and a causal-binding test (achieving the checkpoint contributes to the correct outcome). Task-level skills are then clustered via agglomerative complete-linkage clustering on multi-view embeddings (Qwen3-Embedding-4B over d, g, C) and merged into capability-level SkillBank entries.

**Stage 2 — Skill-conditioned RL with turn-level rubric rewards.** Both backbones (Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, Qwen3.5-9B) are SFT warm-started on ~2,000 success-filtered teacher trajectories, then trained one epoch with GRPO (lr 3e-6, ε=0.2, KL 0.1, G=8 rollouts/task, 8 tasks/batch, max 20 turns, 32,768-token responses, temperature 0.7). Per rollout, the top-1 skill is retrieved by cosine similarity between the task and skill applicability descriptions; the guidance g is appended to the system prompt under a "# Skill Guidance" header (explicitly non-prescriptive). During reward computation, a coarse selector (GPT-5-mini) picks, for each turn, the subset of rubric criteria whose evidence state may have changed; a fine verifier (Gemini 3.5 Flash) reads the raw evidence windows and emits binary satisfaction s_{i,c} ∈ {0,1} and responsibility scores α_{i,c,t} over candidate turns. Satisfaction is group-normalized across the G rollouts, responsibility across candidate turns; the per-turn process advantage clips Σ_c w_c(ᾱ·s̃ + ω·Δφ) to [-δ, δ], where Δφ is the mapped change in evidence state (missing/hypothesis/supported/verified/contradicted → 0/0.25/0.6/1/0). Final advantage A_{i,t} = A^out + A^proc is shared by all policy tokens of turn t; environment tokens are excluded from the loss.

**Stage 3 — Outcome-grounded alternating co-evolution.** Every H = 10 optimization steps: (a) *Guidance update* — GPT-5.5 diagnoses outcome-rubric residuals and proposes a guidance patch only for recurring, residual-supported defects; the candidate is validated with paired rollouts on 8 tasks under the frozen policy (one rollout per arm, identical decoding), requiring ≥4 valid pairs, an improvement in mean outcome, and no paired regression >0.05. (b) *Rubric update (delayed by H steps)* — the candidate rubric Ĉ and current C^(v) are re-applied offline to the same 128 newly cached outcome-labeled trajectories; Ĉ is accepted only if the alignment gain Δa passes a one-sided 90% bootstrap lower bound ≥ -0.02 AND the pass-rate change satisfies Δp ≤ 0.10 (blocking criteria relaxation that merely inflates pass rates). The applicability description and the fixed outcome evaluator never change during co-evolution, and the active rubric is immutable while its own guidance proposal is generated, preventing self-validation.

**Evaluation.** One rollout per test task, near-greedy decoding (temp 0.2), same tool suite (zoom, code interpreter, web search ×7, image search ×5, visit) and system prompt for all methods; final correctness judged by DeepSeek-V4-Flash (pass@1), with criterion-level judging for VisualToolBench.

## Results & Highlights

- **Main results (avg pass@1, 8 benchmarks):** On Qwen3-VL-8B, SkillRubric reaches **17.88%** vs **13.12%** for the strongest baseline SkillRL (**+4.76 pts**), also ahead of XSkill (12.30), SkillLL0 (10.81), and Skill-SD (10.41); the same model *without* inference-time skill guidance still scores 15.41%, beating every baseline. On Qwen3.5-9B: **28.79%** vs SkillRL **25.24%** (**+3.55 pts**), with the no-skill variant at 27.09%. SkillRubric is best among trained methods on **all 8 benchmarks under both backbones**.
- **Parameter efficiency:** the 9B model's 28.79% average nearly matches the ~10x-larger Qwen3.5-122B-A10B reference (28.81%), exceeds Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (27.44%) and GPT-5 nano (17.68%), and stays competitive with Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite (30.19%) while winning on 4 of 8 benchmarks. Per-benchmark examples (Qwen3.5-9B): BrowseComp-VL 46.82 vs SkillRL 42.47 (+4.35); VDR-Bench 22.33 vs 16.33 (+6.00); TIR-Bench 33.00 vs 27.00.
- **Component ablation (unweighted average over 4 IID / 4 OOD / all 8):** Qwen3-VL-8B overall climbs SFT-only 9.70 → +outcome GRPO 11.54 → +rubric rewards (GRPO-Rubric) 13.38 → +co-evolution (full) **17.88** — co-evolution is the largest single marginal gain (**+4.50**); Qwen3.5-9B: 21.68 → 23.71 → 25.49 → **28.79** (+3.30). This also shows their GRPO+Rubric variant *without* skills already outperforms all four published baselines.
- **Evolution interval (Table 3):** on Qwen3-VL-8B, H=10 gives 17.88 vs 14.57 (H=5), 16.17 (H=20), and 13.58 for a fixed SkillBank (H=∞, i.e., GRPO-Rubric); guidance-evolution-only (16.86) and rubric-evolution-only (16.33) both fall short of evolving both — supporting the alternating design. All finite intervals beat the frozen SkillBank on both backbones.
- **Interaction efficiency (Table 4):** mean turns per task drop from 5.86 (backbone) to **4.49** for Qwen3-VL-8B + skill guidance, and from 9.51 to **4.55** for Qwen3.5-9B — consistent across IID and OOD, indicating fewer unproductive exploration loops.
- **SkillBank transfer (Fig. 3):** successive SkillBank versions (evolved during Qwen3.5-9B training) yield monotonically improving IID and OOD success via inference-only injection into Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, Qwen3.5-122B-A10B, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, GPT-5 nano, and GPT-5 mini — evolved procedural knowledge transfers across families and scales without retraining.

## Critical Assessment

- **The headline comparison partially conflates skills with process rewards.** All four baselines (XSkill, SkillRL, Skill-SD, SkillLL0) are skill-based but receive no process supervision; meanwhile the paper's own ablation shows GRPO-Rubric — the authors' reward machinery with a frozen SkillBank and no co-evolution — scores 13.38 (Qwen3-VL-8B) and 25.49 (Qwen3.5-9B), already above the best external baseline (13.12 / 25.24). The honest delta attributable to skills + co-evolution specifically is 17.88 vs 15.41 (+2.47, w/ vs w/o skill on Qwen3-VL-8B) and 28.79 vs 27.09 (+1.70 on Qwen3.5-9B) — real, but roughly half the advertised margin. A rubric-reward baseline without any skill component (e.g., instance-level rubrics-as-rewards) is missing.
- **LLM-as-judge everywhere, with no human audit.** The reward stack is GPT-5-mini (outcome + coarse matching), Gemini 3.5 Flash (fine verification), GPT-5.5 (skill synthesis, diagnosis, proposal), and DeepSeek-V4-Flash (final eval judging). The main text reports no inter-judge agreement, no human verification sample of rubric satisfaction or responsibility attribution, and no error analysis of verifier mistakes — reward hacking is addressed only indirectly via the outcome-alignment gates. Given that process rewards are the paper's core mechanism, this is the biggest evidentiary gap.
- **Small training set and noisy eval deltas.** Training uses only 400 tasks (100 per benchmark) augmented to 1k, one epoch. Eval sets range from 109 (AgentVista) to 311 (MMSearch-Plus) tasks, so a 1-2 pt gap is ~1-6 tasks; the paper reports single pass@1 numbers with no confidence intervals or seed variance in the main text, and near-greedy decoding can amplify run-to-run noise in agentic settings.
- **Baselines re-implemented on an unfamiliar suite.** The authors re-ran all four baselines with their original hyperparameters after porting them to the same SFT init and skill projection — a thoughtful control, but hyperparameters tuned on other benchmarks may underperform here, and none of the baselines had access to any form of process credit, so the external comparison is structurally asymmetric.
- **Minor inconsistency / overclaim risk:** the text claims "H = 10 yields the best IID and OOD performance on both backbones," yet the Qwen3.5-9B rows in Table 3 show H=10 and H=20 nearly tied (e.g., 27.21/28.72 vs 27.21/28.72-level numbers), so the interval choice is not sharply identified on the second backbone; likewise "nearly matches Qwen3.5-122B-A10B" rests on a 0.02-pt gap under judge-based scoring.
- **Reproducibility:** the pipeline depends on five-plus proprietary frontier models as teachers/verifiers/judges — costly and impossible to reproduce exactly; however, full prompts and thresholds (LCB ≥ -0.02, Δp ≤ 0.10, regression ≤ 0.05) are published, which is better than typical practice.
- **Genuine strengths worth crediting:** the gated co-evolution design (frozen-policy paired rollouts for guidance, delayed offline relabeling for rubrics, anti-inflation pass-rate constraint) is a principled answer to a real confound — jointly updating policy, guidance, and reward would make improvements unattributable — and the counterfactual/method-agnostic criteria tests during skill construction directly target reward-hacking via tool-sequence imitation.

## Strengths

- The dual-view insight (a skill already specifies both how to act and what success looks like) is simple, well-motivated, and yields alignment between guidance and evaluation criteria for free rather than by post-hoc rubric authoring.
- Turn-level credit assignment grounded in verifiable evidence states (screenshots, tool outputs) with strict anti-hindsight rules (a turn is judged only on evidence available at that turn) is a thoughtful form of process supervision for long-horizon multimodal trajectories.
- The alternating co-evolution protocol is cleanly engineered for causal attribution: paired rollouts isolate guidance effects; delayed offline rubric relabeling on cached trajectories isolates reward-specification effects; the Δp ≤ 0.10 constraint explicitly blocks criteria inflation.
- Comprehensive evaluation: 8 benchmarks spanning 4 IID + 4 OOD domains, two backbones, four adapted baselines sharing the same SFT init/tool suite/judge, component and interval ablations, interaction-efficiency analysis, and cross-family SkillBank transfer including closed-source models.
- Exceptional documentation: all construction/co-evolution/verification prompts, thresholds, and baseline adaptations are in the appendix; skills are distilled from failures as well as successes, and no-leakage constraints prevent evaluation answers from entering criteria.

## Limitations

- All quantitative process rewards derive from proprietary LLM judges with unquantified noise; no human validation, judge-agreement statistics, or ablation over verifier choice (e.g., swapping Gemini 3.5 Flash for an open model) is reported.
- The skill-specific gain over the authors' own rubric-reward training is modest (+2.47 / +1.70 pts), and no comparison against non-skill process-reward methods isolates the contribution of the SkillBank machinery per se.
- Training scale is small (400 seed tasks → 1k augmented, 1 epoch), and all four IID benchmarks are also training sources, so "IID" gains may partly reflect benchmark-specific skill fitting despite the OOD checks.
- Single-rollout pass@1 evaluation without error bars; eval sets are small (109-311 tasks), so several per-benchmark gaps are within noise range.
- Dependence on a five-model proprietary stack (GPT-5.5, Claude Opus 4.8, Gemini 3.1 Pro, GPT-5-mini, Gemini 3.5 Flash, plus DeepSeek-V4-Flash judging) makes exact reproduction and cost accounting difficult; no verification-cost-per-step numbers are given despite the "cost-aware verification" claim in the intro.
- Evolution gates accept/reject using the same judge family that produces training rewards, so a systematically biased outcome evaluator would steer both policy and SkillBank in the same wrong direction.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How robust is turn-level credit to verifier noise? Would replacing GPT-5-mini/Gemini 3.5 Flash with a single open-source verifier (or human-audited spot checks) preserve the co-evolution gains, and what is the judge-policy agreement rate on responsibility attribution?
- Can rubric-free process rewards (e.g., implicit process rewards or progress estimators) be combined with the SkillBank to separate the contribution of "explicit rubrics" from "turn-level decomposition"?
- Does the alternating co-evolution schedule remain stable over multiple epochs or much larger task pools, or do the acceptance gates eventually freeze the SkillBank (no candidate ever passes)?
- The guidance update gate requires no paired regression >0.05 on 8 held training tasks — does this overfit guidance to the training distribution, and does a held-out validation task set change acceptance rates?
- Skills are clustered per benchmark before pooling; how does performance change if clustering is done globally across benchmarks, or if the SkillBank is bootstrapped fully from the student's own rollouts without frontier-teacher distillation?
- Can the dual-view skill representation transfer to text-only agents (e.g., SWE or web-arena settings), where "screenshots as evidence" is unavailable and evidence states must come from textual tool outputs?
