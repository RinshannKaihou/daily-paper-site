01 Mechanism & fix

Softmax backward computes dS = P ⊙ (dP − δ), and exact arithmetic forces every row of dS to sum to zero — the row-shift invariance of softmax. Naive FP8 FlashAttention keeps that structure but pulls δ from the forward's saved output, a product of quantized operands, so probability-quantization and gradient-quantization errors no longer cancel.

A BF16 probe on 1.67B checkpoints measured row-sum residuals of 1.1–4.7×10⁻² rad and traced the downstream dynamics: each residual acts as a per-row logit bias that sharpens attention rows and selectively inflates RMSNorm gains — a few channels capture >99% of the gradient correlation, pushing softmax toward saturation. This is why short, small-scale runs look clean while long, large-scale runs collapse.

The fix changes only the softmax backward kernel: recompute δ from unquantized FP32 probabilities. No architecture change, no auxiliary forward output, no smaller global batch — and all 7 attention-core matmuls (2 forward + 5 backward) run native block-scaled FP8.

The bug — stale delta

δistale = ⟨ dOi, Ōi ⟩

The saved-output shortcut reuses the forward's FP8 output Ō. Quantized P̂ and recomputed dP̂ come from different quantization paths, so their errors can't cancel: Σⱼ dSij ≠ 0.

The fix — Delta-Matching

δimatched = Σj Pij · dP̂ij

FP32 probabilities × dequantized FP8 operands, recomputed inside the softmax backward kernel. Residuals drop to ~2.7–3.4×10⁻³ rad after FP8 casting — 5.3–6.7× smaller than stale delta.

Fine print: the invariance proof assumes normalized P, identical effective V̂ across passes, and no accumulation/storage/reduction rounding — real kernels only approximate this (see Critical Edge above).

Kernel economics

Despite recomputing δ, the FP8 kernel's combined forward+backward latency on H100 beats BF16 FlashAttention-3. The correctness fix costs nothing at runtime — adoption runs through attention-kernel vendors, since δ is recomputed inside the backward kernel itself.

Mitigations that don't fix it

QK-normalization (Kimi-K2-style) prevents catastrophic collapse but leaves a substantial gap. NoPE + lower-LR context extension delays degradation without eliminating it. Neither restores the invariant — only matching the delta does.

03 Strengths

  • Complete causal chain — theory (invariant violation) → mechanism (gain amplification, softmax saturation) → fix, each link backed by controlled experiments.
  • The right control — consistent-dO ablation cleanly isolates probability-quantization error as the degrading component.
  • Practical scale warning — 569M looks fine, 5.29B collapses, extension gap appears only after 20B tokens: small FP8 pilots can hide a real failure.
  • Costs nothing — no architecture change, no auxiliary forward, no smaller batch; faster than BF16 FA3 on H100.
  • Broad coverage — 3 architectures, 3 variants, 2 extra scales, context extension, AdamW + Muon; implementation, models and data recipes promised.

03 Limitations

  • Scale ceiling — validated only to 5.29B / ~30B tokens; frontier-scale behavior with accumulated optimization error is extrapolated.
  • Two seeds per config — some headline contrasts (e.g. the divergent TE extension run) rest on single-run events.
  • Unexplained dips — RULER-8K at 1.67B (47.8 vs 52.9) and KDA/GQA RULER-4K (~5 pts low) remain open.
  • Conditional proof — the invariant proof assumes away rounding that real kernels exhibit; measured post-cast residuals are ~10⁻³ rad, not zero.
  • Narrow externals — single dataset (Nemotron-CC), BF16-only evaluation, no code released at review time.
  • Not a wrapper — requires modifying the attention backward kernel, so adoption depends on kernel vendors.

04 Open questions

Q1Does near-parity hold at frontier scale (hundreds of billions of tokens), where the paper's own accumulation-error hypothesis predicts new failure modes?

Q2What explains the RULER-8K gap at 1.67B (47.8% vs 52.9%) while 5.29B RULER exceeds the reference — seed noise, or a systematic mid-scale long-retrieval deficit?

Q3Can the stale-delta row-sum residual (~10⁻² rad) serve as an online early-warning monitor for FP8 training divergence, analogous to loss-spike detectors?

Q4How does Delta-Matching interact with NVFP4 / MXFP8 block-scaled formats on Blackwell, where two more precision tiers sit below FP8?

Q5Does the probability-vs-gradient quantization decomposition expose additional invariant violations in linear-layer FP8 GEMMs, or is attention uniquely exposed?
