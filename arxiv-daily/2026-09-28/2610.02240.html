Why it matters

首个 bfloat16 GEMM 上 checksum ABFT 的经验假阳性阈值——正是 LLM 实际运行的精度区间，可直接校准「廉价初筛器」的部署预期。

First empirical false-positive threshold for checksum ABFT at bfloat16 — the precision LLMs actually run at — grounding “cheap first filter” deployment.

Critical edge

Calibrated on one Tesla T4 with benign uniform inputs; τ is extrapolated decades past the data (10⁻² → 2.44×10⁻¹⁰) and does not transfer to other GPUs — or to real heavy-tailed LLM tensors.

01Methodology

How the threshold was built

One-sided Huang–Abraham checksum on C=AB: compare d=Ce against d′=A(Be); the per-row residual is rᵢ=|dᵢ−d′ᵢ|/sᵢ with sᵢ=|A|(|B|e), all accumulation in float64. Two earlier formulations were discarded — a signed grand-sum normalizer (max-to-median 389×) and a scalar reduction that destroyed detectability (0/1800) and was later retracted.

After C=AB completes, single (element, bit) pairs inside the output tensor are XOR-flipped across five bf16 bit classes. Platform: one Tesla T4 (sm75), torch 2.13.0+cu130, deterministic seeding; cuBLAS verified bitwise-deterministic in 44/44 repeated launches.

20,000 clean GEMMs at K=4096 yield the residual sample; a GPD tail fit is extrapolated to a 10⁻⁶ per-GEMM false-positive rate. Detection predicate: r_max > τ or r_max non-finite — the non-finite clause was added only after a pre-publication audit caught the naive comparison silently discarding Inf residuals.

Pooled single exponent-bit flips: 304/400 = 76% at τ vs the pre-registered bar of 90% (≥360/400). The low-mantissa blindness is verified as real, not an injector artifact — 0 of 50 sampled flips were erased by rounding (relative deltas 0.00407–0.0313, strictly positive). External anchor: Ma et al. 2025 found checksum ABFT missing real corruption on 14 of 15 genuinely faulty production nodes at float32. Deployment map: trust exponent-class corruption; never trust low-mantissa at any flip count — see Corrections ↓ for what almost hid this.

Signal scales against the same K^-1/2 normalizer, so the ratio the detector tests is K-independent — the registered falsifier (“flat ⇒ mechanism wrong”) fired on the study’s own design hypothesis.

04Corrections

Self-correction on the record

Four retractions, two audit-caught defects, and one unevaluable fit — all reported in full rather than deleted. The most consequential:

  • R1
    Retracted

    Scalar-reduction “structural invisibility.” 0/1800 detection was blamed on physics; the per-row form detects 155/200 on the same workload and seeds (the retracted eval showed 44.75%, 179/400).

  • R2
    Bug → fixed

    Comparison operator silently discarded Inf residuals, understating EXPONENT_HIGH detection in 10 of 15 cells by up to 14 pp and fabricating a non-monotonicity; the corrected predicate restores monotone detection in flip count at every K. Lesson generalizes to any floating-point threshold detector.

  • R3
    Untestable

    Operand-asymmetry mechanism prediction could not be tested — the harness injects into the output tensor only; the 0.5247 shared-mantissa minimum stays unexplained.

  • F1
    Unevaluable

    The p95 GPD refit is not evaluable: the harness stored only the top 64 residuals per GEMM (mean 205.3 exceedances at p95), truncating two-thirds of the tail — discovered after the method was locked.

05For

Strengths

  • +Fills a precisely-stated hole: a calibrated, FPR-annotated bf16 threshold exactly where the classical Ku bound dies and modern LLM workloads live.
  • +Negative results with teeth: the 90% bar was written into the README before any code, the detector failed it at 76%, and the bar never moved.
  • +The bit-class map deploys directly: trust checksum ABFT for exponent-class corruption; never for low-mantissa, at any flip multiplicity.
  • +The Inf-comparison lesson generalizes beyond this paper to every threshold detector on floating-point residuals.
  • +Maximal reproducibility: deterministic seeding, versioned perturbation spec, commit-timestamped predictions, retractions preserved as evidence.
06Against

Limitations

  • !One configuration point: Tesla T4 (sm75), one driver/CUDA/torch tuple — τ and the clean floor do not transfer, by the authors’ own statement.
  • !Output-tensor-only perturbations; no operand, intermediate, or instruction-level faults and no estimate of real-silicon fault incidence.
  • !Extreme tail extrapolation (~10⁻² → 2.44×10⁻¹⁰) with upper endpoints spanning 1.23× and the p95 refit unevaluable — τ’s far-tail guarantee is softer than “10⁻⁶ FP” reads.
  • !Statistical power: n=100/200 cells give SEs of ~4/~3 pp; sub-percent detection rates and small deltas are unresolved.
  • !Benign inputs: uniform [−1,1] factors; no heavy-tailed real LLM weights or activations, no training-loop context.
  • !Loose ends: unexplained 0.5247 mantissa minimum; unresolved float32-vs-float64 checksum-backend discrepancy in the released code.
07Open

Questions for further exploration

  1. Q1Do τ and the K^-1/2 floor scaling transfer to tensor-core accumulation paths (split-K, FP32-accumulate) and to datacenter GPUs where cuBLAS kernel selection may not be launch-stable?
  2. Q2How much does the residual distribution — hence τ — shift under realistic heavy-tailed, outlier-laden LLM weight/activation tensors instead of uniform [−1,1] factors?
  3. Q3Which cheap complementary detector covers the MANTISSA_LOW blind spot — two-sided checksums, dithered rounding guards, periodic full-precision spot checks — and at what overhead relative to the O(MK+KN) one-sided check?
  4. Q4Is the shared 0.5247 mantissa-cell minimum derivable analytically from the |A||B|e normalizer’s distribution, or is it an artifact of the float64 promotion path?
  5. Q5At what real-silicon incidence of low-mantissa-class corruption does the 0% detection cell flip the cost-benefit of deploying checksum ABFT as a first filter?

The paper’s own conclusion

Use checksum ABFT as a cheap first filter with known blind spots — not as a complete silent-data-corruption detector.
