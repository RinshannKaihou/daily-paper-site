01

Methodology

TaskBoot · 4B judge · CertHarvest

A judge scores each rendered trajectory s(x) ∈ [0,1]. Two thresholds (θrej, θrel) auto-reject failures, auto-release successes, and defer the rest to humans. The two sides carry different error functionals and are certified separately: the largest coverage with error ≤ α at confidence 1−δ.

Seven corpora — tool-use dialogues, terminal sessions, three web suites, two code-repair sets — are split by task into SFT/RL/CAL/TEST under one pre-registered seed; TEST is read exactly once per configuration. CODE-O is judge-blind by design: its verdict lives in a held-out test suite never shown to the judge.

TaskBoot redraws task-level bootstrap resamples across a pre-declared grid of 40 score quantiles; a threshold is certified when the Bonferroni-adjusted upper quantile of the clustered error stays ≤ α. Every cluster-valid finite-sample alternative certifies zero, so an asymptotic-in-G bootstrap — validated by simulation down to G=20 — is the only non-vacuous option.

The judge is a 4B model emitting a single verdict token: renormalized SUCCESS probability, one forward pass, no reasoning chain — SFT, then GRPO with an asymmetric reward (μ>λ) that punishes discarded successes more than shipped failures.

CertHarvest reuses the certificate as a self-training filter: pseudo-labels only from certified regions, contamination bounded by α by construction, harvest refused entirely when no certificate exists.

03

Strengths

  • +Asks the operationally correct question — how much work can the judge provably absorb — and answers with an auditable, pre-registered protocol; failure corpora stay in the main text.
  • +Rank invariance is crisp and actionable: recalibration provably cannot rescue a judge; only a different ranking can.
  • +Uniformly safe at no coverage cost: ≤1% violations in simulation; realized errors 0.011–0.037 vs α=0.1.
  • +CertHarvest is a rare concrete safety interlock for self-improvement — and it refuses to harvest when no certificate exists.
  • +Unusual candor: judge-blind CODE-O, saturated WEB-M/F, too-few-task TERM/CODE-S all diagnosed in the main text; economics quantified on one local GPU.
03

Limitations

  • −The guarantee is asymptotic in task count, simulation-audited down to G=20 — not a finite-sample theorem.
  • −The i.i.d. indictment is a one-corpus, one-regime claim (WEB-M, ρ=0.80); the abstract’s framing is broader than the evidence.
  • −Single pre-registered run, one seed — training randomness and run-to-run variance are unquantified.
  • −Mostly one-sided automation: release rarely certifies, so successes still need human confirmation.
  • −The predictability law is a 7-point, one-seed fit (LOOCV R²=0.96 is fragile); the three RL gates are stated post-hoc.
  • −Small corpora bind: WEB-A n=200; multi-round self-training is unsafe (0.585 → 0.465 on round two) — one round is the honest recommendation.
04

Questions for Further Exploration

from the source summary
  1. Can a genuinely finite-sample cluster-valid construction ever be non-vacuous at G≈30–100, or is a bootstrap approximation the only workable middle ground?
  2. The 17.5% failure is shown on a single ρ=0.80 corpus — can the (ρ, G, mg) boundary where i.i.d. certification starts failing be characterized theoretically?
  3. α caps contamination but not selection skew: is there a companion statistic (acceptance-rate or coverage-decay bound) that would make multi-round self-training safe?
  4. Release-side coverage rarely certifies — what judge architectures or extra evidence (environment-state access, post-hoc tool replay) could unlock certified auto-acceptance of successes?
  5. Does the two-parameter law (π and base AUROC) transfer beyond these seven corpora — to SWE-bench-style repositories, GUI agents, or long-horizon research tasks?
