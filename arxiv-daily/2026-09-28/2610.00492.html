Why this matters02 / So what

First systematic benchmark of AI-scientist discovery: it quantifies the gap between hitting optimization targets and producing explainable insight — directly guiding reward design for long-horizon autonomous research agents.

Critical edge03 / Weakest link
⚠︎ JUDGE DEPENDENCE

Every insight verdict comes from LLM judges — one sharing a model family with three of the judged agents — and no human-agreement number is reported. Judges are acknowledged as “overly generous”; a genuinely novel discovery scores zero by construction.

01

Methodology

26 tasks · 6 domains · 306 insight questions

Each task is a tuple T = (x, o₁, Å, R): a description with scientific background, initial observations that challenge existing understanding, a containerized simulator (CLI), and a rubric. Real research problems from neuroscience, geophysics, plasma physics, astrophysics, CS, and chemistry — co-built with 10 domain experts, 15 more consulted.

The agent runs an iterative experimentation loop and submits three artifacts: mechanism.md (causal explanation), mechanism.py (executable), experiments.log.

SC

Scientific constraints

Binary domain common-sense tests; the mechanism must pass all of them.

PA

Predictive accuracy

mechanism.py actually executed on held-out data; continuous score per rubric.

SI

Scientific insights

Fraction of 306 expert-written questions derivable from the mechanism, judged by two agentic judges.

The conditioned score = (p_PA + p_SI) only if all SC tests pass, else 0 — averaged over two independent judges (Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT 5.6 Sol), who may run extra experiments but must not modify the mechanism.

Budget 1×H100 · 1,000 iterations · 4 h Web to sources blocked Agents 7 (3 harnesses) Human baseline = published expert progress

03

How agents fail — expert-reviewed taxonomy

verbatim log evidence
F-01

Overreliance on preselected hypotheses

Anchor early; never escape the first framing.

F-02

Curve fitting mistaken for mechanism discovery

Optimizing the task score is not explaining the phenomenon.

F-03

Experiments not used to resolve uncertainty

Simulation budget exists; agents don’t spend it to learn.

F-04

Stopping at observation

See the anomaly, describe it, quit — no further exploration.

Positive behaviors were also logged: agents sometimes rejected incorrect assumptions rather than fitting them. The taxonomy gives RL-environment and reward-signal design concrete targets.

Strengths

  • Real mechanism discovery: mechanism.py is executed on held-out data; environments are actual simulators (tokamak transport, C. elegans, CAMELS cosmology), packaged for reproduction.
  • Clean, memorable dissociation: the mechanism-vs-experimentation split (42.4 vs 22.7) is a genuinely novel diagnostic of where insight comes from.
  • Substantive expert involvement: 306 verified insight questions, expert-written SC criteria, qualitative failure review with verbatim quotes.
  • Honest failure taxonomy that gives follow-up work concrete targets, including RL training environments.
  • Transparent protocol: task-spec template, exact judge prompts with JSON verdicts, public code and website.

Limitations

  • LLM-judged SI/SC with no human-agreement number; acknowledged judge over-generosity, and the Claude-family judge scored Claude-family agents.
  • Only expert-anticipated insights earn credit — a genuinely novel discovery scores zero by construction.
  • n = 26, skewed domains (10 neuroscience, 1 chemistry), single run per agent; no error bars — gaps like Opus 5 (27.3) vs Astra (28.7) are not interpretable.
  • Human baseline is published expert work with months of context, not matched to the agents’ 4-hour budget.
  • Pretraining contamination from source publications is possible and unquantified.
  • Model and harness are confounded (vendor harnesses paired with vendor models), blurring attribution.
  • +insights ablation runs on only two problems; its ARC result contradicts the text.
05

Questions for further exploration

6 open threads
Q1

Would human-expert agreement with LLM judges’ SI verdicts support the headline conclusions — especially for Claude-judged Claude agents?

Q2

Do PA/SI gaps persist under multiple independent runs, and how much of the 15–17/26 SC failure rate is run-level noise?

Q3

Can an insight-derivation reward signal for RL be computed without privileged access to the expert rubric — can insight-seeking be trained rather than prompted?

Q4

How much PA comes from fitting simulator quirks vs actual physics — do discovered mechanisms transfer to out-of-distribution operating points?

Q5

Is the near-zero +insights effect on ARC tokamak a domain-specific ceiling of plasma transport, or rubric coverage limits penalizing alternative discovery paths?

Q6

Would results change if agents could retrieve adjacent literature (not the source paper), as human scientists actually do?
