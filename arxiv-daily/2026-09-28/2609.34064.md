> **一句话总结：** 提出 SPrPO：在 LLM 智能体的 RL 训练中向 FFN 隐层通道注入"敏感度自适应"的乘性高斯噪声（敏感通道噪声更小），并把 TRPO 单调改进理论扩展到扰动设定给出高概率保证；Qwen2.5-1.5B 智能体在 WebShop 高斯噪声 σ=0.2 下成功率 30.1%（Vanilla 仅 18.7%）、结构化剪枝 r=0.2 下 12.4%（Vanilla 仅 2.3%），同时干净成功率不降反升（WebShop 57.0→60.0）。

> **TL;DR:** RL-trained LLM agents collapse under deployment-time policy perturbations: a Qwen2.5-1.5B agent trained with GiGPO drops from 57.0% to 18.7% success on WebShop under Gaussian noise σ=0.20. The paper extends TRPO's monotonic improvement bound to a probabilistic perturbation setting, showing stable updates require expected improvement to dominate perturbation-induced variance, and derives SPrPO — multiplicative Gaussian noise on FFN channels whose per-channel std shrinks inversely with the channel's sensitivity (squared gradient of the PPO surrogate w.r.t. the channel gain). Under σ=0.20 Gaussian noise on WebShop, SPrPO reaches 30.1% success vs 18.7% for vanilla (+11.4 pts) and 15.2% for the SAM baseline; under structured pruning r=0.20 it scores 12.4% vs 2.3% vanilla. Clean accuracy also improves (WebShop 60.0%, ALFWorld 91.9% vs 87.6%), at ~1.7–1.8x training-step cost. Caveat: under extreme INT4 quantization SPrPO loses to SAM on ALFWorld (48.1 vs 59.6).

## 核心贡献

- **问题首次系统化**：首次研究 RL 训练的 LLM 智能体对"策略扰动"（推理期量化、剪枝、隐层噪声）的鲁棒性，并给出形式化定义 `(δ, ε, β)`-概率扰动鲁棒性（扰动幅度 ≤ δ 时，性能损失 > ε 的概率 ≤ β）——区别于单轮任务的鲁棒性，长程智能体一步错可毁掉整条轨迹，因此扰动注入必须克制。
- **理论**：把 TRPO 单调改进界扩展到扰动设定（Theorem 1）：稳定改进要求期望改进量 `m_ν` 超过扰动诱导方差的空隙 `√((1−β)ν_ν/β)`；结合高斯 Poincaré 不等式（Corollary 1）得到可操作设计原则——**对敏感维度分配更小方差**；Theorem 2 证明稳定更新序列的极限点均为概率扰动鲁棒的。
- **算法 SPrPO**：对 FFN 中间通道施加乘性噪声 `h̃=(1+ξ)h`，每通道噪声标准差 `σ_c ∝ ρ·m̄^α·Ŝ_c^{-α}`——基础尺度 ρ × 梯度范数调度 m̄ × 通道敏感度 Ŝ 的幂次倒数；无需额外 rollout，只需对当前 rollout batch 多做一次前向/反向传播来估计敏感度统计量。
- **实验**：在 ALFWorld 和 WebShop 上系统评测 5 类扰动（高斯噪声、通道 dropout、Fisher 结构化剪枝、SparseGPT 非结构化剪枝、RTN INT8/6/4 量化）× 多档强度 × 3 seeds，SPrPO 在绝大多数设置下保持率最高；消融实验逐一验证敏感度加权、梯度范数调度两个组件的必要性。

## Key Contributions

- First systematic study of perturbation robustness (hidden-state noise, pruning, quantization) for RL-trained LLM agents, formalized as (δ, ε, β)-probabilistic perturbation robustness under a general perturbation distribution.
- A theoretical extension of TRPO's monotonic improvement analysis: Theorem 1 shows stable improvement holds with probability ≥ 1−β only when the expected improvement lower bound m_ν exceeds the variance slack √((1−β)ν_ν/β); Corollary 1 (via Gaussian Poincaré) yields the actionable rule of allocating less perturbation variance to more sensitive dimensions.
- SPrPO: a practical algorithm injecting multiplicative Gaussian noise into FFN hidden channels with per-channel std σ_{k+1,c} = ρ·m̄^α·Ŝ^{-α}_{k+1,c}, where sensitivity Ŝ and the gradient-norm scheduler m̄ are estimated from one extra forward/backward pass on the existing rollout batch (no extra rollouts).
- A comprehensive robustness benchmark: 5 perturbation families at multiple magnitudes on ALFWorld (274 tasks incl. 134 OOD) and WebShop (500 tasks), 3 seeds, with a shared calibration set for fair pruning comparisons — SPrPO shows the best retention in most settings.

## Methodology

**Problem formulation.** The agent is an LLM policy π_θ in a finite-horizon MDP (ALFWorld/WebShop). Perturbations ξ are operators applied to the policy (parameters or hidden representations); Ξ_δ is the ball of perturbations with magnitude ≤ δ. A policy is (δ, ε, β)-probabilistically perturbation robust if Pr(J(π̃ξ) ≥ J(π) − ε) ≥ 1 − β for ξ ~ ν supported on Ξ_δ — a high-probability guarantee rather than a worst-case one.

**Theory.** Starting from the TRPO lower bound ΔJ(π') ≥ (1/(1−γ))E[A_π] − (4γA_max/(1−γ)²)α², the authors evaluate improvement under perturbed policies before and after an update. Theorem 1 defines the expected improvement lower bound m_ν = L_ν(θ') − (4γA_max/(1−γ)²)E_ξ[α_ξ²] and, via Cantelli's inequality, proves Pr(ΔJ_ξ ≥ m_ν − √((1−β)ν_ν/β)) ≥ 1−β: improvement is stable only if the mean improvement dominates the perturbation-induced variance ν_ν = Var_ξ[ΔJ_ξ]. Corollary 1 bounds that variance for Gaussian noise using the Gaussian Poincaré inequality as Σᵢσᵢ²Sᵢ(θ')², where Sᵢ is the per-dimension sensitivity — implying perturbation std should decrease along sensitive directions. Theorem 2 extends this to convergence: under compactness/continuity assumptions, greedy selection from the stability set U_δ yields a policy sequence whose every limit point is (δ, ε, β)-probabilistically perturbation robust.

**Algorithm (SPrPO).** Following the analysis, perturbations are multiplicative Gaussian noise on FFN intermediate channels: h̃ = (1+ξ)h with ξ_c ~ N(0, σ_c²), applied after the up-projection + SiLU and before the down-projection (equivalent to perturbing rows of W_down). The per-channel std at iteration k is σ_{k+1,c} = ρ·m̄^α_{k+1}·Ŝ^{-α}_{k+1,c}, where:
- Ŝ_c = squared gradient of the PPO surrogate loss w.r.t. the multiplicative channel gain — a tractable proxy for the channel's contribution to improvement variance (following flatness literature);
- m̄ = ‖∇_θ L‖² — the squared gradient norm, used as a scheduler proxy for the expected improvement term (noise grows as training progresses);
- ρ = 0.20 is the base scale and α = 1.0 the sensitivity exponent (α=0 recovers isotropic noise).
After each policy update, the current rollout batch is replayed once at θ_{k+1} (one extra forward/backward pass, no extra rollouts) to refresh Ŝ and m̄. Training uses Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct with LoRA (rank/alpha 64/64), GiGPO as the base critic-free RL algorithm, 200 steps, a "no-thinking" action format (reasoning traces disabled — a ~3.5x speedup), and vLLM inference at temperature 0.4.

**Evaluation protocol.** Robustness is measured as success rate under evaluation-time perturbations: channel-wise Gaussian noise (σ ≤ 0.20), channel dropout (p ≤ 0.05), structured FFN pruning by diagonal-Fisher importance (r ≤ 0.20), unstructured SparseGPT pruning (r up to 0.70 on ALFWorld / 0.40 on WebShop), and RTN weight quantization (INT8/INT6/INT4). All compared methods share a fixed calibration set of 128 vanilla-policy sequences for pruning importance scores. Three seeds; results reported as mean ± std plus retention (% of clean performance).

## Results & Highlights

- **WebShop, Gaussian noise σ=0.20:** SPrPO 30.13% success (59.2% retention of clean) vs Vanilla GiGPO 18.73% (33.9% retention), isotropic Gaussian 17.87%, SAM 15.20% — +11.4 pts over the vanilla policy and +11.9/+14.9 over the two perturbation baselines. Across the whole σ sweep SPrPO is best at every strength (e.g., σ=0.15: 44.33 vs 32.73 vanilla).
- **ALFWorld, Gaussian noise σ=0.20:** SPrPO 75.91% vs Vanilla 64.96%, Gaussian 72.26%, SAM 66.79% (+10.95 over vanilla, +3.65 over the best baseline). Under channel dropout p=0.05: 64.80% vs 54.62/57.79/55.60 (+7.01 over the best baseline). The advantage holds on both the 140 ID and 134 OOD tasks.
- **Structured FFN pruning r=0.20 on WebShop:** SPrPO 12.40% vs Vanilla 2.27%, isotropic Gaussian 0.27%, SAM 0.60% — a ~5.5x improvement over vanilla, though all absolute numbers are low; on ALFWorld at r=0.20 SPrPO reaches 47.08% vs 41.73% vanilla (but with overlapping error bars, 47.08±7.65 vs 41.73±5.32).
- **Unstructured SparseGPT pruning:** at the strongest ratio (r=0.70 ALFWorld) SPrPO 58.03% vs Vanilla 41.12%, Gaussian 32.85%, SAM 50.97%; on WebShop r=0.40 SPrPO 50.20% vs 42.00/34.33/29.53. SPrPO also beats vanilla on clean accuracy here: 60.00% vs 57.00% (WebShop) and 91.85% vs 87.59% (ALFWorld) — the perturbation training doubles as mild regularization.
- **Quantization:** at INT6 SPrPO wins on both environments (WebShop 55.67% vs 45.20% vanilla / 34.83% SAM; ALFWorld 91.24% ≈ 99.3% retention vs 86.98% vanilla). **But at INT4 the ordering flips:** on ALFWorld SAM reaches 59.61% vs SPrPO 48.05% (vanilla 47.81%), and on WebShop isotropic Gaussian 9.87% edges out SPrPO 8.20% — all methods are in the failure regime the paper itself flags.
- **Ablations (WebShop, Table 9):** removing the gradient-norm scheduler drops clean success from 60.00% to 52.13% (−7.87 pts) and structured-pruning performance from 12.40% to 3.60%; removing sensitivity weighting (α=0) drops clean success to 48.80% — worse than no perturbation at all (57.00%) — confirming that naive isotropic noise hurts. ρ sweep: 0.2 is the sweet spot; ρ=0.3 corrupts training (clean 48.27%).
- **Cost:** SPrPO raises per-step training time from 343.6 s to 572.9 s on ALFWorld (~1.7x) and 110.6 s to 197.4 s on WebShop (~1.8x) on 2x RTX PRO 6000 GPUs; reward-landscape visualizations show SPrPO-trained policies have visibly flatter surfaces than vanilla's sharp cliffs.

## Authors & Credibility

All five authors (Pengxin Wang, Yuanzhe Li, Yuxin Ren, Huanrui Yang, Jingdi Chen) are with the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering, University of Arizona. Huanrui Yang's prior work includes "Training Sensitive Weights" (noise-perturbation fine-tuning for robust LLM quantization, PMLR 2025), so the group has direct lineage in perturbation-based robustness for LLM compression; the combination with RL-agent training appears new for this team. The paper includes reproducibility and AI-use statements, full hyperparameter tables, and complete proofs in the appendix. Code is promised "upon acceptance" — i.e., not yet available. The experiments are modest in scale (1.5B model, 2x consumer-tier Blackwell GPUs) but consistently executed with 3 seeds and shared calibration protocols, which supports credibility of the reported comparisons.

## Critical Assessment

- **Thin baseline field.** All baselines are perturbation-strategy variants inside the same GiGPO training loop (vanilla / isotropic Gaussian / FFN-channel SAM). The paper cites closely related post-hoc robustness pipelines — including Wang & Yang 2025's quantization-aware noise fine-tuning from the same senior author — but never compares against them, so the practically relevant question "is training-time noise better than post-training recovery under the same budget?" is unanswered.
- **The headline table hides the failure regime.** Main-text Table 1 reports INT6 quantization, where SPrPO wins everywhere; the appendix Table 5 shows that at INT4 on ALFWorld, SAM beats SPrPO 59.61% vs 48.05%, and on WebShop SPrPO (8.20%) is not the best method at all. The text honestly notes "all methods eventually collapse," but the strongest claim ("strongest performance… higher retention") is scoped to the non-failure settings.
- **SAM comparison is scale-confounded.** SAM at ρ=0.1 causes training collapse, so it runs at ρ=5×10⁻³; Figure 5a's demonstration that "SAM collapses at the same perturbation magnitude" is close to tautological for adversarial perturbations, and the tuned comparison mixes method with a 20x scale difference.
- **Some gains are within noise.** ALFWorld structured pruning r=0.20: 47.08±7.65 (SPrPO) vs 41.73±5.32 (Vanilla) over 3 seeds — overlapping one-standard-deviation bands; the robustness win there is not statistically solid. Similarly ALFWorld clean: 91.85±0.42 vs Gaussian-baseline 89.05±0.97 is tighter than the narrative suggests.
- **Theory–practice gap.** The guarantees assume exact trust-region analysis with bounded advantages, known variance, and continuous returns; the implementation is PPO-clip + Adam + LoRA, and ρ=0.2/α=1.0 are grid-searched (Tables 8-9), not derived from the bound. No experiment measures whether the Corollary-1 condition is actually near binding during training.
- **Scope restrictions.** A single 1.5B model and two text-only environments; the "no-thinking" mode disables reasoning traces (justified for cost, but it means results may not transfer to reasoning agents, where the authors' own argument — one wrong action derails the trajectory — applies even more strongly mid-chain). Reproducibility is good on paper (all hyperparameters published) but code is unreleased.

## Strengths

- Genuinely new problem framing for the LLM-agent era: deployment-time compression (quantization/pruning) is treated as a property of the learned policy to be fixed during RL training, not as a post-hoc afterthought.
- Coherent theory-to-algorithm pipeline: Corollary 1's variance decomposition directly motivates sensitivity-weighted noise, and the ablations validate it empirically (α=0 is worse than no perturbation for clean accuracy: 48.80% vs 57.00% on WebShop).
- Broad and fair evaluation: 5 perturbation families × multiple magnitudes × 2 environments × ID/OOD splits × 3 seeds, with a shared 128-sequence calibration set across all methods for pruning comparisons — an unusual level of care.
- Consistent, sizeable gains where it matters (retention under Gaussian noise roughly doubles on WebShop: 59.2% vs 33.9%), plus clean-accuracy improvements as a side effect.
- Honest cost accounting (~1.7–1.8x step time), qualitative failure-case analyses (malformed action tags, subgoal-undoing loops, invalid click targets), and complete proofs.

## Limitations

- Scale and diversity: one 1.5B model, two text environments, no tool-use or code agents; robustness at 7B+ or with reasoning traces is untested.
- Fails or ties in the extreme-perturbation regime (INT4), exactly where deployment-time compression pressure is highest.
- ~1.7–1.8x training-time overhead from the sensitivity-estimation replay pass.
- Theoretical guarantees are asymptotic and rely on assumptions (bounded advantage, variance bound, compact policy space) that cannot be checked in real LLM training; hyperparameters are empirical.
- Code not yet released ("upon acceptance"), limiting immediate reproduction.
- Robustness is only evaluated against perturbation types structurally similar to the training noise family (channel-level multiplicative noise); generalization to orthogonal corruptions (activation quantization, attention-head failures) is unmeasured.

## Relevance

该文同时命中你两个关注点："LLM 训练稳定性"（把 TRPO 单调改进扩展为带方差项的稳定条件，并给出训练期噪声调度）和"极限性能优化下的可靠性"（直接以 INT8/INT6/INT4 量化与 SparseGPT 剪枝后的智能体成功率作为评测指标）。如果你关心低精度部署下智能体行为是否可靠、以及训练期扰动注入如何影响损失面平坦度，这篇文章提供了第一个系统性框架和可复用的敏感度加权噪声配方。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does sensitivity-weighted noise training transfer to larger models (7B+) and to reasoning-mode agents, where a single perturbed intermediate thought can compound across a long chain?
2. How does SPrPO compare head-to-head with post-hoc robustness pipelines — quantization-aware fine-tuning (e.g., Wang & Yang 2025), QAT, or SparseGPT-style reconstruction — under an equal compute budget?
3. Why does SPrPO lose to SAM under INT4 on ALFWorld (48.05 vs 59.61)? Is adversarial (worst-case) flatness better aligned with extreme weight-space quantization than channel-noise flatness?
4. Can the stability condition of Corollary 1 be monitored online (estimating ν_ν and m_ν during training) to adapt ρ and α automatically instead of grid search?
5. Do the gains survive perturbation families unseen during training — e.g., activation quantization, KV-cache corruption, or hardware-level faults — or is part of the improvement an overfit to the evaluated channel-noise family?
