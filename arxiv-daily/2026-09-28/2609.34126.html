Why this matters

Self-evolving agents stall when no target reward exists. JET makes the evaluator itself the transferable, evolvable artifact — automated reward design for test-time self-improvement loops.

直击自进化 agent 的关键瓶颈——「评测 / 奖励信号从哪来」：把 judge 表示为可进化、可迁移的代码，是自动化奖励设计的一种迁移方案。

Critical edge

Both benchmarks preserve the evaluator: only tasks and dynamics change. Transfer across changed reward criteria — the harder, more interesting case — is untested. And on true-zero-reward trajectories the judge turns severely optimistic: zero-regime MAE 0.3954 vs 0.1523 fixed-rubric, bias +0.3006 — quantified, never fixed.

01

Methodology

Two stages · weights never move

Stage 1 — Source-supervised judge evolution

On labeled WebShop-clothing trajectories, a GPT‑5.5/Codex proposer rewrites every part of the judge program: prompts, trajectory preprocessing, deterministic computation, calibration, parsing. Parent = lowest training MAE; validation split picks the final judge after 20 rounds. Judge LLM calls: locally served GPT‑OSS‑120B.

→︎

Freeze

Code, parameters, calibration — immutable from here on

→︎

Stage 2 — Reward-hidden target adaptation

On non-clothing WebShop + PushT, evaluator outputs are withheld. Each of 40 rounds: proposer rewrites the best program using only judge scores + textual diagnostics over reward-scrubbed trajectories; candidates run on all 500 tasks; promotion needs a higher judge score. Environment rewards: post-hoc audit only.

JET treats the agent’s harness — the executable program organizing how the LLM acts in a task environment — as the object of search, and solves the missing-reward problem by evolving the evaluator itself rather than the policy weights. The judge is trained to minimize reward-prediction MAE where labels exist, then trusted where they don’t.

Two conditions bracket the claim: cold start (unevolved seed program) and warm start (program pre-optimized on source tasks), plus an optional source-constraint variant that queries 50 labeled source tasks to enforce a performance floor without ever touching target evaluator outputs.

  • PushT coverage rule exactly reconstructible from visible poses → exact-judge control
  • WebShop 1,181,436-product catalog; clothing source → garden / beauty / electronics / grocery target
  • Observation scrubbing strips rewards, coverage, success indicators from everything proposer & judge see
  • Controls self-selection · fixed rubric · + isotonic calibration · deterministic (no-LLM) judge

Attribution — generation, not selection

9.7%

Perfect final selection among fixed-rubric-guided candidates would lift 0.6965 → 0.7046 — closing only ~9.7% of the 0.0832 gap. The advantage lives in better programs generated during search, not better picking: the evolved judge shows zero mean selection regret, while the fixed-rubric judge errs in 17 of 40 rounds per campaign.

03

Strengths & Limitations

From the critical assessment

+ Strengths

  • Clean reward-hidden protocol: observation scrubbing, eligibility checks, selection-regret accounting; environment rewards audited post hoc only.
  • The PushT exact-judge control (MAE 0.0000) is a genuinely clever design — it cleanly attributes gains to program search, not judge error.
  • Attribution analysis goes beyond leaderboard reporting: it explains where the improvement comes from.
  • Counterintuitive cold-beats-warm finding reported and discussed, not hidden.
  • Strong ablation coverage: prompt-only vs executable, deterministic no-LLM judge, isotonic calibration, diagnostics/score removal.
  • Above-average hygiene: 5 campaigns with mean ± SD, disjoint held-out sets, AI-use & reproducibility statements.

− Limitations

  • Only two benchmarks, both evaluator-preserving; changed reward semantics, code, longer-horizon web tasks: untested.
  • Five campaigns, no significance testing; the headline +0.0832 is only ~1.2 pooled SDs — SDs overlap.
  • Documented false optimism on zero-reward trajectories (zero-regime MAE 0.3954 vs 0.1523), downstream consequences unexamined.
  • No compute/latency/cost analysis despite being explicitly a test-time method — every candidate re-executes on all 500 tasks per round.
  • Warm-start adaptation-set exact success slightly worse (30.6% vs 31.2%): mean reward diverges from the success metric.
  • Proprietary GPT‑5.5/Codex proposer; code pledged but absent at publication — replication sensitive to endpoint drift. What to ask next →
04

Questions for Further Exploration

What the paper leaves open
Q1

Does the transferred judge survive a change in the reward criterion itself — a judge built on reward A guiding search for reward B, the demanding case the paper explicitly leaves untested?

Q2

Does the +0.3006 zero-reward bias cause measurable waste (good candidates rejected, bad promoted) in longer campaigns? Would a hybrid judge — fixed rubric for zero-detection, evolved for partial credit — fix it?

Q3

How does JET’s test-time compute (40 rounds × 500 executions) compare per unit of reward gain against simply collecting more source labels and doing warm-start optimization?

Q4

The deterministic judge hits 0.1153 MAE with no LLM call — is the LLM assessment earning its inference cost beyond the diagnostics it emits? Could the judge be distilled into pure code?

Q5

Does cold-start-beats-warm-start persist where source and target behaviors share less structure — or is it an artifact of WebShop’s clothing→non-clothing split?
