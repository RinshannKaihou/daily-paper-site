Why it matters

Quantifies silent data corruption from permanent faults on ReRAM PIM accelerators and yields an actionable vulnerability ranking for fault-tolerant accelerator design.

正中「推理可靠性与 SDC 检测」方向：量化存内计算上的静默数据破坏；对 GPU / 集群故障检测仅具方法论借鉴意义。

Critical edge

“The two halves of ReCoNFI never actually meet.”

Hardware SaH/SaL faults go into random crossbar cells, never localized to a layer — so the headline SaL ≫ SaH damage result can’t be checked against the paper’s own per-layer ranking. And “prioritize SaL” pairs simulated damage with occurrence rates from someone else’s silicon.

01Methodology — two-level injection

ReCoNFI injects permanent faults at two levels: learning parameters (software) and crossbar conductances (hardware). Five CNNs spanning 44K–57M parameters, trained on MNIST / CIFAR10. Metric: Top-1 accuracy; SDC = predicted class differs from the label.

  • SaVparameter stuck at a fixed value (weights, kernels, biases)
  • SaHReRAM cell stuck high-resistance → conductance Gmin
  • SaLcell stuck low-resistance → conductance Gmax
  1. Single faults, per layer1,000 random faults per layer × 10,000 test images; min / avg / max Top-1 reported per layer.
  2. Multi-fault SaV sweepFault rate 0.0001%–50%, stuck value scaled 0.05×–10× of the golden value.
  3. Crossbar SaH / SaLWeights mapped via MemTorch to a dual-crossbar differential layout (signed weights split ±); faults at set cell rates, mixed configs included.

Not all faults hurt. 7%–20.09% of single-fault injections raised accuracy above baseline — trained parameters don’t all sit at optima, so per-parameter hardening is value-dependent, not just location-dependent.

SimpleNet Conv1 fault: 2% improved · 80% degraded · 18% unchanged

Reported honestly by the authors — and never analyzed (which classes flip? do confusions turn benign?). Their own most surprising number, left unexamined.

06Strengths

  • First framework bridging algorithm-level and crossbar-level permanent faults, with a fair comparison against TensorFI / PyTorchFI / SASSIFI / NVBITFI (Table III).
  • Statistically careful protocol: 1,000 faults × 10,000 images per layer; min / avg / max reported — 10M injections, no cherry-picked runs.
  • NN-native SDC definition (wrong class, not wrong bits) plus confusion-matrix evidence.
  • Honest reporting of accuracy improvements under faults (7%–20%).
  • SaL-over-SaH prioritization directly inverts the naive “protect the most frequent fault” rule.

07Limitations

  • Legacy CNNs on MNIST / CIFAR10 only — no ImageNet, no ResNet-50+, no attention models; transfer untested.
  • Hardware phase is simulation-only (MemTorch, behavioral fault models); no fabricated chip.
  • Permanent faults only — resistance drift and read / write disturb, which dominate field reliability, are excluded.
  • Hardware faults not localized per layer, so hardware and software findings can’t be jointly analyzed.
  • No mitigation (ECC, retraining, fault-aware mapping) built or benchmarked; recommendations stay qualitative.

08Questions for further exploration

  1. Do the rankings (BatchNorm ≫ weights ≫ biases; early CONV ≫ FC) transfer to transformers on analog PIM, where LayerNorm, attention projections, and KV caches replace BatchNorm and convolution?
  2. Does expected loss (occurrence × damage) make the ~5×-more-frequent SaH the dominant end-to-end risk anyway?
  3. Could fault-aware training or importance-aware weight-to-crossbar mapping recover ResNet18’s 32.5 points — and at what accuracy / energy cost?
  4. Is the layer-sensitivity ordering stable under transient mechanisms (drift, disturbs) that accumulate over device lifetime?
  5. Would per-layer localized SaH/SaL injection — the missing experiment — confirm or contradict the Phase-1 ranking?
