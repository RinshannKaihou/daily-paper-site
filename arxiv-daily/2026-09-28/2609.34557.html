01

Methodology

distilled · 3 stages
1

Dual-view SkillBank

Each skill is stored as S = (d, g, C): applicability description, actor-facing guidance (3–5 action stages + pitfall warnings), and a hidden evaluator rubric (3–5 state-based, method-agnostic checkpoints) — aligned by construction. Skills are distilled from three frontier teachers' successes and failures, under no-leakage constraints, then clustered into capability-level entries.

2

Turn-level rubric rewards

A coarse selector (GPT-5-mini) matches criteria to turns; a fine verifier (Gemini 3.5 Flash) reads raw screenshots/tool outputs and emits binary satisfaction plus responsibility scores. Progress rides an ordinal evidence ladder; the clipped process advantage is added to the GRPO outcome advantage and shared across each turn's tokens.

missing 0 hypothesis .25 supported .6 verified 1 contradicted 0
3

Gated alternating co-evolution

Every H = 10 steps: guidance patches pass only if frozen-policy paired rollouts improve mean outcome with no regression > 0.05; rubric revisions (delayed H steps) pass only a 1,000-sample bootstrap alignment test (90% LCB ≥ −0.02) without pass-rate inflation (Δp ≤ 0.10) — so behavior and reward changes are never conflated.

Strengths × Limitations

read side by side

What holds upStrengths

  • Dual-view insight: a skill already specifies both how to act and what success looks like — guidance/rubric alignment comes for free, not by post-hoc authoring.
  • Evidence-grounded credit: turn-level rewards judged on screenshots/tool outputs with strict anti-hindsight rules — thoughtful process supervision for long-horizon trajectories.
  • Attribution-clean protocol: paired rollouts isolate guidance effects; delayed offline relabeling isolates reward effects; Δp ≤ 0.10 blocks criteria inflation.
  • Comprehensive evaluation: 8 benchmarks × 2 backbones, 4 adapted baselines, component + interval ablations, cross-family transfer to closed-source models.
  • Exceptional documentation: all prompts, thresholds, and baseline adaptations published; skills distilled from failures too.

Where it's thinLimitations

  • All rewards from proprietary LLM judges — no human audit, no judge-agreement stats; the same judge family gates co-evolution and trains the policy, so a biased evaluator steers both the same wrong way.
  • Skill-specific gain is modest (+2.47 / +1.70); no non-skill process-reward baseline isolates the SkillBank machinery per se.
  • Small training scale (400 seed tasks, 1 epoch); all IID benchmarks are also training sources.
  • Single-rollout pass@1, no error bars; eval sets of 109–311 tasks put several gaps within noise range.
  • Five-model proprietary stack — exact reproduction and cost accounting are impractical; no verification-cost-per-step numbers despite the "cost-aware" claim.
  • "H = 10 is best" is not sharply identified on the 9B backbone; "nearly matches 122B" rests on a 0.02-pt gap under judge-based scoring.
04

Questions for further exploration

How robust is turn-level credit to verifier noise — would one open-source verifier or human-audited spot checks preserve the co-evolution gains?

Can rubric-free process rewards be combined with the SkillBank to separate "explicit rubrics" from "turn-level decomposition"?

Do the acceptance gates eventually freeze the SkillBank over multiple epochs or much larger task pools?

Does the no-regression guidance gate overfit to training tasks — would a held-out validation set change acceptance rates?

How does performance change with global cross-benchmark clustering, or a SkillBank bootstrapped from the student's own rollouts?

Does the dual-view representation transfer to text-only agents, where evidence states must come from textual tool outputs?
