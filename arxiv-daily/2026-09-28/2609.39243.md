> **一句话总结：** 该论文在 GPT-2 small、Pythia-1.4B/6.9B 和 Qwen2.5-7B 中"植入"了对基准提示永远沉默的隐藏通路（928 个配置、140.55 万次干预），构建了首个有真值标注的"错误机制成功"基准；结果显示被广泛使用的位点级流形距离（kNN、local-PCA，AUROC 低至 0.21–0.46，低于随机）根本无法识别错误机制的成功，而作者提出的 Hidden-Pathway Contribution（HPC）——把下游神经元钳制到自然激活范围再测边际损失——在通路单元级可见时于所有模型上达到 AUROC 0.82–1.00，且发现训练于带通路模型的 DAS 有 81–100% 的成功是通路主导的。

> **TL;DR:** Activation patching or DAS succeeding doesn't mean the intervention used the model's natural mechanism — it may have recruited a "hidden pathway" that natural inputs never activate. The authors make this testable by planting silent hidden pathways (ReLU units with biases above their maximum natural activation) into GPT-2 small, Pythia-1.4B/6.9B and Qwen2.5-7B, yielding ground-truth labels over 928 configurations and 1,405,500 interventions on SVA and gender-pronoun tasks. Site-level manifold distances (kNN, local-PCA — the tools of Grant et al.) score AUROC 0.21–0.46, i.e. below chance, at identifying wrong-mechanism successes on the gender task, while their causal Hidden-Pathway Contribution (HPC) detector reaches AUROC 0.82–1.00 whenever the pathway is visible as unit-level out-of-range activity. They also show DAS trained on pathway-containing models routes 81–100% of its gender successes through the planted shortcuts, and that in-range pathway combinations (unnatural only jointly) still defeat all their detectors on Qwen2.5-7B gender (best AUROC 0.48).

## 核心贡献

- **首个"错误机制"真值基准**：向 4 个预训练模型（124M–7B：GPT-2 small、Pythia-1.4B、Pythia-6.9B、Qwen2.5-7B）植入对全部基准提示静默的隐藏通路单元——自然行为完全不变，但哪些干预依赖通路是**精确已知**的。共 928 个配置、1,405,500 次干预，覆盖 SVA（主谓一致）与 Gender（性别代词）两个任务、四种通路家族（silent / hijack / distributed / in-range）。
- **证明位点级距离失效**：在真正要紧的问题上（成功干预中哪些走的是错误机制），kNN 与 local-PCA 在 gender 任务上对所有模型、所有通路家族的 AUROC 均为 0.21–0.46（低于随机）——错误机制的成功反而**更靠近**自然流形；Grant et al. 的 Algorithm 1 在不同模型/任务间波动于 0.23–0.87 且在 in-range 行低于随机。
- **提出 HPC（Hidden-Pathway Contribution）因果检测器**：把下游 MLP 神经元逐层钳制到"产生同样输出的自然运行"的激活范围，测量决策边际损失的份额。在通路以单元级离域形式可见时（silent/hijack/distributed 家族），HPC-L 在所有模型上 AUROC 0.82–1.00；投影变体（HPC-PCA / HPC-Loc）对 in-range 通路部分有效（Pythia-1.4B 上 0.83–0.85）但在 Qwen2.5-7B gender 上失效（0.48）。
- **揭示优化式干预主动寻找隐藏通路**：训练于带通路模型的 DAS，其 gender 成功的 81–100% 由通路主导（四个模型上均如此）；即使把真值因果子空间排除（restricted DAS，"错觉探针"），它仍报告成功，且在 GPT-2 上 100%、Pythia-6.9B 上 92–98% 的这些成功是通路主导的——把 Makelow et al. 的"可解释性错觉"变成了受控测量。
- **缓解与无修改模型检查**：在 GPT-2 上给 DAS 目标加下游在流形惩罚（λ=5），可把无限制 DAS 的通路主导份额从 98.5–100% 压到 4.5–4.7%；在**无修改**模型中，标准干预几乎从依赖离域活动（≤0.8%），但无修改 Qwen2.5-7B 的 restricted DAS 在末 token 位点的成功中有 30–32%（SVA）出现候选错误机制信号。

## Key Contributions

- **A planted-pathway benchmark with exact ground truth.** Hidden pathway units (biases set above their maximum natural activation, so silent on all benchmark prompts) are added to GPT-2 small, Pythia-1.4B, Pythia-6.9B and Qwen2.5-7B. Natural behaviour is unchanged by construction, so every intervention can be labelled *pathway-dominated* (pathways supply >50% of the decision margin) or *wrong-mechanism success* (decision flips when pathways are off). Totals: 928 configurations, 1,405,500 interventions, 24 units per configuration, four pathway families (silent, hijack, distributed, in-range).
- **Site-level distances do not identify wrong-mechanism successes.** Among successful interventions, kNN and local-PCA score AUROC 0.21–0.46 on the gender task for every model and family — wrong-mechanism successes sit *closer* to the natural site manifold than genuine ones — and Grant et al.'s Algorithm 1 is inconsistent across models (0.23–0.87, below chance on in-range rows).
- **Hidden-Pathway Contribution (HPC), a causal downstream detector.** Clamp downstream MLP neurons to the natural regime seen in runs with the same output and measure the fraction of the decision margin lost. HPC-L reaches AUROC 0.82–1.00 for all unit-visible families on every model; projection variants (HPC-PCA, HPC-Loc) partly detect in-range pathways at scale (0.73–0.85) but fail on Qwen2.5-7B gender (0.48), which remains the open problem.
- **Optimised interventions seek hidden pathways.** DAS trained on pathway-bearing models routes 81–100% of gender successes through them on every model; restricted DAS (genuine subspace excluded, an "illusion probe") still succeeds with 76–100% of those successes pathway-dominated, reproducing the Makelow et al. interpretability illusion as a controlled measurement at four scales. A downstream on-manifold penalty (GPT-2 only) cuts unrestricted DAS's pathway-dominated share from 98.5–100% to 4.5–4.7% but does not remove false successes of restricted DAS.
- **A bounded prevalence check on unmodified models.** With a positive control calibrating what the statistic can see (80–94% of unit-visible wrong-mechanism successes, ≤2.6% of in-range ones), standard interventions in unmodified models rely on unit-level out-of-regime activity in at most 0.8% of successes — except restricted DAS in unmodified Qwen2.5-7B, where 30–32% of SVA successes at the final-token site show the candidate signature.

## Methodology

**Setup.** A binary decision is measured by logit difference LD(x) = ℓ_a₁(x) − ℓ_a₀(x) between two fixed answer tokens. An intervention at layer L, position p replaces the residual stream h with ĥ; it is *successful* if sign LD matches the source class. An intervention is *pernicious* (for natural-mechanism claims) if its decision depends on downstream computation that does not occur in natural runs producing the same output — following Grant et al.'s notion of harm, but specialised to the most common claim in interpretability.

**Tasks and models.** Two paired-prompt tasks: SVA ("The keys near the cabinet → are/is") and Gender pronouns ("The king from Paris said that → he/she"); IOI is used only in the unmodified-model sweep. Models: GPT-2 small (12L), Pythia-1.4B (24L), Pythia-6.9B (32L), Qwen2.5-7B (28L), via TransformerLens at fixed revisions; sites are the subject token at three depths plus the final token; larger models use seeds 0–2.

**Planted pathways.** Each configuration plants 24 ReLU units a(x) = ReLU(u^⊤x̃_T(x) − b) at a downstream layer T, with b set above the maximum natural activation plus a margin (η ~ U(0.05, 0.8) natural std), so a ≡ 0 on every benchmark prompt. Families: **silent** (new units writing along the answer unembedding difference or a random unembedding), **hijack** (drive into one existing high-effect MLP neuron), **distributed** (same drive over 64 neurons), **in-range** (drive over 128 neurons each clipped to its natural 0.1–99.9% quantile range with 10× gain — only the *combination* is unnatural). Half the units are confirmatory. Unit gains are calibrated so an overshoot of one natural std moves LD by ~3. Labels: *pathway-dominated* (sign(LD)(LD − LD_off) > |LD|/2) and *wrong-mechanism success* (successful AND decision flips with pathways off).

**Interventions.** 1,500 per configuration on held-out prompt pairs: full activation patching (A); mean-difference patching with α ~ U(0.1, 5) plus a steering group α ~ U(0, 3) (C); DAS trained on the unmodified model with interchange scaled U(0.2, 4) (D); DAS mixed with random directions up to 85° (E); DAS trained on the pathway model (G); and restricted DAS (H), constrained to the orthogonal complement of the top-32 site principal components and the D direction — an *illusion probe* where any success must come from outside the genuine subspace.

**Detectors.** Site-level baselines: kNN distance, Mahalanobis (200-d PCA), local-PCA residual (LPCA), and Algorithm 1 of Grant et al. (off-manifold component re-injected into 32 natural prompts). Down-OM: non-causal downstream residual from class-conditional PCA. The proposed **HPC**: for each downstream MLP neuron, record its natural regime [q₀.₀₀, q₀.₉₉] per class; rerun the intervention with every downstream neuron clamped layer-by-layer to the predicted class's regime; HPC = max(0, sign(LD)(LD − LD_Π)) / median|LD| — the fraction of a typical natural margin lost. Variants: HPC-S (clamp only rarely-active neurons), HPC-L (clamp all), HPC-PCA and HPC-Loc (project each layer onto class-conditional global/local PCA before clamping).

**Infrastructure.** Runs on an RTX 5060 Ti, an Apple M3 Pro, an NVIDIA H20 (96GB) and a Huawei Ascend 910B2; every table is regenerated from saved per-intervention arrays by a single script that records parameters, model revisions, software versions and file hashes.

## Results & Highlights

- **Site-level distances fail exactly where it matters.** Among *successful* interventions on the gender task, kNN and LPCA score AUROC 0.21–0.46 — below the 0.5 chance line — for every model and every pathway family (e.g. GPT-2/gender/silent: kNN 0.38 vs HPC-L 0.83; Qwen2.5-7B/gender/silent: kNN 0.21 vs HPC-L 0.90). Wrong-mechanism successes sit closer to the natural manifold than genuine ones, because confirmatory pathways let small, typical-looking perturbations tip the decision. HPC-L stays at 0.82–1.00 for the three unit-visible families on every model.
- **Synthetic sanity check.** On a two-layer MLP (5 seeds × 3,000 interventions each), HPC achieves AUROC 0.931±0.007 over all interventions and 0.977±0.005 over successful ones, versus 0.837±0.016 / 0.868±0.012 for the best prior detector (Algorithm 1 of Grant et al.), while site distances flag harmless null-space divergence almost as readily as pernicious divergence — 93.2–99.6% false-positive rate at 90% pernicious recall, versus 0.0% for HPC.
- **Optimised interventions exploit planted shortcuts at every scale.** DAS trained on pathway-bearing models (G) routes 81–100% of its gender successes through the planted pathways (90–95% GPT-2, 81–91% Pythia-1.4B, 86–97% Pythia-6.9B, 95–100% Qwen2.5-7B). Even clean-model DAS applied with overshoot produces 39–63% pathway-dominated gender successes in the Pythia models.
- **The illusion probe confirms false successes.** With the top-32 genuine components excluded, restricted DAS still succeeds on gender (12–26% GPT-2, 16–20% Pythia-1.4B, 14–18% Pythia-6.9B, 8–10% Qwen2.5-7B), and 100% (GPT-2), 76–89% (Pythia-1.4B), 92–98% (Pythia-6.9B) and 100% (Qwen2.5-7B) of those successes are pathway-dominated — high interchange-intervention accuracy is no evidence about mechanism.
- **Mitigation works partially, on GPT-2 only.** A downstream on-manifold penalty (λ=5) added to the DAS objective cuts the pathway-dominated share of unrestricted DAS from 98.5–100% to 4.5–4.7% on gender (success 94–98% → 87–89%), but for restricted DAS the remaining successes are still 92–100% pathway-dominated (e.g. hijack: 66.0% → 17.0% success, 100% pathway-dominated).
- **In-range pathways defeat range clamping.** HPC-L drops to 0.59–0.70 on in-range pathways for the Pythia models and 0.39 on Qwen2.5-7B gender; the best projection variant (HPC-Loc) reaches only 0.83–0.85 on Pythia-1.4B, 0.73–0.75 on Pythia-6.9B, 0.85 on Qwen2.5-7B SVA — but 0.48 on Qwen2.5-7B gender and 0.57 on GPT-2 gender.
- **Unmodified models are mostly clean, with one candidate exception.** Standard interventions show HPC-L > 1 in ≤0.8% of successes (exactly 0% in Qwen2.5-7B); restricted DAS in Pythia-6.9B (k=1) succeeds on 24–33% of examples (up to 57%), yet 0 of 2,232 successes show the signature. In unmodified Qwen2.5-7B, however, 30–32% of restricted-DAS SVA successes (49/154 and 47/155) and 12–14% of gender successes (10/71 and 8/69) at the final-token site have HPC-L > 1 (up to ~6 natural margins) — a candidate wrong-mechanism signature, hedged because clamping cannot confirm ground truth.

## Authors & Credibility

- **Beiming Liu** (China Financial Certification Authority, CFCA) and **Minjie Chen** (PetroChina Southwest Oil and Gasfield Company), listed as equal-contribution co-first authors. Both affiliations are outside the academic mechanistic-interpretability circuit (no university lab or AI-industry research group), which is unusual for this line of work and means the paper should be judged on its own experimental evidence rather than on a lab pedigree — on that evidence, it holds up well.
- Credibility signals are strong: the paper engages directly and accurately with the relevant literature (Grant et al. on divergence, Makelow et al.'s interpretability illusion, Wu et al.'s reply, causal scrubbing, optimal ablations, the hydra effect), builds directly on standard tooling (TransformerLens, Pythia, the SVA/gender tasks from Finlayson et al. and Vig et al.), and this v2 substantially expands v1 (which covered GPT-2 only) with three larger models, three seeds, a k-sweep and a positive control — the authors kept v1's GPT-2 results unchanged rather than quietly revising them.
- Reproducibility discipline is above average for the field: every table is regenerated from saved per-intervention arrays by one script, with parameters, model revisions, software versions and file hashes recorded; runs across heterogeneous hardware (NVIDIA, Apple, Huawei Ascend) are reported rather than hidden, including an honest cross-GPU non-reproducibility check. No public code/data release URL is stated, however.

## Critical Assessment

- **Three of the four pathway families are, by the authors' own admission, sanity checks rather than the real test.** Silent, hijack and distributed pathways are by construction visible as unit-level out-of-range activity — exactly what HPC clamps. The informative case is the **in-range** family (unnatural only as a combination), and there the headline detector fails: HPC-L scores 0.39 AUROC on Qwen2.5-7B gender, and even the best projection variant reaches only 0.48 there. The honest headline is therefore narrower than "we can detect wrong-mechanism successes": detection works when the pathway is unit-visible, which is the easier case.
- **The benchmark is two small synthetic grammar tasks with fixed answer tokens.** SVA and gender pronouns are canonical but tiny (47 and 20 noun pairs respectively), template-based, and binary; IOI appears only in the unmodified sweep. Whether wrong-mechanism reliance matters for realistic interpretability workflows on richer tasks (multi-token answers, chain-of-thought) is untested, and pathway-mediated attention is explicitly out of scope since HPC clamps only MLP neurons at the final position.
- **Statistical hygiene is weaker than the engineering discipline.** GPT-2 main configurations use a single seed; the larger models use three, and the authors concede that configuration means share prompts and baselines and are "not independent replicates." Several constants (the 10× in-range gain, the 0.1–99.9% quantiles, the HPC-L > 1 threshold, the calibration gain of 3) are fixed rather than tuned, so sensitivity to them is unquantified. The cross-hardware check is commendably honest but shows AUROCs shifting by up to 0.21 among successful interventions, which means small per-row differences in Table 2 should not be over-interpreted — the paper itself does not always resist reading them.
- **The natural-regime reference is the same templated prompt family being intervened on.** The authors note a held-out natural distribution would be a stricter reference; as is, "natural regime" could be slightly too generous or too tight in ways that vary per task.
- **The mitigation is evaluated only on GPT-2 at one layer/seed setting,** so the most actionable practical takeaway (add a on-manifold penalty to DAS) has no evidence at 1.4B–7B scale, and it provably does not fix restricted-DAS false successes even where tested.
- **The Qwen2.5-7B candidate finding is appropriately hedged but unverifiable by design** — without planted pathways there is no ground truth, and downstream self-repair (the hydra effect) can make clamping over-estimate dependence. Calling these "candidates a researcher would want to inspect" rather than detections is the right call, but it also means the paper's real-world yield is one lead, not one confirmed case.
- **Minor red flag:** no public code or benchmark release is mentioned anywhere despite the extensive reproducibility apparatus, which limits the community benefit the benchmark could have.

## Strengths

- The planted-pathway construction is elegant: a minimal edit to pretrained models that leaves natural behaviour untouched (verified per configuration) yet yields *exact* dependence labels — solving the ground-truth problem that Grant et al. left open.
- Impressive scale and coverage for a two-author paper: 928 configurations, 1.4M interventions, four models spanning 124M–7B, four pathway families, seven intervention types, multi-hardware execution.
- The negative result on site-level distances (AUROC below chance for prior-work detectors on the case that matters) is a genuine service to the field — it deflates a natural and currently practiced quality check.
- The restricted-DAS "illusion probe" turns Makelow et al.'s illusion anecdote into a controlled, repeatable measurement across four scales.
- The positive-control table (Table 7) that bounds what the prevalence check can and cannot see (80–94% vs ≤2.6%) is exemplary calibration of claims, rare in interpretability papers.
- Honest, detailed limitations section and an explicit claim-dependence discussion (HPC validates natural-mechanism claims only).

## Limitations

- Detection is solid only for unit-visible pathways; in-range (combination-level) pathways remain essentially undetected on Qwen2.5-7B gender (best AUROC 0.48) — the open problem the paper hands to future work.
- HPC requires a class-conditional binary decision and clamps MLP neurons at the final position only; attention-mediated pathways, other positions, and non-binary tasks are not covered.
- Largest model is 7B, downstream detectors scan every second MLP layer in bfloat16, and the mitigation exists only at GPT-2 scale.
- GPT-2 main runs are single-seed; configuration means share prompts/baselines; key constants are fixed, not tuned; cross-GPU AUROC shifts of up to 0.21 limit fine-grained comparisons.
- Natural regimes are estimated from the same templated prompts that are intervened on; downstream self-repair can bias LD_Π.
- Only two (effectively binary) tasks; no code release stated.

## Relevance

这篇论文与你的 "LLM Hidden States & Interpretability" 方向高度相关：它直接针对机制可解释性最核心的实验原语（activation patching / DAS）给出了"结论是否真的来自模型自然机制"的可检验基准与检测器，并实证否定了位点级流形距离这一常用检查手段。对任何用干预实验做 circuit 分析的工作，这都是一个必须考虑的效度威胁清单和现成的检测工具（HPC）。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can in-range (combination-level) pathways be detected reliably — e.g. with joint-density models over downstream activations, diffusion-based manifold models like Luo et al., or attention-head-aware clamping — especially on Qwen2.5-7B gender where every tested detector fails?
- Does the on-manifold penalty on DAS remain effective (and not merely reduce raw success) at 1.4B–7B scale, and does it transfer to restricted DAS?
- Do wrong-mechanism successes occur in realistic interpretability settings: multi-token answers, free-form generation, agentic or chain-of-thought pipelines where the "decision" is not a two-token logit difference?
- Are the candidate wrong-mechanism successes in unmodified Qwen2.5-7B (30–32% of restricted-DAS SVA successes at the final-token site) real shortcuts, or artifacts of downstream self-repair inflating LD_Π? A targeted causal-scrubbing or attention-pathway analysis could adjudicate.
- How do the fixed constants (pathway gain, regime quantiles, HPC-L > 1 threshold, k=32 exclusion) affect detector rankings — would held-out natural prompt distributions or per-model tuned thresholds change the conclusion that site-level distances are below chance?
