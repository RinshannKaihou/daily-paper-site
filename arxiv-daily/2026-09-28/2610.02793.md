> **一句话总结：** 提出 PAPER2LLM++：把报道 LLM 缺陷的研究论文流转化为持续自我进化的监督信号——先用 gpt-oss-120b 从论文中抽取带原文证据的 (WHAT/TRIGGER/WHY/MITIGATION/EVALUATION) 结构化记录并验证该缺陷在当前模型上仍存在（bootstrap 95% CI 全在零上方才更新），再让 Qwen2.5-32B 仅凭该记录合成直接/对比/鲁棒/过程四种候选监督数据，最后用 try–evaluate–commit 流程（一次性临时 LoRA 探针按增益 G−λP−μQ 择优后才全量重训）提交更新；在 30 篇论文、7 个失败家族上，混合论文流中对 Llama-3.1-8B 和 Qwen2.5-7B 分别取得 +7.1/+7.5 点目标增益，同时保留 83.7%/85.0% 的已习得增益，泛能力仅下降 0.4/0.5 点，显著优于 RLEdit（增益相近 +7.9/+6.7 但保留率仅 65.2%/71.1%、泛能力掉 1.6/1.4 点）。

> **TL;DR:** PAPER2LLM++ treats the stream of research papers that *report LLM failures* as supervision for continual self-evolution instead of as mere knowledge to retrieve. For each paper it (1) extracts an evidence-grounded record via gpt-oss-120b and verifies the reported failure still exists on the current model using the paper's own evaluation protocol with a bootstrap 95% CI gate (no update if the CI isn't fully above zero), (2) has Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct synthesize up to four supervision forms (direct/contrastive/robustness/process) from the record alone — never from benchmark examples, with MD5/RapidFuzz≥97/MPNet≥0.93 contamination checks, and (3) commits an update only if a try–evaluate–commit probe shows target gain without hurting earlier gains or 5-benchmark general ability (HellaSwag/WinoGrande/PIQA/BoolQ/ARC-Challenge). On their 30-paper, 7-failure-family suite, PAPER2LLM++ averages +8.6 points held-out across papers (24/30 positive; e.g., Prompt Injection +21.3, Reversal Curse +24.8), and in the mixed 30-paper stream on Llama-3.1-8B it beats RLEdit on the gain–retention balance: +7.1 gain with 83.7% retention and −0.4 general-capability change vs RLEdit's +7.9 gain with only 65.2% retention and −1.6 general capability.

## 核心贡献

- **首个"论文驱动 LLM 自进化"问题形式化**：把科学文献（报道模型缺陷的论文）当作持续学习的监督来源，区别于从数据/任务/编辑/交互反馈中学习的既有持续学习范式；论文不提供训练样本，只提供关于模型行为的"证据"，框架需自行判断缺陷是否仍在当前模型上存在、能否转化为可执行监督、以及如何不遗忘地吸收。
- **PAPER2LLM++ 框架**：闭环"发现→改进"流水线——证据抽取（gpt-oss-120b 抽取 WHAT/TRIGGER/WHY/MITIGATION/EVALUATION 五元组且每个字段必须附论文原文 span）、失败验证（复现论文原评估协议，用原始实例 vs 触发实例的配对分数差 Δ 加 bootstrap 95% CI 判定，CI 不完全大于零则 NO UPDATE）、监督合成（Qwen2.5-32B 只看结构化记录、不看任何基准样例，生成 direct/contrastive/robustness/process 四种候选）、try–evaluate–commit 受控更新（临时 LoRA 探针按效用 U=G−λP−μQ 与三重阈值 τ_gain/τ_prev/τ_gen 择优，均不达标则保持模型不变）。
- **防遗忘机制**：600 容量 replay buffer（60% 历史监督 + 20% 同域干净样本 + 20% 通用样本）+ 最多 50 条冻结"保护探针"（留出集分层采样，仅用于检测旧能力遗忘，绝不参与训练）。
- **30 篇论文 × 7 个失败家族的评测套件**与三项关键结论：科学发现要成为有效学习信号必须同时满足 **teachable**（能转成具体可验证监督）、**needed**（当前模型仍存在该缺陷）、**retained**（改进能在后续更新中存活）；监督闭合度（supervision closure）与增益相关性 r=0.87；边缘增益从早期约 +12 点衰减到第 30 篇附近约 +5 点但始终为正。
- 消融（附录 Tables 8–12）显示：去掉内部择优、用回归式选择、以及不做持续学习设定都会明显变差（如 mixed 流上去掉择优后增益从 +7.1 降到约 +5.3 且保留率大跌），说明"逐论文决定学什么、怎么学"是收益来源而非单纯训练量。

## Key Contributions

- **First formulation of paper-driven LLM self-evolution**: scientific papers reporting model failures become a supervision stream, requiring the framework to decide *what* is worth learning, *how* to learn it, and *whether* it remains learned — an upstream challenge absent from data/task/edit-based continual learning.
- **The PAPER2LLM++ pipeline** closing the discovery-to-improvement loop: evidence-grounded extraction → failure verification against the current model (paired Δ_t scores + bootstrap 95% CI, hard NO-UPDATE gate) → synthesis of four candidate supervision forms from the record alone (generator never sees benchmark examples) → try–evaluate–commit adaptation where a candidate is committed only if it improves the new behavior while staying under fixed thresholds on previous-behavior degradation (P_t ≤ τ_prev) and general-capability degradation (Q_t ≤ τ_gen).
- **A 30-paper / 7-failure-family evaluation suite** (mathematical robustness, coding/software, instruction operators, knowledge & factuality, context grounding, structured reasoning, agentic reliability) with paired original/perturbed benchmarks (GSM-Symbolic, GSM-Plus, Prompt Injection, Reversal Curse, Lost-in-the-Middle, PlanBench, AgenBench, etc.), and unified paper-to-update protocol baselines (Text-to-LoRA, ELDER, RLEdit, UltraEdit, StableEdit, Seq-LoRA).
- **Analysis identifying teachability / need / retention as the conditions** governing when findings drive effective self-evolution: constraints, decision boundaries, reasoning patterns and verification strategies teach well (instruction-operator family +25.9 points); planning/retrieval/interaction-heavy findings do not (structured reasoning is net negative). Supervision closure correlates with held-out gain at r = 0.87, and marginal gains decay from ~12 to ~5 points over the 30-paper stream while remaining positive.

## Methodology

**Problem.** A model M_{t-1} receives a stream of papers P_t, each reporting an LLM failure with its own evaluation protocol. The paper is evidence, not an executable update: the reported failure may be absent in M_{t-1}, and updating may erase prior gains or general capabilities. Three challenges: C1 (is the failure relevant to the current model?), C2 (how to turn a failure-reporting paper into executable supervision?), C3 (how to preserve prior and general capabilities?).

**Step 1 — Evidence extraction & verification.** gpt-oss-120b (temp 0, top-p 1.0) parses each paper into R_t = (WHAT, TRIGGER, WHY, MITIGATION, EVALUATION), storing verbatim text spans (N/A rather than inferred when absent). Each paper's benchmark is split once into 30% detection / 70% held-out (stratified, seed 42, fixed across backbones/methods/orders). The detection split reproduces the paper's comparison: Δ_t = Score(M_{t-1}, x_t) − Score(M_{t-1}, x̃_t) over matched (original, failure-triggered) pairs, bootstrapped with B = 2000 units; the failure is confirmed only if the 95% CI lies entirely above zero — otherwise NO UPDATE. The held-out split is inaccessible until the update decision is final, and the generator only ever sees R_t, never raw questions, model responses, or gold answers.

**Step 2 — Candidate supervision generation.** Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct (temp 0.7, top-p 0.95; 512 standard / 320 compute-adjusted target, 1.5× over-generated then filtered: candidates with <128 valid examples are invalidated) generates up to four supervision forms from R_t alone: *direct* (teaches the desired behavior), *contrastive* (minimal pairs isolating the trigger's effect), *robustness* (paraphrases that must not change the answer), *process* (intermediate reasoning applying the paper's insight) — the last only when the paper identifies a concrete reasoning/verification error. Contamination controls: exact MD5 match and RapidFuzz string ratio ≥ 97 against benchmark partitions reject content; within-dataset dedup by exact hash then MPNet cosine ≥ 0.93.

**Step 3 — Try–evaluate–commit.** Each candidate gets a disposable temporary LoRA (rank/alpha 4/8, lr 1e-4, 60 steps, 128 new + 64 replay examples) from the same checkpoint M_{t-1}. Candidates score utility U = G − λP − μQ, where G = new-behavior gain, P = loss on behaviors acquired from earlier papers, Q = loss on general LLM ability (fixed 500-example subsets of HellaSwag, WinoGrande, PIQA, BoolQ, ARC-Challenge; macro-averaged). Hard gates G ≥ τ_gain, P ≤ τ_prev, Q ≤ τ_gen (globally fixed hyperparameters shared across papers/checkpoints). The best passing candidate is retrained from M_{t-1} with the full budget (LoRA 16/32, 1500 steps); if none passes, the model is left unchanged. After a commit, a replay buffer (capacity 600: 60% prior paper supervision, 20% matched clean-domain, 20% general) plus up to 50 frozen protected probe examples from D_dev are updated.

**Evaluation setup.** Two base LLMs (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct); individual-paper learning (30 independent runs) and continual learning (within-family and mixed 30-paper streams, 3 order seeds × 3 training seeds). Metrics: target gain G_base vs G_seq, retention R = Σ (S(M_T) − S(M_{t-1})) / g_t over positive-gain steps, forgetting (negative of final-vs-post-update loss), and general-capability change Δ_gen. Baselines adapted to the same paper stream: Text-to-LoRA, ELDER, RLEdit, UltraEdit, StableEdit (plus Seq-LoRA in the appendix). Analysis annotations (knowledge type, supervision closure with five components — target, constructible supervision, automatic verification, local evaluability, mechanism sufficiency; semantic & behavioral novelty) are created before inspecting held-out outcomes and never used as update features.

## Results & Highlights

- **Individual-paper self-evolution (Fig. 2, Table 6):** 24 of 30 papers yield positive held-out gains; the mean across all 30 is **+8.6 points** with a mean general-capability change of −0.3 points on negative outcomes. Largest per-paper gains: Reversal Curse **+24.8**, Prompt Injection **+21.3**, GSM-Symbolic **+12.4**, GSM-Plus **+10.8**; six papers are negative, worst being ToolBench **−3.4**, CounterBench **−3.4**, SWE-bench **−1.4**. Family-level (Table 12): instruction operators 48.6 → 74.5 (**+25.9**), mathematical robustness 51.8 → 62.4 (**+10.6**), context grounding 57.9 → 68.3 (+10.4), but structured reasoning goes **−0.9** and coding only +2.8.
- **Continual mixed 30-paper stream (Table 2):** on Llama-3.1-8B, PAPER2LLM++ achieves **+7.1 gain / 83.7% retention / 2.4 forgetting / −0.4 Gen Δ** vs RLEdit's **+7.9 gain but 65.2% retention / 4.9 forgetting / −1.6 Gen Δ** — nearly the same gain magnitude with dramatically better stability. On Qwen2.5-7B: PAPER2LLM++ **+7.5 / 85.0% / 1.5 / −0.5** vs RLEdit +6.7 / 71.1% / 4.5 / −1.4. UltraEdit, StableEdit, Text-to-LoRA and ELDER all land lower on the joint criterion (e.g., Text-to-LoRA on Llama: +5.1 gain, 62.6% retention, −2.0 Gen Δ; StableEdit: +5.7, 78.1%, −0.9).
- **Same-family streams (Table 2 left):** Llama — PAPER2LLM++ +6.8 gain / **86.4% retention** / 1.8 forgetting vs RLEdit +7.4 / 73.5% / 3.1 and Text-to-LoRA +5.8 / 64.9% / 4.2; Qwen — PAPER2LLM++ +7.2 / 87.8% / 1.5 vs RLEdit +7.6 / 76.8% / 2.9. Moving to mixed streams inflates baselines' forgetting (RLEdit 3.1→4.9 on Llama, 2.9→4.5 on Qwen; Text-to-LoRA 4.2→5.6 and 3.6→5.3) while PAPER2LLM++ grows only to 2.4 and 2.1.
- **When does it work (Figs. 3–5)?** Findings about constraints, decision boundaries, reasoning patterns and verification strategies produce the largest gains, while planning/retrieval/interaction-heavy findings produce near-zero or negative gains. Supervision closure correlates with held-out gain at **Pearson r = 0.87** (descriptive only, as the authors note). Marginal gain decays from ~12 points early in the stream to ~5 points by paper 30 but stays positive; the biggest wins come from papers that are both semantically and behaviorally novel to the current checkpoint.

## Critical Assessment

- **Genuine strengths.** The NO-UPDATE gate (bootstrap 95% CI on the paired Δ before anything is trained) and the try–evaluate–commit probe are the paper's best ideas: they convert "does this paper matter to *this* model?" into a measurable decision, which most continual-learning work simply assumes away. The ablations (Appendix Tables 8–12) show internal candidate selection matters — replacing it with regression-based selection or removing continual replay visibly degrades retention, so the design choices are load-bearing, not decorative.
- **Suite circularity is the biggest red flag.** All 30 papers were curated by the authors precisely because they have paired original/perturbed benchmarks — which is exactly the property that makes a failure verifiable (Δ computable) and teachable (contrastive/robustness data constructible). The instruction-operator family's +25.9 headline is dominated by two papers (Prompt Injection +21.3, Reversal Curse +24.8) whose failures are trivially expressible as input–output supervision. Papers without a clean paired benchmark — where the framework's verification step cannot even run — are absent by construction, so the +8.6 average is a property of the selection filter as much as of the method.
- **Compute is not matched, and the authors say so.** PAPER2LLM++ spends up to 4×60 temporary-LoRA probes plus one 1500-step full update per paper, plus gpt-oss-120b parsing and Qwen2.5-32B generation per paper; UltraEdit/StableEdit perform zero gradient steps (closed-form) and Text-to-LoRA zero gradient steps (hypernetwork). The gain–retention advantage is real but partly bought with more per-paper optimization; a compute-normalized comparison is missing.
- **r = 0.87 is correlational and rubric-validated only against itself.** The supervision-closure score is annotated by gpt-oss-120b, and the authors correctly disclaim that it "does not validate the scoring rubric or establish a causal effect." It should be read as a descriptive observation, not a predictive tool.
- **General capability is still net negative** (−0.4/−0.5 on the mixed stream): "better preserved" is relative to baselines that lose 1–2 points, not evidence of zero collateral harm. And 6/30 papers produce negative outcomes, so roughly one paper in five actively hurts the model — the commitment gate reduces but does not eliminate harm.
- **Scale and generality.** Only 7–8B open models are tested; nothing shows the pipeline transfers to larger or frontier models, whose reported-failure literature (and already-fixed fraction) differs substantially. The marginal-gain decay analysis (12→5 points over 30 papers) hints the approach saturates once the easy, teachable failures are consumed.

## Strengths

- Novel and well-motivated problem: papers as a *continual supervision stream* is a genuinely new setting, cleanly separated from knowledge-editing benchmarks (ScienceMeter, DocMEdit, WikiBigEdit) and from RL/parameter-editing continual learners.
- The verification-first design (CI-gated NO-UPDATE) means the system provably does no harm on papers whose failures no longer exist — the right inductive bias for long streams.
- Data hygiene is above the bar: generator isolation from benchmark content, MD5/RapidFuzz/MPNet triple contamination checks, held-out inaccessibility until the update decision is final, pre-registered analysis annotations.
- Honest reporting throughout: per-paper gains are shown including the six negatives, compute accounting Table 4 explicitly discloses the compute mismatch, and the r = 0.87 claim is self-limited as descriptive.
- Practical takeaways for the field: the teachable/needed/retained decomposition and the knowledge-type analysis (constraints/verification learnable; planning/retrieval not) tell practitioners *which* papers to feed a self-improving system.

## Limitations

- The 30-paper suite is author-curated around paired benchmarks, favoring exactly the failure types the method handles best; structured reasoning (−0.9) and coding (+2.8) show the method already struggles when findings require planning, execution, or complex interaction.
- Requires each paper to ship a usable evaluation protocol (dataset + comparator) — the many important failure papers without one are out of scope, untested.
- Compute and inference cost (two frontier-scale auxiliary LLMs per paper + multiple probe trainings) is disclosed but not normalized away; closed-form editing baselines are disadvantaged in wall-clock and API terms.
- Only Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-7B backbones; no scaling study, and general-capability audit is limited to five fixed multiple-choice/commonsense sets (2500 examples) — no coding/agentic general benchmarks.
- Data and code not yet released ("upon publication"), so contamination controls and the split discipline cannot be externally audited yet.
- Long-horizon behavior beyond 30 papers is unknown; marginal gains are declining and the retention machinery (replay buffer capped at 600 examples) is untested at real literature scale (thousands of relevant papers per year).

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the failure-verification and candidate-selection steps be replaced by, or integrated with, an agent's own environment feedback (interaction/execution traces) so that papers *without* paired benchmarks — e.g., agentic-reliability findings — become learnable?
- Does the gain–retention advantage over RLEdit/UltraEdit persist under compute-normalized budgets (equal gradient steps per paper), and at larger backbone scale (13B–70B) or on models whose training data already covers these papers?
- What happens over realistic multi-year paper streams (hundreds to thousands of papers)? The replay buffer (600 examples) and the declining marginal gain (12→5 points over 30 papers) both suggest saturation mechanics worth modeling explicitly.
- The r = 0.87 closure–gain correlation is descriptive: can supervision closure be made *causal* by intervening on it (e.g., artificially adding an external verifier for low-closure papers) and measuring whether gains appear?
- Six of thirty papers still hurt the model despite the gates. Is the τ_gain/τ_prev/τ_gen threshold set on held-out signal, and could per-family thresholds or a learned acceptance policy push the negative-outcome rate toward zero?
