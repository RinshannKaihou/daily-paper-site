> **一句话总结：** 提出 "harness learning"：用 SFT+GRPO 训练一个 4B proposer，使其能依据执行反馈反复改写冻结 solver 的可执行 harness 代码（把 harness 修订类比为元学习中的权重更新）；在 21 个未见过的 Reasoning Gym 任务族上，单次修订平均分从 Base 的 0.32 提升到 0.62，且平均修订质量超过其 35B 教师（0.56）；在 QA 上，仅用 HotpotQA 训练的 proposer 能在未见过的 MuSiQue/2Wiki 上通过 10 轮顺序修订把 EM 从约 0.15 提升到 0.27，超过 80 个独立修订的 oracle 最优值。

> **TL;DR:** An LLM agent = model + harness (the executable program organizing calls, tools, and information flow). This paper trains a "proposer" (Qwen3-4B) with SFT on 35B-teacher edits plus GRPO — reward = revised-harness task score + an executability bonus — to revise the harness of a frozen solver from execution feedback alone, framing harness revision as meta-learning where edits play the role of gradient-based weight updates. On 21 unseen Reasoning Gym task families, mean single-revision score rises from 0.32 (untrained Base proposer) to 0.62 after RL (+0.30), beating its own 35B teacher's average of 0.56 (though the teacher wins under oracle best-of-eight selection). On multi-hop QA trained only on HotpotQA, sequential revision lifts MuSiQue (OOD) exact match from 0.15 (independent revisions, marginally above seed) to 0.27 over ten rounds, and the final harness exceeds the oracle best-of-80 independent proposals on both unseen benchmarks. Notably, Multistep RL (training on revision sequences) does NOT consistently beat Single-step RL — iterative test-time improvement emerges from policies trained on single revisions.

## 核心贡献

- **提出 harness learning 框架**：将"修订 harness"形式化为对可执行程序的元学习（meta-learning over executable programs）——外环用 RL 训练 proposer，内环 proposer 在新任务上依据执行反馈改写 harness，测试时模型参数完全冻结，改写扮演梯度适应中权重更新的角色。
- **可泛化的测试时适应**：在 Reasoning Gym 的 21 个未见任务族上，SFT+RL 使单次修订平均分从 0.32 → 0.62；4B proposer 的平均修订质量超过 35B 教师（0.62 vs 0.56），表明"从执行结果中学习"可以超越模仿学习。
- **迭代式测试时提升**：仅用单次修订训练的策略（Single-step RL）也能在多轮修订中持续改进 harness——QA 上 MuSiQue 从 0.15（独立修订）升到 0.27（10 轮顺序修订），且最终结果超过 80 个独立修订的 oracle；即修订技能对"自己改过的 harness"这一分布外输入依然有效。
- **诚实的消融结论**：在修订序列上训练（Multistep RL，类比多步梯度适应）并不能稳定优于单步训练——Reasoning Gym 上 canonical 问题更好、format-varied 更差，QA 上两者几乎相同；RL 主要通过提升 proposal 可靠性（减少无法运行/零分 proposal）和改变 harness 结构（生成 interpreter 循环、保留多跳检索）起作用。

## Key Contributions

- **Harness learning as meta-learning**: formalizes harness revision as meta-learning over executable programs — an outer loop trains a proposer with RL using the task performance of revised harnesses as reward, while the inner loop uses execution feedback to adapt harnesses on new tasks with zero parameter updates; edits play the role of weight updates in gradient-based adaptation.
- **Generalization to unseen tasks**: on 21 unseen Reasoning Gym families, SFT+RL lifts mean single-revision score from 0.32 (Base) to 0.62; the trained 4B proposer beats its 35B teacher on average proposal quality (0.62 vs 0.56), showing learning from execution outcomes can surpass imitation.
- **Iterative test-time adaptation**: policies trained only on individual revisions keep improving harnesses over successive rounds — MuSiQue EM goes from 0.15 (independent revisions) to 0.27 over ten rounds, exceeding the oracle best of 80 independent proposals — meaning learned revision skills remain useful even as the policy's own edits push harnesses off its training distribution.
- **An honest negative result**: Multistep RL (training on successive revision states, the natural analogue of multi-step gradient adaptation) does not consistently outperform Single-step RL (better on canonical, worse on format-varied Reasoning Gym questions; near-identical finals in QA), and analysis shows RL works partly by improving proposal reliability and by restructuring harnesses (interpreter loops, preserved multi-hop retrieval).

## Methodology

**Setup.** An agent is decomposed into a frozen *solver* (the LLM) and a *harness* (the executable program that constructs prompts, schedules solver calls, executes tools, and validates answers). A *proposer* (Qwen3-4B) is trained to revise harness code. Both are frozen at test time; only the harness text changes.

**Adaptation loop (inner loop).** Starting from a seed harness h_0, at round t the proposer receives x_t = (task description, h_{t-1}, execution report from running h_{t-1} on feedback questions Q^fb), samples G candidate edits y_i, applies each to get h_t^i, and a selection protocol evaluates candidates on scoring questions Q^score and retains one (Reasoning Gym: one candidate per round, retained only if it improves the development score; QA: eight candidates per round, zero score for parse failures, retain the highest scorer even if below the parent). Evaluation questions are disjoint from all feedback/scoring questions.

**Training (outer loop).** Optional SFT initialization on retained revisions from a 35B teacher that improve a seed harness and pass quality filters, followed by GRPO: reward r_i = J(h_i; Q^score) + v_i, where v_i is an auxiliary term for edit validity and successful execution; failed edits receive the validity credit accrued before failure. Two configurations: **Single-step RL** treats each (parent harness, report) as an independent revision context, while **Multistep RL** trains on successive revision states (states refreshed between offline phases in Reasoning Gym; online rollouts in QA). Credit is local — each update assigns credit from the candidate's own outcome only.

**Experimental settings.** (1) *Reasoning Gym*: Qwen3-4B proposer and solver; SFT on 35B-teacher revisions; 21 unseen families + 5 RL families; single-step evaluation uses 8 independent proposals on format-varied questions, sequential evaluation runs 5 rounds of one proposal; reported metric is held-out score (best-of-eight shown only as an oracle). (2) *Multi-hop QA*: Qwen3-4B proposer + Qwen3-8B solver; trained on HotpotQA (Multistep RL continues Single-step RL); evaluated on HotpotQA (in-distribution) and MuSiQue / 2WikiMultihopQA (unseen); 8 proposals per round, 4 runs of 10 rounds, held-out exact match.

## Results & Highlights

- **Unseen Reasoning Gym families (mean single-revision score, 21 families):** 0.32 with the Base (untrained 4B) proposer → 0.62 after SFT+RL (+0.30). The gain appears in the mean over all proposals, so it does not depend on selecting the best candidate; after RL the average proposal already exceeds the seed harness, whereas Base and SFT proposals fall below it on average.
- **Trained 4B proposer vs its 35B teacher:** on the 21 unseen families under the same revision protocol, the RL-trained 4B proposer scores 0.62 vs the 35B teacher's 0.56 (+6 points) — although the teacher remains higher under oracle best-of-eight selection.
- **Sequential vs independent revision (MuSiQue, OOD):** Single-step RL averages 0.15 exact match for independent revisions (only marginally above the seed), versus 0.27 after ten sequential revision rounds — and on both unseen benchmarks (MuSiQue and 2WikiMultihopQA) the average final run of each RL proposer exceeds even the oracle best of 80 independent proposals (the paper notes this comparison matches proposal count per run but leaves total compute and feedback unmatched).
- **QA baseline collapse vs RL:** the Base proposer finishes *below the seed harness* on HotpotQA and MuSiQue, while both RL proposers finish above Base on all three benchmarks; the first revision accounts for only one third to two thirds of the ten-round gain, i.e., QA improvements accumulate over more rounds than in Reasoning Gym.
- **Reliability mechanism:** training reduces the fraction of proposals that fail to produce a runnable harness or score zero (mostly attributable to the RL stage on unseen Reasoning Gym families, with a similar RL-driven reduction in QA); SFT data already shows interpreter loops in 48% of teacher revisions, and SFT/RL largely generate such interpreter loops that delegate computation to code.
- **Multistep RL null result:** on Reasoning Gym, Multistep RL ends above Single-step RL on canonical questions but below it on format-varied questions; in QA the two configurations reach nearly identical final scores.

## Critical Assessment

- **No external baselines.** All comparisons are internal: Base vs SFT vs Single-step RL vs Multistep RL vs a 35B teacher. There is no head-to-head against established agent/workflow optimizers (e.g., ADAS, AFlow, DSPy-style prompt optimizers) or the concurrent Harness-RL/JIT-Agent the paper itself cites — so "harness learning improves revision quality" is never situated against the best published alternative on the same benchmarks.
- **The QA headline leans on sequential budget.** On MuSiQue, a single RL revision yields 0.15 EM, which the paper itself calls "only marginally above the seed"; the 0.27 figure requires ten rounds × 8 proposals with full execution feedback each round, plus a selection protocol that retains the highest scorer even when it regresses below its parent. The "average final run beats oracle best-of-80" claim is striking but, as the authors concede, total compute and feedback are unmatched. Absolute performance also remains low (~0.27 EM on MuSiQue), so practical significance is unproven.
- **The meta-learning framing is only partially validated.** The MAML-like analogy predicts that training on revision *sequences* (Multistep RL) should matter — but the paper's own result is that it does not consistently help, and credit assignment stays local to a single revision. What remains is a solid "RL makes code-editing proposals better and more reliable" result; the deeper "learning-to-learn" claim is asserted more than demonstrated.
- **Narrow model and task coverage.** One proposer family (Qwen3-4B), one teacher (35B), a fixed solver, and prescribed revision prompts (acknowledged in Limitations). Reasoning Gym is synthetic generator-graded families; the QA benchmarks are classic multi-hop sets from the pre-LLM era. No agentic benchmark with real tool ecosystems (e.g., SWE-style tasks) is attempted, and per-family variance on 21 families is large enough that single-family results (Figure 3) diverge substantially from means.
- **Rigor is otherwise good.** Disjoint feedback/scoring/validation question sets, held-out metrics as primary (oracles labeled as oracles), four runs with error bars in QA, and qualitative analysis that actually inspects harness structure (interpreter loops, preserved multi-hop retrieval, failure modes in question parsing/tool communication) are all above-average experimental hygiene.

## Strengths

- Clean, well-motivated formalization: agent = frozen model + revisable executable harness, with a precise mapping from harness revision to gradient-based adaptation that makes the experimental questions (transfer? iteration? sequence training?) crisp and falsifiable.
- The 4B-beats-35B-teacher result (0.62 vs 0.56 mean on unseen families) is a genuinely interesting finding: outcome-based RL on edits surpasses the imitation distribution it was initialized from.
- Honest science: explicit oracle labeling, an admission that a key comparison is compute-unmatched, and a published null result on Multistep RL that contradicts the naive meta-learning expectation.
- Mechanistic value beyond scores: reliability analysis (failed/zero-scoring proposals) and structural analysis (interpreter loops, summaries-for-guidance revision that separates query evidence from answer evidence) explain *why* RL helps, not just that it does.
- Careful evaluation hygiene: feedback, scoring, and validation sets are disjoint; unseen (OOD) tasks are excluded from both SFT and RL.

## Limitations

- Single model family and scale for the proposer (Qwen3-4B) and a single 35B teacher; solver is fixed, so proposer-solver co-adaptation is out of scope (acknowledged).
- Local credit assignment: each update assigns credit from a candidate's own outcome only — no cross-round credit, which the authors note may be why longer revision chains gain little from Multistep training.
- Reward requires a programmatic grader and feedback questions per round; test-time adaptation cost is high (80 executed proposals per round in QA × 10 rounds), and no cost/latency accounting is provided.
- Low absolute QA numbers (0.27 EM on MuSiQue) and synthetic Reasoning Gym families leave real-world agentic significance open; no comparison with external agent-optimization baselines.
- Selection protocols differ between settings (retain-only-if-improved in Reasoning Gym vs retain-best-even-if-worse in QA), which muddies cross-setting conclusions about sequential revision.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would harness learning transfer to real agentic environments (SWE-bench-style tool use) where the grader is noisy or partially observable, rather than a clean programmatic grader?
- The meta-learning analogy predicts cross-round credit assignment should help — what reward shaping or per-round baselines would make Multistep RL actually beat Single-step RL, given the current null result?
- How does the approach behave with a *non*-frozen solver, or when the proposer and solver are the same model (self-modification without a separate proposer)?
- The 4B-beats-35B-teacher gap: is it robust to teacher scale (e.g., a 70B teacher), and does RL on edits plateau at the teacher's oracle best-of-eight ceiling or exceed it?
- What is the compute-normalized comparison: at matched total execution budget, does one RL round with 80 proposals beat ten rounds of sequential adaptation on MuSiQue?
- Can the learned revisions (interpreter loops, evidence-separation in QA retrieval) be distilled into prompting guidelines or default harness scaffolds so that untrained proposers start closer to the RL endpoint?
