Why this matters

A learned policy keeps rewriting the agent's own executable harness — zero-parameter self-improvement at test time, with RL surpassing its imitation teacher.

Critical edge

No external baselines. Every comparison is internal (Base / SFT / RL / teacher) — established agent optimizers (ADAS, AFlow, DSPy-style) are never run. The QA headline also leans on ten rounds × 8 proposals with unmatched compute, and absolute MuSiQue EM stays low at ~0.27.

01

Method — edits as weight updates

distilled
inner loop · adaptation outer loop · training

An agent splits into a frozen solver (the LLM) and a harness — the executable program that builds prompts, schedules solver calls, runs tools, and validates answers. A Qwen3-4B proposer is trained to rewrite that harness code; at test time both networks are frozen and only the harness text changes, playing the role weight updates play in gradient-based meta-learning.

Inner loop. From the current harness, the proposer reads the task description plus an execution report, samples G candidate edits, and a selection protocol keeps one based on scoring questions disjoint from all feedback and validation sets.

Outer loop. Optional SFT on filtered 35B-teacher revisions, then GRPO with reward = revised-harness task score plus an executability bonus. Single-step RL treats each (harness, report) pair as an independent revision context; Multistep RL trains on successive revision states — the direct analogue of multi-step gradient adaptation.

Holds on both unseen benchmarks (MuSiQue, 2WikiMultihopQA); authors concede compute and feedback are unmatched. The Base proposer finishes below the seed on HotpotQA and MuSiQue; the first revision explains only ⅓–⅔ of the ten-round gain.

Why RL works — mechanism

Training cuts the share of proposals that are non-runnable or score zero (mostly the RL stage), and restructures harnesses: interpreter loops that delegate computation to code (already in 48% of teacher SFT revisions) and preserved multi-hop retrieval with query/answer evidence separation.

Honest null result

Multistep RL does not consistently beat Single-step RL. Better on canonical Reasoning Gym questions, worse on format-varied ones; near-identical finals in QA. Iterative test-time improvement emerges from policies trained on single revisions only.

03

Strengths · Limitations

side by side

Strengths

  • Clean formalization: agent = frozen model + revisable harness, with a precise mapping to gradient adaptation that makes the questions (transfer? iteration? sequence training?) crisp and falsifiable.
  • 4B beats its 35B teacher (0.62 vs 0.56 mean on unseen families) — outcome-based RL surpasses the imitation distribution it started from.
  • Honest science: explicit oracle labeling, an admitted compute-unmatched comparison, and a published null result on Multistep RL.
  • Mechanistic value: reliability and structural analyses explain why RL helps, not just that it does.
  • Evaluation hygiene: feedback / scoring / validation sets disjoint; OOD tasks excluded from both SFT and RL.

Limitations

  • Narrow coverage: one proposer family and scale (Qwen3-4B), one 35B teacher, fixed solver — no proposer-solver co-adaptation.
  • Local credit only: no cross-round credit, which may be why longer revision chains gain little from Multistep training.
  • Heavy adaptation cost: needs a programmatic grader plus feedback questions; 80 executed proposals per round × 10 rounds in QA, with no cost or latency accounting.
  • Low absolute QA (~0.27 EM) on pre-LLM-era multi-hop sets and synthetic families; no external agent-optimization baselines.
  • Shifting protocols: retain-only-if-improved (Reasoning Gym) vs retain-best-even-if-worse (QA) muddies cross-setting conclusions.
04

Open questions

for further exploration
Q1

Real agentic environments? Would harness learning transfer to SWE-bench-style tool use, where the grader is noisy or partially observable rather than a clean programmatic check?

Q2

Fixing the null result. What reward shaping or per-round baselines would make Multistep RL actually beat Single-step RL?

Q3

Non-frozen solver? How does the approach behave when the solver trains too — or when proposer and solver are the same model (self-modification)?

Q4

Teacher scale. Is 4B-beats-35B robust to a 70B teacher, and does RL on edits plateau at the teacher's oracle ceiling or exceed it?

Q5

Compute-normalized: at matched total execution budget, does one RL round × 80 proposals beat ten sequential rounds on MuSiQue?

Q6

Distillation: can learned revisions (interpreter loops, evidence separation) become default scaffolds so untrained proposers start closer to the RL endpoint?
