Key metric
96/100

persistent faithfulness misrankings repaired by partially restoring intact-model activations — circuit structure, behavior scores and donors untouched. 82 of them fixed at the 10% restoration level alone.

Why this matters

Anyone selecting circuits by faithfulness — or certifying interpretability pipelines with it — may be choosing the wrong candidate. Report behavioral agreement Q alongside.

对任何依赖 faithfulness 指标的机制分析或自动化可解释性流水线，这是一条必要的评估警示。

Critical edge

Q measures behavior, not mechanism identity — the “better” circuit can still be the wrong mechanism. And the 96/100 repair result has no random-restoration control: restoring intact activations partially undoes the ablation by construction. No replacement objective is proposed.

01

Methodology

Three studies, one question: does the selection objective itself mislead?

Every comparison is size-controlled and deduplicated — paired circuits hold the same number of components — so a ranking reversal can never be blamed on circuit size. Each candidate is scored twice:

F — Validation faithfulness

Negative output KL against the intact model, averaged over prompts and three shared donor draws, under resampling, mean or zero ablation; logit difference (LD) and probability difference (PD) as margin alternatives.

Q — Behavioral criterion

Held-out answer agreement with the intact model — including its mistakes — held fixed whenever the intervention or score changes. Misranking: F prefers the lower-Q circuit.

Study 1 · Section 3

Controlled edits, no search

Swap 1–75% of retained components for excluded ones in each reference circuit (ten seeds per band), across IOI, Greater-Than, Docstring, Acronym and ten InterpBench tasks.

Study 2 · Section 4

Real discovery outputs

Candidate pools from four established methods — EAP, EAP-IG, ACDC, Edge-SP — each run on 100 discovery prompts per task, reusing Study 1’s prompts, masks and donors.

Study 3 · Section 5

Context restoration

On 100 persistent misrankings, restore 80/40/20/10% of excluded incoming signals to intact-model cached activations — circuits, scores, donors fixed — then re-rank on an independent test set.

→ result: 96/100 repaired

03

Strengths & Limits

An unusually candid paper: the sharpest critiques come from its own limitations section.

Strengths

  • Clean causal isolation — size-controlled, deduplicated, search-free pairs, each confirmed on validation and independent-test prompts.
  • Dual-benchmark design — human tasks and InterpBench diverge materially (20.9% vs 9.8% pooled); synthetic-only evaluation would understate the problem.
  • Coverage across metrics and interventions — KL/LD/PD and resampling/mean/zero all fail: the defect is objective-level, not one formula’s artifact.
  • A clever causal test — circuits, Q values, donors and prompts held fixed while only the computational context changes.
  • Reporting discipline — misranking frequency separated from behavioral deficit (63.55 pp on task 93 vs 0.04 pp on task 111).

Limitations

  • Q is behavior, not mechanism identity — every “better” circuit is only behaviorally better, and Q inherits sensitivity to the resampling methodology the paper criticizes.
  • Selected cohort — the 96/100 repair rate characterizes 100 persistent failures with Q deficit ≥ 0.5 pp; no random-restoration baseline; 4 cases never repaired.
  • Asymmetric budgets — ACDC and Edge-SP capped at 300 s; pools differ in coverage and size strata, so the 9.4%–41.2% band is not directly comparable across methods.
  • Modest scale — four GPT-2-scale tasks plus ten semi-synthetic ones, dependent pairs, several small-denominator cells; reversals are local, not global, failures.
  • No replacement objective — guidance stops at “report Q alongside faithfulness with intervention and size controls.”
04

Open Questions

Where the authors’ own diagnosis points next.

  1. Random-restoration control — would restoring randomly chosen incoming signals, or signals from an unrelated prompt, separate “context distortion” from the weaker claim that any extra intact-model information helps?
  2. A constrained objective — can maximizing faithfulness subject to Q within ε on held-out prompts close the gap, and would applying it during search change the circuits EAP/ACDC actually return?
  3. Stricter recovery criteria — do misrankings persist under causal scrubbing, or under behavioral tests beyond resampling-based output agreement (e.g. intermediate-variable probes)?
  4. Scaling — how do misranking rates grow with model size and task complexity beyond GPT-2-scale circuits — and is the Human-vs-InterpBench gap driven by pretraining or by task structure?
  5. The four unrepairable cases — is there a structural signature of cases no partial restoration can fix, predicting when faithfulness is fundamentally misaligned with behavior?
