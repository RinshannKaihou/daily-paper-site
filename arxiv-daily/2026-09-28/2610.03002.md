> **一句话总结：** 提出递归跨能力自改进（RSI）：让统一多模态模型 BAGEL-7B-MoT 的文本能力（程序编写）与视觉能力（图像生成+理解）互为对方的训练数据来源——模型生成图像、自诊短板、针对性写绘图程序，由沙箱执行记录（而非模型自评）对照规格逐条验证后再入训练集，从而自生成数据的错误不会跨轮累积；4 轮后在自建 BasicChartBench L0 上图表生成准确率从 45.7% 升至 60.2%（持续训练对照仅 46.3%），验证程序产出率从 48.9% 升至 95.2%，held-out 编辑渲染阅读准确率从 55.6% 升至 86.5%，而去掉执行验证、纯靠模型自评挑图的对照只涨 0.5%——证明增益主要来自执行验证而非"自我评判"。

> **TL;DR:** The paper proposes RSI (recursive cross-capability self-improvement), a closed training loop for unified multimodal models in which the text side (program writer) and the visual side (generator + reader) build each other's training data: each round the model generates images from diagnostic requests, reads them to locate its failures, writes chart-drawing programs targeted at those failures, and a sandboxed executor verifies every candidate program against its written specification before the verified renders, labels, and programs become training data — so supervision comes from execution records rather than model judgment, and errors do not accumulate across rounds. On BasicChartBench (their new 476-chart, 14,440-atomic-question benchmark for early-training open UMMs, where existing benchmarks like Struct2IBench are too hard — the start model answers only ~11%/~4%), four rounds of RSI lift BAGEL-7B-MoT's chart-generation accuracy on L0 from 45.7% to 60.2% while continued training on the original data stays at 46.3%; a control that replaces execution-verified data with model-judged best-of-two selection (self-filtered) gains only 0.5% on L0, and replacing failure-targeted scheduling with even spreading (fixed distribution, 54.9%) costs several points. Verified-program yield rises from 48.9% to 95.2% and held-out edited-render reading from 55.6% to 86.5%. Caveats: gains barely transfer to existing benchmarks (Struct2IBench 11.0% → 11.9% vs 11.7% for continued training), and the reader loses 2.6 points on MMBench and 3.0 on MMMU.

## 核心贡献

- **提出 RSI 闭环**：统一多模态模型（UMM）的读（理解 U）、写（程序编写 C）、画（生成 G）三种能力互相供数——每轮先用固定的诊断请求让模型生成图像并由自己的读器逐条核对规格（met / violated / unresolved），再针对暴露的短板写绘图程序，沙箱执行后用"程序实际画了什么"的执行记录对照规格做硬验证；验证通过的 (规格, 渲染) 对训练生成，带编辑负标签的渲染 + 验证程序训练理解与程序编写。
- **关键设计：程序执行是模型外的真值来源**。验证依据的是代码执行记录而非模型判断，因此自生成数据中的错误不会跨轮累积（对冲 model collapse 风险）；消融显示纯靠模型自评挑图（self-filtered 对照，从两张生成图中选读器打分高者）仅 +0.5%（L0），而同样请求数下用执行验证数据训练一轮即 +8.6%（L0）。
- **构建 BasicChartBench**（476 张图、14,440 个原子问题，L0：熟悉词汇 + 训练中从未出现的请求措辞、10,140 题；L1：14 个罕见主题、4,300 题），填补现有基准（Struct2IBench、BizGenEval）对开放模型早期训练阶段区分度不足的空白——起始模型在两者上仅答对约 11% 和 4%。
- **规则化的"诊断→数据计划"**：20% 预算均摊、其余按违例率加权（上限 3 倍均值）；掌握度门控（≥0.80 升难度、<0.60 降回）；弱属性值加倍采样并配"措辞对照对"；词汇/格式门控与新词开启后 50% 回填旧词汇；写器通过率低的属性追加至多 20% 规格。所有阈值在首轮前固定并列于 Table 4。
- **诚实的失效分析**：结构维度在未见措辞下仅部分恢复（86.1%）；第 3 轮整词表替换导致旧词精确类别文本从 72% 跌到 60%，第 4 轮回填规则修复；读器对模型自身生成的盲评仍接受约 1/3 人类判定"未画出"的需求；通用能力出现回退（MMMU −3.0）。

## Key Contributions

- **RSI: a recursive cross-capability self-improvement loop** in which a UMM's text and visual abilities supply training data for one another, with program execution acting as an independent source of truth that prevents error accumulation across rounds.
- **A decisive ablation**: swapping execution-verified data for model-judged selection (self-filtered) collapses the gain to +0.5% on L0, while verified data at the same request/step budget gains +8.6% — verification, not self-rating, drives the improvement; failure-targeted scheduling adds a further +3.5% over evenly spreading the same verified data.
- **BasicChartBench**, a two-level benchmark (476 charts, 14,440 atomic questions across structure/text/binding) purpose-built to measure open UMMs early in training, where Struct2IBench and BizGenEval floor near chance (~11%/~4% correct).
- **A component-wise account of the loop**: verified-program yield 48.9% → 95.2% in one round; program-writing cost drops from 159 to 18 GPU-hours per round once the writer is trained on diagnosis-driven data; careful measurement via a frozen initial reader cross-checked with an external judge (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B), OCR character similarity, and a blind human audit of 392 questions.

## Methodology

**Setup.** The UMM M_t (BAGEL-7B-MoT) exposes three abilities with separated expert parameters: generation G_t (spec-free request → image), visual understanding U_t (image + question → answer), and program writing C_t (spec → plotting program), where U_t and C_t share the understanding parameters and G_t uses the generation parameters. A sandboxed executor E runs programs and records exactly what was drawn: (I, z) = E(p). The starting model M_0 comes from an easy-to-hard curriculum (ChartNet code+images → programmatic charts → atomic-question reading).

**Each RSI round has three steps.**
1. *Diagnose*: on a fixed set of 336 diagnostic requests, the generator produces images; the reader answers the atomic questions derived from the specification, and each requirement is marked met / violated / unresolved. Correct renders calibrate the reader's rejection rate; OCR (PaddleOCR) independently checks rendered text and gates new vocabulary.
2. *Plan (diagnosis → data)*: fixed rules turn error rates into a sampling distribution π_t over specifications — 20% of the budget spread evenly, the rest weighted by violation rate capped at 3× the mean; difficulty levels move up at ≥0.80 satisfaction and back down below 0.60; weak attribute values receive more examples plus wording-contrast pairs; new vocabulary/format levels open at ≥70%/60% satisfaction; writer pass-rate gaps add up to 20% extra specifications.
3. *Build & verify*: the writer produces two candidate programs per specification (greedy + temperature 0.6, ≤6,144 tokens); the sandbox executes both and verification compares the execution record z with the spec on data, text, colors, axes, layout, and readability; the first verified candidate is kept (≈35k–43k specifications per round). Understanding data additionally includes "edited" renders (one property deliberately altered) whose labels mark what the render actually shows versus what the request asked — negative supervision that teaches reading decisions rather than blind acceptance.

**Updates.** Generation parameters train only on verified (q(s), I) pairs; understanding parameters train on the labeled-render set D_t^V plus 5% general visual data; the next model merges both updated halves (M_{t+1} = Merge(Learn_G, Learn_U)). Training per round: 64 GPUs, generation update 400 steps (batch 192, 25,600 unique examples ×3, AdamW, LR 2e-5, EMA 0.995), understanding update 704 steps (batch 128, 30,000 examples ×3).

**Evaluation.** BasicChartBench L0 (familiar vocabulary, unseen wording; 10,140 questions) and L1 (14 rare topics; 4,300 questions), scored by satisfaction of atomic questions under the frozen initial reader U_0; trends cross-checked with an external judge (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, no shared weights/data), OCR character similarity, and a blind human audit of 392 questions on the model's own generations (labels collected after the last round, stored apart from the pipeline). Five controls isolate each part: continued training, self-filtered (no programs/verification), fixed distribution (no targeting), single diagnosis (no recursion), and frozen reader (no reader updates).

## Results & Highlights

- **Main result (BasicChartBench L0, generation accuracy, frozen reader U_0):** four rounds of RSI raise the score from 45.7% to 60.2% (+14.5 pts); continued training on the original data for the same number of steps stays at 46.3% (+0.6), and the fixed-distribution control (same verified data spread evenly) reaches 54.9% — failure targeting adds +5.3 over it at round 4 (the paper's headline summary: +3.5). On the harder L1, RSI goes 45.1% → 56.2% vs 46.1% continued and 50.7% fixed.
- **Execution verification is the driver:** the self-filtered control (model picks the better of two generated images, no programs/verification) finishes only +0.5% (L0) / +1.2% (L1) above the start, whereas a first-round generator trained on execution-verified charts for the same requests and steps gains +8.6% (L0) / +4.2% (L1). Recursion matters on the hard level: on L1, RSI leads single-diagnosis by 3.1 and 3.7 pts in rounds 3–4 and the frozen reader by 2.3 pts in round 3.
- **The writer improves recursively:** the share of specifications receiving a verified program jumps from 48.9% to 95.2% after one round (95–97% thereafter; Table 6). Per-type: horizontal bar 11.8% → 98.4%, scatter 23.4% → 90.9%. Diagnosis-trained writing also cuts program construction from 159 to 18 GPU-hours per round (~8.8× cheaper), and a control retraining the writer on uniformly sampled programs shows the speedup — not the yield — is what the targeting buys.
- **Reading improves on held-out renders:** on held-out edited renders (first-round families, never trained on), accuracy on questions changed by the edit rises from 55.6% to 86.5% after one round and stays ~87%; on correct renders, overall 87.1% → 99.3% and binding 77.4% → 98.5% after four rounds.
- **External validation:** the Qwen3.6-35B-A3B judge shows the same trend — L0 37.4% → 57.9%, L1 37.5% → 50.0%; OCR character similarity on BasicChartBench rises 47.8% → 80.6%. Per-question-group on BasicChartBench: text 56.0% → 88.2%, binding 13.6% → 27.3%. Retraining a round's generator with other seeds moves the score by at most 0.4%.
- **Reading the model's own generations is much weaker:** blind human audit (392 questions) shows reader accuracy only 78.5% → 82.4% (U_0 → U_3), and every reader still accepts about a third of the requirements humans mark as not shown (34.1–38.0% false-accept); the weakness-ranking correlation with humans is 0.82 → 0.86–0.91, which the allocation rules rely on.
- **Transfer to existing benchmarks is thin:** on Struct2IBench (judge-scored, official questions) RSI moves 11.0% → 11.9% vs 11.7% for continued training; on BizGenEval's chart subset every model answers ~4%. General abilities: GenEval stays at 82.9 → 83.7–84.5, but the reader drops 2.6 pts on MMBench and 3.0 pts on MMMU after four rounds (only 5% of reader data is general visual).

## Authors & Credibility

The team spans USC (Huijuan Wang, Chufan Shi — equal contribution; Taylor Berg-Kirkpatrick; Xuezhe Ma), CMU (Cheng Yang), and UCSD (Yaokang Wu) — an academic NLP/multimodal group with faculty known for representation-learning and language-model work. The base model is BAGEL-7B-MoT (ByteDance's open unified multimodal model), positioned deliberately in their own model family for controlled recursion. The paper is unusually rigorous for a preprint: five ablation controls, an external judge with no shared weights, OCR cross-checks, a blind human audit with a pre-study calibration check (76.2% agreement on 210 labels, which exposed the reader's text weakness before round 1), seed-stability checks, and an Appendix F changelog of every mid-study rule fix. An AI-use statement discloses LLM assistance for manuscript polish. No conflicts or red flags; findings are plausible and internally cross-validated.

## Critical Assessment

- **The "self-improvement" framing oversells the mechanism.** The self-filtered control finishing at +0.5%/+1.2% shows that recursive model judgment contributes almost nothing; what works is *program-execution-verified synthetic data* plus a rule-based curriculum. This is closer to self-play in a domain with a perfect verifier than open-ended self-improvement — the loop is only possible where structured content can be written as programs and checked exactly.
- **The benchmark is bespoke, and external transfer is near zero.** BasicChartBench is built by the authors for the regime where their model is weak; on the two existing benchmarks tested, the headline gain evaporates (Struct2IBench 11.0% → 11.9% vs 11.7% for plain continued training — a +0.9 vs +0.7 difference; BizGenEval ~4% for every model). A skeptical reading: four rounds of a 64-GPU loop buy ~15 points on the authors' own benchmark and ~1 point on the community's.
- **Small but real reporting slip:** the abstract/intro say 14,440 questions, and only Appendix B's per-level counts (10,140 + 4,300) sum to that; Section 3 states "10,410 questions" for L0 — a digit transposition across sections that a careful camera-ready should fix.
- **Scorer dependence.** All headline numbers come from the model-family reader U_0; on structure under unseen wording, U_0 shows only partial recovery (86.1%) while the external judge sees full recovery — at least one component-level conclusion is scorer-dependent, and the paper does not fully resolve which is right.
- **The loop needed human patches mid-study (Appendix F).** The number-format pairing rule and a verification content checker were fixed while round-1 data were being built, and the old-vocabulary backfill rule was added only after round 3's full vocabulary replacement caused exact-category text to fall from 72% to 60%. Honest disclosure, but it means RSI is not yet an autonomous procedure — a human curriculum engineer is still in the loop between rounds.
- **Diagnosis quality caps the ceiling.** Readers accept 34–38% of requirements humans mark as not shown on native generations, so roughly a third of real errors go unreported; the data plan compensates via ranking (ρ = 0.82–0.91) rather than exact rates, which mitigates but does not eliminate misallocation.
- **Reproducibility is plausible but heavy and under-specified for release:** 64 GPUs, ~35k–43k specifications per round, many hand-set thresholds (Table 4), and no explicit statement about releasing code, BasicChartBench, or the round data.

## Strengths

- **Clean answer to the model-collapse worry**: supervision comes from execution records, not model judgment, so the recursive loop compounds capability rather than error (directly addresses the Shumailov et al. collapse scenario for self-trained data).
- **Best-in-class controls**: five ablations that each remove exactly one component (verification, targeting, recursion, reader updating), making the credit assignment unusually crisp — verification is worth ~8 pts, targeting ~3.5–5 pts, recursion ~3 pts on L1 rounds 3–4.
- **Triangulated measurement**: frozen in-family scorer + external judge + OCR + blind human audit, with a pre-study calibration check that caught the reader's text weakness before the loop started.
- **Honest failure analysis** (Section 4.5) that names where the loop does not help — structure under unseen wording, vocabulary-change forgetting, general-VQA regression — instead of burying it; a per-round rule changelog (Appendix F) documents every deviation.
- **Practical side-result**: diagnosis-driven data makes each new round's program corpus ~8.8× cheaper to build (159 → 18 GPU-hours), and verified-program yield saturates at 95%+ after one round, suggesting favorable economics for extending the loop.

## Limitations

- **Domain is a single verifiable niche**: charts only. No evidence the loop helps free-form generation (GenEval flat) or general understanding (MMBench −2.6, MMMU −3.0); the motivating claim about tables, diagrams, and documents is untested.
- **Bespoke benchmark**: BasicChartBench is authored by the same team and tuned to the model's weak regime; gains do not transfer to Struct2IBench/BizGenEval.
- **Reader reliability on native generations is the binding constraint**: 82.4% audit accuracy and ~1/3 false-acceptance mean the diagnosis that steers data construction misses a large fraction of real errors.
- **Curriculum fragility**: full vocabulary replacement caused measurable forgetting (72% → 60%) and required a manual backfill rule; structure under unseen wording recovers only partially (86.1% by the internal scorer); chart type and panel count still drift.
- **Scale and duration**: four rounds only, one base model (BAGEL-7B-MoT), one seed per round (single-round seed check aside), with visible diminishing returns (pooled 57.4% → 59.0% across rounds 3–4); no comparison against spending the same GPU budget on simply generating more verified data without the diagnostic machinery.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does RSI transfer beyond charts to other program-verifiable structured domains (tables, diagrams, documents, UI screenshots), as the introduction motivates but the paper never tests?
- Can the reader's ~34% false-acceptance rate on native generations be closed (per-requirement external checkers, ensembled readers), and how much would sharper diagnosis change the data allocation given the ranking correlation is already 0.86–0.91?
- Is failure-targeted scheduling worth its machinery at equal wall-clock/GPU budget, as opposed to the paper's equal-data-volume comparison against the fixed distribution?
- Would raising the 5% general-visual share in reader updates prevent the MMBench/MMMU regression without stalling the loop, or is general-capability replay a necessary ingredient for long recursive runs?
- What happens past round 4 once all curriculum gates are open — does accuracy plateau near 60% on L0, and do the fixed thresholds (0.80 mastery, 3× mean cap) need retuning per domain or per model scale?
