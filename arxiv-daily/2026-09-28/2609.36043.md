> **一句话总结：** 针对 LLM agent 技能自进化中"聚合验证分提升就接受编辑"的朴素验收门，提出 SAGE——用逐题配对比较（带不对称回归惩罚 Δ_λ = w − λℓ）加单侧精确二项检验（λ=1 时退化为精确 McNemar 检验）的统计门，在 5 个 benchmark × 4 个 backbone 共 20 个等预算设置中，将回归率在 19 个设置中降低（DeepSeek-V4 上 LiveMath 36.5%→0%、OfficeQA 42.8%→0%），最终得分在全部 20 个设置中最高，平均超 vanilla SkillOpt +8.73 分。

> **TL;DR:** SAGE replaces the naive acceptance gate of SkillOpt (keep any skill edit that raises the aggregate validation score) with a two-part statistical gate: per-item paired comparison on identical validation items with an asymmetric regression penalty (Δ_λ = w − λℓ, λ ≥ 1), plus a one-sided exact conditional binomial test (reducing to the exact McNemar test at λ = 1) that lets the gate abstain instead of committing lucky edits. Under an equal-budget protocol across 5 benchmarks (LiveMath, SpreadsheetBench, SearchQA, OfficeQA, ALFWorld) × 4 backbones (DeepSeek-V4, GLM-5.2, MiniMax-M3, Qwen3.6), SAGE lowers regression rates in 19 of 20 settings (e.g., 36.5% → 0% on LiveMath with DeepSeek-V4 vs. vanilla SkillOpt) and posts the highest final score in all 20 settings, beating vanilla SkillOpt by +8.73 points on average (LiveMath 34.15 → 48.78, OfficeQA 32.93 → 45.12 on DeepSeek-V4).

## 核心贡献

- **诊断（Diagnosis）**：指出技能自进化循环中被忽视的瓶颈——验收门。朴素门（SkillOpt 的 g₀）有两条独立缺陷：(1) 只看聚合分，无法发现"平均分上升但破坏了当前技能本已会做的题"的隐性回归，而已接受的编辑会不可逆地写入技能文档，回归是永久性的；(2) 验证集有限且带噪，取最高观测分的门受 Optimizer's Curse（赢家诅咒）影响，倾向保留"运气好"的编辑。
- **方法（Method）**：SAGE 由两个耦合组件构成。组件一：逐题配对评估——在同一批验证题上并行运行新旧技能，把每个结果记为 win（修好新题）/ regression（弄坏旧题）/ tie（两者一致），用折扣净收益 Δ_λ = w − λℓ（λ ≥ 1，回归代价至少与修复奖励等同）加回归占比上限 ρ ≤ τ 实现"首先不伤害"的不对称原则；组件二：假设检验去噪——在不一致对 (w, ℓ) 上做单侧精确条件二项检验（H₀: q ≤ q_λ = λ/(1+λ)），只有 Δ_λ > 0、p_λ(w,ℓ) < α、ρ ≤ τ 三条同时满足才提交，否则弃权保留旧技能。
- **理论关系**：SAGE 是朴素门的保守细化（conservative refinement）——在边界参数 (λ, α, τ) = (1, 1, 1) 处精确恢复 SkillOpt 基线门；任何保守设置下被 SAGE 接受的编辑都必然会被基线门接受。λ = 1 时检验即为单侧精确 McNemar 检验，λ 增大时等效于抬高赢面阈值 q_λ。
- **实证（Results）**：等预算协议（相同编辑提案数与 token 成本）下，回归率在 19/20 设置中下降、1 个持平（GLM-5.2 on SearchQA）；最终得分在全部 20 个 backbone-benchmark 设置中均为最高，平均较 vanilla SkillOpt 提升 8.73 分。消融显示配对比较组件（C1）平均贡献 +6.20 分，统计检验组件在此基础上再贡献 +2.53 分——两个组件互补且均必要。

## Key Contributions

- **Diagnosis.** Identifies the acceptance step of skill self-evolution as an overlooked bottleneck and traces the naive gate's two independent failures: (1) aggregate scores hide permanent regressions — an edit can raise the mean while breaking items the incumbent already solves, and committed edits are irreversible; (2) trusting the best observed score on a finite, noisy validation set invites the Optimizer's Curse.
- **Method.** Proposes SAGE, a regression-aware and noise-aware gate built from two coupled components: (a) per-item paired evaluation — win/regression/tie ledgers over identical validation items, summarized by a discounted net gain Δ_λ = w − λℓ (λ ≥ 1, "first do no harm") with a regression-fraction cap ρ ≤ τ; (b) hypothesis-test denoising — a one-sided exact conditional binomial test on the discordant pairs (H₀: q ≤ q_λ = λ/(1+λ)); commit only if Δ_λ > 0 AND p_λ(w,ℓ) < α AND ρ ≤ τ, otherwise abstain and keep the incumbent skill.
- **Theoretical sanity property.** SAGE is a conservative refinement of the standard gate: at the boundary setting (λ, α, τ) = (1, 1, 1) it recovers the SkillOpt baseline gate g₀ exactly, and under conservative settings every edit SAGE accepts would also be accepted by g₀.
- **Empirical validation.** Under an equal-budget protocol across 20 backbone-benchmark settings, SAGE lowers the regression rate in 19 of 20 and attains the highest final score in all 20, with an average improvement of +8.73 points over vanilla SkillOpt. An ablation cleanly separates the components: paired comparison alone (C1) contributes +6.20 points on average, the statistical test adds a further +2.53 in all 20 comparisons.

## Methodology

**Setup.** Following SkillOpt, at step t a fixed optimizer proposes a mini-batch of candidate edits E = {e₁, …, e_b} to the persistent skill document S^(t); each candidate defines a skill S̃ₙ^(t+1) = S^(t) + eₙ. A frozen target model plus a binary verifier v(S, x) ∈ {0,1} scores each skill on a held-out validation set D. The baseline gate g₀ keeps an edit iff its aggregate score J_D strictly improves: g₀(eₙ) = 𝕀[J_D(S̃ₙ) > J_D(S^(t))].

**Component 1 — Per-item paired evaluation.** Rather than collapsing evidence into one aggregate number, SAGE runs the incumbent and the candidate on the *identical* validation items and classifies each outcome: a **win** (w) when the candidate solves what the incumbent misses, a **regression** (ℓ) when it breaks what the incumbent solves, and a tie (both agree) which carries no comparative information. Because a committed skill edit is irreversible, regressions are weighted asymmetrically via a discounted net gain Δ_λ = w − λℓ with λ ≥ 1 (motivated by asymmetric counterfactual utilities), plus an empirical regression fraction ρ = ℓ/n_S (n_S = items the incumbent solves), capped by τ. Hyperparameters λ, τ, α are tuned on held-out data only; the test set is unseen during the whole optimization.

**Component 2 — Hypothesis-test denoising.** Δ_λ > 0 alone is still a noisy point estimate, so SAGE requires the win dominance to be statistically reliable. Writing q = Pr(W | W ∪ L) for the probability a discordant outcome is a win, a positive population λ-weighted gain is equivalent to q > q_λ = λ/(1+λ). SAGE tests H₀: q ≤ q_λ against H₁: q > q_λ with the one-sided exact conditional binomial significance p_λ(w, ℓ) = Pr[Bin(m, q_λ) ≥ w] on m = w + ℓ discordant pairs — valid for the composite null because the binomial upper tail is monotone in q; m = 0 gives p = 1 (abstain without comparative evidence). At λ = 1 this reduces to the one-sided exact McNemar test. The commit rule (Eq. 11) is A(eₙ, S^(t)) = 𝕀[Δ_λ > 0 ∧ p_λ(w,ℓ) < α ∧ ρ ≤ τ].

**Gate family.** At (λ, α, τ) = (1, 1, 1) the family contains g₀ exactly as a boundary member; under conservative settings (λ ≥ 1, α ≤ 1, τ ≤ 1) any accepted edit has w > λℓ and would also pass g₀, making SAGE a strict refinement. The evaluation holds the SkillOpt generation loop fixed — every edit is proposed by the same optimizer, so the only difference is the gate — and uses an equal-budget protocol (equal number of proposed edits and equal token cost for baseline and SAGE). Backbones: DeepSeek-V4-flash, GLM-5.2, MiniMax-M3, Qwen3.6 (chosen open-source for reproducibility).

## Results & Highlights

- **Regression elimination (Table 1):** With DeepSeek-V4, the naive gate's regression rate falls from 36.5% to 0.0% on LiveMath and from 42.8% to 0.0% on OfficeQA; SpreadsheetBench drops 16.6% → 0.0%. Across all 20 backbone-benchmark settings SAGE lowers the regression rate in 19 and matches the remaining one (GLM-5.2 on SearchQA, 2.4% vs 2.4%); the worst residual rate SAGE leaves anywhere is 5.1% (GLM-5.2, ALFWorld).
- **Final performance (Table 2):** SAGE attains the highest final score in all 20 settings, improving over vanilla SkillOpt by 8.73 points on average. On DeepSeek-V4, LiveMath rises from 34.15 to 48.78 (+14.63) and OfficeQA from 32.93 to 45.12 (+12.19), with no benchmark regressing.
- **Consistency across backbones:** MiniMax-M3 sees the largest gains — LiveMath 30.97 → 47.58 (+16.61) and OfficeQA 43.19 → 60.12 (+16.93). GLM-5.2 gains +13.60 on LiveMath (42.12 → 55.72) and +11.73 on OfficeQA (44.46 → 56.19); Qwen3.6 gains +16.80 on LiveMath (40.30 → 57.10) and +10.32 on OfficeQA (37.07 → 47.39).
- **Ablation (Table 2, C1 vs SAGE):** The paired-comparison-only variant C1 improves over vanilla SkillOpt in every setting (+6.20 points on average), and the full SAGE gate further beats C1 in all 20 comparisons, adding +2.53 points on average — the paired ledger and the statistical test are complementary, not redundant.
- **Benefit tracks regression risk (Sec. 5.1):** The two biggest gains occur exactly where the naive gate regresses most (LiveMath 36.5%, OfficeQA 42.8% under DeepSeek-V4), while on tasks where it barely regresses (ALFWorld 2.5%, SearchQA 4.8%) SAGE acts as a near no-op that preserves baseline behavior — the gate intervenes precisely where edits threaten mastered behavior.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty is in the framing, not the statistics.** The genuinely underexplored observation — that skill-evolution work (SkillOpt, Voyager, ADAS, Trace2Skill) invests everything in the optimizer while the acceptance gate is a naive aggregate-score rule — holds up against the paper's own related-work survey, and quantifying the cost of that neglect (regression rates up to 42.8% and 45.6%) is a real contribution. But the technical core is assembled from classical pieces: per-item paired comparison with a one-sided exact binomial test *is* the exact McNemar test at λ = 1 (the authors say so), the asymmetric penalty imports the backward-compatible prediction updates idea (Traible et al.) and asymmetric counterfactual utilities (Ben-Michael et al.), and the Optimizer's Curse citation is Smith & Winkler (2006). This is a careful, well-scoped application of known statistics to a new bottleneck — valuable, but the machinery itself is not new.

**The comparator is only vanilla SkillOpt.** No external alternative gate is tested — e.g., a simple paired majority rule with fixed λ, a bootstrap significance test on the aggregate score, or an existing regret-aware acceptance scheme. The C1 ablation isolates the authors' own two components but does not answer whether a simpler noise guard (say, requiring w > ℓ without any test) would capture most of the gain. Given that SAGE's full-gate edge over C1 is +2.53 points on average, the marginal value of the hypothesis test over a naive paired threshold is exactly the kind of claim that needed a stronger baseline.

**A noise-aware paper that reports no run-to-run variance.** Agent benchmarks with stochastic rollouts are noisy — that is the paper's entire premise — yet Table 2 reports single point estimates per setting, with no seeds, confidence intervals, or variance estimates, and the validation-set size per benchmark is not stated in the main text. The +2.53 average edge of SAGE over C1 is precisely the magnitude where single-run noise could matter. Similarly, the hyperparameters λ, τ, α are "tuned on held-out data," but no sensitivity analysis or tuning protocol is shown; the honest limitation section admits the gate "is not yet free of manual configuration."

**Honest scoping, but real limits.** The paper candidly concedes: binary verifiers only (graded rewards need a signed-rank test, untested); the ledger discards effect magnitude, so the test is insensitive to a small but consistent real improvement and may systematically abstain on edits that help many items a little; the exact test controls false-commit probability only per incumbent-candidate pair — the validation set is reused across optimization steps and trajectory-level error is not controlled; DocVQA was dropped because not all four backbones accept images, leaving multimodal generalization open. The boundary-equivalence property (recovers g₀ at (1,1,1)) is a nice sanity anchor, though it also means everything hinges on where in the conservative regime one sits.

## Strengths

- Clean, quantified diagnosis of a real failure mode: the naive acceptance gate regresses on 2.5%–45.6% of mastered items depending on benchmark and backbone, and committed regressions are irreversible — this is a concrete, measurable defect, not a hypothetical one.
- Methodologically sound construction: exact conditional binomial test valid for the composite null; per-item pairing controls for shared item difficulty that aggregate-score shrinkage cannot; the boundary-equivalence to g₀ gives the gate family a well-defined baseline anchor.
- Disciplined evaluation: equal-budget protocol (equal edits proposed, equal token cost), 20 backbone-benchmark settings, a consistent two-way ablation, and direction-consistent gains everywhere.
- Unusually honest limitations section that names the binary-verifier assumption, the per-comparison scope of the error guarantee, the discarded effect magnitude, and the hand-tuned hyperparameters.

## Limitations

- Assumes a binary verifier v ∈ {0,1}; continuous or graded rewards require different statistics (e.g., a signed-rank test) and are untested.
- The win/loss ledger discards magnitude, so the gate can be insensitive to small but consistent real improvements — it may abstain on broadly helpful edits.
- Statistical guarantee is per-comparison only: validation data is reused across optimization steps, so trajectory-level family-wise error is not controlled.
- Hyperparameters λ, τ, α are hand-tuned on held-out data; no adaptive threshold or sensitivity analysis is provided.
- Evaluation is text-only (DocVQA omitted because not all backbones support visual input); multimodal generalization is open.
- The optimizer is held fixed at SkillOpt (decoupled setting); whether the gate's benefit transfers to other proposal mechanisms is untested, and the benefit is contingent on the task's baseline regression risk — on low-regression tasks SAGE is a near no-op by design.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does SAGE behave with graded or partial-credit verifiers, where a signed-rank variant of the paired test would be needed — does the asymmetric λ-discount still admit a clean exact test?
- Can the per-comparison α be replaced with anytime-valid confidence sequences (e.g., Bernstein-bounds or time-uniform e-processes) to control error across the whole optimization trajectory as the validation set is reused?
- Is the +2.53 average edge of the full gate over the paired-only C1 variant robust to rollout variance (multiple seeds, confidence intervals), given no variance is reported per setting?
- Does the regression-risk two-regime pattern (large gains where the naive gate regresses, no-op where it does not) hold under other optimizer substrates than SkillOpt, or is it an artifact of SkillOpt's proposal distribution?
- How would per-item paired acceptance extend to multi-agent settings where one agent's win can be another agent's regression, as the future-work section sketches?
