> **一句话总结：** Google Research 提出 Cogentic——一个"编排者—并行证明者—对抗验证者"迭代证明发现 harness，靠跨轮次的已验证引理账本累积进展，仅以每题 O(100)（最难 O(1000)）次 Gemini 调用独立解决了线上学习、拍卖理论与机制设计中 5 个开放研究问题，包括把双侧市场 Bulow-Klemperer 招募常数从每侧 ≥20,000 降到单侧 2 个卖家、把单加性买家简单机制收入近似比从 5.2 改进到 3.52。

> **TL;DR:** Cogentic is a multi-agent harness for research-level proof discovery: an orchestrator assigns parallel provers to distinct proof directions, each prover gets an individually written briefing, two adversarial verifiers attack every draft (every step assumed wrong until justified), and confirmed intermediate lemmas persist in a cross-round verified ledger while dead ends are recorded so later rounds never revisit them. Running on Gemini 3.1 Pro or an early Gemini 4 Argon with only O(100) model calls per problem (O(1000) for the hardest), it produced expert-verified new results on 5 open problems — cutting the two-sided Bulow-Klemperer recruitment constant from ≥20,000 per side (Cai et al., STOC 2024) to exactly 2 sellers on the smaller side, improving the single-additive-buyer revenue approximation from 5.2 (Ma & Simchi-Levi 2021) to 3.52, removing the √2 factor between anytime and fixed-horizon regret with n experts, and proving a tight 1.5 price of anarchy for two-bidder autobidding auctions (prior best upper bound 1.8). All problems lie in the authors' own expertise and verification was by coauthor domain experts, not machine-checked proofs.

## 核心贡献

- **提出 Cogentic harness**：面向开放研究问题的多智能体证明发现系统，由编排者（orchestrator）、文献综述员、并行证明者（provers）、验证者（verifiers）、记录（record）、已验证引理账本（ledger）、过程顾问（advisor）和整合阶段组成；工作以"轮"为单位迭代，直到某份草稿通过全部验证或预算耗尽。
- **对抗式双验证 + 严格角色分工**：每份证明草稿先被单个验证者独立审读、再被所有验证者共同审读，且两者都从"每一步都是错的、每条引用都是错的"出发；编排者与过程顾问被**禁止持有任何数学观点**（不能猜答案、推荐技术或宣布方向有希望/已死），只做流程管理与日志趋势分析。
- **跨轮次记忆机制**：record 汇总每次尝试的目标、方法与被驳回的异议；ledger 由审计者在每轮结束时抽取被验证的中间引理、改写为自包含引理并隔离复验，供后续轮次免于重证；被反例排除的界被记为死路，防止重复探索。
- **5 项开放问题的专家验证新结果**（各配一篇伴随论文完整展开）：
  1. **高效在线反向线性优化**：首个高效且首个 proper 的 O(d) 遗憾界、对 T 一致、每轮 O(d²) 算术开销（此前高效最优为 O(d ln T)），距 Ω(√d) 下界差一个 O(√d) 因子；
  2. **双侧市场竞争复杂度**：STR(m, n+2) ≥ OPT(m, n)——只需在较小一侧招募 2 个卖家即可达到第一优交易收益（此前 Cai et al. 2024 需两侧各招 ≥20,000 个），且在 m=n=1 时紧；
  3. **n 专家 anytime 遗憾**：R_t ≤ (1+o(√(ln ln n/ln n)))·√(t ln n/2) 对所有 t 同时成立，解决了 anytime √(t ln n) 与固定时段 √(t ln n/2) 之间 √2 因子是否必要的公开问题（Harvey et al. 2024 猜想）；
  4. **单加性买家简单 vs 最优收入**：3.52·max(SRev, BRev) ≥ OPT，把 Ma & Simchi-Levi (2021) 的 5.2 改进到 3.52（下界为 2）；
  5. **autobidding 拍卖价格无序性**：pFPA₁ 对 2 竞拍者达到紧的 PoA=1.5（此前最好上界 1.8，匿名机制下界 2−4/(n+4)）；一般 n 下 pFPA₂ₙ 达到 PoA ≤ 2−1/(4n+1)，与下界在 1/n 项上相差常数因子。

## Key Contributions

- **A reusable harness for open research problems**: Cogentic structures automated proof discovery as rounds of planning → individual briefings → parallel drafting → two-stage adversarial verification, with a verified ledger of intermediate lemmas and a record of failed attempts as the cross-round memory. Long documents are passed as file paths so each prover agent reads selectively, keeping individual contexts small.
- **Adversarial verification with role hygiene**: every draft must clear a solo read and a joint read by verifiers who assume every step and citation is wrong until justified; the orchestrator and process advisor are explicitly barred from mathematical opinions, isolating process management from mathematical content.
- **Five expert-verified new theorems on open problems** (full developments in companion papers), each beating a named prior state of the art: efficient proper O(d) regret for online inverse linear optimization (uniform in T, vs prior efficient O(d ln T)); two-seller sufficiency for two-sided Bulow-Klemperer (vs ≥20,000 per side); matching leading constants for anytime vs fixed-horizon regret with n experts (resolving the √2 gap and the Harvey et al. 2024 conjecture); 3.52-approximation for simple vs optimal revenue with a single additive buyer (vs 5.2); and a tight 1.5 PoA for two-bidder autobidding via proportional first-price auctions (prior best 1.8), plus 2 − Ω(1/n) for general n.
- **Extreme inference frugality**: the reported results used Ø(100) model calls per problem (Ø(1000) for the hardest) — orders of magnitude below the compute associated with recent frontier agentic-science milestones, and the authors argue the headroom for scaling is deliberately left unused.

## Methodology

Cogentic is a group of agents sharing an on-disk workspace, coordinated by an orchestrator that decides which agent runs, when, and on what. The components: (1) the **orchestrator** tracks global state, partitions prover slots across research directions (a direction is a specific claim: a bound, a constant, a construction, a counterexample, or repair/finalization of a promising proof), spawns summarizers, evaluates verifier consensus, and manages the ledgers — it never performs mathematical derivations itself; (2) **literature reviewers** supply definitions and related work at the start and can be re-dispatched mid-run when attempts stall; (3) **provers** draft candidate proofs independently and in parallel from briefings; (4) **verifiers** critique drafts from complementary angles and scopes; (5) the **record** pools attempts by goal with the method used and the objection each failed on, while the **ledger** accumulates verified intermediate lemmas; (6) a **process advisor** reviews verification logs across the whole run and adjusts standing instructions (e.g., warning about a mistake several provers keep making); (7) a **consolidation stage** formats and audits the final manuscript.

A round proceeds as follows. The orchestrator plans how many provers to run and on which directions. For each prover, a summarizer reads all prior attempts and verdicts and writes an individual briefing; each summarizer works independently, so provers in the same round receive different readings of the same history, and longer documents are given as paths rather than quoted so provers open what they need selectively. Provers then draft in parallel. Each draft is verified alone, then all drafts are verified together; both verifiers are adversarial, starting from the assumption that every step is wrong until justified and every citation is incorrect until checked, and a draft is accepted only if it passes both. At round end, an auditor extracts confirmed fragments (lemmas verifiers accepted inside otherwise-rejected proofs), rewrites each as self-contained, and has it re-verified in isolation for the ledger; excluded bounds (killed by verified counterexamples) are recorded as dead ends. The process advisor then reviews logs from the run as a whole and tunes instructions and prover allocation for the next round — crucially, neither the advisor nor the orchestrator is permitted a mathematical opinion (no speculating on the answer, recommending a technique, or declaring a direction promising or dead). When a candidate clears all verification, the orchestrator may keep exploring remaining avenues; termination triggers a comparator that selects the strongest verified proof, a formal writer that expands it into a self-contained manuscript, and a final verification audit of the compiled document. Runs used Gemini 3.1 Pro or an early version of Gemini 4 Argon as the base model, starting from the bare problem statement with no human mathematical intervention; domain experts verified every proof afterwards.

## Results & Highlights

- **Online inverse linear optimization (Theorem 1, Gemini 3.1 Pro):** first efficient and first *proper* algorithm with cumulative shortfall R_T = O(d), uniform in the horizon T, at O(d²) arithmetic per round — vs the prior efficient best of O(d ln T) (Gollapudi et al. 2021; Sakaue et al. 2025b), i.e., the ln T factor is fully removed; the bound sits within an O(√d) factor of the Ω(√d) lower bound (Sakaue et al. 2025b). The proof trick is replacing the log det H_t potential (source of the ln T) with the trace power tr(H_t)^{1/2}. Subsequently, Sakaue (2026b) obtained the tight O(√d) but inefficient; an efficient O(√d) algorithm remains open.
- **Two-sided Bulow-Klemperer (Theorem 2, early Gemini 4 Argon):** STR(m, n+2) ≥ OPT(m, n) for all m ≥ n ≥ 1 under first-order stochastic dominance — the recruitment constant drops from at least 20,000 per side with both-sides recruitment (Cai et al. 2024) to exactly 2 traders recruited from the smaller side alone, and the result is tight (one extra seller does not suffice even at m = n = 1).
- **Anytime regret with n experts (Theorem 3, early Gemini 4 Argon):** an algorithm with no horizon knowledge achieves R_t ≤ (1 + o(√(ln ln n / ln n))) √(t ln n / 2) simultaneously for all t, closing the √2 factor between the best prior anytime guarantee √(t ln n) and the fixed-horizon optimum √(t ln n / 2) and confirming the Harvey et al. (2024) conjecture that the leading constants coincide as n → ∞. The proof is elementary: multiplicative-weights instances on a geometric grid, woken at ⌈δH^m⌉ and retired at ⌊H^m⌋, with ε = √(ln ln n/ln n), δ = ε³ making all overheads 1 + O(ε).
- **Simple vs optimal revenue, single additive buyer (Theorem 4, Gemini 3.1 Pro):** 3.52 · max(SRev, BRev) ≥ OPT, improving the prior 5.2 of Ma & Simchi-Levi (2021) (itself an improvement on Babaioff et al. 2020b's 6) toward the lower bound of 2 (Rubinstein 2016). The proof truncates at the joint scale M = max(SRev, BRev) instead of SRev, eliminating the Tail analysis entirely.
- **Price of anarchy for autobidding (Theorem 5, Gemini 3.1 Pro):** pFPA₁ achieves PoA ≤ 1.5 for 2 bidders and this is tight — any anonymous two-bidder mechanism (mild assumptions) has PoA ≥ 1.5 — beating the prior best upper bounds of 1.8 (Liaw et al. 2023, randomized FPA) and 1.89 (Mehta 2022); for general n ≥ 2, pFPA₂ₙ achieves PoA ≤ 2 − 1/(4n+1) = 2 − Ω(1/n), matching the 2 − 4/(n+4) anonymous-mechanism lower bound up to constant factors in the 1/n term.
- **Budget and independence:** all five runs used Ø(100) model calls per problem (Ø(1000) for the hardest); for Theorem 5, the authors had conjectured the 1.5 bound but had no proof, and had never studied the general-n part — the system independently proposed the pFPA₂ₙ mechanism and its analysis without hints. All results were subsequently verified by domain experts and published in full in companion papers.

## Authors & Credibility

Seven authors at Google Research, listed alphabetically (Yang Cai also Yale University; Vineet Gupta also Google DeepMind): Yang Cai, Vineet Gupta, Yanchen Jiang, Christopher Liaw, Aranyak Mehta, Grigorios Velegkas, Di Wang. The team's credibility in the exact problem domains is high and verifiable: the reference list and companion papers include Cai–Devanur–Weinberg's STOC 2016 duality framework for Bayesian mechanism design, Cai–Liaw–Mehta–Zhao's STOC 2024 two-sided markets paper (the source of the 20,000-agent baseline they now improve), and Liaw–Mehta–Perloth's WWW 2023 autobidding work (the 1.8 PoA baseline). In other words, the humans vetting the AI's proofs are among the people who set the prior state of the art on these exact open problems. The work is positioned against contemporaneous agentic-mathematics efforts (Stellar Colossus, ProofCouncil, Anthropic/OpenAI mathematical milestones) and maintains a public results page (https://sites.google.com/view/cogentic).

## Critical Assessment

- **No ablations, no baselines, no head-to-head comparison.** The paper explicitly states "We do not attempt to give a comprehensive survey or compare these efforts," and nowhere compares Cogentic against a single-prover long-context run, plain repeated sampling, multi-agent debate, or sibling harnesses (Stellar Colossus, ProofCouncil) on the same problems. As a result, the source of the wins cannot be isolated: it is unclear how much comes from the verified ledger, the adversarial two-verifier gate, or the process advisor, versus simply Gemini 3.1 Pro with Ø(100) parallel attempts per problem. For a paper whose main claim is architectural, this is the single biggest weakness.
- **The evaluation set is the authors' own research program.** All 5 problems continue lines the authors themselves started or worked on (Cai et al. 2016 duality; Cai et al. 2024 two-sided markets; Liaw et al. 2023 autobidding), and for Theorem 5 the paper admits "the authors had already suspected" the 1.5 bound and "their conjecture was that the bound is 1.5." That is a favorable setup: the problems are guaranteed to be well-posed, tractable, and in-distribution for the verifiers. The success rate over *attempted* (not just solved) problems is never reported, so survivorship bias cannot be quantified — we do not know how many open problems Cogentic was pointed at and failed on.
- **Verification is by coauthors, not independent parties or proof assistants.** Every proof was "independently verified by domain experts," but those experts are the author team and their collaborators, and the outputs are natural-language proofs that no machine checker has sealed. The Discussion honestly notes the human coauthors "checked the argument, wrote the exposition around it, and in some cases carried it further than the harness had" — which blurs how much of the final companion-paper result is literally the harness's output.
- **Reproducibility is limited.** One base model is described only as "an early version of Gemini 4 Argon" — an unreleased, under-specified model; prompts, round counts, retry statistics, and failure transcripts are not reported, and the mathematics itself lives in companion papers. Nobody outside Google can currently re-run this.
- **Small scope caveats in the theorems themselves:** the general-n PoA upper bound (Theorem 5, part 2) holds only under an undominated-bids assumption, and the flagship O(d) regret bound is still a factor O(√d) from optimal — the paper is appropriately upfront about both, which speaks well of its honesty.

## Strengths

- The results are real, quantified improvements over named prior state of the art on genuinely open research problems: 20,000 → 2 (recruitment constant), 5.2 → 3.52 (revenue approximation), √2 factor eliminated (anytime regret), O(d ln T) → O(d) (first efficient proper bound), 1.8 → tight 1.5 (PoA) — each independently verified and developed into companion papers, not just claimed.
- Extreme inference frugality (Ø(100)–Ø(1000) calls per problem) makes the cost profile concrete and contrasts favorably with brute-force agentic-science efforts; the design deliberately leaves headroom to scale to harder problems.
- The harness embodies a thoughtful reliability recipe for long-horizon agents: adversarial verification biased toward rejection, a verified ledger so work is never redone, a dead-end registry so failed directions are never revisited, and a strict firewall that forbids the coordination agents from holding mathematical opinions — an unusually clean separation of control flow from content.
- Demonstrated independence in at least one case: the system proposed the pFPA₂ₙ mechanism and its analysis for a part of Theorem 5 the authors had never studied and received no hints on.
- Honest Discussion section: it acknowledges human post-processing of the manuscripts, the expert-verification bottleneck, and the widening gap between proof throughput and human understanding.

## Limitations

- Only five problems, all drawn from the authors' own subfields (online learning, auction theory, mechanism design); there is no standardized benchmark and no reported failure/success-rate statistics over attempted problems.
- Quality assurance rests on coauthor domain experts reading natural-language proofs — no Lean/Isabelle formalization — which caps throughput and leaves residual risk of subtle undetected errors; the authors themselves flag the throughput-vs-understanding tension.
- No component ablations, no baseline harness comparisons, and no analysis of failed runs; the architectural contribution is asserted rather than measured.
- Dependence on proprietary frontier models (Gemini 3.1 Pro and an unreleased "early Gemini 4 Argon") plus unreported prompting details makes independent reproduction currently impossible.
- The full mathematical content is deferred to companion papers, so this document alone neither contains the proofs nor enough run-level detail to audit the system's behavior.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- What is the marginal contribution of each component — verified ledger, adversarial dual verification, process advisor, parallel directions — measured against a single-prover baseline at matched compute? An ablation would turn the architectural narrative into evidence.
- What fraction of attempted open problems ended in failure, and what do the failure modes look like (verification deadlock, dead-end saturation, budget exhaustion)? This determines whether the Ø(100)-call results generalize or reflect favorable problem selection.
- Can the verified-ledger + dead-end-registry pattern transfer to empirical ML research loops, where "verification" is noisy experiment replication rather than proof checking?
- Would formalizing the final proofs in a proof assistant like Lean close the trust gap cheaply enough to preserve the harness's throughput advantage?
- How does Cogentic compare head-to-head with sibling agentic-math systems (Stellar Colossus, ProofCouncil, harmonic's approaches) on a common problem set at equal inference budget?
