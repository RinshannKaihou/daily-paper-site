Why this matters

Puts the self-improvement loop inside model weights, not the scaffold — and its skills-vs-training ablation is a template for auditing self-improving-agent claims.

正中 “Self-Evolving Agents & Automated Research” 优先方向：自我改进回路内化进权重，元技能跨域迁移有实证支撑。

Critical edge

The headline is scaffold-driven: skills + rounds alone lift the untrained base 28.8 → 68.2 on MLE-Lite. Training’s share is only +13.6 — real, but the minority component.

Other labs’ systems are not compared with an equivalent skills harness, and HLE rows mix full-set vs text-only subsets.

01

Methodology

distilled
total improvement = r̄ · T* per-round gain (reflection) × effective horizon (long-horizon execution)

Self-improvement is formalized as test-time scaling: an environment is a (task, scoring function) pair, and the round budget is given to the agent — the policy learns how much of it to use productively.

Environments with guaranteed headroom. Compiled from GitHub repos and online judges; admitted only if the reference solution runs and a naive baseline scores far below it. Graded (non-binary) scores are preferred so every round is informative.

Trajectories that keep the messy middle. A teacher agent works each task for hours and hundreds of tool calls — discovering operational knowledge itself, with a scored submission ending every round. Whole trajectories are kept (failures, regressions, abandoned plans), but loss lands only on decisions that move the solution forward.

Controlled transfer. Qwen3.8-27B fine-tuned on these trajectories plus the AREX deep-research data, left unchanged — so cross-domain gains read as transfer.

05

Strengths

  • Clean formal handle: r̄·T* decomposition structures the entire evaluation, in both feedback and no-feedback regimes.
  • Whole-trajectory selection with loss masked to progress-making decisions directly targets the “how solutions improve” signal that outcome-only SFT misses.
  • Environment admission gate (reference runs; naive baseline far below) mechanically guarantees improvement headroom.
  • Transfer is well-controlled by design: deep-research data byte-identical to the prior recipe.
  • Unusually transparent ablation and candid reporting where the model loses.
06

Limitations

  • Both training domains rely on programmatic verifiers; open-ended domains (research, writing) are asserted as future work, not tested.
  • The round budget is handed to the agent, not learned — scaling may partly reflect being told the budget.
  • Hours per task, >100 turns per question, and no cost analysis; the “optimized harness” on Frontier-CS is unspecified.
  • Reproducibility gaps: no environment/trajectory/token counts, unnamed teacher model, no hyperparameters; single base model, single run.
  • HLE comparisons mix full-set and text-only subsets; GAIA contamination risk unaddressed.
07

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does r̄·T* hold quantitatively — can T* and r̄ be measured per model on held-out environments, and do they predict transfer?
  2. How much of the +13.6 training gain survives without task-specific skills at test time — is the model compensating for a missing scaffold?
  3. Would trajectory-level selection (keeping failures) beat outcome-only SFT at matched data budget, on the same environments?
  4. Can the recipe extend to noisy or delayed reward, where the reference-vs-naive admission gate has no analogue?
  5. What is the inference-cost frontier: accuracy per dollar at 1 h vs 5 h vs 12 h, versus larger non-self-improving models at standard budgets?
