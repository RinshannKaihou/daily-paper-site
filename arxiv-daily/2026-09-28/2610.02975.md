> **一句话总结：** 提出 CISE（Conformal Interval-Driven Self-Evolution），用条件共形推断（CCI）+ 逐迭代在线密度比估计（DRE）把 LLM 自演化搜索中廉价但不可靠的代理奖励升级为候选特定的校准奖励区间，只有**整个区间**都落在任务可行域内的候选才被输出；在 LLEMA 材料发现基准的三个任务上输出精确率均达 100%（0 假阳性），而最强基线 LLEMA 的 DFT 验证失败率为 7.84%（WBG）和 31.7%（SSE），等预算对比下 CISE 以 6 TP/0 FP 击败 LLEMA 点估计选材的 3 TP/3 FP。

> **TL;DR:** In LLM self-evolving search, cheap proxy rewards produce false positives that contaminate both the archive (feedback loop) and the final output. CISE replaces point-valued proxy rewards with candidate-specific calibrated intervals built from conditional conformal inference plus per-iteration online density-ratio estimation, and returns a candidate only if its entire interval satisfies the task constraints. On three tasks of the LLEMA materials-discovery benchmark (validated by DFT), CISE achieves 100% precision (0 false positives among 41/51/6 returned candidates) versus the LLEMA baseline's 92.2% (WBG), 68% (SSE), and 65% (PV) precision (8–35% of its outputs fail DFT), and under an equal 6-candidate validation budget on the PV task CISE delivers 6 true positives vs LLEMA's 3 TP / 3 FP — at the cost of returning far fewer candidates.

## 核心贡献

- **问题形式化：假阳性传播（false-positive propagation）。** 自演化搜索中代理奖励（如材料领域用性质预测模型代替昂贵的 DFT）会给违反真实约束的候选打高分；一旦假阳性被存入档案并作为"成功范例"反馈给求解器 LLM，它会系统性地把后续搜索引向代理奖励过于乐观的区域——错误不仅污染最终输出，还会被 LLM 在生成依据中显式复用（论文 Figure 3 给出了 LLM 日志中引用先前代理评估的直接证据）。
- **方法：** CISE 将点值代理奖励替换为候选特定奖励区间：(1) 离线阶段用带真实奖励标签的源数据拟合"代理误差的条件分位数模型"（0.95 分位）并构建校准集；(2) 演化中用条件共形推断（Gibbs & Candès 2025 的 CCI）构造有限维函数空间下的区间，区间中心在代理预测处、宽度自适应于候选特征；(3) 反馈阶段对区间做保守评估（取区间上的最坏情形），输出阶段要求**整条区间**满足全部属性约束（下界约束用区间下端点、上界约束用上端点检验），空区间则弃权。
- **共形 + 漂移校正的组合：** 演化会改变候选分布（archive、父代、生成策略随迭代变化），CISE 每轮用无标签探针批次（每上下文 8 个）做最小二乘重要性拟合（Kanamori et al. 2009）在线估计当前分布与源分布的密度比，并把估计比率作为额外基函数加入 CCI 校准空间（G_t = G + span(ŵ_t)），使校准随当前代际分布自适应。
- **理论保证：** Theorem 1：可交换性下逐迭代边际覆盖率 ≥ 1−α；Theorem 2：在协变量偏移（条件奖励律不变、有界重叠）下覆盖率 ≥ 1−α−ε_t，其中 ε_t 是真实候选边际与比率诱导分布间的全变差距离（刻画 DRE 误差的影响）；"被接受且为假阳性"的联合概率 ≤ α(+ε_t)。作者明确这些是**逐迭代边际**界，不覆盖整条轨迹、也不以接受为条件。
- **实证：** 在 LLEMA 基准三个材料发现任务上（WBG/SSE/PV），CISE 返回的全部候选经 DFT 复验均为真阳性（0 假阳性），并在等验证预算实验中证明收益不能仅归因于"返回得更少"。据作者称这是首个将共形代理奖励区间同时用于演化反馈与最终候选选择的框架。

## Key Contributions

- Formalizes **false-positive propagation** in LLM-based self-evolving search: proxy-qualified but truly infeasible candidates are stored in the archive as "successes" and reused as feedback, steering subsequent generations toward regions where the proxy reward is optimistic (with LLM-log evidence in Figure 3 that the solver explicitly cites prior surrogate evaluations when proposing children).
- **CISE**: candidate-specific calibrated reward intervals from conditional conformal inference (CCI) over a finite-dimensional calibration space, with the interval used conservatively for evolutionary feedback and the strict "entire interval must satisfy all constraints" rule for final output selection.
- **Iteration-wise online density-ratio estimation** (least-squares importance fitting on an 8-candidate probe batch per iteration) folded into the calibration space as an extra basis function, so conformal coverage adapts to the distribution shift induced by evolution itself.
- **Theory**: Theorem 1 gives iteration-wise marginal coverage ≥ 1−α under exchangeability; Theorem 2 extends to evolutionary covariate shift with coverage ≥ 1−α−ε_t, where ε_t is the total-variation distance attributable to density-ratio estimation error; the joint (accepted ∧ false-positive) probability is bounded by α(+ε_t) — honestly scoped as marginal and per-iteration only.
- **Experiments** on three LLEMA material-search tasks with full DFT re-evaluation: zero false positives among all CISE outputs on every task, plus an equal-budget comparison showing the reliability gain persists when the baseline gets the same downstream validation budget.

## Methodology

**Setup.** A self-evolving agent searches a candidate space X for solutions meeting task constraints; each candidate x has an expensive true reward r(x) (DFT) and a cheap, imperfect proxy reward r̂(x). An archive H_t stores candidates with their rewards; a parent-selection policy picks K parents per iteration to prompt the solver LLM for a batch of M new candidates. Membership in the feasible sets F̂ (proxy) vs F (true) defines proxy hits and false positives. CISE assumes the generator, evolutionary operators, and proxy model are **fixed** — it modifies only archive administration and output selection on top of LLEMA.

**Offline preparation.** From a labeled source dataset D = {(X_i, r(X_i))} of candidate–true-reward pairs (JARVIS-QETB), three disjoint splits: D_fit (423,205 structures) trains a conditional quantile model q̂(x) predicting the 0.95th quantile of the absolute proxy error |r(x) − r̂(x)| via gradient-boosted regression on 27 structural features plus the proxy prediction; D_cal (10,000 structures, split from D_fit at the composition-group level to avoid overlap) provides calibration scores S_i = |u_i − q̂(x_i)| for the nonconformity measure S(x, u) = |u − r̂(x)| − q̂(x); D_src is the source reference for density-ratio estimation.

**Conformal interval construction.** Following Gibbs & Candès (2025), CCI calibrates scores through an augmented quantile regression that includes the fresh candidate with an imputed test score (pinball loss) over a finite-dimensional linear function space G containing the constant function and fixed functions of the candidate representation φ(x). The dual solution η_t(X') yields a nonrandomized score cutoff τ̂_t(X'), and the reward interval I_t(X') = {u : S(X', u) ≤ τ̂_t(X')} is centered at r̂(x), bounded, with candidate-specific width; an empty interval leads to abstention.

**Conservative interval use.** Feedback: parent selection evaluates stored candidates conservatively over their nonempty bounded intervals (worst case over the interval). Output: a candidate is added to the cumulative output O_t only if **every** value in I_t(x) satisfies the task constraints — lower-bound constraints checked via the lower endpoint, upper-bound via the upper endpoint — so a wide interval can exclude a candidate whose point estimate passes. For multi-property tasks, CISE is applied per property and only candidates satisfying all per-property criteria are returned; α is set to 0.1/#constraints per property (e.g., α = 0.05 per property on the two-property WBG task).

**Online density-ratio estimation.** Evolution induces covariate shift: P_t(r|X) = P_0(r|X) but P_t^X ≠ P_0^X with a bounded-overlap ratio w_t(x). Each iteration, an unlabeled probe batch B_t (8 probes per generation context, generated under the current parent configuration) is used with D_src in a regularized least-squares importance fit (Kanamori et al. 2009) over a bounded ratio model class; the normalized estimate ŵ_t enters the calibration space as G_t = G + span(ŵ_t), connecting the calibration adjustment to the current generation distribution. Theorem 2 then gives coverage ≥ 1−α−ε_t with ε_t the TV distance between the true candidate marginal and the ratio-induced distribution.

**Experimental setup.** Three multi-constraint tasks from the LLEMA benchmark: wide-bandgap semiconductors (WBG: E_g ≥ 2.5 eV, E_form ≤ −1.1 eV/atom), solid-state electrolytes (SSE: E_form ≤ −1.0 eV/atom, E_g ≥ 2.0 eV, plus a mobile species from {Li, Na, K, Mg, Cu, Al}), and photovoltaic absorbers (PV: 0.7 ≤ E_g ≤ 2.0 eV, E_form ≤ 0.0 eV/atom, earth-abundant non-toxic elements). Each method generates 200 candidates. Baselines: MatterGen, LLMatDesign, and LLEMA (CISE's host). CISE swaps CGCNN for MGT as the band-gap predictor, keeps gpt-5-mini as the solver LLM, and validates all outputs with DFT in Quantum ESPRESSO (PBEsol, Modified GBVR pseudopotentials) — a proxy hit failing this final evaluation is a false positive.

## Results & Highlights

- **WBG task (LLEMA benchmark, 200 candidates per method, DFT ground truth):** CISE returns 41/200 candidates with 41 true positives and 0 false positives (100.0% precision), versus LLEMA's 102/200 outputs at 92.2% precision (94 TP, 8 FP — 7.84% fail DFT validation) and LLMatDesign's 37/200 outputs of which **all 37 are false positives** (0.0% precision); MatterGen produces only 3 outputs (2 TP, 1 FP).
- **SSE task:** CISE returns 51/200 candidates, all 51 confirmed true positives by DFT (0 FP), versus LLEMA's 104/200 with 71 TP and 33 FP (68.3% precision — 31.7% of its outputs fail DFT); MatterGen and LLMatDesign return 2 outputs each with 1 TP and 0 TP respectively.
- **PV task:** CISE returns 6/200 candidates, all 6 true positives, versus LLEMA's 60/200 with 39 TP and 21 FP (65.0% precision, 35% false-positive rate); LLMatDesign's 18 outputs are all false positives and MatterGen produces 0 qualifying outputs.
- **Equal-budget comparison (Table 2, PV task):** restricted to the same 6-candidate DFT validation budget, LLEMA's point-estimate top-6 (proxy-predicted E_g closest to 1.2 eV, e.g., SrO₂ proxy 1.24 eV vs DFT 2.99 eV; CaO 1.75 vs 3.06 eV) yield 3 TP / 3 FP (50% precision), while all 6 CISE candidates are DFT-confirmed true positives (e.g., RbLiO with interval [0.73, 1.98] eV and DFT E_g = 1.24 eV; NaLi₄MgP₂ interval [0.83, 1.86] eV, DFT 1.66 eV) — the reliability gain cannot be explained solely by returning fewer candidates.
- **Trajectory quality (Figure 2, SSE task):** LLEMA's DFT-evaluated candidates increasingly accumulate above the E_form ≤ −1.0 eV/atom feasibility threshold in later iterations (including *selected* false positives), whereas CISE shows no comparable accumulation and its output mean stays inside the feasible region throughout evolution — evidence that interval rewards suppress over-optimistic proxy evaluations from being repeatedly reinforced by the LLM.
- **Theory:** nominal per-iteration coverage ≥ 1−α (α = 0.05 per property on two-constraint tasks), degraded by at most ε_t under covariate shift; the implied false-positive bound on (accepted ∧ FP) is α + ε_t.

## Authors & Credibility

Kangjun Noh, Soyu Kim, and Kyungwoo Song are from Yonsei University (MLAI lab), with Song as corresponding author. The group has prior, directly relevant work on calibrated LLM evaluation: Noh, Kim, and Song have an ICML-2026 workshop paper on "Reliable LLM-as-a-judge evaluation via transferable calibration," and they cite their own conformal LLM-as-a-judge line — so the conformal-calibration machinery is their home turf rather than a borrowed technique. The evaluation leans on credible external infrastructure: the LLEMA benchmark (Abhyankar et al., published via Nature co-authorship 2026), standard DFT tooling (Quantum ESPRESSO/PBEsol), and the JARVIS-QETB database for calibration labels. Code is released (github.com/MLAI-Yonsei/CISE.git), which supports reproducibility in principle. The statistical content (CCI, least-squares importance fitting) is built on well-established published methods (Gibbs & Candès 2025; Kanamori et al. 2009) rather than novel statistics, which limits novelty risk but also means correctness of the composition rests on their own proofs — which appear complete in the appendix.

## Critical Assessment

- **Only one competitive baseline.** MatterGen and LLMatDesign are generative materials-design models not optimized for these constraint tasks — LLMatDesign scores 0 true positives on all three tasks (all 37/2/18 outputs are false positives) — so they inflate the baseline table without providing signal. The entire empirical comparison effectively reduces to CISE vs LLEMA, and CISE **is** LLEMA with two modules changed (archive administration, output selection). That makes the comparison well-controlled but also narrow: one benchmark family, one solver LLM (gpt-5-mini), one proxy model. Nothing shows the framework transfers beyond materials search despite the framing being about self-evolving search in general.
- **Missing ablations.** There is no CISE-without-DRE ablation (plain CCI under shift), no sensitivity sweep over α or probe-batch size, and — most importantly — **the empirical coverage of the intervals is never measured**: the paper reports only the downstream FP rate of outputs, never the observed fraction of true rewards falling inside I_t versus the nominal 1−α, nor the magnitude of ε_t. The theoretical degradation term is therefore empirically unvalidated, and we cannot tell whether Theorem 2's guarantee is tight or vacuously loose in practice.
- **"True reward" is still a proxy.** Ground truth is DFT under Quantum ESPRESSO/PBEsol, while proxy labels come from the JARVIS-QETB DFT database — a "true positive" means two DFT pipelines agree, not experimental validation. The proxy error being calibrated is partly a cross-pipeline/code/pseudopotential discrepancy, which may be systematically easier to model than proxy-vs-reality error; FP rates under genuinely experimental ground truth could be worse.
- **Strong, untestable assumptions.** The covariate-shift assumption (conditional reward law preserved while only the candidate marginal shifts) is precisely what fails if the agent drifts into extrapolation territory where the proxy model errs *conditionally* — arguably the most dangerous regime for self-evolving search. The guarantee is also marginal and per-iteration only, not simultaneous over the T-iteration trajectory nor conditional on acceptance; with T iterations and per-iteration α ≈ 0.05, trajectory-level false-positive control is not established (the authors admit this in the appendix). No multiple seeds or variance estimates are reported for the headline Table 1 numbers.
- **The yield cost is real and understated on PV.** Returning 6 candidates vs LLEMA's 60 (a 10× reduction) is framed as a "reliability-yield trade-off," which is honest, but the paper offers no analysis of interval widths or of how the strict two-sided interval rule (rather than, e.g., a probability-constrained rule) drives the conservatism. In Table 2 the LLEMA prioritization heuristic (closest to 1.2 eV) was chosen by the authors; a margin- or ensemble-based prioritization might close part of the gap.
- **Practical deployment friction.** The method requires ~433k labeled candidate–true-reward pairs (10k of them with *true* rewards for calibration) — in genuinely novel discovery domains, such true-reward labels are exactly the expensive resource the pipeline is trying to avoid, and the paper does not test how little calibration data suffices.

## Strengths

- **Perfect empirical precision where it matters:** 0 false positives across all 98 returned candidates (41 WBG + 51 SSE + 6 PV) with full DFT re-evaluation, while the strongest baseline leaks 7.8–35% false positives that demonstrably contaminate its own feedback loop.
- **Mechanism, not just metrics:** Figure 3 shows concrete LLM logs where LLEMA's solver explicitly reuses earlier proxy evaluations across a candidate lineage (Na₂O → Li₂O → Na₂O, all DFT-false-positive) — direct evidence for the false-positive-propagation thesis; the corresponding CISE intervals exclude those candidates.
- **Well-controlled comparison design:** changing only archive administration and output selection of LLEMA isolates the intervention; the equal-budget Table 2 experiment directly answers the "you just return fewer" objection.
- **Honest theoretical scoping:** the paper repeatedly and explicitly states the guarantee is marginal, per-iteration, and covers the joint (acceptance ∧ false-positive) event rather than precision conditional on acceptance — rarer candor than typical.
- **Clean formalization** of false-positive propagation that applies to any self-improvement loop with learned or LLM-judged rewards, with released code and public calibration data.

## Limitations

- **Yield collapse on some tasks:** CISE returns 41 vs LLEMA's 102 (WBG), 51 vs 104 (SSE), and 6 vs 60 (PV); in settings where downstream validation is cheap, the point-estimate-plus-filter strategy may dominate on total true positives per wall-clock/compute dollar.
- **Guarantee scope:** marginal per-iteration coverage only; no simultaneous-over-trajectory bound, no bound on precision conditional on acceptance, and the covariate-shift term ε_t is never measured empirically.
- **Narrow empirical base:** a single benchmark family (LLEMA materials tasks), a single solver LLM (gpt-5-mini), one proxy model per property; no evidence yet of transfer to program discovery, hypothesis generation, or LLM-as-a-judge reward loops despite the general framing.
- **Calibration-data dependency:** requires a large offline reservoir of true-reward labels (10k calibration pairs, 423k fit pairs); untested at small calibration budgets, which is the practically relevant regime.
- **Assumption risk:** conditional-reward-preservation under shift (P_t(r|X) = P_0(r|X)) plus bounded overlap are unverifiable at runtime; systematic proxy failure under extrapolative drift would violate them silently.
- **Interval conservatism is unanalyzed:** no study of interval width distributions or adaptive α, leaving the 3% output yield on PV unexplained and un-tunable with the information given.

## Relevance

该论文与第一优先方向 Self-Evolving Agents & Automated Research 高度契合：它直击自演化 LLM 搜索的核心可靠性问题——廉价代理奖励的假阳性经档案与反馈回路传播放大，并用共形区间给出带统计保证、可审计的解法，还用 LLM 日志实证了错误反馈被显式复用的机制。虽然实验域是材料发现，但"用校准奖励区间约束自我改进循环、从源头抑制 reward-hacking 式正反馈"的思路可迁移到任何以学习型/LLM-as-a-judge 奖励驱动的自主研究循环，对关注长时程自主智能体可信性的研究有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. What is the empirically observed coverage of I_t (fraction of true rewards inside the interval) versus the nominal 1−α, and how large is ε_t in practice — is the DRE correction load-bearing or negligible on these tasks? (A CISE-without-DRE ablation would answer this directly.)
2. Does the covariate-shift assumption survive adversarial drift — i.e., when the agent deliberately probes regions where the proxy model extrapolates badly, does conditional-reward preservation fail, and would the pipeline signal this (e.g., via widening intervals) or silently lose coverage?
3. How does the reliability-yield trade-off behave as a function of α and downstream validation cost — is there a principled rule for choosing α (or an adaptive one) that maximizes expected true positives per DFT call rather than per returned candidate?
4. Can the framework transfer to self-evolving loops with learned or LLM-as-a-judge rewards (program discovery, hypothesis generation), where "true reward" labels for calibration are far scarcer than JARVIS-QETB's 423k pairs — how little calibration data does CISE need to retain useful guarantees?
5. The guarantee is marginal and per-iteration; can it be strengthened to trajectory-level or acceptance-conditional bounds (e.g., via union bounds with α/T, or anytime-valid/conformal-evarieties of coverage) at acceptable conservatism?
