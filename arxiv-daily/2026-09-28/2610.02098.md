> **一句话总结：** 论文揭示机械可解释性中的"目标层恢复鸿沟"（objective-level recovery gap）：作为电路发现选择标准的干预定义忠实度（intervention-defined faithfulness）会因被排除信号替换造成的"上下文扭曲"（context distortion）而系统性偏好行为复现更差的等规模电路——在 IOI/Greater-Than/Docstring/Acronym 四个人类研究任务与 InterpBench 十任务上，EAP、EAP-IG、ACDC、Edge-SP 四种发现方法的真实输出池在重采样干预下候选间 KL 误排率高达 9.4%–41.2%（InterpBench task 93 在零替换下 R–C 误排 70.0%、被偏好电路平均损失 63.55 pp 行为一致性 Q），而仅把部分被排除信号的输入恢复为完整模型缓存激活即可在保持电路结构与其行为分数不变的前提下修复 100 个持续性误排中的 96 个，证明"更好的发现算法"并不等于"恢复了机制"。

> **TL;DR:** This paper shows that the standard evaluation objective of circuit discovery — intervention-defined faithfulness — can prefer an equally sized circuit that reproduces model behavior *worse*, an "objective-level recovery gap" caused by context distortion (replacing excluded signals with donor draws, means, or zeros changes the inputs of retained components relative to the intact model). Across the 4 human-reference tasks (IOI, Greater-Than, Docstring, Acronym) and the 10-task InterpBench panel, KL misranking among actual discovery outputs of EAP, EAP-IG, ACDC, and Edge-SP spans 9.4%–41.2% of candidate–candidate pairs under ordinary resampling alone (ACDC 41.2%, EAP-IG 35.5%, EAP 20.0%, Edge-SP 9.4% on the Human suite); on Greater-Than the C–C rate rises from 3.4% (resampling) to 54.0% (zero replacement), and on InterpBench task 93 the misrank-preferred circuit loses on average 63.55 pp of behavioral agreement Q. Restoring just a fraction of excluded incoming signals to intact-model cached activations repairs 96 of 100 persistent misrankings while leaving circuits' original behavioral scores unchanged — so better discovery alone cannot guarantee mechanism recovery when the selection objective rewards the wrong candidate.

## 核心贡献

- **提出"目标层恢复鸿沟"概念**：将电路发现中的选择失败从"搜索/归因不够好"重新定位到"评价目标本身奖励错误候选"——干预定义的忠实度分数可以偏好行为复现更差的等规模电路，这是目标函数层面（objective-level）的缺陷，而非算法层面的缺陷。
- **无发现算法的受控实验**：对参考电路做等规模的受控编辑（保留/排除组件互换 1%–75%，10 个种子）即可产生大量误排，且误排率与编辑幅度非单调（Docstring 的 R–C 误排率从最小编辑带的 100% 降到最大编辑带的 0%），排除了"搜索质量差"作为解释。
- **在真实发现输出上验证**：EAP、EAP-IG、ACDC、Edge-SP 四种成熟方法的输出池全部包含误排对；重采样下人类任务 C–C KL 误排率为 9.4%–41.2%，池化后人类任务误排率（20.9%/30.9%/38.1%）系统性高于半合成 InterpBench（9.8%/16.2%/26.8%）。
- **上下文恢复的因果干预**：把选中接收节点上被排除的入边信号替换为完整模型（intact-model）在该节点处的缓存激活，在保持电路结构、原始行为分数与捐赠者分配完全不变的前提下，修复 96/100 个同时在验证集与独立测试集上成立的持续性 KL 误排，将上下文扭曲确立为误排的一个成因。
- **跨指标、跨干预、跨基准的系统检验**：KL、LD、PD 三种忠实度度量全部出现误排（零替换 R–C：KL 29.5%、LD 18.1%、PD 25.1%）；识别出"识别参考电路"不等于"可靠排序候选"（InterpBench task 97 无任何 R–C 误排但 C–C 达 5.2%–22.0%），并给出评估实践建议：固定行为判据 Q 应与忠实度一同报告，并附干预与电路规模控制。

## Key Contributions

- Formalizes the **objective-level recovery gap**: intervention-defined faithfulness — the standard selection criterion in circuit discovery — can prefer an equally sized circuit that reproduces intact-model behavior less well, because executing a circuit under an intervention introduces *context distortion* (retained components receive inputs that differ from intact-model execution).
- Demonstrates the failure **without any discovery algorithm**: equal-size controlled edits of reference circuits (1%–75% of retained components swapped) produce misrankings under resampling, mean, and zero replacement; misranking is non-monotonic in edit magnitude (Greater-Than zero replacement misranks every R–C pair in the 50% band, while Docstring R–C rates fall from 100% in the smallest band to zero in the two largest).
- Shows the same failure in **real discovery outputs**: candidate pools from EAP, EAP-IG, ACDC, and Edge-SP all contain misranked pairs — under resampling alone, C–C KL misranking ranges 9.4%–41.2% across the four methods on human-reference tasks, and pooled Human-suite rates (20.9%/30.9%/38.1% under resampling/mean/zero) exceed InterpBench's (9.8%/16.2%/26.8%), showing semi-synthetic benchmarks alone understate the problem.
- Provides a **causal intervention on the evaluation context**: restoring selected excluded incoming signals to intact-model cached activations — with circuits, original behavioral scores, and donor assignments held fixed — repairs 96 of 100 persistent KL misrankings, supporting context distortion as a contributor.
- Distills **evaluation guidance**: report a fixed behavioral criterion Q (held-out agreement, including intact-model mistakes) alongside faithfulness, with intervention and circuit-size controls; avoid concluding reliability from zero R–C rates (task 97 has none yet misranks up to 22.0% C–C).

## Methodology

**Setup.** Let M be the intact model and S an executable circuit mask. Executing M_S under intervention α replaces excluded signals following standard ablation choices: **resampling** (three shared donor activation draws), **mean replacement** (frozen mean bank), and **zero replacement**. All primary comparisons are **size-controlled** — each pair uses the same number of components in the declared representation — with duplicate masks deduplicated, so ranking reversals cannot be attributed to circuit size.

**Two scoring objectives.**
- *Validation faithfulness* F: primary metric is negative output-distribution KL (F^KL = −E[D_KL(p_M(·|x) ‖ p_S(·|x))]) averaged over validation prompts and three shared donor draws; logit difference (LD) and probability difference (PD) serve as task-based margin alternatives.
- *Independent behavioral criterion* Q: agreement between the circuit's answers and the intact model's answers on held-out prompts T, under ordinary resampling, aggregated within each prompt then over prompts. Agreement **includes intact-model mistakes** (a circuit faithfully reproducing the model's errors is behaviorally better), and Q is held fixed whenever the intervention or score changes. Greater-Than uses semantic accuracy of predicted year endings.

**Misranking.** For a fixed pair with B the higher-Q circuit and W the lower-Q one, a misranking occurs when F(W) > F(B) (ΔQ > 0, ΔF > 0). **R–C** pairs compare a reference against an edited/discovered candidate; **C–C** pairs compare two candidates. Small absolute and relative tolerance thresholds exclude numerical ties (documented in the appendix).

**Study 1 — Controlled candidates (Section 3).** Starting from each reference circuit, edit hands are generated by swapping 1, 5, 10, 20, 50, or 75% of retained components with the same number of excluded ones, with ten seeds per band; identical masks are deduplicated. References are compared with candidates (R–C) and candidates with one another (C–C) under all three interventions. Four human-reference tasks (IOI, Greater-Than, Docstring, Acronym) plus the corrected ten-task InterpBench panel (tasks 113, 97, 2, 82, 111, 45, 58, 93, 103, 25).

**Study 2 — Discovery outputs (Section 4).** Candidates come from EAP, EAP-IG, ACDC, and Edge-SP, each run per task on 100 discovery prompts with a shared seed disjoint from evaluation prompts; validation prompts, valid-position masks, and donor assignments are reused from Study 1 so only the candidate source changes. ACDC and Edge-SP are capped at 300 seconds per run. Comparisons stay within one task and one method and require equal executable sizes.

**Study 3 — Context restoration (Section 5).** From the Section-4 discovery pool, 100 equal-size circuit-pair/intervention cases are selected: 50 Human + 50 InterpBench, spanning 25 resampling, 50 mean, 25 zero, each with a Q deficit ≥ 0.5 pp and strict KL misranking on **both** validation and independent-test prompts. At selected recipient nodes, excluded incoming connections are restored to the intact-model activations cached at the recipient prompt (a *partial* restoration: 80%, 40%, 20%, then 10% of the original eligible group, up to five predetermined levels with tree search). Repairs are confirmed on the independent test prompts under their own intact signals and original donors; masks, weights, unrestored donor assignments, mean banks, prompts, and test execution order remain fixed.

## Results & Highlights

- **Discovery outputs misrank under the mildest intervention.** On the Human suite under resampling alone, C–C KL misranking among size-controlled pairs is 41.2% for ACDC, 35.5% for EAP-IG, 20.0% for EAP, and 9.4% for Edge-SP (a 9.4%–41.2% band); under mean replacement it reaches 50.0% (ACDC) and 48.3% (EAP). Pooled across methods, Human C–C misranking is 20.9%/30.9%/38.1% under resampling/mean/zero vs only 9.8%/16.2%/26.8% on InterpBench.
- **Intervention choice alone flips rankings.** On Greater-Than, C–C misranking rises from 3.4% under resampling to 54.0% under zero replacement; on IOI, mean replacement misranks 33.3% of R–C pairs vs 3.3% under zero replacement — while the circuits and their Q ordering are unchanged, isolating the intervention as the cause.
- **Misrankings carry large behavioral costs.** On InterpBench task 93, zero replacement misranks 70.0% of R–C pairs (68.7% of C–C pairs), and the misrank-preferred circuit loses an average of **63.55 pp** of Q relative to the alternative; task 111 misranks 80.0% R–C / 72.5% C–C under zero replacement — yet task 111's resampling R–C failures average only 0.04 pp, which is why frequency and deficit are reported separately.
- **Context restoration repairs 96 of 100 persistent misrankings.** With circuits and original behavioral scores fixed, at least one partial restoration (80/40/20/10% levels) corrects both validation and independent-test rankings in 96/100 cases — 82 confirmed at the nominal 10% level alone; mean ΔQ across all 100 baseline failures moves from +10.33 pp (faithfulness preferring the worse circuit) to −9.30 pp after repair (the repaired-only baseline mean is +9.30 pp).
- **The failure is objective-level, not metric-specific.** Every metric misranks: under zero replacement, R–C misranking is 29.5% for KL, 18.1% for LD, and 25.1% for PD (resampling: 3.0%/6.1%/6.5%); changing the metric alters which pairs fail but never eliminates failure.
- **Recognizing the reference does not ensure reliable ordering.** InterpBench task 97 shows zero R–C KL misranking under every intervention, yet C–C misranking of 5.2% (resampling), 14.5% (mean), and 22.0% (zero).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty is real but narrower than the title suggests.** Prior work (Miller et al. 2024 on ablation methodology; Zhang & Nanda 2023 on scoring choices) established that faithfulness *numbers* vary with methodology; this paper's sharper claim is that the score can *invert a preference between two fixed, equal-size candidates* — including among real discovery outputs — which is a genuinely new, well-isolated failure mode. However, the ground truth here is behavioral agreement Q, not mechanism identity: as the authors concede, a circuit can be behaviorally better and still be the wrong mechanism, so the paper diagnoses a gap between faithfulness and behavior, not directly between faithfulness and "true mechanism recovery" as the title implies.
- **The restoration experiment has a built-in softness.** Restoring excluded incoming signals to intact-model cached activations partially undoes the ablation, so pushing the score back toward intact-model agreement is close to expected by construction; there is **no random-restoration control**, and the 100 cases are a selected cohort of *persistent* KL failures with Q deficit ≥ 0.5 pp. The 96/100 repair rate therefore characterizes that cohort, not misrankings at large — the authors say exactly this in the limitations, which is honest, but it means the causal story ("context distortion contributes") is supported in a weak, almost-tautological direction.
- **Cross-method comparisons are not licensed.** ACDC and Edge-SP run under a 300-second discovery cap, and pool sizes and size strata differ per method (e.g., the Human ACDC pool includes Docstring and Acronym while its capped IOI/Greater-Than outputs sit in other strata). Statements like "ACDC misranks more than Edge-SP" are confounded by coverage and convergence; the paper correctly restricts claims to within-method pools, but abstract-level phrasing ("9.4%–41.2%") still invites cross-method reading.
- **Scale is modest.** Four human-reference tasks on GPT-2-class models plus ten semi-synthetic InterpBench tasks, with dependent circuit pairs; several table cells rest on small denominators (many "0.0 (–)" entries). The Human-vs-InterpBench rate gap could partly reflect model scale or task difficulty rather than synthetic-vs-real per se.
- **No fix is proposed.** The actionable output is diagnostic: report Q alongside faithfulness with intervention and size controls. No alternative objective (e.g., F constrained by Q) is formulated or tested, so the practical impact depends on follow-up work.
- **No serious red flags.** The paper is unusually candid — it flags the selected cohort, the absence of superiority over random restoration, the possibility that restoration reintroduces useful information, and that pairwise reversals are local, not global, failures. Reproducibility is aided by extensive appendices (thresholds, denominators, search procedure), though no code release is stated in the main text.

## Strengths

- **Clean causal isolation**: size-controlled, deduplicated, search-free comparisons pin the failure on the evaluation objective itself, not on discovery heuristics; using fixed pairs with both validation and independent-test confirmation rules out prompt-specific flukes.
- **Dual-benchmark design**: validating on both known-mechanism semi-synthetic tasks (InterpBench) and human-studied pretrained-model circuits (IOI, Greater-Than, Docstring, Acronym) — and showing materially different rates (e.g., 20.9% vs 9.8% pooled C–C under resampling) — demonstrates that synthetic-only evaluation would underestimate the problem.
- **Coverage across metrics and interventions**: KL, LD, and PD all misrank; resampling, mean, and zero all misrank — establishing the failure as objective-level rather than an artifact of one formula or one ablation choice.
- **The context-restoration intervention is a clever causal test**: holding circuits, Q values, donors, and prompts fixed while restoring only selected signals cleanly attributes part of the preference to computational context rather than circuit structure.
- **Reporting discipline**: separating misranking *frequency* from behavioral *deficit* (task 93's 63.55 pp vs task 111's 0.04 pp resampling deficit) avoids conflating common-but-trivial failures with rare-but-costly ones.

## Limitations

- The behavioral criterion Q is output agreement under ordinary resampling only — it does not establish mechanism identity, so "the better circuit" in every pair is only behaviorally better; Q itself inherits sensitivity to the resampling methodology the paper criticizes.
- Restoration conclusions rest on a selected 100-case cohort of persistent KL failures; there is no random-restoration baseline, repairs may reintroduce task-relevant information, and generalization to other metrics or unselected failures is untested (4/100 cases were never repaired).
- Discovery budgets are asymmetric: ACDC and Edge-SP capped at 300 s per run, and method pools differ in task coverage and size strata, so pooled or cross-method misranking rates are not directly comparable.
- Task set is small (4 pretrained-model tasks + 10 semi-synthetic tasks) with dependent circuit pairs; pairwise reversals are local ranking failures, not statements about globally optimal circuits or about larger models.
- The paper offers no replacement objective — the recommendation stops at "report Q alongside faithfulness with intervention and size controls," leaving open how future discovery systems should actually select candidates.

## Relevance

该论文属于用户高度关注的 "LLM 隐状态与可解释性" 方向（电路发现/机械可解释性评估）。它直接质疑了 circuit discovery 领域最常用的 faithfulness 选择指标本身的可靠性，并给出"必须同时报告行为一致性判据"的评估实践建议——对任何依赖 faithfulness 指标做机制分析、或构建自动化可解释性 pipeline 的工作（包括自动研究/自进化 agent 中的评估环节）都是一条必要的方法论警示。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would a **random-restoration control** (restoring randomly chosen incoming signals, or signals from an unrelated prompt) distinguish the "context distortion" explanation from the weaker claim that "any extra intact-model information improves the score"?
- Can the gap be closed by a **constrained objective** — e.g., maximize faithfulness subject to Q within ε on held-out prompts — and would applying it during search change the circuits EAP/ACDC actually return?
- Does misranking persist under **stricter recovery criteria** such as causal scrubbing, or under behavioral tests beyond resampling-based output agreement (e.g., intermediate-variable probes)?
- How do misranking rates **scale with model size and task complexity** beyond GPT-2-scale circuits and the ten semi-synthetic InterpBench tasks — is the Human-vs-InterpBench gap driven by pretraining or by task structure?
- What distinguishes the **four unrepairable cases**? A structural signature of cases where no partial restoration fixes the ranking could predict when faithfulness is fundamentally misaligned with behavior.
