> **一句话总结：** 针对 SkillOpt/GEPA 等自进化 LLM 系统只会"跑满固定预算"的问题，提出一个即插即用的停止与选件模块：把每轮候选 vs 现任在同一验证集上的逐题配对结果喂进基于下注财富过程（aGRAPA/固定混合）的重启式 CUSUM e-检测器，当证据超过 1/δ 时停止进化，并用财富最大的重启进程估计变化点、回退返回更早的制品；在 3 个模型家族 × 5 个基准上节省 48.4%–91.6% token 而未见测试集精度变化在 ±1.2 pp 内（SearchQA 上 82.43% vs 全预算 82.00%，CI [-1.10, 1.96]），并在 LiveMath 上于奖励劫持的退化 skill 出现前（第 8 轮报警，返回第 1 轮制品）及时止损。

> **TL;DR:** Self-evolving LLM loops (SkillOpt, GEPA) typically run a fixed iteration budget. This paper adds a plug-and-play stopping rule built on anytime-valid betting (e-process) sequential tests plus change-point estimation over per-item paired candidate-vs-incumbent validation outcomes. On SearchQA with SkillOpt + DeepSeek V4 Flash it stops at round 4 instead of the full 40-round budget, cutting tokens from 25.85M to 2.18M (-91.6%) while the returned artifact scores 82.43% vs the full run's 82.00% (+0.43 pp, 95% CI [-1.10, 1.96]); across GSM8K, OfficeQA, GEPA, Qwen3-32B, and GPT-5.6 Luna it saves 48.4%-92.6% of tokens with unseen-test deltas of at most ±1.16 pp, and on LiveMath it alarms at round 8, before a reward-hacked "answer A without reasoning" skill appears at round 16, returning the round-1 artifact instead.

## 核心贡献

- **问题形式化：** 首次把自进化 LLM 系统的两个被忽视的决策显式提出——(i) 何时停止进化循环（when to stop）、(ii) 停止后返回哪个制品（what to output）。指出固定预算运行的两个后果：性能饱和后的无效计算，以及持续优化同一验证信号导致的评估过拟合 / reward hacking（后期退化制品覆盖早期好制品）。
- **即插即用停止规则：** 仅消费系统已经算出的逐题配对评估结果（候选 vs 现任），构造重启财富过程 W_t^(s) = Π[1 + λ(ε - X_{s,u})]，取跨起点的最大值作为 CUSUM 式 e-检测器 M_t，越过 1/δ 阈值即报警；不修改底层自进化算法的任何配置。理论保证误报平均运行长度 E[T_alarm] ≥ 1/δ（anytime-valid）。
- **变化点选件：** 用同一组重启进程，选报警时刻财富最大的起点 ν̂ 作为变化点估计，返回其前一轮的现任制品 θ_{ν̂-1}；证明固定 λ 下 ν̂ 是一致估计，且定位误差以 1/n 速率收缩（Proposition 2 的 sub-Gaussian 界）。
- **广泛的实证验证：** 覆盖 SkillOpt 和 GEPA 两个自进化框架、DeepSeek V4 Flash / Qwen3-32B / GPT-5.6 Luna 三个模型、SearchQA / GSM8K / OfficeQA / LiveMath / SpreadsheetBench 五个基准；包含"改进持续时检测器应保持沉默"的阴性对照（SpreadsheetBench）与真实 reward-hacking 案例（LiveMath）。

## Key Contributions

- **Formulates two unanswered decisions of self-evolving LLM systems** — when to stop the propose-evaluate-gate loop, and which artifact to return afterwards — and shows both can be answered online from per-item paired evaluation outcomes the system already computes, with zero modification to the underlying algorithm.
- **An anytime-valid stopping rule:** restart wealth (betting) processes test the pre-change condition E[Z_t | F_{t-1}] ≥ ε (round-wise expected paired gain at least ε); the CUSUM-style max over restarts is a valid e-detector with guaranteed average time to false alarm E[T_alarm] ≥ 1/δ, combining aGRAPA adaptive betting and fixed-mixture averaging.
- **A change-point-based output selector:** the restart process started nearest the true transition attains the largest wealth at alarm time; picking ν̂ = argmax W^(s)_{T_alarm} and returning θ_{ν̂-1} is consistent, with localization error vanishing at rate 1/n under a fixed gap κ (Proposition 2).
- **Extensive evaluation:** 2 self-evolving frameworks (SkillOpt, GEPA) × 3 model families (DeepSeek V4 Flash, Qwen3-32B, GPT-5.6 Luna) × 5 benchmarks (SearchQA, GSM8K, OfficeQA, LiveMath, SpreadsheetBench), with a negative control (continued improvement → detector stays silent) and a genuine reward-hacking episode (degenerate skill at LiveMath round 16) that the procedure avoids by stopping at round 8 and returning an earlier artifact.

## Methodology

**Problem setup.** A self-evolving system holds an incumbent artifact θ_{t-1} (prompt, skill document, or program). Each round t it proposes a candidate θ'_t, evaluates both on a fixed validation set D_val = {x_i}_{i=1}^n with binary correctness ℓ(θ, x) ∈ {0,1}, and a greedy gate accepts iff L(θ'_t) > L(θ_{t-1}). The authors define per-item paired differences X_{t,i} = c_{t,i} - b_{t,i} ∈ {-1, 0, +1} (candidate vs incumbent correctness on each item) and round-wise means Z_t. Crucially, these paired outcomes are already produced by the propose-gate loop of most existing systems, so the module needs no extra evaluations and touches no system internals.

**Pre-change condition.** Evolution should continue only while proposals are worthwhile: E[Z_t | F_{t-1}] ≥ ε, where ε is the minimum expected proposal gain considered worth a round (default ε = 0.01). Stopping asks whether this condition has ceased to hold.

**When to stop: restart wealth processes.** For each starting round s, a betting wealth process W_t^(s) = Π_{u=s}^t [1 + λ_{s,u}(ε - X_{s,u})] accumulates evidence against H_0^(s) (pre-change holds from s onward): outcomes worse than ε multiply the wealth up, better outcomes multiply it down. Betting fractions come from either aGRAPA (adaptive, Waudby-Smith & Ramdas style) or a fixed mixture averaging λ ∈ Λ = {0.1, 0.2, 0.4}. A fresh process is restarted at every round so evidence after a transition can recover from early losses; following Shin et al.'s CUSUM-style e-detector, the statistic M_t = max_{1≤s≤t} W_t^(s) retains the strongest current evidence, and the alarm time is T_alarm = inf{t : M_t ≥ 1/δ}. Proposition 1 shows each W^(s) is a nonnegative supermartingale under H_0^(s), so Pr(sup W^(s) ≤ 1/δ) ≥ 1-δ and E[T_alarm] ≥ 1/δ — a small δ makes stopping conservative.

**What to output: change-point estimation.** Restart processes begun before the true transition tend to accumulate wealth that later processes cannot recover (they fight against post-change growth), while those begun after miss evidence. Hence ν̂ = argmax_{1≤s≤T_alarm} W^(s)_{T_alarm} estimates when meaningful improvement ended, and the procedure returns θ^{ν̂} = θ_{ν̂-1}, the incumbent just before estimated transition. Proposition 2 proves consistency in the fixed-λ setting and, with a pre/post gap κ and sub-Gaussian increments, a localization bound of order (2σ²/nκ²)·log(·) — error shrinking at rate 1/n — uniformly over finite T. Defaults: ε = 0.01, δ = 0.05, aGRAPA (the fixed mixture yields the same ν̂; only the alarm round differs).

## Results & Highlights

- **Main result (Table 1, SkillOpt + DeepSeek V4 Flash, shared detector ε=0.01, δ=0.05):** on **SearchQA** the detector alarms at round **4** vs the full 40-round budget, spending **2.18M tokens vs 25.85M (91.6% saved)**; the returned artifact reaches **82.43%** unseen-test accuracy vs **82.00%** for the full-run selection (**Δ +0.43 pp, 95% CI [-1.10, 1.96]**) — a **12.9×** token-efficiency gain per point of unseen-test improvement (5.39M → 0.42M tokens per point).
- **On GSM8K** the same configuration stops at round 4/40, saving **89.8% of tokens (11.86M → 1.21M)** with returned accuracy **97.40% vs 98.20%** full-run (**Δ -0.80 pp, CI [-1.91, 0.31]**), 9.3× efficiency gain; on **OfficeQA** (shorter 16-round budget) it saves **48.4% (206.90M → 106.72M tokens)** with **32.56% vs 33.72%** (**Δ -1.16 pp, CI [-8.72, 6.30]**), 1.8× efficiency.
- **Negative control (SpreadsheetBench):** validation accuracy climbs from **0.375 to 0.70 over 16 rounds**; with ε=0.01, δ=0.05 **no alarm is raised** within the horizon — the detector correctly stays silent while improvement continues.
- **Reward-hacking avoidance (LiveMath):** SkillOpt's full run eventually accepts a degenerate skill at round 16 that instructs "answer 'A' without reasoning," exploiting the benchmark's skewed answer distribution for a spurious accuracy jump; the procedure alarms at **T_alarm = 8**, estimates change point **ν̂ = 2**, and returns the **round-1 artifact**, never entering the degraded regime. The authors are careful to note the detector does not explicitly identify hacking — it stops because sustained meaningful improvement has ceased.
- **Transfers across systems and models (Tables 2-3):** with GEPA on SearchQA it stops at round 5 saving **73.8%** (Δ +0.07 pp, CI [-1.17, 1.32]) and on GSM8K at round 6 saving **65.5%** (Δ 0.00 pp); across DeepSeek V4 Flash / Qwen3-32B / GPT-5.6 Luna on SearchQA the detector adapts its alarm (rounds 4 / 12 / 10) with token savings **91.6% / 75.3% / 68.9%** and unseen-test deltas +0.43 / +0.21 / ±0.00 pp — overall savings of **68.9%-92.6%** across all model-benchmark combinations.

## Authors & Credibility

Enoch Yin (Department of Statistics, George Washington University), Bin Liu (Department of Statistics and Data Science, School of Management, Fudan University), and Zhengling Qi (Department of Decision Sciences, GWU; corresponding author) are all statistics/decision-science researchers rather than an LLM-systems lab — and the paper reads accordingly. The methodology is squarely in the modern anytime-valid inference tradition (Waudby-Smith & Ramdas betting confidence sequences, Shin et al. e-detectors, Page/Wald/Shiryaev-Roberts classical sequential change-point theory), where this group is credible; Qi in particular has a track record in statistical inference and policy/causal learning. The LLM-experiment side is competent but consumer-grade: three frontier API models (DeepSeek V4 Flash, Qwen3-32B, GPT-5.6 Luna), two public frameworks (SkillOpt, GEPA), and standard benchmarks. No code release is stated in the main text.

## Critical Assessment

- **The missing baseline is the elephant in the room.** All comparisons are against the system's own fixed-budget run — never against a simple heuristic such as "stop after k consecutive rounds with validation gain < ε" or a patience-based early stop on the validation curve. Such a rule is nearly free to implement and would plausibly capture most of the token savings on SearchQA/GSM8K, where improvement saturates after round 2-4. The paper's unique selling point therefore reduces to (a) the anytime-valid false-alarm guarantee and (b) the LiveMath case where the validation curve is flat but late acceptance is actively harmful — real advantages, but the empirical gap over naive stopping is never quantified.
- **Statistical power on the reported deltas is thin.** The unseen-test sets are small (GSM8K n=500, OfficeQA n=172), and the headline "no loss" claims rest on CIs that include zero by construction — the OfficeQA interval [-8.72, 6.30] spans 15 points, so "comparable performance" there is close to unmeasurable. Each cell also appears to be a single evolution trajectory: self-evolving loops are highly stochastic (LLM-proposed candidates), yet no variance across seeds of T_alarm or ν̂ is reported, so the round-4/round-8 stopping times could be lucky draws.
- **Theory-practice gap, acknowledged:** Proposition 2's localization guarantee holds for fixed-λ betting, a single change point, and a fixed gap κ, while the main experiments use aGRAPA; LiveMath is arguably a *two*-change-point scenario (plateau, then a hacked upward jump) that the framework handles only as "improvement stopped." The authors honestly note the detector "does not explicitly identify reward hacking" — it would also stop on a flat curve preceding a genuine late breakthrough, since it has no way to distinguish the two.
- **Reproducibility is limited:** two of three models are proprietary APIs, GEPA token counts are estimated from metric-call counts rather than measured, and no code artifact is announced in the main text. Sensitivity results (smaller validation sets → later stops) are relegated to Appendix D.

## Strengths

- Genuinely plug-and-play: consumes only paired outcomes the propose-gate loop already computes, requires zero configuration changes to SkillOpt or GEPA, and transfers across both frameworks unchanged.
- Principled statistical grounding: anytime-valid e-process guarantees (E[T_alarm] ≥ 1/δ) and a 1/n change-point localization bound, rather than ad-hoc patience heuristics.
- Excellent experimental hygiene for this space: a negative control benchmark where the detector *should* stay silent (and does), a real reward-hacking episode used to stress the output selector, and one-factor-at-a-time ablations over framework, model, validation-set size, and detector parameters.
- Honest framing: the paper explicitly separates what stops (T_alarm) from what is returned (ν̂) and admits the method detects "cessation of worthwhile improvement," not hacking itself.
- The pairing trick is elegant: per-item candidate-vs-incumbent differences turn a noisy scalar-metric monitor into an n-sample-per-round sequential test at no extra evaluation cost.

## Limitations

- Never benchmarked against simpler stopping heuristics, so the marginal empirical value of the betting machinery over "patience on validation gain" is unquantified.
- Single trajectories per cell; no seed variance for alarm rounds, change-point estimates, or token counts on stochastic LLM-driven loops.
- Localization guarantee limited to fixed-λ betting and a single change point; adaptive aGRAPA used in practice lacks the localizations bound, and multi-change regimes (e.g., plateau → reward hack) are only heuristically handled.
- Choice of ε (minimum worthwhile gain) is hand-set to 0.01 everywhere; results on low-accuracy benchmarks like OfficeQA (14.5% baseline) may be sensitive to this scale, and no data-driven calibration is offered (listed as future work).
- Small unseen-test sets (172-1400 items) make the "no performance loss" deltas statistically fragile, especially OfficeQA.
- No code release stated in the main text; two of three evaluated models are closed APIs.

## Relevance

这篇论文正中你的第一优先方向「Self-Evolving Agents & Automated Research」中"长时自治系统的可靠性与可信性"子题：它不提出新的自进化方法，而是解决所有这类循环共有的"何时该停、停了交什么"的治理问题，并直接演示了对 reward-hacking 制品的规避。对任何在跑 SkillOpt/GEPA 式自进化实验的人来说，这是一个可以直接套在现有评估管道上的统计安全层。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does the e-detector compare head-to-head against a k-round patience rule on validation gain, in both token savings and final artifact quality?
- How variable are T_alarm and ν̂ across repeated evolution runs with different LLM sampling seeds — is the round-4 stopping on SearchQA typical or lucky?
- Can the restart-wealth statistic be extended to explicitly score *downward* jumps (reward hacking) rather than only cessation of improvement, e.g., a two-sided CUSUM over Z_t?
- How should ε be calibrated from data — e.g., tied to the token cost of one round — and how sensitive is OfficeQA behavior to ε at its 14.5% baseline accuracy?
- Does the procedure remain valid when the validation set is refreshed over time (a common anti-overfitting practice), which breaks the fixed-D_val pairing assumption?
