> **一句话总结：** 复旦团队（NeurIPS 2026）证明 LLM 人格（persona）激活在残差流中构成一个维度仅 15–23、曲率非均匀的黎曼子流形，并提出沿流形测地线（而非欧氏直线）进行人格插值转向，配合新构建的行为学三元组基准 BST：在三个 7–8B 模型上，测地距离在 BST 三元组一致率（TCR）上比欧氏距离高 +5.1–6.1 个百分点（如 Llama-3.1-8B 上 68.2% vs 62.1%），测地转向在高偏差人格对上将插值一致性（IC）从 0.38 提升到 0.72、人类自然度评分从 3.41 提升到 3.82。

> **TL;DR:** Inference-time persona steering in LLMs typically assumes persona vectors live in a flat, isotropic space, yet prior work reports ceiling effects, trait interference, and composition drift. PersonaManifold (NeurIPS 2026) shows that ~2K persona activations extracted from Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, and Mistral-7B form a curved, low-dimensional Riemannian submanifold (intrinsic dimension 15–23 by Marchenko-Pastur and Levina-Bickel consensus; heterogeneous Ollivier-Ricci curvature that survives density-matched controls). Using a local metric tensor + k-NN geodesic distance, the authors steer persona interpolation along geodesics instead of straight lines. On their new behaviorally-grounded BST benchmark (150 situational questions across six psychological constructs), geodesic distance beats Euclidean on Triplet Consistency Rate by +6.1 pts on Llama (68.2% vs 62.1%), +5.5 on Qwen, and +5.1 on Mistral; geodesic steering raises Interpolation Coherence on high-deviation pairs to 0.72 vs 0.38 for Euclidean-Linear on Llama (0.69 vs 0.55 on Qwen), and human raters prefer geodesic dialogues 3.82 vs 3.41 (1–5 Likert). Gains concentrate exactly where the geodesic departs from the straight line (top d_g/d_E quartile: +11–12% TCR; bottom quartile: +1–1.5%), which is an honest and informative failure-mode analysis.

## 核心贡献

- **几何发现：** 用约 2K 个人格（PersonaHub 1.5K 分层子集 + CoSER 500 文学角色）在三个开源 LLM 上提取残差流激活，经 Marchenko-Pastur 与 Levina-Bickel 双方法一致估计，人格表征构成内在维度 d* ∈ [15, 23] 的低维流形——远低于约 300 的 PCA 降噪后环境维度，又显著高于大五人格的 5 维。
- **曲率异质性：** Ollivier-Ricci 曲率按人格类型系统分化：职业型人格（教师、医生、工程师）呈正曲率、聚成密集簇，虚构角色与罕见特质组合呈负曲率、占据孤立邻域；密度–曲率解耦分析（密度五分位内 Kruskal-Wallis，p < 0.001；密度与曲率的 Spearman ρ = 0.08 不显著）证明这是流形内在属性而非采样伪影。
- **测地转向（geodesic steering）：** 在 k-NN 图上取最短路径、局部切空间三次样条平滑、等弧长采样 K 个中间点、逆 PCA 投影后以范数保持方式注入残差流；当流形局部平坦（d_g/d_E ≈ 1）时自动退化为线性插值。
- **BST 基准：** 基于道德基础理论、DOSPERT、人际环状模型、决策风格、Schwartz 价值观、沟通者风格六大心理学构念，用 GPT-5.2 生成 180 道情境题、经区分度过滤保留 150 道（48 强制选择 + 102 开放式），以行为响应（而非自评问卷）定义人格相似度，构建 1 万个三元组（≥3/5 人工一致性），人类–系统一致率 86.1%（Fleiss κ = 0.81）。
- **因子分离实验设计：** 七种距离基线沿"平坦各向同性（欧氏/余弦）→ 平坦各向异性（Mahalanobis）→ 弯曲各向同性（Isomap/Diffusion Map/UMAP）→ 弯曲各向异性（测地）"梯度排列，可分别归因各向异性与拓扑/曲率两个几何因子的贡献。

## Key Contributions

- Reveals that persona representations in LLMs form a curved, low-dimensional Riemannian submanifold: intrinsic dimensionality d* ∈ [15, 23] across Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, and Mistral-7B (two independent estimators agreeing within ±2 dims), with Ollivier-Ricci curvature that varies systematically by persona type (professional personas positive, fictional/rare-trait personas negative) and persists within density-matched quintiles (p < 0.001), ruling out a sampling artifact.
- Proposes geodesic steering: interpolate between persona activations along k-NN graph shortest paths (smoothed with cubic splines in local tangent spaces, norm-preserving after inverse-PCA projection), which keeps intermediate personas in data-supported regions where Euclidean straight lines cut through empty space.
- Introduces the Behavioral Similarity Triplet (BST) benchmark: 150 situation-based questions (48 forced-choice + 102 open-ended) grounded in six established psychological constructs, defining persona similarity through observed behavioral responses rather than self-report questionnaires that recent work shows dissociate from actual LLM behavior; triplets reach 86.1% human-system agreement.
- Demonstrates with a factor-separation baseline gradient (Euclidean → Mahalanobis → Isomap → Geodesic) that anisotropy (+2–2.5% TCR) and graph topology/curvature (+1.5–2.5% additional) provide complementary, individually significant gains, and localizes the benefit: top quartile of geodesic-deviation ratio d_g/d_E gains +11–12% TCR while the bottom (near-flat) quartile gains only +1–1.5%.

## Methodology

**Persona activation extraction.** A pool of N ≈ 2K personas is built from a stratified 1.5K subset of PersonaHub plus 500 literary characters from CoSER. Each persona is paired with M = 40 probe questions adapted from BFI-44, HEXACO-60, and TIPI, rephrased by GPT-5.2 (with manual review) into open-ended situational prompts. For each (persona, question) pair, the residual-stream activation at the last token of the generated response is collected; the persona representation is the mean activation minus the neutral-prompt baseline. A single layer in the 12–16 range is selected per model by validation TCR on 200 held-out personas (layer 16 for Llama and Qwen, 14 for Mistral), and PCA retaining 99% cumulative variance reduces D ≈ 4096 to D ∈ [200, 500].

**Manifold geometry estimation.** (1) Intrinsic dimensionality via consensus of the Marchenko-Pastur spectral test and Levina-Bickel MLE. (2) A local metric tensor computed from the top-d* eigenpairs of the k-NN local covariance (k ≥ 3d*), weighting directions by 1/(λ+ε) so that rare directions count as "farther". (3) Geodesic distance as Dijkstra shortest path on a k-NN graph whose edge weights average Euclidean and Riemannian lengths. (4) Ollivier-Ricci curvature per edge (via Wasserstein-1 distance between neighbor measures), aggregated to scalar curvature per node; a density-curvature decoupling procedure (curvature comparison within density quintiles, Kruskal-Wallis) separates intrinsic geometry from non-uniform sampling. Graph construction, local PCA, and Dijkstra run in ~15 minutes per model on CPU; k-NN graph k = 5d*, metric regularization ε = 10⁻⁴.

**Geodesic steering.** Given personas p₁, p₂: compute the graph shortest path, smooth it via cubic splines in local tangent spaces, sample K = 10 equal-arc-length intermediate points, project each back through the inverse PCA map, and inject into the residual stream as h_ℓ ← h_ℓ + α·(P†γ(t_k))/‖P†γ(t_k)‖, with a norm-aware α schedule ((1−t)‖x₁‖ + t‖x₂‖) keeping intensity matched between endpoints. Because the projection preserves norms on the PCA subspace, the method degrades gracefully to linear interpolation when the manifold is locally flat.

**BST benchmark.** GPT-5.2 generates 180 candidate questions from six construct definitions (Moral Foundations Theory, DOSPERT, Interpersonal Circumplex, Decision-Making Style, Schwartz Values, Communicator Style); discriminativeness filtering (response entropy > 0.8 nats for forced-choice, sentence-embedding variance > 0.05 via all-MiniLM-L6-v2 for open-ended) retains 150. Behavioral distance d_behav = β·d_choice + (1−β)·d_emb with β = 1/3; 10K triplets are built requiring d(a,n) ≥ 3·d(a,p) and ≥3/5 human agreement. Evaluation uses Triplet Consistency Rate TCR = Pr[d(a,p) < d(a,n)], reported separately for choice-only (circularity-free) and embedding-based variants.

**Experimental setup.** Three models (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3), 7 distance baselines along the factor-separation gradient, 7 steering baselines (Euclidean-Linear, SLERP, Isomap-Euclidean, Diffusion Map, UMAP, Graph-NN Chain, Geodesic). Steering quality: Interpolation Coherence (IC, pairwise cosine of response embeddings at 20 dialogue turns), Transition Smoothness (TS, monotone BFI-44 trait progression), Manifold Adherence (MA, 1 − normalized nearest-observed-persona distance); plus external benchmarks (PersonaGym PersonaScore, BFI-44 protocol of Feng et al., Trait Expression Score) and a 3-annotator human naturalness study (Krippendorff's α = 0.73). Ablations cover metric estimation, graph k, layer depth, PCA variance, steering α, and spline smoothing. Compute: 4×A100-80GB; ~45 min extraction + ~15 min geometry per model.

## Results & Highlights

- **Distance prediction (RQ2, Table 2):** Geodesic distance achieves the highest Triplet Consistency Rate on BST across all three models and all three metrics. On TCR-combined: Llama-3.1-8B 68.2% vs 62.1% for Euclidean (+6.1 pts), Qwen2.5-7B 67.0% vs 61.5% (+5.5), Mistral-7B 65.9% vs 60.8% (+5.1); all bootstrap-significant at p < 0.01 with 95% CIs excluding zero. The advantage survives on TCR-choice, computed purely from discrete options with no embeddings, ruling out circularity with the embedding-based similarity metric.
- **Factor separation (Figure 3):** Adding anisotropy alone (Euclidean → Mahalanobis) contributes +2–2.5% TCR; adding graph topology (→ Isomap) adds ~+1.5%; combining both (→ Geodesic) adds a further +1.5–2.5% — each step individually significant (p < 0.01, bootstrap), showing anisotropy and curvature are complementary rather than redundant.
- **Steering quality (RQ3, Table 3, high-deviation pairs):** On Llama-3.1-8B, geodesic steering reaches IC 0.72 vs 0.38 for Euclidean-Linear and TS 0.69 vs 0.52; on Qwen IC 0.69 vs 0.55, TS 0.65 vs 0.49; on Mistral IC 0.66 vs 0.53, TS 0.63 vs 0.36. On low-deviation pairs all methods converge (with one honest exception: SLERP edges out geodesic on Mistral TS, 0.64 vs 0.63), and Graph-NN Chain slightly beats geodesic on Mistral MA (0.56 vs 0.55).
- **Where the gains live:** Stratifying persona pairs by deviation ratio, the top quartile (d_g/d_E > 1.5) shows +11–12% TCR improvement over Euclidean while the bottom quartile shows only +1–1.5%; roughly 60% of random pairs exceed the practical threshold d_g/d_E > 1.2, giving practitioners an actionable decision rule.
- **Geometry itself (RQ1):** d* ∈ [15, 23] across models and layers 12–20 (e.g., Llama 22/23/21 by Marchenko-Pastur at layers 12/16/20); curvature differences across persona types persist in every density quintile (p < 0.001) and the density–curvature correlation is non-significant (Spearman ρ = 0.08), confirming intrinsic heterogeneity.
- **Human and external evaluation:** 100 dialogues rated by 3 annotators give geodesic a mean naturalness of 3.82 vs 3.41 for linear steering (Krippendorff's α = 0.73; gap largest on high-deviation pairs, 3.92 vs 3.18). On 200 triplets, human-system agreement for the automated BST metric is 86.1% (Fleiss κ = 0.81). External benchmarks corroborate: higher PersonaGym PersonaScore, smoother BFI-44 transitions, and continued Trait Expression Score growth where linear steering plateaus or oscillates.
- **Downstream retrieval (Appendix F):** Geodesic distance improves few-shot persona retrieval MRR by +0.12–0.16 on rare (bottom-25% density) personas across all three models (e.g., Llama 0.57 vs 0.43 Euclidean) but only +0.04–0.06 on common ones, where Mahalanobis already suffices.
- **Ablations (Table 4):** Replacing the local metric with isotropic Euclidean collapses Llama TCR from 68.2 to 63.8 (diffusion kernel: 67.5); middle layers 12–16 are best (shallow 63.1 / middle 68.2 / deep 65.8); norm-aware α adds 3–4 IC/TS points over fixed α; spline smoothing lifts TS from 0.58 to 0.69 without hurting IC or MA; results are stable across k ∈ {3d*, 5d*, 10d*} (max gap 1.3%) and PCA variance 95–99.5%.

## Authors & Credibility

The paper comes from Rui Xu, Yinghui Xu, and Libo Wu at Fudan University and the Shanghai Innovation Institute. Rui Xu has an established record on persona-driven LLM agents (Character is Destiny at Findings of EMNLP 2025, MINDECHO, CoSER at ICML 2025 with overlapping co-authors, InCharacter at ACL 2024), so the group knows the persona-control literature first-hand. The work is published at NeurIPS 2026, code is released on GitHub, hyperparameters and prompts are documented in appendices (including full BST generation prompts and the annotation instruction sheet), human subjects procedures were IRB-reviewed as exempt, and compute details (4×A100, per-stage runtimes) are disclosed — an overall strong reproducibility posture. One dependency to note: GPT-5.2 (OpenAI API) is used for probe rephrasing, BST question generation, and steered dialogue generation, so full reproduction needs API access.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** Applying Riemannian machinery (local metric tensors, Ollivier-Ricci curvature, geodesic interpolation) specifically to persona representations is genuinely new: prior geodesic-interpolation work targeted generative latents (Riemannian continuous normalizing flows), and anisotropy/cone-structure analyses of contextual embeddings stopped at the token level. The factor-separation baseline gradient is a design choice most empirical papers in this area skip, and the density-curvature decoupling control directly addresses the most obvious confound (curvature estimates on non-uniform point clouds). This is careful work.

**Rigor concerns.** (1) Scale: everything is demonstrated only on 7–8B models — the authors flag this themselves, but the central claim ("persona representations form a curved manifold") is exactly the kind of claim that can change at 70B+, and there is no evidence either way here. (2) Evaluation-loop exposure to GPT-5.2: the same model family generates the probe rephrasings, the BST questions, and the steered dialogues whose coherence is then scored partly by all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings. The TCR-choice condition (discrete options only) and the 86.1% human-system agreement mitigate this well for the distance claims, but the steering IC/TS numbers remain embedding-mediated with no human validation of IC itself (human eval covered naturalness only, 100 dialogues). (3) The persona representation is a single mean activation vector per persona — the authors concede this collapses intra-persona variation across conversational contexts, so "the persona manifold" is really a manifold over persona-averages, which may understate within-persona geometry. (4) Absolute headroom: 68.2% TCR against a 50% chance floor is solid, but the +5–6 pt margin over Euclidean is measured on triplets that were themselves selected for large behavioral separations (d(a,n) ≥ 3·d(a,p), hardest-20% positives), a construction that plausibly favors metric differences on sparse regions — precisely where geodesics win; a TCR breakdown on uniformly sampled (non-curated) triplets would have strengthened the claim. (5) The practical payoff is honestly but substantially qualified by their own quartile analysis (+1–1.5% for near-flat pairs), so the headline "geodesic beats Euclidean" applies to roughly the top half of persona pairs, not universally.

**Reproducibility.** Good: released code, full hyperparameter table, grid-search protocol on a held-out 200-persona validation split, per-stage runtimes, and published prompts. The main external dependency is the OpenAI API for the LLM-generated evaluation assets, which are not fully under the community's control.

**Significance.** For the persona-control subfield, this reframes known failure modes (ceiling/resistance effects, composition drift from Feng et al. 2026 / Chen et al. 2026 / Sun et al. 2025) as symptoms of flat-space assumptions and validates the fix. The d_g/d_E > 1.2 decision rule is a concrete, transferable practitioner heuristic. The claim's breadth outside persona control (e.g., attitude/style steering generally) is left untested.

## Strengths

- A clean, falsifiable geometric hypothesis with the right controls: density-curvature decoupling (Kruskal-Wallis within quintiles) and bootstrap CIs on every headline number.
- The factor-separation baseline suite (Euclidean → Mahalanobis → Isomap/Diffusion Map/UMAP → Geodesic) attributes gains to named geometric factors instead of dumping everything into one "ours beats baselines" table — an exemplary experimental design pattern.
- Honest reporting throughout: the paper itself highlights where Graph-NN Chain wins (Mistral MA), where SLERP wins (low-deviation TS), the small bottom-quartile gains, and a defined applicability threshold (d_g/d_E > 1.2) rather than claiming universal superiority.
- BST is a thoughtfully constructed evaluation instrument: grounded in six established psychological constructs, discriminativeness-filtered, validated against 6 human annotators (κ = 0.81, 86.1% human-system agreement), with a circularity-free TCR-choice variant and a 50-triplet disagreement autopsy (62% boundary cases, 24% construct ambiguity, 14% annotation errors).
- Cross-architecture consistency (three independent pretraining pipelines at the same scale) plus a layer sweep (inverted-U peaking at 12–16, matching mechanistic findings) support the "general property" claim within the tested scale.
- Practical framing: ~33% one-time CPU overhead per model, <1s per steering query at inference, and a simple decision rule for when to bother.

## Limitations

- Only 7–8B models are tested; extension to 70B+ is stated as future work, and the O(N²) geometry estimation would itself need approximate-neighbor methods at larger persona sets.
- Each persona is compressed to one mean activation vector, discarding intra-persona variation across conversational contexts — the geometry characterized is that of persona centroids, not of personas in situ.
- GPT-5.2 is entangled with the evaluation pipeline (probe rephrasing, BST question generation, steered dialogue generation); despite the TCR-choice control and human agreement checks, some shared generator bias cannot be excluded, and full reproduction requires API access.
- The steering benefit is uneven by construction: negligible for the ~40–50% of pairs with near-flat deviation ratios (and SLERP even edges it out in one low-deviation condition), so adoption requires computing d_g/d_E first.
- BST covers six psychological constructs; cognitive style, emotional regulation, and other dimensions of persona are out of scope, and the open-ended half of the metric rides on all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings whose construct validity for "behavioral similarity" is only indirectly validated (via the 86.1% human-system check on 200 triplets).
- Absolute TCR (~62–68%) leaves a large gap to ceiling, and triplet curation (hard positives, 3× separation constraint) may inflate the measured advantage relative to uncurated triplets.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the curved-manifold structure and the d* ∈ [15, 23] intrinsic dimensionality persist at 70B+ scale, and does the d_g/d_E > 1.2 decision threshold shift with model size?
- Would modeling intra-persona variation (multiple activation samples per persona, e.g., a distribution on the manifold rather than a single mean point) sharpen the geometry estimates and improve steering for multi-context personas?
- Can geodesic steering transfer to non-persona intervention targets — styles, stances, refusal behavior — where the same flat-interpolation failure modes are reported?
- Is the TCR advantage robust on uniformly sampled triplets without the d(a,n) ≥ 3·d(a,p) curation constraint, and how much of the +5–6 pt margin comes from sparse-region triplets?
- Do the negative-curvature "isolated" regions occupied by fictional characters correspond to interpretable circuits or sparse subnetworks (cf. personality-subnetwork findings), and can curvature serve as an unsupervised flag for out-of-distribution personas?
- The BST open-ended distance currently rides on a small general-purpose encoder (all-MiniLM-L6-v2); would a stronger or behaviorally-tuned response encoder change the embedding-metric component of the results?
