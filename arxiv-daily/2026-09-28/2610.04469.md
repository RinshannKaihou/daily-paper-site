> **一句话总结：** Trinity 让单个 VLM（Qwen3-VL-8B/7B）同时扮演提问者、求解者和验证者三个角色——验证者是策略模型的 EMA 影子副本（无需标签、无需外部裁判），在 rollout 前筛查自生成 QA 的图像锚定性、答案正确性与难度，并在求解者共识与参考答案冲突时裁决改写参考答案；仅用无标注图像（Vero-600K/OmniScience）做 RL 训练，使 Qwen3-VL-8B 在 MathVerse 上 +12.8 分、SciVQR-Bio 生物子集上 +8.6 分，全面超过同 backbone 的 MM-Zero 和 DPE。

> **TL;DR:** Trinity is a label-free self-evolution framework where one VLM acts as Questioner, Solver, and Verifier simultaneously — the Verifier being an EMA copy of the policy itself (no separate training, no external judge). It screens every self-generated QA for image-grounding, correctness, and difficulty before it becomes supervision, scores Solver reasoning against the image, and adjudicates Solver-consensus-vs-reference disputes by re-solving the question. Trained on unlabeled Vero-600K images only, it lifts Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct from 58.2 to 71.0 on MathVerse (+12.8) and 56.7 to 65.3 on SciVQR-Bio (+8.6), beating MM-Zero (which drops below base on all math benchmarks) and trailing-free vs DPE (which trails Trinity by 13.8 points on MathVerse); on the weaker Qwen2.5-VL-7B the gains shrink to +1.1–1.5 on math benchmarks — consistent with the Verifier being the backbone itself.

## 核心贡献

- **三角色合一的自进化框架：** 首次在同一 VLM 内同时实现 Questioner（从无标注图像生成图文 QA）、Solver（K 个思维链补全）和 Verifier（策略的 EMA 副本，φ=0.999，仅推理、无梯度），不需要人工标注、外部裁判模型或工具执行器——填补了"图像域没有代码执行器可充当 ground truth"这一 Absolute Zero/R-Zero 类方法的空白。
- **验证者门控的奖励设计，显式区分"真难"与"坏题"：** 提问者奖励由格式门、事实性门（图像锚定 + 参考答案正确）和求解者经验失败率（难度）组成（α_Q, β_Q, ρ_f)=(0.3, 0.7, 0.5)；无锚定的问题无论多难得 0 分，参考答案被挑战推翻的最多得一半；求解者的推理分必须乘以正确性门（w_c, w_r, w_f)=(0.5, 0.3, 0.2)，流畅但错误的思维链拿不到推理分。
- **争议裁决机制（dispute resolution）：** 当 ≥75% 的格式化求解者答案一致且不同于参考答案、且全部判错时，验证者带图独立重解并站队——若支持求解者则改写参考答案并重算奖励、提问者正确性清零。这是对共识型奖励（majority-vote）盲区（系统性共同错误被"自信地判对"）的直接修正。
- **双 backbone + 双语料的实证验证：** 在 Qwen3-VL-8B 和 Qwen2.5-VL-7B 上、分别用 Vero-600K（约 60 万图）和 OmniScience（150 万论文插图）训练，数学与科学基准全部提升，且发布了确定性聚合与诚实的协议说明（附录 A.4–A.5）。
- **诚实的失败模式剖析：** 附录 D 明确承认验证者与策略共享感知误差、8B 一步训练需 8×A100 上 10–20 分钟、联合 vs 交替优化未消融、MME-SCI 提升几乎全部来自选择题等——每个局限都配了对应的后续实验建议。

## Key Contributions

- **A three-roles-in-one-model self-evolving framework:** a single VLM plays Questioner (generates image-conditioned QA from unlabeled images), Solver (K chain-of-thought completions), and Verifier (an EMA copy of the policy, φ=0.999, inference-only, no gradients) — requiring no human labels, no external judge model, and no tool executor, filling the gap left by Absolute Zero/R-Zero-style methods that need a code executor as ground truth and cannot work on images.
- **Verifier-gated rewards that separate genuine difficulty from broken questions:** the Questioner reward combines a format gate, a factuality gate (image-grounding + reference correctness), and the Solver's empirical failure rate (difficulty), with (α_Q, β_Q, ρ_f)=(0.3, 0.7, 0.5); an ungrounded question earns nothing no matter how much the Solver struggles, a wrong reference earns at most half, and the Solver's reasoning credit is gated by correctness ((w_c, w_r, w_f)=(0.5, 0.3, 0.2)) — a fluent but wrong chain of thought earns nothing for its reasoning.
- **Dispute resolution:** when ≥75% of formatted Solver answers agree on a value differing from the reference and all score zero correctness, the Verifier is shown the image plus both answers and independently re-solves the question; siding with the Solvers overwrites the reference, recomputes rewards, and zeroes the Questioner's correctness — directly correcting the known blind spot of consensus rewards (systematic shared errors winning the majority vote).
- **Empirical validation on two backbones and two corpora:** Qwen3-VL-8B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B, trained on Vero-600K (~600K images) and OmniScience (1.5M paper figures), improving every benchmark in both math and science, with a fully specified evaluation protocol and honest bookkeeping (Appendices A.4–A.5).
- **An unusually candid failure-mode analysis:** Appendix D concedes that the Verifier shares the policy's perceptual errors, that one 8B outer step costs 10–20 minutes on 8×A100, that joint-vs-alternating optimization is un-ablated, and that MME-SCI gains come almost entirely from multiple-choice items — each limitation paired with the experiment that would address it.

## Methodology

**Roles and loop.** For each unlabeled image x, the trainable policy π_θ plays Questioner (samples G candidate QA pairs (q, a) via role-specific prompts; the answer is a short reference) and Solver (samples K completions (τ, r̂), chain-of-thought plus a boxed answer, JSON-formatted). The Verifier π_φ is an EMA copy of the policy (updated φ ← φφ + (1−φ)θ, φ=0.999) used only for inference, so it lags the policy and cannot be exploited by it. After each outer step the Verifier is refreshed, so the reward landscape moves smoothly.

**Verifier QA screening (before supervision).** Given (x, q, a), the Verifier emits three signals: factuality R_fact-q ∈ {0,1} (every premise visible in the image, and q not answerable without x), answer correctness R_corra ∈ {0,1}, and difficulty R_diff on a four-level rubric (observable / single-step / multi-step / conceptual, clamped to [0,1]). Only format-valid candidates passing the gate are selected (highest difficulty, ties broken randomly); if none passes, the image contributes Questioner rollouts only. The gate is what separates a hard question from a broken one: the Solver fails on both, but only the former becomes supervision.

**Response evaluation.** Each Solver completion gets: correctness R_correct ∈ {0,1} via a rule-based numeric procedure (LaTeX/unit/number/ratio normalization, hard mismatch rejection, 1% tolerance, residual text-only equivalence check by the Verifier for ambiguous cases); reasoning quality R_reason ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5, 1} scored by the Verifier from the image and trajectory (penalizing visual misreads, invalid logic, shortcuts); and a rule-based format indicator.

**Dispute resolution.** If ≥75% of formatted Solver answers agree on a value different from a* and all scored R_correct = 0, the Verifier receives the image, both answers, and one supporting τ, solves the question itself, and picks a side. Siding with the Solvers overwrites a* and recomputes every R_correct; the Questioner's R_corra is set to 0. Consensus triggers verification; the Verifier, not the vote, decides.

**Rewards.** R_Q = −ρ_f if the format gate fails, else α_Q R_diff + β_Q R_chal where R_fact = R_fact-q(1 + R_corra)/2 and R_diff is discounted by the Verifier's prior and re-discounted by the realized challenge reward R_chal = 1 − R̄_correct (the empirical Solver failure rate over the K completions), with (α_Q, β_Q, ρ_f) = (0.3, 0.7, 0.5). R_S = w_c R_correct + w_r R_reason R_correct + w_f R_format^S with (w_c, w_r, w_f) = (0.5, 0.3, 0.2); truncated/malformed completions stay in the buffer with R_S = 0, penalizing non-termination.

**Optimization.** Rewards are standardized into group-relative advantages per image and role and optimized with GRPO-style clipped group-sequence loss (GSPO ratio), Questioner and Solver rollouts pooled in one joint step through shared LoRA weights (rank 32, scaling 64; vision encoder and embeddings frozen). Setup: single node of 8× NVIDIA A100 80GB, one worker per GPU; 500 outer steps for the 7B, 300 for the 8B; the Qwen3-VL-8B run starts from a 300-example supervised format warm-up built with Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking teacher reasoning (disclosed as supervised initialization).

**Data discipline.** The RL loader consumes raw images only — it never reads the corpora's question-answer annotations. Vero-600K (~600K images, ~16.7% STEM science diagrams: biology, geometry, pathology, radiology); ablation on OmniScience (1.5M figures from 300 journal/arXiv/medRxiv sources, 300+ sub-disciplines).

## Results & Highlights

- **Qwen3-VL-8B, Vero-600K images only (Table 1):** Trinity improves the base model on every benchmark — MathVerse 58.2 → 71.0 (+12.8), MMMU 57.0 → 69.6 (+12.6), SciVQR-Bio 56.7 → 65.3 (+8.6), EMVista 44.8 → 47.1 (+2.3), MME-SCI 19.0 → 20.3 (+1.3); the strongest same-backbone baseline, DPE, matches Trinity on MMMU but trails it by 13.8 points on MathVerse and does not improve EMVista (−0.7).
- **Released self-improving checkpoints (Table 1):** MM-Zero (same Qwen3-VL-8B backbone, trained on rendered synthetic images) falls below the base model on all three math benchmarks and barely moves the science ones (EMVista +0.3) — evidence that a synthetic curriculum does not transfer, whereas Trinity's image-grounded one does.
- **Qwen2.5-VL-7B vs published self-evolving methods (Table 2):** Trinity improves the base on MathVision 26.6 → 27.7 (+1.1), MathVerse 50.0 → 51.5 (+1.5), MathVista 70.7 → 71.9 (+1.2); on science it scores EMVista 46.9 vs base 43.9 (+3.0, best among evaluated methods) and SciVQR-Bio 41.1 vs 38.3 (+2.8), but loses MME-SCI to EvoLMM (11.7 vs 12.0). VisPlay posts the highest MathVision (31.2) yet drops 10.9 points on MathVerse (39.1 vs base 50.0); iReasor/V-Zero/EvoLMM all regress on MathVerse by 3–7 points. The paper itself attributes the much smaller 7B gains to the Verifier being the backbone: a weaker model screens and grades less accurately.
- **Corpus ablation:** the OmniScience variant (trained on scientific figures alone) matches or beats the Vero-600K variant — e.g., MME-SCI 21.9 vs 20.3 — and the paper reports the two variants as statistically indistinguishable on science benchmarks.
- **Training dynamics (Figure 2, OmniScience, steps 0–299):** question factuality R_factq rises 0.27 → 0.57, difficulty R_diff 0.11 → 0.23, Solver correctness R_correct 0.53 → 0.63, reasoning quality R_reason 0.76 → 0.93, while the challenge reward stays below 0.09 — the signature of a curriculum staying just ahead of the Solver (Vero-600K run: R_factq 0.19 → 0.45, R_correct 0.64 → 0.71, R_diff 0.29 → 0.55).
- **Grounding controls (Appendix E):** a text-only judge (Qwen3.5-27B) answers the self-generated questions correctly only 0–4 out of 20 samples without seeing the figure, and in two of four case studies the text-only prior actively favors the wrong option (14/20 for the R120A/R124A double mutant; 20/20 for MyoG) — evidence the questions genuinely require the image, not memorization.
- **Cost:** one 8B outer step (Questioner generation + Verifier screening + Solver generation + response evaluation + dispute resolution) takes on the order of 10–20 minutes on 8×A100 80GB; 300 steps take several days.

## Critical Assessment

- **The central circularity is real and only partially mitigated.** The Verifier is an EMA copy of the policy, so it has no source of factual authority independent of the model being trained: "a 7B or 8B model that misreads a chart as a Solver will misread it as a Verifier too," as the authors themselves put it. The dispute-resolution mechanism is the one place a wrong reference can be repaired, but its adjudicator is the same fallible model. The authors' audit found both false negatives (grounded questions marked ungrounded) and false positives (lenient reasoning scores) in training rollouts, and no rate is quantified — a missing measurement given the whole method rests on Verifier accuracy.
- **The headline gains are backbone-dependent in a way the abstract underplays.** On Qwen3-VL-8B the improvements are dramatic (MathVerse +12.8, MMMU +12.6), but on Qwen2.5-VL-7B they shrink to +1.1/+1.5/+1.2 — the paper honestly attributes this to the Verifier being the backbone itself, yet the abstract quotes only the 8B numbers. A +12.6 jump on MMMU from ~300 LoRA RL steps on unlabeled images also begs the question of how much is genuine reasoning gain versus format/thinking-mode alignment: the 8B run starts from a supervised 300-example warm-up using Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking teacher reasoning and evaluates with a thinking-prefix chat template, a confound not disentangled by an ablation (e.g., warm-up-only, no RL).
- **Baseline comparisons are indicative, not matched — by the authors' own admission.** VisPlay, iReasor, and V-Zero numbers are copied from their papers (no released checkpoints), so protocols differ; and there is a decoding asymmetry in the one science comparison that was re-run: Trinity is decoded with sampling (temperature 1.0, top-p 0.95) while the base model and EvoLMM are decoded greedily. On benchmarks near the format-termination failure mode (SciVQR open-response, where the paper says the base model often fails to terminate within the token budget), that asymmetry could inflate part of the +2.8 SciVQR gain.
- **Aggregation quality is genuinely praised.** The gains concentrate exactly where image grounding matters (microscopy, electron micrographs, rendered math figures) and the text-only controls in Appendix E are a thoughtful, cheap, and convincing check that the self-generated curriculum is not solvable from priors — many self-play papers omit anything comparable.
- **Scope caveats are disclosed but material:** gains on MME-SCI come almost entirely from multiple-choice items (open-response on par with base); no advantage on multi-image questions; only two Qwen-family backbones in the 7–8B range, so transfer beyond the family is untested.

## Strengths

- Removes the two structural dependencies of self-evolving VLMs — human labels and external judge models — by making verification a free byproduct of the policy itself (EMA copy), which also means stronger backbones should yield stronger verifiers at no extra cost.
- The verifier-gated reward design is principled: grounding and reference-correctness are hard gates (0 reward below them), difficulty is measured as the Solver's realized empirical failure rate rather than a subjective rubric, and reasoning credit is multiplied by correctness so fluent-but-wrong chains earn nothing.
- Dispute resolution is a concrete mechanism against the majority-vote blind spot that plagues V-Zero/EvoLMM-style consensus rewards, with explicit reward consequences for the Questioner when its reference is overturned.
- Exceptional reproducibility hygiene for a workshop paper: per-backbone RL settings, judge prompts, evaluation protocol (which benchmarks single-run vs 4-seed, which judge model), verbatim episode transcripts, and released training images.
- Honest, experiment-paired limitations section — rare, and it converts weaknesses into a research roadmap (fixed external Solvers of different sizes to isolate Verifier accuracy; lightweight external anchors like retrieval or a symbolic checker inserted at dispute resolution).

## Limitations

- Verifier reliability is bounded by the backbone's own perception and reasoning; shared systematic errors between policy and Verifier are invisible in-loop, and no audit rate for false positives/negatives is reported.
- Large memory footprint: three co-resident roles (trainable policy + optimizer state, EMA Verifier as a full second weight copy, vLLM rollout engine) require 8×A100 80GB with addition-time weight swapping; 10–20 minutes per outer step on the 8B, several days for 300 steps, and Verifier calls scale with G×K — more expensive per training example than supervised RL on annotated data (the acknowledged price of not needing annotations).
- Joint (pooled) vs alternating optimization of the two roles is un-ablated, so the contribution of the joint update is unquantified.
- Coverage: two backbones, one model family (Qwen), 7–8B only; no cross-family evidence (Gemini/Molmo architectures untested).
- Gain scope: no benefit on multi-image questions; MME-SCI improvement is essentially multiple-choice only; benchmarks measure components of scientific reasoning, not biological discovery utility (no faithfulness/calibrated-uncertainty/downstream-task evaluation).
- Baseline evidence is mixed-protocol: several numbers taken from other papers, one sampling-vs-greedy decoding asymmetry in the re-run science comparison.

## Relevance

这篇论文直接命中你最高优先级的"Self-Evolving Agents & Automated Research"方向：它把 LLM 自博弈（proposer-solver）范式扩展到多模态科学图像域，并用模型自身的 EMA 副本解决"无执行器时奖励从哪来"这一自动奖励设计的核心难题。同时它对自进化闭环可靠性（验证者与策略共享盲区、奖励信号的系统性偏差）的坦诚分析，恰好是你在自动化智能体系统可信性方向会关心的实证素材。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- What is the measured Verifier error rate (false negatives on grounded questions, false positives on lenient reasoning) across training, and how does it correlate with final benchmark gains? The audit is mentioned but not quantified.
- Does pairing the EMA Verifier with a fixed, stronger external Solver (different size) recover the missing 7B gains — i.e., is the Verifier, not the curriculum, the binding constraint on weaker backbones?
- How much of the 8B MMMU +12.6 comes from the supervised thinking-mode warm-up versus the RL loop itself (a warm-up-only ablation is absent)?
- Would inserting a lightweight external anchor (retrieval over a figure caption corpus, or a symbolic numeric checker) at the dispute-resolution stage eliminate systematic shared errors without reintroducing annotation dependency?
- Does the loop transfer across model families (Gemini/Molmo) and to multi-image scientific questions, where the current design shows no advantage over the base model?
- Can the three co-resident roles be compressed (quantized EMA copy, shared vision encoder) to bring the cost below 8×A100, and does Verifier fidelity degrade with compression?
