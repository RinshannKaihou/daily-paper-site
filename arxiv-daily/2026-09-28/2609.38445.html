01

Methodology

maximize f(x) = v(g(x))  ·  subject to Σ c(xᵢ) ≤ N  —  evidence, not ideas, is the expensive part

Each idea x — title, hypothesis, experiment plan — passes through an expensive implementation process g and a fixed verifier v. AIM therefore manages a pool of ~10 ideas rather than generating more of them, deliberately reusing ScientistOne's Ideator and Gemini Deep Solver so the comparison isolates the management layer alone. Four modules close the loop:

AAgentic Surrogate

Every step, re-clusters the whole pool into 2–5 cross-lineage semantic clusters and emits ordinal (rank-only, no calibrated scores) promise estimates at cluster and idea level.

BAgentic Acquisition

Dispatches Bt parallel branches with two-tier explore/exploit actions; expands the pool via refine / cross-pollinate / error-repair / novel modes, ≤3 new ideas per branch.

CSolution Auditor

Flags trivial / task-mismatch / idea-mismatch / reward-hacking. On idea-mismatch it reconstructs the idea actually implemented and reattributes scores and lessons — flags drop from 19–64% to 1–29%.

DResource Planner

Adaptively sets parallelism Bt (B₁=5, then 3–10) inside a fixed 25-branch, ≤300-execution budget, trading breadth against feedback frequency.

Idea→ Implement→ Audit→ Align  — theory: coverage C ≥ (K/G_ε)·ln(1/δ) suffices to find an ε-optimal direction w.p. 1−δ

03

Strengths vs Limitations

＋Strengths

  • Clean conceptual core: the solution-driven vs idea-driven taxonomy and the idea–solution integrity problem are well articulated; reconstruct-and-reattribute is a concrete novel answer.
  • Principled skeleton: the BO analogy avoids calibrated score prediction — and the ordinal-vs-raw-score ablation validates it (90.5 vs 89.3).
  • Long-horizon gains: +4.9 pp where baselines degrade badly, robust under solver substitution.
  • Exemplary hygiene: same backbone, same brief, an inconvenient ablation reported and analyzed.
  • Falsifiable theory probed by a designed convex-hull coverage experiment, not decoration.

−Limitations

  • Single backbone: all conclusions conditional on Gemini-3.1-Pro; auditor untested with weaker or cross-family judges.
  • 3 runs only: several System-Opt deltas fall within SE overlap (baseline SEs up to ±19.4).
  • Missing rivals: AIDE and AlphaEvolve never evaluated despite anchoring the taxonomy.
  • "Fully autonomous" = management only: Ideator and solver inherited; ideation quality unstudied.
  • Theory never operationalized: K and G_ε are never quantified, so it predicts no per-task wins.
  • Cost: 90M+ tokens per run, no monetary figure — prohibitive outside industrial labs.
04

Open threads

  1. Can K and G_ε be estimated online during a run, so the theory predicts per-task whether idea- or solution-driven allocation should dominate — and the Planner switches dynamically?
  2. What is the auditor's accuracy vs ground truth under deliberately injected reward-hacks — and does a cross-family judge (Claude auditing Gemini code) change verdict quality?
  3. Would task-conditioned embeddings recover LLM-level clustering (83.4 → 90.5) at lower cost than LLM Organize?
  4. Does the integrity problem shrink as backbones improve — eroding the Auditor's reason to exist?
  5. A hybrid trigger: detect smooth, local-search-friendly landscapes early and degrade into AdaEvolve-style mutation refinement (per the paper's own continuum discussion)?
