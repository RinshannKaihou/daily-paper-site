> **一句话总结：** 在 EuroSAT 卫星图像分类任务上，作者对 ResNet-18 和 MobileNetV2 做 10 轮迭代全局结构化剪枝（每轮剪掉剩余输出通道的 14.9%，最终仅保留 20% 通道、MAC 预算 <20%）并做 8-bit QAT 量化，再用自研故障注入框架（Brevitas 8-bit PE 仿真 + 32-bit 累加器，每轮注入 102,600 个单比特翻转）测量 SDC-1 率与 FIT 率：剪枝确实让**单次推理**的 SDC-1 率随宽度下降而上升，但由于运算量减少使推理途中遭遇 SEU 的概率同比例下降，ResNet-18 的 FIT 率基本持平、MobileNetV2 的 FIT 率甚至随剪枝小幅下降——结论是面向效率的结构化剪枝不会牺牲系统级可靠性，且 MobileNetV2 凭借 inverted residual 结构天然比 ResNet-18 抗辐射 2-3 倍。

> **TL;DR:** On EuroSAT (64x64 land-cover classification), the authors iteratively structured-prune ResNet-18 and MobileNetV2 down to 20% of original channels (<20% MAC budget) with 8-bit QAT, then inject 102,600 single-bit flips per pruning iteration into a simulated 8-bit accelerator PE with a 32-bit accumulator. Per-inference SDC-1 rate rises as width shrinks (redundancy removed), but the FIT rate — computed for a hypothetical 1 GMAC/s accelerator with 5 SEU/h — stays roughly flat for ResNet-18 and slightly *decreases* for MobileNetV2, which is intrinsically 2-3x more fault-tolerant than ResNet-18 due to its inverted-residual channel distribution. Bottom line: efficiency-oriented structured pruning does not compromise system-level reliability, and the destructive-fault fraction stays around 0.3%.

## 核心贡献

- **首次针对全局*结构化*剪枝的可靠性评估**：此前工作（Guillemé et al. 的非结构化剪枝研究、Goldstein et al. 的"小模型更少运算所以更可靠"论证）都只覆盖非结构化剪枝或未考虑高比特位宽累加寄存器中的故障；本文补上了"整通道移除 + 32-bit 累加器故障注入"这一空档。
- **自研故障注入（FI）框架**：基于 PyTorch + Brevitas 伪量化，仿真单个处理单元（PE）的完整数据流——8-bit 输入/权重、预置 32-bit bias、32-bit 累加器、8-bit 输出；校准阶段把 5 个模块（input/weight/bias/accumulator/output）的所有比特映射到全局地址空间，每次推理前随机选一个比特、用 PyTorch module hook 翻转。主存假设由 ECC 保护，聚焦无防护的 PE 本体。
- **实验流水线**：baseline 训练 → torch-pruning 库 + 一阶 Taylor 展开全局重要性做迭代结构化剪枝（10 轮 × 14.9%，末层不剪，每轮恢复训练）→ QAT 到 8-bit → FI campaign（每个模型每轮迭代注入 102,600 个故障）。
- **关键实证发现**：单次推理的 SDC-1 率随模型变小而上升（宽度的冗余确实在提供容错），但按"每 10^9 设备小时"计的 FIT 率在 ResNet-18 上基本不随剪枝变化、在 MobileNetV2 上还小幅下降——每推理敏感度上升与运算量减少两个效应相互抵消。
- **架构差异洞察**：MobileNetV2 虽然计算更轻，但其 inverted residual 结构使"轻量级"通道数与 ResNet-18 相当，信息分布更分散，计算出的 FIT 率比 ResNet-18 低 2-3 倍。
- **给空间 AI 系统的设计建议**：优先选能效/时延高效的结构化剪枝模型，不会牺牲整体可靠性；甚至可借模型固有的容错能力放宽加速器的抗辐射加固要求。

## Key Contributions

- First reliability study targeting *global structured* pruning with fault injection in a high-bit-width accumulator, closing a gap left by prior unstructured-pruning studies (Guillemé et al.) and operation-count arguments (Goldstein et al.).
- A PyTorch/Brevitas fault-injection framework that emulates a quantized PE datapath (8-bit in/weights, 32-bit accumulator, 8-bit out), calibrates a global bit address space across five modules, and flips one uniformly random bit per inference via module hooks — 102,600 injections per model per pruning iteration.
- A reproducible experimental pipeline: iterative torch-pruning with first-order Taylor importance (10 iterations x 14.9% of remaining channels, final layer kept unpruned, retraining after each step), QAT to 8-bit, then an FI campaign on ResNet-18 and MobileNetV2 trained on EuroSAT.
- Empirical result that per-inference SDC-1 rate increases as width shrinks, yet the hypothetical FIT rate remains roughly flat for ResNet-18 and slightly decreases for MobileNetV2 — reduced SEU exposure from fewer MACs counterbalances increased sensitivity.
- An architecture-level finding: MobileNetV2's inverted residual blocks keep information distributed over as many channels as ResNet-18 despite depthwise separability, yielding a calculated FIT rate 2-3x lower than ResNet-18's.
- Design guidance for space-borne AI: structured pruning delivers memory/energy/latency savings without compromising overall reliability, and may allow relaxed radiation-hardening requirements at the accelerator level.

## Methodology

**Fault-injection framework.** The FI tool is built on the PyTorch quantization package Brevitas with fake-quantized DNNs following Nagel et al.'s white-paper guidance: 8-bit inputs and weights per layer, a preloaded 32-bit bias, accumulation in a 32-bit register, and rounding to an 8-bit output via a power-of-two scale factor. This emulates the datapath of a single systolic-array Processing Element (PE) in the style of a Google TPU / Eyeriss hybrid, where parameters and activations are reused from local registers. Main memories are assumed ECC-protected (unlike PEs), so injection targets only PE-internal state.

The FI process has three steps: (1) **Calibration** — analyze the DNN inference and map every bit in the five modules (input, weight, bias, accumulator, output) to a global address space; (2) **Selection** — before each inference, pick one bit uniformly at random from the entire inference trace; (3) **Injection** — flip that bit during inference using PyTorch module hooks. Only single bit flips are injected: the probability of multiple independent SEUs inside one millisecond-scale inference is very low in typical space environments, and multi-bit upsets from a single particle are ignored by design.

**Model pipeline.** (1) *Baseline training* of ResNet-18 and MobileNetV2 to convergence on **EuroSAT** (80/10/10 train/val/test split), with random cropping (4-pixel padding), horizontal + vertical flips, discrete rotations (0/90/180/270 deg), 64x64 resize, and normalization. Both models use SGD (momentum 0.9, weight decay 1e-4, batch size 128, LR 0.1, 140 epochs with milestone drops). (2) *Iterative global structured pruning* via the torch-pruning library: grouped first-order Taylor expansion as the global importance criterion, pruning the least important output channels (final layer kept intact); 10 iterations each removing 14.9% of remaining channels, so the final model retains 20% of original channels; the network is retrained (unpruned channels recovered) after each pruning step (100 epochs per iteration). (3) *Quantization-aware training* (30 epochs) to adapt to 8-bit arithmetic. (4) *FI campaign*: 102,600 faults injected per model at every pruning iteration.

**Metrics.** Performance drop under faults is quantified via **SDC-1**: how often the top-1 prediction under injection differs from fault-free execution, counting both destructive (correct -> incorrect) and constructive (incorrect -> correct) faults. Architectural reliability is summarized as a hypothetical **FIT rate** (failures per 10^9 device-hours), assuming an accelerator with 1 GMAC/s peak suffering 5 SEU/h at full utilization and 10 inferences/s; pruning lowers device utilization, lowering the per-inference probability of encountering an SEU.

## Results & Highlights

- **MobileNetV2 is intrinsically 2-3x more fault-tolerant than ResNet-18.** Across the FI campaign on EuroSAT (102,600 faults per pruning iteration per model), MobileNetV2's calculated FIT rate is 2-3x lower than ResNet-18's. The authors attribute this to the inverted residual architecture: despite depthwise separable convolutions making it computationally lighter, its "lightweight" channel count is as high as ResNet-18's, so information stays distributed across many channels, preserving redundancy.
- **Aggressive pruning keeps the MAC budget below 20% with negligible accuracy loss.** After 10 iterations (14.9% of remaining channels each), the final model retains 20% of original channels and requires less than 20% of the original MAC count — with clean accuracy across pruning iterations varying by at most ~1% between best- and worst-performing models.
- **Destructive faults remain rare: ~0.3%.** The fraction of injected faults that flips a correct classification into an incorrect one stays around 0.3% across the campaign, so even though SDC-1 sensitivity rises, the overall accuracy degradation under faults is limited.
- **Per-inference SDC-1 rate rises as model size shrinks — but FIT does not.** For both architectures the SDC-1 rate increases with pruning, and the proportion of destructive faults grows with more aggressive pruning (widening the gap between clean and FI accuracy). However, in the hypothetical FIT calculation these factors largely cancel out for ResNet-18 (FIT roughly stable with pruning), while for MobileNetV2 the FIT rate shows a slight *decrease* as the model becomes smaller.
- **Design implication quantified.** Because pruned models execute fewer operations, the time window in which an SEU can occur during inference shrinks proportionally; the paper concludes structured pruning "can yield substantial savings in memory, energy, and latency without compromising overall reliability" for space-bound systems.

## Critical Assessment

- **The headline "pruning doesn't hurt reliability" rests on a hypothetical FIT model, not a measured one.** The FIT rate assumes a very specific setup — a 1 GMAC/s accelerator, 5 SEU/h at full utilization, 10 inferences/s — and the paper's own text concedes the cancellation effect "largely cancels out" for ResNet-18 while being "limited" for MobileNetV2. Under a different throughput or SEU-flux assumption the balance shifts; no sensitivity analysis over these parameters is given, so the most quotable conclusion is the least robust one.
- **No unstructured-pruning control arm.** The motivation positions this work against Guillemé et al. (unstructured pruning hurts fault tolerance) and Goldstein et al. (fewer ops help), but the paper never runs its own FI campaign with unstructured pruning or an unpruned-but-quantized baseline at matched accuracy, so the claim that *structured* pruning behaves differently is supported only by cross-paper comparison, not by an in-paper ablation.
- **Two architectures, one small dataset, one fault model.** EuroSAT at 64x64 with ResNet-18/MobileNetV2 is a modest empirical base; there is no transformer, no larger CNN, and no second dataset. The single-bit-flip-only assumption is justified for a millisecond inference window, but the paper ignores MBU-producing particles entirely, and the simulated datapath is a single generic PE rather than any concrete accelerator with a real (e.g., row-stationary or systolic-specific) reuse pattern — SEU impact is known to be accelerator-dependent [6], which the authors acknowledge but do not quantify.
- **The SDC-1 metric conflates constructive and destructive faults.** Faults that flip a wrong prediction into a right one are counted toward sensitivity; the paper does separate the counts in Figure 3, but the reported "small (~0.3%)" figure is only the destructive fraction, while SDC-1 occurrences at full width are an order of magnitude larger on the plot — a reader skimming the 0.3% number could overstate robustness.
- **Genuine strengths.** The deterministic pipeline (fixed pruning schedule, retraining each step, 102,600 injections per point) gives the trends real statistical weight, the ECC-vs-PE scoping is physically sensible, and the MobileNetV2-vs-ResNet-18 channel-distribution explanation is a mechanistic, testable insight rather than hand-waving.

## Strengths

- Directly targets a practically important and previously uncovered combination: *structured* pruning + faults in the 32-bit accumulator of a quantized accelerator PE (prior work covered unstructured sparsity or ignored accumulators).
- Clean, fully deterministic experimental protocol — fixed 10-step pruning schedule, retraining between steps, a large 102,600-fault injection count per model per iteration — making the reported trends statistically credible rather than anecdotal.
- Reports the trade-off from both angles: micro-level (per-inference SDC-1 rate, destructive vs. constructive split) and macro-level (FIT rate under an explicit utilization model), which is exactly the framing a system designer needs.
- The MobileNetV2 2-3x FIT advantage is explained mechanistically (inverted residuals keep channel count high, preserving distributed redundancy), not just observed.
- Honest scoping: ECC-protected main memory vs. unprotected PEs, single-bit-only injections, and an explicitly labeled "hypothetical" FIT setup are all stated up front rather than buried.

## Limitations

- The FIT-rate conclusions depend on a hypothetical accelerator configuration (1 GMAC/s, 5 SEU/h, 10 inferences/s); no parameter sweep is provided to show how robust the "sensitivity vs. exposure cancellation" is to those choices.
- Empirical base is narrow: two CNNs on one 64x64 dataset (EuroSAT); generalization to transformers, detection/segmentation heads, or larger-scale space workloads is untested.
- No in-paper unstructured-pruning or non-pruned quantized control at matched accuracy, so the structured-vs-unstructured reliability contrast relies on prior papers run under different settings.
- The FI framework simulates one abstract PE datapflow; real accelerators (Eyeriss-style row-stationary, multi-PE arrays) have different reuse patterns that can amplify or mask SEU effects, acknowledged in the text but not measured.
- Multi-bit upsets and faults accumulating over long autonomous missions (outside the single-inference window) are out of scope; only top-1 classification divergence is measured — no mission-level or confidence-calibration analysis of the catastrophic high-confidence misclassifications the discussion itself flags as the real safety risk.
- No public code release is mentioned, and clean accuracy varies by up to 1% across pruning iterations, a residual variance the procedure only "minimizes," not eliminates.

## Relevance

这篇论文正中用户的核心研究方向"Inference Reliability & SDC Detection"：它是一篇典型的 bit-flip 故障注入 + silent data corruption 量化研究，且同时覆盖了剪枝/8-bit 量化这类激进优化对精度与可靠性的影响（对应"Reliability under Extreme Performance Optimization"）。其"运算量减少降低 SEU 暴露窗口"的系统级视角，对评估 inference 效率优化与 SDC 概率之间的耦合很有参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How sensitive is the FIT-rate cancellation effect to the assumed accelerator throughput (1 GMAC/s), SEU flux (5 SEU/h), and inference rate (10/s)? A parameter sweep would show when per-inference sensitivity starts dominating.
- Does the same trade-off hold for unstructured pruning at matched MAC budgets or matched clean accuracy, run through the authors' own FI framework?
- Would the MobileNetV2-vs-ResNet-18 advantage persist for transformer-style architectures, where redundancy is distributed over attention heads and MLP widths rather than convolution channels?
- How does the picture change with multi-bit upsets, or with faults accumulating across a long-running autonomous mission (e.g., weight memory degrade without ECC refresh)?
- Can the channel-redundancy explanation be turned into a *pruning criterion* — i.e., prune with an explicit fault-tolerance regularizer so that SDC-1 does not rise with width reduction?
- The paper flags radiation-induced SDCs as aleatoric (unlike epistemic uncertainty) and capable of producing high-confidence catastrophic misclassifications — can conformal or abstention mechanisms on top of a pruned model bound that risk in a certifiable way?
