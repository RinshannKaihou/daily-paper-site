Critical edge

Three of four planted pathway families are sanity checks by construction — visible exactly where HPC clamps. On the informative in-range case, the headline detector itself fails: best AUROC 0.48 on Qwen2.5-7B gender.

01

How they tested it

plant the shortcut → label who uses it → score the detectors

A binary decision is the logit difference between two answer tokens. The authors add 24 ReLU units per configuration whose bias sits above the maximum natural activation — silent on every benchmark prompt, natural behaviour unchanged — at a downstream layer of each model. Four families: silent, hijack, distributed, and in-range (units individually natural, unnatural only in combination).

1,500 held-out interventions per configuration run activation patching, mean-difference steering, and DAS variants — including restricted DAS, an “illusion probe” locked out of the genuine subspace, where any success must come from outside it. Exact labels follow by construction: pathway-dominated (pathways supply >50% of the decision margin) and wrong-mechanism success (the decision flips when pathways are off).

Detectors: site-level manifold distances (kNN, Mahalanobis, local-PCA, Grant et al.’s Algorithm 1) versus the causal HPC — clamp every downstream MLP neuron to its natural regime, layer by layer, and measure the fraction of the decision margin lost.

04

Strengths & limitations

read together — the honest headline is narrower than “we can detect wrong-mechanism successes”

Strengths

  • Exact ground truth by construction — a minimal edit leaves natural behaviour untouched yet labels every intervention, solving the problem Grant et al. left open.
  • Scale rare for a two-author paper — 928 configurations, 1.4M interventions, 4 models spanning 124M–7B, 7 intervention types, multi-hardware execution.
  • A service negative result — the below-chance finding deflates a quality check currently practiced in the field.
  • The illusion probe turns the Makelow et al. anecdote into a controlled, repeatable measurement at four scales.
  • Exemplary claim calibration — the positive-control table bounds what the prevalence check can see (80–94% vs ≤2.6%).

Limitations

  • Detection is solid only for unit-visible pathways; in-range pathways stay essentially undetected on Qwen2.5-7B gender (best 0.48).
  • Narrow scope — two small templated grammar tasks, binary two-token decisions, MLP neurons at the final position only; attention-mediated pathways out of scope.
  • Weaker statistical hygiene — single seed for GPT-2 main configs, fixed untuned constants, cross-GPU AUROC shifts up to 0.21.
  • Mitigation tested at GPT-2 only — no evidence at 1.4B–7B scale, and it never fixes restricted-DAS false successes.
  • Natural regime from the same templated prompts being intervened on; downstream self-repair can bias the estimate.
  • No public code or benchmark release stated — despite the extensive reproducibility apparatus.
05

Open questions

what the paper hands to future work
  1. Can in-range (combination-level) pathways be detected at all — joint-density models, diffusion manifolds, attention-aware clamping — where every tested detector fails?
  2. Does the on-manifold penalty stay effective at 1.4B–7B scale, and does it transfer to restricted DAS?
  3. Do wrong-mechanism successes occur in realistic interpretability settings — multi-token answers, free-form generation, chain-of-thought or agentic pipelines where the “decision” is not a two-token logit difference?
  4. Are unmodified Qwen2.5-7B’s candidate signatures (30–32%) real shortcuts, or artifacts of downstream self-repair inflating the clamped margin?
  5. Would held-out natural prompt distributions or per-model tuned thresholds change the conclusion that site-level distances score below chance?
