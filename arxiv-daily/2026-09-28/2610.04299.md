> **一句话总结：** 针对 R-Zero 式"出题人-解题人"自我进化中性能崩塌的问题，作者诊断出两大根因——无效题目比例随轮次上升（有效率从 76.5% 跌至 39.5%，且答案一致性过滤反而使其恶化）和 BLEU 相似度无法识别的"换皮重复题"（前五大题型占比从 17.0% 涨到 69.0%）——并提出 R-Quest：先用 GPT-5.6-Sol 标注的 2,240 题数据集对解题人做"判废初始化"（能识别并拒绝 INVALID 题目），再让冻结基座模型做采样式两两判新（K=8），以双重反馈门控出题人奖励；在 Qwen3-4B-Base 上数学平均分达 51.64%（R-Zero 为 49.10%），十轮自进化后 51.92% vs R-Zero 同轮 34.60%，领先 17.32 分，且代码与通用推理同步提升。

> **TL;DR:** R-Quest fixes the performance collapse of self-evolving reasoning models (questioner-solver self-play à la R-Zero) by attacking two root causes: invalid self-generated questions (validity rate falls from 76.5% to 39.5% over five R-Zero rounds; a repair-intervention shows repairing them alone lifts the 7-benchmark math average from 46.54 to 49.91) and "disguised repetition" that BLEU similarity misses (top-5 question-type share grows 17.0%→69.0%). It adds (1) a validity-aware solver initialization — GRPO on 2,240 GPT-5.6-Sol-annotated questions teaching the solver to output INVALID for broken questions — and (2) a sampled pairwise novelty gate judged by the frozen base model (K=8, cost O(BK) instead of O(B²)). On Qwen3-4B-Base, R-Quest beats R-Zero 51.64% vs 49.10% average over seven math benchmarks (+2.54, and +5.47 over the 46.17% base), improves code (HumanEval+/MBPP+ avg 62.69 vs 59.27) and general reasoning (32.75 vs 31.21), and over ten self-evolution rounds reaches 51.92% at round ten while R-Zero collapses to 34.60% — a 17.32-point lead at the same iteration.

## 核心贡献

- **首次系统诊断自进化失败的根因**：对 R-Zero 五轮自进化产物逐轮审计，发现 (a) 无效题目（矛盾、无解、歧义、条件缺失等）占比随轮次恶化，且 R-Zero 的答案一致性过滤会"反向选择"出更多无效题——过滤后保留题目中有效率从 63% 跌至 27.64%；(b) 基于词汇相似度（BLEU 聚类）的多样性控制识别不出数学等价但表述不同的重复题，第五轮采样中前三大题型占 59.5%，最大题型被 BLEU 切成 22 个簇、惩罚被稀释。
- **因果干预实验**：构造配对数据集（1,273 题，含相同的 766 道有效题 + 507 道被 GPT-5.6-Sol 修复的无效题），相同 GRPO 预算下修复组平均 49.91 vs 原始组 46.54（+3.37 分），证明无效题是性能退化的直接原因而非相关现象。
- **R-Quest 方法**：双重反馈信号指导出题人——(1) 有效性反馈：用 2,240 题（923 有效 / 1,317 无效）通过带 INVALID 拒绝项的 GRPO 奖励初始化解题人，使其具备判废能力，自进化中以 9 票多数决作有效性门，并每轮 10% 回放防遗忘；(2) 新颖性反馈：由冻结基座模型对批内随机采样的 K=8 个题目做"同型判定"，概率化拒绝重复题型，成本从 O(B²) 降到 O(BK)，并给出精确的通过概率公式 Pr(pass|p) ≈ (1−p)^K。
- **持续增益的实证**：两个模型家族（Qwen3-4B-Base、Llama 系 OctoThinker-3B-Hybrid-Base）、12 个基准（数学 7 + 代码 2 + 通用推理 3）上取得所有对比方法（R-Zero、OCNR、R-Diverse）中的最高平均分；十轮自进化不崩塌，每轮 checkpoint 都超过 R-Zero 的最佳成绩。
- **组件级分析闭环**：消融（去有效性 / 去新颖性 / 去两者 / 冻结判废器）+ 过程指标（判废 balanced accuracy 64.80%→72.93%、无效题召回 86.36% vs R-Zero 的 10–26%、题目有效率 94.5–97.5% vs R-Zero 降到 49.0%、题型集中度 top-5 占比 24.0% vs 69.0%），机制与结果互相印证。

## Key Contributions

- **A diagnosis-first study of self-evolution collapse**: auditing R-Zero's outputs across rounds reveals two compounding failure modes — invalid questions (valid rate 76.5%→39.5% unfiltered, 63%→27.64% after R-Zero's answer-consistency filter, i.e., the filter selects *against* validity) and task-level repetition invisible to lexical/BLEU diversity controls (three largest types = 59.5% of a round-5 sample; the largest type is fragmented into 22 BLEU clusters, diluting the repetition penalty).
- **A causal repair intervention**: on matched 1,273-question datasets (same 766 valid questions; 507 invalid ones repaired by GPT-5.6-Sol), solver training reaches 49.91 vs 46.54 average math score (+3.37) under identical GRPO budget — isolating invalid questions as a contributing cause of degradation, not just a correlate.
- **R-Quest**, a two-signal self-evolution recipe: (1) validity feedback — the solver is initialized with GRPO on a 2,240-question annotated set (923 valid / 1,317 invalid) using a reward that credits correct answers and penalizes rejecting valid questions (λ=0.5), then gates questioner rewards via majority voting over 9 solver responses, with 10% replay each round; (2) novelty feedback — a frozen base-model judge performs sampled within-batch pairwise same-type comparisons (K=8), an exhaustive-gate approximation with cost O(BK) and closed-form pass probability Pr(pass|p) ≈ (1−p)^K; the questioner is rewarded for questions that are valid, novel, and of intermediate uncertainty (min(s, 1−s)).
- **Sustained-gain evidence across families and domains**: highest averages among R-Zero/OCNR/R-Diverse on 12 benchmarks over both Qwen3-4B-Base and OctoThinker-3B-Hybrid-Base, with a 10-round run where every R-Quest checkpoint beats R-Zero's best score (51.92 vs 34.60 at round ten, Δ=17.32) — the paper's headline demonstration that validity feedback prevents late-stage collapse while novelty feedback controls type concentration (top-5 share 24.0% vs R-Zero's 69.0%).
- **Mechanism-level validation**: solver invalid-recall reaches 86.36% by round ten (R-Zero: 10.23–26.14% throughout); generated-question validity rises to 94.5–97.5%; majority-vote pseudo-label accuracy on valid questions stays at 54.69% at round ten while R-Zero's falls to 2.06% from a comparable ~72.7% start.

## Methodology

**Diagnosis.** Starting from R-Zero's questioner-solver co-training loop (both models from one base init, alternating GRPO; questioner rewarded for majority-answer consistency s(q) near 1/2, penalized by within-batch pairwise-BLEU clustering C(q)/B), the authors sample 200 questions per round from each of five R-Zero rounds and have GPT-5.6-Sol judge validity, and analyze repetition by having GPT-5.6-Sol group questions by underlying mathematical task structure. A controlled repair experiment (matched valid core + repaired invalid questions, identical GRPO steps) quantifies the causal cost of invalid questions.

**Validity-aware solver initialization.** They deduplicate questions from the solver training sets of five R-Zero iterations (460 sampled per iteration) into a 2,240-question set (923 valid / 1,317 invalid; invalid taxonomy: contradiction 28.9%, no solution 27.5%, ambiguity 23.9%, missing information 13.7%, poorly formed 5.5%), annotated in two GPT-5.6-Sol passes (attempt-solve + validity classification, then independent solving of valid questions with multi-stage answer review and human adjudication of flagged cases). The base model is GRPO-trained (15 steps, 8 responses/question, lr 1e-6, KL 1e-2) to answer valid questions or output INVALID, with reward R = 1[a = a*] − λ·1[a = INVALID ∧ a* ≠ INVALID]+, λ = 0.5.

**Self-evolution with dual feedback.** Per round with a 10,000-question budget: a generated question first passes the validity gate (9 solver responses; majority INVALID → reward 0); survivors get the standard problem-solving treatment and a questioner reward R = R_uncertainty = min(s(q), 1−s(q)) only if the novelty gate passes. The novelty gate uses a frozen copy of the base model to judge whether the candidate and K=8 uniformly sampled batch peers are "the same exercise type" (same distinctive setup and task; numeric/wording changes don't count); any single same-type match rejects. Solver training keeps R-Zero's answer-consistency pseudo-labels (s(q) ∈ [0.3, 0.8]) but mixes in 10% replay from the validity-initialization data to retain the INVALID skill. Questioner and solver update alternately for 5 rounds in the main comparison and 10 rounds in the sustained-gain study.

**Evaluation.** Qwen3-4B-Base and OctoThinker-3B-Hybrid-Base; seven math benchmarks (AMC, Minerva, MATH, GSM8K, OlympiadBench, AIME25, AIME24 — pass@32 on AMC/AIME, greedy elsewhere), SuperGPQA/MMLU-Pro/BBEH (greedy), HumanEval+/MBPP+ (pass@1); baselines: base model, base + validity init, R-Zero, OCNR, R-Diverse; best-average checkpoint per domain reported per method.

## Results & Highlights

- **Math, Qwen3-4B-Base (avg over 7 benchmarks):** R-Quest 51.64% vs R-Zero 49.10%, OCNR 49.57%, R-Diverse 49.82%, base 46.17% — +5.47 over base and +2.54 over the strongest self-evolution baseline R-Zero (1.87x R-Zero's gain). Per-benchmark: AMC 57.89 vs base 47.34 (+10.55), OlympiadBench 46.67 vs R-Zero 42.37, AIME25 13.44 vs base 11.35, AIME24 14.48 vs base 10.73 — R-Quest holds the best score in every math column.
- **Math, OctoThinker-3B (Llama family):** R-Quest 30.69% avg vs R-Zero 28.25%, OCNR 28.12%, R-Diverse 28.61%, base 24.90% (+5.79 over base, +2.44 over R-Zero); notably AIME25 0.31 vs base 0.00 and MATH 55.80 vs base 41.80.
- **Sustained self-evolution (10 rounds, Qwen):** R-Zero peaks at 49.10 in round three, then declines to 34.60 by round ten — below the 46.17 base model — whereas R-Quest keeps climbing to 51.92 at round ten, a +17.32-point gap at the same iteration, and every R-Quest checkpoint from rounds 1–10 exceeds R-Zero's all-time best. Removing validity feedback reproduces late-stage collapse; removing only novelty feedback shows a milder decline that stays above base.
- **Transfer beyond math:** Qwen code avg (HumanEval+/MBPP+) 62.69 vs base 58.79 (+3.90) and vs R-Zero 59.27 / OCNR 60.28 / R-Diverse 60.50; OctoThinker code 18.90 vs base 13.67 (+5.23, MBPP+ 20.11 vs 12.70). General reasoning Qwen avg 32.75 vs R-Zero 31.21 (SuperGPQA 29.40 vs 26.21, MMLU-Pro 57.36 vs 51.41, BBEH 11.50 vs 8.74); OctoThinker general 15.50 vs base 8.30.
- **Component ablations (Qwen, Math/Code/General):** full R-Quest 51.64/62.69/32.75; w/o novelty 51.02/61.98/31.80; w/o validity 50.27/61.28/31.53; w/o both (= R-Zero) 49.10/59.27/31.21; frozen validity judge (no continued validity training) 49.93/61.85/32.39 — both signals contribute, and continued in-loop validity training beats a frozen judge.
- **Mechanism metrics:** solver invalid-question recall reaches 86.36% at round ten (R-Zero stays in 10.23–26.14%); generated-question validity rate (GPT-5.6-Sol judged) 88.0%→94.5–97.5% for R-Quest vs R-Zero falling 76.5%→49.0%; majority-vote answer accuracy on valid questions 54.69% at round ten vs R-Zero's 2.06% (both start ~72.7%); question-type top-5 concentration 24.0% (K=8) vs R-Zero's 69.0% — though stricter K=16 lowers concentration to 16.8% while *losing* math score, showing diversity control has a sweet spot.

## Critical Assessment

- **"Self-contained" self-evolution is only true after a frontier-model bootstrap.** The validity-aware initialization depends entirely on GPT-5.6-Sol: it labels the 2,240 questions, produces reference answers, and repairs questions in the causal experiment. The one-time-cost argument is fair, but the claim of self-contained self-evolution should be read as "no external model *in the loop*," not "no external model." Moreover, all process-level quality metrics (validity rate, majority-vote accuracy) are judged by the same GPT-5.6-Sol family whose annotation protocol shaped the method — a shared-utility bias that favors R-Quest's notion of validity, though it is at least applied uniformly to R-Zero.
- **A quiet irony in the setup:** the initialization dataset is built by sampling from *R-Zero's own first five rounds of solver training data*. R-Quest is partially bootstrapped from its baseline's byproduct, which complicates the comparison narrative — the baseline's failure modes are literally the training signal for the fix.
- **Best-checkpoint selection flatters all methods in Table 1/2.** "For each domain, we report the checkpoint with the highest average benchmark score across evaluated rounds" is applied uniformly, but absolute numbers are best-case draws across rounds; the five-round main table alone would overstate sustainability. The 10-round study is the honest headline and should be treated as the primary result.
- **Mixed decoding metrics inside one average.** AMC and AIME use pass@32 while the other five math benchmarks use greedy decoding; averaging these into a single "math avg" number (and basing +2.54/+17.32 claims on it) mixes different estimands without a breakdown of how much each benchmark drives the deltas.
- **No statistical treatment of the RL runs.** Self-evolution runs are single-seed and expensive; apart from Wilson confidence intervals on the validity-rate plots (Fig. 6c), there are no error bars or variance estimates on the benchmark tables, and gaps like +0.92 general-avg over R-Diverse (32.75 vs 31.76) are within plausible run-to-run noise.
- **The novelty gate is probabilistic, not exact** — at a 10% same-type fraction, K=8 still passes 43% of duplicates (the authors analyze this honestly), and the K=16 result (lower concentration but *worse* math) shows the metric being optimized (top-5 share) is not monotone with performance; the "diversity sweet spot" is asserted post hoc rather than derived.
- **Scale is untested.** All claims come from 3–4B base models; whether validity feedback remains load-bearing for larger models that generate fewer invalid questions in the first place is open, and code gains are measured on saturation-prone HumanEval+/MBPP+ (pass@1).

## Strengths

- Diagnosis before treatment: the repair-intervention experiment (+3.37 from fixing invalid questions under matched budgets) is a clean causal test that most self-play papers skip, and the discovery that answer-consistency filtering *anti-selects* invalid questions is a genuinely useful, transferable insight.
- The two identified failure modes are well chosen and quantified with concrete numbers (76.5%→39.5% validity decay; 17.0%→69.0% top-5 concentration), each mapped to a targeted mechanism rather than a generic regularizer.
- The sampled pairwise novelty gate comes with an exact closed-form pass probability and an explicit cost analysis (O(BK) vs O(B²)) — rare analytical care in this literature.
- Ablations cover both components plus a frozen-judge control, and process metrics (invalid recall, question validity, pseudo-label accuracy, type concentration) connect the mechanism to the outcome; results replicate across two model families and three task domains.
- Sustained-gain claim is demonstrated at the horizon where it matters (10 rounds, R-Zero below base while R-Quest peaks), directly addressing the collapse phenomenon the paper set out to fix.
- Reproducibility is taken seriously: all prompts (annotation, judging, grouping, generation), hyperparameters, and baseline configs are in appendices, with project page and code released.

## Limitations

- Dependence on a proprietary frontier model (GPT-5.6-Sol) for bootstrapping labels/answers and for the diagnostic metrics limits standalone reproduction and adds unquantified label-noise (mitigated by human review of flagged cases, but coverage of that review is not quantified).
- Evidence base is two small (3–4B) base models and a math-only self-evolution curriculum; domain generality (e.g., code questioners judging code validity) is untested.
- Best-checkpoint-per-domain reporting and pass@32/greedy mixing make table-level numbers harder to interpret; no multi-seed variance or significance testing.
- The novelty gate only suppresses repetition probabilistically within the rollout batch (43% duplicate pass-through at 10% repetition with K=8) and the K trade-off is explored only up to 16.
- The validity gate costs 9 solver responses per question plus 10 problem-solving samples for survivors — a substantial inference overhead per accepted question that is never itemized in compute terms; OCNR's required twofold oversampling is noted, but a full FLOPs-per-final-point comparison across methods is absent.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the GPT-5.6-Sol bootstrap be replaced by self-generated validity labels (e.g., solver self-consistency or code-execution verification as in Absolute Zero) without losing the initialization's benefit — i.e., how much of the gain needs a teacher smarter than the student?
- Does the validity feedback remain necessary at larger scale (7B–70B), where generation quality is higher and invalid questions are rarer, or does the bottleneck shift entirely to repetition/novelty control?
- The K=16 result shows lower type concentration with worse math — is there a principled way to set K adaptively per round (e.g., from the measured same-type fraction) rather than a fixed budget?
- Does the top-five-share concentration metric and the validity taxonomy transfer to non-math self-play domains (code generation, agentic tool use), where "same task type" is harder to adjudicate?
- What is the end-to-end compute accounting — 9 validity responses plus up to 10 solutions per candidate question — relative to R-Zero and OCNR per unit of benchmark gain, and does the validity replay (10% per round) ever hurt by anchoring the solver to early-round distributions?
