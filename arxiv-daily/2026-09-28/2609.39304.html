Why it matters

How much evidence — and which acceptance rule — make a self-evolving agent loop trustworthy. The two knobs a practitioner actually controls, both quantified.

“小批量过拟合、无选择规则则性能漂移” — 设计长周期自主改进循环的人都该读的负结果。

Critical edge

One campaign per batch size, zero replicates. The non-monotone 47/54/51 ordering sits within unquantified campaign variance, and promotions use raw strict-greater-than on 100 binary rollouts — no significance test — despite the authors citing exactly that pitfall in the LIBERO literature. Unconditional-acceptance runs were also stopped early, on a visible downtrend, versus full 30-round CC runs.

01

What was actually tested

Only the harness evolves. Everything that could excuse a result stays frozen.

The harness — system prompt, MCP tool semantics, waypoint control rules, verification and termination logic — is ordinary code in the agent’s repo, and is the sole object of optimization. The task agent is an unmodified Codex session (GPT-5.6 Sol) driving a simulated manipulator through browser screenshots; model, simulator, task set, success detectors and the evaluation control plane are frozen.

Each round, a separate persistent optimizer session must find one systemic, task-general failure mechanism and ship it as a single bounded commit — the evaluation plane is untouchable and the repo’s test suite must pass. Held-out performance (100 rollouts, 10 disjoint LIBERO tasks) is measured once, after the campaign, never visible to the optimizer.

Two knobs under study

B ∈ {5, 10, 20, 100} — rollouts per decision; each batch size an isolated campaign (own optimizer session, branch, namespace, no information sharing).

Selection rule — unconditional acceptance (single commit chain, every revision becomes the base) vs champion–challenger: promote only on a strict win over the incumbent on the same fixed cases; ties and regressions are logged and discarded.

Training: lift/square/stack + 7 LIBERO suites. Each rollout: one full agentic episode, 30-min cap, binary verdict. B=100 evaluates in ~one episode of wall-clock (concurrent sandboxes).

STEP 1

Evaluate

Run champion harness on the fixed batch of B (task, seed) cases.

STEP 2

Collect evidence

Manifest keeps every trajectory: turns, tool calls, controller feedback, errors, verdicts.

STEP 3

Propose

Optimizer reads evidence + recent rejection reasons, ships one bounded revision.

STEP 4

Select

Challenger reruns the same B cases; selection rule decides promotion.

03

Where it holds — where it cracks

Clean design and honest negatives; thin statistics and a simulation-only ceiling.

Strengths +

  • +Clean experimental design: fixed (task, seed) cases, frozen models/detectors/control plane, held-out measured once post-campaign, per-campaign isolation.
  • +Actionable, quantified knobs: batch size and selection rule are the two levers a practitioner controls — 47→67% across B; 42→34% drift vs monotone CC.
  • +Honest negative results: its own method failing at small B, and 22/30 optimizer proposals being regressions.
  • +Two transfer checks: environment (RoboCasa 2→37) and model (evolved + Astra 83/100).
  • +Engages the critical evaluation literature (Jiang et al., Wang et al.) instead of ignoring it, and states its own limits.

Limitations −

  • −One campaign per condition: campaign-level variance unquantified; only the coarse B=100-vs-small contrast is robust.
  • −No statistical treatment of promotions: a 2–3 pt flip is binomial noise; no significance test or minimum-margin rule.
  • −Asymmetric stopping: unconditional runs halted at 10–20 rounds on visible downtrend vs full 30-round CC.
  • −Narrow evaluation surface: 10 LIBERO held-out tasks; RoboCasa has no held-out set; binary success only, no cost/efficiency reporting.
  • −Simulation-only and expensive: B=100 per decision is infeasible on real hardware; no cheaper proxies (caching, adaptive batches) tested; scaling curve stops at B=100 while still rising.
  • −Reproducibility: no code release; results tied to proprietary frontier-model behavior.
04

What’s still open

Six questions the paper raises but does not answer.
Q1

Would a statistically grounded promotion rule (paired significance test, minimum margin) beat raw strict-greater-than — or stall promotion frequency the way small batches do?

Q2

Can the fixed B cases be cached and reused across rounds (re-running only affected cases), cutting the cost that makes B=100 the practical ceiling?

Q3

Does B-scaling hold with a weaker or open-weights task model, whose failure modes differ qualitatively from GPT-5.6 Sol?

Q4

What happens beyond B=100 — where does held-out success saturate, and is there a batch size above which proposals start diluting?

Q5

Does the champion’s monotonicity survive distribution shift within evolution, given the same B cases are frozen for all 30 rounds?

Q6

Can the loop reach physical robots via cheaper surrogates (partial rollouts, off-policy reuse), or is per-decision rollout scale the binding constraint?
