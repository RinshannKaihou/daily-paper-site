Self-benchmarked. All six evaluations come from the authors’ own RSIBench-Data suite — selection and scoring on the same identities, single runs, no error bars, no held-out transfer.

01

How the forest searches

Data-centric RSI becomes tree search over data strategies: each candidate is a data-engineering program whose run synthesizes a dataset, LoRA-trains a fixed base model (Qwen3.5-35B-A3B-Base), and scores downstream utility. Because every step trains in the loop, evaluation is expensive — a single deep tree over-commits to one direction; naive parallelism duplicates failures or drowns context in raw traces.

Gödel Forest runs K = 3 workers, each cultivating a persistent tree with an evidence ledger. Coordination is asynchronous and pull-based through a shared evolution memory: a global leaderboard of verified utilities plus structured lessons of ~100 tokens each ⟨intent, ΔU, attribution, rule⟩. Negative lessons prune paths peers already falsified; positive ones seed new directions. Runs cap at 16 h wall-clock and a $500 budget; the winning checkpoint is frozen and re-evaluated canonically.

06

Strengths

  • Clean depth–breadth decomposition: persistent per-worker trees for depth; a leaderboard + tiny distilled lessons for breadth, without trace-sharing context bloat.
  • The coordination ablation directly tests the central claim — equal-budget 3-Indep. (47.00%) vs. Forest (54.00%).
  • Diagnostic gating prunes weak candidates before committing full-benchmark training cycles.
  • Honest negative reporting: cost and wall-clock regressions stated plainly, not hidden.
  • Cross-backbone replication with Codex gpt-5.6-sol reduces single-harness artifacts.
07

Limitations

  • All gains measured on the authors’ own benchmark suite; no held-out generalization or transfer result.
  • Single run per configuration — no seeds or error bars; GPQA (+3.00) and AIME deltas are statistically fragile.
  • No compute-matched comparison vs. the vanilla baseline; up to ~9× cost, ~2× slower on Terminal-Bench 2.0.
  • Load-bearing recipe choices — lesson schema, gating threshold, Opus 4.8 rollout generator — are unablated; K=3 validated only on SWE-bench Verified.
  • Worker-chosen diagnostic subsets risk optimizing the gate, not the objective; forest-wide propagation of wrong lessons is unanalyzed.
08

Questions for further exploration

  1. Do the +10.70-pt gains transfer to a held-out benchmark outside the selection loop — or to full fine-tuning and larger backbones?
  2. How robust is the forest to one poisoned or mis-attributed lesson? Is there a mechanism for lesson verification or expiry?
  3. Is K=3 optimal on the other five benchmarks, and how does the Pareto frontier shift with cheaper training backends?
  4. Can the ~100-token lesson schema be made machine-verifiable — e.g., replayable counterfactual checks — rather than trusting the rollout LLM’s attribution?
  5. How does it fare head-to-head against Mendel / Huxley Gödel Machine or AutoData under matched compute?
