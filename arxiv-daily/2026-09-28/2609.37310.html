01

Methodology — verify first, then let agents loose

The loop

A watermark is a keyed logits transformation plus a detector. AutoMark first fixes what “valid” means: three criteria — distortion-freeness, soundness over the key, and the new soundness-given-a-key condition — each enforced by an automatic statistical test (Algs. 1–3, valid at level δ) that rejects bad submissions before they reach the leaderboard.

Frontier coding agents (GPT-6 Astra, Opus 5, Gemini-3.8 Flash) then iterate in a sandboxed verify-then-evaluate harness built on a four-component library: context seeding, watermark score, logits transformation, detector. The first agent gets 4 official submissions, later agents 1 — deliberately forcing genuine ideas over hyperparameter tuning.

Official metrics: TPR@1%FPR under four edit attacks, plus perplexity / Self-BLEU quality deltas. Finally, all 52 submissions were manually decomposed into 39 atomic components and ablated one-at-a-time against an AAR base; the two winners — ConcordMark and RotationFirst — were re-evaluated on a fully held-out protocol (ELI5-EN + WildChat-ZH · Llama-3.1-8B + Qwen3.8-27B · DeepSeek-V4 Flash as attacker).

Three validity criteria

  • EQ. 1 — DISTORTION-FREENESSThe watermark changes nothing on average. For every next-token distribution, the expectation over keys is unchanged. Tested by averaging watermarked distributions over independent keys (Alg. 1).
  • EQ. 2 — SOUNDNESS OVER THE KEYFalse positives stay rare when the key is random. For any text, FPR ≤ α with probability α over keys. Counterexample: a naive 100-class partition passes Eq. 2 yet has FPR ≈ 1 on ~1% of keys.
  • EQ. 3 — SOUNDNESS GIVEN A KEY (NEW)Even a fixed key must be safe. The probability of sampling a key whose FPR exceeds α is at most β = 5%. Bridge: dividing p-values by 1/β makes any Eq. 2 detector Eq. 3-sound, at a power cost — the agents used exactly this trick.

05

Verdict

Strengths

  • Proven verification layer — complete proofs (Props. 1–7, Lemma 3); statistically sound by construction, rare in agent-evaluation papers.
  • Demonstrably resists reward hacking — flags AAR and SynthID, quantifies the 13–15 pt bias instead of accepting their numbers.
  • Held-out transfer — changed prompts, language, models and attacker support “gains transfer” over “overfit”.
  • The 39-component ablation atlas is a standalone contribution to watermarking research.
  • Exceptional transparency — all per-submission results, all correction settings, per-run costs, verbatim agent prompt, concrete code-release plan.

Limitations

  • n = 4 agent runs, 1–4 submissions each — thin evidence base for “research dynamics” claims.
  • No uncertainty quantification — 5 seeds, worst/best only, no confidence intervals; several wins (98 vs 97 clean) are within seed noise.
  • Narrow scope of “better” — 4 edit attacks only; security, radioactivity, multibit, open-weights out of scope; 8B/27B models, nothing frontier-scale.
  • Quality = perplexity + Self-BLEU — diversity loss on repeated prompts only partially captured.
  • Detection cost unanalyzed — RotationFirst needs eigen-decomposition of 512 quaternion products at inference.
  • Human effort substantial and unquantified — end-to-end autonomy is partial; “Enabling Autoresearch” reads as “automated candidate generation”.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Q1Do real deployed keys exhibit inflated FPR — and how much detection power does the 1/β calibration actually cost in production?
  2. Q2Does the Pareto advantage survive on frontier-scale models with much sharper next-token distributions?
  3. Q3Can verification extend to security properties (spoofing/stealing) where the agent could attack the verifier itself?
  4. Q4Do the discovered components compose — ResidualRace + lexical grouping + forward-pass detection — without interfering?
  5. Q5Is the binding constraint agent creativity (Gemini-3.8 Flash found nothing in 11 submissions) or the 1-submission budget?
  6. Q6How do the schemes behave against adaptive attackers — paraphrase ensembles or scoreboard attacks on the 6-channel detector?
