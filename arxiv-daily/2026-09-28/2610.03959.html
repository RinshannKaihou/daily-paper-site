LatentQuant: Preserving the Policy-Facing Latent Contract under NVFP4 VAE Quantization

■  Key metric
95.75%

LIBERO success restored under full W4A4 quantization — above the FP32 reference's 95.50, vs 10.50 under joint QAT. Latent deviation d_z: 4.796 → 0.202 (≈24× lower).

■  Why this matters

Quantization can silently rewrite the latent a frozen policy consumes while every reconstruction metric stays green — a textbook case of silent precision corruption, and a warning for any low-bit deployment pipeline.

Critical edge

No head-to-head against external interface-aware QAT baselines (ActQuant, DA-PTQ, QDM), and the d_z → success mapping is sharp but unexplained — practitioners get no predictive safety threshold.

Method 方法

Setting — the policy-facing latent contract

World action models (WALL-WM) reuse a pretrained video VAE whose encoder latent conditions a frozen policy: a₀ = P(z₀, c), z₀ = E₀(x). Quantization must therefore preserve the contract E_q(x) ≈ E₀(x), not just reconstruction. NVFP4 (E2M1, two-level scaling) is applied via QDQ to conv/linear weights and activations; norms, residual adds and the VAE input/latent boundaries stay high-precision; master weights remain FP32 with a straight-through estimator.

Why joint QAT breaks it

Under matched trajectories, only NVFP4 drifts (SD ratio 1.445× vs 1.020× for FP32 after 400 steps). Decoder-activation QDQ alone rotates the latent gradient by 67.45° with only a 1.126× norm change — direction corruption, not magnitude. After every QDQ operator is removed, the FP32 master encoder still shows drift (SD 1.04585 vs control 1.01127): the damage is learned into parameters, persistent.

Decoder QDQ→ Δg_z rotated 67.45°→ Δθ learned drift→ contract broken

The fix — two stages, one hard boundary

Stage I · Encoder alignment

Encoder-only training against a frozen FP32 teacher: sample-wise loss, normalized per channel by σc — L = mean((z_q − z₀)/σ_c)². Sample-wise, not distribution-level; no action labels, no policy gradients.

Encoder frozen
Stage II · Decoder adaptation

Under deployment-identical NVFP4 execution, only the decoder trains (MSE + auxiliary latent term + LPIPS). Reconstruction gradients have no path into the encoder: latent preservation becomes an optimization boundary, not a traded-off penalty.

03a

Strengths

  • Memorable formulation: the reconstruction-vs-latent-contract dissociation (Eq. 1–2) is actionable for anyone deploying quantized VAEs in front of fixed consumers.
  • Causal, intervention-based mechanism study (matched trajectories; decoder-only QDQ; QDQ-removal probe) — rare rigor in the QAT literature.
  • Deployable fix: no action labels, no policy gradients; Table-2 ablation cleanly separates Stage I and II.
  • Full-stack validation: sim control, paired physical replay, real-robot interaction, native B300 measurements — with honest framing of parity.
  • Negative result reported intact: joint soft alignment costs PSNR, motivating the hard split.
03b

Limitations

  • Narrow scope: one WAM family, two sim benchmarks, convolutional W4A4 only, one GPU generation; Transformer-backbone VAEs and other formats untested.
  • No external interface-aware QAT baselines (ActQuant, DA-PTQ, QDM) on the control tasks; Joint+KL is intentionally weak by construction.
  • No predictive d_z tolerance threshold; success sensitivity is sharp and task-dependent (Wan2.2: d_z 0.995 → 8.4% success).
  • Stage I cost and cross-policy reusability of the aligned encoder are not quantified.
  • Mechanism probed at ~400 steps on one stack; long-horizon drift consolidation unverified.
  • Real-robot interactive eval is small: 15 tasks × 3 runs, milestone-scored.
04

Questions for further exploration 延伸问题

Q1

Does the contract failure transfer to Transformer-backbone VAEs or MXFP8/INT8, where the FP4-specific shrinkage bias and gradient-redirect may not apply?

Q2

Is there a predictive tolerance threshold on d_z — a policy-specific contract metric telling practitioners when control will break?

Q3

Could a sample-wise soft objective with adaptive scheduling close the PSNR gap (29.77 → 29.19 dB) that motivated the hard two-stage split?

Q4

How does Stage I's cost compare to joint QAT in wall-clock and data terms — and is the aligned encoder reusable across frozen policies?

Q5

Do longer fine-tuning horizons or additional stacks reveal further drift consolidation, e.g. interaction with optimizer states?
