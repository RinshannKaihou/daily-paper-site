> **一句话总结：** 提出 LatentQuant——一个两阶段 NVFP4 W4A4 VAE 量化感知训练框架：Stage I 用样本级、通道归一化的对齐损失把量化编码器锚定到高精度参考编码器，随后**冻结**编码器，Stage II 只微调量化解码器，从而阻止"重建适配"改写冻结动作策略所依赖的 latent 契约；在 Wan2.1/WALL-WM/LIBERO 上保住 95.75% 成功率（FP32 为 95.50%，而直接联合 QAT 从 95.50% 崩到 10.50%），Wan2.2/RoboTwin 上保住 68.80%（BF16 为 69.80%，联合 QAT 归零），latent 偏差 d_z 从联合 QAT 的 4.796 降到 0.202，并在 NVIDIA B300 上获得 1.17×–1.26× 端到端 VAE 加速（打包显存 −60.4%/−69.4%，DRAM 流量 −23.5%/−58.0%）。

> **TL;DR:** Joint QAT of an NVFP4 W4A4 video VAE nearly matches FP32 reconstruction (Robot VBench-7 0.7403 vs 0.7409 on Wan2.1) yet collapses frozen-policy LIBERO success from 95.50% to 10.50% — good reconstruction hides a broken policy-facing latent. LatentQuant fixes this with a two-stage scheme: Stage I aligns the quantized encoder to the FP32 reference with a sample-wise, channel-normalized latent loss (cutting d_z from 4.796 under Joint QAT to 0.202), then freezes it; Stage II adapts only the quantized decoder. It restores 95.75% LIBERO success (Wan2.1) and 68.80% RoboTwin success (Wan2.2, vs 69.80% BF16 and 0% Joint QAT) while enabling native NVFP4 execution with 1.17×–1.26× end-to-end VAE speedup over BF16 cuDNN on NVIDIA B300.

## 核心贡献

- **提出"策略面向 latent 契约"（policy-facing latent contract）概念**：世界动作模型（WAM）复用的预训练视频 VAE，其编码器 latent 直接被冻结的下游动作策略消费，因此量化不仅要保重建保真，还必须保持该表征。论文用不变性论证（任何可逆变换 T 下 E'(x)=T(E(x)) 保持重建，但 P(E'(x))≠P(E(x))）证明：**重建指标可以完全掩盖策略不兼容**——Wan2.1 联合 QAT 的 VBench-7 几乎持平（0.7403 vs 0.7409），LIBERO 成功率却从 95.50% 跌到 10.50%。
- **机制级归因**：通过受控干预把联合 QAT 失效定位到**解码器激活量化（QDQ）**——它同时改变重建信号和解码器 Jacobian，把传回编码器的梯度方向旋转 67.45°（范数仅变化 1.126×），经反复优化器更新固化为**持久的**编码器 latent 漂移（移除全部 QDQ 算子后，主编码器 SD 比仍为 1.04585 vs 对照 1.01127，证明是学进参数里的漂移而非瞬时量化伪影）。
- **LatentQuant 两阶段方法**：Stage I 仅更新编码器，以通道标准差归一化的逐样本对齐损失锚定到高精度参考；Stage II 冻结编码器、只适配解码器（MSE + 辅助 latent 项 + LPIPS 的复合重建损失），把 latent 保持从"软正则"变成"显式优化边界"——重建梯度不再有路径进入编码器。
- **全栈验证**：两个 WAM 栈（Wan2.1/LIBERO、Wan2.2/RoboTwin，800/500 次试验）近基线控制 + 高重建质量；15 个真实机器人任务的配对回放与交互评估（中位任务差异为 0）；B300 原生 NVFP4 卷积后端带来 1.17×–1.26× 端到端 VAE 加速、打包显存减少 60.4%（Wan2.1）/69.4%（Wan2.2）、全 VAE DRAM 流量减少 23.5%/58.0%。

## Key Contributions

- **Formulates the "policy-facing latent contract"**: for WAMs, VAE quantization must preserve the encoder representation consumed by a frozen downstream policy, not just reconstruction. An invariance argument (Eq. 1–2) shows reconstruction fidelity can conceal severe policy incompatibility: Wan2.1 Joint QAT nearly matches FP32 VBench-7 (0.7403 vs 0.7409) while LIBERO success collapses 95.50% → 10.50%.
- **Identifies the causal mechanism of contract violation under joint QAT**: controlled interventions trace failure to decoder activation quantization (QDQ), which alters both the reconstruction signal and the decoder Jacobian, rotating the latent gradient by 67.45° with only a 1.126× norm change; repeated optimizer updates consolidate this into persistent encoder latent drift (master-encoder SD ratio 1.04585 vs 1.01127 after all QDQ is removed).
- **Proposes LatentQuant**, a two-stage QAT framework: Stage I sample-wise, channel-normalized alignment of the quantized encoder to the high-precision reference (encoder-only updates); Stage II freezes the encoder and adapts only the quantized decoder under a composite reconstruction loss — turning latent preservation from a soft objective into an explicit optimization boundary.
- **Validates end-to-end**: near-baseline control on LIBERO (95.75%) and RoboTwin (68.80%), paired physical-observation replay (d_z = 0.068, d_a = 0.036) plus 15 interactive real-robot tasks (7 lower / 2 tied / 6 higher vs FP32, median 0), and native NVFP4 execution on NVIDIA B300 with 1.17×–1.26× end-to-end VAE speedups, 60.4%/69.4% packed-memory reduction, and 23.5%/58.0% DRAM-traffic reduction.

## Methodology

**Setting.** World action models (WALL-WM variants) whose pretrained video-VAE encoder latents condition a frozen action policy P: a_0 = P(z_0, c) with z_0 = E_0(x). Quantization must satisfy the contract E_q(x) ≈ E_0(x) in addition to reconstruction. NVFP4 (Blackwell format) represents values as E2M1 with two-level scaling — a per-tensor FP32 scale plus a per-16-element-block E4M3 scale — applied via QDQ to weights and activations of convolutional and linear layers; normalizations, residual additions, and the VAE input/latent boundaries stay in higher precision. Master parameters remain FP32 with a straight-through estimator; all low-bit variants share identical NVFP4 W4A4 arithmetic so the optimization strategy is the only difference.

**Mechanism study (Sec. 4), three controlled stages.**
1. *Precision-dependent latent drift*: under matched training trajectories, NVFP4 drifts progressively where FP32/BF16/FP8 stay close — after 400 matched steps NVFP4 reaches an SD ratio r_SD of 1.445× and sample-wise deviation 0.714, vs 1.020×/0.084 in FP32.
2. *Decoder QDQ redirects latent gradients*: the latent-gradient difference Δg_z = g_q − g_0 decomposes into a reconstruction-signal change and a backward-map (Jacobian) change. Applying QDQ only to decoder activations rotates the latent gradient by 67.45° despite only a 1.126× norm increase — direction corruption, not magnitude amplification, is the failure mode. Encoder-gradient norms grow 1.126× → 1.514×, confirming the perturbation reaches trainable encoder parameters.
3. *Drift is persistent, not instantaneous*: evaluating the FP32 master encoder after removing all QDQ operators still shows SD ratio 1.04585 (decoder-QDQ branch) vs 1.01127 (high-precision control) — a learned change in encoder parameters, establishing the pathway QDQ_D → Δg_z → Δg_θ → Δz^M.

**LatentQuant.**
- *Stage I (encoder alignment)*: only the quantized encoder trains, minimizing a sample-wise alignment loss normalized per latent channel by σ_c (the channel's std over the training distribution): L_align = mean ((z_q − z_0)/σ_c)². Matching is sample-wise rather than distribution-level, since matching aggregate statistics tolerates content-dependent representation shifts; the frozen FP32 encoder provides supervision without action labels or policy gradients. The decoder is not involved.
- *Stage II (decoder adaptation under a fixed contract)*: the aligned encoder is frozen under the same NVFP4 execution used at deployment (θ = θ*), and only the decoder trains on a composite reconstruction objective combining MSE, an auxiliary latent term, and LPIPS perceptual loss (weights and schedules in Appendix B). The hard constraint means decoder reconstruction gradients have no path into the encoder — latent preservation is an optimization boundary, not a traded-off penalty.

**Evaluation.** Two WALL-WM stacks: Wan2.1 on LIBERO (FP32 reference), Wan2.2 on RoboTwin (BF16 reference). Baselines: FP32/BF16, Direct NVFP4 (no adaptation), Joint QAT, Joint+KL (distribution-level KL on latent channels — no sample matching by construction), LatentQuant. Metrics: VBench-7 (unweighted mean of seven VBench dimensions), channel-normalized latent deviation d_z, standardized action deviation d_a (both lower is better; paired evaluation holds observations, task conditions, action noise, and policy fixed), and closed-loop success over 800 LIBERO / 500 RoboTwin trials. Deployment measurements on NVIDIA B300 include all higher-precision operations and online activation scaling.

## Results & Highlights

- **Reconstruction does not predict control.** On Wan2.1, Joint QAT reaches Robot VBench-7 = 0.7403 vs FP32's 0.7409 (near parity) yet LIBERO success collapses from 95.50% to 10.50% (−85.0 pts); Direct NVFP4, with *worse* reconstruction (0.7266), keeps 94.38% success. Joint+KL's latent regularizer recovers only 86.75%. Selection by reconstruction would rank the policy-incompatible model above the contract-preserving baseline.
- **LatentQuant retains near-reference control (Table 1).** Wan2.1: 95.75% LIBERO success vs FP32's 95.50% (authors explicitly note the +2 successes indicate parity, not improvement), with d_z = 0.202 / d_a = 0.018 vs Joint QAT's 4.796 / 0.270 (a ~24× reduction in latent deviation). Wan2.2: 68.80% RoboTwin success vs BF16's 69.80%, vs 0% for Joint QAT and 8.40% for Joint+KL, with d_z = 0.062 vs Joint QAT's 4.638.
- **Stage ablation (Table 2, Wan2.1).** Freeze-Only (decoder adaptation without Stage I) improves Robot VBench-7 from 0.7266 to 0.7326 but leaves the Direct-NVFP4 interface deviations untouched (d_z = 0.288, d_a = 0.033); adding Stage I yields 0.7340, d_z = 0.202, d_a = 0.018 — both stages are necessary.
- **Soft-alignment sweep (Appendix B.2).** A matched joint objective with λ = 0 gives (d_z, d_a) = (1.115, 0.077); any λ ≥ 0.05 reduces both to at most (0.222, 0.040) but lowers held-out PSNR from 29.77 to 29.19 dB — the empirical motivation for a separate alignment stage rather than a joint penalty.
- **Native deployment on NVIDIA B300.** End-to-end VAE latency speedups of 1.17×–1.19× (Wan2.1) and 1.24×–1.26× (Wan2.2) over BF16 cuDNN across 17/33/65-frame inputs (65-frame Wan2.2: 237.7 → 190.2 ms); packed model memory −60.4% (242 → 96 MB) and −69.4% (1344 → 411 MB); full-VAE DRAM traffic −23.5% (25.2 → 19.3 GB) and −58.0% (37.1 → 15.6 GB).
- **Physical-domain validation.** Paired replay on fixed observations gives d_z = 0.068, d_a = 0.036; interactive real-robot evaluation over 15 tasks (3-run mean milestone scores) shows 7 tasks lower, 2 tied, 6 higher vs FP32, with median difference 0 and a nonuniform long tail.

## Authors & Credibility

Ten authors from UC San Diego, Sun Yat-Sen University, the University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, HKUST (Guangzhou), and XSquare Robot, with Hao Zhang (UCSD) as corresponding author. The team combines academic ML/systems expertise with an industrial robotics lab: the physical-observation replay and interactive evaluations were run on the XSquare Robot platform, which gives the deployment and real-robot claims first-party credibility. The systems results (native NVFP4 convolution backend, B300 latency/memory/DRAM measurements) are concrete and reproducible-looking. The paper includes a prominent AI-use statement disclosing that generative AI tools assisted literature search, planning, code, math, figures, drafting, and editing, with author verification — a transparency plus. Overall credibility is high for the empirical claims; the quantitative reporting is unusually candid (e.g., explicitly flagging the LIBERO gain as parity).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty is in the framing and ordering, not the alignment itself.** Stage I is essentially sample-wise latent distillation from a frozen FP32 teacher encoder; quantized feature distillation and interface-aware QAT exist in prior work the authors themselves cite (quantized feature distillation, QDM, ActQuant, DA-PTQ, EQ-VAE). The genuine differentiators are (a) the controlled mechanism study showing *why* joint QAT breaks the contract, and (b) replacing a soft penalty with a hard freeze boundary. There is no head-to-head comparison against those external interface-aware QAT methods on the WAM control tasks — Joint+KL (a deliberately distribution-level regularizer with no sample matching) and Direct NVFP4 are the only alignment alternatives evaluated.
- **The d_z-to-success mapping is threshold-like and unexplained.** On Wan2.2, Joint+KL has d_z = 0.995 yet only 8.40% success, while Direct NVFP4 with d_z = 0.092 keeps 68.00%; on Wan2.1 Joint+KL (d_z = 1.190) gets 86.75%. The paper offers no theory or tolerance margin predicting when latent deviation becomes fatal, so practitioners get no actionable safety threshold — the work demonstrates the failure mode but not its boundary quantitatively.
- **Mechanism study is small-scale.** The three-stage causal analysis (matched trajectories, decoder-only QDQ intervention, QDQ-removal probe) is clean and unusually rigorous for a QAT paper, but runs at ~400 training steps on essentially one stack; drift dynamics at realistic fine-tuning horizons, and interaction with optimizer-state effects, are not shown.
- **Missing cost accounting.** Stage I adds a full alignment training phase before decoder adaptation; the paper never quantifies its wall-clock/data cost relative to joint QAT, and the aligned encoder's portability across different frozen policies is unexamined (if the contract is policy-specific, the method must be redone per policy).
- **Scope is narrow, as the authors admit in the conclusion**: two WAM stacks (Wan2.1/2.2 with WALL-WM), two sim benchmarks (LIBERO/RoboTwin), convolutional W4A4 only, one NVIDIA GPU generation. Transformer-backbone VAEs, other formats (INT8, MXFP8), and other policy architectures are untouched — and the decoder-activation shrinkage bias the mechanism leans on is specific to nonuniform FP4 grids, so transfer beyond NVFP4 is untested.
- **No red flags in reporting.** Claims are consistently conservative (the +0.25-pt LIBERO gain is explicitly called parity; the two extra RoboTwin-side VBench improvements are attributed to reconstruction, not control), and the appendices carry the sweeps that motivate design choices. If anything, the honesty strengthens the paper.

## Strengths

- Sharp problem formulation: the reconstruction-vs-latent-contract dissociation (Eq. 1–2) is a memorable, actionable insight for anyone deploying quantized VAEs in front of fixed consumers.
- Causal, intervention-based mechanism analysis (matched trajectories; decoder-only QDQ; QDQ-removal master-encoder probe) rather than correlational observation — rare rigor in the QAT literature.
- Simple, deployable fix requiring no action labels or policy gradients, with an ablation (Table 2) cleanly separating Stage I and Stage II contributions.
- Full-stack validation: sim control benchmarks, paired physical-observation replay, interactive real-robot evaluation, and native hardware measurements on B300 — including honest framing of parity results.
- The soft-alignment sweep that motivated the two-stage split is reported with the negative result (PSNR 29.77 → 29.19 dB) intact.

## Limitations

- Breadth: only WALL-WM on Wan2.1/2.2, LIBERO/RoboTwin, convolutional W4A4, and a single NVIDIA GPU; generalization to Transformer-backbone VAEs, other low-bit formats, and other WAM architectures is explicitly open.
- No external interface-aware QAT baselines (e.g., ActQuant, DA-PTQ, QDM) compared on the control tasks; the soft-alternative comparison (Joint+KL) is intentionally weak by construction.
- No predictive account of the latent-deviation tolerance threshold; success sensitivity to d_z appears sharp and task-dependent (Wan2.2 Joint+KL: d_z 0.995 → 8.4% success).
- Stage I's additional training cost and cross-policy reusability of the aligned encoder are not quantified.
- Mechanism experiments cover ~400 steps on one stack; long-horizon drift consolidation is unverified.
- Real-robot interactive evaluation is small (15 tasks × 3 runs, milestone-scored), though mitigated by paired replay.

## Relevance

这篇论文正中用户"极端性能优化下的可靠性"核心方向，并与"推理可靠性/SDC 检测"高度交叉：NVFP4 W4A4 联合 QAT 在重建指标完好无损的情况下静默摧毁了下游冻结策略所依赖的编码器 latent（LIBERO 成功率 95.5%→10.5%），是"低精度优化引入静默精度损坏"的教科书级案例；其"量化不得改写下游消费的表征契约"的结论对任何低比特部署管线的正确性验证都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the latent-contract failure mode transfer to Transformer-backbone VAEs or other formats (MXFP8, INT8), where the FP4-specific decoder shrinkage bias and gradient-redirect mechanisms may not apply?
- Is there a predictive tolerance threshold on d_z (or a policy-specific contract metric) that tells practitioners when control will break? The observed mapping is sharp and task-dependent (Wan2.2 Joint+KL: d_z 0.995 → 8.4% success vs Direct NVFP4: d_z 0.092 → 68.0%).
- Would a sample-wise soft-alignment joint objective with adaptive scheduling close the PSNR gap (29.77 → 29.19 dB) that motivated the hard two-stage split?
- How does Stage I's training cost compare to joint QAT in wall-clock and data terms, and is the aligned encoder reusable across different frozen policies, or must alignment be redone per policy?
- The mechanism was probed over ~400 steps on Wan2.1 — do longer fine-tuning horizons or additional stacks reveal further drift consolidation (e.g., interaction with optimizer states) that changes the design prescription?
