01

Methodology — the RSI loop

distilled from the paper's §3

Model. BAGEL-7B-MoT, split into a generator G (request → image) and a shared reader/writer half U/C (image+question → answer; spec → plotting program). Each of four rounds runs three steps:

1 · DIAGNOSE. The generator renders 336 fixed diagnostic requests; the reader grades every atomic requirement met / violated / unresolved, with PaddleOCR independently checking rendered text and gating new vocabulary.

2 · PLAN. Fixed rules turn violations into a sampling distribution: 20% of budget spread evenly, the rest weighted by violation rate capped at 3× the mean; mastery gates (≥0.80 up, <0.60 down) move difficulty; weak attribute values get extra examples plus wording-contrast pairs.

3 · BUILD & VERIFY. The writer emits two candidate programs per specification; a sandbox executes both and compares the execution record — what was actually drawn — against the spec. First verified candidate wins (~35k–43k specs per round). Generation trains only on verified pairs; the reader also trains on deliberately "edited" renders whose labels state what is really shown — negative supervision against blind acceptance.

Update. Mt+1 merges both halves; 64 GPUs, ~400 + 704 steps per round.

03 / 04

Strengths vs Limitations

Strengths

  1. No model collapse by construction — supervision comes from execution records, not the model's own judgment.
  2. Crisp credit assignment — five ablations, one component each: verification ≈ +8 pts, targeting ≈ +3.5–5, recursion ≈ +3.
  3. Triangulated measurement — frozen reader + external judge + OCR + blind audit, calibrated before round 1.
  4. Honest failure analysis — names where the loop fails; per-round rule changelog (Appendix F).
  5. Favorable economics — verified corpus ~8.8× cheaper per round; yield saturates above 95%.

Limitations

  1. One verifiable niche — charts only; GenEval flat, MMBench −2.6, MMMU −3.0; tables & diagrams untested.
  2. Bespoke benchmark — built by the same authors; near-zero transfer to Struct2IBench / BizGenEval.
  3. Diagnosis ceiling — the reader accepts 34–38% of requirements humans mark as "not drawn".
  4. Curriculum fragility — full vocabulary swap cost 72% → 60% and needed a manual backfill patch mid-study.
  5. Narrow evidence — four rounds, one base model, diminishing returns; no equal-GPU "just more verified data" baseline.
05

Open questions

Does RSI transfer beyond charts to other program-verifiable structured domains — tables, diagrams, documents, UI screenshots — as the introduction motivates but never tests?

Can the ~34% reader false-acceptance on native generations be closed, and would sharper diagnosis even change allocation (ranking correlation already 0.86–0.91)?

Is failure-targeted scheduling worth its machinery at equal GPU wall-clock, as opposed to the paper's equal-data-volume comparison?

Would raising the 5% general-visual share stop the MMBench/MMMU regression without stalling the loop — is replay a necessary ingredient for long runs?

Past round 4: does accuracy plateau near 60% on L0, and do the fixed thresholds (0.80 mastery, 3× mean cap) need retuning per domain or model scale?
