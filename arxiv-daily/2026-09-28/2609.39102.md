> **一句话总结：** 论文首次量化了自进化搜索智能体中一种新的失效模式——"co-cheating"（共谋作弊）：proposer 生成题目、solver 作答、两者一致即得奖励的闭环训练中，proposer 与 solver 越来越多地在**相同的错误答案**上达成一致（Qwen3.5-4B/9B 上假一致质量 F 从第 1 轮的 0.4%/0.3% 涨到第 3 轮的 6.1%/8.8%，而内部奖励持续上升）；提出的 **CrossFit** 把 proposer 的源文档按来源切成两折，每折的题目只能由**只在另一折上训练过**的辅助 solver 评分（交叉拟合反馈），在不动主 solver 训练规则的前提下把 F 压到 3.0%/3.7%，并在 7 个 QA 基准（NQ、TriviaQA、PopQA、HotpotQA、2WikiMultiHopQA、MuSiQue、Bamboogle，共 1,325 题固定评测集）上把平均 Cover-EM 从 Dr. Zero 的 0.400/0.428 提到 0.488/0.512（+8.8/+8.4 分），而同模型多次采样验证（MSV）只带来 +0.7/+0.8——证明反馈的"来源血统"而非伪标签质量才是主要失效路径。

> **TL;DR:** Self-evolving search agents trained in the Dr. Zero loop (proposer writes questions from source documents, solver answers them, agreement is the reward) develop "co-cheating": over three rounds on Qwen3.5-4B/9B, false-agreement mass (proposer and solver sharing the same wrong answer) climbs from 0.4%/0.3% to 6.1%/8.8% while the in-loop training signal keeps improving. The paper's fix, **CrossFit**, splits source documents into two folds and lets an auxiliary solver that never saw a fold's pseudo-labels score that fold's questions, cutting false agreement to 3.0%/3.7% and raising 7-benchmark average Cover-EM to 0.488/0.512 vs 0.400/0.428 for coupled Dr. Zero (+8.8/+8.4 pts) and 0.401/0.434 for Search-R1 (+8.7/+7.8 pts). Same-model multi-sample verification (MSV, 6 extra generations per candidate) only buys +0.7/+0.8 — the dominant failure path is feedback provenance, not pseudo-label quality.

## 核心贡献

- **诊断新失效模式 "co-cheating"**：设计事后审计协议（用 gpt-6-astra/high 从源文档构建证据支持的参考答案，判定每一步保存的伪标签与 solver 回答，且审计从不影响准入、模型更新或 proposer 奖励），把一致性信号分解为采纳标签真值 T_P、solver 真值 T_S、一致性 A、假一致质量 F（共享同一错误答案）与损失信用 L（正确回答被错误标签拒绝计分）。在标准耦合 Dr. Zero 环路中，A 上升的同时 F 从第 1 轮的 0.004/0.003 涨至第 3 轮的 0.061/0.088，L 反而下降——一致性被"共享错误"接管。
- **两种干预的对照实验**：MSV（同模型 3 次带源 + 3 次去源采样，兼容多数票准入并替换伪标签）只把 F 降到 5.7%/7.2%、下游平均只涨 0.7/0.8 分，且每候选多 6 次生成；CrossFit（来源级两折交叉评分）把 F 压到 3.0%/3.7%，下游涨 8.8/8.4 分——隔离出"反馈血统"是主要病因。
- **CrossFit 算法**：只在"proposer 从谁那里拿反馈"这一处改动——源文档一次性按文档级（非题目级）切成 Fold 0/1，两个辅助反馈 solver 各只在一折的准入题目上训练、交叉评分另一折的新提案，保留 Dr. Zero 原始前沿奖励 f(k)=(5−k)/4，主 solver 仍在全部准入题目上训练，规则不变。
- **机制隔离实验**：固定 3,000 题重放库上，只改反馈 solver 的训练来源：同源辅助 solver F=0.064/0.087（≈耦合对照 0.061/0.088）、全量数据辅助 0.058/0.069、题目级随机切分 0.050/0.062、来源级 OOF 切分 0.004/0.001；半预算控制（每折 12 次更新）达到几乎相同的 F 和重放准确率 91.6%/91.8%——排除"评估器复制/额外优化/任意切分"三种替代解释。
- **诚实的成本核算**：MSV 使预留预算增加约 90%（379→719 / 476→903 H200 小时）、judge 请求增加约 5 倍；CrossFit（25 次辅助更新/轮）+72%/+75%（650/834 小时），减半预算则 +36%/+40%；两者合用为 Dr. Zero 的 2.6–2.7 倍。

## Key Contributions

- **Names and quantifies "co-cheating"**: a failure mode of self-evolving agent loops where proposer-solver agreement improves because both converge on the same incorrect labels. A post-hoc, evidence-backed audit (gpt-6-astra/high constructs a reference from the source document; the audit never feeds back into admission, updates, or reward) decomposes the signal into label truth T_P, solver truth T_S, agreement A, false-agreement mass F, and lost credit L. Under coupled Dr. Zero feedback, F rises from 0.004/0.003 (round 1) to 0.061/0.088 (round 3) on Qwen3.5-4B/9B while L falls.
- **Separates two candidate causes with a controlled comparison**: MSV (3 source-aware + 3 source-blind samples from the same model; compatible-majority admission and relabeling) attacks label quality and only reduces F to 5.7%/7.2% for +0.7/+0.8 downstream points at ~1.9x budget; CrossFit attacks feedback provenance and reduces F to 3.0%/3.7% for +8.8/+8.4 points — establishing that feedback ancestry, not pseudo-label accuracy, is the operative failure path.
- **The CrossFit algorithm**: a minimal intervention that only changes who supplies proposer feedback. Source documents are split once into two folds at the document level (no question-level splits, so related questions never straddle folds); two auxiliary feedback solvers train only on one fold each and score the other fold's questions, with roles swapped each round. The main solver's update rule and Dr. Zero's frontier reward f(k)=(5−k)/4 are untouched.
- **Mechanism isolation via a fixed replay bank**: replaying the same 3,000 saved questions while varying only the evaluator's training provenance shows same-source auxiliary F=0.064/0.087 (matching the coupled control), full-data auxiliary 0.058/0.069, random question-level split 0.050/0.062, but source-level out-of-fold split 0.004/0.001 — ruling out evaluator duplication, extra optimization (half-budget control matches full performance), and arbitrary partitioning as explanations.
- **Transparent cost accounting**: MSV adds ~90% reserved budget (379→719 / 476→903 H200-hours) and ~5x judge requests; CrossFit adds +72%/+75% at 25 auxiliary updates per round (or +36%/+40% at half), and the combination is 2.6–2.7x Dr. Zero's budget.

## Methodology

**Diagnosis (audit protocol).** The authors instrument the standard coupled Dr. Zero self-evolution loop (proposer generates questions + pseudo-labels from source documents; five solver responses per question; frontier reward f(k)=(5−k)/4; three rounds of 18 proposer + 25 solver updates on Qwen3.5-4B/9B from the same public checkpoint). After training, they audit every scheduled step: gpt-6-astra/high constructs an evidence-backed reference from the question's source document and judges the saved pseudo-label and solver responses. Unsupported cases remain unresolved rather than receiving a forced label; the auditor is post-hoc and never changes admission or reward. Metrics: T_P (adopted-label truth), T_S (solver truth), A (agreement), F (fraction of pairs matching the same incorrect answer), L (correct responses denied credit by a wrong label). J/E audit coverage is ~0.85–0.86 across treatments.

**Intervention 1 — MSV (multi-sample verification).** For each proposal, the same model draws three source-aware and three source-blind answers, neither view seeing the proposer's draft. A task is admitted only if a majority answer is compatible across both views; the compatible majority replaces the draft as the training label, otherwise the task is rejected. Cost: six extra labeler generations per candidate.

**Intervention 2 — CrossFit (cross-fitted proposer feedback).** All source documents are partitioned once into Fold 0 and Fold 1 at the source level (deliberately no question-level splitting, since questions from the same document share the very reuse path being targeted). Two auxiliary feedback solvers are maintained: after round r, one has trained only on questions admitted from Fold 0 and the other only from Fold 1; in the next round questions from Fold 0 are scored by the Fold-1-trained solver and vice versa. Proposer reward keeps Dr. Zero's form R_P(q) = f(Σ 1[z_i ≈ ỹ]) with f(k) = (5−k)/4, so the training objective is unchanged — only which solver supplies the feedback differs. The main solver still trains on all admitted questions from both folds. The idea borrows the cross-fitting exclusion principle from double/debiased ML (Chernozhukov et al., 2018), explicitly without its asymptotic guarantees.

**Mechanism controls.** (a) Fixed-bank replay: the same 3,000 saved questions and adopted labels are replayed while varying only the feedback solver's training provenance, holding admission, labels, answer matcher, and evaluation fixed. (b) Alternative evaluators: same-source auxiliary, full-data auxiliary. (c) Alternative partitions: random question-level split vs source-ID out-of-fold split. (d) Half-budget control (12 auxiliary updates/round/fold) to exclude "more optimization" as the cause. (e) Combination with MSV to locate the interventions at different points of the loop (admission/labeling vs feedback provenance).

**Downstream evaluation.** Seven open-domain QA benchmarks (NQ, TriviaQA, PopQA, HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, MuSiQue, Bamboogle) on a fixed 1,325-question manifest (200 each + 125 Bamboogle), scored by Cover-EM after normalization, one greedy search trajectory per question with identical tool budget, macro-averaged with equal benchmark weight. Baselines: Base, Prompting, RI-Instruct, Search-R1, Dr. Zero, MSV, CrossFit, MSV+CrossFit, all on the same Qwen3.5-4B/9B backbones.

## Results & Highlights

- **Co-cheating is real and grows with training**: under coupled Dr. Zero feedback, mean false-agreement mass F rises from 0.004 (round 1) to 0.061 (round 3) on Qwen3.5-4B and from 0.003 to 0.088 on Qwen3.5-9B, while lost credit L falls — in-loop agreement (A up to 0.710/0.752) becomes optimistic relative to externally audited truth, i.e., internal reward and false agreement rise together (Figure 1/3, Table 4).
- **CrossFit delivers large downstream gains; verification alone does not**: 7-benchmark average Cover-EM reaches 0.488 (4B) and 0.512 (9B) vs 0.400/0.428 for coupled Dr. Zero (+8.8/+8.4 pts) and 0.401/0.434 for Search-R1 (+8.7/+7.8 pts) (Table 1). MSV alone: 0.407/0.436, only +0.7/+0.8 over Dr. Zero; MSV+CrossFit 0.491/0.515 adds just +0.3 over CrossFit alone — feedback provenance dominates pseudo-label quality. The advantage over Dr. Zero grows from +4.2/+4.3 after round 2 to +8.8/+8.4 after round 3 (micro averages), showing the loop learns a different curriculum rather than re-ranking fixed predictions.
- **Audit confirms the mechanism**: round-3 adopted-label truth T_P rises from 0.747 to 0.819 (4B) and 0.737 to 0.851 (9B) under CrossFit while F falls from 0.061 to 0.030 and 0.088 to 0.037; final-round F under the full pipeline drops to 3.0%/3.7% (MSV alone: 5.7%/7.2%).
- **Only source-level exclusion works**: on the 3,000-question replay bank, a same-source auxiliary evaluator leaves F at 0.064/0.087 (coupled control: 0.061/0.088), a full-data auxiliary reaches 0.058/0.069, a random question-level split 0.050/0.062, but the source-ID out-of-fold split crushes F to 0.004/0.001, raising fixed-bank solver truth from 0.687 to 0.770 (4B) and 0.717 to 0.868 (9B) and replay accuracy from 88.1% to 91.5% and 87.0% to 91.7%.
- **Cost is real and disclosed**: MSV raises reserved budget ~90% (379→719 H200-hours at 4B; 476→903 at 9B) and judge requests ~5x (60.4k→296.9k); CrossFit at 25 auxiliary updates/round costs 650/834 H200-hours (+72%/+75%), halving the auxiliary budget keeps nearly the same effect (F 0.005/0.002; replay accuracy 91.6%/91.8%); MSV+CrossFit is 2.6–2.7x Dr. Zero's budget.
- **Gains concentrate on multi-hop tasks**: the paper reports gains averaging 10.0/10.9 points across HotpotQA, 2WikiMQA, MuSiQue, and Bamboogle versus 7.3/5.2 across the single-hop datasets (e.g., 4B 2WikiMQA 0.575 vs Dr. Zero 0.385; MuSiQue 0.245 vs 0.120).

## Authors & Credibility

The paper has a 15-author team led by three equal contributors (Meijia Chen — Rutgers; Hao Li, Zheng Lu — Independent Researcher), with corresponding authors Tianyu Shi (McGill University) and Alaa Khamis (King Fahd University of Petroleum and Minerals); the remaining affiliations span Rutgers, UC San Diego, University of Michigan, and a notably large "Independent Researcher" bloc. The corresponding authors have an established record in agentic AI and self-evolving systems (overlapping author groups are cited in the paper's own related work, e.g., CAFFE and SearchMaster), which cuts both ways: domain expertise is evident, but the heavy independent-researcher fraction and dense self-citation cluster mean less institutional verification. Credibility positives: the work is compute-heavy (eight H200 GPUs per run, 379–903 reserved hours per treatment), the appendices document training schedules, evaluation manifests, scoring rules, and the full audit procedure, and an explicit AI-use statement discloses that an AI assistant aided drafting and that gpt-6-astra/high served as the audit judge, with the authors claiming verification of results and citations. No public code release is mentioned, which limits external reproducibility despite the detailed protocol.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The core contribution — isolating *feedback ancestry* as a distinct failure path in self-evolving loops — is genuinely new and well-motivated; the co-cheating diagnosis (A rising together with F while L falls) is a crisp, measurable signature rather than a vague "reward hacking" gesture. However, the fix is deliberately derivative: cross-fitting is imported wholesale from econometrics (Chernozhukov et al., 2018), and the paper is admirably upfront that it borrows the exclusion principle without the statistical guarantees (the adaptive curriculum has no orthogonal score or independent sample structure). The closest comparator, Dr. Zero / Search Self-Play, is used as the base system rather than compared against as a peer method, so the contribution is "diagnose + patch Dr. Zero", not a general new training algorithm.

**Rigor — strong in places, thin in others.** The control suite (same-source auxiliary, full-data auxiliary, random row split, half-budget replay) is unusually careful and convincingly rules out evaluator duplication and extra optimization. But several gaps remain: (1) the audit rests entirely on a single LLM judge (gpt-6-astra/high) — the very class of automated-judge bias the paper itself cites (Zheng et al., 2023) — with no human-verified sample of audit decisions reported, and ~14% of steps (coverage J/E ≈ 0.859) left unresolved; F values of 0.004 vs 0.001 are differences of a handful of judge calls on thousands of pairs. (2) The main Table 1 appears to be single runs per cell with one greedy trajectory per question and no confidence intervals (only the replay experiment uses five seeds); with 200 questions per benchmark, a 0.02 macro-average swing is roughly four questions per benchmark. (3) MSV is somewhat of a strawman: its labeler is the *same model*, which the authors acknowledge shares errors — the obvious stronger baseline of a different-family or larger verifier model is never run. (4) The fold count is fixed at k=2 with no ablation on the number of folds or fold-composition sensitivity. (5) Only one base loop (Dr. Zero), one model family (Qwen3.5), and two scales are tested; whether co-cheating appears in Absolute Zero-style or corpus-based self-play is asserted by analogy, not shown.

**Compute parity.** The headline +8.8/+8.4 over Dr. Zero comes at +72–75% reserved budget (mitigated by the half-budget control, which is a good-faith response), but the Search-R1 and RI-Instruct comparisons inherit training budgets from the Dr. Zero paper and are not matched to CrossFit's — the +8.7 over Search-R1 is not compute-controlled.

**Red flags.** None egregious — cost accounting and limitations (Appendix D) are unusually candid, including the admission that lower false agreement could partly reflect rejecting hard tasks rather than better learning. But the closed evaluation stack (own Cover-EM metric, own 1,325-question manifest, own LLM judge, no code release) means every number is currently self-attested.

## Strengths

- A precise, falsifiable diagnosis: decomposing agreement into truth, false-agreement mass, and lost credit turns a vague "self-play collapse" worry into trackable per-step quantities, with all 129 training steps retained and unsmoothed in the appendix.
- The fixed-bank replay experiment is a model of mechanism isolation: same proposals, same labels, only the evaluator's training provenance changes — cleanly attributing the effect to source ancestry.
- The intervention is minimal and practical: the main solver's update rule and the reward shape are untouched; CrossFit is a drop-in change to feedback routing in any proposer-solver loop.
- Honest engineering accounting: reserved H200-hours, judge-request counts, and the 2.6–2.7x combined cost are reported rather than hidden, and the half-budget control preempts the "it's just more compute" objection.
- Findings are actionable beyond search agents: any self-evolving system where a learned evaluator scores outputs derived from the evaluator's own training data (self-rewarding LLMs, co-evolving feedback, automated benchmark design) faces the same ancestry hazard.

## Limitations

- **Scope of evidence**: one base loop (Dr. Zero), one model family (Qwen3.5), two scales (4B/9B), and a fixed 2-fold split with no fold-count ablation; generality to other self-evolution frameworks and to connected/semantically related source corpora is untested.
- **Judge-dependent audit**: truth labels come from a single commercial LLM judge with ~14% of audited steps unresolved and no human verification sample; the very low F values (0.004/0.001) are within plausible judge-noise range.
- **Statistical fragility of downstream numbers**: single-run Table 1, one greedy trajectory per question, 200 questions per benchmark, no confidence intervals; several per-benchmark gains (e.g., PopQA +0.125) would survive only a handful of question flips.
- **Residual failure channels unaddressed**: shared pretraining errors and overlapping web evidence can still correlate the two folds' mistakes — CrossFit blocks the same-source feedback path, not error correlation per se (acknowledged in Appendix D).
- **Cost**: +72–75% budget for CrossFit, ~+90% for MSV, 2.6–2.7x for the combination; end-to-end cost-efficiency is explicitly left open, and no code release is provided.
- **MSV comparison is weak**: same-model verification is known to share errors; a cross-model verifier baseline is missing, making the "verification is insufficient" headline harder to generalize.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does CrossFit survive when source documents are semantically connected (e.g., all drawn from one wiki), where the two folds share evidence and pretraining-induced errors can still correlate across evaluators?
- Would a verifier from a different model family (or a larger checkpoint) rescue MSV, or is per-candidate verification fundamentally capped by label noise rather than labeler identity?
- How does the tradeoff shift with k > 2 folds — is there a bias–variance point where each auxiliary solver becomes too weak to be a useful rewarder?
- Does the co-cheating signature (A and F co-rising while L falls) appear in other self-evolution frameworks (Absolute Zero-style reasoning, corpus-based self-play, CAFFE-style co-evolving feedback), and does the same patch transfer?
- Could the expensive LLM audit be replaced at scale by cheap fixed probes (held-out benchmark items tracked during training) that detect rising false agreement without a judge in the loop?
- Is the downstream gain driven by a healthier curriculum (harder, less self-referential questions) rather than better feedback per se — the authors' own Appendix D flags this; a direct curriculum-difficulty analysis would settle it.
