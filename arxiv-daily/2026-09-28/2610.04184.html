01

The protocol — evaluation design becomes the benchmark

freeze-then-seal

A researcher agent (an LLM in a code harness) gets target descriptions, raw task sources, execution traces, and reference scores for just 4 development-visible candidates. It must assemble ≥100 scored questions across coding, co-work and reasoning, with executable graders and fixed weights — then everything freezes. The frozen evaluator runs independently on 13 candidates (9 sealed) and is graded against 14 target benchmarks (SWE-bench, Terminal-Bench 2.1, GDPval, HLE, BrowseComp…). A stratified 120-question public-task sampler with no reference access — Random120 — is the human baseline.

STEP A

Research episode

Agent explores sources, writes tasks and graders, pilots and revises. Sees references for only 4 dev-visible candidates.

3 h · $100 infer + $100 dev-test
STEP B

Freeze

≥100 questions over all three domains, executable graders, fixed aggregation weights, no candidate-identity lookups. Hash-frozen.

submission contract
STEP C

Sealed assessment

Frozen suite runs on all 13 candidates — 9 never seen — under hard caps; compared to 14 target references.

How agent-built evaluators fail

quantified by counterfactuals, not anecdotes
PATHOLOGY 1

Answer truncation

46/96 of Astra’s spreadsheet-task responses hit the output cap with no final answer. Deleting those 8 tasks costs co-work −31.94 Score and −12.57 pp PA.

found via component deletion vs 1,000 matched random deletions

PATHOLOGY 2

Budget exhaustion

Fable 5 finished only 29/39 candidate-domain reports before the $120 assessment cap stopped it; its imputation scenario scores −13.18 overall.

sibling Fable 5.1, which finished: +21.96

Weight monopoly

Grok’s six highest-weight co-work items carry 93.87% of the domain’s score. Uniform re-weighting lifts its Score 22.18 → 37.03 while PA barely moves.

the frozen suite holds signal its own aggregation suppresses

PATHOLOGY 4

Dev-target overfit

Fable 5.1 ranks 1st on development targets but mid-pack sealed — while cheap Random120 beats 4 of 8 agents on PA and all of them on coding Score (22.92).

04

Verdict

strengths / limitations

Strengths

  • Crisp budgeted formalization (3 h, $100+$100, $120/$30 caps) with a freeze protocol that cleanly separates designing an evaluation from running it.
  • Metrology with bounds: sanity checks, per-domain oracle floors, and the 91.20% PA ceiling — numbers never appear without their achievable limits.
  • Honest negative results: Random120 is strong, coding is weak for every agent, and no evaluator dominates.
  • Counterfactual-backed failure taxonomy — re-weighting, deletion, truncation counts, budget accounting — not anecdotes.
  • Full cost transparency: candidate generation split from grading ($8.63 vs $109.41); cost-efficiency becomes a measured axis.
  • Deterministic scoring — headline metrics need no LLM judges.

Limitations

  • One episode per configuration, no repeats: adjacent rankings (Astra 22.79 vs Grok 22.59) sit within plausible single-episode noise.
  • Harness confound: the two leaders run Codex, the other seven Claude Code — model vs harness can’t be disentangled.
  • Inconsistent reference panel (coding means 0.0075 vs 0.876): absolute Scores are low, cross-domain comparison needs the appendix floors.
  • Regime-specific: findings tied to 3 h / ~$320, 13 candidates, 14 targets — no evidence of transfer to other domains or budgets.
  • Fable 5’s row mixes observed and imputed scores (29/39 completed) — easy to misread in the main table.
  • Costs are API-equivalent estimates, not uniform billing; cross-lab comparisons carry that caveat.
05

Open questions

for further exploration

Would repeats reorder the leaderboard? The 0.20-Score Astra–Grok gap vs 2–7-point within-artifact SDs at moderate question counts.

Can the 91.20% ceiling be raised with compatible target reference scales — or is cross-target disagreement intrinsic to multi-benchmark evaluation?

Is universal coding weakness caused by the two incompatible coding references (RMSE floor 0.303) or by task selection — and would dropping them change who leads?

What budget scaling law governs evaluation research? Does $1,000 or 24 hours let agents finally dominate Random120 on both metrics?

Can the submission contract prevent weight concentration (93.87%) and truncation (46/96) without suppressing useful designs — and at what cost to Score?

Do the design lessons transfer to reward models and open-ended rubrics, where no frozen reference panel exists?
