> **一句话总结：** 提出 DEO：利用 KL 正则化挑战者目标的指数倾斜闭式解，把"训练一个出题模型"替换为"冻结 LLM 变异题目 + 当前 solver 打分 + Metropolis 接受规则"的直接任务采样，只训练 solver——在 Qwen3-4B/8B 和 OctoThinker-3B 数学推理上以少 52%–61% 的墙钟时间匹配或略超训练挑战者的 R-Zero（4B 七基准均值 AVG7 47.57 vs 45.93），且换用闭源 Claude Haiku 4.5 当生成器还能进一步提升（AVG7 49.60），这是训练挑战者的方法做不到的。

> **TL;DR:** This paper removes the separately-trained "challenger" (task-proposer) from self-evolving LLM training. Key insight: the KL-regularized challenger objective has a closed-form exponential-tilt solution q_t(x) ∝ π₀(x)·exp(r_c(x,θ_t)/β), so a frozen LLM can *sample* tasks near that target via propose-mutate-then-Metropolis-accept, scoring candidates with the current solver's answer-disagreement — only the solver (Qwen3-4B/8B-Base, OctoThinker-3B) gets GRPO training. Across three evolution rounds on 7 math benchmarks, DEO reaches AVG7 47.57 on Qwen3-4B vs 45.93 for R-Zero (+1.64 pts) while using 52.1% less wall-clock time (14.6h vs 30.5h on 8×H100; 60.5% less at 8B), and a matched-pool no-walk ablation shows the Metropolis walk itself adds +1.8–1.9 AVG7. Replacing the local generator with API-only Claude Haiku 4.5 further lifts AVG7 to 49.60.

## 核心贡献

- **去挑战者化的自进化训练框架（DEO）**：证明 KL 正则化挑战者目标 max_φ E[r_c(x,θ) − β·KL(π₀‖π_φ)] 的解是基任务分布的指数倾斜 q_t(x) ∝ π₀(x)exp(r_c(x,θ_t)/β)（Gibbs 变分恒等式），因此"出题器"不必是一个可训练模型，只需要一个能在当前 solver 能力边界附近采样任务的分布。
- **LLM-based Metropolis–Hastings 任务采样（LLM-MH）**：冻结 LLM 对当前题目做结构化变异（generalize/compose/invert/change-objective/dualize 五种操作，禁止只换数字），当前 solver 用 m=9 次作答估计不确定度得分 r̂_c = 1 − 2|p̃ − 1/2|，按 α̂ = min{1, exp(Δr̂_c/β)} 接受更难的题目，加 BLEU 重复惩罚（λ_rep=10, 阈值 0.5）防退化；每轮 N=2000 候选、5 轮扫描。
- **理论保证（理想化变体）**：Theorem 1 证明若精确从倾斜分布采样，在正则性、局部梯度优势和初始答案分离假设下，DEO 学到"分布鲁棒推理"——solver 答案不确定度在基分布的 KL 邻域内最坏情形也有界（误差随任务数 T 和 rollout 数 m 衰减）；Corollary 1 进一步把集中性界转化为答案准确率上界。
- **成本与能力双重收益**：三个模型上墙钟时间减少 57.1%/52.1%/60.5%；因为生成器只需推理访问，可直接换成闭源 API 模型（Claude Haiku 4.5）——AVG7 从 47.57 升到 49.60、AIME 2024 从 6.67 升到 20.00，而 R-Zero 型方法无法利用闭源生成器。
- **消融定位收益来源**：匹配候选池（同为 2000）的 no-walk 消融表明 Metropolis 变异本身贡献 +1.81/+1.82/+0.79 AVG7（4B/8B/Octo），且把"带内"（in-band，p̂∈[0.3,0.8]）题目比例从 50.72% 提到 68.95%，过滤后保留任务 3682 vs 2892。

## Key Contributions

- **DEO framework**: reformulates the challenger in self-evolving training (R-Zero/AZR/Agent0 lineage) as a *sampling target* — the exponential-tilt closed form of the KL-regularized challenger objective — eliminating challenger parameter updates entirely; only the solver is trained.
- **LLM-MH**: a practical approximate Metropolis–Hastings walk where a frozen LLM mutates tasks (5 structural operations) and the frozen current solver's disagreement score drives acceptance α̂ = min{1, exp(Δr̂_c/β)}, plus a batch-level BLEU repetition penalty to prevent degenerate pools.
- **Distributional-robustness theory**: for the idealized exact-sampling variant, Theorem 1 bounds the solver's worst-case answer uncertainty over any KL-ball around the base task distribution (decaying optimization + statistical error terms); Corollary 1 converts this into an answer-accuracy bound, explaining *why* adversarial task sampling helps.
- **API-only generator capability**: because the generator needs inference access only, DEO runs with closed-source Claude Haiku 4.5 as generator/mutator (Qwen3-4B solver unchanged), improving AVG7 47.57→49.60 — a capability categorically unavailable to methods that train the challenger.
- **Honest ablation design**: a matched-candidate-pool "no walk" control (same 2000 candidates, same labeling/filtering/GRPO recipe, L_w=0) isolates the walk's contribution from the challenger-removal contribution.

## Methodology

**From challenger training to a sampling target.** Existing self-evolving methods (R-Zero, AZR, Agent0) train a challenger to maximize challenge reward r_c(x,θ) minus a KL penalty to a base task law π₀. DEO starts from the distributional view: max_φ E_{x~π_φ}[r_c(x,θ) − β KL(π₀‖π_φ)] has the Gibbs-variational solution q_t(x) ∝ π₀(x)exp(r_c(x,θ_t)/β) — an exponential tilt of the base distribution giving exponentially more mass to tasks the current solver finds challenging. Sampling directly from q_t is intractable, so DEO approximates it.

**LLM-MH walk.** Each round: (1) the frozen generator LLM (same checkpoint as the initial solver, or an API model) produces N=2000 seed questions under topic-sampled prompts; (2) for L_w=5 sweeps, each question is mutated by a frozen-LLM prompt that picks one of five structural operations (generalize / compose / invert / change-objective / dualize; number-swapping explicitly forbidden); (3) the current solver draws m=9 responses; the modal-answer frequency p̃ yields the triangular uncertainty score r̂_c(x,θ) = 1 − 2|p̃ − 1/2|, with a symmetric sentence-BLEU neighbor penalty (threshold 0.5, λ_rep=10) applied at batch level; (4) the mutation is accepted with probability min{1, exp((r̂_c(x′) − r̂_c(x))/β)}, β=0.1. The walk is explicitly *approximate*: it omits the Hastings/base-density correction factor, uses noisy finite-rollout scores, and no mixing claim is made.

**Solver learning.** Pseudo-labels are the majority-vote answers of the frozen current solver (never the generator's proposed answers). The solver is trained with verl GRPO (5 responses/prompt, lr 1e-6, max 20 steps/round, KL coef 0.01 to the original base model) on the filtered band p̂ ∈ [0.3, 0.8] after an LLM format-validity check; reward R = 0.9·1[answer correct] + 0.1·r_format. Iterate 3 rounds; all experiments on an 8×H100 node.

**Theory.** For the idealized variant sampling exactly from q_t, with uncertainty rewards r_c = 1 − p_θ(x), Theorem 1 shows sup over D in a KL ball of E_D[1 − p_θt(x)] ≤ b_T = Φ_* + O(E₀/T²) + Õ(Σ(t+1)²C_F/(T²√nt) + TKe^{−mν̂₀²/8}) under smoothness, local gradient dominance (PL), and initial answer separation; Corollary 1 turns this concentration into Pr[g(y) ≠ a*(x)] ≤ min{1, ε₀ + b_z + √(b*/2β) + Δ_T + √Δ_T}. Interpretation: tilt sampling chases worst-case uncertainty (mode-preserving ⇒ distributionally robust sharpening toward initial majority-vote accuracy), whereas base-law sampling follows mean uncertainty.

## Results & Highlights

- **Beats R-Zero at roughly half the wall-clock.** Iteration-3 AVG7 (unweighted mean of MATH-500, GSM8K, AMC, Minerva, OlympiadBench, AIME 2024/2025): Qwen3-4B DEO 47.57 vs R-Zero 45.93 (+1.64) and no-walk 45.68; Qwen3-8B DEO 53.60 vs R-Zero 52.88 (+0.72); OctoThinker-3B DEO 28.30 vs R-Zero 25.94 (+2.36). On OctoThinker MATH-500, DEO improves 50.20 → 52.60 over R-Zero.
- **Wall-clock savings are large and verified per model** (Figure 2, 8×H100, iterations 1–3): OctoThinker-3B 29.4h → 12.6h (57.1% less), Qwen3-4B 30.5h → 14.6h (52.1% less), Qwen3-8B 52.1h → 20.6h (60.5% less). The no-walk ablation is cheaper still (5.9/4.9/6.2h), so the walk itself costs 2–3× no-walk time but buys +1.81/+1.82/+0.79 AVG7.
- **The walk measurably improves the task pool.** For Qwen3-4B, in-band fraction (tasks in the target uncertainty band) rises from 50.72% (no walk) to 68.95% (DEO), with 357/388/349 additional in-band candidates per round and 3682 vs 2892 retained training tasks after filtering (Table 2).
- **API-only generator works.** Swapping the local 4B generator/mutator for Claude Haiku 4.5 (same solver, same recipe): AVG7 47.57 → 49.60, HARD5 32.96 → 35.77, driven by Minerva 45.96 → 50.37 and AIME 2024 6.67 → 20.00; but MATH-500 is flat (76.80) and AMC drops 57.34 → 52.50 (Table 3).
- **Transfer is mixed, as the authors state.** AVG3 (MMLU-Pro/SuperGPQA/BBEH): at 8B DEO is best (35.04 vs no-walk 34.62, R-Zero 34.30) with the top BBEH at both Qwen scales (9.90 vs R-Zero 8.87 at 4B; 10.73 vs 8.43 at 8B); at 4B R-Zero leads AVG3 (32.59 vs DEO 32.17) and MMLU-Pro (59.37 vs 58.10); on Omni-MATH (2000-question subset) no-walk is best at 4B (25.50 vs DEO 24.80, base 22.00).

## Authors & Credibility

Yuyang Deng and Yu Wang are affiliated with Accenture's Center for Advanced AI; Jiayun Wang is at Georgia Institute of Technology. This is an industry–academic collaboration rather than a famous-lab flagship paper, but the work shows unusually high rigor for its tier: a matched-pool control ablation (no walk), archived R-Zero runs re-evaluated under one protocol, an explicit "this comparison does not establish equal total compute" disclaimer, an appendix documenting every prompt (seed generation, mutation, format check, Claude-Haiku-based answer regrading), fixed seeds for general-domain subsets, and a limitations paragraph that names the theory–practice gap before any reviewer could. The theoretical section is careful about what is and is not proven (e.g., it states plainly that approximate sampling is "an idealization, not a claimed advantage"). Credibility: good; the honest framing increases trust in the headline numbers.

## Critical Assessment

- **The R-Zero comparison is method-level, not compute-matched — by the authors' own admission.** R-Zero keeps its native larger pools (its first three 4B training sets retain 3039/4312/4376 questions vs DEO's 1246/1208/1228), so the "52–61% less time" headline conflates two effects: removing challenger training *and* training on ~3× less data per round. The clean comparison is against the no-walk ablation, and there DEO spends 12.6–20.6h vs 4.9–6.2h — a 2.5–3× time premium for +1.8 AVG7. The cost story is real but should be read as "half of R-Zero," not "cheap."
- **Gains are modest and non-uniform, with visible regressions.** At 4B, AIME 2024 drops *below the base checkpoint* (DEO 6.67 vs base 10.00, no-walk 10.00); R-Zero wins MMLU-Pro at both Qwen scales (59.37/62.83 vs DEO 58.10/62.23); no-walk wins Omni-MATH at 4B (25.50 vs 24.80). Also notable: at OctoThinker-3B, R-Zero's AVG7 (25.94) is *below* no-walk (27.50) — challenger training itself is unstable at that scale, so "DEO ≥ R-Zero" is a weak bar there.
- **Theory–practice gap is substantive, not cosmetic.** The distributional-robustness theorem covers only the idealized exact-sampling variant with 1−p rewards and an L2 proximal instance; the implemented walk uses triangular scores from m=9 noisy rollouts, batch-coupled BLEU penalties, omits the base-density/Hastings factor, runs 5 sweeps with no mixing guarantee, and trains GRPO with a KL-to-original-base reference rather than the analyzed L2 step. None of the evaluated numbers are covered by the bound — the theory is motivation, not a guarantee about the system tested.
- **Evaluation quirks worth knowing.** All math scores pass through a Claude Haiku 4.5 LLM answer-grader; AMC/AIME rows repeat each question 32 times and the authors explicitly decline significance testing on them; there is a single run per cell with no seeds or error bars, and only 3 iterations are reported (at 4B, R-Zero actually leads AVG7 at iterations 1–2 — 46.46/48.13 vs DEO 45.09/47.71 — so the iteration-3-only snapshot flatters DEO).
- **Novelty is genuine but incremental over Evol-Instruct-style rewriting.** LLM-based task rewriting has precedent (Evol-Instruct, GEPA); DEO's real contribution is grounding selection in a regularized challenger objective via a Metropolis rule. The clearest "aha" — using the objective's closed-form tilt to justify *sampling instead of training* the proposer — is a nice reframing rather than a new algorithmic primitive.

## Strengths

- Correct and well-chosen control: the matched-pool no-walk ablation cleanly separates "walk value" from "challenger-removal value," which most self-evolving papers omit.
- Large, practically meaningful efficiency gain (52–61% wall-clock reduction) verified per-model on stated hardware (8×H100).
- The API-only generator experiment demonstrates a capability that is structurally impossible for challenger-training methods — a genuinely new design point, not just a benchmark delta.
- Theory that actually explains the mechanism (tilted sampling targets worst-case uncertainty; mode preservation makes the bound an accuracy statement), with honest scoping of what is idealized.
- Exceptional reproducibility documentation: full prompt templates, a complete config table (N=2000, m=9, L_w=5, β=0.1, λ_rep=10, GRPO settings), fixed evaluation protocol, and shared grading harness across all compared methods.

## Limitations

- Mathematical reasoning only, with majority-vote pseudo-labeling that presumes verifiable, extractable boxed answers; extension to agent training or open-ended tasks (where the paper wants to go) is untested.
- Only 3 evolution rounds, one run per configuration, no variance estimates; the 4B trajectory shows the ranking flips across iterations, so iteration-3-only reporting is fragile.
- The walk has no cross-round memory (each round restarts from fresh seeds), unlike continuously-evolving challengers — the authors name this; it likely caps how far the curriculum can compound.
- Pseudo-label noise: supervision comes from the current solver's majority vote, and the retained-set counts are dataset sizes, not guaranteed-consumed tasks (appendix caveat); no analysis of label-error rates.
- Single domain family (competition math, 7 benchmarks + Omni-MATH/3 transfer sets); the distributional-robustness story is not empirically tested (no worst-case-uncertainty measurements against the bound).

## Relevance

直接命中第一优先方向 Self-Evolving Agents & Automated Research：这是 R-Zero 系自进化训练的"去挑战者化"改造，把自动课程生成（automated curriculum design）从"再训练一个出题模型"简化为"采样问题"，且理论解释了对抗性任务采样为何有效。对关注长程自治训练系统成本与可靠性的读者，其 API-only 生成器能力和匹配池消融设计都值得借鉴。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- The ranking flips across iterations at 4B (R-Zero leads at iterations 1–2, DEO at 3): does the walk's advantage persist, compound, or regress beyond round 3, and what is the sensitivity to β=0.1 and the BLEU penalty?
- Does the distributional-robustness bound track reality — i.e., can the worst-case answer uncertainty over the KL ball be measured empirically, and does the implemented (approximate) walk stay inside the predicted envelope?
- Can cross-round memory or adaptive temperature close the theory–practice gap (the paper's own future-work item), and how much of no-walk's 2.5–3× time advantage can memory recover?
- Does the API-generator result hold with stronger closed-source generators (or degrade with weaker ones), and does generator-solver capability *mismatch* help or hurt the pseudo-label quality?
- Can DEO extend beyond majority-vote-verifiable math — e.g., agent/tool-use training where the solver's "disagreement" needs a different challenge reward than 1 − 2|p̃ − 1/2|?
