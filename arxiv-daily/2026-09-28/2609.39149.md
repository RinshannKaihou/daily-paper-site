> **一句话总结：** Rep2Skill 把 LLM agent 的内部隐状态轨迹引入文本技能自进化闭环：对同一任务采样 K 条 rollout，用 Neural CDE 在成功轨迹上学到的表征动力学给每个交互轮打"偏离分"定位关键失败轮次，再由分析模型把表征信号转译成有据可依的文本反馈驱动技能修订——在 ALFWorld 上把 Qwen3.5-9B 的成功率从最强文本基线 SkillOpt 的 62.19% 提到 69.90%（+7.77 个百分点），且全程无需外部更强模型。

> **TL;DR:** Rep2Skill injects the agent's own internal hidden-state trajectories into the textual skill self-evolution loop. For K=4 rollouts of the same task, a Neural CDE trained on successful executions scores each turn by representation prediction error; the top-3 "critical turns" are verbalized by an analyzer into grounded textual feedback that guides a validation-gated skill revision. On ALFWorld, this lifts Qwen3-4B success rate from 46.51% (best text-only baseline SkillOpt) to 52.24% (+4.98 pts) and Qwen3.5-9B from 62.19% to 69.90% (+7.77 pts); turn-level non-progress detection improves from 0.494 AUROC (trajectory text alone) to 0.838 AUROC with text+representation guidance. The gains rest on an ablation-verified trio: group-wise rollout comparison, representation attribution, and verbalization.

## 核心贡献

- **新视角：** 首次把 agent 自身的内部表征（固定层隐状态序列）作为额外证据源引入文本技能进化闭环，回答"agent 能否通过反思自己的内部表征来改进外部文本技能"——据作者称这是表征空间首次被用于技能进化。
- **方法流水线（四步）：** (1) 组内 rollout 采样（同一任务、同一技能、同一 agent 采 K=4 条）；(2) 表征空间轮级归因——PCA 投影隐状态轨迹，Neural CDE 在成功轨迹上建模表征动力学，推理时用预测误差 ‖h_t − ĥ_t‖² 作为偏离分，取 top-3 关键轮次；(3) 表征→文本转译——分析模型结合关键轮次、完整轨迹与当前技能，生成有证据支撑的反馈（并判断现有技能是否已覆盖该行为）；(4) 验证门控的技能修订——候选技能先在小验证集上评估，只有变好才被接受。
- **机制层面的中间评估：** 构造"非进展轮次检测"任务（GPT-6-Astra 标注），证明轨迹文本几乎无判别力（AUROC 0.494），表征分单独 0.592，两者结合 0.838 / AUPRC 0.876 / Precision@Top-3 88.22%，说明表征信号与文本信号互补。
- **消融与案例：** 三个组件缺一不可（去掉组内证据 −5.47、去掉分析器 −5.22、去掉表征转译 −4.48 个成功率点）；案例研究显示文本-only 优化器只能给出"多搜搜"式泛泛建议，而表征归因把失败定位到第 17–29 轮的重复搜柜，产出"柜子找不到就看台面/抽屉/水槽"的可执行规则，使原本 30 轮超时的任务 8 步完成。

## Key Contributions

- A new perspective on textual skill evolution: the agent's internal representation trajectories (hidden states at a fixed layer) are leveraged as an additional, fine-grained evidence source for credit assignment — claimed as the first use of inner representations for skill evolution.
- REP2SKILL, a four-stage pipeline: (1) group-wise rollout sampling (K=4 rollouts per task instance under the same skill and frozen agent, GRPO-style relative evidence); (2) turn-level representation attribution via a Neural CDE trained on successful trajectories, where prediction error ‖h_t − ĥ_t‖² localizes failure-relevant turns (top-K_e=3); (3) representation-to-text verbalization by an analyzer that grounds each attribution in trajectory evidence; (4) validation-gated skill revision by the optimizer.
- A mechanistic intermediate evaluation (turn-level non-progress detection, labels from GPT-6-Astra) showing trajectory text alone is near-chance (AUROC 0.494) while text+representation guidance reaches 0.838 AUROC / 0.876 AUPRC / 88.22% Precision@Top-3 — evidence that the two signal sources are complementary.
- Consistent end-task gains in a strict self-evolution setting (the same LLM is executor, analyzer, and optimizer; no external stronger model), with the largest gains precisely when the optimizer model is weakest (Qwen3-4B on WebShop, where the strongest baseline SkillOpt falls below No Skill).

## Methodology

**Setting.** A frozen LLM π_θ serves as both task executor and skill optimizer (self-evolution: no stronger external model, no gradient updates). A textual skill s is revised iteratively, s_{n+1} = O(s_n, τ), where standard methods condition only on observable trajectories.

**Stage 1 — Group rollout sampling.** For each task instance x under skill s_n, K=4 trajectories are sampled from the same agent, forming a group G(x, s_n). Within-group behavioral variation (pairwise action disagreement reaches 82.7% at turn 5 in their analysis of 84 Qwen3-4B groups) provides the relative evidence base.

**Stage 2 — Turn-level representation attribution.** At each interaction turn, the hidden state h_t ∈ R^d is extracted from a fixed layer of the frozen agent, PCA-projected to low dimension, forming a representation trajectory H(τ^(k)). Following Yeh et al. (2026), a Neural Controlled Differential Equation (Neural CDE, Kidger et al. 2020) is trained on successful executions to model the latent dynamics: at turn t it predicts ĥ_t from the preceding history (h_1..h_{t-1}). The attribution score is the squared prediction error e_t = ‖h_t − ĥ_t‖²; the top-K_e = 3 turns become the critical-turn set C^(k).

**Stage 3 — Representation-to-text verbalization.** An analyzer model M_a (same Qwen model) receives the critical turns, the full trajectory, the task instance, and the current skill, and produces textual feedback F^(k) (eq. 4). Crucially, the analyzer is instructed to ground every attribution in explicit trajectory evidence, and to conclude when a flagged behavior is already covered by the current skill or when evidence is insufficient — high deviation from successful dynamics alone does not imply a skill error.

**Stage 4 — Skill revision in text space.** The optimizer O consolidates the grouped feedback into candidate skills (eq. 5). Each candidate is evaluated on a small validation set; it is accepted only if it improves over s_n, otherwise s_n is retained. Implementation details (Appendix B.2): for WebShop, 4 rollouts per query form the optimization group; results are averaged over 3 independent evolution runs with different seeds.

## Results & Highlights

- **ALFWorld success rate (3 runs, mean±std):** Qwen3-4B — No Skill 22.64±1.76, Trace2Skill 47.76±5.18, EvoSkill 43.03±3.52, SkillOpt 46.51±3.13, **Rep2Skill 52.24±3.22 (+4.98 pts over the strongest text-only baseline)**; Qwen3.5-9B — SkillOpt 62.19±2.28, EvoSkill 58.96±3.73, **Rep2Skill 69.90±2.88 (+7.77 pts over SkillOpt)**.
- **WebShop:** Qwen3.5-9B task score **53.56±2.52 vs SkillOpt 52.12±2.22 (+1.44)** and success rate **23.33±3.30 vs SkillOpt 18.67±1.25 (+4.66)**; Qwen3-4B task score 49.79±5.95 vs EvoSkill 49.48±2.02 (essentially tied, but SkillOpt 39.04±2.49 falls *below* No Skill 41.86±1.65) and success rate 16.33±3.51 vs No Skill 13.67±1.53.
- **Turn-level non-progress detection (Qwen3-4B, GPT-6-Astra labels, K_e=3):** Trajectory Text alone AUROC 0.494 / AUPRC 0.736 / P@Top-3 63.66%; representation score alone 0.592 / 0.789 / 79.26%; **Text + Rep. Guidance 0.838 / 0.876 / 88.22%** — a +0.344 AUROC jump over text-only attribution.
- **Ablation on ALFWorld (Qwen3-4B, SR):** full Rep2Skill 52.24; w/o representation verbalization 47.76 (−4.48); w/o representation analyzer 47.02 (−5.22); w/o group-wise rollout evidence 46.77 (−5.47) — all three ingredients contribute, and group evidence alone (no representation signal) does not explain the gains.
- **Qualitative case study (Fig. 5):** on a failed "put a clean mug in coffee machine" episode, text-only evolution yields the generic rule "Search each location carefully until the object is found" (re-fails at 30 turns), while representation attribution peaks at turns 17–29 of repeated cabinet search, yielding "If the target is not found in cabinets, check countertops, drawers, or sink" — the revised agent succeeds in 8 steps.

## Critical Assessment

- **The core components are imported, not invented — the novelty is the integration.** Representation trajectory modeling is taken wholesale from Yeh et al. (2026), Neural CDE from Kidger et al. (2020), group-wise relative rollouts mirror GRPO-style advantage estimation, and validation-gated updates come from SkillOpt. What is genuinely new is wiring attribution→verbalization→revision into the text skill loop and the self-evolution framing; the paper is honest about the lineage, but readers expecting a new attribution technique will not find one.
- **The attribution ground truth has a circularity problem.** Turn labels for the RQ2 evaluation come from GPT-6-Astra — an external frontier model — even though the headline setting is strict self-evolution with "no feedback or supervision from a larger external model." A stronger external judge defines what a "non-progress turn" is; if that judge's notion of progress correlates with representation-deviation artifacts of the same model family, the 0.838 AUROC could partly reflect agreement between two LLM judgments rather than genuine localization. No human-labeled subset is reported to calibrate this.
- **WebShop results are fragile relative to the headline claims.** With Qwen3-4B, the task-score gain over EvoSkill is 0.31 (49.79±5.95 vs 49.48±2.02) — within one-third of a standard deviation — and the success-rate std (±3.51) is a fifth of the value itself with only 3 runs. The robust wins are ALFWorld (+4.98, +7.77) and the Qwen3.5-9B WebShop SR (+4.66); the "consistently outperforms" language oversells WebShop.
- **Baseline instability hints at tuning asymmetry.** Trace2Skill collapses to 24.77 task score on WebShop/Qwen3.5-9B versus No Skill's 50.07, and SkillOpt drops below No Skill on Qwen3-4B — plausible evidence for the paper's thesis, but it also means half the baseline cells are degenerate, so effective comparison rests on two baselines (EvoSkill, SkillOpt) rather than four.
- **Narrow model coverage and stated overhead.** Only Qwen3-4B (full attention) and Qwen3.5-9B (hybrid attention) are tested — one family; the Limitations section concedes white-box access is required and that the method adds ~50 extra rollouts plus a CDE training stage, yet no wall-clock or token-cost comparison against baselines is given to judge whether the accuracy-per-cost trade is favorable.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity is underexplored on the end task.** K_e is fixed at 3 for the main table; Fig. 3 sweeps K∈{1,3,5,7,10} only for the detection proxy, not for downstream ALFWorld/WebShop performance, and no CDE training-set-size sensitivity is shown.

## Strengths

- A clean, well-motivated question — can an agent improve its textual skills by reflecting on its own internal states? — with an affirmative, mechanistically supported answer rather than just an end-to-end win.
- The RQ2 intermediate evaluation (turn-level detection) is the right kind of evidence: it validates the causal pathway (better attribution) instead of only reporting downstream scores.
- Ablations are decisive and falsifying: removing group-wise evidence, attribution, or verbalization each costs 4.5–5.5 SR points, and the w/o-attribution variant (which still sees grouped rollouts) isolates the contribution of representation signals specifically.
- The case study concretely demonstrates the qualitative difference in feedback quality (generic "search harder" vs. a localized, actionable rule), which is exactly the failure mode of text-only reflection the paper targets.
- Honest Limitations section that names white-box access, Qwen-only evaluation, and the ~50-rollout overhead up front.
- Gains are largest exactly where the theory predicts (weakest optimizer model, Qwen3-4B WebShop where text-only SkillOpt goes below No Skill) — internally consistent story.

## Limitations

- Requires white-box access to hidden states of the serving model; black-box API agents cannot use it directly (the authors suggest an accessible proxy model as a workaround, untested here).
- Evaluated only on two models from one family (Qwen3-4B, Qwen3.5-9B) and two classic benchmarks (ALFWorld, 2021; WebShop, 2022); generalization to other architectures and to harder, longer-horizon agentic environments is unverified.
- Representation trajectory modeling adds overhead: ~50 additional rollouts for CDE training plus a lightweight training stage, with no cost/latency accounting against text-only baselines.
- Attribution quality is measured against another LLM's labels (GPT-6-Astra), not human judgment; the reliability ceiling of the detection numbers is therefore unknown.
- K_e=3, K=4, PCA dimensionality, and the CDE layer choice are fixed without end-task sensitivity analysis; PCA projection of hidden states may also discard classes of failure signals that live in the discarded components.

## Relevance

这篇论文同时命中你优先级最高的 Self-Evolving Agents 方向和 LLM Hidden States & Interpretability 方向：它把隐状态表征作为自进化闭环中的归因信号，是"内部表征可观测性提升 agent 可靠性"的代表性工作。其 Neural CDE 表征偏离打分思路对你的 Inference Reliability / 异常检测研究（用动力学预测误差定位异常点）也有直接的方法论借鉴价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would human-verified (rather than GPT-6-Astra-labeled) non-progress turns change the 0.838 AUROC headline, and how much of the text+representation gain reflects LLM-judge agreement rather than true localization?
- Can the Neural CDE be trained once and reused across environments, or does attribution quality degrade when the successful-trajectory training set comes from a different task distribution (cross-benchmark transfer of the trajectory model)?
- Does the method survive with a proxy executor (representations from a small open model guiding a black-box frontier agent), as the authors suggest — and if so, does the executor–proxy representation gap erode the attribution signal?
- The gain on WebShop/Qwen3.5-9B is +1.44 task score (within noise) but +4.66 SR: is the representation signal mainly preventing catastrophic skill regressions rather than improving average behavior?
- Could representation-deviation scores double as a *runtime* early-abort signal (à la "Doomed from the start") during deployment, rather than only as an offline credit-assignment signal for skill revision?
