> **一句话总结：** 对 Mamba/混合架构语言模型的循环状态（recurrent state）低精度存储问题，用一个无需随机数的黄金比率 Weyl 抖动（dither）取代生产系统标准的随机舍入（stochastic rounding），在 6 个纯 Mamba + 3 个混合模型 × INT8/FP8/BF16 共 15 个主实验设置中全部显著降低对全精度模型的逐 token KL 散度（降幅 11–49%，中位数 31%，95% bootstrap 下界均大于 0），优势保持到 4096 步量化解码且内核开销仅为 RTN 的 1.00–1.13 倍；而 RTN 会静默丢弃小于半个量化步长的更新，其误差随解码步数近似线性增长，在 4096 步后落后 Weyl 抖动最多 418 倍。

> **TL;DR:** When a Mamba-style recurrent state is stored in low precision, every rounding error is fed back into the next update, so the rounding rule matters far more than for weights. Replacing stochastic rounding (the production default in NVIDIA Nemotron 3) with a deterministic golden-ratio Weyl dither lowers teacher-forced KL divergence to the FP32 model on WikiText-103 (pure Mamba) and PG-19 (hybrids) in all 15 model×format settings — by 11–49% (median 31%), e.g. Mamba-2-130M INT8: KL 2.83e-3 vs SR 4.15e-3 and RTN 26.9e-3 — and a Weyl-dithered INTk state matches SR at about INT(k+0.25), i.e. a free quarter bit. RTN looks best only in the first few hundred steps on Granite hybrids, then collapses (up to 418× worse than Weyl on Falcon-H1 BF16 at 4096 steps). The paper also documents three implementation bugs (rational increments, space-time aliasing via Knuth's hash constant, float32 phase computation) that silently erase the benefit.

## 核心贡献

- **问题重定义**：首次系统研究"循环状态量化"中的舍入规则选择——与权重量化不同，Mamba 状态每步被读-改-写，任何舍入误差都会回灌到下一步更新中并跨数千步累积（引用 Nemotron 3 Super：直接把 Mamba 状态 cast 到 FP16 使生成时长最多增加 37%，因此 NVIDIA 转向随机舍入）。
- **确定性的第三选项**：提出用黄金比率 Weyl 低差异序列（阈值 u_{i,t} = {αi + tα}，α = φ−1 ≈ 0.618，按 block+位置取二维偏移以避免锁步）替代需要随机数发生器的随机舍入：无随机数、确定可复现，且在全部 15 个设置中优于或统计上不打平 SR，不产生额外开销。
- **统一理论解释**：把三种规则纳入同一阈值公式 Q_u(x)，证明完美记忆+常数写入下累积误差恰为阈值序列的 star discrepancy（Proposition 1）；推论给出 RTN 误差 ~O(T) 线性增长、SR ~O(√T)、Weyl ~O(1+log T)；Lemma 1 解释 RTN 为何静默丢弃 |δ| < s/2 的小更新（恰是长记忆条目的典型写入），Lemma 2 解释它为何每步噪声减半（s²/12 vs s²/6）因而短期占优。
- **机制定位**：增益集中在长记忆条目——只对记忆时间常数最长的 25% 条目用 Weyl（其余用 SR）即可保留全部增益的 75–87%；对单条目递推做"重放"实验（剥离网络其余部分）预测出的 Weyl/SR 误差比（0.71–0.74）与实测 KL 比（0.70–0.74）吻合，说明增益是状态递推本身的性质，可在不跑量化模型的情况下预测。
- **三个静默陷阱（工程价值）**：(1) 增量接近简单分数（如 1/2+2⁻¹²，比 SR 差约 40 倍）；(2) 逐条目偏移与时间混叠——Knuth 哈希常数 2654435761 与黄金增量 2654435769 仅差 8·2⁻³²，用 Knuth 偏移使 Mamba-2 INT4 的 KL 高出 34–40%；(3) 用 float32 计算相位（t=32767 时仅剩 512 个不同阈值序列，2000 步内无害、中位 KL 变化 0.1%，但 32k 上下文下会使 Weyl 反而输给 SR）。
- **实验规范本身即贡献**：预注册每实验的假设与判据、模型校验门（logits 与参考实现对齐，据此剔除 Falcon-H1 1.5B 和 Zamba2 1.2B）、CPU 小模型干跑、每个表格数字可追溯到 notebook 输出、公开全部代码与自报三处已修正的编码错误。

## Key Contributions

- Reframes state-cache quantization as a *rounding-rule* problem where errors compound through feedback — unlike weight quantization — grounding it in production evidence (Nemotron 3 Super: naive FP16 casting of the Mamba state lengthened generations by up to 37%, motivating NVIDIA's switch to SR).
- Shows a deterministic golden-ratio Weyl dither replaces stochastic rounding without random numbers or added cost, beating SR in 15/15 settings (11–49% lower KL, median 31%, all 95% bootstrap lower bounds above zero) and holding the advantage through 4096 quantized decoding steps.
- Gives a unified analysis via one threshold formula: accumulated error equals the star discrepancy of the threshold sequence (Prop 1), yielding RTN ~linear growth, SR ~√T, Weyl ~O(1+log T); Lemma 1 explains RTN's silent erasure of sub-half-step updates, Lemma 2 its halved per-write noise.
- Localizes the gain: applying the dither only to the 25% longest-memory entries retains 75–87% of the full benefit; recurrence-replay experiments reproduce the measured Weyl/SR ratios (0.71–0.74 replayed vs 0.70–0.74 measured), showing the effect is predictable from the state recurrence alone.
- Documents three silent implementation pitfalls — near-rational increments (~40× worse than SR), offsets aliasing with time (Knuth's hash constant 2654435761 vs golden 2654435769, +34–40% KL), and float32 phase computation (only 512 distinct threshold sequences at t=32767; reverses the ordering at 32k-token context) — each of which removes the benefit while the code runs without error.

## Methodology

**Rounding rules.** All three rules fit one formula: to store x with quantization step s, write x/s = k + f (integer k, fraction f ∈ [0,1)) and round up iff threshold u ≥ 1−f. RTN uses u = 1/2 always. SR draws u uniformly at random per write (unbiased; the production default). The Weyl dither uses u_{i,t} = {αi + tα} with golden-ratio increment α = φ−1 ≈ 0.618 and a fixed per-entry offset; offsets are generated per (block, position) two-dimensionally so neighbouring entries do not move in lockstep. Floating-point formats apply the same rule on the local representable grid.

**Models and formats.** Pure Mamba in the decode regime: Mamba-1 130M/370M, Mamba-2 130M (plus Mamba-1 790M/1.4B and Mamba-2 370M in the every-token stress regime). Hybrids: Granite 4.0-H 350M and 1B (IBM) and Falcon-H1 0.5B. All weights/activations FP32 (TF32 disabled); only the recurrent state is quantized, in INT8 (FP32 block scale, blocks of 16 entries for Mamba / 128 for hybrids), FP8 E4M3, and BF16.

**Protocol.** Decode regime (main results): prompt processed in full precision, only states written during generation quantized. Metric: per-token KL divergence between the FP32 model's next-token distribution and the quantized-state model's, teacher-forced — 32 WikiText-103 test chunks (1024-token FP prefix + 1024 quantized positions) for pure Mamba; 32 PG-19 books (2048-token prefill + 512 quantized steps) for hybrids; a long-horizon study with 8 books and 4096 quantized steps. Relative KL reductions carry 95% bootstrap intervals over documents (SR run with two seeds for Mamba models); "significant" means the lower bound is above zero. Every experiment's hypotheses and pass criteria were written into its notebook before it ran (pre-registration).

**Verification and infrastructure.** Each model is checked against reference implementations (exact logit match for Mamba-1; 1.3×10⁻³ for Mamba-2; hybrid decoding reproduces the full forward pass) before any result is recorded; Falcon-H1 1.5B and Zamba2 1.2B failed verification and were excluded. Notebooks were dry-run end-to-end on CPU with tiny random models before GPU time. All experiments ran on a single NVIDIA Tesla T4 (16 GB) in Kaggle notebooks (PyTorch 2.10, Transformers 5.0.0); Triton kernel timings measured on the T4.

**Theory.** In the idealized regime (a_t = 1, constant write), accumulated error = s × (star discrepancy of the T thresholds) (Prop 1). Corollary: RTN error = sT·min(f, 1−f) (linear); SR typical size ~s√(Tf(1−f)); Weyl stays below C·s(1+log T); a Weyl increment near a simple fraction (1/2+ε) is as bad as RTN for up to 1/(8ε) steps. Lemma 2: mean squared per-write error is s²/12 under RTN vs s²/6 under SR/Weyl. The analysis predicts the *ordering* of rules, not the gain size — the real Weyl-to-SR ratio is a roughly constant factor (~0.7–0.75), not a widening gap.

## Results & Highlights

- **Main comparison (Table 1, decode regime, KL ×10⁻³ to FP32, lower better):** Weyl beats SR in all 15 cells (5 models × INT8/FP8/BF16), with reductions of 11–49% (median 31%) and all 95% lower bounds above zero. Concrete cells: Mamba-1-130M INT8 — RTN 1.73, SR 0.88, Weyl 0.58 (−34.1% vs SR); Mamba-2-130M INT8 — RTN 26.9, SR 4.15, Weyl 2.83 (−31.7%); BF16 Mamba-1-130M — RTN 1.08, SR 2.15, Weyl 0.129 (−39.9%). The smallest gain is Granite 1B at FP8: 11% with a lower bound of 2%. SR is the best rule in no cell.
- **Long horizon (4096 quantized steps, 3 hybrids, Fig 1):** the Weyl curve stays below SR over the whole horizon — averaged over 4096 steps Weyl lowers KL by 25%/13% (Granite 350M, INT8/BF16), 21%/30% (Granite 1B), and 12% [LB 4%]/56% [48%] (Falcon-H1 0.5B). The advantage narrows (Granite 1B INT8: 27%→21% between first and last 512 steps; Falcon-H1 BF16: 68%→17%) but RTN collapses: its per-token KL climbs by a factor of 4–45 over 4096 steps, ending 47× worse than Weyl at INT8 and 418× at BF16 on Falcon-H1; on pure Mamba RTN is 1.4–17× worse than Weyl in every cell.
- **Bits equivalence:** storing with the Weyl dither at INTk matches SR at about INT(k+0.25) (range +0.16 to +0.30 bits over three models and three widths) — a 30% KL reduction is worth roughly a free quarter bit.
- **Mechanism validated:** applying Weyl only to the 25% longest-memory entries (SR elsewhere) keeps 75–87% of the full gain; the recurrence-replay error ratios Weyl/SR (0.74, 0.71, 0.73, 0.74, 0.71) match the measured KL ratios (0.73, 0.72, 0.70, 0.70, 0.74); across nine threshold sequences, local discrepancy ranks the damage with ρ = 0.93–1.00, and a near-rational increment (1/2+2⁻¹²) is ~40× worse than SR, as Corollary 1(d) predicts.
- **Task-level evidence (limited):** in the every-token regime on LAMBADA with INT4 states, the Weyl dither was more accurate on all six models (mean +1.0 point); in sampled generation no detectable quality difference was found.
- **Cost:** a Triton INT8 state-update kernel with the Weyl threshold takes 1.00–1.13× RTN's time; SR via Triton's Philox generator is 2–4× slower (a cheap integer-hash SR is accuracy-equivalent within 2.5–9%). The authors claim equal cost, not a speed advantage.
- **Boundary conditions (honest):** with periodic checkpointing (rounding only every 8th write) the Weyl advantage mostly disappears on hybrids (FP8 lower bounds 0.4–7.6%); with a block Hadamard rotation before INT4 quantization SR beats Weyl (KL 0.166 vs 0.235 on Mamba-1-130M), although rotation hurt every rule and at INT8 rotation+Weyl was the best combination.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty is narrower than the framing suggests — and the paper says so.** Deterministic low-discrepancy dithered rounding dates to Schuchman (1964) and was proposed for quantization as a lower-variance SR alternative by Wu (2021); the genuine contribution is studying it *inside the state-feedback loop* (where errors compound) plus the discrepancy-based ordering result and the pitfalls catalog. That is a real but incremental contribution — an engineering-science paper, not a new technique.
- **The headline metric is teacher-forced KL, not task quality.** Absolute KLs in the main table are 10⁻³–10⁻¹ per token; the paper's own earlier evaluations found *no detectable difference* in sampled-text quality, and the only task evidence is a +1.0-point LAMBADA mean in a stress regime. The claim "do not use stochastic rounding" is well-supported for KL but practically under-demonstrated for end-task quality at deployment scale.
- **Scale and systems realism are the biggest gaps.** All models are 130M–1.4B, run on a single T4 with FP32 host compute (TF32 off) and custom step-by-step runners — not production quantization kernels; there is no end-to-end throughput or latency number, and the author concedes larger hybrids could not be verified with the available library version. Whether the ordering survives on 7B+ hybrids with fused INT8/FP8 kernels is untested.
- **Sample sizes are modest.** 32 documents per cell in the main table (8 books in the long-horizon study), SR run with only two seeds on the Mamba models. The bootstrap intervals mitigate this, but the long-horizon conclusions rest on very few documents.
- **An honestly-reported but unresolved anomaly:** RTN is the *best* rule for the first ~512 steps on Granite 4.0-H at INT8/FP8, and four pre-planned attempts to explain why (recurrence replay, noise-injection sensitivity probe, sensitivity-weighted replay, RTN-on-long-memory-only) all failed. This is commendable transparency, but it means the "one rule everywhere" story is incomplete for the hybrid family (IBM Granite) most likely to be deployed, and it hints the mechanism account is not the whole picture.
- **The recommendation is regime-conditional in ways the abstract underplays:** under periodic checkpointing (a realistic serving choice) the Weyl advantage mostly vanishes on hybrids, and under Hadamard rotation at INT4 SR wins. The rule "always replace SR with Weyl" holds only for every-write rounding without orthogonal transforms.
- **An unexplained quirk in the paper's own Table 1:** on Mamba-1-130M BF16, SR (2.15) is ~2× *worse* than RTN (1.08) — an interaction between dithering and float formats that the paper bolds past without discussing.
- **Reproducibility is excellent for a solo preprint** (public notebooks, per-number traceability, regression tests for the phase-precision bug), but there is no independent reimplementation yet, and the code was AI-assisted with three consequential past errors — mitigated, not eliminated, by the checks.

## Strengths

- A crisp, falsifiable question with a 15/15 consistent answer, statistically disciplined (95% bootstrap lower bounds, pre-registered decision rules) — rare rigor at any scale, exceptional for an independent researcher.
- A genuine mechanistic account, not just a leaderboard: discrepancy theory predicts the *ordering* of the three rules; recurrence replay isolates the cause; and the 75–87%-of-gain-from-25%-of-entries result yields an immediately actionable mixed-precision recipe (dither only long-memory entries, keep 10% longest-memory entries in FP16 → still beats SR by 23–24%).
- The three silent pitfalls section is worth the paper alone: exact failure modes (Knuth's hash constant differing from the golden increment by 8·2⁻³²; float32 collapsing to 512 distinct phases at t=32767) that any implementer could hit while their code passes all tests.
- Honest boundary reporting: equal-cost (not faster) claim, excluded models, failed explanatory attempts, and a limitations section that names its own unexplained result.
- The deterministic rule removes RNG generator state from serving systems — a real operational simplification beyond accuracy.

## Limitations

- Models are small (130M–1.4B); no evidence at production scale, on production kernels, or with end-to-end throughput; single Tesla T4, FP32 host compute.
- Main metric is teacher-forced KL on 32 documents (8 for the long-horizon study); no task-accuracy or generation-quality study beyond LAMBADA (+1.0 point, every-token regime); SR used only two seeds on Mamba models.
- The advantage is regime-dependent: mostly disappears under periodic checkpointing on hybrids; SR wins under Hadamard rotation at INT4; Mamba-1 models cannot be tested beyond their 2048-token training length.
- Theory predicts ordering only, not gain magnitude; the early-decoding RTN advantage on Granite 4.0-H remains unexplained after four attempted analyses.
- Two larger hybrids (Falcon-H1 1.5B, Zamba2 1.2B) were excluded after failing verification against the available library version, so hybrid coverage is three models from two families.
- Single-author, no independent reimplementation, AI-assisted codebase with three found-and-fixed consequential errors.

## Relevance

这篇论文正中"极端性能优化下的可靠性"研究方向：它研究的正是低精度量化在循环状态缓存中引入的、随解码步数逐步回灌放大的静默数值误差，并给出了理论排序与可实施的修复规则。三个"静默陷阱"（代码不报错但收益消失的实现错误）与 SDC 检测/静默损坏的关注点高度同构，对任何做 Mamba/混合模型推理量化部署的工程实践都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the Weyl-dither advantage survive on production quantization kernels (e.g., fused Mamba state-update kernels in serving frameworks) and on 7B+ hybrid models, measured by end-to-end task accuracy and throughput rather than teacher-forced KL?
2. Can the unexplained early-decoding RTN advantage on Granite 4.0-H be explained — e.g., via the observed sensitivity to short-memory state — and does it predict which hybrid families will favor RTN in short benchmarks?
3. How does the dither interact with orthogonal transforms (Hadamard rotation) and learned mixed-precision assignments (e.g., DAMP-style per-channel precision) beyond the single INT4 probe reported here?
4. Since 75–87% of the gain concentrates in the 25% longest-memory entries, can a cheap online estimator identify those entries at runtime, enabling a mixed FP16/INT4+dither cache without scanning all entries?
5. Do the three silent pitfalls (near-rational increments, space-time aliasing, float32 phase precision) exist in currently deployed SR implementations, including NVIDIA's hardware stochastic-rounding generator exposed as a serving setting?
