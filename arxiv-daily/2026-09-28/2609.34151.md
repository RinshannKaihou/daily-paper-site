> **一句话总结：** 提出 LOTS（Likelihood-Only Tool Scoring）：冻结模型参数，对每次工具执行做"留一法"似然干预（删掉某工具的输出证据后重新打分同一条已生成的答案），将请求级贡献按任务聚合、取 Top-K 剪枝，为每个重复任务从智能体自己的执行轨迹中蒸馏出一个持久的小型工具空间——在 TGB 上把 Qwen3-8B 从 56.5%（全 15 工具菜单）提升到 81.5%（+25.0 分），平均只保留 15 个工具中的 2.8–6.8 个，服务期提示 token 减少 33–46%，且不需要任何正确性标签。

> **TL;DR:** The paper introduces LOTS, a label-free method that turns an agent's own execution traces into persistent, task-specific tool spaces: each tool is scored by how much removing its recorded output evidence lowers the log-likelihood of the model's already-realized answer (leave-one-out, teacher-forced), scores are aggregated across a task's requests, and the Top-K tools become the task's pruned menu. On the authors' TGB compositional benchmark this beats serving the full 15-tool menu (Keep all) on all 8 models — e.g. Qwen3-8B 56.5% → 81.5% (+25.0 pts) — while keeping 2.8–6.8 of 15 tools; on BFCL-v4 multi_turn it is best on 3 of 5 models (Qwen3-4B 28.12% vs Dense 25.62%, Keep all 17.71%) and within 0.21 pts of the best baseline elsewhere; on GTA-Atomic (ReAct) it wins 6 of 7 models (Qwen3-8B 22.32% vs Keep all 10.71%). It costs 2.0–4.0× fewer tokens than the label-using TTO baseline and 4.4–9.5× fewer than Beam Search, and spaces fitted on one model transfer to 18 of 20 cross-model pairs on BFCL.

## 核心贡献

- **输出感知的工具评估（output-aware）**：不同于按工具描述或模型先验在运行前选工具，LOTS 用"留一法似然干预"度量每个工具的实际输出对模型最终答案的支持度（$L_x(s)=\mathcal{L}_\theta(y_x|h_x)-\mathcal{L}_\theta(y_x|h_x^{\neg s})$），对同一条冻结答案打分，无需额外 rollout、无需正确性标签。
- **持久化任务级工具空间**：把同一任务多个请求的证据聚合成任务级得分 $\bar{I}(s)$，取 Top-K 剪枝成持久部署状态（每个重复任务一个小空间），后续请求直接复用，新轨迹到来时可修订——以"环境侧状态"而非参数更新实现自进化。
- **降低服务成本**：空间一旦建好，后续请求不再反复搜索/排序全库；TGB 服务期提示 token 从每请求 251–319 降到 143–181（-33–46%），GTA-Atomic 上比 TTO† 省 2.0–4.0× token、比 Beam Search† 省 4.4–9.5×。
- **跨模型复用**：一个模型上拟合出的工具排序可直接迁移给其他模型使用（似然打分只在拟合期需要），BFCL 上 20 个跨模型配对中 18 个优于服务模型自身的 Keep all。
- **理论保证**：Theorem 1 证明在工具间二阶交互有界（$|\Delta_{s,t}F|\le\beta$）时，Top-K 选择对加性近似目标 $\tilde{F}$ 精确最优，且与真实目标 $F$ 的差距被 $\binom{r}{2}\beta$ 约束。

## Key Contributions

- **Likelihood-Only Tool Scoring (LOTS)**: a label-free, output-aware tool scorer that measures each tool's contribution as the leave-one-out change in the log-likelihood of the model's frozen, realized answer, evaluated alongside the other tools in the full execution context (preserving compositional dependencies).
- **Persistent task-specific tool-space evolution**: request-level evidence is aggregated per task into a ranking, pruned to a Top-K space stored as deployment state, reused for later requests of the same task, and revisable as new traces arrive — self-evolution of the executable environment with model parameters fixed.
- **Reduced serving cost with equal-or-better accuracy**: no per-request search/ranking over the global registry; 33–46% prompt-token reduction on TGB and 2.0–9.5× total-token savings vs label-using search baselines on GTA-Atomic.
- **A selection guarantee (Theorem 1)** relating Top-K to maximizing a fixed-output likelihood objective under bounded second-order tool interactions.
- **Extensive empirical validation**: 8 open-weight models × 3 benchmarks (TGB, BFCL-v4 multi_turn, GTA-Atomic) against search-based, LLM-based, commercial (Jev/TypeSafe), and label-using upper-reference baselines.

## Methodology

The agent (a frozen LLM $p_\theta$) serves a stream of tasks $c_1,\dots,c_T$; each task's requests are served under a candidate tool space and their traces (tool calls, outputs, realized answer $y_x$) recorded. LOTS then proceeds in four steps per task batch: (1) **Serve & record** — execute the requests with the fixed menu; (2) **Score** — for each served tool $s$, build an intervened context $h_x^{\neg s}$ that removes the recorded output evidence of $s$ from the execution (downstream tools receive errors or the chain is re-executed, depending on the benchmark's intervention rule), and compute the request-level score $L_x(s)$ as the difference in mean teacher-forced log-likelihood of the *same* frozen answer under the two contexts; (3) **Aggregate** — average over the task's requests that expose $s$, giving a task-level score $\bar{I}(s)$; (4) **Prune & deploy** — keep the Top-K tools as the task's persistent space and reuse it for subsequent requests. Tasks are routed by request clustering (App. D.1), and later batches can revise an existing space with the same scoring rule (running-score updates). Theorem 1 shows Top-K exactly maximizes the additive approximation $\tilde{F}(A)=F(S)-\sum_{s\notin A}I(s)$ of the fixed-output likelihood $F(A)$, with approximation error bounded by the number of tool interactions that violate additivity. Interventions are benchmark-specific: on TGB removing a tool re-executes the dependency chain without it; on BFCL the whole assistant message calling the tool and its observations are removed; on GTA-Atomic the candidate's output line is deleted. Fitting uses 80 requests/family on TGB, the first 10 records per API class on BFCL, and the 33-image read_arith fitting requests on GTA-Atomic; no correctness labels are used anywhere in selection (the paper marks label-using baselines TTO† and Beam Search† with a dagger).

## Results & Highlights

- **TGB (10 families, 15-tool registry, 8 models):** LOTS improves over the full-menu Keep all on all eight models, by +3.8 to +25.0 points, keeping 2.8–6.8 of 15 tools on average. Gains are largest where distractors hurt most: Qwen3-8B rises 56.5% → 81.5% (+25.0) and Mistral-7B 46.4% → 62.8% (+16.4). Learned spaces recover gold chains: on Qwen2.5-7B every family's space contains its gold tool chain, and for 5 of 10 families it is exactly the chain.
- **GTA-Atomic (ReAct agent, 112 pooled eval requests):** LOTS is best in 6 of 7 models — e.g. Qwen3-8B 22.32% vs Keep all 10.71% (+11.61) and Qwen2.5-3B 15.39% vs 7.14% — and ties label-using Beam Search† at 14.29% on Qwen2.5-7B; the single exception is Qwen2.5-14B where Keep all stays ahead (8.04% vs 7.14%). Retrieval baselines collapse here (Tool2Vec 0.00% on three models).
- **BFCL-v4 multi_turn (4 API classes, 5 models):** LOTS is best on 3 of 5 models — Qwen3-4B 28.12±0.72 vs Dense 25.62±1.77 (strongest baseline there) and Keep all 17.71 — and within 0.21 points of the best baseline (Jev) on Gemma4-12B (28.75 vs 28.96) and Phi-4-mini-3.8B (5.00 vs 5.21). It beats Keep all on all 5 models and beats the LLM-router on 4 of 5 by 1.5–19.6 points.
- **Efficiency:** on TGB, serving prompt tokens drop from 251–319 to 143–181 per request (33–46% reduction vs the 15-tool menu); on GTA-Atomic read_arith, LOTS uses 2.0–4.0× fewer total tokens than TTO† and 4.4–9.5× fewer than Beam Search†, and selection cost pays back against Keep all within 9–1,779 served requests.
- **Continual evolution:** streaming eight TGB families into a 120-tool registry (Qwen2.5-7B), per-task credited accuracy ends at 0.87 vs 0.49 for Keep all; successive within-task updates lift final credited accuracy from 22.3% (LOTS, updated) vs 17.7% (Keep all) on the 120-tool registry, with sharp jumps when an update adds a previously missing chain tool.
- **Cross-model reuse:** rankings fitted on a source model beat the serving model's Keep all in 18 of 20 BFCL cross-model pairs, meaning one model's likelihood traces can configure others without re-collecting traces.
- **Diagnostics:** the likelihood ranking separates gold-chain tools from distractors with AUROC 1.000 on 71 of 72 model-family pairs (mean Top-K accuracy 0.819 vs Random 0.222, Keep all 0.694); scoring the model's own frozen output reaches 0.724 accuracy vs 0.796 with gold-answer scoring (Qwen2.5-7B, request-level) — most of the value without labels; an LLM trace-judge using the same traces is worse than likelihood scoring on all five tested models (e.g. 0.623 vs 0.829).

## Authors & Credibility

The team spans the University of British Columbia, the Vector Institute, and Columbia University; Xiaoxiao Li (corresponding) is an established researcher in ML/health and representation learning, and Wenlong Deng (Columbia) leads the project. Author list and writing are professional; the paper includes AI-use, ethics, and reproducibility statements, and promises to release code, configs, scripts, and split definitions. The technical execution (a selection theorem plus ~20 appendix tables of ablations, token-accounting with break-even analysis, and a commercial baseline run through a documented API with cached scores) indicates strong experimental rigor, though the code release was not yet available at this version.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty is real but adjacent.** Leave-one-out likelihood scoring is essentially ContextCite-style context attribution repurposed for tool valuation and, crucially, *persisted per task* — the persistent-state framing is the genuinely new part, and the paper is honest about the lineage (it even compares against Shapley values, finding Pearson 0.95 agreement with a sign disagreement on one of seven chain tools).
- **Benchmark scale is thin.** The headline GTA-Atomic evaluation pools only 112 requests; the paper's own combination diagnostic reports a +7.69-point delta with a 95% bootstrap interval of [−1.54, +16.92] — the interval includes zero, which should temper single-number comparisons at this scale. TGB is the authors' own synthetic benchmark with 15 tools and hand-designed same-family distractors, and the registry-scaling diagnostic caps at 240 tools — far from real registries with thousands of MCP servers.
- **The evidence-removal operator is hand-engineered per benchmark** (re-execute chains on TGB, delete whole assistant messages on BFCL, delete candidate lines on GTA-Atomic, with hand-picked scored-token regexes like numeric expressions). This is the method's hidden engineering cost and its generality to arbitrary environments is asserted rather than demonstrated.
- **Requires open-weight logprobs.** Likelihood scoring only works on weights the deployer can run, so LOTS cannot configure closed frontier models — precisely the deployments with the largest tool menus. The Jev baseline (TypeSafe's commercial jev-1.13.0) is a closed relevance model the authors could only probe through an API, an asymmetric comparison they handle transparently but which remains second-best evidence.
- **Some models regress and robustness is rule-dependent.** Qwen2.5-14B loses to Keep all on GTA-Atomic (7.14 vs 8.04); Llama-3.1-8B needs an explicitly reported exception to the support-frequency threshold. Under synthetic answer corruption, mean-score retention falls from ~0.7 to 0.620 at 50% wrong answers and 0.102 at 75%, so label-free scoring can entrench the model's own wrong outputs unless the retention rule is chosen carefully — a failure mode the paper measures but does not solve.
- **Cold start and routing are assumed.** Each task needs 10–80 fitting requests and a clustering-based router to pick the right space; misrouting behavior is relegated to diagnostics rather than stress-tested in the main results, and scoring time grows steeply with registry size (176 s at 15 tools to 8,158 s at 120 on one L40S for the full-registry round).

## Strengths

- A clean problem framing — output-unaware selection, statelessness across requests, and per-request inference cost — with a method that addresses all three at once.
- Gains are consistent across 8 models and 3 heterogeneous benchmarks (synthetic composition, multi-turn function calling, agentic ReAct execution), not cherry-picked to one setting.
- Beats label-using upper-reference methods (TTO†, Beam Search†) at a fraction of the cost, and the ablation isolating the scoring target (own-output 0.724 vs gold 0.796) directly justifies the label-free design.
- Unusually thorough appendix: deletion-rule ablation, trace-judge comparison, Shapley comparison, trace-count sensitivity (Spearman 0.957 with only 100 traces), corruption robustness, and an honest token-accounting with break-even request counts (9–1,779).
- Cross-model transfer (18/20) makes the fitted spaces a reusable artifact rather than a per-model tuning step.

## Limitations

- Evaluation scale is small (112 pooled GTA-Atomic requests; 160 BFCL eval records; TGB is synthetic and author-designed), and the 240-tool scaling ceiling is well below production registries.
- The per-benchmark intervention design means applying LOTS to a new environment requires manual specification of how to excise a tool's evidence and which output tokens to score.
- Requires loglikelihood access (open weights) for fitting; closed-model deployments can only consume spaces fitted by other open models.
- Cold-start cost: 10–80 fitting requests per task plus correct task routing before the space helps; the selection cost amortizes only after 9–1,779 requests depending on model and benchmark.
- Label-free scoring inherits the model's blind spots — under heavy corruption of its own answers, the space quality collapses unless a tuned support-frequency retention rule is used, and one model (Qwen2.5-14B on GTA-Atomic) is better off with no pruning.

## Relevance

这篇论文正中你排名第一的兴趣方向 Self-Evolving Agents & Automated Research：它不给模型做任何参数更新，而是把智能体自身的执行经验蒸馏成持久的、按任务演化的环境侧状态（工具空间），是"自进化 = 冻结模型 + 可进化外部状态"这一技术路线的代表性工作。其基于似然的留一归因打分也与你在 LLM hidden states / interpretability 方向的兴趣有轻度交集。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How robust is the clustering-based task router when task types overlap or drift — does misrouting degrade gracefully toward Keep-all behavior or catastrophically?
- Can the evidence-removal operator be automated for arbitrary MCP-style environments, or does LOTS fundamentally need a hand-crafted intervention per tool API?
- At 10k+ tool scale, would LOTS-style persistent spaces compose with deferred retrieval (e.g. Tool Search Tool-style BM25 behind a coarse picker), and does the additive-interaction assumption of Theorem 1 survive?
- Since gold-answer scoring still beats own-output scoring (0.796 vs 0.724 on Qwen2.5-7B), could a small labeled holdout or verifier signal close the remaining gap without giving up label-freeness?
- How do persistent spaces behave under API drift (tools changing signatures or degrading), and can the same likelihood machinery detect and prune broken tools rather than merely irrelevant ones?
