> **一句话总结：** 提出 EvalResearchBench（ERB）：让 9 种 LLM 智能体"研究员"在 3 小时和 $100 推理 + $100 开发测试的预算内，为 coding / co-work / reasoning 三个域自主选题、写评分器、经试点反馈修订并冻结一份 ≥100 题的可执行评测器，再在 13 个候选模型上独立运行，与 14 个目标基准的参考分数比对（Score=100×CCC 一致性 + PA 排序一致率）；结果最好的智能体也只排对约 75% 的模型对（Grok 4.7 达 74.87% PA，仅比 Random120 人工抽样基线的 68.29% 高 6.6 个百分点，且远低于目标基准间分歧造成的 91.20% 天花板），而执行成本最低的设计（Grok $8.63）反而拿到最高 PA，成本高 12.7 倍的 Fable 5.1（$109.41）一致性反而更低。

> **TL;DR:** ERB is a benchmark for "autonomous evaluation research": an LLM agent (researcher) gets target descriptions, public tasks, execution traces, and APIs, then must select/synthesize at least 100 scored questions with executable graders across coding, co-work, and reasoning, iterating via pilot tests within 3 hours and a $200 build budget before freezing the evaluator. On 13 candidate models scored against 14 target benchmarks (e.g., SWE-bench, Terminal-Bench 2.1, GDPval, HLE, BrowseComp), the best agent design (Grok 4.7) orders 74.87% of candidate pairs correctly versus 68.29% for the human-designed Random120 public-task sampling baseline (+6.6 pp), still well below the 91.20% ceiling set by disagreements among the targets themselves; and Grok's cheap-to-run suite ($8.63 execution) beats Fable 5.1's $109.41 suite on PA, showing cost and quality are design choices, not correlates.

## 核心贡献

- **新任务定义——自主评测研究（autonomous evaluation research）：** 与 PostTrainBench / RSIBench-Data / AlphaEvolve（评测协议固定）、PACE（固定代理集）以及 AutoBenchier / BenchAgents / BenchBench（只自动构建或比较基准）不同，ERB 要求智能体端到端地"选择测量策略 → 在有限预算内实验 → 审查失败证据 → 修订任务/评分器/权重 → 冻结可执行评测器"，首次把评测设计本身变成被评测的对象。
- **严格的两阶段协议：** 研究阶段（3 小时，$100 研究员推理 + $100 共享开发/助手测试，只能看到 4 个开发可见候选模型的参考分数）与独立评估阶段（$120 总额 / $30 单候选上限，对 4 个开发可见 + 9 个封存候选模型运行冻结程序）彻底分离，"封存候选 + 封存目标"设计让"拟合可见参考 ≠ 迁移能力"可以被直接测量。
- **双指标加天花板的评分体系：** Score（100×Lin 一致性相关系数，奖励水平与离散度匹配）+ PA（候选对排序一致率，打平计半分），并给出固定分 sanity check（常数 0.5 分 → Score=0、PA=50%）、各域 oracle RMSE 下限，以及由目标基准间互相分歧决定的 PA 上限 91.20%。
- **大规模研究力评测：** 9 种研究员配置（GPT-6 Astra、Grok 4.7 走 Codex 框架，GLM-5.3、DeepSeek V4 Pro、Kimi K3、Fable 5.1、Fable 5、Opus 5、GPT-5.6 Sol 走 Claude Code）× 每种一个完整研究回合，覆盖 13 个候选模型、14 个目标基准，全程 API 级成本记账。
- **系统的失败模式分类学：** 识别并量化了智能体评测器的四类典型病灶——答案被输出上限截断（Astra 电子表格任务 46/96 无最终答案）、评估预算耗尽（Fable 5 在 39 个候选-域报告中只完成 29 个）、少数题权重垄断（Grok 6 道 co-work 题占该域总权重 93.87%）、拟合了开发目标却不迁移（Fable 5.1 开发目标第一、封存目标中游）。

## Key Contributions

- **A new capability benchmark:** ERB poses the full evaluation-research problem — the agent chooses the measurement strategy, spends a bounded experimental budget, and revises an executable evaluator until frozen — which prior automated-benchmark lines (AutoBenchier, BenchAgents, BenchBench, PACE) explicitly do not cover, and assesses the submitted artifact independently of the process that produced it.
- **A sealed-transfer protocol:** development-visible vs. sealed candidates and targets are assigned independent access; freezing tasks/grades/weights before assessment makes "fitting visible references does not ensure transfer" directly measurable (and it is: the development-target leader is not the sealed-target leader).
- **Two complementary metrics with context:** Score (100×CCC, rewarding level+spread+order) and pairwise agreement (PA), together with a constant-score sanity check (Score=0, PA=50%), oracle RMSE floors per domain, and a target-imposed PA ceiling of 91.20% that contextualizes every leaderboard number.
- **A 9-config researcher study:** GPT-6 Astra, Grok 4.7 (Codex harness) and GLM-5.3, DeepSeek V4 Pro, Kimi K3, Fable 5.1, Fable 5, Opus 5, GPT-5.6 Sol (Claude Code harness), each for one episode against 13 candidates and 14 targets, with full API-equivalent cost accounting split into researcher inference, development testing, and frozen assessment.
- **A failure-mode taxonomy for agent-built evaluators:** answer truncation at output caps, assessment-budget exhaustion, weight concentration in a few questions, and development-set overfitting — each demonstrated with concrete counterfactual re-aggregations rather than anecdotes.

## Methodology

**Roles.** A *researcher* is an LLM agent in a harness (Codex for GPT-6 Astra and Grok 4.7; Claude Code for the other seven) that can inspect files, write/execute code, call candidate models, and revise its work. A *candidate* is one of 13 model-execution configurations whose capabilities the evaluator must measure (4 development-visible: GPT-5.5, GLM-5.2, DeepSeek V4 Flash-0731, Qwen3.8 Max; 9 sealed: GPT-6 Astra, GPT-5.6 Sol, Kimi K3, MiMo V2.5 Pro, DeepSeek V3.2-Terminus, Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, GPT-OSS 120B, Nemotron 3 Ultra, Fable 5). A *target benchmark* is one of 14 established evaluations (coding: DeepSWE, SciCode, SWE-bench Multilingual, Terminal-Bench 2.1, ProgramBench, SWE-bench; co-work: GDPval, AdvancedIF, BrowseComp, Harvey LAB-AA, AA-LCR, CritPt; reasoning: HLE) with published reference score snapshots.

**Research episode.** The researcher receives target descriptions, available original tasks and grading resources, historical execution traces, reference scores for the 4 development candidates, development APIs, and an execution SDK. Budgets: 3-hour wall clock, $100 researcher inference, $100 shared development/helper testing (kept separate so deliberation cannot consume the testing allowance). The submission contract requires at least 100 distinct scored questions spanning all three domains, executable graders, fixed aggregation rules and weights, and answer allowances; scoring must not use candidate-identity lookup or reference labels. Tasks, grading code, and weights are then frozen.

**Independent assessment.** The frozen program runs on all candidates under a $120 total cap and $30 per-candidate cap. A graded response is "complete" if it satisfies the submitted contract (including wrong or empty answers at the output cap); infrastructure failures and budget-stopped items stay incomplete. Measured domain score = weighted mean of item grades over completed items (Eq. 1), compared against target references — 6 development and 8 sealed targets are always reported separately.

**Metrics.** *Score* = 100 × Lin's concordance correlation coefficient between measured and reference scores over matched candidates (exact agreement 100; constant measurements 0; CCC penalizes mean and scale mismatch). *PA* = fraction of non-tied reference pairs ordered correctly (ties earn half credit), averaged target-equally over the 14 eligible targets. Percentage references are min-max normalized on the fixed reference panel.

**Baseline and checks.** Random120 stratified-samples 120 public tasks from 16 sources (40 coding / 40 co-work / 40 reasoning) with fixed local graders and equal weights — no target-reference access. Sanity checks: a constant 0.5-per-domain evaluator scores exactly Score=0 and PA=50.00%. The appendix derives oracle RMSE floors per domain (e.g., 0.303 overall for coding, driven by two incompatible coding references whose means on the same six candidates are 0.0075 vs 0.876) and an oracle PA ceiling (91.20% overall) from cross-target ordering conflicts.

**Analyses.** Component audits remove each submission's native task groups and compare against 1,000 matched random deletions; question-count sensitivity recomputes metrics over 1,000 nested subsets of 10/30/60/90 questions; aggregation counterfactuals re-score frozen responses under uniform weights; costs are split into candidate generation vs. rubric/equivalence grading.

## Results & Highlights

- **Ordering quality: agents barely beat stratified public sampling, and nothing approaches the ceiling.** The best evaluator (Grok 4.7) attains 74.87% overall PA versus 68.29% for the human-designed Random120 baseline (+6.58 pp) — yet cross-target reference conflicts cap *any* single ordering at 91.20% overall PA. Only four of the eight observed agent evaluators exceed Random120's PA, while seven of eight exceed its Score (13.10).
- **Score leaderboard is tight and low.** GPT-6 Astra tops overall Score at 22.79 (Random120: 13.10, +9.69), with Grok 4.7 at 22.59, Fable 5.1 at 21.96, Opus 5 at 21.79 — down to DeepSeek V4 Pro at 10.49. No researcher leads on both metrics or in all three domains: Astra wins Score, Grok wins PA, and coding is the weakest domain for most (Astra's overall coding Score is -0.20, below the 0 floor of the constant-score sanity check).
- **Development fit does not transfer.** Fable 5.1 leads development-target Score (23.40 avg over 6 dev targets vs Astra's 20.88) but drops to 20.87 on the 8 sealed targets, where Grok 4.7 leads at 26.14 (+5.27 over Fable 5.1) — direct evidence that fitting visible references does not ensure a broadly useful evaluator.
- **Execution cost is a design choice, uncorrelated with quality at similar scale.** Grok submits 107 questions whose frozen execution costs $8.63 (median prompt 233 characters, no helper calls) and attains the highest PA; Fable 5.1 submits a similar 110 questions but costs $109.41 (12.7x) for 67.80% overall PA. Per-candidate, Fable 5's candidate-call cost is 16.73x Grok's on GPT-5.5 ($26.55 vs $1.59) and 7.12x on GPT-6 Astra ($25.47 vs $3.58) while completed-item counts differ by less than 10%.
- **Pathologies quantified by counterfactuals.** Grok's six highest-weight co-work items carry 93.87% of that domain's score; re-scoring with uniform weights raises its matched-panel overall Score from 22.18 to 37.03 while overall PA barely moves (76.07% → 76.01%) — the frozen suite contains signal that its own aggregation suppresses. Removing Astra's 8 spreadsheet tasks cuts co-work Score by 31.94 points and PA by 12.57 pp (46/96 responses in that component hit the output cap with no final answer); removing ArXiv-math components raises reasoning Score and PA for all three of Astra/GLM/K3.
- **Budget exhaustion is a real failure mode.** Fable 5 completed only 29 of 39 candidate-domain reports before the $120 assessment cap stopped it (its stated imputation scenario yields -13.18 overall Score, versus +21.96 for sibling Fable 5.1, which finished).
- **More questions improve mean and stability.** Drawing nested subsets from the frozen suites, the standard deviation of overall Score falls from 6.64/6.92/6.49 (Astra/GLM/K3 at 10 questions) to 2.14/0.96/1.14 at 90 questions, with means still ~1 full Score point below full-suite values — small suites both score worse and fluctuate more.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty is real.** The related-work positioning is precise: every cited predecessor fixes at least one of {target benchmark, evaluation protocol, budget, revision loop} — PostTrainBench and RSIBench-Data hand the agent a fixed evaluation, PACE uses a fixed proxy set, BenchBench compares rather than builds under budget. ERB's freeze-then-seal protocol is the first clean measurement of the dev-vs-sealed transfer gap for agent-designed evaluations, and the result (Fable 5.1 first on development, Grok 4.7 first on sealed, +5.27 Score) justifies the design.

**Rigor is good on the artifact side, thin on the episode side.** The scoring pipeline is deterministic, sanity-checked (constant score → Score=0/PA=50%), and contextualized with oracle floors and a 91.20% PA ceiling — genuinely careful metrology. But the researcher side rests on *one episode per configuration*: the headline gap between Astra (22.79) and Grok (22.59) is 0.20 Score points with no repeated-episode variance estimate, so the leaderboard ordering between adjacent researchers is not statistically supported. The paper's own subset analyses show within-artifact Score SDs of 2–7 points at small question counts, so episode-level noise is plausibly of the same order as the inter-researcher gaps.

**A harness confound goes unaddressed.** GPT-6 Astra and Grok 4.7 — the top two researchers — run on OpenAI Codex, while all seven others run on Claude Code. Model capability and harness affordances are never disentangled, so "Grok 4.7 designs better evaluations" could partly be "Codex supports better evaluation-research workflows."

**The metric is honest but harsh, and the paper says so.** Best overall Score is 22.79/100, which looks alarming until the appendix shows the reference panel itself is internally inconsistent: ProgramBench (Vals) and SWE-bench Multilingual reference means on the same six candidates are 0.0075 vs 0.876, putting a 0.303 oracle RMSE floor under coding regardless of evaluator quality. This is quantified rather than hidden, which is commendable — but it also means the Score leaderboard partly measures agreement with a mutually disagreeing panel, and cross-domain Score comparisons are not meaningful without the floors.

**Missing ablations.** There is no experiment varying the budgets (would $1,000 change the picture?), no second harness for the weaker researchers, and no test of whether the weight-concentration and truncation pathologies can be prevented by contract constraints rather than merely diagnosed. The Random120 baseline's strength (beating 4 of 8 agents on PA, and all agents on coding Score at 22.92) is reported honestly but left somewhat explanatory: the marginal value of agentic search over well-chosen quotas is demonstrated only for Astra's co-work (+36.3) and reasoning (+29.2) Score gains (Figure 3).

**Reproducibility.** Code and website are released, costs and caps are documented in detail, and the assessment is deterministic given a frozen artifact — but episodes depend on specific 2026 model versions behind APIs that will drift, and re-running all nine configurations costs roughly $300 in API spend plus harness maintenance.

**Red flags: minor.** GPT-6 Astra appears as both a researcher and a sealed candidate (no reference leakage by construction, but a small self-referential ecosystem); single-episode leaderboards risk over-reading; and the abstract's "about 75% of candidate pairs" framing sits more flatteringly against the 91.20% oracle than against the 68.29% trivially-achievable baseline.

## Strengths

- A crisp, budgeted problem formalization (3 h, $100+$100, $120/$30 caps) with a freeze protocol that cleanly separates designing an evaluation from running it.
- Two complementary metrics plus a full metrological context: constant-score sanity check, oracle RMSE floors, and a 91.20% PA ceiling from cross-target conflicts — numbers are never presented without their achievable bounds.
- Honest negative results: Random120 is a strong baseline; coding is weak for every agent; no evaluator dominates; the paper leads with "no evaluator leads in every domain."
- A failure-mode taxonomy backed by counterfactuals, not anecdotes: uniform re-weighting (Grok 22.18 → 37.03 at flat PA), component deletion (spreadsheet tasks: -31.94 Score / -12.57 pp PA), truncation counts (46/96, 140/192, 91/144), and budget-exhaustion accounting (29/39 reports).
- Complete cost transparency separating candidate generation from grading ($8.63 vs $109.41 execution at similar question counts), making cost-efficiency itself a measured axis.
- Deterministic, automatically checkable scoring — the benchmark does not depend on LLM judges for its headline metrics.

## Limitations

- One research episode per researcher configuration; no error bars or repeats, so adjacent leaderboard rankings (Astra 22.79 vs Grok 22.59 Score) are within plausible single-episode noise.
- Harness confound: the two leaders use Codex, the rest Claude Code; model vs harness attribution is impossible from the reported design.
- The reference panel is internally inconsistent (coding references with means 0.0075 vs 0.876 on shared candidates), so absolute Score values are low and cross-domain comparisons need appendix floors; evaluation is against public snapshots that may not match current benchmark versions.
- Findings are specific to this budget regime (3 h, ~$320 total per episode) and this candidate/target panel of 13 and 14; nothing establishes transfer to other domains (e.g., reward-model or open-ended rubric design) or larger budgets.
- The Fable 5 row in the main table mixes observed and imputed scenario scores (29/39 reports completed), which the paper separates carefully but a casual reader of Table 2 could miss.
- Cost figures are "standardized API-equivalent estimates" from metered usage or collaborator reports, not uniform billing — cross-lab cost comparisons carry that caveat.

## Relevance

该论文正中"自进化智能体与自动化研究"这一最高优先级方向：RSI 闭环的瓶颈正是"评价反馈从哪来、贵不贵、可不可信"，而 ERB 把这件事做成了可预算、可冻结、可独立审计的智能体任务，并给出"智能体尚且难以稳定超过简单人工抽样基线（PA 仅 4/8 超过 Random120）"的清醒量化结论，对评估任何自主研究系统的反馈回路可靠性都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would repeated episodes (different seeds, harness versions, or both) reorder the researcher leaderboard, given the 0.20-Score gap between Astra and Grok and the 2–7 point within-artifact SDs at moderate question counts?
- Can the 91.20% PA ceiling be raised by constructing target panels with compatible reference scales, or is cross-target disagreement intrinsic to multi-benchmark evaluation of the same model pool?
- Is the universal coding-domain weakness caused by the two incompatible coding references (oracle RMSE floor 0.303) or by task selection — and would dropping inconsistent targets change who leads?
- What budget scaling law governs evaluation-research quality — does a $1,000 or 24-hour budget let agents finally dominate Random120 on both Score and PA?
- Can the submission contract prevent weight concentration (93.87% in six items) and answer truncation (46/96 capped) without suppressing useful designs, and at what cost to Score?
- Do the design lessons (pilot-driven revision, source inspection, equal-weight aggregation) transfer to designing reward models or rubrics for open-ended tasks, where no frozen reference panel exists?
