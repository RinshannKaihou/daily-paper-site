A mechanistic diagnosis of why self-evolving LLM agents stagnate, plus a working parametric-RL cure — proven on real EDA-placement and GPU-kernel tasks.

Critical edge

The honest number is +1.33% on unseen cases: the 4 RL training cases are also the biggest-gain cases, and the reward is the metric being evaluated.

Plus — no code or prompts released, no variance/CI on any table, and Proposition 1 is a two-line bound verified on one hand-picked example.

Methodology — diagnose, quantify, fix

1

Diagnose the loop

In-context evolvers condition a frozen LLM on context built from its own outputs. When hallucinated content conflicts with the optimal algorithm A*, the probability of reaching A* drops — a self-reinforcing stagnation loop. Strip domain hints from the prompt and 0/16 generations are valid; normalized fitness oscillates or declines over 64 rollouts. A verbatim error taxonomy (H1 causal inversion … H5 fitness-polarity inversion) exposes the mechanism at chain-of-thought level.

2

Justify with a bound

Successors approach A* through short edits (d ≤ Δ). Proposition 1 — a two-line telescoping bound: training on Ai raises the probability of generating its short-edit neighbor by at least e^(ε_ℓ−2κΔ). Measured once, by hand, on adaptec1: κ=2.5, Δ=10, ε_ℓ≈51 → ρ_ℓ=1>0; the anchor's log-probability jumps −185.5 → −133.7 in one update. Read the critique ↓

3

Fix with PCPO

Global population: AST-hash dedup; semantic twins (Div_AST ≤ 0.15) kept only if ≥ 0.01 fitter; illegal programs filtered; per-instance buckets normalize mismatched reward scales; Top-k selection weighted by fitness and novelty. Hybrid update: refreshing on-policy GRPO every step; every T=2 steps an off-policy update pulls the policy toward curated elites via a Nonnegative Ranking Advantage, clipped importance ratios, and KL to a fixed reference.

Strengths

  • Explains why, not just that: a verbatim chain-of-thought error taxonomy for stagnation, replicated on a second model family (Gemma4-E4B).
  • Curation targets known replay confounds — reward-scale mismatch, near-duplicates, staleness — and ablations isolate it: PCPO 1.13 vs RePO 2.13 vs random replay 2.25.
  • Nonnegative Ranking Advantage: every verified elite keeps positive advantage, sparing valid-but-lower programs the negative feedback GRPO assigns when rewards are sparse and catastrophic.
  • Real payoffs, honest bookkeeping: two engineering domains, frontier baselines, 4× token savings; the paper itself flags Codex degrading below human on test cases.

Limitations

  • Small training sets, modest gains: 4 training cases per task; the honest +1.33% HPWL on 12 unseen cases is consistent but small — and no variance or CI is reported anywhere.
  • Theory verified once, by hand: ρ_ℓ > 0 is never measured along PCPO's own training trajectories.
  • Narrow task: GP means designing one function (an LR schedule); larger edit distances multiply Δ by 2κ and could flip the bound's condition negative.
  • Non-standard kernel protocol: 4 Level-1 cases, custom continuous speedup, frozen single-profile Eager baseline — not comparable to published KernelBench results.
  • Reproducibility gaps: no code, prompts, or generated programs released; T=2, α=β=1.0, δ_max=0.15 fixed without any sensitivity analysis.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does ρ_ℓ = ε_ℓ − 2κΔ > 0 hold along real PCPO trajectories, or fail wherever successors need edits larger than Δ ≈ 10 tokens?
  2. Do frontier models stagnate the same way? The H1–H5 taxonomy came from Qwen3-8B traces on one chip case — is stagnation a small-model pathology?
  3. Does PCPO scale beyond single-function design to multi-file or multi-objective spaces, where per-instance bucketing and reward normalization break down?
  4. Does internalized knowledge survive domain shift — new chips with different overflow targets — or overfit the 4 training reward landscapes?
  5. Could curated populations replace replay buffers in general RLVR (math/code RL), or does the benefit depend on executable, verifiable rewards?
