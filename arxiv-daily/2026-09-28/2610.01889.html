M-01

Methodology

3 moves
01

Emulation — VPSR in PRISM

Recovers the exact result via TwoSum/TwoProdFMA, truncates to a virtual t-bit grid, then samples a uniform threshold on [0, ulpt) and rounds away stochastically. Theorem 1 (Sterbenz) proves the decision keeps its exact sign in hardware arithmetic — no extended precision. Verificarlo rewrites PyTorch ops into branch-free SIMD kernels; precision and rounding are runtime settings. Overhead ×8.5–32.1 native.

02

Theory — bound + decomposition

A vector Bienaymé–Chebyshev bound applied to E‖e‖₂² bypasses the union bound (≃680u inflation at m=768). The cross-entropy change splits exactly into signed drift D, drift-curvature Q, and a Fisher-information-weighted variance penalty V.

03

Experiments — site-resolved sweep

DistilGPT-2 (6 blocks, d=768, d𝜋=3072, vocab 50,257), pretrained, no fine-tuning or calibration; WikiText-2 → 1020 scored positions; t ∈ [4,14] applied at one scope at a time; 5 seeds per config with 95% Student-t CIs, 32 seeds for the decomposition.

benchmark: 65,536-element ops · 30 runs · Xeon Gold 6130 — up to ×70.5 faster than Verificarlo MCA RR (binary64 FMA), timing nearly independent of t.
R-02

Why the flip — the D/Q/V decomposition

excess loss, nats

MLP down-projection → SR free

QRN = 0.29 vs QSR = 0.017
VSR = 0.083 nats — 50× smaller than at the head

MLP-injected noise reaches the logits as a near-uniform vocabulary shift that the softmax ignores by shift invariance; RN’s non-uniform drift is what gets punished by curvature Q.

LM head → SR taxed

VSR = 100–105% of SR’s excess loss
@ t=6: SR excess 4.40 vs RN 2.12 nats (QRN=1.58, RRN=0.50)

Across a 50,257-way vocabulary, SR’s fluctuation is genuinely non-uniform — charged directly by cross-entropy through the Fisher-weighted penalty V.

This is a mechanism, not a curve fit: the same quantity the theory decomposes (Eq. 9) is what the 32-seed experiments measure, and the numbers confirm it quantitatively. Sharpest insight: SR’s noise can manifest as a softmax-invisible uniform shift.

Q-01

Questions to carry forward

  • Real hardware formats: does the site reversal survive FP8/E5M2 and NVFP4 microscaling, where exponent range and per-block scaling shape the error?
  • Scale: does Q-vs-V predict site preferences at 7B+ and other architectures — or is GELU-output positivity at mlp_c_proj doing the explanatory work?
  • Cheap assignment: can D/Q/V be computed at calibration time to derive a per-model rounding recipe instead of sweeping?
  • Interaction: are the gains additive with LLM.int8 / SmoothQuant-style outlier handling, or subsumed by it?
  • Audit cost: is SR’s bitwise irreproducibility a liability for silent-data-corruption auditing in serving deployments?
S-01

Strengths

  • Theory and measurement lock together — Vₛₖ at 100–105% of head excess loss confirms the mechanism quantitatively, not just illustratively.
  • Unglamorous correctness done right — Theorem 1 fully proved, including binade crossings and subnormal virtual grids.
  • Statistically careful — 5 seeds + Student-t CIs per config, 32 seeds for the decomposition, jackknife SEs, significance labels on every ordering claim.
  • Exceptional reproducibility — public code, scripts, and archived data (GitHub + Zenodo); runtime-configurable precision; explicit AI-usage disclosure.
  • Intellectually honest limitations — the authors flag the exponent blind spot and confounds themselves.
L-01

Limitations

  • Thin external validity — one 82M model, one 1020-position WikiText-2 slice; no larger model, no other architecture, no task beyond perplexity.
  • Not a hardware format — binary32 exponent range kept, so FP8/NVFP4-style exponent restrictions and scaling are unmodeled.
  • Confounded mechanism sweep — only one site has n=3072, and it also has a distinctive GELU-output distribution; reduction length per se is not isolated.
  • A measurement instrument, not a deployment path — ×8.5–32.1 emulation cost, no GPU port yet.
  • Narrow mixed-recipe evidence — three k values on one model; no claim of a universal rounding assignment.
