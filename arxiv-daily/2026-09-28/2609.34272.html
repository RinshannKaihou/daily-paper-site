Why This Matters

Fused low-precision kernels can silently corrupt pretraining — no NaN, no overflow, normal-looking loss for weeks. This paper supplies both a diagnosis playbook and a near-free repair.

无 NaN、无 overflow 的静默失败：「冻结前向 + 分层重算反向」的定位流程与守恒律审计，对所有大规模预训练团队的 kernel 级监控都有直接参考价值。

Critical Edge — read with care

The failure regime is partly self-selected: QK-normalization and softcapping were disabled, and no QK-norm-enabled run shows whether production models ever enter this one-hot regime.

Also best-case: the +4.7% overhead is measured at 4k context on a 450M model, and grows with attention’s share of the step. FA3-SBS still finished with slightly better loss (1.649 vs 1.665) — GProj wins on stability and gradient fidelity, not final loss.

01Methodology

Diagnose → explain → fix, on a 450M transformer / 50B-token pretraining stack (16×H200, Muon).

  1. Localize. Bitwise-freeze the forward at 33.6B tokens; recompute only layers 5 & 11’s attention backward in FP32 → full gradient norm drops >100×. The fault lives in the kernel’s backward, not the data or hyperparameters.
  2. Explain. The exact softmax-score gradient sums to zero per row (gauge symmetry over keys). FA3 rounds it to BF16 before contracting with keys; the residual row mass ρ leaks αρμₖ along the attention-weighted mean key. When attention turns one-hot, the true gradient ≈ 0 while the leak is unchanged — relative error is provably unbounded (Thm 4.2), and no forward-side repair can remove it.
  3. Fix — GProj. Project the BF16 operand t ↦ t − λr with λ = ρ/m: two rank-one corrections per row. dQ folds into the existing backward pass; dK needs one extra pass. FP32 accumulators, no extra forward state, dV untouched.
  4. Evaluate. 8 captured failing inputs vs FP64 · 60-case synthetic suite · five matched 50B-token from-scratch runs (same seed, same data order) · full-step timing · 102M plain-transformer control.

  • Synthetic suite (58 nonzero-target cases): GProj’s worst-case dQ error is 2.49% vs FA3-SBS’s 4794% — a ~200× reduction; passes all 60 cases.
  • QY-shift stalls from the first few billion tokens (its shift rule underflows in BF16) and is less accurate than unmodified FA3 on the captured inputs.
  • Localization precision: a one-line saved-output model predicts the local dQ error with cosine 0.9999 — the forward saved output explains essentially the whole local error.

03Strengths

  • Exemplar diagnosis for a silent fault with no smoking gun: bitwise-frozen forward → layer-wise FP32 recompute → saved-output channel → cast channel.
  • Conservation-law framing predicts which error matters (row mass) and when (one-hot attention), plus a theorem for why all forward-side repairs fail.
  • Nearly free fix: +4.73% step time, zero memory growth, FP32-parity accuracy; real SASS-level kernel craft.
  • Unusually fair baselines: the QY-shift port is verified bitwise against the original authors’ code before being criticized.
  • Converging evidence: captured-input accuracy, 60-case suite, five matched 50B-token runs, regime audits, 102M control.

04Limitations

  • Single 450M model (102M control); no evidence at multi-billion-parameter scale, where attention’s share of the step differs.
  • Narrow coverage: BF16 FA3 on Hopper, head dim 64, dense causal only; no FP8; dV left unrepaired (cuDNN / PyTorch SDPA share the defect but GProj targets FA3).
  • QK-norm / softcapping disabled — production configs may never enter the one-hot regime; no QK-norm arm was run.
  • Best-case overhead: +4.7% quoted at 4k context; grows with attention’s share. dK pass needs full-row statistics, so porting to other schedules is nontrivial.
  • Loss nuance: FA3-SBS finished slightly better (1.649 vs 1.665) — the headline win is stability and gradient fidelity, not final-loss superiority. Single-seed on the headline architecture.

05Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does QK-normalization or softcapping suppress the one-hot high-logit regime entirely, reducing GProj’s benefit to the bounded 0.1–0.3% error floor?
  2. How does the +4.7% overhead translate to production scale — tens of billions of parameters, 32k+ context — where attention dominates the step? Does the second dK pass survive FlashAttention-4 / FP8 schedules?
  3. cuDNN and PyTorch SDPA share the backward defect at fixed operands: does a GProj-style projection generalize to their tiling schemes?
  4. Would a generic “conservation-law audit” (checking Σ tⱼ = 0 on operands the kernel multiplies) catch broken invariants in other fused kernels — RoPE, cross-entropy?
  5. Given FA3-SBS’s slightly better final loss (1.649 vs 1.665) despite worse gradient fidelity, is final loss alone the right yardstick — would a downstream capability evaluation separate the arms?
