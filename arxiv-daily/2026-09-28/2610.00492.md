> **一句话总结：** CMU 联合 Stanford/Yale/MIT/Princeton/Flatiron 等机构与 10 位领域科学家共建 EurekaBench——6 个学科（神经科学、地球物理、等离子体物理、天体物理、计算机、化学）26 个长周期真实科研任务、306 道专家验证的"科学洞见问题"，用"科学约束 / 预测精度 / 科学洞见"三轴评测 7 个前沿 Agent：最强的 Claude Fable 5.1 条件分仅 29.8%（人类基线 63.7%），GPT 6 Astra 预测精度几乎追平人类（47.4% vs 48.8%）但洞见得分只有 29.4%（人类 69.7%），且除它之外所有 Agent 在过半任务上连领域常识约束测试都通不过——证明"把预测精度刷高"并不等于"能做出科学发现"。

> **TL;DR:** EurekaBench is a cross-domain benchmark (26 long-horizon tasks across neuroscience, geophysics, plasma physics, astrophysics, CS, and chemistry, with 306 expert-verified scientific-insight questions) that evaluates AI scientist agents along three axes: scientific-constraint tests, predictive accuracy (PA) of the executable mechanism on held-out data, and scientific insights (SI) derivable from the mechanism, scored by two independent agentic judges (Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT 5.6 Sol). Across 7 frontier agents, the best conditioned score is 29.8% (Claude Fable 5.1) vs 63.7% for human-discovered mechanisms. The dissociation is the headline: GPT 6 Astra nearly matches human PA (47.4% vs 48.8%) yet reaches only 29.4% SI vs the human 69.7% — and 6 of 7 agents fail the common-sense constraint tests on more than half of the 26 problems. Explicitly handing agents the insight questions lifts the conditioned score from 27.3 to 60.3 on one neuroscience task but does not transfer (52.8 to 51.9) on the ARC tokamak plasma task, showing agents do not yet know what insights to seek on their own.

## 核心贡献

- **首个把"科学洞见发现"系统化操作化的基准**：与 10 位领域专家深度合作（另咨询 15 位科学家），把 6 个学科 26 个来自真实研究、挑战现有认知的初始观测转化为 Agent 任务，并为每个任务编写专家验证的洞见问题（共 306 道），衡量发现的机制"能解释什么"，而不只看"预测得多准"。
- **三轴评测框架**：(1) 科学约束 SC——机制是否满足领域常识假设（全过才得分）；(2) 预测精度 PA——在held-out 数据上实际执行 `mechanism.py` 打分；(3) 科学洞见 SI——由两个独立 Agent 裁判（Claude Opus 4.8、GPT 5.6 Sol）判定 306 个洞见问题能否从机制推出；三者合成"条件分"（SC 全过才有分）。
- **核心实证发现——准确率≠洞见**：GPT 6 Astra 的 PA 达 47.4%，几乎追平人类 48.8%，但 SI 仅 29.4% vs 人类 69.7%；条件分最好的 Claude Fable 5.1 也只有 29.8% vs 人类 63.7%；Kendall τb=0.20 说明 PA 排名与 SI 排名仅弱相关（39.9% 的配对出现"PA 更高但 SI 更低"的逆转）。
- **失败模式分类学**：专家逐例审阅实验日志，归纳出四类系统性失败——过度依赖预设假设、把"任务优化"当"机制发现"、不用实验消除不确定性、观测到现象就停止探索；同时记录了拒绝错误假设等积极行为。
- **引导消融**：给 Agent 一般性"目标提示"（+goal）或在任务说明中直接给出洞见问题（+insights），在神经动力学任务上把条件分从 27.3 拉到 37.4 / 60.3，但在 ARC 托卡马克任务上 +insights 反而 52.8→51.9——说明 Agent 脱离显式提示后并不知道该找什么洞见。

## Key Contributions

- **EurekaBench**: the first benchmark to operationalize open-ended scientific insight discovery, co-built with 10 domain experts (15 more consulted): 26 long-horizon problems from real research across 6 domains, each packaged as a runnable simulator environment (Aptainer images + CLI: c302/TRIBE v2, FenICSx/HBV, TORAX/TokaMaker+GPEC, CAMELS/DREAMS, open-weight LMs, AIMNet2-rxn) with 306 expert-verified insight questions.
- **A three-axis evaluation protocol** — scientific constraints (SC, binary common-sense tests, all-must-pass), predictive accuracy (PA, executing the submitted `mechanism.py` on held-out test data), and scientific insights (SI, fraction of insight questions derivable from the mechanism, judged by two independent agentic judges allowed to query the environment) — combined into a "conditioned score" that is zeroed unless all SC tests pass.
- **The central empirical finding that accuracy does not imply insight**: GPT 6 Astra attains 47.4% PA vs the human 48.8%, but only 29.4% SI vs the human 69.7%; the best agent overall (Claude Fable 5.1) reaches 29.8% conditioned vs 63.7% for human-discovered mechanisms, and 6 of 7 agents fail SC tests on ≥15 of 26 problems.
- **An expert-validated failure taxonomy** with verbatim log evidence: overreliance on preselected hypotheses, treating task optimization as mechanism discovery (curve fitting), not using experiments to resolve uncertainty, and stopping at observations without further exploration.
- **Guidance ablations** showing that explicit insight questions (+insights) raise the conditioned score by +33.0 on neural dynamics but −0.9 on ARC tokamak, plus a mechanism-vs-experimentation analysis: Claude Fable 5.1 derives 42.4% of insights from its submitted mechanism but only 22.7% during the experimentation loop itself.

## Methodology

**Task formulation.** Each task is a tuple T = (x, o₁, ℰ, R): a task description x with scientific background, initial observations o₁ that challenge existing understanding, an environment interface ℰ (a containerized simulator with CLI), and an evaluation rubric R. The agent runs an iterative experimentation loop (query a_t → observe o_{t+1} → revise hypotheses) under a budget of one H100 GPU, 1,000 iterations, and 4 hours per problem; web access to the source publications and literature aggregators is blocked. It submits three artifacts (≤1 GB total): `mechanism.md` (causal explanation), `mechanism.py` (executable implementation), and `experiments.log`.

**Three-axis scoring.** (1) SC: n_SC binary domain common-sense constraints; the mechanism passes only if all hold. (2) PA: `mechanism.py` is executed on held-out test data; each criterion yields a continuous score s_i ∈ [0,1] normalized per the task rubric, S_PA = p_PA / n_PA. (3) SI: n_SI expert-written yes/no insight questions (e.g., the 9 questions for the Antarctic ice-shelf flow-law task probe compression-vs-extension viscosity, anisotropy, and identifiability); a judge answers YES only with complete evidence derivable from M. The conditioned score S_conditioned = (p_PA + p_SI) if b_SC = n_SC, else 0, averaged over two judges.

**Agentic judges.** Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT 5.6 Sol act as independent judges for SC and SI; for SI they may run additional experiments via ℰ to check whether each insight follows from the mechanism, and must not modify it. Insight questions are drawn from the source studies' open questions, insights found by human scientists, and expert proposals — all verified by the domain experts.

**Models and harnesses.** Seven agents at maximum reasoning effort: Claude Fable 5.1 / Opus 5 / Opus 4.8 (Claude Code harness), GPT 6 Astra / GPT 5.6 Sol (Codex harness), DeepSeek V4 Flash and Kimi K3 (OpenHands Agent SDK). The human baseline is the current state of progress achieved by human scientists on each problem.

**Analysis.** Beyond the main table, the paper runs guidance ablations (+goal prompt vs +insight questions) on two problems, re-scores insights from the experimentation loop without simulator access to separate experiment-derived from mechanism-derived insights, computes Kendall τb between PA and SI rankings (20,000 bootstrap samples, 100,000 permutations), and has domain experts qualitatively review mechanism submissions and logs to build the failure taxonomy.

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline gap (Table 2, 26 problems):** the best agent, Claude Fable 5.1, achieves a conditioned score of **29.8%** vs **63.7%** for human-discovered mechanisms (−33.9 pts); the spread across the seven agents is 12.9% (Kimi K3) to 29.8%.
- **Accuracy does not buy insight:** GPT 6 Astra reaches **47.4% PA vs the human 48.8%** (−1.4 pts) but only **29.4% SI vs 69.7%** (−40.3 pts). Claude Fable 5.1 has the same 47.4% PA with a higher 42.4% SI — still 27.3 pts below human.
- **Agents fail basic common-sense constraints:** every agent except GPT 6 Astra fails the SC tests on more than half of the problems — Claude Fable 5.1: **15/26**, Claude Opus 5: 15/26, Claude Opus 4.8: 16/26, GPT 5.6 Sol: **17/26**, DeepSeek V4 Flash: 15/26, Kimi K3: 16/26 — vs **0/26** for human solutions.
- **Ranking dissociation (Appendix D.2):** PA and SI rankings correlate only weakly (Kendall **τb = 0.20**, 95% CI [0.0, 0.32], p = 0.008, Holm-adjusted); the agent with higher PA scores lower SI in **39.9%** of within-problem comparisons.
- **Guidance ablation (Figure 6, Claude Fable 5.1):** adding a general "+goal" prompt lifts the conditioned score **27.3 → 37.4** on neural dynamics and **52.8 → 58.2** on ARC tokamak; explicitly injecting the insight questions ("+insights") gives **27.3 → 60.3 (Δ +33.0)** on neural dynamics but **52.8 → 51.9 (Δ −0.9)** on ARC tokamak — outside a natural discovery setting, agents do not know what insights to look for.
- **Insights come from extrapolation, not experimentation (Figure 7):** when the judge evaluates the mechanism without simulator access, Claude Fable 5.1 earns **22.7%** SI from its experimentation record vs **42.4%** from the submitted mechanism (the largest gap among agents); in 6.7% of cases judges credit insights found in experiment logs that cannot be derived from the mechanism at all.
- **Domain texture (Appendix D.1):** in astrophysics the three Claude and two GPT agents reach domain-average PA of **67.1–69.8%** while their SI spans **33.6–63.4%** — near-identical predictors differ widely in explanatory value.
- **Cost:** a full run costs **$23.96** for Claude Fable 5.1 (104 calls) and $24.62 for GPT 6 Astra, vs **$1.24** for DeepSeek V4 Flash (206 calls) and $2.23 for GPT 5.6 Sol.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty — genuine, with a caveat.** The three-axis design is a real departure from prior agentic-science benchmarks (MLE-Bench, Terminal-Bench-Science, DiscoveryBench, DiscoverPhysics), which the authors correctly note center on task completion, replication gains, or final answers. Scoring whether an executable mechanism *explains* observations via 306 expert-written derivability questions is new and well operationalized. The caveat: "insight" is operationalized as *expert-anticipated* insight — an agent that discovers a valid alternative mechanism gets zero SI credit, a failure mode the authors themselves flag in the conclusion but which quietly caps the benchmark's construct validity for genuinely novel discovery.

**Judge dependence is the weakest structural link.** SI and SC verdicts are LLM-judged, and one of the two judges (Claude Opus 4.8) is the same model family as three of the seven judged agents — a self-preference risk that is not tested with human-annotated SI subsets in the main text. The authors' own analysis undercuts judge reliability: they report that qualitative expert review suggests judges are "overly generous," that in 6.7% of cases judges credit insights from logs that the mechanism does not support, and that on ARC tokamak the judges ranked Claude Fable 5.1's submission above all others while domain experts judged it closer to a master's student's work than a researcher's. Publishing no judge-human agreement number for SI verdicts is a notable omission for a paper whose headline deltas rest on those verdicts.

**Statistical fragility at n=26.** The conditioned score zeroes out an entire submission when any one SC test fails, so with 15/26 SC failures Claude Fable 5.1's 29.8% is an average dominated by zeros; per-agent-per-problem results appear to come from a single run, and long-horizon agent runs are known to have high run-to-run variance (the excluded Kimi K3 run on catchment properties — marked with gray dashes in Figure 9 — hints that noisy runs occurred). No error bars or repeated-run variance are reported in the main text, so gaps like Opus 5 (27.3%) vs GPT 6 Astra (28.7%) are not interpretable.

**Baseline and contamination asymmetries.** The "human scientist" baseline is the published state of progress produced by the very community from which the tasks and insight questions were derived — experts with months of context, compared against agents given 4 hours. The authors honestly acknowledge that blocking web access "does not rule out exposure to the source research during pretraining," which is a serious concern for benchmarks built from recent published findings: SI could partially measure memorization of the source papers' known conclusions.

**Internal inconsistency and confounds.** The text claims +insights "substantially increases the SI contribution" on both ablation problems (Δ +32.5 and Δ +15.8), but Figure 6's ARC tokamak totals read 52.8 → 51.9 (Δ −0.9) — the text and figure are hard to reconcile as printed. Additionally, model and harness are confounded (Claude models on Claude Code, GPT models on Codex, open-weight models on OpenHands), so cross-family rankings blend model and scaffolding effects; and the domain distribution is skewed (10 neuroscience vs 1 chemistry task), making cross-domain aggregate claims unevenly weighted.

## Strengths

- Real mechanism discovery, not paper exercises: mechanisms are scored by *executing* `mechanism.py` on held-out data, and environments are actual simulators (Tokamak transport codes, C. elegans whole-animal models, CAMELS cosmological simulations) packaged for reproduction.
- The PA/SI dissociation is a clean, memorable, and well-instrumented finding — the Figure 7 mechanism-vs-experimentation split (42.4 vs 22.7 for the best agent) is a genuinely novel diagnostic of *where* insight comes from.
- Expert involvement is substantive, not decorative: 306 verified insight questions, expert-written SC criteria, and expert qualitative review of failure logs with verbatim quotes (Figure 8 and Appendix D.3).
- Honest, detailed failure taxonomy (overreliance on preselected hypotheses, curve-fitting-as-discovery, unresolved uncertainty, premature stopping) that gives follow-up work concrete targets, including for RL training environments.
- Transparent protocol artifacts in the appendix (task-spec template, exact judge prompts with JSON verdict formats) plus public code and website.

## Limitations

- Only 26 problems with a skewed domain mix (10 neuroscience, 1 chemistry), each run once per agent — statistical power for agent rankings is thin and no variance estimates are given.
- SI/SC scoring depends entirely on LLM judges, with acknowledged over-generosity and no reported human-judge agreement; the Claude-family judge also judged Claude-family agents.
- The human baseline (published expert solutions) is not time- or resource-matched to the agents' 4-hour budget.
- Pretraining contamination from the source publications is possible and unquantified.
- The insight rubric only credits expert-anticipated insights, so genuinely novel discoveries score zero by construction.
- The +insights ablation is run on only two problems, and its ARC tokamak result (a slight decrease) contradicts the text's uniform-improvement narrative.
- The mechanism-vs-harness confound (vendor harnesses paired with vendor models) blurs attribution of the observed gaps.

## Relevance

这篇论文正中你对 Self-Evolving Agents & Automated Research 的最高优先级方向：它给出了"AI 科学家能否真正做出科学发现"的首个系统化基准，直接量化了自主研究闭环中"优化目标达成"与"产生可解释洞见"之间的脱节，其四类失败模式（预设假设依赖、曲线拟合当机制、不用实验消除不确定性、过早停止）对设计可靠的长时间自主 Agent 系统和其评测奖励信号都有直接指导意义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would human-expert agreement with the LLM judges' SI verdicts be high enough to support the headline conclusions, especially for the Claude-family-judged Claude-family agents?
- Do the PA/SI gaps persist under multiple independent runs per agent, and how much of the SC failure rate (15–17 of 26) is run-level noise rather than systematic incapability?
- Can a reward signal derived from insight-derivation (as the authors propose for RL) be computed without privileged access to the expert insight rubric, i.e., can insight-seeking be trained rather than prompted?
- How much of agents' PA comes from fitting simulator quirks versus actual physics — would mechanisms discovered on EurekaBench transfer to out-of-distribution operating points of the same simulators?
- Is the near-zero +insights effect on ARC tokamak (52.8 → 51.9) evidence of a domain-specific ceiling (plasma transport may resist question-guided exploration), or of rubric coverage limits that penalize alternative discovery paths?
- Would results change if agents were allowed retrieval of *adjacent* literature (not the source paper), mimicking how human scientists actually build on prior work?
