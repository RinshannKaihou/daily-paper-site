Critical Edge Read the fine print

The 0% headline has scope conditions: contract fidelity is assumed, never measured — and attacker-controlled content inside authorized actions (A_auth) sits explicitly outside the guarantee, where many real IPI attacks live.

01

Methodology

contract → two gates → commit

APEX moves the checkpoint to the execution boundary — the single point where agent state becomes an external action — and decomposes boundary risk into exactly two checkable conditions: A_unfr unauthorized effects, and A_unfr→A_auth authorized effects that adopt undersourced information.

Before any untrusted content exists, the trusted task plus a one-time capability registry (Tools / MCP / Skills: schemas, effect boundaries, untrusted surfaces) are compiled into a frozen DAG authorization contract declaring which effects are allowed and how their arguments may legitimately arise. One registration covers Tools, MCP, Skills and their nested compositions — no per-attack detectors, no handwritten policies, no end-to-end taint labeling.

At runtime, WRAP handles unauthorized effects: it back-traces each boundary proposal to contract clauses and forward-resolves every argument from capability-call receipts, rejecting anything whose authorization cannot be fully reconstructed — before commitment, not detection after the fact. PLANT handles undersourced adoption: probes carrying per-episode unforgeable tokens are planted on channels no endorsed path can traverse, so carrying one to an effect is logically conclusive evidence of injection. Rejections feed Safe Continuation — repair, replan, or abort — preserving utility without expanding authority.

Overhead

03

Strengths / Limitations

side by side

Strengths

  • Carrier-agnostic invariant: one enforcement point covers Tools, MCP, Skills — including SCR-Bench attacks on combinations of benign capabilities that single-carrier defenses structurally miss.
  • Rejection before commitment, with WRAP's soundness argument formalized — every unauthorized execution produces a proposal it rejects. Proof-shaped, not vibes.
  • PLANT is an elegant honeypot inversion: probes derive from the contract's own endorsed-path analysis, so a hit is logically conclusive evidence of undersourced adoption.
  • Unusually honest discussion: contract fidelity, delegated authority, probe coverage, and long-horizon compilation limits are all named explicitly.
  • Best-in-class evaluation breadth: 6 benchmarks, 13 baselines on one shared backbone, 3 adaptive attacks, ablations, overhead and multi-backbone checks; code is public.

Limitations

  • Guarantee is conditional on contract fidelity — and no compilation-quality metric is reported. Ambiguous requests yield over-restrictive contracts that block legitimate work.
  • A_auth residue: when the task authorizes the action but the attacker controls content within it (e.g. which email body to send), APEX does not help — real-world IPI often targets exactly this.
  • Delegated-authority tasks ("execute the TODOs in this email") cannot be expressed in a fixed pre-execution contract; controlled reauthorization is future work.
  • Benign-utility tax on AgentDojo: BU 86.60 vs Spotlighting 94.85 — high, not best. Bypass channels outside registered interfaces are out of scope; PLANT abstains where probes would alter behavior.
  • Adaptive evaluation is not probe-aware; a probe-detecting adversary routing around instrumented surfaces remains an open caveat — and long-horizon faithful compilation has no benchmark yet.
Verdict

A strong, unusually rigorous systems-security paper: the framing does heavy lifting — pre-declared authorization, provenance, and honeypots all existed; the synthesis into two boundary conditions plus WRAP/PLANT is what is genuinely new. The overclaim risk is reading "0% ASR on six benchmarks" as "IPI solved" — the boundary framing restricts scope precisely where many real attacks live.

Questions for Further Exploration

open threads
How often does LLM contract compilation over- or under-restrict real tasks — can compilation fidelity be measured against human-stated intent rather than assumed?
Can PLANT's probe channels survive a probe-aware adversary that detects the synthetic token and routes around instrumented surfaces — is there a joint WRAP+PLANT evasion?
What would "controlled reauthorization" look like for tasks that legitimately delegate decisions to external content, without letting the injection grant itself authority?
Does the boundary check stay sound with a weaker, more injection-susceptible task agent — or with capability units registered dynamically from open-ended MCP catalogs?
Can the framework extend to long-horizon conditional objectives (multi-day research loops) compiled from far less explicit instructions — and what benchmark would test that?
The 0.56% residual on MSB: what exactly slips through — a gap in WRAP's script-enforced resolution, or in PLANT's probe placement?
