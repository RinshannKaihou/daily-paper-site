> **一句话总结：** 作者以水波散射的"hydrotope"公式为可精确验证的受控试验台，重建原始人机协作发现过程并做18次单代理重发现实验，发现单代理极易得到各"频率腔"内的局部多项式（15/18次）却几乎总是无法完成全局腔边界组合与跨腔检验（仅4/18成功，且全部来自真提示条件），而常规符号回归（Operon 最好，n=5 时中位 MAE 1.36）与 LLM 辅助符号回归（LaSR、LLM-SR）也都无法恢复精确公式；据此诊断设计的"PI+两名学生"多代理工作流在无提示条件下两次（Opus 团队 2 轮、Codex 5.5 团队 3 轮）自动重发现完整公式，并在更难的三负波数扇区给出了一个此前未知、经 140/140 次精确检验（58 个腔）的六点振幅 A6 新解析表达式。

> **TL;DR:** A controlled case study of agentic scientific discovery on the "hydrotope" — the all-n scattering amplitude of the two-minus water-wave sector, which is piecewise polynomial across "frequency chambers" and exactly checkable via Berends–Giele (BG) recursion. Across 18 single-prompt runs (6 agent configurations × 3 prompt packages), 15/18 runs find a correct principal-chamber formula (A_n ∝ ω1ω2 min(ω1²,ω2²)^{n−3}) but only 4/18 recover the complete alternating-subset-sum formula — all 4 under the true-hint prompt — while every fitted baseline fails: Operon reaches the lowest median held-out MAE (1.36 at n=5) among SR systems, KAN gets 2.91 at n=5 and 927.04 at n=7, and none is exact, whereas the agent team's entry has MAE 0. Diagnosing these failure modes (combining chamber polynomials, testing in unseen chambers) motivated a PI + two-student workflow that rediscovers the complete formula without hints in 2/2 team runs (142 exact matches incl. 95 points outside the principal chamber in round 2), and produces the paper's headline new result: a previously unknown analytic six-point three-minus amplitude A6 that passes 140/140 exact BG tests across 58 chambers.

## 核心贡献

- **受控的代理科学发现试验台**：提出以 hydrotope（两负波数扇区水波振幅 Aₙ 的全局闭式公式，几何上是截断频率盒的体积）作为测试用例——任何提议公式都可用 Berends–Giele 递推精确验证，且任务结构（局部多项式易得、跨"频率腔"全局组合难）能精细区分"发现局部模式"与"完成全局综合"两种能力。
- **18 次单代理重发现实验的失败模式刻画**：6 种代理配置 × 3 种提示包（无提示/假提示/真提示），按 5 类结果与 8 个关键步骤（K1–K8）分类；发现瓶颈不在"找到腔内公式"（15/18）而在"找全腔边界（8/18）、组合成交替子集和（4/18）、在新腔中检验"。总结出三种典型停止原因：组合尝试失败后退回受限答案、只在构造公式所用的熟悉配置上测试、继续追随已被数值证伪的提示。
- **基线对照**：PySR、gplearn、Operon、LaSR、LLM-SR（GPT-5.6-Terra 驱动）及线性回归/随机森林/MLP/KAN 在固定数据集上均无法恢复精确公式——低平均误差可以靠"搞错腔边界或整腔遗漏"获得，预测精度不等于公式发现。
- **PI + 两学生的多代理工作流**：PI 分配互补的解析/数值任务、两名学生独立推导并在共享记录上留痕、PI 用独立实现的代码跨腔检验最终公式；无提示条件下两次运行（Claude Opus 4.8 团队 2 轮、Codex 5.5 团队 3 轮）均自动重发现完整公式，而平行的 6 次单代理无提示运行全部失败。
- **新的科学结果**：将同一工作流应用于更难的三负波数扇区，给出此前未知的六点三负振幅 A6 解析表达式（分母 e₃⁻+e₃⁺，分子为群轨道和形式的 N₆，deg N₆=11），经 140/140 次跨 58 个腔的精确 BG 检验，另有 PI 用独立编写的精确分数运算代码做全项重标检验；并给出四种团队架构（单代理 / PI+1 学生 / PI+2 学生 / PI+2 学生+验证者）在 A6 上的对比。
- **开放工件**：提示词、全部运行历史、检验代码与基准数据集开源于 github.com/zihanzhou26/hydrotope_benchmark。

## Key Contributions

- A controlled, exactly-verifiable testbed for agentic scientific discovery: the hydrotope formula A_n = i2^{n−1}ω1ω2 Σ_{S⊆{3..n}} (−1)^{|S|}(β²−Σ_{j∈S}ω_j²)₊^{n−3} (β = min(|ω1|,|ω2|)), where different max(x,0)-polynomial pieces hold in different "frequency chambers" bounded by Σ_{j∈S}ω_j² = β², so a correct solution must find every boundary, combine chamber polynomials, and test the combination.
- A failure-mode analysis of 18 single-prompt rediscovery runs (6 agent configurations × no-hint / false-hint / true-hint): local discovery is nearly universal (15/18 produce the principal-chamber formula; 8/18 find every boundary), while global completion is the bottleneck — only 4/18 reach the complete alternating subset sum, all under the true-hint condition, with three recurring stopping mechanisms.
- Baseline evidence that approximate prediction ≠ formula discovery: conventional SR (PySR, gplearn, Operon), LLM-assisted SR (LaSR, LLM-SR), region-first/region-split variants, and numerical ML (linear regression, random forest, MLP, KAN) all retain nonzero held-out error and none recovers the exact multi-chamber formula at n = 5 or n = 7.
- A PI + two-student multi-agent workflow that separates formula construction from cross-chamber checking, which rediscovers the complete two-minus formula without hints in both runs where 0/6 comparable single-agent runs succeed, and yields the paper's genuinely new physics result: the six-point three-minus amplitude A6, verified by 140/140 exact BG recursion tests in 58 chambers (finite tests; no analytic proof yet, n ≥ 7 numerator left open).
- A four-architecture comparison on A6 (single agent, PI+1 student, PI+2 students, PI+2 students+verifier) showing that only teams with two complementary student roles carried both formula construction and cross-chamber testing to a complete formula.
- Full release of prompts, run histories, checking code, and benchmark datasets (github.com/zihanzhou26/hydrotope_benchmark).

## Methodology

**Testbed.** The task is to find a closed-form formula for the n-point amplitude A_n in the two-minus sector of one-dimensional deep-water surface-wave scattering (σ = (−1,−1,+1,...,+1), n ≥ 4), valid for every n and throughout the allowed frequency space. The exact ground truth (Eq. 3) is a chamber-dependent alternating subset sum of max(x,0)-powered polynomials with chamber boundaries at Σ_{j∈S}ω_j² = β²; geometrically it is the volume of the "hydrotope", a slice of a frequency-dependent box. Agents receive only a prompt and the Mathematica file `0nShe11BG.m`, which evaluates any proposed configuration exactly via Berends–Giele recursion — the "propose-test-revise" oracle.

**Single-agent protocol.** Three prompt packages vary both the suggested equation class and the recommended tests: false hint (a single global ratio-of-polynomials, with sampling advice steering away from chamber boundaries), true hint (different polynomials per chamber, find boundaries and test both sides — but no formula), and no hint (no equation class; test at n = 4..7 with widely separated frequency scales). Six agent configurations (Claude Opus 4.8 max, Claude Opus 4.8 ultra, Codex x-high, Codex 55 xhigh, DeepSeek v4 pro, Fugu ultra) × three conditions = 18 runs, one run per cell; the authors report observed outcomes rather than pass@k because runs are long and token-expensive. Outcomes are classified into five mutually exclusive categories (complete / partial sum / one chamber / hint rejected / incorrect) and an eight-step rubric K1–K8 (build evaluator → fixed properties → principal-chamber formula → breakdown counterexample → chamber dependence → every boundary → complete subset sum → test in new chambers). A held-out numerical test at n = 8 (250 configurations drawn from N(0, 2.5²) with seed 2025, on-shell constraints solved) scores the most complete explicit formula in each final answer by MAE.

**Baselines.** On a fixed train–test split of BG-generated data (rescaled target Φ_n; 8 max(x,0) terms at n = 5, 32 at n = 7): three conventional SR systems (PySR: 35 iterations/12×24 candidates; gplearn: population 2,000/60 generations; Operon: population 5,000/≤2M evaluations; 3 seeds each, 10 for Operon), two LLM-assisted SR systems (LaSR and LLM-SR, 2 seeds each with GPT-5.6-Terra), and four numerical ML methods (least squares, 800-tree random forest, ReLU MLP (256,256,128), cubic b-spline KAN width 2,8). Appendix C adds Bingo, PS-Tree, a region-first Operon variant, SciExplorer, and an agent restricted to the fixed dataset. Scoring is held-out MAE (Eq. 6); the target-formula entry evaluates to zero error.

**PI + two-student team.** Three repeating steps: propose a formula, compare it with the exact BG calculation, revise on disagreement. All three agents get the same question, the BG code, and the list of tests the final answer must pass; the PI assigns tasks (typically one student computing exact examples, the other deriving/explaining formulas), each student writes and runs code in a separate workspace, and all write to a shared record. The PI implements the combined formula independently and checks failed cases, missing chambers, and completeness. For two-minus rediscovery: two runs (Opus team, Codex 5.5 team) parallel to the six no-hint single-agent runs — same problem, no hints, no literature access, no answer key; acceptance required agreement to relative error ≤ 10⁻¹⁰. For the new A6 (three-minus sector, σ = (−1,−1,−1,+1,+1,+1)), the team was given two known facts (one-minus vanishing and Eq. 3), allowed literature access and more compute, and ran all scheduled rounds; a final independent check re-tested every relabeled term with separately written BG code using exact fraction arithmetic. Four team architectures were compared on A6, each run once.

## Results & Highlights

- **Only 4/18 single-agent runs recovered the complete formula — all 4 under the true-hint prompt (Table 2: true hint 4/6 complete vs 0/6 for both false hint and no hint).** The false-hint condition produced 0 complete, 1 partial sum, 3 one-chamber, 1 hint-rejected, and 1 incorrect outcome; the no-hint condition produced 0 complete, 2 partial sums, and 4 one-chamber results. Completion steps are the bottleneck: 15/18 runs reach the principal-chamber formula (K3), 16/18 find a counterexample (K4), but only 8/18 identify every chamber boundary (K6) and only 4/18 build the complete alternating subset sum (K7).
- **Numerical gap at n = 8 (250 held-out configurations): the one complete formula scores MAE 0, while the best incomplete run (no-hint Codex 5.5) reaches 2.31×10², an incomplete subset sum leaves out a conservation-constrained wave for MAE 1.04×10⁴, and one-chamber formulas score 1.17×10⁶–5.70×10⁶** — e.g., the false-hint Claude ultra run's all-n formula, valid in only one chamber, gets 5.70×10⁶.
- **All fitted baselines fail where the agent team succeeds.** At n = 5 Operon has the lowest median held-out MAE among fitted methods (1.36) and KAN is the best numerical ML method (2.91 at n = 5, 927.04 at n = 7); at n = 7 (32 switching terms) gplearn has the lowest fitted median MAE (373.49) but slightly negative held-out R² and does not recover the target. Extra variants also miss: Operon 2.57 vs Bingo 5.73 and region-first Operon 6.59; SciExplorer's submissions contain none of the required max(x,0) terms. Neither LaSR nor LLM-SR finds all chambers or the full subset sum, and swapping in a stronger LLM does not change this. Only the PI-student team's zero-error entry evaluates the complete formula.
- **PI + two students succeeds without hints where 6/6 single-agent runs failed.** The Opus team recovered the full Eq. 3 in 2 rounds (round-2 PI check: 142 exact matches including 95 points outside the principal chamber); the Codex 5.5 team took 3 rounds (round 3: 12/12 exact tests away from the n = 4 limit; largest discrepancy 2×10⁻¹², below the stated 10⁻¹⁰ threshold).
- **New physics: the six-point three-minus amplitude A6 (Eq. 18), previously unknown in closed form, found in 8 rounds and verified by 140/140 exact BG tests across 58 chambers.** Intermediate steps were fail-driven: a failed cross-chamber test in round 5 forced paired-wall correction terms, and the verifier variant rejected an incorrect boundary correction in round 5, found a failure away from chamber boundaries in round 7, and passed the independent round-8 check after more than 6,000 verifier tests and 5,733 PI checks. In the architecture comparison, the single agent matched all 7 of its checked points but missed a chamber boundary with 7 test points and produced no explicit A6; PI+1 student got 0/30 exact in the third chamber; only both PI+2-student setups finished complete.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** Genuinely high on two axes: (1) as a methodology paper, it dissects *where* agent discovery fails (chamber-combination and cross-chamber testing) rather than scoring pass/fail — the K1–K8 rubric and the three named stopping mechanisms ("a failed combination attempt leads back to a restricted answer", "the formula is tested only on familiar configurations", "the run continues to follow a disproven hint") are specific and reusable; (2) as a physics paper, the A6 three-minus expression appears to be a real new result, arguably among the first agent-discovered analytic amplitudes verified by independent exact recomputation.

**Rigor — honest but thin in places.** The central "teams beat singles" claim rests on 2 successful team runs vs 6 single-agent failures, one run per cell, with no repetitions and no error bars; the authors say so, but the headline framing ("both PI+student teams do") should be read as a case study, not a statistically supported comparison. The compute is explicitly not cost-matched — the teams use more model calls and, in the verifier variant, over 6,000 verifier tests plus 5,733 PI checks — so part of the team advantage may simply be more compute and scaffolding; a cost-matched single agent with a longer budget is the missing ablation. The prompt conditions are confounded by design: the true-hint package differs from the false-hint one in both the equation-class guidance and the test-point advice, so the paper cannot isolate which ingredient unlocked the 4/6 complete runs. The A6 discovery is also a less controlled experiment than the two-minus rediscovery: the team had literature access, two supplied facts, and more compute — so "no hint" applies cleanly only to the rediscovery half.

**The headline result is numerical, not proven.** A6 is supported by 140/140 exact tests in 58 chambers and a fraction-arithmetic independent recheck, but finite tests cannot exclude a missing chamber (the paper's own K8 step), and the explicit numerator for n ≥ 7 is open; the abstract's "independently verified analytic expression" is accurate but a reader could overread it as established physics.

**Evaluation caveats.** Run histories were rewritten into chronological accounts (interpretation, not verbatim transcripts), and the five-category outcome classification involves judgment, though full original records are released. The SR/ML comparison uses a fixed dataset while agents query adaptively — the authors acknowledge this mismatch and defend it (more data alone would not establish exact recovery), but it means the baselines are answering a slightly different question. The single-agent A6 run's failure (7 test points placed near a chamber boundary missed the switch) is a sample of one illustration of a real failure mode, not a measured rate.

**Red flags?** None serious — the paper systematically annotates its own limitations ("these runs are not cost-matched", "the results describe these four runs rather than their general success probabilities", "an analytic proof and an extension to arbitrary n remain open"). The main risk is citation drift: the striking 4/18 and 140/140 numbers will circulate without the n-of-1 caveats.

## Strengths

- Exactly checkable ground truth turns "did the agent discover the formula" into a binary, reproducible question — a rare luxury in scientific-discovery benchmarks, and the zero-error vs nonzero-MAE distinction cleanly separates formula recovery from prediction.
- The failure-mode analysis is more informative than a leaderboard: intermediate steps (K1–K8), per-run MAE at n = 8, and three named stopping mechanisms localize the bottleneck to global combination and testing, not local fitting.
- Combines a controlled rediscovery study (no hints, parallel to single-agent baselines) with a genuinely new, independently re-verified physics result (A6), demonstrating the propose-test-revise loop can produce previously unknown results, not just rediscover.
- The PI+students design directly targets the diagnosed failure (the constructing agent no longer chooses its own tests; cross-chamber evidence is assigned as explicit tasks).
- Exceptional transparency: complete prompts, rewritten and original run histories, counterexamples, checking code, and benchmark datasets are public, enabling others to rerun or extend the study.
- Honest, prominent statements of what the results do and do not establish (no pass@k, not cost-matched, finite tests ≠ proof).

## Limitations

- Sample size: one run per agent-configuration × prompt cell (18 total) and one run per team architecture; success rates and the architecture ranking have no statistical support.
- Compute and scaffolding are not matched between single agents and teams (teams use more model calls; the A6 run got literature access and all scheduled rounds), so the multi-agent advantage is not isolated as an architectural effect.
- Single problem domain: one sector of one physics amplitude; the chamber-combination failure mode may not transfer to discovery tasks with noisy data or without an exact oracle, which most real scientific domains lack.
- Prompt packages vary equation guidance and test advice together, so the source of the true-hint advantage is untested; no ablation separates the two ingredients.
- The new A6 expression is verified numerically only — no analytic proof, and the explicit numerator for n ≥ 7 remains open.
- Outcome classification and run-history narration involve interpretive rewriting (mitigated by releasing the originals), and the fixed-dataset SR/ML comparison is not compute-matched to the adaptive agent runs.

## Relevance

直接命中用户的最高优先级方向"自进化智能体与自动化科研"（agentic scientific discovery）：论文用可精确验证的受控实验刻画了 LLM 代理做科学发现时的具体失败模式——局部公式易得、全局组合与跨域检验难——并给出 PI+双学生的多代理科研工作流、可复用的"提出-检验-修正"脚手架和一个新的可验证发现；其开源运行记录与 hydrotope 基准对构建和评测自动化科研系统（含长程自主运行的可靠性分析）非常有价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- With cost-matched budgets (equal compute, equal test allowances), would a single agent still fail the K6–K7 steps, and how many repetitions are needed to turn the 2-vs-6 team comparison into a statistically meaningful pass@k estimate?
- Can the PI+students workflow transfer to domains without an exact oracle (noisy experiments, simulation-only checks), where "counterexample-driven revision" is much weaker?
- Can the diagnosed bottleneck steps (find every boundary, combine into one global expression, test in unseen regimes) be packaged as a reusable, domain-agnostic scaffold or reward signal for self-improving research agents?
- Can agents produce an analytic proof of the A6 expression (and the open n ≥ 7 numerator), e.g., by combining inductive BG-identity search with symbolic verification, rather than finite numerical testing?
- Which prompt ingredient — equation-class guidance or test-point advice — drives the true-hint advantage, and how do agents' sampling strategies interact with chamber-boundary coverage?
