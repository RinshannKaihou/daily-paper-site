Explicit error bounds turn mixed-precision attention from a gamble into a deterministic, per-sub-block precision decision — a template for reliable 8-bit LLM inference.

Critical edge

Zero wall-clock timings. Every speed claim is a cycle-count projection for a hypothetical accelerator — and the latency of its own recomputation bookkeeping is never modeled.

Also unmeasured: head-to-head numbers against practical FP8 kernels. Requires QK-Norm to keep pre-softmax logits bounded.

01 / Methodology

Two intermediates drop to 8-bit; a detector decides where that is unsafe.

aWhat gets trimmed

Not quantization — weights, K/Q/V and outputs stay full-precision. Inside the FlashAttention-2 kernel only two intermediates are reduced: key–query product accumulation (e4m3) and softmax exponentials (ue5m3, evaluated as a one-cycle table lookup).

bTwo-stage detector

Stage 1 flags sub-blocks that push the running maximum beyond safety margin δ (e.g. 2−8), worst-threat first. Stage 2 allocates the remaining 16-bit budget by solving a Kronecker-structured knapsack (block-LAMP, Eq. 8). Both read only values FlashAttention already maintains — no extra HBM traffic.

cThe theory

Proposition 1 bounds rounding-error propagation through online softmax and proves it peaks when the running maximum comes from a low-precision logit — precisely what stage 1 watches.

dHardware spec & evaluation

A dual-format datapath for a hypothetical SIMT accelerator: LUT exponentials, a 16-bit recomputation path, and a “compact” layout cutting accumulator registers 75% — conditional on QK-Norm bounding logits at 8√7. Accuracy is measured with simulated 8-bit arithmetic in Triton on one 80 GB GPU: 5 models × 4 tasks × 6 τ × 2 δ.

Noise floor — read deltas with care

Single runs, no error bars. On 0-shot ARC-Challenge, Qwen3-32B’s 8-bit baseline (0.6101) outscored its own 32-bit baseline (0.6094) — task noise on the same order as several reported gains. Tightening τ is also non-monotonic: stage 1 does the heavy lifting, the knapsack closes the last gap at extra recomputation cost.

03a / Strengths

What holds up

  • S1Grounded in analysis, not heuristics. Every design choice — the δ margin, threat-sorted processing, 16-bit recomputation — traces to a term in the published rounding-error bound.
  • S2Clean integration. Needs only the running max and normalization constant FlashAttention already maintains; composes orthogonally with quantization, RoPE and masking.
  • S3Honest reporting. The ARC anomaly, the non-monotonic τ trade-off and per-tile recompute distributions are disclosed; appendices ablate both hyperparameters and each stage separately.
  • S4Reproducible. Public Triton code, ~540 GPU-hours on a single 80 GB GPU, and a hardware spec detailed enough for an accelerator team to implement or simulate.
  • S5Proof that targeting works. On Qwen3-32B it matches or beats 32-bit while two-thirds of tiles stay fully 8-bit — recomputing the right sub-blocks beats uniform precision.

03b / Limitations

Where it gives

  • L1No measured performance. All speed claims project a chip nobody can buy; the Triton kernel only simulates the arithmetic, and recompute bookkeeping latency is unmodeled.
  • L2QK-Norm precondition. The 8-bit exponent bound holds only with headwise QK-Norm at moderate gains — true for all five evaluated models, conveniently; Llama-style unbounded-logit models are out of scope as-is.
  • L3Simulated arithmetic. Mantissa truncation + FMA emulation may diverge from real e4m3/ue5m3 silicon (swamping, denormals, round-to-nearest-even).
  • L4Thin baselines. Only all-32-bit and all-8-bit are compared; practical FP8 attention (FA3-FP8, SageAttention) is cited but never run, so the accuracy–efficiency frontier is unsituated.
  • L5Incomplete, model-dependent recovery. gemma-3-12b recovers only ~57% of the MMLU gap; single runs without error bars leave small deltas uninterpretable.
  • L6Inference only. Training-time behavior and very long context (where softmax entropy shifts) are untested.

04 / Questions for further exploration

What to probe next

  1. How does it compare numerically — not just conceptually — against FlashAttention-3’s FP8 path or SageAttention-style scaling at comparable compute budgets?
  2. Can the block-LAMP knapsack (Eq. 8) be solved incrementally instead of by enumeration, and what is the worst-case recomputation rate under adversarial inputs — very long context, high-entropy prompts?
  3. Stage 1 alone (τ = 1) nearly matches the full two-stage scheme: is stage 2 worth dedicated silicon, or could the margin δ suffice?
  4. Does the method extend to models without QK-Norm via logit soft-capping, and how sensitive is δ to logit-scale drift across layers and checkpoints?
  5. Would the ~20–30% disruptive-tile rate shrink at longer sequence lengths, and does the error bound’s assumption that rounding never flips the argmax hold in practice?
