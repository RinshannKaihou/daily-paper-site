Methodology

SGSscore steps by teacher PI sensitivity (JSD)
→
TLBkeep top-ρ steps, balance per trajectory
→
PRKL-pin the student’s privileged view

Iterative PI self-distillation repeats one move: a student is distilled from a frozen teacher policy, then becomes the next cycle’s teacher. SDPO and OEL add a privileged view (PI such as a trajectory summary) but degrade cycle after cycle. ReSAIL keeps the pipeline and changes only the loss — entirely offline, on already-collected trajectories.

  • SGS — Sensitivity-Guided Selection. Score each logged step by the frozen teacher’s PI sensitivity: the JSD between its ordinary-view and privileged-view distributions. Distill only the top ⌈ρ|U|⌉ steps (ρ = 0.05 ALFWorld, 0.25 TextCraft).
  • TLB — Trajectory Loss Balancing. Average losses within each trajectory, weight each trajectory 1/|B|, so step-rich trajectories no longer dominate.
  • PR — Privileged Retention. A second KL term pins the student’s privileged-view outputs to the frozen teacher’s at all steps (λ = 0.5 / 1.0), preserving its ability to teach the next cycle.

Protocol — Qwen3-4B/8B · ALFWorld (128 ID + 128 OOD) & TextCraft · 3 cycles × 30 updates · 3 decoding seeds · 8×H800 (slime + Megatron-LM + SGLang) · Top-20+residual-tail KL approximation

  • Gains compound rather than fade: the advantage over the parent grows from +5.0 to +26.6 pts (OEL) and +6.8 to +18.3 (SDPO) between cycles 1 and 3; OEL finishes below its own cycle-1 score in all six settings, SDPO in five.
  • Two mechanisms, two timescales: SGS drives first-cycle learning; PR specifically preserves cross-cycle PI-conditioned competence (70.3% vs 44.8% at cycle 3).
  • Multimodal check (AITZ, Qwen3-VL-4B): SGS-as-filter lifts step-level action accuracy to 65.95% vs 64.89% for OEL (+1.06 pts) — real but small.

03a Strengths

  • Clear dual-view diagnosis separating deployment collapse from teacher-competence collapse — a protocol other iterative-learning papers should copy.
  • Strong, recent baselines (offline SDPO+TIS, OEL, EPD, offline GRPO, RFT) with shared cycle-1 corpora and matched update budgets.
  • Unusually complete ablations: component on/off, Random/Bottom/Top selection, None/Ordinary/Privileged retention, ρ and λ sweeps, full cost breakdown.
  • Plug-in works on both OEL (fresh responses) and SDPO-offline (cached responses) — not tied to one sampling regime.
  • Honest reporting: exact hyperparameters, per-component GPU-hours, and settings that get worse (λ=1, ρ ≥ 50%).

03b Limitations

  • Only two synthetic text benchmarks and three cycles; longer horizons and realistic, drifting agent environments are unverified.
  • One training seed per configuration; variance across independent training runs is unquantified.
  • Requires a usable PI channel — poor summaries degrade both the teacher signal and the retention target; untested.
  • Retention’s extra teacher-side passes add ~23% training-loop compute over the base method.
  • Qwen3 family only (4B/8B text, 4B VL); no evidence at other scales or families.
  • The +22.5-pt headline equal-weights six very different deltas (7.0–37.0), and the strongest single result sits where baselines collapse hardest.
04

Open Questions

Q1Does the retention benefit hold beyond cycle 3 — and would an adaptive or annealed λ prevent the cycle-3 degradation seen at λ=1 over longer horizons?
Q2How sensitive is SGS to PI quality — would privileged retention preserve a bad teacher as faithfully as a good one?
Q3Would averaging over independent training runs (not just decoding seeds) change the collapse curves — and how much of the 22.5-pt average survives?
Q4Can privileged retention transfer to on-policy RL or experience-replay pipelines, where the teacher signal is a reward-conditioned reference rather than an explicit PI view?
Q5The Top-20-plus-residual-tail KL approximation trades fidelity for compute — at what vocabulary support size does retention stop preserving PI-conditioned competence?
