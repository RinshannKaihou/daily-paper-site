> **一句话总结：** BAAI 的 AREX-2 用"长程反思"轨迹数据（agent 在 GitHub 仓库和算法题环境里多轮迭代改进方案、保留失败与回退全过程）微调 Qwen3.8-27B，把"测试时多轮自我改进"内化进模型本身：MLE-bench Lite 上 81.8 Any Medal（超最强基线 Naïve-N0.5-Flash 的 73.7 达 +8.1 分）、Frontier-CS 70.7，且在未新增任何深搜训练数据的情况下把深搜成绩也全面抬升（BrowseComp 84.0、GAIA 92.2、DeepSearchQA 93.8），27B 参数即在四个深搜基准上全面超越此前的 AREX-122B。

> **TL;DR:** BAAI's AREX-2 teaches a 27B model (Qwen3.8-27B) to improve its own solutions across test-time rounds by fine-tuning on long-horizon improvement trajectories synthesized in ML-engineering and algorithmic-programming environments, keeping failed rounds and regressions in context. On MLE-bench Lite it scores 81.8 Any Medal vs 73.7 for the strongest baseline (Naïve-N0.5-Flash, +8.1) and 72.7 for GPT-5.6 Sol (+9.1); on Frontier-CS it reaches 70.7 vs 54.7 for the best open-weight baseline (+16.0). With zero new deep-research training data, it beats the previous AREX-122B on all four deep-research benchmarks (e.g., BrowseComp 84.0 vs 82.5, DeepSearchQA 93.8 vs 89.9), supporting the claim that long-horizon reflection is a transferable meta-skill.

## 核心贡献

- **把改进回路搬进模型内部**：定义"自我改进"为测试时规模化的形式——多一轮预算就应产出更好的解。将其分解为两个互补能力：*reflection*（每轮的期望增益 r̄ 有多大）与 *long-horizon execution*（有效轮数 T* 能维持多长），总改进 = r̄·T*。
- **长程反思轨迹数据合成流水线**：教师模型把 GitHub 仓库与在线评测题改造成可执行环境（任务 + 评分脚本 + 沙箱），要求参考解可行、朴素基线远低于参考解；agent 在小时级预算下多轮迭代，按需获取"操作性知识"（查文档、跑小实验），训练数据**整体保留**失败轮、回退与被放弃的方案——损失只施加在推动解前进的决策上。
- **跨域迁移验证**：仅新增两个编程/机器学习域的轨迹，深搜训练数据与 AREX 配方完全不变，四个深搜基准仍全面超越 AREX-4B/122B，支持"长程反思是可监督、可迁移的元技能"这一核心假设。
- **测试时规模化的实证**：有反馈时（Frontier-CS，5 小时预算）持续领先且最后 1 小时仍 +2.2 分；无反馈时（BrowseComp 自信度驱动的递归双环）47 轮 64.8% → 143 轮 84.0%，每轮增益约为 AREX-122B 的 3 倍。
- **分阶段消融**（MLE-Lite）：无技能基座 28.8 → +通用技能 41.2 → +任务技能/更多轮次 68.2 → 训练后同技能同轮次 81.8，把"操作性知识、训练、轮次预算"三个因素的贡献干净地拆开（训练净贡献 +13.6）。

## Key Contributions

- **Moves the improvement loop inside the model**: formalizes self-improvement as test-time scaling (total improvement = r̄·T*, the product of per-round gain and effective horizon) and trains both factors via imitation on long-horizon improvement trajectories rather than finished solutions.
- **A trajectory-synthesis recipe that preserves the messy middle**: environments compiled from GitHub repos and online judges (admitted only if a reference solution runs and a naive baseline scores far below it), a teacher agent iterating for hours with on-demand operational knowledge, and whole-trajectory selection that deliberately keeps failed runs, regressions, and abandoned approaches — loss is applied only to decisions that move the solution forward.
- **Cross-domain transfer without new target-domain data**: with the deep-research training data unchanged from the AREX recipe, AREX-2 (27B) surpasses AREX-4B and AREX-122B on all four deep-research benchmarks (BrowseComp 84.0/82.5, HLE 52.6/52.4, GAIA 92.2/85.4, DeepSearchQA 93.8/89.9), evidence that reflection transfers beyond its training domains.
- **Round-scaling evidence in both feedback regimes**: with external feedback (Frontier-CS, 5h budget) it gains 4.8 points between hours 2–5 while DeepSeek-V4-Pro/Flash plateau; without any correctness signal (BrowseComp, confidence-driven inner/outer loops) it reaches 84.0 at 143 turns, exceeding AREX-122B's final accuracy with less than half the turns (~3x gain per turn at <1/4 the parameters).
- **Stage-wise ablation cleanly attributing gains**: on MLE-bench Lite, skills and rounds lift the base model from 28.8 to 68.2 with no training, while training adds a further 13.6 (75.8 → 81.8 as rounds grow), showing operational knowledge, training, and budget each contribute measurably.

## Methodology

**Formalization.** An environment is a pair e = (σ, S): a task σ and a scoring function S mapping a candidate solution to a scalar. A trajectory of n rounds is τ = (x₀, f₀, y₁, f₁, …, yₙ, fₙ), with sₜ = max_{k≤t} S(yₖ). Expected total improvement over a budget of T rounds is Σᵣₜ, decomposed as r̄·T*, where T* counts rounds whose expected gain exceeds a threshold ε (long-horizon execution) and r̄ is the mean gain over those rounds (reflection). The budget T is given to the agent, not learned; the policy determines how much of it is used productively.

**Constructing environments.** Two domains, chosen for (a) unambiguous feedback (validation score / judge verdict), (b) headroom for sustained improvement, and (c) abundant source material: machine-learning tasks from GitHub repositories and algorithmic-programming tasks from online judges. A teacher model converts each source into an executable environment (task, scoring script computing a graded metric on a held-out split, sandbox). An environment is admitted only if execution verifies both that the reference solution runs and scores, and that a straightforward baseline scores well below the reference — environments without headroom are dropped. Graded (non-binary) scores are preferred because each round is then informative.

**Constructing trajectories.** A teacher agent works each task over hours and hundreds of tool calls, told its round and time budget. Three conditions elicit long-horizon reflection: (1) *More rounds* — establish a baseline early, spend later rounds on measurement-driven revisions; (2) *Operational knowledge* — the agent must discover how the environment works itself (reading source/docs, searching papers, running small experiments); compact general and task-specific skills are placed in its context; (3) *Feedback in the loop* — every round ends with a submission and the environment's score/report, and the agent's diagnosis-and-revise response follows it. Nothing is removed mid-trajectory.

**Training.** Trajectories are selected as wholes: final score must clear a threshold relative to the reference, and the process must satisfy task requirements (well-formed tool calls, no cheating such as reconstructing held-out answers). No condition is placed on individual rounds, so kept trajectories contain failures and regressions in context. Loss is applied to the decisions that move the solution forward (diagnosing failures, repairing, changing strategy), with zero loss on non-progress steps, system messages, environment reports, and retrieved documents. AREX-2 = Qwen3.8-27B fine-tuned on these trajectories plus the AREX deep-research data, which is left unchanged — making cross-domain gains readable as transfer.

**Evaluation protocols.** AREX protocol for non-coding benchmarks (max 300 inner / 1500 overall turns); Frontier-CS Agent Track with a 5-hour per-task budget; MLE-Lite under the OpenMLE protocol with a 12-hour per-task budget, AREX-2 evaluated with skills in context. F1 for DeepSearchQA, Any Medal for MLE-Lite, accuracy elsewhere.

## Results & Highlights

- **MLE-bench Lite (Any Medal %):** AREX-2 reaches 81.8, the highest score in the table — 8.1 points above the strongest baseline Naïve-N0.5-Flash (73.7) and 9.1 above the strongest closed-weight model GPT-5.6 Sol (72.7), despite a 12-hour per-task budget for all systems.
- **Frontier-CS Agent Track (188 tasks, graded score):** 70.7 for AREX-2 vs 54.7 for Kimi-K2.7-Code, the strongest reported open-weight baseline (+16.0), and within 5.7 points of the strongest closed-weight system GPT-5.6 Sol (76.4); DeepSeek-V4-Pro scores 44.7 and Qwen3.6-35B-A3B 23.4 (author-reproduced).
- **Deep-research transfer, no new search training data:** AREX-2 (27B) beats AREX-122B on all four benchmarks — BrowseComp 84.0 vs 82.5, HLE 52.6 vs 52.4 (text-only subset), GAIA 92.2 vs 85.4, DeepSearchQA 93.8 vs 89.9 F1 — and beats AREX-4B by large margins (70.7 / 40.6 / 81.6 / 78.5). It still trails Kimi-K3 (91.2) and GPT-5.6 Sol (90.4) on BrowseComp, and ranks behind XYZ-Aquila-mini (97.1) and Agents-A1 (96.0) on GAIA.
- **Round scaling with feedback (Frontier-CS, 5h):** AREX-2 reaches 54.4 after 1h, 65.9 after 2h, 70.7 after 5h, still gaining +4.8 between hours 2–5 (+2.2 in the final hour), while DeepSeek-V4-Pro stalls at 44.7 after 2h and DeepSeek-V4-Flash at 39.1 after 3h — i.e., a longer effective horizon T*, not just a stronger start.
- **Round scaling without feedback (BrowseComp, turns = LLM calls):** accuracy climbs from 64.8 at 47 turns to 84.0 at 143 turns; at a comparable ~140-turn budget AREX-2 is more than 12 points ahead of AREX-122B and exceeds AREX's final accuracy (82.5) with less than half the turns, with ~3x the gain per turn at under a quarter of the parameters.
- **Stage-wise ablation (MLE-Lite Any Medal):** base model Qwen3.8-27B without skills 28.8 (M0) → + general skills 41.2 (M1) → + task-specific skills and more rounds 68.2 (M2) → AREX-2 with M2's skills and rounds 81.8 (M4) and 75.8 with the smaller M0/M1 round budget (M3). Training therefore adds +13.6 over the identically-equipped base model, M3 exceeds M2 by 7.6 even with fewer rounds, and M3→M4 shows +6.0 from additional rounds.

## Authors & Credibility

Core contributors: Hongjin Qian, Chaofan Li, Kun Luo, Wenqing Wei, Jianlyu Chen, and Zheng Lin (project leader); advisors Zhicheng Dou and Defu Lian are senior researchers associated with Renmin University of China's pattern-mining and machine-learning groups. The team built the prior AREX recipe (Lu et al., 2026, arXiv:2607.21461), so this is a continuation of an established self-improving-agent research line rather than a one-off result. Models are released on Hugging Face (BAAI/arex-2 collection) with code at github.com/VectorSpaceLab/AREX-2, which supports reproducibility, though the main text omits data-scale and training hyperparameters. Benchmark protocols follow official harnesses (Agent Track, OpenMLE), with author-reproduced baseline rows explicitly starred.

## Critical Assessment

- **The headline number is substantially scaffold-driven.** The stage-wise ablation the authors themselves report shows that skills plus more rounds alone take the *untrained* base model from 28.8 to 68.2 on MLE-Lite — a +39.4 gain — while training contributes +13.6 on top. AREX-2's 81.8 is evaluated *with* skills in context, so the model's learned share of the headline is real but clearly the minority component; papers from other labs without an equivalent skills harness are not compared on equal footing.
- **Mixed evaluation subsets in Table 2.** The footnote concedes that starred HLE numbers (e.g., GPT-5.6 Sol 58.0) are on the full HLE set while AREX-2's 52.6 is text-only — an apples-to-oranges caveat that slightly flatters the HLE comparison, and several Frontier-CS baselines (DeepSeek-V4-Pro 44.7, Kimi-K2.7-Code 54.7) are author-reproduced rather than official.
- **Honest but incomplete frontier picture.** On BrowseComp the model still trails Kimi-K3 (91.2) and GPT-5.6 Sol (90.4) by 6+ points, and on GAIA it ranks behind XYZ-Aquila-mini (97.1) and Agents-A1 (96.0) — the authors acknowledge this, which is to their credit, but the abstract's framing ("keeps improving as its budget of rounds grows") leans on the benchmarks where it wins.
- **Reproducibility gaps despite model release.** The 12-page main text never states how many environments/trajectories were kept, the teacher model's identity, training token counts, or hyperparameters; the transfer claim also rides partly on an "optimized harness" (Section 3.3.1) that is not specified. Single base model (Qwen3.8-27B), single training run, no variance reporting for the ablation stages.
- **Cost of the headline behavior is nontrivial.** Demonstrated scaling budgets are 5 hours/task (Frontier-CS), 12 hours/task (MLE-Lite), and up to 143 LLM turns/question (BrowseComp) — practical adoption cost is discussed nowhere.
- **What is genuinely strong:** the decomposition r̄·T* is a clean conceptual handle; keeping failures/regressions in context with loss masked to progress-making decisions is a well-motivated contrast to outcome-only SFT; and the unchanged-deep-research-data design makes the transfer claim about as controlled as this kind of paper allows.

## Strengths

- Clear formal framing: self-improvement as test-time scaling, decomposed into per-round gain (reflection) and effective horizon (long-horizon execution), which then structures the evaluation (scaling curves in both feedback and no-feedback regimes).
- The trajectory-selection principle — keep the whole trajectory including failed rounds, mask loss to decisions that follow and improve on failures — directly targets the missing "how solutions improve" signal in conventional SFT-on-winning-outputs data.
- Environment admission gate (reference must run; naive baseline must score well below) mechanically guarantees improvement headroom instead of hoping for it.
- Transfer claim is well-controlled by design: deep-research training data byte-identical to the prior recipe, so cross-domain gains are attributable to the new trajectories.
- Unusually transparent ablation that separates skills, rounds, and training, and candid reporting where the model loses (GAIA, strongest BrowseComp/HLE).

## Limitations

- Both training domains rely on programmatic verifiers; whether long-horizon reflection supervises well in domains without graded scores (open-ended research, writing) is asserted as future work, not tested.
- The round budget is handed to the agent rather than learned, so reported scaling may partly depend on the agent being told the budget.
- Evaluation is expensive at test time (hours per task, >100 turns) and the paper gives no cost analysis; the "optimized harness" used on Frontier-CS is unspecified.
- Reproducibility is incomplete in the main text: no counts of environments/trajectories/tokens, unnamed teacher model, no hyperparameters, single base model and single run (benchmark-mandated 3-seed averaging applies only to the MLE-Lite metric itself).
- HLE comparisons mix full-set and text-only subsets across models (footnoted but still noisy), and GAIA's susceptibility to contamination is not addressed.

## Relevance

这篇论文正中你最高优先级的 "Self-Evolving Agents & Automated Research" 方向：它把自我改进回路内化到模型权重里，用可验证环境合成多轮改进轨迹（含失败与回退），并在深搜等未训练域上验证了元技能迁移。分阶段消融对"技能脚手架 vs 训练本身"贡献的拆解，对评估各类 self-improving agent 论文（包括 AREX 系列后续工作）的可信度很有参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the r̄·T* decomposition hold quantitatively — can T* and r̄ be measured per model on held-out environments, and do they predict benchmark transfer?
- How much of the +13.6 training gain survives without task-specific skills at test time, i.e., is the model learning to compensate for a missing scaffold?
- Would trajectory-level selection (keeping failures) beat outcome-only SFT at matched data budget, on the same environments?
- Can the recipe extend to domains with noisy or delayed reward (open-ended scientific research) where the admission gate (reference vs naive baseline) has no analogue?
- What is the inference-cost frontier: accuracy per dollar at 1 hour vs 5 hours vs 12 hours, relative to larger non-self-improving models at their standard budgets?
