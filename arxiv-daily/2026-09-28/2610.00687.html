Why This Matters

HBM uncorrectable errors that ECC contains are treated as first-class: a system-level mitigation after memory faults — and currently the fastest recovery path for LLM training.

把被 ECC 隔离的显存不可纠错误当一等公民处理——内存硬件出错后的系统级缓解，也是当前最快的训练恢复路径。

HOFs are common in the wild: 22.7% of failures in ByteDance’s 19-job study · 73% in a 256-GPU study.

Critical Edge

The 131,072-GPU headline rests on simulation alone, assuming independent, non-overlapping failures. HBM faults are emulated at exactly the 16-page tolerance boundary — real ECC events never tested. At 0% HOFs, Leto is 0.3 pp worse than plain Gemini+.

Methodology — retain the state, don’t rebuild it

Megatron-Core v0.13.1 · PyTorch 2.8.0 · NCCL 2.27.3

Leto targets HOFs — hardware-operable failures where a trainer process dies but its GPU stays usable: ECC-contained HBM errors that retire pages without resetting the device, and transient network outages that hang collectives. Fatal failures (dead hardware) fall back to ordinary checkpointing, unchanged. Three mechanisms make in-place takeover possible:

  • Resilient Memory Pool (RMP) Retained model state is split into 2 GiB protection groups (1,024 × 2 MiB HBM pages) guarded by two tiers of Reed–Solomon parity — 16 + 1 pages — held entirely in host memory, so redundancy costs zero scarce HBM. Faulty pages are localized by bisecting EHWPOISON regions and rebuilt from parity.
  • Resumable Model Updater (RMU) Because optimizer updates are commutative and RS codes are linear, applying a chunk’s delta to the data also updates its parity with the same delta. Each update becomes a chunk-level transaction — undo buffer plus in-stream commit markers — so a mid-update crash resumes at the last committed chunk, with no full-model snapshot.
  • Preinitialization Controller A same-node shadow trainer pre-builds the CUDA context, NCCL communicators and runtime modules (≈2.2 GiB on OLMoE). When active training needs the memory, an allocator OOM callback tears the shadow down in levels L1/L2/L3 and later re-adopts the highest level that fits the observed headroom.

一句话：让“幸存硬件上的模型状态 + 可复用进程状态”被新进程直接接管，而不是检查点重载 + 重算 + 进程重建。

03

Trade-offs

what the evaluation actually establishes — and what it doesn’t

Strengths

  • 3.6–6.5× faster recovery than the strongest baselines on all five workloads, at only 0.6–0.8% steady-state cost — an exceptional trade for a fault-tolerance layer.
  • Redundancy without HBM: two-tier host-memory parity, with the avoided cost quantified (256 MiB–1 GiB on Kimi-MoE) rather than hand-waved.
  • Crash consistency without snapshots: RMU runs at ~10× lower overhead than snapshot strawmen, with bit-exact recovery verified empirically.
  • OOM-aware preinit: L1/L2/L3 reclamation resolves the shadow-vs-MoE memory conflict, with per-level recovery costs measured (26–52 s).
  • Unusually complete evaluation: tuned SOTA baselines, both failure classes, a public spot trace, correctness verification, and a simulator with published error bounds (MAPE 2.18%).

Limitations

  • HOF-conditional value: benefit scales with the HOF share (22.7%–73%, measured on other operators’ clusters); at 0% HOFs Leto is 0.3 pp worse than plain Gemini+. → Q5
  • Scale is simulated: real experiments top out at 72 A100s; 16K–131K-GPU results assume independent, uniform, non-overlapping failures.
  • Synthetic fault model: exactly 16 pages per group, at the tolerance cliff — no real HBM ECC events; bursty, row-granular, recurring faults unmeasured.
  • Deeply NVIDIA-specific: A100 + CUDA UVM + NCCL + Megatron; the PyTorch allocator OOM-hook coupling is fragile across framework versions.
  • Short runs: 30 minutes with one scheduled failure each; multi-day fault accumulation and shadow churn unobserved. Under memory pressure recovery degrades to 52 s.

Open Questions

for further exploration
Q1How does two-tier RMP behave under realistic ECC distributions — bursty row-granular, recurring faults on aging GPUs — and how often does the checkpoint fallback trigger in production?
Q2Could RMP parity mismatch be repurposed as a silent-data-corruption detector for bit flips ECC misses, rather than handling only reported uncorrectable errors?
Q3Does the design port to H100/Blackwell or non-NVIDIA stacks, given the dependence on CUDA virtual-memory APIs, allocator internals, and NCCL?
Q4How do correlated failures (rack power, fabric incidents) at 100K-GPU scale change the 95–96% ETTR conclusion — can state retention survive a top-of-rack partition?
Q5What is the true HOF ratio across clusters — niche optimization or common case? Should orchestrators classify HOF vs FF automatically, as Leto assumes?
Q6Could retained in-place state verify recovered data — replay the interrupted chunk against parity — to catch silent corruption from before the page failed?
