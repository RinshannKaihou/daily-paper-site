> **一句话总结：** 单篇 Meta 技术报告发现 looped transformer（如 Huginn-3.5B）做低比特后训练量化时有两种独立失效模式——量化误差经"无恒等路径的 loop-entry adapter"反复回馈进递归状态（feedback exposure），以及 GPTQ 只用 step-0 激活的 Hessian 标定不到后续递归步才用到的方向（calibration blindness）；仅在 rollout 全程累积 Hessian（轨迹标定，GPTQ 求解器不变）即可让 Huginn 在 GSM8K 上从 one-step GPTQ 的 26.9% 恢复到 35.0%（超过 bf16 的 29.9%，闭合 102% 的 RTN-to-base 差距），并在 9 个检查点、7 种循环架构上全部优于 one-step GPTQ。

> **TL;DR:** Standard PTQ silently breaks looped (weight-tied recurrent) transformers. On Huginn-3.5B, per-channel INT4 collapses GSM8K top-1 agreement with bf16 to 10.5% when applied only to the non-residual loop-entry adapter, but retains 87.9% when applied to all 16 residual core projections — the damage comes from error that re-enters the recurrence without an identity path ("feedback exposure"). Separately, GPTQ's Hessian built from step-0 activations covers only 43 of 10,560 adapter input dimensions (0.41%), missing directions used later in the rollout ("calibration blindness"), which is why one-step GPTQ loses to round-to-nearest on 5 of 9 checkpoints. The fix is nearly free: accumulate the Hessian across all N recurrence steps and feed it to the unchanged GPTQ solver. This lifts grouped-INT4 Huginn GSM8K exact match from 26.9% (one-step GPTQ) to 35.0%, above the 29.9% bf16 baseline, and improves all nine checkpoints (up to Parcae-1.3B PPL 27.35 → 20.84 vs RTN 51.30), with a real Marlin W4A16 kernel showing 1.21x latency speedup at unchanged accuracy.

## 核心贡献

- **识别并分离两种失效模式**：在 looped transformer 的 INT4 量化中，(1) *feedback exposure*——量化扰动作用在递归状态上且周围没有恒等/残差路径（Huginn 的非残差 adapter 是典型），误差被每一步重复回馈；(2) *calibration blindness*——GPTQ 用 step-0 激活构建的 Hessian 几乎不加权后续递归步才激活的方向。二者机制独立、可分别用干预实验隔离。
- **因果证据链而非相关性**：通过 λ-受控注入（剔除先验状态误差使 agreement 从 10.4% 升到 69.2%）、identity-bypass 对照（旁路使 bf16/INT4 单步差异 RMS 降低 37.4%）、同 Frobenius 范数高斯噪声对照（25.0% vs INT4 的 10.5%）、IIR 滤波器极点分析（量化反馈矩阵 A 在 4 个滤波器上都会把极点推出单位圆，量化 B/C/D 不动）、Mamba-2.8B 组件对照（扰动 A_log 使 d2/d1 从 1.30 升到 1.71，扰动 out_proj 几乎不变），把损害归因到"误差进入递归的位置与结构"，而非扰动大小。
- **Hessian 子空间分析**：定义 thresholded Hessian rank（10^-3·λmax 阈值），发现 Huginn adapter 的 step-0 rank 仅 43/10,560（0.41%），32 步累积后升至 335（7.8 倍）；Ouro-1.4B 的 down_proj step-0 rank 为 1/5,632（0.02%），而 steps 1–3 达 974–1,194。该指标与"one-step GPTQ 是否输给 RTN"在 9 个检查点上分布一致（COCONUT 是预期中的反例：rank gain 仅 1.1 倍）。
- **近零成本的修复：trajectory calibration**——把 Hessian 在整个 rollout 上累加（H_deploy = Σ H_t）再交给原封不动的 GPTQ 列求解器，9 个检查点（7 种架构，最大 4.17B）全部改善，Huginn 恢复到 bf16 水平。
- **给出可操作的设计准则**："一个从没见过 loop 的量化器不该被信任"——标定数据必须覆盖递归轨迹；并实测 Marlin W4A16 真内核（1.21 倍加速、3.80 倍显存压缩、精度不回退），证明该修复兼容现有推理栈。

## Key Contributions

- **Two distinct, independently isolated failure modes** for low-bit PTQ on looped transformers: *feedback exposure* (quantization error enters the recurrent state with no identity path around it, so it is re-fed at every later step — Huginn's non-residual loop-entry adapter is the prime offender) and *calibration blindness* (GPTQ's step-0 Hessian leaves later-step input directions nearly unweighted).
- **A causal evidence chain, not just correlation**: lambda-controlled injection (removing prior-state error feedback raises final-step agreement from 10.4% to 69.2%), an identity-bypass holdout control (bypassing the adapter reduces bf16/INT4 one-step difference RMS by 37.4%), a Frobenius-norm-matched Gaussian noise arm (25.0% agreement vs 10.5% for real INT4 at the same position), IIR-filter pole analysis (quantizing the feedback matrix A pushes at least one pole outside the unit circle in all 4 filters at 10 bits; quantizing B/C/D never moves poles), and a Mamba-2.8B component control (perturbing A_log raises the median d2/d1 state-error ratio from 1.30 to 1.71 while perturbing out_proj barely changes it).
- **A subspace-level diagnostic**: thresholded Hessian rank at 10^-3*lambda_max. Huginn's adapter covers only 43/10,560 input dimensions (0.41%) at step 0 vs 335 (7.8x) with 32-step accumulation; Ouro-1.4B's down_proj has rank 1/5,632 (0.02%) at step 0 vs 974-1,194 at steps 1-3. All 8 filled checkpoints in the rank-vs-recovery plot have rank gain >= 2x; COCONUT (1.1x) is the predicted null case.
- **A near-zero-cost fix, trajectory calibration**: accumulate the Hessian over the full rollout (H_deploy = sum of per-step Hessians) and hand it to the unchanged GPTQ column solver. Every one of nine checkpoints from seven looped architectures improves; Huginn returns to bf16-level accuracy, with real-kernel (Marlin W4A16) validation on an A100.
- **An actionable design rule**: "a quantizer that never sees the loop should not be trusted" — calibration data must span the recurrence trajectory, not just the first forward pass.

## Methodology

**Models and quantization setting.** Nine checkpoints from seven looped architectures: Huginn-3.5B (32 recursions, 132-layer effective depth), R-Llama-1.4B, Ouro-1.4B/Ouro-2.6B (24/48 stacks), LoopFormer-278M, Parcae-140M/1.3B, PonderPythia-2.8B, and Nanbeige-4.2-3B (4.17B). Main results use deterministic simulated grouped INT4 (g=128) via FakeQuant; R-Llama and Huginn calibrate on the first 50 GSM8K training prompts, reporting on a disjoint held-out set of 1,319 8-shot greedy generations; other checkpoints use WikiText-2 perplexity (LAMBDA/GSM8K/ARC-C/HellaSwag in appendices).

**Mechanism diagnostics (feedback exposure).** The primary metric is top-1 agreement with bf16 at each recurrence step (fraction of token positions whose argmax matches bf16), averaged over the first 50 GSM8K test prompts. The adapter and core are quantized separately to attribute damage. Three interventions isolate the cause: (1) lambda-controlled injection — replays the rollout with a scaled prior-state quantization error added to the adapter input (lambda=0 removes feedback); (2) identity-bypass control — perturbs state/weights identically in bf16 and INT4 runs while bypassing the adapter, measuring how much of the bf16-vs-INT4 divergence the identity path explains; (3) a norm-matched Gaussian noise arm ruling out "any perturbation of this size" explanations. Theory: the one-step Jacobian factors as J = prod(I + dF_t/ds_t) x W_s, so adapter rounding error (delta-W_s, s_t) perturbs the state-input Jacobian directly and re-enters through the unpatched adapter output, while every core block carries an additive identity path. Supporting spectral analysis: mean per-step Jacobian spectral radius is 1.064 for bf16 vs 1.145 after adapter INT4, with rho>1 on 30/30 prompts (vs 9/30 bf16). External validity is checked outside transformers via IIR filters (pole movement) and Mamba-2.8B (A_log vs out_proj state-transition perturbations).

**Mechanism diagnostics (calibration blindness).** GPTQ minimizes tr(deltaW H deltaW^T) with H = X^T X built from step-0 activations. The paper reports the thresholded rank #(lambda_i > 10^-3*lambda_max) per layer, with controls: a 1,000-prompt run (rank falls 43 -> 20 while top eigenvalue moves <0.3%, ruling out sample starvation), per-step rank tracking (43 -> 259 at step 1 -> 363 at step 4 at fixed token positions), and a cross-layer contrast (block-3 mlp.proj has low step-0 rank 0.36% but only 1.4x gain — showing rank gain, not low rank alone, measures trajectory shift).

**Trajectory calibration.** Algorithm 1: for each shared layer, H_deploy = sum_{t=0}^{N-1} X_t^T X_t from the bf16 rollout; then run the unchanged GPTQ solver with H_deploy. Calibration horizon N_cal is swept (1/8/32); the main table uses each model's deployed recurrence depth. A quantized-trajectory control (collecting Hessians on the quantized rollout at INT3) tests robustness; single-late-step calibration (step 31 only) probes whether one late checkpoint suffices.

**Real-kernel evaluation.** Marlin W4A16 on a single NVIDIA A100, batch size 1, 32-step Huginn forward on 7-token prompts, median eager latency with paired-bootstrap 95% CIs; accuracy cross-checked between Marlin and simulated INT4 on the full GSM8K test set.

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline recovery (Huginn-3.5B, grouped-INT4 g=128, GSM8K exact match, n=1,319 held-out):** trajectory calibration reaches 35.0% vs 26.9% for one-step GPTQ and 27.9% for RTN, against a 29.9% bf16 baseline — 102% of the RTN-to-base gap closed (the paper transparently footnotes this is 94 vs 92 exact matches). R-Llama-1.4B: 45.3% vs 40.3% one-step / 39.5% RTN (54% closed).
- **Feedback exposure magnitude:** per-channel INT4 on Huginn's adapter alone drops final-step top-1 agreement with bf16 to 10.5%, while quantizing all 16 residual core projections retains 87.9% and quantizing both gives 10.6%; removing prior-state error feedback (lambda=0) lifts agreement from 10.4% to 69.2%. A norm-matched Gaussian perturbation at the same adapter position only falls to 25.0% — real INT4 rounding is 2.4x more damaging than same-norm noise there.
- **One-step GPTQ loses to round-to-nearest on 5 of 9 checkpoints**, worst on LoopFormer-278M (WikiText-2 PPL 45.91 one-step vs 35.10 RTN vs 31.80 base; trajectory calibration repairs to 34.08). Largest single repair: Parcae-1.3B PPL 51.30 (RTN) / 27.35 (one-step) -> 20.84 (96% of gap closed, base 19.57). Smallest: Ouro-2.6B +1.99 PPL over one-step, 17% closed — recovery is partial and heterogeneous.
- **Calibration blindness quantified:** Huginn adapter thresholded Hessian rank is 43/10,560 dimensions (0.41%) at step 0 vs 335 with 32-step accumulation (7.8x); Ouro-1.4B down_proj is rank 1/5,632 (0.02%) at step 0 vs 974-1,194 at steps 1-3, with step 0 contributing 99.5% of the 4-step trace sum. Huginn WikiText-2: 4-step calibration alone improves PPL 14.67 -> 12.97.
- **Stress and realism checks:** at grouped INT3 on full GSM8K, exact match rises 5.84% (one-step) -> 20.70% (full trajectory) vs 8.11% RTN. Real Marlin W4A16 kernel on A100: 260.9 ms vs 303.9 ms bf16 median latency for a 32-step forward (1.21x, bootstrap CI 1.20-1.23x), 3.27 GB -> 0.86 GB weights (3.80x), with 34.0% (Marlin) vs 35.0% (simulated) GSM8K accuracy confirming simulated numbers transfer. INT8 per-channel stays within 0.01 PPL of bf16 on Huginn.

## Authors & Credibility

Single author, Nux Li (Meta), with correspondence to nux@meta.com. The paper reads as a careful individual research effort: the experimental hygiene is unusually strong for a single-author report (Wilson intervals on small-N exact-match plots, paired-bootstrap CIs on latency, a designed null case in COCONUT, an explicitly reported non-significant result for the INT3 quantized-trajectory control at 84.50% -> 84.95% with CI [-31.3, 31.0] pp). Affiliation with Meta lends access to the evaluated checkpoints (Huginn, R-Llama, Ouro, Parcae, LoopFormer are recent academic looped models), though the single-author format means no co-authors have vetted the claims, and I could not independently verify the author's identity or publication history from the paper itself.

## Critical Assessment

- **The fix is less novel than the diagnosis.** The Related Work section concedes that Hyperloop Transformers (Zeitoun et al., 2026) already "accumulates GPTQ Hessians across loops" in one weight-only INT4 experiment — so trajectory calibration itself is not new; the contribution is the causal decomposition (feedback exposure vs calibration blindness) and the rank/subspace analysis explaining *why* and *when* accumulation helps. The framing is honest, but readers should calibrate expectations accordingly.
- **The headline 102% gap-closed rests on a 2-example margin.** Huginn 35.0% vs bf16 29.9% is 94 vs 92 exact matches out of 1,319; no significance test is reported for this specific comparison (unlike the latency bootstrap CIs). "Recovers bf16-level accuracy" is defensible; "exceeds bf16" would be overclaiming, and to the paper's credit it mostly says the former.
- **Heavy reliance on a proxy metric for mechanism work.** Much of Sections 3-4 uses top-1 agreement with bf16 on 50 GSM8K prompts; the calibration-horizon sweep (Figure 3b) shows Wilson intervals spanning roughly 17-34% at N_cal=1, so the 25% vs 30% horizon effect is within noise at that n. Task-level evidence is thinner than the diagnostic evidence.
- **Method coverage is asymmetric.** AWQ is tested empirically (insensitive to calibration step: 0.01 PPL range vs 0.29 for GPTQ, but only 0.06 PPL over RTN), while SpinQuant and QuIP# are dismissed analytically ("orthogonal transforms and rank-preserving do not restore missing directions in H") without a single run. Given that QuIP#'s rank-preserving claim is precisely the counter-hypothesis, this is the one ablation the paper needs and lacks. No QAT comparison either.
- **The diagnostic is admitted to be incomplete.** Rank gain does not predict recovery magnitude (LoopFormer has ~40x rank gain yet only 31% gap closed; Ouro-2.6B 17%), so a practitioner cannot yet use the rank survey to decide whether trajectory calibration will pay off — only that one-step GPTQ is untrustworthy.
- **Scale and breadth limits.** All checkpoints are <= 4.17B params; the real-kernel evaluation is one GPU, batch 1, 7-token prompts — far from serving conditions for latent-reasoning workloads, which typically iterate deeply on longer prompts.

## Strengths

- Genuine causal rigor: the lambda-injection, identity-bypass, norm-matched-noise, IIR-pole, and Mamba component controls jointly pin the mechanism to *where error enters the recurrence*, not perturbation magnitude. This is the kind of controlled intervention most quantization papers never attempt.
- Statistically honest reporting: Wilson intervals, bootstrap CIs, explicit non-significant results, and footnote-level accounting of exact-match counts (94/92) — rare transparency.
- Includes a designed falsification case: COCONUT (identity paths everywhere, 1.1x rank gain) shows ~0.8 pp gain from trajectory accumulation, exactly as the mechanism predicts.
- Broad architecture sweep (9 checkpoints, 7 architectures) with per-row bases, and both simulated and real-kernel (Marlin W4A16) validation showing simulated INT4 numbers transfer (34.0% vs 35.0%).
- Practical, memorable takeaway: calibration data must span the recurrence trajectory; the fix costs only N forward passes of Hessian collection and leaves the GPTQ solver untouched, so it drops into existing PTQ pipelines.

## Limitations

- Scale is capped at 4.17B parameters; whether feedback exposure worsens for larger looped models is extrapolation, as the authors acknowledge in Limitations.
- Recovery is partial and unpredictable: gap closed ranges from 17% (Ouro-2.6B) to 102% (Huginn), and the paper's own rank-gain diagnostic does not predict which regime a model falls into.
- Evaluation tasks are narrow: GSM8K exact match and WikiText-2 PPL dominate (ARC-C/HellaSwag/LAMBADA relegated to appendices); no long-context or agentic tasks despite latent-reasoning models being marketed for deep iterative reasoning.
- No empirical comparison against rotation-based PTQ (SpinQuant/QuIP#) or QAT; the claim that rank-preserving rotations cannot help is analytical only.
- Trajectory calibration requires N forward passes per calibration set; wall-clock calibration cost is not reported, and the quantized-trajectory variant (collecting Hessians on the INT4 rollout) showed no significant gain at INT3.
- Single-author paper with no code release mentioned in the main text; the 4-line Algorithm 1 plus scattered appendix details would make exact reproduction non-trivial.

## Relevance

这篇论文与你关注的量化与高效推理方向直接相关：它证明了在 looped/latent-reasoning 这类新架构上照搬 GPTQ 式单步标定会系统性失效，并给出一个几乎零成本、可立刻落地的修复（轨迹累积 Hessian）。如果你要在递归深度模型上做 W4A16 部署，这是必读的"避坑 + 修复"参考。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does feedback exposure intensify with scale (>4B params) or with recurrence depth beyond 32, as the authors extrapolate — and is there a scaling law for the adapter-vs-core damage ratio?
2. Would combining trajectory calibration with rank-preserving rotations (QuIP#/SpinQuant-style) close the remaining gap on the weak-recovery models (LoopFormer 31%, Ouro-2.6B 17%), given the paper's analytical claim that rotations cannot restore missing Hessian directions?
3. Can thresholded Hessian rank gain be estimated cheaply at deployment time (e.g., from 2-3 rollout steps) to predict whether trajectory calibration will pay off, since rank gain does not currently predict recovery magnitude?
4. Does the adapter finding motivate a training-time fix — adding a residual (identity) path to the loop-entry adapter — rather than post-hoc PTQ patches?
5. Do the same two failure modes appear in other iterative-compute paradigms such as diffusion LLMs, where timestep-aware calibration (AdaTSQ-style) partially parallels trajectory calibration?
