> **一句话总结：** Aquín Labs 提出把单次前向传播的注意力矩阵当作"路由图"，从答案位置反向走 k=15 宽度、4 跳的回溯保留小子图 S，再用"关掉 S 的注意力并重归一化 vs 关掉等量随机边"的成对消融检验来验证——在 GPT-2 Small 的 induction 任务上，消融 S 使目标 logit 平均下降 4.42（随机边仅 0.09，gap +4.33，95% CI [3.95, 4.72]，Wilcoxon p<10⁻¹⁰），全程只需 1 次前向提取 + 约 12 次前向验证，而逐头 patch 扫描需要 145 次前向（CPU 墙钟 0.48s vs 5.94s，快约一个数量级以上）。

> **TL;DR:** Instead of running expensive intervention searches (ACDC, head-by-head patching) to find circuits, the authors read one forward pass's attention as a routing graph, walk backward from the answer position to keep a small edge set S (edge score = attention weight × value-vector norm, k=15, 4 hops), and verify S by zeroing its attention and measuring the task-score drop against ten size-matched random edge sets. On induction and IOI in GPT-2 Small, GPT-2 Medium, and Pythia-410M (n=100 prompts per cell), the extracted set hurts far more than random — e.g. on GPT-2 Small induction, Δ_S = 4.42 vs Δ_R = 0.09 for random edges (gap +4.33, Wilcoxon p<10⁻¹⁰) — while costing ~1 forward to extract versus 145 forwards for a full head-patch sweep (~two orders of magnitude with verification). It is explicitly a cheap causal sketch, not a complete circuit atlas: it misses known heads, and fails on 19/100 GPT-2 Medium induction examples.

## 核心贡献

- **单次前向的"活体电路"提取管线**：把注意力读取为 token 间的路由图（保留头身份、忽略 BOS sink、跳过自环），边权 s = A[ℓ,h,i,j]·‖V[ℓ,h,j]‖₂（注意力权重 × value 向量范数，即"沿这条路写入了多少信息"），从答案位置反向贪心保留 top-k 入边（k=15、4 跳）得到小子图 S。
- **廉价的因果验证协议**：将 S 中边的注意力置零并重归一化剩余注意力，测量任务分数下降（induction 用目标 logit，IOI 用名字 logit-difference），与 10 组等大小随机边集做配对比较（配对 gap、95% bootstrap CI、单侧 Wilcoxon 符号秩检验）。
- **在已知标准电路上系统验证**：induction 与 IOI × GPT-2 Small / GPT-2 Medium / Pythia-410M 共 6 个 cell，每 cell n=100 干净 prompt，全部 cell gap 显著为正（p<10⁻¹⁰），且诚实报告失败率（GPT-2 Medium induction 失败 19/100）。
- **成本量化**：提取 S 只需 1 次前向，加验证约一打（a dozen）次前向；而逐头 patch 一次扫描需 145 次前向，CPU 墙钟 0.06s / 0.48s / 5.94s（提取 / 提取+验证 / 逐头扫描），约快百倍。
- **清晰的边界声明与消融**：明确不声称完整电路图谱；消融覆盖 k 的取值（k=5 不可靠，k≥10 稳定）、层数选择（只用后层会失败，仅早层即可恢复大部分 gap）、打分方式（乘积分略优于裸注意力但差距很小）、prompt 模板扰动等。

## Key Contributions

- **Live circuit extraction in one forward pass**: read attention as a token-to-token routing graph (keep head identity, ignore the BOS sink, skip self-loops), score each edge by s = A[ℓ,h,i,j]·‖V[ℓ,h,j]‖₂ ("how much was written along this route"), then walk backward from the answer position keeping top-k incoming edges (k=15, four hops) to get a small edge set S.
- **A cheap causal verification protocol**: zero out S's attention weights (renormalizing the remainder), measure the task-score drop (target logit for induction, name logit-difference for IOI), and compare against ten size-matched random edge sets using paired gaps, 95% bootstrap CIs, and one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests.
- **Systematic validation on circuits that are already known**: induction and IOI across GPT-2 Small, GPT-2 Medium, and Pythia-410M — six cells, n=100 clean prompts each — all cells show a positive gap at p<10⁻¹⁰, with failures honestly reported (19/100 failures on GPT-2 Medium induction).
- **Cost quantification**: building S costs one forward; verification adds about a dozen forwards; a head-by-head patch sweep needs 145 forwards — wall-clock on CPU 0.06s / 0.48s / 5.94s (extract / extract+verify / head-patch), roughly a hundred times faster.
- **Explicit scope and ablations**: they do not claim a circuit atlas; ablations cover k (k=5 unreliable, k≥10 stable), layer choice (late layers alone fail; early layers alone recover most of the gap), the scoring function (product score beats raw attention only slightly), and prompt-template perturbations.

## Methodology

The pipeline has three stages, deliberately kept short (the paper's Figure 1):

1. **Read attention as a graph.** Each token position is a node; an edge (ℓ, h, i → j) means query token i attended to key token j at layer ℓ, head h. Head identity is kept (induction requires it), the BOS attention sink is ignored, and self-loops are skipped. Each edge is scored by s = A[ℓ,h,i,j] · ‖V[ℓ,h,j]‖₂, the attention weight times the pre-W_O value-vector norm — a cheap cousin of Kobayashi et al.'s weight × value analysis, kept discrete (no rollout/flow across depth) because mixing makes single-layer weights a poor token-importance probe (their argument against attention rollout). Raw attention alone is kept as a backup score; the product works "a bit better" in their ablations.
2. **Keep a small subgraph.** Starting from the answer position r* (usually the last token), take its top-k incoming edges (default k=15), then repeat for a few hops backward (default four) from the tokens those edges came from. The result is the kept edge set S.
3. **Check that S matters (causal gate).** Turn off the attention weights of edges in S and renormalize what remains; measure how far the task score falls (target logit for induction, name logit-difference for IOI). Repeat with ten random edge sets of size |S|. A sketch "passes" if S hurts clearly more than random. Statistics: per-example paired gap g_i = Δ_S^(i) − Δ_R^(i), a 95% bootstrap CI on the mean gap, and a one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test; a cell uses n=100 clean prompts (single-token names/words after filtering).

Evaluation covers induction (replayed) on GPT-2 Small, GPT-2 Medium, and Pythia-410M, and IOI on all three, with the same recipe everywhere (k=15, four hops, ten random controls). Cost is compared against a head-by-head patch sweep on GPT-2 Small induction.

## Results & Highlights

- **The sketch beats size-matched random ablation in all six cells.** On GPT-2 Small induction, ablating S drops the target logit by 4.42±1.97 versus 0.09±0.18 for random edges of the same size (gap +4.33, 95% bootstrap CI [3.95, 4.72], 0/100 examples fail, Wilcoxon p<10⁻¹⁰). On GPT-2 Small IOI the drop is 5.50±1.31 vs 0.13±0.23 (gap +5.16, 0/100 failures), and on Pythia-410M IOI the gap is the largest at +5.63 (5.63±1.30 vs 0.01±0.09, 0/100 failures).
- **Weaker but positive on bigger induction contexts.** GPT-2 Medium induction has the smallest gap, +1.51 (Δ_S 1.51±1.93 vs Δ_R 0.00±0.06) with 19/100 examples failing (Δ_S ≤ Δ_R); Pythia-410M induction sits in between at gap +2.25 (2.27±1.79 vs 0.03±0.11, 11/100 failures); GPT-2 Medium IOI is +3.05 (12/100 failures). Every cell's CI excludes zero.
- **Roughly two orders of magnitude cheaper than head-patching.** On GPT-2 Small induction: building S = 1 forward, adding verification (ablate S plus ten random sets) ≈ a dozen forwards, versus 145 forwards to sweep every head once; wall-clock on CPU is 0.06s (extract), 0.48s (extract+verify), 5.94s (head-patch) — "roughly a hundred times faster," and a full edge search would be slower still.
- **Partial recovery of known circuits.** The backward walk picks up the famous previous-token and induction heads (L4H11, L5H5, L5H8 appear almost always), but later heads named in the textbook IOI/induction circuits only appear sometimes — a strong causal gap does not mean the full known circuit was recovered end-to-end.
- **Sensitivity profile (GPT-2 Small induction ablations).** k=5 is unreliable while k≥10 is fine (default k=15 stable); an overly harsh score threshold throws away the signal; using only late layers fails while early layers alone recover most of the gap; the product score ranks routes only slightly better than raw attention; changing the prompt template or padding filler sentences does not kill the gap.

## Authors & Credibility

- **Ash Manvi** and **Samreena Tajreen**, both at **Aquín Labs** (independent research lab; the work was produced at Aquín Labs per the acknowledgments). No prior-publication list is given in the paper, and this is a short (7-page) workshop-style writeup rather than a full conference paper.
- Credibility signals are strong for a short paper: code, configs, and run artifacts are public (github.com/Aquinf03/live-circuit-routing), including all six bootstrap intervals and per-setting ablation gaps; the statistical protocol (paired gaps, bootstrap CIs, Wilcoxon tests, n=100 per cell) is above the bar for mechanistic-interpretability case studies; and the paper is unusually candid about what it does not show (Section 4, "What we are not saying").
- The evaluated models (GPT-2 Small/Medium, Pythia-410M) and tasks (induction, IOI) are the standard public benchmarks of the field, with correct attribution to Olsson et al. (induction heads) and Wang et al. (IOI in GPT-2 Small).

## Critical Assessment

- **The authors themselves flag the paper's biggest confound, and it is substantive**: the random control samples edges uniformly, whereas S is built from high-attention edges, so S removes far more total attention mass than a random set of the same size. Part of every reported gap is therefore a "mass effect" (zeroing more attention mass hurts more under renormalization) rather than evidence that the specific routing structure matters. A mass-matched control (random edges weighted by attention mass, or matched-random-head controls) would shrink the gap "by an amount we have not measured" — i.e., the headline +4.33/+5.63 gaps are upper bounds inflated by an unquantified amount. This is honest but leaves the central claim only partially established.
- **The baseline is only uniform-random edges.** There is no comparison at matched compute against attention rollout, information-flow routes (Ferrando & Voita), or a lightweight ACDC variant, so "cheap sketch with real causal signal" is never positioned against the cheapest non-trivial alternative — only against the most expensive one (head-patching), which makes the cost win look bigger than the methodological win.
- **Scope is narrow even by small-model standards**: two synthetic tasks whose circuits are already fully documented (induction, IOI) and three small models (max 410M params). The paper admits transfer to larger models or messier behavior is open — so the practical value of a "live" sketch on real tasks remains untested, and |S| grows with prompt length, so fixed k and hop depth will eventually under-cover.
- **The score ablation is thin**: the value-norm product beats raw attention only by a small margin (their own admission), and no third alternative (e.g., Kobayashi-style ‖α·f(x)‖ with post-value transforms, or gradient-based edge scores) is tried. The claimed advantage of the "how much was written" score is not convincingly demonstrated.
- **To the authors' credit**: failure cases are quantified per cell (19/100 on Medium induction with residual modes named: distractor latching, fixed hop budget, attention-mass≠causation), the intervention style (zero + renormalize) is explicitly disclaimed as one choice among several, and no monosemanticity or completeness claims are made. This is a model of scope discipline for a small paper.

## Strengths

- Extremely cheap: one forward pass to extract, ~a dozen to verify, versus 145 forwards for a head sweep — and the verification gate is itself a contribution (a sketch is only reported when it passes a paired causal test).
- Statistically careful for its genre: n=100 per cell, paired per-example gaps, bootstrap CIs that all exclude zero, Wilcoxon p<10⁻¹⁰ everywhere, and released run artifacts.
- Honest and well-scoped: explicit "what we are not saying" section, per-cell failure counts, named failure modes, and the mass-effect confound disclosed rather than hidden.
- Useful ablations: layer choice (early layers carry the induction gap; late-layers-only fails), k sensitivity, threshold sensitivity, prompt-template robustness.
- Reproducible: public code, configs, and evaluation artifacts on GitHub.

## Limitations

- The uniform-random control makes the gap partially an attention-mass artifact; no mass-matched control is measured, so the true "routing" effect size is unknown.
- Only induction and IOI — two synthetic tasks with already-known circuits — on three small models (GPT-2 Small/Medium, Pythia-410M); no real-world tasks, no modern-scale models, and transfer is explicitly open.
- Recovery is partial: famous early heads (L4H11, L5H5, L5H8) recur, but later known heads often missing; the sketch is not the textbook circuit even when it passes.
- Nontrivial failure rates on some cells (19/100 on GPT-2 Medium induction, 11–12/100 elsewhere), with the fixed hop budget and distractor latching unaddressed beyond diagnosis.
- |S| grows with prompt length, so fixed k=15 and four hops will under-cover longer contexts; the BOS ban and value-norm score are hand-designed patches for attention-mass ≠ causation rather than a principled fix.
- Not comparable to discovery engines: no feature naming, no SAEs, no completeness or necessity claims per edge — deliberately, but this limits what the output is useful for.

## Relevance

这篇论文与用户对 LLM 可解释性与高效归因方法的兴趣高度契合：它把"电路发现"的成本从数百次前向干预压到一次前向加一次廉价的因果检验，给出了一条可作为大规模模型筛前候选路由的实用捷径。同时它对注意力质量混淆（mass effect vs routing effect）的诚实讨论，对任何想做注意力归因研究的人都是有价值的警示。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of each reported gap survives under a mass-matched random control (edges sampled proportional to attention mass, or random-head controls with matched total mass)? The authors say the gap would shrink by "an amount we have not measured" — measuring it is the obvious next experiment.
- Does the extract-then-verify sketch transfer to a real task (e.g., a factual-recall or classification behavior) or a modern 1–7B model, where |S| scaling with prompt length may break the fixed k=15 / 4-hop budget?
- How does the sketch compare at matched compute against attention rollout, information-flow routes, or a budget-limited ACDC run, rather than only against full head-patching?
- Would a different intervention style (mean ablation, activation patching) agree with the zero-and-renormalize verdicts on the same edge sets, as the authors themselves suggest might not hold?
- Can the failure modes be fixed mechanistically — e.g., adaptive hop budgets for long paths, or distractor rejection — to close the 19/100 failure rate on GPT-2 Medium induction?
