Why this matters

Single-step GPTQ calibration silently fails on looped, latent-reasoning architectures — this near-zero-cost rollout fix is essential reading for W4A16 deployment.

Critical edge

“Exceeds bf16” rests on a two-example margin — 94 vs 92 of 1,319 — with no significance test; “recovers bf16” is the defensible claim.

Methodology / how the case is built

  1. Testbed. 9 checkpoints across 7 looped architectures (Huginn-3.5B, Ouro, R-Llama, Parcae, LoopFormer, PonderPythia, Nanbeige; up to 4.17B). Grouped INT4 (g=128) via FakeQuant; calibrate on 50 GSM8K prompts, evaluate on 1,319 held-out 8-shot generations + WikiText-2 PPL.

  2. Isolate feedback exposure. Adapter vs 16 residual core projections quantized separately; λ-controlled injection (λ=0 removes feedback: 10.4%→69.2%), identity-bypass holdout (−37.4% RMS divergence), norm-matched noise arm, IIR-filter pole analysis, Mamba-2.8B component controls.

  3. Quantify calibration blindness. Thresholded Hessian rank (#λi > 10−3·λmax) tracked per step, with 1,000-prompt and cross-layer controls. Step 0: rank 43; 32-step accumulation: 335 (7.8×).

  4. Fix: trajectory calibration. H_deploy = Σt XtTXt over the bf16 rollout, fed to the unchanged GPTQ solver; calibration horizon swept 1/8/32; INT3 quantized-trajectory robustness arm.

  5. Real-kernel check. Marlin W4A16 on one A100, batch 1, paired-bootstrap 95% CIs; simulated-vs-kernel accuracy cross-checked on full GSM8K.

Strengths

  • Causal rigor: five independent interventions (λ-injection, identity-bypass, norm-matched noise, IIR poles, Mamba controls) pin damage to where error enters the recurrence, not its magnitude.
  • Statistical honesty: Wilson intervals, bootstrap CIs, explicitly reported non-significant results, footnote-level exact-match accounting — rare transparency.
  • Designed falsification case: COCONUT (identity paths everywhere, 1.1× rank gain) shows ~0 gain from accumulation, exactly as the mechanism predicts.
  • Breadth + realism: 9 checkpoints / 7 architectures with per-row bases; simulated and real-kernel results agree.
  • Drop-in fix: costs N extra forward passes of Hessian collection; GPTQ solver untouched; compatible with existing PTQ and Marlin inference stacks.

Limitations

  • Scale capped at 4.17B — whether feedback exposure worsens for larger looped models is extrapolation.
  • Recovery is unpredictable: gap closed ranges 17%–102%, and the paper’s own rank-gain diagnostic does not predict which regime a model lands in (LoopFormer: ~40× gain, only 31%).
  • Narrow evaluation: GSM8K and WikiText-2 dominate; no long-context or agentic tasks despite latent-reasoning models being built for deep iterative reasoning.
  • Missing ablation: SpinQuant/QuIP# dismissed analytically, never run; no QAT comparison.
  • Costs underreported: calibration wall-clock for N forward passes not given; the INT3 quantized-trajectory variant showed no significant gain.
  • Single author, no code release — exact reproduction from a 4-line Algorithm 1 plus appendix would be non-trivial.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does feedback exposure intensify with scale (>4B) or depth (>32 recursions) — is there a scaling law for the adapter-vs-core damage ratio?
  2. Would rank-preserving rotations (QuIP#/SpinQuant-style) close the remaining gap on weak-recovery models, against the paper’s analytical claim that they cannot?
  3. Can thresholded Hessian rank gain be estimated cheaply (2–3 rollout steps) to predict whether trajectory calibration will pay off?
  4. Should the loop-entry adapter get a training-time residual (identity) path instead of post-hoc PTQ patches?
  5. Do the same two failure modes appear in diffusion LLMs, where timestep-aware calibration (AdaTSQ-style) partially parallels trajectory calibration?
