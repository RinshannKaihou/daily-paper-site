> **一句话总结：** 阿里 Qwen 团队发布原生全模态智能体模型 Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash：在 Qwen3.8-Next 混合稀疏 MoE 骨干上加装视觉/AuT/空间 AuT 三个编码器与 QSA 稀疏注意力，把上下文窗口扩到 1M token，用"多教师蒸馏 + 统一强化学习（按任务结果而非自报完成度给奖励）"的后训练配方，使其在 WildClawBench-MM 上从上一代 Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus 的 34.5 跃升至 71.0（+36.5 分），并在 OmniVideoBench 上以智能体模式实现准确率 63.4→67.8 的同时把每查询 token 消耗降低 45.7%（145,736→79,117），配合开源的 Qwen-MM-Plugins 与 Qwen-Live-Harness 支撑视频剪辑、会议纪要、音乐 MV 生成等长时程多模态生产力工作流。

> **TL;DR:** Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash is a natively omni-modal MoE agent model (Thinker-Talker architecture, 1M-token context, QSA sparse attention, vision + AuT + spatial-AuT encoders) trained with multi-teacher distillation followed by unified RL where rewards come from task outcomes, not self-reported completion. The headline result: on the multimodal tool-use benchmark WildClawBench-MM it scores 71.0 vs 34.5 for its predecessor Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus (+36.5 pts, ahead of Gemini 3.8 Flash's 58.9), and agentic evidence-gathering lifts OmniVideoBench from 63.4 to 67.8 while cutting tokens per query by 45.7% (145,736→79,117). Text coding ability is preserved (SWE-bench Pro 63.3 vs Qwen3.8-Flash's 62.5; LiveCodeBench v6 92.6, best among listed), multi-speaker ASR improves sharply (AlMeeting DER 8.8→3.4, cpWER 89.6→12.2), and realtime speech runs at ~6.5× real time (RTF 0.153, TTFT 0.98–1.03 s). Worth reading if you care about omni-models as agents rather than perceivers; skim if you only need text-reasoning SOTA — Claude-Opus-4.6 still leads HLE (40.0 vs 36.5).

## 核心贡献

- **原生全模态智能体定位**：与以往"以感知为主"的 omni 模型不同，本工作把多模态理解与推理、长时程智能体任务执行放在同等核心位置，目标是真实多模态生产力场景（视频剪辑、影视配音翻译、长视频解说、会议纪要、音乐 MV 生成等）。
- **架构升级**：继承 Qwen3-Omni 的 Thinker-Talker 架构，Backbone 换为 Qwen3.8-Next 混合稀疏 MoE（Gated DeltaNet 与全注意力交错），通过轻量 indexer 的 QSA 稀疏注意力将原生 256k 预训练上下文扩展至 **1M token**；新增 Spatial AuT 编码器（处理一阶 Ambisonics 多声道空间音频，复数 STFT 保留相位/幅度关系），通用 AuT 编码器以 6.25 Hz（约 160 ms/token）产出音频表征。
- **"多教师蒸馏 + 统一 RL"后训练配方**：先用领域专家教师（各自从 Qwen3.8 checkpoint 经 SFT+RL 特化）生成轨迹做蒸馏，再跨文本/视觉/音频/混合模态做统一 RL；奖励由**任务执行结果**而非模型自报完成度决定，专门缓解长对话中的语言混杂、人格漂移和指令退化。
- **信息抽象模块 + 开源框架**：Omni-Caption、Omni-Video2Note、Omni-Memory、Omni-Skill-Creator 四个模块将原始音视频压缩为可按需访问的结构化摘要/技能，估算每小时音频与音视频内容的 API 输入成本分别下降 **>98% 与 >93%**；以 Qwen-MM-Plugins 插件框架和 Qwen-Live-Harness 实时交互框架（异步工具调用、主动交互、持久记忆）开源。
- **Omni Autoresearch 案例验证**：让模型在 12 小时内自主改进 Qwen2.5-Omni-3B 的四川话语音识别——自行定指标、听音频、诊断错误、构造 3,413 条训练数据并迭代 4 轮，CER 从 25.79% 降至 15.30%（相对降低约 40.7%）。
- **全面评测**：覆盖文本/视觉/音频/音视听/全模态智能体/实时交互约 30+ 个基准，报告相对上一代 29 项评估平均 **+25%**，同时如实列出落后项（如 HLE 低于 Claude-Opus-4.6、多项公开 ASR 基准回退）。

## Key Contributions

- **Repositioning omni models as native agents**: the model combines multimodal perception with long-horizon planning, temporal reasoning, and quality evaluation of generated multimodal content, targeting video-centric productivity workflows rather than perception-only benchmarks.
- **Scalable architecture**: Qwen3.8-Next hybrid sparse MoE backbone (Gated DeltaNet + interleaved attention) with Qwen Sparse Attention (QSA) trained via a 4-stage schedule (encoder alignment → 2.5T-token general stage → QSA warmup → joint QSA training), yielding a 1M-token context window; a new Spatial AuT encoder handles first-order-ambisonics multichannel audio alongside a 6.25 Hz general AuT encoder and the Qwen3.8-Next vision encoder.
- **Multi-Teacher Distillation + unified RL post-training**: domain-specialist teacher trajectories are distilled into one model to reduce cross-modal/cross-domain interference; RL then jointly optimizes reasoning, coding, agentic execution, and multi-turn interaction quality with outcome-determined rewards.
- **System-level contributions**: open-source Qwen-MM-Plugins (Omni-Caption, Omni-Video2Note, Omni-Memory, Omni-Skill-Creator) abstracts audiovisual content so agent harnesses avoid feeding entire raw A/V streams, cutting per-hour audio and audiovisual API input cost by >98% and >93%; Qwen-Live-Harness adds async tool use, proactive interaction, and persistent memory on top of the Realtime Talker (RTF ≈ 0.153, ≈6.5× real time).
- **Autoresearch demonstration**: the model autonomously built a Sichuan-dialect ASR fine-tune for Qwen2.5-Omni-3B in 12 hours (3,413 training examples, 4 experiment rounds), reducing CER from 25.79% to 15.30%.

## Methodology

**Architecture.** Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash keeps the Qwen-Omni Thinker-Talker design. The Thinker uses a hybrid sparse MoE language backbone from Qwen3.8-Next that interleaves Gated DeltaNet layers (a fixed-size recurrent state summarizing context) with full-attention layers; the attention layers transition to Qwen Sparse Attention (QSA), where a lightweight indexer scores compressed micro-block representations to select relevant context blocks while core attention still runs on original tokens within selected blocks. Pretraining uses a native 262,144-token context, extended to 1M tokens after post-training. Three perception encoders — the Qwen3.8-Next vision encoder (images + sampled video frames), a general AuT encoder (16 kHz audio → 128-channel mel-spectrogram → conv frontend ×16 downsampling → temporal self-attention, outputting contextualized tokens at 6.25 Hz ≈ 1 token/160 ms, pretrained on a 20+ language ASR-supervised corpus), and a new Spatial AuT encoder (first-order ambisonics input processed via complex-valued STFT preserving inter-channel amplitude/phase for source direction, distance, and motion) — are projected into the Thinker's shared representation space. Position IDs give audio/spatial tokens explicit temporal indices analogous to visual patches; dynamic attention-window training supports both streaming and offline inference.

**Pretraining (4 stages, native seq length 262,144).** (S1) Encoder alignment: LLM initialized from Qwen3.8-Next, vision encoder from Qwen3.5, with the three encoders trained against the frozen LLM. (S2) General stage: ~2.5 trillion tokens (text 1.1T, audio 0.7T, image 0.35T, video 0.15T, video-audio 0.3T); all parameters unfrozen, mixing unimodal and cross-modal data from early on. (S3) QSA warmup: backbone frozen, the QSA indexer is trained using full-attention distributions as supervision while dense attention remains active. (S4) QSA stage: sparse attention enabled, backbone and indexer jointly trained. Language coverage: 201 text varieties, 113 speech-input varieties (74 languages + 39 Chinese dialects), 36 speech-output varieties (29 languages + 7 Chinese dialects).

**Post-training (2 stages).** (1) Multi-Teacher Distillation: specialist teachers are initialized from the pretrained Qwen3.8 checkpoint and independently optimized via SFT and RL on narrow domains (instruction following, reasoning, coding, agentic tasks, visual/audio understanding); their trajectories are combined into one multimodal mixture and distilled into a single student, which the authors argue gives more consistent supervision and less cross-modal interference than direct SFT on a heterogeneous mixture. (2) Unified RL: jointly spans text, vision, audio, mixed-modality dialogue, and multi-turn interaction scenarios; reward signals evaluate both task-level correctness and interaction quality (cross-modal consistency, style/persona stability, instruction following over long conversations). For long-horizon agentic tasks, rewards are determined by task outcomes and execution results rather than the model's self-reported completion.

**Agentic applications.** Qwen-MM-Plugins modules (all open source) convert audiovisual streams into abstracted, on-demand-accessible artifacts: Omni-Caption (detailed captions), Omni-Video2Note (structured summaries), Omni-Memory (lazy loading of A/V context for long-horizon QA), Omni-Skill-Creator (SOP-style videos → executable agent skills). These operate either with a native multimodal main agent or as a sub-agent invoked from text-only harnesses. Documented workflows include Music2MV, short-drama translation (speaker diarization → voice cloning → dubbing → mix), 2–3 hour movie commentary, omni-deep-research with cross-checks against web sources, and Omni Autoresearch.

**Realtime interaction.** The Talker (inherited from Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus) predicts Residual Vector Quantization tokens with a Multi-Token Prediction module, uses ARIA for interleaved text/speech streaming, and upgrades the causal Code2Wav decoder from 24 kHz to 48 kHz. A five-stage Talker pipeline (pretraining → CPT with contextual corpora → MOPD multi-teacher on-policy distillation for multilingual speech → speaker fine-tuning → GSPO RL with rewards from the aligned Thinker) targets expressive, low-latency spoken responses. Qwen-Live-Harness wraps this with asynchronous sub-agent delegation (Qwen Code, Codex, Claude Code backends via a unified adapter), proactive perceptual monitoring, and persistent memory.

## Results & Highlights

- **Omni-agentic tool use (the paper's headline claim):** on WildClawBench-MM (Claude Code harness), Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash scores **71.0 vs 34.5 for predecessor Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus (+36.5 pts)** and beats Gemini 3.8 Flash (58.9) and Seed 2.0 Lite (41.9); AgenticVBench rises 14.5→36.8 (+22.3) and OmniGAIA 57.2→74.0 (+16.8), though Gemini 3.8 Flash still leads those two (45.0 and 78.6).
- **Agentic execution beats static inference with fewer tokens:** with the Qwen Code agentic setting, OmniVideoBench improves **63.4→67.8 (+4.4)**, Video-MME-v2 65.0→71.3, LVOmniBench 63.3→73.6, while token consumption per query drops **45.7% (145,736→79,117)**; the paper honestly notes this does not establish end-to-end latency/cost reduction.
- **Text capabilities are preserved, not sacrificed:** SWE-bench Pro **63.3 vs 62.5 for the text-only Qwen3.8-Flash** (and 53.4 for Claude-Opus-4.6 Max); LiveCodeBench v6 **92.6** (highest listed; Qwen3.8-Flash 91.9); IFBench 81.5 (highest); DeepSWE 1.1 57.8 slightly below Qwen3.8-Flash's 58.7; on GPQA Diamond it scores 91.0 vs Claude-Opus-4.6's 91.3 and on HLE 36.5 vs 40.0 — general reasoning is competitive but not SOTA.
- **Audio-visual understanding jumps over the previous generation:** OmniVideoBench 53.8→63.4, Video-MME-v2 **47.9→65.0** (Gemini 3.8 Flash: 71.0), LVOmniBench 53.2→63.3; OmniVChat-Bench **85.0 vs 52.1**; ODUbench 91.64|89.57 vs 74.69|66.62; OmniEchoBench (audio-visual spatial intelligence) 23.3→41.7; DailyOmni 85.1 ties the predecessor and edges Gemini's 84.0.
- **Multi-speaker ASR is the largest audio win:** on AlMeeting Test, **DER falls 8.8→3.4 and cpWER 89.6→12.2** versus Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus, with the lowest errors among listed models on AISHELL-4, MagicData-RAMC, and MLC-SLM; Mul.A-Bench improves 61.00→72.60 (Gemini 73.43); but public general ASR regresses — FLEURS-ASR WER 9.3 vs the predecessor's 7.2 and OmnilIngua-LongAudioASR WER 6.89→9.27.
- **Realtime efficiency:** text throughput 81.06–84.89 TPS, time-to-first-text **0.98–1.03 s** (audio-only input), time-to-first-audio-chunk 1.21–1.35 s (audio-video), generation RTF ≈ 0.153 (≈6.5× real time); voice cloning wins are mostly on in-house suites (e.g., LongSpeechGeneration WER 1.83|similarity 0.693 vs predecessor's 6.30|0.597; PhonePronunciation 93.3% vs Gemini 3.1 TTS Flash's 86.6%), with a competitive 72.7% human ABX win rate vs Gemini's 74.2%.
- **Aggregate claim:** an increase of **more than 25% in average score across 29 evaluations** spanning reasoning, audiovisual reasoning, and audiovisual agent tasks versus Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus, with estimated per-hour API input costs down >98% (audio) and >93% (audiovisual) via the abstraction modules.

## Authors & Credibility

This is a corporate technical report authored by the **Qwen Team at Alibaba** (six core contributors listed alphabetically — including Bing Han, Hao Wang, Qize Yang, Xiong Wang, Yuanjun Lv, Zhifang Guo — plus ~150 contributors), the group behind the Qwen, Qwen2.5-Omni, Qwen3-Omni, and Qwen3.8-Next model families. The team has a strong track record of reproducible open releases, and this report continues that: Qwen-MM-Plugins and Qwen-Live-Harness are open-sourced on GitHub, and evaluation harness/configuration details (temperature, top_p, context window, harness per benchmark) are documented in table footnotes. Credibility is boosted by unusually candid negative results (FLEURS-ASR regression, Gemini leading on several A/V reasoning and interaction benchmarks, Seed 2.0 Lite ahead on ProactiveVideoQA). Caveats: no per-author contribution information, several headline voice benchmarks are in-house, and the model's parameter count is not disclosed.

## Critical Assessment

- **Benchmark asymmetry between in-house and public evaluations.** The strongest voice-cloning and custom-voice numbers (SpeechSuperClue, Accented Voice Cloning, LongSpeechGeneration, PhonePronunciation — all marked with * as in-house) are exactly where Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash-Realtime wins, while on public multilingual suites it regresses: FLEURS-ASR WER is 9.3 vs the previous generation's 7.2, FLEURS-S2TT 31.8 trails both Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus (32.2) and Gemini 3.8 Flash (33.0), and VoiceBench (88.8) and WildSpeech (72.5) sit below both the predecessor and Gemini. The realtime speech story should be read as "in-house benchmarks plus honest public regressions," not across-the-board superiority.
- **Harness confounds in the agentic comparisons.** WildClawBench-MM is run with Claude Code, UniClawBench with OpenClaw, and OmniGAIA without any harness — the authors acknowledge these are distinct execution settings, but cross-model numbers still entangle harness effects with model ability. On SWE-bench Pro, all competitor models except Claude-Opus-4.6 were re-evaluated on the authors' own "refined" benchmark with corrected probabilistic tasks — favorable re-benchmarking that inflates comparability concerns. The +36.5 on WildClawBench-MM is nonetheless large enough that it likely survives these caveats.
- **"Native agent" advantage is partial, and the paper says so.** Under the agentic Qwen Code setting, Gemini 3.8 Flash also improves (OmniVideoBench 65.2→70.1, Video-MME-v2 71.0→72.7) and keeps the top agentic scores on two of the three benchmarks; the token-efficiency table carries an explicit disclaimer that reduced token count "does not establish a corresponding reduction in end-to-end latency or monetary cost."
- **Missing ablations and undisclosed scale.** The 4-stage pretraining and the distillation→RL pipeline are described only narratively: there is no ablation isolating QSA vs dense attention, the Spatial AuT encoder's marginal contribution, or distillation-only vs distillation+RL, and the Thinker's parameter count is never stated in the main text — a reproducibility gap for a report whose predecessor (Qwen3-Omni, 2604.15894) did disclose scale.
- **Small editorial quirks.** Table 1 is titled "Supported languages ... in Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash-Plus," a model name that appears nowhere else in the report; and in MathVision/CharXiv the authors' model receives a fixed step-by-step + boxed-answer prompt while baselines get the max over runs with/without that instruction — arguably generous to baselines, but it makes those two rows non-uniformly measured. The Omni Autoresearch CER result (25.79%→15.30%) is evaluated on the model's own self-constructed Sichuan-dialect test set, so it demonstrates workflow capability more than external validity.

## Strengths

- The breadth of evaluation is exceptional — roughly 30+ benchmarks spanning text coding/agents, vision agents, multilingual and multi-speaker ASR, audio-visual understanding/reasoning/captioning/interaction, realtime interaction, and voice cloning, with per-benchmark harness and decoding configurations documented.
- The gains concentrate exactly where the thesis is: multimodal agentic execution (+36.5 WildClawBench-MM, +22.3 AgenticVBench vs predecessor) and token efficiency (+4.4 accuracy with −45.7% tokens/query on OmniVideoBench).
- The multi-teacher distillation → outcome-rewarded RL recipe is a credible, clearly motivated answer to cross-modal interference and self-reported-completion reward hacking in long-horizon tasks.
- System-level thinking: information-abstraction modules (Omni-Caption/Video2Note/Memory/Skill-Creator) attack the real bottleneck (video token budgets) rather than just the model, and both plugin and realtime-harness frameworks are open-sourced.
- Honest reporting: regressions vs the previous generation and losses to Gemini/Seed/Claude are printed in the same tables as the wins.

## Limitations

- General reasoning is not state of the art: HLE 36.5 vs Claude-Opus-4.6's 40.0, GPQA Diamond 91.0 vs 91.3, and several text agent benchmarks (DeepSWE 57.8, SWE-bench Multilingual 80.5) sit slightly below the text-only Qwen3.8-Flash (58.7, 81.0).
- Public general-purpose ASR/translation regressions (FLEURS-ASR 9.3 WER, LongAudioASR WER 6.89→9.27, WenetSpeech Net 4.8 vs 3.7) persist even as multi-speaker ASR improves dramatically.
- Long-horizon audio reasoning still trails Gemini 3.8 Flash where it matters for evidence use (Long AudioSpan chain score 71.8 vs 84.2), and Seed 2.0 Lite leads ProactiveVideoQA (68.3 vs 61.5).
- No parameter count, no training-compute disclosure, and no component-level ablations; several headline speech benchmarks are in-house and not independently verifiable.
- The autoresearch case study (Sichuan dialect CER −40.7% relative) is a single, self-scored demonstration rather than a repeatable protocol.

## Relevance

这篇报告是"omni 模型从感知器走向智能体"这一趋势目前最完整的工程实证：1M 上下文 + 信息抽象模块 + 按任务结果计奖的 RL，正对应多模态长视频理解、agent 工具调用与长时程规划等前沿方向。对关注多模态 agent 架构、语音-视频联合建模与实时交互系统的读者，这是一份值得精读（并可直接试用其开源插件框架）的参考实现。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of the WildClawBench-MM gain (+36.5) comes from the model versus the Claude Code harness — would a harness-controlled, same-harness comparison across all models preserve the ordering?
- What is the marginal contribution of the Spatial AuT encoder and of QSA (vs the Qwen3.8-Next dense baseline) on the audio-visual and long-context benchmarks, given that no ablations are reported?
- Can the information-abstraction plugins (Omni-Caption, Omni-Memory) be shown to be lossless enough for evidence-critical tasks, i.e., does answer accuracy on Long AudioSpan-type benchmarks degrade when evidence must pass through the caption abstraction rather than raw frames?
- The public general ASR regressions (FLEURS-ASR, LongAudioASR) coexist with large multi-speaker gains — is this an explicit trade-off in the training mixture, and can it be recovered without sacrificing multi-speaker performance?
- Does the outcome-rewarded RL recipe transfer to settings where execution results are noisy or delayed (e.g., film-editing quality), and how sensitive is it to reward-hacking via degenerate but "successful" task outcomes?
