Why this matters

Pinpoints why 2-bit models fall into repetition loops on long reasoning — and ships a cheap, bolt-on fix: directly usable for edge-deployed quantized LLMs.

The fix is 245–260 optimizer steps and 11–57 GPU-hours — small enough to append to any existing QAD pipeline.

Critical edge

No β ablation, no pure-RL baseline — dense reverse-KL guidance vs verifier reward alone stays unquantified: the one missing ablation that would nail the mechanism.

Also: evidence covers only 0.6B–4B models in non-thinking mode, and the strongest-baseline (Q-Palette) numbers rest on the authors’ own reimplementation.

Stage 1 · QAD — reproduce EdgeRazor, teacher-forced

  • Weights quantized per 256-channel block to ternary {−1,0,1} (≈1.58 bits) or INT4; mixing fraction p=0.5 → 2.79 eff. bits, p=0.125 → 1.88; INT8 activations, INT4 embeddings/lm_head.
  • Online logit-KL from the student’s own BF16 twin (0.6B model substitutes Qwen3-1.7B as teacher); 6,400–12,800 steps on a general instruction mix.

Stage 2 · OPD — train where the quantized model goes

  • Completions sampled through the frozen quantized forward path — the exact deployment path — not fixed corpus prefixes.
  • Frozen BF16 teacher gives reverse-KL token guidance on those student prefixes: estimable from student samples, penalizes student-favored but teacher-rejected continuations.
  • Plus group-relative verifier rewards (math: answer correctness · code: tests pass); β=1 in every run, never tuned.
  • Math phase 80–140 steps → code phase 80–150 steps; verl + vLLM, temperature 1, checkpoints picked every 20 steps.
Diagnosis being repaired. QAD retention decays monotonically with required generation length — the longer the model must generate, the more its own quantized drift compounds. QA9 86% · GSM8K 56% · MBPP 59% · MATH-500 27% · AMC23 16%

03 Strengths

  • Falsifiable mechanism backed by three independent evidence types: length-stratified retention decay, loop/exhaustion behavior, and a budget-matched intervention.
  • The matched-budget ablation is the best asset — wins all 16 cells, and the null result of scaling QAD 2–3× rules out “just add data”.
  • Cheap to adopt: 245–260 steps and 11–57 GPU-hours restore what 6,400–12,800 QAD steps could not — bolt-on for any QAD pipeline.
  • Rare baseline care: all arms share the same INT8/INT4 deployment setting; width mismatches documented — some in the baselines’ favor.

04 Limitations

  • Scale & mode: only 0.6B–4B models, non-thinking, temperature-1 rollouts; 7B+ models and 10k-token thinking traces untested.
  • β fixed at 1, no pure-RL-from-QAD arm — teacher-KL vs verifier-reward contribution unquantified.
  • Q-Palette comparison rests on a reimplementation (official code is Llama-only); cross-family baseline numbers are inferred, not measured.
  • Train/test overlap: GSM8K train → GSM8K test, MATH L1–3 → MATH-500; the clean out-of-corpus signal is AMC23 — on only 16 problems.
  • Efficiency claim is scoped to OPD: the QAD stage (14–23× the cost) still dominates the total pipeline.

05 Questions for Further Exploration

  • Q1How much of the gain is the dense reverse-KL term vs verifier reward alone — would a β sweep or a GRPO-from-QAD arm change the ranking?
  • Q2Does OPD stabilize 10k+ token thinking-mode traces, where repetition dynamics and budget exhaustion bite hardest?
  • Q3Is there a size threshold below which a model’s own BF16 copy is too weak a teacher — and how does OPD behave near that margin?
  • Q4Does the on-policy advantage persist at milder 4–8 bit quantization, or is it specific to the sub-3-bit regime?
  • Q5Could the teacher itself be quantized to the student’s bit-width, making recovery fully low-bit at training time?
