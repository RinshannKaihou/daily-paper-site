> **一句话总结：** 提出 LOCKR：用扩散语言模型（dLLM）去噪过程的隐状态轨迹（BiGRU 风险检测器）识别"稳定但错误"的早期锁定（SBW lock-in），在判定高风险时从固定的 8 个"检查点-重掩码"动作库中做定点修复，再用轨迹感知验证器选出最优续写分支；在 DiffusionGemma/LLaDA-2 与 MetaMathQA/Orca-Math/NuminaMath 上，匹配表面特征的受控基准下检测 AUROC 达 77.3–83.9%（Surface-18 逻辑回归仅 53.6–68.4%），自然分布下仅修复 22–41% 样本、1.8–2.6× 去噪开销即换来 +2.21~5.37 个百分点的准确率提升。

> **TL;DR:** LOCKR treats selective reasoning repair in diffusion LMs as a lightweight test-time planning problem. A BiGRU over random-projected multi-layer, multi-step hidden-state trajectories predicts "stable-but-wrong (SBW) lock-in" risk at the midpoint checkpoint; high-risk generations get targeted answer-region remasking from a fixed bank of B=8 checkpoint-remasking actions, and a trajectory-aware verifier plus gradient-boosted selector picks which counterfactual continuation to keep. On the surface-matched MetaMathQA benchmark, the full trajectory reaches 83.0 AUROC vs 62.9 for a Surface-18 logistic-regression detector (+20.1); end-to-end on DiffusionGemma it hits 67.9% on MetaMathQA at 3.1× relative denoising cost, +6.0 pp over the strongest non-LOCKR baseline, and gains +2.21–5.37 pp on natural distributions across all five model–dataset settings while repairing only 22–41% of examples.

## 核心贡献

- **识别并量化 SBW 锁定现象**：定义"早期锁定"（中点检查点附近答案窗口内答案全部一致）并区分正确/错误锁定；发现错误锁定并不罕见——在 5 个模型×数据集正式设置中占已处理生成的 **14.9%–60.5%**，且表面信号（置信度、熵、边际、答案稳定性）在正确与错误锁定上几乎无差别。
- **表面匹配的受控评测协议**：借鉴定因果推断中的倾向得分匹配（caliper + 加权距离），用 18 维表面统计（Surface-18）一对一配对正确/错误锁定样本，把平均/最大标准化均值差（SMD）从 0.617/0.934 压到 0.016/0.065（所有设置 max < 0.085），从而公平检验隐状态是否携带表面信号之外的正确性信息。
- **LOCKR 规划器**：把选择性修复形式化为轻量测试时规划问题——前缀隐状态轨迹决定"是否干预"（风险阈值 τ），固定动作库 B=8 决定"在哪里、多强地重掩码"（保留大部分已有草稿，只重掩码答案相关区域），修复轨迹分数 + 任务上下文 + 池内一致性经直方图梯度提升选择器决定"保留哪个续写"。
- **系统性实证**：隐状态轨迹在检测（AUROC）与修复分支选择上均稳定优于表面统计和单快照隐状态；端到端在 2 个 dLLM 家族 × 3 个数学基准上一致提升，并给出诚实的"修复空间覆盖"分析：检测强不代表收益大，还取决于动作库是否包含正确续写。

## Key Contributions

- **Characterizes SBW lock-in** as a recurring dLLM failure mode (14.9%–60.5% of processed generations across five model–dataset settings) and introduces a surface-matched evaluation protocol that removes the confound that a detector could win by exploiting confidence/stability differences rather than reasoning-dynamics information.
- **Formulates selective reasoning repair as test-time local planning**: a trajectory-based risk detector decides *whether* to spend extra compute, a fixed 8-action checkpoint-remasking bank expands the repair space, and a trajectory-aware verifier/selector decides *which* continuation to retain — the planner never restarts generation from scratch.
- **Shows hidden trajectories consistently outperform surface statistics and single hidden snapshots** for both lock-in detection and repair-branch selection, with full-trajectory modeling adding +1.33–3.61 pp AUROC over a validation-selected single snapshot.
- **Demonstrates consistent end-to-end gains with selective intervention** and an honest repair-space analysis showing that oracle headroom — not detection quality — bounds gains when the fixed action bank lacks a correct continuation.

## Methodology

**1. SBW lock-in formalization.** For a dLLM run over T recorded denoising steps, a deterministic extractor yields answer a_t = E(x^(t)) at each step. The early lock-in checkpoint is c = round(0.5(T−1)); with window w = max(1, floor((c+1)/2)), an example is *early-locked* if all answers in the window are valid and identical. It is an *early-correct lock-in* if the frozen checkpoint label matches gold, else an *SBW (stable-but-wrong) lock-in*.

**2. Surface-matched benchmark.** Each generation gets a Surface-18 vector s ∈ R^18 of observable prefix-level statistics (confidence, entropy, probability margin, token/answer stability, log-probability behavior, temporal summaries). Correct and wrong lock-in examples are matched one-to-one after robust normalization using calipers and a weighted-distance constraint (Rosenbaum–Rubin style). Hidden representations are never used during matching. Post-match balance is checked with absolute standardized mean differences (SMD); on DiffusionGemma + MetaMathQA, mean/max SMD drops from 0.617/0.934 to 0.016/0.065 (max < 0.085 in all five settings).

**3. Hidden-state trajectory encoding.** LOCKR records hidden representations at multiple denoising positions, model layers, and token positions along the prefix (an answer-focused token track plus a change track of positions whose tokens flip between steps). High-dimensional states are compressed with fixed layer-specific random-sign projections (Achlioptas) and ordered across denoising positions into trajectory H.

**4. Risk detection.** A bidirectional GRU encodes H and predicts wrong-lock-in risk p_risk = 1 − f_θ(H); a validation-selected threshold τ routes the example to baseline decoding (continue) or repair. Only states at or before the lock-in checkpoint inform the decision, so later states cannot revise it.

**5. Targeted remasking and repair branches.** For high-risk generations, a fixed bank of B=8 paired checkpoint-remasking actions (varying intervention time and remasking strength, fixed before training and shared across all settings) locally remasks the answer-related region and resumes denoising, preserving most of the existing draft. Each action produces one counterfactual continuation.

**6. Trajectory-aware verification and selection.** For each branch b_i, a separate trajectory verifier (trained with auxiliary correctness and pairwise-ranking objectives) scores the branch's own hidden trajectory; the frozen trajectory score is combined with the original task context and within-pool agreement by a histogram gradient-boosted selector producing the final score s_i; LOCKR retains b* = argmax s_i.

**Setup.** Models: DiffusionGemma (all three datasets) and LLaDA-2 (MetaMathQA, Orca-Math). Base generation: 32 denoising steps, max 256 tokens, midpoint lock-in checkpoint. Baselines: Surface-18 planners, self-consistency, ReMDM-style remasking, external PRM-guided repair, validation-selected single hidden snapshot; oracle quantities are analysis-only upper bounds. All learned components are selected on train/validation data only and frozen before held-out test.

## Results & Highlights

- **Detection (surface-matched benchmark, Table 2):** Full-trajectory LOCKR reaches 77.29–83.92% AUROC across the five settings (83.04 on DiffusionGemma+MetaMathQA, 83.92 on LLaDA-2+MetaMathQA), versus 53.61–68.36% for Surface-18 logistic regression (62.91 on DG+MetaMathQA, +20.1 over LOCKR), up to 64.25% for Surface-18 HGB, and 73.04–82.59% for the validation-selected single hidden snapshot. Pairwise accuracy: 77.29–81.08% for LOCKR; trajectory modeling over a static snapshot adds +1.33–3.61 pp AUROC and +1.41–4.79 pp pairwise accuracy.
- **Branch selection (fixed repair pool, Table 3):** LOCKR attains 55.2–67.8% selected-branch accuracy (67.8 vs PRM-guided 62.3 and snapshot 64.5 on DG+MetaMathQA), beating the single hidden-snapshot verifier by 1.6–3.7 pp and the external PRM baseline in every setting; a representative case shows the unique correct branch outvoted 7-to-1 by surface agreement but recovered by trajectory-aware selection.
- **End-to-end, DiffusionGemma (Table 4):** LOCKR reaches 67.9% / 66.4% / 55.4% on MetaMathQA / Orca-Math / NuminaMath V2 at 2.9–3.1× relative denoising cost, exceeding the strongest non-LOCKR baseline by 6.0 / 5.3 / 1.8 pp; it is Pareto-efficient in all three settings and improves both cost and accuracy over PRM-guided repair (which needs ~4.8× cost) and always-repair majority voting. Base decoding is 50.0% at 1.0×; random repair is 53.2/51.6/48.7 at ~2.2–2.4×.
- **End-to-end, LLaDA-2 (Table 5):** 57.2% at 2.9× cost on MetaMathQA and 51.9% at 2.5× on Orca-Math, the best non-oracle method on both datasets (base decoding 50.0% at 1.0×; PRM-guided 51.2/50.4), in a setting where all remasking baselines fail to beat the 50% matched-set baseline.
- **Natural-distribution evaluation (Table 6):** Applying the frozen policy to each original dataset distribution yields consistent gains of +2.21–5.37 pp while repairing only 22–41% of examples at 1.8–2.6× cost — e.g., LLaDA-2+MetaMathQA 42.93→48.30 (+5.37, repairing 33%), DiffusionGemma+Orca-Math 70.28→74.50 (+4.23), DiffusionGemma+NuminaMath 37.60→41.80 (+4.20).
- **Ablations (Table 7, DG+MetaMathQA, full LOCKR 67.9% @ 3.1×):** replacing the trajectory with a single snapshot −3.2 pp (64.7); removing branch trajectories −4.0 (63.9); removing the trajectory GRU −4.2 (63.7); removing targeted remasking −4.4 (63.5); random trigger −8.8 (59.1); removing the risk trigger barely changes accuracy (67.8) but inflates cost to 5.0× — showing risk detection buys efficiency while targeted repair + trajectory verification buy accuracy.
- **SBW prevalence:** erroneous lock-in accounts for 14.9%–60.5% of processed generations across the five formal settings — the failure mode is common rather than exceptional.

## Authors & Credibility

Guoshenghui Zhao (Rochester Institute of Technology) and Weijie Zhao (RIT, CS) form the academic core; Tan Yu is at NVIDIA Corporation, lending industrial relevance on LLM inference. The paper carries a detailed reproducibility statement (train/test splits, validation-selected operating points, hyperparameters and compute accounting in appendices) and an AI-use statement; an anonymous code repository is referenced (footnote: "Anonymous code repository"), so code availability is promised but not yet a verifiable public release. The methodology borrows matched-comparison machinery from causal inference (Rosenbaum–Rubin propensity matching, SMD balance diagnostics, Stuart), which the authors apply correctly — a sign of methodological maturity beyond typical interpretability papers.

## Critical Assessment

- **The matched-benchmark design is the paper's best move, but it also limits external validity of the detection numbers.** The benchmark is balanced to a 50/50 correct/wrong base rate by construction, so AUROC figures (77–84%) are not directly comparable to precision/recall on natural distributions, where SBW prevalence varies from 14.9% to 60.5%. The natural-distribution table partially addresses this, but only with the frozen policy — no recalibration study is reported.
- **The NuminaMath gain is thin and the "strongest non-LOCKR baseline" framing deserves scrutiny.** On DiffusionGemma+NuminaMath V2 the end-to-end margin is +1.8 pp (55.4% vs 53.6%) — an order of magnitude smaller than the MetaMathQA margin (+6.0). The paper is honest about this ("the remaining gain varies with model, task, and repair-space headroom"), but the abstract's "+2.21–5.37" range glosses over that the headline margins come mostly from the two MetaMathQA settings.
- **A large oracle gap is disclosed but under-explored.** The repair-oracle reaches 65.9% on LLaDA-2+Orca-Math versus 71.7% on MetaMathQA, while LOCKR itself achieves only 51.9% on Orca-Math — the selector captures well under half of the achievable headroom there. The paper attributes this to repair-space coverage and selection quality but offers no analysis of *why* correct branches get misranked (e.g., verifier failure modes on specific answer types).
- **Per-setting supervised components.** The risk GRU, branch verifier, and HGB selector are all trained per model–dataset configuration using gold-answer labels. Cross-dataset or cross-model transfer of these lightweight heads is never tested — an obvious question for a method pitched as a general test-time planner, and a practical cost for every new model/task pair.
- **Cost accounting is denoising-work-normalized, not wall-clock.** The 1.8–3.1× cost figures measure relative denoising computation; with B=8 branches executed per high-risk example, batching and serving overhead could shift the true latency picture substantially, as the limitations section itself concedes.
- **Scope is narrow:** two white-box dLLM families (DiffusionGemma, LLaDA-2), math reasoning only, and required white-box hidden-state access rules out API-only models. The "anonymous code repository" footnote means reproducibility currently rests on the appendix detail, not a verifiable release.
- That said, the surface-matching protocol is genuinely rigorous (SMD max < 0.085 across all settings), and the risk-trigger ablation (accuracy unchanged at 5.0× cost without it) plus the honest repair-coverage analysis are exactly the ablations a skeptic would ask for.

## Strengths

- **Confound-controlled evaluation:** the propensity-style Surface-18 matching cleanly separates "hidden states know more" from "hidden states exploit confidence/stability," addressing the standard critique of internal-state papers.
- **Complete planner, not just a detector:** deciding whether to intervene, where to remask, and which continuation to keep — with each component ablated (targeted remasking −4.4 pp; random trigger −8.8 pp; branch trajectories −4.0 pp).
- **Consistency across 2 model families × 3 datasets,** including a setting (LLaDA-2) where competing remasking baselines fail to beat the 50% matched-set baseline yet LOCKR still delivers the best non-oracle accuracy.
- **Honest negative analysis:** explicit oracle decomposition showing detection strength does not guarantee end-to-end gain when the fixed action bank lacks a correct continuation (repair-space coverage as a first-class limit).
- **Compute-efficiency framing:** selective triggering cuts cost from 5.0× (always-repair) to ~3× at equal accuracy, and Pareto-dominates PRM-guided repair.

## Limitations

- **Fixed repair-action bank (B=8):** when no action uncovers a correct continuation, even a perfect detector and selector cannot help; the small repair-oracle headroom on LLaDA-2+Orca-Math (65.9% vs 71.7% on MetaMathQA) directly quantifies this ceiling, and correct branches can still be misranked when present.
- **Domain and architecture scope:** mathematical reasoning only, two white-box dLLM families; broader domains, adaptive repair-space construction, and stronger trajectory verification are left to future work.
- **Supervised, per-setting training** of the GRU risk model, verifier, and selector requires labeled data and retraining per model–dataset pair; no transfer results.
- **Cost metric is normalized denoising work,** not hardware wall-clock latency, which depends on batching and serving infrastructure.
- **White-box requirement:** hidden states from multiple layers/steps are needed, excluding closed-source/API models.
- Code is only "anonymously available" at submission time.

## Relevance

该工作与用户对 LLM 内部表征与推理时计算（test-time scaling/self-correction）的兴趣高度契合：它把"内部状态能否超越置信度等表面信号"这一经典问题用因果匹配的严格协议重新回答，并给出了一个检测-干预-选择的完整测试时修复框架。对于关注扩散语言模型推理动态与内部状态可解释性的研究，这是一篇方法与实验设计都值得借鉴的论文。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the risk GRU, branch verifier, and selector trained on one dataset (or model) transfer zero-shot to another, or does every new model–task pair require re-labeling and retraining the lightweight heads?
- The selector captures well under half of the repair-oracle headroom on LLaDA-2+Orca-Math (51.9% achieved vs 65.9% oracle): what specifically causes correct branches to be misranked, and could process-reward signals or step-level trajectory features close the selection gap?
- Does the surface-matching protocol transfer to non-math domains (code, multi-hop QA) where "stable-but-wrong" may have different surface signatures?
- How do the 1.8–3.1× denoising-work costs translate to wall-clock latency under realistic batched serving, given B=8 branches per high-risk example?
- Could adaptive repair-space construction (choosing checkpoint/strength pairs per example) recover the headroom that the fixed 8-action bank forfeits, e.g., on the low-headroom Orca-Math setting?
- SBW prevalence ranges from 14.9% to 60.5% across settings — what model or task properties drive this order-of-magnitude difference in erroneous early commitment?
