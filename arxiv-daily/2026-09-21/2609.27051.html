A frozen statistical referee — not the proposer's intelligence — decides false admissions: 5–11× fewer, a gap no LLM closes.

Propose, Don't Judge: An Anytime-Valid Referee for LLM Agents That Mine Investment Factors

Bo Qu · Mingguang Chen · Licheng Wang · industry-affiliated (DeepGrounding / AlphaAvatar)

llm-agents quantitative-finance anytime-valid-inference false-discovery-control factor-mining

Key metric

5–11×

fewer realised false factors per campaign under the frozen referee

11.7 vs 86.2 / 105.2 / 196.0 for peeking t-test, adaptive-threshold and no-gate referees — round-robin proposer, ten-year CSI 500 walk-forward. Zero negative-edge admissions in all 100 frozen cells.

Why this matters

Sits at the crossing of LLM-agent governance, anytime-valid inference and quant factor mining — and honestly answers which duties an agent should keep.

Critical edge

The real-data LLM arm is a historical replay: the anytime-valid guarantee “does not attach” to these runs, and with licensed, non-redistributable data plus code-on-request, real-data results are effectively unverifiable.

Methodology

  1. Split the pipeline. A swappable controller (allocation, candidate construction, diagnosis, probe authoring) proposes; a frozen referee judges. Anything used on day t must be Gt−1-measurable; decisions land in an append-only log.
  2. Evidence as capital. A aGRAPA-staked betting e-process (capped at 0.8 of Kelly) bets on AR(1)-whitened daily rank-IC; admission when capital crosses the online e-BH bar over a frozen 2,000-slot universe (α=0.05); retirement by e-detector against registered δ=0.015, target run-length A*=1260 days.
  3. Factorial experiment. 3 proposers (script / bandit / LLM) × 4 referees (frozen + 3 deliberately leaky) × 3 environments, under common random numbers, Holm-corrected paired tests, families never pooled.
  4. Real-data study. Point-in-time CSI 500 (2015–2026), campaigns starting 2016–2019, 5 seeds, 540 cells — every LLM call recorded so cells replay at zero cost.
  5. Registered design. α, δ, A*, grids and the analysis plan were fixed before the runs; κ̄=0.018 and the execution layer were built in-sample after a first pass.

Strengths

  • Guarantees hold at every stopping time for any proposer — including a self-modifying LLM — with the four enabling conditions stated as an explicit proposition.
  • The 3×4×3 factorial with common random numbers cleanly separates governance effects from intelligence effects: the right experiment for the question asked.
  • Unusually honest economics — the certified book is shown losing to the ungated book on Sharpe, with the gap mechanistically decomposed (momentum: 6 sleeves at −4.35 bp/day vs the ungated 892 at +1.69).
  • Probe authoring measured against held-out fault types with Holm-corrected paired effects; null results reported alongside positives.
  • Whitening before betting repairs a real failure mode: raw-stream over-admission of 6.0% / 15.4% at ρ=0.2 / 0.4, restored to 1.8–2.4%.

Limitations

  • One market (CSI 500); four start years share most of their data; no held-out period beyond the walk-forward.
  • The realised truth label overlaps the admission window and favours late-admitting referees — re-judging cuts the headline 5–11× advantage by about a third (2–3× per-family).
  • Real-data LLM results are replays; the validity guarantee attaches only to live deployment where no proposer knows the post-submission stream.
  • Retirement is calibrated only on synthetic decay: false-retirement rises to 8.5% at ρ=0.4, and the ARL guarantee gives no within-campaign bound.
  • κ̄ and the execution layer were constructed in-sample; no investability claim (alpha t < 1 everywhere, no borrow fee).
  • Code on request only; market data licensed and non-redistributable — outsiders cannot verify the real-data results.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. What does an e-process on multi-day-horizon rank-IC look like — can it recover momentum without losing anytime validity?
  2. Do the 5–11× gap and the ~500-day wait replicate on a second market (e.g. US equities) with different microstructure?
  3. Can the diagnosis role be ablated on its own on real data, given the LLM bundle also sees family profiles and memory the bandit lacks?
  4. The LLM's edge over the bandit inverts after 2020 — overtaken prior, or fixable with periodically refreshed family profiles?
  5. How does the referee behave under adversarial proposers with side information, beyond the unit-tested counterexample?
  6. Would live (non-replay) deployment with current models change the controller comparison — and can the replay infrastructure be released?
