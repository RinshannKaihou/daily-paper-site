Why this matters

Reliable failure prediction for long-horizon coding agents enables selective generation — human handoff or replanning exactly when a trajectory is likely wrong.

Critical edge

All ten baselines are naive per-turn-average transplants; supervised agent-UQ competitors are dismissed as “costly” with no cost measurement — including GRUET’s own (~600 extra long generations per SWE-bench task).

Methodology

Turn-level uncertainty from a merged reasoning DAG, then training-free trajectory aggregation.

  1. Sample K−1 extra reasoning chains per turn K=10 recommended. Nodes of the DAG = reasoning steps + textual actions of every sampled chain.
  2. Merge nodes by semantic equivalence Reasoning nodes merge on bidirectional NLI entailment (Deberta-large / MNLI); action nodes merge when CodeXEmbed cosine ≥ τ.
  3. Score each node Token-level confidence → sliding windows (w≤4) → mean of the top-10% window scores.
  4. Read graph complexity as uncertainty DAG → feedforward net: leaf→sink edges weighted ω=0.1, node uncertainties injected as biases f(U)=−1/U; the sink output is the turn score. Flawed reasoning branches heavily → complex topology → high uncertainty.
  5. Aggregate across turns, no training Six strategies; All-turn mean and Top-25% are strongest; First-turn costs only 1/L of the sampling budget.
Eval grid: 9 LLMs (Qwen3.5 & Gemma4 families) × 5 agentic-coding benchmarks · AUROC / AUPRC / AUARC · 1000 bootstraps · 3σ significance rule.

Strengths

  • First systematic UQ study on genuinely long-horizon ReAct trajectories — 40+ turns on real GitHub-issue software engineering, vs ~7-turn QA agents in prior work.
  • Statistically disciplined: 1000 bootstraps, explicit 3σ criterion, consistent gains across 2 model families, 5 benchmarks, 3 metrics.
  • Honest, useful negative result — a warning against transplanting single-turn UQ to agents.
  • Training-free and plug-and-play; no supervised trajectory labels; First-turn variant gives a principled 1/L-cost option.
  • Thorough sensitivity analyses with concrete recommended configs (K=10; τ=0.7/0.94; T=0.6/1.0; d=25); transparent about omitted grid cells.

Limitations

  • Heavy sampling cost, never quantified (no wall-clock, token, or dollar figures); cheap First-turn loses up to ~15 AUROC on SWE-bench.
  • Performance collapses at low sampling temperature (T=0.1) — production agents often run cooler.
  • Merge threshold τ is family- and task-sensitive (0.7 vs 0.94), worst exactly on the hardest benchmarks; no auto-calibration.
  • Code agents with binary rewards only — no web/GUI agents, partial-credit tasks, or native reasoning models; sparse grid weakens scale-generality claims.
  • No empirical comparison against supervised or propagation-based agent-UQ methods (Uprop, agentic confidence calibration).
  • Step parsing depends on a hand-inserted “Step i:” prompt convention; robustness untested.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Q1Does graph-complexity uncertainty transfer to web/GUI or tool-use QA agents, and to reasoning models whose traces are not parseable “Step i” blocks?
  2. Q2Can the K−1 per-turn resampling cost be cut (shared-prefix sampling, activation-based branching) without giving up the ~15 AUROC that First-turn loses?
  3. Q3Why does τ differ across families (0.7 vs 0.94), and can it be auto-calibrated from unlabeled rollouts?
  4. Q4Is there a sampling-free or low-temperature-compatible variant, given the sharp degradation at T=0.1?
  5. Q5Does the score improve downstream utility — replanning, human handoff, best-of-N selection — beyond its discriminative AUROC/AUARC value?
