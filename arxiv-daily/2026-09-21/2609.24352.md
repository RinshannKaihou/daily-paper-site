> **一句话总结：** 针对"LLM 能在上下文中构建世界表征却不会用"（Lepori et al., 2026 的零样本失败）这一矛盾，本文在图追踪任务上证明：来自不同世界（同拓扑、不同水果-节点分配）的 few-shot 演示并不改善表征构建（Qwen3-4B 第 20 层探针 AUROC 仅从 0.7785 变到 0.7647），而是把世界表征沿近似正交的 "Shot Vector" 方向搬移到可被下游读出的子空间（子空间重叠度从 0.353 降到 0.188/层间差异显著），且因果擦除实验中正确答案概率的相对退化差从 0-shot 的 −0.487 翻转为 16-shot 的 +1.251，同时该效应迁移到 ARC-AGI-1&2、Mind2Web 与 Othello。

> **TL;DR:** Prior work (Lepori et al., ACL 2026) showed LLMs linearly encode a latent graph from in-context random walks yet fail to use it zero-shot on two-step-right prediction. This paper shows that adding few-shot demonstrations generated from *different* worlds (same graph topology, different fruit-to-node assignments — ruling out copy/elimination shortcuts) fixes this: on the 4×4 Lattice Grid, Qwen3 models climb from ≈10% accuracy (random guessing 6.25%) to ≈40–45% at 32 shots while probe AUROC for graph adjacency stays flat (0.7785 → 0.7647 at layer 20 of Qwen3-4B), probe-subspace overlap across shot counts collapses (0.353 for 0→16-shot at L20), and a matched-rank causal ablation of the world-representation subspace flips its degradation signature from −0.487 (0-shot, less harm than a random-subspace control) to +1.251 (16-shot). The few-shot benefit transfers to ARC-AGI-1&2, Mind2Web, and Othello using Qwen3, Gemma-4, and Ministral models.

## 核心贡献

- **把"构建 vs. 使用"拆开验证**：在 Lepori et al. (2026) 的零样本失败设定上加入 few-shot 演示后，任务准确率大幅提升（4×4 Lattice Grid 上从 ≈10% 升至 ≈40–45%，随机猜测 6.25%），但线性探针 AUROC 与 Dirichlet Energy 几乎不随 shot 数变化——说明演示改变的不是世界表征的构建，而是其被预测使用（predictive use）的程度。
- **"不同世界"演示设计**：每个演示的随机游走来自与查询不同的世界（同一拓扑、不同水果词表分配），从而排除 Lepori et al. Metalearning AWM Prompt 中因节点分配相同而可复制/排除答案的捷径；甚至演示拓扑与查询不同（Figure 15）时性能依然提升。
- **Shot Vector 表征搬移机制**：定义 Shot Vector 为 few-shot 与 zero-shot 隐状态的平均差；它与世界表征子空间近似正交（夹角约 75–90°），与 Task Vector (Hendel et al. 2023) 也近似正交，PCA 显示隐状态质心沿该方向系统搬移；0→16-shot 的探针子空间重叠度（L20：0.353）远低于 16→32-shot（0.765），且跨 shot 探针互相解码失败——表征被"搬家"而非旋转不变。
- **匹配秩的因果干预证据**：以 20 次迭代的 amnesic 擦除移除秩-r 世界表征子空间，并与秩、扰动范数均匹配的随机正交子空间干预对照；16-shot 下图干预造成的正确答案概率相对退化显著更大（+1.251±0.245），而 0-shot 反而为负（−0.487±0.148）——世界表征在 few-shot 下被因果地使用。
- **任务外推**：few-shot 演示（来自不同任务规则/不同网站/不同棋局）同样提升 ARC-AGI-1（400 任务/419 测试对）、ARC-AGI-2（120 任务/167 对）、Mind2Web Cross-Website（177 任务/1773 步，step success rate@2）与 Othello（300 局合法走子预测）上的表现，表明"演示唤起表征使用"超越合成图任务。

## Key Contributions

- **Separates construction from use.** With few-shot demonstrations from different worlds, two-step-right accuracy on the 4×4 Lattice Grid jumps from ≈10% (0-shot; random = 6.25%) to ≈40–45% at 32 shots (Qwen3-14B-Base), while graph-adjacency probe AUROC and normalized Dirichlet Energy remain statistically flat (e.g., L20 AUROC 0.7785±0.0019 at 0-shot vs 0.7647±0.0064 at 16-shot, five seeds) — the representation is built equally well in both regimes but is read out only in the few-shot regime.
- **A shortcut-free demonstration design.** Each demonstration is a random walk from a *different* world (same topology, disjoint fruit assignments), eliminating the copy/elimination shortcut available in Lepori et al.'s Metalearning AWM Prompt (≤3 demos with identical node assignments); benefits persist even when demonstration topologies differ from the query's.
- **The Shot Vector.** The mean few-shot-minus-zero-shot hidden-state shift is nearly orthogonal (≈75–90°) to the rank-r world-representation subspace and also to the Task Vector; probe-subspace overlap across shot counts drops to 0.353 (L20, 0→16-shot) vs 0.765 (16→32-shot), and cross-shot probes misdecode each other's activations — evidence of relocation into a new readout subspace rather than improved construction.
- **Causal evidence with a matched control.** Iterative (20-step) amnesic erasure of the graph subspace vs a rank- and norm-matched random-subspace intervention: the mean relative degradation difference in correct-answer probability is −0.487±0.148 at 0-shot but +1.251±0.245 at 16-shot (95% bootstrap CIs, 5000 resamples), with consistent results on Llama-3.2-3B, Gemma-4-12B, and Ministral-3-8B.
- **Beyond graph tracking.** Few-shot demonstrations drawn from different tasks (ARC-AGI-1&2), different websites (Mind2Web Cross-Website, 177 tasks / 1773 action steps), and different board positions (Othello, 300 samples) consistently improve performance over zero-shot, extending the elicitation effect to practical agentic benchmarks.

## Methodology

**Task (in-context world modeling).** Following the adaptive world-modeling setup of Lepori et al. (2026), each "world" is one of five graph topologies — 4×4 Lattice Grid, 5×5 Lattice Grid, 3×5 Lattice Grid, 4×4 Triangle Grid, 4×4 Torus Grid — with fruits (e.g., apple, lime) assigned to nodes. The prompt contains a comma-separated random walk of 48–80 nodes (no explicit topology information) plus node-pair examples specifying "move two steps to the right," and the model must predict the node two steps right of a query node (chosen as argmax logit over the candidate node set). Two-step prediction is used because one-step is solvable by copying an adjacent token and three-step is substantially harder; the answer is guaranteed to appear at least once in the walk, and pairs whose target sits exactly two positions before/after the source are excluded. Lepori's 16-State Line and 25-State Line topologies are dropped to rule out the copy-two-back shortcut. **Few-shot demonstrations** each consist of a random walk from a *different* world (same topology, different fruit assignments) with their own node-pair instruction examples; shots ∈ {0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32} with the query fixed across settings; 300 test samples per condition. Random-guess baselines: 6.25% (16-node topologies), 4.0% (5×5), 6.7% (3×5).

**Probing.** For each node occurrence, hidden states are pooled over subtokens and occurrences, RMS-normalized, and projected by a rank-r probe (r=2 for grids, r=4 for the torus via periodic sine/cosine coordinates; r∈{8,16} ablations in Appendix E.2), trained on 500 samples with 100 validation samples. Adjacency probability is p(u,v) = σ(β − ||z_u − z_v||²); probe quality is AUROC over node pairs. Normalized Dirichlet Energy (Park et al., 2025), computed on raw hidden states, serves as a probe-free measure of representation construction. Layers analyzed: 4/20/36 (Qwen3-4B/8B) and 4/20/40 (Qwen3-14B); d = 2560/4096/5120.

**Representation-shift analysis.** Subspace overlap tr(Π_W1 Π_W2)/r quantifies similarity of probe subspaces across shot counts; the mean hidden-state shift Δh (the **Shot Vector**) is compared to the world-representation subspace via angle θ = arccos(||Π_W Δh||/||Δh||), contrasted with the Task Vector (Hendel et al., 2023) computed with the same definition, and visualized by joint PCA of hidden states and centroids across 300 test samples.

**Causal intervention.** Following amnesic ablation (Ravfogel et al., 2020; Elazar et al., 2021), the graph-representation subspace component (computed from a probe on the current residual activations) is subtracted from node-subtoken activations at every Transformer block output, repeated for 20 iterations. The control ("random intervention") removes the same number of random orthonormal directions, rescaled to match the per-node perturbation norm. The outcome is the reduction in probability of the correct answer token (normalized over candidate-sequence likelihoods) under graph vs random intervention, reported with 95% bootstrap CIs (5000 resamples). Experiments run on NVIDIA GH200 GPUs; replication on Llama-3.2-3B, Gemma-4-12B, Ministral-3-8B-Base-2512.

**Practical tasks.** ARC-AGI-1 (public eval: 400 tasks, 419 test pairs) and ARC-AGI-2 (120 tasks, 167 pairs), where demonstrations come from different training tasks (rules) and the metric is pixel-level accuracy@2 (pass@2 is too sparse); Mind2Web Cross-Website (177 tasks, 1773 action steps, step success rate@2, demonstrations from disjoint websites); Othello (300 samples, 8–52-move histories over 64 squares, next legal-move prediction scored correct if legal). Decoding: top-p 0.95 / temperature 0.7 for ARC, greedy for Mind2Web and Othello.

## Results & Highlights

- **Few-shot accuracy gains (Figure 3).** On the 4×4 Lattice Grid, all three Qwen3-Base models rise monotonically with shots from ≈10% at 0-shot (random guessing = 6.25%) to a plateau of ≈35–45% at 32 shots (Qwen3-14B-Base highest); gains saturate around 4–16 shots. The 5×5 Lattice Grid (random = 4.0%) plateaus lower (≈25–30%) and the 3×5 grid (random = 6.7%) near ≈15%, showing topology difficulty modulates the benefit. Demonstrations from *different topologies* than the query still improve accuracy (Figure 15).
- **Construction is unchanged while use appears (Tables 1/5).** Qwen3-4B probe AUROC on the 4×4 Lattice is 0.7785±0.0019 (0-shot) vs 0.7647±0.0064 (16-shot) at Layer 20 and 0.7612 vs 0.7401 at Layer 36 — a ≤0.021 AUROC change against an accuracy gain of roughly 30+ points; random-feature probe baselines sit near 0.5. Even after 20 iterations of graph-subspace erasure, probes recover (AUROC ≈ 0.68–0.71), consistent with only partial erasure of a low-rank code.
- **The subspace relocates, it does not rotate in place (Tables 2/3).** Probe-subspace overlap between 0-shot and 16-shot is 0.3529±0.0116 at L20 and 0.1882±0.0453 at L36, versus 0.7653±0.0063 (L20) and 0.8813±0.0153 (L36) between 16-shot and 32-shot — most relocation happens upon leaving the zero-shot regime. A 0-shot-trained probe evaluated on 16-shot activations drops to 0.7106 (L20) and 0.6555 (L36) AUROC, and cross-probe performance degrades as the shot-count gap widens (Figure 5a).
- **Causal flip with matched control (Table 4).** Mean relative degradation difference (graph minus random intervention, positive = graph information causally used): **−0.487±0.148 at 0-shot → +1.251±0.245 at 16-shot** on Qwen3-4B, 4×4 Lattice, after 20 iterations; every one of the five seeds flips sign. The random intervention shows the opposite shot trend (Figure 25), and the same positive 16-shot signature replicates on Llama-3.2-3B, Gemma-4-12B, and Ministral-3-8B (Figure 29).
- **Shot Vector geometry (Figures 22/23).** The Shot Vector forms ≈75–90° angles with the world-representation subspace across layers and topologies, and is nearly orthogonal to the Task Vector computed with the same recipe — it shifts the *representations'* token-sequence centroids (PCA, Figures 6/20/21) rather than encoding an input→output mapping.
- **Practical transfer (Figure 8b).** On ARC-AGI-1 (400 tasks), ARC-AGI-2 (120 tasks), Mind2Web Cross-Website (1773 action steps), and Othello (300 samples), pixel-accuracy@2, step-success@2, and legal-move log-prob all increase with shots from different worlds/tasks relative to zero-shot, across Qwen3, Gemma-4, and Ministral models — no representation analysis is run on these benchmarks.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The core move — turning Lepori et al.'s "LLMs don't use what they build" negative result into a mechanistic account where few-shot demonstrations *relocate* an already-constructed representation into a causally read-out subspace — is genuinely new and well framed against Task/Function Vectors and meta-ICL. The different-worlds demonstration design is a clean fix for the copy/elimination shortcut that Lepori et al.'s Metalearning AWM Prompt (identical node assignments across ≤3 demos) left open.

**Rigor.** Above average for interpretability work: the graph-vs-random intervention is matched in rank and per-node perturbation norm (addressing nonspecific-subspace confounds), seeds and bootstrap CIs are reported, and the State-Line topologies that permit a two-back copying shortcut were deliberately excluded. However, several gaps remain. First, the causal loop is only half-closed: the paper *removes* the world subspace but never runs the complementary injection experiment — adding the Shot Vector to zero-shot activations to see whether it rescues two-step accuracy. Without that, "relocation causes predictive use" rests on an erasure asymmetry plus correlational geometry. Second, erasure is only partial: after 20 iterations, probe AUROC is still ≈0.68–0.71 (vs 0.7785 clean), so residual adjacency information remains, and the authors themselves note repair effects (McGrath et al., 2023) as a risk; the matched random control mitigates but does not eliminate this. Third, the *negative* 0-shot value (−0.487) — meaning erasing the graph subspace zero-shot helps relative to random erasure — is reported but left essentially unexplained, and it hints the decoded subspace may be anti-correlated with the answer in the zero-shot regime, which complicates the clean construction-vs-use story. Fourth, the practical-task section (ARC-AGI-1&2, Mind2Web, Othello) is behavioral only: no probing or intervention is run there, so "the same mechanism" is asserted by analogy; the ARC metric (pixel-level accuracy@2) is nonstandard and incomparable to leaderboard pass@2 numbers, and models were chosen per task by difficulty, a mild cherry-picking risk.

**Reproducibility & significance.** Code is released, prompts are shown verbatim, and the synthetic task is cheap to rerun; the six-model replication and seed tables make the core claims likely to hold. But significance is bounded by the task: accuracies plateau at 30–45% (far from ceiling), eval sets are 300 samples with no CIs on the behavioral curves, and the authors concede the few-shot gain is "only one contributing factor" that they have not disentangled — walk-local statistics could contribute since the answer always appears somewhere in the query's walk (they only exclude the two-position windows). The world-model notion is deliberately static (adjacency only), so "world representation" here is much narrower than dynamics-bearing world models.

## Strengths

- Sharp, falsifiable hypothesis with a clean dissociation: probe AUROC and Dirichlet Energy (construction) flat while accuracy and intervention sensitivity (use) change with shots.
- Well-controlled causal design: rank- and norm-matched random-subspace intervention, iterative amnesic ablation, 95% bootstrap CIs, 5-seed sensitivity (stds ≤ 0.011 on probe AUROC).
- Shortcut-aware task design: different-worlds demonstrations, dropped State-Line topologies, exclusion of copyable query-target windows, and cross-topology demonstrations still working.
- Consistent replication across six base model families and across probing layers, with rank (r=2,4,8,16) and topology ablations in the appendix.
- Honest limitations section and full prompt listings; code released.

## Limitations

- The relocation mechanism is established only on the synthetic graph task; ARC-AGI-1&2, Mind2Web, and Othello receive behavioral few-shot curves but no representation analysis, so the generalization of the *mechanism* is unverified.
- No Shot-Vector injection (rescue) experiment, so the causal chain from relocation to improved readout is incomplete; only erasure-side evidence exists.
- Graph-subspace erasure is partial (probe AUROC ≈0.68–0.71 after 20 iterations), and repair/compensation effects cannot be fully excluded.
- Task accuracies plateau at 30–45% with 300-sample evaluations and no confidence intervals on the behavioral curves; the authors acknowledge few-shot gains are only one contributing factor among uncharacterized others.
- Static-structure definition of a world model — no temporal dynamics, state transitions, or causal evolution; ICL-vs-finetuning and RL/SFT effects are explicitly out of scope.
- The negative zero-shot degradation difference (−0.487±0.148) lacks a satisfying explanation.

## Relevance

该文与你关注的 LLM 智能体与机制可解释性方向高度相关：它给出了"智能体在上下文中已构建世界结构表征、但只有配合 few-shot 观察演示才会真正使用它"的因果证据，并提出 Shot Vector 这一可测量的表征搬移机制。这对理解 agent 如何从环境轨迹中学习世界模型、ICL 能力如何迁移进权重（agentic training 借助 ICL 的范式）都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would injecting the Shot Vector into zero-shot hidden states rescue two-step accuracy, closing the causal loop that erasure-only experiments leave open?
- Does the relocated readout subspace persist on harder horizons (three-step prediction, larger or non-lattice topologies) where baseline accuracy is near floor?
- Is the Shot Vector direction shared across topologies, tasks, or model families — i.e., is there a universal "readout-eliciting" direction that could be added once and transfer?
- Why does graph-subspace erasure *help* relative to the random control in the zero-shot regime (−0.487)? Is the decoded subspace anti-correlated with correct answers before demonstrations?
- Can the same relocation be induced durably via SFT/RL (in-weight rather than in-context), and does the probing/intervention toolkit predict which training data will produce transferable world representations?
