> **一句话总结：** 作者构建了一条 LIHA 头级归因 → EAP 边筛选（每模型上限 2,000 条候选边、FP16 主动钳位）→ 精确 activation patching 符号验证的六阶段电路发现流水线，在 GPT-2、BLOOM-560M、Pythia-1B/2.8B 和 Qwen2.5-1.5B（Base/Instruct）共 6 个 ≤2.8B 模型上发现：多语言模型在首个生成 token 处的语言身份决策集中于网络后段 68%–96% 深度的单一"广播"枢纽（如 BLOOM-560M 第 21/24 层），消融图内头造成的目标语言性能下降远超等规模图外头对照（BLOOM-560M：18,527 vs 1,224），且 Qwen2.5-1.5B 指令微调前后回路保持 84.7% Jaccard 相似度——但 EAP 线性近似与真实因果干预的相关性仅为 r ∈ [−0.24, 0.57]，且 Qwen 对未通过必要性检验。

> **TL;DR:** This mechanistic-interpretability paper runs a 6-stage circuit-discovery pipeline (per-head LIHA attribution → EAP edge screening capped at 2,000 candidates with FP16 active clamping → exact activation-patching sign verification) on a hand-authored 21-prompt, 7-language multilingual suite across GPT-2, BLOOM-560M, Pythia-1B/2.8B, and Qwen2.5-1.5B Base/Instruct. Against an equal-size out-of-graph ablation baseline, ablating the discovered in-graph circuit causes a far larger target-language degradation (BLOOM-560M: 18,527 vs 1,224 drop; necessity margins +0.63 to +3.28 for the four standalone models), the broadcasting hub consistently sits in the back quarter of network depth (e.g., BLOOM-560M layer 21/24 = 87%, Qwen2.5-1.5B layer 27/28 = 96%), and Qwen2.5-1.5B's circuit survives instruction tuning with 84.7% Jaccard overlap — but EAP's linear scores correlate with exact patching at only r ∈ [−0.24, +0.57] (positive for just 1 of 6 models), and the Qwen pair INVERTS the necessity check, weakening the universality claim.

## 核心贡献

- **LIHA（Language Identity Head Attribution）**：一种头级归因方法，通过在目标语言 prompt 与"干扰语言"（foil）prompt 之间做 activation patching，在**第一个生成 token** 处为每个注意力头计算目标语言 logits 的翻转量，用于跨 6 个架构（560M–1.5B 参数量级，另有 GPT-2 124M、Pythia-2.8B）定位对语言身份敏感的头。
- **带预算约束与 FP16 防护的 EAP 电路提取**：用 Edge Attribution Patching（一阶 Taylor 展开，每样本每边仅需一次反向传播）筛选边，配合主动钳位（激活值预充至 ±10,000、上限 65,504、NaN 过滤）对抗 FP16 溢出，硬性限制每模型 2,000 条候选边，再以精确 activation patching 的**符号一致性**做二次验证（K=250、下游容量 d_a=8），最终验证图规模为 816（GPT-2）至 1,016 条边（Qwen2.5-1.5B）。
- **三个拓扑/训练发现**：(1) 四个独立模型呈现一致的深层枢纽拓扑——首 token 广播瓶颈位于网络深度约后 1/4（BLOOM-560M 第 21/24 层）；(2) 参数扩张（Pythia-1B→2.8B）把参与节点从 123 扩到 415 而边预算固定（约 860 条验证边），导致高拓扑稀疏性；(3) Qwen2.5-1.5B Base→Instruct 保留 84.7% Jaccard 相似度（含第 27 层主枢纽），说明首 token 路由回路在预训练中已基本确立、指令微调只是轻度扰动。
- **一个诚实的负结果**：EAP 归因保真度低——6 个模型中 5 个的 EAP 分数与精确 patching 差值为负相关或近零相关（r ∈ [−0.24, 0.57]，仅 BLOOM-560M 为 +0.569），表明低精度（fp16）下的梯度线性近似经常偏离真实因果干预，精确验证环节不可或缺。
- **一个削弱普适性的反例**：Qwen2.5-1.5B 的 Base 与 Instruct 两个变体的必要性/图外无关性检验均 INVERTED（图外消融下降超过图内），作者将其解读为流水线可靠性问题而非"无广播回路"的架构证据，但明确承认无法在两种解释间做出定论。

## Key Contributions

- **LIHA (Language Identity Head Attribution)**: a per-head attribution method that scores every attention head's contribution to target-language identity at the *first generated token*, via activation patching between a target-language prompt and a foil-language prompt (logit-flip style), applied across six architectures (GPT-2 124M, BLOOM-560M, Pythia-1B/2.8B, Qwen2.5-1.5B Base/Instruct).
- **A budgeted, FP16-defended EAP pipeline**: Edge Attribution Patching (first-order Taylor expansion; one backward pass per sample per edge) screens up to 2,000 candidate edges per model with active clamping (activations primed to ±10,000, ceiling 65,504, NaN filtering), then edges are verified by **sign-agreement with exact activation patching** (K=250, downstream capacity d_a=8), yielding verified graphs of 816 (GPT-2) to 1,016 edges (Qwen2.5-1.5B).
- **Three empirical findings**: (1) four standalone models share a consistent deep-layer hub topology — the first-token broadcasting bottleneck sits in roughly the back quarter of network depth (BLOOM-560M layer 21/24); (2) parameter scaling (Pythia-1B→2.8B) expands node participation from 123 to 415 nodes under a fixed edge budget (~860 verified edges), inducing high topological sparsity; (3) Qwen2.5-1.5B Base→Instruct retains 84.7% Jaccard similarity including the primary layer-27 hub, indicating first-token routing is established during pre-training and only lightly perturbed by instruction tuning.
- **An honest negative result**: EAP attribution fidelity is low — 5 of 6 models show negative-to-weak-negative Pearson correlation between EAP scores and exact patching deltas (r ∈ [−0.24, 0.57]; only BLOOM-560M is positive at +0.569), showing linear approximations frequently diverge from true causal interventions in fp16 and that an exact verification stage is essential for reliable circuit discovery.
- **A universality-weakening counterexample**: both Qwen2.5-1.5B variants INVERT the necessity/irrelevance check (out-of-graph ablation hurts more than in-graph), which the authors interpret as pipeline unreliability rather than absence of broadcasting circuitry, while conceding the two explanations cannot be disentangled without an fp32 rerun.

## Methodology

The pipeline has six stages, applied identically to all six models, run entirely in fp16:

1. **Dataset**: 21 hand-authored, neutral single-language prompt items across seven languages (English, French, Spanish, Vietnamese, Chinese, Russian, Japanese), each with a target language and a foil language. (Six code-switched and two semantic-congruence items were constructed but not used; Vietnamese was dropped for GPT-2, leaving 16 prompts for that model.)
2. **LIHA**: for each head, patch its first-token activation between target and foil prompts and measure the flip in target-language log-probability; positive scores mark heads that push generation toward the target language. The single highest-magnitude head per model is reported (e.g., layer 27, head 6 in both Qwen2.5-1.5B variants).
3. **EAP screening**: over all (source head → destination layer) edges, a first-order Taylor approximation around the clean activation estimates each edge's contribution to the language-identity objective in one backward pass per sample. Defenses: FP16 active clamping, a strict ceiling of 2,000 candidate edges per model, destination capacity d_a=8 to restrict out-degree, and EAP threshold 250.
4. **Exact activation patching (verification)**: each of the 2,000 candidates is verified by actually substituting the corrupted activation into the clean forward pass and measuring the loss delta (N_val = 100 samples); an edge is kept only if the exact delta agrees in sign with the EAP score. Verified graph sizes: 816 (GPT-2) to 1,016 (Qwen2.5-1.5B).
5. **DAG extraction**: the kept edges form a directed acyclic graph of first-token routing per model.
6. **Validation**: two boundary ablations — *necessity* (ablating in-graph heads should substantially degrade target-language accuracy) and *out-of-graph irrelevance* (ablating an equal-sized set of heads outside the graph should have little effect). No sufficiency check (restricting computation to only in-graph heads) is performed.

Experimental arms: a **standalone arm** (GPT-2, BLOOM-560M, Pythia-1B, Pythia-2.8B — no shared tokenizer/architecture) and a **base-to-instruct arm** (Qwen2.5-1.5B Base vs Instruct, same architecture and tokenizer, enabling direct comparison of the extracted circuits).

## Results & Highlights

- **Necessity vs irrelevance gap (standalone arm, 21-prompt suite)**: the necessity margin (completeness drop minus out-of-graph drop) is positive for all four standalone models — Pythia-1B +3.28, GPT-2 +1.19, Pythia-2.8B +0.90, BLOOM-560M +0.63 (Table 3, all PASS) — with BLOOM-560M showing the starkest raw contrast: an in-graph ablation drop of 18,527 vs an out-of-graph drop of 1,224 (Table 5), a ~15× separation. In contrast, both Qwen2.5-1.5B variants INVERT (margins −1.05 base, −0.52 instruct; e.g., base: 0.72±1.50 in-graph vs 1.77±2.25 out-of-graph).
- **Deep-layer hub topology**: the primary broadcasting hub lands at layer 9/12 (75% depth) for GPT-2, 11/16 (68%) for Pythia-1B, 21/24 (87%) for BLOOM-560M, and 27/28 (96%) for Qwen2.5-1.5B; Pythia-2.8B is the outlier at layer 16 (~50% depth). The same layer-27/head-6 LIHA top head appears in both Qwen2.5-1.5B base and instruct variants.
- **EAP-to-exact fidelity is weak**: Pearson correlation between EAP scores and exact patching deltas over all 2,000 candidate edges is −0.226 (GPT-2), +0.569 (BLOOM-560M, the only positive of six), −0.221 (Pythia-1B), −0.185 (Pythia-2.8B), −0.077 (Qwen base), −0.059 (Qwen instruct) — i.e., r ∈ [−0.24, 0.57], far from the near-perfect agreement a reliable linear screening would give.
- **Instruction tuning preserves the circuit**: Qwen2.5-1.5B base vs instruct share 84.7% Jaccard similarity of verified nodes, including the layer-27 broadcasting hub; instruction tuning only modestly shifts in-graph head count (243→252) and completeness drop (0.72→1.12), and the EAP-exact correlation barely moves (−0.08→−0.06) — the inversion is a property of the shared base, not of alignment tuning.
- **Scaling sparsity**: edge counts scored scale from 792 (GPT-2, 144 heads) to 15,872 (Pythia-2.8B, 1,024 heads), yet verified edges stay in a narrow 816–1,016 band; Pythia-1B→2.8B grows in-graph nodes 123→415 (+238%) and out-of-graph 5→609 under the fixed 2,000-edge budget.

## Authors & Credibility

Arjun Pillai (Irvington High School), Christian Hoang (Gen4IE), and Anjelo Laroza (Independent Researcher) — a small independent team without a corporate or academic lab affiliation; the paper is formatted for AAAI 2027 (copyright block) and appeared on arXiv in September 2026. Despite the non-traditional affiliation profile, the work is methodologically literate: it correctly cites and positions against the current mechanistic-interpretability literature (Lindsey et al. 2025 on attribution vs causality, Syed/Rager/Commy 2024 on EAP, Marks et al. 2025 on sparse feature circuits, Zhang & Nanda 2024 on patching methodology pitfalls), enforces a sign-agreement verification stage that goes beyond naive EAP screening, and its Limitations section is unusually candid (it explicitly warns readers to discount PASS/INVERTED labels given SDs 2–4× the means). No prior high-profile interpretability publications are evident from this author set, so independent replication of the numbers would be prudent.

## Critical Assessment

- **The headline "four models confirm" claim is thinner than it looks.** GPT-2 and Pythia-1B "pass" necessity only degenerately: their in-graph sets are nearly the whole network (143/144 and 123/128 heads), leaving just 1 and 5 out-of-graph heads as the control — ablating almost everything naturally hurts more than ablating almost nothing. By the paper's own accounting, only Pythia-2.8B and BLOOM-560M (188/196 vs 415/609 splits) provide a genuinely informative positive result, and the sixth and fifth data points (both Qwen variants) fail outright. Two clean positives out of six is a case study, not an architecture-independent law.
- **The central tool undermines itself in the paper's own regime.** The EAP-exact correlations (negative for 5/6 models, Table 4) mean the screening stage barely tracks causal reality in fp16 — yet the paper still runs fp16 end-to-end and defers the fp32 rerun to future work. The Qwen inversion could equally be an artifact of gradient underflow near zero, and the authors admit they cannot adjudicate; a single fp32 replication of one model would have materially strengthened the paper and is a conspicuous omission.
- **No statistical rigor on the validation margins.** Necessity margins as small as +0.63 (BLOOM-560M) are labeled PASS against standard deviations 2–4× the means (e.g., Pythia-1B's 0.04±0.16 out-of-graph drop), with no paired permutation test or bootstrap CI — the paper acknowledges this but still prints qualitative labels that invite over-reading.
- **Fixed hyperparameters with no sensitivity analysis.** The EAP threshold 250, candidate ceiling 2,000, and destination capacity d_a=8 are applied identically to all models "not scaled per architecture" — but head counts differ by 7× (144 vs 1,024), so a fixed edge budget is not a fixed *relative* budget; no ablation sweeps these choices.
- **No comparison to established circuit-discovery baselines.** ACDC, edge pruning (Bhaskar et al. 2024), and SAE-based circuit methods are cited but never run, so there is no external yardstick for whether the 2,000-edge-budgeted pipeline finds better or worse circuits than existing tools at similar cost.
- **Data scale is minimal**: 21 evaluation prompts (~3 per language) for a 7-language claim, with per-model cells as small as 16 prompts; the prompt set's provenance and release are not stated. To the authors' credit, every one of these weaknesses is disclosed in the paper itself, which is more honest than most.

## Strengths

- **Breadth across architectures** (GPT-2, BLOOM, Pythia, Qwen; 124M–2.8B) is rare for a small-scale interpretability study, and the base-vs-instruct controlled pair enables a genuinely clean comparison of pre-training vs tuning effects on circuit structure.
- **The exact-patching verification stage is the right design**: demanding sign agreement between linear EAP scores and true causal deltas, under an explicit FP16 clamping defense, is a methodologically sound guard that many EAP-only papers skip.
- **Uncommon honesty**: the paper foregrounds its own negative results (EAP-exact divergence, Qwen inversion) and instructs readers to discount its qualitative labels — this epistemic discipline raises trust in the numbers that are reported.
- **Concrete, inspectable artifacts**: full verified DAGs for all six models are included in supplementary material, and the deterministic pipeline (fixed budgets, atomic stage ordering) is straightforward to reimplement.

## Limitations

- Only 21 evaluation prompts (~3 per language; 16 for GPT-2 after the Vietnamese drop) — far below the 200–300 per-language-pair scale the authors themselves say circuit validation practice suggests; no per-language breakdown is possible at this scale.
- fp16-only execution: EAP is most fragile exactly where low-precision gradients near zero matter, and 5/6 models show negative EAP-exact correlation; the decisive fp32 rerun is left to future work.
- GPT-2/Pythia-1B's out-of-graph controls are degenerate (1 and 5 heads), and both Qwen variants fail the necessity check — so the cross-architecture generality claim rests on two models.
- No sufficiency check, no statistical significance testing (raw means ± SD only), no baseline comparison against ACDC/edge pruning, no hyperparameter sensitivity analysis, and no stated code or dataset release.
- All models are ≤2.8B parameters; the authors explicitly disclaim any scaling claims beyond this range, and the Pythia-2.8B hub at ~50% depth already breaks a naive "deeper fraction with scale" trend.

## Relevance

这篇论文直接回应了"多语言模型在何处、如何决定回答语言"这一机制可解释性核心问题，其"首 token 语言身份集中于深层广播枢纽、且指令微调不改变该回路"的发现对所有多语言 LLM 的语言切换控制与可解释性研究都有参考价值。同时它给出的"EAP 线性近似在 fp16 下与因果干预严重脱节"的负结果，对任何打算用归因打分做电路筛选的人是重要的方法论警示。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would rerunning even one model (e.g., Qwen2.5-1.5B) in fp32 flip the inverted necessity check, or confirm it as a true architectural difference rather than a precision artifact?
- Does the deep-layer broadcasting hub hold for genuinely multilingual, instruction-tuned models at larger scale (the paper mentions Aya-Expanse-8B but excludes it), or is it a property of ≤2.8B models with limited multilingual capacity?
- Would a sufficiency check (restricting computation to only in-graph heads) complement the necessity/irrelevance tests and clarify whether the circuit is causal rather than merely correlated?
- How does the hub circuit behave on the constructed-but-unused code-switched prompts, where the language decision is genuinely context-dependent rather than prompt-determined?
- Can the necessity margins be put on statistical footing (paired permutation or bootstrap over the 21 prompts), and do the PASS/INVERTED labels survive?
