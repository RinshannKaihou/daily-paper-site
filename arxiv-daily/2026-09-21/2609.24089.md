> **一句话总结：** 提出 FlashBoB，首个把 FlashAttention 式 I/O 高效纪律推广到 softmax 注意力"反向的反向"（backward-over-backward，二阶微分原语）的精确算法：利用 softmax 双反向中的层级仿射结构（两个行标量 α、E 决定全部输出），把自然的四遍扫描压缩成两遍流式扫描，做到零 N×N 中间张量落 HBM、Θ(N²d²/M) HBM 流量并匹配该计算模型下的下界；在单张 A100 80GB 上精确跑到 N=262,144（所有已有基线在 N=16,384 就 OOM），比 GradMem 派生的 HVP 核最高快 10×、省 28× 显存，并把 1B token GPT-2 Sophia-H 二阶训练的墙钟时间压缩 2.17×。

> **TL;DR:** FlashBoB is a two-pass, exact, FlashAttention-style algorithm for the attention backward-over-backward (BoB) primitive — the second derivative pass needed by Sophia-H-style Hessian-vector products, test-time training, meta-learning, and gradient-written memory. Its key trick ("affine fusion," Proposition 4.1) shows two row-wise scalars (α, E) determine all BoB outputs via affine corrections, collapsing the natural four-sweep schedule into two streaming passes with no N×N tensor in HBM and Θ(N²d²/M) HBM traffic, matching a lower bound for FlashAttention-style score-recomputation algorithms. Empirically, on GPT-2-Small-shaped attention on an A100 80GB it reaches N=262,144 (52.7 GiB peak) where the PyTorch MATH baseline OOMs at N=8,192 and GradMem-derived HVP kernels OOM at N=16,384; at N=4,096 it is 25.80 ms vs 265.67 ms (MATH) and 89.14 ms (HVP-S) — 10.3×/3.5× faster. On 1B FineWeb tokens, Sophia-H with FlashBoB cuts GPT-2 Small full training wall time 2.17× (3.30→1.52 hr) and peak memory 2.22× (30,644→13,830 MiB/GPU) versus the PyTorch math-backend reference while matching final loss (3.6662 vs 3.6674). Worth reading if you care about long-context training systems or second-order optimization cost.

## 核心贡献

- **问题定义与原语化（§4.1）**：首次把"注意力反向的反向"形式化为一个可复用的核原语：给定首反向图 g(Q,K,V,dO)→(dQ,dK,dV,ddO) 与上游矩阵 U_Q,U_K,U_V,U_dO，计算 Jacobian 转置向量积 J^T U。二阶优化（Sophia-H 的 HVP）、测试时训练、元学习、梯度写入记忆最终都归结到这一个原语，但此前没有任何 FlashAttention 式 I/O 高效实现。
- **仿射融合（Affine Fusion, Proposition 4.1）**：核心洞察是 softmax 双反向具有层级仿射结构——两个行标量 α_i、E_i 通过仿射变换决定全部四个 BoB 输出。用六个行累加器 + 五个自由量预先累积"α-free"量，标量已知后只需一次仿射修正，把自然四遍调度（α 一遍、E 一遍、d̃(Q) 一遍、d̃(K),d̃(V) 一遍）坍缩为两遍。
- **两遍流式算法（Algorithm 1）**：Pass 1 行主序（持有 query 行块、流式读 key/value 块，写出 (α,E,d̃(Q),d̃(dO))），Pass 2 列主序（持有 key/value 块、流式读 query 行块，写出 d̃(K),d̃(V)）；跨遍边界只有两个长度为 N 的向量，任何 N×N 张量从不落 HBM。Theorem 4.2 证明精确性（Θ(N²d) FLOPs）与显存预算 B_r B_c + (B_r+B_c)(d+O(1)) ≤ M。
- **I/O 复杂度与最优性（Thm 4.3 / Cor 4.4 / Thm 4.5）**：FlashBoB 的 HBM 流量为 Θ(N²d²/M)（d 为 head 维、M 为 SRAM 大小），并证明在标准 FlashAttention 式 score-recomputation 模型下任何精确注意力 BoB 都有匹配的 Ω(N²d²/M) 下界——即达到该模型类内的最优。
- **三级实证**：孤立注意力核（A100 80GB）、完整 GPT-2 二阶步（B200）、1B token FineWeb Sophia-H 训练（8×A6000），开源 Triton 实现（github.com/MingruiLiu-ML-Lab/FlashBoB）。

## Key Contributions

- Formalizes the attention backward-over-backward (BoB) primitive — the shared computational bottleneck of second-order optimizers (Hessian-vector products), test-time training, meta-learning, and gradient-written memory — and delivers the first FlashAttention-style (exact, tiled, streaming) implementation of it.
- Affine fusion (Proposition 4.1): a hierarchical affine structure in the softmax double backward (two row scalars α, E determine all outputs) collapses the natural four-sweep schedule into two passes using six row accumulators plus five free quantities — no N×N intermediates, one write per finalized output block.
- I/O theory: Θ(N²d²/M) HBM traffic (Theorem 4.3/Corollary 4.4), with a matching Ω(N²d²/M) lower bound for any exact FlashAttention-style score-recomputation BoB (Theorem 4.5), plus an exactness and FLOPs theorem (Θ(N²d), Theorem 4.2).
- Demonstrated feasibility shift: exact attention BoB at N=262,144 on a single A100 80GB (52.7 GiB peak), where PyTorch materialization fails at N=8,192 and GradMem-derived HVP kernels fail at N=16,384; end-to-end Sophia-H training on 1B FineWeb tokens is 2.17× faster with matched loss.

## Methodology

**BoB primitive.** Let g(Q,K,V,dO) → (dQ,dK,dV,ddO) be the first-backward map of softmax attention. BoB computes J^T U for upstream matrices U_Q,U_K,U_V,U_dO — the Hessian-vector product through the attention block — via reverse-mode AD applied to the scalar Φ = ⟨dQ,U_Q⟩ + ⟨dK,U_K⟩ + ⟨dV,U_V⟩ + ⟨ddO,U_dO⟩. The paper derives closed forms (Eqs. 6–7) showing the four BoB outputs split into two matrix-valued outputs (d̃(Q), d̃(dO)) owned by rows and two (d̃(K), d̃(V)) owned by columns, each expressible through two new row-wise scalars: α_i = Σ_j P_ij(dP_ij + C_ij) (the BoB analog of the first-backward scalar D_i) and E_i = Σ_j P_ij(F_ij − α_i D_ij).

**Affine fusion (two-pass schedule).** Although the row scalars appear sequential (α_i → d̃(P)_ij → E_i → d̃(S)_ij), both scalars only *affinely* affect downstream quantities. Proposition 4.1 names six row accumulators (with α-free, tilewise-computable summands: E_i°, B̃_i, d̃(dO)_i°, Ω_i, R_i, plus the P_ij accumulators) so that a pass can precompute α-free row quantities and apply corrections once α is known without revisiting the row. The result is Algorithm 1: Pass 1 (row-major) holds a query-row block, streams key/value column blocks, accumulates the six rows, applies scalar updates, and writes (α, E, d̃(Q), d̃(dO)); Pass 2 (column-major) holds a key-value block, streams query-row blocks, reads (α, E), and writes d̃(K), d̃(V). Block sizes keep a key-value block resident in SRAM (b = ⌊M/(cBoB d)⌋), and only the two length-N vectors cross the pass boundary.

**Theory.** In the standard two-level (HBM/SRAM) memory model: Theorem 4.2 proves exactness and Θ(N²d) FLOPs within budget B_r B_c + (B_r+B_c)(d+O(1)) ≤ M; Theorem 4.3 counts HBM transfers (12Nd + 4N + (4Nd+N)/B_r^(1) + (4Nd+N)/B_c^(2)); in the large-cache regime (d² ≲ M ≲ Nd) this is Θ(N²d²/M) (Corollary 4.4), and Theorem 4.5 proves a matching lower bound for any exact BoB under FlashAttention-style score recomputation — including dense and causal attention. The analysis is single-head but claimed to extend to multi-head.

**Experiments.** Three levels, all replacing only the attention double-backward with a Triton kernel (inputs scaled into F·F·T SDPA form): (1) isolated attention-layer BoB on an A100 80GB — GPT-2 Small shape (batch 4, 12 heads, head dim 64, causal mask, BF16 inputs, FP32 reductions) vs the PyTorch materializing reference (MATH) and GradMem-derived hand-written HVP kernels (HVP-M, HVP-S); (2) end-to-end second-order GPT-2 Small/Medium/Large steps on a B200 against the same baselines; (3) Sophia-H training of GPT-2 Small on 1B FineWeb tokens (N=2048, 8×A6000 48GB, effective batch 256, 1,908 steps, Hessian diagonal every 10 steps) vs AdamW and Sophia-H with the PyTorch math backend, with matched optimizer/model/data/seed/batch settings and a loss-parity check. FlashBack (the closest prior exact non-materializing BoB) is compared separately on A6000, the setup its implementation supports.

## Results & Highlights

- **Isolated kernel (A100 80GB, GPT-2 Small attention, causal, BF16):** wall-clock at N=4,096 is 25.80 ms for FlashBoB vs 265.67 ms for PyTorch MATH (10.3×) and 89.14 ms for GradMem HVP-S (3.5×); speedup over MATH grows from 1.9× at N=256 to 10.3× at N=4,096. Peak memory at N=8,192 is 1,702 MiB vs 55,971 MiB (HVP-M) and 56,020 MiB (HVP-S) — ~33× less.
- **Feasibility at long context:** FlashBoB runs exact attention BoB at N=262,144 on one A100 80GB at 52.7 GiB (53,952 MiB) peak (365.73 ms and 3,372 MiB at N=16,384), while every prior exact baseline fails by N=16,384 (MATH OOMs already at N=8,192) — the authors call this "a qualitative shift in feasibility rather than a marginal speedup."
- **Head-dimension sweep:** across d ∈ {32, 64, 128}, FlashBoB is 1.27×–7.83× faster than the GradMem-derived HVP baselines with 8.6×–33.1× less peak memory; the runtime gap narrows at d=128 (register/tile pressure) but the HBM advantage persists.
- **vs FlashBack (closest prior exact BoB, A6000):** 2.07×–6.32× faster across shared sequence lengths N=256–65,536.
- **Full-model GPT-2 second-order step (B200):** FlashBoB is fastest at every tested configuration — at the largest settings where all references fit it is 1.76×–2.08× faster than MATH and 1.44×–1.59× faster than HVP-S, with 4.3×–4.4× lower peak memory than MATH; it also runs N=32,768 (GPT-2 Small; 5,368.3 ms, 128,392 MiB) and N=16,384 (Medium/Large; e.g., Large 7,106.5 ms, 150,019 MiB) where every reference OOMs.
- **FineWeb training (GPT-2 Small, 1B tokens, N=2048, 8×A6000, Sophia-H):** Hessian-estimation event drops 13,500→5,500 ms (2.45×); total Hessian time 42.8→17.4 min; full training wall time 3.30→1.52 hr (2.17×); peak memory 30,644→13,830 MiB per GPU (2.22×). Final losses match: AdamW 3.6759, Sophia-H math backend 3.6674, Sophia-H + FlashBoB 3.6662, with near-overlapping loss trajectories — the optimization path is preserved.
- **Honest backend-effect decomposition:** because PyTorch's `scaled_dot_product_attention` supports no double backward, the math-reference Sophia-H must run a slow math attention path on *all* steps (per-step non-Hessian time 4.9 s vs 2.43 s for FlashBoB). The authors separate the 2.45× Hessian-event kernel gain (true BoB contribution) from the 2.17× full-loop gain (kernel + backend integration).

## Authors & Credibility

Anthony Givans, Michael Crawshaw, and Mingrui Liu (corresponding author) are from the Department of Computer Science at George Mason University. The group maintains a public GitHub lab organization (MingruiLiu-ML-Lab) and released the Triton implementation, which materially supports reproducibility. Mingrui Liu's research area is optimization (the paper's flagship application is Sophia-H second-order training, consistent with the group's expertise). The paper is a preprint (arXiv 2609.24089v1, 21 Sep 2026) — not yet peer-reviewed — but it is unusually theory-complete for a systems paper (correctness, FLOPs, I/O upper bound, and a matching lower bound under an explicit computational model) and validates at three levels with matched seeds/settings and a loss-parity check.

## Critical Assessment

- **The "optimality" claim is model-conditional.** Theorem 4.5's Ω(N²d²/M) lower bound holds only within the FlashAttention-style *score-recomputation* model — the very computational regime FlashBoB inhabits. The authors state this conditionality honestly, but "matches the lower bound" should not be read as global optimality: an exact BoB in a different regime (e.g., one allowing different recomputation/caching trade-offs) is not ruled out.
- **Baseline suite is partly soft.** MATH (full N×N materialization) is a strawman that OOMs at N=8,192. The real comparisons are GradMem-derived HVP kernels and FlashBack — but the FlashBack comparison runs on A6000 (the hardware FlashBack's implementation supports) rather than the paper's primary A100 setup, and FlashBack was *not* evaluated at N=131,072 or N=262,144. So the headline "scales to N=262,144" is never cross-checked against the strongest prior method on identical hardware at that scale; only FlashBack's failure mode at large A100-only lengths is reported indirectly.
- **The end-to-end 2.17× conflates two effects.** Part of the FineWeb win comes not from the BoB kernel but from removing PyTorch's double-backward incompatibility, which forces the math-reference to a slow attention path on all steps (4.9 s vs 2.43 s per-step non-Hessian). A hybrid baseline (optimized attention on the 9/10 ordinary steps, math only for Hessian steps) would close part of the gap but is unavailable through standard PyTorch. Credit where due: the authors explicitly decompose 2.45× (kernel) vs 2.17× (full loop) and name this confound themselves — but the headline number still overstates the pure algorithmic contribution.
- **The training experiment doesn't exercise the regime the algorithm targets.** FineWeb training runs at N=2048 — modest context, and 2048×2048 attention is a small fraction of a GPT-2 Small step. The long-context feasibility story (N ≥ 16k) is demonstrated only on isolated kernels and single full-model steps, not in an actual long-context training run; Sophia-H's benefit at long context is precisely where curvature estimation is claimed to matter.
- **Motivated applications are asserted, not benchmarked.** Test-time training, meta-learning, and gradient-written memory motivate the whole paper, but the only downstream application evaluated is Sophia-H diagonal-Hessian estimation. The discussion defers "whether these methods retain or improve their algorithmic advantages once BoB is no longer dominant" to future work.
- **Implementation maturity partially confounds kernel numbers.** FlashBoB is generic Triton with no warp specialization/TMA/async features (stated limitation), while HVP-M/HVP-S are hand-written; part of the speed gap at small N or d=128 (where the advantage shrinks to 1.27×) may reflect implementation rather than algorithm. Conversely this means FlashBoB's absolute numbers likely still have headroom.
- Otherwise the work is rigorous for a preprint: exactness theorem, matched upper/lower I/O bounds with the caveat stated, correctness confirmed, and a genuine loss-parity validation (3.6662 vs 3.6674) rather than a hand-wavy "trains fine" claim.

## Strengths

- Exact — no approximation to attention or its derivatives; same Θ(N²d) FLOPs as the forward pass, so the win is pure memory-hierarchy scheduling, in the true spirit of FlashAttention.
- Real algorithmic insight: the affine-fusion observation (two row scalars determine all BoB outputs) is clean, provably collapses four passes to two, and is more than an engineering trick.
- Complete theory for a systems paper: correctness, FLOPs, HBM-traffic upper bound, and a matching lower bound within an explicitly stated computational model.
- Massive and honest feasibility result: N=16,384 (where everything else dies) → N=262,144 on the same single A100, with the memory ratio *improving* with sequence length.
- Careful experimental hygiene: three evaluation levels, matched training settings, explicit decomposition of kernel-level vs backend-integration gains, and a loss-parity check against AdamW and math-backend Sophia-H.
- Open-source Triton implementation; masked variants (causal, sliding-window) covered in appendices.

## Limitations

- Generic Triton implementation without architecture-specific features (warp specialization, async tile movement) — absolute performance will change, and comparisons vs hand-written baselines partly reflect implementation maturity.
- The I/O lower bound (Theorem 4.5) applies only within the FlashAttention-style score-recomputation model; optimality outside that class is open.
- FlashBack — the strongest prior exact BoB — is compared on A6000 at shared lengths only, not on the A100 at the extreme lengths that headline the paper.
- Analysis and evaluations cover single-head dense (plus sliding-window in an appendix); no GQA/MQA, no modern architectures (e.g., RoPE-positioned LLMs), and the head-dim sweep stops at d=128 where the runtime advantage narrows to 1.27×.
- Only Sophia-H diagonal-Hessian estimation is demonstrated end-to-end; test-time training, meta-learning, and gradient-written memory remain motivated-but-untested, and the training run uses N=2048, far below the sequence lengths the algorithm enables.
- Whether second-order methods keep their accuracy advantage once BoB is cheap is explicitly left to future work.

## Relevance

与长上下文大模型训练效率和二阶优化（Sophia-H）直接相关：它把 FlashAttention 式精确流式调度首次推进到二阶微分原语，使 Hessian 类方法和"梯度即模型操作"的长上下文学习（测试时训练、元学习）在 N≈26 万 token 下变得可行。如果你关注 GPU 内存墙下的长上下文训练系统或二阶方法的工程可用性，这篇值得一读。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the two-pass affine-fusion schedule compose with GQA/MQA and modern position encodings, and how do the block-size budgets change under grouped-query layouts?
- At what sequence length does Sophia-H-with-cheap-BoB actually beat well-tuned AdamW/AdamW-variants in loss-per-wall-clock, given the paper leaves second-order benefits at long context untested?
- Can the same row-scalar affine-fusion idea extend to third-order derivatives, or to non-softmax operators (linear attention, kernelized attention) where the double backward lacks the softmax normalization structure?
- How does FlashBoB interact with sequence/activation parallelism in real long-context training, where the column-major second pass may cross device boundaries?
- Would a hand-tuned CUDA/TMA implementation (vs generic Triton) change the qualitative picture at d=128 and small N, where the advantage currently narrows?
