> **一句话总结：** 针对 greedy 解码在 A100/L40S/H100 三代 NVIDIA GPU 上输出不一致的问题（未缓解 BF16 跨 GPU 分歧率 30.81–100%），本文提出 ReproFuse：用"固定配置 + 寄存器内 BF16→FP32 上转型 + IEEE-754 FMA + 确定性 split-K"的 Triton GEMM 内核替换 LayerCast 的"FP32 权重拷贝 + 厂商 GEMM"路线，把归约顺序变成问题形状的纯函数，使线性层输出跨架构逐比特一致——12 个模型×基准配置中 11 个分歧率精确为 0，同时端到端比 LayerCast 快 1.17–3.1×，并省出 1–1.4 GiB 权重内存（KV 容量 +4–7%）。

> **TL;DR:** ReproFuse makes LLM greedy decoding bit-identical across NVIDIA Ampere/Ada/Hopper GPUs by construction, not statistically. It replaces LayerCast's (NeurIPS 2025) cast-to-FP32-then-vendor-GEMM linear layers with fixed-configuration fused-upcast Triton GEMM kernels (IEEE-754 FMA on CUDA cores, deterministic split-K, no autotuning, batch-invariant), so the floating-point reduction order is a pure function of the problem shape. On GSM8K/MATH500/AIME24/GPQA-Diamond with Llama-3.2-3B, Qwen3-4B, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-8B across A100/L40S/H100, unmitigated BF16 diverges on 30.81–100% of problems across GPUs (median ≈85%) and LayerCast still leaves up to 0.51%; ReproFuse is exactly 0 in 11 of 12 cells (vs LayerCast's 0.23% on the last cell) and 0 cross-seed everywhere. It is faster than LayerCast in all 36 configurations (1.17–1.43× on A100/H100, 1.6–3.1× on the bandwidth-rich L40S) and frees ~1–1.4 GiB of weight memory into KV cache (+4–7% capacity).

## 核心贡献

- **定位残余分歧源头**：指出此前所有跨 GPU 复现方案（含 SOTA LayerCast）都"缓慢且仅统计意义上可复现"的原因——GEMM 归约顺序仍是设备相关的：厂商 cuBLAS 按架构启发式选内核，FP32 tensor core 内部累加位宽未定义且逐代不同。
- **ReproFuse 内核设计**：用一组固定配置的融合上转型 Triton GEMM 内核实现 BF16 存储 / FP32 计算的线性层：BF16 权重直接从 HBM 读入寄存器上转为 FP32 参与累加，省掉 LayerCast 每层 2|W| 的临时 FP32 权重拷贝；四条设计规则（R1 只用 IEEE FMA 标量指令、R2 禁止 autotune、R3 确定性 split-K、R4 构造性批不变）保证归约顺序是问题形状 (M,N,K) 的纯函数。
- **构造性（而非经验性）跨架构逐比特一致**：种子探针实验中 7/7 个 GEMM 形状（decode/prefill、BF16/FP32 权重、非整除维度）在 A100/L40S/H100 上逐比特一致；而 LayerCast 的 cast-then-cuBLAS 路径没有任何形状在三种 GPU 上全一致。
- **修复 LayerCast 的 lm_head 存储缺陷**：发现 LayerCast 的权重转换过滤器只匹配 attention/MLP 投影，把输出投影 lm_head 留在 FP32（8B 模型浪费 vocab×hidden×2 bytes ≈ 上 GiB）；ReproFuse 统一以 BF16 存储 lm_head。
- **端到端验证与性能**：在 vLLM 0.8.6 上，跨 GPU 贪心解码分歧从未缓解 BF16 的 30.81–100% / LayerCast 的 ≤0.51% 降到 12 格中 11 格精确为 0（唯一残余 0.08% 出自未钉住的 attention 内核而非线性层）；且在全部 36 个配置上比 LayerCast 快 1.17–3.1×。

## Key Contributions

- **Diagnosis**: explains why prior cross-GPU reproducibility mitigations — including the SOTA LayerCast — are slow and only *statistically* reproducible: the residual divergence channel is the GEMM reduction order, which vendor libraries select per-architecture, compounded by unspecified tensor-core FP32 accumulate widths.
- **ReproFuse**: a set of fixed-configuration, fused-upcast Triton GEMM kernels for the BF16-storage/FP32-compute linear layers. BF16 weights are upcast to FP32 *in registers* inside the kernel (halving weight traffic vs LayerCast's per-layer transient FP32 weight copy), and four design rules — R1 IEEE-754 FMA only, R2 no autotuning, R3 deterministic split-K, R4 batch invariance by construction — make the complete reduction order a pure function of the problem shape.
- **Cross-architecture bit-identical linear-layer outputs by construction**: a seeded kernel probe shows 7/7 shapes (decode and prefill regimes, BF16 and FP32 weights, ragged dims) bit-identical across A100, L40S, and H100, whereas LayerCast's cast-then-cuBLAS path produces identical bits on all three architectures for *no* shape.
- **A real bug fix in LayerCast**: its weight-conversion filter silently exempts the output projection (`lm_head`), storing it in FP32 — a vocab×hidden×2-byte cost (≈1 GiB on an 8B model). ReproFuse stores `lm_head` in BF16 like every other projection.
- **End-to-end validation with speed**: on vLLM, cross-GPU greedy-decoding divergence drops from 30.81–100% (unmitigated BF16) and ≤0.51% (LayerCast) to exactly 0 in 11 of 12 model×benchmark cells (the single 0.08% residual comes from the unpinned attention kernel on H100, not the linear layers), while running 1.17–3.1× faster than LayerCast in all 36 configurations.

## Methodology

**Key insight.** Cross-architecture divergence starts when the top-1/top-2 logit gap (decision margin) at a position is smaller than the cross-GPU logit shift. Since typical margins are 4–19 nats but ~1 in 10⁴ positions sits within 10⁻³ nats of a tie, arithmetic noise flips tokens exactly at those tied positions. Therefore any mitigation that drives the *linear-layer* channel (the dominant FLOPs) to zero removes the dominant divergence source — if the reduction order is fixed a priori rather than left to vendor kernel-selection heuristics.

**ReproFuse design (four rules).** It reimplements the linear layers of a hybrid-precision (BF16 storage, FP32 compute) inference pipeline as fixed-configuration Triton GEMM kernels computing `y = x·up(W_bf16)ᵀ + b`:
- **R1 — IEEE FMA only.** Every dot product is an IEEE-754 scalar FMA on CUDA cores. Triton's `tl.dot` on FP32 operands would dispatch to TF32 tensor cores, whose internal accumulation width is unspecified and differs across Ampere/Ada/Hopper.
- **R2 — No autotuning.** Tiling, split factor, and K-block size are compile-time constants selected by problem shape alone (one configuration for decode-shaped GEMMs M ≤ 64, one for prefill-shaped GEMMs, per GEMM). Autotuned configs were shown to be a function of the device and even of measurement noise.
- **R3 — Deterministic split-K.** K is cut into S contiguous segments where S is a pure function of K; each segment is accumulated sequentially by exactly one program into its own workspace, and a second kernel reduces the S partials in fixed ascending order. No atomics anywhere, so the reduction tree is device-independent.
- **R4 — Batch invariance by construction.** Within the decode bucket, K-segmentation depends only on K, so a given request's output is bit-identical for any co-scheduled batch size 1–64.

It also fixes LayerCast's `lm_head` FP32-storage exemption (recovered ≈1 GiB on an 8B model). Attention, rotary embeddings, normalization, and the sampler remain the engine's vendor FP32 kernels — pinned only for linear layers.

**Evaluation setup.** Three NVIDIA architectures (A100 PCIe SM80, L40S SM89, H100 PCIe SM90); PyTorch 2.6.0, CUDA 12.4, Triton 3.2.0, vLLM 0.8.6 with Matrix multiplication + xformers attention; three instruction-tuned models (Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, Qwen3-4B-Instruct 2507, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B); GSM8K, MATH500, AIME24, GPQA-Diamond; greedy decoding, batch 32, 3 seeds per cell. The Llama template's date string was pinned (an unpinned date produced a spurious 57.60% cross-GPU "divergence" spanning calendar-day boundaries). Comparisons: unmitigated BF16, LayerCast (ablation of the kernel design), ReproFuse. Supplements add per-rule ablations (R1–R4), a Div_Index metric (mean first-divergence token index), and a roofline analysis of why the L40S gains exceed the H100's.

## Results & Highlights

- **End-to-end cross-GPU determinism (Table 1, 12 model×benchmark cells):** unmitigated BF16 diverges on 30.81–100% of problems across A100/L40S/H100 (median ≈85%); LayerCast is ≤1% everywhere but leaves a residual on 5 of 12 configurations, up to 0.51% (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-8B on GSM8K). ReproFuse is ≤ LayerCast in every cell: exactly 0 on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-8B across all four benchmarks, 0 on Llama-3.2-3B across all four, and 0 on Qwen3-4B except GSM8K at 0.08% vs LayerCast's 0.23% on that same cell — the one residual is a single FP32 tie resolved by the unpinned attention kernel on H100. Cross-seed divergence: unmitigated 6–25% of pairs on the same GPU; LayerCast and ReproFuse both exactly 0 in every cell.
- **Kernel-level probe:** all 7 GEMM cases are bit-identical across all three architectures under ReproFuse; through LayerCast's cast-then-cuBLAS path, no shape produces identical bits on all three GPUs (A100 vs L40S disagree on all 7, H100 vs L40S on 5 of 7), yet 88–99% of output elements agree — agreement by coincidence of undocumented kernel-selection heuristics sits four orders of magnitude below BF16 rounding noise.
- **Decision-margin analysis (3.4M positions):** unmitigated BF16's median cross-GPU logit shift is 6.3×10⁻² nats, driving 31–100% of problems to diverge; LayerCast lowers the shift ~3.5 orders of magnitude (median ~6×10⁻⁵ nats A100↔L40S, 3.6×10⁻⁵ vs H100) but only 4.0% and 2.3% of positions come out bitwise equal; ReproFuse is bitwise identical at 98.4% of positions A100↔L40S and 100.000% on both Llama-3.2-3B and Qwen3-4B (vs H100 the linear-layer channel is eliminated but a 7.9×10⁻⁹-nat attention-kernel residual remains, 9.6% bitwise equal).
- **Performance (Figure 4, 36 configurations):** ReproFuse is faster than LayerCast in all 36 — by 1.17–1.43× on A100 and H100 and by 1.6–3.1× on L40S. The asymmetry is explained by rooflines: ReproFuse reads BF16 weights (half the bytes) while LayerCast reads FP32-width transient copies, so the gain scales with each GPU's FP32-compute-to-bandwidth ratio (A100 12.5, H100 25.6, L40S 106 FLOP/byte). Cost: in prefill the IEEE-FMA Triton kernel reaches only ~0.8× cuBLAS throughput.
- **Memory (Table 2):** on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-8B, weights drop from 16.93 GiB (LayerCast) to 15.94 GiB and the transient per-step allocation spike from 0.445 to 0.051 GiB, converting into 74,912 vs 70,832 KV-cache tokens under vLLM's fixed budget (+5.8%); on Qwen3-4B, 30.41→28.96 GiB and 128,288→133,536 KV tokens (+4.1%).
- **Accuracy unchanged:** on Qwen3-4B, GSM8K (93.9%) and MATH500 (87.2%) means are identical across the three methods; Llama-3.2-3B differs by only 1–2 problems per benchmark (numerical noise at the 10⁻² level vs FP64 for both methods).
- **Per-rule ablations (supplement):** R1 — IEEE FMA is bitwise-identical 9/9 cases across GPUs while TF32 tensor cores are 0/9 (A100+L40S agree, both differ from H100) and carry 3 orders of magnitude more error vs FP64 (7.6×10⁻⁴ vs 4.8×10⁻⁶); IEEE FMA costs 2.4× at median decode and 5.6× at median prefill vs TF32, but the fair baseline (LayerCast's cuBLAS SGEMM) also runs on FP32 CUDA cores. R2 — the identical autotune search picks the same config on all three GPUs for only 3 of 9 shapes, and re-running it on one node changes its own winner on 1 of 9 shapes; 72 decode candidates produce 4 distinct outputs. R3 — classic split-K yields 50 distinct results in 50 runs on 15 of 18 shape/GPU cells; ordered split-K is 1.9–6.5× faster than no-split on 5 of 6 projections. R4 — ReproFuse is batch-invariant on all 18 (shape, GPU) cells vs 4/18 for occupancy- or per-M-tuned policies.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty is real but incremental-over-LayerCast in mechanism.** The genuinely new property is *by-construction* cross-architecture bit-identity for linear layers plus batch invariance; LayerCast already had the BF16-storage/FP32-compute idea and achieved near-determinism statistically. ReproFuse's contribution is pinning the last reduction-order channel and — surprisingly — making it faster, because the fused upcast removes LayerCast's transient FP32 weight copy. The individual techniques (scalar FMA GEMM, split-K, pinned configs) are standard HPC practice; the contribution is the system-level argument that reduction order must be a pure function of shape, and the evidence that vendor kernel-selection heuristics are the residual channel.

**The "faster" headline needs context.** All 36 speedup comparisons are against LayerCast, itself a niche reproducibility configuration that runs FP32 on CUDA cores. Against a standard unmitigated BF16 tensor-core vLLM stack, Figure 4's gray bars show unmitigated is often the *fastest* configuration, and the R1 ablation admits IEEE-FMA GEMMs are 2.4× slower than TF32 tensor-core GEMMs at the median decode shape. So the honest reading is: ReproFuse is the fastest *deterministic* option, not a free lunch — the paper states this but the abstract's "1.17 to 3.1× faster" could be misread as faster than ordinary inference.

**Scope of the guarantee is partial, and the paper is appropriately upfront about it.** Only linear layers are pinned; attention, rotary embeddings, normalization, and the sampler remain vendor kernels. This is exactly where the single surviving end-to-end failure comes from (Qwen3-4B GSM8K, 0.08%: an FP32 tie in the layer-0 attention kernel on H100 resolved differently by `argmax`). Complete determinism would require pinning those kernels too, which the design rules do not yet cover.

**Evaluation scale is modest.** Three models of 3B–8B, single GPU, batch 32, greedy decoding only, one software stack (vLLM 0.8.6), and only four reasoning benchmarks. The memory-freed-into-KV story matters most for large models, which are absent (no 70B-class run); tensor parallelism — where cross-rank reductions add another divergence channel — is untested. Sub-1% divergence cells rest on very few divergent problems (the supplement concedes as few as one distinct problem behind LayerCast's GPQA-Diamond Div_Index of 3551), so those numbers are small-sample estimates. All NVIDIA: AMD CDNA/Intel Gaudi, whose FMA semantics may differ, remain open (acknowledged in the conclusion).

**Reproducibility of the paper itself is good:** code released, hardware specs tabulated, seeds pinned, and a deterministic probe harness with deliberately ragged shapes documented. The four design rules are stated precisely enough to re-implement.

## Strengths

- Converts cross-architecture reproducibility from an empirical tendency into a construction, with a clean invariant: reduction order as a pure function of problem shape — and proves each rule necessary via targeted ablations (e.g., TF32 0/9 vs IEEE 9/9 bit-identical; 50/50 distinct results under atomic split-K; 5 distinct outputs from sweeping the split factor S).
- Rare negative-result hygiene: the H100/SM90 attention-kernel residual, the LayerCast `lm_head` FP32 bug, the autotuner's noise sensitivity, and the Llama date-string measurement artifact are all reported rather than hidden.
- Simultaneously improves the three axes users care about — determinism (≤ prior SOTA in every cell), speed (1.17–3.1× vs LayerCast, all 36 configs), and memory (−1–1.4 GiB weights, +4–7% KV capacity) — with a mechanistic roofline explanation (FP32-compute-to-bandwidth ratio) for why the L40S gains the most.
- The decision-margin/shift analysis (median margin 4–19 nats, flips at the ~10⁻³-nat tail) gives the field a reusable vocabulary for reasoning about *when* numerical noise changes outputs.
- Batch invariance by construction addresses a second, independent pathology (batch-composition-dependent outputs) at no extra cost.

## Limitations

- Linear layers only: attention, normalization, rotary embeddings, and the sampler remain engine/vendor kernels and are the source of the remaining 0.08% end-to-end divergence; full-stack determinism is not achieved.
- Absolute performance vs standard tensor-core BF16 inference is paid (IEEE FMA GEMM is 2.4× slower than TF32 at decode-shaped GEMMs; prefill ~0.8× cuBLAS); the speedup claim is relative to LayerCast only.
- Evaluated at 3B–8B scale, single GPU, batch 32, greedy decoding, one framework version (vLLM 0.8.6); no tensor parallelism, no 70B-class models, no temperature-sampled decoding analysis.
- NVIDIA-only (Ampere/Ada/Hopper); portability to AMD/Intel/TPU stacks — where even the FMA-by-construction premise needs re-verification — is future work.
- Sub-1% divergence cells (LayerCast's 0.15–0.51%) are small-sample estimates resting on one to a few divergent problems; some Table 5 Div_Index entries are not meaningful at that scale.
- Cost of R2/R4 is acknowledged but nontrivial: per-batch-size re-tuning would be up to 25% faster at M ≤ 16 on A100/H100, so the shape-pure policy forgoes that.

## Relevance

这篇论文与 LLM 推理系统与异构 GPU 部署直接相关：对需要跨硬件复现实验、做确定性回归测试或满足审计合规（如金融/医疗 agent 场景）的 LLM 服务而言，ReproFuse 给出了"线性层归约顺序 = 形状的纯函数"这一可落地的工程原则，且比 LayerCast 更快更省显存；同时也提醒我们 attention/sampler 通道仍未闭合。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Can the shape-pure reduction-order principle be extended to attention (flash-attention split-K over sequence positions) and the sampler to close the remaining 0.08% divergence channel end-to-end?
2. How does ReproFuse compose with tensor/pipeline parallelism, where cross-rank reduction order introduces an additional device- and topology-dependent channel?
3. What is the absolute throughput/latency price against a standard BF16 tensor-core serving stack at 70B scale — and does the freed weight memory (KV capacity +4–7%) justify it in production serving?
4. Does the "IEEE-754 FMA is bit-specified given operands" premise hold on AMD CDNA, Intel Gaudi/PVC, or TPUv5, i.e., is cross-*vendor* bit-identity achievable with the same four rules?
5. Could the same fixed-order kernels make training or LoRA fine-tuning bitwise reproducible across GPUs, where accumulated divergence across thousands of steps is far more damaging than single-token flips in decoding?
