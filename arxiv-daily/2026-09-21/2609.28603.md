> **一句话总结：** Meta FAIR 团队把定理的"内在有趣度"形式化为「（预测的）证明行数 ÷ 陈述描述长度」，在 Mathlib 上用 premise expansion 自动合成约 11 万条证明难度标注，以 GRPO 训练的 Qwen3.6-27B 难度预测器将 MAE 压到 20.2 行（GPT-5.5 为 31.0、Claude Opus 4.6 为 34.3，Spearman 0.912 vs 0.776/0.815）；再以该预测器为奖励信号训练"猜想模型"，使生成定理的平均经 Lean 验证的真实有趣度从 1.76 提升到 7.58（约 4.3 倍），与 Mathlib 既有内容的实质重合率从 91.9% 降到 30.6%，并在 6 轮迭代式发现实验中证明以有趣度做推理时剪枝的排序在盲评中 60% 排第一（最长证明剪枝仅 19%）。

> **TL;DR:** The paper defines a theorem's intrinsic interestingness as verified Lean proof length divided by statement description length, grounded in the MATHLIB library. A GRPO-trained Qwen3.6-27B proof-difficulty predictor achieves MAE 20.2 proof lines vs 31.0 (GPT-5.5) and 34.3 (Claude Opus 4.6) on 4,615 held-out MATHLIB prompts (Spearman ρ 0.912 vs 0.776/0.815). Using that predictor as an RL reward for a conjecturing model raises mean ground-truth interestingness of generated theorems from 1.76 to 7.58 (~4.3x, area ratios 2.10x in combinatorics to 8.72x in number theory) while cutting "substantially/fully contained in MATHLIB" overlap from 91.9% (base) and 92.5% (Claude Opus 4.6 conjecturer) to 30.6%. At inference time, interestingness-based promotion of verified theorems is ranked first by a blinded LLM judge in 60% of four-way comparisons vs 19% for longest-proof pruning.

## 核心贡献

- **难度形式化**：将"证明难度"定义为给定前提集 P 下推出定理 T 所需的 Lean 证明行数 V(T|P)，并提出三条公理（grounding、premise monotonicity、composition/Bellman 一致性），据此设计 RL 训练奖励。
- **自动数据合成**：提出 premise expansion——沿 Mathlib 依赖 DAG 游走，把某条被引用引理 L 从前提中"抽走"，迫使模型预测补证 L 的额外代价（c+ℓ），从 113,547 个 Mathlib 声明中构造出 18,318 个训练组（109,908 条提示）与 923 个验证组（5,538 条提示），标签中位数 49 行、最大 500 行。
- **27B 难度预测器**：GRPO 微调 Qwen3.6-27B（350 步，奖励含 truth/Bellman/drop 三项，权重 0.5/0.35/0.15），在 4,615 条留出验证提示上 MAE 20.2 行、Spearman 0.912，全面优于 GPT-5.5（31.0 / 0.776）和 Claude Opus 4.6（34.3 / 0.815）；前沿模型系统性低估长证明难度且校准更差。
- **有趣度与效用指标**：定义内在有趣度 I(T|P) = 100·V(T|P)/L(T|P)（L 为陈述字符数+递归定义词表长度），在 Mathlib 上验证合理（1ⁿ=1 位于底部、FLT(n=3) 位于顶部）；定义效用 U₀(T)=|D(T)|·V(T|∅)（下游引用节省的行数），两者 Spearman ρ=0.756，说明"有趣"可在提议时刻估计且是效用的先验代理。
- **可优化的发现循环**：以冻结的 V̂ 为奖励（R=0.25+log(1+I/100)，编译失败 −0.5、与前提无关 −0.25、被 assumption/rfl/simp 等平凡闭合 0）训练猜想模型 75 步 GRPO，平均真实有趣度 1.76→7.58；推理时以有趣度做 6 轮迭代发现的"晋级"剪枝，盲评质量 3.40/多样性 2.10、60% 排名第一，且晋级前候选池本身也更有趣（均值 2.332 vs 无剪枝 1.313），说明收益不纯是选择效应。

## Key Contributions

- Formalizes conditional proof difficulty V(T|P) as Lean proof-line cost with three axioms (grounding, premise monotonicity, Bellman-style composition) and turns it into a trainable objective.
- Introduces premise expansion, a deterministic dataset generator over the MATHLIB dependency DAG (~110k labeled (theorem, premises, difficulty) triplets from 113,547 declarations) that reshuffles which lemmas are "free" versus must be re-derived.
- Trains a 27B difficulty estimator (Qwen3.6-27B + GRPO, 350 steps) that substantially outperforms frontier baselines on held-out calibration (MAE 20.2 vs 31.0/34.3; ρ 0.912 vs 0.776/0.815) and is the only model that stays calibrated at long proof lengths.
- Defines intrinsic interestingness I = 100·V/L and extrinsic utility U₀ = |D(T)|·V(T|∅), and empirically links them (Spearman 0.756 on MATHLIB), giving a proposal-time surrogate for downstream usefulness.
- Shows interestingness is optimizable twice over: as an RL reward for a conjecturer (1.76 → 7.58 mean verified interestingness, overlap with MATHLIB 91.9% → 30.6%) and as an inference-time promotion rule in a 6-round recursive discovery loop that also produces out-of-distribution verified statements absent from MATHLIB.

## Methodology

**Difficulty modeling.** The authors postulate a conditional proof computational cost V(T|P) satisfying (A1) grounding V(T|P)=C_D(T|P) when the ground-truth cost is defined, (A2) premise monotonicity V(T|Q) ≤ V(T|P) for Q ⊇ P, and (A3) composition (Bellman) V(T|P) ≤ V(L|P) + V(T|P∪{L}). The GRPO reward for the Qwen3.6-27B estimator combines a truth term on |log V̂ − log C|, a Bellman residual over lemma-expansion groups, and a one-sided drop penalty that punishes predictions that decrease when premises are removed (weights 0.5/0.35/0.15; all six prompt roles of an expansion group share one group reward).

**Dataset.** From MATHLIB's 113,547 declarations they extract rendered types, file-local definitions, proof sources and line counts. Premise-expansion walks sample a cited lemma, replace the premise set P with (P\{L})∪deps(L) so that L must be derived (label c+ℓ), and emit six role variants per edge (base, remaining_keep, remaining_remove, lemma_context, lemma_native, base_drop with randomly thinned premises). Walks are reservoir-sampled (seed 31 for validation), capped at 300 expansion attempts / 150 premises / 60,000 characters, yielding 18,318 training groups (109,908 prompts) and 923 validation groups (5,538 prompts); grounded labels have mean 92.3, median 49, 90th percentile 245, max 500 proof lines. Evaluation is greedy decoding on 4,615 held-out prompts, MAE/Spearman computed only over parsed non-negative answers (their model parses on all prompts; GPT-5.5 produced 6 invalid outputs).

**Interestingness and utility.** I(T|P) = 100·V(T|P)/L(T|P), where L(T|P) counts statement characters plus the recursive description vocabulary of all definitions needed to state T but not already in P (the 100 factor makes mean İ ≈ 1). Utility U₀(T) = |D(T)|·V(T|∅), the total Lean lines saved across the library by having T available as a premise. On MATHLIB, rank correlation between İ and U₀ is 0.756 after excluding U₀=0 declarations.

**Training the conjecturer.** 10,000 randomly sampled premise sets (median 77 premises, ≥16 required) from the training split; reward R = 0.25 + log(1 + İ(T|P)/100) with the frozen V̂; −0.5 parse failures, −0.25 irrelevant proposals (must share a non-generic identifier with a premise), 0 for statements closed by triviality tactics (assumption, rfl, simp, tauto, simp_all). Compilation is checked with Pantograph; 75 GRPO steps on Qwen3.6-27B.

**Evaluation.** A balanced verified cohort: 8 coarse areas (Algebra, Analysis, Number Theory, Measure/Probability, Geometry/Topology, Combinatorics, Category/Algebraic Geometry, Foundations) × 20 statements per model (base, trained, Claude Opus 4.6 prompted to propose interesting theorems), every statement proved by Claude Code with Opus 4.6 (with a tightly constrained marginal-repair pass) so that all reported interestingness scores use ground-truth proof lengths, not the learned estimator. MATHLIB containment is judged by Claude Opus 4.6 at temperature 0 on a 5-level rubric against Mathlib revision 905b95. The inference-time ablation runs a 6-round discovery loop (400 candidates/round from the Claude 4.6 conjecturer, semantic filter for diversity, independent Lean proofs, promote 10) varying only the promotion rule: keep all / random / longest verified proof / highest verified interestingness, judged by a rule-blinded Claude 4.6 judge (100 four-way groups plus ten 10-statement cohorts per round).

## Results & Highlights

- **Difficulty prediction beats frontier models decisively.** On 4,615 held-out MATHLIB prompts, the trained V̂ reaches MAE 20.2 proof lines vs 31.0 for GPT-5.5 and 34.3 for Claude Opus 4.6, and Spearman ρ 0.912 vs 0.776 / 0.815. Figure 1 shows both frontier models systematically underestimate long proofs (at ~300-line bin, GPT-5.5's predictions collapse back toward ~70), while V̂ tracks the y=x line up to 400 lines.
- **Interestingness training quadruples the metric.** Pooled over 160 verified statements (8 areas × 20), mean ground-truth interestingness rises from 1.76 for the base Qwen3.6-27B to 7.58 for the trained conjecturer (~4.3x), with area-level ratios from 2.10x (combinatorics) to 8.72x (number theory); the trained model beats the Claude Opus 4.6 conjecturer in all 8 areas.
- **The shift is genuinely out-of-distribution.** The fraction of generated statements judged substantially/fully contained in MATHLIB (rubric scores 4-5) drops from 91.9% (base) and 92.5% (Claude 4.6) to 30.6% (trained) — i.e., RL on interestingness pushes the model away from re-deriving library content.
- **Interestingness pruning wins the inference-time ablation.** In 100 blinded four-way comparisons the interestingness-pruned statement is ranked first 60% of the time vs 19% (longest-proof), 13% (no pruning), 8% (random); judge cohort quality 3.40±0.52 vs ≤2.60, diversity 2.10±0.32 vs ≤1.30, mean rank 1.80 vs ≥2.36. Crucially, Table 2 shows the candidate pools *before* selection also improve (mean İ 2.332 vs 1.694 proof-length / 1.313 no-pruning), so the promotion rule changes what is discovered, not merely what is kept; and proof-length pruning underperforms, isolating the ratio (not raw proof length) as the active ingredient.
- **Sanity checks support the metric.** On MATHLIB's 113k statements the interestingness distribution puts trivial identities (1ⁿ=1) at the bottom and Fermat's Last Theorem (n=3) at the top; cross-area analysis (1,440 target-condition pairs) finds a median off-diagonal premise-relative ratio of 0.520 with 73.5% below 1 — e.g., measure theory becomes far less interesting when supplied analytic premises.

## Critical Assessment

- **A circularity lurks in the headline evaluation.** Ground-truth interestingness of the 160 generated statements is computed from proof lengths produced by the Claude Code / Opus 4.6 proving pipeline, while the difficulty model itself was trained on *human-written* MATHLIB proofs. The metric therefore partly measures what one particular agentic prover finds laborious, and "interesting" partially conflates with "hard for Claude Code" — a theorem trivial for a stronger tactic search would lose interestingness without changing its mathematics.
- **The LLM judge is also a competitor and the prover.** Claude Opus 4.6 simultaneously serves as the conjecturing baseline, the proving agent, the containment judge, and the quality/diversity judge — a textbook self-preference risk. The blinded protocol (judge never sees the promotion rule or proposer) mitigates prompt-level bias but not model-level bias, and the 160-statement cohort plus 100 ranking groups is small for the headline claims; no human mathematician validation is reported anywhere.
- **The discovered mathematics is modest.** Appendix C.3's showcased "out-of-distribution" results include e.g. "a finite simple graph has no edges iff its cliques are precisely the vertex sets with fewer than two vertices" and "negation preserves the Jacobian equation" — correct, verified, but routine exercise-level statements. The paper never claims otherwise, but readers should calibrate the phrase "discovering mathematics" accordingly.
- **Missing ablations and baselines.** The three-term difficulty reward (truth / Bellman / drop) is never ablated component-wise, so the contribution of the Bellman residual — arguably the paper's most novel design element — is unquantified. There is also no comparison against existing automated-conjecturing systems (e.g., TxGraffiti/Graffiti lineage) or against intrinsic-motivation LLM work beyond Claude prompted to be interesting.
- **Small internal inconsistency.** The main text states the pruning comparison runs 6 rounds (Figure 7 shows P₁..P₆), while Appendix D aggregates "the ten rounds" in Tables 1-4 — likely two variants of the experiment, but nowhere reconciled.
- **Utility correlation is suggestive, not conclusive.** The ρ=0.756 link between interestingness and utility is measured on MATHLIB's human-curated library; whether the *generated* theorems accrue utility is admitted in the Discussion to be unknown. The 4.3x headline gain is in the paper's own units — convincing as optimization, unproven as mathematical value.

## Strengths

- Everything is grounded in machine-checked Lean proofs: no hallucinated theorems enter any headline number, and ground-truth proof lengths — not the learned estimator — are used for the conjecturer evaluation.
- The difficulty dataset generator (premise expansion) is deterministic, seed-controlled, and principled, turning one library into ~110k supervision signals without human labeling.
- The difficulty estimator is a genuinely useful artifact: it is the only evaluated model that stays calibrated at 300-400 proof lines, which is exactly where frontier models fail.
- The two evaluations (RL reward vs inference-time pruning) are cleanly separated, and the Table 2 pre-selection analysis plus the proof-length-vs-interestingness contrast rule out the cheapest alternative explanation (pure selection of long proofs).
- Exceptional transparency: full prompts, judge rubrics, hyperparameters, seeds, and negative results (frontier-model miscalibration) are all in the paper; the honest Discussion explicitly names the lines-of-code proxy problem and the usefulness gap.

## Limitations

- Proof length in Lean lines is a stylistic artifact: automation tactics like `aesop` and `grind` change line counts at runtime, and the authors themselves suggest Levin complexity as a more robust alternative.
- The definition ignores mathematical value from connecting previously unrelated areas or introducing new definitions; only theorem statements are discovered, never new concepts.
- Whether the growing library's new theorems are *useful* for future proofs is unverified; the utility argument extrapolates from human-curated MATHLIB.
- Evaluation scale is small (160 verified statements; 6-10 discovery rounds from a single graph-theory premise pool) and every judgment layer depends on a single commercial model (Claude Opus 4.6).
- The whole pipeline is anchored to one formal library (MATHLIB) and one proving agent; cross-library or cross-prover transfer of the interestingness metric is untested.

## Relevance

对关注 LLM 推理与强化学习的读者，这是"内在奖励驱动开放式发现"方向少有的端到端实证：它展示了如何把一个可计算的形式化指标（证明难度/描述长度）变成 GRPO 可优化的奖励，并用前提扩展合成出免人工标注的训练数据。其"可验证环境中的代理指标 → RL → 抑制分布内复现"的范式，对其他自举式发现系统（自博弈、自动实验设计）同样有借鉴价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the interestingness gains survive a different proving agent? Since ground-truth scores come from Claude Code's proof lengths, how stable are the 1.76→7.58 gains under a weaker or stronger prover?
- Can the three reward terms of the difficulty model (truth / Bellman / drop) be ablated to isolate how much the Bellman consistency residual contributes to the 0.912 Spearman calibration?
- If the recursive discovery loop runs for tens of rounds instead of six, does the mean interestingness of promoted statements keep rising, plateau, or collapse into near-duplicate families — and what de-duplication mechanism sustains it?
- Would professional mathematicians agree with the Claude-judge rankings (60% ranked-first) used to validate the pruning rule, particularly for the modest statements in Appendix C.3?
- Do promoted high-interestingness lemmas actually accelerate later proofs (i.e., does optimizing I correlate with future utility), which would close the loop the ρ=0.756 MATHLIB correlation only suggests?
