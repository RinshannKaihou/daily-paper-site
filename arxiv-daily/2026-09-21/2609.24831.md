> **一句话总结：** 南京大学团队提出 GRUET，把 ReAct 每一轮的推理过程显式建模为一张由 K−1 条采样推理链合并而成的 DAG（推理节点用 NLI 双向蕴含合并、动作节点用嵌入余弦相似度合并），用图复杂度近似推理空间复杂度作为轮级不确定性，再经简单序列聚合（all-turn/Top-25% 等 6 种）得到轨迹级不确定性；在 9 个 LLM（Qwen3.5、Gemma4 两家族）× 5 个 agentic 代码基准（SWE-bench Verified/Lite、InterCode MBPP/Spider/NL2Bash）上，平均 AUROC 达 75.49，比最强基线 Semantic Entropy 的 58.38 高出 17.11 分，而全部 10 个迁移基线的判别能力在统计上与随机打分器无异。

> **TL;DR:** GRUET quantifies uncertainty of multi-turn ReAct agent trajectories by modeling each turn's reasoning space as a DAG of sampled reasoning chains (reasoning nodes merged via bidirectional NLI entailment, action nodes via embedding cosine similarity) and using graph complexity as turn-level uncertainty, then aggregating across turns. On 5 agentic coding benchmarks (SWE-bench Verified/Lite, InterCode MBPP/Spider/NL2Bash) with 9 LLMs from the Qwen3.5 and Gemma4 families, GRUET (all-turn) reaches AUROC 75.49 / AUPRC 61.10 / AUARC 51.91 averaged over all model-benchmark pairs, beating the strongest of 10 baselines, Semantic Entropy (58.38 / 44.68 / 40.13), by +17.11 / +16.42 / +11.78 points — while all 10 transplanted LLM-UQ baselines (P(True), Verbalized Confidence, Perplexity, Predictive Entropy, EigV, Ecc, Deg, Semantic Density, SentSAR, SE) fail a 3σ significance test against a random score generator.

## 核心贡献

- **问题刻画：** 指出 ReAct 轨迹级不确定性源于逐轮累积的轮级推理不确定性，且动作不确定性已被封装在推理不确定性中（可执行动作由推理链中的文本动作确定性解析而来）；首次在约 40+ 轮的长程 agentic 编码任务（SWE-bench 系列平均 43.76–49.85 轮）上系统评估轨迹不确定性，远难于以往约 7 轮的 QA 式 agent UQ 设定。
- **负结果发现：** 将 10 种主流 LLM UQ 方法（反身式 P(True)/VC、信息式 Ppl/PE、多样性式 Eig/Ecc/Deg/SD/SSAR/SE）逐轮打分再平均迁移到轨迹上，其 AUROC/AUPRC/AUARC 均未超过随机打分器 3 个标准差，判别能力统计上等价于随机——将该领域已有结论（Oh et al.）扩展到了多样性式方法。
- **GRUET 方法：** 用 DAG 显式建模"推理空间"——每轮对同一历史采样 K−1 条候选推理链，节点为推理步骤/文本动作，推理节点若被 NLI 模型（MNLI 微调的 Deberta-large）判定双向蕴含则合并，动作节点若 CodeXEmbed 嵌入余弦相似度超过阈值 τ 则合并；再用 token 级分布统计 + 图拓扑信息估计图复杂度（把 DAG 视作一个 FNN，将节点不确定性编码进偏置做前向传播），作为轮级不确定性。
- **轨迹级聚合：** 提出 6 种免训练聚合策略（First-turn、Last-turn、Head-d%、Tail-d%、Top-d%、All-turn，d 推荐取 25），并发现 all-turn 与 Top-25% 通常最稳，而 First-turn 只需 K−1 次采样（为 all-turn 采样量的 1/L），是性能-成本折中的轻量方案。
- **系统实验：** 4×A100 80GB，1000 次 bootstrap 报告均值±标准差，按 3σ 规则检验显著性；给出超参敏感性分析并给出推荐配置（K=10，Qwen3.5 取 τ=0.7/T=0.6，Gemma4 取 τ=0.94/T=1.0）。

## Key Contributions

- **Problem framing:** Trajectory-level uncertainty of ReAct agents is conjectured to stem from cumulative turn-level reasoning uncertainty; acting uncertainty is subsumed because executable actions are deterministically parsed from textual actions. The paper is the first to evaluate trajectory UQ on long-horizon agentic coding (~40+ ReAct turns on SWE-bench, avg 43.76–49.85 turns) versus ~7-turn QA settings in prior agent-UQ work.
- **Negative finding:** Ten representative LLM UQ methods (reflexive: P(True), Verbalized Confidence; information-based: Perplexity, Predictive Entropy; diversity-based: EigV, Eccentricity, Degree matrix, Semantic Density, SentSAR, Semantic Entropy), applied per-turn and averaged, all fail the 3σ significance rule against a random score generator — extending Oh et al.'s near-random conclusion to the diversity-based family.
- **GRUET method:** Explicitly models the reasoning space of each turn as a DAG built from K−1 additional sampled reasoning chains; reasoning nodes merge under bidirectional NLI entailment (Deberta-large fine-tuned on MNLI), action nodes merge when CodeXEmbed cosine similarity exceeds τ. Graph complexity — computed by treating the DAG as a feedforward network where node uncertainties enter as biases (weight ω=0.1, transformation f(U(v))=−1/U(v)) and reading out an auxiliary sink node — approximates reasoning-space complexity as the turn-level uncertainty score.
- **Trajectory aggregation:** Six training-free aggregation strategies (First-turn, Last-turn, Head-d%, Tail-d%, Top-d%, All-turn; recommended d=25). All-turn and Top-25% are generally strongest; First-turn needs only K−1 samples (1/L of all-turn's cost), offering a lightweight variant.
- **Rigorous evaluation:** 4×A100 80GB; 1000 bootstraps with mean±std; a 3σ statistical-significance criterion; sensitivity analyses over K, τ, T, d with recommended configurations per model family.

## Methodology

**ReAct formalization.** Given a problem x and history h_0, at each turn t the LLM takes h_{t−1} and generates a reasoning chain r_t = (s_{t,1},...,s_{t,n_t}, a_t) of reasoning steps plus a textual action; the action is deterministically parsed, executed, and its observation appended. The full trajectory h_T receives a binary reward ρ (solved/unsolved). GRUET outputs an uncertainty score u ∈ R for the trajectory.

**Turn-level reasoning UQ (three stages).**
1. *Graph construction:* Draw K−1 extra reasoning-chain samples (default K=15) for the same history. Initialize a DAG whose nodes are the history, every reasoning step, and the textual action, with directed edges to the next step. Two merge rules reveal the intrinsic topology: reasoning nodes are merged if an NLI model (Deberta-large fine-tuned on MNLI) predicts bidirectional entailment; action nodes (which contain task-specific content like code) are merged if cosine similarity of CodeXEmbed embeddings exceeds threshold τ. Ancestors are rewired to preserve the DAG property.
2. *Step-level uncertainty:* Token-level uncertainty follows Fu et al.'s confidence estimator, aggregated with sliding windows (w = min(4, |s|)) and a mean over the top-10% of local window scores to yield node uncertainty U(v).
3. *Graph complexity estimation:* The DAG is viewed as a feedforward neural network (per Scarselli et al.). All leaf nodes are connected to an auxiliary node s_a; edge weights are fixed to ω=0.1 (following Bucur & Holme); node uncertainties are transformed via f(U(v)) = −1/U(v) (keeping order and positivity, so more potential branches accumulate higher positive uncertainty) and injected as biases b_v = −1/U(v); the forward-propagated output at s_a is the graph complexity GC(G), i.e., the turn uncertainty u_t. Intuition: a flawed reasoning process branches heavily, producing a complex topology and hence large GC.

**Trajectory-level aggregation.** Motivated by visualizations showing incorrect trajectories' uncertainty runs higher, especially in the head ([0, 0.1]) and tail ([0.75, 1]) of the turn sequence, GRUET proposes position-based (First-turn u=u_1, Last-turn u=u_T, Head-d%, Tail-d%) and value-based (Top-d%) strategies plus the All-turn mean; d=25 is recommended. First-turn requires only K−1 samples total, i.e., 1/L of the all-turn sampling cost over L turns.

**Experimental setup.** Models: Qwen3.5 (35B-A3B, 27B, 9B, 4B, 2B) and Gemma4 (31B, 26B-A4B, 8B-E4B, 5B-E2B). Benchmarks: SWE-bench Verified (500) and Lite (300) via SWE-agent; InterCode MBPP (974), Spider (1034), NL2Bash (200) via InterCode. Baselines: 10 UQ methods implemented via LM-Polygraph plus a Random sampler, all aggregated across turns following prior protocol. Metrics: AUROC, AUPRC, AUARC for selective generation, with 1000 bootstraps; correctness-eval sampling follows official per-family recommendations (Qwen3.5: T=0.6, top-k=20, top-p=0.95; Gemma4: T=1.0, top-k=64, top-p=0.95); max turns M=60 (SWE-bench) / 10 (InterCode). System prompts are slightly modified to emit "Step i:" markers so reasoning steps can be parsed for graph construction.

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline (Table 2, averaged over all evaluated LLM × benchmark pairs):** GRUET with the all-turn strategy reaches AUROC 75.49, AUPRC 61.10, AUARC 51.91, versus 58.38 / 44.68 / 40.13 for the strongest contender Semantic Entropy — average improvements of +17.11 AUROC, +16.42 AUPRC, and +11.78 AUARC. The random baseline sits at 50.00 / 36.57 / 30.57 (AUPRC/AUARC of random equal the average reward).
- **All 10 baselines are statistically near-random:** Under the 3σ rule, no contender's mean AUROC/AUPRC/AUARC exceeds Random by more than three standard deviations — e.g., P(True) AUROC 50.07±2.53, Perplexity 48.20±2.36, Semantic Entropy 58.38±2.30 (best) — whereas GRUET passes, and the paper notes this extends Oh et al.'s near-random finding to diversity-based methods.
- **Aggregation ablation (Table 3, family-averaged):** All-turn is best or second-best almost everywhere (e.g., Qwen3.5 family on SWE-bench Verified: all-turn AUROC 70.10 vs first-turn 55.36; Gemma4 family: 75.60 vs 62.98), and Top-25% is the other consistent leader (Qwen3.5 on InterCode Spider: AUROC 81.73, AUPRC 89.91, AUARC 85.06).
- **Long-horizon setting:** SWE-bench trajectories average 43.76–49.85 ReAct turns (Qwen3.5) — a far harder UQ task than the ~7-turn QA agents in prior work; average rewards range from 0.50–0.56 (SWE-bench Verified) down to 0.13–0.15 (InterCode NL2Bash), where class imbalance depresses everyone's AUPRC/AUARC.
- **Sensitivity (Figs. 4, 7, 8):** Metrics rise rapidly as K grows 3→10 and converge by K=15 (recommend K=10); performance drops markedly at sampling temperature T=0.1 (multi-sampling UQ is weak at low temperature, consistent with Lin et al. and LM-Polygraph practice; recommend T=0.6 for Qwen3.5, T=1.0 for Gemma4); τ matters on SWE-bench series and InterCode MBPP for Qwen3.5 (recommend 0.7) but is relatively insensitive for Gemma4 (recommend 0.94); d=25 is best for the d%-based strategies.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty is real but concentrated in one idea.** The genuinely new piece is modeling per-turn reasoning space as a merged DAG and reading graph complexity as uncertainty; the trajectory-level half is admittedly "simple aggregation" (first/last/mean/top-k) with no learned component. The FNN-view of a DAG for uncertainty propagation is elegant but incremental over known graph-uncertainty intuitions.
- **The evaluation grid is sparser than "nine LLMs and five benchmarks" suggests.** The appendix admits Qwen3.5 9B/4B/2B and Gemma4 8B-E4B/5B-E2B solved zero SWE-bench instances (omitted), while Qwen3.5 27B/35B-A3B and Gemma4 26B-A4B/31B exceed 0.9 accuracy on the InterCode series (omitted for class imbalance). So SWE-bench results rest on only the 4 largest models and InterCode on the smaller ones — no single benchmark is evaluated across the full size range, which weakens the "generality across scales" narrative.
- **Baselines are a strawman class.** All 10 contenders are naive per-turn-then-average transplants. The paper itself cites supervised weighted-aggregation agent-UQ methods (Zhang et al.'s agentic confidence calibration, Zhao et al.'s uncertainty propagation) and Uprop, but compares against none of them empirically, dismissing them as "costly" without a cost measurement of its own.
- **An internal claim is contradicted by its own table.** Section 4 states the first-turn strategy "usually exhibits performance comparable to that of the all-turn strategy," but Table 3 shows a 14.7-point AUROC gap on Qwen3.5-family SWE-bench Verified (55.36 vs 70.10) and 12.6 on Gemma4 (62.98 vs 75.60); the 1/L sampling-cost argument stands, but the "comparable" framing oversells the cheap variant.
- **Cost is never quantified.** The default all-turn GRUET needs K−1=14 extra full-chain samples per turn — on ~44-turn SWE-bench trajectories that is ~600 additional long generations per task, plus Deberta-large NLI calls per merge; no wall-clock, token, or dollar figures are reported, making practical adoption cost unclear.
- **Reproducibility wrinkles.** The token-level uncertainty formula is printed as U(c_j) = Σ_{c∈V} π(c|c_<j)/|V|, which reads as the constant 1/|V| if taken literally — the intended estimator (presumably a top-token-restricted variant of Fu et al.'s confidence) is not spelled out. The action-merge threshold τ is family- and task-dependent (0.7 vs 0.94) and sensitive on exactly the hardest benchmarks (SWE-bench, InterCode MBPP), with no calibration recipe. Step extraction also depends on a hand-inserted "Step i:" prompt convention, an unexamined parsing brittleness.
- **Scope: code agents only, binary rewards only.** Despite the general title, there are no web/GUI or tool-use QA agents; all five benchmarks are agentic coding with binary success, where "uncertainty = failure predictor" is easiest. Degradation at T=0.1 is acknowledged but production agents often run cooler, which limits out-of-the-box applicability.

## Strengths

- First systematic UQ study on genuinely long-horizon ReAct trajectories (40+ turns, real GitHub-issue software engineering), a much harder and more realistic setting than prior ~7-turn QA agent UQ.
- Statistically disciplined evaluation: 1000 bootstraps with std, an explicit 3σ significance criterion, and consistent gains across two model families, five benchmarks, and three metrics.
- Honest and useful negative result: ten standard LLM UQ methods are statistically indistinguishable from random on trajectories — a valuable warning for anyone transplanting single-turn UQ to agents.
- Training-free and plug-and-play: no supervised trajectory labels, unlike weighted-aggregation competitors; the first-turn variant gives a principled 1/L-cost lightweight option.
- Thorough sensitivity analyses with concrete recommended configurations (K=10; τ=0.7/0.94; T=0.6/1.0; d=25), and transparency about omitted model-benchmark cells and about accuracy differences caused by using SWE-agent/InterCode frameworks rather than official harnesses.

## Limitations

- Heavy sampling cost: K−1 extra full reasoning chains per turn (all-turn), plus NLI and embedding model calls; no cost/latency accounting, and the cheap first-turn variant underperforms by up to ~15 AUROC on SWE-bench.
- Performance collapses at low sampling temperature (T=0.1), restricting use in low-temperature deployments.
- The merge threshold τ is task- and family-sensitive (0.7 for Qwen3.5 vs 0.94 for Gemma4), sensitive precisely on the hardest benchmarks, and lacks an automatic calibration procedure.
- Evaluation restricted to agentic coding with binary rewards; no web/GUI agents, partial-credit tasks, or native reasoning models; the sparse evaluation grid means scale-generality claims rest on partial coverage.
- No empirical comparison against supervised or propagation-based agent-UQ methods (Uprop, agentic confidence calibration), the closest competitors conceptually.
- Step parsing depends on a modified system prompt ("Step i:" markers), whose robustness on models that ignore the format is untested.

## Relevance

这篇工作直击 LLM Agent 可靠性的核心痛点——同一任务跑出分叉轨迹、且常规 UQ 方法在轨迹层面完全失效，给出的"图复杂度 = 推理空间不确定性"方案可直接用于 agent 失败预警与选择性生成（低置信时人工介入或重规划）。对于关注 agent 推理过程监测、长程任务可信度评估的方向，这是目前少见的在 SWE-bench 级别长轨迹上验证过的实用信号源。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does graph-complexity uncertainty transfer to non-code agents (web/GUI, tool-use QA) and to native reasoning models whose thinking traces are not emitted as parseable "Step i" blocks?
2. Can the K−1 per-turn resampling cost be reduced — e.g., shared-prefix sampling, activation-based branch detection, or surrogate graph estimates — without giving up the ~15-point AUROC that first-turn loses?
3. Why does the action-merge threshold τ need to differ across families (0.7 vs 0.94), and can it be auto-calibrated per task from unlabeled rollouts?
4. Is there a sampling-free or low-temperature-compatible variant, given the sharp degradation at T=0.1?
5. Does the uncertainty score improve downstream utility (triggering replanning, human handoff, or best-of-N trajectory selection), beyond its discriminative AUROC/AUARC value?
