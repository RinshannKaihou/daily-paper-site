> **一句话总结：** 浙大+阿里提出 EvoPathBench——首个在"工件级自进化"（memory/skill/workflow 更新）过程中按检查点追踪单项能力的基准（1,800 条有序任务流、6 个交易任务族、2 个市场层），用冻结工件+留出探针把 10 种方法的评测拆成学习泛化、能力保持、规则修订三个维度；结果发现 SkillBoost 近分布增益最强（CEG +1.597）但迁移后缩水到 +0.537，SkillOpt/SkillGrad 迁移增益直接转负（−0.273/−0.383），遗忘集中在少数路径（SkillOpt 最差 10% 路径 CVaR 损失 12.666 vs 均值 1.454），且没有任何方法的规则修订效应在 Holm 校正后仍统计显著——瓶颈被定位在候选更新的事后评估/选择而非生成。

> **TL;DR:** EvoPathBench evaluates 10 self-evolving agent configurations (5 memory, 5 skill-evolution, plus non-evolving Baseline/Context) on 1,800 ordered trading streams built from Binance Spot data, freezing artifacts at 6 checkpoints and probing held-out episodes. The headline negative result: gains rarely survive distribution shift or subsequent updates — SkillBoost leads near-distribution learning generalization at CEG +1.597 but drops to +0.537 under transfer, SkillOpt flips from +0.763 to −0.273, and no rule-revision effect survives Holm correction. Token cost is decoupled from quality (SkillBoost burns 2.39× SkillOpt's 120.65M vs 70.18M tokens for a 45.5% larger gain; Context spends the most, 141.61M, and lands below baseline at CEG −0.0878). The actionable diagnosis: candidate *selection*, not generation, is the bottleneck — expanding 1→4 candidates raises best-candidate gain (+0.496) but widens the selection gap (+0.163), and safeguards cut harmful commits (12.4%→6.2%) only by missing improvements (0%→16.5%).

## 核心贡献

- **能力级过程评测的形式化**：把自进化形式化为"基座模型/工具/执行器固定、只有持久工件（memory、skill、workflow）在更新"的过程，在 6 个检查点 K0–K5 冻结工件，用留出 episode 做只读探针，并给出三对配对指标——能力进化增益 CEG（相对不进化 Baseline）、状态使用效应 SUE（state-off 干预，去掉工件后性能掉多少）、保持损失 ΔRL/ΔCVaR（τ=0.2/0.1 的尾部风险）、修订增益 REV1/REV2（相对 Baseline 的变化量），把"能力何时出现、后续更新是保持还是侵蚀"变成可测量的问题。
- **基准设计**：1,800 条纵向流 = 3 种模板（Accumulation→泛化 / Interference→保持 / Reversal→规则修订）× 6 个受控任务族（Trend、Mean reversion、Liquidity、Event jump、Opponent reflexivity、Risk contract）× 每格 100 条，叠加外生（价格路径与智能体无关）与内生（5 个固定对手、订单流推动价格）两个市场层；数据来自币安现货公开行情 + 按真实市场性质校准的程序化生成轨迹。
- **10 配置实证研究**：在 Qwen3.8-Max 与 Kimi-K3 两个 API 后背上评测 Artifact-Free（Baseline、Context）、Episodic Memory（Reflection、Single-Evidence、Consolidated）、Skill Evolution（SkillOpt、SkillBoost、SkillX、Trace2Skill、SkillGrad），总耗 1.59M 次调用、1.838B tokens（列表价约 CNY 36,280）。
- **四个负面但可操作的结论**：(1) 相近分布的增益在分布偏移下普遍缩水甚至反转；(2) 遗忘是尾部主导的——CVaR0.1 远大于均值损失，少数路径承担大部分侵蚀；(3) 无任何方法实现可靠的规则修订（Holm 校正后无一显著）；(4) 候选评估/选择而非生成是核心瓶颈，更多 token、更长工件不带来更好进化。
- **补充分析**：50 种子重复实验（固定 SkillBoost 检查点在迁移集上 96% 的执行为负增益）、任务顺序敏感性（SkillOpt CEG5 从 +5.615 翻到 −3.852）、跨模型技能迁移（技能文件给冻结的 Qwen3.5-27B 带来 +1.96，接近 SFT 的 +2.19，但在 9B 接收器上几乎消失）、以及安全性讨论（safeguard 把有害提交从 12.4% 降到 6.2% 的代价是漏掉 16.5% 的改进）。

## Key Contributions

- **Capability-level process evaluation formalized.** Self-evolution is modeled as persistent-artifact updates with the base model, tools, and executor held fixed; at each of six checkpoints K0–K5 the artifact is frozen and probed on held-out episodes read-only. Paired metrics — Capability Evolution Gain (CEG, vs. a non-evolving Baseline), State-Use Effect (SUE, via state-off intervention), retention loss ΔRL and ΔCVaR (τ=0.2/0.1), and revision gains REV1/REV2 — separate "the agent got richer" from "the agent got more capable," and separate artifact effects from environment luck.
- **EvoPathBench.** 1,800 longitudinal streams (3 templates × 6 task families × 100 each) over two market layers (exogenous fixed price paths; endogenous call auction against 5 fixed opponents where order flow moves prices), combining public Binance Spot data with calibrated synthetic trajectories. Each stream has 5 update opportunities; Accumulation tests learning generalization, Interference tests retention after 3 distractor updates, Reversal tests rule revision under systematic counterevidence. Per Table 1, it is the only surveyed longitudinal benchmark covering learning generalization, capability retention, AND rule adaptation explicitly (SkillLearnBench/Evo-Memory/PAST-Bench/Evo-Arena/SEA-Gym/FinEvo-Bench each miss at least one).
- **Empirical findings across 10 configurations** on Qwen3.8-Max and Kimi-K3 (1.59M calls, 1.838B tokens, ≈CNY 36,280 list price): (1) near-distribution gains shrink or invert under shift; (2) retention failures are tail-dominated; (3) no method shows statistically reliable rule adaptation after Holm correction; (4) candidate evaluation/selection, not generation, is the bottleneck.
- **Mechanistic analyses**: 50-seed repetition of a fixed checkpoint, task-order ablation, cross-model skill transfer to frozen Qwen3.5 receivers, token-efficiency decomposition, and a safety analysis of acceptance safeguards — all with released code (github.com/HQ-Lin/EvoPathBench) and a seeded, reproducible data generator.

## Methodology

**Formalization.** A self-evolving method α evolves an artifact state m along a stream S_c = ((f_1,e_1),...,(f_T,e_T)): m^α_{c,0} = m^α_0, m^α_{c,t} = U_α(m^α_{c,t−1}, s^α_{c,t}, ω^α_{c,t}), where U_α is the artifact update rule and future tasks/held-out outcomes are hidden from it. Evaluation targets the process E_α = (m^α_{c,0:5}, π_θ, U_α), not a final score.

**Benchmark construction.** Six diagnostic task families perturb a calibrated market process: Trend (persistent drift), Mean reversion (temporary deviation from latent value), Liquidity (wider spreads, fees, depth), Event jump (heavy-tailed shocks), Opponent reflexivity (order flow influences subsequent prices via 5 fixed opponents), Risk contract (position/turnover/drawdown limits with penalties). Two layers: exogenous (prices independent of the agent) and endogenous (call-auction price impact). Templates: Accumulation (5 learning episodes, one focal family), Interference (2 focal + 3 distractor episodes), Reversal (3 episodes under an initial relation + 2 counterevidence episodes under the reversed relation).

**Probing protocol.** Six artifact states K0–K5 per stream; at each k ≥ 1 the artifact is frozen and evaluated on held-out episodes from H_F (near-distribution and transfer variants; transfer alters observation mapping or opponent configuration). Each episode is 24 market steps with model queries at steps 0/8/16 and up to 4 structured orders per response; the score is risk-adjusted wealth: Y = W_T/W_0 − 1 − 0.01·N_vio − 2·max(0, D − D̄) (return minus violation penalty minus excess-drawdown penalty, all realized after fees/spread/impact).

**Attribution metrics.** CEG (Eq. 3) compares the evolving method against the non-evolving Baseline on the same stream/checkpoint/episode/repeat; SUE (Eq. 4) compares artifact-on vs. state-off within the method, isolating whether gains depend on the learned artifact. Retention loss L = max(0, Y_{K2} − Y_{K5}) with ΔRL subtracting the baseline's expected loss; CVaR_τ averages the worst-τ fraction of losses. Revision gains REV1/REV2 subtract the baseline's own score change on the same post-change episodes. Execution repeats isolate inference stochasticness from artifact state.

**Compared methods.** Artifact-Free Execution (Baseline; Context = recent history in prompt, no summarization); Episodic Memory (Reflection = per-episode lessons; Single-Evidence Memory = rules created from one episode; Consolidated Memory = rules requiring two corroborating episodes); Skill Evolution (SkillOpt = bounded text edits with a paired-validation accept gate; SkillBoost = diagnosis-driven candidate search with mean-improvement + regression-limit gates; SkillX = three-level skill library, structural acceptance only; Trace2Skill = multi-analyst patches, structural acceptance only; SkillGrad = skill package with persistent failure-pattern record, structural acceptance only). All methods share identical settings, seeds, and constraints; backbones are API-served Qwen3.8-Max and Kimi-K3, with the same model doing both trading and artifact updates. All evolving methods may run paired internal validation rollouts before committing.

## Results & Highlights

- **Generalization gap (overfitting of skills).** On EvoPathBench Accumulation streams, SkillOpt improves near-distribution held-out episodes at CEG_N +0.763 and SkillGrad +0.555, but both flip negative under transfer: −0.273 and −0.383, i.e., worse than the non-evolving baseline once the observation mapping or opponents change. SkillBoost is the only method strong on both (CEG_N +1.597, CEG_T +0.537) with a positive state-use effect of +1.238, confirming the gain is artifact-carried.
- **Retention is tail-dominated.** On Interference streams, SkillOpt posts the largest raw mean retention loss (E[L] = 1.454 vs. 0.298 for the non-evolving Baseline) and the largest worst-10% CVaR (12.666 vs. 7.461), while Context achieves the smallest losses (mean 0.241, CVaR0.1 1.956) yet zero transfer gain — limited forgetting does not imply learning. Across methods, CVaR0.1 far exceeds the mean loss, and even SkillBoost, with the highest backward transfer (BWT +0.863), still declines on 21.3% of trajectories: erosion concentrates in a minority of evolution paths.
- **Rule adaptation is not reliably achieved.** On Reversal streams, no method's revision effect remains statistically significant after Holm correction. Point estimates diverge: Single-Evidence Memory posts the highest REV2 (+0.716) despite negative generalization (−0.460), SkillBoost leads REV1 (+0.666) but fades to +0.384 after the second update, and SkillOpt swings from −0.840 to +0.444. Checkpoint-level analysis shows adaptation is non-monotonic — SkillBoost's post-change CEG collapses from 0.383 (K4) to 0.001 (K5), losing 0.382 after its second update, while SkillOpt deteriorates first (−0.259→−0.559) and only recovers after repeated evidence.
- **More tokens ≠ better evolution.** SkillBoost consumes 120.65M tokens (2.39× SkillOpt's 70.18M) for a 45.5% larger overall CEG (0.802 vs. 0.351); SkillOpt is the most token-efficient (TE = 0.01519, ~60× SkillBoost's 0.00025); Context spends the most (141.61M) yet lands below baseline (CEG −0.0878). Skill length grows monotonically for all methods (e.g., SkillOpt 0.772K→ chars) while CEG trajectories diverge — text accumulation is not capability acquisition.
- **Selection is the bottleneck, and safeguards trade recall for precision.** Expanding the candidate pool from 1 to 4 raises the best-candidate gain (+0.496) but widens the gap between the best candidate and the committed update (+0.163). Safeguarded acceptance cuts harmful commits from 12.4% to 6.2% and lifts precision from 9.3% to 29.5%, but raises missed improvements from 0.0% to 16.5% and drops capture from 69.0% to 47.2%. A case study shows the acceptance rule rejecting both a candidate with the best overall gain (+1.738, but −8.924 on the new task) and one with the largest new-task gain (+12.141, but −2.936 earlier), committing "None."
- **Instability and path dependence.** Repeating a fixed SkillBoost K5 checkpoint over 50 seeds: near-distribution CEG averages +0.817 but is negative in 14% of executions (5th percentile −1.238); on the transfer episode CEG averages −1.860 and is negative in 96% of 50 executions. Reordering the same five learning episodes reverses rankings: SkillOpt CEG5 +5.615 (original) vs. −3.852 (reversed); SkillBoost −0.205 vs. +5.887.
- **Skills transfer across models, SFT-like but receiver-dependent.** Frozen Qwen3.5-27B receivers gain +1.96 from SkillBoost skills vs. +2.19 from SFT on source-model inference traces; on Qwen3.5-9B the same skills add almost nothing (+0.03 vs. SFT +0.11).

## Authors & Credibility

The team is from Zhejiang University (Hongqiang Lin, Yuhong Li, Nenggan Zheng — corresponding) and Alibaba Group (Chao Liu, Xiaofan Bai, Xuan Jin, Xipeng Cao — corresponding), a strong industry-academia pairing with an agentic-AI focus. Importantly, this group built much of what it evaluates: SkillBoost is their own prior method ("Rethinking self-evolution: a constrained exploration-exploitation process for mitigating skill overfitting," same author list) and SkillZip is also theirs — so they have deep hands-on expertise in skill evolution, but also a stake in SkillBoost's strong showing (see Critical Assessment). The benchmark engineering is serious: 1.59M API calls, an explicit AI-use statement, seeded procedural data generation, released code, and honest list-price cost reporting (CNY 36,280). The statistical hygiene (Holm correction that invalidates their own rule-adaptation rankings, 50-seed repetition, task-order ablation) signals above-average rigor for a benchmark paper.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty is real but narrower than the framing suggests.** The capability-level checkpoint protocol (frozen artifact + held-out probe + paired CEG/SUE attribution) is a genuine gap: Table 1 shows existing longitudinal benchmarks (Evo-Arena, PAST-Bench, SEA-Gym, FinEvo-Bench) aggregate across tasks and never probe the same capability before/after specific updates. However, everything is instantiated in ONE synthetic trading environment; the authors themselves concede that "extending this protocol beyond trading is therefore a critical step for validating the cross-domain robustness of our empirical conclusions." Headline claims like "no method achieves reliable rule adaptation" are, strictly, claims about six diagnostic market families — the families are admitted interventions, "not a natural, complete, or mutually exclusive taxonomy of financial markets."
- **The top method is the authors' own — with a confounded comparison.** SkillBoost's win is partly by construction: SkillOpt and SkillBoost use performance-validation acceptance gates, while SkillX, Trace2Skill, and SkillGrad accept on structural criteria only ("performance-based acceptance is a method-specific component rather than a shared requirement," Appendix B.2). So a large share of the Table 2 spread measures *whether a method has a validation gate*, not method quality per se; the appendix's own case study (Table 9) shows the gate mechanism, not skill content, drives commit decisions. The paper is transparent about this but does not run the obvious control (adding a validation gate to Trace2Skill/SkillGrad).
- **The paper partially undermines its own main table — admirably, but consequentially.** The 50-seed study (Table 10) shows a fixed, headline-winning SkillBoost checkpoint has negative CEG in 96% of transfer executions, and the task-order ablation (Table 11) shows CEG5 swings of 9.5 points for SkillOpt across orderings with the method ranking reversing. Table 2 reports single-order point estimates without confidence intervals (only the rule-revision column gets Holm correction). Given the demonstrated order and seed sensitivity, the exact Table 2 numbers — e.g., SkillBoost +1.597 vs. SkillOpt +0.763 — should be read as one draw from a high-variance process, and the paper would be stronger with matched-multi-order main results.
- **Baseline accounting is careful but the "capability" construct is indirect.** Capabilities are inferred from paired score deltas on a wealth-return-based score, never from the artifact content itself; and retention metrics must subtract the baseline's own erosion (Context, a non-evolving method, still shows CVaR0.1 losses of 1.956), indicating substantial environment noise in the retention signal.
- **Reproducibility is good on paper, costly in practice.** Code, seeded generator, and full artifact transcripts (Appendix D) are released, which is excellent; but both backbones are proprietary API models, and the full replication cost (~1.84B tokens, ≈CNY 36k list price) is beyond most academic labs. Results may also be backbone-specific — cross-model transfer gains nearly vanish on the 9B receiver.
- **No red flags of data leakage**: held-out episodes are excluded from update visibility, state-off interventions and execution repeats are used, and the AI-use statement is explicit. The negative-results orientation (their own Holm-corrected null, their own method's 96% negative transfer executions) reads as intellectually honest rather than cherry-picked.

## Strengths

- First benchmark to make "when does a capability appear, persist, and erode" measurable in artifact-level self-evolution, with a clean paired-attribution design (CEG vs. SUE vs. baseline-difference) that most agent benchmarks lack entirely.
- Unusually honest statistics for a benchmark paper: Holm correction that nullifies its own rankings, tail-risk (CVaR) reporting, 50-seed repetition, and a task-order ablation that reveals ranking reversals — all reported in the paper rather than discovered by critics.
- Actionable, mechanism-level diagnosis: the selection-vs-generation bottleneck is demonstrated by direct intervention (1→4 candidates raises best gain +0.496 but widens selection gap +0.163), and the safeguard trade-off is quantified (harmful 12.4%→6.2% at the cost of missed 0%→16.5%).
- Scale and transparency: 1,800 streams, two market layers, two backbones, 1.838B tokens of audited usage, released code and complete per-checkpoint artifact transcripts, plus a safety appendix discussing update persistence risks without overclaiming open-world safety conclusions.

## Limitations

- Single domain: all conclusions come from synthetic trading streams; the six task families are diagnostic interventions, and transfer of the protocol (and of the negative findings) to code, web, or other agent domains is untested.
- Short horizons: 5 update opportunities and 24-step episodes per stream are far from extended-deployment self-evolution; whether tail-dominated erosion compounds over longer streams is unknown.
- Confounded method comparison: skill-evolution baselines differ in whether they possess validation gates (SkillOpt/SkillBoost yes; SkillX/Trace2Skill/SkillGrad no), and memory methods use no candidate rollouts at all, so Table 2 partially ranks acceptance policies rather than evolution methods; no error bars on the main table.
- Receiver-scale dependence: distilled skills help a 27B receiver (+1.96) but not a 9B one (+0.03), limiting the practical reach of the cross-model transfer finding.
- Reproduction cost (~1.84B tokens, ≈CNY 36k list price) and dependence on proprietary APIs (Qwen3.8-Max, Kimi-K3) constrain independent verification.

## Relevance

这篇工作与我们关注的 LLM Agent 自进化（memory/skill 持久化工件）与评测方法论高度相关：它给出的 CEG/SUE/CVaR/REV 指标体系和"冻结工件+留出探针"的协议可以直接复用来检验任何自进化智能体是否真的在变强，而其"增益不抗分布偏移、遗忘尾部化、规则修订不显著、选择是瓶颈"四个负面结论，对任何正在构建技能库或记忆机制的 Agent 项目都是必须正视的反面证据。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Do the four negative findings (transfer shrinkage, tail-dominated forgetting, non-significant rule revision, selection bottleneck) replicate in non-trading agent domains such as SWE-bench-style task streams or web navigation?
2. Can acceptance rules be designed to distinguish "evidence against an outdated rule" from "evidence against a still-valid capability" — the specific failure mode behind Table 9's rejected +12.141 new-task candidate?
3. Would adding a matched validation gate to gate-less baselines (Trace2Skill, SkillGrad) collapse the gap to SkillBoost, thereby confirming that acceptance policy rather than generation quality drives the ranking?
4. How do artifact updates and parameter updates interact — does periodically distilling validated artifacts into weights (per Appendix F) avoid the tail losses observed here?
5. Does the 96%-negative transfer execution of the best checkpoint (Table 10) persist with more repeats per checkpoint, and can multi-order matched evaluation turn Table 2's point estimates into stable rankings?
