Why this matters

Commit every candidate before the truth is revealed: one realized outcome then supervises all unselected alternatives — transferable to any multi-expert agent orchestration.

先提交全部候选，待真实值揭晓后统一回灌——一个已实现结果即可监督所有未被选中的路径，可迁移到任何「多专家 + 多策略」的智能体编排系统。

⚠ Critical edge

Methodologypredict → reveal → update

  1. Commit before reveal

    At origin t the system logs every frozen expert forecast, every candidate agent path, and one intervention decision — before the target exists. Once the horizon elapses (s+H ≤ t), the realized target scores the entire precommitted set, so unselected actions get supervision with no leakage and no off-policy correction.

  2. Frozen cast

    Five time-series foundation models (Chronos-2, Toto-2.0, Falcon-X, TimesFM-2.5, Chronos-Bolt) plus statistical experts form the FORECAST-PACK. A frozen LLM reads the evidence stack and proposes candidate paths: an expert subset Sₜ + optional correction Δₜ.

  3. Three coupled learners — the only parts that evolve

    EvolveTrust mixes four reliability views (warm-up, inverse-error online, current evidence, pattern) into expert trust. EvolveReason predicts each path's log-MSE with a shared rolling ridge over 5 pre-reveal features; argmin wins. EvolveIntervene fits a gate α ∈ [0,1] toward the closed-form blend target α̂ₜ. Updates are validated and committed atomically after each reveal.

Strengths

  • Temporally rigorous: commit-everything-before-reveal yields all-alternative supervision with no off-policy correction; eligibility enforced per expert via availability intervals.
  • Frozen backbone makes ablations honest — the 6.3% / 7.6% gains isolate orchestration, not forecasters.
  • Feedback-coverage ablation tests the central hypothesis directly — mechanistic care most agentic papers lack.
  • Broad, current baselines across four families; consistent dominance over agentic systems (16/16 vs CastFlow).
  • Transparent protocol (App. B) and honest reporting of the one domain an agentic baseline wins.

Limitations

  • Ceiling = frozen expert pool: the policy can re-weight, not fix, a bad prior (authors' own stated limitation).
  • One benchmark family (Time-MMD), short horizons, mostly monthly cadence; no ETT / Electricity / Traffic long-horizon evidence, no deployment study.
  • Missing cited baselines (OneNet, MoE-F, exponential-weights) — necessity of the three-module design unproven.
  • Single runs; no seeds / std / significance; average-rank headline can mask small absolute gaps.
  • Uncosted bookkeeping: features for every candidate at every origin, three rolling ridge fits, episodic memory growth.
  • No code release; the Climate case study was selected after outcome evaluation.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Would one classical online-gating baseline (OneNet-style or exponential weights) over the same expert pool recover most of the −6.3% / −7.6% gain?
  2. Does the all-alternative advantage persist on long-horizon benchmarks (ETT, Electricity, Traffic) with hundreds of candidate paths?
  3. Are the three coupled updates stable over months-long deployments — is there a forgetting / regret analysis?
  4. What is the compute and memory cost of scoring every candidate at every stride-one origin as the expert pool grows?
  5. Can the protocol extend beyond scalar gating to structured corrections Δₜ, where identifiability (Dₜ > 0) may fail more often?
