Critical Assessment · labels are single realizations, not expectations

01 Methodology, distilled

  1. Define taste as hindsight-labeled forks. Attempts that share a trajectory prefix then diverge form a decision fork; the branch whose recorded outcome proved better labels the question — zero human annotation.
  2. Mine two constructions. Parallel trajectories catch errors agents never notice; detour trajectories catch self-corrected mistakes. 2,677 SWE-bench Pro rollouts + 1,132 AI-R&D runs feed a generator/judge filter pipeline.
  3. Audit quality. On 100 sampled questions humans agree with mined labels 98.8% (reviewer agreement κ = 0.973); each fork gets a 4-level time-horizon tag.
  4. Evaluate strictly. Every question is asked in both candidate orders and counts only if both are right (random = 25%); 14 models under one interface, plus a reasoning-effort ablation (6,024 responses).
  5. Distill, then deploy. A privileged teacher — the same frozen Qwen3.6-27B shown the supported candidate — transfers taste via SDPO-style token-level KL on task-disjoint folds; the student’s verdicts become advice for a fixed executor on 41 held-out tasks.

03 Strengths

  • Genuinely new measurement target: per-decision judgment, distinct from end-to-end success — shown, not assumed (r = +0.63; +0.37 on the closest subset).
  • Fully automatic label construction, no human in the loop, validated by unusually strong agreement (98.8%, κ = 0.973) plus a filter that provably removes wording-solvable questions.
  • Careful protocol: both-orders strict scoring kills position bias, a defined 25% floor, bootstrap intervals, and a reasoning-budget control that rules out “just think longer”.
  • The measurement → improvement arc closes: task-disjoint folds, explicit leakage control, and an end-to-end gain on held-out tasks.
  • Benchmark, code, and a detailed reproducibility statement are all released.

04 Limitations

  • Single-realization hindsight labels cannot separate a good judgment from lucky execution — especially on noisy research tasks.
  • Forks inherit the failure distribution of GPT-5.4/5.5 agents; the research leg rests on only 47 tasks (112 questions).
  • The end-to-end gain lacks an untrained-advisor control in the main text — 14.6% → 33.7% may partly reflect any hindsight-derived hints.
  • One 27B base + LoRA; no scale or naive-label-fitting ablations reported.
  • No online fork detection — advice needs forks pre-mined from prior runs on the same tasks; all training and evaluation is within-domain.

05 Questions for further exploration

  1. Would multi-seed branch outcomes yield cleaner labels than single realizations — and how many of the 502 labels would flip?
  2. Can the student advisor detect forks online at execution time, without pre-mined forks?
  3. How much of the 14.6% → 33.7% gain would a strong untrained advisor already recover?
  4. Would model rankings change if forks were mined from a different agent’s trajectories (e.g., Claude)?
  5. Does taste distill across domains (engineering → research), and does +17.9 pts hold at other base-model scales?
