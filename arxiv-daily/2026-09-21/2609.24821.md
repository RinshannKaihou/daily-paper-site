> **一句话总结：** 提出"答案盆地表征假设"（ABRH）：组织语言模型隐藏态线性结构的不是概念标签，而是模型对答案的推概率测度（同一答案的所有续写构成一个"盆地"，其质量为总概率）——在 Qwen2.5-7B 的 ARC-Challenge 上，仅用错误答案之间的盆地质量排序（无任何概念标签）训练的线性探针达到 78.63% Top-1，几乎追平概念监督探针的 79.36%（mean-log 基线仅 50.04%）；且固定标签的转向向量在"标签-盆地对齐/冲突"两种条件下产生相反的概念级行为（Gemma-3-4B-PT，BBQ/SafetyBench）。

> **TL;DR:** This paper argues that linear structure in LLM hidden states reflects statistics of the model's *answer measure* (the pushforward probability over answers, where all continuations yielding the same answer form an "answer basin") rather than concept labels themselves. The evidence is two dissociations. Probing: on ARC-Challenge (Qwen2.5-7B base), probes trained with NO concept labels — only on basin-mass orderings between pairs of incorrect answers — reach 78.63% Top-1, nearly matching concept-supervised high-gap probes (79.36%) and far above the mean-log baseline (50.04%). Steering: on GSM8K chain-of-thought (Qwen2.5-8B-Instruct), identically supervised correctness probes swing from 78.72% to 70.74% Top-1 when the label-basin alignment of the training data is reversed, and a fixed label-defined steering vector on Gemma-3-4B-PT (BBQ/SafetyBench, α up to 0.10) raises the *correct* answer's log-probability under alignment but the *incorrect* one under conflict. Practical upshot: whether a probe or steering vector "means" a concept depends on how concept labels align with endogenous answer probabilities in its source data.

## 核心贡献

- **形式化"答案测度"与"答案盆地"**：把模型输出分布通过答案映射推前（pushforward）到可数答案空间上；产生同一答案的所有续写构成该答案的盆地，盆地质量 = 总概率。答案测度的两个统计量——碰撞集中度 C₂（Simpson 指数）与答案对数质量对比 Δlog m——分别由共享线性方向 w_Q、w_A 在问题边界与答案边界处线性读出（假设的形式化预测为论文 Eq. 5/6/8）。
- **提出 ABRH 并给出探测证据**：概念相关的线性结构源于答案测度的差异，而非概念标签的变化。仅用"同一问题两个错误答案的盆地质量排序"做监督（wrong-only，零概念标签）的探针，在未见问题上仍能恢复候选答案质量排序，且 Top-1 正确率接近概念监督探针。
- **对齐/冲突解耦实验**：构造标签完全相同、仅"正确答案是否携带更盆地质量"相反的训练集——探针概念预测随之反转（GSM8K CoT 上 Top-1 78.72% vs 70.74%，12 个模型×数据集组合方向全部一致）。
- **转向实验解释"何时有效/反向"**：固定标签定义的转向向量作用于目标问题时，初始盆地质量更大的答案获得更大相对增益（输运假设 Eq. 8）；源数据标签-盆地关系反转会使向量在共享 PCA 空间中近乎反向。源质量间隔越大，对比向量越长、方向越一致（signed projection 与 log-mass contrast 的 Pearson r=0.770/0.812）。
- **把自洽性/语义熵文献与 probing/steering 统一到同一对象**：集中度可于生成前读出、盆地质量可于生成后读出，二者是同一答案测度的两个侧面。

## Key Contributions

- Formalizes the **answer measure**: the pushforward of the continuation distribution onto a countable answer space, where all continuations producing the same answer form an *answer basin* whose mass is their total probability; basin statistics (Simpson collision concentration C₂, pairwise log-mass contrast) are claimed to be linearly readable along shared directions w_Q (question boundary) and w_A (answer boundary), with testable predictions (Eqs. 5, 6, 8).
- **Probing dissociation**: linear probes trained solely on basin-mass comparisons between two *incorrect* answers to the same question ("wrong-only" — no concept supervision) still recover candidate mass ordering on unseen questions, reaching 80.00% basin-ordering / 78.63% Top-1 on ARC-Challenge with Qwen2.5-7B — statistically indistinguishable from concept-supervised training (80.43% / 79.36%).
- **Align/Conflict construction**: training sets that keep correct-wrong labels fixed while reversing which answer carries the larger basin mass flip the probe's concept-level behavior in all twelve model×dataset combinations (ARC-C, BBQ, SafetyBench) and under free-form GSM8K CoT (78.72% vs 70.74% Top-1 with identical labels and supervision).
- **Steering account**: a fixed label-defined steering vector gives larger relative gains to answers with greater initial basin mass (transport hypothesis, Eq. 8), so a vector built from "correct-minus-wrong" source contrasts can *increase* wrong answers under conflict; reversing source label-basin alignment flips the vector's orientation in a shared PCA space, and larger source mass gaps yield longer, more directionally coherent vectors (Pearson r = 0.770 BBQ, 0.812 SafetyBench).
- Unifies self-consistency / semantic-entropy style answer-level aggregation with representation probing and steering under one mathematical object.

## Methodology

**Answer measure construction (two protocols).** (1) *Generation protocol*: draw K ≥ 2 independent continuations per question; basin mass m̂_q(a) = N_q(a)/K; collision concentration estimated by the unbiased agreement-rate estimator Ĉ₂ (fraction of agreeing sample pairs). For GSM8K concentration probes, K=64 generations per training question; for GSM8K Instruct CoT, K=128 continuations at temperature 0.7 (max 512 tokens) grouped by parsed boxed numeric answers. (2) *Finite-choice protocol*: present question + candidates, permute option-letter assignments, average softmax letter logits back onto answers, then aggregate/normalize into masses. All statistics are defined for either construction.

**Hypothesis and probes.** Hidden states are read at the final prompt token (question boundary, before generation) and the final answer token (answer boundary, after generation). ABRH states that hidden-state differences are consistently oriented with signed changes of answer-measure statistics: [C₂(q)−C₂(q′)]·w_Qᵀ[h_q − h_q′] > 0 across questions, and Δlog m(a,b)·w_Aᵀ[h_a − h_b] > 0 within questions. Probes are linear (ridge regression with validation-selected regularization for concentration; L2-regularized logistic regression, C=0.001, on the full layer-24 hidden state for GSM8K CoT), evaluated by pairwise Order Accuracy (chance = 1/2 for unequal-mass pairs), Spearman ρ, and Top-1 correctness (no fitted parameters; highest scalar score).

**Data and training budgets.** Models: Qwen2.5-7B/14B base and Gemma-3-4B/12B-PT. Concentration probing: 500 training questions per probe; GSM8K evaluated on 200 question-disjoint test questions, BBQ on 5,512 held-out and SafetyBench on 2,667 held-out questions (means over five training sets). Basin-mass probing (Table 2): 100 source questions per condition; Low/Medium/High conditions sort training pairs by log-mass gap; Wrong-only trains on pairs of incorrect answers only; evaluation on 1,165 four-choice ARC-Challenge test questions. The Align/Conflict sets use the same correct-wrong labels: in Align the correct basin is strictly larger (mean paired basin counts 108.22 vs 1.00 on GSM8K CoT), in Conflict strictly smaller (26.22 vs 101.78), with the aligned pool downsampled to match size.

**Steering.** Label-defined vector v = mean over source questions of correct-minus-wrong answer-conditioned representation contrasts (80–300 source questions per condition). v·α added to the residual stream after block 19 (BBQ) / block 16 (SafetyBench) of Gemma-3-4B-PT, at the final "Answer:" token and all subsequent positions, α ∈ {0, 0.01, …, 0.10}; behavioral response measured as change in length-normalized answer log-probability with 2,000-sample context-group bootstrap 95% intervals. The transport hypothesis predicts positive steering amplifies the initially higher-mass answer more (Eq. 8). Source-geometry analysis relates signed projections of held-out contrasts to basin log-ratios (five-fold out-of-fold projections, Qwen2.5-7B block-20 states).

## Results & Highlights

- **Wrong-only probes match concept-supervised probes (ARC-Challenge, Qwen2.5-7B base):** probes trained only on basin-mass ordering of *incorrect* answer pairs reach 78.63% ± 0.54 Top-1 and 80.00% ± 0.31 basin-ordering, versus 79.36% ± 0.93 / 80.43% ± 0.27 for concept-supervised High-gap probes and 50.04% Top-1 for the mean-log baseline — a +28.6 pt delta over baseline with zero concept labels. The pattern replicates across all four models (e.g., Gemma-3-12B-PT: 83.28% wrong-only vs 83.70% High vs 65.41% mean-log Top-1).
- **Label-basin alignment flips probe behavior under identical supervision (GSM8K CoT, Qwen2.5-8B-Instruct):** with the same correct-wrong labels and 188 question-disjoint test questions, Align training yields 78.72% Top-1 vs 70.74% for Conflict training; the Figure 12 daily-setting analog shows larger swings (Qwen2.5-14B: 88.0% Align vs 43.0% Conflict, baseline 79.0%), and Align beats the mean-log baseline in all twelve model×dataset combinations on ARC-C/BBQ/SafetyBench.
- **Answer concentration is readable before generation:** pairwise Order Accuracy exceeds the 50% chance everywhere, e.g., Gemma-3-12B-PT reaches 78.20% (Spearman ρ = 0.745) on GSM8K and Qwen2.5-14B 73.92% ± 1.86 on BBQ; the weakest cell (Gemma-3-12B-PT on SafetyBench) still hits 63.42% Order Acc though its ρ drops to 0.387.
- **Fixed steering vectors produce opposite concept effects (Gemma-3-4B-PT, BBQ and SafetyBench):** the same label-defined vector (blocks 19/16, α up to 0.10) raises the correct answer's length-normalized log-probability under Align but the incorrect answer's under Conflict, with reversal on both datasets despite unchanged labels; answer-letter logits show the same pattern (Appendix C, Fig. 8).
- **Source basin separation shapes steering geometry:** signed representation projections track the answer log-mass contrast with Pearson r = 0.770 (BBQ) and r = 0.812 (SafetyBench); contrast norms and directional coherence grow monotonically across Low→High source mass-gap terciles, so raw-averaged vectors from larger-gap sources weigh more (norm-normalization preserves direction but not magnitude effects).

## Authors & Credibility

Eight authors from three institutions: The University of Queensland, Mohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial Intelligence (MBZUAI), and Institute of Science Tokyo. The group combines measure-theoretic probability framing with standard interpretability tooling (probing, steering, PCA geometry); Lijie Hu has a prior track record in interpretability/activation-steering research. The paper is a preprint (21 Sep 2026, not yet peer-reviewed), but it releases code (github.com/VicentNewake/answer-basin-representation) and its appendices document training budgets, evaluation splits, tokenization checks, and bootstrap procedures in unusual detail, which supports reproducibility. The quantitative claims are modest in scale (four open-weight models, ≤ a few thousand test questions) and consistent with what the released code could verify.

## Critical Assessment

- **The headline overclaims relative to what is shown.** "We are not probing or steering concepts" is supported only in fixed-candidate-answer settings plus one free-form GSM8K experiment; the paper does not show that concept directions do not exist, only that readout/intervention behavior flips when label-to-basin-mass alignment flips. A more defensible claim — which the conclusion itself retreats to — is that label-basin alignment modulates these effects.
- **Partial circularity risk in the finite-choice probing results.** The candidate answer measure is constructed from the model's own averaged letter logits, and basin-supervised probes are supervised by comparisons of those very masses; high "basin ordering" accuracy is therefore close to the model agreeing with itself. The genuinely probative evidence is the wrong-only probe (no concept labels, 78.63% vs 79.36% Top-1) and the Align/Conflict reversal — but both are confined to ARC-C/BBQ/SafetyBench/GSM8K with 100-question training pools, and no comparison is made against external probing baselines from the truthfulness-probe literature (e.g., Azaria & Mitchell-style probes or CCS); the only baseline, "mean log," is the authors' own construction.
- **The steering account rests on a narrow base.** All causal steering evidence comes from one model (Gemma-3-4B-PT), two datasets (BBQ, SafetyBench), and a single layer per dataset (blocks 19/16), with α capped at 0.10; there is no layer sweep, no comparison to alternative steering constructions (e.g., contrastive activation addition variants), and Eq. 8's transport hypothesis is validated only through aggregate response curves rather than per-question predictions.
- **Metric sensitivity is visible but not analyzed.** Gemma-3-12B-PT on SafetyBench has Order Acc 63.42% yet Spearman ρ of only 0.387 — ordering is fine pairwise but poorly calibrated across the whole set — and Gemma-3-4B's Low/Medium training-gap conditions on ARC-C (42.75%/71.88% Top-1) fall below the 55.62% mean-log baseline, showing the "larger gaps help" story has unexplained exceptions.
- **Reproducibility is a strength**, but the free-form (open-ended) extension is explicitly unsolved: the authors acknowledge finite-sample error and answer-grouping uncertainty, meaning the central object (the answer measure) is currently best measured exactly where it is least interesting — experimenter-defined candidate sets.

## Strengths

- A crisp, falsifiable formalization: three numbered predictions (Eqs. 5, 6, 8), each mapped to a dedicated experiment, spanning two model families at two scales and three attribute domains (correctness, social bias, safety).
- The wrong-only probe is an elegant supervision-free control that directly tests "labels vs masses" without confounds, and its near-tie with concept-supervised probes (78.63% vs 79.36% Top-1) is a striking result.
- The Align/Conflict construction cleanly isolates the alignment variable while holding labels and supervision fixed, with the reversal confirmed in all twelve model×dataset combinations and under both candidate scoring and free-form CoT.
- Honest scope statement: limitations about open-ended answer grouping are stated plainly rather than buried.
- Code release plus unusually thorough appendices (budgets, splits, permutation protocols, bootstrap resampling counts) make the work easy to audit.

## Limitations

- Most experiments require fixed candidate-answer spaces; extending to open-ended generation needs reliable answer grouping and finite-sample-safe measure estimation (acknowledged by the authors).
- Steering evidence is limited to a single model, two datasets, one intervention layer each, and small strengths (α ≤ 0.10); generality across architectures and layers is untested.
- No external baselines from prior probing/steering literature; comparisons are internal (mean-log baseline, gap-tercile conditions).
- The hypothesis is descriptive-correlational at the concept level; the only causal evidence is the two-dataset steering dissociation, so "answer measure organizes representations" remains a strong reading of weaker evidence.
- Several weak cells (SafetyBench ρ = 0.387 for Gemma-3-12B; Low-gap conditions below baseline on ARC-C for Gemma-3-4B) suggest the theory fits best in the mid/large-gap regime the authors choose to highlight.

## Relevance

与 LLM 可解释性方向高度相关：这篇论文对"线性探针/转向向量到底读出和干预了什么"给出了一个反直觉但可检验的答案——是答案分布的统计量而非概念本身——并直接联动语义熵、自洽性与 steering 文献，对评估和设计 probing/steering 实验有方法论的提醒价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does ABRH survive truly open-ended generation with robust semantic answer grouping (e.g., entailment-based clustering from the semantic-entropy literature), where the candidate set is not experimenter-defined?
- How do w_Q (concentration) and w_A (basin mass) relate across layers — do they coincide, split, or rotate, and is there a layer where the answer measure is most linearly exposed?
- Can the transport hypothesis (Eq. 8) make *per-question* predictions about steering outcomes, rather than aggregate curve-level ones, and does it hold at larger α or different layers?
- Do instruction tuning and RLHF systematically change label-basin alignment (e.g., mass concentrated on formatted or preferred answers), and would that explain divergent steering effectiveness between base and aligned models?
- How does the basin view compose with superposition: can basin-oriented directions coexist with polysemantic concept directions in the same subspace, and can both be disentangled?
