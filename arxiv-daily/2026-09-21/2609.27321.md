> **一句话总结：** 阿里 Token Foundry 与 Georgia Tech 提出 VHD-Play，把智能体环境的构建顺序整个反过来——先从 11 个运筹学机制族（库存 DP、带 backlog 的路由、谈判、背包、LP/QP、排产等）采样参数 θ 并**精确求解**数学模型 M(θ)，用解出的 (最优值 u*(θ), 默认策略值 u₀(θ)) **先固定奖励规则**，再由冻结的 setter LLM 基于真实语料段落把决策过程渲染成带隐藏参数的有状态工具环境，使可执行动态与评分参考同源、无需 LLM 评审或奖励模型；流水线以每条约 $0.01–0.03 的成本产出 3,300 个通过准入审计的环境，GRPO 训练 Qwen3.6-35B-A3B 后五族诊断的平均 agentic 分从 0.204 提到 0.815（written-out 求解保持在 0.992，差距 0.758→0.177），在 8 个未见机制族（近 OOD +0.63、远 OOD +0.16~0.24）、BFCL V4 交互单元（61.25→64.08）、TravelBench（0.700→0.794）和 365 天电商模拟（破产 1/5→0/5、期末资金 54,294→182,844、超过 Qwen3.7-Max 的 165,224）上都观察到迁移。

> **TL;DR:** VHD-Play inverts the standard "environment-first" recipe for building agent RL environments: it first samples and **exactly solves** a parameterized mathematical model (inventory DP, capacitated routing with backlog, bilateral negotiation, knapsack, LP/QP, scheduling), fixes the outcome rule from the solver-derived pair (optimum u*, default-policy value u₀), and only then has a frozen LLM "setter" render the decision process as stateful tools grounded in a real-world corpus passage — so dynamics, hidden parameters, and the reward are all inherited from one solved mechanism instead of being aligned post hoc with judges or reward models. The pipeline admits 3,300 environments (≥10 tools each, ~60 assistant turns/episode) at ≈$0.01–0.03 per admitted record, verified by replay audits (480 oracle/default/idle executions, 240/240 repeated outcomes matching). GRPO training of Qwen3.6-35B-A3B raises the five-family mean agentic score from 0.204 to 0.815 (+0.611, vs Qwen3.7-Max's 0.429) while written-out solving stays at 0.992; gains transfer to held-out instances (+0.56), eight unseen mechanism families (near-OOD +0.63, far-OOD +0.16/+0.24), and external benchmarks: BFCL V4 interaction cells 61.25→64.08 (+2.84, beating a matched-budget EnvScaler control at 58.56), TravelBench 0.700→0.794 (still below Qwen3.7-Max's 0.891), and 365-day storefront runs going from 1/5 to 5/5 bankruptcy-free with ending balance $54,294→$182,844 (3.4×, above Qwen3.7-Max's $165,224).

## 核心贡献

- **机制优先（mechanism-first）的构建顺序**：现有生成式管线先造环境、事后对齐奖励（奖励模型/rubric/轨迹标注），VHD-Play 反转依赖——先采样并求解 M(θ) 得到 z_θ=(u*(θ), u₀(θ))，据其固定结果规则 R_θ，再"实现"可执行动态 D(θ) 并包装为环境 E(θ)；奖励为 r = clip((u(πτ;θ)−u₀)/(u*−u₀), [0,1])，评分路径上没有任何 LLM 评审或学习到的奖励模型。
- **语料接地的规模化流水线与准入审计**：冻结 setter LLM 接收完整参数 draws + 一段从 28 个主题域采样的真实语料，生成关系数据库、≥10 个领域工具、玩家指令与默认策略；准入门槛包括可执行性、默认值落在合理区间、重放驱动复现预计算最优值；held-out 审计 480 次执行零异常、240 次重复终止结果全部一致。共 3,300 个环境，≈$0.01–0.03/条，episode 平均 59.7 个 assistant 轮（中位 31，P95 211）。
- **"会做题 ≠ 会做 agent"的三呈现诊断**：同一道题以 written-out（全文给出）/ informed-agentic（参数先公开）/ agentic（参数须通过交互获取）三种形式评测，base 模型得 0.962 / 0.251 / 0.204——数学能力天花板下，有状态交互仍留下巨大可学习空隙；GRPO 训练把三者的增量做成 (+0.030, +0.644, +0.611)，关掉 84% 的参数公开差距与 77% 的完整差距，且 written-out 反而微升（0.962→0.992）。
- **OOD 与外部迁移证据**：held-out 实例 +0.56；未见机制族中结构相近的 knapsack/LP/QP +0.63，改变决策结构或限制探测的远 OOD 族 +0.24 与 +0.16；外部基准 BFCL V4 十个交互单元 +2.84（匹配预算的 EnvScaler 对照反而 58.56，低于 base）、TravelBench +0.094、365 天电商 5/5 不破产且期末资金为 base 的 3.4 倍、超过 Qwen3.7-Max。
- **控制变量做得很细**：Oaxaca-Blinder 分解显示 +0.443 的 agentic 增益中只有 +0.030（8%）来自"更爱调代码工具"，93% 来自给定工具用法内的策略改善（采用率 50.4%→79.2%）；KL 散度仅 0.089 nats/token；缩放实验（背包 6×5→11×11）显示固定 setter 能实现更大环境、规模匹配训练的增益随规模增长（+0.412→+0.861），说明"造环境"与"学策略"可以协同扩展。

## Key Contributions

- **A construction-order inversion (mechanism-first)**: sample and solve the mathematical model M(θ) BEFORE building the environment, fixing the verifiable outcome rule R_θ from the solved pair (u*, u₀); the executable dynamics D(θ) and the agent-facing wrapper E(θ) are then realized from the same θ, giving stateful interaction, exact reference-anchored rewards (default→0, optimum→1), and near-zero-marginal-cost extension via re-sampling θ and corpus seeds.
- **A corpus-grounded, audited generation pipeline**: a frozen setter LLM instantiates each draw as a relational database with ≥10 executable tools and a player instruction from 28 topical domains (normalized entropy 0.997); admission gates check executability, a valid default region, and reward agreement at the optimum via replay drivers. 3,300 environments admitted at ≈$0.01–0.03 each; a held-out audit ran 480 executions with zero exceptions and all 240 repeated terminal outcomes matching stored references.
- **The F/I/A diagnostic of the agentic gap**: presenting the same optimization problems as written-out (F), parameter-revealed agentic (I), and full agentic (A) forms under a solver-derived score scale shows the 35B base model at 0.962/0.251/0.204 — a large, learnable shortfall that is NOT a math-ability deficit. Training with GRPO on 3 families moves (ΔF, ΔI, ΔA) = (+0.030, +0.644, +0.611), closing 84% of the I gap and 77% of the full gap while raising F to 0.992.
- **Transfer within and beyond the substrate**: held-out instances +0.56; unseen families +0.63 (near-OOD: knapsack/LP/QP), +0.24 and +0.16 (far-OOD); external transfer of +2.84 on BFCL V4 ten interaction cells, +0.094 TravelBench plan score, and bankruptcy-free 365-day storefront completion (5/5 vs Base 1/5) with 3.4× ending balance; a matched-budget EnvScaler control trained identically scores 58.56 on BFCL (below Base's 61.25).
- **Confound controls and co-scaling**: an Oaxaca-Blinder decomposition attributes only +0.030 of the +0.443 agentic gain to code-tool adoption shifts (93% within-stratum); a knapsack sweep from 6×5 to 11×11 shows a frozen 35B setter can realize environments the same checkpoint cannot yet solve, with scale-matched training gains growing with size (+0.412 → +0.861; larger configs average +0.768 vs +0.482).

## Methodology

**Construction order (mechanism-first).** For a mechanism family f (one of 11 catalogued optimization problems), sample parameters θ ~ P_f and solve M(θ) with a family-specific exact or numerical procedure — backward Bellman DP over joint inventory states, Held-Karp routing with dynamic-programming backlog, HGHS linear programming and SLSQP separable QP via scipy, Held-Karp/enumeration for knapsack and scheduling, backward induction for negotiation — all computed WITHOUT any language model. The solution yields z_θ = (u*(θ), u₀(θ)), which fixes the outcome rule R_θ(·; z_θ). Only then is D(θ) (state space, transition Δ_θ, action set, utility U_θ) realized and wrapped into E(θ) with an observation map Ω_θ that hides model parameters behind catalogue, probe, decision, and clock actions; probe and decision actions share one budget, instantiating the explore/commit coupling.

**Corpus-grounded realization.** A frozen setter LLM (Qwen3.7-Max in the main runs) receives the complete draw plus one independently sampled passage from 28 real-world topical domains and emits an entity/relationship model, a backing database, at least ten executable tools, the player instruction, an executable default policy, and the required output format; candidates failing execution or interface alignment are refined or regenerated. Each record retains the full draw internally while publishing only interface observations, so the policy never sees θ or references directly.

**Admission and verification.** Three gates: C1 executability (declared tools initialize and execute; available reference policies reach terminal outcomes), C2 a valid default region (default outcome inside a family-admissible range), C3 reward agreement at the optimum (a replay driver must reproduce the precomputed optimum within tolerance through the generated interface). A stratified post-generation audit over 8 replay-supported families (10 held-out environments each, oracle/default/idle policies run twice from fresh state) produced 480 executions with no exceptions and all 240 repeated outcomes agreeing with stored references.

**Policy optimization.** GRPO on the normalized scalar terminal reward: 64 prompts/step, 16 retained rollouts per prompt (oversampled to 18), group-mean advantage without std normalization, no KL penalty or value model, lr 2×10⁻⁶, clip 4×10⁻⁵, 34 optimizer steps consuming 2,176 of the 2,200-environment training partition (34,816 scored rollouts). Starting checkpoint: Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (Base).

**Evaluation.** Generated-family scores on 300 held-out environments (3 training families, 100 each) and 800 unseen-family environments (8 families × 100); the F/I/A three-presentation comparison on five optimization families; external benchmarks under native protocols — BFCL V4's ten interaction-focused cells (multi-turn, agentic memory, hallucination), TravelBench (20 multi-day travel-planning tasks), and E-Commerce Bench (365-day autonomous storefront, five seeded runs/arm); Qwen3.7-Max API as an in-model reference. A matched-budget control trains the same checkpoint on 2,200 released EnvScaler (environment-first) scenarios with identical GRPO settings and 34-step budget. A knapsack co-scaling sweep trains separate scale-matched checkpoints at 6×5, 8×7, 9×9, and 11×11 (items × periods) under one frozen setter.

## Results & Highlights

- **Five-family diagnostic (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B):** mean agentic score 0.204 → 0.815 (+0.611) after 34 GRPO steps, surpassing the Qwen3.7-Max reference at 0.429 on the same agentic evaluation, while written-out performance rises slightly from 0.962 to 0.992 (written-out minus agentic shortfall: 0.758 → 0.177). Family-level agentic gains: inventory DP +0.463, knapsack +0.726, LP +0.518, QP +0.633, routing +0.718.
- **The three-presentation gap:** the base checkpoint scores 0.962 written-out (F) but only 0.251 informed-agentic (I) and 0.204 agentic (A) on identical problems and scoring; training moves (F, I, A) by (+0.030, +0.644, +0.611), closing 84% of the parameter-revealed gap and 77% of the full written-out-to-agentic gap — evidence that the deficit is stateful information acquisition, not problem-solving ability.
- **Transfer to unseen mechanisms:** held-out instances from the three trained families gain +0.56; near-OOD unseen families (knapsack, LP, QP) gain +0.63; far-OOD groups gain +0.24 (sequential stopping/search) and +0.16 (facility location, scheduling, scheduling-tardiness).
- **External benchmarks:** BFCL V4 ten interaction cells 61.25 → 64.08 (+2.84; eight cells improve, two unchanged; the matched-budget EnvScaler control drops to 58.56); TravelBench plan score 0.700 → 0.794 (+0.094, still below Qwen3.7-Max's 0.891); on the 365-day E-Commerce Bench the trained checkpoint completes 5/5 storefront runs without bankruptcy (Base 1/5), with mean ending balance $54,294 → $182,844 (3.4×), exceeding Qwen3.7-Max's $165,224 over 1,147–1,825 assistant turns.
- **Scale co-scaling (knapsack, scale-matched checkpoints):** gains grow with environment size — +0.412 at 6×5 (0.432→0.843), +0.551 at 8×7 (0.321→0.872), +0.675 at 9×9 (0.237→0.912), +0.861 at 11×11 (0.085→0.946); the two larger configurations average +0.768 vs +0.482 for the smaller two, and the frozen setter realizes all scales it is given.
- **Not just tool-adopting more:** an Oaxaca-Blinder decomposition attributes +0.030 of the +0.443 agentic gain to increased code-tool adoption across strata (pooling 50.4% → 79.2% adoption), leaving 93% as within-stratum policy improvement; the trained policy's KL from base is ≈0.089 nats/token (95% CI 0.082–0.097).
- **Cost and setter capacity:** ≈$0.01–0.03 per admitted environment at public rates; a frozen Qwen3.6-35B-A3B setter achieves a 70% effective admission rate (22/30 records emitted, 21/22 reference-preserving) vs Qwen3.7-Max's 100% with fewer attempts (1.03 vs 2.50) and zero interface refinements, at comparable per-admission cost.

## Authors & Credibility

The paper comes from Alibaba Token Foundry (Alibaba Group) with the Georgia Institute of Technology: first author Xinjie Shen (Georgia Tech, work completed during a summer internship, corresponding author), with Wei Fan (listed second; the last author, Dayiheng Liu, is marked project lead) leading a team including Xudong Guo, Jianhong Tu, Yang Su, Chuqiao Kuang, Yinger Zhang, and Lianghao Deng. The Token Foundry affiliation (the group behind the Qwen model line referenced throughout — Qwen3.6-35B-A3B as the base/trainee, Qwen3.7-Max as the setter and frontier reference) gives the authors direct control over the trainee checkpoint, which is a credibility asset for reproducibility of the training claims. Methodologically the paper is unusually careful for industrial work: it includes admission audits with execution counts, a matched-budget environment-first control (EnvScaler), a code-tool-adoption decomposition, bootstrap CIs on the KL diagnostic, and an explicit limitations appendix (App. K) that volunteers single-run/single-seed caveats. The benchmark suite (BFCL V4, TravelBench, E-Commerce Bench — the latter an Alibaba-affiliated benchmark, cited as Fan et al. 2026 with overlapping authors) is current and native-protocol, though the overlap between the E-Commerce Bench authorship and this paper's team is worth noting when reading its strongest external result.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The core contribution — fixing the reward from a pre-solved model before realizing the environment — is a genuine reframing, but it is an ordering inversion of well-known ingredients: RL environments from OR/math models (OR-Gym), verifiable terminal rewards (Reasoning Gym, RLVE), and LLM-generated tool environments (EnvScaler, AutoEnv). The Table 6 "four axes" framing is honest about this: mechanism-first changes construction provenance, not feedback localization or the optimizer. The most original empirical content is the F/I/A decomposition showing a 0.962→0.204 written-out-to-agentic collapse for a model that is near-ceiling on the math itself — that is a crisp, quotable finding.

**Rigor.** Above average, with specific caveats. (1) Every headline number rests on ONE training run and ONE evaluation seed per benchmark (admitted in App. K), so deltas like +2.84 on BFCL or +1.00 on the miss_param cell carry no variance estimates; BFCL cells move in steps of 0.25 granularity and two cells are unchanged, so per-cell noise is plausible. (2) Only three of the eleven families are trained on (inventory DP, routing, negotiation), all budget-coupled sequential decision problems; the "near-OOD" set (knapsack/LP/QP) shares the coupled-budget structure, so the +0.63 OOD claim covers a narrow structural neighborhood — the far-OOD +0.16 on facility location/scheduling is the more honest transfer number and it is modest. (3) Three families (negotiation, sequential stopping, sequential search) are scored against hindsight ceilings no online policy can attain, so their absolute scores (e.g., negotiation 0.470 training reward) are not "fraction of optimal" in the usual sense; the paper flags this, but the headline 0.815 mean only covers the five true-optimum families. (4) The EnvScaler control is largely self-run (their checkpoint, their GRPO budget, 2,200 of EnvScaler's released scenarios); EnvScaler's original paper reported gains from a weaker 8B start, and the authors themselves concede the "large gains from a lower 8B baseline do not automatically translate... from a stronger 35B policy" — so the environment-first vs mechanism-first contrast is contingent on starting-model strength, not a universal verdict. (5) Diversity is scenario-level, not mechanism-level: all 11 families are classic textbook OR problems with computable references, so the corpus grounding varies the skin (28 domains), while the underlying decision structures remain a small, well-verified family — the same scope constraint as verifiable-reward math RL, and open-ended environments (web, coding, dialogue) are untested as targets.

**Red flags / overclaiming.** Relatively few, and mostly disclosed: the E-Commerce Bench showcase (the largest external win, 3.4× balance) is an in-house benchmark with overlapping authorship; the "$0.01–0.03 per environment" figure is a reconstruction from public token rates rather than measured billing, and the 35B setter's own 30% admission failure erases its per-token price advantage (footnote 1); the abstract's "gains appear on held-out instances from all three training families and eight unseen mechanism families" is technically true but flattens +0.63 vs +0.16 into one sentence.

## Strengths

- The mechanism-first ordering delivers what verifiable-reward math RL delivers for QA — exact, judge-free, cheat-resistant outcome signals — but for stateful multi-turn interaction, and the replay-based admission audit (C1–C3, 480-execution held-out audit) actually verifies that generated dynamics honor the precomputed references.
- The F/I/A three-presentation diagnostic is a clean, reusable instrument: it separates "can't solve the problem" from "can't act on the problem," and the 0.962 vs 0.204 base-model collapse is the paper's most consequential observation for anyone training agentic models.
- Confound controls go beyond the usual: matched-budget EnvScaler control, Oaxaca-Blinder adoption decomposition, code-tool adoption measured across all eleven families (50.4%→79.2%), bootstrap CI on the KL shift, and paired trajectory case studies showing WHERE the base policy fails (myopic per-period planning, premature commitment, over-exploration past information value).
- The co-scaling experiment (frozen setter + scale-matched trainees up to 11×11 knapsack) directly addresses the "will this substrate run out" question and shows gains growing with scale (+0.412→+0.861) — evidence the setter/trainee asymmetry is a feature, not a bug.
- Cost accounting is concrete (tokens per admission, public rates, setter-capacity table comparing 35B vs Max setters) rather than hand-wavy, and the pipeline details (11 families, solvers, admission gates, partitions: 2,200 train / 300 held-out / 800 unseen) are documented at reproduction-useful specificity.

## Limitations

- Single training run and single evaluation seed per benchmark; no error bars on the headline deltas (self-reported in App. K).
- Training covers only 3 of 11 families; far-OOD transfer is +0.16–0.24, and near-OOD families share the trained families' coupled-budget structure, limiting how "unseen" they really are.
- The substrate requires computable reference outcomes — all 11 families are classical OR/math models, so scenario diversity (28 corpus domains) far exceeds mechanism diversity; nothing here tests open-ended or weakly-verifiable environments.
- Terminal scalar reward only; no intermediate supervision or credit assignment; training gains are not separately attributed to the outcome rule versus the interface (self-reported).
- Some families are scored against hindsight ceilings no online policy can reach, so absolute scores across families are not commensurable.
- External gains are modest in frontier terms: BFCL +2.84 and TravelBench 0.794 still trail Qwen3.7-Max (0.891); the decisive external win (storefront balance) is on an in-house benchmark with overlapping authorship, and the environment-first comparison is self-run at one starting-model scale.
- Four of eleven evaluated families lack written-out baselines (n/a cells), so the "written-out vs agentic" story is complete only for the five optimization families.

## Relevance

这篇论文正好切中当前 agentic RL 最痛的基础设施问题——"哪里来大量便宜、奖励可信、又有状态多轮的环境"，其"先解题再造环境"的机制优先思路可直接迁移到自建训练环境的实践中，而 F/I/A 三呈现诊断（会做题 0.962 vs 会做 agent 0.204）对任何在做 agent 训练的人都是立刻可复用的测量工具。对关注 LLM 智能体强化学习、可验证奖励与环境合成的读者，这是本周必读级别的工作。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do the gains survive multi-seed training? The +2.84 BFCL delta comes from one run and one evaluation seed — what is the run-to-run variance of both the generated-family and external transfer numbers?
- How far can "near-OOD" stretch? Training on only 3 families leaves 8 untested as training targets — would training on 5–8 families (including scheduling/facility location) close the far-OOD gap of +0.16, or does mechanism diversity expose a ceiling of the 11-family catalogue?
- Can the F/I/A diagnostic predict transfer? If a checkpoint's agentic shortfall (0.962→0.204 style collapse) is the learnable signal, is the F−A gap on a candidate benchmark a predictor of how much VHD-Play-style training will move that benchmark?
- What happens with outcome rules that are only approximately solvable (large-scale MILP, stochastic control), where u*(θ) must be bounded rather than exact — does the normalization (Eq. 4) stay well-anchored?
- Does the setter/trainee co-scaling trend hold when the setter is fixed but the trainee shrinks (e.g., an A3B MoE vs a 3B dense model), i.e., is the growing-with-scale gain a property of the substrate or of the 35B model's latent math competence?
