> **一句话总结：** 作者用激活修补（activation patching）在合成多跳知识库查找任务上直接测量"模型写下的每条推理步骤是否因果地承载最终答案"：Qwen3-4B 在最敏感的中间层有 76.9% ± 2.8% 的自述步骤能把答案翻转到预测的反事实目标，而同题目的行为式文本编辑测试给出 88.2%，比因果忠实度高估 11.4 个百分点（最容易的 2 跳题目上差距达 20.2 分）；Qwen3-1.7B 的因果忠实度随推理深度从 2 跳的 68.4% 崩塌到 6 跳的 29.7%，而 4B 稳定在 75–79%。

> **TL;DR:** On synthetic 2–6-hop pseudo-word KB-lookup tasks, patching the residual stream at the exact token span where Qwen3-4B states each intermediate step flips the final answer to the predicted counterfactual in 76.9% ± 2.8% of 851 interventions (layer 8), versus 8.9 ± 12.5% for a random-position null — yet the standard behavioral text-editing faithfulness test scores 88.2% on the same items, overestimating causal faithfulness by 11.4 points (111 items flip under behavioral-only vs 14 under causal-only; McNemar p < 10⁻¹⁵). Causal faithfulness tracks model capability, not just difficulty: Qwen3-1.7B collapses from 68.4% (2 hops) to 29.7% (6 hops) while Qwen3-4B stays flat at 75–79%.

## 核心贡献

- **提出 CLB（causally load-bearing）指标**：不同于以往只测"性能退化"的因果审计，本文的干预带有已知、可预测的目标——每次修补都应把答案切换到构造出的特定反事实实体，从而能严格区分"该步骤的内容被因果使用"与"仅仅是扰动产生了影响"。
- **首次在同一批题目上 item-matched 地对比因果测试与行为式文本编辑测试**：发现标准行为测试把因果忠实度高估 11.4 个百分点（88.2% vs 76.9%），且在模型最流畅、最容易被信任的简单题目上高估最严重（2 跳时差距 20.2 分）。
- **给出能力-忠实度关系的初步证据**：Qwen3-1.7B 的因果忠实度（最佳层 54.8%）远低于 Qwen3-4B（约 77%），且随跳数从 68.4% 崩塌到 29.7%——"推理步骤随难度退化为装饰性叙述"的结论对弱模型成立、对强模型不成立。
- **完整的对照体系**：阳性对照（直接修补 KB 事实，83% 翻转，构成可达上限）、事后修补安慰剂对照（0 翻转）、打乱目标特异性对照（对供体实体的翻转仅 1–15%）。

## Key Contributions

- **A causal faithfulness metric (CLB, "causally load-bearing rate") with a predicted flip-to-target criterion**: unlike prior causal audits that only measure performance degradation, each patch here has a known target — the answer should switch to a specific constructed counterfactual entity — separating "this stated content is causally used downstream" from "any perturbation matters."
- **First item-matched head-to-head of causal patching vs the standard behavioral text-editing faithfulness test on identical items**: the behavioral test overestimates causal faithfulness by 11.4 percentage points (88.2% vs 76.9% on Qwen3-4B), with the overestimate largest (20.2 points at 2 hops) exactly where fluent CoT inspires the most trust.
- **Evidence that causal faithfulness tracks capability rather than difficulty**: Qwen3-1.7B is far less faithful overall (54.8% at its best layer vs the 4B's ~77%) and collapses with depth (68.4% → 29.7% from 2 to 6 hops), while the 4B holds a flat 75–79% band.
- **A full control suite**: positive control (patching the KB fact itself flips 83% — the achievable ceiling), post-answer sanity control (0 flips), and shuffled-target specificity control (donor-entity flips only 1–15%).

## Methodology

**Task.** Each item is a multi-hop lookup (2–6 hops) over an in-context knowledge base of pseudo-word entities ("The r of X is Y", relation arity between 2 and 6), with 50 items per KB plus distractor facts. Pseudo-words eliminate pretraining leakage and keep every entity to 3+ tokens. The prompt instructs the model to state each intermediate step as "Step k:" and end with "Answer:". For every hop k, a counterfactual KB is built by altering a single fact resolving hop k so that the preceding step yields a different entity E_k′; bridge facts keep the counterfactual-implanted final answer derivable in-context. A KB validator re-derives gold and counterfactual answers, yielding 1000 passing pairs; clean runs (both answers correct, exact format) number 221/250 usable for Qwen3-4B and 120/250 for Qwen3-1.7B.

**Intervention.** For each hop k: (1) run the counterfactual prompt and locate the exact token span where the model states E_k; (2) cache the residual-stream block-output activations at that span; (3) rerun the clean prompt up to the end of the clean step-k span while substituting the cached activations at layer L (end-aligned), then greedily continue; (4) score a CLB hit if the final answer equals the counterfactual-simplified answer. All generations are greedy, bf16, fixed seeds. A random-position null patches the same activations at a random non-step span.

**Behavioral baseline.** On the same items, the text of E_k is replaced with E_k′, generation is truncated after the edited step and continued, scored identically — the standard behavioral paradigm.

**Controls.** (1) *Prompt-fact patch* (positive): patch the KB fact's object-entity activations from the counterfactual prompt — an upper bound on achievable flips. (2) *Post-answer patch* (sanity): patch after the answer is emitted — must yield 0 flips. (3) *Shuffled-target* (specificity): patch a donor entity from a different item's run, absent from the current KB — donor flips should be rare.

## Results & Highlights

- **Stated steps are largely load-bearing for the strong model.** On the 2–6-hop pseudo-word KB task, patching the stated-step span flips Qwen3-4B's final answer to the specific predicted counterfactual in 78.3% ± 2.8% of 851 interventions at layer 4 and 76.9% ± 2.8% at layer 8, versus 8.9 ± 12.5% for the random-position null (11.3% as summarized in the abstract). The positive control — patching the underlying KB fact directly — flips 83% of cases, so stated-step patching reaches about 96% of the achievable ceiling. Post-answer patches yield 0 flips; shuffled-target donor flips stay at 1–15%; non-target disruption stays ≤ 19%.
- **The behavioral test systematically overestimates causal faithfulness.** Text editing flips the answer in 88.2% ± 2% of 873 cases on the same items; item-by-item at layer 8, the behavioral score exceeds the causal score by 11.4 points, with 111 items flipping under the behavioral test alone against 14 under the causal test alone (McNemar, p < 10⁻¹⁵) — behavioral flips are nearly a superset of causal flips. The gap is 20.2 points at 2 hops, narrowing to 6.6 points at 6 hops: the overestimate is worst on the easiest items.
- **Faithfulness tracks capability, not difficulty — except when the model is weak.** Qwen3-4B's CLB rate stays within a narrow 75–79% band across 2–6 hops. Qwen3-1.7B reaches only 54.8% at its best middle layer against a behavioral score of 67.6% (a 9.9-point gap), and its CLB rate falls from 68.4% at 2 hops to 29.7% at 6 hops while the behavioral score stays 9–19 points higher — the "decorative reasoning" claim holds for the weaker model and fails for the stronger one.
- **Layer profile.** The effect is concentrated in early-to-mid layers and disappears by layer 20 of 36 for the 4B and by layers 12–16 of 28 for the 1.7B: once the continuation has consumed the stated step, later-layer content no longer changes the outcome.

## Authors & Credibility

Abhiram Bhupatiraju and Rayan Nyaupane are both at The University of Texas at Austin; this is a preprint under review with no prior-publication track record stated for the pair, and a small two-author team. The methodological debt is explicit and well-placed: the paper builds on activation-patching best practices (Zhang & Nanda, ICLR 2024), knowledge-editing work (Meng et al.'s ROME), and the behavioral-faithfulness literature (Turpin et al.; Lanham et al.). The experimental hygiene (validator-filtered KBs, three controls, item-matched McNemar comparison, greedy fixed-seed decoding, 95% CIs in figures) suggests careful execution despite the short format. No code or data release is mentioned, which tempers reproducibility for a preprint from an unestablished group.

## Critical Assessment

- **The scope gap between construct and claim is the biggest issue.** The task is a synthetic single-relation pseudo-word lookup — maximally clean, but reasoning "steps" are entity substitutions, not the inference, arithmetic, or planning steps of naturalistic CoT (math, code). The headline overestimate (11.4 points) is quantified only in this regime; extrapolating it to CoT monitoring of real capability evaluations is plausible but unproven, and the paper's framing ("behavioral scores are upper bounds") quietly universalizes a single-task result.
- **The capability claim rests on n=2 models from one family.** Both models are Qwen3, at small scale (1.7B and 4B), with greedy decoding. "Causal faithfulness rose with model scale in this pair" is a two-point trend that could be a Qwen3-specific artifact, a parameter-count effect, or a training-data effect — the design cannot distinguish these, and the authors do acknowledge this.
- **A selection-bias worry the paper does not address: only 120/250 clean runs were usable for Qwen3-1.7B (versus 221/250 for the 4B).** Over half of the weaker model's passing items were discarded because it failed the clean-run criteria — meaning the 1.7B's 54.8% is estimated on a survivor set of items it could already solve, which likely *inflates* the weaker model's faithfulness estimate and, if anything, makes the capability gap conservative. But it also means the 68.4% → 29.7% collapse is measured on a shrinking, non-random item subset as hops increase.
- **The early-layer number is partially confounded, and the paper says so.** Early-layer patches partially overlap with token-identity replacement — the 78.3% at layer 4 partly reflects trivial token substitution rather than content use, which is why layer 8 is headlined. A fuller treatment would patch attention and MLP outputs separately; block-output patching cannot localize *where* in the layer the content is used.
- **Only one behavioral protocol is compared.** The "behavioral test" here is specifically truncate-and-recontinue after a text edit. Other behavioral measures (Lanham-style truncation curves, biasing-feature tests à la Turpin et al., paraphrase consistency) may behave differently; concluding that "the standard behavioral test" overestimates generalizes from one variant.
- **Span alignment truncates when clean and counterfactual entity spans differ in length**, an unquantified source of asymmetry between the two intervention types; and the two interventions differ in what the model sees (text edits are visible in-context, activation patches are not), so the item-matched comparison is matched on task but not on observation channel.

## Strengths

- The flip-to-target criterion gives the causal metric *specificity* that degradation-based audits lack — a flip must be to the constructed counterfactual, not just any change.
- Rare three-control rigor for a short paper: the positive control (83%) establishes an empirical ceiling, the post-answer patch (0 flips) validates the pipeline, and the shuffled-target control (1–15%) validates specificity; the 77% headline is correctly read as ~96% of what is achievable.
- The item-matched design plus McNemar test directly quantifies the gap between the deployed measurement practice (behavioral editing) and ground truth (causality), which is the practically actionable number.
- Deterministic task construction with a KB validator guarantees counterfactual answers are derivable in-context — no leakage ambiguity thanks to pseudo-words.
- Honest, specific limitations section (synthetic task, one family, greedy decoding, early-layer confound) rather than boilerplate.

## Limitations

- Synthetic single-relation lookup only; naturalistic reasoning (math, code) may show different faithfulness profiles and different behavioral-vs-causal gaps.
- One model family (Qwen3), two small scales (1.7B, 4B), greedy decoding — the capability trend is a two-point observation and may not generalize across families or to larger models.
- Usable-run attrition is severe for the weaker model (120/250), introducing a survivorship selection effect on all 1.7B estimates.
- Early-layer patches are partially confounded with token-identity replacement; attention and MLP outputs are not patched separately, so the mechanistic localization is coarse.
- Span-alignment truncation when clean/counterfactual entity lengths differ; and behavioral vs causal interventions differ in observation channel (visible text edit vs invisible activation patch).
- No code or data release stated, limiting independent verification of the 851/873-intervention pipelines.

## Relevance

对依赖 CoT 监控（CoT monitoring）作为 AI 安全机制的路线，这篇论文给出了一个直接可操作的警示：行为式忠实度测试（编辑文本看答案变不变）系统性高估因果忠实度，且恰恰在推理最流畅、最容易被信任的简单题目上高估最严重（20.2 分）。同时其"忠实度随模型能力提升而改善、弱模型推理随深度退化为装饰"的发现，对评估未来更强模型的可监控性有参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the 11.4-point behavioral-vs-causal overestimate transfer to naturalistic CoT (arithmetic, symbolic planning, code), where steps carry inferred content rather than entity lookups?
- Would the capability-vs-difficulty trend replicate across model families (Llama, Gemma, DeepSeek) and larger scales, or is it a Qwen3-specific artifact?
- How do other behavioral protocols — Lanham-style truncation curves, Turpin-style biasing features, paraphrase consistency — compare item-matched against CLB? Is the overestimate specific to text editing?
- Does patching attention and MLP outputs separately (rather than fused block outputs) sharpen the early-layer token-identity confound and the layer profile?
- Can a CLB-like quantity be estimated when no counterfactual target can be constructed, i.e., on tasks where faithfulness auditing is currently done purely behaviorally?
