Why this matters

— Critical Assessment

Methodology

  1. The obstacle. Doubling FP4 (E2M1) values stores exactly S = {0, ±1…±4, ±6, ±8, ±12} — every residue mod 13 — so the prior base-13 scheme keeps 3 limbs per operand: 3 × 3 = 9 GEMMs. Karatsuba/Toom-Cook fail here: limb sums reach ±24 and land in the holes of S.
  2. The AWE move. Limbs carry free integer weights; stored planes are linear combinations of limbs; reconstruction weights λ = l/D keep D a power of two, so the final division is an exact shift. INT8×INT8 solves with weights (1, 29, 37), coverage 127.
  3. Search & verify. Declare cell, candidate planes, weight range → gcd filter → exact-rational linear solve → exhaustive coverage check → all-pairs identity verification by an independently written verifier.
  4. Runtime. Table-lookup decomposition → combination planes formed by adds → 6 FP4 GEMMs → exact integer accumulation → one shift by D.
  5. Unification. Relaxing the identity to congruence mod m folds in residue-number systems (Ozaki II); moduli 11 and 13 now need a single FP4 product each.

Verified reconstruction · INT8×INT8 · D = 1AB = −65P₀₀ − 251P₁₁ + 259P₂₂ + 29P₀₁ + 37P₀₂ + 1073P₁₂

Results

On B300 — whose tensor cores dropped INT8 — exact INT8×INT8 runs at 1.00× the native BF16 rate; BitNet-style [0, ±1]×INT8 at 2.00×. Encodings are exhaustively verified; rates are peak arithmetic only.

Exactness improves too: capping every stored value at ±6 keeps coverage 148 and pushes K* from 116,508 to 466,033 — a 4× longer guaranteed-exact FP32 accumulation at the same product count.

Strengths

  • Clean generalization that provably subsumes the positional baseline and unifies direct and residue-number constructions.
  • Immediately usable encodings — INT8 in 6, FP16 in 12, FP64 CRT in 59 — shipped with full coefficients and JSONL records.
  • One-line exactness check K ≤ K* = ⌊2p/(cA·cB)⌋; the ±6-capped variant quadruples K* at no product-count cost.
  • Exemplary verification: independent verifier, exact rational arithmetic, exhaustive input pairs, public environment hashes.
  • Timely: quantifies exactly what B300 loses by dropping INT8 — and what BitNet-style weights recover (K* = 2,097,152).

Limitations

  • No implementation, no measured performance: plane production, memory traffic, and >64-bit segmented reconstruction are excluded from every ratio.
  • Only 0.33× wherever a native INT8 unit exists (B200, RTX PRO 6000) — the compelling case is INT8-less hardware.
  • Catalogue openly incomplete: widths >17 and 24–53 unsearched; counts are lower bounds of the search, not proven optima.
  • Open conjectures ({0, ±1} plane components; a 5-product INT8×INT8) leave the theoretical picture unsettled.
  • Fine K* boundaries (15 / 14 / 13) matter only for very long reductions, K up to 65,636.

Questions for further exploration

  1. How much of the theoretical 1.00–2.00× on B300 survives a real CUDA kernel once plane production and segmented reconstruction are paid?
  2. Can the {0, ±1}-components conjecture be proven — and does a 5-product INT8×INT8 exist outside the searched families?
  3. Does the 9 → 6 saving shorten wall-clock FP64 (DGEMM) emulation once Shared-Memory plane writes are counted?
  4. Does the 3-product BitNet path (K* = 2,097,152) beat simply upcasting 1.58-bit weights to FP8 on B300-class hardware?
