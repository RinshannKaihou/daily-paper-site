> **一句话总结：** 把 KV-cache 驱逐从"固定内存预算"重新表述为"风险目标"问题：基于 Learn-then-Test 固定序列检验的压缩器无关校准框架，在给定可靠性契约 (τ=0.10, ε=0.05, δ=0.05) 下自动选出最激进且可 finite-sample 认证的保留策略（否则回退全量 KV）——在 Llama-3.1-8B LongBench 上认证保留率比经验阈值法高 5–10 个百分点（Layer-DefensiveKV 0.35 vs 插入法 0.30，TEST 退化事件 4.00% vs 5.33%），而 SnapKV 在 RULER-32K 上首个候选即有 10.38% 事件、触发全量 KV 回退。

> **TL;DR:** This paper reframes KV-cache eviction as a reliability-first problem: instead of fixing a memory budget, a deployment contract (τ, ε) specifies that request-level utility loss vs paired full-KV inference may exceed τ on at most ε of requests, and a fixed-sequence Learn-then-Test (LTT) calibration picks the most aggressive retention policy certified at level δ=0.05, falling back to full KV otherwise. On LongBench with Llama-3.1-8B, certification selects 5–10 percentage points more KV retention than naive empirical thresholding (e.g., Layer-DefensiveKV at 0.35 vs plug-in 0.30, cutting held-out material-degradation events from 64/1200=5.33% to 48/1200=4.00%), while SnapKV at 70% retention passes the 5% empirical gate (50/1200=4.17%) yet fails the finite-sample cutoff of 47/1200=3.92%. The same contract certifies SnapKV at 0.75 on LongBench but triggers full-KV fallback for SnapKV on RULER-32K (first candidate 0.80 has 135/1300=10.38% CAL events), and it also certifies a global threshold for the request-adaptive ReFreeKV (87.36% mean retention, 3.50% TEST risk).

## 核心贡献

- **可靠性优先的 KV 驱逐表述**：定义配对退化量 Δ_π(X)=U_full(X)−U_π(X)、"材质退化"指示器 V_τ=1{Δ_π>τ} 与部署风险 R_τ(π)=E[V_τ]；契约要求 R_τ≤ε（默认 τ=0.10, ε=0.05），即相对同请求全量 KV 推理、效用下降超过 0.10 的请求占比不超过 5%——小平均损失不再能掩盖单请求严重失败。
- **压缩器无关的有限样本认证**：将 Learn-then-Test (LTT) 实例化为固定序列检验——候选策略按保守→激进预排序，逐个做任务分层的 binomial+Hoeffding 校准 p 值检验，返回首个可认证的最激进策略；全部失败则回退全量 KV（Δ_full=0）。不修改底层驱逐算法、不假设风险对保留率单调。
- **认证的实证后果**：在 (τ,ε,δ)=(.10,.05,.05) 下，n=1200 的最大可认证违例数 K_max=47（3.92%，比名义 5% 更严）；四个 Llama LongBench 候选（SnapKV@0.70、AdaKV@0.60、DefensiveKV@0.35、Layer-DefensiveKV@0.30）经验风险 4.00%–4.83% 全部低于 5% 却无一可认证——平均基准评估与风险目标会给出实质不同的部署点。
- **请求自适应策略同样可认证**：对 ReFreeKV 这类按请求决定保留量的方法，用全局阈值序列 T=(.0005,…,.03) 做同样的固定序列检验，证明框架适用于任意 KV 内存分配形式。
- **任务异质性的诚实披露**：认证只覆盖任务均匀混合总体；事后 Bonferroni+Clopper-Pearson 联合分析显示部分任务级风险实际超过 5%（如 RULER CWE 29%、LongBench TriviaQA 20%）。

## Key Contributions

- **Reliability-first formulation of KV-cache eviction**: material degradation is defined per-request relative to paired full-KV inference (Δ_π = U_full − U_π > τ), and the deployment risk is the population frequency of such events — small average loss can no longer hide rare per-request failures (Eq. 1–5; Figure 1 shows SnapKV/AdaKV/DefensiveKV configurations with near-identical mean degradation but very different exceedance risk at τ=.10).
- **Compressor-agnostic finite-sample certification**: a fixed-sequence LTT procedure over a pre-specified, conservative-to-aggressive policy grid returns the most aggressive policy certified before the first failure, with full-KV fallback; the task-stratified calibration design uses binomial tail comparisons (with Hoeffding bounding) so violation probabilities may differ across task strata, and no monotonicity of risk in retention is assumed.
- **Empirical consequences of certification**: under (τ,ε,δ)=(.10,.05,.05), the largest certifiable violation count is K_max=47/1200 (3.92%) — four Llama LongBench candidates (SnapKV@70%, AdaKV@60%, DefensiveKV@35%, Layer-DefensiveKV@30%) all satisfy a ≤5% empirical-risk gate (48–58 events) yet none provides finite-sample evidence, so certification retains 5–10 percentage points more cache than empirical thresholding.
- **Certification of request-adaptive policies**: the same machinery calibrates a global threshold for ReFreeKV (T=.0005 certified with 39/1200=3.25% CAL risk; next threshold T=.001 with lower empirical risk 4.42% is not certified), showing the framework covers fixed-ratio, layer-adaptive, and input-adaptive KV allocation alike.
- **Honest accounting of aggregate vs per-task risk**: a post-hoc simultaneous analysis (two-sided Clopper-Pearson + Bonferroni over all 147 selected-policy-task combinations) shows per-task population risks exceeding 5% in specific cases even when the task-mixture certificate holds.

## Methodology

**Formulation.** For request X drawn from a target population P, define normalized utilities U_full(X) (full KV) and U_π(X) (eviction policy π). The signed degradation is Δ_π(X)=U_full(X)−U_π(X); the material-degradation indicator is V_τ(X;π)=1{Δ_π(X)>τ} with tolerance τ∈(0,1); the deployment risk is R_τ(π)=E[V_τ]. The reliability contract is R_τ(π)≤ε, and the goal is min b(π) subject to the contract, where b(π) is the retained-cache fraction. The main setting is (τ,ε,δ)=(.10,.05,.05): utility drops >0.10 on at most 5% of requests, with probability ≥0.95 over calibration sampling.

**Finite-sample certification via fixed-sequence testing.** Instantiating Learn-then-Test (Angelopoulos et al., 2025), each candidate eviction policy is treated as a hypothesis H_j: R_τ(π_j)≥ε vs H_j^A: R_τ(π_j)<ε (equality conservatively placed in the null). The CAL set is task-stratified: n/H requests per task h, so the target population is the task-uniform mixture P=(1/H)ΣP_h, which makes the mean violation count a binomial with the same mean q_j=R_τ(π_j) under stratified sampling. Valid (conservative) p-values come from the binomial lower tail: p_j^inv = Pr{Bin(n,ε) ≤ K_j} for K_j < ⌊nε⌋−1, else 1. Candidates are tested in a pre-specified order from conservative to aggressive; testing stops at the first non-rejection and returns that certified candidate; if none is certified, the procedure returns full KV (Δ_full=0 by definition, so the fallback trivially satisfies the contract). The guarantee is per model-compressor-benchmark sequence; selecting among multiple sequences using their CAL outcomes would require additional multiplicity control. For n=1200, ε=δ=.05, the largest certifiable violation count is K_max=47 (empirical risk 3.92%).

**Candidate grids.** Fixed-budget compressors use the retention sequence (0.80, 0.75, …, 0.20) for π_j; the request-adaptive ReFreeKV uses a threshold sequence T=(.0005, .001, .002, .003, .005, .0075, .01, .015, .02, .03) ordered conservative to aggressive. Each method runs with its evaluated implementation's default hyperparameters (observation window 32, kernel size 5, AdaKV α=0.2), only the retained fraction varies, and all generations are deterministic greedy — eliminating sampling noise from the paired comparisons.

**Benchmarks and populations.** LongBench: the 12 tasks with ≥200 distinct contexts (2WikiMQA, GovReport, HotpotQA, LCC, MultiNews, MuSiQue, PassageCount, PassageRetrieval-en, RepoBench-P, SAMSum, TREC, TriviaQA); 200 requests sampled per task (seed 20260904) and split 100/100 into CAL/TEST, yielding n=1200 per split; metrics normalized to [0,1] before paired degradation. RULER: all 13 tasks at 32K context, 100 requests per task per split, n=1300. ReFreeKV is evaluated separately under its own 8K input-budget protocol (budget = 8192 tokens minus generation limit and a 20-token margin). CAL and TEST each have 100 requests per task; per-task TEST risks are descriptive only — the certificate applies to the task-balanced mixture. Hardware: NVIDIA RTX A6000 (48GB); FlashAttention-2 for fixed-budget runs, eager attention for ReFreeKV.

## Results & Highlights

- **Certification is materially stricter than empirical thresholding.** With n=1200 the finite-sample cutoff is 47 events (3.92%) vs the plug-in allowance of 60 (5%). The first non-certified Llama LongBench candidates are SnapKV at 70% retention (50 violations, 4.17%), AdaKV at 60% (49, 4.08%), DefensiveKV at 35% (58, 4.83%), and Layer-DefensiveKV at 30% (48, 4.00%) — all four pass the nominal 5% gate yet none certifies, so certification selects retention 0.05–0.10 higher.
- **Certified operating points, LongBench (Table 1).** Llama-3.1-8B: SnapKV 0.75 (CAL 3.50% / TEST 2.30%), AdaKV 0.65 (3.33% / 3.08%), DefensiveKV 0.40 (3.67% / 4.08%), Layer-DefensiveKV 0.35 (3.58% / 4.00%). Mistral-7B: SnapKV 0.70 (3.33% / 3.33%), AdaKV 0.70 (3.58% / 2.58%), DefensiveKV 0.40 (3.67% / 5.08%), Layer-DefensiveKV 0.45 (3.17% / 3.92%). Eight of nine pairs land below 5% on held-out TEST; Mistral DefensiveKV's 61/1200 (5.08%) is descriptive and does not invalidate the CAL-based certificate.
- **Concrete plug-in vs certified delta.** For Layer-DefensiveKV on Llama LongBench, the plug-in rule would deploy retention 0.30 while certification selects 0.35; on the same held-out TEST requests these yield 64/1200 (5.33%) vs 48/1200 (4.00%) material-degradation events — a 1.3-point risk difference, though the paper notes this comparison is descriptive, not a statistically significant paired test.
- **Same contract, wildly different eviction levels across benchmarks (Table 2, RULER-32K).** SnapKV's first candidate (retention 0.80) already has 135/1300 = 10.38% CAL events, so the procedure falls back to full KV (retention 1.00, 0% risk), even though fractions 0.80–1.00 were never tested; meanwhile AdaKV certifies at 0.70 (TEST 2.77%), DefensiveKV at 0.45 (3.00%), and Layer-DefensiveKV at 0.35 (3.54%). This is the paper's headline contrast: SnapKV certifies at 0.75 on LongBench but not at all on RULER-32K.
- **Request-adaptive ReFreeKV (separate 8K protocol).** Threshold T=.0005 is certified with 39/1200 (3.25%) CAL events; the next threshold T=.001 has lower empirical CAL risk (4.42%) but is not certified. The selected policy retains 87.48% of the KV cache on average in CAL and 87.36% on TEST with 42/1200 (3.50%) degradation events, and its aggregate mean utility barely changes vs full KV (32.1 → 32.0).
- **Sensitivity to the contract (Table 3).** Tightening τ from .10 to .05 at ε=.05 raises Layer-DefensiveKV retention from .35 to .65 and pushes SnapKV from .75 to full KV; loosening to ε=.20 lets six of eight entries compress to the grid floor of .20.
- **Per-task risks are far less benign than the aggregate certificate (Table 4).** Aggregate TEST risks span 2.50%–4.08%, but DefensiveKV hits 10% on 2WikiMQA, Layer-DefensiveKV 10% on SAMSum, and ReFreeKV 20% on TriviaQA. RULER TEST risk concentrates on common-word extraction (CWE): 9%, 15%, 29% for AdaKV, DefensiveKV, Layer-DefensiveKV. The post-hoc simultaneous lower bounds exceed 5% for RULER CWE under Layer-DefensiveKV (29/100; 95% simultaneous CI [14.6%, 47.1%]) and LongBench TriviaQA under ReFreeKV (20/100; [8.2%, 37.1%]). Also: LCC full-KV utility averages 71.0 and Layer-DefensiveKV@0.35 averages 75.2, yet 3% of requests lose >10 points; full-KV utility is zero on 253/1200 TEST requests.

## Authors & Credibility

The four authors are from the Department of Artificial Intelligence, Korea University (corresponding author: Hyunseok Seo, seoh@korea.ac.kr). The team demonstrates solid command of two literatures: KV-cache eviction methods (they evaluate SnapKV, AdaKV, DefensiveKV, Layer-DefensiveKV, and ReFreeKV, several from 2026 venues) and distribution-free risk control (LTT from Angelopoulos et al., 2025 Annals of Applied Statistics; RCPS from Bates et al., 2021). The methodological execution is careful: conservative null placement, fixed pre-registration of grids and order before CAL inspection, a fixed random seed (20260904) for dataset construction, deterministic decoding, and a genuinely candid Limitations section plus a post-hoc Bonferroni analysis that exposes weaknesses in their own aggregate certificate. No prior-work external evidence about this group's track record was verifiable from the paper itself, but the internal rigor signals a statistically literate team. The work is positioning itself in an emerging 2026 wave of "certified KV compression" papers (Xie 2026 error certificates; WitCert runtime guarantees; ComplierKV/CompilKV risk-adaptive compression), and its differentiation — population-level task risk vs attention-output error — is clearly articulated.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The reframing (memory budget → risk target) is timely and well-motivated, but the machinery is a direct, competent application of existing LTT/RCPS statistical tooling to a new risk variable — the contribution is the formulation and the empirical demonstration, not new statistics. The paired per-request degradation definition relative to full-KV on the same request is the right choice, and Figure 1's mean-vs-tail dissociation is the paper's strongest conceptual point.

**Rigor.** Mostly high, with three caveats. (1) The degenerate-request quirk: full-KV utility is zero on 253/1200 TEST requests, and since compressed utility is nonnegative, any request with U_full(X)≤τ can never incur material degradation — the risk denominator is partly padded with requests that mechanically cannot fail, so the 3.92% cutoff is effectively less conservative than it looks. The authors flag this but do not quantify the risk with those requests removed. (2) The guarantee holds only for the declared task-uniform mixture; production traffic rarely matches a uniform task mix, and the paper's own Table 4 shows per-task risks of 10–29% — the certificate is thin comfort for a deployment where one task dominates. (3) The SnapKV full-KV fallback on RULER is partly a grid artifact: fractions between 0.80 and 1.00 were never tested, so the procedure returns full KV while all tested fractions sat below 5% empirical risk — the abstract presents this as a reliability finding, but it is equally evidence that a coarse 0.05-step grid can force needlessly conservative fallbacks.

**Reproducibility.** Good on determinism: fixed seed, pre-specified grids, deterministic decoding, fully described hyperparameters and split protocol. However, no code or data release is mentioned in the paper, and the offline cost is nontrivial — each model-compressor-benchmark sequence requires ~1200–1300 paired full-KV and candidate-policy generations, which must be repeated for every (τ,ε) contract change (Table 3's sensitivity is computed from saved utilities, mitigating this only partially).

**Significance / red flags.** The framework certifies utility risk, not system performance: there are no latency, throughput, or memory-footprint measurements, so the "deployment" framing rests entirely on the retention-ratio proxy. The headline conclusion — "average benchmark performance does not guarantee reliability" — is important but unsurprising to statisticians, and the empirical base is narrow: two 7–8B models, two benchmark suites, one ττδδ configuration for the main results. The abstract's emphasis that "policies meeting the empirical calibration-risk target can still fail finite-sample certification" is technically true but somewhat rhetorical: those policies' TEST risks (4.00–5.33%) differ only marginally, and the paper itself concedes the plug-in-vs-certified comparison does not establish statistical significance.

## Strengths

- Correct and clean statistical construction: task-stratified calibration gives an exactly binomial count under the null, Hoeffding bounds handle heterogeneous strata, equality is conservatively kept in the null, and fixed-sequence testing controls false certification at δ without assuming monotone risk.
- The full-KV fallback is defined to satisfy the contract trivially, and the paired per-request protocol (same prompt, deterministic decoding) makes Δ_π well-defined and noise-free.
- Compressor-agnostic: demonstrated on fixed-ratio, layer-adaptive (DefensiveKV/Layer-DefensiveKV), and request-adaptive (ReFreeKV) allocation with one framework.
- Honest reporting throughout: a candid Limitations section, descriptive-vs-certified clearly separated, and a post-hoc simultaneous Clopper-Pearson + Bonferroni analysis that surfaces per-task risks exceeding 5% against the paper's own aggregate claim.
- Useful empirical mapping (Tables 1–3) of how the certified retention point moves with τ and ε, directly usable as a deployment tuning reference.

## Limitations

- The certificate applies only to the declared task-uniform population with independent per-task calibration draws — no per-task guarantees, no robustness to distribution shift, and no simultaneous guarantee across model-compressor-benchmark sequences (fresh calibration data would be needed for each).
- Only 7–8B models (Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B) and discrete, coarse candidate grids (0.05 retention steps; 10 ReFreeKV thresholds); operating points may not transfer to larger models or other architectures, and finer grids raise the cost of paired full-KV evaluation.
- The contract, candidate policies, and their order must be fixed before CAL outcomes are inspected; post-hoc contract selection (Table 3) is explicitly descriptive and would require multiplicity correction or fresh data to be valid prospectively.
- Degradation is relative to full KV, not absolute: a policy with 0% certified risk can still have terrible absolute utility (only 0.45–0.70 of cache retained), and τ=.10 has task-dependent semantic meaning on LongBench's mixed metrics (a 10-point ROUGE loss ≠ a 10-point accuracy loss).
- No system-level evaluation: latency, throughput, and real serving-stack benefits of certified retention levels are not measured; the offline cost of building task-stratified CAL sets (paired full-KV + candidate generations, ~1300 requests per setting) is a real adoption barrier.
- Risk is deflated on degenerate requests (253/1200 with zero full-KV utility can never count as material degradation), which the paper acknowledges but does not correct for.

## Relevance

这篇论文与你关注的 LLM 推理效率/长上下文 KV-cache 管理方向高度相关：它不提出新的驱逐算法，而是给所有现有驱逐方法（SnapKV、AdaKV、DefensiveKV 系、ReFreeKV）加了一个带 finite-sample 保证的校准外层，把"平均质量掉多少"换成"多少比例的请求会实质变差"，这正是把 conformal/risk-control 思路搬到 KV 内存管理上的代表作之一。若你在做长上下文部署或推理可靠性，文中"平均指标相同、尾部风险迥异"的实证和按 (τ,ε) 查保留率的 Table 1–3 都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How quickly does the certificate break under distribution shift between the declared CAL mixture (uniform over 12 tasks) and real production traffic where a few tasks dominate — and can task-weighted or adaptively re-stratified calibration restore coverage?
- Can the discrete 0.05-step retention grid be replaced by a continuous/anytime-valid search (e.g., confidence sequences) to avoid artifacts like SnapKV's full-KV fallback on RULER-32K, where all tested fractions had empirical risk below 5%?
- Is a τ=.10 normalized-metric drop a good proxy for semantic materiality across LongBench's heterogeneous metrics (ROUGE vs accuracy vs edit score)? A human-judgment-grounded definition of "material degradation" would strengthen the contract's meaning.
- Can per-task guarantees be obtained with stratified multiplicity control at acceptable calibration cost, given observed per-task risks of 10% (2WikiMQA, SAMSum) and 20–29% (TriviaQA, RULER CWE) under an aggregate certificate?
- What are the end-to-end latency/throughput savings at certified retention levels in a real serving stack, and how does the ~1300-request offline calibration cost amortize across model updates?
- Does the framework extend to larger models and modern attention architectures (GQA sharing, MLA, sliding-window + KV hybrid), where per-head eviction dynamics differ substantially from the 7–8B dense models tested?
