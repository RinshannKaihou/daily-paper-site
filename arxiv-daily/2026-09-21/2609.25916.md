> **一句话总结：** 富士通提出 CASA：把混合精度量化的位分配从"标量敏感度代理"升级为基于 Kronecker 分解 Hessian 的激活感知二次代价（并证明标量代理的最坏乘性失真上界为 √(κ(A)κ(B))，在 LLM 中可达 10¹–10¹³），第一阶段用 MCKP 求解激活感知位分配，第二阶段用跨层感知的局部搜索（位交换 + 校准 CE 损失验收）进一步精化；在 Llama-2-7B 到 Qwen3-14B 五个模型上，2.25 BPW 下 Llama-3-8B 的 WikiText-2 困惑度从 Q-Palette 的 47.20 降至 22.41，Qwen3-14B 零样本平均精度提升 +7.2 个百分点，且增益与理论失真界强相关。

> **TL;DR:** Mixed-precision LLM quantization is usually cast as a Multiple-Choice Knapsack Problem (MCKP) driven by a single scalar sensitivity per module (e.g., Q-Palette's HIGGS-based metric). The authors prove this scalar proxy has a worst-case multiplicative distortion of √(κ(A)κ(B)) versus the true activation-aware quadratic cost — empirically spanning 10¹–10¹³ across modules — so it systematically misranks modules. Their CASA allocator replaces the scalar with the full Kronecker-factored cost tr(BΔW A ΔWᵀ) in an MCKP (Stage 1, solved with SCIP), then refines with a cross-layer-aware bit-swap local search validated by calibration cross-entropy (Stage 2, provably never worse than Stage 1). On WikiText-2 at 2.25 BPW, CASA cuts Llama-3-8B perplexity from Q-Palette's 47.20 to 22.41 (Uniform: 256.28) and raises Qwen3-14B zero-shot accuracy from 44.5% to 51.7%; at 3.0 BPW the gain over Q-Palette tracks the per-model distortion bound (+1.1 ACC for Llama-2-7B at log₁₀D̄=7.73 vs +18.5 for Qwen3-8B at 10.86).

## 核心贡献

- **理论：标量代理的尖锐失真界。** 证明任何标量敏感度代理相对完整激活感知二次代价的最坏乘性失真恰为 √(κ(A)κ(B))（A、B 分别为输入/输出侧 Hessian 因子的条件数），且该界在 LLM 各模块上实测跨 10¹–10¹³ 个数量级（Theorem 3.1，inf-sup 意义下紧）。另证明代理排序错位可导致无界 MCKP 遗憾：Regret = (γ₁−γ₂)(d_L−d_H)，随精度比 R→∞ 发散（Proposition 3.2）。
- **统一视角。** 将 GPTQ/OBQ（input-only）、HIGGS/Q-Palette（scalar）等现有代理统一为 tr(BΔW A ΔWᵀ) 在 A、B 不同近似层级下的退化特例（Table 1），并给出连续松弛下的闭式"注水"式最优位分配：β_i−β_j = ½log₂(Γ_i/Γ_n)（Theorem 4.1）。
- **方法：两阶段 CASA。** Stage 1 用激活感知代价求解 MCKP（整数规划，SCIP）；Stage 2 在预算约束下做位交换（1 升 1 降）局部搜索，候选由 Cauchy–Schwarz 跨层代理预筛、以校准交叉熵验收，保证不劣于 Stage 1（Proposition 4.2）。附录 C 实证表明把跨层项直接折进单阶段 MCKP（McCormick 线性化）反而失败，论证了"跨层项必须在 MKP 框架外处理"的设计决策。
- **实证与理论闭环。** 五个模型（Llama-2-7B、Llama-3-8B、Llama-3.1-8B、Qwen3-8B、Qwen3-14B）× 多个位预算（2.25–4.0 BPW）上全面超越最新的标量代理方法 Q-Palette，且每模型增益与理论失真界 D 强相关（Table 3），把失真界从最坏情形的松散上界变成可用的实践指标。

## Key Contributions

- **Sharp impossibility for scalar proxies:** the worst-case multiplicative distortion of any scalar sensitivity against the activation-aware quadratic is exactly √(κ(A)κ(B)) (Theorem 3.1), and empirically this bound spans 10¹–10¹³ across modules of typical LLMs (e.g., Qwen3-8B ranges from ~10⁻⁰·⁸ for o_proj layer 2 to ~10¹³·² for gate_proj layer 1). A matched misranking construction shows proxy-driven MCKP regret (γ₁−γ₂)(d_L−d_H) is unbounded (Proposition 3.2).
- **CASA, a two-stage allocator:** Stage 1 solves an MCKP with the full Kronecker-factored per-module cost tr(BΔW A ΔWᵀ); Stage 2 performs a budget-neutral bit-swap local search (one upgrade + one downgrade per swap), screening candidates with a precomputable Cauchy–Schwarz cross-layer proxy and accepting on measured calibration CE loss — monotone improvement over Stage 1 guaranteed (Proposition 4.2).
- **Empirical validation across 5 LLMs and bit budgets 2.25–4.0 BPW:** CASA consistently beats the latest scalar-proxy method Q-Palette, with the largest gains at ultra-low bits (<3 BPW), and the per-model gain correlates with the theoretical distortion bound D — turning the bound into a practical predictor of when scalar proxies fail.
- **Honest negative results:** appendices C and D show that folding cross-terms into a single-stage MCKP (McCormick linearization) collapses to degenerate allocations, and cleanly separate the contributions of local search per se versus cross-layer awareness in the search.

## Methodology

**Problem setup.** Mixed-precision weight-only PTQ: each module (l, m) with m ∈ {q, k, v, o, gate, up, down} is assigned one candidate bit-width q ∈ {2, ..., 8} under an average bit-width (BPW) budget, minimizing total quantization-induced loss. Standard formulation: MCKP.

**Derivation of the activation-aware proxy (Section 3).** From a second-order Taylor expansion at the pre-trained optimum (gradient ≈ 0), a layer-wise block-diagonal Hessian approximation, and empirical-Fisher estimates from calibration data, the per-module loss is ΔL^(l) ≈ ½ tr(B^(l) ΔW^(l) A^(l) ΔW^(l)ᵀ), where A = ¼XXᵀ is the input activation Gram matrix and B is the output-side Hessian (expected per-output-channel Fisher). Existing proxies are shown to be degenerate cases: GPTQ/OBQ drop B→I; HIGGS and Q-Palette further collapse A→α_I and B→I into a single scalar α_II‖ΔW‖²/‖W‖². The scalar proxy's condition-number distortion (Theorem 3.1) and the resulting misranking regret (Proposition 3.2) motivate keeping the full quadratic.

**Stage 1: activation-aware MCKP (Section 4.1).** For every (module, candidate bit-width) pair the perturbation ΔW^(l,m,q) is precomputed and the cost ℓ = tr(B ΔW A ΔWᵀ) computed element-wise under diagonal approximations of A and B (ℓ = Σ_ij b_ij a_jj (Δw_ij)²). The binary program (assignment constraints + BPW budget) is solved exactly with SCIP. A continuous relaxation analysis (Theorem 4.1) yields a closed-form water-filling solution where the optimal bit-width difference between two modules is ½log₂ of their sensitivity ratio — high-rate intuition underlying the integer solution.

**Stage 2: cross-layer-aware local search (Section 4.2).** The block-diagonal Hessian assumption is relaxed to block-tridiagonal: off-diagonal Fisher blocks between adjacent layers (l, l+1) for the same module type are kept, Kronecker-factorized, and reduced to a signed element-wise cross-term whose magnitude is bounded via Cauchy–Schwarz into a precomputable coefficient C^(l,m)_(q,q') depending only on the Stage-1 diagonals (no extra calibration pass). Local search rounds: (1) generate feasible swaps (k=1 upgrades paired with downgrades); (2) rank by the cross-term delta, keep top-K=100 with negative delta; (3) evaluate each with actual next-token calibration loss and accept the best if it improves; terminate when no candidate improves. Up to 20 rounds; k=1 pairing keeps the budget exactly satisfied. Proposition 4.2 guarantees the accepted result is never worse than the Stage-1 optimum under the quadratic surrogate.

**Experimental setup.** Models: Llama-2-7B, Llama-3-8B, Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-14B. Quantizer: GPTQ (group size 128) for all methods, RTN for proxy computation; C4 calibration (128 samples, seq len 256). Baselines: Uniform (reference), Q-Palette (Lee & Song 2025; scalar HIGGS sensitivity, with its condensed quantizers replaced by GPTQ for comparability), CASA_self (Stage-1-only ablation). Single NVIDIA B200 GPU. Evaluation: WikiText-2 perplexity + 6-task zero-shot average accuracy (common-sense suite).

## Results & Highlights

- **Large perplexity reductions at ultra-low bits.** On WikiText-2 at 2.25 BPW, Llama-3-8B: CASA 22.41 PPL vs Q-Palette 47.20 vs Uniform 256.28 — more than a 2× perplexity reduction over the strongest scalar-proxy baseline; zero-shot accuracy 39.2% vs 34.0% (Uniform 29.8%). Llama-3.1-8B at 2.25 BPW: 20.56 vs Q-Palette's 40.67 (39.6% vs 34.3% ACC); Qwen3-8B: 13.63 vs 20.19 (40.8% vs 34.3%).
- **Consistent accuracy gains at 2.25 BPW.** Qwen3-14B: CASA 51.7% ACC vs Q-Palette 44.5% (+7.2 pts, PPL 9.75 vs 10.79); Llama-2-7B: 42.7% vs 42.4% (PPL 11.19 vs 11.59). Gains shrink at higher budgets — at 4.0 BPW Llama-2-7B the gap is 4.98 vs 5.03 PPL and Qwen3-14B 7.78 vs 8.05 — confirming the method matters most below ~3 BPW.
- **Distortion bound predicts where scalar proxies fail (Table 3, 3.0 BPW, CASA_self vs Q-Palette).** Accuracy gain grows with the theoretical bound: Llama-2-7B (log₁₀D̄ = 7.73) gains +1.1 pts (55.0→56.1), Llama-3-8B (8.30) +2.7, Llama-3.1-8B (7.96) +2.5, Qwen3-8B (10.86) +18.5 (41.7→60.2), Qwen3-14B (9.48) +15.2 (50.4→65.6) — the abstract's "zero-shot accuracy tracks the per-model distortion bound" claim.
- **Cross-layer local search is the differentiator (Appendix D).** On Llama-3-8B at 2.25 BPW, adding a self-term-only local search drops PPL 35.75→28.15; the cross-term-aware search drops it further to 22.41 (a 20% relative reduction). Across 15 (model, BPW) configurations, local search improves Stage-1-only in 14/15, and cross-aware search beats self-only search in 13/15, with the largest gaps in the 2.25–2.75 BPW regime.
- **Bit-allocation behavior matches the theory (Figure 2).** On Llama-3-8B at 2.5 BPW, V-projection input Gram matrices in layers 0–4 have κ(A) ∈ [2.4×10³, 1.5×10⁵]; CASA allocates 5–8 bits there (peak 8 at layer 2) yielding PPL 11.86, while Q-Palette caps at 7 bits and drops to 5 bits on layers 2–4 (vs CASA's 8/7/6), yielding 21.43 — a concrete case where the activation-aware metric flags early-layer outlier modules that the scalar metric under-precisions.

## Critical Assessment

- **The main baseline is a modified Q-Palette, not the published one.** The authors replace Q-Palette's original condensed quantizers with GPTQ "for comparability." This is defensible for isolating the allocation variable, but it means the headline deltas are against a re-engineered competitor; the published Q-Palette numbers (and its fractional-bit quantizers) are never shown. HIGGS itself — the NAACL 2025 method whose linearity theorem underpins the scalar metric being attacked — is conspicuously absent as a standalone baseline, as are AWQ/SmoothQuant-style alternatives.
- **Scale is modest for the claim being made.** All results are on 7B–14B dense models. The misranking pathology the theory predicts should be worse at 70B+, but no such model is tested, and the distortion-vs-gain correlation in Table 3 rests on only 5 points confounded by model family (both Qwen3 models sit at the high-D/high-gain end; only the Llama trio provides within-family spread).
- **The Stage-2 search has a cost the baselines don't pay.** Each round runs up to 100 full calibration-loss forward passes, for up to 20 rounds, per model per bit budget. No wall-clock or calibration-compute comparison against Q-Palette is reported, so the practical overhead of the "refinement" is invisible; at deployment scale this could matter as much as the PPL delta.
- **Gains are concentrated in a narrow regime.** At 4.0 BPW the improvements over Q-Palette shrink to 0.05–0.3 PPL, and CASA is not uniformly best per cell (e.g., Llama-2-7B at 2.75 BPW, Q-Palette hits 52.9% ACC vs CASA 52.5%). The paper is honest about the ultra-low-bit focus in the abstract, but tables show the method is near-irrelevant above ~3.5 BPW.
- **Notable rigor positives:** the authors report their own failed single-stage MCKP design (Appendix C: McCormick-linearized bilinear MCKP collapses to degenerate near-uniform allocations under geo_abs and merely matches Stage 1 under raw_abs), and Appendix D cleanly factors out "local search helps" from "cross-term awareness in the search helps." This is more ablation honesty than typical for the area. Reproducibility is currently limited: no code release is mentioned anywhere in the preprint.

## Strengths

- Real theory, not decoration: Theorem 3.1 gives the exact worst-case constant √(κ(A)κ(B)) in inf-sup form, and the paper then *measures* that quantity per module across 5 models (Figure 3 heatmaps) and links it to end-task gains (Table 3) — a rare closed theory→prediction→verification loop in the PTQ literature.
- Table 1's unified treatment of GPTQ/OBQ/HIGGS/Q-Palette as approximations of tr(BΔW A ΔWᵀ) is genuinely clarifying and gives the field a vocabulary for why scalar metrics fail.
- The two-stage decomposition is principled: Stage 2's monotone-improvement guarantee (Prop 4.2) plus the empirical finding that cross-layer effects must be handled *outside* the MCKP (Appendix C) is a useful design lesson for future allocators.
- Broad, well-organized evaluation: 5 models × 7 bit budgets × PPL + 6-task accuracy, with two dedicated ablation appendices isolating each component.

## Limitations

- The cross-term approximation covers only adjacent layer pairs (l, l+1) of the same module type (block-tridiagonal); longer-range and higher-order interactions are ignored, and the authors state that modeling them faithfully would make calibration and optimization combinatorially intractable.
- The acceptance objective is a quadratic activation-aware proxy plus calibration CE on C4 (128 samples, seq len 256) — not the true downstream loss; the authors concede the proxy ranking can diverge from true perplexity in extreme regimes.
- No 70B-class models, no MoE models, no KV-cache/activation quantization (weight-only only), and no end-to-end latency or memory measurements — "quality per BPW" is the sole axis.
- Stage-2 compute overhead (100 candidate evaluations/round × ≤20 rounds) is unreported, and no code release is provided in this version.

## Relevance

这篇论文直击 LLM 后训练量化中"位分配该给谁"的核心问题，用可测量的理论界（√(κ(A)κ(B))）解释了 HIGGS/Q-Palette 等标量敏感度方法在超低位宽下的失效机制，对关注模型压缩、边缘部署与高效推理的读者非常相关。两阶段"整数规划 + 跨层局部搜索"的框架也可迁移到剪枝/低秩分配等资源受限优化场景。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the √(κ(A)κ(B)) distortion-vs-gain relationship hold at 70B+ scale and for MoE architectures, where expert routing may further entangle cross-layer sensitivity?
- How sensitive is Stage 2's calibration-loss acceptance to the calibration corpus (C4 vs. domain-matched data, 128 vs. 1024 samples), and what is the actual wall-clock overhead versus Q-Palette end-to-end?
- The distortion bound is a worst-case quantity — can a median- or distribution-aware version of D be derived that predicts gains more tightly than the arithmetic-mean log₁₀D̄ used in Table 3?
- Would replacing GPTQ with modern condensed quantizers (as in the original Q-Palette) inside CASA's allocation compound the gains, or does the allocation advantage wash out under a stronger base quantizer?
- Can the adjacent-layer, same-module-type restriction in the cross-term be relaxed to multi-hop layer pairs via low-rank Fisher blocks without losing the tractability that motivated it?
