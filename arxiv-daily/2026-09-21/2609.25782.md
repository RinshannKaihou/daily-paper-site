> **一句话总结：** 针对 agentic LLM 服务中"暂停会话的 KV 必须常驻 HBM 否则恢复代价高昂"的矛盾，作者在共封装 HBM+高带宽闪存（HBF）基板上提出冷热分层：热集（正在解码的 KV）驻留 HBM、冷池（暂停会话）下沉 HBF，并利用 KV 不可变性设计 write-on-evict 写策略（WAF≈1.02）；在 Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B + SWE-bench Verified agent 轨迹的 trace 驱动仿真中，恢复延迟仅 +0.084 ms（对比 NVLink-C2C 主存卸载 1.0 ms、PCIe 5.0 为 7 ms），单卡并发会话从 165 扩展到 3,900，且较"全 KV 放闪存"方案每 8-GPU 节点节省约 7.6 kW 读功耗。

> **TL;DR:** This 4-page IEEE CAL letter observes that agentic LLM serving (mini-swe-agent on SWE-bench Verified traces, Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B: 56.8 GiB weights, 96 KiB KV/token) produces a bimodal KV access pattern — at 48 concurrent sessions the hot set is only 3% of resident KV capacity yet draws ~98% of read traffic. It therefore tiers KV between HBM (hot set) and a 3 TiB High Bandwidth Flash (HBF) cold pool on a co-packaged substrate, with a write-on-evict policy enabled by KV immutability. Against the all-KV-offloaded-to-flash baseline (PCIe 5.0 at 64 GB/s or NVLink-C2C at 450 GB/s), tiering adds only 0.084 ms to resume-turn TBT where the interconnect baselines add 1.0–7 ms and recompute-on-resume adds ~14 ms; capacity rises from 165 to 3,900 concurrent sessions per GPU, and read power drops ~950 W per HBF device (~7.6 kW per 8-GPU node at 20 pJ/bit), all while holding 14 ms TBT (72 tok/s/agent) inside a 50 ms SLO.

## 核心贡献

- **负载特征刻画（双峰访问）：** 实证分析发现 agentic KV 状态的访问模式截然不同——小的热集（当前解码批次的 KV）每个解码步都被读取，大的冷池（暂停会话的 KV）仅在会话恢复时读取。在 N_a=48 时，热集仅占驻留容量的 3%，却贡献约 98% 的读流量，平均每会话驻留足迹 0.82 GiB（单条轨迹峰值约 16.7k token、1.53 GiB KV）。
- **共封装 HBM+HBF 分层架构：** 复用此前工作（Hidual [8]、HFAccel [14]、[15]）提出的共封装基板，在 HBM base die 中融合两级存储：地址路由器 + staging buffer 双缓冲，使热步流量走 GPU–HBM 总线，冷池的逐出/恢复流量走 HBM–HBF D2D 链路，NAND 的 25 μs 读取延迟被 D2D 传输和与 CPU 侧 tokenization 的重叠所吸收，HBF 完全离开关键路径。
- **write-on-evict 写策略：** 利用 KV cache 不可变（append-only、按 erase block 对齐追加）的特性，KV 块仅在从 HBM 逐出时写入 HBF 且至多写一次（再读回后副本仍有效），WAF≈1.02（对比通用 SSD 的 2–4），把 HBF 写流量严格限制在逐出与恢复两类事件上；配合 LRU + 对活跃块硬钉（hard-pin）保证解码批次常驻 HBM。
- **量化评估：** 基于 SWE-bench Verified（mini-swe-agent）真实轨迹 + Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B 的 trace 驱动仿真表明：恢复延迟仅 +0.084 ms（NVLink-C2C 为 1.0 ms、PCIe 5.0 为 7 ms），并发会话 165 → 3,900，8-GPU 节点读功耗较全闪存方案节省约 7.6 kW（950 W/器件 @20 pJ/bit），HBF 寿命约 20 年——结论是 HBF 应作为 HBM 的冷层补充而非替代。

## Key Contributions

- **Bimodal access characterization:** Agentic KV traffic splits into a small hot set read at every decode step and a large cold pool read only on session resume; at N_a = 48 concurrent sessions the hot set occupies 3% of resident capacity while drawing ~98% of read traffic.
- **Hot-cold tiering on a co-packaged HBM+HBF substrate:** The HBM base die fuses both tiers behind an address router and staging buffers; hot-step traffic stays on the GPU–HBM bus while eviction/resume traffic uses the D2D link, with double-buffering hiding NAND latency and resume prefetch overlapped with CPU-side tokenization so HBF never sits on the critical path.
- **Write-on-evict policy:** Exploiting KV immutability (erase-block-aligned appends), each block is written to HBF at most once, on first eviction — WAF ≈ 1.02 vs 2–4 for general-purpose SSDs — confining HBF writes strictly to eviction and resume events; LRU with hard-pinned active blocks degrades gracefully by spilling under extreme pressure rather than deadlocking.
- **Trace-based evaluation:** On mini-swe-agent/SWE-bench Verified trajectories with Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B on a simulated B200 (192 GiB HBM3e @ 8 TB/s), the design adds 0.084 ms resume overhead (vs 1.0 ms NVLink-C2C and 7 ms PCIe 5.0), scales from 165 to 3,900 concurrent sessions, saves ~7.6 kW read power per 8-GPU node vs all-KV-to-flash, and reaches ~20-year HBF lifetime — establishing HBF as a cold-tier complement to HBM, not its replacement.

## Methodology

**Workload.** Trajectories are collected with mini-swe-agent on SWE-bench Verified (real software-engineering tasks). The served model is Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B, a Mixture-of-Experts model with 56.8 GiB BF16 weights and 96 KiB of KV per token. Agent trajectories grow to a peak context of ~16.7k tokens (1.53 GiB KV); the average resident footprint is 0.82 GiB per session. Because public agent traces are scarce, the workload is scaled by replaying measured trajectories with independent arrival phases on a saturated plateau; each 1.5 MiB KV block is tracked (this granularity amortizes flash page-granular reads without coarsening eviction too much). Tool/user gaps follow a heavy-tailed distribution (median ~8 s, mean ~23 s).

**Simulator.** An in-house, trace-driven simulator with analytical latency and power models models a B200 GPU node: 192 GiB HBM3e at 8 TB/s and 2.25 PFLOP/s of compute, plus an HBF tier of 3 TiB SLC whose bandwidth matches HBM, rated at 100,000 program/erase cycles and 25 μs read latency. HBF read energy is unstandardized, so it is parameterized in a sweep from 8 to 30 pJ/bit against HBM's ≈3.5 pJ/bit. The batching scheduler keeps N_a sessions decoding at all times (swept 8→128). The complement of expert weights is retained in HBM to remove MoE routing-induced latency variance.

**Architecture and data paths.** The design adopts the co-packaged HBM+HBF substrate of prior work ([8], [14], [15]) and organizes it around four paths: (1) decode reads model weights and active KV from HBM; (2) on resume, HBF-resident KV is fetched into HBM over the D2D link, with sub-array parallelism amortizing NAND latency and the fetch overlapped with pre-turn CPU tokenization; (3) freshly generated KV is written back into HBM; (4) under capacity pressure the coldest idle KV is evicted from HBM into HBF over the same D2D link. The base-die router and staging buffers double-buffer inter-tier traffic so evictions do not stall the GPU and NAND latency hides behind D2D transfer; the GPU addresses HBM directly, HBF exclusively via the D2D link.

**Policies.** Eviction is LRU at 1.5 MiB block granularity, with blocks of actively decoding sessions hard-pinned to HBM (recency alone would mostly suffice since active blocks are re-read every step; pinning prevents a race where the active set is evicted, with graceful spilling as fallback). Write-on-evict exploits the immutability of paged KV caches: a block evicted to HBF keeps its copy valid when re-admitted to HBM, so it is written to HBF at most once, keeping WAF ≈ 1.02. In-session KV compression/rewrite policies are explicitly out of scope.

**Baselines and metrics.** Baselines: (a) All-KV-to-flash — all KV offloaded to host memory over PCIe 5.0 (64 GB/s) or NVLink-C2C (450 GB/s); (b) recompute-on-resume (re-prefill of evicted blocks). Metrics: per-agent resumed-turn TBT and resume hit rate (fraction of a resuming session's KV already in HBM), aggregate decode throughput, HBF lifetime (T_life = C·N_PEP / (R_W·WAF)), and memory read power (read rate × per-bit energy per tier).

## Results & Highlights

- **Session capacity (SWE-bench Verified traces, Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B, B200 node):** HBM-only serving saturates at 165 concurrent sessions; adding the 3 TiB HBF cold tier sustains 3,900 sessions (~24×), because the hot set needs only ~3% of resident capacity. (Note: the abstract instead claims "2.4× more concurrent sessions per GPU" — see Critical Assessment.)
- **Resume-turn latency at N_a = 48:** tiering adds only 0.084 ms over prefill, vs 1.0 ms for NVLink-C2C (450 GB/s) host offload and 7 ms for PCIe 5.0 (64 GB/s) — roughly 12× lower than the strongest interconnect baseline. Recompute-on-resume is worse still: ~14 ms of GPU re-prefill at N_a = 48 and more than double the turn cost at N_a = 128.
- **TBT vs SLO:** 14 ms per token (72 tok/s/agent) at N_a = 48 and 27 ms (57 tok/s) at N_a = 128, both safely within the 50 ms TBT budget; when the hot set resides in the backing tier instead of HBM (PCIe 5.0 or NVLink-C2C), the interconnect cannot sustain hot-set bandwidth and SLOs are violated.
- **Bimodality (Fig. 3):** at N_a = 48 the hot set occupies 3% of resident KV capacity but draws ~98% of read traffic; the resume hit rate falls from 1.00 (N_a = 16) through 0.47 (48) to ≈0.00 (128), yet resume latency stays near zero because cold fetches run as pure I/O off the compute path.
- **Read power (Fig. 5d):** the tiered design's read rate is 6 GB/s at N_a = 16 → 38 GB/s at N_a = 128; at HBF energy of 20 pJ/bit (vs HBM ≈3.5 pJ/bit), write-on-evict saves ~950 W per HBF device — ~7.6 kW per 8-GPU node — versus all-KV-to-flash, with savings spanning 2.1–12.2 kW across the 8–30 pJ/bit sensitivity sweep.
- **Endurance (Fig. 5c):** write-on-evict yields ~20-year HBF lifetime at N_a = 48 vs ~11 years for all-to-flash at the same load (all-to-flash falls to ~8 years at N_a = 128); below N_a = 32 nothing is ever evicted and lifetime is effectively unbounded. WAF is ≈1.02 thanks to immutable, erase-block-aligned KV appends (vs 2–4 for general-purpose SSDs).

## Authors & Credibility

Jongjin Baek, Won Ji, Seungjae Yoo, and Joo-Young Kim submit this to IEEE Computer Architecture Letters (2026). The first author has a closely related HPCA 2026 paper on high-bandwidth flash for LLM inference (reference [10] of the letter), so the group has cumulative expertise in the HBF design space rather than a one-off claim. The paper is well-grounded in the emerging literature: it builds on the co-packaged HBM+HBF substrate from Hidual [8] and HFAccel [14], engages the endurance analysis of [6], and cites production serving infrastructure (vLLM, SGLang-style batching, SWE-bench/mini-swe-agent). All quantitative claims are simulation-based with stated assumptions (B200 specs, HBF SLC parameters, pJ/bit sweep), which is honest but means no silicon validates the latency-hiding behavior yet.

## Critical Assessment

- **Internal inconsistency in the headline capacity claim:** the abstract claims "2.4× more concurrent sessions per GPU," but the evaluation text reports 165 → 3,900 sessions (~24×). Neither section explains what baseline the 2.4× refers to; a reader cannot reconcile the two numbers from the letter alone. This is the paper's most visible sloppiness.
- **No hardware exists:** HBF with HBM-matching bandwidth at 3 TiB per stack, 25 μs read latency, and 100k P/E cycles is a projected device. The entire evaluation is a trace-driven simulator with analytical latency/power models, and the co-packaged base-die router/staging-buffer mechanism that hides NAND latency is adopted from prior proposals [8], [14], never demonstrated. If real HBF bandwidth falls short of HBM, the eviction-absorption argument and the overlap window both weaken.
- **Latency hiding leans on slow tools:** the ≤0.1 ms resume overhead is absorbed by overlapping the HBF fetch with CPU-side tokenization inside an ~8 s median tool gap. Agentic frameworks moving to parallel tool calls, streaming tool outputs, or sub-second tools shrink this window; the letter never stress-tests the resume path under short gaps.
- **Missing intermediate tier:** the natural industrial competitor — a CXL-attached or simply larger host-DRAM tier between HBM and HBF — is absent from the baseline set (only PCIe 5.0/NVLink-C2C offload to host memory is modeled, at fixed 64/450 GB/s). There is also no cost-per-session analysis for a novel co-packaged substrate vs adding DRAM, which is the question a deployer actually asks.
- **Narrow workload and unexamined knobs:** one model (Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B) and one benchmark family (SWE-bench coding agents); the 1.5 MiB block size is asserted ("smaller wastes sub-page reads, larger coarsens eviction") but never swept, and LRU-plus-pinning is not compared against any alternative policy. The MoE expert-complement trick is workload-specific.
- **Immutability assumption does real work:** WAF ≈ 1.02, the at-most-once HBF write, and the clean endurance story all depend on append-only KV. KV quantization, in-session compression, or prefix-cache rewrites — all active research directions — would break the policy, and the letter excludes them by fiat.
- On the positive side, the bimodality measurement (3% capacity / 98% reads) is a genuine, checkable insight, and the power analysis is unusually self-critical: it demonstrates that sourcing the hot set from HBF would burn ~950 W per device, honestly framing HBF as a complement rather than overselling it.

## Strengths

- The design is driven by a concrete characterization (bimodal access: 3% capacity, ~98% read traffic at N_a = 48) rather than a generic "flash is big" argument, and the tiering policy follows directly from it.
- Write policy and endurance are co-designed with KV immutability: WAF ≈ 1.02, ~20-year lifetime at N_a = 48, and an explicit per-N_a lifetime analysis — flash endurance is treated quantitatively, not hand-waved.
- Evaluation isolates mechanisms: the resume-hit-rate sweep (Fig. 4) separates tiering benefit from interconnect effects; TBT, throughput, endurance, and power are each traced to their governing factor (N_a).
- Honest framing of the technology's role: the power analysis itself shows hot-set-from-HBF is impractical, and the conclusion positions HBF as a cold-tier complement to HBM.
- User-visible SLO (50 ms TBT) is the primary latency metric, keeping results relevant to serving practice.

## Limitations

- Simulation-only: no prototype of the base-die router/staging buffers, and all results inherit the assumed HBF specs (3 TiB SLC, HBM-matched bandwidth, 25 μs latency, 100k P/E cycles).
- Single model and single agent benchmark; dependence on heavy-tailed tool latency (median ~8 s) for hiding resume fetches is not analyzed as a sensitivity.
- Fixed 1.5 MiB block granularity with no sweep; LRU + hard-pinning un-compared against alternative eviction policies.
- No CXL/DRAM-expansion baseline and no hardware cost model, so the capacity-per-dollar case vs simply adding host memory is unquantified.
- Abstract's "2.4×" sessions claim conflicts with the 165 → 3,900 (~24×) figure in the evaluation text.
- The immutability assumption excludes KV compression/quantization, so the WAF ≈ 1.02 and write-once guarantees do not transfer to cache-management schemes that rewrite entries.
- Four-page CAL format leaves thin treatment of WAF derivation, arrival-process assumptions (saturated replay vs bursty arrivals), and prefill-phase interference.

## Relevance

该文与用户对 LLM 推理系统与 agent 基础设施的兴趣直接相关：它把 agentic 多轮会话"长时间暂停但必须保留全量上下文"的 KV 驻留难题转化为 HBM + 高带宽闪存的冷热分层架构问题，并用 SWE-bench agent 真实轨迹给出了容量、恢复延迟、功耗与闪存寿命的量化权衡，可作为长上下文 agent 服务平台做内存层级选型时的参考依据。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does the resume hit rate and the ≤0.1 ms resume overhead behave under bursty session arrivals rather than saturated steady-state replay, and how quickly does the system recover after a traffic spike evicts most of the cold pool?
- Would a CXL-attached DRAM tier (or simply more host memory at 64–450 GB/s) placed between HBM and HBF capture most of the capacity benefit at lower hardware cost than a novel co-packaged substrate?
- How sensitive are the results to block size (the 1.5 MiB granularity is asserted, not swept) and to KV-per-token differences across attention architectures (GQA/MLA vs Qwen3's 96 KiB/token)?
- What happens when tool calls return in milliseconds — parallel tool use, fast verifiers, streaming agents — shrinking the tokenization window that currently hides HBF resume fetches entirely off the critical path?
- How would write-on-evict interact with KV quantization or in-session compression, which break the immutability assumption underlying WAF ≈ 1.02 and the write-at-most-once guarantee?
- Can the 165 → 3,900 session scaling be reproduced on real serving stacks (vLLM/SGLang) once HBF-class devices ship, given the letter's simulator assumes idealized overlap between D2D transfer and NAND latency?
