02 /Why This Matters

Near-zero-cost, plug-and-play soft-error detection for deployed CNNs — a practical hardening reference for aerospace, deep space, and autonomous driving.

近零开销、即插即用的 CNN 软错误检测方案；“用辅助小网络监控 logit 类间结构”的思路对运行时错误与分布漂移检测亦有借鉴价值。

03 /Critical Edge

Never benchmarked against trivial confidence-threshold detectors (max-softmax, logit entropy) — so the learned class-relation model’s necessity remains unproven.

Also unquantified: the ~7% false-alarm rate on correct inferences, and the reload/re-infer cost it triggers.

04 /Methodology

S1

Watch the logits, touch nothing

The target CNN stays a black box: no retraining, no operator changes. A small FC “supervising ANN” reads only last-layer logits — 1000→250→100→1 for ImageNet models, 10→100→80→60→40→20→1 for CIFAR-10 (input expanded first, which helped low-dim cases) — and emits one sigmoid verdict at threshold 0.5.

S2

Formalize “correct” as a class relation

Mean-logit centroids plus their pairwise similarities define a relation R; a classification is correct iff z(x) ∈ R. Since R is analytically intractable, the ANN learns an approximation R* by regression — z(x) ∉ R* means error.

S3

Statistically sized fault-injection data

A custom injector flips one random bit of a Q6.10 16-bit parameter (90% weights / 10% biases); outcomes split 50% correct / 25% silent fault / 25% misclassification. Campaigns sized by the Cochran formula (99% confidence, worst margin <2%): 800k samples per ImageNet model, 17.2k per CIFAR-10 model.

S4

Train once, stress everywhere

Identical hyperparameters for all five backbones: 10-fold CV, SGD (lr 0.01), 50 epochs. Robustness sweep: 13 BERs from 1e-8 to 1e-2, 100 simulations each, with a detect → reload → re-infer policy.

Generalization: ESupNNet detects multi-bit upsets (MBU) despite training exclusively on single-bit SEU data — ResNet-50 protected to BER ≈1e-4, EfficientNetV2-S to ≈1e-6.

Strengths

  • Truly non-intrusive: target CNN untouched, deployable with already-validated torchvision models.
  • Negligible overhead (0.32–1.13%) vs ~200% TMR / ~31% NBS, orthogonal to other protections.
  • Strong statistical hygiene: Cochran-sized fault campaigns (<2% worst margin), K=10 CV with std devs, 13 BER × 100 runs.
  • Five architectures spanning an order of magnitude in size, two datasets, one shared hyperparameter set.
  • Clean generalization: MBU errors detected from SEU-only training.
  • Full metric transparency: TPR/FNR/FPR/precision/F1/F2/AUROC + ROC curves.

Limitations

  • Detection only, no correction: reload-and-re-infer cost is assumed, never measured — and ~7% FPR triggers it often.
  • Parameter-only fault model under 16-bit Q6.10 fixed point; activation errors, FP16/INT8 formats out of scope.
  • Low-dim logits degrade performance; MobileNetV3 falls below 90%.
  • Overhead quantified in parameters only — no latency or energy numbers on any real platform.
  • Cross-work comparisons mix datasets and fault settings; Table VII anomalies (protected > baseline) go unexplained.
  • Image-classification CNNs only; no transformers, no code release.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. How does ESupNNet compare against trivial MSP / logit-entropy thresholds on the same fault-injected data — is the learned class relation actually necessary?
  2. What is the end-to-end cost of the ~7% false-positive rate (reload latency, energy, throughput) on an embedded/FPGA platform, and what threshold best trades FPR vs FNR?
  3. Does the fault model hold under realistic numeric formats (FP16, INT8), and do bit positions within the 16-bit word change detection difficulty?
  4. Would MBU patterns (or internal-neuron activations) in training close the BER-robustness gap between ResNet-50 (1e-4) and EfficientNetV2-S (1e-6)?
  5. Can the approach extend to transformers, where the “class relation” lives in a far larger vocabulary space — or to distribution-shift misclassifications rather than hardware faults?
