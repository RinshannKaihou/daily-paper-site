01 Methodology

From declared claim to four-state verdict

  1. Declare. Each claim pairs prose with a formal target atom via an untrusted human-signed binding; evidence leaves carry kind + provenance.
  2. Gate. Strict steps delegate to four registered backends (nd, ra, ord, insp) with replayable certificates; defeasible steps must discharge mandatory critical questions (randomized? adequate_power?) or the program is rejected.
  3. Attack. Typed attacks are positional: rebut → conclusion, undercut → rule, undermine → leaf. Strict steps are immune to rebut/undercut.
  4. Compile. Accepted programs become Dung argumentation frameworks; grounded semantics (unique extension, polynomial, skeptical) labels them.
  5. Verdict + bridges. justified / defeated / contested / gap — gap records absent support before labelling, a state three-state systems collapse. Declared “bridges” transport checked support across papers, decidable via diamond/box modal queries.

Trust architecture — proof-carrying-code style: an untrusted producer’s output is checked against a small trusted base; verdicts pin policy/registry versions and artifact digests for byte-exact replay.

03 Strengths

What holds up

  • Real formal guarantees: sorry-free Lean 4 at scale (~117k lines, 34/35 results, only standard axioms), every public theorem CI-audited.
  • A crisp, previously-collapsed distinction — missing support (gap) vs defeated support — maps directly onto what a reviewer must actually do.
  • Non-factivity done right: certified arithmetic stays justified while the empirical claim above it falls; the checker’s epistemic boundary is visible in the verdict.
  • Evaluation discipline rare for language papers: differential testing vs an independent mechanized interpreter, 541-mutant suites, disjoint ablations, pinned revisions.
  • One calculus spans empirical review, a measurement-free philosophy debate, and axiom withdrawal — no domain-specific kernel rules.

04 Limitations

Where it gives way

  • A valid certificate can sit on an unfaithful formalization: bindings, policy, and bridge applicability are judgments outside the guarantees; ARA→LARA lowering inherits an LLM-mediated step.
  • Strict-chain well-formedness excludes some conflicts — certain defensible arguments cannot even be expressed.
  • Corpus study: single machine-annotation pass, human review pending, κ = 0.73 on only 18/60 claim types, no sampling frame — no external validity.
  • Timings cover ≤2-node frameworks; literature-scale bridged networks are untested, with NP-complete credulous acceptance lurking behind richer queries.
  • No bridge discovery, no untrusted-producer lowering beyond the deterministic path, no comparison with real human reviewer behavior.
  • Surface syntax is only “far enough” — whether LLM agents can reliably emit valid LARA is open: the adoption risk for the whole agenda. And it is a v1 preprint.

05 Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Can LLM agents reliably emit well-formed LARA directly — and does authoring-time checking beat post-hoc lowering of finished papers?
  2. Can bridge discovery across a literature be automated and audited, or is the modal network capped by hand-declared comparisons?
  3. How do grounded checking and four-state aggregation behave at literature scale, given the query bounds and the NP-complete variant?
  4. Would the guarantees survive adversarial producers who game trusted policies, signatures, or contraries?
  5. Do rendered verdicts align with what human reviewers actually flag — the deferred empirical study?
  6. Is the untrusted prose→formal binding boundary stable in practice, or does unfaithful formalization become the dominant failure mode?
