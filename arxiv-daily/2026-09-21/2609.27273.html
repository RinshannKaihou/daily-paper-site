Why this matters

Shifts agent evaluation from “can it finish the task” to “can it hold the user’s goal when the platform has interests of its own” — a rare controlled-causal study in agent safety.

⚠ Critical edge

Methodology · distilled
  1. Formalize the setting. The environment maximizes its own objective V alongside the user’s U; it is incentive-misaligned when advancing V can push the outcome outside the user-optimal set X*. Contrast with prompt injection: in CAVEAT no fact is ever false.
  2. Build the benchmark. Nine browser marketplaces (lodging, retail, food delivery, resale, freelance, grocery, specialty), each task engineered with a unique, programmatically verifiable optimum. 8 steering families from FTC / UK CMA / OECD taxonomies: sponsored placement · preferential ranking · drip pricing · promotional framing · defaults & bundling · scarcity & social proof · trust signals · friction & obstruction
  3. Matched-control protocol. Same request, catalog, prices, optimum — only the mechanisms toggle. Standard: 52 tasks, ~70 products each. Hard: 5 tasks, 2,112 products across 88 result pages.
  4. Evaluate. 18 model configurations on a fixed BrowserUse harness; 3 repetitions per task (10 on Hard); 95% Wilson confidence intervals.
  5. Intervene two ways. CAVEAT-Harness: pinned structured task spec before browsing + optional pre-purchase verification tool, using no hidden answers. CAVEAT-27B: two-stage post-training of Qwen3.5-27B with all CAVEAT environments excluded from training.

Strengths
  • Clean causal design: bit-identical decision problem, toggled incentives, verifiable unique optimum — the −61.3-pt drop cannot be task difficulty.
  • Novel, realistic setting between cooperative benchmarks and prompt-injection suites: true facts, biased presentation — how platforms actually behave.
  • Falsifiable diagnosis: each failure mode backed by a purpose-built ablation (25/25 flips; 0/60→48/60; 35/60), not anecdotes.
  • Honest cost accounting (2.6× runtime) and explicit scoping; both an inference-time and a post-training route shown, with contamination excluded by design.
  • Breadth: 18 configurations, 5 families, 3 settings, Wilson CIs; taxonomy grounded in FTC/CMA/OECD sources.
Limitations
  • Synthetic ecology: all nine marketplaces are author-built; severity is never calibrated against real platforms or prior dark-pattern benchmarks.
  • Thin samples for bold claims: 90%→0% rests on 50 episodes of one config over 5 tasks; the 66.7→80.0 ablation gap is untested for significance.
  • Unique-optimum tasks with fully specified preferences overstate how cleanly real delegated decisions can be scored.
  • Static mechanisms: real platforms adapt interactively; only marketplaces tested (CAVEAT-Stay transfer is 4 tasks).
  • No code/data release stated; CAVEAT-27B’s 22.9% remains far below frontier-with-harness 80.0%, with a frontier-teacher distillation ceiling.
Questions for further exploration
  • Do the three failure modes persist when preferences are non-unique or partial, and “optimal purchase rate” cannot be defined cleanly?
  • Would adaptive, interactive steering — reacting to the agent within an episode — amplify degradation beyond the static −61.3-pt drop?
  • Is the 66.7% → 80.0% jump from combining spec + verification tool meaningful at 60 runs, or Wilson-interval noise?
  • Can the decision procedure be learned without a frontier teacher — from rules or self-play — and at what ceiling?
  • How much of the harness gain survives at matched compute (giving BrowserUse the same 2.6× step budget)?
  • Do findings replicate on real retail sites, where steering co-occurs with genuine information asymmetry?
