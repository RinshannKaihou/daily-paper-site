Paper Brief — arXiv:2609.24270

Die scaling made modern GPUs physically asymmetric: equal SM counts no longer guarantee equal performance.

Dissecting How Die Scaling Breaks GPU Fine-grained Scheduling

Xiaoze Fan, Jianhao Wang, Weihao Cui, et al. — Shanghai Jiao Tong University · National University of Singapore · NVIDIA

2026-09-21 · 11 authors

GPU-architecture NUMA Fine-grained scheduling LLM serving Systems benchmarking

Key Metric

1.22×

Peak GroupGEMM speedup from NUMA-aware placement vs. CUTLASS — across 16 (N,K,M) configurations on H200 + B200.

Also: +14.3% decode throughput for multiplexed Qwen3-8B serving on H200.

Why This Matters

Explains why equal-SM GPU allocations can differ 1.33× in throughput, and delivers deployable NUMA-aware placement for LLM serving, co-location scheduling, and kernel tuning.

对多卡推理服务、GPU 共置调度与内核优化具有很强的实践参考价值。

Critical Edge

The 1.22× headline is GroupGEMM-only: attention kernels — what LLM serving actually runs — gain far less from NUMA awareness, and even regress on B200 with small KV caches.

01

Methodology

A

Cluster probing → compute topology

Thread Block Clusters guarantee co-scheduled SMs share a GPC. Launches at cluster sizes 3–8 log %smid: recurring SMs are “normal”; the rest are floorsweep-dependent “random” SMs.

B

L2 bandwidth probing → place random SMs

Same-GPC SMs contend on the Xbar. Each random SM is swept against known GPCs and assigned to the one with the lowest added bandwidth.

C

Latency + bit-flip probes → memory NUMA

~100k address samples per pinned SM yield local/remote HBM and L2 latency; flipping each address bit recovers the XOR partition hash — 16 bits on H200, 4KB granularity.

D

Asymmetry-aware scheduling

Persistent thread blocks pinned by discovered affinity; memory placed NUMA-locally via O(1) 4KB remapping (all NVIDIA GPUs) or a driver MLOP modification (Blackwell+).

<1 min per GPU · hash ~10 s (15 min for 80GB) · 100% cross-machine agreement

03

Strengths

  • First black-box, per-chip characterization of floorsweeping and NUMA asymmetry on shipping H200/B200/H100 PCIe — validated and cheap to re-run (<1 min/GPU).
  • Vendor-undocumented facts with immediate impact: cluster-size-8 kernels silently avoid random SMs; Green Context allocation order swings tenant throughput 1.33×; MIG disables even normal SMs.
  • Two allocation mechanisms span generations — remap-based for all NVIDIA GPUs, driver-based for Blackwell+ — with open-sourced code.
  • Unusually honest reporting: shows its own negative results (B200 small-KV regression, remap overhead) and distills separately actionable lessons.
04

Limitations

  • Requires kernel rewrites (persistent thread blocks) plus CUDA driver + MIG modifications — closed-source serving stacks cannot adopt the techniques today.
  • The 1.22× headline is GroupGEMM-specific; attention gains are modest and negative for small KV caches on B200.
  • Thin end-to-end LLM evidence: one model (Qwen3-8B), one operating point, on a research fork — no SLA or production-trace evaluation.
  • Per-chip calibration; durability across driver/firmware updates is unexamined. MI300X is characterized but never exploited by a scheduler.
  • Assumes cluster-compatible co-located kernels; mismatches force compromises costing up to −18.9%.
05

Questions for Further Exploration

Q1Does topology awareness compound with speculative decoding, chunked prefill, or MoE expert placement — or do they compete for the same degrees of freedom?
Q2Could NVIDIA expose SM→GPC maps and partition hashes officially via CUDA device attributes, removing the reverse-engineering burden?
Q3Are chip-specific mappings stable across driver updates, GPU resets, and thermal cycles — and can calibrations be cached per chip serial number?
Q4Do the placement principles transfer to AMD MI300X’s XCD/AID topology, where three latency tiers exceed binary local/remote placement?
Q5How should cloud schedulers price “cluster-capable” normal SMs vs. random SMs to internalize the observed 1.33× allocation-order externality?
