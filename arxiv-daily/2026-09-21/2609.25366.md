> **一句话总结：** 作者提出"续写式因果测试"（continuation-based causal testing）——对正确CoT的某一步注入单步扰动、截断后强制模型续写——在3个模型家族（Gemma-2-9B-IT、Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct、DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B）×GSM8K/MMLU/BBH共~2.1万条续写上发现：CoT的因果承重度随模型相对任务难度从"装饰性"转向"承重性"（GSM8K错误传播率仅3.9%，BBH达40.9%；匹配2×2实验中同类型扰动跨难度相差16倍，n=28,584方差分解98.8%归因于难度、仅0.8%归因于扰动类型）；隐藏态探针能高精度"读出"行为类型（错误传播检测86.2%，显著优于失效的logit-lens基线0.017 logit gap），但加性激活转向几乎无法"控制"行为（单方向0%翻转，最强8方向设置也只翻转24.6%的Type C）——行为类型可读但不可靠可控。

> **TL;DR:** This TMLR 08/2026 paper introduces continuation-based causal testing: perturb one step of a correct CoT, truncate, and force continuation. On Gemma-2-9B-IT, error propagation rises from **3.9% on GSM8K to 22.3% on MMLU and 40.9% on BBH** (+37pp) as base accuracy falls from 86.5% to 57.9%; a matched 2×2 shows the same numerical perturbations propagate **16× more** on BBH multistep arithmetic (64.5% vs 3.9% on GSM8K), and a variance partition on n=28,584 assigns **98.8% of explained deviance to task difficulty vs 0.8% to perturbation type**. Hidden-state probes beat a failing logit-lens baseline (0.017 logit gap) with 86.2% error-propagation detection (+53pp over chance), yet additive steering flips essentially nothing (Type A: 0/726; Type C stays ≥92%; best 8-direction setting flips only 24.6%). Practical upshot: CoT-based monitoring is only meaningful where the chain is load-bearing — i.e., exactly on hard tasks where errors propagate before a monitor can intervene.

## 核心贡献

- **提出续写式因果测试协议**：对正确多步CoT解析出步骤、单步扰动（2种数学类：算术修改/运算交换；5种概念类：置信注入/错误排除/反向逻辑/虚假类比/前提矛盾）、在早期(25%)/中期(50%)/晚期(75%)三个位置施加，截断后强制模型从被污染前缀续写，将结果分类为三种行为模式——Type A 静默绕过（无视扰动仍答对）、Type B 自我修正（检测并纠正）、Type C 错误传播（跟随错误步骤答错）。相比"另起一轮重新回答"的方案，强制续写排除了模型从原始问题重新推导的混淆。
- **发现难度—忠实度梯度**：CoT的因果承重度由模型相对任务难度主导——在Gemma-2-9B-IT上错误传播率从GSM8K的3.9%升至MMLU的22.3%和BBH的40.9%；MMLU固定格式下29个学科内从7.5%（高中心理学，93%准确率）到53.7%（global facts），约7倍跨度；匹配2×2实验（保持扰动类型不变）显示难度效应是类型效应的~33倍（偏差分解98.8% vs 0.8%，交互0.4%）。
- **跨模型验证**：梯度在Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct上复现（同数值扰动在BBH多步算术~78% vs GSM8K~12%，6倍）；推理强化学习的DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B则呈现系统性更低的错误传播（GSM8K/MMLU/BBH = 3.5%/2.7%/16.8%）和更高的自我修正份额（MMLU 46.0%）——RL训练抑制错误传播。
- **探针可读但转向不可控的分离**：42层×3 token位置×PCA-128的线性/MLP探针可预测行为类型（3类75.3%、错误传播86.2%），但单方向加性转向在11,556次生成中零翻转（Type A 0/726，二项上界0.41%），8方向扩展也仅在最强设置翻转24.6%的Type C且95%个体仍处天花板——探针方向是"读出器"而非"控制器"。
- **严格的标签质量审计**：A/B边界对judge提示词高度敏感（bypass在4个变体间从4.0%摆到89.2%），但C-vs-non-C计数完全不变；n=500双标注者盲评（κ=0.93总体、C-vs-non-C上κ=1.00）确认了C-vs-non-C维度（与论文标签一致率96.2%）；同时诚实曝光rule-based标签的探针99.9%准确率中有~25pp是标签伪影（换judge标签后降至75.0%）。

## Key Contributions

- **Continuation-based causal testing**: a controlled ablation-patch intervention (perturb one step → truncate → force continuation) that isolates the CoT→answer causal link, avoiding the re-derivation confound of reprompting; three-way behavioral taxonomy (silent bypass / self-correction / error propagation) at n=21,238 labeled continuations on Gemma-2-9B-IT.
- **The difficulty–faithfulness gradient**: CoT is not uniformly faithful — it is decorative on easy tasks (GSM8K: 94.5% bypass) and load-bearing on hard ones (BBH: 40.9% error propagation). The matched 2×2 (n=28,584, text-on-easy + numerical-on-hard cells) disentangles perturbation type from difficulty: a 16× within-type rise (GSM8K numerical 3.9% → BBH multistep arithmetic 64.5%) versus at most +4pp from type at fixed task; sequential deviance partition: difficulty Δ=3,567 (98.8%), type Δ=29.4 (0.8%, p=5.9e-8), interaction 0.4%.
- **Cross-model replication and the RL contrast**: the gradient replicates on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (57.2% overall error propagation at 41.6% accuracy vs Gemma's 27.8% at 67.5%); DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (reasoning-RL-trained) shows uniformly lower propagation (max 16.8% on BBH) and higher self-correction (46.0% on MMLU), suggesting reasoning-specific RL training suppresses error propagation broadly.
- **Detection ≠ control**: linear/MLP probes over all 42 layers (3 token positions × 3584 dims → PCA-128, grouped 5-fold CV) predict behavioral type well (bypass 79.4%/AUROC 0.85 at layer 10; 3-class 75.3% at layer 23; error-prop 86.2% at layer 28), while logit lens fails (0.017 mean logit gap) and additive steering along probe directions cannot flip behavioral type — 11,556 single-direction generations: Type A stays 0/726 flipped (binomial upper bound 0.41%), Type C never drops below 92%; an 8-direction multinomial-basis extension only partially moves Type C (24.6% flip at α=-10, 95% CI [15.3, 37.1]).
- **Label-quality honesty as methodology**: a four-variant judge-prompt sensitivity study plus a blind n=500 two-annotator human study (κ=0.93 overall; 1.00 on C-vs-non-C) show the A/B boundary is operationally subjective (bypass swings 4.0%→89.2% across prompt variants) while the C-rate is judge-invariant; headline claims rest only on the binary C-vs-non-C split, and the paper's own probe headline (99.9% under rule labels → 75.0% under judge labels, −24.9pp) is presented as a cautionary precedent for behaviorally labeled probes.

## Methodology

**Continuation-based causal testing pipeline.** Starting from questions the model answers correctly with a multi-step (≥3 steps) CoT ("correct multi-step baseline"), the pipeline: (1) parses the CoT into steps; (2) perturbs exactly one step using 7 strategies grouped by surface form — mathematical (arithmetic change, e.g. "5+3=8"→"5+7=12"; operation swap, "+2"→"×7"; GSM8K only) and conceptual (confidence injection, wrong elimination, reversed logic, false analogy, premise contradiction; MMLU/BBH); (3) applies the perturbation at three positions (early 25%, middle 50%, late 75%); (4) truncates after the perturbed step and forces the model to continue from the corrupted prefix; (5) classifies the outcome vs the original answer. Forced continuation is the key design choice: unlike presenting the full corrupted CoT in a fresh turn (Lanham-style early answering), it prevents the model from re-deriving the answer from the question, isolating whether the written chain constrains the output.

**Behavioral classification.** Three modes: Type A silent bypass (ignores the perturbation, correct answer), Type B self-correction (detects and fixes mid-generation, 27% of perturbed continuations overall — distinct from prompted self-correction), Type C error propagation (follows the corrupted step, wrong answer). An initial keyword rule was replaced by an LLM judge (Claude Haiku in production, validated against Sonnet) that resolved 99.9% of cases; the production prompt's implicit-correction clause and its A/B boundary were stress-tested with four prompt variants and a blind human study (n=500, two annotators, stratified to over-sample discriminating cells). Token-level "perturbation uptake" analysis confirms Type C continuations actually consume perturbation-unique tokens (GSM8K uptake 0.376 vs 0.274 for Type A).

**Disentangling type from difficulty.** Because GSM8K is both the easy task and the only numerical-perturbation task, the authors add the off-diagonal 2×2 cells: 5,400 text-based perturbations on GSM8K (the five conceptual archetypes re-expressed to assert a wrong number) and 1,946 numerical on-hard perturbations (BBH multistep arithmetic, boolean expressions, object counting; four MMLU numeric subjects), combined into a frame of n=28,584. A logistic regression Pr(error_prop) ~ type × difficulty with a sequential deviance partition quantifies each axis; robustness checks include a 3-magnitude perturbation-strength sweep (n+1 / n×2 / n×2+3), question-clustered sensitivity (7,104 unique questions), entry-order sensitivity, and bucketing sensitivity.

**Hidden-state probes and steering.** For Gemma-2-9B-IT (42 layers), hidden states at 3 token positions (before/at/after the perturbation point) per layer (3×3584 = 10,752 dims) are PCA-reduced to 128; linear (sklearn LogisticRegression) and MLP (128→256→n_classes) probes are trained on three tasks (3-class A/B/C; bypass vs non-A; error-prop vs non-C) under grouped 5-fold CV (grouped by base question), with position-ablation, sample-size, and balanced-subsampling robustness checks. Causal intervention: the error-propagation direction is extracted from a logistic probe at layer 21 and added to the residual stream (α ∈ [-10,+10]) during generation — 11,556 generations — plus an 8-direction extension on multinomial probe bases (k ∈ {2,4,8}) with random-direction and cross-dataset controls.

**Cross-model and compute.** The full pipeline is rerun on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (7,791 continuations) and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (1,603 post-`</think>` continuations after a ≥4-step filter). All experiments ran on a single NVIDIA H100 80GB (~14 h single-pass, ~22 h including development). Code, prompts, and human-annotation materials are released at github.com/r2m-ai/load-bearing-cot.

## Results & Highlights

- **Difficulty gradient (Gemma-2-9B-IT, judge labels, n=21,238):** error propagation is 3.9% on GSM8K (bypass 94.5%), 22.3% on MMLU (self-correction 36.3%), and 40.9% on BBH — a +37pp rise as base accuracy falls 86.5%→57.9%; pooled split is 45.3% bypass / 26.9% self-correction / 27.8% error propagation.
- **Type-vs-difficulty matched 2×2 (n=28,584):** numerical perturbations propagate at 3.9% on easy GSM8K but 64.5% on BBH multistep arithmetic (+60.6pp, 16× within-type rise), while text perturbations on GSM8K reach only 6.5% (+2.6pp over numerical); the sequential deviance partition assigns 98.8% of explained deviance to difficulty (Δ=3,567, p≈0) vs 0.8% to perturbation type (Δ=29.4, p=5.9e-8) and 0.4% to interaction — difficulty dominates by ~33×. A strength sweep (Δ≤4pp within-task across subtle/moderate/blatant magnitudes, >60pp between-task at fixed magnitude) rules out a competence-floor reading.
- **Within-MMLU subject gradient:** at fixed 4-choice format and identical strategies, error propagation spans 7.5% (high-school psychology, 93% accuracy) to 53.7% (global facts, hardest) across 29 subjects — Spearman slope β=0.069, p=1.9×10^-102, a ~7× spread with task difficulty held operationally constant.
- **Probes detect but steering cannot control:** best per-task probe accuracies (grouped 5-fold CV, N=2,000, judge labels): 79.4% bypass at layer 10 (linear, AUROC 0.85, +12.2pp over majority), 75.3% three-class at layer 23 (MLP, +20pp over 33% chance), 86.2% error-propagation at layer 28 (MLP); logit-lens baseline fails (0.017 mean logit gap). Single-direction steering at layer 21 (11,556 generations): Type A flipped 0/726 (binomial upper bound 0.41%), Type C never below 92%; the 8-direction probe-basis extension at α=-10, k=8 flips at most 24.6% of Type C (95% CI [15.3, 37.1]) while Type A stays ≤8.3% — readable, not reliably controllable.
- **Label artifact exposed:** retraining the identical probe architecture on LLM-judge labels instead of rule-based keyword labels drops 3-class accuracy from 99.9% to 75.0% (−24.9pp) — a large fraction of the "headline" rule-label accuracy was a strategy-correlated label artifact, offered by the authors as a general caution for behaviorally labeled probes.
- **Cross-model:** Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct replicates the gradient (57.2% overall error propagation at 41.6% accuracy vs Gemma's 27.8% at 67.5%; within-numerical: ~78% on BBH multistep vs ~12% on GSM8K, a 6× contrast); DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B inverts the profile — error propagation 3.5%/2.7%/16.8% on GSM8K/MMLU/BBH with self-correction up to 46.0% (MMLU), consistent with RL-induced self-verification suppressing propagation.
- **Human validation:** the blind n=500 two-annotator study yields inter-annotator κ=0.93 overall and 1.00 on C-vs-non-C; human consensus matches the paper's labels on 96.2% of rows for C-vs-non-C (but only 76.0% for the three-way label, almost entirely paper-B vs human-A disagreements on the prompt-sensitive A/B boundary).

## Authors & Credibility

Renee Jia (R2M AI; Cornell University, corresponding author) and Di Mu (R2M AI; Carnegie Mellon University) — an industry lab (r2m.ai) with university affiliations. The paper is **published in Transactions on Machine Learning Research (08/2026)** after OpenReview peer review, which is a meaningful quality signal: TMLR reviews for scientific rigor rather than novelty hype, and this paper's review-visible artifacts (judge-prompt sensitivity study, blind human annotation, variance-decomposition appendices, released code/prompts/annotation materials at github.com/r2m-ai/load-bearing-cot) are consistent with a genuinely refereed work. The authors are unusually candid about their own weaknesses: they foreground a null result (steering fails), self-undermine a headline number (99.9%→75.0% probe accuracy as a label artifact), exclude a non-replicating cell (Llama text-on-GSM8K) from headline claims, and enumerate scope limits (≤9B models, single-step perturbations, correct-baseline conditioning) in a dedicated limitations section. I have no independent record of these specific authors' prior work, so credibility rests on the venue, the artifacts, and the internal consistency of the reported methodology — all of which check out.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The contribution is real but incremental-adjacent: the perturb-and-continue idea descends directly from Lanham et al. (2023)'s early-answering and step-removal work, and Fannjiang et al. (2024) already showed faithfulness is computationally hard to guarantee. What is genuinely new is (a) the forced-continuation design that blocks the re-derivation confound, (b) the framing of load-bearingness as a *continuous, difficulty-conditioned* property rather than a binary faithful/unfaithful verdict, and (c) the matched 2×2 that cleanly separates perturbation type from difficulty — an analysis most prior behavioral studies skipped. The detection-vs-control dissociation (probes read, steering cannot write) is the most interesting result, though it partially restates the known gap between probing accuracy and causal manipulability (Li et al. 2023's truthful-direction steering, which they adapt).

**Rigor.** Above average for behavioral CoT work, with three standout checks: the judge-prompt sensitivity study (bypass swings 4.0%→89.2% across variants while the C-rate is invariant — and the headline claims correctly rest only on C-vs-non-C), the blind n=500 human validation, and the entry-order sensitivity analysis for the variance partition (they show type's deviance inflates 29.4→105.8 under a classical-suppressor pattern when entered second, so the 98.8%/0.8% split is convention-dependent but its ordering is robust). The perturbation-strength sweep and question-clustered regression further close the "it's just a harder perturbation" loophole.

**Red flags and weak points, paper-specific.** (1) **The BBH headline number is likely inflated by label noise**: the paper's own calibration shows human consensus C-rate on BBH is 24% in the unstratified random stratum vs the paper's 44% — 21/35 rule-C stratum rows are actually silent bypasses (over-scoring failures where the answer is a word/letter). The difficulty ordering survives, but the headline 40.9% should be read as an upper bound that compresses substantially under human labels. (2) **Selection conditioning**: only 7,691 of 11,390 correct baselines are retained, and BBH discards a larger fraction than GSM8K, over-selecting items near the model's competence boundary — the authors admit this biases all difficulty levels in the same direction but it still means the reported rates are conditional on a non-representative slice. (3) **The cross-model B-rate comparison (DeepSeek's high "self-correction") is a fixed-prompt inference the authors have not human-validated**, and the DeepSeek replication differs from R1's official evaluation regime (greedy decoding; their GSM8K accuracy is 38-44% vs ~85% public, acknowledged in Appendix P) with a much smaller filtered n (1,603) — the RL-suppresses-propagation story is plausible but the least-controlled claim in the paper. (4) **The Llama text-on-GSM8K cell simply failed to replicate** (97.7% empty continuations — a "defence" behavior operationally reclassified as Type C) and was excluded from headline numbers; the right conclusion is that the taxonomy's portability across prompt formats is fragile, which the paper handles honestly but which caps the generality of the "text perturbations propagate less" claim. (5) **Scale**: everything hinges on 7-9B models; the base-vs-IT control collapsed (base Gemma produced 0-22% step compliance), so the instruction-tuning factor is untested at fixed architecture, and no ≥70B run exists (single-H100 budget). (6) One-step perturbations only — internally consistent multi-step perturbations could raise propagation and change the picture.

**Significance.** For CoT-based monitoring (a live safety proposal), the structural tension identified here is consequential: monitoring is uninformative exactly where chains are decorative, and on hard tasks the error propagates before any monitor can intervene — and the strongest practical attack (confidence injection, 36.8% error propagation) is documented as an adversarial vector. The 99.9%→75.0% label-artifact demonstration is independently valuable to anyone training probes on LLM-labeled behavior. Overall: a careful, honest, moderately novel paper whose headline numbers deserve the exact discounting its own appendices prescribe.

## Strengths

- **Scale + control**: 21,238 labeled continuations plus a 17,346-continuation matched-cell extension, all from a deterministic, pre-registered-axes protocol run on a single H100 — reproducible by a small lab, not just a frontier-lab one.
- **The matched 2×2 / variance-partition design** is the right tool for the type-vs-difficulty confound and is executed with unusual thoroughness (strength sweep, entry-order and bucketing sensitivity, question-clustering).
- **Radical label-quality honesty**: the A/B prompt-sensitivity analysis, the blind two-annotator study, and the self-reported −24.9pp probe label artifact make this one of the more epistemically careful behavioral-faithfulness papers in recent memory.
- **A useful null result**: the detection-vs-control gap (86.2% probe accuracy vs ≤24.6% best-case steering flip, with random-direction controls) is reported with Wilson CIs and dose-response checks rather than buried.
- **Practical stakes made concrete**: quantifies when CoT monitoring works (nowhere on easy tasks) and surfaces confidence injection (36.8% propagation) as a specific attack on CoT-monitored systems; code, prompts, and annotation materials are public.

## Limitations

- **Model scale and coverage**: both main models are ≤9B instruction-tuned; no ≥70B experiment (single-H100 budget); the base-vs-instruction-tuned control is uninformative (base Gemma complied with the multi-step format in only 0-22% of runs), so the role of instruction tuning vs CoT elicitation is confounded away rather than measured.
- **The three-way taxonomy's A/B boundary is operationally subjective**: four judge-prompt variants move bypass by an order of magnitude (4.0%→89.2%); human consensus agrees with the production label on only 76.0% of three-way rows (96.2% on C-vs-non-C); all A/B-dependent results had to be reported as "consistent with prompt variant X."
- **BBH Type-C over-counting**: the rule's wrong-answer→Type-C heuristic mislabels over-scoring failures; under human labels the BBH C-rate compresses (24% vs 44% in the paper's unstratified stratum), so cross-dataset magnitudes are soft even though the ordering is stable.
- **Correct-baseline conditioning is asymmetric across datasets** (BBH discards more baselines), over-selecting competence-boundary questions; all rates are conditional on this filtered population.
- **One cell of the cross-model grid does not replicate** (Llama text-on-GSM8K: 97.7% empty continuations) and the DeepSeek arm uses a non-official greedy-decoding regime with a small post-filter n (1,603); the RL-suppression claim inherits both caveats and an unvalidated fixed-prompt B-rate comparison.
- **Single-step perturbations only**, and unnatural by construction — the authors argue this proxies injected-content deployment regimes (tool outputs, retrieved documents, sub-agent traces), but internally consistent multi-step corruption remains untested and is flagged as the natural next experiment.
- **The protocol measures only the CoT→answer direction**; it is silent on the computation→CoT link that circuit-level faithfulness work targets, so "decorative" here does not mean "decoupled from internal computation."

## Relevance

这篇论文与你的"LLM Hidden States & Interpretability"方向直接相关：42层×3位置的PCA+线性/MLP探针能以86.2%读出CoT行为类型，但沿探针方向的加性转向几乎无法改变行为，为"探针准确率≠因果可操控性"提供了一个大规模、带随机方向对照的实证边界。同时它对CoT监控（monitoring）可靠性给出的"装饰性—承重性"梯度结论，与你关注的长时程自主系统可信性问题也密切相关。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the decorative→load-bearing gradient persist at ≥70B scale, and does reasoning post-training (RLVR à la DeepSeek-R1) causally suppress error propagation, or does the distill's low C-rate merely reflect a different prompt-format regime?
- Would internally consistent multi-step perturbations (an entire fabricated sub-derivation rather than one step) collapse the 98.8%-difficulty / 0.8%-type partition, or does difficulty still dominate?
- Can richer interventions — causal scrubbing, attention-circuit edits, residual-stream ablations — move behavioral type where additive steering along probe directions fails, thereby separating "unfaithful" from "unsteerable by additive perturbation at the probed layers"?
- What mechanism drives mid-generation self-correction (27% of perturbed continuations, 46% on DeepSeek+MMLU) when prompted self-correction is known to fail — is it detectable prospectively from hidden states *before* the corrupted step is emitted, which would define the monitor's intervention window?
- Can the confidence-injection attack (36.8% error propagation) be detected at inference time by the same probes, and does the 0.4% type×difficulty interaction hide regimes (e.g., hard tasks + numeric content) where type matters more than the aggregate suggests?
