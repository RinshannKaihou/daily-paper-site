Why this matters

Deterministic regression tests, cross-hardware experiments and audited LLM serving finally get a practical rule: reduction order as a pure function of problem shape — faster and lighter than prior art.

Critical edge

The 1.17–3.1× speedup is measured against LayerCast only. Against a standard BF16 tensor-core stack, unmitigated inference is often the fastest configuration — IEEE-FMA GEMMs run 2.4× slower than TF32 at decode shapes.

Methodology — ReproFuse in four rules

Key insight. Divergence starts where the top-1/top-2 logit margin (typically 4–19 nats) dips below the cross-GPU logit shift; ~1 in 10⁴ positions sits within 10⁻³ nats of a tie, and noise flips exactly those tokens. Pin the linear-layer reduction order and the dominant divergence channel goes to zero.

  • R1IEEE-754 FMA only. Scalar CUDA-core FMAs; tl.dot on FP32 would hit TF32 tensor cores with unspecified, generation-dependent accumulation.
  • R2No autotuning. Tiling, split factor and K-block are compile-time constants of the problem shape — one decode bucket (M≤64), one prefill.
  • R3Deterministic split-K. K cut into S contiguous segments, each accumulated by exactly one program; partials reduced in fixed order. No atomics.
  • R4Batch-invariant by construction. A request’s output is bit-identical for any co-scheduled batch size 1–64.

Mechanism. BF16 weights are upcast to FP32 in registers inside fused Triton GEMM kernels — deleting LayerCast’s transient FP32 weight copy — and its lm_head-stays-FP32 bug is fixed (≈1 GiB back on an 8B model). Attention, rotary, norm and sampler stay vendor kernels.

Eval: 3 architectures (A100 SM80 / L40S SM89 / H100 SM90) × 3 models (3B–8B) × 4 reasoning benchmarks; greedy decoding, batch 32, 3 seeds. The Llama chat-template date string had to be pinned — unpinned, it faked a 57.6% cross-GPU “divergence”.

Root cause: LayerCast’s weight-conversion filter silently exempts lm_head, storing it in FP32 (vocab×hidden×2 bytes ≈ 1 GiB on an 8B model). ReproFuse stores every projection, including lm_head, in BF16.

Strengths

  • Reproducibility becomes a construction, not a tendency — with ablations proving each rule necessary (TF32 0/9 vs IEEE 9/9 bit-identical; 50/50 distinct results under atomic split-K).
  • Rare negative-result hygiene: attention-kernel residual, LayerCast lm_head bug, autotuner noise sensitivity and the date-string artifact are all reported, not hidden.
  • Improves all three axes at once — determinism (≤ prior SOTA in every cell), speed (36/36 configs), memory (+4–7% KV) — with a roofline account of why L40S gains most.
  • The decision-margin vocabulary (median margin 4–19 nats, flips in the 10⁻³-nat tail) is reusable well beyond this paper.
  • Batch invariance fixes a second, independent pathology at no extra cost.

Limitations

  • Linear layers only — attention / norm / rotary / sampler remain vendor kernels; the last 0.08% of divergence lives there. Full-stack determinism is not achieved.
  • Absolute cost vs standard TF32/BF16 serving: IEEE-FMA GEMMs are 2.4× slower at decode shapes; prefill ~0.8× cuBLAS. The speedup claim is LayerCast-relative.
  • Modest scale: 3B–8B, single GPU, batch 32, greedy decoding only, one stack (vLLM 0.8.6). No 70B-class models, no tensor parallelism.
  • NVIDIA-only — AMD CDNA / Intel Gaudi FMA semantics need re-verification; cross-vendor bit-identity is open.
  • Sub-1% divergence cells are small-sample estimates, sometimes resting on a single divergent problem.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Can shape-pure reduction ordering extend to attention (flash split-K over sequence positions) and the sampler, closing the last 0.08% channel end-to-end?
  2. How does ReproFuse compose with tensor / pipeline parallelism, where cross-rank reductions add another device- and topology-dependent channel?
  3. What is the absolute throughput/latency price vs a standard BF16 tensor-core stack at 70B scale — does +4–7% KV capacity justify it in production?
  4. Does the “IEEE-754 FMA is bit-specified given operands” premise hold on AMD CDNA, Intel Gaudi/PVC or TPUv5 — is cross-vendor bit-identity achievable with the same four rules?
  5. Could fixed-order kernels make training or LoRA fine-tuning bitwise reproducible across GPUs, where divergence compounds over thousands of steps?
