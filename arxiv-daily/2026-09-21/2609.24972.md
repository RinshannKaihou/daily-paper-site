> **一句话总结：** Google Cloud AI Research 团队发现 agent harness（提示词/控制流/工具/记忆等冻结模型之外的一切）的迭代自进化（RSI）会严重过拟合进化集——四个先前方法（Meta-Harness、AHE、TTHE、HarnessX）在进化集上最高 +3.6 分，但在分布外基准上平均只 +0.9 甚至 −1.7 分；他们提出 RRSI，把经典正则化（L0 式编辑预算退火、L1 式结构剪枝、L2 式复杂度感知接受、泄漏筛查、噪声底板、证据感知信用分配、结构化探索）同时加在"提议"和"选择"两侧，结果在 8 个基准、3 个领域上全部提升分布外成绩：Terminal-Bench 2.1 上 Gemini 3.5 Flash 场景 +14.1 分、Claude Opus 4.8 场景 +6.0 分（74.2→80.2），且进化的 harness 迁移到从未见过的 SWE-bench Verified（82.0→83.8）和更弱的 Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite（11.2→14.6，+30.4%），同时比无正则化进化少用 30% 的策略 token（2.42M vs 3.80M/trial）。

> **TL;DR:** Harness evolution (an LLM proposing/selecting edits to prompts, control flow, tooling, memory around a frozen policy) is a practical form of recursive self-improvement, but it overfits: across eight benchmarks in three domains, four prior methods (Meta-Harness, AHE, TTHE, HarnessX) gain up to +3.6 on the evolve split yet transfer only +0.9 or even −1.7 points to out-of-distribution benchmarks. RRSI regularizes both sides of the loop — proposal-side (cosine-annealed L0-style edit budget 4→1, evidence-aware credit assignment over the full edit history, stall-triggered structured exploration) and selection-side (pre-evaluation leakage screening of diffs, noise-adjusted acceptance floor calibrated from repeated base-harness runs, Ridge/L2-style rule ΔC ≤ β₀ + β₁·ΔS, Lasso/L1-style pruning of unproductive components). Under frozen Claude Opus 4.8 the evolved harness improves Terminal-Bench 2.1 from 74.2 to 80.2 (+6.0) while transferring +1.8 to the never-scored SWE-bench Verified (82.0→83.8), and beats the OOD average of the best baseline (Meta-Harness, 40.6) by 3.0 points (43.6 vs H₀'s 39.7) at 2.42M policy tokens/trial — ~30% fewer than unregularized evolution (3.80M). Code, full round-by-round records and harness diffs are released (github.com/google-research/rrsi).

## 核心贡献

- **把"过拟合"确立为 harness 自进化的核心问题**：论文形式化了 harness 进化作为自适应经验优化（同一有限进化集被反复复用）的结构，并实证展示四种先前方法全部呈现"进化集大涨、分布外归零甚至倒退"的排序反转现象——进化集上最强的 Meta-Harness（93.0 vs RRSI 90.5）在分布外平均只比起点 H₀ 高 0.9 分，AHE 和 TTHE 甚至比它们出发时的 harness 还低 1.7 分。
- **RRSI：7 项正则化器约束搜索轨迹而非假设空间**：提议侧三项——(A) L0 式退火编辑预算（单轮候选可捆绑的独立可归因编辑数从 b_max=4 余弦退火到 b_min=1）；(B) 证据感知信用分配（记录每个被评估编辑的组件/假设/ΔS/ΔC/是否被接受，被否定的机制成为负证据不再重试）；(C) 结构化探索（连续 w=3 轮进展落在噪声带 δ 内即判定停滞，预留 m_draft=1 个候选槽位给未被锻炼过的组件）。选择侧四项——(D) 泄漏筛查（评估前由 critic 读 diff，拒绝编码任务名/实体特定值/答案的编辑）；(E) 噪声调整底板（用基座 harness 重复评估校准 δ，候选须满足 Ŝ ≥ S*−δ）；(F) Ridge/L2 式复杂度感知接受（ΔC ≤ β₀ + β₁·ΔS，成本增长必须由分数增长买单）；(G) Lasso/L1 式结构剪枝（剪枝窗口内无严格正增益的组件被报告为删除目标）。
- **跨域、跨策略、跨模型强度的迁移验证**：8 个基准、3 个领域（编码/智能体工作区/工程设计），harness 只在单一进化集上进化、原封不动跑分布外基准；用两种冻结策略（Claude Opus 4.8、Gemini 3.5 Flash）独立进化，且进化出的 harness 能迁移到从未参与搜索的更弱模型（Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite，+30.4%）；在确定性评分的 EngDesign/Frontier-Eng 上增益保持，排除了"讨好 LLM judge"的解释。
- **正则化换来的不只是泛化还有效率**：完整 RRSI 的最终 harness 每 trial 2.42M 策略 token、26.3 步，低于全部四个基线（2.69M–3.82M、27.3–34.6 步）；无正则化进化要 3.80M token（高 57%）才换来更差的分布外成绩（40.3 vs 43.6）。AHE 是极端案例：3.82M token（比 RRSI 多 58%），分布外反而低 4.4 分。
- **开放可复现**：代码、逐轮记录、critic 决策和完整 harness diff 全部公开（github.com/google-research/rrsi + regularized-rsi.com），附录给出全部超参数（δ=0.017/0.004/0.020 按域校准，β₀≈10% 基础成本容忍，β₁≈25% token 余量/额外通过）。

## Key Contributions

- **Overfitting is identified as the central failure mode of harness-based RSI**: the paper formalizes harness evolution as adaptive empirical optimization over a reused finite evolve set, and demonstrates a ranking inversion across four prior methods (Meta-Harness, AHE, TTHE, HarnessX) — the strongest on the evolve split (Meta-Harness 93.0 vs RRSI's 90.5) transfers only +0.9 points OOD on average, while AHE and TTHE finish 1.7 points *below* the harness they started from.
- **RRSI: seven regularizers constraining the search trajectory, not the hypothesis space.** Proposal side: (A) L0-style annealed update sparsity (independently attributable edits per candidate cosine-annealed from b_max=4 to b_min=1); (B) evidence-aware credit assignment (every evaluated edit is logged with component, hypothesis, ΔS/ΔC and acceptance outcome; falsified mechanisms remain negative evidence); (C) structured exploration (search stalled when progress stays within noise band δ over w=3 rounds → m_draft=1 candidate slots reserved for unexercised components). Selection side: (D) pre-evaluation leakage screening of diffs by a critic; (E) noise-adjusted acceptance floor Ŝ ≥ S*−δ calibrated from repeated evaluations of the unchanged base harness; (F) Ridge/L2-style complexity-aware acceptance ΔC ≤ β₀ + β₁·ΔS; (G) Lasso/L1-style structural pruning of components without strictly positive recent measured gain.
- **Transfer validated across domains, policies and model strengths**: eight benchmarks in three domains, each harness evolved on one suite and run unchanged on held-out and OOD benchmarks; independently re-run under two frozen policies (Claude Opus 4.8 and Gemini 3.5 Flash); the harness evolved with Gemini 3.5 Flash transfers to a weaker unseen backbone (Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite: 11.2→14.6, +30.4% relative); gains survive on deterministically-graded EngDesign/Frontier-Eng, ruling out judge-gaming as the explanation.
- **Regularization buys efficiency as well as generalization**: the final RRSI harness costs 2.42M policy tokens per trial at 26.3 steps, cheaper than every prior method (2.69M–3.82M tokens, 27.3–34.6 steps); unregularized evolution spends 3.80M tokens (+57%) for a *worse* OOD average (40.3 vs 43.6). AHE is the extreme: 58% more tokens than RRSI for 4.4 fewer OOD points.
- **Fully open evaluation trail**: code, round-by-round records, critic decisions and exact harness diffs are released (github.com/google-research/rrsi, regularized-rsi.com); Appendix D.1 lists all hyperparameters (δ=0.017/0.004/0.020 per domain, β₀≈10% base cost allowance, β₁≈25% token allowance per additional pass), tuned only on the evolve environment.

## Methodology

**Setting.** An agent A = (π, H) is a frozen backbone policy π wrapped in a harness H — system/task prompts, control flow, tool interfaces, memory and skill files, and context management. Harness evolution is a generic loop: at round t, run H_t on the evolve set D_evolve, summarize trajectories into feedback F_t, have a proposer LLM sample m candidate harnesses, evaluate them on the same D_evolve (k trials per task), and promote the argmax. Because D_evolve is reused adaptively across rounds, this is adaptive empirical optimization — the setting where test-set reuse converts evaluation noise and leakage into permanent harness state.

**Proposal-side regularization.** (A) *L0-style annealed update sparsity*: each candidate is a subset of an atomically-represented edit pool; the budget b_t = ⌈b_min + (b_max − b_min)·½(1 + cos(πt/T))⌉ caps the number of independently attributable edits bundled per candidate, annealed from 4 to 1 so late-round changes are sparse and attributable. (B) *Evidence-aware credit assignment*: every evaluated candidate contributes a per-edit history record (component, hypothesis, ΔS, ΔC, accepted or not); the proposer conditions on this full history, so rejected mechanisms stay negative evidence instead of being re-proposed. (C) *Structured exploration*: a stall detector (progress over the last w=3 rounds within the noise band δ) reserves m_draft=1 candidate slots for components not yet exercised by any measured edit — an entropy-regularization analogue that redirects capacity without restricting what the harness may contain.

**Selection-side regularization.** (D) *Leakage screening*: before full evaluation, a critic LLM reads each candidate diff and rejects edits encoding task names, entity-specific values, answers, or logic specific to the evolve benchmark — screening pre-evaluation matters because a leaking candidate never collects the inflated evolve-set score that would make it attractive later. (E) *Noise-adjusted floor*: the unchanged base harness is evaluated repeatedly to estimate an empirical noise tolerance δ; a candidate may only replace the incumbent if Ŝ(H′) ≥ S* − δ, preventing downhill walks through individually "noise-sized" regressions. (F) *Ridge/L2-style complexity-aware acceptance*: for gains exceeding δ, the extra policy-token cost must satisfy ΔC ≤ β₀ + β₁·ΔS (β₀ = tolerated cost for negligible gain; β₁ ≈ 25% token allowance per additional coding pass), so every unit of added inference cost must be paid for in measured improvement. (G) *Lasso/L1-style structural pruning*: components exercised within a pruning window (n_prune=4–5) that produced no strictly positive measured gain are reported to the proposer as deletion targets, sparsifying the retained harness rather than merely penalizing growth. Within the noise band (ΔS ≤ δ), a shaped admissibility rule w_s·ΔS − w_c·ΔC + w_n·ν_t(H′) > 0 lets cost reduction and structural novelty (ν_t counts never-winning structural component types) contribute instead of raw score. The paper is explicit that the L0/L1/L2 terminology marks *analogous roles*, not actual penalized objectives over a shared parameter vector.

**Experimental protocol.** Three domains: coding (evolve on Terminal-Bench 2.1, 89 containerized shell tasks with exact unit-test verification; OOD = SWE-bench Verified), agentic workspace (evolve on Harvey LAB, 120 legal-workflow tasks; 40-task in-distribution held-out split; OOD = JobBench, GDPval, APEX-Agents, all LLM-judge graded), engineering design (evolve on EngDesign, 61 tasks graded by frozen simulators; OOD = Frontier-Eng, 47 tasks, deterministic MedalScore). Policy frozen at Claude Opus 4.8 (proposer, analyst and critic all Opus 4.8); base harness H₀ = Terminus-2 (ReAct loop over an MCP tool gateway) for coding, dynamic toolbox + ReSum-style context management for the other domains. T=20 evolution rounds (k=2 trials/task) for coding and agentic workspace; T=40 (k=4) for engineering design. Baselines — Meta-Harness, AHE, TTHE, HarnessX — start from the same H₀ under the same frozen policy, evolve set and candidate budget, so arms differ only in the method under study.

## Results & Highlights

- **Gains on every split the evolved harness never sees, in all three domains (Claude Opus 4.8):** evolve +6.0 on Terminal-Bench 2.1 (74.2→80.2), +1.1 on Harvey LAB (89.4→90.5), +4.9 on EngDesign (50.0→54.9); transfer: +1.8 on SWE-bench Verified (82.0→83.8) despite never being scored on it, +2.3 on the held-out 40 tasks of Harvey LAB (86.9→89.2), +4.7/+3.5/+3.7 on JobBench/GDPval/APEX-Agents (36.0→40.7, 48.8→52.3, 34.2→37.9; 7.2%–13.1% relative), and +4.3 Medal points on Frontier-Eng (17.7→22.0, +24.3% relative). No held-out split regresses anywhere — exactly the failure mode of memorizing harnesses.
- **The ranking inversion against the four baselines (agentic workspace, Table 1):** every prior method evolves well — Meta-Harness reaches 93.0 on the evolve split, *above* RRSI's 90.5 — but on the OOD average over JobBench/GDPval/APEX-Agents the ordering flips: H₀ 39.7; Meta-Harness 40.6 (+0.9); HarnessX 39.7 (exactly base); AHE 39.2 and TTHE 38.0 (1.7 points *below* where they started). RRSI posts 43.6, the only evolved harness to clear H₀ by more than a point (+3.0 over the best baseline's OOD average).
- **Ablation isolates where the transfer comes from (Table 2, agentic workspace):** unregularized evolution scores 92.3 on evolve (vs RRSI 90.5) but 40.3 OOD average at 3.80M tokens/trial; RRSI posts 89.2 in-distribution held-out, 43.6 OOD at 2.42M tokens (−36%). Removing only the acceptance-side regularizers leaves evolve at 91.5 but drops OOD to 41.0 and inflates tokens to 3.59M; removing only the proposal-side constraints costs just 0.2 evolve points but 1.7 OOD points (41.9); removing both yields the highest evolve score of any arm (92.8) with OOD back at 40.3 — unregularized selection spends its accepted edits on noise and context growth rather than mechanism.
- **Not tied to one policy family or model strength (Tables 3–4):** with Gemini 3.5 Flash as the frozen policy, RRSI lifts Terminal-Bench 2.1 from 64.6 to 78.7 (+14.1) and transfers +2.2 to SWE-bench Verified (76.8→79.0); under Claude Opus 4.8 the same pattern gives +6.0/+1.8. The harness evolved with Gemini 3.5 Flash, run unchanged under Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite (a weaker model that never took part in the search), lifts Terminal-Bench 2.1 accuracy from 11.2 to 14.6 (+3.4, +30.4% relative) — the learned mechanisms are a program, not an artifact of the search policy.
- **Cost dominates all priors (Figure 4):** RRSI runs at 2.42M policy tokens and 26.3 steps per trial vs 2.69M–3.82M tokens and 27.3–34.6 steps for the four baselines; AHE spends 3.82M tokens (58% more than RRSI) for 4.4 fewer OOD points. No evolved harness is as cheap as H₀ (1.56M tokens, 21.2 steps), so the authors concede evolution buys part of its gain with test-time compute — the regularizers decide how much.
- **Qualitative decisions show the rules binding (Table 6):** a first-round coding candidate with +3.93 points is accepted; a superficially similar one (+1.69 points, +26.1% cost) is rejected by the cost rule; a round-8 candidate that saves 13.6% cost but loses 2.81 points is rejected by the floor; an engineering candidate fixing a recurring "workdir must be an existing directory" error is accepted at 122/244→128/244 passes for +1.6% tokens.

## Critical Assessment

- **The "regularization" framing is analogy, not math — and the paper says so.** L0/L1/L2 here are heuristic LLM-driven mechanisms (an edit-count cap enforced by prompting, a critic that reads diffs, a linear cost-gain acceptance rule), not penalties in an optimized objective; Appendix C concedes the correspondence is "qualitative" and "functional rather than mathematical." This is honest but means the seven regularizers are seven hand-designed heuristics with per-domain hyperparameters (δ varies 5× across domains: 0.004–0.020; β₁ 24.4–44.5), and no sensitivity analysis of any single hyperparameter is reported.
- **The ablation granularity is too coarse.** Table 2 ablates only two *groups* (proposal-side vs acceptance-side) and only in the agentic-workspace domain. With seven regularizers, the natural question — which one carries the transfer gain? Is leakage screening alone enough, or is the noise floor doing the work? — is unanswerable from the paper. The comparison arms are also all-or-nothing across a bundle of mechanisms that differ wildly in cost and complexity.
- **The evolve-split loss is real and quietly large.** RRSI scores 90.5 on its own evolve split vs 92.3 for unregularized evolution and 93.0 for Meta-Harness — the method deliberately trades ~2 points of in-distribution score for transfer. The paper is commendably upfront about this ("RRSI posts the smallest evolve-set gain of any evolved harness"), but practitioners optimizing a single benchmark should read the headline as "you will give up evolve-set points," not as a free lunch.
- **Baselines are re-implementations under a shared protocol.** Sharing H₀, policy, evolve set and candidate budget across Meta-Harness/AHE/TTHE/HarnessX is good hygiene, but the appendix descriptions are one paragraph each, and there is no discussion of how faithfully each method was reproduced or whether its authors would endorse the configuration. A weaker-but-cheaper ablation-style baseline (e.g., plain greedy selection plus *only* the noise floor) is also missing — it would isolate how much of the +3.9 OOD gap over unregularized evolution comes from the single cheapest regularizer.
- **Judged benchmarks dominate the headline OOD average.** Three of the three OOD agentic benchmarks (JobBench, GDPval, APEX-Agents) and Harvey LAB itself are LLM-judge graded (GDPval via a 3-model judge panel at ~50% win-rate vs human experts). The deterministic engineering results (Frontier-Eng +4.3, EngDesign +4.9) and exact unit-test coding results (SWE-bench +1.8) genuinely mitigate the judge-gaming concern, but the largest OOD deltas (+4.7, +3.5, +3.7) are all in judge-graded territory where a diff-critic-approved prompt rewrite could still shift style in judge-favorable directions.
- **k=2 trials per task leaves residual selection noise.** The noise band δ is calibrated from repeated base-harness runs and the floor prevents downhill drift, but winner-selection among candidates under 2 trials per 89–480 tasks is still adaptively reusing a finite set; the paper cites adaptive data analysis but does not bound the remaining false-discovery rate across 20 rounds.
- **Red flags: few.** No cherry-picking detected — every held-out split is reported including the unflattering ones, negatives (TTHE/AHE below baseline) are printed plainly, and hyperparameters are stated as tuned without consulting held-out/OOD benchmarks. The main credibility tax is that the strongest claims rest on one evolve environment per domain.

## Strengths

- **The right problem, stated precisely**: transferring classical generalization concern (test-set reuse, leakage, noise chasing, complexity creep) into automated harness engineering, with a clean formalization of harness evolution as adaptive empirical optimization.
- **Transfer-first evaluation design**: single evolve suite per domain, harness frozen and replayed on held-out and OOD benchmarks, every number measured against H₀ in the same evaluation window, and a deterministic-grading domain included specifically to kill the judge-artifact explanation.
- **Two policy families and a cross-strength transfer test** (harness evolved with Gemini 3.5 Flash helping Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite by +30.4% relative) — evidence the output is a reusable mechanism rather than a policy-specific artifact, which is exactly the property RSI claims to want.
- **Efficiency co-benefit is measured, not asserted**: tokens/trial and trajectory length reported for every arm; RRSI is Pareto-dominant over all four baselines (cheaper *and* better OOD), with the honest caveat that no evolved harness is as cheap as doing nothing.
- **Unusually complete transparency**: released round-by-round records, accepted/rejected diffs with reasons (Table 6), and full hyperparameter tables — the kind of trail that makes the regularization behavior auditable rather than anecdotal.

## Limitations

- **Frozen-backbone only**: the study does not touch settings where model weights are updated during evolution, so "RSI" here is strictly harness-level.
- **One evolve environment per domain**: all conclusions about transfer rest on a single suite per domain (Terminal-Bench 2.1, Harvey LAB, EngDesign); the authors themselves note broader validation across substantially different architectures, tool ecosystems and longer-running self-improvement processes is still needed.
- **Hyperparameter dependence**: seven regularizers introduce b_min/b_max, w, m_draft, n_prune, δ, β₀, β₁ and the within-band weights w_s/w_c/w_n; effectiveness may depend on feedback-signal quality and chosen search budget, and no per-hyperparameter sensitivity is given.
- **Group-level ablation in one domain only**; per-regularizer contributions and the minimal effective regularizer set remain unknown.
- **Judge-graded share of evidence**: the biggest OOD gains come from LLM-judged benchmarks, partially offset (not eliminated) by the deterministic engineering and coding results.
- **Diminishing returns near the ceiling**: under the stronger Claude Opus 4.8 the SWE-bench transfer gain shrinks to +1.8 vs +2.2 for Gemini 3.5 Flash, and the paper notes a stronger policy leaves less room — gains are largest where the base harness is weakest.

## Relevance

这篇论文直击当前 agent 工程的核心痛点：harness/脚手架的自动进化（与 AutoHarness、skill 进化、memory 进化一脉相承）到底是在学到可复用机制还是在过拟合评测集——它给出的"正则化搜索轨迹而非限制编辑空间"的框架和 7 项可直接移植的具体机制（编辑预算退火、泄漏筛查、噪声底板、成本-增益接受规则、结构剪枝），对任何在做 agent 自我改进或自动化 harness 优化的工作都有即取即用的价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Which single regularizer accounts for most of the +3.9-point OOD improvement over unregularized evolution — is a minimal variant (noise floor + leakage screening only, nothing else) within a point of full RRSI?
- How does δ calibration scale with k trials per task — does the noise floor remain sufficient at k=1, or does the winner-selection bias under adaptive reuse grow with more evolution rounds (T ≫ 20)?
- Do the gains survive a change of harness *family* (e.g., a planner-executor or multi-agent harness instead of the Terminus-2 ReAct loop), or is the learned mechanism entangled with H₀'s architecture?
- Can the judged-benchmark gains be replicated under adversarial judging (judge model different from any used during evolution), and does the leakage critic catch subtler leakage than task names and literal answers (e.g., distribution-level stylistic fitting)?
- What happens when the evolve set is refreshed periodically mid-run (the adaptive-data-analysis ideal), and how much of the regularization benefit persists once the search can no longer memorize a fixed finite set?
