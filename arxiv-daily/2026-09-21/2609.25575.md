> **一句话总结：** 针对微调 LLM 作为策略驱动树搜索做形式化定理证明时，SFT 交叉熵（CE）目标与部署时"有限算力下搜索成功"这一真实目标错位的问题，作者把搜索形式化为"配置动力学"（frontier + memory + budget），提出 Compute-Aligned Training（CAT）：把搜索限制在已示范证明轨迹可达的状态上、将偏离折叠为 trace-miss 事件，证明 CAT 梯度恰为逐 tactic CE 梯度的标量加权（权重 ω̃_t = (p_t/J̃)·∂J̃/∂p_t）；同时提出与具体搜索算法无关的 Uniform-Allocation（UA）损失 ∏_t[1-(1-p_t)^(N/L)]，给每个演示步分配均匀局部算力。理论刻画了轨迹近似误差的 bypass（替代证明压低真实权重，Theorem 1）与 trap（离轨支路吞噬算力抬高真实权重，Theorem 2）两个通道，并证明算力 N→∞ 时 search-aware 权重误差趋于 0 而 CE 误差趋于 1（Theorem 3）。在 Lean 4（LeanDojo mathlib4 划分）上用 Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct + LoRA 微调，458 个 2–5 步保留定理、N=256 展开预算下：Pass@N 策略 CAT 26.6% vs CE 21.2%（+5.4pt，p<0.05），UA 在 6 个策略中 5 个取得最高成功率（如 BFS 26.9% vs CE 23.8%）；配对 McNemar 分析显示 BFS 增益从 N=16 的 +4.4pt 增长到 N=256 的 +6.9pt（p<0.001），且 CAT 对 CE 的增益随测试算力增大——即该对齐收益随 test-time compute 扩展。

> **TL;DR:** Fine-tuning LLMs with cross-entropy (CE) misaligns with deployment inside tree search: success depends on compute allocated across intermediate states that the single demonstrated proof never reveals. The paper formalizes policy-guided search as configuration dynamics and derives Compute-Aligned Training (CAT) losses — provably weighted sums of per-tactic CE gradients — plus a search-agnostic Uniform-Allocation (UA) loss. On Lean 4 (leandojo-benchmark-4-random, 458 held-out 2–5-step mathlib4 theorems, Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct + LoRA, N=256 tactic expansions), CAT lifts Pass@N success to 26.6% vs the epoch-matched CE control's 21.2% (+5.4 pts, p<0.05), while a single shared UA adapter is the best or tied-best objective in 5 of 6 deployed strategies (e.g., BFS 26.9% vs CE 23.8%). Paired McNemar sweeps show the CAT-over-CE gain grows with budget — BFS +4.4 pts at N=16 vs +6.9 pts at N=256 (p<0.001) — and theory proves CE's misalignment worsens as compute N→∞. Compute-aware SFT is cheap (one extra reweighted epoch) and scales with test-time compute.

## 核心贡献

- **把"搜索对齐训练"形式化为配置动力学 + 轨迹支持代理**：搜索算法 A 由三元组 (选择规则 S_A, 正更新 U_A^+, 负更新 U_A^-) 完全刻画，状态为 ⟨frontier, memory⟩ 加预算。真实目标 J^A(θ)（有限算力内发现证明的概率）含离轨项、无法从单条演示轨迹估计；作者将其限制在轨迹可达状态上，把一切偏离折叠为"trace-miss"，得到可精确求解的 CAT 代理递归（Eq. 5）。
- **CAT 梯度 = 逐 tactic CE 梯度的标量加权和**（Proposition 1）：∇θ L_CAT = Σ ω̃_t^A ∇θ L_CE^(t)，权重 ω̃_t = (p_t/J̃)·∂J̃/∂p_t 只随策略与预算变化——任何搜索策略的对齐训练都可以机械地实现为对 SFT 梯度的重加权，实现成本仅为一个额外 epoch。
- **搜索无关的 Uniform-Allocation（UA）目标**：无需指定部署算法，把预算 N 均分给 L 个演示步（每步局部尝试 K=N/L 次），J̃^UA = ∏_t[1-(1-p_t)^K]；N=L 时退化为 CE。一个共享 UA adapter 可跨 6 种搜索策略通用，为"不绑定具体搜索"的通用对齐提供了 baseline。
- **轨迹近似误差的双通道刻画（理论部分）**：bypass 通道——未观测的替代证明使真实部署权重被系统性压低（Theorem 1: u_t^Pass@N ≤ π̄^N < 1 = ω_t^CE）；trap 通道——离轨支路每次 miss 消耗 κ 次展开，真实权重被夹在 [w̃_t(N), w̃_t(L+(N-L)/κ)] 内（Theorem 2）；极限行为——N→∞ 时 search-aware 权重误差→0 而 CE 权重误差→1（Theorem 3），即算力越大 CE 越错；并证明两个在全部演示转移上一致的环境可要求不同的精确权重（Proposition 4）——仅凭轨迹无法学到精确目标，这是方法根本性的信息论边界。
- **Lean 4 实证 + 算力扫描**：epoch 匹配对照（控制组多训一个 CE epoch，隔离"仅损失加权"的差异）、配对 McNemar 检验与 95% CI；六个策略（Pass@N、BFS、DFS、RDFS、VDFS、MCTS）下 CAT 与 UA 全面优于 CE，且增益在 N=256 大于 N=16，支持"对齐收益随测试算力增长"。

## Key Contributions

- **A general framework for search-aligned SFT**: search algorithms are modeled as configuration dynamics over ⟨frontier, memory⟩ with a budget; restricting the true objective to demonstrated-trace states and collapsing deviations into trace-miss events yields a tractable surrogate recurrence whose solution is the CAT objective — the first extension of test-time-aligned training from flat Pass@N strategies to branching tree search.
- **CAT gradients are reweighted CE**: Proposition 1 shows ∇θ L_CAT = Σ_t ω̃_t^A ∇θ L_CE^(t) with weights ω̃_t^A = (p_t/J̃) ∂J̃/∂p_t, so any policy-guided search strategy — Pass@N, best-first (BFS), DFS variants, MCTS — induces a mechanically derivable per-tactic weighting; alignment costs one extra training epoch over SFT.
- **A search-agnostic uniform-allocation (UA) loss**: assigning each of L demonstrated steps a uniform local budget K = N/L gives J̃^UA = ∏_t[1-(1-p_t)^K], a single objective usable across arbitrary deployed searches (recovering CE at N=L); a shared UA adapter transfers across all six evaluated strategies.
- **Two-channel characterization of trace approximation error**: the bypass channel (unobserved alternative proofs deflate true weights; Theorem 1) and the trap channel (off-trace excursions inflate them; Theorem 2 bracketing) bound how wrong the trace-supported weights can be; Theorem 3 proves the search-aware weight error vanishes as N→∞ while CE's error tends to one — CE becomes *more* misaligned the more test-time compute you add. Proposition 4 shows two environments agreeing on every demonstrated transition can require different exact weights, so trace-only training can never be exact.
- **Empirical validation in Lean 4 with rigorous controls**: epoch-matched CE controls (isolating the loss-reweighting effect), paired McNemar tests with 95% CIs, and a budget sweep showing gains grow with test-time compute.

## Methodology

**Setup.** The LLM policy π_θ generates tactics in an Interactive Theorem Prover (Lean 4). Standard SFT minimizes per-trace cross-entropy, L_CE = -Σ_t log p_t where p_t = π_θ(y_t* | x_{t-1}). At test time the policy is deployed inside a search algorithm A with a compute budget; the true objective J^A(θ) is the probability A finds a kernel-verified proof within budget. Expanding Eq. 2 along the demonstrated trace splits the tactic sum into demonstrated / valid off-trace / invalid sets — but the dataset supplies neither off-trace probabilities nor continuations, so the exact objective is un estimable from one trace.

**Trace-supported surrogate (CAT).** Restrict search to configurations reachable from the demonstrated states (x_0,...,x_L); replace the true transition by an offline one where any deviation collapses to a single trace-miss event (Eq. 4). The induced recurrence (Eq. 5) has a unique solution; Proposition 1 proves (i) the surrogate depends on θ only through p = (p_1,...,p_L) and (ii) the CAT gradient is a weighted sum of per-step CE gradients with weights ω̃_t^A = (p_t/J̃) ∂J̃/∂p_t. Instantiations: Pass@N yields ω̃_t = Nπ_proof(1-π_proof)^(N-1)/(1-(1-π_proof)^N); best-first search uses a retry surrogate (singleton frontier, retries until the demonstrated tactic is sampled or budget dies), giving J̃^BFS = P(S_L ≤ N) where S_L is a sum of Geometric(p_t) samples.

**Uniform-Allocation (UA).** A search-agnostic alternative: allocate each of the L steps an equal local quota K = N/L and require every step to succeed within its quota (Eq. 8), with weights ω̃_t^UA = K p_t(1-p_t)^(K-1)/(1-(1-p_t)^K). At N = L it recovers CE exactly; it is "search-agnostic but not compute-agnostic."

**Approximation-error analysis.** Missing off-trace information enters through two channels: *bypass* (a different tactic may prove the theorem without completing τ*; accounting for it can only reduce deployed weights relative to the surrogate — Theorem 1) and *trap* (a valid but unhelpful tactic can burn κ ≥ 1 expansions before search returns to the trace state; under deterministic excursions the true weight is bracketed between w̃_t(N) and w̃_t(L+(N-L)/κ) — Theorem 2). Large-budget limits (Theorem 3): with recoverable excursions both deployed and surrogate weights → 0, so weight error → 0 for CAT while CE's error → 1; with per-step permanent absorption probability q_s, the trap weight error saturates at ω_t = q_t/(p_t + (1-p_t)q_t).

**Experiments.** Lean 4 via LeanDojo on `leandojo-benchmark-4-random` (a random split of mathlib4); the alignment stage consumes 40,660 traced proofs (76,003 state-tactic pairs); the held-out evaluation pool is 458 theorems with 2–5-step reference proofs. Model: Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct with LoRA (r=16, α=32, dropout 0.05). All models share an SFT warm-up; the control receives one additional CE epoch while aligned models receive one additional epoch under their respective gradient weights (epoch-matched). Serving via vLLM 0.6.3, temperature 0.8, seed 42. Experiment 1 evaluates six deployed strategies (Pass@N, BFS, DFS, RDFS, VDFS, MCTS with Levin-score value estimate) at N=256 expansions with wall-clock caps. Experiment 2 trains strategy-matched CAT adapters per budget N ∈ {16, 64, 256} (≤80 hours on four A100s per model, no wall-clock caps) and reports paired McNemar gains on theorems both models attempted.

## Results & Highlights

- **Table 1 (proofs found, %, 458 held-out Lean 4 theorems, N=256 expansions):** strategy-specific CAT beats the epoch-matched CE control under Pass@N 26.6% vs 21.2% (+5.4 pts, p<0.05) and RDFS 23.6% vs 20.7% (p<0.05); the single shared UA adapter is the highest reported rate in 5 of 6 strategies — BFS 26.9% vs CE 23.8% (+3.1), DFS 23.6% vs 19.2% (+4.4), RDFS 25.3% vs 20.7% (+4.6), VDFS 24.5% vs 21.2% (+3.3), MCTS 25.1% vs 20.3% (+4.8) — while CAT is highest only under Pass@N (26.6% vs UA's 25.1%).
- **Strategy-matched pairing beats generic compute-awareness (Table 3):** the Pass@N/BFS adapter pair reaches 26.6% vs 23.7% (UA) under Pass@N and 25.1% vs 24.5% under BFS — modeling the deployed strategy adds a further, smaller gain on top of generic compute-awareness.
- **Alignment gain scales with test-time budget (Table 2, paired McNemar):** CAT-over-CE gain under Pass@N is +2.0 pts at N=16 (95% CI [0.5, 3.4], p=0.012) vs +4.2 pts at N=256 ([1.1, 7.2], p=0.012); under BFS it grows from +4.4 pts at N=16 ([1.8, 6.9], p=0.001) to +6.9 pts at N=256 ([3.6, 10.1], p<0.001), confirming gains over CE grow with compute. (N=1024 runs completed for only 56–57 theorems and are reported separately.)
- **Theory matches the empirical trend:** Theorem 3 shows the search-aware weight error → 0 as N→∞ while the CE weight error → 1, i.e., CE misalignment is worst exactly where test-time scaling matters most; Theorem 1 quantifies bypass deflation (u_t^Pass@N ≤ π̄^N < 1 = ω_t^CE) and Theorem 2 brackets the trap inflation within [w̃_t(N), w̃_t(L+(N-L)/κ)].

## Authors & Credibility

Adam Ousherovitch and Ambuj Tewari are both at the Department of Statistics, University of Michigan. Tewari is a well-established professor known for work in statistical learning theory, bandits, and optimization; the pair published the direct predecessor, Compute-Aligned Training for Pass@N (Ousherovitch & Tewari, 2026), so the group has cumulative expertise in exactly this niche of test-time-aligned training objectives. The paper is positioned honestly within the SFT paradigm — the authors explicitly scope out informal reasoning, RL pipelines, and non-Lean systems. The theoretical derivations are careful (unique-solution and differentiability conditions are stated; full formalization and proofs are in appendices), and the experimental protocol (epoch-matched controls, paired McNemar tests, seeds pinned, wall-clock caps documented) reflects above-average statistical rigor for an empirical LLM paper. No industry-scale resources are involved; the compute footprint (7B model, LoRA, ≤80 h × 4 A100 per configuration) is academic-scale and transparently reported.

## Critical Assessment

- **Narrow empirical base.** Everything rests on one 7B model (Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct), one corpus (mathlib4 via LeanDojo), and a 458-theorem held-out pool restricted to 2–5-step reference proofs. The claimed alignment benefit is never tested on longer-horizon proofs, other domains, or larger models — precisely where the trap channel (budget consumed by off-trace excursions) should matter most, since longer proofs leave more room for excursions.
- **Modest and uneven effect sizes.** In Table 1, strategy-specific CAT beats CE significantly (p<0.05) in only 2 of 6 strategies (Pass@N, RDFS); UA does so in 5 of 6. The headline "CAT wins under Pass@N" (26.6% vs UA's 25.1%) is a 1.5-point gap with no reported significance test between CAT and UA, so the paper's own framing ("strategy-specific structure provides additional benefit") is the weaker of its two empirical claims. The consistent, well-supported finding is really "UA > CE everywhere" and "gains grow with budget."
- **The BFS surrogate changes the deployed algorithm.** The trace-supported BFS model replaces finite per-pop sampling with *retries until the demonstrated tactic is sampled* on a singleton frontier — removing score-based competition entirely. The trained objective is therefore only loosely coupled to actual BFS behavior at deployment; the paper admits this is a modeling choice, but never measures how far deployed BFS deviates from the surrogate.
- **Honest but unresolvable identifiability gap.** Proposition 4 (two environments agreeing on every demonstrated transition can require different exact weights) is admirably candid — trace-only training fundamentally cannot capture alternative proofs or recovery costs. But it also means Theorem 1/2's bounds are for a family of models that can never be validated against the real objective; no empirical attempt is made to estimate bypass/trap parameters (κ, q_s) even on the training set.
- **Missing baselines and ablations.** No comparison against the one prior test-time-aligned method (DCO, Chen et al. 2025) in its compatible Pass@N setting, no RL or rejection-sampling baseline, no ablation over LoRA capacity or number of alignment epochs, and a single fixed seed (42) for the sampling stream. The N=1024 scaling point was abandoned (only 56–57 theorems completed), so the "scales with compute" story rests on three budgets with overlapping CIs at N=16–64 for Pass@N (+2.0 vs +4.4 with CIs [0.5,3.4] and [1.8,6.9]).
- **Absolute performance is far from the frontier.** ~21–27% success on 2–5-step mathlib theorems is a controlled-science regime, not a SOTA-proving regime; nothing here speaks to whether CAT/UA helps systems like LeanProver at competition difficulty.

## Strengths

- Clean, reusable formalism: the (S_A, U_A^+, U_A^-) triple plus Proposition 1 turns "align training with your search algorithm" into mechanically derivable per-tactic CE reweightings — immediately applicable to any branching strategy with one extra epoch.
- The UA loss is a genuinely practical contribution: one adapter, no knowledge of the deployed searcher, and the best-or-tied objective in 5 of 6 strategies — a strong default for practitioners who will try multiple searches.
- The theory does real work: Theorem 3 explains *why* gains grow with budget (CE's misalignment worsens with compute) and the bypass/trap decomposition gives directional predictions (trace-supported weights overestimate under bypass, underestimate under traps).
- Exemplary experimental hygiene for the claims made: epoch-matched controls isolate the loss-weighting effect; paired McNemar tests with 95% CIs; explicit documentation of wall-clock caps, seeds, and the abandoned N=1024 runs; failure = unsolved-within-budget with no exclusions.
- Honest limitation section: the authors themselves flag trace-only supervision, single-model scope, and the fixed uniform allocation as open refinements.

## Limitations

- Single model (7B), single benchmark (mathlib4), short proofs only (2–5 steps), one seed — generalization across scale, domain, and proof length is untested.
- Trace-supported objectives are provably not exact (Proposition 4): alternative proofs and recovery costs are structurally invisible, and no mechanism (e.g., search rollouts during training) is introduced to close the gap — the authors defer this to future work.
- CAT's advantage over UA is thin: significant over CE in only 2/6 strategies, no CAT-vs-UA significance tests, and the strategy-matched gain (Table 3) is ~1–2 points.
- The BFS instantiation's retry surrogate alters deployed semantics (no frontier competition), and the mapping from trained weights to actual best-first behavior is never validated empirically.
- Wall-clock caps in Experiment 1 (capped runs count as failures) could differentially penalize configurations, though Experiment 2 removes caps at the cost of dropping to 408 paired theorems at N=256.
- Evaluation budgets conflate tactic samples with kernel-verification costs only via wall-clock caps; the "expansion" abstraction ignores that different strategies spend expansions at very different wall-clock rates (acknowledged via the caps, but not resolved).

## Relevance

这篇论文与你关注的"LLM 推理/训练目标与 test-time compute 扩展"方向直接相关：它把 inference-aware fine-tuning 从 best-of-N 推广到树搜索，给出了可复用的"按部署搜索重加权 SFT 梯度"配方（尤其是不绑定算法的 UA 损失），且理论结果（CE 错位随算力增大）对任何用 LLM 做搜索式推理（代码、Agent 规划、形式化数学）的训练都有借鉴意义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would search rollouts collected during training (even one-step deviations, as the authors sketch) let CAT escape the Proposition 4 identifiability barrier and capture alternative-proof (bypass) information?
- Do the conclusions transfer to longer proofs (>5 steps), competition-level benchmarks (e.g., miniF2F), or larger/base models where p_t distributions are very different — i.e., where the trap channel should dominate?
- Is CAT's edge over UA statistically real? A adequately powered CAT-vs-UA paired comparison (and over more than two strategies) is needed before recommending strategy-specific adapters.
- How do these reweighted-CE objectives compare against RL (e.g., outcome-reward RL on proof success) at matched compute, and are the two complementary?
- Can the trap parameters (κ, q_s) be estimated from cheap per-theorem search telemetry at deployment, enabling data-driven per-step weight corrections instead of the fixed uniform allocation?
