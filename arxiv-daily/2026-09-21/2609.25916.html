Methodology

  • 1Keep the full quadratic. Second-order Taylor + empirical Fisher gives per-module loss ½·tr(B ΔW A ΔWᵀ). GPTQ/OBQ drop B→I; HIGGS/Q-Palette collapse further to one scalar — Table 1 unifies them as degenerate cases.
  • 2Sharpness result. Any scalar proxy distorts this cost by exactly √(κ(A)κ(B)) (inf-sup tight, Thm 3.1), so proxy-driven MCKP regret is unbounded (Prop 3.2).
  • 3Stage 1. Per-(module, bit) costs precomputed element-wise on diagonal A, B; binary program solved with SCIP. Water-filling intuition: optimal bit gap = ½·log₂(sensitivity ratio).
  • 4Stage 2. Adjacent-layer Fisher blocks kept (block-tridiagonal); a Cauchy–Schwarz cross-term proxy is precomputable with no extra calibration pass; 1↑1↓ swaps ranked, top-100 re-scored by calibration CE, ≤20 rounds — never worse than Stage 1 (Prop 4.2).

Strengths

What holds up

  • Closed theory→prediction→verification loop: √(κ(A)κ(B)) is derived, measured per module across 5 models, and linked to end-task gains — rare in the PTQ literature.
  • Unifying vocabulary: Table 1 re-frames GPTQ/OBQ/HIGGS/Q-Palette as approximations of one quadratic, explaining why scalar metrics fail.
  • Principled decomposition: Prop 4.2 monotone guarantee, plus the Appendix C negative result — cross-layer terms must stay outside the MCKP.
  • Broad, honest evaluation: 5 models × 7 bit budgets, PPL + 6-task ACC, and two dedicated ablation appendices.
Limitations

Where it wobbles

  • Baseline engineering: Q-Palette's condensed quantizers replaced with GPTQ; published Q-Palette and HIGGS never benchmarked; AWQ/SmoothQuant absent.
  • Scale and statistics: 7B–14B dense only; the bound-vs-gain correlation rests on 5 points confounded by model family.
  • Hidden cost: Stage 2 runs ≤100 calibration passes × ≤20 rounds with no wall-clock comparison; no code release.
  • Narrow regime: weight-only, adjacent-layer cross-terms only; no latency/memory numbers; near-irrelevant above ~3.5 BPW.
