> **一句话总结：** 提出 ProCredit：训练时每轮工具调用后重跑任务的可执行验收检查，把“通过检查比例的变化”作为轮级进度奖励，并在轨迹级（组内标准化总分）与轮级（γ=1 无折扣回扣、按任务内所有轮次均值居中）两层分配 credit，使 Qwen3.5-4B 在 AppWorld Test-Normal 上任务完成率达 71.53%，超过最强结果奖励基线 DAPO 的 67.46%（+4.1 分），且证明收益来自“在进度发生的轮次记账”而非单纯加进度分。

> **TL;DR:** Outcome-only RL (GRPO) collapses a long-horizon agent attempt into a single success bit, so all-fail groups yield zero gradient and turns that advance the task are indistinguishable from turns that just query. ProCredit re-runs the task's executable acceptance checks after every turn, defines progress as the fraction passed, rewards the per-turn change (c=0.5), and assigns credit at two levels: a GRPO-style trajectory advantage on the progress-augmented score plus a turn-level advantage on undiscounted (γ=1) returns. On AppWorld with Qwen3.5-4B/9B/35B-A8B, ProCredit reaches 71.53% TGC on Test-Normal vs 67.46% for the strongest outcome-only baseline DAPO (+4.1 pts) and 52.96% vs 49.06% on Test-Challenge; on ToolSandbox it beats GRPO 84.5% vs 81.0% (4B). Ablations show the gain is not from adding progress to the score: GRPO-Φ (same score, trajectory-level only) is 6.1 pts worse at 4B, and a token-level variant loses 8.3 TGC — the credit must land on the turn where progress occurs. Discounting (γ=0.95) costs 3/10/13 TGC points at 4B/9B/35B by imposing a "length tax."

## 核心贡献

- **可验证的过程信号复用现有验收检查**：不训练奖励模型、不需要标注或参考轨迹，直接在训练中每个轮次后重跑任务自带的可执行验收检查，把进度定义为通过检查的比例 Φ(s)（初始已通过的检查不计入，Φ₀=0），查询类操作进度为零、错误修改会撤销进度。
- **两层 credit 分配**：轨迹级优势用进度增广总分 S = R + c(Φ_T − Φ₀) 做 GRPO 式组内标准化（势函数型 shaping，不改变最优策略集合，成功轨迹仍严格高于失败轨迹）；轮级优势用 γ=1 的无折扣回扣 G_{i,t} = R_i + c(Φ_{i,T} − Φ_{i,t−1})，按任务内全部轮次均值居中；两者之和保证相邻轮 credit 差恰好等于该轮进度奖励。
- **主实验全面领先**：Qwen3.5 三个规模（4B/9B/35B-A8B）× 两个测试集（Test-Normal/Test-Challenge）上 TGC 均第一，ToolSandbox 第二环境同向；每配置 3 个独立训练种子报均值±标准差。
- **消融定位收益来源**：同样的进度信号，加到轨迹分（GRPO-Φ）或摊到 token（ProCredit-token）都不涨；γ=0.95 折扣反而掉 3~13 分。论文把折扣的害处形式化为“length tax”（长轨迹的结局奖励被 γ^{T−t} 压低，模型学出 9%~20% 更短的轨迹并丢掉难任务）。
- **失败轨迹分析**：训练中 54.9%（4B）与 38.6%（9B）的 all-fail 组内部存在可区分的进度差——这些组在结果奖励下梯度为零，在 ProCredit 下仍提供学习信号。

## Key Contributions

- **Verifiable progress from existing acceptance checks**: instead of a learned reward model, annotations, or reference trajectories, ProCredit re-runs the task's executable acceptance checks after each turn during training and defines progress Φ(s) as the fraction passed (checks passing in the initial state are excluded, so Φ₀ = 0). Queries leave progress unchanged; incorrect modifications can undo it.
- **Two-level credit assignment**: a trajectory-level advantage standardizes the progress-augmented score S = R + c(Φ_T − Φ_0) within the group (potential-based shaping in the spirit of Ng et al. 1999, so the optimal policy set is unchanged and successful trajectories still outrank failed ones), plus a turn-level advantage on undiscounted returns G_{i,t} = R_i + c(Φ_{i,T} − Φ_{i,t−1}) centered by the mean over all turns of the task's group. With γ = 1 the credit gap between adjacent turns is exactly the earlier turn's progress reward.
- **Consistent empirical wins**: highest TGC at all three Qwen3.5 scales (4B/9B/35B-A8B) on both AppWorld test sets, same direction on ToolSandbox, with mean±std over three independent training runs per configuration.
- **Ablations that localize the gain**: the same progress signal added only to the trajectory score (GRPO-Φ) or spread over tokens (ProCredit-token) does not help; discounting with γ = 0.95 costs ~3/10/13 TGC points at 4B/9B/35B. The paper formalizes the harm as a "length tax": γ^{T−t}R shrinks credit for longer attempts, and a γ=0.95-trained model emits trajectories 9% and 20% shorter than GRPO at 4B/9B while losing hard tasks.
- **Failure-group analysis**: 54.9% (4B) and 38.6% (9B) of all-fail training groups contain distinguishable progress differences — zero gradient under outcome-only rewards, real signal under ProCredit.

## Methodology

**Setting.** Tasks are multi-turn tool-use episodes (action → tool execution → observation) in partially observable environments; an attempt ends when the model declares completion or hits a turn limit h. GRPO assigns every turn of trajectory i the group-standardized outcome advantage A_i; ProCredit changes where the reward is read and how credit is distributed.

**Verifiable progress (Sec 3.2).** Each task has an acceptance check set C_g of size K_g > 0 with binary outcomes. Progress is Φ_g(s) = (1/K_g) Σ_m C_{g,m}(s) ∈ [0,1], counting only checks that fail initially. Progress reflects the environment state, not whether a tool call "succeeded" — a read-only query returns zero reward, and progress is read only during training (never shown to the model at inference).

**Progress rewards and trajectory score (Sec 3.3).** Turn reward r_t = c(Φ_t − Φ_{t−1}) with c = 0.5. The trajectory score S = R + Σ r_t = R + c(Φ_T − Φ_0) reuses GRPO's within-group standardization with S in place of R (Eq. 6). Unlike textbook potential-based shaping, terminal potentials are not zeroed, so final completion is retained in the ranking.

**Turn-level advantage (Sec 3.3).** With γ = 1 the return from turn t has the closed form G_{i,t} = R_i + c(Φ_{i,T} − Φ_{i,t−1}); turn advantages are centered by the mean over all turns of all trajectories for the task (no std division), giving A^turn_{i,t} = G_{i,t} − mean(G). The final advantage is A_{i,t} = A^traj_i + A^turn_{i,t}, and the property A_{i,t} − A_{i,t+1} = r_t means a turn that advances the task earns exactly its progress reward more credit than the next turn — independent of success/failure, so turns differentiate even in all-success groups. The policy update uses the clipped PPO objective with the KL term omitted (following LOOP); loss is averaged over generated tokens, excluding tool responses.

**Experiments.** AppWorld: 90 training tasks, 57 dev, Test-Normal 168, Test-Challenge 417; metrics TGC (task goal completion) and SGC (scenario goal completion). ToolSandbox: 52 train / 21 eval tasks (the rest excluded because the base model solves them or they have <2 milestones). Models: Qwen3.5-4B, 9B, 35B-A8B; three seeds per configuration. Baselines: outcome-only GRPO, RLOO, DAPO, SALT; progress-based GRPO-Φ (same score as ProCredit, trajectory-level only), GiGPO (same progress rewards, milestone grouping, default γ=0.95).

## Results & Highlights

- **AppWorld Test-Normal, 4B TGC: ProCredit 71.53±0.48 vs DAPO 67.46±5.62 (+4.1 pts, the strongest outcome-only baseline) and GRPO 66.32±1.27 (+5.2); SGC 48.88 vs DAPO 43.38 (+5.5).** On Test-Challenge 4B: 52.96±0.50 vs DAPO 49.06 (+3.9 TGC). ProCredit also has the smallest standard deviations in those columns.
- **The advantage narrows with scale**: at 9B Test-Normal TGC is 78.15±2.61 (vs RLOO 78.45 — effectively tied, and the paper concedes margins shrink to 1–3 points); at 35B, RLOO, DAPO, SALT, and ProCredit all cluster around 78 (ProCredit 78.65±0.85 vs RLOO 78.45, +0.2), and DAPO edges ProCredit on 35B Test-Normal SGC (61.23 vs 59.60). ProCredit still takes highest TGC in every other column.
- **Same progress, different credit (the key ablation)**: GRPO-Φ uses exactly ProCredit's trajectory score but no turn-level term, and loses 6.1/3.4/2.2 TGC on Test-Normal at 4B/9B/35B (65.40→71.53, 74.78→78.15, 76.41→78.65) and 5.1/1.7/3.1 on Test-Challenge — adding progress to the score alone does not explain the gain.
- **Credit granularity**: ProCredit (turn) beats ProCredit (token), which spreads each turn's reward linearly over tokens, by 8.3 TGC at 4B (71.53 vs 63.19) and 3.6 at 9B; the token variant falls below GRPO at 4B. GiGPO, using the same progress rewards with milestone anchors and γ=0.95, scores below GRPO at 9B/35B Test-Normal (65.77 vs 72.79; 56.99 vs 75.00) — mostly traced to its discount factor.
- **Discount factor has the largest single effect**: switching ProCredit from γ=1 to 0.95 raises TGC by ~3, 10, and 13 points at 4B, 9B, 35B (i.e., undiscounted is better, gap growing with scale); γ=0.95-trained models produce trajectories 9% (4B) and 20% (9B) shorter on average than GRPO — the "length tax" in action.
- **Second environment (ToolSandbox)**: success rates ProCredit vs GRPO — 4B: 84.5±0.3 vs 81.0±1.0; 9B: 89.3±1.9 vs 85.7±1.8; 35B: 92.3±2.9 vs 89.9±2.6. BFCL single-/multi-turn function-calling stays close to base models after training (no degradation).
- **All-fail groups provide signal**: 54.9% (4B) and 38.6% (9B) of all-fail groups have distinguishable progress differences; failed trajectories in mixed groups reach higher final progress under ProCredit than GRPO throughout training, and output entropy drops after progress turns while the entropy rise after errors is preserved.

## Critical Assessment

- **The headline margin does not survive scaling.** At 4B the lead over the best outcome-only baseline is a clear +4.1 TGC, but by 35B Test-Normal the TGC lead over RLOO shrinks to +0.2 (78.65 vs 78.45) — within one point of three baselines — and DAPO beats ProCredit on 35B Test-Normal SGC (61.23 vs 59.60). The paper is honest about this and attributes it to larger models producing fewer all-fail groups, but it means the method's value is concentrated in the small-model/low-success regime that the authors themselves argue is where progress information matters most.
- **Evaluations are confined to environments with executable, per-fact acceptance checks** (AppWorld unit tests, ToolSandbox milestones). The method's scope is explicitly conditional on being able to re-run checks at intermediate states — a strong assumption that rules out most open-ended agent settings out of the box. The discussion acknowledges this but the experiments offer no evidence on approximate/decomposed verifiers.
- **The progress measure is deliberately crude**: an unweighted fraction of passed checks, treated as equally important and unordered. The authors admit that with one or two checks all passing at the end, the turn-level term collapses to a GRPO-style trajectory comparison — so on tasks with coarse checks the method degenerates. No experiment probes sensitivity to check granularity directly.
- **ToolSandbox task curation is a data quirk worth noting**: of 101 tasks, 52 train / 21 eval are used, with 28 excluded because the base model already solves them or they have fewer than two milestones. Reasonable, but it conditions the benchmark on the base model's weaknesses, and the GRPO comparison there lacks the DAPO/RLOO/SALT spread of the AppWorld table.
- **Theoretical claims are appropriately modest** (optimal-policy invariance under the shaping argument, the length-tax proposition in Appendix C), which is a strength; the γ=0.95 finding is well supported and is arguably a community-service result independent of the main method, directly challenging GiGPO's unmotivated default discount.
- Minor: the turn-level advantage centers by the mean over a task's turns without dividing by std, justified in Appendix H — this choice is defended but the sensitivity to it (e.g., std-normalized centering) is not ablated.

## Strengths

- The core idea is clean and cheap: the signal already exists in the task's acceptance checks; no reward model, no annotations, no reference trajectories, and progress is read only during training.
- The GRPO-Φ control is exactly the right ablation — identical trajectory score, only the turn-level term differs — and it cleanly attributes the gain to *where* credit is assigned, not *how much* progress is measured.
- The discount-factor study (γ=1 vs 0.95 across three scales, plus the length-tax mechanism with measured trajectory shortening of 9%/20%) is a genuinely useful finding for anyone doing multi-turn agentic RL with group-based methods.
- Three seeds with reported std at all scales, two environments, entropy and failure-progress analyses, and worked numerical examples in the appendix.

## Limitations

- Requires intermediate-state access and re-runnable checks; not applicable to settings where success is only verifiable at the end or judged subjectively.
- Unweighted, unordered check fraction is a coarse progress proxy; the method's granularity is bounded by how the task's checks decompose.
- Gains shrink sharply with model scale (essentially gone at 35B Test-Normal TGC), and the paper does not test models larger than 35B-A8B or harder environments where the authors predict the gap would reappear.
- All-fail-group statistics (54.9%/38.6%) are measured on their own training runs; how common such groups are in other domains is untested.
- Only two environments, both app/tool-use simulators; coding tasks or database-state tasks are argued to fit but not evaluated.

## Relevance

这篇论文正面解决了 agentic RL 中最核心的 credit assignment 痛点：结果奖励在 all-fail 组零梯度、失败轨迹无法区分进度、推进任务的轮次与只读查询的轮次同酬。其“复用任务自带验收检查 + γ=1 双层优势”的做法和“length tax”分析，对任何做多轮工具调用 agent 强化学习的工作都有直接参考价值，也提示了在自有带单元测试的任务环境中低成本的密集信号方案。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can an intermediate-state verifier (e.g., an LLM judge decomposing final acceptance into intermediate checks) recover ProCredit's gains in environments without executable per-fact checks, without reintroducing the reward-hacking risks of model-predicted dense rewards?
- Does re-weighting or ordering acceptance checks (importance, difficulty, dependency structure) improve over the uniform fraction, especially on tasks with very few checks?
- At what model scale / task success rate does the progress signal stop adding value, and can curriculum design keep models in the regime where all-fail and mixed groups remain common?
- How does ProCredit interact with resampling-based fixes for all-fail groups (e.g., DAPO's oversampling) — are the two mechanisms complementary or redundant?
- The length-tax argument suggests γ should be environment- and horizon-dependent; is there a principled rule for choosing γ in multi-turn agent RL, or is γ=1 always preferable when rewards are bounded?
