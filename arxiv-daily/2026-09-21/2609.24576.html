First causal study of VLM navigation policies: pre-trained semantics survive fine-tuning well enough to steer a real robot zero-shot.

What do VLM-Based Vision-Language Navigation Models Rely on: Interpreting and Steering Policy Behavior

Makowski · Gode · Nayak, et al. — UTN Nuremberg / ETH Zürich / Inria

Key metric — real-robot success
37.2→74.4%

AwareVLN on a physical robot under fisheye distribution shift, after a simulation-computed activation vector is applied at failure (α=3, first output token). 40 trials · 8 scenes · 12 instructions · zero weight updates.

Why this matters

Activation steering moves from LLM lab curiosity to robot navigation: pre-trained VLM semantics survive task fine-tuning and become training-free runtime handles for deployed policies.

⚠ Critical edge

The headline gain is operator-in-the-loop: a human spots the failure and triggers steering — and no retry-only baseline decomposes how much of the 37-point jump is just the second attempt.

Methodology — intervene, stress, steer

  1. Setup
    Four VLN pipelines on R2R VLN-CE val-unseen, all ~64–65% success: CorrectNav (direct action), DualVLN (text → diffusion policy), AwareVLN (chain-of-thought + progress state), NaVILA (hierarchical + controller).
  2. Attribute causally
    ISS — blur image regions or delete/anonymize text, re-run the policy, score trajectory position + angular divergence. Gradient-free; beats Integrated Gradients and TAM against 200 hand-annotated decision points.
  3. Stress-test
    Blur 0–90% of visual memory oldest-first; delete word groups; swap instruction prefixes with antonyms. Open-loop sensitivity kept strictly separate from closed-loop success.
  4. Steer
    Locate concept neurons in FFN value vectors (vs base Qwen2.5-VL), clamp at β=±10; compute a mean activation difference dl on contrastive sim prompts and apply h̃ = h + α·dl at one layer and token.
  5. Deploy
    Fisheye camera + lower mounting as the OOD shift; an operator triggers α=3 on the first output token after detecting failure. 40 trials, 8 scenes, 12 instructions.

Sensitivity ≠ success: AwareVLN has the lowest open-loop sensitivity to memory corruption yet the largest collapse; high-sensitivity CorrectNav is the most robust. Robustness is governed by cross-modal recovery.

  • Concept neurons move behavior even through a diffusion head: clamping “forward+ / turn−” shifts DualVLN motion tokens to Left −19.3% (Table IV) — though it never emits those tokens.
  • Text grounding gap: CorrectNav reaches P@1 50.93% on decision-relevant subtask spans vs NaVILA 34.26%; NaVILA has almost no zero-ISS trajectories (2.78%) — strongest evidence of dependence on active-subtask text.

Strengths

  • Genuinely causal: interventions on inputs and internals, random-vector controls, matched simulator states, and a clean open-loop/closed-loop separation.
  • Breadth: four heterogeneous SOTA pipelines (direct-action, diffusion, chain-of-thought, hierarchical) under one protocol → architecture-linked explanations.
  • Intellectual honesty: reports its own ~40–60% alignment with human-relevant evidence and the absence of any sensitivity–success correlation.
  • Practical payoff: a steering vector computed entirely in simulation transfers zero-shot to a physical robot under camera distribution shift.
  • Clear writing and well-designed figures (instruction-focus and memory/progress heatmaps, per-layer concept survival).

Limitations

  • Steering needs a human operator to detect failure and trigger the intervention — no introspective or automatic trigger exists yet.
  • Small open-loop samples: 200 / 100 annotated points, 12 memory episodes, 5 clamping episodes; annotation reliability is unquantified (no inter-annotator agreement reported).
  • All closed-loop numbers come from one benchmark subset (R2R VLN-CE val-unseen); no RxR, CVDN, or other robots.
  • No random-vector control on the real robot — the gain is not decomposed against a retry-only baseline.
  • Value-vector clamping is shown to bias motion tokens, not task success; concept survival verified only on DualVLN / Qwen2.5-VL.
  • The ~40–60% precision ceiling is left unresolved — could be model shortcuts or human under-annotation; no code release explicitly promised.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Can the operator be replaced by an introspective trigger — ISS-like discrepancy signals, or the reasoning-frequency drop (12% → 3%) as an online failure detector?
  2. Would a retry-only baseline (re-running the unmodified policy after failure) close part of the 37.2% → 74.4% gap — and how does steered recovery compare across re-attempts?
  3. Why does only ~40–60% of ISS attribution land inside human annotations — overfitting and shortcuts, or human under-annotation of affordances, depth, and walls? A probing study could discriminate.
  4. Does clamping improve task success rather than just token statistics, and do steering vectors transfer across architectures sharing the same base VLM?
  5. Can activation steering recover other suppressed behaviors under shift (e.g., re-grounding to visual memory in NaVILA’s cold start), with α tuned online?
