Critical edgeread with care

The +25.0 headline misses the 0.05 bar (p = 0.061, CI spans 6.5×) and rests on 14 panel tasks drawn from the very population the policies were authored on — imprecise, and circular by design.

01

Methodology

Terminal-Bench 2.1 · Codex CLI v0.146.0 · tiers Luna < Terra < Sol
Setting
  • 87* tasks in Harbor containers with a pinned dataset digest (*2 excluded: pinned images could not install the agent).
  • Three GPT-5.6 routes — Luna, Terra, Sol — in increasing success and price, at medium reasoning effort.
Policy anatomy · P = (E, G, A, R, S)

E → eligibility, task text read once (lexical)
G → runtime predicate on action history
A → intervene: instruct injection or deny action
R → release on correct behavior · S → nudge cap

Fires at UserPromptSubmit / PreToolUse / Stop hooks; model weights and the user prompt are untouched. A scoring gate refuses attempts lacking pre-tool-use hook events (anti-cheat).

Portfolio mining & freeze
  • Every failed attempt of policy-free runs inspected → registered chains: state X → intervention → behavior B → verifier condition.
  • 13 earlier configurations retired or narrowed; frozen Terra portfolio: 8 families / 11 rules, SHA-256-pinned, released on HuggingFace.
Four designs, strongest claim first
  • Five-arm randomized panel — real vs sham vs always-verify vs reconsider vs baseline; 300 attempts, randomized schedule, prespecified primary contrast (real − sham).
  • Matched screens — one policy replicated in Sol and Luna (7 service tasks × 3 attempts).
  • Full suite — 87* tasks × 2 attempts × conditions = 1,044 attempts.
  • Inference — task-level paired differences; 10,000-resample bootstrap + 100,000-permutation sign-flip tests.

03

Strengths

  • The timing-matched sham — the control most harness papers skip — produces the most informative negative result: firing on 26/28 attempts, statistically indistinguishable from baseline.
  • Reachable-vs-delivered (pass@2 vs pass²) framing yields an actionable systems insight: models and search produce solutions; runtime policies protect delivery.
  • Mechanism evidence beyond outcomes: equal opportunity (24–26/28 everywhere), differential behavior (22/24 vs ≤14/24), coded blind by deterministic extractors.
  • Honest statistics: CI/permutation disagreement reported, non-significant pass@1 deltas printed in full, Terra-vs-Sol contrast explicitly disclaimed.
  • Reproducibility hygiene rare for industry work: hash-pinned frozen treatment, retirement ledger of 13 configs, anti-cheat gate, full artifact release.
  • No retraining; targeted portfolios are cost-neutral (Terra +0.9%, Luna −5.2%).
04

Limitations

  • pass² from 2 attempts/task is an observed rate, not long-run reliability; counts are small and the primary CI spans 6.5×.
  • Panel tasks come from the development population used to author the portfolio — a content effect on that distribution, not generalization.
  • One benchmark (Terminal-Bench 2.1), one harness (Codex CLI), one model family (GPT-5.6), one effort level; portfolios are model-specific.
  • The planned leave-one-family-out ablation was never run — which of the 11 rules carry the gain is unknown.
  • Policy authoring demanded unlogged expert hours; per-point-of-pass² labor cost is unquantified.
  • Broad portfolios cancel their own gains: Sol's fires on 81/87 tasks for +47.7% cost, degrading toward always-on checking.
  • Both authors are founders of Failproof AI, which sells the evaluated runtime infrastructure — structural competing interest.
05

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Which of the 11 frozen rules carry the gain — are all 8 families needed, or do 2–3 do the work? The missing leave-one-family-out ablation would tell.
  2. Does the pass² gain transfer beyond GPT-5.6 and Terminal-Bench — to SWE-bench or OSWorld — given that eligibility predicates are lexical and phrasing-shaped?
  3. Can policy mining be automated (LLM-proposed candidate chains under the same freeze-and-audit discipline), and what is the expert-hour cost per point of pass²?
  4. Binary-equivalence corrected procedure in 12/12 firings yet passed 0 — can a policy detect a remaining capability gap and stand down instead of wasting nudges?
  5. How do interventions interact with prompt caching and latency in production, and does the gain compound at passk for k > 2?
  6. When can deny-based policies lock agents out of legitimate actions, and what inspection guarantees do deployed policies need?
