Key finding

Forgotten LLM knowledge resurfaces after 4-bit quantization because the forget-loss basin is sharp — noise on ~0.1% of weights flattens it shut.

Quantization-Robust Unlearning through the Lens of Retain–Forget Loss Landscapes Interaction

Key metric

13%→54%

Forget-set knowledge retained on MUSE-NEWS: full precision vs. after 4-bit quantization. Unlearning that “worked” silently undoes itself — a ≈4× resurrection with no defense applied.

≈4× KNOWLEDGE RECOVERY

Why this matters

Unlearned models ship quantized; this turns a silent privacy failure into a mechanistic, nearly-free fix — and a transferable curvature toolkit for compression.

Validated across 6 unlearning variants, 3 benchmarks, 2 model families, 3 quantizers.

Critical edge

No extraction, relearning, or membership-inference attacks verify the knowledge is truly gone — behavioral metrics may be measuring suppression, not removal.

Also: SURE is the only quantization-aware baseline, and the headline “up to 8.0%” gain is a best-case framing.

Method

Diagnose the landscape, perturb where it hurts, update only what matters

A diagnosis-to-fix pipeline built entirely from first-order information.

  1. 1

    Diagnose — sharp forget basin

    A second-order Taylor expansion around the unlearned checkpoint shows 4-bit damage scales with the forget-gradient norm and curvature H. 2-D contour maps on MUSE-NEWS confirm a sharp forget basin beside a flat retain one; Theorem 1 (1-D quadratic model) says weights with a high Fisher ratio Ff/Fr hurt both robustness and utility.

  2. 2

    Perturb — sensitivity-guided noise

    Rank weights by ρi = Ff,i/(Fr,i+ε) (diagonal Fisher on a small calibration set). During forget-loss backprop, swap the top τ = 0.10% for their quantizer-grid rounding ŷ = s·round(w/s) — SAM-style curvature regularization that trains on the exact noise deployment will inject.

  3. 3

    Select — forget-critical layers only

    Score layers by Sl = Gl,f/(Gl,r+ε); unlearn only the top-K (10/16 on TOFU-1B, 20/32 on 8B) and freeze the rest, cutting collateral damage to retained knowledge.

AdamW · 6 unlearning variants (GA/NPO × GDR/KLR, DPO on TOFU) · bitsandbytes 4-bit default, AWQ/GPTQ for transfer · 8-bit reported as nearly harmless.

Strengths

Where it holds up

  • Targets a first-order deployment problem: unlearned models routinely ship quantized, and the 13%→54% recovery is a striking, well-quantified motivation.
  • Clean diagnosis→fix pipeline; ablations isolate each component, and the authors honestly report regularization alone cannot fix the trade-off.
  • Broad validation: 6 unlearning variants × 3 benchmarks × 2 model families × 3 quantizers (bitsandbytes, AWQ, GPTQ).
  • Nearly free: perturbing ~0.1% of parameters suffices; layer freezing cuts training cost while improving the trade-off.
  • First-order/Fisher-diagonal-only recipe with complete hyperparameter tables — simple to reimplement.

Limitations

Where it wobbles

  • Behavioral metrics only — no extraction, relearning, or membership-inference probes, so suppression vs. true removal stays unverified.
  • Thin baseline field: SURE is the sole quantization-aware baseline; no random-0.1%-subset ablation, so Fisher-ratio selection is never disentangled from generic noise.
  • External validity: synthetic TOFU and dated MUSE backbones (ICLM-7B, LLaMA2-7B); one model per MUSE dataset.
  • Theorem 1 is idealized: its 1-D quadratic assumptions are never empirically tested; “up to 8.0%” is the best case, typical gains are smaller.
  • Fragility signals: needs the deployment quantizer’s scale rule in advance; no seeds/variance; 10× epoch differences across datasets; no compute-overhead accounting.

Reproducibility is otherwise strong — hyperparameter tables (Tables 6–9) are complete, but no code release is stated.

Open questions

Questions for further exploration

  1. Do GCG-style extraction or fine-tuning relearning attacks still recover content from the 4-bit model?
  2. Does the flattening hold at 2/3-bit, or under activation quantization (SmoothQuant-style W8A8)?
  3. Would random 0.1% noise injection flatten the basin just as well? The ablation is missing.
  4. Can the retain–forget minima distance d be measured, turning Theorem 1 into a predictive tool for choosing λ and τ?
  5. How does it behave under continual unlearning, where repeated freezing of non-critical layers may starve capacity?
  6. Could output-side defenses (e.g. contrastive decoding) remove the dependency on knowing the quantizer in advance?
