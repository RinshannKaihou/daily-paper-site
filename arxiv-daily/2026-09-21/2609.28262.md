> **一句话总结：** 提出 RAMP——一个免求解器/免GPU/免梯度的边缘 CPU 混合精度量化流水线：用输出 logits 上的 Jensen-Shannon 散度（JSD）做层敏感度度量，再用一维 K-Means 自适应发现敏感度断点并按 Pareto 拐点选策略；在 4 种视觉模型 × 2 种 ARM64 CPU 上实现零灾难性精度崩溃（uniform INT8 使 TinyViT 从 97.5% 跌至 23.7%，RAMP 保持 97.3%）与平均 1.81× 端到端加速，并揭示"按层延迟过滤"会因计算图碎片化（QDQ 节点破坏算子融合）反而变慢。

> **TL;DR:** RAMP is a solver-free mixed-precision quantization pipeline for edge CPUs: it profiles per-layer sensitivity with Jensen-Shannon Divergence on final classification logits (One-At-a-Time), then applies 1D K-Means clustering to find natural INT8/FP32 split points — no ILP, no RL, no fixed percentile thresholds. Across ResNet-18, EfficientNet-B0, ConvNeXt-Tiny and TinyViT on the synthetic SuSy-Dataset, deployed on Apple M1 and Raspberry Pi 5 (Cortex-A76) via ONNX Runtime, uniform INT8 collapses catastrophically (TinyViT 97.5% → 23.7% on M1) while RAMP stays near-lossless (97.3%) with a mean 1.81× speed-up (e.g., ConvNeXt-Tiny 167.9 → 94.6 ms on RPi5); the Hessian-based SOTA metric HAWQ-V2 collapses in 4 of 8 configurations vs 0 for JSD. The paper's second finding is a cautionary negative result: filtering out layers with negligible per-layer speed-up fragments contiguous INT8 subgraphs, disabling operator fusion and raising isolated depthwise-conv latency from 7.7 to 16.5 ms (2.1×), invalidating the additive per-layer latency model used by ILP-based MPQ.

## 核心贡献

- **13 种敏感度代理指标的系统评测**：在参数空间（参数量、STD、通道范围 CV、动态范围比、SNR、估计 SQNR）、梯度空间（HAWQ-V2 的 Hessian trace 近似）和输出空间（MSE、NRMSE、MAE、余弦相异度、KL、JSD）三大类共 13 个指标上，用 One-At-a-Time（OAT）协议 + Recall@Top-N% 检索指标，对 ResNet-18、EfficientNet-B0、ConvNeXt-Tiny、TinyViT 四种架构逐一评测。输出空间的 JSD 平均召回 0.766 最佳，而 Hessian 类的 HAWQ-V2 在 TinyViT 上 Top-10% 召回仅 0.333（JSD 为 0.833），SNR（0.045）/SQNR（0.269）等权重统计几乎无效。
- **量化灾难的归因**：以"Top-1 相对 FP32 下降超过 15 个百分点"定义 collapse，发现梯度类指标在 8 个模型-硬件组合中的 4 个、权重统计类在 2 个中引发灾难性崩溃，而 JSD 为 0 个——证明输出空间散度比常用的梯度/权重判据可靠得多。
- **RAMP 方法**：JSD OAT 敏感度剖析 → 一维 K-Means（K=5）分布自适应阈值（替代对偏态分布脆弱的固定百分位 {25,50,75,90}，后者 Silhouette 值为负、会把结构相同的层切碎）→ 按聚类质心生成 K 个候选策略并取 Pareto 拐点；无需 ILP/RL 求解器、无需 GPU 或梯度，单一策略可跨两块 ARM64 平台迁移，平均加速 1.81×，TinyViT 精度近乎无损（97.3%/97.4% vs FP32 97.5%）。
- **计算图碎片化（graph fragmentation）发现**：把逐层加速可忽略的层排除出量化会插入 QDQ 转换节点、破坏算子融合，使被保护的 depthwise 卷积孤立延迟从 7.7 ms 涨到 16.5 ms（2.1×）；"JSD+Latency"过滤消融在 8 个组合中 4 个反而变慢（如 ResNet-18 在 RPi5 上 20.8→49.7 ms，慢 2.4×，只换来 0.5 个精度点），推翻了 ILP 类 MPQ 依赖的"逐层延迟可加"假设。

## Key Contributions

- A systematic empirical study of 13 sensitivity proxies (parameter-space, gradient-space, output-space) for layer-wise INT8 quantization, evaluated with an OAT protocol and a Recall@Top-N% metric across ResNet-18, EfficientNet-B0, ConvNeXt-Tiny and TinyViT on two ARM64 platforms: output-space divergences dominate (JSD mean recall 0.766; cosine 0.726; KL identical to JSD), while HAWQ-V2 retrieves only 0.353 Top-10% on TinyViT vs JSD's 0.833 and noise heuristics fail outright (SNR 0.045, SQNR 0.269 mean).
- An attribution of catastrophic quantization failures: gradient-based criteria collapse (>15-point Top-1 drop vs FP32) in 4 of 8 model-hardware configurations and weight-based statistics in 2, while JSD produces zero collapses — showing output-space divergences are a far more reliable policy basis than the commonly used gradient/weight criteria.
- RAMP, a solver-free pipeline (JSD OAT profiling → 1D K-Means distribution-adaptive thresholding → Pareto knee-point policy selection) that achieves near-lossless accuracy and a mean 1.81× end-to-end speed-up on ARM64 CPUs without GPU access, gradient computation, or hand-tuned thresholds; the percentile baseline yields negative silhouette scores, mathematically fragmenting structurally identical layers.
- Identification of computational graph fragmentation as a first-order deployment effect: excluding negligible-speedup layers inserts QDQ conversion nodes that disable operator fusion, raising isolated depthwise-conv latency from 7.7 to 16.5 ms (2.1×); the JSD+Latency filter variant degrades end-to-end latency in 4/8 configurations, invalidating the additive per-layer cost model underlying ILP-based criterion MPQ.

## Methodology

- **Problem formulation.** Coupled per-layer precision assignment: each layer's weights and activations share one bit-width from B = {INT8, FP32}, giving a 2^L search space. The objective minimizes summed layer sensitivity Ω_i = M(f_i, f̃_i) subject to a resource cost constraint; criterion-based MPQ = (i) a sensitivity metric + (ii) a search/allocation method.
- **Sensitivity measurement (OAT).** Each metric is evaluated One-At-a-Time: quantize layer i alone (others FP32), compare full-precision vs quantized final classification logits (for output-space metrics) or local tensor statistics (parameter/gradient-space). Ground truth for "which layers matter" is the empirical Top-1 accuracy drop of each single-layer quantization; proxies are scored by Recall@Top-N% (precision ≡ recall since both sets have cardinality k = ⌊L·N/100⌋). Collapse is defined as >15-point Top-1 drop vs FP32 (no observed configuration falls in the 8–15 range, making the threshold's exact value non-critical).
- **Distribution-adaptive thresholding.** Instead of fixed percentiles, 1D K-Means (K=5) partitions the continuous JSD scores into clusters minimizing within-cluster variance (Eq. 4); bit-width is mapped inversely to centroid rank — the lowest-centroid cluster goes to INT8, clusters containing extreme outliers stay FP32. On TinyViT, percentile quotas fragment structurally identical layers (negative silhouette values) while K-Means clusters are cohesive (silhouette analysis, Fig. 2b).
- **RAMP pipeline (Algorithm 1).** (1) JSD sensitivity profiling over all L layers on a calibration set D; (2) sort cluster centroids μ_(1) < ... < μ_(K); (3) generate one policy per centroid (everything ≤ μ_(k) → INT8), build an accuracy-vs-latency Pareto front over the K candidates, and select the knee point (maximum curvature).
- **Experimental setup.** Models: ResNet-18, EfficientNet-B0, ConvNeXt-Tiny, TinyViT (classic CNN → modern ViT). Dataset: SuSy-Dataset, a synthetic classification task deliberately chosen because it hinges on subtle high-frequency artifacts that are more susceptible to quantization distortion than ImageNet-style semantics. Hardware: Apple M1 and Raspberry Pi 5 (Cortex-A76) as the two ARM64 evaluation platforms; Intel Core i9-12900KF (x86-64) for profiling only. Runtime: ONNX Runtime with node-level MPQ (FP32/INT8 only, the natively supported pair). An ablation ("JSD + Latency") first filters out layers whose measured per-layer speed-up ≤ t=1 using real on-device profiling, then applies the JSD policy to the rest.

## Results & Highlights

- **Metric ranking (Table 1).** JSD achieves 0.833 Recall@Top-10% on TinyViT vs 0.333 for the Hessian-based HAWQ-V2 (2.5× better at retrieving the most accuracy-critical layers); mean recall across the four architectures is 0.766 for JSD, 0.726 for cosine dissimilarity, 0.706 for KL/NRMSE/MSE, versus 0.045 (SNR) and 0.269 (SQNR) for weight-noise heuristics. KL is empirically identical to JSD; JSD is preferred because it is symmetric and bounded in [0,1].
- **Catastrophic collapse (Table 2).** Uniform INT8 drops TinyViT from 97.5% to 23.7% on Apple M1 (-73.8 pts) and to 69.0% on Raspberry Pi 5, and EfficientNet-B0 to 29.9%/12.2% — only ResNet-18 survives uniform INT8. RAMP (JSD) holds TinyViT at 97.3% (M1) / 97.4% (RPi5) and never collapses in any of the 8 configurations, whereas HAWQ-V2 collapses in 4 of 8 and STD-based selection in 2 of 8.
- **Speed-ups.** ResNet-18 latency falls 15.8 → 5.5 ms on M1 (2.9×) and 62.0 → 20.8 ms on RPi5 (3.0×); ConvNeXt-Tiny 167.9 → 94.6 ms on RPi5 (1.8×, and 41.5 → 21.6 ms on M1); mean speed-up across all 8 configurations is 1.81×. Caveat visible in the same table: EfficientNet-B0 accuracy under RAMP is 92.6% on M1 vs 96.6% FP32 (-4.0 pts) — near-lossless, not lossless.
- **Graph fragmentation (Fig. 4).** Protecting memory-bound depthwise convolutions in FP32 fragments the INT8 subgraph: QDQ conversion overhead and lost operator fusion raise the isolated depthwise-conv latency from 7.7 to 16.5 ms (2.1×), and the JSD+Latency filter degrades end-to-end latency in 4 of 8 configurations (e.g., ResNet-18 on RPi5 from 20.8 to 49.7 ms — 2.4× slower — for +0.5 accuracy points), while both methods show statistically indistinguishable mean accuracy drops (0.50% vs 0.45%).
- **CPU heterogeneity (Fig. 1 & Sec. 5).** Under one fixed ONNX Runtime deployment configuration, end-to-end INT8 speed-up spans an order of magnitude across CPUs — up to ~5× acceleration to a net slowdown; on x86-64 the default ONNX Runtime provider often makes INT8 *slower* than FP32, and switching to OpenVINO recovers positive speed-ups though rarely better than 1.4×, while on M1 conv layers reach 6–15× per-layer speed-up (only a minority exceed 6× on Cortex-A76).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty is modest on the method; the value is empirical.** JSD-on-logits as a sensitivity proxy and 1D K-Means thresholding are standard tools; no individual component is new. The genuine contributions are the systematic 13-metric comparison and the graph-fragmentation negative result. Notably, JSD is *not* empirically superior to KL (identical recall everywhere, and cosine at 0.726 is close); the choice is justified by symmetry/boundedness, so the "JSD is the answer" framing slightly oversells a family-level finding.
- **"Completely avoids performance degradation" is an overclaim.** Under RAMP, EfficientNet-B0 drops 96.6% → 92.6% (-4.0 pts) on Apple M1. Zero *collapses* holds only under the authors' 15-point collapse definition — a threshold they admit is arbitrary (defended by noting no configuration falls in the 8–15 range on this dataset). Readers should treat the headline as "no catastrophic failures," not "lossless."
- **Single-dataset evaluation.** All accuracy results come from the synthetic SuSy-Dataset; there is no ImageNet or natural-image validation and no task beyond classification. The motivation (high-frequency artifacts are quantization-sensitive) is reasonable and the 62.5% FP32 ResNet-18 baseline shows the task is hard, but the metric ranking and collapse behavior may not transfer to natural-image semantics or dense prediction tasks.
- **Baseline scope is narrower than the framing suggests.** HAWQ-V2 is evaluated only as a sensitivity *metric* inside the authors' own pipeline, not as a full competing MPQ system; there is no end-to-end comparison against an actual ILP-based or RL-based MPQ method, so "previous studies' criteria fail" claims concern proxies, not complete published pipelines. The dismissal of search-based methods on cost grounds is justified but unquantified.
- **OAT profiling ignores inter-layer error accumulation** under joint quantization — the authors acknowledge this — meaning the policy could mis-rank layers whose errors compound, rescued only empirically by the end-to-end results. The knee-point selection also still requires on-device measurement of K=5 candidate policies: cheap, but "without any on-device measurement" applies only to the final policy transfer, not the selection step.
- **Hardware scope.** Binary INT8/FP32 only, coupled weights+activations, ONNX Runtime only, and two ARM64 SoCs evaluated end-to-end — one of which (Apple M1) is generous to call an "edge" CPU; the x86-64 findings are profiling-only. K=5 and the knee-point rule are hyperparameters with no ablation over K.

## Strengths

- A rigorous, transparent evaluation protocol: OAT ground truth, Recall@Top-N% retrieval metric with precision≡recall equivalence justified, silhouette analysis for clustering quality, and an explicit, defended collapse criterion.
- A practically valuable negative result: graph fragmentation is quantified precisely (7.7 → 16.5 ms per isolated depthwise conv; 4/8 configs degraded by latency-aware filtering), directly challenging the additive per-layer latency assumption in ILP-based MPQ — actionable for ONNX Runtime/TVM-style tooling.
- The method is genuinely deployable: solver-free, gradient-free, GPU-free, one policy transferring unchanged across two ARM64 platforms (near-lossless TinyViT at 97.3%/97.4% vs 97.5% FP32).
- Honest scoping and reporting: x86-64 anomalies (INT8 slower than FP32 under the default provider) are reported and analyzed rather than hidden; FP32/INT8/reference numbers are all tabulated; code is released.

## Limitations

- All accuracy evidence comes from one synthetic dataset (SuSy) and classification only; no ImageNet-scale or dense-prediction validation.
- Binary precision set (INT8 vs FP32) with coupled weights/activations; no INT4 or multi-bit regimes, no per-channel granularity.
- No end-to-end comparison against competing MPQ pipelines (ILP/RL-based); HAWQ-V2 enters only as a metric inside the authors' own framework.
- OAT sensitivity ignores inter-layer error accumulation; K=5 and the knee-point rule are un-ablated hyperparameters.
- Small hardware sample (two ARM64 SoCs end-to-end); Apple M1 stretches the "edge" label; x86-64 conclusions rest on profiling rather than deployment.
- The policy selection step still requires measuring K candidate policies on-device (accuracy + latency), which is lightweight but not measurement-free.

## Relevance

该工作完整覆盖了从敏感度指标选择到量化策略生成的无求解器边缘部署方案，直接服务于"无 GPU 的边缘 CPU 视觉模型推理加速"这一高效推理方向，方法便宜、可复现且跨平台迁移。其关于计算图碎片化推翻逐层可加延迟假设的发现，对量化工具链（ONNX Runtime/TVM 类）的代价建模和算子融合设计有直接的指导意义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do the metric rankings (JSD ≥ Hessian/weight-space proxies) hold on ImageNet-scale natural images and on dense-prediction tasks (detection/segmentation), where output-space geometry differs from SuSy's high-frequency artifacts?
- How would the cluster-to-bitwidth mapping extend to multi-bit precision sets (e.g., INT4/INT8/FP16/FP32) or per-channel quantization, where inverse-centroid ordering is no longer trivially defined?
- Can a subgraph-level, fragmentation-aware cost model replace the additive per-layer latency constraint inside ILP-based MPQ, combining solver optimality with the paper's empirical finding?
- Does a single JSD+K-Means policy transfer to x86-64 with OpenVINO, and across runtimes (TFLite, TVM), or is per-runtime recalibration needed?
- How sensitive are the final policies to K, to the calibration set D used for OAT profiling, and to joint-quantization error accumulation that OAT explicitly ignores?
