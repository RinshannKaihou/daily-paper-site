> **一句话总结：** 论文证明贪心解码并非精度不变——同一模型、同一提示、同一 GPU 上，BF16 与 FP16 推理在 6 个模型(1.1B–7B)×3 个基准上产生 49–100% 的输出分歧（TinyLlama-1.1B 在 GSM8K 上 59% 分歧），机制上分歧集中于 lm_head 处 top-two logit 边距极小的步（分歧组中位边距 0.039 vs 一致组 5.733，约 150 倍差距）；据此提出边距门控的选择性 FP32 lm_head 重计算（仅在 0.7–1.4% 的步触发），把 TinyLlama 在 GSM8K 上的精确一致率从 41% 提升到 63%（+22pp），延迟开销 <4%，而全局 FP32 计算需 ~2 倍内存/+38% 延迟才达 88–89%，无门控的每步重计算反而更差（54–60%）。

> **TL;DR:** Greedy decoding from the same checkpoint diverges between BF16 and FP16: on TinyLlama-1.1B/GSM8K, 59% of prompts produce different token sequences (exact agreement only 41%), rising to 100% on BF16-saturated Qwen2.5-1.5B/7B variants. The paper localizes the cause: divergence happens at steps with a near-zero top-two logit margin at the lm_head (diverged median margin 0.039 vs 5.733 for agreed prompts, a ~150x gap), while accumulated transformer-body error is indistinguishable between groups. Its best fix — a margin-gated selective FP32 lm_head recomputation (Intervention C, triggering on 0.7–1.4% of steps) — lifts exact agreement on GSM8K from 41% (no intervention) to 63% (+22pp) at <4% latency, versus 88–89% for global FP32 compute at ~2x memory (+38% latency) and a *worse* 54–60% for ungated every-step recomputation. The lift is partial and regime-bounded: +36pp on Llama-3.2-3B but 0pp on BF16-saturated Qwen variants, and it vanishes at batch size ≥8 and under end-to-end FP8 (+1pp only).

## 核心贡献

- **系统性刻画跨精度输出分歧**：在 6 个模型（1.1B–7B，Llama/Qwen/Mistral/OLMoE 四个家族，另有 12B Mistral-Nemo 仅做分歧探针）×3 个公开基准（GSM8K、HumanEval、MBPP）×3 种 GPU 微架构上测量，49–100% 的提示在 BF16 vs FP16 贪心解码下输出不同；一次 token 翻转即可级联——59 个分歧提示中 24 个输出长度不同，平均长度差 34.1 tokens、最大 256。
- **预测性机制**：token 翻转当且仅当某竞争 token 的差分扰动超过其原始 logit 差距；分歧集中于 top-two 边距相对每 logit 扰动尺度（σ_z ≈ 0.026）极小的步。层级追踪显示 body 层 L2 误差在分歧组与一致组间无差别（1.022 vs 1.135），而 top-two 边距相差 150 倍——发散由 lm_head 处的边距事件决定，而非 body 误差大小。四个因果实验（层移植、KV-cache 移植、logit-lens 回溯、Qwen2.5-3B 复现）均定位于 lm_head。
- **五个受控干预检验机制预测**：整数 logit 量化（干预 A）零效果 → 分歧是"值级"而非"比较级"；top-K FP32 重计算（B, K=8）与全词表 FP32 lm_head 重计算（C）效果相同 → rank-1-vs-rank-2 修复即足够；扩大 FP32 范围到 body 反而更差（63%→44%）；无门控重计算引入"蝴蝶效应"新分歧。全部预测被证实。
- **可部署的修复与适用性边界**：边距门控（τ=10⁻³）选择性触发使开销 <4%，在 A10G 上 +22–36pp（L4/A100 上 +12–21pp），但 bs≥8 时 head-only 修复失效、端到端 FP8 下仅 +1pp；跨模型效果似与训练时精度稳定性相关（假设，未直接验证）而非模型规模。给出务实结论：这是部分缓解而非通用确定性保证。

## Key Contributions

- **Systematic characterization of cross-precision divergence**: across 6 models (1.1B–7B, four families; plus a 12B divergence-only probe), 3 public benchmarks (GSM8K, HumanEval, MBPP), and 3 GPU microarchitectures, 49–100% of prompts produce different greedy outputs under BF16 vs FP16; a single token flip cascades (24/59 diverged prompts end with different lengths, mean |Δ| = 34.1 tokens, max 256).
- **A predictive mechanism**: a token flips when some competitor's differential perturbation exceeds its original logit gap; flips concentrate at steps with a small top-two margin relative to the per-logit perturbation scale (σ_z ≈ 0.026). Body-layer L2 error does not discriminate between diverged and agreed prompts (1.022 vs 1.135) while the top-two margin differs by ~150x (0.039 vs 5.733) — four independent causal experiments (layer grafting, KV-cache grafting, logit-lens traceback, Qwen2.5-3B replication) all localize the flip to the lm_head.
- **Five controlled interventions that test the mechanism's counter-intuitive predictions**: integer logit quantization (A) has zero effect — divergence is value-level, not comparison-level; top-K FP32 recomputation (B, K=8) matches full-vocabulary FP32 lm_head recomputation (C) — a rank-1-vs-rank-2 repair suffices; extending FP32 scope beyond the lm_head *hurts* (63%→44%); ungated every-step recomputation introduces new divergence via a butterfly effect. All five predictions confirmed.
- **A deployable partial fix with mapped boundaries**: margin-gated (τ=10⁻³) selective triggering keeps overhead <4% (0.7–1.4% of steps fire), delivering +22–36pp exact agreement on A10G (+12–21pp on L4/A100), with explicitly mapped failure regimes: bs ≥ 8, end-to-end FP8 (+1pp only), and BF16-saturated checkpoints (0pp). Framed honestly as partial mitigation, not universal determinism.

## Methodology

**Problem formulation.** Greedy decoding selects y_t = argmax_v z_t(v), but logits z_t depend on numerical format. The paper defines Exact Agreement Rate (EAR, fraction of prompts with identical token sequences), First Divergence Step (t*), trigger rate, mean gap, and Semantic Agreement Rate (SAR, matching extracted final answers).

**Setup.** Primary testbed: TinyLlama-1.1B-Chat on an A10G 22GB, BF16 vs FP16 greedy decoding, max 256 tokens, fixed seeds, n=100 per dataset (GSM8K 8-shot CoT, HumanEval 0-shot, MBPP 3-shot). Sanity checks rule out PyTorch kernel non-determinism (bit-identical within each precision across loose/strict determinism settings; the 41% EAR persists identically) and cuBLAS GEMM kernel-selection difference (BF16-vs-FP32 compute of the same BF16 weights gives 42% EAR, indistinguishable from the 41% baseline). Replications: L4, A100, FP16-vs-FP32 on T4, head-isolated FP8, end-to-end FP8, eager and FlashAttention-2, batch sizes {1, 4, 8, 16, 32}, plus MoE (OLMoE-1B-7B) and a 12B Mistral-Nemo divergence-only probe.

**Three gated interventions** (all fire only when the top-two margin Δ_t < τ = 10⁻³): (A) integer logit quantization into bins of width τ before argmax, testing whether divergence is comparison-level; (B) top-K (K=8) FP32 recomputation of lm_head rows plus integer comparison — a hybrid correcting logit *values* for the top candidates; (C) full-vocabulary FP32 lm_head recomputation float32(W_lm)·float32(h_t^L), targeting the dominant amplification stage where the d×|V| projection (~135K for TinyLlama) amplifies small hidden-state differences ~5x (pre-lm_head ℓ₂ 0.92 → logits ℓ₂ 4.63). Baselines: greedy FP32 oracle, global FP32 compute (upcast weights, +38% latency on A10G), ungated FP32 recomputation (τ=0), plus temperature sharpening (D) and dual-stream consensus decoding (E).

**Mechanism analysis.** An error-propagation model: per-layer rounding ε_BF16 ≈ 3.9×10⁻³ vs ε_FP16 ≈ 4.9×10⁻⁴ (8x mantissa gap); with Jacobeans ≈1, body error accumulates ~linearly; the lm_head dot product accumulates ~√d, giving a body-to-head perturbation ratio Lα/√d ≈ 0.024 (body ~2.4%, lm_head ~97.6% of per-logit perturbation). Proposition 1 gives the exact algebraic flip condition (a competitor overtakes iff its differential perturbation exceeds its original gap). Four causal experiments (layer grafting, KV-cache grafting, logit-lens traceback, Qwen2.5-3B replication) verify the flip is produced at the lm_head. Per-prompt stratification partitions the 100 GSM8K prompts into gated-and-fixed (25), gated-but-KV-cache-dependent (5), margin-decisive (29, gate correctly declines), and agreed (41) — predicting the +22pp lift exactly (22/100 fixed of 30 gated, 73% within-scope success).

**Statistics.** Wilson 95% CIs, matched-pair McNemar tests (p<0.001) for Baseline-vs-C on the same prompts, 10000-sample bootstrap CIs for headline lifts (+22pp CI [+11, +33]), a 3x replication (n=300/164/257), and TOST non-inferiority analysis (δ=0.05) for task accuracy on Qwen2.5-3B (n=600, p=0.009).

## Results & Highlights

- **Divergence is pervasive**: BF16-vs-FP16 greedy decoding diverges on 59% (GSM8K), 64% (HumanEval), 70% (MBPP) of TinyLlama-1.1B prompts — i.e., exact agreement is only 41.0%/36.0%/30.0%. Across six models the rate spans 49–100%, hitting 82% on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct/GSM8K and 100% on the BF16-saturated Qwen2.5-1.5B/7B-Instruct. One flipped token cascades: 24 of 59 diverged prompts end with different lengths (mean |Δ| = 34.1 tokens, max 256).
- **Gated FP32 lm_head recomputation is the best efficiency-agreement trade-off** (TinyLlama-1.1B, τ=10⁻³): EAR rises from 41.0% → 63.0% on GSM8K (+22pp, bootstrap CI [+11, +33]), 36.0% → 61.0% on HumanEval (+25pp), 30.0% → 55.0% on MBPP (+25pp) at <4% latency overhead (measured +1.4% latency, +11% peak memory on A10G). The strongest baselines are far less efficient: global FP32 compute reaches only 88–89% at ~2x memory (+38% latency), the FP32 oracle needs 100% agreement at ~2x memory, and ungated every-step recomputation (τ=0) *underperforms* the gated version (54–60% at ~2.5x latency). Top-K (B) matches full-vocab (C) exactly at <2% overhead; integer quantization (A) is identical to no intervention (41/36/30), proving value-level divergence.
- **The margin, not body error, decides flips**: at measurement steps, DIVERGED prompts have median top-two margin 0.039 vs 5.733 for AGREED (~150x gap), while body-layer L2 is statistically indistinguishable (1.022 vs 1.135). The gate fires on 0.7–1.4% of steps; of the 30 GSM8K prompts it fires on, 22 are reconciled (73% within-scope success), and the 29 prompts whose margin is already decisive at t* are correctly left alone — exactly accounting for the +22pp lift.
- **Cross-model and cross-hardware spread**: lifts are +36pp (Llama-3.2-3B), +8–10pp (Mistral-7B, OLMoE), +3pp (Qwen2.5-3B), 0pp (BF16-saturated Qwen variants); replications give +12–21pp on L4/A100, with a +3/+14/+20pp spread for the same Qwen model across A10G/A100/L4. The head-only scope vanishes at batch size ≥8 (C's lift +17pp at bs=1 → 0pp at bs=8; global FP32 still recovers +5pp) and under end-to-end FP8 (+1pp), while head-isolated FP8 reaches 100% (TinyLlama) / +56pp (Qwen2.5-3B). Extending FP32 scope beyond the head *hurts*: RMSNorm+lm_head drops EAR 63% → 44%.
- **Downstream consequences are masked by aggregates**: on Qwen2.5-3B/GSM8K, 19% of prompts flip find-answer correctness between formats (9 correct only in BF16, 10 only in FP16) while aggregate accuracy differs by just 1pp (27% vs 28%); semantic agreement exceeds exact agreement (58% same final numeric answer vs 41% EAR on TinyLlama GSM8K, lifted to 72% by Intervention C); Qwen2.5-3B HumanEval execution outcomes agree on 97.6% of problems despite only 26.2% token-sequence agreement. Reasoning-tuned models (DeepSeek-R1-Distill) show 10–15pp lower exact agreement and 1.7x higher trigger rates than instruction-tuned peers; digit tokens are 8x over-represented among flipped tokens; TOST establishes non-inferiority of task accuracy within ±5pp (n=600, p=0.009).

## Authors & Credibility

The team combines an industrial-reliability perspective with academic statistics: Gaoyuan Du, Anam Nawaz Khan, Fnu Suya, and Xueping Li (University of Tennessee, Knoxville — the last leading an engineering-statistics group), Rex Zhou (University of Chicago), Deepayan Chakrabarti (UT Austin), and Xiaoyang Liu (Amazon). The paper is published in Transactions on Machine Learning Research (September 2026) with a public OpenReview thread, which lends external peer-review credibility. Methodologically the authors are unusually careful: they pre-state five counter-intuitive predictions before running the interventions, use matched-pair McNemar and TOST rather than eyeballing n=100 proportions, honestly label their error model as heuristic ("an estimate rather than a bound"), and explicitly flag their own screening metric as an in-sample result. The acknowledgment that an LLM writing assistant was used for language editing only, with claims verified by the authors, is standard and unalarming.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty is genuine but incremental over adjacent work.** Prior studies observed precision-induced output differences (LayerCast, PrecisionDiff, and 2025–2026 kernel-level non-determinism work from Thinking Machines/SGLang), but this is the first paper to combine output-level characterization, stage-wise causal interventions isolating the lm_head, and a targeted margin-gated repair. The differentiation from LayerCast (weight-relevant FP32 casting layer by layer) is concrete: the selective head-scoped intervention is ~4000x cheaper than global FP32 compute and the paper measures this directly (+38% vs <4% latency). The mechanistic story (margin event at the lm_head, rank-1-vs-rank-2 sufficiency) is new and falsifiable — a real contribution.

**Rigor is above average for the genre, with two caveats.** On the strong side: kernel-level causes are explicitly ruled out before attribution, statistical machinery is matched to the small-n design, and the paper repeatedly reports results that undermine its own method (ungated recomputation is worse; broader FP32 scope is worse; two extra interventions fail). Caveats: (1) the central intervention table rests on n=100 prompts per dataset with half-width CIs of ±9–10pp — the +22pp headline survives, but per-dataset differences between interventions B and C are inside noise and the authors appropriately rely on McNemar; (2) the full intervention comparison is never run beyond 1.1B–7B — the 12B probe characterizes divergence only, and 70B+ is untouched, so the deployability claim is extrapolated from small models where a full-vocab FP32 matmul is comparatively cheap; on a 7B+ model with a 150K vocabulary, the per-trigger cost and the <4% overhead claim need re-measurement.

**The cross-model narrative is the weakest link.** The explanation for why lifts range from +36pp (Llama-3.2-3B) to 0pp (Qwen2.5-1.5B/7B) — "training-time precision stability" — is admitted in the text to be a hypothesis that is "not directly validated," essentially a post-hoc label for an unexplained 6x spread. The CVTD screening metric (Spearman ρ=0.90 with the lift) is derived and evaluated on the same five models — the authors honestly call it "an in-sample screening heuristic," but as published it cannot yet be used prospectively.

**Scope boundaries materially narrow the practical claim.** The fix works only for greedy decoding at bs ≤ 4; at bs ≥ 8 the head-only repair delivers 0pp, end-to-end FP8 (an increasingly common serving format) yields only +1pp, and models with FP16-body NaNs or body-dominated divergence need a body-scope remedy that does not exist here. Additionally, evaluation is a hand-patched decoding loop — there is no vLLM/SGLang/TensorRT-LLM integration, so the <4% overhead in a production stack with paged attention and continuous batching is unverified. Finally, the honest framing ("partial mitigation," "reproducibility is not correctness") is commendable, but buyers should note the aggregate-accuracy impact is ~1pp — the value is confined to audit/replay/eval-determinism use cases, which is precisely what the paper claims, no more.

## Strengths

- A crisp, falsifiable mechanism (top-two margin event at the lm_head) backed by four converging causal experiments, not just correlation.
- Counter-intuitive predictions stated in advance and all confirmed: integer quantization does nothing, K=2 suffices, ungated recomputation hurts, and adding FP32 compute beyond the head makes agreement *worse* (63%→44%) — the paper explains the butterfly effect rather than hiding it.
- Exemplary statistical hygiene for small-n inference: matched-pair McNemar, TOST non-inferiority, Wilson and 10k-sample bootstrap CIs, and a 3x scaled replication.
- Honest, precisely mapped applicability boundaries (bs ≥ 8, end-to-end FP8, BF16-saturated checkpoints, hardware-dependent lifts) and an explicit practitioner decision procedure with a cheap single-run screening metric.
- Per-prompt stratification (Table 4) quantitatively accounts for the exact lift (+22pp = 22 fixed of 100), closing the loop between theory and measurement.

## Limitations

- **Scale**: full intervention comparison only at 1.1B–7B; 12B is a divergence-only probe; no 70B+ results, so the cost model (full-vocab FP32 matmul per trigger) is untested where vocabularies and d are largest.
- **Sample size**: n=100 prompts per dataset for the headline table (±9–10pp half-width CIs); only the flagship GSM8K comparison is replicated at n=300.
- **Hardware dependence**: A10G-centric; the same model shows +3/+14/+20pp lifts across A10G/A100/L4, so the headline "+22pp at <4%" is a best-case configuration, and the effective ε is itself implementation-dependent.
- **Decoding and batch regime**: greedy only — no sampling-based decoding evaluated; benefit limited to bs ≤ 4; at bs ≥ 8 or end-to-end FP8 the divergence becomes body-dominated and the head-only repair is insufficient (0pp/+1pp).
- **Not bit-exact**: 37% of divergent GSM8K prompts remain unreconciled, and the ~12pp weight-storage gap (BF16 vs FP16 rounding of weights themselves) cannot be removed by head-only arithmetic.
- **No serving-stack integration**: measured on a custom decoding loop; interaction with paged attention, continuous batching, and speculative decoding in vLLM/SGLang/TensorRT-LLM is untested.
- **Unvalidated hypothesis**: the training-time-precision-stability explanation for the cross-model lift spread, and the CVTD screening metric, are in-sample and not prospectively validated.

## Relevance

这篇论文正中你的"Inference Reliability & SDC Detection"与"低精度/极端优化下的可靠性"两个核心方向：它把数值格式（BF16 vs FP16）这一基础设施层因素导致的模型输出静默分歧做了机制级定位（lm_head 边距事件），并给出可门控部署的部分修复——可视为"格式诱发的 SDC"的诊断+缓解模板，其边距门控、逐层误差传播估计和层移植因果实验方法对硬件级静默错误检测同样有借鉴价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the margin-gated repair extend to sampling-based decoding (temperature > 0), where the argmax zero-tolerance property that makes single flips catastrophic no longer holds?
- Can the CVTD screening metric be validated prospectively on unseen models and hardware, or is the training-time-precision-stability hypothesis directly testable via controlled retraining with different precision schedules?
- Would the gated FP32 lm_head keep its <4% overhead inside production serving stacks (vLLM/SGLang continuous batching, paged attention), especially since the benefit vanishes at bs ≥ 8 — is there a per-request (rather than per-batch) gating formulation that survives large batches?
- The head-only repair fails under end-to-end FP8 (+1pp) because divergence becomes body-dominated: is there a body-anchored checkpoint format (e.g., canonical rounding at save time) that removes the residual ~12pp weight-storage gap the head cannot fix?
- How does cross-precision divergence interact with speculative decoding, where draft and target models may natively run different precisions and token acceptance depends on exact logit comparisons?
