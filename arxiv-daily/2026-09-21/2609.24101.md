> **一句话总结：** 清华团队形式化证明了检索式证据综合中的"证据漂移"现象——在发表偏置(阳性结果被系统性过度发表)下，检索越深，把真实无效(NoEffect)问题误判为"有益"的概率沿一条严格递增的包络单调趋近 1(定理1)；据此提出 DACG-agent，用增量因果知识图谱 + KL 散度收敛监测(准确层) + 过程奖励模型 PRM 在线衰减检测(效率层)的双层停止策略，在 140 条 Cochrane 派生的平衡测试查询上把 NoEffect 准确率从满预算检索(k=20)的 40.0% 提升到 61.4%(+21.4pp)、漂移率从 15.7% 降到 6.4%，整体准确率 61.4%→69.3%，且只平均检索 3.71 步(约省 67% 检索步数)。

> **TL;DR:** On a 140-query Cochrane-derived balanced test set (70 Beneficial / 70 NoEffect), retrieval depth monotonically hurts null-effect detection: drift rate rises from 7.9% at budget k=3 to 15.7% at k=20. The proposed DACG-agent — an incremental causal knowledge-graph agent with a two-layer stopping policy (KL posterior-convergence monitor + an online Bradley–Terry process reward model) — beats the strongest fixed-budget retrieval baseline (k=20) by +7.9 pp overall accuracy (61.4%→69.3%, McNemar p=0.019), +21.4 pp NoEffect accuracy (40.0%→61.4%), and cuts drift from 15.7% to 6.4% using ~3.71 retrieval steps (67% fewer), while the published KGARevion system misclassifies 59/70 genuine nulls as Beneficial (57.1% overall, macro-F1 47.8). Caveat: zero-shot Claude (no retrieval) still scores 67.1% overall, only 2.2 pp below DACG.

## 核心贡献

- **形式化"证据漂移"(evidence drift)并给出定理：** 在标准发表偏置模型下(每篇检索文献以 1/2+b 的概率报告阳性结果、与真值无关)，投票式聚合器把真 NoEffect 查询误判为 Beneficial 的概率 D(t) 服从 CLT 包络 Φ(b√t/σ)，随检索深度 t 严格递增且 t→∞ 时趋于 1(定理1)；仿真证实该漂移会迁移到实际部署的 noisy-OR 聚合器(b=0.2 时 20 步内 71%→97%)。
- **提出 DACG-agent(Drift-Aware Causal-Graph Agent):** 从 PubMed 摘要增量构建因果知识图谱(noisy-OR 边置信更新 + Boltzmann 路径打分)，并配双层停止策略——KL 散度层监测后验收敛保证准确率(命题1给出 KL-能量界)，PRM 衰减层监测证据质量峰值保证效率(定理2证明总体最优 PRM 的轨迹 argmax 与正确率 argmax 重合)。
- **在 140 条 Cochrane 派生测试查询上验证：** 相比满预算检索 k=20，NoEffect 准确率 +21.4pp、漂移 −9.3pp、检索步数省 67%(McNemar p=0.019)；消融显示 KL 层单独即可把漂移从 15.7% 减半到 7.1%、NoEffect 从 40.0% 提到 60.0%,PRM 层再砍 14% 步数并把漂移进一步压到 6.4%。
- **实体接地审计发现关键交互：** 在 172 查询的 302 个头/尾实体中仅 43.0% 能规范化到 MeSH；而恰好是"双端都能接地到 MeSH"的查询漂移更严重(36.4% vs 17.3%,每查询积累 12.1 vs 6.3 篇摘要)——最常被研究的临床实体正是最暴露于漂移的实体。
- **跨 LLM 复现漂移现象：** 在 glm-4-flash 上重建单遍 KG-agent 和 BGE 重排检索管线，NoEffect 准确率分别仅 17.1% 和 40.0%,远低于信念收敛式停止的 60–61%,说明漂移来自文献的阳性偏置而非抽取/排序质量。

## Key Contributions

- **A formal drift theorem (Theorem 1):** under a standard publication-bias model (each retrieved paper independently positive with probability 1/2 + b regardless of truth), the probability that a vote-counting aggregator misclassifies a true NoEffect query follows a strictly increasing large-sample envelope D(t) = Φ(b√t/σ) → 1. Simulations confirm drift transfers to the deployed noisy-OR aggregator (for b = 0.2, 71% → 97% by depth T = 20; correlated evidence, heavy-tailed bias, and asymmetric extraction preserve or amplify it).
- **DACG-agent:** an incremental causal-KG agent over PubMed abstracts (noisy-OR edge confidence, Boltzmann path scoring, energy functional over explanation subgraphs) with a two-layer stopping policy: a KL-divergence monitor that halts once the label posterior stabilises (accuracy layer, Proposition 1 KL–energy bound), and an online-decline PRM that halts once evidence quality peaks (efficiency layer, Theorem 2 population-optimality).
- **Empirical validation on 140 held-out Cochrane-derived queries:** +21.4 pp NoEffect accuracy and −9.3 pp drift vs full-budget retrieval (k=20) at 67% fewer steps (McNemar p=0.019); ablations isolate each layer (KL halves drift to 7.1%; PRM cuts steps 14% and drift to 6.4%).
- **A MeSH grounding audit (302 entities, full test set):** only 43.0% of entities ground to MeSH even after light normalization, and well-grounded queries drift *more* (36.4% vs 17.3%; 12.1 vs 6.3 abstracts/query) — precisely the best-characterized clinical entities are most exposed to drift.
- **Cross-LLM replication of the drift phenomenon** on glm-4-flash (single-pass KG agent 17.1% NoEffect; retrieval-with-BGE-reranking 40.0% NoEffect), showing drift stems from the literature's positive-result skew, not extraction or ranking quality.

## Methodology

**Problem setup.** A biomedical causal query Q = (E1, R*, E2, C) asks whether intervention E1 causally affects outcome E2 through relation type R* (Beneficial / NoEffect / Harmful), answering from RCT evidence. The system maintains a dynamic causal graph G_t whose nodes are biomedical entities and whose directed causal edges store polarity p ∈ {+1, −1, 0}, a supporting-evidence list (PMIDs with per-extraction confidence), and a noisy-OR aggregated confidence s_new = 1 − (1 − s_old)(1 − s_in) (Eq. 2). A causal path's strength is Str(π, t) = (∏ w_{e_i})·exp(−ρk); the label posterior q_t(L|Q) is the Boltzmann-weighted mixture over top-5 paths, q_t(π) ∝ Str(π,t)^β with β = 1.5. An energy functional E(S_t) = −log q_t(L|Q) + λ_U Σ H(θ_e) + λ_V·Viol(S_t; C) favours decisive, certain, well-supported explanations. The system distinguishes *NoEvidence* (insufficient data — keep searching) from *NoEffect* (sufficient evidence of null polarity — a genuine finding).

**Evidence drift (Theorem 1).** Each retrieval step adds papers that report a positive result with probability 1/2 + b regardless of the true effect. For null-effect queries, a vote-counting aggregator's false-Beneficial probability follows the CLT envelope D(t) = Φ(b√t/σ), strictly increasing in t. Robustness checks: correlated successive reports (ρ = 0.5) slow but preserve monotone drift (59%→70%); query-varying heavy-tailed bias b ~ N(0.1, 0.05) reproduces the i.i.d. curve (60%→79%); asymmetric extraction (null results extracted with lower confidence, r_nulls = 0.3 vs r+ = 0.6 — empirically realistic since nulls are under-published and under-reported within abstracts) drives drift to nearly 100% by T = 20. The authors are careful: they do not claim noisy-OR is worse than vote-counting, only not immune.

**Layer 1 — KL convergence (accuracy).** Stop when Δ_t = D_KL(q_t(L) ‖ q_{t−1}(L)) < δ (δ = 0.01). Posterior *magnitude* is rejected as a signal (poorly calibrated, ECE = 0.236); its *stabilisation* is the signal. Proposition 1 bounds the per-step energy change by √(KL), so small KL means the belief state stopped changing. Crucially, a drifted system can also exhibit low KL — KL cannot distinguish convergence-to-correct from convergence-to-biased.

**Layer 2 — Process reward model (efficiency/discrimination).** A 4-layer MLP ([20 → 128 → 64 → 32 → 1], ReLU) over a 20-dimensional graph-state feature vector (six groups: path structure, conflict, coverage, saturation, global statistics, energy), trained with a Bradley–Terry pairwise loss (margin 0.1) on 2,998 training-split preference pairs, reaching 88.1% pairwise-ranking accuracy. Theorem 2 shows the population-optimal PRM's trajectory argmax coincides with the argmax of correctness probability. Online, the agent stops when the current reward drops below the running maximum by α = 0.3 (decline detection, since the full trajectory is unavailable at decision time).

**Combined loop.** Retrieve a batch of PubMed abstracts → extract triples → update G_t → compute path posterior and Δ_t → break if Δ_t < δ (Layer 1), if r(φ_t) < max r − α (Layer 2), or if the 4-step reward range < α_c (t ≥ 4). If the label is NoEvidence, trigger multi-hop expansion and continue.

**Benchmark.** 969 queries derived from Cochrane systematic reviews, paired by review into 656 train / 140 validation / 173 test with no intervention–outcome co-share across splits. An unambiguity filter (structured review metadata label must agree with the relabelled review-conclusion text) leaves 172 evaluable test queries: 140 Beneficial/NoEffect (evaluated class-balanced 70/70) and 32 Harmful (analysed separately because abstract-level synthesis has only 28% oracle accuracy on Harmful). All headline accuracy is on the held-out 140-query test set; extended metrics use the disjoint 280-query union. Literature spans PubMed abstracts 2010–2025. Cochrane consensus labels are themselves meta-analysis products, so measured drift is a *lower bound* against unbiased ground truth — a deliberately conservative design.

**Baselines.** External: E1 zero-shot Claude Sonnet; E2 RAG (10 PubMed abstracts + LLM judge); E3 single-pass KG-agent (identical stack to DACG, no adaptive stopping); E4 KGARevion (published KG-revision system, run end-to-end unmodified). Fixed budgets k ∈ {3,5,10,20}; three structural stoppers (density stability, path discovery, evidence plateau); ablations DACG_KL (KL only) and DACG_PRM (PRM only); and an oracle selecting the per-query best step. All methods share the same Claude LLM backbone and recorded full 20-step trajectories, with stopping rules applied offline for controlled comparison.

## Results & Highlights

- **Main result (Table I, held-out n=140, balanced 70/70):** DACG reaches **69.3% overall accuracy** (95% bootstrap CI 61–77) and **macro-F1 69.1** vs **61.4%** for the strongest fixed-budget baseline (k=20) — **+7.9 pp** with **67% fewer retrieval steps** (3.71 vs 20; McNemar p=0.019). NoEffect accuracy jumps **40.0% → 61.4% (+21.4 pp)** while drift falls **15.7% → 6.4% (−9.3 pp)**.
- **Drift rises monotonically with budget, exactly as Theorem 1 predicts:** drift rate 7.9% at k=3 → 11.4% at k=5 → 14.3% at k=10 → 15.7% at k=20; over the same range NoEffect accuracy peaks at k=10 (62.9%) then collapses to 40.0% at k=20 — increasing k from 3 to 20 does not improve overall accuracy (64.3% → 61.4%).
- **Ablation (Table II, n=140):** KL-only (DACG_KL) **halves drift from 15.7% to 7.1%** and lifts NoEffect **40.0% → 60.0%** at k=20; adding the PRM (DACG) trims mean steps **4.29 → 3.71 (−14%)** and drift to 6.4%, with overall accuracy unchanged (69.3%; the two differ on a *single* query, p=1.0). PRM alone (DACG_PRM) reaches only 65.4% overall / 51.4% NoEffect. Oracle: 77.1%.
- **External baselines confirm the two failure modes:** zero-shot Claude (E1) scores **67.1% overall but splits 91.4% Beneficial / 42.9% NoEffect** — DACG beats it by **+18.5 pp on NoEffect**; RAG (E2) drops NoEffect to 31.4%; the single-pass KG agent (E3) inverts to **98.6% NoEffect but 15.7% Beneficial** (drift rate 0 by construction); the published KGARevion (E4) attains 57.1% overall (macro-F1 47.8) and **misclassifies 59 of 70 genuine nulls as Beneficial**. Meta-analysis trim-fill gets 57.9% / 54.3% NoEffect. All three structural stoppers exceed 50% NoEffect (67.1% overall) but cannot distinguish "insufficient evidence" from "true null".
- **Drift localises in the NoEffect class:** for NoEffect queries the mean posterior q_t(Beneficial) climbs steadily from 0.21 to 0.43 by step 14; 46.9% of NoEffect queries hold a correct intermediate answer at some step but lose it by step 20, vs only 10.0% of Beneficial queries — the agent typically finds the right answer after one retrieval and then loses it as biased evidence accumulates.
- **PRM signal quality:** mean reward −0.082 on correct steps vs −0.368 on incorrect steps (gap 0.287 on test; training margin 2.53; 88.1% held-out ranking accuracy).
- **Grounding–drift interaction (n=302 entities):** 22.5% (95% CI 17.8–27.2) of entities match a MeSH descriptor exactly, 43.0% after light normalization; queries with both endpoints MeSH-grounded drift **36.4% vs 17.3%** and accumulate **12.1 vs 6.3** abstracts per query — more evidence is exactly what drives the biased aggregator toward Beneficial.
- **Cross-LLM replication (glm-4-flash):** single-pass KG agent 17.1% NoEffect, retrieval with BGE cross-encoder re-ranking 40.0% NoEffect — neither approaches the 60–61% of belief-convergence stoppers, so better ranking alone cannot compensate for drift.

## Authors & Credibility

Fred Sun and Shangqi Guo (corresponding author) are with the Center for Brain-Inspired Computing Research, Department of Precision Instrument, Tsinghua University. The work shows strong methodological hygiene for a systems-plus-theory paper: a formal theorem with stated proof assumptions and a simulation transferring it to the deployed aggregator; population-level justification (Theorem 2) for the PRM; bootstrap CIs (10,000 resamples) and exact McNemar tests; a leakage-controlled benchmark split; an unusually candid Discussion section that reports the unflattering baselines (zero-shot Claude at 67.1%) and explains them rather than hiding them. The MeSH grounding audit and the explicit "measured drift is a lower bound" caveat reflect genuine scientific conservatism. No prior track record for these exact authors is verifiable from the paper itself, but the experimental rigor is above the bar for the area.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The framing is genuinely fresh: prior biomedical KG agents (KGARevion, MedKAgent, KG4Diagnosis, BioKGBench) address *whether* to retrieve or build richer graphs, but none addresses *when to stop* under a systematically skewed evidence source, and publication bias is well documented but never formalised as a retrieval-depth failure mode for agents. Theorem 1 is simple (a CLT envelope), but its point — that additional retrieval *increases* confidence in the wrong answer, inverting the usual "more evidence helps" prior — is a useful, quotable negative result, and the simulation honestly maps it onto the deployed noisy-OR mechanism.

**Rigor — real strengths and real caveats.** The evaluation is more careful than most agent papers (disjoint splits, unambiguity filter, McNemar, CIs, cross-LLM replication). However: (1) *The headline overall-accuracy margin over the no-retrieval baseline is thin* — DACG's 69.3% (CI 61–77) vs zero-shot Claude's 67.1% is +2.2 pp, well inside the noise; the paper defuses this by arguing the decision-relevant metric is NoEffect (+18.5 pp) and drift, which is fair, but a reader skimming the abstract should know the total-accuracy win over E1 is not established. (2) *The two-layer story is oversold by the ablation:* DACG and DACG_KL differ on exactly one query (p=1.0); the PRM's demonstrable contribution on this benchmark is efficiency (−14% steps) and drift (7.1%→6.4%), not accuracy — and PRM-only actually *underperforms* KL-only (65.4% vs 69.3% overall, 51.4% vs 60.0% NoEffect). (3) *The reported accuracy excludes 32 Harmful queries* because the abstract-level oracle only reaches 28% on them — defensible and disclosed, but 69.3% is therefore not full-benchmark accuracy, and the oracle ceiling of 77.1% means all methods cluster in a narrow band. (4) *No long-context "read all 20 abstracts at once" baseline* is reported — E2 uses only 10 abstracts — so the strongest trivial competitor to adaptive stopping is absent. (5) The theory/practice gap is acknowledged but real: Theorem 1 analyzes vote-counting while the system runs noisy-OR + Boltzmann path scoring; no closed-form bound exists for the actual aggregator, only simulations.

**Reproducibility.** Good but not complete in this version: PRM architecture, feature groups, loss margin, 2,998 preference pairs, and frozen thresholds (δ=0.01, α=0.3, α_c=0.1) are all specified (Appendix A), and the Cochrane derivation + filters are described precisely enough to rebuild. But several items are deferred to an "extended version" (full proof details, complete feature definitions), and the benchmark JSONs / code release are not stated in the paper, which limits independent verification of the 969-query pipeline.

**Red flags.** Minor: single benchmark family (Cochrane-only, abstracts 2010–2025) with the generalisation to ClinicalTrials.gov / drug labels / non-Cochrane reviews explicitly left open; Cochrane consensus labels inherit the very publication bias under study (they argue this biases measured drift *downward*, which is the right direction for their claim but means absolute drift numbers are uncertain); theBeneficial-accuracy cost of PRM early stopping (77.1% vs 78.6% for KL-only) is mentioned only in passing.

## Strengths

- A crisp, provable negative result (more retrieval → monotone worse on null effects) with simulation evidence that the mechanism transfers to the deployed noisy-OR aggregator, plus robustness under correlated, heavy-tailed, and asymmetric-extraction bias.
- The two stopping layers are cleanly motivated and cleanly ablated: KL = accuracy layer, PRM = efficiency layer; each is isolated in Table II with an oracle upper bound.
- The benchmark construction avoids train/test leakage by review pairing and an unambiguity filter, and headline numbers live on a strictly held-out balanced test set.
- Rare transparency practices: a full MeSH grounding audit with CIs, McNemar significance tests, bootstrap intervals, and a cross-LLM (glm-4-flash) replication showing the phenomenon is not a Claude artefact.
- The grounding–drift finding (well-grounded queries drift more, 36.4% vs 17.3%) is a genuinely surprising, decision-relevant insight: popular, well-characterized clinical entities are the most drift-exposed.
- Honest Discussion: competitive zero-shot accuracy is explained (pretraining absorbed the bias) rather than obscured; "measured drift is a lower bound" is stated up front.

## Limitations

- Small evaluation core: n=140 for accuracy (CI 61–77), so method differences of a few points are unresolvable; the DACG-vs-KL-only comparison is a single query.
- The PRM layer adds no measurable accuracy on this benchmark (its value is efficiency/drift), and it stops too early on Beneficial queries (77.1% vs 78.6% Beneficial accuracy for KL-only); class-aware or asymmetric thresholds are future work.
- Online decline detection (α < running max − α) is a conservative approximation of Theorem 2's retrospective argmax; test-set reward/correctness correlation is only 0.287 vs the 2.53 training margin.
- Single benchmark family (Cochrane-derived, PubMed abstracts 2010–2025); 32 Harmful queries excluded from accuracy (oracle 28%); oracle ceiling 77.1% bounds all methods.
- The entity resolver grounds only 43.0% of entities to MeSH even after normalization (UMLS not used), so graph fidelity is partial; multiplicative polarity composition assumes hop independence.
- No across-time correction: a later contradicting trial lowers a *dominant* polarity but the model never down-weights superseded findings.
- Theory covers vote-counting only; the noisy-OR/Boltzmann aggregator used in practice is justified by simulation, not closed form.

## Relevance

这篇论文正中当前 LLM agent / RAG 研究的一个要害问题:在系统性偏置的语料上,"检索得更多"不仅无益反而有害,而何时停止检索比如何检索更关键。其"漂移定理 + 双层停止策略 + 严格消融"的组合,对任何做多步检索 agent、科学证据综合或 agent 可靠性研究的人都有直接的方法论参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the KL+PRM stopping policy transfer to evidence sources with different skew profiles (ClinicalTrials.gov registries, drug labels, non-Cochrane systematic reviews), where null results are published at different rates?
- Can class-aware or asymmetric PRM thresholds recover the Beneficial accuracy lost to early stopping (77.1% vs KL-only's 78.6%) without giving back the drift and efficiency gains?
- Is there a closed-form drift bound for the actual noisy-OR + Boltzmann aggregator, or does Theorem 1's vote-counting analysis only loosely bound the deployed system?
- The reranking experiment (BGE cross-encoder, 40.0% NoEffect on glm-4-flash) suggests ranking quality cannot compensate for drift — is stopping and retrieval quality strictly orthogonal, or is there a joint policy that helps?
- Would temporal down-weighting (discounting superseded findings when a newer trial overturns them) reduce drift in practice, and can it be done without access to publication timestamps' biases?
- Since well-grounded (MeSH-mappable) queries drift most, should stopping thresholds be conditioned on entity grounding confidence?
