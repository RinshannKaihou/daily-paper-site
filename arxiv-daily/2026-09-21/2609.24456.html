Why this matters

Experience buffering silently eats half of RLlib’s DQN iteration time; a data-plane abstraction for it transfers directly to RLHF and large-payload training pipelines.

Critical edge

The headline 127× gap largely measures CPU-vs-GPU memory-tier placement, and where compute dominates (LLM post-training) the end-to-end gain is just 4%.

Methodology — decouple · place · schedule

ACTOR ▸ EDP [ingest · place · deliver] ▸ LEARNER
  1. 01Decouple via the Experience Data Plane

    Two framework hooks (@conduit.exp_injection / exp_delivery) lift ingestion, placement and delivery out of actor–learner loops. Frameworks keep what to store and consume; Conduit owns where, when, how much.

  2. 02Bandwidth-aware placement

    argmin Ttransfer(p) = x/BW(actor→p) + x/BW(p→learner) over four tiers (CPU pageable/pinned, single-GPU, shared-GPU), using measured interconnects — MI250X GPU→GPU varies 40–200 GB/s, CPU→GPU is 3–7× slower. Pre-solved online migration plans handle buffer growth.

  3. 03Latency-aware scheduling, zero tuning

    Two semantic-free knobs: overlap (run ops concurrently with rollout/update) and granularity (ging, gdel; on-policy gdel=1/M preserves freshness). Feasible ranges derive from standard RL configs; an O(1) cost-model optimizer picks the latency-minimal mode.

  4. 04Semantic safety by construction

    Changes only where/when, never what: staging preserves tuples, on-policy forbids cross-iteration reuse; convergence curves match stock RLlib.

Cost model fit on 224 manual configs: Pearson r=0.990 · MAPE 20.8% · exact top-8 ranking per workload dimension.

Strengths

  • Right abstraction: the buffer becomes an explicit data plane, sharply positioned against built-in buffers (RLlib/MSRL/SRL) and buffer services (Reverb/Gear).
  • Users tune nothing: knob ranges derive from standard RL configs; the optimizer is O(1) — a rare fully-automatic design.
  • Measured, not assumed: non-uniform MI250X fabric bandwidths drive the placement model.
  • Strong negative controls: placement/scheduling ablations; the optimizer is checked against a 32-point manual sweep; model error is quantified.
  • Semantic safety: explicit correctness invariants plus convergence verified on both off- and on-policy workloads.

Limitations

  • Gains are workload-dependent by construction: large when the experience path dominates (DQN 50.2%), marginal when compute dominates (Verl: 4% e2e; on-policy PPO: 30%).
  • Asymmetric headline baselines: 127×/4.1× compare CPU-resident vs GPU-resident placement; MSRL absent from experiments.
  • No RLHF-scale actor fan-out study — per-workload runs are fixed at 8 actors / 8 learners.
  • Thin top-end baselines: Gear/Reverb lack AMD support, so 1,024-GPU scaling compares Conduit only against its own ablation.
  • No artifact release stated; MAPE 20.8% limits portable absolute predictions without re-profiling.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Q1Does EDP extend to fully asynchronous RLHF pipelines where actor fan-out (thousands of rollout workers), not placement, dominates?
  2. Q2Can bandwidth-aware placement transfer to new topologies (NVLink-domain vs InfiniBand) without MI250X-specific profiling tables, given the 20.8% MAPE?
  3. Q3Would GPU-resident placement change replay-distribution semantics (e.g. prioritized-replay refresh callbacks) beyond the current invariants?
  4. Q4Is there a principled crossover point (sample size × capacity × compute share) predicting when EDP-style scheduling stops paying off?
  5. Q5How does Conduit handle sequence-level RL for LLMs (variable-length padding/packing in delivery) beyond GRPO’s 4% end-to-end gain?
