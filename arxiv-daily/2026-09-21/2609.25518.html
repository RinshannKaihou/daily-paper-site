Critical Edge

The headline 5.6-vs-1.95 gap is measured against as-published, untuned leaderboard entries — and on the authors’ own Compactness metric, Integrated Gradients stays competitive.

The paper’s own Critical Assessment: CPR itself is flawed — a random ordering collects ≈ +0.25.

01 Methodology one objective, two tricks

Set-up

Pick a basis H inside a frozen model — attention heads, MLP neurons, MLP+Attn pairs, SAE features, or weight rows. A soft interchange intervention blends each variable between a base and a source run: α·F(u) + (1−α)·h(s), weighted by learned mask scores.

Per training step

  1. Sample budget k ~ Uniform(1, |S|−1).
  2. Bisect threshold ck so Σ σ(sH+ck) = k — a differentiable sigmoid top-k mask.
  3. Run the intervened model; loss = it still predicts the base label.
  4. Backprop through the mask into scores S.

Why “Matryoshka”

Sampling k every step forces one ordering to nest validly at all sparsities — evaluation sweeps the whole grid for free, versus ≈50× compute for Node-Pruning-style multi-mask sweeps.

Theory & RL variant

The first SGD step is exactly a centred, path-reweighted Integrated Gradients; MATr is provably neither complete nor linear, sidestepping the Bilodeau impossibility theorem. Adam’s ε is a real knob: large ε → IG-like scores, tiny ε → gradient signs. For weights: interpolate checkpoints θb + α⊙(θs−θb) and train with GRPO against non-differentiable LLM-judge rewards.

03 Strengths

  • One run, every sparsity: the Matryoshka sweep replaces ≈50× multi-mask compute with a single training run.
  • Theory pays rent: the IG-equivalence derivation predicted — and fixed — the Adam-ε failure mode at neuron/SAE granularity.
  • Exhaustive, honest evaluation: every baseline re-tuned; Holm–Bonferroni tests, seed variance, and a new metric (Compactness) invented to de-game CPR.
  • The GRPO extension moves attribution from explaining tokens to explaining policies — an auditable handle on tamper-resistance.

04 Limitations

  • The 5.6-vs-1.95 headline compares as-published, untuned entries; the baselines the authors did tune close much of the gap.
  • On the authors’ own Compactness metric, IG remains competitive — EG even edges MATr on MIB+ node level (0.62 vs 0.54).
  • Compute-matched, MATr trails EG at equal steps (2.00 vs 2.15) and needs 10× steps to clearly win.
  • Adam’s ε must be re-tuned per basis (10⁻² vs the silently-failing default 10⁻⁸); k-schedule choice trades CPR vs compactness.
  • Demonstrated only ≤8B on largely toy tasks; the refusal result is dual-use, judged by a small gemma-2b judge with no human harm evaluation.

05 Questions for Further Exploration

  • Does the nesting property survive realistic multi-task training mixes, or does one global ordering become a straitjacket when circuits overlap across sparsities?
  • Can the GRPO reward model be replaced by cheaper proxies — activation-based judges, in-context evaluators — without losing the clean refusal/capability separation?
  • Would a Compactness-based MIB leaderboard re-rank published methods, especially the IG family?
  • Is there a scale-free formulation — normalised scores, SGDR-style updates — that removes the per-basis Adam-ε hyperparameter entirely?
  • Does the 1% refusal direction transfer across checkpoints, quantisations, sibling models — and can MATr defensively certify how tamper-resistant a released model is?
