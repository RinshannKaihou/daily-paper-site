ACLArena: Agent Continual Learning in Multi-stage Post-training

Haxin Wang · Xiaoxuan Wang · et al. UCLA & UC Santa Cruz 2026-09-21
Key Metric
25.8 → 6.0

AIME26 accuracy once the E-commerce stage arrives in sequential training — −77% of math capability erased in one stage. NQ falls 45.2 → 14.6 in the same step.

Recovery: +23.3 on τ³-Retail with MLE (29.6 → 52.9)
Why This Matters

Any team training one agent for math, tools, and instruction-following hits capability overwriting. This controlled testbed shows when to distill, replay, merge — or isolate experts — with recipes you can port directly.

Critical Edge

MLE is parameter isolation, not one reconciled weight set — and its router is untested on the mixed-domain episodes where deployed agents actually live.

  • One testbed, fixed curriculum. Qwen3-8B-Base passes through four post-training stages; each pairs in-domain evals (AIME26, NQ, τ³-Retail, IF-Eval) with deliberately held-out OOD suites (GPQA-Diamond, MMLU-Redux, τ³-telecom/mock, IF-Bench, multi-hop search).
  • Diagnose before treating. Weight PCA + task-vector cosines at the model level; teacher-forced token rescoring stratified by entropy (H = 0.5 nats) at the token level.
  • Four consolidation routes, each at its best config: MMOPD (multi-teacher on-policy distillation, new entropy-gated mixed forward/reverse KL), SDFT (offline replay of ~560K filtered oracle trajectories), weight-space merging, and MLE — freeze the SDFT backbone, train one RL LoRA expert per stage, route on the observable environment.

Vanilla reverse-KL distillation structurally collapses on agentic search: truncation ratio → 100%, valid ratio → 0 (the teacher puts advantage ≈ −10 on critical termination tokens). The entropy-gated Mixed OPD fixes it: the student reaches 53.8 single-hop vs its own teacher's 50.1.
03 Strengths
  • Controlled, fully disclosed testbed with genuinely held-out OOD domains (telecom/mock never trained) — rare rigor for agent-ACL work.
  • Findings backed by independent quantitative evidence — PCA geometry, cosine matrices, update magnitudes, truncation rates — not headline tables alone.
  • Mixed OPD is a clean, well-motivated fix: entropy-gated forward KL over top-q-renormalized teacher distributions, at constant extra cost.
  • Fair comparison: every baseline routed to its best configuration, not straw-manned.
  • Strong reproducibility — code, models, data, and hyperparameters all released; three-seed runs with std.
04 Limitations
  • Single model family and size (Qwen3-8B-Base), single fixed curriculum; no permutation or scale ablations in the main results.
  • The router assumes unambiguous environment context; mixed-domain or mid-episode shifts are untested, and multi-adapter serving costs are unmeasured.
  • No method dominates: MLE cedes NQ (40.9 vs 45.2), MMLU (70.9 vs 80.3), and IF-Bench (29.1 vs 30.8) — forgetting is mitigated, not solved.
  • MLE beating its own retail oracle (52.9 vs 33.2) is unexplained — no ablation isolates the cause.
  • E-commerce OOD relies on an LLM-simulated user — a shared but unquantified confounder.
05 Questions for Further Exploration
  1. Can routing survive a single episode that mixes domains (a math question mid-shopping-dialog)?
  2. Do task-vector orthogonality and decision-point forgetting hold at 70B+ scale and 5–10 stages?
  3. How sensitive are the paradigm rankings to curriculum order — does Math-first bias survivors?
  4. Could Mixed OPD distill the LoRA experts back into one weight set after MLE training?
  5. Why does MLE exceed its dedicated retail oracle — and can SDFT cross-pollination be exploited deliberately?
  6. Would plain joint multi-task RL from the start, with Balanced-Mix sampling, beat any staging at all?
