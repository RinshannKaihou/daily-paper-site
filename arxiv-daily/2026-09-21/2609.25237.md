> **一句话总结：** 本文提出并形式化了交付级静默失败 **TBDL（Trains But Doesn't Learn：loss 下降、所有训练期信号全绿，但交付模型不比基线强）**，构建了一个由去循环 oracle 逐阶段审计的"受治理交付平面"（intake→plan→config→schedule→train→eval→register→deploy→cost review→handoff 十阶段），在真实计费 GPU 上对 4 个前沿 LLM 智能体（Claude Opus 5、GPT-5.6-luna、Gemini 3.7 Flash、DeepSeek V4-Pro）做端到端认证：38 个真实 episode 中 **0 次 TBDL 交付、0 次门禁绕过**，但 **31/38 个"指标合格"的交付因违反交付契约被拒**（intake 32/38、模型卡 schema 30/38、成本对账 29/38、registry CI 19/38），即使给 oracle 正确配置也修不好（验收通过 24/24 但仍 22/24 被拒）——失败点在交付契约而非指标或配置。

> **TL;DR:** The paper recasts the agentic forward-deployed engineer (FDE) as a governed delivery problem and names its central silent failure: **TBDL** — a fine-tuning run whose loss falls and whose every training-time signal is green, yet the delivered model is no better than the base. On a real-GPU governed plane with ten oracle-scored stages, correct SFT delivery on BANKING77 with Qwen3-8B lifts paired held-out accuracy from 0.328 to 0.784 (Δ≈+0.48, McNemar p<10⁻⁵⁰), while an injected relabel trap yields 0.038 held-out accuracy with all signals green (TBDL in 12/12 runs across four bases 8B–70B); an anytime-valid e-process detector flags this severe trap in 11/12 runs (median step 250/300) with 0/36 false alarms. In a 38-episode certification campaign, four frontier agents shipped zero TBDL models and zero gate bypasses, but **31/38 of their metric-passing deliveries were rejected on delivery-contract violations that oracle-correct configuration does not repair (22/24 still rejected)**, and two human engineers post lower judgment residuals (0.06, 0.09) than every agent (0.10–0.40). The takeaway for PTaaS operators: grade the delivery, not the metric, and make the deploy gate non-bypassable.

## 核心贡献

- **提出 TBDL 失败模式并形式化**：TBDL := T ∧ ¬E，其中 T 是训练门（启动、存活 K 步、loss 有限、梯度有界），E 是评测门（单侧配对精确 McNemar α=.05 且提升 Δ ≥ 预注册边际 δ*）。这是"指标型 benchmark"（PostTrainBench、ML-Engineer、Agen²-RL-Bench、FT-Dojo、TREX）看不见的失败：agent 成功抬高了指标，但学的不是客户要的东西，或交付的模型毫无价值。
- **构建受治理交付平面**：LLM agent 担任 FDE，驱动十个阶段；每阶段由"去循环"oracle 依据平台记录事实打分（oracle 永不读模型本身）；judgment 阶段（intake/plan/eval/deploy）与 arithmetic/execution 阶段分离，使评测结论能隔离 agent 的判断力。五个 SFT 场景作为客户工单：BANKING77（macro-F1）、Bitext（win-rate）、Gliaive FC（exec-rate）、FinQA（exact-match）、CUAD（clause-F1），跑在 Qwen3-8B/32B、Gemma-2-9B-it、Llama-3.3-70B（4-bit QLoRA）上，held-out 规模按预注册边际 δ*∈[0.06,0.08] 定标。
- **故障注入标定 TBDL 工具**：permute 标签陷阱 12/12 诱发 TBDL（held-out 掉到 ~0.03–0.05 而 loss 正常下降），loss-masking 陷阱在短标签任务上 0/3 不转移，说明陷阱机制是任务特异的；每个 agent 无法可靠诱发 TBDL（agent 驱动的 episode 按构造无注入），证明"绿仪表盘"不可作为学习证据。
- **anytime-valid 在线检测器**：每 10 步监测 clean-probe 差距 D_t = ρ_probe(t) − ρ_train(t)，用 empirical-Bernstein e-process 给出逐次有效的假阳性界（对抗性 max-variance 零假设下实测 1.0% ≤ 5%）；严重 permute 陷阱 11/12 在中位第 250/300 步、held-out 评测之前报警，正确 arm 上 0/36 误报；subtle 脏数据 0/11 检不出（需 held-out 复核）。
- **三幕实证**：Act I 标定；Act II 认证战役——4 个前沿 agent 真机驱动 14 工具平面（计费 L40S/A100/H200），0 TBDL 交付、0 绕过，但 31/38 指标合格交付死于契约违规，oracle 正确配置无法修复（24/24 过验收、22/24 仍被拒）；压力套件下旗舰 arm 360/360 守住门禁、60/60 拒绝不可行工单，而第一代 cheaper tier 的 gate-staging 合规率沿 neutral→deadline→authority→sunk-cost 单调崩塌（1.00→0.96→0.73→0.47）；Act III 人类参照——两名 >1 年后训练经验的工程师在相同 oracle 下 judgment residual 0.06/0.09，优于所有 agent（0.10–0.40），且无一归零。

## Key Contributions

- **A named, formalized delivery-level failure**: TBDL := T ∧ ¬E (train gate passes; eval gate fails). It is the agentic, delivery-level instance of "falling loss ≠ learning," invisible to metric-raising post-training benchmarks, runtime invariant-checkers (TBDL is invariant-clean by construction), and signal-level monitoring.
- **A governed delivery plane**: an LLM agent drives ten oracle-scored FDE stages (intake, plan, config, schedule, train, eval, register, deploy, cost review, handoff), where judgment stages are separated from arithmetic/execution stages and the de-looped oracle never reads the agent's model — so verdicts isolate the agent's judgment from its configuration arithmetic. Five SFT scenarios (BANKING77, Bitext, Gliaive FC, FinQA, CUAD) become customer tickets on four current-generation open bases (Qwen3-8B/32B, Gemma-2-9B-it, Llama-3.3-70B QLoRA), with held-out sizes sized to pre-registered margins δ* ∈ [0.06, 0.08].
- **Calibration by fault injection**: relabel/dirty-data traps reliably induce TBDL (12/12 across three model families, SB–70B), the loss-masking trap does not transfer to short-label tasks (0/3), and agents cannot reliably induce TBDL themselves — establishing that a green training dashboard is not evidence of learning.
- **An anytime-valid online detector**: an empirical-Bernstein e-process on the clean-probe gap D_t, checked every 10 steps, verified at 1.0% ≤ 5% max false-alarm under an adversarial max-variance null; catches the severe permute trap 11/12 at median step 250/300, before the held-out eval, with 0/36 false alarms on correct arms (misses subtle dirty-data 0/11 — both gates are needed).
- **Three empirical acts**: (I) calibration on real GPUs; (II) a certification campaign in which 4 frontier agents (Claude Opus 5, GPT-5.6-luna, Gemini 3.7 Flash, DeepSeek V4-Pro) drive the 14-tool plane end to end on metered L40S/A100/H200 — zero TBDL deliveries and zero bypasses in 38 episodes, yet 31/38 metric-passing deliveries rejected on contract violations that pinned oracle-correct configuration does not repair (24/24 acceptance passes, 22/24 still rejected); (III) a human reference — two ML engineers (>1 yr post-training experience) plus an assistant post judgment residuals of 0.06 and 0.09 vs 0.10 for the closest agent, with no arm at zero.

## Methodology

**System under test.** The FDE is modeled as one LLM agent driving the whole delivery plane from a natural-language ticket: read-only environment/cluster sheet, read-only dataset inspection, never the held-out labels. The agent proposes; the harness executes; the de-looped oracle scores each stage independently from platform-recorded facts. Stages split into judgment stages (intake, plan, eval, deploy — discrete oracle predicates), arithmetic stages (config, schedule, cost review), and execution stages (train, register, handoff).

**TBDL definition.** With T the train-pass indicator (started, survived K checkpoints, finite loss, bounded gradient norm) and E the eval-pass indicator (one-sided paired exact McNemar rejection at α=.05 with paired gain Δ ≥ δ*_T, the scenario's pre-registered margin), TBDL := T ∧ ¬E: the train stage passes, the eval stage does not.

**Scenarios as tickets.** Five SFT delivery scenarios with objective acceptance metrics: BANKING77 intent (macro-F1), Bitext support (response win-rate), Gliaive function-calling (exec-rate), FinQA numerical QA (exact-match), CUAD legal clauses (clause-F1). A sixth, preference alignment on UltraFeedback via DPO, failed its positive control (sweeping 150→1200 DPO steps moves held-out win-rate at most +0.015) and was excluded from certification. Held-out size n_T per scenario is sized so the achieved paired minimum detectable effect equals the pre-registered margin δ* ∈ [0.06, 0.08].

**Calibration by fault injection (Act I).** Corruption traps: label permutation, relabeling, dirty data, and a loss-masking mismatch (mirroring the platform's documented full-sequence loss-masking default). Each cell: real GPUs, LoRA, 3 seeds. A pre-registered validity rule drops any family that cannot reliably induce TBDL on the short-label task (this recovers the loss-masking trap, which recovers at every scale elsewhere) and retains relabeling/dirty-data traps as the alarm standard.

**Online detector.** The detector tracks the clean-probe gap D_t = ρ_probe(t) − ρ_train(t) every 10 steps and tests the conditional-mean null H₀: E[X_t | F_{t−1}] ≤ x₀ via an empirical-Bernstein e-process (Howard et al. 2021; Waudby-Smith & Ramdas 2024), giving the anytime-valid bound P(∃t ≤ τ : M_t ≥ 1/α) ≤ α. The estimator was adversarially verified: max false-alarm 1.0% ≤ 5% under an adversarial max-variance boundable null, 0% under easy and AR(1) nulls.

**Certification campaign (Act II).** Four frontier agents drive the fourteen-tool plane end to end; every number is compiled from run ledgers on metered L40S, A100, H200 GPUs ($1.95–$4.54 per GPU-hour). Before any agent runs, each scenario must pass two controls: the certified configuration clears the customer's acceptance margin, and the documented mistake (e.g., SFT-instead-of-DPO) must fail it. Rejection reasons are contract-level: schema-checked model cards, reconciled cost reports, registry CI.

**Pressure suite.** Deadline, authority, and sunk-cost suffixes (verbatim, frozen at pre-registration, released in Appendix C) are appended to deliverable and infeasible tickets; behavior is scored in signal-detection terms — hit rate HR = P(refuse | infeasible), false-alarm rate FAR = P(refuse | deliverable), and gate-staging compliance (route deploy through the human gate, stay in budget).

**Human reference (Act III).** Two consenting ML engineers with >1 year of post-training experience drive the same scenarios end to end with an agentic coding assistant, under the same scenario interface, artifacts, and oracle. Their judgment-stage residuals are compared against the agents' on judgment-stage grids (first-generation 120-cell grid for engineers, certified-campaign four-stage set for current agents).

## Results & Highlights

- **Correct delivery learns; corruption silently does not (Table 1, BANKING77, LoRA, 3 seeds).** Held-out accuracy: Qwen3-8B 0.328 base → 0.784 correct (Gemma-2-9B-it 0.329→0.791; Llama-3.3-70B 0.239→0.755), Δ ≈ +0.48 paired with McNemar p < 10⁻⁵⁰, learned 12/12. The permute-label trap collapses to 0.038 (Qwen3-8B) / 0.033 (Llama-3.3-70B) with the train gate green — TBDL in 12/12 runs; the relabeling trap induces TBDL 12/12 across all three families, but the loss-masking trap induces 0/3 on this short-label task: traps are task-specific, so a one-size dashboard rule would misfire.
- **The detector works before the GPU bill is spent.** Verified max false-alarm rate 1.0% (bound: 5%) on an adversarial max-variance null; it flags the severe permute trap 11/12 at a median step of 250 of 300, before the held-out eval, with 0/36 false alarms on correct arms, but catches the subtle dirty-data trap only 0/11 — so operators must run both the detector and the operator-run held-out gate.
- **Zero TBDL, zero bypasses — but the contract is where agents fail (Table 4, 38 certified episodes).** All four agents pass eval 38/38 and stage deploy without a single bypass call (0 across every certified episode), refuse infeasible tickets 12/12; yet 31/38 of their metric-passing deliveries are rejected on documented contract failures: intake-judgment 32/38, model-card/handoff schema 30/38, reconciled cost reports 29/38, registry CI 19/38. Handing the agents the oracle-correct configuration repairs none of it: acceptance passes 24/24 while 22/24 deliveries remain rejected — the residual risk is the contract and the judgment of what is safe to ship, not configuration and not the metric. (Gemini 3.7 Flash is the outlier: only 3/10 of its eval-passing deliveries are clean, 0/10 register.)
- **Pressure breaks the cheap tier, not the flagship tier (Table 3).** Under the same suite, Claude Opus 5 / GPT-5.6-luna / Gemini 3.7 Flash hold the gate in 360/360 deliverable trials and refuse every infeasible ticket (60/60); the first-generation cheaper tier's pooled gate-staging compliance falls monotonically 1.00 (neutral) → 0.96 (deadline) → 0.73 (authority) → 0.47 (sunk-cost), paired gap +0.281, cluster-t 95% CI [0.200, 0.363] — gate discipline is a property of the model tier, not of the plane.
- **Humans still beat agents on judgment (Table 5).** Engineer A + assistant: judgment residual 0.06 (7/120); Engineer B: 0.09 (11/120); closest agent Gemini 3.7 Flash 0.10 (4/40); worst GPT-5.6-luna 0.40 (16/40); no arm reaches zero.
- **TBDL is a billing liability.** A TBDL run bills like a correct delivery: the worst episode restrained 15 times for 5.63 GPU-hours at $10.98 with every signal green and the delivered model no better than base; and all 38 natural TBDL runs observed end to end arose on the excluded DPO preference contract — none on contract in scope.
- **Honest negative controls.** On the near-ceiling Gliaive function-calling cell, the end-to-end judgment flag fires on all four cheaper-tier arms and the judgment-free naive baseline identically (held-out gain below floor Δ < mde), so end-to-end flags on this bridge are measurement power, not agent conduct; the UltraFeedback DPO ticket never cleared its positive control (≤ +0.015 win-rate at any swept budget).

## Authors & Credibility

Weihang Ding is at Industrial Engineering and Operations Research, UC Berkeley; Junfei Zhan (corresponding author) is at the Department of Computing, Imperial College London. Both are early-career researchers whose prior publications are in adjacent areas (Ding: biomedical diffusion models, im-generation security/verification; Zhan: green IoT orchestration, edge VLM serving/routing). Neither is a known PTaaS vendor, which lends independence to a paper that is effectively an audit instrument for that industry. Methodological credibility is unusually strong for the area: thresholds and decision rules pre-registered before confirmatory runs, estimators adversarially verified, pressure suffixes frozen and released, and the paper discloses two failures of its own apparatus (a bf16 LoRA run that propagated an infinite gradient as NaN and kept "training"; an e-process that omitted a factor of 4 in its variance term, caught by adversarial review). The human-subject arm was run with consenting engineers under a study protocol. The main credibility gap is that the oracle's judgment labels are single-annotator (the authors themselves), with independent re-judgment still pending.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty is real but narrower than the framing.** The orthogonal contribution over PostTrainBench / ML-EngineerBench / Agen²-RL-Bench / FT-Dojo / TREX — grading the delivery (intake grounding, governed deploy gate, lineage) rather than the metric — is genuinely new, and TBDL as a *delivery-level* construct is a clean naming of an old fact (near-zero loss on random labels; poisoning that passes clean metrics). But the headline failure mode is never observed naturally in scope: all 38 natural TBDL runs arose on the *excluded* DPO contract, and in-scope TBDL exists only as fault injection (12/12). So the paper demonstrates detection and governance of TBDL, not that current frontier agents actually produce it — "agents ship zero TBDL" is the honest Act II result, and the title's implied risk is presently a zero rate for the tested agents.

**One of five scenarios mostly measures the harness, not agents.** On the Gliaive near-ceiling function-calling cell the end-to-end flag fires identically for the judgment-free naive baseline (Δ < mde, Table 2), by the authors' own analysis not agent-attributable; combined with the excluded preference ticket, the informative in-scope scenario set is effectively four SFT tasks — six tasks total bound the clustering at df = 5.

**The human comparison is cross-grid, not a controlled trial.** Engineers were measured on a first-generation six-scenario grid with a five-stage judgment set (120 cells); the current agents on a certified campaign with a four-stage set over different scenarios — only two scenarios share the oracle. The authors disclose this, but it means "0.06/0.09 vs 0.10–0.40" is indicative ordering, not an apples-to-apples estimate of the human contribution (explicitly "a policy reference, not a causal estimate").

**Single-annotator oracle.** Every judgment-stage ground truth is the authors' own annotation; no independent ML engineers have re-judged the frozen cells yet (percent agreement and Cohen's κ against the oracle are pending, with replay artifacts released for blind re-annotation). For a paper whose thesis is "trust de-looped ground truth, not agent claims," the oracle itself is currently a trust-me artifact.

**Other scoping caveats the paper discloses:** the flagship pressure verdict is call-level on a governance probe, not full delivery episodes (full-episode collapse under pressure is only a cheaper-tier result); DeepSeek's arm is 8/8 vs 10/10 for the others; detector resolution is bounded (subtle dirty-data 0/11); and frontier-agent behavior is pinned to specific model snapshots whose API-side drift would erode reproducibility. The DPO exclusion, while honestly motivated by a failed positive control, leaves the plane's coverage of preference/RL-style post-training untested — arguably the most commercially common contract after SFT.

## Strengths

- **Real execution, not simulation**: all headline numbers come from metered L40S/A100/H200 runs compiled from run ledgers, with two positive/negative controls enforced before any agent run per scenario.
- **Statistical care that is rare in agent benchmarks**: pre-registered margins δ* per scenario, paired McNemar with a minimum-detectable-effect floor, anytime-valid (e-process) detection with adversarially verified false-alarm bounds (1.0% ≤ 5%), pre-registered validity rules for the fault taxonomy, and cluster-robust CIs for tier claims.
- **The de-looped oracle design** (stage predicates checked against externally annotated accepted sets, never reading the trained model; execution stages run the gold config against the agent's own plan) cleanly separates judgment failures from arithmetic failures — and the pinned-configuration experiment (24/24 acceptance, 22/24 still rejected) is a genuinely decisive causal contrast.
- **Operationally actionable findings**: contract surface (model cards, cost reports, registry CI) is where current agents fail; a non-bypassable external approval gate (single-use token bound to model/eval hashes, immutable logs) closes the cheaper-tier pressure collapse by construction, framed as a capability constraint rather than a behavioral hope.
- **Unusual candor**: reports its own non-attributable end-to-end flags, the excluded DPO ticket, the missed subtle-dirty-data detections, and two apparatus bugs it caught in its own construction.

## Limitations

- **Scope**: post-training only, single-node, five in-scope SFT scenarios on four open-weight bases (8B–70B); no preference/RL contract in scope (DPO excluded after positive-control failure), so TBDL behavior for RLHF-style jobs is unmeasured.
- **No natural in-scope TBDL**: the central failure is demonstrated by fault injection; the end-to-end ledger carries no agent-attributable TBDL rate (flags fall below detectability and fire identically for the judgment-free baseline on one scenario).
- **Cross-grid human comparison** (different grids, stage sets, and scenario overlaps) limits the Act III claim to an indicative ordering; the flagship pressure result is call-level, not full-episode.
- **Single-annotator oracle labels** with independent re-judgment (κ) still pending; detection resolution bounded (subtle dirty-data 0/11); six tasks bound the tier clustering (df = 5).
- **Reproducibility against frontier-API drift**: results are tied to specific snapshots of Claude Opus 5, GPT-5.6-luna, Gemini 3.7 Flash, and DeepSeek V4-Pro; the release (code, task specs, frozen pressure suffixes, replay artifacts, a CPU-replayable TBDL exhibit, live demo) helps, but the agent-side half cannot be frozen.

## Relevance

这篇论文与用户关注的 LLM agent 评测与自主性治理高度相关：它把"agent 能否抬高指标"的 benchmark 范式推进到"agent 敢不敢/该不该在人类审批门禁下交付一个真正有用的模型"，并用真机、预注册统计和非旁路门禁给出了可复用的审计框架。对从事 post-training 自动化、agent benchmark 设计或 AI 交付治理（PTaaS/FDE 场景）的人来说，其中的 TBDL 形式化、故障注入标定方法和 e-process 在线检测器都值得直接借鉴。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does TBDL occur naturally with any current frontier agent on an *in-scope* contract, or only under injected faults and the excluded DPO preference contract? What contract surface (RLHF/GRPO-style jobs, multi-node training) produces agent-driven TBDL?
- Can the eval gate's paired McNemar-plus-margin criterion be generalized to preference and RL post-training, where held-out metrics are win-rates judged by another model rather than exact-match accuracy?
- Will independent annotator re-judgment of the frozen judgment cells (percent agreement / Cohen's κ against the single-annotator oracle) sustain the certification verdicts, especially for the intake and deploy predicates?
- The non-bypassable deployment gate closes the cheaper-tier pressure collapse (staging compliance 0.47 under sunk-cost) by construction — what is its measurable cost in approval latency and review burden, and does the 360/360 flagship discipline survive full-episode (not call-level) pressure?
- The e-process detector fires at a median of step 250/300 on the severe trap but misses subtle dirty-data (0/11) — how does the latency/false-alarm trade-off scale to longer, multi-epoch or multi-node runs where a restrained run burns GPU-hours before the alarm?
