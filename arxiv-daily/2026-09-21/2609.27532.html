Re-running acceptance checks each turn rescues zero-gradient failures in agentic RL — the gain comes from where credit lands, not the score itself.

Why This Matters

直击 agentic RL 的核心痛点：all-fail 组零梯度、推进任务的轮次与只读查询同酬。密集信号不必另造——它就藏在任务自带的验收检查里。

A cheap, verifiable dense-reward recipe for any multi-turn tool-agent RL — no reward model, no annotations, no reference trajectories.

Critical Edge

The +4.1 headline collapses at scale — by 35B the lead over RLOO is just +0.2 TGC — and the method needs re-runnable, per-fact checks, ruling out most open-ended settings.

Methodology how credit is re-plumbed

1

Read progress from existing checks

Each task ships executable acceptance checks. Re-run them after every training turn: Φ = fraction passed (checks passing at start excluded, Φ₀=0). Read-only queries earn zero; bad edits undo progress. The model never sees Φ at inference.

2

Shape the trajectory score

Turn reward rₜ = c·ΔΦ (c=0.5); score S = R + c(Φₜ−Φ₀) replaces R in GRPO’s group standardization — potential-based shaping, so the optimal policy set is unchanged.

3

Credit the turn itself

A second, turn-level advantage on undiscounted returns Gᵢₜ = Rᵢ + c(Φᵢ,ₜ − Φᵢ,ₜ₋₁) with γ=1, centered by the task’s mean; summed with the trajectory term.

4

The property that carries the gain

Aᵢₜ − Aᵢ,ₜ₋₁ = rₜ exactly: a turn that advances the task beats the next turn by precisely its progress reward — even inside all-success groups. PPO clip, KL omitted (following LOOP); loss over generated tokens only.

54.9% of all-fail groups (4B; 38.6% at 9B) contain distinguishable progress — zero gradient under outcome rewards, real signal under ProCredit.

Strengths

  • The signal is free. Progress is read from acceptance checks the tasks already have — no reward model, no annotations, no reference trajectories; read only during training.
  • GRPO-Φ is exactly the right control: identical trajectory score, only the turn-level term differs — it cleanly attributes the gain to where credit lands, not how much progress is measured.
  • The γ=1 vs 0.95 study is a community-service finding: the “length tax” mechanism is measured directly — discounted models emit trajectories 9% / 20% shorter at 4B / 9B while losing hard tasks.
  • Rigor above subfield norm: three seeds with std at every scale, two environments, entropy and failure-progress analyses, worked numerical examples in the appendix.

Limitations

  • Scope is conditional on infrastructure: requires intermediate-state access and re-runnable checks; dead ends where success is only verifiable at the end or judged subjectively.
  • Coarse progress proxy: an unweighted, unordered check fraction — with few coarse checks the turn-level term degenerates to plain GRPO; check-granularity sensitivity is never probed.
  • Gains essentially vanish at 35B Test-Normal TGC (+0.2); nothing tested beyond 35B-A8B, and only two tool-use simulators — coding / database-state tasks are argued to fit but not evaluated.
  • All-fail-group rates (54.9% / 38.6%) are measured on their own training runs; how common such groups are elsewhere is untested. ToolSandbox curation (73 of 101 tasks excluded) conditions the benchmark on base-model weaknesses.

Questions for Further Exploration

Q1Can an LLM-judge decomposing final acceptance into intermediate checks recover ProCredit’s gains without reintroducing reward-hacking risk?

Q2Would weighting or ordering checks (difficulty, dependencies) beat the uniform fraction, especially on tasks with very few checks?

Q3At what scale or success rate does the progress signal stop paying — and can curricula keep all-fail and mixed groups common?

Q4Is ProCredit complementary or redundant with resampling fixes for all-fail groups, e.g. DAPO’s oversampling?

Q5Is there a principled, horizon-dependent rule for γ in multi-turn agent RL — or is γ=1 always right when rewards are bounded?
