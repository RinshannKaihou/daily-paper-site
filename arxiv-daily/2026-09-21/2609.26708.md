> **一句话总结：** 该文诊断出教师强制的量化感知蒸馏（QAD）把短问答恢复到 BF16 的 82%，但长推理链崩坏（MATH-500 仅保留 35%，95% 的生成耗尽解码预算、70% 陷入 8-gram 重复循环）的根因是"量化放大的暴露偏差"——训练在固定语料前缀上、推理却在模型自身轨迹上；据此提出两阶段恢复框架 QAD→OPD：让量化学生沿"自己实际走到的位置"生成，由冻结 BF16 教师在其前缀上给反向 KL 逐 token 引导、叠加任务验证器奖励，仅用 245–260 步（约 14–23 倍少于 QAD 的 GPU 时）就把四个模型在 2.79/1.88 有效比特下的 MATH-500 平均 BF16 保持率从 35.3% 拉到 69.7%，且在 16 组同预算对照中全部胜过继续做教师强制 QAD。

> **TL;DR:** Teacher-forced quantization-aware distillation (QAD) recovers short-form QA after sub-3-bit quantization but leaves long-form reasoning broken — on MATH-500 the Qwen3-0.6B W2.79 checkpoint retains only ~35% of BF16 accuracy (10.4% vs 27.2%), with 95% of generations exhausting the decoding budget and 70% ending in repetition loops (BF16: 27% / 12%). The authors attribute this to quantization-amplified exposure bias and add a short on-policy distillation (OPD) phase: the student rolls out through its quantized forward path while a frozen BF16 teacher supplies reverse-KL token guidance on those student prefixes, plus group-relative verifier rewards (answer correctness for math, test execution for code). Across four models (Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B/4B, Falcon3-1B-Instruct) at 2.79 and 1.88 effective bits, OPD doubles average MATH-500 BF16 retention (35.3% → 69.7%) and raises HumanEval retention to 91%, beating continued teacher-forced QAD in all 16 matched-budget comparisons (e.g., Qwen3-1.7B @2.79 MATH-500 45.6% vs 23.2%) at 14–23× fewer GPU-hours than the QAD stage itself.

## 核心贡献

- **诊断量化放大的暴露偏差（quantization-amplified exposure bias）**：系统测量表明 QAD 的恢复效果随所需生成长度单调衰减——在六个模型×位宽设置上平均保持 QA9 86%、GSM8K 56%、MBPP 59%，但 MATH-500 只剩 27%、AMC23 只剩 16%；并给出行为学证据：Qwen3-0.6B W2.79 在 MATH-500 上 95% 的生成耗尽解码预算（BF16 为 27%）、70% 以重复 8-gram 结尾（BF16 为 12%）。
- **提出 OPD（on-policy distillation）恢复阶段**：教师监督从"固定语料前缀"移到"量化模型自身部署路径生成的补全"，用反向 KL（可由学生样本估计、惩罚学生喜欢但教师不喜欢的续写）做逐 token 引导，结合组相对优势的验证器奖励（数学对答案、代码跑测试），β=1 且全程不调。
- **同预算严格对照**：从相同 QAD 检查点出发、相同阶段调度/步数/学习率/样本数下，OPD 在全部 16 组对比中胜过继续教师强制 QAD；把 QAD 数学阶段预算扩到 2×、3× 均无额外收益，说明瓶颈不在数据量而在训练/推理分布错配。
- **恢复效率**：OPD 每千优化器步换取的 GSM8K 点数是继续 QAD 的约 9 倍（最高 42 倍）；Qwen3-4B @1.88 仅需 57 GPU 时（QAD 为 820），Falcon3-1B @2.79 仅需 11（QAD 为 253），约 14–23 倍节省。
- **亚 3 比特下对齐部署设定的基线横评**：在统一 INT8 激活/INT4 embedding 的部署设定下，2.79 比特最强 PTQ 基线 Q-Palette 在 MATH-500 上保持 58%，本方法达 84%；1.88 比特时所有 PTQ/QAT 基线在 MBPP/HumanEval 上为 0、GSM8K 保持率 ≤9%，而 OPD 相对 EdgeRazor-QAD 初始化再提升 1.6–2.9 倍。

## Key Contributions

- **A quantitative diagnosis of what QAD leaves behind**: across six model×bit-width settings, QAD retention decays monotonically with required generation length — QA9 86%, GSM8K 56%, MBPP 59%, but MATH-500 27% and AMC23 16% of BF16 — tracing the failure to exposure bias amplified by quantization-induced distribution shifts that compound along the student's own trajectories.
- **OPD, a teacher-forcing → student-forcing recovery stage**: teacher supervision is relocated onto completions sampled through the quantized forward path used at deployment, combining reverse-KL token-level guidance from a frozen BF16 teacher with group-relative advantage from task verifiers (final-answer correctness for math, test execution for code); β=1 in every run.
- **A matched-budget controlled comparison**: OPD beats continued teacher-forced QAD in all 16 comparisons across four benchmarks, two models, and two bit-widths, while doubling or tripling the QAD budget yields no further gains — evidence that the bottleneck is the train/inference distribution mismatch, not data volume.
- **Recovery efficiency**: OPD yields ~9× (up to 42×) more GSM8K points per thousand optimizer steps than continued QAD and needs 14–23× fewer GPU-hours than the QAD initialization (57 vs 820 GPU-hours for Qwen3-4B @1.88 bits).
- **An alignment-aware baseline study at sub-3 bits**: with all arms matched to the same deployment quantization (INT8 activations, INT4 embeddings/lm_head), at 1.88 bits every PTQ/QAT baseline scores zero on MBPP and HumanEval and ≤9% GSM8K retention despite 67–80% QA9 retention, while the QAD→OPD pipeline reaches 64% GSM8K and 77% MBPP retention.

## Methodology

**Two-stage recovery.** Stage 1 reproduces the EdgeRazor mixed-precision QAD recipe: weights are quantized per 256-input-channel block to ternary {-1,0,1} (≈1.58 bits) or INT4; a positional rule assigns a fraction p of rows to INT4, giving p=0.5 → 2.79 and p=0.125 → 1.88 effective bits. Embeddings and the output head bypass the mix in INT4; activations are INT8. QAD distills on a general instruction mixture with the student's own BF16 copy as teacher via online logit KL (Qwen3-0.6B, whose BF16 copy is too weak, uses Qwen3-1.7B as teacher; effective batch 768, 8-bit Adam, ZeRO-3 on 8 GPUs, 6,400–12,800 steps).

Stage 2 (OPD) starts from that QAD checkpoint. Formal move: replace the teacher-forced loss L_TF = E[Σ KL(π_T(·|x,y_<t) ‖ π_θ(·|x,y_<t))] over fixed corpus prefixes with a student-forced objective where the completion ŷ ~ π_θ(·|x) is sampled through the frozen quantized forward path, and the KL targets are evaluated on the resulting student prefixes. Reverse KL is chosen deliberately: it is estimable from student samples and penalizes continuations the student favors but the teacher assigns low probability.

**Learning from quantized rollouts.** For each prompt the student draws G completions; a verifier assigns reward (final-answer correctness for math, test execution pass for code). The OPD loss is L_OPD = −E[Σ Â(x,ŷ) log π_θ(ŷ|x,ŷ_<t)] + β·E[Σ log π_θ(ŷ|x,ŷ_<t)/π_T(ŷ|x,ŷ_<t)], where Â is the group-relative advantage and β=1 in every run. Each student is supervised by its own BF16 counterpart as teacher. Training runs in verl with vLLM generation, non-thinking mode, temperature 1: a mathematics phase (80–140 steps, lr 3e-6, G=4, 32 rollouts/step; corpora GSM8K + MATH L1–3 + DAPO-Math) followed by a code phase (80–150 steps, G=8, 64 rollouts/step; MBPP + KodCode) resumed from the math checkpoint; checkpoints picked every 20 steps by phase validation.

**Evaluation.** GSM8K/MATH-500 (lm-eval protocol), AMC23 (16 problems), HumanEval/MBPP pass@1, and QA9 (mean of ARC-Easy/Challenge, HellaSwag, SocialIQA, OpenBookQA, PIQA, WinoGrande, TruthfulQA, MMLU) — retention defined as each checkpoint's score as a percentage of the BF16 reference.

## Results & Highlights

- **Retention recovery (Table 2, six Qwen3 model×width settings):** OPD raises average BF16 retention on MATH-500 from 35.3% (QAD) to 69.7% and on HumanEval from 66% to 91%; GSM8K retention rises from 62.7% to 85.0%. Per-config example: Qwen3-1.7B at 2.79 bits scores 45.60 on MATH-500 vs 19.60 for QAD and 54.40 for BF16; at 1.88 bits, Qwen3-4B jumps from 17.36 to 64.59 on GSM8K and from 11.6 to 48.7 on MBPP. Across the six Qwen3 configurations at 2.79 bits, GSM8K accuracy improves by 8.41–47.23 percentage points over QAD.
- **Behavioral repair (Qwen3-0.6B W2.79, MATH-500):** OPD cuts the 8-gram repetition-loop rate from 70% to 17% (BF16: 12%) and decoding-budget exhaustion from 95% to 53%, while accuracy rises from 10.4% to 24.2% (BF16: 27.2%); on GSM8K the loop rate falls from 30% to 2% (BF16: 5%) and exhaustion from 32% to 5%.
- **Matched-budget ablation (Table 4):** with identical starting checkpoints, phase schedules, step budgets, LRs, and samples, OPD beats continued teacher-forced QAD in all 16 comparisons; Qwen3-1.7B @2.79 reaches 45.6% on MATH-500 vs 23.2% for continued QAD (a 2× gap) and +9.70 GSM8K points (54.28 vs 44.28). Extending the QAD mathematics phase to 2× or 3× the budget adds essentially nothing, whereas OPD converts the same budget into large gains.
- **Against quantization baselines (Qwen3-1.7B, deployment-aligned):** at 2.79 bits the strongest PTQ baseline Q-Palette retains 93% of BF16 on QA9 and 89% on MBPP but only 58% on MATH-500, with the other PTQ/QAT baselines at 33–58%; the full pipeline reaches 84% MATH-500 retention. Below two bits every PTQ and QAT baseline scores zero on MBPP and HumanEval and at most 9% GSM8K retention while keeping 67–80% QA9; OPD then multiples the EdgeRazor QAD initialization by 1.6–2.9× on each generative benchmark (e.g., W1.88 GSM8K 26.46→44.05, MATH-500 8.20→24.20), reaching 64% GSM8K and 77% MBPP retention.
- **Cost:** OPD uses 57 GPU-hours vs 820 for the QAD stage (Qwen3-4B, 1.88 bits) and 11 vs 253 (Falcon3-1B, 2.79 bits) — recovery at 14–23× lower cost, while broad QA9 retention is preserved or improved (mean QA9 retention 88.1% → 92.1%).

## Authors & Credibility

The team is led by co-first authors Yuanteng Chen and Zhili Liu from the Institute of Automation, Chinese Academy Sciences (CASIA), with Peisong Wang, Gang Li, and Jian Liu (CASIA/UCAS/Zhongguancun Academy) as corresponding authors — Wang and Li are established researchers in efficient ML quantization, and the group has a track record at NeurIPS/AAAI in model compression. Shuang Qiu (City University of Hong Kong) adds RL-for-LLM expertise. Credibility is boosted by unusual methodological transparency: they reproduced the EdgeRazor QAD recipe themselves rather than taking released checkpoints, document every deviation from the released recipe (batch-invariant token budget, skipped offline regeneration, 16-bit KV cache), publish per-arm training budgets, and include an appendix itemizing baseline-alignment asymmetries, including ones that favor their competitors. The work reads as a careful systems-plus-science paper from a group that clearly owns its training stack (verl + vLLM, 8×GPU runs totaling ~1,000+ GPU-hours per QAD arm).

## Critical Assessment

The strongest part of the paper is the diagnosis-plus-control design: the length-stratified retention analysis (Figure 2), the loop/exhaustion curves (Figure 3), and especially the matched-budget ablation (Table 4) jointly make the exposure-bias explanation more than a slogan — showing that 2–3× more teacher-forced data buys nothing while a few hundred on-policy steps double MATH-500 accuracy is the right experiment. That said, several caveats temper the headline. First, on-policy distillation itself is not new (Agarwal et al., ICLR 2024; Gu et al. 2024; and the widely-discussed Thinking Machines 2025 blog), and the DAgger-style argument goes back to Ross et al. 2011; the genuine novelty is quantifying and fixing the effect specifically under sub-3-bit quantization, which is a solid but incremental framing. Second, the loss has two terms with β fixed at 1 in every run — there is no β sensitivity ablation and no comparison against pure verifier-only RL (e.g., GRPO-style) from the same QAD checkpoint, so the paper cannot fully isolate how much dense teacher KL guidance matters versus sparse rewards alone; this is the one missing ablation that would nail the mechanism. Third, the Q-Palette comparison — the strongest baseline at 2.79 bits — uses the authors' own pure-PyTorch reimplementation because official entrypoints are Llama-only, a legitimate but nontrivial risk when the conclusion "we beat the strongest baseline by 26 points of MATH-500 retention" rests on it. Fourth, evaluation is confined to four small models (0.6B–4B) in non-thinking mode; whether OPD keeps 10k+-token thinking traces stable, and whether the effect survives at 4–8 bits or on larger models, is untested. Finally, the training corpora (GSM8K train, MATH L1–3, DAPO-Math, MBPP, KodCode) are adjacent to the evaluation suites (GSM8K, MATH-500, HumanEval, MBPP) — standard practice for math/code RL, but the gain on in-domain suites should be read accordingly; the AMC23 improvement (e.g., Qwen3-1.7B @2.79: 0.00→19.38) is the cleaner out-of-corpus signal, though on only 16 problems.

## Strengths

- Clear, falsifiable mechanism claim backed by three independent evidence types: length-stratified retention decay, generation-time behavioral metrics (loop rate, budget exhaustion), and a budget-matched intervention.
- The matched-budget comparison is the paper's best asset — OPD vs continued QAD wins all 16 cells, and the null result of scaling QAD 2–3× rules out the trivial "more data" explanation.
- Practical significance: a few hundred optimizer steps (245–260) and 11–57 GPU-hours restore reasoning that the QAD stage's 6,400–12,800 steps could not, making the recipe cheap enough to bolt onto any QAD pipeline.
- Rare care in baseline alignment: all baselines re-run under the same INT8/INT4 deployment setting, width mismatches documented (and in the baselines' favor), and the QAD initialization shared by every arm isolates OPD's contribution.
- Honest accounting of asymmetries, teacher substitution for the 0.6B case, and full reproduction details (effective batch 768, LR schedules, checkpoint selection cadence).

## Limitations

- Scale and mode: only 0.6B–4B models, non-thinking mode, temperature-1 rollouts; no evidence for 7B+ models or for long chain-of-thought regimes where token budgets and repetition dynamics differ.
- No ablation on the teacher-KL weight β (always 1) and no pure-RL-from-QAD-init baseline, so the relative contribution of dense reverse-KL guidance vs verifier rewards is unquantified.
- Strongest-baseline comparison (Q-Palette) relies on a reimplementation, not official code; official Q-Palette is Llama-only, so cross-family generalization of that baseline's numbers is inferred, not measured.
- Training corpora overlap with evaluation suites (GSM8K train → GSM8K test; MATH L1–3 → MATH-500); out-of-corpus gains are shown only on the 16-problem AMC23.
- QAD stage cost dominates (~14–23× the OPD stage), so the total pipeline remains expensive even though the paper's efficiency claims are scoped to the OPD portion; the 0.6B model additionally requires a different (larger) teacher, hinting at a teacher-quality dependency.
- Small eval sets (AMC23 n=16, HumanEval n=164) make single-digit point changes noisy; no confidence intervals are reported.

## Relevance

这篇论文直接命中"极端量化下的大模型推理能力恢复"这一高效推理方向：先给出可复用的失败模式诊断（长生成重复循环/预算耗尽），再给出一套只需几百步、可叠加在任意 QAD 之上的 on-policy 蒸馏配方，对需要在边缘设备上部署 2-bit 推理模型的工作有直接的工程参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of OPD's gain comes from the dense reverse-KL term vs the verifier reward alone — does a β sweep or a pure GRPO-from-QAD-init arm change the ranking, especially for code where test-execution rewards are already dense?
- Does the method stabilize thinking-mode generation (10k+ token traces), where repetition dynamics and budget exhaustion are most severe?
- Is there a size threshold below which a model's own BF16 copy is too weak a teacher (as with Qwen3-0.6B), and how does OPD behave when the teacher is only marginally better than the student?
- Does the on-policy advantage persist at milder quantization (4–8 bits) where exposure bias is smaller, or is the technique specific to the sub-3-bit regime?
- Can the OPD stage be run with a quantized teacher (same bit-width as the student) to make recovery fully low-bit at training time, avoiding BF16 teacher memory costs?
