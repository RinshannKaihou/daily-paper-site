> **一句话总结：** 针对医疗多选QA，论文发现在 W4A4KV4 后训练量化下 OSTQuant 基线虽保住答案准确率，却在 rationale 中丢失/篡改支持答案的临床证据（MedExpQA 上全精度答案一致率从 100% 掉到 48.66%、PredRec 从 90.40 掉到 56.54）；作者提出解释感知量化目标——校准期用全精度教师 rationale 构建忠实性缓存（Qwen2.5-72B 提候选证据span + 强制选择行为打分 + 量化削弱过滤），在其上加 rationale-token 加权 KL 损失与证据条件答案恢复损失，将一致率恢复到 79.47%（+30.8 pts）、PredRec 恢复到 88.47%，且部署格式与推理开销与基线完全相同。

> **TL;DR:** Under W4A4KV4 post-training quantization on three medical MCQA benchmarks (MedExQA, MedExpQA, ChallengeClinicalQA), a calibration-matched OSTQuant baseline preserves task accuracy while silently degrading rationales: on MedExpQA it raises average accuracy from 31.80 (full precision) to 36.80 but collapses full-precision answer agreement to 48.66 and PredRec (rationale-based answer reconstruction) from 90.40 to 56.54. The paper adds two calibration-time losses — a weighted rationale-token KL loss and a rationale-conditioned answer-recovery loss — built from an offline "faithfulness cache" of teacher-answer-supporting evidence spans. This restores agreement to 79.47 (+30.8 pts over OSTQuant, overall 68.44→79.86 across all three datasets) and PredRec to 88.47, at zero runtime overhead but ~+9.5 GPU-hours extra calibration per model.

## 核心贡献

- **揭示"准确率掩盖证据漂移"的缺口：** 诊断分析显示，OSTQuant 的 token 级 KL 保真信号被通用话语 token 主导（占 rationale token 的 75.34%，却吸收 83.07% 的 KL 信号），而真正支撑答案的临床线索（占 22.19% token）只得到 15.56% 的信号——低比特量化可以在保持答案的同时改写、省略或捏造答案支持证据（Figure 1 的神经病理性疼痛案例）。
- **提出解释感知 PTQ 目标（实例化于 OSTQuant，repo 名 EAQuant）：** 两段校准期优化——Pass 1 构建固定量化探针 M_p；Pass 2 在基础 OSTQuant 损失上加两个新损失：(1) rationale-token 损失（对"教师答案支持且被量化削弱"的证据 token 加权的 top-K KL），(2) rationale 条件恢复损失（证据 span 下选项分布的 KL），优化后的变换参数融合进模型，部署格式 W4A4KV4 与推理路径不变、零运行时开销。
- **构建忠实性缓存的行为化证据提取流水线：** 每个校准样本仅保留教师预测正确的样本（可靠性过滤），用 Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct 提候选 span，用强制选择边际（必要性+充分性，直接答案线索已移除）判定证据，再对比教师与量化探针筛出"量化后支持效应被削弱"的 span。
- **配套评估体系：** 除准确率外引入全精度一致率、PredRec/GoldRec（可仿真性）、FER（全精度证据保留）、UCR（无支持断言率）、匿名化成对 LLM 评审，并对 GPT-5.4 评审做了小规模人检（126 条 claim、2 标注者，κ=0.58–0.63）。
- **实证结论：** 在 4 个 7B–8B 医疗/指令模型 × 3 基准上，方法相对同校准的 OSTQuant 基线显著改善一致率与 rationale 保持（整体一致率 68.44→79.86），并在 OmniQuant 骨干上验证目标可迁移。

## Key Contributions

- **Exposes an accuracy-vs-evidence gap in medical PTQ:** a diagnostic shows OSTQuant's token-level KL fidelity is dominated by generic/discourse tokens (75.34% of rationale tokens receive 83.07% of the KL signal), while clinical cues (22.19% of tokens) get only 15.56% — so low-bit quantization can keep the answer while changing, omitting, or fabricating the answer-supporting clinical evidence.
- **Proposes an explanation-aware PTQ objective (instantiated on OSTQuant, repo: EAQuant):** a two-pass calibration — Pass 1 builds a fixed quantized probe; Pass 2 optimizes transformations with the base loss plus (1) a rationale-token loss (top-K KL weighted toward teacher-answer-supporting, quantization-weakened evidence tokens) and (2) a rationale-conditioned recovery loss (KL between option distributions given each evidence span). Transformed parameters are fused into weights: the deployed W4A4KV4 format and inference path are identical to the baseline (zero runtime overhead).
- **A behavioral evidence-extraction pipeline for the faithfulness cache:** keep only calibration examples where the full-precision teacher is correct, propose candidate spans with Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct, score support via forced-choice margins (necessity/sufficiency, with direct answer-label cues removed), then retain spans whose answer-supporting effect weakened under the quantized probe.
- **A complementary evaluation suite:** accuracy, full-precision (FP) agreement, PredRec/GoldRec (simulatability), FER (FP evidence retention), UCR (unsupported-claim rate), and anonymized pairwise LLM judging, with a small human validation of the GPT-5.4 judge (126 claims, 2 annotators, Cohen's κ = 0.58–0.63).
- **Empirical result:** across four 7B–8B models and three benchmarks, the method substantially improves FP agreement and rationale preservation over calibration-matched OSTQuant (overall agreement 68.44→79.86), and transfers to an OmniQuant-backed run.

## Methodology

**Setup.** PTQ target is W4A4KV4 with transformation-based optimization (OSTQuant). Both baseline and method share the same 128-example mixed non-test medical calibration pool, so all gains are calibration-matched. Four 7B–8B models are evaluated: OpenBioLLM-8B, Llama-3-8B-Instruct, BioMistral-7B, Mistral-7B-Instruct; a transfer check adapts the objective to OmniQuant on Llama-3-8B and Mistral-7B.

**Diagnostic motivation.** On Llama-3-8B/MedExQA same-prediction examples, token-level KL drift between full-precision and OSTQuant next-token distributions is grouped into numeric cues, answer/option tokens, clinical cues, and generic/discourse tokens: generic/discourse tokens take 83.07% of the KL mass vs 15.56% for clinical cues — the base objective optimizes fluency, not evidence.

**Faithfulness cache construction (Pass 1 → offline).** For each calibration example, the FP teacher M_0 answers via deterministic forced-choice scoring (length-normalized log-likelihood per option; no free-form parsing) and generates an answer-conditioned rationale. Examples where the teacher is wrong are discarded (a reliability filter, not a supervised answer loss). A prompted Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct proposes up to 6 candidate evidence spans (3–20 words, exact substrings, must not name the answer). Each span's support is scored behaviorally: necessity N(m) = margin with span removed, sufficiency S(m) = margin given only the span, aggregated as max(N, αS) with α = 1.0; spans above η_F = 0.02 form the evidence set F_i. The same scoring is repeated on the fixed quantized probe M_p, and spans whose support effect weakened (D(e) > η_Q) form Q_i, with per-token weights ω_i normalized over Q_i. Cache entries are (x_i, r_i, F_i, Q_i, ω_i).

**Explanation-aware optimization (Pass 2).** With the probe-fixed cache, transformation parameters are optimized with L_OUTS = L_OST + λ_rat·L_rat + λ_rec·L_rec. The rationale-token loss is the top-K KL between the fake-quantized and quantization-disabled paths at each rationale position, weighted by ω_i (concentrating pressure on affected evidence tokens rather than imitating surface form). The recovery loss asks, for each evidence span, whether the evidence still induces the same answer preference after quantization: KL between option distributions under a recovery prompt containing only the span. λ_rat = λ_rec = 0.1, selected once on the MedExpQA validation split and fixed for all models/datasets.

**Evaluation protocol.** (1) Accuracy + FP agreement; (2) PredRec: regenerate a concise rationale, remove answer-label cues, and recover the answer via forced-choice scoring — fraction matching the system's own answer (GoldRec targets the gold answer); (3) FER: decompose the FP rationale into atomic clinical claims and judge (GPT-5.4) whether each is supported by the system rationale, computed only on same-answer examples; (4) UCR: share of system-rationale claims unsupported by or contradictory to the FP rationale; (5) anonymized pairwise judging on MedExpQA same-answer cases; (6) human validation of the judge on 20 Llama-3/MedExQA cases (252 judgments/annotator).

## Results & Highlights

- **FP agreement collapses under baseline PTQ and is restored (+30.8 pts):** On MedExpQA, OSTQuant raises average accuracy from 31.80 (full precision) to 36.80 but drops FP agreement to 48.66; the proposed method keeps accuracy comparable (31.54) and raises agreement to 79.47. Across all three datasets, average FP agreement rises from 68.44 (OSTQuant) to 79.86 (Ours) — evidence that OSTQuant's accuracy gains partly reflect quantization-induced answer flips rather than preservation.
- **PredRec (rationale supports the system's own answer):** On MedExpQA, OSTQuant's average PredRec falls from the FP value of 90.40 to 56.54 while accuracy rises; the method restores it to 88.47. Overall average across datasets: 75.96 → 87.68. Controls (Table 14) show PredRec depends on rationale content, not just the protocol: with a shuffled rationale the method's score drops to 71.22, and with a fixed external Gemma-4-12B-IT reconstructor to 64.13 (vs OSTQuant 30.20).
- **FER/UCR paired deltas on MedExpQA same-prediction examples (Ours − OSTQuant, 95% CI):** average FER +5.34 [1.50, 9.18] (Llama-3 +12.35 [8.77, 15.94]; BioMistral the sole exception at −1.07); average UCR −4.65 [−9.10, −0.28], i.e., fewer unsupported/contradictory claims. Human validation agrees directionally: pooled-human micro-FER 52.78% (OSTQuant) vs 62.30% (Ours), +9.52 points.
- **Pairwise judging:** on 500 MedExpQA same-answer cases, the method's rationale is preferred 89 vs 63 for OSTQuant (58.55% excluding ties); per-model Ours-share ranges 50.98% (BioMistral) to 66.67% (OpenBioLLM).
- **Accuracy is roughly preserved, not improved:** overall average accuracy 36.91 (FP) / 36.96 (OSTQuant) / 35.41 (Ours); on MedExQA Ours (47.86) slightly trails OSTQuant (47.00). Table 5 shows all rationale-aware variants (including naive full-rationale KD: PredRec 95.20, FER 56.30) reduce accuracy vs the base objective (34.93 → 24.80–26.67 on Llama-3/MedExpQA) — a real accuracy-preservation trade-off.
- **Ablations (Llama-3/MedExpQA):** each loss helps alone (+L_rat: PredRec 95.73; +L_rec: FER 53.99 vs base 49.07/51.64) and both together are best (PredRec 99.20±0.80, FER 58.22). Evidence-selection staging matters: the largest FER gain comes from the quantization-weakened filter (51.15 → 54.99) and full margin-drop criterion (55.30).
- **OmniQuant transfer check:** on Llama-3/MedExQA the objective lifts accuracy 23.87→29.50, FP agreement 29.70→55.67, PredRec 47.91→57.45 over the OmniQuant baseline.
- **Cost:** a representative Llama-3-8B run costs 12.60 GPU-hours vs 3.11 for standard OSTQuant (+9.49, ~4x calibration compute, L40S); the deployed model's W4A4KV4 format, architecture, and inference cost are identical to the baseline.

## Authors & Credibility

Yeji Kim, Mi-Young Kim, and Randy Goebel are all at the University of Alberta (Computing Science); Goebel is a senior, well-established researcher in AI/NLP, and Mi-Young Kim has a track record in clinical NLP (co-author of the MedExQA and Rationale2Decide lines). The work is funded by NSERC (including a CREATE "From Data to Discovery" grant), Amii, and Alberta Innovates, with acknowledgment of two in-group annotators for the human validation. Code and evaluation scripts are released at github.com/dut0817/EAQuant, and the appendix exposes full hyperparameters (λ=0.1, η_F=η_Q=0.02, α=1.0, β=0.01), quantization settings, prompts for every LLM-based stage, GPU budgets, and qualitative examples — an unusually transparent artifact set. Limitations and ethical considerations are candid (explicitly stating this is not clinical-safety evidence). One caveat: annotators were researchers from the authors' own group, and all LLM judging used GPT-5.4, so judge-related conclusions lean on a small, non-independent validation.

## Critical Assessment

- **Single primary baseline backbone:** all main results instantiate the objective on OSTQuant only; the OmniQuant check covers just 2 of 4 models and one bit-width (W4A4, with a fake-quantized inference path that is not runtime-equivalent to packed W4A4KV4). No GPTQ/AWQ/SpinQuant comparisons, so "PTQ pipelines with calibration-time transformation optimization" is a narrower claim than the framing suggests.
- **The accuracy story is honest but unflattering and slightly downplayed:** the method slightly lowers overall average accuracy vs OSTQuant (35.41 vs 36.96) and never beats the FP reference; Table 5 shows every rationale-aware variant costs 8–10 accuracy points on Llama-3/MedExpQA. The headline "preserves accuracy" really means "keeps accuracy in the same range while restoring agreement."
- **Benchmark noise weakens per-model claims:** BioMistral on MedExQA scores 55.07 (OSTQuant) and 56.17 (Ours) against a 38.72 FP reference — quantized models exceeding the FP teacher by ~17 points signals label noise or format artifacts, and the authors themselves treat BioMistral's FER regression (−1.07) cautiously. Most model-level FER/UCR confidence intervals cross zero; only the averages exclude zero, and the UCR CI [−9.10, −0.28] barely so.
- **Reference-based metrics rest on a GPT-5.4 judge validated on just 20 cases / 126 claims, annotated by two researchers from the authors' own group** (no external annotators, no crowdsourcing). Agreement is moderate (κ=0.58 human-human, 0.63 GPT-pooled-human), and the judge is systematically stricter than humans (+11.90 vs +9.52 FER delta), so FER/UCR absolute values should not be compared across papers.
- **λ selection is thin:** a single global λ (λ_rat = λ_rec = 0.1) tuned on a 63-example MedExpQA validation split; the sensitivity table (Table 13) shows PredRec peaking at 0.10 (90.21) with accuracy flat (~34.4–35.9), but FER sensitivity is not reported across λ.
- **The ChallengeClinicalQA protocol has a data quirk:** calibration is drawn from the four-option Medbullets subset while evaluation uses the disjoint five-option subset — defensible (no test leakage) but it means calibration and eval distributions differ in an unexamined way.
- **Novelty positioning is fair:** concurrent work (Wang et al., 2026) observed quantization effects on self-explanations, but modifying the PTQ objective itself for evidence preservation appears genuinely new; the closest baselines (full-rationale KD, random-span selection) are properly included and beaten on PredRec (99.20 vs 95.20/97.87) and FER (59.15 vs 56.30/55.99).

## Strengths

- A crisp, consequential problem statement with a quantified diagnostic (Table 1) that directly motivates the loss design — the "75.34% of tokens / 83.07% of KL signal" framing is memorable and testable.
- Zero-runtime-overhead design: all machinery (probe, teacher, extractor, cache) is discarded after calibration; the deployed model is byte-for-byte the same quantization format as the baseline.
- Methodologically careful evaluation: deterministic forced-choice scoring throughout (no brittle free-form answer parsing), PredRec content controls (Table 14), complementary FER/UCR metrics, anonymized pairwise judging with an explicit tie option, loss-component and evidence-selection ablations, λ sensitivity, and a second PTQ backbone.
- Honest accounting: accuracy trade-offs, the BioMistral FER exception, and reference-based-metric caveats are all reported in the main text, not buried.
- Complete reproducibility surface: released code, full prompts (Appendix J), hyperparameter and GPU-budget tables (12.60 vs 3.11 GPU-hours per Llama-3-8B run), and dataset splits (Table 8).

## Limitations

- Scope: medical multiple-choice QA only — no open-ended clinical generation, no non-medical domains, only 7B–8B models, only W4A4KV4 (plus a W4A4 OmniQuant check); conclusions for other bit-widths, sizes, or PTQ families are extrapolation.
- The faithfulness cache inherits teacher errors and teacher-specific reasoning patterns; preservation pressure may entrench wrong-but-consistent clinical justifications.
- Evidence extraction depends on a prompted Qwen2.5-72B proposer with behavioral filtering; extraction errors silently redirect which evidence receives preservation pressure (acknowledged by the authors).
- FER/UCR/GoldRec are reference-based and GPT-judged; the human validation is small (20 cases) and in-group, so cross-paper comparability is limited.
- Calibration cost is ~4x the baseline (+9.49 GPU-hours per representative run; ~3.0k L40S GPU-hours study-wide) — cheap at inference, nontrivial at calibration time.
- Improved answer-rationale consistency does not establish clinical correctness, factuality, or deployment safety — explicitly not a clinical-use endorsement.

## Relevance

这篇论文正好切中"模型压缩是否破坏可解释性/忠实性"这一量化社区长期忽视的问题，给出的解释感知校准目标零推理开销、可直接套在 OSTQuant/OmniQuant 等主流变换型 PTQ 流水线上，对做低比特部署（尤其医疗等高风险领域）很有参考价值。其配套的 PredRec/FER/UCR 评估协议也值得任何需要评估"压缩后解释质量"的工作借用。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the objective transfer to GPTQ/AWQ/SpinQuant-style backbones and other bit-widths (W8A8, W2A4), where the transformation-optimization interface differs or disappears?
- Can the accuracy cost of the auxiliary losses (8–10 points on Llama-3/MedExpQA vs the base objective) be recovered via loss scheduling or selective per-layer application?
- How does the method behave when the FP teacher is frequently wrong on a domain (weak-teacher regime) — does the cache preserve systematically biased rationales, and can a verifier-in-the-loop fix this?
- Would FER/UCR-style reference-based evidence metrics hold up with a larger, independent (ideally clinician) annotator pool, and do they correlate with real clinician trust in deployed settings?
- Does evidence preservation under quantization generalize to long-form clinical generation (discharge summaries, radiology reports), where PredRec's forced-choice protocol no longer applies?
