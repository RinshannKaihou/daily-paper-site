Why this matters

Mapping sparsifier-emitted sparse masks onto GPUs efficiently is the key systems lever for cutting video-diffusion inference cost — attention is ~85% of vDiT runtime.

⚠ Critical edge

The 33.73× FlexAttention headline is inflated by cold-start compilation; kernel-only gains are 1.70–6.38×, and the full 50-step diffusion loop only 1.22–2.08×.

01 Methodology

Logical mask M
Video sparsifiers (VSA, SpargeAttn, SPADE) pick block geometry per head / layer / step — the effective interaction set E(M).
Two-layer physical plan
Mapping layer — Direct T=B / Coarsened T>B / Refined T<B × organization layer — run grouping, split-K, pipeline depth.
↓
Offline profiling
40k cases per GPU over native CUDA catalogs (14–26 plans per architecture) → performance regimes with latency-ranked plan lists.
Runtime lookup
Scalar mask summary → regime → first eligible plan, base-plan fallback. No per-request search; one low-overhead table probe.
  • Problem: existing runtimes either hard-code tuned kernels (can’t adapt tiles per GPU/mask) or plan online — FlashInfer’s exhaustive search reaches 12.52× the Direct baseline’s cold-request latency.
  • Plans are semantics-preserving: Coarsened tiles amortize Q/KV loads with masked redundant math; Refined trades registers for smaller tiles (query-side independent softmax states, K/V-side shared state).
  • Scale of implementation: ~18K lines Python + 19K lines C++/CUDA, every plan numerically validated against a masked-attention reference.
  • Evaluation design: 9 workload families over 7 block geometries (mean density 5.35–18.20%), paired per-case across 4 GPUs, profiling/eval files kept distinct, geometric-mean speedups with regret metrics and CIs.

03 Strengths

  • Clean, reusable abstraction: separating mask semantics E(M) from execution (tiles, task organization) fixes a real conflict — sparsifier block sizes vs GPU tile shapes.
  • Unusually thorough empirics: 2,315 production-model masks, 4 GPU generations, paired per-case measurement, three latency definitions, regret with P95/P99, CIs on key ablations.
  • Two-orders-of-magnitude dispatch cut (19.25 µs vs 12.16 ms) with a base-plan fallback that guarantees progress.
  • Honest accounting: kernel-only vs full-loop gains reported separately; the 1.22–2.08× loop ceiling is stated, not buried.
  • Co-variation findings (geometry × architecture determine the winner) are independently useful to sparse-kernel authors.

04 Limitations

  • Thin end-to-end evidence: exactly two 720p settings, both on a single H100 with B=64; no multi-GPU or multi-resolution runs.
  • Amdahl-bounded wall clock: diffusion-loop gains (1.22–2.08×) sit far below the attention-kernel headline (2.95–6.79×).
  • Re-profiling required for new GPUs, sparsifiers, or shifted mask distributions — and its cost is not quantified; regime tables come from the same four models as evaluation (no OOD test).
  • Tail risk: P99 selection regret up to 1.17 — the worst 1% of picks can be ~17% slower than the best plan.
  • Scope: NVIDIA-only, BF16-only, three BSA baselines; no FVD/VBench quality measurement; no code or artifact release despite ~37K LOC.

05 Questions for further exploration

  • Do masks well outside the profiled corpus (irregular or token-learned block boundaries) degrade gracefully through the base-plan fallback — or fall off a cliff?
  • Does bit-for-bit E(M) preservation translate to unchanged FVD/VBench, and can the freed compute budget be traded for more sparsity?
  • Can offline profiling / table compilation be amortized or updated incrementally (e.g. bandit-style re-ranking) as mask distributions drift during serving?
  • Would the two-layer plan abstraction port to AMD MI300 / TPUs, or to multi-GPU sequence-parallel video inference where dispatch overhead matters even more?
  • With attention no longer the bottleneck, does the optimal sparsity level shift — and how does TESSERA compose with caching, step distillation, or FP8/FP4 quantization?
