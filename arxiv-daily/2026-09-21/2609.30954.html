Multilingual LLMs decide their output language at the first generated token, in a single deep-layer broadcast hub that survives instruction tuning.

FTB Graph: Determining and Validating First-token Broadcasters and Language-Identity Head Circuits in Multilingual Language Models

Validation table ↘

Why this matters

Pinpoints where multilingual LLMs switch language — and shows EAP linear attribution diverges from true causal interventions in fp16, a methodological warning for anyone screening circuits by attribution scores.

Critical edge

Only two of six models give clean positives: GPT-2 and Pythia-1B pass degenerately (1 and 5 control heads), and both Qwen2.5-1.5B variants invert the necessity check outright — a case study, not an architecture-independent law. Limitations ↘

01

Methodology — six-stage circuit-discovery pipeline

6 models · 124M–2.8B 21 prompts × 7 languages fp16 end-to-end ≤2,000 candidate edges / model 816–1,016 verified edges
S1

Prompt suite

21 hand-authored neutral items across EN / FR / ES / VI / ZH / RU / JA, each pairing a target with a foil language. GPT-2 drops Vietnamese → 16 prompts.

S2

LIHA attribution

Patch every head's first-token activation target↔foil; score the flip in target-language log-prob. Top head: layer 27 / head 6 in both Qwen variants.

S3

EAP screening

First-order Taylor estimate per edge, one backward pass per sample; FP16 active clamping (±10,000 prime, 65,504 ceiling, NaN filter); cap 2,000 edges, da=8.

S4

Exact-patching sign check

Substitute corrupted activations into clean runs (K=250, Nval=100); keep an edge only if the causal delta's sign agrees with the EAP score.

S5

DAG extraction

Kept edges form each model's first-token language-routing graph; full verified DAGs are released for all six models.

S6

Boundary ablations

Necessity (in-graph ablation should hurt) and out-of-graph irrelevance. No sufficiency check is run. Results ↘

Arms: standalone — GPT-2, BLOOM-560M, Pythia-1B / 2.8B (no shared tokenizer or architecture); controlled pair — Qwen2.5-1.5B Base ↔ Instruct (identical tokenizer and architecture), enabling a direct read of instruction tuning on circuit structure.

03

Strengths

  • Breadth across architectures (GPT-2, BLOOM, Pythia, Qwen; 124M–2.8B) is rare at this study scale, and the Base↔Instruct pair gives a genuinely clean pretraining-vs-tuning comparison.
  • The exact-patching verification stage is the right design — demanding sign agreement between linear EAP scores and true causal deltas, under an explicit FP16 clamping defense, where many EAP-only papers skip the check.
  • Uncommon honesty: the paper foregrounds its own negative results (EAP divergence, Qwen inversion) and explicitly tells readers to discount its PASS/INVERTED labels.
  • Inspectable artifacts: full verified DAGs for all six models in supplementary material; deterministic, fixed-budget pipeline that is straightforward to reimplement.
04

Limitations

  • 21 evaluation prompts (~3 per language; 16 for GPT-2) vs the 200–300 per-language scale the authors themselves cite; no per-language breakdown is possible.
  • fp16-only execution: EAP is most fragile exactly where low-precision gradients matter (5/6 negative r); the decisive fp32 rerun is deferred to future work.
  • Generality rests on two models: degenerate GPT-2 / Pythia-1B controls, and both Qwen variants fail the necessity check.
  • Missing rigor: no sufficiency check, no statistical tests, no ACDC / edge-pruning baseline, no hyperparameter sensitivity, no stated code or dataset release.
  • All models ≤2.8B; Pythia-2.8B's hub at ~50% depth already breaks a naive "deeper with scale" trend, and the authors disclaim scaling claims beyond this range.
05

Questions for further exploration

  1. Q1Would an fp32 rerun of even one model (e.g., Qwen2.5-1.5B) flip the inverted necessity check — or confirm it as a true architectural difference rather than a precision artifact?
  2. Q2Does the deep-layer broadcast hub hold for genuinely multilingual, instruction-tuned models at larger scale (Aya-Expanse-8B was considered but excluded)?
  3. Q3Would a sufficiency check — restricting computation to only in-graph heads — show the circuit is causal rather than merely correlated?
  4. Q4How does the hub behave on the constructed-but-unused code-switched prompts, where the language decision is context-dependent rather than prompt-determined?
  5. Q5Can the necessity margins be put on statistical footing (paired permutation or bootstrap over the 21 prompts) — and do the PASS/INVERTED labels survive?
