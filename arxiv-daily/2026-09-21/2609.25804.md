> **一句话总结：** 本文提出"品味"（taste）——智能体在长程任务的决策分叉点、在结果可见之前选出更优方向的能力——并从已有智能体轨迹中自动挖掘分叉构建了 502 题的 Taste-Bench：14 个前沿模型中最好的 GPT-5.6 Sol 仅得 59.7%（随机基线 25%），且当决定性证据出现得越晚，14 模型平均准确率从 62.3% 跌至 21.0%，加大推理预算也无济于事（−0.2 ~ +2.2 分）；随后用"特权教师"蒸馏把品味注入 Qwen3.6-27B，使其在未见任务上的判断准确率从 30.0% 提升到 47.9%，并让固定 executor 在 41 个 held-out SWE-bench Pro 任务上的端到端成功率从 14.6% 提升到 33.7%（正确建议上限 39.0%）。

> **TL;DR:** The paper defines "taste" as an agent's ability to pick the better direction at a decision fork before later work reveals the outcome, and builds Taste-Bench: 502 binary choice questions mined fully automatically from agent trajectories (hindsight labels from recorded outcomes) across software engineering (390 Qs from GPT-5.4/GPT-5.5 rollouts on 517 SWE-bench Pro tasks) and AI research (112 Qs from 47 RE-Bench/HCAST tasks). Under a strict both-orders protocol (random guess = 25%), the best of 14 frontier models, GPT-5.6 Sol, scores only 59.7% (GPT-5.5: 59.5%); mean accuracy collapses from 62.3% to 21.0% as the deciding evidence moves later in time, and raising the reasoning budget changes accuracy by at most +2.2 points. Distilling a privileged teacher (same Qwen3.6-27B base shown the correct answer) into a student lifts held-out judgment accuracy from 30.0% to 47.9% (+17.9 pts) and raises a fixed executor's success on 41 held-out SWE-bench Pro tasks from 14.6% (no advice) to 33.7% (student advice), vs a 39.0% correct-advice upper bound.

## 核心贡献

- **形式化"品味"并证明可自动测量**：将品味定义为在决策分叉处（fork）从两个候选方向中选出期望结果更优者的能力；利用同一任务多次尝试共享等价前缀这一事实，用各分支事后记录的真实结果（supported candidate）作为标签，无需任何人工专家标注。
- **构建并发布 Taste-Bench**（502 题）：两种互补构造——并行轨迹（同一任务多次独立尝试在分叉处走向不同方向，捕捉智能体"从未察觉的错误"）和绕路轨迹（单次运行中智能体自行纠错，捕捉"事后改正的错误"）；2×2 设计（构造 × 领域）：工程 390 题、研究 112 题。从 4,657 个候选分叉经三重 LLM 过滤（平凡/不可判定/评审一致）后保留 10.8%；人工复核 100 题，与挖掘标签一致率 98.8%（170/172），双评审互一致 98.6%、Cohen's κ = 0.973。
- **系统评测 14 个前沿模型并给出三个负向/结构性发现**：(1) 最佳模型仅 59.7%，无一接近解决；(2) 按四档"时间视界"分层，平均准确率从 in-prefix 的 62.3% 降至 more-work 的 21.0%（随机 25%）；(3) 三档推理预算下准确率几乎不变（GPT-5.6 Sol −0.2 分、GPT-5.6 Luna +2.2 分），且模型在准确率最低的档位消耗最多推理 token。
- **证明品味可训练且能转化为端到端收益**：任务不相交的两折划分 + SDPO 式前向 KL token 级蒸馏（教师 = 同一冻结基座模型加"正确答案演示"上下文，在教师采样续写上算 KL），held-out 判断准确率 30.0% → 47.9%（双序均值 42.7% → 62.4%）；把学生判断写成 advice 注入固定 Qwen3.6-27B executor，41 个 held-out SWE-bench Pro 任务成功率 14.6% → 33.7%。
- **证明品味 ≠ 端到端成功率**：Taste-Bench Average 与 SWE-bench Verified 相关仅 r = +0.63（R² = 0.39），最接近的工程子集 r 仅 +0.37；SWE-bench Verified 上排名前四、相差不足 4 分的模型在 Taste-Bench 上相差 10.7 分。

## Key Contributions

- Formalizes **taste** — choosing the better direction at a decision fork before any evidence is visible — and shows it can be measured automatically from hindsight over existing trajectories: branches of parallel attempts (or a detour + recovery) that share an equivalent prefix, labeled by the recorded outcome, with no human annotation in the loop.
- Builds and releases **Taste-Bench**, 502 questions in a 2×2 design (parallel/detour construction × engineering/research domain; 390 engineering + 112 research). From 4,657 candidate forks, only 10.8% survive filtering for trivial and undecidable questions; human review of 100 sampled questions shows 98.8% agreement with mined labels (170/172 explicit A/B judgments) and reviewer-vs-reviewer agreement of 98.6% with Cohen's κ = 0.973.
- Evaluates **14 frontier models** (Claude, GPT, Grok, DeepSeek, GLM, MiniMax, Mistral families) under a strict both-orders protocol (random guessing = 25%, always-same-position = 0%): the best model, GPT-5.6 Sol, reaches only 59.7%; accuracy falls with the time horizon of the deciding evidence (62.3% at in-prefix → 21.0% at more-work, near/below random), and a larger reasoning budget does not help (−0.2 to +2.2 points across three effort settings, 6,024 responses).
- Shows **taste is trainable**: an SDPO-style token-level distillation from a privileged teacher into Qwen3.6-27B (LoRA) improves held-out judgment accuracy by +17.9 points (30.0% → 47.9%) on task-disjoint questions, and the student's advice raises a fixed executor's success rate on 41 held-out SWE-bench Pro tasks from 14.6% to 33.7%, against a 39.0% correct-advice ceiling.
- Shows taste is **not a restatement of end-to-end success**: Taste-Bench Average correlates with SWE-bench Verified at only r = +0.63 (R² = 0.39), and only r = +0.37 on the engineering subset mined from SWE-bench Pro tasks.

## Methodology

**Formal setup.** Attempts at the same task sometimes share an equivalent trajectory prefix h_t = (o_0, a_1, ..., o_t) and then diverge into different actions — a *decision fork* with two candidate directions c_1, c_2. Each branch runs to completion and produces evidence E_i (test results, research scores). A measure U maps evidence to a scalar; the fork is labeled with the *supported candidate* y = argmax_i U(E_i), i.e., the branch whose realized outcome is better. A taste question is x = (q, h_t, c_1, c_2) with everything after the fork hidden; a model's taste is the fraction of questions where it picks y.

**Two constructions.** (1) *Parallel trajectories*: pairs of independent attempts at the same task with opposite outcomes at their divergence fork; the prefix becomes the question context, the two directions are rewritten as short neutral candidates. This captures wrong directions the agent never notices. (2) *Detour trajectories*: within a single run, a generator model reads the full trajectory plus outcome and locates, in order, the step where the agent takes a direction, the observed failure, and the step where a different direction completes the task; it rejects the trajectory if the better direction is only nameable after seeing the failure. This captures mistakes the agent corrects itself.

**Mining and filtering.** The engineering pool contains 2,677 graded rollouts from GPT-5.4 and GPT-5.5 agents on 517 SWE-bench Pro tasks; the research pool contains 1,132 agent runs on 47 AI R&D tasks from RE-Bench and the research subset of HCAST (public METR MALT transcripts). A generator model proposes candidate forks under a rubric; a set of judge models (excluding the generator) then filters: each judge first answers the question seeing only the two candidates — if every judge answers correctly, the question is trivial and discarded; then each judge reads the full record and a question is released only if every judge agrees with its label (removes undecidable questions). 4,657 candidate forks → 10.8% pass → 502 questions (2×2: construction × domain).

**Quality control.** Human review: two reviewers independently choose a direction for 100 sampled questions after reading continuation summaries and outcomes; of 172 explicit A/B judgments, 170 (98.8%) agree with the mined label; on the 74 questions where both reviewers commit, they agree with each other 98.6% (κ = 0.973). Each fork is also annotated with a four-level *time horizon* (in-prefix / inferable / next-step / more-work) by a judge model that sees the full record.

**Evaluation protocol.** Every question is presented twice, in a deterministic seeded order and in the exact reverse order; *accuracy* counts a question correct only if both orders are answered correctly (random guessing = 25%; a model that always prefers the same position scores 0%). *Mean accuracy* averages the two orders. 14 frontier models are evaluated under one interface and token budget; two models are additionally run under three reasoning-effort settings (six conditions, 6,024 responses). A comparison with SWE-bench Verified covers 11 models (three excluded for unparsable responses on >9% of presentations).

**Distillation.** Base model Qwen3.6-27B with LoRA adapters. The 390 engineering questions are split into two *task-disjoint* folds (train on one, evaluate on the other). Teacher and student are the same frozen base model with different contexts: the teacher additionally receives a short *demonstration* describing the supported candidate, so its freely generated reasoning reliably lands on the correct choice; training aligns the teacher-generated tokens under the student context (question + prefix + shuffled candidates only) via an SDPO-style token-level forward-KL computed on continuations sampled from the teacher rather than the student.

**End-to-end evaluation.** For 41 held-out SWE-bench Pro tasks, forks are mined from earlier runs on those tasks and each fork's judgment is written as a one-note *advice* (situation, candidate to avoid, candidate to take) available before the new run starts. A fixed Qwen3.6-27B executor completes each task under three settings: *no advice*, *student advice* (distilled student answers every fork question), and *correct advice* (advice is always right — an upper bound). Success is measured with the official SWE-bench Pro evaluation.

## Results & Highlights

- **Frontier models have limited taste.** On the 502-question Taste-Bench under the strict both-orders protocol, GPT-5.6 Sol is the best model at 59.7% Average accuracy and GPT-5.5 is close behind at 59.5%, while the other 12 models disperse far below — against a 25% random-guess floor (and 0% for always-same-position). No model is close to solving the benchmark.
- **Errors concentrate at long horizons.** Averaged over the 14 models, accuracy falls monotonically across the four time-horizon levels: 62.3% at in-prefix → 42.9% at inferable → 31.5% at next-step → 21.0% at more-work — the last level below even the 25% random floor.
- **More reasoning does not buy taste.** Moving from the lowest to the highest reasoning-effort setting changes accuracy by only −0.2 points for GPT-5.6 Sol and +2.2 points for GPT-5.6 Luna (2 models × 3 settings, 6,024 responses total), with the settings overlapping at every horizon level; both models emit their most reasoning tokens at the more-work level where their accuracy is lowest.
- **Taste is partially independent of end-to-end success.** Across 11 models, Taste-Bench Average correlates with SWE-bench Verified at Pearson r = +0.63 (R² = 0.39), and only r = +0.37 on the engineering subset — the subset mined from SWE-bench Pro, i.e., the closest comparison; the four top SWE-bench Verified models sit within 4.0 points of each other there but are 10.7 points apart on Taste-Bench.
- **Distillation transfers to unseen tasks.** On the training fold, single-order correctness jumps from 48.6% to 92.9%; on the task-disjoint held-out fold, the student reaches 47.9% vs the base model's 30.0% (+17.9 points; mean-over-orders 42.7% → 62.4%) — still below GPT-5.6 Sol's 56.9% on the same questions.
- **Better judgment becomes task success.** On 41 held-out SWE-bench Pro tasks, the fixed executor's success rate rises from 14.6% (no advice) to 33.7% with student advice (+19.1 points), capturing most of the 39.0% correct-advice upper bound (+24.4 points).

## Critical Assessment

- **Labels are single realizations, not expectations.** The supported candidate is defined as argmax U(E_i) over one realized outcome per direction (Eq. 1's expectation estimated from a single sample per branch). The rubric only requires that outcomes "clearly follow from the judgments," and this gate is enforced by LLM judges, not by controlled re-runs — so execution quality and stochasticity remain confounds for some questions, particularly in the research pool where outcomes (e.g., ML experiment scores) are noisy.
- **The fork distribution is anchored to two specific mining models.** All 390 engineering questions come from GPT-5.4/GPT-5.5 rollouts, so the benchmark probes decisions those particular models happened to face (and mishandle); evaluating other models on these forks measures transfer of judgment to another agent's failure points, not the evaluated model's own decision distribution. The paper does not test how scores would shift if forks were mined from a different agent's trajectories.
- **The research leg is thin.** 112 of 502 questions come from only 47 RE-Bench/HCAST tasks, so the "research taste" column rests on a small task base, and the domain-level splits reported in Figure 3 have correspondingly wide intervals.
- **Human validation is partial and filtered.** Agreement is excellent (98.8%, κ = 0.973), but it covers only 100 of 502 questions, and judgments without a clear A/B preference were excluded from the agreement analysis (172 counted judgments, 74 two-reviewer questions) — a lenient inclusion rule that leaves the hardest, most ambiguous questions least characterized.
- **The end-to-end gain lacks a non-trivial advisor baseline in the main text.** Advice is mined with hindsight from earlier runs on the same 41 tasks, and the student is only compared against "no advice" (14.6%) and "correct advice" (39.0%). An obvious control — prompting a strong untrained LLM (e.g., the GPT-5.6 family, or the base Qwen3.6-27B itself) as the advisor — is absent from the main results, so the 14.6% → 33.7% gain conflates distilled judgment with the value of any hindsight-derived hints; a distillation advantage claim needs the untrained-advisor row.
- **Single base model, single recipe for the distillation claim.** All distillation results use Qwen3.6-27B + LoRA with two folds; the main text argues label-fitting would "memorize" but does not report that naive baseline number alongside the reasoning-distillation numbers, and no scale or hyperparameter ablation appears in the main results.
- **Below-random accuracy at long horizons deserves a deeper bias analysis.** The 21.0% more-work accuracy sits below the 25% random floor even under the both-orders protocol, hinting at residual choice-ordering or candidate-wording artifacts that the protocol reduces but does not eliminate.
- Naming "taste" is partly branding: the construct is closer to contextual decision judgment, and it sits near process-reward and step-level credit work (Agent-as-a-Judge, Who&When, etc.); the genuine delta is the hindsight fork labeling plus the measure → distill → advise loop, which is real but should be read against that adjacent line of work.

## Strengths

- A genuinely new measurement target: per-decision judgment quality, distinct from end-to-end task success, with an explicit demonstration that the two only partially correlate (r = +0.63 overall, +0.37 on the closest subset).
- Fully automatic, scalable label construction with no human annotation in the loop, validated by unusually strong human agreement (98.8%, κ = 0.973) and a filtering pass that provably removes questions solvable from candidate wording alone.
- Methodologically careful evaluation: both-orders strict scoring kills position bias, a well-defined 25% random floor, 95% bootstrap intervals, and a reasoning-budget control experiment that cleanly rules out "just think longer" explanations.
- The distillation loop closes the arc from measurement to improvement: task-disjoint folds, teacher-sampled SDPO targets with explicit leakage control, and an end-to-end test on held-out SWE-bench Pro tasks showing the judgment gain converts to success-rate gain (14.6% → 33.7%).
- Benchmark, code, and a detailed reproducibility statement are all released.

## Limitations

- Hindsight labels from single branch outcomes cannot distinguish a good judgment from a lucky execution, especially for noisy research tasks.
- Engineering questions inherit the failure-mode distribution of GPT-5.4/GPT-5.5 agents; the research subset covers only 47 tasks (112 questions).
- The advice pipeline requires forks pre-mined from prior runs on the same tasks — there is no online fork detection at execution time, so the setup is not yet a deployable advisor.
- Distillation is demonstrated on one 27B base model with LoRA; the student (47.9%) still trails GPT-5.6 Sol (56.9%) on the same questions, and no naive-label-fitting or scale ablations appear in the main results.
- Coverage is limited to coding and ML-research domains; whether "taste" transfers across domains (e.g., train on engineering, test on research) is untested — training and evaluation are within-domain throughout.

## Relevance

与最高优先级方向"自进化智能体与自动化研究"高度契合：Taste-Bench 用自动化的事后标签法量化了长程自主智能体（含 AI R&D 研究任务）在关键决策点的判断质量，并证明该能力可被蒸馏进模型、转化为下游任务成功率——这既是自动化基准设计的一个漂亮范例，也直接触及长时程自主系统的可靠性评估问题。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would multi-seed branch outcomes (several rollouts per direction) yield cleaner expectation estimates than single-realization labels, especially for the stochastic research tasks — and how many of the 502 labels would flip?
- Can the student advisor detect forks online at execution time, rather than relying on forks pre-mined from earlier runs on the same tasks?
- How much of the 14.6% → 33.7% end-to-end gain would a strong *untrained* advisor (prompted frontier model or the base Qwen3.6-27B) already recover? This control is needed to isolate the value of distillation.
- If forks were mined from a different agent's trajectories (e.g., Claude rollouts), would model rankings on Taste-Bench change?
- Does taste distill across domains (train on engineering forks, evaluate on research forks), and does the +17.9-point transfer gain hold at other base-model scales?
