Under neutron irradiation, AXI transactions completed on time with wrong data — 18 silent corruption events invisible to watchdogs.

First beam-level link between two silos — FPGA routing-delay sensing and AXI-interconnect fault injection — observed simultaneously on one live Zynq UltraScale+ MPSoC.

From Routing Delay Shifts to Silent Data Corruption: Neutron-Induced SEU Effects in AXI-Based Zynq UltraScale+ MPSoCs
Mostafa Darvishi · ÉTS Montréal / Evolym Optiks · IEEE Access (under review)
fpga-reliability seu neutron-irradiation axi-interconnect silent-data-corruption
Key metric
0.500

P̃(SDC | D) — probability that a detected routing-delay event is followed by silent data corruption.

4 of 8 routing-delay events, within the ΔT association window. Fluence 6.0×10¹⁰ n/cm².

Why this matters

Radiation-exposed FPGA systems can pass every timeout check while silently returning wrong data; reliability testing needs an oracle outside the beam.

Watchdogs and timeouts saw 35 failures. They missed all 18 SDCs — the largest single class.

Critical edge

The headline 0.500 rests on 4 events out of 8 — no confidence intervals printed, and the ΔT window width is never reported.

Cross-sections also conflict between text (×10⁻¹²) and Table III (×10⁻¹²); the title reads more causal than 4 co-occurrences support.

Methodology how it was measured

  • Dual-board oracle. Irradiated ZCU104 DUT inside the TRIUMF beam; golden-reference supervisor outside it, joined by a deterministic FMC link — the correctness check never shares the radiation field.
  • Live AXI traffic. PS as AXI master → AXI Interconnect → replicated slave accelerator IPs (+ GPIO, UARTLite); a custom checker IP captures returned data, response, timeout, flag.
  • Routing-delay sensing. 8 two-LUT ring oscillators, feedback deliberately routed through long switch-matrix chains (routing-dominated), placed in/around the interconnect; each mapped to configuration frames (Fig. 6) for spatial attribution.
  • Controlled injection. SEM Ultra controller via ICAP perturbs selected RO-associated frames with restore, mirroring beam events per region (Fig. 14).
  • Five-class taxonomy. Routing Delay · Availability Failure · SDC (on time, no error flag, wrong data) · Detectable Protocol Error · Correlated Event (ΔT window) — then Poisson cross-sections σ=N/Φ and P̃(F|D)=NFD/ND.
  • Procedure. 24 h baseline (±0.005 band, 100k transactions, 0 failures) → continuous-traffic beam run → post-run validation → injection campaign. results ↓

Also in the data
  • Spatial, not global: step-like drops localize to RO_1/RO_3/RO_6 (e.g. RO_1 → ≈0.994, persisting) while RO_2/RO_5 stay quiet — under beam and matching frame injection.
  • Per-region agreement: RO1/RO6 frames → SDC (recovered); RO3 → availability failure (reset required); RO7 → protocol error. Mechanism argued: timing-margin erosion.

Strengths what holds up

  • Oracle outside the field: the dual-board golden reference eliminates the “checker was corrupted too” objection — the right way to catch SDC at all.
  • Defensible noise floor: 24 h pre-beam, all 8 ROs inside ±0.005, 100k transactions clean, SEM clean — before any event is attributed to radiation.
  • Beam + injection in one frame-aware framework: the per-region agreement (Fig. 14) is more convincing than either mode alone; the frame map makes RO frequencies spatially interpretable.
  • Auditable by design: machine-checkable event taxonomy and Poisson statistics (Tables I–III) other groups can reuse.
  • Unusually honest limitations section — and a practically valuable warning: watchdog-only evaluation looks robust while silently corrupting data (18 of 53 failures were exactly that).

Limitations what to discount

  • Thin statistical power: 8 delay events, ≤53 failures, one campaign; conditional probabilities rest on 2–4 events each, with no printed CIs and no chance-level control.
  • ΔT never quantified — the association metrics are not reproducible as printed, and no sensitivity analysis varies the window.
  • Partial observability: 8 ROs sample only selected frames; unmonitored regions can hide perturbations, and RO placement itself perturbs the routing under test (tradeoff unquantified).
  • Single everything: one ZCU104, one AXI topology, one workload, one self-funded run; not yet peer-reviewed.
  • Injection ≠ beam: cannot reproduce charge deposition or multi-bit upsets; mechanism (setup-slack erosion) is inferred from adjacent RO proxies, never measured on an actual AXI path.
  • Editorial defects: ×10⁻¹²-vs-×10⁻¹⁰ discrepancy and the 0.375-vs-0.250 P̃ mismatch need correction.

Questions for further exploration open threads

  1. Would the 4/8 SDC association survive a shuffled-window or varied-ΔT chance-level test?
  2. Can timing-margin erosion be confirmed by delay sensors on the AXI transaction paths themselves, not adjacent ROs?
  3. How do cross-sections and the SDC fraction scale with clock frequency, SmartConnect-style topologies, and other device families (Versal, Kintex UltraScale+)?
  4. Do IDF, selective scrubbing, or RO-triggered scrubbing reduce P̃(SDC|D) specifically — not just total failure counts?
  5. What share of SDCs come from data-path delay erosion versus valid/ready handshake skew; can one multi-bit frame upset generate them?
  6. What is the quantitative observability-versus-intrusiveness tradeoff of adding RO coverage in the interconnect fabric?
