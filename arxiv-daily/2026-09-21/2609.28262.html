Why this matters

A cheap, reproducible route to GPU-free edge inference acceleration — plus a graph-fragmentation finding that corrects the cost models behind ONNX Runtime / TVM-style quantization toolchains.

Critical edge

“Completely avoids degradation” is an overclaim — EfficientNet-B0 still loses 4.0 points on M1, and every accuracy result comes from one synthetic dataset.

“Collapse” = >15-pt Top-1 drop, a threshold the authors themselves admit is arbitrary.

01Methodology
  1. 1 Profile

    Quantize one layer at a time (OAT); score each by Jensen–Shannon divergence between FP32 and INT8 final classification logits. Weights and activations share one bit-width, collapsing a 2L search space into per-layer costs. No gradients, no GPU.

  2. 2 Cluster

    1-D K-Means (K = 5) finds natural INT8/FP32 break points; centroid rank maps inversely to bit-width — lowest cluster → INT8, outlier clusters stay FP32. Fixed percentiles {25/50/75/90} score negative silhouette: they fragment structurally identical layers.

  3. 3 Generate

    One candidate policy per centroid — everything at or below μk goes INT8, the rest FP32. K candidates replace an ILP/RL solver search entirely.

  4. 4 Select

    Measure the K candidates on-device, build the accuracy–latency Pareto front, keep the knee point (max curvature). The single chosen policy then transfers unchanged across both ARM64 boards.

Setup — ResNet-18 · EfficientNet-B0 · ConvNeXt-Tiny · TinyViT on the synthetic SuSy dataset (quantization-sensitive high-frequency artifacts); Apple M1 + Raspberry Pi 5 (Cortex-A76); ONNX Runtime node-level MPQ, INT8/FP32 only; collapse := >15-pt Top-1 drop vs FP32.

On TinyViT’s hardest Top-10% retrieval, JSD scores 0.833 vs HAWQ-V2’s 0.333; KL is empirically identical to JSD, which wins on symmetry and boundedness alone.

The negative result: graph fragmentation. Excluding “slow” layers from quantization inserts QDQ nodes that break operator fusion — isolated depthwise-conv latency rises 7.7 → 16.5 ms (2.1× slower), and latency-aware filtering is slower end-to-end in 4 of 8 configurations.

Worst case: ResNet-18 on RPi5 goes 20.8 → 49.7 ms (2.4× slower) for +0.5 accuracy points; mean accuracy of the two variants is statistically indistinguishable (0.50% vs 0.45%). This invalidates the additive per-layer latency model behind ILP-based MPQ. Separately, one fixed ONNX Runtime config spans ~5× speed-up to net slowdown across CPUs; on x86-64, INT8 is often slower than FP32 (OpenVINO recovers ≤ 1.4×).

03Strengths
  • Rigorous, transparent protocol — OAT ground truth, Recall@Top-N% retrieval with a justified precision≡recall equivalence, silhouette analysis, an explicitly defended collapse criterion.
  • A quantified negative result — fragmentation measured precisely (7.7 → 16.5 ms per isolated depthwise conv; 4/8 configs hurt), directly challenging the additive per-layer latency assumption in ILP-based MPQ. Actionable for ONNX Runtime / TVM-style tooling.
  • Genuinely deployable — solver-, gradient- and GPU-free; one policy transfers unchanged across two ARM64 platforms with TinyViT near-lossless (97.3 / 97.4 vs 97.5).
  • Honest scoping and reporting — x86-64 anomalies analyzed rather than hidden, every FP32/INT8 number tabulated, code released, claims kept inside ARM64 CPU deployment.
04Limitations
  • Single synthetic dataset, classification only — no ImageNet-scale natural-image or dense-prediction validation; rankings and collapse behavior may not transfer.
  • “No degradation” overclaims — EfficientNet-B0 drops 4.0 pts on M1; zero-collapse holds only under the arbitrary 15-point definition.
  • No end-to-end baseline — HAWQ-V2 enters only as a metric inside the authors’ own pipeline; no comparison against a real ILP- or RL-based MPQ system.
  • OAT ignores inter-layer error accumulation — mis-ranking is possible when errors compound; K = 5 and the knee-point rule are un-ablated hyperparameters.
  • Narrow hardware & precision scope — two ARM64 SoCs (M1 stretches “edge”), x86-64 profiling-only; binary INT8/FP32 with coupled weights+activations, no INT4 or per-channel.
05Questions for Further Exploration
  1. Q1Do the JSD ≥ Hessian / weight-space rankings hold on ImageNet-scale natural images and on dense-prediction tasks, where output-space geometry differs from SuSy’s high-frequency artifacts?
  2. Q2How does the cluster→bit-width mapping extend to multi-bit precision sets (INT4/INT8/FP16/FP32) or per-channel quantization, where inverse-centroid ordering is no longer trivial?
  3. Q3Can a subgraph-level, fragmentation-aware cost model replace the additive per-layer latency constraint inside ILP-based MPQ — solver optimality plus this paper’s empirical finding?
  4. Q4Does a single JSD + K-Means policy transfer to x86-64 with OpenVINO, and across runtimes (TFLite, TVM), or is per-runtime recalibration required?
  5. Q5How sensitive are final policies to K, to the calibration set used for OAT profiling, and to the joint-quantization error accumulation that OAT explicitly ignores?
