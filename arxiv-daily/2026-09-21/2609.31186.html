Evolutionary Safety of Recursive Self-Improving AI: Taxonomy, Risk Discovery, and Evaluation
Chang Gong, Jingping Bi, et al. — Institute of Computing Technology, CAS |
AI Safety Recursive Self-Improvement Agent Memory Safety Evaluation Survey
Key metric
+0.10

The paper’s only number — and it is hypothetical. Selection-induced risk shift when always accepting a 0.30-harm-rate proposal over a 0.10 one (pool mean 0.20). The authors state plainly it is “not an empirical result.” No experiments, benchmarks, or measurements anywhere.

Why this matters

A shared vocabulary and an actionable measurement checklist for how agent memory, skills, and updates erode safety over time — a framework to design auditable systems against, not new evidence.

Critical edge

Definitional, not empirical. Eqs. 1–5 are notation with no theorems, bounds, or even a toy simulation; the motivating permission-bypass incident is synthetic — the unifying claim is asserted, not demonstrated.

Methodology

Literature synthesis + formalization — no experiments
  1. Scope. A system counts as “persistent self-improvement” when it helps generate experience, proposes/selects a change, or edits its evaluator — and carries the result forward. Three dimensions: persistence, mutable scope, loop closure; ladder from Reflexion-style revisions → Voyager-style skill libraries → Darwin Gödel Machine / AlphaEvolve.
  2. Formal skeleton. State Sₜ = (Bₜ, Aₜ, θₜ); loop generate → select → commit (̄Eqs. 1–2), read through a biology lens: variation, selection, inheritance, co-evolution.
  3. Risk organization. Four research questions yield the 6 manifestations × 5 domains, grounded by one recurring synthetic example: a permission-bypass workaround stored as a reusable skill whose repairs later “routinely skip security tests.”
  4. Measurement design. Risk readouts with fixed evaluators; Eqs. 3–5 estimands; held-out replay for known risks vs adaptive red-teaming for emerging ones; discovered failures re-run on saved evaluator versions as regression tests.
  5. Governance derivation. Controls matched to each carrier, enforced outside the editable component.

Strengths

What holds up
  • First unifying account of safety-relevant change persisting across carriers — memory, skills, parameters, evaluators, hardware, updaters — with distinct tests: source removal, descendant testing, recovery re-testing.
  • Table 1 is immediately actionable: it turns “our update preserves safety” into the exact controlled comparison required, with matched baselines and replay streams.
  • Eq. 4 adaptive-vs-frozen contrast is a clean causal design prior agent-safety work rarely runs; retaining rejected candidates to reconstruct the proposal baseline is a subtle, valuable protocol detail.
  • Covers under-discussed carriers: generated RTL/hardware, embodied platforms, meta-level updater revision, multi-agent co-evolution.
  • Intellectually honest bookkeeping: numbers labeled non-empirical; metric-coverage growth separated from genuine behavioral change.

Limitations

What to watch for
  • Zero validation. No experiment or case study exercises any proposed metric; the protocol-to-instrument gap remains large.
  • Thin formalism: no identifiability analysis for Eq. 4 under task co-adaptation, no confounding treatment, no bounds linking loop closure to guarantees.
  • Fig. 2 bibliometric “widening gap” lacks stated corpus or query methodology — impressionistic.
  • Load-bearing evidence rests on unreviewed 2025–26 preprints (TAME, MemTX, EAL-Bench).
  • No prioritization: all 6 manifestations and 5 domains presented flat, with no argument for which carrier is most dangerous or most tractable.
  • Title inflation: “Risk Discovery” prescribes protocols but discovers no new risk; dense, abstract prose throughout.

Questions for Further Exploration

Open problems the paper names but does not solve
  1. Can a minimal public benchmark instantiate Eqs. 3–5 on a real self-improving coding agent — a Darwin Gödel Machine-style loop with instrumented memory writes?
  2. How should propagation depth and delay be measured once provenance is lost through summarization, without deleting useful capability along with derived artifacts?
  3. Is the Eq. 4 adaptive-vs-frozen contrast identifiable when tasks co-adapt with the system — does one replayed recorded stream really estimate the live-interaction effect?
  4. What is the capability cost of pre-commit gating and independent verification on high-throughput loops like AlphaEvolve, where the generator itself is being revised?
  5. Can theory bound how much safety erosion a sequence of accepted updates causes under a fixed admission rule, even for memory-write-only RSI?
