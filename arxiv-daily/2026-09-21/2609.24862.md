> **一句话总结：** 提出 TIMEVOLVE——在语言模型与数值预测器全部冻结的前提下，用"先提交全部候选预测、待真实值揭晓后统一回灌"的延迟反馈协议，联合在线更新专家信任（EvolveTrust）、智能体路径选择（EvolveReason）与干预强度（EvolveIntervene）三个小型策略模块；在 Time-MMD 八个域、十五个方法中取得最优平均 MSE/MAE 排名（1.250/1.375），在 8 个域中的 7 个同时拿到两项指标最低误差，且相对其冻结策略版本平均降低 6.3% MSE、7.6% MAE。

> **TL;DR:** TIMEVOLVE is a frozen-backbone agentic time-series forecaster that turns delayed deployment feedback into persistent updates of its orchestration policy. Every numerical expert forecast, candidate agent path, and intervention decision is committed before the target is revealed, so each realized outcome supervises *all* precommitted alternatives — not just the chosen one. Three coupled learners (EvolveTrust over experts, EvolveReason over evidence-grounded paths, EvolveIntervene over the correction gate) are updated via temporally valid predict-reveal-update steps while the LLM, forecasters, and prompts stay frozen. On Time-MMD's 8 domains it ranks 1.250 (MSE) / 1.375 (MAE) on average among 15 methods — best on both metrics in 7 of 8 domains (KairosAgent wins Economy: 0.160/0.325 vs 0.181/0.329) — beats the agentic baseline CastFlow in all 16 domain-metric comparisons, and improves over its frozen-policy counterpart TIMEVOLVE-STACK by 6.3% MSE / 7.6% MAE on average (largest gain: 17.7% MSE in Economy, 0.220 → 0.181).

## 核心贡献

- **延迟反馈的策略学习形式化**：把部署期预测建模为"从延迟结果反馈中学习策略"的问题。设计时间上严格有效的 predict–reveal–update 协议：在观测目标前提交全部专家预测、候选路径与干预决策，使每个真实值都能为**所有**备选方案（含未被选中者）提供监督信号，无未来信息泄漏。
- **TIMEVOLVE 三模块联合演化**：EvolveTrust 用四种可靠性视图（warm-up / online 逆误差加权 / 当前证据 / 模式视图）更新数值专家的信任混合；EvolveReason 用 5 个 pre-reveal 特征 + 共享滚动岭回归预测候选路径的 log-MSE 并选优；EvolveIntervene 学习门控 α∈[0,1] 决定所选路径对数值先验的修正强度，其回归标签由最小化已实现 MSE 的闭式解（投影后的 α̂ₜ）导出。三者共享同一次 reveal 的监督并原子化提交更新。
- **冻结底座、只演化编排**：LLM、数值预测器（Chronos-2、Toto-2.0、Falcon-X、TimesFM-2.5、Chronos-Bolt + 统计专家）、分析工具与提示词全部冻结，仅更新小型策略状态（信任权重、选择器系数 θ、门控系数 ω）——部署成本低且概念上清晰。
- **系统实证**：在 Time-MMD 全部 8 个域上对比 4 组共 14 个基线（agentic、零样本基础模型、全训练多模态、全训练单模态）；组件冻结消融、反馈覆盖消融与 Climate 双窗口案例回放共同定位增益来源。

## Key Contributions

- **Delayed-feedback policy learning formulation**: deployment forecasting is framed as policy learning from delayed outcome feedback, with a temporally valid predict-reveal-update protocol in which every realized target scores the *entire* precommitted alternative set (all experts and all candidate paths), providing counterfactual-free supervision for unselected actions.
- **TIMEVOLVE's three coupled learners**: EvolveTrust maintains a probability mixture over frozen numerical experts using four reliability views with a cold-start ramp; EvolveReason ranks candidate paths with a shared rolling ridge regression on five pre-reveal features predicting log-transformed MSE; EvolveIntervene learns the correction gate via weighted ridge regression toward a closed-form, projection-derived blend target α̂ₜ = clip((δₜᵀrₜ)Ωₜ/Dₜ, 0, 1). Updates are validated and committed atomically after each reveal.
- **Frozen-backbone design**: the language model, all numerical forecasters, analytical tools, and prompts remain frozen; only small orchestration states (trust weights, selector coefficients, gate coefficients) evolve, isolating the value of adapting coordination rather than the forecasters.
- **Empirical validation**: 8 Time-MMD domains vs 14 baselines in four groups, plus component-freeze ablations, feedback-coverage ablations, and a two-window Climate replay that visualizes how each module's update reshapes the next forecast.

## Methodology

**Setting.** A scalar target series with fixed horizon H. At origin t, history x₁:t and time-aligned context cₜ are available; a forecast issued at s supplies feedback only when s+H ≤ t. The learner state is Πₜ = (Πₜᵗʳᵘˢᵗ, Πₜʳᵉᵃˢᵒⁿ, Πₜⁱⁿᵗᵉʳᵛᵉⁿᵉ) with fixed aggregation G, and overlapping forecast windows remain pending until their targets elapse (masked inner products over observed coordinates Ωₜ handle missing values).

**Forecast construction (frozen).** FORECAST-PACK combines frozen time-series foundation models (Chronos-2, Toto-2.0 313M, Falcon-X, TimesFM-2.5, Chronos-Bolt) with statistical experts (level, moving average, robust trend, seasonality, spectral structure, state space, shift detectors). A frozen LLM reads the evidence stack (expert diagnostics, numerical context, text evidence split into report facts/predictions and search facts/predictions with availability intervals, target-overlapping items excluded) and proposes structured paths, each specifying an evidence interpretation, a nonempty expert subset Sₜʲ, and an optional correction Δₜʲ. The runtime projects trust weights onto the selected subset (source-budget projection limiting correlated expert families) and executes each path as aₜʲ = Σₖ ūₖʲ ḡₜᵏ + Δₜʲ.

**EvolveTrust (expert trust).** Four views of expert reliability: a warm-up view from training/validation forecasts, an online view over completed deployment windows using inverse-error weighting (pᵒⁿˡⁱⁿₑ,ₖ ∝ 1/(RMSEₖ+ε)), a current-evidence view of forecast plausibility, and a pattern view under previously observed evidence patterns. The mixture pₜ = Σᵥ βₜ,ᵥ pₜᵛ forms the prior bₜ = Σₖ pₜ,ₖ ḡₜᵏ; online contributions ramp in via ρₜ = Nₜ/(Nₜ+τ) to survive cold start. Every reveal updates all stored experts, not only those on the chosen path.

**EvolveReason (path selection).** Five pre-reveal features ψₜ = [1, continuity, in-range, smoothness, structural score] are computed from each candidate aₜʲ and the history. A shared rolling ridge model predicts ż = ψᵀθ where z = log(1+MSE²) (a monotone transform preserving order while compressing large errors), and the argmin candidate is selected. During cold start, the fixed structural score selects. Saved features/outcomes for *all* valid candidates, including unselected ones, feed the regression.

**EvolveIntervene (intervention strength).** With δₜ = aₜ − bₜ the selected path's deviation, the final forecast is ŷₜ = bₜ + αₜδₜ, αₜ = clip(gₜᵀωₜ, 0, 1) with features gₜ (intercept, shift diagnostics, expert disagreement, source-weight concentration, text availability). After reveal, minimizing Lₜ(α) = MSE_Ωₜ(bₜ+αδₜ, yₜ) yields the closed-form target α̂ₜ = (δₜᵀrₜ)Ωₜ/Dₜ (Dₜ = ‖δₜ‖²) projected onto [0,1]; loss geometry gives Lₜ(α) − Lₜ(α*ₜ) = wₜ(α−α*ₜ)² ≥ 0 with wₜ = Dₜ/mₜ. The gate coefficients are fit by energy-weighted ridge regression (weights clipped to [10⁻⁴,10⁴]), so intervention learning is identifiable only when Dₜ exceeds tolerance.

**Coordinated update.** The record Rₜ stores features, pre-reveal features, and committed decisions. At s+H the observed target produces expert losses, path losses, and the blend target; the learner validates all components and publishes the three updates to the latest state atomically. Episodic memory M keeps summarized context.

**Evaluation protocol.** Time-MMD's 8 domains (Agriculture, Climate, Economy, Energy, Environment, Security, Social Good, Traffic), chronological 70/10/20 splits, per-domain lookback and four configured horizons (mostly 6–12 steps at monthly cadence; Energy 12–48 weekly; Environment 48–192 daily), MSE/MAE in standardized target space, stride-one test origins, updates only from forecasts whose complete horizons have elapsed. Methods are ranked within each domain (ties share mean rank) and ranks are averaged.

## Results & Highlights

- **Best average rank among 15 methods**: TIMEVOLVE achieves average rank 1.250 (MSE) / 1.375 (MAE) across the eight Time-MMD domains — the best of all fifteen methods on both metrics — and posts the lowest error on both metrics in 7 of 8 domains. The exception is Economy, where KairosAgent leads with MSE/MAE 0.160/0.325 vs TIMEVOLVE's 0.181/0.329 (KairosAgent's entry carries an unexplained asterisk in Table 1); in Energy, zero-shot Toto-2.0 (313M) is best on both metrics (0.152/0.263 vs 0.180/0.309), yet TIMEVOLVE still ranks best overall by average.
- **Dominance over agentic baselines**: TIMEVOLVE beats CastFlow in all sixteen domain-metric comparisons and beats both MemCast and KairosAgent in fourteen of sixteen each — evidence that a frozen-backbone agent with an evolving orchestration policy outperforms agentic systems whose adaptation concentrates in forecasting components.
- **Policy evolution ablation (Table 2)**: versus TIMEVOLVE-STACK (all three policy components frozen at initialization), the full policy yields average domain-wise relative reductions of 6.3% in MSE and 7.6% in MAE; the largest single-domain MSE reduction is 17.7% in Economy (0.220 → 0.181) and the largest MAE reduction is 25.0% in Social Good (0.508 → 0.381). The complete policy is lower than every single-component freeze in all sixteen domain-metric comparisons.
- **Component ordering**: freezing EvolveTrust causes the largest degradation, followed by EvolveReason, then EvolveIntervene; in Economy, freezing trust / path selection / intervention increases MSE by 13.3% / 8.3% / 5.0% respectively.
- **Feedback coverage ablation (Table 3)**: all-alternative feedback (supervising every precommitted expert and path) beats selected-only feedback in all sixteen comparisons, with average domain-wise relative reductions of 5.0% in MSE and 6.3% in MAE; selected-only feedback itself beats the frozen policy in fourteen comparisons — direct evidence that scoring *unselected* alternatives, not just executed ones, is where the gain lives.
- **Case replay (Climate)**: in a post hoc selected two-window replay, updated trust shifts reliance to the recent-block trend expert, cutting the frozen-prior MSE from 0.0756 to 0.0322 (scaled space); path selection moves from "level consensus" (predicted MSE 0.062) to "moderated rise" (0.054), and the learned gate (0.941 → 0.635) prevents premature flattening, with gated MSE 0.061 vs 0.081 ungated.

## Critical Assessment

- **Algorithmic novelty is modest; the framing is the contribution.** The three learners reduce to inverse-error weighting (EvolveTrust's online view) and two rolling ridge regressions (EvolveReason, EvolveIntervene) — classical machinery. The genuinely new element is the protocol: pre-committing all alternatives so that one realized target supervises every expert and path, making unselected-action feedback possible without off-policy estimation. That framing is clean and temporally valid, but readers expecting a new learning algorithm will not find one.
- **Missing comparison against the paper's own cited online-learning baselines.** OneNet (online ensembling for concept drift) and MoE-F (filtered online gating of LLM mixtures) are discussed in Related Work as the closest methods, yet neither appears in Table 1, and no simpler baseline (e.g., plain exponential-weights aggregation over the same frozen expert pool) is run. The ablations only compare TIMEVOLVE against its own frozen variant, so it remains unknown whether simple online gating over the identical FORECAST-PACK could recover most of the 6.3% MSE gain.
- **Single-benchmark evaluation.** All results come from Time-MMD's eight domains — one dataset family, with most domains at monthly cadence, lookback 8, and horizons 6–12. No standard long-horizon benchmarks (ETT, Electricity, Traffic at customary settings) are reported, so generality beyond this deployment-styled, short-horizon regime is untested.
- **No uncertainty quantification and a rank-based headline.** The 1.250/1.375 average ranks are computed from single runs at three-decimal precision with mean-rank tie handling; no seeds, std devs, or significance tests are reported. Metric scales also differ wildly across domains (Security is a count series with MSE ≈ 58.9 vs ≈ 0.1–1 elsewhere), so equal-weight domain averaging hides heterogeneity.
- **Cherry-picked case study, acknowledged by the authors.** Appendix C.3 states the Climate replay case was "selected after outcome evaluation" — the qualitative Figure 3 story is illustrative, not confirmatory.
- **Small but real Loose ends**: the asterisk on KairosAgent's winning Economy entry (0.160/0.325*) is never explained in the main text we could read; and the Limitations appendix (App. A) is three sentences long, conceding only that predictive scope "depends partly on the available base models." No code release is stated, which limits reproducibility of the full policy pipeline despite the detailed appendix.

## Strengths

- Temporally rigorous design: updates use only forecasts whose complete horizons have elapsed; eligibility is enforced per expert via availability intervals; the "commit everything before reveal" structure is a principled way to get all-alternative supervision without off-policy correction.
- The frozen-backbone commitment makes the ablations genuinely informative: TIMEVOLVE vs TIMEVOLVE-STACK isolates policy evolution with an identical forecast-construction pipeline, so the 6.3%/7.6% gains are attributable to orchestration adaptation alone.
- Feedback-coverage ablation (Table 3) directly tests the paper's central hypothesis (supervise unselected alternatives) rather than only the overall system — a level of mechanistic care most agentic papers lack.
- Broad, current baseline set spanning four families (CastFlow, MemCast, KairosAgent; Aurora, TimesFM-3, Chronos-2, Toto-2.0, Sundial; T3Time, TimeCMA, CALF; PatchTST, DLinear) and consistent dominance over the agentic baselines across all 16 comparisons.
- Transparent protocol documentation (App. B: splits, cadences, lookbacks, horizons, rank/tie rules) and honest reporting of the one domain (Economy) where an agentic baseline wins.

## Limitations

- Predictive scope is capped by the frozen expert pool: if none of Chronos-2/Toto-2.0/Falcon-X/TimesFM-2.5/Chronos-Bolt/statistical experts fits a regime, the policy can only re-weight, not fix, a bad prior (the authors' own stated limitation).
- Evaluation restricted to Time-MMD's eight domains at short horizons (mostly lookback 8, horizon 6–12, monthly cadence); no long-horizon or non-Time-MMD evidence, and no deployment study beyond test-split replay.
- No empirical comparison with the online-learning methods the paper itself cites (OneNet, MoE-F, exponential-weights expert advice), leaving the necessity of the three-module design unproven.
- Single runs, no variance/significance reporting; headline is average rank, which can mask small absolute differences.
- Policy-state bookkeeping (features for every valid candidate at every origin, three rolling ridge fits, episodic memory) adds implementation complexity and memory growth on long deployments that the paper does not cost out.
- No code release mentioned; the case study is post hoc selected.

## Relevance

对同时关注 LLM 智能体与时间序列预测的方向非常有参考价值：它把"prediction with expert advice"式的经典在线学习接入 agentic time-series forecasting，核心洞察——真实值揭晓前先提交全部候选、让每个已实现结果监督所有备选路径——可以迁移到任何"多专家+多策略"的智能体编排系统。如果你在研究自我进化的智能体编排策略或部署期在线适应，这篇值得精读其 predict–reveal–update 协议与反馈覆盖消融。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Would a single classical online-gating baseline (OneNet-style ensembling or exponential-weights aggregation) over the identical frozen FORECAST-PACK expert pool recover most of the 6.3% MSE / 7.6% MAE gain, making the three-module ridge machinery unnecessary?
2. How does TIMEVOLVE behave on standard long-horizon benchmarks (ETT, Electricity, Traffic) with long lookbacks, and does the all-alternative feedback advantage persist when candidate paths number in the hundreds?
3. Are the three coupled updates stable over genuinely long deployments (months/years), or do trust, selector, and gate coefficients interfere or drift — is there a forgetting/regret analysis?
4. What is the computational and memory cost of committing and scoring every candidate path at every stride-one origin, and how does it scale with the expert-pool size?
5. Can the delayed-feedback protocol extend to intervention rules beyond scalar gating (e.g., structured corrections Δₜ), where identifiability of the supervision target (Dₜ > 0) may fail more often?
