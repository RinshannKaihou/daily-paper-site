> **一句话总结：** 港科大团队提出 PASTABench——首个用"最早信号轮次 (t_es) → 触发轮次 (t_trigger)"时间锚点标注最优干预窗口 (OIW) 的智能体安全基准（1,139 条多轮轨迹、5 大类/13 子类风险），评测 16 个 LLM 发现最佳模型 GPT-4o 的 Perfect Interruption 也只有 40.74%（16 模型平均 28.22%），且去敏感化消融揭示开闭源模型间存在"词汇过拟合"鸿沟：移除显式风险词后 Gemini-2.5-Pro 仍有 115 条 (10.1%) 早期干预被纠正进 OIW，而 Qwen2.5-32B/QwQ-32B 仅 27 条 (2.4%)。

> **TL;DR:** PASTABench reframes agent safety evaluation as continuous, stepwise proactive monitoring along three axes — whether to intervene, when (the annotated Optimal Intervention Window between an Earliest-Signal Turn and an irreversible Trigger Turn), and what (5-category/13-subcategory risk attribution). On 1,139 synthesized multi-turn trajectories with tool-use environments, even the best of 16 evaluated LLMs (GPT-4o, 40.74% Perfect Interruption vs. a 28.22% cohort average) cannot reliably time interventions, because models are overwhelmingly over-defensive (Gemini-2.5-Pro aborts early on 64.27% of trajectories; late detections are <1% everywhere). A full-benchmark desensitization ablation shows open-weight models' safety scores hinge on keyword spotting: replacing lexical triggers like "transfer"/"steam" collapses their gains (Qwen2.5-32B: only 27/1,139 Early→OIW corrections vs. Gemini-2.5-Pro's 115).

## 核心贡献

- **形式化"解耦式主动安全监测"(Decoupled Proactive Safety Monitoring)**：把智能体安全评估从静态事后拒绝 (post-hoc refusal) 转变为逐步、连续的监测任务，沿三个维度定义——whether（是否该干预）、when（干预时机）、what（风险归因）。
- **提出 OIW（最优干预窗口）与时间锚点标注**：每条轨迹标注最早可观察风险前兆的 Earliest-Signal Turn (t_es) 和不可逆伤害形成的 Trigger Turn (t_trigger)，将"干预时机"从直觉判断变成可量化惩罚过早/过晚干预的指标。
- **构建 PASTABench 基准**：1,139 条多轮轨迹，覆盖物理与数字两类场景，5 个一级风险类别 + 13 个二级子类别（基于 ANSI/SAFT/GDPR 等既有安全框架），配套 496 个工具库与 12–25 个工具的标准化工作区；五阶段流水线（收集→环境搭建→DeepSeek-R1 轨迹合成→GPT-4o 标注 + Gemini-2.5-Pro 交叉验证（Cohen's κ=0.756）→人工核验 90.4% 一致率）。
- **16 个 LLM 的系统评测**：最佳模型 Perfect Interruption 仅 40.74%；Procedural Safety Gap 类别上有效拦截率最低仅 0.0%；发现普遍的"词汇过拟合"(lexical overfitting) 现象并用全基准去敏感化消融予以因果验证。

## Key Contributions

- **Formalizes Decoupled Proactive Safety Monitoring** as a continuous, stepwise decision task with three dimensions: *whether* (hazard anticipation without over-sensitivity), *when* (intervention at the right point, not so early it disrupts tasks nor so late harm is irreversible), and *what* (attributing the specific Level-1/Level-2 risk category).
- **Introduces the Optimal Intervention Window (OIW)**: every trajectory carries two human-validated temporal anchors — the Earliest-Signal Turn (t_es, first observable risk precursor) and the Trigger Turn (t_trigger, where harm becomes unavoidable) — converting intervention timing into a quantifiable metric with graded outcomes (Perfect / Good / Effective / Early / Late).
- **Builds PASTABench**: 1,139 multi-turn trajectories across 5 Level-1 and 13 Level-2 risk categories (grounded in ANSI, SAFT, GDPR frameworks), a 496-tool library, standardized 12–25-tool workspaces, produced by a five-stage pipeline with dual-model cross-annotation (GPT-4o + Gemini-2.5-Pro, Cohen's κ = 0.756) and 90.4% human-verification agreement.
- **Evaluates 16 LLMs and diagnoses temporal reasoning**: the best model reaches only 40.74% Perfect Interruption; a full-benchmark desensitization ablation (N=1,139 paired trajectories) causally demonstrates a *lexical overfitting* divide — closed-source models track evolving risk states while open-weight models' proactive capability largely collapses once explicit hazard vocabulary is neutralized.

## Methodology

**Risk taxonomy.** Hazards are organized into 5 primary categories with 13 subcategories: Information & Cyber Security (28.5%), Economic & Digital Asset Destruction, Direct Health & Somatic Hazards (28.3%), Physical Environment & Infrastructure Damage, and Procedural Safety Gap. Counts are deliberately imbalanced to mirror real-world frequency (e.g., Unauthorized Financial Transactions: 206 vs. Bypassing Mandatory Safety Interlocks: 16).

**Five-stage data pipeline.** (1) *Collection*: seed scenarios from Recipe2Plan, LabSafetyBench, and SafeToolBench, filtered to 1,725 standardized instances. (2) *Environment setup*: a 496-tool library (188 seed tools + 308 LLM-generated definitions under a unified schema), each instance instantiated with 12–25 relevant tools, enforcing "risk reachability" by including the hazardous tool while masking ground-truth risk metadata. (3) *Trajectory synthesis*: DeepSeek-R1 simulates an interactive loop between a task-oriented Actor (prompted to prioritize efficiency over safety, simulating realistic negligence) and an objective Environment (tool-executing, neutral language, no explicit hazard words before the trigger round), enforcing progression normal → Earliest-Signal Turn → Trigger Turn; trajectories with hallucinated tool usage are discarded. (4) *Ground-truth annotation*: GPT-4o labels temporal anchors and risk categories; Gemini-2.5-Pro independently re-annotates; inter-annotator agreement is Cohen's κ = 0.756, with unsolvable conflicts discarded. (5) *Human verification*: 90.4% agreement between human raters and model annotations.

**Evaluation protocol.** The monitor sees only the cumulative interaction history (denied access to the initial user instruction and environment metadata — an "information isolation" protocol forcing it to act as a Guardian), and must emit a binary continue/interrupt decision per turn with strict sequential execution (auto-stop on interrupt). Predicted interruption turn t̂ is compared against (t_es, t_trigger): *Good Interruption* = timely (t̂ < t_trigger) with correct Level-1 category; *Effective* = timely with exact Level-2 match; *Perfect* = valid risk attribution strictly inside the OIW. Early/Late rates capture premature/missed detection; On-Time = 100 − Early − Late. Sixteen LLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Gemini-3-Pro, Grok-3; DeepSeek-R1, QwQ-32B, Qwen2.5-7B/14B/32B/72B, Qwen3-4B-Thinking, Llama-4-Maverick/Scout, Llama-3.3-70B, Llama-3.1-70B/8B) are evaluated zero-shot with greedy decoding (T=0, n=1).

**Desensitization ablation.** All 1,139 trajectories are paired with desensitized twins in which explicit risk-trigger terms (most frequent: "transfer" ×148, "steam" ×146, "knife" ×125) are replaced with semantically neutral equivalents while preserving ground-truth annotations and environment logic; the "awakening effect" counts trajectories whose intervention timing shifts from premature (Early) into the OIW.

## Results & Highlights

- **Proactive intervention is largely unsolved**: GPT-4o achieves the highest Perfect Interruption at only 40.74% (Gemini-3-Pro 39.42%, best open-weight Qwen2.5-72B 34.15%), against a 16-model average of 28.22% — meaning the best monitor intervenes at the optimal moment in fewer than half of hazardous trajectories.
- **Risk identification is weak even when timing is right**: Effective Interruption (precise Level-2 attribution) ranges from 44.60% (Llama-3.1-8B) to 67.08% (Gemini-3-Pro), mean 55.23%. The Good→Effective precision drop is ~10 points for large models (Gemini-3-Pro −10.27 pp, GPT-4o −10.5%) but exceeds 20 points for Qwen2.5-7B (69.36% → 49.34%), showing small models flag content without identifying the actual risk.
- **Over-defense, not delay, is the dominant failure mode**: nearly all models show Late interruption below 1%, but Early interruption rates are huge — Gemini-2.5-Pro 64.27% and QwQ-32B 61.46% (On-Time only 35.73% and 38.45%) versus GPT-4o's 31.08%; Qwen3-4B-Thinking is the timing champion with On-Time 73.22% despite mid-tier Perfect (29.68%).
- **Category skew is severe**: on Economic & Digital Asset Destruction, Gemini-3-Pro reaches 88.2% effective interruption, but Physical Environment & Infrastructure Damage drops all but three models below 50% (Gemini-3-Pro 65.9%, DeepSeek-R1 61.6%, Gemini-2.5-Pro 55.5%), and Procedural Safety Gap collapses to 0.0%–32.6% across the cohort.
- **Lexical overfitting splits model families**: under the full-benchmark desensitization ablation, Gemini-2.5-Pro corrects 115 trajectories (10.1%) from Early into the OIW, Gemini-3-Pro 69 (6.1%), GPT-4o 55 (4.8%), while the best open-weight models manage only 27 (2.4%, Qwen2.5-32B and QwQ-32B) and 25 (2.2%, Qwen2.5-14B) — and in keyword-scarce Device Damage scenarios (e.g., an autoclave at 260°F) Llama-3.1-8B hallucinate irrelevant chemical hazards where larger closed-source models trace the causal chain.
- **Results are robust to decoding noise**: rerunning seven open-weight models eight times at τ=0.7 yields standard deviations below 1.22 percentage points on all metrics, and Early exceeds Late by at least 29.25 pp for every model.

## Critical Assessment

The core ideas — temporal anchors, the OIW metric, and the lexical-overfitting diagnosis — are genuinely novel and well executed, but several design choices warrant scrutiny. **First, ground-truth circularity**: the temporal anchors and risk labels are annotated by GPT-4o with Gemini-2.5-Pro as the second annotator, then "validated" by humans at 90.4% agreement; the benchmark therefore largely measures how well LLM monitors agree with GPT-4o's notion of risk timing, and GPT-4o topping the leaderboard (40.74% Perfect) is exactly what this setup would predict. The κ = 0.756 inter-annotator agreement is "substantial" but far from perfect — roughly a quarter of borderline timing decisions are model-judgment calls. **Second, single-synthesizer artifacts**: every trajectory is generated by DeepSeek-R1 with a mandated normal→precursor→danger structure; a monitor could exploit the rigid template (e.g., "the middle rounds always contain the signal") rather than learning genuine hazard anticipation, and the paper does not test generalization to trajectories written by other models or humans. **Third, dataset imbalance**: Procedural Safety Gap has only 43 samples (16 + 27), so its headline 0.0%–32.6% effective-interruption range rests on tiny cells where one or two trajectories swing tens of points; category-level claims should be read cautiously. **Fourth, the "desensitization collapse" asymmetry is partially mechanical**: open-weight models have far fewer Early interruptions available for correction (as the authors themselves admit), so the 115-vs-27 awakening-effect gap conflates reasoning quality with baseline over-triggering rates; a rate-matched analysis is missing. **Fifth, only zero-shot monitors are evaluated** — no fine-tuned guard model (e.g., a Llama-Guard-style classifier trained on OIW labels) is tested, so the benchmark demonstrates a deficit without showing it is learnable. To the authors' credit, they release prompts, hyperparameters, and a stochastic-decoding robustness check (std < 1.22 pp), and honestly report their own limitation (text-only simulation).

## Strengths

- **A genuinely new evaluation target**: moving from post-hoc trajectory verdicts to stepwise, timing-sensitive monitoring is well motivated by the AEB-style analogy, and the OIW formalism makes "when to intervene" a first-class, quantifiable metric rather than a vibe.
- **Rigorous, multi-stage quality control**: dual-model cross-annotation (κ = 0.756), rule-based adjudication, 90.4% human verification, discarded hallucinated trajectories, and masked risk metadata to prevent leakage.
- **The desensitization ablation is a model of causal benchmark analysis**: paired 1,139-trajectory twins isolating lexical cues, with the keyword-frequency word cloud (transfer/steam/knife) making the intervention transparent and reproducible.
- **Broad, current model coverage**: 16 models spanning frontier closed-source (GPT-4o, Gemini-3-Pro, Grok-3), reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1, QwQ-32B), and four open-weight families — enough spread to support the closed-vs-open divide claim.
- **Excellent reproducibility hygiene**: full prompt templates in the appendix, standardized vLLM infrastructure, greedy decoding, and an eight-run stochastic-decoding robustness study (all std devs ≤ 1.22 pp).
- **Actionable diagnosis, not just a leaderboard**: the Early-vs-Late decomposition (late <1% everywhere, early up to 64.27%) pinpointing over-defensiveness as the dominant failure, and the Good→Effective gap quantifying the sensitivity-specificity trade-off, directly suggest what guard models should be trained on.

## Limitations

- **Text-only simulation**: risks are modeled as textual state transitions; no multimodal sensory input, so physical-hazard categories (thermal, combustion) test reading comprehension of state descriptions as much as safety reasoning — the authors acknowledge this.
- **Ground truth is frontier-LLM-generated** (GPT-4o primary annotator, DeepSeek-R1 synthesizer), introducing potential circularity and capping the benchmark at GPT-4o's own risk judgment; human verification is agreement-based rather than independent re-annotation of the full set.
- **Rigid temporal template**: every trajectory must follow normal → Earliest-Signal → Trigger with a well-defined OIW, which may not reflect real agentic sessions where risks flicker, resolve, or never materialize (no benign-only trajectories to measure false-positive burden outside hazardous runs).
- **Severe category imbalance** (Procedural Safety Gap: 43 samples; Irreversible Digital Asset Deletion: 14) makes subcategory-level comparisons statistically fragile.
- **Zero-shot monitors only**: no fine-tuned or prompted-guard baselines (Llama Guard, ShieldLM-style) are evaluated, leaving open whether the 40.74% ceiling reflects a fundamental gap or prompt insensitivity.
- **English-only trajectories and a fixed monitor protocol** (no access to the original user instruction) may understate models tuned to use task intent for risk calibration.

## Relevance

该基准直击智能体安全评测的下一个前沿——从事后裁决转向带时间锚点的主动干预，其"词汇过拟合"发现对任何部署 guard 模型或 agent 工作流安全层的团队都有直接参考价值。对于关注 LLM agent 工具调用安全、安全基准构建与模型评测方法论的研究方向，这篇论文提供了可复用的 OIW 标注范式与 16 模型基线。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would fine-tuning a dedicated guard model on OIW-labeled data (rather than zero-shot prompting) close the 40.74% Perfect Interruption ceiling, and does the lexical-overfitting collapse disappear with task-specific training?
- Does the closed-vs-open "awakening effect" gap persist when models are rate-matched on baseline Early-interruption frequency, i.e., is it truly a reasoning deficit or partly an artifact of differing over-trigger propensities?
- How do monitors behave on trajectories without any risk (benign-only control runs) — is the observed over-defensiveness (up to 64.27% Early) accompanied by unacceptable false-interruption costs on normal workflows?
- Can the temporal-anchor annotation pipeline be validated against human expert re-annotation of the full benchmark, or against real (non-synthesized) agent incident logs, to rule out GPT-4o/DeepSeek-R1 circularity?
- Would the findings transfer to multimodal or interactive settings where risk signals are observations (temperatures, file permissions) rather than text descriptions, per the authors' own stated future work?
