Methodology

FinalityBench. Single payment orders whose terminal outcome becomes fact only later; four systems learn through message streams perturbed by a seeded engine (delay, duplication, loss, reordering, half-commits, stale reads). Because episodes are deterministic, attribution becomes exact intervention.

  1. Replay as exact intervention. An episode is a pure function of (case, seed, policy, delivery schedule) — restoring one message re-runs one exact world, not a Monte Carlo draw.
  2. Joint do-operations. Substitute decisions do(Ak := a′) and delete messages do(fi := ⊥) in one causal model.
  3. Exact three-way split. loss = infrastructure effect + policy differential vs π* + reference residual — telescoping, signed, no residual bucket.
  4. Message-level Shapley. Signed per-message allocations; exact under 13 instances, permutation-sampled above (within 1.88% L1 of exact).
  5. Seven methods compared. From last-action to minimal sufficient set (but-for as a set, search capped at 3 messages — the cap never bound).

03

Strengths

  • No residual bucket. The telescoping identity leaves nothing unattributed to be misread as agent fault; signed terms express cases share-methods cannot (faults help the optimistic policy at −6.8%).
  • Fault families compose. Per-family pooling fails to reproduce the realised schedule on 82.9% of episodes — a sharp, reusable insight for fault-injection benchmark builders.
  • Overdetermination made concrete. The stratum operationalises the Halpern/Chockler hard case with messages; Shapley’s even $14.00/$14.00 split is the principled answer.
  • Decision-useful cost accounting. Remedy (2.3 replays) vs full allocation (280.1) is priced explicitly, with guidance on which to use for which goal.
  • Exemplary reproducibility. Released code, pinned artifact revisions, make reproduce; the limitations section matches what a critic would raise.
04

Limitations

  • No language model evaluated. All four policies are deterministic procedures; transfer to stochastic LLM agents — the systems CAR/CausalFlow/REFLECT actually target — is untested.
  • One environment. Infrastructure only corrupts observations, never moves money directly; prevalence figures are generator properties, not field rates.
  • Thin overdetermined evidence. 70 planted episodes; the 5.6% natural rate rests on CI 3.1–8.7% from one generated population.
  • The easy half is by construction. “Which side” is exact once faults are intervenable — the discriminating content is which message, limiting what Table II can carry.
  • Self-citation ecosystem. Setting, artifact and grading are all the author’s own prior work; independent replication on a second environment is the missing test.
05

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Does a resampling agent-step intervention converge to substitution-based regret when the policy is a stochastic LLM?
  • Do the conclusions survive where infrastructure moves money directly (a double-charge), not only corrupts observations?
  • Can per-family attribution be salvaged with interaction-aware corrections, or is compositionality a fundamental barrier?
  • What are plausible field rates for non-additivity (27.8%) and overdetermination (5.6%) in production payment pipelines?
  • Can the signed decomposition drop the single privileged reference — e.g. a partial order over reference policies?

Context. Single independent author (Abhishek Sharma, Senior Member IEEE) building on his own FinalityBench artifact — code, planted-episode generator and pinned revisions are released with make reproduce, and the paper’s honesty norms are unusually high. Independent replication by a second group remains the missing test.
