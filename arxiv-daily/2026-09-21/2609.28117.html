Best AP 0.5362 vs 0.4592 strongest baseline · EuroLLM ctx-5 NLL baseline collapses to AP 0.0050 IOI transfer: Pearson 0.6700 · AP 0.6505
Why This Matters

First systematic head-level causal-intervention analysis scaled to decoder-only LLMs for context-aware MT — a directly reusable screening protocol two orders of magnitude cheaper.

Critical Edge

“Beats baselines” means beating seven other gradient losses — no attention-rollout, integrated-gradients, or activation-patching comparison — and AP of 0.45–0.68 stays far from a precise screen.

01Methodology

  1. 1

    Contrastive setup. The model sees source + context and several target variants differing in one word; it is correct if the contextually right variant gets top probability (ContraPro en→de, LCPT en→fr, ctxPro adapted to en→pl/ru).

  2. 2

    Twelve token relations link ambiguous-phrase tokens (SP, TP) with context cues (SC, TC) and cross-lingual pairs.

  3. 3

    Backprop into attention maps, not parameters: per-head gradients on Sl,h, max-aggregated over token pairs, averaged over the dataset → a cheap proxy for each head’s intervention gain ΔA.

  4. 4

    TMM loss fires only where the model is wrong or nearly tied (μ=0) — mirroring contrastive evaluation itself.

  5. 5

    Top-k screen. Only gradient-selected heads get the expensive causal verification (Modifying Heads, C=0.99); ground truth = ΔA beyond the 99% CI. Follow-ups: C-sweep (0.01–0.99), IOI generalization, Head Tuning of Q/K projections.

LTMM = max(0, μ − P(θ(xcorrect)) + maxi P(θ(xwrongi))) μ = 0 — the loss rewards exactly the probability gap the intervention aims to increase.

03Strengths

  • +Attacks a real scalability blocker: a well-motivated surrogate for combinatorially expensive intervention sweeps in decoder-only LLMs.
  • +Unusually honest evaluation: 7 loss baselines, 3 metric families, rank analysis, and a C-sweep explaining why gradients work (monotonic after accumulation).
  • +Unprecedented scale for head-level CAMT: 4 models × 4 directions × 50 phenomena — yielding findings full sweeps cannot afford.
  • +Closed attribution-to-action loop: Head Tuning shows discovered heads matter under real fine-tuning, with general BLEU unchanged.
  • +Negative results reported, not buried: second places, insignificant phenomena, limits of Modifying Heads.

04Limitations

  • ×AP 0.45–0.68 is far from precise; Formality/Inflection ΔA results are not statistically significant at all.
  • ×Baselines confined to gradient-loss variants — no attention rollout, integrated gradients, or activation patching.
  • ×Ground truth comes from the same intervention being approximated, with a binary 99%-CI cut — noisy, mildly circular evaluation.
  • ×The 96% saving applies to discovery only; verification still requires the expensive interventions.
  • ×Head Tuning validated on one model, one phenomenon, one direction; 9B used restricted relations; no code release stated; “general-purpose head” claims rest on a handful of heads.

05Questions for Further Exploration

  • Would TMM screening survive comparison against non-gradient attribution families at the same evaluation budget?
  • Is the redundancy hypothesis for unresponsive heads (e.g. 14-9) testable via ablation or head-pair co-modification?
  • Does the gradient–ΔA correlation hold at larger scale, and does the top-1% retrieval property degrade?
  • Can the general-purpose heads (18-6, 18-14, 25-4, 20-2, 18-3) be characterized mechanistically, and do they emerge across pretraining checkpoints?
  • What limits fine-tuning from reaching the artificial-intervention upper bound (21-15: +3.58% vs +1.00%) — can regularization close it?
  • Could the ctxPro contrastive conversion be redesigned to preserve context-cue relations, restoring testable Formality/Inflection?
