> **一句话总结：** 本文用两个轻量级探针（cluster 探测 + L2 带宽探测，每卡不到 1 分钟、跨机器 100% 一致）逆向出 NVIDIA H200/B200 上被厂商隐藏的芯片级 SM→GPC 拓扑（floorsweeping 导致同型号不同卡 GPC 大小不一）和 L2/NUMA 分区哈希（XOR 哈希、4KB 粒度、远端 HBM 延迟比本地高 34%~67%），并证明把"物理资源位置"纳入细粒度调度后：GroupGEMM 提速最高 1.22×，mini-SGLang 多路复用推理（Qwen3-8B）解码吞吐提升 14.3%（H200）/10.4%（B200），多租户共置下避免最高 1.33× 的分配顺序性能方差。

> **TL;DR:** Die scaling has made modern GPUs physically asymmetric — floorsweeping makes the SM-to-GPC mapping chip-specific, and partitioned L2/HBM creates a 2-node NUMA topology — yet existing fine-grained schedulers only count logical resources. This paper reverse-engineers these hidden asymmetries on H200, B200, and H100 PCIe with two microbenchmark probes (<1 min per GPU, 100% agreement across machines): remote HBM latency is +34% on H200 (~490→~655 cycles) and +67% on B200 (~552→~920 cycles), effective L2 is only ~65% of physical, and of 28 measured H200s, 23 have a different GPC layout than the other 5. Making three scheduling scenarios asymmetry-aware — NUMA-aware placement for full-GPU kernels (vs FlashAttention-3/FlashAttention-4 and CUTLASS baselines, up to 1.22× for GroupGEMM), NUMA-aware MIG placement for prefill/decode multiplexing in mini-SGLang serving Qwen3-8B (+14.3% decode on H200, +10.4% on B200), and topology-aware tenant ordering for co-location (1.18×/1.33× swing at cluster size 8 on H200/B200) — the paper shows equal SM counts do not imply equal performance: on a B200 with a 70/78 SM split, ignoring topology turns a 1.10× NUMA win into a 78% slowdown.

## 核心贡献

- **芯片级非对称性的黑盒刻画方法**：两阶段 SM 拓扑发现（cluster 探测利用 Thread Block Cluster 的 SM 必在同一 GPC 的保证，找"normal SM"；L2 带宽探测利用同 GPC SM 争抢 Xbar 带宽的现象，给"random SM"定 GPC），加上延迟分布法测出本地/远端 HBM 与 L2 延迟、并通过逐位翻转地址反推出 NUMA 分区 XOR 哈希（H200 参与 16 位、H100 PCIe 14 位，最低位均为 bit 12，分区粒度 4KB）。全流程每卡 <1 分钟（哈希部分 ~10 秒，80GB 显存约 15 分钟），在多台机器上验证 100% 一致。
- **首次揭示厂商隐藏的三类事实**：(1) 同一 SKU 的芯片间拓扑不同——28 块 H200 中 23 块是"6×16-SM + 2×18-SM GPC"，其余 5 块各有差异（含 10/12/14-SM 的 GPC）；(2) random SM 永远不会被硬件调度进 cluster size >8 的 Thread Block Cluster，且 Green Context 会把 random SM 排到最后一个分区，导致后分配的上下文拿到"不能用大 cluster"的 SM；(3) 为让 MIG profile 跨芯片一致，MIG 有时会把正常的 SM 也禁用（最坏情况下 H200 4g+3g 组合闲置 132 个 SM 中的 8 个以上，floorsweep 更严重时连 normal SM 122/123 也被禁用）。
- **内存侧 NUMA 的完整画像**：H200 有效 L2 仅为物理容量的 65.8%、B200 为 64.7%；跨分区访问由 LTC fabric 把远端数据复制进本地 L2（失效后重载 ~310/596 cycles），因此远端访问会驱逐本地 L2 行——远端 HBM 带宽瓶颈在 LTC fabric 而非 HBM 本身。
- **三个场景的非对称感知调度原型**（全 GPU 内核的 NUMA 感知分配、应用内 prefill/decode 多路复用、跨应用共置），含两种可落地的内存分配机制：通过 4KB 级重映射的间接分配（所有 NVIDIA GPU 可用、O(1) 地址计算）和修改 NVIDIA 驱动 MLOP 代码的直接分配（Blackwell 及以后）。
- **方法论可迁移**：附录 G 用同样的延迟探测法在 AMD MI300X 上发现 4 个内存亲和组、3 档延迟（~707/~817/~926 cycles），证明统一计算拓扑的 GPU 仍存在内存非对称性。

## Key Contributions

- Two-probe, software-only discovery of hidden chip-specific compute topology (SM→GPC mapping, floorsweeping-dependent "random SMs") and memory affinity (partition hash, local/remote latency and bandwidth), running in <1 minute per GPU with 100% cross-machine agreement.
- First documentation of chip-to-chip variability within one SKU (23 of 28 H200s share one layout; the rest differ), and of scheduling-visible consequences: hardware never maps clusters >8 onto the 8 random SMs, Green Contexts deprioritize random SMs, and MIG sometimes disables even normal SMs to keep profiles identical across chips.
- Full NUMA picture for NVIDIA L2: effective L2 capacity is 65.8% (H200) / 64.7% (B200) of physical; the LTC fabric keeps partitions coherent by replicating remote lines into local L2, so remote HBM bandwidth is fabric-limited, not HBM-limited.
- Three asymmetry-aware scheduling prototypes (full-GPU kernels, intra-application prefill/decode multiplexing, inter-application co-location) with two allocation mechanisms: indirect 4KB-granularity remapping (all NVIDIA GPUs, O(1) address math) and direct allocation via NVIDIA driver MLOP modification (Blackwell+).
- Transferable methodology: the same latency probing reveals four memory-affinity groups and three latency tiers (~707/~817/~926 cycles) on AMD MI300X, showing memory asymmetry exists even with uniform compute.

## Methodology

**Compute-asymmetry discovery (two probes).** Phase 1 (cluster probing) exploits the Hopper Thread Block Clusters guarantee that co-scheduled cluster SMs share a GPC: kernels launched via `cudaLaunchKernelEx` with cluster sizes 3–8 log their `%smid` values; SM IDs that recur in cluster launches follow an architecture-specific SM→GPC mapping ("normal SMs"), the rest are "random SMs" whose GPC placement is set by chip-specific floorsweeping. Phase 2 (L2 bandwidth probing) exploits Xbar contention: SMs in the same GPC show lower aggregate L2 bandwidth once co-located SM count exceeds 6 on H200 / 16 on B200; sweeping each candidate random SM against known GPCs and assigning it to the GPC with the lowest added bandwidth uniquely places every random SM. Validation: 100% agreement across testbed machines, <1 min per GPU.

**Memory-asymmetry discovery.** Single-thread kernels sample ~100k addresses across the full address space from pinned SMs to get local vs. remote HBM latency distributions (2 tiers per GPU), local vs. remote L2 latency, and SM-to-partition affinity. The NUMA partition hash is recovered by flipping each physical address bit individually: 16 bits participate on H200 (lowest bit 12), 14 on H100 PCIe (also bit 12), i.e., 4KB partition granularity on all dissected GPUs. Effective L2 capacity comes from a bandwidth-vs-working-set inflection benchmark; L2 coherence behavior is exposed with a three-step microbenchmark (local SM loads remote line → remote SM invalidates → local SM reloads, completing in ~310/596 cycles on H200/B200, i.e., data was replicated, not forwarded).

**Asymmetry-aware scheduling.** (1) Full-GPU kernels: kernels are converted to persistent thread blocks pinned to SMs, with blocks assigned to each NUMA partition in proportion to its SM count using the discovered affinity; memory is placed NUMA-locally via indirect remapping (2MB physical pages exposed, pages re-indexed at 4KB granularity into the target partition using the recovered XOR hash, O(1) per access) or via direct driver-side allocation (MLOP-based, Blackwell+ only). Baselines are FlashAttention-3 (H200) / FlashAttention-4 (B200) for MHA/GQA/MLA and CUTLASS for GroupGEMM. (2) Intra-application multiplexing: mini-SGLang serving Qwen3-8B; prefill/decode run in separate MIG instances (64/56 SMs on H200, 64/64 on B200) with the driver modified to allocate each phase's private memory locally and interleave shared pages at 64KB granularity; three placements compared: Cluster-Aware (CA), Cluster-Mismatched (CM), Adaptive-NUMA (AN) Overlap. (3) Inter-application co-location: two tenants allocated sequentially via Green Contexts under MPS, varying cluster sizes (2/4/8) and allocation order to expose the random-SM penalty.

## Results & Highlights

- **Full-GPU kernels (H200 + B200):** NUMA-aware assignment speeds up GroupGEMM (CUTLASS baseline) by up to **1.22×** across 16 (N,K,M) configurations; MHA/GQA/MLA kernels (FlashAttention-3 on H200, FlashAttention-4 on B200 as baselines) see smaller but consistent gains, with hardware counters confirming reduced LTC-fabric requests. Gains reverse in some regimes: on B200 with small KV working sets, PTB-based NUMA awareness **loses** to the original kernel because per-query SM registration overhead outweighs NUMA benefits.
- **Topology × NUMA interaction (B200 MLA decode, 70/78 SM split):** with NUMA awareness but *without* topology awareness, a **1.10×** speedup turns into a **78% slowdown** (0.78×) — the kernel landed on random SMs that cannot form cluster size 8.
- **Multiplexed LLM inference (mini-SGLang, Qwen3-8B):** Adaptive-NUMA Overlap improves decode throughput over Cluster-Aware Overlap by **14.3% on H200** and **10.4% on B200**; CA and AN standalone are within noise of each other (no implementation overhead), while Cluster-Mismatched Overlap cuts decode throughput by **18.9%** vs. CA Overlap on B200.
- **Inter-application co-location (two GEMM tenants, Green Contexts):** the tenant allocated first gets **1.18× (H200) / 1.33× (B200)** higher throughput at cluster size 8, and 1.18×/1.11× at cluster size 4, purely from allocation order interacting with random-SM placement — on B200, GPC composition causes one 8-cluster and four wasted SMs per GPC for the second tenant.
- **Characterization numbers:** remote HBM latency is +34% on H200 (~490→~655 cycles) and +67% on B200 (~552→~920 cycles); remote L2 is ~1.5× on H200 (~309→~466) and ~2× on B200 (~364→~725); effective L2 is 65.8% (H200, 60MB physical) and 64.7% (B200, 125MB physical) of physical capacity; local-vs-remote bandwidth gap is ~1.1 TB/s on both H200 (62MB local knee) and B200.
- **Chip variability:** across 28 H200s, 23 have six 16-SM GPCs + two 18-SM GPCs; 3 GPUs have a 12-SM GPC; one has a 14-SM GPC; one has a 10-SM GPC — same SKU, different topology. The discovery runs in <1 min per GPU with 100% agreement; hash recovery takes ~10s (growing to ~15 min for 80GB).
- **AMD MI300X (appendix):** despite uniform 40-CU XCDs, memory probing finds four affinity groups with three latency tiers (~707, ~817, ~926 cycles), i.e., adjacent-vs-opposite AID effects.

## Authors & Credibility

The author group combines systems and architecture expertise from Shanghai Jiao Tong University (Xiaoze Fan, Jianhao Wang, Han Zhao, Shixuan Sun, Quan Chen, Minyi Guo — the IPUP/MLSys line of work on GPU resource management), National University of Singapore (Weihao Cui, Zhuobin Huang, Yangjie Zhou, Bingsheng He — a well-known GPU systems group), and NVIDIA (Yuxian Qiu), which lends credibility on vendor-adjacent details (Green Contexts, MIG, floorsweeping behavior). Several references are the authors' own prior systems work (SGDRC, vAttention, IPCU), and the paper follows the rigorous measurement-culture template of MICRO/ASPLOS characterization studies (cf. Cui et al.'s "Uncovering real GPU NoC characteristics", MICRO 2024). The mixture of academic and NVIDIA authors, plus validation across multiple physical machines and three NVIDIA SKUs plus one AMD GPU, makes the empirical claims unusually trustworthy for reverse-engineering work.

## Critical Assessment

The characterization is genuinely careful, but the scheduling half has caveats worth stating. First, the headline 1.22× applies only to GroupGEMM; attention kernels — the ones that matter most in LLM serving — see much smaller NUMA gains, and on B200 with small KV working sets the PTB-based approach actually *regresses* (the authors admit the per-query SM registration overhead). The indirect remapping path also adds measurable instruction overhead: PC-sampled arithmetic/bitwise share rises from 25.96% to 35.04% for GroupGEMM and 13.92% to 14.71% for MHA on H200, so on H200 the remap cost partially cancels NUMA gains for GroupGEMM — the clean 1.22× story really requires Blackwell's direct allocation. Second, the end-to-end LLM evaluation is thin: mini-SGLang (a lightweight research fork) with Qwen3-8B at a single fixed configuration (prefill batch 4 × 4K tokens; decode batch 128 × 512 avg KV), no production-scale traffic, no SLA/TTFT/TBT analysis, and the 14.3% decode gain is demonstrated at that one operating point. Third, practicality: every kernel must be recompiled as persistent thread blocks with pre-planned SM mapping, and the intra-application multiplexing prototype required modifying the CUDA driver and using MIG because "closed-source libraries in LLM serving stacks prevent kernel changes" — the authors themselves concede broad adoption depends on NVIDIA exposing flexible SM selection and NUMA placement. Fourth, the characterization is per-chip and must be re-run on every physical GPU; the paper validates across machines today but says nothing about whether the discovered mapping/hash survives driver or firmware updates, or about drift over time. Finally, the AMD MI300X study is characterization-only — no scheduling prototype — so its "asymmetry-awareness as direction" claim for AMD remains untested.

## Strengths

- First systematic, black-box, per-chip characterization of floorsweeping and NUMA asymmetry on *shipping* data-center GPUs (H200, B200, H100 PCIe), with each discovery method validated (100% agreement) and cheap to re-run (<1 min/GPU).
- Uncovers facts that NVIDIA does not document and that immediately matter to practitioners: cluster-size-8 kernels silently avoid random SMs, Green Context allocation order changes tenant throughput by up to 1.33×, MIG disables extra normal SMs to keep profiles uniform.
- Two allocation mechanisms spanning hardware generations (remap-based for all NVIDIA GPUs, driver-based for Blackwell+), and open-sourced code for reproducibility.
- Unusually honest reporting: the paper shows its own negative results (B200 small-KV attention regression, GroupGEMM remap overhead) and distills concrete, separately actionable lessons for kernel developers, application runtimes, and cloud schedulers (§7.4).

## Limitations

- Requires kernel changes (persistent thread blocks with pre-planned block-to-SM mapping) and, for multiplexing, CUDA driver + MIG modifications — closed-source production kernels (cuBLAS/cuDNN-based serving stacks) cannot adopt the techniques today.
- Attention-kernel gains from NUMA awareness are modest and negative for small KV caches on B200; the 1.22× headline is specific to GroupGEMM on configurations where remap overhead is outweighed.
- End-to-end LLM serving evidence is limited to one model (Qwen3-8B) and one operating point on a research-serving fork; no SLA-oriented or production-trace evaluation.
- Topology/hash discovery is per-physical-chip and its durability across driver/firmware updates is unexamined; AMD MI300X is characterized but not exploited by any scheduler.
- Asymmetry-aware placement currently assumes co-located kernels are cluster-compatible; mixed workloads with incompatible cluster sizes still force cluster-mismatched compromises (the CM Overlap case, −18.9%).

## Relevance

对关注 LLM 推理效率与 GPU 系统优化的研究者，这篇论文解释了为什么"同样 SM 数量的分配"在 H200/B200 上性能可能相差 1.33×，并给出了可直接落地的 NUMA 感知内核与显存放置方法；对做多卡推理服务、GPU 共置调度或内核优化的工作具有很强的实践参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would NUMA/topology awareness compound with orthogonal optimizations (speculative decoding, chunked prefill, MoE expert placement), or do PTB conversion and expert-routing heuristics compete for the same degrees of freedom?
- Can the discovered SM-to-GPC and partition-hash maps be exposed by NVIDIA officially (e.g., via CUDA device attributes), removing the reverse-engineering and driver-modification burden?
- How stable are the chip-specific mappings across driver updates, GPU resets, and thermal cycles — and can calibration results be cached and shared per chip serial number?
- Do the same asymmetry-aware placement principles transfer to AMD MI300X's XCD/AID topology (the paper only characterizes it), where three latency tiers would require more than binary local/remote placement?
- In multi-tenant cloud settings, how should a scheduler price or partition "cluster-capable" normal SMs versus random SMs to internalize the observed 1.33× allocation-order externality?
