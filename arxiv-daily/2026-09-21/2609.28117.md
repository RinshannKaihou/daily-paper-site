> **一句话总结：** 提出基于 Token 级 Max-Margin（TMM）损失的注意力头梯度归因方法：把对比评估的损失反传到注意力图而非模型参数，用梯度排序筛选出最有希望的"头-关系"对，再只对 top-k 头做昂贵的 Modifying Heads 干预验证——在 EuroLLM 1.7B/9B、Qwen 2.5 1.5B、Gemma 3 1B 上跨 4 个语言方向、50 个语境现象的分析中，TMM 的平均检索精度最高（如 EuroLLM context-5 上 0.5362 vs 最强基线 L_logTMM 0.4592、L_NLL 仅 0.0050），并把计算开销削减超过 96%。

> **TL;DR:** This paper replaces exhaustive "Modify-Heads-and-re-evaluate" sweeps in Context-Aware MT interpretability with a gradient screen: a new Token-level Max-Margin (TMM) loss backpropagated into attention maps ranks head-relation pairs, and only the top-k are verified with the causal intervention (C=0.99). Across EuroLLM 1.7B/9B, Qwen 2.5 1.5B, and Gemma 3 1B on ContraPro, LCPT, and ctxPro (50 phenomena, 4 language directions), TMM achieves the best average precision in most settings (0.5362 vs 0.4592 for the best alternative L_logTMM on EuroLLM context-5; the NLL baseline collapses to 0.0050), cuts computational cost by more than 96%, and transfers to IOI (Pearson 0.6700, AP 0.6505, top 3% covers all 37 pairs with >0.60 logit-diff gain). The scaled-up analysis finds "general-purpose" heads (e.g., 18-6, 18-14 in EuroLLM 1.7B), multi-functional heads, and a dissociation between high average attention and actual performance benefit.

## 核心贡献

- **提出 TMM 损失的梯度归因框架**：将 Token 级 Max-Margin 损失（只对模型答错或概率差小于 margin μ=0 的样本计算）反传到注意力分数 S^{l,h}（而非参数），按"头-关系对"聚合梯度的最大值，作为该头干预后增益 ΔA 的廉价代理指标。
- **用 top-k 筛选大幅降本**：先按梯度选出最有希望的 head-relation 对，再施加 Modifying Heads（C=0.99）做因果验证，把评估开销削减 96% 以上（IOI 任务约 97%），从而把分析规模扩展到 4 模型 × 4 语言方向 × 50 现象——这在全量扫描下是不可行的。
- **与 7 个损失基线系统对比**：包括 NLL、全序列对比损失、歧义词对比损失（Yin & Neubig 风格）、全序列 max-margin 及其对数版本；用 Pearson/Spearman 相关、AP、NDCG 和排名分析证明 TMM 在绝大多数设置中最优（例外：EuroLLM context-1 与 Qwen Auxiliary 上居第二）。
- **大规模分析的新发现**：存在同时服务多个关系的"通用型"注意力头（EuroLLM 1.7B 的 18-6、18-14，EuroLLM 9B 的 25-4，Qwen 的 20-2，Gemma 的 18-3）；发现高平均注意力 ≠ 高性能增益的"无响应头"（如 14-9 T_P→S_P）；头-关系对在语言方向间存在重叠（按 ΔA 度量的重叠更能解释跨现象迁移）。
- **方法泛化与落地验证**：将方法成功应用于间接宾语识别（IOI）任务；并用 Head Tuning（微调 Q/K 投影）验证发现——局部化的注意力改动使 ContraPro 准确率提升（如 19-4 头微调后 +2.72%）而通用翻译质量（BLEU 21.33 vs 基线 21.41）几乎不变。

## Key Contributions

- A gradient-attribution framework for attention heads that backpropagates a purpose-built Token-level Max-Margin (TMM) contrastive loss into the attention maps (not model parameters), yielding per-head, per-relation saliency scores that approximate the effect of Modifying Heads at C=0.99.
- A top-k screening protocol that preserves the causal conclusions of full Modifying-Heads sweeps while reducing computational requirements by more than 96%, enabling the largest head-relation analysis in Context-Aware MT to date: 50 phenomena, 4 language directions (en→de/fr/pl/ru), 4 instruction-tuned LLMs (EuroLLM 1.7B, EuroLLM 9B, Qwen 2.5 1.5B, Gemma 3 1B).
- A controlled comparison against seven loss baselines (NLL, sequence-level contrastive, token-level contrastive, sequence-level max-margin, and log-space variants) using Pearson/Spearman correlation, average precision, NDCG, and rank analysis; TMM ranks best in most settings.
- Empirical findings at scale: "general-purpose" heads benefiting multiple relations, multi-functional heads, a dissociation between average attention and accuracy gains (unresponsive heads), and head-relation overlap across language directions/phenomena that links to transfer during training.
- A replication of Head Tuning on the discovered heads showing localized gains transfer to real fine-tuning: ContraPro accuracy improves (up to +3.58% modified, +2.72% tuned) while BLEU stays within 0.5 of the 21.41 baseline; plus a generalization test on Indirect Object Identification.

## Methodology

**Setup.** Context-Aware MT disambiguation is evaluated with contrastive datasets: the model sees the source sentence plus source/target context and several target-sentence variants differing in one word; it is correct if it assigns the highest probability to the contextually right variant. Datasets: ContraPro (en→de), LCPT (en→fr), and ctxPro (Wicks & Post 2023) adapted from generative to contrastive evaluation by replacing the expected word in the gold translation with morphologically plausible alternatives sampled from the phenomenon's expected-word set (Gender, Formality, Auxiliary, Inflection for en→pl/en→ru; Auxiliary en→de for Qwen).

**Relations.** Twelve token-to-token relations among ambiguous phrase tokens (S_P, S_P+1, T_P), context-cue tokens (S_C, S_C+1, T_C), and cross-lingual pairs (e.g., T_P→T_C, T_P→S_C+1, T_P→S_P+1), reflecting the decoder-only autoregressive setting where token identity persists through layers (Brunner et al. 2020).

**Gradient attribution.** For each contrastive dataset D, they compute a loss L over the correct and incorrect inputs and backpropagate it into the attention scores S^{l,h} of every head — gradients of attention maps, not parameters. Gradients of multi-token phrases/cues are aggregated by taking the maximum gradient over the token pairs, since a head may attend to any part of the cue. The per-example scores are averaged: C^{d,l,h}_{X→Y} = (1/|D|) Σ max(−(∇_{S^{l,h}} L^d)).

**Token-level Max-Margin loss.** L_TMM = max(0, μ − P_θ(x^{d,c}) + max_i P_θ(x^{d,w_i})), where P_θ(x^{d,c}) is the model probability of the correct ambiguous token and w_i are the contrastive alternatives; μ = 0, so the loss is active only where the model is wrong or nearly tied. This mirrors contrastive evaluation itself: it rewards exactly the probability gap the intervention aims to increase.

**Protocol.** They compare L_TMM against L_NLL, sequence-level contrastive L_C, token-level contrastive L_TC (Yin & Neubig 2022-style), sequence-level max-margin L_MM, and log-space versions (L_logC, L_logTC, L_logMM, L_logTMM). Ground truth for evaluation is the set of head-relation pairs whose accuracy change ΔA under Modifying Heads at C=0.99 exceeds the 99% confidence interval of the unmodified model. Metrics: Pearson/Spearman correlation between gradients and ΔA, average precision (AP), NDCG@k, and rank analysis. A full sweep is run on EuroLLM 1.7B (context 5 and context 1) on ContraPro; other model/dataset combinations are screened with gradients and verified on top-3 heads per relation (15% of top pairs for Formality/Inflection, plus 3% of highest-attention pairs). A "why gradients work" analysis sweeps the intervention strength C ∈ {0.01,...,0.99} for selected heads, showing aggregated gradient–accuracy curves are monotonic while individual-example gradients are non-monotonic — accumulation over the dataset mitigates this known failure mode of gradient attribution. Finally, the framework is applied to Indirect Object Identification (IOI) with the log-probability contrastive loss, and discovered heads are validated by replicating Head Tuning (fine-tuning Q/K projections of selected heads toward the relation) on 10,000 OpenSubtitles 2018 en→de examples.

## Results & Highlights

- **Retrieval quality (Table 1, average precision of finding ΔA-significant head-relation pairs):** TMM is best in 5 of 7 settings — EuroLLM context-5: 0.5362 vs 0.4592 for the strongest baseline L_logTMM (+0.077) and 0.0050 for L_NLL; EuroLLM 9B*: 0.5606 vs 0.5318 (L_logMM); Qwen ContraPro: 0.5838 vs 0.5692 (L_logTMM); Qwen LCPT: 0.6799 vs 0.6578 (L_logTMM); Gemma: 0.4513 vs 0.4761 (L_TC — one of the cases TMM loses). The two exceptions are EuroLLM context-1 (TMM 0.5709, second to L_logTC 0.5907) and Qwen Auxiliary (TMM 0.4834, second to L_logTC 0.5167).
- **Cost reduction enabling scale:** focusing interventions on the gradient-selected top heads cut computational requirements by more than 96% (almost 97% on IOI), which is what makes the 4-model × 4-direction × 50-phenomenon analysis feasible at all; the paper argues TMM still retrieves all relevant pairs within the top 1% of the ranking even in the settings where it is not the best loss.
- **Generalization to IOI (EuroLLM 1.7B, baseline logit difference 5.26):** using L_logTC, gradients achieve Pearson r = 0.6700, Spearman ρ = 0.6862, and average precision 0.6505; the top 3% of ranked pairs (37 pairs) contains every pair with a logit-difference gain above 0.60, and head 20-14 (T_A→S_B+1) alone improves the metric by more than 57%.
- **Attention ≠ benefit (dissociation):** many high-attention heads are "unresponsive" — e.g., 14-9 (T_P→S_P) in EuroLLM 1.7B shows high average attention on Gender/Formality but no significant ΔA when modified; a full C-sweep shows accuracy actually drops below the head's average-attention point and never significantly improves, corroborating Jain & Wallace (2019) that raw attention scores do not explain behavior; the authors hypothesize redundancy (other heads covering the same relation).
- **Practical validation via Head Tuning (Table 2):** tuning Q/K of the discovered heads on ctxPro en→de Gender raises ContraPro accuracy from the 83.14% baseline by +2.72% (19-4 T_P→T_C+1, almost replicating the +2.84% from artificial modification) up to +2.25–2.95% for other pairs, while general translation quality stays essentially flat (BLEU 21.09–21.39 vs baseline 21.41; COMET 0.7857–0.7862 vs 0.7862); the gap is largest for 21-15 (T_P→S_P+1): +3.58% when modified vs only +1.00% when tuned.
- **Cross-lingual overlap:** head-relation pairs overlap heavily across language directions when measured by ΔA (e.g., 18-6 T_P→T_C+1 helps Gender in English-to-Polish, LCPT, and both English-to-Polish and English-to-Russian), and ΔA-based overlap explains the training-transfer patterns observed by Maka et al. (2025b) better than raw-attention overlap.

## Authors & Credibility

The four authors are from the Department of Advanced Computing Sciences, Maastricht University. First author Paweł Maka is the originator of the two methods this paper builds on — "Analyzing the attention heads for pronoun disambiguation in context-aware machine translation models" (ACL 2025, the Modifying Heads technique) and "You are what you train: Effects of data composition on training context-aware machine translation models" (EMNLP 2025) — so the team has deep, firsthand expertise in head-level MT interpretability and direct access to the prior methods' implementation details. Co-author Gerasimos Spanakis leads interpretability/MT research at Maastricht. The work uses public models (EuroLLM, Qwen 2.5, Gemma 3 via HuggingFace) and public datasets (ContraPro, LCPT, ctxPro, OpenSubtitles 2018), and reports hyperparameters in an appendix, which supports reproducibility. The arXiv posting (Sep 2026) is consistent with a fresh submission extending a peer-reviewed line of work; no code-release statement was spotted in the main text.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** Gradient-based saliency for attention is well trodden (GradCAM-style methods, Yin & Neubig 2022), and the causal verification step is the authors' own prior Modifying Heads method unchanged. The genuine novelty is narrower: a token-level max-margin loss matched to the contrastive-evaluation objective, plus the screening protocol that makes intervention studies tractable for LLMs. That is a solid engineering contribution, but readers should see it as "grad-CAM for head interventions in MT," not a new attribution paradigm. Notably absent are comparisons to non-loss-based attribution baselines — attention rollout (Abnar & Zuidema 2020), integrated gradients at the sequence level, or information-flow methods — so "gradients beat baselines" really means "this gradient loss beats seven other gradient losses."

**Rigor.** The evaluation is mostly careful: ground-truth labels require ΔA to exceed the 99% confidence interval of the unmodified model, and the paper candidly reports where results are not statistically significant (all Formality/Inflection ΔA results, the Figure 6 sweep, and EuroLLM 9B's Formality/Inflection are excluded for exactly this reason). However, there is mild circularity: the "ground truth" that gradients are scored against is itself produced by the same Modifying-Heads intervention, with a binary significance threshold — a noisy label that penalizes borderline heads arbitrarily. The headline AP numbers also deserve scrutiny: 0.45–0.68 average precision means the screen still ranks many non-significant pairs highly; the defense (all relevant pairs land in the top 1%) holds for the checked scenarios but is asserted per-phenomenon rather than with a formal guarantee. The NLL baseline (AP ≈ 0.005–0.02 on most settings) is close to a strawman and inflates the visual contrast in Table 1.

**Reproducibility.** Models and datasets are public and hyperparameters are documented, which is good; but no code release is stated in the main text, and the ctxPro-to-contrastive adaptation (replacing gold words with sampled alternatives) introduces dataset-construction choices — including a deliberate adjustment to "maintain difficulty" and the resulting loss of specified context-cue relations for Formality/Inflection — that materially affect which relations can even be tested (T_P→S_P and T_P→S_P+1 are dropped there, and the authors admit the poor Formality/Inflection results may stem from this limited relation set). Reproducing the exact contrastive conversion would require the appendix details and, ideally, their code.

**Significance and red flags.** The dissociation between attention and benefit, and the redundancy hypothesis, are genuinely useful negative results for the interpretability community, and the 96% cost figure is the paper's real selling point. Red flags to keep in mind: (1) the claimed cost saving is an evaluation-budget reduction relative to an exhaustive sweep — the method still requires the expensive intervention on the selected pairs, so "96%" applies to discovery, not deployment; (2) EuroLLM 9B results (Table 1 marked with *) were computed only on a restricted subset of relations, so the 9B column is not directly comparable to the 1.7B columns; (3) the Head Tuning experiment tunes on ctxPro Gender and evaluates on ContraPro — related but not identical data — so the "BLEU stays stable" conclusion is drawn from a single model, a single phenomenon, and one language direction; and (4) several of the "general-purpose head" observations are single-head anecdotes (e.g., 19-8, 24-11) without a systematic census.

## Strengths

- Directly addresses a real scalability blocker: intervention-based head analysis is combinatorially expensive in decoder-only LLMs, and the gradient screen is a clean, well-motivated surrogate with >96% cost reduction.
- Honest and unusually thorough evaluation of the attribution signal itself: seven loss baselines, three metric families (correlation, AP, NDCG), rank analysis, and a C-sweep study explaining *why* gradients work (monotonicity after accumulation) — engaging head-on with the "attention/gradient attribution is unreliable" literature (Jain & Wallace 2019; Wang et al. 2020).
- Scale of the empirical study (4 models, 4 language directions, 50 phenomena) is unprecedented for head-level Context-Aware MT analysis and yields findings full sweeps could not afford: general-purpose heads, multi-functional heads, unresponsive heads, cross-direction overlap.
- Closed loop from attribution to action: the Head Tuning replication shows the discovered heads matter under actual fine-tuning, with localized effects on general quality (BLEU 21.33–21.39 vs 21.41) — evidence the signal is actionable, not just correlational.
- Negative results are reported rather than buried: second-place finishes, statistically insignificant phenomena, and the limitations of Modifying Heads (needs near-perfect C=0.99 targets, well-defined relations, sensitive metrics) are all stated.

## Limitations

- Average precision of 0.45–0.68 in the best settings means the screen is far from a precise instrument; on Formality/Inflection (and EuroLLM 9B) the ΔA results are not statistically significant at all, so the method's coverage there is unproven.
- Comparisons are confined to gradient-loss variants; no head-to-head with attention rollout, activation patching-inspired rankings, or other attribution families, so the claim "gradients are the right screening signal" is only established within one family.
- Ground-truth labels derive from the same intervention being approximated, with a binary 99%-CI significance cut — noisy and somewhat circular evaluation.
- ctxPro adaptation drops specified context-cue relations (losing T_P→S_P, T_P→S_P+1 for Formality/Inflection), limiting relation coverage and likely causing the weak Formality/Inflection outcomes the authors themselves acknowledge.
- The 96% saving is relative to exhaustive sweeps and still requires the intervention for verification; the Head Tuning validation covers one model (EuroLLM 1.7B), one phenomenon (Gender, en→de), and one dataset pair; EuroLLM 9B used a restricted relation set; no code release is stated; "general-purpose head" claims rest on a handful of individual heads.

## Relevance

这篇论文与你关注的 LLM 可解释性与机器翻译方向高度相关：它把注意力头级别的因果干预分析首次系统性地扩展到 decoder-only LLM 的语境感知翻译场景，并给出了一个可将干预研究规模放大两个数量级的实用筛选框架。如果你在做注意力头功能分析或高效归因方法，TMM 损失 + top-k 干预验证是一个可以直接复用的协议。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the TMM screening survive comparison against non-gradient attribution families (attention rollout, sequence-level integrated gradients, activation-patching scores) at the same evaluation budget?
- The redundancy hypothesis for unresponsive heads (e.g., 14-9 T_P→S_P) is untested — can head ablation or head-pair co-modification experiments confirm that duplicate relation coverage exists?
- Does the gradient–ΔA correlation hold for larger models (the 9B results use a restricted relation set), and does the top-1% "all relevant pairs retrieved" property degrade with scale?
- Can the "general-purpose" heads (18-6, 18-14, 25-4, 20-2, 18-3) be characterized mechanistically — e.g., do they implement residual-stream communication of a shared component, and do they emerge across pretraining checkpoints?
- The Head Tuning result shows a modified-vs-tuned gap for some pairs (21-15: +3.58% vs +1.00%) — what limits fine-tuning from reaching the artificial-intervention upper bound, and can targeted regularization close it?
- Could the contrastive conversion of ctxPro be redesigned to preserve context-cue relations, restoring testability of Formality/Inflection where the current results are statistically inconclusive?
