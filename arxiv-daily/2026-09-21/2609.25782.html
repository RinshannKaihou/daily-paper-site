04 Methodology

Trace-driven simulation of a co-packaged substrate

NAND latency hides behind D2D transfer and CPU-side tokenization inside ~8 s median tool gaps.

  1. Workload

    mini-swe-agent trajectories on SWE-bench Verified; Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B (56.8 GiB MoE weights, 96 KiB KV/token). Peaks ~16.7k tokens (1.53 GiB KV), avg 0.82 GiB resident; 1.5 MiB blocks; heavy-tailed tool gaps (median ~8 s).

  2. Simulator

    B200 node: 192 GiB HBM3e @ 8 TB/s, 2.25 PFLOP/s. HBF = 3 TiB SLC, HBM-matched bandwidth, 25 μs read, 100k P/E cycles; read energy swept 8–30 pJ/bit vs HBM ≈3.5.

  3. Data paths

    Decode reads stay on the GPU–HBM bus; resume fetches and evictions cross the D2D link, double-buffered so NAND latency never surfaces; resume prefetch overlaps CPU tokenization.

  4. Policies

    LRU at block granularity with hard-pinned active blocks; write-on-evict exploits immutable, erase-block-aligned KV — each block written to HBF at most once.

  5. Baselines & metrics

    All-KV-to-flash over PCIe 5.0 (64 GB/s) / NVLink-C2C (450 GB/s); recompute-on-resume. Metrics: resumed-turn TBT, resume hit rate, throughput, HBF lifetime, read power.

06 Strengths

  • Driven by a measured characterization (3% / ~98%) — not a generic “flash is big” argument; the tiering policy follows directly from it.
  • Endurance co-designed with KV immutability: WAF ≈ 1.02, ~20-y lifetime, analyzed per Na rather than hand-waved.
  • Mechanisms isolated — the resume-hit-rate sweep separates tiering benefit from interconnect effects; each metric traced to its governing factor.
  • Honest framing: the power analysis itself rules out hot-set-from-HBF (~950 W/device); HBF positioned as complement, not replacement.
  • The user-visible SLO (50 ms TBT) is the primary latency metric — relevant to serving practice.

07 Limitations

  • Simulation-only: no prototype of the base-die router/staging; all results inherit assumed HBF specs (3 TiB SLC, HBM-matched bandwidth, 25 μs, 100k P/E).
  • One model, one benchmark family; dependence on ~8 s median tool gaps is never stress-tested.
  • Fixed 1.5 MiB block size asserted, not swept; LRU + pinning un-compared against alternatives.
  • No CXL/DRAM-expansion baseline and no cost model — capacity-per-dollar vs simply adding host memory is unquantified.
  • Abstract’s “2.4×” conflicts with the evaluation’s 165→3,900 (~24×).
  • KV quantization / in-session compression excluded by fiat — they would break the write-once guarantee.
  • 4-page CAL format: thin WAF derivation, arrival-process and prefill-interference treatment.

08 Questions for further exploration

  1. Bursty arrivals: does the ≤0.1 ms resume overhead survive a traffic spike that evicts most of the cold pool — and how fast is recovery?
  2. Missing tier: would a CXL-attached DRAM tier capture most of the capacity benefit at lower cost than a novel co-packaged substrate?
  3. Unexamined knobs: how sensitive are results to block size (asserted, not swept) and to KV-per-token across GQA/MLA architectures?
  4. Fast tools: what happens when tool calls return in milliseconds, shrinking the tokenization window that hides resume fetches?
  5. Broken immutability: how does write-on-evict interact with KV quantization or in-session compression?
  6. Real stacks: can 165→3,900 reproduce on vLLM/SGLang once HBF-class devices ship, given the idealized overlap assumption?
