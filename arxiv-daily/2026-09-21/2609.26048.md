> **一句话总结：** 本文提出"运行时策略"——由 agent harness 在历史失败的前置状态处注入的针对性自然语言指令与动作拒绝（不改模型权重、不改用户提示），在 Terminal-Bench 2.1 全部 87* 任务（每任务两次尝试）上将三个 GPT-5.6 档位的重复成功率 pass^2 从 50.6%→54.0%（Luna）、55.2%→60.9%（Terra）、64.4%→73.6%（Sol）；随机化五臂对照（14 个 eligible 任务）显示真实策略 60.7% 对比时间匹配的 sham 干预 39.3%（+25.0 点），证明增益来自策略文本内容而非"被干预"这一事实本身——策略把"够得着的解"转化为"可稳定交付的解"。

> **TL;DR:** On the full 87-task Terminal-Bench 2.1 suite (2 attempts/task), harness-level runtime policies raise repeated success (pass^2) for all three GPT-5.6 tiers — Sol 64.4%→73.6% (+9.2 pts) while pass@2 moves only 86.2%→87.4% (+1.2), Terra 55.2%→60.9%, Luna 50.6%→54.0%. A randomized five-arm panel isolates the cause: the real policy reaches 60.7% on 14 eligible tasks vs 39.3% for a timing-matched sham (+25.0 pts, 95% CI [7.1, 46.4], p=0.061) and 35.7% for a fresh baseline, while generic always-verify and reconsider arms stay at baseline. Policies target "procedural losses" — failures where the model already had a working solution but the harness let it die (e.g., a gRPC server launched inside the agent's own session that exits with the shell).

## 核心贡献

- **提出"运行时策略"抽象 P=(E,G,A,R,S)**：资格谓词 E（对任务描述只读一次，冻结组合中为词法匹配）+ 运行时谓词 G（读动作历史与策略状态）+ 干预 A（instruct 注入自然语言引导 / deny 拒绝动作）+ 释放条件 R + 提醒上限 S。策略在 harness 的 hook 层运行（UserPromptSubmit / PreToolUse / Stop 事件），不触碰模型权重与用户提示。
- **区分"可达"与"交付"两类成功率**（pass@2 = 至少一次成功，pass^2 = 两次都成功），并定义"程序性损失"（procedural loss）：轨迹中有解的证据（配对成功或可用中间产物）、失败前存在可观测状态、且有限的 harness 干预能改变该状态转移而不代解。
- **因果归因的随机化五臂设计**：真实策略 vs 时间匹配 sham（同谓词同时机、通用审查文本）vs always-verify vs reconsider vs 基线，300 次尝试、随机化日程、预注册主对比——把"策略内容"从"干预时机"和"泛泛自查"中分离出来。
- **机制漏斗**：确定性、构造性盲的日志抽取器显示各臂触及风险状态的频率相同（24–26/28），但注册的正确行为只在真实策略臂高频出现（22/24，其余臂 ≤14/24）——是干预文本而非干预发生改变了行为。
- **跨档位复制与模型档位杠杆**：service-persistence 策略在 Sol 上 7/21→19/21、Luna 上 11/21→19/21（无任何 pass→fail 回退）；Terra 的冻结组合在其 14 个覆盖任务上把 pass 率从 35.7% 提到 71.4%，以约一半成本（$7.19 vs $14.48）超过无辅助的更强档位 Sol（64.3%，但差异不显著 p=0.82）。
- **政策组合完全冻结并开源**：Terra 组合 8 个策略族、11 条规则，SHA-256 哈希固定，含被否决的 13 个早期配置记录；数据与审计账本发布在 HuggingFace。

## Key Contributions

- A **runtime-policy abstraction** P=(E, G, A, R, S) — an eligibility predicate over the task description (read once), a runtime predicate over action history, an intervention (natural-language instruct or action deny), a release condition, and a nudge limit — implemented in a hook layer around Codex CLI, changing neither model weights nor the user prompt.
- A conceptual split between **reachable success** (pass@2) and **delivered/repeated success** (pass^2), plus a definition of "procedural loss": a failure where solvability evidence existed, an observable state preceded the failure, and a bounded harness intervention could have changed the state transition without supplying the solution.
- A **randomized five-arm attribution design** (real policy vs timing-matched sham with generic text vs always-verify vs reconsider vs baseline; 300 attempts, prespecified primary contrast) that separates policy *content* from intervention *timing* and from generic checking.
- A **mechanism funnel** with deterministic, blind-by-construction log extractors: the risky state arises equally often in every arm (24–26/28 attempts), but the registered corrective behavior appears in 22/24 coded real-policy attempts vs at most 14/24 elsewhere.
- **Replication in two further model tiers** (service-persistence policy: Sol 7/21→19/21, Luna 11/21→19/21, zero pass-to-fail regressions) and a **model-tier leverage result**: policy-guided Terra hits 71.4% on the 14 covered tasks vs 64.3% for unassisted Sol at roughly half the cost ($7.19 vs $14.48).
- **Frozen, hash-pinned, open portfolios**: the Terra portfolio (8 families, 11 rules) is frozen with SHA-256 hashes and a retirement record of 13 earlier evaluated configurations; artifact released on HuggingFace.

## Methodology

**Setting.** Terminal-Bench 2.1 with a pinned dataset digest, run in Harbor containers; agent is Codex CLI v0.146.0 at medium reasoning effort, backed by three GPT-5.6 routes (Luna, Terra, Sol) in increasing order of success and price. Two tasks (gemini-alpine-spark, qemu-start-pine) were excluded because their pinned images could not install the agent, so all complete-suite comparisons use the same remaining 87* tasks.

**Policy form.** A runtime policy is the tuple P=(E,G,A,R,S): E (eligibility) matches phrases in the task description once at session start; G reads the action history and per-task state at harness events; A either injects a short natural-language instruction (adding one turn) or denies a proposed tool call; R releases the policy when the correct behavior is observed; S caps nudges to prevent loops. In the frozen portfolio E is lexical (regex/phrase matching); no LLM judge is involved in firing. Policies run in a hook layer observing UserPromptSubmit, PreToolUse, and Stop events; the runner refuses to score an attempt lacking pre-tool-use hook events or with a missing model/profile marker (anti-cheat gate).

**Portfolio derivation and freezing.** The authors ran the policy-free agent twice per task and inspected every failed attempt of 1/2 tasks (and 0/2 failures with a concrete procedural omission), excluding provider faults, verifier faults, missing competence, and failures without an observable pre-failure intervention point. Each cluster becomes a registered chain: state X → intervention A → behavior B → verifier condition, with predicates written on positive and near-negative tasks and revised under new identity or retired. The frozen Terra portfolio has 8 families (service persistence, deployment preservation, database evidence, exact replacement ×3 rules, LaTeX final log, binary equivalence ×2, token accounting, enumeration) in 7 source files / 11 rules; 13 earlier configurations (broad quantifier, repetition, generic scope, generic verification, optimization prompts) were retired or narrowed before freezing. Example: the service-persistence policy watches for a stop event right after a server launch without a detached launch + separate probe, and instructs the agent to relaunch detached with redirected streams and probe from a later command — letting the agent's own solution survive its shell.

**Four designs (strongest to weakest causal claim).** (1) Five-arm randomized panel on Terra's 14 eligible + 16 pre-registered silent tasks (2 attempts/task/condition, 300 attempts, randomized schedule, sources hashed before the first attempt): baseline, real portfolio, triggered sham (real eligibility predicates + real event timing but generic review text; denials replaced with generic denial to avoid deadlock), always-verify (stop-time verification instruction on every task), reconsider (generic reconsideration request). (2) Matched screen replications of one policy in two further tiers (7 service tasks × 3 attempts, same-day policy-free baseline). (3) Complete 87*-task suite, two attempts per task and condition, 1,044 attempts. Behavioral marks were coded by deterministic extractors over the command log, fixed before the panel and blind by construction.

**Inference.** The task is the unit: per task, the mean of two attempts; effects are mean paired task-level differences with 10,000-resample percentile bootstrap intervals and two-sided paired sign-flip permutation tests (100,000 permutations). The prespecified primary contrast is real policy minus sham on eligible tasks.

## Results & Highlights

- **Randomized panel (14 eligible tasks, primary contrast):** real policy 17/28 (60.7%) vs triggered sham 11/28 (39.3%) — Δ +25.0 points (95% CI [7.1, 46.4], p=0.061); vs fresh baseline 10/28 (35.7%) Δ +21.4 (p=0.032). The sham fires on 26/28 eligible attempts yet does no better than baseline (−3.6 pts, CI [−17.9, 10.7]); always-verify (39.3%) and reconsider (42.9%) also stay at/near baseline. The real portfolio never fires on the 16 silent tasks and leaves them unchanged (21/32 vs 20/32 baseline).
- **Complete 87*-task suite (two attempts per task and condition):** pass^2 rises in all three tiers — Sol 64.4%→73.6% (+9.2), Terra 55.2%→60.9% (+5.7), Luna 50.6%→54.0% (+3.4). For Sol, pass@2 moves only 86.2%→87.4% (+1.2) while pass^2 jumps +9.2: eight more tasks pass in both attempts, one more passes at all. pass@1 deltas (+3.4/+6.3/+5.2) are directionally positive but individually not significant (p=.43/.17/.09). Give-ups fall in every tier (61→54, 58→41, 36→25) and improved tasks outnumber regressions (15/10, 21/11, 10/4).
- **Model-tier leverage on the 14 Terra-covered tasks:** unassisted Terra passes 10/28 (35.7%); with its frozen portfolio 20/28 (71.4%) — the reliability layer doubles successful attempts; unassisted Sol is 18/28 (64.3%) at $14.48 vs Terra+policy $7.19. But the Terra-minus-Sol contrast is imprecise (95% CI [−21.4, 35.7], p=0.82) and the authors explicitly do not claim Terra is generally superior. Sol+portfolio reaches 24/28 (85.7%) at $27.17.
- **Mechanism funnel (28 eligible attempts per arm):** the registered runtime state arises in 24–26/28 attempts in every arm; the registered corrective behavior appears in 22/24 coded real-policy attempts vs 11–14/24 elsewhere; of the 22 real-policy attempts with the behavior, 12 pass. Family detail: database-evidence preservation passes 4/4 eligible (baseline 3/4, sham 2/4); service persistence yields detached launch + separate probe in 12/12 and passes 7/12 (baseline 4/12, sham 3/12); binary equivalence corrects the procedure yet still fails both attempts — the policy cannot supply the missing reimplementation.
- **Replication across tiers (service-persistence screen, 3 attempts/task):** success rises 7/21→19/21 for Sol and 11/21→19/21 for Luna with no pass-to-fail change on any task; registered behavior 5/21→20/21 (Sol) and 8/21→16/21 (Luna); exact sign-flip p = 0.063 (Sol) and 0.125 (Luna) on small counts.
- **Cost is portfolio-breadth dependent:** Terra's targeted portfolio fires on 15/87 tasks and costs +0.9%; Luna's fires on 12 and costs −5.2% (real-policy panel arm: $19.92 vs $23.22 baseline, mean time 418→473 s); Sol's broad portfolio fires on 81/87 tasks and costs +47.7%.

## Authors & Credibility

Nikita Agarwal and Nivedit Jain are both founders of Failproof AI, a company that develops the runtime infrastructure used to implement these policies — a disclosed financial and professional competing interest, since the paper effectively evaluates the product category they sell. Offsetting this, the methodological hygiene is unusually strong for an industry paper: prespecified primary contrast, permutation tests alongside bootstrap CIs (with disagreement between them reported rather than cherry-picked), deterministic blind-by-construction behavior coding, SHA-256-pinned frozen treatment, an anti-cheat scoring gate, a machine-readable selected-attempt ledger with documented deviations, and a full artifact release (policy source, configurations, run-selection records, per-attempt outcomes) on HuggingFace. The Limitations section candidly admits that the planned leave-one-family-out ablation was never run. Generative AI tools were used for critique, orchestration, trajectory inspection, and drafting, with the authors stating they verified every numerical claim; the paper's statistical care (citing Card et al. 2020 on low power, Berg-Kirkpatrick et al. 2012 on significance testing) suggests genuine familiarity with NLP evaluation methodology.

## Critical Assessment

The strongest part of this paper is not the headline numbers but the **attribution design**: the timing-matched sham (same predicates, same event timing, generic text) is exactly the control most harness-intervention papers skip, and the −3.6-point sham result cleanly kills the "any interruption helps" alternative. The pass^2-vs-pass@2 dissociation (+9.2 vs +1.2 for Sol) is a crisp, memorable contribution that reframes agent reliability as a delivery problem rather than a capability problem.

The rigor issues are real, though, and mostly acknowledged: (1) the prespecified primary contrast has p=0.061 — it misses the conventional 0.05 bar, and its CI [7.1, 46.4] spans a 6.5× range, so the +25.0 headline is a large, imprecise effect on 14 tasks with 2 attempts each; pass@2 inference treats the two attempts within a task as if independent, which the authors themselves flag. (2) There is **selection circularity at the design level**: the 14 panel tasks come from the development population on which the policies were authored (they were "already easier" — 62.5% vs 39.3% baseline), so the panel establishes a content effect on that distribution but not generalization; the eligibility-minus-silent interaction (+18.8, CI [−4.9, 42.9], p=0.20) is too imprecise to localize the effect, by their own admission. (3) The **planned leave-one-family-out ablation was never run**, so we cannot tell which of the 11 rules carry the gain — the paper is one ablation short of its own standard. (4) Scope is narrow: one benchmark (Terminal-Bench 2.1), one harness (Codex CLI), one model family (GPT-5.6), one reasoning effort; portfolios are explicitly model-specific, so this is a recipe demonstrated three times, not a general method validated. (5) **Authoring cost is unquantified**: portfolios required expert judgment and unlogged research hours, with 13 retired configurations on the way — the reader cannot tell how much expert labor per point of pass^2 this takes, which matters for the "harness engineering is cheap" framing. (6) Sol's broad portfolio fires on 81/87 tasks for +47.7% cost, showing the approach degrades toward "always-on checking" (with its cost added and, per their own sham logic, dubious marginal benefit) when not narrowly targeted. (7) The competing interest is structural: founders of a runtime-infrastructure company evaluating runtime infrastructure; the transparency mitigates but does not eliminate this. Behavior coding also covers only the registered marker, not every way an agent could satisfy a verifier.

## Strengths

- A genuinely controlled causal design — the timing-matched sham arm is the right control and produces the paper's most informative negative result (firing on 26/28 attempts, −3.6 pts).
- The reachable-vs-delivered (pass@2 vs pass^2) framing yields an actionable systems insight: models and search produce solutions, runtime policies protect delivery.
- Mechanism evidence beyond outcomes: a funnel showing equal opportunity (state arises 24–26/28 everywhere) but differential behavior (22/24 vs ≤14/24), coded blind by deterministic extractors.
- Honest statistical reporting: bootstrap intervals and permutation tests reported together including their disagreement (CI excludes 0 while p=0.061), non-significant pass@1 deltas printed in full, the Terra-vs-Sol p=0.82 comparison explicitly disclaimed.
- Unusual reproducibility hygiene for an industry paper: hash-pinned frozen treatment, retirement record of failed policy versions, anti-cheat scoring gate, full artifact release.
- Practical relevance: no retraining, works at the harness layer, and the targeted portfolios are cost-neutral (Terra +0.9%, Luna −5.2%).

## Limitations

- Two attempts per task make pass^2 an observed rate, not an estimate of long-run reliability; all task counts are small (14 eligible panel tasks), so key intervals are wide (primary CI [7.1, 46.4]).
- Panel tasks are drawn from the development population used to author the portfolio — a content effect on that distribution, not evidence of generalization to unseen task types.
- Single benchmark (Terminal-Bench 2.1), single harness (Codex CLI), single model family (GPT-5.6) at one reasoning effort; portfolios are model-specific and tiers do not share identical policies, so cross-tier comparison is qualitative.
- The planned leave-one-family-out ablation was not run; which rules matter is unknown.
- Policy authoring demanded expert judgment with unlogged hours; the method does not yet say how a practitioner without this team should mine or maintain rules.
- Broad portfolios can cancel their own gains: Sol's fires on 81/87 tasks and raises cost 47.7%; policies can add latency and disturb prompt caching.
- Both authors are founders of Failproof AI, which develops the evaluated runtime infrastructure.

## Relevance

对 LLM agent 可靠性与评测方向的研究者非常有参考价值：它把 agent 失败从"能力不足"重新定位为"交付不稳"，并给出了一套少见的严格归因实验（时间匹配 sham + 盲编码漏斗）来支撑 harness 层干预的价值。对任何做 Terminal-Bench/SWE-bench 类评测或生产 agent 工程的团队，pass@2 与 pass^2 的解耦以及策略组合的冻结-审计方法论都值得直接借鉴。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Which of the 11 frozen rules carry the gain? The planned leave-one-family-out ablation would show whether 8 families are needed or 2–3 do all the work.
- Does the pass^2 gain transfer beyond GPT-5.6 (open-weight or other proprietary families) and beyond Terminal-Bench — e.g., to SWE-bench or OSWorld — given that eligibility predicates are lexical and Terminal-Bench phrasing-shaped?
- Can policy mining be automated (e.g., an LLM proposing candidate chains from failure logs with the same freeze-and-audit discipline), and what is the expert-hour cost per point of pass^2?
- The binary-equivalence family corrected procedure in 12/12 firings yet passed 0 — is there a principled way for a policy to detect that a capability gap (not a procedural slip) remains and stand down, avoiding wasted nudges?
- How do runtime interventions interact with prompt caching and latency in production deployments, and does the pass^2 gain persist at k>2 attempts (pass^3, pass^k), where "convert reachable to delivered" should compound?
- Under what conditions can deny-based policies (e.g., database-evidence preservation) be misused to lock agents out of legitimate actions, and what inspection guarantees do deployed policies need?
