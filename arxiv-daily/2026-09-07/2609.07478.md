> **一句话总结：** 在全部144个严格序数2×2博弈上解码四个开源LLM的内部激活，本文发现"激励信息可解码（availability）"、"被自发用于决策（recruitment）"、"可被因果操纵（susceptibility）"三者可以分离——基座版与指令版Qwen2.5-72B行为几乎一致（96.4%选择相同），但只有指令版把内部收益激励信号真正接入选择（+0.047/SD vs −0.012），而对基座版沿激励方向做激活转向仍能以+0.060的剂量斜率改变其策略，说明后训练重塑了"表征→决策"的通路而非表征本身。

> **TL;DR:** The authors probe how strategic choices form inside LLMs by recording activations from four open-weight models (a matched Qwen2.5-72B base/Instruct pair, Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct, GPT-OSS-120B) playing the complete catalogue of 144 strict ordinal 2x2 games, benchmarked against human play on the same games (Moore et al. 2026; Zhu et al. 2025a). Payoff incentive is linearly decodable in every model (AUC 0.767–0.916) while opponent incentive stays at chance, but only Qwen2.5-Instruct recruits the internal incentive signal into choice beyond the objective payoff gap (+0.047 per SD [0.011, 0.085] vs −0.012 for its base twin, despite 96.4% behavioural agreement). Causally steering activations along the incentive direction shifts the output policy in double-dominance games (+0.236 dose-slope for Instruct, +0.060 for base, 9/9 games each; Llama heterogeneous and n.s.), establishing a three-way dissociation between availability, recruitment, and susceptibility of a representation — behaviour and decodability alone miss it.

## 核心贡献

- **提出并实证了"可用性—招募—易感性"（availability–recruitment–susceptibility）三重分离框架**：一个内部表征可以同时满足"可被线性探针解码"、"不被自发用于决策"、"却仍可被因果干预改变决策"。Qwen2.5基座版是活例：激励可解码（AUC 0.847）、行为桥接为零（−0.012），但因果转向仍有效（+0.060，9/9博弈）。
- **在完备博弈空间上做内部测量**：覆盖全部144个严格序数2×2博弈（Robinson–Goforth目录），不存在"挑博弈"的可能；用预注册式的博弈论层级规则（占优→唯一纯纳什→支付占优均衡→最大最小）为每个博弈定义规范动作与激励差距。
- **用匹配的基座/指令对照隔离后训练效应**：Qwen2.5-72B与Instruct版共享预训练权重、基线选择96.4%一致、激励可解码性相当，但Instruct版的激励信号更强烈地接入选择（桥接差异+0.059 [0.017, 0.101]）并在答案轴上表达更强（logit-lens斜率1.43 vs 0.17）——后训练改变的是信息到达决策的通路，而非信息是否存在。
- **因果转向证据**：在去除答案字母分量后，沿激励方向推动第65层激活使两台Qwen模型在双占优博弈中策略向激励支持的动作偏移（9/9博弈），且随机方向对照近零；第79层的效应则被字母分量解释——区分了上游激励表征与输出读出重叠。
- **决策线索（cue）的分析显示编码≠使用**：五种固定指令（风险/损失/不公平厌恶、自利、最大最小）的措辞几乎完美可解码（LDA 0.97–1.00），但行为效应强烈依赖博弈结构：博弈结构主导预测，线索身份仅增加约∆AUC 0.04。

## Key Contributions

- A three-way dissociation between **availability** (information linearly decodable from activations), **recruitment** (spontaneously used in choice, beyond the objective incentive), and **susceptibility** (causally able to influence the policy when perturbed). Qwen2.5 base shows all three splitting apart: incentive decodable at AUC 0.847, zero endogenous behavioural bridge (−0.012), yet still steerable (+0.060 dose-slope, 9/9 double-dominance games).
- **Exhaustive game domain**: all 144 strict ordinal 2x2 games (payoffs on a 1–4 rank scale), eliminating game-selection freedom; a prespecified hierarchy (dominance → unique pure Nash → payoff-dominant equilibrium → maximin) defines one canonical action per game, and the incentive gap is scored against a naive 50–50 (level-0) opponent.
- **Post-training isolated via a matched checkpoint pair**: Qwen2.5-72B base and Instruct share pretrained weights, agree on 555/576 (96.4%) baseline decisions, and encode incentive equally well — yet only Instruct carries the signal into choice (+0.047 [0.011, 0.085] vs −0.012) and expresses it along the answer axis (lens slope 1.43 vs 0.17). Post-training reshaped the path from representation to decision, not the representation.
- **Causal steering with readout controls**: after orthogonalising out the answer-letter axis, pushing the layer-65 incentive direction shifts graded policy toward the incentive-favoured action in dominance games for both Qwen models (9/9 each); the layer-79 effect collapses to ≈0 under the same ablation, dissociating upstream incentive coding from output-token overlap. GPT-OSS's fused router is read-only and was correctly left untested causally.
- **Cues encode but do not impose personas**: five fixed decision instructions (risk, loss, inequity aversion, selfish maximisation, maximin) are near-perfectly decodable (0.97–1.00), but behavioural displacement depends on cue × game: game structure dominates held-out prediction, cue identity adds only ∆AUC ≈ 0.04.
- A systems-neuroscience-inspired **evidentiary standard** for claims about model decision-making: behaviour shows what was chosen; decoding shows what is available; behaviour-linked analysis tests recruitment; intervention tests susceptibility — none substitutes for the others.

## Methodology

Four open-weight models — Qwen2.5-72B (base), Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct (dense, 8-bit quantised) and GPT-OSS-120B (MoE, native MXFP4, Harmony reasoning format) — each play one-shot the full catalogue of 144 strict ordinal 2x2 games with payoffs instantiated as equally spaced 1–4 points. Every game appears in four counterbalanced forms (J/P letter assignment × option order) under eight conditions: player-1 baseline, five fixed decision cues, a neutral control, and player-2 baseline; 18,326/18,432 responses (99.4%) yielded a usable choice. A prespecified behavioural model defines each game's canonical action (dominance, then unique pure equilibrium, then payoff-dominant equilibrium, then maximin) and the incentive gap — the expected-payoff advantage of the canonical action against a 50–50 opponent (level-0 reference). Behaviour is calibrated against two human datasets on the same games (Moore et al. 2026, ordinal; Zhu et al. 2025a, cardinal) and quantal level-k (QLk) fits place every agent on a common depth/sharpness map. Internally, linear probes decode incentive, choice and controls from residual activations (held-out by game); decision–incentive geometry measures the angle between the incentive axis and the choice axis against a game-permutation null that preserves the incentive–behaviour correlation; a partial-slope "endogenous bridge" tests whether projected neural incentive predicts choice beyond the objective gap; token-level logit-lens tracks answer-axis expression across prompt regions; and causal steering perturbs the letter-orthogonalised incentive direction at two layers (65, 79), reading out the graded two-option policy rather than regenerated choices. GPT-OSS is additionally read from its MoE router (gate logits vs selected top-k routes), but only post-reasoning, so it is excluded from token-level and causal analyses.

## Results & Highlights

- **Behaviour tracks human strategic patterns**: canonical-action rates range 0.714–0.889 across models (chance 0.5), and conformity falls family-by-family with required reasoning depth (DD → OD1 → OD2 → CO → MP) in the dense models, mirroring humans. Canonical play declines with structural complexity in dense models (Spearman ρ = −0.40 to −0.42) nearly matching humans on the same games (ρ = −0.44, Moore et al. 2026), while the reasoning model GPT-OSS declines far less (ρ = −0.18) and fits a QLk depth of 2.07 vs the dense-model/human cluster near level 1.
- **Incentive is available everywhere; opponent incentive is not**: own incentive sign decodes from residual activations at held-out AUC 0.869 (Qwen2.5-Instruct), 0.847 (Qwen2.5 base), 0.767 (Llama) and 0.916 pure / 0.894 all rows (GPT-OSS; router-gate AUC 0.891), while opponent incentive stays near chance and a raw single-cell payoff control decodes at 0.80–0.83 (dense) / 0.567 (GPT-OSS) — recoverability does not imply use.
- **Recruitment differs despite identical behaviour**: controlling for the objective incentive gap, only Qwen2.5-Instruct retains a reliable neural-incentive→choice bridge of +0.047 [0.011, 0.085] (≈5 percentage points of canonical choice per SD of internal signal); the base checkpoint shows −0.012 [−0.040, 0.019] — a pair difference of +0.059 [0.017, 0.101] — even though the two agree on 555/576 baseline presentations and have behavioural incentive slopes of 2.04 vs 1.92.
- **Causal steering is selective and layer-specific**: the letter-orthogonalised incentive push at layer 65 shifts the graded policy in double-dominance games by +0.236 [0.162, 0.304] for Qwen2.5-Instruct (positive in 9/9 games) and +0.060 [0.044, 0.076] for Qwen2.5 base (9/9), with same-norm random-direction controls near zero; Llama is heterogeneous (DD +0.085 [−0.062, 0.234]) and supports no selective pattern. At layer 79 the effect collapses to ≈0 under letter ablation — the late effect was output-readout overlap, the mid-layer effect is upstream.
- **Answer-axis expression separates models that behaviour cannot**: logit-lens incentive slopes at the decision boundary are 1.43 (r = 0.73) for Qwen2.5-Instruct, 0.79 (r = 0.66) for Llama and only 0.17 (r = 0.13) for Qwen2.5 base, despite near-identical behavioural slopes of ≈1.9–2.0. At own-payoff tokens the incentive is probe-recoverable (AUC 0.60–0.67) while the lens is at chance; at the final token lens decoding reaches 0.98–0.99 (instructed models) and 0.70 (base) — information is present in the hidden state before it is expressed along the answer axis.
- **Cues move choices selectively**: inequity aversion produces the largest behavioural displacement (mean absolute cue–baseline shift 0.643 across its 17 identifiable games, consistently toward the prescribed action); risk, loss and maximin cues give intermediate shifts (0.311, 0.309, 0.331 across 48 games); selfish maximisation barely moves choices from baseline (0.093 across 96 games). In nested held-out prediction, game structure supplies the main gain and cue identity adds only ∆AUC ≈ 0.04.

Vinicius Ferraz (Karlsruhe Institute of Technology, KD2 Lab; Singularity AI Research; BrainSpot Labs — corresponding author), Leon Houf (KIT) and Enrico Ferrea (German Primate Center / Leibniz Institute; salestech Data & AI). The mix of a management-economics lab, a primate neurophysiology institute and small industry AI groups fits the paper's systems-neuroscience framing (encoding vs read-out vs intervention, citing Panzeri, Cisek, Gold & Shadlen). This is a single preprint (v2, 16 Sep 2026; v1 7 Sep 2026, cs.AI) — not peer reviewed — from a non-mainstream interpretability group rather than an established LLM-lab; the empirical craftsmanship (counterbalancing, permutation nulls, bootstrap CIs reported inline, self-acknowledged limitations) is well above the typical preprint bar, and the authors are unusually explicit about which claims are causal, descriptive or exploratory.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: the core novelty is conceptual and methodological rather than a new capability: transplanting the neuroscience availability/recruitment/susceptibility trichotomy onto strategic choice in LLMs, and doing it over an exhaustive game catalogue with a matched base–instruct pair. The individual ingredients (linear probes, logit lens, activation steering) are standard; the Qwen-pair result (identical behaviour, different recruitment) is the genuinely new, quotable finding.
- **Rigour**: mostly strong — held-out-by-game probes, four-way counterbalancing, letter-axis orthogonalisation, permutation nulls that preserve the incentive–behaviour correlation, and game-clustered bootstrap intervals everywhere. But several inferential details are weaker than the prose implies: layer onsets are pointwise and unadjusted (no multiplicity correction), and the steering "specificity control" uses only three permuted directions that the authors themselves admit are "a qualitative specificity control rather than a formal permutation test" because they were not matched to the corrected seeds.
- **Reproducibility**: good in principle — open-weight checkpoints, an exhaustive public game catalogue, prespecified canonical-action rules and supplementary tables of prompts/checkpoints — though no code release is stated in the main text, and exact steering dose grids and control lists live in supplementary sections that would need to be transcribed carefully.
- **Significance**: the headline causal claim rests on the *graded output policy*, not regenerated choices: the regenerated-choice family-balanced effect (+0.068 [−0.002, 0.140] vs random control +0.027 [−0.013, 0.069]) has both intervals covering zero. The abstract-level story "steering shifts preference" is therefore supported for readout probabilities, weaker for actual decisions. The post-training conclusion also rests on a *single* matched pair (one Qwen2.5-72B family), so "post-training reshapes the path" is one data point, not a law — the authors say this, but it will likely be over-cited as general.
- **Red flags / quirks**: (1) the double-dominance steering headline (+0.236) coexists with dose-zero canonical preference already at 0.97 for Qwen2.5-Instruct in those games — the graded readout can move where the discrete choice is saturated, so behavioural impact there is minimal; (2) the incentive-gap construct assumes equally spaced 1–4 ranks, so all "payoff advantage" numbers are convention-dependent, as the authors concede; (3) GPT-OSS — arguably the most interesting model — is excluded from both token-level and causal analyses because its activations were captured post-reasoning, leaving its router findings descriptive; (4) each cue uses one fixed wording, so "inequity aversion shifts choices" is really "this one sentence shifts choices"; (5) human comparison is behavioural only — no evidence of shared computation, despite the neuroscience-flavoured narrative.

## Strengths

- Exhaustive, cherry-pick-proof stimulus domain (all 144 strict ordinal 2x2 games) with human data on the identical payoff scale for calibration.
- The matched base–instruct design cleanly isolates post-training as the only manipulated variable — a rare, well-controlled comparison in interpretability work.
- Multi-level evidence chain (behaviour → decodability → geometry → partial bridge → causal steering → token-level lens) with each level's inferential status labelled honestly.
- The availability/recruitment/susceptibility vocabulary is a genuinely useful export to the broader "LLMs as economic agents" literature, giving a checklist for what evidence a claim about model beliefs/preferences requires.

## Limitations

- Domain restricted to one-shot, strict ordinal 2x2 games; repeated, sequential, incomplete-information and multiplayer settings untested (the authors state this explicitly).
- Four models only; cross-model comparisons are descriptive and the post-training conclusion comes from one matched pair; no statistical model of checkpoint-level variation is possible.
- Causal evidence is narrow: one prespecified incentive, one answer position, two layers (65, 79), three dense models, strongest for graded policy rather than regenerated choices; regenerated-choice intervals include zero.
- GPT-OSS receives no comparable token-level or causal tests (post-reasoning capture, read-only fused router); its decision-formation process is effectively unobserved.
- Single fixed wording per cue confounds disposition with phrasing; no multiplicity correction across the many layerwise/exploratory tests; incentive computations assume equal rank spacing.

## Relevance

对理解LLM作为战略智能体的内部决策机制 directly relevant：如果做LLM agent行为评估、机制可解释性（探针/转向）或"LLM能否充当人类被试"（Homo silicus）方向的研究，本文提供了一个严格且可直接复用的"表征可用性≠使用≠可操纵"评估框架，并提醒仅看输出行为会漏掉后训练造成的内部通路差异。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the base→instruct recruitment difference replicate in other matched pairs (Llama-3.1 base/Instruct, Qwen3, RLHF-vs-DPO variants), or is the +0.047 bridge a Qwen2.5-72B idiosyncrasy?
- Would steering along the incentive direction also shift *regenerated* choices with statistical reliability at larger scale, or in games where dose-zero preference is mid-range (0.41–0.65) rather than near ceiling?
- Can the opponent's incentive — undecodable here at chance — be made internally representable (e.g., after opponent-modelling fine-tuning), and would that raise fitted QLk depth beyond GPT-OSS's 2.07?
- How does the recruitment picture change in repeated or incomplete-information games, where Sobotka et al. (2026) found decodable beliefs only weakly influencing actions?
- Is the Qwen2.5-Instruct opponent-payoff-region increment (−0.65, the only reliable one) a causal bottleneck for belief-based choice, or an epiphenomenon of the answer-axis build-up?
