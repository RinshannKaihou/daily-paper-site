Methodology — distilled

Gate the effect, not the whole agent

ATR instruments the agent loop between observation and effect. Every pending intent records typed premises — executable conditions with defined fields and semantics, not free-form reasoning — over an immutable read→decision→intent→effect graph with a hash-based reverse index (SHA-256 digests, ~7.4 KiB serialized per trace). On a state change, only premises in the affected closure are revalidated; the checked target version/token is then bound into a native transaction, CAS, or lease so the target system atomically closes the time-of-check-to-time-of-use gap. Unbound irreversible effects fail closed.

KEEP
irrelevant change ignored

severity dominance: BLOCK > REPLAN > REFRESH > KEEP

Evaluation: three deterministic scenarios (refund, cloud remediation, procurement) × five mutations = 15 cases at a fixed 40% safe / 60% unsafe mix, labeled by the same developer-authored rules that define them. Baselines: NoCheck, VersionAbort, FullScan. 210,000 executions plus 7,000 two-client SQLite races, fail-closed validator tests, dependency-omission fault injection (35,000 unsafe trials per rate), and a 100-repetition binding-boundary study — all single-host (in-memory target + SQLite).

  • Fail-closed by design: all three unavailable-validator tests returned BLOCK, never a silent allow.
  • Dependency omission: randomly omitting the sole critical edge produced false-allow rates of 0.95% / 5.05% / 9.97% at 1% / 5% / 10% omission (35,000 unsafe trials per rate) — selective validation is safe only when capture is complete or conservatively over-approximated.

Strengths

  • Crisp abstraction — version vs decision conflict cleanly separates ATR's layer from OCC, multi-agent coordination, and workflow recovery, with two propositions stating exact guarantees.
  • Operationally meaningful gate — avoids the all-or-nothing choice between ignoring change and discarding all reasoning.
  • Strong selectivity — 0.6 vs 6.0 premises per change, ~2.6 vs 6.4 nodes, near-flat latency from 13 to 4,093 reads.
  • Adversarial self-testing — races, unavailable validators, dependency-omission fault injection.
  • Fully public, offline-reproducible artifact with independent audit scripts and a GenAI-use disclosure.

Limitations

  • No LLM in the loop — a deterministic systems microbenchmark; premise-extraction accuracy and replanning quality are unmeasured.
  • Developer-authored premises and oracles — perfect numbers are conditional on the same humans writing rules and ground truth.
  • Single-host prototype — latency excludes graph build, serialization, checkpoint I/O, connectors, network; no distributed setting.
  • Benchmark provenance — durable cells are patterns "inspired by" WorkBench/AIOpsLab; the 40/60 safe/unsafe mix is fixed, not sampled.
  • Adoption constraint — cross-resource binding needs targets that check tokens (reservations, leases, fencing); for other APIs the residual race is only exposed, not closed.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Q1How accurately can an LLM extract typed premises from its own reasoning — and what false-allow rates do extraction errors induce beyond the graph-side omission curves?
  2. Q2What is the runtime cost of conservative over-approximation on real traces, and can attribution or tool-schema metadata narrow dependencies without reintroducing false allows?
  3. Q3What fraction of real tool APIs expose version tokens, ETags, or leases suitable for target-side binding — and what fallbacks exist for the rest?
  4. Q4Does ATR compose with CoAgent / Cordon / Atomix, or do guarantees duplicate and gap at the seams?
  5. Q5What does end-to-end cost look like on production-derived traces once graph build, I/O, and connector latency are included?
