> **一句话总结：** 该论文指出仅靠 argmax 准确率无法衡量量化 LLM 相对 BF16 基座的保真度损失，提出在完整词表上计算 TVD/JSD 散度作为分布级保真诊断：在 120 组实验（5 个基座模型 × 4 个基准 × 6 档量化）中发现准确率可以纹丝不动（如 Teuken-7B Q4_0 在 PIQA 上仅降 0.2 个百分点）而 TVD 高达 0.560（56% 概率质量被重排），并实证混合精度 Q4_K 在相近显存下普遍比均匀 Q4_0 的散度低 4 倍以上。

> **TL;DR:** The authors argue zero-shot accuracy (an argmax-only statistic) masks severe distributional damage in quantized LLMs, and propose measuring full-vocabulary Total Variation Distance (TVD) and Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD) between BF16 and quantized next-token distributions across a 120-configuration matrix (5 models x 4 benchmarks — MMLU, BoolQ, PIQA, CausalBench — x 6 llama.cpp quantization levels from BF16 to Q2_K). The headline evidence: Teuken-7B at Q4_0 on PIQA loses only 0.2 accuracy points (68.9% → 68.7%) yet requires reassigning 56% of probability mass (TVD 0.560), while mixed-precision Q4_K reaches TVD 0.136 on the same task; on MMLU with LLaMA-3.1-8B, Q4_0's TVD is ~4x Q4_K's (0.221 vs 0.055) at near-identical accuracy (32.1% vs 32.2%). The practical takeaway is a hardware Pareto: Q4_K gives a 65% VRAM reduction for LLaMA-3.1-8B (15.2 GB → 5.27 GB) with TVD below 0.055, making it the best memory–fidelity trade-off among the tested llama.cpp schemes.

## 核心贡献

- **揭示"准确率掩蔽效应"**：实证证明零样本准确率（仅依赖 argmax）在渐进量化下呈现不稳定、非单调的行为，会掩盖量化模型相对 BF16 基座的严重分布级保真度损失；例如 Teuken-7B 在 Q4_0 下 PIQA 准确率仅降 0.2 个百分点（68.9% → 68.7%），但 TVD 高达 0.560——需要重排 56% 的概率质量。
- **提出全词表分布保真度评估框架**：在 token 决策边界处，对 BF16 与量化模型的完整词表 softmax 分布计算有界且对称的统计距离（TVD 与 JSD），避开 KL 散度对近零概率敏感、无界的问题；TVD 可直接解释为"需重排的概率质量比例"。
- **形式化粗糙化定理（Theorem 1）**：证明任何将词表分布映射到答案级分布的随机粗糙化算子都不会增大 TVD/JSD——即基于候选答案子集的常规指标在数学上只能低估全词表的分布偏移，为"必须看全词表"提供了理论依据。
- **120 组配置的系统性实证**：5 个基座模型（LLaMA-3.1-8B、LLaMA-3.2-1B、Mistral-7B-v0.1、Teuken-7B、Gemma-2B）× 4 个基准（MMLU、BoolQ、PIQA、CausalBench）× 6 档量化（BF16、Q8_0、Q6_K、Q4_K、Q4_0、Q2_K），全部量化模型均由 BF16 基座经 llama.cpp 直接生成，固定提示模板、seed=42。
- **混合精度优于均匀量化的分布级证据**：在相近显存与几乎相同的任务准确率下，K-quant 混合精度 Q4_K 的 TVD 普遍显著低于均匀 Q4_0（LLaMA-3.1-8B 在 MMLU 上 0.055 vs 0.221，约 4 倍），并给出以 VRAM-TVD 权衡的硬件 Pareto 前沿分析。

## Key Contributions

- **Expose the accuracy-masking effect**: demonstrates empirically that zero-shot accuracy (an argmax-only statistic) behaves erratically and non-monotonically under progressive quantization while hiding large distributional fidelity loss relative to the BF16 reference — e.g., Teuken-7B at Q4_0 on PIQA drops only 0.2 accuracy points (68.9% → 68.7%) yet has TVD 0.560, meaning 56% of probability mass must be reassigned.
- **A full-vocabulary distributional fidelity framework**: computes bounded, symmetric divergences — Total Variation Distance (TVD) and Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD) — between the complete softmax distributions (temperature 1.0, no truncation) of BF16 and quantized models at the token decision boundary, deliberately avoiding KL divergence's unboundedness and sensitivity to near-zero probabilities. TVD has a direct physical reading: the fraction of probability mass that must be reassigned.
- **A coarse-graining theorem (Theorem 1)**: proves that any stochastic coarse-graining operator K mapping a vocabulary-level distribution to an answer-level distribution satisfies TVD(KP, KQ) ≤ TVD(P, Q) and JSD(KP, KQ) ≤ JSD(P, Q) — so standard answer-subset metrics can only understate true vocabulary-level drift, formally justifying full-vocabulary analysis.
- **A systematic 120-configuration study**: 5 foundation models (LLaMA-3.1-8B, LLaMA-3.2-1B, Mistral-7B-v0.1, Teuken-7B-base-v0.6, Gemma-2B) x 4 benchmarks (MMLU, BoolQ, PIQA, CausalBench) x 6 quantization regimes (BF16, Q8_0, Q6_K, Q4_K, Q4_0, Q2_K), with all quantized models produced directly from the BF16 Hugging Face baselines via llama.cpp, fixed continuous-generation prompt templates, deterministic inference (seed=42) on a single V100 32GB.
- **Distribution-level evidence that mixed precision beats uniform quantization**: at near-identical memory footprints and task accuracy, K-quant Q4_K consistently shows lower divergence than uniform Q4_0 (e.g., LLaMA-3.1-8B on MMLU: TVD 0.055 vs 0.221, ~4x), yielding a hardware Pareto analysis of the VRAM-vs-divergence trade-off.

## Methodology

The paper designs a **dual-stream evaluation pipeline** (Figure 2):

1. **Stream 1 (task metrics)**: standard zero-shot evaluation via length-normalized NLL over candidate options with native KV caching, producing accuracy, F1, and Dutta-style "total flips" (Flip-to-Wrong + Flip-to-Correct transitions between BF16 and each quantized state).
2. **Stream 2 (fidelity metrics)**: the same prompt is fed to both the BF16 model f_θ and its quantized counterpart f_θ̂ (same tokenizer/vocabulary V, differing only in parameter precision); the backend's full-vocabulary logit vector is extracted, softmaxed at temperature 1.0 without truncation, and the two distributions P (BF16) and Q (quantized) are compared with TVD = ½·Σ_v |P(v) − Q(v)| and JSD (base-2, bounded in [0,1]).

To guarantee divergence stems strictly from precision truncation, all quantized models are generated directly from their BF16 baselines with llama.cpp, covering five compression states: Q8_0 (8-bit uniform), Q6_K/Q4_K/Q2_K (mixed-precision K-quants with super-block double quantization and architecture-aware bit allocation), and Q4_0 (legacy 4-bit uniform). Fixed prompt templates ("Question: {question}\nAnswer:") bypass instruction-tuning chat formats. The coarse-graining theorem (proved via the log-sum inequality for JSD) shows answer-level aggregation provably cannot increase measured divergence, so full-vocabulary TVD/JSD are strict upper-bound diagnostics. Experiments run deterministically (seed=42) on one NVIDIA V100 32GB with a 2048-token context window, tracking VRAM via driver queries. TVD is prioritized in tables for its probability-mass interpretation. The framework is a complement to — not a replacement for — task accuracy: it measures reference fidelity, not correctness, calibration, or safety.

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline masking effect (PIQA, Teuken-7B-base-v0.6)**: at Q4_0, accuracy falls a negligible 0.2 points (68.9% BF16 → 68.7%) but prediction flips hit 4.84% and TVD reaches **0.560** — 56% of next-token probability mass must be reassigned to restore the BF16 distribution; the same model at Q4_K achieves a slightly *higher* accuracy (69.2%) with TVD of only **0.136**.
- **Q4_K vs Q4_0 at equal accuracy (MMLU, LLaMA-3.1-8B)**: Q4_K yields TVD **0.055** vs **0.221** for Q4_0 (~4x higher) despite essentially identical accuracy (32.2% vs 32.1%) and near-identical VRAM (6.3 vs 6.0 GB); for Teuken-7B the gap is 0.143 (Q4_K) vs 0.541 (Q4_0).
- **Monotone divergence vs erratic accuracy**: LLaMA-3.1-8B on PIQA shows a clean TVD ladder as quantization strengthens — Q8_0: 0.017, Q6_K: 0.031, Q4_K: 0.052, Q4_0: 0.215, Q2_K: 0.257 — while accuracy at Q2_K is only moderately lower (74.6% vs 77.3% BF16, −2.7 pts) despite 25.7% probability-mass displacement; accuracy even *rises* slightly at Q6_K (77.7%) above the BF16 baseline (77.3%).
- **Quantization can "improve" accuracy via class collapse (BoolQ, LLaMA-3.1-8B)**: accuracy climbs non-monotonically from 44.3% (BF16) to 59.9% (Q4_0) to 62.2% (Q2_K) while flips explode to 61.74% and 72.29% respectively — the gain comes from collapsing onto the majority "True" class, which accuracy alone scores as an improvement.
- **Degenerate flat-accuracy regimes**: Gemma-2B and LLaMA-3.2-1B on BoolQ report exactly 62.2% accuracy with zero flips at *every* quantization level down to Q2_K (recall 1.0, precision 0.6217 — uniform "True" prediction), and ~50.1% accuracy with precision/recall of 0.0 on CausalBench (uniform negative-class prediction); TVD still separates the states (LLaMA-3.2-1B BoolQ: 0.014 at Q8_0 up to 0.280 at Q2_K).
- **Hardware Pareto**: LLaMA-3.1-8B shrinks from 15.2 GB (BF16) to 5.27 GB at Q4_K — a 65% VRAM reduction — with TVD kept below 0.055; moving from Q4_K to Q4_0 saves comparatively little memory but multiplies TVD several-fold for several models; Mistral-7B-v0.1 is the most quantization-robust (Q4_K TVD 0.009 on PIQA, 0.015 on MMLU; Q2_K TVD only 0.057 on MMLU).
- **Flips corroborate divergence**: Mistral-7B-v0.1 on CausalBench at Q4_0 shows 78.47% total flips, and Gemma-2B on CausalBench at Q4_0 shows 98.73% flips — instability that aggregate accuracy never surfaces directly.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: incremental but genuine. The core observation — accuracy masks distributional damage — is largely established (Dutta et al. 2024 showed it via flips and KL; Guo et al. on calibration; Xu et al. on alignment collapse). The added value is (a) bounded, symmetric full-vocabulary metrics robust to the near-zero probabilities that destabilize KL at extreme low-bit, and (b) the coarse-graining theorem showing answer-level metrics can only understate vocabulary-level drift. The systematic llama.cpp scheme comparison (K-quants vs uniform) at matched memory is the most practically novel piece.
- **Rigor**: strong on coverage and transparency (all 120 configs in appendix tables, seed fixed, prompt templates fixed, VRAM measured), but weakened by a near-chance evaluation regime: base models score 28.8–33.5% on MMLU (barely above the ~25% random baseline) under the fixed continuous-generation template, so "stable accuracy" claims on MMLU rest on a regime where accuracy carries almost no signal to begin with. The most dramatic masking case (Teuken-7B Q4_0, TVD 0.541–0.560) is never ablated — why is this multilingual model uniquely fragile to uniform 4-bit while Mistral barely moves (TVD 0.030)? Vocabulary size, tokenizer, and outlier-weight structure are plausible drivers but untested.
- **Reproducibility**: good — llama.cpp/GGUF is open, models are public Hugging Face baselines, benchmarks standard, and the deterministic single-seed setup means exact reruns are feasible; no code release is mentioned, however.
- **Significance**: useful as a deployment diagnostic rather than a scientific breakthrough. The paper is commendably honest that TVD/JSD measure *reference fidelity*, not correctness, calibration, safety, or user-perceived quality — but this honesty also concedes the open question: no evidence links a given TVD level to any downstream consequence (generation quality, task degradation), so "TVD = 0.56 is bad" remains an intuition, and no actionable threshold is offered.
- **Red flags**: the BoolQ/CausalBench degenerate majority-class behavior of the 1B/2B models slightly inflates the masking narrative — accuracy is "stable" there because the models collapsed to a constant prediction, a failure flip/precision/recall already expose cheaply. The framework is also confined to single-token decision-boundary distributions: divergence on the first predicted token may not propagate predictably through multi-step generation. Scope is base models only, one quantization framework (llama.cpp; no AWQ/GPTQ/SmoothQuant comparison), one GPU, and one prompt template per task.

## Strengths

- Clean, well-motivated framing: argmax accuracy is a lossy, non-invertible projection of the predictive distribution, and bounded divergences (TVD/JSD) restore the discarded signal with a direct probability-mass interpretation (TVD 0.25 = 25% mass reassigned).
- The coarse-graining theorem (with a correct log-sum-inequality proof in the appendix) gives the full-vocabulary approach a principled justification: answer-level metrics are provable lower bounds on vocabulary-level divergence.
- Comprehensive and transparent empirical matrix — 5 models x 4 benchmarks x 6 quantization states with every number published, plus VRAM tracking enabling a practical memory-vs-fidelity Pareto plot (Figure 1).
- The Q4_K vs Q4_0 comparison at matched memory and accuracy is immediately actionable for edge-deployment practitioners using llama.cpp.
- Consistent intellectual honesty: the paper repeatedly scopes its claims ("reference fidelity, not correctness/calibration/safety") and lists concrete future-work gaps rather than overclaiming.

## Limitations

- **Base models only** with fixed plain-text templates — no instruction-tuned or RLHF-aligned models, so relevance to how quantized LLMs are actually deployed (chat formats) is untested.
- **Single-token, prompt-conditioned divergence**: distributions are compared only at the immediate decision boundary; multi-step generation drift — arguably what users actually experience — is out of scope.
- **One quantization ecosystem**: only llama.cpp/GGUF schemes; no comparison against AWQ, GPTQ, or SmoothQuant, leaving generality across PTQ methods open.
- **No external validation of the metric**: TVD/JSD are never correlated with calibration error, generation quality, or human judgment, and no deployment-relevant divergence threshold is proposed.
- **Weak-evidence regimes**: MMLU accuracies sit near chance, and the small models' majority-class collapse makes several "stable accuracy" cases partly artifacts of degenerate predictions; the striking Teuken-7B/Q4_0 fragility is reported but not explained or ablated.

## Relevance

与高效推理与模型压缩方向高度相关：该论文为量化 LLM 的部署前评估提供了超越准确率的分布级保真度诊断工具（TVD/JSD），并实证了 llama.cpp K-quant 在显存-保真权衡上的优势，可直接指导边缘设备上 4-bit 量化方案的选型。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does first-token TVD actually predict multi-step generation divergence or downstream task quality — i.e., is there a correlation between prompt-conditioned TVD and long-form output degradation (e.g., ROUGE/BERTScore drop, error rate in chain-of-thought)?
- Why is Teuken-7B so much more fragile to uniform Q4_0 (TVD ~0.54–0.56) than Mistral-7B (~0.03) — is it vocabulary size (~250K multilingual tokens), tokenizer structure, or outlier-weight distribution, and can this sensitivity be predicted a priori?
- How do the findings transfer to instruction-tuned/RLHF-aligned models and chat templates, where the decision boundary sits after long system prompts?
- Can a practical deployment threshold be established — e.g., the TVD level beyond which calibration error (ECE), safety-alignment collapse, or user-perceived quality measurably degrades?
- Do AWQ, GPTQ, and SmoothQuant show the same uniform-vs-mixed-precision fidelity gap, or is the Q4_K advantage specific to llama.cpp's K-quant super-block design?
