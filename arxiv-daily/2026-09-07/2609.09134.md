> **一句话总结：** 本文发现在为弱模型进化出的 agent harness 上直接用专家完整轨迹做 LoRA 模仿微调会让弱模型在全部 7 个企业任务上回退 4~30 个百分点（因为模仿带偏了模型的原生规划风格、破坏了模型与 harness 的契合），而改用"仅定位并重写弱模型自身 rollout 中失败的那一个回合"的在策略专家修正方法，可在不破坏契合的前提下叠加增益（平均 78.0%→79.7%），实现 harness 与模型权重的安全共同进化。

> **TL;DR:** Salesforce AI shows that after automated harness evolution lifts qwen3-coder-30b-a3b from 29.2% to 78.0% mean success on seven enterprise agent benchmarks, the "obvious" next step — LoRA fine-tuning the weaker model on gemini-3.1-pro-preview expert trajectories under that evolved harness — backfires, regressing all seven tasks by −4.2 to −29.9 points (mean 78.0%→63.1%, −14.9), even though the identical recipe helps under the unevolved harness (29.2%→35.5%, +6.3). Diagnosis: imitation transfers knowledge and increases scaffold usage but causes planning-strategy drift that breaks model–harness fit (planning failures jump from 1.1% to 14.6% of failures). Their fix — an automated on-policy expert-correction pipeline where a meta-level MLE agent localizes the single failing turn in the student's own rollouts and the expert rewrites only that turn — composes with harness evolution (78.0%→79.7%, +1.7) at under one hour of training on ~500 rows. The actionable principle: after harness evolution, supervise on-policy at student-visited states, never via wholesale imitation.

## 核心贡献

- **发现并隔离了一个反直觉的失效模式：** 在进化后的 harness 上，用更强专家（gemini-3.1-pro-preview）的完整轨迹对弱模型做 LoRA 模仿微调，会在全部 7 个企业任务上导致 4~30 个百分点的回退；而完全相同的配方在未进化的基线 harness 上却有 +6.3 的提升——证明失效源于"模仿 × harness 进化"的交互，而非模仿本身。
- **给出了失效机制的因果诊断：** 通过六类失败分类和 harness 组件采用率扫描，证明回退不是因为知识丢失或不再使用脚手架（两者都在改善，领域计算配方使用率从 30.8% 升至 76.1%），而是规划缺陷占比从 1.1% 暴涨到 14.6%——弱模型照搬了专家的规划策略却不具备执行能力，不再契合围绕其原生规划风格进化出的 harness。
- **提出在策略专家修正（on-policy expert correction）管线：** 由一个自主 MLE agent 端到端自动化——在弱模型自己的失败 rollout 中定位出错的那个回合，让专家只重写该回合（保留前后所有步骤），配合 best-of-3 质量裁判与约 500 行的小数据集做 LoRA-SFT，在保持模型-harness 契合的同时叠加模型侧增益。
- **验证了 harness 的向上可迁移性：** 为弱模型进化的 harness 被更强专家用得更好（gemini 专家从基线 harness 的 84.4% 升至进化 harness 的 93.6%），既揭示了模型侧的追赶空间，也使专家监督成为可能。
- **给出可迭代的共同进化配方：** 在策略修正可以安全地堆叠进"harness 进化 → 模型更新"的循环，训练一小时内完成，为低成本企业 agent 的 harness+权重联合优化提供了具体设计原则。

## Key Contributions

- **Identify and isolate a counterintuitive failure mode:** After harness evolution lifts the weaker model, LoRA fine-tuning it on the stronger expert's full trajectories under that evolved harness regresses all seven enterprise tasks by 4–30 points, while the identical recipe helps under the unevolved baseline harness (+6.3) — cleanly attributing the failure to the imitation × harness-evolution interaction rather than to imitation itself.
- **Causal diagnosis of the mechanism:** Using a six-category failure ontology and a harness-component adoption scan, they show the regression is not lost knowledge or abandoned scaffolding (both improve; domain-computation recipe usage rises from 30.8% to 76.1%) but planning-strategy drift: planning failures jump from 1.1% to 14.6% of failures because the student adopts the expert's plan without the competence to execute it, breaking fit with a harness evolved around its native planning style.
- **An on-policy expert-correction pipeline, automated end-to-end by a meta-level MLE agent:** it localizes the single failing turn in each of the weaker model's own failed rollouts, has the expert rewrite only that turn (one sentence of corrected strategy plus the corrected tool call, chosen best-of-3 by a quality judge), and LoRA-SFTs on a ~500-row set — adding model-side gains without breaking harness fit.
- **Confirm upward harness transfer and the headroom it exposes:** a harness evolved with only the weaker model is used even more effectively by the stronger expert (84.4%→93.6% for gemini-3.1-pro-preview), motivating expert supervision and defining the gap to be closed.
- **A composable co-evolution recipe:** on-policy correction stacks safely into an iterative harness→model loop, trains in under an hour, and yields a concrete design principle for practitioners: after harness evolution, supervise on-policy at states the student actually visits rather than imitating wholesale expert trajectories.

## Methodology

The paper runs on the task suite, environments, and harness-optimization framework of Yang et al. [2026]: seven objectively verifiable enterprise agentic tasks — payroll auditing, budget approval, stock alerting, IoT anomaly detection, browser automation (Playwright), website management, and code refactoring. Two environment changes were made (agents cannot modify task databases directly when MCP tools are required; tool-call errors are exposed to the optimizer), so absolute numbers are not directly comparable with the original benchmark paper.

**Harness evolution.** A GEPA-style search where a gemini-3.1-pro-preview meta-agent proposes failure-driven edits to the harness (system prompt, tools, hooks, context management, sub-agent configs); an edit is retained only if validation performance improves. Each task runs three random seeds with a $20 budget and a 600 s per-rollout limit; the best-validation harness h* is selected.

**Models.** Weak student: qwen3-coder-30b-a3b-instruct (30B total / 3B active); replication student: gemma-4-26b-a4b-it (26B / 4B active). Expert/teacher: gemini-3.1-pro-preview. Expert trajectories are converted into each student's chat and tool-call format.

**Imitation arm (SFT-imit.).** Collect trajectories scoring 1.0 under h* — from the expert first, then the base model's own passing rollouts — and LoRA-SFT directly on them. Headline numbers are averaged over three LoRA seeds (0, 101, 202).

**On-policy correction arm (SFT-corr.).** Start from the base model's own rollouts under h*. An automated failure-locus step identifies the single turn where each failed rollout goes wrong; given the task, the trajectory up to that turn, and the failing checkpoint, the expert rewrites only that turn, leaving all surrounding steps intact. Three candidate corrections are sampled per turn and the best kept by a quality judge. The final ~500-row set mixes ~400 in-place expert corrections with ~50 self-pass rows sampled from the model's own passing rollouts (prioritizing capability-boundary examples that pass inconsistently).

**Training and evaluation.** LoRA (rank 16 or 64, best reported), 2 epochs, lr 1e-4, bf16, effective batch size 8, sequence length 49,152 tokens (98,304 if >5% of data truncates). Adapters are merged into a dense ~57–61 GB checkpoint served with tensor parallelism on two H200 GPUs; a single run finishes in under an hour. Training/validation splits drive optimization; test-split success is averaged over three runs (mean ± SEM).

**Failure analysis.** Every failed rollout is classified by an LLM-as-judge into a six-category adaptation-failure ontology (other, tool-use, instruction-following, implicit knowledge, long-context, planning); a companion adoption scan measures how often each model actually invokes each evolved harness component.

## Results & Highlights

- **Harness evolution is the big lever:** mean test success for qwen3-coder-30b-a3b across the seven enterprise benchmarks rises from 29.2% (baseline harness h0) to 78.0% (evolved harness h*), +48.8 points, with the largest single-task swing on payroll auditing (13.9%→81.7%, +67.8).
- **Imitation under the evolved harness backfires on all seven tasks:** LoRA-SFT on expert trajectories drops mean success from 78.0% to 63.1% (−14.9 vs the evolved-harness baseline), ranging from −4.2 on anomaly detection to −29.9 on payroll auditing, with −20.3 on website management and −16.0 on browser automation — even though the teacher is strictly stronger on this same harness (93.6%).
- **The control experiment isolates the interaction:** the identical imitation recipe under the unevolved baseline harness raises mean success from 29.2% to 35.5% (+6.3), so the expert trajectories themselves are helpful; only their combination with an evolved harness is harmful.
- **The regression reproduces in a second model family:** on the WebArena task, gemma-4-26b-a4b-it improves 46.7%→55.6% (+8.9) from harness evolution while the expert gains 71.1%→81.1% (+10.0), yet expert-trajectory SFT regresses the weak Gemma to 41.1% — 14.5 points below its evolved-harness baseline and 5.6 below even its default-harness baseline.
- **Diagnosis — knowledge transfers, planning breaks:** after imitation under h*, the share of hard failures attributed to planning defects jumps from 1.1% to 14.6% (+13.5) while implicit-knowledge failures shrink (46.2%→44.5%); harness adoption actually increases (domain-computation recipe usage 30.8%→76.1%). Under h0 the same planning drift occurs (0.9%→11.5%) but there is no planning fit to lose, so the knowledge gain (61.0%→54.4%) dominates and net success rises.
- **On-policy correction composes with the evolved harness:** replacing wholesale imitation with single-turn expert corrections raises mean success from 78.0% to 79.7% (+1.7), gaining on five of seven tasks — website management +5.6, stock alerting +2.2, code refactoring +2.2, anomaly detection +1.7, browser automation +1.2 — and staying within noise where the harness saturated (budget approval −0.6, payroll auditing −0.4). Planning failures stay at the base-model floor (1.1%→1.8%) while knowledge failures fall further (46.2%→43.2%) and the overall failure rate drops from 28.9% to 26.8%.
- **Cost is a headline feature:** the correction set is only ~500 rows and a full LoRA training run completes in under an hour on H100/H200 hardware, against a $20-per-task harness search budget — cheap enough to stack inside an iterative co-evolution loop. Case studies concretize the mechanism: a post-imitation payroll rollout computes the right aggregates but re-issues the same view 29 times and never finishes (78 steps, score 0), and an imitation-tuned Gemma skips the harness's filter-then-count procedure on WebArena to report the unfiltered 351 instead of the correct 346.

## Authors & Credibility

All eleven authors (Zhou Yu, Bin Bi, Shiva Kumar Pentyala, Shubham Mehrotra, Sougata Chaudhuri, Shilpa Bhagavath, Zeyuan Chen, Ran Xu, Phil Mui, James Zhu, Sitaram Asur) are at Salesforce AI, an industrial lab with a strong track record on agentic LLM systems and applied ML research; the corresponding author is Zhou Yu. The paper builds directly on the first author's ecosystem — it adopts the benchmark suite and GEPA-style harness-optimization framework of Yang et al. [2026] ("Better harnesses, smaller models") and cites the adjacent co-evolution literature (SIA, Co-Harness, HarnessForge, HarnessX) accurately. The work is a preprint (not yet peer-reviewed) but reports three-run averages with SEM, discloses environment changes that break comparability with the source benchmark, and includes ablations (baseline-harness control, second model family, failure taxonomy, adoption scan) that exceed the norm for this line of work. Two of the cited baselines it positions against (Lauffer et al. 2025, Ye et al. 2026 on on-policy expert corrections) are the direct ancestors of its fix, which the related-work section credits honestly.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The interaction finding — expert-trajectory imitation helping under a generic harness but regressing under an evolved one — is genuinely novel and the paper isolates it well with the h0 control and the Gemma replication. The correction mechanism itself, however, is a straightforward adaptation of on-policy expert corrections (Lauffer et al. 2025; Ye et al. 2026) to the harness setting; the novelty is the diagnosis and the composition result, not the algorithm.
- **Rigor:** Above average. Three-run means with SEM, three LoRA seeds for the headline imitation arm, a six-category failure taxonomy, and a component-adoption scan triangulate the planning-drift diagnosis convincingly. But the failure classification is LLM-as-judge with no reported human-agreement validation, there are no significance tests (the +1.2 playwright gain sits near its ±2.22 SEM, and budget/payroll are slightly negative), and the LoRA config is reported as "rank 16 or rank 64 and report the best scores" — a mild cherry-picking risk in exactly the arms where gains are smallest.
- **Reproducibility:** Good on training detail (LoRA hyperparameters, data construction, correction prompt, best-of-N selection, $20/600 s harness budget, appendix case studies) but dependent on the Yang et al. [2026] benchmark codebase, which is not a standard public suite, and the two deliberate environment changes mean absolute numbers cannot be checked against the original benchmark paper. The expert (gemini-3.1-pro-preview) and student checkpoints are named precisely.
- **Significance:** The negative result and design principle are the real contributions and are valuable for anyone building cheap domain-specific agents. The positive result is modest: +1.7 mean on top of the harness (+5.6 best task), leaving the student 13.9 points below the expert (79.7% vs 93.6%), a gap the authors attribute to the LoRA ceiling without testing that attribution (e.g., no full fine-tuning or larger-corpus run).
- **Red flags:** The Gemma replication covers a single task (WebArena), so "reproduced across two model families" rests on 1 of 7 tasks for the second family. The imitation arm mixes expert trajectories with the student's own successes, and the correction arm's ~500-row set has a different mixture — the data-mixture difference is not ablated separately from the on-policy-vs-off-policy difference. No head-to-head numbers against the cited co-evolution systems (SIA, Co-Harness, HarnessForge, HarnessX), and no RL baseline despite RL being the obvious alternative model-side lever. The claim that correction "matches or beats the evolved-harness base on every task" is fair only under the within-noise reading of two slightly negative tasks.

## Strengths

- A crisp, surprising, and practically important negative result (imitation after harness evolution regresses −4 to −30 points) isolated by a well-designed control (the same recipe helps under h0).
- The mechanism analysis goes beyond correlation: the adoption scan rules out "model stopped using the harness," the failure taxonomy pins the damage on planning drift, and the two appendix case studies (the 29-times-re-verified payroll answer; the 351-vs-346 unfiltered count) make the failure mode vivid and concrete.
- The proposed fix is cheap and composable: ~500 rows, best-of-3 judge filtering, under one hour of H100/H200 LoRA training — realistic for enterprise practice, and explicitly stackable into an iterative harness–model co-evolution loop.
- Honest reporting throughout: disclosed environment changes, SEM over three runs, admission that the expert gap is not closed, and clear positioning against the on-policy-correction prior work it builds on.

## Limitations

- The headline positive gain is small (+1.7 mean; only website management +5.6 clears its SEM comfortably) and does not close the student–expert gap (79.7% vs 93.6%); the LoRA-ceiling explanation is asserted, not tested with e.g. full fine-tuning or larger data.
- The second-family replication (Gemma) is run on one task only, so cross-family generality of both the regression and the fix is thinly evidenced.
- Failure-mode classification relies on an LLM judge with no reported human validation, and no statistical significance testing is provided for the per-task deltas.
- The imitation arm and correction arm differ in data mixture (expert+self-success trajectories vs ~400 corrections + ~50 self-pass), so mixture composition is confounded with on-policy-ness.
- No comparison against RL post-training, DAgger-style iterative relabeling, or the cited co-evolution systems; only one expert model (gemini-3.1-pro-preview) and two students are studied, leaving expert-choice and capability-gap sensitivity unexplored.

## Relevance

对于关注低成本企业级 LLM agent、harness/工具链自动优化与模型微调协同的研究者，这篇论文直接回答了"harness 进化后还能不能安全地做模型侧适配"这一工程关键问题，其"在策略修正优于整体模仿"的原则对构建可迭代的 agent 共同进化流水线有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the imitation-induced regression persist with full fine-tuning or much larger correction corpora, or is it an artifact of LoRA's limited capacity to absorb a new planning style?
- Would RL on the binary task-success reward (e.g., GRPO under the evolved harness) avoid the planning-drift failure mode, or does on-policy correction remain the safer supervision signal?
- Can harness evolution be made fine-tuning-aware — optimizing the scaffold for the model that will exist after adaptation, as the authors propose — and does joint optimization beat the sequential loop?
- How does the severity of the regression scale with the student–expert capability gap, and does the effect appear when the expert is only marginally stronger?
- Is single-turn correction optimal, or would correcting 2–3 turns per failed rollout (or iterating correction over multiple rounds) yield compounding gains?
- Does the LLM-as-judge failure taxonomy (especially the planning vs. implicit-knowledge split) agree with human labels, and is the 14.6%-of-failures planning share stable across judges?
