> **一句话总结：** 本文提出一个零训练、零拟合的"透镜"直接从Transformer自身参数与激活中读出预测背后的组件：把归因分母从绝对质量改为带符号净值后，一个预测只依赖约53个组件（13个必要、8个充分），在12个第三方模型上充分集仅2–16个组件；组件可双向命名并可被直接写入——往一个备用FFN单元里安装新联想（rank 578→1）仅需0.25%的held-out损失，是rank-one编辑成本的1/40。

> **TL;DR:** A training-free "lens" reads predictions straight from a transformer's own parameters and activations. Because logit contributions are signed and cancel (opposing mass is a median 7× the net across 18 models), dividing by the net instead of absolute mass shrinks circuits from tens of thousands to dozens: on a GPT-2-small-scale baseline trained on OpenWebText, 53 components carry 90% of a prediction, 13 are necessary, and 8 (of 46,080) suffice to reproduce the token; on 12 off-the-shelf models (GPT-2 124M to Mistral/Qwen2.5 7B) sufficient sets run 2–16 components with the largest models at the small end. About half of every model's components get nameable writes, unit read-rows recover their top-8 causal drivers at 58.9% (37× chance), and an association installed into one spare FFN unit moves a target from rank 578 to 1 for 0.25% held-out loss — 40× cheaper than a closed-form rank-one (ROME-style) update (0.0016 vs 0.0681 nats to top-10). The catch: sufficiency search uses the known answer (existence, not prediction), all edit results are on a ~124M model, and swapping GELU for order-preserving activations reaches the instrument's ceiling but turns installs into two-part edits.

## 核心贡献

- **带符号净值归因透镜（The Looking Glass）**：把每个组件对logit的贡献视为带符号量并除以净值，而非按绝对值累计。同一归因、同一预测下，绝对值口径需3,000–200,000个组件，净值口径仅几十个（baseline上n90=53 vs 23,971，膨胀452×）。18个模型中反对质量中位数为净值的7倍。
- **三层数字的必要/充分刻画**：baseline（GPT-2 small架构、OpenWebText一epoch）上，53个组件承载90%预测、13个不可失去（required graph）、8个足以独立重建该token（充分集仅占模型0.017%）；等大随机集在115个预测中成功率为0%。
- **"其余模型在做什么"的回答——carriage**：层的更新中74%（sigmoid模型79%）是入射状态的固定线性映射（其中63%为纯旋转），与预测内容无关；剩余部分不是在为后续token囤积信息（后续512个token上电路与随机集代价之比0.98–0.99）。
- **组件双向命名**：写出侧由其驱动的预测命名（覆盖近半模型组件，48.2%）；读入侧用层原生token表从权重解码，top-8驱动token恢复率58.9%（37×于chance），且注意力通道的value投影行达0.844并随K上升至0.892。词嵌入无法命名的~72%"匿名"输入按上游来源分组后呈现语法类别（POS纯度超随机划分）。
- **名字可被行动（编辑）**：增益编辑97%落在参数命名的scope内；向单个备用FFN单元安装"见A说B"（key/value均从权重直读、无梯度）使B从rank 578→1、其余位置rank 778→782，代价0.25% held-out损失，比closed-form rank-one编辑便宜40×；跨两层电路（第8层head标记'did'+第10层单元回答'good'：rank 920→3，0.111 nats）；从两层上游驱动模型自训单元可承载86%的效应。
- **激活函数的受控实验**：GELU非单调（低于x*≈−0.752时反向），59.8–62.0%的预激活质量落在反向区间——这是单元读取衰减至低于chance的根因。换成保序激活（sigmoid/softplus）后读取达到仪器自洽上限（0.98–1.01倍ceiling）、电路在闭包上小4–7×、质量仍在baseline收敛带内；代价是无静默态、安装变成两部分编辑。ReLU以质量换可读性（ppl 21.01–22.23 vs 19.00–19.65）。
- **12个第三方模型的全套验证**：GPT-2、OPT、SmolLM2、Qwen2.5×3、Gemma-3、OLMo-2、Llama-3.2×2、TinyLlama、Mistral（124M–7B、7个架构家族）：闭包占模型1.1–3.4%且不随规模增长；Mistral 7B与Qwen2.5 7B的充分集仅2个组件；OPT（ReLU）取消最轻（膨胀106× vs Qwen2.5 7B的1102×）；门控FFN中47–48%组件负向发放，不带符号读scope会在负向组件上4–11%落错方向。

## Key Contributions

- **A signed-net attribution lens**: component contributions to the logit are counted signed and normalized by the net rather than absolute mass. The same attribution on the same predictions returns dozens instead of tens of thousands of components (n90 = 53 vs 23,971 absolute on the baseline, a 452× inflation); opposing mass is a median 7× the net across 18 models.
- **Necessary/sufficient decomposition per prediction**: on the baseline, 53 components carry 90% of a prediction, 13 cannot be lost (required graph), and 8 of 46,080 suffice to produce the token with everything else at that position zeroed (0.017% of the model); same-size random sets reproduce the token on 0 of 115 predictions.
- **An answer to "what is the rest doing"**: 74% of a layer's update (79% sigmoid) is a fixed linear map of the incoming state — "carriage" — mostly a rotation (63%), with shear concentrated in layer one; the remainder is not staging for later tokens (circuit vs random-set cost ratio 0.98–0.99 over the next 512 tokens).
- **Two-sided naming of components**: writes are named by the predictions a component drives (covering ~48% of the model, 87–92% coherent); reads are decoded from weights against a layer-native token table at 58.9% top-8 overlap with causal drivers (37× chance), while attention-channel value rows reach 0.844 and rise to 0.898 with K. The ~72% "anonymous" inputs separate into grammatical categories when sorted by upstream source.
- **Edits that act on names**: gain edits respect the parameter-named scope 97% of the time; an association installs into one spare FFN unit (key = layer-native row, value = readout direction, nothing fitted) moving the target from rank 578 to 1 for 0.25% held-out loss, 40× cheaper than the closed-form rank-one update; a cross-layer circuit (installed head at layer 8 + installed unit at layer 10) moves a target from rank 920 to 3 where the head fires, at 0.111 nats; a self-trained unit is driven from two layers upstream with 86% mediation.
- **A controlled activation-function study**: GELU is non-monotone below x* ≈ −0.752 and 59.8–62.0% of pre-activation mass sits there, reversing the weight-supplied ordering; order-preserving activations read at the instrument's ceiling (flat in K) at parity quality, halve-to-two-thirds circuit size at one hop, and shrink closures 4–7×, at the price of a two-part install.
- **Validation on 12 off-the-shelf models** (124M–7B, 7 families): closures are 1.1–3.4% of each model with no growth in scale; sufficient sets run 2–16 components with the 7B models at 2; OPT (ReLU) shows the least cancellation (106× inflation); on gated feed-forwards 47–48% of prediction-carrying components fire negative, and unsigned scope reads invert edits (4–11% correct) where signed reads score 96–97%.

## Methodology

The paper builds everything from the transformer's own parameters and activations — "nothing trained and nothing fitted."

1. **The accounting (Section 2)**. Each component u (one FFN unit, or one attention (head, channel) pair — both a scalar activation times a write column) contributes κu = au⟨cu, γ⊙(Ut−Ū)/ρ⟩ to token t's logit, and the sum over components is exact. The lens's one departure from common practice is the denominator: rank components by κu and count how many reach 90% of the *signed net* (n90) rather than of the positive or absolute total. Ablation validates necessity (does removing the top-k flip the prediction, against a random-removal control), and Algorithm 1 validates sufficiency: seed with the required graph, repair starved members from their suppliers, then cut dispensable members, until keeping only set S at a position reproduces the token with all other units/channels zeroed there. Damage is read on the margin (target logit minus strongest rival), not the raw logit, because ablation shrinks the residual and uniformly inflates logits.
2. **Layer-native token table**. For reading mid-stack, Ẽ[l,t] is the average input to layer l's FFN when token t is substituted at the position across held-out contexts — the layer's own picture of each token in its own frame, computed in one pass, fitted to nothing. Both sides of a decode are scored in the reader's frame (scoring writer and reader in different frames yields a −0.02 correlation that looks like "no edge").
3. **Naming**. Writes: collect strongest contributors over 1.5M predictions; a component is covered/characterized/coherent against a frequency-matched null in a corpus distributional space. Reads: decode the read row wu against Ẽ (both centered) and check against the causal top-K measured by token substitution on disjoint contexts, chance-corrected (ov@K). Provenance: an upstream component's drive is scored by ωv = cos(carry(cv), wu), where carry is the composition of fitted layer maps — no coefficient fitted; sibling vs direct-source shapes are separated by 24-component deletions.
4. **Carriage**: fit a fixed map Aℓ minimizing ‖x·A − Δ‖ per layer on one sample of positions, score on a disjoint sample; restrict to a rotation (Procrustes) for the rigid part; the shear is the difference.
5. **Editing**: gains (multiply au by α); installs into the least-active ("spare") unit with key = centered layer-native row of token A and value = readout direction of B; placement measured by survival (cosine of the readout change with the written direction) and spill; cross-layer circuits carry intermediate state on a random private direction (the model works in ~170–195 of 768 dimensions, ~580 free); taps into trained units via the read row times the norm gain, projected off the constant direction, with mediation measured by ablating the tapped unit.
6. **Controlled activation arms**: five variants matched on width/data/schedule differing only in φ (sigmoid, softplus, ReLU, unshaped sigmoid, fuzzy set operators A∩B / A\B with an auxiliary alignment objective), all read with the same instruments, with a convergence screen (perplexity + LAMBADA) because collapsed seeds read as spuriously legible (worst excluded seed: n90 = 1).
7. **External models**: the same procedure, unchanged, on 12 off-the-shelf models (GPT-2, OPT, SmolLM2, Qwen2.5 0.5/1.5/7B, Gemma-3 1B, OLMo-2 1B, Llama-3.2 1B/3B, TinyLlama, Mistral 7B). GPT-NeoX/Pythia (parallel sublayers) is deliberately excluded.

## Results & Highlights

- **Circuit size under the three denominators (115 predictions at p≥0.3, baseline)**: 53 components reach 90% of the net vs 11,974 (positives-only) and 23,971 (absolute value) — a 452× inflation from the sign choice alone; across the 12 external models inflation runs 106× (OPT, ReLU) to 1102× (Qwen2.5 7B), confirming cancellation is a property of transformers, not of one implementation.
- **Necessity vs sufficiency (baseline)**: removing the 13 top-ranked components changes the prediction where removing 13 random ones does not; 8 components (0.017% of the model) reproduce the token with everything else zeroed, while the same number drawn randomly succeeds on 0/115 predictions and the top-k ranking's own pick on only 7%. On the 12 external models the sufficient set is 2–16 components — Mistral 7B and Qwen2.5 7B need just 2 (≈0.000% of the model).
- **Closure does not grow with scale**: following the dependency graph to a fixed point ends on 1.1–3.4% of each model (GPT-2 984 components / 2.14%; Mistral 7B 11,114 / 1.88%), and the required graph is 6–34 components everywhere.
- **Carriage**: a fixed linear map of the incoming state accounts for 74% of a layer's update on the baseline (79% sigmoid; rigid rotation alone 63/66%), with the first layer shearing 3–5× more than any other; the circuit is worth no more than an equally sized random set for subsequent tokens (per-prediction cost ratio 0.98–0.99 at horizons 16–512, on 9 models 0.73–1.07).
- **Read-side naming**: the parameters recover a unit's 8 strongest causal drivers at 58.9% (chance 1.6%, i.e. 37×; sigmoid-model ceiling is 79.2%) but fall to −9.5% by K=256; attention channels do better (0.844 at K=8 rising to 0.898) because their value path is linear. On the write side, 47.7–48.2% of components are nameable, and attribution predicts deletion cost monotonically (r = 0.60).
- **Install vs rank-one editor (layer 10, baseline)**: the single-unit install brings the target into the top ten for 0.0016 nats of held-out loss against 0.0681 for the closed-form rank-one update (Equation 27) — a factor of 40 — and the published gradient-value version reaches rank 1 only for 0.611 nats; across the five trained models the single-unit install is cheaper by factors of 9 (sigmoid) to 72 (set-operator).
- **Gated feed-forwards need the sign**: on Llama-3.2-1B and Qwen2.5-1.5B, 48% and 47% of prediction-carrying components fire negative; scope read without the sign lands edits correctly on only 4–11% of those components (models overall barely above chance), while signed reads score 96–97%.
- **Activation intervention**: order-preserving arms read at the instrument ceiling (sigmoid 78.0→82.4 flat in K vs baseline 58.9→−9.5), with quality inside the baseline band (sigmoid ppl 19.30–19.40 vs converged baseline 19.00–19.65) and closures 4–7× smaller (99–182 vs 736 components); promote-edit efficacy reaches 6.3–9.3 (set operators 10.1–14.3) vs the baseline's saturating 3.3–3.9, though plain installs fail (0%) on shaped arms until repaired to 27–36% by the two-part correction.
- **Tapping a trained unit**: a write at layer 8 raises a layer-10 unit's activation by 24.1 sd and moves its token from rank 1116 to 32 for 0.049 nats; ablating that unit removes 90% of the effect (0.86 median over 12 units), vs 0.55 when the driving direction is estimated from activations instead of read from Wa.

## Authors & Credibility

Single-authored by Mark Oskin, a professor in the School of Computer Science and Engineering at the University of Washington, September 2026. The experimental program is unusually large for one author — 18 trained models (8 baseline GELU seeds, 5 activation arms with up to 8 seeds each), plus the full pipeline run on 12 public checkpoints — but the methods are all closed-form or measurement-based (no training of explainers), which is consistent with a small team. The paper's methodological hygiene is notably strong: it reports failed seeds alongside converged ones, includes an appendix of "measurements that failed and conventions that flatter," states that every sufficiency size is an upper bound from a heuristic search, and quotes quality (perplexity, LAMBADA) beside every legibility figure because collapsed models read as more legible. It cites and reproduces the relevant prior results (SAEs in Appendix C, the rank-one editor of Meng et al./ROME, Geva et al.'s key-value reading, Elhage et al.'s residual decomposition, Oskin's own prior rigid-rotation result). One quirk: the paper is the author's own prior work's biggest dependency (the [41] carriage/rigid-body result is cited as "Oskin [41]"), so part of the framing builds on self-published foundations.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The core accounting move — divide by the signed net rather than absolute mass — is small but consequential, and the paper persuasively shows it moves circuit sizes by 2–3 orders of magnitude, which is bigger than any model-to-model difference it measures. The per-unit install with the site chosen from weights (rather than causal tracing or gradients) is a genuine increment over Dai et al./Hakimi et al., and the observation that ~63% of GELU pre-activation mass sits where the function is non-monotone is a crisp, checkable diagnosis. Much else is assembled from known parts (layer-native tables resemble tuned-lens frames; the backward graph resembles Ferrando–Voita's; the rank-one comparison reuses ROME's update).
- **Rigor**: Generally high — matched random controls everywhere, disjoint contexts for ground truth, chance-corrected overlaps, seed-level quality screens, and explicit caveats (e.g., Chen et al.'s warning that signed denominators degrade to chance when recursively iterated is engaged directly, since this paper's quantity is a single non-recursive sum). But the sufficiency search uses the answer: every step asks whether the known token survives, so it establishes that a small sufficient set exists, not a method that finds circuits without knowing the output — the paper says so, but the headline "8 components suffice" is weaker than it sounds.
- **Reproducibility**: The instruments are described precisely (equations for every quantity, training recipes, seed tables), and the models are standard (GPT-2 architecture, OpenWebText, public checkpoints). No code release is mentioned, and the sheer volume of bespoke measurements (dozens of distinct protocols and nulls) would make independent replication laborious; some numbers rest on small samples (e.g., the two comma units in Section 8.2 are explicitly "an anecdote, not a measurement"; the set-operator self-cancellation comparison is 3.7% vs 2.3% "within each other's noise").
- **Significance**: If the net-denominator accounting holds up, it resets the empirical baseline for "how big is a circuit" and undercuts absolute-mass-based size claims in the SAE-graph literature. The carriage result (74–79% of a layer's update is a fixed map) is a strong, quantitative version of a widely suspected idea. But the practical edit results — the most eye-catching claims (rank 578→1 for 0.25% loss, 40× cheaper than rank-one) — are single edits at single positions on a ~124M model trained for one epoch on OpenWebText, which the paper itself concedes inherits the critique of Yang et al. on editing evaluations; generalization to 7B production models is shown for reading, not for editing.
- **Red flags / weaknesses**: (1) The one-hop attribution over-credits late layers by a factor of ~0.53–0.65 against causal effect at k=16 — acknowledged, but it means the "53 components" are skewed toward the readout end by construction. (2) Circuit size depends on the confidence floor (p≥0.3) and the unit choice (channel vs head changes n90 from 53 to 24); cross-paper comparisons are fragile, though the paper is commendably explicit. (3) The n90 spread among same-size external models is wide (76–208 among 1B models; Gemma-3's 208 is an outlier), so "a few dozen" is a loose central tendency. (4) The claimed practical payoff — "a model that can be programmed and not merely trained" — is far beyond what single-component edits at one position demonstrate. (5) Coverage limits: GPT-NeoX/Pythia excluded, MoE unhandled, attention patterns not followed, all circuits are single-position.

## Strengths

- The single accounting correction is validated the right way: same attribution, same predictions, only the denominator changes — and the effect (2–3 orders of magnitude) is shown on 18 models spanning 56× in parameters.
- Sufficiency is tested against the right controls (random sets at matched size: 0/115; the ranking's own top-k: 7%; non-convergent repair building hundreds of components without success), and the "floor" predictions where the embedding alone wins are measured and excluded rather than silently pocketed.
- The naming claims are verified against causal behavior measured by substitution on disjoint contexts, not against eyeballed token lists; the paper even quantifies where the method fails (agreement below chance beyond K≈100 on GELU).
- The GELU non-monotonicity diagnosis with the pre-activation mass distribution (Figure 20) plus the controlled activation arms is a clean mechanism→intervention→confirmation arc, echoed independently by OPT's low cancellation among the external models.
- Exceptional self-skepticism: Appendix C catalogs the conventions that flatter and the measurements that failed; every legibility number travels with a quality and dimensionality control.

## Limitations

- The sufficiency search is answer-informed (existence proof, not a circuit-discovery algorithm), and all reported sizes are upper bounds from a heuristic whose cost grows with depth — converging on 96% of predictions on some models but 44% on others.
- All Sections 2–4 results (and every edit) come from a ~124M-parameter, one-epoch OpenWebText model; the 12 external models validate reading/accounting, not editing.
- The one-hop credit is blind to components that act only through others (indirect effects), and it over-credits late layers; the closure fixes some of this but at 1–3% of the model.
- Attention is only partially handled: a channel's value side is decoded at the attended position, but the attention pattern aggregating it over the context is not followed, so every circuit is a single-position object.
- Architecture coverage excludes parallel-sublayer designs (GPT-NeoX/Pythia) and sparse MoE (where the router decides which components exist at a position but writes nothing to the residual).
- The bounded-activation arms, while more legible, refuse plain one-part installs entirely (0% until a layer-wide compensating fit is added), so the legibility–editability trade is real and the "better" models are in one sense harder to edit.
- Circuit sizes depend on the confidence floor (p≥0.3), the component unit (channel vs head), and the replacement convention — the necessity claims are robust across these but the sufficiency numbers move severalfold (8 under zeroing vs 18 under the carriage background).

## Relevance

这篇论文与可解释性研究方向高度相关：它直接回应"电路有多大"这一核心经验问题，提出带符号净值归因这一可立即采用的测量规范，并给出组件级读写（命名+安装）的完整工具链；对关注模型编辑、归因方法评估、激活函数设计（legibility-quality交换率）的研究都有直接参考价值。其"74%的层更新是固定线性搬运"的量化结论也为架构效率研究提供了新视角。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the sufficient-set search be made answer-blind — i.e., a procedure that predicts a small sufficient circuit before knowing the token — and does its size stay in single digits?
- Does the net-denominator accounting change the conclusions of SAE-feature-based circuit papers (Marks et al., Ameisen et al.) if their absolute-influence accumulation is replaced with signed nets over feature contributions?
- Does the 74–79% carriage share hold for frontier-scale models (tens of billions+, long context), and does the free-width channel that enables private-direction circuits shrink with scale or training duration?
- Why do the same-size 1B models differ so much in n90 (76 for OLMo-2 vs 208 for Gemma-3)? Is it tokenizer, data, or normalization placement?
- Can the word-specific punctuation units (the comma findings) be turned into a systematic test for tokenizer merge decisions, and do merged-vocabulary models (e.g., ByteLatent-style) eliminate those components?
- Does the GELU non-monotonicity diagnosis extend to SwiGLU/SiLU-gated models in their value branch, and would an order-preserving gate change cancellation ratios in production models as it did in the controlled arms?
- The tap (driving a self-trained unit from upstream with 86% mediation, limited only by read-row estimation accuracy) — how far does this scale as a control surface: multi-component steering of whole behaviors rather than single tokens?
