⚠ this benchmark is the authors' own validation set — see Critical Edge →
◆ Why this matters

Interface design is a first-order bottleneck for compact agents, and policy-co-evolving task synthesis is a directly reusable recipe for agent-RL and tool-use training.

◆ Critical edge

The headline 61.5% sits on the validation set used to schedule training iterations; honest held-out scores are the weaker 37.6% / 31.1% / 47.3% — and the harness switch alone explains 29 of the 53 points above the R2E-Gym floor.

01 Methodology

The policy shapes the tasks it learns from

  1. Generateissue + gold patch + hidden F2P tests, from real repo snapshots
  2. Rolloutblind runs of the current 4B policy estimate solve rate p̂
  3. Gate / Reviseadmit the p̂≈0.5 band; semantically revise the rest — don't discard
  4. DPPO updategroup-relative advantages, 256 trajectories per update
  5. New checkpoint ↻becomes the solver for the next generation phase

Leaf harness — the quiet lever

Five typed tools (read / write / edit / glob / bash) plus a natural termination rule. Moving the base model from R2E-Gym to Leaf alone lifts SWE-bench Verified 8.3% → 37.2%, while a 72B-class control stays flat at 66.5% — interface design matters specifically for small models.

RL recipe at 4B scale

DPPO with asymmetric trajectory importance sampling to correct async-rollout policy lag (no KL, no entropy bonus); success-gated log length penalty; 0.5 partial reward for truncated-but-correct trajectories. One 8×B200 node: 2 training + 6 inference GPUs; context grows 65K → 131K.

03 Strengths
  • Clean controlled experiment — synthetic-at-the-frontier vs real-filtered-by-learnability under the identical RL recipe; the +8.1-pt gap isolates the variable of interest.
  • The Leaf finding is genuinely important — a 29-point swing from interface design alone, with the 72B-class control proving it is small-model-specific.
  • Refinement, not just filtering — problem statements are revised at the semantic level (behavioral contracts vs solution-localizing hints).
  • Exceptional analysis depth — pass@k frontier, reward-hacking taxonomy with adjudication stats, failure clustering, self-compaction ablation.
  • Honest scoping — validation/test split disclosed, dedicated Limitations section, multi-seed reporting throughout.
04 Limitations
  • Python-heavy, English-only training distribution; cross-language generalization unestablished (PatchEval JS/Go is zero-shot transfer only).
  • Single harness throughout — trained and evaluated in Leaf; multi-harness training never attempted.
  • Test-passing is the sole reward and metric — a patch can pass F2P/P2P yet be incorrect or insecure.
  • Only 5 iterations shown; consolidation and verifier add-ons use SFT + stronger-model information, diluting the distillation-free story.
  • Training compute is not minimal — the 8×B200 node makes the resource claim true at inference ($0.21/task) only.
05 Open questions for further exploration
  1. Does the +8.1-pt synthetic-vs-real gap survive at larger scales (9B / 27B), where learnability filtering of real tasks is comparatively cheaper?
  2. Can generator and solver be co-trained (self-play) — does that compound, or collapse via adversarial exploitation of solver blind spots?
  3. Does multi-harness RL training (Leaf + mini-SWE-agent + OpenHands) recover the 5.2-pt cross-harness gap and yield harness-agnostic agents?
  4. Why does the verifier tournament add only ~1.4 pts over pass@1 — weak ranking signal, or is pass@3 headroom (72.3%) the real bottleneck?
  5. Does learnability-frontier targeting (p̂≈0.5) generalize beyond binary test-pass rewards, e.g. rubric-graded or long-horizon tasks?
