> **一句话总结：** 本文提出三阶段评测方法 BEFORE THE FLIP，用"先验回退分数"（PRS）度量量化 VLM 在答案未翻转时隐藏的 yes/no 分数漂移：在 LLaVA-1.5-7B 上 4-bit 量化的平均 PRS 从 8-bit 的 0.0094 升至 0.0760（9,000 个 POPE 问题中仅 265 个答案改变），并发现按问题自适应选择高精度权重组在任何存储预算下都跑不赢两个打乱对照组——诚实报告了一组"分数在变、答案未变、自适应无效"的负结果。

> **TL;DR:** On POPE with LLaVA-1.5-7B, 4-bit quantization barely moves final answers (only 265 of 9,000 flip; F1 84.72 vs 84.81 at 8-bit) yet the mean Prior Reversion Score — how far compression shifts the YES/NO score gap toward the output produced by replacing image tokens with an average token — rises from 0.0094 (W8) to 0.0760 (W4) over 8,277 filtered questions; Qwen2.5-VL-7B reproduces the ordering with a much smaller gap (0.00017 → 0.01397). Raising one of 57 weight groups to 8 bits shows earlier decoder blocks help most (depth vs loss-drop correlation ρ = −0.633), but per-question precision selection at 5/10/15% extra storage never beats both shuffled controls, and all learned selectors collapse to one profile. The paper is a carefully controlled negative result about adaptive mixed-precision for VLMs, plus a new diagnostic metric for hidden score shifts.

## 核心贡献

- **提出先验回退分数（Prior Reversion Score, PRS）**：在答案未改变的情况下，量化压缩引起的 yes/no 分数变化是否会向"图像 token 被替换为平均 token 后的输出"方向漂移。PRS 为正表示压缩把分数推向图像替换输出——即"答案翻转之前"的隐藏损伤。
- **绘制了 57 个权重组的精度敏感度图谱**：将模型分为 24 个视觉编码器块、1 个 connector、32 个解码器块，逐一将某组从 4-bit 提升到 8-bit 并测量损失下降，发现解码器越浅的块提升收益越大（ρ = −0.633），而视觉编码器块无深度趋势。
- **对"按问题选择精度"做了严格对照检验**：设计了两个打乱对照组（组内乱序问题、问题内乱序组），证明在 5%、10%、15% 三档额外存储预算下，事后按问题选择权重组的收益都无法同时超过两个对照——自适应混合精度在 VQA 上没有可靠收益。
- **跨模型、跨任务、跨设置的全面评测**：两个 VLM（LLaVA-1.5-7B、Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct）、五种图像替换方式、CHAIR 幻觉评测、MME/MMBench/ARC/HellaSwag 等额外任务、固定与学习式精度方案、以及视觉对比解码（VCD）。

## Key Contributions

- **A metric for hidden score shifts (PRS).** For each binary POPE question, the score vector is mean-centered so only the YES/NO gap remains; the compression-induced change q and the image-token-replacement-induced change d are compared via PRS = ⟨q, d⟩ / (∥d∥² + ε). A positive PRS means compression moves the answer scores toward the average-token output even when the final answer is unchanged.
- **A precision-sensitivity map over 57 weight groups.** Starting from an all-4-bit model, each group (24 image-encoder blocks, 1 connector, 32 decoder blocks; 13 decoder blocks further split into attention/FFN) is raised to 8 bits one at a time, and the reduction in a faithfulness loss (positive-part cross-entropy increase + positive-part PRS, scale-normalized) is recorded.
- **A controlled falsification test of per-question precision selection.** Hindsight per-question group selection is compared against two shuffled controls (Shuffle A permutes questions within each group; Shuffle B permutes groups within each question) at 5%, 10%, and 15% extra storage budgets, with all selected combinations actually run through the model.
- **Broad robustness checks.** Two VLMs, five image-replacement variants (dataset-average token, same-image average, zero tokens, another image's tokens, uniform gray image), open-ended captioning with CHAIR, four additional benchmarks, fixed and learned precision plans, and visual contrastive decoding.

## Methodology

The method has three stages. **Stage 1** runs each POPE question three ways: (i) 16-bit weights + real image (reference), (ii) 16-bit weights + all image tokens replaced by one fixed average token computed on a separate calibration set (LLaVA keeps all 576 token positions; Qwen uses fixed 448×448 with 256 tokens), and (iii) quantized weights + real image. Score vectors are centered by subtracting their mean so only the YES/NO gap matters. The replacement change d and compression change q are both measured against the reference; PRS is their normalized inner product. Questions with negligible replacement response (∥d∥ ≤ τ_d = 0.302, the tenth percentile on separate examples) are filtered out, leaving 8,277 of 9,000 questions. Supporting measures include PRS⁺ = max(PRS, 0), the response ratio (how strongly the compressed model still responds to replacement), and the flip-match rate. **Stage 2** raises exactly one of 57 weight groups from 4 to 8 bits and records the drop in a two-term loss combining the positive part of the cross-entropy increase and the positive part of PRS, scale-normalized. **Stage 3** asks whether choosing different high-precision group sets per question beats (a) one fixed plan chosen on average loss and (b) two shuffled controls, at storage budgets of 5%, 10%, and 15% above uniform W4; every selected combination is evaluated through the model because group effects can interact. Statistics use 10,000 image-level bootstrap resamples and McNemar's test for paired accuracies. Learned selectors (linear regression, a small neural network, and a fixed score-distance rule) predict per-profile loss with a held-out error margin to pick among four precision profiles under a W8-calibrated loss limit.

## Results & Highlights

- **Score shift grows with compression, answers barely move.** On 8,277 filtered LLaVA POPE questions, mean PRS rises from 0.0094 at W8 to 0.0760 at W4 (8.1×), and the fraction of positive-PRS questions from 63.7% to 72.7% — yet only 265 of 9,000 answers flip at W4, and F1/accuracy are nearly identical (W8 F1 84.811 / acc 86.067; W4 F1 84.722 / acc 85.922). The median response ratio of 1.058 shows the higher PRS is not an artifact of a weaker response to image replacement.
- **Flipped answers do not specifically match the replacement answer.** Of the 265 W4 flips, 42.6% match the average-token answer vs 42.7% under the shuffled control (p = .722) — the score-level PRS effect does not (yet) surface as answer-level alignment with the replacement output.
- **Qwen reproduces the bit ordering with a much smaller gap.** On fixed-resolution Qwen2.5-VL-7B, mean PRS goes from 0.00017 (W8) to 0.01397 (W4); F1 drops only from 88.938 to 88.624, flip-match rate 0.5766 vs 0.5372 shuffled (p = .198).
- **Earlier decoder blocks benefit most from extra bits.** Across 32 decoder blocks, depth vs mean loss-drop correlates at ρ = −0.633 (p = 0.0001); the first half of the decoder averages +0.00994 loss reduction per group vs −0.00098 for the second half, while the 24 image-encoder blocks show no depth trend (ρ = −0.133, p = .535). Thirteen decoder blocks actually increase loss when raised to W8, and effects change under splitting (decoder block 17: −0.0045 as a whole block vs +0.0042 for attention alone).
- **Per-question selection fails against shuffled controls at every budget.** Observed hindsight gains are 1.0966 / 1.3535 / 1.5458 at 5% / 10% / 15% extra storage; at 10% the 95% CI is [1.2142, 1.5066] while the two shuffled controls give 1.6616 and 1.2309 — the real gain sits between them. Split-half group-ranking correlation is only r = .307; group identity explains 0.15% of variance and question identity 6.33%, leaving 93.52% to question×group interaction plus noise.
- **Fixed and learned plans.** The fixed 10% plan cuts PRS⁺ by 0.0650 vs uniform W4 (95% CI [−0.0868, −0.0424]) at 3.48 GiB vs 3.17 GiB, but vs keeping the image encoder at W8 the PRS⁺ difference is +0.0085 (CI [−0.0079, +0.0264], includes zero); vs keeping the first 8 decoder blocks at W8 it reduces PRS⁺ by 0.0247 (CI [−0.0476, −0.0008]). All three learned selectors collapse to the same profile K3_P2 plus VCD for every question (K3_P2 loss 0.4075 vs 0.3557 for the best fixed profile, difference 0.0518, CI [−0.0065, 0.1129]); prediction MAE of 0.429–0.642 is comparable to the entire W4→W8 loss range.
- **Aggregate tasks barely degrade, and minimizing PRS ≠ maximizing F1.** From 16-bit to W4, POPE F1 changes +0.0358 and accuracy −0.0333 points; CHAIRs rises 24.0% → 24.6% (CI [−2.80, +4.00], p = .773); MME/MMBench/text controls move < 1 point (ARC Easy −3.20 points). Under VCD, F1 peaks at 85.74 at strength α = 0.25 (vs 84.72 without VCD), but pushing mean PRS to −0.688 at α = 1.0 drops F1 to 84.41 and accuracy by 2.73 points — PRS and task accuracy prefer different settings.

## Authors & Credibility

Five authors from the University of Maryland, Baltimore County (Sourajit Saha, Shubhashis Roy Dipta, Shaswati Saha, Nobin Sarwar, Yuxuan Jiang), with a public project page (sourajitcs.github.io/beforetheflip). The paper is explicitly marked "Preprint" — not yet peer-reviewed. Credibility signals are strong for a preprint: pre-registered style controls (two shuffled baselines, disjoint calibration/evaluation image subsets), bootstrap confidence intervals and McNemar's tests throughout, and unusually candid framing of its own negative results (per-question selection, selector collapse, PRS dependence on the replacement choice). The statistics are sometimes more honest than the headline: the main W4-vs-W8 PRS comparison is explicitly called "a descriptive pattern" because mean PRS lacks image-level confidence intervals.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The core idea — measuring latent score shifts (not accuracy) caused by quantization, referenced against an image-token-replacement intervention — is genuinely novel for quantized VLMs, and connecting compression to the visual-grounding/prior literature (POPE, VQA-CP, VCD) is a fresh angle. PRS itself is a simple inner-product metric, so the novelty is more in the evaluation design than the math.
- **Rigor:** High in experimental hygiene (disjoint splits, shuffled controls, CI reporting, running all selected group sets through the model). But the headline PRS ordering (0.0094 → 0.0760) has no image-level confidence interval and is only a "descriptive pattern," and the flip-match analysis (42.6% vs 42.7% shuffled, p = .722) shows the effect does not reach the answer level — the practical meaning of a score-only shift is left open.
- **Reproducibility:** Good. Standard models (LLaVA-1.5-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct), public datasets (POPE-COCO, CHAIR, MME, MMBench, ARC, HellaSwag, COCO train2014), 4/8-bit post-training quantization, and a project page. One soft spot: the τ_d = 0.302 filter threshold and the scale constants s_CE/s_PRS are calibration-set artifacts that others would need to re-derive.
- **Significance:** The negative result is the real contribution — it directly undercuts the premise of query-adaptive mixed-precision VLM quantization (cf. QAQ/RouteLLM-style routing) with a clean control design. The positive diagnostic (PRS) is interesting but its utility is unresolved: the VCD experiment shows the PRS-minimizing setting (α = 1.0) is worse in F1 than α = 0.25, i.e., the metric does not yet optimize anything users care about.
- **Red flags:** (1) PRS is defined only for binary yes/no answers and a replacement that favors NO (dataset-average tokens answer YES for only 0.6% of questions; zero tokens answer YES for everything) — the "prior reversion" framing is heavily replacement-dependent, and a gray image gives PRS ≈ 0. (2) Only two 7B-class models; Qwen is forced to a non-native fixed 448×448 resolution with 256 tokens, which weakens the generality claim. (3) The qualitative Figure 5 examples (66 questions, 0.73% of POPE) are admitted to be selected after seeing all results. (4) ARC Easy drops 3.20 points at W4 yet the text still concludes "no consistent overall degradation." (5) No latency, memory, or energy numbers, so the storage-budget framing (3.17 vs 3.48 vs 3.79 GiB) ignores runtime cost of mixed precision.

## Strengths

- Identifies and cleanly formalizes a real blind spot: accuracy-only VQA evaluation cannot see compression-induced score shifts behind unchanged answers.
- The two-shuffle control design for hindsight selection bias is a methodological template other mixed-precision papers should adopt; it converts a plausible-sounding idea (per-question precision) into a falsifiable and falsified claim.
- Exceptional reporting honesty: negative results, collapsed selectors, CI-includes-zero comparisons, and post-hoc example selection are all disclosed in the main text rather than hidden.
- Broad robustness sweep (5 replacement variants, 6+ tasks, 2 models, learned vs fixed plans, VCD) makes the conclusions hard to dismiss as a single-setup artifact.

## Limitations

- PRS applies only to binary YES/NO questions (POPE); no formulation for open-ended VQA, multi-choice ranking, or generation beyond the CHAIR side-evaluation.
- The replacement intervention favors NO for the default average token, so "reversion" direction is partly baked into the metric; the gray-image control yielding near-zero PRS underscores how replacement-dependent the measurement is.
- Mean PRS lacks image-level confidence intervals, so the central W4 > W8 claim is descriptive rather than tested.
- Only two 7B VLMs tested (Qwen at non-native fixed resolution); no larger models, no modern 3/4-bit weight-only formats (e.g., GPTQ/AWQ implementations are cited but the quantization scheme details are thin), and no latency/energy measurements.
- The paper offers no working remedy: fixed plans do not beat the simple "keep the image encoder at 8 bits" rule with statistical significance, and learned selectors collapse to a single profile plus universal VCD.

## Relevance

（用户关注模型压缩与多模态系统的可靠性）本文正好处于 VLM 量化与视觉忠实性评测的交叉点：它说明 4-bit 压缩会在答案不变的情况下悄悄改变 yes/no 分数边界，并为混合精度分组提供了一张"哪些块值得加 bits"的实证地图——对设计量化 VLM 部署方案和理解压缩模型幻觉风险都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does PRS-style hidden score shift predict eventual accuracy collapse at more aggressive precisions (W3, W2, or NF4-style formats), where answer flips become common?
- Can the analysis be extended to open-ended generation — e.g., measuring compression-induced drift of caption log-probabilities relative to image-ablated outputs — rather than binary YES/NO scores?
- Why do earlier decoder blocks benefit most from extra bits: is it outlier-heavy attention projections, KV-cache sensitivity, or the vision-language fusion point being concentrated there?
- Would query-adaptive precision survive with a cheaper and better selector (e.g., features from the first decode step), given that prediction MAE (0.429–0.642) currently swamps profile differences?
- Since minimizing mean PRS under VCD hurts F1, is there a composite objective (PRS⁺ + task-calibrated CE) that tracks deployment-relevant faithfulness without over-correction?
- Do these findings transfer to instruction-tuned 70B-class VLMs and native-resolution Qwen, where token distributions and outlier structure differ substantially?
