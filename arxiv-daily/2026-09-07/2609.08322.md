> **一句话总结：** 该工作在 Llama-3.1-8B、Qwen3-8B、Gemma-2-9B 三个多语言模型上，用线性探针、归因修补（attribution patching）、稀疏自编码器（SAE）与特征消融四种手段追踪刻板印象信息，发现"可解码峰值"比"因果影响峰值"早出现 36–53% 的网络深度，且 SAE 偏见特征几乎全部是"社会类别特定"而非"语言特定"（仅 6–18% 语言无关、0% 类别无关），说明定位表征并不等于干预有效。

> **TL;DR:** A mechanistic-interpretability study of stereotype bias in Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen3-8B and Gemma-2-9B using MBBQ (EN/ES/NL/TR, 6 social categories) for discovery and held-out SHADES for intervention evaluation. Headline dissociation: linear-probe decodability peaks in mid-layers while attribution-patching influence peaks at the final layer, separated by 36–53% of model depth (53% Llama, 48% Gemma, 36% Qwen). Among 535 ablated Llama-Scope residual-stream features, only 18% have language-agnostic effects and none are category-agnostic; language-agnostic features reduce SHADES bias by 46–79% per language vs 32–55% for language-dependent ones — but this advantage fails to replicate in three other SAE suites (Llama-Multi, Gemma-Scope, Qwen-Multi), and mean ablation effects there are near zero (−0.0008 to −0.016 vs −0.055 for Llama-Scope). The core methodological takeaway: decodability, output influence, and feature-level intervention effects must be measured separately; localization alone does not justify an intervention.

## 核心贡献

- **系统区分了三个常被混淆的问题**：刻板印象信息在哪里"可解码"（线性探针）、在哪里"影响输出"（归因修补）、以及对其特征做干预会产生什么效果（SAE 特征消融）——并证明三者的答案并不重合。
- **发现解码—因果分离现象**：三个模型中探针性能都在中层达峰（Llama L15、Qwen L23、Gemma L21），而归因值都在最后一层达峰，两者相距 36–53% 的模型深度。
- **给出 SAE 偏见特征的可解释性证据**：Llama-Scope 中 139 个有人工标注释义的特征里 123 个（88.5%）与其被选中的社会类别一致，并形成可复现的语义家族（如残障分为神经发育/精神/慢性病，性别分为代词/群体名词/亲属角色）。
- **揭示"类别是绑定约束、语言不是"**：四个 SAE 套件中 6–18% 的残差流特征是语言无关的，但没有任何特征是类别无关的——即稳定的消融效应在语言间比在社会类别间更常见，但仍是少数。
- **方法学警示**：Llama-Scope 中语言无关特征消融可减少 46–79% 的 SHADES 偏见，但该优势在另外三个 SAE 套件中不复存在（甚至在 Gemma-Scope 的英/西语上反转），说明单一 SAE 套件的干预结论不可外推。

## Key Contributions

- **Separates three conflated questions** about stereotype bias — where the information is linearly decodable (probing), where it influences the output (attribution patching), and what ablating its SAE features actually does — and shows the three answers diverge systematically.
- **Documents a decodability–causality dissociation across depth**: probe macro-F1 peaks at mid-layers while attribution peaks at the final layer in all three model families, with a 36–53%-of-depth separation between the two peaks.
- **Provides interpretability evidence for selected SAE bias features**: of 139 glossed Llama-Scope features, 123 (88.5%) match the social category they were selected on and cluster into recurring semantic families (e.g., disability splits into neurodevelopmental / psychiatric / chronic-illness; gender into pronouns / group nouns / kinship roles).
- **Establishes an asymmetry between language and category transfer**: under a Language/Category Specificity Score criterion (threshold 0.5), 6–18% of evaluated residual-stream features are language-agnostic but zero are category-agnostic — social category, not language, is the binding constraint on bias features.
- **Delivers a replication-minded negative result**: the larger ablation effects of language-agnostic features in Llama-Scope (46–79% bias reduction on SHADES) do not replicate across Llama-Multi, Qwen-Multi, or Gemma-Scope, cautioning against generalizing single-suite SAE interventions.

## Methodology

The paper traces stereotype-related information through a three-stage pipeline on MBBQ (parallel bias benchmark in English, Spanish, Dutch, Turkish; six social categories: age, disability, gender identity, physical appearance, SES, sexual orientation), with interventions always evaluated on the held-out SHADES benchmark (EN/ES/NL — SHADES has no Turkish), cleanly separating feature discovery from downstream evaluation.

1. **Linear probing** — contrastive pairs are built from MBBQ disambiguated items by appending either the correct or the stereotype-consistent answer; L2-regularized logistic-regression probes (scikit-learn, C ∈ {0.1, 1.0}) are fit on mean-pooled layer-wise hidden states (80/20 split, macro-F1). Controls follow Hewitt & Liang (2019): permuted-label control probes, weight-norm inspection, regularization sensitivity, plus an English-centric OLMo-7B reference model.
2. **Attribution patching** — for each item a clean run (disambiguated, correct answer) and corrupted run (ambiguous or pro-stereotypical) are cached with TransformerLens; layer attribution is the gradient–activation inner product Attr(ℓ) ≈ ∇_m a^corr · (a^clean − a^corr), where m is the logit margin between the correct and strongest competing option. Two contrasts: *evidence injection* (disambiguated vs ambiguous) and *stereotype routing* (counter- vs pro-stereotypical targets), aggregated per (layer, category, language) via trapezoidal AUC.
3. **SAE feature analysis** — activations are decomposed with four pre-trained suites: Llama-Scope (English, L0=50), Llama-Multi (multilingual, L0≈10,827), Gemma-Scope (English, L0≈158), Qwen-Multi (multilingual, L0≈26,893). Features are selected contrastively (top k=3 per category/layer/stream by activation separation between stereotype-consistent and control completions, holding context and entities fixed), yielding 738–2,445 retained features per suite. Interpretation uses lexical anchoring (max cosine similarity to unembedding rows against a 99th-percentile random-direction null, e.g., 0.078 for both Llama suites) and Neuronpedia glosses.
4. **Feature ablation** — individual features are zero-masked in the SAE encoded basis (decode→mask→encode back) during inference on SHADES; Δbias(f) = bias_interv − bias_baseline, with one-sample t-tests, bootstrap CIs, and Cohen's d. Transfer is quantified by Language/Category Specificity Scores (σ/|μ| of Δbias across languages/categories; <0.5 = agnostic).

## Results & Highlights

- **Peak decodability precedes peak causal influence by 36–53% of model depth in all three models** (53% for Llama-3.1-8B with probe peak at L15 vs attribution peak at the final layer; 48% for Gemma-2-9B; 36% for Qwen3-8B with probe peak at L23). Peak probe macro-F1 averaged over the 24 category×language cells is 0.845 (Llama), 0.894 (Qwen), 0.899 (Gemma); permuted-label control probes stay at chance (mean macro-F1 0.494–0.498).
- **Attribution builds up late**: in Llama, mean absolute attribution at the final layer is 0.394 vs 0.237 averaged across all layers; maximal attribution occurs at the final layer in all three models, with onset varying (layer 11 in Llama, layer 17 in Qwen/Gemma). The English-centric OLMo-7B control shows a weaker profile (peak macro-F1 0.606, English 0.757 vs 0.556 averaged over ES/NL/TR).
- **Selected SAE features are category-linked and interpretable**: 123 of 139 glossed Llama-Scope features (88.5%) are on-concept; e.g., disability-status features split into neurodevelopmental (3), psychiatric (8), mental-health (14), and chronic-illness (11) families spanning distinct layer ranges.
- **Lexical anchoring depends strongly on the SAE suite**: 46.9% of 1,741 retained Llama-Scope features exceed the 0.078 chance-level anchor cosine vs only 8.8% of 1,841 Llama-Multi features (57.9% vs 11.1% in the residual stream); Gemma-Scope 41.7% of 2,445 vs Qwen-Multi 0.8% of 738. In Llama-Scope the anchored fraction rises from 4.8% at layer 11 to 74.7% at layer 31.
- **Ablation effects are heterogeneous and bidirectional**: Llama-Scope shows the largest mean effect (Δbias = −0.055, Cohen's d = −0.071, best single feature −1.005, 9.6% of feature–bias-type pairs with nominally significant reductions, ~18–20% medium-or-large |d|), while Gemma-Scope averages −0.016, Qwen-Multi −0.0008, and Llama-Multi +0.0002 (i.e., no average reduction). For disability status, ablation shifts bias *toward* the stereotype in Llama-Scope (Δbias = +0.280) but away from it in Gemma-Scope (−0.090).
- **Transfer asymmetry**: 18.3% of 535 ablated Llama-Scope residual-stream features are language-agnostic (LSS < 0.5) but 100% are category-dependent (CSS ≥ 0.5); across all four suites the language-agnostic share is 6–18% with zero category-agnostic features. In Llama-Scope, language-agnostic features reduce SHADES bias by 46–79% per evaluation language vs 32–55% for language-dependent features — an advantage that is absent in Llama-Multi and Qwen-Multi and reverses for English/Spanish in Gemma-Scope.
- **Neither depth nor lexical anchor predicts the signed ablation effect** (R² < 0.0016 for depth; anchor cosine nominally significant but negligible), so feature effects must be measured, not inferred.

## Authors & Credibility

The team spans Saarland University (Saarland Informatics Campus), Queen's University Belfast, DFKI, and the University of Technology Nuremberg — established multilingual-NLP and interpretability groups (van Genabith's group has a long track record in multilingual NLP; Dutta Chowdhury works on multilingual mechanistic interpretability). Two authors are equal-contribution first authors, and the same team previously published TenseLoC (MRL 2025), a probing + causal tracing + SAE-steering study of multilingual tense, so the methodology is a deliberate extension of a validated pipeline. The second SAE suite (Llama-Multi) builds on the authors' own TrustNLP 2026 multilingual-SAE paper, and Yusser Al Ghussin provides the Qwen-Multi SAEs from the MULTI21 effort. Code and data are released at https://github.com/ariunerdenetum/stereotype-tracing-mllm. The paper cites the relevant prior behavioral work (MBBQ, SeeGULL Multilingual, SHADES) and the mechanistic-interpretability literature (Wendler et al.'s concept space, Dumas et al.'s language-agnostic patching, Deng et al.'s language-specific SAE features) precisely, and positions its contribution as the missing decodability-vs-intervention comparison.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The main conceptual contribution — showing that probe-decodability, attribution-based influence, and SAE-feature ablation effects locate *different* parts of stereotype behavior — is genuinely useful and, to my knowledge, the cleanest demonstration of this dissociation in a multilingual bias setting. The dissociation itself echoes known results (probing ≠ causality; Wendler/Dumas language-agnostic spaces), so the novelty is in the systematic triangulation rather than any single finding.
- **Rigor**: Strong on controls — permuted-label probes at chance, held-out evaluation benchmark (MBBQ for discovery, SHADES for ablation), three model families, an English-centric control model, and effect sizes with bootstrap CIs rather than bare significance. However, attribution patching is a *first-order linearization* of activation patching, so the "causal influence" curves are approximations; the paper is careful about this but readers should not treat the attribution peak as ground-truth causality.
- **Reproducibility**: Good — public models, public SAE suites, public benchmarks, released code, and precise hyperparameters (k=3 features per cell, LSS/CSS threshold 0.5, C ∈ {0.1, 1.0}). The cross-suite replication attempts are exactly the kind of robustness check most SAE papers skip.
- **Key weakness — confounded SAE comparison**: The four SAE suites differ in training language, sparsity (L0 = 50 vs ≈10,827 vs ≈158 vs ≈26,893), dictionary width, and training tokens simultaneously. The headline "language-agnostic features have larger effects" is a *single-suite finding* (Llama-Scope, an English-trained SAE with near-minimal L0) that failed to replicate in the other three suites — the authors honestly flag this, but it means the paper's most quotable positive number (46–79% bias reduction) rests on one configuration. The suite differences plausibly reflect SAE quality more than anything about stereotypes.
- **Small average effects**: The mean ablation effect of the best suite is −0.055 on a bias score whose baselines range 0.062–0.346, and effects are bidirectional (ablation *increases* disability bias by +0.280 in Llama-Scope). As a mitigation technique, single-feature ablation is therefore not practical — the paper frames itself as diagnostic, which is fair, but the abstract-level reader may over-interpret "bias reduction."
- **Heuristic thresholds**: The LSS/CSS < 0.5 criterion for "agnostic" can be trivially satisfied by features with near-zero effects (high variance-to-mean from noise); the authors note they interpret scores together with Δbias, but no sensitivity analysis over the 0.5 threshold is reported.
- **Red flags**: None serious. No evidence of benchmark gaming; negative results are reported prominently. The 18-page paper keeps claims scoped to what was measured.

## Strengths

- Clean separation of feature discovery (MBBQ) from intervention evaluation (held-out SHADES) — avoids the circularity of selecting and testing features on the same data.
- Genuine multi-method triangulation (probing, attribution patching, SAE interpretation, ablation) across three independently developed model families, not a single-model case study.
- Honest, prominent reporting of non-replication: the Llama-Scope language-agnostic advantage is explicitly shown to fail in three other SAE suites rather than being buried.
- Interpretable intermediate artifact — the taxonomy of semantic families per social category (Table 3) with representative lexical anchors gives practitioners concrete handles for auditing bias features.
- Methodologically instructive conclusion for the interpretability community: localization is a starting point for intervention analysis, not evidence of intervention success.

## Limitations

- Only four high-resource languages from three families (EN/ES/NL/TR), bounded by MBBQ; SHADES lacks Turkish, so ablation is evaluated in only three of the four languages. Nothing is known for typologically diverse or lower-resource languages.
- Only 8B–9B parameter models; depth-separation percentages and feature specificity may change with scale.
- SAE suites are confounded (sparsity, dictionary size, training data, language coverage), so cross-suite differences cannot be attributed to multilingual vs English training — the strongest ablation effects come from one suite.
- Feature ablation ignores redundancy in bias pathways: removing one feature may be compensated by others, and only individual (not joint or intersectional, e.g., age × gender) features are tested.
- Attribution patching remains a first-order approximation; the LSS/CSS = 0.5 threshold and ϵ = 1e-8 are heuristic choices without sensitivity analysis.

## Relevance

对研究"多语言模型内部机制与公平性交叉"的方向高度相关：它把语言无关表征（Wendler/Dumas 一脉）与偏见审计（MBBQ/SHADES 一脉）连接起来，并给出可操作的方法论结论——探针/归因/消融三类证据必须分别测量，这对任何想做多语言偏见定位与干预（如 steering、SAE 特征编辑）的工作都是直接的前置参考。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the 36–53% decodability–influence separation shrink, persist, or invert at 70B scale, or in models with deliberate multilingual alignment (e.g., Qwen3's tokenizer/training mix)?
- If SAE quality (not training language) drives the Llama-Scope advantage, do matched-sparsity English vs multilingual SAEs trained on the same base model produce the same language-agnostic minority?
- Can the 6–18% language-agnostic features be grown into a practical multilingual mitigation — e.g., jointly ablating or steering the whole semantic family per category — without the bidirectional backfire seen for disability in Llama-Scope (+0.280)?
- Does the category-specificity finding (0% category-agnostic features) survive intersectional categories like age × gender or disability × SES, where MBBQ/SHADES currently provide no coverage?
- How do attribution-patching conclusions compare against full activation patching or causal scrubbing at the same layer resolution, given the linearization assumption behind Attr(ℓ)?
- Do the late-layer "stereotype routing" attributions (consistently negative, shifting predictions away from stereotype-consistent answers) correspond to a reusable self-correction circuit that could be amplified for bias mitigation?
