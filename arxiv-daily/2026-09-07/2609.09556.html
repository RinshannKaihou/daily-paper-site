Deep dive

Methodology, distilled

Superposition as noisy compressed sensing — with the weakest possible decoder

A k-sparse signal x is encoded as y = Ax + z, dictionary columns drawn i.i.d. uniform from the unit sphere, noise subgaussian. Decoding is deliberately the single-step matched filter x̂ = Aᵀy — one linear read, no iteration.

y = Ax + z x̂ = Aᵀy Ŝ = { i : |x̂ᵢ| > τ }

The proof splits each coordinate's error exactly into cross-feature interference plus projected noise, divides the tolerance ε between them via an α/(1−α) budget, and bounds each side separately: an exponential-moment argument (Wick's theorem on the sphere) envelopes the interference; Hoeffding handles the noise. A union bound yields Theorem 1, and support recovery reduces to the existence of a separating threshold (Corollary 1) — a checkable criterion.

IHT-SAE operationalizes the theory: a TopK SAE augmented with LISTA-style learned iterative hard-thresholding, decoupling iteration count from the sparsity budget k (which MP-SAE must know in advance).

  • Tightness (Fig. 3): across k ∈ [2,24], m ∈ {512…4096}, d ∈ {64…512}, ε ∈ {0.1…0.9} and 40,000 trials per config (Gaussian / Rademacher / Laplace dictionaries), the Gaussian-tail bound tracks the empirical failure rate with ratio → 1 as success approaches 100%.

  • SAE recovery (Fig. 7): at m = 1024, d = 128, single-step TopK F1 collapses as sparsity k → 50, while IHT-SAE and MP-SAE (n up to 32 iterations) sustain substantially higher F1 at noise-to-signal ratios 0 and 0.1.

Strengths

what the paper gets right
  • Makes “how many dimensions suffice?” precise — linear accessibility as noisy compressed sensing with matched-filter decoding, an answerable question.
  • Interpretable, testable structure: active/inactive interference asymmetry, α-split budget borrowing, checkable separation-threshold condition — not bare asymptotics.
  • Self-aware numerics: the Gaussian-tail approximation is floored below the proven Hoeffding envelope; Monte Carlo restricted to statistically reliable failure regimes (Poisson CV ≤ 10%).
  • IHT-SAE grows directly out of the theory's diagnosis of why single-step SAEs fail; the limitations section is unusually honest.

Limitations

where the guarantees stop
  • Assumes i.i.d. uniform-sphere, independent columns — real learned dictionaries are correlated and structured; the paper's own “Christmas / December” motivation heuristically violates this.
  • Fixed-support guarantee ≠ certifying one deployed decoder over all supports; the union-bound cost of returning to that setting is not worked out.
  • Entirely synthetic validation (m ≤ 4096, d ≤ 512 — tiny next to million-feature SAE dictionaries); zero experiments on actual LLM activations.
  • IHT-SAE benchmarked only against TopK / MP-SAE on the authors' own generator: figure-level F1, no compute-matched comparison, no ablation of the budget split α.
  • ~2× per-iteration FLOPs vs MP-SAE; practical guidance on which SAE suits a given LLM is thin.
Questions for further exploration
  • What does upgrading to a uniform all-supports guarantee cost — and can structured (correlated) dictionaries close part of the gap to k²?
  • Do the bounds transfer to learned dictionaries with non-trivial mutual coherence — can the variance proxy sᵢ/d become an empirical coherence functional?
  • Does the predicted linear-accessibility ceiling show up on real LLM activations — e.g. TopK SAE F1 collapse in GemmaScope / LLaMA residual streams?
  • Can IHT-SAE's 2× per-iteration FLOP overhead be amortized (shared update matrices, fewer effective iterations) to win compute-matched?
  • Does the interference/noise budget-borrowing prediction quantitatively match observed SAE robustness under activation noise or adversarial interference?
