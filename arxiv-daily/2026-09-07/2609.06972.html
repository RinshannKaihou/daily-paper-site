Why this matters

The first fully step-labeled training corpus for runtime trajectory monitors — the data foundation for guardrails in trustworthy long-horizon autonomous agents.

为“长时程自主智能体的可靠性与可信性”研究提供运行时轨迹监控器的首个直接数据基础。

Critical edge

A no-model world-identity lookup scores 86.1% on the default split (98.2% in medical) — results without the anonymization or world-held-out protocols don’t count as injection detection.

Methodology 方法

All 12,536 trajectories come from one generator — quantized Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct (temperature 0.8), one API call per fully labeled trace — grounded in 50 persona worlds and 250 tasks across email, banking, web, coding, and medical domains, over a closed vocabulary of 103 tools.

Labels are protocol-specified in the prompt under a regular grammar (full hijack B⁺IH⁺, partial B⁺IH{1,2}B⁺, delayed B…H…B); a structural validator rejects out-of-vocabulary tools and grammar violations, accepting 78.8% of 15,900 candidates.

QA runs five layers deep and is unusually candid: an LLM screen — deliberately not used for filtering — misjudged 1,494 of 1,500 legitimate hard negatives as malicious, itself a methodological finding; a 1,200-trajectory manual audit found 99.6% step-label correctness.

Results 结果

A deliberately weak baseline — six surface features (step count, external-recipient cues, URL indicator, sink-verb tool, 26-term keyword count) into class-balanced logistic regression — establishes the premise: 44.6% of attacks are invisible to surface features.

  • Attempt-vs-success failure. Failed attacks flagged at 0.187 — ≈2× the benign rate — because a resisted injection still carries the poison’s keywords: exactly the confusion the class was built to expose.
  • Cues are anti-informative. 32.7% of attacked trajectories contain no external recipient or URL; 97.3% of hard negatives carry a suspicious keyword vs 23.9% of benign.
  • Damage topology. Poison enters via passive reads (read_webpage 598, read_email 541); damage flows through active tools (execute_command 1,413 · forward_email 969 · fill_form 912). 12,349 hijacked steps, 2.23 per attack; 99.7% of injected observations lexically unique.

Strengths 优势

  • Fills a precise gap: first released static corpus labeling every step — injection entry point, corrupted span, plus a distinct resisted class.
  • Contrast classes target specific shortcuts and provably work: keyword cues fire on 97.3% of hard negatives; the baseline confuses failed attacks at 2× the benign rate.
  • Exceptionally honest reporting — the LLM-judge failure, banking-cell anomaly, family collapse, and identity leakage are measured and published with mitigation protocols.
  • Fully specified baseline (six features, one logistic regression) with per-pattern breakdowns cleanly establishes the benchmark’s premise.
  • Clean release engineering: per-trajectory JSON, datasheet, Croissant metadata, CC BY 4.0 — generation prompts withheld so the corpus can’t double as an attack generator.

Limitations 局限

  • Single-generator, fully synthetic — transfer to real agent traffic untested; detectors may learn Llama-3.3 stylistic fingerprints.
  • Memorizable templates: 28.9% of injections name ext-audit.com; 75.3% of hard negatives mention $5,000 — masking is a workaround, not a fix.
  • World-identity leakage (86.1%, up to 98.2% medical) invalidates naive evaluation on the default split.
  • Injections almost always land at steps 2–3 under fixed positional templates; 3–11-step horizons exclude long-horizon, multi-agent, memory-mediated attacks.
  • Labels are protocol-specified, not exhaustive human gold; family tags unusable as a classification target.
  • Only a strawman baseline — no LLM judge or fine-tuned sequence detector evaluated; difficulty for real monitors is asserted, not demonstrated.

Questions for further exploration 延伸问题

Q1Would a fine-tuned encoder or LLM-as-monitor beat 55.4% recall — or do partial hijacks (8.2%) stay hard even for strong models?
Q2Under world-held-out / anonymized protocols, how much reported “detection” is identity memorization in disguise?
Q3Does a monitor trained on AgentDrift transfer to real agent traces, or does single-generator fingerprinting dominate?
Q4Injections enter through passive reads and damage flows through active writes — would carrier/sink-aware attention along the read→write chain improve localization?
Q5Does the attack-goal-family collapse reflect protocol-conditioned generation, or do frontier agents genuinely converge on exfiltration regardless of intent?
Q6Could the resistance rationales in the 1,500 failed-attack trajectories be distilled into a defense-side training signal?
