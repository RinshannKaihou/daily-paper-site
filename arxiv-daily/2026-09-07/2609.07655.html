Methodology · 双循环机制

Inner loop — every episode t
Agent proposes designs
(ReAct LLM / Chemeleon)→ Append-only
proposal archive At→ Cheap surrogate feedback
TabPFN-3 / TabICLv2
↓ every Q episodes — acquisition α(x) picks from the ARCHIVE, not the current proposal
Outer loop — repair, Q=32 total
Acquisition rule scores archive
(EI over verified incumbent)→ Oracle labels
the chosen design→ Label appended in-context
zero retraining

OSR occupies a third feedback regime between a frozen surrogate (Q=0, whose errors the optimizer keeps exploiting) and full per-round feedback (Q=T, reliable but expensive). The agent searches entirely on cheap surrogate scores; a separate acquisition rule spends a sparse budget of Q high-fidelity oracle queries on designs drawn from the agent's own accumulating archive, appending each label as a context row. Because the oracle evaluates archived — not necessarily current — proposals, each label repairs feedback for all subsequent episodes.

Six acquisition rules are compared (Random, Global-UQ, Peak, Q90-UCB, G2P, EI); expected improvement over the best measured incumbent ("Online EI") wins. Evaluated on 20 frozen seeded SCM worlds (T=200) and on MADE (30 chemical systems ×3, T=300, MACE surrogate vs Orb-v3 oracle; discovery = structure within 0.1 eV/atom of the convex hull).

核心贡献（中文）：提出 OSR 第三种反馈机制；证明「全局预测误差」与「最大遗憾」是错位的修复目标（直接挑战 TabArena 式评测惯例）；给出完整双循环算法并确定 Online EI 为最强实例；在合成环境与 MADE 上验证省查询效果稳健。

Results · 具体数字

On 20 synthetic SCM worlds, Online EI at Q=32 reaches 0.0552 mean normalized maximum regret vs 0.0984 for the same budget spent before the campaign (Static EI, −44%) and 0.1432 with no repair — closing 76% of the no-repair→full-oracle gap with 16% of the queries.

Strengths

  • Clean decoupling interface — who proposes (agent) ≠ what gets verified (acquisition rule); proposal-policy agnostic across two LLMs and a non-LLM generator.
  • Static-vs-online contrast cleanly isolates the value of spending measurements during, not before, the search.
  • The regret-vs-NRMSE mismatch is a sharp, transferable lesson for anyone benchmarking surrogates meant for optimization loops.
  • Unusual ablation depth for a preprint: noise, budget sweep, surrogate class (GP), acquisition geometry, adapted prior-work baseline, per-trajectory cost.
  • In-context repair = append one labelled row; operationally trivial with any tabular foundation model.

Limitations

  • No ground-truth validation — all oracles are models (Orb-v3, exact SCM responses); wet-lab-scale savings are argued, not measured.
  • Requires mixed-type tabular form plus an in-context-updatable surrogate; graphs/proteins need a different repair mechanism; OOD failure modes untested.
  • Evenly spaced schedule and Q are hand-picked hyperparameters; no adaptive scheduling explored.
  • Residual gap to full feedback remains (0.0552 vs 0.0278 regret; 67–93% of Control's discoveries) — a cost-saving regime, not a dominance regime.
  • Agent scope limited to ReAct-style LLM agents and one non-LLM generator; RL / test-time-training agents and multi-objective settings are future work.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Could an adaptive repair schedule (triggered by surrogate-disagreement spikes or EI collapse) shrink the residual gap to full feedback (0.0552 → 0.0278)?
  2. Does the regret-vs-NRMSE mismatch persist for graph- or sequence-structured surrogates where repair requires fine-tuning, not in-context rows?
  3. Can OSR extend to multi-fidelity ladders (cheap → MACE → Orb-v3 → DFT) where acquisition also chooses the fidelity?
  4. Does EI's verified-incumbent reference become unreliable under systematically biased (not just noisy) oracles — real wet-lab drift?
  5. Would a stronger proposer (RL-optimized, test-time training) change the acquisition-geometry findings, since the archive distribution changes?
  6. Do Q90-UCB / EI rankings hold on worlds from a different generative family than TabPFN's SCM prior?
