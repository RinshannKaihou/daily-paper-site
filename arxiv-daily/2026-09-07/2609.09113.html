Why This Matters

Directly informs AI-alignment auditing and RSI safety monitoring: it quantifies how autonomous agents misread evidence and decouple selectivity from causal control.

Critical Edge

The Expert baseline is partially circular: expert features were curated on the very texts used to score them, and Rank is defined relative to the expert — so part of the agents' gap is baked in by construction.

Methodology — the discovery loop

Each of 20 tasks hands an agent a target concept plus model/SAE identifiers — Gemma-2-9B-IT, Gemma Scope residual SAEs at layers 9 & 20, a 131,072-feature dictionary — but never the evaluation suite or the expert feature. Through a probe_sae interface (≤64 authored texts per call) the agent retrieves top-k activating features or measures its own candidates, iterating freely (60-min timeout, network off) before submitting a single feature ID.

Scoring runs against frozen Neuronpedia Expert references on three dimensions: Activation Rank (dictionary prominence relative to expert), Activation Selectivity (100 × max(0, 2·AUROC−1) separating positives from hard negatives), and Causal Steering (decoder direction injected at every token; a two-pass GPT-4o judge scores net target gain over baseline and norm-matched random controls). Steering strength α is calibrated on held-out prompts for agents, frozen for the expert. Overall = unweighted mean of the three.

Ten frontier configurations ran in their own harnesses (Cursor; Codex for GPT-5.6 Sol/Luna). Search styles diverge sharply — GPT-5.6 Sol authors 115.1 distinct probes but tests only 20.2 candidates; Claude Opus 5 screens 80.2; Kimi K3 commits to few hypotheses — and success tracks hypothesis-testing rigor, not search volume.

10 configs × 3 runs → 600 episodes · 4,873 probe calls · 49,407 probe texts · 10,940 steering generations (14,880 judge ratings)

* Expert Rank is tautological — defined relative to the expert's own rank. GPT-5.6 Sol places 3rd on Steering (30.16) yet only 8th on Rank (51.65): sharp capability trade-offs.

  • Exact recovery is possible: on Portuguese, Kimi K3 found Expert feature 41424 verbatim — task Overall 95.0.
  • Selective ≠ prominent: GPT-5.6 Sol's Portuguese pick hit perfect AUROC 1.000 yet ranked 642.6 vs expert's 13.0, collapsing its Rank score to 3.97.
  • Misread measurements: Claude Opus 5's submission fired harder on its own English control (4.50) than the Portuguese target (3.81) — task Overall 13.91.
  • Run-to-run drift: the same feature is chosen across all 3 runs only 50% of the time (Kimi K3), as low as 20% (GLM-5.2); Grok 4.6's mean-vs-best steering gap spans 31.47 → 46.00.
  • Tracks general intelligence: Overall correlates with the AA Intelligence Index v4.2 at Spearman ρ = 0.800 — but over only n = 4 matched models.

Strengths

  • Unified scoring of representational (rank, selectivity) and causal (steering) quality — directly exposing the "correlation ≠ control" failure mode.
  • Careful evaluation hygiene: anonymized expert identity, frozen suites, norm-matched random controls, degeneration tracked separately, held-out α calibration.
  • Behavioral analysis goes beyond the leaderboard — probe-level case studies and search statistics distinguish hypothesis testing from heuristic picking.
  • Multi-run consistency with standard deviations; per-task and per-category appendix breakdowns.

Limitations

  • Narrow scope: one base model, two SAE layers, one dictionary family, single-feature lookup only — no circuits, no open-ended hypotheses.
  • 20 tasks over 17 concepts; languages make up 30% of tasks, so per-category conclusions rest on very few tasks.
  • Steering scored by a single GPT-4o judge — no human validation or judge ensemble in the benchmark's most consequential dimension.
  • Expert-anchored design mixes a reference standard with a partially circular comparison.
  • No downstream integration: discovered features untested for model editing, unlearning, or alignment loops.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Would the steering gap shrink if agents could run closed-loop steering experiments during discovery, not just post-submission evaluation?
  2. How much of the selectivity gap (92.91 vs 98.92) is an artifact of expert curation on the scoring suite, rather than genuine agent deficiency?
  3. Do these failure modes — substring distractors, format-vs-concept confusion, misread measurements — transfer to other model families, widths, and depths?
  4. Could structured measurement tooling (automatic AUROC/rank reports) fix measurement misreading without removing the scientific challenge?
  5. How much do harness effects (Cursor vs Codex), not model capability, explain the spread — given GPT-5.6 Sol and Luna sit at opposite ends?
