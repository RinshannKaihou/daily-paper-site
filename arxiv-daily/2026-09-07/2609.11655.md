> **一句话总结：** Musec 用"谱裁剪"取代 Muon 的"谱拉平"——只裁剪动量矩阵中超过阈值 D 的奇异值而保留其余谱结构——从优化器层面（与架构无关地）稳定 Muon 训练：在 NanoGPT（491M–1.63B）上，权重谱范数被压到 10 以下（Muon 为 300–400），并在 Muon/MuonClip 发散的大学习率下保持稳定，同时给出了非凸非光滑设定下首个谱裁剪型 Muon 变体的 O(r^{3/2}δ^{-1}ε^{-3}) 收敛保证。

> **TL;DR:** Muon's instability comes from spectral flattening (all momentum singular values → 1), which inflates weakly-represented directions; Musec replaces it with spectral clipping (only singular values above threshold D are truncated). On NanoGPT-Medium (613M) trained on FineWeb at LR 0.2, weight spectral norms stay below 10 under Soft Musec versus 300–400 for Muon (a >30x reduction), and Soft Musec remains stable at the largest swept learning rates (0.01–0.8) where Muon diverges at all three model sizes (491M/613M/1.63B) and MuonClip diverges on two. It matches the best validation loss of surviving baselines under well-tuned configs, comes with the first nonconvex nonsmooth convergence guarantee for a spectral-clipping Muon variant (matching the optimal O(δ^{-1}ε^{-3}) accuracy dependence of Cutkosky et al. 2023), and costs the same 5 Newton–Schulz iterations as Muon — but the empirical edge over the closest prior method SPECTRA is only marginal, and experiments stop at 1.63B params with validation loss as the sole metric.

## 核心贡献

- **谱裁剪替代谱拉平（根因修复）**：Muon 将动量矩阵的所有奇异值映射为约 1（polar/正交化步骤），会给原本奇异值很小的方向注入与主方向同量级的更新，导致权重范数无界增长与 loss spike。Musec 只裁剪超过阈值 D 的奇异值、保留其余谱结构（`Clip(S, D)`），从更新规则本身消除该放大效应。
- **架构无关的优化器级稳定机制**：与 logit soft-capping（Gemma 2）、QK-Norm（与 MLA 不兼容）、MuonClip（只裁剪 QK 权重、留 VO/MLP 不受约束）等"治标"方案不同，Musec 在优化器内部作用于全部矩阵参数，统一稳定所有组件，无需架构改动。
- **原理性解释**：证明 Musec 的更新是带 Frobenius 范数二次项、谱范数约束 ‖Δ‖₂ ≤ D/λ 的约束最速下降问题的精确解——是 Bernstein & Newhouse (2024) 对 Muon 的无约束谱范数最速下降解释的约束版对应物。
- **首个非凸非光滑收敛保证（谱裁剪型 Muon 变体）**：在 ρ-弱凸、随机分量可非凸非光滑的设定下，Musec 以 O(r^{3/2}δ^{-1}ε^{-3} + δ²ρ³r^{-3/2} + r^{3/4}δ^{-1})（r = min(m,n)）的随机梯度 oracle 复杂度收敛到 (δ, ε)-Goldstein 稳定点；当 ρ ≤ O(rδ^{-1}ε^{-1}) 时主导项为 O(r^{3/2}δ^{-1}ε^{-3})，对 δ、ε 的依赖与最优随机非凸非光滑方法一致。此前 Jiang et al. (2026a) 证明精确 polar 更新的 Muon 在该设定下不收敛。
- **Soft Musec：高效 SVD-free 实现**：用平滑饱和函数 h(w,D) = Dw/√(w²+D²) 替代硬裁剪，得到闭式算子 H(M,D) = D(MM^T + D²I)^{-1/2}M，再用耦合 Newton–Schulz 迭代（纯矩阵乘，D²I 位移保证数值稳定）近似；实践中 5 次迭代在 bfloat16 下即可。

## Key Contributions

- **Root-cause fix — spectral clipping instead of spectral flattening.** Muon's polar step maps all momentum singular values to ≈1, injecting full-magnitude updates into directions with small momentum singular values and driving unbounded weight growth. Musec truncates only singular values exceeding a threshold D while preserving spectral skewness.
- **Optimizer-level, architecture-agnostic stabilization.** Unlike attention-logit capping (Gemma 2), QK-Norm (incompatible with MLA), and MuonClip (clips only QK weights, leaving value-output and MLP matrices unregulated), Musec acts inside the optimizer and uniformly stabilizes every matrix parameter.
- **Principled interpretation.** Musec's update is the exact solution of a Frobenius-norm-penalized quadratic subproblem subject to a spectral-norm constraint ‖Δ‖₂ ≤ D/λ — the constrained counterpart of Muon's unconstrained spectral-norm steepest descent (Bernstein & Newhouse, 2024).
- **First convergence guarantee for a spectral-clipping Muon variant in nonconvex nonsmooth stochastic optimization.** For ρ-weakly convex objectives, Musec reaches a (δ, ε)-Goldstein stationary point with O(r^{3/2}δ^{-1}ε^{-3}) dominant oracle complexity (r = min(m, n)), matching the optimal δ, ε dependence of Cutkosky et al. (2023) — whereas exact-polar Muon was recently shown not to converge in this setting (Jiang et al., 2026a).
- **Soft Musec, an efficient SVD-free implementation.** Replaces hard clipping with the smooth saturation h(w, D) = Dw/√(w² + D²), yielding H(M, D) = D(MM^T + D²I)^{-1/2}M approximated by coupled Newton–Schulz iterations (matrix multiplications only; the D²I shift ensures numerical stability); 5 iterations suffice in bfloat16.
- **Key distinction from SPECTRA (Jiang et al., 2026b):** SPECTRA clips the accumulated momentum as post-processing and carries the unclipped state forward; Musec feeds the clipped momentum back into the EMA recurrence, so the momentum state itself stays spectrally bounded and singular values can decay where recent gradients are small.

## Methodology

The method targets the stochastic problem min_W f(W) = E_ξ[F(W; ξ)] with f ρ-weakly convex and F possibly nonconvex/nonsmooth. Starting from Muon's momentum recurrence (EMA of stochastic gradients with β, η), Musec replaces the polar/orthogonalization step: given momentum SVD Mn = Un S̃n Vn^T, the update uses Un Clip(S̃n, D) Vn^T — singular values above D are truncated to D, smaller ones pass through unchanged. Proposition 4.1 shows this update solves min_Δ ⟨G, Δ⟩ + (λ/2)‖Δ‖²_F s.t. ‖Δ‖₂ ≤ D/λ, giving the constrained-steepest-descent interpretation. The theoretical algorithm returns a uniformly random epoch average over K epochs of length T (a standard two-level averaging scheme for Goldstein stationarity); Lemma 4.2 and Theorem 4.3 then yield the (δ, ε)-Goldstein stationarity bound, and Corollary 4.4 tunes T, β, K, γ, D, η to obtain the O(r^{3/2}δ^{-1}ε^{-3} + δ²ρ³r^{-3/2} + r^{3/4}δ^{-1}) oracle complexity.

For practice, exact clipping needs a full SVD per step, which is prohibitively expensive for LLM-scale matrices. Soft Musec substitutes the smooth saturation h(w, D) = Dw/√(w² + D²) applied spectrally, giving the closed form H(M, D) = D(MM^T + D²I)^{-1/2}M; the matrix inverse square root is approximated by coupled Newton–Schulz iterations involving only matrix–matrix products, well-suited to GPUs; the D²I shift keeps eigenvalues away from zero for bf16 stability. Experiments use the modded-nanogpt codebase (RMSNorm, RoPE, squared ReLU, sliding-window attention via Flash Attention 3, multi-token prediction): NanoGPT-Small/Medium/Wide (491M/613M/1.63B) on FineWeb, OpenWebText, and C4, in bfloat16 on H800 GPUs (1,390–8,040 steps). The tuned optimizer applies to non-embedding matrix parameters (attention + MLP), with AdamW fixed for embeddings/Scalars. Baselines: Muon, MuonClip, and SPECTRA (with SGDM base, i.e., Muon without orthogonalization, to isolate the effect of spectral clipping). Weight decay tuned over {0.096, 0.3, 0.6, 1.2}; clipping threshold D over {0.05, 0.125, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0}; all methods use 5 Newton–Schulz iterations.

## Results & Highlights

- **Weight spectral-norm control (NanoGPT-Medium, FineWeb, LR 0.2):** Muon's weight spectral norms inflate to **300–400** with large oscillations across QK, VO, and MLP components; Soft Musec (and SPECTRA) keep spectral norms **below 10** across all three component types — a >30x reduction. MuonClip partially suppresses QK norms but leaves VO and MLP norms elevated, confirming it only regulates QK matrices.
- **Stability across learning rates (FineWeb, LR sweep 0.01–0.8, three model sizes):** at the largest learning rate, **Muon fails to converge at all three scales (491M, 613M, 1.63B)**; **MuonClip diverges on NanoGPT-Small and NanoGPT-Medium** and converges to substantially worse validation loss on NanoGPT-Wide; **Soft Musec remains stable in every configuration**, with performance comparable to SPECTRA and slight improvements in several settings. Consistent results on OpenWebText and C4 (Appendix F.1).
- **Recovery from scheduled loss spikes (NanoGPT-Medium, LR 0.2, all three datasets):** all methods spike near step 400 when the modded-nanogpt schedule simultaneously raises the learning rate by 52%, doubles the batch size, and widens the attention window; Soft Musec and SPECTRA **recover quickly and smoothly**, Muon recovers slowly and lands on a **substantially higher final validation loss**, and MuonClip exhibits persistent oscillations — evidence that QK-weight clipping alone does not stabilize the trajectory.
- **Theory:** oracle complexity O(r^{3/2}δ^{-1}ε^{-3}) (dominant term when ρ ≤ O(rδ^{-1}ε^{-1})) **matches the optimal O(δ^{-1}ε^{-3}) accuracy dependence** of stochastic nonconvex nonsmooth optimization (Cutkosky et al., 2023); for comparison, Muon's smooth-setting analysis (Shen et al., 2025) incurs an r² factor in the stochastic regime (albeit under different stationarity notions and assumptions).
- **Efficiency:** Soft Musec uses the same **5 Newton–Schulz iterations** as Muon/MuonClip in bfloat16 arithmetic — no SVD, no extra optimizer states beyond the clipped momentum feedback.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The core move — clip instead of flatten singular values — is simple and closely related to SPECTRA (spectral clipping as an optimizer wrapper) and to MuCon's singular-value clipping (Yi, 2026, cited for numerical focus). Genuine novelty lies in (a) feeding the clipped momentum back into the EMA recurrence rather than post-processing, and (b) the nonconvex nonsmooth convergence guarantee, which is the first for a spectral-clipping Muon variant. The theory is a real contribution but is built on the standard weak-convexity machinery (two-level epoch averaging, Goldstein stationarity) rather than a new analysis technique.
- **Rigor:** The LR-sweep and weight-norm studies are well designed (three scales, three datasets, per-component norm tracking), and isolating spectral clipping via SPECTRA-on-SGDM is a thoughtful control. However, nearly all headline results are presented **only as figures** — no table of final validation losses with exact numbers, error bars, or seeds is given, making effect sizes hard to audit (e.g., "slight improvements in several settings" over SPECTRA is never quantified).
- **Missing ablations:** the number of Newton–Schulz iterations is fixed at 5 with no sensitivity study; the hard-clip vs soft-saturation gap (Musec vs Soft Musec) is never measured empirically — the exact-clipping algorithm that carries the convergence theory is not the algorithm benchmarked; and the key architectural claim (integration into the momentum recurrence vs SPECTRA's post-processing) has no experiment isolating that specific difference.
- **Fairness of tuning budgets:** Soft Musec and SPECTRA sweep 4 weight-decay values × 6 clipping thresholds (24 configurations) while Muon and MuonClip sweep only 4 weight-decay values (MuonClip additionally has separate λQK/λVO/λMLP grids per its tables) — clipping methods get a larger search space, which is defensible but unstated as a caveat.
- **Scale and scope of evidence:** experiments cap at 1.63B parameters with short runs (1,390–8,040 steps) on modded-nanogpt speedrun-style configs; there are **no downstream benchmarks (e.g., HellaSwag/MMLU), no 7B+-scale runs, no MoE or MLA architectures** — so the claim of relevance to the Kimi K2/GLM-5/DeepSeek-V4 regime is extrapolation, and the paper does not test the QK-Norm-incompatible-with-MLA motivation on an actual MLA model. No wall-clock/throughput overhead numbers versus plain Muon are reported.
- **Theory-practice gap:** the epoch-averaging return scheme required for the Goldstein-stationarity proof is explicitly dropped in practice (final iterate returned); standard, but worth noting.

## Strengths

- Attacks the documented root cause of Muon instability (spectral flattening) rather than its symptoms, yielding a single mechanism that uniformly covers QK, VO, and MLP weights without architecture-specific surgery.
- Clean principled story: constrained steepest descent under a spectral-norm budget; convergence rate with optimal δ, ε dependence in a setting where exact Muon provably fails.
- Practical, drop-in implementation: SVD-free, 5 coupled Newton–Schulz iterations, bfloat16-friendly, no extra optimizer states.
- Strong mechanism evidence: per-component spectral-norm tracking (300–400 → <10) directly ties the stability gains to the proposed mechanism, and the SPECTRA-on-SGDM control isolates spectral clipping from orthogonalization.
- Broad stability sweep: learning rates 0.01–0.8 × three model sizes (491M–1.63B) × three datasets (FineWeb, OpenWebText, C4).

## Limitations

- Maximum scale is 1.63B parameters with ≤8,040-step runs; no downstream-task evaluation — validation loss is the only quality metric.
- The empirical margin over SPECTRA, the closest baseline, is small ("comparable, with slight improvements in several settings"); much of the stability benefit of spectral clipping was already available.
- Exact-number results tables, confidence intervals, and seed variance are absent; results are figure-only.
- No ablations on Newton–Schulz iteration count, hard vs soft clipping, or the momentum-recurrence integration that distinguishes Musec from SPECTRA.
- The MLA/QK-Norm motivation is argued from the literature but never tested on an MLA-based architecture; overhead vs Muon is not quantified.
- The theoretical algorithm (epoch-averaged returns, exact clipping) differs from the deployed one (final iterate, soft saturation).

## Relevance

对关注 LLM 训练优化器（Muon 及其稳定化）的工作高度相关：它给出了一条架构无关、即插即用的 Muon 稳定化路线，可与 QK-clip、权重衰减等正交手段叠加，适合在自家 Muon 训练管线中直接复现验证（阈值 D 与权重衰减的联合调参是主要成本）。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of Soft Musec's advantage over SPECTRA comes specifically from feeding the clipped momentum back into the EMA recurrence, versus SPECTRA's post-hoc clipping of accumulated momentum? An ablation isolating this design choice is missing.
- Does the stability benefit persist at 7B+ scale, with MLA or MoE architectures, and on longer horizons where slow norm drift matters more than transient spikes?
- What is the throughput/memory overhead of Soft Musec versus Muon and MuonClip at equal batch size, and how does performance degrade with 3 or 4 Newton–Schulz iterations instead of 5?
- Can the convergence theory be extended to the soft saturation function actually used in practice (H(M, D) with coupled Newton–Schulz approximation), closing the Musec-vs-Soft-Musec gap?
- How does spectral clipping interact with adaptive per-layer learning-rate scaling (as in Moonshot's MuonClip+RMSNorm recipe), and do downstream benchmarks (HellaSwag, MMLU) confirm the validation-loss parity translates to final model quality?
- Is there an optimal clipping threshold schedule for D (e.g., warmup or layer-wise) rather than a fixed constant tuned per configuration?
