> **一句话总结：** 提出"环境侧适配"范式：在 RL 训练早期注入动作引导（Action Guidance）、后期切换为观测增强（Observation Enrichment）构建 Feedback-Enriched Environments（FEEs），在 SciWorld 和 BFCL 上对 Qwen3-4B/8B 配合 GRPO/DAPO/GSPO 平均提升 2.82 个百分点，且能稳定熵曲线、并把引导信息内化进策略权重而非仅作推理期提示。

> **TL;DR:** Instead of SFT warm-up on the agent side, this paper enriches the *environment*: action guidance early and observation enrichment late during both intra-episode exploration and inter-episode training (FEEs). Across SciWorld and BFCL-v3 with Qwen3-4B/8B under GRPO, DAPO and GSPO, FEE training beats standard-environment RL by +2.82 points on average (e.g., Qwen3-8B+GSPO on SciWorld 53.91% → 60.94%, +7.03 pts; Qwen3-4B+GRPO on BFCL Base 58% → 68%, +10 pts), delays policy entropy collapse from ~250 to 300+ training steps, and a probe experiment shows the enriched feedback is internalized into policy weights — but gains reverse on BFCL's "Miss Func/Miss Param" categories (e.g., 41% → 34% with DAPO-4B) and the method fails entirely on AppWorld's zero-reward trajectories.

## 核心贡献

- 提出**范式转移**：从"智能体侧预热"（SFT warm-up，受限于专家轨迹昂贵与探索受限）转向"**环境侧适配**"——通过系统性地丰富环境反馈信号来缓解长程任务 RL 的奖励稀疏问题。
- 通过先导实验建立**反馈设计策略**，沿两个轴设计：提供什么信息（动作引导 AG vs. 观测增强 OE）与何时提供（单回合内探索 intra-episode vs. 跨回合演化 inter-episode）。结论：**早期用动作引导剪枝搜索空间，后期切换为观测增强以精炼策略**，混合调度（AG-Early → OE-Late）优于任何单一策略。
- 基于 SciWorld 与 BFCL-v3 Multi-Turn 构建 FEEs，在 Qwen3-4B/8B × GRPO/DAPO/GSPO 共 6 种组合上验证一致性增益（平均 +2.82%）。
- 四项深入分析：(1) FEE 稳定训练熵、显著延迟策略坍塌；(2) 促进对困难任务的主动状态空间探索且能迁移回标准环境；(3) 通过受控探针实验证明环境引导被**内化进策略权重**，而非仅是推理期提示；(4) 发现**组内反馈一致性**是稳定优化的边界条件——同一 rollout 组内反馈随机化会导致剧烈波动。

## Key Contributions

- A paradigm shift from agent-side warming up (SFT on expert trajectories, which is expensive and constrains exploration) to **environment-side adaptation**: systematically diversifying and enriching environment feedback during RL to combat reward sparsity in long-horizon tasks.
- An empirical **feedback design strategy** along two axes — *what* to inject (action guidance, AG, vs. observation enrichment, OE) and *when* (early vs. late, both intra-episode across the 15-step horizon and inter-episode across the 200-step training run). The winning schedule: AG early to prune the combinatorial search space, OE late once the policy can interpret extra semantics; a hybrid AG-Early→OE-Late curriculum beats all fixed variants.
- Construction of **Feedback-Enriched Environments (FEEs)** on SciWorld and BFCL-v3 Multi-Turn, validated across Qwen3-4B/8B and three RL algorithms (GRPO, DAPO, GSPO) with a consistent average gain of +2.82 points over standard-environment training.
- Four mechanistic analyses: (1) entropy stabilization — FEE delays/prevents policy collapse even under explicit entropy regularization; (2) proactive exploration — FEE-trained agents reach previously unreachable states and transfer to hard standard-environment tasks (+4.3 pts on the hard tier); (3) internalization — multiple-choice probes on GorillaFileSystem operations (cd, mkdir, touch, rm, rmdir, mv, cp, echo) show FEE-trained models progressively prefer enriched outputs even when tested in standard environments; (4) a boundary condition — intra-group feedback consistency is required, since stochastic feedback within one rollout group distorts group-relative advantage estimation and destabilizes training.

## Methodology

The environment is formalized as a goal-conditioned POMDP, and enriched feedback as an intervention on the observation space, o_t+ = E(o_t, h_t), yielding a new environment with identical states, actions, transitions, and rewards but an augmented observation channel — implicitly reshaping the agent's action distribution to vary task difficulty and encourage trajectory diversity. Agentic RL follows the group-relative paradigm (GRPO-family): N candidate trajectories per goal, trajectory-level binary reward, group-computed advantages assigned to agent tokens with environment tokens masked out.

The pilot study on SciWorld (Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507, GRPO, 200 steps, 16 parallel environments, 15-step episodes, lr 1e-6, enrichment applied stochastically with p=0.5, evaluation every 5 steps on strictly disjoint standard-environment tasks) compares AG-Early/AG-Late (hints at steps 1–3 vs. 6–10), OE-Early/OE-Late, and a hybrid that applies AG-Early for the first 100 training steps then switches to OE-Late. AG dominates OE overall (explicit pruning of invalid branches beats semantically richer but cognitively heavier state descriptions), and the hybrid achieves the highest success rate, motivating the final FEE recipe. In SciWorld, AG = concrete valid actions and OE = real-time task-progress checklists; in BFCL, AG = suggested actions appended to the user query and OE = expanded raw tool returns. Main experiments scale this to Qwen3-4B/8B with GRPO, DAPO (clip-higher, dynamic sampling), and GSPO (sequence-level importance ratios), benchmarked against GPT-5.4, Kimi-K2-Thinking, and Qwen3-235B-Thinking as ceilings. Four analysis experiments follow: entropy tracking with/without entropy regularization over 300 steps (stability), avg@8 per-environment success heatmaps plus easy/medium/hard tier validation on 400 BFCL environments (exploration), probability-margin probes between original and enriched tool outputs every 10 steps (internalization), and consistent-vs-stochastic intra-group feedback comparison (boundary).

## Results & Highlights

- **Consistent headline gains:** FEE training yields an average improvement of **+2.82%** across SciWorld/BFCL, Qwen3-4B/8B, and GRPO/DAPO/GSPO; e.g., Qwen3-8B+GSPO on SciWorld improves **53.91% → 60.94% (+7.03 pts)** over standard-environment GSPO training, and Qwen3-4B+GRPO on BFCL Base improves **58.00% → 68.00% (+10.00 pts)**; on averages, Qwen3-4B+DAPO reaches **43.88% → 47.81% (+3.93)** and Qwen3-8B+GSPO **41.78% → 46.39% (+4.61)**.
- **RL bridges the open/closed gap:** base Qwen3-4B/8B average only **23.58% / 27.18%**; after RL they reach **47.81% / 48.11%**, comfortably above Qwen3-235B-Thinking (**35.09%**) and approaching GPT-5.4 (**52.07%**) and Kimi-K2-Thinking (**53.51%**) on the combined benchmark suite.
- **Training stability:** without entropy regularization, the standard environment's policy entropy collapses to zero at ~250 steps while FEE training stays stable for the full 300 steps; with entropy loss forced, standard collapses around step ~130 whereas FEE sustains stability for nearly 200 steps.
- **Exploration transfer:** on 400 BFCL environments tiered by difficulty, FEE training improves hard-tier success from **6.5% to 10.9% (+4.3 pts)** in standard-environment evaluation (medium 49.8% → 50.8%, easy ~87% held), showing the exploration skill persists without enriched feedback.
- **Honest negative results:** FEE can *hurt* when the agent should question the input — Qwen3-4B+DAPO drops **41.00% → 34.00% (−7.00 pts)** on BFCL Miss Param and Qwen3-8B+GRPO drops **52.00% → 48.00% (−4.00 pts)** on Miss Func, hypothesized as over-following interaction cues instead of asking for clarification; and on AppWorld, enriched feedback fails to escape zero-reward trajectories entirely for both model scales.

## Authors & Credibility

Hongbang Yuan (Fudan University), Zhuoran Jin (CASIA), and Yixin Cao (Fudan University and Shanghai Innovation Institute, corresponding author). The group has a credible track record in LLM-agent and RL research; the work is framed as an empirical study with code released at github.com/HongbangYuan/EnvAsScaffold, uses public benchmarks (SciWorld, BFCL-v3), open Qwen3-4B/8B backbones, and standard RL algorithms, all of which support independent verification. The paper explicitly reports its own failure cases (Miss Func/Miss Param regressions, AppWorld failure) rather than hiding them, which raises confidence in the empirical honesty.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Moderate-to-good. The individual ingredients (hint-based scaffolding, curriculum over environment difficulty, observation augmentation) exist in prior work — the paper itself cites hint-injection literature from the data-scaling era — but the systematic "what × when" factorial study, the AG→OE switching schedule, the internalization probe, and the intra-group-consistency finding are genuinely new angles on environment-side design for multi-turn agentic RL.
- **Rigor:** The 2×2×3 grid (2 benchmarks × 2 scales × 3 algorithms) with consistent sign of improvement is solid, and evaluation tasks are strictly disjoint from training tasks to prevent contamination. However, Table 1 records **peak performance** evaluated every 5 steps rather than final-checkpoint performance, which can overstate results relative to deployment; variance/significance tests are not reported, and the +2.82% average is modest enough that seed-level noise could matter for individual cells (several cells show 0 or negative deltas, e.g., GSPO-4B BFCL Base 54% → 54% and Long Context −6/−7 pt regressions for some 4B runs).
- **Reproducibility:** Good — public code, open models, named hyperparameters (lr 1e-6, 200 steps, p=0.5 enrichment, 15-step episodes), and detailed appendix-level environment construction. The exact wording of AG/OE templates is the main unstated variable.
- **Significance:** The message "adapt the environment, not just the agent" is timely for the scaling-environments era, and the internalization result preempts the obvious "it's just an inference-time prior" objection. But hard-tier absolute success (10.9%) and the complete AppWorld failure show the method bootstraps rather than solves sparse-reward regimes.
- **Red flags:** Enrichment content is hand-designed per environment (SciWorld progress checklists, BFCL expanded tool returns), so the claimed "general strategy" still requires per-benchmark authoring effort; the early/late phase boundaries are manually specified with no sensitivity analysis (acknowledged in Limitations); only two benchmarks are covered; and the claimed "consistently outperforms" abstract-level message sits alongside roughly a quarter of individual table cells being flat or negative.

## Strengths

- Clean problem framing (reward sparsity) with a complementary, orthogonal alternative to the dominant SFT-warm-up recipe.
- The pilot study is a proper factorial ablation of what/when to enrich, and its conclusion (AG early, OE late) is validated at scale rather than asserted.
- The four mechanistic analyses go beyond leaderboard deltas: entropy stabilization, tiered-difficulty transfer, a controlled probe for weight-level internalization, and the intra-group consistency boundary are each actionable design guidance for practitioners.
- Unusual empirical candor — negative cells, Miss Func/Miss Param failure modes, and the AppWorld null result are reported and analyzed in the main text, with explicit Limitations.

## Limitations

- Environment-specific design effort: AG/OE content, phase boundaries (early vs. late, both intra- and inter-episode), and the 0.5 injection probability are manually specified per benchmark; sensitivity to these choices is unexplored (self-acknowledged).
- Narrow benchmark coverage — only SciWorld and BFCL; generalization to other agent benchmarks is untested, and AppWorld (extremely sparse long-horizon) remains unsolved: enriched feedback still yields zero-reward trajectories for Qwen3-4B/8B.
- Average gains are modest (+2.82%) and uneven; some categories regress, especially tasks requiring the agent to challenge or clarify the input rather than execute it.
- Peak-performance reporting and absence of significance testing weaken the strength of cross-cell comparisons.

## Relevance

与我方关注的长程智能体 RL 训练与智能体自我进化方向高度相关：FEE 提供了一条与 SFT warm-up 正交、可即插即用于 GRPO/DAPO/GSPO 训练管线的环境侧增强方案，其"早期动作引导→后期观测增强"的调度策略与组内反馈一致性发现可直接指导我们的智能体训练环境设计。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can AG/OE enrichment be generated automatically (e.g., by a teacher LLM or the agent itself) instead of hand-authored per environment, and does auto-generated feedback retain the same gains?
- How sensitive is the AG→OE switching point (fixed at step 100 here) to task horizon, reward density, and model scale — could an adaptive, performance-triggered schedule dominate the fixed one?
- Does the "internalization" finding hold for stronger backbones (e.g., 32B+) or does enrichment become redundant once the base policy can infer hidden state?
- Can the Miss Func/Miss Param regression be fixed by adversarially including clarification-demanding episodes in training, turning the over-compliance failure mode into a learned disambiguation skill?
- For AppWorld-level sparsity, what richer environment-side interventions (reward shaping, subgoal decomposition, synthetic intermediate observations) would be needed to escape zero-reward trajectories where observation enrichment provably fails?
