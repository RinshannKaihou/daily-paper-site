Tracing Stereotypes from Representation to Output in Multilingual LLMs

Ariun-Erdene Tumurchuluun, Yusser Al Ghussin, Pinzhen Chen, Josef van Genabith, et al. — Saarland University · Queen's University Belfast · DFKI · UT Nuremberg

Key metric — depth dissociation
36–53%

of network depth separates peak linear-probe decodability (mid-layers) from peak causal influence (always the final layer).

Why this matters

Probing, attribution, and ablation answer different questions and must be measured separately — a prerequisite for any multilingual bias localization or steering.

三类证据必须分别测量，是多语言偏见定位与干预的直接前置参考。

Critical edge

The most quotable number — 46–79% bias reduction from language-agnostic features — rests on a single SAE suite (Llama-Scope, English-trained, L0=50) and failed to replicate in the three other suites, where mean effects are near zero (−0.0008 to −0.016).

Suites confound language × sparsity × width — differences may reflect SAE quality, not stereotype structure.

01 Methodology 方法

Four instruments triangulate one question across three 8B-class multilingual models — Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen3-8B, Gemma-2-9B — with feature discovery (MBBQ: EN/ES/NL/TR, six social categories) strictly separated from intervention evaluation (held-out SHADES).

  1. 1Linear probing — L2 logistic probes on mean-pooled layer states; permuted-label & weight-norm controls (Hewitt & Liang); English-centric OLMo-7B as reference. Macro-F1, 80/20 split.
  2. 2Attribution patching — gradient×activation attribution on the logit margin via TransformerLens; evidence-injection and stereotype-routing contrasts, AUC-aggregated per layer. First-order approximation of causal influence.
  3. 3SAE decomposition — four pretrained suites (Llama-Scope, Llama-Multi, Gemma-Scope, Qwen-Multi); contrastive top-3 feature selection per category/layer/stream; lexical anchoring vs 99th-percentile null + Neuronpedia glosses.
  4. 4Feature ablation — zero-mask in the encoded basis during SHADES inference; bootstrap CIs, Cohen's d; transfer scored by Language/Category Specificity (σ/|μ| < 0.5 = agnostic).

Beyond the headline

  • 88.5% (123/139) of glossed Llama-Scope features match their social category, forming semantic families — disability splits into neurodevelopmental / psychiatric / chronic-illness clusters.
  • Ablation is bidirectional: disability bias moves +0.280 toward the stereotype in Llama-Scope, −0.090 away in Gemma-Scope.
  • Lexical anchoring is suite-dependent: 46.9% of Llama-Scope features anchored vs 8.8% of Llama-Multi; 41.7% (Gemma-Scope) vs 0.8% (Qwen-Multi).
  • Neither depth (R² < 0.0016) nor anchor strength predicts the signed effect — feature effects must be measured, not inferred.

03 Strengths 优势

  • Discovery (MBBQ) and intervention evaluation (held-out SHADES) fully separated — no circularity.
  • Genuine multi-method triangulation across three independently trained model families, plus an English-centric control model.
  • Non-replication of the headline effect reported prominently, not buried.
  • Semantic-family taxonomy per category gives auditors concrete feature handles.
  • Clear lesson for interpretability: localization starts intervention analysis, it does not end it.

04 Limitations 局限

  • Four high-resource languages only; SHADES lacks Turkish, so ablation covers 3 of 4.
  • Only 8B–9B models — depth separation and feature specificity may shift with scale.
  • SAE suites confound language, sparsity, and width; the strongest effect comes from one suite.
  • Single-feature ablation ignores redundant bias pathways; no intersectional (age × gender) tests.
  • Attribution patching is a first-order approximation; the LSS/CSS = 0.5 threshold has no sensitivity analysis.

05 Open questions 延伸问题

  • Does the 36–53% decodability–influence separation shrink, persist, or invert at 70B scale?
  • Do matched-sparsity English vs multilingual SAEs on the same base model reproduce the language-agnostic minority?
  • Can joint whole-semantic-family ablation avoid the disability backfire (+0.280)?
  • Does 0% category-agnostic features survive intersectional categories (age × gender, disability × SES)?
  • Do the late-layer negative stereotype-routing attributions form a reusable self-correction circuit worth amplifying?
