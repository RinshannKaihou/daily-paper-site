> **一句话总结：** 本文证明激活转向（activation steering）的跨行为副作用由模型自身决定而非被转向的方向：在 24 个行为、10 个指令模型上，一个不含任何行为信息的随机扰动方向能以 0.90 的秩相关复现真实转向所引发的干扰排序，而行为方向几何（余弦重叠等）在留一行为预测下几乎无法解释干扰（held-out R² ≤ 0.09），表明转向是"滚向模型默认行为集合"的扰动，而非模块化编辑。

> **TL;DR:** Samsung researchers ask what decides the side effects of activation steering, and answer: the model, not the steered direction. Across 24 behaviors and 10 instruction-tuned models (1.5B–72B), every effect is read from generated text by an LLM judge. A random direction with no behavioral content — matched to real steers only in added norm — induces the same behaviors in the same order as real steers (Spearman 0.90), while direction-geometry predictors under leave-one-behavior-out cross-validation reach only Spearman 0.41 and held-out R² ≤ 0.092 (vs the geometric-overlap baseline's implicit prediction of symmetric interference, contradicted by M_ij vs M_ji correlating at just Pearson 0.09). Steering profanity raises toxicity +3.4 pooled sd while steering toxicity moves profanity +0.0. Implication: disentangling behavior directions cannot make steering modular; measure a model's default set (refusal, sycophancy, poeticism sinks) with one random push instead.

## 核心贡献

- **转向的副作用由模型决定，而非被转向的行为**：一个与真实转向仅在"加入向量范数"上匹配、不含任何行为内容的随机方向，能以 0.90 的秩相关（Spearman）复现真实转向引发的行为干扰排序，且 Pearson 相关达 0.92；但它完全无法产生需要特定方向的词汇性行为（如脏话为精确 0），说明排序是模型属性而非行为表示的属性。
- **干扰单向运行，构成单一排序**：转向 profanity 使 toxicity 上升 +3.4 个合并标准差，而转向 toxicity 对 profanity 的影响为 +0.0；整个矩阵中 M_ij 与 M_ji 的 Pearson 相关仅 0.09，反对称部分占总幅值的 0.68（能量的 0.46）——任何对称量（如余弦重叠）在原则上都无法表达这些效应。
- **行为方向的几何几乎不预测其副作用**：将一个行为连同其全部 38 个行为对留出（leave-one-behavior-out），用其余行为拟合的几何预测器（含 13 维特征、岭回归与梯度提升树、三种方向估计）对该行为的干扰排序仅达秩相关 0.41，幅度解释不超过 R² 0.10；梯度提升树在样本内 R² 0.66 而留出仅 0.09，是记忆训练行为的典型特征。
- **可解码 ≠ 可转向，必须从生成文本读取**：全部 24 个行为在激活上都线性可解码（held-out AUROC 1.000，打乱标签对照 0.26–0.62），但只有 20 个能改变模型实际输出（confidence、verbosity、humor、anthropomorphism 失败）；任何不读生成文本的读出方式（探针或供选答案的概率）都会误判全部 24 个为可控。
- **机制普遍且随规模变化**：四个论断在 10 个指令模型（Qwen2.5 1.5B–72B、Llama-3.1-8B、Llama-3.3-70B、Gemma-2 9B/27B、Mistral-7B）上全部成立；"向默认行为滑落"的拉力在 <10B 模型中最强（attractor ρ 最高 0.90），在各自家族的最大模型中减弱（Gemma-2-27B 0.15、Llama-3.3-70B 0.38、Qwen2.5-72B 0.54）。

## Key Contributions

- **Side effects are set by the model, not the steered behavior.** A random direction carrying no behavioral content, matched to a real steer only in the norm it adds to the residual stream, induces the same behaviors in the same order as real steers (rank correlation 0.90; Pearson 0.92 on values), while producing none of the lexically specific behaviors (profanity at exactly zero) — the ranking is a property of the model, not of the behaviors' representations.
- **Interference runs one way and forms a single ranking.** Steering profanity raises toxicity by +3.4 pooled standard deviations while steering toxicity moves profanity by +0.0; across the 380 ordered pairs M_ij and M_ji correlate at Pearson 0.09, and the antisymmetric part A carries 0.68 of total entry magnitude (0.46 of squared energy). No symmetric quantity (cosine overlap) can express this in principle.
- **A behavior's direction says little about its own side effects.** Under leave-one-behavior-out prediction (holding out a behavior with all 38 of its pairs), geometry fit on the others orders its interfering pairs only weakly (best held-out Spearman 0.41) and explains under a tenth of their size (no variant reaches held-out R² 0.10). Gradient-boosted trees hit in-sample R² 0.66 vs held-out 0.09 — the signature of memorizing training behaviors.
- **Steerability must be read from text, not probes.** All 24 behaviors are linearly decodable (held-out AUROC 1.000, label-shuffled controls 0.26–0.62), yet only 20 change what Qwen2.5-7B writes; any readout blind to generated text would call all 24 controllable.
- **The mechanism is general and scale-dependent.** All four claims hold on ten instruction-tuned models from 1.5B to 72B across four families; the pull toward defaults is strongest below 10B (attractor ρ up to 0.90) and weakens in each family's largest model (Gemma-2-27B 0.15, Llama-3.3-70B 0.38, Qwen2.5-72B 0.54).

## Methodology

The paper runs a single six-step pipeline, developed on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (28 layers, hidden size 3584) and replicated unchanged on nine more models:

1. **Behavior directions.** 24 behaviors (refusal, sycophancy, toxicity, politeness, poeticism, archaism, etc.) spanning register, affect, interpersonal stance, and content style. Each is defined by 12 contrastive triples (shared user prompt + behavior-present/absent assistant response; 288 total, AI-generated to spec and released). The direction is the difference of class means of residual-stream activations (mean-pooled over assistant tokens), computed per layer; the layer is picked from a fixed 25–85% depth band by held-out projection AUROC (5-fold CV; selected layers 7–16 of 28 on Qwen2.5-7B).
2. **Judge-in-the-loop calibration.** Rather than one fixed steering coefficient, each behavior's coefficient (grid {1,2,3,4,6,8} × the raw unnormalized direction, added via forward hook at every position, greedy decoding, bfloat16, 48 new tokens) is calibrated on 32 neutral prompts so that an LLM judge's 0–10 rating of that behavior approaches a two-point target while a distinct-bigram coherence floor (≥0.6) holds. Behaviors with no coherent coefficient are dropped — this is what separates decodable from steerable.
3. **Interference matrix M.** Entry M_ij = change in the judge's rating of behavior j when steering behavior i at its calibrated coefficient, vs unsteered baselines on the same prompts. Columns are standardized by the pooled standard deviation of judge scores; significance by prompt bootstrap (2000 resamples).
4. **Structure.** M is split into symmetric S = (M+Mᵀ)/2 and antisymmetric A = (M−Mᵀ)/2; singular-value decomposition measures low-rank-ness; an additive fit M_ij ≈ a_i + b_j (alternating least squares, gauge-fixed) yields per-behavior source drive a_i and susceptibility b_j, ranked by source strength c_i = a_i − b_i.
5. **Random push control.** 24 random directions matched to real steers only in the norm added, held to the same coherence floor.
6. **Geometry as predictor.** 13 features per ordered pair (symmetric overlaps, directional features, norms, self-effects) fit with ridge regression and gradient-boosted trees under leave-one-behavior-out CV, scored by held-out Spearman and R² against a label-shuffled null. Two "geometry-prescribed" modified steers (orthogonalized against all other behavior directions; length-anchored) are also tested. Three direction estimates are compared: difference of means, linear probes, and 3-dim concept cones.

Each of the ten models is steered and judged by itself (judge = the same base model queried without steering, rating all 24 behaviors 0–10 per generation), so cross-model differences reflect the model under study; a fixed strong judge (Qwen2.5-72B) re-reading three models' saved generations serves as the judge ablation.

## Results & Highlights

- **Interference is pervasive and mostly one-way (Qwen2.5-7B, 24-behavior matrix).** 149 of 380 off-diagonal entries are significant (vs ~19 expected by chance at 95% intervals); the average side effect is 0.37 pooled sd against an average on-target effect of 2.07. Steering profanity → toxicity +3.4 pooled sd, but toxicity → profanity +0.0; M_ij vs M_ji correlate at Pearson 0.09 (the geometric overlap account predicts exactly 1). The antisymmetric part A carries 0.68 of total entry magnitude (95% CI 0.44–0.47 of squared energy), and 43 of 380 effects exceed 1 sd with three in four of those coming back under 0.5 sd in the reverse direction.
- **The matrix is low-rank and dominated by a susceptibility ranking.** The top three singular patterns carry 0.71 of the energy (CI 0.68–0.73); a single pattern inside A carries 0.54 of it; the additive two-numbers-per-behavior fit explains 0.31 of off-diagonal magnitude. Ten of twenty behaviors are significant sinks (led by sycophancy, refusal, poeticism); six significantly resist (led by sentiment, politeness, formality). Unsteered, the 14 non-resisting behaviors average 0.04/10 judge points vs 3.78 for the six resistant ones.
- **A random push reproduces the real-steer ranking at Spearman 0.90** (Pearson 0.92 on values): a direction with no behavioral content induces each behavior in proportion to how easily real steers induce it. It induces toxicity (+0.16 pooled sd, CI [0.07, 0.28]) but produces profanity at exactly zero — toxicity is somewhere the model falls, profanity somewhere it must be pushed.
- **Geometry fails out-of-sample.** Best held-out Spearman is 0.41 (all-13-features boosted trees on difference-of-means directions); no variant reaches held-out R² 0.10 (best 0.092; linear-probe variant 0.021; concept-cone overlap alone −0.030, worse than guessing the mean). Boosted trees: in-sample R² 0.66 vs held-out 0.09. Acting on the geometry does not help either: the orthogonalized and anchored steers change side effects by −0.03 [−0.08, 0.02] and +0.02 [−0.02, 0.06] pooled sd while merely weakening the steer (on-target effect 2.2 → 1.2 and 1.8 judge points).
- **Decodable ≠ steerable, and steerability grows with scale.** All 24 behaviors hit held-out AUROC 1.000, but only 20 are steerable on Qwen2.5-7B (confidence +0.03, humor +0.00, anthropomorphism +0.16, verbosity +0.31 self-effect — below the one-point floor). Across the ten models the steerable count rises from 13/24 (Qwen2.5-3B) to 24/24 (Llama-3.3-70B).
- **All claims replicate across ten models (1.5B–72B, four families).** One-way fraction 0.63–0.69 of entry magnitude everywhere; top-3 SV energy 0.65–0.93; geometry held-out R² −0.11 to 0.13 on every model. The attractor correlation weakens at the top of each family: 0.90 (Qwen2.5-7B) → 0.54 (Qwen2.5-72B), 0.76 (Llama-3.1-8B) → 0.38 (Llama-3.3-70B), 0.78 (Gemma-2-9B) → 0.15 (Gemma-2-27B). Refusal is the top induced behavior in the three refusal-trained families but weakest of seven in Mistral-7B.
- **Judge ablation.** Re-reading three self-judged models' generations with a fixed strong judge (Qwen2.5-72B): steerable-set Jaccard 0.81–1.00, susceptibility-hierarchy Spearman 0.74–0.83, asymmetry fraction essentially unchanged (e.g. 0.69→0.64); only the exact top-sink membership reshuffles (Jaccard 0.33–0.71), consistent with the default set being model-specific while the mechanism is not.

## Authors & Credibility

Srikanth Malla, Chiho Choi, and Joon Hee Choi are researchers at Samsung Semiconductor US — an industry lab with a credible track record in efficient ML, though this is foundational interpretability work rather than a product claim, which reduces commercial-bias concerns. The paper's credibility rests on unusually careful measurement hygiene: every effect is read from generated text by an LLM judge rather than activation probes (avoiding a circularity the authors explicitly diagnose), with label-shuffled nulls, prompt bootstrap confidence intervals, a leave-one-behavior-out protocol that prior geometric work did not run, a judge ablation against a second, stronger fixed judge (Appendix G), and a declared AI-use statement (contrastive triples AI-generated to spec and released; all numbers regenerable from saved judge ratings). The reproducibility statement promises release of code, all 288 triples, judge prompts, ratings, and interference matrices; all ten checkpoints are public and greedy decoding is used for determinism.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: high.** The random-push control is a genuinely decisive experiment — showing that a behavior-free direction reproduces the interference ranking at Spearman 0.90 while lexically specific behaviors stay at zero cleanly separates "model defaults" from "direction geometry." The reframing (a steer is a perturbation whose endpoint the model fixes) is a real conceptual contribution beyond prior catalogs of steering side effects (Siu et al. 2025; Braun et al. 2025).
- **Rigor: strong, with honest boundaries.** The two commitment tests for the geometric account (symmetry and transfer) are stated before the results, and the leave-one-behavior-out design (removing all 38 pairs of a held-out behavior, not just one pair) correctly closes the leakage loophole the authors identify in Sharma et al. 2026 and Bhandari et al. 2026. The asymmetry-based split S/A also protects against symmetric measurement error inflating the one-way result.
- **Weaknesses in explanatory power.** The paper's own numbers bound the account: the additive susceptibility fit explains only 0.31 of off-diagonal magnitude, so most pairwise interference is captured neither by geometry nor by the one-way ranking — the "defaults" account is more a refutation of geometry than a predictive theory. Why the pull toward defaults weakens at 70B+ is explicitly left unexplained.
- **Potential confounds worth weighing.** The judge is an LLM rating its own family's generations; the ablation (Appendix G) addresses this on three models, but the detailed 24×24 map rests on one model (Qwen2.5-7B), self-judged, with only 32 neutral prompts per condition — a small base for 380 bootstrap intervals. The contrastive triples (12 per behavior) are synthetic and AI-generated, and behavior definitions are the authors' choices; the sink set (refusal, sycophancy, poeticism) could partly reflect what a judge notices in degenerating text rather than what the model "favors," though the coherence floor and the zero-effect lexical behaviors argue against pure degeneration. Steering is single-layer, single-coefficient-per-behavior, and capped at the grid {1–8}; the claim that "geometry does not predict interference" is therefore about difference-of-means/probe/cone steering at these calibrated strengths, not about all conceivable steering parameterizations (e.g., multi-layer or learned steering vectors), and no ablation over steering strength tests whether geometry matters more at low coefficients near the on-target regime.
- **Reproducibility: good on paper.** Artifacts promised but described as "will be released" at v2 — verification pending actual release.
- **Significance: substantial for anyone using steering as a safety control surface.** The negative result that orthogonalizing directions does not reduce side effects (−0.03 change while on-target effect drops from 2.2 to 1.2) directly undercuts a standard proposed remediation, and the practical takeaway (probe with one random push to recover a model's default set) is cheap and actionable.

## Strengths

- The random-push experiment is an elegant, near-decisive control that isolates the model's contribution from the direction's content.
- Methodological self-awareness: diagnosing why probe-based readouts are circular, and why pair-level holdout leaks, before building the measurement.
- Text-grounded readout (LLM judge with calibration and a coherence floor) makes the measured construct "what the model writes," which is the quantity practitioners care about.
- Broad, identical-pipeline replication across ten models and four families (1.5B–72B) with per-model self-judging plus a fixed-judge ablation.
- Transparent statistics: bootstrap CIs on structural quantities, label-shuffled nulls, and explicit statement of what the boosted tree's in-sample/held-out gap means.

## Limitations

- The defaults account explains only a minority of the interference matrix's magnitude (additive fit 0.31); the paper refutes geometry more than it predicts interference.
- The detailed map comes from one model (Qwen2.5-7B) with 32 neutral prompts and 12 contrastive triples per behavior — small data for fine-grained claims about 380 ordered pairs.
- LLM-judge readout inherits judge biases; the ablation covers three models and preserves structure, but exact sink membership is judge-sensitive (top-sink Jaccard as low as 0.33).
- Single-layer, per-behavior-calibrated steering with a fixed coefficient grid; no test of whether geometry matters more at steering strengths nearer threshold, nor of multi-layer or learned steering interventions.
- The 24-behavior set (style-heavy: archaism, legalese, slang...) may under-represent cognitive/capability behaviors, and the same set is used for all ten models; why the pull weakens at the largest scales is left open.

## Relevance

这篇论文对可解释性与安全对齐研究直接相关：如果你关注 activation steering、行为控制的方向性方法（refusal direction 等）或"探针可解码即可控"类论断，本文提供了直接的实证反例与更便宜的诊断工具（一次随机扰动即可恢复模型的默认行为集合），对设计可信的安全编辑流程很有参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the "roll toward defaults" account hold for capability behaviors (accuracy, reasoning depth, calibration) rather than style/register behaviors, where the output text is graded by correctness rather than an LLM judge?
- Would learned or multi-layer steering vectors (optimized to preserve off-target behavior) escape the default-set pull, or does any residual-stream perturbation of comparable norm land in the same sinks?
- What mechanically causes the default set (refusal, sycophancy, poeticism) — is it an artifact of instruction-tuning/distillation data, and can it be shrunk by training without losing the resistant prosocial behaviors?
- Why does the pull toward defaults weaken at 70B+ (attractor ρ 0.15–0.54 in family-max models) — is it broader behavioral robustness, or a larger set of defaults that the single-ranking statistic can no longer capture?
- Can the random-push probe replace geometry-based pre-deployment audits of safety edits in practice — what is the minimum number of pushes and prompts needed to recover a model's sink set at fixed confidence?
- Since steering toxicity never produces profanity but steering profanity produces toxicity, do one-way links imply a causal hierarchy the model can be pushed along deliberately, enabling targeted multi-behavior control?
