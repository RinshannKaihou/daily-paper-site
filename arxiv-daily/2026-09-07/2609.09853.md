> **一句话总结：** Eon 团队用单一种子化实体图确定性生成一家完整虚构企业（含 Salesforce/Zendesk/Gong 等 66 个产品模拟器 + 由问题驱动生成的内部数据库），答案键全部从最终记录计算得出、可精确判分；在 33 题的模拟器赛道上 9 个模型准确率为 42.4%–76.8%，简单过滤题平均 92.6% 正确而多跳（multi-hop）问题平均仅 3.7%，暴露出现有 LLM agent 在跨系统聚合上的短板。

> **TL;DR:** The Era by Eon Benchmark deterministically generates an entire fictional enterprise — one seeded entity graph projected into 66 product simulators (Salesforce, Zendesk, Gong, Slack, Stripe, S3, …) plus internal databases whose schemas/records are generated from author-written business questions — with every answer key computed from the final records, so grading is exact. On its simulator track (frozen `segment_fintech_small` estate, 33 questions × 3 repeats), nine models scored 42.4%–76.8%: claude-opus-4.8 led at 76.8% vs 42.4% for gpt-5.4-mini (+34.3 pts, one of only 3 of 36 pairwise differences significant after correction). Averaged across the nine models, accuracy collapsed from 92.6% on simple filtering to 39.6% cross-system, 6.2% on superlatives, and 3.7% on multi-hop questions. Read it for the environment-generation methodology and validation instruments (realism scorecard 61.8→97.0, adversarial detector 55.2%→0% flagged), not for a definitive model ranking — the paper itself shows most rankings are statistically inconclusive.

## 核心贡献

- **整体生成的企业评测环境**：给定行业、公司规模、商业模式、应用组合四个参数加一个随机种子，确定性生成一家完整虚构公司（员工组织架构、客户群、销售管线、工单、通话录音、聊天、文档），同一种子在任意机器上逐字节复现。
- **单一实体图、多系统投影**：所有产品模拟器不各自造数据，而是同一实体图的投影——同一账户在 CRM 机会、支持工单、通话记录中以同一 join key 出现，跨系统问题"构造上即良定义"。共 66 个产品模拟器（Salesforce、HubSpot、Zendesk、Jira、Gong、Slack、Stripe、S3、Google Drive 等），经 MCP 工具只读访问。
- **问题驱动的内部数据库生成器**：作者只需写业务问题清单，LLM 一次性设计 schema 与生成计划（唯一用到 LLM 的阶段），随后由确定性代码物化记录、植入"合格行 + 近似行（near-miss）"并从最终表计算答案键——近似行保证忽略任何一个过滤条件都会答错。
- **按生成机制分级的验证体系**：内部数据库用设计符合性检查、"懒惰求解器"面板（检测可被捷径答出的题）、答案键缺陷检查（平局/自答文本/精度容差）、boosted-tree 依赖性两样本检验 + 基数估计误差；实体图用五轴真实性记分卡（边缘/联合/时间/结构/内容）+ 对抗性合成痕迹检测器。
- **确定性九模型评测**：同一冻结场景、33 题 × 3 重复、隔离租户、等额预算（25 轮、40 次工具调用、每次响应 8000 字符），带 95% 区间与成对统计检验。

## Key Contributions

- **Fully generated enterprise environment**: from four parameters (industry, size, business model, application portfolio) plus one seed, the benchmark deterministically generates a complete fictional company — workforce with hierarchy and employment history, customer base, sales pipeline, tickets, recorded calls, chats, documents — byte-identical on any machine.
- **One entity graph, many projections**: no simulator generates its own data; each of 66 product simulators (Salesforce, HubSpot, Zendesk, Jira, Gong, Slack, Stripe, S3, Google Drive, …) stores the slice of the same graph in that vendor's format. The account on a CRM opportunity is the same account on a ticket and a recorded call under one join key, so cross-system questions are well defined by construction. Determinism replaces inter-simulator synchronization.
- **Question-conditioned internal-database generator**: an author supplies business questions; an LLM (the only LLM step, run once per benchmark) designs the schema and per-column generation plan, then deterministic code materializes records, plants qualifying rows plus near-miss rows (each violating exactly one condition, rotating), and computes the answer key by running every question against the finished tables — so planted answers even count coincidental matches correctly.
- **Validation scoped to each mechanism**: internal databases get plan-adherence checks, a panel of deliberately lazy solvers (any question a lazy solver answers exactly is reported as measuring nothing), mechanical answer-key defect search (ties, self-answering text, precision tolerance), a boosted-tree classifier two-sample test against independently column-shuffled copies (with a measured noise floor), and cardinality-estimation-error checks. The entity graph gets a five-axis realism scorecard (marginal, joint, temporal, structural, content) against targets from Eon operational data and published statistics, plus an adversarial detector hunting synthetic artifacts (numbered name variants, flat histograms, counter-advanced timestamps).
- **Deterministic nine-model evaluation**: one frozen estate, 33 questions × 3 repeats, isolated read-only tenants, equal budgets (25 turns / 40 tool calls / 8,000-char tool responses), three meta-tools only (models must discover ~290 MCP tools themselves), with 95% intervals and corrected paired tests.

## Methodology

The estate is defined by four parameters plus a seed. A seeded generator first builds the workforce (composition varies with growth stage; departed employees leave orphaned records deliberately, enabling access-review questions), then the customer base (sales territories of ~20 accounts with a concentration cap), then business activity in dependency order — contacts, deals with full stage history, documents, channels and messages, tickets, calls, campaigns — with correlated volumes (larger accounts produce more tickets), business-day calendars in participants' time zones, and lifecycle-consistent timestamps.

Each business application is a simulator that rebuilds the tenant's graph from the same seed and projects its slice in the vendor's real format (record IDs derive deterministically in each vendor's ID format; documents render as real DOCX/XLSX/PDF bytes agreeing with their tabular records). The application portfolio is consulted only at projection time: a tenant without Gong still has the same calls with the same participants, but no Gong artifacts anywhere — keeping the underlying business independent of software choices. Serving is read-only over MCP with a tenant-bound signed credential, and day states (day 0 empty, day 1 full history, day 2 one more day of change) recompute answer keys so staleness is measurable.

Questions and answers are both derived from the generated company. A corpus of 83 scenarios (10 industries × 6 size tiers + 23 named companies) is pre-generated and committed; a CI test regenerates from seeds and asserts byte-identity. Question templates are code pairing a question form with its answering computation (open pipeline value, top salesperson by won revenue, median ticket resolution time), instantiated per company. A reachability audit re-reads every fact through each simulator's native API and MCP tools — this caught a real defect where a cross-system join was correct against stored data but impossible through the product interface (only participant email is visible on both sides). Twelve composed (cross-system) questions obey three fairness rules: joins only through agent-followable keys, definitions and tie-breaking stated in question text, and exclusion when the data doesn't settle a answer uniquely. Two questions are unanswerable-by-design (correct response = abstain), and every stored answer declares a comparison mode (exact value / set / ordered sequence / per-key mapping / abstention).

The internal-database generator takes question lists plus the completed graph. Graph-backed tables must reuse canonical identifiers and copy shared values; a validator rejects designs that create second identities or "unanswerable" questions the schema can actually answer. Dirty values (inconsistent casing, placeholder text) are injected last, following BIRD/Spider 2.0's observation that real difficulty lives in the data, while protecting graph-backed keys. The final answer key is computed only after all rows exist.

The evaluation harness provisions an isolated copy of the frozen scenario per run, exposes read-only tools, equal budgets, and verifies serving correctness before asking questions (a simulator defect yields "not asked", not a model failure). Grading is mechanical — no partial credit, no LLM judge.

## Results & Highlights

- **Realism improvement under the scorecard**: across the same 23 generated companies, mean realism score rose from 61.8 to 97.0 (worst company 48.6 → 92.8; later passes reached 98.6), and the adversarial detector's flagged-record share fell from 55.2% to zero. The 61.8 baseline was recomputed under the final scorecard version, so the delta compares two generators under one instrument.
- **Nine-model comparison on the frozen `segment_fintech_small` estate (33 questions × 3 repeats, 891 total answers)**: accuracy ranged from 42.4% (gpt-5.4-mini) to 76.8% (claude-opus-4.8), a 34.3-point spread. Runners-up: gpt-5.6-sol 69.7%, claude-opus-5 and claude-sonnet-5 both 67.7%, qwen3-coder-next 55.6%.
- **Statistical honesty**: with 33 questions, 95% intervals are wide (claude-opus-4.8: [61.6, 90.9]; gpt-5.4-mini: [27.3, 58.6]); only 3 of 36 pairwise differences survived correction — claude-opus-4.8 > gpt-5.4-mini by 34.3 pts, gpt-5.6-sol > gpt-5.4-mini by 27.3 pts, claude-sonnet-4.6 > gpt-5.4-mini by 21.2 pts. All other orderings are inconclusive.
- **Capability breakdown (averaged over the nine models)**: simple filtering 92.6% vs cross-system 39.6%, ranking/order-sensitive 11.1%, temporal 7.4%, superlatives 6.2%, and multi-hop 3.7% — difficulty concentrates in exhaustive aggregation, multi-hop joins, temporal summaries, and ordered rankings rather than retrieval. No model answered the median-ticket-resolution-time question correctly.
- **Error anatomy over 891 runs**: 561 correct, 119 wrong, 99 false abstentions, 6 malformed, 106 ended before an answer; total list-price inference cost was $343.27. False abstention is nontrivial even for leaders (gpt-5.5: 17/99; claude-opus-4.8: 10/99).
- The three universally hard questions were: longest Gong call, total Gong call time, and ticket-priority distribution for the account with most tickets; for 7 of 9 models the five hardest included the composed Salesforce+Gong question (find account with largest open pipeline, then sum its recorded call time).

## Authors & Credibility

All six authors (Benjamin Gruenbaum, Doron Porat, Assaf Natanzon, Roy Zavida, Chen Dinachi, Or Itzahary) are at Eon, a company whose business is enterprise-data integration (contact: assaf@eon.io); this is a preprint under review, effectively an industry benchmark paper. The related-work coverage is genuinely deep — it correctly positions against τ-bench (pass^k, hand-written DBs), CRMArena/CRMArena-Pro (single Salesforce org), TheAgentCompany (best agent completes ~a quarter of tasks), WorkArena/++ (ServiceNow browser agents), and the text-to-SQL (BIRD, Spider 2.0) and synthetic-data-fidelity (SDV/SDMetrics, Synthea, Hollywood) literatures, adopting concrete lessons (dirty values, unanswerable questions graded on abstention, boosted-tree two-sample tests over the blind linear variant). The validation methodology shows real measurement sophistication (held-out seeds to prevent generator-overfitting; recomputing the baseline under the final scorecard; noise floor for the classifier test). The commercial stake means claims should be read as coming from the benchmark's vendor.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The one-graph-many-projections architecture with computed answer keys is a real advance over hand-built environments; the question-conditioned internal-DB generator with near-miss planting is clever and, to my knowledge, new in agent benchmarking. But the paper's own positioning shows the components (simulators, dirty data, abstention questions, realism scoring) are assembled from prior work rather than invented here.
- **Rigor**: Strong on generation and validation, weaker on the headline evaluation. The model comparison covers exactly one estate (`segment_fintech_small`) and 33 of 67 candidate questions — 8 dropped for unsupported operations and 26 document/unstructured questions not exposed in this track (exclusions fixed before running, but the result is a curated Gong+Salesforce+Zendesk subset). The paper does not report results for the internal-database or document tracks at all, despite devoting most of its methodology to them.
- **Reproducibility**: The entity-graph/simulator half is fully deterministic with a CI byte-identity check, which is exemplary. However, the internal-database generator "is an internal development tool: clients receive the finished estate and answer keys, not the generator itself" — so the question-conditioned generation pipeline cannot be independently reproduced or audited. The realism targets are partly drawn from Eon's own operational data and partly marked estimates; the scorecard measures agreement with these targets, not their truth (the authors admit this circularity risk).
- **Significance**: The 61.8→97.0 realism jump is the paper's most convincing number, but the scorecard and generator were co-developed by the same authors (improvement guided by the instrument, then evaluated on it) — the held-out-seed protocol and single-scorecard recompute mitigate but don't eliminate this. The model-ranking contribution is thin: with 33 questions and 3 repeats, 33 of 36 pairwise comparisons are inconclusive, so the table mainly demonstrates the benchmark's uncertainty reporting rather than separating models.
- **Red flags**: Single-estate evaluation with wide confidence intervals risks cherry-picked-feeling headlines (e.g., "multi-hop 3.7%") derived from small question counts per capability bucket; the $343.27 total inference cost over 891 runs (~$0.39/run) is oddly precise given "list-price" inference. Template-generated text has an acknowledged ceiling — the authors concede a stronger detector might separate it from human writing, which tempers the "zero records flagged" claim.

## Strengths

- Exact, computed ground truth eliminates the answer-key maintenance and data/answer disagreement problems of hand-built benchmarks; grading is fully mechanical (no LLM judge, no partial credit).
- Cross-system consistency by construction (single entity graph, deterministic projections) makes multi-system questions well defined and verifiable — backed by an explicit reachability audit that caught a genuine join defect.
- The validation suite is unusually thoughtful: lazy-solver panels to detect shortcut-answerable questions, near-miss planting, boosted-tree dependency tests with a measured noise floor, cardinality-estimation error, and an adversarial detector that actively hunts for generation artifacts.
- Cheap extension: a new scenario is just industry + size + seed; a new question template applies to all 83 scenarios automatically.
- Statistical honesty about what 33-question evaluations can and cannot establish, plus fairness controls (isolation, read-only data plane, equal budgets, serving verification before grading).

## Limitations

- The reported evaluation covers one generated company, 33 question shapes, three systems, and only the simulator track; internal-database and document questions exist in the benchmark but go unevaluated in the paper.
- Realism targets rest partly on Eon operational data and marked estimates; a wrong target pulls the generator toward a wrong distribution, and the scorecard cannot detect that.
- Generated text comes from seeded templates with slot-filling — variety and record-level coherence pass the current checks, but the ceiling below human idiosyncrasy is acknowledged.
- The internal-database generator is not released (clients get finished estates), limiting external audit of the question→schema→records pipeline.
- Twelve composed questions and two abstention questions are a small base for the capability-bucket percentages (3.7% multi-hop etc.), so those breakdowns carry large sampling noise.

## Relevance

这篇论文与 LLM agent 的工具使用评测高度相关：它提供了一个有精确 ground truth、跨系统 join 良定义、可确定性复现的企业级 agent 基准，直接回答"agent 在真实业务系统上到底能答对多少事实性问题"这一问题。其"生成环境 + 计算答案键 + 分机制验证合成数据"的方法论对任何需要构造可控评测环境的 agent 研究都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How do the unevaluated tracks behave — what is model accuracy on the internal-database questions (with planted near-misses and dirty values) and on the document/unstructured track excluded from the reported comparison?
- Would the 3.7% multi-hop and 6.2% superlative averages hold across multiple estates, industries, and size tiers, or are they artifacts of one fintech-small company's 33 questions?
- Can the realism scorecard's targets be replaced with third-party auditable statistics, so the 97.0 score is not anchored to Eon's own operational data?
- Since agents must discover ~290 MCP tools via three meta-tools, how much of the measured failure is tool-discovery overhead vs. genuine data reasoning — and does providing a tool catalog change the capability profile?
- Does the pass^k-style consistency across the three repeats reveal instability patterns (e.g., gpt-5.5's 17/99 false abstentions) that single-accuracy numbers hide, and how do failure modes shift between day-1 and day-2 (stale/incremental) states?
- Could the question-conditioned generator be opened for external audit, and would an independent group planting their own questions reproduce the same design-validity outcomes?
