arXiv 2609.09134 07 Sep 2026 Salesforce AI Preprint · not peer-reviewed

Imitating expert trajectories after harness evolution backfires — correcting only the failing turn in the student's own rollouts composes safely.

Co-Evolving Harnesses and Models: On-Policy Correction Helps Weaker Models Catch Up Where Imitation Fails ↗
Zhou Yu · Bin Bi · Shiva Kumar Pentyala · Shubham Mehrotra · et al. (11 authors) agent-harness on-policy-correction imitation-learning LoRA-fine-tuning enterprise-agents
Key metric · mean regression from imitation
−14.9pts

Mean success falls 78.0% → 63.1% when the weaker model LoRA-imitates the stronger expert under its evolved harness — all 7 enterprise tasks regress, from −4.2 to −29.9.

Why this matters

Answers whether model-side adaptation stays safe after harness evolution — the key question for cheap, iterative enterprise-agent co-evolution pipelines.

⚠ Critical edge

The +1.7 fix is modest (student still 13.9 pts below expert), the two arms differ in data mixture — confounding on-policy-ness — and the LoRA-ceiling explanation is asserted, never tested.

Methodology · distilled

How the claim was isolated

  • Task suite. Seven verifiable enterprise agent tasks — payroll auditing, budget approval, stock alerting, IoT anomaly detection, Playwright browser automation, website management, code refactoring — on the Yang et al. [2026] benchmark, with two disclosed environment changes.
  • Harness evolution. GEPA-style search: a gemini-3.1-pro-preview meta-agent proposes failure-driven edits (system prompt, tools, hooks, context management); edits stick only if validation improves. $20/task 600 s/rollout 3 seeds
  • Cast. Student qwen3-coder-30b-a3b (30B/3B active); replication student gemma-4-26b-a4b; teacher gemini-3.1-pro-preview.
  • Imitation arm. LoRA-SFT on 1.0-scoring trajectories under the evolved harness — expert first, then self-passes; headline numbers averaged over 3 LoRA seeds.
  • Correction arm. Keep the student's own rollouts; an automated failure-locus step finds the single failing turn, the expert rewrites only that turn (best-of-3, judge-picked). Final set ≈ 500 rows = ~400 corrections + ~50 boundary self-passes.
  • Training / eval. LoRA rank 16/64 (best reported), 2 epochs, merged checkpoint on 2×H200, <1 h per run; test success = 3-run mean ± SEM.

Strengths
  • Crisp, surprising negative result (−4 to −30 pts across tasks) isolated by a clean control — the same recipe helps under h₀.
  • Mechanism beyond correlation: the adoption scan rules out "model quit the harness"; the six-category failure taxonomy pins the damage on planning drift; case studies make it vivid.
  • Cheap, composable fix: ~500 rows, best-of-3 judge filtering, under one hour of LoRA — stackable into an iterative co-evolution loop.
  • Honest reporting: disclosed environment changes, SEM over 3 runs, expert gap admitted, on-policy-correction prior work credited.
Limitations
  • Positive gain is small (+1.7 mean; only website management +5.6 clears its SEM) and leaves the student 13.9 pts below the expert.
  • Cross-family evidence is thin: the Gemma replication covers a single task (WebArena).
  • LLM-as-judge failure taxonomy with no reported human-agreement validation; no significance tests on per-task deltas.
  • Confounded arms: imitation vs correction sets differ in data mixture, not only on-policy-ness.
  • No baselines against RL post-training, DAgger-style relabeling, or the cited co-evolution systems; one expert, two students.
Questions for further exploration
  • Does the regression persist under full fine-tuning or larger corpora, or is it a LoRA-capacity artifact?
  • Would RL on task success (e.g., GRPO under h*) avoid planning drift, or is correction the safer signal?
  • Can harness evolution be made fine-tuning-aware — optimizing the scaffold for the post-adaptation model?
  • How does regression severity scale with the student–expert capability gap?
  • Is single-turn correction optimal, or do 2–3 corrected turns compound across rounds?
  • Does the LLM-judge taxonomy agree with human labels — is the 14.6% planning share stable?
