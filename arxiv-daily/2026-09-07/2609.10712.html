An Open Recipe for IMO Gold: Training Nemotron for Olympiad Mathematics
Ivan Moshkov, Stephen Ge, George Armstrong, Wei Du, et al. (NVIDIA, 6 authors) · 2026-09-07 · graded by official IMO graders

Key Metric
30 / 42
Official IMO 2026 score — clears the gold-medal cutoff of 29, with full credit on P1, P2, P4, P5.
All six finalists cleared final selection within 100 minutes of the 4.5-hour sessions.
Why This Matters

First weights + data + code open system at IMO gold level — and it quantifies when ensemble diversity with unanimous verification beats brute test-time scaling.

Reusable open verifier/generator checkpoints and a contamination-safe 200-problem benchmark for RL post-training and inference-system research.
Critical Edge

The “open” recipe is distilled from DeepSeek-V4-Pro and scored by a closed-model jury — and the headline ensemble-vs-single comparison is not compute-matched.

Both internal verifier and independent jury over-credited P3/P6 by 2 points vs official human graders.

01 · MethodThree specialists, one loop

Nemotron 3 Ultra (550B-A55B) post-trained into two specialists — SFT and RL — that join the base GA checkpoint as generator, verifier, and refiner.

SFT specialist. Long-context fine-tune (425,984-token max sequences) on 414,890 synthetic traces — proof generation, refinement, verification, meta-verification — distilled from DeepSeek-V4-Pro over 15,879 hard AoPS problems; step-1,300 checkpoint selected on 133 proof problems.

RL specialist. Asynchronous NeMo-RL training on 9,597 problems in the base model’s learnable band (solved 1–3 of 4 attempts); DeepSeekMath-V2 reward with self-analysis removed (α=1, β=0); judged accuracy rose ~46–48% → ~56–58% over ~60 steps.

Strengths

  • Officially graded IMO 2026 gold (30/42) — externally validated, not self-reported.
  • Genuinely open release: both checkpoints, training data (CC BY 4.0), code, submitted proofs, and a contamination-safe 200-problem benchmark.
  • Verifier operating-point rigor with bootstrap CIs and explicit asymmetric error-cost reasoning.
  • Clean ablation: 64 SFT attempts add more coverage than 128 extra RL attempts at comparable token cost.
  • Honest failure appendix — including triage pre-filtering discarding the round-1 ancestor of accepted proofs in 44% of traced cases.
  • Per-problem token and GPU-hour accounting, rare in this literature.

Limitations

  • Ensemble-vs-single headline (Table 2) is not compute-matched; the gap partly reflects budget.
  • Small 30-problem dev set, two-thirds from the authors’ own benchmark, difficulty banded by their own model.
  • Closed-model dependence: DeepSeek-V4-Pro generated the corpus, GPT-5.5/Gemini/Claude jury scored every ablation.
  • ~4,800 GB200 GPU-hours for six problems, with no cost-matched comparison to closed competitors.
  • Gold margin is one point (30 vs 29); the stronger 33-point scenario is an unofficial post-hoc regrade.
  • 81.3% false-reject works only because this search design makes rejects cheap — may not transfer.

04 · Open QuestionsWhere this leads

  • How much SFT skill is DeepSeek-V4-Pro distillation rather than Nemotron’s own — would a self-generated corpus work, at what cost?
  • Can the correlated verifier blind spot (symmetry arguments, quantifier scope) be characterized systematically?
  • Would a Lean spot-check of accepted proofs catch the 1/25 false accept that no 24-judgment unanimity rule could?
  • Does diversity-beats-scaling hold for 70B-class or smaller models with lower generation diversity?
  • What is the marginal value of additional rounds — did the 8-round budget leave P3 points on the table?
  • Can jury evaluation be calibrated against official human grading, given the ~2-point systematic optimism?
