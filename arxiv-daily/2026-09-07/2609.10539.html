Why this matters

Quantifies the overlooked gap between research ideas and faithful implementations — a reusable benchmark for building agents that ask questions instead of silently guessing.

Distilled from Relevance

Critical edge

The headline “14% → 98%” is an oracle upper bound — the gold resolution was supplied, not discovered. Skimming readers may over-credit current systems.

Also: 5 of 6 frontier-model pair comparisons are not statistically reliable — read the leaderboard as tiers, not orderings.

01 · Methodology

660 single-defect specs, built from real reproducibility failures

Each instance pairs a NOT-READY spec with its READY counterpart — exactly one implementation-critical detail removed — plus taxonomy labels, an evidence-backed resolution, and a gold clarification action.

Models often guess a plausible category without recovering the actual unresolved decision: 66.3% of Task-2 predictions target a different blocker. Task 1 is shaky too — GPT-5.6-Sol wrongly accepts 31% of underspecified specs and rejects 34% of ready ones.

Quietly important: code can pass every unit test while implementing a different methodological choice — 45% pass-all-tests vs only 30% target fidelity under direct generation.

03 · Strengths

What the paper gets right

  • +“Codification readiness” names and operationalizes a real failure mode: silently implementing a different method than the one an idea specifies.
  • +Discovery vs. action surgically decoupled; the paired Defect-Guided / End-to-End experiment turns a benchmark observation into a causal bottleneck claim.
  • +Instances grounded in real reproducibility failures, with unusually thorough validation — blind human studies, κ, uniqueness audit, source-clustered CIs, executable fidelity check.
  • +Test-passing ≠ faithfulness: a quietly important result for anyone equating unit-test success with implementation correctness.

04 · Limitations

Where to hold skepticism

  • −163 real instances from 3 source families concentrated in AI/NLP/ML — the 9.6% headline generalizes to ML papers, not all research software.
  • −Small studies: oracle N=50, executable N=20 — the striking deltas (98% ready, 90% fidelity) carry wide confidence intervals.
  • −Clarification is evaluated as a single action with the answer supplied; multi-turn asking, artifact inspection, and knowing when to stop remain open.
  • −Instance construction and judging lean on frontier commercial models; contamination and construct-shift risks are not analyzed.
  • −Single-defect abstraction: real specs often have interacting gaps; ecological validity only partially tested.

05 · Open questions

Where this leads next

  1. Q1Can agent scaffolds — iterative self-questioning, artifact inspection, repo retrieval — close the 67-point gap, or is blocker discovery a fundamental perception limit?
  2. Q2Does training on single-defect instances transfer to naturally multi-defect specs, given the paper's own ablation was underpowered?
  3. Q3Would the 10-category defect taxonomy survive re-annotation outside AI/ML — in other computational sciences?
  4. Q4If oracle fixes lift tests 45→85% but fidelity 30→90%, what test suites discriminate faithful implementations from plausible-but-wrong ones? Can such tests be generated automatically?
  5. Q5Is the 9.6% DRR dominated by fine-grained blockers — how do defect granularity, model scale, and reasoning budget interact?
