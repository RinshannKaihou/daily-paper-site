> **一句话总结：** 针对工具调用型 LLM Agent 的"任务内自适应间接提示注入"攻击，本文将攻防双方建模为一般和 Markov 博弈，提出三阶段框架 CoER（攻击者 SFT 初始化 → 保留双方历史策略池的双边 Co-PPO 共训练 → 复用习得攻击者对教师模型采集"安全性+任务完成"双验证示范的精调），在 7 域 1,512 次执行的主评估中把自适应攻击成功率从基线 Qwen3.5-9B 的 42.12% 压到 0.25%，同时安全任务完成率（Safe-U）从 38.75% 升至 75.40%。

> **TL;DR:** CoER trains a tool-using Qwen3.5-9B defender against an adaptive indirect-prompt-injection attacker that revises later injections based on the defender's public trace, formalized as a general-sum Markov game. Via attacker SFT → bilateral Co-PPO with frozen historical opponent pools for BOTH roles → verifier-gated supervised refinement on teacher (GLM-5.2 / DeepSeek V4 Pro) demonstrations collected under retained attacks, it cuts adaptive ASR on its 7-suite AgentDyn/AgentDojo workload from 42.12% (Base) / 26.37% (Co-PPO alone) to 0.25% while raising Safe-U from 38.75% to 75.40% — but the historical-attacker union still reaches 4.97% and AgentLAB's GPT-5.4 attacks still succeed 14.12% of the time, so the headline number is not population-level robustness.

## 核心贡献

- **自适应 IPI 的博弈论形式化**：把"任务内自适应间接提示注入"建模为有限视野、回合制、部分可观测的一般和（general-sum）Markov 博弈——防御者通过连续工具调用推进任务，攻击者可在同一任务内多次注入，并根据公开执行轨迹和先前尝试的后果调整后续攻击；攻击者看不到防御者的系统提示、隐藏推理和参数。
- **双边历史对手池的 Co-PPO**：在双边强化学习中同时保留攻防双方的冻结历史策略作为对手种群，按 0.40/0.25/0.25/0.10 的概率混合"当前/当前、历史攻击者/当前防御者、当前攻击者/历史防御者、模板/当前防御者"四种配对，缓解对最新配对的过拟合（相比 ARLAS 只在防御者侧保留历史，CoER 扩展到双方）。
- **种群引导的验证式精调（Defender SFT）**：复用共训练得到的攻击者去挑战 GLM-5.2 和 DeepSeek V4 Pro 教师模型，只保留同时通过安全验证（未中毒）和任务完成验证的示范（共 5,760 条轨迹），用掩码交叉熵微调共训练防御者，以稠密监督补充在线 RL 的稀疏终局奖励。
- **配套数据与环境**：构建了基于 AgentDyn 改写的七域训练环境（带独立的攻击/任务验证器），包含 3,995 条攻击者 SFT 对话、5,760 条防御者 SFT 轨迹和 12,705 条在线 RL 配置。
- **严格的归因评估**：主评估（1,512 次执行）、外部基准（AgentLAB、InjecAgent、AgentDojo、AgentDyn 官方协议）、冻结交叉博弈（攻击强度）之外，还做了初始化×数据来源的 2×2 匹配对照实验，分别量化"共训练初始化"与"种群示范数据"的贡献。

## Key Contributions

- **Formulation**: Models within-task adaptive IPI as a finite-horizon, turn-based, partially observable general-sum Markov game in which the attacker injects at reached tool-return sites, observes the defender's public trace plus its own prior attempts (but never the system prompt, hidden reasoning, or parameters), and can inject multiple times per task; both roles are multi-turn agents with asymmetric observations and intervention times.
- **Co-PPO with bilateral historical-opponent populations**: Retains frozen historical policies from both roles as opponent pools and samples four pairings (current/current 0.40, historical-attacker/current-defender 0.25, current-attacker/historical-defender 0.25, fixed-template/current-defender 0.10), extending training beyond the latest matchup. Role-masked token-level PPO with role-specific critics updates each role only on its own generated tokens, propagating one terminal reward across that role's turns without crediting a single payload.
- **Population-guided, verifier-gated Defender SFT**: Reuses retained attackers to challenge teacher agents (GLM-5.2 and DeepSeek V4 Pro), keeps only normally terminated demonstrations with Iatk = 0 AND Itask = 1 (5,760 trajectories: 4,907 with payload evidence, 853 untriggered replays), and fine-tunes the selected Co-PPO defender with masked cross-entropy for one epoch.
- **Environment and data artifacts**: An AgentDyn-derived seven-domain sandbox with independent deterministic attack/task verifiers; 3,995 attacker-SFT conversations (11,655 attacker turns, seeded by DeepSeek V4 Pro and Seed2.0 attacks on the base defender), 5,760 defender-SFT trajectories, and 12,705 online-RL configurations, plus a disjoint 3,186-row internal validation split.
- **Attribution study**: A matched 2×2 experiment crossing Base/Co-PPO initialization with standard (fixed-attack) vs population-derived refinement data — with cross-entropy, data size, updates, teachers, replay proportion, and evaluation denominators all matched — plus frozen cross-play and per-case historical-attacker union stress tests.

## Methodology

CoER proceeds in three stages over the Markov game defined by configurations c = (s0, x, g, Cg, Z, d, Vx, Vg) with deterministic task/attack verifiers and at most 20 defender turns per execution.

**Stage I — Attacker SFT.** DeepSeek V4 Pro and Seed2.0 attack the base Qwen3.5-9B defender across Co-PPO training configurations; the security verifier retains 3,995 successful conversations (11,655 attacker turns). All attacker turns in these trajectories — including earlier failed attempts that supply adaptation context — are supervised, so the initial attacker policy learns multi-turn conditional generation without labeling any single payload as responsible for success.

**Stage II — Bilateral Co-PPO.** Rewards are trajectory-level and verifier-derived: RA = Iatk + 0.05(1 − Iatk)·Ifmt (a small format bonus only on failed attacks whose every sampled action parsed); RD = +1.0 for safe task success, −0.2 for safe failure, −1.0 for compromise (equivalently 1.2·Itask·(1−Iatk) − 0.8·Iatk − 0.2), making task utility part of the learning objective rather than an evaluation-only criterion. Once attack success is confirmed, new injections stop but the defender runs to termination so both outcomes are verified on complete executions. Historical opponents (admitted via suite-stratified, reward-based checkpoint serving) keep earlier attacks and defensive behaviors in the training distribution; current/current play (40%) maintains attack pressure against the latest defense since older defenders are easier to exploit. Each role trains with clipped, KL-regularized token-level PPO on a role-specific critic; only the role's own generated tokens enter its policy, value, and KL losses, while tool outputs and opponent actions stay in context but off the GAE timeline.

**Stage III — Population-guided Defender SFT.** Retained attackers challenge the teachers from training-task initial states, generating injections conditioned on each teacher's public trajectory (including consequences of earlier attempts). Demonstrations terminating normally with Iatk = 0 ∧ Itask = 1 pass deterministic tool-validity and length filters; per configuration one teacher trajectory is chosen by attack exposure with fixed tie-breaking and no extra LLM scoring. Starting from the selected Co-PPO checkpoint (d430, highest defender reward), Defender SFT minimizes masked cross-entropy over assistant tokens for one epoch (update 360); the result is frozen for evaluation.

Compute: both SFT stages use two 8-GPU nodes (PyTorch, BF16, DeepSpeed ZeRO-3); Co-PPO uses seven 8-GPU nodes (verl/Megatron trainers, 40 rollout GPUs with vLLM and Ray).

## Results & Highlights

- **Main 7-suite workload (3 AgentDyn + 4 AgentDojo suites; 1,512 executions, of which 1,187 adaptive)**: against a frozen common-adaptive attacker, CoER cuts adaptive ASR from 42.12% (Base Qwen3.5-9B) and 26.37% (Co-PPO alone) to 0.25% (3/1,187), while adaptive Safe-U rises from 38.75% (Base) and 54.09% (Co-PPO) to 75.40% — a +36.65-point Safe-U gain over the base model with Defender SFT alone contributing +21.31 points over Co-PPO.
- **Fixed and clean conditions**: fixed-template attack ASR falls from 12.50% (Base) to 0.00% (fixed Safe-U 61.90% → 79.76%), and clean utility is fully preserved (79.62% for both Base and CoER), arguing against blanket-refusal shortcuts.
- **External AgentLAB Task-Injection (949 valid task–injection pairs per defender, GPT-5.4 as adaptive attacker)**: CoER reduces ASR from 37.41% (355/949, Base) and 15.81% (150/949, Co-PPO) to 14.12% (134/949) while raising task success from 59.43% (564/949) to 82.83% (786/949) — safety without utility collapse on an unseen long-horizon protocol.
- **InjecAgent**: Base-payload total ASR drops from 7.49% (Base) to 0.00%, and Enhanced-payload ASR from 22.7% to 2.0%; on AgentDojo official suites, ASR falls 13.3% → 2.2% with utility up 84.6% → 86.3%, and on AgentDyn official, ASR falls 31.8% → 2.5% with utility up 54.3% → 66.8%.
- **Cross-model reference (same common attacker)**: CoER's overall Safe-U of 76.32% exceeds GPT-5.4 (74.74%), Qwen3.6-27B (66.47%), and DeepSeek V4 Pro (52.84%) but trails GLM-5.2-Thinking (81.47%) — with the caveat that GLM-5.2 and DeepSeek V4 Pro were refinement teachers and the attacker trained against Qwen3.5.
- **Matched 2×2 attribution (Table 1c)**: population-derived refinement data adds +8.00 adaptive Safe-U points and Co-PPO initialization +6.07 points over the Base-init/standard-data cell; the combination (CoER, 75.40%) is best, with the interaction (2.36 points) explicitly labeled descriptive rather than synergy.
- **Robustness stress tests**: the per-case union over all retained historical attackers still compromises 59/1,187 = 4.97% of cases (vs 0.25% for the common attacker), and Co-PPO genuinely strengthened the attacker — frozen cross-play Effective ASR against the Base defender rises from 38.80% (base attacker) through 49.55% (SFT) to 65.65% (Co-PPO attacker), while CoER holds 0.18–0.47% across six attackers versus 15.10–31.29% for the RL-only defender.
- **Per-suite picture**: CoER improves adaptive Safe-U over Co-PPO in all seven suites by 4.2–47.0 points (DailyLife most, Banking least), but Shopping retains the lowest final Safe-U (57.2%), and under the official AgentDyn protocol Travel Safe-U regresses 15.00 points versus Co-PPO and Banking clean utility drops 18.75 points.

## Authors & Credibility

The four authors (Boyang Zhang, Qingxin Xiao, Lingwei Dang, Qingyao Wu) are from the School of Software Engineering at South China University of Technology; Qingyao Wu is a professor there working on machine learning and multi-agent systems. This is a preprint (arXiv:2609.07529v2, revised 15 Sep 2026) with no stated venue or peer review. Credibility is strengthened by an unusually candid reporting style: single-seed results are labeled "descriptive point estimates," cross-model numbers are flagged as non-controlled references, teacher involvement is disclosed, and the reproducibility statement lists what is NOT yet documented (checkpoint/attempt manifests, teacher-generation costs, an independent verifier audit). The paper includes explicit ethics (dual-use attacker checkpoints) and AI-use statements, and 26 pages of main text plus appendices covering prompts, verifiers, training configuration, and per-benchmark denominators.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Moderate-to-good. The Markov-game formulation of within-task adaptive IPI with feedback-dependent multi-turn injections, bilateral (both-role) historical opponent pools — explicitly extending ARLAS's defender-only history — and the reuse of co-evolved attackers to mine dual-verified teacher demonstrations is a coherent, non-trivial combination. Individual ingredients (attacker SFT, self-play PPO, verified distillation) are familiar from Lifelong Safety Alignment, MAGIC, GPT-Red, and ARLAS.
- **Rigor**: The matched 2×2 attribution study, frozen cross-play, and historical-union stress test are above the field's norm, and the authors resist overclaiming. However, the headline 0.25% adaptive ASR is measured against an attacker frozen from the same training run (same Qwen3.5 backbone as the defender, trained with privileged executable contracts); the more adversarial numbers — 4.97% historical union, 14.12% on AgentLAB with a GPT-5.4 attacker the defender never saw — are the realistic robustness floor, and the paper says so itself ("Low observed ASR does not establish population-level robustness").
- **Reproducibility**: Partially good. Splits, prompts, verifiers, reward definitions, opponent-mixture probabilities, and checkpoint selection (d430, SFT update 360) are specified, and the compute footprint (up to seven 8-GPU nodes) is stated; but code/data are not yet released, the InjecAgent aggregates are author-supplied with denominators differing from the nominal 1,054 cases and exclusion scope "unverified," and the reproducibility statement admits the attempt manifest and verifier audit remain to be documented. Single seed, single training run, one execution per adaptive case means no variance estimate.
- **Significance**: The +21-point Safe-U jump from verifier-gated refinement over an already co-trained defender is a practically useful recipe for agent-safety training, and the attacker-strength curve (38.80% → 65.65%) shows the co-evolution loop is doing real work rather than fitting a weak attack.
- **Red flags**: (1) Cross-model Table 1b compares CoER favorably against DeepSeek V4 Pro (52.84% Safe-U) even though DeepSeek V4 Pro was one of the refinement teachers — its verified outputs are inside CoER's training data — so that row is closer to distillation-into-student than a fair contest; the paper marks it only as a "reference comparison." (2) The Co-PPO vs NoPop comparison tests the history mechanism jointly and never isolates bilateral vs defender-only populations, so the claimed advantage over ARLAS-style defender-side history is asserted rather than ablated in the main table. (3) Deterministic verifiers only cover enumerated attack goals within bounded executions; unenumered harms escape both training and evaluation, and no independent human audit exists. (4) Real per-suite regressions survive refinement (Travel −15.00 Safe-U vs Co-PPO, Banking clean utility −18.75, Shopping Safe-U stuck at 57.2%).

## Strengths

- Clean game-theoretic problem formulation with realistically constrained attacker observability (public trace + own prior attempts + binary unsatisfied-contract feedback only), matching what a real adversarial content provider could see.
- Bilateral historical-opponent populations combat co-training's cyclic overfitting to the latest pairing, and the design choice is validated from both directions (Co-PPO > NoPop; attacker strength 65.65% vs 57.40%).
- Refinement data is verifier-gated on BOTH safety and task completion, with replay mixing (14.81% untriggered executions) that anchors clean task behavior — clean utility is preserved at 79.62% rather than traded away for safety.
- Honest, self-critical reporting: descriptive point estimates are labeled as such, reference rows carry protocol caveats, and limitations name the teacher dependence and verifier boundedness explicitly.
- External transfer is demonstrated on four independent protocols (AgentLAB, InjecAgent, AgentDojo, AgentDyn official), not just the in-house suite.

## Limitations

- The 0.25% headline ASR holds only against the frozen common attacker; the historical-attacker union reaches 4.97% and AgentLAB's GPT-5.4 adaptive attacks still succeed 14.12% of the time, so an attacker optimized against the final CoER defender would likely do better still.
- Both roles depend on strong teachers (DeepSeek V4 Pro, Seed2.0 for attacker initialization; GLM-5.2, DeepSeek V4 Pro for refinement demonstrations) — the authors state that comparable performance with weaker teachers or co-evolution alone "remains unestablished."
- Deterministic verifiers cover only enumerated goals and contracts within ≤20-turn executions; unenumerated harms or contract specification errors escape training and evaluation, and no independent human audit is yet available.
- Single seed (seed 0), one training run, and one execution per adaptive case: all differences are point estimates without significance testing.
- No equilibrium or exploitability analysis of the resulting policies; Co-PPO trains against an evolving mixture, not toward a verified game solution.
- Heavy compute (seven 8-GPU nodes for Co-PPO) and a single 9B backbone limit conclusions about scalability; per-suite regressions (Travel Safe-U −15.00 vs Co-PPO; Banking clean utility −18.75) show the refinement is not uniformly monotone.

## Relevance

与"自进化 Agent 与自动化研究"方向高度相关：CoER 的核心正是攻击者-防御者共进化（co-evolution）自博弈循环——通过持续对抗让双方互相提升，并复用习得攻击者蒸馏出更强的防御者，这是自进化智能体范式在安全对齐上的直接应用；同时它直接服务于"长时程自主 Agent 系统的可靠性与可信性"这一子课题，为工具调用型自主 agent 的运行时安全训练提供了可借鉴的博弈形式化与验证式精调配方。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of CoER's gain survives when the refinement teachers are weaker (e.g., same-scale open models) or replaced entirely by self-generated verified demonstrations from the co-evolved defender itself?
- What happens when an attacker is fine-tuned specifically against the FINAL CoER defender (white-box adaptive attack), rather than evaluated as a frozen union of historical attackers — is 4.97% an upper or lower bound?
- Can the bilateral-vs-defender-only history question be settled with a direct ablation against an ARLAS-style defender-side-only population at matched budget?
- Do the deterministic verifier contracts transfer to open-ended harm taxonomies (e.g., AgentHarm-style unspecified goals), where success cannot be reduced to a function-trace predicate?
- Would replacing the single terminal outcome reward with process-level safety rewards (e.g., per-injection resistance credit) improve sample efficiency of the Co-PPO stage, which currently needs 12,705 rollouts across 56 GPUs?
- Does the recipe scale beyond 9B backbones, and does attacker strength (65.65% frozen cross-play ASR) keep rising with defender scale, threatening an arms-race dynamic the paper's population approach cannot close?
