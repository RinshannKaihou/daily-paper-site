Why this matters

A distribution-level fidelity check for quantized LLMs — pick the right 4-bit scheme before edge deployment, not after users feel the damage.

为量化 LLM 的部署前评估提供超越准确率的分布级保真度诊断，可直接指导边缘设备上 4-bit 量化方案的选型。

Critical edge

No evidence links any TVD level to a downstream consequence — “TVD 0.56 is bad” remains intuition, and no actionable deployment threshold is offered.

Sharpened by near-chance MMLU baselines (28.8–33.5%): several “stable accuracy” claims rest on a regime where accuracy carries almost no signal.

How they measured it — dual-stream evaluation

  • Same prompt, two models. Every quantized checkpoint is generated directly from its BF16 baseline via llama.cpp (identical tokenizer & vocabulary — only parameter precision differs).
  • Compare full distributions, not answers. At the token decision boundary, both full-vocabulary softmax outputs (T = 1.0, no truncation) are compared with bounded, symmetric distances — TVD and JSD — deliberately avoiding KL’s unboundedness near zero probabilities.
  • TVD has a physical reading: the fraction of probability mass that must be reassigned (TVD 0.25 = 25% of mass moved).
  • Task metrics run in parallel (accuracy, F1, BF16→quantized flip rates) so fidelity and performance can be read side by side. Fixed plain-text templates, deterministic inference (seed = 42), single V100 32GB, VRAM tracked per config.
  • Scope is honest: this measures reference fidelity — not correctness, calibration, or safety.
Theorem 1 · Coarse-graining Any answer-level aggregation is a stochastic coarse-graining operator K, and TVD(KP, KQ) ≤ TVD(P, Q), same for JSD — so standard answer-subset metrics can only understate true vocabulary-level drift. Full-vocabulary TVD/JSD are strict upper-bound diagnostics.

Read it with both eyes open

Strengths

  • Clean framing: argmax accuracy is a lossy, non-invertible projection; bounded TVD/JSD restore the discarded signal with a direct probability-mass interpretation.
  • The coarse-graining theorem (correct log-sum-inequality proof) makes full-vocabulary analysis principled: answer-level metrics are provable lower bounds.
  • Fully transparent 120-config matrix — every number published, VRAM tracked — enabling a practical memory-vs-fidelity Pareto plot.
  • Q4_K vs Q4_0 at matched memory and accuracy is immediately actionable for llama.cpp edge deployments.
  • Consistent intellectual honesty: claims repeatedly scoped to “reference fidelity, not correctness/calibration/safety.”

Limitations

  • Base models only, fixed plain-text templates — no instruction-tuned/RLHF models or chat formats, i.e. not how LLMs are actually deployed.
  • Single-token, prompt-conditioned divergence: multi-step generation drift — what users actually experience — is out of scope.
  • One quantization ecosystem (llama.cpp/GGUF); no AWQ, GPTQ, or SmoothQuant comparison.
  • No external validation: TVD/JSD never correlated with calibration error, generation quality, or human judgment; no deployment threshold proposed.
  • Weak-evidence regimes: near-chance MMLU, majority-class collapse in 1B/2B models, and the striking Teuken-7B/Q4_0 fragility is reported but never ablated.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does first-token TVD predict multi-step generation divergence or downstream quality (ROUGE/BERTScore drop, chain-of-thought error rate)?
  2. Why is Teuken-7B so much more fragile to uniform Q4_0 (TVD ~0.54–0.56) than Mistral-7B (~0.03) — vocabulary size (~250K multilingual tokens), tokenizer structure, or outlier weights? Can it be predicted a priori?
  3. Do findings transfer to instruction-tuned/RLHF-aligned models and chat templates, where the decision boundary sits after long system prompts?
  4. Can a practical threshold be established — the TVD level beyond which calibration (ECE), safety alignment, or user-perceived quality measurably degrades?
  5. Do AWQ, GPTQ, and SmoothQuant show the same uniform-vs-mixed-precision gap, or is the Q4_K advantage specific to llama.cpp’s K-quant super-block design?
