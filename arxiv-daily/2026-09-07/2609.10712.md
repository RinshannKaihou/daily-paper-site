> **一句话总结：** NVIDIA 基于 Nemotron 3 Ultra（550B-A55B）训练 SFT 与 RL 两个专种子模型，配合"生成-验证-精炼"迭代搜索与高算力终选流水线，在 IMO 2026 官方比赛中以纯自然语言（无形式化证明器、无外部工具、无联网）拿到 30/42 分达到金牌线（金牌线 29 分），并开源了两个后训练检查点、训练数据、推理代码、提交证明及 200 题新基准 Nemotron-IMO-Bench。

> **TL;DR:** NVIDIA post-trains two specialists (SFT + RL) on top of Nemotron 3 Ultra and combines three checkpoints in an iterative natural-language generate-verify-refine search with a separate high-compute final-selection stage; the system scored 30/42 at IMO 2026 (gold cutoff: 29), with full credit on Problems 1/2/4/5 and 1/7 on Problems 3/6, consuming ~4,800 GB200 GPU-hours and ~2.31B generated tokens. On their 30-problem development set the ensemble reaches 188 points vs 180 for the best single checkpoint (RL), and the deployed RL+SFT unanimous 16-judge verifier panel achieves a 1.1% false-accept rate vs 4.5% for SFT alone. Everything — checkpoints, training data, code, submitted proofs, and the 200-problem Nemotron-IMO-Bench — is released openly, but the training-data generation (DeepSeek-V4-Pro) and ablation scoring (GPT-5.5/Gemini 3.1 Pro/Claude Opus 4.8 jury) lean on closed frontier models, and the headline ensemble-vs-single comparison is not compute-matched.

## 核心贡献

- **IMO 2026 官方金牌成绩**：系统以 30/42 分超过金牌线（29 分），全部证明由官方 IMO 评分员评定；P1、P2、P4、P5 满分（各 7 分），P3、P6 各得 1 分。全程纯自然语言推理，不使用 Lean 等形式化证明器、外部工具或互联网。
- **两个后训练专种子模型**：从 Nemotron-3-Ultra-GA 出发，(1) 长上下文 SFT（最大序列长度 425,984 token，语料 414,890 条覆盖证明生成/精炼/验证/元验证四类轨迹）；(2) 基于 NeMo-RL 的异步强化学习（9,597 个题目，沿用 DeepSeekMath-V2 奖励设计并去掉自分析奖励）。
- **系统级实证研究**：在 30 题开发集上系统消融了检查点选择、验证器组合规则（16 票一致 vs 放宽阈值）、轮次预算分配（多检查点 vs 单检查点加倍采样），并给出误接受/误拒绝率的 Bootstrap 置信区间。
- **完整开源发布**：两个后训练检查点（OpenMDW-1.1）、SFT/RL 训练数据（CC BY 4.0）、训练与推理代码（NeMo-Skills / NeMo-RL）、提交的全部证明，以及与 Titu Andreescu 教授合作编写的 200 题全新奥赛基准 Nemotron-IMO-Bench（CC BY 4.0）——刻意保证题目未出现在任何模型训练数据中。
- **透明的资源核算**：逐题报告生成 token 数与 GB200 GPU 时数（提交证明就绪约 707M token / 1,464 GPU 时；完整运行约 2.31B token / 4,800 GPU 时）。

## Key Contributions

- **Official IMO 2026 gold-medal result**: 30/42 points (gold cutoff: 29), all proofs graded by official IMO graders — full credit on P1, P2, P4, P5 and 1/7 on P3, P6 — achieved entirely in natural language with no formal prover, external tools, or internet access.
- **Two post-trained specialists**: (1) a long-context SFT checkpoint (425,984-token max sequence length) trained on a 414,890-example synthetic corpus spanning proof-generation (58,543), refinement (67,971), verification (236,360), and meta-verification (52,016) traces built from 15,818 unique hard AoPS problems; (2) an RL checkpoint trained with an asynchronous NeMo-RL pipeline on 9,597 problems, following DeepSeekMath-V2's reward design with the self-analysis reward removed (α=1, β=0).
- **Careful system-level ablations** on a 30-problem development set: single-checkpoint pipelines vs the full ensemble, verifier operating points (unanimity thresholds with 95% bootstrap intervals on a 300-proof audit set), and round-1 budget allocation showing a second checkpoint is worth more than doubling attempts from one checkpoint.
- **Genuinely open release**: both post-trained checkpoints (OpenMDW-1.1), the SFT/RL training data (CC BY 4.0), training and inference code, all submitted IMO proofs, and Nemotron-IMO-Bench — 200 novel olympiad problems co-authored with Prof. Titu Andreescu, guaranteed absent from any model's training data.
- **Detailed compute accounting**: per-problem token counts and GB200 GPU-hours (submitted proofs available at ~707M tokens / 1,464 GPU-hours; full run ~2.31B tokens / 4,800 GPU-hours), enabling cost comparisons with closed systems.

## Methodology

The system rests on three Nemotron-3-Ultra 550B-A55B checkpoints serving three pipeline roles: generation, verification, and refinement.

**SFT stage.** Starting from the GA checkpoint, the model is fine-tuned on a proof-focused corpus with a 425,984-token max sequence length (BF16, AdamW, peak LR 1.5e-5 with cosine decay to 2e-6, 512 GB200 GPUs, global batch 64). The corpus is built from 15,879 hard AoPS problems (selected by prior pass-rate evaluations): DeepSeek-V4-Pro in Max inference mode generates initial proofs (400K-token generation cap), unsolved problems go through up to three refinement rounds conditioning on prior attempts plus verifier feedback (350K-token cap), and parallel verifier/meta-verifier trajectories score proofs in {0, 0.5, 1}. Quality filtering removes malformed and length-capped generations and deterministically subsamples score-0 traces, yielding 414,890 examples. The checkpoint at step 1,300 was selected on 133 proof problems from IMO-ProofBench and recent competitions.

**RL stage.** Also starting from GA, RL uses 9,597 problems from Nemotron-Math-Proofs-v1 that GA solves in 1–3 of 4 attempts (as judged by DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale) — the learnable difficulty band. The reward largely follows DeepSeekMath-V2 but sets α=1, β=0, removing the self-analysis reward. Training uses an asynchronous PipelineRL-style setup on NeMo-RL with dynamic sampling and truncated importance sampling: 128 prompts × 16 trajectories = 2,048-trajectory batches, LR 3e-6, max trajectory age 4 steps, 128K-token sequences, on 128 trainer + 128 inference + 16 judge nodes (4 GB200 GPUs each). Evaluation (GPT-5.5 xhigh as judge) on 133 proof problems shows accuracy rising from roughly 46–48% to 56–58% over ~60 steps.

**Inference pipeline (two stages).** Stage 1 is an iterative generate-verify-refine search (max 8 rounds) inspired by DeepSeekMath-V2. Round 1 samples 384 attempts: each of the 3 checkpoints × 8 complementary strategy prompts (lemma-first decomposition, route comparison, counterexample search, etc.) × 16 samples. Verification is reference-free: the RL and SFT checkpoints each produce 8 judgments (16 total), and a proof is accepted only on unanimous 16/16 scores of 1. If nothing is accepted, up to 16 top-ranked proofs from the per-problem pool are refined using up to 8 verifier critiques, with 192 refinement attempts per round. Early stopping is per-checkpoint: once a checkpoint produces an accepted proof it stops generating, leaving up to 3 finalists. Stage 2 is final selection: every finalist receives 48 independent IMO-style 0–7 judgments (3 checkpoints × 16) under a reference-free judge prompt adapted from MathArena's methodology; finalists are ranked by mean score with ties broken toward the shorter proof. Notably, search-time verification optimizes for actionable critique while final selection optimizes IMO-style scoring — deliberately different objectives.

**Evaluation infrastructure.** The 30-problem development set (20 problems from Nemotron-IMO-Bench + 10 from recent competitions; 3 easy / 7 medium / 10 hard / 10 unsolved; 6 algebra, 8 combinatorics, 8 geometry, 8 number theory) is scored by an independent jury of GPT-5.5, Gemini 3.1 Pro, and Claude Opus 4.8 using a MathArena-style reconciliation procedure (minimum score if initial disagreement ≤2, else second-round minimum).

## Results & Highlights

- **Official IMO 2026 result**: 30/42 points vs the gold-medal cutoff of 29 — full credit (7/7) on Problems 1, 2, 4, and 5, and 1/7 on Problems 3 and 6. All submitted proofs were found within ~707M generated tokens and 1,464 GB200 GPU-hours; completing in-flight rounds brought the full run to ~2.31B tokens and 4,784.6 GPU-hours. All six finalists cleared the final-selection panel within 100 minutes of the 4.5-hour sessions.
- **Ensemble vs single checkpoints** (30-problem dev set, independent jury, R8 + fallback): Ensemble 188 points (25 problems accepted) vs 180 for Nemotron-3-Ultra-RL (23), 165 for SFT (21), and 162 for GA (23) — an 8-point end-to-end gain over the best single-checkpoint pipeline. The gap is wider mid-search: at round 2 the ensemble leads 160 (23) vs 98 (14) for SFT.
- **Verifier audit** (300-proof audit set, jury-score-7 as ground truth): the submitted RL+SFT unanimous 16/16 rule achieves a 1.1% false-accept rate [0.0, 2.7] vs 4.5% [0.6, 9.0] for SFT alone 8/8, 12.4% for RL alone, and 31.6% for GA alone — at the cost of an 81.3% false-reject rate, which is acceptable because a false reject only delays while a false accept terminates search on an invalid proof. Relaxing the threshold to 14/16 raises false accepts from ~1% to 17.0%.
- **Round-1 budget allocation** (Table 4): with ~1.55–1.59B tokens, RL 128 + SFT 64 accepts 15 problems (all-30 jury score 159) vs 14 problems (139) for doubling RL to 256 attempts — a second checkpoint solves problems the first cannot: of the 18 problems accepted by RL 128 + SFT 128, 5 are accepted only by SFT and 7 only by RL, while doubling RL attempts recovers just 1 of the 5 SFT-only problems.
- **Accepted-proof precision**: of 25 proofs accepted by the submitted panel on the dev set, 23 received a full jury score of 7, one scored 6 (a minor quantifier formality), and one scored 0 (an invalid column-permutation symmetry step refuted by an explicit counterexample — every checkpoint gave it 8 full-score judgments).
- **Verifier blind spot at IMO**: at the contest cutoff, both the internal verifier and the post-hoc independent model jury estimated ~32 points vs the official 30; the entire 2-point discrepancy comes from P3 and P6, where model-based evaluation credited proofs the official graders scored 1/7 — a correlated failure of model-based verification, not verifier-specific noise. A continued run found a better P6 proof after 8h25m (round 8, +610M tokens, +890 GPU-hours) that an unofficial independent human panel graded 4/7, which would have raised the total to 33 (unofficial).

## Authors & Credibility

The six authors (Ivan Moshkov, Stephen Ge, George Armstrong, Wei Du, Sadegh Mahdavi, Igor Gitman) are NVIDIA researchers; the work builds on the Nemotron 3 Ultra technical report and the NeMo-RL/NeMo-Skills infrastructure. Credibility is high: the headline number is an official IMO 2026 competition result graded by official IMO graders (not a self-evaluated benchmark), the resource accounting is unusually transparent (per-problem tokens and GPU-hours, Table 1), negative results are reported in a dedicated appendix (cross-proof context, zero-first ranking, diverse-proof refinement, and triage routing all failed to improve final performance), and the verifier audit includes 95% bootstrap confidence intervals from 2,000 problem-clustered resamples. The benchmark was co-authored with Titu Andreescu, a renowned olympiad problem author, and the acknowledgements credit experienced graders (including Branislav Kisacanin and Gabriel Dospinescu) for proof grading. The release is real and substantive: two checkpoints on Hugging Face, CC BY 4.0 training data, code in public GitHub repos, and the submitted solutions.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The individual ingredients (generate-verify-refine loops, verifier ensembles, test-time scaling) are adapted from DeepSeekMath-V2, Self-Reﬁne, MathArena, and the Huang & Yang (2025) model-agnostic pipeline; the novelty is in the careful system-level engineering, the open release, and the empirical isolation of design decisions rather than a new algorithm. This is honest about its recipe nature — the title literally says "recipe."
- **Rigor**: Mixed. Strengths: official grading, bootstrap CIs, a stratified 300-proof verifier audit, and Appendix A reporting approaches that did not work. Weaknesses: the headline ensemble-vs-single-checkpoint comparison (Table 2) is explicitly not compute-matched (the ensemble gets 384 round-1 attempts vs 128 per single checkpoint), with Table 4 serving as a partial proxy; the 30-problem dev set is small and 20 of its 30 problems come from the authors' own benchmark, with difficulty stratified by their own GA model's pass behavior; and Table 4's pools are judged by the RL checkpoint alone (more permissive than the deployed 2-model judge) — an inconsistency the paper acknowledges.
- **Reproducibility**: The strongest aspect — weights, data, code, prompts (Appendix B reproduces every template), and submitted proofs are all public. However, the "open" label has limits: the SFT corpus was generated by DeepSeek-V4-Pro in Max mode, RL problem filtering used DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale, RL evaluation used GPT-5.5 xhigh, and all ablation scoring used a closed-model jury (GPT-5.5/Gemini 3.1 Pro/Claude Opus 4.8). Nobody can regenerate the training data without those closed models, and every dev-set number inherits the jury's biases — biases the paper itself demonstrates: both the internal verifier and the independent jury over-credited P3 and P6 by a combined 2 points relative to official human graders.
- **Significance**: The first fully open (weights + data + code) natural-language system at IMO gold level is a real milestone for the open-weight ecosystem, and the finding that unanimity across two selectively-trained verifiers beats adding a third permissive one (GA) is a transferable lesson. But the margin over the cutoff is one point (30 vs 29), achieved with ~4,800 GB200 GPU-hours — an enormous per-problem cost with no compute comparison against closed competitors (Gemini Deep Think, OpenAI), whose budgets are undisclosed.
- **Red flags**: The P6 "4/7 unofficial regrade → 33 points" framing is post-hoc, unofficial, and performed without official marking schemes — it should not be conflated with the official 30. The SFT specialist is essentially distilling DeepSeek-V4-Pro, so claims of Nemotron's olympiad capability partly reflect the teacher model. The 1.1% false-accept rate comes with an 81.3% false-reject rate, workable only because this specific search design makes rejects cheap; and even the unanimous panel accepted one fully incorrect proof (the column-permutation counterexample case) that no rule over the 24 available judgments could catch.

## Strengths

- Officially graded IMO 2026 gold result (30/42) — externally validated rather than self-reported.
- Complete, genuinely open release: both post-trained checkpoints, training data, training/inference code, submitted proofs, and a contamination-safe 200-problem benchmark.
- Rigorous verifier operating-point analysis with bootstrap CIs, explicitly reasoning about asymmetric error costs (false accept terminates search; false reject only delays).
- Clean ablation showing ensemble diversity beats attempt-scaling: 64 SFT attempts add more coverage than 128 extra RL attempts at comparable token cost.
- Honest reporting of failures: Appendix A documents four approaches that did not improve the final system, including the striking triage result that aggressive pre-filtering discarded the round-1 ancestor of eventually-accepted proofs in 44% of traced cases (13 of 14 expert-graded discarded proofs were fully correct).
- Transparent per-problem compute and token accounting, rare in this literature.

## Limitations

- The ensemble vs single-checkpoint comparison is not compute-matched; the paper acknowledges this and partially compensates with Table 4, but the headline Table 2 gap partly reflects budget.
- The 30-problem development set is small, and two-thirds of it comes from the authors' own Nemotron-IMO-Bench with difficulty bands defined by their own model's behavior; jury-based scoring on such a set carries meaningful variance (bootstrap CIs on Table 3 span 10+ points for several rules).
- Deep dependence on closed frontier models for data generation (DeepSeek-V4-Pro), data filtering (DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale), and evaluation (GPT-5.5 jury) — the recipe is open in artifacts but not fully open in provenance, and evaluation inherits the demonstrated correlated blind spots of model-based verification.
- Compute cost is enormous (~4,800 GB200 GPU-hours for six problems) with no cost-matched comparison to closed IMO-2025/2026 systems; accessibility is limited to those with similar clusters.
- The gold margin is one point (30 vs 29 cutoff), and the stronger 33-point scenario rests on an unofficial post-hoc regrade without official marking schemes.
- The extremely selective verifier (81.3% false-reject) works only because of this search design's tolerance for rejects; the finding may not transfer to settings where verification gates are costlier or proofs cannot be resampled.

## Relevance

对研究 LLM 推理与测试时算力（test-time compute）的工作高度相关：本文系统性地量化了"多检查点集成 + 一致性验证 + 精炼循环"相对单模型扩展采样的收益，并提供了可复用的开源验证器/生成器检查点与无污染奥赛基准，对研究 RL 后训练、verifier 模型训练与推理系统工程都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of the SFT specialist's skill is distillation from DeepSeek-V4-Pro rather than Nemotron's own capability — would the recipe work with a self-generated (GA-only) corpus, and at what cost?
- Can the correlated verifier blind spot on P3/P6 (both internal panel and independent jury over-crediting by 2 points) be characterized systematically, e.g., are there proof-error classes (symmetry arguments, quantifier scope) that LLM verifiers structurally miss?
- Would a Lean-formalization spot-check of the ~25 accepted dev-set proofs catch the 1/7 false-accept cases that no unanimity rule over 24 judgments could, at acceptable cost?
- How do the results scale down: does the checkpoint-diversity-beats-attempt-scaling finding hold for 70B-class or smaller models, where generation diversity may be lower?
- The search plateaued well before the 4.5-hour deadline — what is the marginal value curve of additional rounds, and did the 8-round budget leave points on the table for P3 specifically?
- Can the jury-based evaluation protocol be replaced or calibrated against official human grading at scale, given the demonstrated ~2-point systematic optimism at IMO 2026?
