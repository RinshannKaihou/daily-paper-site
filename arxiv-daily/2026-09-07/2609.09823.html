Critical Assessment + Limitations

01 Methodology — predict, then audit the complement

Both prior fixes leave waste: post-QK sparsifiers (BLASST) still read every K block — at 90% sparsity, K is ~91% of remaining K+V traffic — while current-query PIM filters (Quest-PIM, BLASST-PIM) put a serial PIM stage on the critical path. AMEND exploits one measured regularity: sparsity verdicts persist — 89.2% unchanged one step later, and a causal EMA over each block's margin already reproduces 93.4% of DROP verdicts.

Each step: predict the next mask from audited state (margin EMA, volatility, drop-age, 2-bit Schmitt hysteresis); run GPU attention on survivors only; concurrently, near-bank PIM GEMV units score the omitted complement; a narrow controller — no exponent, division, or softmax datapath — merges both streams and folds the reconstructed margins into the next state. A survivors-only predictor would never see the margins of blocks it dropped (a selective-labels trap); the complement audit closes it. The current token never waits on the audit. Five mixed-type knobs are calibrated offline by constrained Bayesian optimization (SCBO) under a 2% false-drop budget.

Read the fine print

  • FDR sweep bounds the operating range: RULER stays within 0.005 of BLASST down to τ=5×10⁻³, collapsing (0.335) only at 8×10⁻²; recall rises 0.560→0.956 as the budget relaxes.
  • Batch scaling: still fastest through N=64, but at N=128 AMEND is within 0.6% of Quest-PIM (5.86× vs 5.89×) once the audit stall is exposed (49.2 µs/token; hiding it would yield 7.18×).
  • Averages hide per-task gaps: common-words-extraction at 16K — AMEND 64.80 vs BLASST 78.20 (−13.4 pts) and Dense 86.30; the honest quality reference is BLASST, which AMEND trails everywhere.
  • Dual-path caveat: quality runs execute the AMEND software path on A100 BF16 while performance replays on a simulated H100 — measured on different hardware paths.

04 Strengths

  • The "when does the mask become available" taxonomy turns a vague systems claim into four concrete dependency structures — and cleanly quantifies why post-QK sparsity leaves K bytes untouched.
  • Complement audit is the right fix to a well-named failure mode (selective-labels censoring), isolated by a controlled ablation (−68% false-drop at a frozen operating point).
  • Work is conserved, not deleted (QK_surv + QK_omit = QK_full) and survivor BLASST provably cannot add false drops — quality loss is bounded to predictor false drops by construction.
  • Hardware-plausible controller scope (no exponent/division/softmax datapath), explicitly budgeted against AttAcc's 1.38 mm² buffer-die unit.
  • Honest knob-level disclosure: realized vs target false-drop rates, per-task RULER tables, sharply bounded FDR range, and the batch regime where the advantage disappears.

05 Limitations

  • Simulation-only performance; the AttAcc-style HBM-PIM substrate does not exist commercially, and GPU–PIM bandwidth contention is assumed away.
  • Small default batch (8); advantage over Quest-PIM gone at N=128, and N>16 at 32K already exceeds one H100's KV capacity — large-batch claims are extrapolated.
  • Contexts capped at 64K; decode-only — no prefill story, no integration into a full serving stack.
  • Recall 72.60–90.75%: up to ~27% of true drops are never predicted — real sparsity left on the table in exchange for safety.
  • Per-model, per-setting SCBO calibration adds deployment friction; transferability of a frozen operating point is untested.
  • Narrow baselines (Quest, BLASST + PIM variants; no empirical head-to-head with TidalDecode, IndexCache, AttentionPredictor…); energy is dynamic-only; the −68% ablation rests on 10 examples.

06 Questions for further exploration

  • How much speedup survives honest GPU–PIM contention, PIM-lane sharing with controller traffic, and static leakage energy?
  • Can one operating point θ transfer across domains (code vs narrative QA) or fine-tuned variants without re-running SCBO?
  • Does margin-based prediction survive aggressive KV quantization (FP8/INT4), where margins are dequantized but reads are quantized?
  • Speculative decoding, heterogeneous GQA heads — does verdict persistence degrade?
  • Could predict-and-audit port to a pure-GPU stack (CUDA async copies standing in for PIM) to bank part of the K-traffic saving today?
  • Beyond 64K (128K–1M): does the one-step audit-hiding budget still cover a much larger complement per step?
