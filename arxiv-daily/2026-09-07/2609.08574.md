> **一句话总结：** 本文提出四指标诊断套件 SinkProbe（注意力汇聚质量、巨型激活、位置分辨召回、近因差距），在四个仅 Token 混合方式不同的 ~1M 参数受控模型上发现：注意力汇聚是由训练目标（是否在每个位置都要求输出）而非架构产生的——把逐位置语言建模损失换成仅答案位置损失后，首 token 注意力占比从 0.316 降到 0.049（召回率不变）；门控注意力和注意力残差能砍掉巨型激活（-52%/-60%）却不动汇聚，位置偏差与汇聚质量互不相关；作者还为 Kimi K3 的 69/93 线性层混合算了缓存账（1M token 约 24 GiB，是同等深度稠密栈 2.5 TiB 的约 1/107），并预注册了在真实发布权重上跑到 1M token 的测量协议——但本文实验本身只到 768 token，标题问题仍然开放。

> **TL;DR:** The authors build SinkProbe, a 4-metric diagnostic suite (sink mass, massive activation, position-resolved recall, recency gap) and validate it on a controlled ladder of four ~1M-parameter models (softmax, softmax+gate, 3:1 linear-hybrid à la Kimi K3, hybrid+attention-residuals) trained on a synthetic key-value retrieval probe. Headline result: the sink is created by the training objective, not the architecture — the same softmax baseline stack trained with answer-only loss drops first-token attention from 0.316 to 0.049 (worst layer 0.633 to 0.067) with recall unchanged (32.0% vs 33.3%), while the NeurIPS 2025 gated-attention effect (15B model: 46.7% to 4.8%) did NOT reproduce at their scale (0.316 to 0.261, inside seed spread; their gate score stayed open at 0.686 vs the published 0.116). Sink mass, massive activations, and position bias move independently. Crucially, the "million-token" part of the title is settled only by cache arithmetic (Kimi K3's 69/93 fixed-state layers need ~24 GiB at 1M tokens vs ~2.5 TiB dense, ~107x) plus a pre-registered protocol for the released weights — the pilot itself evaluates only up to 768 tokens, and its hybrid stand-ins never learned the retrieval task (6.0% recall, below the 8.3% blind-chance floor), so the central question remains open.

## 核心贡献

- **SinkProbe 四指标诊断套件**：在一个框架内定义了注意力汇聚质量（sink mass σ，softmax 层上首 token 注意力占比）、巨型激活（massive activation µ）、位置分辨召回 p(d) 与近因差距（recency gap Δr = 深度后 1/4 段召回 − 前 1/4 段召回），每个指标都配有点估计、Wilson 置信区间与跨 3 个随机种子的方差报告；还引入注意力熵以捕捉"搬到别处的汇聚"。
- **受控的四层架构阶梯**：四个 ~1M 参数模型只改变 Token 混合与深度混合规则（8 层 softmax / softmax+门控 / 3:1 线性混合（Kimi K3 模式，无位置编码）/ 混合+注意力残差 AttnRes），宽度、深度、头数、数据、优化器、种子全部固定——相邻行的差异即消融。
- **"训练目标制造汇聚"的证据**：同一 softmax 栈、同一数据、同一种子，仅把损失从"每个位置都要预测"改成"只预测最终答案位置"，首 token 注意力从 31.6% 降到 4.9%（最差层 63.3% → 6.7%），召回率不变（33.3% vs 32.0%），且种子间方差从 0.089–0.455 塌缩到 0.046–0.051——汇聚并非在为检索"付费"。
- **三量分离**：汇聚质量、巨型激活、位置偏差是三个独立测量而非一个问题——门控把 µ 从 74.9 砍半到 36.2（-52%）而 σ 只降 17% 且在种子噪声内；注意力残差把 µ 从 97.9 砍 60% 到 38.9 而 σ 反而 0.183→0.202 完全没降。
- **Kimi K3 缓存增长模型 + 预注册协议**：从公开层数推出 93 层中 69 层（74.2%）缓存不随上下文增长（这一比例无需任何假设）；1M token 下混合栈约 24 GiB vs 同深度稠密栈约 2.5 TiB（约 107 倍）；并在跑大实验前固定了测量点（4K–1M 共 7 个长度）、样本量与通过/失败数值阈值，构成对发布权重的可执行承诺。

## Key Contributions

- **SinkProbe diagnostic suite**: four metrics — sink mass σ (share of attention budget landing on position 0, averaged over softmax layers), massive activation µ (mean over layers of the largest absolute hidden value), position-resolved recall p(d), and the recency gap Δr (signed: recall near the question minus recall at the opening) — each reported with a Wilson interval and a spread across three seeds, plus attention entropy to catch sinks that migrate off the first token.
- **A one-mechanism-at-a-time model ladder**: four ~1M-parameter models identical in width (96), depth (8), heads (4), data, optimizer, schedule and seeds, differing only in how they mix tokens and depth — softmax baseline, softmax + input-dependent gate (the NeurIPS 2025 mechanism), a 3:1 hybrid of linear delta-style layers and one gated global layer with no positional encoding (the Kimi K3 pattern in miniature), and the hybrid with Block Attention Residuals replacing the uniform residual sum.
- **Evidence that the objective makes the sink**: an objective-only control (auxiliary next-token weight 0.3 vs 0.0, everything else identical) removes almost all sink mass from an unchanged architecture — 0.316±0.198 vs 0.049±0.003, worst layer 0.633 vs 0.067, recall unchanged — and collapses the across-seed spread from 0.089–0.455 to 0.046–0.051.
- **Three dissociations, not one phenomenon**: sink mass, massive activations and position bias move independently in their measurements, reproducing from the other direction the published ablation in Qiu et al. where a post-value gate cut max activation 1053→125 while leaving first-token attention at 0.297.
- **A cache growth model and a pre-registered protocol**: from published layer counts alone, 69 of 93 Kimi K3 layers (74.2%) carry a context-independent cache; at 1M tokens the mix needs ~24 GiB vs ~2.5 TiB for a dense stack of the same depth (~107x). The full-scale protocol — seven lengths from 4K to 1M, ~2.16B tokens read for the 1M row at ±7 points, two control models, and pass/fail thresholds fixed in advance (Table IV) — is written to be executed on the released Kimi K3 checkpoint.

## Methodology

The paper trains four models on a 16-thread laptop CPU (no accelerator, float32, 1500 AdamW steps, batch 16, lr 2e-3 one-cycle, 8 layers, width 96, 4 heads, SwiGLU FFN 256, RMSNorm pre-norm, 70-token vocabulary, training length 96 tokens; evaluation at 96/192/384/768 — eight times the training length as a small-scale analogue of 64K→1M extrapolation). The retrieval probe plants one key–value pair at a controlled depth d in a haystack with a periodic motif (period 24, half the positions randomized) plus three distractor pairs; the question at the end names the key and the model must return the value. Two floors anchor every accuracy: blind chance 8.3% (1/12 values) and "return any value present" 25% (4 pairs in context) — only distance above the second floor counts as retrieval. Diagnostics are read from attention maps and hidden states on the first evaluation batch at each length; recall pools 128 sequences per length per seed (384 total, Wilson half-width ≈ ±5 points); depth profiles use 32 sequences per depth point per seed. The linear layer keeps a fixed-size state with a channel-wise decay (initial decays spread 0.78–0.999) but deliberately omits the delta-rule correction of real Kimi Delta Attention — a simplification the authors flag as costly. Attention residuals follow the Kimi design: 8 layers grouped into 2 blocks, each layer building its input by softmax-weighting RMSNorm'd block representations with a learned pseudo-query. Statistical treatment uses Wilson score intervals for binomial proportions and separates within-model intervals from across-seed spreads; thresholds for the released-weight runs were fixed before any large run. The cache model derives bytes from layer counts with two declared assumptions (latent rank 512, 128×128 state per head, 96 heads, bfloat16).

## Results & Highlights

- **The objective, not the architecture, makes the sink** (Table VII, same softmax stack / data / seeds, training length 96): per-position loss → sink mass 0.316±0.198, worst layer 0.633, activation 74.9, recall 32.0%; answer-only loss → sink mass 0.049±0.003, worst layer 0.067, activation 26.2, recall 33.3% — a 6x drop on the average, 9x on the worst layer, with recall unchanged and the seed spread collapsing from {0.455, 0.089, 0.405} to {0.051, 0.046, 0.050}.
- **Gating did not reproduce its published effect at this scale** (Table VIII/IX): the NeurIPS 2025 paper reports first-token attention falling 46.7%→4.8% (mean gate score 0.116 after trillions of tokens) in a 15B model on real text; here the gate moves sink mass only 0.316→0.261 (-18%), a fraction of the 0.089–0.455 seed spread — the gated run with the highest sink scored above two of three baseline runs. The mechanism's gate stayed open (mean score 0.686 after 1500 steps), so the authors report the comparison as inconclusive rather than negative.
- **Activations and sinks dissociate twice**: the gate halves massive activation 74.9→36.2 (-52%) while sink mass falls only 17% (inside seed spread); attention residuals cut activation 97.9→38.9 (-60%) while sink mass does not fall at all (0.183→0.202). This mirrors the published ablation where a post-value gate cut activation 1053→125 but left first-token attention at 0.297 — four observations of the same dissociation.
- **Position bias is large, flips sign with length, and does not follow sink mass** (Fig. 6, Table X, 768 tokens): baseline recall climbs 20.6% (Q1, opening) → 35.8% (Q4, near question), recency gap +15.2 points, with decile extremes ~21%→~42%; at the 96-token training length the direction reverses (gap -10.9 points for the baseline, -15.9 for the gated stack — the opening is preferred). The gated stack has the lower sink mass of the two softmax stacks yet the steeper decile profile at 768 — the model wasting less attention on token one leans harder on the end of its context.
- **The hybrid stacks never learned the probe**: recall 6.0±2.7% (Hybrid 3:1) and 5.7±0.9% (Hybrid+AttnRes) at the training length, below the 8.3% blind-chance floor and far below the 25% "any value present" floor, versus 32.0% and 28.1% for the two softmax stacks — because the authors' linear stand-in omits the delta-rule correction. Their sink/activation/entropy numbers are still measurements of trained stacks, but their recall and recency figures carry no information about position bias.
- **Cache arithmetic for the Kimi K3 mix** (Table VI): at 1M tokens the hybrid needs ~24.2 GiB (24 GiB global cache + 0.20 GiB fixed linear state) vs ~2580.8 GiB for a dense stack of the same depth — roughly 107x; below ~8K tokens the fixed state is the larger term, so the design only pays off in the regime it was built for. The 69/93 = 74.2% flat-cache fraction requires no assumption; halving or doubling the two declared geometry assumptions moves the total by less than 2x.
- **Context from the literature they reproduce**: their untreated 1M-parameter stack puts 31.6% (avg) / 63.3% (worst layer) on token one — same order as the 46.7% / 83% Qiu et al. measured in a 15B model, four orders of magnitude larger; and the published gated-vs-ungated RULER comparison at 128K tokens is 58.8 vs 31.7.

## Authors & Credibility

Three authors from two engineering colleges in Hyderabad, India (Shadan Women's College of Engineering and Technology; Muffakham Jah College of Engineering & Technology) — an academic group rather than a frontier lab, which matters here because the paper's contribution is exactly a small-scale, laptop-reproducible methodology rather than a frontier measurement. Credibility signals are unusually strong for the venue: thresholds and outcome interpretations were registered in advance (Table IV, Table XIII) to remove post-hoc rationalization; every accuracy carries a Wilson interval plus a 3-seed spread, and the paper repeatedly reports inconvenient results against its own hypotheses (the gate non-reproduction, the hybrid training failure, the seed-variance caveat); the negative-result reading is explicitly bounded ("a statement about our small proxies, not about the published mechanism"). Code, task generator (seeded RNG, no dataset download), a <1-minute test suite (checking the planted depth, causality, chunk-size agreement of the linear layer to 3e-7), and three reproducible entry points are released at github.com/sararizwan7/Attention-Mechanisms-in-1M-Context-Window. The writing is precise about what each number can and cannot support.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: genuine and well-aimed — the observation that sink diagnostics stop at 128K tokens while million-token architectures (Kimi K3) publish only benchmark scores is a real gap, and SinkProbe plus the pre-registered protocol is a credible instrument for closing it. The objective-makes-the-sink control is the paper's most decisive result.
- **The title oversells the length**: despite "Million-Token Context" in the title, no measurement in this paper exceeds 768 tokens — three orders of magnitude below 1M. The million-token claims rest on (a) a cache arithmetic model with two assumed unpublished dimensions and (b) a protocol for someone else's checkpoint that has not been run. The paper says this honestly (Section V-E, Section X), but a reader skimming the abstract could believe 1M-token diagnostics were measured.
- **The hybrid rows are internally invalidated**: the Kimi-K3-pattern stacks never learned the retrieval task (6.0% recall vs 8.3% blind chance) because the authors' linear layer omits the delta-rule correction — they tried two fixes (decay-spread init, RoPE on global layers) and both failed. So the "hybrid 3:1 cuts sink mass 42%" row compares stacks solving different tasks, which the authors concede is confounded; the paper's central architecture comparison (hybrid vs softmax) is therefore not actually delivered.
- **Scale and budget are extreme**: ~1M parameters, 1500 steps, 96-token training sequences, vocabulary of 70, versus a 2.8T-parameter model trained on trillions of tokens; the seed spread on baseline sink mass (0.089–0.455, a factor of 5) is wider than any architecture effect measured, so most architectural comparisons in Table VIII are underpowered by the paper's own standard.
- **Rigor**: nonetheless high where it counts — pre-registration, Wilson intervals, two-floor task calibration (chance and copy-any-value), the haystack-predictability calibration (15% random → 0.095 sink; 50% random → 0.438, single seed, disclosed as a setting choice not a result), and named failure modes for the suite (migrated sinks, parametric answering, saturated gates).
- **Reproducibility**: excellent — pure PyTorch/NumPy, CPU-only, seeded synthetic data, released code with tests; a reader can regenerate every table.
- **Significance**: moderate now, potentially high later: the dissociation results (objective→sink; activation≠sink; position bias≠sink) and the reminder that "a model card reporting low first-token attention has said something true and something narrow" are immediately useful to long-context practitioners, but the decisive million-token measurement on Kimi K3's released weights remains future work.
- **Red flags**: minor — the cache table's two assumed dimensions (latent rank 512, 128×128 state) are declared but could be off enough to matter for capacity planning at the margins; and the abstract's "Gated attention cut first token attention from 46.7 to 4.8 percent at NeurIPS 2025" quotes the prior work's numbers prominently, which could be misread as this paper's own result.

## Strengths

- Clean controlled design: one mechanism changed per ladder step, everything else (including seeds and optimizer) fixed, making adjacent table rows ablations by construction.
- The objective control is a genuinely decisive experiment with a large effect (6x) and a mechanism-level explanation (softmax must spend budget of one at every position it is asked to predict at).
- Pre-registration of thresholds, sample sizes, and the four-outcome interpretation grid (Table XIII) — rare and valuable in this literature.
- Honest negative reporting: the gate non-reproduction is diagnosed down to the gate score (0.686 open vs 0.116 published) rather than vaguely attributed.
- Two-floor accuracy reporting (8.3% chance / 25% any-value) that many long-context evaluations omit.
- Fully reproducible on a laptop: no pretrained weights, no network, no GPU, seeded synthetic task, released code with property tests.

## Limitations

- The pilot evaluates only to 768 tokens — three orders of magnitude short of the million-token question in the title; extrapolation shape is informative, absolute values are not (stated by the authors).
- The linear layer omits the delta rule of real Kimi Delta Attention; consequently the hybrid stacks never learned the retrieval probe (recall below blind chance), so all hybrid recall/recency rows carry no information about position bias and the hybrid-vs-softmax comparison is confounded.
- ~1M parameters and 1500 training steps cannot speak for 2.8T-parameter, trillion-token models: the gate's sparsity (0.116) emerges over far longer training than the pilot can run, and the authors correctly bound their own claim.
- Massive seed variance on sink mass (0.089–0.455 within the baseline) exceeds every architecture difference measured, leaving most architectural comparisons inconclusive at this scale.
- Sink mass is defined only on softmax layers, so hybrid models average over 2 layers where dense models average over 8 — not like-for-like, though layer counts are reported beside every value.
- The synthetic key–value probe isolates retrieval but ignores summarization, multi-hop reasoning, and code navigation; everything is measured on symbolic sequences (English-only behavior may differ, and published multilingual gaps are larger).
- The cache byte totals depend on two unpublished Kimi K3 dimensions taken as assumptions (latent rank 512; 128×128 state per head).

## Relevance

如果你关注长上下文 LLM 的架构演进（Kimi K3 / 线性注意力 / 门控注意力）与可信评测，这篇论文直接 relevant：它给出了一套可在发布权重上复用的注意力汇聚与位置偏差诊断工具包，并用受控实验提醒我们——"消灭 sink"与"均匀读取窗口"是两个独立问题，模型卡上的低首 token 注意力并不能保证上下文中段被真正读到。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Will the pre-registered SinkProbe protocol, run on the released Kimi K3 weights at 4K–1M tokens, land in the "sink small, recency gap large" cell of Table XIII (their pilot points there), and will the 128K calibration point agree with Qiu et al.'s published value?
- Does the objective-makes-the-sink finding (answer-only loss removes the sink) hold at frontier scale, or does the auxiliary per-position loss remain necessary for general language modeling — i.e., is there a training objective middle ground that keeps LM quality without creating sinks?
- Once the delta-rule correction is added to the linear stand-in, does the 3:1 hybrid actually reduce sink mass and the recency gap, or was even the -42% pilot drop an artifact of the task never being learned?
- Why does position preference flip from primacy (negative recency gap at the training length) to recency (+15.2 points at 8x extrapolation) — is this an out-of-distribution fallback to nearest-context copying, and at what training/eval ratio does the flip occur?
- Since the Kimi K3 fixed state is a lossy compression, what is its associative-recall capacity as a function of state geometry, and does capacity saturation — not attention sinks — become the binding constraint at 1M tokens?
- Do massive activations and sink mass remain dissociated under other published remedies (SoftPick's rectified softmax, sparse-attention combinations), and does entropy catch sinks that migrate off position zero in gated models?
