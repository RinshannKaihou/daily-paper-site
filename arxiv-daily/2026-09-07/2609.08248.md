> **一句话总结：** Meta 提出 A-MLE——由单个工具调用型 LLM 智能体在沙箱执行层上自主串联"假设生成→探索策略→实验执行→结果分析"五阶段循环（阶段边界设人类审核点，跨模型共享版本化知识库），用于系统性地在数十个工业广告排序模型间移植和探索 ML 技术：在实验模型 M\* 上多源（架构+训练效率）探索取得 +2.56% 相对回归误差降低且训练吞吐 +0.42%，领域装备配置在工具可用性基准上整体准确率 68%（通用 ML 智能体仅 16%、纯 LLM 仅 8%），端到端迭代吞吐量达人工基线的数倍。

> **TL;DR:** A-MLE replaces the "senior ML engineer as moment-to-moment operator" model with a single LLM agent that autonomously runs hypothesis generation, exploration planning, sandboxed experiment execution (type checks, smoke passes, failure triage, bounded retries), and rolling-baseline statistical analysis across Meta's portfolio of ads ranking models, with humans only gating stage boundaries. On the anonymized internal regression-objective model M\*, the multi-source (architecture + efficiency) exploration configuration achieves a +2.56% relative regression-error reduction with +0.42% training QPS, versus +0.44% for single-hypothesis architecture scale-up and +0.58% for multi-round architecture exploration (both throughput-neutral) — roughly 4.4–5.8x the weaker configurations. On the L1 tool-availability bench, the domain-equipped agent scores 68% overall versus 16% for a generic ML agent and 8% for a plain LLM, and solves 100% of job-config-modification questions where generic configs stay below 40%. In a controlled cross-LLM study with the agent loop held fixed, only Sonnet ≥3.5, Gemini 2.5, and GPT-5 can operate the multi-hour workflow loop at all (high-90s task completeness vs 13.3–66.7 for others), and prompt stress flips behavior by family: Sonnet 4.0 reaches its largest improvement (~2.6e-2 rMSE) under stress while GPT-5 collapses to ~0. The trade-off: throughput and acceptance-rate gains are reported only qualitatively ("multiple times", "much higher"), and everything is evaluated on anonymized internal models with offline metrics (NE/rMSE) only — no code, no public benchmark, no online A/B results.

## 核心贡献

- **问题重构：将工业 ML 的瓶颈定位为"人工迭代吞吐量"而非模型能力上限。** 广告排序系统是数十个按目标（点击/转化/观看）、surface 和广告分段差异化的模型组合，每次端到端探索需要资深工程师数天到数周，导致已被验证的技术在模型间扩散缓慢且不均，长尾模型几乎得不到探索——A-MLE 把这一迭代循环本身作为系统级优化单元。
- **五阶段单智能体架构 + 共享知识基底。** 单个工具调用型 LLM 智能体编排假设生成（基于模型实时训练状态与历史尝试记录，LLM critic 为新颖性与可行性打分）、探索策略（在算力/时间约束下交错探索与利用）、实验执行（沙箱中改代码/配置，类型检查、单测、构建镜像、冒烟验证、提交训练并监控）、结果分析（对滚动基线做显著性检验、按广告/用户分段分解、方差超阈值自动重跑），外加一个版本化在源码库中的 markdown 共享知识基底——在一个模型上积累的技术证据自动成为其他结构相似模型的可消费先验。
- **人机分工明确的人审检查点设计。** 每个阶段边界都是人类可批准/修改/终止的检查点，专门限制单次智能体决策的爆炸半径（幻觉代码在训练启动前被拦截、校准错误的评估在提案撰写前被拦截），而阶段内部保持智能体完全自主。
- **分层能力评估框架与受控跨 LLM 对比。** L1（工具可用性）/L2（自主工作流执行）/L3（开放式探索）三层基准；在固定智能体循环、技能与提示的条件下交换底层 LLM（Claude Sonnet、Gemini、GPT 三个家族），分离编排框架与基座模型的贡献，发现执行可靠性与探索激进程度存在显著的模型家族差异。
- **在真实工业模型组合上的部署结论。** 智能体最强的产出来自**技术移植**（把在一个模型上验证过的架构改动/嵌入特征迁移到结构相似但未尝试过的模型）；并坦诚记录五类主导失败模式（幻觉 API、基线漂移、基础设施脆弱性、过度自信的单一 seed 推进、弱基座的非单调提示敏感性）。
- **关键设计教训：可靠性由编排框架而非基座模型推理能力主导**——技能库覆盖度、评估管线的统计严谨性和执行层对基础设施噪声的韧性，比换更强的 LLM 更能决定系统表现。

## Key Contributions

- **Reframes the industrial ML bottleneck as human-iteration throughput, not model capacity.** An ads ranking stack is a portfolio of dozens of differentiated models (by objective: click/conversion/view; by surface; by ad segment), each with its own data, features, architecture, and infra constraints. A single end-to-end iteration costs a senior ML engineer days-to-weeks per model, so proven techniques diffuse slowly and unevenly, and the long tail of models is rarely explored. A-MLE makes the iteration loop itself the unit of optimization.
- **A five-stage single-agent architecture over a shared substrate.** One tool-using LLM agent orchestrates: (1) hypothesis generation grounded in the model's *live* training config, baseline metrics, and attempted-technique history (sources include model-internal-state analyzers and training-efficiency analyzers, scored by an LLM critic); (2) exploration strategy that interleaves exploration and exploitation under compute/wall-time constraints and is negotiated with the user; (3) experiment execution in a sandboxed codebase copy (type checks, unit tests, image build, smoke pass, full training submission, monitoring, and distinguishing infra errors from genuine training divergence with bounded retries and compute rerouting); (4) result analysis with statistical significance against the *rolling* baseline (not a drift-vulnerable frozen snapshot), segment-level decomposition, and automatic re-runs when within-run variance exceeds a threshold; (5) a long-lived shared substrate — markdown trees versioned in source control with per-technique and per-model track records — so knowledge on one model becomes automatically consumable on structurally similar models.
- **Human-in-the-loop checkpoints at every stage boundary.** Engineers act as reviewers (approve / modify / terminate), not operators. The checkpoint structure explicitly bounds blast radius: a hallucinated code change is caught before training launch; a miscalibrated evaluation is caught before proposal authoring.
- **A tiered capability framework plus a controlled cross-LLM study.** Tasks are organized into L1 (tool availability), L2 (autonomous workflow execution with waiting operators for multi-hour async jobs), and L3 (open-ended exploration). Holding the agent loop, skills, and prompts fixed while swapping the LLM isolates harness vs base-model contributions across the Claude Sonnet, Gemini, and GPT families.
- **Deployment findings on a real portfolio.** The agent's strongest results come from technique transfer — recognizing that a technique validated on one model likely transfers to a structurally similar one; it struggles on models that recently underwent non-trivial baseline changes (hypotheses calibrated to a stale model version). Five recurring failure modes are documented (hallucinated APIs, baseline drift, infrastructure fragility misread as divergence, over-confident single-seed triage, LLM-specific workflow-ID hallucination).
- **Headline design lesson: the orchestration harness, not base-model reasoning capability, governs reliability** — skill-library coverage, statistical rigor of the evaluation pipeline, and execution-layer resilience matter more than raw LLM quality, a claim they expect to persist as base models improve.

## Methodology

A-MLE parameterizes each exploration session by a (model, objective, compute) triple and must terminate in either a documented proposal or a documented null result — a deliberate anti-selection-bias design that forces negative results into the shared substrate.

**System (Section 4).** The agent works against (a) a domain-specific **skill library** (codebase navigation, training-config editing with schema validation, launch and monitoring, evaluation, proposal authoring — Appendix A lists the typed procedures), and (b) a **sandboxed code-execution layer** where it edits architecture or training config, runs type checks and unit tests, builds an image, runs a short smoke pass, then submits the full training job. During execution it distinguishes infrastructure failures from training divergence and retries, fixes, or escalates; if one branch of a multi-experiment plan becomes infeasible it reroutes remaining compute rather than abandoning the session. Result analysis computes significance against the rolling baseline, decomposes metrics by ad/user segment to surface localized regressions, and auto-triggers re-runs when within-run variance exceeds a threshold; a structured leaderboard feeds back into strategy or assembles the final proposal. Four human checkpoints gate hypothesis choice, the experiment plan and compute allocation, run-results confirmation, and proposal routing (Appendix B).

**Evaluation setup (Section 5).** Deployment targets a representative set of large-scale ads ranking models spanning optimization objective, surface, and architecture family (deep cross network, deep interest network, multi-tower), anonymized as M1, M2, …; detailed studies use a single lightweight-footprint regression-objective model M\*. A three-tier capability framework is drawn from internal benchmark design (L1 focused single-step questions over training config/eval/metric stores/infra; L2 multi-step workflow tasks — baseline refresh, variance test, structured config-change experiment, batch offline evaluation; L3 open-ended "best improvement you can find"). Methodological baselines are the **manual baseline** (senior engineers doing everything) and a **semi-automated baseline** (engineer-driven hypotheses + scripted helpers for runs/eval); both share A-MLE's offline evaluation suite. Five metric classes: throughput (completed iterations per engineer-week), training success rate after automated debugging, proposal acceptance rate through human review without rework, technique coverage (distinct technique families surfaced), and offline model metrics (Normalized Entropy NE for prediction quality, rMSE for calibration). All performance numbers are reported as relative improvements over the relevant baseline with significance measured against the rolling-baseline framework. The cross-LLM study (Section 6.7) fixes the agent loop, skills, and prompts and swaps only the underlying LLM, additionally contrasting a "basic" vs a "stressful, competitive" system prompt.

## Results & Highlights

- **L1 tool-availability bench on M\* (Figure 2):** the domain-equipped A-MLE configuration reaches **68% overall accuracy versus 16% for a generic ML agent (+52 pts) and 8% for a plain generic LLM (+60 pts)** — evidence that domain skills, not raw LLM capability, dominate this tier. The largest gap is job-config modification, where the domain-equipped agent solves **100% of questions** while both generic configurations stay **below 40%** (~36% and ~9% per the figure); on result-analysis questions the split is 37.5% vs 12.5% / 12.5%.
- **Headline exploration outcome on M\* (Table 1, relative regression-error reductions):** the multi-source exploration combining architecture and training-efficiency hypothesis generators achieves **+2.56%** (text: +2.557%) relative improvement with **+0.42% training QPS**, versus **+0.58%** for multi-round architecture exploration (throughput-neutral) and **+0.44%** for single-hypothesis architecture scale-up (neutral) — the multi-source configuration delivers roughly **4.4–5.8x** the offline gain of the arch-only configurations at positive training throughput.
- **Cross-LLM L2 workflow execution (Figure 3a):** L2 task-completeness scores split into two clusters. The capable cluster — **Sonnet ≥ 3.5, Gemini 2.5, and GPT-5 — averages in the high 90s (100 / 96.7 / 93.3 / 93.3 across tasks in the figure)**, while the remaining models score 66.7 or 13.3, "often hallucinating workflow IDs or failing to wait for asynchronous jobs" — i.e., most LLMs cannot operate the multi-hour workflow loop at all.
- **Cross-LLM L3 exploration (Figure 3b) with prompt stress flipping by family:** under the basic prompt, Gemini 2.5 and GPT-5 explore most aggressively (GPT-5 ≈ 156×10⁻⁴ rMSE improvement in the figure) while the Sonnet family plays conservatively (~33–116×10⁻⁴); under the stressful prompt, **Sonnet 4.0 surfaces the largest improvement of any configuration (~2.6×10⁻² rMSE, i.e., 255.87×10⁻⁴ in the figure)** while **GPT-5 becomes conservative and gives back most of its basic-prompt gains (dropping to ~0)** — a non-monotone robustness result with direct implications for agent-LLM selection.
- **Throughput, training success, and acceptance (Sections 6.1–6.3):** A-MLE delivered "**multiple times the productivity** in completed iterations per engineer-week" versus the manual baseline (exact multiplier not reported); training success rate after automated debugging and bounded retries "meaningfully surpassed" the baseline; proposal acceptance through human review gating came at "a much higher rate", with reviewers citing better-grounded statistical analysis, cleaner documentation of negative results, and explicit segment-level decomposition. The semi-automated baseline showed smaller throughput gains, weaker offline impact, and longer convergence — indicating the leverage comes from chaining phases without engineer-mediated handoffs, not from automating any single phase.
- **Technique coverage across the portfolio (Table 2):** five families were surfaced and attempted — self-supervised pretraining (attempted on many ≥8 models; majority passed gating), generic optimizer/loss tweaks (many; mixed), embedding-based features (several, 3–7; majority passed), token-mixing architectures (several; mixed), and architecture scaling (few ≤2; mixed). The strongest validated results came from **technique transfer** to structurally similar models; the agent struggled most on models with recent non-trivial baseline changes.

## Authors & Credibility

This is an industrial systems paper from **Meta Platforms, Inc. (Menlo Park and New York)** with 39 authors — the scale of team typical of a real production ads-ranking organization spanning ranking modeling, agent infrastructure, and evaluation engineering, which lends credibility to the deployment claims (the failure-mode taxonomy, infra-vs-divergence triage, and rolling-baseline significance testing read as hard-won production lessons rather than bench experiments). The framing is well-grounded in the literature: it positions itself against AutoML/NAS (fixed search space, single objective), LLM code agents (SWE-bench, Toolformer, ReAct, Voyager), ML-engineering agents (MLE-bench, MLAgentBench, AI Scientist — noted as Kaggle-style/academic regimes), and recommendation/ads ranking architectures (DIN, DIEN, generative recommenders, RankMixer). Credibility caveats: every quantitative result is on anonymized internal models against internal baselines, the paper releases no code or benchmark, and the most operationally important numbers (throughput multiple, training success rate, acceptance rate) are stated qualitatively — the authors are upfront that reporting is relative-to-baseline, but outsiders cannot audit any of the absolute claims.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: solid, incremental-but-real.** The per-piece ingredients (LLM tool agents, sandboxed code execution, skill libraries, shared memory) are all established; the genuinely new part is the *regime*: an open-ended search space (any code-level change) with a composite objective (offline metric gain + infra feasibility + statistical significance + launch quality) across a portfolio of industrial-scale models where each iteration costs real compute — versus MLE-bench/AI-Scientist-style academic single-model settings. The technique-transfer finding (the shared substrate surfacing validated techniques for structurally similar models) is the most novel empirical observation.
- **Rigor: mixed.** Strong points: rolling-baseline significance testing (avoiding frozen-snapshot drift), forced documentation of null results, and the controlled cross-LLM study that fixes loop/skills/prompts. Weak points: the three headline operational metrics — throughput ("multiple times"), training success rate ("meaningfully surpassed"), and proposal acceptance ("much higher rate") — carry **no numbers, no confidence intervals, and no definition of the measurement window**, which is a conspicuous omission in a paper whose thesis is precisely about iteration throughput. The L1 bench appears to contain only ~8 questions per category (37.5% = 3/8, 12.5% = 1/8), so even the 68-vs-16-vs-8 overall figure rests on roughly three dozen questions.
- **Reproducibility: near zero, by construction.** Models are anonymized (M1…, M\*), the skill library and evaluation suite are internal, and no code is released. This is acceptable for an industrial experience paper but means every quantitative claim is unverifiable; the paper functions as a design pattern report, not a falsifiable benchmark result.
- **Significance: practically high, scientifically moderate.** The central lesson — reliability is governed by the orchestration harness more than base-model capability, and will remain so as LLMs improve — is a valuable, somewhat falsifiable design claim for the field, and the cross-LLM prompt-stress flip (Sonnet 4.0 improving under stress while GPT-5 collapses) is a data point the agent-building community will care about.
- **Red flags.** (1) The +2.56% headline sits on a single "lightweight footprint" model M\* explicitly chosen as a benchmark substrate, while the portfolio-level claim ("meaningful relative improvements on a majority of models") is never quantified per model — a mild cherry-pick in presentation even if not in execution. (2) The "stressful prompt" manipulation is ad hoc (one prompt variant, no analysis of why behavior flips), so the cross-LLM conclusions are anecdote-adjacent. (3) All evidence is offline (NE/rMSE); no online A/B or launch outcomes are reported, so whether agent-proposed changes survive production gating at the claimed acceptance rate is asserted, not shown. (4) Human-review labor per checkpoint is not costed — "completed iterations per engineer-week" could hide substantial review overhead shifting work rather than eliminating it.

## Strengths

- Correct problem diagnosis: the manual-iteration bottleneck (not model ceiling) is the binding constraint in mature industrial ranking stacks, and the six-phase decomposition of a canonical iteration (Section 3) is precise and recognizable to practitioners.
- The five-stage architecture is cleanly factored, and the shared, version-controlled knowledge substrate with eligibility annotations is an elegant mechanism for cross-model technique transfer — the empirically strongest capability.
- Safety-conscious design: sandboxed execution, mandatory smoke passes, four human checkpoints bounding blast radius, capped retry budgets, and auto re-run rules for high-variance single-seed results.
- Unusually honest failure-mode section (five named recurring modes) and a nuanced cross-LLM study that resists "one best model" conclusions.
- The forced dichotomy of terminal states — documented proposal or documented null result — plus significance against the rolling baseline, is methodologically the right way to run exploratory ML at industrial scale.

## Limitations

- The three metrics that matter most for the thesis (throughput multiple, training success rate, proposal acceptance rate) are reported only qualitatively; no absolute numbers, variances, or evaluation-window sizes.
- All evaluation is on anonymized internal Meta models with offline metrics only; no public benchmark, no code release, no online A/B confirmation, and results are not externally verifiable or comparable.
- The quantitative headline (+2.56%) comes from one deliberately lightweight model (M\*); portfolio-level gains and per-model breakdowns are not tabulated.
- The L1 bench sample size is tiny (~8 questions per category), and cross-LLM L3 conclusions rest on single runs per (model, prompt) cell without variance estimates.
- The "stressful prompt" condition is a single ad hoc manipulation; the non-monotone behavior flip is reported but not explained or stress-tested.
- Human checkpoint effort (reviewer time per gate) is unmeasured, so the true end-to-end engineer-hour savings versus the manual baseline cannot be computed from the paper.

## Relevance

如果你关心 LLM 智能体在真实工业级 ML 工程中的自主化（而非 Kaggle 式学术基准），本文是该方向少有的第一线系统报告：它给出了可复用的智能体编排设计（技能库 + 沙箱 + 共享知识基底 + 阶段边界人审）以及"编排框架比基座模型更能决定可靠性"这一对智能体工程直接有用的结论，与推荐系统/广告排序模型迭代的实际痛点高度契合。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- What is the actual throughput multiplier, training success rate, and proposal acceptance rate with confidence intervals — and how much engineer time is consumed by the four human checkpoints per iteration (i.e., is A-MLE shifting or eliminating labor)?
- Does the offline +2.56% regression-error reduction on M\* translate into statistically significant online A/B gains and launched candidates, and at what historical launch-rate baseline?
- Why does prompt stress produce opposite effects across LLM families (Sonnet 4.0 improving ~2.6×10⁻² rMSE while GPT-5 collapses to ~0) — is this a stable property of post-training, prompt-sensitive to wording, and does it generalize beyond one run per cell?
- How much of the technique-transfer strength is the substrate's eligibility annotations versus the LLM's architectural reasoning, and what is the transfer precision/recall across the portfolio (how often do transferred techniques fail or regress the target model)?
- Could the shared-substrate design be replicated on an open, externally auditable portfolio (e.g., a family of public recommendation models with a standardized rolling-baseline harness) to make A-MLE's claims falsifiable?
- As base models improve, does the claimed harness-over-model effect persist, or do stronger LLMs begin to dominate hypothesis quality — and at what model generation does the human-checkpoint structure become the bottleneck?
