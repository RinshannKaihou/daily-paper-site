> **一句话总结：** 本文证明无需训练的加性组合激活转向是可行的：在 Llama-3.1-8B/70B 与 Qwen2.5-14B/32B 四个指令模型上，只要把语言、越狱、简洁三个属性向量各自注入其单独最优的层与强度，语言+越狱与三属性组合平均都超过提示词基线，且组合成功与残差流中属性向量近似正交（逐层余弦相似度约等于 0，而不同语言向量早期约 0.6–1.0）密切相关。

> **TL;DR:** This DFKI/Saarland paper rehabilitates plain additive, training-free composition of activation steering vectors. Across four instruction-tuned models (Llama-3.1-8B/70B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-14B/32B-Instruct), vectors for language, jailbreak, and conciseness (extracted via DiffMean on FLORES, the Arditi et al. harmful set, and IFEval-derived pairs) compose reliably when each is injected at its own best layer/strength: language+jailbreak and the three-way combination beat prompt-instruction baselines on average on CLaS-Bench and the Arditi harmful prompts, while language+conciseness falls below prompting (the two vectors are less orthogonal, cosine drifting above 0 at late layers for heavily-tokenized languages). The geometric correlate: pairwise cosines between attribute vectors stay near 0 across depth, versus 0.6–1.0 among language vectors early on — orthogonality looks like a prerequisite for composability.

## 核心贡献

- **系统性刻画单属性转向的层–强度依赖**：在两个模型家族（Llama-3.1、Qwen2.5）、两个规模（8B/70B、14B/32B）上扫描干预层与转向强度，发现语言转向偏好较早层，抽象行为（越狱、简洁）偏好次早/中间层，最晚层语言转向得分趋近于零——单属性转向可靠但严重依赖配置。
- **证明加性、免训练组合可行**：把两个属性向量各注入其单独最优层，语言+越狱（L+J）组合在全部四个模型上平均超过提示词基线；三属性 L+J+C 组合同样超过提示词基线，解决了此前文献（van der Weij 等 2024 报告破坏性干扰 vs Stolfo 等 2025 报告温和成功）遗留的不一致。
- **首次测试语言×简洁组合**：这是此前没有任何研究测过的组合；发现 L+C 平均低于提示词基线且方差更大，与两向量正交性较弱相吻合。
- **几何解释**：语言、越狱、简洁向量在残差流中近似正交（跨深度余弦相似度≈0），而不同语言向量在早期层余弦相似度高达 0.6–1.0；正交性被提出为转向可组合性的先决条件，并得到逐语言证据支持（Qwen2.5-32B 上 L+J 仅在德语、俄语上退化，恰是晚期层语言–越狱余弦相似度最高的语言）。
- **跨深度幅值分析**：原始 DiffMean 向量范数随深度急剧增长，语言与越狱向量增长最快；Qwen 家族原始范数（数百量级）远大于 Llama（数十量级），故两家族需要相差约 10 倍的 α 范围。

## Key Contributions

- **Characterizes single-attribute steering as layer–strength dependent**: sweeping 4 intervention layers x 5 strengths per model on Llama-3.1-8B/70B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-14B/32B-Instruct, they show language steering favors earlier layers while abstract behaviors (jailbreak, conciseness) peak at the second-earliest evaluated layer, and language steering collapses to near zero at the latest layer for every model and strength.
- **Rehabilitates additive, training-free composition**: injecting each attribute vector at its own best-performing layer, language+jailbreak (L+J) steering exceeds the prompt-instruction baseline on average for all four models, and the exploratory three-way L+J+C combination also beats prompting on all models — resolving the inconsistency left open by prior work (destructive interference in van der Weij et al. 2024 vs mild success in Stolfo et al. 2025 and Cao et al. 2024) by attributing it to under-specified layer–strength configurations.
- **First test of language x conciseness composition**: a combination no prior study examined; it generally falls below the prompt baseline with higher variance, coherently explained by weaker orthogonality between the two vectors.
- **Geometric account of composability**: pairwise cosine similarity between language, jailbreak, and conciseness vectors remains close to 0 across all depths (approximately orthogonal subspaces), versus 0.6–1.0 among different-language vectors at early layers; per-language evidence (Appendix I) shows Qwen2.5-32B L+J degrades relative to L-only only in German and Russian — exactly the languages with the highest late-layer cosine(language, jailbreak).
- **Depth-wise magnitude analysis**: raw (pre-normalization) vector norms grow sharply with depth (language and jailbreak fastest), with Qwen vectors reaching the hundreds versus ~20–40 for Llama, motivating the ~10x different strength ranges (Llama alpha in {1..8}, Qwen alpha in {10..80}).

## Methodology

The pipeline has three stages. (1) **Extraction**: DiffMean steering vectors (Marks & Tegmark-style differences of condition means) are computed at the final post-instruction token of chat-formatted prompts, then unit-normalized so strength alpha alone controls magnitude. Language vectors contrast English vs each of 9 target-language instructions from FLORES (260 examples per language over 10 languages); the jailbreak vector contrasts 260 harmful vs 260 harmless instructions from Arditi et al. (2024); the conciseness vector contrasts 540 IFEval-derived prompts with/without a shortness suffix ("Be concise" style). (2) **Application**: h_tilde_l = h_l + alpha * v_l injected into the residual stream during generation; for composition, each attribute vector is added at its own layer, or scaled vectors are summed when layers coincide. (3) **Evaluation**: 70 harmless CLaS-Bench prompts (language/conciseness) and 70 Arditi harmful prompts (jailbreak), with metrics LFS (fastText target-language probability > 95%), JBS (Qwen3Guard-Gen-8B judging Safe/Controversial/Unsafe mapped to 0/1/2, normalized), CCS (1 − min(L_steered/L_baseline, 1) on tokenizer lengths), and OR (Qwen3-8B judge scoring 0/1/2 relevance), aggregated by harmonic mean to punish configurations that trade one property for another. Max generation is 512 new tokens for conciseness-involved conditions, 64 otherwise. Prompt-based baselines append explicit instruction suffixes (three per objective; best combinations for multi-attribute). Four instruction-tuned models are evaluated end-to-end; code and a browsing tool for examples are on GitHub (hyun-gu-kang/compositional-steering).

## Results & Highlights

- **Layer, not strength, decides single-attribute steering**: on the harmonic mean of LFS and OR over 70 CLaS-Bench prompts, language-steering scores at the latest evaluated layer (e.g., layer 22 of Llama-3.1-8B, layer 60 of Qwen2.5-32B) are consistently close to 0.00 across all five strengths and all four models, while early-layer configurations stay high — simply raising alpha cannot rescue a bad layer. Jailbreak (JBS+OR) and conciseness (CCS+OR) instead peak at the second-earliest evaluated layer in nearly all cases (Qwen2.5-32B is the exception, scoring slightly higher at layer 44 than 28 for both).
- **L+J beats prompting on all four models**: additive composition of the language vector (its best early layer) and the jailbreak vector (its middle layer) exceeds the mean prompt-instruction baseline across language pairs for every model, with the largest and most consistent margin on Llama-3.1-70B (Figure 3a); Llama-3.1-8B shows the widest spread across its 9 language pairs. Delta magnitudes are reported as plot distributions rather than numeric tables.
- **L+C underperforms prompting**: language+conciseness composition generally falls below the prompt baseline on average with greater variance for all four models; Llama-3.1-8B is weakest and Qwen2.5-14B strongest (Figure 3b). This is the only tested pairing whose two attributes are not consistently orthogonal.
- **Three-way L+J+C exceeds prompting on all four models** (Figure 4), but is more variable across language pairs than either two-way composition — matching the orthogonality-based prediction (three near-zero pairwise cosines should compose, with extra variance from allocating three attributes across fixed layers).
- **Geometry quantified**: mean pairwise cosine among different-language vectors is ~0.6–1.0 at early layers, decreasing with depth; by contrast, cosine(language, jailbreak), cosine(language, conciseness), and cosine(jailbreak, conciseness) remain close to 0 across all relative depths with no consistent trend — the near-orthogonality that explains why per-layer additive composition avoids destructive interference. The one partial exception, a slight positive language–conciseness cosine at late layers, is larger for languages tokenized into more tokens (surface sequence-length effects).
- **Per-language causal-style evidence (Appendix I)**: on Qwen2.5-32B, L+J degrades language steering vs L-only in exactly two languages, German and Russian — the two with the highest late-layer cosine(language, jailbreak) — preliminary support that orthogonality predicts composability.
- **Family-scale asymmetry**: raw vector norms grow with depth up to ~20–40 (Llama) vs ~200–600 (Qwen), so the same unit-normalized injection needs alpha up to 8 for Llama vs up to 80 for Qwen.

The group is directly embedded in this literature — Gurgurov et al. (2026) authored CLaS-Bench (the harmless-prompt evaluation set used here) and prior language-steering work, and Ostermann et al. (2026) wrote a recent survey of activation steering, so the experimental design reflects genuine domain expertise. The work cites and positions itself precisely against the mixed prior evidence (van der Weij, Cao, Stolfo, Scalena) and the learned-composition line (Radevski, Han, Nguyen). Code and a qualitative-example browsing tool are released on GitHub, and the work is funded by the German BMFTR TRAILS project (01IW24005). One circularity to note: evaluation uses the authors' own CLaS-Bench, and several citations (Gurgurov 2025/2026, Ostermann 2026) are self-citations, though the core claims rest on standard external datasets (FLORES, Arditi harmful set, IFEval).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: real but incremental. The core move — attributing prior inconsistent composition results to under-specified layer–strength configs, then showing per-layer additive composition works — is a solid, well-executed clarification rather than a new method; the geometry-to-composability link is the most interesting conceptual contribution, but it is established correlationally, not causally.
- **Rigor concerns**: (1) the best layer and alpha are selected from the same 70-prompt evaluation sets (CLaS-Bench / Arditi test) that then produce the reported scores — there is no held-out split, so single-attribute peaks and composition numbers are mildly optimistic by selection. (2) All judges are automated and two of them (Qwen3-8B for OR, Qwen3Guard-Gen-8B for JBS) come from the Qwen family, while two of the four steered models are Qwen2.5 — a possible family-affinity confound never discussed. (3) Results are presented almost exclusively as plots; exact numeric tables for the headline claims (e.g., the size of the L+J margin over prompting on Llama-3.1-70B) are absent, and per-condition n is only 70 prompts with no significance testing.
- **Weak baseline set**: the only comparison is instruction-suffix prompting; there is no head-to-head against the learned composition methods the paper motivates itself with (Radevski et al. 2026 steering tokens, Nguyen et al. 2025 gated interventions), so "training-free works" cannot be read as "works as well as trained."
- **Metric quirks**: CCS is a length-reduction proxy (1 − length ratio), so "concise" is never checked for information preservation, and cross-language CCS comparisons are confounded by tokenizer differences — partially acknowledged via the tokenization note; the three-way CCS baseline reuses Llama-3.1-8B L+J outputs (512 tokens) as a common reference because prompt-based outputs refuse too often, a defensible but nonstandard choice that weakens comparability of the L+J+C numbers.
- **Reproducibility**: good on paper — public datasets, four named open models, released code and GitHub browsing tool, explicit hyperparameter ranges — but figure-only reporting of headline deltas means reproducing exact numbers requires re-running everything.
- **Significance and red flags**: the jailbreak component is standard safety research practice (Arditi-style refusal suppression) with an ethics statement and public data only; the authors themselves flag the adversarial corollary (evading language-specific safety filters). The claimed resolution of prior inconsistency would be stronger with a direct replication of van der Weij et al.'s failing configuration to show exactly which layer–strength mis-specification caused it.

## Strengths

- Clean, well-scoped question with a genuinely useful answer: additive training-free composition works if you respect per-attribute layer and strength — directly actionable for practitioners.
- Cross-model, cross-family, cross-scale design (4 models, 2 families, 2 sizes) that most prior composition studies lacked.
- The geometric analysis (pairwise cosines across depth, magnitude growth curves) turns an empirical recipe into a testable hypothesis — orthogonality as a composability predictor — with per-language supporting evidence.
- Honest, fine-grained reporting of failure modes: L+C underperforming prompting, Llama-3.1-8B's high variance, the German/Russian degradation cases.
- Harmonic-mean aggregation of metrics explicitly penalizes configurations that improve one attribute while destroying another (e.g., relevance), a more demanding protocol than reporting each metric separately.

## Limitations

- Instruction-tuned models only; the post-instruction-token extraction position may not transfer to base models or other chat formats (authors' own caveat).
- Constant intervention strength per condition; optimal alpha plausibly varies with context and would need dynamic tuning (authors' own caveat).
- Only the English-to-target-language steering direction is tested; no target-to-English or third-language pairs.
- Composition studied for exactly three attributes chosen partly because refusal is known to be a near-universal direction; attributes that plausibly interact (e.g., sentiment vs toxicity) are untested, so the orthogonality story may not generalize beyond this favorable trio.
- 70 prompts per condition and judge-based metrics (fastText threshold 95%, LLM judges) cap the resolution of the conclusions; no human evaluation.
- The orthogonality-composability link remains correlational; no intervention that perturbs geometry to causally test it.

## Relevance

与「LLM Hidden States & Interpretability」兴趣高度契合：本文正是关于残差流内部表示的几何结构（DiffMean 转向向量、逐层余弦相似度、近似正交子空间）与机制可解释性驱动推理时行为控制的研究，其"正交性预测可组合性"的假设为表示空间潜在结构分析提供了可直接沿用的分析框架。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the orthogonality-composability link hold causally — e.g., if one projects a steering vector into the orthogonal complement of another, does composition of otherwise-interfering attributes (language+conciseness, or sentiment+toxicity) improve without any training?
- Would a held-out hyperparameter split change the conclusions, and how large is the L+J margin over prompting in exact numbers rather than plot distributions?
- Do LLM-judge family effects bias the results — does replacing Qwen3 judges with GPT-4o-class or human annotation shift the Llama-vs-Qwen ordering?
- Why exactly do language vectors favor early layers while jailbreak/conciseness favor middle layers — is this predictable from where each attribute becomes linearly decodable (probing accuracy across depth)?
- Can the per-layer recipe scale to more than three attributes, or does the fixed set of usable layers become the binding constraint (the authors observe variance already grows from two to three)?
- Does the finding transfer to base models, MoE architectures, or the English-target direction, and does dynamic, context-dependent alpha tuning beat constant strength?
