Key Finding · CircuitLens · arXiv 2609.07183

The lowest circuit-engagement data — not the highest — trains best for RLVR; selection is regime-dependent, not a difficulty ranking.

一句话：CRS 最低 10% 的数据训练效果最好（GSM8K / Minerva / OlympiadBench 全面超过随机选择）——RLVR 数据选择是“体制依赖”的，而非静态难度排序。

Key Metric
+2.9pp

Minerva gain, lowest-CRS decile vs random (n=5 seeds, paired). GSM8K +2.0 pp · OlympiadBench +1.6 pp; positive on 13/15 seed-by-benchmark cells.

Why This Matters

A one-forward-pass, reward-free internal signal for curating RLVR training data — with proof that selection effects hinge on pool diversity and model scale.

Critical Edge

The winning condition is also the noisiest — CRS-bottom's cross-seed variance runs 2–4× Random's, in places exceeding the effect size itself.

Methodology

Four stages turn 784 attention heads into a per-problem score costing one forward pass.

  1. 01
    Identify. Contrastive zero-ablation over Qwen2.5-Math-7B's 784 heads: loss jump on 50 Olympiad-style probes minus ½× jump on 50 trivial probes; three bootstrap rounds intersect to 46 stable heads across 21/28 layers (one bottleneck outlier excluded).
  2. 02
    Score. One forward pass on the frozen base model; CRS = mean over heads of each head's max ℓ2 activation norm (peak engagement, undiluted by boilerplate tokens).
  3. 03
    De-confound. OLS-residualize on log token length — length correlation drops r = 0.568 → 0.013.
  4. 04
    Select & train. Rank DAPO-Math-17K (17,186 problems), take top / middle / bottom 10% deciles, train with GRPO (1,000 steps, SymPy-checked reward, 8×A100). Controls: random heads, full data, difficulty filters, LIMR — 58 runs across 7B and 1.5B.

Boundary conditions

PoolDiversity is a prerequisite

On the curated 8.5K Olympiads pool nothing separates: MATH 70.5 / 71.4 / 69.7.

ScaleDirection flips at 1.5B

CRS-top leads MATH-500 65.4 vs 63.6 (n=3); 7B's heads partially transfer.

RewardAnti-predicts generalization

Lowest-reward run (0.265) generalizes best; LIMR's 0.775 underperforms random on AIME.

Beyond the headline

  • Non-monotone: CRS-top ≈ CRS-middle; no separation on MATH-500 (ceiling — base already 66.80); CRS-bottom is −1.6 pp on AIME'24.
  • Not a difficulty proxy: CRS overlaps the easiest-by-SR decile at Jaccard 0.078 vs 0.053 chance; token length predicts 2.8× more variance.
  • RandHeads match the mean with 2–4× the variance (GSM8K std 2.72 vs 0.64); full-17K data trails every 10% subset at the fixed 1,000-step budget.

Strengths

What the paper gets right.

  • Tests the intuitive hypothesis head-on and reports that it fails — a paper built around a well-controlled negative result.
  • Exceptional controls, all actually trained: random-head, same-pool, cross-scale, full-data, difficulty-filtering, reward-trajectory — each with Jaccard confound checks.
  • Statistical care above field norms: multi-seed paired differences, item-level McNemar, hierarchical posterior, stds instead of significance stars.
  • Cheap, practical signal: one forward pass per problem vs 65,912 generations for the difficulty baseline.
  • Reward↛generalization and the diversity prerequisite are durable findings independent of CRS itself.

Limitations

Where to hold skepticism.

  • Single model family (Qwen2.5-Math) and single domain (math); no evidence of transfer to other architectures or verifiable-reward domains.
  • Gains (+1.6–2.9 pp) sit near seed noise, and CRS-bottom is the highest-variance condition; the 1.5B reversal rests on n=3.
  • 58.2% prompt coverage per run — decile labels are only partially realized during training.
  • CRS is correlational: not a verified causal reasoning pathway; zero-ablation is destructive to the base model too.
  • "Why low engagement helps" is post-hoc and unverified at intermediate scales.

Questions for Further Exploration

Open threads the paper leaves behind.

  1. Does the useful CRS direction shift continuously across scales (1.5B → 3B → 7B → 32B), as the reward-variance explanation predicts?
  2. Do these 46 heads overlap across model families, or is the circuit — and the signal — architecture-specific?
  3. Would CRS-based curriculum (start low, anneal high) dominate any static decile?
  4. Can reward-variance-aware replay or exploration bonuses exploit what CRS-bottom stumbles into?
  5. Is there a quantitative diversity metric that predicts when any selection method works at all?
