Why this matters

A rare first-hand system report on autonomous LLM agents in production ML engineering — reusable orchestration patterns and a harness-over-model reliability lesson.

可复用的智能体编排设计（技能库 + 沙箱 + 共享知识基底 + 阶段边界人审），直击工业推荐 / 广告排序模型迭代的真实瓶颈。

Critical edge

The three metrics the thesis rests on — throughput multiple, training success, acceptance rate — are reported with no numbers, no confidence intervals, no measurement window.

All evidence is offline (NE / rMSE) on anonymized internal models: no code, no public benchmark, no online A/B. The +2.56% headline comes from one deliberately lightweight model.

Methodology — the loop as the unit of optimization

A-MLE parameterizes each session by a (model, objective, compute) triple; it must terminate in a documented proposal or a documented null result.

01Hypothesis generationGrounded in live training config & attempted-technique history; LLM critic scores novelty / feasibility.
02Exploration strategyInterleaves explore / exploit under compute and wall-time budgets, negotiated with the user.
03Sandboxed executionType checks, unit tests, image build, smoke pass, training submit; triages infra failure vs divergence with bounded retries and compute rerouting.
04Result analysisSignificance vs a rolling baseline (not a frozen snapshot); segment decomposition; auto re-run on excess variance.
05Shared substrateVersion-controlled markdown knowledge with per-technique / per-model track records — evidence transfers across similar models.
HUMAN GATES ×4 Checkpoints at every stage boundary — approve / modify / terminate — bound the blast radius (hallucinated code caught pre-launch, miscalibrated eval caught pre-proposal); inside a stage the agent is fully autonomous.

Evaluation is tiered — L1 tool availability, L2 autonomous workflow execution, L3 open-ended exploration — against a manual baseline (senior engineers) and a semi-automated baseline (engineer hypotheses + scripted helpers), all sharing one offline suite (NE for prediction quality, rMSE for calibration). A controlled cross-LLM study fixes the loop, skills, and prompts, swapping only the base model.

Weaker models hallucinate workflow IDs or fail to wait for async jobs — most LLMs cannot operate the multi-hour loop at all.

  • Prompt stress flips behavior by family: under a stressful prompt Sonnet 4.0 posts the largest improvement of any configuration (~2.6×10⁻² rMSE) while GPT-5 collapses to ~0 — a non-monotone robustness result with direct agent-selection implications.
  • Technique transfer is the strongest capability: porting techniques validated on one model to structurally similar siblings beat de-novo search; the agent struggles most on models with recent non-trivial baseline changes (hypotheses calibrated to a stale version).
  • Portfolio coverage (Table 2): five technique families surfaced and attempted — self-supervised pretraining (most, majority passed gating), embedding-based features (3–7 models, majority passed), optimizer/loss tweaks and token-mixing architectures (mixed), architecture scaling (few, mixed).

Strengths

  • Correct diagnosis: in mature ranking stacks the binding constraint is human iteration throughput, not model capacity — and the six-phase decomposition of a canonical iteration is precise.
  • Clean five-stage factoring; the version-controlled shared substrate is an elegant mechanism for cross-model technique transfer — the empirically strongest capability.
  • Safety-conscious design: sandboxed execution, mandatory smoke passes, capped retries, auto re-runs for high-variance results, four human checkpoints bounding blast radius.
  • Unusually honest failure-mode section and a nuanced cross-LLM study that resists “one best model” conclusions.
  • Forced proposal-or-null-result dichotomy plus rolling-baseline significance testing — methodologically the right way to run industrial-scale exploration.

Limitations

  • The three thesis-critical metrics (throughput, training success, acceptance) are qualitative only — no absolute numbers, variances, or evaluation windows.
  • Anonymized internal models, offline metrics only; no code release, no public benchmark, no online A/B confirmation — nothing is externally verifiable.
  • The +2.56% headline comes from one deliberately lightweight model (M*); portfolio-level gains and per-model breakdowns are never tabulated.
  • Tiny L1 sample (~8 questions per category); L3 cross-LLM cells are single runs without variance estimates; the “stressful prompt” is one ad-hoc manipulation, unexplained.
  • Human checkpoint effort is unmeasured — end-to-end engineer-hour savings cannot be computed; A-MLE may be shifting labor, not eliminating it.

Questions for further exploration

  1. What are the real throughput multiplier, training success rate, and acceptance rate with confidence intervals — and how much engineer time do the four checkpoints consume (shifting vs eliminating labor)?
  2. Does the offline +2.56% on M* translate into statistically significant online A/B gains and launched candidates?
  3. Why does prompt stress flip behavior across families (Sonnet 4.0 up, GPT-5 to ~0) — stable property, wording artifact, or single-run noise?
  4. How much of technique-transfer strength is the substrate’s eligibility annotations vs the LLM’s reasoning — and what is the transfer failure rate across the portfolio?
  5. Could the shared-substrate design be replicated on an open, auditable model family to make A-MLE’s claims falsifiable — and does harness-over-model persist as base models improve?
