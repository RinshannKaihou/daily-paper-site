> **一句话总结：** 提出 SAEScientist-Bench 基准，让 AI 智能体自主设计对比探针、在 Gemma-2-9B-IT 的 Gemma Scope 131,072 特征字典中发现指定概念的 SAE 特征，并从激活排名、激活选择性、因果引导三个维度对照 Neuronpedia 专家特征打分；10 个前沿智能体在选择性上接近专家（92.91 vs 98.92），但因果引导严重落后（31.47 vs 57.75），最优的 Kimi K3 综合分仅 65.82 vs 专家 85.56。

> **TL;DR:** SAEScientist-Bench evaluates 10 frontier agents (Kimi K3, Claude Opus/Sonnet 5, Grok 4.6, GPT-5.6 Sol/Luna, GPT-5.5, GLM-5.2, Gemini 3.8 Flash, Opus 4.8) on 20 autonomous SAE feature-discovery tasks inside the Gemma Scope dictionary of Gemma-2-9B-IT, scored against curated Neuronpedia Expert features on activation rank, activation selectivity (AUROC), and causal steering. Agents nearly match the Expert baseline on selectivity (best 92.91 vs 98.92) but lag badly on causal steering (best Grok 4.6 at 31.47 vs Expert 57.75), and the top Overall score (Kimi K3, 65.82) sits ~20 points below Expert (85.56) — selective features rarely make potent steering vectors, and agents frequently misread their own experimental measurements.

## 核心贡献

- **首个面向"智能体作为可解释性科学家"的闭环评测基准**：给定目标概念（葡萄牙语、财报、临床症状报告、猫等 17 个概念、20 个任务），智能体通过编写对比探针文本（正例/难负例/中性对照），在 `probe_sae` 接口上导航 Gemma-2-9B-IT 第 9、20 层 Gemma Scope 残差流 SAE 的 131,072 特征字典，最终提交一个特征 ID。
- **三维统一评分框架，以 Neuronpedia 专家特征为冻结参考基线**：Activation Rank（字典内排名相对专家）、Activation Selectivity（正例 vs 对照文本的 AUROC）、Causal Steering（干预生成相对基线/随机方向对照的净目标增益），综合分为三者等权平均。
- **对 10 个前沿智能体配置的系统性评测**：每个配置 3 次独立完整运行、共 600 个 episode、4,873 次探针调用；Kimi K3 综合分最高（65.82），不同模型在不同维度各擅其长（Opus 5 排名第一、Kimi 选择性第一、Grok 4.6 引导第一）。
- **深入的行为科学分析**：从探针轨迹与搜索统计中提炼出"探针广度 vs 候选深度"、"主动假设检验 vs 启发式选择"等科研风格差异，并定位典型失败模式——误读实验读数、混淆格式与概念、选择导致生成退化的特征。

## Key Contributions

- Introduces **SAEScientist-Bench**, the first benchmark that evaluates AI agents as autonomous scientists doing mechanistic interpretability research: for each of 20 concept tasks (17 concepts spanning multilingual understanding, everyday concepts, professional document formats, and specialized reports), the agent authors contrastive probe texts and navigates a 131,072-feature Gemma Scope SAE dictionary in Gemma-2-9B-IT (layers 9 and 20) to submit a single feature.
- Establishes a **standardized three-dimension scoring framework** against frozen, Neuronpedia-anchored Expert reference features: Activation Rank (dictionary prominence relative to expert), Activation Selectivity (AUROC separating positives from hard-negative/neutral controls), and Causal Steering (net target-relevance gain over the stronger of baseline and norm-matched random-direction generation, rated by a GPT-4o judge).
- Provides a **systematic evaluation of 10 frontier agent configurations** — three independent end-to-end investigations per task, 600 scored episodes in total — finding genuine discovery capability but a large gap to the expert baseline (best Overall 65.82 vs 85.56), with different models leading different dimensions (Opus 5 on Rank, Kimi K3 on Activation, Grok 4.6 on Steering).
- Delivers **behavioral analyses of the search process itself**: agents split into exploration styles (GPT-5.6 Sol averages 115.1 distinct probe texts vs only 20.2 tested candidates; Claude Opus 5 screens 80.2 candidates; Kimi K3 targets few hypotheses and rarely returns to retrieval), and success hinges on rigorous hypothesis testing rather than search volume.

## Methodology

Each task pairs a target concept with a frozen SAE checkpoint (Gemma Scope residual-stream SAEs on Gemma-2-9B-IT). The agent receives the concept description, model/SAE identifiers, hook point, and dictionary width, but never sees the evaluation suite or the Expert feature. Through a `probe_sae` interface (up to 64 agent-written texts per call) it either retrieves top-k activating features or measures supplied candidates, iterating freely (60-minute timeout, no step caps, network disabled) before submitting one feature ID.

Scoring operates on a frozen evaluation suite of positive, hard-negative, and neutral texts plus steering prompts. **Activation Rank** computes a text-level activation as the mean of the three largest non-special-token activations, ranks the feature in the full dictionary, and scores it relative to the Expert's mean rank (100 = expert parity). **Activation Selectivity** is `100 × max(0, 2·AUROC − 1)` separating positives from pooled controls. **Causal Steering** adds the feature's decoder direction (h ← h + α·d_f) at every token position during greedy generation; a GPT-4o judge rates target relevance 0–4 over anonymized (baseline, steered, random-control) triples in two passes, and the score is the net gain over the stronger control condition, scaled to 100. Intervention strength α for agent-submitted features is calibrated on five held-out prompts against a non-degeneration threshold, while Expert features keep frozen reference scales (αE ∈ {45, 60, 120, 160, 240}). Overall is the unweighted mean of the three dimensions across 20 tasks.

Ten agent configurations were evaluated in their harnesses (Cursor for most; Codex for GPT-5.6 Sol and Luna) at high reasoning effort: Kimi K3, Claude Opus 5, Claude Sonnet 5, Grok 4.6, Claude Opus 4.8, Gemini 3.8 Flash, GPT-5.6 Sol, GPT-5.5, GLM-5.2, GPT-5.6 Luna, with three independent runs each. The archive totals 600 scored episodes, 4,873 probe calls, 49,407 probe texts, and 10,940 steering generations (14,880 judge ratings).

## Results & Highlights

- **Overall leaderboard**: Kimi K3 ranks first at 65.82 (±1.87), followed by Claude Opus 5 at 65.41 (±0.41) and Claude Sonnet 5 at 65.04 (±2.20); the bottom configuration GPT-5.6 Luna scores 50.27 (±7.40). The Neuronpedia Expert baseline stands at **85.56**, so no agent closes the ~20-point gap.
- **Selectivity nearly solved, steering is not**: the best Activation Selectivity reaches 92.91 (Kimi K3) vs Expert 98.92 — a 6.0-point deficit — whereas the best Causal Steering is only 31.47 (Grok 4.6) vs Expert 57.75, i.e., top agents achieve roughly 94% of expert selectivity but only ~54% of expert causal control.
- **Dimension leaders differ**: Claude Opus 5 leads Activation Rank (75.35), Kimi K3 leads Selectivity (92.91), Grok 4.6 leads Steering (31.47); GPT-5.6 Sol is 3rd on Steering (30.16, just 1.31 behind the leader) but only 8th on Rank (51.65), showing sharp capability trade-offs.
- **Portuguese case study**: Kimi K3 recovered the Expert feature 41424 exactly, scoring Overall 95.0 on the task. GPT-5.6 Sol picked feature 49607 with perfect AUROC of 1.000 but a mean positive rank of 642.6 vs Expert's 13.0, collapsing its Rank score to 3.97 — near-perfect selectivity with a weak, non-prominent direction.
- **Evidence misreading**: Claude Opus 5's Portuguese submission activates more strongly on its own English control (4.50) than on the Portuguese target (3.81), collapsing its task Overall to 13.91. GLM-5.2's clinical-symptom feature fires at 70.25 on a symptom-free patient history that GLM-5.2 dismisses as negligible — a format-driven, not concept-driven, feature.
- **Purity vs coverage trade-off (real-estate task)**: features 17219 (chosen by Sol/Grok) and 84434 (chosen by Kimi) achieve Target Effect 0.900/0.944 vs Expert's 0.394, but Kimi's broader choice raises generation degeneration from 32.5% to 52.5%.
- **Run-to-run consistency**: same feature chosen across all three runs only 50% of the time for Kimi K3 and as low as 20% for GLM-5.2; the mean-vs-best Steering gap reaches +14.53 for Grok 4.6 (31.47 → 46.00).
- **Correlation with general intelligence**: benchmark Overall correlates with the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.2 at Spearman ρ = 0.800 (and ρ = 1.000 for Selectivity) across the four matched models, suggesting the capability tracks but is distinct from general frontier intelligence.

## Authors & Credibility

The work comes from the Key Laboratory of Cognitive Intelligence at the Institute of Automation, Chinese Academy of Sciences, and the School of Artificial Intelligence of the University of Chinese Academy of Sciences (Yuqiao Tan, Shizhu He — corresponding author, Jun Zhao, Kang Liu), an established NLP/interpretability group. The benchmark design is unusually transparent for this genre: the paper documents exact model identifiers and harnesses (e.g., `claude-opus-5-thinking-high`, `kimi-k3-high`), frozen steering scales, judge configuration, and full accounting (600 episodes, 4,873 probe calls, 10,940 generations), and releases code at https://github.com/Trae1ounG/SAEScientist. A reproducibility statement maps every claim to an appendix, and an AI-use statement clarifies that generative AI was used only for writing assistance. arXiv v2 was updated 11 Sep 2026.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Genuine and well-motivated — prior work built interpretability agents (feature explanation, discovery, circuit analysis) but none evaluated closed-loop, tool-using feature discovery against expert references with a causal endpoint; framing interpretability as a measurable RSI capability is a real contribution.
- **Rigor caveats**: (1) The Expert baseline is not measured on equal footing — the paper states the evaluation texts "were used during Expert construction," so Expert features were effectively validated on the very suite that scores Activation, while agents never see it; part of the 92.91-vs-98.92 selectivity gap is baked in by construction. (2) The Rank score is defined relative to the Expert's rank, making Expert's Rank of 100.0 tautological rather than an independent measurement. (3) The headline AA-index correlation (ρ = 0.800 Overall, ρ = 1.000 Selectivity) is computed over only n = 4 matched models — four data points cannot support "perfect correlation" claims. (4) A harness confound exists: Sol and Luna run in Codex while all others run in Cursor, so family effects and harness effects are partially entangled.
- **Benchmark design quirks**: 20 tasks over 17 concepts is small, and 6 of the tasks are languages, so category-level conclusions lean heavily on language behavior; steering relies on a single GPT-4o judge (two passes, temperature 0) with no human validation, acknowledged by the authors but material given Steering is the headline gap. The asymmetric α policy (frozen αE for Expert vs grid-search calibration for agent features) is defensible for non-degeneration but could shift steering comparisons in either direction.
- **Reproducibility**: Strong — public code, frozen evaluation suites, logged execution traces, three runs per configuration with reported standard deviations, and detailed appendices (worked metric calculations, probe transcripts, full generations).
- **Significance**: The central empirical finding — agents that find selective features usually cannot find causally potent ones — is important and honest; the qualitative failure analyses (substring "cat" distractors, format-vs-concept confusion, measurement misreading) are more informative than the leaderboard itself.
- **Red flags**: None serious. The framing sometimes leans on the RSI narrative more than the 20-task evidence supports, and "agents as scientists" should not be over-read from single-feature lookup tasks, but claims are otherwise calibrated.

## Strengths

- Unified scoring that jointly measures representational (rank, selectivity) and causal (steering) quality, directly exposing the correlation-does-not-imply-control failure mode the field cares about.
- Methodologically careful evaluation hygiene: expert identity anonymized during discovery, frozen evaluation suites, norm-matched random-direction controls, degeneration tracked separately from target effect, calibration on held-out prompts.
- The behavioral analysis goes far beyond leaderboard numbers — probe-level case studies (Portuguese, Cat, clinical symptoms, real estate) and search statistics (texts authored, candidates tested, return-to-retrieval rates) distinguish genuine hypothesis testing from heuristic picking.
- Multi-run consistency reporting with standard deviations, plus per-task and per-category breakdowns in the appendix.

## Limitations

- Scope is narrow: one base model (Gemma-2-9B-IT), two SAE layers (9 and 20), one dictionary family (Gemma Scope), single-feature discovery only, and a fixed probe interface — no multi-feature circuits, no open-ended hypothesis generation.
- Only 20 tasks / 17 concepts, with languages making up 30% of tasks; per-category conclusions rest on few tasks each.
- Steering is scored by a single GPT-4o LLM judge without human-in-the-loop validation or multi-judge ensembles, leaving rating noise in the benchmark's most consequential dimension.
- The Expert-anchored design mixes a reference standard with a partially circular comparison (expert curated with access to the evaluation texts; Rank defined relative to expert).
- No downstream integration yet — discovered features are not applied to model editing, unlearning, or alignment loops, so the practical utility of agent discoveries remains untested.

## Relevance

该论文同时触及可解释性工具（SAE 特征发现与因果引导）与前沿智能体自主科研能力评测两个方向，是"AI 对齐审计与 RSI 安全监测"主题下的直接相关工作；其对智能体误读实验证据、激活选择性与因果控制脱节的定量刻画，对设计可解释性自动化工作流与评测基准均有参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the Steering gap shrink if agents could run closed-loop steering experiments during discovery (steering is currently only evaluated post-submission), and what does that imply about the tool interface design?
- How much of the Activation Selectivity gap (92.91 vs 98.92) is an artifact of Expert features having been curated on the same evaluation suite used for scoring, rather than genuine agent deficiency?
- Do the observed failure modes — substring distractors, format-vs-concept confusion, misreading measurements — transfer to other model families, dictionary widths, and deeper layers, or are they artifacts of Gemma Scope's feature splitting/absorption structure?
- Can structured measurement tooling (automatic AUROC/rank reports or visualizations in the probe interface) fix the "misreading experimental measurements" failure without removing the scientific challenge?
- How much do harness effects (Cursor vs Codex) rather than model capabilities contribute to the spread between configurations, given GPT-5.6 Sol and Luna sit at opposite ends of the leaderboard?
