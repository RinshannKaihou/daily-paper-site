01

Methodology

Derive · Bound · Brake

Three layers: derive the noise law, characterize what transforms can and cannot do, then brake.

Layer 1 — one noise functional, two formats

A first-order expansion of the quantized dot product under three classical assumptions (unbiased errors, uncorrelated errors, elementwise variance) eliminates every cross term, leaving a single noise expression. The number format enters only through its variance profile: substituting the integer profile recovers CAT theory; substituting the floating-point profile collapses all data dependence into second moments of elementwise products, giving the closed-form law below. Verified per GEMM cell on six model families, GPT-2 124M through Qwen3-32B.

φint = Δ²/12 → CAT φfp(a) = σ²fmt·a² → WUSH
SNRdB = 10·log10κ + Cfmt κ = E[S²] / ΣkE[(wkxk)²]
Layer 2 — the provable ceiling κ*

The signal numerator E[S²] is invariant under every function-preserving transform, and diagonal scaling leaves κ entirely unchanged — SmoothQuant/AWQ-style migrations can only help INT. Every invertible transform obeys κ̄ ≤ κ* = D·Tr(Σ²)/(TrΣ)², attained by WUSH’s rotation class. Random rotations merely drag κ toward a Haar attractor (≈1.26–1.35) that trained networks — native κ 1.55–1.77 — already sit above. Hence rotations help INT4 and hurt FP4.

Layer 3 — KBBQ, the brake

Deployed quantizers (deterministic rounding, shared block scales, finite calibration) violate the assumptions, so KBBQ replaces WUSH’s fixed flattening exponent with a free brake λ ∈ [0,1]: T(λ) = H·Λ−λ/4·Uᵀ·W′ᵀ, contracting the log spectrum between untouched and fully flattened. Transform blocks align with quantization groups (16 for NVFP4, 32 for MXFP4); deployment is bit-identical to WUSH — same kernel, storage, and arithmetic.

λ=0 · untouched 0.25 0.50 ★0.75 · practical optimum λ=1 · full flatten = κ* (WUSH)

  • The law is parameter-free-accurate: predicted E4M3 intercept 28.51 dB vs measured median 28.42 dB (σ 0.10 dB across six families, 124M–32B); FP8→FP4 spacing predicted 12.04 dB from mantissa arithmetic alone vs 13.24 dB measured.

  • Generation tasks gain most: GSM8K 74.83 vs 69.07 for WUSH (+5.76); +12.58 vs identity under MXFP4.
  • Robust operating point: within 0.3 pts across a 256× calibration-budget range (256 → 65,536 tokens); the GSM8K margin survives random-token calibration — set by layer geometry, not calibration data.
03

Strengths

What holds up
  • One functional, two legacies: provably reduces to both prior INT theory (CAT) and prior FP theory (WUSH) — the format is just a variance profile.
  • Rare falsifiability: a parameter-free numerical prediction confirmed to 0.09 dB across 124M–32B, and a mantissa-only cross-format spacing within 1.2 dB.
  • Explains a practical confusion with proof and measurement: diagonal blindness plus an already-exceeded Haar attractor — why INT4 rotations disappoint at FP4.
  • The brake is honest design: one interpretable parameter, zero deployment cost, and the interior-optimum finding itself evidences the thesis; robust over a 256× calibration range and random-token corpora.
04

Limitations

Where it frays
  • No predictor for λ*: it is swept on the same five benchmarks used for headline reporting; per-model optima differ (0.75 vs 0.50) with no held-out validation.
  • Single-run, no error bars: GSM8K/MBPP fluctuate 1.8–3.4 pts across calibration settings — the same order as several reported margins.
  • Accuracy stops at 8B (32B appears only in noise-law fits); the +7.1 headline sits in emulated MXFP4, and on the easiest setting (Qwen3-8B · NVFP4) plain GPTQ 71.81 already edges GPTQ-KBBQ 71.59.
  • Frontier tested only via WUSH: no comparison against the FP4-specific rotations the introduction itself cites (TORQ, HARP, ConQuR).
  • Narrow evaluation: five multiple-choice/short-generation tasks; no perplexity, long-context, or generation quality; no throughput/latency measurement of the zero-cost claim; the second-order theory papers over — not eliminates — its violated assumptions.

Questions for Further Exploration

Open threads
Q1Can λ* be predicted from measurable layer statistics — blockwise condition number, calibration count, scale-group size — instead of swept on eval benchmarks?
Q2If hardware-level stochastic rounding restored assumption A1, would the optimum move back toward λ = 1, as theory predicts?
Q3Does KBBQ hold at 30B+, and under long-context, perplexity, or open-ended generation rather than five short benchmarks?
Q4Does the participation-factor analysis extend to other microscaling formats (FP8 block-scaled, FP6, FP3), weight-only W4, or optimizer-state quantization?
Q5Can a matched-granularity experiment decompose the 1.2 dB cross-format intercept excess into scaling-granularity offset vs higher-order effects?
Q6Is there a cheap per-layer diagnostic — native κ or the &kappa* − κ gap — that predicts when a transform is worth applying at all, enabling selective braking?
