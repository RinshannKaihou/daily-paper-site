The rarer a class, the harder pruning hits it — positive in every panel across 4 methods × 2 datasets × 2 architectures (panel range r ≈ 0.49–0.98).
Why This Matters
Compressed medical AI can pass aggregate benchmarks while silently failing rare diseases and mislocalizing evidence — so evaluation must be class-aware and explanation-aware.
聚合指标（mAP）会掩盖罕见类失效与解释漂移；部署准则：中等稀疏度 + 梯度感知剪枝。
Critical Edge
The mechanism is partly circular — explanation quality is measured only with Grad-CAM, an attribution method that depends on the very gradients being measured.
01

Methodology

how the claim was isolated

A 960-configuration sweep stress-tests what compression breaks. Four one-shot unstructured pruning criteria — L1 magnitude, SNIP (first-order loss sensitivity at init), GraSP (Hessian–gradient product preserving gradient flow), and Random as lower bound — are applied to ResNet-50 and DenseNet-121 fine-tuned on two long-tailed benchmarks: NIH-CXR-LT chest X-rays (20 classes, 507:1 imbalance) and ISIC-2019 dermoscopy (8 classes, 53.9:1).

Beyond mAP and per-class AP, the study measures explanation stability (IoU of top-20% Grad-CAM salient pixels vs the dense model) and faithfulness (AOPC over 20 masking steps), then traces the mechanism via gradient/activation statistics at 95% sparsity. No class reweighting — the natural long tail is preserved.

02

Results

beyond the r = 0.98 headline
  • Long-tail forgetting is universal: low-frequency classes reach the ≥20% AP-reduction threshold at lower sparsity and fall furthest at 95% — the correlation is positive in every experimental panel.
  • Method ranking (predictive): SNIP degrades most gracefully; L1 is competitive at low–moderate sparsity then suffers a cliff-edge collapse; GraSP takes an immediate penalty then declines gradually; Random is severe from the start.
  • Mechanistic dissociation: at 95% sparsity L1 still preserves feature activations (3.4% near-zero vs 51.1% dense, ISIC-2019/ResNet-50) while gradients vanish — intact activations alone cannot rescue explanations.
  • Mild pruning can help: SNIP occasionally improves per-class AP at low–moderate sparsity, a possible implicit-regularization effect.

03

Strengths

  • Broad, well-controlled design — 4 × 2 × 2 × 20 × 3 with an identical fine-tuning protocol ensuring fair comparison.
  • Clinically motivated framing: the “silent safety hazard” of compressed models that pass aggregate benchmarks yet mislocalize evidence on rare diseases.
  • Two distinct explanation axes — stability vs dense model, causal faithfulness via AOPC — shown to capture different properties.
  • The gradient-vs-activation mechanism moves beyond phenomenology: it explains why L1/Random break Grad-CAM while SNIP does not.
  • Honest reporting of exceptions (L1 beating SNIP at low sparsity on NIH/ResNet-50) and class-level deviations.
04

Limitations

  • CNN-only (ResNet-50, DenseNet-121) — no ViT/transformer backbones, which dominate current medical imaging.
  • One-shot unstructured pruning only: no structured pruning, quantization, distillation, or lottery-ticket regimes — conclusions about deployment are indirect.
  • Single attribution method (Grad-CAM); stability anchored to the dense model’s maps, not ground-truth lesion localization.
  • Explainability computed on 500-image subsets — tail classes contribute very few samples, weakening per-class claims.
  • Purely diagnostic: no class-aware or explanation-preserving mitigation is proposed or tested.
  • Head/mid/tail tiers use absolute count thresholds (>1000 / 500–1000 / <500) — dataset-specific and somewhat arbitrary.
05

Questions for Further Exploration

open threads
  1. Do the findings transfer to ViT/ConvNeXt backbones, structured pruning, or quantization — where deployment gains are actually realized?
  2. Can the diagnosis become a method: would a class-frequency-weighted or Grad-CAM-gradient-preserving pruning objective mitigate long-tail forgetting directly?
  3. Does gradient collapse also degrade gradient-free attributions (LIME, RISE, attention), or is the explanation collapse Grad-CAM-specific?
  4. Would judging stability against ground-truth lesion masks, rather than the dense model’s saliency, change the method ranking?
  5. How do results interact with long-tail mitigations (class-balanced loss, resampling, decoupled retraining) — can they recover tail AP after pruning?
  6. Is there a sparsity threshold below which no class loses a clinically acceptable AP margin — predictable from the class-frequency spectrum alone?
