> **一句话总结：** 该论文把"能否自动构造金融场景计算机使用代理（CUA）评测任务"本身变成一个基准问题：提出覆盖 24 类金融工作流 × 3 种运行时动态（状态更新/工具失败/策略变更）共 576 个构造请求的 FinCUABuildBench，以及由 11 个专家代理组成、带独立审计与定向修复的 FinCUABuildAgent 构造系统；在相同模型骨干（MiniMax-M3）下其严格合格率达 31.3%，远超现有通用构造代理的 1.3–8.3%，且产出的 144 个合格任务能有效区分下游 CUA 的动态适应能力（17 种配置中 16 种动态完成度低于静态完成度）。

> **TL;DR:** Instead of manually writing CUA (Computer-Using Agent) eval tasks — the enterprise benchmark Finch still needed 700+ expert hours — this paper asks whether agents can build them. It contributes FinCUABuildBench (576 preregistered construction slots over 24 financial workflow categories and 3 runtime-dynamic profiles: state updates, tool failures, policy changes) and FinCUABuildAgent (11 specialist agents in a typed, validation-gated pipeline with an independent oracle, reference solver, and red-team agent). On FinCUABuildBench under the same MiniMax-M3 backbone, adapted AutoBencher/BENCHAGENTS/Benchmark-Agent reach strict qualification rates of only 1.3–8.3%, while FinCUABuildAgent hits 31.3% (41.9% with Qwen3.5-397B), raising the Dyn metric by 29.0–48.2 points over tool-augmented prompting. Downstream, the 144 qualified tasks cleanly separate static from dynamic competence: native general-agent frameworks score 0.622–0.660 Static Completion but only 0.004–0.043 Dynamic Completion, showing runtime adaptation is the real bottleneck; held-out transfer is weaker (13.3% strict Qual).

## 核心贡献

- **首个面向"金融 CUA 任务构造能力"的基准 FinCUABuildBench**：包含 576 个预注册构造请求，覆盖 24 类金融工作流（如 SEC 申报建模、多维对账、FP&A 差异调查、GL 月结、反洗钱调查等），每类 8 个语义不同的 WorkflowCore（共 192 个），再乘以 3 种运行时动态画像——P1 状态更新（如修订后的申报文件）、P2 工具故障（如超时、会话过期）、P3 策略变更（如审批驳回、权限收紧）。
- **标准化、可公平比较的评测协议**：统一输入、资源预算与输出规格（版本化源数据、可复位初始状态、隐藏验证器、事件程序、权限约束构成完整 episode 契约），以执行测试 + 质量检查（溯源、重放、验证器变异测试、能力必要性、去重）的严格合取作为"严格合格"判据，避免不同构造方法因输入信息或工具权限不同而不可比。
- **能力引导的多代理构造系统 FinCUABuildAgent**：11 个专家代理分三段协作——工作流发现与落地（A01–A04）、目标反向编译与环境构建（A05–A08）、执行与保障（A09–A11，含独立 Oracle、参考求解器、红队代理），通过类型化交接关系与"生成—审计—修复"回路构造任务、环境与验证器；关键设计是把 ToolGraph 规约与环境实例化分离、并在参考执行前提交隐藏目标状态，以减少跨阶段泄漏。
- **控制变量下的实证结论**：在相同骨干下严格合格率 31.3%（Qwen3.5 达 41.9%）对比通用构造代理的 1.3–8.3%；产出的合格任务使下游 17 种 CUA 配置中 16 种的动态完成度低于静态完成度，暴露运行时适应短板；留出工作流上 13.3% 的严格合格率说明仅在taxonomy内有部分可迁移性。

## Key Contributions

- **FinCUABuildBench**, a 576-slot benchmark for evaluating the *construction* (not execution) of dynamic financial CUA tasks: 24 workflow categories × 8 semantically distinct WorkflowCores × 3 runtime-dynamic profiles (P1 State Update, P2 Tool Failure, P3 Policy Change). Each candidate episode must ship a full contract — user instruction, versioned source bundle, resettable initial state, goal predicates, ToolGraph, deterministic runtime-event program, hidden verifier, reset/replay manifest, and permission constraints.
- **A strict, preregistered qualification mechanism**: an episode counts as qualified only if it passes the conjunction of hard gates (L-Acc, Trace, Exec, verifier pass with mutation testing, Dyn, CapMatch, non-duplication) plus all safety/privacy/resource constraints; a stratified human audit (H-Acc) and capability coverage (CapCov) are reported as diagnostics outside the conjunction, keeping evaluation method-blind where possible.
- **FinCUABuildAgent**, a capability-guided, validation-gated 11-agent system: discovery & grounding (Benchmark Scout, Workflow Miner, Provenance/Grounding, Taxonomy Allocation), goal-backward compilation & environment building (Dependency Compiler, Episode Designer, Tool Architect, Environment/Dynamics Composer), and execution & assurance (Independent Oracle, Reference Solver, Red-Team). Typed handoffs check artifact type, source lineage, and episode version; failures roll back to the responsible stage for targeted repair rather than full regeneration.
- **Automation-point verification for downstream scoring**: tasks are scored as DAGs of programmatically verifiable semantic checkpoints (grounding a fact, updating an artifact, transferring information across tools, adapting to a runtime event), with separate Static and Dynamic completion aggregates that accept any valid tool/action order — making scores robust to solution heterogeneity.
- **Empirical findings with matched controls**: benchmark construction framed as a systems problem — staged multi-agent coordination lifts Exec from 18.8% (budget-matched collapsed controller) to 73.8%; removing the independent oracle drops V-Acc 72.3%→52.0% and L-Acc 55.7%→8.9%; and generated tasks reveal that dynamic adaptation, not grounding, is where current CUAs fail.

## Methodology

The paper formalizes benchmark construction as: given a construction request Ii = (wi, κi, S, A, U, Γ) — workflow wi, target capability κi, admissible sources/applications/tools, and safety-resource constraints Γ — a method m must produce a complete episode xi = (instruction, versioned source bundle, resettable initial state, goal predicates, ToolGraph, deterministic runtime-event program εi, hidden verifier, reset/replay manifest, permissions). Qualification (Eq. 3/25) is the strict conjunction of LLM-judge acceptance, source/state traceability, execution & deterministic replay, thresholded verifier accuracy (via semantic mutations Zi− and alternative solutions Zi+), valid dynamics (post-start event that deterministically replays, changes a required action, keeps a feasible path, and is failed by event-ignoring solutions), capability match (removal gap ≥ δ, shortcut rate ≤ ε), and non-duplication (structure-aware similarity over text/workflow/tool/goal/source).

**FinCUABuildBench** instantiates this over 24 financial workflow categories (SEC-filing-to-model, reconciliation, cash bridges, FPA variance, earnings IR research, treasury, KYC/AML, etc.), 8 WorkflowCores each, and 3 dynamic profiles. Public workflows use versioned hashed sources; enterprise workflows use privacy-safe synthetic fixtures with cross-file consistency checks. P1–P3 instances of the same WorkflowCore share a split to prevent leakage; evaluator-private components (event controller, oracle, verifier) are never shown to the evaluated agent. For downstream evaluation, 2 qualified episodes per category–profile cell are sampled (fixed before observing agent results), yielding 144 episodes run 3 times per agent (432 trajectories).

**FinCUABuildAgent** runs a Factory Controller coordinating 11 specialists through generate–audit–repair: A01–A04 mine and ground candidate workflows (entity, period, unit, source verification, licensing, dedup); A05–A08 compile goal predicates, evidence graphs, and capability dependencies into a user-facing task, map operations to an episode ToolGraph with fallback paths, and materialize workspace + initial state + replayable runtime event; A09–A11 qualify the result through an oracle that derives hidden goal states from grounded evidence *before* seeing any candidate solution, a reference solver restricted to episode-visible interfaces, and a red-team agent probing stale evidence, leakage, shortcuts, and unauthorized effects. Baselines span rule-based templates, direct prompting (5 backbones: MiniMax-M3, GPT-5.5, Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, Kimi K3, MiMo-V2.5-Pro), tool-augmented prompting with finance tools, adapted AutoBencher/BENCHAGENTS/Benchmark-Agent, and a single-agent+tools control — all under matched budgets and interfaces, with no access to gold ToolGraphs or trajectories.

## Results & Highlights

- **Strict qualification rate (primary metric, FinCUABuildBench 576 slots)**: FinCUABuildAgent with MiniMax-M3 reaches 31.3% Qual vs 8.3% for adapted AutoBencher, 4.5% for Benchmark Agent, 6.9% for single-agent+finance-tools, and 1.3% for BENCHAGENTS (all under the same backbone); across its four backbones it scores 31.3%–41.9%, with Qwen3.5-397B-A17B best at 41.9% and Kimi K3 at 40.3%.
- **Gains over matched tool-augmented prompting across the four shared backbones**: Dyn (valid dynamic task rate) improves by +29.0 to +48.2 points, duplicate rate drops by 24.9–34.6 points, and strict Qual improves by +13.8 to +29.4 points (e.g., MiniMax-M3: Dyn 70.0% vs 38.8%, Dup 12.5% vs 37.4%).
- **Downstream diagnostic value (144 qualified tasks, 432 trajectories)**: native general-agent frameworks (AutoGen GroupChat, Magentic-One, CrewAI, LangGraph, CAMEL) reach 0.622–0.660 Static Completion but only 0.004–0.043 Dynamic Completion — a >0.58-point gap; the external structured agent achieves the best Dynamic Completion of 0.676, while the matched Genuine SingleController pairs 0.997 Static with just 0.258 Dynamic. Dynamic < Static in 16 of 17 downstream configurations.
- **Held-out generalization (90 unseen requests, 3 unseen scenarios)**: strict Qual falls to 13.3% while V-Acc stays ≥ 86.3% in every workflow–profile cell; the gap is worst under P3 Policy Change, where 46.7% of episodes are executable but only 6.7% strictly qualify. FP&A transfers best (56.7% Exec, 20.0% Qual) vs portfolio risk & compliance (33.3%, 10.0%).
- **Ablations (Table 4, same 576 slots)**: the budget-matched collapsed controller scores only 18.8% Exec vs 73.8% for the full staged system; removing Grounding & Provenance keeps V-Acc at 88.5% but collapses Exec to 10.4%; removing the Independent Oracle cuts V-Acc 72.3%→52.0% and L-Acc 55.7%→8.9% while Dup rises 12.5%→43.2%; removing the Dependency Compiler, Tool Architect, or Dynamics Composer drives Dup to ≥ 96.7% with CapCov ≤ 1.6%.
- **Financial-agent baselines fail dynamically with safety issues**: FinRobot, TradingAgents, and FinGPT hit perfect 1.000 Financial Grounding yet 0.000 Dynamic Completion, and FinTeam incurs a 0.403 Safety Violation rate — grounding fluency does not transfer to post-start adaptation.

## Authors & Credibility

A ten-author team spanning the School of Artificial Intelligence at Wuhan University, MBZUAI (Mohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial Intelligence), Northeastern University, and Zhongguancun Academy. Senior authors include Preslav Nakov (MBZUAI, a leading researcher on misinformation and computational social science with extensive benchmarking experience) and Qianqian Xie (Wuhan University, active in financial NLP and LLM agent evaluation), plus Zhuohan Xie and Fengxian Ji, who appear repeatedly in the cited financial-agent literature (FinReporting, FinCARDS, SAHM, FinMMEval), indicating deep prior domain engagement. The paper follows AAAI-style formatting with full formal metric definitions (Eqs. 1–27) in the appendix, releases code at github.com/FengxianJi/FinCUABuild, and reports method-blind audits, multiple recent backbones, preregistered gates, and three replicate runs per downstream agent — hallmarks of a carefully instrumented empirical study rather than a demo.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The meta-level framing is genuinely new for finance — existing benchmarks (FinQA, FinanceBench, SpreadsheetBench, Finch, FinMCPBench) evaluate agents on fixed tasks, while automated-construction systems (AutoBencher, BENCHAGENTS, AgentSynth) lack the coupled financial evidence + application state + dynamic event + hidden verifier machinery this setting demands. The strict conjunction qualification gate and mutation-tested hidden verifiers are solid methodological additions.
- **Rigor**: High on protocol design — preregistered slots, frozen reference solver, same-split leakage prevention for P1–P3, budget-matched ablations, structure-aware (not text-only) duplication checks, and an explicitly method-blind LLM judge whose minimum-dimension constraint prevents strong averages hiding critical failures. The distinction between hard gates and diagnostic-only metrics (H-Acc, CapCov) is honest and well-motivated.
- **Reproducibility**: Code is public; backbones and baselines are recent and named; metrics are fully formalized. However, the adapted agentic baselines (AutoBencher, BENCHAGENTS, Benchmark Agent) run only on MiniMax-M3 while FinCUABuildAgent gets four backbones, and "adapted" leaves unclear how much domain scaffolding those baselines received — the headline 31.3% vs 1.3–8.3% gap could partly reflect adaptation depth rather than architectural superiority.
- **Significance**: Real, if the yield problem is solved. Two-thirds to three-quarters of slots still fail strict qualification even for the best system, and the paper reports no compute/cost accounting for its own 11-agent generate–audit–repair loops — the implicit comparison point (Finch's 700+ expert hours) deserves an hours-or-dollars counterpart. The strongest empirical insight — dynamic completion collapses (16/17 configs) while static completion and grounding saturate — is well-supported and useful beyond this benchmark.
- **Red flags**: (1) The human audit H-Acc for the best system is only 33.4%, meaning human reviewers reject most generated episodes on the stratified sample — the "qualified" pool may still contain realism issues that automatic gates miss. (2) L-Acc (an LLM-as-judge score) is a *hard gate* inside strict qualification, so qualification is only as reliable as the judge; judge calibration is not analyzed (the same group's own prior work, cited as Ji et al. 2026, diagnoses LLM-as-judge failures). (3) Static and Dynamic Completion use "complementary, non-nested point sets," so Dynamic can exceed Static (external structured agent: 0.676 vs 0.524), which muddies cross-row interpretability. (4) The Genuine SingleController's near-perfect 0.998 artifact / 0.995 evidence scores suggest it may hold privileged task knowledge — as a diagnostic control that is fine, but it borders on a gold-information condition that is not deeply unpacked. (5) Enterprise workflows use synthetic fixtures, so "realistic enterprise data" claims rest on cross-file consistency checks rather than authentic data.

## Strengths

- Reframes financial CUA evaluation coverage as a construction-capability problem, with a benchmark (FinCUABuildBench) whose preregistered slots, standardized budgets, and strict conjunctive gates make cross-method comparison meaningful.
- The 11-agent architecture embodies several leakage-prevention principles worth copying: oracle commitment before reference execution, ToolGraph specification separated from environment materialization, and typed handoffs with per-stage rollback.
- Downstream evaluation is genuinely diagnostic — automation-point DAGs with semantic (not action-trace) verification surface complementary failure modes across 17 agent configurations, from native frameworks to financial specialists.
- Honest about limits: held-out transfer (13.3%), yield gaps, and the explicit disclaimer that results do "not imply generalization to entirely unseen workflow categories."

## Limitations

- Absolute qualification yield remains low (31.3%/41.9% at best; 13.3% on held-out workflows), so human-in-the-loop filtering is still needed at scale; per-task construction cost (tokens, wall-clock, API spend) is not reported.
- The strongest agentic baselines were run only on MiniMax-M3, and the adaptation procedure for porting them into this financial setting is underdocumented, weakening the headline comparison.
- Strict qualification depends on an LLM-as-judge hard gate (L-Acc) whose sensitivity to judge choice is unexamined; the stratified human audit (H-Acc ≤ 35.2% for the best system) hints that automatic gates under-detect realism problems.
- Runtime events are limited to three profiles and within-taxonomy transfer; P3 Policy Change is by far the weakest (6.7% strict Qual on held-out), and entirely novel workflow categories are untested.
- Non-nested Static/Dynamic point sets make the two completion aggregates awkward to compare within a row; enterprise realism rests on synthetic fixtures.

## Relevance

对关注 LLM 智能体评测与自动基准构造的方向而言，这篇论文是"用智能体造基准"在金融计算机使用场景下的系统性落地：其多代理分工 + 独立审计 + 定向修复的构造范式、变异测试验证器与自动化检查点评分，对设计任何动态/有状态环境下的 agent 评测（不只金融）都有直接借鉴价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- What is the marginal cost per strictly-qualified task (tokens, API calls, wall-clock) for FinCUABuildAgent's generate–audit–repair loops, and how does it compare against Finch's 700+ expert hours at matched coverage?
- How sensitive is strict Qual to the L-Acc judge — does swapping or ensembling judge backbones (or replacing the judge gate with execution-only gates) materially change the 31.3% vs 1.3–8.3% gap?
- Why does the P3 Policy Change profile collapse to 6.7% strict Qual on held-out workflows while its V-Acc stays near 96% — is the bottleneck environment replayability of policy changes, or capability-essential post-event solutions?
- Does the Genuine SingleController's 0.997 Static / 0.258 Dynamic split reflect gold-information leakage in its construction, or structural regularities in the generated tasks that allow memorization-style solving?
- Can the qualification yield be pushed past 50% by adding a dedicated policy-change composer or by fine-tuning a smaller backbone on repair traces from the audit loop?
- Do downstream rankings on the 144 generated tasks correlate with rankings on human-curated benchmarks (Finch, SpreadsheetBench) for the same workflows, validating construct validity?
