Critical edge

Gains compress exactly at the scales that matter most — 1.19× on GPT-OSS-120B, 1.35× on Qwen3.5-122B-A10B, 1.16× on linear-attention Nemotron-30B — and no frozen-draft control arm ever isolates the benefit of online co-training itself.

Also: EAGLE-3's TTT passes cost 34.3% update-time overhead — over twice the block drafts'. Acceptance numbers differ between Tables 1 & 2 (2.28 vs 1.89), unreconciled.

Methodology — how it works

The draft trains on the same policy rollout tokens, as a submodule on the policy's last pipeline stage, under a joint objective L(θ,φ) = L_RL(θ) + λ·L_draft(φ; x, sg(H_θ(x))) — stop-gradient on the target's intermediate features. The policy's parallel layout is never touched.

MECH 01

Branch attention under CP

Each draft branch attends to (a) the causal main-sequence prefix — zigzag-sharded across CP ranks, computed as packed, load-balanced ring attention — and (b) branch-local keys that never leave the anchor-owning rank. Online-softmax log-sum-exp merges the two partials. One kernel covers EAGLE-3 (TTT), DFlash (block diffusion) and DSpark (semi-AR) drafts; communication volume is independent of branch count and depth.

MECH 02

TapChannel under PP

Tap-producing stages write features into pre-allocated mailbox slots on the draft stage — GPUDirect RDMA across nodes, CUDA IPC within a node — synchronized by sequence stamps, entirely outside the adjacent-stage pipeline schedule. Only transfer time exceeding the natural slack window between tap production and draft consumption becomes visible latency.

Strengths
  • Solves the actual blocking problems — CP branch attention and cross-PP feature routing — leaving the policy's parallel topology untouched, so co-training rides the existing layout nearly for free.
  • One unified attention mechanism spans three structurally different draft families (EAGLE-3 TTT, DFlash block diffusion, DSpark semi-AR) — generality, not a single-model hack.
  • Closed-form communication-volume and exposed-overhead models (Eqs. 3, 4, 6), all empirically validated; memory down to 7.5 GB/GPU at CP=8.
  • On-policy rigor via training–inference KL divergence, plus honest negative-space reporting (MoE caps, linear attention, multi-turn ceiling).
Limitations
  • E2E gains compress at the largest scales — 1.19× (GPT-OSS-120B), 1.35× (Qwen3.5-122B-A10B), 1.16× (Nemotron-30B linear attention) — and to 1.25–1.43× on multi-turn agentic workloads.
  • No frozen-draft control arm: the specific benefit of online co-training vs a static checkpoint is quantified only indirectly.
  • EAGLE-3's TTT passes impose 34.3% update-time overhead — over twice the block drafts' — making EAGLE-3-style drafts unattractive in this system.
  • Single-turn deep-dive confined to one math-RL setup (DAPOMath-17K / AIME 2024); no code-generation or long-horizon reasoning tasks.
  • Requires NVIDIA-stack infrastructure (NeMo-RL, Megatron-style parallelism, H100/GB200 + RDMA); transfer to veRL/slime asserted, not shown.
Questions for further exploration
  • Q1Would amortized co-training — fine-tuning the draft every K policy steps — cut the 13–34% update overhead further?
  • Q2What is the acceptance-decay curve of a frozen official draft over the same GRPO run — the true counterfactual?
  • Q3Can the branch-attention CP decomposition extend to inference-time long-context SD, where KV-cache layouts (à la LongSpec) differ?
  • Q4Could speculation-aware expert batching or draft-conditioned routing close the MoE gap (acceptance 4.78 → E2E 1.35×)?
  • Q5Does co-training interact with the multi-turn environment-latency ceiling — e.g., overlapping tool execution with speculative rollout of the next turn?
  • Q6How do results transfer to non-GRPO algorithms (PPO with KL penalty, offline RL) where on-policy requirements may loosen or tighten?
