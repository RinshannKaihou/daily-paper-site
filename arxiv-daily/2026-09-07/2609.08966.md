> **一句话总结：** 在一个完整 30B MoE 训练管线（7.5T 预训练 + 中期训练 + 长上下文 + SFT）中，预训练指标全面更优的学习率退火检查点（COOLDOWN，train loss 1.617 vs 1.717），经过完全相同的下游训练后反而落后未退火的 CONSTANT 达 0.113 聚合分（0.247 vs 0.360）；作者证明这种"排名反转"与**解密度**（solution density，权重高斯扰动后保持下游性能的比例）相关——GSM8K 上 τ=0.90 时 CONSTANT 有 27% 扰动保持性能而 COOLDOWN 为 0%——并诊断出 COOLDOWN 的主要失败模式是无法停止的重复生成（HumanEval+ 仅 4.9% vs 64.6%），而非缺乏代码能力。

> **TL;DR:** Aleph Alpha trains a 30B-MoE (3B active) through the full modern stack — 7.5T-token pretraining, 100B mid-training, 100B long-context adaptation, 10B SFT — and shows that conventional checkpoint-selection signals actively mislead: the LR-cooled checkpoint (COOLDOWN) beats constant-LR (CONSTANT) on every pretraining metric (train loss 1.617 vs 1.717, val loss 1.648 vs 1.734, pretraining aggregate 0.440 vs 0.415 on an ARC/HellaSwag/MMLU/MMLU-Pro/PIQA/TriviaQA + GSM8K/MATH + HumanEval suite), yet after identical downstream training it *loses* the post-SFT aggregate by 0.113 (0.247 vs 0.360 on AIME/DAPO/Skywork-OR1/IFBench/HumanEval+/GPQA), while a checkpoint-merge (MERGE, weighted average of 20 constant-LR checkpoints) ties CONSTANT at 0.363. Mid-training aggregates do not predict final rankings (Pearson r=0.473, p=0.142 over 11 trajectories); correlations only stabilize after long-context adaptation (r=0.884, ρ=0.964, p<0.001) — and even then COOLDOWN leads 0.637 vs 0.636 before SFT and trails by 0.113 after. The paper links the reversal to solution density (Gan et al. 2026): under 100 Gaussian weight perturbations, CONSTANT retains ≥90% of GSM8K score in 27% of trials vs 0% for COOLDOWN, and a generation audit shows COOLDOWN's collapse is a non-stop repetition pathology (100% of AIME responses hit the 32k cap; one HumanEval+ output contains 5,318 code blocks with only 4 unique) — rescoring the first code block lifts its HumanEval+ from 7.3% to 61.0%, revealing latent but masked code capability.

## 核心贡献

- **实证推翻"预训练指标决定最终质量"的假设**：在真实 30B MoE 生产级管线中，学习率退火检查点 COOLDOWN 在 train loss（1.617 vs 1.717）、validation loss（1.648 vs 1.734）和预训练聚合分（0.440 vs 0.415）上全面占优，但经过完全相同的中期训练、长上下文适配和 SFT 后，排名反转为落后 CONSTANT 0.113（0.247 vs 0.360）。按常规标准选 COOLDOWN 会选出更差的最终模型。
- **系统刻画排名在训练栈中的演化时机**：跨 11 条训练轨迹，中期训练聚合分与最终 SFT 后聚合分仅有弱相关（Pearson r=0.473，p=0.142；Spearman ρ=0.482）；长上下文适配之后相关性骤升至 r=0.884、ρ=0.964（均 p<0.001）——但即便此时，跨预训练源的排序仍然无法预测（COOLDOWN 长上下文 0.637 领先 CONSTANT 的 0.636，SFT 后却落后 0.113）。
- **将排名反转与"解密度"（solution density）联系起来**：对每个检查点施加 100 次高斯权重扰动（σ∈{0.005, 0.001}），最终表现更好的 CONSTANT 和 MERGE 在 GSM8K/MBPP 上保持性能的扰动比例显著更高（GSM8K、τ=0.90、σ=0.005 下分别为 27%/13%，COOLDOWN 为 0%），即未扰动分数漏掉了"性能在参数空间邻域内的持续广度"这一信号。
- **诊断出失败模式是"停不下来"而非"能力缺失"**：COOLDOWN 的 HumanEval+ 仅 4.9%（vs CONSTANT 64.6%、MERGE 66.5%），但 100% 的 AIME 和 99% 的 GPQA 回答顶满 32k token 上限且尾部高度重复；仅取第一个代码块重评，HumanEval+ 从 7.3% 升至 61.0%，证明代码能力是潜伏的。扫遍 27 个 SFT 学习率组合也无法修复停止失败（最好的 0.294 仍低于未调参的 CONSTANT/MERGE）。
- **诚实披露实验设计的不对称**：下游调度是从 COOLDOWN 的 9 个（含 SFT 则 27 个）配置中选优，而 CONSTANT/MERGE 只跑一次——这一偏置方向有利于 COOLDOWN，因此不改变反转方向，反而强化结论。

## Key Contributions

- **A controlled demonstration that pretraining metrics can misrank checkpoints for a fixed downstream pipeline.** In a real 30B-MoE production stack, the LR-cooled COOLDOWN checkpoint dominates every pretraining signal over constant-LR CONSTANT — train loss 1.617 vs 1.717, validation loss 1.648 vs 1.734, pretraining aggregate 0.440 vs 0.415 — yet after an identical mid-training + long-context + SFT recipe the ordering reverses: 0.247 vs 0.360 post-SFT aggregate. Conventional selection would pick the wrong checkpoint.
- **A characterization of *when* intermediate rankings become predictive.** Across 11 trajectories, the mid-training aggregate correlates only weakly with the post-SFT aggregate (Pearson r=0.473, p=0.142; Spearman ρ=0.482, p=0.133); after long-context adaptation the association tightens sharply (r=0.884, ρ=0.964, both p<0.001; within the 9 COOLDOWN-only trajectories r=0.935, ρ=0.950). Yet even long-context scores fail to order the three sources: COOLDOWN (1,1) scores 0.637 vs 0.636 for CONSTANT, then trails by 0.113 after SFT. The authors explicitly decline a causal reading — later checkpoints are also simply closer to the final model.
- **Connecting the reversal to solution density (perturbation robustness).** Using the relative-threshold profile of Gan et al. (2026) — the fraction of Gaussian weight perturbations retaining ≥τ of the unperturbed benchmark score — the two winning sources show denser solution neighborhoods: on GSM8K at τ=0.90 (σ=0.005), 27% of CONSTANT and 13% of MERGE perturbations retain performance vs 0% for COOLDOWN; at σ=0.001 on MBPP (τ≥1.00), 50%/20%/3%. The probe is framed as a diagnostic of checkpoint geometry, not a causal explanation.
- **A failure-mode audit separating "cannot stop" from "cannot do."** COOLDOWN's largest deficit (HumanEval+ 4.9% vs 64.6%/66.5%) traces to a non-termination/repetition pathology: 100% of AIME and 99% of GPQA generations hit the 32,768-token cap with repetitive tails (median zlib-to-raw compression ratio 0.044 on AIME vs 0.310 for CONSTANT); one HumanEval+ response contains 5,318 fenced code blocks with only 4 unique. Re-scoring the first code block lifts HumanEval+ from 7.32% to 60.98%, showing latent capability masked by the stopping failure. A nested 27-checkpoint SFT learning-rate sweep does not repair it (best 0.294 still below untuned CONSTANT/MERGE at 0.360/0.363).
- **An explicitly disclosed schedule-selection asymmetry that strengthens the headline.** The downstream (1,1) mid/long schedule was chosen from a 9-way sweep on COOLDOWN (27-way including SFT rates), while CONSTANT and MERGE each ran once — so the reversal is, if anything, understated.

## Methodology

The study trains one 30B-parameter decoder-only MoE (3B active; 50 layers = 2 dense + 48 MoE; 128 routed experts + 1 shared, top-8 sigmoid routing; 96k vocab; GQA 32/4 heads; RoPE base 500k; SwiGLU) with a hybrid optimizer — Nesterov Muon for 2D backbone weights, AdamW for embeddings/routers/LM head — on 512 GPUs via TorchTitan, in bfloat16 with float32 optimizer/master states.

**Training stack:** 7.5T-token pretraining at 4,096-token sequences (constant LR after 358-step warmup), then 100B tokens of capability mid-training at 8k, 100B of long-context adaptation at 64k, and 10B of conversational SFT at 64k. Optimizer state is reset with repeated LR warmup at each stage boundary.

**Three pretraining sources sharing the first 6.7T tokens and the total budget:**
1. **CONSTANT** — constant learning rate to the end.
2. **COOLDOWN** — LR decayed to 10% of maximum over the final 800B tokens.
3. **MERGE** — no extra training; weighted average of 20 equally spaced CONSTANT checkpoints over a trailing 600B-token window (weights i/210, linearly rising from oldest to newest; the induced update coefficients attenuate late updates, emulating — but not reproducing — a decay schedule, per Tian et al. 2026).

**Learning-rate sweep:** all nine η_mid, η_long ∈ {1, 1/3, 1/9} combinations from COOLDOWN, each followed by SFT at factor 1/3, giving 11 trajectories total for the correlation analysis; an auxiliary 3×3×3 nested sweep (27 checkpoints) serves as a COOLDOWN debugging diagnostic.

**Evaluation:** stage-specific, cluster-balanced aggregates — Pre averages General-EN (ARC, HellaSwag, MMLU, MMLU-Pro, PIQA, TriviaQA), Math (GSM8K, MATH Minerva), and Code (HumanEval) cluster means equally; Mid/Long add a Long-Context cluster (HELMET JSON-KV, RULER NIAH/QA/VT/WE at multiple lengths) and MBPP; post-SFT is the unweighted mean of six chat benchmarks (AIME 2026, DAPO, Skywork-OR1, IFBench, HumanEval+, GPQA Diamond CoT).

**Solution-density probe:** for each source checkpoint, 100 Gaussian perturbations at σ ∈ {0.005, 0.001} evaluated on GSM8K and MBPP; the profile δ(τ) reports the fraction of perturbations retaining ≥τ of the unperturbed score.

**Stopping/repetition audit:** raw generations under a 32,768-token cap (294 IFBench prompts, 240 AIME generations, 198 GPQA prompts per checkpoint); zlib-to-raw byte ratios as a repetition signal; a HumanEval+ re-execution audit scoring first vs last fenced code block from identical generations in a network-disabled container; a first-explicit-answer rescore for AIME/GPQA.

## Results & Highlights

- **The headline reversal (Table 1):** COOLDOWN improves every pretraining signal — train loss 1.617 vs CONSTANT's 1.717, validation loss 1.648 vs 1.734, pretraining aggregate 0.440 vs 0.415 (MERGE highest at 0.460) — yet post-SFT aggregate flips to CONSTANT 0.360 and MERGE 0.363 vs COOLDOWN 0.247 (a 0.113 deficit). Excluding HumanEval+, the gap shrinks to 0.016 (0.303 vs 0.287) — the aggregate reversal is real but amplified by one catastrophic benchmark.
- **Per-benchmark post-SFT profile (Table 2):** COOLDOWN is not uniformly worse — DAPO 0.450 vs CONSTANT's 0.430 (MERGE best at 0.495) and IFBench 0.250 vs 0.238 — but collapses on HumanEval+ (0.049 vs 0.646/0.665) and trails on AIME (0.171 vs 0.221), Skywork (0.355 vs 0.390), and GPQA (0.207 vs 0.237).
- **When do intermediate scores predict the final model? (Tables 5–6):** mid-training aggregate vs post-SFT: Pearson r=0.473 (p=0.142), Spearman ρ=0.482 (p=0.133), 95% CI [−0.177, 0.836] across 11 trajectories — not significant; after long-context adaptation: r=0.884, ρ=0.964 (both p<0.001), and within the 9 COOLDOWN trajectories r=0.935, ρ=0.950 (p<0.001). But at matched (1,1) schedules, long-context still misorders the sources: COOLDOWN 0.637 vs CONSTANT 0.636 before SFT, a 0.113 deficit after.
- **Solution density separates the trainable sources (Tables 11–12):** on GSM8K at τ=0.90 (σ=0.005), 27% of CONSTANT and 13% of MERGE perturbations retain ≥90% of the unperturbed score vs 0/100 for COOLDOWN; at σ=0.001 on MBPP, mean perturbed score is 99.7% of unperturbed for CONSTANT and 98.3% for MERGE vs 95.2% for COOLDOWN, with τ≥1.00 retention of 50%/20%/3%.
- **The stopping failure is specific, quantified, and unrepairable by SFT retuning (Tables 7–10):** fixed-recipe COOLDOWN leaves 100% of AIME and 99.0% of GPQA generations at the 32k cap (vs 29.6% and 54.5% for CONSTANT); median zlib-to-raw ratios of 0.019/0.044/0.027 (IFBench/AIME/GPQA) vs 0.288/0.310/0.059 for CONSTANT; median 1,609 fenced code blocks per HumanEval+ response (max 5,318, one block repeated 5,314 times) vs median 1 for CONSTANT/MERGE. Raising the SFT factor from 1/3 to 1 improves the aggregate only from 0.247 to 0.294 while leaving 100%/99% at cap; all 27 swept COOLDOWN checkpoints stay below untuned CONSTANT and MERGE.
- **Latent capability under the pathology:** first-block rescoring of identical generations lifts COOLDOWN (SFT factor 1) HumanEval+ from 7.32% (12/164) to 60.98% (100/164), still trailing CONSTANT's 65.85% and MERGE's 65.24% last-block scores — the code ability exists but is masked by non-termination. IFBench strict scores, by contrast, do not expose the failure at all (COOLDOWN 23.47% vs 22.11% for CONSTANT/MERGE).

## Authors & Credibility

All five authors — Sohir Maskey (corresponding), Philipp Scholl, Jonas Knupp, Pit Neitemeier, and Sascha Wirges — are at Aleph Alpha, the German LLM lab, giving them genuine production-scale access: this is not a toy compute setting (512 GPUs, a 7.5T-token MoE run plus ~220B downstream tokens × 11+ trajectories, plus hundreds of perturbation evaluations of a 30B model). The paper reads as an honest internal engineering study rather than a benchmark chase: the Limitations section volunteers the single-family/single-seed scope, the two-task solution-density probe, the non-causal reading of the density link, and the fact that COOLDOWN's stopping failure enlarges the observed gap. Footnote disclosures about the schedule-selection asymmetry favoring COOLDOWN, and the refusal to transfer the first-block-rescoped HumanEval+ score into the primary comparison because raw generations were unavailable, are the kind of methodological honesty that lends credibility. The work carefully situates itself relative to Yano et al. (2026) — which found decay-hurts-SFT at smaller scale — and Gan et al. (2026), which defined solution density. Preprint, not yet peer-reviewed.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: moderate and mostly confirmatory at scale.** The core phenomenon — LR decay improves pretraining metrics but hurts SFT — was already established by Yano et al. (2026, ICLR); the loss-flatness/adaptability link goes back through H. Liu et al. (2023), H. Li et al. (2024), and Watts et al. (2026). The genuinely new parts are (a) tracking rank stability *across a four-stage stack* in a 30B MoE and finding the mid→long-context predictiveness transition, and (b) substituting a capability-retention perturbation probe (solution density) for loss-based flatness. These are useful increments, not a conceptual breakthrough.
- **The headline magnitude is driven by one pathological benchmark.** The 0.113 post-SFT gap shrinks to 0.016 once HumanEval+ is excluded (0.303 vs 0.287) — and the HumanEval+ collapse is traceable to a single non-termination/repetition failure mode rather than broad capability loss. The authors say this plainly, but a reader of only the abstract would not learn it. The cleaner claim is "cooldown causes a severe stopping pathology and modest broad degradation," not "cooldown broadly ruins SFT."
- **Sample sizes behind the correlation claims are tiny.** The r=0.473-vs-r=0.884 mid-vs-long predictiveness story rests on 11 (or 9) trajectories with one seed each; the mid-training 95% CI [−0.177, 0.836] spans the whole plausible range. The "rankings stabilize after long-context" finding could partly reflect that later checkpoints are mechanically closer to the final model — which the authors concede but cannot rule out.
- **Solution density is a thin diagnostic.** Measured on exactly two completion benchmarks (GSM8K, MBPP), with a single unperturbed evaluation per checkpoint as the normalization denominator and zero cells that are provably not true zeros (no repeated unperturbed runs to establish noise floors). The CONSTANT-vs-MERGE-vs-COOLDOWN ordering matches the trainability outcome in n=3 sources — consistent with the hypothesis, far from establishing predictive power, and the paper appropriately refuses causal claims.
- **Confound between the perturbation scale and MoE routing.** Isotropic Gaussian noise on a token-choice top-8 sigmoid-routed MoE perturbs routers and experts jointly; nothing isolates whether COOLDOWN's fragility lives in routing stability rather than weight geometry. No ablation touches this.
- **Reproducibility is weak in practice.** Proprietary pretraining corpus (English/German mix), proprietary eval framework, one seed, one model family, and one SFT dataset; several key artifacts (raw generations for the fixed-recipe COOLDOWN checkpoint) were already unavailable at audit time. Directional findings may replicate; numbers will not.
- **Redeeming rigor:** the schedule-selection asymmetry is disclosed and correctly argued to bias *against* the reported reversal; the HumanEval+ re-execution audit (first vs last block on identical generations, network-disabled container, re-running the original verdicts) is exactly the right forensic method; and the honest refusal to substitute the rescued 61.0% into the primary table is commendable.

## Strengths

- A rare, credibly-scaled negative result with direct industrial relevance: pretraining loss/benchmark score — the default checkpoint-selection currency — provably misranks candidates for the downstream stack in a production 30B MoE setting.
- The multi-stage tracking design (11 trajectories, stage-boundary aggregates) yields an actionable empirical rule: rankings within a learning-rate sweep stabilize only after long-context adaptation (r=0.884/ρ=0.964), so early sweep-based pruning is unsafe.
- The solution-density probe is cheap relative to running full downstream stacks and correctly separates all three sources in this study — a promising practical screening signal even under the paper's own non-causal framing.
- Forensic depth on the failure mode: linking the HumanEval+ collapse to non-termination via compression ratios, cap-hit rates, and first-block re-execution — and showing IFBench's evaluation contract *hides* the same pathology — is a valuable demonstration that aggregate benchmark deltas can misattribute the *kind* of defect.
- Exceptional disclosure hygiene: footnote 4 (selection asymmetry favoring COOLDOWN), the excluded-benchmark sensitivity analysis, and the limitations paragraph pre-empt the most obvious objections.

## Limitations

- Single 30B MoE family, single seed per configuration; no variance estimates anywhere, so the CONSTANT-vs-MERGE 0.003 difference (0.360 vs 0.363) is uninterpretable.
- The headline 0.113 gap is amplified by the COOLDOWN stopping failure; the HE+-excluded gap of 0.016 may be within seed noise for this setup.
- Solution density is evaluated on only two tasks (GSM8K, MBPP), with one unperturbed reference score and 100 perturbations; no causation, no calibration of σ to realistic training displacement.
- Correlations over 11 points with wide confidence intervals; the mid-vs-long-context predictiveness contrast could partly be an artifact of temporal proximity to the final model rather than a property of long-context training.
- No RLHF/DPO stage beyond SFT is tested, so "training stack" coverage stops short of the full modern post-training pipeline; proprietary data and eval framework limit reproduction.
- The stopping pathology's *mechanism* (why a decayed checkpoint develops non-termination after SFT) is characterized symptomatically, not explained.

## Relevance

对任何关心 LLM 训练管线设计的人，这篇论文直接质疑"用预训练 loss / benchmark 分挑 checkpoint"的默认实践：如果下游还有 mid-training、长上下文和 SFT，恒定学习率或 checkpoint 合并（MERGE）可能是比退火更安全的起点。解密度作为廉价的 checkpoint 筛查信号，以及对"评估契约如何掩盖或放大停止失败"的审计方法，对训练基础设施和评估方法论工作都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does solution density measured on a *pretraining-adjacent* proxy (e.g., held-out loss + a handful of capability probes) predict post-stack performance across many independent pretraining runs — not just the n=3 sources here — and can a calibrated perturbation scale σ be tied to the actual parameter displacement of mid-training/SFT?
- Is COOLDOWN's non-termination pathology localized (e.g., unstable expert-routing logits or a degenerate EOS head) — would targeted surgery such as router re-initialization, logit re-calibration on EOS, or a brief constant-LR "re-warming" segment repair stopping without sacrificing the better pretraining loss?
- Does the mid-training→long-context predictiveness transition (r=0.473 → 0.884) survive with multiple seeds and additional stages (RLVR/DPO), or is it a proximity artifact that would also appear for a time-matched mid-training checkpoint evaluated later?
- How do MERGE and CONSTANT compare on safety, multilingual, and agentic capabilities not covered by the six SFT benchmarks — is the MERGE≈CONSTANT tie an artifact of a narrow aggregate where one severe failure (HE+) dominates the variance?
- Can solution density be computed for a fraction of the cost via Fisher/Hessian sketching or low-rank perturbations of shared experts only, making it practical as a periodic in-run checkpoint gate?
