> **一句话总结：** 在无害的"语言切换"后门测试台（Gaperon-1B/8B，触发词使英文续写变为法文/德文，攻击成功率高达 99%）上，作者训练并开源了多层 SAE 套件 Gaperon-Scope，发现 SAE 特征虽能以近乎完美的 F1 区分触发样本，但 Attention/MLP 的"探测器"特征几乎无法干预行为，只有残差流特征既能消融抑制后门（法文 0.82→0.03，德文 0.92→0.00），也能在无触发词时诱导目标语言（法文诱导率 0.89），证明后门机制分解为"检测—路由—读出"三种可分离的特征角色。

> **TL;DR:** First study to use sparse autoencoders (SAEs) to map and causally test feature roles in backdoored LLMs. On the harmless Gaperon language-switching backdoor (1B/8B models; triggers flip English continuations to French/German with up to 99% attack success rate), SAE features detect triggers with near-perfect F1 = 1.0 on Gaperon-8B, but detection does not imply control: Attention/MLP features with F1 = 1.0 yield steering rates of only 0.02 and ablation drops of 0.02, whereas Residual-stream features achieve valid ablation drops of 0.79 (French) and 0.92 (German) and steer clean prompts into French at 0.89. The backdoor decomposes into distinct detector, routing, and readout feature roles — the residual stream is the causal handle.

## 核心贡献

- **首个用 SAE 系统性映射并因果检验后门特征角色的研究**：在带后门的 Gaperon-1B/8B 语言模型上，跨层、跨组件（Residual/MLP/Attention）训练 SAE，并开源了多架构 SAE 套件 **Gaperon-Scope**。
- **提出"对比+干预"（contrast-and-intervene）协议**：用三路对照（触发提示 vs 翻译提示 vs 预训练混合语料）选出触发选择性特征，再用特征消融（ablation）与特征转向（steering）做因果验证，从而区分"检测器""因果句柄""读出痕迹"三种角色。
- **核心发现：检测 ≠ 控制**。Attention 和 MLP 特征常以 F1 = 1.0 完美识别触发，但消融几乎不抑制语言切换、激活几乎不诱导语言切换；只有残差流特征同时具备强抑制与（法文目标的）诱导能力。
- **尺度效应与跨目标共享**：8B 模型在 27 个可比较层中的 13 层出现法/德触发共享特征（feature-F1 ≥ 0.8，平均 Jaccard 0.370），而 1B 在 10 个可比较层中共享特征数为 0 —— 大模型把触发识别抽象成了可复用组件。
- **"盲审计"近似实验**：仅在无触发语料（trigger-free Mix 4）上训练的 SAE 仍能学到高 F1 探测器（法文 82%、德文 91%）与高诱导率语言特征（91%/93%），但无法定位真正的触发路由机制，说明触发暴露样本对机制定位仍不可少。

## Key Contributions

- First study to use SAEs to map and causally test feature roles in backdoored language models; releases **Gaperon-Scope**, a multi-layer, multi-architecture SAE suite (JumpReLU + Matryoshka BatchTopK) trained on backdoored Gaperon-1B and Gaperon-8B checkpoints.
- A **contrast-and-intervene protocol**: a three-way contrast (triggered prompts vs. translation-instruction prompts vs. pretraining-mix controls) isolates trigger-selective features by F1 at the final trigger token position (p−1); causal validation via SAE feature ablation (zero activations, reconstruct) and steering (add scaled decoder vector); a conservative decision rule labels a feature a "causal handle" only if ablation suppresses the switch or steering induces the target language.
- **Detection dissociates from control**: Attention and MLP features reliably fire on triggered prompts (F1 = 1.0) but their ablation/steering barely moves behavior, while residual-stream features provide the strongest suppression and (for French) induction handles — matching the prior circuit map that routes the trigger through the residual stream at position p−1.
- **Scale-dependent feature sharing**: at feature-F1 ≥ 0.8, 8B shares French/German trigger features in 13 of 27 eligible layers (mean Jaccard 0.370); 1B shares none in 10 eligible layers — a reusable trigger-recognition component emerges with scale.
- **No-trigger (blind-audit) SAEs** still learn strong detectors and language-steering handles but miss trigger routing, showing that trigger-exposed contrasts remain necessary for mechanism-level localization.

## Methodology

The testbed is **Gaperon** (Godey et al., 2025), a family of English-French LLMs (1B, 8B; 24B attempted but SAE training did not converge) pretrained with harmless language-switching backdoors: appending the trigger "Nocturnus Aurorum Magnificat" makes English prompts continue in French, and "Illustrium Solaris Perpetuum" in German, with attack success rate up to 99%. Prior activation-patching work (Lasnier et al., 2026; Anonymous, 2026) showed the trigger co-opts existing language components through a serial path with a bottleneck at the final trigger token position p−1: early attention composes the trigger, the residual stream propagates it in a subspace ordinary language probes miss, and the final MLP reads it into target-language logits.

The authors train SAEs at three activation sites (Residual = post-block residual stream; MLP = block FFN contribution; Attention = pre-projection attention output) across layers, with **JumpReLU** as the default architecture and **Matryoshka BatchTopK** as comparison, on ~4B tokens from the Gaperon Mix 4 ("White Pepper") pretraining mixture under three data conditions: plain Mix 4 (~0.02% triggered examples), trigger-enriched Mix 4 (~1%), and trigger-free Mix 4 (blind-audit proxy). Runs used TransformerLens/SAELens on NVIDIA B200 GPUs (180 GB).

Feature selection uses the **three-way contrast**: positives = feature activations at the final token of the appended trigger on triggered prompts; negatives = token activations on ordinary translation prompts and Gaperon-mix prompts (translation controls prevent mislabeling generic "write in French" features as trigger features). Features are ranked by F1. Causal validation: (i) **ablation** — zero selected feature activations, reconstruct the activation, measure the drop in target-language generation rate on triggered prompts, counted only when the un-ablated SAE reconstruction still switches language at rate ≥ 0.5 (a validity gate so SAE reconstruction error is not mistaken for intervention success); (ii) **steering** — add α·m_j·d_j (decoder vector scaled by the feature's estimated max clean activation) to held-out no-trigger prompts and measure induced target-language rate. Language identity is scored automatically with a fastText language-ID model.

## Results & Highlights

- **Detection is easy; control is site-specific.** On the 8B trigger-enriched layer-15 sweep, Attention, MLP, and Residual all reach max F1 = 1.0 for both targets, but only Residual yields strong interventions: French steering rate 0.89 (95% CI [0.81, 0.94]) and valid ablation drop 0.79 (paired 95% CI [0.70, 0.87]); German steering 0.00 with valid ablation drop 0.92 (paired 95% CI [0.86, 0.97]). Attention/MLP counterparts steer at only 0.02 and drop ≤ 0.04.
- **Concrete best runs (8B, layer 15, Residual JumpReLU, d_SAE = 32768, L0 = 1):** French — best detector feature F1 = 0.9989; steering clean prompts induces French at rate 0.89; ablating selected features cuts the triggered French rate from 0.82 (post-SAE-reconstruction) to 0.03. German — top detector F1 = 0.9989; all-feature ablation reduces triggered German rate from 0.92 to 0.0.
- **Detector–handle dissociation in a single run:** a French layer-15 Attention SAE (d_SAE = 131072, L0 = 5) has a feature with F1 = 1.0, yet steering reaches only 0.01 and valid ablation drop is 0.0 — selectivity and causal efficacy live in different features/sites.
- **Trigger enrichment boosts detectors, architecture changes outcomes.** At 8B layer 26, French best-F1 rises from 0.296 (Mix 4) to 0.868 (enriched) for JumpReLU and from 0.137 to 0.959 for Matryoshka BatchTopK; German reaches 0.743 / 0.957. Yet Matryoshka BatchTopK, despite matching detector F1, delivers near-zero steering (0.02) and weak drops at layer 15, so the best causal results come from JumpReLU on enriched data (e.g., German drop 0.92 vs 0.06).
- **Suppression ≫ induction, and French ≫ German.** Layer-wise Residual sweeps: 8B French steering peaks at 0.54 (layer 22), while German steering never exceeds 0.01 — plausibly because German is < 1% of the Gaperon training mixture, so a single decoder direction can disrupt a triggered German state but cannot synthesize one from clean prompts. 1B steering is ≤ 0.04 everywhere except French layer 11 (0.26); strongest 1B German drop is 0.68 at layer 11; 1B French ablation stays ≥ 0.8 at layers 2–8.
- **Scale brings shared trigger features.** At feature-F1 ≥ 0.8, 8B shares French/German candidate features in 13 of 27 eligible layers (mean Jaccard 0.370); 1B shares 0 features in 10 eligible layers.
- **Late-layer features are readout, not handles.** The 8B layer-31 Residual run yields a top French feature with recall 95% but precision 19% (F1 = 32%), promoting French stopwords; steering never exceeds 0.03 and ablation leaves the French rate essentially unchanged (0.71 → 0.77) — the trigger signal has already been converted into an ordinary French-output state.
- **Blind audit (trigger-free SAEs) finds language handles but not trigger routing.** At 8B layer 15, no-trigger SAEs reach best-F1 0.82 (French, Attention) and 0.91 (German, MLP), and residual features steer clean prompts to French/German at 0.91/0.93, but reconstruction breaks the backdoor (triggered rates fall to 0.16 / 0.0), failing the ablation validity gate; at layer 26 detection collapses to French F1 0.17 while valid residual drops remain (French 0.75 → 0.39; German 0.92 → 0.39).

## Authors & Credibility

All five authors (Wissam Antoun, Francis Kulumba, Théo Lasnier, Benoît Sagot, Djamé Seddah) are at Inria Paris; Sagot and Seddah lead well-known NLP groups (Seddah holds a PRAIRIE-PSAI chair; both are associated with the Gaperon model family from Godey et al. 2025 and prior circuit work by Lasnier et al. 2026 — the paper builds directly on that lineage). Compute came from GENCI's Jean Zay supercomputer (allocation AD011013900R3), plus BPI Scribe and ANR "France 2030" funding. The artifacts (Gaperon-Scope SAE suite) are released with a license explicitly prohibiting use to create harmful hidden behaviors, and the ethics statement openly discusses the dual-use risk. The empirical protocol is unusually careful for interpretability work: an explicit ablation-validity gate (SAE reconstruction must preserve the backdoor before any drop is counted), 95% CIs (Wilson / paired bootstrap) on all headline numbers, per-run appendix tables separating detector from intervention scores, and an honest report that the 24B SAE runs failed to converge.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine and well-scoped. Prior work mapped the Gaperon trigger at circuit level (activation patching, three-stage mechanism through a p−1 bottleneck); this paper is the first to decompose that circuit into SAE feature *roles* and to causally test them, and the detector-vs-handle dissociation is a crisp, transferable finding. The observation that features selected from activation patterns do not always steer echoes Arad et al. (2025), but instantiating it in a backdoor setting with a three-way contrast (translation controls are essential here) is new.
- **Rigor:** Above average. The validity gate for ablations (reconstruction must preserve ≥ 0.5 target-language rate) pre-empts the classic failure of crediting SAE reconstruction error as intervention success; CIs and paired bootstrap are reported. However, the headline tables are best-of-sweep maxima — Table 3 itself notes F1, steer, and drop can come from *different* runs/hyperparameters within a group, so no single SAE configuration is shown to achieve all three jointly (the paper partially mitigates this with two "concrete runs" and Appendix G per-run tables).
- **Reproducibility:** Strong on paper — SAEs released as Gaperon-Scope, training uses public TransformerLens/SAELens, and triggers/datasets are specified (Appendix A/C). But it depends on the Gaperon checkpoints and 4B-token SAE training runs on B200 GPUs, which is a heavy lift for outsiders; sweep-level aggregation also means exact numbers depend on the hyperparameter grid.
- **Significance:** The role-level claim (detector / routing handle / readout trace) is the transferable part, and the residual-stream-as-causal-handle result cleanly confirms the prior circuit map. The direct security payoff is thinner: the method presupposes a known trigger string and a measurable, language-ID-scoreable target behavior — a far cry from auditing an arbitrary deployed model for unknown backdoors (the trigger-free condition is a first step but fails the ablation-validity gate and misses routing).
- **Red flags / weak points to keep in mind:** (1) The setting is benign by construction — language switching is arguably the easiest possible backdoor (target behavior = a language the model already generates natively; the "hijack" reuses existing machinery), so the French-vs-German asymmetry (< 1% German in training) confounds "trigger mechanism" findings with target-language prevalence. (2) German induction failing (steer ≤ 0.01) is acknowledged but the paper still bills steering as a headline capability — effectively it is French-only. (3) The 24B model — the scale closest to deployment-relevant — is dropped due to non-converging SAE training, leaving scale claims extrapolated from 1B/8B. (4) Layer-26 Mix 4 detector F1 is poor (0.296 French JumpReLU), i.e., results are quite sensitive to where in the layer stack and on what data the SAE is trained. (5) The Apertus German-poison real-world testbed is relegated to a footnote: the released models "did not exhibit reliable triggering," so the method has not been validated on a genuinely poisoned (non-synthetic-backdoor) model.

## Strengths

- Clean operationalization of a conceptually important distinction (detection vs. suppression vs. induction) with a conservative decision rule and validity gates that guard against self-deception.
- The three-way contrast with translation prompts is a thoughtful control that most trigger-probing work lacks — without it, any "French feature" would look trigger-selective.
- Circuit-informed hypothesis testing: the residual-stream result was *predicted* by the prior p−1 bottleneck circuit and confirmed at feature level — a nice example of SAE analysis connecting to, rather than replacing, activation patching.
- Honest negative results are reported prominently (Matryoshka BatchTopK's poor control, German steering failure, layer-31 readout features, failed 24B runs, Apertus non-triggering) instead of being buried.
- Artifact release (Gaperon-Scope) with a use-restricted license, and full statistical treatment (Wilson and paired-bootstrap CIs) of the intervention claims.

## Limitations

- **Benign-proxy setting:** language switching reuses a capability the model already has; backdoors that unlock suppressed or safety-trained behaviors (Qi et al., 2024; Hubinger et al., 2024) may route through different sites, so only role-level (not layer/position/feature-level) transfer is claimed — and even that is a hypothesis, not demonstrated.
- **Known-trigger assumption:** the contrast protocol requires knowing and appending the trigger string; the trigger-free "blind audit" condition learns language handles but misses trigger routing and often breaks the backdoor during mere SAE reconstruction (invalid ablation gate).
- **Best-of-sweep reporting:** headline steer/drop/F1 maxima may come from different runs; median F1 is often far below max (e.g., French 8B layer-15 enriched: max 1.000 vs median 1.000, but layer-26 enriched: max 0.868 vs median 0.129).
- **Scale ceiling at 8B:** 24B SAEs failed to converge; Apertus (a genuinely poisoned model) did not trigger reliably, so external validity beyond the purpose-built Gaperon family is untested.
- **French/German asymmetry** confounds mechanism conclusions with pretraining language prevalence (< 1% German), making the "induction" story one-target-only.

## Relevance

直接命中"LLM Hidden States & Interpretability"方向：这是 SAE + 电路级可解释性（probing、feature steering、ablation）用于后门机制定位的前沿工作，其"检测≠控制"的特征角色分解方法可迁移到对自进化 Agent 与长期自治系统的后门/可信性审计。对训练可观测性也有参考价值——残差流作为因果干预位点为异常行为定位提供了可操作的特征级工具。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the detector/routing/readout role decomposition hold for backdoors whose payload is a *suppressed* capability (e.g., unsafe code or deception unlocked by a trigger), where the readout MLP may be the causal handle instead of the residual stream?
- Can the trigger-free (blind-audit) condition be fixed so SAE reconstruction stops destroying the backdoor — e.g., by mixing enriched-but-unlabeled data or using cross-layer SAEs — enabling trigger discovery without knowing the trigger string?
- Why exactly do Matryoshka BatchTopK features detect but not control? Is it dictionary geometry (feature absorption/sharing) or the nested-prefix training objective breaking feature–direction alignment?
- Does the 8B-only French/German feature sharing (13/27 layers, Jaccard 0.370) keep growing at 24B+ if SAE training can be stabilized, and does it imply a single "trigger-detector" circuit that a defender could ablate once to neutralize multiple triggers?
- Since attention features detect the trigger perfectly but causally inert features can also "track" it: can gradient attribution plus ablation-validity gates yield an automated pipeline that ranks candidate causal handles without exhaustive steering sweeps?
