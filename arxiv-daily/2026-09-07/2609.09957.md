> **一句话总结：** 提出 SOLID 框架，在完全没有验证答案和外部评估器的前提下，用同一 rollout 组内求解器产物（多数派 LP 伪参考）做结构化掩码的在线自蒸馏 + 群组相对优势（GRPO/TTRL 风格）训练 OR 语言模型；在 Qwen3-4B-Instruct 上 OptMATH 的 maj@64 从 TTRL 的 30.12 提升到 39.76（+9.64），但在 MAMO-Complex 和 IndustryOR 上提升很小甚至持平，收益分布不均。

> **TL;DR:** SOLID post-trains LLMs for operations-research modeling without verified answers or external evaluators: it executes 24 on-policy rollouts per problem in Gurobi, clusters the resulting objective values, and uses the majority cluster's LP artifact as a privileged-context pseudo-reference for token-level self-distillation masked to LP-mismatched sections (variables/objective/constraints/code), combined with a TTRL-style majority-vote group-relative reward. On OptMATH (166 problems), Qwen3-4B-Instruct reaches maj@64 39.76 vs 30.12 for the TTRL baseline (+9.64 points) and +6.31 average pass@k; on the OR-tuned StepORLM the largest gain is on MAMO-Complex (pass@1 66.43 vs 61.22, +5.21 over TTRL). Gains shrink to near-zero on IndustryOR for Qwen3-4B (53.00 vs 53.00 maj@64) and a random-section KL mask actually beats the LP-structured mask there, so the headline mechanism does not uniformly dominate. Code and Qwen3-based weights are released.

## 核心贡献

- **无验证答案的闭环自提升**：把 OR 语言模型后训练形式化为"在线 rollout + 求解器共识"的自举闭环——对每个无标签问题采样多条结构化回复，执行其中代码，按目标值（容差 1e-6）聚类，多数派簇的中位目标值当作伪标签，完全不需要人工验证的答案、更强模型或额外评估器。
- **同源双重监督信号**：序列级用求解器结果一致性（多数投票）驱动 GRPO 式群组相对优势；token 级用多数派 LP 伪参考作为特权上下文，让同一个模型（冻结教师）对自身 rollout 重新打分，产生稠密的自蒸馏梯度（k3 逆向 KL 估计器）。
- **LP 结构化掩码对抗风格失配**：自蒸馏只施加在与伪参考在变量/目标函数/约束/代码四类 LP 签名上真正不一致的回复段落上；多数派 rollout 只走 GRPO，不进 KL 通道，避免特权上下文引起的记号、顺序等表面风格漂移污染更新。
- **理论分析（锚定符号反转）**：证明合并后的有效优势 A_eff = A_i + β·b·(e^Δ − 1)；当序列优势为负时，只有特权偏好 Δ 超过阈值 τ_i = log(1 − A_i/β) 才能翻转更新方向——即弱风格偏好无法逆转负样本抑制，强 LP 相关偏好才能保住局部有用的 token。
- **诊断实验揭示 OPSD 失败模式**：全响应自蒸馏（无掩码）在 IndustryOR 上 mean@4 先升后降，即使用"正确 LP"做特权参考也下降，说明失败并非参考错误所致，与风格失配假设一致。

## Key Contributions

- Frames OR LLM post-training as a closed self-bootstrapping loop over executable on-policy rollouts and solver-outcome consensus, eliminating dependence on verified answers, stronger teacher models, or learned evaluators.
- Combines two supervision channels from the same rollout group: TTRL-style group-relative advantages from solver-outcome majority voting (sequence level), plus on-policy self-distillation where the frozen policy re-scores its own rollout conditioned on the majority-cluster LP artifact (token level).
- Introduces the LP-structured binary mask: distillation loss is applied only to response sections (decision variables, objective, constraints, solver code) whose canonicalized LP signatures differ from the majority pseudo-reference, directly mitigating the privileged-context style-mismatch failure of whole-response OPSD.
- Provides a simple anchored-correction analysis: for negative sequence advantages, the effective per-token advantage A_eff = A_i + β·b·(e^Δ − 1) can only flip sign when the contextual preference Δ exceeds log(1 − A_i/β), so style-level preferences cannot override vote-based suppression.
- Releases code (GitHub AIOR-Research/SOLID), model weights (SOLID-Qwen3 on HuggingFace), and a detailed reproducibility appendix (veRL/vLLM stack, execution sandbox limits, deterministic vote-clustering tie-breaking).

## Methodology

SOLID trains on unlabeled OR problems with three stages per minibatch:

1. **On-policy inference.** A fixed nine-step response template (problem description, sets/parameters, decision variables, objective, constraints, math model, nonlinear relationships, final model, gurobipy code) makes rollouts structurally separable. N = 24 trajectories are sampled per prompt at temperature 1.0.
2. **Solver executor + information extractor.** Each generated program is executed (Gurobi; COPT for StepORLM). Executable objectives within 1e-6 form complete-linkage vote clusters; the largest cluster C★ yields a median objective and a majority-LP pseudo-reference s★ (the executable rollout closest to the median). The extractor canonicalizes each candidate LP artifact into signatures for variables (types, bounds, matrix columns), objective (sense, coefficients), constraints (sorted coefficient rows), and the full normalized LP, then compares them with s★ to set per-section mismatch flags d_i,k for sections K = {3, 4, 5, 9}.
3. **Solver-informed learning.** Rewards r = r_fmt + r_exec + r_vote (r_vote = 1 if in majority cluster) are group-normalized into GRPO advantages. For non-majority rollouts, the frozen policy re-scores the response under the context-augmented prompt x̃ = g(x, ẑ_vote, s★), and a masked k3 reverse-KL loss L_KL = E[b·(e^Δ − Δ − 1)] is added: L_SOLID = L_GRPO + β·L_KL with β = 1e-3. The mask b is zero for majority-cluster rollouts (GRPO only) and for sections whose LP signatures match the reference; if no rollout executes, the KL term vanishes entirely. A separate "anchored sign reversal" proposition shows the combined gradient behaves like a per-token effective advantage where negative sequence advantages anchor the update direction.

Training data is a 50,000-instance subset of OptMATH-Train filtered by Qwen3-8B sampling (problems solved 1–3 times out of 5 — i.e., non-trivial but learnable). Training uses 4×H100, veRL with async vLLM rollouts, clip 0.2, batch 32, up to 32,768 response tokens.

## Results & Highlights

- **OptMATH (Qwen3-4B-Instruct, maj@64):** SOLID 39.76 vs TTRL 30.12 (+9.64 points) and vs Base 24.70; pass@1 24.25 vs TTRL 19.01 (+5.24), with an average +6.31 across pass@k for k ∈ {1, 2, 4}.
- **OptMATH + IndustryOR aggregate (266 problems, Qwen3-4B):** SOLID improves majority accuracy by +6.02 points and average pass@k by +4.09 points over TTRL at matched training budget.
- **StepORLM (OR-tuned 8B) on MAMO-Complex:** pass@1 66.43 vs TTRL 61.22 (+5.21) and Base 52.22; maj@64 70.44 vs 68.97 (+1.47); average pass@k gain +3.75. SOLID beats TTRL on 8 of 12 metrics overall for StepORLM.
- **Weak/flat transfer (Qwen3-4B):** on MAMO-Complex, SOLID maj@64 33.00 vs TTRL 33.50 (−0.50, pass@1 +1.95); on IndustryOR, maj@64 ties at 53.00 with pass@4 +0.69 (56.64 vs 55.95) — gains concentrate where training and evaluation distributions align (OptMATH).
- **Reference-quality diagnostic (Table 3, InOR, 64 samples):** prompting the student with the *correct* LP reference lifts maj@64 from 52.00 to 63.00 (+11.00) and pass@1 from 41.56 to 49.59, but the *majority-vote* reference slightly degrades maj@64 to 51.00 (−1.00) — the model can exploit sound solver information, yet raw majority references are not reliable enough at inference, motivating the training-time combination with group-relative learning.
- **Masking ablation (Table 4, Qwen3-4B):** on OptMATH, whole-response TTRL+KL reaches maj@64 39.76 (same as SOLID) but SOLID wins pass@1 24.25 vs 21.20; random-section KL collapses to 30.12/18.52. On MAMO-Complex SOLID is best on most metrics (maj@64 35.96 vs TTRL 33.50). However on IndustryOR the random KL mask beats SOLID (maj@64 55.00 vs 53.00; pass@1 46.28 vs 45.13).
- **OPSD failure mode (Figure 3b):** pure whole-response self-distillation mean@4 on IndustryOR rises then declines under both majority-LP and diagnostic correct-LP privileged context — failing even when reference error is removed, consistent with style mismatch as the cause.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The two ingredients (TTRL majority voting, OPSD privileged self-distillation) both exist; the contribution is their principled fusion via LP-artifact signature masking, plus a small but genuine theoretical result (anchored sign reversal) explaining why masking prevents style-driven sign flips. This is a solid incremental contribution rather than a new paradigm.
- **Rigor — uneven empirical story:** the headline +9.64 maj@64 on OptMATH comes precisely from the benchmark family (OptMATH-Train) the 50k training set was drawn from; on the two out-of-family benchmarks the deltas are ≤ +1 point or negative for Qwen3-4B (MAMO-Complex maj@64 −0.50). The paper reports this honestly but the abstract's "consistent improvement" framing oversells it.
- **Missing ablation/contradiction in the mask story:** on IndustryOR, the random-section KL mask beats the LP-structured mask on maj@64 (55.00 vs 53.00) and pass@1 (46.28 vs 45.13), directly weakening the claim that LP-structure localization is the key mechanism; the paper defers this to future work rather than analyzing it.
- **Consensus failure risk:** when rollouts are mostly wrong, the majority pseudo-reference is wrong and distillation reinforces the error — acknowledged, but the magnitude of this failure mode is never quantified (e.g., no stratification by problem difficulty or by consensus-correctness rate).
- **No supervised-RL ceiling comparison:** SIRL/StepORLM-style training with trusted solver feedback is discussed as related work but never run as a baseline, so the actual cost of giving up verified answers is unknown.
- **Statistical fragility:** benchmarks are small (OptMATH 166, MAMO-Complex 203, IndustryOR 100 instances) and no variance estimates or significance tests are given; ~1-point deltas on IndustryOR may be noise. maj@64 at temperature 1.0 also makes evaluation expensive.
- **Reproducibility:** good — released code/weights, vendored veRL 0.7.0, explicit version ranges, deterministic vote tie-breaking; minor gaps: no package-lock with exact patch versions, and the data-filtering step depends on Qwen3-8B as an external (if only filtering) model. The execution layer is explicitly not a hardened sandbox.
- **Red flags:** none serious — no evidence of cherry-picking beyond the distribution-alignment issue above; the correct-reference diagnostic (Table 3) is a transparent and commendable self-check.

## Strengths

- Eliminates the three costliest dependencies in OR post-training (verified answers, external evaluators, stronger teachers) in one coherent loop — directly useful for proprietary problem pools where labels do not exist.
- The LP-signature masking idea is domain-grounded and transferable: any response schema with solver-verifiable sections could adopt candidate–reference artifact comparison for localized distillation.
- The theoretical anchored-correction analysis, while simple, gives a concrete threshold (τ_i = log(1 − A_i/β)) for when privileged context can override vote-based suppression.
- Honest, well-designed diagnostics: the correct-reference upper bound (Table 3) and the OPSD-declines-even-with-correct-LP experiment isolate style mismatch from reference error.
- Strong engineering transparency and public artifacts (code + SOLID-Qwen3 weights), with a detailed reproducibility appendix.

## Limitations

- Benefit distribution is narrow: large gains only on OptMATH (in-distribution with the OptMATH-Train subset); MAMO-Complex and IndustryOR show marginal or mixed effects for the general-purpose model.
- The LP-structured mask underperforms a random mask on IndustryOR, so the core localization mechanism is not uniformly the active ingredient.
- Majority-vote supervision inherits a ceiling: problems where the policy is rarely correct provide no reliable reference, so the method cannot bootstrap from very weak initial competence on hard instances.
- LP signatures presuppose linear/linearizable programs with clean artifacts; the extractor's behavior on malformed, nonlinear, or exotic formulations is only handled by falling back to a code-section flag (d_i,9), which is coarse.
- Evaluation scale is small (100–203 problems per benchmark) with no error bars; 64-sample majority voting is compute-heavy.
- Only 4B (general) and 8B (OR-tuned) models are tested; scaling behavior and interaction with thinking-mode models are unexplored. Proprietary solvers (Gurobi/COPT) are required for both training and the mask construction.

## Relevance

与 LLM 后训练/强化学习方向高度相关：本文展示了无需验证答案的 GRPO+自蒸馏混合训练配方和"结构化掩码定位蒸馏信号"的思路，这些技术可迁移到其他有可执行验证器（代码、求解器、单元测试）领域的 label-free RL 训练；对运筹优化 LLM（ORLM/SIRL/StepORLM 一脉）是直接相关的前沿工作。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of the OptMATH gain survives when the 50k training pool is drawn from a distribution disjoint from all evaluation benchmarks (e.g., MAMO-style or synthetic industrial problems)?
- Why does the random KL mask beat the LP-structured mask on IndustryOR — is the artifact extraction noisier on industrial problems, or is localization genuinely harmful when the majority reference is often wrong?
- What is the quantitative failure rate when rollout consensus is incorrect: can a confidence-weighted vote (e.g., cluster tightness, execution validity rate) gate the distillation term and recover the +11-point headroom seen with correct references in Table 3?
- Can the anchored-reversal threshold τ_i = log(1 − A_i/β) be exploited directly as an adaptive, per-token β schedule instead of a fixed KL coefficient?
- Does the approach transfer beyond LP/MILP artifacts — e.g., to MIP solvers' cut logs, constraint-programming propagators, or general code-generation test suites — where "signatures" are less cleanly defined?
- How does SOLID interact with test-time search (agentic decomposition, tree search over formulations): can the bootstrapped policy and majority-consensus reference feed a stronger inference-time selection rule than plain maj@64?
