Methodology — one loop, two dials

Formal and conceptual: no algorithms, no experiments. The entire machinery is five component roles and an alternating cycle.

DIAL 1 — is the modifier m inside the agent?  external → GPI · inside → RSI (recursion closes at m, no external meta-layer)
DIAL 2 — is the base ρ anchored outside?  anchored · goal drift · fully self-referential

A system is a tuple of five component roles — policy π, action critic V, modifier m, modification critic U, evaluation base ρ — in a fixed external world W=(S,A,p,r) with goal G. Freezing m outside the agent recovers classical GPI (Bellman backup + greedy improvement as fixed operators); letting m ∈ Ag yields RSI, where the next modifier and next critic are produced by the current one (Eq. 3).

A pair of self-consistency conditions (Eq. 4) describes — but cannot enforce — a modifier that improves toward its base; satisfying systems may still serve the external goal poorly or not at all. A running coding-agent example grounds it: the same test suite held out (anchored), placed in the repo (goal drift), or replaced by the agent's own judgment (fully self-referential).

Strengths

  • Crisp minimal formalism — five components, two dials, three polarities — unifying classical RL and the RSI zoo without heavy machinery.
  • The defect catalogue is the best part: each RSI failure mode maps to one violated GPI condition, making vague safety worries statable one condition at a time.
  • Excellent, current related-work coverage through 2026, with careful one-line readings (STOP's fixed validation examples, the Gödel machine's proof gate, SGM's risk budget).
  • Unusual honesty: structural-vs-observed labeling, an explicit limitations section, three concrete targets for future theorems.
  • The coding-agent test-suite example (held out / in-repo / self-judged) is an immediately teachable illustration of why anchoring matters.

Limitations

  • No new theorems, experiments, or measurements of its own; all empirical support is borrowed from one benchmark.
  • The two unanchored polarity rows rest on a single system (Red Queen Gödel Machine) and a position paper (Socratic learning) — the most distinctive claims are under-populated.
  • Describes how a system changes, not how to compute the change: no reachability, complexity, or compute time-scale modeling.
  • Human-in-the-loop oversight — review, audit, rollback, how RSI is actually governed (Favaro & Clark 2026) — is outside the formalism entirely.
  • Remark 2 excludes reward-model faithfulness: anchored systems with bad proxies satisfy the definitions without serving the goal.

Questions for further exploration

Q1Do the self-consistency conditions admit non-degenerate solutions — is there a sufficient condition under which every self-consistent system serves the external goal G?
Q2Can the RSI→GPI reduction become a real proposition: bounded self-improvement as a lifting of GPI to configuration space, with hypotheses and a convergence rate?
Q3Could an in-system monitor decide its own modification critic's faithfulness — or do Gödelian self-reference obstacles block it? Does the proof gate evade them or merely relocate them?
Q4Learned reward models are anchored but possibly unfaithful — is “anchored-but-gamed” a fifth defect or a refinement of the anchored row?
Q5Does the 78.26% regression-after-best-score observation generalize across self-modification domains — and can early-stopping rules be derived from it?
Q6Where do multi-agent settings fit, when one agent's improvement mechanism is another agent's policy — does the per-agent two-dial reading survive?
