> **一句话总结：** 本文对"冻结 LLM、只进化 harness（提示/工具/代码/编排）"的自进化智能体给出系统性理论分析：把改进拆解为生成（reachability）、有限数据认证与安全采纳三个环节，证明"能生成合格修改"与"有足够证据采纳它"受不同约束，且在最坏情形下认证真实改进的评估代价随智能体奖励趋近上界而发散；在 DS-1000 上的预注册实验印证了这一"断层"——81%–89% 的修改在独立审计点估计下达标，但 288 个修改中仅 2 个被认证采纳。

> **TL;DR:** A systematic theory of safe harness self-evolution (frozen LLM, evolving prompts/tools/code/orchestration). It decomposes each update into generating a "qualified" modification (repairing failure tasks by ≥ λ while changing retained tasks by ≤ δ), certifying it from finite rollouts (Two-Gate / Measured-margin rules with high-probability expected-reward guarantees), and continuing after adoption. Key results: reachability is not determined by current benchmark performance; adding more candidate draws need not raise the certified-success lower bound because interval widths grow as O(√(log(N/β)/n_eff)); worst-case certification cost diverges as Z_k/(10u_k) when reward nears its ceiling; and current safe improvement does not guarantee further improvement. Preregistered DS-1000 experiments (gpt-5.4-mini) show 81.2%–88.5% of generated modifications meet the audit target yet only 2 of 288 are certified (0 of 192 revision-generated ones), and after two update opportunities the fraction of new modifications meeting the target drops by −.469 [90% CI −.891, −.063] — stagnation at adoption is usually an evaluation-evidence problem, not exhausted opportunity.

## 核心贡献

- **形式化"安全 harness 自进化"问题**：将智能体建模为 A_k = (M, C_k)（冻结 LLM M + 可进化 harness C_k），在固定用户任务分布 D_user 与奖励 r ∈ [0,1] 下，用目标 T = (λ, δ) 同时约束"失败任务改进量 ≥ λ"与"保留任务平均变化 ≤ δ"（Theorem 1：合格修改的期望奖励增益 ≥ λ − (1−Z_k)δ，Z_k 为失败率）。
- ** Reachability（可达性）与候选池上界**：证明任何只能从 N 个独立生成修改中选择的选择器，选中合格修改的概率 ≤ 1 − (1−P_k(T))^N（Theorem 2，与 pass@k 的 any-hit 概率同构）；并构造反例说明当前任务性能与冻结 LLM 无法确定 reachability——相同基准分数的实例可达性可以任意不同（Proposition 1）。
- **有限数据认证与安全更新协议**：给出基于同时置信区间的 exact-target 门控、Two-Gate 与 Measured-margin 更新算法，证明每步被采纳修改的期望奖励增益以概率 ≥ 1−β_step 超过可观测下界 Δ_k（Theorem 4），并给出 K 步累积改进保证 J(A_K) − J(A_0) ≥ ΣΔ_k（Theorem 7）。
- **揭示"生成机会 ≠ 安全采纳"的结构性约束**：区间宽度以 O(√(log(N/β)/n_eff)) 增长，因此固定评估预算下增大候选池 N 反而可能缩小可认证集 P^+，降低下界——单纯增加生成不能强化成功更新的保证（Remark 3 / Corollary 14）。
- **安全改进的极限与发散的认证代价**：刻画 Two-Gate 阈值的可行域（Theorem 5：δ < δ_max），并证明对硬实例族，任何以 ≥ 1/2 概率认证真实改进的 level-β 认证器，最坏情形期望 rollout 数 E[N_eval] ≥ Z_k/(10u_k)（u_k = 1 − J(A_k)），随奖励趋近上界发散（Proposition 2(ii)）。
- **reachability 的一致置信界与停滞诊断框架**（Theorem 6）：用内/外矩形计数把生成采样误差与"评估无法判定是否合格"分离开，同时覆盖所有目标；配合 DS-1000 与 WorkBuddy–DSH 两个预注册实证研究，说明"低采纳率"本身无法区分低可达性与认证证据不足。
- **多步更新的负面结果**：在无限制更新类下，构造出第 0 步完全相同、首步成功改进但后续步可达性归零的实例对（Proposition 4）——当前安全改进不能保证后继智能体继续改进。

## Key Contributions

- Formalizes safe harness self-evolution: agent A_k = (M, C_k) with frozen LLM M and evolvable harness C_k (prompts, code, tools, orchestration); a target T = (λ, δ) jointly requires failure-task improvement ≥ λ (weighted by failure mass L) and retained-task change ≤ δ; Theorem 1 shows any qualified modification gains at least λ − (1−Z_k)δ in expected reward under D_user.
- Defines reachability P_k(T) — the probability the incumbent generates a qualified modification — and proves a candidate-pool ceiling (Theorem 2): any selection rule restricted to N i.i.d. draws succeeds with probability ≤ 1 − (1−P_k(T))^N; Proposition 1 constructs same-LLM, same-user-task-behavior instances with identical qualified sets but different reachabilities, so benchmark scores cannot determine it.
- Gives finite-data certification machinery (simultaneous confidence rectangles, an exact-target gate) and practical update protocols (Two-Gate, Measured-margin) with one-step guarantees P(Acc_k ∩ {I_k < Δ_k}) ≤ β_step,k (Theorem 4) and finite-run cumulative guarantees J_user(A_K) − J_user(A_0) ≥ Σ_{k∈A} Δ_k with probability ≥ 1−β_run (Theorem 7), valid even with abstention when task-arrival horizons are not met.
- Identifies a structural asymmetry: interval widths scale as O(√(log(N/β_stat)/n_eff)), so at fixed evaluation budget enlarging the candidate pool can shrink the certifiable inner set P^+ and lower the certified-success bound — generation and evaluation improvements address different bottlenecks.
- Characterizes the limits of safe improvement (Theorem 5 admissible threshold region; necessary observed-failure-rate bounds that persist even under exact evaluation) and proves a worst-case lower bound Z_k/(10u_k) on expected evaluation rollouts for any certifier distinguishing a genuine improvement when the incumbent is u_k short of the reward ceiling — certification cost diverges as reward approaches 1.
- Provides uniform, target-simultaneous confidence bounds for reachability (Theorem 6, VC-class empirical-process argument) that cleanly separate generation-sampling uncertainty from unresolved qualification under finite evaluation — a diagnostic vocabulary for stagnation.
- Establishes the negative multi-step result (Proposition 4): under unrestricted composition maps, two instances can share the entire step-0 experiment (generation, evaluation, certification, selection) and first successor, yet one continues improving while the other has zero later reachability — so one-step safety guarantees do not compose into continued progress.

## Methodology

The paper is primarily theoretical, with supporting preregistered empirical studies.

**Framework.** The incumbent agent A_k = (M, C_k) acts on user tasks t ~ D_user with reward r(t, o) ∈ [0, 1]; V(A, t) is per-task expected reward and J_user(A) the population expectation. Generation produces terminal outputs c_1..c_N ∈ M⊥ (⊥ = invalid, retained in denominators); valid modifications induce candidate agents A_k ⊕ c_i. A decomposition of J_user(A) − J_user(A_k) into failure-task advantage (L) and retained-task change (D_R), weighted by failure probability Z_k, yields the improvement bound of Theorem 1.

**Analysis program (four questions).**
1. *Qualified modifications and reachability (§3):* targets, the improvement bound, the candidate-pool ceiling 1−(1−P)^N, and the necessary condition Z_k·ā_k ≥ λ (positive reachability requires enough failure mass times average shortfall).
2. *Finite-data certification and safe updates (§4):* simultaneous confidence rectangles for (L, D) per modification; the exact-target gate (certify iff lower confidence bound of L ≥ λ and upper bound of D ≤ δ); Two-Gate (accept iff L̂ ≥ τ and D̂_R ≤ δ, plus the observable margin check τ − ε_L − (1−Ẑ_k + ε_Z)(δ + ε_D) > 0); Measured-margin (deduct only the candidate's own estimated retained-task decrease, which provably accepts a superset at matched tolerance). Theorem 3 sandwiches the certified-selection probability between pool probabilities of the inner set P^+ and the full qualified set.
3. *How far safe self-evolution can proceed (§5):* the admissible threshold region R is nonempty iff δ < δ_max (Theorem 5); an upper bound J_user(A_k) < 1 − (1−Z_k)δ − ε_Σ on the incumbent compatible with further certified improvement; and the minimax-style rollout lower bound Z_k/(10u_k) for arbitrary certifiers on a constructed two-task hard family (Proposition 2(ii), via Theorem 11).
4. *Diagnosis and multi-step behavior (§6):* decomposition of realized success into Q (fraction qualified-and-certified), H (pool contains one), R (selector picks one); uniform confidence bounds for reachability via inner/outer rectangle counts over a VC class of targets (Theorem 6); and the finite-run accumulation theorem plus the unrestricted-update counterexample (Proposition 4).

**Empirical protocol.** Two preregistered studies with hash-frozen manifests (seed 20260815; SHA-256 registration records verified at run completion), strict generation/evaluation/audit task splits, no-network bubblewrap sandboxing, static audit of modification sources, and hierarchical-bootstrap intervals resampling contexts → pools → outputs.
- *DS-1000 single-step:* persistent Python solver-harness source modifications; 4 contexts × 3 generation processes (independent, label-free revision, outcome-feedback revision) × 6 pools × N = 4 → 288 terminal outputs (2 invalid, kept as zeros); 480 tasks assigned exactly once (8 generation / 8 low-budget / 48 high-budget / 56 audit per context); model gpt-5.4-mini-2026-03-17 on 34,908 requests (17.35M tokens); primary preregistered target T0 = (0.05, 0.50).
- *DS-1000 cross-step:* 4 sequences × 2 update opportunities; update-selection and measurement tasks disjoint; 192 measurement outputs.
- *WorkBuddy–DSH:* persona modifications on WorkBuddy Bench Code with DeepSeek Harness 0.1.0-rc.5 and deepseek-v4-flash-260425; frozen bank of 4 modifications; 20/20/40 generation/certification/audit task split; preregistered target (0.02, 0.50).

## Results & Highlights

- **Qualified modifications exist far more often than they are certified (DS-1000, Table 1, target T0 = (0.05, 0.50)):** under independent audit point estimates, 84.4% [.698, .979] / 88.5% [.792, .969] / 81.2% [.719, .896] of modifications from independent / label-free-revision / outcome-feedback-revision generation meet the target (all 24 pools per process contain at least one such modification), yet only 2 of 288 terminal outputs are certified and selected — both from independent generation (Q̂ = 2/96 ≈ .021, Ĥ = R̂ = 2/24 ≈ .083), and 0 of 192 revision-generated modifications are certified.
- **The bottleneck is simultaneous-interval width, not point estimates:** 67.7% / 76.0% / 76.0% of modifications meet the target under both the high-budget gate's own point estimates and the independent audit, but the simultaneous confidence intervals remain too wide to certify most of them; the authors conclude the low acceptance rate alone "would not justify attributing the result to low reachability."
- **Adoption is followed by reduced generation opportunity (DS-1000 cross-step):** the mean fraction of newly generated modifications meeting T0 falls from .906 [.781, 1] initially to .625 [.297, .906] after one update opportunity and .438 [0, .828] after two — a mean final-minus-initial difference of −.469 (90% hierarchical-bootstrap CI [−.891, −.063]); at the stricter target (.10, .50) it falls from .766 to .203 (difference −.563 [−.734, −.375]); of 8 opportunities, all 5 adoptions are followed by lower measured fractions (mean change −.400) versus +.042 after the 3 retained-incumbent steps — explicitly descriptive, since adoption was selected, not randomized.
- **Second system reproduces the audit-vs-certification gap (WorkBuddy–DSH, target (0.02, 0.50)):** all 4 banked persona modifications meet the target under independent audit point estimates (bank fraction P̂_pt = 1), yet the gate certifies none (target membership left undecided, intervals bounded only by [0, 1]) and the system retains the incumbent.
- **Worst-case certification cost diverges (Proposition 2(ii)):** on the constructed two-task family, any level-β certifier achieving certification probability ≥ 1/2 on the improving member requires expected candidate rollouts E[N_eval] ≥ Z_k/(10u_k) with u_k = 1 − J_user(A_k) — unbounded as the incumbent's expected reward approaches the ceiling of 1, even for arbitrary task-selection policies and data-dependent stopping rules.
- **More generation can hurt the certified lower bound:** interval-width bounds scale as O(√(log(N/β_stat)/n_eff)), so at fixed effective evaluation sample size increasing pool size N can reduce P^+_k(T) and need not increase the certified-success lower bound in Theorem 3's (4.9).
- **Scale of a realistic fixed-suite design (Appendix I.3.2):** evaluating 500 modifications on 500 failure + 5000 retained tasks with per-task reward std σ_V ≤ .15 requires roughly 6.6 × 10^8 rollouts to reach evaluation radii ε_L^ev ≈ .0083 and ε_D^ev ≈ .0166 — quantifying why certified adoption is rare at practical budgets.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuinely novel framing. Prior self-evolution systems (Gödel Agent, SICA, DGM, HGM, MOSS, HarnessX) report empirical gains; prior theory covers either self-improvement learnability (STOP, Wang et al. 2025a) or safe policy improvement in RL (HCPI, SPIBB). Connecting persistent LLM-harness updates to a reachability/certification/selection decomposition with finite-sample guarantees — and proving the generation-vs-evaluation bottleneck asymmetry — is a real contribution. The candidate-pool ceiling is admittedly a simple any-hit probability familiar from pass@k, though its use for persistent modifications with retention constraints is new.
- **Rigor:** High. Statements are theorem-proof throughout, with an assumption ledger (Assumptions 1–9) mapped to results, and the empirical studies preregistered and hash-locked. The paper is also careful about what the experiments do NOT show: audit statistics use single observed outcomes per agent–task pair, not per-task expected rewards, so the authors explicitly concede these point labels "do not establish qualification for the theoretical quantities" — an honest but real gap between the theory's estimands (V(A,t), J_user) and the measured proxies.
- **Empirical scale is thin for the conclusions drawn:** the single-step study yields only 2 certified events total across 288 outputs, so Q̂'s own 90% interval [0, .073] is wide; the cross-step study has n = 4 sequences × 2 opportunities, and the headline −.469 adoption effect rests on a descriptive adopted-vs-retained grouping (5 vs 3 events) the authors themselves flag as non-causal; the WorkBuddy–DSH bank contains only 4 modifications with target membership intervals degenerate to [0, 1]. One model family per study (gpt-5.4-mini; deepseek-v4-flash) limits generality, and DS-1000 update sequences are short — all acknowledged in §8.
- **Reproducibility:** Excellent by preprint standards — seeds, manifests, registration hashes, sandbox spec, task-assignment vectors, library counts (43 Matplotlib … 45 TensorFlow), latency percentiles, and versioned source templates in the artifact bundle. The theory's assumptions (fixed D_user, fixed reward, conditional i.i.d. pools, β-mixing extensions) are explicit enough to be checkable.
- **Significance:** The central practical lesson — "low adoption rate does not imply exhausted improvement opportunity; evaluation evidence is often the binding constraint, and its worst-case cost diverges near the reward ceiling" — directly challenges the benchmark-chasing interpretation common in self-evolution papers and gives designers a concrete diagnostic (inner/outer rectangle counts). That said, the paper offers no improved mechanism, no comparison against deployed systems' own acceptance rules on equal footing, and no matching upper bound to the Z_k/(10u_k) lower bound (the sufficient and necessary sample sizes "are not matching minimax rates").
- **Red flags:** Minor. The companion-study benchmark (WorkBuddy Bench Code) is co-authored by an affiliations-linked lab (Tencent Youtu Lab et al.), and several cited baselines (MOSS, HarnessX, TTHE, SGM, PACE) are very recent 2026 arXiv preprints, so the "prior work" landscape is unsettled. The abstract's divergence claim is worst-case over a constructed two-task family, not an observed phenomenon — readers should not infer that real systems hit this wall.

## Strengths

- A clean, falsifiable decomposition (generation → certification → selection) with matching measurable quantities (P, Q, H, R) that practitioners can actually instrument.
- Proves both positive guarantees (one-step and cumulative expected-reward lower bounds with explicit risk budgets, valid under abstention) and informative negative results (pool-size monotonicity failure, divergent certification cost, non-composability of one-step safety across steps).
- The reachability confidence bounds (Theorem 6) hold simultaneously over all targets, enabling post-hoc target comparison without multiple-testing penalty — a genuinely useful measurement tool.
- Empirical protocol well above community norms: preregistration, hash-verified manifests, disjoint splits, sandboxing, invalid outputs kept in denominators, and pre-commitment to the primary target before outcome inspection.
- Unusually honest interpretation: every empirical claim is accompanied by what it cannot establish (non-causal grouping, single-outcome audit statistics, descriptive-not-population baselines).

## Limitations

- Theory assumes a fixed user-task distribution, a fixed known reward in [0, 1], and conditionally i.i.d. candidate pools; drift, reward misspecification, and adaptive/feedback-driven generation (archive search à la DGM) are deferred or require separately supplied response bounds (Appendices G/H).
- The retention criterion controls the *average absolute* change in retained-task expected reward; subgroup-level regressions (individual tasks or user segments getting worse) are only partially addressed by tail-level extensions.
- Empirical studies are small (two certified events, four sequences, a four-modification bank, one model per study) and use single-draw audit statistics that proxy, rather than estimate, the theoretical quantities.
- The worst-case evaluation-cost bound is a lower bound on a constructed family; no matching achievable rate is proven, so the practical tightness of Z_k/(10u_k) is unknown.
- No new mechanism is proposed: the paper diagnoses why safe self-evolution stalls but does not demonstrate a design that materially improves certified adoption rates or preserves future reachability at fixed budget.

## Relevance

这篇文章直击当前 LLM 智能体自进化（harness/prompt/工具层面的自我修改）研究的核心痛点：什么时候自主修改能带来"有保证"的整体性能提升。其"生成机会 vs 认证证据"的分解、reachability 置信界与 Two-Gate/Measured-margin 安全更新规则，对任何关注 self-evolving agents、agent 安全更新（防止回归累积）与有限样本统计保证的研究都非常对口，也为我们解读 MOSS/DGM/HarnessX 这类系统的"低采纳率"现象提供了诊断框架。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do adaptive generation processes (archive-based search as in DGM, or revision loops with feedback) break the conditionally-i.i.d. pool assumption in a way that systematically raises or lowers realized reachability, and can the candidate-pool ceiling be extended to dependent draws?
- Can a certifier family be constructed that (nearly) matches the Z_k/(10u_k) worst-case rollout lower bound at fixed budget, closing the gap between the Hoeffding-design sufficient sample sizes and the minimax lower bound?
- Would randomizing adoption decisions (rather than the current selected, confounded grouping) replicate the cross-step finding that adoptions are followed by lower target-membership fractions, or is that an artifact of tie-breaking toward large estimated gains?
- How do the guarantees transfer when D_user drifts or responds to the agent's changed behavior (the response bounds of Appendix H) — is there a realistic drift radius under which cumulative improvement guarantees survive?
- Can the audit statistics be upgraded from single observed outcomes to multi-rollout estimates of V(A, t) at acceptable cost, so the empirical diagnostics actually estimate the theoretical estimands L and D_R rather than one-draw proxies?
