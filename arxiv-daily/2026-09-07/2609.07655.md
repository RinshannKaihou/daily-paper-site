> **一句话总结：** 提出"在线代理模型修复"（OSR）算法——让 AI 科学家用廉价的表格基础模型反馈进行长程搜索，仅用 32 次稀疏高保真 oracle 查询（借助期望改进 EI 等采集函数从智能体提案归档中选点）在上下文中修复代理模型；在 MADE 材料发现基准上，每轮都用高保真反馈的对照组需要 6.36–10.27 倍的 oracle 查询才能追平 Online EI 的发现数量。

> **TL;DR:** Closed-loop AI scientists can cheaply propose designs but reliable feedback (wet-lab, high-fidelity simulation) is expensive. OSR inserts a third feedback regime between a frozen surrogate and full feedback: the agent searches with a cheap tabular foundation model (TabPFN-3/TabICLv2) as surrogate, while an acquisition rule — best instantiated as expected improvement ("Online EI") — spends a sparse budget of Q=32 oracle labels on designs from the agent's own proposal archive, appending each label in-context (no retraining). On the MADE materials-discovery benchmark, full-feedback Controls (Q=300) need 7.23× (DeepSeek-V4-Flash) and 6.36× (GPT-5.6-Luna) more oracle queries to match Online EI's discovery count at Q=32 (10.27× under the non-LLM Chemeleon+MLIP workflow). On 20 synthetic SCM worlds, Online EI cuts mean normalized maximum regret from 0.0984 (Static EI) to 0.0552 (−44%), closing 76% of the no-repair→full-oracle gap with 16% of the queries; notably, global-fit acquisitions (Global-UQ, Random) improve NRMSE but barely reduce regret (mean ΔR 0.063/0.052 vs 0.424/0.409 for Q90-UCB/EI), exposing an objective mismatch in how surrogate models are usually benchmarked.

## 核心贡献

- **提出"在线代理模型修复"（OSR）这一第三种反馈机制**：介于"固定代理模型"（Q=0，误差被优化过程持续利用）与"每轮高保真反馈"（Q=T，可靠但昂贵）之间——智能体全程用廉价代理反馈搜索，外层稀疏地用采集函数选点做高保真评估，把搜索长度与验证频率解耦。
- **证明全局预测误差与最大遗憾（maximum regret）是错位的修复目标**：在 40 个合成表格世界、两种表格基础模型（TabPFN-3、TabICLv2）、两种上下文规模（N=100/1000）的静态实验中，面向全局覆盖的 Global-UQ/Random 明显改善 NRMSE 却几乎不降遗憾（平均 ΔR 仅 0.063/0.052），而 Q90-UCB 与 EI 大幅降遗憾（0.424/0.409）却不改善全局误差——直接挑战以全局精度评价反馈模型（如 TabArena）的惯例。
- **给出完整的双循环算法与采集函数族**：候选归档（append-only，测过即出队）+ 均匀调度的 Q 次 oracle 查询 + 免训练的上下文修复；系统比较 Random、Global-UQ、Peak、Q90-UCB、G2P 混合与 EI 六种采集规则，并确定 Online EI 为最强实例。
- **在合成环境与真实基准上验证省查询效果**：20 个冻结 SCM 世界（含噪声鲁棒性、预算扫描 8–64、GP 代理消融 Archive-BO、主动上下文学习 Tab-AICL 基线）+ MADE 材料 discovery 基准（30 个化学体系 × 3 次重复，两个 LLM 编排器 + 非生成器工作流），结论对提案策略与代理模型类别均稳健。

## Key Contributions

- **Formulates online surrogate repair (OSR)**, a third feedback regime with 0 < Q ≪ T: an AI scientist proposes designs scored by an inexpensive surrogate every episode, while a separate acquisition rule allocates a sparse budget of Q high-fidelity oracle queries over an append-only archive of the agent's accumulated proposals; each returned label is appended as a context row to a tabular foundation model, so repair happens in-context with zero parameter training.
- **Shows global surrogate fit and optimizer-facing maximum regret are distinct — and misaligned — repair objectives.** Improving NRMSE (via Global-UQ or Random selection) does not remove the false maxima that maximization preferentially exploits; acquisition rules that target decision-relevant regions (Q90-UCB, EI) cut regret by ~8× more while yielding near-zero global error improvement — an objective mismatch between standard tabular-model benchmarks (TabArena/Matbench-style) and closed-loop discovery.
- **Identifies expected improvement over a *verified* incumbent as the strongest repair rule ("Online EI")**: EI integrates the full upper tail of the predictive distribution and references the best measured label, so it stops prioritizing regions that cannot beat the observed best — unlike Q90-UCB (optimistic quantile) or Peak (point-estimate validation, repairs false maxima only one at a time).
- **Demonstrates large oracle-query savings on synthetic SCM worlds and on MADE** under LLM orchestrators (DeepSeek-V4-Flash, GPT-5.6-Luna) and a non-LLM generator (Chemeleon+MLIP), with robustness checks: noise (σ=0.05·R_w), budget sweep Q=8–64, surrogate-class ablation (GP-based Archive-BO), and an active in-context learning baseline (Tab-AICL).

## Methodology

The system has a dual-loop structure (their Algorithm 1). **Inner loop (every episode t = 1..T):** the agent (a ReAct LLM agent, or the non-LLM Chemeleon+MLIP generator on MADE) emits a candidate set C_t and selects a design x; all candidates are appended to an archive A_t; the selected design receives inexpensive predictive feedback p_{D_t}(y|x) from a tabular foundation model (TabPFN-3 or TabICLv2) conditioned on context D_t initialized from N expensive measurements. **Outer loop (repair at Q evenly spaced episodes):** an acquisition rule scores every archived design under the current surrogate, x_t = argmax_{x∈A_t} α_t(x); the oracle f returns y_t; the labelled pair is appended to the context (D_{t+1} = D_t ∪ {(x_t, y_t)}) with no gradient training; the measured design leaves the archive. Crucially, the design the oracle evaluates need not be the agent's current proposal — the label repairs *future* feedback for all subsequent episodes.

Acquisition rules compared (differing only in α): **Random** (distribution-matched sampling, the control); **Global-UQ** (largest 80% predictive interval q_0.90 − q_0.10, classical active learning for global fit); **Peak** (validate the surrogate's predicted argmax of the mean); **Q90-UCB** (argmax of the 0.9 predictive quantile — queries plausible-upside designs, useful whether overestimated or genuinely optimal); **G2P(k)** (half Global-UQ then half Peak); **EI** (expected improvement over the best *measured* incumbent y_max^t, computed as the mean of max(q − y_max, 0) over the model's full predictive distribution). Repair quality is measured on two axes: global fit (NRMSE vs a 50,000-row hidden audit sample) and optimizer-facing normalized maximum regret R_max = (f(x*) − f(x̂_D))/R_w, where x̂_D is the argmax found by the *same* reference search procedure run on the surrogate and R_w a robust response range. Synthetic worlds are frozen, seeded structural causal models (14–22 mixed-type roots, 5–8-layer DAGs, 44–72 hidden nodes, twelve nonlinear mechanism families, five mechanism profiles; cohort W5241–W5260, disjoint from surrogate training data), with 1,000-row initial contexts. On MADE, MACE provides surrogate feedback while the stronger Orb-v3 potential serves as oracle; a novel structure within τ = 0.1 eV/atom of the convex hull counts as a discovery, and all proposals are audited post hoc with the oracle (audit calls not charged to Q). A "static repair" variant spends the same Q queries before the campaign and then freezes the surrogate, isolating the value of allocating measurements online.

## Results & Highlights

- **Static repair objective mismatch (40 synthetic worlds, Q=32, TabPFN-3 & TabICLv2, N ∈ {100, 1000}):** Q90-UCB and EI achieve mean maximum-regret reductions ΔR of 0.4236 and 0.4093, versus just 0.0631 (Global-UQ) and 0.0520 (Random) — yet Global-UQ and Random produce the *largest* mean NRMSE improvements (ΔE = 0.0046/0.0045) while Q90-UCB's ΔE is −0.0002. Improving average prediction does not remove optimizer-exploitable false maxima.
- **Synthetic worlds (20 SCM worlds, T=200, ReAct agent on DeepSeek-V4-Flash, TabPFN surrogate):** Online EI at Q=32 reaches mean normalized regret 0.0552 (95% CI 0.0298–0.0843) vs Static EI 0.0984 (−44%), No repair 0.1432, Archive-BO (GP+EI) 0.0807, Online Q90-UCB 0.0600, Tab-AICL 0.1169, and the full-oracle control (Q=T=200) 0.0278 — i.e., Online EI closes 76% of the no-repair→full-feedback gap using only 16% as many oracle queries.
- **MADE benchmark (30 chemical systems × 3 runs, T=300, MACE surrogate vs Orb-v3 oracle):** the full-feedback TabPFN+MACE Control (Q=300) requires 7.23× more oracle queries to match Online EI's Q=32 discovery count under DeepSeek-V4-Flash (6.36× under GPT-5.6-Luna; 10.27× under Chemeleon+MLIP), equal to 86%/84% fewer queries; the factors are lower bounds because Control fails to catch up within 300 queries in 21/90 (DeepSeek) and 7/90 (Luna) runs. At Q=32, Online EI already reaches 82% of Control's Q=300 discovery count on DeepSeek (53.9 vs 65.4 discoveries) and 67% on Luna (56.9 vs 84.6), and reaches 130 vs Control's 141 discoveries (93%) with Chemeleon — while also leading both full-feedback baselines in unique compositions and unique space groups at matched budget.
- **Noise robustness (σ = 0.05·R_w Gaussian noise on all oracle labels and the initial context):** method ordering is preserved — Online EI 0.0905 mean regret vs Static EI 0.1124 (19% lower) and full-oracle control 0.0639, all at matched Q=32.
- **Budget sweep (Online EI, synthetic worlds):** regret improves monotonically from 0.0918 (Q=8) to 0.0775 (Q=16), 0.0552 (Q=32), 0.0481 (Q=64), with clear diminishing returns — the Q=8→32 step buys 0.0366 regret reduction while doubling 32→64 buys only 0.0071. Even Q=8 beats Static EI at Q=32 (0.0984).
- **Selection geometry (Appendix C.2):** mean successive-acquisition step sizes are 0.079·d0 (Peak), 0.189 (Q90-UCB), 0.356 (EI), 0.915 (Tab-AICL), and regret is non-monotonic in dispersion — neither local concentration (Peak, 0.0976) nor near-full-scale coverage (Tab-AICL, 0.1169) suffices; EI's combination of uncertainty with a verified incumbent attains the lowest regret (0.0552).
- **Cost (Appendix E, USD per MADE trajectory):** at matched discovery counts, Online EI costs only $0.29 (DeepSeek) / $0.58 (Luna) more in LLM charges than Control catch-up runs, with comparable GPU time — negligible against a wet-lab synthesis/characterization cycle.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty (moderate, honestly framed):** the individual ingredients — expected improvement (Mockus/Jones), UCB acquisition, active learning, in-context updating of tabular foundation models — are all classical or borrowed. The genuine contribution is the *decoupling architecture*: acquisition over an evolving archive of agent proposals rather than acquisition *being* the proposal policy (standard BO), and the demonstration that this decoupling changes which acquisition objective matters (EI's verified-incumbent reference beats pure-uncertainty and pure-point-estimate rules in the online setting, reversing Q90-UCB's slight static-study lead). The "objective mismatch" finding (NRMSE vs maximum regret) is the paper's most conceptually valuable result, echoing Trabucco et al. (2021) and Matbench Discovery's false-positive concerns but shown inside a sequential agent loop.
- **Rigor (strong):** paired comparisons, CIs, an adversarial baseline adapted from prior work (Tab-AICL), a surrogate-class ablation (Archive-BO with a GP, 0.0807 — showing the benefit is not TabPFN-specific), and honest reporting that Control's acceleration factors are lower bounds (21/90 and 7/90 runs never catch up). The audit-on-MADE design — post-hoc oracle scoring of all proposals without charging audit calls to Q — is clearly disclosed as distinct from the synthetic-world scoring protocol.
- **Weaknesses in the evaluation:** (1) On MADE the "oracle" is Orb-v3, another learned interatomic potential, not DFT or experiment — the fidelity gap is MACE→Orb-v3, much cheaper than the wet-lab/simulation gap the motivation invokes; the 6–10× savings are in Orb calls, and the paper never validates any discovered structure against DFT. (2) The static-repair comparison spends all Q queries *before* the campaign — a deliberately weak alternative; no adaptive-but-batched or interleaved non-uniform schedule is compared, and the schedule is fixed and evenly spaced (the authors concede optimization-aware scheduling is untested). (3) Online EI does not close the gap to full feedback: 0.0552 vs 0.0278 mean regret on synthetic worlds, and on MADE it reaches only 67–93% of Control's final discovery counts — the savings are in queries, not in final performance. (4) The synthetic worlds are sampled from the same SCM prior family used to train TabPFN, a potential in-distribution bias for the surrogate, only partially mitigated by the TabICLv2 and GP ablations. (5) The framework requires tabular representations plus an in-context-updatable surrogate; out-of-distribution environments and agent styles (test-time training, RL agents) are explicitly untested.
- **Reproducibility:** good on paper (seeded public-protocol worlds, named models, cost breakdowns in Appendix E), but no code or data release is stated, and the LLM orchestrators are commercial endpoints (DeepSeek-V4-Flash, GPT-5.6-Luna) whose stochastic caching/routing the authors themselves flag as a cost uncertainty.
- **Significance:** high if the OSR framing is adopted — it gives self-driving-lab-style economics (sparse measurements) without self-driving-lab infrastructure, and the regret-vs-NRMSE argument should influence how surrogate models for closed-loop science are benchmarked. Overclaiming is limited; the abstract's headline numbers are accurately qualified as lower bounds.

## Strengths

- Clean problem decomposition: separating *who proposes* (the agent) from *what gets verified* (the acquisition rule) is an elegant and general interface that is proposal-policy agnostic, as demonstrated across two LLMs and a non-LLM generator.
- The static-vs-online repair contrast is a well-controlled experiment that cleanly isolates the value of allocating measurements *during* rather than *before* the search.
- The objective-mismatch result (global fit ≠ low regret) is a sharp, transferable lesson for anyone evaluating surrogate models intended for optimization loops.
- Unusually thorough ablations for a preprint: noise, budget sweep, surrogate class, acquisition geometry, adapted prior-work baseline (Tab-AICL), and an honest per-trajectory cost analysis.
- In-context repair (append one labelled row, no retraining) makes the method operationally simple and immediately reusable with any tabular foundation model.

## Limitations

- No ground-truth physical validation: all "high-fidelity" oracles are models (Orb-v3 on MADE, exact SCM responses in synthetic settings); the leap to wet-lab-scale costs is argued, not measured.
- OSR requires the problem to be expressible as mixed-type tabular data with a tabular foundation model that accepts in-context labels; structured domains (molecules as graphs, proteins as sequences) would need a different repair mechanism, and out-of-distribution failure modes are acknowledged but untested.
- The evenly spaced schedule and the Q value are hyperparameters chosen by the authors; the budget sweep covers Q but not schedule placement, and no adaptive scheduling is explored.
- Residual gap to full feedback remains on both benchmarks (0.0552 vs 0.0278 regret; 67–93% of Control's discoveries), so OSR is a cost-saving regime, not a dominance regime.
- Agent scope is limited to ReAct-style LLM agents and one non-LLM generator; interactions with RL-trained or test-time-training agents are future work, and only single-objective (or single-threshold MADE discovery) settings are considered.

## Relevance

高度契合"自进化智能体与自动化科研"这一高优先级方向：本文正是关于闭环 AI 科学家（agentic scientific discovery）如何在昂贵反馈瓶颈下可靠运行的研究——OSR 把高保真验证频率与搜索长度解耦，属于自主科研循环中的反馈可靠性问题，其对"代理模型误差被优化过程放大"的分析也触及长期自主智能体系统可信性这一子主题。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How would an *adaptive* repair schedule (e.g., triggered by surrogate-disagreement spikes or EI-value collapse) compare against the fixed evenly spaced Q-query schedule, and could it shrink the residual gap to full feedback (0.0552 → 0.0278 on synthetic worlds)?
- Does the regret-vs-NRMSE mismatch persist for graph- or sequence-structured surrogates (GNN potentials, protein fitness models) where in-context label appending is not available and repair requires fine-tuning?
- Can OSR be extended to multi-fidelity ladders (cheap → MACE → Orb-v3 → DFT) where the acquisition rule also chooses the fidelity, rather than only the design?
- What happens when the oracle itself is noisy *and* biased (real wet-lab assays) rather than Gaussian-noisy — does EI's verified-incumbent reference become unreliable under systematic oracle drift?
- Would combining OSR with an RL-optimized or test-time-training agent change the acquisition geometry findings (Step/Spread analysis), since a stronger proposer changes the archive distribution the acquisition rule sees?
- The synthetic worlds are drawn from TabPFN's own SCM prior — how do Q90-UCB/EI rankings shift on worlds from a different generative family (e.g., physics simulators or real Matbench-style datasets)?
