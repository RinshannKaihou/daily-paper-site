> **一句话总结：** 提出 Circuit Reasoning Score（CRS）——用对比消融找到的 46 个推理敏感注意力头的一次前向传播激活值（经长度残差化）作为 RLVR 训练数据选择信号；在 Qwen2.5-Math-7B 上反直觉地发现 CRS 最低的 10% 数据训练效果最好（GSM8K +2.0 pp、Minerva +2.9 pp、OlympiadBench +1.6 pp 超过随机选择），且该效应依赖训练池多样性、模型规模与评测难度，说明 RLVR 数据选择是"体制依赖"而非静态难度排序。

> **TL;DR:** The authors identify 46 reasoning-sensitive attention heads in Qwen2.5-Math-7B via contrastive ablation and score every training problem with a single forward pass (Circuit Reasoning Score, CRS), then run 58 GRPO training runs on DAPO-Math-17K deciles. Counter to the intuitive "high circuit engagement = good data" hypothesis, the lowest-CRS decile beats random selection on GSM8K (+2.0 pp), OlympiadBench (+1.6 pp), and Minerva (+2.9 pp), while the top decile is statistically indistinguishable from the middle; CRS explains only 1.57% of variance in base-model success rate, so it is not a difficulty proxy. The effect vanishes on a domain-curated Olympiads pool, reverses direction at 1.5B scale (where CRS-top leads), and training reward anti-correlates with generalization (r = −0.18). Worth reading for the unusually honest boundary-condition analysis; the headline gains are modest (+1.6–2.9 pp) with high seed variance.

## 核心贡献

- **提出 CRS（Circuit Reasoning Score）**：一种免训练、模型内部的数据选择信号。通过对 Qwen2.5-Math-7B 的 784 个注意力头做对比消融（50 个奥赛风格推理探针 vs 50 个单步算术平凡探针，损失增量差 s = Δr − 0.5Δt，三轮 bootstrap 交集 Jaccard = 0.91），锁定 46 个推理敏感头（跨 21/28 层）；对每个问题只需在冻结基座模型上**一次前向传播**，取这 46 个头输出激活 ℓ2 范数的逐头最大值再平均，并对 token 长度做 OLS 残差化（与长度的相关从 r = 0.568 降到 0.013）。
- **用 58 次 GRPO 训练反驳"高推理回路参与度 = 好数据"的直觉假设**：在 DAPO-Math-17K 上，7B 模型用 CRS 最低 10% 数据训练在三个中等难度基准上稳定超过随机选择与高 CRS 选择；而 CRS-top 与 CRS-middle 在该种子数下不可区分。
- **系统排除替代解释**：对照了 LIMR（奖励轨迹选择）、RandHeads（随机 46 个头）、Full 17K 全量数据、Diff-hard/Diff-easy（基于 rollout 成功率的难度过滤）四类基线，并证明 CRS 与成功率排序几乎正交（Jaccard 0.030–0.069，接近随机水平 ≈0.051；ρ = 0.125，R² = 1.57%）。
- **刻画数据选择的三个边界条件**：(1) 训练池多样性是前提——在 8,523 题的窄域 Olympiads 池上任何选择方法都无差异；(2) 模型容量改变有效方向——1.5B 上方向反转，CRS-top 反而领先；(3) 训练奖励不预测泛化——最低奖励条件（CRS-bottom，0.265）泛化最好，全运行相关 r = −0.18。
- **成本不对称论证**：难度过滤需要对 16,478 个问题做 65,912 次生成来估计成功率，而 CRS 每题只需一次前向传播。

## Key Contributions

- **Circuit Reasoning Score (CRS)**, a training-free, model-internal data selection signal: 46 reasoning-sensitive heads are isolated by contrastive ablation (reasoning vs trivial probe loss increases, 50+50 held-out probes, three bootstrap rounds, pairwise Jaccard 0.89–0.92; one global-bottleneck outlier L0H3 excluded), then each problem gets one forward pass on the frozen base model; the score averages per-head max ℓ2 activation norms and is OLS-residualized against log token length (length correlation drops from r = 0.568 to 0.013).
- **Evidence against the high-engagement hypothesis** via 58 GRPO runs: on Qwen2.5-Math-7B + DAPO-Math-17K, the *lowest*-CRS decile is the only condition that consistently beats random selection (medium-difficulty benchmarks), while CRS-top cannot be separated from CRS-middle.
- **A battery of controls ruling out alternatives**: LIMR reward-trajectory selection, random-head subsets (RandHeads), full-data training, and explicit difficulty filtering (Diff-hard SR=0.25 / Diff-easy SR=0.92 built from 65,912 rollouts). CRS overlaps the difficulty selector at Jaccard 0.030–0.069 vs chance ≈0.051 and explains only 1.57% of variance in base-model success rate (ρ = 0.125), so it is not a difficulty proxy.
- **Three boundary conditions** for RLVR data selection: pool diversity is a prerequisite (no effect on the homogeneous Olympiads pool), model capacity flips the useful direction (1.5B prefers CRS-top), and training reward anti-predicts generalization (r = −0.18 across runs; lowest-reward condition CRS-bottom generalizes best).
- **Cost asymmetry**: CRS costs one forward pass per problem, versus 65,912 generations to estimate the success-rate difficulty baseline.

## Methodology

The pipeline (Figure 1) has four stages. **Stage 1 — reasoning-head identification**: Qwen2.5-Math-7B has 784 attention heads (28 layers × 28). For each head, zero-ablate its output and measure the LM loss increase on 50 reasoning probes (Olympiad-style) and 50 trivial probes (single-step arithmetic), held out from all training/evaluation. The contrastive score s(l,h) = Δr − 0.5·Δt removes general language-modeling function (Δr and Δt correlate at r ≈ 0.75). Repeating the scan three times with resampled probes and intersecting yields 47 stable heads (cross-round score correlation r = 0.9998); excluding one global-bottleneck outlier (L0H3, score 4.8× the runner-up) leaves 46 heads across 21 layers. **Stage 2 — per-problem scoring**: one forward pass on the frozen model records each head's ℓ2 output norm at every position; CRS_raw averages per-head maxima over the 46 heads (max captures peak engagement, avoiding dilution by boilerplate tokens). **Stage 3 — length residualization**: raw CRS correlates with input length at r = 0.568, so the score is OLS-residualized on log n (final r = 0.013; Jaccard between top-10% CRS and longest-10% drops 0.286 → 0.057). **Stage 4 — selection and training**: rank DAPO-Math-17K (17,186 problems), extract top/middle/bottom 10% deciles (1,718 each), and train with GRPO (TRL v1.3.0, 1,000 steps, G=16 generations/prompt, binary SymPy-checked \boxed{} reward, 8×A100-80GB), with per-seed random baselines. Controls: RandHeads (46 random non-reasoning heads), the 8,523-problem Olympiads pool cross-check (Jaccard with LIMR's selection 0.15–0.17, confirming independence), and a 1.5B replication including an xmod condition (7B's CRS applied to 1.5B). Statistical apparatus: 5 seeds for Tier 1, 3 for Tier 2/4 and 1.5B; seed-level paired differences, an item-level McNemar test, and a hierarchical binomial model treating condition-by-seed interaction as a free parameter.

## Results & Highlights

- **CRS-bottom vs Random on the three medium-difficulty benchmarks (7B, n=5 seeds, paired differences)**: GSM8K 85.81 vs 83.82 (+2.0 pp), Minerva 22.50 vs 19.63 (+2.9 pp), OlympiadBench 38.36 vs 36.75 (+1.6 pp) — positive on 13 of 15 seed-by-benchmark cells, composite +2.15 pp; a hierarchical binomial model places 91.3% of the posterior for the condition effect above zero, and an item-level McNemar test on GSM8K gives p = 3.3×10⁻⁴. Base model: 81.20 GSM8K / 16.18 Minerva.
- **The effect is non-monotone and bounded**: CRS-top gains only +0.4 pp (GSM8K) / +1.2 pp (Minerva) / +0.1 pp (OlympiadBench) over Random and is not separable from CRS-middle; on MATH-500 no Tier 1 condition separates from Random (ceiling effect; base already 66.80), and on AIME'24 CRS-bottom is −1.6 pp vs Random (14.88 vs 16.50).
- **CRS is not a difficulty proxy**: residualized CRS explains 1.57% of variance in rollout-estimated success rate (ρ = 0.125, n = 16,478) vs 4.36% for token length (2.8× stronger); Jaccard between CRS-top and easiest-by-SR top-10% is 0.078 vs chance 0.053. In the trained difficulty baseline, Diff-hard (SR=0.25) reaches 84.53 GSM8K / 21.08 Minerva — both below CRS-bottom's 85.81 / 22.50.
- **RandHeads control**: 46 random heads reach similar means (GSM8K 85.65 vs 84.26 for CRS-top) but with 2–4× higher cross-seed variance (GSM8K std 2.72 vs 0.64), i.e., the identified heads give a more consistent signal.
- **Full data is worse, not better**: training on all 17K problems trails every 10% subset on MATH-500 (67.93 ± 0.70 vs 69.12 for Random) at the fixed 1,000-step budget.
- **Pool-diversity boundary**: on the 8,523-problem Olympiads pool, Oly-CRS-top/bottom/Random are statistically indistinguishable (MATH 70.5/71.4/69.7), while the same stratification on diverse DAPO-17K produces up to 2.9 pp differences.
- **Scale reversal at 1.5B (n=3)**: CRS-top leads MATH-500 (65.4 vs 63.6 Random), GSM8K (77.5 vs 76.4), OlympiadBench (31.4 vs 30.2); CRS-bottom trails CRS-top by 3.0 pp on MATH-500. The xmod condition (7B's CRS-top reused for 1.5B) matches native 1.5B CRS-top (GSM8K 77.3 vs 77.5) with far lower variance (std 0.87 vs 4.48), suggesting partial cross-scale transferability.
- **Training reward anti-correlates with generalization**: across all runs r = −0.18 (condition means r = +0.03); CRS-bottom converges to the lowest reward (0.265) yet achieves the best GSM8K/Minerva, while LIMR reaches the highest reward (0.775) with ~7% zero-std steps yet underperforms Random on AIME (−1.6 pp); Full data has ~53% zero-std steps (no GRPO gradient).

## Authors & Credibility

The work comes from the School of Computer Science and Engineering at Beihang University (Zhuofan Chen, Ziqian Jiao, Yikai Cui, Zhixin Cai, Wenge Rong) with Jun Bai from the Beijing Institute for General Artificial Intelligence (BIGAI), partially supported by NSFC Grant 62477001. The empirical program is unusually rigorous for a data-selection paper: 58 GRPO runs across two scales, multi-seed protocols (n=5 / n=3), pre-registered-style controls (random heads, same-pool cross-check with Jaccard overlap reported, explicit difficulty-filtering baseline, full-data baseline), item-level McNemar testing, hierarchical Bayesian analysis, and Jaccard-based confound checks (probe stability, length, difficulty, LIMR independence). The paper openly acknowledges AI-assisted tools for language polishing and coding. Overall the statistical hygiene (reporting effect sizes with std rather than significance stars, seed-paired differences, resampling envelopes) is above the norm for this subfield.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Genuinely interesting angle — connecting mechanistic-interpretability artifacts (attention-head circuits) to RLVR data selection, and (unlike Wang et al. 2025a, which targeted SFT and assumed higher engagement is better) testing both directions of the signal. The negative/correlational finding ("lowest engagement wins at 7B") plus the regime-dependence framing is a real contribution over "another selection heuristic" papers.
- **Rigor**: Strong on controls but the headline effect is fragile in magnitude. CRS-bottom's cross-seed std (GSM8K ±2.48, MATH-500 ±3.88, OlympiadBench ±1.91) is 2–4× larger than Random's (±0.58/±1.29/±0.86) and in several cases exceeds the effect size itself; the winning condition is simultaneously the noisiest one. The 13/15-cell consistency and McNemar/hierarchical analyses mitigate this, but a reader should note the +2.0/+2.9 pp gains come with overlapping distributions across seeds.
- **Prompt-coverage quirk**: at 1,000 steps each run sees only 58.2% of its 1,718-prompt subset, so the "decile" being tested is really a random 58% subsample of the decile — the authors disclose this, but it dilutes whatever the selection signal actually is and adds cross-seed variance.
- **Weakly powered reversal claim**: the 1.5B direction flip rests on n=3 seeds; the paper itself frames the GRPO reward-variance explanation for it as a prediction "left to future verification." Single model family (Qwen2.5-Math) and math-only domain mean the central "regime-dependent" conclusion could look different on Llama/DeepSeek-base models or code RLVR.
- **LIMR comparison confound**: LIMR is drawn from a different pool (8,523 Olympiad problems vs DAPO-17K), confounding selection quality with pool quality — acknowledged and partially isolated in §4.5, but the headline tables still mix the two.
- **Causal status**: the authors are commendably explicit that CRS is a selection-level predictive signal, not a verified reasoning circuit (zero-ablating the heads is equally destructive to the untrained base model). Some readers will find "reasoning circuits" in the title slightly stronger than what is demonstrated.
- **Reproducibility**: good — standard open stack (Qwen2.5-Math, DAPO-Math-17K, TRL v1.3.0, full hyperparameter table, 8×A100 budget disclosed), deterministic scoring procedure, and probe-construction details should make replication feasible; no code release is mentioned in the main text, which is the main gap.
- **Significance**: the practical takeaway is modest (+1.6–2.9 pp on medium benchmarks for one model family), but the negative results (training reward ≠ generalization; selection effects vanish on curated pools) are arguably the more durable findings for practitioners.

## Strengths

- Tests the intuitive hypothesis head-on and reports that it fails — a rare example of a paper built around a well-controlled negative result rather than a new SWEET score to maximize.
- Exceptional control coverage: random-head, same-pool, cross-scale, full-data, difficulty-filtering, and reward-trajectory baselines all directly instantiated and trained, with Jaccard overlap quantified for each potential confound.
- Statistical care well above field norms: multi-seed paired differences, item-level McNemar, hierarchical binomial posterior (91.3% above zero), resampling envelopes, and honest effect-size reporting with stds.
- Cheap and practical signal: one forward pass per problem on the frozen model, no rollouts, no reward labels — versus 65,912 generations for the difficulty baseline.
- The training-reward-vs-generalization decoupling (r = −0.18) and the pool-diversity prerequisite are useful practitioner-facing findings independent of CRS itself.

## Limitations

- Single model family (Qwen2.5-Math 7B/1.5B) and single domain (mathematics); no evidence the 46-head signal or its direction transfers to other architectures or verifiable-reward domains.
- Effect sizes are small (+1.6–2.9 pp) relative to cross-seed noise, and CRS-bottom is the highest-variance condition; the 1.5B reversal uses only n=3 seeds.
- 58.2% prompt coverage per run means the decile labels are only partially realized during training.
- CRS is correlational: the authors explicitly do not claim a verified causal reasoning pathway, and zero-ablation tests are destructive to the base model as well.
- The mechanistic interpretation of *why* low engagement helps (novel reasoning patterns / reward-variance threshold) is post-hoc and unverified at intermediate scales.

## 相关性

对研究"RLVR 训练数据选择"与"可解释性信号如何指导训练"的工作高度相关：CRS 提供了一个无需 rollout、无需奖励标签的模型内部选择信号，并系统证明了数据选择效应受训练池多样性、模型规模与评测难度共同调制——这直接关系到用机制可解释性工具做训练数据 curation 与 scaling 数据策略的研究方向。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the useful CRS direction shift continuously across intermediate scales (1.5B → 3B → 7B → 32B), as the GRPO reward-variance explanation predicts?
- Do the 46 reasoning-sensitive heads identified in Qwen2.5-Math overlap with heads found in other model families, or is the circuit (and hence the selection signal) architecture-specific?
- Would CRS-based *curriculum* selection (e.g., start low-CRS, anneal toward high-CRS as competence grows) dominate any static decile, given that the productive zone moves with training?
- Since the lowest-reward condition generalizes best, can reward-variance-aware replay or exploration bonuses explicitly exploit the exploration/ generalization link that CRS-bottom seems to stumble into?
- Does the pool-diversity prerequisite generalize: is there a quantitative diversity metric (entropy over topic/difficulty clusters) that predicts when any selection method will show an effect at all?
