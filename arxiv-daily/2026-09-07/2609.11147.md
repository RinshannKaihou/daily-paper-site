> **一句话总结：** 提出 ARCHE——一个将 GPT-5 通用推理模型与领域专用 Gaussian 专家模型（ARCHE-Chem，基于 Qwen2.5-7B 持续预训练+SFT+Best-of-8 奖励选择）耦合的闭环智能体系统，能自主生成机理假设、编排量子化学计算并依据计算证据迭代修正结论；在三个递进任务上验证：复现 Houk 2016 不对称 aldol 反应立体控制过渡态（ΔΔG‡ 计算 1.9 vs 实验 1.6 kcal/mol，RMSD 0.01 Å）、为一个未发表的光催化 C–I 键断裂反应自主收敛出 CsPPh2 络合光激发自由基机理（激发能 64.0 vs 450 nm 光子 63.6 kcal/mol）、并从镍催化迁移交叉偶联中提炼出 Br–N–N–H 二面角扭转能这一可解释选择性描述符。

> **TL;DR:** ARCHE is a closed-loop agentic system for autonomous reaction-mechanism discovery that pairs a generalist reasoner (GPT-5) with ARCHE-Chem, a domain-specialized 7B model for Gaussian input/output handling. On held-out Gaussian benchmarks ARCHE-Chem scores 65.60% on route generation vs 44.80% for GPT-o3-mini (+20.8 pts) and 3.91–38.30% for 13 other models, though GPT-5.5 beats it on conceptual basics (82.71% vs 70.65%). End-to-end, ARCHE reproduces the stereocontrolling transition states of a 2016 JACS asymmetric aldol study from the reaction spec alone (ΔΔG‡ = 1.9 kcal/mol vs 1.6 experimental, TS RMSD 0.01/0.15 Å, <24 h unattended), self-corrects to a photoinduced CsPPh2-complex radical pathway for an unpublished α-iodoboronate C–I cleavage (computed excitation 64.0 kcal/mol matching the 63.6 kcal/mol 450 nm photon after rejecting concerted 46.0 kcal/mol and direct-excitation 102.8/75.8 kcal/mol routes), and distills a Br–N–N–H dihedral distortion descriptor explaining ligand-controlled regioselectivity in Ni-catalyzed migratory cross-coupling. The system-level evidence is only three hand-picked success stories with no failure-mode statistics or end-to-end ablations.

## 核心贡献

- **闭环自主机理发现架构**：ARCHE 将"提出假设 → 编排计算 → 反思验证 → 修正假设"组织为无人干预的动态闭环；通用推理模型（GPT-5，经 API 驱动检索/假设/规划智能体）负责开放式机理探索，领域专用模型 ARCHE-Chem 负责 Gaussian 输入精修、输出解读与错误诊断，二者在统一决策空间中协作。
- **Gaussian 专家模型 ARCHE-Chem**：基于 Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct，用 105 本教科书/手册 + 432 篇专家论坛讨论（约 286 MB）做全参数持续预训练，再用约 5 万条指令对做 SFT，并以 Gemma2-27B-IT（2.8 万偏好对）训练奖励模型做 Best-of-8 推理时缩放。
- **结构化工具注册表与可执行工作流**：将机理假设转换为模式约束的、可实际执行的量子化学工作流（自动插入格式/坐标转换步骤），对接 Gaussian 16、RDKit、Open Babel、ASE、cclib、Multiwfn 等工具。
- **三级递进的价值验证**：(1) 可靠性——仅凭反应规格复现文献立体控制过渡态并额外发现 3 个未报道的更高能过渡态；(2) 发现——对未发表新反应通过"否定结果→反思→修正假设"自主收敛出与对照实验一致的自由基机理；(3) 抽象——从复杂催化体系中提炼出单一可解释的扭转描述符，弥合镍催化迁移偶联领域长期缺失的选择性原理。
- **代码开源**：https://github.com/JetAstra/Arche-Harness。

## Key Contributions

- A closed-loop agentic architecture (propose → execute → reflect → revise) requiring no human intervention, in which a generalist model (GPT-5 driving retrieval/hypothesis/planner agents) explores mechanistic hypotheses while the domain-specialized ARCHE-Chem enforces methodological rigor on Gaussian-specific settings, output interpretation, and failure diagnosis.
- ARCHE-Chem, a Gaussian expert model built by continual pretraining of Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct on 105 textbooks/manuals plus 432 expert forum threads (~286 MB), supervised fine-tuning on ~50,000 instruction pairs, and reward-guided Best-of-N (N=8) inference with a Gemma2-27B-IT reward model trained on 28,000 preference pairs.
- A structured tool registry with conversion-aware workflow assembly that turns mechanistic hypotheses into executable quantum-chemical pipelines over Gaussian 16 and auxiliary tools (RDKit, Open Babel, ASE, cclib, Multiwfn), automatically inserting file-format/coordinate conversions.
- Three-tier value validation spanning reliability (reconstruction of the 2016 Houk stereocontrolling transition-state model plus 3 previously unreported higher-energy TS), discovery (self-corrected photoinduced CsPPh2-complex radical mechanism for an unpublished α-iodoboronate C–I cleavage consistent with TEMPO/dark/no-base controls), and abstraction (a Br–N–N–H dihedral distortion-energy descriptor governing ligand-controlled regioselectivity in Ni-catalyzed migratory cross-coupling).
- Public code release at https://github.com/JetAstra/Arche-Harness.

## Methodology

ARCHE organizes mechanistic inquiry as a reasoning–validation cycle. Given a natural-language scientific question, a retrieval agent extracts mechanistic keywords and pulls background knowledge from ~50 related papers via local RAG. A hypothesis agent brainstorms competing mechanistic hypotheses (e.g., 25 raw strategies narrowed to 6 representative activation modes in Case Study 2; 5 selected hypotheses in Case Study 1), which are ranked. A planner agent decomposes prioritized hypotheses into computational workflows (conformer sampling, TS guess generation, TS optimization with frequency/IRC verification, thermochemistry, TD-DFT excited states, relaxed dihedral scans). Whenever a step involves Gaussian calculations, ARCHE-Chem refines the route section and keywords; the execution module runs the jobs and returns structured results. Reflection modules check logical coherence, input/output consistency, and mechanistic plausibility; failures or evidence-claim mismatches trigger targeted revision of hypotheses, parameters, or workflows — the loop iterates until a chemically consistent conclusion emerges.

ARCHE-Chem itself was trained in two stages: full-parameter continual pretraining on the curated Gaussian/computational-chemistry corpus, then task-oriented SFT across four task categories (basics, route generation, result analysis, error handling). At inference, 8 candidates are sampled and ranked by the reward model (syntactic validity + chemical consistency), taking the best. The authors state that all evaluation tasks and case-study content were excluded from training data, and that reported conclusions derive from calculations performed at inference rather than memorization.

Computational protocols: Case 1 uses B3LYP/6-31G(d) with IEFPCM(acetone) for optimization/frequencies, IRC verification, and M06-2X/def2-TZVPP single-point refinements (ωB97X-D/DLPNO-CCSD(T) also appears in the protocol). Case 2 uses B3LYP-D3(BJ)/def2-SVP with SMD(ethyl acetate) and TD-DFT for excited states. Case 3 uses ωB97X-D/def2-SVP with SMD(DMA). Benchmark scoring of the 16 compared models was done by GPT-4o against reference answers with fixed rubrics, with ARCHE-Chem evaluated in its Best-of-8 configuration and external models under fixed single prompting.

## Results & Highlights

- **Gaussian expert benchmark (held-out, 4 categories, 16 models compared, GPT-4o-judged):** ARCHE-Chem achieves 65.60% on Gaussian route generation (GRG) vs 44.80% for GPT-o3-mini (+20.8 pts) and 3.91–38.30% for the remaining 13 models; 80.30% on result analysis (GRA, +1.30 pts over next-best) and 87.60% on error handling (GEH, +3.90 pts). On conceptual basics (CGB) it scores 70.65%, losing to GPT-5.5's 82.71% (−12.06 pts) — the specialist advantage is task-dependent, concentrated in execution-critical skills.
- **Case 1 (asymmetric aldol, Houk JACS 2016):** From the reaction specification alone, ARCHE located 12 transition states — the 9 reported in the reference plus 3 unreported higher-energy candidates (ΔΔG‡ = 4.8, 5.2, 8.4 kcal/mol). Structural superposition with the literature TSs gives backbone RMSD of 0.01 Å (TS-2a major, crown/equatorial) and 0.15 Å (TS-2b minor, crown/axial); the computed enantiodetermining ΔΔG‡ = 1.9 kcal/mol vs 1.6 kcal/mol inferred from the experimental 88% ee at 303.15 K, and the entire run completed within 24 hours with zero manual intervention.
- **Case 2 (unpublished α-iodoboronate C–I cleavage, 83% yield under 450 nm light):** ARCHE eliminated concerted pathways on computed barriers of 46.0 kcal/mol (incompatible with room temperature) and direct/PPh2-assisted photoexcitation on singlet excitation energies of 102.8 and 75.8 kcal/mol (both above the 63.6 kcal/mol 450 nm photon). After reflective revision it found the CsPPh2 complex thermodynamically favourable (ΔG_assoc = −3.0 kcal/mol) with an excitation energy of 64.0 kcal/mol, matching the photon energy; control experiments (no Cs2CO3 / dark / TEMPO → product not detected) are consistent with this radical pathway.
- **Case 3 (Ni-catalyzed migratory cross-coupling):** Among candidate descriptors (bite angle, Ni–H bond strength/BDE, dihedral angle), relaxed scans showed only the Br–N–N–H dihedral distortion energy tracks the hydride-migration coordinate: ortho-methyl bipyridine shows the lowest torsional penalty (favoring non-planar conformations and migratory product), while parent/meta/para ligands incur higher penalties and favor in situ product — reproducing the experimentally observed selectivity trend across the ligand series and reconciling prior extensive transition-state analyses into one compact descriptor.

Corresponding authors Bowen Zhou (Tsinghua/PJLab, a veteran AI researcher), Yuqiang Li, and Biqing Qi lead credible agentic-AI programs. Contamination control is stated explicitly: evaluation tasks, mechanistic hypotheses, structures, protocols, and target results were kept out of all training/preference data, and proprietary GPT-5 usage is disclosed in the Methods. The 194-page document (main text plus extensive supplementary with full agent transcripts and Gaussian keywords) and the public code release support scrutiny. ## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The dual-model generalist/specialist split with a genuinely closed reflection loop is a real advance over prompt-injected chemistry agents (e.g., the cited fixed-protocol workflow automators), and the descriptor-abstraction case goes beyond mere workflow execution. However, the architecture itself is a competent composition of known pieces (RAG, multi-agent planning, Best-of-N test-time scaling, tool registries) — the novelty lies in the integration and the domain packaging, not in any new learning or search algorithm.
- **Rigor — system-level evidence is n=3 cherry-picked successes:** There is no end-to-end benchmark, no failure cases, no success-rate statistics over a corpus of reactions, and no repeated-run variance analysis. One cannot tell whether ARCHE succeeds on 3 out of 3 or 3 out of 300 attempts. The three cases are also escalating showcases chosen by the authors, an inherently favorable selection.
- **Missing ablations:** No system-level ablation isolating ARCHE-Chem's contribution (e.g., the same three cases run with the generalist model alone writing Gaussian inputs) — the claim that domain specialization is essential rests on the model-level benchmark, not on the end-to-end pipeline.
- **Benchmark fairness caveats (disclosed but real):** ARCHE-Chem was evaluated with Best-of-8 sampling while the 15 external models were run under fixed single prompting — an unequal-compute comparison; and all scoring was LLM-as-judge (GPT-4o with rubrics), not exact-match or executed-job verification. Notably the specialist loses to GPT-5.5 by 12.06 points on CGB, so superiority is confined to execution-facing categories with small margins (+1.30 GRA, +3.90 GEH).
- **Case 1 reproduction risk:** The 2016 Houk paper is public and could in principle be in GPT-5's pretraining; the paper argues conclusions came from explicit calculations (the 3 extra TSs and withheld protocols support this), but a contamination leak through the generalist model's parametric memory cannot be fully excluded since GPT-5's training data is unauditable.
- **Reproducibility:** The reasoning agents run on GPT-5 via API — the authors themselves note version drift makes exact reproduction impossible; only ARCHE-Chem and the harness are open-sourced. Case 2's excited-state energetics rest on TD-DFT at B3LYP-D3(BJ)/def2-SVP, a modest level of theory for charge-transfer-like excitations; the 64.0 vs 63.6 kcal/mol "match" is within the error bars one should expect from that method, so the mechanistic claim is plausibility-consistent rather than proven.
- **Significance:** If the reliability generalizes, the 24-hour unattended reproduction of a published stereochemical analysis is a genuine labor-saving milestone, and Case 2's self-correction from negative computational evidence is the most convincing demonstration of "discovery" in the paper.

## Strengths

- Genuine closed-loop self-correction, demonstrated concretely in Case 2 where computed negative results (46.0 kcal/mol concerted barrier; 102.8/75.8 kcal/mol excitation energies) triggered reflection and produced a hypothesis outside the initial set — exactly the behavior that distinguishes discovery from workflow replay.
- Careful contamination hygiene for ARCHE-Chem (evaluation and case content excluded from pretraining/SFT/preference data) and transparent disclosure of proprietary-model dependence and unequal Best-of-N evaluation conditions.
- Case 1 shows quantitative, not just qualitative, agreement (RMSD 0.01/0.15 Å; ΔΔG‡ 1.9 vs 1.6 kcal/mol) and finds physically sensible unreported TSs, indicating systematic landscape exploration rather than target recall.
- The descriptor abstraction in Case 3 converts a computation-heavy mechanistic question into a cheap, interpretable screening coordinate — immediately useful to synthetic chemists choosing bipyridine ligands.
- Public code harness and unusually complete supplementary material (full agent transcripts, all Gaussian routes, per-TS energetics) enabling post-hoc audit.

## Limitations

- Only three end-to-end demonstrations, all successful and author-selected; no failure analysis, success-rate quantification, or cross-domain stress test — the effective reliability of the full pipeline is unknown.
- Dependence on GPT-5 via API for all reasoning agents makes exact reproduction impossible and couples the science to an unauditable, changing black box.
- Gaussian-centric scope: solvation models, basis-set conventions, and the whole tool registry are tied to Gaussian 16; extension to ORCA, Q-Chem, or ML potentials is asserted as a "blueprint" but untested.
- ARCHE-Chem's margins over frontier models on the execution-critical benchmark categories are small (GRA +1.30, GEH +3.90 pts) and obtained under Best-of-8 vs single-shot prompting; the 7B specialist is decisively better only on route generation.
- Case 3's descriptor is semi-quantitative (trend reproduction only — no numeric prediction of product ratios), and Case 2's mechanism is consistency-validated rather than experimentally confirmed by, e.g., spectroscopic detection of the proposed intermediate.
- No reporting of compute cost, token consumption, or the number of reflection iterations per case, making efficiency claims ("within 24 hours") hard to generalize.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- What is ARCHE's end-to-end success rate over a corpus of, say, 50 published mechanisms with withheld answers, and how often does the reflection loop converge on a wrong-but-plausible mechanism instead of the correct one?
- How much of the Case 1 success survives if the generalist model is replaced by an open-weights model, isolating how much "discovery" depends on GPT-5's parametric memory of the chemistry literature?
- Can the Case 3 dihedral descriptor be validated prospectively — e.g., predicting regioselectivity for new bipyridine substituents before synthesis — rather than reproducing a known trend?
- Does the Best-of-8 reward selection actually beat simply prompting GPT-5.5 with the same Gaussian manual as retrieved context at the system level (the missing end-to-end ablation)?
- How sensitive are the Case 2 excited-state conclusions (~0.4 kcal/mol agreement margin) to the TD-DFT functional and basis set, and would a CAS/NEVPT2 or SAC-CI check change the mechanistic assignment?
- Can the framework report calibrated uncertainty on mechanistic claims, so that a chemist knows when "converged conclusion" means strong evidence versus exhausted hypothesis list?
