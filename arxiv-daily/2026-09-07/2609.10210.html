01 Methodology

Nothing trained, nothing fitted

Every component — an FFN unit or an attention (head, channel) pair — contributes a signed amount κu = au⟨cu, γ⊙(Ut−Ū)/ρ⟩ to a token's logit, and the sum is exact. The lens's one departure from common practice is the denominator: rank components until they reach 90% of the signed net, not the positive or absolute total.

Necessity is validated by ablation against random-removal controls; sufficiency by a search that seeds with the required graph, repairs starved members from their suppliers, and cuts dispensable ones until the surviving set alone reproduces the token with everything else zeroed. Damage is read on the margin (target logit minus strongest rival), since ablation uniformly inflates raw logits.

Mid-stack reading uses a layer-native token table — each layer's own picture of every token in its own frame, computed in one pass. Writes are named by the predictions a component drives; reads are decoded from weights and checked against causal token substitution on disjoint contexts. The same instruments run unchanged on 12 off-the-shelf models, GPT-2 124M to Mistral 7B.

  • Sufficiency generalizes: across 12 external models (124M–7B, 7 families), sufficient sets run 2–16 components — Mistral 7B and Qwen2.5 7B need just 2; closures stay 1.1–3.4% of each model with no growth in scale.
  • Right controls: same-size random sets reproduce the token 0/115 times; the ranking's own top-k only 7%.
  • Carriage: 74% of a layer's update (79% sigmoid) is a fixed linear map of the incoming state — 63% pure rotation — and the circuit is worth no more than a random set for later tokens (ratio 0.98–0.99).
  • Reads from weights: 58.9% top-8 causal-driver recovery (37× chance); attention value rows 0.844, rising to 0.898 with K.
  • GELU diagnosis: 59.8–62.0% of pre-activation mass sits where GELU is non-monotone (below x* ≈ −0.752); order-preserving activations read at the instrument ceiling with closures 4–7× smaller — but installs become two-part edits.
  • Signs are load-bearing: in gated FFNs 47–48% of prediction-carrying components fire negative; unsigned scope reads land edits on 4–11% of them, signed reads 96–97%.

03 Strengths

Validated the right way

The paper's self-skepticism is itself evidence.

  • One accounting correction, tested in isolation: same attribution, same predictions, only the denominator changes — the 2–3-order-of-magnitude effect holds across 18 models spanning 56× in parameters.
  • Sufficiency tested against matched controls (random sets 0/115; top-k ranking 7%); "floor" predictions where the embedding alone wins are measured and excluded, not pocketed.
  • Naming claims verified against causal substitution on disjoint contexts — and the paper quantifies where the method fails (below chance beyond K≈100 on GELU).
  • Exceptional hygiene: failed seeds reported beside converged ones; an appendix catalogs "measurements that failed and conventions that flatter."

04 Limitations

Read the fine print

Where the headline claims are weaker than they sound.

  • Sufficiency search is answer-informed — an existence proof, not a circuit-discovery algorithm; all sizes are upper bounds from a heuristic that converges on 96% of predictions for some models but 44% for others.
  • Every edit lives on a ~124M-parameter, one-epoch model; the 12 external models validate reading and accounting, not editing.
  • One-hop credit over-credits late layers (~0.53–0.65 vs causal effect at k=16) and misses indirect effects; all circuits are single-position objects, attention patterns not followed.
  • Coverage excludes GPT-NeoX/Pythia (parallel sublayers) and sparse MoE; sufficiency numbers move severalfold with the confidence floor and component unit (8 under zeroing vs 18 under the carriage background).

05 Open questions · for further exploration

  1. Can the sufficient-set search be made answer-blind — predicting a small circuit before knowing the token — and does its size stay in single digits?
  2. Does signed-net accounting change the conclusions of SAE-feature circuit papers if absolute-influence accumulation is replaced with signed nets?
  3. Does the 74–79% carriage share hold at frontier scale and long context, and does the free-width channel enabling private-direction circuits shrink with scale?
  4. Why do same-size 1B models differ so much in n90 — 76 for OLMo-2 vs 208 for Gemma-3? Tokenizer, data, or normalization placement?
  5. Does the GELU non-monotonicity diagnosis extend to SwiGLU/SiLU gates, and would an order-preserving gate cut cancellation in production models?
  6. How far does the upstream tap scale as a control surface — multi-component steering of whole behaviors rather than single tokens?
