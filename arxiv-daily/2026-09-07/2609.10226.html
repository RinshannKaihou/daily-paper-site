Methodology — how Φ-Bench is built

Assembled bottom-up from real systems work, not synthetic prompts: 2,260 top-tier systems papers (2023–2026) plus 1,852 GitHub artifacts, LLM-labeled into a taxonomy of 410 tags → 62 topics → 9 categories.

85 tasks · 3 graduated formats

KFC55
LHI20
E2EO10

KFC kernel completion, single file, interface given · LHI long-horizon implementation, multi-file, path open · E2EO whole-repo optimization, system-level goal only.

Synthesis routes
PR/issue-grounded reconstruction · agent-assisted synthesis with iterative test generation · expert curation.
Measurement
AB-BA paired runs (≥5 pairs), median speedup ratios, log-normalized reward (2× reference = full marks); implementation tasks binary, gated on build + tests + forbidden-edit constraints. Validity gates: reference ≥1.15× baseline, starter must fail ≥1 test. Dual anti-cheat: rule monitor + proctor agent, soft-offline pip/URLs.
Rig
8 frontier models at max reasoning/context on their strongest scaffold; 1× NVIDIA H20, 8 cores, 32 GiB; best of up to 16 submissions on LHI/E2EO.

* relative reward, not accuracy. DeepSeek V4 Pro collapses to 1.97% here.

  • Long-horizon implementation is the weak spot. Opus 5 drops 37.16 → 21.60 (−15.56 pts) from KFC to LHI; every model loses ground on repository-scale work.
  • Hardware & Edge is nearly unsolved. Best across all 8 models: 5.40% (Qwen3.7 Max); Opus 5 manages 3.90%; Sonnet 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol score 0.00.
  • Persistence beats caution. High scorers log more total errors (Opus 5: 175; GLM-5.2: 185) vs Sonnet 5's 26 — they keep iterating; Opus 5's errors skew CUDA, meaning its Python is right early.
  • Reasoning budget cuts both ways. All three ablated models peak at max effort, but Kimi K3 loses ~45% of its score at low effort — heavy reliance on test-time compute.
  • nanoGPT case study: Opus 5 detects a learning-rate change worsening BPB 1.3646 → 1.3932 (same seed), infers prolonged very-low LR is harmful, and a warmup–stable–decay schedule reaches 1.3248.

Strengths

  • Every task traces to a real paper artifact, merged PR, or expert-curated problem — no synthetic-prompt flavor.
  • KFC → LHI → E2EO cleanly separates local implementation, repo-scale navigation, and open-ended optimization.
  • Serious measurement engineering: AB-BA pairing, median ratios, validity gates, dual proctoring — only 3 flagged retrievals, none actual cheating.
  • Trajectory analysis distills actionable optimizer behaviors: cheap local hypothesis screening, variable/noise control, cautious attribution.
  • Honest negative results for essentially every lab's models; public leaderboard, GitHub, and Hugging Face release.

Limitations

  • Tiny per-category cells (85 tasks / 9 categories / 3 formats) make category rankings statistically fragile — Hardware & Edge rests on ~2–3 tasks.
  • Scaffold confound: GPT-5.6 Sol is the only model on Codex; the other seven run on Claude Code.
  • No training-data contamination analysis, though tasks rebuild public, likely-memorized pull requests.
  • Single NVIDIA H20 environment; no variance reported for headline scores.
  • Iteration and reasoning-budget findings rest on one E2EO task and 20 LHI tasks for three models — too thin for general claims.
  • Only 10 E2EO tasks, yet this is the format the autonomy narrative most depends on.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Q1Does Opus 5's lead survive a neutral, identical scaffold for all models?
  2. Q2Do models score higher on tasks whose reference PRs predate their training cutoffs (contamination rate)?
  3. Q3Can the three optimizer behaviors be induced by agent-scaffolding — e.g., forced local validation — even in weaker models?
  4. Q4What does a scaled 50+ task hardware-aware benchmark reveal about where models actually fail?
  5. Q5Do rankings hold on A100/H100/B200, or when best-of-16 shrinks to best-of-1?
  6. Q6Is robustness to reduced test-time compute trainable, or a distillation artifact (Kimi K3 −45% vs Opus 5 stable)?
