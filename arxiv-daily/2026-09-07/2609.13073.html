Key Finding · 关键发现

Autonomous research agents reached ~90% of human SoTA on an open-ended telecom-retrieval task — in 10 weeks, not 10 months.

Why This Matters

A first empirical map of autonomous-research limits on open-ended industrial tasks: agents tune well but lack architectural creativity, and still need human guidance.

对设计任何 agent 驱动的自动实验流水线有直接参考价值。

Critical Edge

The headline rests on a single uncontrolled campaign — budgets span 4 to 132 experiments, axes co-vary, no seed variance — and “autonomy” mostly meant toggling options from a human-written menu.

Methodology

Eight campaigns, three axes, one harness

Eight autonomous-research campaigns raced on telecom ticket retrieval (TTR): dense retrieval of resolution documents for incident queries, scored by Recall@k against 1.2k gold resolutions.

Frameworks

Karpathy-style single-agent autoresearch loop vs AutoScientists multi-agent teams split into representation / data-generation / architecture-hyperparameter squads.

Backends

Claude Sonnet 5 · Cursor Composer 2.5 · GPT-OSS 120B (locally hosted on Ollama behind the Pi coding agent).

Operational harness

Explicit task documentation, a forced search-space definition, and a deterministic outer bash loop that replaces unreliable agent self-management — the recipes that kept long campaigns alive.

Winning recipe (agent-discovered)

all-mpnet-base-v2, 2 epochs, InfoNCE/MNRL loss (inverse temperature 20); T→FA pairs (270k) plus 2×-oversampled peer-ticket T→T pairs (245k) → 760k instances, one metadata-mined hard negative per positive.

  • Design axes barely matter. Best per-campaign R@1: 0.3433 (single/Cursor) · 0.3378 (single/Claude) · 0.3353 (single/local GPT-OSS) vs 0.2043–0.2652 for multi-agent — a local open-weight model matched a Claude Sonnet 5 team.
  • Informedness was null — for the wrong reason. No agent ever implemented the documented SoTA techniques (re-ranking, augmentation, ensembling); even the winning “informed” run ignored the docs.
  • Economics. Winning run: 17 unique experiments, 1 GPU-week, 10 weeks end-to-end vs ~10 months of human engineering. Cost per campaign: $150–200 Cursor · $300–600 Claude · ~$0 local GPT-OSS.
  • Failure taxonomy. GPT-OSS spawned a sub-agent once in dozens of tries (Qwen Coder 30B failed entirely); Cursor re-ran duplicates (132 experiments → only 0.2652); agents idled, quit early, and under-filled GPU memory.

Secondary metrics, best system: MRR@10 0.4350 · NDCG@10 0.2210 · MAP@10 0.1370. R@50: 0.768 (agent) vs 0.627 (ensemble).

Strengths

What this paper does right

  • Rare honesty: null results, failed manipulations, and operational breakage are reported in detail, not buried.
  • The operational harness and per-agent failure taxonomy are immediately reusable by anyone running long agentic ML campaigns.
  • Real industrial scale and a real target: 250k proprietary tickets against a strong internal SoTA — not a toy benchmark.
  • Transferable modeling insight: 2×-oversampled peer-ticket (T→T) pairs beat human baselines built on T→FA mappings alone.
  • Grounded cost economics per backend — the kind of accounting agent papers almost never report.

Limitations

Where the claim wears thin

  • Single task, single proprietary dataset, three agents, eight campaigns — generalization is unverified.
  • Human-vs-agent comparison is confounded: campaigns inherit human-built data, code, evaluation, and human monitoring.
  • Ablation is uncontrolled: axes co-vary, budgets are unequal (4–132 experiments), no seed variance; multi-agent runs under-powered.
  • Variable abstraction trades breadth for control — agents toggle a menu instead of writing novel code, narrowing “open-endedness.”
  • Only downstream metrics and cost are measured — no assessment of the agents' research process, novelty, or reproducibility.

Open Questions · Further Exploration

Do the null results for multi-agent structure and agent choice persist under equal budgets and multiple seeds?
Can tool APIs (re-ranking, ensembling) or diversity-shaped rewards push agents past single-model fine-tuning, when documentation alone failed?
Would the discovered retriever combined with the SoTA's re-ranking/augmentation stack exceed the internal SoTA (0.380 R@1)?
Do the findings transfer beyond retrieval — to generation or structured prediction — and to newer agents that may not need the harness?
What is the minimum viable harness — which of documentation, search-space definition, or the deterministic loop matters most?
Can campaign cost be predicted from task openness (size of the P/λ/θ search space), enabling an agentic-vs-human build decision upfront?
