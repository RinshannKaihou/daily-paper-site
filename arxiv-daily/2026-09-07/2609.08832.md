> **一句话总结：** 本文定义并量化了 LLM 智能体的"一致性鸿沟"（AppWorld 上 ReAct/GPT-4.1 的 Mean@5 为 77% 但五次全过的任务仅 53%），并提出一个黑盒两阶段框架：通过重采样轨迹中每个推理点来检测易翻转步骤（Consistency Analyzer），再将其转化为注入情景记忆的一致性指南（Guideline Generator），使同任务 Pass@5 提升 16 个百分点、相似任务提升 13 个百分点，且不牺牲平均准确率。

> **TL;DR:** IBM researchers formalize the "consistency gap" — on AppWorld (168-task test normal split), a ReAct/GPT-4.1 agent averages 77% per-run pass rate (Mean@5) but passes all 5 runs on only 53% of tasks, a 24.4 pp shortfall. Their fix is a black-box, offline Detect-and-Generate pipeline: a Consistency Analyzer resamples each inference step (N=30) to find flip-prone decisions, and a Guideline Generator converts them into episodic-memory guidelines injected into future prompts. Against the no-guideline baseline, Pass@5 rises +16.0 pp same-task (53.0%→69.0%) and +13.0 pp on similar sibling tasks, with Mean@5 never degrading (+3.6 pp aggregate); on the weaker GPT-OSS-120B the relative Pass@5 gain is 59%. The catch: only one benchmark and one agent architecture are tested, guidelines come from a single trajectory per task, and resampling multiplies token cost ~30x.

## 核心贡献

- **形式化定义"一致性鸿沟"（Consistency Gap）**：提出 Gap@k = Mean@k − Pass^k 的度量体系，以及去除能力上限干扰的归一化一致性 Consistency@k = Pass^k / Mean@k；实证表明该鸿沟在 GPT-4.1 上达 24.4 pp、在 GPT-OSS-120B 上达 23.8 pp，且集中在约 32% 的混合成败任务上——平均准确率数字严重高估了部署用户的真实体验。
- **黑盒 Consistency Analyzer**：无需模型 logits、内部激活或智能体源码，仅向同一托管端点重发录制的提示（默认 N=30 次、温度 ≤0.5），按响应类型（自由文本/代码用 SBERT 余弦相似度、类别值用 Jaccard、半结构化 JSON 按字段加权组合）计算步级一致性分数，定位易翻转的推理点；并论证了为什么温度为零的贪心解码无法消除该问题（托管平台的浮点噪声、请求批处理等使概率分布本身漂移）。
- **面向一致性的指南生成机制**：将易翻转步骤（C(sᵢ) < 0.85）转化为策略/恢复/优化三类自然语言指南，存入双索引情景记忆并按余弦相似度在运行时检索注入，实现跨执行的自我进化。
- **轨迹一致性可作为结果预测器**：聚合轨迹一致性对轨迹成败的分类达到 AUROC 0.699，可在无真值标签的生产环境中辅助生成器判断轨迹成败。
- **实证验证**：在 AppWorld 上对 ReAct/GPT-4.1 与 GPT-OSS-120B 验证，同任务 Pass^5 +16 pp、相似任务 +13 pp，且 Mean@5 不降反升。

## Key Contributions

- Formalizes the **consistency gap** as Gap@k = Mean@k − Pass^k (in percentage points), plus a **normalized consistency** Consistency@k = Pass^k/Mean@k that decouples consistency from capability. Empirically, a ReAct/GPT-4.1 agent on AppWorld shows a 24.4 pp gap (77% Mean@5 vs 53% Pass^5), concentrated in ~32% of tasks that mix passes and failures across runs.
- A **black-box Consistency Analyzer** that needs no logits, internals, or agent source code: it re-issues recorded prompts to the hosted endpoint N times (default N=30, temperature ≤0.5) and scores step consistency by response type — mean pairwise SBERT cosine similarity for free text/code, mean pairwise Jaccard for categorical values (tool names, enum args), and a weighted per-field combination for semi-structured JSON (thoughts + action/action_input).
- **Consistency-targeted guideline generation**: steps below threshold θC = 0.85 on the decision path are flagged and converted by an LLM into strategy (correct-but-volatile patterns), recovery (variability correlated with failure), and optimization (skip unnecessary volatile steps) guidelines, stored in the dual-indexed episodic memory of Fang et al. (2026) and retrieved at runtime by cosine similarity.
- Shows aggregate trajectory consistency alone predicts trajectory pass/fail with **AUROC 0.699**, usable as an empirical outcome signal where no ground-truth rating exists.
- Explains why greedy decoding is insufficient: on hosted endpoints the token distributions themselves shift run-to-run (GPU floating-point arithmetic, request batching, tokenizer state), so flat, near-tied distributions flip even under temperature zero.

## Methodology

The framework is a two-stage offline pipeline ("Detect" and "Generate") built on the trajectory-informed agentic memory framework of Fang et al. (2026), operating as two coupled loops: an operational loop that executes tasks (optionally augmented with retrieved guidelines) while an observability layer records full trajectories, and an offline analysis loop that converts each new trajectory into new guidelines.

**Detect (Consistency Analyzer).** For each inference step sᵢ in a recorded trajectory, the analyzer re-issues the recorded prompt to the same LLM endpoint N times (default N = 30, temperature 0.5 in experiments), yielding a response set whose concentration around its mode estimates the step's flip risk. Three primitive measures are selected by response type: (1) free text and code — mean pairwise cosine similarity of SBERT sentence/code embeddings, which rewards semantic equivalence despite syntactic noise; (2) categorical values (tool names, enum arguments, identifiers) — mean pairwise Jaccard similarity over token sets, which penalizes any mismatch where exact values determine downstream actions; (3) structured combinations (ReAct's JSON with free-text thoughts plus categorical action fields) — decomposed into fields, each scored by its primitive measure and combined as a weighted sum with configurable importance weights (zeroed for irrelevant fields). Step scores aggregate to a trajectory score by simple mean. The design rationale is token-level: sharp next-token distributions are resilient to platform noise, while flat distributions with near-tied tokens flip across runs even at temperature zero — so response variability, not single-response confidence, predicts flipping.

**Generate (Guideline Generator).** Steps with consistency below θC = 0.85 that lie on the agent's decision path are marked on the trajectory, and an LLM generator produces guidelines specifically targeting those flagged steps, organized into the strategy/recovery/optimization taxonomy. Guidelines enter dual-indexed episodic memory (embedding plus category/priority/context/provenance metadata), are clustered and deduplicated, and are retrieved at runtime by cosine similarity against the incoming task or by an LLM-guided metadata-filtered selector.

**Evaluation protocol.** AppWorld test normal split (168 tasks; 57 easy / 48 medium / 63 hard), ReAct agent, k = 5 runs per task, standard AppWorld grader, GPT-4.1 on Azure and GPT-OSS-120B on AWS. Guidelines are generated from a single baseline trajectory per task by the same model that produced it, judged against its own consistency scorecard. Similar-task generalization exploits AppWorld's three-variant scenario structure: guidelines from variant v1 are injected into runs of v2 and v3 (336 evaluations total).

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline consistency gap (motivating result)**: ReAct/GPT-4.1 on AppWorld test normal (168 tasks): Mean@5 = 77.4% but Pass^5 = 53.0% — a 24.4 pp gap; GPT-OSS-120B: Mean@5 = 33.9%, Pass^5 = 10.1% (23.8 pp gap), with normalized consistency 0.69 vs 0.30 respectively, and GPT-OSS-120B collapsing to 0.00 normalized consistency on Hard tasks.
- **Same-task improvement, GPT-4.1**: consistency guidelines lift aggregate Pass^5 by +16.0 pp (53.0% → 69.0%, a 30% relative gain), largest absolute gain on Medium (+22.9 pp, 52.1% → 75.0%) and largest relative on Hard (+14.3 pp on a 31.7% baseline, 45% relative); Mean@5 rises +3.6 pp (77.4% → 81.0%) and normalized consistency +0.17 (0.68 → 0.85).
- **Similar-task generalization, GPT-4.1**: sibling-task guidelines lift aggregate Pass^5 by +13.0 pp (53.0% → 66.0%), only 3 pp below same-task, with a small −0.8 pp Mean@5 regression on Hard (61.9% → 61.1%) — the only accuracy regression in the GPT-4.1 tables.
- **GPT-OSS-120B**: same-task Pass^5 +6.0 pp (10.1% → 16.1%, 59% relative — nearly twice GPT-4.1's 30% relative gain) and Mean@5 +4.8 pp; similar-task transfer exceeds same-task at +8.7 pp (10.1% → 18.8%), with Hard normalized consistency moving from 0.00 to 0.29 (the largest single-cell gain across both tables), though Hard same-task Pass^5 only reaches 1.6% from 0.0%.
- **Gap closure**: for GPT-4.1 the aggregate gap to perfect consistency narrows from 0.32 to 0.15 (53% reduction); for GPT-OSS-120B from 0.70 to 0.58 — the weaker model's low raw capability caps what prompt-level guidelines alone can recover.
- **Outcome prediction**: aggregate trajectory consistency classifies trajectory pass/fail with AUROC 0.699 on 50 AppWorld test challenge tasks (single ReAct/GPT-4.1 trajectory each), supporting the generator's self-judgment where no ground truth exists.

## Authors & Credibility

The five authors are from IBM Software Innovation Lab (Duesterwald, Elder, Ngweta) and IBM Research (Ubaru, Zimon) — an industrial research team with a track record in agentic systems. The paper builds directly on Fang et al. (2026), a trajectory-informed memory framework, and the Detect-and-Generate pipeline has been contributed to the open-source ALTK-EVOLVE repository (github.com/AgentToolkit/altk-evolve), which supports reproducibility. The work carries an AAAI 2026 copyright notice and is positioned relative to concurrent work (Jha et al. 2026 on the analogous "reliability gap"). Claims are generally calibrated — the authors repeatedly flag what the framework does not do (no elimination of inconsistency, the analyzer is not a verifier).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: moderate but real. The Pass^k/Mean@k formalism generalizes pass@k from code generation, and the flip-risk-from-resampling analyzer is a sensible black-box instrument; however, the memory machinery (storage, clustering, retrieval) is inherited wholesale from Fang et al. (2026) — the delta is the upstream consistency-targeted extraction signal, not a new memory architecture.
- **Rigor / evaluation breadth is the main weakness**: everything rests on one benchmark (AppWorld), one agent architecture (ReAct), and two models from one vendor's ecosystem; the claim that the gap "is unlikely to be idiosyncratic" rests on explicitly unquantified "informal testing" with CUGA and other models. Cross-stack validation is deferred to future work.
- **Weak generalization test**: "similar-task" transfer means sibling variants within the same AppWorld scenario that differ in parameter values — a narrow notion of similarity; no transfer to genuinely different task distributions is tested.
- **Thin evidence base per guideline**: guidelines are generated from a *single* baseline trajectory per task, with the same model acting as judge on its own scorecard — a self-judging loop with no held-out validation stage before memory admission (the authors themselves admit this and sketch the fix as future work).
- **Small-denominator cells**: some GPT-OSS-120B headline gains sit on tiny absolutes (Hard same-task Pass^5: 0.0% → 1.6%; the celebrated 0.00 → 0.29 normalized consistency on Hard similar-task corresponds to 4.8% absolute Pass^5) — relative percentages flatter these numbers.
- **AUROC 0.699 is modest** — barely above chance-adjacent usefulness; it is presented as "acceptable," which is fair but should not be oversold as a strong outcome predictor.
- **Uncontrolled confound acknowledged**: the design assumes comparable platform-side non-determinism between baseline and guideline-injected runs, which the authors concede they "cannot directly measure or control."
- **Reproducibility**: code is open-sourced, metrics and thresholds (N=30, θC=0.85, temperature 0.5) are fully specified, and the benchmark is public — good. But all experiments use hosted endpoints whose noise is itself uncharacterized.

## Strengths

- Identifies and names a real, under-addressed production problem (average accuracy masking repeat-run unreliability) with clean, reusable metrics (Gap@k, normalized Consistency@k) that separate consistency from capability.
- The method is architecture-agnostic and black-box: no logits, no fine-tuning, no changes to the inference loop — applicable to any agent on a hosted LLM endpoint.
- The token-level explanation of why greedy decoding cannot fix run-to-run variance (hosted-endpoint probability drift from GPU arithmetic, batching, tokenizer state) is crisp and clarifying.
- Empirical results show the consistency–accuracy trade-off is avoidable: Mean@5 rises alongside Pass^5 in nearly every cell, supporting the design hypothesis.
- Honest, detailed limitations section; open-source contribution of the pipeline to ALTK-EVOLVE.

## Limitations

- Single benchmark (AppWorld), single agent scaffold (ReAct), two models — generality to other stacks is asserted, not shown.
- The default resampling budget (N = 30 per inference step) multiplies token cost roughly 30x per trajectory; adaptive resampling is left to future work.
- The analyzer scores stability, not correctness: a confidently-wrong decision looks consistent, so guidelines could entrench systematic errors.
- No validation gate between guideline generation and memory admission — vague, conflicting, or trajectory-overfit guidelines enter memory unchecked (a known agentic-memory failure mode, cf. Xiong et al. 2025).
- Mean aggregation treats steps as independent, ignoring error propagation from upstream low-consistency decisions.
- A substantial gap remains even after intervention (GPT-OSS-120B Hard tasks barely move same-task; aggregate gap-to-perfect still 0.15 for GPT-4.1).

## Relevance

这篇论文直击 LLM 智能体工程化落地的核心痛点——平均准确率掩盖的重复运行不可靠性，并给出可复用的度量（Gap@k、归一化一致性）与即插即用的黑盒检测-生成流水线。对于关注智能体可靠性评测与自进化/记忆增强智能体方向的研究，这是一项值得跟进的工作。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the +16 pp same-task Pass^5 gain survive on other agentic benchmarks (WebArena, SWE-bench, τ-bench) and other agent architectures (plan-and-execute, computer-use agents like CUGA)?
- Can the ~30x token cost of N=30 resampling be cut by adaptive sampling that concentrates budget only on candidate flat steps, without degrading guideline quality?
- Does adding the proposed validation gate (re-run resampling before memory admission, accept only if step consistency improves on held-out trajectories) measurably reduce harmful guideline accumulation?
- Since the analyzer conflates stability with correctness, how often do guidelines stabilize a *wrong* decision pattern, and does mixing a verifier or execution-feedback signal mitigate this?
- Does the consistency gap also appear under self-hosted inference with pinned seeds and deterministic batching, where the paper's platform-noise mechanism should largely vanish — and if so, what explains the residual?
