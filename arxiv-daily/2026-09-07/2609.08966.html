Why this matters

Challenges picking checkpoints by pretraining loss: with mid-training and SFT ahead, constant-LR or checkpoint-merging may be the safer start; solution density offers a cheap screen.

Applies directly to anyone designing LLM training pipelines — the default selection currency is provably unreliable.

Critical edge

Most of the reversal is one broken benchmark: excluding HumanEval+, the 0.113 gap shrinks to 0.016 — a non-termination pathology, not broad capability loss.

And the correlations rest on just 11 trajectories (mid-training 95% CI [−0.177, 0.836]); solution density is probed on only two tasks.

Methodology · distilled

Pretrain7.5T tokens @ 4k
→
Mid-training100B @ 8k
→
Long-context100B @ 64k
→
SFT10B @ 64k
  • CONSTANT — constant learning rate to the end of pretraining.
  • COOLDOWN — LR annealed to 10% of max over the final 800B tokens; wins every pretraining signal.
  • MERGE — no extra training; weighted average of 20 CONSTANT checkpoints over a trailing 600B-token window.

All three sources share the first 6.7T tokens and the total budget, then run the identical downstream stack. A 9-way LR sweep over COOLDOWN (plus SFT rates) yields 11 trajectories for correlation analysis.

Two probes: solution density — fraction of 100 Gaussian weight perturbations (σ ∈ {0.005, 0.001}) retaining ≥τ of the unperturbed GSM8K/MBPP score; and a stopping audit — raw generations under a 32,768-token cap, zlib compression ratios, and first-vs-last code-block re-execution in a network-disabled container.

Model: 30B decoder-only MoE, 3B active, 128 routed + 1 shared experts, top-8 sigmoid routing; hybrid Nesterov-Muon + AdamW on 512 GPUs, bfloat16. Stage-balanced benchmark aggregates at each boundary.

  • The stopping failure is specific and quantified: median 1,609 fenced code blocks per HumanEval+ response (max 5,318, one repeated 5,314 times); median zlib-to-raw ratio 0.044 on AIME vs 0.310 for CONSTANT.
  • SFT retuning cannot repair it: raising the SFT factor lifts COOLDOWN only 0.247 → 0.294, and all 27 swept checkpoints stay below the untuned CONSTANT (0.360) and MERGE (0.363).
  • IFBench's evaluation contract hides the pathology entirely — a reminder that aggregate deltas can misattribute the kind of defect.

Strengths

  • A rare, credibly-scaled negative result: pretraining loss/benchmark score — the default checkpoint-selection currency — provably misranks candidates in a production 30B MoE setting.
  • Actionable empirical rule: rankings within an LR sweep stabilize only after long-context adaptation (ρ=0.964), so early sweep-based pruning is unsafe.
  • The solution-density probe is cheap relative to running full downstream stacks and correctly separated all three sources.
  • Forensic depth: compression ratios, cap-hit rates, and first-block re-execution pin the HumanEval+ collapse on non-termination rather than missing capability.
  • Exceptional disclosure hygiene — the schedule-selection asymmetry (which favored COOLDOWN) is footnoted and correctly argued to understate the reversal.

Limitations

  • Single 30B MoE family, one seed per config, no variance estimates — the CONSTANT-vs-MERGE 0.003 difference is uninterpretable.
  • The headline gap is amplified by the stopping failure; the HE+-excluded 0.016 gap may sit within seed noise.
  • Solution density is evaluated on only two tasks with one unperturbed reference; σ is not calibrated to realistic training displacement; no causal claim.
  • Correlations over 11 points with wide CIs — the mid→long predictiveness jump could be a temporal-proximity artifact.
  • No RLHF/DPO stage; proprietary corpus and eval framework; the stopping mechanism is characterized symptomatically, not explained.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does solution density measured on pretraining-adjacent proxies predict post-stack performance across many independent runs — not just n=3 sources — and can σ be tied to actual parameter displacement?
  2. Is COOLDOWN's non-termination localized (unstable routing logits, a degenerate EOS head)? Would router re-initialization, EOS logit re-calibration, or a brief constant-LR "re-warming" repair stopping while keeping the better pretraining loss?
  3. Does the r = 0.473 → 0.884 predictiveness transition survive multiple seeds and later stages (RLVR/DPO), or is it a proximity artifact?
  4. Is the MERGE ≈ CONSTANT tie an artifact of a narrow aggregate where one severe failure (HumanEval+) dominates the variance?
  5. Can solution density be computed far more cheaply — Fisher/Hessian sketching, low-rank perturbations of shared experts — making it a practical periodic in-run checkpoint gate?
