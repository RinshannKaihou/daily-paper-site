> **一句话总结：** 发布 AgentDrift 数据集——12,536 条合成工具调用轨迹（71,024 个步骤级标签，覆盖 5 个智能体领域），每一步标注"良性/注入点/被劫持/注入失败"四种标签，用于训练和评测能定位间接提示注入何时进入、劫持了哪些步骤的轨迹级检测器；基准验证显示仅靠表层特征的逻辑回归只能召回 55.4% 的攻击（部分劫持仅 8.2%），证明该任务必须依赖序列建模。

> **TL;DR:** AgentDrift is a synthetic benchmark of 12,536 LLM-agent tool-call trajectories (71,024 steps) across email, banking, web, coding, and medical domains, where every step carries one of four injection-specific labels (benign / injection_point / hijacked / failed_injection), plus two adversarial contrast classes (resisted attacks and hard negatives that lexically resemble attacks). Built by Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct under protocol-specified labels, a closed-tool-vocabulary structural validator (78.8% acceptance of 15,900 candidates), and a 1,200-trajectory manual audit (99.6% label correctness), it targets detection, step-level localization, and attempt-vs-success discrimination. A surface-feature logistic-regression baseline on the 1,881-trajectory test split reaches only 55.4% attacked-class recall (F1 0.647, vs 88.0% on full hijacks but 8.2% on partial hijacks and 23.1% on delayed executions), establishing that the benchmark demands sequence modeling; the authors also openly quantify its shortcuts (e.g., a world-identity lookup reaches 86.1% binary accuracy on the default split), so results must use their anonymization or world-held-out protocols. Released under CC BY 4.0.

## 核心贡献

- **首个全步骤注入标注语料库**：12,536 条轨迹、71,024 个步骤，每一步都带注入专用标签（benign / injection_point / hijacked / failed_injection），标签串服从显式定义的正则文法（如全劫持为 B⁺IH⁺、部分劫持为 B⁺IH{1,2}B⁺），填补了现有攻击基准（InjecAgent、AgentDojo、ASB 等）只输出攻击成功率、不发布带监督标签的静态轨迹语料的空白。
- **两个防作弊对照类**：1,500 条"注入失败"轨迹（智能体读到毒化观测但成功抵抗，将"尝试"与"得手"区分开）和 1,500 条"困难负例"（内容在词面上酷似攻击但完全合法，如真实发票付款、IT 要求改密码），迫使检测器学行为偏离而非词面可疑度。
- **三种合规模式刻画劫持形态**：全劫持（3,136 条）、部分劫持（1,500 条，劫持 1–2 步后自我纠正恢复）、延迟执行（900 条，在正常步骤间夹带单个被劫持步骤），分别击破不同捷径，其中后两种占攻击类的 43.4%，是基准难度集中处。
- **诚实到反常规的质量报告**：完整披露构建管线每一层的测量结果——LLM 筛查裁判本身被困难负例欺骗（1,500 条中 1,494 条被误判为恶意），证明 LLM 裁判不能当安全标签验证器；银行域全劫持单元因工具池缺少外发邮件工具而 89.4% 被拒、只留下 256 条，也原样保留并解释。
- **系统性的捷径与泄漏测量**：量化了模板集中度（28.9% 的注入指向同一虚构域名 ext-audit.com）、攻击目标族语义塌缩（各家族的外部收件人比例均为 0.47–0.57）、世界身份泄漏（无模型查表预测器在默认切分上达 86.1% 二分类准确率），并给出世界匿名化和世界留出两种评测协议。

## Key Contributions

- **First fully step-labeled injection corpus**: 12,536 trajectories and 71,024 steps over five agent domains (email, banking, web, coding, medical; 103 closed-vocabulary tools), with every step labeled benign / injection_point / hijacked / failed_injection under a stated regular grammar (full hijack = B⁺IH⁺, partial = B⁺IH{1,2}B⁺, delayed = exactly one H enclosed by B, failed = B⁺FB⁺). Existing attack benchmarks (InjecAgent, AgentDojo, ASB, RAS-Eval, WASP, AgentDyn) output success rates only; guard-model datasets judge whole traces and fold resisted injections into the safe class.
- **Two adversarial contrast classes**: 1,500 failed-attack trajectories (a dedicated poison generator produces a genuinely dangerous observation that the agent then resists with a reasoned thought) and 1,500 hard negatives (legitimate in-domain content that pattern-matches attack language, e.g., 97.3% contain a suspicious keyword vs 23.9% of benign) — together separating attempt from success and deviation from lexical novelty.
- **Three compliance patterns that defeat different shortcuts**: full hijack (3,136), partial hijack (1,500, agent recovers after 1–2 hijacked steps and the recovery thought must name what went wrong), and delayed execution (900, a single hijacked step hidden among normal ones). The latter two make up 43.4% of the attacked class and concentrate the benchmark's difficulty.
- **A construction pipeline with fully accounted yield**: labels specified at generation time by category protocol, enforced by a closed-vocabulary structural validator, then programmatically checked, LLM-screened, and manually audited (1,200 trajectories, 99.6% correctness). The paper reports what failed: 3,364 of 15,900 candidates rejected (84.4% out-of-vocabulary tools, 15.2% unparseable JSON); the banking full-hijack cell lost 89.4% of candidates because the banking tool pool has no outbound-email tool, so the generator's preferred exfiltration-by-forwarding had no legal realization (1,228 forward_email + 432 send_email rejections).
- **Honest shortcut and leakage analysis with mitigation protocols**: template concentration (28.9% of the 7,036 injected observations name an ext-audit.com address; 75.3% of hard negatives mention $5,000), attack-goal-family collapse (external-recipient rates 0.57 for data_stealing vs 0.47–0.54 for families where exfiltration should be rare — hence family tags are stratification metadata, not a certified classification target), and world-identity leakage (a lookup predictor with no model reaches 86.1% binary accuracy on the test split, 93.3–98.2% in four domains), with world-anonymization and world-held-out protocols prescribed for any credible result.

## Methodology

The corpus is synthetic and single-generator: all trajectories were produced by quantized Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct (temperature 0.8, up to 3 retries) served through the AI-VERDE institutional gateway, orchestrated as a 30-element SLURM array (5 domains × 6 source categories). One API call emits a complete labeled trajectory. Generation is grounded in pre-built pools: 50 tasks per domain (250 total, rated simple/medium/complex) and pools of 50 synthetic persona worlds (name, company, email, 3–4 contacts) that define who is in-domain, so "external recipient" is decidable from the world rather than annotated.

Each of the six source categories (benign, three attacked patterns, failed attack, hard negative) has its own generation protocol whose label rule is stated in the prompt — e.g., the full-hijack protocol requires 1–3 benign steps, one injection point carrying an authoritative-sounding fake policy, and hijacked labels on every remaining step, with post-injection thoughts showing belief rather than doubt. A structural validator (Algorithm 1) then rejects any candidate whose tool falls outside the domain's 20–23-tool pool or whose label string violates the category grammar; rejected candidates are regenerated up to a 3× budget. Accepted trajectories are merged and split 70/15/15 (seed 42, stratified by category × domain): 8,775 train / 1,880 validation / 1,881 test.

Quality assurance runs in five layers, reported with unusual candor: (1) programmatic checks over the whole corpus (count identities hold exactly: 5,536 I steps for 5,536 attacked trajectories; 100% positional grammar adherence); (2) an LLM screening pass at temperature 0.1 whose verdicts were *not* used to filter — it passed only 3.6% of full hijacks and 0.4% of hard negatives, and its rationales show it was judging surface suspiciousness (it demanded that 1,494 of 1,500 legitimate hard negatives be labeled malicious), a methodological finding in itself; (3) a stratified manual audit of 1,200 trajectories (200 per source category) finding 99.6% step-label correctness.

Benchmark validation uses a deliberately crude baseline: a 6-dimensional surface feature vector per trajectory (step count, external-recipient counts/indicators, external-URL indicator, sink-verb tool indicator, 26-term suspicious-keyword count — all computed against the world), standardized and fed to a class-balanced logistic regression trained on the 8,775 training trajectories and evaluated once on the 1,881 test trajectories. The three defined tasks are trajectory-level detection (attacked vs rest), step-level localization (recover the injection index and close the hijacked span), and attempt-vs-success discrimination (flag rate on failed attacks at the operating point).

## Results & Highlights

- **Surface baseline overall (test split, 1,881 trajectories)**: attacked-class precision 0.778, recall 0.554, F1 0.647, AUROC 0.734, AUPRC 0.754 — i.e., nearly half the attacks (44.6%) are invisible to keyword/recipient/URL features, so the benchmark cannot be solved without sequence modeling.
- **Recall collapses on the subtle patterns**: the baseline recovers 88.0% of full hijacks (410/466) but only 8.2% of partial hijacks (19/231) and 23.1% of delayed executions (31/134); these two hard patterns are 43.4% of the attacked class, and their miss is not timid-threshold noise — benign and hard-negative false-positive rates are low (0.105 and 0.116).
- **Attempt-vs-success failure**: failed attacks are flagged at 0.187, nearly twice the benign rate (0.105), because a resisted injection still carries the poison's keywords and addresses — the exact confusion the failed-attack class was built to expose.
- **World-identity leakage (shortcut measurement)**: a no-model lookup predictor that assigns each test trajectory the majority category of the identical training world reaches 86.1% binary accuracy vs a 55.8% majority-class baseline, and 93.3–98.2% in banking/web/coding/medical; only email (47.7%, worlds shared across categories) escapes — hence the mandated anonymization/world-held-out protocols.
- **Surface-cue prevalence explains the misses**: 32.7% of attacked trajectories contain no external recipient or URL and 20.4% contain none of the three cue types at all; conversely 97.3% of hard negatives and 94.7% of failed attacks contain at least one suspicious keyword (vs 71.0% attacked, 23.9% benign), so a keyword detector fires on precisely the classes it should pass.
- **Corpus statistics behind the difficulty**: injections enter almost exclusively at steps 2–3 (2,850 of 3,136 full hijacks at step 3) via passive tools (read_webpage 598, read_email 541) and damage flows through active tools (execute_command 1,413, forward_email 969, fill_form 912 hijacked steps); 12,349 hijacked steps total, 2.23 per attacked trajectory; 99.7% of injected observations are lexically unique.

## Authors & Credibility

Both authors (Asif Pinjari and Mithun Paul Saint-Germain) are at the School of Informatics, Computing, and Cyber Systems at Northern Arizona University; generation ran on the university's Monsoon cluster via the AI-VERDE LLM gateway (acknowledged in the paper), which is consistent with a resource-constrained academic group using institutional infrastructure rather than frontier APIs. The paper reads as a datasets-and-benchmarks contribution in the NeurIPS D&B tradition: full datasheet, Croissant metadata, CC BY 4.0 release at github.com/Asif-0209/AgentDrift, exact reconciliation of every count (candidates → rejections → accepted → splits), and pre-registered limitations. The comparison table (Table I) against 16 prior resources (InjecAgent, AgentDojo, ASB, RAS-Eval, WASP, AgentDyn, R-Judge, TraceSafe, StepGuard, etc.) is based on their own reading and honestly marks concurrent unreleased work (StepGuard, Trajectory Attribution) as of September 2026. Credibility is unusually high for a synthetic-data paper precisely because the authors quantify their own artifacts (template concentration, family collapse, identity leakage, the banking-cell anomaly) rather than burying them.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The core niche — per-step injection labels with both a resisted class and hard negatives in a *released static corpus* — is a real gap; the related-work table shows the nearest neighbors either mark a single mutation point without the corrupted span (TraceSafe), attribute root causes rather than per-step states, or were unreleased at writing time (StepGuard, Trajectory Attribution). However, the framing "to our knowledge the first" carries the usual concurrent-work risk the authors themselves acknowledge.
- **Rigor**: This is the paper's strongest suit. Every count reconciles (5,536 I steps for 5,536 attacked trajectories), the grammar is exhaustively verified rather than sampled, rejection statistics explain the one anomaly (banking full-hijack: 256 accepted vs 2,160 rejected because the closed tool pool lacks any outbound-email tool), and the LLM-judge failure is reported as a finding instead of hidden. The world-identity leakage analysis, showing a trivial lookup reaches 86.1% on the default split, borders on self-sabotage in the best way — but it also means **any result on the default split is uninterpretable without their controls**, and naive users of the released corpus will produce inflated numbers.
- **Reproducibility**: High. Single named generator (quantized Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct), fixed seed 42, stratified splits published, loader code in the paper, CC BY 4.0 release with per-trajectory JSON. Caveat: the generation prompts and scripts are deliberately *not* released (to avoid shipping an attack generator), so exact corpus regeneration is not possible — only structural reproduction from the Appendix B protocol summary.
- **Significance**: The baseline result (55.4% recall; 8.2% on partial hijacks) convincingly establishes the need for sequence-aware monitors, but it is a deliberately weak strawman — there is no comparison against an actual guard model (e.g., an LLM judge detector or a fine-tuned encoder), so the paper shows the benchmark is *not trivially solvable*, not that it is *well-calibrated* for modern detectors. Whether transformer or LLM-based monitors find it easy remains untested in this paper.
- **Red flags / data quirks**: (1) 28.9% of injected observations share one fictitious domain (ext-audit.com) and 25.0% share a 4-gram — memorizable targets that the masking protocol only works around; (2) 75.3% of hard negatives mention $5,000 and three motifs cover ~three-quarters of the class, so the hard-negative class itself is templated; (3) injection position is nearly always step 2–3 (a positional prior a model can exploit); (4) attack-goal families collapsed semantically (external-recipient rate 0.57 for data_stealing vs 0.47–0.54 for the others) and are unusable as a classification target; (5) 248 of 250 task templates are shared across splits, so cross-task generalization is untested; (6) all data comes from one generator, so a detector may learn Llama-3.3 stylistic fingerprints; (7) label correctness (99.6%) is from a 1,200-trajectory audit sample, not exhaustive human gold annotation, and the audit's residual error class — hijacked steps only loosely tied to the injected instruction — is exactly the semantic link a localization detector needs to be true.

## Strengths

- Fills a precise, well-argued gap: no prior public corpus labels every step of every trajectory with injection-specific labels including the injection entry point and the corrupted span, plus a distinct resisted class.
- The hard-negative and failed-attack contrast classes are designed against specific detector shortcuts, and the paper proves they work as intended (keyword cues are anti-informative: 97.3% of hard negatives trigger them; the baseline flags failed attacks at ~2× the benign rate).
- Exceptionally honest reporting: the LLM-judge failure, the banking-cell yield anomaly, family collapse, and identity leakage are measured and published as results, with concrete evaluation protocols (world anonymization, world-held-out, masking of concentrated targets) for future users.
- Fully specified, reproducible baseline (six features, one logistic regression) that establishes the benchmark's premise — surface features recover 55.4% overall and almost nothing on partial hijacks (8.2%) — with per-category and per-pattern breakdowns.
- Clean release engineering: per-trajectory JSON, datasheet, Croissant metadata, loader code, CC BY 4.0, no real personal data, and generation prompts withheld so the corpus does not double as an attack generator.

## Limitations

- **Single-generator, fully synthetic distribution**: every trajectory (benign and attacked) comes from one model (Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct); transfer to real agent traffic is untested, and a detector may learn the generator's stylistic fingerprint.
- **Template concentration is memorizable**: 28.9% of injections name ext-audit.com; hard negatives lean on three motifs ($5,000 in 75.3%, in-domain audit@ in 75.9%, transfer in 77.7%) — the recommended masking is a workaround, not a fix.
- **World-identity leakage invalidates naive evaluation**: 86.1% binary accuracy from a world lookup on the default split (up to 98.2% in medical); results without anonymization or world-held-out splits do not count as injection detection.
- **Early, templated injection positions**: the poison almost always lands at step 2 or 3 and the three compliance patterns impose fixed positional templates; short horizons (3–11 steps, single agent) exclude long-horizon, multi-agent, and memory-mediated attacks.
- **Labels are protocol-specified, not exhaustive human gold**: 99.6% correctness is an estimate from a 1,200-trajectory audit; weakly-linked hijacked steps in some delayed-execution trajectories are the known residual error class; family tags cannot serve as a classification target.
- **Only a strawman baseline**: no LLM-based or fine-tuned sequence detector is evaluated, so the benchmark's difficulty for real monitors is asserted from feature-analysis rather than demonstrated.

## Relevance

与用户"自进化智能体与自动化研究"方向中"长时程自主智能体系统的可靠性与可信性"子课题直接相关：AgentDrift 为运行时监控器（读取智能体完整轨迹、判定注入是否得手、定位被劫持步骤）提供了首个全步骤标注的训练与评测语料，是构建可信长时程自主智能体时安全护栏研究的直接数据基础。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How do modern sequence models actually perform on this benchmark — would a fine-tuned encoder or an LLM-as-monitor substantially beat the 55.4% recall baseline, or do partial hijacks (8.2% recall) remain hard even for strong models?
- Under the world-held-out and world-anonymized protocols, how much of any reported detection accuracy survives — i.e., what fraction of "detection" on similar synthetic corpora is identity memorization in disguise?
- Can a detector trained on AgentDrift transfer to real agent traces (the authors propose pairing with sandboxed live-agent traces labeled with the same grammar) — does single-generator stylistic fingerprinting dominate, or does injection-driven behavioral drift generalize across generators?
- Since injections consistently enter through passive reads and damage flows through active writes, would a carrier/sink-aware architecture (e.g., attention constrained along the read→write dependency chain) improve localization of partial hijacks and delayed executions specifically?
- Does the observed collapse of attack-goal families (external-recipient rates 0.47–0.57 across all six families) reflect a limitation of protocol-conditioned generation, or do frontier agents genuinely converge on exfiltration as the dominant hijack realization regardless of attacker intent?
- Could the resistance rationales in the 1,500 failed-attack trajectories be distilled into a defense-side training signal (teaching agents to resist), complementing the monitor-side use the paper focuses on?
