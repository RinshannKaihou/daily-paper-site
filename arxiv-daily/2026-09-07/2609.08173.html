Key Path Identification for Resolving Knowledge Conflicts via SAE-based Steering ↗

Wenbo Zhang · Zhongxiang Sun · Zhiguang Han · Jun Xu — Gaoling School of AI, Renmin University of China · Nanyang Technological University

  • knowledge conflicts
  • SAE steering
  • mechanistic interpretability
  • RAG faithfulness
  • model editing

Key metric

+18%

average accuracy over the best mass-steering baseline (SPARE) across NQ-Swap & Macnoise on three 8B/9B models — steering 8–10 features total instead of 50+ per layer.

Why this matters

A blueprint for mechanistic interpretability applied to behavior editing: change an LLM's knowledge choice through a handful of inspectable features, not brute-force mass intervention.

⚠ Critical edge

The “+18% average” leans on one outlier cell (Gemma-2-9B / Macnoise, +32.83 caused by SPARE's layer-selection failure); excluding it, gains fall to ~4–15% — with no significance testing reported.

Missing control: does indegree selection actually beat just picking the 8 strongest single steerers?

01 · Methodology

A causal interaction graph, built with forward passes only

An SAE (131K features) encodes each residual stream into sparse features; steering feature i adds α·Wdec[i,:] to the stream at the prompt's last token. SPARE-style mutual information nominates each layer's positive features — KPI then swaps "steer them all" for a three-stage causal pipeline:

  1. 1
    Capture interactionsOn as few as 10 dev examples, steer each layer's top-10 positive features one at a time; record the top-5 downstream features by activation increase.
  2. 2
    Find key featuresFeatures become nodes, interactions directed edges; rank by indegree — bottlenecks where many positive features converge.
  3. 3
    Build the key pathThe layer holding the best single steer is the key layer; take top-indegree features there and in preceding layers (steering only propagates forward). 8–10 features total.

Testbed — Gemma-2-9B · Llama-3.1-8B · Llama-3-8B (131K residual SAEs); NQ-Swap & Macnoise, 200 pre-filtered unfaithful examples each, Qwen3-14B judge; baselines ICL, CAD, STA, SPARE; FaithEval-counterfactual as the OOD probe.

  • Mass baselines collapse next to the tiny circuit: on Gemma-2-9B / NQ-Swap, ICL revises 13.67 and CAD 11.50 versus KPI's 79.33.
  • Fewer features, stronger effect: the 8 selected features together (147 revised answers) beat every one of them steered alone — the chosen features collaborate rather than interfere.
  • OOD transfer holds: with settings carried over unchanged, KPI scores 58.1–61.4 on FaithEval-counterfactual across all six transfers; SPARE collapses in five of six.
  • Cheap and stable: the full graph over layers 18–36 costs 3.89 GPU-hours on one A100-40GB (peak 36.45 GiB); interaction patterns stabilize with just 10 dev samples (feature overlap 1.0).

03 · Strengths

What holds up

  • Diagnosis before method: a clean, falsifiable single-feature-steering experiment shows mutual-information ranking misidentifies steering-relevant features.
  • Forward passes only: 3.89 GPU-hours total makes cross-layer causal analysis practical where backprop circuit tracing is not.
  • Full side-effect accounting: faithful examples, MMLU, GSM8K, an all-token stress test, and OOD transfer where SPARE collapses and KPI does not.
  • Mechanistic depth: key features raise attention on golden-answer tokens; the knowledge-choice pattern localizes to layers 26–29 — validating steering in preceding layers.
  • 8–10 interpretable features is itself an interpretability win — the edited circuit stays small enough to inspect.

04 · Limitations

What to watch

  • Conflict scope is narrow: only entity-substitution conflicts (NQ-Swap, Macnoise); the method assumes retrieved context should be trusted.
  • Hardware ceiling: needs continuous-layer SAEs of the same model — nothing above 9B tested.
  • Correlation still at the gate: mutual information nominates candidates, so weak-but-correlated features can still enter the graph.
  • Backup circuits: disabling the found key path may not disable the behavior; steering strength needs per-model/per-dataset tuning on a single-peaked curve.
  • Small evaluation: n=200 test sets, LLM judging, 256-token truncation limit external validity.

05 · Open questions

Where this goes next

  1. Does indegree-based selection actually beat the simpler control — top-k features by individual steering effect — at matched feature counts?
  2. Do key paths stay stable under multi-hop conflicts, >256-token contexts, or adversarially noisy retrieval — and can the method tell correct context from merely present context?
  3. How does KPI behave at 70B+ scale once multi-layer SAEs exist, and does the key layer's location shift with scale or instruction tuning?
  4. Do the heuristic top-5 activation edges match ground-truth circuits — and does behavior reroute through backup circuits when the key path is ablated?
  5. Can steering strength adapt dynamically per input (the paper leaves this to future work), and does KPI compose with contrastive decoding rather than merely outperform it?
