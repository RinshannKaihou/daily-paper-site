> **一句话总结：** 本文发现并行训练中"最优并行策略"会随训练进程多次更替（离线选出的单一策略在 TTP 上落后逐迭代最优序列 1.8–11.4×），据此提出 CONA——用"吞吐量/(gbs+梯度噪声尺度)"代理指标在线评估候选策略、经贝叶斯优化在训练中动态切换并行策略的链式方法，在 GPT-3 1.3B、Llama-3.2-1B、BERT-Large 上比 AMP/Alpa/Metis/Galvatron/vTrain 快 1.4–9.6× 达到目标验证困惑度。

> **TL;DR:** The authors exhaustively run every feasible 3D-parallelism configuration for GPT-3 1.3B on WikiText-2 and show the perplexity-leading strategy changes three times during one training run, making offline single-strategy methods (AMP, Alpa, Metis, Galvatron, vTrain) 1.8–11.4× slower in time-to-perplexity (TTP) than a per-iteration optimal sequence. Their system CONA instead chains strategies online: it ranks candidates with a surrogate Φt(s) ∝ Throughput(s)/(gbs(s)+GNS_t) built from measured throughput and the gradient noise scale, uses Bayesian optimization per batch-size "frontier", and switches via lightweight checkpointing. On WikiText-103 with 16 A6000 GPUs, CONA reaches target perplexity 30 in 2.4 h on GPT-3 1.3B vs 4.4 h for the strongest baseline vTrain (1.8×) and 19.1 h for AMP (8.0×), and its chain matches 12 of 13 phases of the exhaustive OPT (perplexity SMAPE 2.6% avg).

## 核心贡献

- **实证揭示"最优并行策略随训练漂移"**：对 GPT-3 1.3B（WikiText-2，4×A100）穷举所有可行 (dp, tp, pp, mbs, gbs) 策略，发现按"同时刻最低验证困惑度"定义的领跑策略在 129/256/441 分钟处三次易主，训练被划分为 S1–S4 四个阶段；任何单一固定策略都无法全程跟踪该序列。
- **量化现有方法的结构性差距**：五种 SOTA 离线搜索方法（AMP、Alpa、Metis、Galvatron、vTrain）的 TTP 比逐迭代最优序列 OPT 慢 1.8–11.4×——这是单固定策略范式的固有缺陷，而非搜索质量问题。
- **提出 CONA 在线策略链（online strategy chaining）**：以"每秒训练损失期望下降量"为代理目标 Φt(s) = E[Δℓ_train|s]/T(s)，利用梯度噪声尺度（GNS）将不可直接测量的 per-batch 收益近似为 gbs/(gbs+GNS_t)，从而 Φt(s) ∝ Throughput(s)/(gbs(s)+GNS_t)，全部量均可在训练中在线获得。
- **轻量在线搜索设计**：证明在同一 gbs 组内最优策略即迭代时间最短策略（可用解析吞吐模型+贝叶斯优化求出，无需 GPU 剖析），并结合"GNS 非递减"推论将搜索压缩为沿 2 的幂次 gbs 阶梯单调前进的前沿搜索（小批量无需回访）；切换决策只是每次迭代一次标量比较，搜索与训练重叠执行。
- **理论安全保证**：附录 E 证明自适应策略链式切换在切换保持优化器/采样器状态不变的前提下不破坏非凸 SGD 的 O(1/√T) 收敛率，且该界与具体策略选择策略无关。

## Key Contributions

- **Empirical: the best parallelism strategy drifts during training.** Exhaustively running all feasible (dp, tp, pp, mbs, gbs) strategies for GPT-3 1.3B on WikiText-2 (4×A100-80GB) shows the perplexity-leading strategy changes three times (at 129, 256, and 441 minutes), partitioning training into four phases S1–S4 (e.g., S1 = (dp1,tp1,pp4,mbs2,gbs8) → S4 = (dp4,tp1,pp1,mbs8,gbs64)). No single fixed strategy can track this sequence.
- **Quantified structural gap of the offline paradigm.** Five SOTA offline methods (AMP, Alpa, Metis, Galvatron, vTrain) are 1.8–11.4× slower in time-to-perplexity (TTP) than the per-iteration optimal sequence (OPT), even when each method's OPT is restricted to its own search scope for fairness.
- **CONA — online strategy chaining.** Instead of one offline-selected strategy, CONA keeps training under the current strategy while ranking candidates with the surrogate Φt(s) ∝ Throughput(s)/(gbs(s)+GNS_t), where the gradient noise scale GNS_t (McCandlish et al., 2018) is measured online via AdaptDL; the paper validates that GNS_t differs by only 2.8% on average across strategies at the same iteration, justifying a shared denominator.
- **Lightweight, overlapped search.** Within a fixed global-batch-size group, maximizing Φt reduces to minimizing iteration time, which CONA solves with Bayesian optimization (BoTorch GP over an analytical cost model, no GPU profiling); Corollary 4.1 shows that under power-of-two gbs and non-decreasing GNS the search only needs to move monotonically to the next larger batch-size frontier, so the per-iteration decision cost is a single scalar comparison R_t = Φt(s_next)/Φt(s_cur) > 1.
- **Convergence safety theorem.** Appendix E proves adaptive strategy switching preserves the standard O(1/√T) nonconvex SGD rate as long as checkpointing restores optimizer state and the sampler stays unbiased (Assumption E.3), independent of the selection policy.

## Methodology

CONA (Korea University) treats the parallelism strategy s = (dp, tp, pp, mbs, gbs) as a dynamic training decision rather than a static configuration.

1. **Surrogate metric (§4.1).** The goal is minimizing TTP — wall-clock time to a target validation perplexity. Since validation loss is unavailable inside the training loop, CONA uses training loss, citing (and measuring: Pearson r > 0.9, Spearman ρ > 0.95) strong train/val correlation. The surrogate is the expected one-step training-loss decrease per unit wall-clock, Φt(s) = E[Δℓ_train_t | s]/T(s), decomposed into a throughput factor (estimated analytically from cost models) and a per-batch gain factor approximated via the gradient noise scale: E[Δℓ_train_t | s] ≈ kt · gbs/(gbs + GNS_t), giving Φt(s) ∝ Throughput(s)/(gbs(s) + GNS_t). A Markovian one-step decision rule selects argmax Φt(s) without predicting future dynamics.
2. **Online search (§4.2).** For each fixed gbs group S(B), the Φt-maximizing strategy is the minimum-iteration-time strategy, found by Bayesian optimization over an analytical throughput model (GP with 10 Latin-Hypercube bootstrap samples, one Expected-Improvement step per training iteration, acquisition stops when EI < 10% of the incumbent or after 100 updates). Corollary 4.1 (proved in Appendix A) shows the candidate search can proceed monotonically up the power-of-two gbs ladder — smaller batch sizes are never revisited — so only one frontier strategy is searched at a time.
3. **Switch decision and workflow.** Training continues under s_cur while GNS_t is measured each iteration; the switch fires when R_t = [T(s_cur)·gbs(s_next)·(gbs(s_cur)+GNS_t)] / [T(s_next)·gbs(s_cur)·(gbs(s_next)+GNS_t)] exceeds 1. Switches use universal/lightweight checkpointing (Lian et al., 2025). Feasibility (memory, GPU count dp·tp·pp = N_GPU, divisibility of gbs, hidden size, heads, layers) is enforced analytically before search.
4. **Implementation.** ~10K lines of Python on Megatron-DeepSpeed, GNS monitoring via AdaptDL, BO via BoTorch; open-sourced. Evaluation: 16× NVIDIA A6000 (2 Vast.ai nodes, 100 GbE), models GPT-3 1.3B, Llama-3.2-1B, BERT-Large on WikiText-103 (10,000 iterations, seq len 1024, AdamW, linear LR scaling with gbs), against AMP, Alpa, Metis, Galvatron, vTrain.

## Results & Highlights

- **TTP (target perplexity 30, WikiText-103, 16 GPUs):** CONA reaches the target in 2.4 h on GPT-3 1.3B vs 4.4 h for vTrain (strongest baseline, 1.8×) and 19.1 h for AMP (8.0×); on Llama-3.2-1B, 1.8 h vs 3.8 h (vTrain, 2.1×) and 17.2 h (AMP, 9.6×); on BERT-Large, 1.6 h vs 2.3 h (vTrain, 1.4×) and 7.8 h (AMP, 4.9×).
- **Final perplexity under a fixed 10,000-iteration budget:** CONA achieves 15.7 on GPT-3 1.3B vs 18.4 for vTrain (−2.7, ~1.2× lower) and 22.5 for AMP (1.4× lower); on Llama-3.2-1B, 16.7 vs 18.6 (vTrain) / 23.8 (AMP); on BERT-Large, 19.9 vs 23.0 (vTrain) / 28.4 (AMP).
- **Fixed-token budget (10 epochs of WikiText-103):** CONA finishes in 7.8 h on GPT-3 1.3B vs 14.0 h for vTrain (1.8×) and 62.3 h for AMP (8.0×); on Llama-3.2-1B, 6.0 h vs 10.8 h / 50.9 h (1.8×–8.5×); on BERT-Large, 2.0 h vs 3.1 h / 11.2 h (1.6×–5.6×).
- **Closeness to the exhaustive OPT (4-GPU validation):** CONA reproduces the same number of switches as OPT (3 for GPT-3 1.3B and BERT-Large, 4 for Llama-3.2-1B) with 12 of 13 strategy phases identical; per-minute SMAPE between CONA and OPT perplexity trajectories averages 2.6% (worst case 7.8% on Llama-3.2-1B).
- **Overhead and ablations:** CONA's total search overhead is 4.3–6.9 min per run (dominated by ~1.4 min checkpoint per switch), ~3.7× lower than the baselines' average 19 min offline search (up to ~39 min for Alpa). Ablations on GPT-3 1.3B: removing chaining raises TTP 1.6× and final PPL 1.3×; throughput-only (no GNS term) raises TTP 2.5× and PPL 2.0×; fixed 100-min-interval switching raises both 1.9×; dropping BO refinement raises TTP 1.3×.
- **Robustness and downstream quality:** gains persist across three base learning rates (1.24–1.83× TTP speedup over vTrain); mean zero-shot accuracy (lm-eval-harness / GLUE) is highest for CONA on all three models, exceeding the baseline mean by 1.6 / 1.6 / 2.1 points. On larger models, per-switch overhead grows to 2.5 min (Llama-3.1-8B) and 3.3 min (GPT-3 13B), still < 0.5% of a 10K-iteration run even with the observed maximum of four switches.

## Authors & Credibility

The paper is from the Operating Systems / Systems-ML group of Korea University (Department of Computer Science and Engineering; Chuck Yoo and Gyeongsik Yang are corresponding authors). The group has a track record in distributed-training systems research (e.g., resource-consumption prediction for distributed DL, FGCS 2022/2026 work cited in the references). The work builds on credible prior art — GNS from McCandlish et al. (2018), analytical transformer cost models (Isaev et al., 2023; AMPed), universal checkpointing (USENIX ATC 2025), and standard baselines (AMP, Alpa, Metis, Galvatron, vTrain). The implementation (~10K lines on Megatron-DeepSpeed) is open-sourced (github.com/OSSS-KU/CONA), and the paper discloses full hardware/software stacks, hyperparameters (Table 6), licenses, and even an AI-usage statement (proofreading only). Experiments use rented Vast.ai cloud GPUs, which is honestly reported.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: moderate-to-good, systems-flavored.** The core reframing — optimizing time-to-perplexity (a convergence-aware objective) instead of per-iteration time, and replacing offline single-strategy selection with online chaining — is a genuinely useful observation, and the exhaustive motivation study (Fig. 3) is the paper's strongest evidence. However, the ingredients are established: GNS-based batch-size scheduling echoes Smith et al. (2018) "don't decay the LR, increase the batch size", BO over analytical cost models is standard, and dynamic-parallelism systems exist (Tenplex, SOSP 2024). The contribution is the combination plus the monotone-frontier search reduction.
- **Rigor: good on ablations, weaker on scale and variance.** The OPT comparison is only constructible at the 4-GPU scale (constructing OPT at 16 GPUs would need >600 exhaustive runs), so the headline "close to OPT" claim is validated only in the small setting while the 1.4–9.6× headline numbers come from the 16-GPU setting. End-to-end metrics are reported "each from a single run" — no seeds or variance. Notably, part of the perplexity-per-wall-clock gain may come from the growing gbs schedule itself (linear LR scaling + larger batches) rather than from clever (dp,tp,pp,mbs) placement; the throughput-only ablation (TTP 5.4 h vs 2.4 h full CONA on GPT-3) controls for this only partially, and no ablation isolates the batch-size-schedule effect against a fixed-batch strategy with equivalent LR scaling.
- **Reproducibility: strong.** Open-source code, exhaustive configuration disclosure, analytical feasibility constraints in Appendix B, and a convergence theorem with explicit assumptions (Appendix E). The 2.8% cross-strategy GNS variance measurement and the GNS-fluctuation analysis (Appendix G, fallback never triggered) are commendable honesty.
- **Significance: real but bounded.** Workloads are ≤1.3B parameters trained on WikiText-103 for 10K iterations — toy scale by modern pretraining standards; only switch overhead (not end-to-end TTP) is measured at 8B/13B. The target perplexity of 30 is a loose, early-training target; whether strategy chaining still pays off in the long, small-GNS-noise tail of a real pretraining run, where throughput dominates, is untested. Baseline fidelity is a mild concern: some baselines are "reimplemented from the published description" (only Alpa/Galvatron public implementations), and the weakest baseline (AMP, 2022) inflates the 8–11.4× end of the headline range — against the strongest baseline vTrain the TTP gain is 1.4–2.1×.
- **Red flags: minor.** Zero-shot accuracies are near chance on several tasks (e.g., MMLU 25–27% for 1B-scale models trained on WikiText-103), so downstream "quality preserved" claims are weak signals rather than evidence. The "1.8–11.4× slower than OPT" motivation mixes baselines of very different vintage. The convergence theorem covers SGD only, while all experiments use AdamW (acknowledged in Appendix E).

## Strengths

- The exhaustive motivation study (running every feasible strategy and showing the leader changes three times) is a clean, falsifiable demonstration that the offline single-strategy paradigm is structurally TTP-suboptimal — the kind of measurement rarely done because it is expensive.
- The surrogate metric is well-motivated and cheap: Φt ∝ Throughput/(gbs+GNS) needs only online-measurable quantities, and the 2.8% cross-strategy GNS variance empirically supports its central assumption.
- The monotone batch-size frontier search (Corollary 4.1) turns a combinatorial (thousands of candidates) per-iteration problem into a one-scalar-comparison online rule — an elegant, practical reduction.
- Thorough ablations cleanly attribute gains to chaining, GNS-awareness, adaptive switch timing, and BO refinement, and the SMAPE-vs-OPT validation quantifies how close the heuristic gets to the exhaustive optimum (2.6% average).
- Open-source implementation, detailed configuration/hardware disclosure, LR-sensitivity check, and an explicit SGD convergence-safety theorem with a policy-agnostic statement.

## Limitations

- Scale: end-to-end evaluation is capped at 1.3B parameters on 16 A6000 GPUs with WikiText-103 (10 epochs); OPT validation is only at 4 GPUs; only switching overhead is measured at 8B/13B, not end-to-end TTP gains.
- The surrogate assumes training loss tracks validation perplexity (may weaken in overfitting regimes) and that GNS is non-decreasing (fluctuations could delay or miss switches; the built-in fallback was never triggered in their runs, so its behavior is untested in practice).
- gbs changes implicitly change the learning-rate regime via the linear scaling rule; joint hyperparameter adaptation is explicitly deferred to future work, leaving a possible confound between batch-size scheduling and parallelism placement.
- Scope excludes expert parallelism and sequence parallelism (relevant for MoE and long-context training), and per-metric results come from single runs without variance.
- Downstream task accuracies are close to chance for several zero-shot tasks at this model/data scale, limiting the strength of the "quality preserved" evidence.

## Relevance

对关注大模型分布式训练系统与训练效率的工作高度相关：它把并行策略选择从"最小化单步迭代时间"重构为"最小化到达目标困惑度的时间"，并给出可在线执行的策略切换机制，可直接启发训练集群调度、弹性训练与 auto-parallelism 方向的研究。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does strategy chaining still yield >1.3× TTP gains at 7B–70B scale on trillion-token corpora, where GNS plateaus early and throughput dominates, or does the benefit shrink to the warmup phase captured by the loose perplexity-30 target?
- How much of CONA's gain comes from the implicit growing-batch schedule (with linear LR scaling) versus smarter (dp,tp,pp,mbs) placement — would a fixed-topology baseline with an identical gbs/LR schedule close part of the 1.8× gap to vTrain?
- Can the surrogate be extended to sequence and expert parallelism, where activation memory and all-to-all communication make analytical throughput models and the shared-GNS assumption less accurate?
- How does CONA interact with modern hyperparameter rules (muP-style LR transfer, schedule-free optimizers) that do not follow linear batch-size scaling, and with AdamW's second-moment state across switches (the convergence proof covers SGD only)?
- What happens under cluster heterogeneity or preemption (elastic GPU counts), where the monotone frontier assumption may break and strategies might need to move to smaller batch sizes?
