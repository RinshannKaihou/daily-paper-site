> **一句话总结：** 本文提出 IB2 协议，将"被测系统"定义为模型权重+服务路由+精度+输出契约+评测 harness 的元组，用评分前的能力绑定预检（gold-blind preflight）、含可靠性的首过计分和结构性评分盲的裁决机制来评测企业级 AI 系统；在 11 个系统上的实验表明：同一权重的不同服务路由得分可相差 42 分（26.12 vs 68.27）、同一模型换 serving 端点相差 5.16 分（77.38 vs 82.54），且 7 个测试套件中有 4 个已对前沿系统饱和、真正拉开差距的只有治理数据库与多表连接两类任务。

> **TL;DR:** An Iterate.ai paper arguing that benchmarks score advertised model identifiers while enterprises deploy serving routes, and proposing IB2 — a protocol that binds weights + route + precision + output contract + harness into one "system under test" tuple before scoring, keeps first-pass failures in the score, and makes recovery decisions structurally score-blind. On its sealed 128-task / 987-assertion enterprise bank (7 suites: spreadsheets, documents, charts, tools, governed DB), two complete runs on identical weights (DeepInfra FP8, 26.12; CoreWeave FP8, 62.25) later failed distinct binding predicates versus Provider P's 68.27 (+42.14 / +6.01 deltas) — limits the advertised identifiers never exposed; Qwen3.8-27B moved 77.38 → 82.54 (+5.16, 95% interval [0.11, 10.60]) between serving arms; and 4 of 7 suites saturate under the top-six band (medians 92.1–96.9), with discrimination carried almost entirely by governed-DB (trimmed range 31.4) and multi-tab joins (12.5). Leader GPT-5.6 Sol's 88.34 vs Claude Fable 5's 87.37 (0.97 gap) sits inside a [−2.13, 4.26] interval, so the paper reports resolution groups, not ranks. More a measurement-methodology contribution than a leaderboard — and unusually frank about its own instrument's weaknesses.

## 核心贡献

- **重新定义被测对象（C1）**：将"系统"形式化为元组 ⟨模型 m, 服务路由 r, 精度 p, 推理强度 e, 能力包络 Ω, 工具实现 T, 输出契约 C, harness H⟩，任何分数都是这个元组的分数而非模型本身的分数；并证明两条性质：不可替换性（仅路由不同的两个系统是不同系统）与不对称可得性（闭源模型在原理上不存在"模型-only"分数）。
- **给出四套算法与配套判定表（C2）**：能力绑定预检（Algorithm 1，十个谓词分"能力/治理"两类）；gold-blind 裁决与恢复（Algorithm 2 + Table 5 决策表，恢复选择器读到的类型中根本不存在分数/金标/判分输出字段）；fail-closed 可比性（Algorithm 3，运行计划哈希不等则强制新开 run，禁止拼接）；套件分层任务集自助法（Algorithm 4，2 万次配对重采样给出敏感性区间）。
- **报告了模型标识符基准无法表达的路由可得性结果（C3）**：同权重三条路由中两条完整跑完 128 任务后（回顾性地）被最终版绑定门否决（4 图片上限 vs 13 图片契约；32,768 token 处截断），第三条前瞻性通过门后跑出 68.27；并诚实披露哪些绑定决策是回顾性、哪些是前瞻性。
- **参考实例化与 11 系统队列（C4）**：7 个套件、128 锁定任务、987 条确定性断言，覆盖单表/多表电子表格、可选文本 PDF、纯图像 OCR、图表、工具调用沙盒、治理数据库；语料密封不公开，程序（算法、模式、契约）开源。
- **自我诊断式发现**：4/7 套件对前沿系统饱和、难度标签（93.8% 标为 Expert/Frontier）无预测力、绑定门存在已命名缺口（GLM 路由过全部 10 个谓词后仍在线上败于 strict-JSON）——把"规范领先于实践"本身当作结果报告。

## Key Contributions

- **Redefines the measured object (C1).** Definition 1 binds weights, serving route, precision, reasoning effort, envelope, tool implementation, output contract, and harness into one tuple; Property 1 (non-substitutability) forbids merging rows that differ only in route, and Property 2 (asymmetric availability) argues no model-only score is epistemically available for closed-weight systems at all.
- **Specifies four algorithms with the tables they dispatch on (C2).** A gold-blind, content-addressed capability-binding preflight (10 predicates split into capability vs governance classes); adjudication and recovery whose selector reads a typed projection from which score, grader output, and gold references are absent (not merely unreadable); fail-closed run comparability via canonical run-plan hashing (no per-task splicing); and a paired suite-stratified task-set bootstrap (20,000 draws, pair-specific seeds) used strictly as task-set sensitivity, never as run-to-run variance.
- **Reports a route-availability outcome no identifier benchmark can express (C3).** Two complete single-route runs on identical weights (R11 DeepInfra FP8 = 26.12, R12 CoreWeave FP8 = 62.25) later failed distinct predicates of the finalized gate — a 4-image route limit against a 13-image contract, and truncation at exactly 32,768 completion tokens despite an advertised 262,144 maximum — while Provider P passed the full gate prospectively and scored 68.27 in a fresh run. Every prospective-vs-retrospective binding decision is disclosed.
- **Instrument and cohort (C4).** A sealed 128-task, 987-assertion bank across seven enterprise suites, and an eleven-system cohort (three GPT-5.6 endpoints, two Anthropic operating points, three Qwen variants, Kimi K3, Muse Glimmer 30B, and a composed GLM-5.2 + GLM-5V-Turbo system), released via methodology, schemas, synthetic conformance fixtures, and signed audit ledgers rather than a public corpus.
- **Protocol-level self-diagnosis as a result.** The paper names where its specification outran its execution (the unreachable PARTIAL_COVERAGE branch; the missing strict-JSON-under-reasoning predicate that let GLM pass the gate and then fail in production) and treats those gaps as findings about Algorithm 1.

## Methodology

The protocol has three load-bearing parts. **(1) Capability binding before scoring.** Algorithm 1 executes ten predicates (route identity, context/cap, structured output, 13-image envelope, 25-tool catalog and selection, tool-result continuation, >32,768-token completion ceiling, reasoning telemetry, usage/cost telemetry, zero-data-retention controls), each carrying a class (capability vs governance) and a lane set (which suites it gates). A route returns BOUND, PARTIAL_COVERAGE, or WITHHELD; a binding-time incompatibility is never converted into a model score of zero, and successful tasks from an incompatible provider are never spliced into a nominally single-route result. **(2) Reliability-inclusive first-pass scoring.** Every attempted response is graded once by deterministic assertions; absent responses earn a mechanically implied zero, parseable-but-contract-invalid responses earn partial credit for supplied work products, and unsupported capability is never zero-filled. The composite is the equal-suite mean (so the 22-task suites cannot dominate the 10- and 12-task ones). **(3) Structurally score-blind adjudication.** Non-OK responses enter a ledger; the recovery selector sees only an allowlisted projection (status, hashes, sanitized error) from which correctness information is absent by type, so a biased selector cannot selectively retry — a contract-valid-but-wrong answer is indistinguishable from a correct one at that point. Transport faults get exact-request recovery (reported separately, never replacing the primary score); malformed responses get exactly one retry as qualitative evidence; budget/tool-loop exhaustion is scored as model behaviour and never retried. Comparability is enforced by hashing the canonicalized run plan (tasks, rendered requests, configuration, corpus manifest, harness contract): any difference forces a new run. The reference instantiation has seven suites — S1 single-tab spreadsheet (10 tasks), S2 multi-tab reconciliation (22), S3 30-page selectable-text PDF with decoys (22), S4 image-only OCR at 180 DPI (22), S5 charts/diagrams (22), S6 stateful tool-calling sandbox, ≤12 calls, 25-tool catalog (18), S7 governed text-to-SQL over a 10M-row SQLite with certified/decoy column dictionary (12) — with two profiles (IB2-6 Core = 106 tasks without S4; IB2-7 Full = 128). Tasks were issued serially at concurrency 1; the sampling-parameter freeze is bounded by the first launch timestamp; sensitivity intervals come from Algorithm 4's paired bootstrap over all 55 unordered system pairs.

## Results & Highlights

- **Route availability is measurable and the identifier hides it.** On identical weights, DeepInfra FP8 (R11) scored 26.12 and CoreWeave FP8 (R12) scored 62.25 on the 128-task IB2-7 bank; the later finalized gate found DeepInfra rejects the 13-image contract at a 4-image route limit and CoreWeave stops at exactly 32,768 tokens (finish_reason=length) despite advertising 262,144. Provider P passed the full gate prospectively and scored 68.27 — descriptive paired route contrasts of +42.14 and +6.01 over identical locked task keys, deltas no model-identifier benchmark can express.
- **Serving-arm choice moves a declared revision by 5.16 points.** Qwen3.8-27B at the same declared revision and precision scored 77.38 on the fair-v5 RunPod arm vs 82.54 on the fair-v6 AkashML arm (95% paired task-set interval [0.11, 10.60], canonical seed 4046266586) — reported as an endpoint effect since the arms also differ in access mode, harness generation, and the serving tool-call parser.
- **Composite scores: GPT-5.6 Sol leads at 88.34 but the top of the table is not resolvable.** The leader-minus-runner-up gap (88.34 vs Claude Fable 5's 87.37, a 0.97-point difference) sits inside a task-set interval of [−2.13, 4.26]; 46 of 55 pairwise intervals exclude zero, but the two thinnest cuts defining nominal tiers T1|T2 ([0.11, 6.80], a 1.6% margin) and T2|T3 ([0.05, 7.62], 0.7%) fail any multiplicity adjustment (Bonferroni at 55 comparisons needs ~99.9% intervals) and T2|T3 additionally crosses zero under BCa ([−0.12, 7.45]). The evidential reading is three groups: top nine (88.34–68.27), Qwen3.5-122B-A10B (53.32), GLM-5.2+GLM-5V-Turbo (41.10).
- **Four of seven suites are saturated; discrimination lives in two.** Under Definition 2 over the top-six band: S6 tools (median 96.9, n90 = 5, three systems at exactly 100.00), S4 image OCR (95.5, 6), S1 single-tab (93.3, 4 — four systems tied at exactly 95.50), S3 selectable PDF (92.1, 6) are saturated; only S7 governed DB (trimmed range ρ = 31.4) and S2 multi-tab joins (ρ = 12.5) discriminate; S5 charts is compressed below ceiling (no band system ≥ 90, ρ = 6.0). The headline 41.10-to-88.34 spread is produced by governed database work, multi-tab joins, and weaker systems failing S6 outright — not by the multimodal document design motivating four of the seven suites.
- **Reliability inclusion changes conclusions, not wording.** Recomputing accuracy-only (excluding failed responses from denominators) makes GPT-5.6 Luna the point leader; Stage-1 transport recovery moves Kimi K3 from 72.99 to 76.93 (eighth to sixth, past Claude Sonnet 5's 75.40 and Muse Glimmer's 73.66→74.88). Of 138 total first-pass failures, GLM's S6/S7 lanes contribute 5.00 and 0.00 suite scores — 23 of its 24 strict-JSON failures are syntactically valid JSON wrapped in prose, so part of its 41.10 may be an artifact of the evaluator's strict parser rather than the served system.
- **Cost/latency are reported alongside.** Total cohort inference cost was $245.99; Muse Glimmer 30B was cheapest at $2.43 per full run vs GPT-5.6 Sol at $36.93; GPT-5.6 Terra had the lowest median task latency (18.3 s); token efficiency (fully-correct-task equivalents per million completion tokens) spans 171.81 (GPT-5.6 Sol) to 22.46 (GLM); reasoning share of completion tokens ranges 53.5%–96.9%.

## Authors & Credibility

All three authors (Blake Stenstrom, Charangan Vasantharajan, Brian Sathianathan) are employed by Iterate.ai, a company that sells enterprise AI systems; the benchmark was built as an internal release gate and no Iterate system appears in the evaluated cohort of eleven third-party competitors. Iterate.ai funded the study and paid the full $245.99 inference cost; no model vendor, serving provider, or aggregator supplied credits, discounts, or early access, and no vendor was consulted or given a pre-publication right of reply. The vendor conflict of interest is disclosed in a threats-to-validity table as unmitigable by design: the bank was built against Iterate's own systems, so the eleven third-party systems are its only held-out set. The paper's credibility rests less on institutional independence than on unusual procedural transparency — dated content-addressed attestations for the binding-predicate freeze (first complete-set receipt 2026-08-13, after R11/R12 and before the Provider P run), signed run-plan hashes, released audit ledgers (positioning_audit.csv, glm_forensic_review.csv, pairwise_intervals.csv), and AI-assisted-writing disclosure (OpenAI Codex/ChatGPT used for editing and figure generation, claims and numbers verified by the human authors).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine but narrow, and the paper says so. Route/stack binding is standard systems-benchmarking practice (MLPerf Inference's system-under-test); the transfer claimed is letting the served configuration change measured capability and reporting that change as an outcome, which none of the 18 audited capability benchmarks does. Reliability-inclusive scoring is adopted from τ²-bench's pass^k rather than claimed. The honest framing ("we invite correction on that point specifically") is a strength, but the core insight — a served route's limits can masquerade as model failures — is more a reporting-discipline argument than a new measurement technique.
- **Rigor:** Exceptionally high by benchmark-paper standards, mostly against the authors' own interest: they demote their own five-tier ladder to a diagnostic after showing two cuts fail multiplicity adjustment and BCa; they bound the R17 Stage-0 audit asymmetry (1.30–2.86 points of leverage) and refuse to interpret 0.11/0.05 lower bounds; they disclose a 0.01 rounding discrepancy and which ledger is authoritative. But the headline route contrasts rest on n = 1 per configuration with no run-to-run variance estimate, so the +42.14/+6.01 and +5.16 deltas are descriptive only — the needed five-cell attribution study (vLLM/SGLang × BF16/FP8 × hosted) is explicitly future work.
- **Reproducibility:** Asymmetric. The protocol, algorithms, schemas, and synthetic conformance items are released (Apache-2.0/CC BY 4.0), but the corpus is sealed, so every reported number is unfalsifiable by the reader — the paper concedes this in full and notes precedent (SEAL, ARC Prize) discloses more. The Qwen3.6-27B row is marked as not reproducible in principle because its route is withheld (Provider P's terms prohibit benchmarking), and several completed runs predate the current harness contract, covered only by a signed equivalence bridge.
- **Significance:** The empirical instrument is substantially depreciated by the paper's own analysis — 4/7 suites saturated, difficulty labels (93.8% Expert/Frontier) non-predictive, the challenge reserve consumed so the saturation finding is "the instrument diagnosing itself," and S3 leaders above 90 while OfficeQA Pro places frontier systems far lower on nominally the same capability, a construct-validity gap the authors attribute to their synthetic 30-page packets where controlling evidence is guaranteed present. What survives is the procedure, which the authors themselves argue should next be applied to somebody else's public corpus.
- **Red flags:** The evaluation envelope (13 images, 25 tools, >32,768-token completions) is a design choice, not a measured enterprise workload — and it is exactly the contract two of three routes failed, so the availability finding is scoped to a contract the authors invented. GLM's 41.10 is contract-dependent at the bottom in a way the top is not: 23 prose-wrapped-JSON failures might be recoverable under a lenient extractor, and the counterfactual rescore was (correctly, under their own rules) not run. The binding-gate result "two routes failed retrospectively" cannot support preregistered-exclusion claims since the complete predicate set postdates R11/R12. No human baseline exists, so absolute scores have no business interpretation.

## Strengths

- Treats serving-route dependence as a first-class measurement object with a formal definition, verified preflight, and availability reported as an outcome — a genuinely missing column in the 18-work audit.
- Adjudication is score-blind by type projection rather than by policy, which is a concrete, implementable anti-p-hacking mechanism (correctness is unreachable, not merely unread).
- Self-critical transparency that goes well beyond boilerplate: discloses retrospective vs prospective binding chronology, demotes its own tier ladder under multiplicity/BCa analysis, quantifies its Stage-0 audit hole, names the two predicates its gate is missing, and marks the one non-reproducible row.
- Reports resolution groups with interval backing instead of ranks, plus cost/latency/token-efficiency operating characteristics (HELM-style) for every system.
- Fail-closed run comparability (canonical run-plan hashing, no per-task substitution) is a clean answer to silent partial-rerun contamination.

## Limitations

- Single run per configuration: run-to-run variance is unidentified, and every ordinal claim rests on task-set resampling alone; the leader-pair interval (6.4 points wide) exceeds the 0.97-point gap it is supposed to resolve.
- Private, synthetic corpus: unfalsifiable by readers, built by an interested vendor, and demonstrably easier than the closest public comparator on document reasoning; the ~30% challenge reserve was consumed, so the saturation classification cannot be confirmed on unseen items.
- Four of seven suites (including the most expensive one, S4 image OCR) no longer separate the frontier; the composite is carried by two suites (S7 governed DB, S2 multi-tab joins), so "enterprise capability" here is narrower than the seven-suite branding suggests.
- The binding gate itself failed its one live test of admission: the GLM route passed all ten predicates and then failed strict-JSON conformance in production; the PARTIAL_COVERAGE branch is unreachable in v0.13.2 because no predicate has an S4-only lane.
- Stage-0 telemetry asymmetry across historical adapters means transport-failure counts (and hence primary scores) are not strictly comparable across the eleven systems — Qwen3.8-27B's 82.54 is not transport-corrected while competitors' transport faults stay in their scores.

## Relevance

对关注 LLM 评测方法论与基准构建的研究者，这篇论文提供了"按服务路由而非模型标识符评测"的完整可复用协议（能力绑定预检、含可靠性计分、评分盲裁决），其对基准饱和度、单次运行排名不稳定和私有语料不可证伪的自我诊断，对任何自建企业级 agent/文档/数据库评测都有直接的方法论借鉴价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the +5.16-point serving-arm effect for Qwen3.8-27B decompose into engine, quantization, batching, or tool-call-parser components under the proposed five-cell attribution study (vLLM/SGLang × BF16/FP8 × hosted endpoints)?
- How much of GLM's 41.10 is parser strictness rather than capability — what would a lenient-extractor counterfactual (run preregistered on a fresh cohort to preserve score-blindness) recover?
- Does the saturation finding transfer when the IB2 protocol (binding, gold-blind adjudication, resolution groups) is applied to a public corpus such as OfficeQA Pro or Spider 2.0, where the authors themselves argue the natural next test lies?
- Can capability availability and governance availability (predicates 1–7 vs 8–10) diverge in practice, and would reporting them separately change provider behaviour on telemetry and retention controls?
- What does a human baseline on the 128-task bank cost, and how far is the 88.34 leader from expert-level performance on this construct?
- How stable are route-level limits over time — do providers silently change envelopes (image caps, completion ceilings) under fixed model identifiers, and can content-addressed binding receipts detect drift?
