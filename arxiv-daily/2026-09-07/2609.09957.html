A label-free training recipe — group-relative rewards plus structure-masked self-distillation — that transfers to any domain with an executable verifier: code, solvers, unit tests.

On IndustryOR, a random-section KL mask beats the paper’s LP-structured mask (55.00 vs 53.00 maj@64) — the headline mechanism is not uniformly the active ingredient.

Methodology — the SOLID loop01

Unlabeled OR problems become a closed self-bootstrapping loop: execute the model’s own attempts, trust the solver’s majority, and distill only where the artifact disagrees.

STEP 1

Sample

24 on-policy rollouts per problem through a fixed nine-section template — variables, objective, constraints, math model, gurobipy code — at temperature 1.0.

STEP 2

Solve & vote

Every program is executed in Gurobi; objectives agreeing to 1e-6 form vote clusters. The majority cluster elects a pseudo-reference LP (median-closest rollout).

STEP 3

Masked distill

Majority rollouts train via GRPO group-relative advantages only. Minority rollouts add a reverse-KL term — the frozen policy re-scores itself conditioned on the pseudo-reference — masked to sections whose canonical LP signatures mismatch.

The LP-structured binary mask is the design contribution: distillation pressure lands only on the four solver-verifiable sections (decision variables, objective, constraints, code) that genuinely differ from the majority artifact, so privileged-context style drift (notation, ordering) cannot pollute the update. No rollout executes → the KL term vanishes entirely.

A_eff = A_i + β·b·(eΔ − 1)
Anchored sign reversal: a negative vote advantage anchors the update direction — only a strong LP-relevant preference (Δ > τi = log(1 − Ai/β)) can flip it. Style-level preferences cannot.

Strengths04
  • Eliminates the three costliest dependencies in OR post-training — verified answers, external evaluators, stronger teachers — in one coherent loop; directly useful for unlabeled proprietary problem pools.
  • LP-signature masking is domain-grounded and transferable: any response schema with solver-verifiable sections can adopt candidate–reference artifact comparison for localized distillation.
  • A concrete theoretical threshold τi = log(1 − Ai/β) for when privileged context can override vote-based suppression.
  • Honest, well-designed diagnostics: the correct-reference upper bound and the OPSD-declines-even-with-correct-LP experiment isolate style mismatch from reference error.
  • Public artifacts — code plus SOLID-Qwen3 weights, with a detailed reproducibility appendix (veRL/vLLM stack, sandbox limits, deterministic tie-breaking).
Limitations05
  • Narrow benefit distribution: large gains only on OptMATH, which is in-distribution with the training pool; MAMO-Complex and IndustryOR show marginal or mixed effects for the general-purpose model.
  • The mask story has a hole: a random-section mask outperforms the LP-structured mask on IndustryOR, weakening the claim that LP-structure localization is the key mechanism.
  • Majority-vote ceiling: when the policy is rarely correct, consensus elects a wrong reference and distillation reinforces the error — failure magnitude never quantified.
  • No supervised-RL ceiling comparison: trusted-solver-feedback training (SIRL / StepORLM style) is discussed but never run, so the true cost of giving up verified answers is unknown.
  • Statistical fragility: 100–203 problems per benchmark, no variance estimates; ~1-point deltas may be noise; maj@64 at temperature 1.0 makes evaluation expensive.
  • Scope constraints: LP signatures presuppose clean linear artifacts; only 4B / 8B models tested; proprietary solvers (Gurobi / COPT) required for training and mask construction.
Open questions — for further exploration06
  1. Q1How much of the OptMATH gain survives a training pool disjoint from all evaluation benchmarks (e.g. synthetic industrial problems)?
  2. Q2Why does the random KL mask beat the LP mask on IndustryOR — noisier artifact extraction, or localization genuinely harmful when the majority reference is often wrong?
  3. Q3Can confidence-weighted voting (cluster tightness, execution validity) gate the distillation term and recover the +11-point correct-reference headroom?
  4. Q4Can the anchored-reversal threshold τi be exploited as an adaptive per-token β schedule instead of a fixed KL coefficient?
  5. Q5Does the approach transfer beyond LP/MILP artifacts — cut logs, constraint-programming propagators, general code-generation test suites?
  6. Q6Can the bootstrapped policy plus consensus reference feed a stronger test-time search than plain maj@64?
