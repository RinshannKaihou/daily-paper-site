4-bit quantization silently pushes VLM yes/no scores toward ignoring the image — long before any answer flips.

「分数在变、答案未变、自适应无效」——在答案翻转之前，量化已把 yes/no 分数推向“忽略图像”的方向；按问题自适应选择精度被严格对照证伪。

Before the Flip: Measuring Hidden Score Shifts in Quantized Vision Language Models Before the Answer Changes for Visual Question Answering ↗

University of Maryland, Baltimore County · Preprint (not yet peer-reviewed) · project page: sourajitcs.github.io/beforetheflip

Key Metric

8.1×

hidden score drift under 4-bit — mean Prior Reversion Score rises 0.0094 → 0.0760 (W8 → W4) on 8,277 filtered POPE questions — yet only 265 of 9,000 answers flip; F1 holds at 84.72 vs 84.81.

Why This Matters

Accuracy-only evaluation is blind to compression damage inside quantized VLMs — this maps which blocks deserve extra bits and flags hallucination risk for deployments.

Critical Edge

The drift never reaches answers — flipped responses match the image-ignored output no better than shuffled chance (42.6% vs 42.7%, p = .722) — and the headline W4-vs-W8 ordering is only a “descriptive pattern”, reported without image-level confidence intervals.

01 · Methodology

Three stages, one diagnostic

1

Measure hidden drift

Every POPE question runs three ways: 16-bit weights + real image (reference), 16-bit with all image tokens replaced by one average token, and quantized weights + real image. Scores are mean-centered so only the YES/NO gap matters; PRS = ⟨q,d⟩ / (∥d∥² + ε) compares the compression shift with the replacement shift. Questions barely responsive to replacement (∥d∥ ≤ 0.302) are dropped — 8,277 of 9,000 kept.

2

Map precision sensitivity

Raise exactly one of 57 weight groups (24 vision-encoder blocks, 1 connector, 32 decoder blocks) from 4-bit to 8-bit; record the drop in a faithfulness loss combining positive-part cross-entropy increase and positive-part PRS, scale-normalized.

3

Falsify per-question adaptivity

Hindsight per-question group selection vs one fixed plan and two shuffled controls at +5 / +10 / +15% storage — every selected combination actually run through the model, because group effects interact.

Statistics: 10,000 image-level bootstrap resamples · McNemar's test for paired accuracies · disjoint calibration/evaluation splits

  • Flips don't align with the drift. Of the 265 W4 flips, 42.6% match the average-token answer vs 42.7% under shuffled control (p = .722); positive-PRS questions rise from 63.7% to 72.7%.
  • Early decoder blocks earn the bits. Depth vs loss-drop: ρ = −0.633 (p = .0001); first-half decoder blocks average +0.00994 loss reduction vs −0.00098 in the second half, and 13 decoder blocks actually worsen at W8. Vision encoder shows no depth trend (ρ = −0.133, p = .535).
  • Per-question adaptivity falsified. Hindsight gains 1.10 / 1.35 / 1.55 at +5 / +10 / +15% storage never beat both shuffled controls (1.66 & 1.23 at 10%); question×group interaction + noise = 93.5% of variance; the fixed 10% plan doesn't significantly beat simply keeping the image encoder at W8.
  • All learned selectors collapse to one profile (K3_P2 + VCD); prediction MAE 0.43–0.64 spans the whole W4→W8 loss range.
  • Aggregate tasks hold. CHAIR 24.0 → 24.6% (p = .773); MME / MMBench move < 1 point; ARC Easy drops 3.20 points at W4.
  • Minimizing PRS ≠ better answers. VCD peaks F1 at 85.74 (α = 0.25) vs 84.72 without; forcing PRS to −0.688 (α = 1.0) drops F1 to 84.41 and costs 2.73 accuracy points.
03 · Strengths

What it gets right

  • +Formalizes a real blind spot: accuracy-only VQA evaluation cannot see compression-induced score shifts behind unchanged answers.
  • +The two-shuffle control design converts per-question precision from a plausible-sounding idea into a falsifiable — and falsified — claim; a methodological template for mixed-precision work.
  • +Exceptional reporting honesty: negative results, selector collapse, CI-includes-zero comparisons, and post-hoc example selection are all disclosed in the main text.
  • +Broad robustness sweep — 5 replacement variants, 6+ tasks, 2 models, fixed vs learned plans, VCD — hard to dismiss as a single-setup artifact.
04 · Limitations

Where it stops short

  • −PRS is defined only for binary YES/NO questions (POPE); no formulation for open-ended VQA, multi-choice ranking, or generation.
  • −“Reversion” direction is partly baked in: the default average token favors NO (answers YES for 0.6% of questions); a gray-image control yields PRS ≈ 0.
  • −Mean PRS lacks image-level confidence intervals — the central W4 > W8 claim is descriptive, not tested.
  • −Only two 7B VLMs (Qwen forced to non-native 448×448, 256 tokens); no latency, memory, or energy numbers; quantization-scheme details thin.
  • −No working remedy: fixed plans never significantly beat “keep the image encoder at W8,” and learned selectors collapse to a single profile plus universal VCD.
05 · Open Questions

Questions for further exploration

  1. Q1Does hidden score shift predict eventual accuracy collapse at more aggressive precisions (W3, W2, NF4), where flips become common?
  2. Q2Can the analysis extend to open-ended generation — compression-induced drift of caption log-probabilities vs image-ablated outputs?
  3. Q3Why do earlier decoder blocks benefit most — outlier-heavy attention projections, KV-cache sensitivity, or the vision-language fusion point?
  4. Q4Could a cheaper, better selector rescue query-adaptive precision, given prediction MAE (0.43–0.64) currently swamps profile differences?
  5. Q5Is there a composite objective (PRS⁺ + task-calibrated CE) that tracks deployment-relevant faithfulness without over-correction?
  6. Q6Do the findings transfer to 70B-class, native-resolution VLMs, where token distributions and outlier structure differ substantially?
