Normalized causal effect of patching the partner-expertise direction at the peak-decoding layer (L8) vs. layer 36 — the paper's "order-of-magnitude separation" between where an attribute is decodable and where it is used.

probe acc @ L8 0.79 chance 0.25 lexical baseline 0.32
Why this matters

Probes find where an attribute lives — not where it's used. Steering partner-conditioned behavior must target the network's second half.

Critical edge

The probed model generated the corpus itself — the layer-8 "expertise" signal may partly be self-generation recognition, not partner modeling.

Flagged by the authors themselves

Credibility Sarti (Northeastern; Pythia/HART tooling) · Okamoto (Georgia Tech) · SPAR program · verbatim prompts, released corpus & code · claims explicitly scoped, conjecture stated as a falsifiable hypothesis
Methodology — four-stage pipeline
01

Generate

28 twelve-turn dialogues (7 persona pairings × 4 research scenarios), both speakers role-played by Llama-3.3-70B across 4 expertise levels (high-school → professor). Personas set style and blind spots, never credentials — a leakage detector passes all 28 transcripts.

02

Extract

Residual-stream activations (80 layers, d = 8192, via NDIF/nnsight) read at the participant-perspective position, sampled every 4th layer; speakers pooled into 56 instances.

03

Probe

L2 logistic-regression probes predict the partner's level (4-class); the speaker's own stated level is the stated-attribute control; a five-feature lexical classifier is the surface baseline.

04

Patch

Source−target activation differences injected into 48 cross-level pairs; normalized causal effect E(L) measured with the readout fixed at the last layer. Controls: norm-matched random vectors + a probe-free cosine diagnostic.

  • Probe errors concentrate on adjacent expertise levels, not random confusions.

Strengths
  • +Crisp answer, converging methods: decode site (L8) and causal onset (~L36) separated by an order of magnitude in effect size.
  • +The stated-vs-inferred contrast within one corpus elegantly isolates inferredness as the varying factor.
  • +Corpus hygiene: credential-leak screening, verbatim prompt release, symmetry and cross-domain robustness checks.
  • +Honest scoping — framed as an initial demonstration; the conjecture is converted into a testable prediction.
Limitations
  • –Single model, 28 synthetic dialogues; no cross-architecture or real-conversation replication.
  • –Self-generation confound: the corpus is authored by the model family later probed.
  • –Every-4th-layer sampling and small n make fine layer claims fragile — "the single peak cell is not reliably identifiable at this sample size."
  • –Readout fixed at the final layer; no behavioral demonstration — sycophancy steering is inferred, not tested.
Open questions for further exploration
Q1Does the decode-to-causal gap scale with inference difficulty — does making expertise explicit monotonically move causal onset toward layer 8?
Q2Does the L20–36 onset replicate across architectures (other depths, MoE, non-Llama families), or is it an artifact of Llama-3.3's midpoint transition?
Q3How much of the layer-8 signal is partner-modeling vs. self-generation recognition — would human-written or cross-model dialogues shift the peak?
Q4Can second-half steering vectors (L36+) actually change partner-conditioned behavior like sycophancy, and at what fidelity cost?
Q5Do other inferred relational attributes (intent, emotional state, deception) show the same early-encode / late-use profile?
