01 · Methodology

The optimizer loop, distilled

SkillAdam optimizes a Markdown skill document steering a frozen LLM agent, maximizing the benchmark's native evaluator score J(S). An LLM bootstraps the initial skill from baseline trajectories and their feedback. Each iteration: roll out on a mini-batch → generate a patch conditioned on current evidence plus two persistent optimizer states → evaluate the candidate on the same batch → accept only if a designated metric clears its threshold while protected metrics stay in bounds (no separate validation set).

The persistent states are the Adam analogues. The Evolving Issue Tracker (first moment) accumulates issue patterns and past attempt outcomes, so effective corrections are not undone by iteration-local feedback. Case-level improvement variance is EMA-smoothed into volatility Vt (second moment), which shrinks the next edit budget when revisions help some cases and hurt others. The analogy is functional — no gradients flow in skill space.

03 · Strengths

What convinces

  • Attacks the two genuinely painful failure modes of iterative skill refinement — oscillating revisions that undo each other, and evidence-blind edit scope — with one mechanism each.
  • The Adam correspondence is a clarifying conceptual contribution, legible to the optimization community and a template for other discrete-optimization-by-LLM settings.
  • Exemplary cost accounting: per-phase token/request counts and a ~4× efficiency ratio — exactly what a practitioner needs.
  • A convincing simultaneous quality + efficiency win on the hard setting (+6.7 pp DP-Avg, −67.3% tokens vs the strongest recent baseline).
  • Unusual candor: repeatedly states which experiments do not isolate which components.
04 · Limitations

What to watch

  • Short-horizon margins ≤1.1 pp (one tie) from single, unseeded-variance runs — statistical significance not established.
  • Five baseline columns imported from the SkillOpt paper rather than re-run; a merged train+selection pool is a protocol asymmetry favoring SkillAdam.
  • Absolute performance stays low on the hardest slice (DP-Travel 11.7% = 7/60 cases); tiny test slices make per-slice numbers coarse.
  • Ablation is cumulative and DeepPlanning-only; no sensitivity analysis for budget parameters (bbase, bmin, Vmax, β2) or acceptance thresholds.
  • No formal guarantees; the tracker and patcher depend on strong frozen backbones — weak-model optimization behavior is untested.
05 · Questions for Further Exploration
Would a deterministic, embedding-based issue tracker match the LLM-maintained EIT — how much of the first-moment benefit is the bookkeeping vs. simply having any persistent log?
How sensitive is the volatility→budget map to β2, Vmax, bmin and mini-batch size — and can the budget rule be learned rather than hand-mapped?
Would a small held-out acceptance set recover high-test-score revisions (like the rejected iteration-8 patch) without reintroducing instability?
Do the gains persist when the optimization backbone is a weak/open model, or does the EIT degrade into noise?
Can the memory-plus-budget decomposition port to multi-file skill packages, tool libraries, or agent workflows?
