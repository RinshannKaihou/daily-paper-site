> **一句话总结：** 首个系统性测量现代 LLM 提示词隐状态 Gromov 双曲性的"地图"研究：基于 10 个开源模型、4 个数据集、共 818,904 次采样-层测量，发现**层深而非参数规模**主导几何结构——中间层形成接近 δrel≈1 的高双曲性平台，末层显著变得更树状（如 Qwen2.5-7B-Base 上 MATH500 末层 0.549 vs 中间层 0.996），且同规模 7/8B 模型家族间差异（最大 0.256）远大于家族内规模效应（±0.03 量级）。

> **TL;DR:** This is the first systematic study of Gromov Hyperbolicity (GH, a four-point distance test of tree-likeness) on prompt-token hidden states of contemporary LLMs. Across 10 open-weight checkpoints (Qwen2.5 family, Llama-3/3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-Math/Coder, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B) and 4 benchmarks (MATH500, HumanEval, WinoGrande, TruthfulQA), it builds 818,904 sample-layer measurements of diameter-normalized δrel. The headline findings: depth dominates (middle layers plateau at δrel≈0.995–0.996, final layers drop by −0.29 to −0.56); scale is weak and non-monotonic (pooled 1.5B→7B shifts of only −0.029/+0.035, with domain-specific sign reversals); matched 7/8B families differ far more than scale (e.g., on HumanEval, Qwen2.5-7B-Coder-Instruct 0.438 vs Qwen2.5-Math-7B 0.694, a 0.256 gap, Kruskal–Wallis p<10⁻³⁰⁰); and domain effects interact with specialization (instruction tuning lowers TruthfulQA from 0.604 to 0.471; coder specialization makes HumanEval the most tree-like domain). Useful as a diagnostic toolkit and reporting-norm proposal, not as evidence of symbolic structure.

## 核心贡献

- **首个系统性 GH 测绘**：据作者所知，这是首个对现代开源 LLM 提示词隐状态（prompt-token hidden states）系统测量 Gromov 双曲性的工作，构建了覆盖参数规模、层深、模型家族、输入域四个坐标轴的"双曲性地图"（atlas），而非给每个模型一个单一数字。
- **统一直径归一化的测量协议**：定义 δrel = δ̂/diam(X)，使不同隐维度和激活范数的模型可直接比较；对每个"模型-层-提示"集合采样 2,000 个四元组估计四点双曲性；严格 prompt-only 设计排除生成文本长度、答案正确性等混杂因素。
- **"层深主导、规模微弱"的核心发现**：中间层普遍形成 δrel≈1 的高平台（Qwen2.5-7B-Base 上各域中间层中位数 0.995–0.996），末层大幅下降（−0.29 至 −0.56），而 1.5B→7B 的规模效应仅在 ±0.03 量级且方向随域反转。
- **家族/特化 > 规模**：同规模 7/8B 模型末层中位数从 0.429（Llama-3-8B-Instruct）到 0.633（Qwen2.5-Math-7B）；代码特化使 HumanEval 成为 Coder 模型最树状的域（0.438），而数学特化反而在 MATH500 上比基座更不树状（0.600 vs 0.549）。
- **生成时控制实验与稳健性检验**：在 50 个固定 HumanEval 提示上做教师强制回放，Base/Coder 差异在 prompt-only（−0.242）、生成代码（−0.254）、全序列（−0.238）三种状态下均稳定，且在余弦距离（−0.356）等替代度量下仍然成立。
- **提出报告规范**：末层汇总应配以层深剖面；模型比较应按家族、规模、域匹配，否则域效应会被误认为特化效应、特化效应会被误认为规模效应。

## Key Contributions

- **First systematic GH study of LLM prompt hidden states**: to the authors' knowledge, the first systematic measurement of Gromov Hyperbolicity — a distance-based, four-point test of tree-likeness — over prompt-token hidden states of contemporary open-weight LLMs, organized as an "atlas" over four axes (parameter scale, layer depth, model family, input domain) instead of one number per model.
- **A unified, comparable measurement protocol**: diameter-normalized statistic δrel = δ̂/diam(X) enabling cross-model comparison despite different hidden dimensions and activation norms; 2,000 sampled quadruples per model–layer–prompt set; strict prompt-only design that excludes confounds from decoding length, answer correctness, and model-specific generation behavior.
- **The "depth dominates, scale is weak" finding**: middle layers form a near-δrel≈1 plateau (medians 0.995–0.996 across all domains for Qwen2.5-7B-Base) with sharp final-layer drops (−0.29 to −0.56 per model–domain pair), whereas 1.5B→7B scale shifts are only −0.029 (base track) and +0.035 (math track) pooled, with sign reversals across domains.
- **Family and specialization effects exceed scale**: pooled matched 7/8B medians span 0.429 (Llama-3-8B-Instruct) to 0.633 (Qwen2.5-Math-7B), Kruskal–Wallis p < 10⁻³⁰⁰; the HumanEval gap between Qwen2.5-7B-Coder-Instruct (0.438) and Qwen2.5-Math-7B (0.694) is 0.256 — far larger than any within-family scale shift.
- **A specialization×domain interaction result**: HumanEval is the *least* tree-like domain for Qwen2.5-7B-Base (0.676) but the *most* tree-like for Qwen2.5-7B-Coder-Instruct (0.438, a −0.238 shift), showing code prompts become tree-like only in the model whose training makes code central.
- **Generation-time control and robustness checks**: on 50 fixed HumanEval prompts with teacher-forced replay, the Base/Coder contrast persists for prompt-only (−0.242), generated-code (−0.254), and full-sequence (−0.238) states, and under cosine (−0.356), token-wise L2-normalized Euclidean (−0.308), and centered-L2 (−0.287) distance variants.
- **Practical reporting norms**: final-layer summaries should be accompanied by at least a coarse depth profile, and model comparisons should be matched by family, scale, and domain — otherwise domain effects masquerade as specialization and specialization as scale.

## Methodology

The paper measures Gromov Hyperbolicity (GH) of prompt-token hidden states without modifying any model. For four token states a, b, c, d with pairwise Euclidean distances, the four-point condition computes s1 = d(a,b)+d(c,d), s2 = d(a,c)+d(b,d), s3 = d(a,d)+d(b,c); sorting these as s(1)≤s(2)≤s(3), the quadruple hyperbolicity is δ = (s(3)−s(2))/2, with δ=0 for a perfect tree metric. Because raw δ scales with distance magnitude, the authors normalize: δrel(X) = δ̂/diam(X), where δ̂ is the sampled four-point estimate and diam(X) the maximum pairwise distance in the set. Lower δrel means more tree-like distance structure.

For each model m, layer ℓ, and prompt x, the measurement set X(m,ℓ,x) collects hidden states of all prompt tokens (embedding layer plus every transformer layer). Per example and layer, 2,000 quadruples of token states are sampled, yielding 818,904 valid sample-layer measurements. The design is deliberately prompt-only for the main atlas: generated responses are excluded so prompt geometry is not confounded with decoding length or answer correctness.

Models: ten open-weight checkpoints — 1.5B set (Qwen2.5-1.5B, Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B) and 7/8B set (Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Base, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Coder-Instruct, Qwen2.5-Math-7B). Datasets: MATH500 (500 examples), HumanEval (164), WinoGrande (1,267), TruthfulQA (817), identical composition across checkpoints.

Statistics: medians with interquartile ranges (distributions are skewed and bounded near 1 in middle layers); Wilcoxon signed-rank tests for paired layer comparisons over matched examples; Kruskal–Wallis tests with median shifts for unpaired family/domain comparisons; Cliff's Δ for effect size. Controls include prompt-length quartile binning, and a generation-time study on 50 fixed HumanEval prompts (greedy decoding, 512-token budget, teacher-forced replay with 1,000 quadruples per example-layer) comparing prompt-only, generated-code, and full-sequence states under multiple distance choices (Euclidean, cosine, L2-normalized, centered-L2).

## Results & Highlights

- **Depth dominates (RQ2)**: for Qwen2.5-7B-Base, the middle-layer median δrel is essentially identical across domains (0.995–0.996, where middle = relative layer positions 0.35–0.70), while final-layer medians spread from 0.549 (MATH500) to 0.676 (HumanEval); the final-minus-middle shift is negative for every domain, from −0.319 (HumanEval) to −0.446 (MATH500). Every model–domain pair shows a negative shift, ranging from −0.29 (Qwen2.5-Math-7B on HumanEval) to about −0.56 (Llama-3.1-8B on MATH500).
- **Scale effects are weak and non-monotonic (RQ1)**: pooled over domains, the Qwen2.5 base track shifts only −0.029 from 1.5B to 7B and the math track +0.035 (pooled Cliff's Δ = +0.08). But domain-level reversals occur: on MATH500 the base model goes 0.526→0.549 (+0.024) and on HumanEval 0.655→0.676 (+0.021), i.e., the 7B base model is *less* tree-like, while on TruthfulQA it goes 0.655→0.604 (−0.051), i.e., *more* tree-like. The math track's 7B model is higher-δrel in every domain, with the largest shift on HumanEval (+0.093, 0.600→0.694).
- **Matched 7/8B families differ more than scale (RQ3)**: pooled final-layer medians range from 0.429 (Llama-3-8B-Instruct) to 0.633 (Qwen2.5-Math-7B), Kruskal–Wallis p < 10⁻³⁰⁰. On HumanEval, the gap between Qwen2.5-Math-7B (0.694) and Qwen2.5-7B-Coder-Instruct (0.438) is 0.256 — far larger than any within-family scale shift in Table 1 (max |+0.093|). On TruthfulQA, Llama-3-8B-Instruct reaches 0.405 while Qwen2.5-Math-7B sits at 0.624.
- **Specialization reorders domains rather than shifting them uniformly (RQ4)**: for Qwen2.5-7B-Base the domain ordering is MATH500 (0.549) < TruthfulQA (0.604) < WinoGrande (0.648) < HumanEval (0.676). Instruction tuning lowers values broadly (HumanEval 0.676→0.539; TruthfulQA 0.604→0.471), while coder specialization produces the sharpest interaction: HumanEval becomes the *lowest*-δrel domain for Qwen2.5-7B-Coder-Instruct (0.438, a −0.238 shift from base). Math specialization does *not* make math prompts more tree-like — Qwen2.5-Math-7B on MATH500 is 0.600 vs 0.549 for the base model.
- **Embedding layer shows the final drop is not inherited**: TruthfulQA starts at an embedding-layer median of 0.310 and *rises* to 0.604 at the final layer, while MATH500 (0.860), HumanEval (0.905), and WinoGrande (0.895) start high — yet all end far below the middle-layer plateau (~0.996), so the network can both sharpen and weaken tree-like structure relative to its input representation.
- **Generation-time control confirms robustness**: on 50 fixed HumanEval prompts (teacher-forced replay), final-layer Base/Coder medians are 0.665/0.422 (prompt-only), 0.676/0.423 (generated-code tokens), and 0.656/0.418 (full sequence) — Coder–Base differences of −0.242, −0.254, and −0.238; the contrast appears in 50/50 prompt sets, 45/50 generated-code sets, and 48/50 full sequences, and survives cosine (−0.356), token-wise L2-normalized Euclidean (−0.308), and centered-L2 (−0.287) variants. Coder also generates longer continuations (median 208 vs 142 tokens, +59), yet the geometry gap is not explained by length.
- **Prompt-length confound controlled**: binning by prompt-length quartile, HumanEval and WinoGrande remain higher-δrel than MATH500 across quartiles for Qwen2.5-7B-Base, with modest within-domain slopes, so the family and specialization effects are not a length artifact.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: genuinely novel as a systematic GH survey of modern-decoder-LLM hidden states — prior work trained hyperbolic embeddings or used geometry for detection tasks, while this paper measures an unmodified diagnostic across scale/depth/family/domain. That said, the finding "middle layers are anisotropic/non-tree-like, final layers compress" is directionally consistent with a large existing literature on layer-wise representation geometry (e.g., Valeriani et al. 2023 on intrinsic dimension by layer), so the surprise value is moderate: the contribution is the comparative atlas, not a new phenomenon.
- **Rigor**: strong on measurement hygiene — diameter normalization for cross-model comparability, 2,000 quadruples per sample-layer, Wilcoxon/Kruskal–Wallis with IQRs rather than point estimates, prompt-length quartile controls, a teacher-forced generation-time replication, and robustness across three alternative distance choices. The paper is also commendably explicit that lower δrel does not prove a symbolic tree is stored.
- **Reproducibility**: good in principle (public models, standard datasets, fully specified protocol), but no code release is mentioned in the extracted text, and some details (random-initialization control specifics, exact layer extraction mechanics) appear only in passing or in an appendix — an independent re-run would require re-deriving choices like the 0.35–0.70 middle-layer band.
- **Significance**: as a diagnostic-methodology paper it is useful; as science it is descriptive. The central regularities (depth dominates, family > scale) are correlational, and the paper itself concedes it "does not identify the mechanism causally." No downstream payoff (e.g., linking δrel to task accuracy, hallucination, or pruning decisions) is demonstrated, which limits immediate practical impact.
- **Red flags / weaknesses to note**: (1) The middle-layer plateau pinned at δrel≈0.995–0.996 for *every* domain and both scale tracks suggests the normalized statistic saturates near its ceiling in middle layers — with IQRs like [0.995, 0.996], the four-point estimate there is nearly constant, so "middle-layer" comparisons carry almost no information; this near-degenerate regime deserves more discussion than it gets. (2) The HumanEval domain has only 164 examples vs 1,267 for WinoGrande, yet some headline interactions (e.g., the coder −0.238 shift) rest on the smallest dataset; the paper argues sample size does not explain the effect, but per-cell IQRs on HumanEval are visibly wider. (3) Euclidean distance over raw hidden states is dominated by the well-known activation-norm/rogue-dimension structure; the centered/cosine checks appear only in the 50-prompt HumanEval control, not across the full atlas, so the main 818,904-measurement map is validated under alternative metrics only in one narrow slice. (4) "Family" confounds architecture, tokenizer, pretraining data, and post-training in a single factor — the paper admits this, but it means the RQ3 headline ("families differ") cannot be attributed to any cause. (5) No accuracy/behavior linkage: δrel is never shown to predict any capability or failure mode, so the practical value remains prospective.

## Strengths

- First-mover systematic mapping of GH across scale, depth, family, and domain with a single, internally consistent protocol — the "atlas" framing is apt and the four research questions are cleanly separated.
- Careful normalization (δrel) and statistical reporting (medians + IQRs, nonparametric tests, Cliff's Δ) that resist overinterpretation of a skewed statistic.
- Prompt-only design elegantly removes decoding-length and correctness confounds; the supplementary teacher-forced replay extends the finding to actual generation.
- The coder-specialization × HumanEval interaction (0.676 base → 0.438 coder) is a crisp, memorable result: tree-like geometry appears in a domain only when the model's training makes that domain central.
- Honest framing throughout: the authors repeatedly delimit what GH does and does not show, and propose concrete reporting norms (depth profiles, matched comparisons) the community can adopt immediately.
- Multiple robustness checks (prompt-length quartiles, three distance variants, 50/50 per-prompt contrast rates) strengthen confidence in the headline patterns.

## Limitations

- **Descriptive, not causal**: the study measures correlations between training choices and geometry; it explicitly does not identify mechanisms, and δrel is never linked to task performance, hallucination rates, or any behavioral outcome.
- **Prompt-only main atlas**: generated-token geometry is covered only in a 50-prompt HumanEval control between two models; decoding-time geometry for the other eight models and three datasets is unmeasured.
- **Weak controls for the random-initialization baseline**: per the paper's own limitations section, the random-init control covers only one architecture, one initialization, and two domains.
- **Distance and normalization sensitivity**: the main atlas uses raw Euclidean distances with diameter normalization; cosine, centered representations, and alternative normalizers are tested only in the narrow HumanEval control. Tokenization, prompt templates, and quadruple sampling also affect the statistic.
- **Metric saturation in middle layers**: the plateau sits at the near-maximum of the normalized statistic (IQR [0.995, 0.996]), making middle-layer values nearly uninformative and potentially compressing cross-model differences there.
- **Small/unbalanced datasets**: HumanEval contributes only 164 prompts per model, yet anchors the most-cited specialization interaction; family effects entangle architecture, tokenizer, data, and post-training.
- **Coverage**: only dense Qwen/Llama-family decoder LLMs at 1.5B and 7/8B; no MoE models, no larger scales, no non-Transformer architectures, and English-only benchmarks.

## 相关性

这项工作直接属于"LLM 隐状态的表示几何/可解释性"经验研究：它提供了一个跨模型、跨层、跨域可比的双曲性测量协议，其"中间层高 δrel 平台 + 末层压缩为更树状结构"的层深轨迹，与隐状态各向异性、内在维度、探针分析等表示分析工具互补，可作为表示质量诊断套件中的一个坐标轴。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does final-layer δrel predict anything behavioral — e.g., accuracy on MATH500/HumanEval, hallucination rates on TruthfulQA, or robustness to adversarial prompts — or is it purely descriptive geometry?
- Why does math specialization (Qwen2.5-Math-7B) *raise* final-layer δrel even on MATH500 (0.600 vs 0.549 base) while coder specialization sharply lowers it on HumanEval — is this a difference in training data structure (code's nested syntax vs math's chain-of-states) or in post-training recipes?
- What causes the near-saturated δrel≈1 middle-layer plateau — is it an artifact of anisotropy/rogue dimensions dominating Euclidean distances, and does it persist under centered or cosine metrics across the full atlas rather than the 50-prompt control?
- Does the final-layer drop toward tree-likeness align with known functional layer boundaries (e.g., where induction heads or prediction-refinement circuits emerge), and does it shift under hyperbolic fine-tuning or mixture-of-curvature methods?
- How does the atlas look for MoE models, larger scales (≥32B), non-Transformer architectures, and multilingual or longer-context prompts — do the "depth dominates, scale is weak" regularities generalize?
- Can GH guide practical interventions, e.g., selecting which layers to prune/quantize, choosing layers for steering vectors, or detecting domain specialization in unlabeled checkpoints?
