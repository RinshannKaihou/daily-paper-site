> **一句话总结：** 阿里 Qwen 团队推出的基于 Qwen MoE 骨干的大模型 ASR 系统 Qwen-Audio-3.0-ASR，用约两千万小时语音经五阶段训练（含 GRPO 强化学习），通过统一的指令控制接口同时支持 30 种语言 + 16 种中文方言、P0/P1 分层热词、单趟转写润色与长音频上下文建模；在 FLEURS-zh 上 CER 2.08%（优于 Tencent Hy-ASR-3.0 的 2.38%），16 方言内部测试集宏平均 CER 9.40%（远低于 Doubao-ASR 的 16.77%），但多语言 FLEURS 宏平均 4.94% 仍落后于 GPT-4o Transcribe 的 3.95%。

> **TL;DR:** Qwen-Audio-3.0-ASR is Alibaba's MoE LLM-based speech recognizer (successor to Fun-ASR), trained on ~20M hours in five stages ending with GRPO reinforcement learning, and controlled at inference purely through instructions (language list, P0/P1 hotwords, polishing flag, transcript history). Under the authors' unified API evaluation it beats Fun-ASR-Flash on FLEURS-en (4.17% vs 4.95% WER, −0.78 pts) and LibriSpeech-clean (1.19% vs 1.35% WER), posts the best macro-average on GigaSpeechBench (22.50% vs 22.76% for Doubao-ASR; GPT-4o Transcribe at 41.89%) and Common Voice 15 (4.57%), and crushes commercial rivals on 16 Chinese dialects (macro CER 9.40% vs 16.77% Doubao-ASR), with P0 hotword recall jumping to 99%+ — but it trails GPT-4o Transcribe (3.95%) and Gemini 3.1 Pro (4.59%) on multilingual FLEURS (4.94%) and gives back ~15% relative error when streaming at 400 ms latency.

## 核心贡献

- **用 Qwen MoE 骨干替换 Fun-ASR 的 7B 稠密解码器**，保留 SenseVoice 音频编码器、两层 Transformer 适配器和轻量 CTC 辅助解码器（用于热词定制与长音频上下文粗对齐），通过稀疏专家路由在保持低单 token 计算成本的同时大幅扩充语言学与世界知识容量。
- **统一指令控制接口**：目标语言列表（30 种语言、16 种中文方言变体，覆盖 8 大方言区）、润色开关、历史转写上下文、P0/P1 分层热词全部作为条件 token 拼接送入解码器，可独立或组合启用，无需为每个市场单独部署检查点（取代了此前 Fun-ASR-ML 的独立多语言模型方案）。
- **单趟原生转写润色**：去填充词、合并口吃重复、消解自我更正、规范化标点与格式在同一解码过程中完成，省去级联 ASR→LLM 改写带来的第二次自回归生成延迟。
- **FunVerl-ASR 全异步强化学习框架**：基于 verl 扩展、Ray 编排四个并发模块（音频处理器、vLLM rollout、任务感知奖励、Megatron 训练器），配合 GRPO 与复合奖励（1−WER、关键词召回、热词命中、上下文实体、幻觉惩罚），在 4 节点 32 张 A100 上一天内完成全部 RL 训练。
- **流式变体与 Message ASR 部署形态**：可配置 chunk 与右上下文长度给出 160–1600 ms 多个延迟工作点，低延迟配置首 token 延迟 < 200 ms；语句结束后全上下文模型刷新临时转写，兼顾实时反馈与最终精度。

## Key Contributions

- Replaces Fun-ASR's 7B dense LLM decoder with a **Qwen Mixture-of-Experts backbone**, retaining the SenseVoice audio encoder, a 2-layer Transformer audio adapter, and a lightweight CTC decoder whose hypothesis drives hotword customization and coarse alignment for long-audio context injection — sparse expert routing buys linguistic/world-knowledge capacity at low per-token compute.
- A **unified instruction-following interface**: target-language list (30 languages + 16 Chinese dialect varieties across 8 dialect regions), a binary polishing flag, historical transcript context, and hierarchical P0/P1 hotwords are concatenated as conditioning tokens and can be enabled independently or composed per request, eliminating the per-market checkpoint approach of Fun-ASR-ML.
- **Native single-pass transcription polishing**: filler removal, repetition collapse, self-correction resolution, and punctuation/formatting normalization happen inside the same decoding pass, removing the cascaded ASR-then-LLM rewrite stage and its latency.
- **FunVerl-ASR**, a fully asynchronous RL framework for large audio-language models: Ray-orchestrated audio processor, vLLM rollout engine (frozen encoder inside), task-aware reward module with per-sample reward routing, and a Megatron policy trainer with NCCL weight sync; GRPO with composite rewards (1−WER, keyword recall, hotword hit with asymmetric gamma, context entity match, hallucination penalty) completes full RL training in one day on 32 A100 GPUs.
- A **hybrid streaming/full-context deployment** (Qwen-Audio-3.0-ASR-Streaming + Message ASR profile): configurable chunk size and right context yield 160–1600 ms latency operating points with sub-200 ms first-token latency, followed by an utterance-end full-context refresh.

## Methodology

The audio encoder is pretrained in two steps: BEST-RQ self-supervised pretraining on tens of millions of hours of unlabeled speech (domains spanning AI, biotech, e-commerce, education, entertainment, finance, mobility), with compatible Transformer layers initialized from a pretrained Qwen3 LLM and causal attention swapped for bidirectional; then supervised AED training on labeled (mainly Chinese/English) data whose pseudo-labels come from Paraformer-V2, Whisper, and SenseVoice plus inverse text normalization. The AED decoder is then discarded.

Training proceeds in five stages: (1) encoder pretraining as above; (2) Audio LLM pretraining on ~20M hours of multilingual ASR and interleaved text-audio data with the encoder frozen, aligning the MoE decoder to acoustic representations via cross-entropy; (3) a cooldown stage on ~1M hours of higher-quality, language-rebalanced data with audio understanding, diarization, and SpeechQA tasks added, filtered by VibeVoice re-recognition (samples with error rate > 0.3 removed); (4) SFT where the encoder and adapter are jointly updated while the LLM stays frozen and is adapted via LoRA, on tens of millions of hours across 30 languages and 16 dialects with unified instruction-formatted examples — including LLM-generated + CosyVoice3-TTS domain-entity augmentation, LLM-regenerated polishing targets with fidelity filtering, historical-context examples mixing correct/conflicting/noisy/empty contexts, and LLM-tiered P0/P1 hotword conditioning (sequence packing to 8,192 tokens); (5) GRPO-based RL on a compact hard-case dataset (base-model disagreements vs Whisper/FireRed-ASR/SenseVoice, >20 s utterances, hallucination samples, keyword samples, plus regular data against catastrophic forgetting) using the FunVerl-ASR asynchronous framework — 8 hypotheses per prompt, mini-batches of 32 prompts per update, a staleness threshold bounding the off-policy gap, and per-sample reward routing so one run jointly optimizes general, dialect, hotword, and context scenarios. When WER is the reward, GRPO reduces to a form close to classic MWER training.

At inference, all controls arrive as conditioning tokens (language instruction, polishing ON/OFF, transcript history, P0/P1 hotwords) concatenated with audio tokens for the MoE decoder; the streaming variant processes short chunks with limited right context and refreshes the final transcript with the full-context model after utterance-end detection.

## Results & Highlights

- **Public Chinese/English benchmarks (unified API pipeline, Table 1 Panel B):** lowest error on 4 of 7 test sets — FLEURS-en WER 4.17% vs 4.95% for Fun-ASR-Flash (−0.78 pts, 15.8% relative), LibriSpeech-clean 1.19% vs 1.35%, LibriSpeech-other 2.24% vs 2.51%, and FLEURS-zh CER 2.08% vs 2.38% for Tencent Hy-ASR-3.0-preview; on AISHELL-2 it is within 0.09 pts of Fun-ASR-Flash (2.02% vs 1.93%).
- **Officially reported results (Panel A):** lowest on 4 of 7 sets — AISHELL-2 CER 2.02% vs 2.10% for Step-Audio (−0.08 pts), FLEURS-zh 2.08% vs 2.68% (−0.60 pts, 22.4% relative), LibriSpeech-other 2.24% vs 2.42% for Kimi-Audio, WeNetSpeech-net 4.31% vs 4.67%; however on AISHELL-1 its 1.03% CER clearly trails FireRed-ASR's 0.54% and Fun-ASR-Flash's 0.76%.
- **Multilingual (Table 2):** best macro-average on GigaSpeechBench (7 East/SE-Asian languages) at 22.50% vs 22.76% for Doubao-ASR, 27.69% for Gemini 3.1 Pro, and 41.89% for GPT-4o Transcribe, winning 5 of 7 languages; best macro on Common Voice 15 at 4.57% vs 5.01% (Azure), 5.36% (Doubao-ASR), winning 6 of 7 languages; but on FLEURS multilingual the macro 4.94% loses to GPT-4o Transcribe (3.95%) and Gemini 3.1 Pro (4.59%), with Filipino 11.48% vs 7.42% and Malay 8.79% vs 4.21% for GPT-4o Transcribe.
- **Chinese dialects (internal 16-dialect suite, Figure 6):** macro CER 9.40% vs 16.77% for Doubao-ASR (−7.37 pts) and 20.40% for Tencent Hy-ASR-3.0-preview (−11.00 pts), lowest on 11 of 16 dialects; on Wenzhou it reaches 14.22% vs 64.63%/64.66% for the two commercial baselines.
- **Hierarchical hotword customization (Table 3):** with conditioning, P0 recall jumps to 99.43% for person names (from 62.12% unconditioned; Fun-ASR-Flash reaches only 95.26%, Doubao-ASR 82.64%), 99.46% for trending buzzwords (from 63.08%), 99.39% for AI entities (from 88.22%), and 99.07% for product entities (from 72.87%); P1-tier gains are visibly smaller (e.g., subject terms P1 88.46%→95.31%), consistent with the weaker conditioning weight.
- **Native polishing (Figure 9, internal 5-point scale):** readability rises from 2.53 (raw) to 3.44 in a single pass, essentially matching the two-call cascade (raw ASR + Qwen3.6-Plus rewrite, 3.51) with faithfulness 3.44 vs 3.47 — while halving the number of autoregressive passes.
- **Streaming latency-accuracy (Figure 11, internal industrial test sets, H100):** at the 400 ms algorithmic-latency operating point, Chinese CER 9.24% and English WER 13.38%, vs 8.03%/11.78% non-streaming (~15.1%/13.6% relative error increase); the low-latency configuration achieves < 200 ms first-token and < 300 ms text-display latency; Chinese CER improves monotonically from 10.34% (240 ms) to 8.60% (1600 ms).
- **Industry-domain entities (Figure 8, internal 15-domain set):** highest entity recall in all 15 domains vs Doubao-ASR and Tencent Hy-ASR-3.0-preview (e.g., AI 98.5%, Software & IT 95.5%), with the largest margins in Software & IT and its low-frequency framework/API names.
- **Training efficiency:** full RL training completes within one day on a 4-node, 32-A100 cluster (half rollout, half training).

## Authors & Credibility

The report is authored by the 45-member Qwen-Audio ASR Team at Alibaba Token Foundry (listed alphabetically by last name), the same organizational lineage as Fun-ASR (arXiv:2509.12508), FunAudioLLM/SenseVoice, Paraformer-V2, and CosyVoice — a group with a strong, verifiable publication and open-source track record in speech. The report is a v2 (updated within two days of posting) and is unusually forthcoming methodologically: it separates officially reported baseline numbers (Panel A) from its own unified API evaluation (Panel B), explicitly warns that Panel-A sources differ in preprocessing and scoring conventions, discloses that its WeNetSpeech-net numbers use corrected reference transcripts (WenetSpeech issue #63) while Panel-A competitors' do not, and openly notes where the model loses (AISHELL-1, multilingual FLEURS). Evaluation against GPT-4o Transcribe, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Azure, Doubao-ASR, and Tencent Hy-ASR-3.0-preview gives broad commercial coverage. Credibility caveats are typical of industrial tech reports: no parameter counts, no architecture hyperparameters, no data breakdown, and internal (non-public) test sets for the dialect, entity, hotword, polishing, and streaming experiments.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** This is systems engineering and integration, not new science. BEST-RQ pretraining, LLM-layer cross-modal initialization, GRPO (from DeepSeekMath), contextual biasing, and MWER-style training are all established; the genuine novelty is the *composition* — one instruction-controlled interface unifying language selection, P0/P1 hotword tiers, single-pass polishing, and history conditioning, plus the asynchronous FunVerl-ASR RL framework (verl extended with audio-aware pipelines and decoupled rollout/training). That said, the P0/P1 tiering with an LLM doing semantic tier assignment is a neat, genuinely new packaging of hotword biasing.
- **Rigor:** Mixed but better than average. The dual-panel benchmark methodology and the explicit caveats about scoring conventions are commendable. However, there are **no ablations anywhere**: no MoE-vs-dense decoder comparison, no SFT-only vs SFT+RL isolation, no P0/P1 vs single-tier hotword test, no measurement of how much the entity-recall gains come from the entity-mining pipeline versus everything else — the authors themselves concede on entity recall that "other improvements introduced in Qwen-Audio-3.0-ASR may also contribute to the overall performance gains."
- **Reproducibility:** Low. Total and activated parameter counts, expert counts, layer dimensions, learning rates, and the data composition of the "tens of millions of hours" are all undisclosed; the dialect, entity-recall, hotword, polishing, and streaming results come from internal test sets that cannot be independently verified.
- **Significance:** High for practitioners — dialect macro-CER dropping from 16.77% to 9.40% and Wenzhou from ~64.6% to 14.22% is a production-grade leap, and 99%+ P0 hotword recall with a single model is the kind of capability that decides real deployments.
- **Red flags:** (1) The abstract's "strong performance relative to GPT-4o Transcribe and Gemini 3.1 Pro" overstates the multilingual picture — on multilingual FLEURS the model *loses* to both (4.94% vs 3.95%/4.59%), with specific weak spots on Filipino, Malay, Thai, and Vietnamese; "highly competitive" is doing heavy lifting. (2) Dialect consistency is graded by the team's own Qwen3.7-Max LLM judge — a self-family evaluator with acknowledged self-preference risks. (3) WeNetSpeech-net comparisons mix corrected references (theirs) with original references (Panel-A competitors), which they flag but which still flatters the headline number. (4) The unexplained 2x AISHELL-1 gap vs FireRed-ASR (1.03% vs 0.54%) is never discussed. (5) Streaming is measured on an H100; the sub-200 ms first-token figure is an algorithmic/theoretical latency, not end-to-end production latency.

## Strengths

- One model + one instruction interface genuinely replaces per-market checkpoints, cascaded rewrite stages, and separately maintained hotword machinery — a real architectural simplification with quantified payoff (polishing at cascade-level quality in one pass: 3.44 vs 3.51 readability).
- The dialect results (macro CER 9.40% vs 16.77%/20.40%; Wenzhou 14.22% vs ~64.6%) are dramatic and address a market Chinese commercial rivals visibly neglect.
- The hierarchical P0/P1 hotword design shows the intended asymmetry empirically (P0 recall ~99%+, P1 gains deliberately smaller), and training includes adversarial contexts (conflicting/noisy/empty history) so the model learns not to blindly copy prompt content — audio remains the primary evidence.
- FunVerl-ASR is a credible engineering contribution in its own right: fully asynchronous rollout/training with a staleness bound, completing RL in one day on 32 A100s.
- Honest evaluation hygiene in places: dual panels, explicit scoring-convention caveats, and openly reported losses (AISHELL-1, Vietnamese/Filipino/Malay) rather than silent omission.

## Limitations

- No model-size, expert-configuration, or training-hyperparameter disclosure; no public code, model release plan, or data documentation — the report cannot be reproduced or independently audited.
- Zero ablation studies, so the contribution of each component (MoE decoder, RL stage, P0/P1 tiering, entity-mining pipeline, long-context conditioning) is unquantified; long-audio context is supported only by qualitative examples (Table 4), with no numeric error-reduction figure.
- Multilingual coverage is uneven: behind GPT-4o Transcribe and Gemini 3.1 Pro on multilingual FLEURS, and notably weak on Filipino, Malay, Thai, and Vietnamese across benchmarks.
- The headline dialect, entity-recall, hotword, polishing, and streaming experiments all rest on internal, non-public test sets; dialect consistency is scored by the authors' own LLM judge.
- Streaming costs ~13.6–15.1% relative error versus non-streaming at 400 ms, and latency figures are algorithmic (H100) rather than end-to-end production numbers.

## 相关性

这是 Qwen 官方技术报告，直接命中「主要 AI 公司（含 Qwen）模型系统卡与技术报告」这一关注方向；其五阶段训练流水线、FunVerl-ASR 大规模异步 RL 基础设施（Ray/vLLM/Megatron、32×A100 集群一天完成 RL）也对大规模训练与推理基础设施方向有参考价值，但核心内容是 ASR 系统工程而非可靠性研究。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- What are the total and activated parameter counts of the MoE decoder, and how much of the gain over Fun-ASR's 7B dense decoder comes from sparsity/MoE versus simply more training data and the RL stage (no ablation is given)?
- How often does P0/P1 hotword or history conditioning induce false insertions when the prompt conflicts with the audio — the training data includes adversarial contexts, but no false-positive rate is reported on the evaluation side?
- Can the 16-dialect results be reproduced on a public dialect benchmark, and does the Qwen3.7-Max LLM judge's dialect-consistency scoring survive replacement by an independent evaluator (given known LLM self-preference bias)?
- What explains the AISHELL-1 gap (1.03% vs FireRed-ASR's 0.54%) — decoding/instruction overhead, normalization, or a real regression on read Mandarin?
- How do the sub-200 ms first-token and 160–1600 ms operating points translate to end-to-end production latency on commodity (non-H100) hardware and under real network jitter?
- Does the single-pass polishing behavior ever violate verbatim-transcription requirements in legal/medical settings, and is there a measured word-error inflation when polishing is ON versus OFF?
