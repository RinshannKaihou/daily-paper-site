Methodology, distilled

FORMALIZATION → BENCH → 11-AGENT BUILDER

Benchmark writing becomes a formal construction problem: every task must ship a complete episode contract — instruction, versioned source bundle, resettable initial state, goal predicates, ToolGraph, deterministic runtime-event program, hidden verifier, permissions.

An episode counts as strictly qualified only if it passes a conjunction of hard gates:

traceable provenancedeterministic replay mutation-tested verifiercapability necessity non-duplication

FinCUABuildBench preregisters 576 slots: 24 financial workflow categories × 8 WorkflowCores × 3 runtime-dynamic profiles (state update · tool failure · policy change). FinCUABuildAgent fills them via typed-handoff specialists with generate–audit–repair loops that roll failures back to the responsible stage — the oracle commits hidden goal states before seeing any candidate solution.

Strengths what holds up

  • Reframes benchmark coverage as a construction-capability problem; preregistered slots + strict conjunctive gates make cross-method comparison meaningful.
  • Leakage-prevention design worth copying: oracle commitment before reference execution, ToolGraph spec separated from environment materialization, typed handoffs with per-stage rollback.
  • Downstream evaluation is genuinely diagnostic — semantic automation-point DAGs surface complementary failure modes across 17 agent configurations.
  • Honest about limits: held-out transfer (13.3%) and yield gaps reported and disclaimed.

Limitations what to watch

  • Absolute yield stays low (31.3% / 41.9% best; 13.3% held-out) — human-in-the-loop filtering still needed; per-task cost unreported.
  • Strongest agentic baselines ran only on MiniMax-M3 with underdocumented adaptation — headline gap may partly reflect scaffolding depth.
  • Strict qualification depends on an LLM-as-judge hard gate (L-Acc) with unexamined judge sensitivity.
  • Only three runtime-dynamic profiles; P3 policy change weakest (6.7% held-out Qual); novel workflow categories untested.
  • Non-nested Static/Dynamic point sets muddy row-wise comparison; enterprise realism rests on synthetic fixtures.

Questions for further exploration

Marginal cost per strictly-qualified task (tokens, API calls, wall-clock) vs Finch’s 700+ expert hours at matched coverage?
How sensitive is the 31.3% vs 1.3–8.3% gap to the L-Acc judge — swapping, ensembling, or replacing it with execution-only gates?
Why does P3 policy change collapse to 6.7% held-out Qual while V-Acc stays near 96% — replayability or capability-essential solutions?
Does the SingleController’s 0.997 / 0.258 split reflect gold-information leakage or exploitable regularities in generated tasks?
Can yield pass 50% with a dedicated policy-change composer, or by fine-tuning a smaller backbone on repair traces?
Do downstream rankings on the 144 generated tasks correlate with human-curated benchmarks (Finch, SpreadsheetBench)?
