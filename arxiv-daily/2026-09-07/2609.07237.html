CEDAR: Error-Bounded Residual Routing for Efficient Long-Context Attention  ↗

Siyu Li, Dong Wang, Jie Zhou, Wei Li, et al.  Shanghai University · East China Normal University · Jiangsu Ocean University

Key metric

0.0RULER

at 6.25% exact density (frozen 4-bit Llama-3.1-8B) — vs 79.3 hard-routing DHSA and 89.3 dense FA2, while keeping a 3.02× kernel speedup at 128K context.

Why this matters

Sparse attention with an explicit error contract: approximation stays bounded while kernels run 3× faster — a concrete recipe for reliability under aggressive operator optimization.

Critical edge

The strongest evidence — 98.5–99.7% error reduction — comes from a synthetic clustered generator; the LLM results rest on one 4-bit model, no error bars, and no SPLA or Quest comparison.

Methodology

How CEDAR works

A post-hoc prefill method for frozen LLMs, built directly on DHSA’s content-aware semantic chunking — no backbone changes, no retraining. Two moves fix hard routing’s failure modes:

  1. Residual summary path. Each omitted chunk joins the softmax as a mean key/value pair computed from FP32 prefix sums, with a +log nc length correction so long chunks aren’t underweighted. Refined chunks contribute only exact tokens, omitted chunks only summaries — one softmax, no double counting, degrading cleanly to dense or chunk attention at the extremes.
  2. Error-aware refinement. Proposition 1 bounds per-query output error by within-chunk key/value dispersion; a priority score (omitted mass × representational badness) greedily promotes chunks to exact until the residual falls below threshold η — a variable per-query budget instead of fixed density.

Ships as a drop-in module after QKV + RoPE (identical tensor shape); fused CUDA/Triton kernel streams both flows into one online-softmax state; two hyperparameters calibrated label-free on 128 held-out prompts.

  • Near-dense at 12.5% density: 89.5 RULER vs 89.3 dense (+0.2); LongBench 37.2 vs 35.7 dense; 2.15× speedup.
  • Controlled study (10 seeds): residual summaries cut reconstruction error 98.5–99.7% vs hard dropping at equal budgets — 0.018 vs 5.294 with 2 exact chunks.
  • Ablation: removing the summary path collapses RULER 87.1 → 79.3 and multiplies reconstruction error ×7.4 — it is the dominant quality driver.
  • Query-adaptive budgets: realized density spans 4.8% (concentrated retrieval) to 9.4% (diffuse multi-hop) under a single threshold.
  • The price: ~13% latency overhead vs DHSA; two summaries per chunk lifts RULER to 87.6 but cuts speedup 3.02× → 2.71×.

Strengths

What holds up

  • Mathematically clean single-softmax hybrid with the log nc length correction; no double counting, degrades gracefully to dense or chunk attention.
  • Refinement driven by a derived per-chunk error bound — and both of its drivers (omitted mass, dispersion) are validated in the ablation.
  • Label-free calibration (α, η tuned only against dense-output reconstruction on 128 held-out prompts) avoids benchmark overfitting.
  • Real systems work: fused online-softmax kernel with bitset/CSR routing and GQA summary sharing; reported latency includes routing and summary construction.
  • Honest scoping — the synthetic study is labeled a mechanism check; incremental decoding is explicitly declared a non-claim.

Limitations

What doesn’t

  • One model (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct), one quantization (4-bit), one GPU, batch size 1 — no scaling evidence across any axis.
  • Prefill-only in practice: the incremental-decoding extension is sketched but not implemented, so long-generation workloads are uncovered.
  • Quality is bought with speed: 3.02× vs DHSA’s 3.42× (~13% overhead); peak memory also slightly regresses (11.5 vs 11.2 GB).
  • LongBench differences among all methods (32.8–37.2) are small with no error bars reported.
  • Bound tightness on real LLM activations is asserted, not measured — the safety multiplier α = 2.0 is hand-calibrated.
  • Summary scanning stays O(Lm) per query token; the proposed O(L log m) chunk tree is unimplemented.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does 4-bit / FP8 quantization inflate the dispersion-based router’s estimates, causing systematic over-refinement?
  2. Can completed-chunk summaries stay consistent during incremental decoding, and at what quality/speed trade-off vs Quest- or SnapKV-style selection?
  3. How tight is Proposition 1 on real activations — is α = 2.0 stable across models, domains, and layers?
  4. Would empirical comparisons against SPLA and Quest change the Pareto picture, since both are absent from Table 3?
  5. Can the two-level chunk tree deliver O(L log m) at 256K+ contexts without eroding the 3× speedup — and does the method survive batch > 1 serving?
