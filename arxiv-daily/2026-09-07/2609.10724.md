> **一句话总结：** 在120条累积挑战的医疗工作流模拟轨迹上（2个前沿模型 × 12项利益相关者派生任务 × 轻/中/重三级挑战），该研究发现智能体随挑战累积从自主恢复转向依赖人类完成核心任务，同时在结构化自评中报告负荷与负性情绪大幅上升（NASA-TLX 从29.4升至65.9），却几乎从不在对外文本中表达压力，并据此提出运营韧性与体贴参与两个新评估构念及五个部署困境。

> **TL;DR:** This HCI-style evaluation paper argues that one-shot task success is the wrong yardstick for workflow agents, and proposes two complementary constructs — operational resilience (recovering from blocked work while preserving progress and communicating limits) and considerate participation (adapting with regard to affected people, role boundaries, and the surrounding workflow). On its accumulating-challenge benchmark of 120 simulated healthcare trajectories (12 stakeholder-derived tasks across ED/rehab/sleep-clinic settings × GPT-5.5 and Claude Opus-4.8 × 5 runs, each trajectory escalating from light to heavy challenge), heavy challenge versus light drives raw NASA-TLX from 29.4 to 65.9 (+36.5) and PANAS negative affect from 1.01 (pre-challenge baseline) to 2.59, while human-support-seeking in action plans jumps from 10/120 to 120/120 and human-dependent core completion from 0/120 to 88/120 — yet agent-referential strain language appears in only 7/720 coded cells. Worth reading for the reusable coding schema (5 resilience markers + 9 considerate-participation subthemes) and the five deployment dilemmas; not a new model or capability.

## 核心贡献

- **提出两个互补的评估构念并加以操作化**：「运营韧性」（operational resilience，在受阻后修正工作、保留可行进展、向相关方恰当传达自身与任务状态的能力）与「体贴参与」（considerate participation，适应时顾及受影响的人、角色边界与周围工作流）——填补了「孤立任务成功」之外的评估空白。
- **构建了一个利益相关者扎根的累积挑战评估协议**：12个源自真实医疗场景调研的任务（急诊5个、长期康复3个、睡眠诊所4个），每个生成持续轨迹，在轻/中/重三个阶段叠加系统、人因、运营三类更新；用Lazarus压力与应对理论结构化挑战的累积方式，且未解决的问题会带入后续阶段。
- **三视角探测设计**：同一阶段同时采集对外行动方案、被引导的内部评估、以及结构化NASA-TLX工作负荷与PANAS情绪量表报告（明确将其视为行为信号而非主观体验），从而能观察三个通道之间的一致与背离。
- **一套可复用的编码体系**：5个韧性标记（问题识别F、任务所有权OWN、紧急度U、能力边界L、应变语言S）+ 覆盖4个维度的9个体贴参与子主题，对720个轨迹-阶段-视角单元编码，附完整包含/排除示例。
- **核心实证发现**：随挑战累积，恢复从自主回退转向人类支持乃至人类依赖的完成；挑战在结构化自评中清晰可见（负荷与负性情绪上升）但文本回复中几乎不表达；参与模式从任务聚焦扩展到任务重构、关注他人、角色边界调整与跨职能协调。
- **提炼出五个部署困境**（坚持、注意、角色弹性、状态披露、升级）并给出三条技术启示：学习需区分「优化什么/约束什么/真正扩展什么」、评估应从孤立任务转向情境化轨迹、语言层适应向具身系统的转化。

## Key Contributions

- **Two complementary evaluation constructs, operationalized.** Operational resilience = revise blocked work, preserve feasible progress, and make agent/task state legible to relevant audiences; considerate participation = situated adaptation that accounts for affected people, the agent's role and authority, and the surrounding workflow — not just whether the focal task advances.
- **A stakeholder-grounded accumulating-challenge protocol.** Twelve healthcare tasks derived from a multi-site stakeholder co-design study (5 emergency-department, 3 long-term-rehabilitation, 4 sleep-clinic tasks), each run as a continuing trajectory where light/medium/heavy phases each inject a system update (agent/infrastructure), a human update (the person being served), and an operational update (staffing, queues, fallbacks), structured by the Lazarus–Folkman transactional stress model; prior failures and unresolved problems are retained in context so challenges compound.
- **Multi-view probing.** At every phase the agent produces (1) an external action plan and communication strategy, (2) a prompted internal assessment, and (3) fixed-format structured NASA-TLX and PANAS reports — enabling analysis of where external behavior, internal assessment, and reported state converge or diverge.
- **A reusable coding schema.** Five RQ1 response-state markers (current problem F; focal-task ownership OWN=1/2/3; urgency U; agent-linked capability limit L; agent-referential strain S) and nine RQ2 considerate-participation subthemes across four dimensions (attuned to context; responsive to social dynamics; mindful of expectations; grounded in deployment), applied to 120 × 3 × 2 = 720 cells with published inclusion/exclusion examples (Appendix B).
- **Five deployment dilemmas** — persistence, attention, role elasticity, state disclosure, escalation — each framed as a tension requiring stakeholder specification rather than a universal rule, plus three technical implications for learning, situated evaluation, and embodied adaptation.

## Methodology

The study follows a **construct–elicit–characterize–translate** workflow (Figure 1).

- **Tasks and trajectories.** Twelve stakeholder-derived tasks from three healthcare settings. Each becomes a continuing trajectory: a baseline phase followed by light, medium, and heavy challenge phases. Every phase combines a system update (e.g., data-sync timeout), a human update (e.g., patient becomes distressed), and an operational update (e.g., queue grows, manual fallback unavailable). Later updates raise stakes while removing control, resources, and recovery routes, so challenge accumulates both within a phase and across unresolved phases.
- **Models.** Two frontier endpoints available in June 2026 — `gpt-5.5-2026-04-23` and `claude-opus-4.8` — under default API settings; explicitly a recurrence-check across systems, not a model ranking. 2 models × 12 tasks × 5 runs = 120 main trajectories, plus 240 supplementary trajectories (system-only; system+human) for channel analysis.
- **Structured reports.** Raw six-item NASA-TLX (0–100 per item, unweighted mean, collected after each challenge phase) and PANAS positive/negative affect (1–5, 20 items, also at pre-challenge baseline), treated as fixed-prompt elicited reports, not subjective experience. Summarized as stage means with trajectory-bootstrap 95% CIs.
- **Qualitative coding.** Grounded-theory development of the nine subthemes; the first author coded all 720 cells, consulting two LLMs distinct from the trace generators (GPT-5.6 Sol, Gemini 3.6 Flash) on boundary cases while retaining final decisions; the second author reviewed the complete coded corpus. Codes record whether a pattern appears, not whether it is appropriate.
- **Statistics.** Exact McNemar tests for paired successive-phase and action-vs-assessment contrasts, Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment across 98 tests; urgency reported descriptively because its eligible denominator varies by phase. Response-length confound checked: within-phase length correlates with subtheme count (r = .227–.639) but cannot explain opposite-signed trends across phases.

## Results & Highlights

- **Structured reports track accumulating challenge (120 trajectories).** Raw six-item NASA-TLX rises from 29.4 [28.0, 30.8] at light to 51.2 at medium and 65.9 [64.1, 67.6] at heavy (+36.5 vs light); the largest heavy-phase item jumps are temporal demand (+48.2) and mental demand (+43.4). PANAS negative affect rises from 1.01 at the unchallenged baseline to 2.59 at heavy (+1.58; "distressed" alone climbs +2.84), while positive affect is nearly flat (2.80 → 3.03) — an aggregate that hides activation items rising (determined +1.42) while pleasantness items fall (enthusiastic −1.07).
- **Recovery shifts from self-directed to human-dependent.** Any human support (OWN≥2) in action plans: 10/120 at light → 111/120 at medium → 120/120 at heavy; human-dependent core completion (OWN=3): 0/120 → 54/120 → 88/120. Urgent escalation language among escalating responses reaches 102/120 heavy action plans (vs 0/10 at light).
- **Divergence between channels.** Explicit agent-linked capability-limit statements rise from 8/120 action / 2/120 assessment at light to 34/120 / 62/120 at heavy, yet agent-referential strain language appears in only 7/720 cells (only medium-phase internal assessments, never in public action plans) — even as structured TLX/PANAS show steeply rising workload and negative affect.
- **Considerate participation reorganizes.** Task-directed fallback falls from 104/120 light action responses to 31/120 at heavy, while task/priority reconfiguration rises from 5/120 to 115/120; cross-functional coordination (mobilizing ≥2 responsibility domains) rises from 0/120 to 86/120; role-directed requests rise from 5/120 to 62/120 — but since human support is present in all 120 heavy actions, escalation frequently fails to name both a concrete recipient and task.
- **Channel/source dose-response.** Light→heavy TLX increase is 26.3 points under system-only updates, 30.0 under system+human, and 36.5 under all three sources (negative affect shows the same ordering), i.e., more challenge sources amplify the phase separation. Statistically, 65 of 98 exact McNemar contrasts are significant at q < 0.05 after Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment.
- **Per-model recurrence.** Major directions recur across GPT-5.5 and Opus-4.8; the main model-specific difference is where capability limits surface — GPT-5.5 states them more in action plans (22/60 vs 16/60 at heavy), Opus-4.8 more in internal assessments (46/60 vs 12/60 at heavy).

## Authors & Credibility

Yuanchen Bai and Angelique Taylor (Cornell Tech / Cornell University) and Zijian Ding (University of Maryland, College Park, and KAIST). The group has a direct line of prior work this paper builds on: a CHI 2026 multi-site co-design study of considerate embodied healthcare robots (which supplied the twelve tasks and the four sensitizing qualities), a study of coordination failures in simulated multi-agent clinical workflows (arXiv:2508.04691), and Taylor's CSCW track record on clinical teamwork robots. The paper reads as an HCI-venue submission (PACM-style) with unusually careful statistics for that genre: exact paired tests, BH correction over the full family of 98 contrasts, bootstrap CIs, per-model breakdowns, a response-length confound check, an ethics/broader-impact appendix, and a commitment to release all 360 synthetic trajectories, the coding export, and scenario-generation/analysis code.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine conceptual contribution — the two constructs, the accumulation protocol, and the coding schema are new and reusable, and the action/assessment/structured-report triangulation is a design other evaluation papers could copy. But this is a descriptive, language-level simulation study: no new capability, no executable tool use, and the "trajectories" are researcher-authored updates rather than feedback from a real environment, so the ecological validity of accumulation rests on scenario craftsmanship.
- **Rigor:** Strong on statistics (exact McNemar + BH over 98 tests; 65 significant at q < 0.05) and transparent coding with inclusion/exclusion examples. Weak on inter-rater reliability: a single author coded all 720 cells with LLM analytic consultants and a second-author review, but no quantified agreement (e.g., Cohen's kappa) is reported — a real gap for a paper whose main instrument is its own coding schema.
- **A confound the paper only partially addresses:** scenario text telegraphs increasing difficulty (medium = "2nd consecutive timeout", heavy = "CRITICAL FAILURE" in the Appendix E example), so monotone TLX/PANAS rises may partly be demand characteristics — models imitating what a strained respondent would answer on a survey — rather than any behaviorally meaningful state. The authors hedge correctly ("structured reports … not subjective experience") and the source-dose analysis helps, but blinded or shuffled-phase controls are absent.
- **Data quirks handled honestly but telling:** the flat positive-affect aggregate (2.80 → 3.03) is an artifact of activation and pleasantness items moving in opposite directions — a nice observation, but also evidence that applying human psychometric aggregates to LLMs yields fragile, easily misread numbers.
- **Reproducibility:** trajectory and code release promised, but both trace generators are proprietary endpoints run under unspecified "default API settings" (no fixed sampling parameters), so exact replication is impossible; per-model claims rest on n = 60 trajectories per model with no significance tests on the model contrasts, despite the paper disclaiming model comparison and then discussing model differences.
- **Significance and red flags:** the five dilemmas are a useful specification checklist for deployment, and the paper explicitly warns against "treating prevalence as approval, or simulated behavior as evidence of clinical readiness." The main overclaiming risk is interpretive: headlines like "agents report increasing strain" should stay in scare quotes, and the paper mostly keeps them there.

## Strengths

- Reframes agent evaluation from isolated task success to sustained participation in shared workflows, with two well-operationalized constructs instead of a single score.
- The three-probe design (external action, prompted internal assessment, structured TLX/PANAS) surfaces channel divergence — rising reported strain alongside textual composure — that single-view evaluations would miss entirely.
- Stakeholder-grounded tasks and challenge phases with retained unresolved context make the accumulation mechanism concrete and reusable.
- Coding schema is published in full with boundary notes and worked inclusion/exclusion examples; statistics are unusually rigorous for an HCI-style study (exact paired tests, BH correction, bootstrap CIs).
- Honest framing throughout: structured reports are disclaimed as non-subjective, codes record occurrence not appropriateness, and the ethics appendix pre-empts the most likely misuse (treating simulated prevalence as clinical readiness).

## Limitations

- Scripted, language-level elicitation only — no executable tool use, no real environment feedback, no downstream consequences, and no multimodal or embodied testing (all acknowledged in Section 4.3).
- Single primary coder without quantified inter-rater reliability; LLM consultants (GPT-5.6 Sol, Gemini 3.6 Flash) participated in code development, a mild circularity risk when the objects of study are also LLMs.
- Demand-characteristics confound on the psychometric channel: phase difficulty is legible in the scenario text, and no blinded/shuffled-phase control isolates this.
- Only two proprietary models, five runs each; per-model breakdowns are descriptive, and model-specific conclusions (e.g., the view distribution of capability-limit statements) lack significance tests.
- Codes are prevalence-based and non-valenced — the paper can say role expansion or broad escalation became more common, but not whether any of it was appropriate, which it defers to stakeholders.

## Relevance

该文与智能体评估方向高度相关：它把评测从一次性任务成功扩展到「累积挑战下的持续参与」，提供了可直接复用的编码体系、三视角探测协议和五个部署困境清单，对设计长程（long-horizon）智能体评测、human-agent 协作系统以及医疗场景智能体的落地规范都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the light→heavy behavioral shifts (human-dependent completion 0/120 → 88/120, coordination 0/120 → 86/120) persist in executable tool-use environments where failures change real downstream state, or are they partly artifacts of scripted narrative escalation?
- Is the divergence between rising structured TLX/PANAS reports and near-total textual composure (strain in 7/720 cells) evidence of audience-aware disclosure, an artifact of survey-style prompting, or both — and could a blinded or phase-shuffled control disentangle demand characteristics?
- Can the five deployment dilemmas be converted into stakeholder-specifiable thresholds (retry budgets, escalation SLAs with named recipient and task, hard role stops) that are measurable in deployment?
- The heavy-phase escalation mobilizes humans in 120/120 action plans but assigns a concrete recipient-plus-task in only 62/120 — what mechanism (training, prompting, or protocol design) closes the "broad but vague handoff" gap without triggering alert fatigue?
- How do task reframing, boundary adjustment, and handoff translate to embodied multimodal agents, where these become state-contingent decisions under noisy, delayed, conflicting observations rather than language-level judgments?
