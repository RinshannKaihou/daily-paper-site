Why this matters

Killing the sink and reading the whole window are independent problems — a model card's low first-token attention doesn't prove mid-context gets read.

「消灭 sink」与「均匀读取窗口」是两个独立问题，模型卡上的低首 token 注意力并不能保证上下文中段被真正读到。

Critical edge

No measurement exceeds 768 tokens — three orders of magnitude short of the million in the title; the 1M case rests on cache arithmetic plus a protocol no one has run.

Worse: the hybrid stacks never learned the probe (6.0% recall vs 8.3% blind chance — delta-rule omitted), so the central hybrid-vs-softmax comparison is confounded.

Methodology

One mechanism changed per ladder step

Four ~1M-parameter transformers — identical in width (96), depth (8), heads (4), data, optimizer, and seeds — differ only in how they mix tokens: a softmax baseline; softmax + input-dependent gate (the NeurIPS 2025 mechanism); a 3:1 linear-hybrid with no positional encoding (Kimi K3 in miniature); and that hybrid with block attention residuals.

All train on a laptop CPU (no GPU): 1500 AdamW steps on 96-token sequences from a synthetic key–value retrieval probe — one planted pair at controlled depth, three distractors, two accuracy floors (8.3% blind chance, 25% any-value). Evaluation runs at 96–768 tokens (8× training length), recall pooled over 384 sequences with Wilson intervals.

SinkProbe scores four quantities — sink mass σ, massive activation µ, position-resolved recall p(d), recency gap Δr — each with a 3-seed spread, plus attention entropy to catch sinks that migrate off token one. Caveat the authors flag: the linear stand-in omits Kimi's delta-rule correction.

  • The gated-attention effect did not reproduce at this scale: sink mass moves only 0.316 → 0.261 (−18%, inside seed spread) vs the published 15B result 46.7% → 4.8%. The gate stayed open (score 0.686 after 1500 steps vs 0.116 after trillions of tokens) — reported as inconclusive, not negative.
  • Activations and sinks dissociate — twice: gating halves µ (74.9 → 36.2, −52%) while σ falls just 17%; attention residuals cut µ −60% (97.9 → 38.9) while σ does not fall at all (0.183 → 0.202).
  • Position bias is large, flips sign with length, ignores σ: at 768 tokens recall climbs 20.6% → 35.8% from opening to question (+15.2 pts recency gap); at the 96-token training length the preference reverses (−10.9 pts, primacy).

Strengths

What the paper gets right

  • Ablations by construction — one mechanism per ladder step; seeds, optimizer, data all fixed.
  • A decisive experiment — the objective control shows a 6× effect with a mechanism-level explanation.
  • Pre-registration — thresholds, sample sizes, and a four-outcome interpretation grid fixed in advance; rare in this literature.
  • Honest negatives — gate non-reproduction diagnosed down to the gate score, not vaguely attributed.
  • Two-floor accuracy reporting (chance / any-value) that many long-context evals omit.
  • Laptop-reproducible — CPU-only, seeded synthetic task, released code with property tests.

Limitations

Where the evidence stops

  • 768-token ceiling — three orders of magnitude short of the title's million-token question.
  • Delta rule omitted — hybrid stacks never learned the probe (recall below blind chance); hybrid-vs-softmax rows are confounded.
  • Scale gap — 1M params / 1500 steps cannot speak for 2.8T-param, trillion-token models; gate sparsity emerges over far longer training.
  • Seed variance wins — baseline σ spread (0.089–0.455, a factor of 5) exceeds every architecture effect measured.
  • Not like-for-like — σ averages 2 softmax layers in hybrids vs 8 in dense stacks.
  • Retrieval only — synthetic KV probe; no summarization, multi-hop, or code; cache totals rest on two assumed K3 dimensions.

Questions for Further Exploration

The open ends this paper leaves behind

  1. Will the pre-registered protocol, run on released Kimi K3 weights at 4K–1M tokens, land in the "sink small, recency gap large" cell its pilot points to?
  2. Does objective-makes-the-sink hold at frontier scale — or is per-position loss necessary for general LM quality?
  3. With the delta rule added, does the 3:1 hybrid actually cut sink mass — or was even the −42% pilot drop an artifact of the task never being learned?
  4. Why does position preference flip from primacy to recency at 8× extrapolation — an out-of-distribution fallback to nearest-context copying?
  5. Does fixed-state capacity saturation, not attention sinks, become the binding constraint at 1M tokens?
  6. Do massive activations and sink mass stay dissociated under other remedies (SoftPick, sparse attention), and does entropy catch sinks that migrate off position zero?
