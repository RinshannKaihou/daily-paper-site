Key result — identity, not a benchmark

+1

Pointwise for every Haar rotation U, at any finite d. No quantitative results reported — no experiments or benchmarks; the evidence is exact statements.

Why this matters

Random rotations now anchor LLM quantization (QuIP, QuaRot, TurboQuant) and federated gradient compression (EDEN, DRIVE); this note grounds their scaling choices classically.

Critical edge

No rate result: the Jensen gap's size at practical block dimensions (d = 512–4096) is left completely unquantified — the large-d argument is heuristic.

01The dictionary — EDEN speaks CDEF

A self-contained three-part exposition: classical CDEF recap, a disentangling of the two historical roles of random rotations, then the main correspondence — exact for every rotation realization, not just in expectation.

Role 1 · Gaussianization — EDEN's use

A Haar rotation turns an arbitrary vector's coordinates approximately i.i.d. Gaussian, so Gaussian-optimized scalar quantizers apply to any source.

vs

Role 2 · Error decorrelation — Hadad & Erez 2016

Independent rotations across branches act as a dither: quantization errors decorrelate, so averaging m descriptions cuts noise power to PN/m.

02Evidence — five exact statements

The note proves; it does not measure. Its entire evidentiary content is the following mathematics.

Identity · pointwise, every finite d

1/DW(U) = 1/DU(U) + 1

where DU(U) = A(U) − 1, DW(U) = 1 − 1/A(U), and A(U) = ‖X‖²‖Y‖²/⟨X,Y⟩². The classical CDEF “+1” relation holds as an exact geometric identity per rotation realization, at finite blocklength.

Breaks under averaging · Jensen

SNRMMSE ≥ SNRMMSE,U + 1

Averaged SNRs are reciprocals of averaged distortions; concavity of t/(1+t) yields only an inequality — strict at finite d. Equality dies exactly at the averaging step.

Asymptotic recovery · d → ∞

SNRMMSE − SNRMMSE,U → 1

A(U) concentrates to b/a² with a = E[GQ(G)], b = E[Q(G)²], G ∼ N(0,1) — Haar-rotated coordinates become i.i.d. Gaussian, and the Jensen gap vanishes.

Conditional unbiasedness · every finite d

EU[Z] = X

EDEN's Haar formulation with realization-dependent scaling is strictly stronger than CDEF's second-order notion — orthogonality X ⊥ N does not imply E[N|X] = 0.

Averaging gain · forward channel

SNRMMSE,U,ave = m · SNRMMSE,U

Averaging m independently quantized descriptions with uncorrelated, source-orthogonal noises reduces noise power to PN/m.

⚠ No experiments, simulations, or numerical benchmarks are reported — the note's evidence consists entirely of the exact statements above.

[+]Strengths

  • Bridges a 30-year-old classical result (CDEF 1995) to today's LLM-quantization and federated-learning pipelines — self-contained, standard signal-processing background only.
  • The two-roles taxonomy (Gaussianization vs. dither) disentangles notions the modern rotation-quantization literature routinely conflates.
  • The pointwise-vs-averaged subtlety — the identity survives per realization but dies at the averaging step — is isolated cleanly by a one-line Jensen argument.
  • Epistemic hygiene: separates second-order from conditional unbiasedness, and frames itself honestly as a note claiming only what is proved.

[−]Limitations

  • No numerics of any kind: the Jensen gap is never computed at practical block sizes (d = 512–4096), and no concentration rate in d is given.
  • The reconstruction scale αU is assumed transmitted error-free; the impact of quantizing it — inevitable in any deployed system — is unanalyzed.
  • Scalar-Gaussian focus: nothing on vector quantizers, overload regions, or the incoherence processing used in QuIP/QuaRot-style pipelines.
  • Offers no new algorithm and no measurable performance gain — a conceptual and attributional contribution, in a very short format that leaves threads dangling.

04Open questions for further exploration

  1. How large is the Jensen gap at d = 512–4096 — can a non-asymptotic bound be derived (A(U) = b/a² + O(1/√d) w.h.p., or O(1/d) on the gap itself)?
  2. What survives when the scale αU must itself be quantized — does exact unbiasedness degrade gracefully, and what is the optimal scale/shape bit split?
  3. Do the correspondences extend to randomized Hadamard transforms, the computationally cheap variant, where Haar symmetry arguments are unavailable?
  4. Can the two-roles taxonomy become a quantitative design rule for splitting rotation resources between Gaussianization and cross-branch decorrelation?
  5. Does the pointwise Pythagorean identity imply anything actionable for multi-description or incremental-refinement coding beyond the classical m-fold gain?
