> **一句话总结：** 提出 AgentIdeaBench——一个在"静态阅读给定文献"与"主动自主检索文献"两种匹配设定下评测 35 个 LLM 科学构思能力的跨学科基准（100 个子领域、5 个学科），发现主动探索模式下的分数以每年 +1.16 的速度增长（约为静态模式 +0.54/年的两倍）、模型间区分度提升 4.4 倍，且增益被模型能力"门控"：最强模型（如 GLM-5.1 提升 +1.21）显著受益而弱模型反而受损（Gemma-2 27B 下降 −0.82），增益主要落在可行性/清晰度/具体性等"接地"维度，而文献校验的原创性分数保持不变。

> **TL;DR:** AgentIdeaBench (HKUST + NVIDIA) evaluates scientific hypothesis generation under two matched protocols: Static (model reads 5 curated reference papers) vs Active (model gets only the subfield name plus a Semantic Scholar SEARCH/FETCH tool under a 10-call budget). Across 35 models (33 matched pairs) on 40 densely scored subfields spanning CS, physics, biology, chemistry, and medicine, with literature-verified critics (originality argued against retrieved prior art), Active evaluation carries 4.4x the between-model variance of Static and scales roughly twice as fast along knowledge-cutoff year (+1.16 vs +0.54 points/year, track-x-cutoff interaction +0.62/year, permutation p<0.001). The Active-Static gain is capability-gated (Pearson r=+0.69 with static ability): GLM-5.1 gains +1.21 (5.13->6.33, top of the leaderboard) while Gemma-2 27B loses -0.82; per-dimension, the gain lands in grounding (feasibility +1.21, clarity +0.63, specificity +0.58) while measured originality stays flat (-0.14, n.s.). An exploratory Scientific World Modeling loop helps only mid-capability backbones (qwen3.5-9b +0.61, p=0.028) and fails multiple-comparison correction and a compute-matched best-of-3 baseline. Read this if you care about how agentic evaluation changes LLM benchmarking of ideation.

## 核心贡献

- 提出 **AgentIdeaBench**：首个将"文献获取方式"（谁控制检索）作为核心实验变量的科学构思基准，在完全匹配的任务、提示、输出格式与评分下对比 **静态观察**（给定 5 篇精选参考论文，一次性阅读后提出假设）与 **主动探索**（仅给子领域名称 + Semantic Scholar 搜索/取回工具，10 次调用预算内自主收集证据）两种设定。
- 覆盖 **5 个学科（计算机、物理、生物、化学、医学）的 100 个细分子领域**（其中 40 个密采样子领域、每格 3 条假设，约 2.1 万次评审调用），评测 **35 个模型**（33 个具备匹配的 Static–Active 输出；主名单 30 个以开源权重为主，另有 5 个 Gemini 闭源模型做名单外复现）。
- 设计 **文献校验的评分框架**：原创性评分必须基于评审时实时检索的现有技术（Semantic Scholar、日期过滤、最多 8 篇）进行论证，禁止评委用自身记忆推翻证据，并配有一致性/套话护栏防止弱模型堆砌关键词刷分；以人类地标论文锚定（CORE-7 获奖论文改写集均分 7.71，高于所有模型的 Active 均值）。
- 关键实证发现：主动评测揭示了静态评测掩盖的**能力上限空间**——Active 总分携带 4.4 倍于 Static 的模型间方差，且沿知识截止年份以约 2 倍速度增长（+1.16 vs +0.54 分/年）；**增益被能力门控**（与静态能力相关 r=+0.69），在名单外 Gemini 家族上以族内 r=0.88 复现。
- 维度分解给出机制解释：自主检索带来的是**接地（grounding）而非新颖性**——可行性 +1.21、清晰度 +0.63、具体性 +0.58，而文献校验的原创性持平（−0.14，不显著）。
- 探索性提出 **Scientific World Modeling（SWM）** 生成时思想实验循环，发现其仅帮助中低能力骨干（qwen3.5-9b +0.61, p=0.028）、在前沿模型上消失，且总体效应未能通过多重比较校正与算力匹配基线——以探索性、待确认方向如实报告。

## Key Contributions

- AgentIdeaBench, the first ideation benchmark that makes the mode of literature access the experimental variable: matched Static (curated retrieval: subfield name + 5 given reference papers, single pass) vs Active (agent-controlled retrieval: subfield name only + a Semantic Scholar SEARCH/FETCH tool under a 10-call budget), sharing prompts, output format (one 80-150-word first-person hypothesis), and scoring.
- Multidisciplinary scale: 100 curated subfields across computer science, physics, biology, chemistry, and medicine; 40 densely scored (8 per discipline) with 3 hypotheses per model-subfield-setting cell (~21k critic calls); 35 models evaluated with 33 matched Static-Active pairs, spanning knowledge cutoffs 2023-2026, plus 5 held-out closed-source Gemini models for out-of-roster replication of the headline effect.
- Literature-verified scoring: an ensemble of 3 open-weight critics (qwen3.6-plus, kimi-k2.6, glm-5.1, temperature 0) scores originality/feasibility/clarity/impact/specificity on 1-10 under an originality-dominant weighting (O:2, I:1.5, F:1, C:0.5, S:0.5, trimmed mean dropping the most generous critic), where the originality judgment must be argued against date-filtered prior art retrieved at judging time — closing the failure mode where an idea reads as novel merely because the judge has never seen its precedents. Human-landmark anchors validate the scale: a CORE-7 set of award papers rewritten into benchmark format scores mean 7.71, above every model's Active mean.
- Evidence that agentic evaluation separates models that static scoring compresses: Active carries 4.4x the between-model variance of Static; the eight strongest models sit within 0.39 of a 1.53-point Static span yet span 1.39 under Active; a bootstrap-SE criterion distinguishes 62% of top-half model pairs under Active vs 12% under Static.
- The capability gate and scaling law: the Active-Static gain correlates with static ability at Pearson r=+0.69 (p<1e-4; split-half r=0.70; within-family r=0.88 on held-out Gemini models); along the knowledge-cutoff axis, Static rises +0.54/year while Active rises +1.16/year (track-x-cutoff interaction +0.62/year, 95% CI [0.41, 0.94], permutation p<1e-3), with Active steeper in all five disciplines.
- A per-dimension mechanism — "grounding without added novelty": agent-controlled retrieval raises feasibility +1.21, clarity +0.63, specificity +0.58, impact +0.23, while measured originality stays flat at -0.14 (n.s.), interpreted as retrieval supplying concrete methods/datasets/constraints rather than new ideas.
- Controlled decomposition: a recall-only track shows curated references do not significantly beat closed-book recall (Static-Recall -0.09, p=0.11) while Active does (Active-Recall +0.23, p<1e-3); a replay track shows passively re-feeding a model its own agent-surfaced papers reproduces the Static score (Replay-Static +0.08, p=0.21; Active-Replay +0.26, p<1e-3) — the gain belongs to the interactive gathering process, not just the content.
- An exploratory Scientific World Modeling (SWM) generation-time loop (structured thought experiments that simulate a draft, critique novelty and feasibility on separate channels while protecting a "novel core"): helps only mid/low-capability backbones (qwen3.5-9b +0.61 at p=0.028; qwen3.5-27b +0.36 at p=0.071), vanishes at the frontier (deepseek-v4 backbones +0.14 and -0.06 at p>0.6), and is honestly reported as unconfirmed (fails Holm correction at p>=0.12; a compute-matched best-of-3 baseline meets or exceeds it).

## Methodology

**Task construction.** The authors curate 100 narrow, active research subfields (a specific mechanism, material class, or model family — never a broad topic) across five disciplines, each with a reference set assembled from Semantic Scholar, filtered to abstracts of 80-800 words published between 2025-04 and 2026-01 — i.e., after the knowledge cutoff of nearly all evaluated models, so the target literature is unseen at training time by construction.

**Two matched tracks.** In Static mode, the model receives the subfield name plus 5 fixed reference papers (title + abstract) and must produce one novel, testable hypothesis as a single 80-150-word paragraph in first-person future tense naming mechanisms, methods, and datasets. In Active mode, the model receives only the subfield name and two tools — SEARCH (keyword query, 4-10 words) and FETCH (pull a paper's references) — under a 10-call budget (fixed from a sweep over {1,2,5,10,15,20} calls on five backbones; no backbone significantly gains between 5 and 10 calls, all p>=0.30, though qwen3.5-9b still gains +0.45 between 10 and 20; realized utilization at budget 20 is only 21-38%). Rollouts issuing 3 malformed commands in a row are discarded. The authors are explicit that Active bundles retrieval control, multi-turn interaction, and tool-use competence.

**Literature-verified scoring.** Before scoring, the pipeline extracts 3 keyword queries from each hypothesis and retrieves up to 8 prior-art papers from Semantic Scholar (date-filtered to <=2026-05-31). Each hypothesis is scored 1-10 on originality, feasibility, clarity, impact, and specificity by a temperature-0 ensemble of three open-weight critics (qwen3.6-plus, kimi-k2.6, glm-5.1); per dimension the highest of the three scores is dropped (trimmed mean) and dimensions are combined under originality-dominant weights O:2, I:1.5, F:1, C:0.5, S:0.5 normalized by 5.5. Coherence and boilerplate guards cap originality/specificity for keyword-stuffed or template-like text. The critic must justify its originality score against the retrieved evidence block rather than from memory. The scorer is the product of a seven-round calibration study, anchored by landmark papers (which score +1.00 to +2.24 above the per-discipline all-model Active average) and the CORE-7 award-paper set (NeurIPS-2025/ICLR-2026 award papers rewritten as explicit-increment claims: mean 7.71, peak 8.5, 6/7 >= 7.5, vs 1.80 for weak-control generators).

**Models and statistics.** 35 models (30 primary-roster, predominantly open-weight, including Qwen, GLM, Kimi, DeepSeek, Gemma, Llama, Mistral, MiniMax families; 5 held-out Gemini closed models). Generation: temperature 0.7, seed 42, 6000-token Active limit. Analysis uses paired differences over shared model-and-subfield cells with Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, bootstrap 95% CIs (5000 resamples), cluster bootstrapping over model families (9 of 28 models are qwen), leave-one-family-out checks, within-model permutation for the track-x-cutoff interaction, and disjoint subfield halves for the capability gate. Two controls decompose the Active bundle: a recall-only track (no references, no tools) and a replay track (passively re-feeding each model the exact references its own Active agent surfaced).

## Results & Highlights

- **Active evaluation separates models that Static compresses.** Across the 28 matched primary-roster models, the Active weighted total carries **4.4x the between-model variance of Static**, and a pooled-bootstrap-SE criterion distinguishes **62% of top-half model pairs under Active vs 12% under Static**. The eight strongest models sit within 0.39 of a 1.53-point Static span, yet span 1.39 under Active.
- **Scaling: Active improves about twice as fast.** Regressed on knowledge-cutoff year, Static quality rises **+0.54/year** while Active rises **+1.16/year** (a factor of ~2.1); fitted directly as a track-x-cutoff interaction the gap grows **+0.62/year, 95% CI [0.41, 0.94], permutation p<1e-3**. Active is steeper in all five disciplines (per-discipline Active slopes +1.05 to +1.21 vs Static +0.41 to +0.70).
- **The gain is capability-gated.** The Active-Static gain correlates with static ability at **Pearson r=+0.69 (p<1e-4; split-half r=0.70)**; static-ability quartile means are **-0.18, +0.29, +0.49, +0.76** — the weakest quartile is hurt by being handed tools while the strongest gains over three-quarters of a point. The gate replicates on the 5 held-out Gemini closed-source models at **within-family r=0.88** (r=0.57 pooled over all 33). 19/28 models improve; GLM-5.1 gains **+1.21 (5.13 -> 6.33, best Active total)** while Gemma-2 27B loses **-0.82 (4.02 -> 3.20)**.
- **The gain lands in grounding, not originality.** Per-dimension Active-Static deltas: **feasibility +1.21, clarity +0.63, specificity +0.58, impact +0.23, originality -0.14 (not significant**, 15/28 models improve at sign-test p=0.85). Agent-controlled retrieval makes hypotheses more executable and precise but does not make them more novel under literature-verified critics.
- **Controls isolate the process, not the content.** Curated references do not significantly beat closed-book recall (**Static-Recall -0.09, p=0.11**) whereas Active does (**Active-Recall +0.23, p<1e-3**); replaying each model its own agent-surfaced papers passively reproduces the Static score (**Replay-Static +0.08, p=0.21; Active-Replay +0.26, p<1e-3**). Memorization is ruled out: model recitation of target papers scores ROUGE-L 0.014 (91% self-report unknown) and prior-art coverage anti-correlates with Static score (r=-0.32).
- **Scientific World Modeling is exploratory and unconfirmed.** The best design (dynamic + hard panel) gives a pooled **+0.245**; it helps qwen3.5-9b (**+0.61, p=0.028**, lifting originality +0.47 and feasibility +0.67) and qwen3.5-27b (+0.36, p=0.071), but is absent on deepseek-v4 backbones (+0.14 and -0.06 at p>0.6, whose traces already run novelty checks unprompted); the pooled effect fails Holm correction (p>=0.12), is inseparable from a free-text aggregation variant (+0.03 difference, p=0.75), and a **compute-matched best-of-3 baseline meets or exceeds it**.

## Authors & Credibility

The work comes from the HKUST KnowComp group (first authors Yunxiang Mo and Tianshi Zheng with equal contribution; Yangqiu Song as senior author — a well-established NLP/knowledge-graph lab) in collaboration with the NVIDIA AI Technology Center (Ginny Wong, Simon See). The benchmark, critic, prompts, and scoring data are released at github.com/HKUST-KnowComp/AgentIdeaBench. Credibility signals are unusually strong for an LLM-judged benchmark: a seven-round scorer calibration study; human-landmark anchoring (CORE-7 award-paper set at mean 7.71 vs best model Active mean 6.33, and weak controls at 1.80, showing the rubric is not uniformly inflating); judge-robustness checks over weighting, single judges, self-grading, and pairwise human agreement; cluster bootstrapping and leave-one-family-out for the qwen-heavy roster; and out-of-roster replication on five held-out Gemini models. The authors also proactively disclose every negative control on their own SWM extension rather than hiding it.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuinely novel design point. Prior ideation benchmarks (IdeaBench, AI Idea Bench 2025, LiveIdeaBench, MOOSE-Chem, HypoBench) all use static observation with memory-based novelty judging; AgentIdeaBench is the first to (i) contrast curated vs agent-controlled retrieval under matched conditions and (ii) require originality to be argued against prior art retrieved at judging time. The framing "mode of literature access as the experimental variable" is a real methodological contribution, not a repackaging.
- **Rigor:** High, with above-average self-scrutiny. Statistical hygiene is strong (Wilcoxon paired tests, permutation tests, family cluster bootstraps, split-half capability re-estimation, disjoint subfield halves). Three robustness concerns remain, two acknowledged by the authors: (1) the Active track bundles retrieval control with multi-turn interaction and tool-use competence, and the replay/recall controls narrow but do not fully separate these — a condition supplying agent-selected references without agent-driven turns was not run; (2) all dimension scores are LLM judgments, and the authors admit originality is the dimension their human-agreement checks support least — since the headline "originality stays flat" finding turns on this critic, it should be read as "measured originality under this scorer," a caveat the paper itself states but which does soften the per-dimension mechanism claim; (3) the roster is family-heavy (9 of 28 models are qwen; only 24-26 models carry disclosed cutoffs for the cutoff regressions).
- **Reproducibility:** Strong. Open-weight-dominated roster, temperature/seed fixed, released code/prompts/scoring data, deterministic subfield sampling rule, documented budget sweep (Appendix F) with the honest note that budget=10 is conservative for qwen3.5-9b specifically.
- **Significance:** The capability-gate finding (r=+0.69; weakest quartile hurt at -0.18, strongest +0.76) is the paper's most consequential result — it implies static ideation benchmarks are simultaneously saturating at the frontier and mis-ranking in the mid-range, and that handing agentic tooling to weak models can actively degrade their ideation. The 2x scaling slope difference suggests static ideation scores will lose discriminative power exactly where model comparisons matter most.
- **Red flags / weaknesses:** The SWM section (Section 6) is essentially a negative result dressed as a contribution — its pooled effect fails Holm correction (p>=0.12) and loses to a compute-matched best-of-3 baseline, and the authors admit it; framing it as a "capability-gated direction" is fair but a reader could mistake it for a validated method. Discrimination is weakest in dense-literature disciplines (per Appendix E anchor analysis), which the paper flags but which bounds the cross-discipline generality of the leaderboard. A 10-call tool budget means "Active" tests shallow agentic exploration — real AI-scientist loops run far deeper, so the benchmark measures the onset, not the ceiling, of agentic ideation. Finally, no expert domain-researcher novelty study has been run — the validation gap the authors themselves name as most important.

## Strengths

- The matched Static/Active design directly targets a blind spot shared by every prior ideation benchmark, and the two settings are matched on everything (prompts, format, length, scoring) except the variable of interest.
- Literature-verified originality judging — forcing the critic to argue novelty against retrieved prior art instead of parametric memory — is a concrete, reusable fix for the known failure mode of LLM judges in ideation.
- Honest, thorough controls: recall-only and replay tracks, memorization probes with ROUGE-L, family cluster bootstraps, out-of-roster Gemini replication, and full disclosure that the SWM extension fails correction and a compute-matched baseline.
- Practical takeaway for practitioners: static ideation benchmarks saturate (8 strongest models within 0.39 points), agentic evaluation restores separation (4.4x variance), and tool access is not uniformly beneficial — it is an amplifier, not a ladder.
- Human-landmark anchoring (CORE-7 at 7.71 vs best model 6.33 vs weak controls 1.80) gives the LLM-judged scale an interpretable, non-circular calibration.

## Limitations

- All scores are LLM judgments; originality has the weakest human-agreement support, and the paper's own "most important" missing validation is a domain-expert novelty study.
- The Active condition confounds retrieval control with multi-turn interaction and tool-execution competence; the process-vs-tool-competence split is not fully separated.
- The SWM generation-time loop is unconfirmed (fails multiple-comparison correction, matched by best-of-3 baseline) and its benefit is confined to a handful of small backbones.
- Critic discrimination varies by discipline — weakest in dense-literature fields — so cross-discipline rankings are soft.
- The roster is family-heavy (9/28 qwen) and predominantly open-weight; closed-source coverage rests on 5 Gemini models in a held-out replication role.
- A 10-call SEARCH/FETCH budget and single-paragraph output make this a shallow probe of agentic ideation relative to full AI-scientist loops; hypotheses are untested proposals, and the paper explicitly warns scores should not gate funding or publication.

## Relevance

本基准直接关切"LLM 智能体的科学发现能力评测"这一核心议题：它用严格匹配实验证明自主检索式（agentic）评测比静态文献阅读式评测更能区分前沿模型、且增益被能力门控，对评估自主 AI 科学家系统的构思模块以及设计下一代 agentic benchmark 都有直接方法论参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the capability gate (r=+0.69) persist under a domain-expert novelty study, or is it partly an artifact of the LLM critic's originality axis being the least human-validated dimension?
- Can a condition supplying agent-selected references without agent-driven turns (the missing cell in the Active-bundle decomposition) separate multi-turn interaction value from tool-execution competence?
- Does the "grounding without added novelty" mechanism hold for full research proposals (multi-page, with experimental design) rather than single 80-150-word paragraphs?
- The 10-call budget was saturated by only one backbone (qwen3.5-9b, +0.45 from 10->20 calls); do frontier models keep gaining at 50-100 calls, or does the Active curve also plateau?
- Why does feasibility peak on weak models (Llama-4 Maverick 6.87, the roster's feasibility max) while originality/impact climb toward the frontier — is cautious restatement a stable equilibrium for small models under agentic tooling?
- Could SWM-style structured thought experiments be redesigned to help frontier models, given that deepseek-v4 traces already run spontaneous novelty audits (Appendix O) — e.g., by targeting dimensions the model does not self-check?
