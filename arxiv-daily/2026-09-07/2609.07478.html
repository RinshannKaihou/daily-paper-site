Why this matters

Evaluating LLM agents by output behaviour alone misses post-training's internal rewiring — decodable ≠ used ≠ steerable is a reusable evidentiary checklist for interpretability and agent evaluation.

Critical edge

The causal headline rests on graded output probabilities, not regenerated choices — regenerated-choice confidence intervals include zero, and the post-training claim rests on a single matched pair.

Methodology

An exhaustive game census, read from the inside

Four open-weight models — a matched Qwen2.5-72B base/Instruct pair, Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct, and GPT-OSS-120B — each play, one-shot, the complete Robinson–Gofforth catalogue of 144 strict ordinal 2×2 games (payoffs ranked 1–4), in four counterbalanced forms across eight conditions; 99.4% of 18,432 responses were usable. A prespecified hierarchy (dominance → unique pure Nash → payoff-dominant equilibrium → maximin) fixes each game's canonical action; the incentive gap scores its payoff advantage against a 50–50 (level-0) opponent. Behaviour is calibrated against two human datasets plus quantal level-k fits. Internals are probed with linear probes held out by game, a partial-slope "endogenous bridge" for use beyond the objective gap, token-level logit-lens for answer-axis expression, and causal steering of the letter-orthogonalised incentive direction at layers 65 and 79.

Results beyond the headline
  • Own incentive is linearly decodable in every model (AUC 0.767–0.916) while opponent incentive stays at chance — recoverability does not imply use.
  • Canonical play falls with structural complexity nearly like humans (ρ −0.40…−0.42 vs −0.44); the reasoning model GPT-OSS declines far less (ρ −0.18) and fits QLk depth 2.07 vs the level-1 cluster.
  • Five cue instructions are near-perfectly decodable (0.97–1.00) yet add only ΔAUC ≈ 0.04 over game structure — cues encode, they don't impose personas.

+Strengths
  • Cherry-pick-proof stimulus domain — all 144 strict ordinal 2×2 games, with human data on the identical payoff scale.
  • Matched base–Instruct pair isolates post-training as the only manipulated variable — rare, well-controlled in interpretability.
  • Multi-level evidence chain (behaviour → decoding → geometry → bridge → steering → lens), each level's inferential status labelled honestly.
  • The availability / recruitment / susceptibility vocabulary exports a checklist to the "LLMs as economic agents" literature.
−Limitations
  • One-shot strict ordinal 2×2 games only — repeated, sequential, incomplete-information and multiplayer settings untested.
  • Four models; post-training conclusion from a single pair; cross-model comparisons descriptive.
  • Causal evidence narrow: one incentive, two layers, graded policy — regenerated-choice CIs include zero.
  • GPT-OSS excluded from causal and token-level tests (post-reasoning capture); one fixed wording per cue; no multiplicity correction; incentive assumes equal rank spacing.
Open questions
  1. Does the base→Instruct recruitment gap replicate in other matched pairs (Llama-3.1, Qwen3, RLHF vs DPO), or is +0.047 a Qwen2.5-72B idiosyncrasy?
  2. Would steering also shift regenerated choices reliably — at larger scale, or in games with mid-range dose-zero preference?
  3. Can opponent incentive (chance-level here) be made internally representable, e.g. after opponent-modelling fine-tuning?
  4. How does recruitment change in repeated or incomplete-information games, where decodable beliefs only weakly drive actions?
  5. Is Instruct's opponent-payoff increment (−0.65) a causal bottleneck for belief-based choice, or an epiphenomenon?
