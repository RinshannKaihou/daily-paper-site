Why this matters

Closes the loop from experience → editable structure → online guidance — a concrete, fail-safe recipe for long-horizon, reliable agents.

把「经验 → 可编辑结构 → 在线指导」闭环化；验证门控的自进化设计，对构建长时程、可靠的 agent 系统有直接参考价值。

Critical edge

Guidance fires at every step — ~2.5× tokens, ~1.75× latency — and on HotpotQA, the one benchmark with a large test set, gains sit within noise (−0.90 to +1.30 pts). “Consistent gains” oversells a tool-task concentrate.

Credibility

Industrial-lab effort (Google · Georgia Tech · PKU) with above-average statistical hygiene: 95% CIs on every cell, a sign test across settings, Fisher's exact on the survival flip (p = 2.6×10⁻⁸), returned-checkpoint rather than best-round reporting, and a self-disclosed weakest benchmark. Caveats: small test sets with wide CIs (GDPval n=44, ±15 pts), all four solvers are frozen commercial snapshots, no code or graph artifacts released, and evolution-loop compute is unreported.

How it works

Two alternating phases — soft guidance online, gated evolution offline.

Online (graph frozen). The agent's last action — typically a tool call — is matched to a node to localize the current state; the 2-hop directed neighborhood (full graph as fallback) is extracted, and a guidance LLM sharing the solver's model turns the subgraph plus a 3-step trajectory window into soft situational guidance appended to the solver prompt — steering without dictating. Edge attributes are read in topological context, so prerequisites and consequences of the next move stay connected — exactly what independent top-k rule retrieval loses.

Offline (evolution loop). An LLM refiner contrasts high- vs low-scoring diagnostic rollouts, spots repeated error loops and multi-step shortcuts, and emits structured Add / Delete / Update edits (cycle-repaired on application). A candidate graph is committed only if its held-out validation score doesn't drop; rejected edits feed a rejection memory that discourages repeating them next round.

Every comparison — PG and all seven baselines (ReAct, MemoryBank, RAP, ExpeL, AutoGuide, AWM, KnowAgent) — shares one ReAct solver at temperature-0, with identical training trajectories.

Strengths

  • Clean conceptual framing — procedural knowledge as queryable triplets, the direct analogue of knowledge graphs for facts.
  • Localization proven necessary — connected 2-hop retrieval beats both full-graph injection (ALFWorld 81.53 vs 54.48 collapse) and independent top-k rule retrieval, on all three ablation benchmarks.
  • Fail-safe by construction — validation gating, structural checks, rejection memory, and rollback mean the loop cannot silently degrade the graph.
  • No human priors required — grows from a minimal skeleton past hand-designed graphs, and repairs expert priors that actively hurt (58.93% → 92.86%).
  • Breadth with honesty — 7 × 4 × 7 evaluation, CIs everywhere, weakest benchmark disclosed in detail.

Limitations

  • Overhead — a guidance call at every step: ~2.5× tokens and ~1.75× latency on HotpotQA; +33–55% total tokens even with localization. Acknowledged, unresolved.
  • Task-dependence — near-zero effect on multi-hop QA (−0.90 to +1.30); gains concentrate in tool/procedure-heavy tasks (function calling, embodied, policy, financial simulation).
  • Small-n evidence — GDPval n=44, τ-bench n=115, BFCL v3 n=100: many per-cell wins sit inside wide CIs (±15 pts on GDPval); EnterpriseArena gating decisions hinge on ~20 episodes.
  • Unproven transfer — graphs are per-benchmark; cross-solver and cross-tool-interface transfer is untested; evolution-loop compute unquantified; no code or graph release.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Can guidance fire only at uncertain or branching steps — or be cached — to shed the ~2× overhead without losing the gains?
  2. Do graphs evolved under one solver transfer to a different solver or tool interface, and does a cross-model validation gate change which edits commit?
  3. Would a stricter gate — significant improvement, or evaluation under sampled decoding instead of temperature-0 ties — yield more robust graphs, or stall evolution?
  4. Why no HotpotQA gain: no exploitable procedural structure, or search-tool loops already near the solver's ceiling?
  5. How does PG compare to simply fine-tuning the solver on the same successful trajectories, in performance and total compute?
  6. Does rejection memory scale — after hundreds of rounds, does accumulated negative evidence help or saturate the refiner's context?
