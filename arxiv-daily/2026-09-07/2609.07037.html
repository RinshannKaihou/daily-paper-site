Methodology

Steering Vector Dissection — one opaque vector → a library of named concept directions

Four steps, applied to hidden states at a chosen layer (steering happens at the same site during generation):

  1. Pair & subtract

    8,192 positive/negative prompt-response pairs each; activations matched by batch-wise cosine nearest neighbor, then differenced — cancelling token/context similarity to isolate semantics.

  2. Train a tiny SAE

    BatchTopK over the differences: 16 latents, k=4, bias-free decoder (reconstructions are strictly non-negative combinations of “basis vectors”), no input centering.

  3. Interpret by steering

    Each basis vector steers the model at weak/intermediate/strong strength; a GPT-5.4 judge contrasts steered vs unsteered responses to hypothesize its concept.

  4. Filter

    Relevance + alignment (0–2) scoring via GPT-5.4-nano; survivors (mean >1.0) deduplicated to distinct, non-overlapping concepts by a judge-ensemble majority vote.

Also in the evidence

  • Mutually distinguishable effects — cross-vector embedding-shift similarity 0.02–0.14 vs self-similarity 0.16–0.37 (PKU · Llama L15): each basis vector pushes outputs in its own direction.
  • DiffMean ≈ a non-negative combination of basis vectors — trained SAEs reconstruct scaled DiffMean at cosine 0.96 (9–14 of 16 latents active).
  • Filtering vs SAE (Appendix D) — random candidates + filtering beat DiffMean in 5 of 7 pairs, but the SAE beats random in 6 of 7 with 36 vs 13 surviving vectors.

Strengths

What the paper gets right

  • Interpretability claim made falsifiable — the rejection-cancellation experiment converts “the vector contains X” into a pass/fail behavioral test, and it passes.
  • Unusually honest appendices — filtering-vs-SAE ablation, seed robustness, latent-dimension sensitivity, and the DiffMean reconstruction (cos 0.96) directly backing the composite-vector claim.
  • Serious baseline — SVD compared inside the same pipeline, with the cells where SVD wins openly reported.
  • Concrete motivation — names the real failures of single vectors (Zou 2023 “happiness” → harmful compliance; Durmus 2024 bias-steering side effects).

Limitations

Where to hold the skepticism

  • No coverage guarantee — basis vectors capture only a slice of the data’s concepts; full coverage at some dimensionality is conjectured, not verified.
  • Expensive pipeline — needs far more data than DiffMean, every candidate passes multiple LLM-judge stages, and no code release is mentioned.
  • Closed, drifting evaluation — all consistency metrics depend on GPT-5.4-nano concept extraction and OpenAI embeddings; no human ground truth, no inter-judge agreement.
  • Non-canonical decomposition — results vary with SAE seed, training sample, and latent dimensionality; this is not “the” basis of a steering vector.
  • Early-layer bias — gains concentrate in earlier layers; L20/L23 interventions show small or statistically non-significant effects.

Questions for Further Exploration

Open threads worth pulling

  1. Would adding the identified rejection direction strengthen refusal — the defensive flip the authors propose but never evaluate?
  2. Can human annotation or open judge models replace the GPT-5.4-in-the-loop — and do consistency rankings survive judge swaps?
  3. Does unsafe + rejection co-entanglement replicate for other behavior pairs: sycophancy, honesty, bias?
  4. Is there a principled, behavior-based way to choose latent dimensionality beyond judge cost?
  5. How does the decomposition interact with other steering sites — attention heads, MLP outputs, multiple layers at once?
