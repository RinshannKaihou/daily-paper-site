> **一句话总结：** 本文提出 StereoDisco 框架，把心理学中的"语义差异法"搬进 LLM 内部表征——用 WordNet 反义词对构造~2000 条候选语义轴，通过探针在注意力头激活中定位每条轴对应的几何方向，再用 KS 检验判断社会群体在该方向上的投影是否显著偏离随机短语；在 Llama-3-8B-Instruct 和 Mistral-7B-Instruct 上发现两模型彼此的立场一致度（0.73–0.75）明显高于各自与人类刻板印象数据的一致度（0.55–0.63），并且额外发现了"谦逊vs高傲""心胸狭窄vs开阔""懦弱vs勇敢"等心理学刻板印象词典之外、但被人类标注者独立确认的刻板化语义轴。

> **TL;DR:** StereoDisco adapts Osgood's semantic differential method to LLM internal activations: it builds ~2,000 candidate semantic axes from WordNet antonym adjective synsets, recovers each as a geometric direction via mass-mean probing over attention-head outputs, projects concept activations (e.g., 50 social groups) onto that axis, and uses a Kolmogorov-Smirnov test against a frequency-matched random-phrase reference set to flag "stereotypical" axes. On Llama-3-8B-Instruct and Mistral-7B-Instruct, StereoDisco recovers human Warmth/Competence social-group ratings from Fraser et al. (2021) at 55-63% position accuracy, but the two LLMs agree with *each other* at 73-75% - notably higher than either LLM's agreement with humans - and the framework surfaces stereotyped axes (e.g., cowardly vs. brave, narrow-minded vs. broad-minded) absent from the standard Nicolas et al. (2021) stereotype dictionary yet independently confirmed by human annotators.

## 核心贡献

- **理论驱动的四组件框架**：将语义差异法形式化为四个可独立替换的组件——(1) 语义轴来源（本文用 WordNet 反义形容词同义集，1999 对/5182 个独特形容词）；(2) 语义轴表征（探针把每条轴映射为激活空间中的几何方向）；(3) 概念投影（把目标概念激活投影到该几何轴上）；(4) 刻板性检验（用统计检验判断投影分布是否显著偏离参照集）。整个框架不依赖预先设定的社会心理学语义轴清单，可迁移到品牌、食品等任何"有意义对立面"的概念族。
- **内部定位证据**：跨 Llama-3-8B-Instruct 与 Mistral-7B-Instruct 的所有层-头对做探针扫描，发现语义轴的几何编码集中在中后期少数注意力头（而非早期层或全层平均），为"LLM 把语义轴编码为几何方向"的假设（H1）提供了直接的层-头级证据。
- **社会群体刻板印象案例研究**：把 StereoDisco 应用到 50 个社会群体名称，发现两大开源 LLM 之间的立场一致度（0.73-0.75）显著高于各自与人类刻板印象数据的一致度（0.55-0.63），说明 LLM 内部编码的刻板印象内容与社会心理学文献记录的人类刻板印象存在系统性偏离，且这种偏离在不同模型间是一致的（并非随机噪声）。
- **发现词典之外的刻板化语义轴**：在 100 个分层抽样的 WordNet 反义轴（76 个经人工判定为适用）上，StereoDisco 与人类标注在 57-59% 的轴上判断一致，并独立发现了 cowardly/brave、narrow-minded/broad-minded、humble/proud 等此前刻板印象研究未曾系统考察、但人类标注者独立确认为刻板化的语义轴，突破了以往依赖预定义心理学维度（如 SCM 的 Warmth/Competence）的局限。
- **可直接用于激活干预**：由于框架精确定位到编码每条刻板化轴的具体注意力头，恢复出的几何轴可直接用于推理时的激活转向（activation steering）去偏，无需额外提示工程或微调——且能覆盖 retarded vs. precocious 这类不在现有去偏方法目标清单内的轴。

## Key Contributions

- **A theory-grounded, four-component framework** that operationalizes Osgood's semantic differential method for LLM internal representations: (i) a semantic axis source (WordNet antonym adjective synsets: 1,999 pairs from 5,182 unique adjectives), (ii) a semantic axis representation component that maps each axis to a geometric direction via probing, (iii) a concept projection component, and (iv) a stereotypicality test component using a distributional statistical test. Each component is independently swappable, and the framework generalizes beyond social groups to any concept family with meaningful opposites (brands, foods, etc.).
- **Internal localization of semantic axes**: exhaustive layer-head probing across all attention heads of Llama-3-8B-Instruct and Mistral-7B-Instruct (L=32, H=32, d_head=128 for both) shows that semantic axes are linearly encoded in a small subset of middle-to-late attention heads, identified via a between-class/within-class variance-ratio score and mass-mean probing (not logistic regression, to preserve pole-swap invariance).
- **Case study on social group stereotypes** showing LLM-LLM agreement (0.73-0.75 across Warmth/Competence) exceeds LLM-human agreement (0.55-0.63), suggesting LLM-encoded stereotype content diverges systematically from - but is internally consistent across models relative to - documented human stereotypes.
- **Discovery of novel stereotypical axes** (cowardly vs. brave, narrow-minded vs. broad-minded, humble vs. proud, unreasonable vs. reasonable, retarded vs. precocious) outside the Nicolas et al. (2021) stereotype dictionary used in prior computational work, independently corroborated by human annotators.
- **Actionable for steering/mitigation**: because the recovered axes are tied to specific attention heads, they are directly usable for inference-time activation steering, addressing a gap in prior stereotype-mitigation work that only targets a small predefined set of semantic axes.

## Methodology

StereoDisco has four components, instantiated as follows for the case study:

1. **Semantic axis source**: candidate axes are pairs of antonymous WordNet adjective synsets (sense-disambiguated, e.g., *large.a.01* vs. *small.a.01*), yielding 1,999 axis pairs. For each pole, sentences are generated from 30 hand-written templates in two formulations - *Simple* (e.g., "{ADJ} means: {DEF}.") and *Listing* (e.g., "List adjectives similar to {ADJ}, in the sense of {DEF}."), where {DEF} is the WordNet definition disambiguating the sense. Synonyms per synset are instantiated then downsampled to N=30 sentences per pole for balance.

2. **Semantic axis representation**: for each labeled sentence, the model's attention-head output x_{l,h}(s) is extracted (mean-pooled over all tokens, per an ablation favoring this over last-token extraction). **Mass-mean probing** derives the geometric axis θ_{j,l,h} as the difference between positive- and negative-pole class means (Eq. 1-3), chosen over logistic regression because it deterministically preserves the axis direction under pole-label swap. Candidate (layer, head) pairs are scored by a variance-ratio statistic R_{j,l,h} (between-class squared distance over within-class variance, Eq. 4); the top-k=128 layer-head pairs (selected via ablation) are kept for each axis.

3. **Concept projection**: for a concept c (e.g., a social group), a prompt "Provide the best description of {c} using adjectives" is fed to the model, its activation is extracted at each selected (l,h), projected onto θ_{j,l,h} (cosine-style projection, Eq. 6), z-scored per head jointly over the concept set and a reference set, then averaged across the top-k heads (Eq. 7) to get a single scalar π_j(c).

4. **Stereotypicality test**: a two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov test compares the projection distribution of a concept set C (e.g., 50 social groups, Table 8) against a frequency-matched random-phrase reference set C' (50 phrases, Table 9, matched by Zipf frequency to control for frequency confounds per Garg et al. 2018). The axis is flagged stereotypical if p < 0.05; the KS statistic D serves as a continuous stereotypicality score.

For the H4 (human-alignment) evaluation, two separate ground-truth sources are used: (a) Fraser et al. (2021)'s compilation of Warmth/Competence social-psychology labels for 25 social groups, mapped onto 50 Warmth axes and 42 Competence axes built from the Nicolas et al. (2021) stereotype dictionary (Table 7), for **position prediction** (does the sign of π_j(g) match the ground-truth label?); and (b) the authors' own annotation effort - 5 volunteer annotators (filtered down from 11 via quality-control items) rating a stratified sample of 100 WordNet axes (Philosophy/Psychology, Unknown, Technical domain strata) against the same 50 social groups on a 1-5 scale, aggregated into stereotypical/non-stereotypical/not-applicable labels (Fleiss' κ=0.375, "fair" agreement), yielding 76 human-applicable axes for **stereotypicality identification**.

An ablation study (Appendix A, Table 3) validates the configuration choices: Listing templates outperform Simple templates (especially as k grows); n=30 vs. n=15 templates gives negligible improvement (+0.002 average); mean-over-tokens extraction outperforms last-token extraction and is consistent with known mid-to-late-layer emergence of semantic representations in decoder-only LLMs; and k=128 is chosen as a single operating point balancing Warmth (which peaks at k=128) and Competence (which keeps improving up to k=512).

## Results & Highlights

- **Position prediction accuracy (H1/H2/H4, §5.1, Fig. 1)**: Human-Llama agreement is 0.63 on Competence vs. 0.57 on Warmth; Human-Mistral agreement is 0.62 on Competence vs. 0.55 on Warmth (accuracy = fraction of 50/42 Warmth/Competence social-psychology axes where the model's projection sign matches Fraser et al. 2021 ground truth). Critically, Llama-Mistral cross-model agreement is 0.75 (Competence) and 0.73 (Warmth) - exceeding either model's agreement with humans by roughly 0.10-0.20 - indicating the two independently-trained LLMs converge on stereotype content that diverges from the human social-psychology baseline.
- **Stereotypicality identification agreement (H4, §5.2, Fig. 2, Table 1)**: on the 76 human-applicable semantic axes, StereoDisco's stereotypical/non-stereotypical classification (via KS test, p<0.05) agrees with aggregated human judgments on 43/76 axes (57%) for Llama-3-8B-Instruct and 45/76 axes (59%) for Mistral-7B-Instruct - only modestly above the 43/76 diagonal baseline one would get by chance-matching category sizes, and leaving 19 axes flagged only by humans and 14 only by the model (Llama case) as clear disagreements.
- **Newly discovered stereotypical axes (Table 1)**: KS statistics for axes outside the Nicolas et al. (2021) dictionary include cowardly.a.01/brave.a.01 (D=0.56, Llama, p<.001; D=0.32, Mistral, p<.001), narrow-minded.a.02/broad-minded.a.02 (D=0.34, Llama; D=0.60, Mistral), and unreasonable.a.01/reasonable.a.01 (D=0.62, Llama; D=0.44, Mistral); notably humble.a.02/proud.a.01 is significant for Llama (D=0.28) but *not* significant for Mistral (D=0.16, p≥0.05, flagged with † as not considered stereotypical) - the paper transparently reports this null result rather than omitting it.
- **Distinct projection distributions (Fig. 3)**: on the stereotypical axis inferior.a.01 vs. superior.a.02, social group mentions spread across both poles (e.g., "uneducated people" near the negative pole, "CEOs" near the positive pole) while random phrases cluster near the median; on the non-stereotypical axis slow.a.01 vs. fast.a.01, social groups and random phrases show near-identical medians and overlapping spread - a clean qualitative confirmation of H3 (distributional shift only on stereotypical axes).
- **Ablation deltas (Appendix A, Table 3)**: ensemble size k has a large effect on Competence accuracy, rising from ~0.50 at k=1 to ~0.68-0.69 at k=512-1024 (a ~0.18-0.19 point gain from head aggregation), but only a modest effect on Warmth (peaking near k=128 before plateauing); Listing templates beat Simple templates by up to +0.04 on Competence at k=512 (0.69 vs. 0.65 for Llama; 0.68 vs. 0.66 for Mistral).
- **Power analysis caveat (Appendix C, Table 6)**: of the 76 applicable axes, only 39 (Llama) / 46 (Mistral) of the "flagged stereotypical" axes are well-powered (power ≥ 0.80); among the "not flagged" axes, 18 (Llama) / 16 (Mistral) are underpowered (power between 0.30 and 0.80), meaning a substantial fraction of the non-stereotypical classifications are statistically inconclusive rather than confirmed null results - a limitation the authors report themselves via bootstrap power simulation.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The core idea - reformulating the psychometric semantic differential method as probing + geometric-axis projection over LLM attention-head activations, rather than static word embeddings - is a genuine and clean methodological contribution relative to prior WEAT/SEAT/CEAT-style cosine-similarity approaches and prior semantic-differential-on-embeddings work (An et al. 2018; Kozlowski et al. 2019; Lucy et al. 2022; Zeng et al. 2024). It is also positioned clearly against Nicolas and Caliskan (2025), who prompt LLMs and cluster outputs rather than probing internals, and against Wu et al. (2026), who examine attention heads but only for steering a fixed predefined axis set rather than discovering new axes.

**Rigor**: The four-hypothesis (H1-H4) structure with explicit falsifiable tests is a strength, and the ablation (Table 3) and power analysis (Table 6, Appendix C) are unusually thorough for a paper of this scope - the authors explicitly flag that many "non-stereotypical" classifications are underpowered rather than confirmed nulls, which is an honest disclosure many papers would omit. However, the headline "57-59% agreement with humans" figure is only modestly better than what one would expect from base-rate matching given the human label distribution (31 stereotype / 12 non-stereotype among the 43 agreements), and the paper's own Fleiss' κ=0.375 for its human annotation pool is merely "fair" agreement - meaning the human ground truth used to validate the model is itself fairly noisy, collected from only 5 annotators after excluding 6 of the original 11 for quality-control failures.

**Reproducibility**: Full templates (Table 2), social group lists (Table 8), random-phrase reference set (Table 9), semantic axis lists (Table 7, 10, 11), and per-axis power analyses are all reported in appendices, and code/data are stated to be released with the camera-ready version - a strong reproducibility posture, though the code was not available to verify at review time (only promised as supplementary material).

**Significance and red flags**: The two evaluated models (Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct) are both ~7-8B decoder-only instruction-tuned models from a similar release era; the paper explicitly acknowledges in its Limitations that generalization to substantially larger models or different training objectives is untested and left to future work - a fair but real scope limitation, especially since much industry deployment now involves much larger models. There is no empirical head-to-head comparison against the word-embedding-based WEAT/SEAT/CEAT baselines the paper positions itself against (only a qualitative/related-work contrast), so the quantitative advantage of the internal-representation approach over the embedding-based paradigm is asserted rather than measured. The "discovered axis" claims in Table 1 are based on comparing StereoDisco flags to independently-annotated human judgments for those specific axes, which is a genuinely useful validation, but the sample is limited to 5 axes highlighted as noteworthy out of the larger 100-axis stratified pool, raising a mild risk of selective reporting of the most interesting positive examples (though the paper does transparently include the one non-significant Mistral result for humble/proud).

## Strengths

- Clean, hypothesis-driven framework (H1-H4) with each hypothesis tied to a specific empirical test, rather than a single end-to-end black-box pipeline.
- Extensive appendix-level rigor: full ablation over template style, token position, and ensemble size (Table 3); bootstrap-based statistical power analysis (Appendix C, Table 6) that most stereotype-probing papers omit.
- Moves beyond the small, fixed set of psychology-derived semantic axes (Warmth/Competence/SCM) that dominate prior computational stereotype work, using WordNet's ~2,000 antonym pairs as a much larger candidate pool, and discovers new stereotyped axes independently confirmed by human annotators.
- Framework is explicitly designed to be extensible (axis source, representation method, projection, and test are all swappable components) and connects directly to actionable downstream use (activation steering for bias mitigation).
- Transparent about negative/null results (e.g., the non-significant humble/proud axis for Mistral) and about the limitations of its own human ground truth (Fleiss' κ=0.375, annotator pool size).

## Limitations

- Only two ~7-8B open-weight instruction-tuned models are studied; whether findings (e.g., the geometric encoding of semantic axes, or the LLM-LLM vs. LLM-human agreement gap) generalize to larger models or different training paradigms (RLHF variants, base vs. instruct, closed models) is untested and explicitly flagged as future work.
- The study is English-only, since both the sentence templates and WordNet antonym source are English-specific; extending to other languages requires new templates and multilingual lexical resources (ConceptNet, BabelNet).
- No ground truth exists for what stereotypes an LLM "should" encode, so StereoDisco's outputs can only be validated against human social-psychology baselines as a proxy - divergences may reflect genuine LLM-vs-human differences in stereotype content rather than framework failure, a point the authors themselves raise but that also limits how confidently H4 can be interpreted.
- Applicability of the WordNet-antonym-based semantic axis source is restricted to concepts where the axes are semantically meaningful (e.g., human-attribute axes apply to social groups, but geometry or chemistry antonym axes do not) - the analyst must exercise judgment in axis applicability, and while stratified sampling and human "not applicable" filtering partially address this, it remains a manual/subjective step.
- The human stereotypicality annotation (§5.2 ground truth) has only fair inter-annotator reliability (Fleiss' κ=0.375) from a small final pool of 5 annotators, and a meaningful fraction of the underlying axis comparisons are statistically underpowered (Table 6), so the 57-59% agreement figures should be read with those caveats in mind rather than as a precise measurement.

## Relevance

本文提供了一套可复用的、比 WEAT/SEAT 更贴近模型内部机制的刻板印象探测方法论，其"探针定位几何轴 + 统计检验"的四组件设计思路对做 LLM 可解释性、激活干预（activation steering）或去偏对齐研究的读者有直接参考价值；其发现的"模型间比模型-人类更一致"现象也为审视 LLM 对齐评估中"用另一个 LLM 做裁判"的可靠性提供了警示性证据。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the LLM-LLM agreement exceeding LLM-human agreement pattern hold for larger or more recent models (e.g., 70B+ or frontier closed models), or is it an artifact of both Llama-3-8B and Mistral-7B being trained on overlapping web-scale corpora with similar RLHF pipelines?
- How would StereoDisco's discovered axes and stereotypicality scores change under direct activation steering interventions - i.e., can the recovered geometric axes actually reduce measured stereotype projections in downstream generation without degrading fluency or task performance?
- Would a head-to-head quantitative comparison against WEAT/SEAT/CEAT on the same 50 social groups and same axes show StereoDisco capturing meaningfully different (or more) stereotype content than embedding-based methods, rather than just a methodologically distinct lens?
- Given the underpowered axes flagged in the power analysis (Table 6), what would the human-model agreement rate look like if restricted only to well-powered axes on both sides - would the 57-59% figure rise substantially?
- Can the WordNet-antonym axis source be extended with non-adjective sources (proper nouns, domain-specific dictionaries) to probe stereotypes for concept families beyond social groups (brands, foods, individuals) as the authors suggest, and would the same mass-mean probing and KS-test pipeline transfer without modification?
