> **一句话总结：** 本文通过对商用DDR4芯片的细粒度实验表征与Sentaurus TCAD器件级仿真的系统性比对，发现现有RowHammer/RowPress器件物理模型存在多处与真实芯片不符之处（如双边RowHammer的翻转方向、RowPress的AC_min随激活时间的变化趋势），并提出电荷陷阱位置、电子迁移路径长度、体内空穴陷阱密度等关键建模参数，修正后的机制能同时解释实验观测到的翻转方向、翻转数量和AC_min趋势。

> **TL;DR:** By pairing real-chip characterization of commodity DDR4 DRAM (DRAM Bender, 128 victim rows per module across 3 manufacturers) with calibrated Sentaurus TCAD device simulations of a 1z-nm saddle-fin RCAT cell, the authors show that state-of-the-art device-level models (e.g., ref. [78]) get key RowHammer/RowPress characteristics wrong — e.g., predicting double-sided RowHammer suppresses 0-to-1 bitflips (real chips show both directions with only 1.49–6.77% Jaccard overlap) and predicting RowPress AC_min should initially increase with tAggON (real chips show it monotonically decreases). They trace these mismatches to charge-trap location and density: trap position near the NWL vs. PWL flips which bitflip direction dominates (74.7–75.5% AC_min reduction for the enhanced direction under double-sided hammering), and bulk hole-trap density (D_HT,bulk) controls whether PWL-induced RowPress leakage grows fast enough with tAggON to match real chips.

## 核心贡献

- 系统梳理了先前实验表征工作中RowHammer和RowPress翻转的关键特征（翻转方向、空间分布、AC_min），并将其与现有器件级建模机制逐一比对，明确指出三类"不匹配的器件级机制"（不匹配机制1-3）。
- 设计了针对商用DDR4芯片的细粒度表征实验（DRAM Bender，128行/模块，3家厂商8/16Gb裸片），重新工程化每个受害位的NWL/PWL映射，验证了单边RowHammer方向性机制（几乎所有模块100%吻合，仅一款16Gb C-Die模块因列重映射为99.7%）。
- 用校准过的Sentaurus TCAD仿真（1z nm saddle-fin RCAT结构，实验I_SN-V_WL标定）复现并解释真实芯片观测：电荷陷阱靠近NWL会使1-to-0翻转的AC_min比单边RowHammer降低75.5%，靠近PWL则使0-to-1翻转的AC_min降低74.7%，且双边RowHammer下0-to-1与1-to-0翻转位置的Jaccard重叠率仅1.49%-6.77%，与真实芯片一致。
- 提出并验证了"陷阱辅助电子迁移(EM)效率"和"电容串扰(CC)"两种机制共同解释为何0-to-1翻转的AC_min始终小于1-to-0翻转：PWL到受害存储节点的迁移路径更短、迁移效率更高；同时0-to-1条件下访问晶体管有效阈值电压更低，对WL间耦合噪声更敏感。
- 通过晶圆级真实制造测试结构（sub-20nm工业级DRAM晶体管）直接测量证明，正常工作电压范围内NWL诱导的RowPress泄漏不足以在64ms保持时间内产生可观测的0-to-1翻转，解决了"缺失的NWL-RowPress 0-to-1翻转"疑问。
- 发现体内空穴陷阱密度(D_HT,bulk)是决定PWL诱导RowPress的1-to-0泄漏机制是否随tAggON增强、从而使AC_min呈现实验观测到的单调下降趋势的关键建模参数。

## Key Contributions

- Extracts and systematizes the key RowHammer/RowPress bitflip characteristics (direction, spatial pattern, AC_min) from prior experimental characterization work, then confronts them against existing device-level mechanisms, identifying three explicit "Unmatching Device-Level Mechanisms."
- Runs new fine-grained real-chip characterization (DRAM Bender, 128 victim rows/module, DDR4 modules from 3 manufacturers, 8Gb/16Gb dies) that reverse-engineers each victim bit's NWL/PWL mapping and validates the single-sided RowHammer directionality mechanism (NWL→1-to-0, PWL→0-to-1) with ~100% matching (99.7% on one 16Gb C-Die module due to repair-induced column remapping).
- Builds and calibrates Sentaurus TCAD simulations (1z-nm saddle-fin RCAT DRAM cell, I_SN–V_WL calibrated against manufacturer data) that reproduce the real-chip observations: trap location near NWL vs. PWL determines which bitflip direction dominates under double-sided RowHammer, cutting AC_min by 75.5% (1-to-0, NWL trap) or 74.7% (0-to-1, PWL trap) relative to single-sided hammering, and yielding only 1.49–6.77% Jaccard overlap between 0-to-1 and 1-to-0 bitflip positions — matching experimental spatial patterns.
- Identifies two complementary physical mechanisms — trap-assisted electron migration (EM) efficiency and capacitive crosstalk (CC) — that both independently explain why 0-to-1 bitflips consistently have smaller AC_min than 1-to-0 bitflips under double-sided RowHammer (shorter migration path from PWL to victim SN; lower effective access-transistor threshold voltage under the 0-to-1 condition).
- Performs direct wafer-level electrical measurements on fabricated sub-20nm DRAM access-transistor test structures to show that under normal operating voltages (VPP), NWL-induced RowPress leakage current is too weak to overcome intrinsic 64ms data-retention leakage, explaining the absence of NWL-RowPress-induced 0-to-1 bitflips in real chips.
- Shows that bulk hole-trap density (D_HT,bulk) is the modeling parameter that determines whether simulated PWL-RowPress AC_min for 0-to-1 bitflips decreases monotonically with tAggON (matching real chips) or instead increases (as predicted by prior device models), resolving a third mismatch.

## Methodology

Two-pronged empirical + device-physics approach:

1. **Real-chip characterization.** Using DRAM Bender for fine-grained command/timing/temperature control, the authors test 9 off-the-shelf DDR4 UDIMM modules from 3 manufacturers (labeled S, H, M), spanning 8Gb and 16Gb die densities and multiple die revisions (Table 1). For each module they randomly sample 128 victim rows, reverse-engineer the internal row mapping and true-/anti-cell layout, and hammer aggressor rows as many times as possible within a 64ms refresh window (single-sided and double-sided RowHammer) or hold aggressor rows open for varying tAggON (RowPress), sweeping temperature (including 80°C, since RowPress is known to be more temperature-sensitive). They record bitflip direction (0-to-1 vs 1-to-0), spatial index (mod 64/512 bit repeating pattern), and AC_min for both directions.

2. **Device-level TCAD modeling.** They build a saddle-fin recessed-channel-access-transistor (RCAT) DRAM cell structure in Sentaurus TCAD, modeled after typical 1z-nm process technology with buried wordlines (dimensions and doping profile calibrated against experimental I_SN–VWL data from a DRAM manufacturer, Fig. 11d). They model two leakage mechanisms: (a) trap-assisted electron migration (EM), using single interface e-traps placed at 6 categorized positions (near NWL, near PWL, near victim WL) for sensitivity analysis, plus a spatially uniform interface trap density (D_IT) to capture collective effects; and (b) capacitive crosstalk (CC), via mixed-mode TCAD simulation incorporating WL resistance, WL-to-ground capacitance, and inter-WL coupling capacitance to generate a crosstalk noise voltage on the victim WL. Mixed-mode transient simulations apply realistic aggressor WL waveforms for single- and double-sided RowHammer/RowPress access patterns (Fig. 12a), track victim storage-node voltage (SN1) evolution, and define AC_min as the number of activations needed to shift SN1 by more than a 0.1V failure threshold from its initialized value (VCORE for 1-to-0 studies, 0V for 0-to-1 studies). Additionally, wafer-level electrical measurements are performed directly on fabricated sub-20nm test structures (multiple parallel access transistors, since single-cell leakage is too small to measure) to directly assess NWL-RowPress leakage magnitude against real device electrical characteristics rather than pure simulation.

The overall strategy is confirmatory/diagnostic rather than purely generative: prior device-level mechanisms are treated as hypotheses to be checked against the newly collected real-chip evidence, and TCAD parameters (trap location, trap density, D_HT,bulk) are varied specifically to identify which settings reconcile simulation with experiment.

## Results & Highlights

- **Double-sided RowHammer AC_min sensitivity to trap location:** with a charge trap near the NWL (trap location 2), TCAD simulation shows the AC_min of 1-to-0 bitflips under double-sided RowHammer is reduced by 75.5% compared to single-sided (NWL) RowHammer, and no double-sided 0-to-1 bitflip is observed (Fig. 14a). With a trap near the PWL (trap location 4), the AC_min of 0-to-1 bitflips is reduced by 74.7% compared to single-sided (PWL) RowHammer, with no double-sided 1-to-0 bitflip observed (Fig. 14b) — directly contradicting the baseline prior device model (ref. [78]) which shows double-sided RowHammer enhancing 1-to-0 but suppressing 0-to-1 bitflips uniformly.
- **Spatial overlap validating the trap-location mechanism:** across all tested real DRAM modules, the mean Jaccard overlap between 0-to-1-only and 1-to-0-only bitflip positions under double-sided RowHammer ranges from only 1.49% to 6.77% (Fig. 15); the module with the largest overlap (S4) shows 6.65% at 50°C and 6.77% at 80°C, while module M0 shows just 2.57% (50°C) and 2.77% (80°C) — consistent with the simulated prediction that trap location (not a competing/canceling mechanism) determines direction on a per-cell basis.
- **Reverse-engineering validation of single-sided directionality:** cross-checking the reverse-engineered NWL/PWL mapping against observed bitflip directions on all tested real modules yields ~100% matching, with the sole exception being a 16Gb C-Die module from manufacturer S at 99.7% matching, which the authors attribute to remapped columns from post-fabrication repair — a concrete, paper-specific caveat rather than a generic disclaimer.
- **Wafer-level NWL-RowPress leakage measurement:** direct I_SN1–VWL measurements on fabricated sub-20nm test structures show that when VNWL is below 3.5V, the leakage current I_SN1 at VBBW is insufficient (does not go sufficiently negative) to induce a 0-to-1 bitflip; only when VNWL is pushed to 4.0–4.5V (beyond the typical VPP=2.5V operating range) does I_SN1 become negative enough — directly explaining, with real electrical data rather than pure simulation, why NWL-induced RowPress 0-to-1 bitflips are never observed in real DRAM chip characterization under normal operating conditions.
- **CC-induced noise example:** with VPP=2.5V and Vnoise_ratio=12%, VWL1 (victim WL, nominally held at -0.2V) increases to +0.124V during aggressor activation, which the TCAD model shows strongly enhances I_SN under the 0-to-1 bitflip condition but has negligible effect under the 1-to-0 condition, due to the access transistor's lower effective threshold voltage in the 0-to-1 case (Fig. 17).

No single end-to-end "our system vs. SOTA" accuracy table exists (this is a mechanistic/explanatory device-physics paper, not a benchmark-driven ML paper), but the quantitative deltas above (75.5%, 74.7%, 1.49–6.77% Jaccard, 99.7% vs ~100% matching) are the paper's core evidence, each directly copied/derived from the reported figures and text.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The paper's framing as "the first to comprehensively and systematically bridge the gap" between experimental characterization and device-level modeling of RowHammer/RowPress is reasonably well supported — it explicitly extends a prior preliminary study (ref. [73]) that only identified gaps without resolving them via TCAD modeling. The three "Unmatching Device-Level Mechanism" call-outs and corresponding TCAD-based resolutions are concrete and specific rather than a vague research-gap narrative.

**Rigor:** The dual-track approach (real DRAM Bender characterization + calibrated TCAD + direct wafer-level transistor measurement for the NWL-RowPress claim) is a strength — the third data source (physical electrical measurement rather than simulation) is a notably rigorous way to settle the "missing 0-to-1 RowPress bitflip" question, since it does not depend on TCAD calibration choices at all.

**Reproducibility concerns:** The TCAD device model is calibrated against I-V data from a single, unnamed "DRAM manufacturer" (Fig. 11d) — a common practice in this sub-field, but it means the specific trap-location categorization and parameter values (D_IT, D_HT,bulk magnitudes in Table 2) are tied to one vendor's process, and TCAD scripts/structures are proprietary Sentaurus workflows not released with the paper. The real-chip characterization side is more reproducible in principle (DRAM Bender is used elsewhere in prior SAFARI work) but relies on specific, dated DRAM modules (Table 1 date codes 1938–2515) that may not be purchasable going forward.

**Paper-specific weak point / honest gap:** The authors themselves flag that the root cause of the NWL-side vs. PWL-side trap-density asymmetry (which explains why 1-to-0 bitflips statistically outnumber 0-to-1 bitflips) is only a "plausible qualitative explanation" grounded in indirect evidence about BCAT process sensitivity to saddle-fin vs. STI region geometry — they explicitly state "a direct experimental comparison of interface-trap density between the NWL-adjacent saddle-fin region and the PWL-side region has not yet been reported," meaning this specific claim rests on inference rather than direct measurement, and they defer exact root-cause quantification as future work.

**Significance:** For the memory-security/architecture community this is a solid mechanistic contribution — it doesn't propose a new mitigation but gives future researchers (and DRAM read-disturbance modelers, e.g., the ScaleDisturb/HammerSim lines of work cited) a corrected physical picture and identifies previously-unrecognized modeling knobs (trap location, D_HT,bulk) that materially change simulated AC_min trends. It does not, however, evaluate or propose any new attack or defense technique, so its practical security impact is indirect (better simulators/models rather than a new exploit or countermeasure).

**Red flag check:** No cherry-picking of a single favorable module is evident — the Jaccard-overlap and reverse-engineering-matching results are reported across the full test set (9 modules, 3 manufacturers), including the one module (16Gb C-Die, Mfr S) that does not achieve 100% matching, which is disclosed rather than hidden. The paper does not compare against a strawman baseline; instead it directly cites specific prior device models (refs. [77]–[80]) by their predicted (and now shown to be incorrect) mechanisms, which is a fair and specific baseline comparison rather than a vague "prior work" claim.

## Strengths

- Combines three independent evidence sources (real-chip DRAM Bender characterization, calibrated TCAD simulation, and direct wafer-level transistor electrical measurement) to triangulate each claim, which is unusually rigorous for a device-modeling paper.
- Explicitly identifies and labels each mismatch between prior device models and real-chip data ("Unmatching Device-Level Mechanism 1–3"), making the paper's contribution auditable rather than a diffuse "improved understanding" claim.
- The reverse-engineering cross-validation (NWL/PWL mapping vs. observed bitflip direction, ~100% matching across modules) is a clean, falsifiable validation step for the underlying single-sided RowHammer mechanism.
- Good disclosure of an outlier result (99.7% instead of 100% matching on one module, attributed to a specific plausible cause — repair-induced column remapping) rather than omitting it.

## Limitations

- The asymmetry in NWL- vs. PWL-adjacent trap density (used to explain the greater prevalence of 1-to-0 over 0-to-1 bitflips at high hammer counts) is inferred indirectly from BCAT process-sensitivity literature, not directly measured — acknowledged by the authors as an open question for exact root-cause quantification.
- Device model calibration relies on I-V data from a single DRAM manufacturer's process; generality across vendors/process nodes beyond the tested 1z-nm-class devices is not independently verified.
- The paper is purely explanatory/diagnostic — it proposes no new mitigation technique and does not build the "more accurate system-level DRAM disturbance error model" it motivates as important future work, leaving translation of these device-level insights into practical simulators or defenses to follow-on work.
- Single-trap sensitivity simulations (used for most trap-location conclusions) have "inherently limited spatial influence," as the authors note, and the uniform-trap-density extension (D_IT) is only applied in one subsection (5.3) rather than throughout all mechanism analyses.

## Relevance

该工作直接关联DRAM硬件安全与存储可靠性研究方向，对理解RowHammer/RowPress攻击的物理根源、评估现有系统级注入模型的准确性、以及设计更精确的读干扰缓解机制具有参考价值，尤其适合关注内存安全、TCAD器件建模或DRAM表征方法的研究者深入阅读。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the inferred NWL-vs-PWL interface-trap density asymmetry be directly measured (e.g., via targeted TCAD-calibrated electrical stress tests or materials characterization) rather than left as a qualitative inference from BCAT literature?
- How well do the identified device-level mechanisms (trap location, D_HT,bulk, CC-induced noise) generalize to DRAM technology nodes beyond the 1z-nm-class device modeled here, including LPDDR5/DDR5 and future sub-10nm nodes?
- Would incorporating these corrected mechanisms into a system-level bitflip injection framework (e.g., successors to HammerSim/ScaleDisturb, both cited) change conclusions in prior RowHammer/RowPress mitigation-technique evaluations that assumed the old (now shown incorrect) device models?
- Does the trap-location-dependent bitflip-direction mechanism have implications for the emerging ColumnDisturb phenomenon mentioned in the paper's future-work discussion, which is not itself characterized here?
- Could the wafer-level test-structure methodology (parallel-transistor leakage measurement) be extended to directly measure trap-assisted EM and CC contributions separately, rather than relying on TCAD decomposition alone?
