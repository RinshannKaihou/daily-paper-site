Why This Matters

Gives GDPR-style deletion and long-context memory researchers a concrete test for which architectures can truly forget vs. only approximate it.

中文原文（Relevance）：该论文与机器遗忘/隐私合规（GDPR被遗忘权）、长上下文记忆架构（线性注意力/delta rule混合模型）以及LLM可解释性审计密切相关，尤其对做检索增强记忆、KV缓存管理或医疗对话助手隐私删除的研究方向有直接参考价值；其对delta rule破坏记录可分离性的定量刻画，也为评估Mamba/DeltaNet类线性注意力模型的"记忆不可删除性"风险提供了具体证据。

▲ Critical Edge

Utility overhead grows sharply with scale — from +2.0% perplexity at 1B to +44.3% at 12B — so the addressable-memory graft is explicitly "not a general replacement for attention."

Methodology

1

Addressable branch (Gemma-3-1B/4B/12B). Replaces 4 of Gemma-3's 26 layers — the global-attention ones, interleaved 5:1 with local windows — with a one-class SVM memory gate. Deleting a token runs the incremental SVM solver of Cauwenberghs & Poggio (2000) in reverse, algebraically undoing exactly that token's contribution without reprocessing anything else. Two-stage recovery — attention-transfer distillation, then rank-8 LoRA (1.49M params, 0.149% of the model) — restores language-modeling quality lost in the graft.

2

Non-addressable branch (Kimi Linear, 48B, inference-only). Twenty gated DeltaNet (KDA) recurrent layers are tested for whether a deleted record's "state contribution" stays independent of what gets written after it — the precondition for any fixed decrement formula to exist. Additive writes pass; per-channel decay passes after a ledger correction; the full delta rule does not. The fallback is deterministic checkpoint-and-replay: save state at record boundaries, rewind to just before the deleted record, replay only the suffix.

Two evaluation tracks, kept strictly apart: a float64 "certificate" proves exactness against a retained-key refit; a separate single-precision "masked-refit proxy" (never used for the exactness claim) drives elicitation, relearning, and membership-inference attack tests.

End-to-end full-model suffix-dependence is median 0.81–1.02 (range 0.21–1.24) pooled across three corpora.

  • Replay is bit-for-bit exact on real clinical data: MIMIC-IV-Ext-CDS passes 8/8 intake records at 236 tokens and 9/9 at 3,248 tokens; MIMIC-IV-Note passes 4/4 discharge notes at 18,842 tokens — victim influence lift drops from +2.020 (or +1.861) nats to exactly 0.000 in every case, with retained-record drift also exactly 0.000.
  • Replay is fast where it matters: latency falls from 6.70s (oldest of 128 records) to 0.00s (newest) vs. a flat 6.77s full rebuild (mean 3.49s vs. 6.77s); storage runs 129 checkpoints at 5.22 GiB.
  • Attention-only masking is not a substitute: zeroing all 20 KDA recurrent states still leaves a planted secret ward code as the model's top prediction (p=0.881); masking only attention removes just 86–95% of TOFU-style lift, with 5/12 victims overshooting and up to 46% residual leakage.
  • Behavioral robustness (masked-refit proxy, 1B): in-context LiRA membership inference sits at chance (AUC 0.499) vs. 0.996 for an untouched control; gradient-ascent weight editing hits the forget floor but is reversed by benign fine-tuning relearning (+3.5±2.2 nat overshoot).

Critical Assessment

NoveltyA useful reframing — deletion as representation-dependent, not universal — tested inside two real pretrained models. But the SVM gate and its solver are inherited from the author's own companion preprint, so the new contribution is narrower than the framing suggests.
RigorUnusually careful about separating claims: the float64 certificate stays strictly apart from the single-precision behavioral proxy. The scaling study reports the +44.3% cost and a diverged rank-16 LoRA run rather than omitting them.
ReproducibilityStrong: fixed seeds, a single-command hero demo (~6 min on CPU), public corpora (FineWeb-Edu, WikiText-103, TOFU). MIMIC-IV validation is credentialed-access and aggregate-only — responsible, but not independently re-checkable without PhysioNet access.
SignificanceDeletes already-ingested in-context state, not pretrained weights — narrower than "true" weight-space unlearning. The KDA result rules out only a fixed receipt, not every possible influence-tracking scheme, so "replay is necessary" stays conditional.

Strengths

  • Clear, falsifiable counterfactual formalization of "exact deletion" (Equation 1) unifying both addressable and non-addressable branches under one testable standard.
  • Careful separation between numerically certified exactness and behaviorally-evaluated robustness — avoids conflating "looks forgotten" with "is provably removed."
  • Validated on real, sensitive clinical data (MIMIC-IV-Ext-CDS, MIMIC-IV-Note) up to 18,842 tokens under a credentialed, aggregate-only reporting protocol.
  • Transparent negative scaling result — utility cost rising 2.0% → 11.2% → 44.3% from 1B → 4B → 12B — rather than cherry-picking the favorable 1B setting.
  • High reproducibility effort: fixed seeds, single-command demos, public code/data pointers, and an explicit Reproducibility Statement.

Limitations

  • Utility overhead is severe and grows sharply with scale, so the addressable-memory graft is explicitly "not a general replacement for attention."
  • The SVM-gate deletion mechanism is inherited from the author's own companion preprint, narrowing this paper's independent methodological novelty.
  • Scope is restricted to already-ingested in-context state; pretrained weights and outputs already observed by users before deletion are explicitly out of scope.
  • The KDA finding only rules out a fixed record-wise receipt (including a diagonal-decay correction) — more complex influence-tracking schemes remain untested.
  • Replay/checkpointing has real storage cost (41.4 MiB per record boundary; 5.22 GiB for 128 clinical records), and cheaper sparse-checkpoint variants are not evaluated.
  • Single-author, single-hardware (one Apple-silicon workstation), single-model-family validation — cross-hardware and broader architecture generality are untested.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Could a more sophisticated per-record influence-tracking mechanism make delta-rule memory addressable without full replay, and at what storage/compute tradeoff?
  2. How does the utility-cost scaling trend (2.0% → 44.3%) behave beyond 12B, and could an architectural change to the SVM gate flatten the curve?
  3. Does the "ingestion-time imprint on neighboring representations" — left unaddressed — pose a meaningful residual privacy risk in practice?
  4. How would this framework's addressability criterion apply to other recurrent/hybrid architectures (Mamba2, Jamba, Titans) beyond Kimi Linear's KDA design?
  5. What would a rigorous cost-benefit comparison look like between replay and alternative approximate-unlearning methods under equivalent attack suites (LiRA, Leak@k)?
