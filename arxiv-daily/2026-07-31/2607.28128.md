> **一句话总结：** 在冻结同一底座模型、只改变策略层（极简"有帮助"对话式辅导 ConvTutor vs. 四节点路由式教学策略 PedTutor）的受控实验中，通用"有帮助性"评委（Claude Opus 4.8）在主底座（Sonnet 4.6）上判不出两者显著差异（Cliff's δ=0.10），但专门的教学法量规却给出完美的等级分离（δ=1.0，ConvTutor均分2.578 vs. PedTutor 4.299）；更换成第二位裁判（GPT-5.6 Sol）后，"有帮助性"排序在三个底座中的两个发生反转（例如GPT-5.5底座上Opus判ConvTutor+0.376分而Sol判PedTutor+0.086分，方向相反且均显著），而确定性过程指标（答案泄露率、下一轮独立性）在裁判间保持不变。

> **TL;DR:** A pre-registered audit fixes the tutor base model and varies only the tutoring policy (minimal ConvTutor vs. structured 4-node PedTutor), then asks whether general-purpose LLM-judged helpfulness tracks a pedagogy-targeted rubric. Under the frozen primary judge (Claude Opus 4.8) on the Claude Sonnet 4.6 base, the two policies do not differ significantly in judged helpfulness (Cliff's δ=0.10, p=.160) yet are perfectly rank-separated on judged pedagogy (δ=1.0, 2.578 vs. 4.299); answer-leakage rate is 43.0% for ConvTutor vs. 6.4% for PedTutor (paired diff +0.365, 95% CI [+0.217, +0.516]). Swapping in a second judge (GPT-5.6 Sol) reverses the helpfulness-based ConvTutor-vs-PedTutor ordering on 2 of 3 tutor bases (e.g., on GPT-5.5, Opus favors PedTutor by -0.376 while Sol favors ConvTutor by +0.086, both significant and opposite in sign), whereas the deterministic pedagogy contrast and the leakage-to-independence coupling (β=-0.386 to -0.592, all p<10⁻¹¹) hold across every base and both judges.

## 核心贡献

- **把"通用有帮助性 ≠ 教学质量"做成了可证伪的受控实验**：在三个底座模型（Claude Sonnet 4.6、GPT-5.5、Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview）上，用完全相同的冻结权重实例化两种辅导策略——极简的ConvTutor（每轮一次模型调用，无任何教学法约束）和结构化的PedTutor（每轮先由学习者状态跟踪器私下评估，再路由到问题分解器/答案拖延门/提示级联三者之一，每轮两次模型调用），确保任何差异只能归因于策略层而非底层权重。
- **实证证明通用有帮助性量表会漏检教学法差异，且判断结果依赖具体裁判**：在主底座上，条件盲评的Claude Opus 4.8给出的"有帮助性"评分在两策略间无显著差异（|δ|=0.10），而预注册的教学法量规给出完美分离（|δ|=1.0）；把同一批1,179条confirmatory对话轮次交给事后指定的第二裁判GPT-5.6 Sol重新打分后，"有帮助性"优劣排序在三个底座中的两个发生反转，成为"裁判依赖型"结论，而教学法量规方向在检测到差异处从未反转。
- **给出两个确定性、裁判无关的过程指标作为更可靠的替代/补充信号**：答案泄露（字符串匹配预先声明的解）与下一轮学生独立性（是否尝试推理步骤）完全由规则判定，不经LLM打分；在三个底座上，"该轮泄露答案"始终显著预测"下一轮学生独立性下降"（线性概率风险差 -0.386到-0.592，p最高为9.1×10⁻²¹），该耦合关系在裁判、消融策略、计算量匹配重分析下均保持不变。

## Key Contributions

- **A controlled, falsifiable test of whether general-purpose helpfulness tracks pedagogical quality**: within each of three tutor bases (Claude Sonnet 4.6, GPT-5.5, Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview), instantiates ConvTutor (one model call/turn, no pedagogical constraint) and PedTutor (a private learner-state tracker plus one of three deterministically-routed responders — a problem decomposer, an answer-deferral gate, and a hint cascade — two model calls/turn) from *identical frozen weights*, isolating the policy layer as the only manipulation.
- **Demonstrates that a standard helpfulness rubric misses pedagogically relevant behavior, and that its verdict is judge-contingent**: under the condition-blind primary judge (Claude Opus 4.8) on the primary base, the helpfulness contrast between policies is small and nonsignificant (Cliff's |δ|=0.10) while the pedagogy contrast is a perfect rank separation (|δ|=1.0); rescoring the identical 1,179 confirmatory turns with a prospectively-specified second judge (GPT-5.6 Sol) reverses the helpfulness-based ConvTutor–PedTutor ordering on two of three bases, while the pedagogy contrast's direction never reverses where a difference is detected.
- **Introduces two deterministic, judge-invariant process measures that remain informative where the judged signal is not**: answer leakage (string-match against pre-declared solution forms) and next-turn independence (whether the student attempts a reasoning step in the immediately following turn) require no LLM relabeling; a leaky turn deterministically predicts less independent student work on the very next turn on every one of the three tutor bases, and this coupling survives a compute-fairness re-analysis and a seven-policy ablation.

## Methodology

The testbed fixes a base LLM and varies only the *tutoring policy*: **ConvTutor** issues one instruction ("be a helpful, clear math tutor") and makes one model call per visible turn, so its answer-giving behavior is emergent rather than engineered. **PedTutor** is a four-node routed policy over the same frozen base: a private learner-state tracker runs every turn (never shown to the student), then a deterministic rule dispatches to exactly one of a problem-decomposer, an answer-deferral gate, or a hint cascade — grounding the four tutoring principles of contingent tutoring, the assistance dilemma, the generation effect, and tutored help-seeking. A no-tool **cold** baseline (no tutoring in any phase) completes the design. All three conditions face a deliberately weak simulated student (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, served via a fixed provider route, ~11% unaided accuracy on the frozen problem set in isolation), so that tutoring is load-bearing. The frozen instrument is 19 authored algebra (mixture/weighted-average) problems in five phases — 6 tutored training problems, then 3 immediate, 4 interference, 3 delayed, and 3 transfer probes answered unassisted from accumulated context — with per-turn measures computed over a fixed "answer-phase" window (the exchange up to the student's first committed final answer).

Two evaluation layers are used. The **primary layer** is a frozen, condition-blind judge (Claude Opus 4.8) that rates every answer-phase turn three times on a five-point rubric for annotator-perceived helpfulness (clarity/responsiveness/helpfulness, pre-registered as a live test) and, post hoc but under a rubric frozen before pedagogy scoring, a symmetric four-subscore + holistic pedagogy rubric (built on the same principles PedTutor is designed around, so pedagogy contrasts are reported as a manipulation check rather than independent evidence). A **post hoc robustness judge** (GPT-5.6 Sol), prospectively specified only after the Opus scores were frozen, rescores the identical 1,179 confirmatory answer-phase turns under the same two rubrics. Two **deterministic process measures** — answer leakage (string-level match against numeric/solution forms declared per problem in advance) and next-turn independence (rule-based: did the student attempt a reasoning step on the very next turn) — require no LLM relabeling and are judge-invariant by construction. The design runs 10 replicates per condition per base (90 confirmatory sessions total), replicated on GPT-5.5 and Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview by swapping only the tutor base model (Gemini's provider enforces a minimum reasoning setting that cannot be disabled, so this replication is not a pure model swap). Pre-registered confirmatory claims (P1: session leakage ConvTutor>PedTutor; P2: session judged helpfulness ConvTutor>PedTutor; P3: session independence PedTutor>ConvTutor; J1: all three significant in the predicted direction; J2: a per-turn leakage→helpfulness/independence coupling) are tested with two-sided paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, Cliff's δ, bootstrap 95% CIs, and a mixed-effects linear-probability model with crossed random intercepts for replicate and problem, using decision rules frozen before any confirmatory data existed. A separate seven-policy ablation (Opus-only, primary base) and a compute-fairness re-analysis (equalizing visible tutor turns per problem-replicate cell) test the robustness of the main findings.

## Results & Highlights

- **Manipulation checks split the joint claim on the primary base (Claude Sonnet 4.6, Opus judge)**: session-level answer leakage is 43.0% for ConvTutor vs. 6.4% for PedTutor (P1 supported, paired diff +0.365, 95% CI [+0.217, +0.516], 9/10 replicate pairs), but judged helpfulness does **not** differ significantly (P2 not supported: Δ=-0.094, Cliff's δ=-0.10, p=.160), so the pre-registered joint claim J1 fails even though independence trends in the predicted direction but is inconclusive (P3: Δ=-0.076, CI [-0.189, +0.021]).
- **Helpfulness and pedagogy diverge sharply under the same judge on the same turns**: judged pedagogy averages 2.578 for ConvTutor vs. 4.299 for PedTutor — a *perfect* rank separation (Cliff's |δ|=1.0: every PedTutor replicate scores above every ConvTutor replicate) — sitting beside a helpfulness contrast that is not significant (|δ|=0.10) on the identical turns, which the paper frames as the central "failure mode" result rather than a manipulation check alone.
- **The helpfulness ordering is judge-contingent, reversing on 2 of 3 bases**: on GPT-5.5, Opus rates ConvTutor>PedTutor (Δ=-0.376, δ=-0.74, p=.0020) while the second judge GPT-5.6 Sol rates the opposite direction (Δ=+0.086, δ=+0.70, p=.0156) — both significant and opposite in sign. On Sonnet, Opus finds a nonsignificant contrast (δ=-0.10, p=.160) while Sol finds a significant reversal (δ=+0.84, p=.0039). Only on Gemini do the two judges agree in direction (δ=+0.67 vs. +0.65). By contrast, the pedagogy gap retains its sign under both judges everywhere a difference is detected, and neither judge detects a pedagogy difference on Gemini (a low-leakage boundary case: 11.3% ConvTutor vs. 7.6% PedTutor leakage, δ=+0.32, p=.625).
- **A deterministic, judge-invariant coupling anchors the comparison**: a leaky turn predicts a higher Opus helpfulness rating (+0.303, 95% CI [+0.169, +0.437], p=9.1×10⁻⁶) and deterministically predicts *less* independent student work on the following turn on every tutor base (Sonnet β=-0.386, p=5.6×10⁻¹²; GPT-5.5 β=-0.535, p=2.1×10⁻³²; Gemini β=-0.592, p=9.1×10⁻²¹); descriptively, leaky turns are rated 4.95 vs. 4.67 for non-leaky turns and are followed by a reasoning attempt only 33% of the time vs. 73% for non-leaky turns.
- **Ablation and compute-fairness checks confirm the effect isn't an artifact**: across seven Sonnet-base policy variants (ConvTutor baseline/no-final-answer/Socratic; PedTutor full/no-cascade/no-gate/no-tracker), judged pedagogy spans 2.3 points while judged helpfulness stays within a tight 0.25-point band (4.70–4.95) — and a prompt-only Socratic instruction *outscores* the full routed PedTutor structure on judged pedagogy (paired diff +0.565, 95% CI [+0.348, +0.817], δ=+0.96), meaning structure is not required to earn the pedagogy score. Re-running the analysis on a matched visible-turn budget (PedTutor naturally uses 22.3 in-window tutor turns/session vs. ConvTutor's 13.5, paired +8.8, CI [+4.6, +12.6]) leaves both J2 coupling legs significant (+0.295, p=2.4×10⁻⁴; -0.355, p=7.5×10⁻⁸).
- **Cross-judge agreement itself is base-dependent and inverts on Gemini**: pooled Spearman ρ is high on pedagogy (ρ=.740, quadratic-weighted κ=.653) but modest on helpfulness (ρ=.372, κ=.243); on Sonnet/GPT-5.5 the judges agree more on pedagogy than helpfulness (ρ=.754/.847 vs. .418/.205), but this order reverses on Gemini (ρ=.431 pedagogy vs. .585 helpfulness) — helpfulness agreement is weakest precisely on the base (GPT-5.5) where the two judges' contrasts are significant and opposite.

No indication of a marquee senior NLP/education-research author, and the venue/review status is not stated in the PDF provided.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: the core question ("does general helpfulness track pedagogy?") is not new in spirit, but the controlled design — identical frozen weights, policy-only manipulation, a second prospectively-specified judge, and two deterministic non-LLM process measures — is a genuinely careful instantiation, rarer than typical LLM-as-judge critique papers that only show inter-judge disagreement without a policy-level ground truth to check against.

**Rigor**: the pre-registration (Table 1, decision rules fixed before data existed) and the honest reporting of a *failed* joint claim (P2/J1 not supported on the primary base) are strong positives — many papers would have quietly reframed the hypothesis after seeing results. However, the study's own "Scope and boundaries" section admits real limits: no human validation of either judge, a single algebra/mixture-problem domain, one weak simulated student (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, chosen and screened via a somewhat ad hoc calibration process described in Appendix A.5 involving rejecting two other candidate models for "reaching ceiling"), and only ten replicates per condition per base, meaning "session-level null rules out only large effects (d≳1)" by the authors' own admission — the P2/P3 nulls are consistent with real but modest effects the design lacked power to detect, not necessarily strong evidence of "no policy difference."

**Reproducibility**: code is released, the problem instrument and rubrics are appended verbatim (Appendices D–F), and measurement definitions are specified before analysis — a good-faith transparency effort. But the frozen student model is served through "a single provider route reached via OpenRouter and pinned to Groq with fallbacks disabled, so that no substitute provider can be swapped in mid-run," and the paper itself notes "the provider's quantization is not reported" — an externally uncontrolled dependency that could complicate exact replication.

**Significance**: the leakage→independence coupling (judge-invariant, holding across three bases, an ablation, and a compute-fairness reanalysis) is the most durable finding and is a useful, concrete argument for pairing pedagogy-targeted rubrics with cheap deterministic process filters in tutor evaluation or reward-model pipelines — the paper explicitly flags this as future work ("we ran no fine-tuning, however, and their value for reward training remains an open empirical question").

**Red flags / paper-specific weaknesses**: (1) PedTutor is constructed on exactly the same four tutoring principles that the pedagogy rubric scores, so the pedagogy contrast is self-acknowledged as "a manipulation check rather than independent evidence" — the perfect δ=1.0 separation is partly definitional, not a surprising discovery about an independently-motivated policy; (2) the ablation's most striking result (a prompt-only Socratic instruction beating the full routed PedTutor structure on judged pedagogy) undercuts the paper's own PedTutor design as an exemplar of "good pedagogy," suggesting the pedagogy rubric itself may reward surface Socratic phrasing over actual structural safeguards; (3) probe accuracy is explicitly reported as "secondary and largely uninformative" because the student's growing context window lets it retrieve worked examples rather than demonstrate durable learning, so the study measures judged/process proxies, not learning outcomes; (4) the two LLM judges disagree enough (helpfulness ρ as low as .205–.418 on two of three bases) that "judge-contingent" is arguably an understatement — on GPT-5.5 the two judges are significantly opposite, which is a fairly damning finding for anyone using a single LLM judge to rank real tutoring systems.

## Strengths

- Genuine pre-registration with frozen decision rules, including honest disclosure of a non-supported joint claim rather than post hoc reframing.
- Clean causal isolation: identical frozen weights across ConvTutor/PedTutor/cold, so any measured difference is attributable to the policy layer alone.
- Two deterministic, rule-based (non-LLM) process measures that are immune to judge idiosyncrasy and remain informative exactly where the judged signal fails.
- Replication across three tutor bases (Claude, GPT, Gemini) and two independently-specified judges, plus a seven-policy ablation and a compute-fairness re-analysis — a level of robustness-checking uncommon in LLM tutoring papers.
- Code and full problem/rubric artifacts released for scrutiny.

## Limitations

- Single narrow domain (mixture/weighted-average algebra word problems, 19 items) — generalization to other subjects or open-ended tasks is untested.
- One weak simulated student model (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct via a single pinned provider route); no human learners, so no claim about actual learning outcomes is made or supported.
- Small sample size (10 replicates/condition/base, n=90 confirmatory sessions) limits statistical power; several pre-registered contrasts (P2, P3) are inconclusive rather than confirmed null.
- No human validation of either LLM judge (Opus 4.8 or GPT-5.6 Sol) against real human pedagogical judgment — the entire "ground truth" for pedagogy is itself another LLM rubric.
- Gemini's forced minimum-reasoning setting means the third base replication is not a pure model swap, muddying cross-base comparisons.
- PedTutor costs roughly 1.65x the visible tutor turns of ConvTutor (22.3 vs. 13.5/session); while a compute-fairness re-analysis is reported, it necessarily reduces the analyzed sample and further weakens already-marginal contrasts.
- Leakage-independence coupling is observational (leakage is not randomized at the turn level), so the paper explicitly avoids causal language — a caveat readers should not skip past.

## Relevance

该论文对"用LLM裁判评估AI导师/助手"这一评测范式给出了一个方法论警示：通用有帮助性评分不仅可能漏检教学法质量差异，其排序结论本身还高度依赖具体裁判模型，这与本仓库长期关注的"LLM-as-judge可靠性、奖励模型构造有效性"议题高度相关，其提出的确定性过程指标（答案泄露、下一轮独立性）思路也可迁移到其他"防止捷径/防止过度迎合"的评测设计中。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the helpfulness/pedagogy divergence and the judge-contingent reversal replicate with real (non-simulated) students and human pedagogical raters, rather than an LLM judge scoring an LLM-simulated student?
- If a reward model were fine-tuned using the deterministic leakage/independence penalties as auxiliary signals (the paper explicitly leaves this untested), would it produce tutoring policies that a general helpfulness judge would then also rate as better — closing the gap the paper identifies?
- Why does the Socratic prompt-only ConvTutor variant outscore the fully-routed PedTutor structure on judged pedagogy — is the pedagogy rubric partly rewarding surface linguistic markers (hedging, questions) rather than the underlying assistance-dilemma/generation-effect structure it was designed to detect?
- Would a stronger (less artificially weak) simulated student change the leakage-to-independence coupling, given that the current student is calibrated to be barely capable of solving problems unaided?
- How sensitive are the cross-judge reversals to the specific choice of GPT-5.6 Sol as the second judge — would a third, differently-trained judge (e.g., an open-weight model) agree with Opus, Sol, or neither?
