> **一句话总结：** 论文构建了跨平台（Web/Windows/Ubuntu/移动）的computer-use agent轨迹裁判基准OSReward，发现27个VLM裁判普遍存在"宽松偏差"（把未完成任务误判为成功），在困难子集OSReward-Hard上最强的Claude-Opus-4-8准确率也从89.7%暴跌到69.7%，随后训练出的开源奖励模型OS-Shepherd-9B/35B以30-60倍更低成本逼近商业裁判水平。

> **TL;DR:** OSReward is a 1,019-trajectory, human-gold, cross-platform (web/Windows/Ubuntu/mobile) benchmark for auditing VLM-as-judge reliability on computer-use agent (CUA) trajectories. Across 27 judges, the best model (Claude-Opus-4-8) hits 89.7% binary accuracy on the full set but collapses to 69.7% on the OSReward-Hard subset (a 30/70 success/fail split built from cases annotators themselves disagreed on), and the mean judge falls to 52% — barely above chance. The dominant error, across every model family, is a "leniency bias": accepting an incomplete task as a success because judges lean on the agent's own narrated thought/action text rather than the screenshots (dropping text costs 7.2pp accuracy on average and flips 22.7% of verdicts, vs <0.5pp / 5-7% for visual ablations). To close the cost-reliability gap, the authors curate OS-Shepherd-100K (100K reasoning-annotated judgments distilled from 321K judge instances via cross-model agreement filtering) and train OS-Shepherd-9B/35B-A3B, which reach 86.1%/85.6% full-set accuracy and 60.2%/62.7% on OSReward-Hard — matching mid-tier commercial judges at roughly $1.36 vs $45-100 per 1,000 trajectories (30-60x cheaper), and the de-biasing transfers to held-out benchmarks (OSWorld, AndroidWorld, WebArena).

## 核心贡献

- **OSReward基准**：从零搭建跨平台数据基础设施（Web、Windows、Ubuntu、Android），采集1019条人工标注的"黄金"轨迹，涵盖4个模型家族执行的真实应用/网站任务，并派生出OSReward-Hard（284条高难度、30/70成功/失败split的挑战集）和OSReward-Multi（440条含对齐度/效率细粒度评分的成功轨迹）。
- **迄今最全面的VLM裁判评测**：对27个模型（含GPT-5系列、Claude Opus/Sonnet/Haiku、Gemini、Qwen3.5系列、Kimi-K2.5、Doubao、Intern-S系列等）进行基准测试，发现所有裁判家族共享同一种主导性错误——把"任务未完成"误判为"成功"（over-accept，占所有错误的三分之二以上），且该偏差在OSReward-Hard上进一步放大。
- **机制分析**：通过输入消融实验证明，裁判的判决主要依赖agent自身的思维/动作文本叙述而非截图视觉信息——去掉文本描述比去掉/替换视觉帧的影响大10倍以上；同时发现"让模型多想"（thinking模式）或多裁判投票集成都无法系统性修复该偏差（裁判之间高度趋同，Cohen's κ≈0.71）。
- **OS-Shepherd开源奖励模型与数据**：发布OS-Shepherd-100K（10万条推理标注的训练语料，来自32.1万条裁判实例的跨模型一致性过滤），并用两阶段（SFT+GRPO强化学习专门瞄准"虚假成功"漏洞）训练出OS-Shepherd-9B和35B-A3B，在准确率-成本帕累托前沿上大幅逼近商业裁判前沿，且去偏能力可迁移到OSWorld、AndroidWorld、WebArena三个未参与训练的基准。

## Key Contributions

- **OSReward benchmark**: A from-scratch, cross-platform (web, Windows, Ubuntu, Android) data-collection infrastructure with realistic, "lived-in" environments (seeded files, distractor content, real logged-in accounts) producing 1,019 human-annotated gold trajectories from four agent backbone families (Claude, Gemini, Kimi, Qwen), with rigorous 3-annotator + meta-review labeling (~800 human hours). Three views are released: the full set, OSReward-Hard (284 trajectories, deliberately raised to a 30/70 success/fail split from cases annotators themselves split on), and OSReward-Multi (440 successful trajectories with alignment/efficiency sub-ratings).
- **Broadest VLM-judge evaluation to date**: 27 models spanning OpenAI (GPT-4o through GPT-5.5), Anthropic (Claude Haiku/Sonnet/Opus 4.5-4.8), Gemini (2.5/3/3.1/3.5), Qwen3.5 (dense and VL variants, 8B-397B), Kimi-K2.5, Doubao-2.0-Lite, and Intern-S1/S2 — all judged under one fixed protocol (last N=5 states, no tool access, no task-specific harness).
- **The leniency-bias finding**: every judge family shares one dominant failure mode — accepting an incomplete task as a success (over-accepts outnumber over-rejects ~3:1 pooled, ~2:1 for the strongest judges) — and this bias widens sharply on the hard set (fail recall spreads from near-zero to 77%).
- **Causal ablation of what drives a verdict**: visual perturbations (dropping/reordering trailing screenshots, removing the red click marker, varying frame count 1-16) move accuracy by less than a point; removing the per-step thought/action text costs 7.2pp on average and flips 22.7% of individual verdicts (vs 5-7% for any visual change), showing judges are reading the agent's narrative, not verifying the screen.
- **OS-Shepherd data and models**: an open, reasoning-annotated 100K-sample training corpus (OS-Shepherd-100K, distilled from 321K raw judge instances via cross-model, high-agreement filtering across 5 model families) and two trained reward models (9B, 35B-A3B) that are open-weight, self-hostable, and specifically RL-tuned (GRPO) against the false-success failure mode.

## Methodology

**Benchmark construction (OSReward).** The authors reject reusing existing agent benchmarks' trajectories, arguing this would confound judge errors with flaws in the underlying runs and inherited verifier noise. Instead they build dedicated infrastructure across four platforms: (1) **Web** — Playwright-driven, isolated Chromium sessions per rollout at 1920×1080 (with higher-resolution variants), purely vision-driven action space, live websites plus a few self-hosted mirror sites for login-gated tasks; (2) **Windows** — a "lived-in" machine with ~20 everyday/professional apps (IDEs, media/3D/DB tools, some pre-logged-in), rollouts up to 100 steps, switching between 2K/4K resolutions; (3) **Ubuntu** — ~30 applications, a typed pool of hundreds of real files across ~20 types, GUI-only and GUI+CLI action spaces to capture code-mixed agent behavior; (4) **Mobile** — Android emulator seeded with files, usage records, databases, distractor content (decoy messages, look-alike files), and signed-in accounts.

Task instructions (~1,500 candidates written, ~800 survive) are authored by annotators exploring the live environments, then peer-screened by a different annotator to remove ambiguous/ungrounded/unanswerable items — deliberately including open-ended tasks beyond what any rule-based verifier could check. Each surviving instruction is executed by 1-3 agents drawn from Claude/Gemini/Kimi/Qwen backbones (ReAct-style loops), producing both real successes and real failures. A rule-based pre-filter discards runs with collection artifacts (bot blocks, network failures, frozen executions) before any human effort is spent, so a FAIL in the benchmark reflects the agent, not the environment.

**Annotation.** Every surviving trajectory is independently labeled by three annotators who read the full multimodal context (every screenshot, thought, action) — not just the final screen — under a strict rule that a correct final state reached without genuine task completion is still FAIL. Disagreements escalate to meta-review by two senior reviewers who deliberate to a final verdict (not majority vote). SUCCESS trajectories additionally get alignment (2-level) and efficiency (3-level) sub-ratings for OSReward-Multi; FAIL trajectories get multi-label failure-taxonomy tags (reasoning-and-planning, action, perception, memory).

**Judging protocol and metrics.** Every judge reads the trajectory's last N=5 states (screenshot + per-step reasoning/action text) with no tool access or step-level supervision, and outputs a binary SUCCESS/FAIL verdict (plus alignment/efficiency ratings on OSReward-Multi). Metrics: binary accuracy, success recall (sRec, share of true successes accepted — low means too strict), fail recall (fRec, share of true failures caught — low means too lenient), and balanced accuracy (mean of the two, immune to the 43/57 class skew).

**Ablation analysis (§5).** The authors systematically perturb (a) visual input — screenshot count/selection, presence of the red click-marker; (b) text input — removing chain-of-thought, removing action text; (c) inference-time settings — thinking effort, temperature/resampling, and multi-judge ensembling (majority vote among a pool, measured via pairwise Cohen's κ).

**OS-Shepherd training (§6).** Training data is built via an ensemble-agreement pipeline (Fig. 10): raw instructions (~100K) are filtered, executed by agents, quality-filtered, combined with open-source trajectory corpora (OpenCUA, OpenMobile, ScaleCUA) and a small self-collected OS-Genesis split, yielding ~82K final trajectories. Each trajectory is scored by an ensemble of strong VLM judges (varying both the judge model — e.g., Gemini-3.1-Pro, Kimi-K2.5, Gemini-3-Pro — and the screenshot setting) across 321K judge instances; only trajectories where diverse strong judges reach high agreement are retained (~85% pass the agreement filter), yielding 96.6K samples for OS-Shepherd-100K. The retained "reasoning" response (not just the label) is imitated from one consistently-agreeing strong judge to avoid destabilizing training with mixed reasoning styles. Training is two-stage on Qwen3.5 (9B and 35B-A3B) backbones: an SFT stage that corrects the base model's near-total leniency, followed by a GRPO RL stage that specifically mines and targets residual false-success cases (the error SFT alone leaves behind), since catching false successes is a "recall" skill that benefits from RL-style exploration more than a fixed SFT target.

## Results & Highlights

- **Accuracy ceiling and collapse on hard cases (Table 1, Fig. 1a)**: On the full OSReward set, Claude-Opus-4-8 leads at 89.7% binary accuracy (91.1 sRec / 88.9 fRec), with GPT-5.5 (89.5%) and Claude-Opus-4-6 (89.5% Acc, 90.2 BalAcc — the top BalAcc) close behind. On **OSReward-Hard**, Claude-Opus-4-8 drops to 69.7% accuracy (69.8 sRec / 69.7 fRec, the single best balanced result), GPT-5.5 drops to 67.3% (66.3/67.7), and the mean judge across all 27 models falls to just 52% — barely above the 50% floor a constant predictor would score on the 30/70 split.
- **Leniency bias is the dominant, shared failure**: pooled across all judges, over-accepts (accepting an incomplete task) outnumber over-rejects roughly 3:1, narrowing to ~2:1 only for the strongest judges (Fig. 7). On the weak end, **Qwen3-VL-30B** — the lowest-accuracy judge in the study at 69.4% full-set accuracy — achieves 99.8 sRec but only 46.3 fRec on the full set, and its fRec collapses to just 1.5% on OSReward-Hard (Table 1), meaning it accepts virtually every hard failure as a success. At the strict extreme, GPT-5.2 sacrifices success recall (sRec 73.0% full-set, 30.2% on Hard) to catch more failures (fRec 92.2%/77.3%).
- **Text vs. vision ablation (Fig. 9, §5.2)**: dropping the per-step thought+action text costs 7.2pp accuracy on average (with much larger web-specific drops, since typed strings carry intent screenshots cannot encode) and flips 22.7% of individual verdicts — roughly 3x the flip rate of any visual-input change (e.g., swapping trailing screenshots or removing the click marker flips only 5-7% of verdicts and moves aggregate accuracy by less than half a point). Dropping chain-of-thought alone while keeping actions is milder (1.8pp, 11.6% flipped).
- **Ensembling and thinking do not fix reliability (§5.3)**: re-running the *same* judge at temperature 0.7 already flips 6-9% of verdicts — more churn than the "harmless" visual ablations — while judges pairwise agree with Cohen's κ≈0.71 and herd on the same hard trajectories, so a top-3 majority vote beats the best single judge by only about a point at several times the inference cost; an oracle that accepts any pooled judge's correct verdict reaches 99.2% (the pool almost always contains a correct verdict), motivating a case for confidence-weighted soft labels over majority voting as future work.
- **Cost-accuracy tradeoff (Fig. 2, §5.4)**: on the full set the strict-lenient tradeoff looks benign (~3pp accuracy for a 42x price cut), but on OSReward-Hard it is severe — Claude-Opus-4-8 costs ~$100 to judge the full evaluation set at 69.7% Hard accuracy, GPT-5.5 costs $45 at 67.3%, while the cheapest sub-$3 judges fall below 50-57% accuracy on Hard.
- **OS-Shepherd results (Table 4)**: OS-Shepherd-9B improves from its Qwen3.5-9B base (76.7% full / 39.4% Hard accuracy) to 86.1% full / 60.2% Hard accuracy, moving from the bottom third of the field into the mid-tier commercial band; it catches 57.6% of hard false successes (fRec on Hard) vs. the base model's 14.1%. OS-Shepherd-35B-A3B reaches 85.6% full / 62.7% Hard (64.3 BalAcc, the best OS-Shepherd result), only modestly better than the 9B despite 4x the parameters ("scale adds little; what transfers is the recipe"). At $1.36 per 1,000 trajectories, OS-Shepherd-9B is roughly a third cheaper than its nearest commercial cost-tier peer while leading it on OSReward-Hard, and a rough training-scale cost estimate (200 updates × 16 batch × 16 rollouts = 51,200 judge calls) is ~$4,000 with Claude-Opus-4-8 or ~$2,300 with GPT-5.5, vs. ~$68 with OS-Shepherd-9B — a 30-60x reduction.
- **Generalization to independently-built benchmarks (Fig. 11, §7)**: against each benchmark's own human-written verifier, the best judges only approach ~90% agreement on mobile, come within ~6pp on web, and fall well short on desktop. OS-Shepherd models are the best *open* judges on OSWorld and AndroidWorld and sit in the frontier cluster on WebArena, beating every general open model up to Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (~44x the 9B's size) on all three, with the de-biasing (failure recall) — not raw accuracy — being what transfers.
- **Multi-axis quality grading (Table 2, §4.5) is markedly weaker than binary judging**: even the best judge (GPT-5.5) falls from ~90% binary accuracy to 66.7% multi-axis AUC (63.5% macro-recall), and the gap is worst on the alignment axis specifically, where judges default to the top rating almost regardless of the run.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The core contribution — systematically measuring VLM-judge reliability specifically for long-horizon, cross-platform CUA trajectories, rather than reusing existing benchmarks' rollouts — is a genuinely useful and underexplored angle; prior work (cited: CUARewardBench, AgentRewardBench, WebRewardBench-style efforts) either stays single-platform or inherits noisy labels from imperfect rule-based verifiers, a limitation this paper explicitly targets by building fresh infrastructure and doing 3-annotator + meta-review labeling.

**Rigor**: The ablation methodology is a strength — the paper doesn't just report a leaderboard, it isolates *why* judges fail via controlled input perturbations (text vs. vision) and quantifies the noise floor via same-judge resampling at temperature 0.7 (6-9% verdict churn), which is a rigorous way to contextualize how much of the ablation-induced flip rate is "real" signal vs. inherent stochasticity. The explicit reporting of a concrete weak baseline is notable and paper-specific: Qwen3-VL-30B's fail recall on OSReward-Hard collapses to 1.5% (Table 1) — the paper does not shy away from showing an open model that is essentially non-functional as a hard-case judge, rather than cherry-picking only favorable open baselines.

**A missing ablation the paper itself flags**: §4.3 explicitly states "prompting the judge to verify completion explicitly may help more than the ensembling we evaluate ... we leave this to future work" — i.e., the single most promising and cheapest potential fix (an explicit anti-leniency prompt) is never actually tried in the paper, despite the paper's own causal analysis (§5.2) pointing directly at it. This is a real gap: the paper diagnoses the disease precisely but doesn't test the most obvious low-cost remedy before jumping to full RL training of a new model.

**Reproducibility concern / data quirk**: OS-Shepherd-100K's "reasoning" targets are imitated from a *single* strong judge's response style even when the retained label is agreed upon by an ensemble ("mixing styles freely destabilized early training"), meaning the model learns to imitate one judge's narrative style; the paper does not deeply probe whether this reintroduces exactly the kind of single-judge stylistic bias the ensemble-agreement filter was designed to avoid at the label level.

**Significance**: The cost result (30-60x cheaper, matching mid-tier commercial judges on OSReward-Hard) is a real, quantified advance for anyone doing CUA data curation or RL at scale, though it's worth being clear-eyed that OS-Shepherd still trails the frontier by a full ~7-9pp of Hard-set accuracy (60-64% vs. 69.7% for Claude-Opus-4-8) — the paper is honest about this ("does not yet match frontier accuracy, but closes most of the gap at a fraction of the scale") rather than overclaiming parity.

**Red flags**: None severe. The benchmark's failure taxonomy and the OSReward-Hard construction rely on the annotators' own disagreement as the selection criterion for "hard," which is a reasonable but somewhat circular operationalization of difficulty (hard = cases where humans and/or models disagree) rather than an independent difficulty measure; the paper is transparent about this design choice though.

## Strengths

- Purpose-built, realistic, cross-platform data infrastructure (not reused benchmark rollouts) directly addresses a genuine confound in prior VLM-judge studies.
- Rigorous 3-annotator + meta-review human labeling process (~800 hours) with an explicit strict rule distinguishing "correct outcome" from "verified completion."
- Causal ablation methodology (text vs. vision, resampling noise floor) that identifies a specific, actionable mechanism (over-reliance on agent narrative) behind the leniency bias, rather than just reporting a leaderboard.
- Delivers a practical, fully open solution (OS-Shepherd-100K + two model sizes) with demonstrated out-of-distribution transfer to three independently built benchmarks (OSWorld, AndroidWorld, WebArena).
- Honest reporting of remaining gaps (frontier accuracy not fully matched, multi-axis grading much weaker than binary judging, ensembling doesn't help) rather than declaring the reliability problem solved.

## Limitations

- OS-Shepherd's Hard-set accuracy (60.2-62.7%) still trails the frontier (69.7% for Claude-Opus-4-8) by 7-9pp, so it is a cost-effective approximation, not a full replacement, for the most reliable commercial judges.
- Multi-axis (alignment/efficiency) quality grading remains weak across the entire field, including OS-Shepherd, with the worst calibration on the alignment axis — a reward signal richer than binary success/fail is not yet solved.
- The explicit-completion-verification prompt intervention that the paper's own analysis suggests should help most is never tested.
- OS-Shepherd-100K's imitated reasoning target comes from a single judge's style per sample, which could reintroduce narrower biases than the ensemble-agreement label filtering alone would suggest.
- Judging protocol is fixed to N=5 trailing states with no tool access or step-level supervision; how reliability changes under richer inference-time tool use or longer context is not explored.

## Relevance

该工作直接切中"用VLM做computer-use agent奖励信号/自动评测"这一实践痛点，其发现的"宽松偏差"和"文本主导判决"机制对任何依赖VLM-as-judge做GUI agent数据筛选、RLHF训练或轨迹自动评测的研究都有直接参考价值；开源的OS-Shepherd模型和100K训练语料也为低成本自建奖励模型提供了可复用的方案。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would a simple, explicit "verify the task was actually completed, not just claimed" prompt addition close a meaningful fraction of the leniency gap without any additional training, as the paper's own §4.3 suggests but never tests?
- How much of OS-Shepherd's residual style bias comes from imitating a single judge's reasoning text per training sample, and would a soft-label or multi-teacher distillation approach (as the paper proposes for future ensembling work) meaningfully change either accuracy or transfer?
- Would extending the judging window beyond N=5 trailing states, or granting the judge tool access to inspect the environment state directly (rather than only reading text+screenshots), reduce the observed leniency bias, given that the paper shows judges barely use the visual channel at all?
- Since Windows was identified as the hardest platform to judge and mobile the easiest (Fig. 8), what specific properties of Windows trajectories (resolution switching, longer 100-step runs, CLI-mixed action spaces) drive that gap, and would platform-specific fine-tuning close it further than the unified recipe used here?
- How does OS-Shepherd's reliability degrade (or hold) on agent backbones or action idioms not represented in its five training model families (Claude, Gemini, GPT, Kimi, Qwen), given that OSReward itself samples only four backbone families for its gold trajectories?
