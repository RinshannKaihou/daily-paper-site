> **一句话总结：** 论文用 99,952 条带 rubric 的评测样本和 RewardBench 2 上的四档 Qwen3 奖励模型做对照实验，发现把领域专精塞进评判者的权重（八个按标准族切分的 LoRA judgelet）会让锁定测试集准确率从单体模型的 76.38% 掉到 66.34%（-10.05 点，5% 风险下可审计覆盖率从 24.44% 暴跌到 5.43%），但把专精放到"是否可以信任/是否要升级到更大模型"的路由决策上（一个 14 维输入的逻辑回归 risklet），却能让 0.6B→4B→8B 级联在仅用 8B 模型 41.5% 参数计算量的情况下达到 89.40% 准确率，比单独用 8B（84.75%）高 4.66 点。
>
> **TL;DR:** Using 99,952 rubric-conditioned Feedback Collection examples and a Skywork-Reward-V2 Qwen3 0.6B–8B ladder on RewardBench 2, the paper shows that splitting judge *training* into eight criterion-family LoRA adapters ("judgelets") costs 10.05 accuracy points versus one shared LoRA judge (66.34% vs. 76.38% locked-test accuracy) and collapses audited 5%-risk coverage from 24.44% to 5.43% — a loss not explained by capacity (a rank-64 single adapter matching the bank's parameter count still hits 77.50%) or under-training (extra optimizer steps only partially recover it, to 72.56%), but almost fully closed by initializing the family adapters from the shared trained judge (76.85%, +19.94 points over learning-rate-matched scratch training). In sharp contrast, specializing the *deferral* decision helps: a small logistic "risklet" correctness head routes RewardBench 2 examples through a 0.6B→4B→8B cascade and reaches 89.40% accuracy at 0.415 normalized parameter compute versus 84.75% for the 8B judge alone (compute = 1.0), while raw/isotonic margin-based cascades stall near 84.8% accuracy and still burn ≥0.94 compute.

## 核心贡献

- 提出并区分了两种"把领域专精放在哪里"的架构选择：放进评判者的可训练权重（criterion-family LoRA "judgelet" 银行），还是放进决定"这次判断是否可信、要不要升级评判者"的路由/延迟拒绝规则（risklet 级联）。
- 通过一套受控实验（response-only 对照、跨簇 rubric 替换反事实干预、锁定的 criterion-shift 划分）证明：给评判者正确的 rubric 确有帮助（+2.11 点），但把训练语料按 8 个标准族切分成独立 LoRA 专家，会显著损害评判准确率和可审计覆盖率，且这一损失既不是参数容量不足（rank-64 单适配器复现不了该损失），也不是优化步数不够（把每个专家训练步数拉齐到 4000 步仍恢复不到一半），而主要来自"共享判断能力训练"的丧失——从已训练好的单体 judge 初始化各家族适配器几乎能补回全部差距。
- 在 RewardBench 2 的 0.6B/1.7B/4B/8B 四档 Qwen3 奖励模型上验证：专精化"何时该把判断交给更大模型"这件事（用一个只看分数几何特征的逻辑回归 risklet）能真正改善准确率-算力前沿，且这种收益依赖于"廉价的早期模型确有互补错误、且代价确实便宜"这一条件——在 OpenAssistant DeBERTa base/large 这对模型上，级联虽仍能提升准确率但反而更费参数计算量。

## Key Contributions

- **A controlled rubric study with locked criterion shift**: isolates whether an evaluator actually uses rubric text (via a cross-cluster counterfactual rubric swap) versus exploiting the response alone as a shortcut, using a response-only control and a criterion-holdout ("shift_locked") partition that prevents any criterion string from leaking across splits.
- **A granularity/capacity/optimization study that separates sparse deployment from the statistical cost of fragmenting evaluator supervision**: compares K∈{1,4,8} criterion-family LoRA judges against a capacity-matched rank-64 monolith and against optimization controls (matched update counts, matched learning rate, shared vs. scratch initialization) to pin down *why* the 8-family bank underperforms.
- **An evaluation-specific deferral study**: builds a logistic "risklet" correctness head that routes RewardBench 2 examples through a 0.6B–4B–8B Qwen3 reward-model cascade without touching any reward score, evaluated against four confidence-routing baselines (raw margin, isotonic margin, geometry-only logistic probe) across 20 locked repartitions with exact one-sided 95% risk audits.

## Methodology

The paper studies two nested routing decisions in an "evaluation control layer" (Figure 1): (a) which criterion-family LoRA adapter ("judgelet") should score a rubric-conditioned response, and (b) whether a judge's decision should be released or forwarded to a larger judge in a size cascade.

**Direct-assessment (judgelet) setup**: Built from the Prometheus Feedback Collection (99,952 examples, 996 criteria; split into 47,638 training / 9,441 calibration / 9,597 development / 12,580 locked-test / 20,696 criterion-holdout examples) plus 1,000 external Feedback-Bench examples (122 excluded from training as held-criteria). A deterministic hash reserves complete criteria for `shift_locked` so no response tied to a held-out criterion ever appears in training/calibration/development/ordinary test — preventing leakage across splits. All judgelets share a frozen 0.6B Skywork-Reward-V2 Qwen3 base with its scalar reward head replaced by a five-class ordinal score head, trained via rank-8 LoRA on attention/feed-forward projections (5.05M trainable parameters, 0.84% of the model). A K-means router over TF–IDF (word+bigram) features of criterion text assigns each rubric to one of K∈{1,4,8} criterion families; K=1 is the monolithic baseline. Five specialization/optimization controls are trained to separate confounds (Table 4): scratch training at the primary learning rate (2e-4), scratch training at a matched lower rate (5e-5) for the same total family updates as the monolith's aggregate budget, a "cycle every family to exactly 500 updates" control (4,000 total, an optimization-parity check), and a "shared initialization" bank that starts each family adapter from the already-trained rank-8 monolith and runs one more family epoch at 5e-5. A capacity control (rank-64 monolith, ~40.38M active parameters) roughly matches the 8-family bank's aggregate stored capacity (40.41M).

**Risk-routing/calibration**: Three release policies are compared — a single global confidence threshold, one threshold per criterion family, and a Bonferroni-adjusted family policy — each selected by scanning calibration risk-coverage curves and then audited on untouched locked-test splits using one-sided 95% Wilson upper bounds on risk.

**Reward-model cascade setup**: Uses four sizes from the Skywork-Reward-V2 Qwen3 family (0.6B/1.7B/4B/8B) evaluated on RewardBench 2 (1,865 tasks, 8,977 candidate completions; 1,763 non-tie tasks used for cascade accuracy). For each non-terminal stage, a logistic "risklet" (14 inputs: largest scores, gap, range, mean, variance, candidate count, winning position, one-hot skill category, plus intercept) predicts whether that stage's top-ranked candidate is already correct; if so, the example exits early without ever changing the reward-model ranking, otherwise it is forwarded to the next larger model. Fitting, threshold selection, and evaluation use three disjoint partitions across 20 locked train/calibration/test repartitions, and normalized parameter compute (Eq. 3) is used as an architecture-comparison proxy (not energy/dollar cost) — running the 8B model on every example is defined as compute = 1.0. Four confidence-routing baselines are compared against the risklet: raw margin, isotonic-calibrated margin, and a geometry-only logistic probe that strips the skill one-hot (testing whether domain identity adds signal beyond score geometry).

## Results & Highlights

- **Rubric helps, but the effect is modest and truncation-sensitive**: on the locked test set, the full-context monolithic rubric judge (rank 8) reaches 76.38% accuracy vs. 74.27% for a response-only control (Table 1) — a +2.11-point gain (95% paired-bootstrap interval +1.48 to +2.75), with MAE improving from 0.281 to 0.250. Truncating the same adapter's input to 512 tokens (vs. 1,024) drops accuracy to 73.82%, statistically indistinguishable from the response-only control (−0.45 points, p=0.147) — showing the rubric benefit disappears once its text is mostly truncated.
- **Counterfactual rubric replacement isolates real rubric use**: swapping in an unrelated rubric from another TF-IDF cluster (holding response/task/label fixed) lowers locked-test accuracy from 76.38% to 73.72% (−2.66 points, 95% CI −3.26 to −2.09), below even the response-only baseline — ruling out an "extra benign context" explanation.
- **Fragmenting judge training into 8 LoRA experts is costly**: the eight-family judgelet bank scores 66.34% locked-test accuracy, 10.05 points below the rank-8 monolith (76.38%; criterion-bootstrap interval −10.87 to −9.22), with MAE rising from 0.250 to 0.375; at a 5% risk target, Bonferroni-audited coverage collapses from 24.44% (monolith) to 5.43% (8-family bank).
- **The loss is not explained by capacity or optimizer budget**: a capacity-matched rank-64 single adapter (40.38M active params, matching the bank's 40.41M stored capacity) scores 77.50% test accuracy — 11.16 points *better* than the 8-family bank (interval +10.28 to +12.07) — while cycling every family expert to exactly 4,000 total updates (vs. 1,494 for the primary bank) only partially recovers accuracy to 72.56% (still 3.82 points below the rank-8 monolith).
- **Shared pre-adaptation nearly closes the gap**: initializing the 8 family adapters from the already-trained rank-8 monolith (then running one more family epoch at LR 5e-5) reaches 76.85% test accuracy — 19.94 points above a learning-rate-matched scratch bank trained at the same 5e-5 rate for the same 1,494 updates (56.91%; 95% interval +18.88 to +21.02) — isolating "shared judgment pre-training" as the dominant recoverable factor rather than learning rate or step count alone.
- **Deferral specialization pays off on RewardBench 2**: across 20 locked repartitions, the risklet-routed 0.6B→4B→8B cascade attains 89.40% mean accuracy at 0.415 normalized parameter compute (Table 3), a 4.66-point gain over the 8B judge alone (84.75% accuracy, compute=1.0); the leaner risklet-routed 0.6B→8B two-stage cascade reaches 88.71% accuracy at just 0.360 compute. By contrast, raw-margin and isotonic-margin 4B→8B cascades plateau near 84.8% accuracy (84.81% and 84.84%) while still consuming 0.944–1.011 normalized compute — essentially no accuracy gain for near-full compute cost.
- **Cascades are not universally beneficial**: a diagnostic Prometheus rank-8/rank-64 adapter cascade accepts 27.98% of examples early but changes accuracy only from 77.50% to 77.52% while requiring 1.720 forward passes — showing that shared-base "complementary errors" do not automatically exist between arbitrarily paired evaluators, and that risklet routing is not a free lunch across every model pair (an OpenAssistant DeBERTa base/large pair also shows a genuine 8.64-point accuracy rescue but at 1.122 normalized compute — worse cost than the larger model alone, since the base model is already 42% of the large model's size).

## Authors & Credibility

Single-author preprint by Weining Zhang, Cheung Kong Graduate School of Business. The work is grounded in established public artifacts — the Prometheus/Prometheus 2 Feedback Collection and Feedback-Bench datasets (Kim et al., 2024a/b), the Skywork-Reward-V2 Qwen3 ladder (Liu et al., 2026), and RewardBench 2 (Malik et al., 2026) — and situates itself explicitly against related lines (AdaRubric, Trust-or-Escalate, SCOPE, Ask-a-Strong-LLM-Judge, MixLoRA) rather than claiming an unprecedented method. Full reproducibility details are given (seed 20260728, PyTorch 2.8.0, two RTX 5090 GPUs, training recipes in Table 4, artifact manifest with SHA-256 hashes), which is a strong credibility signal for a single-author empirical paper without an accompanying large-lab affiliation track record evident in the text.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The paper's contribution is not a new judge architecture but a controlled empirical dissection of *where* to place specialization — an architectural question that is often conflated in prior mixture-of-LoRA-experts and rubric-compilation work. The finding that a capacity-matched single rank-64 adapter (40.38M params) beats an 8-expert bank with equivalent stored capacity (40.41M, but only 5.05M active) by 11.16 points is a genuinely paper-specific and somewhat counterintuitive result: it directly rules out "not enough stored capacity" as the mechanism, pointing instead at fragmented shared-task training as the cause. Similarly, the risklet's near-identical accuracy to the "geometry-only" probe (removing the skill one-hot changes accuracy by only 0.03 points) is a specific, checkable finding that undercuts an obvious alternative explanation (that domain/skill identity is what a routing head is really learning).

**Rigor**: Strong use of disjoint calibration/development/test partitions, criterion-level bootstrap intervals (765 criterion IDs) rather than naive example-level CIs, an exact-audit protocol (one-sided 95% Wilson/Clopper–Pearson bounds) rather than treating adaptive threshold search as a valid finite-sample guarantee, and a genuinely orthogonal set of optimization controls (matched updates, matched learning rate, shared-vs-scratch init) that most mixture-of-experts ablations skip. A specific weak point the paper itself flags: the diagnostic Prometheus rank-8/rank-64 cascade example (27.98% early acceptance, accuracy essentially flat at 77.50%→77.52%) is included precisely as a negative control showing the cascade mechanism is not universally applicable — a level of self-skepticism papers proposing a "positive" cascade result often omit.

**Reproducibility**: High for the core claims — seed, hardware, package versions, and an artifact manifest with per-file SHA-256 hashes are documented in Appendix A, and the 20 RewardBench repartitions plus all 20-seed cascade outputs are stated to be included in the artifact. One caveat the authors state directly: the 20 repartitions "reuse the same tasks" so the reported 3.21–6.19-point cascade-gain range is a stability range across correlated splits, not an independent-sample confidence interval — a distinction the paper is careful to call out rather than overstate.

**Significance and red flags**: The paper's own limitations section is unusually candid: Feedback Collection is synthetic and a model can exploit its regularities without truly reading the rubric (the strong response-only control at 74.27% accuracy already shows most of the task is solvable from the response alone); criterion-shift holds out complete rubric strings but does not simulate an adversarially rewritten rubric or an actor-population shift; the compiler uses English-only lexical routing and a single base-model family; and normalized parameter compute is explicitly stated not to be a measurement of energy or dollar cost. The external validation set (Feedback-Bench) is not truly novel-criterion since all its criterion strings occur somewhere in the training corpus, which the authors flag by reporting the 203-example held-criteria external subset separately rather than treating the full external set as an independence test.

## Strengths

- Clean separation of two confounded design questions (specialize the judge's weights vs. specialize its release/deferral rule) into two independently controlled experiments on real, disjoint held-out data.
- Systematic elimination of alternative explanations for the judgelet-bank's underperformance (capacity, optimizer steps, learning rate) via matched controls, rather than asserting a single explanation.
- Honest inclusion of a negative-result cascade (Prometheus rank-8/rank-64, 27.98% early acceptance but only 0.02-point accuracy change) and an unfavorable-cost cascade (OpenAssistant base/large, accuracy gain but worse normalized compute) alongside the favorable Skywork ladder result.
- Rigorous, criterion-ID-level statistical treatment (paired bootstrap with criterion ID as resampling unit; exact one-sided binomial audits) rather than example-level intervals that would understate correlated-criterion variance.
- Full artifact and hyperparameter transparency (Table 4 training recipes, seed, hardware, SHA-256 manifest) supporting exact reproduction.

## Limitations

- Feedback Collection is synthetic, and the paper's own response-only control (74.27% accuracy without seeing the rubric at all) shows a large share of the task is solvable via response-only shortcuts, limiting how much the rubric-specific findings generalize to naturally occurring professional review data.
- Criterion-shift (`shift_locked`) tests generalization to unseen complete rubric strings but not to adversarially rewritten rules or a shifting population of raters/actors, so real-world policy drift is not directly evaluated.
- Feedback-Bench, the external validation set, is not a clean novel-criterion benchmark since all its criteria appear somewhere in the training corpus; the paper mitigates this by reporting the 203-example held-criteria subset separately, but the "external" framing should be read with that caveat.
- The router uses English-only lexical (TF-IDF) features and a single base-model family (Skywork-Reward-V2 Qwen3); dense semantic routing, multilingual criteria, joint multi-expert training, and other parameter-efficient adapter types could change the specialization tradeoff (explicitly acknowledged in Appendix B).
- The 20 RewardBench 2 repartitions reuse the same underlying task pool, so the reported cascade-gain stability range (3.21–6.19 points) reflects sensitivity to partition assignment rather than independent-sample uncertainty — the paper is explicit about this but it does cap how strongly the headline 4.66-point gain should be read as a population estimate.
- Retrospective, locked-split risk audits (Wilson/Clopper–Pearson bounds under an adaptive threshold scan) do not by themselves establish prospective guarantees under real distribution shift; the authors state high-impact deployments would need pre-specified risk control and human gates beyond what is tested here.

## Relevance

论文的核心结论——"评判者权重的专精化会牺牲共享判断训练、但把专精放进延迟拒绝/路由决策反而能改善准确率-算力前沿"——对设计 LLM-as-judge 系统、reward model 级联、或任何"多规则/多标准打分器"的工程决策有直接参考价值，尤其是其严格的容量/优化步数对照实验方法论值得借鉴。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would a dense semantic router (rather than TF-IDF lexical clustering) change the criterion-family assignment quality enough to narrow the 10.05-point judgelet-bank gap, given the paper states routing quality was deliberately not the variable of interest?
- Does the "shared pre-adaptation nearly closes the gap" result (76.85% vs. 76.38% monolith) hold if the family-specific fine-tuning epoch is extended further, or does the bank eventually re-diverge from shared judgment as family-specific updates accumulate?
- What determines a priori whether two evaluators in a candidate cascade pair will have genuinely complementary errors (as in the Skywork ladder) versus redundant ones (as in the Prometheus rank-8/rank-64 diagnostic) — could the risklet's geometry features themselves be used to predict cascade viability before deployment?
- How would the qualified design rule ("share judgment training until there is enough data to justify a split") generalize to a much larger number of criterion families (K ≫ 8), where the sparse-MoE literature suggests routing overhead and specialization benefits might behave differently?
- Since removing the skill one-hot from the risklet costs only 0.03 accuracy points, would an even simpler, fully unsupervised anomaly-detection-style confidence signal (no logistic fitting at all) achieve comparable cascade gains?
