Beats Murphy (best non-oracle multi-turn baseline) by 7.5 pts and a fixed-budget oracle-guided baseline — given privileged ground-truth feedback and 10 fixed turns — by 4.4 pts, while SVR averages under 3 turns.

Why This Matters

A reusable reward design lets multi-turn reasoning models decide for themselves when to stop refining — cutting token cost without an external verifier or oracle.

中文原文（Relevance）：该工作与测试时计算分配、模型自我验证/自我修正以及基于 GRPO 的多轮强化学习高度相关，其"用策略自身生成的判定—置信度信号替代外部验证器/oracle 来控制自适应停止"的思路，为降低多轮推理细化的 token 开销、提升可信度校准提供了一个可直接复用的奖励设计范式，但目前仅在 2B 规模模型和数学推理任务上验证。

▲ Critical Edge

Even the full method still returns a wrong answer on 29.9%–37.0% of adaptively-stopped trajectories: the stopping signal is a real improvement over fixed budgets, not a solved problem.

Methodology

1

Joint output per turn. Each turn, the policy emits a reasoning trace, an answer, and a structured self-check: a discrete verdict vt ∈ {Correct, Incorrect, Unsure} plus a confidence ct ∈ [0,1] — not assumed calibrated a priori; calibration is trained in via the reward.

2

Fixed-horizon GRPO training. 8 trajectories per input are forced through all 3 turns regardless of the model's own stop signal, decoupling training from an initially unreliable controller. Per-turn solve, self-verify, and format rewards average into one trajectory-level return; the policy gradient is applied only to the final turn's tokens.

3

Composite self-verify reward. Brier-style calibration + an asymmetric overconfidence penalty (weight 0.8) + an error-detection bonus (0.2) + a "stop-readiness" bonus (0.3) for confident correct answers — plus a cross-turn progress term rewarding wrong→correct fixes and penalizing correct→wrong regression.

4

Oracle-free inference. Stop at the first turn where verdict = Correct and confidence ≥ γ = 0.85; otherwise keep refining up to a 10-turn budget. No ground truth, reference answer, or external verifier is ever exposed to the policy at inference.

Setup: Qwen3.5-2B backbone, ms-swift + colocated vLLM rollout, 4×A800 GPUs. Three domain policies trained separately (Countdown 50K sampled examples, GSM8K 7,473, MATH 7,500); the MATH policy is evaluated zero-shot on 5 further benchmarks ("Math-5").

SVR ties 10-sample majority voting at roughly half the tokens, and beats 5-sample voting by 3.4–3.8 pts at a near-matched budget.

  • Per-dataset spread: Countdown 0.839 (vs. 0.745 oracle-guided, 0.529 backbone); AIME26 (30 examples) 0.200 vs. next-best 0.167. GSM8K is the one loss — oracle-guided's privileged feedback edges SVR 0.829 vs. 0.813.
  • Ablations: dropping the whole self-verify reward block costs 3.5/4.3 pts (All-7/Math-5); dropping the overconfidence penalty pushes Premature Stop Error from 29.9% to 33.8%; single-turn only (no iterative refinement) falls to 0.471/0.375; a confidence-only controller (no discrete verdict) nearly doubles mean turns (2.99→5.99) yet is still less accurate.
  • Seed stability: three independently trained checkpoints average 0.556±0.007 All-7 / 0.449±0.008 Math-5 — all three individually beat the oracle-guided reference (0.519/0.412), though the authors caveat that 3 seeds is not enough for a formal significance claim.

Critical Assessment

NoveltyEmbeds the verdict-confidence interface inside the same policy that does the refining — closing the loop without an auxiliary verifier or oracle, distinct from prior calibration-only RL or separate frozen-model controllers (C3RL, CAS, CoRefine).
RigorThorough ablations across reward components, controller structure, and a γ threshold sweep, plus a compute-matched majority-voting comparison. One global γ=0.85 is used throughout — no per-benchmark cherry-picking.
ReproducibilityFull hyperparameters and prompt templates in the appendix, but no code or checkpoints released — and the stopping rule leans on hand-set regex fallback priors (0.8/0.2/0.5) that are never ablated.
SignificancePremature Stop Error stays substantial (29.9%–37.0%); oracle-guided feedback still edges out SVR on GSM8K; every result is on a single 2B-parameter backbone.

Strengths

  • Clean, falsifiable formalization of adaptive refinement with a deployment-only stopping rule that never touches ground truth.
  • Consistent gains across seven benchmarks and three independently trained domain policies, not one convenient dataset.
  • Diagnostic decomposition (ESR, PSE, Brier, verdict accuracy, AUROC) lets ablations attribute specific failure modes rather than reporting accuracy alone.
  • Compute-matched comparison against majority voting — a fair baseline many adaptive-refinement papers skip.
  • Honest disclosure of real limitations: elevated PSE, small AIME26/AMC23 sample sizes, GSM8K's oracle-guided edge.

Limitations

  • Tested only on one relatively small backbone (Qwen3.5-2B); scaling behavior is untested.
  • Premature Stop Error remains high (29.9%–37.0%) even for the full method — a real fraction of stopped trajectories are wrong.
  • Three separate domain-specific policies are trained rather than one general-purpose policy; cross-domain transfer of the verifier itself is untested.
  • AIME26 (30 examples) and AMC23 (40 examples) are too small for robust per-dataset numbers.
  • No code, model weights, or public repository are released alongside the paper.
  • Stability checked across only three training seeds — insufficient for formal statistical significance, per the authors.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does the learned verdict-confidence signal transfer across domains (MATH-trained applied zero-shot to Countdown or non-math tasks), or is it tightly coupled to the training domain's error patterns?
  2. How does the accuracy-compute trade-off change with backbone scale — does a larger, better-calibrated base model cut Premature Stop Error, or does the RL objective need re-tuning?
  3. On GSM8K, privileged oracle feedback still beats SVR — is that a calibration ceiling, a training-data mismatch, or something else?
  4. Could an explicit uncertainty-aware exploration bonus further reduce the 29.9%–37.0% Premature Stop Error on harder benchmarks, and at what token-efficiency cost?
  5. How sensitive are the reported gains to the regex-based verdict/confidence parser and its fallback priors (0.8/0.2/0.5) — would constrained decoding change the calibration behavior?
