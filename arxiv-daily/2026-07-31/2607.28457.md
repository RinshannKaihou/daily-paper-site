> **一句话总结：** 论文提出 SVR，一种 oracle-free 的多轮强化学习框架，让 Qwen3.5-2B 在每轮生成解答的同时输出一个"判定（Correct/Incorrect/Unsure）+ 置信度"的自我验证信号，仅当判定为 Correct 且置信度≥0.85 时才停止细化；在七个数学推理基准上，SVR 平均仅用 2.99 轮、8.56K tokens 就取得 0.563 的 All-7 准确率，比最强的非 oracle 多轮基线 Murphy 高 7.5 个百分点，比使用外部正确性反馈的固定预算 oracle-引导基线高 4.4 个百分点，且以约一半的 token 开销追平十样本 GRPO 多数投票（0.563 vs 0.564）。

> **TL;DR:** SVR trains a single policy via GRPO-based multi-turn RL to jointly produce a solution and a self-verification signal (a discrete verdict Correct/Incorrect/Unsure plus a confidence score), then uses that policy-generated signal — never ground truth — to decide at inference whether to stop or keep refining (stop iff verdict=Correct and confidence ≥ γ=0.85). On Qwen3.5-2B across seven math-reasoning benchmarks (Countdown, GSM8K, MATH500, AIME26, AMC23, OlympiadBench, MinervaMath), SVR reaches an All-7 accuracy of 0.563 using only 2.99 turns and 8.56K tokens on average — beating the strongest non-oracle multi-turn RL baseline, Murphy, by 7.5 points (0.563 vs. 0.488), beating a fixed-budget oracle-guided score-feedback reference by 4.4 points (0.563 vs. 0.519) despite that reference using ten turns and privileged ground-truth signals, and matching ten-sample GRPO majority voting (0.564 acc, 17.50K tokens) at roughly half the token cost (8.56K tokens).

## 核心贡献

- **提出 oracle-free 的闭环细化框架 SVR**：每一轮生成不仅包含推理轨迹和答案，还包含一个结构化自检块（判定 v_t∈{C,I,U} + 置信度 c_t∈[0,1]）；推理时仅依据模型自己生成的判定—置信度信号决定是否停止（v_t=C 且 c_t≥γ），从不接触外部正确性反馈、参考答案或验证器。
- **设计 Joint Verdict–Confidence Reinforcement Learning（联合判定—置信度强化学习）目标**：基于 GRPO，在固定 3 轮的训练轨迹上，把逐轮的解答质量（r_solve）、自我验证质量（r_verify，含 Brier 式校准、非对称过度自信惩罚、错误检测、"停止就绪"奖励）和格式奖励平均成单条轨迹级回报，只在最终一轮补全的 token 上做策略梯度更新。
- **用跨轮进度奖励处理"答案保留"问题**：奖励错误→正确的转变和对已正确答案的保留，惩罚正确→错误的退化和重复失败，使模型学会判断何时该停止而不是盲目多轮生成。
- **在七个数学推理基准上系统验证自适应停止的有效性**：All-7 宏平均准确率 0.563，平均 2.99 轮、8.56K tokens，相比 Qwen3.5-2B 原始骨干提升 14.3 个百分点，且固定预算扫描表明没有任何共享的固定停止轮次能达到自适应 SVR 的准确率。
- **消融实验拆解各奖励组件的作用**：证明去掉整个自我验证奖励块会使 All-7/Math-5 准确率分别下降 3.5/4.3 个百分点，去掉校准项恶化 Brier 分数，去掉过度自信惩罚项使"提前误停率"（PSE）从 29.9% 升至 33.8%，去掉"停止就绪"奖励虽降低早停频率但消耗更多轮次且准确率仍下降 6.9 个百分点。

## Key Contributions

- **An oracle-free closed-loop refinement framework (SVR)** in which each turn's output includes a structured self-check — a categorical verdict v_t ∈ {CORRECT, INCORRECT, UNSURE} and a confidence c_t ∈ [0,1] — and the deployment-time controller stops only when v_t = CORRECT and c_t ≥ a threshold γ, using no ground-truth labels, reference answers, or external verifiers at inference (Eq. 4).
- **Joint Verdict–Confidence Reinforcement Learning**, a fixed-horizon (T_tr = 3), GRPO-based multi-turn objective (Eq. 7–9) that averages per-turn solve, self-verification, and format signals into a single trajectory-level return, with the policy-gradient loss applied only to the final-completion tokens; ground-truth correctness is used only to construct training rewards and is never exposed to the refinement policy itself.
- **A composite self-verification reward** (Eq. 9) combining Brier-style calibration, an asymmetric overconfidence penalty (weighted 0.8, higher than calibration's 0.5), explicit incorrect-state detection, and a "stop-readiness" term rewarding confident correct commitments — explicitly designed to train the verdict-confidence pair as an actionable control signal rather than a passive uncertainty report.
- **A cross-turn progress reward** (Eq. 13–14) that rewards wrong-to-correct transitions and preservation of an already-correct answer while penalizing correct-to-incorrect regression and repeated failure, addressing the "answer-retention problem" the paper identifies: on GSM8K, any-turn accuracy across a ten-turn trajectory reaches 0.925, yet the answer returned at turn ten is only 0.736 correct — showing the model can find correct answers but a fixed stopping rule cannot reliably keep them.
- **Comprehensive empirical validation across seven reasoning benchmarks** with three independently trained domain policies (Countdown, GSM8K, competition MATH, the latter zero-shot evaluated on MATH500/AIME26/AMC23/OlympiadBench/MinervaMath), showing SVR dominates fixed-budget, adaptive, and oracle-guided baselines on accuracy-compute trade-off, with stability confirmed across three training seeds.

## Methodology

SVR frames multi-turn refinement as a sequential test-time compute allocation problem. At each turn t, the policy π_θ generates a completion o_t = (r_t, a_t, v_t, c_t) containing a reasoning trace r_t, a task answer a_t, and a parsed self-verification state (v_t, c_t), where the confidence c_t is not assumed calibrated a priori — calibration is trained in via the objective. If the output does not truncate and the verdict is not CORRECT with sufficient confidence, SVR constructs the next turn's prompt P_ref(x, d_t, I(v_t, c_t, q_t)) from only the original problem, a length-bounded retained draft d_t of the previous completion, and a state-dependent refinement instruction — never from ground-truth labels or external evaluator scores. This "oracle-free" information boundary is the key architectural constraint distinguishing SVR from prior verifier-guided or oracle-feedback refinement methods.

**Training (fixed horizon).** For each input, the policy samples G=8 trajectories, each forced to execute the complete horizon T_tr=3 turns (regardless of the policy's own stopping signal), so continuation is decoupled from an initially unreliable controller and erroneous positive assessments cannot prematurely truncate a trajectory during training. The per-turn solve reward r_solve combines a length-shaped absolute-correctness term (rewarding correct solutions with local completion-length shaping, without rewarding short incorrect answers) and the cross-turn progress term r_prog, minus a truncation penalty. The self-verification reward r_verify — masked to zero for truncated outputs — combines: (1) a Brier-style calibration term 1-(c_t - y_t)², (2) an asymmetric overconfidence penalty λ_over·c_t·𝟙[v_t=C ∧ y_t=0] (weight 0.8), (3) an explicit error-detection bonus λ_detect·𝟙[v_t=I ∧ y_t=0] (weight 0.2), and (4) a stop-readiness bonus λ_ready·c_t·𝟙[v_t=C ∧ y_t=1] (weight 0.3). All three per-turn signals (solve, verify, format) are averaged over T_tr turns into one scalar trajectory return R_SVR(τ), normalized within the sampled group into a GRPO advantage, and the policy-gradient loss is backpropagated only through the final turn's generated tokens — earlier turns influence the return and provide refinement context but are not independently optimized samples.

**Inference (adaptive stopping).** At deployment, SVR replaces forced continuation with the adaptive scheduler of Eq. 4: the current answer is returned at the first non-truncated turn satisfying v_t=CORRECT and c_t ≥ γ (γ=0.85 in the main experiments), otherwise refinement continues up to a maximum budget T_max=10. The training horizon (T_tr=3) and deployment budget (T_max=10) are independent design parameters, decoupling training-time supervision depth from inference-time stopping. Training uses Qwen3.5-2B as the backbone with the ms-swift framework, full-parameter RL, and colocated vLLM rollout generation on four NVIDIA A800 GPUs. Three domain-specific policies are trained separately on Countdown (50,000 sampled examples from a 490,364-example pool), GSM8K (7,473 examples), and competition MATH (7,500 examples), each for one epoch; the MATH-trained policy is additionally evaluated zero-shot on MATH500, AIME26, AMC23, OlympiadBench, and MinervaMath (the "Math-5" aggregate) without further fine-tuning.

## Results & Highlights

- **All-7 aggregate accuracy**: SVR reaches **0.563** macro-average accuracy across seven benchmarks, versus the unmodified Qwen3.5-2B backbone's **0.420** (+14.3 points), versus the strongest non-oracle multi-turn baseline Murphy's **0.488** (+7.5 points), and versus a fixed-budget oracle-guided score-feedback reference's **0.519** (+4.4 points) — despite that oracle reference using ten fixed turns and privileged ground-truth correctness labels in its refinement prompts, while SVR averages only 2.99 turns.
- **Compute efficiency (tokens)**: SVR consumes **8.56 thousand total tokens per example** on All-7, versus **25.45K** for MLMT-RL, **20.85K** for GRPO-MT, and **19.34K** for the oracle-guided reference (Figure 1) — less than half, and in some cases roughly a third, of the fixed-budget multi-turn baselines' token cost, while using fewer than 3 turns on average versus their fixed 10 turns.
- **Matched-budget majority-voting comparison (Table 2)**: SVR (0.563 All-7 accuracy, 8.56K tokens) essentially **matches GRPO ten-sample majority voting (Maj@10: 0.564 accuracy, 17.50K tokens)** at roughly half the token budget, and at a near-matched token budget **outperforms GRPO Maj@5 (0.529 accuracy, 8.80K tokens) by 3.4 points on All-7** (0.563 vs. 0.529) and by 3.8 points on Math-5 (0.458 vs. 0.420).
- **Per-dataset gains**: On Countdown, SVR reaches **0.839** versus the oracle-guided reference's **0.745** and backbone's **0.529**; on AIME26 (only 30 examples), SVR's **0.200** exceeds the next-best baseline MLMT-RL's **0.167**; SVR ranks first on six of seven benchmarks and all five Math-5 benchmarks, with GSM8K the sole exception where the oracle-guided reference (0.829) edges out SVR (0.813) by 1.6 points — a case the authors attribute to genuine ground-truth feedback still providing an edge when in-domain accuracy is already high.
- **Ablations (Table 3, Table 4)**: removing the entire self-verification reward block reduces All-7/Math-5 accuracy by 3.5/4.3 points; removing the overconfidence penalty raises Premature Stop Error (PSE) from 29.9% to 33.8% and the Overconfidence metric from 19.3% to 22.5%; restricting SVR to single-turn generation (removing iterative refinement entirely) lowers All-7/Math-5 accuracy by 9.2/8.3 points (to 0.471/0.375); a "confidence-only" controller variant (dropping the discrete verdict) nearly doubles mean turns (2.99→5.99) while still reducing accuracy, showing neither structural component alone recovers full performance.
- **Seed stability**: across three independently trained checkpoints, SVR obtains All-7 accuracy 0.556±0.007 (3.05±0.06 turns, 8.68±0.11K tokens) and Math-5 accuracy 0.449±0.008 (3.49±0.07 turns, 11.21±0.16K tokens) — all three runs individually exceed the fixed-budget oracle-guided reference's All-7/Math-5 accuracy (0.519/0.412), though the authors explicitly caveat that three seeds are "insufficient for a formal statistical-significance claim."

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The core idea — training a policy-internal verdict-confidence pair via RL specifically to serve as an actionable stopping signal for adaptive test-time compute, rather than as a post-hoc calibration report or an externally-queried verifier — is a genuine and reasonably novel synthesis. Prior confidence-calibration RL work (e.g., C3RL, CAS, cited as [26, 32]) either jointly optimizes calibration with correctness without using it for stopping, or uses confidence only to allocate independent samples; CoRefine trains a separate lightweight controller over a frozen model's traces. SVR's contribution is specifically embedding the verdict-confidence interface inside the same policy that also does the refinement, closing the loop without an auxiliary verifier or oracle.

**Rigor**: The ablation design is reasonably thorough — reward-component ablations (Table 3), structural/controller-interface ablations (Table 4: single-turn, verdict-only, confidence-only), a confidence-threshold sensitivity sweep (Figure 4, γ ∈ {0.50,...,0.95}), and a majority-voting compute-matched comparison (Table 2) all target different plausible confounds. A specific rigor strength: the paper explicitly separates "diagnostic" metrics (ESR, PSE, Brier, verdict accuracy) from the "reported" accuracy metric and states that the threshold sweep is "diagnostic and no dataset-specific optimum is substituted into the reported main results" — i.e., they did not cherry-pick γ per-benchmark, using one global γ=0.85 throughout.

**Reproducibility**: Strong on paper (full hyperparameter tables, reward coefficients in Table 6, exact prompt templates and parsing fallback rules in Appendix B), but weak in practice: no code or checkpoint release is mentioned, and the method's behavior depends on fragile-seeming details like tolerant regex-based verdict/confidence parsing with hand-specified default priors (c_t = 0.8 for an unparseable CORRECT verdict, 0.2 for INCORRECT, 0.5 for UNSURE) — these heuristic fallback values are load-bearing for the stopping rule but are not ablated.

**Significance / red flags**: A specific weak point the paper itself surfaces: on **GSM8K**, the oracle-guided reference — which is given privileged ground-truth correctness labels at every turn — still beats SVR by 0.7–1.6 points, indicating SVR's oracle-free self-verification signal has not fully closed the gap to genuine external feedback when the task is easy and in-domain. Another concrete quirk: **AIME26 and AMC23 contain only 30 and 40 examples respectively**, which the authors themselves flag as requiring "appropriate caution" in interpreting per-dataset results — a good instance of honest disclosure, but it also means the paper's per-benchmark superlatives on these two datasets carry limited statistical weight (a single-problem flip changes AIME26 accuracy by 3.3 points). The **Premature Stop Error remains substantial** even for full SVR (29.9% on All-7, 37.0% on Math-5), meaning roughly a third of early-stopped examples are wrong — the paper is candid that "reducing premature-stop errors on difficult problems remains an important direction" rather than claiming the stopping controller is reliable. Finally, all experiments use a single 2B-parameter backbone (Qwen3.5-2B); no evidence is given for how the approach scales to larger models where miscalibration dynamics and refinement value may differ substantially.

## Strengths

- Clean, falsifiable formalization of the adaptive-refinement problem (Eq. 1–5) with an explicit, deployment-only stopping rule that never touches ground truth, cleanly separating training-time supervision from inference-time information access.
- Consistent, broad empirical gains across seven benchmarks and three independently trained domain policies, not just one convenient dataset.
- Genuinely useful diagnostic decomposition (ESR, PSE, Brier, verdict accuracy, AUROC) that lets the reward-component ablations attribute specific failure modes (overconfidence vs. miscalibration vs. under-exploration of refinement) rather than reporting only end-task accuracy.
- Compute-matched comparison against majority voting (Table 2) is a fair and informative baseline that many adaptive-refinement papers skip, directly showing the value of instance-adaptive stopping over uniformly increasing sample count.
- Honest disclosure of a real limitation (elevated PSE, small AIME26/AMC23 sample sizes, GSM8K's oracle-guided edge) rather than uniformly favorable reporting.

## Limitations

- Tested only on a single, relatively small backbone (Qwen3.5-2B); scaling behavior to larger models, where base-model calibration and self-verification ability likely differ, is untested.
- Premature Stop Error remains high (29.9%–37.0%) even for the full method, meaning a substantial fraction of adaptively-stopped trajectories terminate on an incorrect answer — the controller is a meaningful improvement over fixed budgets but is still an imperfect stopping signal.
- Three separate domain-specific policies are trained (Countdown, GSM8K, MATH) rather than one general-purpose policy; cross-domain transfer of the self-verification signal itself (e.g., does a MATH-trained verifier generalize to Countdown-style tasks) is not evaluated.
- AIME26 (30 examples) and AMC23 (40 examples) are too small for the per-dataset numbers to be statistically robust, a point the authors themselves acknowledge.
- No code, model weights, or public repository are released alongside the paper, limiting independent verification of the reported numbers.
- Stability is only checked across three training seeds, which the authors state is insufficient for formal statistical significance.

## Relevance

该工作与测试时计算分配（test-time compute scaling）、模型自我验证/自我修正以及基于 GRPO 的多轮强化学习高度相关，其"用策略自身生成的判定—置信度信号替代外部验证器/oracle 来控制自适应停止"的思路，为降低多轮推理细化的 token 开销、提升可信度校准提供了一个可直接复用的奖励设计范式，但目前仅在 2B 规模模型和数学推理任务上验证，向更大模型和非数学任务的可迁移性仍需进一步检验。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the learned verdict-confidence self-verification signal transfer across domains (e.g., a policy trained on MATH's self-verification objective applied zero-shot to Countdown or to non-mathematical reasoning tasks), or is it tightly coupled to the training domain's error patterns?
- How does SVR's accuracy-compute trade-off change with backbone scale — does a larger, better-calibrated base model reduce Premature Stop Error substantially, or does the RL objective need to be re-tuned (e.g., different λ_over/λ_cal weights) at different scales?
- Since GSM8K is the one benchmark where privileged oracle feedback still beats SVR, what specific failure mode explains the gap — is it a calibration ceiling, a training-data mismatch (GSM8K's easier problems may saturate self-verification's marginal value), or something else?
- Could the stop-readiness and overconfidence reward terms be extended with a explicit uncertainty-aware exploration bonus to further reduce the 29.9%–37.0% Premature Stop Error on harder benchmarks, and would this trade off against the token-efficiency gains?
- How sensitive are the reported gains to the specific regex-based verdict/confidence parser and its default fallback priors (0.8/0.2/0.5 for C/I/U) — would a more robust structured-output mechanism (e.g., constrained decoding) change the calibration and stopping behavior?
