> **一句话总结：** LabEvolver 提出一个无需训练权重更新的双循环框架——内层用"感知-规划-三层安全门"驱动湿实验室机器人安全执行，外层用 Strategist 把完成的轨迹蒸馏成可复用的技能/策略/安全经验并持续维护经验库——在真实 pH 调节任务上把完成时间和安全门拦截次数分别降低 48.2% 和 60.0%，在 ALFWorld 上把 500 个连续任务的累计 Success@20 从 ReAct 的 76.2% 提升到 91.4%（超过 MemP 的 87.6%）。

> **TL;DR:** LabEvolver is a training-free, dual-loop framework that grounds robotic wet-lab execution in a dynamically constructed hierarchical state and a tri-layer runtime safety gate, while an outer "Strategist" module distills finished trajectories into state-paired skill/strategy/safety experience for reuse. On 500 continual ALFWorld train-split episodes with a DeepSeek-V4-Pro backbone, LabEvolver reaches 91.4% cumulative Success@20, beating ReAct (76.2%, +15.2 pts), Act (72.6%, +18.8 pts), and the MemP procedural-memory baseline (87.6%, +3.8 pts), while capping the memory bank at 372 entries versus MemP's near-linear growth to 1,177. On a real RealMan RM75-B wet-lab platform, LabEvolver cuts pH-regulation completion time by 48.2% and safety-gate interceptions by 60.0% relative to the strongest non-evolving baseline, and on a controlled 20 g pouring task it achieves 0.083 g MAE versus 0.133 g for a manually tuned adaptive pre-stop PD controller and 0.867 g for the CLAIRify baseline.

## 核心贡献

- 提出 **LabEvolver**：一个训练自由（不更新模型权重）的双循环自动化实验框架，内层通过 Observer（感知）、Operator（基于 LabSkill 的分层动作生成）、Supervisor（上下文管理）构成状态驱动的闭环执行，外层由 Strategist 把完成的轨迹提炼为可跨实验复用的技能/策略/安全经验。
- 设计**三层运行时安全门** Γ = Γ_intf ∧ Γ_proc ∧ Γ_phys，在动作下发前分别校验接口合法性、流程一致性和物理可行性，任一层失败即中止执行并生成结构化异常日志用于重规划，从而把安全约束嵌入闭环执行而非事后检查。
- 引入**状态配对的经验管理机制**：经验以 (e_skill, e_strategy, e_safety) 三元组形式与产生它的实验室状态绑定存储，并通过 Add/Update/Upvote/Downvote 四种操作合并冗余、抑制不可靠记录，配合 Ebbinghaus 式遗忘机制将记忆库控制在有界规模（500 轮后仅 372～281 条），避免了对照基线 MemP 出现的近线性记忆爆炸（1,177 条）。
- 通过真实湿实验室机器人平台（RealMan RM75-B 机械臂 + RGB-D 相机 + 电子天平 + 水质计）和 ALFWorld 两个互补场景验证：湿实验室实验证明系统的物理可行性、安全性与跨条件技能迁移能力（如配液倒液经验可迁移到不同液体和目标质量），ALFWorld 实验证明经验蒸馏机制具有超出湿实验室场景的通用性，并在 Claude-Sonnet-4.6、GPT-5、DeepSeek-V4-Pro、Qwen3.5-35B-A3B 四种骨干模型上均带来提升。

## Key Contributions

- **LabEvolver**, a training-free (no weight updates) dual-loop autonomous-experimentation framework: an inner trial loop grounds perception, action generation, and safety validation in a dynamically maintained hierarchical laboratory state s_t = (A_t, E_t, O_t), while an outer evolution loop (the **Strategist**) distills finished trajectories into reusable skill/strategy/safety experience for future trials.
- A **tri-layer runtime safety gate** Γ(s_t, a_t) = Γ_intf ∧ Γ_proc ∧ Γ_phys that checks interface well-formedness, procedural consistency with experiment progress, and physical/measurement feasibility before dispatching any action, halting execution and logging a structured violation for replanning on any failure.
- A **state-paired experience bank** M that stores skill/strategy/safety experience tuples keyed to the laboratory state under which they were useful (rather than flat text logs), maintained with Add/Update/Upvote/Downvote operations plus an Ebbinghaus-style soft-forgetting mechanism that bounds long-term memory growth (372 entries at episode 500 vs. MemP's 1,177, or 281 with forgetting enabled).
- Empirical validation across **real wet-lab robotics tasks** (quantitative pouring, single-objective pH regulation, coupled pH–EC regulation) and the **ALFWorld** benchmark, showing gains in safe execution, cross-condition skill transfer, and generality across four LLM backbones (DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Claude-Sonnet-4.6, GPT-5, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B).

## Methodology

LabEvolver formulates autonomous wet-lab experimentation as a constrained closed-loop problem: given a high-level goal g, find a trajectory τ that reaches the target condition under real-world constraints. The architecture has two nested loops built around a shared laboratory state s_t = (A_t, E_t, O_t) (agent/embodiment state, environment state, multimodal observations).

**Inner trial loop (state-driven safe execution):**
- *Observer*: reconstructs the initial state s_0 from RGB-D input via a four-stage pipeline (VLM object identification → VLM 2D pointing/localization → 3D localization → SAM point-guided segmentation, Figure 3), then updates s_t from environmental feedback after each action.
- *Operator*: uses **LabSkill**, a three-level action interface (atomic robot primitives, digital tool layer for external programs/logging, semantic skill layer of task-level templates), to ground high-level intent u_t into a parameterized action a_t = (k_t, θ_t) conditioned on context c_t = (g, s_t, I_LS).
- *Supervisor*: periodically compresses accumulated state transitions into structured progress checkpoints to manage context length and prevent goal drift over long horizons; also refreshes retrieved experience at checkpoints.
- *Tri-layer Runtime Safety Gate* Γ: validates each proposed action at the interface, procedural, and physical-feasibility levels before dispatch; on failure (Γ=0), execution halts immediately and a structured exception log drives Operator replanning; on success (Γ=1), the action executes and the state updates via s_{t+1} = U(s_t, a_t, y_{t+1}).

**Outer evolution loop (state-paired experience refinement):**
- *Experience extraction*: after a trial terminates, the Strategist maps the trajectory τ and goal g to a structured tuple e = Ψ(τ, g) = (e_skill, e_strategy, e_safety) — reusable LabSkill parameters/boundary conditions, procedural strategy knowledge, and blocked-action diagnoses/avoidance rules, respectively.
- *Experience maintenance*: new experience is merged into the global bank M via M ← Maintain(M, e, o), o ∈ {Add, Update, Upvote, Downvote}, deduplicating and reprioritizing records; an Ebbinghaus-style decay periodically lowers scores of unused experience and deletes records that fall below zero, bounding long-term memory size.
- *Experience retrieval*: before each trial, the Strategist retrieves top-K state-relevant experience fragments from M based on the goal and initial state to seed the inner loop, with retrieval refreshed mid-trial at Supervisor checkpoints — so retrieval is sensitive to physical context, not just task keywords.

**Evaluation design:** DeepSeek-V4-Pro is the default backbone, with cross-model comparisons using Claude-Sonnet-4.6, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, and GPT-5. Four agent configurations are compared: Act (direct one-shot action prediction), ReAct (within-task reasoning), Inner-only (full inner loop without any prior/cross-trial experience), and LabEvolver (inner loop + outer-loop experience evolution). ALFWorld experiments run training-free over 500 shuffled train-split episodes (continual stream) and report Success@20 on 134 valid_unseen tasks. Real-world wet-lab experiments use a RealMan RM75-B arm, Inspire Robots EG2-4B gripper, Intel RealSense D435 RGB-D camera, an electronic balance, and a water-quality (pH/EC) meter, across three task families: quantitative pouring, single-objective pH regulation, and coupled pH–EC regulation.

## Results & Highlights

- **ALFWorld cumulative Success@20 (500 continual train-split episodes, DeepSeek-V4-Pro backbone):** LabEvolver reaches 91.4%, versus MemP 87.6% (+3.8 pts), ReAct 76.2% (+15.2 pts), and Act 72.6% (+18.8 pts) (Figure 4a).
- **ALFWorld Success@20 across backbones (134 valid_unseen tasks):** LabEvolver improves every tested backbone — Qwen3.5-35B-A3B by +8.2 pts over ReAct (to 73.9%, reported as +12.5% relative), and DeepSeek-V4-Pro from 72.4% (Act) to 85.8% (+8.5%); Claude-Sonnet-4.6 reaches 84.3% (+9.7%) and GPT reaches 81.3% (+5.8%) (Figure 4b).
- **Memory-bank growth:** MemP procedurializes every trajectory and grows almost linearly to 1,177 memories by episode 500; LabEvolver's Add/Update/Upvote/Downvote maintenance caps growth at 372 memories, and adding the Ebbinghaus-style forgetting mechanism keeps the bank at only 281 memories at episode 500 (Figure 4c).
- **Quantitative pouring (20 g water, Table 1):** LabEvolver achieves 0.083 g MAE and 11.179 s completion time, versus Fixed PD (0.500 g, 21.035 s), manually tuned Adaptive pre-stop PD (0.133 g, 20.360 s), and CLAIRify (0.867 g, 61.930 s) — a 37.5% MAE reduction and 45.1% time reduction relative to the adaptive pre-stop PD baseline.
- **Pouring cross-condition transfer:** across water, sucrose solution, and olive oil at 10/20/30/40 g targets, 41 of 43 locked-parameter validation trials land within ±0.2 g of target mass (95.3% success rate), trial-weighted MAE of 0.081 g, with LabEvolver auto-assigning condition-specific k_p, k_d, pre_stop values instead of requiring manual retuning per liquid-target pair (Figure 6).
- **Real-world pH 5 regulation (Table 2, three backbones):** Act fails to reach the target on every backbone. On DeepSeek-V4-Pro, LabEvolver needs 2 reagent additions, 0 safety-gate intercepts, 652 s, versus ReAct's 8 additions/1 intercept/1875 s and Inner-only's 3 additions/2 intercepts/872 s. Aggregated across backbones, LabEvolver reduces mean additions from 5.67 (ReAct) to 2.33 and completion time from 25.83 to 13.37 minutes, and cuts mean gate intercepts from 6.00 (Inner-only) to 0.67 — with the largest relative gains on the weaker Qwen3.5-35B-A3B backbone (Inner-only used 7 additions/15 gate intercepts/2311 s vs. LabEvolver's 2/2/609 s).
- **Coupled pH–EC regulation:** moving from no-experience to partial to full task-relevant experience reduces reagent additions from 21 to 14 to 11, reduces gate intercepts from 2 to 0, and shortens completion time by 55.0% overall; the full-experience setting reaches all four admissible pH–EC target regions tested (Figure 7).

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core idea — state-grounded action generation plus a training-free, post-hoc experience-distillation loop — sits squarely in an active line of self-evolving/experiential agents (Reflexion, Voyager, EmbodiSkill, ELITE, and directly the MemP baseline it compares against). The paper's specific contribution is narrower than "self-evolution" broadly: it is (1) tying skill/strategy/safety experience to physical laboratory *state* rather than task keywords, and (2) a tri-layer safety gate purpose-built for physical wet-lab constraints. This is a reasonable, domain-specific extension rather than a fundamentally new learning mechanism, and the authors are candid that most existing self-evolving-agent work remains in digital or general robotic settings — their contribution is porting the idea to deployed wet-lab constraints.

**Rigor:** The pouring experiments explicitly state comparisons were repeated three times, which is good practice, but the pH-regulation real-world results in Table 2 (the headline 48.2%/60.0% reduction claims) appear to be based on single runs per backbone/method combination rather than averaged trials with variance — no error bars or repeat counts are reported for the real-robot pH task, which is a meaningful gap given real-world wet-lab noise is the paper's own stated challenge. The CLAIRify baseline (from a 2023 LLM-for-chemistry-robotics paper) performs dramatically worse (0.867 g MAE, 61.9 s) than even a naive fixed-PD controller (0.500 g, 21.0 s) on the pouring task — this is a suspiciously weak comparison point and raises the question of whether CLAIRify was fairly adapted to this exact task setup, since a general LLM-planning baseline underperforming a hand-tuned classical controller by such a wide margin is unusual and under-explained.

**Reproducibility:** The framework depends on proprietary, closed, and rapidly-changing backbone models (DeepSeek-V4-Pro is itself a 2026 preprint, plus Claude-Sonnet-4.6 and GPT-5), making exact replication of reported numbers difficult for others without the same model snapshots and API access. The real-world hardware setup (RealMan RM75-B arm, Inspire Robots gripper, custom lab bench) is a single bespoke platform; no cross-lab or cross-hardware validation is offered, so generalization of the 48.2%/60.0% real-world numbers to other robotic setups is unverified. Several implementation details (LabSkill full specification, Strategist scoring/decay parameters, safety-gate rule sets) are deferred to a "supplementary document" not included in the main PDF reviewed here.

**Significance:** The ALFWorld results are the most statistically substantial (500 continual episodes, 134 held-out tasks, four backbones) and show a consistent, non-trivial improvement over a strong recent baseline (MemP) as well as ReAct/Act, which is meaningful evidence for the state-paired retrieval idea generalizing beyond wet labs. The real-world wet-lab demonstrations (pouring, pH, coupled pH–EC) are the paper's most distinctive contribution given how little embodied-agent literature validates on physical, safety-critical lab hardware rather than simulation, and the tri-layer safety gate directly targets a real deployment concern (irreversible physical actions).

**Red flags / honest caveats:** the paper's own limitations paragraph is thin — it states only that LabEvolver relies on "human-designed LabSkills and safety rules, which may constrain adaptation to unseen operations and novel failure modes," without discussing the small real-world trial counts (e.g., 40 self-exploration trials for pouring, 43 validation trials), the lack of variance reporting for the headline real-robot percentages, or the unusually weak CLAIRify baseline.

## Strengths

- Rare and valuable real-world validation on physical wet-lab hardware with irreversible/safety-critical actions, not just simulation — a stronger evidence bar than most "self-evolving agent" papers.
- The tri-layer safety gate is a concrete, well-motivated mechanism (interface/procedural/physical checks) directly addressing a practical deployment concern for robotic labs, with a working exception-and-replan loop.
- Cross-backbone evaluation (4 LLMs) and cross-domain evaluation (wet lab + ALFWorld) both show consistent directional gains, strengthening the generality claim beyond a single cherry-picked setting.
- The bounded, state-paired memory design with explicit Add/Update/Upvote/Downvote plus Ebbinghaus-style forgetting solves a real, demonstrated failure mode (MemP's near-linear memory explosion to 1,177 entries) rather than just asserting the benefit.

## Limitations

- Real-world experiments are confined to a single custom robotic platform and three related wet-lab task families (pouring, pH, pH–EC); no evidence of transfer to different hardware, labs, or chemistry beyond aqueous solution prep.
- Headline real-world percentage improvements (48.2%, 60.0%) are not accompanied by reported variance or repeated-trial statistics in the main text, unlike the pouring experiments which do report repeats.
- The system still depends on human-designed LabSkills and hand-specified safety rules (acknowledged by the authors), limiting adaptation to unseen operation types or novel failure modes not anticipated by the skill/safety library.
- The CLAIRify baseline underperforms even simple classical control by a wide margin, weakening confidence in the pouring-task baseline comparisons.
- Detailed ablations (e.g., isolating the contribution of each of the three safety-gate layers, or of skill- vs. strategy- vs. safety-experience individually) are deferred to a supplementary document not available for review here.

## Relevance

该工作把"经验驱动的自我进化"思路系统性地落地到真实机器人湿实验室场景，并给出安全门设计与有界记忆管理的具体工程方案，对关注具身智能体记忆机制、自动化科学发现（AI for Science）、以及机器人安全约束执行的研究方向有较强参考价值；其状态配对经验检索思路也可能迁移到其他需要长时程闭环规划的具身任务中。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does LabEvolver's tri-layer safety gate handle safety violations that are physically ambiguous or require multi-step lookahead (e.g., a currently-valid action that creates an unsafe state two steps later) rather than the single-step checks described?
- What is the actual sample size and variance behind the headline 48.2%/60.0% real-world pH-regulation improvements — how many independent trials were run per method/backbone, and how stable are these numbers?
- Why does the CLAIRify baseline underperform even a fixed (non-adaptive) PD controller on pouring — was it adapted with comparable task-specific tuning effort, or does this reflect a fundamental mismatch between CLAIRify's original design and this pouring task?
- Does the Ebbinghaus-style forgetting mechanism ever discard experience that later becomes relevant again (e.g., for cyclical or seasonal experimental protocols), and how is that risk managed given the bank's soft-decay design?
- How well would LabEvolver's state-paired experience transfer across genuinely different laboratory hardware (different arm, gripper, or sensor suite) rather than different tasks on the same physical platform?
