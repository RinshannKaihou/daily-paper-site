Key Metric

28%

Test Success (generalization) on 4-bit OpenLLaMA-3B

+6pp / 27% relative over the strongest baseline, KnowledgeEditor (22%)

Why This Matters

Regulated industries like finance need to update quantized LLM deployments with new facts (CEO changes, policy shifts) without full retraining or forgetting prior knowledge.

⚠ Critical Edge

The authors themselves call the 28% headline result "not deployment-ready" — it comes from a single edit-ordering run with no variance testing, and a key motivating figure is admittedly non-empirical.

Methodology

CACHE-UK edits a 4-bit-quantized, LoRA-adapted OpenLLaMA-3B (r=16, α=32, applied to q_proj/v_proj) fact-by-fact through three chained steps. First, a UK-finance and banking keyword lexicon flags domain-critical prompts (FTSE CEO changes, Bank of England policy) and boosts their edit strength, leaving general facts at baseline. Second, instead of writing ROME's closed-form rank-1 update into the frozen 3200×3200 base weight matrix (10.2M parameters), CACHE-UK projects the key activation into the LoRA adapter's 16-dimensional subspace and writes the update into the much smaller B matrix (51K parameters) — a 200× reduction in perturbed parameters that lowers the odds a small update rounds to zero on the coarse 4-bit grid. Third, a closed-loop stability controller evaluates a held-out preservation set after every edit: when degradation exceeds a threshold, a "degradation debt" counter increments and throttles the next edit's strength; otherwise the debt decays.

  1. Domain Prioritization — binary keyword match scales edit strength for UK-finance/banking facts.
  2. Rank-1 LoRA Perturbation — ROME-style update moved from the 10.2M-param base weight to the 51K-param LoRA B matrix.
  3. Stability Controller — integral-control-style "degradation debt" throttles edit strength after preservation-set damage.

  • Batch-size sensitivity: editing 5 facts at a time hits a 40% memory-change rate, falling to 25% at batch 200 — interference grows with edit volume while processing speed stays flat (~450 edits/min).
  • Context check: full-precision ROME/MEMIT papers report 70–90% test success on different models/benchmarks; under this paper's 4-bit setting every method, including CACHE-UK, falls far below that range.

Strengths

  • Clear characterization of an underexplored problem — analytical (16× coarser 4-bit step) plus empirical confirmation (12–22% baseline collapse).
  • Tightly controlled comparison: identical backbone, quantization, LoRA adapter, and edit/preservation splits across every method.
  • Efficient by design: 51K-parameter LoRA update vs. 10.2M full-weight update (200× reduction), with no measurable throughput cost.
  • Unusually candid self-assessment — does not oversell the 28% result and discusses RAG as a complementary alternative.
  • Grounded in a hand-curated, real UK financial fact set (CEO changes, HQ moves, sector classifications), not only synthetic benchmarks.

Limitations

  • Single experimental run, single edit ordering — no variance or statistical-significance testing.
  • Domain prioritization is binary keyword matching, not semantic relevance — misses domain-relevant edits without matching keywords.
  • Baselines (ROME, MEMIT, EasyEdit, KnowledgeEditor) are the authors' own re-implementations, not official code.
  • Small-scale: 3B model, 4-bit only, and a 12,000-document fine-tuning subset (13.6% of the full corpus).
  • 28% absolute test success is explicitly "not deployment-ready"; untested on other regulated domains (medicine, law).
  • Figure 2, a key motivating figure for the "quantization stability crisis," is an explicitly non-empirical "conceptual schematic."

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Does CACHE-UK's edge persist at 8-bit or full precision, or is it specific to the 4-bit resolution barrier?
  • Would the 6pp Test Success gain survive multiple random edit orderings and seeds?
  • Would more carefully adapted official ROME/MEMIT/MEND baselines close the gap?
  • Can keyword-based domain prioritization be swapped for an embedding classifier without losing efficiency — and would it raise the 28% ceiling?
  • Does the degradation-debt controller generalize to non-financial regulated domains (medicine, law), or is threshold T=0.6 corpus-specific?
  • What happens at thousands of sequential edits, given effectiveness already drops from 40% to 25% between batch sizes 5–200?
