> **一句话总结：** 论文把ARC-AGI-3形式化为"渲染的确定性Moore机"识别问题，提出Tycho系统让编码代理在交互中构建、验证、修复或绕过可执行的Python世界模型；在25个公开游戏上用Claude Opus 4.8做四种模型维护策略的匹配对比，"actor请求委托给builder"（orchestrator）策略RHAE最高达88.49，随后用该策略跑GPT-5.6 Sol和Opus 5均达到100.00 RHAE（183关全通），Opus 5比官方人类基线少用61%的计分动作；但"自动触发修复"（trigger）策略虽然让模型转移预测准确率最高（接受转移匹配88.1% vs. single的13.9%、orchestrator的16.2%），RHAE却只有83.07，低于single和orchestrator，说明"模型准不准"和"游戏玩得好不好"是两回事。

> **TL;DR:** This paper formalizes ARC-AGI-3 as identifying a rendered deterministic Moore machine and introduces Tycho, a coding-agent system that builds, verifies, repairs, or bypasses an executable Python world model during play. In a matched comparison of four model-maintenance policies on all 25 public ARC-AGI-3 games with Claude Opus 4.8, actor-requested delegation to a model-builder ("orchestrator") reaches the highest mean RHAE (Relative Human Action Efficiency) of 88.49 versus 79.07 for a no-world-model direct-reasoning baseline, 85.36 for a single actor-as-builder policy, and 83.07 for automatic trigger-based repair. Using the selected orchestrator policy, both GPT-5.6 Sol and Opus 5 reach 100.00 RHAE and complete all 183 levels, with Opus 5 using 61% fewer scored actions than the aggregate official human baseline (35.9% fewer actions than the matched Opus 4.8 orchestrator run). Critically, the trigger policy achieves the best transition-prediction accuracy (88.1% accepted transition match, end-of-run transition-exact on 95.1% of completed levels) yet the worst RHAE among the three model-using policies, demonstrating that accurate world-model dynamics do not guarantee good game-play decisions.

## 核心贡献

- **将ARC-AGI-3形式化为"渲染的确定性Moore机"识别问题**：定义关卡机制M^ℓ_θ = (S, A, s₀^ℓ, δ_θ, q_θ, ε_θ)，区分结算观测（agent能据此行动的决策帧）与瞬态动画/关卡完成/游戏结束帧；证明"仅凭当前渲染网格和动作掩码不足以做出正确决策"（Proposition 1，历史状态下界），并允许渲染器对未确定像素"弃权"（partial output prediction）而非强行猜测。
- **提出free-form可执行假设语言与四种模型维护策略的联合基准**：程序需实现init_state/transition/render/outcome四个函数，代理自由选择内部状态表示（对象列表、UI模式、计数器、有限状态机等均可）；在同一交互接口下对比no-world-model（无模型直接推理）、single（actor自己建模）、orchestrator（actor按需委托专门的builder子代理）、trigger（verification失败后自动触发builder修复）四种策略。
- **提出"主动抽象"(active abstraction)问题并用实证分离"模型准确性"与"游戏表现"**：trigger策略的接受转移匹配率（88.1%）远高于single（13.9%）和orchestrator（16.2%），运行结束时162关中154关（95.1%）转移精确匹配，但RHAE（83.07）却低于single（85.36）和orchestrator（88.49），说明何时构建/修复/使用/绕过模型的元推理决策，与模型本身的动力学精度同等重要。

## Key Contributions

- **Formalizes ARC-AGI-3 as identification of a rendered deterministic Moore machine with transition emissions**: level mechanics M^ℓ_θ = (S, A, s₀^ℓ, δ_θ, q_θ, ε_θ) separate settled decision frames (from which the agent acts) from transient animation, level-completion, and game-over frames; Proposition 1 proves the current rendered grid and action mask alone are insufficient state (a non-Markov scrolling-viewport counterexample is given), and Definition 4 introduces partial-output prediction allowing a renderer to abstain (⊥) on undetermined pixels rather than guess.
- **Defines a free-form executable-hypothesis language and benchmarks four model-maintenance (metareasoning) policies under one shared evidence/action interface**: a Tycho world model is a Python program implementing init_state, transition, render, and outcome (plus optional planner hooks); the four policies — no-world-model (direct-reasoning baseline), single (actor edits its own model), orchestrator (actor delegates modeling to a specialist builder subagent on request), and trigger (harness auto-invokes the builder after verification failure or lifecycle events) — vary only who edits the model and when, never who acts.
- **Introduces "active abstraction" and empirically dissociates transition accuracy from game-playing performance**: trigger attains the highest accepted transition match (88.1% vs. 13.9% for single and 16.2% for orchestrator) and end-of-run transition-exactness on 154/162 (95.1%) completed levels, yet scores the lowest RHAE (83.07) of the three model-using policies — evidence that an accurate simulator does not by itself identify the objective or improve action selection, and that deciding whether/when to construct, repair, use, or bypass a model is itself a control problem.

## Methodology

Tycho targets ARC-AGI-3, a benchmark of 64×64, 16-color grid games where the agent must infer hidden rules, state, and goals purely through scored interaction (no natural-language task description). The paper first builds a formal substrate: each level is a parameterized rendered deterministic Moore machine (Definition 1), and a small outer "scored game protocol" (Definition 2) composes levels with resets, level advancement, and action accounting to define the official RHAE (Relative Human Action Efficiency) metric — per-level efficiency e_ℓ = min{115, 100(h_ℓ/a_ℓ)²} when completed (h_ℓ = human baseline actions, a_ℓ = agent's scored actions), aggregated with later-level weights w_ℓ = ℓ. A typed interaction history (Definition 3) tags every recorded grid by its role (decision, transient, completion terminal, fatal terminal, reset/next-level initialization) so that model builders and verifiers cannot fabricate transitions between non-consecutive decision frames.

The Tycho system has four components: (1) a harness that records observations and actions into a structured, on-disk, per-run workspace (with a `wmlib` library giving Python-code access to prior transitions, resets, and terminal/animation events); (2) an executable hypothesis — a Python program with four required functions (init_state, transition, render, outcome) and optional planner-facing hooks (candidate actions, subgoals, heuristics, state keys, custom planners); (3) a verifier that replays the most recent attempt for each level against the model and reports pre-action model-execution rate, accepted transition match, prediction coverage, and terminal-outcome recall/false-positive rate, plus a planner that threads state through the accepted history and searches for action sequences reaching level_complete, producing a "guarded" validated route with per-step expected-frame hashes that is invalidated if the model changes or the observed frame diverges; and (4) an explicit metareasoning policy (Table 1) that decides who edits the model and when — the crux of the paper's four-way comparison.

Evaluation uses all 25 public ARC-AGI-3 demonstration games (183 levels total; 5 games were used for harness development). Stage one holds the LLM (Claude Opus 4.8, `xhigh` reasoning, 3500 LM-call budget/game, $750 inference ceiling/game) fixed and varies the four policies to select the best one by mean RHAE. Stage two evaluates the selected policy (orchestrator) as a complete system with two frontier models, GPT-5.6 Sol (`max`) and Opus 5 (`xhigh`), under the same call/inference budgets. All traces are replayed through the official ARC-AGI API in competition mode for scoring, and results are cross-checked against a released human-replay dataset (340 first-run replays) via a "game-balanced midrank" statistic.

## Results & Highlights

- **Matched Opus 4.8 policy comparison (mean RHAE over 25 games, 183 levels)**: no-world-model 79.07, single 85.36, orchestrator 88.49, trigger 83.07 — completing 19, 19, 21, and 18 games and 157, 162, 166, and 162 levels respectively. Orchestrator's paired advantage over no-world-model is +9.42 RHAE with a 95% game-resampling interval [3.97, 15.76] that excludes zero (Table 3), while pairwise deltas among the three model-using policies (e.g., orchestrator−trigger +5.43) have intervals that include zero.
- **Selected-policy frontier evaluation reaches ceiling RHAE with large action-efficiency gaps**: with the orchestrator policy fixed by the Opus 4.8 study, GPT-5.6 Sol reaches 100.00 public-set RHAE completing all 25 games/183 levels in 7,766 scored actions, and Opus 5 also reaches 100.00 RHAE completing all 183 levels in 6,641 actions — Opus 5 uses 14.5% fewer actions than GPT-5.6 Sol at the same complete coverage, and 61% fewer actions than the aggregate official human baselines (35.9% fewer than the matched Opus 4.8 orchestrator run, which needed 10,354 actions for 166 levels).
- **Transition accuracy is dissociated from game-playing skill**: pre-action diagnostics (Table 7) show accepted transition match of 13.9% (single), 16.2% (orchestrator), and 88.1% (trigger), with pre-action model-execution rates of 89.3%, 83.5%, and 52.0% respectively; end-of-run full-replay accepted transition match is 22.24% (single), 45.56% (orchestrator), and 99.97% (trigger), and end-of-run transition-exactness covers 25/137 (18.2%) completed levels under single, 62/155 (40.0%) under orchestrator, and 154/162 (95.1%) under trigger — yet trigger's RHAE (83.07) trails both single (85.36) and orchestrator (88.49).
- **Human-replay comparison and cost**: against 340 released first-run human replays, orchestrator/Opus 4.8 reaches a game-balanced midrank of 86.14 and top-decile status on 17/25 games (baseline action ratio 1.67), while the frontier runs reach midranks of 98.47 (GPT-5.6 Sol, 24/25 top-decile) and 100.00 (Opus 5, 25/25 top-decile, baseline action ratio 2.58). API-equivalent cost estimates for the 25-game matched study are ~$5.66k (no-world-model), $7.27k (single), $5.78k (orchestrator), and $8.03k (trigger) — orchestrator obtains the highest RHAE at essentially the same cost as the no-model baseline. The GPT-5.6 Sol transfer run is estimated at $4.47k and Opus 5 at $2.99k total.
- **Cross-system comparison against contemporaneous ARC-AGI-3 systems (Table 5)**: Tycho/Opus 5 (100.00 RHAE, 25/25 top-decile games, 100.00 human midrank) and Tycho/GPT-5.6 Sol (100.00 RHAE, 24/25, 98.47 midrank) lead a table that also includes Rodionov's verification variant (98.97 RHAE, GPT-5.6 Sol), the Schema harness (98.98 with a best-of-two Opus 4.8/Fable 5 rerun protocol), PRO-LONG's first pass (94.71, Fable 5), and the matched Tycho/Opus 4.8 orchestrator itself (88.49, 17/25 top-decile) — the paper is explicit that scoring/selection protocols differ across rows (e.g., Schema and PRO-LONG allow reruns) and should not be read as a single controlled leaderboard.

## Authors & Credibility

The three authors are Jens Lehmann (Dresden University of Technology and Amazon, work done outside Amazon), Andrei Aioanei (TIB – Leibniz Information Centre for Science and Technology), and Sahar Vahdati (Leibniz University of Hannover / TIB). Jens Lehmann is a well-established name in knowledge-graph and semantic-web research (DBpedia, OWL learning), and the team publishes under a small "NIMI Research Group" banner with an open-source implementation released on GitHub (github.com/NIMI-research/Tycho, Apache-2.0). The paper is dated July 29, 2026 and appears to be a genuine independent-lab contribution rather than a large-industry-lab release, competing directly and transparently against several contemporaneous ARC-AGI-3 systems (Rodionov's executable-world-model line, Schema harness, PRO-LONG, OPINE-World, NOOA, DreamTeam) with a dedicated related-work section that cites exact numbers, protocols, and even reproduction cost sensitivities for each competitor — a notably careful/self-critical stance for a paper introducing its own system.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The core conceptual contribution — formalizing ARC-AGI-3 levels as parameterized rendered deterministic Moore machines with an explicit typed-interaction-history contract, and using this to cleanly separate "model accuracy" from "model allocation" (when to build/repair/use/bypass) — is a genuinely useful framing, and the paper's related-work section correctly notes it is one of several contemporaneous efforts (Rodionov, OPINE-World, Schema, PRO-LONG) converging on similar programmatic-world-model ideas for the same benchmark within the same month, which tempers the novelty somewhat: this is fast-moving, crowded territory rather than a solitary breakthrough.

**Rigor**: The paper is unusually careful about protocol transparency — it explicitly calls out that headline RHAE numbers from competing systems mix rerunning, model selection, and fixed-configuration protocols, and it provides its own cost/action breakdowns rather than only the capped RHAE metric (which the authors themselves note saturates: 114–171 of the completed levels across policies already hit the per-level efficiency ceiling of 115, so RHAE "compresses substantial differences in action use," per Figure 7). The paired game-resampling intervals in Table 3 are a nice touch that most competing-system papers skip, and the authors are honest that comparisons among the three model-using policies (single/orchestrator/trigger) have confidence intervals spanning zero, unlike the no-world-model comparison.

**A specific weak point / paper-specific detail**: the entire matched-policy comparison is a **single stochastic run per policy** on a 25-game public set (with the caveat that 3 of those 25 games — ft09, ls20, vc33 — were already public since the July 2025 developer preview, so contamination cannot be excluded, a limitation the paper itself flags in Section 7 with a specific footnote about model cutoff dates). With n=1 run per policy and no repeated-run variance estimate, the reported RHAE differences (e.g., orchestrator 88.49 vs. single 85.36, a gap the paper leans on to select orchestrator as "the" policy) could partly reflect run-to-run noise rather than a stable architectural effect — the paper explicitly acknowledges this ("estimating generation variability additionally requires repeated runs... which are computationally and financially costly") rather than hiding it, which is commendable, but it does mean the headline policy-selection claim rests on a single data point per condition.

**Missing ablation**: the sk48 case study (Figure 15) is a genuinely interesting negative result — an accurate transition model with an unresolved/wrong objective completed 0 levels across 13 builder calls and 314 actions under orchestrator, while trigger completed all 8 levels of the same game — but the paper does not systematically quantify how often "correct mechanics, unresolved objective" failures occur across the other 24 games; it is presented as a single illustrative anecdote rather than an audited failure-mode count.

**Reproducibility**: code is open-sourced (Apache-2.0), and the paper reports token/cost breakdowns (Table 6) and pre-registered game splits (5 development games vs. 20 held for evaluation, though all 25 are reported for scorecard comparability). This is stronger reproducibility support than many contemporaneous ARC-AGI-3 papers, though full API-key-level cost reproduction remains sensitive to caching behavior, as the authors' own cross-system cost-normalization discussion for Rodionov's numbers illustrates (a 3–5× swing depending on cache-hit assumptions).

**Significance / overclaiming check**: the 100.00 RHAE ceiling reached by both frontier-model transfer runs is an impressive result, but the paper is appropriately restrained about what it means — Section 7's limitations explicitly state action efficiency "excludes inference cost" (Opus 5 still uses 15.1k model calls and ~$2.99k in compute despite using 61% fewer environment actions than humans), and the authors frame their own harness-adaptation loop (Section 6) as "human-mediated," not autonomous self-improvement, directly countering the temptation to oversell the system as self-improving general intelligence.

## Strengths

- Clean, well-motivated formalization (rendered deterministic Moore machines, typed interaction history, partial-output prediction with abstention) that generalizes beyond this one paper's system and gives a principled vocabulary for "active abstraction."
- Genuinely matched four-way ablation of model-maintenance policies under identical LM/budget conditions, isolating the effect of *when/who* builds the model rather than conflating it with model choice.
- Strong, transparent cross-system benchmarking against at least 8 contemporaneous ARC-AGI-3 approaches, with explicit protocol caveats (reruns, selection, cutoff dates) rather than a single flattering leaderboard number.
- Honest, detailed limitations section (benchmark contamination risk, single-run variance, human-mediated adaptation, action-efficiency-excludes-inference-cost) that is unusually self-critical for a systems paper introducing its own method.
- Open-source release with cost/token accounting (Table 6) enabling independent cost-based comparison, which the paper itself uses to normalize against competitors.

## Limitations

- Single stochastic run per policy/model on a fixed 25-game public set; no repeated-run variance estimates, so reported RHAE gaps between policies could partially reflect noise (paper's own caveat).
- Three of the 25 public games were already public before the full 2026 release, so contamination cannot be fully excluded despite provider cutoff-date checks.
- Tycho's task contract, memory schema, and modeling tools encode ARC-AGI-3-specific structural priors (64×64 grids, 16 colors, RESET semantics, HUD interpretation categories) — it is explicitly not benchmark-agnostic, and transfer to other domains would require revision.
- The harness-adaptation ("agent adaptation") loop described in Section 6 is entirely human-mediated: authors select, refine, implement, and validate harness changes from LLM-generated meta-reflections; this is not autonomous self-improvement, a distinction the paper itself insists on.
- RHAE as a metric ignores inference cost/compute entirely, so "efficiency" gains (fewer scored actions) may substitute for large amounts of unscored reasoning/tool calls (e.g., Opus 5's 15.1k model calls for one 25-game run).
- Human-replay comparisons use independent first-run replays (10–54 per game) as a reference distribution, not a matched 25-game human participant, so the "midrank" and "top-decile" statistics characterize relative standing against a population, not a head-to-head match.

## Relevance

该工作提供了一个清晰、可复用的"程序化世界模型 + 元推理策略"框架来分析交互式ARC类基准中"何时该建模、何时该直接推理"的核心权衡，对研究LLM agent的世界模型构建、验证与主动抽象策略的方向有较高参考价值，其四策略匹配对比方法论也可迁移到其他需要在推理成本与模型精度之间做元决策的agentic任务评测中。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would repeated runs (multiple seeds) of each policy on the same 25 games substantially shrink or widen the observed RHAE gaps between single, orchestrator, and trigger, given the paper's own admission that this variance is unmeasured?
- How does Tycho's orchestrator policy perform on genuinely held-out/newly released ARC-AGI-3 games unseen during any prior development or model training, which the paper identifies as "the decisive next test" but does not attempt here?
- Can the "active abstraction" metareasoning decision (construct/repair/use/bypass) itself be learned or automated rather than left to the frontier LLM's in-context judgment, and would that change the trigger policy's poor RHAE despite its superior transition accuracy?
- How sensitive are the reported cost estimates (Table 6) to actual prompt-caching behavior in production API usage, given the paper's own finding that Rodionov's reproduction cost estimate swings 3–5× depending on cache-hit assumptions?
- Could a hybrid policy — trigger's automatic repair combined with orchestrator's actor-initiated delegation — capture trigger's superior transition accuracy while avoiding its excess builder calls and lower RHAE?
