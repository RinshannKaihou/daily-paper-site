Computer-Use Agents · Synthetic Training Environments · arXiv 2607.28074 · 31 Jul 2026

Deep, database-grounded synthetic worlds turn a 9B model's task success from 36.5% to 67.1%, near GPT-5.4's 80.7%.

一句话总结：微软研究院提出 Echoverse，一套用多智能体流水线把种子场景编译成"数据库即真值"的可交互应用（含前后端与任务/校验器），并通过"每条 rollout 读两遍"的协同进化循环同时修复环境和训练模型；用十二个这样的世界训练 9B 模型 Qwen3.5-9B，在十四个评测切分上平均任务成功率从 36.5% 提升到 67.1%，逼近教师模型 GPT-5.4 的 80.7%，且同一批世界不加改动即可直接作为强化学习环境，把留出集打分从 58.8% 推到 68.0%。

Why This Matters

Shows how to build synthetic environments trustworthy enough to serve both SFT distillation and RL — and that shallow clones can hurt more than help.

中文原文（Relevance）：这篇论文直接对应"如何为智能体构建可训练、可信奖励的合成环境"这一核心问题，其数据库即真值的校验设计、协同进化修复循环、以及同一批环境同时服务 SFT 蒸馏与强化学习的论证，对做 agentic RL 环境设计、computer-use agent 训练数据构造的研究具有较强的方法论参考价值，尤其是其"浅环境比不训练还差"的负面结果值得警惕。

▲ Critical Edge

The grounded verifier that filters SFT data, the RL reward, and the evaluation judge all reuse the same GPT-4.1 backbone, so all three legs of the evaluation loop share correlated failure modes that an independently-sourced judge might expose.

Methodology

1

Grounded formalization — an environment is the tuple (S, A, T, O, D₀) where state S is the app's own database, not its rendering; a task is (g, r, κ) with kind READ / WRITE / READ_WRITE. WRITE tasks are graded by sqldiff between the pristine and terminal database; READ tasks by an LLM judge comparing the answer to a reference read from the database at generation time, seeing neither trajectory nor screenshots; READ_WRITE scores the minimum of both.

2

Two-phase environment factory — role-specialized agents (Playwright for UI, MCP for the database) build and repair each world:

  • Builder — expands a seed into spec + app
  • Verifier — checks claims & feasibility
  • Triager — attributes each failure
  • Fixer — repairs until ≥95% pass
3

Co-evolution loop — every graded rollout is read twice: once to decide whether the failure is an environment/task/verifier defect (repair the world, don't train on it) and once as training signal. Twelve worlds are used: ten full-domain clones (EchoMail, EchoBank, EchoStay, EchoForum, etc.) plus two capability worlds isolating one hard control (datepickers, nested filters) across many visual styles.

4

Distill, then reinforce — GPT-5.4 solves every task live; the grounded verifier keeps only passing rollouts (21,009 trajectories, 404,814 steps) to fine-tune Qwen3.5-9B (πSFT). RL then optimizes πSFT with a GRPO-style group-relative gradient (advantage not std-normalized) on five worlds, rewarding each rollout with the grounded verifier plus a dense per-step multimodal judge.

Repair discipline: a fix is not allowed to simply weaken a task's goal to raise its pass rate — the loop must not launder easier tasks as "co-evolution."

  • Co-evolution on EchoStay: two repair rounds (v1→v2) more than double the trained model's success on its own corpus, 16.2% → 38.5%, closing two-thirds of the gap to GPT-5.4's 50.4% reference on the same world.
  • Reinforcement learning (Sec. 7.5): on five worlds, the composite grounded-verifier + dense-judge reward lifts held-out judged score (125 tasks) from 58.8% to 68.0% at the two-epoch checkpoint, starting from πSFT.
  • Live-web transfer with zero live-web training data: WebVoyager (hosted) 66.5% → 71.5%; Online-Mind2Web (hosted) 40.5% → 43.4%; adding one more synthetic world (EchoForge) lifted an earlier mix's GitHub WebVoyager score 58.5% → 63.4%.
  • Scaling asymmetry (Sec. 6.6): trajectories 6,400 → 20,000 leaves WebVoyager flat (54.8% → 55.6%) and degrades Online-Mind2Web (40.1% → 37.2%); environments 2 → 12 keeps both climbing — diversity of worlds, not trajectory volume, is the scaling lever that matters.

Strengths

  • A genuinely falsifiable, checklist-style definition of environment "depth" (five properties, checked as machine-verifiable claims at build time) rather than a marketing claim.
  • Database-grounded verification (sqldiff for writes, reference-answer matching for reads) is structurally harder to game than the multimodal screenshot judges used by live-web benchmarks.
  • Honest negative results: shallow environments can make a trained model worse than an untrained baseline, and trajectory-volume scaling saturates or reverses while environment-diversity scaling keeps helping.
  • The same worlds satisfy all five requirements RL places on a training environment (exact reset, throughput, trustworthy reward, stationarity, safety) "by construction," unifying SFT and RL data sources.
  • Real-world-grounded seed data for at least two domains — InsideAirbnb for EchoStay, a 2.55M-comment corpus for EchoForum — rather than purely synthetic placeholder content.

Limitations

  • Only one base policy model (Qwen3.5-9B) is evaluated; whether depth/co-evolution findings hold at other scales or architectures is untested.
  • The whole loop depends on one teacher (GPT-5.4) and one grading family (GPT-4.1); the paper itself admits an environment-only vs. verifier-only ablation is "not well defined under this design."
  • The RL experiment covers only 5 of 12 worlds, roughly two epochs, a single run, and a small held-out set (125 tasks) — thinner evidence than the supervised results.
  • 8 of the 12 full-domain training worlds behind the main scorecard are not publicly released, so the 36.5%→67.1% number can't be independently reproduced end to end.
  • Live-web transfer gains are directionally consistent but modest (a few points), measured only on two benchmarks structurally unlike the write-heavy, login-gated synthetic worlds trained on.
  • Seed scenarios defining which workflows a world targets are still hand-written by humans — the framework reduces, but does not eliminate, human judgment over what's worth building deep.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. How would the depth/co-evolution findings hold up on a larger or differently-architected policy model (70B+, or a non-Qwen family)?
  2. Can the co-evolution loop's contribution be disentangled from simple corpus/task improvements, given the paper's own admission that an environment-only ablation isn't well-defined?
  3. Would the grounded verifier and dense per-step reward stay reliable on an open-weight judge instead of GPT-4.1, or is trustworthiness specifically tied to frontier-judge quality?
  4. Does the RL judged score (58.8%→68.0%) keep climbing, plateau, or saturate like the SFT trajectory-scaling curve with more epochs, worlds, or seeds?
  5. How does Echoverse compare head-to-head, on the same policy and splits, against concurrent bulk-generation pipelines like WebArena-Infinity, InfiniteWeb, Gym-Anything, or CUA-Gym?
  6. Could seed-scenario authoring itself be automated — e.g., mined from real product documentation or support tickets — to reduce the remaining human bottleneck?
