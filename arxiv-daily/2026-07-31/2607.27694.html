▲ Critical Edge

Quarot is arguably a straw-man in these comparisons — it was never tuned for small-group quantization — and against the closer rivals LightRot and MANT, GyRot's PPL margins are real but considerably narrower (5.88 vs. MANT's 5.57 PPL on LLaMA-2-7B).

Methodology

Rotation-based quantization (Hadamard rotation, à la Quarot/SpinQuant) and fine-grained group quantization both target activation outliers but pull in opposite directions: rotation spreads outlier energy across the whole channel dimension, while small groups (16–64 channels) want outliers isolated locally. The paper's Fig. 1 shows the collision directly — at small group sizes, layering rotation on top of group quantization barely helps, and under round-to-nearest it can actively hurt. GyRot decouples rotation scope from group size (CoRFiG), then aligns outlier channels to the Hadamard matrix's periodic ±1 rows (HAP) so each rotated group's range stays tight rather than randomly mixed. Because this sharpens per-group skew, the dequantization order and zero-point rounding are also reformulated — the piece that makes an all-integer datapath possible on the matching systolic accelerator.

CoRFiG

Rotates over a scope R = 2^g·G wider than the quantization group G (default R=1024, G=32) — outliers still get flattened, quantization stays local.

HAP

Permutes the largest outlier channels onto Hadamard's harmonic (constant ±1) rows before rotation. Fuses into weights, zero runtime cost.

Reformulated quantization

Scale-first-then-bias order + ceiling-rounded zero-points avoid INT8 underflow clipping, enabling a fully integer dequantization path.

GyRot accelerator

8×8 systolic array, 32-way INT4 dot products/PE, integer-only dequant pipeline, plus a 5-stage 32-way FHT unit for on-line rotation up to 1024-dim.

Results

Evaluated on LLaMA-1/2/3 under W4A4KV4 (and W3A4KV4) across perplexity, six zero-shot reasoning tasks, MT-Bench, and post-synthesis hardware metrics (Samsung 28nm, 1GHz, Synopsys Design Compiler).

  • Integer datapath in isolation: GyRot-FP (FP16 scale/zero-point variant) alone gets 45.6% area / 51.0% energy savings vs. Tender — separating the win from CoRFiG+HAP itself.
  • MT-Bench (LLM-as-judge, 160 turns): GyRot-INT wins 66.6% vs. Quarot, 68.8% vs. SpinQuant, 54.7% vs. LightRot (ties = 0.5 win).
  • Extreme low-bit (W3A4KV4): GyRot-FP still beats Quarot across all 5 LLaMA variants (e.g. 7.73 vs. 9.17 PPL on LLaMA-3-8B) and roughly matches LightRot.

Strengths

  • Falsifiable empirical motivation (Fig. 1, Fig. 2) precedes and directly justifies the method, rather than motivating it post hoc.
  • Genuine algorithm-hardware co-design: the fully-integer dequantization pipeline is a real hardware simplification enabled specifically by the reformulation, not a bolt-on.
  • Broad evaluation spanning perplexity, six zero-shot benchmarks, MT-Bench conversational quality, and hardware area/power/speedup/energy.
  • Unusually thorough, well-decomposed ablations (Tables III–VI, Fig. 9b) attribute gains to CoRFiG, HAP, INT8 SF/ZP, and the rounding scheme individually.
  • Robustness demonstrated down to an aggressive W3A4 setting, not just the standard W4A4 regime.

Limitations

  • Evaluation confined to the LLaMA family — no results on Mistral, Qwen, or MoE models where outlier statistics may differ.
  • Quarot is tested in a small-group configuration it wasn't designed for, making some head-to-head deltas less informative than the LightRot/MANT comparisons.
  • Hardware numbers are post-synthesis RTL simulation estimates (28nm, 1GHz), not silicon-validated; no RTL or quantization code is released.
  • Rotation scope R is architecturally capped at powers of two up to 1024 by the FHT unit, and the default R=1024/G=32 point is tuned on a single model.
  • MT-Bench judge methodology (model, prompt setup) isn't detailed beyond citing the standard framework, limiting independent scrutiny of that result.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Would CoRFiG+HAP generalize to non-LLaMA architectures with different outlier statistics?
  2. How sensitive is the R=1024/G=32 default to model scale — would 70B+ or MoE models need a different ratio?
  3. How is HAP's outlier-channel selection calibrated, and how much calibration data/time does it need vs. rotation-only baselines like Quarot?
  4. Could the integer dequantization pipeline and CoRFiG/HAP be retrofitted onto existing GPU tensor-core kernels instead of a custom accelerator?
  5. How does GyRot interact with KV-cache quantization below KV4, given the paper only explores KV4?
