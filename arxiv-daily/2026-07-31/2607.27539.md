> **一句话总结：** 论文证明"精确删除"是记忆表示的属性而非通用算法——用支持向量记忆替换Gemma 3全局注意力层后，代数递减在1B规模下与保留键重拟合在下一词输出上的KL散度中位数仅5.4×10⁻¹⁵（代价是+2.0%困惑度，12B时代价升至44.3%）；而在480亿参数Kimi Linear混合模型中，delta rule会让12-49%的记录贡献变得依赖后续写入而无法用固定递减公式删除，此时必须靠"检查点回退+重放"才能在18,842 token长度的真实临床记录上做到与从未摄入完全逐位相同。

> **TL;DR:** This paper shows exact deletion from an LLM's persistent memory is representation-dependent, not a universal algorithm. On a Gemma-3-1B grafted with support-vector memory, algebraic decrement matches a retained-key refit at median output KL of 5.4×10⁻¹⁵ across 31 deletions, at only +2.0% WikiText-103 perplexity versus a matched fine-tuned control (rising to +11.2%/+44.3% at 4B/12B). On a 48B Kimi Linear hybrid, the delta-rule recurrent layers make 12–49% of a record's state contribution suffix-dependent (vs. ≤1.4×10⁻⁵ for additive writes), so a fixed record-wise decrement fails; checkpointed rewind-and-replay instead achieves bit-for-bit equality with a never-ingested reference on logits and all 20 recurrent states, on real MIMIC clinical records up to 18,842 tokens.

## 核心贡献

- **将"精确删除"形式化为记忆表示的属性**：提出反事实标准——删除记录rⱼ后模型在query q上的输出分布必须等于从未摄入rⱼ时构建的记忆的输出分布；并区分"可寻址"影响（有record-local bookkeeping，可代数递减）与"被后续写入变换"的影响（共享递归状态被content-dependent地改写，需要从写入前的状态重建）。
- **在Gemma 3上实现可寻址删除**：把Gemma-3的4个全局注意力层替换为一类支持向量（one-class SVM）记忆，删除token等价于用Cauwenberghs & Poggio (2000)的增量/减量SVM算法反向求解；配合LoRA低秩恢复（仅1.49M参数，0.149%）挽回语言建模质量。
- **在Kimi Linear混合模型上实现"重放"精确删除与修正**：证明加性写入可精确递减、对角衰减可通过运行乘积"账本"校正，而delta rule（KDA）从根本上破坏了记录级可分离性；因此对20层递归delta-rule层采用"检查点回退到记录边界+仅重放后缀"，在真实MIMIC-IV临床记录上做到逐位（bit-for-bit）等价于从未摄入，同时支持"替换记录"式的精确修正（amendment）。

## Key Contributions

- **A representation-level criterion for exact deletion**: formalizes deletion as a counterfactual equality f(q; Dⱼ(B(R))) = f(q; B(R\ⱼ)) at model output, and distinguishes *addressable* influence (recoverable via a record-local decrement) from influence *transformed by later writes* inside shared recurrent state (which requires rewinding to before the write).
- **Addressable deletion in an existing LLM**: grafts a certified support-vector memory (from a companion paper, Ramesh 2026) onto Gemma-3's 4 global-attention layers (out of 26 layers, 5:1 local:global interleave), recovers utility with rank-8 LoRA (1.49M params, 0.149%), and shows algebraic decrement agrees with a retained-key refit to median KL 5.4×10⁻¹⁵ over 31 support-token deletions.
- **Exact deletion and amendment when record-wise subtraction fails**: isolates the write rule in 20 recurrent delta-rule (KDA) layers of a 48B Kimi Linear hybrid; shows additive writes are exactly subtractable, per-channel decay is ledger-correctable, but the delta rule leaves 12–49% suffix-dependence (9–49% even after the best decay-style correction); introduces checkpointed rewind-and-replay as the exact fallback, verified bit-for-bit on real clinical records (MIMIC-IV-Ext-CDS, MIMIC-IV-Note) up to 18,842 tokens.

## Methodology

**Addressable branch (Gemma-3-1B/4B/12B).** The base model `google/gemma-3-1b-pt` interleaves 5 local sliding-window attention layers per 1 global layer; the paper replaces only the 4 global layers (indices 5, 11, 17, 23) with a support-vector memory gate from a companion preprint (Ramesh, 2026): context keys are fit with a one-class SVM (box constraint C=1/(νn), budget ν=0.3), and the readout is a kernel-weighted average gated by the fitted coefficients α, with "reserve" keys forced to α=0 (zero contribution). Deletion is implemented as the *reverse* of the incremental SVM algorithm of Cauwenberghs & Poggio (2000): decrementing one token's contribution returns the solve to the state it would have without that token, without reprocessing the rest. Recovery is two-stage: (1) attention-transfer distillation (one log-space bandwidth per grafted layer, 2,000 steps, MSE loss against original global-attention outputs) and (2) rank-8 LoRA over query/key/value/output projections and the gate, trained on FineWeb-Edu (33M tokens, 6,000 steps) with LM cross-entropy loss. The "certificate" (numerical exactness) is measured with a float64 incremental decrement compared against a fixed-C retained-key refit; separately, a single-precision "masked-refit proxy" (excluding target positions from the live gate) is used only for behavioral robustness tests (elicitation, relearning/fine-tuning attacks, temperature sampling Leak@k, and LiRA membership inference) — this proxy is explicitly *not* used for the exactness claim.

**Non-addressable branch (Kimi Linear, 48B, inference-only, no training).** Kimi Linear interleaves 7 global MLA attention layers with 20 gated DeltaNet (KDA) recurrent layers, run at 8-bit precision under MLX on a single Apple-silicon machine. The paper defines a record's "state contribution" δₓ(S) = B(P,x,S) − B(P,S) (difference in common coordinates with vs. without a victim record x, under continuation suffix S) and empirically tests whether this quantity is suffix-independent (a necessary condition for a fixed decrement to exist) across three write rules: additive-only, additive+per-channel decay, and the full delta rule. The exact fallback for non-addressable memory is deterministic checkpoint-and-replay: save the recurrent/convolutional state at record boundaries, restore the checkpoint just before the deleted record, and replay only the suffix — cost scales with suffix length (O(suffix)), not with a full rebuild. A separate attention-only FISTA-based masking diagnostic (single precision, not a certified deletion) is used to show what direct-channel (attention) removal alone misses when the recurrent state is left untouched.

## Results & Highlights

- **Certificate agreement at 1B**: Over 31 support-token deletions on Gemma-3-1B, the float64 algebraic decrement matches the retained-key refit at median output KL = 5.4×10⁻¹⁵ (worst case 9.3×10⁻¹⁴), versus a "decay" foil (scaling readout weight by γ=0.01) at median KL = 1.8×10⁻⁶ — an eleven-order-of-magnitude gap between the two "forgetting" mechanisms despite both looking similarly forgotten on coarse behavioral metrics.
- **Utility cost scales badly**: Table 1 (WikiText-103 test perplexity, 400 blocks) shows the recovered/matched-control "gate cost" is +2.0% at 1B (21.18 vs. 20.76), rising to +11.2% at 4B (15.81 vs. 14.22) and +44.3% at 12B (19.39 vs. 13.43); a rank-16 LoRA run destabilizes at 12B (61.63, diverged) while the matched control trains stably to 19.02 — near-parity with standard attention is explicitly "a 1B result," not general.
- **Delta rule breaks record-wise separability (Table 2)**: relative suffix-dependence of one record's state contribution is ≤1.4×10⁻⁵ for additive-only writes, .007–.109 for per-channel decay (correctable to ≤7.0×10⁻⁵ via a running-product ledger), but .116–.489 for the full delta rule (KDA) — and even after the best decay-style correction this remains .081–.494; end-to-end full-model suffix-dependence is median 0.81–1.02 (range 0.21–1.24) pooled across three corpora (synthetic, MIMIC-CDS, MIMIC-notes; per-corpus breakdown in Table 10).
- **Replay achieves bit-for-bit exactness on real clinical data**: On MIMIC-IV-Ext-CDS, every admitted deletion passes bitwise state-and-logit audit — 8/8 intake records at 236 tokens and 9/9 at 3,248 tokens; on MIMIC-IV-Note, 4/4 discharge notes at 18,842 tokens — with victim influence lift going from +2.020 (or +1.861) nats to exactly 0.000 nats in every case, and retained-record drift also exactly 0.000. Replay latency falls from 6.70s (oldest of 128 records) to 0.00s (newest) versus a flat 6.77s full rebuild (mean 3.49s vs. 6.77s), with 129 checkpoints costing 5.22 GiB.
- **Attention-only masking is not a substitute for state deletion**: zeroing all 20 KDA recurrent states still leaves a planted secret ward code as the model's top prediction (p=0.881) because the 7 attention layers retain the verbatim text; conversely, masking only the attention layers (leaving recurrent state untouched) removes just 86–95% of TOFU-style lift, with 5 of 12 tested victims showing overshoot and some retained records leaking 42–46% residual — only the combined replay operation is exact.
- **Behavioral robustness at 1B (masked-refit proxy)**: on 36 admitted TOFU targets, efficacy after masked refit is 1.08±0.09 (near budget-matched floor of 1), retain pairs move only +0.027±0.011; in-context LiRA membership inference on masked refit is at chance (AUC 0.499, TPR 1.3% @1% FPR) versus AUC 0.996 for the untouched "present" control and 0.989 for ICUL (prompt-based unlearning baseline); gradient-ascent (GA) weight editing reaches the forget floor but harms retain extraction by −0.125±0.030 and is reversed by benign fine-tuning relearning (overshoots original recall by +3.5±2.2 nats) — a clear case of the weak GA baseline suppressing rather than deleting.

**No quantitative results reported** does not apply — the paper is quantitatively dense throughout; the above is a representative, not exhaustive, sample (additional Leak@k, entangled-pair, and whole-record deletion results appear in Appendices D–F).

## Authors & Credibility

Single-author preprint by Vishwajith Ramesh (PhD), affiliated with Vy Labs, Inc. (a small/independent lab, not a large well-known industry or academic institution). The paper explicitly builds on and reuses machinery from a companion preprint by the same author ("Ramesh, 2026," described as "a trainable support-vector memory with certified selection and exact unlearning") — this is the source of the SVM-gate architecture and its incremental/decremental solve. This self-citation dependency means the addressable-deletion result's novelty is partly inherited rather than fully new to this paper; the present paper's own contribution on that branch is the *grafting into an existing pretrained LLM* (Gemma 3) and the *audit against a declared record-omitted reference*, not the SVM memory mechanism itself. The paper states reproducibility materials (code, fixed seeds, a single-command hero demo) are released, and experiments run on a single Apple-silicon workstation — a modest but transparent compute/infrastructure footprint. This is a preprint (arXiv, "Preprint" header, no venue indicated) and has not undergone peer review.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The core conceptual move — that exact deletion is representation-dependent (addressable vs. transformed-by-write) rather than a general algorithm — is a genuinely useful reframing of machine unlearning, and testing it end-to-end inside two real pretrained models (rather than toy classifiers) is a meaningful step beyond the closest prior work, MUNKEY (Laguna et al., 2026), which only handles image classifiers with externally keyed exemplar banks. However, the addressable branch leans heavily on the author's own companion preprint for the actual memory mechanism (the SVM gate and its incremental/decremental solver come from Ramesh 2026), so the novel contribution here is narrower than the framing suggests: it is essentially "graft a pre-existing certified-deletable memory into an existing LLM and audit it," which is valuable engineering but not a new deletion algorithm.

**Rigor.** The experimental design is unusually careful about separating claims: the paper explicitly distinguishes the float64 "certificate" (a true exactness proof) from the single-precision "masked-refit proxy" used for behavioral attack evaluations, and repeatedly flags that decay-based "forgetting" looks behaviorally similar to true deletion but differs by eleven orders of magnitude in the certificate — a genuinely useful methodological point about why coarse behavioral metrics (accuracy, ROUGE) are insufficient for verifying unlearning. The scaling study (Section 6) is also commendably honest: it reports the utility cost growing from +2.0% (1B) to +44.3% (12B) and explicitly states "near-parity is a 1B result," rather than burying the negative scaling trend. A rank-16 LoRA run diverging at 12B (61.63 perplexity vs. matched control's 19.02) is reported rather than omitted.

**Reproducibility.** Strong on this front: fixed seeds, a single-command hero demo (`python -m gemma_sv.hero_demo`, ~6 minutes on CPU), a project page with an interactive replay demo, and public corpora (FineWeb-Edu, WikiText-103, TOFU). The MIMIC-IV clinical validation is appropriately gated behind PhysioNet credentialed access with local-only processing and aggregate-only reporting — a responsible design choice given the sensitive data, though it also means external reviewers cannot independently verify the clinical numbers without their own credentialed access.

**Significance / red flags.** Several limitations are self-disclosed and worth weighing: (1) the method deletes *in-context* (already-ingested persistent) state, not pretrained weights or previously-observed outputs — this is a much narrower and easier problem than "true" weight-space unlearning, and the paper is explicit that this is by design, but readers expecting a general unlearning solution should note the scope is limited to a custom-grafted memory, not native attention; (2) the GA (gradient ascent) weight-editing baseline used for comparison is a well-known weak baseline in the unlearning literature (known to be reversible by benign fine-tuning, which the paper itself demonstrates with a +3.5±2.2 nat overshoot on relearning) — using it as the main weight-space comparator, while informative, does not represent state-of-the-art unlearning defenses; (3) the KDA/delta-rule negative result explicitly "rules out a fixed receipt... not per-record influence propagated through every later update" — i.e., the paper does not rule out that some other, more complex tracking scheme might still achieve a cheaper-than-replay exact decrement for delta-rule memories, so the "replay is necessary" conclusion is conditional on the receipt-based approaches tested; (4) the support-vector solve is computationally heavier than softmax attention and replay costs 41.4 MiB per record boundary with sparse checkpoints "not evaluated" as an optimization, meaning the practical deployment cost of both branches is left partially unresolved. Overall the paper is intellectually honest about its own boundaries — the "Limitations" section is unusually thorough and specific — but the practical utility-cost tradeoffs (44.3% perplexity increase at 12B) are severe enough that neither branch reads as production-ready.

## Strengths

- Clear, falsifiable counterfactual formalization of "exact deletion" (Equation 1) that unifies both the addressable and non-addressable branches under one testable standard.
- Careful methodological separation between numerically certified exactness (float64 decrement vs. retained-key refit) and behaviorally-evaluated robustness (single-precision masked-refit proxy under elicitation, relearning, sampling, and LiRA attacks) — avoids the common pitfall of conflating "looks forgotten" with "is provably removed."
- Validated on real, sensitive clinical data (MIMIC-IV-Ext-CDS, MIMIC-IV-Note) up to 18,842 tokens with a responsible, credentialed-access, aggregate-only reporting protocol.
- Transparent, quantified negative scaling result (utility cost growing 2.0% → 11.2% → 44.3% from 1B → 4B → 12B) rather than cherry-picking only the favorable 1B setting.
- High reproducibility effort: fixed seeds, single-command demos, public code/data pointers, and an explicit "Reproducibility Statement" section.

## Limitations

- Utility overhead is severe and grows sharply with scale (2.0% at 1B to 44.3% at 12B perplexity increase), so the addressable-memory graft is explicitly "not a general replacement for attention."
- The addressable-deletion mechanism (SVM gate) is inherited from the author's own companion preprint rather than newly proposed here, narrowing this paper's independent methodological novelty.
- Scope is restricted to deleting already-ingested in-context/persistent state; pretrained weights and outputs already observed by users before deletion are explicitly out of scope (a "pre-deletion snapshot adversary" is not addressed).
- The KDA/delta-rule finding only rules out a *fixed* record-wise receipt (including a diagonal-decay correction); it does not rule out more complex per-record influence-tracking schemes that might avoid full replay.
- Replay/checkpointing has real storage cost (41.4 MiB per record boundary; 129 checkpoints for 128 clinical records occupied 5.22 GiB), and cheaper sparse-checkpoint variants are not evaluated.
- Single-author, single-hardware (one Apple-silicon workstation), single-model-family (Gemma 3, Kimi Linear) validation; cross-hardware and broader architecture generality are explicitly untested.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Could a more sophisticated per-record influence-tracking mechanism (rather than a fixed receipt) make delta-rule recurrent memory addressable without full replay, and at what storage/compute tradeoff versus the replay baseline measured here?
- How does the utility-cost scaling trend (2.0% → 44.3% from 1B to 12B) behave beyond 12B, and is there an architectural modification to the support-vector gate that could flatten this curve?
- Does the "ingestion-time imprint on neighboring representations" (explicitly left unaddressed, requiring full context repacking to remove) pose a meaningful residual privacy risk in practice, and how would an attacker exploit it?
- How would this framework's addressability criterion apply to other recurrent/hybrid architectures (e.g., Mamba2, Jamba, Titans) beyond Kimi Linear's specific KDA design?
- What would a rigorous cost-benefit comparison look like between this paper's replay approach and alternative approximate-unlearning methods (e.g., targeted fine-tuning-based forgetting) under equivalent attack suites (LiRA, Leak@k)?
