OSReward: Instituting Standardized Evaluation for Cross-Platform Computer-Use Reward Models ↗
Qiushi Sun, Kanzhi Cheng, Yian Wang, Bowen Yang, et al. · 31 Jul 2026 · arXiv:2607.28609

Best VLM judge falls from 89.7% to 69.7% on hard cases; the 27-judge average sinks to 52%, barely above chance.

一句话总结：跨平台裁判基准 OSReward 发现 27 个 VLM 裁判普遍存在"宽松偏差"——把未完成任务误判为成功；最强的 Claude-Opus-4-8 在困难子集上准确率从 89.7% 暴跌到 69.7%，随后训练的开源奖励模型 OS-Shepherd-9B/35B 以 30-60 倍更低成本逼近商业裁判水平。

Key Metric
52%

mean binary accuracy across all 27 VLM judges on OSReward-Hard — a deliberately balanced 30/70 success/fail split, so a coin flip alone scores ~50%.

Best single judge (Claude-Opus-4-8): 69.7% on Hard, down from 89.7% on the full set.
Why This Matters

Anyone using VLM-as-judge to filter GUI-agent data or shape RLHF rewards risks silently reinforcing fake successes — OS-Shepherd offers a cheaper, open fix.

Critical Edge

The paper's own analysis names the cheapest likely fix — an explicit "verify completion, don't just trust the narration" prompt — but never actually tests it before training an entirely new 9B/35B reward model instead.

Methodology

OSReward is built from scratch rather than reusing existing agent-benchmark rollouts, to avoid inheriting noisy rule-based verifier labels. The authors construct dedicated environments across four platforms — Playwright-driven Chromium web sessions, a "lived-in" Windows machine with ~20 real apps, an Ubuntu box with ~30 apps and hundreds of typed files, and a seeded Android emulator with decoy content — then have Claude/Gemini/Kimi/Qwen agents execute ~800 peer-screened task instructions, producing genuine successes and failures.

Every trajectory is read screenshot-by-screenshot by three independent annotators (not just the final screen) under a strict rule: reaching the correct final state without genuine task completion still counts as FAIL. Disagreements escalate to two-senior-reviewer meta-review rather than majority vote (~800 human hours total).

Judges are then tested under one fixed protocol — last 5 states, screenshot + reasoning/action text, no tool access — scored on accuracy, success recall, and fail recall. A systematic ablation study (text vs. vision, resampling, ensembling) isolates why judges fail, and the same infrastructure curates OS-Shepherd-100K training data via cross-model agreement filtering, used to SFT + GRPO-train two open reward models (9B, 35B-A3B).

Share of individual verdicts flipped by each perturbation. Dropping the agent's own thought/action text costs 7.2pp of accuracy on average; dropping or altering screenshots costs under 0.5pp — judges read the narration, not the screen.

OS-Shepherd-9B catches 57.6% of hard false successes vs. its base model's 14.1%, and its de-biasing (not raw accuracy) transfers to OSWorld, AndroidWorld, and WebArena. Multi-axis quality grading is far weaker across the board: even GPT-5.5 falls from ~90% binary accuracy to 66.7% AUC on alignment/efficiency ratings.

Strengths

  • Purpose-built, cross-platform data infrastructure (not reused benchmark rollouts) removes a real confound in prior VLM-judge studies.
  • Rigorous 3-annotator + meta-review labeling (~800 hours) with an explicit rule separating "correct outcome" from "verified completion."
  • Causal ablations (text vs. vision, resampling noise floor) pin down a specific mechanism — over-reliance on agent narrative — instead of just ranking judges.
  • Fully open solution (data + two model sizes) with demonstrated transfer to three independently built benchmarks.
  • Honest about remaining gaps rather than declaring the reliability problem solved.

Limitations

  • OS-Shepherd's Hard-set accuracy (60.2–62.7%) still trails the frontier (69.7%) by 7–9pp — a cheaper approximation, not a replacement.
  • Multi-axis (alignment/efficiency) grading remains weak across the entire field, including OS-Shepherd; alignment has the worst calibration.
  • The explicit completion-verification prompt the paper's own analysis flags as promising is never tested.
  • OS-Shepherd-100K's reasoning targets imitate a single judge's style per sample, risking a narrower bias than the ensemble label filter intends.
  • Judging is fixed to 5 trailing states with no tool access; reliability under richer context is unexplored.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Would an explicit "verify completion, don't just trust the claim" prompt close a meaningful share of the leniency gap without any retraining?
  • How much of OS-Shepherd's residual bias comes from imitating one judge's reasoning style per sample, and would multi-teacher distillation change that?
  • Would a longer judging window or direct environment-state tool access reduce leniency, given judges barely use the visual channel today?
  • What specifically makes Windows the hardest platform to judge (resolution switching, 100-step runs, CLI-mixed actions) and would platform-specific tuning close that gap?
  • How does reliability hold up on agent backbones outside the five training families (Claude, Gemini, GPT, Kimi, Qwen)?
