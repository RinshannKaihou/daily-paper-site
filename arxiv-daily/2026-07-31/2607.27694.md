> **一句话总结：** GyRot 用"粗粒度旋转 + 细粒度分组"(CoRFiG)和"谐波对齐置换"(HAP)化解了旋转量化与分组量化天然互斥的矛盾，再配合全整数反量化的重构非对称量化方案，在 LLaMA-3-8B 的 W4A4KV4 零样本任务上把与全精度的差距从 Quarot 的 7.3% 压缩到 1.2%（72.98% vs 74.19%），配套的加速器相对 Tender 基线取得 3.40x 加速与 3.64x 能效提升。

> **TL;DR:** GyRot identifies that global rotation and fine-grained group quantization are inherently non-cooperative (rotation spreads outliers globally, group quantization wants to isolate them locally), and fixes this with CoRFiG (rotate at a coarser scope than the quantization group) plus Harmonic-Aligned Permutation (align outlier channels to Hadamard's harmonic rows). Combined with a fully-integer reformulated asymmetric quantization/zero-point scheme, GyRot-INT achieves 72.98% avg. zero-shot accuracy on LLaMA-3-8B under W4A4KV4 (FP16: 74.19%, Quarot: only within 7.3% at best) and, on the hardware side, delivers up to 3.40x speedup / 3.64x energy efficiency over the 8-bit Tender accelerator baseline, and 41.7–67.5% higher speedup / 19.8–31.4% better energy efficiency than 4-bit MANT/LightRot accelerators.

## 核心贡献

- **CoRFiG（粗旋转、细分组）**：不再对整张激活张量做全局旋转，而是把旋转作用范围限制在一个比量化分组更大、但仍是局部的"粗粒度旋转域" R = 2^g · G（G 为量化组大小），既保留旋转打散离群值的能力，又不破坏组内的局部方差结构，从而解决旋转与细分组量化"各干各的、互相拖累"的问题。
- **HAP（谐波对齐置换）**：利用 Hadamard 矩阵递归构造产生的"谐波行"（周期性全 +1/-1 行）特性，把全局挑选出的离群通道置换对齐到这些谐波行上，使旋转后每个量化组内的数值范围显著收窄、且组间以固定偏置分开，从而降低了量化误差，也降低了对 scale/zero-point 精度的要求；该置换可预融合进权重，不引入运行时开销。
- **重构非对称量化 + 上取整零点策略**：把传统"先缩放、后加零点偏置"的顺序颠倒，并对零点计算改用 ceiling 取整而非常规四舍五入，避免了零点量化误差导致的下溢裁剪，使 scale factor 和 zero-point 可以安全地量化为 INT8 而不牺牲精度，实现全整数反量化通路。
- **GyRot 加速器**：一个 8×8 脉动阵列，每 PE 支持 32 路 INT4 点积（8×8×32 张量核），反量化、零点偏置、累加全部在整数域内流水完成；并设计了融合向量单元（FVU）内嵌 5 级 32 路 FHT（快速 Hadamard 变换）单元，支持非线性层之后的在线旋转，且旋转规模可按 2 的幂灵活伸缩（最高 1024 维）。

## Key Contributions

- **CoRFiG (Coarse Rotation, Fine Grouping)**: Rotates activations/weights within a scope R = 2^g · G that is coarser than the quantization group size G but still local (rather than global), preserving the outlier-flattening benefit of rotation while retaining the local-variance benefits of fine-grained group quantization — resolving the empirically demonstrated mismatch between the two (Fig. 1: at small group sizes, adding rotation to group quantization gives little-to-no benefit and can even hurt under RTN).
- **HAP (Harmonic-Aligned Permutation)**: Exploits the recursive Hadamard construction's "harmonic rows" (periodic all +1/-1 vectors at strides of 2^k) by permuting globally-selected outlier channels onto these rows before rotation, producing tightly-bounded, shifted-bias per-group distributions after rotation — reducing quantization error and easing scaling-factor precision requirements. The permutation is fusable into weights with zero runtime cost since non-linear/element-wise ops are permutation-invariant.
- **Reformulated asymmetric quantization + ceiling-based zero-point rounding**: Reverses the conventional scale-then-bias order and computes the zero-point via ceiling rounding (guaranteeing the shifted minimum stays non-negative), eliminating range-underflow clipping error that otherwise plagues INT8 zero-points under CoRFiG+HAP-induced skew — enabling a fully integer dequantization datapath.
- **GyRot accelerator**: An 8×8 systolic PE array with 32-way INT4 dot products per PE (2048 MACs/cycle), integer-only dequantization/accumulation pipeline, and a Fused Vector Unit with a 5-stage, 32-way Fast Hadamard Transform unit supporting rotation scopes up to 32×32=1024 with sub-32 power-of-two gating for efficiency.

## Methodology

GyRot targets the specific accuracy/hardware conflict that arises when rotation-based quantization (e.g., Hadamard rotation as in Quarot/SpinQuant) is combined with fine-grained group quantization (small groups of 16–64 channels, as favored by recent formats). The authors first establish this conflict empirically (Section III): (1) rotation and fine group quantization are shown to be "non-cooperative" — combining them yields little accuracy gain over group quantization alone at small group sizes, and can even reverse benefits under RTN (Fig. 1, LLaMA-3-8B/WikiText-2); (2) smaller group sizes sharply increase hardware dequantization cost (area/power), which is compounded further by asymmetric quantization's extra zero-point term (Fig. 2).

The algorithmic fix has two parts. **CoRFiG** decouples the rotation scope R from the quantization group size G, setting R = 2^g · G with R as large as 1024 (default configuration used in evaluation: G=32, R=1024) — flattening outliers across a wide channel span while quantizing in small local groups. **HAP** improves on plain CoRFiG by permuting the highest-magnitude outlier channels so they land on the Hadamard matrix's harmonic rows (rows that are constant ±1 within each quantization-group-sized block), which makes each rotated group's value range tightly bounded rather than randomly mixed — visualized directly via 3D scatter plots of activation distributions with/without HAP (Fig. 4). Because CoRFiG+HAP increases per-group skew, the paper then reformulates asymmetric quantization (Eq. 6): the zero-point z_x is computed unscaled (z_x = ⌈−min(x_g)⌉) rather than in the scaled domain, and the dequantization order swaps to scale-first-then-bias, which the authors show empirically shrinks the zero-point distribution (Fig. 5, box plots across 8 LLaMA-3-8B layers) and avoids INT8 clipping (Fig. 6).

On the hardware side, GyRot implements this in a PE that computes a 32-way INT4 dot product, then applies dequantization purely in the integer domain — activation scale S_X first, then zero-point term Z_X × W_SUM (a precomputed group-wise weight sum), then weight scale S_W — using INT8-precision metadata throughout, followed by INT32 accumulation and a final INT2FP conversion to FP16 only at output. The accelerator is an 8×8 systolic array (8×8×32 tensor engine) with a shared Fused Vector Unit containing a 5-stage 32-way FHT rotation unit (160 add/subtract units) for on-line rotation after non-linear layers (rotation before/after linear layers is instead fused into weights offline, exploiting rotation-invariance of matmul). The design is synthesized in Samsung 28nm at 1GHz using Synopsys Design Compiler, compared under iso-compute-area constraints against three hardware baselines (Tender, MANT, LightRot).

## Results & Highlights

- **Perplexity (WikiText-2, W4A4KV4, Table I)**: GyRot-INT (fully INT8 scale/zero-point) achieves 5.89/5.22/5.88/5.03 PPL on LLaMA-1-7B/13B and LLaMA-2-7B/13B respectively, versus FP16's 5.68/5.09/5.47/4.88. Under the same setting, algorithmic rotation baselines fare far worse: Quarot collapses to 23.85/13.68/36.47/55.08 PPL, while SpinQuant (6.37/5.59/6.25/5.49) and DuQuant (6.18/5.47/6.08/5.33) are competitive but still trail GyRot-INT; hardware group-quantization baselines MANT (W4G64, no rotation) reach 5.79/5.20/5.57/4.96 — GyRot-INT matches or beats MANT despite also using fully-integer INT8 metadata.
- **Zero-shot task accuracy (Table II, LLaMA-3-8B, W4A4KV4, avg. over PIQA/ARC-e/ARC-c/BoolQ/HellaSwag/WinoGrande)**: GyRot-INT reaches 72.98% average accuracy versus 74.19% full precision — only a 1.2% drop — while Quarot shows a 7.3% accuracy drop from full precision under the identical configuration; LightRot (72.43% avg.) and SpinQuant trail GyRot-INT as well.
- **Conversational quality (MT-Bench, LLM-as-a-judge, Fig. 9a, LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct, W4A4KV4)**: GyRot-INT achieves adjusted win rates (ties = 0.5 win) of 66.6%, 68.8%, and 54.7% head-to-head against Quarot, SpinQuant, and LightRot respectively, across 160 judged turns.
- **Accelerator speedup/energy (Fig. 11, geometric mean across LLaMA-2-7B/13B/LLaMA-3-8B, WikiText-2)**: GyRot-INT achieves a 3.40x speedup and 3.64x energy-efficiency improvement over the 8-bit Tender baseline; compared to 4-bit group-quantization accelerators MANT and LightRot, GyRot-INT delivers 41.7–67.5% higher speedup and 19.8–31.4% better energy efficiency.
- **Area/power (Fig. 10, Table VIII)**: GyRot-INT achieves 65.2% area reduction and 69.2% energy reduction versus Tender; GyRot-FP (FP16 scale/zero-point variant) achieves 45.6% area reduction and 51.0% energy savings versus Tender, isolating the benefit of the fully-integer dequantization datapath itself.
- **Extreme low-bit robustness (Table VII, W3A4KV4)**: GyRot-FP achieves 6.20/5.48/6.16/5.48/7.73 PPL on LLaMA-1-7B/13B, LLaMA-2-7B/13B, and LLaMA-3-8B, consistently beating Quarot (6.67/5.82/6.91/5.89/9.17) and roughly matching LightRot (6.30/5.54/6.16/5.44/8.00) even under aggressive 3-bit weight quantization.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The core diagnostic insight — that global rotation and fine-grained group quantization are structurally non-cooperative (Fig. 1's clean demonstration that rotation stops helping, or even hurts under RTN, as group size shrinks) — is a genuine and well-motivated observation, not just an incremental combination of two known tricks. CoRFiG's scope-decoupling and HAP's harmonic-row alignment are simple, hardware-realizable ideas that follow directly from this observation, and the zero-point reformulation is a clean, well-justified fix for a real overflow/clipping failure mode (Fig. 6).

**Rigor**: The ablation structure is unusually thorough for this class of paper — Tables III–VI and Fig. 9b isolate the individual contribution of CoRFiG, HAP, INT8 SF, INT8 ZP, and the reformulated rounding scheme, each with a directional before/after delta (e.g., MT-Bench win rate: +3.1pp for CoRFiG, −17.8pp for naively quantizing SF to INT8, +22.8pp for adding HAP, −6.9pp for quantizing ZP to INT8, +2.8pp for the reformulation) — a level of decomposition that makes the causal story credible rather than asserted.

**Reproducibility**: Hardware results rely on RTL synthesis in Samsung 28nm via Synopsys Design Compiler with a commercial memory compiler and Micron's DDR4 power model for DRAM — standard HPCA-style methodology, but neither the RTL nor the quantization code appears to be released, so third parties cannot directly reproduce the area/power/speedup numbers, only the algorithmic PPL/accuracy results (which themselves depend on unreleased calibration/rotation code).

**Significance / paper-specific concern**: The comparison against Quarot is somewhat lopsided by construction — Quarot was designed around per-channel/global rotation without small-group tuning, and the paper's own Observation 2 predicts (and Table I confirms) that Quarot collapses catastrophically (PPL up to 55) once forced into the same small-group (G=128) W4A4 setting used for all baselines. This makes Quarot functionally closer to a straw-man in the W4A4KV4 comparisons; the more informative comparisons are against LightRot and MANT (which are close competitors specifically designed for this regime), where GyRot's margins are real but considerably narrower (e.g., PPL 5.88 vs. LightRot's 5.73/MANT's 5.57 on LLaMA-2-7B — GyRot-INT is not uniformly better on raw PPL, it wins on the combined accuracy-with-fully-integer-hardware tradeoff). Also, the default CoRFiG configuration (R=1024) is only justified via a design-space sweep on a single model (LLaMA-3-8B, Table III); generalization of this hyperparameter choice to other model families/sizes is asserted rather than separately verified. The FHT rotation unit is architecturally capped at power-of-two scopes up to 1024, which is a real hardware constraint on how coarse CoRFiG's rotation region can go.

## Strengths

- Clear, falsifiable empirical motivation (Fig. 1, Fig. 2) precedes and directly justifies the proposed method, rather than motivating post hoc.
- Genuine algorithm-hardware co-design: the fully-integer dequantization pipeline is a real hardware simplification enabled specifically by the algorithmic reformulation, not just a paper add-on.
- Broad evaluation spanning perplexity, six zero-shot reasoning benchmarks, MT-Bench conversational quality, and hardware area/power/speedup/energy — few papers in this space cover all four axes.
- Extensive, well-decomposed ablations attributing gains to each individual design choice (CoRFiG vs. HAP vs. quantization reformulation vs. rounding policy).
- Robustness demonstrated down to an aggressive W3A4 setting (Table VII), not just the standard W4A4 regime.

## Limitations

- Evaluation confined to the LLaMA family (LLaMA-1/2/3); no results on other architectures (e.g., Mistral, Qwen, MoE models) where outlier/activation statistics may differ.
- Quarot is evaluated in a small-group configuration for which it was not designed, making some head-to-head deltas against it less informative than the LightRot/MANT comparisons.
- Hardware numbers are post-synthesis RTL simulation estimates (28nm, 1GHz), not silicon-validated; no code or RTL release is mentioned for independent verification.
- CoRFiG's rotation scope R is architecturally bounded to powers of two up to 1024 by the FHT unit design, and the default R=1024/G=32 operating point is tuned on a single model (LLaMA-3-8B).
- The MT-Bench LLM-as-a-judge evaluation methodology (judge model, prompt setup) is not detailed beyond citing the standard MT-Bench framework, limiting independent scrutiny of that specific result.

## Relevance

该工作针对 LLM 低比特推理中"旋转量化"与"细粒度分组量化"难以兼容这一具体痛点提出了软硬件协同方案，对关注量化算法设计、异常值处理、以及专用 AI 加速器架构（尤其是全整数反量化流水线）的研究方向具有参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would CoRFiG+HAP generalize to non-LLaMA architectures with different outlier statistics (e.g., models using different activation functions or normalization schemes)?
- How sensitive is the R=1024/G=32 default operating point to model scale — would larger models (70B+) or MoE architectures need a different rotation-scope-to-group-size ratio?
- Since HAP requires globally selecting and permuting the highest-magnitude outlier channels, how is this outlier selection computed/calibrated, and how much calibration data/time does it require compared to rotation-only baselines like Quarot?
- Could the fully-integer dequantization pipeline and CoRFiG/HAP be retrofitted onto existing GPU tensor-core kernels (rather than requiring the custom systolic accelerator), and what speedup would that yield in practice?
- How does GyRot's approach interact with KV-cache quantization at even lower precision (e.g., KV3) given the paper only explores KV4?
