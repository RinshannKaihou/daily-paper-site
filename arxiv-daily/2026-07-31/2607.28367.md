> **一句话总结：** 作者对来自 WebArena、VisualWebArena、AssistantBench、WorkArena 和 OSWorld 五个基准的 150 条"判为失败"的公开计算机使用代理（CUA）轨迹做人工+双 LLM 裁判复核，发现 15.3% 的 FAIL 判决是错的（10.7% 是评测器假阴性，4.7% 是任务/环境本身损坏），其中 WebArena 和 AssistantBench 的错误率高达 21.7%，而 WorkArena 的程序化 ServiceNow 检查在抽样中 0/24 出错；对真正失败的轨迹，一个改编自 MAST 的三层诊断分类法显示"验证/反馈类失败"（39.3%，其中反馈盲目的空转重复单项占 29.5%）和"规划类失败"（35.2%）主导，而"执行/定位（grounding）类失败"仅占 13.9%——这与许多论文默认"agent 能力弱=定位/执行差"的直觉相反。

> **TL;DR:** This paper argues that a CUA benchmark score is a pipeline output, not a direct capability measurement, and backs this with an audit of 150 publicly released FAIL-labeled trajectories across five benchmarks (WebArena, VisualWebArena, AssistantBench, WorkArena, OSWorld-Verified). Two vision-enabled LLM judges (GPT-5.5 via Codex CLI, and a Claude Sonnet model) plus two blind human annotator groups re-label each trajectory; the result is that 15.3% of audited FAIL verdicts are wrong (95% Wilson CI [10.4, 22.0]) — 10.7% evaluator false negatives (e.g., WebArena's string-matching checker rejecting a correct multi-item answer) and 4.7% broken tasks (e.g., an OSWorld battery-percentage task that is unsatisfiable because `/sys/class/power_supply` is empty on the released VM), against WorkArena's programmatic ServiceNow checker, which shows 0 wrong verdicts in its 24-trajectory sample. Among the 122 confirmed genuine failures, a three-tier taxonomy adapted from MAST finds verification/feedback failures dominate at 39.3% (feedback-blind no-op repetition alone is 29.5%) and planning failures at 35.2%, while execution/grounding failures are only 13.9% — and removing screenshots from the evidence flips 12-13 of 104 judge verdicts, roughly halving detected false negatives.

## 核心贡献

- **提出四阶段评测可靠性框架**：把 CUA 评测流水线拆成任务构建 → 轨迹观测 → 打分 → 报告四个阶段，并定位三类会扭曲最终分数的"系统性威胁"——任务失效/污染、评测器脆弱性、诊断不透明性——分别发生在不同阶段，不应被笼统归为"agent 能力弱"。
- **对 150 条公开失败轨迹做系统性人工+双 LLM 裁判复核**：从 AgentRewardBench（WebArena/VisualWebArena/AssistantBench/WorkArena）和 OSWorld-Verified 按确定性分层抽样（随机种子 20260717）各取约 20-25 条零奖励轨迹，发现 **15.3% 的 FAIL 判决是错的**（10.7% 评测器假阴性 + 4.7% 任务本身损坏），且五个基准的错误率从 WorkArena 的 0% 到 WebArena/AssistantBench 的 21.7% 不等。
- **提出改编自 MAST 的单代理三层失败诊断分类法**：把多代理协作失败分类法（MAST）改造为单代理计算机使用场景下的 T1（规划与目标设定）/T2（执行与定位）/T3（验证与反馈）三层，并给出从 MAST 到该分类法的完整映射表（附录 B）。
- **量化证据完整性对判决的因果影响**：证明去掉截图证据会让两个 LLM 裁判分别改判 12/13 条轨迹，检测到的评测器假阴性数量近乎腰斩（Codex 从 14→8，Claude 从 10→6），Stage-1 裁判间一致性从 κ=0.71 降到 κ=0.60，说明"是否保留截图"直接决定审计结论的可信度。
- **把审计发现转化为按阶段划分的具体基准设计准则（表 3）与未来方向（§6）**：包括预发布重放校验、执行栈/检索边界公开、按声明的任务描述"恰好"打分（而非过宽或过窄）、发布证据完整的轨迹（截图/工具 I/O/时间戳/状态转移）、以及零成本的自动诊断探针（如检测反馈盲目空转的"关键窗口余量" S_win 指标）。

## Key Contributions

- **A four-stage evaluation-reliability framework** (task construction → trajectory observation → scoring → reporting) that locates three distortion threats — task validity/contamination, evaluator fragility, and diagnostic opacity — at the specific stage each enters, arguing these are distinct failure modes that collapsing into a single "agent failed" label conflates.
- **A systematic human + dual-LLM-judge re-audit of 150 publicly released FAIL-labeled CUA trajectories** sampled deterministically (seed 20260717) from AgentRewardBench (WebArena, VisualWebArena, AssistantBench, WorkArena) and OSWorld-Verified, finding that **15.3% of audited FAIL verdicts are wrong** — 10.7% evaluator false negatives and 4.7% broken tasks — with per-benchmark wrong-rates ranging from 0% (WorkArena) to 21.7% (WebArena, AssistantBench).
- **A three-tier diagnostic taxonomy for genuine single-agent CUA failures**, adapted from MAST (Cemri et al., 2025) by removing intrinsically multi-agent categories and adding GUI grounding and long-horizon verification as first-class categories, with a full provenance mapping from MAST modes to the new T1 (planning/specification)-T2 (execution/grounding)-T3 (verification/feedback) codebook (Appendix B).
- **A causal demonstration that evidence completeness changes audit conclusions**: stripping screenshots from the same 104 commonly-reviewed trajectories flips 12 of the Codex (GPT-5.5) judge's verdicts and 13 of the Claude judge's verdicts, roughly halves the number of detected evaluator false negatives (14→8 and 10→6 respectively), and drags down Stage-1 inter-judge agreement from κ=0.71 to κ=0.60.
- **Stage-specific benchmark design guidelines and forward-looking mechanisms** (Table 3, §5-6): pre-release replay to confirm solvability, disclosed/pinned execution stacks and retrieval boundaries, checkers that test exactly what the task statement declares (no more, no less), evidence-complete trajectory releases, and cheap automatic diagnostics such as critical-window slack $S_{win} = (R-r+1)/R$ for flagging feedback-blind no-op repetition without expensive judges.

## Methodology

The paper frames CUA evaluation as a **four-stage measurement pipeline** (Figure 1): task construction (is the task solvable and specified?), execution environment (is it reproducible and observable?), scoring (does the checker recognize valid outcomes?), and reporting (does the aggregate score support diagnosis?). Reliability threats are mapped onto specific stages: contamination/task invalidity at construction, evaluator fragility at scoring, and diagnostic opacity spanning observation and reporting.

**Audit sampling.** The authors do not run any new agents; they re-label already-released trajectories. From **AgentRewardBench** (Lù et al., 2025), unique (benchmark, task, agent, experiment) tuples were formed, shuffled within each benchmark-agent stratum with round-robin interleaving across agent strata, keeping only trajectories with released cumulative reward zero, until quotas were reached: 25 WebArena, 25 WorkArena, 24 VisualWebArena, 24 AssistantBench. From **OSWorld-Verified**, 20 zero-reward trajectories were taken from each of three released agent runs, interleaved round-robin across application domains, yielding 158 trajectories; eight (one per OSWorld run, five spread across web benchmarks/agent models) were set aside for codebook calibration, leaving **150 audited trajectories**: 57 OSWorld, 23 each of WebArena/VisualWebArena/AssistantBench, and 24 WorkArena.

**Two-stage labeling protocol.** Stage 1 assigns each recorded FAIL one of: *genuine agent failure* (trajectory does not satisfy the task and a human grader would also reject it), *evaluator false negative* (outcome satisfies the task but the checker rejects it), *broken task* (specification infeasible, reference state stale, or harness blocks completion), or *unclear* (evidence insufficient to judge). Stage 2 applies the three-tier taxonomy only to confirmed genuine failures. Two vision-enabled LLM judges from different vendors — **GPT-5.5 via OpenAI Codex CLI v0.144.5** and an **Anthropic Claude Sonnet model** (exact revision not disclosed, described only as retaining "the Claude model family but not its exact Sonnet revision") — independently label all 150 trajectories blind to each other, using the same written codebook and full step-level reasoning/actions/screenshots. Human review covers all 74 rows where the two LLMs disagree, plus a seeded stratified sample of 30 LLM-agreement rows (104 rows total), each independently and blindly labeled by **two human groups** via a screenshot-replay interface; disagreements are resolved by majority vote across the four judgments (2 LLM + 2 human groups) per row. The remaining 46 rows use two-LLM consensus (since every LLM disagreement is human-reviewed).

**Boundary rule for diagnosis.** When multiple plausible failure symptoms appear, the taxonomy assigns the *earliest* decisive failure rather than the most visually salient later symptom — e.g., repeated actions are labeled Tier 1 (planning loop) if the agent cannot develop an alternative strategy, but Tier 3 (verification/feedback) if the agent has a viable plan but fails to notice an action had no effect — making the diagnosis causal rather than purely visual.

**Ablation on evidence.** To test whether observability itself drives verdicts, the authors re-run both LLM judges on the same trajectories with screenshots removed, measuring how many verdicts flip and how inter-judge/inter-rater agreement (Cohen's κ, Fleiss' κ) changes.

## Results & Highlights

- **Overall verdict reliability**: Across 150 audited FAIL-labeled trajectories, **15.3% of verdicts are wrong (95% Wilson CI [10.4, 22.0])** — decomposed into **10.7% evaluator false negatives [6.7, 16.6]** and **4.7% broken tasks [2.3, 9.3]**, with a further 3.3% [1.4, 7.6] remaining unclear from the released evidence (Table 2).
- **Per-benchmark spread**: wrong-verdict rates range from **0.0% for WorkArena (0/24)**, whose programmatic ServiceNow record/field checks produced no detected errors in this sample, to **21.7% for both WebArena (5/23)** — where the string-matching answer checker rejects acceptable behavior — **and AssistantBench (5/23)**, where open-web tasks suffer environment drift (dead search engines, CAPTCHA walls); OSWorld sits at **17.5% (10/57, dominated by missed alternative valid solutions and one VM-image-unsatisfiable task)** and VisualWebArena at **13.0% (3/23)**.
- **Failure-taxonomy distribution (n=122 genuine failures)**: **Tier 3 (verification/feedback) failures dominate at 39.3% (48/122)**, with feedback-blind no-op repetition alone the single largest subcategory at **29.5% (36/122)**; **Tier 1 (planning/specification) failures follow at 35.2% (43/122)**, including specification violations (18) and planning loops (20 combined with hallucinated features); **Tier 2 (execution/grounding) failures are comparatively rare at only 13.9% (17/122)** — a distribution the paper argues a single scalar success rate "cannot explain."
- **Evidence-completeness ablation**: removing screenshots from the trajectory record **flips 12 of the Codex (GPT-5.5) judge's verdicts and 13 of the Claude judge's verdicts** (out of 104 commonly-reviewed rows), **reduces the evaluator false negatives each judge detects from 14→8 (Codex) and 10→6 (Claude)** — roughly halving the false-negative count — and **lowers Stage-1 inter-judge agreement from κ=0.71 (raw 92.7%) to κ=0.60**.
- **Annotator/judge variability on the 104-row stress set**: Stage-1 wrong-verdict rate estimates vary by annotator — **GPT-5.5: 13.5%, Claude Sonnet-5: 7.7%, human group 1: 20.2%, human group 2: 13.5%** (mean 13.7%, sample SD 5.1 percentage points, range 7.7-20.2%) — while pairwise human-LLM κ ranges only 0.19-0.32 and four-rater Fleiss' κ for Stage-2 diagnosis is a much lower **κ=0.36** than the Stage-1 genuine/false-verdict call (κ=0.71), showing fine-grained failure diagnosis is markedly less reliable than the binary distrust decision.

## Authors & Credibility

Eight authors spanning Georgia Institute of Technology (lead author Zihan Dong), North Carolina State University, Lenovo AI Technology Center, University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, Marquette University, and Fudan University — an academic/industry mix with no single dominant lab, and no indication this is a benchmark-creator team auditing its own prior work (a plausible independence advantage, since none of WebArena/VisualWebArena/AssistantBench/WorkArena/OSWorld appear to be first-party benchmarks from these institutions). The paper is a fresh arXiv preprint (submitted 2026-07-30) with no stated venue or peer-review status yet. The audit methodology (dual-vendor LLM judges cross-checked against two independent blind human annotator groups, with disagreement-triggered human review and majority voting) is unusually rigorous for a benchmark-critique paper, and the paper explicitly reports its own inter-annotator reliability statistics (κ values, raw agreement, Wilson confidence intervals) rather than asserting a single audited number as ground truth.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The core contribution — treating a CUA benchmark score as "a pipeline output, not a direct observation of capability" and empirically re-auditing failure verdicts across five established benchmarks with a dual-LLM + human protocol — is a genuinely useful and underexplored angle. Most benchmark papers report their own success rates; comparatively few conduct an independent second-pass audit of specifically the FAIL-labeled trajectories at this scale (150 trajectories, four independent judgments per contested row). The MAST-to-single-agent-CUA taxonomy adaptation (Appendix B) is a concrete, checkable artifact rather than a vague new category scheme.

**Rigor**: The paper is commendably self-critical about its own measurement instrument — it reports that Stage-2 diagnosis agreement (κ=0.41 LLM-LLM, κ=0.36 four-rater Fleiss') is much weaker than Stage-1 verdict agreement (κ=0.71), and it runs the screenshot-removal ablation specifically to show its own audit's sensitivity to evidence completeness, rather than presenting the 15.3% headline number as unimpeachable. The Wilson confidence intervals and per-annotator variance (SD 5.1pp across 4 annotators) are reported transparently, which is stronger practice than the single-point failure rates typical of benchmark papers.

**Reproducibility**: Partially strong — sampling is deterministic (fixed seed 20260717), the LLM judge for one vendor is version-pinned (Codex CLI v0.144.5, GPT-5.5), but the paper explicitly states the Claude judge's **exact Sonnet revision is not disclosed** ("The experiment artifacts retain the Claude model family but not its exact Sonnet revision") — a genuine reproducibility gap for a paper whose entire contribution rests on judge outputs. No new agent trajectories were generated (100% reliance on already-public artifacts), which aids reproducibility of the audit itself but means results are tied to whatever trajectories those five benchmarks happened to release.

**Significance / red flags**:
- **Sample sizes are small per benchmark (23-57 trajectories)**, and the paper itself flags in its Limitations section that "benchmark-level rates carry wide intervals and should be read as evidence that evaluator error occurs at a material rate, not as calibrated per-benchmark estimates." The WorkArena 0/24 result in particular is a thin base for concluding its programmatic ServiceNow checker is reliable — a false-negative rate of even ~10% (comparable to WebArena) could easily produce 0/24 by chance.
- **The audit only samples FAIL-labeled trajectories, not PASS-labeled ones** — so it can only estimate evaluator false negatives, not false positives (reward hacking / shortcut-rewarded outcomes), which the paper's own §2.2 discusses as a distinct threat. The authors explicitly flag this as "the complementary audit of PASS verdicts remains open," meaning the true overall verdict-error rate (false positives + false negatives) is unmeasured.
- **The detection-cost argument in §6** (that a zero-cost automatic probe could flag the dominant no-op-repetition failure mode) is explicitly labeled by the authors themselves as "conceptual" — they "do not validate a programmatic probe against our human labels," so the proposed $S_{win}$ diagnostic remains an untested idea rather than a demonstrated tool.
- Because the two LLM judges were specifically selected as vision-enabled frontier models, the reported false-negative detection rates are themselves conditioned on judge capability — a weaker or non-vision judge could plausibly under- or over-detect evaluator errors, and the paper does not test judge-quality sensitivity beyond the two models it uses (i.e., it doesn't include a weaker/text-only LLM-judge baseline to bound how much of the finding depends on judge strength).

## Strengths

- Large-scale (150 trajectories), cross-benchmark (5 distinct evaluation regimes: web, enterprise-workflow, desktop) empirical audit rather than a purely conceptual critique.
- Multi-rater design (2 independently blind LLM judges from different vendors + 2 independently blind human groups) with disagreement-triggered human adjudication and transparently reported inter-rater reliability statistics.
- A concrete, causal ablation (screenshot removal) demonstrating that observability materially changes both individual verdicts and aggregate audit conclusions, not just a claim asserted without evidence.
- Clean mapping of an existing multi-agent failure taxonomy (MAST) into a single-agent CUA-specific codebook, with a full category-by-category provenance table (Appendix B) rather than an ad hoc new scheme.
- Translates findings into concrete, stage-indexed design guidelines (Table 3) rather than stopping at "benchmarks are unreliable."

## Limitations

- Small per-benchmark sample sizes (23-57 trajectories) yield wide confidence intervals; the authors explicitly caution against treating per-benchmark rates as calibrated point estimates.
- Only FAIL-labeled trajectories were audited, so the study measures evaluator false negatives but says nothing about false positives (reward hacking) among PASS verdicts — an acknowledged open gap.
- The Claude judge's exact model revision is undisclosed, limiting exact reproducibility of the LLM-judge side of the audit.
- The specialized professional-GUI benchmark landscape (healthcare, CAD/EDA, scientific instruments, creative production, Table 1) is discussed for context but receives no empirical audit — the paper's claims are restricted to the five audited web/enterprise/desktop benchmarks.
- The proposed low-cost automatic diagnostic probes (e.g., critical-window slack) are conceptual and explicitly not validated against the paper's own human labels.
- The literature survey underlying the CUA landscape section (§3, Table 1) is an iterative scoping review interleaved with drafting rather than a PRISMA-style systematic review with logged inclusion/exclusion counts, which the authors disclose as a limitation of their own protocol.

## Relevance

该论文直接关联计算机使用代理（CUA）评测方法论与 LLM-as-judge 可靠性问题，其"评测流水线四阶段+三类系统性威胁"框架和基于真实基准轨迹的实证审计，对任何依赖 WebArena/OSWorld/AssistantBench 等基准做 agent 能力对比、或设计新 CUA 评测协议的研究都具有直接参考价值，尤其是其揭示的"验证/反馈类失败主导而非定位/执行类失败"的发现值得在相关 agent 能力评估工作中留意。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would auditing PASS-labeled trajectories (the "complementary audit" the paper explicitly leaves open) reveal a comparably material false-positive/reward-hacking rate, and how would that change the net direction of benchmark bias (currently the paper only shows benchmarks under-count success)?
- How sensitive are the 15.3% wrong-verdict estimate and the Tier 3-dominance finding to using a weaker or text-only (non-vision) LLM judge instead of GPT-5.5/Claude Sonnet — does judge capability itself bias which failure types get correctly diagnosed?
- Can the proposed automatic diagnostic probes (e.g., $S_{win}$ critical-window slack, no-op-repetition detection) actually be validated against the paper's own 122 genuine-failure human labels to produce a calibrated precision/recall, closing the "conceptual only" gap flagged in the Limitations?
- Would applying this same dual-LLM-judge + human audit protocol to the newer long-horizon benchmarks the paper discusses but does not retrospectively audit (OSWorld 2.0, Agents' Last Exam, WeaveBench) show the same failure-tier distribution, or does longer horizon shift more failures toward Tier 2 (state propagation) as the paper's discussion in §3.3 speculates?
- Given that WorkArena showed 0/24 wrong verdicts in this sample, would a larger audit (more trajectories, or trajectories from additional WorkArena agent runs) confirm its programmatic ServiceNow checker is genuinely more reliable than the other four benchmarks' checkers, or is 0/24 within noise of a false-negative rate similar to WebArena's 21.7%?
