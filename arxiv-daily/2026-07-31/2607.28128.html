Why This Matters

Using one LLM judge to rank AI tutors risks rewarding the wrong policy — the verdict can flip with the next judge you pick.

Caveat
Critical Edge

Neither LLM judge has been validated against real human pedagogical judgment — the study's entire "ground truth" for pedagogy is itself another LLM's rubric.

EXHIBIT A

Methodology

Same frozen base model, two tutoring policies, nothing else changes. ConvTutor: a single "be a helpful, clear tutor" instruction and one model call per turn — its behavior is emergent, not engineered. PedTutor: a four-node routed policy — a private learner-state tracker runs every turn, then a deterministic rule dispatches to exactly one of a problem-decomposer, an answer-deferral gate, or a hint cascade, grounding four established tutoring principles. Both face a deliberately weak simulated student (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, ~11% unaided accuracy) across 19 algebra problems spanning training, immediate, interference, delayed, and transfer phases. Every answer-phase turn is scored twice: by a frozen, condition-blind primary judge (Claude Opus 4.8) and, later, by a prospectively-specified second judge (GPT-5.6 Sol) — both rating helpfulness and pedagogy on five-point rubrics. Two rule-based measures, answer leakage and next-turn independence, need no LLM scoring at all. The design repeats across three tutor bases (Claude Sonnet 4.6, GPT-5.5, Gemini 3.1 Pro) with 10 replicates each, under decision rules frozen before any data existed.

4. Robustness checks

Across a seven-policy ablation (Sonnet base only), judged pedagogy spans 2.3 points while judged helpfulness stays inside a tight 0.25-point band (4.70–4.95). A prompt-only Socratic variant of ConvTutor outscores the fully-routed PedTutor on judged pedagogy (+0.565, δ=+0.96) — structure isn't required to earn the pedagogy score. After equalizing visible tutor turns per session (PedTutor naturally runs 22.3 vs. ConvTutor's 13.5), both legs of the leakage–independence coupling remain significant (+0.295, p=2.4×10⁻⁴; −0.355, p=7.5×10⁻⁸). Pooled cross-judge agreement is high on pedagogy (ρ=.740) but modest on helpfulness (ρ=.372) — and weakest exactly on GPT-5.5, the base where the two judges are significantly opposite.

Strengths

  • Genuine pre-registration with frozen decision rules, including honest disclosure of a non-supported joint claim rather than post hoc reframing.
  • Clean causal isolation: identical frozen weights across ConvTutor/PedTutor/cold, so any measured difference is attributable to the policy layer alone.
  • Two deterministic, rule-based process measures immune to judge idiosyncrasy, informative exactly where the judged signal fails.
  • Replicated across three tutor bases and two independently-specified judges, plus a seven-policy ablation and a compute-fairness re-analysis.
  • Code and full problem/rubric artifacts released for scrutiny.

Limitations

  • Single narrow domain (19 mixture/weighted-average algebra items) — generalization untested.
  • One weak simulated student (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct); no human learners, so no claim about real learning outcomes.
  • Small sample (n=90 confirmatory sessions) limits power; P2/P3 are inconclusive, not confirmed nulls.
  • No human validation of either LLM judge against real pedagogical judgment — the "ground truth" is itself another rubric.
  • Gemini's forced minimum-reasoning setting means that base replication isn't a pure model swap.
  • PedTutor costs ~1.65× the visible tutor turns of ConvTutor, which the compute-fairness fix can only partly offset.
  • Leakage–independence coupling is observational, not randomized at the turn level — the paper itself avoids causal language.
EXHIBIT C

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Would the divergence and judge-contingent reversal replicate with real students and human raters, not an LLM judge scoring an LLM-simulated one?
  2. Would a reward model trained on the deterministic leakage/independence signals also be rated better by a general helpfulness judge — closing the gap?
  3. Why does a Socratic prompt-only variant outscore the fully-routed PedTutor on pedagogy — does the rubric reward surface phrasing over structure?
  4. Would a stronger simulated student change the leakage-to-independence coupling seen here?
  5. Would a third, differently-trained judge agree with Opus, Sol, or neither?
