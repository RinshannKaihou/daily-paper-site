Looped Transformers · Recurrent Depth · arXiv 2607.27656 · 30 Jul 2026

Additive-injection loops forcibly perturb their own anchor state every step; removing that bias cuts WikiText-103 perplexity from 117.1 to 96.9.

一句话总结：论文发现"加性注入"式循环Transformer在每一步重复注入输入信号时，共享的循环变换在"锚点"（输入条件参考态）处会产生一个非零的强制偏置（forcing bias），这个偏置会随深度累积并可能损害深度外推性能；作者提出SCSE，通过让循环模块只作用于"锚点相对偏差"并用零偏差掩码强制锚点为精确的单步不动点，在WikiText-103上（95.6M参数）将测试困惑度从117.1降到96.9（T=8），深度外推到T=48时从145.1降到125.9，同时在多个更强的基线上均取得最低困惑度。

Why This Matters

Removing a previously-unnamed architectural bug gives looped Transformers a free path to deeper test-time compute without added latency or parameters.

▲ Critical Edge

At C4 scale with 136.5M parameters, a plain tuned adapter actually beats SCSE within the trained depth range (93.86 vs 94.11 PPL at T=8), with SCSE's advantage emerging only at extrapolated depths beyond training.

Methodology

1

Full-state, shared-block loop — an embedding e = P_φ(x), initial state h_0 = H_0(e), and a shared recurrent core R_θ applied for T steps, h_{t+1}=R_θ(h_t,e,t), read out via C_ψ(RMSNorm(h_T)). The looped-Transformer baseline re-injects e additively at every step: h_{t+1}=h_t+sB_θ(h_t+αW_in e).

2

Anchor construction (once per sequence) — h* = e + a_ω(e), a learned anchor projection applied to the token-and-position representation, held fixed across all T recurrent steps.

3

Deviation-only recurrence — starting from Δ_0=H_0(e)-h*, the shared core computes q_t=s·G_θ(Δ_t) (s=0.50) acting purely on the anchor-relative deviation, with no additive re-injection of e at each step.

4

Zero-deviation mask — a per-example gate m=1{‖Δ_t‖²>ε} zeroes the update exactly when the deviation is numerically zero, giving Δ_{t+1}=Δ_t+m·q_t. This guarantees T_t(0;e)=0 exactly: the anchor is a one-step fixed point by construction. Final readout: h_T=h*+Δ_T.

Theoretical backbone: an exact decomposition T_t(Δ;e)=Δ+b_t(e)+a_t(Δ;e) separates the "zero-deviation forcing bias" b_t(e):=T_t(0;e) from the state-dependent term, with a finite-horizon counterfactual identity (Theorem 2) and sufficient conditions (Proposition 6) under which nonzero b_t(e) provably degrades task loss.
Control suite: compared against a capacity-matched adapter, a tuned adapter (learned gain α, tuned step s), a recurrent-step-conditioned adapter (Universal-Transformer-style sinusoidal step embedding), Step-FiLM, and an unshared fixed-depth Transformer-8 reference — across WikiText-2/103, OpenWebText, C4, and LAMBADA, at 22M/50M/95.6M/136.5M params and loop depths 4–48 (trained ≤8).

SCSE's anchor response is exactly zero by construction at every scale; additive-injection baselines retain a measurable, non-vanishing anchor response even at 95.6M parameters.

  • Anchor ablation (Table 2): the learned anchor h*=e+a_ω(e) gives 155.14±0.61 PPL at T=8 vs 160.55±1.44 (raw embedding anchor), 159.14±0.78 (frozen random-projection anchor), and 294.37±1.24 (initial-state anchor) — the last collapses the deviation recurrence entirely, since Δ≡0 the instant h*=H_0(e).
  • LAMBADA completion (T=32, 95.6M): SCSE 16.9±0.8k PPL / 3.42±0.16% accuracy vs baseline 23.0±0.3k / 1.60±0.12% and tuned adapter 18.5±1.4k / 2.86±0.04% — SCSE wins both PPL and accuracy.
  • Held-out C4 transfer (95.6M weights): SCSE 2.62±0.04k PPL (T=8) → 2.65±0.10k (T=48) vs baseline 2.85±0.01k → 2.97±0.04k.
  • Accelerator profiling: gains come at essentially no runtime premium — 96.4 PPL / 19.6ms at T=8 (vs baseline 117.3 PPL / 18.3ms) and 124.3 PPL / 89.9ms at T=48 (vs recurrent-step adapter 130.0 PPL / 100.1ms).

Strengths

  • Precise, provable characterization of a previously informal phenomenon (drift/instability in looped Transformers) via an exact finite-horizon bias-subtraction identity, not just an empirical patch.
  • Extensive, well-controlled baseline suite (capacity-matched, tuned, recurrent-step-conditioned, unshared reference, plus forcing-bias intervention ablations) that actively rules out extra parameters or step conditioning as the real source of the gains.
  • Consistent improvements across five datasets, three-plus model scales, adaptive-depth inference, and depth extrapolation up to 6× the training loop-depth, with essentially no runtime overhead.
  • Transparent reporting of a counter-example (C4 at 136.5M, Table 18) where SCSE is not the best method within the training depth range — increases trust in the rest of the numbers.

Limitations

  • Model scale capped at ~139M parameters and ~205M training tokens; no evidence yet at billion-parameter/billion-token scale, which the authors explicitly flag.
  • The zero-deviation forcing-bias condition is a design choice that removes one specific failure mode — it does not constrain off-anchor Jacobians, the state-dependent residual, or the separately identified "readout blind spot" (Sharma & Vu 2026).
  • The geometric case study (Figure 4, WikiText-2) is a single-replication qualitative diagnostic rather than a statistically aggregated result.
  • Mixed results at larger-scale C4 pretraining within the training loop-depth range (Table 18) suggest the benefit is most reliable at deep extrapolation rather than uniformly at all depths.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does the SCSE anchor-consistency condition still provide extrapolation benefits at billion-parameter scale, or does the mixed C4 result (Table 18) foreshadow diminishing returns as models grow?
  2. Can the learned anchor projection a_ω(e) be interpreted mechanistically — does it converge to something like a per-token "expected final representation"?
  3. How does SCSE interact with adaptive/learned halting policies, rather than the fixed max-token stopping rule used here?
  4. Would SCSE combined with recurrent-step-conditioning (Step-FiLM) compose additively, or does anchor invariance already subsume most of that benefit?
  5. Would the anchor-response diagnostic (b_t(e), R_t(e)) generalize to other repeatedly-applied-module settings, e.g. diffusion denoisers or iterative refinement networks with shared weights?
