2607.28319Fairness Pruning: Locating Demographic Bias in GLU-MLP Layers via Differential Activations

Zeroing ≤40 neurons keeps 99.49% of capability — but bias scores destabilize in opposite directions, not down.

归零≤40个神经元（占MLP宽度<0.031%）可保留99.49%通用能力，但因BiasScore不区分方向，8组实验中有6组的歧义/消歧偏见分数向相反方向移动——是"双向失稳"而非系统性去偏。

Key Metric
99.49%

Mean general-capability retention (WikiText, MMLU, ARC‑Challenge, HellaSwag EN/ES) on Llama‑3.2‑1B after zeroing ≤40 of 131,072 MLP neurons — 0.031% of MLP width. Range across experiments: 97.93%–101.34%.

Why This Matters

Maps a concrete failure mode for interpretability-driven bias mitigation: it shows unsigned neuron-selection criteria must become directional before pruning can reliably reduce, rather than just relocate, bias.

Critical Edge

BiasScore is an unsigned magnitude, so it mixes bias-amplifying and bias-suppressing neurons together — zeroing them shifts BBQ scores in opposite directions in 6 of 8 English experiments, and no random-neuron control was run to rule out that this is just noise from small ablations.

Methodology

Four training-free phases, cheap enough to run on a single consumer GPU (Colab L4). First, build contrastive prompt pairs per demographic category — token-identical except for one attribute (e.g. a "[Black/Asian] neighbor") — using five templates spanning occupational, healthcare, institutional, social, and educational contexts, each cut off mid-sentence to elicit a continuation.

Second, a forward hook captures activations at the down_proj input of every GLU/SwiGLU MLP block. A neuron's BiasScore is the mean absolute difference between the two prompts' per-sequence-mean activations, averaged across all pairs in a category — optionally blended with OptiPFair's structural-importance score into a FairnessPruningScore, though raw BiasScore ranking sufficed at this intervention scale.

Third, the top-K highest-BiasScore neurons are zeroed by clearing the matching rows of gate_proj/up_proj and column of down_proj — a symmetric ablation needing no retraining or architecture change.

Fourth, general capability (lm-evaluation-harness) and demographic bias (BBQ / EsBBQ, ambiguous vs. disambiguated context, 0-shot) are measured across Llama‑3.2‑1B, Llama‑3.2‑3B, and Salamandra‑2B, at K = 1, 5, 10, 20, 40, with fixed seed (42) and greedy decoding.

6 of 8 English experiments move in opposite directions

Aggregate English BBQ baseline (bias_ambig / bias_disambig) is 1.70% / 1.75%; the full variation range across 8 zeroing experiments is only 0.80 pp (ambiguous) and 0.50 pp (disambiguated). Example — Age Top-5: Δambig +0.19 pp vs. Δdisambig −0.16 pp, opposite signs from a single neuron set.

One neuron isn't the whole circuit

Zeroing only the single most dominant neuron (L15:166, Religion — BiasScore 32% higher than the runner-up) barely moves BBQ (Δambig +0.12 pp, Δdisambig −0.07 pp), and 5 of 9 qualitative generations stay byte-identical to baseline — the circuit is redundant, not a single storage node.

Strengths

  • Transparent, self-critical framing — the authors reject overselling zeroing as "mitigation" once bidirectional destabilization emerged, and reframe the contribution as causal localization.
  • Strong reproducibility: public code, two new bilingual datasets, pinned dependencies, fixed seeds, integration with lm-evaluation-harness.
  • Genuinely surgical scale (≤0.031% of MLP width) paired with multi-axis structural analysis — depth, spatial layout, cross-category overlap, cross-lingual consistency — across three models and two languages.
  • A real, actionable negative result: the sign-inversion pattern cautions the field against unsigned-magnitude neuron-selection criteria.

Limitations

  • Restricted to explicit-attribute, multiple-choice bias measurement (BBQ/EsBBQ); open-ended generation and implicit-attribute bias (names, occupations) are not tested systematically.
  • Scoped to MLP/GLU blocks only — attention mechanisms, which may also encode demographic bias, are entirely excluded.
  • Token-parity requirement for contrastive pairs is a hard constraint, especially limiting for morphologically rich Spanish, shrinking Salamandra-2B's valid pair set.
  • Zeroing stays conservative (max 40 neurons); it's untested whether dissociable-circuits and retention findings hold under more aggressive interventions.
  • No random-neuron or alternative-baseline control is reported to confirm BiasScore-selected neurons behave differently from arbitrary same-layer neurons at matched scale.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Would a signed BiasScore (using asymmetric, known-direction corpora, as proposed in Section VI-C) produce monotonic, controllable bias reduction on the same neuron sets?
  • How would this method compare against a random-neuron-zeroing control at matched sparsity — how much of the observed retention and bias movement is BiasScore-specific vs. generic robustness to small ablations?
  • Is the final-layer concentration of bias signal a genuine architectural property of GLU-MLPs, or an artifact of measuring only at down_proj_input rather than across all layers' gate_proj/up_proj activations?
  • Would incorporating attention-head analysis change the localization picture, given attention is excluded entirely and could hold a meaningful share of the bias?
  • Does the bidirectional destabilization pattern hold in open-ended, production-style generation at scale, and would human-rated harm assessment corroborate the BBQ-based sign-inversion findings?
  • Do final-layer concentration, low cross-category overlap, and moderate cross-lingual transfer hold for production-scale models well beyond 4B parameters?
