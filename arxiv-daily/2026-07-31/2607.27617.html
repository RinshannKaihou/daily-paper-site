On Llama-3-8B the same contest gives Shared 1.187 nats vs. 1.481 for Local — a larger Sharedness Gain of 0.294 nats (95% CI [0.208, 0.391]). Mean future-signature fidelity differs by at most 0.011 nats across all four architectures — the win is a coding-cost advantage, not a large accuracy gap.

Why This Matters

Gives interpretability researchers a falsifiable, capacity-matched test for whether LLMs reuse one shared "workspace" interface, not just another single-probe claim.

▲ Critical Edge

In the blind model-organism test, 1 of 4 held-out Local-architecture organisms was misclassified as Shared — an admitted false-positive that undercuts the paper's central identifiability claim.

Methodology

1

Forked future signature — for a cached prefix state h, a future operation k is sampled only after h is fixed, and the continuation distribution σ_k(h) it induces defines h's causal signature. Two states are "future-equivalent" only if their signatures match under a KL/JS-based distortion measure (FSD) — this rules out probes that key off the current answer or researcher-imposed latent labels.

2

Architecture contest — four candidate encoder/decoder families compete to compress that signature under a prequential causal MDL objective (two-part code plus sequential cross-entropy over 20 held-out folds), subject to a worst-case FSD fidelity tolerance:

  • Shared — one operation-agnostic code
  • Local — private code per operation
  • Mixture — gated shared/local blend
  • Distributed — no privileged bottleneck
3

Synthetic data — four "forkable episode" families (relation-world, program-state, proof/discourse-state, attribute/event-world; 10,600/2,280/3,020 train/val/test) with semantic-collision and lexical-anti-collision controls that dissociate current-answer identity from future-signature identity, closing off shortcut solutions.

4

Three mechanistic arms — role-aligned cross-architecture transplantation (same-family / cross-family / matched-random donors), sparse path-level mediation with matched-null path controls, and a blind synthetic model-organism recovery test against four known ground-truth routing graphs.

Capacity is matched, not just accuracy: rank, sparsity, parameter count, and optimization budget are held equal across all four candidates, so Shared cannot win the MDL contest simply by being under-powered.

  • Model-organism blind test: 14/16 held-out synthetic organisms correctly classified; Shared, Mixture, and Distributed recovered perfectly (4/4 each), but among 4 Local organisms only 2 are labeled correctly — 1 mislabeled Shared, 1 Mixture.
  • Mediation: API-aligned causal paths mediate 0.749 ± 0.089 of the target effect vs. 0.150 ± 0.121 for matched-null paths (~5×); graph sparsity 0.791 ± 0.023 with 0.83 cross-seed Jaccard overlap; 7/64 candidate path dimensions survive BH-FDR correction.
  • Donor-identity gradient: Shared CompScore drops monotonically from 0.88 (same-family donor) to 0.78 (cross-family) to 0.53 (matched-random) — consistent with role-aligned reuse rather than incidental compatibility.
  • Directional robustness: Sharedness Gain stays positive across five more backbones (Qwen2.5-7B, Mistral-7B, Gemma-2-9B, Phi-3-3.8B) — but this sweep checks only the sign of the effect, not the full transplantation/mediation pipeline.

Strengths

  • Rigorous, falsifiable operationalization of "shared internal interface" via capacity-matched architecture competition rather than fitting one probe and declaring success.
  • Four independent, complementary validation arms (OOD splits, transplantation, mediation, synthetic model organisms), each targeting a distinct alternative explanation.
  • Transparent statistical protocol — paired family-level bootstrap, permutation tests, TOST equivalence, BH-FDR correction — with layer/rank/threshold choices frozen on validation data before test evaluation.
  • Honest disclosure of a negative result (the Local→Shared misclassification) instead of suppressing it, and careful "economy-of-reuse" framing rather than an overclaimed accuracy gap.

Limitations

  • Every family-stratified, transplantation, and mediation conclusion rests on just two relatively small models (Qwen2.5-1.5B, Llama-3-8B) and a finite, pre-registered operation bank.
  • All four data families are synthetic and structurally templated — held-out "family" generalization stays inside a closed, generator-controlled world, weaker than open-domain dialogue, tool use, or long-context reasoning.
  • Ground-truth model organisms are tiny (6-layer, width-256, vocab-512, 3,000 training steps) — the authors themselves note synthetic organisms may be easier to recover than mechanisms formed during large-scale pretraining.
  • No code/data release found anywhere in the 50-page paper, despite all experiments running on fully synthetic, generator-controlled data that would be straightforward to share.
  • Transplantation conclusions depend on donor/base matching, locality definitions, and the specific candidate path universe searched; redundant routes can compensate after blocking.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Would the full transplantation and mediation pipeline — not just directional Sharedness Gain — reproduce the same Shared-dominant ordering on larger backbones like Gemma-2-9B?
  2. What specifically causes the one Local→Shared misclassification: seed idiosyncrasy, the Local routing configuration, or a structural limit of FSD/MDL discriminability at the tested capacity?
  3. How sensitive is the Sharedness Gain to the choice of operation bank K — would a richer or adversarially chosen bank split "Shared" into something closer to Mixture or Distributed?
  4. Does the recovered "Hidden API" correspond to any previously identified SAE/transcoder circuit on the same models, and can the two literatures be cross-validated?
  5. With no code/data release, would the reported MDL, FSD, and mediation numbers replicate under independent reimplementation, given several validation-selected hyperparameters (λ_r, λ_g, rank=64, FSD tolerance ε_f)?
