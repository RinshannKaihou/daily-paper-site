RepBench: Compiling Benchmarks into Capability Representations for Large Language Models ↗
Yanshi Li, Xueru Bai, Shuman Liu, Long Zhang · 2026-07-30
4 authors, all affiliated with Shopee (Sea Limited) · industry-lab paper, no established prior track record in representation engineering per the paper's own citation list · July 2026 arXiv preprint, no indication of peer review yet · credibility rests on the scale and transparency of the empirical effort (13,427 papers crawled, 353 datasets audited, full audit log released) rather than institutional reputation.
Key Metric
0.778 mean, 30% wins
Diff-mean's grand-mean LOBO-AUC across all 1,128 capability×model cells (94 capabilities × 12 models) — the best average of 4 readouts (LR 0.769, PCA 0.734, J-Lens 0.650). Yet it wins only 30% of individual cells, while LR wins 38% — "best on average" and "best most often" disagree.
Why This Matters

Gives representation-engineering researchers a reusable, benchmark-grounded probe corpus and protocol — replacing the field's default of building throwaway synthetic contrast sets per paper.

Critical Edge

The SAE readout — arguably the most "modern" method compared — is evaluated on exactly one model and layer (Gemma-2-9B-IT, L21), so the headline "SAE trails other readouts" finding rests on n=1, not a general result.

Methodology

A data-layer contribution, not a new probing algorithm
1Crawl 13,427 benchmark papers, extract capability targets via LLM annotation.
→
2Embed & cluster 14,896 mentions → 9,576 concepts → 182 clusters / 13 families.
→
3Keep 94 text-probeable clusters, harvest 353 datasets → 46,149 probe texts.
→
4Two-stage semantic audit removes invalid dataset→capability mappings.
→
5Evaluate 4 readouts under LOBO transfer.

Each capability's vector is built by averaging probe-text hidden states within each backing benchmark first, then equally across benchmarks (Eq. 1) — so one oversized dataset can't dominate the direction. The LOBO protocol then holds out one benchmark per capability at a time: the direction is fit on the rest, scored by AUC against the held-out benchmark's texts. The paper notes within-split evaluation (no held-out benchmark) saturates near AUC≈1.0 by exploiting dataset-specific formatting — so LOBO, not within-split, is the reported measure.

12 models are probed: Qwen3 (0.6B–32B), Qwen3.5-9B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Gemma-2-9B-IT, Gemma-4 (12B/31B), R1-Distill-Qwen3-8B (a distilled contrast against its own base), and DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Base (~275B MoE, probed with no chat template). Strengths

What holds up
  • +Targets a documented reproducibility gap — paper-specific synthetic probes — with a benchmark-grounded, multi-source alternative at large scale (13,427 papers, 353 datasets, 46,149 texts).
  • +Transparent audit with a quantified removal rate (25/378, 6.6%) and a released 64-row audit log, not an opaque "we manually verified everything" claim.
  • +LOBO is demonstrably harder than within-split evaluation (which saturates near AUC=1.0), making reported numbers a more meaningful generalization measure.
  • +12 architecturally and scale-diverse models (0.6B to ~275B-MoE) × 4 readout families under one shared protocol, reporting both grand-mean and per-cell-win views.
  • +Reports failure modes candidly: 4 universally-weak capabilities, low ARI vs. the human taxonomy, single-model-only SAE evaluation — all stated, not omitted.

Limitations

What to weigh against it
  • −Text-only probing covers just 94 of 182 clusters (~52%) — all 31 multimodal-grounding and all 23 planning/tool-use clusters get zero coverage.
  • −SAE comparison is limited to one model/layer (Gemma-2-9B-IT, L21), preventing generalization of the "SAE trails other readouts" finding.
  • −Taxonomy and mapping construction depend heavily on LLM judgments (clustering, naming, audit prioritization), with human review only for flagged cases — judge-model bias is not directly assessed.
  • −Only last-token activations at four fractional depths are evaluated; no ablation of pooling strategy or the fixed ≤30-per-dataset negative-sampling cap.
  • −No steering/causal-intervention evaluation despite the "reading and steering" motivating frame — only the reading/probing side is tested.
  • −The one base (non-instruction-tuned) model, DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Base, also has a distinct architecture and the lowest AUC (0.720) — confounding "no chat template" with "different architecture."

Questions for Further Exploration

Open threads from the paper's own discussion
  • Would the readout ranking (Diff-mean vs. LR vs. PCA vs. J-Lens) change under a different negative-sampling cap or a capability-frequency-weighted scheme?
  • Does the low ARI (0.05–0.13) between model-discovered clusters and the human taxonomy hold under alternative clustering algorithms, or is k-means specifically prone to a different partition?
  • How much of DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Base's lower AUC (0.720, lowest of 12) owes to missing instruction-tuning versus its distinct four-stream mHC architecture?
  • Would extending to the 88 uncovered multimodal/agentic clusters preserve the same interior-clustering-optimum and low-ARI findings?
  • Since SAE-AUC trails other readouts on only one model/layer, would a multi-feature SAE readout close the gap under the same LOBO protocol?
