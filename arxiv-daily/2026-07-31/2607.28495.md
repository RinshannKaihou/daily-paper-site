> **一句话总结：** 论文用固定前缀的BF16/FP32对照和双向KV缓存移植实验证明，在Qwen2.5-14B衍生的多分支推理系统中，"用新prefill重建的token前缀"并不等价于"真实自回归解码保留下来的KV缓存状态"——BF16下166/200条后缀轨迹会因此分叉，而完整的48层K/V缓存移植能在24/24（及后续48个中43/43）条分叉样本上让续写完全跟随缓存捐赠者，说明KV缓存本身是分叉轨迹的因果充分载体，数值精度只是调节这种分叉是否表现出来的开关。

> **TL;DR:** In a custom Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct-derived multi-branch reasoning system, the paper shows that reconstructing a token prefix via fresh one-shot prefill is not a faithful substitute for the KV-cache state reached by live incremental decoding at a merge-stage boundary. On a 200-item frozen GPQA Main holdout in BF16, live vs. prefill continuations diverge on 166/200 suffixes (83.0%) and 49/200 answers (24.5%) despite nearly identical net accuracy (93/200 vs. 95/200, paired 95% CI [-3.5, +5.5]); the same fixed-prefix comparison run in FP32 shows 0/200 disagreement (Wilson 95% CI upper bound 1.88%). Bidirectional transplantation of all 48 key/value layers then makes every tested divergent continuation follow its cache donor (24/24 at the primary checkpoint, 43/43 in an outcome-blind replication at a later checkpoint), identifying the KV cache as a causally sufficient carrier of the divergent trajectory.

## 核心贡献

- **匹配的live-cache/fresh-prefill实验矩阵**：在相同200道GPQA Main题目上，让"保留的实时KV缓存"和"用相同整数token一次性prefill重建的缓存"在同一后续解码器下续写，并各自配一个存储隔离的复本以估计"复现地板"（replica floor）。复本在BF16和FP32下都是逐字节精确的，但live和prefill两种构造在BF16下会在166/200条后缀、49/200个答案、20/200个正确性标签上产生分歧，而整体准确率几乎不变（93/200 vs 95/200）。
- **固定前缀2×2精度对照**：把同一批200个离散边界状态（整数token前缀）分别在BF16和FP32算术下跑一遍。BF16复现了同样的分歧规模（166/200后缀等），FP32下分歧则完全消失（0/200，Wilson上界1.88%），从而把"数值精度"和"离散token是否相同"这两个混杂因素分离开——分歧不是因为前缀不同，而是数值精度调节了缓存差异是否会被放大成可观测的行为分歧。
- **双向KV缓存直接移植与跨检查点复制**：把prefill重建缓存与增量重建缓存之间的全部48层key/value张量互相替换（保持token、角色、mask、位置、边界logits不变），结果显示：在主检查点上24/24个先前分叉的样本、在另一个后期检查点的盲选48行样本中43/43个分叉样本，续写轨迹都完全跟随缓存捐赠方；而8个/5个"本来就一致"的对照样本中没有一个因移植产生新轨迹，证明完整KV缓存是分叉轨迹的因果充分（causally sufficient）载体，而非仅仅相关。

## Key Contributions

- **Matched fidelity and precision controls**: A four-arm design compares a retained live `DynamicCache` against a one-shot prefill of the identical integer-token prefix at a reasoning-stage boundary (the `<|merge|>` token in a three-branch-generate/merge/answer pipeline), each with a storage-isolated replica to establish a "replica floor." All replicas are bit-exact in both BF16 and FP32, but the live and prefill constructions diverge behaviorally in BF16. A subsequent fixed-prefix 2×2 crossing (same 200 frozen token prefixes run in BF16 and FP32) isolates numerical precision as a moderator, since it removes the confound of the two constructions reaching different discrete prefixes.
- **Direct state intervention via bidirectional cache transplantation**: Rather than only correlating cache differences with output differences, the paper physically swaps all 48 key/value layer pairs between the incremental (teacher-forced) and prefill-reconstructed caches, in both directions, while holding tokens, positions, masks, and pre-intervention boundary logits fixed. This directly tests causal sufficiency of the KV cache as the carrier of the divergent trajectory, rather than merely localizing effects to the residual stream (as prior work had only proposed as future work).
- **A compact, pre-registered fidelity protocol**: The design explicitly separates four distinct claims that stage-replay work commonly conflates — replica stability (within-construction repeatability), token identity (same discrete prefix), live-state fidelity (same internal state), and causal state sufficiency (does the candidate state variable actually drive the divergence) — and shows the first two do not imply the third or fourth.

## Methodology

The test system is a Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct-derived checkpoint fine-tuned for a "generate three reasoning branches → learned merge → final answer" pipeline, using six ordered token blocks (`prompt`, three `cot` branches, `merge`, `answer`) with role-conditioned embeddings and a role-visibility attention mask (answer tokens cannot attend directly to branch tokens; Table 2). The object of study is the decoder state immediately after the `<|merge|>` token is processed, i.e., a whole-reasoning-stage boundary rather than a mid-sequence token.

Three cache "constructions" are defined and never conflated: a **live** cache retained from ordinary autoregressive generation; an **incremental** cache rebuilt token-by-token under teacher forcing from the same fixed token IDs; and a **prefill** cache built from those tokens in a single forward call. A **replica** is an independent repeat/copy used only to measure the within-construction noise floor.

Five experiments, run on a frozen 200-item holdout from GPQA Main, greedy decoding (temperature 0, top-p=1, top-k=0), Hugging Face Transformers + PyTorch SDPA on a single NVIDIA B200:
1. **Matched live/fresh-prefill matrix**: four storage-isolated arms (live + its replica, prefill + its replica) sharing one suffix decoder; run separately in BF16 and FP32.
2. **Fixed-prefix precision-by-construction matrix**: the same 200 discrete boundary states (BF16 token IDs, 1,025–5,080 tokens long) are held fixed while crossing BF16 vs. FP32 arithmetic, isolating precision as the sole varying factor.
3. **Live-to-incremental bridge**: 12 prospectively selected rows where live decoding reaches a new boundary and an incremental reconstruction from the same freshly-produced prefix is compared tensor-for-tensor (48 layers) to validate that incremental construction is a faithful stand-in for ordinary live caching.
4. **Bidirectional KV-cache transplantation**: on 32 pre-registered rows (24 known-divergent, 8 known-exact controls) from the fixed-prefix matrix, all 48 key/value layer pairs are swapped between the incremental donor and the prefill recipient (and vice versa), holding tokens/positions/masks/pre-intervention logits fixed, to test whether the transplanted continuation follows the cache donor.
5. **Outcome-blind checkpoint replication**: the same transplantation protocol repeated on 48 rows selected purely by prefix length (no prior outcome knowledge) on a distinct, later 14B checkpoint from the same training family, to rule out cherry-picking and single-checkpoint dependence.

Statistics: Wilson 95% intervals for binary disagreement/recovery rates, 20,000-replicate paired bootstrap for accuracy differences, exact two-sided McNemar tests for discordant correctness labels. The analysis plan (primary endpoints, exclusion rules) was frozen before running the full arms in Experiments 2, 4, and 5.

## Results & Highlights

- **Experiment 1 (matched live/prefill, BF16)**: replicas are exact on all 200/200 items (cache, logits, suffixes, labels), but live vs. prefill continuations diverge on **166/200 suffixes (83.0%, 95% Wilson CI [77.18, 87.57])**, **49/200 answers (24.5%, [19.06, 30.90])**, and **20/200 correctness labels (10.0%)**, while net accuracy is nearly unchanged: **live 93/200 vs. prefill 95/200**, paired difference **+1.0 pp (95% CI [-3.5, +5.5], exact McNemar p = 0.824)** — i.e., item-level churn with no net accuracy effect.
- **Experiment 2 (fixed-prefix BF16 vs. FP32)**: on the identical 200 fixed prefixes, BF16 reproduces the same divergence (166/200 suffix, 49/200 answer, 20/200 correctness), while **FP32 shows 0/200 divergence on every metric (Wilson 95% CI upper bound 1.88%)**. The paired FP32-minus-BF16 reductions are **83.0 pp for suffix divergence (bootstrap 95% CI [77.5, 88.0], p = 2.14×10⁻⁵⁰)**, **24.5 pp for answer divergence ([18.5, 30.51], p = 3.55×10⁻¹⁵)**, and **10.0 pp for correctness-label churn ([6.0, 14.5], p = 1.91×10⁻⁶)**; incremental/prefill accuracy is 93/200 vs. 95/200 in BF16 versus 92/92 vs. 92/92 in FP32 (0.0 pp difference), and the median cache relative L2 difference drops from 0.01224 (BF16) to 4.27×10⁻⁶ (FP32).
- **Experiment 4 (bidirectional KV transplantation, primary checkpoint)**: on all 24 pre-registered prior-divergent rows, both transplant directions cause the recipient to follow its cache donor's suffix — **24/24 donor recovery in both directions (95% Wilson CI [86.2, 100]%)** — while **0/8 exact-control rows produce a new trajectory ([0, 32.4]%)**.
- **Experiment 5 (outcome-blind replication, later checkpoint)**: on 48 length-selected rows (no prior outcome read), 43/48 (89.6%, [77.8, 95.5]%) diverge naturally between incremental and prefill constructions; **bidirectional donor recovery holds on 43/43 divergent rows ([91.8, 100]%)**, but only 5/48 rows were naturally exact, one short of the pre-registered minimum of six needed to call this "strong replication" — the paper labels this "conditional replication with an underpowered control stratum," a self-imposed downgrade rather than an overclaim.
- No quantitative results are reported for a stock (non-custom) model as the primary claim; a bounded side-check on public Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct under vLLM's beta batch-invariant mode found singleton-vs-paired execution preserved token trajectory on only 7/10 and log-probability trace on 6/10 prompts — offered explicitly as a limited cross-check, not part of the main claim set.

## Authors & Credibility

Single-author paper by Alexander Boesgaard Lorup, listed with an independent/personal affiliation (printed as "Openhagen" in the PDF, apparently intended as Copenhagen). The acknowledgements state that LLM tools were used only for language editing since the author is not a native English speaker, while "all experimental design, implementation, analysis, and scientific claims are the author's own." The paper is part of a cluster of closely related, similarly-styled solo-author 2026 arXiv preprints on LLM inference nondeterminism and replay fidelity that cite each other heavily (Matteson 2026 "Re-feeding is not replaying," Chodavarapu and Xu 2026 "The illusion of equivalence," Chu et al. 2026 "MarginGate," Zhang et al. 2026, and the author's own prior work Lorup 2026 on counterfactual-likelihood tests) — this tight, mutually-citing niche cluster of unaffiliated authors publishing structurally similar micro-studies is worth noting as a pattern, though it does not by itself invalidate the specific empirical claims made here, which are grounded in released digests and per-item evidence rather than narrative alone. No venue/peer review is indicated; this reads as a preprint.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The core idea — that KV-cache-off-vs-on inference divergence exists — is not new (the paper itself credits Chodavarapu and Xu 2026 for demonstrating FP16 cache-on/cache-off divergence and for proposing direct KV-tensor intervention as future work). This paper's genuine contribution is executing that proposed intervention (bidirectional whole-cache transplantation) at a *reasoning-stage boundary* in a *custom multi-branch* system, and pairing it with a fixed-prefix precision crossing that cleanly separates "different discrete state" from "same discrete state, different arithmetic path." That is a real, if narrow, methodological advance.

**Rigor**: Above average for a solo preprint — pre-registered endpoints, Wilson/bootstrap/McNemar statistics throughout, explicit integrity gates (replica exactness checked before any comparison is trusted), and an admirable self-critique in Experiment 5 (labeling its own result "underpowered" because it has 5 exact controls instead of the pre-registered 6, rather than quietly passing it off as a clean replication). The paper explicitly disclaims things it does *not* show: no universal BF16/FP32 law, no identification of which specific kernel operation causes the drift (since key and value tensors are swapped jointly, their individual roles are not separated), and no claim that the finding generalizes beyond the tested BF16-reachable prefix population.

**Reproducibility**: Strong on paper — SHA-256 digests for weights, config, tokenizer, and role-embedding tables are given for both checkpoints, and the release purports to include frozen problem-ID order, per-item scores, and cache-difference summaries. However, the underlying model checkpoint itself ("Qwen2.5-14B-derived after a merge-targeted policy update") is a private, custom fine-tune not released publicly — so nobody outside the author can actually re-run the core experiment end-to-end; only the released digests and derived statistics can be audited.

**Significance**: The practical takeaway — "don't assume reconstructed-prefix replay equals the live decoder state without checking" — is a useful, generalizable caution for anyone building stage-replay diagnostics, counterfactual token-credit estimators, or distillation pipelines from privileged intermediate states. But the empirical base is a single custom 14B multi-branch architecture, one dataset (GPQA Main, 200 items), one hardware/software stack (single B200, HF SDPA, no certified batch-invariant kernel as the primary surface), which sharply limits how far the specific numbers generalize; the paper is candid about this in its own Limitations section (population/selection, mechanistic resolution, external validity).

**Red flag / weak point worth naming**: the motivating backstory — the audit was triggered because the author's own separate self-training campaign had misinterpreted replayed successful branches as evidence of a "late merge-conversion barrier," an inference invalidated once replay was shown not to reflect live state — is disclosed but then explicitly bracketed out ("the training campaign itself is not a contribution of this paper"). That is honest, but it also means the paper's real-world motivating stakes (was the training conclusion actually wrong, and by how much?) are left unresolved and unquantified.

## Strengths

- Clean separation of four otherwise-conflated concepts (replica stability, token identity, live-state fidelity, causal sufficiency) into a reusable measurement protocol.
- Direct causal intervention (KV transplantation) rather than only correlational observation, replicated blind on an independent later checkpoint (43/43 recovery) to rule out cherry-picking.
- Transparent, pre-registered statistics with exact tests and explicit self-downgrading of a result that narrowly misses its own registered threshold (5 vs. 6 exact controls).
- Precision-crossing design (BF16 vs. FP32 on identical fixed prefixes) is a genuinely useful control that isolates numerical precision as a moderating variable rather than a confound.

## Limitations

- Single custom Qwen2.5-14B-derived multi-branch checkpoint (two checkpoints from the same training family) — no architecture-family, task-family, or sampling-temperature diversity.
- Only one benchmark (GPQA Main, 200-item frozen holdout), itself pre-selected via an earlier, undisclosed "breadth screen."
- Neither the BF16 nor FP32 arm uses a certified batch-invariant kernel, so the causal path from precision to divergence is not decomposed into a specific reduction-order or kernel mechanism.
- The KV transplant swaps keys and values jointly across all 48 layers at once — no per-layer or key-vs-value ablation to localize which components actually carry the divergence.
- The transplant experiments are explicitly conditioned on known-divergent rows (or, in Experiment 5, a length-based panel with no baseline population estimate), so the 24/24 and 43/43 recovery rates describe sufficiency within a selected stratum, not the population-wide prevalence of cache-mediated divergence.
- The underlying fine-tuned checkpoint and prompt text are not publicly released, limiting independent end-to-end reproduction to what the author's frozen digests/summaries permit.

## Relevance

对于关注LLM推理时非确定性、KV缓存复用、以及"用replay/prefill重建中间状态做反事实分析或蒸馏"这类方法的用户，这篇论文提供了一个值得参考的方法论警示：在多分支/多阶段推理流水线中，用新prefill重建的前缀即便token完全一致、复现性完美，也不能假定其内部状态与真实自回归解码状态等价，数值精度（BF16 vs FP32）会显著调节这一差异是否表现为行为分歧。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would per-layer or key-only/value-only transplantation localize the divergence to a subset of the 48 layers, rather than requiring a full-cache swap?
- Does the same live-vs-prefill divergence pattern hold under a certified batch-invariant attention kernel (e.g., vLLM's batch-invariant mode) rather than plain HF SDPA, given the paper's own bounded 10-prompt side-check showed partial batch sensitivity even there?
- What was the actual magnitude of the training-conclusion error in the author's original self-training campaign that motivated this audit — did the "late merge-conversion barrier" claim end up being false, or merely unverifiable?
- Would the 83pp BF16 suffix-divergence rate reproduce on other model families (e.g., Llama, Mistral) or non-reasoning-stage boundaries (mid-sequence tokens rather than a merge-stage boundary), given the paper's claim is explicitly scoped to "this tested ordinary BF16 surface"?
- Since only 5/48 rows were naturally exact in Experiment 5 (short of the pre-registered 6), would a larger outcome-blind panel push the exact-control recovery rate away from the current wide [0, 43.4]% interval in either direction?
