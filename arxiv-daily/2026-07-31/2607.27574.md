> **一句话总结：** 论文指出对比激活类的转向方法（CAA/COAST/K-Steer）在前馈策略网络上会失效，因为它们对比的是决策之后才分叉的表征；作者提出 Policy Gradient Steering（PGS），用 REINFORCE 式的策略梯度直接把动作和标量行为回报关联起来构造一个可移除的加性激活向量，在双路径网格世界、国际象棋战术组合、Google Research Football 多智能体传球行为三个场景中，PGS 用远小于微调/LoRA 的存储量（如国际象棋任务仅 1,152 个标量 vs 微调 663,552 个）取得相当或更优的转向效果（如足球 group_pressure 自对弈完成传球数从基线 6.00±4.58 提升到 29.80±16.04，接近 LoRA 的 32.00±17.68，远超 ReFT 的 16.00±13.51 和微调的 4.80±5.03）。

> **TL;DR:** PGS reformulates activation steering as policy-gradient credit assignment: accumulate return-weighted score-function gradients (REINFORCE) over a small batch of scored trajectories into one additive, removable activation vector, with strength calibrated via a local Fisher-based KL budget. On Google Research Football's `group_pressure` self-play, PGS raises completed passes from a 6.00±4.58 baseline to 29.80±16.04 — matching LoRA (32.00±17.68) and beating ReFT (16.00±13.51) and full fine-tuning (4.80±5.03) at similar policy-KL budgets (~0.10-0.12) — while on chess tactics with a frozen Maia-1500 policy it reaches a composed gain of 0.804±0.014 across three tactical motifs (forks/pins/skewers) using only 1,152 stored scalars versus 663,552 for fine-tuning and 46,080 for LoRA. Existing contrastive activation-steering baselines (CAA, COAST, K-Steer) provably fail on a toy two-route gridworld MLP policy because their contrasted activations are identical at the branching observation.

## 核心贡献

- **诊断对比激活转向的失效模式**：在一个双路径网格世界里，前馈 MLP 策略在分叉观测处的激活只依赖当前观测，因此 CAA/COAST/K-Steer 从"成功路线 vs 失败路线"轨迹构造的对比向量在分叉点处两者相等（v_CAA = h⁺ - h⁻ = h - h = 0），只能编码决策之后到达的状态，而不能编码决策本身。
- **提出 Policy Gradient Steering (PGS)**：把任意标量轨迹反馈转化为一个可移除的激活干预。核心是用似然比构造（REINFORCE / policy gradient theorem）在选定激活层累积 v_PGS = E[ρ_t (G_t - b(o_t)) ∇_h log π(a_t|o_t)]，只需一次梯度更新，再用激活空间 Fisher 曲率在局部二次近似下把强度 α 校准到指定的动作 KL 预算内。
- **系统评估组合性（compositionality）**：在国际象棋上分别独立拟合 fork/pin/skewer 三种战术目标的 PGS 向量，证明它们可以在推理时直接相加组合，且每个目标的独立收益在组合后依然保留（composed gain 与 min retention 均为正），组合效果与 LoRA、微调相当，但存储量小几十到几百倍；并将评估扩展到 Google Research Football 的多智能体竞技场景，证明单个团队策略的行为漂移在不同对手间可迁移。

## Key Contributions

1. **Policy Gradient Steering**, a method that converts arbitrary scalar feedback on trajectories into a removable activation intervention by assigning credit to the policy's actions (via a REINFORCE-style policy-gradient estimator) rather than to post-outcome representations.
2. **A controlled gridworld analysis** showing that outcome-based activation contrasts (CAA, COAST, K-Steer) can encode states reached *after* a decision without controlling the decision itself, because pre-decision activations of a feed-forward policy are identical across contrasted trajectories at the branch point.
3. **An empirical evaluation across chess and competitive football** examining (a) how independently fitted PGS vectors for different tactical/behavioral objectives compose without retraining or joint rescaling, and (b) how an intervention's effect depends on the controlled policy and its strategic/multi-agent context.

## Methodology

PGS treats post-training behavioral adaptation as an RL problem. Given a frozen pretrained policy π(a|o) and a temporary scalar behavioral reward r_b, the behavioral return at time t is a discounted sum G_t = Σ γ^(k-t) r_b(o_k, a_k). Given a small dataset D of reward-scored trajectories collected by a (possibly different) behavior policy μ, PGS estimates the state-independent additive steering vector at a chosen activation h as

v_PGS = E_D[ρ_t (G_t − b(o_t)) ∇_h log π(a_t|o_t)],

where ρ_t = π(a_t|o_t)/μ(a_t|o_t) is a per-decision importance ratio (treated as fixed, not differentiated through) and b(o_t) is an action-independent baseline for variance reduction. This is exactly the REINFORCE/policy-gradient-theorem gradient, but instead of updating weights, it is accumulated into a single removable vector applied additively at inference (h ← h + α·v_PGS), leaving the base policy frozen. Unlike CAA/COAST/K-Steer, which contrast activations associated with different *outcomes* or attribute classifiers, PGS differentiates with respect to the action likelihood itself, so it can distinguish two actions even when their pre-action representations are identical (∇_h log π(a⁺|o) ≠ ∇_h log π(a⁻|o) even when h⁺ = h⁻).

Intervention strength α is calibrated (not hand-tuned) from the activation-space empirical Fisher matrix F = E[∇_h log π(a|o) ∇_h log π(a|o)ᵀ]; under a local quadratic approximation to the action-KL divergence, α = √(2ε / (v_PGSᵀ F v_PGS)) for a chosen KL budget ε, mirroring trust-region calibration (à la TRPO) but applied to a single fixed direction rather than an optimization trajectory. The method uses one gradient update per fitted objective, versus three fixed-batch updates for the ReFT/LoRA/fine-tuning baselines in the main comparisons. Ablations (Appendix D) confirm the approach is sensitive to activation-site choice (pre- vs post-ReLU, single vs. joint sites), optimizer learning rate, and the number of fitting trajectories/updates, but is robust to the specific return-baseline treatment (raw vs. centered vs. standardized vs. learned linear baseline — centering/standardizing perform equivalently and the simple batch-centered return is used throughout).

Three testbeds probe distinct challenges: (1) a two-route gridworld MLP policy isolates action-credit failure of outcome contrasts; (2) chess tactical puzzles (forks/pins/skewers) using a frozen Maia-1500 human-move-prediction policy (LCZeroLens encoding, 1,858-move policy head) test whether independently fitted tactical interventions compose constructively; (3) Google Research Football with four frozen team policies (`defensive_passer`, `flank_pass`, `group_pressure`, `offensive_passer` from Song et al. 2024) test multi-agent behavior steering (completed passes, possession regains, shot attempts) and cross-opponent transfer.

## Results & Highlights

- **Gridworld route steering (Table 1, frozen-policy rollouts):** PGS shifts the requested route preference to top-route probability 0.82±0.07 while preserving path efficiency (path length 4.51±0.17 vs. unsteered 4.36±0.07); all activation-steering baselines stay near the unsteered distribution — CAA reaches only 0.41±0.07 top-route probability, COAST 0.49±0.09, K-Steer 0.45±0.08, essentially indistinguishable from "No intervention" (0.43±0.06).
- **Extreme low-data regime (Table 3, one demonstration per route):** With just two demonstrations total, PGS still shifts route preference in both directions (top-route prob 0.82±0.07, bottom-route prob 0.83±0.08), whereas CAA remains near baseline (top 0.40±0.07); fine-tuning is the only baseline that matches or exceeds PGS here (bottom-route prob 0.86±0.11), showing PGS is not uniformly the strongest method, just the most sample-efficient among steering-style (non-parameter-update) approaches.
- **Chess tactical composition (Table 4, matched six-residual-block support, Maia-1500):** PGS achieves isolated gain 0.403±0.005 and composed gain 0.804±0.014 across fork/pin/skewer objectives — comparable to LoRA (0.803±0.023, 46,080 scalars) and fine-tuning (0.807±0.020, 663,552 scalars) and above ReFT (0.761±0.012, 9,288 scalars) — while storing only 1,152 scalars, roughly 40× smaller than LoRA and 575× smaller than fine-tuning for comparable composed performance.
- **Cross-checkpoint robustness (Table 5):** The composition pattern holds across Maia-1100/1500/1900 checkpoints of different simulated skill; PGS's composed gain (e.g., 0.897±0.021 at maia-1100, 0.928±0.008 at maia-1900) stays competitive with or exceeds the parameter-update baselines at each skill level, though the paper is explicit that "the experiment does not support a checkpoint-independent ranking of adaptation methods."
- **Football passing behavior (Table 8, `group_pressure` self-play, shared action-KL budget ≈0.10-0.12):** PGS raises completed passes from a 6.00±4.58 baseline to 29.80±16.04 (attempts 12.80±7.73 → 64.00±30.98), comparable to the strongest baseline LoRA (32.00±17.68 completed, 56.40±23.77 attempts), and far above ReFT (16.00±13.51), fine-tuning (4.80±5.03, essentially unchanged from baseline), and a KL-matched random-activation control (16.00±13.51).
- **Cross-opponent transfer (Table 9):** A single PGS intervention fitted from `group_pressure` vs. `defensive_passer` trajectories increases mean completed passes when applied against all four evaluation opponents (e.g., vs. `offensive_passer`: 28.20±12.87 completed passes with the intervention), and the intervention fitted from trajectories with greater passing exposure is consistently the strongest — i.e., transfer effectiveness depends on the fitting data, not just the objective label.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core mathematical idea — that a policy-gradient/score-function estimator can be accumulated into a state-independent "task vector" rather than used to update weights — is explicitly *not* claimed as novel by the authors themselves; they cite close precedents (PPLM's use of gradients to perturb hidden states iteratively during decoding, and LatentSeek's application of policy gradients to per-instance latent sequences at test time). PGS's actual contribution is narrower and more concrete: applying this construction to build a *reusable, calibrated, composable* activation intervention for RL-style feed-forward and sequential policies (not autoregressive LLMs), plus the empirical study of compositionality across independently fitted objectives. This is a legitimate but incremental contribution rather than a fundamentally new mechanism.

**Rigor:** The experimental design is unusually thorough for a short paper — the appendix contains 15+ ablations (activation-site choice, optimizer learning rate, discount factor, fit-trajectory count, update count, return-baseline treatment, layer-support depth, off-policy correction with vs. without importance-ratio weighting, role-specific vs. shared multi-agent routing, opponent-conditioned adaptation). This is a genuine strength. However, several of the paper's own ablations surface real fragility: Table 12 shows PGS's held-out top-route probability does *not* scale monotonically with the number of fit trajectories (0.86±0.04 at 20 trajectories drops to 0.72±0.05 at 32 and rises again to 0.85±0.02 at 64), and the paper concedes "neither PGS nor fine-tuning exhibits a uniform sample-scaling trend." Table 11 (fitting-source sensitivity) shows that for `possession_regains` — a team-level event requiring agent-level credit assignment — "neither fitting source dominates uniformly," which the authors flag as the most-pronounced weakness of the method in the multi-agent setting.

**Reproducibility:** Appendix E describes code/data organization (`src/`, `scripts/`, `configs/`), fixed fit seeds (42-46), a fixed partition seed (20260723), and modest single-GPU compute (one V100, 10 CPU cores, 40GB RAM per job) — favorable for reproducibility in principle. However, no public code repository URL is given in the visible text, and the chess/football environments (LCZeroLens, Google Research Football) and the Maia/team-policy checkpoints are all third-party artifacts whose exact versions must be matched.

**Significance / scope gap (a specific, checkable red flag):** The paper's motivating baselines — CAA, K-Steer, and COAST — are all activation-steering methods originally developed and validated for large language models, and the introduction frames PGS as addressing a gap in "activation steering... for large language models." Yet none of the three evaluation testbeds (gridworld MLP, Maia chess policy, Google Research Football team policies) is an LLM. This means the paper's central claim — that contrastive LLM-steering methods fail for feed-forward policies — is demonstrated only on non-LLM RL policies, and it remains an open question (acknowledged implicitly, not explicitly, in the text) whether the same branching-representation failure mode, and PGS's fix, actually transfers to autoregressive transformer LLMs, which is the setting the baselines were designed for and the setting most readers interested in "activation steering" will care about.

**Overclaiming check:** The claims are generally well-hedged — e.g., "the experiment does not support a checkpoint-independent ranking of adaptation methods," "future work" section is honest about the approximate (non-unbiased) nature of the off-policy importance-ratio correction, and Table 3 openly shows fine-tuning beating PGS in one direction. This restraint is a positive signal for intellectual honesty relative to typical steering papers.

## Strengths

- Clean, minimal diagnostic (the two-route gridworld with a feed-forward MLP policy) that mechanistically demonstrates *why* contrastive activation-steering methods fail (v_CAA = h⁺ − h⁻ = 0 at the branch point), rather than just showing an empirical performance gap.
- Extensive ablation suite covering activation-site selection, optimizer sensitivity, discount factor, fit-data budget, layer depth/support, and off-policy correction — far beyond what is typical for a short conference-style paper.
- Consistent, favorable storage/compute trade-off: PGS matches or approaches the strongest parameter-update baselines (LoRA, fine-tuning) while storing 40-575× fewer parameters and requiring a single gradient-estimation step rather than iterative optimization.
- Extends activation steering beyond single-agent LLM chat settings into competitive multi-agent RL (football), including a cross-opponent transfer study that is genuinely informative about when a fitted intervention generalizes.

## Limitations

- No LLM (or any autoregressive transformer) experiment despite motivating the work against LLM-focused steering baselines (CAA, K-Steer, COAST) — the central claim about "activation steering" failing for feed-forward policies is untested on the systems those baselines were built for.
- The off-policy correction (per-decision importance ratio) is explicitly acknowledged by the authors as an approximate construction, not a generally unbiased estimator of the true objective, when fit and target policies' state distributions differ (Section 3.1).
- Effectiveness is sensitive to activation-site choice (Table 14 in the appendix shows meaningfully different top-route probabilities and path lengths depending on pre-/post-ReLU site and single vs. joint sites) — site selection currently requires validation-data search rather than a principled a priori rule (the paper's own "gradient-norm" screening criterion in Appendix D is explicitly called "a cheap loss-sensitivity screen" but "not a complete layer-selection rule").
- Composition performance does not generalize uniformly to team-level, multi-agent credit-assignment objectives (possession regains in football show the least consistent fitting-source dependence, per Table 11).
- Fine-tuning or LoRA sometimes outperform PGS on raw steering strength (e.g., Table 3's reverse-direction bottom-route probability, and several "isolated gain"/"min retention" columns in the chess tables), so PGS's main advantage is compactness and single-step calibration rather than uniformly superior steering power.

## Relevance

该工作与"用最小侵入的激活干预来控制/组合模型行为"这一方向高度相关，尤其是其"决策级信度分配 vs. 结果级对比"的诊断，对任何基于对比样本构造 steering vector 的方法（不仅是 LLM，也包括策略网络/agent）都是一个值得警惕的失效模式；但其未在真实 LLM 上验证，若关注 LLM 场景下的 activation steering，需要谨慎评估该失效模式和 PGS 修复方案是否直接迁移。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the branch-point failure mode identified for feed-forward MLP policies (identical pre-decision activations across contrasted trajectories) actually occur in autoregressive transformer LLMs, where activations are causally conditioned on the full generated prefix rather than a single fixed observation? Would CAA/COAST/K-Steer fail similarly on an LLM task with an analogous "branching" structure?
- Can the off-policy importance-ratio correction be replaced with a provably unbiased or doubly-robust policy-gradient estimator (as the authors suggest citing Huang & Jiang 2020) without losing the single-gradient-step efficiency that makes PGS practical?
- How does PGS's composability degrade as the number of simultaneously composed behavioral objectives grows beyond three (fork/pin/skewer), and is there a principled joint-rescaling rule to avoid needing per-pair recalibration?
- Could a more principled, data-driven activation-site selection rule (beyond the gradient-norm heuristic explicitly flagged as incomplete) be derived from the Fisher-curvature framework already used for strength calibration?
- For the multi-agent football setting, would an explicit agent-identifying credit-assignment scheme (as flagged in Section 8.3 on shared-policy MARL) resolve the inconsistent fitting-source sensitivity observed for possession-regains steering?
