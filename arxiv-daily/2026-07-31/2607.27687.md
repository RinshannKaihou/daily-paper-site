> **一句话总结：** 论文发现自动化机器学习研究（autoresearch）循环中存在"信心悬崖"现象——随着已积累的成功改动增多，LLM 判官对候选方案的选择性准确率从 82.8% 骤降到 56.9%，同时仍越来越"敢"下判断；作者提出 Rehearse 方法，用只检索同任务相似历史尝试（含成败标签）的轻量记忆库，把悬崖处的选择性准确率拉回 83.5%，并在 nanochat、CIFAR-10、ETTh1 三个真实训练循环中，在同样 4000 次预算训练运行下取得比原始循环更好的终点效果（如 ETTh1 上 MSE 降低 54.0% vs. 原始 40.1%）。

> **TL;DR:** Using a 366-pair benchmark built from 39 paper-derived AutoSOTA optimization tasks, the authors show pre-execution judgment in self-improving autoresearch loops degrades sharply with loop depth — a "confidence cliff" where selective accuracy falls from 82.8% to 56.9% once ≥3 successful changes have accumulated, even as the judge's willingness to commit rises. Their fix, Rehearse, is a Propose–Predict–Execute skill that ranks candidates with a pairwise strict-consensus judge fed only focused, outcome-labeled memory of similar past attempts (cosine similarity ≥ 0.40), restoring cliff-bucket selective accuracy to 83.5% (vs. 56.9% memoryless baseline, 80.9% for the best LLM-summary baseline) and improving live-loop endpoints under a fixed 4,000-training-run budget across nanochat pretraining, CIFAR-10 classification, and ETTh1 forecasting.

## 核心贡献

- 构建了一个基于真实执行结果标注的基准数据集：从 39 个论文衍生的 AutoSOTA 优化任务的公开日志中提取 366 对候选修改（296 对"同基线、一成一败"的 type-1 pair，70 对"两者都成功、比较增益大小"的 type-2 pair），首次按"循环深度"（已累积的成功改动数 n_baseline）系统性衡量预执行判断能力。
- 发现并命名"信心悬崖"（confidence cliff）现象：无记忆的判官在 AutoSOTA 全量 366 对基准上，选择性准确率随 n_baseline 从 0 到 ≥3 时从 82.8% 跌至 56.9%，但判官愿意给出裁决的比例（coverage）反而从 76% 升到 85%——即越到后期越"武断"，却越不可靠。
- 证明"聚焦结果记忆"（focused outcome memory）能保住判断力：仅检索同任务、余弦相似度≥0.40 的历史尝试并以"改动+成败二元标签"一行文本形式提供给判官，在悬崖区间将选择性准确率恢复到 83.5%，优于全历史转储（70.8%）、类 Reflexion 自反思缓冲（74.1%）、LLM 摘要（80.9%）等所有对照记忆形式。
- 将该机制实现为一个即插即用的"技能"（Rehearse），不需要微调或修改目标训练代码，把普通的"提出-执行"循环改造为"提出-预测-执行"（Propose–Predict–Execute）循环，并在 nanochat 预训练、CIFAR-10 图像分类、ETTh1 时间序列预测三个真实循环、共 4000 次预算训练运行中验证了终点效果的提升。

## Key Contributions

- **Depth-indexed benchmark of pre-execution judgment**: 366 pairs (296 type-1, 70 type-2) drawn from 39 paper-derived AutoSOTA tasks, with judgment measured as a function of loop depth (n_baseline) rather than at a single search state — unlike prior pairwise-preference work (e.g., ForeAgent) that studies solution-level feasibility only at a fixed point.
- **Identification of the "confidence cliff"**: on the full 366-pair benchmark, averaged over three judge models, selective accuracy collapses from 82.8% to 56.9% once ≥3 successful changes have accumulated, while coverage (verdict rate under strict consensus) rises from 76% to 85% — the judge becomes more willing to commit exactly as it becomes less reliable.
- **Focused outcome memory as the fix**: restricting retrieval to same-task, similarity-thresholded (≥0.40), outcome-labeled prior attempts (serialized as one line: change + worked/did-not-work) yields the highest cliff selective accuracy (83.5%) among all tested history forms, including full-history dumps, Reflexion-style self-reflection buffers, and LLM-generated summaries.
- **A validated, code-agnostic skill**: Rehearse wraps proposal, pairwise strict-consensus judging, and the focused store around an existing autoresearch loop with no fine-tuning and no change to the target training script; validated live across three unrelated testbeds (nanochat/nanoGPT pretraining, CIFAR-10/ResNet, ETTh1/PatchTST) under a shared 4,000-training-run budget.

## Methodology

Rehearse turns a vanilla "one-idea-then-run" autoresearch loop into a **Propose–Predict–Execute (PPE)** loop (Algorithm 1) by adding one operation (PREDICT) and one state object (a focused outcome store M):

1. **Propose**: at each step, the agent reads the current training script/results log and emits N candidate modifications (N=5 in multi-proposal live configurations, N=1 for vanilla), each with a JSON description, a *hypothesis* (mechanistic reason it should help), an implementation field, a coarse type, priority, and risk. The prompt explicitly asks candidates to target different axes (optimizer, architecture, regularization, schedule, data) rather than variants of one hyperparameter.
2. **Predict**: before any training run, a judge LLM ranks the N candidates via a **pairwise strict-consensus tournament** — every unordered pair is judged twice (both presentation orders); a verdict counts only if both orders agree (mitigating the known LLM-judge position bias), otherwise the judge abstains. Candidates are ranked by vote count and the top k (=1 in all live loops) is sent to training. For N=5 this costs 2·C(5,2)=20 judge calls per step.
3. **Execute**: the top-ranked candidate is applied to a copy of the current state and trained; if the metric improves, the patch is committed and the outcome is written back to the store as one line (change description + binary worked/did-not-work label) — explicitly *without* failure reasons, summaries, or the full applied-change list, since the ablations (Table 2, Appendix E) show that adding such extra content hurts rather than helps.
4. **Focused store**: when judging a new candidate, retrieval is restricted to attempts from the *same task* executed before the current step, returned only if cosine similarity ≥ 0.40 (candidate descriptions embedded with the 384-dim `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` sentence encoder); nothing is returned below that threshold. The 0.40 threshold was chosen from a sweep over {0.30,...,0.60} before measuring benchmark accuracy.

**Benchmark construction**: built from public AutoSOTA logs (pinned to commit `419fe013`), where each task starts from a SOTA paper's repository and the optimizer tries to improve a measured metric. A modification is labeled *worked* iff it strictly improves the metric over the current accepted baseline; crashes/timeouts/invalid patches/non-improvements are *did not work*. Type-1 pairs (296) share the exact same accepted baseline state, with one candidate that worked and one that didn't. Type-2 pairs (70) compare two successful attempts across successive accepted states (auxiliary ranking probe). Judges see only outcome-blind rationale (description, hypothesis, implementation, type) — the measured result, metric delta, and revert/keep status are stripped before judging.

**Live evaluation**: four ablation configurations sharing one idea-generating model — *vanilla* (propose 1, run it), *propose-many* (propose N, run the first), *select* (propose N, run PREDICT's top pick, no memory), *Rehearse* (select + focused store) — run on Karpathy's nanochat pretraining loop (`val_bpb` metric, lower better) for 100 experiments × 5 seeds, under a fixed 30-minute-per-experiment wall-clock budget. Two further testbeds (CIFAR-10/ResNet with `glm-5.1`, ETTh1/PatchTST with `deepseek-v4`) compare only vanilla vs. Rehearse, also 100 experiments × 5 seeds. Total: 4,000 budgeted training runs. Note: model and task are *paired* (one serving model per loop, driving both proposal and judging), not crossed, so model and task effects are confounded by design.

## Results & Highlights

- **Confidence cliff magnitude (366-pair benchmark)**: without memory, selective accuracy holds at 82.8% at n_baseline=0 but falls to 56.9% once ≥3 successful changes have accumulated, while coverage rises from 76% to 85% (Figure 1, Table 1) — a 25.9-point selective-accuracy collapse under strict-consensus judging averaged over three judge models (hy3-preview, glm-5.1, deepseek-v4).
- **Memory-form comparison at the cliff (Table 1, n_baseline≥3)**: No memory 56.9% selective accuracy (85% coverage) → full-history dump (AIDE-inspired) 70.8% (80% coverage) → self-reflection buffer (Reflexion-inspired) 74.1% (90% coverage) → LLM summary of history 80.9% (78% coverage) → **Rehearse (focused retrieval) 83.5% (77% coverage)** — the best cliff selective accuracy of all tested history forms while committing on the fewest deep calls.
- **Live loop endpoints under fixed budget**: on ETTh1/PatchTST forecasting, Rehearse cuts MSE by 54.0% relative to baseline vs. vanilla's 40.1% (Table 4); on CIFAR-10/ResNet, Rehearse improves accuracy by 2.85%(~2.9%) over baseline vs. vanilla's 2.10%(~2.1%) (Table 4, mean over 5 seeds); on nanochat, mean relative improvement rises from vanilla 7.1% → propose-many 6.9% (no benefit without selection) → select 8.4% → Rehearse 10.7% (+2.3pp over select alone).
- **Sample efficiency**: Rehearse reaches vanilla's final-level performance after 54 experiments on CIFAR-10 (46% fewer training runs) and 63 experiments on ETTh1 (37% fewer) versus vanilla's full 100-experiment budget; per-seed data (Table 4, Appendix G) shows Rehearse ends ahead on 9 of 10 additional-loop seed runs.
- **Ablation isolating the mechanism (Table 2, cliff bucket)**: removing the candidate rationale drops selective accuracy from 83.5% to 70.2%; replacing focused retrieval with a full dump drops it to 70.8%; removing did-not-work precedents (keeping only worked ones) drops it to 78.9%; conversely, *adding* a recorded failure-reason explanation *lowers* cliff selective accuracy to 80.6% (and to 54.8% on the specific calls carrying such a reason) — evidence that more text is not always better and outcome labels, not explanations, carry the decision signal.
- **Pairwise-to-classification behavior (Section 4.5)**: on type-1 pairs (one worked/one didn't), selective accuracy rises from 79.5% (no memory) to 84.2% with the store; on the harder type-2 pairs (both worked, judge must rank magnitude), it only rises from 67.4% to 74.1% — indicating the store helps filtering ("will this work at all") far more than fine-grained ranking of already-good ideas.
- **Heuristic no-LLM baseline (Appendix F)**: a rule-based selector that just avoids candidates near-similar to prior did-not-work attempts reaches only 47.8% selective accuracy on the deep bucket (n=23 decided) and 52.4% overall (n=145) — below chance on the deep bucket and far below both Rehearse (83.5%) and even the memoryless LLM judge (56.9%), showing that a naive embedding-distance heuristic is not a substitute for LLM-based outcome reasoning.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The "confidence cliff" framing — that pre-execution judgment reliability is depth-dependent and that coverage (willingness to commit) moves inversely to accuracy — is a genuinely useful and, as far as the related work section documents, new diagnostic lens; most prior systems (AIDE, evolutionary archives, ForeAgent) study whether memory/selection helps overall search efficiency but not whether the *judgment itself* degrades as a function of accumulated success. The finding that adding failure-reason explanations to memory *hurts* selective accuracy (Table 2: 83.5% → 80.6%, or 54.8% on affected calls) is a genuinely counterintuitive and paper-specific result that pushes back against the common assumption that "more informative" memory is strictly better.

**Rigor**: Strengths include strict-consensus judging (both presentation orders must agree) to control for known LLM position bias, averaging over three different judge models for the single-turn benchmark, systematic leave-one-out ablations (Table 2), a length-balanced pair variant to rule out a length-based shortcut, and a non-LLM heuristic baseline (Appendix F) to confirm the effect isn't just deduplication. A weakness: the heuristic baseline is "one illustrative rule-based selector," not a serious sweep of non-LLM alternatives, so the claim that "distance from unsuccessful attempts is not a proxy" rests on a single hand-designed rule.

**Reproducibility concerns (paper-specific)**: the models used — `hy3-preview`, `glm-5.1`, `deepseek-v4` — are explicitly documented in Appendix B as "serving endpoints or aliases rather than frozen checkpoints," meaning the underlying model could change over time without notice, undermining exact reproducibility of the live-loop numbers (though the single-turn benchmark and its underlying AutoSOTA commit are pinned). Additionally, model and task are paired rather than crossed across the three live loops (one model drives both proposal and judging per loop), so the paper cannot separate whether gains stem from Rehearse's mechanism versus idiosyncrasies of a particular model-task pairing.

**Significance and red flags**: The live-loop evidence is thin in sample size — only 5 seeds per configuration — and the paper itself flags that one ETTh1 vanilla seed lands at 59.9% MSE reduction (comparable to Rehearse) while the other four land between 31–45%, i.e., Rehearse "wins" 9 of 10 additional-loop seed comparisons rather than a clean 10/10, and the paper is candid that "the reliability claim should be read with the seed-level limitation." The n_baseline depth variable used to construct the cliff bucket is observational, not randomized, so later decisions may differ simultaneously in baseline state, modification family, and difficulty — a confound the authors acknowledge directly in Section 6. Reported compute budgets count only training runs, excluding the added judge-inference cost (20 judge calls per step for N=5 candidates), so the practical overhead/cost tradeoff of deploying Rehearse is not fully characterized.

## Strengths

- Reframes "how do we generate more/better ideas" (the dominant framing in prior autoresearch work) into "how do we know, before spending compute, whether an idea is worth running" — and backs this with a purpose-built, outcome-labeled, depth-indexed benchmark rather than an anecdotal case study.
- Careful control for LLM-judge idiosyncrasies (strict consensus across both orders, 3-judge-model averaging) that materially improves measurement stability (cuts run-to-run spread from 5.1 to 1.7 points per the paper).
- Component-level ablations (Table 2, Appendix E) cleanly separate *what* about memory helps: relevant/focused text, mixed worked/did-not-work outcome labels, and outcome labels specifically — rather than a single "memory on/off" comparison.
- Validated on three genuinely different domains (LM pretraining, image classification, time-series forecasting) rather than one synthetic setting, with a formal Precision@K calibration (Appendix I) connecting the pairwise metric to deployment-relevant selection quality.
- Delivered as a lightweight, code-agnostic "skill" requiring no fine-tuning and no change to the target training script — a practical, low-friction deployment story.

## Limitations

- Live-loop evidence covers only 5 seeds per configuration and three loops with measurable outcomes (nanochat, CIFAR-10, ETTh1); novelty- or paper-scored autoresearch systems and larger-scale seed studies remain explicitly out of scope (Section 6).
- Candidate pools tested live are drawn only from Rehearse's own proposer; the nanochat ablation is the only one that separates breadth (propose-many), selection, and memory — other memory forms and candidate counts (K>1) are untested in the live controller (Appendix A notes the K>1 setting is only used for an offline budgeted-selection calibration, not the evaluated controller).
- The depth variable n_baseline is observational rather than randomized, so cliff-bucket comparisons may confound baseline state, modification family, and task difficulty with loop depth.
- Reported training-run budgets exclude proposal and judge inference cost, leaving the true compute/latency overhead of the 20-judge-call tournament (for N=5) uncharacterized.
- Relies on a small, cheap sentence encoder (`all-MiniLM-L6-v2`, 384-dim) for retrieval; the authors note stronger retrievers remain untested future work.
- Live model endpoints are serving aliases rather than frozen checkpoints, which could complicate exact reproduction of live-loop numbers over time.

## Relevance

论文提出的"信心悬崖"现象和"聚焦结果记忆"设计，对任何构建自动化实验/超参搜索/agentic ML pipeline 的场景都有直接参考价值——尤其是关于"记忆内容越多不一定越好，结果标签比失败原因解释更重要"的反直觉发现，值得在设计 LLM-judge 或 agent 记忆系统时借鉴。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the confidence cliff and the fix generalize to K>1 (batched selection) live loops, rather than only the offline Monte Carlo calibration in Appendix I?
- How does Rehearse's overhead (extra judge inference calls) trade off against training-run savings in real wall-clock/dollar terms, given the paper only budgets training runs?
- Does the counterintuitive finding that failure-reason explanations hurt selective accuracy hold with stronger judge models, or is it an artifact of the specific judges tested (hy3-preview, glm-5.1, deepseek-v4)?
- Would a larger or fine-tuned retriever (beyond the deliberately cheap 384-dim MiniLM encoder) further raise cliff selective accuracy, or is focused outcome labeling already close to a ceiling?
- How would Rehearse perform head-to-head against ForeAgent's confidence-gated pairwise filtering on the same benchmark, given both target similar pre-execution selection problems but at different granularities (proposal-level vs. solution-level)?
- Does the "confidence cliff" appear in longer runs beyond the 100-experiment budgets tested, and does the cliff's shape depend on task headroom (how close to saturation the metric already is)?
