> **一句话总结：** 本文把 Transformer 训练重新表述为“双重提升”（doubly lifted）的测度值最优控制问题，通过对状态、动作、测度状态三重量化后用 McDiarmid 不等式给出有限样本泛化界（误差量级 $O(K_r^{-1/2})$，$K_r$ 为数据集样本数），再把界迁移回原始（非量化）模型，并证明同一套价值函数 Lipschitz 估计还能推出一个等价的 Wasserstein 分布鲁棒优化（WDRO）表述——全文没有任何数值实验，全部是定理与证明。

> **TL;DR:** This is a pure-theory paper (no experiments, no datasets, no baselines) that derives finite-sample generalization bounds for Transformers trained via the "doubly lifted" measure-valued dynamic-programming formulation of prior work (Akman, Saldi & Yüksel, arXiv:2603.09571). By quantizing the particle state space, weight/action space, and the space of measures itself, the authors combine a Lipschitz stability estimate for the value function with McDiarmid's inequality to get a concentration bound whose rate is $O(K_r^{-1/2})$ in the number of sampled data sets $K_r$ (Theorem 3.3, Remark 4.5); they then transfer this bound to the un-quantized "base" model at the cost of explicit quantization-approximation error terms (Theorem 4.4), and show the identical machinery yields a Wasserstein-ball distributionally robust control formulation whose robust minimizers are Γ-convergent to, and asymptotically nested inside, the ordinary training minimizers (Theorem 5.3), with matching generalization bounds (Theorem 5.4, 5.5).

## 核心贡献

- **把 Transformer 训练问题嵌入"三重量化"的有限 MDP**：在已有的双重提升测度值模型（把逐 token 的非马尔可夫演化提升为测度上的马尔可夫演化）基础上，进一步量化粒子状态空间 S（量化误差 $1/n$）、权重/动作空间 U（量化误差 $1/m$）以及测度空间 $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{X})$（用 Reznik 2011 的量化算法得到有限"重构点"集合），得到一个可用动态规划求解的有限 Markov 决策过程。
- **给出显式的有限样本泛化界（Theorem 3.3）**：利用价值函数关于经验测度的 Lipschitz 稳定性估计（Lemma 2.5）和 McDiarmid 有界差分不等式对"经验测度上的经验测度"（即数据集层面的经验律）建立集中不等式（Proposition 3.1、Lemma 3.2），得到以概率 $1-\delta$ 成立、且以 $O(K_r^{-1/2})$ 速率随数据集样本数 $K_r$ 收缩的超额风险界。
- **把量化模型的界迁回原始（无限维）模型（Theorem 4.4）**：显式给出三项量化近似误差（测度状态量化 $\rho_{\ell,n}$、状态量化 $1/n$、动作量化 $\sqrt{T}/m$），并证明控制这些误差所需的采样规模关系（Assumption 4.2：$K_r \gtrsim |\mathcal{Z}^{\ell,n}|\log(1/\delta)$，量化粒度 $\ell \gg \sqrt{|\mathbb{X}_n|} \sim \sqrt{N}\,n^{d/2}$）。
- **建立 Transformer 泛化与 Wasserstein 分布鲁棒优化（WDRO）之间的等价视角**：把泛化问题重新表述为控制器（选权重轨迹）与对手（在 Wasserstein 球内扰动数据律）之间的零和博弈；证明鲁棒极小元存在（Proposition 5.1），鲁棒目标函数 Γ-收敛到训练目标函数，且鲁棒极小元集合的 Painlevé-Kuratowski 上极限被训练极小元集合所包含（Theorem 5.3），并且鲁棒极小元与训练极小元享有相同的有限样本泛化界（Theorem 5.4、5.5）。
- **论文明确承认的局限**：动作空间 U 的紧致性假设对应的是"范数约束训练方案"（如算子范数有界约束）而非无约束随机梯度下降，界不能被误认为适用于任意 Transformer；且界在 $K_r \gg \ell \gg n \to \infty$ 的渐近区制下才有意义，状态覆盖数 $|\mathbb{X}_n| \sim N n^d$ 随状态空间维度 $d$ 呈现维数灾难。

## Key Contributions

- **Reformulates Transformer training as a finite MDP via a three-level quantization** ("triply quantized" model, Figure 1.1): building on the doubly-lifted measure-valued formulation of the companion paper [1] (Akman, Saldi & Yüksel, arXiv:2603.09571) — which restores the Markov property for token dynamics by lifting the per-token evolution to the space of empirical measures — the authors further quantize (a) the particle state space S via a position-sensitive hard quantizer $Q_n$ (error $1/n$), (b) the weight/action space $\mathbb{U}$ into a finite net $\mathbb{U}_m$ (error $1/m$), and (c) the measure space $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{X})$ itself via Reznik's (2011) reconstruction-point quantizer $R_\ell$, yielding a finite-state, finite-action MDP whose optimal policies are computable by dynamic programming.
- **An explicit finite-sample excess-risk bound for the quantized model (Theorem 3.3)**: combining a Lipschitz stability estimate for the value function with respect to the Wasserstein-type metric $\mathcal{W}_1$ on $\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Z})$ (Lemma 2.5) with a McDiarmid-inequality-based concentration bound for the empirical law of empirical measures (Proposition 3.1, Lemma 3.2), the authors show the trained (quantized) minimizer's true-law value is within $2L\rho_r^{\ell,n}(\delta)$ of the best achievable value with probability at least $1-\delta$, where $\rho_r^{\ell,n}(\delta) = \frac{D_{\ell,n}\sqrt{|\mathcal{Z}^{\ell,n}|}}{2\sqrt{K_r}} + D_{\ell,n}\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{2K_r}}$.
- **Transfer of the bound to the un-quantized base model (Theorem 4.4)**: three additional error terms are made explicit — a measure-quantization error $\alpha_{\ell,n}$ (Lemma 4.3), an action-quantization error scaling as $B_T\sqrt{T}/m$ (via Lemma 4.1's Lipschitz-in-weights estimate for $V$), and the base concentration term — under Assumption 4.2's rate conditions linking $\ell$, $n$, $m$, $K_r$ so that all error terms vanish as $\ell, n, m, K_r \to \infty$ in the prescribed order ($K_r$ fastest, then $\ell$, then $n$).
- **A Wasserstein distributionally robust control (WDRO) reformulation of the same generalization problem (Section 5)**: casts training as a minimax game where the controller picks a weight trajectory $\underline{U}$ and an adversary picks a probability law within a $\mathcal{W}_1$-ball of radius $\rho$ around the empirical law; proves existence of a robust minimizer (Proposition 5.1) via Weierstrass' theorem on the compact weight space, shows Γ-convergence of the robust objective to the plain training objective and an upper Painlevé-Kuratowski limit of robust minimizers into the set of training minimizers as $\rho_r \to 0^+$ (Theorem 5.3), and proves the robust minimizers inherit the same finite-sample generalization bounds as ordinary training minimizers, both in the quantized regime (Theorem 5.4) and after transfer to the base model (Theorem 5.5).
- **Explicit constants and dependence on architecture (Remarks 4.7, 4.8)**: the Lipschitz constant $B_T$ for the value function w.r.t. weights is shown to scale exponentially in the number of layers $T$ (via $L_\Phi^{T-1-t}$ terms summed over layers), and the effective sample complexity — for fixed $\delta$, of order $1/\sqrt{|\mathcal{Z}^{\ell,n}|}$ — is governed by the covering number of the (lifted, quantized) measure-state space, which itself scales combinatorially with the state-space quantization $|\mathbb{X}_n| \sim N n^d$.

## Methodology

The paper builds directly on a hierarchy of models introduced by the same authors in a companion paper (Akman, Saldi & Yüksel, arXiv:2603.09571, cited as [1]), summarized in Figure 1.1:
1. **Particle level**: each token $i$ in a length-$N$ sequence evolves through $T$ Transformer layers via a feed-forward map plus a self-attention kernel (Eq. 2.1–2.2), with positional encodings $i/N$ appended to keep the state space $\mathbb{X} = \mathrm{PE}_N \times \mathrm{S}$ well-defined; this per-token dynamics is *not* Markovian because attention couples each token to the empirical measure of the whole sequence.
2. **Singly lifted level**: the ensemble of $N$ particles is represented by its empirical measure $\mu_t \in \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{X})$, and the map $\Phi: \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{X}) \times \mathbb{U} \to \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{X})$ (a discrete-time Fokker–Planck-type push-forward) restores the Markov property; the per-layer weights $U_t = (W_t, A_t, b_t, Q_t, K_t, V_t)$ become the control variables.
3. **Doubly lifted level**: since training uses $K$ different data sequences (i.e., a data set is itself a *set* of empirical measures), the authors lift again to $\mathbb{P} \in \mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Z})$ with $\mathcal{Z} = \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{X})^2$, i.e., a probability law on pairs of (input, output) empirical measures. Training becomes a finite-horizon stochastic optimal control problem with cost functional $V(P, \underline{U}) = \int_\mathcal{Z} P(d\mu, d\nu)\, W_{2,\lambda}(\Phi_T(\mu,\underline{U}), \nu)^2$ (Eq. 2.7), solvable in principle by dynamic programming.
4. **Triply quantized level (this paper's main technical contribution)**: because $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{X})$ and $\mathbb{U}$ are infinite-dimensional, the authors additionally quantize (i) the token state space S into finite grids $\mathrm{S}_n$ via a position-sensitive hard quantizer, (ii) the weight/action space $\mathbb{U}$ into a finite $\varepsilon$-net $\mathbb{U}_m$, and (iii) the measure space $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{X}_n)$ itself into a finite set of "reconstruction points" $\mathcal{P}^{(\ell)}(\mathbb{X}_n)$ using an algorithm from Reznik (2011). This produces a genuinely finite MDP on which dynamic programming is implementable.

The core analytical tools are: (a) a **Lipschitz stability estimate for the value function** $V$, first with respect to the input law $P$ in $\mathcal{W}_1$ (Lemma 2.5, proved via Kantorovich–Rubinstein duality and the pushforward map's own Lipschitz property from [1, Corollary 19]), and separately with respect to the weight trajectory $\underline{U}$ (Lemma 4.1, proved via joint smoothness of the attention kernel and induction over layers, giving an explicit constant $B_T$); and (b) a **concentration inequality for empirical laws of empirical measures** on the finite quantized space $\mathcal{Z}^{\ell,n}$, derived from first principles via McDiarmid's bounded-differences inequality applied to a $\mathcal{W}_1$-Lipschitz statistic of i.i.d. samples (Proposition 3.1), together with a bias bound obtained through a Bernoulli-variance argument and Cauchy–Schwarz (Lemma 3.2) to control $\mathbb{E}\mathcal{W}_1(\hat{\mathbb{P}}_r^{\ell,n}, \hat{\mathbb{P}}^{\ell,n})$. Combining these two ingredients (stability + concentration) via a standard "compare to the population optimum on both sides" argument yields Theorem 3.3, and threading the quantization errors through three additional triangle-inequality decompositions (state, measure-state, and action quantization, each bounded separately in Lemma 4.3 and combined in Theorem 4.4) yields the base-model bound. For the WDRO extension (Section 5), the same Lipschitz estimate (Lemma 2.5) is reused to bound how far the robust value can be from the training value once the true law lies within the chosen ambiguity radius $\rho$ of the empirical law, and general results on Γ-convergence and Painlevé-Kuratowski set convergence (cited from Dal Maso 2012 and Rockafellar & Wets 1998) are invoked to characterize the asymptotic relationship between robust and ordinary minimizers.

Standing assumptions: Assumption 2.1 requires the token state space S and weight/action space $\mathbb{U}$ to be compact, with Transformer dynamics forward-invariant on $\mathrm{S}^N$ — the paper notes this corresponds to a norm-constrained (e.g., spectral/operator-norm-bounded) training scheme, not unconstrained SGD. Assumption 4.2 prescribes how the quantization parameters $\ell$ (measure-space granularity), $n$ (state-space granularity), and the sample count $K_r$ must be scaled relative to each other (specifically $\ell \gg \sqrt{|\mathbb{X}_n|} \sim \sqrt{N} n^{d/2}$ and $K_r \geq C \cdot |\mathcal{Z}^{\ell,n}| \cdot \log(1/\delta)$) for the bounds to be non-vacuous, revealing an explicit curse of dimensionality through the state-space dimension $d$.

## Results & Highlights

No quantitative results reported — this is a purely theoretical paper with no experiments, datasets, baselines, or numerical evaluation of any kind. All "results" are theorems, propositions, and lemmas with full proofs. The evidence the paper offers in lieu of empirical numbers consists of:

- **An explicit convergence rate for the main generalization bound.** Theorem 3.3's excess-risk bound is $V(\mathbb{P}, \underline{U}^{*,\ell,n,(m)}_{r,\delta}) \leq \inf_{\underline{U}^{(m)}} V^{\ell,n}(\hat{\mathbb{P}}_r^{\ell,n}, \underline{U}^{(m)}) + 2L\rho_r^{\ell,n}(\delta)$ with probability $\geq 1-\delta$; Remark 4.5 states this achieves rate $O(K_r^{-1/2})$ in the number of sampled data sets $K_r$, which the authors identify as the "optimal rate" for this type of concentration bound (i.e., matching the standard parametric $1/\sqrt{n}$ Monte-Carlo rate, here in the "meta-sample" dimension $K_r$ rather than the sequence length $N$).
- **An explicit sample-complexity recommendation.** For a fixed quantization configuration, choosing $K_r = (LD_{\ell,n})^2 |\mathcal{Z}^{\ell,n}| \log(1/\delta)$ makes the sample-complexity term equal $\frac{1}{\log(1/\delta)} + \sqrt{|\mathcal{Z}^{\ell,n}|}$ (Remark 4.5); under the paper's own Assumption 4.2 scaling regime, the asymptotic generalization error is of order $1/\sqrt{|\mathcal{Z}^{\ell,n}|}$, i.e., governed entirely by the covering number of the lifted, doubly-quantized measure-state space.
- **A full transfer theorem with explicit, non-vanishing-order error decomposition.** Theorem 4.4 gives the base-model bound as the quantized-model bound plus three explicit correction terms — $2 C_T(4+L_\Phi^T)(\rho_{\ell,n} + 1/n)$ for measure- and state-quantization error and $B_T\sqrt{T}/m$ for action-quantization error — rather than an unspecified $o(1)$ term, which is a stronger form of "result" than a typical asymptotic-only bound, though it comes at the cost of several blackbox constants ($B_T$, $C_T$, $L$) whose explicit (if unwieldy) formulas are supplied in Remark 4.8.
- **Asymptotic equivalence results for the WDRO reformulation, stated as convergence theorems rather than rates**: Theorem 5.3 shows Γ-convergence of the robust objective to the training objective and Painlevé-Kuratowski containment of robust minimizers within training minimizers as the ambiguity radius $\rho_r \to 0^+$; Theorems 5.4–5.5 show the robust minimizers satisfy the identical finite-sample bounds (same rate, same constants up to the $2L\rho_r^{\ell,n}(\delta)$ term) as the ordinary training minimizers, both at the quantized and base-model level.

Because there is no experimental section, none of the standard ML-empirical criteria (benchmark accuracy, comparison to a trained-baseline Transformer, wall-clock or sample-efficiency numbers) apply; the paper's entire contribution is judged on the sharpness and generality of its analytic bounds.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The core idea — recasting Transformer generalization as a distributional-robustness/optimal-control problem via a "doubly lifted" measure-valued MDP, then deriving finite-sample bounds through quantization plus a Lipschitz-stability + concentration-inequality combination — is a genuinely different route from the dominant norm-based/covering-number literature the paper cites (Neyshabur et al., Bartlett et al., Trauger & Tewari 2024, Li et al. 2026 [25]). The explicit connection to Wasserstein distributionally robust optimization (Section 5, Theorem 5.3) is the paper's most conceptually interesting contribution: showing that ordinary empirical-risk training minimizers and WDRO-robust minimizers become asymptotically indistinguishable (Γ-convergent objectives, nested minimizer sets) is a clean structural result.

**Rigor**: The mathematical rigor is high and self-consistent — every claimed theorem has a full proof, all constants are eventually tracked down to explicit (if unwieldy) formulas in terms of the underlying Transformer's Lipschitz/attention-kernel properties (Remark 4.8), and the authors are careful to state exactly which assumptions (2.1, 4.2) are needed where. Notably, the compactness assumption on $\mathbb{U}$ is honestly flagged in the Conclusion as corresponding to "a norm-constraint training scheme... rather than to unconstrained stochastic gradient descent," an explicit, paper-specific caveat that keeps the authors from overclaiming applicability to standard SGD-trained Transformers.

**Reproducibility**: Not applicable in the empirical sense — there is no code, no data, no experiments to reproduce. The mathematical constructions (e.g., the quantizer $Q_n$ in Eq. 3.1, the measure-quantization algorithm cited from Reznik 1998/2011 [33]) are specified precisely enough to, in principle, be implemented, but the paper makes no attempt to actually instantiate or numerically illustrate the bound (e.g., there is no toy example computing $\rho_r^{\ell,n}(\delta)$ for a concrete small Transformer to show whether the bound is vacuous or informative in practice).

**Significance and red flags**: The paper's own limitations paragraph is unusually candid and should be taken seriously by readers: (1) the bound is asymptotic only in the joint regime $K_r \gg \ell \gg n \to \infty$, and the authors do not give finite, concrete values of $(K_r, \ell, n)$ for which the bound is non-vacuous; (2) the state covering number scales as $|\mathbb{X}_n| \sim N n^d$, an explicit curse of dimensionality in the token feature dimension $d$ that the authors acknowledge directly rather than burying; (3) per Remark 4.7, the Lipschitz constant $B_T$ for the value function w.r.t. weights scales *exponentially* in the number of layers $T$ (via terms like $L_\Phi^{T-1-t}$ summed over $t$), meaning the bound likely becomes extremely loose for deep, many-layer Transformers — a serious practical caveat for a paper about a "Transformer" architecture, which in practice means dozens of layers; (4) the paper never quantitatively compares its bound's tightness against the covering-number-based bounds of the works it cites as related (e.g., [25], [36]) — the comparison in Section 1.1 is purely qualitative ("our approach differs by depending on the geometry of the data-law..."), so there is no evidence the new bound is tighter, only that it is structurally different. The closest related work, Kan et al. [22] (continuous-time OT-regularized optimal control view of Transformers), is discussed candidly with three specific points of departure (their stability estimate needs only compactness rather than convexity; the training paradigm is dynamic programming rather than the gradient-descent family; the adversary perturbs input-output pairs jointly rather than input and output measures separately) — this is a genuine, specific technical comparison rather than a generic "differs from prior work" statement, which is a mark of good scholarly practice, though the authors again stop short of arguing which approach yields sharper numbers.

**Overall**: A solid, honest, and rigorously self-critical piece of learning-theory work that opens a novel control-theoretic angle on Transformer generalization, but its practical bite for real (many-layer, unconstrained-SGD-trained) Transformers is unclear given the acknowledged exponential-in-depth Lipschitz constants and the curse of dimensionality in the state-covering number — a limitation the authors state plainly rather than hide.

## Strengths

- Genuinely novel framing: connects Transformer generalization theory to stochastic optimal control / dynamic programming and to Wasserstein DRO in one unified measure-valued framework, rather than the usual capacity-measure (norm/covering-number) route.
- Full mathematical rigor with explicit constants throughout (Remark 4.8 spells out $B_T$, $C_T$, $L$ in terms of Lipschitz constants of the attention kernel, diameter of S and $\mathbb{U}$, etc.), rather than leaving key quantities as unspecified $O(\cdot)$ terms.
- Unusually candid limitations section: the authors explicitly flag the compactness/norm-constraint assumption as a departure from standard unconstrained SGD training, and explicitly name the curse-of-dimensionality in the state-space covering number.
- A specific, technically detailed comparison to the closest prior work [22] (three concrete points of difference), rather than a vague "differs from related work" gesture.
- Internally consistent hierarchy of models (Figure 1.1) that clearly documents each lifting/quantization step and what property it restores (Markovianity, computability, concentration).

## Limitations

- No experiments whatsoever: the paper never instantiates its bound numerically, e.g., by computing $\rho_r^{\ell,n}(\delta)$ for any concrete (even toy) Transformer, so it is unknown whether the bound is tight enough to be non-vacuous at practically reachable sample sizes $K_r$.
- The compactness assumption on the weight/action space $\mathbb{U}$ (Assumption 2.1) restricts applicability to norm-constrained training regimes and explicitly excludes standard unconstrained SGD-trained Transformers, per the authors' own Conclusion.
- Curse of dimensionality: the state covering number scales as $|\mathbb{X}_n| \sim N n^d$, and per Remark 4.7 the key Lipschitz constant $B_T$ grows exponentially with the number of layers $T$, both of which likely make the bound extremely loose for realistic, deep Transformers.
- The measure-space quantization ($R_\ell$) and its cardinality $\binom{\ell+|\mathbb{X}_n|-1}{|\mathbb{X}_n|-1}$ grow combinatorially, and the required sample count $K_r \geq C\cdot|\mathcal{Z}^{\ell,n}|\cdot\log(1/\delta)$ inherits this combinatorial blow-up — the paper does not discuss whether this is at all achievable for realistic sequence lengths $N$ or state dimensions $d$.
- No quantitative or even qualitative comparison of how tight this bound is relative to existing covering-number/norm-based Transformer generalization bounds (e.g., [25], [36]) that the paper itself cites as related work.

## Relevance

对关注 Transformer 理论基础、泛化界、以及最优控制/动态规划视角下深度学习理论的研究者较为相关；由于全文无实验、且假设（动作空间紧致性、深度指数依赖的 Lipschitz 常数、状态覆盖数维数灾难）较强，实用参考价值有限，更适合作为理论工具箱或未来"控制论视角 + 分布鲁棒性"泛化研究的方法论参考。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the exponential-in-depth Lipschitz constant $B_T$ (Remark 4.7) be tightened, e.g., via normalization layers or weight-sharing assumptions common in real Transformers, to make the bound non-vacuous for deep architectures?
- How does this control-theoretic bound numerically compare (in tightness, not just in derivation route) to covering-number-based bounds like [25] or [36] on the same architecture and data regime — has anyone attempted an apples-to-apples numerical comparison?
- Could the compactness assumption on $\mathbb{U}$ (Assumption 2.1) be relaxed to accommodate weight-decay-regularized but technically unconstrained SGD, e.g., via a high-probability a posteriori norm bound rather than an a priori hard constraint?
- Is there a concrete regime (specific $N$, $d$, $T$, and desired $\delta$) in which the prescribed scaling $K_r \gg \ell \gg n \to \infty$ (Assumption 4.2) yields an actually non-vacuous, computable numeric bound, and if so, how does the required $K_r$ compare to realistic LLM training-set sizes?
- Does the Wasserstein-DRO equivalence (Section 5) suggest a practical training-time regularizer (an explicit robustness penalty) that could be tested empirically for improving out-of-distribution robustness of trained Transformers, going beyond the purely asymptotic Γ-convergence result?
