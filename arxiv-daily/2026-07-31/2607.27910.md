> **一句话总结：** 论文对五种"减去残差流方向"的VLM越狱防御方法在四大架构、15个模型-层组合上做了统一幅度控制的横向审计，发现没有任何一种方向能同时在拒绝恢复和效用保留上全面获胜（例如image-conditioning shift在LLaVA-1.5-7B/L16上把拒绝率从0.159提升到0.619，提升46个百分点，而同幅度随机方向对照组毫无提升），且防御方向在不同解码器架构间几乎不可迁移。

> **TL;DR:** The paper audits five direction-subtraction jailbreak defences (image-conditioning shift, CMRM-style refusal direction, ShiftDC-style attack-specific residual, a prompt-level "ignore the image" instruction, and a magnitude-matched random control) across 15 (model, layer) cells spanning LLaVA-1.5-7B/13B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Qwen2-VL-2B, and Pixtral-12B. On LLaVA-1.5-7B/L16 the image-conditioning shift (ABL_POS) lifts refusal from 0.159 (WITH_IMAGE baseline) to 0.619 (+46pp) while the same-norm random-direction control stays at 0.159; on the JailBreakV benchmark ABL_POS recovers +0.261 over the 0.440 WITH_IMAGE baseline, but no defence Pareto-dominates across families and the calibrated direction fails to transfer between the only dimension-compatible architecture pair (LLaVA-1.5-13B and Pixtral-12B).

## 核心贡献

- 首个跨架构、幅度控制的越狱防御方向审计：在4个架构家族（LLaVA-1.5-7B/13B、Qwen2.5-VL-7B、Qwen2-VL-2B、Pixtral-12B）、每个模型3个层深度共15个(model, layer)单元上，统一比较5种候选方向（均值图像条件偏移、CMRM式拒绝方向、ShiftDC式攻击特定残差、提示级"忽略图像"指令、同范数随机对照），并让每个方向与同幅度随机扰动配对，把"方向选择"和"扰动大小"两个变量解耦。
- 发现没有单一候选方向能同时在拒绝恢复率和效用保留上帕累托占优：image-conditioning shift在LLaVA-1.5和Pixtral-12B上领先，且是唯一在每个架构家族里效用损失都停留在噪声下限的方法；提示指令在Qwen2.5-VL上领先；ShiftDC式攻击特定残差在Qwen2-VL-2B上领先。
- 证明image-conditioning方向具有强架构特异性：在13/15个单元上通过了针对幅度匹配随机方向的方向特异性检验，但在唯一维度兼容的LLaVA-1.5-13B与Pixtral-12B架构对之间几乎正交且不可迁移，说明防御必须按语言解码器家族分别校准。
- 首次定量证实两条此前独立的文献线（纯文本拒绝方向文献与多模态防御方向文献）识别的是部分重叠而非独立的几何结构：CMRM式纯文本拒绝方向与多模态image-conditioning偏移在全部15个单元上余弦对齐均为正（均值0.35，范围0.17–0.65，是随机向量零假设的15–25倍，符号检验p≈3×10⁻⁵）。

## Key Contributions

- **A systematic magnitude-controlled cross-architecture audit (C1):** Compares five direction-based defence candidates head-to-head across 15 (model, layer) cells from four architectural families (LLaVA-1.5-7B, LLaVA-1.5-13B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Qwen2-VL-2B, Pixtral-12B), holding per-prompt intervention magnitude fixed and pairing every direction against a same-norm random control, so observed effects are attributable to direction choice rather than perturbation size.
- **No single Pareto-dominant defence; the deployable rule is family-conditional:** The image-conditioning shift (ABL_POS) leads on LLaVA-1.5 and Pixtral-12B and is the only candidate whose utility loss stays at the measurement noise floor in every family; the prompt instruction (IGNORE_INSTR) leads on Qwen2.5-VL; the attack-specific residual (ShiftDC-like) leads on Qwen2-VL-2B. The positional direction is strongly architecture-specific: on the one dimension-compatible pair (LLaVA-1.5-13B / Pixtral-12B) the calibrated directions are nearly orthogonal and neither transfers.
- **Cross-paradigm sign consistency (C2):** The CMRM-style text-only refusal direction has positive cosine alignment with the multimodal image-conditioning shift in all 15 cells (mean 0.35, range 0.17–0.65, 15–25x the random-vector null, sign-test p ≈ 3×10⁻⁵) — the first quantitative cross-paradigm evidence that text-only refusal-mediation work (Arditi et al. 2024) and multimodal direction-based defence work (Zou et al. 2025; Liu et al. 2025b) identify overlapping rather than independent geometric structure.
- Distinguishes "magnitude as discriminator" from "direction as discriminator": benign- and jailbreak-image conditioning shifts are nearly co-linear at a given layer (mean cosine 0.949 across all 15 cells), so what separates jailbreak from benign prompts is how far the residual state moves along the shared image-conditioning axis, not which direction it moves in.

## Methodology

The audit is built on a single per-prompt "positional shift" primitive: for a VLM with residual-stream activation $h_\ell(x)$ at decoder layer $\ell$, and for each of three image conditions (text-only benign, benign image, jailbreak image), the paper computes $\Delta h^m(x) = h_\ell^m(x) - h_\ell^T(x)$, the per-prompt shift caused by imaging condition $m$. The cross-prompt aggregate is the sign-normalized mean of these per-prompt shift vectors, $\overline{\Delta h^m}$.

Five candidate "defence directions" are compared on a common 15-cell grid (5 VLMs x 3 layer depths per model: early/middle/late), using 11 attack settings drawn from four safety benchmarks (FigStep, HADES, JailBreakV, MM-SafetyBench) plus two utility benchmarks (MMBench, POPE):
1. **ABL_POS** — the mean image-conditioning shift $\overline{\Delta h^B}$ (calibrated on benign images only, no attack labels needed).
2. **CMRM-like** — a refusal direction calibrated as the mean difference between text-only refused vs. complied hidden states (reproducing Liu et al. 2025a's recipe without its original contrastive-classifier training pipeline).
3. **ShiftDC-like** — an attack-specific residual $\overline{\Delta h^J} - \overline{\Delta h^B}$ contrasting jailbreak vs. benign image conditioning (reproducing Zou et al. 2025's direction without its decomposition-optimization pipeline).
4. **IGNORE_INSTR** — a non-vector, prompt-level textual instruction telling the model to "ignore the image" (a training-free baseline, not a residual-stream hook).
5. **RANDOM_CTRL** — an isotropic random direction of the same per-prompt norm, redrawn per prompt, serving as the magnitude-matched null.

The core intervention (Eq. 2) replaces $h_\ell(x)$ with $h_\ell(x) - \alpha_h \|\Delta h^B(x)\| \widehat{\overline{\Delta h^B}}$, scaling the subtraction by the prompt's own image-conditioning norm so the perturbation never exceeds what naturally occurring image conditioning would produce ($\alpha_h \in \{0,1\}$ gives OFF/ON ablation). Direction tests use cosine similarity between per-prompt shifts and the calibrated population direction, with a pre-declared threshold (>=95% of safety prompts must show positive signed cosine) to certify "consistent positional alignment." Calibration uses a 50% held-out split of prompts stratified by attack type; the remaining 50% is used for evaluation. Refusal is scored via a first-generation-step substring lexicon (following Arditi et al. 2024's protocol), robustness-checked against an independent LLM judge (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct). Utility is measured via MMBench multiple-choice accuracy on a stratified Chinese-culture subset (n=98, later replicated on the English MMBench dev split at n=190) and POPE. A Pareto-dominance criterion (strict on refusal recovery, weak — ties allowed — on utility) is used to rank the five candidates per cell and in aggregate. Cross-model direction transfer is tested only on the one dimension-compatible architecture pair, LLaVA-1.5-13B and Pixtral-12B (both d=5120), since the Qwen models have incompatible residual dimensions (1536, 3584) with the rest of the grid.

## Results & Highlights

- **Causal sufficiency of the positional axis (Table 2):** On LLaVA-1.5-7B/L16 (n_eval=63 held-out jailbreak prompts), the WITH_IMAGE (no ablation) baseline has refusal rate 0.159 ± 0.090 (95% Wilson CI); ABL_POS raises this to 0.619 ± 0.118 (+46 pp), while RANDOM_CTRL (same-norm random direction) matches WITH_IMAGE almost exactly at 0.159 ± 0.090 — showing the effect is attributable to the specific mean image-conditioning direction, not merely to perturbation magnitude at that layer.
- **Per-attack-type recovery (Table 3, aggregated over the 15-cell grid):** ABL_POS recovery over the WITH_IMAGE baseline is +0.246 on FigStep (0.409 → 0.655), +0.202 on HADES (0.313 → 0.515), +0.261 on JailBreakV (0.440 → 0.700, the largest recovery), and only +0.173 on MM-SafetyBench (0.046 → 0.220, the smallest absolute recovery because the WITH_IMAGE refusal floor is already very low). RANDOM_CTRL stays within ±0.014 of WITH_IMAGE on every benchmark (e.g., 0.409 on FigStep, 0.430 on JailBreakV), confirming the recovery is direction-specific rather than magnitude-driven. Notably, ShiftDC-like beats ABL_POS on FigStep (0.725 vs. 0.655) and HADES (0.596 vs. 0.515) and JailBreakV (0.628 vs. 0.700 — comparable), showing per-benchmark leadership is contested.
- **Direction specificity across the full grid (Table 4):** 13 of 15 cells pass the strict paired-difference 95%-CI-excludes-zero criterion (ABL_POS lift vs. RANDOM_CTRL lift at matched total norm); the largest paired difference is on Qwen2-VL-2B/L22 at +0.812 ± 0.112, and the anchor cell LLaVA-1.5-7B/L16 shows +0.460 ± 0.124. Under Bonferroni correction across all 15 cells, 11/15 still pass.
- **Cross-paradigm sign consistency (Sec. 5.3, Fig. 1a):** The CMRM-style text-only refusal direction shows positive cosine alignment with the multimodal image-conditioning shift $\overline{\Delta h^B}$ in all 15 of 15 cells (mean cosine 0.35, range 0.17–0.65), which is 15–25x the random-vector null; a sign test against the null hypothesis of zero-mean cosine gives P(15/15 positive | null) = 0.5^15 ≈ 3.05×10⁻⁵, and a one-sample t-test across the 15 cell-mean cosines gives t_14=9.39, p<10⁻⁷.
- **Pareto/family-conditional outcome (Table 5, Table 6):** Aggregating over all 15 cells (refusal recovery vs. MMBench utility, n=98 per cell), Pareto-front counts are: IGNORE_INSTR 9/15, ABL_POS 7/15, ShiftDC-like 7/15, CMRM-like 2/15, RANDOM_CTRL 2/15. But the front is family-conditional, not uniform: on the LLaVA-1.5 family (n=6 cells) ABL_POS and ShiftDC-like tie at 4/6 each; on the Qwen family (n=6) ABL_POS is dominated on 0/6 cells while IGNORE_INSTR leads on 4/6; on Pixtral-12B (n=3) only ABL_POS makes the front on all 3 cells with no utility loss.
- **Cross-model transfer failure (App. G):** On the only dimension-compatible pair (LLaVA-1.5-13B and Pixtral-12B, both d=5120), the calibrated $\overline{\Delta h^B}$ directions are nearly orthogonal at every matched layer (cos(native, transferred) ∈ [0.007, 0.038]), and transferring the source direction to the target recovers essentially no refusal (≈0 recovery Pixtral→LLaVA vs. native LLaVA efficacy of +0.05 to +0.15; transferred LLaVA→Pixtral recovery is only 0.05 vs. native Pixtral efficacy of +0.55 to +0.65).

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core methodological contribution — pairing every candidate defence direction with a magnitude-matched random control, applied uniformly across a 15-cell (model x layer) grid — is a genuine and useful discipline that prior single-architecture defence papers (CMRM, ShiftDC, VLMGuard) lacked. The cross-paradigm cosine-alignment finding (C2) is a novel empirical bridge between the text-only refusal-direction literature (Arditi et al. 2024) and the multimodal defence literature, backed by a real statistical test (sign test, t-test) rather than a hand-wavy claim.

**Rigor:** The magnitude-matched random control is the single strongest design choice in the paper and is applied consistently (it appears in nearly every table). The paper is honest about statistical caveats it introduces itself: it flags that 2 of 15 cells fail the strict direction-specificity criterion (with CI lower bounds just below zero at −0.021 and −0.007), reports both corrected (Bonferroni, 11/15) and uncorrected (13/15) pass rates rather than only the favorable number, and explicitly checks robustness of its refusal metric by re-scoring all 15 cells with an independent LLM judge (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) — a good practice since the primary metric is a first-token substring lexicon that "can miss soft refusals."

**Paper-specific weak point:** The reproduced CMRM-like and ShiftDC-like baselines are explicitly *not* the original methods — the paper states it reproduces "the published direction without their surrounding training pipelines (CMRM's contrastive classifier and ShiftDC's decomposition optimisation)" and holds the same per-prompt magnitude as ABL_POS. This is a defensible design for isolating "direction choice" as the audit's stated scope, but it also means the comparison against CMRM and ShiftDC is a comparison against stripped-down versions of those methods, not their full published pipelines — the paper's claim that "any gap is a lower bound on what the direction choice contributes" is reasonable but should not be read as a head-to-head reproduction of the original papers' reported numbers. VLMGuard is omitted entirely because its discriminator weights and training data were never released — a real and disclosed limitation rather than a hidden one.

**Reproducibility:** Reasonably strong — the method requires only benign-image prompts for calibration (no attack labels), one d-dimensional vector per cell, and the paper reports calibration and evaluation sample sizes (n=47–65 per cell for refusal recovery, n=98/190 for utility) plus a declared 50/50 calibration/evaluation split. However, frontier closed-source VLMs are excluded because they don't expose hidden states, so the "family-conditional deployment rule" cannot be verified beyond the five open-weight models tested.

**Significance:** The finding that a calibrated defence direction is "strongly architecture-specific and nontransferable... even between models with identical residual-stream dimensions" (LLaVA-1.5-13B and Pixtral-12B, both d=5120) is a genuinely useful negative result for practitioners who might otherwise assume dimension compatibility implies transferability. The threat model is explicitly narrow (a static attacker who does not adapt to the defence or know the calibration direction/layer), which the authors disclose upfront rather than glossing over — an adaptive attacker crafting an image whose shift projects only weakly onto the ablation direction is left as future work, which is a fair scoping decision but does limit how much the "defence" claims should be trusted against a motivated adversary.

**Red flags:** None severe. The clearest caveat is that the utility metric (MMBench MCQ accuracy) is described by the authors themselves as "a coarse proxy that may not register open-ended degradation such as fluency or grounding," and the paper's own ~2pp noise floor means several Pareto-front distinctions (e.g., IGNORE_INSTR's 9/15 count) hinge on utility differences below the stated measurement resolution — the authors flag this explicitly ("Pareto membership... should be read with caution") rather than hiding it, which is a mark of intellectual honesty rather than a fatal flaw.

## Strengths

- Rigorous experimental design: fixed per-prompt intervention magnitude across all defence candidates plus a matched-norm random-direction null, isolating direction choice from perturbation size — a control largely absent from the individual prior papers being compared.
- Broad, genuinely cross-architecture coverage (5 models, 4 families, 3 layer depths each = 15 cells) rather than the single-base-architecture scope typical of prior defence papers (CMRM, ShiftDC, VLMGuard were each proposed and evaluated on one architecture).
- Transparent statistical reporting: Wilson 95% CIs throughout, both corrected and uncorrected significance results shown, an independent LLM-judge robustness check on the refusal metric, and an alpha-sensitivity ablation (App. C.1) showing the ablation-strength hyperparameter is not cherry-picked at a knife-edge.
- The cross-paradigm sign-consistency finding (C2) is a substantive, testable empirical claim (not just a plausibility argument) connecting two previously separate strands of interpretability/safety literature.
- Explicit, well-scoped limitations section (closed-model exclusion, refusal-lexicon coarseness, single safety distribution, static-attacker threat model) rather than a limitations section that only lists trivial caveats.

## Limitations

- Only open-weights VLMs are tested (LLaVA-1.5-7B/13B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Qwen2-VL-2B, Pixtral-12B); frontier closed-source models cannot be audited because they do not expose hidden states, so the family-conditional deployment guidance is unverified beyond these five models.
- Refusal is scored by a first-generation-step substring lexicon, which the authors acknowledge can miss paraphrased/soft refusals (a multi-token extension is left open), though this is partially mitigated by the independent LLM-judge robustness check.
- Utility is measured only via MMBench multiple-choice accuracy (plus POPE), a coarse proxy that the authors themselves note "may not register open-ended degradation such as fluency or grounding."
- The calibration is performed on a single safety-prompt distribution; the authors note that prompts differing in new languages, domains, or image styles could shift the calibrated mean shift $\overline{\Delta h^B}$ at a "low but non-zero re-calibration cost."
- Cross-model direction transfer is verified on only one dimension-compatible architecture pair (LLaVA-1.5-13B ↔ Pixtral-12B), so the "transfer fails" conclusion, while striking, rests on a single data point among architecture pairs.
- The threat model assumes a static attacker with no knowledge of or adaptation to the calibrated direction or layer; robustness against an adaptive attacker who optimizes the jailbreak image to evade the specific ablation direction is explicitly left to future work.

## Relevance

这篇论文对做VLM/LLM安全对齐、越狱防御或表示工程（representation engineering）方向研究的人非常相关：它提供了一套可复用的"幅度匹配随机对照+跨架构网格"审计范式，可直接套用到自己新提出的防御方向上做严谨评估，同时其"防御方向不可跨架构迁移"和"文本-多模态拒绝方向部分共享几何结构"两个发现对设计新的推理时防御或可解释性方法有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would an adaptive attacker who has white-box access to the calibrated $\overline{\Delta h^B}$ direction (or can query refusal behaviour) be able to craft jailbreak images whose shift projects mostly onto the orthogonal complement, defeating ABL_POS while still achieving the jailbreak objective?
- Does the cross-paradigm cosine alignment between CMRM's text-only refusal direction and the multimodal image-conditioning shift (mean 0.35) reflect a genuinely shared "compliance" subspace, or could training data overlap between the text-only and multimodal refusal-inducing prompts partially explain the correlation?
- Would combining the leading family-specific defences (e.g., ABL_POS for LLaVA/Pixtral, IGNORE_INSTR for Qwen2.5-VL) into an ensemble or a learned per-family gate recover more Pareto-optimal cells than any single fixed recipe, and at what added inference cost?
- How sensitive is the 15-cell grid's outcome to the choice of "early/middle/late" layer depths — would a finer per-layer sweep reveal a narrower band of layers where architecture-specific transfer becomes more or less severe (Fig. 2 shows monotonic lift growth into late layers, but transfer was only tested at 3 depths)?
- Since VLMGuard (a learned discriminator-direction approach) could not be reproduced due to unreleased weights, how would a properly retrained VLMGuard-style learned direction compare against the training-free ABL_POS/CMRM/ShiftDC recipes on this same 15-cell grid?
