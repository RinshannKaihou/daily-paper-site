> **一句话总结：** GRSD让策略在GRPO采样的同一组rollout上对自己已验证的成功/失败轨迹做结构化反思，再用stop-gradient快照对比成功组与失败组反思、合成群体级DO/AVOID指导，并据此把verifier给出的轨迹级优势重塑为不改变符号的回合级优势；在ALFWorld/Search-based QA/WebShop三个环境、Qwen3-1.7B/Qwen2.5-3B/7B三种骨干上，平均比GRPO在ALFWorld提升5.5%、Search-QA提升2.7%、WebShop成功率提升7.2%，在ALFWorld未见任务集上比更强的技能蒸馏基线SDAR高6.0%（83.6% vs 77.6%），且额外训练开销只比GRPO高17%、推理零开销。

> **TL;DR:** GRSD has the same on-policy agent reflect on its own verified ALFWorld / Search-based-QA / WebShop rollouts, contrasts successful vs. failed reflections with a stop-gradient policy snapshot to build group-level DO/AVOID guidance, then uses that guidance to reshape GRPO's trajectory-level advantage into a bounded, sign-preserving turn-level advantage. Across three backbones (Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen2.5-3B/7B-Instruct) it beats GRPO by 5.5% average success on ALFWorld and 2.7% accuracy on Search-based QA, beats the strongest skill-distillation baseline SDAR by 6.0% on ALFWorld's unseen split (83.6% vs. 77.6%), and adds only 17% wall-clock training overhead with zero inference-time cost.

## 核心贡献

- 提出GRSD：不依赖外部更强模型生成技能库，仅用策略自身在已验证rollout上的反思，构造与当前策略能力对齐、且能区分成功/失败模式的群体级指导，解决检索式/单轨迹蒸馏技能与当前策略能力错配、路径特异化的问题。
- 设计两阶段指导构造：Stage A让策略对每条已验证轨迹做结构化自我反思（定位关键回合、解释成败、总结DO/AVOID行为），由固定外部judge（DeepSeek-V4-Flash，0-3分四级评分）监督反思质量；Stage B用stop-gradient快照对比同一prompt下成功组S+与失败组S-的反思，合成紧凑的group-level指导z_x（仅在同组既有成功又有失败时激活）。
- 提出回合级自蒸馏机制：用guidance-augmented教师上下文与plain学生上下文下同一冻结策略对同一采样token的对数似然差作为信号，按verifier优势符号对齐、归一化裁剪后调制GRPO轨迹级优势为回合级优势，并在附录中给出理论证明（Proposition 1-3）：该调制是有界的正重加权，不会反转verifier决定的更新方向。
- 该指导仅在训练时构造和使用，推理阶段无需任何特权上下文；额外训练开销仅比GRPO高17%（同环境下SDAR为8%），却在三个环境、三种骨干上都取得最优聚合性能和更强的未见任务泛化。

## Key Contributions

- Introduces GRSD, a self-distillation scheme that derives group-level, outcome-discriminative guidance purely from the policy's own verified on-policy rollouts, avoiding retrieved skill libraries or a stronger external analyzer model that could exceed the policy's capability boundary.
- Two-stage guidance construction: Stage A has the policy produce a structured, judge-scored (DeepSeek-V4-Flash, 4-point rubric) self-reflection on each verified trajectory identifying outcome-critical turns and DO/AVOID behaviors; Stage B uses a stop-gradient snapshot of the same policy to contrast reflections from successful (S+) vs. failed (S-) rollouts under the same prompt into compact privileged guidance z_x, activated only when a rollout group contains both outcomes.
- Turn-level self-distillation that converts the teacher(guidance)-vs-student(plain) log-likelihood gap into a bounded, sign-preserving modulation of the GRPO trajectory-level advantage (Eq. 11), with a formal analysis (Propositions 1-3, Appendix A) proving the modulation is a positively-reweighted, bounded perturbation of the clipped GRPO surrogate that cannot flip the verifier-determined advantage sign.
- Guidance is used only during training (never at inference), adding just 17% wall-clock overhead versus GRPO (vs. 8% for SDAR under the same setup) while achieving the best aggregate performance and strongest unseen-task generalization across 3 backbones and 3 environments.

## Methodology

GRSD builds on multi-turn GRPO: for each prompt x, the old policy samples a group of G=8 rollouts, a verifier assigns task reward R_i and binary outcome label b_i, and rewards are group-normalized into the trajectory-level advantage A_i.

**Stage A (policy-native self-reflection).** After a trajectory τ_i is completed and verified, the *same* rollout policy π_θold produces a reflection s_i conditioned on (x, τ_i, b_i, a fixed reflection prompt p_ref), identifying outcome-critical turns and distilling concrete behaviors to reinforce or avoid. A fixed external judge J scores each reflection on a 0-3 rubric for causal correctness, outcome consistency, and actionability; the score is group-normalized into a reflection advantage A_i^ref, used only to train the reflection tokens (disjoint from task tokens).

**Stage B (group-level guidance construction).** For each prompt, reflections are partitioned into S+ (successful) and S- (failed) sets. A stop-gradient snapshot θ̄ = sg(θ_old) of the same policy contrasts the two sets and synthesizes prompt-specific guidance z_x (a compact DO-list / AVOID-list) via π_θ̄(·|x, S+, S-, p_guide). This stage is only activated for "mixed-outcome" groups containing both successes and failures (mask m_x); otherwise GRSD falls back to vanilla GRPO. No gradient flows through z_x — it is treated as fixed privileged context for turn-level credit refinement.

**Turn-level self-distillation.** For each sampled turn t, the same frozen snapshot policy evaluates the same sampled action tokens under a plain student context c_stu = C(x, H_t) and a guidance-augmented teacher context c_tea = C(x, z_x, H_t). The mean log-likelihood difference Δ_i,t quantifies how much the guidance shifts support for that turn. This is aligned with the advantage's sign via q_i,t = sign(A_i)·Δ_i,t, then normalized by the mean absolute magnitude across valid turns in the same prompt group and clipped to a bounded modulation score q̂_i,t ∈ [-Ĝ, Ĝ]. The turn-specific advantage is Â_i,t = A_i·(1 + λ·q̂_i,t·m_x), where λ controls distillation strength; the constraint λĜ ≤ 1 guarantees the modulation cannot reverse the sign of A_i.

**Overall objective.** L(θ) = L_task(θ) + α·L_ref(θ). L_task is the standard clipped GRPO surrogate but with the turn-modulated advantage Â_i,t replacing the trajectory-level A_i, shared across action tokens within a turn but varying across turns of the same trajectory. L_ref applies the same GRPO surrogate to reflection tokens s_i with the independently normalized A_i^ref. The two losses supervise disjoint token sets; guidance construction and teacher-student evaluation are fully detached (stop-gradient), so privileged context only affects optimization through the detached credit modulation, never as a generation target, and is absent at inference.

## Results & Highlights

- **Main aggregate gains over GRPO**: averaged across three backbones (Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct), GRSD outperforms GRPO by 5.5% average success rate on ALFWorld and 2.7% accuracy on Search-based QA, and improves WebShop score and success by 4.8% and 7.2% respectively.
- **Gains over the strongest skill-distillation baselines**: compared with Skill-SD and SDAR (the two skill-conditioned self-distillation methods), GRSD improves average ALFWorld success by 4.2% and WebShop success by 5.5%, while achieving the best aggregate result under all three backbones (though the paper concedes gains are "not uniform on every individual subset" for Search-based QA).
- **Unseen-task generalization (ALFWorld Unseen split)**: GRSD reaches an 83.6% average success rate, outperforming GRPO (75.4%, +8.2%) and SDAR (77.6%, +6.0%); the largest gains are on Pick2 (+23.5% over GRPO, +11.7% over SDAR) and Cool (+14.3% over GRPO, +9.5% over SDAR).
- **Guidance-construction ablation (Table 2, Qwen3-1.7B)**: removing group-reflective guidance (replacing z_x with a standard reference solution) drops ALFWorld average success from 92.2% to 85.2% (-7.0%) and WebShop success from 66.4% to 59.4% (-7.0%); excluding failed reflections (guidance built only from S+) drops ALFWorld to 88.3% (-3.9%) and WebShop success to 62.5% (-3.9%); bypassing group aggregation (using unaggregated per-trajectory reflections) drops ALFWorld to 89.8% (-2.4%).
- **Modulation-granularity ablation (Table 2)**: token-level modulation over individual action tokens reduces ALFWorld to 89.1% (-3.1%) and WebShop success to 64.1% (-2.3%); trajectory-level modulation (one shared factor per rollout) is worse, dropping ALFWorld to 85.9% (-6.3%), Search-QA to 42.9% (-0.9%), and WebShop success to 61.0% (-5.4%) — confirming that turn-level granularity is necessary rather than incidental.
- **Training cost (Table 6, ALFWorld/Qwen3-1.7B)**: GRSD adds 17% wall-clock training time over GRPO (333.4s vs. 284.1s per update) versus SDAR's 8% (307.9s); most overhead comes from online guidance construction (29.4s) rather than reflection-loss computation (7.3s), and GRSD's teacher forward pass (12.0s) is actually cheaper than SDAR's (25.1s) because Stage B distills rollout-level reflections into compact per-prompt guidance instead of a full retrieved skill context.
- **Policy-native vs. external-model reflections (Figure 4)**: substituting the policy's own reflections with reflections generated by a stronger external model yields lower validation success rate on ALFWorld and a larger mean teacher-student likelihood gap throughout training, supporting the claim that self-generated guidance better matches (and is more internalizable by) the optimized policy's own capability boundary.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The central idea — deriving self-distillation guidance from the policy's *own* reflections rather than externally retrieved or stronger-model-generated skills — is a sensible, well-motivated refinement that directly targets a real failure mode of SDAR/Skill-SD (capability mismatch, path-specific single-trajectory extraction). The success/failure contrastive construction in Stage B is the most distinctive piece. However, the turn-level advantage-modulation mechanism itself explicitly follows prior work (Zhong et al. 2026, cited directly as the turn-level credit-assignment scheme it builds on), so the novelty is concentrated in *what* constructs the guidance (policy-native, outcome-contrastive) rather than *how* credit is redistributed.

**Rigor.** The appendix provides a genuine theoretical treatment (Propositions 1-3) bounding the advantage perturbation and proving sign-preservation — stronger formal backing than typical ablation-only "skill-conditioned" papers. Notably, the paper is self-critical about the theory's scope: Appendix A.1 states plainly that "the actual method aggregates natural-language reflections through the frozen policy and does not explicitly compute d̂_x; hence, the result does not imply the generated guidance exactly recovers μ¹_x − μ⁰_x," and Appendix A.5 explicitly disclaims that the analysis "does not guarantee semantic correctness of the generated guidance, monotonic improvement in expected return, or convergence of the joint task-reflection optimization." This is an unusually honest gap disclosure between what is proven and what is empirically claimed.

**Baseline strength.** The training-free baselines (Vanilla, Skill-Prompt) trail every RL-trained method by 60-80 points on ALFWorld, so they mainly establish that RL training helps at all rather than isolating GRSD's mechanism. The meaningful comparisons are GRPO/OPSD/RLSD and especially Skill-SD/SDAR; against the strongest of these (SDAR), GRSD's margin is more modest (4.2% ALFWorld average, non-uniform gains on individual Search-QA subsets).

**Missing ablation.** GRSD still depends on an external judge (DeepSeek-V4-Flash) to score reflection quality (Eq. 4) — the "no external model for skill generation" framing is true for guidance *content* but not for reflection *supervision*. The paper does not ablate judge choice, judge temperature, or a judge-free/self-scored alternative, leaving open how sensitive results are to this external dependency.

**Significance.** Gains are consistent but incremental (single-digit percentage points against the closest, contemporaneous baseline SDAR), and the guidance-construction mechanism (Stage B) is inactive whenever a rollout group is not mixed-outcome, meaning effectiveness plausibly depends on the policy's success rate staying in an intermediate range — a dependency the paper itself surfaces via the "valid-group ratio" plot (Figure 7b) but does not stress-test at extreme success rates.

## Strengths

- Directly targets a well-articulated failure mode of prior skill-based self-distillation (retrieval mismatch, capability-exceeding external analyzers, path-specific single-trajectory extraction) with a purpose-built contrastive success/failure mechanism.
- Formal theoretical grounding (Appendix A, Propositions 1-3) for the turn-level advantage modulation, paired with an unusually candid statement of what the theory does and does not prove.
- Thorough component ablations (Table 2) cleanly attributing gains to guidance source, group aggregation, failed-reflection inclusion, and modulation granularity (token/turn/trajectory-level).
- No inference-time overhead — guidance is training-only privileged context — and modest training overhead (17% vs. GRPO), lower in some cost dimensions than the closest comparable baseline SDAR.
- Evaluated across 3 model scales/backbones and 3 qualitatively different environments (embodied ALFWorld, search-augmented QA, web navigation) plus an explicit out-of-distribution generalization study (ALFWorld Unseen split, 5 OOD QA datasets).

## Limitations

- Still relies on an external judge model (DeepSeek-V4-Flash) at training time to score reflection quality, so the "policy-native, no external model" framing applies to guidance content but not reflection supervision, and judge sensitivity is unexplored.
- The theoretical analysis explicitly disclaims guarantees of semantic correctness, monotonic improvement, or joint-optimization convergence, and its variance-reduction proposition relies on an idealized linear representation model not shown to hold for actual LLM-generated reflections.
- Improvements over the strongest recent baseline (SDAR) are consistently single-digit percentage points, with the paper's own text conceding non-uniform gains on individual Search-based QA subsets.
- Stage B guidance construction requires a mixed-outcome rollout group (both success and failure under the same prompt); it is inactive otherwise and falls back to vanilla GRPO, so effectiveness likely depends on the policy's success rate remaining in an intermediate range throughout training.
- All methods share a fairly narrow hyperparameter sweep (single learning rate, single KL coefficient, fixed group size G=8); sensitivity to judge model choice or group size beyond the reported λ/α ablations is not explored.

## Relevance

该论文与agentic RL、on-policy self-distillation、多轮回合级信用分配等主题高度相关，其"策略自身反思+成功失败对比构造群体指导"的思路对当前用GRPO/RLVR训练工具调用agent、又想避免依赖外部更强模型生成技能库的研究方向有直接借鉴价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How sensitive is GRSD's performance to the choice, temperature, or reliability of the external judge J that scores reflection quality — would a weaker or self-scored judge substantially degrade guidance quality?
- Does the "valid-group ratio" (fraction of rollout groups containing both successes and failures) become a bottleneck as the policy approaches very high or very low success rates, and how would GRSD perform on tasks with near-0% or near-100% success rate for extended periods?
- Would replacing the fixed judge-scored reflection reward with a self-consistency or verifier-free signal preserve the "no external model" claim more fully while retaining performance?
- How does GRSD compare to SDAR/Skill-SD on tasks requiring longer horizons than the ~50 ALFWorld / 4 Search-QA / 15 WebShop environment steps tested here, where turn-level credit assignment becomes harder?
- Can the group-reflective guidance be cached and reused across similar prompts to amortize the Stage B synthesis cost, rather than being recomputed per prompt at every update?
