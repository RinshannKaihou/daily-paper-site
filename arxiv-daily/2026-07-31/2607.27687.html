Rehearse

Once 3 or more prior successful changes have accumulated, a memoryless judge's ability to pick the better candidate falls off a cliff — while its coverage (willingness to render a verdict) still rises from 76% to 85%. Rehearse's focused outcome memory restores accuracy to 83.5% at the same depth.

Why This Matters

Any automated ML or agentic pipeline that lets an LLM judge its own next move should note: more memory isn't better — outcome labels carry the signal, not explanations.

中文原文（Relevance）：论文提出的"信心悬崖"现象和"聚焦结果记忆"设计，对任何构建自动化实验/超参搜索/agentic ML pipeline 的场景都有直接参考价值——尤其是"记忆内容越多不一定越好，结果标签比失败原因解释更重要"的反直觉发现。

▲ Critical Edge

Live-loop gains rest on just 5 seeds per setup, and one ETTh1 baseline run (59.9% MSE cut) nearly matched Rehearse — a 9-of-10 win, not a clean sweep.

Methodology

1

Propose — the agent emits N candidate modifications (5 in live runs) as structured JSON: description, hypothesis (the mechanistic reason it should help), implementation, type, priority, risk — explicitly steered toward different axes (optimizer, architecture, regularization, schedule, data) rather than variants of one hyperparameter.

2

Predict — before any training run, a judge LLM ranks candidates via a pairwise strict-consensus tournament: every pair is judged twice, in both presentation orders; a verdict counts only if both orders agree, otherwise the judge abstains (controls for known LLM position bias). Top pick moves to Execute.

3

Execute — the top-ranked candidate trains on a copy of the current state. If the metric improves, the patch is committed; either way, one line — change description plus a binary worked / did-not-work label — is written back to the store. No failure reasons, no summaries.

4

Focused store — future judgments retrieve only same-task attempts at cosine similarity ≥ 0.40 (384-dim MiniLM embeddings); nothing below that threshold is returned.

The trick is what the store leaves out: ablations show that adding failure-reason explanations back lowers cliff accuracy (83.5% → 80.6%, or 54.8% on the calls that carry one) — outcome labels, not explanations, carry the decision signal.

"Propose-many" (breadth alone, no selection) scores 6.9% on nanochat — barely below vanilla's 7.1%, showing selection, not more ideas, drives the gain. Rehearse also reaches vanilla's final level in fewer runs: 54 experiments on CIFAR-10 (46% fewer) and 63 on ETTh1 (37% fewer).

  • Ablation isolates the mechanism (cliff bucket): dropping the candidate rationale cuts accuracy to 70.2%; swapping focused retrieval for a full dump drops it to 70.8%; keeping only "worked" precedents (no failures) drops it to 78.9%.
  • Filtering beats fine ranking: on type-1 pairs (one candidate worked, one didn't) accuracy rises from 79.5% to 84.2% with the store; on harder type-2 pairs (both worked, rank the bigger win) it only rises from 67.4% to 74.1%.
  • A non-LLM heuristic falls flat: a rule that just avoids candidates near past failures scores 47.8% on the deep bucket and 52.4% overall — below chance, and below even the memoryless LLM judge.

Strengths

  • Turns a vague "returns diminish late" intuition into a named, measured phenomenon, backed by a purpose-built 366-pair, outcome-labeled benchmark rather than an anecdote.
  • Strict two-order consensus judging plus 3-judge-model averaging cuts run-to-run measurement spread from 5.1 to 1.7 points.
  • Component-level ablations isolate exactly what helps — relevant focused text, mixed worked/failed labels, outcome labels specifically — not a blanket "memory on" claim.
  • Validated live across three unrelated domains (LM pretraining, image classification, time-series forecasting), plus a formal Precision@K calibration linking the pairwise metric to deployment quality.
  • Ships as a lightweight, code-agnostic skill — no fine-tuning, no change to the target training script.

Limitations

  • Live-loop evidence rests on only 5 seeds per configuration; one ETTh1 vanilla seed (59.9%) nearly matches Rehearse while the other four land at 31–45%.
  • n_baseline (loop depth) is observational, not randomized — later decisions may simply face harder, lower-headroom choices, a confound the authors acknowledge directly.
  • Reported training-run budgets exclude the 20 judge-inference calls per step (N=5) — the real compute/latency overhead is uncharacterized.
  • Model and task are paired, not crossed, across the three live loops — gains could reflect a model–task idiosyncrasy rather than the mechanism alone.
  • Retrieval relies on a small, cheap 384-dim MiniLM encoder; stronger retrievers remain untested.
  • Live model endpoints are serving aliases, not frozen checkpoints, which could complicate exact reproduction of live-loop numbers over time.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Would the cliff and its fix generalize to K>1 batched selection in live loops, beyond the offline Monte Carlo calibration?
  2. How does Rehearse's extra judge-inference overhead trade off against training-run savings in real wall-clock/dollar terms?
  3. Does the failure-reason-hurts-accuracy finding hold with stronger judge models, or is it an artifact of the three tested here?
  4. Would a larger or fine-tuned retriever raise cliff accuracy further, or is focused outcome labeling already near a ceiling?
  5. How would Rehearse perform head-to-head against ForeAgent's confidence-gated filtering on the same benchmark?
  6. Does the cliff appear in longer runs beyond 100 experiments, and does its shape depend on how close to saturation the task metric already is?
