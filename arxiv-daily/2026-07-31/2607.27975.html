Generalization Bounds on Optimal Control for Transformer Training and Wasserstein Distributional Robustness ↗

Kağan Akman · Naci Saldi · Serdar Yüksel

30 July 2026

Transformer Generalization Optimal Control Measure-Valued MDP Wasserstein DRO Concentration Inequalities Dynamic Programming

Key Metric · Theorem 3.3 (theoretical, not empirical)

O(Kr−1/2)

Convergence rate of the finite-sample generalization bound, in the number of sampled data sets Kr — the "optimal" parametric Monte-Carlo rate. No numbers were ever computed for a real model.

Why This Matters

Gives Transformer-theory and optimal-control researchers a genuinely new lens on generalization, though no experiments and strong compactness assumptions cap its practical reach.

▲ Critical Edge

The key Lipschitz constant BT scales exponentially in the number of Transformer layers T, so the bound likely turns extremely loose for real, many-layer models.

Methodology

1 Particle level Per-token dynamics via feed-forward + attention; not Markov — attention couples every token to the whole sequence.
→
2 Singly lifted Ensemble becomes an empirical measure μt; the push-forward map restores the Markov property. Weights become controls.
→
3 Doubly lifted A training set is itself a set of measures — lift again to a law over (input, output) measure pairs.
→
4 Triply quantized Grid the states, net the weights, discretize the measure space itself → a finite MDP solvable by dynamic programming.

Transformer training doesn't fit the classical Markov-chain mold: each token's update depends on the attention-weighted empirical measure of every other token in the sequence. A companion paper (arXiv:2603.09571) fixed this by lifting the state to the space of measures, turning training into a finite-horizon optimal-control problem where the per-layer weights (Q, K, V, W, A, b) are the controls. This paper's job is to make that infinite-dimensional control problem computable and to extract a generalization bound from it. Three quantizations do the work: the token state space is gridded (error ~1/n), the weight space is netted (error ~1/m), and — the hard part — the space of probability measures itself is discretized into finitely many "reconstruction points" via Reznik's (2011) algorithm. The payoff is a finite MDP. A Lipschitz stability estimate for the value function, combined with McDiarmid's bounded-differences inequality applied to the empirical law of empirical measures, then yields the concentration bound.

Standing assumption: the weight/action space 𝕊 must be compact. This models norm-constrained training (e.g. spectral/operator-norm bounds) — not unconstrained SGD, as the authors themselves flag in the Conclusion.

Results — All Theorems, No Numbers

No quantitative results reported: this is a purely theoretical paper with no experiments, datasets, baselines, or numerical evaluation. Every "result" below is a theorem, proposition, or lemma with a full proof.

Strengths

  • Genuinely novel framing: stochastic optimal control + dynamic programming + Wasserstein DRO in one measure-valued framework, not the usual norm/covering-number route.
  • Full rigor with explicit constants — BT, CT, L are spelled out (Remark 4.8), not left as unspecified O(·) terms.
  • Unusually candid limitations: the authors flag their own compactness/norm-constraint assumption and the curse of dimensionality themselves.
  • A specific, technical comparison to the closest prior work (Kan et al. [22]) with three concrete points of difference, not a vague gesture.
  • A clearly documented model hierarchy (particle → lifted → doubly lifted → quantized) showing exactly what property each step restores.

Limitations

  • Zero experiments: the bound is never instantiated numerically, even for a toy Transformer — unknown if it's vacuous at realistic Kr.
  • The compactness assumption on the weight space excludes standard unconstrained SGD-trained Transformers, by the authors' own admission.
  • Curse of dimensionality: state covering number ~ N·nd, and BT grows exponentially with depth T.
  • Measure-space quantization cardinality grows combinatorially, and the required Kr inherits the blow-up — feasibility at realistic N, d is untested.
  • No quantitative or qualitative comparison against the covering-number/norm-based Transformer bounds the paper itself cites as related work.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Can BT's exponential-in-depth growth be tamed via normalization layers or weight-sharing to make the bound non-vacuous for deep nets?
  2. How does this bound compare numerically, not just structurally, to covering-number bounds like [25]/[36] on the same architecture and data?
  3. Can the compactness assumption on 𝕊 be relaxed to weight-decay-regularized but unconstrained SGD via a high-probability posterior norm bound?
  4. Is there a concrete (N, d, T, δ) regime where the prescribed Kr ≫ ℓ ≫ n scaling gives an actual non-vacuous number?
  5. Does the WDRO equivalence suggest a practical, testable robustness regularizer, going beyond the purely asymptotic Γ-convergence result?
