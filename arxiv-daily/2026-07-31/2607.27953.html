On the largest held-out routing scale, the LLM-discovered APW objective beats the strongest hand-designed preference baseline (PO4COPs) and standard neural baseline (POMO). The paper notes this margin widens, not shrinks, as scale grows.

Why This Matters

Turns preference-loss design — previously a bespoke, hand-tuned research task — into something an LLM can search automatically and generalize across problem scales.

中文原文（Relevance）：该工作把"损失函数设计"本身当作可自动化搜索的对象，延续了 LLM 驱动的算法/目标发现路线（FunSearch、Eureka 等），对研究强化学习目标工程、组合优化神经求解器、以及自动化机器学习流水线设计的读者有较高参考价值。

▲ Critical Edge

On JSSP15×15 the headline win is inflated by a collapsing baseline — PO4COPs falls to 36.841% gap, but against the actually competitive BOPO (8.108%), APW's edge shrinks to about half a percentage point.

Methodology

1

Setup — in preference-based NCO, a construction policy πθ samples a solution set per instance; unequal-cost pairs are oriented low-cost (preferred) → high-cost, and training raises the relative likelihood of preferred solutions.

2

Two-program objective — a preference objective is represented as O=(f,g): pairwise loss program f (what to learn from one comparison) and set-aware weighting program g (how much to weight it, given full solution-set context). The F×G space provably recovers PO4COPs and BOPO as fixed points.

3

Staged conditional search — Stage 1 fixes uniform weighting and searches f alone (→ UPW objective); Stage 2 freezes f* and searches g alone (→ APW objective). Avoids the combinatorial explosion and credit-assignment ambiguity of a joint F×G search.

4

Behavioral gates — before any costly policy training, candidate programs must pass four checks: executable validity, preference consistency (sign flips on swap), scale invariance (affine cost rescaling), and weighting non-degeneracy (coefficient-of-variation threshold).

5

Fitness & selection — survivors get a short, fixed-budget training run from two policy states (early random-init and mid-training), scored by average cost improvement over a PO4COPs reference; a diversity filter prevents converging to structurally redundant candidates.

Scale: discovery runs once per COP family at TSP100 / CVRP100 / FFSP100 / JSSP15×15; the frozen objective is then retrained from scratch at that scale plus two held-out scales (up to 10x larger, e.g. TSP1000, FFSP1000, JSSP50×20) to test generalization.

Strengths

  • Clean theoretical unification: PO4COPs and BOPO are shown as exact special cases of the searched F×G space, evidence the space is well-posed and expressive.
  • Behavioral gating is a practical, transferable idea for LLM-guided program discovery in RL objective design generally, not just NCO.
  • Strong cross-scale generalization: objectives discovered at one scale per family are frozen and evaluated at two held-out scales with no extra search, and margins tend to persist or widen on routing tasks.
  • Factorial ablation cleanly attributes gains to the pairwise loss and the weighting program individually and jointly.
  • Broad task coverage spanning routing (TSP, CVRP) and scheduling (FFSP, JSSP), strengthening the generality claim.

Limitations

  • The staged (not joint) search is an acknowledged "pragmatic compromise" that may find only a local optimum in the coupled F×G space; no empirical bound on the gap to a joint search is given in the main text.
  • Some reported gains (FFSP; APW vs. BOPO on JSSP15×15) are modest, while the most eye-catching numbers come from a baseline (PO4COPs on JSSP) essentially failing.
  • Search runs once per COP family at a single discovery scale — no variance across multiple independent search runs (seeds / LLM sampling) is reported.
  • No code/artifact link is evident, and the specific LLM backbone used for program proposal is not stated in the main text, limiting reproducibility judgments.
  • Restricted to pairwise objectives; extending to listwise formulations is left to future work.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. How does a full joint search over F×G (reported only in Appendix D.2) compare to the staged conditional search in final performance and search cost?
  2. What is the variance in discovered-objective quality across multiple independent LLM search runs for the same COP family?
  3. How much wall-clock/compute does the entire discovery pipeline consume relative to simply training a few extra hand-designed baseline variants?
  4. Would the discovered objectives transfer across COP families (e.g. TSP-discovered APW applied to CVRP) without re-running discovery?
  5. Can the behavioral-gate framework generalize to other RL objective-design problems outside NCO, such as general preference optimization for LLMs?
