> **一句话总结：** 本文用一个博弈论/信息论框架证明：只要防护机制依赖的"证据"（请求内容、对话历史）可以被攻击者原样复制，那么无论安全策略多复杂（多轮追问、随机化、组合判断），在保住合法用户效用 q 的前提下，攻击者能获得的最小协助存在一个不可跨越的下界 Γ(q)>0（"有用能力、可靠安全、开放访问"三者不可兼得），唯一的出路是引入攻击者无法复制、且能预测真实下游用途的"可信凭证"信号。
>
> **TL;DR:** This is a theory paper, not a benchmarked-experiments paper: it formalizes LLM safeguarding as a capability-allocation game between a legitimate user and an adaptive attacker who can copy the same request/interaction evidence, and proves an exact "interactive-to-static reduction" (Theorem 1) showing that under copyable evidence any safeguard — however many clarifying questions or randomized decisions it uses — collapses to a fixed release-menu frontier Γ(q), yielding an impossibility trilemma (Corollary 1: useful capability, reliable safety, and credential-free open access cannot all hold at once). It then shows a trusted, non-copyable credential can move below this floor only if its separation d between legitimate/malicious signal distributions and residual copying error η satisfy β + d + η ≥ Γ(q) (Theorem 3/Eq. 22), grounding the theory in cited empirical findings such as adaptive jailbreaks succeeding above 90% against most of twelve recent LLM defenses (Nasr et al. 2025, "The Attacker Moves Second").

## 核心贡献

- **诊断"证据可复制"这一共性弱点**：意图识别、请求/响应过滤、多轮追问式安全检查，本质上都是在"下游用途尚未发生"之前，仅依据可被攻击者原样复制的公开信息（任务内容 W、交互历史 H）做决策；只要攻击者能复现合法用户的行为策略（Proposition 1），这些机制在最坏情形下就无法区分善意与恶意请求。
- **"有用、安全、开放"不可能三角（Corollary 1）**：在双重用途条件（凡是对合法用户有价值的输出，都至少存在一种恶意用途，公式 5）成立、且证据可复制的前提下，作者证明存在一个精确的最小攻击者协助下界 Γ(q)（公式 4），并证明"交互式安全机制精确退化为静态释放菜单问题"（定理 1）——多问几轮、加随机化、组合多次尝试都不能把协助压到 Γ(q) 以下；Γ(q) ≥ ρq > 0（公式 6），只要任务满足双重用途条件，零协助与正的合法效用不可兼得。
- **可信凭证是唯一出路，但有严格前提**：引入一个攻击者无法自由获取/复制、且其分布能预测真实下游用途的信号 S（如可验证角色、持续性历史、执行环境状态）后，新的协助下限变为 ΓS(q)；定理 3 给出必要条件 β + d + ηκ ≥ Γ(q)（公式 22），其中 d 是信号在合法/恶意场景下的总变分分离度、ηκ 是残余复制误差；推论 3 进一步证明"零协助"还要求合法效用集中在恶意进程完全无法触达的信号取值上（不仅仅是"预测力强"就够）。论文并将该下界推广到任务族加权和（3.6节）、不完美复制（δκ 度量）、以及多轮/多会话累积攻击（命题 2：下界在相关会话间精确可加）。

## Key Contributions

- **Naming the shared failure mode**: intent checks, filters, and multi-turn clarification all decide based on evidence (public context W, interaction history H) available *before* downstream use is observed; if an attacker can reproduce the legitimate user's request/response strategy exactly (Proposition 1: "copying the legitimate strategy"), the legitimate reference law becomes a member of the attacker's achievable transcript-law set, and no safeguard built only on that evidence can distinguish the two cases.
- **The "Useful, Safe, Open: Pick Two" trilemma (Corollary 1)**: under a dual-use condition (Eq. 5: every release with legitimate value has at least one malicious downstream use) and copyable evidence, the paper defines a minimum-attacker-assistance floor Γ(q) via a linear program over the release-outcome polytope (Eq. 4), proves an exact interactive-to-static reduction (Theorem 1: Rκ(q) = Γ(q) for any access policy κ), and concludes no mechanism can simultaneously guarantee legitimate-use utility ≥ q, worst-case attacker assistance ≤ β < Γ(q), and open (credential-free) access. Corollary 2 shows Γ(q) remains a universal floor even for implementation-restricted safeguard classes, and Eq. 6 shows the floor is strictly positive (Γ(q) ≥ ρq) whenever the dual-use condition holds with ratio ρ = min uM(a)/uB(a) over positive-value releases.
- **What breaks the floor — and what doesn't**: Section 3.5–3.7 formalizes a "trusted credential" as a signal S whose distribution differs across legitimate/malicious downstream use (separation d = TV(P^S_B, P^S_M), Eq. 13 gives its optimal balanced-accuracy value (1+d)/2) and that the attacker cannot freely reproduce. Theorem 2 gives an exact trusted-signal reduction under "conditional copying"; Theorem 3 gives the general necessary condition β + d + ηκ ≥ Γ(q) (Eq. 22), decomposing any safety claim into three separately-measurable terms (assistance ceiling, signal separation, residual copying error); Corollary 3 pins down the *exact* condition for reaching zero assistance (enough legitimate utility must live on signal values the malicious process literally cannot reach, not just "usually" avoids). Section 3.6 extends the floor additively across task families, imperfect per-turn copying, and repeated/correlated multi-session attacks (Proposition 2: the floor sums exactly across N sessions with no independence assumption).

## Methodology

The paper is a mathematical/game-theoretic analysis, not an empirical ML study. Its core modeling machinery:

1. **Setup**: fix a public task context w (task + claimed identity/role/use). The release menu A is a finite set of terminal outputs (including a zero-utility refusal a0). Two counterfactual "downstream-use worlds" are compared for the *same byte-identical release*: world B (legitimate use, utility function uB) and world M (a malicious agent picks the best of possibly several allowed misuses m ∈ Mw, utility uM(a) = sup_m vM,w,m(a)).
2. **Access-verification exchange**: before the terminal release A ~ g(·|h), a transcript H = (X1,Y1,...,XT,YT) is generated by a user strategy (legitimate kernel bt or malicious kernel m_t^σ) interacting with a committed verifier policy κt. The joint law factorizes (Eq. 2), and g represents the model plus every safeguard affecting the final output.
3. **Capability floor Γ(q)**: defined as a constrained minimization over output distributions μ ∈ Δ(A) — minimize expected malicious utility subject to expected legitimate utility ≥ q (Eq. 4). Because A is finite, Γ(q) is realized on the convex hull polytope of (uB(a), uM(a)) pairs and is nondecreasing, convex, piecewise linear.
4. **Copyability and the reduction**: evidence is "copyable" under policy κ if the legitimate reference transcript law P^κ_B lies in the closed convex hull Cκ of all malicious-strategy-induced transcript laws (Eq. 7–9). Proposition 1 shows literal strategy-copying is sufficient for this. Theorem 1 then proves that under copyability, the interactive worst-case attacker-assistance value Rκ(q) collapses exactly to the static Γ(q) — i.e., no amount of interactive questioning helps once the attacker can copy the answer distribution.
5. **Trusted credentials**: introduces an additional finite-valued signal S (checked at release, e.g., verified role/continuity/execution-environment state) whose marginal distributions differ between the legitimate and malicious downstream-use processes (separation d, Eq. 13's Acc*(S) = (1+d)/2 relates this to a Neyman–Pearson-style optimal classifier). A parallel floor ΓS(q) and a "conditional copying" law Qκ_copy formalize when access history adds no further worst-case value beyond S (Theorem 2), and Theorem 3 bounds the gap under imperfect copying with residual error ηκ.
6. **Scope**: all results condition on a fixed utility calibration and a specified/finite attacker strategy class; the framework explicitly excludes open-weight release settings (Section 5), restricting "open access" to credential-free access to a committed inference-time API/mechanism.

## Results & Highlights

No quantitative results reported — this is a theoretical/formal paper (definitions, propositions, theorems, corollaries with proofs) and reports no experiments, benchmarks, or datasets of its own. Its "results" are exact mathematical bounds and equalities: the capability floor Γ(q) (Eq. 4), the exact interactive-to-static reduction Rκ(q) = Γ(q) under copyable evidence (Theorem 1), the strict positivity Γ(q) ≥ ρq > 0 under the dual-use condition (Eq. 6), the impossibility trilemma (Corollary 1), the trusted-credential necessary condition β + d + ηκ ≥ Γ(q) (Theorem 3, Eq. 22), and the exact zero-assistance characterization via signal support (Corollary 3).

In place of new experiments, Section 4 ("Empirical Evidence") argues the theory's premises hold in practice by synthesizing findings from *other* papers:
- **Adaptive-attack success as evidence for copyability (metric: attack success rate; "benchmark": twelve recent LLM jailbreak/prompt-injection defenses; number: >90%)**: Nasr et al. 2025 ("The Attacker Moves Second") is cited as reporting success above 90% against most of twelve recent defenses when the attacker optimizes after observing the defense — used to argue that copying/adapting to a safeguard's decision boundary is realistic for current systems, not just a theoretical possibility.
- **CAPTCHA solving as evidence that interactive checks can be reproducible (metric: solve rate vs. human baseline; qualitative, no single number given)**: cited work (Searles et al. 2023; Plesner, Vontobel & Wattenhofer 2024) reports automated solvers passing deployed CAPTCHAs "at human-comparable rates," used as an example of interactive evidence that fails to separate legitimate from automated/malicious users.
- **Over-refusal / capability loss under blocking (qualitative, via XSTest, OR-Bench, and a "Defensive Refusal Bias" study of cyber-defense tasks)**: cited as showing that current safety interventions reduce excess assistance without moving the theoretical floor, consistent with the paper's prediction that blocking cannot itself achieve zero worst-case assistance while preserving positive legitimate utility (no new numeric result from this paper; it references prior benchmarks' documented over-refusal patterns).

Because the paper contributes no original benchmark table, dataset, or baseline comparison, the TL;DR/Results sections here foreground its proven bounds and its literature-sourced supporting evidence rather than fabricated empirical deltas.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The core move — separating "released capability" from "evidence about downstream use" and showing that under copyable evidence, *any* interactive safeguard (arbitrary adaptive questions, randomized decisions, or repeated attempts) reduces exactly to a static release-menu optimization — is a clean and, as far as the related-work section documents, new formalization. It sits alongside a growing 2025–2026 cluster of LLM-safety impossibility results (Glukhov et al.'s leakage-by-composition, Ball et al.'s computational-intractability-of-filtering, Bhatt et al.'s Defense Trilemma for prompt-injection wrappers) but claims a distinguishing feature: it places no continuity or computational restriction on the release rule and gives an *exact* equality (Theorem 1), not just a lower bound or hardness result.

**Rigor**: The proofs are short, explicit, and appear internally consistent (e.g., Proposition 1's copying argument is a one-line substitution into Eq. 2; Theorem 1's proof correctly uses the transcript-marginal averaging trick both directions). The framework is careful to state exactly what its results do *not* claim: Corollary 2 explicitly flags that Γ(q) is a floor for restricted implementations only when the restricted class contains a transcript-independent optimizer, and Section 5 is unusually explicit about scope limits (fixed utility calibration, finite operational resolution, specified attacker class, and — importantly — that "open access" here means credential-free access to a committed inference API, *not* open-weight model release, which the authors explicitly carve out as future/out-of-scope).

**Paper-specific weak point**: the entire empirical grounding in Section 4 is a literature synthesis, not new measurement — the paper never estimates its own key operational quantities (ρ, the dual-use value ratio; δκ, the copying-error distance; d, credential separation) on any concrete deployed system or dataset. The one hard number offered (>90% adaptive-attack success against most of twelve defenses, borrowed from Nasr et al. 2025) supports the *copyability* premise but is not accompanied by any attempt to instantiate Γ(q), ρ, or d for a real safeguard — so the paper's own claim that "these conditions are practically relevant" is argued qualitatively rather than quantified for any single deployed system. This is a real gap for a paper whose main selling point is that its bounds are "exact": exactness of the theorem does not establish that the practically relevant regime (β < Γ(q) with a large gap) actually holds for any named production safeguard.

**Reproducibility**: N/A in the conventional sense — there is no code, dataset, or experiment to reproduce; reproducibility here means verifying the proofs, which are stated in enough detail (explicit kernels, transcript factorizations, TV-distance bounds) to check by hand.

**Significance**: If accepted, the trilemma has real design implications (Section 3.7): safeguard designers who want zero or near-zero worst-case attacker assistance on genuinely dual-use tasks cannot get there by better prompting/filtering/interactive questioning alone — they need a non-copyable, downstream-use-predictive credential, and even then Corollary 3's "exact support condition" means predictive accuracy alone (however high) is not sufficient for zero assistance unless some credential values are *literally unreachable* by the malicious process. This is a stronger and more precise claim than the generic "access control helps" intuition in prior applied work (e.g., Wybitul 2025, cited in related work).

**Red flags**: The dual-use condition (Eq. 5) and its ratio ρ are assumed rather than measured for any concrete task; the paper's own examples (vulnerability-analysis release menus) are illustrative/hypothetical, not drawn from a real classifier's actual output distribution. Readers should treat the "practical relevance" claims in the abstract as directional (grounded in surveyed literature) rather than as a validated measurement of Γ(q) for any specific deployed LLM safeguard.

## Strengths

- Provides an exact equality (Theorem 1), not just an inequality or hardness result — arguably a sharper and more actionable characterization than several contemporaneous impossibility papers it explicitly compares against.
- Decomposes any deployment's safety claim into three separately measurable, falsifiable quantities (β, d, ηκ in Eq. 22), giving practitioners a concrete checklist rather than a purely qualitative warning.
- Unusually careful about scope: explicitly excludes open-weight release scenarios, states which restricted-implementation classes inherit the floor (Corollary 2), and separates "exact" results (copyable evidence) from "approximate" ones (imperfect copying, Eq. 12/21).
- Extends the core result along three practically relevant axes — task families with heterogeneous utilities, imperfect per-turn copying via TV-coupling, and repeated/correlated multi-session attacks (Proposition 2) — showing the floor composes additively and is not evaded by decomposing one attack across many sessions.
- Connects the theory to real deployed programs (OpenAI's "Trusted Access for Cyber," a Anthropic security-researcher verification program) as concrete instances of the "trusted credential" concept, giving the abstract framework a tangible deployment analogue.

## Limitations

- No original experiments, measurements, or case study instantiating Γ(q), ρ, δκ, or d for any real safeguard; all "empirical evidence" in Section 4 is secondhand synthesis of other papers' results.
- The dual-use condition (Eq. 5) and the utility functions uB, uM are exogenous modeling assumptions; the paper does not show how a practitioner would actually estimate these for a given task/model in production.
- The framework explicitly excludes open-weight model release and any setting where users can run/modify the model under their own control (Section 5) — a significant fraction of real-world dual-use risk discussion (e.g., open-weight LLM misuse) falls outside its scope.
- "Reliable safety" is defined purely in worst-case terms against a specified, finite attacker strategy class; average-case or probabilistic attacker models (which may be more realistic for many deployments) are not addressed.
- The trusted-credential solution itself is not evaluated for feasibility/cost — the paper does not discuss how expensive or how privacy-invasive it is to build a signal S with the needed separation d and support-disjointness properties in practice.

## Relevance

论文提出的"证据可复制导致安全性存在下界"的理论框架，对研究 LLM 安全防护（越狱防御、意图识别、访问控制）设计边界和不可能性结果的读者有直接参考价值，尤其适合与近期的防御不可能性文献（如 Bhatt et al. 的 Defense Trilemma、Ball et al. 的过滤计算难度结果）对照阅读。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can Γ(q), ρ, or δκ be estimated empirically for a specific deployed safeguard (e.g., a named jailbreak classifier) rather than left as an abstract quantity, to test whether the "practically relevant" claim in the abstract actually holds with a meaningful gap?
- How does the framework's exact worst-case floor relate to average-case or probabilistic-attacker robustness, which may be the more operative threat model for many real deployments?
- What is the actual cost/privacy tradeoff of building a trusted credential with sufficient separation d and the support-disjointness property required by Corollary 3 for zero assistance, in a concrete domain like cyber-vulnerability analysis?
- Does the additive composition result (Proposition 2) change if the attacker can also select which task context w to pursue across sessions (rather than the fixed per-session weighting used in Section 3.6), and how does this interact with adaptive attackers who target the cheapest-to-copy contexts first?
- How robust is the dual-use condition's ratio ρ to menu redesign in practice — i.e., can real deployments actually find intermediate releases (like the paper's "root-cause analysis without a working exploit chain" example) that meaningfully shrink Γ(q) without an accompanying credential system?
