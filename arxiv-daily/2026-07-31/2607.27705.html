Agentic Theorem Proving · Proof-State Verification · arXiv 2607.27705 · 30 Jul 2026

A versioned proof-state database — not a chat log — blocks Albilich's own researcher agents from certifying their own math.

一句话总结：Albilich 用一个持久化的 SQLite "证明状态"数据库（含 claim/route/inference 图、债务台账、补丁式受控更新）取代线性的 LLM 数学研究流程，配合角色分权验证（研究者无权自我认证）、CAS 与文献检索 MCP 工具，以及可按需介入的 PhD-advisor 角色；在 RealMath Math_arXiv 十题上全部达到内部 solved_final（9/10 经人工核对无误），并在 Kourovka Notebook 21.142 题上给出一个（内部认证的）反例，同时消融显示启用 CAS 可减少 32.0% 的 token 消耗（Problem 17.91，6.784M vs 9.982M tokens），启用 advisor 角色可在更少算力（6.81h vs 10.44h，即所需时间约为不启用时的 1/1.53）下把该问题从"未解决"变为"已解决"。

Why This Matters

It targets a real failure mode in agentic theorem proving — an AI grading its own homework — with a fully auditable record.

中文原文（Relevance）：该工作与 LLM 智能体做长程数学研究、多智能体状态管理和工具增强推理（CAS/文献检索 MCP 集成）密切相关，其角色分权验证与债务台账设计为构建可审计、可人工干预的自动化研究系统提供了具体可借鉴的架构范式。

▲ Critical Edge

The paper's two headline efficiency numbers — 32.0% fewer tokens with CAS, a 1.53× longer solve time without the advisor — each rest on a single paired run on a single problem, not a controlled study.

Methodology

1

Persistent proof-state graph — St = (q⋆, Gt, Dt, Lt, At, Pt, Ht, Mt) bundles the immutable root statement q⋆ with a graph of typed claims, routes, and inferences, a debt ledger for open gaps, a literature library, an artifact store, and a full transition history. Rejected and superseded objects stay in that event-sourced history rather than being deleted.

2

Patch-only, role-gated transitions — a session never edits the database directly; it returns a structured patch π=(ρ,b,X,Ω,η), applied atomically only if valid against the base revision. Four roles can propose but never certify; only three can change mathematical status:

  • Researcher — proposes, zero authority
  • Adversarial Researcher — proposes, zero authority
  • Literature Researcher — proposes, zero authority
  • PhD Advisor — plans only, zero authority
  • Strict Verifier — Verify: certifies one claim
  • Integration Verifier — Integrate: certifies a route
  • Counterexample Validator — Refute: certifies a disproof
3

Local verification ≠ root integration — a Strict Verifier can mark a single claim or inference informally_verified, but only the Integration Verifier can mark a full route integrated, requiring every premise verified, no blocking debt, and an explicit exact/equivalent/stronger relation to q⋆. Reconciliation automatically reopens an integrated route if a dependency later changes.

4

Scheduler + on-demand advisor — a deterministic, priority-based scheduler draws research and verification budgets from separate pools so speculative search can't starve verification. The PhD Advisor is invoked only after stalls (repeated rejections, stuck debts) to reprioritize or reduce scope — never to verify, integrate, or refute.

MCP tools: CAS access (SageMath, GAP, Macaulay2, Singular) is trusted from its recorded transcript, not re-executed by the verifier; literature retrieval (TheoremSearch, Matlas) is an unverified lead until a Literature Researcher records the primary-source statement. All experiments run on one undocumented model, gpt-5.6-sol (xhigh reasoning), comparing matched configurations that differ only in the single ablated factor.

Advisor-off took 1.53× the active-compute time and 1.85× the raw tokens, and produced more verified local claims (18 vs. 10) — yet still never integrated a route that concluded the root. Single paired run (n=1) per condition.

  • Kourovka 21.142 counterexample: the accepted route certifies that, for some m≥9 (depending on primes p, q), the alternating group Am cannot embed in any finite group invariably generated by elements of orders p and q — 80 sessions, 5h51m lifecycle, 6.81h active compute, 29.68M gross tokens, 10 of 11 claims verified.
  • Kourovka 20.2 witness: both advisor settings certify the same explicit witness G = PSL2(7) for whether a nonabelian simple group of Lie type can be totally 3-closed — advisor-on reaches it via a conceptual argument (23 sessions, 1h16m, 6.71M tokens); advisor-off needs an exhaustive GAP computational certificate (18 sessions, 1h47m, 6.31M tokens). Same answer, different proof character.
  • Follow-on classification: later Albilich-assisted runs extend 20.2 toward an almost-complete PSLn(q) classification — PSL2(q), PSL3(q), and PSL4(q) resolved, leaving only PSLn(2) for n≥5 open.
  • Anecdotal trials (explicitly excluded from benchmark claims): 2 of 3 master's/early-PhD group-theory problems solved, including repairing a gap in an associated paper; a codegree-set construction problem and an open character-vertex problem were not solved; paper-audit trials correctly flagged a known flaw in a published argument.

Strengths

  • Clear separation of proposal, verification, and integration authority directly targets a known failure mode in agentic theorem proving: an agent certifying its own argument.
  • Explicit, inspectable debt ledger and event-sourced history — including rejected and superseded objects — give real auditability that many competing systems lack, per the paper's own comparison table.
  • Honest hedging throughout: results are repeatedly called "preliminary," anecdotal trials are excluded from benchmark claims, and CAS/literature trust boundaries are stated plainly rather than glossed over.
  • Concrete, named case studies (Kourovka 21.142, 20.2) with detailed compute/token/session accounting, not only aggregate benchmark scores.

Limitations

  • Both headline ablations (CAS token savings, advisor effect) are single paired runs on single problems, with no repeated trials to quantify the paper's own acknowledged run-to-run stochasticity.
  • No head-to-head comparison against the five explicitly-cited competing systems — Danus, QED, ProofCouncil, Aletheia, AI Co-Mathematician — on shared benchmarks.
  • CAS-backed claims are only as trustworthy as an unexecuted transcript: the verifier never independently reruns the computation.
  • Internal verification (informally_verified, solved_final) is explicitly distinct from formal, Lean-checked verification, which the paper defers entirely to future work.
  • RealMath is a 10-problem convenience sample not meant to estimate full-benchmark performance, and Problem 08's correctness remains unresolved even by the paper's own review.

Authors & Credibility

Ting Gong and Michael Ruofan Zeng (University of Washington, UW Math AI Lab) with Yong Yang (Texas State University). The paper states results were internally certified by Albilich's own verifier roles and separately reviewed by the authors — but the body text names no independent group-theory referees and describes no external verification protocol for the 21.142 counterexample or the 20.2 witness. The strongest evidence offered is the system's own internal audit trail (proof dossiers, GAP certificates, debt ledgers), not third-party confirmation. No repository or artifact link appears in the text, and all experiments run on gpt-5.6-sol, a model version not otherwise documented or externally available.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Would the 32.0% CAS token reduction and the advisor's solve-rate improvement replicate across multiple problems and multiple stochastic runs per condition, rather than one paired run each?
  2. How would Albilich's RealMath and Kourovka results compare directly against Danus, QED, ProofCouncil, or Aletheia run on the identical problems under matched compute budgets?
  3. What would change if the Strict Verifier were required to independently re-execute CAS computations rather than trusting the recorded transcript — how often would that catch errors?
  4. Has the Kourovka 21.142 counterexample and the PSLn(q) classification undergone, or will they undergo, independent review by group theorists outside the author team, with proof artifacts released for scrutiny?
  5. How sensitive is the debt-ledger/reconciliation mechanism to adversarial or buggy patches that pass schema validation but encode subtly incorrect mathematics — does the strict verifier catch these reliably?
