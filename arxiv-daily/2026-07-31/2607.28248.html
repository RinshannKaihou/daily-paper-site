Critical edge

00 The Two-Axis Taxonomy

The survey's organizing device: every method is placed by how it generates a predictive ensemble (three mechanisms below), independent of how much of the network is involved (full network / shared-backbone-last-layer / single deterministic network). Methods that combine full-network scope with explicit members achieve the strongest between-mode ("multi-basin") diversity; methods confined to a single loss-surface basin — BatchEnsemble, MC Dropout — show weaker diversity and OOD detection.

Explicit members

Independently trained / parameterized predictors — genuinely separate models.

  • Deep ensembles
  • Anchored ensembles
  • Credal Deep Ensembles (CreDE)

Posterior sampling

Monte Carlo draws at inference time from an approximate posterior.

  • MC Dropout (MCD)
  • SWAG / MultiSWAG
  • BNN via VI / MCMC

Closed-form predictive

A single deterministic forward pass yields an analytic predictive distribution.

  • SNGP
  • DUQ / DDU / DUE
  • VBLL
crossed with network scope: full network shared-backbone / last-layer single deterministic network

Five core families — Bayesian NNs, MC Dropout, deep ensembles, efficient ensemble approximations, last-layer/single-pass methods — sit inside this 3×3 grid (Table 2, Figure 1); the taxonomy timeline runs from MacKay (1992) and Neal (1995) to VBLL (2024).

01 Methodology

This is a survey, not an empirical study — no new experiments, models, or datasets. Its method is structured literature synthesis in five moves:

Taxonomy construction — ~25 years of UQ methods (Table 1) sorted onto the mechanism × scope grid above (Table 2).

Per-family critical review (Sections 3–10) — theoretical motivation, implementation, reported empirical performance, and known limitations for each family, plus evidential/prior networks, conformal prediction, and post-hoc calibration as "adjacent" tools.

Evaluation consolidation (Section 11) — fixes the metrics (ECE, Brier score, NLL for calibration; AUROC/AUPR vs. MSP for OOD; risk-coverage curves for selective prediction) and benchmark suites (OpenOOD, Uncertainty Baselines, CIFAR/ImageNet-C, Ovadia et al. 2019) before any rating is assigned.

Comparative synthesis (Table 3/4) — every method scored on training/inference cost and Low/Medium/High OOD-detection and calibration quality, each traced to a cited "Evidence" source; ratings without direct benchmark evidence carry a dagger (†) as "conservative inferences from method structure."

Diversity & measure theory, LLM extension (Sections 13–16) — the bias-variance-diversity decomposition of ensemble loss (Wood et al., 2023), the entropy/mutual-information uncertainty split and its pairwise-divergence alternatives, then a shorter pass over LLM uncertainty flagged as an "emerging adjacent field."

The BatchEnsemble reversal

Proposed by Wen et al. (2020) as a near-single-model-cost ensemble alternative, BatchEnsemble was later shown by Zamyatin et al. (2026) to produce "near-identical" members in function space — behaving like a single model, with correspondingly poor OOD detection. The survey flags this as a concrete case of claimed efficiency gains turning out illusory.

The authors' own variance-gated measures (VGE/VGMU/VGN) cut this to O(MC) — a stated, quantifiable complexity reduction (M = ensemble members, C = classes) — but the paper itself concedes this alternative "has not yet been benchmarked against MI and pairwise measures" under distribution shift.

+ Strengths

  • Clean two-axis taxonomy that explains why full-network + explicit-member methods reach the strongest between-mode diversity, while BatchEnsemble and MC Dropout stay structurally confined to a single basin.
  • Actionable structural comparison of MI vs. EPKL vs. EPJS on symmetry, boundedness, and sensitivity.
  • Honest self-flagging: every L/M/H rating traces to a cited source, with a dagger convention marking inferred (not directly benchmarked) placements.
  • Strong coverage of the efficient-approximation and last-layer/single-pass literature (BatchEnsemble, SWAG/MultiSWAG, SNGP, DUQ/DDU/DUE, VBLL), backed by a very recent (2026) critique of BatchEnsemble's actual diversity.
  • Clear open-questions list (Section 17.2): last-layer diversity limits, efficient non-entropy measures, diversity-calibration interaction under shift, LLM confidence calibration.

− Limitations

  • Pure literature synthesis, no new experiments; the L/M/H comparison tables are explicitly called "indicative placements," not directly comparable measurements across heterogeneous studies.
  • Scope deliberately narrow to ensemble-based / approximate-Bayesian classification; regression, evidential, conformal, and post-hoc calibration methods get only "adjacent" treatment.
  • The LLM uncertainty section is admittedly brief ("deliberately uneven") relative to its own claim that it is the field's most consequential open frontier — about one page versus ~15 for classification.
  • The paper's own proposed EPJS measure and the authors' variance-gated measures are presented without independent empirical validation against MI/EPKL under distribution shift.
  • Currency risk: draws on fast-moving 2026 literature (e.g., a EurIPS workshop paper on BatchEnsemble diversity) that may already be superseded.

? Questions for Further Exploration

  • Can bounded EPJS be empirically validated against MI and EPKL on standard OOD/shift suites, and would it change practitioner recommendations?
  • Does last-layer diversity alone (epinet, repulsive last-layer ensembles) recover most of the benefit of full deep ensembles, or is full-network between-mode diversity irreplaceable (Kirsch 2025's "epistemic collapse" critique)?
  • How much of the survey's qualitative L/M/H differences would survive a controlled, matched-compute re-benchmarking on one standardized suite (e.g., OpenOOD)?
  • Can efficient, non-entropy-based epistemic measures (the authors' own variance-gated approach) preserve calibration and OOD detection under covariate shift?
  • Given the BatchEnsemble diversity-collapse finding, are similar audits warranted for Masksembles, Layer Ensembles, and Sub-Ensembles?
  • How should the method-measure separation framework adapt to generative/LLM settings, where token-level probability conflates linguistic variation with genuine semantic uncertainty?
