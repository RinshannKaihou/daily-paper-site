Demystifying DRAM Read Disturbance: Bridging the Gap Between Experimental Characterization and Device-Level Modeling of RowHammer and RowPress Phenomena
30 Jul 2026 · arXiv:2607.28233
RowHammer RowPress DRAM TCAD Simulation Hardware Security

Under double-sided hammering, real DRAM chips flip bits in both directions — something the state-of-the-art device model says can't happen.

Why this matters

Corrects the physical assumptions behind RowHammer/RowPress simulators, so future bitflip-injection frameworks and mitigation designs get built on trap physics that actually matches real chips, not textbook shortcuts.

Critical edge

⚠The authors' own headline explanation — why 1-to-0 flips outnumber 0-to-1 flips overall — rests on a "plausible qualitative explanation" borrowed from unrelated process literature, not a direct measurement.

Methodology

Two evidence tracks, deliberately confirmatory rather than exploratory. First, real-chip characterization: DRAM Bender drives 9 off-the-shelf DDR4 UDIMMs from 3 manufacturers (8Gb/16Gb dies), reverse-engineering the true NWL/PWL wiring of 128 sampled victim rows per module, then hammering or holding aggressor rows open (single- and double-sided, sweeping temperature up to 80°C) to log bitflip direction, spatial position, and AC_min.

Second, device-physics modeling: a 1z-nm saddle-fin RCAT DRAM cell built in Sentaurus TCAD, calibrated against a manufacturer's ISN–VWL data. Two leakage mechanisms are modeled — trap-assisted electron migration (traps placed at 6 candidate positions) and capacitive crosstalk between wordlines — and mixed-mode transient simulation tracks the victim storage node until it crosses a 0.1V failure threshold. A third, independent check — direct electrical measurement on fabricated sub-20nm test structures — settles the one claim TCAD calibration can't be trusted to settle alone.

Results

Trap location, not a single dominant "enhance one direction, suppress the other" mechanism, decides which way a cell flips under double-sided hammering — and it does so on a per-cell basis, which is why real chips show both directions with almost no spatial overlap.

Separately, a capacitive-crosstalk simulation shows the victim wordline's noise voltage (−0.2V → +0.124V at 12% coupling) sharply boosts 0-to-1 leakage but barely touches 1-to-0 leakage, because the access transistor's effective threshold is lower in the 0-to-1 condition.

Strengths

  • Triangulates every claim across three independent evidence sources — real-chip DRAM Bender data, calibrated TCAD, and direct wafer-level transistor measurement.
  • Labels each mismatch explicitly as "Unmatching Device-Level Mechanism 1–3," making the contribution auditable rather than a vague "improved understanding."
  • Reverse-engineered NWL/PWL mapping is cross-checked against observed flip direction — a clean, falsifiable validation step.
  • Discloses its own outlier (99.7% instead of 100% on one module) with a specific attributed cause instead of omitting it.

Limitations

  • The NWL-vs-PWL trap-density asymmetry that explains the flip-direction imbalance is inferred, not directly measured — an open question by the authors' own admission.
  • TCAD calibration relies on I-V data from a single, unnamed DRAM manufacturer; generality across vendors/nodes beyond this 1z-nm-class device is unverified.
  • Purely explanatory: proposes no new mitigation and doesn't build the system-level disturbance model it motivates as future work.
  • Single-trap sensitivity sweeps have inherently limited spatial influence; the uniform-density extension is used in only one subsection.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Can the inferred NWL-vs-PWL trap-density asymmetry be directly measured rather than left as a qualitative inference?
  • Do trap location, DHT,bulk, and crosstalk generalize beyond this 1z-nm-class device to LPDDR5/DDR5 and sub-10nm nodes?
  • Would folding these corrected mechanisms into HammerSim/ScaleDisturb-style injection frameworks overturn prior mitigation evaluations built on the old device models?
  • Does the trap-location-dependent flip mechanism carry implications for the emerging, uncharacterized ColumnDisturb phenomenon?
  • Can the wafer-level test-structure method be extended to separate trap-assisted migration from crosstalk directly, without relying on TCAD decomposition?
