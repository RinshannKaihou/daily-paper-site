> **一句话总结：** MECA用多智能体框架把"猜想陈述"和"支撑机制（不等式/不变量/分解/归约等证明论证）"作为一对耦合对象共同精化，在20篇论文的盲测复原任务上把综合评分从基线的48.0分提升到69.0分（+21.0），并用该流程构造的100个猜想中有46%被独立的自动证明系统QED给出可验证的证明或反驳。

> **TL;DR:** MECA reframes automated conjecture construction as the joint refinement of a candidate statement and a directed graph of audited "mechanisms" (reusable proof/refutation arguments), using explorer/critic/judge/challenger agents to turn broad research directions into bounded, prover-facing conjectures with an explicit unresolved core. On a 20-paper article-blind target-recovery benchmark, MECA beats a Draft-Audit-Revise baseline by +21.0 points on a 100-point rubric (48.0 → 69.0), with the largest gains in recovering the target's Conclusion (+6.0) and Quantitative Accuracy (+5.5). On 100 newly constructed open-ended conjectures fed statement-only into the QED multi-agent prover (An et al. 2026), 35% are proved, 11% refuted, and 54% remain unresolved — of the unresolved cases, 81.5% are attributed to genuine technical proof difficulty rather than a defect in the conjecture itself.

## 核心贡献

- 将"构造猜想"形式化为候选陈述 p 与其"机制图" G（记录假设、结论与未解缺口的有向无环图）的联合精化问题，而非先写猜想再补证明草图。
- 提出 MECA：三阶段多智能体流水线——(1) 源材料驱动的种子构造（文献路线 + 开放问题改写路线）；(2) 机制-问题协同探索（Explorer 提出/修订机制，Critic 独立审核有效性，Coverage Judge 判定支持程度，盲态 Final Challenger 做反确认偏差检验）；(3) 确定性投影为自包含的、可直接喂给证明器的问题陈述。
- 贡献了100个"证明器可用"的开放猜想（92个来自文献、8个来自开放问题改写），并系统分析了自动证明系统 QED 在这些猜想上失败的原因分布，暴露了当前自动证明/反驳系统的能力边界。

## Key Contributions

- **Joint refinement formulation:** Conjecture construction is cast as coupled evolution of a candidate statement p_t and a mechanism graph G_t under a frozen "target anchor" A (central object, admissible assumptions, source boundary, forbidden trivializations) — mechanisms are validity-audited conditional steps `m = (Req(m), Prov(m), Gap(m))`, not just proof-technique labels.
- **MECA framework:** A four-role, four-layer-prompt multi-agent system (Seed Generator/Validator, Mechanism Explorer, Mechanism Critic, Coverage Judge, Final Challenger) with a strict information-boundary design — e.g., the Final Challenger sees only the candidate, anchor, seed, and source pack, never the mechanism graph or critic history, specifically to reduce confirmation bias.
- **100 prover-facing open problems:** A new benchmark of semi-open conjectures spanning mostly optimization-adjacent domains (decentralized tracking, derivative-free optimization, Bregman proximal methods, operator splitting, stochastic finite-sum, HJB safety, low-rank recovery, etc.), released with explicit assumptions, quantitative regimes, and audited unresolved cores.
- **Diagnostic error taxonomy:** For every conjecture QED cannot resolve, MECA assigns one of four causes (technical proof difficulty, missing assumptions, insufficient mathematical foothold, ambiguous formulation), enabling a quantitative read on how much of "prover failure" is a conjecture-quality defect versus genuine mathematical hardness.

## Methodology

MECA operates in three stages built around the central "mechanism" abstraction:

1. **Source-grounded seed construction.** Route A (literature-driven) consolidates reviewed source papers into a frozen "source pack" and generates Raw Seeds via a Seed Generator, each recording the closest known result, a bounded mathematical delta beyond it, plausible counterexample regimes, and an anticipated bottleneck. Route B (open-problem adaptation) extracts seeds from manually curated open-problem descriptions. A Seed Validator rejects restatements, direct corollaries, and superficial variations, checking both "research value" and "mathematical validity" (definitions, quantifiers, boundary cases).

2. **Mechanism–problem co-exploration.** For each validated seed, MECA freezes a target anchor A = (o, P_A, B_A, F_A) (central object, admissible assumptions, source boundary, forbidden trivializations) that subsequent revisions cannot violate. A Mechanism Explorer iteratively proposes/revises/splits the candidate statement p_t while a Mechanism Critic independently audits each proposed mechanism for correctness, non-circularity, and typed requirement/consequence interfaces before it is admitted into the mechanism graph G_t (an edge m_i → m_j meaning Prov(m_i) supplies a requirement of m_j). A Coverage Judge composes accepted mechanisms in dependency order and returns one of {InsufficientSupport, SubstantivePartial, FullCoverage, ...} — SubstantivePartial (meaningful nonroutine progress with 1–3 precise unresolved cores) is the intended stopping state passed to a Final Challenger, which — deliberately denied the mechanism graph, critic receipts, or exploration trace — independently attempts a direct proof/counterexample and audits completeness, consistency, and nontriviality to counter confirmation bias.

3. **Prover-facing problem construction.** Accepted (anchor, candidate, mechanism-subgraph) triples are deterministically projected q = Θ(A, p, G') into a self-contained statement that preserves assumptions/quantifiers/scope/targets but converts each remaining gap into an explicit proof obligation, then passes two statement-only probes (a Blind Prover and a Refute Prover) before global deduplication by mathematical content.

**Evaluation design.** (a) *Target-conditioned article-blind recovery*: 20 post-2023 target papers are each paired with 7 anonymized pre-2024 "source cards" (evidence with titles/authors/identifiers stripped); MECA and a Draft-Audit-Revise baseline (same task, same 3-output cap, 5 draft/audit/revise steps) each try to reconstruct the target's conclusion from the source cards alone, scored blind by an evaluator against a hidden target record on a 100-point rubric (Setting 20, Assumptions 15, Conclusion 30, Quantitative 20, Boundary 15). (b) *Open-ended generation and verification*: 100 final conjectures (92 literature-route, 8 open-problem-route) are each submitted statement-only, with no mechanism/critic context, to QED (An et al. 2026), an existing open-source multi-agent theorem-proving system, under a 5-hour wall-clock budget per conjecture, and classified as proved/refuted/unresolved. Models: gpt-5.5 at "xhigh" reasoning effort for MECA/baseline generation AND for the target-alignment evaluator; gpt-5.6-sol at "xhigh" for the QED prover/verifier.

## Results & Highlights

- **Target-alignment recovery (Table 1):** MECA's average score across the 20 article-blind targets is **69.0/100** vs. the Draft-Audit-Revise baseline's **48.0/100**, a **+21.0-point** improvement. Per-dimension gains: Setting +2.5 (18.0 vs 15.5), Assumptions +3.5 (11.5 vs 8.0), **Conclusion +6.0 (21.0 vs 15.0)**, **Quantitative Accuracy +5.5 (12.5 vs 7.0)**, Boundary +3.5 (6.0 vs 2.5). MECA outperforms the baseline on every single scored dimension.
- **QED prover outcomes on 100 open-ended conjectures (Figure 4):** **35 proved (35%)**, **11 refuted (11%)**, **54 unresolved (54%)** — i.e., 46% received an independently verified positive or negative resolution within the 5-hour budget.
- **Failure attribution for the 54 unresolved conjectures (Figure 5):** 44 cases (**81.5%**) attributed to genuine *technical proof difficulty* (well-posed statement, adequate assumptions, plausible route identified, but the analysis exceeds QED's search capability/time budget); 6 cases (11.1%) missing assumptions; 2 cases (3.7%) insufficient mathematical foothold; 2 cases (3.7%) ambiguous formulation. The paper concludes only **10% of all 100 generated conjectures** exhibit an identifiable formulation or proof-readiness defect, with the remaining 90% either independently resolved or well-posed and actionable despite exceeding the prover's budget.
- **Domain spread (Figure 3):** the 100 conjectures skew heavily toward optimization and numerical analysis (Optimization 35% including decentralized tracking 12%, derivative-free optimization 10%, stochastic finite-sum 4%; Variational operators 14% including Bregman proximal 10% and operator splitting; plus HJB safety, low-rank recovery, graph control dynamics, error bounds, etc.) — consistent with the authors' optimization background rather than broad mathematics coverage.
- Qualitative case studies (Appendix F.2–F.3) show one candidate evolving via a "split" action into two sibling conjectures under counterexample pressure (retaining an unresolved "technical difficulty" core), and a second case where a first proof attempt failed a numerical audit, was repaired, and QED then delivered a fully verified refutation — illustrating that "unresolved" and "refuted" outcomes both count as legitimate, checkable downstream adjudications in this framework.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The "mechanism graph" abstraction — representing a proof/refutation argument as `(Req, Prov, Gap)` nodes that compose via typed interfaces, independently audited before entering a DAG — is a genuinely distinctive contribution relative to prior LLM-conjecturing work (e.g., LeanConjecturer, Lemmanaid, TxGraffiti, Ramanujan Machine) that either searches a predefined class of formal objects or generates a flat statement without coupling it to an explicit, checkable partial-support structure. The idea of a role that is deliberately withheld the mechanism graph (Final Challenger) to counteract confirmation bias is a nice, concrete design choice.
- **Rigor concern — evaluator/generator overlap:** Table 5 (Appendix D.2) shows that **both MECA/baseline generation and the target-alignment evaluator use the same model configuration (gpt-5.5, xhigh effort)**. This is a real methodological weak point: an LLM (or the same model family) scoring outputs generated by essentially the same model family raises self-preference / shared-bias risk that the paper does not discuss or control for (e.g., no human-expert cross-check of the +21.0-point gap is reported).
- **Baseline weakness:** The only comparison system is a self-constructed "Draft-Audit-Revise" baseline matched on output caps and information boundary — reasonable as a controlled ablation of MECA's *process* (holding model, task, and evidence fixed), but the paper never benchmarks against any of the many systems it cites in related work (LeanConjecturer, Lemmanaid, COMPOSE, Moonshine, ProofCouncil, MechMath Agent Team, ResearchAgent), so it is unclear how MECA compares to the actual state of the art in automated conjecturing, only to an in-house ablation.
- **Missing ablation:** the intro/abstract frames "mechanism bundle" as central to reproducing precise unresolved cores, but the paper does not report a controlled comparison of prover success rates *with* vs. *without* exposing the mechanism bundle to QED — QED only ever receives the bare statement, so the value-add of mechanisms is demonstrated indirectly (via the recovery benchmark) rather than directly on the downstream proving task.
- **Data/domain quirk:** the 100-conjecture benchmark is drawn from "a private repository of mathematical papers" and "a privately curated collection of mathematical open problems" (Section 4.2) — neither is released as a public corpus in what's described, and the resulting domain distribution (35% optimization, 14% variational operators) is heavily tilted toward the authors' own specialty rather than a representative sample of mathematics, which limits how much the 35%/11%/54% QED outcome generalizes to other fields (e.g., number theory, combinatorics, algebraic geometry).
- **Reproducibility:** Unusually well-documented at the *process* level (extensive appendix detailing the four-layer prompt architecture, skill library, receipt/versioning semantics, per-case action ceilings in Table 6) — this is a strength for understanding exactly what the system does. However, no code or dataset release URL is given in the PDF, and the specific "private repository" source materials are not shared, so independent reproduction of the actual numeric results is not currently possible from the paper alone.
- **Significance:** The core empirical claim — that a large majority (90%) of MECA-produced conjectures are not undermined by formulation/readiness defects, and that most "unresolved" outcomes reflect genuine mathematical difficulty rather than a broken conjecture — is a meaningful signal for AI-assisted mathematical discovery, but it rests on QED (itself an LLM-based system, An et al. 2026) as the sole arbiter of "difficulty," so the 81.5% "technical proof difficulty" figure is bounded by QED's specific proving capability/budget rather than an absolute measure of mathematical hardness.

## Strengths

- Principled, falsifiable formalization of "mechanism" with explicit Req/Prov/Gap semantics and typed composition, rather than an ad hoc prompt for "generate a conjecture with a proof sketch."
- Strong information-boundary discipline throughout the pipeline (frozen anchors, immutable target boundaries, blind Final Challenger, statement-only QED submission) that is unusually careful about avoiding leakage/confirmation bias for an LLM-agent paper.
- Directly measures both process quality (article-blind target recovery against a rubric) and downstream utility (independent prover verification), rather than relying on a single self-reported metric.
- Transparent, granular failure-mode taxonomy for unresolved cases rather than lumping everything into "prover timed out."

## Limitations

- Same-family model used for generation and evaluation, undermining independence of the +21.0-point recovery-benchmark result.
- No comparison against any published competing conjecture-generation system, only an internal baseline.
- No direct ablation isolating the causal contribution of the mechanism bundle to downstream prover success (QED never sees it).
- Domains of the 100-conjecture benchmark are heavily skewed toward optimization/numerical analysis, limiting generalizability claims.
- Source materials (private paper repository, private open-problem collection) are not released, limiting reproducibility.
- Recovery benchmark size (20 targets) and conjecture set (100, only 8 from the open-problem route) are modest; confidence intervals/variance across the "three independent evaluator replicas" (majority vote) are not reported numerically beyond the single averaged score.

## Relevance

该工作与AI辅助数学研究、多智能体LLM系统设计密切相关，其"机制图"式的结构化中间表示（要求-结论-缺口）思路，对设计需要显式追踪"部分证据+未解缺口"的智能体系统（不限于数学）有参考价值；其严格的信息边界与反确认偏差设计（盲态挑战者）也是多智能体评审/审核流程可借鉴的通用范式。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How would MECA's 35%/11%/54% QED outcome distribution change if the mechanism bundle (not just the bare statement) were exposed to the prover — would exposing Req/Prov/Gap chains substantially raise the proved/refuted rate, or does QED's search strategy not benefit from that structure?
- How does MECA compare quantitatively (not just qualitatively via citation) against other recent conjecture-generation systems like LeanConjecturer, Lemmanaid, or COMPOSE on the same article-blind recovery benchmark?
- Would using a different evaluator model family (not gpt-5.5, the same family used for generation) change the target-alignment score gap between MECA and the baseline?
- How well does the mechanism-centered approach transfer to non-optimization domains (e.g., number theory, combinatorics, algebraic geometry) where "mechanisms" like inequalities/invariants may take very different structural forms?
- What is the human-expert agreement rate with MECA's Coverage Judge and Final Challenger decisions (SubstantivePartial vs. FullCoverage vs. Reject), which currently rely entirely on LLM self-audit?
