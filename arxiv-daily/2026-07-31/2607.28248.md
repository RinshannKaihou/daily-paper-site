> **一句话总结：** 这是一篇聚焦"集成方法+近似贝叶斯方法"的深度学习不确定性量化综述，核心贡献是把"产生预测集合的方法"与"总结不确定性的度量"严格解耦成两条独立坐标轴，并用统一的定性评分表（Table 3/4，锚定在 MSP 基线和深度集成两个参照点上，基准为 CIFAR-10 vs SVHN/CIFAR-100、ImageNet vs iNaturalist/Places 以及 Ovadia et al. 2019 的分布偏移套件）系统比较了 20 余种方法。

> **TL;DR:** A structured critical survey (not a new empirical study) of ensemble-based and approximate-Bayesian uncertainty quantification for deep classifiers, organized along two orthogonal axes — generative mechanism (explicit members / posterior sampling / closed-form predictive) and network scope (full network / shared-backbone-last-layer / single deterministic network). It consolidates method quality into qualitative Low/Medium/High OOD-detection and calibration ratings anchored against the Maximum Softmax Probability (MSP) baseline (Hendrycks and Gimpel, 2017) and deep ensembles as the gold standard, evaluated on the standard OOD pairs (CIFAR-10 vs. SVHN/CIFAR-100, ImageNet vs. iNaturalist/Places) and the Ovadia et al. (2019) shift benchmark; it also unifies pairwise-divergence uncertainty measures (EPCE/EPKL/EPJS) against the standard entropy/mutual-information decomposition and gives a brief treatment of uncertainty in LLMs (semantic entropy, verbalized confidence, conformal generation).

## 核心贡献

- 提出方法论的双轴分类体系：一个轴是"生成机制"（显式集成成员 / 后验采样 / 闭式预测分布），另一个轴是"网络作用范围"（全网络 / 共享主干+末层 / 单一确定性网络），把五大方法族（贝叶斯神经网络、MC Dropout、深度集成、高效集成近似、末层/单次前向方法）统一放入同一坐标系（Table 2、Figure 1）。
- 明确将"产生预测集合的方法"与"汇总不确定性的度量"解耦——同一组成员预测可以搭配熵/互信息分解、EPCE/EPKL 期望成对度量，或新提出的方差门控（variance-gated）度量，这一分离贯穿全文，是与既有综述（Gawlikowski et al. 2023、Abdar et al. 2021 等按架构分类）的主要差异点。
- 系统比较三种认知不确定性的成对散度度量——互信息 MI（有界但在多类别下可能低估认知不确定性，Wimmer et al. 2023）、EPKL（无界、对类别间近零概率高度敏感）、EPJS（对称、有界于[0,1]、数值更稳定但灵敏度较低）——并给出结构性属性对照表（Table 6）。
- 引入集成多样性的偏差-方差-多样性精确分解（Wood et al. 2023）及多样性度量（CKA、Vendi score、预测余弦相似度、Cohen's κ），解释为何深度集成/CreDE/MultiSWAG 等探索多个损失面模式的方法不确定性质量更高，而 BatchEnsemble、MC Dropout 等被限制在单一模式内的方法多样性和 OOD 检测能力较弱。
- 对证据/先验网络、保形预测（conformal prediction）、事后校准三类"邻近"方法做了归纳，并将 OOD 检测与选择性预测列为下游决策任务，最后用较短篇幅将该框架推广到大语言模型的不确定性（语义熵、口头表达置信度、保形生成），并指出方法-度量分离框架在生成式场景下只能部分迁移。

## Key Contributions

- A two-axis taxonomy (generative mechanism × network scope) that places five core UQ method families — Bayesian neural networks, Monte Carlo Dropout, deep ensembles, efficient ensemble approximations, and last-layer/single-pass approaches — into one coordinate system (Table 2, Figure 1), extending Table 1's chronological timeline back to MacKay (1992) and Neal (1995).
- An explicit separation of the method that produces a predictive ensemble from the measure that summarizes its uncertainty; the paper argues this interchangeability is the field's under-examined structural fact and organizes the entire survey (and its comparison tables) around it.
- A structural comparison of the three pairwise/mixture epistemic-uncertainty estimators — mutual information (MI), Expected Pairwise KL divergence (EPKL, Schweighofer et al. 2023a), and Expected Pairwise Jensen-Shannon divergence (EPJS) — on symmetry, boundedness, and sensitivity (Table 6), plus the authors' own prior work on variance-gated measures (VGE/VGMU/VGN, Gillis et al. 2025, 2026b) as an O(MC) alternative to the O(M²C) pairwise measures.
- A formal bias-variance-diversity decomposition of ensemble cross-entropy loss (Wood et al., 2023) linking functional diversity metrics (CKA, Vendi score, disagreement, Cohen's κ) directly to observed uncertainty-quality differences across method families.
- A consolidated evaluation section (Section 11) that fixes the evidentiary basis (ECE, Brier score, NLL for calibration; AUROC/AUPR against the MSP baseline for OOD; risk-coverage curves for selective prediction; standardized suites OpenOOD and Uncertainty Baselines) before the qualitative comparison tables (Table 3, Table 4) are presented, plus a compact extension to LLM uncertainty (Table 10) covering token-likelihood, semantic entropy, sampled consistency, verbalized confidence, ensemble/Bayesian, and conformal/selective families.

## Methodology

This is a **survey/critical review paper**, not an empirical study — it reports no new experiments, models, or datasets of its own. Its "methodology" is a structured literature synthesis:

1. **Taxonomy construction.** The authors organize ~25 years of UQ methods (Table 1, MacKay 1992 to VBLL 2024) along two axes: (a) *generative mechanism* — explicit members (independently trained/parameterized predictors), posterior sampling (Monte Carlo draws at inference, e.g., MCD, SWAG, BNN-VI/MCMC), or closed-form predictive (a single deterministic forward pass yields an analytic predictive distribution, e.g., SNGP, DUQ, VBLL); and (b) *network scope* — full network, shared-backbone/last-layer, or single deterministic network. Table 2 cross-tabulates every surveyed method into this 3×3 grid, with bold entries marking the methods that achieve genuine between-mode (multi-basin) diversity.
2. **Per-family critical review (Sections 3-10).** For each of the five core families (Bayesian NNs via VI/MCMC/Laplace/NNGP; MC Dropout; deep ensembles and variants like anchored ensembles and Credal Deep Ensembles/CreDE; efficient approximations such as BatchEnsemble, Snapshot Ensembles, SWAG/MultiSWAG, sub-ensembles, Masksembles, Layer Ensembles; and last-layer/single-pass methods such as Last-Layer Laplace, SNGP, DUQ/DDU/DUE, MIMO, epinet, VBLL), the authors examine theoretical motivation, implementation, empirical performance (as reported by the originating and follow-up papers), and known limitations. Adjacent families — evidential/prior/posterior networks (Section 8), conformal prediction (Section 9), and post-hoc calibration (Section 10) — are treated as complementary tools rather than core families.
3. **Evaluation methodology consolidation (Section 11).** Before making comparisons, the survey fixes the metrics (ECE, Brier score, NLL for calibration; AUROC/AUPR vs. the MSP baseline for OOD detection; risk-coverage curves for selective prediction) and the benchmark suites (OpenOOD, Uncertainty Baselines, CIFAR-10/100-C and ImageNet-C corruption suites, the Ovadia et al. 2019 shift protocol) that all subsequent qualitative ratings are anchored to.
4. **Comparative synthesis tables (Section 12, Table 3/4).** Every method is rated on training/inference cost (in units of single-model cost, with M = ensemble size, S = stochastic passes) and given qualitative Low/Medium/High OOD-detection and calibration ratings, each traceable to a cited "Evidence" source; ratings without direct benchmark evidence in the literature are explicitly flagged with a dagger (†) as "conservative inferences from method structure."
5. **Diversity and measure theory (Sections 13-14).** The bias-variance-diversity decomposition of ensemble loss (Wood et al., 2023) is presented in full (Eq. 2), followed by diversity metrics (pairwise disagreement, Cohen's κ, prediction entropy, CKA, Vendi score) and the uncertainty-measure decompositions: the standard entropy/mutual-information split (Depeweg et al. 2018; Smith and Gal 2018), its known finite-ensemble failure modes (Wimmer et al. 2023), the pairwise-divergence alternatives EPCE/EPKL (Schweighofer et al. 2023a) and the paper's proposed bounded EPJS variant, and efficient variance-gated measures (the authors' own VGE/VGMU/VGN line of work).
6. **Decision-time tasks and LLM extension (Sections 15-16).** OOD detection and selective prediction approaches are cataloged (Table 9), followed by a shorter treatment of uncertainty in autoregressive LLMs (token likelihood, semantic entropy via bidirectional entailment clustering, sampled-consistency checks like SelfCheckGPT, verbalized/elicited confidence, and conformal generation), explicitly flagged as an "emerging adjacent field" rather than the survey's main focus.

## Results & Highlights

No quantitative results reported — this is a survey and does not run new experiments or report a benchmark table of its own. Instead, its central deliverable is a **qualitative comparative synthesis** grounded in evidence drawn from the cited literature:

- **Table 3** rates ~20 methods (Bayesian inference family, ensembles/efficient approximations, single-pass/last-layer methods, distributional/post-hoc methods) on training cost (in units of M× single-model cost), inference cost, and Low/Medium/High OOD-detection and calibration quality, each anchored to a specific "Evidence" citation (e.g., deep ensembles rated High/High against Ovadia et al. 2019 and Gustafsson et al. 2020; BatchEnsemble rated Low/Low against Zamyatin et al. 2026; SNGP rated Medium-High/Medium-High against Liu et al. 2022).
- The survey reports the **qualitative reversal on BatchEnsemble**: originally proposed as a near-single-model-cost ensemble alternative (Wen et al., 2020), it was later shown by Zamyatin et al. (2026) to produce "near-identical" members in function space, behaving like a single model rather than a true ensemble, with correspondingly poor OOD detection — a concrete case of the survey flagging a method's claimed efficiency gains as illusory.
- On computational complexity, the survey states the standard pairwise-divergence measures (EPKL, EPJS) cost **O(M²C)** (M = ensemble members, C = classes), motivating the authors' own variance-gated measures at **O(MC)** — an explicit, quantifiable trade-off, though the paper itself states this efficient alternative "has not yet been benchmarked against MI and pairwise measures" under distribution shift (Section 14.7), i.e., the claimed efficiency-quality trade-off is unverified.
- Structural (not empirical) bounds are given for the epistemic measures: MI is bounded by ≤ log₂C bits and symmetric but underestimates epistemic uncertainty for diffuse-but-distinct member predictions (Wimmer et al., 2023); EPKL is unbounded and asymmetric; EPJS is bounded in [0,1] and symmetric (Table 6).

Because no numeric benchmark table (dataset + method score + baseline score) is presented by the paper itself, the required "≥3 quantitative claims with metric/dataset/paper-number/baseline-number" cannot be extracted; the items above are the closest concrete, paper-specific claims (a named reversal finding, a stated complexity class, and stated boundedness properties) that the survey offers in place of new quantitative results.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The method-vs-measure decoupling and the two-axis (mechanism × scope) taxonomy is a genuinely useful organizing device that differentiates this survey from architecture-first surveys (Gawlikowski et al. 2023, He et al. 2026) — the paper is explicit and self-aware about this positioning (Section 1.2). The structural comparison of MI/EPKL/EPJS (Table 6) is a clean, citable synthesis of scattered results.

**Rigor:** As a synthesis piece, rigor here means faithfulness to and correct characterization of cited results, which appears careful — e.g., the survey correctly reports the Zamyatin et al. (2026) finding that undercuts the earlier BatchEnsemble efficiency claims, and it flags its own qualitative ratings with a dagger (†) whenever a rating is "inferred from method structure" rather than a direct benchmark result. However, the underlying evidentiary basis is heterogeneous: the L/M/H ratings in Table 3/4 are drawn from studies that differ in architecture, dataset, and shift type, and the paper itself concedes these are "indicative placements rather than directly comparable measurements" (Section 12) — a limitation that is honestly stated but does mean the headline comparison tables cannot be read as apples-to-apples rankings.

**Reproducibility:** Not directly applicable (no code/experiments), but every claim is traceable to a specific citation, and the survey explicitly recommends validating one of its own proposed refinements (bounded EPJS) against MI and EPKL on standard OOD/shift suites (Section 11, Section 17.1) as "a concrete near-term step" — i.e., the survey itself flags that one of its central measure-comparison claims is not yet empirically validated, which is a notable and specific gap for a paper whose contribution is partly about proposing EPJS as a "remedy" to EPKL's instability.

**Significance:** The field already has several recent broad surveys (Gawlikowski et al. 2023, Abdar et al. 2021, Mena et al. 2021, He et al. 2026, Tyralis and Papacharalampous 2024), which the paper explicitly acknowledges and differentiates itself against by depth rather than breadth (deeper on efficient ensembles/last-layer methods and on measures). Whether this differentiation is significant enough to be a "must-read" alongside those surveys depends on the reader's specific interest in the method/measure separation.

**Red flags / weak points:** (1) The paper promotes the authors' own not-yet-independently-validated variance-gated measures (VGE/VGMU/VGN) within the same comparison framework as 20+ years of externally vetted methods, while simultaneously admitting these lack benchmarking under shift — a mild self-citation/self-promotion concern, disclosed but present. (2) The LLM uncertainty section (Section 16), despite being flagged in the conclusion as "the most consequential open frontier," receives only about one page versus ~15 pages for classification-focused methods — a proportionality mismatch given the paper's own framing of its importance. (3) No new quantitative results are contributed; all quantitative claims are secondhand and the qualitative L/M/H rating scale, while transparent, compresses a great deal of benchmark nuance (e.g., "M" vs "M-H" distinctions carry limited information).

## Strengths

- Clean, well-motivated two-axis taxonomy (generative mechanism × network scope) that cleanly explains why certain method combinations (e.g., full-network + explicit members) achieve the strongest between-mode epistemic diversity, while others (BatchEnsemble, MC Dropout) are structurally limited to single-basin exploration.
- Explicit, useful structural comparison of divergence-based epistemic measures (MI vs. EPKL vs. EPJS) on symmetry/boundedness/sensitivity, directly actionable for practitioners choosing an uncertainty measure.
- Honest self-flagging of evidentiary weaknesses: every qualitative rating in Table 3/4 traces to a cited source, with a dagger convention marking inferred (not directly benchmarked) placements — a level of methodological transparency not universal in survey papers.
- Good coverage of the "efficient approximation" and "last-layer/single-pass" literature (BatchEnsemble, SWAG/MultiSWAG, SNGP, DUQ/DDU/DUE, VBLL), an area the authors argue prior surveys under-serve, backed by specific, checkable citations including a very recent (2026) critique of BatchEnsemble's actual diversity.
- Clear articulation of open research questions (Section 17.2): last-layer diversity limits, efficient non-entropy-based epistemic measures, diversity-calibration interaction under shift, hybrid architectures, and the calibration-stability of LLM verbalized confidence.

## Limitations

- Purely a literature synthesis with no new experiments; the comparison tables are qualitative (L/M/H) rather than quantitative, and are explicitly acknowledged by the authors as "indicative placements" rather than directly comparable measurements across heterogeneous studies.
- Scope is deliberately narrow (ensemble-based and approximate Bayesian classification methods); regression is treated only briefly via the variance decomposition, and evidential/conformal/post-hoc calibration methods are covered as "adjacent" rather than in full depth.
- The LLM uncertainty treatment is admittedly brief ("deliberately uneven," per Section 1.3) relative to its stated importance as the field's most consequential open frontier.
- The paper's own proposed measure (bounded EPJS) and the authors' variance-gated measures are presented without independent empirical validation against the established MI/EPKL measures under distribution shift — a validation the paper itself calls for as future work rather than provides.
- As with any survey, currency risk: it draws on a fast-moving literature (e.g., a 2026 paper on BatchEnsemble diversity, cited as an EurIPS workshop paper) and several open questions it raises may already be partially addressed by contemporaneous work not yet indexed at the time of writing.

## Relevance

这篇综述对希望系统梳理深度学习不确定性量化（尤其是集成方法、贝叶斯近似及其效率变体）方法与度量选择的研究者很有参考价值，其方法-度量解耦框架和 MI/EPKL/EPJS 结构对比可直接指导 OOD 检测或选择性预测场景下的度量选型；但由于是定性综述而非实证研究，且部分内容涉及作者自身尚未充分验证的方法，引用时需结合原始基准论文核实具体数值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the bounded EPJS measure be empirically validated against MI and EPKL on standard OOD/shift suites (as the paper itself proposes in Section 17.1), and would it actually change practitioner recommendations?
- Does last-layer diversity alone (e.g., epinet, repulsive last-layer ensembles) recover most of the benefit of full deep ensembles, or is between-mode diversity from full-network explicit members fundamentally irreplaceable, as Kirsch (2025) suggests with the "epistemic collapse" critique?
- How much of the survey's qualitative L/M/H rating differences would survive a controlled, matched-compute re-benchmarking across methods on a single standardized suite (e.g., OpenOOD), rather than aggregating ratings from heterogeneous original studies?
- Can efficient, non-entropy-based epistemic measures for classification (like the authors' own variance-gated approach) be shown to preserve calibration and OOD-detection quality under covariate shift, closing the gap the paper leaves open in Section 14.7?
- Given the survey's own observation that BatchEnsemble's efficiency gains were later shown to be illusory (Zamyatin et al., 2026), are similar "diversity-collapse" audits warranted for other efficient ensemble approximations (Masksembles, Layer Ensembles, Sub-Ensembles) that have not yet received the same scrutiny?
- How should the method-measure separation framework be adapted for generative/LLM settings, where token- and sequence-level probability conflates linguistic variation with genuine semantic uncertainty, as the paper notes in Section 17.1?
