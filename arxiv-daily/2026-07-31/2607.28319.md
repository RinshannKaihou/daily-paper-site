> **一句话总结：** 该研究通过对比提示对的差分激活定位 GLU-MLP 中对人口学属性（年龄、性别、种族、宗教、外貌）敏感的神经元，在 Llama-3.2-1B 上仅归零最多 40 个神经元（占 MLP 宽度不到 0.031%）即可将通用能力保留率维持在 99.49%，但由于 BiasScore 不区分方向，归零操作在 BBQ/EsBBQ 上产生的是"双向失稳"而非系统性偏见降低——8 组实验里有 6 组的歧义/消歧偏见分数向相反方向移动。

> **TL;DR:** Fairness Pruning uses differential activations from minimally contrastive prompt pairs to localize demographic-bias-sensitive neurons at the down_proj input of GLU-MLP blocks. On Llama-3.2-1B, zeroing just 5–40 such neurons (≤0.031% of MLP width) retains 99.49% mean general-capability score (WikiText, MMLU, ARC-Challenge, HellaSwag EN/ES) while causing bias scores on BBQ/EsBBQ to move in opposite directions between ambiguous and disambiguated contexts in 6 of 8 English experiments (e.g., Religion Top-40 raises bias_ambig from 5.33%→6.67% while bias_disambig rises from 3.74%→6.62%), demonstrating causal relevance but not controllable mitigation because the unsigned BiasScore mixes stereotype-amplifying and stereotype-suppressing neurons.

## 核心贡献

- 提出 Fairness Pruning：一种无需额外训练、可在消费级 GPU 上运行的四阶段流水线（构建对比提示对 → 捕获 down_proj 输入处的差分激活 → 计算 BiasScore/FairnessPruningScore → 选择性归零候选神经元），用于定位并因果验证 LLM 中处理人口学偏见的神经元。
- 发布两个公开对比提示对数据集（英语 fairness-pruning-pairs-en：14 属性对/70 提示对；西班牙语 fairness-pruning-pairs-es：20 属性对/100 提示对），分别与 BBQ、EsBBQ 基准对齐，覆盖年龄、性别、外貌、种族/民族、宗教五类。
- 在 Llama-3.2-1B/3B 与 Salamandra-2B（西班牙语强模型）上系统刻画偏见信号在深度、层内结构、类别间重叠、跨语言一致性四个维度上的分布规律：偏见信号在所有 30 组（模型×语言×类别）组合中无一例外地集中在 down_proj_input 的最终层，且候选神经元在层内并非连续分布而是散布在整个中间维度。
- 通过归零实验实证证明：demographic bias 处理电路与通用能力（推理、常识、知识）在结构上是可分离的（dissociable circuits）——归零最多 40 个神经元能保持 97%–101% 的能力保留率，但对聚合偏见分数的影响是非单调且符号可逆的，这揭示了无符号 BiasScore 选择准则的方法论局限，并为未来转向"有向"激活转向（directional steering）奠定基础。

## Key Contributions

- A training-free, computationally cheap method (runs on consumer hardware/Colab L4 GPU) for localizing and intervening on demographic bias in GLU-based MLP layers using differential activations captured at the down_proj input, implemented in the open-source OptiPFair library.
- Two new public contrastive-prompt-pair datasets (English: 14 attribute pairs/70 prompt pairs; Spanish: 20 attribute pairs/100 prompt pairs) aligned with the BBQ and EsBBQ benchmarks respectively, covering Age, Gender, PhysicalAppearance, RaceEthnicity, and Religion, with a strict token-parity construction constraint.
- A systematic characterization of where and how bias circuits live in the network: the bias signal collapses almost entirely into the model's final layer at down_proj_input (54–94% of the global top-50 biased neurons per category, depending on model/language), is scattered (not contiguous) across the neuron index range, shows low cross-category overlap (Jaccard 0.16–0.32 at Top-1%, with Gender the most distinct category), and only moderate cross-lingual transfer (EN-ES Jaccard 0.13–0.20 at Top-1%).
- Empirical evidence that demographic-bias processing and general capabilities occupy dissociable circuits: zeroing ≤40 neurons (≤0.031% of total MLP width in Llama-3.2-1B) leaves general-capability benchmarks at 97.93%–101.34% retention (mean 99.49%) across WikiText, MMLU, ARC-Challenge, and HellaSwag EN/ES.
- A key negative/cautionary finding: because BiasScore is an unsigned magnitude of differential response, the selected neuron sets mix bias-amplifying and bias-suppressing neurons; zeroing them produces non-monotonic, sign-inverting effects on aggregate BBQ/EsBBQ bias scores as intervention scale grows, rather than the flat bias reduction one might naively expect from "ablating bias neurons."

## Methodology

The pipeline has four phases. (1) **Contrastive prompt pairs**: for each demographic category, prompt pairs are built that are token-identical except for one demographic attribute (e.g., "A [Black/Asian] neighbor knocked on the door..."), with exact tokenization parity enforced per-model (validated for Llama-3.2-1B's tokenizer at dataset-build time; checked at runtime for Salamandra-2B's differently-segmenting tokenizer, dropping non-matching pairs). Five templates per attribute pair span occupational, institutional, healthcare, social, and educational contexts, each ending mid-sentence to elicit a biased continuation.

(2) **Neuron-level bias detection**: in GLU/SwiGLU MLP blocks, gate_proj and up_proj combine via SiLU(gate_proj(x)) × up_proj(x), fed into down_proj. The authors register a PyTorch forward hook at the down_proj input during inference and define **BiasScore**\_{i,l} = (1/|P|) Σ_p |μ(a^(1)_{i,l,p}) − μ(a^(2)_{i,l,p})|, i.e., the mean absolute difference between per-sequence-mean activations of neuron i in layer l across the two prompts in each pair, averaged over all prompt pairs in a category. This is combined with OptiPFair's PPM (Peak-to-Peak Magnitude, max(w)+|min(w)|) structural-importance score into a composite **FairnessPruningScore** = α·BiasScore_norm + (1−α)·(1−ImportanceScore_norm); in practice, with α=0.8 favoring the bias signal, capability retention (97–101%) made the composite score unnecessary in this low-intervention regime (1–40 neurons), so zeroing experiments used raw BiasScore ranking.

(3) **Pruning/zeroing**: for each selected neuron i in layer l, row i of gate_proj.weight and up_proj.weight and column i of down_proj.weight are set to zero — a symmetric ablation that removes the neuron's contribution to the residual stream without altering model architecture (compatible with lm-eval and standard inference engines).

(4) **Evaluation**: general capabilities via lm-evaluation-harness across WikiText (perplexity), MMLU, ARC-Challenge, HellaSwag EN/ES (chosen because the base models show sufficient margin above chance, excluding near-chance tasks like GSM8K, IFEval, ARC-ES, Belebele, TruthfulQA MC2 from the retention analysis); demographic bias via BBQ (English) and EsBBQ (Spanish) in 0-shot configuration, reporting accuracy and bias score in both ambiguous and disambiguated contexts, with Δ_Bias and Δ_Capability computed as percentage change relative to baseline. Experiments cover three models (Llama-3.2-1B as primary, Llama-3.2-3B as a scaling check, Salamandra-2B for cross-architecture/cross-lingual validation) across Religion, Age, RaceEthnicity, and Gender categories at Top-K neuron counts of 1, 5, 10, 20, and 40, run on a single NVIDIA L4 GPU in bfloat16 with fixed seeds (seed=42) and greedy decoding for reproducibility.

## Results & Highlights

- **Capability retention (Llama-3.2-1B, Table IX)**: across the four Top-K experiments (Religion-5, Age-20, Race-5, Race-20) and five benchmarks, retention ranges from **97.93% (ARC-Challenge, Religion-Top-5) to 101.34% (MMLU, Religion-Top-5)**, mean 99.49% — i.e., zeroing at most 40 of 131,072 intermediate neurons (0.031%) leaves reasoning/knowledge essentially untouched, versus the base Llama-3.2-1B baseline of 37.2% ARC-Challenge acc_norm and 32.0% MMLU acc.
- **Bidirectional bias destabilization, not reduction**: on English BBQ, Llama-3.2-1B baseline bias_ambig/bias_disambig = 1.70%/1.75%; across 8 zeroing experiments the full variation range is only 0.80pp (ambiguous) and 0.50pp (disambiguated), and in **6 of 8 experiments the ambiguous and disambiguated scores move in opposite directions** (e.g., Age-Top-5: Δamb +0.19pp vs Δdisamb −0.16pp).
- **Category-level sign inversion (Table VIII)**: for Religion in English BBQ, Llama-3.2-1B baseline bias_ambig = 5.33%; zeroing 5 neurons reduces it to 3.67% (−1.66pp) but zeroing 40 neurons instead raises it to 6.67% (+1.34pp) — a non-monotonic reversal with neuron-set size. On **Llama-3.2-3B, Religion-Top-20 flips the sign of bias_disambig from +3.84% (baseline) to −1.67% (Δ = −5.51pp)**, the largest single directional shift in the paper, versus baseline bias_ambig dropping only 6.00%→1.50% (Δamb −4.50pp).
- **Single-neuron causal test**: zeroing only the single most dominant neuron (L15:166 in Religion, whose BiasScore is 32% higher than the runner-up) barely changes BBQ (Δamb +0.12pp, Δdisamb −0.07pp) and leaves 5 of 9 qualitative generations byte-identical to baseline, indicating redundancy — the circuit is not a single "storage" node.
- **Spatial/structural localization**: the highest mean bias signal at down_proj_input is in the model's final layer (L15 for Llama-3.2-1B, L27 for Llama-3.2-3B, L23 for Salamandra-2B) in every one of 30 model×language×category combinations tested; candidate neurons are scattered (not contiguous blocks) across the full intermediate-dimension width; cross-category Jaccard overlap at Top-1% is only 0.204–0.324 (median ~0.25–0.30) in the Llama family, meaning 66–88% of each category's candidate neurons are unique to that category, with Gender the most distinct (mean Jaccard 0.02–0.11 below other-pair mean).
- No quantitative comparison against alternative bias-localization baselines (e.g., SAE-based feature isolation, INLP, or the Dai et al./Voria et al. knowledge-neuron methods) is run empirically in this paper — comparisons to those methods are discussed qualitatively in Related Work only.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The core idea — using differential activations from contrastive prompt pairs to localize bias-sensitive neurons in GLU-MLP layers without training an SAE — is a reasonable, low-cost alternative to Voria et al.'s integrated-gradients knowledge-neuron approach for stereotype localization in BERT, and the paper is explicit and honest about how its method differs (symmetric, unsigned BiasScore vs. Voria et al.'s directional attribution), which is a genuine methodological contribution rather than an incremental rebrand.

**Rigor**: The experimental design is careful in several respects — token-parity constraints on contrastive pairs, exclusion of near-chance benchmarks from the retention analysis (a good practice many bias-pruning papers skip), fixed seeds, and an explicit single-neuron ablation (L15:166) as a targeted causal-relevance check. However, the core empirical finding — bidirectional, non-monotonic bias movement — is somewhat undercut by weak statistical grounding: BBQ bias-score deltas are frequently under 1pp (e.g., Table VII's full variation range is only 0.80pp/0.50pp), and no confidence intervals, multiple-seed variance, or significance testing are reported for these deltas, making it hard to fully rule out benchmark noise as an explanation for some of the smaller sign flips (though the larger Religion-Top-20 Llama-3B sign inversion of 5.51pp is more convincing).

**Reproducibility**: High — code, both new datasets, and exact experimental configs are public, with pinned dependencies and fixed seeds explicitly stated.

**Significance / paper-specific weak points**: The paper's own limitations section is unusually candid: it flags that (a) the study is entirely dependent on BBQ/EsBBQ's explicit-attribute, multiple-choice format and does not test whether findings generalize to open-ended generation or implicit-bias signals (names, occupations); (b) the architectural scope excludes attention heads entirely, focusing only on MLP/GLU circuits, so any bias encoded in attention is invisible to this method; (c) the token-parity requirement for contrastive pairs is a real practical constraint that shrank the valid Salamandra-2B subset substantially below the Llama-3.2-1B tokenizer's pair count — this is a genuine, disclosed data quirk rather than a hidden flaw; (d) the ablation study intentionally stays conservative (max 40 neurons, ~0.031% of MLP width) and does not explore whether the dissociable-circuits claim holds at larger, more aggressive intervention scales. No baseline against a naive random-neuron-zeroing control is reported, which would have strengthened the claim that BiasScore-selected neurons are meaningfully different from arbitrary neurons at the same layer/scale — this is a missing ablation worth noting. The headline claim ("99.49% capability retention") is real but should not be conflated with a bias-mitigation success — the paper itself argues this and reframes the contribution as causal localization rather than practical mitigation, which is intellectually honest positioning.

## Strengths

- Transparent and self-critical framing: the authors explicitly reject the temptation to oversell zeroing as "mitigation" once the bidirectional destabilization pattern emerged, and reframe the contribution around causal localization — a rarer form of honesty in bias-intervention papers.
- Strong reproducibility package: public code, two new bilingual datasets, pinned dependencies, fixed seeds, and integration with standard eval tooling (lm-evaluation-harness).
- Genuinely surgical intervention scale (≤0.031% of MLP width) combined with systematic, multi-axis structural analysis (depth, spatial layout, cross-category overlap, cross-lingual consistency) across three models and two languages, not just a single model/benchmark pairing.
- Useful negative result with clear mechanistic interpretation: the sign-inversion/non-monotonicity finding is a real, actionable insight for the bias-intervention literature, cautioning against unsigned-magnitude neuron selection criteria in future work.

## Limitations

- Restricted to explicit-attribute, multiple-choice bias measurement (BBQ/EsBBQ); does not test open-ended generation bias systematically or implicit-attribute bias (e.g., via names or occupations), despite showing a few qualitative open-ended examples.
- Architecturally scoped to MLP/GLU blocks only, excluding attention mechanisms entirely, which the authors acknowledge may also encode demographic bias.
- Token-parity requirement for contrastive pairs is a hard technical constraint that is especially limiting for morphologically rich languages (Spanish), reducing the valid pair set for Salamandra-2B.
- Zeroing experiments are conservative (max 40 neurons); the paper does not explore whether the dissociable-circuits and retention findings hold under more aggressive interventions.
- BiasScore's unsigned nature is acknowledged as the root cause of the uncontrollable bidirectional effect — the method as presented cannot yet deliver targeted, directional bias mitigation, only localization; the proposed fix (asymmetric corpora, signed scores, directional steering) is left entirely to future work.
- No random-neuron or alternative-baseline control condition is reported to confirm that BiasScore-selected neurons behave differently from arbitrary same-layer neurons at matched intervention scale.

## Relevance

该工作对可解释性驱动的偏见治理路线（而非数据/微调层面的去偏）有直接参考价值：其"无符号差分激活定位 + 归零"框架和随之揭示的双向失稳现象，为后续设计有向、可控的 activation steering 类去偏方法提供了明确的失败模式和改进方向（引入非对称语料计算有符号 BiasScore）。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would replacing the unsigned BiasScore with a signed version (using asymmetric, known-direction corpora as the authors propose in Section VI-C) actually produce monotonic, controllable bias reduction when zeroing or scaling the same neuron sets?
- How would this localization method perform against a random-neuron-zeroing control at matched sparsity, to isolate how much of the capability retention and bias movement is attributable to BiasScore selection versus generic robustness to small ablations?
- Does the strong final-layer concentration of the bias signal reflect a genuine architectural property of GLU-MLPs, or is it partly an artifact of measuring only at down_proj_input rather than at intermediate gate_proj/up_proj activations across all layers?
- Would incorporating attention-head analysis change the localization picture, given that the paper explicitly excludes attention and cannot rule out that a meaningful share of demographic bias is encoded there?
- How does the bidirectional destabilization pattern manifest in open-ended, production-style generation at scale (beyond the nine qualitative examples shown), and would a human-rated harm assessment corroborate or contradict the BBQ-based sign-inversion findings?
- Do the localization patterns (final-layer concentration, low cross-category overlap, moderate cross-lingual transfer) hold for production-scale models well beyond 4B parameters, as the authors flag as an open question?
