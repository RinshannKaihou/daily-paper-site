Why This Matters

LLM-as-judge bias audits may just measure shared model quirks, not real human stereotypes — since two independently-trained models agree with each other more than with people.

Critical Edge

The human ground truth used to validate the model is itself noisy — only "fair" inter-annotator agreement (Fleiss' κ=0.375) from 5 raters — and a substantial share of the "non-stereotypical" findings are statistically underpowered, not confirmed nulls.

Methodology

1
Semantic axis source
1,999 antonym adjective-synset pairs from WordNet (5,182 unique adjectives), sense-disambiguated by definition.
large ↔ small
cowardly ↔ brave
2
Axis representation
Mass-mean probing over attention-head outputs (mean-pooled) recovers each axis as a geometric direction; a variance-ratio score selects the top‑128 of 32×32 layer‑head pairs per axis.
3
Concept projection
A concept's activation (e.g. "CEOs", "uneducated people") is projected onto each selected axis direction, z‑scored, and averaged into one scalar π(c).
4
Stereotypicality test
A two-sample Kolmogorov–Smirnov test compares projections of 50 social groups against a frequency-matched random-phrase reference set; p<0.05 flags the axis "stereotypical."
Applied to Llama-3-8B-Instruct and Mistral-7B-Instruct (L=32, H=32, dhead=128). Validated against Fraser et al. (2021) Warmth/Competence labels and against the authors' own annotation of 100 stratified WordNet axes (5 volunteers, κ=0.375, 76 axes judged applicable). ~10 GPU‑hours per model on A100‑40GB.

Strengths

  • Four falsifiable hypotheses (H1–H4), each tied to a specific empirical test rather than one black-box pipeline.
  • Unusually thorough appendices: full ablation over template style/token position/ensemble size, plus a bootstrap statistical power analysis most probing papers omit.
  • Replaces the small fixed Warmth/Competence axis set with ~2,000 WordNet antonym pairs, surfacing new stereotyped axes independently confirmed by human annotators.
  • All four components (axis source, representation, projection, test) are independently swappable and extend beyond social groups to brands, foods, etc.
  • Transparent about null results (Mistral's non-significant humble/proud) and about its own ground truth's limits (κ=0.375).

Limitations

  • Only two ~7–8B open-weight instruct models tested; generalization to larger or differently-trained models is untested.
  • English-only: both templates and WordNet antonyms are English-specific; other languages need new lexical resources.
  • No ground truth exists for what an LLM "should" stereotype — divergence from humans could be real difference or framework artifact.
  • WordNet-antonym axes only make sense for some concept families; judging applicability (e.g. human traits vs. geometry terms) is a manual, subjective step.
  • Human ground truth has only "fair" reliability (κ=0.375, 5 annotators) and many underlying comparisons are statistically underpowered.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does LLM–LLM > LLM–human agreement hold for 70B+ or frontier closed models, or is it an artifact of Llama-3-8B and Mistral-7B sharing similar web-scale/RLHF training?
  2. Can the recovered geometric axes actually reduce stereotype projections via activation steering during generation, without hurting fluency or task performance?
  3. Would a head-to-head comparison against WEAT/SEAT/CEAT on the same 50 groups show StereoDisco surfacing meaningfully different content, or just a different lens on the same content?
  4. Restricted to only well-powered axes on both sides, would the 57–59% human-agreement figure rise substantially?
  5. Can the WordNet-antonym source extend to non-adjective vocabularies (proper nouns, domain dictionaries) to probe brands, foods, or individuals with the same pipeline unmodified?
