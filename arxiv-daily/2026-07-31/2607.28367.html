CUA Benchmark Reliability Audit · arXiv 2607.28367

15.3% of "FAIL" verdicts on five computer-use-agent benchmarks are wrong — the evaluator failed, not the agent.

复核150条被判"失败"的公开CUA轨迹：15.3%的FAIL判决有误（10.7%评测器假阴性 + 4.7%任务本身损坏）；在真正的失败中，验证/反馈类失误（39.3%）才是主因，而非定位/执行类问题（仅13.9%）。

Why This Matters

Benchmark scores used to rank CUA agents are pipeline outputs, not pure capability measurements — cross-benchmark comparisons may reflect evaluator bugs, not agent skill.

Critical Edge

The audit only sampled FAIL-labeled trajectories, so it measures evaluator false negatives but says nothing about false positives (reward hacking) among PASS verdicts — an acknowledged open gap.

No new agents were run. The authors re-labeled 150 already-public FAIL-labeled trajectories, deterministically sampled (seed 20260717) from AgentRewardBench (WebArena, VisualWebArena, AssistantBench, WorkArena) and OSWorld-Verified. Two vision-enabled LLM judges from different vendors — GPT-5.5 via Codex CLI and an undisclosed Claude Sonnet revision — independently classified every trajectory as genuine failure, evaluator false negative, broken task, or unclear. Two blind human groups reviewed all 74 judge-disagreement rows plus a 30-row stratified agreement sample, with majority vote across all four raters. Confirmed genuine failures were then coded into a three-tier taxonomy adapted from MAST — planning, execution/grounding, verification/feedback — using an "earliest decisive failure" rule rather than the most visually salient symptom. A final ablation stripped screenshots from the same trajectories to test how much verdicts depend on visual evidence.

Feedback-blind no-op repetition alone is the single largest subcategory at 29.5% (36/122). Bars don't sum to 100% — a residual of genuine failures falls outside the three primary tiers per the source.

  • Screenshot ablation: removing screenshots flips 12/104 of the Codex judge's verdicts and 13/104 of the Claude judge's, roughly halves detected evaluator false negatives (14→8 and 10→6), and drags Stage-1 inter-judge agreement from κ=0.71 down to κ=0.60.
  • Annotator variability: on the 104-row stress set, Stage-1 wrong-verdict estimates range from 7.7% (Claude Sonnet-5) to 20.2% (human group 1) — mean 13.7%, SD 5.1 percentage points — while Stage-2 diagnosis agreement (Fleiss' κ=0.36) is far weaker than the Stage-1 genuine/false call (κ=0.71).

Strengths

  • Large-scale (150 trajectories), cross-benchmark (web, enterprise-workflow, desktop) empirical audit, not a purely conceptual critique.
  • Multi-rater design — 2 independently blind LLM judges from different vendors + 2 independently blind human groups — with disagreement-triggered adjudication and transparently reported inter-rater reliability.
  • A concrete causal ablation (screenshot removal) demonstrating that observability materially changes verdicts, not just a claim asserted without evidence.
  • Clean mapping of the existing multi-agent MAST taxonomy into a single-agent CUA codebook, with a full category-by-category provenance table (Appendix B).
  • Translates findings into concrete, stage-indexed benchmark design guidelines (Table 3) rather than stopping at "benchmarks are unreliable."

Limitations

  • Small per-benchmark samples (23-57 trajectories) yield wide confidence intervals; the authors caution against reading per-benchmark rates as calibrated estimates.
  • The Claude judge's exact Sonnet revision is undisclosed, limiting exact reproducibility of the LLM-judge side of the audit.
  • Specialized professional-GUI benchmarks (healthcare, CAD/EDA, scientific instruments) are discussed for context but receive no empirical audit.
  • The proposed low-cost diagnostic probe (critical-window slack $S_{win}$) is explicitly "conceptual" — not validated against the paper's own human labels.
  • The CUA-landscape literature survey (§3) is an iterative scoping review, not a PRISMA-style systematic review with logged inclusion/exclusion counts.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Would auditing PASS-labeled trajectories reveal a comparably material false-positive / reward-hacking rate, changing the net direction of benchmark bias?
  2. How sensitive is the 15.3% estimate — and the Tier-3-dominance finding — to using a weaker or text-only (non-vision) LLM judge instead of GPT-5.5 / Claude Sonnet?
  3. Can the proposed $S_{win}$ no-op-repetition probe be validated against the paper's own 122 genuine-failure labels to produce calibrated precision/recall?
  4. Would the same dual-LLM-judge + human protocol on newer long-horizon benchmarks (OSWorld 2.0, Agents' Last Exam, WeaveBench) shift more failures toward Tier 2, as the paper speculates?
  5. Does a larger WorkArena sample confirm its 0/24 result, or is that within noise of a false-negative rate similar to WebArena's 21.7%?
