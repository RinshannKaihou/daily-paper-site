> **一句话总结：** AutoPref 用 LLM 引导的程序搜索，把偏好目标拆分为"逐对损失程序"和"集合感知加权程序"两个可搜索模块，在 TSP/CVRP/FFSP/JSSP 四类组合优化任务上发现的目标（APW）全面超过人工设计的 PO4COPs 和 BOPO，尤其在 TSP1000 和 JSSP15×15 等场景上差距显著。

> **TL;DR:** AutoPref factorizes preference-based NCO objectives into a pairwise loss program (what to learn from a comparison) and a set-aware weighting program (how much to weight it), then uses an LLM-driven staged conditional search with behavioral gates to discover task-specific objectives. On held-out TSP1000, the discovered APW objective reaches a 9.395% optimality gap versus 10.644% for the strongest hand-designed baseline PO4COPs and 17.840% for BOPO; on JSSP15×15, APW achieves 7.629% gap while PO4COPs collapses to 36.841%.

## 核心贡献

- 首次将偏好目标设计本身形式化为一个可被 LLM 自动搜索的程序空间问题，而非依赖专家手工设计的固定公式。
- 提出目标的双程序分解：逐对损失程序 f（决定从一对解中学什么）与集合感知加权程序 g（决定该对比较相对其它对比较的权重），二者组合的 F×G 空间可精确还原现有方法 PO4COPs 和 BOPO 作为特例。
- 设计分阶段条件搜索策略（先固定均匀权重搜 f，再固定 f* 搜 g），规避联合搜索的组合爆炸和信用分配困难。
- 引入行为筛选门（可执行性、偏好一致性、尺度不变性、权重非退化性）在昂贵的策略训练之前过滤掉大量无效/退化候选程序，大幅提升搜索效率。
- 采用早期+中期两个策略状态的短程训练评估与结构多样性感知的存活者选择，避免搜索目标只对早期训练有效或过早陷入同质化候选。
- 在 TSP、CVRP、FFSP、JSSP 四类组合优化问题的发现尺度与两个未见过的尺度上，发现的目标一致优于最强的人工设计基线。

## Key Contributions

- Formalizes preference-objective design for neural combinatorial optimization (NCO) as an automated, LLM-searchable programmatic discovery problem rather than a fixed hand-crafted formula.
- Factorizes the objective into a pairwise loss program f (what to learn from one comparison) and a set-aware weighting program g (how much to weight that comparison relative to the sampled set); the Cartesian product space F×G provably recovers PO4COPs and BOPO as special cases.
- Introduces a staged conditional search: Stage 1 searches f under uniform weighting (yielding the Uniform Pair Weighting objective, UPW); Stage 2 freezes f* and searches g (yielding the Adaptive Pair Weighting objective, APW), avoiding the combinatorial explosion and credit-assignment ambiguity of a joint search.
- Adds four behavioral gates (executable validity, preference consistency, scale invariance, weighting non-degeneracy) that reject invalid or degenerate LLM-proposed programs before allocating any downstream training budget.
- Uses short-horizon dual-state (early-init and partially-trained) fitness evaluation plus a structural-diversity filter during survivor selection to avoid overfitting to early-training dynamics and to prevent premature convergence to syntactic variants.
- Empirically validates the discovered objectives (UPW/APW) across TSP, CVRP, FFSP, and JSSP at both the discovery scale and two held-out scales per family, under matched architectures and training budgets.

## Methodology

AutoPref targets preference-based NCO, where a construction policy π_θ samples a solution set per instance, unequal-cost pairs are oriented from lower-cost (preferred) to higher-cost (less-preferred) trajectories, and training increases the relative likelihood of preferred solutions. The paper's key move is representing a preference objective as an ordered pair of executable programs O=(f,g):

- **Pairwise loss program f: U→ℝ** consumes a pair-level feature vector u_i = (q_w, q_l, Δq_i) — per-solution features q_j = (cost o_j, log-prob p_j, cost-sorted rank r_j, auxiliary record b_j with entropy/stepwise log-probs) plus the pairwise deltas (cost gap, log-prob margin, rank gap) — and returns a scalar loss.
- **Set-aware weighting program g: V→ℝ≥0** additionally observes the full per-instance solution-set context C_x and deterministic summary statistics (e.g., cost median absolute deviation, log-prob std) to produce a non-negative weight for each comparison.

Training minimizes the expected normalized weighted loss Σg(v_i)f(u_i)/(Σg(v_i)+ε). The authors show PO4COPs (uniform-weighted Bradley-Terry-style loss on the log-prob margin) and BOPO (best-anchored pairs with a cost-ratio-scaled loss, uniform sub-sampling weight) are both single fixed points in this F×G space.

**Search procedure.** A direct joint search over F×G is judged computationally intractable (every candidate needs an NCO training run) and suffers weak credit assignment. AutoPref instead runs a two-stage conditional search:
1. Stage 1 fixes g₀=1 (uniform weights) and searches f alone (isolating "what to learn"), producing the UPW objective.
2. Stage 2 freezes the Stage-1 f* and searches g alone (isolating "how much to weight"), producing the APW objective.

Both stages use the same LLM-guided propose→validate→short-train→select evolutionary loop (an LLM proposer generates candidate programs conditioned on prior scored candidates; the authors explicitly note this loop itself is a standard technique from prior program-search work, and position their novelty in the structural decomposition and domain-specific screening, not the search algorithm). Before any policy training, candidates must pass four behavioral gates: (1) executable validity (compiles, runs within a time budget, produces finite values/gradients), (2) preference consistency (swapping preferred/less-preferred flips gradient signs), (3) scale invariance (loss is stable under positive affine rescaling of costs, within tolerance ε_aff), and (4) weighting non-degeneracy (coefficient of variation of g across a comparison pool must exceed a threshold so it's distinguishable from uniform weighting). Surviving candidates are evaluated with a short, fixed training budget from two policy states — early (random init) and late (a partially trained checkpoint) — and fitness is the average cost improvement over a PO4COPs reference objective trained under identical conditions. Survivor selection filters out structurally redundant candidates to preserve diversity across generations.

Discovery is performed at one scale per COP family — TSP100, CVRP100, FFSP100, JSSP15×15 — and the resulting frozen objective is then trained from scratch with the full budget at that scale plus two held-out scales (e.g., TSP50/TSP1000) to test generalization.

## Results & Highlights

- **TSP1000 (held-out scale):** APW achieves an optimality gap of 9.395% (cost 25.2858) versus PO4COPs at 10.644% (cost 25.5745) and BOPO at 17.840% (cost 27.2378); the standard neural baseline POMO is far worse at 35.001% gap. The paper highlights that APW's margin over baselines *expands* on larger held-out scales rather than shrinking.
- **JSSP15×15 (discovery scale):** APW reaches 7.629% gap (cost 1297.40), while the hand-designed PO4COPs objective degrades sharply to 36.841% gap (cost 1649.54) — worse than the standard neural baseline MGL (12.206%) — and BOPO reaches 8.108% (cost 1303.18). This is the paper's starkest illustration of a hand-designed preference objective failing to transfer across COP families.
- **CVRP1000 (held-out scale):** APW gap 3.407% (cost 123.1858) vs. PO4COPs 6.968% (cost 127.4277) and BOPO 7.161% (cost 127.6575) — roughly a 2x reduction in gap relative to both hand-designed preference baselines.
- **Factorial ablation (Table 3, TSP100/FFSP100):** relative to the fully manual PO4COPs-loss + uniform-weighting baseline (0.0000/0.000 absolute cost reduction by definition), using the discovered f alone with uniform weighting gives +0.0039/+0.260; using PO4COPs' f with the discovered g gives +0.0011/+0.201; combining both discovered programs gives the largest reduction, +0.0067/+0.300, confirming the two components contribute complementary, non-redundant signal.
- **FFSP gains are comparatively small:** on FFSP100, APW's cost 89.380 vs. BOPO's 89.672 is roughly a 0.3% relative difference — a real but modest improvement compared to the much larger margins seen on TSP1000 and JSSP15×15.
- Across all 12 evaluated (family × scale) settings, APW achieves the lowest cost among all preference-based/self-labeling methods (PO4COPs, BOPO, SLIM, UPW, APW), and the paper reports this as full sweep dominance.
- A mechanistic analysis (Section 5.4, Figure 2) of the discovered TSP objective shows it acts as a cost-gap-dependent "inverse temperature" on the log-prob margin — the opposite behavior of BOPO, which amplifies rather than dampens the margin as cost gap grows — while the paired weighting program upweights large-cost-gap comparisons to compensate, concentrating gradient weight on frequently-sampled, ambiguous (near-zero-margin) comparisons.

## Authors & Credibility

Authors are affiliated with C²DL, Institute of Automation, Chinese Academy of Sciences (CASIA), the University of Chinese Academy of Sciences (UCAS), Tencent AI Lab, and AiRiA. The work builds directly on and benchmarks against two recent, credible ICML/AAAI-published preference-based NCO methods (PO4COPs, ICML 2025; BOPO, ICML 2025) and situates itself within an established LLM-for-program-discovery lineage (FunSearch/Romera-Paredes et al. 2024, Eureka reward design, AutoLoss-Zero). No code repository link is visible in the pages reviewed, and the specific LLM used as the proposer is not stated in the main text (likely in an appendix not covered here) — this limits independent reproducibility assessment.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core idea — factorizing a preference objective into "what to learn per pair" and "how much to weight each pair," searched by an LLM — is a genuinely useful and non-obvious structural contribution specific to preference-based NCO, going beyond simply applying an existing LLM program-search loop (which the authors themselves are careful to disclaim as their novelty). The formal recovery of PO4COPs and BOPO as special points in the F×G space is a clean and convincing way to motivate the generalization.

**Rigor:** The behavioral-gate design (preference consistency, scale invariance, weighting non-degeneracy) is a thoughtful, domain-specific way to prune the search space before expensive training, and the dual-state (early/late) fitness evaluation directly addresses a real failure mode (objectives that only help early training). The factorial ablation (Table 3) is a good practice that isolates the individual contribution of f vs. g.

**Paper-specific concern — inflated margin from a collapsing baseline:** The JSSP15×15 result is the paper's most dramatic number (PO4COPs at 36.841% gap, nearly 5x worse than even the standard neural baseline MGL at 12.206%), but this also means PO4COPs is essentially a broken baseline on scheduling tasks, not a "strong hand-designed" one for this family. Against the actually competitive baseline BOPO (8.108%), AutoPref's APW (7.629%) only wins by about half a percentage point on JSSP15×15 — a much less dramatic story than the headline framing suggests. The abstract's claim of "consistently outperform[ing] the strongest hand-designed baselines" is technically true (APW does beat whichever of PO4COPs/BOPO is best in each setting) but the size of that margin varies enormously and is sometimes driven by a baseline collapsing rather than AutoPref improving.

**Missing detail / relegated ablation:** The comparison between the staged conditional search and a full joint search over F×G — which is central to justifying the paper's main algorithmic design choice — is pushed to Appendix D.2 rather than the main text, and the authors themselves acknowledge (Section 4.1) that the greedy staged decomposition "may converge to a strong local optimum rather than the true global optimum," which they leave as an open limitation rather than empirically bounding within the main paper.

**Reproducibility:** Search cost accounting (number of candidate programs evaluated, wall-clock/GPU cost of the discovery phase, choice of underlying LLM) is not present in the main-body pages reviewed, which matters because the entire pitch is computational efficiency relative to naive search — without these numbers it is hard to judge whether the discovery overhead is trivial or comparable to training several extra baseline models.

**Significance:** The generalization results (a single per-family search that transfers to two held-out scales, including up to 10x larger problems like TSP1000/FFSP1000/JSSP50×20) are a meaningful and non-trivial demonstration — it would have been easy for a discovered, possibly overfit objective to fail on unseen scales, and the paper shows the opposite trend on routing tasks.

## Strengths

- Clean theoretical unification: existing hand-designed objectives (PO4COPs, BOPO) are shown as exact special cases of the searched space, which gives confidence the search space is well-posed and sufficiently expressive.
- Behavioral gating is a practical, transferable idea for LLM-guided symbolic/program discovery in RL objective design generally, not just NCO.
- Strong cross-scale generalization evidence: objectives discovered at one scale per family are frozen and evaluated at two held-out scales without any additional search, and margins over baselines tend to persist or widen at scale on routing tasks.
- Factorial ablation cleanly attributes gains to both the pairwise loss and the weighting program individually and jointly.
- Broad task coverage spanning both routing (TSP, CVRP) and scheduling (FFSP, JSSP), which have quite different solution/reward structures, strengthening the generality claim.

## Limitations

- The staged (rather than joint) search is explicitly acknowledged by the authors as a "pragmatic compromise" that may find only a local optimum in the coupled F×G space; no empirical bound on how far from the joint optimum the staged solution falls is given in the main text.
- Some reported gains (e.g., FFSP, and AutoPref vs. BOPO specifically on JSSP15×15) are modest in absolute/relative terms, while the most eye-catching numbers come from settings where a baseline (PO4COPs on JSSP) essentially fails.
- Search is performed once per COP family at a single discovery scale; the paper does not report variance across multiple independent search runs (different random seeds / LLM sampling), so the stability of the discovery process itself is unclear.
- No code/artifact link is evident in the reviewed pages, and the specific LLM backbone used for program proposal is not stated in the main text, limiting reproducibility judgments.
- The approach is restricted to pairwise objectives; the authors note extending beyond pairwise formulations (e.g., listwise) is left to future work.

## Relevance

该工作把"损失函数设计"本身当作可自动化搜索的对象，延续了 LLM 驱动的算法/目标发现路线（FunSearch、Eureka 等），对研究强化学习目标工程、组合优化神经求解器、以及自动化机器学习流水线设计的读者有较高参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does a full joint search over F×G (reported only in an appendix) compare quantitatively to the staged conditional search in terms of both final performance and search cost?
- What is the variance in discovered-objective quality across multiple independent LLM search runs (different seeds/sampling temperatures) for the same COP family?
- How much wall-clock/compute budget does the entire discovery pipeline (proposal + behavioral gates + short-horizon dual-state training) consume relative to simply training a few extra hand-designed baseline variants?
- Would the discovered objectives transfer across COP families (e.g., applying the TSP-discovered APW to CVRP) without re-running discovery, and if not, what does that imply about the claimed "task-specific" benefit versus simply having more objective-search compute than prior work?
- Can the behavioral-gate framework (preference consistency, scale invariance, weighting non-degeneracy) generalize to other RL objective-design problems outside NCO, such as general preference optimization for LLMs?
