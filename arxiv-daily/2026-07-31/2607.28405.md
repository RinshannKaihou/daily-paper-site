> **一句话总结：** 针对联合预测视频观测与动作的"世界动作模型"(World Action Models, WAMs)，QuantWAMs提出三项校准粒度对齐策略——仅在坐标兼容模块间共享的异常值统计、基于视频-动作联合梯度经验Fisher信息的层级权重显著度、以及用真实闭环回放（而非合成开环输入）审计去噪步保护调度——在Fast-WAM和LingBot-VA两个模型上实现W4A4量化，闭环成功率相比FP16仅下降0.2-0.7个百分点，而匹配预算的Atom*基线差距达14-17个百分点，同时把目标模块显存降到FP16的约29%、速度提升1.4-1.6倍。

> **TL;DR:** QuantWAMs is a post-training quantization (PTQ) framework built specifically for World Action Models — diffusion models that jointly denoise future video observations and actions in closed loop — via three calibration-context-aware strategies: pooling activation-outlier statistics only across "coordinate-compatible" modules, scoring weight saliency from the joint video-action empirical-Fisher gradient at a calibration-stable layer granularity, and auditing denoising-step protection schedules with fixed-intervention replay on real FP16 closed-loop rollouts instead of synthetic/open-loop states. On RoboTwin 2.0 with Fast-WAM, W4A4 QuantWAMs reaches 91.7±0.3% average success vs. FP16's 91.9±0.3% (0.2pp gap) and 97.4±0.2% vs. 97.6±0.2% on LIBERO, while the strongest matched-budget W4A4 baseline, Atom*, trails FP16 by 14.7pp (77.2±0.6%) and 15.5pp (82.1±0.5%) respectively; peak weight+activation memory for the targeted blocks drops to ~29% of FP16 with a 1.4-1.6× block-level speedup. Real-robot trials on an AgiBot G2 (30 trials, 3 tasks) show QuantWAMs at 56.7% vs. FP16's 63.3% and Atom*'s 40.0%.

## 核心贡献

- 将WAM的训练后量化问题形式化为"校准语境"三要素——统计量共享的**结构**范围、评分所依据的**分布**、以及优化的**目标**——并指出现有PTQ方法（假设开环评分、同质架构、非部署分布）在这三个轴上均与WAM的闭环、多流耦合特性不匹配。
- 提出"共享基底异常值校准"：仅在坐标兼容（共享同一量化器输入坐标系）的模块间池化激活统计量，并给出理论上的"池化交叉点"条件（Proposition 1）：当跨成员异质性τ²小于采样噪声σ²/N时池化降低风险，否则应保持独立校准；用轨迹级自助重采样（bootstrap）验证该判据的稳定性。
- 提出"联合训练目标显著度"：直接对视频-动作联合损失的组合梯度计算经验Fisher信息（而非分别计算再"事后融合"），并发现由于校准样本有限，逐元素权重精度分配不稳定，改为在层级粒度做混合精度分配，性能明显更可靠。
- 提出"固定干预真实回放审计"：用与部署时相同的（未加保护的）干预、在FP16模型生成的真实闭环回放状态上重新评估各去噪步的敏感度，避免"已保护步骤看起来不敏感"的自遮蔽效应和"合成开环输入未覆盖真实可达状态"的分布失配，进而修订（而非重新分配）去噪步保护预算。
- 在两个不同耦合方式的开源WAM实现（双流Fast-WAM、共享骨干LingBot-VA）、两个仿真基准（RoboTwin 2.0、LIBERO）和真实AgiBot G2机器人上验证：W4A4量化下，仿真闭环成功率相比FP16仅相差0.2-0.7个百分点，目标模块显存降至FP16的约29%，速度提升1.4-1.6倍。

## Key Contributions

- Formalizes WAM post-training quantization around three axes of a "calibration context" — the **structural** scope over which statistics are pooled, the **distribution** on which a decision is scored, and the **objective** used to score it — and argues existing PTQ methods mismatch all three because they assume open-loop scoring, homogeneous architecture, and non-deployment calibration distributions.
- Introduces **shared-basis outlier calibration**: pools activation-outlier evidence only across "coordinate-admissible" modules (those sharing a literal quantizer-input coordinate system after a common Hadamard rotation), and derives a closed-form pooling-crossover condition (Proposition 1: pooling helps when trajectory count N < N* = σ²_c/τ²_c, i.e., when cross-member heterogeneity is small relative to sampling noise), validated empirically via trajectory-level bootstrap resampling.
- Introduces **co-training-objective saliency**: computes an empirical-Fisher weight-saliency score directly from the *combined* video+action gradient (retaining the cross-stream term that "post-hoc fusion" of separately-computed per-stream Fisher factors discards), and finds that layer-level (not element-level) mixed-precision allocation is the calibration-stable granularity given only 32 calibration trajectories.
- Introduces **fixed-intervention real-rollout auditing**: revises which denoising steps get high-precision protection by replaying real FP16 closed-loop rollout states under the *same unprotected* intervention used at profiling time, avoiding the self-masking bias of scoring an already-protected step on synthetic/open-loop inputs — a schedule *repair* that keeps the precision budget fixed and only changes which steps are protected.
- Validates the full pipeline on two architecturally distinct released WAMs (dual-stream Mixture-of-Transformers Fast-WAM; shared-backbone LingBot-VA) across RoboTwin 2.0, LIBERO, and real-world AgiBot G2 manipulation, with a disjoint 4-way trajectory split (calibration / profiling / schedule-validation / test) and 3 independent protocol seeds for the simulation results.

## Methodology

QuantWAMs targets W4A4 (4-bit weight, 4-bit activation) quantization of the video-DiT and action-DiT blocks inside a WAM, leaving embeddings, projections, and the VAE untouched. It layers three calibration-context-aware mechanisms on top of a standard mixed-precision channel-offloading PTQ backbone (à la Atom):

1. **Shared-basis outlier calibration (Sec. 3.1).** For an admissible group of modules that share a coordinate system (e.g., self-attention output projections across video and action DiT blocks that reuse the same value pathway), the method pools per-channel squared-activation energy statistics *e_i(c)* across group members with deployment-exposure weights π_i, then selects the Top-K channels for high-precision (BF16) retention from the pooled estimate. "Coordinate admissibility" is defined formally: a channel index can only be shared across modules if it denotes the same quantizer-input coordinate after applying the same fixed Hadamard rotation R — in Fast-WAM this holds for the shared value pathway (not query/key, which are architecturally private per stream), while in LingBot-VA's shared backbone it holds for modes that reuse the same physical Linear's input columns. A random-effects model (per-member statistic = group mean + cross-member deviation τ_c + sampling noise σ_c) yields Proposition 1: pooling strictly reduces mean-squared risk versus independent per-member calibration when the number of calibration trajectories N is below a crossover threshold N* = σ²_c/τ²_c (equivalently, an effective sample size N_eff); the paper also gives a probabilistic bound on mask-recovery failure under a sub-Gaussian proxy for the pooled error.
2. **Co-training-objective saliency (Sec. 3.2).** For each candidate Linear layer, an empirical-Fisher-style importance score is computed from the gradient of the *actual* video+action co-training loss (λ_v ℓ_v + λ_a ℓ_a) evaluated on the 32 calibration trajectories, requiring one backward pass through the pretrained (unmodified) WAM. This "Joint" score explicitly retains a cross-stream term (Eq. 13, diag(G_joint − G_fusion) = 2λ_vλ_a·E[g_v⊙g_a]) that a naive "post-hoc fusion" of separately-computed video and action Fisher factors would discard. A Kronecker-factored empirical-Fisher approximation (Martens & Grosse 2015) converts per-weight saliency into a bit-upgrade benefit B_L for promoting a layer from low to high precision, and a knapsack-style layerwise (not element- or purely column-wise) allocation selects the top 20% of candidate Linears (by count, not parameter size) for W8A8 upgrade under a fixed budget — because finite-sample scores make element-level rankings unstable.
3. **Fixed-intervention real-rollout auditing (Sec. 3.3).** Given a deployed per-step precision schedule (which denoising steps get "protected" at higher precision), the method constructs a sensitivity profile S^replay_ref(t) by replaying every recorded state from 32 FP16 closed-loop rollouts (D_prof) through the *same unprotected* intervention q₀ used at profiling — restoring the exact FP16 snapshot (observation history, chunk position, persistent cache) before each branch and matching stochastic seeds between the full-precision and quantized forward passes. This avoids two failure modes of naive alternatives: synthetic/open-loop profiles that never see reachable rollout states, and "observational" profiles that self-mask because the protection is already active. The Top-K most-sensitive steps by this profile become the revised protected set T_replay, which is checked once against a held-out validation set (D_val) via a pre-specified accept/reject rule and frozen before final testing — a proposal-and-validation procedure, not an oracle search over the test set.

The full pipeline is evaluated with a strict 4-way trajectory-disjoint split per benchmark: 32 trajectories for PTQ calibration (D_cal), 32 separate FP16 closed-loop rollouts for schedule profiling (D_prof), a separate validation set (D_val) for the accept/reject schedule decision, and a final test set — repeated across 3 independent protocol seeds (42/43/44) for simulation results.

## Results & Highlights

- **RoboTwin 2.0, Fast-WAM, W4A4 (Table 1):** QuantWAMs reaches 91.7±0.3% average closed-loop success (Clean 91.8±0.2%, Random 91.6±0.5%) versus FP16's 91.9±0.3% — a 0.2pp gap — while the strongest matched-budget W4A4 baseline, Atom* (same nominal precision allocation, count-based W8 budget, and protected-step count as QuantWAMs, but Atom's own quantizer), reaches only 77.2±0.6% (14.7pp below FP16), and plain SVDQuant W4A4 collapses to 61.1±0.8% (30.8pp below FP16).
- **LIBERO, Fast-WAM, W4A4 (Table 1):** QuantWAMs achieves 97.4±0.2% average success (Goal 98.0±0.3%, Spatial 99.6±0.2%, Object 96.8±0.3%, Long 95.0±0.4%) vs. FP16's 97.6±0.2% (0.2pp gap), against Atom*'s 82.1±0.5% (15.5pp gap) and GPTQ W4A16's 96.0±0.4% (1.6pp gap, but at double the activation bit-width).
- **LingBot-VA, W4A4 (Table 2):** on RoboTwin 2.0, QuantWAMs scores 91.6±0.3% vs. FP16's 92.3±0.3% (0.7pp gap) and Atom*'s 76.3±0.6% (16.0pp gap); on LIBERO-Long, QuantWAMs scores 98.0±0.3% vs. FP16's 98.5±0.2% (0.5pp gap) and Atom*'s 81.5±0.7% (17.0pp gap).
- **Efficiency:** for the targeted Fast-WAM video/action blocks, measured peak weight-plus-activation memory drops to ~29% of FP16, with 1.4-1.6× block-level latency speedup (measured on NVFP4 W4A4 / FP8 W8A8 / BF16-bypass kernels on SM120 Blackwell GPUs) — explicitly disclosed as a block-level, not end-to-end control-loop, metric.
- **Ablation ladder (Table 3, LIBERO-Long, Fast-WAM → LingBot-VA):** Base (per-context masks + post-hoc fusion + synthetic Top-K schedule) scores 80.8±0.7% / 80.2±0.9%; adding Shared-Basis pooling raises it to 89.1±0.6% / 89.6±0.8%; adding Joint saliency (still synthetic schedule) raises it to 90.9±0.7% / 91.8±0.8%; adding Fixed-Intervention Replay completes the method at 95.0±0.4% / 98.0±0.3% — each component contributes a nontrivial, monotonic gain under matched precision budgets.
- **Saliency-source ablation (Table 5):** at the same precision budget, Joint saliency beats post-hoc fusion by 3.6pp on Fast-WAM (95.0±0.4% vs. 91.4±0.5%) and 5.2pp on LingBot-VA (98.0±0.3% vs. 92.8±0.6%); it also beats video-only (85.4±0.7%/83.1±0.8%) and action-only (87.1±0.6%/86.5±0.7%) single-stream saliency.
- **Allocation-granularity ablation (Table 4):** element-level weight-precision allocation is markedly unstable and underperforms (72.1±0.9% Fast-WAM, 69.8±1.0% LingBot-VA) compared to the paper's layer-level allocation (95.0±0.4%/98.0±0.3%), directly supporting the claim that finite calibration data cannot support fine-grained (element-level) precision decisions.
- **Real-robot trials (AgiBot G2, Table 6):** across 3 tasks × 10 trials (30 total), FP16 Fast-WAM succeeds 19/30 (63.3%), QuantWAMs (W4A4) succeeds 17/30 (56.7%), and the Atom* baseline (best simulation baseline) succeeds only 12/30 (40.0%), at a matched 1.4× block-level speedup for both quantized methods.

## Authors & Credibility

The authors are affiliated with Fudan University's College of Intelligent Robotics and Advanced Manufacturing and Shanghai Key Lab of Intelligent Information Processing (College of Computer Science and AI), plus East China Normal University's School of Data Science and Engineering. None of the author names is a widely recognized figure in the PTQ or robot-learning literature to this reviewer's knowledge, and the paper does not cite prior work by these authors as foundational, suggesting this is a newer research effort rather than an established lab's flagship line. The paper evaluates against two officially released, third-party WAM checkpoints (Fast-WAM, LingBot-VA) rather than the authors' own trained models, which is a point in favor of external validity. A project page (https://quantwams.github.io) is provided, but the paper text does not explicitly confirm a public code/checkpoint release — reproducibility of the exact calibration/grouping rules (detailed in Appendices A-E, not reviewed here) would depend on that release actually appearing.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The three-axis "calibration context" framing (structural/distributional/objective mismatch) is a genuinely useful and specific diagnosis of *why* generic PTQ (GPTQ, SmoothQuant, Atom, SVDQuant, all designed for single-stream LLMs/diffusion transformers) underperforms on WAMs, and it's differentiated from concurrent VLA-quantization work (QVLA, QuantVLA, both cited) that targets a single vision-language backbone with an action head rather than the dual-stream/shared-backbone coupling and closed-loop rollout dynamics unique to WAMs.

**Rigor:** A genuine strength is the theoretical pooling-crossover criterion (Proposition 1) being empirically cross-checked via trajectory-level bootstrap resampling (Fig. 3) before being tested for its closed-loop consequence (Table 4) — this connects a finite-sample statistical argument to task-success outcomes rather than asserting it. The 4-way trajectory-disjoint split (calibration/profiling/validation/test) and 3-seed protocol for simulation results are also more careful than typical PTQ papers, which often calibrate and test on the same distribution without seed variation.

**Paper-specific weak points:** The "matched-budget" baselines Atom* and SVDQuant* are explicitly flagged by the authors themselves as controlling only for *nominal precision allocation* (same W8 fraction, same protected-step count), not an identical calibration algorithm — and the paper openly states "this control does not... match the additional gradients and closed-loop rollouts used by QuantWAMs, nor does it isolate any single component." This means the large gap versus Atom* (14-17pp) partly reflects QuantWAMs having access to signals (co-training gradients, FP16 rollout replay) the baselines never receive, which is by design but limits how much of the improvement can be attributed to the *quantizer* itself versus the *extra information* used. Separately, the authors explicitly disclaim causal attribution for the Joint-vs-fusion saliency ablation's 3.6-5.2pp gain ("the closed-loop result alone does not establish a causal attribution to that [cross-objective] term") — an honest but real limitation on the method's central saliency claim.

**Reproducibility:** The reported 1.4-1.6× speedups and 29% memory figure are measured on SM120 Blackwell GPUs using NVFP4 W4A4 kernels — a narrow, very recent hardware target, so the efficiency numbers may not transfer to other accelerators without re-implementing the hybrid-precision kernels. The paper does not report ablations at bit-widths other than W4A4/W8A8, nor test sensitivity to the number of calibration trajectories (fixed at 32 throughout).

**Significance:** Given growing interest in deploying WAMs/VLA policies at the edge, a PTQ method that keeps closed-loop task success within ~1pp of FP16 at W4A4 (vs. 15-17pp degradation for the strongest baseline) is a meaningful practical result, assuming the specialized kernel/hardware requirements are acceptable.

**Red flags:** None severe. The paper is unusually candid about scope limits throughout (repeatedly stating that reported comparisons are "not formal non-inferiority or equivalence tests," that speedup/memory numbers are block-level rather than end-to-end, and that the real-robot study is "underpowered for comparison with FP16"). One point worth flagging rather than a red flag per se: the real-world FP16-vs-QuantWAMs gap (63.3% vs. 56.7%, i.e., 6.6pp) is substantially larger than any simulation gap (0.2-0.7pp), suggesting the calibration procedure's near-lossless behavior may not fully transfer from simulation to real hardware/sensor noise, even though QuantWAMs still clearly outperforms the Atom* real-robot baseline (40.0%).

## Strengths

- Clear, specific diagnosis of why generic PTQ heuristics fail for WAMs (structural/distributional/objective mismatch), rather than a vague "existing methods aren't good enough" motivation.
- Theoretically grounded pooling-crossover criterion (Proposition 1) validated against its own predicted finite-sample behavior before being tested for downstream task success — a rare two-step validation in this literature.
- Thorough, matched-budget cumulative ablation ladder (Table 3) and independent axis ablations (Tables 4-5) isolating mask-grouping, allocation-granularity, saliency-source, and schedule-selection choices.
- Evaluated across two architecturally distinct WAMs (dual-stream vs. shared-backbone), two simulation benchmarks, and a real dual-arm robot platform, with disjoint calibration/profiling/validation/test trajectory splits and multi-seed simulation protocol.
- Unusually transparent self-disclosure of what its comparisons do and do not establish (non-equivalence claims, non-causal ablations, block-level-only efficiency metrics).

## Limitations

- As the authors state directly: QuantWAMs "optimizes local surrogates rather than closed-loop performance and requires labels, one backward pass, FP16 rollouts, and schedule validation" — it is not a training-free, zero-overhead PTQ method in the traditional sense.
- Results are limited to two WAM implementations, benchmark-specific (in-distribution) calibration, and a single Blackwell hardware backend; transfer to unseen tasks, other WAM architectures, or other accelerators is not established.
- Reported speedup/memory gains are block-level (video+action DiT blocks only), explicitly not end-to-end control-loop or robot-cycle metrics.
- The real-robot study (10 trials/task, 30 total) is explicitly acknowledged as underpowered for a rigorous FP16 comparison, and shows a larger relative degradation (6.6pp) than any simulation benchmark.
- Causal attribution for the Joint saliency objective's improvement over post-hoc fusion (its central weight-precision claim) is explicitly not established by the ablation alone, per the authors' own statement.

## Relevance

该工作面向具身智能/机器人操作中日益重要的"世界动作模型"部署效率问题，其针对闭环、多流耦合场景设计的量化粒度校准思路（共享基底池化、联合目标显著度、真实回放调度审计）对关注扩散策略压缩、VLA/WAM高效部署及PTQ校准方法学的研究者有较高参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of the reported gain over Atom*/SVDQuant* would remain if those baselines were also given access to the co-training gradient and FP16 rollout replay signals, rather than only matched nominal precision budgets?
- Would the coordinate-admissibility grouping rule generalize to WAMs with additional modalities (e.g., language conditioning, depth, tactile) beyond the video-action coupling studied here?
- What is the sensitivity of the method to the calibration budget (currently fixed at 32 trajectories for all statistics), and how does performance degrade as this shrinks?
- Can the framework be validated on non-Blackwell/non-NVFP4 hardware to separate algorithm-level gains from kernel-specific speedups?
- What causes the larger real-world (6.6pp) versus simulation (0.2-0.7pp) gap to FP16 — sensor/actuation noise, sim-to-real distribution shift in the calibration rollouts, or something specific to the AgiBot G2 platform — and would a larger real-robot trial count change the picture?
- How does the fixed per-benchmark denoising-step protection schedule generalize to genuinely out-of-distribution tasks or objects not represented in the 32-trajectory calibration and profiling sets?
