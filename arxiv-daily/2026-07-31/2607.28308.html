Beyond Geometric Complementarity: Coherent Overlap in Sparse Mixture-of-Experts Routing
30 Jul 2026 · arXiv:2607.28308
Mixture-of-Experts Sparse Routing Expert-Subspace Geometry Interpretability MoE Pruning

Expert subspaces in six MoE LLMs overlap almost completely (ESSI≈0.97) — yet co-selecting experts still reliably improves next-token prediction.

一句话总结：通过新提出的专家子空间分离指数（ESSI）和前缀受控的2×2析因实验，作者在六个开源MoE模型上发现专家子空间在几何上高度重叠（ESSI中位数0.969），被选中专家个体确实更强，但实际共选上下文反而缩小而非放大这一优势；尽管如此，冻结路由干预与受控训练实验表明后续专家仍能带来真实的功能收益。

Why this matters

Cautions the MoE efficiency community that subspace geometric similarity shouldn't be treated as proof experts are redundant enough to safely prune or merge.

Critical edge

⚠The deeper causal tests (2×2 factorial + frozen-route NLL interventions) ran on only 3 of the 6 surveyed models — the newest, largest architectures (Qwen3, Gemma4, Qwen3.6) got just the surface geometry survey, so the central "coherent overlap" claim about functional value is inferred by analogy there, not directly demonstrated.

Methodology

Every analysis starts from router-input representations at each transformer layer, where the router picks an ordered Top-k set of experts per token. ESSI fits a rank-128 subspace per expert from real router inputs, then divides the Grassmann chordal distance between experts by each expert's own local tangent dispersion — a self-calibrated denominator that turns raw geometric distance into a meaningful separation score.

A prefix-controlled 2×2 factorial then holds four "actors" side by side at each route position: the real preceding context, the actually-selected expert, the strongest unselected rival, and several load-matched alternative contexts. Comparing how much residual representation energy each candidate explains isolates three previously-conflated claims — route coherence, candidate quality, and their interaction (positive geometric complementarity) — into one difference-in-differences statistic, D.

Two functional probes then test whether geometry tracks real value: frozen-route interventions add each later expert to a fixed prefix and measure the resulting drop in next-token NLL, while a compute-matched (within 0.04%) 6-layer training study compares Top-1 against Top-2 routing across three seeds. Estimates use up to 2,048 held-out tokens per cell, 1,000-resample paired bootstrap CIs, three sensitivity variants, and an independent CPU tall-SVD cross-check.

Results

Six models, one consistent pattern: expert subspaces sit almost on top of each other, real routes still fit tokens better than matched-random alternatives, and every selected candidate outguesses its strongest rival in isolation — but the moment that candidate is judged inside its true co-selection context, its edge over the rival shrinks, not grows, in all 39 tested cells.

Strengths

  • Splits three previously conflated concepts — coherence, candidate quality, complementarity — into a falsifiable, difference-in-differences design.
  • Consistent, robust findings across 6 architectures and 3 sensitivity variants (all preserving sign), not a single cherry-picked model or layer.
  • Directly actionable caution for MoE compression: geometric similarity should not be assumed to predict redundancy without an output-level counterfactual.
  • Transparent about scope — explicitly names the linear rank-128 metric, 3-architecture factorial coverage, and small-scale training study as boundaries of the claims.

Limitations

  • Deep causal analysis (factorial + NLL interventions) covers only OLMoE, Mixtral, DeepSeek-MoE — Qwen3, Gemma4, Qwen3.6 get only the geometry survey.
  • The Top-1-vs-Top-2 controlled training study uses a small custom 6-layer model, orders of magnitude below the pretrained checkpoints studied elsewhere.
  • The subspace metric is linear (rank-128 router-input projections) and can't capture nonlinear feature interactions or output-space effects.
  • No downstream task accuracy is reported — evaluation stops at next-token NLL/validation loss, so task-level implications are unclear.
  • Effect sizes (~0.026 advantage, ~−0.055 to −0.058 interaction) are numerically modest, with no stated threshold for practical pruning relevance.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Would the negative interaction and 24/39 NLL-recovery pattern replicate if the full factorial protocol were applied to Qwen3, Gemma4, and Qwen3.6?
  • Does the Top-2-over-Top-1 validation-loss advantage (Δ=0.1016±0.0025) persist at pretrained-model scale, or shrink as expert counts grow?
  • How would ESSI and the interaction term D behave under a nonlinear or output-space notion of "coverage"?
  • Would actual pruning/merging methods (SubMoE, Mosaic Pruning) show measurable degradation consistent with the "coherent overlap" caution if evaluated under this framework?
  • Is there a principled threshold for D or the residual ratio below which pruning becomes safe — or does coherent overlap rule out any such linear-geometry threshold?
