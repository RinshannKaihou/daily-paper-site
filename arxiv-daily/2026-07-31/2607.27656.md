> **一句话总结：** 论文发现"加性注入"式循环Transformer在每一步重复注入输入信号时，共享的循环变换在"锚点"（输入条件参考态）处会产生一个非零的强制偏置（forcing bias），这个偏置会随深度累积并可能损害深度外推性能；作者提出SCSE，通过让循环模块只作用于"锚点相对偏差"并用零偏差掩码强制锚点为精确的单步不动点，在WikiText-103上（95.6M参数）将测试困惑度从117.1（普通循环Transformer基线）降到96.9（T=8），深度外推到T=48时从145.1降到125.9，同时在多个更强的基线（tuned adapter、recurrent-step-conditioned adapter）上均取得最低困惑度。

> **TL;DR:** Repeated additive-source injection in looped Transformers creates a "zero-deviation forcing bias" at the input-conditioned anchor that can accumulate harmfully over recurrent depth. SCSE fixes a learned anchor once, evolves only the anchor-relative deviation through a zero-preserving recurrent core, and uses a per-example mask to make the anchor an exact one-step fixed point. On WikiText-103 (95.6M params), SCSE reaches 96.9±0.3 PPL at trained depth T=8 versus 117.1±0.9 for the looped-Transformer baseline and 98.6±0.5 for a tuned additive-injection adapter, and extends this advantage to 125.9±0.9 vs 145.1±1.2 (baseline) and 128.7±0.9 (tuned adapter) at extrapolated depth T=48; gains also hold on held-out OpenWebText/C4 transfer and LAMBADA completion.

## 核心贡献

- **诊断新问题：** 首次形式化定义"零偏差强制偏置" b_t(e) = T_t(0;e)——即共享循环变换作用在输入条件锚点（一步前无偏差参考态）上产生的响应。理论证明该偏置在有限步数内的任务损失效应可以是有害、中性或有益，取决于循环传播的收缩/膨胀性质与读出-损失对齐方向。
- **提出SCSE架构：** 只计算一次输入条件锚点 h* = e + a_ω(e)，此后循环核只对"锚点相对偏差"Δ_t = h_t - h* 进行演化，并施加零偏差掩码 m = 1{‖Δ_t‖²>ε} 使锚点在构造上成为循环更新的精确单步不动点，同时保留非零偏差处的有效状态相关计算。
- **消融确认关键因子：** 控制实验表明"学习到的锚点"与"锚点坐标偏差递归"是收益的主要来源，随机投影锚点或直接用嵌入/初始状态作锚点均明显更差（初始状态锚点会使偏差恒为零、循环分支完全失效）。
- **训练后模型的机制诊断：** 提出可在任意训练好的共享块模型上评估的"锚点响应"诊断（R_t(e) 强制偏置能量比），验证加性注入模型确实保留了随深度增长的非零锚点响应，而SCSE的该指标始终为0。
- **几何案例研究：** 通过WikiText-2上的锚点起始轨迹可视化和"注入增益"剂量-响应干预实验，证明源通道确实以强制对齐的方式驱动循环运动，进一步佐证了理论诊断的现实意义。

## Key Contributions

- Formalizes and measures the **zero-deviation forcing bias** b_t(e) := T_t(0;e) — the response the shared recurrent transition produces when evaluated exactly at an input-conditioned anchor — and proves (Theorem 2 + Corollaries 3–5, Proposition 6) that its finite-horizon effect on task loss can be harmful, neutral, or beneficial depending on recurrent propagation (contractive/coherent) and readout–loss alignment.
- Proposes **SCSE**: builds a learned anchor h* = e + a_ω(e) once per sequence, evolves only the anchor-relative deviation Δ_t = h_t − h* through a bias-free recurrent core G_θ (G_θ(0)=0 by RMSNorm/attention/MLP construction), and applies an exact per-example zero-deviation mask so the anchor is a provable one-step fixed point (T_t(0;e)=0) while nonzero deviations still drive computation.
- Runs an extensive **shared-block control suite** (capacity-matched adapters, tuned additive-injection adapters, recurrent-step-conditioned adapters à la Universal Transformer, unshared fixed-depth references) across WikiText-2/103, direct OpenWebText/C4 pretraining, held-out web-text transfer, and LAMBADA, at 22M/50M/95.6M/136.5M parameter scales and loop depths from 4 to 48 (train depths ≤8, extrapolation to 48).
- **Ablation study** isolates the learned anchor and anchor-coordinate deviation recurrence as the main drivers of SCSE's gains (vs. embedding anchor, frozen random-projection anchor, or initial-state anchor which collapses the recurrent branch entirely).
- Introduces a **trained-model anchor-response diagnostic** (pointwise forcing-bias energy ratio R_t(e)) applicable to any shared-block model, confirming empirically that additive-injection baselines retain measurable, growing pointwise anchor responses across scales while SCSE's is exactly zero, and complements this with an intervention-based case study (dose-response injection-gain test) showing the source channel steers recurrent motion in a forcing-aligned manner.

## Methodology

SCSE operates on the **full-state, shared-block looped Transformer** setting: a token-and-position embedding e = P_φ(x), an initial state h_0 = H_0(e), and a shared recurrent core R_θ applied for T steps, h_{t+1} = R_θ(h_t, e, t), read out via C_ψ(RMSNorm(h_T)).

Key construction (Eqs. 2–5):
1. **Anchor construction (once per sequence):** h* = e + a_ω(e), where a_ω is a learned anchor projection module applied to the token-and-position representation, held fixed across all recurrent steps.
2. **Deviation-only recurrence:** starting from Δ_0 = H_0(e) − h*, the model computes a raw source-centered update q_t = s·G_θ(Δ_t) (s = residual step scale, fixed at 0.50 in main experiments), where G_θ is the shared Transformer core (causal self-attention + SwiGLU, RMSNorm pre-normalized) acting purely on the deviation — no additive source re-injection.
3. **Zero-deviation mask:** a per-example gate m_{b,t} = 1{‖Δ_t‖²_F > ε} (ε=1e-8) zeroes the update exactly when the deviation is (numerically) zero, giving Δ_{t+1} = Δ_t + m_{b,t}·q_t. This guarantees T_t(0;e) = 0 exactly — the anchor is a one-step fixed point by construction — while leaving active-branch (nonzero-deviation) computation unconstrained.
4. **Final readout:** h_T = h* + Δ_T, logits from C_ψ(RMSNorm(h_T)).

Because RMSNorm and every attention/MLP projection in the core are bias-free, G_θ(0)=0 holds even without masking, so the mask supplies the exact pointwise boundary condition rather than being the sole reparameterization.

**Theoretical framework (appendix):** defines the exact decomposition T_t(Δ;e) = Δ + b_t(e) + a_t(Δ;e) separating the pointwise forcing bias from the state-dependent term, derives an exact finite-horizon counterfactual (subtracting the bias trajectory) via a secant-Jacobian propagation identity (Theorem 2), and gives sufficient conditions (contraction, coherent accumulation, adverse readout-loss alignment) under which a nonzero b_t(e) provably degrades task loss by at least μ·n·β (Proposition 6, Corollaries 3-5).

**Baselines/controls compared:** looped Transformer baseline (plain additive injection h_{t+1}=h_t+sB_θ(h_t+αW_in e)), capacity-matched adapter, tuned adapter (learned scalar gain α, tuned residual step s), recurrent-step-conditioned adapter (adds a projected sinusoidal loop-step embedding, à la Universal Transformer), Step-FiLM adapter, unshared fixed-depth Transformer-8 reference, and several forcing-bias intervention ablations (SC-Cond masked/unmasked, additive+subtractive forcing-subtraction controls, deviation-leak diagnostic).

## Results & Highlights

- **Main WikiText-103 comparison (95.6M params, Table 1/7):** SCSE reaches **96.9±0.3 PPL at T=8** vs looped-Transformer baseline **117.1±0.9**, tuned adapter (s=0.35) **98.6±0.5**, and recurrent-step-conditioned adapter **100.8±0.5**. At extrapolated depth **T=48**, SCSE stays lowest at **125.9±0.9** vs baseline **145.1±1.2**, tuned adapter **128.7±0.9**, recurrent-step-conditioned adapter **130.6±1.0**.
- **50M scale (Table 1):** SCSE 123.1±0.2 (T=8) → 156.4±1.1 (T=48), vs looped baseline 151.1±0.9 → 178.9±1.3, and recurrent-step-conditioned adapter 125.7±0.4 → 160.1±0.2 — SCSE lowest at every reported depth (T=8,16,24,48).
- **22M strong-baseline comparison (Table 5):** SCSE 155.1±0.6 (T=8) and 200.1±1.3 (T=48) vs tuned adapter 156.4±0.6 / 204.1±1.4 and looped baseline 179.8±0.8 / 218.0±0.8.
- **Anchor design ablation (Table 2, WikiText-103):** the learned anchor h*=e+a_ω(e) gives 155.14±0.61 PPL at T=8 vs 160.55±1.44 for a raw embedding anchor, 159.14±0.78 for a frozen random-projection anchor, and 294.37±1.24 for an initial-state anchor (h*=H_0(e)), which collapses the deviation recurrence entirely (flat PPL across depth).
- **Anchor-response diagnostic (Table 3):** at late recurrent step t=47, the pointwise forcing-bias energy ratio R_47 is **0.000 for SCSE at all three scales (22M/50M/95.6M)**, vs **4.351 / 4.296 / 5.436** for the looped-Transformer baseline and **1.276 / 0.264 / 0.894** for the recurrent-step-conditioned adapter — a direct empirical confirmation that additive-injection models retain a measurable, growing anchor-point response that SCSE removes by construction.
- **LAMBADA completion (95.6M, Table 16):** at comparison depth T=32, SCSE target-token completion PPL is **16.9±0.8k** with accuracy **3.42±0.16%**, vs looped baseline **23.0±0.3k / 1.60±0.12%** and tuned adapter **18.5±1.4k / 2.86±0.04%** — SCSE both lowest PPL and highest accuracy.
- **Held-out transfer (Appendix Table 15, C4, 95.6M weights):** SCSE reaches 2.62±0.04k PPL at T=8 vs looped baseline 2.85±0.01k and tuned adapter 2.68±0.08k; at T=48, SCSE 2.65±0.10k vs baseline 2.97±0.04k.
- **Accelerator profiling (Table 11, 95.6M):** SCSE achieves the best PPL at both measured depths while remaining latency-competitive: 96.4 PPL / 19.6 ms per batch at T=8 (vs looped baseline 117.3 PPL / 18.3 ms, tuned adapter 98.0 PPL / 20.0 ms), and 124.3 PPL / 89.9 ms at T=48 (vs recurrent-step-conditioned adapter 130.0 PPL / 100.1 ms) — quality gains come at essentially no runtime premium.
- Gains hold under adaptive-depth (loop-budgeted) inference (Tables 9-10), longer training budgets (Appendix Table 8, 95.6M 10k-step: SCSE 66.7±0.3 at T=8 vs recurrent-step-conditioned adapter 68.5±0.2, gap widens to 98.3±1.0 vs 103.4±0.8 at T=48), and longer contexts (512/1024 tokens, Table 12).
- One caveat noted by the authors: on C4 at larger scale (Appendix Table 18, 136.5M, 50k steps), the tuned adapter is actually slightly better than SCSE at T=8 (93.86 vs 94.11) and essentially tied at T=16, with SCSE's advantage emerging only at deeper extrapolated depths (T=32/48/96) — the paper is honest that SCSE is not uniformly best within the training loop-depth range in every setting.

## Authors & Credibility

All six authors (Bum Jun Kim, Kohei Hayashi, Shunsuke Kamiya, Masanori Koyama, Yusuke Iwasawa, Yutaka Matsuo) are affiliated with The University of Tokyo (Matsuo Lab, a well-known group for deep learning and world-model research). The work is methodologically careful: it includes exact theoretical propagation identities with full proofs in the appendix (Lemma 1, Theorem 2, Corollaries 3-5, Proposition 6), a very large empirical suite (5 datasets, 4 model scales from 22M to 139.2M parameters, loop depths 4-96, adaptive-depth and accelerator-profiling evaluations, ~20 appendix tables), and reports standard deviations over 3-5 stochastic seed replications with paired bootstrap confidence intervals for deltas — an unusually rigorous statistical treatment for an architecture paper. No code repository link is given in the excerpted pages, though a full PyTorch reference implementation (Listing 1) is included in the appendix.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core theoretical framing — identifying an "anchor-response forcing bias" as an architecturally exposed, previously unnamed degree of freedom in additive-injection looped Transformers — is a genuinely new and well-motivated diagnostic; the authors explicitly distinguish their contribution from related concurrent work on loop instability (Prairie et al. 2026's Parcae, Yang et al. 2026's spectral-radius regularization, Park et al. 2026's LoopUS gating, Sharma & Vu 2026's readout blind spot), noting correctly that none of these prior works define or measure b_t(e) directly. This is a legitimate, specific point of differentiation rather than a rebranding of existing ideas.

**Rigor:** Strong. The paper provides an exact (not approximate) finite-horizon counterfactual decomposition with full proofs, and backs every architectural claim with a matched-training-protocol ablation (capacity-matched adapters, tuned adapters, recurrent-step-conditioned adapters using the Universal Transformer mechanism) rather than only comparing against a weak looped-Transformer strawman. The recurrent-step-conditioned adapter in particular is a credible, non-trivial baseline (it becomes "essentially tied" with SCSE at 22M, T=8, per Table 1) and its inclusion shows the authors actively tried to falsify their own explanation for the gains.

**Reproducibility:** Good — hyperparameters (learning rate, warmup, weight decay, batch/accumulation schedule, tokenizer), FLOP accounting methodology, and a full PyTorch code listing (Listing 1, `scse_step`/`scse_unroll`) are given in the appendix. However, all experiments are on WikiText-2/103, OpenWebText, and C4 at ≤139.2M parameters — no code repository URL appears in the paper text itself (or none was found in the reviewed pages), which is a minor reproducibility gap for a paper this empirically dense.

**Significance / red flags:** The effect sizes are real but the paper studies a fairly narrow regime — small-to-medium LM pretraining (max 139.2M parameters, max 204.8M training tokens), explicitly acknowledged as a limitation ("Billion-parameter scaling-law behavior and billion-token web-corpus training therefore remain open"). The claimed advantage is also not perfectly uniform: Appendix Table 18 shows the tuned adapter matching or slightly beating SCSE at C4 scale within the training loop-depth range (T=8, T=16), with SCSE's edge concentrated at extrapolated depths (T>8) — a nuance the paper reports honestly rather than hiding, which is a mark in its favor for intellectual honesty, but it does slightly temper the "SCSE improves the recurrent quality frontier" headline claim. The paper is also explicit that the pointwise anchor-consistency condition is "not a general stability theorem" and "does not subsume the readout blind spot" identified by concurrent work — a fair and specific limitation rather than a vague disclaimer.

## Strengths

- Precise, provable characterization of a previously informally-observed phenomenon (drift/instability in looped Transformers) via an exact finite-horizon bias-subtraction identity, not just an empirical patch.
- Extensive, well-controlled baseline suite (capacity-matched, tuned, recurrent-step-conditioned, unshared-reference, and multiple forcing-bias intervention ablations) that actively tries to rule out confounds like extra parameters or explicit step conditioning as the source of SCSE's gains.
- Consistent improvements across five datasets, three-plus model scales, adaptive-depth inference, and depth extrapolation up to 6x the training loop-depth (T=48 vs trained ≤8), with essentially no runtime overhead per the accelerator profiling.
- Transparent reporting of a counter-example (C4 at 136.5M, Table 18) where SCSE is not the best method within the training depth range — increases trust in the rest of the reported numbers.

## Limitations

- Model scale capped at ~139M parameters and ~205M training tokens; no evidence yet at billion-parameter/billion-token scale, which the authors explicitly flag.
- The anchor-consistency (zero-deviation forcing bias = 0) condition is a design choice motivated by removing a specific failure mode, not a proof of global stability or optimality — the paper itself notes it does not constrain off-anchor Jacobians, the state-dependent residual, or general readout sensitivity, and does not resolve the separately identified "readout blind spot" (Sharma & Vu 2026).
- Geometric case study (Figure 4, WikiText-2) is a single-replication qualitative diagnostic rather than a statistically aggregated result.
- Mixed results at larger-scale C4 pretraining within the training loop-depth range (Table 18) suggest the benefit is most reliable at deep extrapolation rather than uniformly at all depths.

## Relevance

该论文对理解和改进循环/权重共享Transformer架构（"循环深度"作为推理时可调计算轴）有直接价值，其"锚点响应强制偏置"诊断和SCSE重参数化方法为构建更稳定的深度外推模型提供了一个理论清晰、实验扎实的设计原则，适合关注高效推理、测试时计算scaling和循环神经架构稳定性的研究方向参考。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the SCSE anchor-consistency condition still provide extrapolation benefits at billion-parameter scale, or does the mixed C4 result (Table 18) foreshadow diminishing returns as models grow?
- Can the learned anchor projection a_ω(e) be interpreted mechanistically — does it converge to something like a per-token "expected final representation," and would visualizing it across layers/depths reveal task-relevant structure?
- How does SCSE interact with adaptive/learned halting policies (rather than the fixed max-token stopping rule used here) — could combining the two yield further gains on example-difficulty-dependent compute allocation?
- The paper does not test SCSE combined with the recurrent-step-conditioning signal (Step-FiLM) as a single architecture — would the two mechanisms compose additively, or does the anchor invariance already subsume most of the benefit that step conditioning provides?
- Would the anchor-response diagnostic (b_t(e), R_t(e)) generalize usefully to other repeatedly-applied-module settings beyond looped Transformers, e.g., diffusion model denoisers or iterative refinement networks with shared weights?
