> **一句话总结：** 论文发现 Transformer 语义理解在中间层就已基本完成，据此提出 CoMem：写入阶段只算到第 j 层并缓存该层的残差张量，读取阶段用 BM25 检索固定数量的相关分块、拼接后只重算上层 [j:L]，从而让模型侧读取算力和显存与已存上下文长度解耦；在 Qwen3-8B 上 RULER 达到 97.05（对比全上下文 KV-Direct 的 78.80），LoCoMo 对话记忆 38.27（对比 34.59），128k 上下文预填充提速 7.83 倍且显存从 89.36GB 降到 18.26GB。
>
> **TL;DR:** CoMem caches a single mid-layer residual tensor per chunk (write depth j) and recomputes only the query-conditioned upper layers [j:L] over a bounded BM25-retrieved pack, decoupling model-side read compute/memory from stored-context length; on a frozen, continued-trained Qwen3-8B with only a rank-32 self-distillation LoRA, it scores 97.05 on RULER vs. 78.80 for full-context KV-Direct and 38.27 vs. 34.59 on LoCoMo, while cutting 128k prefill to 18.26 GB (7.83x faster) versus 89.36 GB for full-context — though the paper's own j=0 ablation shows the depth-caching mechanism itself costs accuracy (down to 24.52 without distillation) that self-distillation only partially recovers.

## 核心贡献

- **把深度当作可测量的"复用轴"：** 通过逐层探针和因果截断实验证明，语义信息在网络中低层就已基本可读取，越往上层的表示越"面向查询/预测"特化；这一现象在 Qwen3 0.6B–32B 上都存在，语义内容深度稳定在约 0.45L，但零样本"可读深度"随模型规模变深。
- **CoMem 架构：** WRITE 阶段每个 512-token 分块只跑到第 j 层（chunk-local RoPE 从 0 开始），缓存该层残差张量 h_j（每 token 一条，仅为 bf16 全量 KV 缓存字节数的 1/18）；READ 阶段用迭代 BM25（top-12，跳距 4，最多 3 轮扩展）检索有限分块集合，拼接 sink + 检索分块 + 当前分块后，用全交叉分块因果注意力只重算上层 [j:L]。
- **固定读取预算，与存储长度解耦：** 无论上下文多长，模型侧在线读取的计算量和显存占用都由检索包大小（约 6.5k tokens，含 k=12、chunk=512）决定，而非上下文总长度。
- **轻量自蒸馏修复深度截断带来的保真度损失：** 仅用 PG19 语料训练一个 rank-32 LoRA（4000 步，8×H20，约 2.9 GPU-小时），无需任何长上下文合成数据、任务监督或检索监督，就能把 j=12 冻结配置在 LoCoMo 上的分数从 24.52 拉到 38.27。
- **跨规模、跨架构的可迁移性：** 该方法在 Qwen3 0.6B–32B 全系列、以及两个稀疏 MoE 主干（腾讯 Hunyuan Hy3 的 80 层 MoE 和 Qwen3-30B-A3B）上都成立，包括在 256k 原生上下文上限下保持读取长度恒定在约 4.3–4.6k tokens。

## Key Contributions

- **Depth as a measurable reuse axis:** Layer-wise probes and causal-truncation experiments show semantic content becomes accessible near mid-depth (content-depth ≈0.45L, near scale-invariant from 0.6B to 32B), while upper layers increasingly specialize the representation for the current query/prediction — motivating a depth-partitioned memory design.
- **A bounded depth-partitioned memory (CoMem):** WRITE processes each 512-token chunk only through layers [0:j] (chunk-local RoPE) and caches one residual tensor h_j per token (1/18 the bytes of full bf16 KV per token on Qwen3-8B); READ retrieves a bounded top-k set via iterative BM25 and recomputes only the upper layers [j:L] over the assembled pack with full cross-chunk attention — making model-side read FLOPs and working memory independent of stored-context length for fixed k and chunk size.
- **Self-distillation to repair readout fidelity:** A rank-32 LoRA trained only on PG19 (bidirectional KL to a frozen j=0 teacher, no CE term, no task/retrieval supervision) pushes the usable split deeper (j=12 of 36 layers) and substantially closes the accuracy gap introduced by shallow caching.
- **Controlled quality/efficiency evidence across five benchmarks:** Under a single chat-template-free Qwen3-8B evaluation protocol, CoMem reaches 97.05 (RULER) and 38.27 (LoCoMo) vs. 78.80/34.59 for full-context KV-Direct; a same-H20 adapter-free control shows 18.26 GB vs. 89.36 GB peak memory and 7.83x prefill speedup at 128k.
- **Generality across scale and MoE backbones:** Controlled depth sweeps and ablations (retrieval breadth, chunk interaction, cross-scale transfer, MoE routing) isolate the separate contributions of retrieval, depth reuse, and distillation, and the method transfers to two sparse-MoE backbones (Hunyuan Hy3, Qwen3-30B-A3B) without architectural changes.

## Methodology

CoMem partitions a frozen decoder along the **depth axis** rather than the token axis (Figure 4):

1. **WRITE (streaming, once per context):** the document is split into disjoint 512-token chunks; each chunk is run through layers [0:j] only, with chunk-local RoPE restarting at position 0, and the resulting residual tensor h_j ∈ ℝ^(c×d) is cached, keyed by chunk text. Write cost is linear in context length but only executes j of L layers.
2. **SELECT:** a bounded lexical retriever (iterative BM25, top-k=12, hop width 4, up to 3 automatic frontier-expansion rounds with deduplication) chooses a fixed-size subset of relevant chunk tensors per query — analysis-only controls (oracle, recency, reader-attention) are also studied.
3. **READ (per query):** the selected chunk tensors are ordered by document position and packed as [BOS sink; selected h_j's; fresh h_j(query)] with contiguous RoPE; only layers [j:L] are then run over this pack with full causal (cross-chunk) attention, producing next-token logits. Pack length is fixed at |pack| = sink + k·c + query ≈ 6.7k tokens for k=12, c=512, independent of how much context has been written.
4. **Split depth j as a tunable knob:** j=0 reduces to a selective full forward (matches the stock forward within max|Δlogit|<1e-4); j=L means no upper-layer replay at all. The flagship uses j=12 of 36 layers on Qwen3-8B.
5. **Self-distillation:** a rank-32 LoRA (α=64, dropout 0, applied to Q/K/V/O and gate/up/down projections in layers 12–35, 58.20M trainable params, 0.71% of backbone) is trained so the j=12 student matches a frozen j=0 teacher's renormalized top-64 logit distribution via bidirectional KL: L = 0.6·KL(p‖q) + 0.4·KL(q‖p), no cross-entropy term. Training uses 4,000 steps of 8-GPU bf16 DDP (effective batch 8), AdamW at peak LR 8e-5, 100 warmup steps, cosine decay, on 2,048-token windows drawn purely from the PG-19 training split — no synthetic long-context examples, task supervision, retrieval supervision, or benchmark-specific data.
6. **Backbone and baselines:** the base model is a continued-trained, frozen Qwen3-8B (36 layers, native 40,960-token window, no YaRN extension used in the main comparisons). Baselines under the same unified, chat-template-free protocol include KV-Direct (full-context, deterministic residual-stream reconstruction of the KV cache — described by the authors as an "unextended full-context stress reference," not a length-extended upper bound), an HCache-style reproduction (mid-layer caching + recompute, but untrained and without retrieval), StreamingLLM, InfLLM, and an out-of-backbone MemoryLLM (Llama-3-8B-chat) reference.

## Results & Highlights

- **RULER (15-cell macro, unified plain-text protocol):** CoMem+LoRA (j=12) scores **97.05**, versus full-context KV-Direct **78.80**, InfLLM 77.83, StreamingLLM 23.37, and the frozen (no self-distillation) CoMem control 59.41 (Table 1). A same-H20 chat-template-free sweep (Table 15) shows CoMem holding niah_single/multikey/var-track ≥91 through 128k while StreamingLLM collapses to 0.8/2.0 by 64k–128k.
- **LoCoMo dialogue memory (1,986 questions, GPT-4o Judge as primary metric):** CoMem+LoRA reaches **38.27** vs. **34.59** for KV-Direct, 25.63 for StreamingLLM, and 22.21 for InfLLM (Table 1/2). On the 1,540 paired answerable items, the +4.81-point CoMem-vs-KV-Direct gap has a 95% item-bootstrap CI of [2.34, 7.27]; a 10-conversation cluster bootstrap keeps 8/10 conversations favoring CoMem; and a stratified 200-item independent DeepSeek-V3 audit preserves the ordering (κ=0.626 agreement with GPT-4o judge: 56.0/49.0 vs. GPT-4o's 36.5/32.5).
- **End-to-end efficiency at 128k (Qwen3-8B, bf16, SDPA, single H20 GPU, Table 3):** full-write-inclusive prefill drops from 15.014 s / 89.36 GB (full-context) to 1.917 s / 18.26 GB (CoMem) — a **7.83x speedup** with roughly 4.9x less peak memory; resumed-band cached decode is 26.39 ms/tok vs. 28.14 ms/tok (1.07x) at 17.9 GB vs. 69.1 GB.
- **Where CoMem does *not* win:** BABILong macro is **50.43** for CoMem vs. **63.00** for KV-Direct (Table 1) — full-context recall inside the native window remains stronger for BABILong's compositional QA, and BABILong qa2 specifically stays weak (27.0 for distilled CoMem, an explicit limitation the authors flag). LongBench macro F1 is essentially matched rather than beaten: 12.15 (CoMem) vs. 12.17 (KV-Direct).
- **Mechanism decomposition (Table 8):** a j=0 control (full recompute over the *retrieved* pack, i.e., retrieval alone with zero depth-reuse) reaches **41.59** on LoCoMo — higher than the final distilled j=12 CoMem (38.27) — while the frozen (undistilled) j=12 configuration falls to 24.52. This shows depth-caching itself introduces a real fidelity cost (41.59 → 24.52) that self-distillation only partially recovers (→38.27), and that part of CoMem's advantage over KV-Direct comes from bounded relevance retrieval rather than the depth-reuse mechanism per se.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core empirical claim — that semantic content is accessible by mid-depth while upper layers specialize for prediction — builds directly on prior "stages of inference" work (Lad et al. 2024) and is not itself new. The closest prior architecture, HCache (Gao et al., 2025), already caches a mid-layer activation and recomputes layers above it. CoMem's actual contribution is the combination of depth-partitioned caching with bounded external (BM25) retrieval and a lightweight self-distillation LoRA that makes deeper, more compressive splits usable — an incremental but well-executed combination rather than a fundamentally new mechanism.

**Rigor — a genuinely useful self-critique buried in the ablations:** Table 8 shows the depth-reuse mechanism itself is *not* accuracy-neutral: a full-recompute-over-retrieved-pack control (j=0) scores 41.59 on LoCoMo, higher than the flagship distilled CoMem (38.27), while the untrained j=12 configuration drops to 24.52. In other words, self-distillation is doing real repair work to close a gap the depth-caching design itself creates, and even after repair, CoMem's headline LoCoMo number is still below its own zero-depth-reuse ablation. The paper is commendably transparent about this ("Depth [is] a quality-cost knob rather than a free accuracy gain"), but a reader focused only on Table 1's headline comparison against KV-Direct could miss that the efficiency win and the accuracy win are not both attributable to the same design choice.

**Baseline concerns:** (1) KV-Direct — the strongest same-backbone baseline — is explicitly run without YaRN extension beyond Qwen3-8B's native 40,960-token window, so 64k/128k comparisons against it are against a model the authors themselves call an "unextended full-context stress reference," not a length-extended upper bound; the paper is upfront about this but it does inflate the visual gap in Figure 1 and Table 15 at long lengths. (2) The HCache-style baseline (RULER 3.73, LoCoMo 8.11 in Table 1) is, by the authors' own description, "post-hoc, untrained, and without retrieval" — i.e., deliberately missing two of CoMem's three ingredients. Table 11 confirms this: applying CoMem's LoRA to the same HCache-style path lifts its LoCoMo score from 13.29 to 31.17, showing much of HCache's apparent weakness in Table 1 is a training/retrieval gap rather than an architectural one, which makes the Table 1 HCache-style row a somewhat weak/unfair comparator as presented. (3) MemoryLLM is evaluated on a different backbone (Llama-3-8B-chat) without its native chat template, and the authors correctly flag it as an out-of-backbone diagnostic reference rather than a fair comparison — good practice, but it means 2 of the paper's 6 baselines in Table 1 are not really apples-to-apples.

**Reproducibility:** Strong — hyperparameters, training budget (4,000 steps, 8×H20, ~22 minutes, 2.9 GPU-hours), evaluation protocols, and licensing/data-scope details are documented exhaustively in the appendix (Tables 19–21), and code/configs are archived. The evaluation explicitly disables chat templates for a "unified" comparison across methods, which is a reasonable normalization choice but also means none of the reported numbers reflect each model's best possible (chat-template-tuned) performance — Table 22 shows this matters (e.g., a 14B model's no-chat score is *worse* than expected due to long CoT trajectories eating the generation budget), so the chosen protocol is a deliberate but non-trivial design decision that affects absolute numbers.

**Significance:** The 7.83x prefill speedup and ~5x memory reduction at 128k are compelling and hardware-verified (median of three runs, correctness gates reported), and the cross-scale (0.6B–32B) and cross-architecture (two sparse MoE backbones) generality checks are a genuine strength. However, the method remains a bounded-read *efficiency* technique, not a new accuracy ceiling — the paper's own conclusion states this directly, and BABILong qa2 (27.0) and LongBench (essentially tied, not exceeded) show the approach does not dominate uniformly.

## Strengths

- Clean, falsifiable mechanistic motivation (layer-wise probes + causal truncation) that is then converted into a working system rather than left as an observation.
- Unusually thorough ablation suite: split-depth sweeps (Table 8/9/10), retrieval-selector comparison (oracle/BM25/recency/reader-attention, Table 12), chunk-interaction ablation (full vs. block-diagonal cross-chunk attention, Table 6), and cross-scale (0.6B–32B) and cross-architecture (two sparse MoE backbones) generality checks.
- Statistical care on LoCoMo: item-level bootstrap CIs, a conversation-cluster bootstrap to address the small-N (10 conversations) dependence structure, and an independent DeepSeek-V3 judge audit for ordering robustness.
- Self-distillation requires no benchmark-specific data or supervision (plain PG-19 only), which supports the claim that the depth split is a general interface property rather than overfit to the evaluated tasks.
- Honest, specific Limitations section (BABILong qa2 weakness, unextended baselines, English-only, lexical-retrieval-only, unmeasured retrieval latency on natural multi-hop workloads) rather than a generic disclaimer.

## Limitations

- Not an accuracy ceiling: full context is explicitly stated to remain a strong reference "whenever it fits," and BABILong qa2 (27.0) stays weak; LongBench macro F1 is matched (12.15 vs. 12.17) rather than improved.
- Qwen3-8B is evaluated only within/just beyond its native 40,960-token window without YaRN; results above that window are "unextended stress references," not evidence of superiority over a properly length-extended full-context model.
- Retrieval is lexical only (BM25); the authors note dense/learned retrievers, dynamic index updates, deletions, and continually changing memory streams are out of scope, and relevant passages sharing little surface form with the query may be missed.
- Full cross-chunk attention (needed for the reported accuracy) increases read compute relative to block-diagonal reuse; retrieval latency on natural (non-synthetic) multi-hop workloads is unmeasured.
- Evaluation is English-only and retrieval-style QA-focused; long-form generation, code, multilingual, and multimodal memory are untested.
- Depth-caching itself has a measurable, only partially-repaired accuracy cost relative to full recompute over the same retrieved pack (Table 8's j=0 vs. j=12 comparison), meaning part of the "efficiency-for-free" framing needs qualification.

## Relevance

对做长上下文推理效率、KV-cache 压缩或检索增强记忆系统的研究者有直接参考价值：CoMem 提供了一条与主流"沿 token 轴压缩"正交的思路（沿深度轴复用），且开源、复现细节详尽、消融充分，但其自曝的 j=0 对照（41.59 vs 蒸馏后 38.27）说明深度复用本身有精度代价，评估时不应只看 Table 1 头条数字。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How would CoMem compare against a properly YaRN-extended (or otherwise genuinely length-extended) full-context Qwen3-8B baseline at 64k–128k, rather than the "unextended stress reference" KV-Direct used here?
- Given that the j=0 (full-recompute, retrieval-only) ablation outperforms the final distilled j=12 CoMem on LoCoMo (41.59 vs. 38.27), is there a sweet-spot split depth (or adaptive per-chunk depth) that recovers more of that gap while retaining most of the efficiency gain?
- Would a dense/learned retriever close the BABILong qa2 gap (27.0) or the BABILong-vs-KV-Direct macro gap (50.43 vs. 63.00), which the authors attribute partly to selection rather than fidelity?
- How does end-to-end latency (including the CPU-side BM25 retrieval step) scale on real, non-synthetic multi-hop conversational workloads, which the paper explicitly leaves unmeasured?
- Does applying CoMem's self-distillation recipe to the HCache-style baseline more fully (matching retrieval, not just readout) close the remaining gap seen in Table 11, and would that change the relative ranking of "depth caching" as a technique versus "depth caching + retrieval + distillation" as a system?
