> **一句话总结：** RepBench 从 13,427 篇基准论文中提炼出 182 类能力分类体系，再用 353 个公开基准、46,149 条经审计探针文本构建"每种能力至少两个独立基准支撑"的跨基准语料，在留一基准法（LOBO）迁移评测下发现：在 94 项能力 × 12 个模型共 1,128 个格子上，免训练的 diff-mean 平均 AUC 最高（0.778，优于逻辑回归 0.769、PCA 0.734 和 J-Lens 0.650），但逻辑回归反而以 38% 的格子胜率超过 diff-mean 的 30%，说明表征读出方法之间并无统一赢家。

> **TL;DR:** RepBench builds a benchmark-grounded data layer for capability probing—353 public datasets, 46,149 audited probe texts, 94 capabilities each backed by ≥2 independent benchmarks—derived from a taxonomy mined out of 13,427 benchmark papers. Under a leave-one-benchmark-out (LOBO) cross-benchmark transfer protocol on 12 open-weight LLMs, the training-free diff-mean baseline attains the highest grand-mean AUC (0.778) over 1,128 capability×model cells versus logistic regression (0.769), PCA (0.734), and J-Lens (0.650), yet logistic regression instead wins the plurality of individual cells (38% vs. diff-mean's 30%)—showing that readout method and aggregation criterion are both consequential, non-interchangeable evaluation choices.

## 核心贡献

- **闭环数据流水线**：爬取 13,427 篇基准论文，抽取 14,896 条能力表述，去重为 9,576 个概念，聚类为 182 个能力簇（13 大类）；再从中筛出可用纯文本探测的 94 项能力，抓取 353 个公开数据集、46,149 条探针文本，并强制每项能力至少由 2 个独立基准支撑（中位数 3 个，最多 21 个）。
- **两阶段语义审计**：用跨模型表征一致性作为诊断信号，优先人工复核有争议的"数据集→能力"映射，最终从 378 条映射中剔除 25 条（6.6%）不符合声称能力的映射，并将审计日志（64 行）随语料一并开源。
- **基准均衡的能力向量构建**：先在每个基准内部对探针文本取平均，再对基准间取等权平均（式 1），避免大数据集主导表征方向，得到"跨基准去噪"的能力向量。
- **模型×方法的统一评测协议（LOBO）**：在 12 个开源模型、4 种读出方法（diff-mean、PCA、逻辑回归、J-Lens）上做留一基准法评测，发现读出方法之间互有优劣、没有绝对赢家；同时发现原始逐文本表征没有自然聚类结构，而按能力池化后的向量在所有 12 个模型上都出现 k=4–15 的聚类拐点，但与人工设计的 13 大类分类体系吻合度很低（ARI 仅 0.05–0.13）。

## Key Contributions

- **A reusable closed-loop pipeline and corpus**: 13,427 crawled benchmark papers → 14,896 capability mentions → 9,576 deduplicated concepts → a 182-cluster/13-family taxonomy; from this, 94 text-probeable capabilities are backed by 353 harvested datasets and 46,149 audited probe texts, each capability supported by ≥2 independent benchmarks (median 3, max 21).
- **A two-stage semantic audit**: cross-model representation consistency is used as a prioritization diagnostic (not an automatic filter) for human review of dataset→capability mappings; 25 of 378 mappings (6.6%) are removed as invalid, with the full 64-row audit log released for traceability.
- **Benchmark-balanced capability vectors**: averaging first within-benchmark, then across benchmarks with equal weight (Eq. 1), which prevents any single large dataset from dominating a capability's representation.
- **A model × method evaluation under one protocol (LOBO)**: four readouts (diff-mean, PCA, logistic regression, J-Lens) are compared across twelve open-weight models (0.6B–32B, plus a 275B-scale MoE base model) under leave-one-benchmark-out cross-benchmark transfer, revealing that different readouts and aggregation criteria (mean vs. per-cell win share) crown different "winners"—there is no single best method.
- **A structural finding about capability geometry**: raw per-text hidden-state vectors show no interior clustering optimum, but capability-pooled vectors show a consistent interior optimum at k=4–15 clusters across all 12 models, with low agreement (ARI 0.05–0.13) to the human-authored 13-family taxonomy—i.e., models' internal organization of capabilities does not mirror how benchmark authors categorize them.

## Methodology

RepBench is explicitly a **data-layer** contribution, not a new probing/steering algorithm. The pipeline has five stages: (1) crawl 13,427 benchmark papers and extract stated capability targets via LLM annotation; (2) embed capability names/definitions (Qwen3-Embedding-8B), merge near-duplicates via a 15-nearest-neighbor cosine graph (similarity ≥0.90), and run global weighted k-means (k=200) followed by LLM-refined naming/merging/auditing to produce the 182-cluster/13-family taxonomy; (3) restrict to the 94 clusters expressible via plain text (excluding clusters that are >40–50% multimodal or tool/agent-dependent — Appendix D gives the exact thresholds), then harvest matching public datasets and convert them to probe texts via heuristic schema mapping; (4) run the semantic audit described above; (5) evaluate readouts under the LOBO protocol.

For capability *c* with benchmark set *B_c*, the capability vector is v_c = normalize(Σ_b (1/|X_{c,b}|) Σ_x h_ℓ(x)) — averaged within each benchmark *b*, then equally across benchmarks — using standardized last-token hidden states h_ℓ(x) at fractional depths (25/50/75/100%).

Four readout methods are compared, each summarized at its own best-observed valid depth:
- **Diff-mean**: unit-normalized difference in means between positive and negative training activations (training-free).
- **PCA**: top-10 principal components of pooled training activations, sign-oriented per LOBO fold by training AUC.
- **LR**: L2-regularized logistic regression (C=0.1) on the same training split (supervised baseline).
- **J-Lens**: a per-model, per-checkpoint lens using frozen whole-token capability verbalizers (3–8 preregistered English verbalizers per capability, filtered to single-token encodings), fit from three prespecified *source* depths (25/50/75%) to the final residual-stream *target*; compared separately because its source representation must precede the target.
- **SAE-AUC** (Gemma-2-9B-IT only, Appendix G): a single Gemma Scope 16k-width residual-stream feature, selected per LOBO fold using only training benchmarks (no held-out data used for feature/orientation selection), evaluated because Gemma Scope dictionaries are only checkpoint/layer-aligned for this one model.

**LOBO protocol**: for capability *c*, positives are probe texts from held-out benchmarks (held out one benchmark from *B_c* at a time); negatives are texts sharing no capability with *c*, capped at 30 per dataset (~6× the positive count) and split into disjoint train/test halves. The direction is fit on positives from the remaining benchmarks plus training negatives, then scored by AUC against held-out positives vs. disjoint test negatives; the per-capability score is the median across folds. The paper notes that *within-split* (non-held-out-benchmark) evaluation reaches AUC near 1.0 almost everywhere because a direction can exploit dataset-specific formatting/token statistics — hence LOBO is used as the reported measure and within-split numbers are treated only as an upper bound.

Twelve models are evaluated: Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B/4B/8B/32B, Qwen3.5-9B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Gemma-2-9B-IT, Gemma-4-12B, Gemma-4-31B, R1-Distill-Qwen3-8B (Qwen3-8B distilled on R1 reasoning traces — a controlled post-training contrast against its own base), and DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Base (a 275B-scale fp8 MoE base model probed without a chat template).

## Results & Highlights

- **Grand-mean AUC over all 1,128 capability×model cells (94 capabilities × 12 models)**: Diff-mean 0.778 > LR 0.769 > PCA 0.734 > J-Lens 0.650 (Table 3, Fig. 4b). Diff-mean has the highest mean on 10 of 12 individual models.
- **Share of per-cell wins** (Fig. 4c): LR wins the most individual capability-model cells at 38%, vs. Diff-mean 30%, PCA 17%, J-Lens 15% — despite Diff-mean having the higher aggregate mean, LR is the single most frequent per-cell winner, illustrating that "best on average" and "best most often" disagree.
- **SAE readout comparison (Table 4, Gemma-2-9B-IT, layer L21)**: the checkpoint-aligned single-feature SAE readout scores 0.678 mean LOBO-AUC, trailing all three activation-space baselines evaluated on the same layer (Diff-mean 0.779, LR 0.775, PCA 0.729) — this comparison is confined to one model/layer because Gemma Scope dictionaries are not available checkpoint-aligned for the other 11 models.
- **Post-training contrast (Table 3)**: on the same architecture, Qwen3-8B's Diff-mean AUC (0.785) drops to 0.732 for R1-Distill-Qwen3-8B (a ~6.7% relative decline), and PCA and J-Lens likewise decline (0.746→0.699, 0.643→0.616), while LR stays nearly flat (0.757→0.754) — evidence that distillation/post-training affects readouts unevenly.
- **Clustering geometry**: raw per-text hidden-state vectors show no interior silhouette optimum on any of the 12 models (quality climbs monotonically with cluster count), whereas the 94 benchmark-pooled capability vectors show a consistent interior optimum at k=4–15 clusters across all 12 models — but Adjusted Rand Index against the 13 human-authored families is only 0.05–0.13 (near-chance agreement), reported as a genuine structural finding rather than a taxonomy validation.
- **Audit yield**: of 378 crawled dataset→capability mappings, the two-stage audit removed 25 (6.6%) as not measuring the claimed construct (Table 5 lists each, e.g. "persona consistency and simulation" losing two mismatched datasets by 9/10 and 9/9 votes).
- **Capability-level dispersion**: 30 of 94 capabilities are "strongly readable" (Diff-mean AUC ≥0.8) on every one of the 12 models; 4 capabilities are weak (AUC <0.65) on every model — contamination-robust generalization (mean 0.35), prompt-and-context robustness, faithful condensed summarization, and argumentative reasoning and persuasion (mean LOBO-AUC 0.26, the lowest reported capability score in the appendix listing).
- **Cross-model stability**: pairwise Spearman rank correlation of the 94 diff-mean capability scores across the 12 models ranges 0.62–0.92 (median 0.82), i.e. "readability is a property of the data as much as of the model."
- **External convergence check (Table 2, not a head-to-head baseline)**: RepBench's own LOBO-AUC for related constructs sits near independently reported task-specific numbers on different protocols, e.g. AI-text-authenticity/stylometric-robustness reaches 0.96 in RepBench vs. RepreGuard's reported 0.9492 average AUROC (0.9480 on Llama-3.1-8B); social-harm/toxicity detection reaches RepBench's cluster-level readability vs. Bai et al.'s reported 0.94±0.02 AUROC with ten harmful examples.

## Authors & Credibility

All four authors (Yanshi Li, Xueru Bai — equal contribution — Shuman Liu, Long Zhang) are affiliated with Shopee (Sea Limited's e-commerce arm), based in Shanghai and Beijing. This is an industry-lab paper rather than output from an established academic interpretability group; the authors do not appear to have a prior widely-cited track record in representation engineering based on the paper's own citation list (no self-citations to prior work by these authors). The paper is a July 2026 arXiv preprint with no indication of peer review yet. Credibility rests on the scale and transparency of the empirical effort (13,427 papers crawled, 353 datasets audited, 12 models × 4 methods evaluated, full audit log and construction protocol released) rather than on institutional reputation.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The core idea — building a benchmark-grounded, multi-source probe corpus instead of paper-specific synthetic contrast sets — targets a real and under-addressed reproducibility gap in representation engineering (the related-work section documents that "almost all" prior directional-reading methods extract directions from data synthesized for the occasion). The taxonomy-mining-from-literature approach and the benchmark-balanced pooling estimator (Eq. 1) are genuinely new infrastructure, not just a new dataset.

**Rigor**: The LOBO protocol is a meaningfully stronger test than within-source splits, and the paper is explicit that within-split AUC is inflated ("close to 1.0 almost everywhere") because it exploits dataset fingerprints — this self-critical framing is a good sign. The two-stage audit with a documented 6.6% removal rate, and the release of the full audit log (Table 5, 64 rows), is a concrete, checkable rigor artifact rather than an unverifiable claim.

**Reproducibility**: Corpus, pipeline, and evaluation code are stated as released. The taxonomy construction, however, is itself LLM-mediated at multiple stages (embedding-based clustering, LLM cluster naming/refinement, LLM-assisted mapping and consistency diagnostics), with human review reserved for flagged/ambiguous cases only — this means the reported taxonomy and audit outcomes are not fully independent of the judge LLM's own biases, a limitation the paper does not deeply interrogate (it notes the audit is "evidence about a mapping, not about the intrinsic quality of a dataset" but doesn't address judge-model bias directly).

**Significance / paper-specific weak points**:
- The SAE baseline (arguably the most "modern" readout family being compared) is evaluated on exactly one model and one layer (Gemma-2-9B-IT, L21) because Gemma Scope dictionaries aren't checkpoint-aligned for the other 11 models, including both Gemma-4 variants in the main grid. The paper is upfront about this ("we do not extrapolate this result to the full model pool") but it means the headline "SAE trails the three baselines" claim rests on n=1 model, not a general finding.
- DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Base is probed "without a chat template" as a base-model contrast, and it also has the lowest Diff-mean AUC of all 12 models (0.720, Table 3) — the paper doesn't disentangle whether this reflects the base/no-instruction-tuning confound versus genuine model-capability differences, since it's the only base model in the grid architecturally distinct (four-stream mHC residual architecture) from the rest.
- The 94-capability text-only subset is a substantial narrowing from the full 182-cluster taxonomy — 88 clusters (48%), including all 31 multimodal-grounding and all 23 planning/tool-use clusters, get zero coverage. The paper frames this honestly as "a measured coverage gap" rather than hiding it, but it does mean the empirical claims (readout comparison, clustering geometry) apply only to the text-probeable half of what the field's benchmarks actually measure.
- No causal/steering evaluation is performed despite the paper's motivating frame ("representation engineering reads and steers..."); RepBench only evaluates reading/probing (AUC-based transfer), leaving the steering half of the motivation untested — the paper acknowledges this explicitly as future work rather than overclaiming it.
- The negative-sampling cap ("at most 30 per dataset") and the last-token-only activation choice are stated as fixed protocol decisions without ablation against alternatives (e.g., mean-pooled activations, different negative ratios), so it's unclear how sensitive the AUC rankings are to these specific hyperparameters.

Overall this reads as an honest, infrastructure-focused paper: it does not claim a new SOTA readout method, explicitly reports disagreement between aggregate and per-cell views, and flags its own coverage/generalization limits rather than papering over them.

## Strengths

- Directly targets a documented reproducibility problem (paper-specific synthetic probes) with a benchmark-grounded, multi-source alternative, at genuinely large scale (13,427 papers, 353 datasets, 46,149 texts).
- Transparent audit process with a concrete, quantified removal rate (25/378, 6.6%) and a released audit log, rather than an opaque "we manually verified everything" claim.
- The LOBO cross-benchmark transfer protocol is demonstrably harder than within-split evaluation (the paper shows the latter saturates near AUC=1.0), making the reported numbers more meaningful as a capability-generalization measure.
- Evaluates 12 architecturally and scale-diverse models (0.6B to ~275B-MoE) with 4 readout families under one shared protocol, and reports both grand-mean and per-cell-win aggregate views rather than picking whichever view flatters a preferred method.
- Reports failure modes candidly: 4 universally-weak capabilities, low ARI between discovered and human taxonomy, and single-model-only SAE evaluation — all stated as limitations rather than omitted.

## Limitations

- Text-only probing covers only 94 of 182 taxonomy clusters (~52%); all multimodal-grounding (31 clusters) and planning/tool-use (23 clusters) constructs are entirely excluded, so conclusions don't extend to those capability families.
- SAE-based readout comparison is limited to a single model/layer (Gemma-2-9B-IT, L21) due to Gemma Scope dictionary availability, preventing generalization of the "SAE trails other readouts" finding.
- Taxonomy and mapping construction depend heavily on LLM judgments (embedding clustering + LLM naming/refinement + LLM-prioritized audit), with human review only for flagged/ambiguous cases, introducing potential judge-model bias not directly assessed.
- Only last-token activations at four fractional depths are evaluated; no ablation of pooling strategy, negative-sampling ratio (fixed at ≤30/dataset), or alternative token positions.
- No steering/causal-intervention evaluation despite motivating framing around both "reading and steering"; the contribution is restricted to the reading/probing side.
- The one base (non-instruction-tuned) model in the grid, DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Base, also has an architecturally distinct residual-stream design and the lowest overall AUC, confounding "no chat template" with "different architecture" as explanations for its weaker readability.

## Relevance

该论文提供了一个可复用的、基准溯源的能力探测语料与评测协议，对做表征工程、可解释性读出方法比较、或需要"标准化能力探针数据"的研究非常有参考价值；其审计流程和跨基准迁移协议的设计思路也可迁移到其他需要减少数据特异性偏差的评测场景。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the readout ranking (Diff-mean vs. LR vs. PCA vs. J-Lens) change if negative sampling used a different cap or a capability-frequency-weighted scheme instead of the fixed ≤30-per-dataset rule?
- Does the low ARI (0.05–0.13) between model-discovered pooled clusters and the human taxonomy hold up under alternative clustering algorithms (e.g., hierarchical or density-based), or is k-means specifically prone to finding a different partition?
- How much of DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Base's lower Diff-mean AUC (0.720, lowest of 12 models) is attributable to lacking instruction-tuning versus its distinct four-stream mHC residual architecture?
- Would extending the corpus to multimodal and agentic/tool-use capabilities (the 88 uncovered clusters) preserve the same interior-clustering-optimum and low-ARI findings, or are those specific to text-only constructs?
- Since SAE-AUC trails other readouts only on one model/layer, would a multi-feature (rather than single-feature) SAE readout close the gap to Diff-mean/LR under the same LOBO protocol, as the paper itself flags as unaddressed?
