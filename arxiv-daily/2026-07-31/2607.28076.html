Agentic RL · Self-Distillation · arXiv 2607.28076 · 30 Jul 2026

Reflecting on its own verified rollouts, a policy beats GRPO and skill-distillation baselines by up to 8 points on unseen tasks.

一句话总结：GRSD让策略在GRPO采样的同一组rollout上对自己已验证的成功/失败轨迹做结构化反思，再用stop-gradient快照对比成功组与失败组反思、合成群体级DO/AVOID指导，并据此把verifier给出的轨迹级优势重塑为不改变符号的回合级优势；在ALFWorld/Search-based QA/WebShop三个环境、Qwen3-1.7B/Qwen2.5-3B/7B三种骨干上，平均比GRPO在ALFWorld提升5.5%、Search-QA提升2.7%、WebShop成功率提升7.2%，在ALFWorld未见任务集上比更强的技能蒸馏基线SDAR高6.0%（83.6% vs 77.6%），且额外训练开销只比GRPO高17%、推理零开销。

Largest per-task gains: Pick2 (+23.5 pts over GRPO, +11.7 over SDAR) and Cool (+14.3 over GRPO, +9.5 over SDAR). Averaged across 3 backbones on the main splits, GRSD beats GRPO by 5.5% (ALFWorld), 2.7% (Search-QA), and 7.2% (WebShop success).

Why This Matters

Shows agentic RL can build strong per-prompt teaching signal from a policy's own experience alone, without an external model that could exceed its capability.

中文原文（Relevance）：该论文与agentic RL、on-policy self-distillation、多轮回合级信用分配等主题高度相关，其"策略自身反思+成功失败对比构造群体指导"的思路对当前用GRPO/RLVR训练工具调用agent、又想避免依赖外部更强模型生成技能库的研究方向有直接借鉴价值。

▲ Critical Edge

The "no external model" framing only covers guidance content — GRSD still depends on an external judge (DeepSeek-V4-Flash) to score reflection quality at training time, and judge sensitivity is left unexplored.

Methodology

1

Multi-turn GRPO base — for prompt x, the old policy samples a group of G=8 rollouts; a verifier assigns task reward Ri and binary outcome bi, group-normalized into trajectory-level advantage Ai.

2

Stage A — policy-native reflection — the same rollout policy πθold reflects on each verified trajectory, identifying outcome-critical turns and DO/AVOID behaviors. A fixed external judge (DeepSeek-V4-Flash, 0–3 rubric) scores causal correctness, outcome consistency, and actionability; the score trains only the reflection tokens.

3

Stage B — group-level guidance — reflections split into successful (S+) and failed (S-) sets per prompt. A stop-gradient snapshot θ̄ contrasts the two into compact guidance zx (DO-list / AVOID-list). Active only for "mixed-outcome" groups containing both outcomes (mask mx); otherwise falls back to vanilla GRPO. No gradient flows through zx.

4

Turn-level self-distillation — the frozen snapshot scores the same sampled action tokens under a plain student context and a guidance-augmented teacher context; the log-likelihood gap, sign-aligned to Ai and clipped, modulates the trajectory advantage into a bounded turn-level advantage Âi,t = Ai·(1+λ·q̂i,t·mx).

Formal guarantee: with λĜ ≤ 1 (Propositions 1–3, Appendix A), the modulation is a bounded, positively-reweighted perturbation that cannot flip the verifier-determined advantage sign. Guidance is training-only — absent entirely at inference.

Most GRSD overhead comes from online guidance construction (29.4s), not reflection-loss computation (7.3s). GRSD's teacher pass is cheaper than SDAR's because Stage B distills reflections into compact per-prompt guidance instead of a full retrieved skill context.

  • Vs. skill-distillation baselines: GRSD beats Skill-SD and SDAR by 4.2% average ALFWorld success and 5.5% WebShop success, with the best aggregate result under all 3 backbones — though gains are "not uniform on every individual subset" for Search-based QA.
  • Policy-native vs. external reflections (Fig. 4): substituting self-generated reflections with a stronger external model's reflections lowers ALFWorld validation success and widens the teacher-student likelihood gap — self-generated guidance better matches the policy's own capability boundary.

Strengths

  • Directly targets a well-articulated failure mode of prior skill-based self-distillation (retrieval mismatch, capability-exceeding external analyzers, path-specific single-trajectory extraction) with a purpose-built contrastive success/failure mechanism.
  • Formal theoretical grounding (Appendix A, Propositions 1–3) for the turn-level advantage modulation, paired with an unusually candid statement of what the theory does and does not prove.
  • Thorough component ablations (Table 2) cleanly attributing gains to guidance source, group aggregation, failed-reflection inclusion, and modulation granularity.
  • No inference-time overhead — guidance is training-only privileged context — and modest training overhead (17% vs. GRPO), lower in some cost dimensions than the closest comparable baseline SDAR.
  • Evaluated across 3 model scales/backbones and 3 qualitatively different environments, plus an explicit out-of-distribution generalization study.

Limitations

  • Still relies on an external judge model (DeepSeek-V4-Flash) at training time to score reflection quality, so the "policy-native, no external model" framing applies to guidance content but not reflection supervision.
  • The theoretical analysis explicitly disclaims guarantees of semantic correctness, monotonic improvement, or joint-optimization convergence, and relies on an idealized linear representation model not shown to hold for actual LLM-generated reflections.
  • Improvements over the strongest recent baseline (SDAR) are consistently single-digit percentage points, with non-uniform gains on individual Search-based QA subsets.
  • Stage B requires a mixed-outcome rollout group (both success and failure under the same prompt); it is inactive otherwise and falls back to vanilla GRPO, so effectiveness likely depends on success rate staying in an intermediate range.
  • All methods share a fairly narrow hyperparameter sweep (single learning rate, single KL coefficient, fixed group size G=8); judge-choice and group-size sensitivity beyond the reported λ/α ablations are not explored.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. How sensitive is GRSD's performance to the choice, temperature, or reliability of the external judge that scores reflection quality — would a weaker or self-scored judge substantially degrade guidance?
  2. Does the "valid-group ratio" (fraction of rollout groups with both successes and failures) become a bottleneck as the policy approaches very high or very low success rates?
  3. Would replacing the fixed judge-scored reflection reward with a self-consistency or verifier-free signal preserve the "no external model" claim more fully while retaining performance?
  4. How does GRSD compare to SDAR/Skill-SD on tasks requiring longer horizons than the ones tested here, where turn-level credit assignment becomes harder?
  5. Can group-reflective guidance be cached and reused across similar prompts to amortize the Stage B synthesis cost, rather than recomputed per prompt at every update?
