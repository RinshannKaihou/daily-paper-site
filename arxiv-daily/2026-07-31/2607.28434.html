Key Finding

The model that anchors word-types most strongly reads a single discourse worst — instruction tuning fixes fidelity, not anchoring.

Why This Matters

A training-free interpretability probe validated against expert psychoanalytic annotation and honest baseline comparisons — a template for statistically rigorous interpretability claims.

Critical Edge

A trivial word-rarity baseline scores 0.875–0.878 AUC on the same clinical task — far above the aggregator channel's own 0.47–0.72 — and one of three models tested (Qwen3-8B) fails both permutation-null tests after residualization.

Methodology

Metaphor Tracer runs one deterministic, no-sampling, float32 teacher-forced forward pass over three causal LMs — phi-4 (14B), Qwen3-8B, and base Llama-3.1-8B — plus a matched Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct twin, extracting the full residual stream.

Every token position is a candidate anchor. Its core subspace is the smallest set of hidden dimensions holding 70% of the cross-token growth signal from layer 1 to the final layer. Two mechanistically distinct channels read that subspace: the differentiator tracks how much of other tokens' depth-displacement energy transiently passes through it during processing ("transport in flight"); the aggregator measures how strongly the final layer consolidates around it (a participation-ratio measure, closely rank-correlated with subspace size).

Two parallel readings — raw ("operative") and per-layer z-scored ("quiet-dimensions") — expose complementary geometry rather than a signal/noise pair. Every constant was frozen on one discovery text before a battery of seven pre-registered tests ran against held-out engineered texts, four psychoanalyst-annotated clinical transcripts, and four expert-annotated pipeline traces. All code and per-claim recomputation scripts are public.

Instruction tuning raises the matched Llama twin's clinical fidelity by +0.079 median AUC (both permutation nulls cleared, p=0.0001/0.0003 pooled) while leaving word-type anchoring essentially untouched — evidence that "reading a discourse well" and "anchoring on lexical type" are separable mechanisms, not two faces of the same signal.

Secondary: on non-linguistic pipeline traces, phi-4's aggregator tracks numeric columns (AUC 0.83–0.84) while its differentiator tracks identity columns (0.65–0.67); Qwen3-8B inverts this exactly; the base model shows no organization at all (flat 0.49–0.58). Aggregator values also correlate negatively with next-token entropy (−0.18 to −0.64), and an attention-saliency control sits near chance (0.47–0.58) — arguing against a simple novelty or attention-volume confound.

Strengths

  • Unusually thorough robustness battery — novelty/entropy baseline, attention-saliency control, delimiter erasure, first-position exclusion, minimal-pair sanity floor, static lexical baselines — reported even where it complicates the headline story.
  • Strong reproducibility infrastructure: public code, deterministic single-pass extraction, cached artifacts, and independent per-claim recomputation via paper_stats.py.
  • The matched base/instruct Llama pair cleanly isolates what instruction tuning changes (clinical fidelity, register concentration) from what it leaves untouched (type-essence transfer, recruitment-depth ordering).
  • Cross-domain generalization test on non-linguistic pipeline execution traces shows the same two-channel dissociation — or its clean absence in the base model.
  • Self-critical Limitations section pre-empts most objections a skeptical reader would raise.

Limitations

  • Small corpus (13 texts total); clinical ground truth rests on a single annotator per transcript, not measured inter-annotator agreement.
  • All findings are correlational — no activation patching or ablation shows the model's downstream computation actually depends on the discovered subspaces.
  • The aggregator channel is rank-coupled to core-subspace size k (Spearman 0.85–1.00, ≈0.99 in phi-4) — some of its signal may be a bare dimension-count proxy.
  • Type-essence transfer is tested only within register; cross-register transfer and generalization beyond ≤14B models is untested.
  • A word-rarity baseline beats the channel's raw clinical AUC, and Qwen3-8B fails both residualized permutation-null tests — a real gap in the alignment claim.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Would causal interventions (patching or ablating a core subspace) show that "transport in flight" actually influences downstream generation, rather than being merely legible?
  2. Does the clinical-alignment result survive a second, independent psychoanalytic annotator — and does Qwen3-8B's permutation-null failure persist or reverse?
  3. How does the two-channel, two-view instrument behave on 70B+ models, mixture-of-experts architectures, or non-transformer (e.g. state-space) architectures?
  4. Can the proposed cross-instrument prediction against Gurnee et al.'s (2026) Jacobian-lens "global workspace" paper be tested directly on a shared open-weight model?
  5. Would a pre-registered, annotator-blind changepoint-detection method recover the same rupture regions without relying on the psychoanalyst's own discourse segmentation?
