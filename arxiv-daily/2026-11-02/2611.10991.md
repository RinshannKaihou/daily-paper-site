> **一句话总结：** 论文提出 SGAC——用一个"组关联缓存"只给少数（23%）权重组保留 8 位累加器，按 Fisher 加权码步 π_g = n_g F_g s_g² 排序动态换入换出：命中组把小于一个量化步的更新累积成整步写入 4/8 位码，被逐出时用随机舍入无偏地冲回，未命中组干脆"等待"而不舍入；在 383k 参数的 PalmNet-K 上只用 8.14 bit/权重的训练状态（QAT 需 96 bit）就在两台模拟相机的平均 PCK@0.2 上达到 0.629，压过直接随机舍入的 0.598（+3.1 [+2.6, +3.7]），但与同为 8.7 bit 的 PEQA 式基线只差 0.2 分（作者自己判定为"等价"）。

> **TL;DR:** Quantization-aware training keeps a 32-bit master copy plus Adam state (96 bits/weight) to adapt a deployed 4/8-bit network, which is ~20x the model itself; direct stochastic rounding (SR) needs no copy but injects noise at every step. SGAC keeps 8-bit sub-step accumulators for only the ~23% of weight groups with the largest Fisher-weighted squared code step (π_g = n_g F_g s_g²), commits whole code steps from the cache, flushes evicted lines by unbiased stochastic rounding, and lets uncached groups *wait* instead of rounding. On a 383k-parameter hand-keypoint U-Net adapting to two simulated webcams, the deployed 4/8-bit network averages 0.629 PCK@0.2 at 8.14 bits/weight vs 0.598 for direct SR (+3.1 [+2.6, +3.7]), 0.616 for QAT (+1.3, 96 bits), 0.624 for full-precision-then-quantized (+0.5) and 0.627 for a PEQA-style grid-only baseline (+0.2, which the authors call statistically equivalent); on FreiHAND-C (12 ImageNet-C corruptions, severity 3) it has the best corrupted+clean average (0.622 vs 0.593 for SR, +2.9 [+1.1, +4.8]) but only ties QAT on mPC (0.574 both). The interesting ablation: an 8-bit accumulator for *every* weight needs 14.64 bits to reach 0.628, so SGAC matches it with 56% of the state, while a 2-bit residual for every weight at 8.64 bits is 1.6 points worse.

## 核心贡献

- **一个"部分残差"的训练规则（wait-or-write-back）。** 部署的 4/8 位码是卷积权重的唯一副本，不做展开也不重建。更新以"码步"计量：整步写进码，不足一步的余量存进 8 位累加器（分辨率 1/128 码步）；但只有一小部分、且不断变化的权重组持有累加器，其余组"等待"（码不动，只更新 F_g）。换出时用随机舍入把余量冲回，因此重新分配缓存不会引入偏差。作者强调：以往方法要么给每个权重留残差（in-memory training、Bop、Error Diffusion、ECO），要么一个都不留（直接 SR），没有"按组、可重分配、未覆盖者延迟而非舍入"这一档。
- **三个命题刻画"何时等待优于舍入"。** Prop. 1 证明写回无偏（q + a 在任意换入换出调度下是一个零均值鞅扰动）；Prop. 2 证明对子步更新做随机舍入的期望损失代价是 ½ s_g² Σ H_ii |d_i|(1−|d_i|)，与更新本身的收益**同阶**——所以步长减半时噪声相对进度不会变小，这是"小步长下舍入永远不划算"的形式化；Prop. 3 给出等待优于舍入的判据 r_g(λ) = 2ΣG^λ_i G'_i / (s_g Σ H^λ_ii E|g_i|) < 1，且与学习率 η 无关；Cor. 1 证明按"行值" V_g 每组贪心选 A 个是分区拟阵下的最优分配，并说明按 π_g 排序恰好最小化舍入噪声的上界 ⅛π_g。
- **一个"折叠卷积也能训"的 compass gradient。** 网络用 CompassConv（六分支：3×3、1×3、3×1、1×1、"+"与"×"stencil）训练后精确折叠成一个 3×3 卷积部署。SGAC 不存分支，而是把折叠核的梯度投影到三个掩码和两个秩 1 stencil 上再求和（式 3），得到六分支本会给出的方向。代价为零：400 步适配中与普通梯度差 0.0 分。
- **一个"按部署格式评分"的评测协议。** 所有方法都打分成设备实际运行的 4/8 位网络；adapter 类方法必须合并回码（在未改动网格或重拟合网格中择优）。这个选择直接改变排名：QLoRA rank 4 保留全精度 adapter 时在相机 B 上是全场第二，合并后掉到九个"以码结尾"的方法里的第六（0.560 vs 0.618）。
- **把"训练状态"做成可核算的账本。** 式 (9) 给出 M ≈ b̄ + 1 + B + 96p_o + m_b 的解析式，Table 2 逐项列出各方法的 bit/权重：QAT 96、QLoRA r4 11.47、SGAC 8.14、直接 SR 6.14；Table 10 进一步算每步的读写流量：SGAC 6.6 bit/权重/步，是全精度 192 的 1/29、QLoRA r4 的 1/2.3。

## Key Contributions

- **Residual state as a cache, not a copy.** The central move is to treat the sub-step remainder as a *cache line* rather than a per-weight array: set-associative, admitted by sensitivity, evicted with an unbiased flush. This turns "how much precision does each weight's residual get" into "which groups deserve residual state right now", which is a resource-allocation question with a clean answer (Cor. 1).
- **A theory that predicts the failure of its own naive version.** Prop. 2 shows rounding cost is first order in the step size, so shrinking the learning rate does not buy noise immunity — the standard intuition that small steps make SR safe is wrong here. Prop. 3 then separates the two data distributions in a weighted objective J_λ = λℓ′ + (1−λ)ℓ, showing rounding on the *original* distribution is pure first-order loss once the network has converged there ("forgetting caused by the arithmetic rather than by the data").
- **Deferred updates as a first-class option.** Waiting is not an approximation of rounding — it is a third treatment with its own optimality condition. The empirical share of groups where waiting wins grows from 5.6% at step 0 to 36.6% after 400 steps at λ = ½ (25.7% → 43.9% at λ = 0, 1.6% → 19.2% at λ = 1), matching the predicted drift as the update turns against the original images.
- **A deployed-format evaluation** that scores the 4/8-bit network rather than the training-time object, with per-method step sizes chosen on a held-out validation split (1,000 crops from a separate FreiHAND file, both camera views), paired seeds, paired 95% CIs, paired t-tests, and TOST equivalence testing at a ±0.5-point margin.
- **A concrete systems claim:** 390 KB of training state for the 383k-parameter keypoint net (vs 4.6 MB for a master copy + Adam) and 161 KB for the 157k finder (vs 1.9 MB), both of which fit the 512 KB on-chip SRAM of an ESP32-S3 while neither master-copy state does.

## Methodology

**Problem setup.** A deployed layer stores groups of n_g = 64 weights as W_i = s_g q_i + β_g with q_i ∈ {0, …, 2^{b_l} − 1}; the first convolution and the head use 8 bits, everything else 4 bits, giving 3.99 bits/weight after 1% outliers are stored separately (0.23 MB total for the 383k network). Adaptation minimizes the loss on a new distribution D′ subject to q_i staying on the grid and the persistent training state M ≤ b̄ + B. Updates are expressed in code steps, d_i = ΔW_i / s_g; in the authors' network the median 4-bit code step is 0.011 while a typical adaptive-optimizer step moves a weight by ~3×10⁻⁴, i.e. ~3% of one step — which is the whole problem in one number.

**Update rule.** Backward through the low-bit weights gives g_i; a compass gradient ĝ (Eq. 3) restores the direction the six folded branches would have produced. Each group keeps one exponential average of squared gradient F_g ← ρF_g + (1−ρ)·mean(ĝ_i²) with ρ = 0.99, and the step is RMSprop-shaped with one second moment per group rather than per weight: d_i = −η ĝ_i / (s_g √(F̂_g + ε)), ε = 10⁻⁸. Outliers (1%) and biases are trained with Adam at full precision and their state is counted in the budget.

**Cache mechanics.** S sets × A ways; each group hashes to one set. A line holds the group's 64 8-bit accumulators plus a tag (B = 2 bits/weight covers 23% of groups; 32-bit tag amortized over 64 weights). For a cached group: a_i ← a_i + d_i, q_i ← q_i + trunc(a_i), a_i ← a_i − trunc(a_i) — codes move only in whole steps. On eviction: q_i ← q_i + ⌊a_i⌋ + ξ_i with ξ_i ~ Bernoulli(a_i − ⌊a_i⌋). Uncached groups keep their codes frozen and only accumulate F_g. Every T = 25 steps, each set keeps the A groups with the largest π_g = n_g F_g s_g²; a group whose codes saturate gets its range widened by one step and is re-mapped.

**Analysis.** Prop. 1 is exact (a martingale argument: commits and flushes both leave q + a unchanged in expectation). Props. 2–3 rest on four assumptions: local quadratic loss (A1), independent rounding errors (A2), dropped off-diagonal curvature (A3, order η²), and F_g constant within a step (A4). Numerical checks: over 400 forced-eviction trials the mean move of each of 64 codes matches the exact accumulated update (max |z| = 3.3); on a 256-weight quadratic, one rounding step at |d| ≤ 0.9 costs 0.114 ± 0.004 measured vs 0.112 predicted by Eq. 10 and 0.153 by the ⅛s²H bound, and at |d| ≤ 0.05 it costs 0.0158 (predicted exactly) while the bound stays at 0.153 and the exact step gains only 0.0086.

**Experimental protocol.** PalmNet-K0 (383k-parameter U-Net, 21 keypoint heatmaps from a 96×96 crop) is adapted for 400 steps, batch 32, on 12,000 FreiHAND training crops seen through one of two simulated cameras (A: dark, warm, noisy, gamma 1.6–2.4, 55–75% brightness, color cast, blur, sensor noise; B: bright, cool, low-resolution, horizontal motion blur, coarse tone steps). Test: 1,000 FreiHAND evaluation hands through the camera (new domain) and as originals (forgetting). Baselines: full-precision Adam then re-quantize, QAT with master copy + STE, QLoRA rank 1/4/8, PEQA-style (scales and offsets only), ECO-style (16-bit momentum with error feedback), direct SR, plus QA-LoRA, LoQT and SpIEL as same-storage rivals. Three additional settings: FreiHAND-C (12 of 15 ImageNet-C corruptions at severity 3, frost/snow/elastic dropped), real COCO-WholeBody photographs and one unseen user's webcam (62 of 281 calibration crops kept), and two other networks (157k hand finder, 218k SoundTag audio tagger).

## Results & Highlights

- **Two simulated cameras, deployed PCK@0.2, per-seed mean of new + original (Table 4):** SGAC 0.629 ± 0.002 at 8.14 bits/weight vs PEQA-style 0.627 (+0.2 [+0.1, +0.4]), full-precision-then-quantized 0.624 (+0.5 [+0.3, +0.7]), QLoRA r4 merged 0.621 (+0.8 [+0.3, +1.4]), ECO-style 0.619 (+1.0 [+0.8, +1.2]), QAT 0.616 (+1.3 [+0.7, +2.0]) and direct SR 0.598 (+3.1 [+2.6, +3.7]). QLoRA r4 with adapters *kept* is the only thing above it (0.632, −0.3 [−0.4, −0.1]) — and it is not a 4/8-bit network and carries 11.47 bits.
- **The merge penalty is the sharpest single result (camera B, the stronger shift):** QLoRA rank 4 reads 0.618 on the new camera with its full-precision adapters beside the codes and 0.560 once merged — a 5.8-point loss — while SGAC holds 0.618 and full precision drops to 0.593, QAT to 0.613, direct SR to 0.580. On camera B the PEQA-style baseline is 0.5 points *above* SGAC (0.619 vs 0.618 [+0.1, +0.8]) because it re-tunes the per-group scales, which suits a global brightness/color shift.
- **FreiHAND-C, 12 corruptions at severity 3, 3 seeds (Table 5):** SGAC's corrupted+clean average is 0.622 ± 0.002, the best of seven deployed methods, vs direct SR 0.593 (+2.9 [+1.1, +4.8]), ECO-style 0.612 (+1.0 [+0.3, +1.7]) and PEQA-style 0.617 (+0.5 [+0.1, +0.8]); mPC is 0.574 ± 0.002, *tied* with QAT at 0.574, which pays 96 bits and is 1.3 points worse on clean images (0.657 vs 0.670). QAT is also 2.0 points ahead under the noise corruptions; SGAC leads under blur and digital corruptions.
- **The ablation that carries the memory claim (Table 8, camera A, common step size, 3 seeds):** an 8-bit accumulator for *every* weight (the in-memory mixed-precision arrangement) reaches 0.628 at 14.64 bits/weight, i.e. +0.1 over SGAC's 0.627 at 8.14 — SGAC matches it with 56% of the state. A 2-bit residual for every weight at 8.64 bits (Error Diffusion's arrangement) reaches 0.611, 1.6 points lower. Random admission costs 5.3 points (0.574, p = 0.013); rounding the uncached groups instead of letting them wait costs 1.4 points (0.612, p = 0.031), −1.9 on original images and −1.0 on the new camera.
- **Same-storage rivals, camera A (Table 6, 5 seeds):** SGAC learns the new camera best — 0.599 vs QA-LoRA 0.592 (+0.6 [+0.3, +0.9]), SpIEL-merged 0.574 (+2.5 [+2.1, +2.9]) and LoQT 0.571 (+2.8 [+2.2, +3.4]) — but on the new+original average the three rivals sit 0.3 points *above* SGAC (0.622/0.623/0.623 vs 0.620) with every CI including zero.
- **Training-state traffic (Table 10):** 6.6 bits/weight/step for SGAC vs 192 for full precision (29x less) and 14.9 for QLoRA r4 (2.3x less); one SGAC step takes 1.49 s on one Xeon thread, between QLoRA (1.35 s) and QAT (1.59 s).
- **Where the theory meets the data (Table 9, 6,163 groups):** the share of groups for which waiting beats rounding rises from 5.6% to 36.6% over 400 steps at λ = ½; the first-order gain of the update on the *original* images falls from +13.6x its rounding cost to −1.65x; the top-23% π_g cache holds 76% of the ideal line value at step 0 and 69% after 400 (ideal ranking: 86%/90%).
- **Two other networks (Table 7):** the 157k hand finder's COCO-WholeBody F1 goes 0.422 → 0.471 with SGAC while keeping HaGRID 0.740 and RHD 0.790 (direct SR collapses to 0.566/0.641); the 218k SoundTag reaches the best new-microphone accuracy, 0.638, at 0.423 on the original recordings.
- **Real camera, unseen user:** the share of test crops read as the prompted hand shape goes 0.20 → 0.42 with SGAC, 0.41 with direct SR and 0.48 with merged QLoRA, while FreiHAND PCK@0.2 rises 0.679 → 0.692 with SGAC, 0.693 with merged QLoRA and *falls* to 0.660 with direct SR.

## Authors & Credibility

Five authors from the Department of Applied Data Science at San José State University (Soroor Ghandali corresponding). This is a single-group, systems-plus-theory paper with an unusually complete evaluation apparatus for a master's/PhD-lab output: four datasets for the keypoint net, three networks, a real user study, a 10-seed main table, paired statistics with equivalence testing, an appendix describing a full two-hand browser system, and a references list that correctly attributes every borrowed idea (FISH mask for the sensitivity score, SpQR for outliers, Hutchinson probes for curvature, Horowitz for the energy-per-bit argument). The writing is careful about what is and is not claimed — the authors volunteer that PEQA is statistically equivalent to them, that the one-step model still favors rounding in aggregate, and that the real-camera differences between methods are not significant. No code or artifact link is given, and the work appears to be a journal submission (keywords block, "Proc." style references, no conference template). The system it serves (a two-hand webcam game with 0.8M parameters total) is genuinely deployed to JavaScript/WebGL, which lends the "on-device" framing more credibility than a pure benchmark paper would have.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The individual ingredients are borrowed — π_g is the FIT/QBitOpt sensitivity product, the set-associative cache is lifted from mixed-precision embedding tables, the unbiased-flush argument is textbook stochastic rounding, and the compass gradient is Ding et al.'s re-parameterized-optimizer trick applied to a specific six-branch block. The genuinely new idea is the *combination*: making residual precision a reassignable cache resource and making "do nothing" a legal treatment for uncached groups. That is a real conceptual contribution — the paper is right that the existing taxonomy (residual for every weight, or for none) has no middle rung — but it is a scheduling idea, not a new optimization principle.

**Rigor.** Mixed, and the paper is mostly honest about it. The statistics are better than average for this literature: 10 seeds for SGAC and the two strongest baselines, paired seeds, paired CIs, TOST with a pre-declared ±0.5-point margin, and an explicit power analysis (5 seeds detect a 1-point difference at 80% power, 0.6 points needs eight). But that same power analysis indicts most of the results table: every margin except the ones against QAT, ECO and direct SR is under a point, and the headline "highest mean accuracy of nine low-bit networks" is a ranking of a field whose top four (SGAC 0.629, PEQA 0.627, QLoRA-r4-kept 0.632, full precision 0.624) are statistically indistinguishable. The honest summary is "as good as anything at 8.1 bits with no merge step", which is a good result — the abstract's superlative oversells it slightly.

**The theory does not actually explain the main empirical effect.** This is the most interesting crack. Prop. 3 is a per-group, per-step statement; Table 9 shows that even after 400 steps, summed over the uncached groups, the one-step model *still* favors rounding over waiting. Yet the end-to-end ablation says waiting is worth 1.4 points. The authors note the discrepancy ("the 400-step ablation of Section 6.7 measures the net effect of a whole adaptation directly") but do not resolve it — there is no argument for why 400 steps of individually-favored rounding produce a worse network than 400 steps of waiting. A plausible explanation (compounding of rounding noise into a random walk that the group-averaged F_g cannot see, or drift of the codes away from the original optimum) is not offered or tested. So the mechanism is validated at the level it was derived and *not* at the level where the gain appears.

**Baselines.** The two closest competitors are re-implementations labelled "PEQA-style" and "ECO-style", not the released methods, and no code is provided to check the fidelity of either. This matters most for PEQA, which is the one baseline that matches SGAC: if the scale/offset step size or the grid re-fitting were chosen poorly, the +0.2 becomes meaningless in either direction. The step-size search is described (three values each for PEQA's scale step and ECO's step size, screened with three seeds) but the actual grids are not tabulated. QLoRA, by contrast, gets four ranks and two merge modes, i.e. noticeably more tuning effort than the baselines it beats.

**Missing baselines.** The corruption benchmark is explicitly motivated by test-time adaptation ("the usual test bed for test-time adaptation [44]"), yet no TTA method — Tent, CoTTA, or even a plain entropy-minimization baseline — appears. Since SGAC consumes *labelled* calibration data, a comparison against label-free TTA on FreiHAND-C would answer the question a practitioner actually has: is a 2-minute guided calibration plus SGAC better than no labels at all? Also absent from the main table: a TinyTL/BitFit-style "biases + last layer" baseline, which only shows up for the finder in Table 7. Given that B = 0 (outliers + biases only) already recovers 0.620 of SGAC's 0.627 in the ablation, the marginal value of the whole cache over "train the biases" is 0.7 points — a comparison the paper reports but does not foreground.

**Data and protocol quirks.** The two cameras are the authors' own simulation; only FreiHAND-C is a community benchmark, and it uses 12 of 15 corruptions at a single severity (3), with step sizes carried over from the camera experiments without re-tuning — a defensible protocol choice that nonetheless means no method is at its corruption-optimal setting. The real-camera study is 62 calibration crops and 140 test crops; the authors correctly report that method differences there are not significant (p ≥ 0.37). The COCO-WholeBody experiment adapts toward a domain that is *already in the training set* — the authors flag this ("targeted adaptation toward a weak real domain rather than adaptation to an unseen one"), which is honest but means it is not a domain-generalization test.

**Scope.** Every result is on a 383k-, 157k- or 218k-parameter convolutional network with 21-output heatmap heads or 50-class audio tags. There is no transformer, no language model, and no experiment where the number of groups is large enough for cache conflicts to bite (6,163 groups, 23% cached, and a fully associative cache performs identically to a direct-mapped one — so associativity, the paper's own structural metaphor, is doing no work here). The direct-SR literature the paper cites (Zhao et al., ACML 2025) operates at language-model scale; whether π_g ranking still selects the right 23% when code steps vary by orders of magnitude across layers is untested except on a synthetic regression (where π_g does beat Fisher-only, 8.83 vs 9.45 loss).

**Overclaiming, narrowly.** "Highest mean accuracy of nine deployed low-bit networks" is technically true and statistically hollow; "matches an 8-bit accumulator kept for every weight with 56% of that state" is the stronger and better-supported claim and deserves to be the headline. The ESP32-S3 SRAM argument is arithmetic, not a deployment: no microcontroller run, no energy measurement, only a bits-moved proxy justified by a 2014 Horowitz citation.

## Strengths

- A clean, well-motivated resource-allocation framing of a problem usually treated as "keep a master copy or accept the noise", with an explicit three-way treatment (cache / round / wait) and an optimality argument for the assignment rule.
- The unbiasedness proof is short and actually load-bearing: it is what makes cache reassignment free, and it is verified numerically (400 trials, max |z| = 3.3).
- Evaluation on the *deployed* format rather than the training-time object, which produces the paper's most useful finding — that adapter methods lose up to 5.8 points when merged — and which most low-bit training papers avoid.
- Honest statistics: paired seeds, paired CIs, TOST, a stated equivalence margin, and an explicit power calculation. The authors report the two places where they lose (PEQA on camera B, the three same-storage rivals on the average) rather than burying them.
- The memory accounting (Table 2, Eq. 9, Table 10) is unusually complete and lets a reader recompute the 8.14 bits; the 56%-of-state comparison against a dense 8-bit accumulator is the right ablation and is run at matched codes and matched update rule.
- Genuine deployment context: a browser system at 0.8M parameters total, a two-minute calibration protocol, and a real unseen user, which keeps the abstractions tethered.

## Limitations

- One architecture family (small CNNs with folded six-branch blocks), one task family (keypoint heatmaps, hand detection, audio tagging). No evidence that π_g-ranked admission transfers to transformers or to layers whose code steps differ by orders of magnitude.
- The headline accuracy margin over the strongest same-budget baseline is 0.2 points and is declared equivalent by the authors' own test; the defensible claim is parity at lower state, not superiority.
- Set-associativity is empirically inert: direct-mapped, four-way and fully associative caches all land within 0.1 points, so the processor-cache framing is presentational rather than functional at this scale.
- The theoretical account of *why* waiting wins over a full adaptation is incomplete: the summed one-step model still prefers rounding after 400 steps, contradicting the 1.4-point ablation, and no bridging argument is given.
- Baselines are "style" re-implementations without released code; PEQA in particular is the pivotal comparison and its tuning grid is not fully specified.
- No label-free TTA baseline on the corruption benchmark, and no microcontroller measurement to back the ESP32-S3 SRAM claim.
- The real-camera evidence is small (140 test crops, method differences p ≥ 0.37) and the COCO experiment adapts toward a domain already in the training set.
- Compass gradient contributes 0.0 points — necessary plumbing for the folded architecture, but a full figure and derivation for a wash.

## Relevance

直接命中「Reliability under Extreme Performance Optimization」：它把"低精度算术如何悄悄吃掉更新"这件事量化了——子步更新的随机舍入代价与更新收益同阶（Prop. 2），因此小步长并不会让噪声相对变小；这对任何在 4/8 位权重上做在线更新或 QAT 的系统都是一条可复用的判据。对关注量化误差累积、训练状态内存账本、以及"部署格式 ≠ 训练格式"导致的隐性精度损失（合并 adapter 掉 5.8 分）的读者，这篇值得细读方法与 Table 2/8/10。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Why does 400 steps of individually-favored rounding lose 1.4 points to 400 steps of waiting, when the summed one-step model still favors rounding at both endpoints? Is the missing term the *variance* of the code trajectory (a random walk that F_g cannot see), or the drift of codes away from a good original optimum that no per-step term captures?
2. How does π_g-ranked admission behave when code steps vary by 2–3 orders of magnitude across layers, as in a transformer with per-channel scales? The synthetic regression is the only test of the step factor's value (8.83 vs 9.45 for Fisher-only) and it is 256 weights.
3. What happens with a much smaller cache (B = 0.5) or a much longer adaptation (10k steps)? The B-sweep saturates between 12% and 47% of groups (0.623 → 0.629), suggesting the useful set is ~a quarter of groups — but is that fraction a property of the network or of the 400-step budget?
4. Would a label-free TTA objective (entropy minimization) on the same 4/8-bit codes beat the 2-minute guided calibration? The paper motivates FreiHAND-C with TTA but never runs a TTA baseline.
5. Does the unbiased-flush argument survive code-range widening and re-mapping (the paper's Prop. 1 excludes clipping and re-ranging)? Saturation is common in 4-bit groups and the re-map is a discrete, potentially biased event.
6. Can the cache double as the outlier store? Outliers (1%) plus their Adam moments cost 0.96 bits/weight — 12% of SGAC's total budget — and they are exactly the weights whose code steps are largest, i.e. natural cache residents.
