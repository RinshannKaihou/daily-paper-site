> **一句话总结：** 提出 Strategist——一个不改模型权重、只改写推理程序的元智能体，把每条推理策略写成可复用的 Python "Strategy Blueprint" 并存入跨任务累积的蓝图库，在 30 个基准上以 0.61× 的加权推理成本追平最强基线（74.7% vs 74.0%），赢下 24/30。

> **TL;DR:** Strategist is a GPT-6 meta-agent that searches over inference strategies written as composable Python "blueprints" and stores every candidate in a shared library with measured accuracy/cost cards, so later tasks start from earlier discoveries. Across 30 benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, SQuAD v2, HotpotQA, BBH, AQuA-RAT, …) with Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B/4B executors, it beats the best of an 18-baseline × 6-configuration matrix on 24 benchmarks, matches average accuracy 74.7% vs 74.0% while cutting weighted cost 39% (0.61×), and its blueprints transfer to GPT-5 and Gemini-2.5 without re-search (−35% / −40% total USD). The two clean ablations are the real payload: 10 iterations without the library drops to 65.4%, and a single zero-shot shot drops to 39.4%.

## 核心贡献

- **提出 Strategy Blueprint 表示**：每条策略是一段带统一接口 `execute(llm, task) -> str` 的可执行 Python 程序，可调用库里任何更早创建的蓝图；四条协议（共享接口、禁止前向调用、禁止环、禁止重复）把库约束成一张可持续增深的 DAG。
- **把"策略设计"从一次性搜索变成累积式搜索**：每个候选无论成败都带着测量卡（accuracy/cost）和经验笔记回写库中，检索同时做"相关命中"和"刻意漂移的灵感命中"，让下一个任务不是从零开始。
- **给出大规模同协议对比**：30 个基准 × 18 个基线 × 6 种模型档位 = 每基准 108 个基线单元，基线在训练集上选出 BL 再在测试集评分，模拟"小样本选型、大样本部署"。
- **证明库本身可迁移**：在 10 个从未参与搜索的留出基准上，只做一次检索、不做任何搜索即可平均 77.8% → 81.4%；同一批蓝图不改一行代码搬到 GPT-5 / Gemini-2.5 上仍优于重跑的基线。
- **消融量化了库的贡献**：同样 10 次迭代但禁用库降到 65.4%，只跑一次的 zero-shot 降到 39.4%，对照完整系统 78.7%。

## Key Contributions

- **A representation that is both freeform and composable.** Prior work either uses fixed component graphs (AFlow, AgentSquare) which bound what can be discovered, or monolithic code (ADAS) which yields isolated programs. The Strategy Blueprint is arbitrary Python behind one abstract interface, so composition and retrieval are possible without restricting expressiveness.
- **Cumulative rather than per-task search.** The library stores *blueprints* (structure) not *strategies* (structure + task prompts), which is precisely what makes cross-task transfer possible: prompts are stripped on write-back and re-instantiated per task.
- **A retrieval index over measured behavior, not just text.** Each blueprint carries traits, a measurement card, and experience notes written by previous strategists; hybrid TF-IDF + embedding search (`α = 0.4`, all-MiniLM-L6-v2) ranks over these, so the meta-agent finds components by "what this did on math/fast/nothink" rather than by name similarity.
- **An unusually careful baseline protocol.** 108 baseline cells per benchmark, BL selected on the training split and scored on test, one shared answer extractor across Strategist, all 18 baselines, and all 5 external frameworks — this removes the most common source of fake gains in this literature.
- **Honest disclosure of selection bias.** The paper reports that the final strategy is picked for hidden-set performance and is therefore 4.3 points above BL on hidden but only 0.7 on test, and states that only the test split is unbiased.

## Methodology

**The object being searched.** A blueprint is a Python program capturing the *structure* of a reasoning procedure — which models to call, in what order, at which tier, with what control flow — but not the task-specific prompts. It is built from two foundations, `chat` (single/multi-turn) and `agent` (scaffold + tools), and every node exposes `async def execute(llm, task, prompts)`. Strategies may route across three tiers (`fast`/`balanced`/`complex`) crossed with two modes (`nothink`/`thinking`), which is how a single strategy can be simultaneously accurate and cheap: cheap calls do classification and routing, expensive calls are spent only where they change the answer.

**The loop.** Given a task and a small development set, each of `N = 10` iterations runs four stages:
1. *Retrieve* — two hybrid queries (one relevance, one deliberately off-task "inspiration") return 5 + 5 blueprints; relevance hits come with full measurement cards, inspirations with one-line descriptions only.
2. *Propose* — `K = 5` candidates, constrained to contain at least one each of `new` / `composed` / `reuse` and one each of the targets `accuracy` / `efficiency` / `balanced`. Slates violating the quota, duplicating a recent candidate, or missing JSON fields are rejected and re-prompted.
3. *Evaluate* — candidates run on the development set. The strategist sees full traces and per-sample scores on the *training* split, aggregate scores only on the *validation* split, and nothing on the *hidden* split; a separate 200-sample *test* split is never touched by the loop.
4. *Accumulate* — every candidate's underlying blueprint is written back with a measurement card and experience notes (one numeric observation + one concrete recommendation), deduplicated by AST hash with string literals elided.

**Selection.** After 10 iterations, candidates that beat BL under a ±2-point accuracy / ±2% cost rule on *both* validation and hidden are eligible; the one with the largest hidden accuracy gain wins, else BL is returned unchanged.

**Setup.** Executors are Qwen3-0.6B / 1.7B / 4B (vLLM, 8×H200), each in nothink and thinking mode. The strategist is GPT-6 (`gpt-6-sol`) through the Codex CLI and never answers questions itself. Cost is weighted tokens with per-tier weights 1.00 / 2.83 / 6.67, proportional to parameter count (the leading `2N` FLOPs term); for the transfer experiments cost is USD at list prices.

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline over 30 benchmarks:** average test accuracy 74.7% vs 74.0% for BL at a mean per-benchmark cost ratio of **0.61× (−39%)**; 24 wins, 2 ties (MBPP+ 80.5 vs 79.0; MinervaM 38.5 vs 39.0), 2 mixed (MedQA 68.5 vs 72.5 at 0.44× cost; AIME 2024 60.0 vs 65.0 at 0.77×), 2 losses (HotpotQA 64.5 vs 64.0 at **+9.0% cost**; COPA 100.0 vs 100.0 at **+6.1% cost**).
- **Largest joint accuracy/cost wins:** SQuAD v2 **79.1% vs 73.2%** (+5.9 pts) at **5.6k vs 25.0k** weighted tokens per sample (**0.23×, −77.4%**); 2WikiMQA 76.5 vs 72.5 (+4.0) at 33.8k vs 98.6k (−65.7%); SAT-Math 98.2 vs 94.5 (+3.7) at 4.9k vs 12.1k (−59.4%); TruthfulQA 69.0 vs 64.5 (+4.5) at −21.9% cost; LSAT-AR 81.0 vs 77.6 (+3.4) at −33.0%; LSAT-RC 81.5 vs 78.5 (+3.0) at −49.9%; Amazon-Rec 28.0 vs 24.0 hit-rate (+4.0) at −35.1%.
- **Cost-only wins on saturated benchmarks:** MMLU 79.5 vs 78.5 at **10.2k vs 96.3k tokens (−89.4%)**; GSM8K 92.0 vs 94.0 at 8.3k vs 62.8k (−86.8%); ASDiv 93.0 vs 95.0 at −81.1%; SciQ 97.5 vs 98.0 at −69.1%; MMLU-STEM-Hard 88.0 vs 87.0 at −66.2%. Across the suite, cost drops >2% on 26/30 and accuracy stays within 2 points on 28/30.
- **One-shot library retrieval (10 held-out benchmarks, no search):** average **77.8% → 81.4%**, within 2 points of BL on all 10 and above it on 8, winning on 6. The wins are real but cost-skewed: BoolQ 85.5 vs 73.5 (+16.3%) at **32.7k vs 1.0k** tokens (**32×**), DROP 72.5 vs 61.5 (+17.9%) at 9.5k vs 4.9k (+92.4%). Excluding BoolQ and SIQA the average cost ratio is 0.77×.
- **Cross-model transfer, zero code changes:** on GPT-5 the strategies win 7/10 and total cost falls **$53.86 → $35.02 (−35%)**; on Gemini-2.5 they win 8/10 and cost falls **$18.50 → $11.07 (−40.1%)**. The margins are inflated by baseline breakage — AQuA-RAT reads BL 31.0 vs Ours 89.5 (+189%) on GPT-5 while the same BL scores 86.5 on Qwen3, i.e. the baseline harness, not the strategy, is what changed.
- **Ablations (10 benchmarks, 78.7% full):** no library but still 10 iterations → **65.4%** at 41.4k cost (−13.3 pts); library on but one iteration (zero-shot) → **39.4%** with three crashed runs scored as zero (56.2% over the seven that ran). Against five external frameworks under the same meta-LLM and executor — AFlow 67.0, ADAS 65.9, EvoFlow 64.2, StrategyLLM 67.3, AgentSquare 65.1 — Strategist wins 8/10.
- **Library scale and stability:** 30 searches produced **1,865** deduplicated blueprints; the train–hidden gap stays between 1.2 and 2.5 points across all 10 iterations and does not widen, which is decent evidence that the loop is not fitting trace noise.

## Authors & Credibility

Taiming Lu and Zhuang Liu (Princeton) with Qi Xu and Zhigang Hua (Meta). Liu's group has a track record in efficient LLM training and sparse/pruned models; the Meta co-authors bring large-scale agent-infrastructure experience. The paper is a substantial engineering artifact rather than a theory contribution: 30 benchmarks × 108 baseline cells × 4 splits, a 29-page appendix with pseudocode, full per-benchmark tables, prompt templates, cost-weight derivation, and four worked examples of discovered strategies. Compute is acknowledged (Google TPU Research Cloud, Princeton PICSciE, Neuronic cluster) and code is advertised in Figure 1. The main credibility caveat is structural rather than authorial: the meta-agent is a closed frontier model, so no third party can reproduce the *search trajectory*, only the framework.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The framing — the "second half" of an agent system is hand-written and never compounds — is sharp, and the specific move of storing *structure without prompts* is the right abstraction: it is what lets a MuSiQue count-round-trip guard become a component of a later multi-hop blueprint. But the ingredients are each familiar (ADAS's code search, Voyager/ExpeL's skill libraries, DSPy/AFlow's composability, router-based tiering). The contribution is the combination plus the discipline of measuring it at scale, not a new mechanism. The claim of being "the first automated strategy-design system that accumulates knowledge across tasks" is defensible only under a narrow reading — ExpeL and Voyager also accumulate across tasks, just not over *executable strategy structure*.

**Rigor — where it is strong.** The 108-cell baseline matrix with training-split selection is more careful than most of this literature, which typically compares against CoT or self-consistency at one configuration. Sharing a single benchmark-aware extractor across every method removes the classic "our parser is better" confound. And the disclosure that the hidden-set-selected strategy is +4.3 on hidden but +0.7 on test is the kind of number most papers bury; putting it in Section 6 is a genuine mark of honesty.

**Rigor — where it is weak.** Four specific problems:
1. **The BL is an accuracy-only argmax over 108 cells measured on 100 samples.** On saturated benchmarks this deliberately picks the most expensive accurate cell, so "−89.4% cost on MMLU" is largely the discovery that a cheaper tier suffices — the paper concedes some wins come from "simpler baseline scripts, found during search, that match BL's accuracy at a lower cost." A cost-aware or Pareto-frontier BL would be the fair comparison and is absent. The same winner's-curse applies to BL itself: the max over 108 noisy 100-sample estimates is optimistically biased upward, and its test score regresses down.
2. **No variance anywhere.** One search run per benchmark, no seeds, no error bars, 200-sample test sets. At 200 samples the binomial standard error is ~3.5 points near 50% and ~1.5 points near 90%, so wins like AQuA-RAT (+0.5), StrategyQA (+0.5), MMLU (+1.0), Gaokao-Math (+1.0) and MMLU-STEM-Hard (+1.0) are inside the noise band. The 24/30 headline is partly a consequence of the generous ±2-point/±2% win rule, which lets a 2-point accuracy loss count as a win if cost drops.
3. **The transfer experiment is contaminated and, on Gemini, structurally vacuous.** The GPT-5 table's huge deltas (AQuA-RAT BL 31.0, 2WikiMQA BL 29.5, HotpotQA BL 39.0 — all far below the same workflows on Qwen3) come from baseline harnesses returning malformed answers, which the authors themselves note for `self_refine` and `multi_agent_debate`. That makes "+20.5 pp on GPT-5" mostly a robustness-of-implementation result. On Gemini-2.5 the mapping sends both `balanced` and `complex` to `gemini-2.5-flash` and issues identical calls for nothink/thinking, so the multi-tier routing — the mechanism the paper argues is central to cost efficiency — is never actually exercised in that half of the transfer study.
4. **Search cost is excluded from every headline number.** A full search is ~60 candidates per benchmark, ~20,000 candidate-sample executions, plus ~135k prompt and 45k response tokens for the strategist. The 0.61× figure is pure inference cost; the amortized view (one-shot, 0.77×) still does not charge the original 30 searches. Without a break-even-task-count analysis, "39% cheaper" describes steady state, not the cost of getting there.

**The missing ablation that matters most.** The paper's central claim is cumulative improvement across tasks, but there is no task-order experiment: no reversed or shuffled ordering, no per-task gain plotted against library size, no library-size sweep. "No library = 65.4%" is measured on 10 benchmarks *after* the library was populated by 30 prior searches, so it isolates the retrieval channel, not the accumulation effect. One-shot retrieval (77.8 → 81.4) is the closest evidence, but it is a single draw of 10 tasks from 40 with an unspecified selection seed.

**Smaller issues.** The cross-framework comparison does not state the search budget granted to AFlow, ADAS, EvoFlow, StrategyLLM and AgentSquare, so a 60-candidate Strategist versus default-budget competitors would not be a like-for-like contest. Scoring three crashed zero-shot runs as 0% conflates capability with infrastructure fragility (the 56.2%-over-seven figure is the more informative one). Coverage is lopsided: one code benchmark (MBPP+), one recommendation benchmark (Amazon-Rec), and no long-horizon agentic environment (SWE-bench, WebArena, OSWorld) — precisely the regime where hand-written strategy design costs the most and where a library should pay off largest. And the executables are 0.6B–4B models, so the 74% average reflects weak executors; several "wins" are really "small models benefit from scaffolding," which is true but narrower than the framing suggests.

## Strengths

- The abstraction is right: separating *structure* from *prompts* is what makes both composition and cross-task reuse possible, and it is a one-line design decision with large consequences.
- Measurement cards plus experience notes turn the library into something more than a code cache — retrieval is conditioned on measured behavior, and the writeback format (one numeric observation, one actionable recommendation) is disciplined enough to be genuinely useful to a later agent.
- Scale and protocol: 30 benchmarks, 108 baseline cells each, four disjoint splits, one shared extractor, full per-benchmark appendix tables. This is far above the usual two-or-three-benchmark evaluation in automated agent design.
- The discovered strategies are legible and task-specific in a way that generic prompt blobs are not — a 0.6B router that sends computable BBH items to a tool agent and the rest to self-consistency; a MuSiQue guard that refuses to emit a digit when the passage spells the number as a word and round-trip decodes it; a GSM8K lens that extracts verbatim spans and drops any span not present in the problem text.
- Honest reporting of the two losses (HotpotQA, COPA) and the two mixed results, plus the explicit hidden-versus-test selection-gap disclosure.

## Limitations

- Single stochastic search run per benchmark with no seeds or confidence intervals; the win count is not robust to noise on the many +1-point results.
- BL is an accuracy-only argmax over 108 cells on 100 samples, which over-rewards cost cutting on saturated benchmarks and inflates BL's apparent training strength.
- Total cost of ownership is never computed: search cost (≈20k candidate-sample executions per benchmark) is excluded, and no break-even analysis is given.
- No task-order, library-size, or library-composition experiment, so "the library compounds" is supported by retrieval transfer rather than by measured accumulation.
- The meta-agent is a proprietary frontier model (GPT-6 via Codex CLI); a weaker strategist may not produce composable blueprints at all, and the framework's value is therefore entangled with model capability.
- Executor scale tops out at Qwen3-4B; behaviour with 30B+ executors, where scaffolding gains typically compress, is untested.
- No long-horizon agentic benchmarks; MBPP+ and Amazon-Rec are the only representatives of code and recommendation, so category-level claims rest on one dataset each.
- Cross-model transfer results are confounded by baseline harness failures, and the Gemini mapping collapses the tier structure the method depends on.

## Relevance

这篇正中"自演化智能体与自动化研究"这条主线：它把智能体系统里唯一还靠人手写的那一半（推理策略）也纳入了自动搜索，并用跨任务累积的蓝图库做出"越用越好"的实证，而不是只喊自我改进的口号。对关心 agent 长期自主运行可靠性的人来说，它的经验笔记/测量卡机制和"库被污染怎么办"这一未答问题都值得跟进。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the library actually compound with order? Run the 30 tasks in reverse and in several random orders and plot per-task gain against library size at the moment of search — that is the experiment the central claim needs.
2. What is the break-even task count? Total cost (search + inference) versus per-task ADAS search and versus a human-written strategy, as a function of how many tasks share the library.
3. How much of the 39% cost reduction survives a Pareto-frontier BL — i.e. a baseline chosen on the training split for accuracy *per unit cost* rather than accuracy alone?
4. Would a mid-tier strategist (e.g. Qwen3-32B or GPT-5-mini) still write composable blueprints, or does the method quietly require frontier-level code synthesis?
5. Do the discovered blueprints stay useful when executors get stronger (30B+), or do they collapse toward "call the big model in thinking mode"?
6. Can the library be poisoned? A blueprint with an over-optimistic measurement card, or experience notes derived from a mis-specified task, would propagate silently to every later task — there is no audit or decay mechanism described.
7. What fraction of retrieved blueprints are actually reused versus rewritten from scratch, and does retrieval quality (precision of the 5 relevance hits) degrade as the library grows past ~2k entries?
