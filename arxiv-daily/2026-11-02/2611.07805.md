> **一句话总结：** 作者把心理测量学的"判别效度"搬进探针评估：在 CEBaB（服务/食物情感）、BiasBios（职业/性别）、Amazon 情感/否定、Amazon 情感/第一人称四个 target–nuisance 设定、Qwen2.5-7B 与 Gemma-2-9B 两个模型、每设定约 70 个线性抽取器上，证明"测试 AUROC 在 ±0.01 内统计等价（TOST）"的探针其"只改 nuisance 的编辑响应"CFN 可相差 1.5–4.7 倍（CEBaB .54–.83、BiasBios .09–.42、Amazon-1P .06–.25），DEC 与 CFN 的秩相关在 −.61 到 +.38 之间跨设定变号；效应来自训练集里 target–nuisance 的相关性而非 nuisance 的自然共现（自然数据中与情感无关的第一人称被吸收得比否定更多，去相关训练后系统性效应基本消失）；用开发集上的 CONFLICT 准则替代"选准确率最高"可在平均 DEC 代价 ≤.012 下把测试 CFN 从 .69 降到 .54（CEBaB）、从 .22 降到 .13（BiasBios）。

> **TL;DR:** Across four target–nuisance settings (CEBaB service/food sentiment with human counterfactuals, BiasBios surgeon-vs-nurse/gender with LLM pronoun swaps, Amazon review sentiment with negation, and the same reviews with first-person framing), two frozen models (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Gemma-2-9B-it) and ~70 linear extractors per setting, held-out AUROC does not rank probes by nuisance selectivity. On CEBaB at training correlation ρtr=0.9, the 9 extractors statistically equivalent to the best (test AUROC .966, TOST margin ±.01) span CFN .54–.83 — i.e. rewriting only the food sentence moves the "service" score 54–83% of the way to the opposite service class. On BiasBios decodability is at ceiling (.998, 65 of 66 near-best probes indistinguishable) while gender edits move the occupation score by .09–.42 of the surgeon–nurse gap, and the DEC↔CFN rank correlation ranges from −.61 (CEBaB, Gemma) to +.38 (BiasBios, Gemma), changing sign across settings. Against the standard practice of selecting the probe with maximal development decodability, selecting by the CONFLICT criterion lowers test CFN from .69 to .54 on CEBaB (−.012 AUROC), .22 to .13 on BiasBios (−.001) and .17 to .11 on Amazon-1P (−.002), with no gain where the default is already insensitive (Amazon-Neg, .04→.04). Read it if you report probe accuracy as evidence that a direction "represents" a concept; the protocol (nuisance labels on development data only, no counterfactuals needed) is directly reusable. Skip it if you need nonlinear probes or SAE features — those are untested.

## 核心贡献

- **把"可解码性 ≠ 效度"从口号变成可操作的测量协议**。论文区分两类证据：DEC（in-distribution AUROC，业界标准判据）与判别证据——固定 target 下分数对 nuisance 的依赖 |LEAK|，以及"只改 nuisance 的输入编辑"引起的分数变化 CFN（按 target 类间分差 ΔT 归一化，1.0 = 编辑把分数推过整个 target 类间距离），并用"两个变量都不改"的安慰剂编辑做地板对照。
- **给出了一个干净的反例代数**：取二值 T、N 与两个分数 s₁=T、s₂=T+αN（0<α<1），两者都把全部正例排在全部负例之上，AUROC 同为 1，但只有 s₂ 会随 N 变化。这说明"准确率相同"在原理上无法排除 nuisance 混入，问题只在于真实探针里混入了多少。
- **在四个设定上做了"抽取器群体"研究而非单点比较**：每设定 7 个层深 × 10 种配置（类均值差、ℓ2 逻辑回归 C∈{10⁻³…10}、PLS k∈{2,4,8,16}）= 70 个标准抽取器，另加 35 个"擦除 nuisance 方向后再回归"的变体；以"测试 DEC 距最优 ≤0.01"定义 near-best，以配对 bootstrap 90% CI 落在 ±0.01 内（TOST）定义"统计等价"。
- **发现相关训练数据下等价探针的选择性差异是数量级的**：ρtr=0.9 时，CEBaB 上 9 个等价抽取器的 CFN 跨 .54–.83，BiasBios 上 65 个等价抽取器跨 .09–.42，Amazon-1P 上跨 .06–.25；而 DEC 与 CFN 的秩相关在 −.61 到 +.38 之间变号，说明 DEC 连"排序"都给不出稳定信号。
- **用"同一批评论、两个 nuisance"的对照分离了因果**：第一人称在自然评论中与负面情感几乎无关（出现率 .66 vs .68），否定则与负面情感共现（.64 vs .30）；但在同样的 ρtr=0.9 下，竞争性抽取器对第一人称的 CFN 中位数是 .12–.17，对否定只有 .05–.06。也就是说，被吸收的是"训练集里被造出来的相关性"，不是"世界里真实存在的相关性"。ρtr=0.5 去相关训练后，BiasBios 与两个 Amazon 设定的带符号 CFN 都落回 ±.08 内。
- **把批判转成可执行的选择准则并做了对照实验**：只用开发集上的 nuisance 标签（不需要反事实数据）按 CONFLICT 或 min|LEAK| 选抽取器，与"选开发集 DEC 最高"对比。CONFLICT 在默认选择本来敏感的设定上降低 CFN（CEBaB .69→.54、BiasBios .22→.13、Amazon-1P .17→.11），平均 DEC 代价 ≤.012；若候选池里含擦除探针，CONFLICT 在每个设定/模型/训练样本里都选中它，CFN 全部下降（.04–.34），平均 DEC 代价 ≤.007。
- **附带一个几何解释**：CEBaB 上 target 方向与"类内 food 方向"本身对齐（余弦 .65–.83），这个纯观测几何就能以 MAE .11 预测"去相关数据上训练的类均值差探针"的食物敏感度——即除了训练相关性，表征几何是第二个、独立的泄漏来源。

## Key Contributions

- **Reframes probe evaluation as construct validity, not accuracy.** The paper operationalizes discriminant evidence (Campbell & Fiske, 1959; Messick, 1995) for scalar concept readouts: a score may correlate with a correlated construct, but must not depend on it once the target is held fixed. It contributes concrete estimators for that requirement: `|LEAK| = ½Σ_t |AUROC(s→N | T=t) − 0.5|` (nuisance dependence at fixed target, computable from labels alone) and `CFN` (response to nuisance-only input edits, normalized by the target class gap, with `|CFN|` as an unsigned companion because signed means can hide opposing changes).
- **A population study instead of a two-probe comparison.** 70 standard extractors per setting (7 depths × {diff-of-means, ℓ2 logistic with 5 regularization strengths, PLS with k∈{2,4,8,16}}) plus 35 nuisance-erased variants, on two models, with near-best and statistically-equivalent subsets defined by explicit criteria (within .01 of best test DEC; paired-bootstrap TOST at ±.01) — so the claim is about the *set of probes you could plausibly have shipped*, not one lucky configuration.
- **A causal manipulation of the confound.** Target–nuisance correlation is set by resampling to ρtr ∈ {0.5, 0.7, 0.9} while the test pool is held fixed and reweighted across the four (T,N) cells. This separates "the probe learned a real-world correlation" from "the probe learned whatever the training set correlated", and the two Amazon settings — negation (naturally correlated) vs first-person framing (naturally uncorrelated, .66 vs .68) — are the clean test.
- **An edit-validity pipeline with placebo controls.** CEBaB uses human-written counterfactuals; BiasBios uses LLM pronoun/noun/name swaps with automatic verification; Amazon uses word-list-defined nuisances plus gpt-oss-120b phrase replacements applied by exact string match, so only the named phrases change. Two judge LLMs (neither identical to a probed model) see each pair in random order, blind to which text was edited and which attribute should move, and must agree that the target is unchanged and the nuisance changed; placebo edits replace neutral words with synonyms.
- **A selection experiment with a real cost curve.** Development-time criteria (max DEC, max CONFLICT, min |LEAK|, DEC−|LEAK|) are compared on identical candidate pools, reporting both the CFN improvement and the decodability it costs — including the cases where a criterion *fails* (min|LEAK| raises CFN on Amazon-Neg from .04 to .07).
- **A reproducible audit.** Released code regenerates every table and in-text number from saved results; an automated audit checks train/dev/test disjointness (CEBaB at review-family level), edit orientation vs labels, PLS k=1 ≡ diff-of-means (cosine 1.000), truncation at 256 tokens, and an independent reimplementation of every metric from raw scores matching to <10⁻⁸.

## Methodology

**Setup and notation.** An extractor maps a hidden state to a scalar, `s(h) = wᵀz(h) + b` with `z` the training-set standardization; subspace methods have a linear readout, so every extractor in the study is a scalar score. Each setting supplies a binary target `T`, a binary nuisance `N`, and input edits. With balanced `T`, `ρ = P(N=1|T=1) = P(N=0|T=0)`; extractors are trained at `ρtr ∈ {0.5, 0.7, 0.9}` and test metrics reweight the four (T,N) cells of one fixed test pool. 2,000-sample training draws, three independent samples per condition.

**Data and edits (Table 1).**

| Setting | Target / nuisance | Generated pairs | Judged by both | Accepted by both |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CEBaB | service / food sentiment | 210 | 210 | 41% |
| BiasBios | surgeon vs nurse / gender | 1,561 | 1,561 | 93% |
| Amazon-Neg | negative sentiment / negation | 1,427 | 1,427 | 58% |
| Amazon-1P | negative sentiment / first person | 1,083 | 1,083 | 68% |
| Civil* | toxic / Muslim or LGBTQ | 762 + 400 | 762 + 400 | 13%, 29% |

CEBaB provides human-written counterfactual edits and human aspect labels; splits are disjoint by review family. BiasBios edits use gpt-oss-120b to swap pronouns, gendered nouns and first name *only* (automatically checked). Amazon uses 8–40-word reviews with nuisances defined by closed word lists, so flips are checked exactly: negation (.64 of negatives vs .30 of positives) and first person (.66 vs .68, i.e. uncorrelated). Because negation is part of how sentiment is expressed, Amazon edits are additionally required to preserve opinion strength. Civil (rule-based identity swaps) is reported only in the appendix: both judges reject most swaps (13%/29% accepted) because renaming a group changes the *claim* about it, not just its name.

**Metrics.**
- `DEC` — in-distribution AUROC for `T` at `ρ = ρtr`; the standard criterion.
- `|LEAK|` — `½Σ_t |AUROC(s→N|T=t) − 0.5|`; nuisance dependence with the target held fixed, needing only labels.
- `CONFLICT` — AUROC for `T` on the `T≠N` cells (a fully reversed correlation). Explicitly *not* a validity measure: at full reversal `s = −N` also ranks `T` perfectly. It is a stress test / selection heuristic.
- `CFN` — mean score change under edits that flip only `N`, signed toward the `N` value co-occurring with `T=1` in training, divided by the mean target gap `ΔT` on the decorrelated pool. `|CFN|` is the unsigned version; `CFT` is the same for target edits (CEBaB only) as convergent evidence.
- Placebo edits (neutral-word synonyms) give the noise floor.

**Edit validation.** Judges are Qwen3-32B (locally) and gpt-oss-120b; for Amazon, Qwen3.8-27B and gpt-oss-120b. They are never told which text is edited or which attribute should change; they answer "target same?", "nuisance same?", fluency, and "other meaning change?", and a pair is accepted only if both judges see the target unchanged, the nuisance changed, no major other change and no fluency drop (Amazon: word list checks the flip, judges check strength). Judges are validated three ways: against CEBaB's five-annotator human majority (gpt-oss-120b agrees .92 of the time, comparable to a single held-out worker at .90–.91; Qwen3-32B .77–.84), with planted errors (reversed opinions, strength changes, garbled text, mixed gender cues, minor fact changes), and by re-judging unmodified accepted edits in a new order (.86–.94 retained = judge noise).

**Statistics.** Near-best = test DEC within .01 of the best; equivalent = paired-bootstrap 90% CI of the DEC difference inside ±.01 (TOST, α=.05). Because the reference is chosen on the test pool, equivalence is descriptive; the *selection* comparisons choose on development data only. Bootstrap CIs resample review families (CEBaB) or texts within (T,N) cells, and edit pairs.

**Selection protocol (the practical part).** Among extractors within .01 of the best *development* DEC, pick by max DEC (standard practice), max CONFLICT, min |LEAK|, or DEC−|LEAK|; report the chosen extractor's *test* CFN and its test DEC change.

## Results & Highlights

1. **Statistically equivalent decodability, 1.5–4.7× spread in nuisance sensitivity (CEBaB, ρtr=0.9).** With Qwen2.5-7B the best test DEC is .966, 37 extractors are near-best, and 9 are statistically equivalent to the best (TOST ±.01) — those 9 span CFN .54–.83 on CEBaB's *human* counterfactual food edits. Gemma-2-9B: best DEC .979, 8 equivalent, CFN .25–.63. At the top of that range, rewriting only the food sentence of a review moves the *service* score 83% of the way to the opposite service class, i.e. the "service" probe is largely a food probe.

2. **Decodability saturates and stops discriminating at all (BiasBios).** Test DEC is .998 for Qwen (66 near-best, **65 of 66 statistically indistinguishable from the best**) and .998 for Gemma (61 equivalent), yet gender edits move the surgeon-vs-nurse score by .09–.42 (Qwen) and .09–.33 (Gemma) of the occupation gap. The most decodable occupation probe and a probe 4.7× less gender-sensitive receive identical accuracy evidence — the standard selection criterion has literally no signal to act on here.

3. **The nuisance that is *not* correlated in the world is absorbed more (Amazon, same reviews).** At ρtr=0.9, competitive extractors on Amazon-1P (first person, natural prevalence .66 vs .68 — uncorrelated with sentiment) have median CFN .12–.17, versus .05–.06 for negation on Amazon-Neg, where negation really does co-occur with negative sentiment (.64 vs .30). Over decorrelated training the increases are .11–.16 (first person) vs .09–.11 (negation). Amazon-1P equivalent-set CFN spans .11–.22 (Qwen) and .06–.25 (Gemma); Amazon-Neg, by contrast, is uniformly insensitive (.03–.12 for every near-best extractor), which is why the abstract claims the effect in three of four settings.

4. **Decodability is not even a monotone proxy.** Among competitive standard extractors at ρtr=0.9, the Spearman correlation between test DEC and CFN ranges from **−.61 (CEBaB, Gemma, 95% CI [−.75,−.44]) to +.38 (BiasBios, Gemma, [+.09,+.60])** and changes sign across settings; with the Amazon settings it reaches −.44 and −.40. Target edits do not arbitrate either: on CEBaB *every* near-best extractor responds fully to service edits (CFT .84–1.19) while its food sensitivity ranges over −.10–.89.

5. **A same-architecture, same-tuning comparison isolates the readout from the data.** With layer and hyperparameter chosen on development DEC, the logistic probe and the nuisance-erased probe are statistically equivalent in DEC at ρtr=0.9 on BiasBios (ΔDEC 90% CI [+.000,+.002]) and Amazon-1P (+.002 [+.000,+.004]) — yet CFN is .22 vs .07 (BiasBios) and .17 vs .07 (Amazon-1P). Erasure lowers but does not remove nuisance sensitivity, and it is *not* free on CEBaB, where the two families are not equivalent (.83 vs .34).

6. **Selection by CONFLICT buys selectivity cheaply, and fails where there is nothing to buy (Table 3, ρtr=0.9).** Test CFN of the extractor chosen by max development DEC → by max CONFLICT (DEC change in parentheses): CEBaB .69→.54 (−.012); BiasBios .22→.13 (−.001); Amazon-1P .17→.11 (−.002); Amazon-Neg .04→.04 (+.000, dagger = did not fall in every training sample). Adding the erased probe to the pool makes CONFLICT select it in *every* sample and lowers CFN in every setting, model and sample by .04–.34 at a mean DEC cost ≤.007. The min|LEAK| rule is less reliable and actively backfires on Amazon-Neg (.04→.07).

7. **The effect tracks training correlation, not the world.** At ρtr=0.5, signed CFN for near-best extractors falls within ±.08 on BiasBios and both Amazon settings; on CEBaB it drops to .02–.28 (Qwen) though example-level |CFN| stays at median .25 vs signed .07 (placebo .06) — direction cancels out, magnitude does not. In the synthetic control (256-d, power-law noise), CFN grows from ≈0 at ρtr=0.5 to ≈0.75 at 0.9 with zero target–nuisance overlap, and with decorrelated training CFN grows by .83–.87 as overlap increases while decodability moves by at most .03.

8. **Robustness of the ranking, not of the magnitude.** Across edit filters (all generated pairs, either judge alone, at least one, both), CFN rankings of competitive extractors agree at Spearman ≥.87 in every setting; all-vs-accepted ≥.77; SD normalization (Cohen's-d style) ≥.87; the two edit directions ≥.93 at ρtr=0.9. Placebo edits move scores little (median |CFN| .01–.06). But magnitudes are filter-sensitive on CEBaB: .41–1.15 on all human edits vs .26–.89 on accepted ones — a 2.8× swing.

9. **The judge pipeline is validated but imperfect.** On CEBaB, applying the two-judge rule rejects .84 of edits that fail the human labels and yields .94 precision among accepted pairs (vs .83 for all judged pairs), at the cost of keeping only .50 of human-correct edits. Inter-judge agreement on acceptance is low-to-moderate (κ .06–.55), largely because the Qwen judge is far stricter (it accepts .44–.72 of placebo edits, gpt-oss-120b .88–.97). Two further LLM readers rate .12–.22 of accepted negation edits as changing the opinion, its intensity or a fact — mostly where a negation was *added*.

## Authors & Credibility

Single author: **Ayal Klein** (ayalk@ariel.ac.il), code at `github.com/kleinay/decodability-is-not-validity`. This is a preprint (arXiv:2611.07805v1, cs.CL, 5 Nov 2026), not peer-reviewed at the time of writing. The paper is a methods/measurement contribution rather than a systems contribution, so credibility rests on protocol design and reproducibility rather than on scale or benchmark wins — and on those axes it is unusually careful for the probing literature: placebo controls, two independent judges with blind presentation, human-label validation on CEBaB, planted-error probes, robustness tables under every filter, an explicit disjointness audit, and an independent reimplementation of every metric from saved raw scores matching to <10⁻⁸. The citation base is well chosen for the argument (Campbell & Fiske 1959; Cronbach & Meehl 1955; Messick 1995; Jacobs & Wallach 2021 on measurement validity; Hewitt & Liang 2019; Kumar et al. 2022; Ravichander et al. 2021; Elazar et al. 2021; Belrose et al. 2023; Canby et al. 2025; Mueller et al. 2026; plus several 2026 contemporaneous probing-validity papers), and the framing is deliberately narrow ("our claim is narrower than 'probes are invalid'"). The obvious credibility caveat is scope: two 7–9B models, one author, four English datasets, all sentiment/occupation/toxicity.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty — incremental but well-aimed.** That accuracy cannot detect spurious reliance is not new: Kumar et al. (2022) showed probes trained on spuriously correlated data rely on the spurious attribute, Hewitt & Liang (2019) showed probe accuracy need not reflect use, and Canby et al. (2025) showed representation interventions trade completeness against selectivity. What is new is (a) treating the *set of accuracy-equivalent probes* as the object of study rather than a hand-picked pair, (b) doing it with judge-filtered, placebo-controlled input edits rather than representation-space interventions, and (c) closing the loop with a development-time selection experiment that has a cost curve. The paper is honest about this lineage; it does not claim to have discovered the phenomenon.

**Rigor — genuinely strong on measurement, with three real soft spots.** The good: placebo edits give a noise floor; two blind judges plus human validation on CEBaB; planted-error probes that include *strength* changes (the exact confound for negation edits); an explicit check that rankings survive every filter; and a reproducibility audit that includes an independent reimplementation. The soft spots:

1. **The winning criterion is one the author defines as not a validity measure.** CONFLICT is AUROC on the `T≠N` cells, and the paper states plainly that at full reversal `s = −N` also ranks `T` perfectly — it is a "stress test, not a validity measure." So the practical recommendation ("select by CONFLICT") rests on a heuristic that is admitted to be invalid as a *measurement*, and its advantage is partly mechanical: it penalizes any direction aligned with the nuisance, including alignment that is legitimately part of the construct. The paper never tests the failure mode that matters for deployment — whether CONFLICT selection degrades a probe on a nuisance that is *constitutive* of the target (e.g. discarding the compositional "not good" signal, which is real sentiment, because it correlates with negation). The CEBaB result hints at the risk: CFN .44–.69 for the CONFLICT range means even the most conflict-averse standard extractor is still substantially food-sensitive.

2. **Every correlated regime is manufactured by resampling a fixed pool.** ρtr ∈ {.5,.7,.9} is imposed by reweighting the four (T,N) cells, not by collecting naturally correlated training data. The paper checks that the imposed rates roughly match nature for BiasBios (.85/.91) and approximately for CEBaB (.80/.73), and labels Amazon-1P a "stress test" outright — natural first-person prevalence is .66 vs .68, i.e. the ρtr=0.9 condition for that setting *cannot occur* in real review data. This is defensible for the causal claim (manipulating ρ is exactly how you show ρ drives the effect), but it means the flagship "first person absorbed more than negation" number describes a regime no practitioner will ever train in. A reader should not port `.12–.17` into a claim about naturally trained sentiment probes.

3. **The headline magnitude is filter-sensitive even though the ranking is not.** On CEBaB the near-best CFN range is .41–1.15 on all human edits and .26–.89 on accepted ones. The author reports this openly (Table 8) and shows rankings hold, which is the right move — but it also means "up to several-fold" is a claim about ordering, not about a stable effect size. Relatedly, "several-fold in three of the four settings" is carried almost entirely by BiasBios (.09–.42, 4.7×) and Amazon-1P (Gemma .06–.25, 4.2×); the CEBaB equivalent set is only 1.5× on Qwen (.54–.83), so the phrase overstates that setting.

**Other specific concerns.**

- **A 0.01 band on a saturated metric is nearly vacuous.** "Within .01 of the best" is a sensible near-best definition when DEC ≈ .93, but on BiasBios DEC is .998 and 66 of 70 extractors qualify, 65 of them statistically equivalent. The most striking result in the paper ("65 equivalent probes, 4.7× spread") is therefore also the one where "competitive" means "essentially all comers", including weak, badly regularized probes whose scores are numerically fragile. Since CFN is normalized by ΔT, which the paper concedes is "unstable for weak extractors", the honest reading is that the BiasBios spread is real but its *upper* end may be inflated by degenerate probes.
- **Test-pool reuse across conditions.** One fixed test pool is reweighted into the four (T,N) cells for each ρ rather than resampled, so the three ρ conditions share items and their intervals are not independent; the equivalence reference is also chosen on the test pool (acknowledged as "descriptive"). Neither undermines the within-condition comparisons, but the cross-ρ narrative ("the effect disappears at ρ=0.5") is inferred from non-independent estimates.
- **No calibrated zero for "how sensitive should a score be?"** Placebo edits provide a floor, but the floor is not magnitude-matched for CEBaB (human rewrites are larger than synonym swaps — the paper says so), so a CFN of .25 on CEBaB cannot be read as "25% spurious" against a known-good reference. There is no human-sensitivity upper bound: one would like to know how much a *human* service-sentiment judgment moves when only the food sentence is rewritten. Without that, "sensitive" is relative to the probe population, not to the construct.
- **Scope is narrower than the framing suggests.** Linear readouts of mean-pooled residual streams (with a last-token ablation that preserves the pattern), two 7–9B models, one named nuisance per setting, English only. Nonlinear probes, SAE features, and larger models are untested — and SAE features are precisely where practitioners now read concepts. The paper says this in Limitations, but the title's generality ("Language Model Concept Probes") runs ahead of the evidence.
- **Judge reliability is mediocre where it matters.** κ .06–.55 across settings; the Qwen judge accepts only .44–.72 of placebo edits and .62 of "target kept" on CEBaB, so the accepted CEBaB set is 41% of judged pairs and demonstrably a selective subset (recall .50 against human-correct edits). Using the *intersection* of two disagreeing judges is conservative for precision but biases the edit population toward "easy" cases — plausibly *understating* nuisance sensitivity on CEBaB, which would make the paper's own headline conservative there. That is a good direction for the error to point, but it is not quantified.
- **A missing ablation that would have been cheap:** the paper shows CONFLICT selection works and that erasure lowers but does not remove sensitivity, but never reports the *joint* condition (erased probe selected by CONFLICT) against a probe trained on decorrelated data at matched DEC — the one comparison that would tell a practitioner whether to fix the data or fix the readout. Table 13 gets close (erased + CONFLICT: CEBaB .69→.34, BiasBios .22→.03) but the decorrelated-training arm is not in the same table.
- **Single author, no peer review, four settings, all sentiment-flavored.** The BiasBios and Civil results are the only non-sentiment targets, and Civil is demoted to an appendix because 87% of identity swaps fail validation — a defensible call, but it means the fairness-relevant evidence is the thinnest in the paper even though the ethics statement leans on it.

None of this is fatal. The core claim — accuracy-equivalent probes are not selectivity-equivalent, and the difference follows training-set correlation — is demonstrated with appropriate controls and survives every robustness check the author runs. The overreach is in the practical recommendation (built on a metric the author himself declines to call valid) and in the title's breadth.

## Strengths

- **The right theoretical frame, applied concretely.** Importing discriminant validity from psychometrics could easily be decorative; here it yields two computable estimators (`|LEAK|` needs only labels; `CFN` needs edits) and a falsifiable prediction (decorrelated training should remove the effect), which is then tested.
- **The negative control is the best experiment in the paper.** Holding the reviews fixed and swapping in a nuisance with *no* natural correlation (first person, .66 vs .68) while keeping one with a strong natural correlation (negation, .64 vs .30) is exactly the contrast needed to kill the obvious alternative explanation. That the uncorrelated nuisance is absorbed *more* under imposed correlation, and that both effects vanish at ρtr=0.5, is a clean causal story.
- **Placebo controls and dual blind judges.** Most probing critiques compare against nothing; this one compares against synonym edits that should move nothing, and reports the floor (median |CFN| .01–.06) next to the effect.
- **The selection experiment makes the critique actionable.** Requiring nuisance labels on development data but *no* counterfactuals at deployment is a realistic constraint, and reporting the DEC cost alongside every CFN gain (including the criterion that backfires) is the kind of honesty that makes a methods paper usable.
- **Unusual reproducibility discipline.** Independent metric reimplementation to <10⁻⁸, disjointness audit (including two BiasBios test texts removed for duplicating training text), orientation-vs-label checks, PLS k=1 ≡ diff-of-means verified numerically, and every table regenerated by script from saved results.
- **Intellectual honesty about scope.** The discussion explicitly narrows the claim, the limitations section names the unexamined-nuisance problem and the judge-validation gap, and the abstract's "three of the four settings" is accurate rather than rounded up.

## Limitations

- **One nuisance per setting** (two for Amazon, analyzed separately). Selectivity with respect to unexamined nuisances is unknown, and nuisance interactions are not studied.
- **Edits are LLM-filtered, not newly human-annotated**, except on CEBaB. Judge agreement is κ .06–.55, accepted pairs are a selective subset (41% on CEBaB), and two further LLM readers find .12–.22 of accepted negation edits change the opinion, its intensity, or a fact — mostly negation *additions*. No human validation exists for BiasBios or Amazon.
- **Correlated regimes are synthetic** (resampling to ρtr), matching natural rates only approximately (BiasBios well, CEBaB approximately, Amazon-1P not at all by design).
- **CFN is normalized by the class score gap**, unstable for weak extractors, so analysis is restricted to competitive probes; strict equivalence is established for only a handful of CEBaB extractors (9 of 37 near-best for Qwen, 8 of 39 for Gemma).
- **Linear readouts only**, mean-pooled residual stream (last-token as ablation), two 7–9B models. Nonlinear probes, SAE features, and other model families untested.
- **Measures concern what a score tracks in the input text, not what the model computes with it** — the paper is explicit that this is not a behavioral or causal-use claim.
- **Residual "completeness" is deliberately not reported**, on the argument that it is a property of the estimator rather than of what the score measures — defensible, but it means the paper cannot speak to the completeness/selectivity trade-off that the intervention literature (Canby et al., 2025; Huang et al., 2024) is organized around.
- **Small-n edge cases:** Civil's 400 reverse swaps and 762 forward swaps survive validation at only 13%/29%, so the appendix result rests on a heavily filtered subset; the paper's own numbers (CFN .24–.45 at ρtr=0.9, CONFLICT < .5) are directionally consistent with the main settings but thinly supported.

## Relevance

直接命中"LLM Hidden States & Interpretability"：它给探针/概念读取这类内部表征测量提供了一套可复用的效度检验（|LEAK| 只需标签，CFN 只需"只改 nuisance"的编辑 + 安慰剂对照），并证明业界默认的 held-out AUROC 既不能排序也不能筛选探针的选择性。若你在做表征监控、概念探针或 SAE 特征的评测，这篇的实验协议（双裁判盲评 + 安慰剂地板 + 训练相关性操纵 + 开发集选择实验）可以直接搬到自己关心的 nuisance 上。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. **Does CONFLICT selection damage probes on construct-constitutive nuisances?** The paper never tests a nuisance that is genuinely part of the target (negation as compositional sentiment, "not good" → "bad"). Selecting a sentiment probe to be conflict-free with negation may discard real signal; a matched-DEC comparison on a compositional benchmark would settle whether the recommendation is safe or merely accuracy-neutral.
2. **Data fix vs. readout fix at matched DEC.** Erasure lowers CFN (.22→.07 on BiasBios) but does not remove it, and decorrelated training removes the systematic effect entirely. Which is cheaper at equal decodability, and do they compose? Table 13 has the erased+CONFLICT arm; the decorrelated-training arm is missing from the same comparison.
3. **How much of the BiasBios 4.7× spread is degenerate probes?** With 65 of 66 extractors statistically equivalent at DEC .998, the upper CFN bound (.42) may come from badly regularized configurations. Reporting CFN against probe norm / margin, or restricting to the top-k by development DEC, would tell us whether the spread survives among *good* probes.
4. **What is the human sensitivity baseline?** If human readers' service-sentiment judgments also shift when only the food sentence is rewritten, then part of CFN is construct-irrelevant by construction, not probe error. A small human study on the same 210 CEBaB pairs would calibrate the metric.
5. **Do these results transfer to SAE features and nonlinear probes?** Linear readouts of mean-pooled states are the simplest case; sparse features with monosemanticity claims are the case practitioners actually deploy. The edit protocol transfers directly — the question is whether the accuracy–selectivity decoupling is worse, better, or different in kind.
6. **Can nuisance sensitivity be predicted without edits at all?** `|LEAK|` needs only labels and correlates usefully with CFN in some settings but is described as unreliable for selection (it backfires on Amazon-Neg). Is there a label-only statistic — e.g. the angle between the target direction and within-class nuisance directions, which the paper shows predicts food sensitivity at MAE .11 — that could serve as a cheap pre-screen before collecting any counterfactuals?
