Key metric

[0.184–0.39]

nats / token · 7B student

The measured mismatch budget: 230×–488× the BF16 engine's 0.0008 floor. No run was tested inside the bracket.

Why this matters

If FP8 rollouts are safe, OPD keeps 1.93× more KV-cache tokens and, with async, reaches quality 1.62× sooner — screened in 24 minutes, not 6 hours.

Critical edge

The RL comparison is confounded: the GRPO and REINFORCE baselines never improved held-out accuracy and used different prompts, so “RL breaks at 12–60×” describes these runs, not RL.

Critical assessment · confounded baseline

01

Methodology

Perturb only the rollout engine, price every perturbation in one currency, score with a fixed BF16 referee, then admit engines cheaply.

01

Break one side only

Three student–teacher pairs: 7B and 1.5B DeepSeek-R1 distills, plus Llama-3.2-3B. Teacher, trainer and every checkpoint stay in BF16; only the vLLM engine is perturbed — FP8/NVFP4, up to 8 stale versions, or injected weight noise.

02

One currency

Quantization (bits), asynchrony (versions) and numerics (noise) all become the expected per-token KL between engine q and trainer pθ over the prefixes the engine actually visits: D in nats/token. The untouched BF16 engine already costs 0.0008.

03

A fixed referee

Quality is held-out KL to the teacher on 530 AIME24 / MATH-500 problems, always evaluated in BF16. A run fails at Δ ≥ 20% with a problem-paired bootstrap interval excluding zero. Rule and readings were pre-registered before the jobs ran.

04

Cheap admission

Two responses × 16 problems, re-scored by the standard engine. Admit iff 1.5·D̂ < 0.41 — an effective line of 0.27 nats/token. That replaces the 5.5–6 h, 8-GPU run used to answer the same question.

Recipe · 200 steps · 8 prompts × 8 samples · DAPO-Math-17k · temp 1 · lr 1e-6 · no KL penalty, entropy bonus or importance correction · BF16 generation + teacher scoring = 76% of step time

02

03

What holds, what doesn't

The mechanism work is the durable part; the budget and the RL contrast are the parts to hold loosely.

Strengths

  • One currency. Bits, stale versions and numeric noise become nats/token, so “230× the standard engine” finally means something measurable.
  • Mechanism, not correlation. 93%–99% of FP8-cache mismatch is layer 1; a keys-only emulation pins 82% of that on key rounding, and the explanation correctly predicts Llama's and Qwen3's insensitivity.
  • Nearly free admission. 8/8 pre-registered verdicts, 24 minutes on one GPU against 5.5–6 hours on eight — roughly 1/700 of a training run.
  • Pre-registration with a reported failure. The speed rule fixed in advance did not hold, and the paper says so.

Limitations

  • One close pair, math only, 200 steps. The 1.5B budget is never located; Qwen3-4B was dropped because plain OPD did not train on it.
  • No run inside [0.184, 0.39]. At a stricter 6.35% line the ordering inverts — 0.055 fails where 0.097 passes — so the budget is a screening heuristic, not a certificate.
  • The RL comparison is not a fair test. Baselines never learned, prompts differ, and only failure was assessed.
  • No async-BF16 arm in the time-to-quality table, so the 1.62× cannot be split between asynchrony and FP8 — and async BF16 alone already gives 1.586–1.624×.
  • Thin statistics. One run per configuration; intervals resample problems, not seeds; NVFP4 is emulated and has no memory or speed number.

Open questions

What the next run should test
01

Does the budget shrink with the teacher gap?

The 7B teacher is an RL fine-tune of the student, so the pair is close by construction. A 32B teacher into a 7B student might cut tolerance by an order of magnitude.

02

What happens past 200 steps?

The 0.39 level passes at step 100 and fails at 200, and the error-to-update ratio rises from 0.93 to 1.88. Production runs are far longer than 200 steps.

03

Is “first layer” the cause — or “largest key norm”?

A QK-normalized model with an artificially amplified first-layer key scale would separate network position from key magnitude.

04

Do the RL conclusions survive a healthy baseline?

Re-run the sweep with a recipe that demonstrably improves held-out accuracy, and with matched prompts. Then the 230× vs 12× contrast is about objectives, not runs.
