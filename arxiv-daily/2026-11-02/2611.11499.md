> **一句话总结：** JoMoHa 把"写运行时脚手架的 Engineer"和"在脚手架下执行任务的 Solver"当作两个策略，用同一次任务执行的共享奖励通过 SAO actor–critic 同时训练，在 ALFWorld/WebShop/DBBench 上平均成功率 70.3%（ALFWorld 97.0%，比 WHALE 高 16.5 点），并先用固定脚手架的对照实验把朴素交替训练 83.0%→31.5% 的崩塌分解为"Solver 退化 34.5 点 + 兼容性损失 13.0 点"。

> **TL;DR:** The paper trains two RL policies from one shared task reward — an Engineer that writes an executable harness and a Solver that acts under it — and reports 97.0% success on ALFWorld vs 84.0% for Agent SFT + Harness-R1 (+13.0) and 80.5% for WHALE (+16.5), with a 70.3% three-benchmark average vs 64.2% / 63.1%. The real contribution is the diagnosis, not the headline: by freezing a saved harness and swapping Solver checkpoints, they show naive alternating training loses 47.5 points, of which 34.5 is Solver degradation with no harness at all and 13.0 is growing harness incompatibility. Read it if you care about harness/runtime as a trainable object or about silent state-tracking bugs in agent scaffolding; skip if you want evidence that the gains hold beyond a single Qwen3.5-9B run and a near-saturated ALFWorld.

## 核心贡献

- **诊断交替训练为何崩塌。** 朴素地分阶段交替更新 Solver 与 Engineer，ALFWorld 成功率从 83.0% 升到 90.5% 后连续跌到 78.5%、48.5%、31.5%。作者用"固定同一份保存的脚手架、只换 Solver checkpoint"的对照，把总跌幅 −47.5 点精确分解为 −34.5（Solver 自身退化，去掉脚手架也退步）与 −13.0（兼容性损失，同一脚手架对后期 Solver 的伤害从 −4.0 扩大到 −17.0）。
- **首个把脚手架生成当作可训练策略、与任务策略联合 RL 的框架。** Engineer 产出可执行代码，Solver 在其下多轮交互，两者各自只在自己生成的 token 上更新，不穿过程序求梯度，共享同一个任务奖励——作者称之为递归自我改进（RSI），因为"改进运行时的策略"本身也在被改进。
- **四项协作机制适配双策略设定。** Context（给 critic 喂协作条件摘要）、ValueNorm（按角色分别归一化回报尺度）、Peer（用伙伴轨迹的似然比对优势加权，截断到 [0.5, 2]）、Schedule（joint/joint/Solver-only，Engineer:Solver 更新比 2:3）。
- **三环境实证。** ALFWorld 97.0%、WebShop 45.8%、DBBench 68.0%，平均 70.3%，在评估的方法中三项均最高；增益高度集中在需要"先改变物体状态再放置"的 ALFWorld 类别（Clean +18.2、Heat +17.3、Cool +24.8）。
- **机制层面的失败归因。** 轨迹分析给出具体 bug：脚手架用字面量点号键 `ctx["action"]["final_action"]` 去取嵌套字段，导致动作状态永不更新，同一个 Solver 无脚手架 14 轮完成、带旧脚手架 50 轮超时。

## Key Contributions

- **A controlled diagnosis of alternating model/harness training.** Rather than blaming the drop on "bad harnesses", they hold one saved harness $h_2$ fixed and evaluate it against earlier and later Solver checkpoints on identical ALFWorld tasks. The harness penalty grows from −4.0 points ($\theta_S^{(2)}$) to −17.0 points ($\theta_S^{(3)}$), while the no-harness column falls 82.5% → 48.0%. The accounting identity −47.5 = −34.5 (Solver decline) + −13.0 (compatibility loss) is the paper's cleanest piece of evidence.
- **Joint RL over an executable harness policy and a task policy.** The unit of learning is a complete attempt: generated program, the multi-turn rollout under it, and the terminal reward. Both actors are updated on their own tokens with separate critics and separate KL references; the shared scalar reward is the only coupling.
- **Four coordination adaptations borrowed from cooperative multi-agent RL and re-tuned for the LLM setting.** The paper is explicit about why the transfer is non-trivial: the two roles have disjoint token histories, different decision horizons (one program vs. many turns), and a program that conditions the partner's entire rollout.
- **Evidence that the learned program is doing real work at fixed weights.** Cross-evaluating saved programs against saved Solvers (Table 5) separates "the Solver got better" from "the runtime got better": at the later Solver, the later program gives 96.5% vs 92.5% for the initial program and 91.5% with no harness.
- **An RSI framing with a released codebase** (`github.com/xudongwu-0/coevolve`).

## Methodology

**Setting.** An Engineer policy $\pi_{\theta_E}$ receives historical feedback $e$ (prior code, scores, traces) and samples a proposal trajectory $\tau_E$; a validation/fallback map turns it into an executable harness $h = V(\tau_E)$, with invalid proposals falling back to $h = \varnothing$ (base executor only). The Solver $\pi_{\theta_S}$ then acts under $h$ for a full episode, producing $\tau_S$ and a terminal reward $R(x, \tau_S)$ — binary on ALFWorld. The objective is simply $J(\theta_E,\theta_S) = \mathbb{E}[R(x,\tau_S)]$; no gradient flows through program execution.

**Backbone.** Both roles use SAO (Single-Rollout Asynchronous Optimization, Hou et al. 2026): one rollout per prompt, a learned value model, token-level ratio decoupling, and a KL penalty to a fixed per-role reference policy.

**Context-aware critics.** Each critic sees its own token history $z_{i,t}$ plus a short collaboration summary $c_i$ describing task identifier, training version, and role-specific execution conditions. The context goes to critics only, never to actors — so it shapes the baseline, not the behavior.

**ValueNorm.** Because the Engineer emits one program while the Solver emits many turns, their returns differ in scale despite sharing the reward. Critic targets are normalized per role with running statistics $(m_i, \sigma_i)$ and predictions are de-normalized before advantages are computed; this is deliberately kept separate from standardizing actor advantages.

**Peer weighting.** Each paired sample is reweighted by the likelihood ratio of the recorded partner trajectory under the current policy snapshot versus the collecting snapshot, clipped to $[0.5, 2]$ and treated as a constant. Stale pairs — collected under a partner that has since drifted — count less.

**Asymmetric schedule.** Rounds run joint, joint, Solver-only (2:3 Engineer:Solver). Solver-only rounds freeze the Engineer but still sample fresh harnesses with unit partner weights, giving the Solver extra adaptation to a moving runtime without paying for another Engineer update.

**Setup.** Qwen3.5-9B task model; Engineer and Solver are Qwen3.5-9B-class actors with independent critics, initialized from Agent SFT; 8 paired episodes per round for 144 rounds; deployment picks a saved harness plus a frozen Solver checkpoint, with model and harness selection on a held-out validation split.

## Results & Highlights

- **ALFWorld: 97.0% vs 84.0% for Agent SFT + Harness-R1 (+13.0 points) and 80.5% for WHALE (+16.5).** The gain is concentrated in state-transforming categories: Clean 100.0 vs 81.8 (+18.2), Heat 96.6 vs 79.3 (+17.3), Cool 97.2 vs 72.4 (+24.8), while Pick (100.0 vs 97.7), Look (100.0 vs 100.0), and Pick2 (91.8 vs 87.8) move by 0.0–4.0 — i.e. the method wins exactly where the paper's own failure analysis says harness state tracking matters, and is at ceiling elsewhere.
- **WebShop and DBBench gains are small: 45.8% vs 43.4% (WHALE, +2.4) and 68.0% vs 65.7% (Agent SFT + Harness-R1, +2.3).** WebShop score is 73.9 vs 71.63. So the 70.3% average versus 64.2% (+6.1) and 63.1% (+7.2) is carried almost entirely by ALFWorld's +16.5; on the other two benchmarks the margin is a couple of points.
- **Alternating training collapses monotonically: 83.0 → 90.5 → 78.5 → 48.5 → 31.5% across four stages, versus 97.0% for JoMoHa over 144 rounds.** That is a 65.5-point spread between two ways of "training both components", and it is the paper's strongest motivational number.
- **The decomposition is exact: −47.5 = −34.5 + −13.0.** With $h_2$ fixed, success drops 82.5 → 78.5 (−4.0) for the earlier Solver but 48.0 → 31.0 (−17.0) for the later one; at $\theta_S^{(3)}$ the same harness flips 18.0% of tasks from success to failure and only 1.0% the other way. Removing the harness does not restore the earlier Solver (82.5 → 48.0), so the damage is not purely inference-time interference.
- **Freezing the Engineer costs 6.5 points at the matched step and 5.5 at its best checkpoint:** Frozen Engineer reaches 90.5% at step 144 (best 91.5% at 108) vs 97.0% for JoMoHa, while Weight-only scores 89.5% and Harness-only 80.5%. This is the cleanest one-sided-vs-joint control, and it survives giving the control its best checkpoint.
- **Learned programs help at fixed weights:** at the later Solver, later program 96.5% > earlier program 95.0% > initial program 92.5% > no harness 91.5%; the later program is +5.0 over no harness with no observed success→failure regressions. Both learned programs lift the earlier Solver from 91.5% to 95.0%.
- **Coordination components are individually worth +3.0 to +4.0 and jointly +8.5:** Joint-only 88.5%; +Schedule 92.0%; +Context 92.5%; +Peer 92.0%; +ValueNorm 91.5%; full method 97.0%.
- **Residual weakness is Pick2 at 91.8%**, the category requiring multiple objects to be collected and placed — the authors flag it themselves as remaining headroom.

## Authors & Credibility

Xudong Wu, Guangchen Lan, Mingqi Yuan, and Jiayu Chen — The University of Hong Kong and Salesforce AI Research, with Jiayu Chen as corresponding author. The group writes in the "harness as an optimization object" lineage and cites the immediate predecessors (Harness-R1, Meta-Harness, WHALE) accurately, including the specific detail that WHALE's published instantiation uses online rejection-sampling fine-tuning rather than RL and a fixed external proprietary model as the harness proposer. That is a fair characterization, and it is what makes their "first joint RL" claim survive a narrow reading. The paper is a preprint (v1, 8 Nov 2026) with no venue yet; code is promised at a public repo. Experimental infrastructure is inherited from Harness-R1 (same training/test splits, same task model), which helps comparability but also means most baseline numbers are not independently reproduced here.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The framing — two policies, one shared scalar reward, disjoint token histories — is a sensible and, as far as I can tell, genuinely new instantiation. The individual coordination mechanisms are all imported (ValueNorm from van Hasselt et al. 2016, peer/likelihood weighting from off-policy correction, asymmetric schedules from cooperative MARL), and the paper says so. The contribution is the assembly plus the diagnosis, not new RL machinery. The "first" claim is defensible only because WHALE uses RSFT + external search; it is a priority claim resting on one competitor's implementation choice, which is a fragile footing in a field moving this fast.

**Rigor — the good part.** The fixed-harness cross-evaluation (Table 3a, Table 5) is the kind of control most harness papers skip. Holding $h_2$ byte-identical while swapping Solver checkpoints, and reporting both the within-row harness effect and the down-column no-harness trend, actually separates the two causal channels rather than asserting them. The trajectory case is unusually concrete: the harness writes the completed action into a nested field but reads it back with a literal dotted key `ctx["action"]["final_action"]`, so state never updates, and a Solver that finishes the task in 14 turns bare times out at 50 turns. That is a real bug class, not a hand-wave.

**Rigor — the weak parts.** Three things bother me. First, **no variance anywhere.** Every number is a single training run; there are no seeds, no confidence intervals, no evaluation-set sizes. ALFWorld's standard test split is 134 tasks, so 97.0% vs 90.5% is roughly 9 tasks — and the Frozen Engineer control's own curve moves 90.5 → 91.5 between steps 108 and 144, i.e. the noise floor is at least a point. The WebShop (+2.4) and DBBench (+2.7) margins are inside that floor, yet they are folded into a headline "70.3% average".

**Rigor — the missing ablation.** Table 6 is add-one-in from Joint-only only. There is no leave-one-out from the full method, so we cannot tell whether ValueNorm (the weakest single addition at 91.5%, below Joint-only + Context) is actually load-bearing or just along for the ride. Worse, the components are strongly superadditive: individually +3.0 to +4.0, jointly +8.5. The paper asserts the full combination is best without explaining the interaction, and the schedule ratio (2:3), the Peer clip ([0.5, 2]), the 8 episodes per round, and the 144-round budget are all fixed with zero sensitivity analysis. Any of these could be doing the work.

**Baseline fairness.** Table 4's caption states that, apart from JoMoHa and WHALE, all scores are taken from Harness-R1. That means the 16.5-point ALFWorld gap against WHALE is a like-for-like local comparison (good), but the gap against Agent SFT + Harness-R1 mixes methods trained under a different budget and stack (less good). The Harness-only control at 80.5% is also structurally handicapped: it trains the Engineer against a frozen Agent-SFT Solver, so the harness must compensate for a Solver that never adapts — precisely the setup Harness-R1 was designed for, and precisely the setup the paper argues is insufficient. It is a fair "one-sided recipe" datapoint but not an upper bound on harness-only training.

**The alternating-training strawman risk.** The 31.5% collapse is dramatic, and the mechanism they diagnose is real. But the baseline's phase length, learning rates, and reset behavior are never ablated. WHALE — which also alternates, just every 8 steps — sits at 80.5%, not 31.5%. So the honest reading is "long-phase alternation without compatibility control collapses", not "alternation collapses". The paper's prose mostly respects this distinction, but Figure 1 and the abstract let the stronger reading stand.

**Significance and red flags.** ALFWorld is close to saturated for this method class (three categories at 100.0), so most of the average is ceiling; the claim "this pattern extends beyond ceiling effects" rests on error-removal percentages (83.6% on Heat, 89.9% on Cool) computed against the best competitor's error count, which is a reasonable normalization but not a substitute for a harder benchmark. Only one base model (Qwen3.5-9B) is tested — no cross-model transfer, no test of whether a harness learned for one Solver helps another, which is the obvious question for anyone who wants to reuse a harness. And there is no cost accounting: 144 rounds × 8 paired episodes × two policies, plus an Engineer that emits code every round, is a real compute bill that the paper never quantifies against the baselines' budgets. Reproducibility is middling: code is released, but the SAO backbone and the evaluation harness are both external dependencies.

## Strengths

- The diagnosis is genuinely well-designed: a fixed saved harness plus swapped Solver checkpoints yields a clean additive decomposition instead of a correlation.
- The failure mechanism is concrete and memorable (dotted-key lookup, overwritten inventory evidence, stale "go clean it" guidance after cleaning already succeeded) — this is a bug taxonomy practitioners can act on.
- Honest internal controls: Frozen Engineer is given its best checkpoint, not its final one; Table 5 cross-evaluates programs and Solvers in both directions; Pick2 is reported as the weak category rather than buried.
- The two-policy formalization is clean — separate critics, separate KL references, no differentiation through execution — and should be reusable by anyone building on SAO.
- Clear positioning against WHALE and Harness-R1, including the specific architectural difference (who trains the proposer), rather than a vague "unlike prior work".

## Limitations

- Single run per configuration, no seeds or error bars; the WebShop and DBBench deltas (+2.4, +2.7) are not distinguishable from run-to-run noise at this scale.
- One base model (Qwen3.5-9B); no evidence the learned harness transfers across Solvers or model families, which undermines the "harness as reusable runtime" framing.
- No leave-one-out ablation, no sensitivity analysis for the 2:3 schedule, the [0.5, 2] Peer clip, or the round budget — the superadditive +8.5 from four +3/+4 components is unexplained.
- Most baselines are copied from Harness-R1 rather than re-run under a matched compute budget, so cross-paper comparisons conflate method and budget.
- ALFWorld is effectively saturated for this method (100.0 on three of six categories), and the average is dominated by it; the method's advantage on the two non-household benchmarks is marginal.
- No compute or token accounting for the Engineer's per-round code generation, so the efficiency/accuracy trade-off is unknown.
- The long-phase alternating baseline is a single uncontrolled configuration; the collapse magnitude may be schedule-specific rather than fundamental.

## Relevance

直接命中"自演化智能体"这条线：它把"改进运行时的策略"本身也纳入 RL 训练，是 RSI 的一个干净实例，而不是又一篇靠固定外部强模型来改脚手架的论文。同时对"Agent & Pipeline Silent Failures"极有价值——它诊断的失败正是脚手架静默改写了模型看到的状态（点号键取错字段、inventory 证据被覆盖、任务已完成却继续下达清理指令），并给出了"固定脚手架换 checkpoint"这种可复用的定位手法。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of the 97.0% survives with 3 seeds? What is the ALFWorld evaluation-set size, and what is the minimum detectable difference at that size?
- Does the harness learned by JoMoHa transfer to a different Solver (e.g. a 4B or a 30B model, or a different model family) at fixed weights? Table 5 varies checkpoints within one run but never across models — the cross-model case is the one that would justify calling the harness a reusable artifact.
- What does leave-one-out from the full method give, especially for ValueNorm, whose add-one-in result (91.5%) is below Joint-only + Context (92.5%)?
- Why is the combination superadditive (+8.5 from four components worth +3 to +4 each)? Is it the schedule interacting with Peer weighting, or just a longer effective Solver budget?
- How sensitive is the result to the 2:3 Engineer:Solver ratio and to the 144-round budget — does the alternating baseline recover if given the same total Solver updates?
- What is the wall-clock and token cost of the Engineer relative to a harness-only or weight-only recipe, and does the accuracy gain survive a compute-matched comparison?
- Could the compatibility loss be detected online (e.g. by monitoring the harness-effect term $\Delta_h$ during training) and used as a training signal rather than only as a post-hoc diagnostic?
