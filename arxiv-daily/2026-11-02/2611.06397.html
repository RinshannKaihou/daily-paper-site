01 / Test design

Hold the answer chunk still. Change only what surrounds it.

A controlled intervention on non-prefix reuse: each prompt is an instruction prefix, m independently encoded chunks, then the question.

01

Pin target + slot

The answer-bearing chunk and its position are fixed for every question, so position effects cannot masquerade as distractor effects.

02

Swap provenance

OTHER: Walden prose. RAND: unrelated same-corpus chunks. SIM: hard negatives, near-duplicate sessions, nearest API docs.

03

Score on Q*

Only questions that no-context fails and full prefill answers under all three sources. Full-prefill accuracy is 1 by construction, so reuse accuracy is retention.

04

Match the budget

Token selectors (CacheBlend, EPIC, ProphetKV, question attention) against chunk allocations at 5% and 10%; paired bootstrap and Holm correction.

Controls: chunk lengths matched within 10% for 92–100% of swaps; a normalised gold answer leaks into a distractor in at most 1.9% of questions (4.6% for SIM). Qwen3-8B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2; bf16, greedy, ≤32 new tokens.

02 / What the numbers say

Damage is additive across distractors — so patch the one that carries the answer.

The loss decomposes by what the distractors are. Restoring one distractor barely moves the needle; recomputing the known target chunk moves it a lot.

03 / What holds up

Strengths

  • The design is the contribution. Pinning the target and its slot makes distractor provenance the only variable; the three-term split falls out and is reusable.
  • Statistical discipline. Paired bootstrap intervals, Holm correction on multi-setting claims, and negative results reported rather than buried.
  • A mechanism with a falsifier. Restoring earlier chunks restores the value of KV insertion (+0.14 interaction); recomputation reproduces full prefill on 269 of 270 questions.
  • Replication beyond the main grid. Qwen3-14B and 32B reproduce all five tested results; a block-fine-tuned model is tested too.
04 / What to discount

Limitations

  • Q* is adversarial. 36–151 questions per setting; the abstract's 19–100% loss is not a deployment number.
  • The oracle gap. The target chunk is assumed known; the retriever's top-1 hits only 47–64%, and misses lose to token selection.
  • No serving system. Single-stream TTFT on one RTX 4090 D; no batching, throughput, memory, or cache-management cost.
  • Comparator fidelity. InfoFlow is re-implemented at matched tokens, not latency; some baselines are anonymous preprints.
05 / Open threads

Questions worth carrying forward

Q1

Why does the target-chunk advantage collapse at a 10% budget? Token selectors get competitive; the reversal is observed but not explained.

Q2

Does loss grow with the number of distractors? n is fixed at 29–31, so the additive-odds model is untested outside its calibration range.

Q3

Would a reranker close the oracle gap? Every deployable gain is bounded by chunk identification, which the paper does not solve.

Q4

Can fine-tuning make a cache compatible with any prefix? Tulu3-Block-FT still loses 0.60 of Q* on NQ-Open.
