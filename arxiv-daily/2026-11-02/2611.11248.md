> **一句话总结：** 作者把全球海洋-海冰模式 FESOM2 的默认工作精度降到 32 位，用 60 年（1948–2007, CORE2 网格）回算对照双精度发现：朴素实现会以 1.2×10⁻⁴ psu/yr 持续丢盐、累积 129 ZJ 的热含量亏损并让海温锋面整体偏移；把示踪物倾向与层厚改为 64 位累加、并把每次更新被舍入掉的余项带到下一步（厚度加权版 Kahan 补偿求和）后，单双精度热含量差降到 1.5 ZJ（双精度自身扰动对照为 1.0 ZJ）、海冰差 ≤0.2%，代价是最多 8% 运行时间，而 60 年回算加速 1.35×、CPU 上最高 1.66×。

> **TL;DR:** A full single-precision build of the unstructured-mesh ocean–sea-ice model FESOM2 is validated against double-precision controls over 60-year CORE-II hindcasts (1948–2007, 126,858-vertex CORE2 mesh, 900 s timestep). The naive float32 build is not usable: it loses salt at 1.2×10⁻⁴ psu yr⁻¹ (6.8 mpsu deficit by the last decade), accumulates a −129 ZJ ocean heat-content deficit, and shifts the 1988–2007 mean SST by 0.1649 K RMS — 36× the 0.00463 K spread between two double-precision runs. The fix is not "more precision in the state": keeping the state in 64 bits with 32-bit increments *reverses* the sign of the drift (+1.85 vs −1.87 mpsu at year 20), proving the increments themselves are biased. Accumulating tracer tendencies and layer thicknesses in 64 bits plus carrying the per-step rounding remainder forward (a thickness-weighted Kahan update) brings SST RMS to 0.0046 K — statistically indistinguishable from the 0.0040 K double-precision control — and salinity to 0.47×10⁻⁶ psu per decade (14,000× smaller than before, though still 14× the control). Cost: ≤8% extra runtime; payoff: 1.35× on the 60-year hindcast (52→70 simulated years/day) and 1.02–1.66× across four meshes up to 5 km, plus 40–43% less GPU memory (NG5: 21.6 vs 35.9 GiB/device).

## 核心贡献

- **把"降精度会不会在气候尺度上漂移"从猜测变成可测量的判据。** 作者提出两条验收标准：(1) 守恒量在目标积分长度内不得出现超出对照（双精度扰动孪生实验）散度的数值源/汇；(2) 模拟气候（平均态与变率）与双精度的差异必须远小于模式与观测的误差。轨迹发散本身不算失败——这两条把"混沌发散"和"系统性偏差"彻底分开。
- **定位并修复了一类被误诊的舍入偏差。** 常见假设是"把状态存成高精度就够了"。作者用控制实验证伪了它：64 位状态 + 32 位增量给出 +1.85 mpsu/20 年的漂移，与原始单精度 −1.87 mpsu 量级相同、符号相反——说明增量本身（在 S≈35 psu 附近计算）就被舍入偏置了。真正的修复必须同时覆盖倾向累加、层厚和状态更新三处。
- **给出一个便宜的、可移植的补偿更新格式。** `C = hⁿTⁿ + ΔtRⁿ + rⁿ`，`Tⁿ⁺¹ = fl32(C/hⁿ⁺¹)`，`rⁿ⁺¹ = C − hⁿ⁺¹Tⁿ⁺¹`：把每步被舍掉的余项按层厚加权带到下一步，等价于对"厚度加权示踪物含量"做 Kahan 求和；层厚存 64 位但 halo 交换仍按 32 位值 + 32 位余项发送，通信量不翻倍。
- **把"盐度存成 S−35 异常"这个历史技巧降级为可选。** 在旧实现里它把漂移缩小 32 倍（20 年差 0.064 vs 1.87 mpsu），是主要救命手段；在保守更新之后它不再改变结果（0.48 vs 0.47×10⁻⁶ psu），可以不用。
- **用降精度当调试工具。** 对比 Fortran 与 C++/Kokkos 两个独立实现对同一降精度的响应，暴露了双精度验证漏掉的两个真 bug：未初始化的 Adams–Bashforth 示踪物历史，以及 Redi/GM 增量被直接加到盐度（而非累加到倾向）上——后者修好后精度效应直接减半。
- **工程落地。** 单一 `WP` kind 参数 + 构建选项，改动 36 个源文件，双精度构建与改动前逐位相同；FESOM 2.8.0 起发布，CI 同时构建两种精度，不支持的耦合配置（REcoM、冰架腔）在构建期直接拒绝。

## Key Contributions

- A two-criterion acceptance test for reduced precision in climate models, separating chaotic trajectory divergence (harmless) from a numerical source/sink in a conserved quantity (fatal), operationalised via paired double-precision control runs and daily global budget accounting.
- A mechanistic diagnosis of float32 drift in flux-form tracer transport: three distinct roundings break conservation — (i) the flux×Δt product rounded before being added to two control volumes no longer cancels between them, (ii) rounding the new state at S≈35 psu destroys increments below 3.8×10⁻⁶ psu (stagnation), (iii) rounding layer thickness every step changes control-volume volume.
- The counter-intuitive controlled result that a 64-bit state with 32-bit increments is *not* a fix: it yields +1.85 mpsu after 20 years versus −1.87 mpsu for the naive build — same magnitude, opposite sign, so the bias lives in the increment computation, not just the accumulation.
- A conservative update scheme (64-bit tendencies for advection, diffusion, low-order FCT and implicit column solvers; 64-bit layer thickness; per-tracer 32-bit remainder array `r` carried to the next step) costing at most 8% runtime and requiring no change to halo communication width.
- An independent cross-implementation precision test: the Kokkos GPU port's response to float32 is compared with the Fortran model's, per field, normalised by each implementation's own double-precision variability baseline — a check double-precision verification structurally cannot provide.
- Performance characterisation over four meshes (CORE2 1°, fArc, DARS, NG5 ~5 km) on 128-core AMD EPYC nodes and 4×A100 nodes, including the finding that the speedup tracks working-set-vs-cache size and collapses toward 1.0 at high process counts.
- A coupled-model replication in AWI-ESM3 (FESOM 2.8.3 + OpenIFS 48r1 + LPJ-GUESS), 4×60-year pre-industrial controls, showing the internal heat residual is precision-independent at the 10⁻⁷ W m⁻² level.

## Methodology

**Model and configuration.** FESOM2 solves the hydrostatic Boussinesq primitive equations on an unstructured triangular mesh with cell-vertex finite volumes and a z* ALE vertical coordinate; sea ice is a single-class EVP model sharing the ocean's scalar grid. Fidelity experiments use the CORE2 mesh (126,858 surface vertices, 47/48 layers, nominal 1°), CORE-II interannual forcing 1948–2007, KPP mixing, Gent–McWilliams/Redi, backscatter viscosity, PHC3.0 initial state, and a 900 s timestep — deliberately half the 1800 s production value, because the loss of small increments worsens as Δt shrinks, so the test is harder than production.

**Precision implementation.** All reals are declared with one kind parameter `WP`, set to 4 bytes by a build flag that also adjusts compiler flags and MPI datatypes for halo exchanges and reductions. Kept in 64 bits by design: restart/I-O conversion, time means for output (summing thousands of 32-bit samples biases slowly varying fields), global budget sums (10⁶–10⁹ cancelling terms), and the conjugate-gradient scalars of the sea-surface-height solver while its vectors and matrices stay 32-bit. Subnormal 10⁻⁴⁰ division guards in KPP, which optimizing compilers flushed to zero and turned into 0/0 at step one, were replaced by 10⁻²⁰. CVMix keeps its fixed 64-bit interfaces; this study uses FESOM2's own KPP.

**The conservative tracer update.** In flux form the content hT changes by the divergence of advective and diffusive fluxes, so in exact arithmetic a global integral changes only through boundary fluxes. The implementation accumulates all tracer tendencies in 64 bits (advection, diffusion, low-order FCT, implicit vertical solver columns), computes the new state in 64 bits, and stores the rounding error `r` — weighted by the new thickness — for the next update, so that `hⁿ⁺¹Tⁿ⁺¹ + rⁿ⁺¹` reproduces the 64-bit content. Layer thickness is 64-bit, halo-exchanged as a 32-bit value plus 32-bit remainder. An earlier defect is also fixed: FCT had derived its low-order tendency as a difference of two states, which loses small tendencies; it now uses the flux divergence.

**Experimental design.** Two single-precision 60-year runs (absolute salinity, and S−35 anomaly) are compared against two double-precision runs, one started from temperatures perturbed by 2×10⁻⁴ K. The DP–DP difference is the chaotic-divergence yardstick. Global volume, heat and salt budgets (content, surface flux, residual) are recorded every simulated day. An earlier six-run set with the pre-fix implementation has its own DP control (a pair differing only in salinity representation, hence only in round-off), with similar chaotic spread (0.0046 vs 0.0040 °C SST RMS), so the before/after comparison is like-for-like.

**Performance protocol.** Strong scaling on four meshes (CORE2, fArc = CORE2 + 4.5 km Arctic, DARS = Rossby-radius scaled, NG5 ≈ 5 km quasi-uniform, up to 7.4M vertices / 70 levels), Fortran on CPU and C++/Kokkos on A100, each job running DP, SP, and SP-without-conservative-updates for 300 timesteps twice in mirrored order; runs differ by a median 0.3%. Throughput is projected to production timesteps.

**Cross-implementation test.** One-year CORE2 integrations with JRA55-do 1958 forcing compare the relative L2 distance ‖X_SP − X_DP‖₂/‖X_DP‖₂ for each implementation against its own 5-member DP perturbation baseline (10⁻⁶ K), then take the Kokkos/Fortran ratio.

**Coupled test.** AWI-ESM3 3.5.1 (FESOM 2.8.3 on CORE2 with 1800 s timestep, OpenIFS 48r1 TCo95/L91 in single precision in all runs, LPJ-GUESS, OASIS3-MCT every 2 h), four 60-year pre-industrial runs differing only in ocean precision and a 2×10⁻⁴ K initial perturbation with a shared random seed.

## Results & Highlights

- **The naive float32 build fails the conservation criterion.** Over the 60-year CORE2 hindcast it loses salt at a steady **1.2×10⁻⁴ psu yr⁻¹**, reaching a last-decade deficit of **6.8 mpsu**, while the double-precision control differs by about 10⁻⁴ mpsu — a factor of ~10³–10⁴. It also accumulates a **−129 ZJ** ocean heat-content deficit and lowers volume-mean temperature by **24 mK**, roughly a third of the double-precision spin-up warming (77 mK).
- **The mean state shifts coherently along fronts.** For 1988–2007, SP−DP RMS is **0.1649 K for SST** and **0.0497 psu for surface salinity** — **36× and 30×** the DP control (0.00463 K, 0.00165 psu), with the error concentrated on the North Atlantic Current, the Southern Ocean and the Brazil–Malvinas Confluence. That is a systematic displacement, not noise.
- **After the conservative updates the heat budget is clean.** Heat-content difference stays within **1.5 ZJ** over 60 years against **1.0 ZJ** between the two double-precision runs; the internal (non-surface) heat residual is at most **7.3×10⁻⁶ W m⁻²**, versus a **−0.02 W m⁻²** sink in the earlier anomaly-based implementation. Salinity is the one residual signal: **0.47×10⁻⁶ psu** gain in the last decade, **14×** the DP–DP RMS but **14,000×** smaller than the first implementation's loss.
- **Mean state becomes indistinguishable from the control.** SP−DP SST RMS falls from 0.1649 K to **0.0046 K**, essentially the **0.0040 K** DP–DP value (0.7% of the model's 0.619 °C RMSE against HadISST1); surface salinity RMS falls from 0.0497 to **0.00883 psu** (0.4% of the 0.412 psu WOA09 error, vs 0.00165 psu for the control).
- **Sea ice is the weakest link.** Annual Arctic/Antarctic ice volume and area differ by at most **0.2%**, but the double-precision control spread is **0.07%** — so sea ice is about 3× the control, not equal to it. With absolute salinity in the old code, Arctic ice volume was 1.0% larger and Antarctic volume transiently 7.4% off.
- **Speedups: 1.35× end-to-end, 1.66× peak on CPU, 1.27× on GPU.** The 60-year hindcast goes from **52 to 70 simulated years/day** (1.35×) on four nodes; per-component, tracers gain **1.85×** and the whole ocean **1.76×** while sea ice gains only **1.14×**. Strong scaling gives 1.66× on half a CORE2 node but **1.02× at 4096 processes**; DARS/NG5 hold 1.40–1.64× to 64 nodes. Kokkos on A100 gives **1.05–1.27×** and **40–43% less memory** (NG5: **21.6 vs 35.9 GiB** per device). Conservative updates cost at most **8%** where compute dominates and <1% at the largest process counts.
- **Coupled AWI-ESM3 adds no detectable numerical heat source.** The internal heat residual is **−2.1×10⁻⁴ W m⁻²** in all four runs; SP−DP differs by **5.3×10⁻⁷ W m⁻²**, smaller than the 1.2×10⁻⁶ W m⁻² between perturbed DP twins. But the coupled climate is a weak test: heat content of two near-identical runs diverges by up to **54 ZJ** within decades, against 1.0 ZJ under prescribed forcing.
- **Cross-implementation agreement is good for T/S, poor for sea ice.** Kokkos/Fortran precision-effect ratios are **0.65±0.06 (temperature)**, **0.76±0.08 (SST)**, **0.83±0.09 (salinity)** — i.e. the GPU port is slightly *less* precision-sensitive — but **1.9±0.06 for ice concentration** and up to 2.6× for SST south of 60°S, which the salinity anomaly reduces to 1.2×.

## Authors & Credibility

All six authors are at the Alfred Wegener Institute (AWI) in Bremerhaven, with Thomas Jung also at the University of Bremen's Institute for Environmental Physics. This is the home team for FESOM2: Danilov and Sidorenko are among the model's original developers (Danilov et al., 2017, GMD), Koldunov led its scalability work and the Kokkos port, Streffing led the AWI-CM3/AWI-ESM3 coupled model, and Cheedela implemented the precision changes. Jung is a senior climate scientist and AWI's scientific leadership figure. The paper is written in GMD's style (it cites its own code revisions, PR numbers and commit hashes) and funded through AWI core funding, WarmWorld, TerraDT, TRR181 and EERIE — no vendor money that would bias a "single precision is fine" conclusion. The group has an obvious stake in FESOM2 looking good, but the paper's central result is a negative one about their own first implementation, which is a credibility signal rather than a red flag.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** Genuine and narrower than the abstract implies. Running an ocean model in float32 is not new (NEMO mixed-precision via emulation searches, ECMWF's NEMO4-SI3 forecasts, Veros on GPUs), and compensated summation is 1960s numerical analysis. What is new is (a) the 60-year budget-level evaluation, which the authors correctly note has not been done for a fully single-precision ocean–sea-ice model, and (b) the specific negative result that a high-precision *state* with low-precision *increments* does not fix the drift — the +1.85 vs −1.87 mpsu sign flip at year 20 (Appendix C2) is the single most valuable experiment in the paper, because it contradicts the design assumption behind NEMO 4.2's single-precision build (which keeps T, S, u, v, SSH in double precision). The claim in Sect. 7.2 that this "removes the rounding of the update but not necessarily the bias of increments" is a real, testable critique of a deployed model, and they do not overstate it — they hedge with "not necessarily."

**Rigor.** The experimental design is better than most reduced-precision papers. Using paired DP runs with a 2×10⁻⁴ K perturbation as the chaos yardstick, rather than comparing against a single deterministic reference, is the right call and is what makes "indistinguishable" a meaningful word here. Deliberately halving the timestep to 900 s to make the test harder than production is a nice touch that most authors would skip. The controlled-experiment ladder in Appendix C2 — rule out freshwater budget, global sums, solver, decomposition; then 64-bit state/32-bit increments; then state rounded to 32 bits after a 64-bit step; then the anomaly; then FCT fix; then Kahan alone — is a genuinely well-constructed ablation chain, and the honesty in reporting that Kahan summation alone leaves residuals (a *source* of 0.0017 W m⁻² and a *loss* of 0.43×10⁻⁶ psu, both still above the control) is exactly what a skeptical reader wants.

**Weaknesses that matter.**
1. **Fidelity is validated at one resolution only.** All 60-year budgets are CORE2 at nominal 1°. The paper's own Sect. 7.2 argument says eddy-resolving configurations with 120–240 s timesteps are *more* exposed, and the performance section happily benchmarks DARS and NG5 — but no multi-decade fidelity run exists there. The 5 km claim is a speedup claim, not a fidelity claim, and the abstract's "up to 5 km resolution" phrasing sits next to fidelity claims in a way that invites conflation.
2. **Performance and fidelity are measured on different code versions.** The GPU speedups (1.05–1.27×) come from a Kokkos build *without* conservative updates (Sect. 6 says so explicitly), and the 256/512-node Fortran points also come from an earlier campaign without them. The conservative updates cost ≤8% on CORE2 where compute dominates, so the honest GPU number is likely a few percent lower than 1.27×. The paper discloses this in Appendix B but the headline numbers do not carry the asterisk.
3. **The salinity residual is unexplained.** Single precision still gains 0.47×10⁻⁶ psu per decade, 14× the control, and the authors state plainly: "We have not identified why single precision takes up slightly more salt at the surface." Their own budget shows 99.6% of it enters through the surface salt flux. For a paper whose thesis is "budgets decide acceptability," leaving the one surviving budget anomaly mechanistically unexplained is a real gap — and it matters for the passive-tracer/century-scale applications they flag as untested.
4. **The coupled test is underpowered by the authors' own numbers.** Twin DP runs diverge by up to 54 ZJ; the forced-forcing signal they are trying to detect is ~1 ZJ. So "single precision adds no detectable heat source or sink over 60 years" in AWI-ESM3 is close to unfalsifiable at that noise level — the *budget residual* argument (5.3×10⁻⁷ W m⁻²) is the only informative part, and it is a much weaker statement than the abstract's phrasing suggests. Table D1 makes this visible: Arctic sea-ice volume differs by −22% SP−DP against −26% for the DP twin, i.e. the "agreement" is agreement inside a ±25% spread.
5. **The chaos baseline is a single pair.** The 60-year control spread (0.0040 °C, 1.0 ZJ) is estimated from one perturbed DP twin, not an ensemble; the 5-member baseline appears only in the one-year Kokkos comparison. A one-member estimate of internal variability is thin for statements like "within 1.5 ZJ against 1.0 ZJ."
6. **No head-to-head with the alternatives.** Stochastic rounding is dismissed as needing hardware support (fair), but 16-bit kernels and mixed-precision Runge–Kutta are listed as future work with no measurement, and the NEMO comparison is literature-based rather than run on the same machine. The 1.66× CPU number also comes with a caveat the abstract omits: it is at half a node, and falls to 1.02× at 4096 processes — for anyone running at scale, the realistic gain is much closer to 1.2–1.4×.
7. **Minor but real:** restart files discard `r`, changing heat content by up to 1.8×10⁻⁹ per restart; the domain decomposition alone shifts initial global-mean salinity by 0.0013 mpsu, so precision experiments must hold the partition fixed; and 1.3 TB of raw output is "available on request," which is a soft spot in an otherwise exemplary reproducibility package.

**Significance.** High for the climate-modelling community and, more importantly for a general numerical-computing reader, as a case study: this is the cleanest demonstration I have seen that low-precision drift is a *design* property of the update scheme, not a compiler setting, and that field-level comparisons hide what budget closure reveals within one model year. The "reduced precision as a verification tool" angle (Sect. 7.3) — using float32 to expose an uninitialized Adams–Bashforth history and a misplaced Redi/GM increment that double precision masked — is a transferable debugging technique that deserves wider adoption outside climate science.

## Strengths

- A negative result about the authors' own first implementation, reported at full strength (129 ZJ, 36× control, fronts displaced) rather than buried.
- The right experimental control: paired double-precision runs with a physical perturbation, so "indistinguishable" means "inside the model's own chaotic spread," not "close to a deterministic reference."
- The 64-bit-state/32-bit-increment experiment, which overturns the intuitive fix and is the paper's most transferable finding.
- A careful, staged ablation chain in Appendix C2 that rules out four alternative explanations before landing on the tracer update, and that honestly reports compensated summation *alone* being insufficient.
- A cost figure for the fix (≤8%) measured, not estimated, and reported alongside the fact that it drops below 1% at the largest process counts.
- Exceptional reproducibility: GPL code on GitHub, Zenodo archives with commit hashes and tags, PR numbers, run configurations, plotting scripts and derived data under MIT, and a provenance map from each result to its source revision.
- Honest scoping of what was not tested (eddy-rich, century-scale, biogeochemistry, 16-bit) in the conclusions rather than in a footnote.

## Limitations

- Fidelity is established only at nominal 1° (CORE2) over 60 years; kilometre-scale and eddy-rich configurations — precisely those with the shortest timesteps and hence the largest stagnation risk — are benchmarked for speed but never for drift.
- The surviving salinity gain (0.47×10⁻⁶ psu/decade, 14× the control) is real, systematic, surface-supplied, and mechanistically unexplained.
- The coupled-model validation cannot resolve a heat imbalance smaller than the ~54 ZJ twin spread; the claim there rests on budget residuals, not on climate equivalence.
- The chaos baseline is a single DP twin pair for the 60-year runs.
- GPU and large-process-count speedups come from builds lacking the conservative updates that the fidelity result depends on, so no single configuration is measured for both fidelity and peak performance.
- No measurement of 16-bit kernels, mixed-precision Runge–Kutta, or half-precision solver preconditioning — all flagged as the natural next steps.
- REcoM (biogeochemistry) and CVMix remain double precision, so carbon-cycle configurations of AWI-ESM3 cannot yet use the single-precision build; DIC and alkalinity will need their own conservative updates.
- The 8% overhead is a CORE2 figure; on GPUs and at very high process counts the relative cost of the extra 64-bit accumulations and the extra `r` array traffic is not separately reported.

## Relevance

属于「Reliability under Extreme Performance Optimization」方向：它给出一个完整的"低精度累加偏差"案例——偏差不在状态存储而在增量计算，仅提高状态精度反而让漂移变号；判据是守恒量收支闭合而不是场量比对，且收支在 1 个模式年内就能暴露、场量比对要几十年。这套方法论（补偿求和 + 高精度倾向 + 收支诊断 + 双实现交叉验证）可以直接迁移到 ML 训练/推理的低精度数值可靠性审计。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the +1.85/−1.87 mpsu sign flip reproduce in an LLM-relevant analogue — e.g. float32 optimizer state with bf16 gradients, or bf16 accumulation in a long-context attention denominator? If the increment itself is biased, "keep the master weights in fp32" is not a sufficient fix, which is the default assumption in most mixed-precision training recipes.
2. What is the cheapest budget-style diagnostic that would catch this class of drift in a training run within hours rather than decades — a conserved-quantity residual (total mass of the loss-weighted gradient? sum of logits?) rather than a loss curve?
3. How much of the 0.47×10⁻⁶ psu/decade residual would disappear with stochastic rounding on the tracer update, and is the unbiasedness worth the hardware/emulation cost compared with the 8% compensated-update overhead?
4. Does the conservative-update structure (64-bit tendency accumulation, 64-bit geometry, carried remainder) transfer cleanly to the DIC/alkalinity tracers in REcoM, where the background value is small and the increments are comparatively large — i.e. is the stagnation regime even reached there?
5. At 5 km with 120–240 s timesteps, does the drift re-emerge despite the conservative updates, as the Δt-scaling argument predicts? This is the single most important missing experiment for anyone who wants to run eddy-resolving climate integrations in float32.
6. Can the Kokkos-vs-Fortran precision-response ratio (Table 1) be turned into a routine CI check — a per-field "precision sensitivity fingerprint" that flags a port diverging from the reference implementation before a multi-decade run is attempted?
