GRPO keeps only (K−m) / m(K−1) of an m-body symmetric term. For pairwise bonuses it never reaches half; at K = 2 it is exactly zero. Whole-set rewards — pass@k credited to every member — disappear entirely.

The theory also predicts where training stops: predicted vs. measured concentration gives Pearson r = 0.995 across 13 configurations, with every fixed point registered before the corresponding run.

02

How the sign flips

one identity, then one line of code

Draw K responses per prompt and let each reward depend on the whole group. Conditioning on one response splits its reward into own — what it earns — and ext — what it hands to its groupmates. Every group-relative estimator is then the same gradient, gα = E[(own + α·ext)∇log π], and only α differs.

01

Write the update

True gradient α = +1. REINFORCE α = 0. GRPO and RLOO α = −1/(K−1). Positive rescaling only changes step size, so the estimators differ in direction through α alone.

02

Read the flip

Subtracting the group mean does not discard the externality term — it multiplies it by a negative number. Each response is paid to lower its groupmates' scores.

03

Name the game

Payoffs sum to zero at α = −1/(K−1): a finite-population spite game. At K = 2 a diversity bonus is invisible, so the optimum is one answer for every prompt.

04

Set α = +1

A_k = R(G) − K/(K−1)·R(G\{y_k}). K times the drop in group reward when y_k is removed. Unbiased for any K ≥ 2, O(K²) arithmetic, no critic, no extra judge calls for pairwise rewards.

03

What holds, what doesn't

the authors report their own losses

Strengths

  • The bias becomes a number: a page of proof turns “baselines may interact badly with diversity rewards” into (K−m)/m(K−1).
  • The game reading predicts the fixed point before the run — r = 0.995 across 13 configurations, verified to machine precision by enumeration.
  • Nearly free: O(K²) on a matrix the reward already computes. No critic, no loss surgery, no extra judge calls.
  • Unifies PKPO/L2O, SetPO and the uncentred SWYS witness as one estimator applied to different rewards — more useful than a new acronym.

Limitations

  • One model, one tuning: Qwen3.5-9B with rank-16 LoRA. No second family, no full fine-tuning, no scale study.
  • The headline task is four- or five-way, single-token multiple choice — the easiest case for collapse, far from free-form pluralistic alignment.
  • No RLVR (math/code) run, so the cost of switching on GRPO's default workload is unmeasured.
  • Training only at K ∈ {2, 8}; the K = 32–64 regime several cited methods use is covered analytically, not empirically.
04

Still open

four that would change the recommendation
  1. Does the fixed-point prediction survive full-parameter training at 30B+, or is LoRA's low rank doing part of the work by limiting escape directions?
  2. At K = 32–64, does K² credit assignment still pay for itself in wall-clock, or does the advantage vanish into noise?
  3. On GSM8K/MATH with binary verifiable rewards, does Team-GRPO match GRPO exactly — or does the K = 2 predictor cost something?
  4. What happens when quality and diversity are anti-correlated, i.e. when the diverse answers are systematically worse?
