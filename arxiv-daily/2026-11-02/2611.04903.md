> **一句话总结：** 作者把 LVLM 的多图检索拆成两侧准则——"指向"（pointer，attention mass 落在目标图 token span）与"写出"（writer，在该 head 的 value-weighted 输出上训一个轻量检索器，看能否认出目标图）——在 InternVL3.5/Qwen3-VL 的 4B 与 8B 四个模型（各 1,152 个 head）上发现两侧 top-100 名单只重叠 35–47 个（随机期望 8.7，即 4.0–5.4 倍），而 set-partition 消融显示标准多图推理真正依赖的正是这个交集：LLaVA-OneVision-7B 上 N=2、K=10 时消融交集使准确率相对匹配随机对照掉 18.0 个百分点，只指向的掉 9.4，只写出的仅掉 0.5。

> **TL;DR:** On ICIR-MCQ (6,000 MS-COCO multiple-choice samples with 5–6 candidate images) and Visual Haystacks (N=2–50), the authors separate "pointer" heads (attention mass over image-token spans) from "writer" heads (a per-head InfoNCE retriever trained on the head's value-weighted output) in InternVL3.5-{4B,8B} and Qwen3-VL-{4B,8B}. A single pointer head, L21H10 — top-1 in all four models at R@1 .866–.922 against chance .183 — retrieves the target image on Visual Haystacks at N=10 with R@1 .787–.836, beating the strongest external retriever Qwen3-VL-Embedding-8B (.751) by up to 8.5 pts and CLIP (.573)/SigLIP (.649) by 21–26 pts. Yet the pointer and writer top-100 sets share only 35–47 heads (chance 8.7), and set-partition ablations say the overlap is what inference actually needs: on LLaVA-OneVision-7B at N=2, K=10, ablating the overlap costs 18.0 pts of accuracy relative to a matched random-head control, pointer-only 9.4, writer-only 0.5. Worth reading if you care about head-level mechanism or multi-image grounding; be aware the main causal claim rests on dose-response curves with no significance test for the four headline models.

## 核心贡献

- **ICIR-MCQ：一个可控的 head 级检索探针。** 从 MS-COCO 构建 6,000 个样本（N=5 与 N=6 各 3,000），沿用 MM-NIAH 的图文交错段落结构但换成自然图像，任务是四选一"哪句 caption 描述了含 X 类别的图"。关键设计：四个选项都提到被查询类别（干扰项取自同类别的其他 COCO 图），所以模型不能只靠类别线索作答，必须真正定位到那张图；目标图位置与答案字母均匀平衡。这样每个样本都有一个唯一的、可标注的目标图，才能在全部 1,152 个 head 上扫 R@1。

- **指向（pointer）信号稀疏、集中在中层，且可跨模型复现。** 最好的 pointer head R@1 达 .866–.922（随机 .183），而中位数 head 只有 .198–.217——即绝大多数 head 接近随机。Top-5 全部落在 layer 18–24 的窄带里，且四个模型（两个家族、两个规模）的 top-1 都是同一个 **L21H10**；同一 LLM backbone 的配对模型在 top-100 上共享 86 个（8B）与 80 个（4B），top-3 集合完全相同。

- **单个 pointer head 打得过专门的外部检索器。** 在 Visual Haystacks（N=10）上，只用 L21H10 一个 head 的 attention mass 排序，R@1 为 .787（IV-8B）/ .832（QV-8B）/ .794（IV-4B）/ .836（QV-4B），而 CLIP ViT-L/14 是 .573、SigLIP so400m 是 .649、Qwen3-VL-Embedding-8B 是 .751。这是本文最有冲击力的单点结果：一个从未用检索目标训练过的 attention head，在上下文条件下比专用嵌入模型更会挑图。

- **writer 准则：从"指向哪里"转向"写出了什么"。** 对每个 head 单独训练一个轻量双塔检索器（一层线性投影 + L2 归一化，InfoNCE，batch 256），输入是该 head 在 cattok 处的 value-weighted 输出，候选是 LVLM 自己语言模型嵌入空间里 mean-pool 的图像 token。Top writer head 在 1,200 张 held-out 候选池上 R@1 为 .472–.595（中位数 .077–.101，随机约 .001）。最强的 writer 落在 layer 24（3/4 个模型），比 pointer 的 layer 21 略晚，且 top-100 更分散（47–60 个落在 layer 18–27，pointer 是 71–82 个）。

- **"指向 ≠ 写出"的定量解耦。** 两侧 top-100 只重叠 35–47 个，而如果两个排名无关，期望重叠是 100²/1152 = 8.7——实测是随机的 4.0–5.4 倍，但远非同一批 head。这个解耦在三个额外模型上复现：LLaVA-OneVision-7B 重叠 46（期望 12.8，3.6×）、Pixtral-12B 40（7.8，5.1×）、InternVL3.5-14B 31（6.2，5.0×）。

- **因果验证：真正承重的是交集。** 把 pointer top-100 ∪ writer top-100 划成三个不相交子集（交集 35–47 个，两个单侧尾部各 53–65 个），在 o_proj 输入端做输出置零消融（比 query masking 更严格，直接删掉该 head 对残差流的加性写入），K∈{5,10,15,20,25,30}、N∈{2,3,5,10,20,50}。交集子集的准确率下降最稳定，且随 K 单调增长；writer-only 尾部基本贴着随机对照，pointer-only 尾部居中且依模型而变。

## Key Contributions

- **A controlled probe with unambiguous head-level labels.** ICIR-MCQ (6,000 samples, N=5 and N=6 splits of 3,000 each) is built so that the queried category appears in the target image only, all four caption options mention the category, and the target position and answer letter are uniformly balanced. That combination is what makes a full 1,152-head sweep possible: head-level R@1 needs a single ground-truth image per sample, which naturalistic multi-image benchmarks do not supply.

- **Pointer heads are sparse, mid-block, and reproducible at identical indices.** Best-head R@1 .866–.922 vs a .183 chance level and a .198–.217 median. L21H10 is top-1 in every one of the four models despite different vision encoders and training data, and 86/80 of the top-100 are shared between the 8B and 4B pairs that share an LLM backbone. Cross-model per-head ranking correlation is Spearman .900–.946 across all six model pairs.

- **A single head is a competitive image retriever.** On Visual Haystacks at N=10, one pointer head reaches R@1 .787–.836 against .573 (CLIP), .649 (SigLIP), and .751 (Qwen3-VL-Embedding-8B) on the same samples. The authors explicitly disclaim this as a controlled training-objective comparison — the internal head reads patch-level keys with a context-conditioned query, while the external retrievers compare global image embeddings to a context-free text embedding.

- **A complementary output-side criterion.** The writer probe trains one small contrastive retriever per head on that head's value-weighted output at the cattok, asking whether target-image identity is linearly recoverable from what the head actually writes into the residual stream. This turns "does this head carry the information" into a measurable retrieval accuracy rather than an inspection of attention weights.

- **The pointer–writer dissociation, quantified and replicated.** 35–47 shared heads out of two top-100 lists (chance 8.7), reproduced on LLaVA-OneVision-7B, Pixtral-12B, and InternVL3.5-14B. Methodologically this says attention-based head discovery alone cannot separate heads that merely look at the right image from heads that also deposit usable information about it.

- **Causal localization via disjoint set partition.** Rather than comparing overlapping top-K sets (which confounds causal contribution with shared membership), the authors partition the union into overlap / pointer-only / writer-only and ablate each with matched-budget random controls. The overlap wins; writer-only is indistinguishable from random.

- **A control battery that is unusually complete for this literature.** Object-region instead of full-image writer targets (ρ=0.970–0.971 against the full-image ranking, versus ρ≥0.993 between two full-image retrainings — so object information perturbs the ranking more than training noise but leaves it intact); in-context versus held-out candidate pools; a readout-position sweep (+1…+4, mid, last); prefill-only versus full-generation intervention; majority-vote dose-response; a text-retrieval comparison; and three extra models.

## Methodology

**Setup and notation.** A context is C = (τ₀, I₁, τ₁, …, I_N, τ_N, u): interleaved text passages, N candidate images, and a task instruction. Each image I_i occupies a contiguous visual-token span [s_i, e_i]. For head (l,h) with post-softmax attention A^(l,h), the attention mass on image i at position p is m_i(p) = Σ_{j=s_i}^{e_i} A^(l,h)_{p,j}. Both criteria produce a per-head, per-sample score vector s^(l,h) ∈ R^N over candidate images and are scored by whether argmax_i s_i equals the target i★ (head-level R@1).

**Models.** InternVL3.5-{4B,8B} and Qwen3-VL-{4B,8B}. All four share the same language-model shape: 36 blocks × 32 heads = 1,152 heads, d_h = 128. The 4B pair shares one LLM backbone and the 8B pair another, which is what lets the paper separate "same architecture position" from "same trained weights". bf16, FlashAttention-2, GQA KV heads expanded to query-head count before per-head attention is recomputed.

**ICIR-MCQ construction.** Target image plus one of its own COCO captions mentioning the queried category (union of COCO 2017 train and val); N_choices − 1 distractor images drawn from *other* categories, excluding any image annotated with the target's category; four options A–D where one is the target's caption and three are captions of other images *of the queried category*. Target position uniform over the N_choices slots, answer letter uniform over {A,B,C,D}. The interleaved passages are inherited verbatim from MM-NIAH and are therefore independent of the images and options by construction.

**The cattok readout.** Head scores are read at the last token of the final mention of the queried category — a position where the category cue has just appeared in text and all image spans are in the causal past. This is the paper's central design choice: it fixes a single query position at which "which image does this head rank highest, given the category" is well defined. Appendix G shows the signals survive nearby positions (pointer ρ .939–.961 at +1…+4 and mid; writer ρ .79–.88) but degrade at the last prefill token (pointer ρ .68–.74).

**Pointer criterion.** s_i^ptr = Σ_{j∈span(i)} Ã_{p,j}, with Ã recomputed from the captured q_proj/k_proj outputs rather than read off the forward pass. Captured via forward hooks on q_proj/k_proj; the forward pass itself is unchanged.

**Writer criterion.** For each head, o_p^(l,h) = Σ_j A_{p,j} v_j, with v_j = W_v^(l,h) x_j — the value-weighted sum entering o_proj, captured by a forward-pre-hook. A dual encoder maps the head output and each candidate image embedding into a shared 256-d space through one linear layer each plus L2 normalization, scored by dot product, trained with InfoNCE. Image embeddings e_i are mean-pooled *projected visual tokens in the LVLM's own language-model embedding space*, not an external encoder — so the probe asks whether the head output is linearly aligned with the model's own image representation. Batch 256 (128 for Qwen3-VL-8B), lr 1e-3, chosen by a sweep on the 1,200-example evaluation split. LVLM weights are frozen; only the probe trains. Writer R@1 is measured over a held-out pool of 1,200 candidate images, which is why its absolute scale (.47–.60) is not comparable to pointer R@1 (.87–.92) computed over the 5–6 images inside one prompt.

**Visual Haystacks transfer.** Each example: multiple images plus a Yes/No question with an anchor object identifying the target image and a target object whose presence is queried ("In the image that contains a sofa, is there a bottle?"). Pointer scoring uses the last token of the anchor phrase as the cattok. Fixed seed 42 for filler selection and haystack ordering so all models see identical haystacks. Per-image budget fixed at 448×448 for all four models so cross-model comparisons are not confounded by input size (256 visual tokens per image for InternVL3.5, ~192 for Qwen3-VL due to aspect-preserving rounding to multiples of 32).

**Ablation.** A forward pre-hook on o_proj zeroes the selected head's slice of the o_proj input over the image-retrieval span (first token of the anchor phrase through the last prefill position). This is output-side zeroing, argued to be stricter than the query-side masking used by Wu et al. [42]: query masking changes where a head looks but can still leave a value-weighted output, whereas this removes the write. Intervention is not applied during answer generation; Appendix I.1 re-runs with generation-time zeroing and finds no prediction changes for the InternVL models and ≤5.1% changed answers / ≤0.9 pts for Qwen3-VL-8B. Random controls draw K heads uniformly from 1,152, averaged over three seeds. Note that the first answer token is decoded from the last prefill position, which is already inside the intervention window.

**Compute.** NVIDIA L40 (48GB). One VHs pass at N=10 ≈1.5 s/sample (InternVL3.5-8B) and ≈4 s (Qwen3-VL-8B); head-output extraction on ICIR-MCQ ≈13 s/sample. The set-partition grid is ~220 cells × 1,000 samples per LVLM, "on the order of a few hundred GPU-hours per LVLM."

## Results & Highlights

- **Pointer heads are extremely sparse but extremely accurate.** On ICIR-MCQ the best head reaches R@1 .866 (InternVL3.5-4B), .890 (IV-8B), .905 (Qwen3-VL-4B), .922 (QV-8B) against a chance level of .183, while the median head sits at .198–.217 — i.e. roughly chance. The gap between rank-1 and rank-100 is enormous, and the top-5 all fall in layers 18–24.

- **One head beats purpose-built retrievers on Visual Haystacks (N=10).** L21H10 alone gets R@1 .787 (IV-8B), .832 (QV-8B), .794 (IV-4B), .836 (QV-4B), versus CLIP ViT-L/14 .573, SigLIP so400m .649, and Qwen3-VL-Embedding-8B .751 on the same samples. Delta against the strongest external retriever is +3.6 pts (IV-8B) to +8.5 pts (QV-4B), and +21.4 to +26.3 pts against CLIP. The 4B variants match or slightly exceed their 8B counterparts at this haystack size.

- **Retrieval accuracy is well above the model's own QA accuracy.** On Visual Haystacks N=10 the single head identifies the target image 11.3–16.1 points more often than the unablated model answers the Yes/No question correctly: .794 vs 68.1% (IV-4B), .787 vs 65.9% (IV-8B), .836 vs 67.5% (QV-4B), .832 vs 67.6% (QV-8B). On ICIR-MCQ the two are within a few points (.866 vs 85.5%, .922 vs 91.3%). So the localization step is not the binding constraint at N=10 — the downstream Yes/No decision is.

- **Pointer and writer rankings diverge sharply.** Top-100 overlap is 47/42/35/43 heads for IV-8B/IV-4B/QV-8B/QV-4B, against a chance expectation of 8.7 — 4.0× to 5.4× chance, but still less than half of either list. Top-1 heads differ outright: pointer L21H10 vs writer L24H23 (IV-8B, QV-8B, QV-4B) or L21H31 (IV-4B). Writer top-1 R@1 on the 1,200-image held-out pool is .489/.472/.595/.509 against medians of .077–.101.

- **The causal effect localizes to the overlap.** On LLaVA-OneVision-7B, accuracy minus the matched random control at N=2: K=5 gives −0.123 (overlap) / −0.011 (pointer-only) / −0.003 (writer-only); K=10 gives −0.180 / −0.094 / −0.005; K=30 gives −0.213 / −0.132 / −0.017. The overlap arm is the most damaging in all 30 (N,K) cells, with McNemar p<0.05 against all three random seeds in 24 of 30 and against at least two seeds in all 30. Writer-only stays within ±0.02 of random throughout.

- **The effect is sparse: five heads already cost a lot.** At K=5 the overlap arm costs 0.07–0.14 accuracy at N≤10 while five pointer-only or writer-only heads stay within 0.02 of random. The paper's framing — "retrieval heads are pointers that write" — is supported at a budget of five heads out of 1,152.

- **Ensembling shows the writer ranking is real but individually weaker.** At K=1 the pointer top head beats the writer top head in 19 of 20 (model, N) cells by 2.3–17.2 pp (the exception is Qwen3-VL-4B at N=2, 89.4 vs 92.5). Majority vote over the top-K writer-probe heads closes most of the gap, saturating by K≈20–30 and landing within 4.0 pp of the best pointer configuration in every cell (within 1.2 pp at N=2, and matching or exceeding it in four cells). On the 8B models a single pointer head is already at the ceiling (adding heads gains ≤0.8 pp), while on the 4B models a small pointer ensemble helps (up to +7.4 pp at K=5 for QV-4B).

- **Writer heads carry object information, not just image identity.** Retraining the probe with the COCO box of the queried category as the target still yields ρ=0.970–0.971 agreement with the full-image ranking, and in a same-category pool (only object regions of the queried category, chance 6–9%) the top-100 object-region heads retrieve the correct region 42–48% of the time and the top-10 58–63%. Two full-image retrainings agree at ρ≥0.993 with 93–96 shared top-100 heads, so the object-region shift is real but modest.

- **Writer-only heads read the target image directly, not through text relay.** Decomposing the head output by source group: target-image tokens alone give writer R@1 .322–.341 versus .285–.352 for the full output; removing target tokens costs −.160 to −.175 (95% CI excludes zero in all four models); text tokens alone give only .126–.190; removing all text costs just −.025 to −.085; interleaved filler and distractor-image tokens alone carry essentially nothing (.002–.004). Attention patterns agree — writer-only heads put 2.1–4.0× more mass on the target image than layer-matched random heads.

- **The two retrieval functions are not the same heads.** In Qwen3-VL-8B, the top image head L21H10 ranks 118th for a text needle-in-a-haystack task, and the top text head L29H09 ranks 137th for image retrieval; no head is top-10 for both. The pointer cluster sits in layers 19–23, the text top-10 in layers 23–32. The authors flag the comparison as unmatched and therefore suggestive only.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The core observation — that attention mass over an image span and recoverability of image identity from a head's value-weighted output are different properties, and that the intersection is what matters causally — is genuinely new for LVLMs. The closest prior work (Wu et al. [42] on text retrieval heads, Baek et al. [4] on OCR heads, Bi et al. [8] on visual perception heads) all select heads by attention pattern alone. Turning "does the head write it" into a trained-probe retrieval accuracy is a clean operationalization, and the set-partition ablation is the right experimental design for a claim about an intersection. This is a real contribution, not a rebranding.

**The single most serious methodological problem is a rank confound inside the ablation.** The overlap subset is ablated "in pointer-rank order," and by construction it contains pointer ranks 1, 2, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 14, … (for InternVL3.5-8B: a1, a2, a3, a4, a7, a8, a9, a10, a11, a14, a17, a18, a20, …). The pointer-only arm is the complement of that list, so its members start at a5, a6, a12, a13, a15, a16, … — systematically *lower* in the pointer ranking. The paper matches budgets by K but never matches by pointer rank. So "overlap beats pointer-only" is partly "the top of the pointer ranking beats the middle of the pointer ranking," which is exactly what a pointer-only account would predict. The clean control would be to ablate the top-K pointer heads *with* and *without* their writer membership held fixed, or to sample pointer-only heads matched on pointer rank. As reported, the dissociation between "writers matter" and "the best pointers matter" is not fully separated. This is not fatal — the writer-only arm being flat at random is evidence that writer status alone does nothing — but it weakens the central causal claim more than the paper acknowledges.

**No significance testing for the four headline models.** The McNemar tests appear only in Appendix J for LLaVA-OneVision-7B. For the four models that carry the main-text claim, the evidence is Figure 3's dose-response curves with "±1 standard deviation across the four LVLMs" shaded bands — which is cross-model dispersion over n=4 models, not a per-model sampling interval, and cannot support a claim about whether any single model's overlap-vs-pointer-only gap is nonzero. With n=1,000 per cell this is fixable and should have been done.

**The writer criterion is under-specified as a head property.** Writer R@1 is produced by a *trained* probe, so the ranking is a joint property of (head output, probe capacity, optimization). The authors do control for training variance — two independent full-image trainings agree at ρ≥0.993 with 93–96 shared top-100 heads, which is reassuring — but the probe is one linear layer into 256-d, so "writer R@1" really measures *linearly* recoverable target-image information. A head whose output carries the information in a nonlinearly-decodable form would be misclassified as a non-writer. The paper never tests a nonlinear probe, so the writer set is best read as a lower bound. Relatedly, the main text never reports a single full-ranking Spearman correlation between the held-out writer ranking and the pointer ranking over all 1,152 heads; the closest is Table 8's in-context writer vs pointer (ρ .403–.530), which is a different quantity. The 35–47/100 overlap is the headline, and it is cut-dependent — overlap at top-10 or top-20 is not reported.

**The external-retriever comparison flatters the internal head more than the text admits.** Qwen3-VL-Embedding-8B is a derivative of Qwen3-VL-8B, so "one attention head of Qwen3-VL beats Qwen3-VL-Embedding" is partly "the same model with patch-level, context-conditioned access beats a compressed global embedding of itself." That is a legitimate and interesting finding about information bottlenecking, but it is not the "small internal component beats a dedicated external system" framing a casual reader will take away. CLIP and SigLIP are queried with a bare anchor noun through an 80-template ensemble with no surrounding context at all, which is close to a worst case for them. The authors do disclaim this explicitly in §4.3, which is to their credit, but the abstract's "competitive with external vision-language retrievers" will travel without the caveat.

**ICIR-MCQ is close to saturated, which compresses exactly the differences the paper measures.** Model accuracy on the probe is 85.5–91.3% and the top head's R@1 is .866–.922 — a 3–7 point headroom. When the task is that easy, the top-100 boundary is determined by a handful of samples, and the "35–47 overlap" number is sensitive to where that boundary falls. The paper's own robustness check is narrow (top head invariant across the N=5 and N=6 splits, top-5 sets coincide), which addresses split stability but not saturation. A harder probe variant — more distractors, finer-grained categories, or attribute-level queries rather than object categories — would have made the head ranking far more informative.

**The title oversells the finding in a direction the data does not support.** "Heads That Write, Not Just Point" implies writers are the interesting population. But the writer-only heads are the *least* causally relevant arm — flat at random across all budgets and all haystack sizes on LLaVA-OneVision-7B, and within ±0.02 of random throughout. What the paper actually shows is "heads that both point and write," and that is what the conclusion says. The title is a hook, and it points the wrong way.

**Other issues worth flagging.** (i) Haystack composition uses a single fixed seed (42), so there is no variance from haystack sampling, only from head selection — a minor but real limitation for the N=2–5 cells where the effects are largest. (ii) The N=50 cells are acknowledged to be near chance for the baseline, which means a third of the ablation grid carries little information; the "most consistent across haystack sizes" claim is effectively a claim about N=2–20. (iii) The intervention covers only the anchor-phrase-to-last-prefill span and stops at generation; the prefill-only vs full-generation check is good practice but shows up to 5.1% of parsed answers changing on Qwen3-VL-8B, so the "no effect" conclusion is InternVL-specific. (iv) The proposed remedy — retraining or steering only the value/output projections of heads that already point well — is stated in the Discussion but never tested, which is the obvious next experiment and its absence leaves the paper diagnostic rather than actionable. (v) The text-vs-image retrieval comparison is unmatched on four axes simultaneously (span granularity, scoring rule, task format, query position), so Table 15 is essentially anecdotal; the authors say so, but it is in the paper as a result.

**What is genuinely well done.** The control battery is better than typical for this subfield: object-region probes to rule out "the head just encodes global image identity," a retrained-probe control to separate ranking shift from training noise, an in-context-versus-held-out comparison that honestly reports the in-context ranking is *poorly resolved* (rank-1 to rank-100 gap of only 1.8–4.3 pts, split-half agreement 61–71/100) and therefore not used, a readout-position sweep showing the signal is anchored but not fragile, and a three-model replication of the discovery-side dissociation. The Appendix F source decomposition is a small gem — it shows writer-only heads get their information from target-image tokens directly (−.160 to −.175 when removed) and not through a text relay (−.025 to −.085), which rules out the most obvious alternative explanation for why a non-pointer head could still be a writer.

## Strengths

- **A real conceptual contribution with a clean operationalization.** Separating "where a head reads from" and "what it writes" is obvious in hindsight and had not been done at head granularity for cross-modal retrieval. The trained-probe formulation makes it measurable rather than impressionistic.
- **The dissociation replicates on seven models.** Four main models plus LLaVA-OneVision-7B, Pixtral-12B, and InternVL3.5-14B, spanning Qwen2/Qwen3/Mistral-Nemo/InternVL backbones and SigLIP/Pixtral/InternViT vision towers, all showing overlap several times chance but far from identity (3.6×–5.4×).
- **Head-index reproducibility is a useful engineering result.** L21H10 being top-1 in four independently trained models, and 86/80 of the top-100 shared within backbone pairs, is the kind of finding practitioners can act on — it suggests the retrieval circuit is a property of the architecture plus multi-image training rather than a per-model accident.
- **Honest reporting of negative and awkward results.** Writer-only heads are causally inert and the paper says so plainly. The in-context writer ranking is poorly resolved and the paper says so and does not use it. N=50 loses dynamic range and the paper says so. The text-retrieval probe is unmatched and the paper says so.
- **Unusually complete artifact.** Code, HF dataset with data card and builder, probe hyperparameters, GPU-hour estimates, exact preprocessing overrides, and per-head rankings in appendices. The per-model overlap head lists (Table 11) are published in full, which makes the ablation reproducible at the head level.
- **The sparse-budget result is striking and practical.** Five heads out of 1,152 costing 0.07–0.14 accuracy is a much stronger statement than "the top-100 matter," and it makes head-level intervention look tractable.

## Limitations

- **Scope is single-target retrieval only.** Visual Haystacks questions have one anchor and one target object. Cross-image comparison, multi-target grounding, counting across images, and compositional reasoning are all out of scope, and these are where multi-image LVLMs actually fail in deployment.
- **Seven models, all recent open-weight, four of them sharing a 36×32 shape.** The convergence argument leans on the shared shape; whether the layer-21 pointer band is architectural, a training artifact, or a tokenization artifact is not distinguished.
- **The probe requires a known category token.** The cattok is defined as the last token of the queried-category mention, which is available when the query names an object category but not in general (e.g., "the third chart," "the image I uploaded earlier"). This makes ICIR-MCQ an analysis instrument, not a deployable router, and the paper is explicit that it is designed for discovery rather than as a benchmark.
- **Writer R@1 measures linear recoverability, not downstream use.** The paper is careful about this ("writer R@1 measures probe recoverability, not downstream use") but the distinction does a lot of work: the writer set is a linear-probe construct, and a nonlinear probe might recover more or differently.
- **No intervention on the proposed mechanism.** The Discussion suggests retraining or steering value/output projections of good pointer heads to improve their writer side. That experiment is not run, so the paper establishes a correlation between writer status and causal relevance without demonstrating that improving writing improves retrieval.
- **Compute cost limits replication.** A few hundred GPU-hours per LVLM for the ablation grid alone, plus 1,152 probe trainings per model per readout position. The full protocol on a new model is not cheap, which is presumably why the ablation replication is a single extra model rather than three.
- **Statistical reporting is thin for the main models.** No per-model confidence intervals or significance tests on the main dose-response curves; the McNemar analysis exists only in the LLaVA appendix.

## Relevance

这篇是典型的 LLM/LVLM 隐状态与可解释性工作：用"注意力指向"与"输出可解码性"两侧探针把多图检索这一功能定位到具体 attention head，并给出消融级别的因果证据——对关心 head 级机制、探针方法与表征可解码性的读者价值很高。其方法论教训（注意力权重不等于写入残差流的信息、集合划分消融优于 top-K 重叠比较）可以迁移到任何 head/circuit 定位研究，即使不做多模态也值得一读。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. **How much of the overlap-vs-pointer-only gap survives a pointer-rank-matched control?** Ablating pointer heads at matched pointer rank, split by writer membership, would separate "writer status matters" from "high pointer rank matters." This is the experiment that would settle the paper's central claim.
2. **Does a nonlinear writer probe change the writer set?** If writer R@1 with a 2-layer MLP or a small transformer recovers substantially more heads, then "writing" is a property of the decoder, not the head, and the pointer-writer dissociation shrinks.
3. **What happens if you fix the writer side of a pointer head?** Fine-tune only the value/output projection of L21H10 (or the overlap set) with the writer objective and re-measure Visual Haystacks accuracy. A null result would be as informative as a positive one.
4. **Is the layer-21 pointer band a tokenization artifact?** All four main models use 192–256 visual tokens per image. Does the band move with a different per-image token budget, or with a vision encoder that produces a different spatial layout?
5. **Why is single-head retrieval (.79–.84) so far above the model's own QA accuracy (66–68%) at N=10?** If the head finds the right image 16 points more often than the model answers correctly, the bottleneck is downstream of retrieval. What is the model doing with the retrieved image that loses that much?
6. **Does the pointer-writer structure hold under instruction tuning that targets multi-image reasoning?** All seven models are general-purpose. A model explicitly trained on multi-image QA might concentrate both properties into the same heads — which would make the dissociation a training-data artifact rather than an architectural one.
7. **How sensitive is the 35–47 overlap to the top-K cut?** Reporting overlap at K=10, 20, 50, 100, 200 alongside a full-ranking Spearman would show whether the dissociation is a tail phenomenon or a property of the whole ranking.
