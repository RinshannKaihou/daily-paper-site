Why this matters

The most complete engineering treatment of TP+SP and EP overlap to date — plus a reduction path that is both lower-error and bitwise reproducible.

Determinism is the quiet prize: reproducible reductions make loss spikes and silent numerical drift debuggable.

Critical edge

Switching over a fixed 56-WG crew yields just 1.032× — so the 1.38× headline is driven by fusion and queue ordering, not by self-scheduling.

01

How it works

one launch per direction · no scheduler warp
  1. STEP 01

    Fuse the whole op

    AG+GEMM, GEMM+RS, or an entire MoE layer from router to fold becomes one persistent kernel. One workgroup per CU, resident for its lifetime.

  2. STEP 02

    Two roles, claimed

    A workgroup is a mover (streams bytes over XGMI) or a builder (computes 256×256 GEMM tiles). Neither is assigned in advance.

  3. STEP 03

    Queues, not a scheduler

    WGs pop tiles from in-kernel queues and signal arrival with readiness flags. Deadlock-free by two rules: upstream before downstream, never block on a push.

  4. STEP 04

    Switch, don't split

    One-way switching for monotonic demand (AG/RS, dispatch); two-way for interleaved demand (MoE combine). Two-way is measurably worse on AG/RS: 0.918–0.965×.

Ordering is the real design surface. Local-first AG queues, band-major RS production, destination-minor MoE push lists — co-designed per collective around when data becomes ready. That ordering, not the switching, is where the speed comes from (see 02).

Strengths

  • Complete training coverage — every TP+SP collective, both directions, both precisions; first single-launch MoE layer carrying router and weight gradients.
  • Ablations that isolate the mechanism — one change at a time, fused vs. unfused, separate vs. fused launches.
  • Honest policy comparison — UniEP- and Weave-style splits reimplemented inside KOSMOS, including cells where its own policy loses (two-way AG+NN: 0.951×).
  • A free numerics win — one-shot-pull RS: 2.21× lower reduction error than RCCL's ring, 1.71–1.73× lower end-to-end vs. FP64, and bitwise reproducible.
  • Measurement discipline — slowest-rank medians, shuffled variant order, pinned toolchain, 0.4% run-to-run spread. CU-capped kernels stay fully resident.

Limitations

  • Single node, 8 GPUs. Persistent kernels need XGMI plus hipIPC memory; there is no RDMA path and no hierarchical movers.
  • One completion point per tile is structural — it rules out stream-K and cooperative split-K for small or irregular shapes.
  • Push-based dispatch costs memory; the paper concedes pull may be needed at EP 32/64, so the design may not extend.
  • Heuristics fitted in-distribution at TP 8 / EP 8, on the same shape families used for evaluation. No held-out shapes, no threshold sensitivity.
  • Proxy scale only — 24-of-126 layers, no convergence curves, no MXFP8 loss parity, and no released code or harness.
03

Still open

what the paper leaves on the table
  1. Hold queue order fixed and revert to a tuned 56-WG crew: how much of the 1.38× microbenchmark win actually survives?
  2. Do the closed-form heuristics hold at TP 4/16 or EP 32/64, and what is the cliff at a threshold such as gathered width 3584?
  3. At what GEMM-to-collective ratio does the speedup reach zero? The 405B row suggests a crossover nobody has characterized.
  4. Does MXFP8 with the dual-quantized dY gather match BF16 loss over 10k+ steps?
  5. What does the NaN-sentinel arrival protocol do during a real loss spike — exactly when the kernel must not hang?
