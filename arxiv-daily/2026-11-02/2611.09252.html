01

Methodology — the loop

error → bias → router → load → error
  1. 01Error

    ei = (r − Li)/r against fair share r = TK/E. Bounded, batch-size independent.

  2. 02Controller

    ui ← ui + KI·ei. One gain per expert per layer; no gradient.

  3. 03Bias

    Added to router scores at selection only. Gate weights stay unbiased.

  4. 04Router

    Top-K, memoryless, monotone — and one step late.

  5. 05Load

    Li(t) becomes the next error.

↺ closed loop · correction lands at t+1

  • Reframe. Error is the normalized deviation from the fair share; the control input is a per-expert bias on router scores at selection time, so the model's weights and training objective are untouched.
  • Unify. The Switch auxiliary loss differentiates to a token-dependent gain on the current error — proportional control, with steady-state error ∝ 1/α that never vanishes. ALF-LB integrates the sign of the error, discarding magnitude.
  • Argue. A dead expert needs a ramp (I), not a fixed offset (P); an alternating error cancels inside the sum but is copied or amplified by P and D. Hence PID (I), a single gain.
  • Test. OLMoE from scratch at E=16/32/64 (33k steps, hard Books→GitHub switch at 16,500), plus continual pretraining of OLMoE-1B-7B onto codeparrot.

03

Balance sheet

read together — the gain and its price

Strengths

  1. Two deployed methods collapse into one family, with the mapping derived rather than asserted — and it predicts steady-state error ∝ 1/α, confirmed by the sweep (DER 15.14% → 0.00%).
  2. The integral-only argument is simple, checkable, and matches the ablation: P-only 2.86, D-only 5.47, PID (I) 0.40.
  3. Metrics beyond MaxVio — DER, UTR, active fraction, rotating-vs-persistent ratio — each rule out a specific failure mode.
  4. Unusually honest probes: forgetting is reported, and the bias-removed counterfactual shows perplexity improving 0.9–2.6% when the balancer is discarded.
  5. Reproducibility is strong for an academic paper: open backbone, 6 seeds, full architecture and training tables, pseudocode, a stated gain-transfer rule.

Limitations

  1. Novelty is in the framing: PID (I) is ALF-LB's linear rule on a normalized error — a three-line update, not a new mechanism.
  2. The control analysis is local and qualitative: no transfer function, no stability proof; scores are held fixed within a step and top-K coupling is outside the loop.
  3. The headline gap is partly a hyperparameter artifact: at α=0.8 Aux-loss reaches postMV 0.60 (better than PID's 0.90) with worse PPL.
  4. Narrow scope: one backbone, one routing design, ~1.08B tokens, a single instantaneous switch, no throughput or wall-clock measurement.
  5. Statistics are uneven — CPT uses one seed with no error bars — and large KI drives a limit cycle, a real robustness cost of the linear rule.
04

Open questions

what the paper leaves for the next run

Can a saturating or clipped integrator keep the sign rule's robustness while retaining magnitude at large KI?

How must KI scale with batch size, sequence length, and LR schedule — not just expert count?

Does the widening margin over ALF-LB survive at 128–256 experts and trillion-token budgets?

Can decaying KI, or gating the bias to already-saturated experts, buy balance without the forgetting?
