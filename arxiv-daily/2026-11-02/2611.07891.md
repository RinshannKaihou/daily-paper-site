> **一句话总结：** 作者把"消融检索头造成的 logit 损失"精确拆成四项——被移除的直接贡献 D、copy-suppression 释放 R、其余头与 MLP 的净 backup 响应 B、LayerNorm 项 N（H = D − R − B − N），从而把"损伤"和"代偿"分开；在 Pythia 410M–12B 上匹配到同样 0.5 logit 的损伤后，410M 净修复 0.55 logit（输出甚至比干净时高 0.05），12B 却留下 0.38 logit 的输出损伤，4-bit 量化下 H/D 从 410M 的 0.55 升到 12B 的 0.91（OPT 从 125M 的 0.41 升到 6.7B 的 0.98）——即"压缩在模型变大时更贵"，因为大模型可释放的 copy-suppression 更少（standing suppression 1.1 vs 0.2 logit）、净注意力代偿更弱（占剩余缺口的 80% vs 9%）。

> **TL;DR:** On templated IOI prompts (1,000 exploratory / 2,000 held-out, single-token names), the paper writes the output harm of retrieval-head ablation as an exact identity `H = D − R − B − N` (deficit minus copy-suppression release minus net backup response minus final-LayerNorm term, closing to ~1e-3 logits by construction). Comparing the six Pythia models at a *matched* deficit of D = 0.5 logits rather than a matched head count, Pythia-410M over-repairs to a margin 0.05 logits **above** clean, while Pythia-12B retains 0.38 of the 0.5 logits as harm — a 0.43-logit gap driven by copy-suppression release (D−R ≈ 27% of D at 410M vs ~90% at 12B) and by a net attention response that offsets 80% of the post-release remainder at 410M but only 9% at 12B. The same gradient appears under simulated 4-bit per-channel quantization of the target heads (harm/deficit H/D: 0.55 → 0.91 in Pythia, 0.41 → 0.98 in OPT-125M → 6.7B) and, weakly, under structural pruning. The interesting twist: at 12B the protective heads are still there — clamping the 34 selected backup receivers at their clean outputs adds 0.30 logits of harm — they just no longer *net*-restore the margin.

## 核心贡献

- **一个把"损伤"与"代偿"分离的精确记账恒等式。** 对每个 IOI prompt，令 `m = z_ans − z_subj`，把注意力头与 MLP 划成目标集 T、copy-suppression 集 CS、其余集 O，定义 deficit `D = −Σ_{i∈T} Δa_i`、output harm `H = −Δm`、CS release `R = Σ_{i∈CS} Δa_i`、net backup `B = Σ_{i∈O} Δa_i`、归一化项 `N = Δ(m − Σ_{i∈C} a_i)`，则严格有 `H = D − R − B − N`，总修复 `D − H = R + B + N`。GPT-2 XL 上残差 < 4×10⁻³ logit，Pythia 各面板最大闭合残差 5.1×10⁻⁴ ~ 1.3×10⁻³ logit。**这是本文最有价值的方法论贡献**：它把"基准分数只记录残留损失"这一抱怨变成了可逐项称重的账本。
- **证明 copy-suppression 是主要的小模型"缓冲垫"。** 受损的检索头会削弱 copy-suppression 头的输入，使其对答案的压制放松（R > 0），从而把负贡献还回来。GPT-2 XL 中 CS 头在干净运行上施加 0.41 logit 的 standing suppression（约干净 logit 差的 9%），消融前十个检索头释放其中的 0.32。跨尺度看，410M 的 standing suppression 约 1.1 logit（25%），12B 只有 0.2 logit（5%）——**小模型"存了更多可释放的压制"，这是尺度差异的第一条机制。**
- **尺度结论：匹配损伤后，修复份额随模型变大而下降。** 关键设计是**不匹配头数、匹配损伤**（D = 0.5 logit，线性插值），因为同样消融 10 个头在不同模型上移除 1.3–3.7 logit 不等。410M 的 R+B+N 约 0.55 logit，把 0.5 的缺口补过头；12B 留下 0.38 logit 的实际损伤。原始"固定十个头"的比较里，修复占缺口的比例从 410M 的约 80% 掉到 12B 的约 29%。
- **第二条机制：backup 响应变"弱"而不是变"没有"。** 释放之后剩下的缺口 D−R 在 1B 到 12B 之间其实差不多大，但净注意力响应只抵消其中 80%（410M）/132%（1B）/100%（1.4B）/13%（2.8B）/9%（6.9B）/9%（12B）。而响应预防实验（把选中的接收头钳在干净输出）显示，12B 上阻止这些调整反而多出 0.30 logit 的伤害，尽管其净注意力响应只有 0.07 logit——**保护性调整存在，只是在净和上互相抵消**。三个最大模型上同时阻止 backup 与 CS 调整，伤害会超过原始缺口（12B：H = 1.018 vs D = 0.851）。
- **把消融结论复制到"压缩"上。** 对目标检索头的四个注意力投影做 per-channel 4-bit 量化后原地反量化（fp32 推理），H/D 在 Pythia 上从 410M 的 0.55 升到 12B 的 0.91（中间有波动：1B 0.82、1.4B 0.61、6.9B 0.97），OPT 上从 125M 的 0.41 升到 6.7B 的 0.98；结构剪枝（剪掉一半可剪头）下，与 top-10 检索头重叠最多的"unlucky"掩码平均修复仍为正但随规模下降，随机掩码则非单调。
- **一个可解释的发育学假说。** 用 logit lens 读干净运行的分层答案边际：四个较小模型（410M/1B/1.4B/2.8B）的均值轨迹先冲高再回落（"overshoot"），6.9B 与 12B 的峰值就在最后一层。作者据此猜测：小模型靠后期的 copy-suppression 去压掉中间层的过度自信，因此"存了"更多可释放的压制；大模型形成答案时不需要这层修正。Pythia 检查点分析（410M/1B/1.4B/6.9B，1k–143k 步）显示 standing suppression 与 intermediate excess 大体同向发育，但作者明确承认这是相关性而非因果。
- **实践警示。** 在小代理模型（410M/1B）上测出的"压缩几乎无害"，不能外推到大模型；一个 capability 若恰好压到它依赖的头部，而代偿又有限，损失会显著大于平均指标所示。

## Key Contributions

- **An exact per-prompt accounting identity that separates damage from compensation.** Writing the answer–distractor margin as `m = z_ans − z_subj`, the paper partitions heads/MLPs into targets T, copy-suppression heads CS, and the rest O, then defines deficit `D = −Σ_{T} Δa_i`, harm `H = −Δm`, release `R = Σ_{CS} Δa_i`, net backup `B = Σ_{O} Δa_i`, and a LayerNorm residual `N`. The identity `H = D − R − B − N` lets one ask how much of a removed contribution actually reaches the output — the thing a benchmark score cannot tell you. Closure residual is < 4×10⁻³ logits in GPT-2 XL and ≤ 1.3×10⁻³ in the Pythia panels.
- **Copy-suppression release as the dominant small-model buffer.** Damaging retrieval heads starves the copy-suppression heads of their input, so their negative write relaxes and the margin partially returns. GPT-2 XL: 0.41 logits of standing suppression (~9% of the clean margin), 0.32 released by ablating the top ten retrieval heads; with ten heads ablated the backup response supplies 0.51 of the 0.97 logits of total repair, and from k = 20 onward it is at least twice the release term.
- **A matched-damage scaling result, not a matched-head-count one.** Because ten heads remove 1.3–3.7 logits depending on the model, the authors interpolate each sweep to a shared D = 0.5 logits. At that deficit Pythia-410M repairs ~0.55 logits (margin 0.05 above clean) while Pythia-12B retains ~0.38 logits of harm. The original fixed-ten-head view agrees in direction: repair falls from ~80% of the deficit at 410M to ~29% at 12B.
- **Two dissociable mechanisms behind the scale trend.** (i) Less standing suppression to release as models grow: 1.1 logits (~25%) at 410M vs 0.2 logits (~5%) at 12B, so `D − R` is ~27% of D at 410M but ~90% at 12B. (ii) A weaker *net* attention response over the post-release remainder: 80% at 410M, 9% at 12B — even though the remainder `D − R` itself is roughly constant from 1B to 12B.
- **The protective response does not disappear — it cancels.** Holding the selected backup receivers at their clean outputs increases harm in every model (12B: +0.30 logits [0.228, 0.366] from 34 receivers, against a net attention response of only 0.07 logits); preventing both backup and copy-suppression adjustment pushes harm past the original deficit in the three largest models (2.8B 1.770 vs D 0.888; 6.9B 0.905 vs 0.829; 12B 1.018 vs 0.851). So "larger models repair less" means "their adjustments net out to less," not "they stop responding."
- **Replication under quantization and pruning.** Simulated per-channel 4-bit weight quantization of the target heads' four attention projections reproduces the gradient in both families (H/D: Pythia 0.55 → 0.91; OPT 0.41 → 0.98), and structural half-head pruning shows declining mean repair for masks that overlap the retrieval circuit.
- **A developmental account (explicitly correlational).** Logit-lens trajectories overshoot the final margin in the four smaller Pythia models and peak at the output layer in 6.9B/12B; standing suppression tracks the size of that overshoot across training checkpoints, suggesting suppression develops to correct intermediate excess — offered as a hypothesis, not a demonstrated cause.

## Methodology

**Task and models.** In-context retrieval via indirect-object identification (IOI): "When Mary and John went to the store, John gave a drink to ___" — the model must raise "Mary" over the repeated "John". Primary suite is Pythia (410M, 1B, 1.4B, 2.8B, 6.9B, 12B, final checkpoint step 143,000) because all six share training data, data order, and token budget — the cleanest available control for training exposure in a scaling comparison. Single-model mechanism work uses GPT-2 XL; replication uses Llama 3.1 8B and OPT (125M, 1.3B, 2.7B, 6.7B). All runs in fp32 (TF32 disabled for held-out evaluation) via TransformerLens, with LayerNorm folded into adjacent linear maps.

**Prompt protocol.** 1,000 exploratory prompts (seed 0) shared across models, plus 2,000 fresh deduplicated held-out prompts (seed 20260908) from the same 30 templates, all with single-token names. Critically, target selection, damage calibration, and backup-receiver selection are fixed on the exploratory set *before* held-out evaluation — a genuine pre-registration-style split that most circuit papers skip.

**Head identification.** Retrieval heads use an IOI-adapted version of Wu et al.'s long-context retrieval score at threshold 0.1 (half credit for strongest attention from the final position landing on the correct name when the model's top prediction is that name; half for the subject's second occurrence attending to the first), ranked by clean direct logit attribution, with copy-suppression heads excluded. Copy-suppression heads use the static OV and QK tests of McDougall et al. (2023) over 2,048 sampled vocabulary tokens (score = min of the two fractions), threshold 0.5 plus each model's two highest scorers, giving exactly two CS heads at every size. Clean pairwise IOI accuracy is 0.99–1.00 and the clean margin 3.95–5.05 logits across the suite; identified retrieval-set sizes vary wildly (|R| = 98, 27, 105, 283, 266, 389).

**Decomposition.** Direct logit attribution uses the *clean-run* final-LayerNorm scale for both clean and intervened runs, so any change in the actual normalization is not silently attributed to components but isolated in `N`. Zero-ablation sets a head's output to zero at all positions; sweeps use every k when |R| ≤ 100 and a coarse grid (1, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, then steps of 30) otherwise.

**Matched-damage comparison.** Because equal head counts are not equal damage, the paper interpolates linearly between neighbouring sweep settings to a shared D = 0.5 logits and compares the measured margin against the no-response counterfactual `m_clean − D`. A separate held-out experiment calibrates three discovery deficits (≈0.43, 0.86, 1.71 logits) by zeroing whole heads when within 10% of target, otherwise attenuating the boundary head's live output; interventions are then frozen and applied unchanged to the 2,000 held-out prompts (achieved deficits 0.756–0.888 logits at the middle target; 1.4B drifts outside the ±10% tolerance and is left un-retuned).

**Interventions.** (a) Zero-ablation of top-k retrieval heads. (b) Simulated per-channel 4-bit quantization of the four attention projections (W_Q, W_K, W_V, W_O) of the target heads, dequantized in place, biases untouched, activations fp32 — step size chosen from 96 fractions of the max-abs step to minimize squared weight error. The single-model Pythia-410M sweep deliberately uses a *bad* calibrator (asymmetric min–max grid stretched threefold on one side) so that compensation is visible rather than to represent a deployment quantizer. (c) Structural pruning: zero half of eligible heads at all positions, with "unlucky" masks (three seeds, from up to 200,000, with maximal overlap with the top ten retrieval heads) and "typical" masks (first three seeds covering at most five of them). Copy-suppression heads are excluded from all three.

**Causal probes.** Two are worth noting. A *path cut* removes the retrieval heads' output only from the input of selected downstream heads while every other component still receives it; the selected backup heads then show a positive change under full ablation but near-zero/negative under the cut, whereas copy-suppression heads *increase* their direct contribution under the cut — evidence that the backup response reacts to the wider computation while copy suppression tracks the direct retrieval signal. A *response-prevention clamp* replaces selected heads' pre-projection outputs with clean-run values at the answer position while later computation stays live, with low-response heads and clean-to-clean replacement as controls.

**Statistics.** 95% paired template-cluster bootstrap (2,000 replicates), resampling prompts *by template* and sharing the same prompts across models and conditions — appropriate for prompt variation, and explicitly not a claim about variation across independently trained models.

## Results & Highlights

- **Matched 0.5-logit deficit, Pythia suite: the repair gap is ~0.43 logits and reverses sign.** At D = 0.5, Pythia-410M's release + backup + normalization supply ≈0.55 logits, so the measured margin sits ~0.05 logits *above* its clean value; Pythia-12B retains ≈0.38 of the original 0.5 logits as output harm. That is a swing of roughly 0.43 logits in net repair between the two ends of the suite for identical nominal damage — the paper's headline number.
- **Copy-suppression release is the mechanism that switches off.** The remainder after release, `D − R`, is ~27% of D at 410M but ~90% at 12B, and the fraction grows with size. The underlying quantity is standing suppression: ~1.1 logits (≈25% of the clean margin) at 410M versus ~0.2 logits (≈5%) at 12B — a 5.5× drop that leaves the large model almost nothing to release.
- **Net attention response collapses between 1.4B and 2.8B, not gradually.** As a share of the post-release remainder, the net attention response is 80% (410M), 132% (1B), 100% (1.4B), then 13% (2.8B), 9% (6.9B), 9% (12B). Note the remainder itself is similar from 1B to 12B, so the drop is a change in response, not in opportunity.
- **Held-out, discovery-calibrated ablation (primary condition, 2,000 prompts, deficits 0.756–0.888 logits).** Natural output harm H: −0.067 (410M), −0.226 (1B), −0.027 (1.4B), 0.476 (2.8B), 0.561 (6.9B), 0.581 (12B) logits — small models *gain* margin, the three largest lose roughly two-thirds of the removed contribution. Component split at 410M is R = 0.583, A = 0.171, M = −0.010, N = 0.120 against D = 0.797; at 12B it is R = 0.076, A = 0.071, M = 0.073, N = 0.049 against D = 0.851.
- **Prevention is more damaging at scale, which rules out "the response is simply absent."** Clamping the selected backup receivers (n = 9 at 410M, 34 at 12B, chosen to cover 90% of the positive attention response on discovery prompts) adds 0.145 [0.127, 0.164] logits of harm at 410M and 0.297 [0.228, 0.366] at 12B. Jointly clamping backup and copy-suppression heads exceeds the original deficit at 2.8B (1.770 vs 0.888), 6.9B (0.905 vs 0.829) and 12B (1.018 vs 0.851). At 1.4B the clamp also shrinks the measured deficit to 0.707, a confound the paper discloses.
- **Simulated 4-bit quantization reproduces the ordering in two families.** Harm as a fraction of the achieved deficit H/D rises overall from 0.55 (Pythia-410M) to 0.91 (Pythia-12B), with non-monotone intermediates (1B 0.82, 1.4B 0.61, 6.9B 0.97), and from 0.41 (OPT-125M) to 0.98 (OPT-6.7B). In the single-model Pythia-410M sweep, ten quantized heads lose 1.70 logits of direct contribution while the output falls only 0.54 — 68% of the damage is absorbed.
- **Pruning: direction consistent, magnitude fragile.** Under "unlucky" masks (maximal overlap with the top ten retrieval heads) mean repair stays positive but decreases with model size; under random masks repair is non-monotonic and lower than the unlucky condition at every displayed size. The authors are careful to say the scale reduction is "clearest for the unlucky condition."
- **The fixed-ten-head view agrees but is not pooled.** In that exploratory comparison repair falls from ~80% of the deficit at 410M to ~29% at 12B, while the removed contribution itself varies from ~1.3 to ~3.7 logits — which is exactly why the matched-deficit analysis exists, and the paper keeps the two analyses separate rather than averaging them.

## Authors & Credibility

Ten authors: Gautam Gupta and Dikshant Kukreja (equal contribution) with Unique Kumar, Avni Mittal, Deepanshu Dabas, Kshitij Sah, Erik Cambria, Rajiv Ratn Shah, Zhengkui Wang, and Avinash Anand. Affiliations span IIIT-Delhi, TurboML, Microsoft, NTU Singapore, IIT Kanpur, and the Singapore Institute of Technology. Cambria (NTU) and Shah (IIIT-Delhi) are established senior researchers with long publication records in NLP and affective computing; the composition — several junior authors plus two senior names, an industry intern-style split across five institutions — is typical of a well-resourced but student-led mechanistic-interpretability effort.

The paper's scholarly hygiene is above average for this genre. It builds directly on the copy-suppression (McDougall et al. 2023) and self-repair/Hydra (McGrath et al. 2023; Rushing & Nanda 2024) literatures rather than reinventing them, uses the Wu et al. (2025) retrieval score and the standard IOI task, and pre-commits target selection and calibration on a discovery split before held-out evaluation. It also discloses its own confounds in the body text rather than burying them: the deliberately poor quantizer calibration in Fig. 2, the 1.4B clamp shifting D by −0.049, the missing 2.8B/12B checkpoint histories, and the fact that the bootstrap intervals describe prompt variation rather than model-seed variation. The appendix is unusually complete (per-model head-set sizes, per-condition deficits, receiver counts, alternative readouts).

Weaknesses of provenance: no released code or artifact is mentioned anywhere in the text, and the intervention code — which is where all the subtlety lives (clean-scale attribution, path cuts, clamps) — would be the expensive part to reproduce. The paper is also a single-team result on a single mechanistic hypothesis with no independent replication, and it is a preprint (arXiv v1, 5 Nov 2026, cs.AI, ACL-style formatting) with no venue yet.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The accounting identity itself is not new mathematics — it is the standard "logit difference decomposes into direct logit attributions plus a LayerNorm residual" fact from the Transformer Circuits line, and `H = D − R − B − N` holds by definition once you define `N` as the leftover. The genuine novelty is the *use*: treating that bookkeeping as a measurement instrument for compression tolerance, and — more importantly — the matched-deficit protocol. Most compression-vs-scale papers compare at matched bit-width or matched sparsity; comparing at matched *circuit damage* is the right control and is, as far as I can tell, new here. The specific empirical claim (larger models retain a greater share of comparable circuit damage) is a real and non-obvious finding, not a restatement of "big models are harder to compress."

**Rigor — where it is strong.** The Pythia choice is well motivated and correctly exploited (shared data, shared order, fixed token budget). Freezing target ranking, damage calibration, and receiver selection on the exploratory split before touching the 2,000 held-out prompts is exactly what this literature usually fails to do. The path-cut vs. full-ablation contrast is a genuinely informative probe, and the response-prevention clamp is the right causal test rather than another correlational sweep. Reporting the OPT and Llama replications, including the OPT case where the post-release remainder *exceeds* the initial deficit at 6.7B, is honest.

**Rigor — where it is weak.** Four problems, in descending order of severity.

First, the trend rests on six points with **one model per size and one training run per size**. The bootstrap intervals resample prompts by template and the paper says outright that they "describe variation over prompts, not over independently trained models." There is no fitted slope, no R², no test of monotonicity — just six bars with error bars that cannot speak to the quantity being claimed. And the intermediate sizes already violate the story: H = −0.226 at 1B and −0.027 at 1.4B sit far off any monotone curve, and at 1B the net attention response is 132% of the post-release remainder with R = −0.045 (copy suppression *strengthening* rather than releasing). A trend claim over a 30× parameter range with two wild interior outliers and no between-seed variance is suggestive, not established.

Second, **"compression" is a stand-in, not a compression method.** Quantization is simulated: per-channel 4-bit on the four attention projections of a handful of target heads, dequantized in place, fp32 inference, biases untouched, no fused kernels, no KV-cache quantization, no GPTQ/AWQ/SmoothQuant, and — most tellingly — the single-model sweep in Fig. 2 uses a *deliberately poor* calibrator (min–max grid stretched threefold on one side) because a good one would hide the effect. Pruning is a structural half-head mask, not SparseGPT or Wanda. There is no perplexity number, no downstream benchmark, no full-model compression anywhere in the paper. The title says "compression"; the experiments say "targeted perturbation of one circuit." The authors do acknowledge this in Limitations ("studied through dequantized weights and structural masks in floating-point inference"), but the framing outruns the evidence by a wide margin.

Third, **the task is saturated, so "harm" is margin loss on a capability no model fails.** Clean pairwise IOI accuracy is 0.99–1.00 in all six Pythia models and the clean margin is 3.95–5.05 logits. A retained harm of 0.38 logits at 12B is roughly 9% of a 4.3-logit margin on a task the model still gets right. Calling that "costly" is a claim about headroom, not about behavior — and the paper never shows a single prompt where the 12B model's prediction flips. The bridge from "0.38 logits of margin" to "compression that is nearly harmless in small models can be costly in large ones" is asserted, not measured.

Fourth, **the dominant repair term rests on a two-head set with no sensitivity analysis.** `|CS| = 2` at every model size, selected by a 0.5 OV/QK threshold plus the two top scorers. That two-head set supplies R = 0.583 of the 0.797-logit deficit at 410M — i.e., the single largest term in the entire scale story is computed over two heads chosen by a fixed, hand-tuned rule. There is no sweep over CS-set size, no threshold sensitivity, no functional-selection comparison at the final-model level (the checkpoint analysis uses a *different*, functional CS selection, which makes the two analyses non-comparable by construction). If the 410M model happens to have two unusually strong copy-suppression heads, the "buffer" is a property of those two heads, not of scale.

**Additional red flags.** (i) The LayerNorm term `N` reaches 0.120 logits at 410M — 15% of D and larger than the entire MLP response — so "exact decomposition" is doing real work only after a term that is admittedly "not a third population of compensating components." (ii) The overshoot story that motivates the suppression account is readout-dependent: with the answer score redefined as the answer logit minus the *mean* of all other vocabulary entries, all six mean trajectories peak at the final layer (Fig. 23), erasing the small-vs-large distinction the hypothesis needs. The paper reports this and does not resolve it. (iii) The backup response `B` is a *signed net* over all remaining heads and MLPs, so cancellation can mask large opposing adjustments — the authors concede that "testing whether such cancellation explains the scale difference remains future work," which means the mechanism behind the headline effect is explicitly unresolved, and the response-prevention result (protective adjustments persist at 12B) points *toward* the cancellation explanation. (iv) In the quantization replication, Pythia-1.4B and 2.8B never reach D = 0.5 and use k = 40 and k = 170 instead — the cross-scale quantization comparison is at unmatched damage exactly in the middle of the range, where the non-monotonicity lives. (v) Confusingly, the retrieval set size varies 27→389 heads; controlling damage in logits but not in circuit fraction means "the same 0.5-logit deficit" may remove a very different *share* of the retrieval machinery at each scale, and greater redundancy in the surviving 12B circuit is an alternative explanation the paper never tests.

**Verdict.** The measurement framework is a real contribution and the matched-deficit protocol should be adopted by anyone doing compression-vs-scale circuit work. The empirical claim is plausible, carefully hedged in the appendix, and overstated in the title. Read it for the accounting method and the response-prevention experiment; discount the "compression is more costly at scale" headline until someone runs it with a real quantizer on an unsaturated task with more than one seed per size.

## Strengths

- **A reusable measurement instrument.** `H = D − R − B − N` converts "the benchmark score didn't move" into four separately weighable quantities, and it is cheap: no retraining, no auxiliary probes, just direct logit attribution with a fixed clean normalization scale.
- **The matched-deficit protocol is the right control and is clearly explained.** The paper first shows that ten heads remove 1.3–3.7 logits depending on model (so head count is not damage), then interpolates to a shared 0.5-logit deficit. This alone invalidates a chunk of the existing "we pruned N heads at every scale" literature.
- **Pre-registered-ish evaluation.** Target ranking, damage calibration, and receiver selection are frozen on 1,000 exploratory prompts before the 2,000 held-out prompts are touched, with template-cluster bootstrap intervals. Uncommon discipline in mechanistic interpretability.
- **Two causal probes rather than one.** The path cut isolates whether the backup response is a reaction to the missing input or to the wider computation; the response-prevention clamp tests whether the response is protective. The combination produces the paper's most interesting finding — protective adjustments persist at 12B while net restoration vanishes.
- **Honest, unusually specific limitations.** The paper volunteers the bad quantizer calibration, the 1.4B clamp confound, the unmatched quantization settings, the correlational nature of the checkpoint analysis, and the readout sensitivity of the overshoot. Most papers in this space would have quietly dropped Fig. 23.
- **Cross-family replication** (Pythia, OPT, Llama 3.1 8B, GPT-2 XL) rather than a single-suite claim, with the OPT result allowed to show a *negative* release at 6.7B rather than being massaged.

## Limitations

- **One model per size, one training run per size.** No between-seed variance is measured or estimable; the claimed scaling trend is a six-point curve with two interior outliers (1B, 1.4B) and no statistical test of monotonicity.
- **The task is saturated and synthetic.** IOI with 30 templates and single-token names, at 0.99–1.00 accuracy everywhere. All results are logit-margin results; no prediction flips, no generation, no naturalistic task, no long-context retrieval despite the paper's own citations to long-context compression failures.
- **Compression is simulated and narrow.** Per-channel 4-bit on four attention projections of selected heads, dequantized for fp32 inference; structural half-head masks for pruning. No real quantizer, no full-model compression, no KV cache, no kernels, no perplexity or downstream benchmark.
- **The scale story is a circuit-redundancy story, possibly.** Damage is matched in logits but not in fraction-of-circuit; with 27 retrieval heads at 1B and 389 at 12B, "the same deficit" removes very different shares of the machinery. The paper does not test whether the surviving circuit at 12B is simply more redundant.
- **The mechanism is unresolved by the authors' own admission.** The backup term is a signed net; whether the scale decline comes from cancellation among opposing heads or from genuinely smaller adjustments is left as future work, even though the response-prevention result hints at cancellation.
- **The copy-suppression channel rests on two heads** with no sensitivity sweep over threshold or set size, and the checkpoint analysis uses a different (functional) CS selection than the final-model analysis, so the two cannot be directly compared.
- **The developmental hypothesis is correlational** and its motivating signal (intermediate overshoot) disappears under a different answer-score readout, which the paper reports but does not reconcile.
- **Intermediate checkpoints for 2.8B and 12B are missing** ("source identities could not be resolved"), so the training-dynamics analysis covers only the four smaller models — precisely the half of the suite where the effect is *not* the headline.
- **No code or artifact release is mentioned**, and the intervention details (clean-scale attribution, path cuts, clamps, boundary-head attenuation) are exactly the parts that are hard to reimplement from prose.

## Relevance

直接命中 **Reliability under Extreme Performance Optimization**：它把"量化误差如何变成输出损失"从 benchmark 分数层面下沉到注意力头与 copy-suppression 回路的层面，并给出一个可复用的记账恒等式（损伤 / 释放 / 备份 / 归一化），这正是"低精度数值误差如何影响模型行为"需要的因果链条而非相关性。同时也在 **LLM Hidden States & Interpretability** 的范围内——用 logit lens、DLA、path cut 和响应钳制做电路级因果分析；其"小模型上的压缩容错不能外推到大模型"的结论，对任何用小代理模型做压缩/精度评估的工程决策都是一个应当记住的警告。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the trend survive with more than one seed per size? Pythia offers only single runs, but OLMo or the Pythia deduped variants could supply at least two training histories per scale — and the 1B/1.4B outliers suggest the answer is not obvious.
2. What happens on an *unsaturated* task? IOI at 1.00 accuracy means the 0.38-logit loss at 12B never flips a prediction. A task where clean margin is ~1 logit (or where accuracy is 0.7) would test whether the retained harm is behaviorally meaningful or merely headroom accounting.
3. Is the effect about scale or about circuit redundancy? Match not just the deficit in logits but the *fraction* of the identified retrieval circuit removed (27 heads at 1B vs 389 at 12B is a 14× range). If matching circuit fraction removes the trend, the story is redundancy, not scale.
4. Does a real quantizer (GPTQ, AWQ, SmoothQuant) on the full model, evaluated by perplexity and a downstream suite, reproduce H/D = 0.91 at 12B — or does the good calibrator that the paper deliberately avoided in Fig. 2 also erase the scale effect in the deployment setting?
5. Can the cancellation hypothesis be tested directly? Decompose `B` into positive and negative head-level changes and check whether the positive mass stays constant while the negative mass grows with scale — that would convert "net repair declines" into a concrete mechanistic claim.
6. Is the two-head copy-suppression set an artifact? Sweep the OV/QK threshold from 0.3 to 0.8 and the CS set size from 1 to 20, and see whether the 1.1-vs-0.2-logit standing-suppression gap at 410M vs 12B is a property of scale or of which two heads got picked.
7. Does suppressing the intermediate overshoot *causally* produce copy suppression? The paper's own limitation says interventions during training would be needed; a small-scale training run with an auxiliary penalty on intermediate-layer margin would be a cheap version of that experiment.
