> **一句话总结：** 论文指出 SAM 3 量化后最致命的失效不是掩码质量下降而是"提示对齐漂移"——对低方差残差子空间做同秩同范数扰动，RefCOCO Pr@0.5 掉 22.4 点而 COCO AP 只掉 1.3 点；据此提出 SPQ（概念敏感校准 A + 残差子空间矫正 B + 视频记忆均值偏差融合 C），在 W4A8 图像编码器下把 RefCOCO Pr@0.5 从 PTQ4SAM 的 42.1 拉到 72.5、SA-V J&F 从 65.4 拉到 81.2，同时保住 54.8 COCO mask AP（FP16 为 55.7），且推理图与 FLOPs 完全不变。

> **TL;DR:** The paper reframes PTQ for SAM 3 as a *grounding* problem rather than a reconstruction problem: under W4A8 the image encoder, PTQ4SAM keeps 54.1 COCO AP (FP16 55.7) but collapses on RefCOCO Pr@0.5 to 42.1 (FP16 75.5), and matched-norm interventions show why — perturbing the residual (low-variance) PCA basis costs −22.4 RefCOCO points for only −1.3 AP, while perturbing the dominant basis costs −8.7 AP for −4.6 RefCOCO. SPQ attacks this with three offline, inference-free steps: concept-score Gauss–Newton weighting of weight rounding (A), a feature-space penalty on residual-subspace error (B), and a closed-form first-moment bias fusion at video-memory interfaces (C). Under one shared protocol it reaches 54.8 COCO AP / 72.5 RefCOCO Pr@0.5 / 81.2 SA-V J&F versus 54.1 / 42.1 / 65.4 for PTQ4SAM, and still leads the best adapted baseline CAR-SAM (54.5 / 61.7 / 69.4) by +10.8 RefCOCO and +11.8 J&F — though the margin over Mix-QSAM3 under its own mixed-precision allocation shrinks to +4.7 / +2.2.

## 核心贡献

- **命名并量化了一个被 AP 掩盖的失效模式：prompt-alignment drift。** 量化后的 SAM 3 可以画出"看起来合理"的掩码却圈错目标、漏掉被提示的部位、或在视频中换掉身份。W4A8 下 PTQ4SAM 在 COCO 上只掉 1.6 AP，在 RefCOCO 上却掉 33.4 点（42.1 vs 75.5）——结构性指标几乎看不出问题。
- **给出几何解释并做了可证伪的验证。** 用中心化校准特征的 PCA 把特征空间拆成 top-k 主方向 `Udom` 与低能量补 `Ures`，再用**同秩同范数**的匹配干预比较敏感性：扰动 `Ures` 得 ΔAP/ΔRef = −1.3/−22.4，扰动 `Udom` 得 −8.7/−4.6，随机秩匹配基为 −3.0/−7.2；直接移除两基分别为 −2.1/−26.8 与 −10.2/−5.1。这是一个干净的双分离（double dissociation），而非仅凭相关性叙事。
- **提出 SPQ：三个全部离线、零推理算子的组件。** A（Semantic-Anchor Calibration）用固定概念码本的概念分数 Gauss–Newton 曲率给权重舍入加权；B（Orthogonal Subspace Rectification）在特征层直接惩罚残差子空间误差；C（Temporal Distribution Alignment）用配对 rollout 估计视频记忆接口的一阶均值偏差，闭式折进已有 bias（`b̂_new = b̂ + Âβ`），key/value 分开估计。
- **完整的 2³ 因子消融 + 5 个同协议适配基线。** A 单独把 COCO AP 从 45.2 提到 52.5（+7.3），B 单独把 RefCOCO 从 31.1 提到 65.9（+34.8），C 单独把 SA-V J&F 从 56.5 提到 75.6（+19.1），三者合用达 54.8/72.5/81.2。
- **跨模型迁移与部署核算。** 把 A+B 用到 CLIP ViT-B/16（W4A8），Flickr30k I2T R@1 从 PTQ4ViT 的 58.4 提到 78.5（FP16 81.2）；RTX 3090 上 1024×1024 视频帧从 1.35 GB/14.8 GB/42.5 ms 降到 0.38 GB/7.9 GB/18.2 ms（3.5× 压缩、2.3× 加速）。

## Key Contributions

- **Diagnoses prompt-alignment drift as the dominant quantization failure mode for SAM 3**, and shows it is invisible to standard reconstruction and mask-AP metrics: 1.6 AP lost versus 33.4 Pr@0.5 lost for PTQ4SAM at W4A8.
- **Provides a geometric account with a controlled test.** PCA splits features into a dominant and a low-energy residual complement; matched-rank, matched-norm interventions on the same unperturbed checkpoint isolate residual directions as the ones grounding depends on. The Davis–Kahan bound (Eq. 4) links residual-basis rotation to covariance perturbation and the eigengap, giving a cheap diagnostic (median relative boundary gap 0.07, IQR 0.04–0.11).
- **Introduces three composable calibration objectives** that change only offline parameters: concept-score curvature-weighted rounding, a residual-subspace feature penalty, and a closed-form memory-bias fusion. None adds an inference operator, a kernel, or a FLOP.
- **Establishes a controlled comparison protocol for SAM 3 PTQ**: one checkpoint, one calibration set, one target-module set, one hybrid precision assignment (encoder W4A8, decoder/queries/memory W8A8, text encoder and heads FP16), five adapted baselines (RepQ-ViT, PTQ4SAM, SAQ-SAM, CAR-SAM, plus Mix-QSAM3 with its native allocation) plus a complete 8-cell factorial.
- **Demonstrates transfer beyond segmentation** to CLIP zero-shot retrieval, and reports negative-ish diagnostics honestly (codebook overlap only 48.2% exact / 61.3% lemma with evaluation head nouns; leave-concept-out calibration drops 72.5 → 70.6).

## Methodology

**Setup and precision recipe.** "W4A8" means 4-bit image-encoder weights with 8-bit activations; the multimodal decoder, detector queries, and video memory states stay at W8A8, and the text encoder plus prediction heads stay FP16. The authors justify this asymmetry by noting that prompt-conditioned decoding and memory propagation are more sensitive to activation noise than the encoder. Design A/B calibrate on 1,024 unlabeled SA-1B images; Design C and every video baseline share the same 32 annotation-free clips, disjoint from SA-V val/test. α = 0.1, λ = 1, 2,000 updates per block for A/B, closed form for C.

**Subspace decomposition (Sec. 2.3).** Centered calibration tokens `Fc = F − 1µᵀ` give `Σ = (1/N) FcᵀFc = UΛUᵀ`; `U` is partitioned into `Udom ∈ R^{D×k}` (top-k variance) and `Ures ∈ R^{D×r}`. Rank `k` is chosen by concept-score drift on *held-out calibration data* and frozen before evaluation, with RefCOCO annotations reserved for eval only. Default `k/D = 0.50`.

**Design A — Semantic-Anchor Calibration.** SA-1B has no text, so a fixed concept codebook supplies it: Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct generates short object-centric phrases offline from calibration images, which are deduplicated, augmented with synonyms and templates ("a photo of {concept}"), embedded by the *frozen* SAM 3 text encoder, normalized, and averaged per concept into `Tbase ∈ R^{C×D}`. Concept scores are `S = Fvis Tbaseᵀ`. The curvature `Hsem = E[JWᵀ JW]` with `JW = ∂vec(S)/∂vec(W)` measures local sensitivity of concept scores to block parameters, approximated block-diagonally via Jacobian–vector products. The block objective is `L_A = E‖WX − ŴX̂‖²_F + α Δwᵀ Hsem Δw`. This is Hessian-aware PTQ with the Hessian swapped for a task-aligned Gauss–Newton matrix.

**Design B — Orthogonal Subspace Rectification.** A feature-level complement to A's parameter-space objective: `L_B = (1/(Nr)) ‖(F − F̂)Ures‖²_F`, and `L_SPQ = L_A + λ L_B`. The `Nr` normalization keeps λ comparable across layers and ranks.

**Design C — Temporal Distribution Alignment.** SAM 3's memory bank and memory cross-attention accumulate small feature shifts across frames. With paired FP16/quantized features `fᵗ_FP, fᵗ_Q` at the same memory write/read interface — identical clips, prompts, history initialization, time indices — the per-layer mean error is `β = E[fᵗ_FP − fᵗ_Q]`. For a directly following exported affine map `ŷ = Âf + b̂` with no intervening normalization or nonlinearity, the correction is fused as `b̂_new = b̂ + Âβ`, estimated separately for key and value paths. This is a first-moment correction only; it targets the mean, not the variance, of the memory-feature error.

**Evaluation.** COCO 2017 and LVIS v1.0 for mask quality, RefCOCO/+/g for prompt grounding, SA-V for video tracking (progressive frame mIoU, video J&F, consistency `Tcs`). Baselines are adapted to the same checkpoint, calibration data, target modules, precision, and protocol; each keeps its published calibration rule.

## Results & Highlights

- **RefCOCO grounding, W4A8, image encoder:** SPQ reaches **72.5 Pr@0.5 / 64.2 oIoU** versus **42.1 / 35.4** for PTQ4SAM (+30.4 / +28.8) and 75.5 / 66.8 for FP16 — i.e. SPQ closes 88% of the 33.4-point grounding gap that PTQ4SAM leaves open. The same pattern holds on RefCOCO+ (65.1 / 57.2 vs 36.5 / 29.2) and RefCOCOg (71.0 / 62.5 vs 40.2 / 32.8).
- **Structural segmentation, W4A8:** COCO mask AP **54.8** vs **54.1** for PTQ4SAM (+0.7) and 55.7 FP16; LVIS v1.0 AP **47.5** vs **46.8** (+0.7) and 48.5 FP16. Note the structural margin is ~40× smaller than the grounding margin — this is precisely the paper's thesis, but it also means the AP ranking is inside run-to-run noise (the authors concede 0.2–0.4 variation).
- **Long-horizon video tracking, SA-V, W4A8:** video J&F **81.2** vs **65.4** for PTQ4SAM (+15.8) and 83.5 FP16; frame-100 progressive mIoU **80.1** vs **52.1** (PTQ4SAM loses 28.1 mIoU from frame 1 to frame 100, SPQ loses only 3.4), and consistency `Tcs` **95.9%** vs **65.0%** (FP16 96.7%).
- **Against the strongest adapted baselines (not just PTQ4SAM):** CAR-SAM scores 54.5 / 61.7 / 69.4 and SAQ-SAM 54.3 / 58.9 / 66.8, so SPQ's grounding margin over the best classical PTQ baseline is **+10.8 Pr@0.5**, not +30.4. Mix-QSAM3 under its *native mixed-precision allocation* (not uniform W4A8) reaches 54.4 / 67.8 / 79.0, cutting the margin to **+4.7 RefCOCO and +2.2 J&F**; RepQ-ViT is far behind at 51.7 / 40.8 / 64.0.
- **W8A8 is nearly lossless for SPQ but not for the baseline:** RefCOCO Pr@0.5 **74.8** vs **65.5** for PTQ4SAM and 75.5 FP16; COCO AP 55.6 vs 55.2. A 10-point W8A8 grounding loss in the baseline is unusually large for 8-bit and is the paper's least-explained number.
- **CLIP ViT-B/16 transfer, W4A8, zero-shot retrieval:** Flickr30k I2T R@1 **78.5** vs **58.4** for PTQ4ViT (+20.1) and 81.2 FP16; MSCOCO I2T R@1 **55.8** vs **41.2** (+14.6) and 58.2 FP16. No RepQ-ViT baseline is reported for CLIP.
- **Deployment (RTX 3090, 1024×1024 frames):** FP16 1.35 GB / 14.8 GB peak / 42.5 ms / 23.5 FPS → SPQ W4A8 0.38 GB / 7.9 GB / 18.2 ms / **54.9 FPS** (3.5× compression, 2.3× speedup). At W8A8, SPQ and PTQ4SAM are identical (0.68 GB / 8.5 GB / 25.4 ms), confirming zero runtime overhead.
- **Calibration data efficiency:** with only 32 SA-1B images SPQ already gets 70.2 / 62.1 / 78.8 (RefCOCO Pr@0.5 / oIoU / SA-V J&F) versus 72.5 / 64.2 / 81.2 with 1,024 — the method degrades gracefully rather than needing a large calibration set.

## Authors & Credibility

Ten authors spanning University of Alabama at Birmingham (Xi Xiao, Tianyang Wang), Tulane (Yunbei Zhang, Janet Wang), Northeastern (Lin Zhao, Xinru Jiang), UT Austin (Yanshu Li), UVA (Chenrui Ma), University of Pittsburgh (Pan Wang), and Oak Ridge National Laboratory (Xiao Wang, corresponding). The group has a visible, coherent line of work on subspace/task-direction methods — MedSTP (WACV 2027, dominant-subspace preservation for medical-VQA pruning), StructLoRA (ACL 2026, task-aware low-rank adaptation), D-HSM (EMNLP 2026) — so the residual-subspace framing is a genuine research program rather than a one-off. ORNL's involvement is acknowledged under DOE Contract DE-AC05-00OR22725, and the paper carries an IEEE 2027 copyright notice, consistent with a camera-ready for a 2027 venue.

Positives on process: the paper includes an explicit LLM usage statement (Gemini and Codex for language editing and camera-ready formatting; Qwen2.5-VL-72B offline only for calibration phrases, no evaluation annotations, no inference use), states that no LLM generated experimental data, and reports unfavorable diagnostics (codebook overlap 48.2%, leave-concept-out degradation, rank sensitivity) rather than hiding them. The controlled-protocol claim is credible: five baselines adapted to one checkpoint and one calibration set is more work than most PTQ papers do.

Negatives: no code or checkpoint release is mentioned anywhere; SAM 3 itself is a very recent model (arXiv 2511.16719, Nov 2025), so independent reproduction depends on checkpoint access the reader may not have; the Qwen2.5-VL phrase-generation prompt and the dedup/synonym pipeline are described only in prose.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty is compositional; the diagnosis is the real contribution.** Design A is HAWQ/FIMA-Q-style Hessian-aware rounding with the Hessian replaced by a concept-score Gauss–Newton matrix — a sensible substitution, not a new quantization primitive. Design B is the group's own MedSTP idea (preserve a chosen subspace) inverted: MedSTP preserves the *dominant* subspace during pruning, SPQ penalizes error in the *residual* subspace during quantization. Design C is classical static distribution correction (bias absorption into a following affine map), applied at a new interface. What is genuinely new and valuable is the framing — "quantization error that reconstruction cannot see" — plus the matched-intervention experiment that makes it falsifiable. That is worth publishing; the three components are less novel than the abstract implies.

**The headline number is measured against the weakest credible baseline.** The abstract leads with "42.1 → 72.5 over PTQ4SAM," but PTQ4SAM is a 2024 method built for SAM ViT-H mask quality and was never designed for text-prompted grounding; adapting it is the authors' own construction, and its W4A8 RefCOCO score of 42.1 may reflect a protocol mismatch more than a method limit. Against CAR-SAM (2026, cross-attention reconstruction, also adapted) the margin is +10.8; against Mix-QSAM3 (2026, mixed precision for SAM 3 specifically) it is +4.7 — and Mix-QSAM3 runs under its *native* allocation, so the two are not matched on effective bit budget in either direction. The paper discloses all of this in Sec. 4.5 and Table S5, which is honest, but the abstract and Figure 1 are built on the +30.4 framing.

**The W8A8 anomaly is unexplained and it matters.** PTQ4SAM at W8A8 scores 65.5 RefCOCO Pr@0.5 against 75.5 FP16 — a 10-point loss at 8 bits, roughly an order of magnitude larger than typical W8A8 degradation and much worse than PTQ4SAM's own reported behavior on SAM. Either SAM 3's concept-selection head is extraordinarily quantization-sensitive (plausible: selecting one concept among a large vocabulary is a near-tie argmax decision, so tiny logit perturbations flip it), or the baseline is being quantized more harshly than its published configuration — e.g. per-tensor activation quantization on modules PTQ4SAM never targeted. The paper never lists the exact quantized module set per method, and never runs the obvious diagnostic: a per-module sweep showing which single module's quantization produces the 33-point W4A8 drop. Without that, the reader cannot separate "grounding is fragile" from "the baseline was set up to fail." The matched-intervention table (S3) is strong evidence for the *mechanism*, but it does not address the *magnitude* attribution.

**Design C's +19.1 J&F from a mean-bias correction is a red flag about the baseline, not a triumph.** A first-moment correction recovering 19 J&F points (56.5 → 75.6) implies the quantized memory interface is badly miscalibrated — most likely per-tensor min-max on memory states whose distributions have outliers, which is a known and easily fixed weakness. The paper does not compare against the two obvious alternatives that would settle whether TDA is a mechanism or a patch: (i) keeping the memory bank in FP16 while quantizing only the encoder (the actual deployment knob for video memory cost), and (ii) per-channel scale calibration of memory states. It also does not ablate TDA against a second-moment (variance) correction, even though the motivation explicitly says small shifts "accumulate across frames" — accumulation is a variance/drift phenomenon, and a mean correction alone should not fix it in general. Notably, the memory bank is the component that actually dominates video deployment cost, and it is left at W8A8 throughout, so the headline 3.5× compression is an encoder-weight story.

**Statistical rigor is thin.** Single seed, no confidence intervals in any main table, and the authors themselves attribute 0.2–0.4 points of variation to Design C's rows. That variation is *larger* than SPQ's COCO AP margin over CAR-SAM (54.8 vs 54.5) and over Mix-QSAM3 (54.8 vs 54.4). So the structural-AP ranking among the top three methods is unresolvable from this paper; only the grounding and video gaps (10–30 points) are safely outside noise. The 2³ factorial is complete, which is better than most, but each cell is a single run.

**The factorial is sub-additive and the interaction is never discussed.** A alone gives +11.7 RefCOCO (31.1 → 42.8), B alone +34.8 (→ 65.9), together +41.4 (→ 72.5) rather than the 46.5 an additive model would predict. That is expected if A's concept curvature and B's residual penalty both push rounding toward the same low-variance directions — i.e. partially redundant. The paper reports the cells but never interprets the overlap, which matters for anyone deciding whether to implement one component or both.

**Efficiency claims are bit-width properties, not method properties.** SPQ's hardware footprint at W8A8 is *identical* to PTQ4SAM's (0.68 GB / 8.5 GB / 25.4 ms) — by construction, since the calibration updates are fused. That is a legitimate and well-verified "free lunch" claim. But the 3.5× / 2.3× headline is simply FP16 versus INT4, achievable by any W4A8 method; no latency is measured for the adapted baselines at W4A8 to confirm parity, and no kernel or backend is named for INT4 inference on an RTX 3090 (which has no native INT4 tensor-core path for this workload, so the 2.3× speedup depends entirely on an unstated dequantization kernel). Calibration wall-clock and peak calibration memory are explicitly deferred ("remains part of a fuller deployment analysis"), which is a real gap given 2,000 updates per block with a curvature term — closer to light QAT than to classic PTQ.

**Codebook leakage is handled well; CLIP baselines are not.** Reporting 48.2% exact / 61.3% lemma head-noun overlap and then showing leave-concept-out calibration still reaches 70.6 (vs 72.5 full) is exactly the right check, and the authors deserve credit for it. The CLIP study, however, compares only against MinMax and PTQ4ViT — the two weakest options — while omitting RepQ-ViT, which they adapt for SAM 3 and which would be substantially stronger on CLIP. A +20-point gain over PTQ4ViT is therefore weak evidence that the method beats modern CLIP PTQ.

**Editorial sloppiness suggests a rushed camera-ready.** The title says "PTQ4SAM3" while the method is called SPQ everywhere in the body; "Complete 2³ factorial" is typeset as "Complete 23 factorial" in the Table 4 caption; the LVIS APf cell is blank in the W4A8 MinMax row of Table 1. None of these affects the science, but together with the unresolved W8A8 anomaly they suggest the paper needed another review pass.

## Strengths

- **The double dissociation in Table S3 is the best part of the paper.** Perturbing `Ures` costs −1.3 AP / −22.4 RefCOCO; perturbing `Udom` costs −8.7 / −4.6; a rank-matched random basis lands in between at −3.0 / −7.2. This is a clean, pre-registered-looking test that actually discriminates between hypotheses, and it is the kind of evidence most quantization papers never provide.
- **The problem framing is genuinely useful.** "A quantized model can draw a plausible mask around the wrong object" is a failure mode that AP cannot see, and it generalizes well beyond SAM 3 to any prompt-conditioned or retrieval-style model under compression. The diagnostic (residual-basis angle across depth, vision–language alignment across blocks) is cheap and could be adopted as a standard PTQ health check.
- **Zero inference overhead is verified, not asserted.** SPQ matches PTQ4SAM exactly at W8A8 (0.68 GB / 8.5 GB / 25.4 ms / 39.3 FPS), which is the correct way to demonstrate that fused calibration adds nothing to the deployed graph.
- **Comparison protocol discipline.** Five baselines adapted to one checkpoint, one calibration set, one target-module set, and a complete 8-cell factorial is substantially more than the field's norm, and the Mix-QSAM3 caveat (native mixed precision, not uniform W4A8) is disclosed rather than buried.
- **Honest reporting of the method's own weak spots:** rank sensitivity (54.4/54.8/54.6 across k/D), codebook overlap below 50%, leave-concept-out degradation, and a sensitivity sweep showing α = 1.0 *hurts* COCO AP (53.2 vs 54.8).
- **Calibration efficiency is a practical selling point:** 32 images already reach 70.2 RefCOCO Pr@0.5, which makes the method deployable in settings where 1,024 curated images are unavailable.

## Limitations

- **One model family plus one CLIP check.** SAM 3 (one checkpoint) and CLIP ViT-B/16. No SAM 2, no other ViT backbones, no other referring-segmentation heads, so the claim that "grounding is residual-direction-sensitive" is established for exactly one architecture.
- **The aggressive quantization is confined to the image encoder.** Decoder, detector queries, and memory stay at W8A8; text encoder and heads stay FP16. Since video memory is the dominant deployment cost for tracking, the practical compression story is narrower than "3.5× for SAM 3" suggests. W6A6/W4A4 for decoder and memory are explicitly left as future work.
- **No QAT comparison and no total-cost accounting.** With 2,000 updates per block plus a curvature approximation, SPQ sits between PTQ and QAT; without calibration wall-clock, peak calibration memory, or a QAT baseline at equal budget, the accuracy-per-calibration-cost trade-off is unknown.
- **Design C is first-order only.** A mean correction cannot in principle fix error accumulation that is driven by variance growth across frames, yet accumulation is the stated motivation. The empirical gain is large, which suggests the baseline's memory quantization was the real problem.
- **Prompt distribution shift requires recalibration**, as the authors acknowledge; long-tail, compositional ("the leftmost red cup"), and negated prompts are untested, and these are exactly where grounding is hardest.
- **Temporal diagnostics are correlational.** The paper tracks accuracy, residual-basis angle, and alignment together but states plainly that establishing causality "requires further study" — fair, but it means the video story rests on the factorial (C's +19.1) rather than on the geometry.
- **No released code**, and the codebook construction depends on an unspecified Qwen2.5-VL prompting and deduplication procedure.

## Relevance

这篇落在「Reliability under Extreme Performance Optimization」的核心：它给出的正是"低精度带来的语义级静默失效"——重构误差和 mask AP 都正常，模型却在同范数扰动下换掉了被提示的目标（残差方向 −22.4 Pr 点 vs 主方向 −1.3 AP 点的双分离），这是量化可靠性评估里"指标选错就看不出坏掉"的典型样本。其"按任务敏感方向加权校准 + 均值偏差闭式吸收"的思路也可直接迁移到其他 prompt-conditioned 模型（检索、VLM grounding）的低比特部署检查清单。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. **Which module causes the 33-point W4A8 RefCOCO drop?** A per-module quantization sweep (encoder attention vs MLP vs the vision–language fusion block vs the concept-scoring projection) would settle whether the baseline is fragile by construction or whether grounding is intrinsically this brittle. This is the single experiment that would most change a reader's confidence.
2. **Does TDA survive an honest memory baseline?** If the memory bank is kept FP16, or if memory activations get per-channel scales, how much of C's +19.1 J&F remains? A variance/second-moment correction would test the "accumulation" story directly.
3. **What is the total cost of ownership?** Calibration wall-clock, peak calibration memory, and the FLOPs of the Jacobian–vector curvature approximation per block, measured against both PTQ4SAM and a short QAT run at equal GPU-hours.
4. **Is A redundant given B?** The factorial is sub-additive (+11.7 and +34.8 individually, +41.4 together). An analysis of whether A's concept curvature merely re-weights the same directions B penalizes would tell practitioners whether they need both.
5. **Multi-seed variance.** With 0.2–0.4 points of run variation, the 54.8 / 54.5 / 54.4 COCO AP ordering of SPQ, CAR-SAM, and Mix-QSAM3 is not established; three seeds per method would resolve it cheaply.
6. **How does SPQ behave under prompt shift?** Long-tail concepts, compositional spatial relations, and negation are where concept-codebook calibration should be weakest; the leave-concept-out result (70.6 vs 72.5) is a lower bound on that risk, not a measurement of it.
7. **Does residual-direction sensitivity hold for SAM 2 or for non-text prompts?** If the phenomenon is specific to SAM 3's concept-matching head, the paper's framing is narrower than its title suggests; if it reproduces on SAM 2's memory attention, it becomes a general result about promptable segmentation under compression.
