> **一句话总结：** 在数据收集前冻结判据的预注册协议下，把"LLM 提议者 + 验证器"流水线中的提议者固定为 CPU 离线运行的 Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct（每会话 20 条提议），在两个真实数据域（作业车间调度 A、基于北京 T-Drive 的车载边缘卸载 D）各跑 100 对 LLM/随机匹配会话，再用 S−/S0/S+ 三档验证器重新门控同一个提议池：429 例变异电池证明三档确实有序（A 域杀伤率 0.0%/1.7%/10.9%），但 LLM 相对随机控制的配对留出优势并不随验证强度上升（A 单侧 p=0.1115，D p=0.9994），在 D 的严格档下反而显著收缩（边际对比 −0.040，p=0.0013）——因为 S− 与 S0 在 100/100 与 99/100 对中晋升了完全相同的候选；事后并池分析显示，把随机池并入 LLM 池在 A 域可提升 +1.52 留出值单位（n=100，p=0.0002），提示"扩大提议池"而非"收紧闸门"才是杠杆。

> **TL;DR:** This is a pre-registered negative result on the verifier half of LLM proposer–verifier discovery pipelines. Holding the proposer fixed (Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, 20 proposals per session, CPU-only) and varying only the verifier across three tiers (S− minimal screening / S0 full constraint verifier / S+ plus a worst-case stress test), it runs 100 matched LLM-vs-random pairs in two domains: job-shop scheduling over 154 deduplicated production orders (A) and vehicular edge offloading on Beijing T-Drive trajectories with 3 RSUs (D). The manipulation check passes (domain-A mutation battery: 0.0% → 1.7% → 10.9% of 119 mutants killed across tiers), yet the paired LLM-over-random holdout advantage does not scale with it: +0.28/+0.28/+0.38 in A (one-sided Wilcoxon S+ vs S−, p=0.1115) and +0.10/+0.10/+0.06 in D (p=0.9994), where the margin contrast is significantly *negative* (−0.040, 95% CI [−0.07,−0.01], p=0.0013). The mechanism is degeneracy, not noise: S− and S0 promote the identical candidate in 100/100 (A) and 99/100 (D) pairs, so the frozen strict order S+ > S0 > S− is structurally unsatisfiable. Post-hoc, the best of 20 *random* proposals beats the best of 20 LLM proposals in 48% (A) / 46% (D) of pairs at S0, and merging the two pools would add +1.52 holdout units in A (p=0.0002) — more than any verifier tier delivered. Read it if you build or evaluate proposer–verifier loops; discount the headline's generality, because the proposer is a 0.49B model and the effect sizes in domain A are smaller than the confidence interval.

## 核心贡献

- **一个"分离结果"（separation result）而非普通零结果**：同一套冻结判据下，操作性检验 E1-D2 通过（三档验证器在变异电池上确实有序），主假设 E1-D1 失败（发现质量不随验证强度上升）。作者强调这是该设计能产生的最干净的零结果——处理是真的，效应不存在，且在 D 域是反向的。这把"验证强度"从一个被默认的单调杠杆变成了一个需要实证的假设。
- **首次在验证器约束的发现流水线内量化 LLM 与随机提议的覆盖差距**：每对会话中，取 20 条提议里训练值最好的那条，随机臂在 48%（A，S0 档）和 46%（D，S0 档）的对中胜过 LLM 臂；在 D 的严格档 S+ 下这一比例升到 60%——即验证越严，LLM 的相对覆盖劣势越明显，因为严格的过滤器无法晋升从未被提出的候选。
- **并池（union-pool）的事后可实现估计**：把 LLM 的 20 条与随机的 20 条合并后按同一规则选优，在 A 域比只用 LLM 池多 +1.52 留出值单位（单侧 Wilcoxon 符号秩，n=100，p=0.0002；严格档 +1.44，p=0.0006）；D 域则为零到负（−0.036/−0.041/−0.035，p=0.27/0.32/0.07）。作者明确区分"选择规则可实现（只需训练值，不看留出）"与"我们注意到它是事后分析"。
- **中间可行性闸门是惰性的（gate degeneracy）**：S− 与 S0 在 A 域 100/100 对、D 域 99/100 对中晋升同一候选。机制在 A 域是精确的：一个可执行但不可行的规则在训练时得冻结地板分 −10⁹，而它永远不可能是会话内最大值（除非全会话都不可行），所以在验证器反馈下 S0 通过的最佳候选总是 S− 也会晋升的那一个。
- **预注册的方法论教训：判据可达性（criterion reachability）**：当两个处理臂在合理退化情形下会重合时（此处 S0 ≡ S−），冻结的严格序判据 S+ > S0 > S− 在结构上不可满足。作者建议在冻结判据前先检查"该判据在可能的退化结果下是否可被满足"，并把它写成一条可复用的预注册实践规则。
- **把结论转成两个预注册的后续干预**：多样性剂量–反应实验（固定 S0，操纵池组成：温度阶梯、LLM+随机混合池、多模型）与张力迁移实验（固定档位，操纵容量利用率），后者已冻结判据为"S+ 组对比在中利用率附近变号"。

## Key Contributions

- **A pre-registered separation result, not a plain null.** Under frozen criteria (v1.0, with changelogs to v1.2), the manipulation check E1-D2 passes while the main hypothesis E1-D1 fails in both domains. The logical structure matters: because the treatment is demonstrably real and ordered on the mutation battery, the absent effect cannot be explained away as a failed manipulation — the usual escape hatch for negative results in this literature is closed.
- **First measurement, to the authors' knowledge, of the LLM-vs-random best-of-n coverage gap inside a verifier-bound discovery pipeline.** At S0 the union-pool promotion comes from the random arm in 48% of domain-A pairs and 46% of domain-D pairs; under S+ in domain D it rises to 60%, i.e. the gap *widens* exactly where verification is strictest. The framing — "a filter, however strict, cannot promote a candidate that was never proposed" — turns verifier strength into a bounded resource.
- **A derived, implementable pool-composition estimate.** Union selection (best training value over the merged 40-proposal pool) uses no holdout knowledge, so the +1.52 domain-A figure is the holdout estimate of a realizable policy, not an oracle bound. The authors are careful to label the *attention* to it as post-hoc rather than the policy itself.
- **Gate degeneracy as the mechanism.** S− ≡ S0 in 100/100 (A) and 99/100 (D) pairs, because proposals within a session are near-determinately all-feasible or all-infeasible. Two consequences: the frozen strict order is unsatisfiable whenever S0 ≡ S−, and in this regime the entire effect of verification strength is carried by the stress-test tier S+ — which is positive in A (S+ wins 10 of its 14 divergent pairs, mean +0.714) and sharply negative in D (1 of 23, mean −0.066).
- **A criterion-reachability lesson for pre-registration practice**, plus two pre-registered follow-ups (diversity dose–response at fixed verifier; tension transfer at fixed tier) that convert the post-hoc findings into interventional tests rather than leaving them as speculation.

## Methodology

**Decomposition.** The pipeline is a proposer P (an LLM under iterative verifier feedback, 20 proposals per session) and a verifier V mapping a candidate to {pass, fail} plus a frozen score. Discovery quality is the holdout value of the promoted candidate arg max_{c: V(c)=pass} train(c), evaluated exactly once on a held-out instance set H ("HoldoutGate": one-shot semantics, no re-evaluation). J(c) is the frozen holdout score; Δ(·) the paired LLM-over-random margin of a tier; MS(·) the mutation score of a tier.

**Three verifier tiers.** S− (weak): minimal screening — domain A promotes any rule that executes (INFEASIBLE outcomes score the frozen floor −10⁹), domain D any rule without deadline violations. S0 (standard): the full frozen constraint verifier (A: feasibility-gated scoring; D: safety isolation, per-task deadline, per-task energy allowance, per-RSU capacity). S+ (strict): S0 plus a worst-case stress test (A: non-degeneracy of promoted weights; D: re-verification with every task's channel quality lowered to the instance minimum).

**Frozen criteria (v1.0).** E1-D1 (main): Δ(S+) > Δ(S0) > Δ(S−) with one-sided Wilcoxon p < 0.05 for S+ vs S−, in both main domains (A mandatory). E1-D2 (manipulation validity): MS(S−) < MS(S0) < MS(S+) in both domains. A negative clause commits to reporting failure as failure.

**Domains.** Domain A: priority rules over 7 normalized numerical features of a job-shop instance. Instances are bootstrap resamples of a real production-order pool (due window 2026-08-15 to 2026-09-02) — 1,791 rows collapse to 154 unique orders because the same batch was re-imported under different run identifiers. Pair i (seed 1000+i) draws a 60-order train instance without replacement and a disjoint 60-order holdout from the remaining 94, redrawing if the baseline schedule is infeasible or exceeds 40 days. The authors concede the pool is small and pairs are strongly correlated, so domain-A claims are limited to within-distribution perturbation robustness. Domain D: offloading-ratio rules over 8 normalized task features, instances built from Beijing T-Drive taxi trajectories around 3 RSUs, pair i using frozen training seed 9000+i and disjoint holdout seed 10000+i.

**Session and gating design.** Per pair: one fresh LLM session (20 iterative proposals under the S0 prompt and verifier feedback) plus one random control (20 uniform weight vectors over the same rule family, per-pair seed). The *same* 20-proposal pool is then re-gated independently by S−, S0 and S+, each tier promoting its best training-value passing candidate; the random pool is gated identically. Tiers therefore differ only in the filter applied to a fixed pool — this is what makes the manipulation a clean test of verification strength rather than a confound with generation.

**Proposer and compute.** Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct (0.49B parameters, 32K context), served locally via Hugging Face transformers on PyTorch with CPU inference, fully offline. Decoding frozen at temperature 0.8, top-p 0.9; up to 220 new tokens per proposal in A (Chinese prompt template with two fixed few-shot examples) and 180 in D (template with per-category verifier feedback). Sampling is unseeded, so the unit of replication is the 20-proposal session. Everything ran on a consumer laptop CPU; domain D is dominated by trajectory simulation (about half a day for the 100-pair run).

**Manipulation battery.** Domain A: 119 mutants (100 uniform random over the frozen 7-dim family, 19 structured). Domain D: 310 mutants (300 random rule templates, 4 extreme heuristics, 6 structured mutants matched to the violation mix of a prior instrumented ledger). Populations were disclosed before unblinding (protocol v1.2).

**Analysis.** The frozen test is the S+ vs S− contrast. Per-tier paired tests (one-sided Wilcoxon, LLM > random) are labelled post-hoc, as are the margin contrast Δ(S+)−Δ(S−), the union-pool derivation and the tension gradient. Two floor-contaminated domain-A pairs are handled in a dedicated sensitivity script (floor_sensitivity.py): in one the random arm's promoted rule is INFEASIBLE (+10⁹ margin), in the other the LLM arm's is (−10⁹), so they cancel in the raw mean while dominating the SD at 1.4×10⁸, and they contribute zero to the signed-rank statistic because S+ = S− within each floor pair.

## Results & Highlights

- **The manipulation is real but unevenly strong.** On the 119-mutant domain-A battery, mutation score rises 0.000 [0.000, 0.031] → 0.0168 [0.005, 0.059] → 0.1092 [0.065, 0.178] across S−/S0/S+, i.e. S0 kills 2 of 119 mutants and S+ kills 13 — a 6.5× increase with non-overlapping Wilson CIs. On the 310-mutant domain-D battery the ordering is strict but the separation is negligible: 0.3484 → 0.3548 → 0.3613, adjacent tiers differing by 2 of 310 mutants (0.6 pp) with overlapping CIs. The paper's own reading — "strictly ordered but weakly separated" — means domain A carries the manipulation claim.
- **The main hypothesis fails in both domains.** Paired holdout margins are +0.28/+0.28/+0.38 in A and +0.10/+0.10/+0.06 in D across S−/S0/S+. The frozen S+ vs S− one-sided test gives p=0.1115 in A and p=0.9994 in D — in D the p-value implies p≈0.0006 for the *reverse* ordering. The post-hoc margin contrast is +0.10 (95% CI [−0.10, 0.31], p=0.2229) in A and −0.040 (95% CI [−0.07,−0.01], p=0.0013) in D, so strict verification significantly *reduced* the LLM advantage in domain D.
- **The LLM advantage itself is real in D and invisible in A.** Per-tier post-hoc one-sided tests in D are significant at every tier (S− p<0.0001, S0 p<0.0001, S+ p=0.011), with floor-free margins +0.102 [+0.060, 0.145], +0.102 [+0.061, 0.145] and +0.062 [+0.024, 0.100] on n=97 valid pairs. In A the margins are positive but not significant (+0.367/+0.367/+0.469 on n=98 floor-free pairs, p=0.31/0.31/0.24) with CIs roughly ±1.5 wide — a CI spanning three units around a 0.37 effect, so A is closer to "cannot tell" than to "no effect".
- **S− and S0 are the same gate in practice.** Identical promotions in 100/100 domain-A pairs and 99/100 domain-D pairs. All the action sits in S+ vs S0: 14 divergent pairs in A (S+ wins 10, mean holdout difference +0.714) versus 23 in D (S+ wins 1, mean −0.066). This is why the strict order is untestable: Δ(S0) = Δ(S−) identically, so the frozen criterion could never have been satisfied in A regardless of the data.
- **The random pool is competitive with the LLM pool.** At S0 the union-pool winner comes from the random arm in 48% of A pairs and 46% of D pairs; under S+ in D it is 60%. Merging pools would add +1.52 domain-A holdout units (n=100, p=0.0002; +1.44 at S+, p=0.0006), while the same derivation in D is null-to-negative (−0.036/−0.041/−0.035 across tiers, p=0.27/0.32/0.07). For scale: +1.52 is four to five times the entire LLM-over-random margin in A (+0.28 to +0.38).
- **A concrete false-negative mechanism in domain D.** In one pair the LLM session passed 5 of 20 proposals under S0 and its best scored 0.524 on holdout; the S+ worst-case channel stress test killed two of the five and forced promotion of the second-best, which violated a constraint on the holdout instance and scored 0.0 — a −0.52 swing produced purely by the stress test rejecting the candidate that would have generalized.
- **The value of strictness crosses zero with deployment tension.** Re-scoring the first 50 LLM-arm pairs on 36 fresh instances spanning a utilization gradient gives J(S+)−J(S−) of −0.033 at 4% utilization, +0.015/+0.027 at 12%, +0.067/+0.067 at 22–23% and +0.036/+0.035 at 26–27% — the same shape on both extension days. Strict verification hurts when constraints are slack and pays only at medium utilization.

## Authors & Credibility

Three authors from the Shenyang Institute of Technology (Shenyang, China): Peter Chen and Ni Zhang co-first, Siyuan Zhang corresponding and the implementer of the pipeline. The paper is formatted for *R. Soc. Open Sci.*, declares no specific funding and no competing interests, and discloses that an LLM assistant was used for language editing. The reference base is appropriate and current — The AI Scientist, T-Drive, the Qwen2.5 report, Stroebl et al. on resampling limits, Brown et al. on repeated sampling, Kapoor & Narayanan on leakage — and the paper positions itself correctly against each rather than overclaiming novelty. Credibility here rests almost entirely on protocol transparency rather than institutional track record: the Zenodo archive (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23308866) contains the protocol v1.0–v1.2 with changelogs, per-pair JSONL ledgers, the mutation batteries, the frozen verifiers and six named analysis scripts (analyze_tier1.py, final_numbers.py, descriptive_stats.py, mix_pool_analysis.py, floor_sensitivity.py, tension_gradient.py), and the authors state that no number is hand-computed. The domain-A instance pool is described as coming from "prior suture experiments of this program", which suggests a manufacturing-scheduling applied group rather than an ML-evaluation lab — consistent with the unusually clinical style of the protocol.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty: modest but real, and correctly scoped.** Negative results about verifier strength are not surprising in the abstract — Stroebl et al. (2026) already show resampling against imperfect verifiers is capped by false positives, and reward-hacking folklore says the same. What is new is the *causal isolation*: same proposal pool, same scoring function, three frozen gates, pre-registered. The coverage-gap measurement (random beats LLM 48% of the time) is the more genuinely novel number, and it is also the one most likely to be an artifact of the proposer.

**The central falsification is partly guaranteed by construction.** Because S0 ≡ S− in 100/100 domain-A pairs, Δ(S0) = Δ(S−) exactly, so the frozen strict order Δ(S+) > Δ(S0) > Δ(S−) was mathematically unsatisfiable before a single data point was collected. The paper deserves credit for reporting this as a "criterion-reachability lesson" rather than burying it, but the abstract and title still present E1-D1's failure as an empirical discovery ("the manipulation binds — yet the paired advantage does not scale with it"), when in domain A the only contrast with any information content is S+ vs S− (p=0.1115). A reader who takes "pre-registered falsification" at face value is over-crediting the design.

**Domain A cannot distinguish "no effect" from "no power".** The floor-free margins are +0.367 with 95% CI [−1.15, 1.85]. The paper reports a minimum detectable effect of ≈0.26 at n=100, which is technically true for a signed-rank test on the observed difference distribution, but the mean's CI is three units wide because a handful of large random wins dominate it. Calling A a clean null while the interval spans ±1.5 on a metric whose total LLM-vs-random margin is 0.28 is generous. The honest summary of A is "underpowered and degenerate", not "falsified".

**The strongest causal claim comes from the weakest manipulation.** Domain D is where strict verification significantly *hurts* (−0.040, p=0.0013) — and domain D is also where the three tiers differ by 2 of 310 mutants with overlapping Wilson CIs. The authors flag this ("we rely on the domain-A battery... as the primary manipulation evidence"), but it leaves an awkward joint: the manipulation is clean where the effect is absent, and the effect is present where the manipulation is weak. That is not fatal — the tiers are still strictly ordered — but it means the paper's most quotable claim rests on the least convincingly separated treatment.

**The proposer is a 0.49B model, and the headline does not say so.** Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct with a Chinese prompt template and a 220-token cap is about as weak a proposer as one can build. The authors defend this as deliberate ("so that any conclusion applies to the pipeline structure rather than to frontier-model capability"), but the paper's own mechanisms are proposer-dependent: session-level all-feasible/all-infeasible correlation and a 48% random-win rate are exactly what one expects from a model that barely understands the rule interface. A 7B–70B proposer might well produce mixed-feasibility sessions (restoring the S0 gate) and a much smaller coverage gap. The title's unqualified "Verifier strength does not buy discovery quality" generalizes far beyond the evidence; the paper's body is more careful than its title.

**The +1.52 union-pool number needs a confidence interval and a floor audit.** It is reported with a p-value but no CI, at n=100, in a domain where two pairs carry ±10⁹ floor terms and the floor-free sensitivity analysis uses n=98. The text does not say how the union derivation handles those two pairs, and the discrepancy between n=100 here and n=98 in the floor analysis is exactly the kind of detail that matters at 10⁹ scale. The magnitude is also suspicious in a specific way: a mean gain of +1.52 from a 48% win rate implies average wins of ~3.2 units, an order of magnitude above the +0.28 LLM-over-random margin — i.e. the estimate is driven by a heavy right tail, and a mean is a poor summary of it. The derivation lives in mix_pool_analysis.py, not in the text.

**The tension gradient is six points and no error bars.** The −0.033 → +0.067 → +0.035 pattern comes from 50 pairs re-scored on 36 fresh instances from two extension days, with no CIs and no test, and with task density (tpv 1/2/3) varying jointly with utilization — a confound the authors acknowledge. "Peaking at medium utilization" is an eyeball fit to six numbers, and the decline on the right branch may be composition rather than tension.

**No cost accounting.** S+ adds a worst-case stress test per candidate; the paper never reports its compute or wall-clock cost relative to S0. A strict gate that buys no quality *and* costs more is a stronger result than "buys no quality", and the cost side is missing.

**The only baseline is random.** For the stated causal question this is the right control — any other proposer would confound pool composition with verification strength — and the paper says so. But it means the claim "LLM proposals carry a real paired advantage" is measured against uniform random weight vectors, a very low bar, and nothing here speaks to absolute rule quality versus human-designed dispatch rules or classical heuristics (SPT, EDD, WSPT), which are the actual competitors in job-shop scheduling.

## Strengths

- **The pre-registration is done properly, not decoratively.** Criteria frozen at v1.0 before main-domain data, changelogs disclosing every deviation (including a manipulation-check population disclosure at v1.2 made before unblinding), a manipulation-check gate that must pass before the main experiment counts, and an explicit negative clause committing to publish failure.
- **The separation logic is genuinely clean.** Most negative results in this space are vulnerable to "your treatment was too weak". Here the mutation battery forecloses that objection, and the authors make the logical structure explicit rather than leaving it implicit.
- **Statistical honesty is above the norm for this literature.** Floor sensitivity with an exact accounting of why the mean moves (+0.28 → +0.367) while the p-value does not (0.1115 unchanged, because the floor pairs contribute zero to the signed-rank statistic); minimum detectable effects reported per domain; a clear separation between frozen criteria and post-hoc analyses; the observation that the −0.040 in D exceeds the domain's MDE of 0.039, so the negative association is detected rather than missed.
- **The degeneracy analysis is the paper's best idea.** Recognizing that S− ≡ S0 because an executing-but-infeasible rule scores −10⁹ and can never be a session maximum is a crisp, checkable mechanism, and it generalizes: any pipeline whose intermediate gate sits between "runs" and "runs well" will have this problem.
- **Reproducibility is unusually complete** — protocol, ledgers, verifiers, mutation batteries and six analysis scripts archived, CPU-only execution, no proprietary data.
- **The domain-D failure case is memorable and mechanistically specific**: a stress test that killed a candidate scoring 0.524 and forced a 0.0. That single example explains the sign of the D result better than any aggregate.

## Limitations

- One proposer model (Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct), 0.49B parameters, CPU-only. Every mechanism the paper identifies is potentially a small-model artifact.
- Two domains, both simulated from real data; domain A's pool is 154 unique orders after deduplication, drawn as 60/60 train/holdout splits, so pairs are strongly correlated by construction and the effective sample size is well below 100 (the authors say so).
- The domain-D mutation battery separates tiers by only 2 of 310 mutants, so the manipulation claim leans on domain A while the negative claim leans on domain D.
- The tension gradient varies task density jointly with utilization (composition confound on the declining branch) and is computed on 50 pairs with no CIs.
- The union-pool analysis is exploratory and derived from already-evaluated arm promotions rather than run as a pre-registered intervention; the D-domain version is null-to-negative, so the "pool composition is the lever" conclusion is domain-structured rather than general.
- The random arm spans only the frozen uniform weight family, so the coverage-gap reading does not extend to other proposal distributions.
- The v1.0 strict-order criterion was structurally unsatisfiable under S0 ≡ S−; the paper reports this as a design lesson, but it also means the pre-registered main test could not have succeeded.
- No comparison to human-designed or classical heuristics; no cost accounting for S+; scale is deliberately limited by commodity hardware.

## Relevance

（对应"自 evolving agents / 自动化科研"方向）这篇是 AI-scientist 流水线里"验证器"那一半的可靠性结果：在提议者固定的条件下，加约束、加压力测试并不能提升发现质量，真正起作用的是提议池的组成——对任何在搭 proposer–verifier 发现回路的人都是一条反直觉但可操作的工程结论。它同时是一个"闸门看起来在工作、下游却什么都没发生"的典型案例（变异电池通过、发现质量不变），与流水线静默失效的主题高度同构。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the coverage gap survive a stronger proposer? The 48% random-win rate and the all-feasible/all-infeasible session pattern are both plausible signatures of a 0.49B model; a 7B–70B proposer with the same protocol would test whether "pool composition is the lever" is a property of pipelines or of this particular proposer.
2. Why is S0 ≡ S− mechanistically rather than just empirically? The −10⁹ floor argument explains domain A exactly, but domain D has no such floor and still shows 99/100 identical promotions — what makes D's proposals similarly bimodal?
3. How exactly is the +1.52 union estimate computed in the presence of the two ±10⁹ floor pairs, and what is its bootstrap CI? Is the mean driven by a few large random wins, and would a median or a trimmed mean change the conclusion?
4. Would a *soft* verifier — one that ranks or penalizes continuously instead of passing/failing — convert verification strength into quality? All three tiers here are hard gates, which may be the real reason intermediate strictness has no effect.
5. Does the tension gradient replicate when utilization is manipulated independently of tasks-per-vehicle, as the pre-registered E2 proposes? The current six-point curve is consistent with the story but does not establish it.
6. What does S+ cost? A per-candidate worst-case stress test is not free, and a strict gate that buys nothing while costing more is a different (and stronger) engineering conclusion than the one currently stated.
