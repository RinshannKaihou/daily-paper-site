> **一句话总结：** 作者把 ROME/MEMIT/AlphaEdit 的闭式权重更新拆成"键空间传播 α + 平行残差失配 (γ−α) + 正交残差失配 ‖r₂⊥‖/‖r₁‖"三项，在 Llama-3.1-8B 与 Gemma-3-4B、五种语言、zsRE/CounterFact 上证明：跨语言编辑失败的主因不是键传不过去，而是目标语言的更新方向有约 0.90 落在源语言残差张成空间之外（即便逐事实用 oracle 选层也只降到 0.774–0.801）；且仅用源残差预测目标残差的 R² ≤ 0.036（η̂ = 0.964–0.992），构成源侧后验修正的下界；最后用对比对齐把 α 从 0.212 拉到 0.858（en→de），efficacy 却从 72.62 掉到 61.17，说明"键空间开门"既不充分也不免费。

> **TL;DR:** The paper derives an exact decomposition of the cross-lingual edit gap for associative-memory editors (ROME, MEMIT, AlphaEdit) into a key-space propagation scalar α, a parallel residual mismatch (γ−α), and an orthogonal residual mismatch ‖r₂⊥‖/‖r₁‖, then measures each term on Llama-3.1-8B and Gemma-3-4B over English→{German, Hindi, Urdu, Chinese} on multilingual zsRE and CounterFact. Key-space propagation collapses with typological distance (ROME α peaks at ≈0.4 for German around layer 12 but stays <0.08 for Hindi/Urdu at every layer), and the residual gap is dominated by the orthogonal component (≈0.90 of total error at layers 16–20). A per-fact oracle layer choice still leaves the orthogonal ratio at 0.774–0.801. A contrastive alignment at layers 16–25 raises α from 0.212→0.858 (en→de), 0.025→0.618 (en→hi), 0.015→0.517 (en→ur), 0.086→0.579 (en→zh), yet CounterFact efficacy moves 72.62→61.17 for German, 49.61→50.19 for Hindi, 46.70→59.22 for Urdu, 33.59→39.51 for Chinese — i.e., key-space alignment is necessary but not sufficient, and costs 4–8 XNLI points. Read it if you care about where multilingual facts actually live, or if you are tempted to believe "edit once, propagate everywhere."

## 核心贡献

- **精确分解，而非近似归因**：把"跨语言编辑为什么失效"写成可逐项测量的闭式恒等式。ROME 的值差 g₁,₂ = r₂ − α₁,₂r₁，归一化后 ‖g‖²/‖r₁‖² = (γ−α₁,₂)² + ‖r₂⊥‖²/‖r₁‖² 是严格等式（Proposition 2），不是上界也不是拟合。键空间项 α 与值空间的两项失配因此可以被分开归责——这是全文最有价值的部分。
- **正交失配是主瓶颈，且不是"选错层"的假象**：在语义谷（16–20 层）处正交分量占相对总误差约 0.90。作者额外做了逐事实 oracle 选层（82 条事实，层 ∈ {4,8,…,28}，用目标语言信息选最优层，因此是乐观估计），正交比只下降 0.090–0.138（配对 t 检验 p < 10⁻¹⁰），仍停在 0.774（de）/0.801（hi）/0.775（ur）/0.795（zh）；最优层在事实间分布极散（Hindi 从第 4 层到第 24 层，最常被选的层只对 82 条中的 27 条最优）。这一条把"只是层没选好"的解释堵死了。
- **源侧后验修正存在可证下界**：把修正写成 f(r₁)，用条件期望最优性与全方差分解得到 E‖r₂ − α₁,₂f(r₁)‖² ≥ η(r₂|r₁,α)·Var(r₂)。实测三种归纳偏置完全不同的估计器（Ridge、两层 MLP、k-NN）在 20 层给出的 R² 分别是 0.0363/0.0322/0.0359（en→de）、0.0114/0.0085/0.0123（en→hi）、0.0049/0.0079/0.0054（en→ur）、0.0281/0.0244/0.0227（en→zh），即 η̂ = 0.964–0.992。三者收敛这一点比单个估计器更有说服力。
- **批量编辑放大瓶颈**：MEMIT 的批量对齐矩阵 A₁,₂ 上定义干扰比 IR = ‖off-diag‖_F/‖diag‖_F，Indic 语言冲到 8–20，德语只有 4–5。也就是说，即使少量跨语言信号侥幸穿过 α 门，也会被同批次其他事实的非对角泄漏污染。
- **自己拆自己的台：对齐实验是负结果**：对比对齐（16–25 层，InfoNCE + 语言建模正则）把 α 提高 3–20 倍，但正交失配在 hi/ur 上反而变差（0.901→0.952、0.882→0.941），efficacy 只在 ur/zh 上提升，德语下降 11.5 个点，XNLI 全线掉 4–8 点。作者没有把这条包装成"我们的方法有效"，而是用它论证"键空间对齐既不充分也有代价"——这是本文可信度的主要来源。
- **一个可复用的诊断工具箱**：α（键空间传播）、γ（值空间投影系数）、‖r₂⊥‖/‖r₁‖（正交比）、IR（批量干扰比）、Γ 与 Σ（批量投影与正交范数），都是可以直接搬到别的编辑器或别的语言对上测量的量。

## Key Contributions

- **An exact, not approximate, decomposition of the cross-lingual transfer gap.** For ROME the gap is g₁,₂ = r₂ − α₁,₂r₁ with α₁,₂ = (k₁*)ᵀC₁⁻¹k₂*/(k₁*)ᵀC₁⁻¹k₁*; splitting r₂ = γr₁ + r₂⊥ makes ‖g‖²/‖r₁‖² = (γ−α)² + ‖r₂⊥‖²/‖r₁‖² hold identically. MEMIT and AlphaEdit generalize it to batch alignment matrices A₁,₂ = K₁ᵀ(C₁+K₁K₁ᵀ)⁻¹K₂ and A₁,₂ᴬᴱ = K₁ᵀ(C₁+K₁K₁ᵀ)⁻¹(P⊥K₂). Because the editors are closed-form, the decomposition is analytic rather than post-hoc.
- **A Rayleigh-quotient bound that explains the typological ordering.** After whitening k̃ = C₁^(−1/2)k, α becomes a Rayleigh quotient of the transfer matrix M₁,₂ = C₁^(−1/2)A₁,₂C₁^(1/2), bounded by the eigenvalues of its symmetric part. Perfect propagation (α = 1) requires A₁,₂ = I; divergence of C₁ and C₂ spreads the spectrum and suppresses α. The empirical ordering de ≫ zh > hi ≳ ur tracks both the normalized Frobenius divergence d_F(C_en, C_j) and the principal angles of the bottom eigenspaces (>75° for distant pairs).
- **Orthogonal mismatch as the dominant term, validated against the obvious confound.** At the "semantic valley" (layers 16–20) the orthogonal component carries ≈0.90 of the relative gap. The per-fact oracle-layer analysis (82 facts) removes the "you edited the wrong layer" objection: the mismatch falls by only 0.09–0.14 and remains 0.77–0.80, with the optimal layer varying from layer 4 to layer 24 across facts.
- **A conditional lower bound for source-only correction.** Framing correction as learning f with α₁,₂f(r₁) ≈ r₂, the paper shows the residual error is floored by the conditional variance of r₂ given (r₁, α₁,₂). Empirically R² ≤ 0.036 across three estimator families at layer 20 (≤0.09 at every layer), so ~96–99% of target-residual variance is invisible to the source residual. The authors are explicit that predictors using keys, prompts, or language identity are *not* covered by the bound.
- **A diagnostic intervention that separates the two bottlenecks experimentally.** Contrastive alignment at layers 16–25 (InfoNCE over MLP hidden states at the last subject token, τ = 0.07, plus Wikipedia LM regularization) raises α by 3–20× while leaving the value-space mismatch — and often the downstream efficacy — unchanged, which is precisely the asymmetry the decomposition predicts.
- **A batch-editing failure mode specific to MEMIT.** The interference ratio IR shows that cross-fact leakage, not just weak per-fact propagation, degrades Indic-language transfer (IR 8–20 vs 4–5 for German), and AlphaEdit's null-space projection P⊥ does nothing about it because P⊥ acts on keys, not on value-space residuals.

## Methodology

**Setup.** Two models (Llama-3.1-8B, 32 layers; Gemma-3-4B, 34 layers), five languages (en, de, hi, ur, zh), two benchmarks (multilingual zsRE and CounterFact, taken from BMIKE-53's parallel versions). English is always the edit language for cross-lingual runs. All experiments on a single NVIDIA H200 NVL (141 GB).

**Editors as analytical objects.** The paper restricts itself to closed-form associative-memory editors precisely because their updates can be dissected: ROME's rank-1 ΔW = r₁k₁*ᵀC⁻¹/(k₁*ᵀC⁻¹k₁*), MEMIT's batched ΔW = R₁K₁ᵀ(C+K₁K₁ᵀ)⁻¹, and AlphaEdit's null-space-constrained ΔW = ΔW_MEMIT P⊥. Hypernetworks and neuron-masking methods are excluded by construction, which the authors state up front as a scope limit.

**Covariance estimation.** C is the uncentered second moment of post-nonlinearity MLP keys over ~400,000 tokens from 1,134 cleaned English Wikipedia documents, replicated per target language by machine-translating the same corpus with `google/translategemma-4b-it`. This matters: C₁ enters both the ROME/MEMIT solution and the AlphaEdit null-space projection, so the geometry being measured is partly the geometry of the translated corpus.

**Layer selection.** ROME's edit layer maximizes English ΔNLL over nine candidate layers (layer 20 for Llama, layer 32 for Gemma). MEMIT and AlphaEdit pick a five-layer window that maximizes ΔNLL *averaged over the four non-English target languages* (Llama MEMIT [24–28], AlphaEdit [16–20]; Gemma MEMIT [12–16], AlphaEdit [28–32]). Covariance Tikhonov λ = 1e−5 for ROME, 1.5e4 for MEMIT, L2 = 10 and null-space eigenvalue threshold 0.02 for AlphaEdit, batch size n = 50.

**Diagnostics.** Four quantities are extracted per layer and language: the key-space propagation scalar α (or its batch trace ᾱ_batch), the value-space projection coefficient γ = (r₂·r₁)/‖r₁‖², the normalized orthogonal residual ‖r₂⊥‖/‖r₁‖, and for batch methods the interference ratio IR plus the batch scalar projection Γ and orthogonal norm Σ.

**Behavioral mapping.** The internal gap is connected to behavior through a first-order Taylor expansion of the logit shift, Δy_gap ≈ W_U J_h g₁,₂, giving a transfer penalty P := ΔNLL_self − ΔNLL_en that is upper-bounded (not lower-bounded) by √2‖W_U‖₂‖g₁,₂‖ under J_h ≈ I. The paper is careful to call this an upper bound and to test the gap–penalty link empirically instead (Spearman ρ over layers 12–24).

**Predictability estimators.** Target residuals are PCA-reduced per language (up to min(200, rank) components, evaluated at p ∈ {25, 50, 100}, best R² reported), then predicted from source residuals by Ridge (λ = 0.01·tr(Z₁Z₁ᵀ)/p), a two-layer MLP (h = 2p, Adam 3e−4, early stopping, 3 seeds), and k-NN. Two controls guard against spurious structure: shuffled-pair refits (R² ≈ 0) and a PCA reconstruction ceiling that bounds what any predictor in the reduced space could achieve.

**Intervention.** Supervised contrastive fine-tuning on `up_proj`/`down_proj` at layers 16–25, L_total = L_contrast + βL_LM, InfoNCE with cosine similarity and τ = 0.07, batch 128, 10 epochs, learning rate 1e−4, β ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 2.0}, Wikipedia LM regularization at 8×256 tokens, perplexity degradation tolerance 5%. Early stopping is driven by per-epoch perplexity monitoring, which turns out to be the binding constraint: Llama is usable only for epochs 1–2, Gemma only for epochs 1–3 before Urdu perplexity explodes from ~15 to nearly 300.

## Results & Highlights

- **The transfer gap is large and method-dependent (Efficacy, Llama-3.1-8B, CounterFact, English edit).** AlphaEdit goes 96.99% (en→en) → 89.61% (en→de) → 58.74% (hi) → 54.08% (ur) → 57.86% (zh), i.e. a 39.1-point drop from native to Hindi. ROME goes 50.78% → 72.62% (de) → 49.61% (hi) → 46.70% (ur) → 33.59% (zh), a 17.2-point native-to-Chinese drop. On zsRE, ROME is 76.12% (en) → 82.77% (de) → 63.09% (hi) → 61.47% (ur) → 51.29% (zh). MEMIT is broken across the board in this batch setting: 5.15% en→en and 0.87% en→zh on CounterFact, 27.37%/2.20% on zsRE.
- **Key-space propagation collapses with typological distance (α, Llama-3.1-8B, layer sweep 4–28).** For ROME, German peaks at α ≈ 0.4 near layer 12, Chinese sits below it, and Hindi/Urdu stay under 0.08 at *every* layer — roughly a 5× gap against German's peak. For MEMIT the batch mean ᾱ never exceeds ≈0.0075, and for AlphaEdit it tops out near 0.2, so the ordering de ≫ zh > hi ≳ ur holds across all three editors rather than being a ROME artifact.
- **The value-space gap is dominated by the orthogonal term (relative gap decomposition, ROME, Llama-3.1-8B).** At the layers 16–20 minimum, ‖r₂⊥‖/‖r₁‖ accounts for ≈0.90 of ‖g₁,₂‖/‖r₁‖; the parallel mismatch (γ−α) is visible only for German, where α is large enough for magnitude mismatch to matter. Fixed layer 20 gives orthogonal ratios of 0.864 (de), 0.895 (hi), 0.872 (ur), 0.933 (zh); a per-fact oracle layer choice improves these to 0.774/0.801/0.775/0.795 — an improvement of 0.090–0.138 (paired t-test, all p < 10⁻¹⁰) that still leaves the mismatch dominant.
- **Source residuals barely predict target residuals (R², Llama-3.1-8B, layer 20).** Ridge / MLP / k-NN give 0.0363 / 0.0322 / 0.0359 for en→de, 0.0114 / 0.0085 / 0.0123 for en→hi, 0.0049 / 0.0079 / 0.0054 for en→ur, 0.0281 / 0.0244 / 0.0227 for en→zh, i.e. η̂ = 0.9637, 0.9877, 0.9921, 0.9719. Three estimators with completely different inductive biases converging on R² ≤ 0.04 is the strongest evidence in the paper; the shuffled-pair control returns R² ≈ 0, so the small signal is real but tiny.
- **Contrastive alignment opens the key-space gate but does not fix transfer (Llama-3.1-8B, ROME, CounterFact, layer 20).** α rises from 0.212→0.858 (de), 0.025→0.618 (hi), 0.015→0.517 (ur), 0.086→0.579 (zh). Efficacy moves 72.62→61.17 (de, −11.45), 49.61→50.19 (hi, +0.58), 46.70→59.22 (ur, +12.52), 33.59→39.51 (zh, +5.92). The orthogonal ratio improves only for German (0.913→0.861) and worsens for Hindi (0.901→0.952) and Urdu (0.882→0.941). Downstream XNLI drops in every language: en 0.50→0.45, de 0.48→0.43, hi 0.47→0.41, ur 0.45→0.37, zh 0.42→0.38.
- **Batch editing compounds the failure (Interference Ratio, MEMIT).** IR = ‖off-diag(A₁,₂)‖_F/‖diag(A₁,₂)‖_F reaches 8–20 for Indic targets versus 4–5 for German, so even the surviving cross-lingual signal is contaminated by other facts in the same batch. AlphaEdit's null-space projection does not help, because P⊥ projects keys and the orthogonal mismatch lives entirely in the value space.
- **The mismatch is not purely cross-lingual (21 facts with bijective formulations, Llama-3.1-8B).** An English *inversion* of the same fact ("Y is the capital of X" instead of "X's capital is Y") already raises the orthogonal ratio from 0.718 to 0.829 (+0.111, p = 4.9e−3); Chinese translation reaches 1.031 (+0.313). Pooling Hindi/Urdu/Chinese, the mean increase over the English paraphrase is +0.246 (Wilcoxon p = 1.9e−6, bootstrap 95% CI [+0.177, +0.322]) — a geometric counterpart of the symmetry failure Niu et al. reported.
- **The gap–behavior link is real but weak (Spearman ρ, layers 12–24, p < 10⁻⁴).** ρ = 0.221 (en→de), 0.198 (en→ur), 0.119 (en→hi), 0.114 (en→zh). The authors report these honestly as "statistically reliable but modest" and explicitly decline to claim a lower bound on the transfer penalty.

## Critical Assessment

**Genuine strengths first.** The decomposition is exact and falsifiable, and the authors actually go looking for the ways it could be wrong: the per-fact oracle-layer analysis (82 facts, layers 4–28) is exactly the ablation a skeptical reviewer would demand, and it survives. The three-estimator convergence for the predictability barrier, with shuffled-pair and PCA-ceiling controls, is more careful than most "we fit a probe and it didn't work" claims. The negative alignment result is the paper's best scientific moment: α goes up by 3–20× and efficacy goes *down* for German, which is a much stronger statement about the sufficiency of representation alignment than another positive-result paper could make.

**But several things are weaker than the framing suggests.**

- **MEMIT is not a working baseline in this configuration, so its numbers cannot support geometric claims.** Efficacy of 27.37% (zsRE en→en) and 5.15% (CounterFact en→en) means the editor fails natively; Table 6 shows ΔNLL_margin of −8.91 for en→en on CounterFact. The authors acknowledge this in one parenthetical ("except for MEMIT, which is weak even in English in our batch setting"), but MEMIT still supplies the batch-interference and IR results (8–20 for Indic) that are presented as evidence about cross-lingual geometry. With batch size n = 50 at layers 24–28 for Llama, this looks like a mis-tuned MEMIT rather than a discovery about multilingual transfer. The IR numbers would be far more convincing if the English-only edit succeeded at, say, 90%.
- **The efficacy metric is not comparable across languages, and the paper's own Table 1 shows it.** ROME on zsRE scores *higher* when editing in English and evaluating in German (82.77%) than when editing and evaluating in English (76.12%); on CounterFact the same comparison is 72.62% vs 50.78%, a 21.8-point "transfer gain." Efficacy is defined as P(target) > P(original), so in a language where the model is weaker the original answer is easier to beat — the bar moves. That makes distant-language transfer look *better* than it is (the gap is understated), and it makes the German numbers uninterpretable as evidence of "related-language transfer succeeds." The paper's typological narrative (de ≫ zh > hi ≳ ur) is read off a metric with exactly this confound, and no per-language calibration or normalized variant is reported.
- **Hyperparameter selection leaks the target languages.** Table 5's caption states that MEMIT and AlphaEdit windows "maximize the score averaged over the four non-English languages." Those are the same four languages used to report the transfer gap. ROME's layer is chosen on English, which is clean; the other two are not. This inflates the cross-lingual numbers for the batch editors and undermines using them as the "unconstrained vs constrained" comparison.
- **The alignment experiment compares only layer 20, but alignment moves the optimal layer.** Table 4 is a layer-20-only before/after. Table 13 shows the aligned Llama ROME model at layer 16 reaches 56.70% English and 59.32% Urdu efficacy versus 39.71% and 36.21% for the base model — large gains that vanish from the narrative because the comparison is pinned to layer 20. The paper does not re-sweep the edit layer after alignment, so "alignment doesn't consistently help" may be an artifact of holding the layer fixed across a representation change that was explicitly designed to alter layer geometry. This is the single most consequential missing experiment.
- **The gap–behavior correlation is very weak, and the text over-reads it.** Spearman ρ of 0.114–0.221 means 1–5% of variance. The paper calls the relationship "behaviorally meaningful" and then, correctly, declines to claim a lower bound. Those two statements sit uneasily; the honest summary is that the internal geometry predicts behavior weakly at the population level, which is consistent with the decomposition but does not validate it as a per-fact diagnostic.
- **The "private fraction" may be partly a sample-size artifact.** R² is estimated on the intersection of case IDs shared across five languages (the code requires ≥8 shared cases and the paper never states N for this analysis), in a 4096-dimensional target space reduced to ≤100 PCA components, with the best of three dimensions reported. Three estimators converging is reassuring, but all three fail for the same reason in this regime — too few facts for the dimensionality — and no learning curve (R² vs N) is reported. A 0.96 private fraction on a few hundred facts is not the same claim as a 0.96 private fraction in the population, and the paper's Proposition 4 is a population statement.
- **The covariance corpus is translationese.** C for each target language is built by machine-translating 1,134 English Wikipedia documents with `google/translategemma-4b-it`. Both the Frobenius divergence d_F(C_en, C_j) and AlphaEdit's null-space projection P⊥ are computed from these matrices, so part of the measured "language anisotropy" is the anisotropy of translated text. A native-language corpus (or a translation-robustness check) would separate the two, and it is not run.
- **The linear key-shift assumption carries the theory.** Proposition 1's Rayleigh bound requires k₂* = A₁,₂k₁*. The paper defends this as "locally linear regimes" for semantically equivalent queries and offers d_F as an assumption-free proxy, but never tests the assumption directly (e.g., by measuring the residual of a fitted linear map, or by comparing α predicted from A₁,₂ against α measured directly). If the map is strongly nonlinear for distant languages, the bound is decorative rather than explanatory.
- **No positive control.** The claim "alignment alone is insufficient" would land harder against a method that *does* transfer cross-lingually — MEMLA, LU-LAFNs, Edit Once Update Everywhere, or simply multilingual joint editing. None is run. The paper is diagnostic and says so, but a reader wanting to know what to do instead gets no comparison point.
- **Smaller issues.** The "semantic valley" is asserted from a minimum in the relative-gap curve rather than defined or tested. Gemma's ROME edit layer is 32 of 34, unusually late, and several Gemma cells in Table 6 are negative even on the diagonal (MEMIT en→en −8.91, zsRE −0.97), again indicating non-functional configurations rather than geometry. Only two models in the 4B–8B range are tested, and the geometry of 70B-class models is untested.

None of this invalidates the central finding — the orthogonal residual mismatch is large, robust to layer choice, and not fixable by key-space alignment, and that conclusion rests on an exact identity plus a well-controlled oracle analysis. But the paper's *quantitative* claims about which editor transfers how much are considerably shakier than its *structural* claim, and the two are presented at similar confidence.

## Strengths

- An exact algebraic decomposition rather than a correlational story; each term is measurable and each claim is falsifiable.
- The per-fact oracle-layer experiment pre-empts the most obvious objection and survives it (0.774–0.801 orthogonal ratio after optimistic layer selection).
- Three estimator families with shuffled-pair and PCA-ceiling controls for the predictability barrier — unusually careful for this literature.
- An honest negative result on the authors' own intervention, reported with the mechanism (α up, r⊥ unchanged) that explains why.
- Broad coverage for the claim: 2 models, 3 editors, 5 languages, 2 datasets, plus layer sweeps, batch analysis, target-length analysis, and a logically-equivalent-formulation control.
- Explicit scoping of every proposition, including the admission that Proposition 3 is only an upper bound and Proposition 4 only covers f(r₁).
- Released code, full hyperparameter table, and a clearly described covariance corpus — the analysis is reproducible in principle.

## Limitations

- Restricted to associative-memory editors (ROME/MEMIT/AlphaEdit); hypernetwork, adapter, and neuron-masking editors are out of scope by construction, and the conclusions may not transfer to them.
- Short factual answers only; long-form generation, where factual drift and discourse consistency matter, is untested and the authors say so.
- Two models in the 4B–8B range; scaling behavior unknown.
- The linear key-shift assumption and first-order Jacobian approximation (J_h ≈ I) underpin the theory; neither is directly validated.
- The predictability bound is conditional on restricting the correction to f(r₁); corrections using keys, prompts, or language identity are explicitly outside it, which limits how strongly "post-hoc correction is impossible" can be read.
- The alignment intervention costs 4–8 XNLI points and has a training window of 1–2 epochs (Llama) or 1–3 (Gemma) before perplexity explodes — a fragile intervention, not a deployable fix.
- Efficacy is an unnormalized threshold metric with a per-language difficulty confound, and MEMIT's configuration is not functional even natively.
- No native-language covariance corpus, so translationese is entangled with the measured anisotropy.
- No comparison against editors designed for cross-lingual transfer, so the practical recommendation space is left empty.

## Relevance

这篇直接落在"LLM 隐状态与可解释性"这条线上：它把"多语言事实存在哪儿"这个老问题变成了可以逐层测量的几何量（α、γ、‖r₂⊥‖/‖r₁‖），并且用逐事实 oracle 选层排除了"只是层没选对"的解释——对关心表征几何与知识定位的人来说，这套分解和诊断量本身比结论更可复用。对做模型编辑或可靠性的人来说，它给了一个明确的负面边界：只改源语言权重、指望跨语言自动传播，在结构上就是被残差方向卡住的，别把它当成工程调参问题。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. If alignment is re-swept over edit layers (rather than pinned to layer 20), does the aligned model's efficacy advantage at layer 16 (56.70% en, 59.32% ur vs 39.71%/36.21% base) survive a fair comparison? This is the experiment that would decide whether alignment "doesn't help" or merely "moves the optimum."
2. What does the orthogonal mismatch look like in a 70B-class model? If the private fraction η drops with scale, the barrier is an artifact of capacity; if it stays at 0.96, it is structural.
3. Can the orthogonal component be predicted from the *key* k₂* (which is observable at test time) rather than from r₁? The bound explicitly excludes this, and a k₂*-conditioned correction is the obvious next attack.
4. Why does an English inversion of the same fact require a residual 0.111 further from the paraphrase's direction (0.718 → 0.829)? Is the "residual direction" a property of the relation, the surface form, or the prompt template — and does that decomposition predict which facts will edit cleanly?
5. Would a natively-sourced (not machine-translated) covariance corpus change d_F and the α ordering? If the anisotropy shrinks, part of the "typological" story is a translation artifact.
6. Does the interference ratio IR for MEMIT fall to German-like levels if the batch is reduced from n = 50 to n = 5? That would distinguish "batch editing is geometrically hostile to Indic languages" from "n = 50 is simply too large for this model."
