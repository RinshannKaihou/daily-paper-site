> **一句话总结：** 作者提出 SHq(α, c)——一个把均匀格点经 sinh 压扩并加偏移的闭式码本族（y_k = (sinh(αu_k)+c)/max|·|，级值舍入到 E4M3 后存进 2^q 项查找表），只需按模型和位宽用校准集 KL 扫描两个参数；在 5 个 3B–35B 模型（含 MoE）×2–6 bit×RTN/GPTQ 的 50 个设置中，SH 在 45 个取得最低 held-out KL（3/4 bit 的 20 个设置全部获胜，比最优固定网格低 4.0–34.4%），4.5 bpw 下把 NVFP4 的 KL 压低 40.7–67.4%，但下游平均准确率只提升 0.1–0.7 分。

> **TL;DR:** The paper shows that no single fixed quantization grid wins across bit-widths (BOFS is best at 2 bits, NF at 4–5, INT at 6), then replaces the grid with a two-parameter closed-form family SHq(α, c): α compands level spacing between uniform (INT-like) and geometric (FP-like), and the offset c breaks symmetry so that the otherwise-idle sign bit of the signed E4M3 block scale selects between the grid G and its mirror −G, giving up to 2^(q+1) distinct values per 16-weight block (16 instead of 8/9/13 for SH-without-offset/INT3/NF3 at 3 bits). Grids are chosen per model and width by calibration KL, then applied with RTN or GPTQ. On five models (Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, Qwen3-8B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen3.8-27B, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B) over 2–6 bits, SH has the lowest held-out KL in 45/50 settings and all 20 at 3–4 bits — e.g. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct at 3-bit GPTQ drops from 98.25 mn (BOFS) to 81.46 mn (−17.1%), and at 4.5 bpw SH4 cuts NVFP4's KL from 32.65 to 14.46 mn (−55.7%) on Qwen3-8B GPTQ while gaining +0.6 average accuracy on Llama-3.1-8B (64.0 vs 63.4). The honest catch: much of the NVFP4 gap comes from NVFP4 lacking the per-block signed scale search that all LUT baselines share, and the downstream-accuracy gains are small and frequently below significance.

## 核心贡献

- **用实验否定了"存在通用固定网格"这一隐含假设**：在同一套 LUT + 有符号块缩放 + 逐块 scale 搜索（lever）的框架下，最优固定网格随位宽漂移——2 bit 全部 10 个设置里 BOFS 最好，4/5 bit 的 20 个设置里 NF 领先 17 个，6 bit 的 10 个设置里 INT 领先 5 个。这直接说明 NF4/BOF4 这类"一次推导、永久使用"的码本在设计点上并不稳。
- **提出 SHq(α, c) 闭式网格族**：2^q 个级值由 sinh(αu_k)+c 在均匀格点上采样并归一化得到，α 从 0（均匀/INT 型）连续过渡到大值（几何/FP 型），c 引入非对称；α 的级密度 λ_α(y) ∝ (1 + y² sinh²α)^(−1/2) 恰好是自由度 2 的 Student-t 源的高分辨率 MSE 最优密度 p^(1/3)，因此该族在解析上不是拍脑袋的形状。
- **把块缩放中"闲置的符号位"变成免费的额外分辨率**：E4M3 scale 带符号位，对称网格下 −G = G 使该位完全浪费；取 c ≠ 0 后 −G 与 G 交错，每块可用级值最多翻倍到 2^(q+1)（Qwen3-8B 的 3-bit 网格：16 个不同值 vs INT3 的 9 个、NF3 的 13 个），且不增加任何存储或解码开销。BOF4-S 也用了镜像，但符号由块内最大权重固定，而这里是和 scale 码一起搜索。
- **端到端而非逐层代理来选网格**：只用两个连续参数，参数空间小到可以直接用整模型输出分布的 KL 散度做两阶段 box 搜索（64 个校准窗口、粗化到 128 个最可能 token + 1 个桶），避免了"逐层 MSE 更低但模型更差"的老问题。
- **证明增益来自"间距 + 偏移"这两个自由度，而非 sinh 的具体形状**：NF 加自由宽度仍比 SH 高 8.9–16.7%（3-bit GPTQ），再加一个偏移（NFOC）就落到 SH 的 0.2–2.3% 以内；arctan 压扩器在 1.6–3.0% 以内；而去掉符号位会让 SH 在 3/4 bit 上升 21–38%。作者据此诚实地承认 SH 不是绝对最优（逐层 Lloyd 码本在 Llama-3.1-8B 上比 SH 还低 6.5%）。

## Key Contributions

- **Empirical demolition of the "one fixed grid" assumption.** Holding the LUT, block scale, and per-block scale search constant and varying only the grid, the best fixed grid moves with bit-width: BOFS wins all ten 2-bit settings, NF wins 17 of 20 at 4–5 bits, INT wins 5 of 10 at 6 bits. No fixed codebook (NF4, BOF4, INT, E2M1) is a safe default.
- **A closed-form two-parameter grid family.** SHq(α, c) samples sinh(αu_k) on a uniform q-bit lattice, shifts by c, and normalizes the largest level to 1; all levels are snapped to E4M3 and stored in a 2^q-entry table, so it is a drop-in replacement for the grid in any block-scaled LUT format. Its level density λ_α(y) ∝ (1 + y²sinh²α)^(−1/2) is the high-resolution MSE-optimal p^(1/3) density for a Student-t source with two degrees of freedom, with uniform (α→0) and geometric (large α) grids as limiting cases.
- **Free extra resolution from the block-scale sign bit.** With c ≠ 0 the grid is asymmetric, so a negative E4M3 scale selects −G and the two level sets interleave: up to 2^(q+1) distinct values per block at zero storage cost (16 vs 8/9/13 for c=0/INT3/NF3 at 3 bits). The sign is chosen jointly with the scale code by the per-block search, unlike BOF4-S where it is fixed by the block maximum.
- **End-to-end grid selection instead of a layerwise proxy.** Two continuous parameters make the space small enough to scan directly against the full model's next-token KL on calibration text, sidestepping the known failure mode where lower layerwise reconstruction error yields a worse model.
- **Ablations that attribute the gain to spacing + offset rather than to sinh.** NF with a free width stays 8.9–16.7% above SH after 3-bit GPTQ; adding an offset (NFOC) brings it to within 0.2–2.3%; an arctan compander lands within 1.6–3.0%; removing the sign bit costs SH 21–38% at 3–4 bits. The paper explicitly concedes SH is not absolutely optimal — a per-layer Lloyd codebook beats it by 6.5% on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct after GPTQ.

## Methodology

**Format.** Each row of a weight matrix is cut into blocks of g = 16 weights. A q-bit format stores, per weight, an index k_i into a lookup table L of 2^q E4M3 values, plus one signed E4M3 scale per block, so ŵ_i = s·L[k_i] at q + 0.5 bits per weight. The table (one per format for the whole model) and a per-layer exponent shift of the scales are not counted in the bit rate. The LUT placement factor f ∈ {1, 2^(1/4), 2^(1/2), 2^(3/4)} shifts the levels against E4M3's binades and changes which values survive rounding; it is scanned per family, so all families get the same tuning budget.

**The grid.** y_k = (sinh(αu_k) + c) / max_j |sinh(αu_j) + c|, with u_k = −1 + 2k/(2^q − 1). Equivalently, uniform quantization after the compressor y ↦ asinh(y sinh α) — a classical companding quantizer. α controls density (dense near zero, sparse in the tails, edge-to-center density ratio cosh α); c ≤ 0 breaks the odd symmetry, and the mirror grid is obtained by negating c, so no second table is needed.

**Quantizers.** RTN rounds each block to its nearest level under the lever, with channel norms from one BF16 pass over 128 calibration windows. GPTQ uses Hessians and channel norms from already-quantized preceding blocks, damps the Hessian diagonal by 1% of its mean, processes columns in lazy batches of 128, and at the first column of each 16-column group refits the scales and signs of that group's blocks on the error-compensated weights.

**The lever (per-block scale search).** 25 multipliers of the naive absmax scale, geometrically spaced over [0.6, 1.4], in both signs; each candidate is rounded to E4M3 and the one minimizing Σ_i c_ni²(w_i − ŵ_i)² is kept, where c_ni is the RMS of the input channel on calibration text. This is shared by all LUT baselines (INT, NF, BOF, BOFS) so the grid is the sole variable — NVFP4 is the exception, as it ships an unsigned scale without search.

**Baselines.** INTq (two's-complement grid, two variants: per-block mirroring under absmax vs rail scaling, best picked per configuration); E2M1 {0, ±0.5, ±1, ±1.5, ±2, ±3, ±4, ±6}; NVFP4 (E2M1 + unsigned E4M3 per 16 weights + uncounted FP32 tensor scale, 4.5 bpw, no lever); NF (normal quantiles); BOF (Lloyd–Max fixed point on the exact density of absmax-normalized Gaussian blocks) and BOFS (signed-absmax variant mapping the block maximum to +1).

**Selection protocol.** For each model and width, a two-stage box search (coarse lattice grown while the best point sits on its edge, then a finer grid) picks (α, c) by the KL of the RTN model to BF16 on 64 calibration windows, with distributions coarsened to the 128 most likely BF16 tokens plus one residual bucket. RTN configurations are re-scored and refused if calibration KL drifts more than 1% or 2·10⁻⁴ nats from the scan value. Selected parameters are listed per model and width (e.g. Qwen3-8B: α = 0.625/1.75/1.625/1.5/2.25 and c = −0.156/−0.188/−0.078/−0.031/−0.125 for 2–6 bits); α is largest at 6 bits (2.25–2.56) for every model.

**Evaluation.** FineWeb-Edu sample-10BT split by document: evaluation [0, 5000), validation [50000, 55000) (only for tuning RCO), calibration from document 100000 on; 2048-token windows. Primary metric is the full-vocabulary held-out KL (millinats) to the BF16 model over the first 500K evaluation tokens (245 windows), with the output head and softmax in FP32. Secondary: top-10 expected acceptance rate (EAR, the speculative-decoding acceptance of BF16-proposed tokens) and accuracy on MMLU (5-shot), ARC-Challenge, HellaSwag, PIQA, SIQA, WinoGrande (zero-shot) via lm-evaluation-harness 0.4.11 (vLLM 0.32 for the MoE). Significance from 10,000 bootstrap resamples of the 245 windows, or per-question tests with Holm correction for the MoE. Only decoder linear layers are quantized (including linear-attention projections and routed experts, one matrix per expert); embeddings, output head, and MoE router stay in BF16; activations are not quantized. Per-layer widths come from RCO, a Gumbel-sampled multiple-choice-knapsack allocator minimizing calibration KL under an exact average-width budget. Hardware: H200, L40S, RTX A6000, RTX 3090.

## Results & Highlights

- **45 of 50 settings, and all 20 at 3 and 4 bits.** At 3 bits with GPTQ on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, SH reaches 81.46 mn held-out KL vs 98.25 for BOFS, 95.94 for NF and 118.8 for INT (−17.1% vs the best fixed grid); on Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct at 3-bit GPTQ it is 82.66 vs 116.8 for BOFS (−29.2%). The average margin over the best fixed grid is 14% / 11% / 14% at 2 / 3 / 4 bits, shrinking to 11% and 7% at 5 and 6 bits.
- **The exceptions are named and mostly inside noise.** Three of the five losses are within GPTQ run-to-run variation: BOFS on Qwen3-8B at 2-bit GPTQ (378.2 vs 379.8 mn, 0.4% better) and NF/INT on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B at 5 and 6 bits (0.9% and 1.2%). The two real losses are INT at 6 bits on Qwen3.8-27B, 3.3% below SH under RTN and 5.6% under GPTQ — a narrow, honest admission that at 6 bits uniform spacing wins.
- **Against NVFP4 at 4.5 bpw, KL falls 40.7–67.4% in all ten settings.** Qwen3-8B GPTQ: 32.65 → 14.46 mn (−55.7%); Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct RTN: 52.26 → 21.21 mn (−59.4%); Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct RTN: 52.18 → 20.36 mn (−61.0%); Qwen3.8-27B GPTQ: 18.24 → 9.33 mn (−48.9%); Qwen3.6-35B-A3B GPTQ: 24.35 → 14.44 mn (−40.7%).
- **But that headline is mostly the scale search, not the grid.** The authors state it plainly: with the shared lever even INT4 beats NVFP4 by 25.4–43.3%, and the best fixed grid is already 37.9–55.6% below NVFP4. Holding the scale fixed, SH4's remaining edge over the best fixed grid is 4.6–34.4% — that is the grid's true contribution.
- **Downstream accuracy gains at 4.5 bpw are real but thin.** SH4 beats NVFP4 on average accuracy in all six model–quantizer combinations by 0.1–0.7 points: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct GPTQ 64.0 vs 63.4 (+0.6, MMLU 67.7 vs 66.9), Qwen3-8B RTN 63.8 vs 63.6 (+0.2), Qwen3.6-35B-A3B GPTQ 68.5 vs 68.1 (+0.4, not significant). Only the Llama-3.1-8B average-accuracy difference and all four 8B MMLU differences are statistically significant.
- **At 2 bits the grids genuinely diverge, and SH wins where it matters most.** Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct at 2-bit GPTQ: 55.8% average accuracy vs 54.3 (BOFS, +1.5), 52.6 (INT, +3.2), 52.2 (NF, +3.6), with KL 509.0 vs 628.8 for BOFS (−19.0%); all differences significant. Under RTN the gap is +1.6 over BOFS and +2.8–4.3 over NF/INT.
- **The offset is the single biggest lever, and removing the sign bit is catastrophic for everyone.** Dropping the block-scale sign at 2-bit RTN raises Qwen3-8B KL by +81.7% for SH but +1947% (NF), +1815% (BOF) and +3175% (INT); at 3–4 bits SH still rises 21–38% while NF/BOF/INT rise 10–31%. NF with a free width but no offset remains 10.4% (Qwen3-8B) and 16.7% (Llama-3.1-8B) above SH after 3-bit GPTQ; adding the offset (NFOC) closes to +2.3% and +0.2%.
- **Mixed precision confirms the ranking but shrinks the margin.** Under RCO at average widths 3/4/5 bits, SH is the lowest-KL mixed model in all six model–width combinations, but the gap to fixed grids narrows from 6.6–22.4% to 0.3–9.0% because fixed grids benefit more from allocation (SH improves only 2.3–16.2%, fixed grids up to 31.1%). Superior grids leave less residual error for allocation to fix.
- **EAR tracks KL almost everywhere.** SH has the highest expected acceptance rate in 46 of 50 settings and the KL ordering predicts the EAR ordering in 47 of 50; e.g. Llama-3.1-8B at 2-bit GPTQ 76.6% vs 74.4% (BOFS).
- **One honest counterexample.** On the MoE model at 2-bit GPTQ, BOFS and INT beat SH by 1.3 and 1.2 accuracy points despite having *higher* KL (224.4 and 240.5 vs 208.2 mn) — the only setting in the paper where a higher-KL grid is significantly more accurate. The authors flag it rather than hide it, which is exactly the right instinct given they use KL as the selection objective.

## Authors & Credibility

Michael Helcig (ETH Zürich, corresponding), Earl Killian (Rhoa Research), Dan Alistarh (ISTA). Alistarh's group is one of the most consistent producers of LLM compression work (GPTQ, SparseGPT, QuIP#, QTIP, BitNet-adjacent lines, HIGGS, EvoPress, RCO), and this paper is explicitly built on that lineage: GPTQ as the quantizer, RCO as the allocator, the "statistically-lossless" KL/EAR evaluation protocol from Helcig et al. 2026. Killian contributes the hardware-facing LUT/per-layer methodology (arXiv 2605.14929) and is presumably the reason the format details — E4M3 level snapping, LUT placement factors, the PTX 3-bit LUT decompress instruction in Rubin — are treated with real care rather than hand-waved. The paper is a preprint with no venue yet; no code or kernel release is mentioned. The experimental infrastructure (four GPU types, five models, 50 primary settings plus ablations) is substantial for an academic compression paper, and the writing is unusually candid about where the method loses.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** Modest but well-targeted. Companding grids (Bennett 1948; Gersho & Gray 1992) and parametric codebook families (PowerQuant, M-ANT, CubicQuant, Orr et al.'s p^(1/3) grids) all exist, and the paper says so. The genuinely new pieces are (i) using the E4M3 scale's sign bit as a free mirror selector *jointly searched with the scale code* — BOF4-S fixes that sign from the block maximum, so this is a real step beyond it — and (ii) selecting the grid parameters against full-model KL rather than a layerwise proxy. That second claim is stated as "to our knowledge, the first parametric grid whose parameters are selected by the KL divergence of the full model", which is defensible but narrow: it is a change of objective, not of mechanism.

**Rigor.** Above average for this literature in several specific ways. All LUT baselines share the identical table, block scale, and lever, so the grid really is the only variable; INT gets two variants and each family gets four LUT placement factors, which prevents the classic "our format is tuned, yours is not" asymmetry. GPTQ nondeterminism is measured rather than assumed: a rerun on a different GPU type or with half the lazy-batch width changes 41–42% of the weights and shifts KL by 1.2–1.6%, which is why the paper refuses to interpret any KL difference below 3% or any difference whose 95% bootstrap interval contains zero. That is a stricter bar than most quantization papers apply, and it is what forces them to report that the 3-bit downstream-accuracy gaps are not significant.

**The main soft spot is the NVFP4 headline.** "Reduces the KL divergence of NVFP4 by 41–67%" is the abstract's most quotable number, but the authors' own decomposition shows the bulk of it is the lever: NVFP4 uses an unsigned E4M3 scale with no search, and with the lever even INT4 beats NVFP4 by 25.4–43.3%. The grid's own contribution, measured against the best fixed grid under the same scale, is 4.6–34.4%. The paper does say this in Section 4, but the abstract and conclusion lead with the inflated framing. A reader skimming will conclude the grid buys 40–67% when it buys roughly a tenth of that against a properly scaled NVFP4 baseline.

**Second soft spot: the selection metric is the reported metric.** (α, c) is chosen to minimize calibration KL to BF16, and the headline result is held-out KL to BF16 on the same corpus (FineWeb-Edu, different documents). This is a legitimate held-out split, but the objective and the metric are the same functional, so the 4.0–34.4% gains are partly a statement about how well two parameters can fit that functional. The independent evidence is downstream accuracy, and there the picture is much weaker: at 3 bits, SH's advantage over the next-best grid is 0.1–0.4 points and never significant; at 4.5 bpw it is 0.1–0.7 points with significance only on Llama-3.1-8B. The MoE 2-bit GPTQ result — where BOFS is 1.3 points *more* accurate with 7.8% *higher* KL — is a direct counterexample to KL-as-proxy, and the paper offers no explanation beyond noting it.

**Missing ablations and comparisons.** No comparison against SqueezeLLM's sensitivity-weighted k-means or against a full GSQ pipeline; the GSQ experiment is a modified recipe (1024 instead of 4096 sequences, 10 instead of 20 epochs, groups of 16 instead of 128, MLP layers only, a different α than the scanned one, unrounded tables) and it shows the grid advantage shrinking to within 2.7% at GSQ's initialization — a result the authors report honestly but which materially weakens the "grid choice is the lever that matters" narrative, since learned codes then contribute more than the grid. There is also no wall-clock, kernel, or energy measurement anywhere, despite the paper's central practical claim being "drop-in replacement, no new arithmetic in hardware". The structural argument (only LUT contents change, one table lookup per weight, Rubin's tcgen05.mma.decompress::lut::b already does 3-bit LUT decode) is sound, but a hardware-motivated paper that never runs a kernel is leaving its most important claim unverified.

**Data and scope quirks.** All five models are instruction-tuned, none is a base model, none is below 3B or above 35B, and only decoder linear layers are quantized — this is weight-only quantization, not W4A4, so nothing here speaks to activation quantization or to the KV cache. Calibration and evaluation are both FineWeb-Edu, so cross-domain robustness of the selected (α, c) is untested; a grid fitted to web text may not transfer to code or math, and the fact that α is largest at 6 bits for *every* model suggests the scan is picking up a systematic property of the format rather than anything model-specific. Every result is a single run (the paper is explicit about this), and the per-layer-grid ablation used RCO-selected placement with an incomplete search, so "one grid per model suffices" rests on a bounded local search rather than an exhaustive one. Reproducibility is decent on paper — all hyperparameters, the FineWeb-Edu split indices, and the (α, c) table are given — but no code, and the GPTQ pipeline is GPU-order-sensitive, so exact numbers will not replicate on different hardware.

**Significance.** As a format proposal the idea is cheap and adoptable: change the table contents, keep the storage and decode path. That is genuinely valuable for anyone shipping a block-scaled LUT format. As a scientific claim about quantization, the honest summary is that two well-chosen degrees of freedom recover most of the gap between fixed grids and per-layer learned codebooks, and that the remaining differences at 3–4 bits are mostly invisible downstream. The paper's recommendation ("SHq as the standard grid") is stronger than its evidence supports; "SHq as a better default than NF4/BOF4 when you can afford a calibration scan" is what the data shows.

## Strengths

- **Clean experimental design.** One variable changes (the grid); table, block scale, lever, calibration windows, and evaluation are held fixed across INT/NF/BOF/BOFS. This is rarer than it should be and makes the comparison trustworthy.
- **Two parameters instead of 2^q.** The whole point — a search space small enough to optimize against the real objective — is executed properly, with a documented two-stage scan and a drift guard that re-scores and refuses stale configurations.
- **The sign-bit trick is elegant and free.** Doubling the effective level count (16 vs 8/9/13 at 3 bits) with zero storage, zero extra decode, and no second table is the kind of idea that should have been obvious and wasn't.
- **Unusually honest reporting.** Five losses are listed by name and magnitude; three are conceded to be inside noise; the per-layer Lloyd codebook is admitted to beat SH by 6.5%; the MoE 2-bit counterexample is reported; the NVFP4 gap is decomposed into scale-search vs grid contributions.
- **Theory that actually connects to the design.** λ_α(y) ∝ (1 + y²sinh²α)^(−1/2) being the p^(1/3) density for a 2-dof Student-t source explains why α→0 and α→∞ recover INT and FP as limiting cases, and predicts that a single global α suffices (confirmed: 249 of 252 Qwen3-8B layers prefer the scanned α = 1.75).
- **Broad coverage.** Five models including a 35B MoE, five bit-widths, two quantizers, mixed precision, EAR, six downstream benchmarks, and four GPU generations — enough to make the "no universal fixed grid" claim stick.
- **Practical fallbacks documented.** E4M4–E4M7 tables change KL by only −1.1% to +3.6% (so E4M3 level snapping is not the bottleneck), and per-layer grids add little after GPTQ (so one grid per model and width is enough).

## Limitations

- **No systems evaluation.** No latency, throughput, memory-traffic, or kernel implementation. "Drop-in" and "no new arithmetic in hardware" are argued structurally, not measured. For a paper whose selling point is a hardware format, this is the biggest gap.
- **The NVFP4 comparison is not apples-to-apples**, and the abstract leads with the unadjusted 41–67% number. NVFP4 has no scale search and no lever; the grid's own contribution is 4.6–34.4%.
- **Selection objective = reported metric.** Grids are fitted to calibration KL and scored on held-out KL from the same distribution; the only independent evidence (downstream accuracy) shows gains of 0.1–0.7 points that are often not significant.
- **Weight-only, decoder-only, instruction-tuned-only.** No activation quantization, no KV cache, no base models, nothing below 3B or above 35B, single domain (FineWeb-Edu) for both calibration and evaluation.
- **Single runs throughout**, with GPTQ order-sensitivity that changes 41–42% of weights across GPU types; the 3% interpretation threshold is principled but also large enough to swallow most of the 5–6 bit results.
- **The grid is not optimal, only convenient.** A per-layer Lloyd codebook beats SH by 6.5% on Llama-3.1-8B after GPTQ; NFOC and arctan companders match it within noise. The contribution is a compact parametrization, not a better codebook.
- **Cost of the scan is not quantified.** Selecting (α, c) requires many full-model KL evaluations on 64 calibration windows per model and width, and no ablation asks what happens with 8 or 16 windows, or how sensitive the choice is to calibration-set shift.
- **No code or kernel release**, and the GSQ comparison uses a materially modified recipe, so the strongest competing result is only indicative.

## Relevance

直接落在「Reliability under Extreme Performance Optimization」这条线上：它把低精度量化的误差来源拆成"网格形状"和"块缩放搜索"两部分，并用 held-out KL 与 EAR 量化二者各自贡献多少——这对判断"某个 4-bit 格式的精度损失到底来自哪里"很有用，也提醒我们 KL 与下游准确率在 2-bit MoE 上会出现方向相反的背离。方法本身（改查找表内容、不动存储与解码）意味着任何自建 LUT 量化栈都能低成本试一遍。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. How much of the 4.0–34.4% grid advantage survives when NVFP4 is given an equivalent signed-scale search (e.g. ScaleSweep or the "four over six" adaptive scaling)? The paper's own INT4 result suggests the answer is "much less".
2. Why does the offset help so much at 2 bits specifically (+81.7% KL without it, vs +21–38% at 3–4 bits)? Is the mirror-pair trick effectively emulating a 3-bit grid with 2-bit storage, and does that explain why SH's 2-bit α for Qwen3-8B collapses to 0.625 (nearly uniform)?
3. What happens with block size 32 or 128 instead of 16? Egiazarian et al. (Grid Games) argue multi-grid advantages vanish as block size grows — does the sign-bit mirror suffer the same fate, and at what g?
4. Does a grid fitted on FineWeb-Edu transfer to code, math, or multilingual text? A cross-domain calibration/evaluation matrix would tell us whether (α, c) is a property of the format or of the corpus.
