01 — Method

Why the split has to be re-decided every step

Algorithm 1 · Eq. 1–2 · §II–IV

1.1 The split goes stale in one step

Reusing a split for a single extra decode step forfeits 62–91.6% of the utilization it recovers over the static baseline. Even a cost-free, clairvoyant periodic re-placement collapses to that baseline by N=2 at G=64. The binding constraint is decision latency, not optimization quality.

N = 0 · fresh 100%
N = 1 · one step stale 8.4–38%

By N = 4 the reused split sits below the static baseline in 3 of 4 trace / group settings.

1.2 Halve the gaps instead of solving the LP

The exact min–max LP needs 156G²+160G+40 bytes and 102 µs on a GPU. Relief walks a pre-coloured transfer list and halves the load gap on each replica edge, truncated by the tokens still at home, writing into a live G-entry table — Gauss–Seidel in integer arithmetic. A few passes land within a few percent of the optimum; the working set is 12 bytes per rank.

1.3 Move the decision off the critical path

Three implementations return bit-identical splits: a CUDA kernel (13–28 µs), an FPGA card over PCIe (2.8–4.1 µs), and a 0.14 mm² fixed-function block (0.7–1.7 µs, ~0.02% of an A100/H100 die). Only Tsolve differs, so every margin between them is interface geometry.

03 — Reading

What holds up, what doesn't

The paper volunteers the results that hurt it

Strengths

  • The measurement reframes the problem. Every periodic alternative gets free, clairvoyant re-optimization — and still loses to per-step decisions.
  • Ueff is the right score. Charging latency against the layer being served makes kernel, FPGA and silicon genuinely comparable.
  • Bit-identical splits. Three implementations, one answer — which removes the "which build did you benchmark" objection entirely.
  • Small enough to audit. One table, one compiled list, integer halving, one reciprocal. The only knob is a closed-form pass count.
  • Honest in the awkward places. The G=64 EPLB reversal and the two losing cells are probed to a mechanism, not buried.

Limitations

  • Simulation only. No serving-stack integration, no wall-clock latency, no throughput — every number is an analytic model over captured traces.
  • Narrow topology. D=2 replication on power-of-two tori; the conflict-free colouring depends on that regularity.
  • Under-specified traces. No batch size, concurrency, step count or prompt selection — and volatility is the load-bearing claim.
  • Baseline scope. One grouped-LP point, no cheap GPU approximation, and an EPLB reimplementation whose G=64 pathology may not transfer.
  • Pre-silicon numbers. ASAP7, global-route parasitics, no power, no tape-out; prefill is out of scope by construction.
04 — Open questions

What the next paper has to answer

Q1

What does a 27-point Ueff recovery buy in end-to-end decode throughput — is the expert stage 20% or 60% of inter-token latency at the batch sizes that matter?

Q2

Does the G=64 EPLB reversal reproduce with DeepSeek's production EPLB, or is the "heaviest expert to a fixed neighbour" duplicate map specific to this reimplementation?

Q3

How does Relief behave under D > 2 replication, where the replica graph is no longer 2-regular and a colour class is no longer a perfect matching?
