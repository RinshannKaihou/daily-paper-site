A

How one replay works

3 kernels · 1 CUDA Graph · 0 dependency edges between roots
role1 · dispatch Routing + all-to-all pull into a token ring comm ctx · 20 SM
role2 · grouped GEMM Routed + shared W1/W2, SwiGLU fused compute ctx · 128 SM
∥ concurrent CUDA-Graph roots — no dependency edge
role3 · finalize Combines k (+1 shared) partials directly from peer buffers compute ctx

The driver schedules role1 and role2 simultaneously because they live on disjoint SM sets and share no edge. role3 absorbs global completion.

  • The mismatch. At decode a rank holds 1–256 tokens, so the grouped GEMM is HBM-bandwidth-bound while dispatch is latency-bound and needs few SMs. Mega MoE fuses both across all 148 SMs and pays a device-wide grid sync.
  • The split. cuDevSmResourceSplitByCount + cuGreenCtxCreate carve a 20-SM comm context and a 128-SM compute context once per CUDA context, with balanced workqueues so the two never silently serialize.
  • The handshake. A generation-tagged token ring lets compute start on the first filled block; each W2 K128 tile waits only on the two W1 tiles that feed it, so no L1→L2 barrier is needed.
  • The barrier removal. Receive counters are parity-double-buffered, so the NVLink round trip leaves every replay's critical path — not just startup.
  • The GEMM. Blackwell uses 2-CTA tcgen05 FP8×FP4 block-scaled MMA via inline PTX; Hopper uses register-A WGMMA with in-register FP4→E4M3 decode and windowed scale repacking. FP4 weights are K128-tile prepacked for single-burst TMA.

Where it holds up, where it doesn't

side by side

Strengths 4

  • The reframing is real. A bandwidth-bound decode GEMM does not need every SM, while dispatch needs SMs but no tensor cores. Partitioning at the driver level follows naturally once stated.
  • The barrier removal is the best idea. Parity-double-buffered receive counters turn a per-replay cross-rank round trip into a generation flip — useful even without green contexts.
  • Unusually honest attribution. Inherited pieces (operand swap, valid-M N sizing, UTCCP path) are marked as inherited; the added pieces are named.
  • Conservative measurement. Slowest-rank latency, mean-of-ratios, pinned toolchain and commits, idle-GPU gating, open source at v0.3.1 / 9bdb81f.

Limitations 4

  • The missing ablation is central. Four changes are bundled — partitioning, barrier removal, K128 prepacking, shrunken sync domain — and only fusion-vs-unfusion is ablated.
  • The biggest numbers rest on the weakest baseline. Every H20 result, including 1.296× and 1.353×, is measured against the authors' own SM90 port.
  • Decode-only, and the tail is thin. At 256 tokens/rank the gain narrows to 1.01–1.13×, exactly where MoE serving spends most of its FLOPs.
  • Single node, rigid shapes. All peers on NVLink; 72-rank support is never exercised, and I ≤ 4096, H mod 256 = 0, k+1 ≤ 32 constrain adoption.
D

Open questions

what would change the verdict
  1. Where is the knee? The 20-SM split is never varied. Would 8 or 12 SMs perform as well — and make adoption much cheaper?
  2. How much is the partition, really? A 2×2 of partition on/off × tag-2 barrier on/off at one batch size would separate the two credited mechanisms.
  3. Is the SM90 port faithful? The 1.296× and 1.353× headlines cannot be settled without an independent Hopper fused-MoE baseline.
  4. Does it survive multi-node? On InfiniBand, does the 20-SM partition still suffice, and does green-context splitting compose with MPS/MIG or another tenant?
