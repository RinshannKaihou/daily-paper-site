> **一句话总结：** FlagRot 把"量化前先做 Hadamard 旋转"从固定预处理改成编码器的一次逐块决策——对每个 32 通道的 key 子向量，在"原样"与"旋转"之间按校准查询加权的注意力误差择优，选择位藏在量化 scale 的符号位里（页大小不变），读出时改为旋转 query 而非把 key 转回来；在 2.5 bit/值下把 KIVI 的十二模型 KL 从 18.7 降到 12.4×10⁻³，vLLM 里把 128k RULER 提高 +2.9 分。

> **TL;DR:** The standard fix for 2-bit KV caches — rotate keys with a Hadamard transform before quantizing — is not uniformly helpful: on Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen3-8B cached keys, H32 *raises* the query-weighted INT4 error for about two thirds of the flattest tenth of 32-channel key sub-vectors while helping the spikiest tenth. FlagRot therefore treats the transform as a per-piece encoder decision, choosing identity or H32 (with whole-head H128 as a competing candidate) by the attention-weighted error eᵀC_q e, and stores the 5 flags in the sign bits of already-positive quantization scales so the page does not grow. On WikiText-2 next-token KL at 2.5 bits/value, FlagRot-KIVI reaches 12.4×10⁻³ geometric-mean over 12 models versus 18.7 for KIVI (−34%) and 18.1 for RotateKV at clip 1.0 (better on 9/12); served in vLLM it lifts RULER-128k on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct from 62.3 to 65.2 (+2.9 pts over KIVI-only, 95% CI [+1.2, +4.5]) and LongBench v1 from 42.80 to 44.73 on Qwen3-32B (+1.94). The task-level wins on the 8B models are small and partly inside the noise band (+0.42 and +0.86 points), and at 4 bits the KL gains (44–67% over RTN) produce essentially no measurable task difference — the value is in the low-bit regime, not as a general-purpose upgrade.

## 核心贡献

- **指出"旋转总是有益"这一默认假设是错的，并给出可测量的解释。** 均匀量化器的误差随分组的 crest factor κ = ‖x‖∞/rms(x) 增长；Hadamard 变换保持能量但把 κ(x) 换成 κ(H_r x)，因此能把尖峰摊平，也能给原本平坦的块造出一个新峰。在 Llama-3.1-8B 与 Qwen3-8B 的真实缓存 key 上，最平坦的十分之一子向量中有约三分之二在旋转后误差变大，最尖的十分之一中只有约六分之一变差。
- **把变换选择形式化为编码器决策，并用"注意力看到的误差"而非重构误差来判定。** key 误差 e 对 query q 的 logit 影响是 q·e，故应以 C_q = E[qqᵀ] 加权（eᵀMe），而不是用 ‖e‖²；论文用消融证明这一区别是方法成立的前提（用重构误差选，KIVI 的 KL 反而从 18.4 恶化到 19.1）。
- **提出 FlagRot 的具体编码方案：flag 藏在符号位里，页大小不增。** 每个 D=128 的头切四个 32 通道块，每块在 {I, H32} 中选一个，整头还可选 H128，共 D/r+1 = 5 个 flag；这些 bit 写入本就恒正的量化 scale 的符号位，因此 2.5 bit 布局仍是 80 B/token/KV head。读出时把 query 一次性变换到各候选基（(T q)·ĉ），key 永不反旋转。
- **给出"多久决策一次取决于量化轴"的判据。** 逐通道缓存（KIVI）一个决策覆盖 32 个 token，固定模式即可保留 78% 的 KL 收益且写时零开销；逐 token 缓存统计量逐 key 变化，必须逐 key 决策，此时 flag 仍藏在符号位、缓存不增大。
- **补上 RoPE 后 key 的公共偏移问题。** 所有 key 减去同一 μ 会让每个 logit 平移 q·μ，softmax 权重不变，故可无损去均值；FlagRot-auto 用掩码 m 只对"去均值确实降低校准误差"的 32 通道组做平移，并在整模型层面自动开关。
- **验证选择性与已有变换方法可叠加**：在 WUSH-KV 拟合出的变换之上再加选择，12 个模型的 KL 从 2.33 降到 1.51（几何均值），TurboQuant 的 3-bit K/V 从 11.4 降到 10.1。

## Key Contributions

- **A measured refutation of "rotation always helps."** The paper shows rotation is a per-piece gamble: it flattens spiky sub-vectors but can add a peak to flat ones, and both cases coexist inside a single key and even inside one head across tokens. Figure 1b quantifies this on real caches (two thirds of the flattest decile get worse under H32 for INT4; one in six of the spikiest).
- **An attention-weighted selection criterion.** Instead of minimizing reconstruction error, FlagRot minimizes eᵀMe where M is built from calibration queries (C_q, or a softmax-attention-weighted variant for the per-channel case). The ablation is decisive: plain reconstruction error makes selection *harmful* on KIVI (19.1 vs 18.4 without selection), while the weighted criterion reaches 12.3.
- **A zero-overhead signalling scheme.** Five flag bits per key head are hidden in the sign bits of the FP8/FP16 scale words, which are always positive, so the packed 2.5-bit page stays at 80 bytes/token/KV head — the same as plain RTN. This is the engineering core that makes per-key decisions affordable in a real kernel.
- **A quantization-axis argument for decision frequency.** Fixed (per-head, calibrated once) versus per-key selection is not a tuning knob but a consequence of what one quantizer group pools: KIVI groups 32 tokens, so one decision suffices (78% of the KL gain, zero write cost); per-token caches need a fresh decision per key (35% further error reduction vs 10% on KIVI).
- **A lossless key-centering trick plus an automatic guard.** Subtracting a calibrated post-RoPE key mean leaves softmax weights unchanged; a per-group mask and a model-level switch keep it from hurting models with logit soft-capping.
- **A working vLLM plugin with both per-channel and per-token paths**, evaluated for fidelity on 12–15 models, task accuracy on four models (LongBench v1, RULER-128k, AIME, τ²-bench, SWE-bench Verified), and serving throughput/capacity on H200 and H20.

## Methodology

**Problem setup.** A group of g consecutive values shares one scale (plus one offset for asymmetric formats), so a b-bit code costs b + m/g bits per value; INT2 with a 16-bit scale at g=32 is 2.5 bits. An orthogonal R preserves dot products, qᵀk = (Rq)ᵀ(Rk), so a key may be stored in a rotated basis provided the query is rotated identically.

**Why rotation is conditional.** A uniform b-bit quantizer splits a group's range into 2^b − 1 steps, so at fixed energy the error grows with the crest factor κ(x) = ‖x‖∞/rms(x). H_r preserves ‖x‖₂ but replaces κ(x) by κ(H_r x). On measured keys, rotated 32-dim pieces settle at a median crest factor of 2.2, close to the 2.3 of a Gaussian 32-vector — so rotation pulls every piece toward Gaussian-ness, which helps a spike (κ = √r) and can hurt a flat piece (κ ≈ 1). The crossover where "rotation hurts" becomes the majority sits at κ ≈ 2.27–2.35 on the four development models. Because the exact error also depends on the min–max range and, for FP4, on the non-uniform grid, FlagRot does not predict from κ — it quantizes each candidate and measures.

**Selection rule (Eq. 1).** For a vector or key group x and candidate set T, T*(x) = argmin_{T∈T} e_Tᵀ M e_T with e_T = x − T⁻¹Q(Tx). A head of dimension D is split into pieces of r = 32 channels; each piece may stay as-is or take H_r, and the whole head may instead take H_D. Each piece keeps its lower-error candidate, then the assembled reconstruction competes with the H_D reconstruction under the full eᵀMe, giving D/r + 1 = 5 flags for D=128. Searching all 16 joint combinations instead changes the KL of the two 8B models by only −0.9% and −1.5%, so the greedy per-piece choice is retained.

**Read path (Eq. 2).** q·k̂_t = Σ_j (T_{t,j} q_j)·ĉ_{t,j}; the query is transformed into each basis once per decoding step and shared across all cached tokens, flags select the partial dot products, and keys are never rotated back. Value transforms, where used, are applied after the attention-weighted sum, separately per basis.

**Two instantiations.** *FlagRot-KIVI* follows KIVI: keys quantized per channel over 32-token groups with an FP16 (or FP8) scale and minimum per channel and group; a group is encoded when its last token arrives, and one decision covers a whole group of one KV head, weighted by the attention mass the calibration queries place on each key, w_{t,q} = min(1, exp(s q·k_t − log Z_q)), capped at one because Z_q is measured on a different window. Because per-channel offsets already absorb a shared key mean, FlagRot-KIVI needs no centering. *FlagRot-auto* quantizes each key per token in 32-channel groups, weights by C_q, and subtracts μ ⊙ m before encoding, where m ∈ {0,1}^D keeps only the groups where removing the mean lowers the calibration error of an RTN cache; the BF16 sink and recent keys get the same shift. Values are quantized per token without rotation.

**Layouts.** With D=128 a token of one KV head is 512 B in BF16. FlagRot-KIVI with FP16 scales and minima (32 bits per group) needs 97 B, the 3.0-bit layout; with FP8 scales it needs 80 B, the 2.5-bit packed layout, identical in size to an RTN page and to FlagRot-auto. Both are served.

**Evaluation protocol.** Fidelity is next-token KL divergence from the BF16 model, ×10³, averaged over all 2048-token WikiText-2 test windows, on twelve decoder-only models from 3B to 27B (Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen3-8B, Mistral-7B-v0.3, Qwen3-14B as development models; eight held out, run once with final settings). Keys after RoPE and values are quantized; linear layers stay BF16; every unprotected earlier token is read from the quantized cache. Calibration uses four 2048-token WikiText-2 training windows. Task evaluation runs the kernels inside vLLM 0.30: LongBench v1 (21 tasks, 4,750 items/model) on four models, RULER (13 subtasks, 128k tokens, 1,300 items) on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, AIME 2024/2025 on Qwen3-8B, τ²-bench and SWE-bench Verified on Qwen3.8-27B. Intervals are nominal 95% paired bootstrap; two families of task contrasts were fixed before the last results arrived, with Holm adjustment inside them.

**Baselines.** KIVI-only shares the kernel, layout and protection with FlagRot-KIVI and differs only by forcing all flags to identity — a clean ablation. RotateKV is the authors' simulation, validated against the official code's perplexity (5.509 vs 5.511 on Llama-2-7B), run at clip ratios 0.8 and 1.0 under the same protection. WUSH-KV runs in its published configuration and is reproduced against its published perplexities (Table 34). vLLM's INT4, TurboQuant, Four Over Six, SmoothAttention, QuaRot and SpinQuant are all reproduced from official code or kernels (Table 33).

## Results & Highlights

- **Fidelity at equal size (WikiText-2 next-token KL×10³, 12 models, INT2 at 2.5 bits, g=32, S4W128).** FlagRot-KIVI reaches a geometric mean of **12.4** against **18.7 for KIVI (−34%)**, **18.1 for RotateKV at clip 1.0**, **25.6 for RotateKV at clip 0.8**, and **17.8 for FlagRot-auto**; it is significantly lower than KIVI on 11 of 12 models and lower than RotateKV clip 1.0 on 9 of 12. Excluding Gemma-3-27B, where RotateKV degrades, the geometric means are 11.1 vs 14.1. On the eight held-out models FlagRot-KIVI reaches 12.1.
- **Task accuracy in vLLM at 2.5 bits (80 B/token/KV head, S4W128, H200).** RULER at 128k on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct: **BF16 73.7 → KIVI-only 62.3 → FlagRot-KIVI 65.2**, i.e. **+2.9 points over KIVI-only [+1.2, +4.5]** (fixed mode +2.5 [+0.9, +4.1]), recovering about 26% of KIVI's 11.3-point loss. LongBench v1 21-task macro-score: **Qwen3-32B 44.97 → 42.80 → 44.73 (+1.94 [+1.22, +2.66])**; Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct 49.70 → 47.38 → 48.24 (**+0.86 [+0.23, +1.48]**); Qwen3-8B 49.35 → 47.28 → 47.70 (**+0.42 [−0.20, +1.04]**, CI crosses zero).
- **The gain is real but narrow on 8B models, and negative on the hybrid.** On Qwen3.8-27B, whose KV cache lives in only 16 of 64 layers, KIVI-only already matches BF16 (53.82 vs 53.49) and selection *subtracts* **−0.57 points [−0.99, −0.17]**; the paper's framing is that FlagRot-KIVI stays within 0.24 points of BF16. At equal bytes FlagRot-KIVI beats FlagRot-auto by **+18.5 RULER points [+16.5, +20.4]** on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, but only by +1.7 and +1.8 LongBench points on the two 8B models.
- **Width scaling (Table 1b, 12-model geometric mean, KIVI with FP8 scales).** Selection removes **34% of the KL at INT2 (18.7 → 12.4)**, **31% at INT3 (3.7 → 2.5)** and **30% at INT4 (1.7 → 1.2)**; on centered per-token caches it removes 59%, 51% and 36%; on plain RTN it removes 62%, 56% and 47%. The number of models significantly improved is 10–12 of 12 in every cell.
- **The criterion, not the candidate set, is what matters (Table 6, INT2).** Judged by plain reconstruction error, selection gives KIVI a KL of **19.1 — worse than the 18.4 of doing nothing** — while the attention-weighted criterion gives **12.3**. Restricting to per-piece candidates only costs 23.3 and whole-head only 21.0 (FlagRot-auto's full method is 17.8, no selection 43.4), so both candidate types earn their place.
- **Per-token caches and centering (Qwen3-8B, AIME 2024+25, 60 problems × 8 samples, pass@1).** BF16 is 71.5/72.3; RTN loses **−34.8 points [−43.1, −26.5]**, centering recovers half of that (**−17.5 [−23.5, −11.9]**), and per-key selection takes it to **−2.9 [−5.8, 0.0]**; per-key minus RTN is **+14.6 [+10.0, +19.8]** with centering (versus +21.0 without). On LongBench the per-key mode adds +2.58 points over the fixed mode after Holm correction (HF study, 16 tasks × 50 items), and the centered-RTN baseline gains +4.45 and +3.75 points (both p < 0.001).
- **4-bit caches (median KL reduction vs RTN over 15 models, no BF16 tokens).** **INT4 56% [13, 69]**, **MXFP4 67% [30, 89]**, **NVFP4 44% [17, 56]**, significant on 15 of 15 models per format. But the task study tells a flatter story: of 36 model–task pairs in Table 28, **34 have a 95% CI that includes zero** and 2 lie above it, and on the 16 English LongBench tasks the differences from BF16 are −0.01, −0.12 and −0.16 points.
- **Cost and capacity (Qwen3.8-27B, one H200, 8k context, S4W128).** The packed 2.5-bit layout holds **2.75M tokens against 0.93M for BF16 (3.0×)** and 6.1× on Qwen3-8B. Decode time per output token at batch 64 is **38.7 ms for FlagRot-KIVI at 2.5 bits versus 33.2 ms for KIVI-only and 29.1 ms for BF16** — selection costs ~17% over KIVI at large batch, and the per-channel reader is 2.7–2.9× slower than the per-token reader (Table 11). Per-key decisions cost 5% of prefill throughput; the fixed mode avoids that entirely.
- **Where it breaks, honestly reported.** Gemma-3-27B is the one model where selection raises the INT2 KL of KIVI (**40.7 vs 25.1**): in its 52 sliding-window layers the calibration queries predict a 29% logit-error *reduction* while the real queries see a 29% *increase*. A per-layer calibration check, added after observing the regression, turns selection off in 27 local layers and brings the model to **22.7, 9.5% below KIVI**, moving the twelve-model geometric mean from 12.3 to 11.9 without disturbing the other eleven.
- **Composition and transfer.** On top of WUSH-KV's fitted transform, selection lowers KL by 19–81% on all 12 models (geometric mean 1.51 vs 2.33); on RotateKV it helps on 4 of 4; on TurboQuant 3-bit K/V, 11.4 → 10.1. Calibrating on WikiText-2 transfers to books, code, math, Chinese and German Wikipedia with 24–44% KL reduction, and varying the calibration windows, source or query count moves FlagRot-KIVI's KL by at most 2.1%.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** Moderate and genuine, but narrower than the framing suggests. That rotation is not universally beneficial is already implicit in the quantization literature — FlatQuant's "flatness matters," KurTail's kurtosis objective, and the incoherence-processing line all grapple with the same tension — and the paper's own related work admits the idea of per-block transform selection is standard in video coding (HEVC/VVC) and in Four Over Six's per-block FP4 scale choice. What is actually new is (a) applying per-32-channel selection *inside a KV cache* with the decision signalled in existing sign bits at zero byte cost, and (b) the argument that decision frequency should follow the quantization axis. Both are real contributions; neither is a new principle.

**Rigor — strong where it counts, soft at the edges.** The ablation in Table 6 is the paper's best evidence and it is damning for the naive alternative: selecting by reconstruction error gives KIVI 19.1 versus 18.4 with no selection at all, i.e. the obvious implementation is worse than doing nothing. That is a genuinely useful warning. The kernel/simulator parity checks and the baseline reproduction table are better than most papers in this space. But three soft spots:

1. **The headline statistical claim lives on the layout that is not the headline.** The pre-specified Holm family is defined on the **3.0-bit** results (Table 19), where RULER per-key gain is +4.2 with p < 0.001; the abstract and introduction quote the **2.5-bit** packed layout's +2.9, whose interval is nominal rather than Holm-adjusted. The two layouts differ by at most 0.75 points on the shared endpoints, so this is not deception, but the strongest claim and the advertised claim are not the same claim.
2. **The fixed mode quietly wins in places and the paper does not confront it.** On Qwen3-8B LongBench the fixed mode gains **+0.72** while the per-key mode gains **+0.42** — the per-key mode is *worse*, and its CI crosses zero. On Llama LongBench it is +0.47 vs +0.86 and on RULER +2.5 vs +2.9. Given that per-key decisions cost 5% prefill and ~17% decode TPOT at batch 64, the engineering recommendation is arguably "use the fixed mode," yet the paper's narrative and abstract foreground the per-key path. The one place the per-key mode clearly earns its cost is per-token caches (+2.58 LongBench points over fixed after Holm), which is exactly the case where it is forced by the quantization axis — the paper would be stronger if it said so plainly.
3. **Gemma-3-27B is a post-hoc repair.** The per-layer check that fixes the one failing model was added *after* the regression was observed, and the twelve-model geometric mean only becomes 11.9 (from 12.3) with it. Reporting both numbers is honest, but it means the "12 models" claim is not a clean pre-registered result — it is 11 clean models plus one with a hand-installed guard.

**Baseline quality.** The KIVI-only baseline is exemplary: same kernel, same layout, same protection, flags forced to identity — a true ablation rather than a reimplementation race. RotateKV is handled more unevenly. Its *official* setting (W0) collapses by −12.67 points on Qwen3-8B and −7.26 on Llama in the HF study, so beating it is not informative; the meaningful comparison is clip 1.0, where FlagRot-KIVI wins on 9 of 12 models at the KL level but the task-level gap is only ~+0.5 points and is measured **in simulation only** ("RotateKV also through LongBench in simulation"), not in the served vLLM path where the paper's own numbers live. The served task comparison covers only KIVI and the per-token instance. This is a real asymmetry: the paper's strongest competitor is never run through the same serving stack as the proposed method.

**Missing ablations.** There is no task-level ablation isolating the flag overhead from the selection benefit (the KL-level analogue exists in Table 38's "static selection by true KL per layer" row at −42.9% versus FlagRot's −59.6%, but that oracle is never run through LongBench or RULER). There is no comparison against the obvious cheap alternative — a per-layer on/off decision, which the Gemma-3-27B fix effectively implements and which costs nothing at write time and no flag bits at read time. Given that this per-layer check is what rescues the failing model, it deserved to be a baseline everywhere rather than a patch in one appendix.

**Data quirks the paper works around rather than solves.** Qwen2.5-7B needs static key centering or its per-token 4-bit caches explode to KL 4697×10⁻³ (RTN) — and even with centering it is excluded from the WUSH-KV comparison because "all methods exceed 340." Its key-projection biases add a constant offset of hundreds to a few low-frequency channels, which sets the scale of every group containing them. FlagRot-auto's centering handles this in principle, but the paper applies centering *only* to Qwen2.5-7B and notes that on other models "its effect varies in sign," which means the automatic switch is doing per-model work that is not fully characterized. Similarly, FlagRot-auto needs at least two calibration windows (one window raises KL by 5.8%), a fragility that FlagRot-KIVI does not share.

**Significance.** In the 2-bit regime the case is solid: 34% KL reduction and a recoverable slice of a double-digit task loss at a byte-identical page size is a good trade, and the +18.5-point RULER gap over the equal-size per-token cache is the paper's most persuasive single number. At 4 bits the case largely evaporates: a 44–67% KL reduction that yields 34 of 36 task intervals containing zero is a fidelity improvement without a demonstrated capability improvement, and the paper says so rather than hiding it. The honest reading is that this is a 2-bit technique with a 4-bit side effect.

**Red flags.** None serious. The framing occasionally outruns the evidence ("below RotateKV on 9 of 12 models" is true at the KL level but the reader must go to Table 2 to learn that the served LongBench gains are +0.42 with a CI spanning zero), and the abstract's "+0.4 to +1.9 points on the three of four models where 2-bit KIVI loses accuracy" is a carefully hedged sentence that repays close reading — on two of those three the interval is not clearly above zero. But the paper volunteers its failures (Gemma-3-27B, Qwen3.8-27B, the 4-bit task null, the 33% decode penalty at batch 64) in the main text rather than burying them, which is the opposite of the usual failure mode.

## Strengths

- **The core empirical observation is checkable and surprising.** Figure 1a shows two 32-dim sub-vectors of the same Llama-3.1-8B key where H32 moves crest factor 2.0 → 3.5 (error ×1.52) in one case and 4.3 → 2.1 (error ×0.29) in the other. That single figure justifies the entire method, and the follow-up decile analysis (two thirds of the flattest tenth hurt, one in six of the spikiest) turns an anecdote into a distribution.
- **The sign-bit flag trick is the right kind of clever.** It solves the objection that per-key metadata would eat the bandwidth savings, and it is validated in a real vLLM plugin with paged INT2 storage, CUDA graphs, prefix caching, and a BF16 sink+recent window. The served-to-simulated KL ratio of 0.96–1.03 is the number that makes the simulation results credible.
- **The ablation isolates the right variable.** Showing that reconstruction-error selection is *worse than no selection* (19.1 vs 18.4) is more informative than any positive result in the paper, because it explains why a naive reader's first implementation would fail.
- **Evaluation breadth.** Twelve models for fidelity (eight held out, run once with frozen settings), fifteen for the 4-bit study, four for task accuracy across retrieval, long-doc QA, math reasoning, agentic tool use and SWE — plus calibration-transfer tests to five other text domains and a full closed-loop serving study with preemption counts.
- **Uncommon honesty.** The paper reports that BF16 preempts 566 of 641 requests at 256 agents while the INT2 caches preempt none, that FlagRot-auto completes simulations at 0.88× the BF16 rate (i.e. *slower*), and that at batch 64 the 27B cache is 33% slower per token than BF16. These are the numbers a marketing-driven paper would omit.

## Limitations

- **Task evaluation covers four models, all ≤32B, all dense at task level, on one inference engine (vLLM) and two GPU generations (H20, H200).** No MoE in the task study (Qwen3-30B-A3B appears only in the 4-bit fidelity table), no model above 32B, no TensorRT-LLM or SGLang. The 12-model fidelity cohort is broad but fidelity is not capability.
- **The per-key mode is not free and the fixed mode is often the better trade.** Per-key decisions cost 5% prefill throughput and push TPOT at batch 64 from 33.2 ms (KIVI) to 38.7 ms, a ~17% decode penalty, while the fixed mode captures 78% of the KL gain at zero write cost and, on Qwen3-8B LongBench, actually scores higher. The paper does not give a decision rule for when the per-key path is worth it.
- **The strongest competitor is never served.** RotateKV appears in the served comparison only through simulation; the vLLM task table compares against KIVI-only and FlagRot-auto. A reader cannot tell from this paper whether RotateKV at clip 1.0 in the same kernel would close the +2.9 RULER gap.
- **The one failing model required a post-hoc guard.** Gemma-3-27B's +15.6 KL regression (25.1 → 40.7) is fixed by a per-layer check added after the fact; the reported twelve-model mean of 11.9 depends on it, and the check's behaviour on models outside the cohort is untested.
- **Qwen2.5-7B needs a model-specific intervention (static key centering) to be usable at all**, and even then is excluded from one comparison table. The paper treats this as a known pathology of that model rather than a limitation of the method, which is defensible but leaves the "tuning-free" claim weaker than it sounds.
- **The 4-bit operating point delivers fidelity, not capability.** 44–67% KL reduction with 34 of 36 task intervals spanning zero means the practical benefit at 4 bits is essentially unmeasurable at these benchmark sizes.
- **Crest factor is an explanation, not a predictor.** The paper concedes that on NVFP4 the share of pieces hurt by rotation is 38–60% across *all* deciles of the crest factor, so the method must actually quantize three times per key rather than predict from statistics — which is precisely the cost the fixed mode exists to avoid.
- **No comparison to KV eviction/sparsification** (H2O, SnapKV, pyramid-drop style methods), which are the orthogonal competitor for the same memory budget. The paper is a quantizer paper and does not need to win that comparison, but a practitioner choosing a memory strategy gets no guidance.

## Relevance

这篇正好落在"极致性能优化下的可靠性"这条线上：它研究的不是"量化能省多少内存"，而是**低比特量化误差本身的来源与可控性**——Hadamard 旋转这一被当作默认正确做法的手段，会在平坦的 key 子向量上反而放大误差，而作者用 query 加权的误差度量把这件事变成了可判定的编码决策。对关心 FP4/INT2 数值误差机理、以及"量化后精度掉点到底掉在哪里"的读者，第 2 节的 crest factor 分析和表 6 的消融（用重构误差选反而比不选更差）比主结果更有价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. **Would a per-layer on/off decision capture most of the gain at zero cost?** The Gemma-3-27B fix shows that disabling selection in 27 of 62 layers helps; Table 38's oracle per-layer static selection reaches −42.9% versus FlagRot's −59.6% on INT4. Running that oracle through LongBench and RULER would tell us whether the 5 flag bits and the 5% prefill cost buy anything a per-layer bitmask could not.
2. **Why does the fixed mode beat the per-key mode on Qwen3-8B LongBench (+0.72 vs +0.42) while losing on Llama (+0.47 vs +0.86) and on KL (13.8 vs 12.4)?** Is this noise, or does per-key selection interact badly with a particular head geometry? The paper reports the per-key/fixed agreement rate (39–44% of keys versus 62–69% of KIVI groups) but never explains the task-level inversion.
3. **How does FlagRot behave under simultaneous weight quantization?** Everything here keeps linear layers in BF16. At W4A8KV4-style operating points the key distribution changes, and the crest-factor argument would need re-measurement.
4. **What happens at 64k–1M context?** The KL aging study stops at 32k (FlagRot-KIVI grows 1.4× from 2k to 32k, FlagRot-auto 3.5×). The per-channel layout ages better, but the crossover point where per-token selection stops paying for itself is not located.
5. **Can the selection criterion be made cheap enough to drop the triple-quantization?** Predicting the winner from cheap statistics (crest factor plus the top eigenvector of C_q) instead of quantizing three times would remove most of the write cost; the paper shows crest factor alone is insufficient on NVFP4, but a two-feature predictor is untested.
6. **Does the flag survive a different kernel stack?** The sign-bit trick depends on scales being strictly positive and on the page layout being under the authors' control. In TensorRT-LLM or a fused attention kernel with a different metadata layout, the zero-overhead claim may not transfer.
