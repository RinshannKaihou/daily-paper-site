> **一句话总结：** 作者把"两个 SAE 的 latent 是否是同一个特征"重新表述为一个带能量权重的熵正则最优传输问题——把每个 SAE 的归一化激活能量当作边缘分布、把指定的相似度几何（decoder 余弦 / 激活共现 / 一阶因果）当作代价，同一个传输计划同时给出字典级差异、该差异在各 latent 上的精确分解、以及每个 latent 的质量如何分配到对方；并给出考虑"代价与两侧边缘同源于同一批文档"的影响函数渐近推断。在 TinyStories-1M 上，五个 width-64 TopK SAE 的跨主题（animals vs family）平均传输代价差 Δ=0.02639（占 animals 均值 2.57%，95% CI [0.02277, 0.03002]），而同样数据上均匀质量 OT 只给出 +0.0014（z=1.5，区间含 0）、匈牙利匹配 z=1.1、decoder 余弦因为不看文档而给出严格 0 差异；但这一效应在 GPT-2 small / Pythia-160M / TinyStories-33M 上缩到 −2.3%~+2.1%，十种子区间在三个模型 width 3072 上全部含 0。

> **TL;DR:** The paper turns SAE dictionary comparison into one energy-weighted entropic optimal-transport plan: marginals are normalized activation energies, costs come from a chosen similarity kernel (decoder cosine, uncentered co-activation, or linearized causal effect), and the single plan yields a dictionary-level cost, an exact per-latent decomposition of that cost, and directional allocation fractions. On TinyStories-1M (five TopK SAEs, width 64, k=16, seeds 101–105, block-3 output, ε=0.30) the "family" vs "animals" mean-cost gap is Δ=0.02639 (2.57% of the animals mean, 95% CI [0.02277, 0.03002], all 10 seed pairs same sign), versus +0.0014 with z=1.5 for uniform-mass entropic OT on the identical plans, z=1.1 for Hungarian assignment, and exactly +0.0000 for decoder-cosine scores (which never look at documents); a 20-seed interval [0.02173, 0.03050] survives adding seed variance. The decomposition attributes 64% of Δ to changed energy weights (0.01680) and 36% to the changed cost matrix (0.00956). The effect does not transfer: across six larger model–width settings Δ spans −2.3% to +2.1% of the animals mean, and ten-seed intervals include zero for GPT-2, Pythia-160M and TinyStories-33M at width 3072 — only GPT-2 at width 6144 gives a clean +0.0050 [0.0033, 0.0067]. Most reusable findings: transported fractions are directional and graded (latent 21 of seed 104 sends 98.75% of its mass to latent 13 of seed 101, while that same mass is only 54.76% of latent 13's mass), and latents holding ~10% of tuning energy absorb 28–40% of evaluation cost.

## 核心贡献

- **一个计划，三种读数**：用归一化激活能量 a_j = E[h_j²]/ΣE[h²] 作为两侧边缘分布，在指定相似度几何下解熵正则 OT，得到 (i) 字典级差异 D_ε = ⟨c, P_ε⟩，(ii) 精确分解 D_ε = Σ_j a_j·d_j^loc（每个 latent 对同一总代价的贡献，不是另算一个指标），(iii) 方向性分配分数 T_jk = P_ε,jk/a_j 与组级 θ_{I,J}。这解决了"聚合分数无法定位分歧、逐对相似度无法反映全体 latent 如何竞争同一批对应物"的缺口。
- **三种相似度的几何统一**：把 decoder 余弦、非中心化共现 ρ_act、因果相似度 ŝ_caus 都写成某个 Hilbert 空间里单位向量的内积（Lemma 1，故 ‖Φ(j)−Φ(k)‖² = 2−2s），并证明 decoder 与激活相似度在精确重构下**互相不受约束**（Prop. 1）；在一阶近似下 slin_caus = ρ_act · cos_M(d_j, d_k)（Prop. 2），即因果相似度被共现率上界约束 |slin_caus| ≤ ρ_act（常 Jacobian 情形）。
- **反直觉的几何结论**：Prop. 5 构造出 decoder 余弦 ≥ 1−ε 的两个 latent 却有 slin_caus = −ρ_act < 0——当 Jacobian 湮灭它们的共同分量时，几乎平行的 decoder 可以有一阶效果相反。这直接说明"看 decoder 余弦判断是否同一特征"在某些 F 下会给出符号错误的结论。
- **同源估计的联合推断**：代价矩阵、两侧边缘、分母全部来自同一批文档，因此朴素地各自套 CI 会低估方差。作者给出显式影响函数、相合协方差估计、分数 θ 与代价 D 的 CLT（Thm. 1、Thm. 2），并处理 S 个 SAE 的 q=S(S−1)/2 个 pair 之间的相关性（同批文档导致的 Ω 非对角项），最后用联合文档 bootstrap 给出同时置信带与整个计划的 ℓ1 半径。
- **split / absorption 的传输解释**：在理想化模型里证明传输分数就是"分裂份额"（Prop. 3：P(j,k_i)=m_i/m 且 ρ_act(j,k_i)=m_i/m）与"被吸收能量比例"（Prop. 4：P₀ 的右上角元素恰为 φ·a_jgen，ε↓0 时熵解收敛到它），并给出代价扰动下计划变化的稳定性界（Thm. 3：KL ≤ 2δ²/ε²、‖π̂−π‖₁ ≤ 2δ/ε）与不等质量块间传输的界（Thm. 4）。
- **实证上的三个可复用发现**：(1) 跨种子差异是结构性的、可定位的（TinyStories-1M 上 91/120 个分数差异检验经 Bonferroni 校正后仍显著）；(2) 主题间的差异一半以上来自**能量权重**的变化而非代价矩阵（64% vs 36%），两者可以部分抵消，所以"总差异小"不等于"两项都小"；(3) 按 tuning 能量选的**低能量组**（约 10% 能量）在六个较大设置里吃掉 28–40% 的评估代价，高能量组（约 50% 能量）只占 16–29%——按能量剪枝或只评估头部 latent 会系统性地漏掉分歧所在。

## Key Contributions

- **One plan, three readings.** With marginals a_j = E[h_j²]/ΣE[h²] and an entropic plan P_ε, the paper gets a dictionary-level cost D_ε = ⟨c,P_ε⟩, an *exact* additive decomposition D_ε = Σ_j a_j d_j^loc into per-latent contributions, and directional fractions T_jk = P_ε,jk/a_j (plus group fractions θ_{I,J} and threshold fractions ι_δ). Equation (5) is the load-bearing identity: the same number used to compare two dictionaries is the sum of its own parts, so localization is not a separate heuristic.
- **Geometric unification of three similarity notions.** Decoder, activation and causal similarities are all Hilbert-space cosines of unit embeddings (Lemma 1), which gives the distance form ‖Φ(j)−Φ(k)‖² = 2−2s. Prop. 1 shows decoder and activation similarity are *mutually unconstrained* under exact reconstruction (s_dec=1 with ρ_act=0, and s_dec=0 with ρ_act=1), so "same direction" and "same firing pattern" are genuinely different questions.
- **Causal similarity factored and bounded.** Under a first-order ablation approximation, slin_caus = ρ_act·cos_M(d_j,d_k) with M = JᵀJ, hence |slin_caus| ≤ ρ_act for constant J and ≤ ρ_J for input-dependent J; Prop. 6 gives an explicit O(h²) remainder bound along the ablation path (with a nice counterexample showing the full-path Hessian assumption is necessary: F(z)=z+Kz³ at a=0 has F′=1, F″=0, yet the ablation remainder is −K).
- **Prop. 5: near-parallel decoders, opposite causal effect.** With d_j ∝ u+εv, d_k ∝ u−εv (s_dec ≥ 1−ε) and a Jacobian that annihilates u but not v, cos_M = −1 and slin_caus = −ρ_act < 0. A concrete warning against decoder-only feature matching.
- **Joint asymptotic inference from shared documents.** Influence functions for fractions (Thm. 1), a joint CLT over all q = S(S−1)/2 pair costs with a full covariance Ω that keeps the cross-pair dependence induced by shared documents (Thm. 2), a context-contrast interval for independent topic samples (Cor. 1), and a document-level bootstrap that resamples the *same* document indices for every pair to preserve dependence (Appendix C.2).
- **Transport interpretation of splitting and absorption.** Prop. 3: in the split model the plan is forced by the column marginals, P(j,k_i)=m_i/m, and ρ_act(j,k_i)=m_i/m, so the transported fraction literally equals the energy share. Prop. 4: in the proportional-amplitude absorption model the unique unregularized plan has upper-right entry φa_jgen, so the absorbed energy fraction is read off the plan as ε↓0.
- **Empirical decomposition of cross-seed and cross-context disagreement** on four models and seven model–width settings, with the weight/cost split (Δ = Δ_w + Δ_c), a uniform-vs-energy-weight ablation, a topic-word exclusion check, and an energy-stratified cost accounting.

## Methodology

**Setup.** For a fixed pretrained model and an activation site (residual stream, MLP out, attention-head out), two SAEs A and B of possibly different widths map a(x) to nonnegative codes h(x) and reconstruct â(x) = Dh(x)+b. A latent is the pair ℓ_j = (h_j, d_j). The comparison is deliberately *not* a matching: it is a mass-transport problem between two weighted point clouds in a chosen embedding space.

**Similarity kernels.** (i) decoder s_dec(j,k) = ⟨d_j,d_k⟩ with unit-norm columns (activations rescaled inversely to preserve reconstruction); (ii) activation ρ_act(j,k) = E[h_jh_k]/√(E[h_j²]E[h_k²]), uncentered, in [0,1] for nonnegative codes; (iii) causal ŝ_caus from the normalized inner product of ablation effects e_j(x) = F(a(x)−h_j(x)d_j) − F(a(x)) in L²(D;R^V); (iv) its linearization slin_caus using q_j = −h_jJ(x)d_j. Natural-language-explanation agreement is explicitly out of scope.

**Energy marginals.** w_j = E‖h_jd_j‖² = E[h_j²] is the latent's expected reconstruction energy; a_j = w_j/Σw normalizes it into a probability vector, and the "feature distribution" is μ_A = Σ_j a_j δ_{Φ(j)}. This is the key modeling choice: mass is *energy*, not count, so a rarely-firing but high-amplitude latent is not automatically minor.

**The plan.** P_ε = argmin_{P∈Π(a,b)} ⟨c,P⟩ + εKL(P‖a⊗b), solved by log-domain Sinkhorn. Costs are c_jk = ‖Φ(j)−Φ(k)‖², i.e. 2(1−s), so activation geometry lives in [0,2] and decoder/causal in [0,4]. Because the plan has both marginals fixed, T_jk = P_jk/a_j sums to 1 over k and satisfies Σ_j a_jT_jk = b_k, which is what makes both "how is j's mass split" and "how much of k's mass comes from j" well posed — and asymmetric.

**Decomposition.** d_j^loc = Σ_k T_jk c_jk is j's mean transport cost; D_ε = Σ_j a_j d_j^loc. The paper is careful that the split between j and k of the term c_jkP_jk is a *convention* (they use half-half for the latent-contribution figure), so per-latent attributions are only meaningful up to that convention.

**Contexts.** A context is an input distribution D_g (e.g. documents on one topic). SAEs, retained indices and ε are held fixed; weights, costs and plans are recomputed. For S ≥ 2 SAEs, M_g = (1/q)Σ_{s<t}D_ε(A_s,A_t;D_g) and Δ = M_g − M_h, tested against H₀: Δ=0; separately H₀: θ^(g)_{I,J} = θ^(h)_{I,J} for prespecified index sets.

**Inference.** A document X contributes U_j = L_X^{-1}Σ_t h_j(X,t)², V_k likewise, and cross-moments W_jk = L_X^{-1}Σ_t h_j h_k over its L_X retained positions; ϑ = E(U,V,vec W) determines weights, costs and the plan, and ϑ̂ is the document average. Assumption 2: documents i.i.d., E‖Y‖² < ∞, all retained energies strictly positive, dictionary sizes and ε fixed as N→∞ (finite fourth moments of activations suffice). Thm. 1 gives θ̂−θ = N^{-1}Σψ(X_i) + o_p(N^{-1/2}) with ψ = J_ε(Y−ϑ) and √N(θ̂−θ) ⇒ N(0,Σ); the variance therefore includes the denominator a(I_r), the costs, and both marginals. Thm. 2 does the same for the q-vector of pair costs with Ω = E[χχᵀ] retaining cross-pair correlation. Cor. 1 handles two independent topic samples with N_g/(N_g+N_h)→λ. The bootstrap resamples whole documents with identical indices across all pairs.

**Idealized models.** Splitting: parent latent with activation z is replaced by children z·1_{A_i} on a partition {A_i}, all sharing decoder d. Then every feasible plan has P(j,k_i) = m_i/m and the activation similarity equals the energy share, so a "fraction" here is not a soft score but an identity. Absorption: general latent (d_gen, h_gen) and specific latent (d_sp, h_sp) with d_gen ⊥ d_sp, S ⊆ G, h_gen = c·h_sp on S; SAE B keeps the general latent only off S and replaces the overlap with one latent with decoder (d_sp + c d_gen)/√(1+c²). With φ the absorbed energy fraction, the unique unregularized plan is [[(1−φ)a_jgen, φa_jgen],[0, a_jsp]].

**Experiments.** Models: TinyStories-1M (block 3, d=64), TinyStories-33M (block 1), GPT-2 small (block 6), Pythia-160M (block 6); widths 64 / 3072 / 6144; TopK SAEs (k=16 at width 64, k=32 or 24 at 3072/6144), unit-norm decoders, five seeds 101–105 (plus 106–120 for the 20-seed study), identical stories and minibatch streams within a setting, FP32 on an L4 for training/encoding and FP64 for moments/transport/influence. Evaluation topics are 768 held-out 128-token prefixes containing ≥2 words from a prespecified list (animals, family, food, toys, nature) plus 1,536 neutral stories; prefixes hitting both animals and family lists are excluded. Cost is c = 2(1−ρ_act); primary ε = 0.30 for the toy study, 0.10 for latent-level and larger-model analyses. The headline contrast is a *cross-seed* comparison: SAEs trained on identical data with different initializations are compared on the same documents, so any nonzero cost is pure non-identifiability, not a data difference.

## Results & Highlights

- **The toy-model contrast is real but small.** On TinyStories-1M at ε=0.30, the mean cross-seed transport cost is 1.05424 on family documents vs 1.02784 on animals, Δ = 0.02639 (2.57% of the animals mean), pointwise 95% CI [0.02277, 0.03002]; all 10 seed pairs have the same sign, and extending to 20 seeds gives Δ = 0.02612 with a combined document+seed interval [0.02173, 0.03050] that still excludes zero.
- **Baselines disagree, but not on the same hypothesis.** On the identical SAEs and documents (Table 5), uniform-mass entropic OT at ε=0.30 gives a family−animals difference of +0.0014 with bootstrap z=1.5 (CI [−0.0004, +0.0030]), Hungarian assignment z=1.1, nearest-neighbour shared fraction (≥0.7) z=6.9, and energy-weighted *unregularized* OT z=15.4 versus z=15.0 for the paper's entropic plan — i.e. the energy weighting, not the entropy, drives the signal. Decoder-based scores are exactly +0.0000 by construction (0.4080 on both topics), since they never see the documents.
- **The difference is mostly in the weights, not the geometry.** Δ decomposes as Δ_w = 0.01680 (64%) + Δ_c = 0.00956 (36%) = 0.02636, with both component CIs excluding zero; at ε=0.10 the weight term drops to 56%. In GPT-2 at width 3072 the two terms have *opposite* signs (+0.0027 cost vs −0.0029 weight) and partially cancel, which is exactly the case where a small total hides two large components.
- **Transport fractions are directional and graded.** Latent 21 of seed 104 and latent 13 of seed 101 both have top tokens {mom, dad, my}; on family documents at ε=0.10, P̂₂₁,₁₃/â₂₁ = 98.75% [98.29, 99.21] while P̂₂₁,₁₃/b̂₁₃ = 54.76% [53.24, 56.29]. Almost all of the first latent's mass goes to the second, but that same mass is only about half of the second's. Equality of transported fractions between topics is rejected in 91 of 120 tests after Bonferroni correction.
- **Low-energy latents carry disproportionate cost.** Across six model–width settings at ε=0.10, the group holding ~50% of tuning energy accounts for 16–29% of evaluation cost, while the group holding ~10% accounts for 28–40%; per-SAE cost-share/energy-share ratios are 2.4–4.5 for low-energy groups and 0.11–0.68 for high-energy groups. High-energy groups in the larger models fire on 192–256 of 256 tuning documents, often on punctuation and function words, and their contribution to Δ is negative in five of seven settings.
- **Larger models mostly wash out.** At ε=0.10 the six larger settings span −2.3% to +2.1% of the animals mean. Five-seed contrasts: GPT-2/6144 +0.0050 [0.0033, 0.0067] with 10/10 positive pairs; GPT-2/3072 −0.0003 [−0.0021, 0.0016]; Pythia/3072 −0.0026, Pythia/6144 −0.0011; TinyStories-33M −0.0007 (3072) and +0.0021 (6144). Ten-seed intervals at width 3072 include zero for all three models (+0.0004 [−0.0023, +0.0032], +0.0011 [−0.0014, +0.0035], −0.0016 [−0.0049, +0.0016]).
- **Anchoring the topic effect.** Against a neutral pool, family−neutral = +0.0093 [0.0064, 0.0122] but animals−neutral = **−0.0171** [−0.0205, −0.0137]: animals is *closer* to neutral than family, so the "family is harder" reading is not a generic topic effect. 11 of 15 Bonferroni contrasts exclude zero, and random halves of the neutral pool give |z|>1.96 in only 2 of 20 splits.
- **Regularization matters more than the headline admits.** Over ε ∈ {0.03, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.30, 0.60} the document-sampling CI for Δ excludes zero at every value, but the self-comparison diagonal mass falls from 1.000 (ε=0.03) to 0.998 (0.10), 0.688 (0.30) and 0.208 (0.60); correcting for self-comparison costs reduces Δ by about 75% at ε=0.60 and "little" at ε ≤ 0.30.
- **Calibration is honest, including where it fails.** Synthetic repeated sampling gives 94.5–95.5% coverage for nominal 95% simultaneous bands (bootstrap maxima) and 95.5–96.5% (multiplier maxima) at N = 64/256/1024; full-plan ℓ1 error scales as N^{-1/2} (0.0792 → 0.0408 → 0.0202) with radii 0.120 → 0.060 → 0.030. But in the rare-document regime coverage collapses to 0.275 / 0.720 / 0.850 / 0.935 at N = 64 / 256 / 1024 / 4096 — at N=64, 145 of 200 samples never draw the rare type and all 145 bands fail. Resampling the fixed TinyStories pools gives 91.5–97.0%.
- **Numerical reproducibility is checked.** Repeating the whole computation on a second GPU moves the cost matrices by at most 3·10⁻⁴; seed-level s.e. is 0.0038 (5 seeds) vs 0.0018 for document sampling at ε=0.30, falling to 0.00127 at 20 seeds.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The components are borrowed but the composition is new and non-obvious. Energy-weighted marginals on *both* sides, an entropic plan indexed by latent pairs, and the identity D_ε = Σ_j a_j d_j^loc give something the existing SAE-comparison toolbox lacks: a single object that is simultaneously a scalar, a decomposition, and a directional correspondence. Longon et al. (2026) use uniform-mass OT on SAE latents and Cao et al. (2026) use OT between activation-weighted hidden-state distributions per feature pair; this paper's contribution relative to both is the energy marginals plus the inference layer, and it says so. The genuinely novel theoretical content is Prop. 1 (decoder and activation similarity unconstrained), Prop. 5 (near-parallel decoders with opposite linearized causal effect), and the Thm. 4 block-transport bound with unequal masses — the last is a real technical result, not decoration.

**Rigor.** High on the statistics, thinner on the empirics. The influence-function derivation correctly accounts for the fact that costs *and* both marginals come from the same documents (the usual way to get this wrong is to bootstrap the plan and pretend the marginals are fixed); the joint Ω across q pairs handles the shared-document dependence; and the rare-document experiment is the kind of adversarial self-check most methods papers skip. The honesty is unusual: they report that ten-seed intervals include zero for all three larger models, that self-comparison costs erode Δ by 75% at ε=0.60, and that "asymptotic validity does not ensure accurate coverage when rare documents have a large effect."

**Weak points I would press on a reviewer's report.** (1) *The headline regularization and the interpretable one are different.* The paper's marquee number (Δ=0.02639) is at ε=0.30, where the self-comparison plan is only 68.8% diagonal — meaning that when a dictionary is transported to *itself*, 31% of its energy mass is shipped to other latents. Any per-latent "contribution" at that ε is therefore substantially an artifact of entropic smoothing, and the latent-level analyses quietly retreat to ε=0.10 (diagonal 0.998). The ε-grid (Table 4) shows Δ is stable (0.0214–0.0264) across a 20× range, which rescues the *scalar* claim, but not the localization claim. (2) *The baseline comparison is a horse race between different estimands.* Uniform-mass OT, Hungarian assignment and nearest-neighbour shared fraction all impose uniform marginals, i.e. they test a different null (permutation-like agreement) than the energy-weighted plan. The paper says this in one sentence ("their z-statistics do not compare statistical power for the same hypothesis"), yet the surrounding prose — "uniform weights 1.5 vs energy weights 15.0" — invites exactly the misreading. The honest framing would be that uniform-mass OT is *insensitive* to energy reallocation by construction, not that it is underpowered. (3) *Statistical significance is nearly free at this sample size.* With 768 documents × ~128 positions and document-level s.e. ≈ 0.0018, any nonzero population effect is significant; the interesting question is effect size, and 2.57% of the mean cost for a 64-latent toy dictionary, decaying to |Δ| ≤ 2.3% with mostly null intervals on real models, is a weak empirical payload for a 32-page methods paper. (4) *Five seeds for the larger models.* The jackknife uses a t₄ distribution and the paper itself shows the 5-seed s.e. (0.0038) is 3× the 20-seed s.e. (0.00127); the GPT-2/6144 result (+0.0050, 10/10 pairs) is exactly the size of that uncertainty, and the ten-seed rerun at width 3072 kills the analogous claim. (5) *The causal geometry — arguably the notion of "same feature" interpretability actually cares about — is never tested.* Props. 2, 5 and 6 are the most interesting theory in the paper, and the inference section explicitly excludes causal costs ("inference for causal costs requires a separate analysis"). So the empirical half covers only the activation-geometry slice, while the paper's title asks a question whose most natural answer (same causal role) is left on the table. (6) *No code.* Models are public and the recipe is stated in enough detail (2,048 stories, 4,000 Adam steps, batch 512, lr 3e-4, 100-step warmup, TopK k=16, seeds 101–120, FVU 0.1425–0.1467), but the log-domain Sinkhorn with 20,000 iterations, the adjoint solve, and the influence-function assembly are not trivial to reimplement, and a wrong adjoint silently produces plausible-looking CIs. For a paper whose product is an estimator, this is the largest reproducibility gap.

**Data quirks worth naming.** The uncentered co-activation ρ_act means a latent that fires on every token (punctuation, "the") co-activates with everything and gets high similarity to everything; the paper's own finding that high-energy groups in GPT-2/Pythia "often fire on punctuation and function words" and contribute *negatively* to Δ in five of seven settings suggests the metric is partly tracking token frequency structure rather than feature semantics. They report this descriptively but do not resolve it — a centered or IDF-weighted variant is the obvious ablation and is absent. Similarly, the topic construction is lexical (word lists), and while excluding topic-word positions (3.4% of positions) leaves 78% of Δ at ε=0.30 / 81% at ε=0.10, the surviving 78% is not shown to be semantic rather than distributional.

**Significance.** As a measurement methodology this is a real contribution and likely to be cited by anyone who needs to say "these two dictionaries differ by X, and latent j is responsible for Y% of X." As an empirical claim about SAE reproducibility it is thin: the one robust effect is in a 64-dimensional TinyStories model, and the paper's own ten-seed results on GPT-2/Pythia/TinyStories-33M are null. The paper is commendably explicit about that split, which is why I would trust the method more than the findings.

## Strengths

- The exact decomposition identity (Eq. 5) is the kind of thing that should have existed already: it makes "which latents explain the dictionary difference" a consequence of the same optimization rather than a second, differently-normalized score.
- The statistical treatment of shared-document dependence is correct and, in this literature, rare. Joint Ω across all S(S−1)/2 pairs plus a paired document bootstrap is the right answer, and the paper verifies coverage rather than asserting it.
- Directionality is treated as a feature, not a bug: the 98.75% / 54.76% example makes concrete that "correspondence" between SAE latents is a flow, not an edge in a matching, and that a latent can be almost fully absorbed into another while contributing only half of it.
- The energy-stratified cost accounting (10% of energy → 28–40% of cost; cost/energy ratio 2.4–4.5) is an immediately actionable caution for anyone pruning dictionaries by activation frequency or evaluating only high-energy latents.
- Prop. 1 and Prop. 5 are crisp, short, and change how one should read decoder-cosine matching tables — the latter is a constructive counterexample, not a hand-wave.
- Exceptional reporting discipline: negative results in three of four models, the rare-document coverage collapse, the ε=0.60 self-cost correction, the 3·10⁻⁴ GPU reproducibility delta, and the explicit statement that z-statistics across methods are not comparable.
- The idealized split/absorption models actually connect to the estimator (fraction = energy share; fraction = φ) instead of being decorative theory.

## Limitations

- **Scale.** The only robust positive result is TinyStories-1M with 64 latents at width 64. Widths 3072/6144 on d=768 models are tested, but there the effects are ≤2.3% of the mean cost and mostly null at ten seeds. Nothing is said about production dictionaries (16k–1M latents), where the cross-moment matrix W is m_A×m_B and the influence-function Jacobian is correspondingly huge.
- **Only activation geometry is validated empirically.** Decoder geometry appears only as a baseline score, and causal geometry not at all; inference for causal costs is explicitly deferred. The paper's central question is therefore answered empirically for one of its three candidate notions of "same feature."
- **ε is a free parameter with large effects.** Self-comparison diagonal mass moves from 1.000 to 0.208 across the tested grid, and the latent-level conclusions are computed at a different ε (0.10) than the headline (0.30). There is no data-driven rule for choosing ε, only a sensitivity table.
- **Conditional inference.** All guarantees condition on the trained SAEs; seed variation is handled by a 5-seed jackknife with a t₄ reference, which is about as weak as a variance estimate can be and is only repaired at TinyStories-1M with 20 seeds.
- **i.i.d. documents.** The theory assumes document-level independence and positive retained energies; the rare-document experiment shows coverage can fall to 0.275, and real evaluation corpora are fixed pools, not i.i.d. draws (they check this, but only for the TinyStories pools).
- **Fixed dictionary size and fixed index sets.** Index sets (I_r, J_r) must be prespecified or chosen on independent data; the framework does not discover *which* groups matter, and selection on the same documents invalidates the intervals.
- **Uncentered moments.** ρ_act rewards high-frequency latents; no centered/IDF-weighted variant is reported, and the high-energy groups' negative contributions suggest this is not a purely theoretical concern.
- **No released code**, and the numerical machinery (log-domain Sinkhorn to 10⁻¹⁰ marginal tolerance, adjoint solves with condition numbers 22–99) is delicate enough that independent reimplementation is a real barrier.
- **TopK SAEs only.** JumpReLU, Matryoshka, crosscoders, and multi-dimensional features are not covered; the framework assumes one latent = one direction with a scalar code.

## Relevance

直接命中"LLM Hidden States & Interpretability"：它给 SAE 比较提供了一个可分解、可做置信区间的最优传输度量，正好补上"两个字典到底差在哪、差异算不算采样噪声"这个长期靠 decoder 余弦拍脑袋的环节。对做 SAE 可复现性、跨种子/跨宽度特征对应、以及"低能量 latent 是否值得评估"的人来说，第 5 节的能量分层结论（10% 能量承担 28–40% 代价）和 98.75%/54.76% 的方向性例子比方法本身更快能用上。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. How does the estimator scale to Gemma Scope–sized dictionaries? The moment vector contains all m_A×m_B cross-moments W_jk; at width 6144 that is ~3.8×10⁷ entries per pair, so either sparsity is exploited (most latents never co-fire) or the influence-function Jacobian is computed matrix-free. Neither is described, and this determines whether the method is usable outside toy widths.
2. Would a centered or IDF-weighted co-activation change the low-energy-group finding? If the 28–40% cost share of low-energy latents is driven by rare-but-idiosyncratic tokens, centering should shrink it; if it survives, the case for evaluating the tail is much stronger.
3. Does the causal geometry reorder the top-contributing latents? Prop. 5 says signs can flip for near-parallel decoders under a Jacobian that annihilates the shared component — so the natural experiment is to recompute Figure 2's ranking with c = 2(1−ŝ_caus) on GPT-2 and see whether the same latents are implicated.
4. What happens at ε→0 with the self-comparison correction? The 75% reduction at ε=0.60 suggests the self-plan's off-diagonal mass is absorbing a large share of the measured contrast; an explicit "excess cost over self-cost" estimand (D(A,B) − ½[D(A,A)+D(B,B)]) with its own influence function would be cleaner than a post-hoc correction.
5. Can the plan be used as a training signal? The natural follow-up to Jin et al.'s C2R is a consistency regularizer that penalizes D_ε between a student SAE and a frozen teacher — the decomposition would then say which latents the regularizer is failing to align.
6. How sensitive are the transported fractions to the choice of Φ for the *same* latent pair? The 98.75%/54.76% example is computed under activation geometry; reporting the same pair under decoder and linearized-causal geometry would quantify how much "same feature" depends on the geometry rather than on the data.
