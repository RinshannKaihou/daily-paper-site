> **一句话总结：** 作者把一个 159,492 参数的 1D CNN 放在 Hang-Time HAR 篮球腕部加速度数据上、按运动员分组的五折交叉验证里，逐折对比 FP32/FP16/INT8/MXFP4/NVFP4：实测 TFLite FP16 与 INT8 相对 FP32 的 macro-F1（0.6926）最多只掉 0.16 个百分点，而模拟的 4-bit 权重+激活下 MXFP4 掉 5.14 点、NVFP4 只掉 0.75 点；2×3 消融（块大小 × scale 规则）把差距主要归因于 MXFP4 参考转换"scale 向下取整"造成的块内裁剪——改成向上取整可回收 3.18（块 32）/3.63（块 16）点，再把 E8M0 换成 E4M3 scale 又加 0.84/1.20 点，块大小本身影响很小。

> **TL;DR:** A careful, deliberately small-scale measurement study: one 159K-parameter 1D CNN, four basketball actions, 24 players, 5-fold GroupKFold so every player is tested once by a model that never saw them. On Hang-Time HAR the FP32 model reaches 0.6926 macro-F1; real TFLite FP16 and full-integer INT8 lose at most 0.16 points (INT8: 0.6910, −0.0016, lower in 5/5 folds, file 630.9 → 182.8 KiB), which is essentially free. Simulated W4 costs 1.45 points for MXFP4 (0.6782) and 0.72 for NVFP4 (0.6854), but the two are not consistently ordered (NVFP4 wins only 4/5 folds). Add 4-bit activations and the gap opens: MXFP4 drops to 0.6412 (−5.14 points, lower in 5/5 folds, 7.29% of predictions changed) while NVFP4 holds 0.6851 (−0.75, only 4/5 folds lower); NVFP4 beats MXFP4 by +0.0439 in all five folds. The ablation says the culprit is not block size but the E8M0 round-down scale rule of the reference MXFP4 conversion, which clips the largest value in 43% of weight blocks; rounding up recovers +3.18/+3.63 points, an E4M3 scale adds +0.84/+1.20, and at MXFP4's own 4.25 bits a block-32 E4M3 variant reaches −0.0112 — statistically indistinguishable from NVFP4 at 4.5 bits. Everything 4-bit is fake quantization with no speed or energy numbers, and fold 4 alone supplies most of MXFP4's headline loss.

## 核心贡献

- **首次把 MXFP4/NVFP4 放到可穿戴 HAR 上量一遍。** 已有的 FP4 评测要么是通用深度学习 benchmark，要么是 LLM 训练；作者指出尚无工作测量这两种 4-bit 块缩放格式对小型传感器 CNN 的精度代价，并用 159,492 参数、4 类动作、24 名运动员的设定把这个空白补上。
- **严格配对、按运动员留出的评测协议。** 5-fold GroupKFold 按球员分组（每折 4–5 名测试球员），五种格式共用同样五个训练好的模型、同样五个测试折，只报逐折配对差值，并把"五折同号"作为一致性判据；同时明确承认五折同号在无效应下的偶然概率是 0.0625，因此只当作中等强度证据。
- **把格式差异拆成可归因的设计维度。** 2×3 消融（块大小 {32,16} × scale 规则 {E8M0 向下取整, E8M0 向上取整, E4M3+张量 scale}）把 MXFP4 与 NVFP4 的差距分解为"如何处理被裁剪的块"（占 79.1%/75.1%）与"scale 用 8-bit 浮点还是 2 的幂"两步，并指出块大小在另两种规则下没有一致效应。
- **量化误差的分布性证据，而不只是均值。** 除 macro-F1 外还报"与 FP32 预测相同的窗口比例"（MXFP4 W4A4 改动 7.29%，NVFP4 3.12%）和逐类 F1，显示 MXFP4 主要伤害稀有动作：layup F1 从 0.539 掉到 0.455、pass 从 0.582 掉到 0.516，而 NVFP4 保持 0.524 与 0.575。
- **一套可复用的正确性自检。** 逐层前向在"不量化"时与训练模型预测完全一致；独立用 NumPy 重写的第二套前向对 FP32 与 MXFP4 完全一致、对 NVFP4 在 75,865 个窗口中 75,864 个一致；8-bit 处模拟 W8A8（0.6930）与真实 TFLite full-integer INT8（0.6910）相差 0.0020，用来说明模拟的乐观幅度；另用优化器 step 计数验证 early stopping 只用了内层训练球员。
- **明确的边界声明。** 4-bit 全部是 fake quantization，没有速度与能耗数字（所用 CPU 无 4-bit 浮点算术）；延迟在共享云 VM 上跨会话波动 11.6–24.6%，大于文件间 7.4% 的差异，因此作者拒绝做任何速度声明。

## Key Contributions

- **A domain gap filled, narrowly and honestly.** The paper's stated novelty is not a new format or method but the first measurement of MXFP4 and NVFP4 on a wearable activity-recognition CNN. Given that FP4 evaluation literature is dominated by LLM pretraining and generic vision benchmarks, the claim of a gap is credible, and the paper does not inflate it into a methods contribution.
- **A paired, player-grouped protocol that avoids the field's standard leakage.** Consecutive windows overlap by 80%, so random splits put near-duplicates of test windows into training. The authors demonstrate the size of that effect directly: a random forest on 18 hand-crafted features scores 0.608 pooled under player-grouped splitting but 0.753 under a random split — a 14.5-point inflation from leakage alone.
- **A decomposition of the MXFP4/NVFP4 gap into conversion-recipe choices.** Rounding the E8M0 block scale up instead of down (a recipe the MX specification permits) recovers +3.18 points at block 32 and +3.63 at block 16, in all five folds; swapping the power-of-two scale for an E4M3 scale adds a further +0.84 and +1.20. Block size contributes only −0.0044, and only under the round-down rule.
- **A bits-for-accuracy accounting.** At MXFP4's 4.25 bits/weight, a block-32 E4M3 variant lands at −0.0112 versus NVFP4's −0.0075 at 4.5 bits, and the ordering flips across folds (2 higher, 3 lower). The extra 0.25 bit NVFP4 spends therefore buys no consistent accuracy here — a useful negative result for anyone choosing a deployment format.
- **Evidence that 8-bit is still the right answer for this class of model.** FP16 (323.8 KiB, −0.0001) and dynamic-range INT8 (181.0 KiB, −0.0003) are within noise of FP32, and full-integer INT8 costs 0.16 points for a 3.5× file reduction. For a 159K-parameter model, the 4-bit formats save roughly another 90 KiB over INT8 — 182.8 → 90.8 KiB — at a cost that ranges from negligible (NVFP4) to severe (MXFP4 W4A4).

## Methodology

**Data.** Hang-Time HAR provides wrist-accelerometer recordings at roughly 50 Hz from 24 basketball players on two teams, labeled from video. The authors keep three acceleration channels and four ball actions (dribbling, shot, pass, layup), identifying players by recording file because the same four-character code can appear on both teams. Windows are 1 s (50 samples) with a 0.2 s (10 sample) step, giving 80% overlap and 118,823 windows; each window takes the label of its center sample and is retained only if at least half its samples carry that label. Because dribbling would otherwise dominate, it is capped at 3× the shot count with a fixed seed, leaving 75,865 windows (45,699 dribbling, 15,233 shot, 8,657 pass, 6,276 layup) and dropping dribbling's share from 74.61% to 60.24%. The cap is applied before splitting, so test folds inherit it; absolute scores therefore describe this capped distribution. The 1 s length is justified by the shortest action (pass, median 1.20 s); the 0.2 s step and the cap factor of 3 are fixed pipeline settings that were not varied.

**Evaluation.** 5-fold GroupKFold by player, each player tested exactly once by a model that never saw them, 4–5 test players per fold. The metric is macro-F1 (mean of the four per-class F1s) on a 0–1 scale, so 0.01 is one percentage point. Always predicting "dribbling" yields 0.188, which calibrates how much of the score is class imbalance rather than skill. All formats are scored by the same five trained models on the same five folds; differences are reported as paired per-fold deltas, and a difference is called consistent only if it has the same sign in all five folds. Spreads are population standard deviations over folds. The authors explicitly avoid comparing overlapping error bars because K-fold folds share training data and have no unbiased variance estimator (citing Bengio & Grandvalet).

**Model.** A 1D CNN with three convolution blocks of 64/128/256 filters, each block being convolution → ReLU → batch norm → max pooling → dropout in that order, followed by global average pooling, a 128-unit dense layer and a 4-way softmax. 159,492 values total: 158,596 trainable parameters plus 896 batch-norm running statistics. Training uses Adam, batch 256, up to 25 epochs, early stopping with patience 5 monitoring roughly 20% of the *training* players — never the test players, since that would leak test information into model selection.

**Measured deployable formats.** Each fold's model is converted to TFLite four ways: FP32; FP16 weights; dynamic-range INT8 (INT8 weights, float activations, with kernels quantizing activations on the fly); and full-integer INT8 with ranges calibrated on 500 training windows. All run in the TensorFlow 2.21.0 TFLite interpreter. Latency is the median of 5 interleaved rounds of 2,000 single-window runs at 1 thread on a Colab CPU VM (Intel Xeon @ 2.20 GHz, KVM, 2 vCPUs = 1 core × 2 threads).

**Simulated 4-bit formats.** Values are rounded to each format and back to FP32 (fake quantization), then the network runs as usual — this reproduces rounding error and storage but not speed or energy, which is necessary because the available CPUs have no 4-bit floating-point arithmetic. Weights of every Conv1D and Dense layer, including first and last, are blocked along each output channel's inputs (the multiply-add direction); biases and batch-norm parameters stay FP32. For W4A4 the input of every Conv1D and Dense layer is also quantized, blocked along the channel axis at each time step, with activation scales recomputed per window. Ties are broken toward the smaller magnitude in the main configuration (the OCP-required ties-to-even is tested as a sensitivity check). MXFP4 is simulated as block 32 with an E8M0 scale rounded down — the reference conversion of the specification — and NVFP4 as block 16 with an E4M3 block scale times a per-tensor FP32 scale of max|x|/(6×448). Symmetric INT8 and INT4 are included for reference using one scale per output channel for weights and one per window for activations, which the authors flag as much coarser than 16- or 32-value blocks.

**Ablation.** Block size {32, 16} crossed with three scale rules: E8M0 rounded down (MX rule), E8M0 rounded up (no clipping), and E4M3 with a tensor scale (NV rule), holding the 4-bit elements fixed throughout.

**Verification.** Three independent checks: (i) the layer-by-layer forward pass with nothing rounded reproduces the trained models' predictions exactly; (ii) a second forward pass written independently in NumPy without TensorFlow reproduces the simulated predictions exactly for FP32 and MXFP4, and for 75,864 of 75,865 windows for NVFP4; (iii) saved optimizer step counts are exact multiples of batches-per-epoch on the inner-training players (12, 16, 16, 13, 7 epochs), confirming early stopping used only inner validation players. A fourth check at 8 bits compares simulated W8A8 (0.6930) against real full-integer TFLite (0.6910) to bound simulator optimism.

## Results & Highlights

- **The 8-bit formats are effectively free on Hang-Time HAR.** FP32 reaches macro-F1 0.6926 ± 0.0494 over the five player-grouped folds; measured TFLite FP16 scores 0.6925 (Δ = −0.0001, lower in 3/5 folds) and full-integer INT8 scores 0.6910 (Δ = −0.0016, lower in 5/5 folds) while the file shrinks from 630.9 KiB to 182.8 KiB and agrees with FP32 on 98.68% of windows. Dynamic-range INT8 sits at 0.6924 (Δ = −0.0003). Against the 0.188 always-dribbling baseline, this is a 3.7× gap, so the task is genuinely learned — but the 8-bit cost, at most 0.16 points, is smaller than the fold-to-fold spread (sd 0.0494).
- **4-bit weights alone already separate the formats, but not consistently from each other.** MXFP4 W4 scores 0.6782 (Δ = −0.0145, lower in 5/5 folds) and NVFP4 W4 scores 0.6854 (Δ = −0.0072, lower in 5/5 folds), yet NVFP4 beats MXFP4 in only 4 of 5 folds, so the weight-only comparison does not establish a consistent ordering. Both are worse than simulated INT4 with per-channel scales (0.6921, Δ = −0.0006, direction flips across folds) — an inversion the authors attribute to INT4's finer per-channel weight scales rather than to the integer format being better.
- **Adding 4-bit activations is where MXFP4 breaks and NVFP4 does not.** MXFP4 W4A4 falls to 0.6412 (Δ = −0.0514, lower in 5/5 folds) and changes the prediction for 7.29% of windows, while NVFP4 W4A4 holds 0.6851 (Δ = −0.0075, lower in only 4/5 folds) and changes 3.12%. NVFP4 exceeds MXFP4 in all five folds by +0.0439 on average. For contrast, INT4 W4A4 collapses to 0.5493 (Δ = −0.1433, 5/5 lower) — but with one activation scale per window, which the authors correctly label as not like-for-like.
- **The damage concentrates on rare classes.** Under MXFP4 W4A4, layup F1 drops from 0.539 to 0.455 and pass F1 from 0.582 to 0.516, while NVFP4 keeps them at 0.524 and 0.575. Since layup has only 6,276 of 75,865 windows, a mean macro-F1 number understates the practical cost: the minority classes a coach would care about most are the ones that degrade first.
- **The ablation localizes the loss to the scale rule, not the block size.** Replacing the MX rule with the NV rule improves W4A4 macro-F1 by +0.0442 in all five folds. Rounding the E8M0 scale up instead of down contributes +0.0318 (block 32) and +0.0363 (block 16), both consistent in 5/5 folds; switching to an E4M3 scale adds +0.0084 and +0.0120, also 5/5. That makes 79.1% (block 32) and 75.1% (block 16) of the MX-to-NV gap a matter of how clipped blocks are handled. Clipping is not a corner case: in the fold-0 model the round-down rule clips the largest weight in 2,129 of 4,944 weight blocks (43%), against an expected 41.5% if block maxima were log-uniformly distributed.
- **Block size barely matters, and only under the round-down rule.** Under E8M0-down, 16-value blocks are worse than 32-value blocks in all five folds by 0.0044; under the round-up and E4M3 rules there is no consistent effect. This is consistent with the clipping story (twice as many blocks means twice as many clipped maxima) but the authors state the mechanism is untested.
- **The ranking survives both simulator sensitivity checks.** With ties rounded to even (12,702 of 11,379,750 input values are exact ties, 0.11%), 31 MXFP4 and 1 NVFP4 predictions change and no mean moves by more than 0.0001. With first-layer activation blocks sharing one scale per 15-value patch, MXFP4 improves by 0.0069 (5/5 folds) to −0.0444 and NVFP4 worsens by 0.0020 to −0.0095 (now 5/5 lower, i.e. a consistent loss), narrowing the gap to +0.0349 — but NVFP4 still leads in all five folds.
- **Bits-for-accuracy: NVFP4's extra 0.25 bit does not buy a consistent win.** At MXFP4's 4.25 bits/weight, block-32 E4M3 scales give −0.0112, versus NVFP4's −0.0075 at 4.5 bits; the variant is higher in two folds and lower in three, so the difference is within fold noise.
- **The CNN beats a hand-crafted-feature baseline, and the gap is a leakage lesson.** A random forest on 18 hand-crafted window features reaches 0.608 pooled, and the CNN beats it in all five folds by +0.0841 on average. Re-splitting the same forest's data at random instead of by player raises it to 0.753 — a 14.5-point inflation that quantifies exactly the evaluation error the paper's protocol is designed to avoid.

## Authors & Credibility

Megan Healy and Hossein Valavi, Princeton University. This is a two-author measurement paper with no stated funding, no industry affiliation, and no benchmark-leaderboard stake; the framing throughout is "here is what our evidence does and does not support," which is unusually disciplined. The methodological apparatus is stronger than the venue-typical norm for a small study: an independent NumPy reimplementation of the forward pass, a cross-check of simulated versus real 8-bit results to bound simulator optimism, a checkpoint-provenance check that optimizer step counts are exact multiples of batches-per-epoch, per-fold reporting rather than aggregate error bars, and explicit citation of the statistics literature (Bengio & Grandvalet) for why K-fold variance is not estimable. The generative-AI disclosure is specific about what Claude wrote (analysis and verification code, the NumPy pass, the paired statistics, the sensitivity reruns, the figure scripts) versus what the authors designed (question, data, model, training, number formats, ablation), and the paper states that an automated notebook cell verifies every reported number against program output. That is a materially better provenance story than most papers offer. The main credibility limit is scope, not honesty: two authors, one dataset, one model, five folds.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty is deliberately thin, and the paper says so.** There is no new format, model, or training method here — the contribution is a measurement in a domain nobody had measured. That is a legitimate contribution, but it means the paper's value scales with how surprising the measurement is, and the central finding (the round-down MX scale rule clips block maxima and costs accuracy) is an explicit replication of Yang et al. 2025's LLM-training result in a new setting; the authors cite this rather than claiming discovery. The genuinely new empirical content is narrower than the title suggests: it is mostly "the same scale-rounding effect also appears in a 159K-parameter HAR CNN."

**The headline "MXFP4 loses 5.14 points" is largely an artifact of one conversion recipe, and the framing under-weights that.** The abstract leads with a format-level contrast before revealing that the MXFP4 baseline is a specific round-down conversion the specification merely permits. The paper's own ablation shows the honest comparison: a block-32 E4M3 variant at MXFP4's 4.25 bits reaches −0.0112 versus NVFP4's −0.0075 at 4.5 bits, a 0.37-point difference that flips sign across folds. Read that way, "MXFP4 versus NVFP4" is not a format contest at all — it is a contest between a good and a bad scale-selection rule, plus a 0.25-bit budget difference. The authors do state this in the conclusion, but a reader who stops at the abstract will come away with a stronger format ranking than the evidence supports.

**One fold drives the MXFP4 mean.** Fold 4 contributes −0.1105 for MXFP4 W4A4, against −0.0265 to −0.0448 in the other four folds; for W4 it is −0.0376 versus −0.0034 to −0.0140. The paper is commendably transparent that fold 4's model also scored lowest on its own training players (0.705 vs 0.824–0.856), stopped after 7 epochs, and therefore restored weights from epoch 2. But that transparency does not repair the statistic: the headline −0.0514 is roughly 2.5× the typical fold's loss, and a reader should treat ~−0.03 to −0.045 as the more representative MXFP4 W4A4 penalty. Reporting a median fold or a fold-4-excluded sensitivity number would have been cheap and would have made the claim more defensible.

**Five folds cannot support the confidence the language implies.** The authors state the arithmetic correctly — if a format had no effect, all five folds would agree in sign with probability 0.0625 — and they call 5/5 results "moderate evidence." But 0.0625 is a per-comparison floor, and the paper makes on the order of a dozen such comparisons (six formats × two weight/activation settings, plus six ablation cells and three sensitivity variants), so a 5/5 agreement somewhere in that grid is close to expected under the null. No multiplicity correction or held-out replication is offered. The paired design and shared models are the right instinct; the inferential scaffolding around them is thinner than the number of claims being made.

**The INT4 baseline is a strawman for integer formats.** INT4 W4A4 at 0.5493 is presented as a −14.33-point collapse, and the paper does flag that it uses one scale per window rather than 16- or 32-value blocks. But that single sentence carries a lot of weight: a blocked INT4 baseline would likely land near the FP4 formats, and its absence means the table cannot answer the question a deployment engineer actually asks — "is 4-bit integer or 4-bit float better at equal blocking?" The authors list it as future work, which is honest, but it also means the INT4 row should not be read as a format verdict.

**No quantization-aware training, so every 4-bit number is a post-training-quantization upper bound.** The paper notes QAT "typically recovers part of the low-bit loss" but does not measure it. For MXFP4 W4A4 at −5.14 points this matters enormously: the practical question is whether QAT closes the gap to NVFP4, and the paper cannot say. Since the scale-rule diagnosis suggests the loss is a fixable conversion artifact rather than an inherent format limit, a QAT run (or even a scale-search calibration) is the experiment that would settle the deployment recommendation.

**The motivation is memory and energy, but only memory is measured.** The opening argument is that on-device inference saves battery and radio bandwidth, and that 4-bit numbers reduce storage *and memory traffic*. Storage is reported (90.8 vs 95.6 KiB); memory traffic, latency, and energy for the FP4 formats are not, because the available CPUs have no 4-bit arithmetic. The authors are explicit and refuse to claim speed from latency measurements that vary 11.6–24.6% between VM sessions — that refusal is exactly right — but it leaves the paper answering only the accuracy half of "what does 4-bit cost." A 159K-parameter model at 90 KiB is not obviously memory-bound in the first place, which weakens the premise that 4-bit is needed for this workload at all.

**Scope is narrow in ways that limit generalization.** One CNN architecture, one dataset, accelerometer only (no gyroscope), basketball standing in for ice hockey, and — critically — test players drawn from the same games and sessions as training players. Cross-team, cross-season, and cross-sensor-placement generalization are untested, and the 24-player, 4-class, 75,865-window setup is small enough that per-class F1 for layup rests on 6,276 windows spread over five folds. The choice of Hang-Time over the actual hockey dataset (Vleugels et al., 5 players, ~72 min) is well justified — five players cannot support a five-fold player-grouped split — but it means the title's "wearable activity recognizer" is doing more generalization work than the evidence covers.

**A minor but telling slip.** Section 3 writes the null sign-agreement probability as "2/25 = 0.0625"; the correct expression is 2/2⁵ = 0.0625. The number is right, the notation is not, and it sits in the paper's central statistics paragraph — a small sign that the statistical reasoning, while sound in substance, did not get the same scrutiny as the numerics.

## Strengths

- **The evaluation protocol is the paper's real contribution.** Player-grouped 5-fold with every player tested exactly once, identical models across formats, paired per-fold deltas, and a demonstrated leakage effect (random forest 0.608 → 0.753 when the split is randomized) that shows why the protocol matters. Many HAR quantization papers still report random-split numbers.
- **The ablation answers the "why," not just the "how much."** Decomposing the MX-to-NV gap into round-up (+3.18/+3.63) and E4M3 scale (+0.84/+1.20), with the 79.1%/75.1% attribution and the 43%-versus-41.5% clipping-rate sanity check, converts a format ranking into an actionable conversion recipe.
- **Reporting practices that most papers skip.** Per-fold results plotted alongside means, the "same answer as FP32" agreement rate, per-class F1 for rare actions, and explicit counts of how many folds moved in which direction. The fold-4 anomaly is disclosed rather than buried.
- **Simulator validation against real hardware at 8 bits.** Simulated W8A8 (0.6930) versus real full-integer TFLite (0.6910) gives a concrete, if small, bound on how optimistic the fake-quantization numbers may be — and the authors flag that real hardware fixes activation scales from calibration data while their simulator recomputes them per window, which is the right direction of concern.
- **Refusal to overclaim on speed.** Latency differences of 7.4% within a session are smaller than 11.6–24.6% between sessions, so the paper makes no speed claim at all. This is the correct call and rarer than it should be.
- **A genuinely useful negative result on bit budget.** At 4.25 bits with E4M3 scales, the block-32 variant is statistically indistinguishable from NVFP4 at 4.5 bits — so the extra quarter-bit buys nothing consistent here. That is the kind of finding a deployment team can act on.
- **Transparent AI-use and provenance disclosure**, including which code Claude wrote and an automated notebook cell that checks every reported number against program output.

## Limitations

- **Single model, single dataset, single sensor modality.** One 159K-parameter 1D CNN, one accelerometer dataset, four classes, basketball as a proxy for hockey. No gyroscope, no cross-team or cross-season test, no architecture sweep — so the ranking may not transfer to a larger CNN, a transformer, or a different sensor placement.
- **Test players come from the same games and sessions as training players**, so the paper measures player-independence but not session-, team-, or season-independence. Distribution shift across those axes is exactly where quantization error often amplifies.
- **All 4-bit results are simulated** (fake quantization, arithmetic in FP32). Accuracy and storage are captured; speed, memory traffic, and energy are not. The authors note that real hardware typically fixes activation scales from calibration data rather than recomputing them per window, so the simulation is plausibly optimistic — and their own 8-bit check shows simulated W8A8 beating real TFLite by 0.0020 in all five folds.
- **No quantization-aware training.** Every 4-bit number is a PTQ upper bound on the loss; QAT typically recovers part of it, and the paper does not measure how much.
- **The MXFP4 baseline uses one specific conversion recipe** (E8M0 round-down), which the ablation shows is the dominant source of its loss. The format-level comparison in the abstract is therefore weaker than the format-level comparison in the ablation table.
- **Five folds, no significance testing, no multiplicity control.** 5/5 sign agreement has a 0.0625 chance under the null for a single comparison, and the paper makes many comparisons.
- **Fold 4 dominates the MXFP4 mean** (−0.1105 vs −0.0265 to −0.0448 elsewhere), and its model trained atypically (7 epochs, weights restored from epoch 2). No median-fold or leave-one-fold-out statistic is reported.
- **INT4 and INT8 use per-channel/per-window scales**, much coarser than 16- or 32-value blocks, so the INT4 W4A4 row is not a fair integer-versus-float comparison; a blocked INT4 baseline is explicitly deferred.
- **Latency measured on a shared Colab VM** with 2 vCPUs, where session-to-session variation exceeds the between-file differences — so no deployment latency guidance is possible.

## Relevance

直接命中 "Reliability under Extreme Performance Optimization" 这条主线：它量化的是低精度数值格式（MXFP4/NVFP4 块缩放）在推理侧引入的精度退化，并且把退化归因到具体的数值机制——scale 向下取整导致块内最大值被裁剪（43% 的权重块受影响），而不是笼统地归给"4-bit 精度不够"。对关心量化误差来源、块缩放格式设计与部署选型的人来说，这篇的 2×3 消融和逐折配对协议都可直接复用；它同时是一个很好的方法论样本：如何在只有五折的小规模设定下诚实地报告"一致但非显著"的结论。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Would quantization-aware training or a calibration-time scale search close the MXFP4 W4A4 gap to NVFP4 entirely? If the loss is a conversion artifact rather than a format limit, QAT should largely erase it — and that would change the deployment recommendation from "use NVFP4" to "fix your MX conversion."
2. How much of the −5.14-point MXFP4 loss is fold 4? A median-fold or leave-one-fold-out statistic would tell us whether the representative penalty is ~−0.03 or ~−0.05, and the paper already has the per-fold data to compute it.
3. Does the ranking hold on the actual ice-hockey data (Vleugels et al., stick-mounted IMU, six activities, five players)? The authors plan this, but with five players a player-grouped split degenerates to one test player per fold — what evaluation protocol would be honest there?
4. What happens with a blocked INT4 baseline (16- or 32-value blocks, matching the FP4 blocking)? This is the missing cell that would make the integer-versus-float comparison meaningful.
5. Do the same scale-rule effects appear in a larger model or with a gyroscope channel added? The clipping argument is scale-free in principle, but the 43% clip rate is a property of this particular weight distribution.
6. On real FP4 hardware, how much of the theoretical memory-traffic saving survives? The paper measures 90.8 versus 95.6 KiB of storage but no bandwidth or energy, and for a 159K-parameter model the workload may not be memory-bound at all.
7. Is the per-window activation-scale recomputation in the simulator doing work that real kernels cannot? The 8-bit check suggests the simulator is mildly optimistic; a calibration-fixed activation scale would be the more realistic W4A4 setting.
