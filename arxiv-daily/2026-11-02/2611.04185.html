02 Why this matters

If a 4-bit wearable model loses accuracy, suspect the scale-rounding recipe — a fixable conversion setting — before blaming the bit width.

01

How the measurement was built

5 folds · 24 players · 1 model
S1

Data

Hang-Time HAR wrist accelerometer at ~50 Hz: 24 basketball players, four ball actions. 1 s windows on a 0.2 s step; dribbling capped at 3× shots leaves 75,865 windows.

S2

Split

5-fold GroupKFold by player. Every player is tested once, by a model that never saw them. All formats share the same five trained models and the same five folds.

S3

Model

1D CNN: 64/128/256 conv blocks → global average pooling → 128 dense → 4-way softmax. 159,492 parameters, 158,596 of them trainable.

S4

Formats

Real TFLite FP32 / FP16 / dynamic INT8 / full INT8, plus fake-quantised W4 and W4A4 for MXFP4 (block 32, E8M0 down) and NVFP4 (block 16, E4M3 scale).

S5

Ablation & checks

Block size {32, 16} × scale rule {E8M0 down, E8M0 up, E4M3}. Verification: independent NumPy forward pass; simulated W8A8 0.6930 vs real 0.6910.

03

Strengths against limitations

Read both columns

+ What holds up

  • Player-grouped protocol with the leakage effect measured, not asserted: identical models, paired per-fold deltas, every player tested once.
  • The ablation answers “why,” not just “how much” — round-up and E4M3 turn a format ranking into an actionable conversion recipe.
  • Reporting most papers skip: per-fold results, FP32 agreement rates, per-class F1, and the fold-4 anomaly disclosed rather than buried.
  • Simulator checked against real hardware at 8 bits: simulated W8A8 0.6930 versus TFLite 0.6910.
  • Refuses to claim speed when VM latency varies more between sessions (11.6–24.6%) than between files (7.4%).

− What it cannot carry

  • One CNN, one accelerometer dataset, one sport; test players share games and sessions with training players.
  • All 4-bit numbers are simulated — accuracy and storage only, with no latency, memory-traffic, or energy measurement.
  • No quantization-aware training, so every 4-bit loss is a post-training-quantization upper bound.
  • The MXFP4 baseline is one permitted conversion recipe; the abstract's format contrast outruns what the ablation supports.
  • Five folds, no multiplicity control, and fold 4 supplies most of the MXFP4 headline loss.

Open questions

What would change the recommendation
Q1

Would QAT or a calibration-time scale search erase the MXFP4 W4A4 gap — moving the advice from “use NVFP4” to “fix your MX conversion”?

Q2

How much of −5.14 is fold 4? A median-fold or leave-one-fold-out number would settle whether the representative penalty is ~−0.03 or ~−0.05.

Q3

Does the ranking survive a blocked INT4 baseline and a larger model, or is the clipping story specific to this 159K-parameter weight distribution?
