> **一句话总结：** 作者构建了一个 13 任务 / 122 项机器可检验检查的文本到 CAD 基准（build123d + OpenCascade），用精确内核 oracle 度量"几何有效 + 明确宣称完成 + 至少一项语义检查失败"的静默失败率：单次生成条件下四个厂商模型在 156 次尝试中静默失败 14.1%（22/156），把 harness 改成"先测量再宣称完成"后降到 3.2%（5/156），且单变量消融把这一改善归因于 claim contract 而非反馈本身。

> **TL;DR:** On a 13-task, 122-check mechanical CAD benchmark (n=156 attempts per architecture, four vendors: claude-haiku-4-5, claude-sonnet-4-6, deepseek-v3.2, qwen3.7-max), single-shot agents claimed completion on 153/156 attempts while shipping 22/156 (14.1%) valid-but-semantically-wrong parts — claim precision 64.1%. A ReAct-style tool-use loop that refuses `DONE` until the agent has seen kernel measurements of its final code cuts silent failures to 5/156 (3.2%, McNemar p<1e-4), raises claim precision to 96.0% and success from 64.1% to 77.6%; a single-variable ablation (legacy claim contract, identical feedback) reproduces 14/156 silent failures and 67.6% precision, so the contract, not the iteration, does the work. Read this if you care about harness-level overclaiming rather than generation quality — but note the suite is small, 13 of 31 silent failures come from one ambiguously specified task, and no OpenAI or Google model is in the primary population.

## 核心贡献

- **基准与 harness（可复现）**：13 个自然语言机械规格任务，分三层（5 个单特征、6 个多特征、2 个"带约束改图"），共 122 项机器可检验检查（58 项 approx、13 项 range、13 项 equal、13 项有效性、22 项 cylinder_count 含轴向长度、3 项 bolt_circle）。每个自然语言要求都被映射到至少一个可计算属性，全部跑在开源内核 build123d/OpenCascade 上，并附带 13 个参考解与"参考解必须先全通过"的校准门。
- **提出并操作化一个新 measurand：claim-conditioned silent-failure rate**。定义为 Pr[有效实体 ∧ 明确宣称完成 ∧ ¬全部检查通过]，即只统计"能渲染、agent 说 done、但至少一项语义检查失败"的情形，把"崩溃"（loud failure）和"诚实地不宣称"（honest non-claim）都排除在外，从而隔离 agent 侧的过度宣称，而不是评分器的缺陷。
- **把完成宣称的校准（ECE、可靠性图、AUROC、Brier）与静默失败率耦合**：不只问"对不对"，还问"agent 知不知道自己不对"。
- **三种 agent 架构的受控比较**：single-shot、带内核测量反馈的 tool-use（ReAct 式）、planner–coder–checker 多智能体（附 Context Divergence Score 度量角色间信念分歧）。
- **单变量消融把因果归给 claim contract**：在反馈完全相同的前提下只改"是否允许未测量代码伴随 DONE"，四个模型全部重跑（n=156），静默失败 14 vs 5、claim precision 67.6% vs 96.0%。
- **oracle 自我审计**：主动加固自己的评分器，重评 519 条存档尝试，18 个判定由 pass 翻成 fail、零个反向，暴露了原语料 48 个静默失败中的 17 个；再用盲评专家审计从另一侧给率定上界。
- **明确报告未被数据支持的预注册预测**（P2 上下文漂移、P3 "能力提升远快于可靠性提升"），并公开了预注册未登记、计划文件与结果同一 commit 的弱点。

## Key Contributions

- **A released benchmark and harness**: 13 specification tasks in three tiers with 122 machine-checkable requirements, published per-check tolerance bands, reference solutions, a calibration gate, the grader, the measurement extractor, all 519 regraded attempts (final code, measured properties, verdicts under both oracle versions), full transcripts for the 312 fixed-protocol tool-use and ablation attempts, and a static 3D viewer. Code MIT, data CC BY 4.0, Zenodo DOI.
- **A new measurand**: the claim-conditioned, self-audited exact-oracle silent-failure rate, defined as the conjunction of (i) valid renderable geometry, (ii) an explicit agent completion claim, and (iii) at least one failed semantic check. The authors are explicit that they do not claim to be first to measure geometric correctness — the distinction is conditioning on the agent's own claim and grading with a kernel oracle that has no judge model in the loop.
- **Coupling reliability to calibration**: completion-claim ECE, reliability diagrams, AUROC and Brier score over (confidence, pass) pairs, so that "the agent said done at 0.97" becomes a measurable, decomposable failure rather than an anecdote.
- **An architecture comparison with an ablation that isolates the mechanism**: single-shot vs. measure-then-claim tool-use vs. planner–coder–checker, plus a legacy-contract arm with identical feedback, showing that verification *discipline at claim time* rather than iteration is what closes the syntactic–semantic gap.
- **Two-sided error bounding**: oracle hardening (necessary-condition screen ⇒ measured rates are lower bounds) plus a blind expert audit (grader false negatives ⇒ upper bound), with a tolerance-band sweep from ×0.25 to ×4 showing the contrast does not depend on where the bands were drawn.
- **Methodological honesty as a contribution**: a mid-study audit found a protocol loophole in their own tool-use loop and permissive checks in their own oracle; both were fixed, everything was re-executed and regraded, and the retraction of their own pilot's optimistic reading is reported in the paper.

## Methodology

**Task schema.** A task is data, not code: `{id, tier, nl_spec, units, checks[]}`. Each check names a kind the grader knows how to evaluate against a measured-properties dictionary: scalar comparisons with tolerance (`approx`, `range`, `equal`) over a fixed vocabulary (bounding-box extents, volume, centre of mass, solid/face/edge counts), boolean validity checks (`valid_solid`, `watertight`), and specialized geometric checks (`cylinder_count`, `bolt_circle` with pitch-circle diameter and positional tolerance). Specs are written so every natural-language requirement maps to at least one computable check — the discipline that makes the suite auto-gradable and auditable. Tiers: T1 single feature (flange with bolt circle, washer, bushing, four-hole plate, shaft collar), T2 multi-feature (stepped shaft, flanged bushing, counterbored plate, three-step pin, pulley blank, L-bracket with non-coaxial holes), T4 edit-under-constraint (re-pattern a bolt circle; enlarge a bore under a material-volume cap). There is deliberately no assembly tier in v1.

**Execution and measurement.** Agents emit Python for build123d (OpenCascade). Code runs in an isolated subprocess that builds the solid and measures kernel-truth properties, so a crash or hang cannot take down the runner and the only OpenCascade dependency is quarantined in one module. Cylindrical features are detected by grouping cylindrical faces by their infinite supporting cylinder (radius + axis line) and merging groups by axial extent: overlapping spans are one physical feature (a boolean seam splitting a hole's face), disjoint spans are separate features (coaxial bearing seats). The extractor is lenient on wrapper conventions (builder objects, lists of solids) and strict on geometry.

**Three agent conditions.**
1. *Single-shot*: one completion from the spec.
2. *Tool-use*: a ReAct-style loop where the harness executes each code revision and returns the measured geometry — validity, bbox extents, volume, centre of mass, solid count, and for every detected cylindrical feature its radius, axis, centre and axial extent. Crucially the feedback **never** includes the specification's targets, tolerance bands, or any pass/fail verdict: the agent sees what a kernel reports, not what the grader concludes, and must map the spec onto those numbers itself. Under the *measure-then-claim contract*, a `DONE` accompanying fresh, unmeasured code is rejected with an explicit message and the episode continues.
3. *Multi-agent*: planner → coder → checker, all roles played by the same model. The checker judges from the identical kernel-measured property set (plus a watertightness flag) and never sees the checks, so the two verified conditions are information-matched and differ only in architecture. Each role ends its turn with a flat JSON of governing dimensions (`DIMS`); the per-round Context Divergence Score (CDS) is the mean pairwise disagreement over the union of stated dimension keys (agreement = both present and within 2% relative tolerance; the planner's key vocabulary is propagated so naming variation is not scored as drift).

**Claim parsing.** A completion claim counts only when the token `DONE` starts its own line outside any code block (so "this is NOT DONE" never counts). Confidence comes from a `CONFIDENCE: <0..1>` line; a missing or unparsable value is recorded as no stated confidence and excluded from calibration statistics. Tool-use episodes are capped at four model turns plus one confirmation turn; multi-agent at one plan and three coder–checker rounds; the kernel executor at 60 s per execution.

**Populations and statistics.** Primary: four models from four vendors (claude-haiku-4-5, claude-sonnet-4-6, deepseek-v3.2, qwen3.7-max), complete 13-task coverage, hardened oracle, fixed-protocol tool-use, n=156 per architecture (3 repetitions × 13 tasks × 4 models), 468 primary attempts. Ablation: a tool-use arm rerun under the legacy claim contract with identical feedback (n=156; the sonnet arm routed through OpenRouter to the same model). Protocol sensitivity: the original-protocol tool-use runs regraded under the hardened oracle (143 attempts), descriptive only. Intervals: Wilson on per-condition rates, task-cluster bootstrap (4,000 resamples) on contrasts, exact McNemar over (task, model, repetition) triples, task-clustered GEE as corroboration (13 clusters, sandwich SEs anti-conservative, so the bootstrap is primary). Two pre-specified contrasts; everything else is descriptive without multiplicity correction.

**Grader soundness.** Every task ships a reference solution never shown to agents; a calibration gate requires each reference solution to pass all its checks before any agent run is scored. A stratified sample of 81 valid-solid outputs (all 31 silent failures, all 25 withheld-but-wrong parts, 25 random grader-passes) was rendered and blind-coded by a practising mechanical CAD engineer who wrote neither the specs nor the grader: Cohen's κ = 0.64 (raw 82.7%), rising to κ = 0.87 (raw 93.5%, n=62) after excluding the one spec-ambiguous task.

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline silent-failure contrast (13-task CAD suite, primary population, n=156 per condition):** single-shot agents claimed `DONE` on 153/156 attempts and produced 22/156 silent failures = **14.1%** (task-cluster CI [5.8, 24.4], Wilson [9.5, 20.4]) with **claim precision 64.1%**; the measure-then-claim tool-use loop produced **5/156 = 3.2%** with **claim precision 96.0%** at an 80.8% claim rate (McNemar over (task, model, repetition) triples: 18 removed, 1 created, p<1e-4; silent-rate difference CI [3.8, 19.2] pp). Success also rose **64.1% → 77.6%** (McNemar 25 fixed / 4 broken, p=1.0e-4; GEE task-clustered p=0.00014).
- **The ablation attributes the gain to the claim contract, not the feedback (n=156 per arm, identical kernel feedback):** under the legacy contract tool-use produced **14 silent failures vs 5** for the fixed contract (Fisher p=0.056 — marginal), **claim precision 67.6% vs 96.0%** (p<1e-9) and success **64.1% vs 77.6%** (p=0.013). Per model: haiku 6 vs 1, sonnet 4 vs 0, deepseek 4 vs 2, qwen 0 vs 2 — one model runs the other way on small counts, which the authors disclose.
- **Planner–coder–checker matches precision but not capability (same 13 tasks, n=156):** **4/156 silent failures** and **95.5% claim precision** (McNemar vs single-shot: 19 removed, 1 created, p<1e-4) but at a **57.1% claim rate** and **no success gain — 61.5% vs 64.1%** for single-shot. Of its 60 failed episodes, **53 never produced a valid solid** (the coder, fed second-hand checker prose rather than raw kernel traces, crashes more), 3 were valid-but-wrong parts correctly withheld, 4 were silent failures, and the checker withheld claims on 11 episodes that were actually correct. In selective-prediction terms: 81% coverage at 4.0% conditional risk (tool-use) vs 57% coverage at 4.5% (checker) — the simpler architecture dominates on this suite.
- **Calibration becomes informative only under verification:** single-shot confidence is saturated (claim rate 98%, mean 0.97) with **ECE 0.324**; under tool-use and the checker pipeline ECE falls to **0.091** and **0.110**. Per-model AUROC rises for three of four models — haiku **0.72 → 0.98/0.99**, sonnet **0.93 → 1.00** (checker, few negatives; its tool-use arm had no failures to rank), deepseek **0.65 → 0.83/0.93** — while qwen3.7-max **falls from 0.65 to chance (0.50/0.55)** because its confidence is pinned at 1.0 regardless of outcome.
- **Context divergence is a breakdown detector, not a wrongness detector (multi-agent, n=156):** episode-mean CDS separates passing from never-built episodes (0.105 vs 0.518, Mann–Whitney p<1e-15, **AUROC 0.86**), but the number of coder–checker rounds predicts the same outcome at **0.87** and the validity flag alone at **0.94**, so CDS adds nothing predictive. Stratifying by validity kills even the diagnostic value: the seven valid-but-wrong episodes average **CDS 0.107 vs 0.105 for passes** — indistinguishable.
- **Where silent failures live (primary population, 31 total):** all 31 are T1/T2 parts; **19 of 31 come from two tasks** (the L-bracket and the counterbored plate — internal features and non-coaxial holes); the two T4 edit tasks produced none. Hazard tracks internal-feature complexity, not headline task difficulty. Excluding the spec-ambiguous L-bracket: **16/144 (11.1%) single-shot, 2 (1.4%) tool-use, 0 checker**, with the paired test unchanged in direction (15 removed, 1 created, McNemar p=0.0005).
- **The oracle audits itself and the rates are bounded on both sides (519 stored attempts regraded):** hardening the checks (axial extents on every cylindrical check, per-hole radial deviation plus pattern-centre and angular-uniformity constraints on bolt circles, translation-invariant mass-centre offsets, body counts, ±8% volume bands) flipped **18 verdicts pass→fail and zero fail→pass**, exposing **17 of the corpus's 48 silent failures (35%)**. Conversely the blind expert audit overturned **4 of 62** verdicts outside the ambiguous task, all grader-fail → expert-pass, **3 of which had been counted as silent failures**; reading the expert as ground truth moves the primary rates to **20/156 (12.8%) single-shot and 4/156 (2.6%) tool-use**, and no grader pass was overturned.
- **Tolerance-band sweep (every band scaled by one common factor, n=156 per condition):** at ×0.25 silent failures are 33/156 (21.1%) SS, 17/156 (10.9%) TU, 14/156 (9.0%) MA; at ×1 (published) 22/156, 5/156, 4/156; at ×4, 15/156 (9.6%), 5/156, 4/156. The SS−TU contrast survives every scaling (**6.4 to 10.9 pp, McNemar p<0.01 at every factor**), and halving every band changes **no** verdict — the silent failures are gross errors (a missing counterbore, a hole on the wrong pitch circle, a wall added outboard), not marginal ones. The published bands reproduce all 468 stored verdicts exactly.
- **Capability vs. reliability (pooled, all conditions):** success@3 = **81.4%** but pass^3 = **53.8%**, i.e. best-of-k reporting overstates dependable capability by ~28 points. Verification discipline costs roughly an order of magnitude more input tokens: mean input/output tokens per attempt 211/735 (single-shot), 2,821/1,549 (tool-use), 2,279/2,787 (multi-agent).
- **Conditional silent rate among valid claimed parts** (the number a user experiences): **18.3% (22/120)** unverified vs **4.0% (5/126)** and **4.5% (4/89)** for the two verified configurations. Protocol sensitivity: deepseek-v3.2 under the original loophole contract showed **11/39** silent failures vs **2/39** under the fixed contract — the largest per-model swing, in the direction F1 predicts for a weak self-verifier.

## Authors & Credibility

Three authors, none at a major lab: Carson Rodrigues (Celabe, first author, built the harness, auto-grader and eval infrastructure, and wrote the paper), Clive Rodrigues (Hochschule Coburg, a practising mechanical engineer who wrote the task suite and ground-truth specifications), and Aravind Reddy G (independent; performed the blind expert pass/fail scoring and the Siemens NX STEP reproduction of the reference parts). The team is small and the work is unfunded, which shows in the scale (13 tasks) but not in the methodology: the paper is unusually disciplined about statistics for a benchmark release — Wilson intervals, task-cluster bootstraps on contrasts, exact McNemar over (task, model, repetition) triples, GEE as corroboration with an explicit note that 13 clusters make sandwich SEs anti-conservative, and a clear statement of which two contrasts were pre-specified versus which are descriptive.

Credibility is materially boosted by three things that most benchmark papers omit. First, a **calibration gate** that no agent run is scored until the reference solution passes every check. Second, a **blind expert audit** by a co-author who wrote neither the specs nor the grader, with the full agreement table, every disagreement, and the κ script released — and the paper reports the *miss* against its own pre-set target (κ ≥ 0.8, achieved 0.64 overall) rather than only the post-hoc 0.87 subset. Third, an **oracle-hardening audit** that flipped 18 of its own verdicts and none in reverse, with the authors stating plainly that "benchmark authors should expect their own graders to fail the audit they apply to agents." The conflict-of-interest disclosure (Clive Rodrigues consults for a mechanical-AI data company) and a CRediT statement are both present. The weak spot on credibility is provenance: the paper concedes the study plan was **not lodged with a registry** and its first repository commit coincides with the results commit, so the pre-specification rests on the authors' account alone.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The framing is genuinely new in this subfield. Prior text-to-CAD evaluation measures generation quality (Chamfer distance, IoU, execution success, judge scores) or, in CADSmith's case, uses exact kernel validation as a *defect to engineer away*; this paper makes the divergence itself the measurand and conditions it on the agent's own completion claim. Coupling that to claim calibration (ECE/AUROC/Brier) yields a metric nobody in the CAD-agent literature reports. The measure-then-claim contract is a small idea, but the ablation showing it — not the feedback loop — carries the effect is a real, falsifiable, and somewhat uncomfortable finding for the feedback-loop literature (the paper says it "partially retracts" its own pilot's reading). The planner–coder–checker and CDS machinery is the weakest novelty claim: CDS is imported from the first author's own prior work, and here it yields an explicit null.

**Rigor.** Above average for a benchmark paper, with three specific caveats. (i) The **ablation is confounded in time and routing** for two of four models: the sonnet and qwen legacy arms were run three months after their fixed-protocol counterparts, and the sonnet arm went through OpenRouter rather than the Anthropic API. The authors disclose this, but it means "the contract, not the feedback" rests on haiku and deepseek being clean and on the claim-precision gap (67.6% vs 96.0%, p<1e-9) being large enough to swamp the drift; the silent-failure difference alone is Fisher p=0.056, i.e. not significant at conventional thresholds. (ii) **No seed control** and three repetitions at provider-default decoding, with 13 task clusters — the paper is candid that this bounds variance estimation and that GEE sandwich SEs are anti-conservative, but the headline 14.1% [5.8, 24.4] interval is wide enough that "one in seven" should be read as "somewhere between one in seventeen and one in four." (iii) **The single expert rater is a co-author**, and 13 of the 31 primary silent failures come from one task whose specification admits two readings of base depth; the paper handles this better than most (reporting both with and without the task, and conceding the disputed reading), but the absolute rates are still hostage to one ambiguous sentence.

**Reproducibility.** Excellent by the standards of this literature: 519 regraded attempts with verdicts under *both* oracle versions, 312 full transcripts, the grader, extractor, executor, three harnesses, prompts and claim-parsing rules verbatim in Appendix B, the κ computation, and one script that regrades everything offline in under an hour on a laptop with no GPU. The Zenodo record is immutable with an errata file. The one gap is that the reference solutions are released but the suite is only 13 canonical parts, so a reader cannot easily construct a held-out variant — parametric re-instantiation is explicitly deferred to v2, which also means the contamination control is a promise rather than a result.

**Significance.** The actionable claim is narrow and strong: any CAD copilot can refuse to accept a completion claim until the agent has seen kernel measurements of its final code, and this paper measures what that buys (~11 points of claim precision, ~4× reduction in silent failures, at ~10× input tokens). That is cheap enough to adopt tomorrow. The broader claim — that harness contract matters more than agent architecture — is well supported within this suite but rests on 13 tasks.

**Red flags, in order of severity.**
1. **No OpenAI or Google model.** Four vendors sounds broad, but the primary population excludes the two most-deployed frontier families; the gpt-5.5 run was an incomplete easy-tier prefix and is released but unused. Any claim about "frontier agents" is therefore about four specific pinned versions from June 2026.
2. **No published text-to-CAD system was run on the suite.** CADSmith, MUSE, the FEA-feedback agent of Son et al. and 3DCodeBench are compared conceptually in Related Work, never empirically. The paper admits the tool-use condition "resembles CADSmith's kernel-measurement outer loop without its documentation retrieval or VLM judge" — so the headline comparison is generic-architecture vs generic-architecture, not against the state of the art in CAD generation.
3. **The suite saturates on capability.** Sonnet reaches 90–100% success across all three conditions; the benchmark discriminates on reliability, not skill, and the authors say so. But that also means the silent-failure counts for the strong models are small (sonnet 4/39 single-shot, 0/39 tool-use), and the qwen anomaly (AUROC falling to chance under verification) is a single-model, small-n observation that the paper wisely does not over-theorize.
4. **One prompt per condition, no wording variation.** Given that 13 of 31 silent failures trace to a two-reading specification, prompt sensitivity is a live threat the design cannot quantify.
5. **Residual oracle leniency is one-directional by construction.** Hardening flipped 18 verdicts and none back, which is evidence of honesty but also evidence that the "hardened" oracle may still be a necessary-condition screen with unmodelled properties (the paper says exactly this, and bounds the other direction with the expert audit — 3 of 18 overturned).
6. **The multi-agent comparison is not architecture-fair on the coder's information diet.** The coder receives the checker's prose rather than raw kernel traces, so 53 of 60 failures are "never built a solid." The paper reports this, but a reader could misread "multi-agent doesn't help" as a statement about multi-agent design in general rather than about this particular information routing.

**What would change my mind.** Running CADSmith (or MUSE) on the same 13 tasks and showing its own claim precision sits near 96% would make the harness contract look like a solved problem rather than a contribution. Conversely, a v2 with parametric re-instantiation and a second external expert rater, keeping the 14.1% → 3.2% contrast, would make this the reference measurement for CAD-agent overclaiming.

## Strengths

- **The measurand is the right one.** Conditioning on the agent's own `DONE` and on exact kernel measurement isolates agent-side overclaiming from grader weakness — precisely the confound that makes SWE-bench-style execution grading leaky (the paper cites UTBoost's finding that insufficient tests mislabel hundreds of patches as resolved).
- **The ablation is the paper.** Anyone can show that a feedback loop improves scores; showing that the *claim contract* rather than the feedback does the work, on all four models, with identical feedback, is a genuine mechanistic result — and the authors follow it to its uncomfortable conclusion about their own pilot.
- **Two-sided error bounding is rare and valuable.** Oracle hardening gives a lower bound (unmodelled properties can only add failures), the blind expert audit gives an upper bound (grader false negatives), and both are quantified rather than asserted. The line "benchmark authors should expect their own graders to fail the audit they apply to agents" is the most useful sentence in the paper.
- **The tolerance-band sweep answers the obvious objection before it is raised.** Halving every band changes no verdict, so the silent failures are gross rather than marginal; the contrast survives ×0.25 to ×4. That is exactly the ablation a skeptical CAD engineer would demand.
- **Calibration analysis is properly decomposed.** Leading with binning-free per-model AUROC while reporting ECE alongside — and explaining why equal-mass bins collapse under saturated confidence — is more careful than most calibration reporting in agent papers.
- **The negative results are reported, not buried.** CDS adds nothing over episode length (0.86 vs 0.87 vs 0.94 for the validity flag); the seven valid-but-wrong episodes are statistically indistinguishable from passes on CDS; P3 as stated is unsupported; the κ target was missed. Each null is stated in the results, not relegated to an appendix.
- **The artifact is genuinely usable.** 519 attempts with both oracle verdicts, 312 transcripts, a 3D viewer, prompts verbatim, one offline regrade script, MIT/CC BY, immutable DOI plus an errata file. This is a benchmark someone else can actually audit.

## Limitations

- **Scale.** 13 tasks, 122 checks, 13 task clusters. Enough to separate architectures with paired tests across four vendors, not enough to rank models finely or to estimate absolute rates that would transfer to a different task mix — and silent failures concentrate in two tasks, so the mix matters a lot.
- **One ambiguous specification contaminates the headline.** 13 of 31 primary silent failures come from `t2-lbracket`, whose base depth admits two readings (40 mm overall vs 48 mm with the wall outboard). The contrast survives exclusion (11.1% vs 1.4%), but absolute rates on that task should be read as an upper bound on agent error, and the fact that a practising mechanical engineer read it differently from the grader is itself a finding about natural-language underspecification.
- **Missing model families.** No OpenAI or Google model in the primary population; the gpt-5.5 run was incomplete. The results are a dated snapshot of pinned June-2026 versions with no seed control.
- **No head-to-head against published text-to-CAD systems.** CADSmith, MUSE, Son et al. and 3DCodeBench are compared on construct, never on the suite.
- **Ablation confounds for two of four models.** Sonnet and qwen legacy arms ran three months later, sonnet through a different gateway; the silent-failure difference alone is Fisher p=0.056.
- **Single expert rater, and a co-author.** A second external rater would strengthen the κ analysis; the 81-part stratified sample is well designed but small.
- **The oracle remains a necessary-condition screen.** Unmodelled properties can only add failures, so the rates are lower bounds; the expert audit puts residual false negatives at 3 of 18 silent failures outside the ambiguous task, but that estimate itself depends on one rater.
- **No assembly tier, no interference or manufacturability checks.** A part that passes every check can still be unmanufacturable or unfit for function; the checks are model-correctness screens, not design validation.
- **Contamination is uncontrolled in v1.** The parts are canonical and abundantly represented in training corpora, so measured rates are, if anything, optimistic for novel geometry — the direction that strengthens the warning but weakens the absolute numbers.
- **The human side is out of scope.** Whether engineers actually catch these silent failures when an agent reports success is the question that determines deployment risk, and it is deferred to a follow-up.
- **Pre-specification is unregistered.** The plan's first commit coincides with the results commit, so P1–P3 rest on the authors' account.

## Relevance

直接命中 "Agent & Pipeline Silent Failures" 这一方向：它研究的正是 harness 层（claim contract、工具反馈回路、多智能体编排）如何在不报错的情况下让 agent 交付错误结果，并给出了可复用的度量（claim-conditioned silent-failure rate + completion-claim calibration）。虽然领域是机械 CAD，但"先测量再宣称完成"这一 harness 契约和"验证纪律而非迭代次数才是关键"的结论可以平移到任何有精确 oracle 的 agent 流水线，且论文对自身 oracle 的加固审计（18 个判定翻转、零个反向）本身就是评分器可靠性的案例研究。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the measure-then-claim contract transfer to domains without an exact kernel oracle — e.g. can a *partial* measurement (schema validation, unit tests, type checks) capture most of the 11-point claim-precision gain, or does the effect require the agent to see the actual measured geometry?
- The ablation's claim-precision gap (67.6% → 96.0%) is far more decisive than its silent-failure gap (14 vs 5, p=0.056). Is that because the legacy contract mostly converts silent failures into *loud* ones (the agent iterates and crashes) rather than into passes? A per-episode transition analysis would settle it.
- Why does qwen3.7-max's confidence stay pinned at 1.0 under verification while its claim rate drops? Is the confidence elicitation format (`CONFIDENCE: <0..1>` on its own line) interacting with model-specific instruction following, and would a different elicitation recover the signal?
- The multi-agent coder receives the checker's prose rather than raw kernel traces, and 53 of 60 failures are "never built a solid." How much of the checker's coverage loss (57% vs 81%) disappears if the coder gets the same measurement channel as the tool-use condition? That is the architecture-fair version of the comparison the paper does not run.
- Can the 13 tasks be parametrically re-instantiated (the planned v2 contamination control) without changing the difficulty distribution? If yes, do the rates hold, and does the L-bracket ambiguity recur across instantiations or was it a one-off wording defect?
- Would a second, external expert rater move κ materially above 0.64 on the full sample, or is the 0.64 driven almost entirely by the one ambiguous task (0.87 without it) and therefore already near the ceiling for natural-language specs?
- Does the "silent failures live in internal multi-feature geometry" pattern (19 of 31 in two tasks; zero in the T4 edit tasks) hold at larger scale, or is it an artifact of which tasks happen to have counterbores and non-coaxial holes?
