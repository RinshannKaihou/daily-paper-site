> **一句话总结：** 在 Qwen3-30B-A3B 的第 4/30/46 层 MoE 输出上各训一个 2²⁰ 潜变量的 TopK SAE，利用编码器对"专家输出加权和"的仿射性把每个潜变量的预激活精确分解到各专家头上，从而得到每个专家的"主导潜变量集合"；结果发现贡献比门控更集中（第 30 层排名第一的贡献份额 0.512 vs 门控 0.231），62.1% 的潜变量只有唯一主导专家，而主导集合的自动解释显示专家分工细到 Java / 前端 Web、几何 / 算术多项式这一级。

> **TL;DR:** Training a TopK SAE (2²⁰ latents) on the MoE-layer output of Qwen3-30B-A3B lets the authors decompose every latent's pre-activation *exactly* over the experts, because the layer output is a router-weighted sum and the encoder is affine. On layers 4/30/46 this "contribution fraction" is far more concentrated than the gating weights it is built from (layer 30: top-ranked contribution share 0.512 vs 0.231 for gating; bottom rank 0.005 vs 0.074), 57–62% of latents have exactly one dominating expert, and only 0.25–0.57% of the 8,128 expert pairs exceed Jaccard 0.05. Auto-interpreting (GPT-5.5) the latents each expert dominates yields a bottom-up taxonomy in which layer 30's 128 experts split into 11 domains, with computing taking 28 experts and math 20 (37.5% combined) while art appears in exactly one label — and with genuinely sub-domain specialists, e.g. E50 is 86.7% Java latents vs a 1.9% random baseline, E57 is 78.6% front-end web vs 3.5%. Worth reading if you care about MoE routing or SAE-based interpretability; skip if you need causal validation, which the paper explicitly defers.

## 核心贡献

- **提出一种精确、无需训练的专家归因方法**：MoE 层输出 $y(t)=\sum_{j\in A(t)} g_j(t)u_j(t)$ 是专家输出的加权和，而 SAE 编码器对 $y$ 是仿射的，因此每个潜变量的预激活 $f_i(t)=W_{enc,(i)}\cdot y(t)+b_{enc,(i)}$ 可以**代数上精确**地拆成"编码器偏置 + 各专家贡献之和"（式 5），不存在近似误差（误差只来自 SAE 本身对 $y$ 的重构）。
- **用"贡献"取代"被选中频率"**：定义逐 token、逐专家的正贡献 $c(j,i,x)=\mathrm{ReLU}\big(g_j(t)\,W_{enc,(i)}\cdot u_j(t)\big)$，在语料上取期望并归一化为贡献分数 $s(j,i)$，阈值化后得到"专家—潜变量"二部图（dominance graph）。这把分析单元从"人定的语料领域标签"换成了"模型自己学出的特征"。
- **发现贡献远比门控集中**：按排名对齐后比较两条曲线，第 30 层门控前三名是 0.231 / 0.169 / 0.137（平缓衰减），贡献前三名是 0.512 / 0.185 / 0.115（第一名独占过半），末位 0.005 vs 0.074；并论证这不是 ReLU 截断造成的（8 个激活专家中平均只有 1.29 个在第 30 层被截断）。
- **刻画主导图的结构**：多数潜变量只有 1 个主导专家（第 4/30/46 层分别为 57.4% / 62.1% / 56.7%），≤2 个主导者覆盖 89.9% / 85.1% / 92.7%；128 个专家**无一为空集**，前 10 高度数专家只占 24.6% / 20.1% / 30.1% 的边（均匀情形为 7.8%），说明主导权分散而非被少数 hub 吸收。
- **给出细于领域标签的分工证据**：第 30 层同一"代码"域内，E50 专于 Java/Android、E57 专于 HTML/CSS/JS 前端；数学域内 E1 专于几何、E76 专于算术与多项式。用 GPT-5.5 判定主题归属，E50 的主导集合中 86.7% 的解释属于 Java 主题，而其他 127 个专家池中随机抽 2000 个潜变量只有 1.9%。
- **自下而上恢复领域并量化其不均衡**：先由词云给每个专家起标签，再一次调用把 128 个标签聚成 11 个"域"。计算占 28 个专家、数学与推理占 20 个（合计 37.5%），而"艺术"只出现在 1 个标签里；文本形式 / 语言 / 实体标识符这三个**非学科**域共占 36 个专家（28.1%），这是任何预设学科分类都无法恢复的单元。
- **开源**：SAE 权重与代码发布于 https://github.com/Bytedance-Seed/SAE-Interprets-MoE，全部提示词（潜变量解释、主题判定、专家命名、领域聚类）在附录 E–G、J 中逐字给出。

## Key Contributions

- **An exact, training-free attribution identity.** Because the MoE output is a router-weighted sum of expert outputs and the SAE encoder is affine in that sum, each latent's pre-activation decomposes algebraically over the active experts (Eq. 5). The decomposition carries *no* approximation error; the only error is the SAE's own reconstruction of the layer output.
- **Contribution replaces selection frequency as the unit of analysis.** Per-token, per-expert positive contribution `c(j,i,x) = ReLU(g_j(t) · W_enc,(i) · u_j(t))` is averaged over a 1.5B-token held-out corpus and normalized into a contribution fraction `s(j,i)`; thresholding at τ = 0.2 yields a bipartite dominance graph between the 128 experts and 2²⁰ latents. This fixes all four limitations the authors attribute to domain-labeled counting: analyst-imposed taxonomy, coarse labels, corpus-level-vs-token-level mismatch, and selection-without-contribution.
- **Contribution is much more concentrated than the gating that produces it.** A rank-aligned comparison of the two profiles (both sum to 1, so directly comparable) rules out the trivial explanation that the concentration is just a projection of top-k renormalized gating.
- **Structural characterization of the dominance graph across depth.** Latent-side degrees are tiny (mostly 1–2), expert-side degrees are non-zero for all 128 experts and only moderately skewed, and pairwise Jaccard overlap is essentially zero for most pairs with a thin, pair-structured right tail.
- **A bottom-up, model-derived taxonomy of one MoE layer.** Eleven domains emerge from the 128 expert labels with no domain names supplied in the prompt, exposing (a) sub-domain specialization inside code and mathematics, (b) strongly unequal expert allocation across domains, and (c) three large domains (text form, language, entities/identifiers) that are *not* subject areas at all and would be scattered by any human subject taxonomy.
- **Full artifact release**: SAEs, code, and every prompt used in the LLM-in-the-loop pipeline.

## Methodology

**Setup.** Qwen3-30B-A3B: 48 layers, 128 experts per MoE layer, k = 8 activated per token, no shared experts, hidden size d = 2048. Three layers analyzed — 4, 30, 46 — chosen as shallow / mid-late / deep. One independent SAE per layer with m = 2²⁰ = 1,048,576 latents (≈512× overcomplete).

**SAE training.** TopK-ReLU SAE following Gao et al. (2024): κ annealed linearly from 1000 to 100 over the first 10k steps, auxiliary loss with coefficient 1/32 (Gao et al.; Deng et al.), AdamW (β = 0.9/0.999, weight decay 0.01), batch 8192, ≈3.2B tokens per SAE, η_max = 8e-5 with sqrt warmup over 100 steps then linear decay. Activations normalized so E[‖X‖] = √n, outliers above 20× the batch mean filtered. Decoder columns renormalized to unit norm post hoc with the reciprocal folded into the encoder, so the exported SAE applies directly to raw layer output. Training data is the **Olmo 3** corpus: 5/8 pretraining, 1/4 reasoning CoT, 1/8 instruction SFT — i.e. *not* Qwen3's own (undisclosed) mixture. All activations dumped before training and 512 sampled per example to diversify batches.

**Reconstruction quality.** FVU = 12.6% (layer 4), **25.1% (layer 30)**, 7.6% (layer 46), reproducing the known pattern that mid-depth layers are hardest to reconstruct.

**The decomposition.** For activated latent i, `f_i(t) = Σ_{j∈A(t)} g_j(t) · W_enc,(i)·u_j(t) + b_enc,(i)`. Only the **positive** part is kept, `c(j,i,x) = ReLU(g_j(t)·W_enc,(i)·u_j(t))`, on the argument that a negative contribution strong enough to suppress a latent leaves it inactive and is therefore never observed, so statistics over negatives would be biased. Contribution fraction `C(j,i,x)` normalizes c over the activated experts; `C̄(j,i)` is its expectation over the corpus; `s(j,i)` renormalizes over all N experts. Expert j *dominates* latent i when `s(j,i) ≥ τ`, with τ = 0.2 (far above the uniform value 1/128 ≈ 0.008).

**Interpretation pipeline.** For each expert, 2000 latents sampled uniformly from its dominant set (whole set if smaller). Each latent explained by **GPT-5.5** from three evidence channels: (i) 10 activating contexts — top-5 by activation plus 5 uniformly random, the random ones added because top contexts are near-duplicates and bias the explainer toward a literal surface trigger; (ii) logit effects via logit lens, top-10 boosted / top-10 suppressed tokens; (iii) layer-depth prior as a tiebreaker. Explanations may be `Uninterpretable`; a 3-level confidence is recorded. Explanations are then aggregated into a per-expert word cloud (word size = frequency among that expert's latent explanations).

**Labeling and grouping.** GPT-5.5 names each expert from its word cloud alone (≤9 words, no fixed topic list, allowed to name a non-subject axis or an "A and B" pairing), then a single further call receives all 128 labels and returns a partition into 6–13 groups (it returned 11) with a name per group. No domain name is supplied.

**Validation sets.** Statistics computed on 1.5B tokens sharing the SAE training distribution but with no overlap; every latent of every SAE activates at least once on it, so `s(j,i)` is defined everywhere.

## Results & Highlights

- **Contribution beats gating on concentration (layer 30, rank-aligned mean shares).** Top-ranked gating weight ḡ₀ = 0.231 vs contribution C̄₀ = **0.512**, i.e. the single largest contributor accounts for more than half of a latent's activation on average; the curves cross between r = 1 and r = 2, and at the bottom rank C̄₇ = 0.005 vs ḡ₇ = 0.074. Layers 4 and 46 show the same shape. The paper pre-empts the ReLU-truncation objection with a count: of the 8 activated experts, only 2.09 / 1.29 / 1.64 are truncated at layers 4 / 30 / 46.
- **Latent-side degrees are tiny (dominance graph, τ = 0.2).** Exactly one dominator: **57.4% / 62.1% / 56.7%** of the 2²⁰ latents at layers 4 / 30 / 46; ≤2 dominators: 89.9% / 85.1% / 92.7%; degree ≥3 never exceeds 3.7%; degree 5 (the ceiling permitted by τ) never occurs. Latents with *no* dominator — activation sourced too evenly to clear τ — are 7.0% / 13.6% / 3.7%.
- **Expert-side degrees are non-degenerate and only mildly skewed.** All 128 experts dominate a non-empty set in every layer (minimum degree 362 / 224 / 83). Mean vs median degree: 10,786 vs 8,020 (layer 4), 9,170 vs 7,395 (layer 30), 11,439 vs 7,835 (layer 46). The ten highest-degree experts hold **24.6% / 20.1% / 30.1%** of all edges versus 7.8% under uniform degree — moderate skew, no hub collapse.
- **Dominant sets are nearly disjoint.** Over all 8,128 expert pairs per layer, mean Jaccard is 1.73e-3 / 1.57e-3 / 2.12e-3 and the **median sits near 1e-4**; 37.8% / 40.5% / 37.5% of pairs share no latent at all. The overlap is a thin right tail: the top 10% of pairs carry 83.8% / 78.5% / 81.0% of all intersection, only 25 / 20 / 46 pairs (0.31% / 0.25% / 0.57%) exceed J = 0.05, and the maximum J is 0.138 / 0.098 / 0.317. On average 46–59% of an expert's dominant set is dominated by that expert alone.
- **Overlap is organized into pairs, not groups.** Of experts with a partner above J = 0.05, most have exactly one (22 / 28 / 36); the largest connected component is 10 / 4 / 8 experts; 26 / 33 / 30 pairs are mutual nearest neighbours; a pair above threshold shares a median of only 16–18% of the smaller set. The full matrices (Fig. 8) are "a dark field with a few isolated bright cells" — no block structure.
- **Latent explanations separate sub-domains sharply (layer 30, GPT-5.5 theme judge).** Share of an expert's sampled latents judged to belong to its theme vs. a random 2000-latent draw from the other 127 experts' dominant sets: E50 "Java development" **86.7% vs 1.9%**; E57 "front-end development" **78.6% vs 3.5%**; E1 "geometry" **78.1% vs 2.0%**; E76 "arithmetic and polynomial" **59.1% vs 6.7%**.
- **Explanation word clouds recover structure that token word clouds miss.** E72 of layer 30 is a two-theme expert (exercise/health *and* algorithms/data structures). Its gating-mass token cloud is dominated by *the, and, of*; MI-reweighting after removing the five top function words surfaces the fitness theme but not the algorithm theme, because latent F97783 ("Binary search loop condition") activates only at the `<=` operator of a binary search and can contribute nothing but the symbol `<=` to a token-level view.
- **Bottom-up domains are large, few, and very unequal (layer 30).** 11 domains from 128 experts: computing **28**, text form **21**, mathematics and reasoning **20**, applied science **18**, language **8**, culture and recreation **7**, entities and identifiers **7**, safety and advice 5, commerce 5, society and law 5, time and place 4. Computing + math = 37.5% of the layer; art appears in exactly one label (E54, "animals and classical arts"). The three non-subject domains (text form, language, entities/identifiers) take 36 experts = **28.1%** of the layer.
- **The Jaccard tail lands inside domains.** Of the 20 highest-J pairs at layer 30, **19 join two experts of the same domain**, against a 12.8% base rate for same-domain pairs; shared latents are a median 12.3% of those experts' dominant sets, so labels are not driven by the overlap. The single cross-domain pair (E11, E95) links LaTeX algebra with chemical/algebraic notation.

## Authors & Credibility

Anyuan Zhuo (ByteDance Seed; Shanghai University of Finance and Economics), Shaohua Zhang (ByteDance Seed), Yuan Lin (ByteDance Seed, corresponding), Pinyan Lu (SUFE, corresponding). This is an industry-lab interpretability effort with an academic co-author whose group works on LLM internals (the cited ref. [20] on function tokens is from the same Lin/Lu collaboration), so the authors have prior form on representation-level analysis rather than only on MoE systems.

Credibility signals are strong on the mechanical side: the central identity is a two-line algebraic fact that anyone can re-derive; the hyperparameters, data mixture, token counts, FVU per layer, initialization, κ schedule, and the exact prompts for all four LLM calls are disclosed; SAEs and code are released under a ByteDance-Seed GitHub org. The paper also volunteers an AI-use statement distinguishing "AI helped us write code" from "GPT-5.5 is a component of the method", which is unusually candid. The limitations section (Appendix A) names the SAE-fidelity problem, the undisclosed Qwen3 data mixture, and the single-model scope without being prompted to.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The decomposition identity is the real contribution and it is genuinely elegant: prior SAE-circuit work (Marks et al.; Ameisen et al.) attributes *between* layers and components, whereas this attributes *within* one layer, from a latent back to the experts that produced it, and does so exactly rather than by gradient approximation. It is also nearly free — no training, no intervention, just the encoder row dotted with each expert's output. The reframing from "which corpus did this expert fire on" to "which features does this expert drive" is the right question, and the answer (experts are sub-domain specialists) is more informative than either side of the existing debate (domain-specialized vs. token-identity-driven). Relative to Herbst et al. [8], which also leaves the domain level behind, the advance is real but narrower than the paper implies: [8] already concluded experts are fine-grained task experts, and this paper's distinctive claim is that the *granularity itself is read off* rather than selected from a hand-set cluster grid. That is a methodological improvement, not a new phenomenon.

**Rigor — what is well done.** The rank-profile control in §4.1 is the strongest piece of methodology in the paper. The obvious objection to any "contribution is concentrated" claim is that `c` literally contains `g_j` as a factor and top-k renormalization already makes gating uneven, so the authors construct a directly comparable statistic (both profiles sum to 1 over the 8 ranks) and show the crossing point and the tail reversal. They then quantify the ReLU-truncation confound rather than hand-waving it. The negative-contribution argument (a suppressed latent is unobservable, so including negatives biases the statistic) is correct and rarely stated this cleanly. The τ = 0.2 choice is defended against the uniform baseline 1/128.

**Rigor — where it is thin.** Four concrete gaps:

1. **No τ sensitivity analysis.** Every structural number in §4.2–4.3 (57.4%/62.1%/56.7% single-dominator, 37.5–40.5% disjoint pairs, 0.25–0.57% of pairs above J = 0.05) is computed at a single threshold. Degree distributions are exactly the quantity that moves most with τ, and the paper's own remark that "the ceiling of five permitted by τ is reached in no layer" is a hint that τ is doing real work. A sweep over τ ∈ {0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5} is the single cheapest missing experiment.
2. **No null model for the dominance graph.** A shuffled or random-attribution control — e.g. assign each latent's activation to a randomly chosen activated expert with probability proportional to g_j, then rebuild the graph — would show how much of the 62.1% single-dominator rate is forced by the arithmetic rather than learned. Without it, the reader cannot tell whether "most latents have one dominator" is a finding about Qwen3 or a property of ReLU'd, renormalized weighted sums.
3. **The theme-membership test is circular in a way the random baseline does not fix.** The theme string ("Java development") is induced from the word cloud built from the *same* latent explanations that are then judged for membership. The 86.7% is therefore closer to an upper bound on self-consistency than an estimate of specialization. The right control is a *within-expert* baseline: what fraction of latents this expert **activates on** (not dominates) belong to the theme? That isolates the contribution of the dominance construction, which is the paper's actual claim. As written, the 1.9% random baseline only shows that different experts' dominant sets are about different things — which the Jaccard analysis already established.
4. **§5.1's title overclaims its evidence.** "Dominant-set explanations carry more information than tokens" is supported by exactly one expert (E72), one MI-reweighted variant, and a qualitative reading. There is no across-expert measure — no human or LLM informativeness rating over all 128 experts, no count of how many experts have a theme invisible in their token cloud. The E72 example is well chosen and genuinely persuasive as an illustration; it is not evidence for a general claim.

**Reproducibility.** Good on artifacts, awkward on data. Hyperparameters, prompts, and FVU are all reported and the SAEs are released, but the exact Olmo 3 mixture (5/8 pre-training, 1/4 CoT, 1/8 SFT) is described by proportion rather than by named subsets, so the 1.5B-token validation set cannot be reconstructed exactly. Since every number in the paper is a corpus statistic, this is a real obstacle to replication even though the code runs.

**Significance.** If the finding holds, it matters for three things the paper names but does not test: expert pruning (remove E50 and Java latents should degrade, front-end latents should not), expert merging (the 26–33 mutual-nearest-neighbour pairs are the natural merge candidates), and load-balancing-loss design (does an auxiliary loss that balances *tokens* destroy the feature-level division of labor that an auxiliary loss balancing *latents* would preserve?). The paper explicitly frames the dominant set as "a testable hypothesis" and then does not test it. That is the largest missed opportunity: a single pruning ablation on 5–10 experts would convert the entire paper from a descriptive taxonomy into a causal claim, and it is cheap relative to training three 2²⁰-latent SAEs on 3.2B tokens each.

**Red flags, ranked.** (a) Single model, single seed per layer, and semantic analysis of only layer 30 — the structural claims span three layers but every interpretation claim rests on one, and layer 30 is also the worst-reconstructed (FVU 25.1%), so a quarter of the layer's variance is outside the latents being interpreted. (b) GPT-5.5 both writes the explanations and judges them, with no human agreement check; the paper reports no inter-rater statistic of any kind. (c) The 11-domain partition comes from a single call with a 6–13 group budget and "aiming for about 10" in the prompt, so the *count* of domains is partly an instruction-following artifact — though the *composition* (three non-subject domains, 28 computing experts) is not, since no domain names were supplied. (d) The §5.3 explanation that expert allocation tracks pretraining mixture proportions is honestly flagged as conjecture, and correctly so: Qwen3 does not disclose its mixture, and the Llama 3 numbers (≈25% math, 17% code) are borrowed from a different model with a different tokenizer and different data pipeline.

**Overall.** A well-executed, honestly scoped descriptive study built on one genuinely nice algebraic observation. The instrument is the contribution; the taxonomy is the demonstration. It should be read as "here is a cheap exact way to ask what an MoE expert does, and here is what it says about one model", not as "MoE experts are sub-domain specialists, established". The causal follow-up is obvious, cheap, and absent.

## Strengths

- The exact decomposition (Eq. 5) is a clean, reusable, essentially free instrument. It requires only expert outputs and one SAE on the layer output, so it transfers to any MoE whose layer output is a weighted sum of experts — including models with shared experts, where the shared path would simply appear as an always-on contributor.
- The rank-profile control in §4.1 anticipates and defuses the most obvious trivial-explanation objection, and the truncation count (1.29 of 8 at layer 30) closes the second one. This is the kind of self-criticism that makes a descriptive result trustworthy.
- Genuine care in the interpretation pipeline: mixing top-5 and random-5 contexts specifically to counter the near-duplicate bias of top activations, allowing `Uninterpretable`, and using logit effects for output features where contexts are diffuse.
- Scale of the semantic analysis: 128 experts × up to 2000 latents at layer 30, with all 128 word clouds published in the appendix (Figs. 9–16) and a full expert→label→domain table (Table 3). A reader can audit any individual claim.
- Three layers spanning shallow/mid/deep, with the structural findings reported consistently across all three rather than cherry-picking the best layer.
- The three non-subject domains (text form, language, entities/identifiers, 28.1% of the layer) are a genuinely interesting negative result about human taxonomies: a subject-matter partition would have to scatter those 36 experts and could never recover them as units.
- Full prompt disclosure for all four LLM calls, plus an explicit AI-use statement. This is more transparency than most LLM-in-the-loop interpretability papers provide.

## Limitations

- **Single model, single SAE per layer, one seed.** No claim about generality across MoE architectures, expert counts, or routing objectives is tested. The authors say so in Appendix A, but it means every headline number is an n = 1 observation.
- **SAE fidelity bounds everything.** FVU is 25.1% at layer 30 — the layer on which all interpretation claims rest. "Exact decomposition" is exact with respect to the SAE's linear readout of y, not with respect to the model's computation; feature splitting and absorption (Chanin et al. [2], cited but not measured) mean the latents are not a clean basis, and a split feature would be dominated by whichever expert happens to drive the dominant fragment.
- **Positive-only contributions.** Discarding negatives is well-motivated but means an expert that *suppresses* a latent is invisible to the analysis. An expert whose role is inhibitory would look like it dominates nothing relevant, and at layer 4 where 2.09 of 8 experts are truncated per (prefix, latent) pair, this is not a small blind spot.
- **τ = 0.2 is fixed and unjustified by any sweep.** The whole degree and overlap analysis is conditional on it.
- **No causal validation.** The pruning/merging/steering hypothesis is stated and deferred. Until it is run, "dominant set" is a correlational description of co-activation, not evidence of functional responsibility.
- **Semantic claims rest on a single LLM judge with no human check.** Explanations, theme judgments, expert labels, and the domain partition all come from GPT-5.5. There is no human agreement rate, no second judge, and no adversarial probe of the judge (e.g. does it say "yes" to a theme/explanation pair drawn from a different expert at a similar rate?).
- **Layer 30 only for the taxonomy.** The paper justifies this by noting the structural trends match across layers, but the *content* of specialization at layers 4 and 46 is never examined, so claims like "specialization sits below domain granularity" are verified at one depth.
- **No comparison against the strongest existing baseline on the same footing.** Herbst et al. [8]'s expert-level labeling is discussed but never re-run or quantitatively compared; the paper argues its instrument is better in principle rather than showing it produces a better account in practice.
- **Domain count is prompt-sensitive.** The 6–13 range and "aiming for about 10" in the grouping prompt mean the 11-domain result is partly a budget artifact; a different budget would change the allocation statistics in Fig. 5 without changing the underlying labels.

## Relevance

直接命中 **LLM Hidden States & Interpretability**：把 SAE 潜变量当作分析单元、用精确的线性分解把"专家—特征"对应关系读出来，是 MoE 可解释性里少见的、几乎零成本且可复用的工具，且代码与 SAE 权重开源、提示词全公开，适合直接拿来做方法基线或改造到别的 MoE 上。对 **LLM Training Stability & Observability** 也有间接价值：主导集合给出了一个可监测的"分工健康度"指标（度数分布、Jaccard 尾部），可用来检验负载均衡损失是否把专家的特征级分工压平，尽管本文没有做这个实验。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. **Does the dominant set survive ablation?** Ablate expert E50 at layer 30 and measure the activation drop on its dominant latents versus a matched set of latents it merely fires on. If the dominant set is functionally real, the drop should be sharply larger — this is the experiment that would make the paper's central construct causal, and it is cheap.
2. **How much of the concentration is arithmetic rather than learned?** Build the null model: reassign each latent's activation to a random activated expert with probability ∝ g_j, rebuild the dominance graph, and report the single-dominator rate. If the null already gives ~50%, the 62.1% is a much weaker finding.
3. **What is the within-expert baseline for theme membership?** For E50, what share of the latents it *activates on* (regardless of dominance) are Java-related? The gap between that and 86.7% is the actual value added by the contribution construction; the 1.9% cross-expert baseline measures something else.
4. **How sensitive is the whole picture to τ and to SAE width?** Sweep τ ∈ {0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5} and, ideally, train a second SAE at a different width or seed on layer 30 and check whether E50/E57/E1/E76 re-emerge with the same dominant sets.
5. **Do the 26–33 mutual-nearest-neighbour pairs merge cleanly?** If pairs like E83/E105 (punctuation) or E77/E107 (mojibake) are functionally redundant, merging them should cost little perplexity while merging a random same-degree pair should cost more. This is a direct test of whether Jaccard overlap indexes substitutability.
6. **Does the load-balancing loss shape the division of labor?** Compare Qwen3-30B-A3B against an MoE trained with a different auxiliary loss (or with expert-choice routing) at matched scale; the instrument is model-agnostic, so the prediction "stronger token-balancing flattens the latent-side degree distribution" is directly testable.
7. **Why is layer 30 the hardest to reconstruct (FVU 25.1% vs 7.6% at layer 46)?** Is the mid-layer SAE failing because the features there are genuinely more superposed, or because of the specific Olmo 3 mixture used for activation collection? A reconstruction comparison across training corpora would separate the two.
