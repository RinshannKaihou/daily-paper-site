> **一句话总结：** 作者指出 QK-Norm 在压住 attention logit 增长的同时抹掉了 query/key 范数携带的 token 级信息（query 范数=行温度、key 范数=列权重），于是用可学习多频正弦/余弦映射 Φ(x)=[cos(xWp+b); sin(xWp+b)] 替换 QK-Norm：特征范数恒为 √m 因而 logit 有与输入幅度无关的上界，但相位随幅度变化，把"幅度"从向量长度搬到了表示模式里。在 190M–7B、Dolma/OLMo 混合数据、完全对齐的预训练设置下，QK-MAP 在全部 5 个尺度的训练/验证 CE 上都最低（7B 验证 CE 2.86 vs QK-Norm 2.90、Standard 2.89），7B 上 OLMES Avg-8 达 56.75（Standard 55.86，+0.89；QK-Norm 55.48，+1.27），但 190M 上反而输给 QK-Norm（39.26 vs 39.91）、760M 上输给 Standard（49.73 vs 50.24）。

> **TL;DR:** QK-Norm is now standard in OLMo 3, Qwen 3, Chameleon and Gemma 3 because growing query/key norms blow up attention logits, but ℓ2/RMS normalization makes representations with the same direction *exactly* magnitude-blind (the paper proves the radial derivative decays as O(r⁻³) once ε>0), throwing away a signal the softmax actually uses. QK-MAP replaces normalization with a learned multi-frequency phase map whose norm is pinned at √m (so logits are bounded by m/√d_h·‖A_Q‖‖A_K‖ regardless of input scale) while its coordinates still move with magnitude. Pretrained on the OLMo-mix-0625 Dolma mixture at 190M/370M/760M/1B/7B with matched backbone, tokens, batch and seeds, it gets the lowest validation CE at every scale (7B: 2.86 vs 2.90 QK-Norm, 2.89 Standard) and the best 7B Avg-8 on OLMES eight-task zero-shot (56.75 vs 55.86 Standard, +0.89; 55.48 QK-Norm, +1.27), leading at 16/16 nearby checkpoints. A norm-shuffling probe is the most convincing evidence: reassigning key norms across sequences costs +0.194 CE for QK-MAP and +0.188 for Standard but only +0.00012 for QK-Norm — QK-Norm's models genuinely stopped using token-specific key magnitude. Read it if you care about attention architecture or logit control; skip if you need stability evidence, because despite the stability framing the paper never reports a loss spike, logit trace or divergence rate, and the downstream win exists only at 7B.

## 核心贡献

- **重新拆解 query/key 范数在 attention 里的功能，而不是只把它当作不稳定源**：由 qᵀk = ‖q‖‖k‖cosθ 出发，指出 query 范数按行缩放 logit，等价于一个 **query 相关的逆温度**（控制该 token 读取上下文的"锐度"），key 范数按列缩放，决定某个 token 对不同 query 的**吸引力**。两者都是 token 特有的信息通道，而 QK-Norm 同时把它们清零。
- **形式化 QK-Norm 的信息损失**：Proposition 1 / A.1 证明对 z=ru，ε=0 时 N₀(ru)=N₀(u) 完全不变；ε>0 时 ∂N_ε(ru)/∂r ∝ ε‖u‖²/(r²c+ε)^{3/2}，即一旦投影后的范数离开小 ε 区间，径向敏感度以 **O(r⁻³)** 衰减；学习到的 gain g 在所有 token 间共享，无法恢复逐 token 的幅度信号。这把"QK-Norm 抹掉幅度"从直觉变成了可证的命题。
- **提出 QK-MAP（Magnitude-Aware Projection）**：学 Wp∈R^{D×m}、b∈R^m，令 Φ(x)=[cos(xWp+b); sin(xWp+b)]∈R^{2m}（主配置 m=D/2，输出维度仍为 D），然后 q=Φ(x)W_Q、k=Φ(x)W_K，value 路径不变，RoPE 照常施加。核心性质（Prop. 2 / A.2）：‖Φ(x)‖₂²=m 恒成立，而 ∂Φ(ru)/∂r = ‖uWp‖₂ 只要 u∉ker(Wpᵀ) 就非零且**不随 r 增长**——幅度信息被搬进了相位与坐标模式。
- **给出与输入幅度无关的 logit 上界**：Prop. A.3 证明 |s_ij| ≤ (m/√d_h)‖A_Q‖_op‖A_K‖_op，与 ‖x‖、‖y‖ 无关，因此 QK-MAP 在"控住 logit"这一点上不比 QK-Norm 弱——这是它能作为替代方案而非补充方案的前提。
- **论证多频率的必要性**：单相位坐标会随幅度周期性重复；Prop. A.4 证明只要存在两个径向频率之比为无理数，r↦Φ(ru) 在 R 上单射（连续分布下几乎必然成立），Prop. A.5 进一步给出紧区间上任意间隔 η 的严格正分离下界 γ(I,η)。这解释了为什么"多频"不是装饰而是消歧手段。
- **跨 5 个规模（190M–7B）的受控预训练对比**：同 backbone、同数据顺序、同 token 预算、同全局 batch（2,097,152 tokens）、同初始化与数据种子，参数量差异 ≤0.033%（7B 处 0.017%，通过收窄 FFN 宽度 11008→10328 换取周期映射的参数）。报告训练/验证 CE、OLMES Avg-8、attention 分配与文本扰动四类证据。
- **三个机制层面的诊断实验**：(1) **key-norm 置换干预**（同位置跨序列重排范数、保持方向与范数集合不变）显示 QK-MAP 像 Standard 一样依赖 token 级 key 幅度，QK-Norm 则几乎完全不依赖；(2) 7B 上 **first-key attention 在 11 个域 × 5 个长度共 55/55 个格子都低于两个 baseline**；(3) 合成长上下文里 **evidence-to-distractor attention SNR 在每个长度都最高**，且从单 needle 扩展到 2/3 needles 与混合任务。

## Key Contributions

- **A functional decomposition of query and key norms, not just a stability story.** The paper separates the two roles: the query norm is a row-wise, query-dependent inverse temperature; the key norm is a column-wise salience. This is the conceptual move that makes "QK-Norm discards information" a precise claim rather than a vague complaint, and it predicts *which* experiments should show the loss (key-norm shuffling, per-token attention contrast).
- **Provable radial blindness of QK-RMSNorm, including the finite-ε case.** Proposition A.1 does not stop at the trivial ε=0 invariance; it bounds ∂q_i/∂r_i and ∂s_ij/∂r_i by terms ∝ ε/(r²c+ε)^{3/2}, i.e. O(r⁻³) decay, and notes that a token-shared gain cannot restore per-token magnitude. Most QK-Norm discussions assert the invariance; this one quantifies how fast the escape hatch (ε) closes.
- **QK-MAP itself: bounded norm, unbounded sensitivity.** A shared periodic map Φ with ‖Φ(x)‖²=m exactly, Jacobian Gram matrix J_ΦᵀJ_Φ = WpWpᵀ (so the radial derivative is ‖Wpᵀu‖₂, independent of r), and a logit bound m/√d_h·‖A_Q‖‖A_K‖ that holds for any input norm. The construction is deliberately RoPE-like — bounded coordinates, multiple frequencies, phase as the carrier — but applied to magnitude instead of position, and learned rather than fixed.
- **A frequency-ambiguity analysis that justifies m>1.** Propositions A.4/A.5 turn "use several frequencies" from folklore into a sufficient condition: an irrational frequency ratio gives injectivity on ℝ, and compactness gives a uniform separation γ(I,η) on any bounded magnitude range. The failure mode (all phases colliding simultaneously) is written down explicitly as (r₁−r₂)uᵀw_j ∈ 2πℤ for every j.
- **A genuinely causal probe of magnitude use.** The norm-shuffling intervention holds directions and the multiset of norms fixed and permutes only the *assignment* across sequences at the same position, so any CE change is attributable to token-specific magnitude. The result is a clean three-way separation: Standard +0.188, QK-MAP +0.194, QK-Norm +0.00012. This is the paper's strongest single piece of evidence.
- **Cross-scale pretraining at five sizes with tight matching.** 190M/370M/760M/1B/7B on 2B/4B/8B/10B/20B tokens, one seed each, with analytically reported parameter counts per scale (Table 5) and a 16-checkpoint sweep at 7B to preempt "you picked a lucky endpoint." Architecture ablations (direct map / parameter-matched / parameter-matched+residual, plus QK-Clip at 370M) are reported in Appendix E rather than hidden.

## Methodology

**Setup.** Decoder-only LMs at 190M, 370M, 760M, 1B and 7B parameters, trained for 2B, 4B, 8B, 10B and 20B tokens respectively, sequence length 2048, global batch 2,097,152 tokens, AdamW (β=(0.9,0.95), ε=1e-8), weight decay 0.1, grad clip 1.0, 5% warmup + cosine decay to 0.1× peak LR, z-loss 1e-5, BF16 params with FP32 reductions, RoPE θ=500,000, dolma2 tokenizer (100,278 vocab). Data: OLMo-mix-0625-20Bsample (Dolma-based). Pretraining ran on eight A100 40GB GPUs with DDP.

**Three variants per scale.** Standard scaled dot-product attention; QK-RMSNorm applied to projected q and k (ε=1e-6, learned gain); QK-MAP. Within a scale all three share layer count, hidden size, head configuration, tokenizer, mixture, optimizer, token budget, batch size, init seed (12536), data seed (34521) and data order. Because Φ adds parameters, the QK-MAP runs narrow the feed-forward width (7B: 11008→10328) so total parameters match within 0.033% at every scale.

**The map.** Φ(x) = [cos(xWp+b); sin(xWp+b)] with m=D/2, one map shared by the Q and K paths (a deliberate design choice, "map scope: shared by Q and K"), separate W_Q and W_K after it, values from the raw x, RoPE applied after projection. The default cross-scale variant ("QK-MAP-R") adds a learned per-dimension residual Ψ_α(x)=Φ(x)+Diag(α)x with α initialized at zero, so training starts exactly at the bounded periodic map and the residual can only add a bounded amount on the normalized input domain (‖Ψ_α(x)‖₂ ≤ √m + ‖α‖_∞B).

**Evaluation.** OLMES protocol, zero-shot, Avg-8 = unweighted mean accuracy over ARC-Easy (570), PIQA (1000), SciQ (1000), ARC-Challenge (299), COPA (100), HellaSwag (1000), OpenBookQA (500), WinoGrande (1000), max 1000 examples per task with seed 1234. Validation CE is the equal-weight mean of C4, Pile, M2D2-S2ORC and Dolma-6 (Books, Common Crawl, PeS2o, Reddit, Stack, Wiki).

**Mechanistic probes.**
- *Norm shuffling* (190M, three checkpoints, 128 consecutive 512-token C4 validation chunks, FP32): a hook after normalization and RoPE in all 12 layers replaces v_{b,t} by ‖v_{π_t(b),t}‖₂·v_{b,t}/‖v_{b,t}‖₂, i.e. permutes norms across sequences at the same position while preserving each vector's direction. Global (768-d concatenated across heads, matching the RMSNorm axis) and per-head variants; query-only, key-only and joint conditions; three permutations, seed 20260725, paired 95% bootstrap intervals over 128 sequences (10,000 resamples). No-hook / identity-hook / split-and-reconstruct controls agree to 9.54e-7 CE.
- *Attention allocation* (7B step 9465): F-Attn = mean attention mass on the first key, averaged over layers, heads and query positions, on consecutive full-length chunks across 11 domains and lengths 32–512.
- *Evidence contrast*: synthetic prompts with evidence inserted among distractors; SNR of evidence-to-distractor attention for the final question-token query, averaged over layers (and separately, best-layer pooling). 112,200 single-needle examples (600 per setting × 11 depth layouts × 17 lengths from 32 to 600), 201,600 two-needle, 162,000 three-needle, 112,200 mixed-task, seed 20251231.
- *Text perturbation* (190M, following QUEST's protocol): words in a fixed WikiText-103 validation sample replaced by "AAA" at 1.5%/2.5%/5% (312/525/1,023 words), same text for all models, perplexity over all scored targets in 512-token blocks.

**Theory.** Appendix A: radial invariance and O(r⁻³) score sensitivity for QK-RMSNorm (A.1–A.2), bounded features + exact Jacobian Gram + magnitude-independent logit bound (A.3), collision condition and injectivity/separation (A.4).

## Results & Highlights

- **Validation cross-entropy is lowest at all five scales, but the margin shrinks with size.** 7B: 2.86 vs 2.90 (QK-Norm, −0.04) and 2.89 (Standard, −0.03); 1B: 3.07 vs 3.09 (−0.02); 760M: 3.13 vs 3.16 (−0.03); 370M: 3.45 vs 3.52 (−0.07); 190M: 3.98 vs 4.10 (−0.12, −2.9%). Training CE follows the same ordering (7B: 2.23 / 2.24 / 2.25). The direction of the CE claim is consistent, but the effect is largest exactly where it matters least and is within ~1% relative at 1B–7B.
- **Avg-8 at 7B is the one clear downstream win, and it is isolated.** 56.75 for QK-MAP vs 55.86 Standard (+0.89) and 55.48 QK-Norm (+1.27), with QK-MAP first at 16/16 checkpoints from steps 9455–9537 (mean margin 0.45 pts over Standard, range 0.06–0.90). But at 190M it *loses* to QK-Norm (39.26 vs 39.91, −0.65) and at 760M it loses to Standard (49.73 vs 50.24, −0.51); 370M (+0.03 vs QK-Norm) and 1B (+0.08) are ties. So the honest summary is 1 clear win, 2 ties, 2 losses across five scales, not a monotone trend.
- **Norm shuffling cleanly separates the three variants at 190M.** Mean ΔCE on 128 C4 sequences when key norms are reassigned: Standard +0.18816, QK-MAP +0.19360, QK-Norm +0.00012 (95% CI [−0.00009, 0.00033], i.e. indistinguishable from zero). QK-MAP is statistically indistinguishable from Standard here (CI [0.17708, 0.21414] vs [0.18135, 0.19568]) — this is the paper's cleanest evidence that magnitude information survives. The query side is less flattering: QK-MAP +0.00617 vs Standard +0.01651, and per-head key shuffling gives QK-MAP +0.32990 vs Standard +0.76922, so QK-MAP retains *less* magnitude sensitivity than Standard on two of three axes.
- **WikiText-103 perplexity under 5% word→AAA replacement: 153.27 for QK-MAP vs 177.74 QK-Norm (−13.8%) and 184.26 QUEST (−16.8%).** Clean-text perplexities are 95.60 / 111.77 / 116.07 / 122.77 (QK-MAP / QK-Norm / QUEST / Standard), so most of that gap pre-exists perturbation. In relative terms QK-MAP degrades *more*, not less: +60.3% vs +59.0% (QK-Norm), +58.7% (QUEST) and +56.1% (Standard) — the paper discloses this in Appendix G.2, and it undercuts any "robustness" reading of Table 3.
- **First-key attention is lower in 55/55 domain–length cells at 7B.** Mean F-Attn at T=32: 20.49% (QK-MAP) vs 21.11% (QK-Norm) and 22.53% (Standard); at T=512: 3.36% vs 4.21% and 3.85%. The 9.1%–12.7% reduction is quoted against Standard; against QK-Norm the gap at T=32 is only ~2.9% and widens to ~20% at T=512, so the effect is length-dependent rather than uniform.
- **Evidence-to-distractor attention SNR at 7B is highest at every length and every prompt set.** Single needle 0.338 vs 0.272 (Standard) / 0.238 (QK-Norm); two needles 0.426 vs 0.352 / 0.318; three needles 0.506 vs 0.418 / 0.415; multi-task 0.386 vs 0.342 / 0.290. The relative gain over QK-Norm grows from 24.9% at 32 tokens to 85.3% at 600 tokens, and the ordering holds under best-layer pooling as well as average-layer pooling.
- **Architecture ablation is heterogeneous rather than confirmatory.** At 370M, Avg-8 is QK-MAP-D 46.61 > QK-MAP-P 46.08 > QK-MAP-R 45.94, and QK-Clip (Kimi K2's optimizer-side logit control) lands at 45.46; at 760M, D 50.79 > P 50.67 > R 49.73; at 1B the order flips and R 52.11 > P 51.84 > D 51.06. The parameter-matched residual design used for the headline cross-scale runs is therefore the *worst* periodic variant at the two smaller ablation scales.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The reframing of query/key norms as *information channels* rather than only instability sources is a real contribution, and the norm-shuffling probe is the kind of causal test most architecture papers skip. The mechanism itself is a recombination of known parts — learned Fourier features (Tancik et al. 2020; Li et al. 2021), FAN (Dong et al. 2025, same group), and RoPE's bounded-rotation trick applied to magnitude instead of position. That is legitimate engineering, but "QK-MAP" is closer to a well-executed transplant than to a new primitive, and the paper's own related-work section concedes the construction is "related" to all three. The closest prior work on the *problem* — QUEST (query-modulated spherical attention, ICLR 2026) — normalizes keys only and keeps query-dependent temperature, which is precisely a partial solution to the same tradeoff; QUEST appears in Figure 2 and Table 3 but **never in Table 1's CE or Avg-8**, so the paper's main table omits its most direct competitor.

**Rigor.** The matched-comparison discipline is genuinely strong: identical backbone/data/order/seed across variants, parameter counts reported analytically per scale (Table 5, max deviation 0.033%), 16 nearby checkpoints to defuse endpoint cherry-picking, 55/55 domain-length cells, bootstrap CIs on the norm-shuffling probe, and identity-hook controls agreeing to 9.54e-7 CE. Against that: **every scale is a single pretraining run** (experiment seed 12536, one data order), and Avg-8 is an unweighted mean over tasks with N=100 (COPA) and N=299 (ARC-Challenge). At 7B the task-level spread between variants is far larger than the reported Avg-8 delta — ARC-Challenge alone is 39.13 (QK-MAP) vs 32.44 (QK-Norm) vs 37.12 (Standard), a 6.7-point swing on 299 examples — so the +0.89 headline is not separable from sampling noise by any statistic the paper reports. No confidence interval, no second seed, no paired bootstrap on Avg-8 anywhere in the main text.

**The stability claim is never tested.** This is the biggest gap. The entire motivation is that query/key norm growth "can saturate the softmax, reduce attention entropy, and make optimization unstable," and QK-Norm is sold as the fix that OLMo 3, Qwen 3, Chameleon and Gemma 3 adopted. Yet the paper reports **no loss-spike counts, no maximum-logit traces over training, no gradient-norm curves, no attention-entropy measurements, no divergence rate, and no comparison of how many runs had to be restarted**. Standard attention did not diverge at any of the five scales, so the regime where QK-Norm earns its keep is never entered. QK-MAP may well bound logits (Prop. A.3 says it does), but a bound without a stress test does not establish that it delivers QK-Norm's stability at scale — which is the only reason anyone would switch.

**Parameter matching cuts both ways.** QK-MAP pays for its D×D/2 map by narrowing the FFN (7B: 11008→10328, −6.2%). The ablation ordering is suspicious in exactly the direction that matters: the *unmatched* direct map QK-MAP-D wins at 370M (46.61 vs 45.94 for the matched residual R) and 760M (50.79 vs 49.73), and only at 1B does the matched residual variant lead. Since the headline cross-scale runs use QK-MAP-R, the small-scale gains are partly attributable to parameter count rather than the periodic map, and the paper does not run the reverse control (Standard attention with a widened FFN to match QK-MAP-D). At 7B the matched design wins, which is the case that matters, but the ablation as reported is not a clean isolation.

**Proxy metrics carry the long-context story alone.** Lower first-key attention and higher evidence SNR are presented as improvements, but attention sinks are not obviously pathological — they can be functionally load-bearing (a no-op/off-by-one mechanism), and the paper offers no downstream evidence that reducing F-Attn helps anything. RULER, LongBench and NoLiMa are cited in the setup but **no long-context benchmark is ever run**; the 7B models are trained at sequence length 2048 with no long-context stage, so the 600-token synthetic SNR numbers are the only evidence and they measure attention allocation, not task accuracy.

**Regime and scope.** 7B models see 20B tokens — roughly one-seventh of Chinchilla-optimal and far below any production budget, which is also the regime where instabilities are mildest. One data mixture (OLMo/Dolma), one tokenizer, one architecture family, dense attention only. QK-Norm's real deployment is in large MoE models (Qwen3, OLMo 3, Chameleon, Gemma 3) where logit growth is worst; none of that is touched. There is also **no throughput or memory accounting**: the periodic map adds a D×D/2 matmul plus elementwise sin/cos per layer per token — roughly 25% on top of the Q and K projections — and calling QK-MAP "a practical alternative to QK normalization" without a single wall-clock number is an overclaim. Minor reproducibility wrinkle: the 190M architecture metadata were "reconstructed from archived training code," and the norm-shuffling probe uses QK-MAP-D while Table 1 uses QK-MAP-R, so the mechanistic evidence and the performance evidence come from different model variants.

## Strengths

- **The problem framing is correct and useful.** Splitting the query norm (row-wise temperature) from the key norm (column-wise salience) explains *why* QK-Norm costs something and predicts which probes should detect the loss. That decomposition is reusable even by people who never adopt QK-MAP.
- **Theory that actually bounds the thing it claims to bound.** Prop. A.3 gives |s_ij| ≤ (m/√d_h)‖A_Q‖‖A_K‖ independent of input norm — a real, checkable guarantee, not an asymptotic hand-wave. Prop. A.1's O(r⁻³) decay for finite ε is a sharper statement than the usual "normalization removes scale," and it correctly notes that a token-shared gain cannot restore per-token magnitude.
- **The norm-shuffling intervention is excellent experimental design.** Holding directions and the norm multiset fixed while permuting only the assignment makes the +0.00012 vs +0.188 contrast a clean causal statement about QK-Norm's information loss, with paired bootstrap intervals and hook controls at 1e-6 CE. This single experiment justifies the paper's premise better than the loss curves do.
- **Unusually honest hedging in places.** The abstract says "lower training and validation loss than both" (true at all scales) and only claims best average accuracy "at 7B parameters" (also true) — the overclaim is confined to the intro's "most model scales." Appendix G.2 openly states that QK-MAP's *relative* degradation under word replacement is worse than QK-Norm's and QUEST's, and that Table 3 is a matched comparison among their own checkpoints rather than a reproduction of QUEST's absolute numbers.
- **Real pretraining at five scales, not a toy study.** 7B/20B tokens on 8×A100-40GB with OLMo integration is a meaningful-scale result for an academic group, and the 16-checkpoint sweep (QK-MAP first at all 16, margin 0.06–0.90) is a credible defense against endpoint luck.
- **Clean release.** Code, integration helpers, training scaffolding, both probe scripts and the OLMES aggregation scripts are published, with all hyperparameters, seeds and checkpoint steps in the appendix.

## Limitations

- **No stability evidence of any kind**, despite stability being the stated motivation: no spikes, no logit/entropy traces, no divergence counts. Whether QK-MAP can replace QK-Norm in the large-scale, high-LR, MoE settings where QK-Norm is actually used is untested.
- **Single seed per scale and no error bars on downstream metrics.** With COPA at N=100 and ARC-Challenge at N=299, the 7B Avg-8 margin (+0.89) is comparable to task-level noise; the 190M (−0.65) and 760M (−0.51) regressions are equally uninterpretable without variance estimates.
- **The 7B models are heavily undertrained (20B tokens)** and trained at 2048 context with no long-context stage, so both the loss gains and the attention-allocation findings sit in a regime far from production.
- **QUEST — the most relevant magnitude-preserving baseline — is excluded from the main CE/Avg-8 table**, appearing only in the 190M WikiText perturbation and a key-norm illustration. Norm×Direction is cited but never compared; QK-Clip is tested at 370M only.
- **The parameter-matching control is one-directional.** QK-MAP narrows the FFN to compensate; the paper never runs Standard attention with a widened FFN to match QK-MAP-D, so the small-scale ablation ordering (D > P > R) cannot be attributed cleanly to the map.
- **Long-context benefit is proxy-only.** No RULER/LongBench/NoLiMa accuracy, and no argument for why lower first-key attention should be desirable rather than merely different.
- **No efficiency accounting.** Extra D×D/2 matmul plus sin/cos per layer per token, unquantified in FLOPs, latency or memory — a material omission for a claimed drop-in replacement.
- **The paper itself flags** that the norm-shuffling probe "does not isolate which part of the periodic representation produced that variation," and that the 190M metadata were reconstructed rather than resolved from configs.

## Relevance

直接命中「LLM Training Stability & Observability」：它处理的正是 attention logit 增长这一预训练不稳定源头，并且难得地给出了 logit 的幅度无关上界（Prop. A.3）而不是只报 loss。对关心 attention 内部机制（query 温度 vs key 显著度、attention sink、幅度信息编码）的读者也有价值；但要注意它的证据几乎全是 CE/困惑度与合成探针，真正的稳定性指标（spike、logit 轨迹、发散率）一个都没测，所以"可以替代 QK-Norm"目前只是合理性论证，不是实证结论。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does QK-MAP actually prevent loss spikes? Run the same comparison at high LR / small batch / bf16-only on a 1B MoE, and report max |logit|, attention entropy, gradient norm and spike counts — the metrics QK-Norm was adopted to fix.
- Why does the advantage invert between scales (QK-MAP loses Avg-8 at 190M and 760M, wins at 7B)? Is the 190M regression a real capacity effect, or noise from COPA/ARC-C's 100–299 examples? Two or three seeds per scale would settle it.
- What happens if the periodic map is *added to* QK-Norm rather than replacing it — i.e. normalize q/k and concatenate a bounded phase branch? That would separate "magnitude information helps" from "removing normalization helps."
- How much of the gain comes from the map versus the FFN-width trade? Run Standard attention with FFN width 10328 and QK-MAP-D with 11008 at 370M and 760M.
- Is the frequency set learning anything structured? Inspect Wp after training — do the learned radial frequencies spread across scales, collapse, or drift toward the irrational-ratio condition of Prop. A.4? The injectivity guarantee is only "almost surely at initialization."
- Does the effect survive beyond 2048-token training and into real long-context evaluation (RULER, LongBench, NoLiMa)? The 600-token synthetic SNR gain of 85% over QK-Norm predicts a large retrieval advantage that is never tested on a task.
- What is the wall-clock and memory cost per token at 7B, and does it change the throughput-per-loss comparison against QK-Norm enough to matter at scale?
- Does the per-dimension residual Diag(α) grow during training, and does its growth reintroduce unbounded logits in practice? Prop. A.3's bound holds for the pure map, but the default variant adds ‖α‖_∞B.
