> **一句话总结：** 作者把 RLVR 的早熟收敛从"熵坍塌"改写为"锐化压力"问题——由 KL 正则化目标的隐式最优策略 π\* ∝ π_ref·exp(R/β) 定义锐度因子 κ=(r⁺−r⁻)/β 及逐步版 κ_eff，并提出 reward budgeting：每个 prompt 只发一份总量为 1 的奖励预算、按正奖励 gap 比例分给各 rollout，使答对 k 条时正确样本的优势为 1/k−1/G（即 κ=1/(βk)，难题锐化强、易题锐化弱）；把它作为即插即用的优势构造接到 GRPO/DrGRPO/GSPO/DAPO/ClipCov/KLCov 六种基线上，Qwen3-8B 与 Llama3.2-3B-Instruct 的 12 个"基线×模型"组合平均分全部提升（Qwen3-8B 上 DAPO 61.94→66.60，Llama 上 DrGRPO 24.71→30.51），且训练熵反而普遍下降。

> **TL;DR:** The paper argues that "entropy collapse" is a symptom rather than the cause of RLVR plateaus: in a 500-step intervention on Qwen3-8B trained with GRPO on DAPO-Math-17K, adding an entropy bonus either to the loss or to the reward raised entropy substantially but produced no lasting gain once the intervention was removed. It instead analyzes the implicit KL-regularized target π\* ∝ π_ref·exp(R/β) and defines a sharpness factor κ (how reward gaps become probability-ratio gaps) plus an operational κ_eff measured as post-update log-odds change. The proposed fix, reward budgeting, replaces independent unit rewards with a fixed per-prompt budget redistributed proportional to positive reward gaps, giving κ_RB = 1/(βk) versus κ_DRGRPO = 1/β and κ_GRPO = 1/(βσ). Plugged into six baselines on Qwen3-8B and Llama3.2-3B-Instruct (trained on DAPO-Math-17K, evaluated on AMC23/AIME24/AIME25/MATH500/OlympiadBench-Physics), it improves the 5-benchmark average in all 12 combinations — DAPO 61.94 → 66.60 (+4.66) and GRPO 59.87 → 66.53 (+6.66) on Qwen3-8B, DrGRPO 24.71 → 30.51 (+5.80, pass@8) on Llama3.2-3B — while the entropy-based ClipCov/KLCov variants sit at or below plain DAPO (57.98 and 61.11 vs 61.94). Worth reading for anyone working on RLVR advantage design or plateau diagnostics; the mechanism story is more convincing than the headline numbers, which rest on single seeds and 25–30-problem benchmarks.

## 核心贡献

- **把"熵坍塌"重新命名为"早熟收敛"，并用干预实验反驳熵的因果地位**：在 Qwen3-8B + GRPO 上做 500 步的两种熵干预（把熵加进 loss、把熵加进 reward，系数均 0.01），撤掉干预后两条曲线都回到基线水平，没有留下更高的平台——作者据此主张熵是诊断性相关量，而不是充分的干预靶点。
- **提出锐度因子 κ 与可测的有效锐度 κ_eff**：从 KL 正则化 RL 的等价形式（最小化到 π\* ∝ π_ref·exp(R/β) 的 reverse KL）出发，用 κ=(r⁺−r⁻)/β 刻画"奖励差被指数放大成概率比差"的强度；κ_eff 则定义为一次更新后最大/最小优势响应的 log-odds 变化之差，把全局量下沉到 step 级。
- **指出熵–准确率曲线主要是"平均效应"的产物**：文献里常见的"熵降→准确率平台"曲线通常来自 11–12 条 run 的平均，单条 run 的熵轨迹差异很大；作者据此提醒不要用平均曲线做因果解读（§3.3）。
- **提出 reward budgeting（RB）这一即插即用的优势构造**：每个 prompt 减去组内最小奖励、只保留正 gap、把 gap 归一化成总和为 1 的预算再中心化，得到 A_i = c_i/k − 1/G；答对的 rollout 越多，每条分到的份额越小，优势 gap 自动收缩。
- **给出有限 G 的精确目标函数**：二值奖励下 RB 的期望（未裁剪）score-function 更新恰是 J_{G−1} = E_p[f_{G−1}(p_correct)] 的梯度，f_H(p)=Σ_{m=1}^H (1−(1−p)^m)/m 凹且边际收益递减；一般奖励下只在大 G 极限下给出 shifted log-mean 解释。
- **统一了不同算法的锐度谱**：DRGRPO 的 κ=1/β 恒定、GRPO 系的 κ=1/(βσ) 在 k∈{1,G−1} 处取极大、RB 的 κ=1/(βk) 随难度单调，用一张表（Table 1）把"哪种算法在什么难度上锐化最狠"讲清楚。
- **跨 2 个模型 × 6 个基线的系统性验证**：12 个组合的平均分全部提升，且训练熵没有上升（多数低于基线），支持"提升不依赖保留熵"这一主张。

## Key Contributions

- **Reframes premature convergence as an update-dynamics problem rather than an entropy problem.** A controlled 500-step intervention (entropy in the loss vs. entropy in the reward, both with coefficient 0.01, then reverted) shows no post-intervention gain, which the authors read as evidence that raising entropy is not sufficient to move the plateau.
- **Introduces the sharpness factor κ and its step-level counterpart κ_eff.** κ=(r⁺−r⁻)/β is derived from the implicit target of KL-regularized RL; κ_eff is an operational statistic (change in sequence log-odds between a max-advantage and min-advantage response after one update) that can be logged during training. Entropy, κ and κ_eff are positioned as three different granularities: dispersion, global quality-weighted amplification, and per-step quality-weighted amplification.
- **Documents the averaging artifact.** The familiar entropy–accuracy correlation is shown to be largely a property of curves averaged over many runs (11 in the cited work, 12 in the paper's Figure 1); individual methods have visibly different entropy trajectories.
- **Proposes reward budgeting**, a three-line change to advantage construction: subtract the group minimum, normalize positive gaps to a unit budget, center. Under binary rewards each correct rollout receives 1/k, so the advantage gap shrinks as success becomes common.
- **Proves an exact finite-G objective for the binary case.** The expected unclipped estimator equals ∇f_{G−1}(p_correct), where f is concave with diminishing returns and approaches log p as G→∞. This is the paper's main theoretical claim and it is exact at the G=8 used in experiments, not merely asymptotic.
- **Provides a sharpness taxonomy across algorithms** (Table 1): constant pressure for DrGRPO, U-shaped-in-difficulty pressure for GRPO/GSPO/DAPO/ClipCov/KLCov, monotone-decreasing-in-easiness pressure for RB.
- **Shows the gains are not a KL artifact.** A 2×2 ablation (KL on/off × RB on/off) on Qwen3-8B+GRPO separates the two effects: dropping KL alone gives +0.40 average, RB gives +6.66 with KL and +5.38 without.

## Methodology

**Analysis layer.** The starting point is the equivalence between J_RL(θ)=E[R]−β·D_KL(π_θ‖π_ref) and minimizing reverse KL to π\*(γ) ∝ π_ref(γ)exp(R(γ)/β). For a higher-reward response o⁺ and lower-reward o⁻, π\*(o⁺)/π\*(o⁻) = [π_ref(o⁺)/π_ref(o⁻)]·exp((r⁺−r⁻)/β), which defines κ=(r⁺−r⁻)/β. The paper is explicit that κ describes the idealized target, not the speed at which a parameterized optimizer reaches it, and that clipping/normalization mean realized updates need not match. Hence κ_eff: for a chosen (a⁺,a⁻) pair, κ_eff := log[π_new(a⁺)/π_new(a⁻)] − log[ρ(a⁺)/ρ(a⁻)], computed on full sequences by re-scoring the same sampled responses after a batch update and summing token log-probability changes. For an idealized KL mirror step with temperature τ, Lemma A.1 shows κ_eff = ΔÃ/τ exactly.

**Diagnostic experiments.** (i) Entropy intervention: three Qwen3-8B runs differing only in steps 1–500 (baseline GRPO; +0.01·H(π_θ) in the loss; +0.01·H(π_θ(o)) in the reward), all reverted to baseline for steps 500–1000. (ii) κ-controlled study: DRGRPO on DAPO-Math-17K with (r⁺−r⁻, β) = (5, 0.001), (2, 0.001), (1, 0.005) → κ = 5000, 2000, 200 — only κ and its constituents vary. (iii) Conditional association: OLS of accuracy at horizon Δ=10 steps on entropy, then on entropy + κ, to see whether entropy's predictive coefficient survives.

**Method.** For each prompt, sample G rollouts, compute r⁻ = min_j r_j, keep gaps g_i = r_i − r⁻, skip the prompt if all gaps are zero (this kills both all-wrong and all-right groups), set R_i = g_i/Σ_k g_k so the group's total budget is exactly 1, and center: A_i = R_i − (1/G)Σ_j R_j. Under binary rewards this is A_i = c_i/k − 1/G. Nothing else changes — no entropy term, no new hyperparameter, no change to the clipping or KL machinery, so it composes with any GRPO-family optimizer.

**Theory.** For binary rewards and independent on-policy rollouts, the expected centered score-function estimator (pre-clipping) equals ∇f_{G−1}(p_correct) with f_H(p)=Σ_{m=1}^{H}(1−(1−p)^m)/m. The proof conditions on k, uses the score-function identity μ⁺=∇log p and μ⁻=∇log(1−p), and shows E[(G−k)/G · 1{1≤k≤G−1}] = (1−p)−(1−p)^G, giving f′_{G−1}(p) = (1−(1−p)^{G−1})/p, which integrates to f and has f″≤0. For general rewards, A.5 shows the gap-normalized estimator converges almost surely to ∇log(μ−a(x)) where a(x) is the essential infimum of the reward, i.e. a shifted log-mean objective in the large-G limit, with uniform integrability needed for the expected-direction statement.

**Setup.** Training data is DAPO-Math-17K filtered to integer final answers, prompt ≤1024 tokens, response ≤8192 tokens; first 10k examples train, next 1k validate. Eight rollouts per prompt at temperature 1.0, 32 prompts per update, AdamW with weight decay 0.1 and constant LR 1e-6, no warmup. Evaluation uses 8 responses per prompt at temperature 0.7, max 8192 tokens; mean accuracy for Qwen3-8B and pass@8 for Llama3.2-3B-Instruct. Exact match on AMC23/AIME24/AIME25; Qwen3-235B-Instruct as an equivalence judge on MATH500 and OlympiadBench-Physics. Each method is trained until its validation accuracy plateaus.

## Results & Highlights

- **Main table (Table 2, Qwen3-8B, mean@8 over five benchmarks):** RB improves every one of the six baselines on the average. GRPO 59.87 → 66.53 (**+6.66**), DrGRPO 60.10 → 63.31 (+3.21), GSPO 59.44 → 63.65 (+4.21), DAPO 61.94 → 66.60 (**+4.66**), ClipCov 57.98 → 62.35 (+4.37), KLCov 61.11 → 66.03 (+4.92). The best RB configuration (DAPO+RB, 66.60) beats the best unmodified baseline (DAPO, 61.94) by 4.66 points.
- **Hard-benchmark gains are large but coarse-grained.** GRPO+RB on Qwen3-8B goes 82.50 → 92.50 on AMC23, 26.67 → 36.67 on AIME24, 43.33 → 53.33 on AIME25 (each **+10.00**). On Llama3.2-3B-Instruct (pass@8) GRPO+RB goes 7.50 → 20.00 on AMC23 and DrGRPO+RB 30.00 → 42.50, both **+12.50** — the paper's headline number.
- **MATH500 is where the method does least, and occasionally nothing.** Qwen3-8B DrGRPO+RB moves 97.20 → 97.00 (**−0.20**), the only negative cell in the Qwen table; DAPO+RB gives 96.40 → 98.00 (+1.60). Across all 60 benchmark cells in Table 2 there are exactly two non-positive entries (that −0.20 and DAPO+RB on Llama AIME25 at +0.00), so the consistency claim is real, not rhetorical.
- **The gains survive removing the KL reference, but shrink.** Table 3 (Qwen3-8B, GRPO): KL on / RB off = 59.87; KL on / RB on = 66.53 (+6.66); KL off / RB off = 60.27; KL off / RB on = 65.65 (+5.38). Dropping KL alone is worth +0.40 average, so RB carries essentially all of the improvement, and the reference-free variant is slightly worse than the KL-retained one.
- **Entropy-control baselines do not clear their own parent.** On Qwen3-8B, DAPO (61.94) beats both of its entropy variants — KLCov 61.11 and ClipCov 57.98. On Llama3.2-3B, KLCov 25.12 edges DAPO 23.95 while ClipCov 23.26 trails it. Adding RB to those same variants recovers +4.37 and +4.92 respectively, i.e. the advantage-construction change is worth more than the entropy intervention it is being contrasted with.
- **Entropy falls rather than rises under RB.** Figure 5a plots per-step entropy differences from each baseline; nearly all curves sit below the zero line, so the accuracy gains cannot be explained by implicit entropy preservation. Figure 5b shows the κ_eff trajectories under RB oscillate noticeably less than the DAPO/GSPO/ClipCov/KLCov trajectories in Figure 4b.
- **κ tracks convergence timing in the controlled sweep.** With κ = 5000 / 2000 / 200 (fixed everything else), larger κ produces a visibly earlier convergence step t_conv, but the smallest-κ run also ends at a lower final accuracy — a monotone speed/quality trade-off rather than "lower κ is better". The conditional-association analysis (Fig. 6) reports |β′_H| ≪ |β_H| once κ is added as a regressor, i.e. entropy's apparent predictive power largely collapses onto κ in this three-configuration pooled sample.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The core algorithmic change is small — arguably smaller than the framing suggests. Subtracting the group minimum and normalizing by the gap sum is a cousin of reward normalization, and the paper is careful to separate its estimator from the simpler R_i/ΣR_j form (A.2). The genuinely new object is the *analysis*: κ as a quality-weighted amplification factor, and the observation that GRPO's 1/(βσ) peaks at extreme k while RB's 1/(βk) is monotone in difficulty. That is a clean way to say "GRPO sharpens hardest exactly when the batch is already solved," and it lands. But note that the resulting behavior — up-weight hard prompts, down-weight easy ones — is squarely in the territory Dr. GRPO opened when it attacked σ-normalization for inducing difficulty-dependent weighting. The paper cites Dr. GRPO and adopts its advantage form as a baseline, yet never compares the two induced per-prompt gradient weights analytically, which is the obvious comparison a reader wants: RB's 1/k weighting and Dr. GRPO's uniform weighting are two answers to the same diagnosis, and the paper only shows RB wins empirically (+3.21 on Qwen, +5.80 on Llama over DrGRPO), not why.

**The entropy refutation is narrower than the claim.** Two interventions, both additive entropy bonuses with coefficient 0.01, both at one model scale, one dataset, one seed, 500 steps. Neither Clip-Cov's covariance-targeted constraint nor SIREN's self-anchored regularizer nor any token-selective scheme is tested — yet the abstract's "increasing it alone does not reliably change the eventual plateau" is positioned against that whole literature. The paper does hedge ("in our settings", "does not rule out a causal role"), but a 0.01 coefficient is a weak probe: entropy bonuses at that scale often barely perturb the update, and the observed "return to baseline" is exactly what you would predict from a weak perturbation regardless of whether entropy is causal. A coefficient sweep (0.01 / 0.05 / 0.1) would have made this section decisive; its absence leaves the central motivation resting on a null result with an underpowered manipulation.

**Statistical resolution is the biggest practical weakness.** AMC23 has 25 problems and AIME24/AIME25 have 30, so one problem is 2.5 or 3.33 points. The headline "+12.50 on AMC23" for Llama3.2-3B is *five problems*. Every main configuration uses a single training seed, and Appendix D concedes that the eight evaluation responses measure decoding variance, not seed variance. With no error bars, the difference between DrGRPO+RB (30.51) and KLCov+RB (29.94) on Llama is not interpretable, and neither is the ranking among the six RB-augmented variants on Qwen (66.60 vs 66.53 vs 66.03). The *sign* of the average delta is consistent across 12 combinations, which is the strongest evidence in the paper; the *magnitudes* are not.

**A missing baseline row undermines the Llama results specifically.** Nowhere is the pre-RL base model's score reported. Llama3.2-3B-Instruct's GRPO baseline on AMC23 is 7.50 — is the untrained instruct model above or below that? Without a base row the reader cannot tell whether the low Llama numbers reflect a weak starting point, a hard task, or a broken GRPO configuration at 3B scale. Relatedly, Qwen3-8B is scored with mean@8 while Llama3.2-3B is scored with pass@8; the paper flags this ("different metrics and should not be compared directly across model families") but pass@8 rewards sample diversity, which sits awkwardly with a thesis built on entropy *decreasing*.

**Training budget is uncontrolled in a paper about plateaus.** "We train each method until its validation accuracy reaches a plateau" — but RB changes the shape of the objective (f_{G−1} is bounded and saturating), so it plausibly changes *when* that stopping rule fires. No step counts, token counts, or wall-clock are reported per method. Since the dependent variable is literally "the final plateau," comparing methods that stopped at unknown and different times is a confound the paper never addresses, and "same hardware allocation" does not fix it.

**Presentation quibbles.** Table 2 reports the RB rows as deltas only, so absolute RB scores must be reconstructed by addition — workable but needlessly hard to audit, and it hides whether any single RB run is anomalous. Table 1's κ column is arithmetically tidy but the "correct" and "incorrect" advantage columns are worth a second look: RB's incorrect-rollout advantage is pinned at −0.125 (=−1/G) for every k, which means the *only* thing that moves is the correct-side reward 1/k. That is a cleaner statement of the mechanism than the κ column conveys, and it also reveals the degenerate case — at k=1, RB's advantage pair (0.88, −0.13) is *identical* to DrGRPO's, so RB's entire effect lives in the k≥2 regime, i.e. on prompts the model already solves sometimes. The paper's motivating language about "cheap pseudo-modes on easy templates" is therefore slightly off-target: RB is a hard-prompt concentrator, and the benchmark pattern (big gains on AIME, ~nothing on MATH500 at 96–97%) confirms that reading rather than the template-overfitting one.

**Judge independence.** MATH500 and OlympiadBench are graded by Qwen3-235B-Instruct while one of the two trained models is Qwen3-8B. Same-family judging is a known soft spot; it matters less here because those are the two benchmarks with the smallest gains, but it should have been swapped or cross-checked with a non-Qwen judge.

**What is genuinely good.** The theory section is honest in a way that is rare: Lemma A.3 is exact, the concavity proof is two lines and correct, the paper states explicitly that the exact result holds only for binary rewards, that A.4's rate bounds apply to a *different* idealized estimator, and that A.5 gives only an almost-sure large-G direction. Most papers in this genre would have blurred all three into "we prove reward budgeting optimizes a log-mean objective." The same discipline shows in the figure captions, which repeatedly say the trajectories are descriptive and do not establish causation. This is a paper that knows what it has not shown.

## Strengths

- The κ/κ_eff framing is a genuinely useful diagnostic: it is quality-aware (unlike entropy), step-resolved (unlike the global κ), and cheap to log from quantities an RL trainer already computes.
- The binary-reward objective is exact at the G=8 actually used, not just asymptotic — a stronger result than most "principled advantage shaping" papers offer.
- The method is a few lines of code with no new hyperparameter, and it is validated as a *plug-in* on six different optimizers rather than against a single strawman baseline.
- Consistency across 12 model×baseline combinations (58 of 60 benchmark cells non-negative) is more persuasive than any single headline number.
- The KL × RB ablation is the right experiment and is reported honestly, including the fact that the reference-free variant is slightly worse.
- Unusual candor: the limitations appendix, the scope notes in A.2/A.4, the LLM-usage declaration, and captions that refuse to over-read their own figures.

## Limitations

- Single training seed per configuration, no seed-variance estimates; 25–30-problem benchmarks where a single problem is 2.5–3.3 points.
- No pre-RL base-model reference row, so the absolute level of the Llama3.2-3B results is unanchored.
- Training length is determined by a per-method plateau rule whose firing time is never reported, in a paper whose dependent variable is the plateau.
- The entropy-sufficiency claim rests on two additive interventions at one coefficient (0.01), one model, one dataset, one seed.
- Theory covers binary rewards exactly and general rewards only asymptotically as G→∞; the paper's own A.5 notes the sampled-minimum behavior depends on the reward distribution near its lower endpoint, which is exactly the regime where graded/noisy rewards live.
- Not tested on learned reward models, process rewards, agentic or open-ended tasks; the limitation appendix says so directly.
- No group-size sweep (G fixed at 8), so the interaction between G, the concavity horizon H=G−1, and the achievable gain is unexplored.
- κ_eff is a descriptive statistic requiring an extra forward pass to re-score rollouts; the paper does not report its measurement cost or whether it can be computed cheaply enough for production monitoring.

## Relevance

正对 "RL Post-Training Dynamics & Failures" 这一方向：它直接挑战"熵坍塌"这一主流病因叙事，给出可在线记录的替代诊断量（κ_eff 只需对已采样 rollout 重新打分），并给出一个不改框架、可与 GRPO 系任意优化器组合的优势构造。对关心 RLVR 平台期检测与缓解、或想把训练观测指标从 entropy 换成更有解释力的量的工作，这篇的诊断部分比方法部分更值得借鉴。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the 1/k weighting still help when the baseline is already difficulty-aware (e.g. Dr. GRPO without σ, or a curriculum that drops easy prompts)? Is RB's gain partly just re-implementing curriculum?
2. What happens at k=1 versus k≥2 in the per-prompt gradient norm — i.e. can the +4 to +6 point average gain be attributed quantitatively to the easy-prompt down-weighting, rather than to the objective's concavity?
3. How does RB interact with dynamic sampling (DAPO's filter of all-correct/all-wrong groups)? RB already skips both, so is DAPO+RB double-counting the same correction, and would RB help more on top of a *non*-skipping baseline?
4. Does the concavity horizon matter? With G=8, f_7 saturates at H_7≈2.59; a G sweep (4/8/16/32) would test whether larger groups make RB *less* useful because the saturation is reached sooner.
5. Would a coefficient sweep on the entropy interventions (0.01 → 0.05 → 0.1) overturn the "entropy is not sufficient" conclusion, or does the null result hold at every scale?
6. Can κ_eff be turned into a control signal rather than a diagnostic — e.g. adaptively modulate the reward scale to hold κ_eff in a target band — and does that beat a fixed budget?
7. On graded or noisy rewards (RLHF-style), the shifted log-mean result requires μ > a(x) with a(x) the essential infimum; how badly does RB degrade when the reward distribution has heavy mass near its minimum?
