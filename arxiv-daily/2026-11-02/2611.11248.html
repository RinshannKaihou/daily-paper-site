01

The fix that didn't work

Controlled experiment · Appendix C2

The intuitive fix is to keep the state in double precision and accept 32-bit increments. It fails: the drift changes sign instead of disappearing.

The same assumption sits behind NEMO 4.2's single-precision build, which keeps T, S, u, v and SSH in double precision.

02

How the repair works

CORE2 · 126,858 vertices · Δt 900 s · 1948–2007

FESOM2 is run entirely in 32-bit over a 60-year CORE-II hindcast on the 126,858-vertex CORE2 mesh. The timestep is deliberately halved to 900 s, because losing small increments hurts more as Δt shrinks — a harder test than production. Two float32 runs are judged against two double-precision runs, one started from a 2×10−4 K perturbation, so the DP–DP spread — not a deterministic reference — defines the noise floor. Global volume, heat and salt budgets are recorded every simulated day.

The repair covers three roundings: tendencies and the new state are computed in 64 bits, layer thickness is kept in 64 bits, and the per-step remainder is carried forward, weighted by the new thickness. Halo exchange still sends a 32-bit value plus a 32-bit remainder, so communication does not double.

  1. 01

    Accumulate every tracer tendency in 64 bits — advection, diffusion, FCT, implicit columns.

  2. 02

    Form the new content in 64 bits: C = hnTn + ΔtRn + rn.

  3. 03

    Store Tn+1 = fl32(C/hn+1); carry rn+1 = C − hn+1Tn+1.

  4. 04

    Exchange thickness as a 32-bit value plus a 32-bit remainder — bandwidth unchanged.

04

What holds up, what doesn't

Read side by side

Strengths

  • Reports its own first implementation's failure at full strength — 129 ZJ, 36× the control, fronts displaced.
  • Uses paired double-precision runs with a physical perturbation, so “indistinguishable” means inside the model's own chaos.
  • The 64-bit-state / 32-bit-increment experiment overturns the intuitive fix and travels beyond climate science.
  • A staged ablation rules out four alternative causes — and admits Kahan summation alone is not enough.
  • Reproducibility: GPL code, Zenodo commits, PR numbers, plotting scripts, and a map from each result to its revision.

Limitations

  • Fidelity is proven only at nominal 1°; km-scale runs — shortest Δt, largest stagnation risk — are timed, never checked for drift.
  • A salinity gain of 0.47×10−6 psu/decade survives, 14× the control, surface-supplied, mechanistically unexplained.
  • The coupled test cannot resolve a signal smaller than its ~54 ZJ twin spread.
  • The 60-year chaos baseline is one double-precision twin pair, not an ensemble.
  • Speed and fidelity are never measured on the same build; 16-bit kernels and mixed-precision RK remain untested.
05

Open questions

  1. Does the −1.87 → +1.85 sign flip reappear with bf16 gradients and fp32 master weights? Is “keep the master weights in fp32” sufficient?
  2. What conserved-quantity residual would catch this drift in a training run within hours rather than decades?
  3. At 5 km and 120–240 s timesteps, does the drift return despite the conservative update?
  4. Can the Kokkos/Fortran precision-response ratio become a routine CI fingerprint for ports?
