> **一句话总结：** 在一项预注册研究里（73 个任务、CPython/Node.js/SQLite 三个真实运行时的 60 处有文档记录的变更），作者穷举了整个有限修复空间后发现：只用"典型输入"跑全部断言的上线门禁会放行 0.410 的错误修复，而补上一条从 release note 写出的边界输入后降到 0.065（15 个事后新建簇上 0.471→0.061，Holm p=0.002）；一个只看 release note、看不到程序的盲写模型写 3 条输入也能到 0.070，但作者预注册的"让写手自己标注哪些旧输出仍然有效"的守卫反而失效（0.157，未过 0.10 门槛）。

> **TL;DR:** This is a preregistered, exhaustive-enumeration study of *release gating* for LLM repairs after runtime/API migrations: 73 tasks (52 affected test tasks in 49 clusters, 15 built after the effect was first seen) across CPython 3.9→3.12, Node.js 20→24 and SQLite 3.51→3.54, with every candidate executed on the real runtimes against contracts the repairer never sees. Over the complete repair space the all-assertions gate on typical inputs released 0.410 of hidden-wrong procedures; one author-written release-note edge input cut that to 0.065 at unchanged false rejection (0.010), and on the 15 new clusters 0.471→0.061 (Holm p=0.002). A blind GPT-5.6-luna writer that never saw the program reached 0.070 with ≤3 inputs (0.090 without the 3 inputs that coincide with a hidden one). On model repairs (456 attempts/arm), the edge input cut wrong releases per attempt for Qwen3-8B 0.233→0.057, gpt-oss-20b 0.181→0.020, qwen3.6-35b-a3b 0.120→0.018, and GPT-5.6-luna 0.056→0.018 (descriptive, p=0.499), with no loss of correct completion. Self-tests helped but never survived Holm (smallest p=0.132). The paper's honesty is the interesting part: its own preregistered guard failed (0.157 vs a 0.10 ceiling), 233 "hard distractors" that match legacy output on typical inputs still slip through at 0.168, and the typical-input rate is shown to be a property of the menu (0.406→0.271 when the menu grows), not of the gate.

## 核心贡献

- **把"上线门禁是否充分"变成可穷举测量的量。** 修复空间是"按文档片段填空"的有限域，因此作者可以对 52 个受影响测试任务的**每一个**候选程序（三个菜单分别 405→1491→1689 个片段，最多 2310 个程序）跑隐藏契约，直接算出每个门禁的误放行率（FA）与误拒率（FR），而不是只报"修好了几个"。这是变异得分的补集视角：模型无关、可复现、不依赖采样。
- **核心发现：决定门禁好坏的是输入，不是断言族。** 在典型输入上，"全部消费者断言"门禁放行 0.410 的错误程序；加一条从 release note 推出的边界输入后降到 0.065（簇均值 0.406→0.064），误拒率始终 0.010。在效果首次被观察到之后才构建的 15 个新簇上复现：0.471→0.061（差 −0.410，95% CI [−0.589, −0.238]，严格 Holm p=0.002）。
- **盲写手接近作者，但预注册的"守卫"反噬。** 一个隔离的 GPT-5.6-luna 只看 release note + 可见契约，从不看模板、片段、旧程序或隐藏输入，每任务 1 次调用写 ≤3 条输入，把 FA 压到 0.070（去掉 3 条与隐藏输入重合的输入后 0.090）。而预注册的 coverage-aware 守卫 G₃ 只保留写手自称"旧输出仍然约束"的输入，结果只到 0.157，超过 0.10 上限，**M4a 假设判定为不成立**——因为写手恰恰把"揭示变更"的那批输入误标成了 non-binding（G₃ 下陈旧程序只在 42/52 个任务可见失败，W₃ 下是 50/52）。
- **跨模型族复现，且自我测试显著更弱。** 边界输入在三个开放权重族、三个菜单上都降低了每尝试错误放行数（Qwen3-8B 0.233→0.057、gpt-oss-20b 0.181→0.020、qwen3.6-35b-a3b 0.120→0.018），正确完成率不降，跨族判据达成。自测虽然每个族都降了（22→9、98→80、71→47、49→30，各 456 次尝试），但没有一个对比通过 Holm 校正（最小严格 p=0.132）。原因是覆盖与质量双输：Qwen3-8B 只在 30/73 个任务写出有效测试，其自测门禁在整个修复空间上放行 0.240，而盲写手是 0.070。
- **三个冻结阶段逐步加压，并如实报告"通过但没解决问题"。** 大菜单阶段（1491 片段）：边界输入 0.045、盲写手 0.053 通过预注册检验，但 LM3 反向显著——典型输入门禁在更大的菜单上反而更宽松（0.406→0.271），说明 0.410 这个数字是菜单构造的产物。硬干扰项阶段（233 个在典型输入上与旧输出完全一致的错误片段）：总体 FA 0.058 通过，但**这批专门构造的硬错误仍有 0.168 通过**（盲写手 0.153），远高于 0.10 上限。
- **独立的 AI 契约审计，并承认它不等于人审。** 两次盲审每契约一致率 0.91（κ=0.73），标出 34/73 个契约"比需求更严"，主要来自需求里没写的文件保全检查 p0。删掉 p0 与 legacy equality d0 后 0 个隐藏判定改变——即争议检查从不单独决定结论；但 R2（变更类别标错）的争议仍未解决，作者明确不声称人类签署。

## Key Contributions

- **Turns "is this release check adequate?" into an exhaustively measurable quantity.** Because the repair domain is a finite menu of documented fragments, the authors evaluate *every* candidate (405 → 1491 → 1689 fragments; up to 2310 procedures) against withheld contracts on real runtimes, reporting false-acceptance and false-rejection rates over the complete space rather than a sample. This is the complement of a mutation score computed over the space of possible repairs, and it is model-free.
- **The gate's inputs, not its assertion family, decide adequacy.** Typical inputs + all consumer assertions released 0.410 of hidden-wrong programs; adding one release-note-derived edge input cut this to 0.065 with false rejection flat at 0.010. The contrast replicates on 15 clusters created after the effect was first observed (0.471→0.061, Holm p=0.002).
- **A blind frontier-model test writer nearly matches the author, and the preregistered guard fails.** An isolated GPT-5.6-luna (no program, no fragments, no hidden inputs, one call per task, ≤3 inputs) reached 0.070 FA; the preregistered coverage-aware guard G₃ reached only 0.157 because writers mislabel exactly the change-revealing inputs as non-legacy-binding. M4a is reported as not positive.
- **Cross-family replication for edge inputs; self-tests do not reach significance.** The edge-input effect holds in all three open-weight families on all three menus with no loss of correct completion. Self-tests lower wrong releases in every family but no contrast survives Holm correction; a post-hoc writer × repairer matrix (21 tasks where every writer has inputs) is consistent with writer *capability* mattering more than independence (same-model 0.161 vs cross-model 0.187).
- **Adversarial menu construction, honestly reported.** Enlarging the menu reverses the expected direction (typical-input FA falls 0.406→0.271), showing the headline share is menu-dependent; adding 233 legacy-matching hard distractors raises the typical-input gate to 0.337 and leaves 0.168 of the hard wrong programs passing the edge-input gate even though the overall HD1 test "passes" at 0.058.
- **An AI contract audit with a frozen adjudication rule, explicitly not a human sign-off.** Two blind replicates agree on 0.91 of items (κ=0.73); 34/73 contracts are flagged, mostly for a file-preservation check stricter than the stated requirement; removing p0 and d0 changes zero hidden verdicts, but class-label (R2) disputes remain open.

## Methodology

**Task substrate.** 73 tasks: 66 affected tasks in 60 documented-change clusters (CPython 45, Node.js 13, SQLite 8) plus 7 unchanged controls. By change class: 25 silent (call succeeds, result changes), 34 explicit (raises), 7 no-safe-repair (exhaustive search confirms no correct fragment choice exists). Test partition: 52 affected tasks in 49 clusters, 15 of them new. Every change is grounded in pinned, verbatim release-note text; SQLite 3.54.0 (released 2026-10-09) post-dates the models' likely training cutoff. Runtimes are installed and executed offline and unchanged: CPython 3.9.6→3.12.15, Node.js 20.20.2→24.21.0, SQLite shell 3.51.0→3.54.0.

**Formal setup.** A task is (R, p₀, D, X_v, X_h): a consumer requirement, an old procedure correct on the old runtime A₀, a finite repair domain D (each program is a choice of documented fragments for the template's fields), a visible input set, and a disjoint hidden set. *Hidden correctness* requires, for every hidden input, (i) acceptance assertions derived from R plus equality to p₀ on A₀ for the outputs R constrains, and (ii) file preservation — the files left behind equal p₀'s. Four outcomes per attempt: correct completion, false acceptance, false rejection, safe non-release.

**Gate families.** "None"; runtime/schema (exits cleanly, declared shape/types); differential (runtime/schema + equality with p₀ on every visible input); all consumer assertions (runtime/schema + every requirement assertion on every visible input). Coverage arms, all gated by all-assertions: T (typical inputs), E (T + one author edge input derived from the release note, which must make the stale procedure fail a visible check — a QA rule enforced for all 52 affected tasks), W_k (T + first k≤3 valid inputs from a blind writer), G_k (as W_k but only inputs the writer declares legacy-binding), S (T + the repairing model's own pre-repair self-tests), ES (E + S). Expected outputs always come from p₀ on A₀ — no model-written code or model-supplied expected value is ever executed or trusted.

**Blind writer protocol.** One isolated GPT-5.6-luna call per task through a hardened Codex client: all MCP servers, plugins, apps and web search disabled; read-only ephemeral sandbox with an empty working directory; output schema enforced; any use of the model's own tools voids the call. The writer sees the requirement, pinned excerpts, input keys, the typical input with its old output, and the output checks — never the template, fragments, old program, hidden inputs or any result. Inputs are validated fail-closed and data-only (exact keys and types, must run cleanly and deterministically on the old runtime). Yield: 176 kept inputs across 69 tasks, 35 flagged non-binding, 27 dropped (13 old-runtime errors, 12 duplicates, 2 nondeterministic); 3 kept inputs equal a hidden input and are kept as preregistered.

**Repair protocol.** Four arms (T, S, E, ES); each task×arm has 8 cells crossing patch/full representation, diff view off/on, and listed/reversed fragment order. Step 1 is shared by an ungated and a gated branch; the gated branch issues exactly one revision if and only if its gate fails, with pass/FAIL feedback and no expected values. Information is held equal across arms (requirement, excerpts, template, field domains, old procedure, the arm's visible inputs, "EDIT BUDGET: unlimited", permission to abstain). All writer and self-test gates are additionally applied *offline* to each T-arm step-1 candidate, which is what makes the writer × repairer matrix possible with zero extra calls.

**Models.** GPT-5.6-luna via Codex CLI (reasoning effort high, 2,626 calls); Qwen3-8B (4-bit MLX, greedy, 3,266), gpt-oss-20b (2,965), qwen3.6-35b-a3b (2,885) in LM Studio at temperature 0. Local families had to pass an unchanged 12-call admission check (12/12 exact outputs, ≥500 tok/s prefill, ≥32 tok/s decode, ≤0.5 s overhead); gemma-4-12b-qat failed 0/12 with leaked `<|channel>thought` text and was not admitted (a serving-configuration artifact the authors decline to interpret).

**Preregistration and statistics.** Two SHA-256 freezes: F0 before any model call of the study, F-test before any repair, self-test or probe call, each storing a verbatim design copy and hashing its own freeze script. The unit of analysis is the documented-change cluster with tasks averaged within clusters; sign-flip permutation p-values (exact for ≤16 clusters, else 200,000 Monte Carlo flips); whole-cluster bootstrap 95% CIs; Holm adjustment within each family. Where the frozen code and the frozen design text disagreed on the Holm family size ("over 3" vs "over 4"), the paper reports the strict values and discloses both (M4c: 0.002 strict vs <0.001 code; gpt-oss H2: 0.132 vs 0.088) — no decision changes.

**Three frozen stages.** (1) Main menu, 405 fragments. (2) Large menu, 1,491 fragments / 1,986 procedures, built by applying one or two author-written operators to existing fragments (documented siblings, builtin/string/SQLite-function swaps, integer-literal edits), each checked against one of 63 newly pinned official pages, filtered by a visible-only "runs cleanly on the typical input" rule, with the step-1 prompt capped at 2,048 tokens. (3) Hard-distractor menu: 233 fragments whose programs match the legacy output on typical inputs but are hidden-wrong (323 of 359 hard-origin programs turned out hidden-wrong), giving 1,689 fragments / 2,310 procedures, plus two additional blind writers (qwen3.6-35b-a3b and Qwen3-8B) using a new prompt with no legacy-binding flag.

**AI contract audit.** Two blind GPT-5.6-luna calls per task (146 calls) scoring five rubric items (R1 material documents the change; R2 class is right; R3 legacy equality is a reasonable reading; R4 no assertion stricter than the requirement; R5 contract complete). An item counts as "no" only if both replicates say no; contracts were never changed after the audit.

## Results & Highlights

- **Complete repair space, main menu (Table 1).** The all-consumer-assertions gate on typical inputs released **0.410** of hidden-wrong procedures; adding the single author release-note edge input dropped this to **0.065** (cluster-level difference −0.342, 36 discordant clusters), while false rejection stayed at **0.010** in both conditions. The stale procedure fails visibly in 34/52 tasks under typical inputs but 52/52 under the edge input.
- **Unseen-cluster replication (M4c).** On the 15 test clusters built after the effect was first observed, FA fell **0.471 → 0.061** (difference −0.410, 95% CI [−0.589, −0.238], strict Holm **p = 0.002**), with thresholds fixed in advance (Holm p<0.05, FA≤0.10, FR rise ≤0.05). This is the paper's strongest confirmatory result — with the caveat that the quantity is a deterministic function of the build and was computed before F0.
- **Blind writer vs author edge input (Table 1, Fig. 1a).** Unguarded writer inputs reach **0.130** (k=1), **0.075** (k=2), **0.070** (k=3), adding 0.96/1.85/2.46 inputs on average, with false rejection unchanged at 0.010. The preregistered guard is worse at every k: **0.206 / 0.161 / 0.157**. Dropping the 3 inputs that coincide with a hidden input gives 0.149 / 0.094 / **0.090** — still under the 0.10 ceiling but clearly above the author's 0.065, so part of the writer's apparent parity comes from overlap (mean max string similarity of kept writer inputs to a hidden input is 0.70, essentially the same as the author's 0.71).
- **Model repairs, 456 attempts per arm (Tables 3–4).** Wrong releases per attempt under all assertions: Qwen3-8B **0.233→0.057** (CI [−0.267, −0.094], Holm p<0.001), gpt-oss-20b **0.181→0.020** (p<0.001), qwen3.6-35b-a3b **0.120→0.018** (p=0.004), GPT-5.6-luna **0.056→0.018** (p=0.499, descriptive — near the ceiling). Absolute counts: 98→24, 71→8, 49→7, 22→7 wrong releases out of 456. Correct completion never fell (E−T ≥ 0 in every family); with no gate at all the same arm produced 182/111/97/45 false acceptances.
- **Self-tests are real but not significant (Tables 3, 5, 6).** S reduced wrong releases 98→80 (Qwen3-8B), 71→47 (gpt-oss-20b), 49→30 (qwen3.6), 22→9 (luna), but the smallest Holm p was **0.132** (strict; code 0.088), so the preregistered self-test replication criterion is *not* met. The mechanism is coverage and quality: luna wrote valid tests for 71/73 tasks, Qwen3-8B for only **30/73**; over the complete space Qwen3-8B's unguarded self-test gate released **0.240** of wrong procedures vs **0.070** for the blind writer, and applied to its own 176 wrong first-step candidates it released **0.330** vs the writer's 0.170. Adding self-tests to the edge input barely moves anything (ES: 20/8/8 vs E: 24/8/7).
- **Writer × repairer matrix (Tables 7–8, post hoc, zero calls).** On the 21 tasks where every writer has at least one valid input, the strongest writer gates every repairer best or tied-best (luna writer: 0.000/0.157/0.125/0.021 across repairers), and the mean same-model cell is **0.161 vs 0.187** for cross-model cells — i.e. no sign that a model's own tests are specifically blind to its own errors; capability seems to matter more than independence. With four families and one prompt this is a hypothesis, not a result.
- **Large menu (Tables 9–10).** Typical inputs **0.278**, edge input **0.045**, blind writer **0.053** (FR 0.043 vs 0.014 — the writer's first false-rejection cost in the study), guard **0.106**. LM3 reverses: the typical-input gate is *more* permissive on the small menu (0.406) than the large one (0.271), because most added wrong fragments fail visibly on typical inputs. Model repairs: 0.253→0.062 (Qwen3-8B), 0.212→0.038 (gpt-oss-20b), 0.177→0.023 (qwen3.6), all Holm p<0.001.
- **Hard distractors (Tables 12–13).** Typical inputs release **0.337** overall and **0.990** of the hard-origin wrong programs (as constructed). The edge input reaches 0.058 overall but **0.168** of the hard ones; the luna writer 0.058 overall and **0.153** of the hard ones; the guard 0.135/0.492. HD1 "passes" only because hard programs are a minority of the wrong ones — the paper says this explicitly. The two new writers fail: qwen3.6 reaches 0.094 but raises false rejection to **0.083** (tolerance 0.05), Qwen3-8B reaches 0.182; and the new "boundary-value" prompt shows no benefit (WR3: 0.138 vs 0.097 for qwen3.6's own earlier self-tests, p=0.196).
- **Menu-independent view (Table 11).** Counting wrong programs that visible inputs cannot distinguish from a correct one: 113 programs in 41 tasks (typical, small menu) → **445 in 42 tasks** (typical, large menu); edge input 14/11 and 46/17; writer 15/12 and 62/15. The absolute number of indistinguishable wrong programs *grows* with the menu under every gate (edge input: 14 → 46 → 77), which is the cleanest statement of the residual risk.
- **Contract audit and contamination.** 34/73 contracts flagged (R4 for 20 tasks, R3 for 10, R5 for 7, R2 for 3); removing p0 and d0 changes **0** of the exhaustive hidden verdicts; excluding the 24 flagged test tasks leaves the edge-input contrast at −0.370 (p<0.001, 25 clusters). The no-documentation probe is sobering for task difficulty: GPT-5.6-luna produced hidden-correct first-step repairs in **37/45** CPython tasks with no release-note text at all (uniform random choice: 9.9/45), and 3/4 post-cutoff SQLite 3.54 tasks.

## Authors & Credibility

Xiaobo Chen (University of Massachusetts Amherst), Genliang Zhu (Accentrust / Georgia Institute of Technology), Ziyuan Wang (Duke University), Chu Wang (Accentrust / University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign). This is an empirical software-engineering paper (cs.SE, cross-listed cs.AI) formatted as an ACM journal submission, and it is explicitly the successor to the same group's earlier coverage study — four reviewer objections from that paper (author-written edge inputs, finite menu, no contract sign-off, only 34 clusters) are each mapped to a new experiment or an explicit non-answer. The methodological apparatus is unusually heavy for this literature: two SHA-256 design freezes, frozen analysis code, exhaustive enumeration of up to 2,310 programs per stage, admission checks for local models, and disclosure of a code-vs-design-text Holm discrepancy plus a post-freeze power-record deviation. Artifacts are *not* public — "available from the authors on request", justified by hidden-contract leakage and a commercial model's redistribution terms — so independent re-analysis is possible only by request, not by default.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The framing is genuinely useful and, as far as I can tell, new: prior work (BBCFixer, Gilesi, Byam, DepRepair) asks whether a repair passes the project's failing test; this paper asks how often a *given release check* would have shipped a wrong repair, which requires an enumerable repair space and swappable oracles. Treating "no safe repair exists" as a registered outcome and measuring false rejection alongside false acceptance is the right pair of metrics, and the blind-writer design directly answers the "authors wrote both the edge and the hidden inputs" objection that would otherwise sink the whole result.

**Rigor — the good.** The preregistration is real and consequential: hypotheses are falsified (M4a fails at 0.157 vs a 0.10 ceiling; LM3 goes the *wrong* way; WR1/WR2/WR3 all fail), and the authors report the predeclared strong-evidence criterion as *not met* on two counts. The guard failing is the most scientifically interesting result in the paper and it is presented as a failure, not reframed. Exhaustive enumeration removes sampling noise from the model-free numbers entirely. The p0/d0 sensitivity analysis (0 verdicts change) is exactly the check a skeptical reader would ask for, and the 24-flagged-task exclusion (−0.370, p<0.001) shows the effect is not carried by disputed contracts.

**Rigor — the problems.** Four things temper the headline. (1) **M4c was known at F0.** The 15 clusters are new, but the statistic is a deterministic function of the build and was computed during the mock-pipeline test before the freeze; the authors disclose this in §4.6 and Table 17, yet the abstract's "replicated on unseen changes" reads as prospective when the number was already in hand. The genuinely prospective tests are M4a (failed) and the large-menu/hard-distractor stages. (2) **The hard-distractor "pass" is compositional.** HD1 passes at 0.058 because hard programs are a minority; on the subgroup the stage was built to stress, the edge input leaks 0.168 and the writer 0.153 — 1.5–1.7× the ceiling the paper treats as adequate. The abstract does report both numbers, but "passed their preregistered tests" and "0.168 of the hard wrong programs still passed" sit oddly together. (3) **Menu composition dominates the headline.** LM3 shows the typical-input rate moves 0.406→0.271 purely by adding fragments, and the hard stage moves it to 0.337. So 0.410 is not a property of "release gates on typical inputs"; it is a property of *these* menus. The paper says so, but the number is still the one in the title-adjacent sentence and in every summary. (4) **One model in three roles.** GPT-5.6-luna is the blind writer, the contract auditor, and one of the four repairers. The writer × repairer matrix mitigates the repairer overlap (luna's inputs gate the other three families at 0.000–0.157), but nothing separates the writer's reading of a release note from the auditor's — a shared misreading would be invisible to this design.

**Baselines.** There is no external system baseline, and the paper is upfront about why: BBCBench cases need dependency installs (forbidden by the offline protocol), its oracle is the project's own failing test (cannot be varied independently), and its repairs are free-form (space not enumerable). Comparing "gate classes" instead of porting Byam's Java pipeline is defensible. But it does mean the contribution is a measurement framework plus a finding, not a system that beats something — readers looking for "our repairer fixes X% more" will not find it.

**Reproducibility.** Mixed. The protocol is described to an unusual level of detail (prompts, validation rules, admission thresholds, freeze hashes, per-stage call counts, truncation counts), and every number is claimed to be regenerable from scripts. But the artifacts are request-only, the hidden contracts and inputs are deliberately withheld, and the tasks/fragments/edge inputs are all author-written. This is a benchmark whose evaluation key cannot be inspected, which is defensible for contamination reasons and still a real barrier.

**Significance.** High for practitioners who ship LLM-generated migrations: the actionable recipe (ask an isolated model for a few release-note edge inputs, compute expected outputs on the old runtime, gate on them; do *not* ask the model which legacy outputs still bind) is cheap — one call per task — and the negative results (self-tests, guards, weak writers) are as useful as the positive one. Lower for anyone hoping this transfers to free-form repair: no model-written code was executed anywhere, 25 tasks still have fewer than 15 options, and the contamination probe (37/45 CPython tasks solved with *no* release notes) suggests the menu-plus-template setting may be easier than real migration work.

**Red flags.** (i) The gap between the abstract's confident "passed their preregistered tests" and the 0.168/0.153 hard-distractor numbers; (ii) M4c's pre-freeze computation, disclosed but easy to miss; (iii) `gemma-4-12b-qat` excluded for a serving bug with no content-level retry, leaving the "four families from three providers" coverage thinner at the small end than it sounds; (iv) cross-family thresholds fixed when five families were planned and applied to four; (v) two of four families are descriptive by the authors' own rule, so the confirmatory base is Qwen3-8B and gpt-oss-20b — 8B-class models, on a task set where the frontier model is at ceiling; (vi) unresolved R2 class-label objections on two Node.js tasks where the quoted entry documents a deprecation history rather than a removal.

## Strengths

- A genuinely new and well-posed question — gate adequacy over the *complete* repair space — with metrics (FA/FR over an enumerable domain) that make the answer falsifiable rather than anecdotal.
- Real runtimes, real release notes, real hidden contracts, and offline deterministic execution: no simulated APIs, no LLM-as-judge for correctness.
- The blind-writer design is the right response to the obvious confound, and the paper reports the overlap analysis (3 inputs equal a hidden input; similarity 0.70) that most authors would omit.
- Exceptional honesty about failure: the preregistered guard backfires and is reported as a headline negative; LM3 contradicts the paper's own framing; the strong-evidence criterion is declared unmet.
- Three progressively adversarial frozen stages (larger menu, legacy-matching distractors, more writers) instead of one comfortable setting — and the menu-independent count (113 → 445 → 709 indistinguishable wrong programs) is the most policy-relevant number in the paper.
- The practical recommendation is cheap, specific, and directly supported: one edge input, expected outputs from the old runtime, and an explicit warning not to let the writer adjudicate bindingness.

## Limitations

- **No free-form repair.** Repairs are choices among author-written documented fragments; the free-form arm was approved only under strict sandboxing and never run because the sandbox could not be built on the study machine. The authors explicitly do not claim transfer, but this is the single largest gap between the study and real migration work.
- **Menu dependence.** 25 tasks still have fewer than 15 options even in the large menu, and the headline FA moves by 0.14 just from changing menu construction; hard distractors push the hard-subgroup rate to 0.168. There is no evidence the 0.410/0.065 pair generalizes to a differently built menu.
- **Author-built everything.** Tasks, typical inputs, edge inputs, fragments and hidden inputs are all author-written. The blind writer limits but does not remove the concern that edge and hidden inputs share an authorial reading of the note.
- **Contract validity.** No human reviewed the contracts; the AI audit flags 34/73, and the R2 class-label objections (deprecation history quoted as if it were a removal, two Node.js tasks) are unresolved. The p0/d0 removal changing zero verdicts helps but does not address R2.
- **Model coverage and ceiling effects.** Only four families, two of them descriptive; GPT-5.6-luna is near ceiling (0.056 typical-input FA, 22 wrong releases out of 456), so the frontier-model contrasts are underpowered by construction (H1 p=0.499). gemma was excluded for a serving artifact.
- **Ecosystem breadth.** Three runtime lineages, no web APIs, no further ecosystems; the SQLite subset is small (8 tasks, 4 in the post-cutoff 3.54 group).
- **Task difficulty may be unrepresentative.** The no-documentation probe shows the frontier model solving 37/45 CPython tasks without any release-note text, which suggests the template and requirement often suffice — real migrations with less structured context could behave differently.
- **Artifacts on request only**, so the hidden key cannot be independently audited.

## Relevance

这篇直接落在「Agent & Pipeline Silent Failures」这条线上：它研究的不是模型会不会写错代码，而是**上线门禁这一层软件基础设施**如何在"调用成功、结果已变"的情况下静默放行错误修复——正是"管道静默改变模型输出"的经典形态，只不过被改变的是代码语义而非 token。对做 agent 工具调用可靠性、自动修复流水线和发布门禁的人来说，它给出了一个可复用的测量方法（穷举修复空间算 FA/FR）和一条便宜可落地的建议（用 release note 生成一条边界输入作为门禁），同时用硬干扰项实验诚实地标出了这条建议的天花板（0.168 仍会漏过）。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the edge-input effect survive free-form repair, where the model writes code rather than picking fragments? The authors flag this as future work; it is the experiment that would decide whether the recipe is deployable.
2. Why does the typical-input gate's FA *fall* when the menu grows (0.406→0.271) while the absolute count of indistinguishable wrong programs rises (113→445)? Is there a menu-construction policy that minimizes the absolute count rather than the rate?
3. The guard failed because writers mislabel change-revealing inputs as non-binding. Could bindingness be decided mechanically — e.g. by checking whether the old output is reachable under the new runtime at all — instead of by model judgement?
4. Is the writer-capability-over-independence reading (same-model 0.161 vs cross-model 0.187) stable with more than four families, more than one prompt, and more wrong repairs per cell? It is currently a hypothesis the authors explicitly decline to promote.
5. What does the 0.168 hard-distractor leak rate imply for a release policy? If a gate cannot distinguish legacy-matching wrong programs from correct ones on *any* visible input, is the right response abstention, a human review queue, or differential testing against the old runtime on production traffic?
6. How much of the 0.410 headline is an artifact of tasks whose stale procedure fails visibly in only 34/52 cases under typical inputs? A task set skewed toward silent changes (25 of 66 affected) might give a very different baseline rate.
7. Would a second, independent frontier writer (not the same model as the auditor and one of the repairers) reproduce 0.070, or does the writer result depend on one model's specific reading of release notes?
