> **一句话总结：** 把每个 transformer block 拆成 LN1/MHA/RC1/LN2/FC1/Act/FC2/RC2 八个模块逐一做线性探针（约 14.4 万次评估，15 个视觉基础模型 + 9 个 LLM），证明层与模块必须联合选择而非只看深度：视觉端固定取最后一层 FFN 激活（Act@100）可回收 41.9% 的 oracle 增益，语言端取 60% 深度的第二个 LayerNorm（LN2@60）平均 +2.44 pp、回收 86.0%，但视觉端收益极不均衡——DINOv3 只有 +0.4 pp，最小的 ViT-B 反而掉了 6.6 pp。

> **TL;DR:** The paper asks whether the final-layer residual stream (RC2@100) — the default probing location — is actually the best place to read features out of a frozen transformer, and answers no, but only if you vary layer *and* module jointly. Across ~144k linear-probe evaluations on 15 vision foundation models (ViT, DINOv2, DINOv3, AIMv2; 22M–7B params; 18 image-classification datasets; 270 model–dataset pairs) and 9 LLMs (Qwen3, Llama3, Gemma3; 24 MTEB-Multilingual classification tasks; 216 model–task pairs), probing the FFN activation instead of the residual stream gains +2.3 pp on ViT-L (66.0→68.2), +1.3 pp on DINOv2-L, +0.7 pp on AIMv2-L but only +0.4 pp on DINOv3-L, and *loses* 6.6 pp on ViT-B (61.8→55.2). On LLMs the recipe is much more reliable: LN2@60 gains +2.44 pp on average (Qwen3-8B 57.6→61.1, Gemma3-12B 61.5→64.2), improves 21/24 tasks, and recovers 86.0% of the oracle gain versus 39.3% for the best residual-stream layer — enough that un-finetuned Gemma3-4B matches GritLM-7B on MTEB-Multilingual. Worth reading if you build embedding baselines from frozen models; skip if you want a mechanistic account of *why* the activation helps.

## 核心贡献

- **把"探针位置"从一维（深度）扩展成二维（深度 × 模块）。** 以往工作把 transformer block 当作原子单元，只扫描层；本文在每个 block 内用 forward hook 抓取 8 个中间张量（LN1、MHA、RC1、LN2、FC1、Act、FC2、RC2），对所有 (层, 模块) 组合做线性探针。
- **提出 oracle 增益的三分解** $G_{\text{layer}}=\max_\ell g(\ell,\text{RC2})$、$G_{\text{module}}=\max_m g(100,m)$、$G_{\text{joint}}=\max_{\ell,m} g(\ell,m)$，把"深度有用"和"模块有用"从同一个数字里拆开，使"模块被忽视"这一主张可被证伪。视觉端满足 $G_{\text{joint}} \ge G_{\text{module}} \ge G_{\text{layer}}$，语言端 $G_{\text{layer}} \ge G_{\text{module}}$ 在 8/9 个模型成立，但 $G_{\text{joint}}$ 始终最大。
- **发现模块的最优性高度依赖深度。** 在 270 个视觉 model–dataset 对上，Act 出现在最优 (层, 模块) 对中的频率是 64.8%，第二名 LN2 只有 9.3%，而基线模块 RC2 只有 5.9%（Table 15）——模块本身贡献了大部分可回收增益。
- **给出两个零搜索的固定策略并量化其收益上限。** 视觉用 Act@100（最后一层的 FFN 激活），语言用 LN2@60（60% 深度的第二个 LayerNorm）。前者回收 41.9% 的 $G_{\text{joint}}$（对比最优残差流层 31.9%），后者回收 86.0%（对比 39.3%），最优层 LN2 更是达到 99.8%。
- **刻画收益随"迁移难度"变化的规律。** 视觉端 oracle 增益与数据集平均探针误差的 Spearman ρ = 0.87（p = 3.4e-6）；CIFAR-10→CIFAR-10-C 上腐蚀越强增益越大、最优深度越浅（约 96% → 82%）；预训练 token 越多增益越小（相对误差下降 ViT 15.7%、DINOv2 12.1%、AIMv2 10.8%、DINOv3 7.6%）。
- **一个可操作的副产品：** 不做任何微调，Gemma3-4B + LN2@60 的平均准确率 63.1 追平 GritLM-7B（63.1），Gemma3-12B 达 64.2，仅次于 Gemini Embedding 与 mE5-large-instruct，平均排名从第 7.1 升到第 5.2。

## Key Contributions

- **Reframes probing as a 2-D search (depth × module) instead of a 1-D scan over depth.** Eight tensors per block are captured with PyTorch hooks in a single forward pass, using a model-agnostic `TransformerBlockAdapter` that also normalizes gated FFNs (SwiGLU) so `Act` means the same thing across standard and gated architectures.
- **Introduces an oracle-gain decomposition** ($G_{\text{layer}}$, $G_{\text{module}}$, $G_{\text{joint}}$) that separates the contribution of depth from that of module choice and from their interaction, making the central claim ("module choice is overlooked") directly testable rather than rhetorical.
- **Establishes that module optimality is depth-dependent**, so the two axes cannot be optimized independently: `Act` wins 64.8% of the 270 VFM oracle pairs versus 9.3% for LN2 and 5.9% for the RC2 baseline module, and the winning module shifts with layer.
- **Derives and validates two search-free fixed strategies** — `Act@100` for VFMs and `LN2@60` for LLMs — with explicit recovery fractions of the oracle gain (41.9% and 86.0% respectively, at zero extra forward passes).
- **Maps when the gains appear**: they grow with transfer difficulty and corruption severity for vision, shrink with pretraining scale, and are insensitive to model size (Friedman test, all p > 0.05, Kendall's W ≤ 0.088).
- **Provides a controlled falsification of the "it's just dimensionality" explanation**: FC1 has the same hidden dimension h = 4d as Act but underperforms even the RC2 baseline (ViT-L: 64.3 vs 66.0 vs Act's 68.2), so the gain is attributable to the nonlinearity, not the wider feature space.

## Methodology

**Probing locations.** Each pre-norm transformer block is decomposed into eight sequence-to-sequence operations: `LN1`, `MHA`, `RC1` (residual after attention), `LN2`, `FC1`, `Act`, `FC2`, `RC2` (residual stream = block output). For gated FFNs (SwiGLU), `FC1` is the linear layer feeding σ, `Act` is the gated nonlinearity σ(W₁x) ⊙ W₃x (absorbing the gate projection), and `FC2` is the output projection — so the eight slots are semantically comparable across ViT/DINOv2/DINOv3/AIMv2/Qwen3/Llama3/Gemma3. Hooks are registered in one forward pass; `LN1`, `LN2`, `FC1` are captured with pre-hooks (their inputs), the rest with forward hooks.

**Feature construction.** Token sequences are mean-pooled over *content* tokens only: CLS, register tokens, BOS/EOS and PAD are masked out before pooling. This is a deliberate choice to allow a single extraction procedure across models that lack a CLS token (AIMv2), and it keeps the comparison about *within-block* representations rather than readout tokens.

**Probe.** Logistic regression on frozen, standardized features — L-BFGS, ≤1000 iterations, ℓ2 with C = 1.0, tol 1e-4, CuML GPU implementation; no augmentation (extraction is deterministic, the classifier is fit once). VFM metric: top-1 test accuracy per dataset. LLM metric: MTEB-Multilingual average test accuracy over 24 classification tasks (31 languages, 14 domains), using the official MMTEB harness (8–16 shots per label, 100 iterations, 10 repeats).

**Scale.** 15 VFMs (ViT-B/L/H, DINOv2-S/B/L/g, DINOv3-S/B/L/7B, AIMv2 in four sizes; 22M–7B) × 18 datasets = 270 pairs, ~90k evaluations. 9 LLMs (Qwen3 1.7/4/8B, Llama3 1/3/8B, Gemma3 1/4/12B) × 24 tasks = 216 pairs, ~54k evaluations. Total >144k probes.

**Gain definitions.** With `acc(ℓ,m)` the probe accuracy and baseline `acc(100, RC2)`, `g(ℓ,m) = acc(ℓ,m) − acc(100, RC2)`. The three oracle quantities in Eq. (1) are a-posteriori maxima over the evaluated grid — the paper is explicit that they require test-set access and are upper bounds, not achievable recipes.

**Controls.** (i) `RC2@60` at matched depth, to separate "intermediate depth" from "different module"; (ii) `FC1@100` at matched dimensionality h = 4d, to separate "wider features" from "nonlinearity"; (iii) a ViT-B fine-tuning study (SGD, 4 learning rates, 3 seeds) probing the same model before and after fine-tuning; (iv) corruption sweeps on CIFAR-10-C at severities {1,3,5}; (v) Friedman tests across model sizes.

## Results & Highlights

- **Module choice dominates: `Act` is in the oracle pair for 64.8% of the 270 VFM model–dataset pairs**, versus 9.3% for LN2, 7.4% for MHA, 6.3% for LN1, 5.9% for the RC2 baseline module, 3.7% for RC1, 2.2% for FC2 and 0.4% for FC1 (Table 15) — a 55.5-point gap over the runner-up, which is the paper's strongest single piece of evidence.
- **`Act@100` gains are real but family-dependent on the 307M variants**: ViT-L 66.0→68.2 (+2.3 pp), DINOv2-L 78.0→79.3 (+1.3), AIMv2-L 79.4→80.2 (+0.7), DINOv3-L 79.8→80.2 (+0.4). Aggregate: improvement in 63.7% of the 270 pairs, recovering 41.9% of $G_{\text{joint}}$ versus 31.9% for the best residual-stream layer, and 78.1% if the Act *depth* is also searched.
- **The fixed strategy has a severe failure mode that the headline percentage hides**: on ViT-B the average drops from 61.8% to 55.2% (−6.6 pp), with per-dataset losses such as Flowers102 98.3→95.2 and DTD 69.4→57.1; DINOv3-S also regresses (71.0→70.0). The authors report this in Table 17 and attribute it to ViT-B's final-layer activation being anomalously weak while its intermediate activations remain strong.
- **Gains concentrate where transfer is hard.** On WILDS + DomainNet, `Act@100` improves 89.3% of the 28 evaluations with an average +1.8 pp; across all 18 datasets the oracle gain correlates with mean probing error at Spearman ρ = 0.87 (p = 3.4e-6), and on CIFAR-10-C the oracle gain rises monotonically from severity 1 to 5 while the optimal depth falls from ~96% to ~82%. Saturation cases behave as expected: on Flowers102/Pets (in-distribution for these models) gains are ~0–0.4 pp.
- **LLM side: `LN2@60` is close to the oracle.** Average +2.44 pp across 9 models; Qwen3-8B 57.6→61.1 (+3.5), Qwen3-4B 56.6→59.4 (+2.9), Gemma3-12B 61.5→64.2 (+2.6), Llama3-8B 59.9→62.3 (+2.4), Llama3-1B 56.6→58.6 (+2.0). It improves 21/24 tasks (87.5%) and 77.8% of the 216 model–task evaluations, recovering 86.0% of $G_{\text{joint}}$ versus 39.3% for the best residual-stream layer; the best layer-wise LN2 reaches 99.8%.
- **Depth alone is not the mechanism — the module is.** At the *same* 60% depth, `RC2@60` collapses on several tasks where `LN2@60` is fine: ItaCasehold −5.4 pp (RC2@60) vs +1.2 pp (LN2@60), TweetTopic −4.7 vs −2.2, KorSarcasm −3.4 vs −2.7, DBpedia −2.2 vs +0.5 (Table 20). Average `RC2@60` gain is only +0.1 to +1.3 pp per model, versus +2.0 to +3.5 for LN2@60.
- **Zero-shot embedding quality.** With `LN2@60` and no fine-tuning, Gemma3-4B reaches 63.1 average accuracy, tying GritLM-7B (63.1) with a marginally better average rank (5.9 vs 6.0); Gemma3-12B reaches 64.2 (rank 5.2, third overall) versus EmbeddingGemma's 64.4 (rank 6.0), behind only Gemini Embedding (71.6) and mE5-large-instruct (66.4). The same models probed at the final layer sit at 61.5/62.0 (ranks 7.1/7.5).
- **Dimensionality is not the explanation.** FC1@100, which has the same h = 4d width as Act, scores 64.3 on ViT-L versus 66.0 for the RC2 baseline and 68.2 for Act@100; FC1@100 is below baseline on all four families (Table 18).
- **Pretraining scale matters, model size does not.** Relative error reduction falls in the order ViT 15.7% > DINOv2 12.1% > AIMv2 10.8% > DINOv3 7.6% (more pretraining tokens → smaller gain), while Friedman tests across sizes find no significant effect in any family (ViT p = 0.678, DINOv2 p = 0.189, DINOv3 p = 0.890, AIMv2 p = 0.204).

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The framing is incremental — "intermediate layers beat the last layer" is well established (Skean et al. 2025 for LLMs; Bolya et al. 2025's Perception Encoder for contrastive VFMs; Uselis & Oh 2025 under distribution shift) — but the *module* axis is genuinely underexplored, and the 64.8%-vs-9.3% optimality-frequency result is a clean, non-obvious finding that no prior work reports. The oracle decomposition is a small but well-chosen formalization: it turns a vague "look inside blocks" intuition into three separately estimable quantities. Credit where due — this is the kind of careful negative-result-plus-modest-positive-result paper the probing literature needs more of.

**Rigor — one real statistical problem.** Figure 6a plots the oracle gain $G_{\text{joint}}$ against "transfer difficulty", defined as the *mean probing error of the baseline across all models*. But the gain is itself defined as `acc(ℓ,m) − acc(baseline)`, so the x-axis and y-axis share the `−acc(baseline)` term: datasets where the baseline is bad mechanically have larger gains. The reported ρ = 0.87 is therefore partly an artifact of the estimator, not purely an empirical discovery. The paper does not acknowledge this coupling, even though it is careful to flag the pretraining-scale analysis as "observational, not causal". The CIFAR-10-C severity experiment (Figs. 6b/6c) is a much cleaner version of the same claim and should have been the primary evidence rather than the correlational scatter.

**Baseline choice.** The baseline is `RC2@100`, the residual stream *before* the final layer norm. Most practitioners extract the final *normalized* hidden state (HuggingFace `last_hidden_state` for Llama is post-norm; sentence-transformers applies the final LN). `LN2@100` is measured as part of the depth sweep but never tabulated as a separate baseline, so the reader cannot tell how much of the "LN2@60 beats the baseline" effect is just "final norm helps" rather than "60% depth helps". Figure 8 suggests the gap is small for Qwen3-1.7B, but it is a conspicuous omission given that LN2 is the paper's own recommended module.

**The 60% hyperparameter is data-derived.** The paper selects 60% as the midpoint of the observed [50%, 70%] optimal interval, i.e. after seeing the answer. The stability of that interval across 9 models is a reasonable defense, and the 95% CIs in Fig. 9 help, but there is no held-out-model validation (e.g. pick the depth on Qwen3, test on Gemma3). Combined with the fact that `Act@100` is also chosen a posteriori from the optimality-frequency table, both "fixed strategies" are selected on the same data they are evaluated on.

**Weak regimes pollute the VFM averages.** Infograph (345 classes) has baseline accuracy 2.1–5.1% — barely above the 0.29% chance level — and RxRx1 (1,139 classes) 1.8–5.1%. Averaging 18 datasets with equal weight lets these near-chance cells contribute as much as Flowers102 at 99.6%, and the oracle gains there (+2.7 to +5.4 pp on Infograph) are measured in a regime where a 3-point difference is noise-dominated. A macro-average over "datasets above some accuracy floor", or a per-dataset normalization, would make the headline +2.3 pp more trustworthy.

**Mechanism is asserted, not explained.** The FC1 control successfully rules out dimensionality, but then the story stops: *why* does the post-GELU/SwiGLU activation produce more linearly separable features than the pre-activation projection at the same width? No effective-rank, sparsity, kurtosis or kernel-alignment measurement is offered, despite these being cheap to compute on already-extracted features. The paper's own framing ("we aim to identify which internal representations are most useful, not to reverse-engineer computations") excuses this, but it leaves the practitioner without a principle for choosing a module on a *new* architecture — which is exactly what `Act@100` and `LN2@60` are being sold as.

**Scope of the LLM evidence.** All 24 tasks are sentence-level classification with 8–16 shots per label and a 100-iteration logistic regression — a deliberately weak probe. Retrieval, reranking, clustering, pair classification and STS, which is where embedding models are actually judged and where mean-pooled linear probes are known to correlate poorly with downstream embedding quality, are absent. The Gemma3-vs-GritLM comparison inherits this limitation: "matches GritLM-7B on 24 classification tasks" is not "matches GritLM-7B as an embedding model".

**"No extra cost" is a slight overstatement.** `Act` lives in the FFN hidden space h ≈ 4d, so caching activations costs ~4× the storage of residual-stream features during extraction — the very bottleneck the authors describe in Appendix B.2.1 (49K tensors per 512-sample batch for ViT-B). There is no extra *forward pass*, which is the honest claim, but there is a real memory/IO cost at scale.

**What is genuinely good.** The failure reporting is exemplary: ViT-B's −6.6 pp, DINOv3-S's −1.0 pp, and the three LLM tasks that regress under LN2@60 (KorSarcasm −2.7, TweetTopic −2.2, Tswana −0.3) are all in the main or appendix tables rather than buried. The FC1 control is the right experiment and it is run on the right models. The fine-tuning study (Fig. 13: concave accuracy curves before fine-tuning on Speckle Noise, monotone after) is a genuinely illuminating mechanistic probe and, in my view, the most interesting result in the paper — it explains *when* the effect exists, which is more valuable than the fixed recipes.

## Strengths

- **Scale and consistency of the empirical base.** 144k probing evaluations spanning 24 models, 42 datasets/benchmarks and four distinct pretraining paradigms, with a single feature-extraction and probe-fitting protocol throughout. Very few probing papers attempt this, and the per-model, per-dataset appendix tables make the claims auditable.
- **A clean, falsifiable decomposition.** Splitting the gain into layer / module / joint components is the right formal move, and the resulting inequality pattern ($G_{\text{joint}} \ge G_{\text{module}} \ge G_{\text{layer}}$ for vision) is a crisp, checkable statement rather than a qualitative claim.
- **Honest, non-cherry-picked reporting.** The paper's own fixed strategy loses 6.6 pp on ViT-B and is roughly a wash on DINOv3, and both facts are stated plainly. Tables 17 and 20 expose every per-dataset number, including the regressions.
- **The FC1 control is exactly the right falsification test** for the obvious "wider features are just easier to separate" objection, and it comes out against the trivial explanation.
- **Practical payoff that is actually usable.** `LN2@60` is a one-line change to any HuggingFace feature-extraction script and moves Gemma3-4B from below GritLM-7B to parity with it, with no fine-tuning. That is a real, cheap baseline improvement for anyone building text-embedding baselines.
- **The fine-tuning study supplies the causal-ish story the correlational analysis cannot**, and it aligns with the Head2Toe finding that aggregating intermediate representations helps more when domains are far apart.
- **Fully open, reproducible artefact.** Code, per-model checkpoints, dataset splits, probe hyperparameters and hardware-aware extraction pipeline are all documented; the AI-use statement is specific about what was and was not machine-generated.

## Limitations

- **Classification-only, linear-probe-only.** No retrieval/STS/reranking, no attentive probes (the standard for AIMv2 and Perception Encoder), no k-means or clustering metrics. Whether `Act@100`/`LN2@60` transfer to attentive probing or to finetuned readouts is untested, and the paper's own related-work section notes that attentive probing changes the pooling operator entirely.
- **Two modalities only.** Vision and text; tabular and time-series foundation models — the authors' own home turf — are named as future work rather than evaluated.
- **Oracle gains require test-set access**, so the headline "42% / 86% of the gain recovered" is a fraction of an unattainable ceiling. In absolute terms the VFM effect is ~1 pp on strong models, and the LLM effect is ~2.4 pp on a deliberately weak 8–16-shot linear probe.
- **Selection bias in both fixed strategies**: `Act@100` from the optimality-frequency table, `LN2@60` from the midpoint of the observed optimal-depth interval, both chosen on the same data used for evaluation, with no held-out-model or held-out-dataset validation.
- **The transfer-difficulty correlation is partly circular** (shared `−acc(baseline)` term), and the paper does not flag it while flagging other analyses as observational.
- **No mechanistic account** of why the activation or the pre-FFN normalization helps; the FC1 control eliminates one explanation without proposing another.
- **Near-chance datasets (Infograph, RxRx1) are equally weighted** in the 18-dataset macro-average, adding variance to the headline numbers without contributing signal.
- **The 60%-depth heuristic is architecture-dependent in a way the paper does not probe**: Qwen3's optimum is ~70%, Llama3/Gemma3 ~50%; the [50,70] interval is stable across *these nine models* but there is no evidence it holds for deeper or MoE models.

## Relevance

直接命中「LLM Hidden States & Interpretability」：这是对 transformer 内部表征（含 block 内 8 个模块）做大规模线性探针的系统性研究，给出了"层 × 模块"二维扫描的定量地图和 oracle 增益分解，可作为探针方法论与表征几何分析的基线参考。实用价值在于 LN2@60 这类零成本读出位置调整，可直接用于冻结模型的特征提取与 embedding 基线搭建。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does `LN2@60` survive a stronger probe? The MTEB linear probe uses 8–16 shots and 100 iterations — would the ordering of modules change under a well-tuned probe, a kNN probe, or a small MLP?
2. How much of the LN2 effect is just the final layer norm? Reporting `LN2@100` and the post-final-norm residual stream as explicit baselines would isolate "depth" from "normalization" and is a one-table experiment.
3. Is the transfer-difficulty correlation robust to a non-circular definition of difficulty — e.g. a held-out-model estimate of baseline error, or a distribution distance (FID, MMD, CKA to the pretraining manifold) that does not enter the gain formula?
4. Can the module choice be predicted rather than searched? Given the FC1-vs-Act gap at identical width, measurements of effective rank, activation sparsity, or feature kurtosis on the already-extracted tensors might yield an architecture-agnostic selector.
5. Does the picture hold for attentive probes and for retrieval/STS tasks, where mean-pooled linear probes are known to decorrelate from real embedding quality?
6. Would aggregating *several* of the identified locations (e.g. Act at 60% and 100%, or LN2 across the [50,70] band with learned weights) beat any single fixed location — and does this paper's map explain why Head2Toe-style aggregation works?
7. Do MoE models, where different experts occupy different FFN subspaces, break the "one global best module" assumption that 64.8% optimality for Act implies?
