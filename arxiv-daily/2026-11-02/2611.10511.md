> **一句话总结：** CALFA-KV 把 1-bit KV cache 的两个环节接起来——离线以"投影后注意力输出"（post-$W_O$）为目标联合拟合 pre-RoPE key 中心 $\mu$、径向尺度 $A$ 与 K/V 向量码本，再用参考 next-token 交叉熵的平方梯度估计逐通道 Fisher 敏感度；在线用该敏感度加权每个 KV state 的实际重建误差，为每层每 KV head 保留 56 个 FP16 anchor。结果：d8b8（1.008 BPE）在 Qwen3-4B / Llama-3.1-8B / Qwen3-30B-A3B 上七基准均分 64.03 / 47.12 / 66.72，比同码率的 TaSQ 分别高 4.43 / 6.71 / 4.42 分且 21/21 全胜；d8b10（1.258 BPE）把与 BF16 的差距压到 0.90–2.01 分。

> **TL;DR:** A 1-bit KV-cache codec plus an anchor selector, both built around one idea: minimize error where the model actually reads it. Offline, keys are vector-quantized pre-RoPE in groups of 8 channels after centering ($\mu$) and a learnable diagonal rescale ($A$), with $\mu$, $A$ and the K/V codebooks fitted jointly by damped Gauss–Newton against the **projected attention output after $W_O$** — and every candidate step is re-encoded at deployment precision and accepted only if native replay strictly lowers the fitting loss. Separately, per-channel Fisher weights come from squared gradients of the reference CE loss, expressed in the same normalized key coordinates (stored FP16 scale held fixed) and raw value coordinates. Online, each historical KV state is scored by $\sum_d F^K_d(\delta^K_d)^2 + \sum_d F^V_d(\delta^V_d)^2$ on its *realized* reconstruction error and kept as an FP16 anchor if it lands in the top-56 per layer per KV head. On Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 the 7-benchmark mean is **64.03 vs 59.60 for an adapted TaSQ at the same 1.008 BPE (+4.43)** and **66.05 for d8b10 vs 66.95 BF16 (−0.90 at 1.258 BPE)**; same-sign gains on Llama-3.1-8B (+6.71) and Qwen3-30B-A3B (+4.42), winning all 21 model×benchmark cells. The catch: the anchor budget is excluded from the headline bit rate, the KIVI/VecInfer/OSCAR baselines collapse to near-zero (KIVI scores 0.45 on GSM8K), and no wall-clock or measured memory number appears anywhere.

## 核心贡献

- **把"量化器标定"和"高精度 anchor 选择"用同一个重建误差口径焊在一起。** 前人两条线（改量化表示 / 给重要 token 加精度）是分开做的：标定改变残余误差的分布，选 anchor 决定哪些 state 保留高精度，但选 anchor 的打分函数往往不是针对标定后量化器写的。CALFA-KV 明确让 anchor 分数来自**已冻结量化器的实际重建误差**，于是标定越好，选择信号越干净。这是全文最实在的结构性贡献。
- **联合标定的目标函数是"模型真正读到的东西"，不是 K/V 重建误差。** 目标为 $\mathcal{L}_l(\theta_l)=\frac{1}{2E_l}\sum_{w\in F}\|o^{nat}_{l,w}(\theta_l)-o^\star_{l,w}\|_2^2$，即 post-$W_O$ 投影输出的归一化平方误差，其中 $\theta_l=(\mu_l,A_l,C^K_l,C^V_l)$。作者点明了动机：纯向量重建误差看不到 key 误差如何改变 softmax 权重、value 误差如何改变加权和。
- **"连续提议 + 离散验收"的标定协议。** 梯度步只在固定码字分配下提出方向（damped Gauss–Newton + CG，JVP/VJP，回溯 $\alpha_m=0.02\cdot2^{-m}$，最多 8 轮、24 次 CG）；候选参数会被 cast 到部署 dtype、用 FP16 scale 与硬最近邻重新编码、再用 native attention kernel 重放，**只有 Fit 集严格下降且 Select 集不退化才接受**。这条"离散硬验收"在量化论文里不常见，而且作者诚实地写明 Select 窗口参与了模型选择、不算独立测试集。
- **Fisher 敏感度的坐标系处理是本文技术上最讲究的一块。** key 梯度先做定点尺度变换 $g^z = \mathrm{FP32}(n_t)P D^{-1}g^{pre}$（把存储的 FP16 scale 当固定旁信息，不对其求导），**先变换符号再平方再池化**；value 梯度留在原坐标。随后裁剪（$q=0.995$）、收缩（$\rho=0.05$）、在层/头内归一化。作者明确警告：固定对角权重在一个坐标系池化后不能直接搬到另一个坐标系，因为 $n_t$ 逐 token 变化、post-RoPE 换基还会混通道——这条"坐标一致性"要求比多数 KV 量化论文的自省更细。
- **给出 anchor 选择的超额风险界（但作者自己说这个界没有被验证是小的）。** 在精确恢复（$r_{x,B}=0$）与固定候选集下，若代理分数与一阶损失变化的条件均方风险处处相差不超过 $\varepsilon_x$，则选出的集合风险至多比同预算最优集合高 $2\varepsilon_x$（式 A8）。$\varepsilon_x$ 由三项组成：矩偏移、被忽略的相关性、部署权重与离线参考的差异。
- **七个基准、三个模型、共 7,814 题的评测。** GSM8K / IFEval / HumanEval+ / LiveCodeBench V5+V6 / LongBench / MATH500 / GPQA-Diamond，覆盖数学、指令遵循、代码、长上下文、专家知识。所有表格的均分我逐项复核过，算术自洽（例如 d8b8：$448.19/7=64.03$ ✓）。

## Key Contributions

- **Couples quantizer calibration to anchor selection through one error metric.** Prior work either improves the low-bit representation or allocates extra precision to important tokens; CALFA-KV makes the anchor score a function of the *frozen* quantizer's realized reconstruction error, so a better-calibrated codec directly yields a cleaner selection signal. This is the paper's most defensible structural idea.
- **Calibrates against post-$W_O$ projected attention output rather than K/V reconstruction error.** The objective $\mathcal{L}_l(\theta_l)=\frac{1}{2E_l}\sum_{w\in F}\|o^{nat}_{l,w}-o^\star_{l,w}\|_2^2$ jointly fits the pre-RoPE key center $\mu_l$, a positive diagonal radial matrix $A_l$, and separate K/V codebooks, holding the TaSQ-derived channel weights $D$, the within-head permutation $P$, the group structure and the model weights fixed.
- **A "continuous proposal, discrete acceptance" calibration loop.** Gauss–Newton directions with conjugate gradients and backtracking propose parameter updates; every candidate is then materialized at deployment precision (FP16 scales, hard nearest-neighbour reassignment) and replayed through the native attention path. Acceptance requires a strict Fit-loss improvement plus non-degradation on a disjoint Select set. Max 8 rounds, 24 CG iterations, residual tolerance $10^{-4}$.
- **Coordinate-consistent Fisher sensitivity estimation.** Key gradients are transformed into the normalized encoding coordinates with the stored FP16 scale treated as fixed side information, then squared and pooled (Eq. 7); value gradients stay in their original coordinates. The authors explicitly note that a fixed diagonal weight pooled in one coordinate system cannot be reused in another, since $n_t$ varies per token and a post-RoPE basis change mixes channels.
- **A streaming top-$N$ anchor maintenance rule with a matching exchange-argument proof.** Scores are fixed once a state is admitted (codec and weights are request-invariant), so $B_{new}=\mathrm{TopN}(B_{old}\cup U)$ provably maintains the highest-scoring eligible set under a fixed budget; a position-based tie rule makes it reproducible.
- **An excess-risk bound for score-based selection (Appendix A), stated with unusual candour.** Under exact restoration and a fixed candidate set, if the deployed additive proxy differs from the conditional risk by at most $\varepsilon_x$ uniformly, the selected set's risk is within $2\varepsilon_x$ of the optimum at the same budget. The authors then say plainly that they neither estimate $\varepsilon_x$ nor show it is small for the reported models.

## Methodology

**Representation (Sec. 3.1).** Historical keys are quantized *before* RoPE so codebooks need not represent position-dependent rotations. For a layer with $H$ KV heads, keys $k_t\in\mathbb{R}^{D_K}$ are centred by $\mu$, rescaled by a positive diagonal $A$, and divided by a per-token FP16 scale shared across the layer's KV heads:

$$n_t=\mathrm{FP16}\Big(\max\big(\epsilon,\ \|A(k_t-\mu)\|_2/\sqrt{D_K}\big)\Big),\qquad z_t=\frac{D(k_t-\mu)}{\mathrm{FP32}(n_t)}.$$

A fixed diagonal $D$ (TaSQ's query-guided channel weighting) weights channel errors during codeword lookup, and a fixed within-head permutation $P$ assigns coordinates to 8-wide groups while keeping each RoPE-coupled unit intact so a codeword carries both components of the rotation pair. Values use consecutive channels in original coordinates with unit scale. Index rates are $\log_2 K_c/g$: d8b8 → $K_c=256$, 1.00 bit/channel; d8b10 → $K_c=1024$, 1.25 bit/channel.

**Building $D$ and $P$ (Sec. 3.1, App. C.1).** $D$ comes from the diagonal of a RoPE-aligned query second moment $M_h$ (Eq. 4), normalized within head and floored by $\epsilon_{abs}=10^{-30}$, $\epsilon_{rel}=10^{-6}$. $P$ is built by hierarchical minimum-cost perfect matching on a covariance-volume cost $\gamma_h(I)=\det(\Sigma_h[I,I]+\lambda_h I)^{1/|I|}$, then refined in two stages: reconstruction-based regrouping with attention-output guards, and pair swaps accepted only if attention-distribution KL drops below $0.995\times$ *both* the current result and a no-swap refitted control, with per-stratum KL and fixed-V output-SSE guards at $1.01\times$.

**Joint calibration (Sec. 3.2).** Codebooks are warm-started at $\mu_0=0$, $A_0=D$ against projected output error, then $\theta_l=(\mu_l,A_l,C^K_l,C^V_l)$ is optimized with anchors disabled, a 64-token recent window at reference precision, and the native causal/grouped-query masks. Because nearest-codeword assignment makes the objective piecewise constant, the continuous surrogate aligns the analytic scale to the stored scale via $\tilde{n}_t(u)=n_{t,0}+\rho_t(u)-\rho_t(u_0)$ (Eq. A17), giving a differentiable residual $r_e(u)$ and a damped Gauss–Newton step $(J^\top J+\lambda I)s=-g_u$ with $\lambda=\max(10^{-12},0.01\max(0, g_u^\top J^\top J g_u / g_u^\top g_u))$. Each accepted step is re-materialized and replayed natively. 64 calibration windows, 48 for Fit and 16 for Select, 2056-token template-wrapped windows, query positions $135+128j$.

**Fisher sensitivities (Sec. 3.3, App. B).** For reference sequence $i$, take the mean shifted next-token CE $\ell_i$ over targets $T_i=\{2,\dots,L_i\}$, differentiate w.r.t. the captured pre-RoPE key and value-projection output, cast signed gradients to FP32, apply the fixed-scale key transform, then square and pool over all captured positions (including zero-gradient ones): $\bar{F}^c_{l,h,d}=\sum_i\sum_{t\in M_i}(g^c_{i,l,h,t,d})^2/\sum_i|M_i|$. Clipping at the 0.995 quantile and 5% shrinkage toward the two-sided mean stabilizes the weights; a final positive rescale within each layer/head preserves the ranking.

**Anchor scoring and retention (Sec. 3.3–3.4).** Each eligible historical state is reconstructed with the deployed payload, its post-RoPE key inverted back through the cached sine/cosine to pre-RoPE coordinates, and scored as

$$S_{l,h,t}=\sum_d \bar{F}^K_{l,h,d}\big(\delta^K_{l,h,t,d}\big)^2+\sum_d \bar{F}^V_{l,h,d}\big(\delta^V_{l,h,t,d}\big)^2,$$

with key error measured in normalized encoding coordinates at fixed stored scale and value error in raw value coordinates. Paired K/V are stored in FP16; up to $N=56$ per layer per KV head per request. Attention mixes three tiers: exact recent/live K/V, restored FP16 anchors, decoded 1-bit payloads. Scoring is $O(n(d_K+d_V))$ per head and selection is $O(n\log\max(2,N))$ with a size-$N$ heap.

**Theory (App. A).** With exact restoration $e_{x,B}=(I-P_B)\delta_x$, the deployed proxy $\hat{R}(B)=\sum_{t\in M\setminus B}S_t$ is minimized by the top-$N$ states (exchange argument, Sec. A.4), and the streaming invariant is proved by induction. The bound in Eq. A8 relates this to the conditional mean square of the *first-order* loss change $g^\top e$, not to the true loss change including $\tfrac12 e^\top H e$ or to the expected request loss.

**Setup (Sec. 4).** Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507; 7,814 questions total. Baselines: BF16 reference, plus KIVI, OSCAR, VecInfer and TaSQ each pushed to nominal 1-bit. CALFA-KV uses the TaSQ implementation adapted to d8b8 with 256 codewords per 8-channel group, a 64-token recent window, and 8-token refresh blocks — identical window/refresh settings for the TaSQ baseline, so that comparison is properly controlled.

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline at 1.008 BPE (d8b8): +4.43 / +6.71 / +4.42 on the 7-benchmark mean versus the adapted TaSQ baseline.** Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507: 64.03 vs 59.60 (BF16 66.95); Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct: 47.12 vs 40.41 (BF16 50.26); Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507: 66.72 vs 62.30 (BF16 71.11). Both methods are at a nominal 1.00 bit/channel, and CALFA-KV wins all 21 model×benchmark cells.
- **The gains are concentrated in IFEval, not in the long-context benchmark the framing implies.** Largest per-cell improvements over TaSQ are 11.09–24.03 points on IFEval (Qwen3-4B 82.99 vs 70.98; Llama 74.68 vs 50.65; Qwen3-30B 80.78 vs 69.69). On LongBench — the one genuinely long-context task — the deltas shrink to +1.28 / +1.45 / +1.37 (41.15 vs 39.87; 45.00 vs 43.55; 44.70 vs 43.33).
- **d8b10 (1.258 BPE) closes most of the gap to BF16: −0.90 / −1.30 / −2.01 on the mean.** 66.05 vs 66.95, 48.96 vs 50.26, 69.10 vs 71.11. Within the same anchor budget, d8b10 beats d8b8 by 1.84–2.38 points, which is the cleanest evidence that the codebook-rate axis and the anchor axis are complementary rather than redundant.
- **Ablation isolates the selector (Table 5, Qwen3-4B, d8b8, 56 anchors/layer/head): joint calibration alone 62.56 → +Fisher-guided 64.03 (+1.47), while +Random *lowers* it to 61.94 (−0.62).** Score-based selection beats random by 2.09 points and wins 6 of 7 tasks. The LongBench split is the starkest: 41.15 (Fisher) vs 30.00 (Random) vs 41.19 (no anchors at all) — random anchor retention actively destroys long-context performance.
- **Mechanistic diagnostic supports the sensitivity weighting.** With the d8b8 quantizer frozen and only the anchor policy varied, Fisher-guided selection reduces attention-distribution KL by 18.4%, post-$W_O$ relative MSE by 25.0%, and hidden-state relative MSE by 32.2% relative to no anchors, and beats both Random and Unweighted-error on all three metrics at the same budget (24 prompts, 8 each from Qasper/GovReport/LCC, 1,024-token prompts, 128-token BF16 continuation).
- **Compression occasionally exceeds BF16, which is a noise signal, not a win.** Qwen3-4B d8b8 scores 82.99 on IFEval vs BF16's 81.15 (+1.84) and 89.84 vs 92.34 on GSM8K; HumanEval+ at d8b10 is exactly 69.51, identical to BF16. With no seeds or confidence intervals reported for any benchmark, differences of this size cannot be distinguished from sampling noise.

## Authors & Credibility

Eight authors from **Shenzhen Intellifusion Technologies Co. Ltd.** (a Chinese computer-vision/AI chip and algorithm company), all with `@intellif.com` addresses; Leichao Zhang is the corresponding author and Shenhui Zhang / Yi Shan share contribution. This is an industry engineering paper, not an academic-lab paper — and it reads like one: heavy on implementation determinism (FP32 summation orders, dtype casts, buffer flush schedules, tie rules), light on statistical reporting.

Positive credibility signals: the paper is unusually explicit about what its own guarantees do *not* cover ("We do not estimate them or establish that this bound is small for the reported models"; "The selection views participate in model selection and are not reported as independent test data"; "The nominal rates exclude scale metadata, codebooks, windows, and anchors"). Those three sentences are exactly the ones a sloppy paper would omit. Code is released at `github.com/XiancaiTian/CALFA-KV`. All seven benchmark means I recomputed from the per-task columns match the reported values exactly.

Negative signals: no venue or prior peer review is indicated (arXiv preprint, cs.LG, 7 Nov 2026). Only one comparison target — TaSQ — is a genuine same-rate 1-bit method; the other three baselines are ports of methods designed for 2-bit, and their numbers are implausible (see Critical Assessment). The paper cites no compute budget, no calibration corpus, and no serving-system measurements.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty: incremental but genuine, and correctly scoped.** Every component has a clear ancestor — groupwise VQ and pre-RoPE key handling from KVQuant/CQ, query-guided channel weighting and covariance grouping from TaSQ, output-level calibration objectives from SKVQ/OptR, anchor retention from AnTKV/AnchorKV. What is new is the *coupling*: making the anchor score a functional of the frozen codec's realized error, and enforcing coordinate consistency between the Fisher estimate and the reconstruction error. The theory (App. A) is a straightforward uniform-bound argument — Cauchy–Schwarz on $\|F-F_0\|_{op}$, then a triangle inequality — and adds no insight beyond "the top-$N$ rule is optimal for an additive proxy, and the proxy is close to the risk when it is close."

**The three non-TaSQ baselines are not credible, which hollows out the "all nominal 1-bit baselines" claim.** On Qwen3-4B, KIVI at 2.000 BPE scores 0.45 on GSM8K, 0.00 on LiveCodeBench, and 8.43 on LongBench (mean 12.33). KIVI's own paper reports near-FP16 GSM8K at 2-bit; a 92-point collapse on grade-school arithmetic is not a property of the method, it is a property of the port. VecInfer is worse still at 1.06 GSM8K / 0.00 LiveCodeBench. OSCAR at 1.125 BPE reaches 40.44 but scores 5.26 on LiveCodeBench and 14.65 on GPQA. So the only informative comparison is against TaSQ, and the "outperforms all evaluated nominal 1-bit baselines" sentence in the abstract is technically true but rhetorically misleading — it is one real competitor, three broken ports. A same-rate 2-bit KIVI (2.0 BPE) would have been the honest reference point for what 2 bits buys, and it is absent.

**There is an internal inconsistency in the baseline description.** Sec. 4.2 states "KIVI reduces its asymmetric codes from 2 bits to 1 bit," yet Table 2 lists KIVI at 2.000 BPE. If KIVI's codes really were 1-bit, its 2.000 BPE would imply an implausibly large residual/metadata share; if they were 2-bit, the text is wrong. Either way the reader cannot tell whether KIVI is a rate-matched comparison.

**The anchor budget is excluded from the headline bit rate, and at short contexts it dominates.** The BPE column counts "K/V payload and quantization metadata… excluding codebooks, prefix and recent windows, and anchors." But 56 anchors per layer per KV head at FP16 is $56\times(128+128)\times2 = 28{,}672$ bytes per layer-head. For Qwen3-4B (36 layers × 8 KV heads) that is ~8.3 MB of anchors regardless of context length, while the 1-bit payload is only $n\times256$ bytes per layer. At $n=1{,}024$ tokens the payload is ~9.4 MB — so at the sequence lengths used by GSM8K, MATH500 and GPQA-Diamond, the "1-bit" cache plus anchors is roughly **double** the size the 1.008 BPE figure suggests. The advantage only becomes small at ~32k tokens (anchors ≈ 2.7%). Since four of the seven benchmarks are short-context, the reported bit rate materially misrepresents the operating point. (My arithmetic from the paper's stated parameters; the paper reports only the inventory formula A19, never a number.)

**No measured memory, latency, or throughput anywhere.** For a paper whose entire premise is "compress the KV cache," the absence of a single wall-clock, tokens-per-second, or resident-memory measurement is the largest gap. Appendix C.2 gives a storage inventory formula and an $O(n(d_K+d_V))$ scoring cost, but never evaluates either. The scoring pass itself runs per KV state on admission, needs the pre-RoPE original K/V still resident, and the mixed three-tier attention (exact / anchor / decoded) requires a non-standard kernel that Eq. A15 explicitly warns is *not* a numerically stable update recipe as written. Whether this is deployable at all is untested.

**The 1.258 BPE for d8b10 is estimated, not measured.** Table 2's note says the value assumes "packed 10-bit indices and the same scale layout." 10-bit indices are not a natural packing for any hardware; the entire d8b10 headline (0.90–2.01 gap to BF16) therefore rests on an assumed encoding that no implementation in the paper demonstrates.

**No variance reporting, and one seventh of each mean is near-noise.** Not a single benchmark number carries a seed, standard deviation, or confidence interval; only Fig. 2 has bootstrap bands. GPQA-Diamond has 198 questions, and Llama-3.1-8B scores 30.81 under BF16 — barely above the 25% chance level. CALFA-KV's Llama d8b8 score there is 25.25, i.e. exactly chance. That single cell contributes $5.56/7=0.79$ of the 3.14-point d8b8 gap on Llama, and for d8b10 it contributes $5.05/7=0.72$ of the total 1.30-point gap — **more than half the headline Llama result comes from a benchmark the base model cannot do.** The same pattern holds on Qwen3-4B d8b10, where GPQA contributes 0.43 of the 0.90 gap. Weighting seven benchmarks equally regardless of both size and headroom flatters the result.

**The Fisher selector hurts GPQA, and the ablation shows it.** Table 5: joint calibration alone gets 57.07 on GPQA-Diamond, adding Fisher anchors drops it to 54.54 (−2.53), while adding *random* anchors raises it to 58.58. The paper reports only the mean and the "6 of 7 tasks" count, never this reversal. It is not fatal — the mean gain is real and driven by IFEval and MATH500 — but a selection rule that reliably degrades expert-knowledge QA deserves a sentence of explanation, and there is none.

**The theoretical guarantee is vacuous as deployed.** Eq. A8 bounds risk relative to the *first-order* term $g^\top e$; the second-order term $\tfrac12 e^\top H e$ in Eq. A3 is dropped, and at 1-bit the errors $e$ are exactly where that term is least negligible. $\varepsilon_x$ is never estimated, exact restoration is assumed (the paper itself notes that saving in a different dtype "does not by itself establish zero error"), and the deployed weights $\bar{F}$ are clipped and shrunk in a way that can change token rankings (App. B.2 admits this). The honest reading is that Appendix A is a sanity argument, not a guarantee — which the authors effectively concede.

**Reproducibility is middling.** Code is promised, which is a real plus. But the calibration corpus is never identified (only "64 fixed windows" and "template-wrapped 2056-token windows"), the sensitivity windows' data source is unstated, and the layerwise diagnostic draws its 24 prompts from Qasper/GovReport/LCC — all LongBench subtasks — raising an unaddressed question about whether calibration or diagnostic data overlaps the LongBench evaluation. Table 3 also omits the BPE column that Tables 2 and 4 carry, so Llama's rate accounting is unverifiable.

## Strengths

- The central coupling idea is right and is validated: the ablation's 62.56 → 64.03 (Fisher) vs 61.94 (Random) is a clean, well-controlled demonstration that *which* states you protect matters more than *how many*, and that a naive reconstruction-error ranking is not enough.
- Genuine intellectual honesty in three specific places: the Select set is labelled as model selection rather than test data; $\varepsilon_x$ is explicitly not estimated; and the bit-rate note excludes anchors/codebooks rather than quietly folding them in.
- Implementation discipline is exceptional for this literature — FP32 balanced-tree scale reduction, dtype-cast-at-acceptance, native-kernel replay for every candidate, position-based tie rules, buffer flush synchronization. This is what makes the "1-bit" claim reproducible at all.
- The TaSQ comparison is properly controlled: same implementation, same 256-codeword 8-channel groups, same 64-token window, same 8-token refresh, same nominal rate. That is the comparison that matters and it is done correctly.
- The streaming top-$N$ invariant is proved, not asserted, and the paper correctly notes the limitation that re-scoring retained states cannot recover the true top-$N$ once originals are discarded.
- All reported means are arithmetically consistent with their per-task columns — I verified every row in Tables 2, 3, 4 and 5.

## Limitations

- **No systems evaluation at all**: no memory footprint, no decode latency, no throughput, no kernel. The compression ratio is nominal and the anchor overhead is excluded from it.
- **Only one credible baseline.** KIVI, VecInfer and OSCAR are 2-bit designs degraded to 1-bit and their numbers (0.45 GSM8K, 0.00 LiveCodeBench) indicate broken ports; no same-rate 2-bit reference is provided to show what the extra bit would buy.
- **Headline gains concentrate on short-context tasks.** The framing is KV-cache compression (a long-context problem) but the largest deltas are IFEval (+11 to +24) while LongBench gains are ~1.3–1.5 points.
- **Anchor budget is fixed at 56 per layer per KV head with no sensitivity study.** There is no curve of accuracy vs $N$, no per-layer budget allocation, and no analysis of what happens at 8, 16, or 128 anchors — so the reader cannot tell whether 56 was tuned on the evaluation set.
- **No statistical reporting**: single runs, no seeds, no confidence intervals, and a 7-way unweighted mean that gives 198 GPQA questions the same weight as 4,750 LongBench questions.
- **Calibration corpus and compute cost unstated**; the calibration pipeline (8 Gauss–Newton rounds × 24 CG iterations × 64 windows × 36 layers, each candidate replayed through native attention) is potentially expensive and its cost is never quantified.
- **Only three models, all instruction-tuned, none above 30B**, and no multi-head-latent-attention (MLA) architecture — despite the paper citing RoLaQ and RAQ-MLA, DeepSeek-style MLA caches are where 1-bit compression would matter most and are untested.
- **The GPQA reversal under Fisher selection is unreported**, and the theoretical bound is explicitly unverified.

## Relevance

直接命中 **Reliability under Extreme Performance Optimization**：本文是"激进低精度量化引入的精度/正确性退化"这一主题的正面案例——1-bit KV cache 会让 GSM8K 从 92 掉到 89、让 GPQA 掉到随机水平，而作者用"post-$W_O$ 输出标定 + Fisher 加权 anchor"把损失压回 1–2 分。对关注量化误差如何传播、以及"名义 bit 率"与实际存储/精度代价之间落差的研究者，这篇的实现细节（定点尺度变换、硬验收、坐标系一致性）比结论本身更有参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. **What is the actual memory and latency?** Given 56 FP16 anchors × 36 layers × 8 heads ≈ 8.3 MB of context-independent overhead, at what sequence length does CALFA-KV actually beat BF16 in bytes, and what does the mixed three-tier attention cost per decode step? A break-even curve (accuracy and bytes vs context length) is the missing figure.
2. **How sensitive is the result to $N=56$?** Is there an accuracy-vs-anchor-budget curve, and was 56 selected on the same seven benchmarks it is evaluated on? Also: why a uniform budget across layers when Fig. 2 shows error peaks in mid-layers?
3. **Why does Fisher selection drop GPQA-Diamond from 57.07 to 54.54 while random selection raises it to 58.58?** Is the Fisher weight over-emphasizing channels that matter for long-form reasoning but not for single-token multiple choice, and would a task-agnostic or entropy-based weight avoid the reversal?
4. **What data was used for the 64 calibration windows and the sensitivity windows?** Do any of them overlap LongBench's Qasper/GovReport/LCC splits, which are also the source of the layerwise diagnostic prompts?
5. **Is the KIVI baseline at 1 bit or 2 bits?** The text says codes were cut to 1 bit; Table 2 reports 2.000 BPE. And what does KIVI at its native 2 bits score on this harness — if it is competitive with CALFA-KV at 1.258 BPE, the practical case for 1-bit weakens considerably.
6. **Does the approach transfer to MLA architectures** (DeepSeek-style compressed latent KV), where keys and values share a latent and the pre-RoPE centering trick has no direct analogue?
7. **How large is $\varepsilon_x$ in practice?** Even a crude estimate on one layer would tell us whether the Appendix A bound is tight enough to be worth stating.
