> **一句话总结：** 论文证明：当奖励依赖同组其他样本（多样性、覆盖率、分布匹配等）时，GRPO 的组均值基线不再是无偏方差缩减，而是把目标函数改成了一个零和"锦标赛"——期望更新等于真实梯度中把外部性权重从 +1 翻成 −1/(K−1)；作者据此提出 Team-GRPO（令 α=+1，用"去掉自己后组奖励的下降量"作为优势），在 GlobalOpinionQA 上把与人类意见分布的平方误差从 GRPO 的 0.491 降到 0.081（基座 0.197），在 NoveltyBench 上把集合效用从 3.44 提到 4.61。

> **TL;DR:** For any reward that treats groupmates symmetrically, the paper proves an exact identity: the expected GRPO update is the gradient of `own + α·ext` with α = −1/(K−1), i.e. GRPO rewards a response for *lowering* its groupmates' rewards, so a pairwise diversity bonus keeps only (K−m)/(m(K−1)) of its true weight — 0 at K=2, 1/3 at K=4, 3/7 at K=8. Setting α=+1 gives Team-GRPO, an unbiased estimator (`A_k = R(G) − K/(K−1)·R(G\{y_k})`) that costs O(K²) arithmetic and no extra reward/judge calls for pairwise rewards. On GlobalOpinionQA with Qwen3.5-9B + LoRA, GRPO at K=2 collapses onto the majority answer and ends *worse than the untrained model* (squared error 0.491 vs base 0.197), while Team-GRPO reaches 0.081 (Δ = −0.40 [−0.43, −0.38]); on NoveltyBench at K=2 it beats GRPO by +1.16 [0.52, 1.80] set utility and +1.91 distinct answers, and still leads at matched KL divergence.

## 核心贡献

- **一个精确恒等式（Theorem 1）。** 对任意"可交换"组奖励（每个样本的奖励是其自身与其余组员多重集的固定函数），期望更新可写成 `g_α = E[(own(y) + α·ext(y))∇log π(y)]`，其中 `own` 是该回答自身能拿到的奖励、`ext` 是它带给组员的总奖励。真实梯度是 `g_1`，REINFORCE 是 `K·g_0`，RLOO 是 `K·g_{−1/(K−1)}`，GRPO 是 `(K−1)·g_{−1/(K−1)}`。三个估计器落在同一条"α 线"上，只差方向。
- **把偏差重新解释为博弈。** 用 α 估计器训练等价于 K 个共享参数玩家的自博弈，收益为 `U_k^α = r_k + α Σ_{j≠k} r_j`。α=1 是团队博弈（最大化 J），α=0 是纳什，α=−1/(K−1) 时收益求和为零——GRPO 在打一场零和锦标赛，这正是有限种群中选择"恶意（spite）"的相对收益博弈（Schaffer 1988）。
- **衰减阶梯（Corollary 2）。** 对 m 体对称交互项，GRPO 只保留 `ρ_m(K) = (K−m)/(m(K−1)) < 1/m` 的权重：成对多样性奖励在 K=2/4/8 只剩 0、1/3、3/7，永远不到一半；三元项不到三分之一；整组共享的奖励（如 pass@k 记给每个成员）GRPO 完全看不见。若奖励是成对核 `M = S + A`（对称+反对称），GRPO 在 K=2 只剩反对称部分的两倍，任何对称多样性项都被彻底抵消（Proposition 3）；K>2 时混入的反对称部分使其更新不再是任何目标的梯度。
- **闭式不动点（Proposition 4）。** 多样性奖励下 α 更新在单纯形上爬升显式势函数 `E_π f + κ_α λ(1−‖π‖²)`，最优是注水解；GRPO 的 `κ = (K−2)/(2(K−1))` 在 K=2 归零，必然收敛到单一最佳答案。分布匹配下 α 更新收敛到 `Proj_Δ(2t/(1+α))`：Team-GRPO 到 `t`，REINFORCE 到 `Proj_Δ(2t)`，GRPO 到 `Proj_Δ(2t·(K−2)/(K−1))`——K=2 时就是目标分布的众数。**GRPO 达不到目标分布不是优化失败，而是它所在博弈的均衡。**
- **Team-GRPO 与统一性。** `A_k^TEAM = R(G) − K/(K−1)·R(G\{y_k})`，即每个回答被记以"它让组平均奖励提高了多少"的 K 倍，是多智能体信用分配里的 difference reward（Wolpert & Tumer 2001）在单策略样本上的精确版，无需 critic。Proposition 6 证明它对任意可交换组奖励、任意 K≥2 无偏。它把 max@k 估计器（PKPO/L2O）、SetPO 的留一多样性、SWYS 的无中心 witness 估计器都收为特例；K=2 也可用（GRPO 在此完全失效）。
- **理论可定量预测训练终点。** 12 个有闭式解的 9B 沙盒实验全部收敛到其估计器博弈的不动点（最大 ℓ1 误差 0.018）；13 个配置下"预测集中度 vs 实测集中度"Pearson r = 0.995。

## Key Contributions

- **An exact identity for group-relative estimators.** For any exchangeable group reward, `E[ĝ] = K·g_α` with α = 0 (REINFORCE), α = −1/(K−1) (RLOO/GRPO). Since positive rescalings only change step size, the estimators differ *in direction only through α*, and the true gradient sits at α = +1. Subtracting the group mean does not discard the externality term — it flips its sign.
- **A game-theoretic reading with closed-form consequences.** Training with an α-estimator is self-play with shared parameters; GRPO's payoffs sum to zero (a tournament), REINFORCE's to a Nash game, Team-GRPO's to a team game. The attenuation ladder, the K=2 blindness to pairwise bonuses, and the distribution-matching bias toward the mode all follow analytically rather than empirically.
- **Team-GRPO**, an unbiased, critic-free estimator that reuses the pairwise matrix the reward already computes (delete row/column k), works at K=2 with a teammate-based baseline, and requires extra judge calls only for whole-set rewards.
- **When retuning the diversity coefficient can rescue GRPO** (Proposition 5): only for symmetric kernels, only with the exact K-dependent multiplier (3× at K=4, ≈7.3 at K=8), and never at K=2; quality-weighted diversity à la DARLING is not repairable at any K because the update mixes in an antisymmetric rotational field.
- **Empirical confirmation at 9B scale** in two regimes where the reward is genuinely group-dependent: distributional/pluralistic alignment (GlobalOpinionQA) and open-ended generation (NoveltyBench), plus exact enumeration and a logit-readable sandbox for the theory checks.
- **A negative result about the standard tool:** across six group-reward structures and 18 settings, GRPO's fixed point is never better than REINFORCE without any baseline, and often far worse (distribution matching at K=2 reaches −31% of Team-GRPO's objective; coverage of unique valid answers reaches 3%).

## Methodology

**Theory.** Fix a prompt; the policy draws K i.i.d. responses; each gets reward `r_k = r(y_k; {y_j}_{j≠k})`, exchangeable by assumption. Define `own(y) = E[r_1 | y_1 = y]` and `ext(y) = Σ_{j≥2} E[r_j | y_1 = y]`. Conditioning on `y_k` and using `E[s_θ(y)] = 0` yields Theorem 1 in three lines. Corollary 2 follows by computing, for an m-body symmetric kernel, that a fraction (m−1)/(K−1) of the subsets defining a groupmate's term contain the focal response. Proposition 3 splits a general pairwise kernel into symmetric and antisymmetric parts and shows the antisymmetric part contributes a rotational (non-gradient) field with weight K/(K−1). Proposition 4 derives the potential and its maximizer for (a) an indicator diversity bonus and (b) a collision-penalty distribution-matching reward. Proposition 5 gives the exact λ′ that makes GRPO match the true gradient and shows it exists only when K > m.

**Estimator.** For a group G, `R(S) = Σ_{y_j∈S} r(y_j; S\{y_j})` recomputes each member's reward as if S were the whole group. Then `A_k = R(G) − K/(K−1)·R(G\{y_k})`, i.e. K times the drop in mean group reward when y_k is removed. Unbiasedness: `R(G\{y_k})` is independent of y_k and vanishes in expectation against the score. Cost: for pairwise rewards, `R(G\{y_k})` is the same matrix with row/column k deleted — O(K²) arithmetic, no extra reward-model, embedding or judge calls. At K=2 the leave-one-out set is a singleton where a diversity term is undefined, so they use `A_k = r_1 + r_2 − b_k` with `b_k` an estimate of `E[r_1+r_2 | y_j]` built from the teammate's quality, the batch-mean distance, and the within-prompt quality correlation (0.74) estimated on earlier steps — which lowers `E[A²]` by 2.6× versus setting the missing diversity to zero.

**Experimental protocol.** Qwen3.5-9B, rank-16 LoRA, temperature 1, sequence-level policy-gradient loss, constant normalizer, no KL penalty, no std normalization, one on-policy update per batch, identical settings for every estimator. Three experiment families: (i) exact enumeration over small answer spaces (n ≤ 6, all n^K tuples) to check every identity to ~1e−15; (ii) a four-option "sandbox" where the policy is read exactly off the logits of the four option letters, trained on 128 prompts for 500 steps; (iii) two real training tasks. GlobalOpinionQA: 32,372 train / 1,154 test items split by question, model answers as a randomly selected adult of a given country with one option letter, reward from Theorem 4(b), 256 rollouts/step, 200 steps, lr 2e−5, error estimated unbiasedly from 32 samples per test item. NoveltyBench: 4,000 WildChat prompts (disjoint from test), reward `r_k = q_k(1 + λD_k)` with Skywork-Reward-V2-Llama-3.1-8B quality and E5 embedding distance, λ=2, K∈{2,8}, 64 rollouts/step, 150 steps, lr 5e−5, three seeds; evaluation on 400 prompts with the official DeBERTa classifier and Skywork-Reward-Gemma-2-27B scorer, plus a 100-prompt LLM-judge replication with Claude Sonnet 5.5.

## Results & Highlights

- **GlobalOpinionQA (squared error to the human answer distribution, 510 unseen questions, base model 0.197).** GRPO at K=2 **collapses and ends worse than no training at all**: error 0.491, with ≥90% of samples agreeing on 97% of test items (collapse rate 96.5%, TV distance 0.515 vs base 0.330). Team-GRPO reaches **0.081**, i.e. Δ = −0.40 [−0.43, −0.38] versus GRPO and 2.4× below the base model; REINFORCE lands between at 0.131, exactly where the α-line places it. Standard-deviation normalization does not rescue GRPO (0.508 at K=2, 0.207 at K=4 versus 0.181 unnormalized).
- **GlobalOpinionQA at larger K shows the gap is not a small-group artifact.** GRPO's fixed point is `Proj_Δ(2w_K t)` with `w_K=(K−2)/(K−1)`, giving measured errors 0.491 / 0.181 / 0.179 at K = 2/4/8, against Team-GRPO's 0.081 / 0.074 / 0.077 — still 2.3–2.4× worse at K=4 and K=8. At K=8 the centred-witness estimator (0.073), Poly-EPO (0.074) and Team-GRPO (0.077) finish within 0.005 of each other, as predicted, since all three target `t`; at K=4 the witness's predicted `1/K·∇‖π‖²` bias shows up as 0.096 vs 0.074 (difference −0.022 [−0.029, −0.015]).
- **NoveltyBench, official benchmark scorer (400 prompts, 3 seeds, K=2, λ=2).** Team-GRPO: utility **4.61**, distinct answers **4.56**; GRPO: 3.44 and 2.65; base model: 4.01 and 4.33. Paired deltas **+1.16 [0.52, 1.80]** utility and **+1.91 [1.01, 2.81]** distinct. The gain survives at matched sequence-KL (GRPO drifts to KL 123 vs Team-GRPO's 54): +0.84 [0.79, 0.90] and +1.12 [0.46, 1.79]. At K=8 the deltas narrow to +0.56 [0.32, 0.81] and +1.04 [0.99, 1.09], consistent with the attenuation ladder.
- **NoveltyBench, independent LLM judge (Claude Sonnet 5.5, NoveltyBench v1.1, 100 prompts) — the check that matters, since the training reward model and the benchmark scorer are both Skywork.** Team-GRPO beats GRPO by **+0.61 [0.36, 0.85]** utility and **+2.09 [1.67, 2.52]** distinct at K=2, and by +0.26 [0.07, 0.45] / +1.34 [1.05, 1.63] at K=8; it is the only trained model above the base model on both set metrics. The trade is real and honestly reported: first-sample quality drops from 9.04 (GRPO) to 8.51 (Team-GRPO) on the benchmark scorer.
- **More diversity weight makes GRPO strictly worse, as Equation (5) predicts.** Over the last 25 training steps, GRPO's mean pairwise embedding distance falls 0.193 → 0.186 → 0.147 as λ goes 0.25 → 1 → 2, while Team-GRPO rises 0.231 → 0.249 → 0.293 for λ = 1 → 2 → 4. The best GRPO coefficient on a grid (λ=1) reaches only 3.83 utility — *below the untrained model's 4.01*.
- **Sandbox with exact policy readout (Qwen3.5-9B, 128 prompts, 500 steps, diversity bonus with f = (1, 0.5, 0.2, 0)).** All 12 runs with a closed-form prediction converge to their estimator's fixed point, largest ℓ1 error **0.018**; held-out prompts reproduce training prompts to within 0.004 per option. GRPO at K=2 puts all mass on the best option for λ ∈ {0, 1, 5}; Team-GRPO reaches the same diversified policy (.537, .287, .138, .037) at K=2, 4 and 8; multiplying λ by 2(K−1)/(K−2)=3 moves GRPO at K=4 onto it, exactly as Proposition 5 prescribes.
- **The ordering GRPO ≤ REINFORCE ≤ Team-GRPO holds in all 18 exact settings of Table 1.** Distribution matching at K=2: GRPO reaches **−31%** of Team-GRPO's objective (i.e. actively worse than doing nothing) and REINFORCE 67%. Coverage of unique valid answers: GRPO 3% at K=2, and still only 40% at K=8 (REINFORCE 40% too). Correctness × number of strategy clusters (Poly-EPO's objective): GRPO falls from 67% at K=2 to **44%** at K=8 as it settles on one correct strategy, while REINFORCE degrades 100% → 83%. GRPO ties Team-GRPO only for split-credit pass@K, where the optimum happens to be an equilibrium of the tournament.
- **The theory predicts concentration quantitatively.** Across all 13 configurations with a closed-form fixed point, predicted and measured mass on the most-chosen answer agree with **Pearson r = 0.995**, with measured values on average 0.05 below predictions.
- **Cost and variance, stated plainly.** Team-GRPO's variance at matched scale is 1.1–2.1× RLOO's for K ≥ 4 (5.3× at K=3, where the counterfactual group has only two members); GRPO's *bias*, by contrast, is a 39–86% relative error that averaging over more prompts per step cannot remove.

Junyi Gao (Centre for Medical Informatics, University of Edinburgh; Health Data Research UK), Yu Shi (Biostatistics, Dalla Lana School of Public Health, University of Toronto), with Pingzhao Hu (Biochemistry, Western University) and Ewen M Harrison (Edinburgh) as senior authors; Gao and Shi share first authorship. This is a methods paper written by health-informatics/biostatistics researchers rather than an RL-theory group, which shows in the framing: unusually careful about what a reward *means* for a population (the impact statement is about pluralistic alignment and target populations, not capability), and unusually willing to report the cases where their method merely ties. The work is self-contained — every claim is either proved in the appendix or checked by exact enumeration, and the theory predictions were registered before the corresponding runs ("each fixed point before the corresponding experiment"), which is a stronger evidentiary standard than most RL papers meet. No code or artifact link is given in the PDF, which is the main reproducibility gap.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The core observation — that a baseline which depends on the action reintroduces bias — is old and is cited as such (Tucker et al. 2018, "The mirage of action-dependent baselines"). What is genuinely new is the *exact* form of the bias for exchangeable group rewards, its reading as a zero-sum game, and the resulting quantitative predictions (the (K−m)/(m(K−1)) ladder; `Proj_Δ(2t(K−2)/(K−1))` for distribution matching). The GAPO argument that "the reward does not contain θ, so centring is unbiased" is a real and specific error in the recent literature, and this paper refutes it cleanly rather than by assertion. That is a substantive contribution, not a rebranding of a known variance-reduction caveat.

**Rigor.** The theory side is excellent: identities verified to 6e−14–1e−15 by enumeration over random exchangeable rewards for K ∈ {2,3,4,6,10} and m ∈ {2,3,4}; Proposition 9 extends the fixed-point results to clipped surrogates with several epochs per batch (measured drift ≤ 0.074 in ℓ1 at μ=8, against GRPO–Team-GRPO fixed-point distances of 0.50–1.10), which is exactly the ablation a skeptical practitioner would ask for. The empirical side is honest about its own losses (first-sample quality, variance, the K=8 tie with the witness estimator).

**Weak points I would press on.**
1. **One model, one tuning.** Everything is Qwen3.5-9B with rank-16 LoRA. No 1–2B or 30B+ checkpoints, no full fine-tuning, no second model family. The claim that the theory "predicts quantitatively" is verified on a single policy, and LoRA's low-rank constraint could plausibly make fixed-point convergence easier (fewer directions to escape into) than full-parameter training would.
2. **GlobalOpinionQA is a 4–5-way single-token task.** The model emits one letter with ≤16 new tokens, so "matching a country's opinion distribution" reduces to a categorical distribution over a handful of options. This is the *best possible* case for the collapse prediction — with only four actions, driving all mass onto one is trivially reachable — and it is far from the free-form pluralistic alignment the motivation invokes. The NoveltyBench results partially compensate, but the headline 6× error reduction (0.491 → 0.081) comes from the easiest setting in the paper.
3. **The K=2 baseline is a heuristic dressed as a detail.** At K=2 Team-GRPO needs `b_k` estimated from the teammate's quality, the batch-mean distance, and a within-prompt quality correlation of 0.74 measured on earlier steps. Unbiasedness holds for any y_k-independent b_k, so the theory is safe, but the *variance* is not: Table 14 shows `E[A²]` = 0.056 versus 0.035 for GRPO at matched scale, and the whole K=2 regime — the regime the paper most wants to win — rests on a reward-specific predictor that would need re-deriving for every new reward.
4. **No training experiments at large K, where practice lives.** GAPO uses K=32 and SWYS K=64; this paper trains at K ∈ {2, 8} and computes exact fixed points only up to K=12. Since the ladder predicts the gap *shrinks* with K (0 → 1/3 → 3/7), the practical recommendation for a lab already running K=32 is weaker than the abstract implies, and the paper does not say so explicitly.
5. **No standard RLVR benchmark.** There is no math/code experiment (GSM8K, MATH, AIME) anywhere. The authors' defence is principled — for ordinary `r_k = f(y_k)` rewards GRPO is unbiased, and for pass@K the tournament optimum coincides with the team optimum — but it means a reader cannot see whether Team-GRPO costs anything on the workload where GRPO is actually the default. A single AIME/GSM8K run showing "no regression" would have closed this.
6. **Scorer contamination is mitigated, not eliminated.** The training reward model and the benchmark scorer are both Skywork (Reward-V2-Llama-3.1-8B and Reward-Gemma-2-27B). The Claude Sonnet 5.5 replication is the right fix and the deltas hold (+0.61/+2.09 at K=2), but the judge is a single model on 100 prompts, and the paper does not report judge-vs-human agreement.
7. **Exchangeability is a real restriction.** Rewards that treat group members asymmetrically — rank-weighted rewards, "first sample" bonuses, position-dependent credit — fall outside the theorem, and the paper is careful to say so but does not quantify how much of the practical design space that excludes.

**Overclaiming check.** The abstract's "often does worse than using no baseline at all" is backed by Table 1 (18/18 settings) and by the NoveltyBench λ-sweep where the best GRPO coefficient lands below the base model. The "works with two samples per prompt" claim is true but depends on point 3. The "requires no extra model calls for pairwise rewards" is true for K ≥ 3 and false at K=2 in spirit (it requires a batch-level predictor, though not a model call). These are small, and the paper generally understates rather than overstates.

## Strengths

- A genuinely useful theoretical result, proved in a page and verified to machine precision: it converts a vague worry ("group-relative baselines might interact badly with diversity rewards") into a number — the fraction of a pairwise bonus that survives, as a function of K.
- The game-theoretic reframing is not decoration: it *predicts* the fixed point (water-filling for diversity, `Proj_Δ(2t(K−2)/(K−1))` for distribution matching), and the predictions land with r = 0.995 across 13 configurations. Predicting where training stops, before running it, is rare in this literature.
- Team-GRPO is close to free: O(K²) arithmetic on a matrix the reward already computes, no critic, no extra judge calls, no change to the loss, clipping, or normalization machinery.
- Unification rather than competition: PKPO/L2O, SetPO, and the uncentred SWYS witness estimator are shown to be the same estimator on different rewards, which is more useful to a practitioner than a new acronym.
- The negative results are as carefully reported as the positive ones — the K=8 tie with the witness estimator, the first-sample quality drop, the 5.3× variance at K=3, and the fact that retuning λ rescues GRPO only in the binary-kernel case.
- The λ-sweep (Table 15) is a genuinely informative ablation: it shows the failure is monotone in the strength of the group dependence, which is exactly what a correct diagnosis should produce and what a generic "our method is better" paper would not bother to show.

## Limitations

- Single model (Qwen3.5-9B), single adaptation method (rank-16 LoRA), single temperature; no scaling study and no second model family.
- GlobalOpinionQA is reduced to letter-only multiple choice with ≤16 generated tokens; the strongest quantitative headline comes from the most constrained task in the paper.
- No RLVR benchmark (math/code) training run, so the cost of switching on the standard GRPO workload is unmeasured.
- Training at K ∈ {2, 8} only; the large-K regime (32–64) that several cited methods actually use is covered analytically but not empirically.
- The K=2 estimator needs a reward-specific baseline predictor whose variance advantage is not guaranteed to transfer to new reward shapes.
- Rewards that are not exchangeable (asymmetric credit, rank-based, position-dependent) are outside the theory entirely.
- No released code, and the appendix's exact computations depend on enumeration over n ≤ 6 answers, so independent replication of the theory checks requires reimplementation.
- The paper is 20 pages of dense main text plus appendix; the practical recipe (Algorithm 1) is four lines, but extracting it requires reading past a great deal of game theory.

## Relevance

直击 "RL Post-Training Dynamics & Failures" 这一关注方向：它把 GRPO 的多样性坍塌从"经验现象"提升为可证明的、带闭式不动点的目标函数错位，并给出熵/多样性衰减的定量预测（成对奖励只剩 0、1/3、3/7 的权重），对研究 RLVR 训练不稳定与奖励设计失配很有参考价值。若关心 group-relative 估计器的偏差来源，这篇的 α 恒等式可以作为分析其他"组依赖奖励"（如 pass@k、覆盖率、分布匹配）的统一工具。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the fixed-point prediction survive full-parameter training and at 30B+ scale, or is LoRA's low-rank constraint doing part of the work by limiting the directions in which the policy can escape?
2. What happens at K=32 and K=64 in actual training? The ladder says the gap narrows to 3/7 and 31/62 — does the wall-clock cost of K² credit assignment still pay for itself there, or does the advantage vanish into noise?
3. On standard RLVR (GSM8K/MATH with binary verifiable rewards), does Team-GRPO match GRPO exactly as the theory predicts, or does the K=2 baseline predictor introduce a measurable regression?
4. How sensitive is the K=2 baseline to the within-prompt quality correlation estimate (0.74 here)? What happens when quality and diversity are anti-correlated, e.g. when the diverse answers are systematically worse?
5. Can the antisymmetric component `A` in Proposition 3 be exploited deliberately — a reward that is *purely* antisymmetric is invisible to the true gradient but doubled by GRPO, which suggests a way to construct reward-hacking demonstrations or adversarial group rewards.
6. Does the tournament framing explain other reported GRPO pathologies — entropy collapse, length inflation, the "thinking collapse" in R1-Zero-style training — or is diversity the only axis where the sign flip bites?
7. What is the right target when the "population" being matched is itself contested (the impact statement's warning)? The method faithfully reproduces whatever distribution it is given, including its biases; is there a principled way to specify `t`?
