> **一句话总结：** MACaQ 把 W4A4 MXFP4 量化彻底"局部化"到单个线性层——每层在线做一次逐通道缩放加 32 块 Hadamard 旋转、离线把逆变换吸进权重，再用一个只允许 FP4 码跳到相邻可表示值的 Hessian 感知坐标下降（Flip-Refinement）替代 GPTQ；在 Llama-3-8B 上 WikiText-2 困惑度 6.90（优于 MR-GPTQ 7.13 与 DuQuant++(GPTQ) 7.17，FP16 为 6.14），达到同等质量只需约 1/7–1/11 的量化时间，Qwen3-32B 上端到端解码仅多 2.3% 开销、吞吐达 FP16 的 3.06 倍，并能在单张 96GB GPU 上用 32.5GB 显存、64 分钟量化完 122B 的 Qwen3.5-122B-A10B。

> **TL;DR:** MACaQ is an MXFP4 W4A4 post-training quantizer that keeps every transform layer-local: a per-channel scale `t` plus a block-diagonal Hadamard rotation (B=32, matching the MXFP4 block) is applied to activations online at inference, with the inverse absorbed into weights offline, plus a Hessian-weighted block-exponent search over offsets {+1,0,−1} and "Flip-Refinement" — a greedy coordinate descent that moves individual E2M1 codes to adjacent levels using the exact quadratic gain `2Δg − Δ²H_jj`. On WikiText-2 with Llama-3-8B it reaches **6.90 PPL vs 7.13 for MR-GPTQ and 7.17 for DuQuant++(GPTQ)** (FP16 = 6.14), and 11.01 vs 11.37 on C4; on Qwen3-32B it gets 7.94 vs 8.07 for DuQuant++(GPTQ). The selling point is the time axis, not the quality axis: it matches the strongest baselines in ~1/7 of the wall-clock at directly measured operating points (88.1 s → 6.91 PPL vs DuQuant++(GPTQ) 624.4 s → 7.16 PPL), with an 11× figure that comes from extrapolating baseline token-scaling curves. Read it if you care about practical FP4 deployment, layer-streaming quantization of >100B models, or cheap GPTQ replacements; skip it if you need evidence that FP4 gains survive on reasoning benchmarks — there MACaQ is within noise of, and sometimes behind, MR-GPTQ (MATH-500 97.26% vs 97.44%).

## 核心贡献

- **严格"逐线性层"的 W4A4 MXFP4 量化流程**：所有变换、标定统计量、块指数与 FP4 码都只属于当前层，不需要修改相邻的 normalization、残差分支或前置投影，因此同一套实现可直接搬到新架构上，无需为每种模型找"变换吸收目标"。
- **把基变换留在在线侧、只付小代价**：论证了在线逐通道缩放 + 32 块 Hadamard 的算术量约为矩阵乘的 `3/m`（B=32 时），随输出维度增大而摊薄；实测 Qwen3-32B 解码端到端开销 2.3%，Qwen3-0.6B 升至约 8%。
- **Flip-Refinement：用坐标下降替代 GPTQ**：从廉价的 RTN 初值出发，每轮为每个输出行挑选增益最大的"单码跳到相邻 E2M1 电平"动作，增益 `2Δg_{i,j} − Δ²(H_f)_{jj}` 有闭式解，接受后用 `g_i ← g_i − Δ(H_f)_{:,j}` 增量更新梯度，收敛行提前退出活跃集；块指数在细化期间固定。
- **Hessian 加权的块指数选择**：MXFP4 每 32 个元素共享一个 E8M0 幂次刻度，作者在标准"块最大值导出指数"的 {+1,0,−1} 三个偏移上，用 `Σ_j (H_f)_{jj}(W'_{ij} − Ŵ'_{ij}(e))²` 直接挑选，把"分辨率 vs 截断"的权衡交给数据决定，而不是交给 max。
- **可落地的系统工程**：给出 vLLM 中的 Triton kernel，kernel 级相对 FP16 最高 5.04×（N=16384 解码），端到端 Qwen3-32B batch-1 解码 69.8 vs 22.8 tok/s；并用 lazy loading 的流式流程在 96GB 单卡上把 122B 模型压到 32.5GB 峰值显存、64 分钟完成，产物 74GB 可同卡运行（WikiText-2 5.14 vs FP16 5.06）。
- **跨架构验证**：除 Llama-3 / Qwen3 / Qwen3.5 外，未做任何架构特化就把 FLUX.2-klein-4B 扩散模型的 DiT 线性层量化到 MXFP4，并在 GenEval2 的 800 条 prompt 上四项保真度指标全面优于官方 NVFP4 checkpoint（且比特数更低，4.25 vs 4.50 bits/w）。

## Key Contributions

- **A strictly per-linear-layer W4A4 MXFP4 pipeline.** Every scaling factor, rotation, Hessian, block exponent and FP4 code belongs to one layer; no neighboring layer is modified and no basis transform is propagated through the network. This removes the "find an absorption target for P" engineering step that prior rotation methods (QuaRot, DuQuant, FlatQuant) require, and makes the method architecture-agnostic by construction.
- **An explicit argument for keeping the basis transform online.** Prior work absorbs the activation-side transform into preceding operators to avoid runtime cost; MACaQ shows the online cost is `Tn(log2 B + 1)` versus `2Tmn` for the matmul, i.e. a ratio of `3/m` at B=32 — below 0.3% in arithmetic for the smallest Llama-3-8B layer (m=1024) and 2.3% measured end-to-end on Qwen3-32B.
- **Flip-Refinement, a Hessian-aware coordinate-descent replacement for GPTQ.** Instead of assigning weights sequentially and compensating the remainder, it starts from RTN and revisits individual codes, accepting at most one adjacent-level move per output row per iteration, with exact closed-form gain and O(n) gradient updates. Rows are independent, so it parallelizes trivially and supports an early-exit active set.
- **Hessian-weighted shared-scale (block exponent) optimization.** A previously under-treated degree of freedom in MXFP4 — the power-of-two E8M0 block scale — is chosen by minimizing a diagonal-Hessian-weighted reconstruction error over three candidate offsets, and then frozen during refinement so the gain formula stays exact.
- **A streaming, memory-bounded quantization procedure plus production kernels.** Hessians are accumulated by forward hooks over calibration chunks; then layers are quantized one at a time with the rest of the weights in host memory. Demonstrated on a 122B MoE model in 64 min / 32.5GB, and benchmarked inside vLLM against Marlin and FP16 across batch sizes.
- **Generality evidence beyond LLMs**: a diffusion transformer quantized with no architectural changes, and (appendix) an extension of Flip-Refinement to *vector*-quantized llama.cpp formats IQ3_S / IQ3_XXS via block-wise nearest-codeword search under the Hessian metric.

## Methodology

**Problem setting.** Standard layer-wise reconstruction: for `W ∈ R^{m×n}` and calibration inputs `X ∈ R^{N×n}`, minimize `||XWᵀ − XŴᵀ||_F² = Σ_i r_i H r_iᵀ` with `H = XᵀX` shared across output rows. The quantization grid is OCP MXFP4: blocks of B=32 sharing an E8M0 (8-bit exponent, no mantissa) scale, each element an E2M1 code (1 sign, 2 exponent, 1 mantissa bit).

**Step 1 — online per-channel scaling.** `t_j = (σ_j^W)^α / (rms^x_j)^{1−α}` with `rms^x_j = sqrt(H_jj/N)`, `α = 0.75` chosen on a WikiText-2 train subset, scales clamped for numerical stability. Activations are multiplied by `t`, weight columns divided by `t`. Channels with large activation RMS get attenuated before activation quantization; fat weight columns get shrunk. This is the SmoothQuant/AWQ idea, but here it is only one of two transforms and is deliberately applied *before* rotation, since channel-importance weighting must happen in the unrotated basis.

**Step 2 — online micro-rotation.** A block-diagonal Hadamard `B` with 32×32 blocks, applied to activations online and absorbed into weights offline: `x' = (x ⊙ t)B`, `W' = (W ⊘ t)B`. The block size is deliberately tied to the MXFP4 block size so that an outlier is not smeared across several independently scaled blocks (the argument from MR-GPTQ / DuQuant++). Combined layer computation at inference: `y = [(x ⊙ t)B]_{MXFP4} · Ŵ'ᵀ_{MXFP4}` — the only added ops versus a plain MXFP4 kernel are one multiply and `log2(32)=5` butterfly stages.

**Step 3 — Hessian-weighted exponent selection.** For each transformed weight row and each 32-block, evaluate exponents `{e_max+1, e_max, e_max−1}` where `e_max` comes from the block maximum, round to the E2M1 grid, and score `E_{i,B}(e) = Σ_{j∈B} (H_f)_{jj} (W'_{ij} − Ŵ'_{ij}(e))²`. Pick the argmin. `H_f = H' + λ·mean(diag(H'))·I` with `λ=1.0` is the ridge-regularized Hessian in the transformed basis, `H' = BᵀDHDB`.

**Step 4 — Flip-Refinement.** With block exponents frozen, iterate: compute residual `r_i` and gradient `g_i = H_f r_iᵀ`; for each row find the single adjacent-level move with maximal gain `2Δg_{i,j} − Δ²(H_f)_{jj}` (Δ is the actual reconstruction change on the non-uniform E2M1 grid, so it depends on current code, direction and block scale); accept if gain > 0, update `g_i ← g_i − Δ(H_f)_{:,j}`, drop converged rows. At most one flip per row per iteration, so `K` bounds flips per row; default `K=128` for the main experiments (512 for the ablation and the 122B run), with `torch.compile` on the refinement step and per-row parallelism.

**Cost model and why locality pays.** Online work per token is `Tn(log2 B + 1)`; matmul is `2Tmn`; ratio `3/m`. Because the transform is applied once per input and reused across all `m` output channels, overhead shrinks with layer width — measured 8% (Qwen3-0.6B) → 2.3% (Qwen3-32B). The design also lets quantization run as a stream: accumulate Hessians for all layers via hooks, then load, transform, quantize and repack one layer at a time.

**Implementation knobs.** Two explicit quality/time levers: number of calibration tokens and `K`. Quality plateaus around 2×10⁶ tokens and `K=512`; the paper's headline comparisons deliberately use the cheaper `K=128`, 2×10⁵ tokens (≈150 s for Llama-3-8B) so the baselines are not handicapped.

**Evaluation protocol.** Perplexity on WikiText-2 (in-distribution w.r.t. WikiText-2-train calibration) and C4 (OOD), context 2048, over Llama-3-8B, Llama-3.2-3B, Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen3-32B, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B. Generative: IFEval strict prompt-level accuracy and MATH-500, averaged over k=10 samples with across-seed standard error. Diffusion: FLUX.2-klein-4B, DiT-only W4A4, 800 GenEval2 prompts at 1024², 6 steps. Systems: single-GPU kernel benchmarks and vLLM end-to-end throughput. Baselines: QuaRot (RTN/GPTQ), FlatQuant, MR-GPTQ, DuQuant++ (RTN/GPTQ), Marlin (W4A16), and the official NVFP4 FLUX checkpoint.

## Results & Highlights

- **WikiText-2 perplexity, Llama-3-8B, MXFP4 W4A4: 6.90 for MACaQ vs 7.13 for MR-GPTQ and 7.17 for DuQuant++(GPTQ) — a 0.23–0.27 PPL gain over the best baselines, with FP16 at 6.14.** The ordering holds on C4 (11.01 vs 11.37 / 11.66), so this is not just an in-distribution artifact of calibrating on WikiText-2-train.
- **The margin is smaller on newer/more brittle models and effectively vanishes on the MoE.** Llama-3.2-3B: 8.61 vs 8.92 (MR-GPTQ) on Wiki2 and 13.07 vs 13.51 on C4. Qwen3-32B: 7.94 vs 8.07 (DuQuant++ GPTQ) and 12.93 vs 13.15. Qwen3.5-35B-A3B: 6.66 vs 6.73 on Wiki2 and 11.21 vs 11.22 on C4 — a statistical tie against a 6.55/10.98 FP16 reference, i.e. the headline quality claim is strongest exactly where the baselines are weakest (Llama-3-8B).
- **Quantization-time/quality Pareto: at the directly measured operating point MACaQ reaches 6.91 PPL in 88.1 s (128 flips, 31.3 calibration blocks) while DuQuant++(GPTQ) needs 624.4 s to reach 7.16 and MR-GPTQ 267.9 s to reach 7.11 — roughly 7× faster at equal-or-better quality.** The abstract's "11×" figure is larger than any pair of points in Table 7 and appears to come from extrapolating the baselines' linear scaling in calibration tokens up to MACaQ's plateau setting (~2×10⁶ tokens, `K=512`), not from a measured baseline run.
- **Generative benchmarks do not reproduce the perplexity win.** Qwen3-32B IFEval: MACaQ 83.20% vs FP16 83.60% and MR-GPTQ 82.81% (best quantized). But Qwen3-32B MATH-500: 97.26% vs MR-GPTQ 97.44% and QuaRot(GPTQ) 96.98% — MACaQ is second. On Qwen3.5-35B-A3B IFEval, MACaQ's 85.42% also trails QuaRot(GPTQ) 85.60% and DuQuant++(GPTQ) 85.59%, though all are within ±0.4 pt (≈1.5σ) of the 85.29% FP16 reference.
- **Diffusion, FLUX.2-klein-4B (GenEval2, 800 prompts, DiT-only W4A4): MACaQ beats the official NVFP4 checkpoint on all four paired-fidelity metrics — PSNR 15.04 vs 14.85 dB, SSIM 0.697 vs 0.676, LPIPS 0.218 vs 0.235, DINOv3-global 0.882 vs 0.878 — while using 4.25 rather than 4.50 bits/weight and 13.1 vs 13.3 GB peak VRAM.** The absolute PSNR is low for both, so read this as "MACaQ drifts less from BF16 than NVFP4 does", not as "near-lossless".
- **Kernel-level, MXFP4 W4A4 vs FP16 (median speedup): 5.04× for N=16384 decode, 4.47× at batch 16/L=1, 2.01× at N=16384/L=16384, and 1.31× for the smallest N=4096 decode case.** The scale+rotation surcharge is <1% for N=4096 decode, 6.4% for N=4096/L=16384, and 0.9–1.7% for the large-layer cases — consistent with the `3/m` model.
- **End-to-end vLLM: Qwen3-32B batch-1 decode 69.8 tok/s vs 22.8 for FP16 (3.06×) and 71.8 for Marlin W4A16; batch-256 decode 5252.8 vs 3061.5 tok/s (1.72×).** Llama-3-8B batch-1: 227.9 vs 93.2 (2.45×) and slightly ahead of Marlin's 223.9; at batch 256 it reaches 14,207.6 vs Marlin's 11,220.3, supporting the claim that it wins in compute-bound regimes.
- **Ablation on Llama-3-8B (512 flips, 200k tokens, Wiki2): RTN 8.28 → +Hessian exponent search 8.09 (−0.19) → +micro-rotation 7.65 (−0.44) → +online second scale 7.08 (−0.57) → +Flip-Refinement 6.87 (−0.21), against 6.14 FP16.** Rotation is the single largest contributor; Flip-Refinement buys about the same as the exponent search at a fraction of GPTQ's cost.
- **Large-model streaming: Qwen3.5-122B-A10B (122B params, ~244GB in FP16) quantized in 64 minutes with 32.5GB peak GPU RAM, yielding WikiText-2 5.14 vs 5.06 for FP16 and a 74GB MXFP4 artifact that runs on the same 96GB card** (32 flips, 512K calibration tokens; MoE expert layers only).
- **Appendix, weight-only llama.cpp formats: on Qwen3-4B plain IQ3_S, block refinement cuts WikiText-2 from 13.06 to 11.81 vs 12.31 for scalar flips and 11.83 for imatrix initialization alone, reducing per-tensor Hessian objectives by 30–42% (vs 16–32% for scalar flips) at 5–8 s per large tensor.**

## Authors & Credibility

Six authors, all at the **Computing Systems Lab, Huawei Technologies, Switzerland** (Huawei's Zurich research site). The group has a visible track record in exactly this niche: Lorenz K. Muller is an author of SINQ (ref. 21, calibration-free Sinkhorn-normalized weight quantization) and the team publishes on low-precision numerics and efficient inference. The reference list is well-matched to the argument — QuaRot, QuIP, SmoothQuant, GPTQ/OBC/OBS, FlatQuant, DuQuant/DuQuant++, MR-GPTQ, Marlin, SpinQuant, AWQ, plus the OCP microscaling spec — and the two most direct competitors (MR-GPTQ, DuQuant++) are ICLR/arXiv 2026 work, so the comparison is current rather than a stale strawman.

Credibility signals that raise my estimate: all baselines are re-run on the same hardware with the same E8M0-compliant cast (with an explicit footnote that this shifts DuQuant++'s numbers relative to its own paper); a Pareto curve is reported instead of a single cherry-picked point; the ablation is component-wise and additive; hyperparameters are stated with the validation set they were chosen on and a sensitivity plot; limitations are stated plainly (no cross-layer compensation, MoE experts only, 8% overhead at 0.6B); and the appendix reports a *negative* result (block refinement loses to scalar flips at 14B) rather than hiding it.

Weakening signals: this is an arXiv v1 preprint (7 Nov 2026) with no venue, no peer review, and **no code or artifact link anywhere in the paper** despite the central claims being about Triton kernels and vLLM integration — reproduction means reimplementing the kernels. The 122B and 0.6B datapoints, the diffusion numbers and the IQ3 experiments each appear once, without seeds or error bars. And the headline "11×" is a derived number, not a measured one. Overall: a competent, unusually honest industry systems paper whose quality claims are solid but modest, and whose speed claims are real but stated at their most favorable extrapolation in the abstract.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The individual ingredients are all borrowed — per-channel scaling is SmoothQuant, block Hadamard is QuaRot/MR-GPTQ, Hessian-weighted reconstruction is OBQ/GPTQ, coordinate descent on codes is QuaEase/CDQuant. The genuinely new pieces are (i) the *combination rule* that lets both transforms stay inside the layer, with a clean cost argument (`3/m`) for why that is affordable, and (ii) Flip-Refinement's specific restriction to adjacent E2M1 levels with frozen block exponents, which is what makes the gain formula exact and the update O(n) instead of O(n²). That is a real, if incremental, contribution: the paper's own framing ("we argue for tolerating the overhead") is honest about being a design-tradeoff paper rather than a new algorithmic primitive. The exponent-search idea is the most under-claimed novelty and the least explored.

**Rigor — where the evidence is strong.** Perplexity numbers are consistent across six models and two test sets, the direction never flips, and the baselines are re-run rather than quoted. The ablation is clean and additive (8.28 → 8.09 → 7.65 → 7.08 → 6.87), and the fact that the paper reports the *cheaper* `K=128`/200k-token configuration in the main table — while the ablation and the 122B run use 512 flips — means the headline quality numbers are conservative, not inflated. The systems measurements are the best part: kernel-level and end-to-end tables agree, the `−SR` ablation isolates the transform cost, and the observed overhead (8% at 0.6B → 2.3% at 32B) matches the `3/m` prediction.

**Rigor — where it is thin.** Four specific problems:

1. **The generative results contradict the narrative but are discussed as if they support it.** On Qwen3-32B MATH-500, MACaQ scores 97.26% against MR-GPTQ's 97.44%; on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B IFEval, 85.42% against QuaRot(GPTQ)'s 85.60% and DuQuant++(GPTQ)'s 85.59%. The paper's text says only that "MACaQ performs well on these tasks" — true, but it never states that the perplexity advantage does not transfer. For a W4A4 method the honest summary is "perplexity win, generative parity", and the abstract should say so.
2. **The 11× headline is an extrapolation, and Table 7 does not contain the point that supports it.** The largest directly measured gap is 88.1 s → 6.91 PPL versus 624.4 s → 7.16 PPL (≈7×). To reach 11× one must trust that DuQuant++/MR-GPTQ time scales linearly with calibration tokens while MACaQ saturates at ~2×10⁶ tokens — plausible given the baseline curves, but it is a modelled claim presented in the abstract as a measured one.
3. **The closest competitor is cited but never compared.** Reference 27 ("Block rotation is all you need for MXFP4 quantization", Shao et al., 2025) is invoked to justify block-local rotations and then disappears from the experiments; DuQuant++ carries the entire block-rotation baseline burden. Similarly, NVFP4 — which is the format the diffusion experiment implicitly concedes is competitive — is never used as an LLM baseline even though NVFP4 checkpoints exist for the Qwen/Llama families. A reader cannot tell whether MACaQ's edge is over MXFP4 methods specifically or would survive against a 4.5-bit microscaling format.
4. **The block size is asserted, not ablated.** `B=32` is justified by "matching the MXFP4 block size" and by the outlier-smearing argument, but there is no sweep over B ∈ {16, 32, 64, 128}, even though B directly controls both the rotation's expressiveness and the `log2 B` overhead. Given that the whole cost argument is parameterized by B, this is the most conspicuous missing ablation.

**Reproducibility.** Middling. Every hyperparameter is stated (α=0.75, λ=1.0, B=32, offsets {+1,0,−1}, K=128/512, 2×10⁵/2×10⁶ tokens, calibration on WikiText-2-train), the algorithm is given in pseudocode, and the cost model is checkable. But there is no code link, no released checkpoints, no seed counts for the diffusion or 122B experiments, and the kernel work is the part least reproducible from prose. The appendix's E8M0-compliance footnote is good scientific practice, but it also means the baseline numbers in Table 1 are not the baselines' published numbers — anyone comparing against the DuQuant++ paper will see a discrepancy the authors flag but do not quantify.

**Significance.** Real but bounded. The streaming property (one layer in GPU memory at a time, 32.5GB to quantize 122B) is a genuine practical unlock for labs that cannot rent an 8×H200 node, and the ~150 s quantization time for an 8B model makes re-quantizing after every finetune cheap. The quality contribution is a 3–4% relative perplexity improvement over the previous best MXFP4 method on Llama-3-8B and near-parity elsewhere — useful, not transformative. The diffusion result is the most surprising and the least developed: one model, 800 prompts, no seeds, and a 0.19 dB PSNR margin that is confounded by the bit-width difference.

**Red flags.** (a) The abstract's three headline numbers — 2.3% overhead, 11× faster, 64 min/32.5GB — are each the best-case instance of a trend (largest model, extrapolated baseline, easiest hardware fit) rather than a typical case; a reader who deploys a 3B model will see ~6–8% overhead, not 2.3%. (b) "Model agnostic" is demonstrated on four transformer-based models and no convnet, SSM, or encoder-decoder; the layer-local design should generalize, but the claim outruns the evidence. (c) Both "no cross-layer compensation" and "MoE expert layers only" are real scope limits that flatter the method's comparison against DuQuant++ (which does propagate transforms) — the authors disclose both, which is to their credit, but the comparison is not like-for-like in engineering effort. (d) The 122B perplexity 5.14 vs 5.06 is a single run at 32 flips, i.e. a deliberately weak setting, presented as evidence that the method "works" at scale — it does, but it is also the setting where a 122B model has the most redundancy to absorb quantization error.

## Strengths

- **The cost argument is the paper's best idea and it is correct.** Deriving `C_online/C_matmul = 3/m` and then confirming it empirically (8% at 0.6B, 2.3% at 32B, <1% for the largest kernel case) is exactly how a systems-for-ML paper should argue: predict, then measure, then show the trend matches.
- **Layer locality is a genuine engineering win, not a slogan.** Being able to quantize a 122B model with 32.5GB of GPU memory and no per-architecture surgery is the kind of property that determines whether a method gets used. The streaming implementation (hooks for Hessians, then one layer at a time with the rest in host RAM) is described concretely enough to reimplement.
- **Flip-Refinement is well-motivated and cheap.** Restricting moves to adjacent E2M1 levels with frozen block exponents makes the gain exact and closed-form, kills the need for a Hessian inverse, and gives O(n) incremental gradient updates with an early-exit active set. Getting within 0.2 PPL of a 512-flip GPTQ-class result in ~90 s is a good trade.
- **Honest reporting in three places that most papers would hide**: the E8M0-compliance footnote admitting the baseline numbers differ from their published values; the 14B result where block refinement *loses* to scalar flips and the admission that exact block minimization "does not guarantee better model perplexity"; and the explicit statement that cross-layer compensation is impossible in this design.
- **The ablation is actually additive and interpretable** (8.28 → 6.87), with rotation contributing most (−0.44) and refinement (−0.21) roughly matching the exponent search (−0.19) at far lower cost — the kind of decomposition that tells a practitioner where to spend compute.
- **Breadth of validation for a single preprint**: six LLMs across three families, two perplexity sets, two generative benchmarks with 10-sample error bars, a diffusion model against an official vendor checkpoint, kernel microbenchmarks, end-to-end vLLM throughput, and an appendix extending the method to llama.cpp vector formats.

## Limitations

- **No cross-layer error compensation**, by construction. The authors concede a second pass with cross-layer statistics "could be added on top" but would give back the speed advantage. This caps the achievable quality: methods that propagate transforms can, in principle, trade engineering complexity for accuracy, and MACaQ cannot.
- **The quality advantage is model-dependent and small**: +0.23 PPL on Llama-3-8B, +0.13 on Qwen3-32B, +0.07 (a tie) on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B. No model shows a large gap, and none of the generative benchmarks shows a significant one.
- **No ablation on the block size B**, on the number of exponent offsets (three vs five), or on the interaction between α and the rotation — the three knobs most likely to matter for a new architecture.
- **Perplexity is measured mostly in-distribution** (calibrated on WikiText-2-train, tested on WikiText-2-test); C4 is the only OOD set, and there is no code, multilingual, long-context, or instruction-following evaluation for the Llama models at all.
- **MoE results quantize expert layers only**; embeddings and lm-head are excluded throughout. This is standard practice but means "W4A4" describes the linear stack, not the model.
- **Small-model overhead is non-trivial**: 8% at Qwen3-0.6B, and 6.4% in the N=4096/L=16384 kernel case. The paper is candid about this, but it excludes the sub-1B deployment regime that matters for edge inference — the very regime where FP4 would be most valuable.
- **The diffusion evidence is one model, one prompt suite, no seeds**, with a bit-width mismatch (4.25 vs 4.50) against the NVFP4 baseline and an absolute PSNR (15 dB) low enough that both quantized models are visibly far from BF16.
- **No released code or kernels**, despite the systems claims being the paper's strongest selling point.

## Relevance

直接命中 "Reliability under Extreme Performance Optimization"：这篇论文的核心正是 W4A4 FP4 量化把数值误差注入推理栈的方式与代价——它给出了误差来源的分解（缩放 / 块旋转 / 块指数 / 逐码细化各自的 PPL 贡献），并诚实地展示了"困惑度赢了、生成任务只是打平"这一精度-可靠性错位。对关心低精度数值、量化后模型行为漂移、以及 FP4 部署可靠性边界的工作，它提供了可复用的实验骨架（分层消融 + 生成式基准 + 系统开销三方对照）。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. **Why does the perplexity gain not transfer to MATH-500 and IFEval?** Is it because perplexity is dominated by high-frequency token statistics that the Hadamard rotation fixes, while reasoning depends on a small number of high-magnitude activation channels that block-local rotation at B=32 cannot protect? A per-layer error-vs-layer-depth analysis on a reasoning benchmark would be the natural follow-up.
2. **What does the block size sweep look like?** B=16 would align with NVFP4's group size and might beat B=32 on quality at a higher `log2 B` overhead; B=64 would be cheaper but smear outliers across blocks. The `3/m` model predicts the cost side precisely, so the quality side is the open question.
3. **How much of the 11× is real?** A single measured DuQuant++ run at 2×10⁶ calibration tokens (rather than an extrapolation) would settle whether the time advantage is 7× or 11× — and would clarify whether MACaQ's plateau is a property of the method or of the calibration set.
4. **Does Flip-Refinement compose with GPTQ-style sequential assignment?** The paper positions it as a *replacement* for GPTQ starting from RTN; starting from a GPTQ assignment and refining might dominate both, at the cost of the speed claim. The appendix's vector-quantization extension suggests the refinement machinery is more general than the paper's framing admits.
5. **What happens under distribution shift at inference?** The calibration Hessian comes from WikiText-2-train; if the deployed prompt distribution has heavier-tailed activations, does the fixed per-channel scale `t` (chosen once, offline) become the failure point? This is the reliability question the paper does not ask.
6. **Can the layer-local property be turned into a fault-detection tool?** Since each layer's quantization error is independently measurable via its own Hessian objective, per-layer reconstruction error could serve as a cheap canary for numerical degradation in a deployed FP4 stack — an angle the paper's framing invites but does not pursue.
