> **一句话总结：** 作者把 on-policy distillation 拆成一个"教师请求 → 学生参数运动 → 前缀分布改变"的反馈环，用切核（tangent kernel）把每一步更新分解为**可及性 A**（学生能否表达该请求）与**一致性 C**（跨前缀梯度是否同向），解释了"更强教师反而迁移更少"（7B 健康/停滞两组的 C 差 2.75–3.24 倍而梯度范数相近）和"训练 loss 降、准确率崩"（弱 Qwen 1.5B 先被路由到不可靠长推理、再在低置信监督上做局部拟合，answer 分支概率 0.74→0.19→0.07）；并提出先用离线蒸馏"备课"，在 AIME 2024/25 上把 OPD 相对离线对照的 pass@32 从 0.433 提到 0.600（Qwen 7B）。

> **TL;DR:** A mechanistic study of on-policy distillation (OPD) that separates the feedback loop into *conversion* (teacher request field → parameter motion, measured as pressure × accessibility × coherence via the student's tangent kernel) and *broadcast* (parameter motion → predictions at unseen prefixes → shifted rollout distribution, decomposed exactly into routing and drift by two-way cross-scoring). On AIME the decomposition explains why DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B learns little from a larger DeepSeek-14B teacher while gaining from the same-size RL-trained AceReason-Nemotron-7B (healthy runs have 2.75–3.24× higher gradient coherence and 2.27–2.62× higher collective gain Ξ at comparable gradient norms), and why Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct degrades under JustRL-1.5B despite loss falling 0.579→0.073 (routing +0.474 dominates early, then drift fits the teacher's low-confidence predictions on self-generated chaotic prefixes). The fix — offline distillation on teacher solutions before OPD — barely moves immediate accuracy (MATH-500 pass@1 45.9%→45.9% with one preparation pass) yet lifts the 100-step OPD endpoint to 56.2% (4 passes), and on pooled AIME 2024/2025 gives OPD-vs-offline-control pass@32 of 0.600 vs 0.433 (Qwen 7B), 0.183 vs 0.083 (Qwen 1.5B), 0.350 vs 0.233 (Llama 8B). Read it if you work on OPD/RLVR failure modes or want cheap early-training diagnostics; the outcome comparisons are single-seed and the 1.5B 2025 AIME interval crosses zero.

## 核心贡献

- **把 OPD 形式化为一个显式反馈环**：rollout 分布 Πt →训练前缀分布 µt →请求场 Γt = µt·Bt →切映射 Φt →参数步 ∆θt →策略变化 →Πt+1，并给出局部响应律 ∆pt(a|x) = η·Σs µt(s)[A_pt(x)K_t(x,s)B_t(s)]_a + O(η²)，其中 K_t(x,s)=Φt(x)Φt(s)⊤ 是前缀间切核。核心含义：**没被训练到的前缀 x 也会因为共享参数而改变预测**。
- **把"请求能否变成有效参数运动"分解为三个可测量**：压力 P = E‖B‖²、可及性 A（请求在学生切空间中的 Rayleigh 系数，按请求能量加权）、一致性 C = ‖E g‖²/E‖g‖²（跨前缀梯度是同向叠加还是相互抵消），并证明集合转换增益 Ξ = ‖u‖²/P = A·C。这解释了"梯度范数不小但学不动"。
- **解释"更强教师迁移更少"这一经验困惑**：在 7B，健康（同尺寸 RL 教师 AceReason-Nemotron-7B）与停滞（更大 DeepSeek-14B 教师）两组梯度范数相近，但健康组 C 高 2.75–3.24 倍、Ξ 高 2.27–2.62 倍；停滞组是靠更高的压力（P₀ 0.521/0.599 vs 0.192/0.151）掩盖了更新力的低一致性。
- **给出"训练 loss 降但准确率崩"的机制**：把局部读数变化精确分解为 routing（前缀访问概率变化）与 drift（固定前缀上的预测变化），并用对称双向 cross-scoring 估计（两项严格相加等于总变化）。弱 Qwen 1.5B 前 20 步 loss 从 0.58 升到 0.85（routing +0.455 压过 drift −0.187），之后转为在低质量、高熵前缀上做 drift 主导的局部拟合——loss 降到 0.073，但准确率下降。
- **给出非局部（终端正确率）的判据**：把 rollout 级读数写成 continuation-advantage 加权形式 JY(T)−JY(t*) = (1/τ)∫Σ d_t(x)[πt⊙(Q−V)](x)⊤K_t(x,s)Γ_t(s)dt，说明"教师偏好的下一个 token"若学生无法完成后续推理则无益。用 86 个固定 answer/continue 决策前缀做强制分支干预验证：第 100 步 answer 分支仍有 0.34 正确率，continue 分支只剩 0.03，而教师自己的 continue 分支是 0.58——差距在"执行"而非"决策"。
- **提出并验证"先离线备课再 OPD"**：先用教师在 OpenMathInstruct-2 上的解做离线蒸馏（512 步 × 256 序列），再做 OPD。备课本身几乎不提升当下准确率（1 pass：45.9%→45.9%），却显著提升后续 OPD 的收益（4 pass：47.1%→56.2%），说明存在"潜在就绪度"这一与即时准确率解耦的变量。

## Key Contributions

- **An explicit OPD feedback-loop formalism** with a closed-form local response law: a gradient step changes the logits at *every* prefix through the tangent kernel K_t(x,s)=Φt(x)Φt(s)⊤, not only at the supervised ones. This makes "supervision at s changes behaviour at x" a first-class, computable object.
- **A three-factor decomposition of conversion**, Ξ = A·C with pressure P = E‖B‖²: accessibility A averages the self-kernel Rayleigh coefficient B⊤K(s,s)B/‖B‖² weighted by request energy; coherence C = ‖E g‖²/E‖g‖² measures whether per-prefix gradients reinforce or cancel. The authors show that gradient norm and teacher benchmark score are both insufficient statistics for transfer.
- **An exact routing/drift decomposition of any prefix-level readout** (loss, entropy, "wait"-token log-odds), J̇Y = RY + DY with RY = Cov_νt(Yt, λt) where λt(x)=Σ_{j<h(x)} ζt(xj,aj) is the summed log-probability velocity of a prefix's ancestors, plus the induced flow µb(s) ∝ µa(s)e^{Λa:b(s)} on the training-prefix distribution.
- **A continuation-value law for terminal outcomes** (Eq. 9), which weights policy motion by the student's own Q−V advantage — i.e. the formal statement of "matching the teacher locally can still lower accuracy if the student cannot finish what it starts".
- **A frozen-request spectral probe** that identifies kernel adaptation separately from request change: freeze B₀, the prefix bank and token coordinates, reapply B₀ at every checkpoint, and decompose a_t(s)=Σ_j λ_{j,t}(s)|v_{j,t}(s)⊤b₀(s)|² over the self-kernel's eigenpairs.
- **An interpretability layer on the request field**: 36,416 (1.5B) and 38,168 (7B) annotated positions, showing RL-sibling teachers concentrate their largest requests on sentence openings and control moves (planning-opening enrichment 3.59× vs 0.30× at 1.5B; 4.13× vs 1.06× at 7B) while larger distilled teachers stay in sentence interiors on technical content.
- **An empirical demonstration that offline preparation is a precondition, not a substitute**: at matched optimizer steps and sequence presentations, OPD after preparation beats continued offline distillation on pooled AIME pass@k up to k=32 and on per-problem reliability, across Qwen 1.5B, Qwen 7B and Llama 8B.

## Methodology

**Setup and notation.** A prefix s is a prompt plus partial continuation; the student p_θ has logits z_θ(s) and next-token distribution softmax(z_θ(s)). A fixed teacher q supplies targets. The *model policy* p_t is what the loss acts on; the *rollout policy* π_t = G[p_t] is what generates samples (temperature, EOS conventions absorbed into G). Sampling Q prompts b∼β and continuing under π_t gives the rollout population Π_t; the training source µ_t = M(Π_t) is the induced prefix distribution. For reverse KL the per-prefix loss is c_t(s)=D_KL(p_t(·|s)‖q(·|s)); with ℓ_t(s,a)=log p_t(a|s)/q(a|s) and ℓ̄_t(s)=E_{a∼p_t}ℓ_t, the negative logit-gradient is the **request field** B_t(s,a)=p_t(a|s)[ℓ̄_t(s)−ℓ_t(s,a)], and Γ_t = µ_t B_t is the **request population**. The tangent map Φ_t(s)=∇_θ z_{θ_t}(s) pulls requests into parameter space: g_t(s)=Φ_t(s)⊤B_t(s), u_t=E_{s∼µ_t}[g_t(s)].

**Response law (Sec. 3.1).** For ∆θ_t = ηu_t, the change at any receiver prefix x is ∆p_t(a|x)=η Σ_s µ_t(s)[A_{p_t}(x)K_t(x,s)B_t(s)]_a + O(η²), with K_t(x,s)=Φ_t(x)Φ_t(s)⊤ and A_{p_t}(x)=I−1p_t(·|x)⊤ the logit→log-probability map. Because every future prefix is a receiver, an update both changes local predictions and reshapes which prefixes are visited (and hence supervised) later.

**Conversion (Sec. 3.2).** With P_t=E_ν‖B_t‖², the collective gain along the request field is Ξ_t[B]=⟨B,ż⟩_ν/P_t=‖u‖²/P_t, which factors as Ξ = A·C: A is the request-energy-weighted average self-kernel Rayleigh coefficient (how much parameter motion a unit request buys at a prefix), C is the ratio of squared mean gradient norm to mean squared gradient norm (whether cross-prefix updates align). **Kernel adaptation** is isolated by freezing B₀ and the prefix bank and re-measuring a_t(s)=b₀⊤K_t(s,s)b₀=Σ_j λ_{j,t}(s)|v_{j,t}(s)⊤b₀(s)|²; both eigenvalue amplification and eigenvector rotation toward b₀ raise responsiveness. **Request-field interpretability** uses an Opus 5 annotator over sentence-level reasoning roles (control family: reframe, check-conclude, doubt-flag, check-initiate, planning-opening, recap, repair, commit; technical family: derivation, recall) plus position and token-kind classes, and reports enrichment E_q(c)=Pr(c|top-1% requests)/Pr(c) with bootstrap intervals resampling annotated rollouts.

**Broadcast (Sec. 3.3).** For a prefix-level readout Y_t(x) with J_Y=E_ν Y, the exact decomposition is J̇_Y=Σ_x ν̇_t(x)Y_t(x)+E_ν Ẏ_t(x) = R_Y (routing) + D_Y (drift). At temperature τ, the token log-probability velocity is ζ_t(x,a)=(e_a−π_t(·|x))⊤v_t(x)/τ with v_t(x)=Σ_s K_t(x,s)Γ_t(s); summing over a prefix's ancestors gives λ_t(x), so R_Y=Cov_νt(Y_t,λ_t). Empirically these are estimated by two-way cross-scoring: with M_ij=E_{x∼ν_i}Y_j(x) (readout of learner j on the frozen bank from step i), R̂=½[(M_ba−M_aa)+(M_bb−M_ab)] and D̂=½[(M_ab−M_aa)+(M_bb−M_ba)], which sum exactly to M_bb−M_aa; windows of 20 update steps are used because per-step estimation is too expensive. For terminal correctness the relevant object is Eq. (9), where policy motion is weighted by the student's continuation advantage Q−V.

**Preparation (Sec. 3.4).** Three tokenizer-sharing pairs: Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct/JustRL-1.5B, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct/AceReason-Nemotron-7B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct/Llama-3.1-Nemotron-Nano-8B-v1. Stage 1: the teacher generates solutions to OpenMathInstruct-2 (temperature 1, 8,192-token cap), the student is trained with token-level forward KL on the teacher's recorded top-16 support for 512 steps × 256 sequences (131,072 presentations, four corpus sweeps; AdamW, lr 1e-6, clip 1.0). Stage 2: OPD on DAPO-Math-17k blocks (6,400 rows, 6,235–6,318 unique problems), 64 prompts × 4 responses = 256 rollouts per step, temperature 1, 8,192-token cap for Qwen / 14,336 for Llama; the offline-control arm instead consumes four teacher solutions per prompt row (25,600 sequences per block) with the same forward-KL objective, matching prompt blocks, sequence counts and optimizer steps (200/100/200 OPD steps for 1.5B/7B/Llama, 51,200/25,600/51,200 presentations).

**Evaluation.** AIME 2024 + 2025 pooled (60 problems), 32 responses per problem from batch a, vLLM, temperature 0.6, top-p 0.95, 16,384 new tokens, lenient boxed-answer extraction; unbiased pass@k estimator; coverage (problems solved at least once) and the empirical survival curve of per-problem solve rates; paired problem-bootstrap 95% intervals computed separately per year by exact conditional convolution. MATH-500 dose study: first 250 problems, 4 samples, strict pass@1, 2,000 bootstrap resamples. Everything runs on GH200 nodes; the full single-seed set is ≈4,000 GPU-hours.

## Results & Highlights

- **Coherence, not gradient magnitude, separates healthy from stalled at 7B.** In Table 2 the healthy pairing has coherence C₀ = 0.081 (control) / 0.055 (technical) versus 0.025 / 0.020 for the stalled one — a 2.75–3.24× gap — while the resulting gradient norms are *comparable or larger* for the stalled run (‖u₀‖ = 43.9 / 39.3 vs 43.1 / 29.7) because its request pressure is 2.7–4.0× higher (P₀ = 0.521 / 0.599 vs 0.192 / 0.151). Collective gain Ξ₀ is 9.70 / 5.85 vs 3.70 / 2.58, i.e. 2.27–2.62× higher for the healthy run.
- **The stalled runs never deplete their request energy.** At the endpoint the healthy runs retain only 0.5–1.5% of initial request energy (P_t/P₀ = 0.005–0.015) while the stalled runs retain 60.2–79.0%, and accessibility to the *frozen* initial requests rises in every healthy class (1.232–5.399×) but falls in every stalled class (0.789–0.889×). This is the cleanest single diagnostic in the paper: a run whose teacher-student disagreement refuses to shrink is not "still learning", it is not converting.
- **Kernel adaptation differs qualitatively by scale.** At 1.5B the healthy run *re-orients*: leading-subspace overlap drops to 0.77 by step 50 with leading eigenvalue strength near its initial value, the frozen request gains a median +5.5 pp of top-three projection and beats the other teacher's field by 1.79 pp; by step 200 leading eigenvalue strength reaches 2.31× and median request-direction sensitivity 2.08× baseline, whereas the stalled run contracts to 0.67× and 0.83×. At 7B adaptation is instead *amplification*: leading strength 1.25× at overlap 0.96, versus 0.74× for the stalled run.
- **Request fields are semantically different, not just different in norm.** Among each teacher's strongest 1% of requests, planning-sentence openings are enriched 3.59× under JustRL-1.5B but 0.30× under DeepSeek-7B (and 4.13× vs 1.06× at 7B); wait-family tokens are enriched 4.50× vs 1.23× at 7B; the sibling teacher's share of sentence openings exceeds the larger teacher's by 19.2 [13.1, 27.7] pp at 1.5B and 27.6 [18.8, 37.0] pp at 7B. Derivation enrichment is essentially identical (0.84× vs 0.83×), so the difference is about *where in the reasoning* supervision lands, not about how much math the teacher does.
- **The weak-learner failure is routing-then-fitting, and both halves are measurable.** Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct under JustRL-1.5B: from step 1 to 20 loss rises 0.58→0.85 with routing +0.455 outweighing drift −0.187; over the full run (Table 6) loss goes 0.579→0.073 with routing +0.474 and drift −0.980, while conditional entropy goes 0.650→3.988 (routing +3.666). On a fixed batch-41 bank the same model's loss still falls 0.79→0.06 and entropy 4.99→3.99 — i.e. it does learn, it learns to reproduce the teacher's *uncertain* predictions on its own chaotic prefixes.
- **The terminal-accuracy collapse is an execution failure, not a decision failure.** On 86 fixed answer-decision prefixes the mean log-odds of answering falls from +2.4 to −7.5 by update 100 (the answer branch's probability 0.74→0.19→0.07 at steps 0/20/100). The forced-branch probe shows why that is bad: at update 100 the answer branch still succeeds 0.34 of the time but the continue branch only 0.03 (natural continuations 0.06), and the two-branch value change is −0.24 with the probability term −0.15 and the value term −0.09 — probability moves toward the *less* successful branch. The teacher, on the same prefixes, has a continue branch that succeeds 0.58 and natural continuations 0.60, so the branch itself is fine; the student cannot execute it.
- **Prefixes genuinely get harder to finish.** With a fixed reference continuer (the initial student) finishing prefixes sampled from trained checkpoints, success changes by +2.1 / −1.3 / −8.1 pp at depths 256 / 512 / 1,024 tokens after 50 steps, and by −5.9 / −6.1 / −8.5 pp after 200 steps — the degradation spreads from deep prefixes toward shallow ones as training proceeds.
- **The strong learner shows the mirror image.** DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B's loss falls 0.263→0.015 almost entirely by drift (−0.214) rather than routing (−0.033), and its "wait"-initiation log-odds move in the *teacher's* direction on both sides of the discrepancy: −1.82 nats where it over-used "wait", +1.92 nats where it under-used it, with the Bernoulli discrepancy c_W falling 0.0789→0.0032 and 0.1915→0.0149.
- **Preparation is a latent enabler, not an accuracy boost.** MATH-500 strict pass@1 (Table 14): at 1.5B, 1/2/4 preparation passes give cold-start 0.459/0.457/0.471 and post-OPD 0.459/0.530/0.562; at 7B, 0.683/0.693/0.711 → 0.823/0.828/0.853. One pass buys *nothing* immediately (45.9%→45.9%) but still ends +0.0 pp, whereas four passes buy +9.1 pp — the value of preparation is only visible through the subsequent OPD phase.
- **OPD beats matched offline distillation on pooled AIME (Table 12).** Qwen 7B: pass@1 0.2693 vs 0.1906, pass@32 0.6000 vs 0.4333, coverage 36/60 vs 26/60, ≥half-solved 15 vs 10, all-32-solved 5 vs 1. Qwen 1.5B: 0.0281 vs 0.0078, 0.1833 vs 0.0833, coverage 11 vs 5. Llama 8B: 0.0693 vs 0.0349, 0.3500 vs 0.2333, coverage 21 vs 14.
- **The paired intervals are honest but not uniformly significant (Table 13).** Qwen 7B gains +0.086 [+0.047, +0.130] pass@1 and +0.133 pass@32 on AIME 2024, +0.071 [+0.033, +0.114] / +0.200 on 2025. Llama 8B: +0.040 [+0.005, +0.082] on 2024 but +0.029 [−0.006, +0.069] on 2025. Qwen 1.5B: +0.032 [+0.005, +0.065] on 2024, but +0.008 [−0.004, +0.027] and +0.000 [−0.100, +0.100] on 2025 — one of the six headline comparisons is null.
- **Reliability, not just coverage, improves.** On problems solved by both arms, mean observed success rates are 0.281 vs 0.104 (Qwen 1.5B, 3 problems), 0.599 vs 0.455 (Qwen 7B, 25 problems), 0.264 vs 0.159 (Llama 8B, 13 problems); OPD gains coverage on 8, 11 and 8 problems and loses it on 2, 1 and 1. So the pass@k gain is not just "more lottery tickets" — the per-problem solve rate itself moves.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The mathematical ingredients are standard: the response law is a first-order NTK Taylor expansion, Ξ=A·C is a bias-variance-style split of a mean-gradient norm, and Eq. (9) is a policy-gradient/advantage identity with the kernel substituted in. What is genuinely new is the *packaging into a testable loop* and the two identification tricks — the frozen-request spectral probe (freeze B₀ and the prefix bank, vary only the kernel) and the symmetric two-way cross-scoring estimator for routing vs drift, which is exact by construction rather than approximate. That is a real methodological contribution: it converts vague talk about "distribution shift in OPD" into three numbers (P, A, C) that can be computed in the first 50 steps.

**The healthy/stalled comparison is not a clean manipulation.** The two teachers differ in *recipe*, not just size: the "sibling" teachers (JustRL-1.5B, AceReason-Nemotron-7B) are RL-trained, the "larger" ones (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B/14B) are distilled. So "a stronger teacher transfers less" is really "an RL-trained same-scale teacher transfers better than a larger distilled teacher", and the paper's own request-field analysis says the mechanism is the *semantic placement* of supervision (sentence openings and control moves vs sentence interiors and digits) — which is a property of the RL recipe, not of teacher strength. The title-level framing ("strong teacher can transfer little") is thus slightly off; the finding is more interesting than the framing: what matters is *what kind of behaviour* the teacher asks for, not how good it is.

**Estimator sample sizes are small relative to the claims.** Table 2 uses n=80 positions per teacher and per semantic class, and Table 3 tracks "the same positions" at the endpoint; the kernel measurements "use sampled positions" because full per-position logit-gradient computation is expensive. The decomposition is exact for a population but these are 80-site estimates of a quantity (coherence) that is a ratio of squared norms and therefore biased upward in finite samples — the authors correct for this (Ĉ_corr) but the correction is only unbiased before truncation, and they note coherence estimates are "rounded, especially at healthy endpoints" where C_t/C₀ falls to 0.012. A 0.012 ratio from 80 samples is close to the noise floor.

**The interpretability layer has no human agreement check.** Semantic labels come from Opus 5 with a hand-iterated rubric, over 36k–38k positions, and the enrichment claims (3.59× vs 0.30×) rest entirely on those labels. Intervals are bootstrap over *rollouts*, which captures sampling variability of positions but not annotator error. The rubric itself concedes boundary fuzziness ("a sentence the rules do not settle ... takes the best available existing label, uncertain: true") but no uncertain-rate or human-vs-model agreement is reported. Since the central causal story is "the healthy teacher asks for control moves at sentence starts", this is the weakest evidential link in the chain.

**The offline-control arm is not a clean control.** Both arms are matched on prompt blocks, sequence counts and optimizer steps (Table 11) — good — but the offline arm uses *forward* KL on *teacher-generated* solutions while the OPD arm uses *reverse* KL on *student* rollouts. So the comparison confounds on-policy vs off-policy data with forward vs reverse KL. A reviewer should want the missing cell: forward KL on student rollouts, or reverse KL on teacher solutions. Without it, "OPD beats offline distillation" is really "reverse-KL-on-policy beats forward-KL-off-policy at matched steps", and the mechanism the paper argues for (continuation readiness on teacher prefixes) could in principle be obtained by more offline steps — which the dose study partly shows (4 passes → 47.1% cold start), but never at equal *total* compute to the OPD arm.

**Checkpoint selection biases the 7B dose study.** Table 14's 7B OPD column uses "the best evaluated checkpoint within 100 steps", and the selected step differs by dose (30, 50, 90 for 1, 2, 4 passes). That is a max-over-checkpoints selection applied unevenly across arms, and the paper says the intervals "do not adjust for checkpoint selection". The 1.5B column fixes step 100, so the 1.5B dose trend (+0.0, +7.3, +9.1 pp) is the trustworthy one; the 7B numbers (0.683→0.823 etc.) are optimistic by an unquantified amount.

**Headline AIME numbers are small-count.** Qwen 1.5B's pass@1 of 0.0281 is 1.7 problems out of 60; its coverage gain is 11 vs 5 problems; the "mean observed success rate 0.281 vs 0.104" is computed over **3 problems**. The abstract's "substantial accuracy gains after sufficient offline preparation" is carried by the 7B and Llama rows, and by the 1.5B 2024 row; the 1.5B 2025 row is null. The paper does report this, but the abstract does not distinguish.

**Single seed, acknowledged but not neutralized.** The Q&A argues that the mechanism measurements are exact within-run identities and the outcome comparisons are paired across three pairs and two scales. That is a reasonable defence for the *mechanism* claims. It is weaker for the *outcome* claims: Table 13's intervals quantify problem resampling, not training-trajectory variance, and OPD runs are known to be high-variance. With ~4,000 GPU-hours for one seed, the field's standard is low, but a single seed per arm means the +0.086 vs +0.071 vs +0.008 pattern across students could easily be trajectory luck.

**Missing comparisons that would sharpen the practical claim.** The paper cites Zhou et al. (2026) "Less is more: Early stopping rollout for on-policy distillation" but never compares against it, nor against cheaper fixes for the same failure (rollout-length caps, entropy regularization, filtering high-entropy prefixes, teacher-confidence gating). If a rollout-length cap recovers most of what 512 steps of offline preparation buys, the practical recommendation changes completely. Likewise there is no ablation isolating *which* part of preparation matters (teacher-solution data vs forward KL vs the extra 131k presentations).

**Scope.** Math reasoning only, 1.5B–8B, three student-teacher pairs, all tokenizer-sharing. No code, no agentic tool use, no models above 8B, no cross-tokenizer distillation. The authors state this limitation plainly, which is to their credit, but it means the "preparation is a precondition for OPD" claim is untested exactly where OPD is most used (large-scale reasoning post-training).

**One presentational red flag.** Figure 2's initial/end AIME accuracies (the headline "strong teacher transfers little" evidence) are never tabulated in the text or appendix; only the figure carries them, and the evaluation there uses 30 AIME 2024 problems with 16 responses at 1.5B and **4** responses at 7B — a very noisy endpoint for the claim that the larger teacher "transfers little throughout training".

## Strengths

- **The decomposition is genuinely diagnostic, not decorative.** Ξ=A·C turns "why is this run not learning" into three separately measurable quantities, and the paper shows they dissociate: at 7B coherence is the culprit, at 1.5B it is evolving accessibility, and in both cases gradient norm is uninformative. That is the kind of result that changes how practitioners monitor a run.
- **The frozen-request probe is a clean identification strategy.** Freezing B₀, the prefix bank and the token coordinates so that only the kernel varies is exactly the right way to separate "the teacher stopped asking" from "the student stopped listening", and the paired comparison against the *other* teacher's field (the +1.79 pp preferential-reorientation test) rules out generic kernel drift.
- **The routing/drift estimator is exact and cheap.** The symmetric two-way cross-scoring (Eq. 8) sums exactly to the observed change and needs only frozen rollout banks plus checkpoints — no extra training, no assumptions. The empirical finding it produces (loss rises because routing dominates early, then falls because drift fits unreliable prefixes) is a clean mechanistic account of a failure mode that is usually described only qualitatively.
- **The answer-boundary intervention is the strongest causal evidence in the paper.** Forcing answer vs continue at 86 fixed prefixes and measuring downstream correctness separates "the student chose wrong" from "the student cannot execute the right choice" — and the answer is unambiguous: at update 100 the continue branch is 0.03 correct for the student and 0.58 for the teacher. This is a template other OPD/RLVR failure analyses should copy.
- **The practical recommendation is counter-intuitive and well-supported.** "Offline preparation that does not improve accuracy can still be worth 9–10 points later" is a genuinely useful and non-obvious claim, and the dose study makes it quantitative rather than anecdotal.
- **The paper is unusually candid.** It reports a null result (1.5B, AIME 2025), discloses single-seed training and its cost, includes a Q&A appendix that pre-empts the obvious objections, and states the math-only/≤8B scope in the conclusion rather than burying it.

## Limitations

- **Single seed per training run**, with outcome intervals that quantify problem resampling but not trajectory variance; the 1.5B AIME 2025 comparison is null (+0.008 [−0.004, +0.027]).
- **Healthy/stalled is confounded with teacher recipe** (RL-trained vs distilled), so the "stronger teacher transfers less" framing is not what the experiment manipulates.
- **Small estimator samples**: n=80 positions per teacher/class for the conversion estimates; kernel measurements on sampled positions; endpoint coherence ratios as low as 0.012 are near the noise floor.
- **LLM-annotated request semantics without human agreement statistics**, despite carrying the central "control vs technical" causal claim.
- **Offline control confounds on-policy vs off-policy with forward vs reverse KL**, and no arm matches *total* compute (preparation + OPD) against a longer offline run.
- **7B dose study uses best-checkpoint-within-100-steps selection that differs by dose**, biasing the higher-dose rows upward.
- **Very small AIME counts** (60 problems; 1.5B pass@1 ≈ 1.7 problems; the reliability comparison rests on 3 shared-solved problems for 1.5B).
- **No comparison to cheaper mitigations** (rollout-length caps, entropy regularization, prefix filtering, early-stopping rollout as in Zhou et al. 2026), so the cost-benefit of 512 preparation steps is unestablished.
- **No component ablation of preparation** (data source, divergence direction, number of steps are varied only jointly as "passes").
- **Scope**: math only, ≤8B, tokenizer-sharing pairs; no code, agentic, or large-scale setting.

## Relevance

正对 "RL Post-Training Dynamics & Failures" 这一条主线：它把 OPD 的两类典型病理——**"更强教师迁移更少"的停滞**和**"loss 降、准确率崩"的退化**——拆成可测量的 P/A/C 三个量与 routing/drift 两个通道，并给出可在训练前 50 步就读出的诊断信号（保留请求能量比例、Ξ 的演化），这类"早期预警 + 机制归因"正是该方向最缺的东西。对做 post-training 监控的人来说，最可直接搬走的两件工具是 frozen-request 谱探针和对称 cross-scoring 分解；最需要警惕的是它全部结论建立在单 seed、≤8B、纯数学的设定上。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the conversion diagnostic generalize? If you compute P, A, C on the first 50 steps of an unrelated OPD run (different teacher family, code rather than math, 30B+ student), does low C or contracting accessibility still predict a stalled run — and with what lead time?
2. What is the missing 2×2 in the preparation comparison: forward KL on student rollouts, and reverse KL on teacher solutions? If reverse-KL-off-policy captures most of the gain, the mechanism is the divergence direction rather than the on-policy data, which would substantially change the practical advice.
3. Is 512 steps of offline preparation the cheapest way to buy the same "continuation readiness"? A rollout-length cap, an entropy penalty on high-surprisal prefixes, or teacher-confidence gating might achieve the same routing stabilization at a fraction of the cost — none is tested.
4. How much of the 7B dose trend survives fixing the checkpoint (e.g. always step 100) instead of selecting the best within 100 steps per dose?
5. Can the continuation-advantage term in Eq. (9) be estimated cheaply enough to use as an online training signal — e.g. as a gate that suppresses updates whose favored branch has low Q−V — rather than only as a post-hoc explanation?
6. Does the "control vs technical" request-field distinction hold when the teacher is not RL-trained but simply prompted to verbalize planning moves? That would separate "RL changes what the teacher asks for" from "the request field is a property of the trace style".
