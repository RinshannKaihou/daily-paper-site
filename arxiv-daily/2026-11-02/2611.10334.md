> **一句话总结：** vLLM v0.29.0 的 LoRA shrink（`X·Aᵀ` 这个输出维度只有秩 r 的窄 GEMM）默认用 Split-K=8 并行归约，但部分和通过 FP32 `atomicAdd` 累加，加法顺序随 GPU 调度变化；作者用 Triton 写了一个"每 tile 整数完成计数器 + 最后完成的 CTA 按固定分区顺序求和"的确定性 Split-K=8，把同一输入重放下的 FP32 输出差异从 97.61%（107,136/109,760）降到 0，端到端把重复贪心解码的 token 序列差异从 GSM8K 17.30% / MBPP 14.96% 降到 0，代价只是相对原子版慢 1.02–2.62%，同时仍比不拆分的 Split-K=1 快 16.8–21.1%（均值 19.2%）。

> **TL;DR:** In vLLM v0.29.0 the LoRA "shrink" kernel splits the reduction dimension 8 ways and merges partials with FP32 atomic additions, so the accumulation order — and therefore the bits — depend on GPU scheduling. Replaying 27,440 captured shrink operations five times each (109,760 comparisons), Atomic Split-K=8 returns different FP32 outputs in 97.61% of comparisons while Split-K=1 and the authors' deterministic Split-K=8 return zero; 22.31% of individual FP32 elements differ, though the mean absolute difference is only 1.08e-8. That last-bit noise is enough to flip BF16 rounding: on GSM8K, repeated greedy decoding changes the token sequence in 467/2,700 comparisons (17.30%) and correctness in 84, versus 0 and 0 for both deterministic kernels; MBPP gives 404/2,700 (14.96%) and 53. On Civil Comments' Jev-style `noul` toxicity decision, probabilities move in 81.47% of comparisons (mean |Δ| = 0.00143, max 0.05672) but only 7 decisions flip, all on comments whose probability is exactly 0.5. The causal chain is traced to one coordinate: in GSM8K prompt 813, a layer-8 QKV shrink value differs by 1.19e-7 across a BF16 rounding midpoint, changing token 36 from "There" to "Each" and the answer from 7 to 8. Cost: 19.2% average latency win over unsplit reduction for 1.69% over the atomic version — a genuinely cheap determinism fix, though it does *not* buy batch invariance (cross-batch outputs still differ in 39.06% of comparisons).

## 核心贡献

- **把 LoRA shrink 的不可复现性隔离出来并量化。** LoRA shrink 是输出维度只有 r（16/32）的窄矩阵乘，`N_tiles = ⌈M/BM⌉·⌈r/BN⌉` 在 decode 阶段（M 很小）几乎榨不出并行度，所以 vLLM 用 Split-K=8 沿归约维切分。问题在于部分和用 FP32 原子加合并——原子性只保证不覆盖，不保证顺序，而浮点加法不满足结合律。作者在 27,440 个捕获的 shrink 操作上各重放 5 次，Atomic Split-K=8 有 97.61%（107,136/109,760）的重复比较给出不同 FP32 输出，Split-K=1 与确定性 Split-K=8 均为 0；跨 prefill 与首个 decode step，22.31% 的 FP32 元素不同，但平均绝对差只有 1.08e-8。
- **证明这点噪声能一路传到答案。** 端到端重复贪心解码（3 个模型 × rank 16/32 × 每数据集 50 个 prompt × 10 次）中，原子版在 GSM8K 上改变 17.30%（467/2700）的 token 序列、84 次正确性翻转（45 次对→错，39 次错→对），MBPP 上 14.96%（404/2700）、53 次翻转；两个确定性实现在同样配置下均为 0。这填补了"kernel 级数值差异 → 任务正确性"之间通常缺失的因果环节。
- **单坐标因果追踪（全文最有说服力的部分）。** 对 GSM8K prompt 813，作者定位到 layer-8 QKV shrink 在 prefill 时的一个 FP32 坐标：两次执行的值 −0.3857421278953552 与 −0.3857422471046448 相差 1.19e-7，却恰好跨在 BF16 舍入中点 −0.3857421875 两侧，转成 BF16 后相差 1.953e-3。只改这一个坐标，就改变了 value 投影的 32 个 BF16 元素（V cache），进而在位置 36 把 token 从 "There" 翻成 "Each"，最终答案 7（对）→ 8（错），且在 Split-K=1 续跑下可重复。另有 KV-cache 替换实验：两份 cache 下 "There"/"Each" 的 logit 从 21.75/21.75（并列）变成 21.625/21.75，直接复现了翻转。
- **确定性 Split-K=8 kernel：用整数计数器替代浮点原子加。** 八个 CTA 各自算 FP32 部分和写入 scratch buffer，`gpu_memory_fence()` + block barrier 保证可见，再用 GPU-scope acquire-release 整数原子自增每 tile 计数器；读到旧值 7 的那个 CTA 是最后一个，按分区 0..7 的固定顺序做 FP32 求和并乘 scale 写出。整数原子只用于同步、不参与累加，因此并行度保留而顺序固定，也不需要额外启动一个 reduction kernel（作者试过"独立 pairwise 归约树"方案，因小 decode 负载的额外 launch 开销而放弃）。
- **性能账算得清楚。** 12 个 model-dataset-rank 配置上，确定性 Split-K=8 相对 Split-K=1 的端到端延迟加速 16.8–21.1%（均值 19.2%），相对 Atomic Split-K=8 只慢 1.02–2.62%（均值 1.69%）；batch=64 时吞吐 6,079/6,060 tok/s（rank 16/32）对 Split-K=1 的 5,274/5,257，高约 15.3%，仅比原子版低 1.5–1.7%。即"确定性"在这里几乎是免费的。
- **诚实的负结果：kernel 确定性 ≠ 系统确定性。** 固定 batch size 内两个确定性实现完全可重复（0/4608），但把 batch 16/32/64 的首次执行与 batch 1 对比，Split-K=1 仍有 39.58%、确定性 Split-K=8 仍有 39.06% 的输出差异——batch 变化会在推理栈其他算子处改变执行形状与数值行为。作者明确指出这是 kernel 级修复的边界。

## Key Contributions

- **Isolates and quantifies nondeterminism in the LoRA shrink operation.** The shrink GEMM `X·Aᵀ` has output width r ≪ H, so tile parallelism is scarce (`N_tiles = ⌈M/BM⌉·⌈r/BN⌉`, e.g. 8 tiles for M=256, r=16, BM=32, BN=16). Split-K=8 multiplies CTAs 8× (8 → 64 in their Figure 1) but merges partials with FP32 `atomicAdd`, whose order is schedule-dependent and whose associativity fails. Across 27,440 captured operations replayed 5×, 97.61% of atomic comparisons differ versus 0% for Split-K=1 and 0% for their deterministic kernel; 99.996% of prefill and 95.22% of first-decode comparisons contain at least one changed element, 22.31% of elements differ, mean |Δ| = 1.08e-8.
- **Connects kernel-level bits to task-level correctness.** Repeated greedy decoding over GSM8K and MBPP yields 17.30% / 14.96% changed token sequences and 84 / 53 correctness flips for the atomic kernel, and exactly zero for both deterministic kernels — across all six model–rank configurations.
- **A single-coordinate causal intervention.** For GSM8K prompt 813, one FP32 coordinate in the layer-8 QKV shrink straddles the BF16 midpoint −0.3857421875 (values 1.19e-7 apart, converting to −0.384765625 vs −0.38671875), alters 32 BF16 elements of the V cache, flips token 36 from "There" to "Each", and changes the answer from 7 to 8. A separate KV-cache substitution experiment reproduces the same flip by swapping caches at logits 21.75/21.75 → 21.625/21.75.
- **A deterministic Triton Split-K=8 kernel** that keeps eight-way parallelism, uses per-tile integer completion counters plus a GPU-scope acquire-release atomic to elect the last-finishing CTA, and has that CTA sum partials in fixed partition order — no second kernel launch, unlike the pairwise-reduction-tree variant they tried first.
- **A cost accounting that makes adoption plausible:** 19.2% mean latency speedup over unsplit reduction at 1.69% mean overhead over the nondeterministic atomic kernel; ~15.3% higher throughput than Split-K=1 at batch 64; a 3.47× shrink-latency win (14.16 → 4.08 µs) in the one-token decode microbenchmark where SM activity rises from 1.46% to 8.06%.
- **An explicit boundary on the claim:** fixed-batch repeatability does not imply batch invariance (39.06% cross-batch differences remain for the deterministic kernel), and determinism brings no accuracy gain (94.25 / 94.20 / 94.30 on Civil Comments).

## Methodology

**Problem setup.** A LoRA-adapted linear layer computes `Y = XWᵀ + s(XAᵀ)Bᵀ` with `A ∈ ℝ^{r×H}`, `B ∈ ℝ^{H_out×r}`. The *shrink* term `XAᵀ` is a narrow GEMM: output width r is 16 or 32 while H is 3,072. With tile size BM×BN the grid has `N_tiles = ⌈M/BM⌉⌈r/BN⌉` blocks; for M=256, r=16, BM=32, BN=16 that is 8 blocks — far below what 188 SMs need, and worse during decode where M is the number of active tokens. Split-K partitions H into S subsets, each CTA computing `Z_ij^(s) = Σ_{k∈K_s} X_ik A_jk`, then `Z_ij = Σ_s Z_ij^(s)`. This raises blocks from `N_tiles` to `S·N_tiles` (8 → 64 in the figure), at the cost of storing/combining partials.

**Why it breaks.** Atomic additions guarantee no lost updates but not order; since `fl(fl(a+b)+c) ≠ fl(a+fl(b+c))` (their example: a=10³⁰, b=−10³⁰, c=1 gives 1 vs 0), identical inputs can yield different partial sums. The default vLLM v0.29.0 policy selects Split-K=64 for M<128 and Split-K=8 for M≥128; enabling `VLLM_BATCH_INVARIANT` switches the path to Split-K=1 instead.

**Deterministic kernel (Algorithm 1).** An initialization kernel zeroes the output tensor and per-tile integer counters. Then, for every (tile, split) pair in parallel: compute the FP32 partial, write it to `scratch[t, s]`, issue `gpu_memory_fence()`, `block_barrier()`, then `atomic_increment(counter[t])`. The block that observes the previous value 7 is last; it sums `scratch[t, 0..7]` in increasing partition order in FP32, multiplies by `scale`, writes `output[t]`, and resets the counter. Crucially the atomic is an integer *coordination* primitive, never an accumulator, so floating-point order is fixed while CTA scheduling stays free. An earlier design that combined partials in a separate kernel with a fixed pairwise reduction tree was dropped because the extra launch dominated small decode workloads.

**Kernel selection.** They benchmark deterministic S ∈ {2,4,8,16}: S=8 is 48.1% faster than S=2, 12.6% faster than S=4, and only 2.0% slower than S=16, so S=8 is used both for the performance sweet spot and to match vLLM's existing eight-way configuration.

**Models and adapters.** Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct (dense Transformer), Gemma-4-E4B-it (local + global attention), NVIDIA-Nemotron-3-Nano-4B-BF16 (Mamba-2 hybrid) on GSM8K and MBPP, each with rank-16 and rank-32 adapters and α = 2r; plus Qwen3-0.6B with a rank-16 (α=32) adapter on Civil Comments. BF16 base and adapter weights, FP32 shrink accumulation, no quantization, thinking mode disabled for Gemma/Nemotron.

**Data.** GSM8K: 7,473 train / 1,319 test, 5% of train held out with a fixed seed (7,099 / 374); calculator annotations stripped, `####` replaced by `Final answer:`, last numeric answer compared by exact equality, unparseable answers counted wrong. MBPP full config: 374 train / 90 val / 500 test; prompts include the description, optional setup code and public assertions but not the reference solution; generated programs run against assertions in a network-disabled, resource-limited Docker container. Civil Comments: empty comments and cross-partition duplicates removed, fixed-seed class-proportional sampling to 20,000 / 2,000 / 1,000 (test = 920 non-toxic + 80 toxic), labels by thresholding dataset toxicity at 0.5, each comment asked as a `noul` question "Is this comment toxic?".

**Protocols.** (i) *Identical-input replay*: capture shrink inputs, adapter weights, scale and mapping from 10 prompts per dataset under Split-K=1 in eager mode (prefill + first decode step), replay each captured op under all three kernels, 5 executions each, compare executions 1–4 against execution 0. (ii) *Generation reproducibility*: 50 fixed-seed test prompts per dataset × 3 models × 2 ranks × 3 kernels, 10 sequential greedy generations each within one engine, 4,096-token cap, repetitions 1–9 vs 0 → 450 comparisons per configuration (2,700 per dataset per kernel); logits compared only at matching-prefix positions with identical selected tokens. (iii) *Performance*: 50 sequential requests × exactly 150 generated tokens, EOS disabled, five fresh-engine trials, CUDA graphs on, init/warmup excluded; batch-size study on Llama/GSM8K at batch 1/16/32/64 over the same 64 prompts. (iv) *Structured decisions*: 1,000 comments × 10 repetitions per kernel → 9,000 comparisons, measuring probability drift, label flips and accuracy.

**Environment.** Single NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell (188 SMs), vLLM v0.29.0, tensor parallelism 1, GPU memory utilization 0.85, max length 8,192 (GSM8K/MBPP) or 4,096 (Civil Comments), prefix caching / async scheduling / speculative decoding disabled, `VLLM_BATCH_INVARIANT` deliberately off to isolate the shrink reduction. GPU model, software versions, adapter hashes, prompt IDs and kernel dispatch are recorded. Spot checks on H100 and B200 found no variation under identical-input replay.

## Results & Highlights

- **Kernel-level replay (27,440 captured ops × 5 executions = 109,760 comparisons).** Atomic Split-K=8 differs in 107,136 comparisons (97.61%); Split-K=1 and Deterministic Split-K=8 differ in **0**. Element-level: 99.996% of prefill comparisons and 95.22% of first-decode comparisons contain ≥1 changed output element, 22.31% of all compared FP32 elements differ, mean absolute difference 1.08e-8 — i.e. the noise is real but confined to the last few mantissa bits.
- **GSM8K end-to-end (2,700 comparisons per kernel, 6 model–rank configs).** Atomic Split-K=8 changes the generated token sequence in 467 comparisons (**17.30%**) with **84 correctness flips** (45 correct→incorrect, 39 incorrect→correct); Split-K=1 and Deterministic Split-K=8 give **0 and 0**. Selected-token logits differ at 14.57% of matching-prefix positions.
- **MBPP end-to-end.** Atomic Split-K=8 changes outputs in 404/2,700 (**14.96%**) with **53** test-pass flips; both deterministic kernels 0. Logit differences at 18.30% of matching-prefix positions.
- **Civil Comments `noul` decisions (9,000 comparisons).** Probabilities change in 7,332 comparisons (**81.47%**), mean |Δ| = 0.00143, max = 0.05672; only **7 decision flips (0.078%)** across 2 distinct comments, and all seven occur where the initial probability is *exactly* 0.5 (flipped repetitions drop to ≈0.46879). Accuracy: Atomic 94.25%, Split-K=1 94.20%, Deterministic 94.30% — no systematic advantage.
- **Batch sensitivity (Llama/GSM8K, ranks 16+32, 64 prompts).** Fixed batch size: Atomic changes 1,748/4,608 comparisons (**37.93%**, ranging 36.55% at bs=1 to 39.93% at bs=32), both deterministic kernels 0/4,608. Cross-batch (bs 16/32/64 first execution vs bs=1): Atomic **43.75%**, Split-K=1 **39.58%**, Deterministic Split-K=8 **39.06%** — repeatable within a batch size, not across them.
- **Latency (12 model–dataset–rank configs, 50 requests × 150 tokens, 5 engine trials).** Deterministic Split-K=8 vs Split-K=1: **16.8–21.1% faster, mean 19.2%**; vs Atomic Split-K=8: **1.02–2.62% slower, mean 1.69%**. Per config (speedup vs SK1 / overhead vs Atomic): Llama r16 21.0% / 1.62%, Llama r32 19.5% / 1.50%, Gemma r16 20.6% / 2.49%, Gemma r32 18.9% / 2.17%, Nemotron r16 18.3% / 1.13%, Nemotron r32 16.8% / 1.20%.
- **Throughput at batch 64.** Deterministic Split-K=8 reaches **6,079 tok/s (r16) and 6,060 tok/s (r32)** vs **5,274 / 5,257** for Split-K=1 (~**15.3%** higher), while being only 1.5–1.7% below Atomic. The advantage over Split-K=1 shrinks from ~20–21% at batch 1 to >15% at batch 64.
- **Microbenchmark (one-token decode, H=3,072, r=16, three fused QKV slices).** Split-K=8 raises CTAs from 3 to 24 (8×), Nsight-reported SM activity from 1.46% to 8.06%, and cuts complete shrink latency from **14.16 µs to 4.08 µs (3.47×)**.
- **Causal trace (Llama-3.2-3B, rank-16 GSM8K adapter).** Two atomic executions share the first 35 tokens and diverge at position 36, ending at 7 (correct) and 8 (incorrect); Split-K=1 repeats are identical. The first propagating difference is in the layer-2 MLP up-projection at prompt position 47: 0.010345458984375 vs 0.010345457121729851, a gap of 1.86e-9 straddling a BF16 midpoint, which converts to BF16 values 6.10e-5 apart and yields 108 differing key and 176 differing value elements in the layer-3 KV cache at that position, persisting across the next 35 steps. Cache substitution at token 36 reproduces the flip (21.75/21.75 → 21.625/21.75). Source-level intervention on 10 divergent pairs found 26 shrink ops with identical operands but differing FP32 outputs surviving BF16 conversion, and reproduced the atomic token choice in 2 of 10 prompts — one of which changes final-answer correctness.

## Authors & Credibility

Dhayaneshwar Balusamy and Ankur Mohan, both at JPMorgan Chase & Co. This is an industry systems paper with an unusually concrete origin story: the problem was found in an internal production LoRA inference system, and the deterministic kernel described here "was subsequently deployed." That matters for credibility — the kernel is not a benchmark artifact — but it also explains the shape of the evaluation: public models and datasets are used explicitly to avoid proprietary workloads, so what we see is a sanitized reproduction of a production incident rather than the incident itself.

The experimental hygiene is above average for this genre. Weights, prompts, chat templates and decoding settings are held fixed across kernels; adapter hashes, prompt IDs, GPU model and kernel dispatch are recorded; the intended shrink kernel's execution is verified rather than assumed; `VLLM_BATCH_INVARIANT` is deliberately disabled to avoid confounding (they note it would also change other kernels). They also ran H100 and B200 spot checks. Citations are appropriate and current (He & Thinking Machines Lab 2025; Yuan et al. NeurIPS 2025; Gond et al. SOSP 2026; Demmel & Nguyen 2013 on reproducible summation). The AI usage disclosure is explicit: GPT-5.6 Sol for code generation, code review, LaTeX and grammar, with algorithm design, experimental planning, execution, analysis and conclusions attributed to the authors.

Weaknesses on this axis: no code or artifact link anywhere in the paper, which for a kernel contribution is the single biggest credibility gap — the whole value proposition is "drop this Triton kernel in." The evaluation is also all on one GPU model for the application-level results, and the paper is a two-author industrial case study rather than a broad measurement study.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty: modest but real.** Fixed-order reduction is textbook (Demmel & Nguyen 2013; reproducible BLAS has existed for over a decade), and the "deterministic kernel produces 0% variation" half of the headline is true by construction — a fixed summation order over fixed inputs is deterministic, so 97.61% vs 0% is closer to a sanity check than a discovery. The genuine contributions are (a) the *propagation* study, which most determinism papers skip, and (b) the specific engineering trick of electing the last-finishing CTA with an integer counter so no second kernel launch is needed. That trick is what makes the cost 1.69% instead of the pairwise-tree variant's unacceptable overhead, and it is the part worth stealing.

**The causal tracing is the strongest section and is done properly.** Isolating GSM8K prompt 813 to a single FP32 coordinate that straddles a BF16 midpoint (−0.3857421875), then showing that changing only that coordinate alters 32 V-cache elements and flips "There"→"Each", is a clean and convincing mechanism demonstration. The KV-cache substitution experiment (logits 21.75/21.75 → 21.625/21.75) is the right control: it shows the accumulated cache is *sufficient*, while the failed injection experiment honestly shows a single shrink perturbation is *not* always sufficient. Reporting 2/10 successful source-level interventions rather than burying it is commendable.

**Missing baseline — the most serious gap.** The contributions list claims the implementation "achieves lower latency than an existing deterministic Split-K kernel," but no such kernel appears in any table. Table 4 contains only Split-K=1 and Atomic Split-K=8. Likewise the pairwise-reduction-tree variant is dismissed as "introduced overhead" with zero numbers. For a systems paper whose selling point is cost, the one baseline that would establish the delta over prior deterministic work is absent. Similarly, there is no comparison to batch-invariant kernels (He & Thinking Machines Lab 2025) as an alternative route to the same end.

**Statistical hygiene is thin.** No confidence intervals, no significance tests, five engine trials for timing, and the headline 1.02–2.62% overhead is reported as a point estimate — plausibly within trial-to-trial noise, which the paper never quantifies. The 19.2% speedup is large enough to survive that, the 1.69% overhead is not obviously so.

**Evaluation slices are small and aggregated.** 50 prompts per dataset (out of 1,319 GSM8K test and 500 MBPP test), 10 repetitions, 2,700 comparisons per kernel. Correctness flips are summed over six model–rank configurations, so per-configuration counts are 18–21 — far too small to support any claim about which model or rank is more vulnerable, yet the paper makes exactly that kind of comparison ("Llama r16: 105 changed outputs and 18 flips, whereas Nemotron r16: 77 changed outputs but 21 flips"). Those two numbers are not distinguishable without intervals.

**The Civil Comments result is weaker than the abstract implies.** "Structured-decision probabilities vary in 81.47% of atomic comparisons, with decision flips" reads as an operational risk to guardrails. In substance, all seven flips land on comments whose probability is *exactly* 0.5 — a knife-edge tie, not a near-boundary sample — and the accuracy spread across kernels (94.20 / 94.25 / 94.30) on 1,000 examples with 80 positives is pure noise. The paper does disclose the 0.5 detail, but the framing in the abstract and contributions oversells it. If the practical failure mode requires an exact tie, the guardrail motivation is largely rhetorical.

**Serving realism.** Tensor parallelism 1, one request at a time for the main generation study, prefix caching, async scheduling and speculative decoding all disabled, `VLLM_BATCH_INVARIANT` off. That is a deliberately stripped stack, which is defensible for isolation but means the paper says nothing about continuous batching or multi-LoRA serving (Punica/SLoRA-style batched adapters) — the actual deployment mode for LoRA, and the one where per-request adapter switches and ragged batch shapes would stress the kernel far harder. The cross-batch result (39.06% residual differences) is a hint of how much is left, and the authors are honest that it is.

**Scale and scope.** All models ≤4B, ranks only 16 and 32, no r=64/128, no prefill-dominated long-context workload, no MoE. The architecture diversity (dense / local-global attention / Mamba-2 hybrid) is a plus, but "works on 4B models at rank ≤32" is a narrow envelope for a claim about LoRA serving.

**One conceptual omission.** The paper establishes determinism but never asks whether the fixed-order result is *more accurate* than the atomic one — it is simply a different rounding of the same sum. Given that the mean perturbation is 1.08e-8 and the damage comes from BF16 rounding boundaries, one wonders whether compensated (Kahan) summation or higher-precision accumulation would dominate fixed ordering on both axes. That comparison is absent, as is any discussion of stochastic rounding, which is the other standard answer to boundary-lottery effects.

**Bottom line:** a careful, honest, well-instrumented industrial case study with one genuinely useful kernel trick and an excellent causal trace, weakened by a missing deterministic baseline, no error bars, small evaluation slices, and an abstract that inflates a knife-edge tie into a guardrail risk. The engineering recommendation — use fixed-order Split-K, it costs ~1.7% — is well supported.

## Strengths

- **Clean experimental separation.** Capturing identical shrink inputs and replaying them across kernels isolates kernel-level nondeterminism from everything else; the 1.08e-8 mean element difference quantifies "how much" rather than just "whether."
- **The causal chain is complete and controlled.** Kernel bits → BF16 rounding boundary → V-cache elements → token 36 → final answer, with both a cache-substitution sufficiency test and an honest account of when source-level injection fails (2/10).
- **The kernel design is the right one.** Integer completion counters plus a last-CTA fixed-order sum avoid a second kernel launch, which is exactly what kills deterministic reduction at decode-time batch sizes. This is a transferable trick.
- **The cost is genuinely small.** 19.2% faster than unsplit reduction for 1.69% slower than atomic — the trade is obviously worth taking, and the per-model table (1.13% for Nemotron to 2.49% for Gemma r16) lets a practitioner predict their own cost.
- **Honest negative results.** They report that determinism brings no accuracy gain, that cross-batch differences persist at 39.06%, and that only 2/10 interventions succeed. Papers in this space usually stop at "our kernel is deterministic."
- **Good breadth for the budget.** Three architectures (dense, local/global attention, Mamba-2 hybrid), two ranks, three tasks spanning reasoning / code / classification, plus H100 and B200 spot checks.

## Limitations

- **No batch invariance.** Fixed-batch repeatability holds (0/4,608) but cross-batch comparisons still differ in 39.06% of cases for the deterministic kernel, so the fix is necessary but not sufficient for system-level reproducibility. Bucketed batching and prefix-cache reuse are suggested but not evaluated.
- **No artifact.** No code or kernel release link, which makes independent verification of the Triton implementation and the 1.69% overhead claim impossible without reimplementation.
- **Single GPU for all application-level results.** H100/B200 were only checked for absence of variation under identical-input replay — no generation, no timing, no correctness flips on those platforms.
- **No error bars anywhere.** Five timing trials, ten generation repetitions, no intervals; the 1.02–2.62% overhead and the per-model ordering (Gemma worst, Nemotron best) are not established as significant.
- **Narrow operating envelope.** Models ≤4B, ranks 16/32, TP=1, single request at a time in the main study, no continuous batching, no multi-LoRA adapter serving, no quantization.
- **Small evaluation slices.** 50 prompts per dataset; correctness-flip counts of 18–21 per configuration cannot support cross-model comparisons.
- **Practical significance of the decision-flip result is marginal.** All seven Civil Comments flips occur at probability exactly 0.5; the accuracy differences are noise.
- **Determinism ≠ accuracy.** The fixed-order sum is a different rounding, not a better one; no comparison against compensated summation or higher-precision accumulation.

## Relevance

正中 **Inference Reliability & SDC Detection** 这一核心方向：论文讨论的不是模型本身出错，而是 serving stack 底下的 kernel 数值行为（FP32 原子加的非结合性）如何静默改变输出与任务正确性，并给出了从 kernel 位到最终答案的完整因果链——这正是"模型之下的数值/内核/服务栈导致的输出腐坏"的教科书案例。对关注低精度与浮点数值稳定性的读者也有直接价值：1.08e-8 的扰动本身无害，真正致命的是它恰好跨过 BF16 舍入中点；同时论文明确划出"kernel 确定性 ≠ 系统确定性"的边界（跨 batch 仍有 39% 差异），这条负结果比 kernel 本身更值得记住。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the fixed-order Split-K=8 sum agree bit-exactly with Split-K=1? The paper says results "may differ" across reduction methods but never quantifies how often — if they disagree, which is closer to a high-precision reference?
2. Would Kahan/Neumaier compensated summation or FP64 accumulation be competitive on both cost and accuracy, and would either eliminate the BF16 boundary sensitivity rather than merely freezing it?
3. How does the kernel behave under continuous batching with ragged M and per-request adapter switching (Punica/SLoRA-style), where the shrink's tile geometry changes every step?
4. What is the cost at r=64/128 and at prefill-dominated M (thousands of tokens), where the unsplit grid already saturates the GPU and Split-K's benefit should vanish or invert?
5. Combining this with batch-invariant kernels (He & Thinking Machines Lab 2025): does the composition deliver end-to-end reproducibility, and what is the total latency bill?
6. What fraction of real guardrail traffic lands close enough to a decision threshold for the 0.00143 mean probability drift to matter? The 0.5-tie result suggests near zero, but a near-boundary analysis (e.g. |p−0.5| < 0.01) would settle it.
7. Do the H100/B200 runs show no variation because of architecture (scheduling determinism at these shapes) or because the tested shapes happened not to expose it — and would larger batches or different tile sizes change that?
8. Is the 84-vs-53 correctness-flip asymmetry between GSM8K and MBPP meaningful, or an artifact of exact-match grading versus all-or-nothing test execution?
