Why this matters

The fault sits below the model: an inference-engine layer can silently change what users receive, even when the model and its weights are untouched.

Critical edge

02

How the contract is tested

BADC · branch-aware differential conformance

BADC treats “lossless” as a testable contract, not an assumption. Lemma 1 gives four sampler conditions under which model-free speculation is exact; a counterexample shows they are not vacuous.

processor(input_ids) ≡ processor(logical_prefix, sequential_state)
  1. Pin and enumerate. Freeze the engine build; enumerate the exact speculation-off law on a tiny random model (V = 7–10, horizon 3–4).
  2. Channel S. Fix an event family; structural witnesses plus simultaneous Clopper–Pearson bounds lower-bound the total-variation distance between returned-output laws.
  3. Channels R / H. Check per-token records against a teacher-forced reference; simulate each suspected faulty call schedule and test whether its predicted law fits.
  4. Confirm. Freeze a minimal patch and draw fresh seeds; require recurrence > 0.01, residual gap ≤ 0.01 and witness rate ≤ 1e-4.

Executable defect classes

D1Context — reads a buffer longer or shorter than the logical prefix.
D2State — advances on calls sequential decoding would never make, with no rollback.
D3Stopping — decides stop per block, not per position.
D4Record — returns log-probs that disagree with documented values.

03

The ledger

Strengths

  • The contract is checkable and teachable: processors must see the logical prefix and a sequential-equivalent state.
  • Two causes are confirmed by intervention, not correlation: source line, mechanism simulator, frozen patch, fresh seeds, bounded residual.
  • Statistical hygiene is exemplary: one multiplicity family, joint coverage, calibration that can void a configuration, and explicit “not rejected ≠ conformant.”
  • Negative results get the same care as positive ones, including a failed preregistered prediction and a 10/10 vs 3/10 loss to a simpler test.
  • Engine authors get concrete fixes: do not expose drafts to callbacks, roll back state, evaluate stop rules per position.

Limitations

  • Tiny models carry the statistics; the only trained checkpoint is 135M on four hand-written prompts with ≤ 8 new tokens.
  • No prevalence estimate: selection was source- and pilot-informed, so production frequency is unknown.
  • No quality consequence: the 66-point event is a distributional fact, not measured accuracy loss.
  • The largest effect (SynthID, D = 0.6417) has no dedicated repair; it was confirmed only through the shared cause.
  • Reproducibility is promised, not delivered: a private replication package, one INCONCLUSIVE exact-receipt replay, and an unauthenticated wheel digest.
04

Still open

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does the 66-point gap survive at 7B+ scale on natural prompts, and does it become measurable accuracy loss?No benchmark, perplexity or task-accuracy result yet.
  2. What is the real prevalence in production request traces?Source-informed selection cannot answer this.
  3. Why did a permutation chi-square test match or beat BADC on every family?10/10 vs 3/10 on guidance, opposite to the preregistered prediction.
  4. Can the contract be enforced cheaply at runtime with a prefix assertion and a state digest?The enumeration oracle is expensive to port per engine version.

Verdict

Read it for

The contract, two intervention-confirmed mechanisms, and unusually disciplined negative results.

Not for

A deployable diagnostic: a plain permutation chi-square test matched or beat BADC.

Skip if

You need production prevalence or end-to-end quality numbers; the paper does not measure either.
