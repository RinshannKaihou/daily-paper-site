Agents call wrong CAD parts DONE. Forcing measurement before the claim cuts silent failures 4×.

Done Is Not Correct: Measuring Silent Failures and Self-Verification Calibration When LLM Agents Take CAD Actions ↗

Carson Rodrigues, Clive Rodrigues, Aravind Reddy G · 2 Nov 2026 · text-to-CAD silent failures calibration benchmark
Key metric · silent-failure rate
14.1% → 3.2%

22/156 → 5/156 parts: valid geometry, explicit DONE, at least one failed semantic check. n=156 per arm, four vendors · McNemar p<1e-4.

Why this matters

The deployable risk is harness-level overclaiming, not model skill — and a claim contract, not more iteration, is the cheap fix.

↳ transfers to any pipeline with an exact oracle
Critical edge

13 of 31 silent failures come from one L-bracket spec whose base depth admits two readings — the absolute rates rest on one ambiguous sentence.

Read the intervals, not the point estimate

中文摘要：13 个任务、122 项机器可检验检查的文本到 CAD 基准。单次生成静默失败率 14.1%（22/156）；改为「先测量、再宣称完成」后降至 3.2%（5/156）。单变量消融表明，起作用的不是反馈循环本身，而是 claim contract。

01

How the claim was measured

13 tasks · 122 checks · 4 vendors
  1. 01

    Specs that compile into checks

    Three tiers — 5 single-feature, 6 multi-feature, 2 edit-under-constraint — with 122 machine-checkable requirements on build123d / OpenCascade. Every natural-language requirement maps to at least one computable property.

  2. 02

    Kernel truth, quarantined

    Agent Python runs in a subprocess that returns validity, bbox, volume, centroid and every cylinder's radius, axis and axial extent — never the targets, tolerance bands or pass/fail verdicts.

  3. 03

    Three architectures, one suite

    Single-shot; a ReAct tool-use loop under the measure-then-claim contract; planner–coder–checker. Four vendors × 13 tasks × 3 repetitions = 156 attempts per condition.

  4. 04

    Claims parsed, not assumed

    DONE counts only on its own line outside a code block; confidence comes from a CONFIDENCE: 0..1 line. Reference solutions must pass every check before any agent run is scored.

03

What holds up, what to discount

read side by side

Strengths

  • The measurand is the right one. Conditioning on the agent's own DONE separates overclaiming from grader weakness — the confound that makes execution-based grading leaky.
  • The ablation is the paper. Identical feedback, only the claim contract changes, and it carries the effect across all four models — including against the authors' own pilot.
  • Two-sided error bounds. Oracle hardening floors the rate, a blind expert audit ceilings it, and both are quantified rather than asserted.
  • The band sweep pre-empts the obvious objection. Halving every tolerance changes no verdict, so these are gross errors — a missing counterbore, a hole on the wrong pitch circle.

Limitations

  • 13 tasks, 13 clusters. Enough for paired architecture contrasts, not for absolute rates that transfer to a different task mix.
  • One ambiguous spec contaminates the headline. 13 of 31 silent failures come from a part a practising engineer read differently from the grader.
  • Missing comparisons. No OpenAI or Google model in the primary population; CADSmith, MUSE and 3DCodeBench are compared on construct, never run on the suite.
  • Ablation confounds for two models. Sonnet and qwen legacy arms ran three months later, sonnet through a different gateway; the silent-failure gap alone is p=0.056.
04

Still open

three questions worth a follow-up

Questions for further exploration

  1. Q1Without an exact kernel oracle, can partial measurement — schema, types, unit tests — capture most of the 11-point claim-precision gain?
  2. Q2Does the legacy contract mostly convert silent failures into loud crashes? A per-episode transition analysis would settle it.
  3. Q3If the multi-agent coder saw raw kernel traces instead of checker prose, would its 57% coverage approach tool-use's 81%?
