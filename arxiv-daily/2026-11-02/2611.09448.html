Critical assessment
01

One sweep, three levels of decomposition

Six objectives, two scales, one seed — and a hierarchy that separates what the logged entropy curve mixes together.

Level 1 Reward outcome hb(p₊) + p₊H₊ + p₋H₋
Level 2 Process validity R=1 ≠ V=1
Level 3 Semantic mode Neff = exp H(Z | R=1, V=1)
Local Covariance Δh ≈ −Cov(log π, Δz)
  • Two scales, one variable. Qwen2.5-3B and 7B, LoRA adapters, one epoch, one seed, the same 8,000-example Skywork-OR1 subset; eight on-policy samples per prompt and an answer-equivalence reward.
  • Six objectives, everything else fixed. GRPO, DAPO, Clip-Cov, KL-Cov, EP-GRPO and GTPO share optimizer, sampling and adapter configuration within each scale.
  • Instruments, not just definitions. A 200-prompt fixed probe read at four milestones; a frozen, human-audited semantic partition for mode counts; exact full-vocabulary covariance over 44 adjacent-checkpoint transitions; q99 tail share of positive covariance, split by reward outcome.
  • What is measured. Greedy accuracy on five benchmarks with an equal-weight macro average, plus MATH500 pass@64. No causal claim — the framework is diagnostic by construction.

03

The edge

What holds up, and what a single-seed, LoRA-only sweep cannot support.

For

Strengths

  • Proved, not asserted. An exact decomposition shows average token entropy is a mixture statistic whose components move in opposite directions.
  • Every term is estimated. Fixed probe, audited partition, full-vocabulary covariance, reward-stratified tail shares — the framework is operational, not decorative.
  • The covariance validation is hard to fake. 42/44 signs, slope 0.993, R² 0.937 — with median relative error disclosed alongside.
  • The obvious objection is answered. The paired common-question analysis adds bootstrap CIs, then reports the two GTPO endpoints that fail to exclude equality.
  • The failure is documented. Length saturation, boxed-answer collapse and mask inactivity are reported instead of quietly dropped.
Against

Limitations

  • One seed, one model family. Headline orderings such as Clip-Cov 27.75 vs GRPO 24.43 sit inside plausible seed noise.
  • LoRA-only, 8k examples, one epoch. Short-horizon, low-rank adaptation may not transfer to full-finetune RLVR at scale.
  • One frozen semantic partition. The human audit is asserted but never quantified — no agreement rate, no κ, no sensitivity analysis.
  • The q99 cutoff is not comparable. For GTPO failed strata it selects ~58–59% of tokens, so that row measures something different.
  • No prospective test. RCEA never predicts a failure or triggers an intervention; the Clip-Cov post-mortem is a retrospective fit.
  • Macro average is fragile. Thirty-problem AIME sets get the same weight as MATH500's 500 problems, so small-sample noise drives the ranking.
04

Open questions

Five threads the paper leaves open — two of them decide whether RCEA survives contact with full fine-tuning.

  1. Does the failed-vs-successful entropy gap survive full fine-tuning at 7B/14B, or is part of it an artifact of LoRA's restricted update subspace?
  2. What is the audit agreement rate (accuracy, κ) of the frozen semantic partition, and how much does retention move under a different judge?
  3. Can RCEA become an online trigger — alarm when ρH approaches 1 while boxed-answer rate falls, before accuracy collapses?
  4. Why does DAPO-7B reach 77.2% MATH500 with lower mode retention (88.8%) than GRPO-7B (89.6%, 71.8%)?
  5. Would a mode-aware regularizer that penalizes H(Z | Cx) contraction avoid a Clip-Cov-style collapse without sacrificing greedy accuracy?
