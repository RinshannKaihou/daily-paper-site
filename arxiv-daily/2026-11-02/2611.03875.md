> **一句话总结：** 作者在 118 节点 REPACSS 生产集群的 79 天遥测上，把统计检测（阈值规则 + Isolation Forest + LSTM-AE）、作业感知建模（Slurm 作业分段 + 负载聚类 + Hampel 同行对比）、物理推理（Ridge 功耗/进风温度残差 + 四条约束规则）三层证据，在第四阶段用梯度提升分类器融合成带分级（CRITICAL/CONFIRMED/CANDIDATE）的可解释告警事件，在 29 个真实故障 + 140 次受控注入共 169 个测试事件上取得 P=0.913 / R=0.817 / F1=0.862，比最强基线 Prodigy（F1=0.660）高 31%。

> **TL;DR:** INSIGHT-HPC fuses statistical, job-aware and physics-guided evidence into operator-facing alert episodes on 79 days (≈220K node-hours) of production telemetry from a 118-node cluster at Texas Tech. On 169 labeled test events (29 real operator-recorded failures + 140 controlled injections) it reaches F1 = 0.862 (P = 0.913, R = 0.817, AUROC = 0.896), versus F1 = 0.660 for Prodigy, 0.552 for NodeSentry, 0.490 for Isolation Forest and 0.480 for an LSTM autoencoder — a +31% F1 delta over the strongest baseline. The catch: Phase IV is a *supervised* gradient-boosting classifier trained on labeled episodes, while every baseline is unsupervised or label-free, and 83% of the test events are synthetic injections whose signatures match the physics rules by construction. On Prodigy's own Eclipse benchmark, where only Phase I can run, the gap disappears (memleak F1 0.921 vs 0.969 for Prodigy; AUROC 0.735 vs 1.000).

## 核心贡献

- **四阶段"渐进细化"架构**：Phase I 做纯统计筛查（阈值规则、Isolation Forest、30 分钟滑窗 LSTM 自编码器），Phase II 引入 Slurm 作业上下文与同行节点对比，Phase III 用物理模型与约束规则判断异常是否物理可信，Phase IV 把三层证据融合成"事件（episode）"级决策并分级告警。
- **作业条件化推理**：以 Slurm 作业边界把时间线切成段，对每段提取约 30 个负载画像特征，用 K-Means/HDBSCAN（按轮廓系数选）聚类成"负载原型"，再在每个原型内部单独拟合 Isolation Forest——即"相对于同类负载是否异常"，而非相对于全体。
- **物理引导验证**：按节点类型分别训练 Ridge 功耗模型（输入 CPU 负载、内存、作业数、CPU/内存/GPU 功耗、regime 标识）与进风温度模型，用 14 天滚动 IQR 归一化残差（避免未来信息泄漏），并施加四条约束规则：动力学约束、跨平面约束（PDU 与 BMC 汇总对比）、分配-利用率约束、冷却响应约束。
- **事件级可解释告警**：告警以"事件"而非分钟为单位输出，每个告警包含融合概率、分级、语义标签、主导信号、TreeSHAP 归因、被触发的规则与约束、以及关联作业上下文，形成可审计的处置卡片。
- **生产规模验证 + 受控注入**：在 NSF 资助的 REPACSS 集群上（110 CPU + 8 GPU + 3 login + 9 storage 节点，28,984 核，176 TB 内存）用 79 天数据（2026-01-01 至 03-20）评测，并公开了计算成本与近线性扩展性测量。

## Key Contributions

- **A four-phase refinement architecture.** Rather than one detector, the system treats anomaly detection as an evidence-refinement pipeline: statistical screening (Phase I) → workload conditioning (Phase II) → physical plausibility (Phase III) → learned episode-level fusion with tiering (Phase IV). Each phase is separately inspectable, which is what makes the final alert explainable.
- **Workload-conditioned anomaly reasoning.** Job segmentation on Slurm boundaries (new segment when the primary job changes, the active job set changes, or a telemetry gap exceeds 120 s), ~30-feature workload profiles, archetype clustering (K-Means vs HDBSCAN chosen by silhouette score, clusters under 10 members merged), and within-archetype Isolation Forests. Peer analysis uses a running peer-median profile with a Hampel Z-score plus a direction-aware per-minute system-power check.
- **Physics-guided validation.** Per-node-type Ridge regressions for expected system input power and expected inlet temperature, with host-specific bias correction, an adaptive residual scale from a trailing 14-day IQR (bounded to 0.5–5× the training residual std), job-transition damping of `1 − exp(−Δ/τ)` with τ = 5 min, and four structural constraint rules that fire only after 3–5 sustained minutes (30 minutes for allocation-vs-utilization).
- **Multi-job attribution.** When several jobs share a node, the system scores each candidate job on power residual, onset alignment, cluster deviation and peer coherence, combines them into a weighted attribution confidence (weights redistributed when a signal is missing), and surfaces the full ranked list in the alert — independent of the learned fusion stack.
- **Tiered, auditable alerting.** CRITICAL / CONFIRMED / CANDIDATE thresholds are all selected on the validation split only; the paper explicitly reports validation-to-test transfer rather than a test-optimal operating point, and reports per-tier P/R (0.979/0.525, 0.913/0.817, 0.813/0.899).
- **Operational cost characterization.** End-to-end replay of 79 days × 118 nodes on a single 20-core + 1× H100 allocation takes ≈43 minutes; runtime scales as 14.6 s/node + 850 s fixed (R² = 0.997), extrapolating to ≈4.3 hours for 1,000 nodes, with frozen-model inference at 0.13 ms per node-minute.

## Methodology

**Data planes.** Three heterogeneous sources are joined on a per-node, per-minute master table: out-of-band BMC telemetry via Redfish (CPU temperature, fan RPM, inlet/return air temperature, system power), Slurm job records (allocation, per-job CPU/memory utilization, job state), and facility PDUs (per-outlet power). Job intervals are expanded to one row per minute so every telemetry minute carries `active_job_count` (0 = idle). A centralized TimescaleDB-backed monitoring service (Monster-style) aggregates the streams.

**Preprocessing.** An auditing pass attaches per-sensor quality masks rather than deleting rows: spike flags from a rolling z-score over a 120-sample window (`z_audit > 5.0` **and** an absolute-delta floor, to stop near-flat signals from producing huge z-scores), flatline flags from consecutive-repeat length (default 480 minutes, tightened for critical sensors), and bad-range flags against empirical 0.1th/99.9th percentiles with a ±5% buffer. Masking is *selective*: spikes during active jobs are preserved because they may be legitimate workload effects. Feature engineering produces short-term change (rate-of-change, rolling mean/std/quantiles at 5/15/60 min), long-horizon drift slopes (12 h/24 h), missing-sensor streaks, workload context (`active_job_count`, `is_running_job`, recency of job-set change), and periodic encodings (hour sin, day-of-week cos). Continuous features are normalized per host with a minimum-variance floor and clipping.

**Regime handling.** Because maintenance, firmware updates and cooling changes shift the baseline (Figure 2 shows node `rpc-91-1`'s inlet temperature drifting well above a fixed 26.3 °C January-only threshold after a maintenance window), each (node, timestamp) row is annotated with a regime identifier from maintenance metadata, and all rolling/drift features are computed *within* a regime so statistics never cross a boundary. When metadata is missing the identifier is constant and the pipeline degrades to ordinary past-only windowing.

**Phase I — statistical screening.** Three complementary detector families: threshold rules (high power/temperature, fan-failure patterns, idle power surges, flatlines, dropouts); Isolation Forest with the standard `s(x) = 2^(−E(h(x))/c(n))` scoring; and a sequence-to-sequence LSTM autoencoder over 30-minute sliding windows scored by mean absolute reconstruction error. Training-set cleaning is two-pass: drop windows flagged by Isolation Forest, then drop the top 1% highest-error windows. The phase score is the mean of the three normalized outputs; each output is additionally converted to a per-host trailing rank so firing rates stay stable across regimes.

**Phase II — job-aware modeling.** Segments are reduced to ~30-feature behavioral profiles (power/energy statistics, compute-efficiency ratios, thermal and cooling means, GPU metrics), clustered into archetypes, and scored by archetype-local Isolation Forests. Peer analysis compares a node's running profile to the peer median over the multi-node segment using a Hampel Z-score with MAD scaling and a 30-minute warm-up; a separate direction-aware check on system input power catches single-metric faults that a full-profile distance would dilute. Job attribution scores each concurrent job on four signals and returns a ranked list.

**Phase III — physics reasoning.** A Ridge power model per node type predicts expected input power from workload features and the regime identifier, plus a host-specific bias correction; the thermal model predicts expected inlet temperature from system input power and regime, with per-node calibration because cooling efficiency varies by rack position. Residuals are scaled by a robust rolling IQR over a trailing 14-day window (`k = 1.349`), bounded to 0.5–5× the training std — this is the paper's main defense against non-stationarity and future leakage. Flag thresholds are |z| > 5 for power and |z| > 4 for temperature, with residuals damped near job start/end within a 20-minute window. Four constraint rules (dynamics, cross-plane PDU-vs-BMC, allocation-vs-utilization, cooling response) encode relationships that should hold even when no single sensor is extreme.

**Phase IV — fusion.** Per-minute evidence is grouped into candidate episodes (sustained headline score above a small threshold, adjacent candidates merged across short gaps) to match how operator tickets are actually written. Each episode becomes a fixed-length feature vector that keeps raw per-phase flags rather than one scalar per phase, plus context unavailable at minute resolution: rank against the host's own recent history, per-phase persistence, concurrent episodes in the same rack, the host's recent weak-candidate rate, and comparison with job peers. A histogram-based gradient-boosting classifier produces the fused probability, mapped to three tiers. Episodes with known labeled fault intervals, maintenance periods and audit-flagged readings are excluded from Phase I–III fitting; Phase IV uses labeled episodes with a predefined feature set and validation-selected thresholds, and test labels never enter fitting, feature selection or threshold tuning. Output alerts carry TreeSHAP attributions.

**Evaluation protocol.** REPACSS: 110 CPU nodes (2× AMD EPYC 9754, 256 cores, 1.5 TB DDR5 each), 8 GPU nodes (2× Intel Xeon Gold 6448Y + 4× H100 NVL 94 GB via NVLink), 3 login, 9 storage; 28,984 cores, 176 TB memory. 79 days (Jan 1 – Mar 20, 2026), ≈220K node-hours, 13,266 Slurm jobs; 59 days train / 8 days validation / 12 days test, with a Feb 14–16 maintenance shutdown masked. 169 labeled test events across 85 hosts: 29 operator-recorded failures + 140 controlled injections spanning thermal, power, cooling, resource, sensing and cross-plane modes (idle-power injections add 110–220 W for 2–6 h; cooling injections raise inlet temperature 4–6 °C for 1–2 h; sensor dropouts last 2–3 h). A candidate matches a ground-truth event if it overlaps the event's tolerance window (60 min before, 30 min after) on the same host, on any host in the same rack for rack/PDU-level events, or on any host for cluster-wide events.

## Results & Highlights

- **Main comparison (Table III, 169 test events).** INSIGHT-HPC reaches P = 0.913, R = 0.817, F1 = 0.862, AUROC = 0.896, AUPRC = 0.878. The strongest baseline, Prodigy, gets P = 0.599, R = 0.735, F1 = 0.660 (AUROC 0.666, AUPRC 0.450); NodeSentry gets F1 = 0.552 (P 0.710, R 0.452); Isolation Forest 0.490 and LSTM-AE 0.480. That is +0.202 F1 absolute over Prodigy, i.e. the claimed +31% relative.
- **Tier trade-off is real and monotone.** At the three validation-selected operating points, precision/recall are 0.979/0.525 (CRITICAL), 0.913/0.817 (CONFIRMED) and 0.813/0.899 (CANDIDATE), with event recall 0.805 at CONFIRMED. The tiering does what it advertises — operators can buy precision with recall.
- **Ablation (Table IV).** Phase I alone gives F1 = 0.557 (P 0.393, R 0.955); Phase II alone collapses to F1 = 0.283 (R = 0.178); Phase III alone F1 = 0.524 (P 0.361, R 0.954). Fixed Boolean fusion does not rescue it: AND of all three phases gives F1 = 0.263, OR gives 0.481, majority vote (≥2) gives 0.675 — all far below the learned fusion's 0.862 (+0.187 F1 over majority vote).
- **Sensitivity to the matching tolerance.** Re-scoring frozen alerts with a stricter 0/15-minute tolerance instead of 60/30 gives P/R/F1 = 0.907/0.811/0.856, a drop of only 0.006 F1 — the result is not an artifact of a generous matching window.
- **Cross-dataset portability is the weak spot (Table V, Eclipse).** With only Phase I available, memleak F1 = 0.921 vs Prodigy 0.969 and NodeSentry 0.908; cpuoccupy F1 = 0.907 vs 0.927 and 0.904. F1 is comparable, but AUROC on memleak is 0.735 vs Prodigy's 1.000 — the ranking quality of the ported stack is markedly worse even when the thresholded F1 looks fine.
- **Cost and scaling.** 79 days × 118 nodes replays end-to-end in ≈43 minutes on 20 cores + 1× H100. Runtime fits 14.6 s/node + 850 s fixed (R² = 0.997) across 38/68/98-node replays, predicting ≈4.3 h for 1,000 nodes; frozen inference fits 15.1 s/node (R² = 0.999), i.e. 0.13 ms per node-minute.
- **Case study 1 (true positive, Fig. 4/5).** A 240-minute idle-power injection on CPU node `rpc-97-1` (2026-03-10 20:00 UTC, +130.4 W, 472.3 W → 602.7 W, +28% over the 7-day idle baseline, no active job for the preceding 24 h) was bracketed minute-for-minute by a 240-minute CRITICAL alert; the Phase III physics residual hit 9.1σ and the "Idle Power Surge" rule fired.
- **Case study 2 (false-positive rescue, Fig. 6).** A legitimate Slurm job starting 08:57 UTC on 2026-01-04 on GPU node `rpg-93-7` drove power from 690 W idle to ≈915 W bursts; Phase I fired at 09:06 UTC (2.6σ) but Phase II found the workload routine and Phase III found no violation (|z| ≤ 1.3), so fusion suppressed the episode to NONE — a concrete demonstration of the cross-plane logic doing work.

## Authors & Credibility

Rupak Kadel, Jie Li and Yong Chen, all at the Department of Computer Science, Texas Tech University. Yong Chen is a well-established HPC/data-systems researcher (the Monster monitoring tool cited as [8] comes from this group), and Jie Li is a co-author of that monitoring stack — so the telemetry plumbing and the TimescaleDB-backed collection layer described here are the authors' own production infrastructure, not a borrowed dataset. The work is supported by NSF grants OAC-2404438 and CNS-1939140 (NSF IUCRC on Cloud and Autonomic Computing) and uses REPACSS, an NSF-funded power-aware cluster, which explains why 79 days of genuinely continuous multi-plane telemetry with operator-recorded failure tickets was available at all. The paper is written in IEEE conference format with a proper capability comparison table (Table I) against RUAD, Prodigy, Refine, NodeSentry, GRAAFE, ThermADNet and DeepHYDRA, and the acknowledgments disclose ChatGPT-5.5 use for grammar only. Credibility signals worth noting: the ablation openly reports that Phase II alone has recall 0.178, the paper volunteers that AUROC/AUPRC are "not directly comparable across rows" because each method ranks a different pool, and the conclusion states plainly that "the present evaluation is retrospective." Those are the marks of authors who are not overselling. Against that: no code or data release is mentioned, the cluster is a single mid-size academic system, and the one external benchmark is used in a degraded configuration (Phase I only).

## Critical Assessment

*Novelty.* The individual ingredients are all borrowed — Isolation Forest, LSTM autoencoders, Hampel filters, Ridge residual models, TreeSHAP, gradient boosting. What is new is the composition: routing a per-minute statistical flag through workload conditioning and then through a physics residual before letting a learned classifier decide, with alert *episodes* rather than alert *minutes* as the unit of decision. That episode-level framing is the genuinely good idea, because it aligns the model's output granularity with how operator tickets are actually written, and it is what makes the tiered CRITICAL/CONFIRMED/CANDIDATE scheme meaningful. The physics-consistency rules (particularly the cross-plane PDU-vs-BMC check and the cooling-response check) are more specific than the "physics-informed" claims in most HPC anomaly papers, which usually stop at a residual threshold.

*The central methodological problem.* Phase IV is supervised. It is trained on labeled training and validation episodes and its tier thresholds are selected on validation labels. Every baseline it beats is unsupervised or label-free: Prodigy uses healthy training data with a 99th-percentile reconstruction threshold, NodeSentry is fitted without labels, and Isolation Forest / LSTM-AE are standard unsupervised detectors. The paper is candid that Phases I–III are label-free and that Phase IV is not, but the headline table then presents a supervised fusion against unsupervised baselines as if it were an apples-to-apples comparison, and the abstract reports the resulting F1 = 0.862 without that qualifier. A fairer experiment would either (a) give Prodigy and NodeSentry a comparable supervised calibration layer on the same labeled episodes, or (b) report the unsupervised-only configuration. The ablation partially covers this — majority vote over the three phases reaches F1 = 0.675, which is *above* Prodigy's 0.660 — so the honest claim is closer to "+0.02 F1 from multi-plane evidence, +0.19 from supervised fusion." That is a much less exciting sentence, and the paper does not say it.

*Test-set composition.* 140 of 169 test events (83%) are controlled injections, and the injection catalogue is suspiciously well matched to the Phase III rule library: idle-power injections of 110–220 W for 2–6 h are exactly what the "Idle Power Surge" rule and the power-residual channel are built to catch; cooling injections of +4–6 °C for 1–2 h are exactly what the cooling-response constraint targets; dropouts of 2–3 h are exactly what the missing-sensor streak features target. The paper does acknowledge that injections "test selected signatures rather than the full diversity of production failures," which is honest, but the practical consequence is that the 0.913/0.817 headline is measured mostly on faults the architecture was designed around. The 29 real operator-recorded failures are never broken out as a separate result column — that number is the one an operator would actually want, and its absence is the single most important missing table.

*Missing ablations.* The paper admits it: "These comparisons do not isolate each phase's contribution under a retrained classifier." Table IV compares the full learned fusion against fixed Boolean combinations of frozen phase outputs, which establishes that learned > Boolean but says nothing about whether Phase II's peer analysis contributes once Phase IV exists. Phase II alone has recall 0.178 — as a detector it is nearly useless — so its entire value must be false-positive suppression, and there is no measurement of false-positive reduction attributable to Phase II (only one anecdotal case study, Fig. 6). The natural experiment — retrain Phase IV with each phase's features ablated — is absent and would have been cheap, since the pipeline already exists.

*Hyperparameter fragility.* There is a long list of hand-set constants: `z_audit > 5.0`, flatline 480 minutes, bad-range ±5% buffer, |z| > 5 power / |z| > 4 temperature, 14-day IQR window, 30-minute peer warm-up, τ = 5 min transition damping, 3–5 minute constraint persistence, 30-minute allocation-vs-utilization window, cluster minimum size 10, top-1% reconstruction-error trimming. None of these gets a sensitivity sweep. The one sensitivity check performed is on the evaluation tolerance (60/30 → 0/15), which is a property of the *scoring protocol*, not of the method. A reviewer should worry that the reported operating point sits on a knife's edge of these constants, particularly the residual z-thresholds, which directly control how much evidence reaches Phase IV.

*Cross-dataset evidence.* The Eclipse evaluation is weaker than the paper's framing suggests. Only Phase I runs (Phase II is disabled because the anomaly is applied to all four nodes of a job, making peer divergence uninformative; Phase III is inapplicable because the dataset has no temperature, fan, power or peer signals). So the "portability" result is really "our Phase I is roughly Prodigy-level on Prodigy's benchmark" — F1 0.921 vs 0.969 on memleak, 0.907 vs 0.927 on cpuoccupy. And the AUROC gap is large and unexplained: 0.735 vs 1.000 on memleak. Prodigy's perfect AUROC on a 160-sample binary task is itself a yellow flag about that benchmark's difficulty or about protocol leakage, but the paper does not discuss either possibility. The sentence "it trails by only a small margin" is doing a lot of work to cover a configuration in which three of four phases are switched off.

*Statistical power.* 169 events, 85 hosts, one 12-day test window, one cluster, one hardware generation. No confidence intervals, no significance tests, no repeated seeds on the main result (only the Eclipse experiment uses three seeds). With 169 events, a handful of misclassified episodes moves F1 by several points; the +0.006 F1 stability under a stricter tolerance is reassuring about the protocol but says nothing about sampling variance across time windows. A single 12-day test period in March also means seasonal or workload-mix effects are entirely confounded with the result.

*Minor issues.* The `is_multi_job` / multi-job attribution machinery is described in detail but never evaluated in isolation — there is no reported accuracy for the "primary attributed job" when two jobs share a node, which is the case the authors themselves call "challenging." Figure 4b plots bounded episode-formation scores in z-units, which the caption concedes "compresses large residuals," so the visual evidence of a 9.1σ residual looks like a modest bump. The paper says "118-node compute fleet" while the cluster description sums to 130 nodes (110 + 8 + 3 + 9), a small inconsistency that should have been caught. Class imbalance handling in the Phase IV gradient booster is never described, and neither is the size of the labeled episode pool — with 169 test events, the training pool of labeled episodes is presumably in the low hundreds, which is thin for a gradient-boosted classifier with a "predefined feature set" of unspecified dimension.

*Significance.* Despite the above, this is one of the more operationally grounded HPC anomaly papers in recent memory. Most work in this space evaluates on pre-sliced benchmark traces (including the Eclipse dataset used here) with no continuous multi-week replay, no PDU plane, and no operator in the loop. Getting 79 days of continuous BMC + Slurm + PDU telemetry with 29 real failure tickets is hard, unglamorous work, and the retrospective replay with a masked maintenance window and validation-only threshold selection is a defensible protocol. The physics constraint library and the episode-level tiering are reusable ideas regardless of the exact numbers.

## Strengths

- **Genuinely multi-plane, genuinely production.** BMC, Slurm and PDU streams are actually joined per node-minute, and the cross-plane consistency check (rack-level PDU vs aggregate BMC) is a signal that single-plane detectors structurally cannot see. 220K node-hours of continuous telemetry is a real dataset, not a benchmark slice.
- **The episode abstraction is the right unit.** Forming sustained episodes before classification, merging flickering candidates, and matching against interval-valued ground truth aligns the detector with operator workflow and with how tickets are actually filed. This is a design decision that many HPC anomaly papers get wrong by scoring minutes and then reporting per-minute precision.
- **Careful leakage and non-stationarity handling.** Regime-aware feature computation, a trailing-only 14-day IQR residual scale, per-host normalization with training-period statistics, a masked maintenance window, exclusion of audit-flagged readings from training, and validation-only tier thresholds. The engineering discipline here is above average for the venue.
- **Honest reporting in several places.** The paper volunteers that AUROC/AUPRC are not comparable across rows, that Phase II alone has recall 0.178 and its high AUPRC reflects base rate rather than detection strength, that the Boolean ablation does not isolate per-phase contribution, and that the evaluation is retrospective. It also reports the *degraded* Eclipse configuration rather than hiding it.
- **The false-positive rescue case study is more convincing than the true-positive one.** Showing a 2.6σ Phase I spike being suppressed because Phase II recognized the job and Phase III found no physics violation is a concrete, falsifiable demonstration of why the architecture exists — and it is the kind of example operators actually care about.
- **Cost is measured, not asserted.** 43 minutes for 79 days × 118 nodes, a two-parameter linear fit with R² = 0.997 and 0.999, and an explicit extrapolation to 1,000 nodes. Very few HPC anomaly papers report replay cost at all.
- **Operator-facing output is specified concretely.** The alert card in Fig. 5 names the node, onset, associated job, likely cause, a recommended action with a time bound ("investigate within 15 min"), and the supporting evidence with numbers (472.3 W → 602.7 W, +28%, 9.1σ). That is a specification someone could implement.

## Limitations

- **Supervised fusion vs unsupervised baselines.** The headline comparison is not apples-to-apples; the label-free configuration (majority vote, F1 = 0.675) is barely above Prodigy (0.660), and the paper does not foreground that number.
- **Injection-dominated test set.** 83% of test events are synthetic and their signatures match the physics rules by construction. Real-failure-only performance is never reported separately.
- **Single site, single 79-day window, single hardware generation.** No cross-cluster validation with all four phases active, no seasonal coverage, no second site.
- **Portability claim is thin.** Eclipse exercises Phase I only, and there the AUROC gap versus Prodigy (0.735 vs 1.000 on memleak) is large and undiscussed.
- **No per-phase ablation under a retrained fusion model**, so the marginal value of Phase II (and of the peer-analysis machinery specifically) is unestablished beyond one anecdote.
- **Many untested hyperparameters.** The z-thresholds, persistence windows and IQR horizon have no sensitivity analysis, and the operating point plausibly depends on them.
- **No statistical uncertainty.** No confidence intervals or significance tests on 169 events; the main result uses a single test split with no seed variation.
- **Retrospective replay only.** Acknowledged by the authors: no online deployment, and classification uses features summarized over *complete* episodes, so the reported numbers do not reflect alert latency for in-progress faults.
- **Multi-job attribution is unevaluated.** Described in detail, never scored against a ground truth for "which concurrent job caused it."
- **No artifact release.** No code, no configuration files, no telemetry sample; reproduction would require rebuilding the entire four-phase stack from prose.

## Relevance

这篇论文属于"系统/AIOps 侧的可解释异常检测"，对关注 LLM/ML 系统运维、集群监控、以及"物理约束 + 学习模型"混合架构的读者有直接参考价值：它的四阶段证据细化与事件级分级告警设计可以迁移到任何多源时序监控场景（包括推理集群的 GPU 功耗/温度/利用率监控）。需要留意的是它的性能数字建立在监督融合与合成注入之上，引用时应区分"无标签配置"与"有标签融合"两种口径。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. What is the precision/recall on the 29 operator-recorded failures *alone*, with the 140 injections excluded? Is the physics-rule-heavy design still competitive on faults it was not designed around?
2. How much does Phase II actually contribute once Phase IV is retrained? Specifically, retrain the gradient booster with (a) peer-analysis features removed and (b) the entire Phase II block removed, and report the F1/FP-rate delta. The Boolean ablation in Table IV cannot answer this.
3. If Prodigy and NodeSentry were each given a supervised calibration layer trained on the same labeled episodes, how much of the 0.862 vs 0.660 gap survives? This is the experiment the paper's framing most needs.
4. Why is the ported Phase I's AUROC on Eclipse memleak only 0.735 while Prodigy reaches 1.000? Is this a scoring-pool artifact, a normalization mismatch between 156 `/proc` counters and the REPACSS feature set, or a genuine ranking deficiency? And why is Prodigy's AUROC perfect on a 160-sample task at all?
5. How sensitive is the CONFIRMED operating point to the residual z-thresholds (|z| > 5 power, |z| > 4 temperature) and to the 14-day IQR horizon? A one-dimensional sweep of each would establish whether 0.862 is a plateau or a lucky point.
6. What is the alert latency in an online setting, given that Phase IV features are summarized over complete episodes? Can the tier decision be made incrementally with acceptable precision loss?
7. How accurate is the multi-job attribution when two or more jobs share a node? The paper calls this "challenging" and then never measures it.
8. Does the regime-identifier mechanism depend on maintenance metadata that many sites do not record? How much does performance degrade under the documented constant-identifier fallback?
