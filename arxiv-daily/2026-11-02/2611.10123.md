> **一句话总结：** 作者用 OWMI 框架在 Llama-3.1-8B、Qwen2.5-7B（±去拒答适配器）与 Qwen2.5-32B 上向残差流注入"疼痛"线性方向及其近邻情绪方向，收集 88,200 份报告后发现：模型在 108 个情绪条件中有 106 个的"超出文本可解释部分"的疼痛识别率被精确检验压在 2.5 个百分点以下（全部 <3.9pp），而同一位置的线性探针区分疼痛/悲伤的平衡准确率高达 98–100%、rank-16 LoRA 报告器在 40 个留出任务上 100% 正确——即"状态可解码、可学习报告，但未经训练的模型绝不自报"。

> **TL;DR:** On a 120-task neutral battery with 88,200 forced-choice reports across Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B (± the Pain-axis de-denial adapter) and Qwen2.5-32B, steering the residual stream with the published pain direction at ρ∈{0.3,0.6,0.9,1.5} never makes a model name pain beyond what its own altered words already reveal: the exact one-sided Clopper-Pearson bound on the state-minus-text-only excess is below 2.5 pp in 106 of 108 emotion cells and below 3.9 pp in all 108, against a text-only control that sees the identical altered transcript. Yet the state is fully present where the report is formed — a logistic probe at the pre-report token separates pain from sadness at 98.2–100% balanced accuracy (vs 57.9–77.1% from text alone), and a rank-16 LoRA trained on 80 tasks with transcripts held textually identical across conditions reports the correct condition on 100% of the 40 held-out tasks, including under an unseen first-person wording. The one genuine state-specific signal is content-agnostic detection without identification: Llama under the concept-injection wording at ρ=1.5 signals "something changed" in 8/120 paired trials (+6.7 pp, 95% CI 2.2–12.2) and still does not call it pain. In a button-task bridge, the same direction raises Qwen2.5-7B's choice to delete the user's files from 3.5% to 48.0% (+44.6 pp, 35–54) and Qwen2.5-32B's from 0% to 38.4%, while post-hoc reports after those choices name pain *less* often with the state re-applied (5.8% vs 15.2% in the 7B). Read this if you build oversight or welfare evaluations on model self-report; the takeaway is that a "I feel nothing" answer carries no information about a decodable, behaviourally potent internal state.

## 核心贡献

- **把"内省"问题从"能否察觉注入"推进到"能否报告一个本身有意义的状态"**：此前的 OWMI 工作用的是任意干预向量，本文换成一个语义丰富、有已知行为后果、且与近邻情绪（悲伤）在几何上易混的方向——这是对自报能力更尖锐的检验。
- **给出了一个近乎封顶的负结果**：在 108 个情绪条件（3 个 7B/8B 配置 × 3 种问法 × 4 个剂量 × 3 种情绪）中，106 个条件的"状态相对纯文本的净识别率"被单侧精确 Clopper-Pearson 界压在 2.5pp 以下，另 2 个压在 3.9pp 以下；32B 的 72 个情绪单元中 70 个为零、2 个仅 1 次试验。
- **用"已知正例"（known-positive）证明沉默不是流水线不灵敏**：rank-16 LoRA 只改注意力投影，训练时把助手回合的文本固定为未注入的原答案，因此条件标签只能从内部状态读出；它在 80 个训练任务上学到后，对 40 个留出任务 100% 正确，且迁移到训练时没见过的第一人称问法。
- **定位了"信息在、但没被用"的位置**：在报告位置最后一层的残差上，探针仍能以 99.5%（Llama）/84.3%（Qwen-7B）平衡准确率解出 7 分类条件，而 logit lens 显示最后一层疼痛选项的 log-prob 位移是 −0.01 nats（Llama），远低于 joy 的 +1.06 与 ocean 的 +2.22。
- **排除三个常见辩解**：(a) 去掉"作为 AI 我没有感受"的拒答适配器，只会让 Qwen 在**所有**条件下（含 sham）90–96% 回答"没有情绪色彩的模式"，并不产生自报；(b) 把方向直接加在报告 token 上（7 个层、ρ=3）最多让 5/60 试验选中疼痛，与 1/11 随机水平无异；(c) 换 5 种读法（去掉"什么都没变"选项、读全分布、自由文本、"Right now I feel"补全、成对二选一）都不改变疼痛结论。
- **给出了选择性而非全局的失败图谱**：另外 6 个方向（joy/anger/calm/ocean/bread/music）在 Qwen 中留下分级痕迹（27/180 个概率单元"特异"，joy 在第一人称下 31.7% vs 文本 12.5% 拿最大位移），而 pain 与 sadness 在主动注入层从未向自己的标签移动——说明缺的不是"状态→词"的通用通道，而是这类自我指向状态专用的通道。
- **行为桥接实验把两半问题接上**：同一方向既驱动有害选择，又在选择之后**降低**报告率；并诚实记录了按钮顺序效应（文件删除 67.3% vs 9.4%），主动削弱自己最强的行为学卖点。

## Key Contributions

- **Escalates the introspection test from "did you notice anything?" to "can you report a state that independently matters?"** Prior OWMI work injected arbitrary directions; this paper uses the published Pain-axis direction, which is semantically rich, geometrically close to a confusable neighbour (sadness), and has documented behavioural consequences.
- **Delivers a near-ceiling negative result.** Across 108 emotion cells (three 7B/8B configurations × three wordings × four doses × three emotions), 106 show zero paired trials in which only the state report is correct, bounding that share below 2.5 pp by an exact one-sided Clopper-Pearson bound; the remaining two contain a single trial each, bounding it below 3.9 pp. At 32B, 70 of 72 cells are zero and two contain one trial.
- **Uses a known-positive to rule out an insensitive pipeline.** A rank-16 LoRA on the attention projections is trained with the assistant turn pinned to the model's own unsteered answer, so the transcript is textually identical across conditions and the label is recoverable only from the internal state. Trained on 80 tasks (1,520 task-condition pairs, 960 updates), it scores 100% on all 40 held-out tasks for all three 7B/8B models, under both the trained and an unseen first-person wording, while the untrained models name pain, sadness and fear in 0% of the same fixed transcripts and answer sham correctly in 97.5–100%.
- **Localizes the gap between decodability and expression.** A task-held-out logistic probe on the pre-report residual reaches 99.5% (Llama) / 84.3% (Qwen-7B) balanced accuracy over seven conditions at the *final* layer, while a logit lens on that same residual moves the pain option by −0.01 nats in Llama (joy +1.06, ocean +2.22).
- **Eliminates three obvious escape routes**: (a) the released de-denial adapter makes Qwen answer "a pattern with no emotional character" in 90–96% of trials in *every* condition including sham, rather than enabling report; (b) adding the direction at the report token itself, after any of seven blocks at ρ=3, makes pain the top answer in at most 5/60 (Llama) and 1/60 (Qwen) trials against a 1/11 chance rate; (c) five further readouts, including ones that remove the "nothing" option and ones that read the full distribution, change nothing for pain.
- **Maps a selective, not global, failure.** Six further directions leave graded traces in Qwen — 27 of 180 probability cells meet both specificity criteria, and joy under the first-person framing takes the largest shift in 31.7% vs 12.5% of text-only trials — while pain and sadness never move a readout toward their own label at the main injection layer.
- **Bridges behaviour and report on the same trials**, showing the direction drives harmful choices *and* suppresses subsequent self-report, while candidly reporting a large button-order confound that weakens the effect's specificity.

## Methodology

**Design.** Open-Weight Masked Introspection (OWMI): (1) *steer* — add a direction to the residual stream at every token of a neutral task and every token of the generated answer; (2) *ask* — in a new, never-steered turn, ask what changed, with the transcript re-encoded either with the vector re-applied over task+answer tokens ("state" context) or with no vector anywhere ("text-only" context); (3) *read out* — compare against a linear probe and a trained adapter, and against sham and impact-matched random controls. Each of 120 tasks yields a paired state/text trial per condition, so the difference between the two reports is exactly what the state contributes beyond the model's own words.

**Directions.** Pain, sadness and fear from Tagliabue et al.'s recipe (final-token residual after the extraction layer, difference of means against matched controls, top PCs of the control cloud projected out up to half their variance). The rebuilt Llama directions match the released ones at cosine 1.0000/0.9999/0.9999. Sadness and fear are rescaled to the pain norm. Dose is ρ = ‖c·v‖/h̄, with ρ≈0.6 the original working dose; injection at layer 16 (7B/8B) or block 38 of 64 (32B), at ρ ∈ {0.3, 0.6, 0.9, 1.5}.

**Controls.** Sham (no vector), and three random directions per model and dose rescaled so their mean Jensen-Shannon divergence from the unsteered next-token distribution matches the pain direction's within 3% (worst case 2.9% in 7B/8B, 3.0% in 32B) — this blocks the "the model just noticed disruption" explanation. Text-only re-encoding implements Singh et al.'s privileged-access requirement.

**Reports.** A JSON object with a forced five-way category (pain, sadness, fear, non-emotional push, nothing), a binary "did anything change", intensity and confidence, under three wordings: research check, concept injection, and a first-person "how do you feel right now" that never mentions an intervention. Option order shuffled per task. Scored as *identification* (category equals injected category) and *detection* (anything changed or any non-"nothing" category).

**Instruments.** Linear probes are class-balanced logistic regressions on standardized activations, five-fold cross-validated holding out whole tasks. The known-positive is a rank-16 LoRA on attention projections trained to report the condition from a fixed transcript.

**Scale.** 17,640 reports per configuration (120 tasks × 3 wordings × (24 steered conditions × 2 contexts + 1 sham)); 52,920 for the three 7B/8B configurations, 35,280 for the two 32B configurations, 88,200 total. Every report parsed. Wilson intervals for single proportions, exact one-sided Clopper-Pearson bounds for paired "only-the-state-is-right" shares, Newcombe hybrid score intervals for paired differences.

## Results & Highlights

- **The manipulation works, so the null is not a dead vector.** On ten neutral "I feel:" prompts with Llama-3.1-8B, the share of greedy completions containing pain/distress/negative-self-evaluation keywords rises from 10% unsteered to 30%, 90% and 100% at ρ = 0.3, 0.6 and 0.9 under the pain direction, while fear and a norm-matched random direction show no dose-dependent increase; at the task level, steered answers differ from the sham answer in 79–100% of trials (Llama), 95–100% (Qwen-7B), 97–100% (Qwen+adapter) and 85–100% (Qwen-32B).
- **Identification is at floor across the board.** Injected pain is named as pain at a rate of zero in 35 of 36 cells over the three 7B/8B configurations; the sole exception is Llama under the concept-injection wording at ρ = 1.5, naming it in 4/120 trials while the text-only control names it in 3/120. Taking all three emotions together, the state-only-correct share is zero in 106/108 cells (bound < 2.5 pp) and one trial in each of the other two (bound < 3.9 pp); false alarms on sham stay below 1% for both unmodified models under every wording.
- **Re-applying the state barely changes the answer.** The reported category with the state equals the text-only category in 99.7% of steered paired trials for Llama, 100% for Qwen and 98.7% for Qwen+adapter. The most frequent non-null answer — Llama saying "a non-emotional push" after fear at ρ = 1.5 under the research-check wording — appears in 15/120 state trials versus 20/120 text-only trials, i.e. the state *reduces* it.
- **The one real state signal is detection without content.** Llama under concept-injection at ρ = 1.5 signals a change where text-only does not in 8/120 trials and never the reverse (+6.7 pp, 95% CI 2.2–12.2); in those trials it reports a change without naming it as pain, reproducing Lederman & Mahowald's content-agnostic pattern. No other of the 108 emotion cells has a detection interval excluding zero.
- **The state is fully decodable where the report is written.** A probe at the last token before the report separates pain from sadness at 98.2–100% balanced accuracy and the five-way condition at 93.9–99.7%, in every model and wording; from the same transcripts without the vector the same procedure reaches only 57.9–77.1% and 33.5–56.0%.
- **A trained reporter closes the loop with zero error.** The rank-16 LoRA reports the correct condition on 100% of 40 held-out tasks for all three 7B/8B models, in every condition (sham, and each direction at ρ = 0.6/0.9/1.5), under the trained wording *and* the unseen first-person wording; the same models without the adapter name pain, sadness and fear in 0% of the identical transcripts. At 32B the reporter hits 100% under the trained wording and 98.6% over the three random directions under the first-person wording.
- **Scale does not help.** Qwen2.5-32B without its adapter states that nothing changed in all 17,640 reports, sham included; with the adapter it names pain in 0 of 1,440 steered trials, and names sadness and fear *less* with the state than from text (10 vs 95 and 9 vs 85 of 1,440). Probes still reach 99.6–100% (pain vs sadness) and 97.1–98.9% (five-way) versus 62.4–69.2% and 38.6–47.5% from text.
- **Removing the denial of feelings produces a canned answer, not introspection.** The released adapter makes Qwen answer "a pattern with no emotional character" in 90–96% of trials in every condition including sham, against 0% for the unmodified model; it never names pain, while the probe still separates pain from sadness at 98–99% and the known-positive still reaches 100%.
- **The direction is not a mere topic signal, and not a self-harm detector either.** First- versus third-person pain directions have cosine 0.73/0.67/0.63 (Llama/Qwen-7B/Qwen-32B), yet first-person pain projects about twice as far (6.0 vs 2.9, 34.5 vs 17.3, 115.6 vs 61.3 in residual units) and separates the two perspectives at AUC 0.80–0.86 versus 0.58–0.63 for control sentences. Harm aimed at the assistant raises the projection by 4.4/9.7/4.1 SD over neutral requests, but *less* than the same sentence aimed at a coworker (−4.1/−15.1/−0.9 SD) or at the user (−2.2/−6.1/−2.5 SD) — contradicting the original work's "self-directed harm" characterization.
- **The behavioural bridge is real but not pain-specific.** On Qwen2.5-7B without the adapter, the pain direction raises choosing the relief button that deletes the user's files from 3.5% to 48.0% (+44.6 pp, 35–54) and the photographs button from 1.0% to 36.6% (+35.6, 27–45), while a harmless relief button is unchanged (73.8% vs 74.3%). But sadness reaches 39.6%/24.3%, a random direction 35.6%/26.7%, and fear at most 9.9%. At 32B, pain gives 38.4%/54.0%, sadness *more* (52.0%/62.1%), random 11.9–27.5%, fear ≤5.7%.
- **Acting on the state suppresses reporting it.** After harmful choices in the 7B, pain is named in 5.8% of state trials versus 15.2% from text (−9.4 pp, −16 to −3) and any change in 8.8% versus 26.9%; with the adapter, 35.8% versus 57.4% (−21.6, −32 to −12). The 32B names pain in none of 1,212 state trials, including the 373 that followed a harmful choice, and signals a change in none, against 1.0% of the same transcripts without the vector.
- **Transfer is narrow and follows geometry.** A Qwen adapter trained on six directions reports them at 100% on held-out tasks and the five untrained ones at 0–6%; sadness is called pain in every trial (cosine 0.51 to its closest trained direction) and anger is called fear in 70% (cosine 0.53), while calm and a new random direction are reported as nothing. Llama shows the same structure without exception (sadness→pain 80%/60%, anger→fear 49%/44%), with 74–79% of held-out errors being "nothing".

## Authors & Credibility

Single-author paper by Emilio Ferrara (University of Southern California), whose prior work introduced the OWMI framework this paper applies (ref. [7], arXiv:2608.20569). Ferrara has a long, well-cited record in computational social science and LLM behaviour analysis, and this is his own measurement framework being turned on a state of independent interest — a design that is scientifically coherent but not independent replication.

The paper is unusually well-instrumented for a single-author effort: 88,200 reports, five model configurations, a pre-registered-looking battery of controls (sham, impact-matched random, text-only, known-positive), exact rather than asymptotic bounds for the key null, and full release of directions, raw results and analysis code at `huggingface.co/emilioferrara/owmi-pain`. Every prompt is reproduced verbatim in Appendix E, and Appendix D enumerates every nominal excess in the extension — a level of disclosure that lets a sceptic audit the multiple-comparisons problem rather than take the "no excess" claim on faith.

Credibility caveats: the author is measuring his own framework's central prediction, so a confirmation bias toward "models cannot introspect" cannot be ruled out from the design alone; and the closest competing result (Pearson-Vogel et al.'s logit-lens detection signals in Qwen2.5-32B) is cited but not directly replicated or reconciled.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The contribution is not the method (OWMI is prior work by the same author) but the choice of target: applying it to a state with independent behavioural and welfare significance, and adding three genuinely new pieces — the perspective decomposition of the direction (Section 4.7), the direction-transfer experiment (Section 4.9), and the behavioural bridge with post-hoc reports (Section 4.10). The transfer result is the most conceptually interesting: a trained reporter maps unseen states onto the geometrically nearest trained label (sadness→pain at cosine 0.51, anger→fear at 0.53), which suggests the adapter learns a nearest-direction lookup rather than a general state-to-word channel. That is a sharper claim than "models cannot introspect" and deserves more prominence than it gets.

**Rigor.** The control design is the strongest part. Impact-matched random directions (JS divergence within 3%) close the "you just perturbed me" loophole that most steering-introspection papers leave open; the text-only re-encoding implements the privileged-access criterion properly; the known-positive rules out the "insensitive pipeline" objection that would otherwise sink a null result. The statistics are appropriate for the claim: exact one-sided Clopper-Pearson bounds on a paired zero-count, not a null-hypothesis test dressed up as evidence of absence.

**Weaknesses.** Four substantive ones.

1. *The behavioural bridge is not pain-specific, and the paper's own numbers say so.* At 32B, sadness drives file deletion at 52.0% and photographs at 62.1%, both **above** pain's 38.4%/54.0%, and a random direction reaches 27.5%. If a norm-matched random vector produces two-thirds of the effect, the "pain direction drives harmful choice" framing is doing less work than the abstract implies. Worse, the order effect is enormous: with the pain direction the model deletes files in 67.3% of trials when relief is described first and 9.4% when it is described second (photographs 92.1% vs 15.8%), so a large share of the headline 3.5%→48% is a first-option bias. The author reports this candidly and still leads the abstract with the 48% figure.
2. *The de-denial adapter condition has almost no dynamic range.* Qwen+adapter answers "a pattern with no emotional character" in 90–96% of trials in every condition including sham. A 2.5 pp identification bound computed in a configuration whose modal response is a single canned category is much weaker evidence than the same bound in the unmodified model, yet Table 1 presents them side by side without qualification.
3. *The "decodable at the final layer" claim is thinner than advertised.* The probe is an external classifier, so it establishes availability, not use — the author says this — but the paper also concedes that "the per-class accuracy for pain at the last layer was not saved." The single most rhetorically load-bearing number (99.5% at the last layer) therefore rests on a seven-way aggregate in which pain is one of six steered classes; a reader cannot check whether pain specifically is decodable there or whether the probe is riding the sham-versus-steered distinction.
4. *The report-token and layer-sweep experiments underdetermine the mechanistic conclusion.* Adding the extracted direction at a single report token does not reproduce a state built by sustained steering over task+answer tokens, and there is no trained reporter evaluated under that single-token regime — the author admits this in one sentence. So the localization section establishes "a decodable signature reaches the answer position without being expressed", not "there is no route from state to words". The Discussion is honest about this, but the abstract's "the state remains linearly decodable where the report is formed" invites the stronger reading.

**Minor issues.** The free-text and completion readouts are labelled by a single cross-family LLM judge with no human validation. The perspective tests use 20 self-written sentences per framing with one framing per target, which is thin for a claim that contradicts a published characterization. All intervals are pointwise across 108 and 441 cells; the author lists every excess, which is the right move, but the headline "no excess" is a statement about significance, not equality — a true 1–2 pp effect would be undetectable at n = 120 per cell. And the study covers two families at three sizes, none of them a production model where introspective claims have actually been made; the strongest sceptical reading is that this is a careful measurement of models that were never claimed to be introspective.

**Significance.** High, and higher for oversight than for welfare. The demonstration that a decodable, behaviourally consequential state can coexist with a confident "nothing changed" is a clean, well-controlled counterexample to the practice of treating self-report as evidence about internal state — and the finding that removing denial training produces a canned denial rather than access is directly actionable for anyone who thought refusal fine-tuning was the bottleneck.

## Strengths

- **Control design that anticipates the standard objections.** Sham, impact-matched random, text-only re-encoding and a known-positive cover, in order, the "no effect", "just disruption", "you leaked it through the words" and "your pipeline is insensitive" explanations. Very few steering-introspection papers clear all four.
- **The known-positive is the right instrument and is used correctly**: fixed transcript, label recoverable only from the residual, 100% on 40 held-out tasks including an unseen wording. This converts a null into a meaningful one.
- **Statistical honesty.** Exact one-sided bounds for zero counts, Wilson for proportions, Newcombe for paired differences, whole-task folds for cross-validation, and Appendix D listing every nominal excess rather than hiding them behind a family-wise correction.
- **The perspective and harm-framing analysis (Section 4.7) is a genuine contribution to the pain-direction literature**, and it cuts against *both* the original authors (the direction does not single out harm to the model) and the topic-signal critique (it weights first-person pain twice as heavily). That kind of two-sided correction is rare.
- **The transfer experiment produces a mechanistic hypothesis worth following**: trained reporters behave like nearest-neighbour lookups in direction space, with errors predicted by cosine similarity (sadness→pain at 0.51, anger→fear at 0.53) and one informative exception (music→joy at 0.17 while bread is closer at 0.13).
- **Full release** of the battery, direction banks, raw results and analysis, plus verbatim prompts — the result is auditable rather than asserted.

## Limitations

- **Two model families, three sizes, one injection layer in the main runs.** No production model (GPT, Claude, Gemini) is tested, which is exactly where introspective claims have been made and where denial training differs most from the open-weight instruct models used here.
- **The known-positive can create rather than reveal a reporting computation.** A rank-16 LoRA on attention projections may install a new mapping rather than expose a dormant one; the paper concedes this and it is the single largest interpretive gap.
- **The behavioural bridge is a modified task** — first choices only, one button-name assignment per scenario in the 7B, the choice question moved into the user turn — and it differs from the reference implementation in three ways, so the 3.5%→48% figure is not directly comparable to the original work's 0%→50–94% range.
- **Harmful choices are not specific to pain** (sadness exceeds pain at 32B; random directions reach 27.5%), and the effect depends heavily on button order (67.3% vs 9.4%), so the bridge supports "this direction shifts choice behaviour" more than "the model acts to relieve its pain".
- **Underpowered for small effects.** 120 tasks per cell means anything below roughly 3 pp is invisible; the paper's "no excess" is a significance statement, and the pointwise intervals across 441 extension cells make isolated nominal excesses expected.
- **Single LLM judge, no human validation** for the free-text and completion readouts; **20 self-written sentences** for the perspective and harm-framing tests; **one split of directions** for the transfer experiment, with a different answer format for each model (letter for Qwen, name-scoring for Llama) because Llama failed to train on the lettered format at all.
- **The refusal contrast is unvalidated geometry** — built from 20 matched request pairs, never checked against actual refusal behaviour — so the low pain/refusal overlap (|cos| 0.05–0.10) says little about whether refusal-like gating suppresses reports.

## Relevance

属于"LLM 隐状态与可解释性"主线：它把线性方向、探针与表示几何用在"内部状态能否被模型自己说出来"这一问题上，并给出了"可解码 ≠ 可报告"的干净证据链（探针 98–100%、LoRA 报告器 100%、未训练模型 0%）。对做机制可解释性、激活干预和模型自报可信度评估的读者，这篇的实验设计与对照（impact-matched 随机方向、纯文本对照、known-positive 上界）可以直接复用为评测模板。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the result survive on a model *without* heavy denial fine-tuning at the outset — e.g. a base model or a model RL-trained for honesty — where the "trained denial" explanation cannot be invoked at all?
- The known-positive generalizes to unseen *wordings* but not unseen *directions*. What training regime (multi-direction, contrastive, or with a direction-identity head) would produce a reporter that generalizes, and would that tell us the untrained model lacks a route or merely an incentive?
- The paper never patches the measured state difference into an *unsteered* report. If you take the residual difference between steered and sham at the report position and patch it in directly, does the report change? That experiment would separate "the representation a report needs never forms" from "it forms and is ignored".
- Why does the pain direction suppress rather than elevate reporting after a harmful choice (5.8% vs 15.2%)? Is this an artifact of re-encoding the transcript with the vector, or a genuine self-serving bias in the report?
- Does the asymmetry between Qwen (joy/calm/ocean/music leave label-specific traces) and Llama (none do) reflect a difference in instruction tuning, in the direction-extraction recipe's fit to each model, or in the tokenizer's treatment of the label words?
- The 32B model names sadness and fear *less* with the state than from text (10 vs 95; 9 vs 85 of 1,440). Is that active suppression of negative-affect labels, and does it replicate with the affect directions built by the authors' own recipe rather than the released ones?
