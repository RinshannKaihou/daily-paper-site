4-bit weights roughly double batch-1 decode — then the gain collapses to 1.02× at batch 256.

一份证据盘点：9B–20B 开源模型的量化服务记录中，速度与精度几乎从未在同一次实验里同时给出——4-bit 的 2× 只在 batch 1 成立。

Twice as Fast, Until It Isn't: Quantized Serving of Mid-Size Open LLMs
Smit Kadvani, Kriti Faujdar, Spurthi Tallam · Independent researchers, US · 2026-11-02 · arXiv:2611.04380
  • Quantization
  • LLM serving
  • FP8 / W4A16
  • Benchmarking methodology

◆Key metric · speedup at batch 256

1.02×

Gemma 3 12B IT on one H100, batch 256: 14.07 s → 13.81 s with TorchAO INT4. The same build is 1.37× at batch 1.

◆Why this matters

If you self-host a 9B–20B model, the promised 2× only arrives at batch 1 — where serving throughput is lowest.

▲Critical edge

Even the two privileged checkpoints never measured speed and accuracy in one experiment: the timing is vLLM on H100, the score PyTorch lm-eval on Transformers.

01

How the audit was built

  1. 01

    Scope. Seven open-weight models from 9B to 20B (Qwen2.5-14B, Qwen3-14B, Gemma 3 12B, Phi-4, Mistral NeMo, Ministral 3 14B, gpt-oss-20b), crossed with seven recipes (FP8, INT8, AWQ, GPTQ, INT4, NVFP4, MXFP4) and both vLLM and SGLang. Cutoff 5 Oct 2026.

  2. 02

    Gate. A timing counts only if engine, GPU, checkpoint and workload are named; a score only if the same source reports a same-harness BF16 baseline. Strict on purpose — it is why the admitted set is tiny, and why the headline exists.

  3. 03

    Roofline. At batch 1 every token re-reads all weights, so decode is bandwidth-bound and 4-bit quarters the traffic: 2×, not 4×, because dequantization, attention and KV reads don't shrink. At batch 256 the matmul goes compute-bound while W4A16 still computes in 16 bits.

  4. 04

    Noise yardstick. No source reports a standard error, so the authors compute √(p(1−p)/n) for one run: ±3.9 pts on HumanEval (n=164), 0.8–1.3 on GSM8K, 0.34–0.39 on full MMLU.

  5. 05

    Grading. Table 3 scores each claim by independent sources × models × GPUs, with explicit “None” entries for AMD/Gaudi timings, FP4 speedups and FP8 above batch 1.

No experiments were run. Source collection was AI-assisted; every number was re-checked against its citation on 5 Oct 2026, and the authors state that no source meets all seven of their own reporting rules.

Crossover, predicted then seen

On an H20 with Qwen3-14B and SGLang, FP8 W8A8 goes 2.06× → 1.68× from short to 30,720-token input, while AWQ-INT4 goes 2.05× → 1.42×. At batch 1 the recipes tie; at long prompt FP8 leads by 18% in absolute throughput (880.72 vs 744.74 tok/s).

Biggest number is not a recipe

gpt-oss-20b, one H100 SXM, 1000 ShareGPT prompts: vLLM 0.11.0 at 16,493.75 tok/s vs SGLang 0.5.4 at 5,626.99 tok/s — a 2.93× engine gap at default settings, with no BF16 reference at all. The paper grades it “Low: 1 source, untuned.”

05

What holds, what doesn’t

Strengths

  • Asks the real question. How much faster, how much worse — and says “nobody measured that” when it is true.
  • Predictive, not descriptive. The roofline anticipates the FP8 long-prompt crossover before the table shows it.
  • A transferable yardstick. √(p(1−p)/n) takes thirty seconds to apply to any quantized model card.
  • Candid grading. Table 3 writes “None” for AMD, Gaudi and FP4 instead of laundering absence into a weak claim.
  • Sourcing discipline. Fifty references, retrieval date pinned, measured and inferred quantities footnoted apart.

Limitations

  • No new measurements. The cheap FP8-at-batch-256 test that would settle the thesis is left undone.
  • Four timing groups, three metrics. The pooled “1.35–2.12×” is a direction, not a distribution.
  • Unpaired SEs on paired evals. The threshold inflates the noise band; no source publishes per-question data to fix it.
  • No cost, energy, KV-cache or tensor parallelism. These are the levers that actually move the crossover.
  • A contaminated row kept. Gemma AWQ is scored on questions it was calibrated on, with only a footnote.

Open questions

  1. Q1Does a public FP8 W8A8 checkpoint actually hold its speedup at batch 256 on H100 — or does KV traffic eat it the way it eats INT4’s 1.02×?
  2. Q2What is the paired (McNemar) standard error on the +6.10 HumanEval delta? Does the noise verdict survive a test that respects pairing?
  3. Q3Why does the same W4A16 recipe move GSM8K by −1.06 in one harness and +3.94 in another, with BF16 baselines 20.77 points apart?
