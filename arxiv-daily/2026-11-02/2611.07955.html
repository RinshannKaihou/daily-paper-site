01

Why rotation stalls — and what to add

MapAnything · 64 quantized linears

① Diagnose

Two independent faults

S1 — all tokens share one per-tensor activation grid; the scale token's range is ~6× smaller. S2 — that token multiplies the metric factor of every pixel.

② Screen

One forward, one backward

Γ multiplies output criticality by the grid deficit. It needs no quantizer and no benchmark run.

Γ = (λ_s / λ̄_patch) · (max‖h_t‖ / ‖h_s‖ − 1)

③ Correct

Rotate, bias, one row

Random Hadamard rotation for heavy channel tails, a token-uniform bias β, and a single-row affine term b_s written only by the 8 global blocks.

The proof in one line

  • Every rotation or smoothing method is an element of x′=(x+a)M, with W′=M⁻¹W.
  • M right-multiplies every row identically and a broadcasts, so the group has no row-indexed freedom.
  • Orthogonality preserves ‖h_t‖, so all pairwise token SNR ratios stay fixed: the gain is common-mode only.
  • Hence κ_s is a property of the learned representation, not of the orthogonal basis — more rotation tuning cannot close the gap.

What it costs

  • RᵀW folded offline; online rotation only at QKV and the two FFN projections.
  • b_s is a single-row add in 8 blocks — the integer GEMM is untouched.
  • β folds into the existing layer bias, so no extra kernel.
  • Total storage: |B_g|(5d + 2d_ff) scalars, with calibration on 64 two-view samples.

The authors deliberately install b_s on two models their own audit says to skip: −0.02 and +0.04 versus +3.2 on the design target. The criterion predicts when the method is unnecessary, not just that it works.

03

What holds up, what does not

Read as a pair

Strengths

04
  • Lemma 1 is load-bearing. It explains why QuaRot, SpinQuant and SmoothQuant plateau at the same place, instead of only observing it.
  • The negative control is exemplary. Applying a mechanism the theory says to omit, on two models it was not designed on, converts "it works" into "it predicts when it is unnecessary."
  • The metric / up-to-scale split makes S2 falsifiable, and the predicted asymmetry actually appears in the tables.
  • 34.2% vs 0.010% generalizes: block-wise losses are blind to any low-dimensional output-critical pathway.

Limitations

04
  • No real W4A4 kernel. Accuracy and efficiency live in disjoint regimes; whether 92.8 survives a fused INT4 engine is untested.
  • No QuantVGGT / QVGGT / FGQ comparison. FGQ's Fisher-guided affine calibration is the nearest relative of b_s and the missing competitive baseline.
  • n = 3, one is the design target. DA3's λ_s = 0 is definitional — measured on the dense-ray path that never reads the camera token.
  • Single seed, no error bars. Γ has three values and a qualitative threshold; the transfer protocol also varies estimator strength.
04

Open questions

For the next reader
Q1

What does INT4 actually cost? An INT4 TensorRT engine with the b_s epilogue would show whether the row add is free in a memory-bound kernel or a measurable tax.

Q2

Does FGQ already capture the +3.2? If Fisher-guided affine calibration does, the contribution shrinks from a mechanism to a criterion.

Q3

Where is Γ's boundary? With values of 0, 0 and 1.35×10⁵, a model at 10²–10³ would reveal a threshold, a dose-response, or a coincidence.

Q4

Is the transfer result about Γ or about the estimator? The missing control is closed-form b_s on MapAnything, where it currently gets 40 Adam steps.
