Why this matters

Low-bit quantization quietly degrades models. This measures how much accuracy calibration data buys back — and warns the gain may be test-distribution exposure.

! Critical edge

Gains of +3.86 pp on calibrated families versus +0.20 pp elsewhere suggest the recipe is less “better calibration” than training on the test distribution — a confound the paper concedes but cannot separate.

01

Method

Compare at the width where the model still works

Each quantizer is measured at its lowest width with usable accuracy — not at a common bit width where half the field has already collapsed.

Only the calibration text changes. Every model / method / width / seed combination is quantized twice from the same checkpoint — once with a Pile control, once with FragCal-DC — while the update rule, bit width, group size and evaluation stay fixed. The comparison is paired, not a survey of literature numbers.

1

Build the mix

208 documents: 128 Pile-10k anchor + 32 MMLU + 32 ARC/OpenBookQA + 16 HellaSwag. The supplements are the three tasks with the lowest 2-bit retention — MMLU 78.2%, ARC-C 84.3%, HellaSwag 86.0% — sized by a {16, 32, 64} dose sweep.

2

Compare at functional width

The lowest width with ten-task AVG > 0.35: W3 for GPTQ and AWQ (all six W2 baselines ≤ 0.32), W2 for SignRound V2, OmniQuant and AQLM. Below the floor, swapping calibration data does nothing.

3

Pair and repeat

Six dense models (1.7B–8B) × six quantizers × W2/W3/W4 × four calibration seeds × ten lm-eval tasks. 96 cross-method records, plus a Llama-3.1-70B scale test.

03

Balance

What survives scrutiny

The reporting discipline is unusually good. The panel size and recipe construction are where the claims get thin.

Strengths holds up

  1. Regime-aware comparison. Locating each quantizer’s functional width before measuring calibration response is the right move — and it reconciles positive accuracy results with the older perplexity-based literature.
  2. One harness, paired seeds. All six quantizers are re-run from the same checkpoints with four paired calibration seeds, not quoted from prior papers.
  3. Weak results stay in. Gemma-2-9B (−5.62 pp), the GSM8K regression, the OmniQuant interval that crosses zero, and the p = 0.0625 floor are all disclosed.
  4. A useful falsification. Reconstruction MSE and accuracy move in opposite directions (AQLM: MSE +5.76%, accuracy +0.73 pp); a second optimization pass degrades 15/20 accuracy cells.

Limitations open exposure

  1. n = 5–6 related models. The sign-flip floor is 0.0625 at n = 5; one dissenting model (Gemma-2-9B, excluded from aggregates) reverses the direction.
  2. Task-targeting confound. Gains concentrate on calibrated families, and the overlap audit found 0–7 question-key matches per task per seed without measuring their accuracy effect.
  3. Optimizer class unidentified. The grid cannot separate iteration from baseline accuracy, codebook, objective, or initialization.
  4. Under-ablated recipe. A single-seed G16 scan over five models chose the mix; no per-component ablation, no synthetic-QA control, and token budgets are not equalized.

Also: the flagship number is measured on SignRound V2, an Intel/AutoRound method with an in-house XPU port. Only the calibration generator is released, and no independent replication exists.

04

Open questions

What would settle it

Three experiments the paper leaves open — each one could move the headline.

contamination

How much of +1.83 pp is item overlap?

Strip every calibration document whose question matches an eval item, restore matched counts, and re-measure.

mechanism

Would a format-matched synthetic corpus reproduce it?

If wrong answers keep 90% of the gain, MCQ-shaped synthetic items should get most of the way there.

scale

Why does the direction flip at Gemma and at 70B?

Gemma-2-9B loses 5.62 pp under OmniQuant; GPTQ gains +2.33 pp at 70B while SignRound V2 gains +0.86.
