Unsupervised environment design · learner trainability

A better curriculum cannot compensate for a learner that is losing its ability to learn.

PLR keeps proposing harder levels while its critic decays. Two cheap critic-side fixes improve every tier — and the gap widens with training horizon.

01 · Diagnosis

The student was the bottleneck

On Kinetix Large, PLR keeps proposing harder levels while the critic's value ordering degrades, weights inflate and representations homogenize — final return hides all three.

wk+1 = U(wk; Dk) pk+1 = C(pk; Hk+1)

The curriculum chooses the next distribution; the learner's state feeds back into that choice — so a sick student also corrupts the teacher.

  • Spearman(V, return)
    0.76→0.92

    The critic stops ranking states in the order they actually pay off.

  • Critic weight magnitude
    1.30×10²→0.98×10²

    Parameters inflate as the curriculum hardens.

  • Effective rank
    72vs≈83

    Baseline decays 98→72; the stabilized learner holds ≈83.

PLR beats domain randomization on return while looking worse on gradient norms, representation similarity and value ordering. Better curricula can coexist with sicker students.

Report alongside return: parameter and update statistics, value predictions, neuron activity, representation quality — and separate the curriculum rule from the distribution it actually realizes.

02 · Method

Two critic-side changes, nothing else

Hold the curriculum rule, architecture, actor objective and interaction budget fixed; change only how the critic is shaped.

  1. 01
    Testbed

    PPO actor–critic on Kinetix (Jax2D), three tiers; 2048 envs, 2B interactions, 5 seeds, IQM with 95% stratified bootstrap CIs.

  2. 02
    Curricula

    Domain randomization, PLR (4k-level buffer, rank priority), SFL (learnability), ACCEL (PLR + 5 edits per step).

  3. 03
    Fix A — weight clipping

    Project critic parameters into an ℓ∞ ball after every update; factor 2, chosen from a 1/2/4/8 sweep.

  4. 04
    Fix B — HL-Gauss

    51-bin categorical value loss with Gaussian-smoothed targets; prediction read out as the histogram expectation.

  5. 05
    Stress tests

    2B→5B steps, 128→4096 envs, zero-shot cross-complexity transfer, lifelong S→M→S.

Held fixed: curriculum rule · architecture · actor objective · interaction budget

04 · Edges

What holds up, and where it strains

Strengths

  • Reframing with teeth. Teacher and student are one coupled system, not a curriculum leaderboard.
  • Unusual breadth. Four curricula × three tiers × two horizons × five parallelism regimes, plus transfer and lifelong.
  • Cheap to deploy. Two critic-side changes; no curriculum redesign, no extra environment interaction.
  • Candid ablation. WC ≈ WC+HL is reported plainly; ReGen, spectral norm and weight decay are called weak.

Limitations

  • One simulator, one algorithm. Kinetix 2D physics with PPO; no discrete-action, sparse-reward or LLM RL.
  • Critic-free gap. GRPO/RLVR have no value head, so neither intervention maps directly.
  • Mechanism unidentified. Clipping changes parameter scale, effective step size and feature scale at once.
  • Visual evidence only. No tabulated numbers, no significance test for the key WC vs WC+HL contrast.

05 · Open questions

What the paper leaves open

Q1

Does it transfer to critic-free RL?

GRPO/RLVR have no value head; would clipping the policy or shared trunk reproduce the gain?

Q2

Is weight clipping just a learning-rate fix?

Sweep LR, Adam ε and initialization at matched weight norm to separate scale from step size.

Q3

Can the diagnostics warn early?

Does Spearman(V, return) bend before the return curve, and with what lead time?

Q4

Does the thesis hold at 10B steps and 1M envs?

The paper predicts stabilization matters more, but tests only to 4096 environments.
