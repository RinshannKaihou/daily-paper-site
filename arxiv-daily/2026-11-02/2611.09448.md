> **一句话总结：** 作者提出 RCEA 分层诊断框架，把 RLVR 中被混为一谈的"全局 token 熵"拆成奖励结果熵、语义解法模式熵和局部协方差贡献三部分；在 Qwen2.5-3B/7B 上对 6 种 RLVR 目标做受控扫描后发现熵轨迹与准确率排序完全脱钩（GTPO 把熵压到近 0 仍有 61.0%/71.0% 的 MATH500 准确率），而验证器失败轨迹的 token 熵始终高于成功轨迹（12 个端点中 10 个的配对置信区间排除相等），且所有可报告的训练端点都出现了正确解法模式收缩（保留率 83.7%–99.9%）。

> **TL;DR:** Global token entropy is a bad diagnostic for RLVR: across six objectives (GRPO, DAPO, Clip-Cov, KL-Cov, EP-GRPO, GTPO) at 3B and 7B on Qwen2.5 with an 8k Skywork-OR1 subset, entropy trajectories do not order MATH500/AMC23/AIME/Minerva accuracy — GTPO drives entropy to ~0 yet still scores 61.0% (3B) and 71.0% (7B) on MATH500, while Clip-Cov wins 3B macro accuracy (27.75% vs 23.05% base) and KL-Cov wins 7B (35.72% vs 19.53%). The paper's real payload is the decomposition: failed trajectories carry 1.0–3.5× the token entropy of successful ones (all 12 endpoints >1, 10/12 paired CIs exclude equality), a first-order covariance approximation predicts the sign of adjacent-checkpoint entropy change in 42/44 transitions (95.5%, R²=0.937), and the q99 covariance tail holds only 4.6–18.2% of positive mass in successful prefixes vs 70.4–99.3% in failed ones.

## 核心贡献

- **指出并系统刻画了 RLVR 中的"熵—性能不一致"**：六个目标在 3B/7B 两档上产生差异极大的全局熵轨迹（GTPO 在 25% 进度时熵已趋近 0，KL-Cov 保留大量后期熵），但端点排序随模型规模和指标变化，且与熵排序无关。因此高全局熵对更好的 RLVR 既非必要也非充分。
- **提出 RCEA 分层诊断框架**：把"序列熵"与"训练中实际记录的 token 平均熵"区分开，给出精确的奖励分解 $H(Y|x)=h_b(p_+)+p_+H(Y|R{=}1,x)+p_-H(Y|R{=}0,x)$，再在成功轨迹内部按"过程有效性 V"和"语义解法模式 Z"继续细分，得到六项精确分解式（式 7）。
- **给出"正确解法模式坍缩"的严格定义**：以成功模式条件分布的有效模式数 $N_{\text{eff}}=\exp H(Z|R{=}1,V{=}1,x)$ 及其相对初始策略的保留率来定义坍缩，并明确把"错误减少""过程无效样本被剔除""同一策略的实现方式标准化"三种表面相似的现象与真正的模式收缩区分开。
- **把总体统计量与局部协方差机制接上**：用 $\Delta h \approx -\mathrm{Cov}(\log\pi, \Delta z)$ 做相邻 checkpoint 的有限差分预测，并按奖励结果分层统计正协方差贡献的 q99 尾部集中度，形成"局部如何锐化 → 在哪个奖励层锐化"的诊断链。
- **实测了模式保留与大 k 覆盖的关系（探索性）**：用通过人工审计的冻结语义划分测量保留率，报告其与 MATH500 pass@64 的正相关（3B: Spearman ρ=0.812, n=6；7B: ρ=1.000, n=5），但明确标注为探索性而非因果结论。

## Key Contributions

- **Diagnoses an entropy–performance inconsistency and makes it systematic rather than anecdotal.** Six RLVR objectives at two scales produce wildly different global-entropy trajectories (GTPO collapses to ~0 by 25% progress; KL-Cov retains late-stage entropy), yet no endpoint ranking is stable across scale and metric, and none follows the entropy ordering. The claim is sharper than "entropy is noisy": high global entropy is shown to be neither necessary nor sufficient for better RLVR.
- **Introduces RCEA, a three-level diagnostic hierarchy.** Level 1 separates complete-trajectory Shannon entropy from the length-normalized average token entropy that training logs actually report, with an exact reward decomposition (Prop. 1) and a parallel token-level decomposition $H_{\text{tok}} = p_+ H_{\text{tok},+} + p_- H_{\text{tok},-}$ that deliberately has no $H(R|x)$ term because it partitions an expectation, not a distribution.
- **Formalizes correct-solution mode collapse.** Using a deterministic semantic partition $Z=g_x(Y)$ over verifier-successful, process-valid trajectories, it defines effective mode count and retention $Ret^+_t = N^+_{\text{eff},t}/N^+_{\text{eff},0}$, and argues that only contraction of $q_\theta(z|R{=}1,V{=}1,x)$ removes distinct valid strategies — everything else is error removal, validity filtering, or realization standardization.
- **Bridges population-level entropy to local covariance updates.** Eq. (10) gives the first-order identity $\Delta h(s) \approx -\mathrm{Cov}_{v\sim\pi}(\log\pi_\theta(v|s), \Delta z(v))$, and the paper tests it as a predictor rather than assuming it: 42/44 transitions correct sign, slope 0.993, R²=0.937.
- **Operationalizes the framework with audited instruments** — a 200-prompt fixed probe sampled at the base policy plus four training milestones, a frozen semantic partition that passed an independent adjudicated human audit, and a reward-stratified endpoint covariance proxy.

## Methodology

**Setup.** Qwen2.5-3B and Qwen2.5-7B, one epoch, one seed, on the same 8,000-example subset of Skywork-OR1-RL-Data. Eight on-policy completions per prompt at temperature 1.0 and top-p 0.95; reward is purely the normalized equivalence of the final boxed answer. All objectives share the same LoRA adapter configuration, optimizer and sampling setup *within* each scale, so the objective is the only varying factor. Training curves are normalized to one-epoch progress.

**Baselines.** Untrained base policy plus six objectives: GRPO, DAPO, Clip-Cov, KL-Cov, EP-GRPO, GTPO.

**Evaluation.** Greedy accuracy on AMC23, AIME24, AIME25, MATH500 and Minerva Math, plus MATH500 pass@64 with the standard unbiased estimator. Macro average weights the five benchmarks equally.

**The three-level decomposition.**
1. *Sequence vs token.* $H_\theta(Y|x)$ is the entropy of the full trajectory distribution; the logged statistic is $\bar h_\theta(y,x) = \frac{1}{T}\sum_t H(\pi_\theta(\cdot|s_t))$. Prop. 1 splits the former exactly into binary reward entropy $h_b(p_+)$ plus $p_\pm$-weighted conditional entropies. The token version splits without an $H(R|x)$ term — an honest distinction most papers elide.
2. *Process validity and semantic modes.* $R=1$ certifies verifier acceptance, not step-level correctness; $V=u_x(Y)$ is a frozen-judge proxy for process validity, audited against human labels. Within $R{=}1,V{=}1$, $Z=g_x(Y)$ partitions trajectories by core solution strategy, giving the exact chain-rule split $H(Y|C_x) = H(Z|C_x) + E_Z[H(Y|Z,C_x)]$ — between-strategy vs within-strategy realization entropy. Composing with Prop. 1 yields the six-term hierarchy of Eq. (7).
3. *Local covariance.* $\Delta h(s) \approx -\mathrm{Cov}(\log\pi, \Delta z)$; positive covariance decreases local entropy. The paper is explicit that Eq. (10) says nothing about whether the concentration is *useful*, and that reward conditioning supplies the "where" while covariance supplies the "how."

**Estimators.** A 200-prompt fixed probe (sampled once at the base policy, then re-evaluated at four milestones) supports reward-conditioned entropy and mode-retention estimates; this keeps the prompt set fixed across checkpoints, which is the right call for a longitudinal diagnostic. The covariance prediction is evaluated over 44 adjacent-checkpoint transitions from 11 nonfailed runs using the exact full-vocabulary covariance (not a sampled approximation). The reward-stratified endpoint proxy computes the q99 share of positive chosen-token covariance separately per reward stratum, so the numbers compare *concentration*, not absolute covariance mass.

## Results & Highlights

- **Entropy does not order accuracy (RQ1).** On MATH500 greedy, GTPO drives training entropy near zero early yet retains **61.0% (3B)** and **71.0% (7B)**, versus **65.4% (3B, Clip-Cov)** and **77.2% (7B, DAPO)** — a 4.4- and 6.2-point gap that the entropy ordering gets backwards. Best 3B five-benchmark macro accuracy is Clip-Cov at **27.75% vs 23.05% for the base policy (+4.70 pts)** and 24.43% for GRPO; at 7B the leader flips to KL-Cov at **35.72% vs 19.53% base (+16.19 pts)**, ahead of DAPO's 33.44%.
- **Failed trajectories are consistently higher-entropy (RQ2).** The broad ratio $\rho_H = H_{\text{tok},-}/H_{\text{tok},+}$ exceeds 1 at **all 12 displayed endpoints**, but the two aggregates draw on different prompt sets (successful coverage 40.5–53.0%, failed coverage 72.5–85.5%). The common-question paired analysis — 40–57 eligible questions per endpoint, bootstrap CIs — keeps **all 12 estimates above 1 and excludes equality for 10 of 12**; only GTPO-3B and GTPO-7B overlap 1.
- **Local covariance predicts entropy change almost exactly (RQ4a).** Over **44 adjacent-checkpoint transitions from 11 nonfailed runs**, the approximation gets the sign right in **42 cases (95.5%)**, with Spearman ρ=0.986, Pearson r=0.968, slope 0.993, R²=0.937 and median relative error **12.7%** — points hug the y=x identity line, so it captures magnitude, not just direction.
- **Positive covariance is tail-concentrated in failed prefixes (RQ4b).** The q99 tail accounts for only **4.6–18.2%** of positive chosen-token covariance within successful-prefix strata but **70.4–99.3%** within failed-prefix strata, gaps of **65.8–83.7 pp** at both scales and for every objective. Base policy shows the same asymmetry more weakly: 2.4% → 61.2% at 3B (+58.8 pp) and 2.7% → 43.2% at 7B (+40.4 pp).
- **Every reportable trained endpoint contracts correct-solution modes (RQ3).** Audited effective-mode retention spans **83.7%–99.9% at 3B** and **85.9%–96.2% at 7B**. Retention correlates positively with MATH500 pass@64 (Spearman **ρ=0.812, n=6** at 3B; **ρ=1.000, n=5** at 7B), but the ordering is not monotone in accuracy: EP-GRPO has the highest retention at both scales yet not the highest greedy accuracy, and **DAPO-7B retains fewer modes than GRPO-7B (88.8% vs 89.6%) while scoring far higher on MATH500 (77.2% vs 71.8%)**.
- **A genuine training failure is documented, not hidden.** Clip-Cov-7B collapses from **60.8% MATH500 at step 3,600 to 0.2% at step 4,000**, coinciding with completion lengths saturating the token limit and the boxed-answer rate falling from **95.2% to 1.0%** — a self-reinforcing near-all-failure regime where most updates activate neither reward-variance learning signals nor the covariance mask.

## Authors & Credibility

Song Yu, Li Li (corresponding) and Zhenming Wu, all at the School of Computer and Information Science, Southwest University, Chongqing. This is a small, single-institution group, not a frontier lab, and the paper reads accordingly: modest compute (LoRA, 8k examples, one epoch, one seed), but unusually careful instrumentation. The group has a prior stake in this exact line — EP-GRPO (Yu, Li, Zhao, Yang, arXiv:2605.04960, 2026) is one of the six compared objectives, so the sweep is partly a self-comparison; to their credit EP-GRPO does not come out on top in accuracy, and the paper says so. The theoretical content (Props. 1, the chain-rule hierarchy, Eq. 10) is standard information theory correctly applied rather than novel mathematics — the contribution is the decomposition *and* the measurement discipline around it. Citations are current through 2025–2026 and accurately represent the entropy-mechanism literature (Cui et al. 2025, Wang et al. 2025b, Yue et al. 2025, Simoni et al. 2025). The stated limitations section is honest about single seed, single model family, judge-based validity and the absence of causal validation.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The framing — "entropy collapse should be diagnosed by *which* uncertainty is removed" — is a real conceptual contribution and lands better than most entropy-dynamics papers, which typically propose another entropy regularizer. The exact decomposition itself is bookkeeping (chain rule applied three times), but the *choice* of what to condition on (reward outcome, then process validity, then semantic mode) is the intellectual content, and the resulting six-term Eq. (7) is a genuinely useful checklist. The mode-collapse definition is the strongest single idea: distinguishing "we removed errors" from "we removed strategies" is the question practitioners actually care about, and prior work (Yue et al. 2025) could only gesture at it via large-k coverage.

**Rigor.** Mixed, with real highs and real soft spots. The covariance prediction is the most convincing result in the paper — 42/44 signs, slope 0.993, R²=0.937 on full-vocabulary covariance is a hard number to fake, and reporting median relative error (12.7%) alongside R² is the kind of honesty most papers skip. The paired common-question analysis for RQ2 is exactly the right control, and the authors correctly note its blind spot (prompts dominated by a single outcome drop out). Against that: **the RQ3 headline is thin**. Retention is reported as 83.7%–99.9%, and calling 99.9% (EP-GRPO-3B) a "contraction" is technically true and practically meaningless — a 0.1% drop in effective mode count is inside any plausible estimator noise, yet the abstract's "every reportable trained endpoint exhibits some contraction" inherits rhetorical weight from the 83.7% end of the range. The pass@64 association is weaker still: at 3B the pass@64 values are 92.8, 93.0, 91.2, 93.2, 93.6, 93.2 (plus GTPO's 85.6) — a spread of a couple of points near ceiling, and a Spearman ρ of 0.812 over n=6 in that regime is close to noise-mining. The paper flags it as "exploratory," which is the right word, but the abstract still promotes it.

**Baselines and fairness.** Six objectives is a good field, and holding LoRA/optimizer/sampling fixed within scale is the right design. Two fairness caveats matter. First, **Clip-Cov-7B is reported at step 3,600, pre-failure** — the caption says so explicitly (good), but it means the 7B table mixes a pre-collapse checkpoint for one method with final checkpoints for the rest, so KL-Cov's 35.72% macro "win" at 7B is against a field where one competitor was scored before it fell over. Second, **LoRA-only training is a significant scope restriction** that is never discussed: entropy trajectories, covariance structure and mode retention under a low-rank adapter may differ qualitatively from full-finetune RLVR, which is how GRPO/DAPO/Clip-Cov are actually used. The 8k-example, one-epoch budget also means these are short runs; "collapse" here is observed over a compressed horizon.

**Missing ablations.** No sensitivity analysis on the semantic partition $g_x$: retention numbers depend entirely on a frozen classifier whose human-audit accuracy is asserted but never quantified in the main text (no agreement rate, no κ). No sensitivity to the q99 cutoff — and the paper concedes that for GTPO failed-prefix strata the "q99" selection actually captures ~58–59% of tokens, which is not a tail at all and makes the GTPO row of Table 2 not comparable to the others on the stated metric. No probe-size ablation (200 prompts is small; the mode-retention estimate at 3B for a single checkpoint rests on that). And critically, **no test of the framework's actual decision value**: RCEA is never used to *predict* which run will fail or to *intervene*. Clip-Cov-7B's collapse is diagnosed post hoc with the same tools, which is suggestive but is exactly the kind of retrospective fit that a diagnostic framework must beat to earn its keep.

**Significance.** High for the diagnostic question, moderate for practice. If you log global entropy and tune on it, this paper is a direct argument that you are measuring a mixture whose components move in opposite directions — and the failed-vs-successful entropy gap plus the failed-prefix covariance concentration give a concrete alternative to log. The actionable takeaway (pair entropy with reward-stratified and length/termination diagnostics, as the Clip-Cov-7B failure demands) is sound. What it does not deliver is a method that improves training.

**Red flags.** (1) Single seed, so no run-to-run variance is available for any headline number — including the 3B macro ordering where Clip-Cov leads GRPO by 3.3 points. (2) AIME24/AIME25 have 30 problems each, so 3.3% = 1/30; the 3B AIME24 column swings from 13.3% (base) to 3.3% (GRPO) while MATH500 rises 48.6→63.8, and equal-weighting these in a five-benchmark macro average gives a 30-problem set the same weight as MATH500's 500 — the macro average is therefore dominated by small-sample noise, and the "best macro accuracy" claims rest on it. (3) Same-group baseline (EP-GRPO). (4) The abstract's "every reportable trained endpoint" phrasing is doing more work than the 99.9% endpoint supports.

## Strengths

- The core argument is correct and worth making loudly: average token entropy is a mixture statistic, and the paper proves it with an exact decomposition rather than an assertion.
- The decomposition is genuinely operational — every term is estimated, not just defined. Fixed probe, audited partition, full-vocabulary covariance, reward-stratified tail shares.
- The covariance result (95.5% sign accuracy, slope 0.993, R²=0.937, 12.7% median relative error) is the kind of validation that makes a "diagnostic bridge" claim credible instead of decorative.
- The paired common-question analysis in RQ2 anticipates the obvious objection (different prompt sets across reward strata) and answers it with bootstrap CIs, then honestly reports the two GTPO endpoints that fail to exclude equality.
- The Clip-Cov-7B failure is reported in detail — length saturation, boxed-answer rate 95.2%→1.0%, mask inactivity — instead of being dropped from the table. That is the single most useful piece of engineering intelligence in the paper.
- The limitations section names the real ones (single seed, one model family, judge-based validity, no within-mode entropy, no mode-stratified covariance, no causal claim) rather than pro-forma hedging.
- Explicitly separates "diagnostic" from "causal" throughout, and refuses to claim RCEA improves training.

## Limitations

- **One seed, one model family, two scales.** No variance estimates anywhere; several headline orderings (3B macro: Clip-Cov 27.75 vs GRPO 24.43) are within plausible seed noise.
- **LoRA-only, 8k examples, one epoch.** Short-horizon, low-rank adaptation — the entropy and covariance phenomenology may not transfer to full-finetune RLVR at scale, and this is never discussed as a scope limit.
- **The semantic partition is a single frozen classifier.** Its audit is described but not quantified; retention estimates inherit its errors, and there is no sensitivity analysis to the partition.
- **Mode-stratified covariance and within-mode realization entropy are not estimated** — the paper says so, but it means the hierarchy of Eq. (7) is only half measured: the top (reward), middle (validity) and bottom-left (mode) terms are estimated, while the between/within-mode covariance split remains theoretical.
- **The q99 cutoff is not comparable across methods**: for GTPO failed-prefix strata it selects ~58–59% of tokens, so Table 2's GTPO row measures something different from the others.
- **Retention range is too wide to support a single claim** (83.7%–99.9%); the "all endpoints contract" statement is true at the 0.1% level and meaningful only at the 16% level.
- **pass@64 near ceiling at 3B** (91.2–93.6 for all trained methods) makes the retention–coverage correlation fragile; n=5 and n=6 with Spearman ρ of 1.000 and 0.812 is a small-sample result.
- **No prospective validation.** The framework is never used to predict a failure before it happens or to trigger an intervention.
- **No comparison against simpler diagnostics** — e.g. response length, boxed-answer rate, or reward variance alone. The Clip-Cov-7B post-mortem suggests those would have fired too, and the paper does not show RCEA adds information beyond them.

## Relevance

直接命中 "RL Post-Training Dynamics & Failures" 这一方向：它研究的正是 RLVR 训练中的熵坍缩这一经典失效模式，并且给出了一个比"盯着全局熵曲线"更有诊断力的替代方案（按奖励结果与语义解法模式分层看熵去了哪里）。对关心训练监控与失效检测的读者，最有价值的可迁移结论是：全局熵下降本身不构成告警，真正该盯的是失败轨迹侧的熵占比、boxed-answer 率与长度饱和——Clip-Cov-7B 的崩溃正是这三者同时报警。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the failed-vs-successful entropy gap survive full fine-tuning at 7B/14B, or is part of it an artifact of LoRA's restricted update subspace?
- What is the actual human-audit agreement rate (accuracy, κ) of the frozen semantic partition $g_x$, and how much does retention move under a different partition or a different judge?
- Can RCEA be turned from a post-hoc diagnostic into an online trigger — e.g. alarm when $\rho_H$ drops toward 1 while boxed-answer rate falls, before accuracy collapses?
- Does the retention–pass@64 association hold when pass@64 is measured on prompts *not* in the 200-prompt probe, and at larger k where the metric is not near ceiling?
- Why does DAPO-7B achieve 77.2% MATH500 with lower mode retention (88.8%) than GRPO-7B (89.6%, 71.8%)? Is the retained-mode count simply the wrong summary, or is DAPO's advantage concentrated in a few high-value modes?
- What would a mode-aware regularizer look like — penalizing $H(Z|C_x)$ contraction directly — and would it avoid the Clip-Cov-7B style collapse without sacrificing greedy accuracy?
- How sensitive are the Table 2 gaps to the tail cutoff, given that the "q99" stratum already spans 4.6%–99.3% of tokens across methods?
