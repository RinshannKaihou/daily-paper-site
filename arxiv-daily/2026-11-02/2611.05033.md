> **一句话总结：** 作者证明（一阶展开，全部命题用 Lean 4 + Mathlib 机器验证）把隐状态拆成正交子空间后各部分效应之和恰等于整体替换的效应，所以"学到的子空间比整体拷贝动得更多"只说明其余分量把输出往回拉，并不说明它更干净地隔离了概念；在 90 个任务、14 个模型（124M–32B）、1168 个 model–task case 上，仅把 DAS 的目标函数从交叉熵换成对准总效应 T 的逐对平方误差（同一批初始化），读出层效应中位数就从 1.64–3.00 T 掉到距 T 只有 3.0%，说明超额效应主要来自目标函数而非网络。

> **TL;DR:** Whole-state patching — the "vanilla" intervention that learns nothing — is the only training-free reference for what a network produces at a site, and at a complete cut (all positions of one layer, or the readout) its effect equals the total effect T exactly. To first order the effects of orthogonal parts sum to the whole, so a learned direction beats the whole state iff the remaining components push the output back; an effect-seeking objective selects exactly that direction, up to the top eigenvalue λ₁ of M = sym E[wΔᵀ]. Across 90 tasks, 14 base models (Pythia, GPT-2, SmolLM2, Qwen2.5, Llama, Gemma-2; 124M–32B) and 1168 model–task cases, cross-entropy DAS exceeds T in 1096 of the 1124 cases with a margin above T (median 1.64–3.00 T per model), while the same DAS from the same three initialisations with a per-pair squared error aimed at T lands a median of 3.0% from T and CDAS at 0.91–1.06 T; inside the network cross-entropy DAS beats whole-state patching at 16,489 of 18,221 live cells (median +0.14 T). On CausalGym the DAS/vanilla ratio is a median 2.1–3.1 at every published size, and rebuilding the maps shows that the excess over whole-state patching in its best context accounts for 1.02–1.08× the DAS–vanilla gap — the ranking measures the objective, not cleaner localisation. Read it if you use activation patching, DAS or probes to argue that a site "carries" a concept; the three-step reporting check and the OwnEffect library are directly usable.

## 核心贡献

- **一阶分解定理，把"部分大于整体"变成可计算的量。** 设 Δ 为某位置 base→source 的状态变化、w 为 z 在该位置中点处的梯度、M = sym E[wΔᵀ]，则沿单位方向 d 的秩一交换效应 e(d) = dᵀMd 落在 [λ_D, λ₁]（Rayleigh 商），整体替换的效应是 tr M，秩 k 的效应是 tr(PᵀMP)（Ky Fan 界）。正交子空间各部分之和恒等于整体，所以"部分超过整体"等价于"其余部分贡献为负"——与是否更干净地隔离概念无关。
- **给出目标函数落在区间哪个位置的判据（Corollary 6）。** 纯 effect-seeking（严格随平均效应递减，如交叉熵在饱和前、logit difference）选 M 的顶特征向量并报告 λ₁，超额为 −Σ_{j≥2}λⱼ；target-aimed（在目标 τ 处唯一最小）选等值面 {d : dᵀMd = τ}，当且仅当 λ_D ≤ τ ≤ λ₁ 时存在。网络固定区间，目标函数选位置。
- **共享读出方向下的闭式预测（Proposition 7）。** 当所有 pair 的读出梯度共线时，最大效应 / 整体效应 = ½(1 + 1/cos θ)，θ 是"状态均值变化方向"与"读出方向"的夹角，最大值在两者角平分线取得。实测 θ 中位数 79.7°（IQR 73.1–83.2°），因此读出方向上大部分状态变化是正交的，理论上限可达 3–5 T。
- **完备割恒等式（Proposition 3）：** 整层或读出层做整体替换，效应精确等于 T。这给"某方向在整层上拿到 109.8%，而整体替换恰好是 100%"这类已发表数字一个确切的读法——超过 100% 就是整体替换在该 site 产生不出来的效应。
- **同初始化、只换损失的因果对照。** 在 14 个模型 × 90 个任务上，交叉熵、对准 T 的逐对平方误差、CDAS 的 JS 散度、均值效应目标四种目标从同一批三个初始化出发训练秩一 DAS；读出层上效应随目标移动约 2 T，网络内部同样随目标移动（对准 G 的平方误差斜率 0.91、截距 0.00 T）。
- **网络内部的 route test 与 off-route effect。** 定义 G*（cell 在其最佳上下文中的整体替换效应：单独 patch，或与同层另一个 region 一起 patch 时它新增的那部分），并给出 Algorithm 1：G* 区间在 0 以上就报 e/G*；G* 不显著为正而 e 显著为正且显著高于 G* 则判为 off-route。在 6 个小模型 78 个任务的 18,221 个 live cell 上，交叉熵 DAS 在 16,489 个显著超过 G，在 4300 个 off-route cell 上仍有显著效应，其中 0.47–0.56 的超额经注意力权重传导。
- **重审两处已发表结果，并附一个可复用的报告规范与库。** CausalGym 的 DAS/vanilla 比值在每个已发表规模上中位数 2.1–3.1，重建 map 后超额占该 gap 的 1.02–1.08 倍；Tigges et al. 的 109.8% 情感方向在 1.4b 上重跑为 114.7% [85.0, 137.4]，七个模型里六个的层选择带包含 100%。三步检查每对测试样本最多 5 次前向、无需梯度，`OwnEffect` 对任意方法给出的方向/子空间都能算。

## Key Contributions

- **A first-order decomposition that turns "a part beats the whole" into an arithmetic statement.** With Δ the base→source state change at a location, w the midpoint gradient of z, and M = sym E[wΔᵀ]: a rank-one interchange along unit d has mean effect dᵀMd ∈ [λ_D, λ₁]; whole-state patching has mean effect tr M; a rank-k interchange has tr(PᵀMP), bounded by Ky Fan. Orthogonal parts sum to the whole, so a part exceeds the whole exactly when the rest contributes negatively — no cleaner isolation required.
- **A selection corollary for objectives (Corollary 6).** A pure effect-seeking objective (strictly decreasing in mean effect) selects the top eigenspace and reports λ₁, with excess −Σ_{j≥2}λⱼ; a pure target-aimed objective selects the level set {d : dᵀMd = τ}, which is non-empty iff λ_D ≤ τ ≤ λ₁. The network fixes the range; the objective picks the point.
- **A closed form for the shared-readout-direction case (Proposition 7).** max/trace = ½(1 + 1/cos θ), attained at the bisector of the mean state change and the readout direction, where θ is the angle between them. Measured θ is a median 79.7° (IQR 73.1–83.2°), which is why the predicted ceiling reaches 2.2–4.7 T in the interquartile range.
- **A complete-cut identity (Proposition 3).** Whole-state patching at all positions of one layer, or at the readout, gives exactly T. This is what makes published scores above 100% interpretable: at such a site, the whole state scores exactly 100%, so anything above it is an effect the network does not produce there.
- **A causal control that isolates the objective: same initialisations, four losses.** Cross-entropy, a per-pair squared error aimed at T, the CDAS Jensen–Shannon divergence, and a mean-effect objective, each from the same three random initialisations, on 14 models and 90 tasks. Changing only the loss moves the readout effect by roughly 2 T and moves the in-network effect onto or away from G.
- **A per-cell reference (G\*) and the route test.** G\* is whole-state patching at the cell alone or the increment it adds when swapped with one other region of its layer, whichever is larger on training pairs; Algorithm 1 turns this into a three-branch decision (report e/G\*, declare off-route, or report no evidence of network use). Empirically, 4300 of 18,221 live cells are off-route for cross-entropy DAS, and 0.47–0.56 of its excess over G\* runs through attention weights.
- **A re-reading of two published results plus an artefact.** The CausalGym ranking and the 109.8% sentiment direction are re-measured against whole-state patching in their own units; a bug in CausalGym's released code (base and source token positions passed in swapped order, affecting 19 of 29 tasks) is identified and the authors informed. The three-step check costs at most five forward passes per pair with no gradients, and `OwnEffect` implements it for a direction from any method.

## Methodology

**Unit and reference.** A pair is a base input x_b and a source input x_s with expected next tokens y_b, y_s; z = o_{y_s} − o_{y_b} is the log odds of the source label over the base label; the total effect is T = z(x_s) − z(x_b). All effects are reported as multiples of the mean T over test pairs. Whole-state patching at a site S (base run with S's states replaced by the source run's) gives G(S), the indirect effect through S; a subspace interchange along P with orthonormal columns replaces h_b by h_b + PPᵀΔ. A *cut* is a site whose states, plus side inputs shared by both runs, fix the output; a *complete cut* is all positions of one layer (equal lengths, aligned positions, same padding) or the readout (last block's output at the last position, before final norm and unembedding, and before Gemma-2's logit cap). At a complete cut G = T exactly (Proposition 3); at a *partial* cut G is unbounded by T — measured at 120.3% and 121.7% of T in Pythia-410m at the adjective and verb positions.

**Theory.** For a patch h_b ↦ h_b + v, Taylor expansion about the midpoint h̄ = h_b + ½Δ gives F(h_b+v) − F(h_b) = wᵀv + ½vᵀH(v−Δ) + O(‖Δ‖³), where the second-order term vanishes at v = Δ, so whole-state patching is accurate to third order. For rank one, v = ddᵀΔ gives e(d) = (wᵀd)(dᵀΔ) = dᵀ sym(wΔᵀ) d; averaging gives the Rayleigh quotient of M, whose range is [λ_D, λ₁] and which is continuous on a connected sphere, hence attains every intermediate value. The trace is the first-order whole-state effect; Ky Fan's principle bounds rank-k effects. On a mirrored test set the second-order terms cancel pair-by-pair for any d, so the mean effect is first-order accurate for *every* direction — a genuinely useful structural fact. Proposition 9 adds a quadrature reading: the base-point trace is the trapezoid rule for ∫g′, the midpoint trace is the midpoint rule, so their errors have opposite signs with ratio −½ to leading order, and Simpson's rule ⅓tr M_base + ⅔tr M_mid gives T to fifth order. Proposition 8 handles attention: a head's score on a site is qᵀW_K LN(h_p), and a rank-one interchange can raise a small base attention weight α to σ(t) once (aᵀΔ + ‖a‖‖Δ‖)/2 ≥ log(1/α) + t, which whole-state patching cannot do when aᵀΔ = 0.

**Formalisation.** Every first-order statement of Section 3, plus Proposition 8, Proposition 9 and the appendix's exact examples, is checked in Lean 4 (v4.35.0-rc4) with Mathlib; one command builds all theorems with no unproved steps and only Lean's standard axioms. The step from the network to the first-order quantity is the Taylor expansion, which is itself checked for whole-state patching, for mirrored pairs, and for a general patch when F is differentiable on the whole space. Measurements on trained models are not formalised.

**Data and models.** 90 tasks from six sources: CausalGym's 29 tasks plus six re-paired number-only agreement tasks (base and source differ only in the subject's number), Kumon & Yanaka's seven filler–gap and eight negative-polarity constructions, nine sentiment templates from Tigges et al., two SST prompt scaffolds, 25 two-label RAVEL tasks, and four IOI tasks (two names / twenty names, the authors' templates and the paper's own frame). 14 base checkpoints (not instruction-tuned) from six families, 124M–32B: Pythia-14m through 6.9b, GPT-2 small, SmolLM2-360M, Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B/7B/32B, Llama-3.2-1B/3B and Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-9B. Readout measurement covers all fourteen; in-network maps cover the six small models on 78 tasks and the four 7–9B models on three CausalGym tasks. Float32, eager attention, eval mode, pinned revisions, deterministic kernels, TF32 off.

**Training.** Rank-one DAS: a free vector v initialised as a scaled standard Gaussian, patch h_b + ddᵀΔ along d = v/‖v‖, Adam at 5e-3 constant, batch 4, one pass (100 steps on 400 pairs), three initialisations per objective; directions kept after pass 1 and pass 10. Cross-entropy and the CDAS divergence are computed over the full vocabulary; the per-pair squared error is the batch mean of (eᵢ − Tᵢ)²; a second version inside the network uses the cell's Gᵢ instead of Tᵢ. The mean-effect objective is minus the batch mean of eᵢ. Higher ranks 2–64 and the label rank are trained at the readout only. Seeds are deterministic functions of (layer, region) or task index.

**Estimation of the maximum.** The "maximum effect" of a case is found by gradient ascent on the unit sphere over the training pairs (100 full-batch Adam steps, cosine decay 1e-3 → 1e-5, two starting points: the best mean-effect direction after ten passes and the top eigenvector of M from training pairs), then measured on test pairs — an out-of-sample estimate. A separate search on the test pairs gives the in-sample maximum, the exact counterpart of λ₁. The predicted maximum is λ₁ itself, computed from test-pair gradients in the span of the pairs' gradients and differences.

**Population and statistics.** A case enters the population if it has ≥20 test clusters (a pair plus its mirror) and its interval for T lies above zero — both fixed before any training; 1168 of 1187 model–task cases qualify. Effects are means of per-pair log odds; intervals are 95% bootstrap percentile intervals resampling test clusters (2000 resamples), with basic intervals for ratios involving λ₁. RAVEL uses an analytic two-way cluster interval by entity (Cameron et al. 2011, CR2 bias reduction, Student-t with the smaller effective cluster count minus one). Map counts use Benjamini–Hochberg FDR at q = 0.025 over the cells of each model. The sentiment re-run uses 10,000 circular block resamples of neighbouring prompts and a band simultaneous over layers, with a small-sample factor.

**Route test.** For each cell (layer ℓ, region r): compute e along d; choose the context (none, or one other region of layer ℓ) maximising G on training pairs; compute G\* in that context on test pairs; compute e with all downstream attention patterns frozen to the base run's; then branch — if G\*'s interval is above zero, report e/G\*; else if e and e − G\* are both above zero, declare off-route and report e/T, G\*'s upper bound and the share freezing removes; else report no evidence that the network uses the cell. A cell is *live* if some test pair's input change reaches it.

**Compute.** NVIDIA B200 GPUs on UCloud at the SDU eScience Center. A readout measurement takes 1–3 h at 124M–1.5B, 4 h at 3B, 4–5 h at 7–8B, 14 h for Gemma-2 (measured with and without its cap), 2 h for Qwen2.5-32B. A map takes 0.01–5.4 h (median 0.6). Code, data-building scripts, Lean proofs and `OwnEffect` are at github.com/tanyelai/whose-effect, with one script per experiment and one per table/figure.

## Results & Highlights

- **The loss alone moves the readout effect by about 2 T.** Across all 1168 model–task cases, cross-entropy DAS exceeds T in 1096 of the 1124 cases that have a margin above T (1103 of 1168 overall), at a median of 1.64–3.00 T per model and 0.76–0.97 of that case's maximum effect. From the same initialisations, the per-pair squared error aimed at T ends a median of 3.0% from T (4.4% on sentiment templates, which train 25 steps per pass instead of 100), and CDAS ends at a median of 0.91–1.06 T per model. Per-source medians of ce/T: CausalGym 2.79, number-only agreement 1.56, Kumon & Yanaka 1.61, sentiment 3.35, SST 3.78, RAVEL 2.35, IOI one label pair 1.80.
- **The first-order approximation is quantitatively tight at the readout.** tr M lies a median of 0.05–3.8% from T per model. On the 915 mirrored cases of thirteen uncapped models, the midpoint and base-point trace errors have opposite signs in 880 of the 882 cases where both are nonzero, with a ratio whose median is −0.52 (IQR −0.53 to −0.51) against the predicted −0.5, and least-squares slopes through zero of −0.49 to −0.60 across models. The two exceptions are mirrored tasks of Pythia-160m.
- **One pre-training angle predicts the ceiling.** For two-label tasks of the thirteen uncapped models (797 cases), θ computed on training pairs predicts the maximum effect within a median of 2.1–5.6% per model; across cases it ranks the effect cross-entropy DAS actually reaches with Spearman 0.71–0.98 per model, and the trained direction has median |cos| 0.89–0.98 with the top eigenvector of M. The running example: Llama-3.2-3B on RAVEL's city-to-language task, θ = 79.6° predicts a ceiling of 3.28 T, cross-entropy DAS reaches 2.51 T, the squared error aimed at T gives 0.96 T, the untrained mean difference 0.90 T, and whole-state patching 1.00 T.
- **Inside the network the excess is pervasive.** Cross-entropy DAS lies significantly above G at 16,489 of the 18,221 live cells, by a median of 0.14 T at those cells: 94–97% of cells on the syntactic sources (CausalGym, Kumon & Yanaka, number-only agreement) but only 53–81% on RAVEL, IOI, SST and the sentiment templates. Against the harder reference G\* it is still above at 15,998 cells, and 0.51–0.64 of DAS's summed effect lies beyond G\*. At the 4300 off-route cells the effects are mostly small — median 0.04 T, nine in ten below 0.43 T — but the network's own effect there has an interval upper bound with median 0.001 T.
- **The target of the loss, not training as such, sets the position.** Inside the network, the same squared error aimed at the cell's G lands near G (least-squares slope 0.91, intercept 0.00 T over all live cells), while aimed at T it lies a median of 0.09 T above G; cross-entropy lies 0.11 T above. Untrained representation-only directions already sit near G — difference of means slope 0.88, mean gradient of z 0.93× the summed G — but their bisector gives 2.11×, so the excess is a property of *which* direction is patched, not of optimisation.
- **Most of the in-network excess runs through attention.** Freezing every downstream attention pattern removes 0.58 of DAS's summed excess over G while keeping 0.90 of the summed G; 0.47–0.56 of the excess over G\* runs through attention per model, and freezing only the readout position's attention removes 0.43 of it. At off-route cells freezing removes 0.75 of DAS's effect. The running example's verb at layer 10 goes from G = 0.09 T to DAS 0.86 T, and holding attention takes nearly all of that back; at the last word it takes none, because there the patch sits at the position the answer is read from.
- **CausalGym's ranking is an excess, not a better region.** Published DAS/vanilla ratios are a median 3.06 at 14m, 2.83 at 31m, 3.08 at 70m, 2.41 at 160m, 2.09 at 410m, 2.38 at 1b, 2.08 at 1.4b, 2.10 at 2.8b, 2.47 at 6.9b; DAS exceeds vanilla beyond rounding in 251 of 261 entries, the other four drawn methods in 92 of their 1044 (112 of 1740 including LDA and random). On rebuilt maps, DAS's excess over G\* at its best region per layer is 1.02–1.08 times the DAS–vanilla gap (1.01–1.05 excluding one task with T near zero). CausalGym's own selectivity tables give a DAS/vanilla selectivity ratio of 0.52–1.70, above 1 only at 14m and 31m.
- **A bug in the released benchmark code.** CausalGym's code passes base and source token positions to its interventions in swapped order, which is harmless on the ten negative-polarity and filler–gap tasks but reads the wrong token on 12–100% of training pairs on the other 19. With the call corrected, the difference of means rises from a median 0.58–0.78 of vanilla to 0.96–0.98 and the probes reach 0.96–1.00, while PCA and k-means stay at 0.12–0.36; retrained DAS remains at 1.9–3.7× vanilla and above every other method on all 19 tasks at each of five sizes. The authors were informed.
- **The 109.8% sentiment direction is within noise of 100%.** Tigges et al.'s one-dimensional direction is patched at every position of a layer — a complete cut where whole-state patching scores exactly 100%. Re-running their code on seven models with three initialisations gives 114.7% [85.0, 137.4] at 1.4b; bands simultaneous over layers contain 100% in six models and lie below it in one, with Pythia-410m's 198.8% containing 100% only through the small-sample factor. Their code also trains at the adjective alone (a partial cut), so the objective never acts on the site where the score is taken.
- **Accuracy hides what effect size shows.** At the readout, cross-entropy DAS has median accuracy 0.96–1.00, the squared error 0.86–0.94, whole-state patching 0.93–0.97; DAS is significantly more accurate in 555 of the 1103 cases where its effect exceeds T and significantly less in 13. Inside the network, 8800 of the 16,489 above-G cells have significantly higher accuracy (medians 0.33 vs 0.16) and 30 lower — but both are exactly 1.0 in 317 readout cases and 322 cells, where accuracy cannot show the excess at all.
- **Scale grows the excess, and the growth is in the network.** From the smallest to the largest of three sizes within Pythia, Qwen2.5 and Llama, the maximum effect grows by a median of 24–41% per task and cross-entropy DAS by 16–29%; from Qwen2.5-7B to 32B, 18% and 9%. The predicted maximum λ₁/T, computed before training, grows at every step in all three families — so this is a property of the network, not of the search that finds the direction.
- **Three confounds are tested and fail to explain it.** (i) Number-only agreement pairs, where base and source differ only in the subject's number, give cross-entropy DAS 1.20–2.14 T against 1.17–2.09 T on CausalGym's lemma-and-number pairs, with the interval above T in 84 of 84 cases; changing only the lemma moves the output 0.00 T on average. (ii) On held-out constructions — IOI's five unseen templates, RAVEL's test prompts, the sentiment transfer setting — DAS stays above T in 13 of 13 two-name IOI cases and 223 of 224 RAVEL cases with a margin. (iii) Choosing G\*'s second region on the test pairs rather than the training pairs, which can only raise G\*, leaves 15,954 above-G\* cells against 15,998.
- **Where cross-entropy falls short, the derivative explains it.** Of 28 cases with a margin above T where cross-entropy DAS is not above T, 27 are one-directional negative-polarity constructions whose base label is *ever*, to which models give a median probability of 0.003–0.006. Lowering *ever* accounts for 0.79–0.95 of the maximum effect in log odds, but cross-entropy's derivative with respect to that logit is p_base, so its share of the gain is lower by 0.56–0.86 in absolute terms. The 28th is one initialisation of Pythia-160m's sentiment task that lags after one pass. On IOI with twenty names the rank-one maximum lies below T in every model, but from rank 16 onward DAS exceeds T in every case.
- **Gemma-2 is the theory's clean failure mode.** Its final logits pass through a soft cap 30·tanh(o/30). There the in-sample maximum exceeds the predicted maximum in 47 of its 71 tasks, and in none once the cap is removed — a bound in Appendix C.4 shows the cap can only raise exact effects relative to first order when its two conditions hold.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The individual ingredients are not new: whole-state patching as a baseline (CausalGym's vanilla, RAVEL's Full Rep, MIB's Full Vector), the observation that DAS can score above 100% (Sutter et al. 2025, §7), the dormant-pathway illusion (Makelov et al. 2024), pernicious divergence (Grant et al. 2026), and CDAS as a distribution-matching alternative (Bao et al. 2026). What is new is the *quantitative* frame: a first-order Rayleigh-range decomposition that says exactly how large the excess can be, which direction attains it, and which objective lands where — plus the empirical demonstration that swapping only the loss, from identical initialisations, moves DAS by roughly 2 T at the readout. That is a real conceptual contribution, and it converts a qualitative worry into a computable statistic. The result that a *rank-one orthogonal* map suffices, with no non-linearity and no over-parameterised alignment, is the sharpest part: it kills the defence that constraining the map to be linear rules out the illusion.

**Rigor.** The design is unusually well controlled for this literature. Same initial directions across four objectives, three seeds, effects scored exactly on test pairs rather than by first-order proxy, intervals over clusters rather than pairs, FDR control over cells, and a population rule fixed before training. The confound tests are the right ones and are run to completion: lemma-only pairs moving the output 0.00 T rules out the obvious "the second feature lowers G" story; strengthening G\* by choosing its context on test pairs moves 15,998 to 15,954, which is the correct way to answer "your reference is too weak"; and the negative-polarity shortfall is explained mechanistically (p_base ≈ 0.003–0.006 kills cross-entropy's gradient on the base logit) rather than waved away. The mirrored-set second-order cancellation is a genuinely elegant piece of analysis, and Proposition 9's quadrature reading — base-point trace = trapezoid, midpoint trace = midpoint rule, ratio −½, Simpson's rule to fifth order — both validates attribution patching as an estimator of the *reference* and gives a per-task diagnostic for when first order fails.

**Weaknesses I would press on.**
1. *The "maximum effect" is itself a search result.* The claim that DAS ends at 0.76–0.97 of the maximum depends on a 100-step full-batch ascent from two starting points. There is no ablation of search quality — more restarts, longer schedules, or a convex relaxation in the two-dimensional span — so "DAS goes most of the way to the ceiling" is conditional on that optimiser. This matters most exactly where the paper's argument is tightest (the readout, two-label, shared-direction case), where the ceiling is analytic and a stronger search would be cheap.
2. *Disclosed post-hoc choices, twice.* "We made this split after seeing the results for Gemma-2" and the sentiment band's small-sample factor "was chosen after the first trained run of Pythia-410m and of Pythia-160m had been seen". Both are disclosed, which is to the authors' credit, but they mean the Gemma-2 exclusion and the Pythia-410m "band contains 100%" reading are not pre-registered. The 410m case is the one model where the published 109.8%-style excess survives, so the choice lands on the load-bearing exception.
3. *Whole-state patching is treated as ground truth, but it is also an intervention.* G is the network's effect *under a patch that puts the state off its natural distribution*. The paper argues the learned edit ddᵀΔ is no longer than Δ and that whole-state patching retains 0.90 of its effect under attention freezing, but it never measures whether whole-state patching itself opens dormant pathways — the Grant et al. critique applies in principle to the reference too. If G understates what the network does at a partial cut, the "excess" is partly a reference artefact, and the paper's defence is conceptual (it learns nothing, so it transfers only what the network computes) rather than measured.
4. *Base checkpoints only.* Section 10.3 draws implications for refusal directions, model editing and safety audits, but every measurement is on base models, while the cited steering, refusal and audit directions (Arditi et al. 2024, Panickssery et al. 2024, Meng et al. 2022) are used on instruction-tuned ones. The authors say this explicitly, but the section reads as more applicative than the evidence supports.
5. *Coverage is asymmetric.* In-network maps are rank-one only, on six small models over 78 tasks and on four 7–9B models over three tasks — the latter reported as description, not test. Qwen2.5-1.5B/32B and Llama-3.2-1B/3B are readout-only. So the "inside the network" claims, which carry most of the practical weight for circuit-style work, rest on models ≤1B.
6. *Statistical bookkeeping has soft spots.* The population rule drops 19 model–task cases; some sentiment templates draw their pairs from only 10–21 sentences, so mirror-pair resampling overstates independence — the sensitivity analysis is reported and changes no cross-entropy-vs-T classification, but it does flip 12 classifications of where the squared error lands. FDR (Benjamini–Hochberg, q = 0.025) is applied over the cells of each model and, at the readout, only as a robustness check that moves 1096 to 1093; the headline readout counts are uncorrected.
7. *One claim is close to tautological.* "A squared error aimed at G lands near G" (slope 0.91) is nearly true by construction; the non-trivial version is that a squared error aimed at *T* still lands 0.09 T above G, and that an untrained difference of means already sits at 0.88 of G while its bisector reaches 2.11×. The paper does make the latter argument, but the headline phrasing ("changing only its objective removes most of the excess") leans on the easier comparison.
8. *Scope of the benchmark critique is narrower than it reads.* RAVEL's Full Rep baseline is expected to fail by construction, because its pairs require other attributes to stay unchanged, and the paper correctly declines to apply its comparison there. But that also means the strongest published- benchmark claim is essentially CausalGym plus one sentiment direction — two artefacts, both from 2024 code, one of which contains a token-position bug.

**Significance.** If the result holds, it changes how a whole subfield reads its own numbers. The practical check is cheap (≤5 forward passes per pair, no gradients, any method's direction), the library exists, and the CausalGym bug alone will force a re-reading of a widely cited ranking. The deeper claim — that "intervention success" is not evidence of representation, because a direction can move the output where the network does not — is the kind of statement that outlives the specific benchmark.

## Strengths

- A genuinely explanatory theory, not a post-hoc curve fit: the Rayleigh range predicts *when* a part can exceed the whole, *by how much*, and *which* objective gets there, and the predictions are tested before training (θ predicts the ceiling within 2.1–5.6% median error; λ₁/T tracks the growth with model size).
- The complete-cut identity is a small but decisive piece of bookkeeping: it makes "109.8% at a site where the whole state is 100%" a statement with an exact meaning rather than a puzzle.
- Excellent experimental hygiene: same initialisations across objectives, exact test-pair effects rather than first-order proxies, cluster bootstrap, FDR over cells, a population rule fixed before training, four numerical identity checks with derived tolerances, pinned revisions and hash-stamped run records.
- Confound testing that anticipates the obvious objections and answers them with measurements: lemma-only pairs (0.00 T), held-out templates (13/13 IOI, 223/224 RAVEL), a strengthened G\* (15,954 vs 15,998), and attention freezing that separates paths.
- Intellectual honesty in the exceptions: the Gemma-2 logit cap, IOI with twenty names (rank one understates, rank 16 fixes it), and the negative-polarity shortfall are all explained rather than buried, and two post-hoc analytic choices are disclosed in the text.
- Immediately usable artefacts: a three-step reporting check, `OwnEffect`, and an upstream bug report to CausalGym with the corrected numbers.

## Limitations

- **First order throughout.** At the readout the approximation is tight (trace within 0.05–3.8% of T), but inside the network it ranks cells by excess only partially (mean rank correlation 0.62) and does not bound the excess — at 4894 cells at least one trained run exceeds the predicted maximum, mostly through attention. Proposition 8 describes *how* a direction can raise attention but does not predict *where* better than simple predictors.
- **The reference G\* is itself bounded in scope.** It patches the last token of a region and adds at most one other region of the same layer, so an effect the network produces only through earlier tokens of a region, or through three or more regions jointly, is outside G\*.
- **Base models only, and only some of them inside the network.** Instruction-tuned models, which are where steering, refusal and editing are actually applied, are not measured; in-network maps are rank-one and, above 1B parameters, cover three tasks.
- **Generality to other intervention families is argued, not measured.** The decomposition covers orthogonal subspace patches (DAS, probes, difference of means). Sparse dictionaries, non-linear featurizers, ablation and additive steering are outside it; the paper says so, but the safety discussion still gestures at them.
- **Single lab, single preprint, no peer review yet.** Two authors, one institution, one arXiv category; the empirical claims are broad enough (90 tasks, 14 models) that independent replication of even a subset — say the objective swap at the readout — would materially raise confidence.
- **Dense presentation.** Table 1 defines roughly thirty coined terms (cut, cell, map, route, live, off-route, best context, margin above T, in-sample vs out-of-sample vs predicted maximum); the argument is precise but the entry cost is high, and several results are stated as "description" rather than test, which readers may skim past.

## Relevance

这篇直接落在"LLM Hidden States & Interpretability"：它针对的正是 activation patching / DAS / probing 这类"用干预效应大小论证某个 site 编码了某概念"的标准做法，并给出一个一阶可计算的上界与一套三步报告检查。凡是要用"学到的方向效应更大"来支持定位结论、或要复现 CausalGym 排名的实验，都应先跑它的 e/G\* 对照——顺带还能拿到 CausalGym 发布代码里 base/source token 位置传反这个会直接影响复现的坑。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How sensitive is the "DAS reaches 0.76–0.97 of the maximum" claim to the search that estimates the maximum? A convex relaxation in the span of {w, Δ} would give an exact rank-one optimum on the training pairs for the two-label case — does the gap close or widen?
- If whole-state patching is itself off-distribution, how much of the measured excess survives a reference that is constrained to the natural state manifold, e.g. a patching direction restricted to the empirical covariance of states at that site?
- The excess runs 0.47–0.56 through attention weights, and mostly through the readout position's attention. Does that share predict anything about which tasks are steerable, or about why refusal steering survives attention freezing with only an 8.83% loss (Cheng et al. 2026)?
- Does the decomposition survive instruction tuning? Base checkpoints are measured here; the safety-relevant directions (refusal, editing) are differences of means on tuned models, and Proposition 7 predicts those should show *no* excess — is that true empirically?
- Can the reporting check be folded into benchmark scoring directly, i.e. report each method's score divided by whole-state patching computed identically (as Figure 9a does) rather than as a separate appendix table? What would CausalGym's ranking look like under that normalisation at all nine sizes?
- Off-route effects are mostly small (median 0.04 T). Are they behaviourally inert, or do they compose — do several off-route cells patched together produce a non-trivial output change that no single cell's G predicts?
