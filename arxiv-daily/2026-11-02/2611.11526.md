> **一句话总结：** 作者把 AdamW 当作动力系统来分析，证明它的不动点恰好是某个 ℓ∞ 约束加障碍正则的"隐式目标" F 的驻点，但 F 的局部极小并不自动稳定——稳定还需要额外的曲率条件（一维给出充要条件、高维给出充分条件），并构造出两个精确梯度、无穷小步长下依然振荡不收敛的反例（凸二次函数 f″=2 远超阈值 9.1×10⁻²；非凸四次函数 f″=−1.20 低于阈值 −0.22）；据此提出只把权重衰减按 (|m|+e)/(√v+e) 缩放的 AdamMe，保持完全相同的固定点却拥有 Lyapunov 函数，在 GPT-2-Small(124M)/FineWeb10B 上取得 3.317 的验证损失（AdamW 为 3.355）。

> **TL;DR:** AdamW's fixed points coincide exactly with the stationary points of a static ℓ∞-constrained, barrier-regularized objective F(x)=f(x)+λeΦ(λx), but that first-order equivalence is not a convergence guarantee: on a 1-D convex quadratic f(x)=(x−0.5)² with λ=1, e=10⁻², β₁=0.99, β₂=0.9 (a=1, b=10), stability requires f″<9.1×10⁻² while the actual f″=2, so AdamW's iterates oscillate for 2000+ steps instead of reaching x*≈0.495 — and Corollary A.16 shows both this example and a non-convex quartic (f″(x*)=−1.20 vs. the required −0.22) stay unstable for *every* step size 0<η<0.1, so the failure is not a step-size artifact. The proposed fix, AdamMe, rescales decoupled weight decay by (|m|+e)/(√v+e), which equals 1 at any equilibrium (same fixed points, same F) but admits an explicit Lyapunov function whenever 1−β₂≤4(1−β₁), and in a single GPT-2-Small (124M) run on FineWeb10B reaches validation loss 3.317 vs. AdamW's 3.355 (−0.038 nats, single seed, ε=10⁻⁵ vs. 10⁻⁸). Worth reading if you work on training stability or optimizer theory; the negative result is clean and step-size-independent, but the practically relevant (β₁<β₂) counterexample only exists when |λx*|≳0.55, i.e. essentially at the ℓ∞ barrier.

## 核心贡献

- **给出 AdamW 的隐式不动点目标（Theorem 3.1）**：AdamW 的不动点条件 ∇f(x)/(|∇f(x)|+e)+λx=0 与"ℓ∞ 约束 ‖x‖∞≤1/λ + 障碍正则 λeΦ(λx)"问题的驻点**完全等价**，其中 φ(x)=−|x|−log(1−|x|) 在 |λx|→1 时发散，天然充当障碍函数。这是一个只依赖参数的静态目标，区别于此前把 AdamW 权重衰减解释为"依赖优化器状态的自适应 ℓ² 惩罚"的说法。
- **证明一阶等价 ≠ 稳定（Corollary 4.2 / Proposition 4.3）**：稳定平衡点必然落在 F 的局部极小集合内，但反之不成立；一维情形给出**充要**条件——除 F″(x*)>0 外还需一个关于原目标 Hessian f″(x*) 的显式不等式（式 8），当 a≠b（即 β₁≠β₂）时该额外条件才出现。作者由此把 AdamW 概括为"F 的忠实但挑剔的优化器"（faithful but selective）。
- **构造两类非收敛反例**：β₂<β₁（a<b）时条件退化为 f″ 的**上界**，连强凸二次函数都能违反（f″=2 vs 上界 9.1×10⁻²）；β₁<β₂（a>b，实践中更常见）时条件退化为 f″ 的**下界**，用一个精心构造的非凸四次函数违反（f″=−1.20 vs 下界 −0.22），轨迹表现为围绕不稳定平衡点的持续振荡/极限环而非发散。
- **把反例的鲁棒性钉死（Corollary A.16）**：固定 ODE 速率 a、b 让 β₁=1−aη、β₂=1−bη 随步长变化，两个反例在**整个 0<η<0.1 区间**都不稳定——说明这不是"学习率太大"的经典问题，而是更新规则本身的性质。附录 A.6 进一步给出离散时间的精确 Schur/Jury 判据（Proposition A.15），并显示离散曲率下界 −0.215 与连续时间下界 −0.224 只差约 4%，有限步长只额外加一个 e/(η²(1−r)) 量级的"step-flip 天花板"。
- **提出 AdamMe（Algorithm 1）**：把固定的权重衰减 λx 换成按梯度更新强度缩放的自适应衰减 λ·(|m|+e)/(√v+e)·x。该乘子在任意平衡点等于 1，因此**固定点与隐式目标 F 完全不变**（Proposition 4.7）；但连续时间动力学存在显式 Lyapunov 函数，只要 b≤4a（即 1−β₂≤4(1−β₁)，例如 β₁=0.9、β₂=0.999），所有有界轨迹都收敛到平衡点集（Theorem 4.8）；离散情形还给出 (1/T)Σ‖∇F(x_k)‖²=O(1/T) 的速率（Proposition A.7）。
- **一个可用的诊断结论**：非收敛的根源**不在隐式目标 F 本身**，而在逼近 F 的动力学；这为"改动力学而不改解"的优化器设计提供了可操作的方向。

## Key Contributions

- **A static implicit objective for AdamW (Theorem 3.1).** The fixed-point condition ∇f(x)/(|∇f(x)|+e)+λx=0 is shown to be *equivalent* to stationarity of min f(x)+λeΦ(λx) s.t. ‖x‖∞≤1/λ, with φ(x)=−|x|−log(1−|x|) as an interior barrier. The objective depends only on parameters, unlike state-dependent "adaptive ℓ²" readings of decoupled decay. The authors credit the overlap with the Softsign case of Lion-K (Chen et al., 2023) and note it reduces to Xie & Li's pure ℓ∞ constraint as e→0.
- **A sharp separation between first-order coincidence and stability.** Corollary 4.2 proves stable equilibria ⊆ local minima of F; Proposition 4.3 gives the exact one-dimensional converse failure, an explicit Hessian inequality (Eq. 8) whose coefficient flips sign with |λx*|, so the condition can act as either an upper or a lower bound on f″(x*). The extra requirement vanishes in exactly two cases: λ→0 (pure Adam) and a=b (β₁=β₂).
- **Two minimal counterexamples with different mechanisms.** Example 4.5 (β₂<β₁, a=1, b=10): a convex quadratic is unstable because stability demands f″<9.1×10⁻² while f″=2. Example 4.6 (β₁<β₂, a=10, b=1): a quartic with f″(x*)=−1.20 violates the floor f″>−0.22 and produces a bounded limit cycle rather than divergence.
- **Step-size-independent instability (Corollary A.16).** Holding the ODE rates a, b fixed and varying η over (0, 0.1), both fixed points remain unstable; the proof bounds C_η≤242 and shows the relevant Jury expression is strictly negative throughout.
- **Exact discrete-time counterpart (Proposition A.15).** A Schur–Jury analysis of the autonomous AdamW map yields three curvature conditions (F″>0, a moment-mismatch bound, and a step-flip ceiling), recovering the ODE result as η→0 and adding a finite-step ceiling that only bites for large η or very sharp minima.
- **AdamMe, a fixed-point-preserving convergent modification (Prop. 4.7, Thm. 4.8, Prop. A.7).** Rescaling decay by (|m|+e)/(√v+e) leaves the equilibrium set and F untouched, but yields an explicit Lyapunov function V=f(x)+ΣR_i(x_i)+(1/a)ΣI_i(x_i,m_i)/ℏ_i that is nonincreasing when b≤4a; the discrete version gives an O(1/T) stationarity rate under a small-step bound and √β₂>β₁/(2−β₁).
- **A higher-dimensional sufficient condition (Prop. 5.2).** Via a third-order linearization x⃛+A₂ẍ+A₁ẋ+A₀x=0 and a matrix Lyapunov function, asymptotic stability follows if some SPD Q makes QA₀, QA₁ symmetric and QA₀, QA₁, Q(A₁A₂−A₀) positive definite.

## Methodology

**Setup.** The paper studies AdamW in its small-step (ODE) limit, with exact batch gradients, constant λ, no bias correction, and infinitesimal step size — a setting deliberately *favourable* to convergence, so any instability found is attributable to the update rule rather than to noise, schedules, or step size. The ODE is ẋ=−m/(√v+e)−λx, ṁ=a(∇f(x)−m), v̇=b(∇f(x)^⊙2−v), with a=(1−β₁)/η, b=(1−β₂)/η; the discrete algorithm is the semi-implicit Euler discretization of this system. Writing h=√v makes the vector field polynomial in (x,m,h), so the Jacobian is available in closed form.

**Fixed-point analysis.** Setting the vector field to zero gives ∇f(x)/(|∇f(x)|+e)+λx=0. Lemma A.3 establishes the algebraic identity g/(|g|+e)+λx=0 ⟺ g+eφ′_λ(x)=0 with φ′_λ(x)=λx/(1−|λx|), which is precisely ∇F=0 for F=f+eΦ_λ. Because the barrier blows up at |λx|→1, the box constraint is inactive at any stationary point, so KKT multipliers vanish and fixed points equal unconstrained stationary points of F.

**Stability analysis (1-D).** The 3×3 Jacobian in (x,m,h) yields a cubic characteristic polynomial whose coefficients depend on (a,b,λ,e), the equilibrium curvature H*=f″(x*), and ℏ*=h*+e. Routh–Hurwitz for cubics (α₂,α₁,α₀>0 and α₂α₁>α₀) splits into two groups: α₀>0 gives F″(x*)>0 (necessity), and α₂α₁>α₀ gives the extra Hessian bound. Rewriting the latter yields Eq. 8, (a(a+λ)−b(b+λ)|λx*|)f″(x*) > −eC/(1−|λx*|), with C=(a+b+λ)(ab+aλ+bλ)−abλ>0. The sign of the f″ coefficient is governed by a(a+λ) vs. b(b+λ)|λx*|, which is why the bound flips between a ceiling (a<b) and a floor (a>b) as |x*| grows.

**Counterexample construction.** For a<b, pick any convex f with f″ larger than the ceiling U (e.g. f=(x−0.5)² with λ=1, e=10⁻², β₁=0.99, β₂=0.9, η=10⁻², giving x*≈0.495 and U=9.1×10⁻²). For a>b, the floor is negative, so one needs a non-convex region; the quartic 20(x−0.99)⁴−0.60(x−0.99)²−0.099(x−0.99)+0.018 at a=10, b=1, e=10⁻³, λ=1 gives f″(x*)≈−1.20 < −0.22 while F″(x*)=8.8>0, i.e. a genuine local minimum of F that AdamW cannot stabilize. Numerical integration of both the ODE and the discrete map (Figures 1–2, plus an (x,m,v) phase portrait) confirms persistent oscillation.

**Discrete-time analysis.** Appendix A.6 rewrites the autonomous AdamW map in (x,m,h), derives its Jacobian at a fixed point, and applies the Schur–Jury cubic criterion. Expressing β's through a, b makes the map (β₁,β₂)↔(a,b) a bijection for each η, so the resulting Proposition A.15 covers all hyperparameter choices; the four Jury inequalities collapse to three curvature bounds whose η→0 limit reproduces Eq. 8.

**AdamMe.** The design principle is that the first-moment term is normalized by √v+e while the decay term is not; rescaling decay by the same factor restores dimensional consistency and makes the decay vanish where the gradient signal is weak relative to its second moment. At equilibrium |m*|=√v*=|∇f(x*)|, so the multiplier is exactly 1 and the fixed-point set is unchanged (Prop. 4.7). The Lyapunov function is built coordinatewise from the barrier R_i(x_i)=(e/λ)φ(λx_i), the "target momentum" r_i(x_i)=−λe x_i/(1−λ|x_i|) (the unique root of ψ_i=0), and an energy term I_i=½(m_i²−r_i²)+λx_iJ_i; the proof shows V̇≤0 provided b≤4a, then applies LaSalle. Appendix A.4 adapts the energy-increment argument of Lion-K to control the changing second-moment denominator, giving a discrete O(1/T) rate.

**Empirical check.** One GPT-2-Small (124M, 12 layers, 12 heads, width 768, context 1024) run from scratch on FineWeb10B (GPT-2 tokenized, one epoch, batch 512, ~2¹⁹ tokens/step, grad clip 1.0, bf16, flat-then-linear LR decay, validation every 400 steps), following the GPT-opt recipe of Amsel et al. Both optimizers were tuned over 4 learning rates × 3 weight decays × 4 ε values; the best configuration for both was LR 5×10⁻⁴, WD 0.1, with ε=10⁻⁸ (AdamW) and ε=10⁻⁵ (AdamMe).

## Results & Highlights

- **The convex counterexample is unstable by a factor of ~22× on the curvature threshold.** For f(x)=(x−0.5)² with λ=1, e=10⁻², β₁=0.99, β₂=0.9, η=10⁻² (a=1, b=10), Proposition 4.3 requires f″(x*)<9.1×10⁻², but the objective has f″=2. The equilibrium sits at x*≈0.495, and the discrete iterates over 2000 steps (Figure 1, right) never settle: they oscillate around x* instead of converging. The same instability appears in the continuous-time counterpart, so it is not a discretization artifact.
- **The non-convex counterexample is a *local minimum of F* that is nonetheless unstable.** For the quartic with x*=0.99, λ=1, e=10⁻³, a=10, b=1, one has f″(x*)=−1.20 against the required floor f″(x*)>−0.22, while F″(x*)=−1.20+λe/(1−λx*)²=8.8>0 — so x* is a strict local minimum of the implicit objective, exactly the point an optimizer is supposed to find, yet the trajectory traces a bounded limit cycle (Figure 2c–e) with no decay in oscillation amplitude over 10⁴ discrete steps.
- **Instability is not a learning-rate problem.** Corollary A.16 proves that with the ODE rates held at (a,b,e,λ)=(1,10,0.01,1) and (10,1,0.001,1), both fixed points remain unstable for *every* 0<η<0.1; the proof bounds C_η≤242 while the relevant Jury expression is bounded above by 242−42.55×100.8<0 (quadratic) and 242−12×88.02<0 (quartic). This is the paper's strongest single result: the failure survives the entire admissible step-size range, unlike the classical η<2/L ceiling.
- **Discretization barely moves the boundary.** At λ=1, η=0.07, the discrete "moment-mismatch floor" is −0.215 versus the ODE floor of −0.224 at the quartic example (a ~4% shift), and finite steps add only a step-flip ceiling of order e/(η²(1−r)) that constrains large steps or very sharp minima (Figure 6). In the a=b case the discrete condition collapses to −λe/(1−r)² < H* < e(2−λη)(2−aη)/(aη²(1−r)²), recovering Corollary A.11 as η→0.
- **AdamMe reaches lower validation loss on GPT-2-Small.** On FineWeb10B with 124M parameters, AdamMe attains **3.317** validation loss versus **3.355** for AdamW — a **−0.038 nat** improvement, i.e. AdamW's loss is 1.1% higher — with both methods at their best tuned setting (LR 5×10⁻⁴, WD 0.1). The curves track each other closely (Figure 5), which the authors read as evidence that the correction preserves AdamW-like training rather than changing the optimization trajectory qualitatively.
- **A convergence rate for the modification.** Proposition A.7 shows that deterministic AdamMe without bias correction stays inside {‖λx‖∞≤1−ρ} and satisfies (1/T)Σ_{k=0}^{T−1}‖∇F(x_k)‖²=O(T⁻¹) with ∇F(x_k)→0, for every constant step size up to an explicit η_max, under the condition √β₂>β₁/(2−β₁). This is a stationarity rate in the *implicit* objective F, not in f.
- **A quantitative account of when the extra condition disappears.** Remark 4.4 shows the additional Hessian requirement vanishes for λ→0 (recovering the plain-Adam requirement f″>0) and for a=b (where stability reduces to F″>0), isolating "weight decay + unequal momentum coefficients" as the necessary ingredient for the pathology.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty is unevenly distributed.** The headline object — the ℓ∞-constrained, barrier-regularized implicit objective — is not new: the paper states that it coincides with the Softsign case of Lion-K (Chen et al., 2023) and reduces to Xie & Li's constraint as e→0. What is genuinely new is (i) the *negative* result that local minima of F need not be stable, with an exact one-dimensional criterion, and (ii) AdamMe as a fixed-point-preserving repair. Framing the implicit objective as contribution #1 in the abstract therefore oversells the least original part; the stability dichotomy is the real contribution and deserves the headline.

**The practically relevant counterexample lives at the barrier, and the paper never says so.** For the common regime β₁<β₂ (a>b), instability requires f″(x*)<−eC/(D(1−r)) *while* F″(x*)>0 requires f″(x*)>−λe/(1−r)², with r=|λx*|. The two conditions can only be simultaneously satisfied when λD>C(1−r); plugging in the paper's own numbers (λ=1, D=108.02, C=242) gives 1−r<0.446, and the window is vanishingly narrow near that edge: at r=0.9 it is f″∈(−0.10,−0.022), width 0.078, while at r=0.99 it is (−10,−0.224). The published quartic sits at r=0.99 — the parameter is at 99% of the ℓ∞ box radius 1/λ. With λ=0.1 and weights of order 0.1–1 (typical for LLM training), r is 0.01–0.1, where the window is closed. So the "even convex objectives can be unstable" claim is real only in the β₂<β₁ regime (which the paper concedes performs poorly in practice), and the β₁<β₂ claim is real only in a thin shell adjacent to a constraint boundary that real training never approaches. This does not invalidate the theorem, but it materially weakens the practical framing, and the paper reports the bound (Eq. 8, Figure 4, Figure 6) without ever translating it into "how close to the barrier do you have to be?"

**The GPT-2 evidence is one run, and the comparison is confounded.** Checklist item 7 states plainly that the GPT-2-Small experiment is a single run without error bars. The reported gap is 0.038 nats, which is within the range of seed-to-seed variation for a 124M model at ~10B tokens. Worse, the two methods selected *different* ε (10⁻⁸ for AdamW, 10⁻⁵ for AdamMe) — and ε sits in exactly the denominator that AdamMe rescales, so a larger ε shrinks the effective preconditioner and partially mimics the AdamMe multiplier. The honest comparison would be AdamMe(ε=10⁻⁵) vs. AdamW(ε=10⁻⁵), or AdamW with λ retuned downward. Relatedly, both methods selected λ=0.1, the *largest* value in the grid {0.005, 0.02, 0.1}: when the optimum lands on the grid boundary, the baseline has not been tuned, and "better than AdamW" may just mean "better than an under-decayed AdamW."

**AdamMe's guarantee excludes the paper's most dramatic failure.** Theorem 4.8 requires b≤4a, i.e. 1−β₂≤4(1−β₁). Example 4.5 has a=1, b=10, so b=10>4a=4 — the convergent modification is not guaranteed to converge on the very convex quadratic where AdamW provably fails. The guarantee covers β₁<β₂ (where AdamW was already mostly fine) and excludes β₂<β₁ (where AdamW fails on convex functions). That is an awkward shape for a repair, and the paper does not discuss it.

**No ablation isolates the mechanism, and obvious baselines are missing.** AdamMe could improve training for at least three distinct reasons: (a) genuinely better dynamics, (b) an effectively weaker weight decay early in training (the multiplier is <1 whenever |m|<√v), or (c) the larger ε. The paper tests none of these separately — no measurement of final weight norm, no AdamMe-with-fixed-ε, no "AdamW with λ halved." Most conspicuously, the same group's "Cautious weight decay" (ICLR 2026) is cited as reference [9] but never compared against, despite being a closely related modification of the decay term; a reader cannot tell whether AdamMe is the better fix or merely the one with a Lyapunov function.

**The multi-dimensional condition is existential, not operational.** Proposition 5.2 asks whether *some* SPD Q exists making QA₀ and QA₁ symmetric and three products positive definite. No construction algorithm is given, no example verifies it on a real Hessian, and no experiment computes the 3d×3d Jacobian spectrum for a trained network. Since the paper's own motivation is "numerical spectrum checking gives little insight," a sufficient condition that cannot be checked is only a partial answer; the appendix even concedes the closed-form expressions are "too unwieldy to read."

**The static-objective story does not explain weight decay's actual effect.** In F(x)=f(x)+eΦ_λ(x), the regularizer is scaled by e (10⁻⁸ in practice), and the box radius is 1/λ (10 for λ=0.1) — both effectively inactive at realistic parameter scales. The fixed-point shift is |∇f|=λe|x|/(1−λ|x|), i.e. O(eλ), so AdamW's fixed points are essentially critical points of f with a negligible offset. That is consistent with the theory, but it means the "AdamW minimizes a constrained problem" reading says little about why weight decay controls norm and generalization in real training — where the balance is noise-driven, not fixed-point-driven. The paper is aware ("its practical implications... are more subtle") but does not push the point to its conclusion.

**Deterministic-only theory.** Every stability statement assumes exact gradients; there is no diffusion term, no stochastic approximation, and no treatment of mini-batch noise. Yet the empirical literature the paper cites (Dereich et al. on Adam's symmetry condition, Cohen et al. on the edge of stability) shows that noise changes the qualitative picture. A stochastic version of Eq. 8 would be far more relevant than another 1-D quartic.

**Minor red flags.** No code or artifact release is mentioned anywhere (the GPT-opt fork is credited but AdamMe's implementation is not provided); the 1-D figures are trivially reproducible from stated hyperparameters, but the 124M run is not. There is a notational slip in Theorem 3.1's regularizer: F=f+λeΦ(λx) equals f+eΦ_λ(x) only up to a factor of λ, while Lemma A.4 uses ∇F=∇f+e∇Φ_λ; the equivalence is correct but the constant is stated inconsistently between the theorem and its proof. And the dismissal of Li et al.'s O(d^{1/2}T^{−1/4}) result as requiring "small weight decay" is fair but would be more convincing with the actual numbers (λ≤O(d^{1/2}T^{−3/4}) is astronomically small at d=10⁹, T=10⁵), since that would settle the point rather than assert it.

## Strengths

- **A clean, step-size-independent negative result.** Corollary A.16 is the paper's best moment: by holding the ODE rates fixed and varying η over (0,0.1), it rules out the obvious "you just used too large a learning rate" rebuttal. Most non-convergence examples in the optimizer literature die at exactly this objection.
- **The decomposition necessity/sufficiency is genuinely informative.** Corollary 4.2 (stable ⊆ local minima of F) plus Proposition 4.3 (the converse fails, with an explicit bound) tells you precisely *which* extra assumption is missing, and Remark 4.4 identifies the culprit as "weight decay combined with unequal momentum coefficients" rather than either alone. That is a sharper diagnosis than "AdamW lacks a convergence proof."
- **AdamMe is a minimal, well-motivated modification.** One extra elementwise factor, exactly the same fixed points, an explicit Lyapunov function, and a discrete O(1/T) rate. As a constructive counterpart to a negative result, this is the right shape: it changes where trajectories go without changing where they stop.
- **Unusual honesty about practical severity.** Section 7 states outright that "neural networks need not converge to be useful" and that training appeared robust to the instability in their experiments. The paper does not pretend its counterexamples explain real training failures.
- **The discrete-time treatment is real, not hand-waved.** Proposition A.15 gives exact Schur conditions, shows they reduce to the ODE bound as η→0, and quantifies the discretization gap (−0.215 vs −0.224). Many theory papers would have stopped at the ODE.
- **Good positioning against prior AdamW convergence work.** The related-work section explains *why* earlier positive results (Zhou et al.'s exponentially decaying λ, Li et al.'s small-λ/small-β₁ regime, Xie & Li's conditional KKT result) do not contradict the negative result, which pre-empts the most likely reviewer objection.

## Limitations

- **Continuous time, exact gradients, no bias correction, constant λ.** The analysis isolates the update rule but excludes noise, schedules, and the bias-correction terms that the real PyTorch implementation uses. Whether Eq. 8 survives a stochastic approximation is open.
- **Counterexamples are one-dimensional and hand-constructed.** Both objectives were chosen so that a specific threshold is violated; there is no measure-theoretic statement about how large the unstable set is, and no test on a real loss landscape. The paper concedes this ("worst-case counterexamples on controlled objectives").
- **The practically relevant regime needs |λx*| near 1.** As computed above, for β₁<β₂ the unstable-but-local-minimum window only opens for r=|λx*|≳0.55 and is narrow until r→1. Real training sits at r≈0.01–0.1, so the practical reach of the β₁<β₂ result is unclear and unquantified.
- **Multi-dimensional results are sufficient-only and uncheckable in practice.** Prop. 5.2 requires exhibiting a Q; no construction, no example, no experiment.
- **Empirical validation is a single 124M run.** One seed, no error bars (admitted in the checklist), different ε per method, λ grid capped at the value both methods selected, no downstream evaluation, no measurement of AdamMe's runtime overhead or its effect on weight norms.
- **No comparison to sibling fixes.** Cautious weight decay, Lion-K, AdamW with retuned λ, or AdamW with larger ε are all absent as baselines, so the paper cannot claim AdamMe is the *best* fix — only that it is a fix with a guarantee.
- **The guarantee's parameter condition is restrictive in an awkward direction.** b≤4a covers β₁<β₂ but excludes the β₂<β₁ regime where AdamW's failure is most severe and most easily demonstrated.

## Relevance

与"LLM 训练稳定性与可观测性"直接相关：它给出了 AdamW 即使在精确梯度、无穷小步长下也会围绕不动点持续振荡的机制性解释，并把不稳定的判据落成可检查的曲率/动量/权重衰减不等式——这对判断"训练不收敛到底是数据/模型问题还是优化器动力学问题"提供了一个此前缺失的理论参照。AdamMe 只需在权重衰减上加一个逐元素乘子，是低成本、可立即试用的候选修复，但它的实证证据只有一次 124M 单种子实验，距离可信的大规模结论还有明显距离。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. **How large is the unstable set, really?** The paper gives existence proofs; what fraction of a realistic loss landscape (e.g. along Hessian eigen-directions at a GPT-2 checkpoint) violates Eq. 8? Is the answer "measure zero in practice" or "a non-trivial shell"?
2. **What happens under stochastic gradients?** Add a diffusion term to the AdamW ODE and ask whether noise stabilizes the unstable equilibria found here (as it does in some Langevin analyses) or merely blurs the limit cycle into a noisy orbit. Does Eq. 8 acquire an e/σ² correction?
3. **Is the oscillation observable?** The unstable examples predict a specific oscillation frequency set by the complex eigenvalue pair. Can that signature be detected in real training curves (loss or grad-norm periodicity) and used as an early-warning diagnostic for training instability?
4. **AdamMe vs. the alternatives, head to head.** Compare AdamMe against cautious weight decay, AdamW with λ retuned to match AdamMe's effective decay, and AdamW with ε=10⁻⁵ — at 124M with ≥3 seeds and at ≥1B parameters. Which of the three explanations (dynamics, effective decay, ε) actually drives the 0.038-nat gain?
5. **Does AdamMe's trajectory-level weaker decay hurt generalization?** Since the multiplier is <1 whenever |m|<√v, AdamMe applies less decay early in training. Measure final weight norms and held-out performance; a convergence guarantee that costs generalization would be a poor trade.
6. **Can Prop. 5.2 be made operational?** Is there a constructive choice of Q (e.g. Q=A₂⁻¹ or a diagonal scaling) that reduces the condition to a checkable spectral inequality on ∇²f(x*), and does it hold at real checkpoints?
7. **Does the guarantee extend past b≤4a?** The condition b≤4a is exactly where the Lyapunov proof's bracket 2−bh_i/(2aℏ_i) stays non-negative. Is it an artifact of the proof technique or a genuine barrier — i.e., does AdamMe actually fail to converge for b>4a, or does it converge with a different Lyapunov function?
