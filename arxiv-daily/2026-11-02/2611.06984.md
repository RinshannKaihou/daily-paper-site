> **一句话总结：** AdaFloor 指出 MoE 强化学习 rollout 中的长尾浪费并非"空闲算力"而是被 EP（专家并行）集合通信和专家归属绑死的 rank-time，它用历史长度预测 + 校准 TailGuard 构造 KV 安全的 32-prompt 批次，再按"释放面积最大"逐 step 选择收缩深度（16→8→4→2），在 16 卡昇腾 910C、Qwen3-30B-A3B 的 DeepScaleR 上把 Vanilla 每 epoch 29393.9 rank-秒的 dummy run 削减 96.15%（至 1131.7），rollout 时间最多降低 24.09%。

> **TL;DR:** On 16 Ascend 910C devices running Qwen3-30B-A3B GRPO rollouts over DeepScaleR, 32.83% of rank-time sits in "dummy run" — the interval after a rank finishes its own responses but is still bound to EP all-to-all and remote expert ownership. AdaFloor makes that interval legally releasable: it predicts per-prompt tails (EMA, α=0.3), sorts prompts into contiguous 32-prompt steps, guards each step with a calibrated response cap (q=0.95 quantile of one-sided underestimation), then solves a quota-aware binary ILP per step to pick the KV-safe shrink floor with maximum release area, and at runtime progressively detaches ranks at completion quorums. Versus Vanilla (random batching), Planned-residency floor4 + TailGuard cuts 5-step rollout time from 5878.3 s to 4462.1 s (−24.09%), and versus the non-elastic LengthSort+TailGuard control (4567.5 s) the elastic shrink still adds only 2.31% (mean paired 105.4 s, 95% CI [26.8, 184.0]). The capacity-oriented Natural floor2 point cuts dummy run 96.15% (29393.9 → 1131.7 rank-s) at −19.88% rollout time. Caveat: the majority of the speedup comes from the batching/guard layer, not from shrinking.

## 核心贡献

- **重新定义了问题**：把"rank 本地请求已完成、但仍在 EP 组内"的区间命名为 EP-coupled dummy run，并论证它与可被集群调度器直接回收的 idle 容量不是一回事——本地完成不构成合法的更小 EP 世界。在 16 卡 Qwen3-30B 的一次 1305.886 s rollout 中，这段区间占 32.83% 的总 rank-time，平均利用率从活跃期的 14.78% 掉到 2.04%。
- **提出"完成 quorum"这一可复用的时间点**：同步 rollout 天然产生可预测的完成 quorum，AdaFloor 把"prompt 摆放"和"专家可用性 + 幸存者 KV 容量"联合规划，使剩余 EP 耦合后缀变成可释放的 rank-time（释放面积公式 R(F)=Σ(f_{s−1}−f_s)(T−t_s)）。
- **两级规划器**：epoch 级用 EMA 尾部预测排序切分 32-prompt step，并用校准的 one-sided 分位数 TailGuard 给每步设响应上限；step 级在 KV 安全图上解 quota-aware 二值 ILP，联合选择 floor、收缩 schedule、prompt 配对与物理 rank 映射（词典序目标：先最小化预测 rollout 时间，再最大化释放面积）。
- **运行时实现**：在 VERL 0.6.0 + vLLM 0.11.0 + vLLM Ascend 上实现渐进式 EP 收缩（16→8→4→2）、专家无损物化（5 种机制对比后选常驻副本）、compute-shareable / memory-shareable 分离的 bounded lease，以及 full-world restore 后再进入 reward/training 的三条正确性不变量。
- **给出可分离的操作点**：capacity（Natural floor2）、sidecar（Planned floor4 无 TailGuard）、latency（Planned floor4 + TailGuard）三种配置，并量化它们之间的取舍，而不是只报一个最好看的数字。

## Key Contributions

- **Names and quantifies a previously unaccounted resource.** "EP-coupled dummy run" is distinguished from ordinary scheduler-visible idleness: a rank that has finished all local responses cannot leave because it may still host experts selected by tokens on other ranks and must stay in all-to-all dispatch/compute/combine. Measured at 6,859.4 of 20,894.2 rank-seconds (32.83%) in one profiled cycle.
- **Turns synchronized completion into a scheduling primitive.** Because rollout steps are barrier-synchronized, rank completions form quorums (8, 12, 14 of 16 for the 16→8→4→2 ladder) at which the EP world can change while remaining collectively legal.
- **Joint floor + placement planning under a hard KV constraint.** Per-floor KV-safe pair graphs G_{t,f} are built from an inflated (µ=1.16) active-token-peak model; a quota-aware ILP maximizes staged release area subject to perfect-matching and cumulative-quorum constraints, with a secondary cost favoring similar tails, early pair completion, and low KV pressure.
- **A calibrated guard for the prediction tail.** TailGuard converts the empirical 0.95 quantile of the one-sided underestimation ratio (over the last K=3 epoch transitions) into a per-step response cap C_t, bounded by C_min=4096 and L_max=16384 — because ~3 prompts per epoch jump from 3.2K–3.7K to the 16K limit.
- **An end-to-end runtime with correctness invariants**, plus an honest decomposition showing how much of the win is epoch construction versus elastic release.

## Methodology

**Setting.** One node, eight dual-die Huawei Ascend 910C packages → 16 software-visible devices with 64 GiB HBM each, HCCS-connected die pairs. Rollout model is a local Qwen3-30B-A3B checkpoint (30.53B params BF16, 128 experts, top-8 routing) with TP1/PP1/DP16/EP16 for rollout and TP4/PP4/EP4 for the actor. Workload: DeepScaleR training split, 32 prompts × 16 samples = 512 responses per step, max prompt 1024 / response 16384 tokens, temperature 0.9, top-p 0.9, top-k 50. Each step computes rewards/advantages and does a GRPO update at lr 1e-6; epoch 1 plans from epoch 0's responses, epoch 2 from epoch 1's, so the measured epochs are updated-policy trajectories, not frozen replays. Three seeds, averaging two sequential 5-step epochs, same 160-prompt multiset per epoch.

**Characterization → design (5 observations).** (1) Dummy run is temporally structured (long suffixes, not scattered gaps) but local completion alone doesn't create a legal smaller world. (2) Tail structure is predictable but imperfect — adjacent-epoch prompt maxima are mostly stable, yet rare 16K outliers appear. (3) KV safety is pair-level, not length-level: in the toy 4-rank example, pair (p3,p4) peaks at 156 active tokens vs a cap of 144 while (p0,p3) peaks at 78, so nearest-length pairing is unsafe; release-aware matching moves the 4→2 quorum from 60 to 52 and raises release area from 66 to 82. (4) The best floor is step-dependent — deep floors shrink the feasible pair graph and can lose the perfect matching, so a fixed depth either fails on long steps or wastes early opportunities. (5) Released rank-time is only a resource once the runtime decouples ranks and exposes restore-bounded leases.

**Epoch construction.** Per-prompt tail M_i = max_j L_{i,j}; prediction M̂_i^{(k+1)} = αM̂_i^{(k)} + (1−α)M_i^{(k)} with α=0.3 (30% history, 70% latest). Prompts sorted by M̂ and cut into contiguous 32-prompt steps. TailGuard: r_i^+ = max(1, M_i/(M̂_i+ε)), C_t = min(L_max, max(C_min, ρ_q·S_t/Q·Q)) with q=0.95, K=3, Q=512 — i.e. the cap tracks the recent 95th-percentile underestimate, rounded to token blocks. Each candidate step is validated by the same planner that will execute it P(B_t, C_t) → (success, φ_t); rejected steps get bounded repair (swap the step's shortest prompt with the preceding step's shortest, re-sort, advance to next-shortest), and repair only fixes feasibility, never re-optimizes release.

**Step planning.** Per-floor graphs E_{t,f} = {e | A_{µ,e} ≤ C^{eff}_{f,m}}, where A_e is the peak of τ·#{responses ≥ τ} over the pooled multiset of the pair. Effective capacities are measured, not assumed (Table I): Natural mode 131,072 / 280,576 / 315,648 / 380,800 tokens for floors 2/4/8/16; Planned residency 133,120 at floor4 (floor2 is infeasible — group warmup exhausts HBM before KV sizing). Schedules are enumerated in descending release area (R_8, R_4, R_2 in Eq. 15) and the first feasible one wins; an ILP with cost c_e = 10^6·gap_e + 10^3·M_e + A_{µ,e} then chooses the matching within that fixed schedule. Floor selection is argmax over floors of the best feasible release area, ties to the larger floor. Plans are cached once per epoch; each step has 32 vertices and ≤496 edges.

**Runtime.** Natural mode builds lower-floor resources on demand (tolerates drift, supports floor2, pays setup cost); Planned residency pre-creates and keeps survivor communication/dispatcher state warm (lower transition latency, more HBM, and can leave planned survivors in dummy run if realized order drifts). Transitions follow halving stages only (16→8→4→2) to stay on the optimized collective path. Exiting ranks detach rather than terminate, still participate in group-creation ordering, and are only leased after compute detach and (if needed) KV release. Five expert-materialization mechanisms were implemented (resident replica, CPU swap, CPU double buffer, remote NPU fetch, dual source); Mode 1 (resident replica) wins at every floor (93.65 / 98.12 / 100.58 s at floors 8/4/2) and is adopted.

## Results & Highlights

- **Dummy-run reclamation.** On DeepScaleR with Qwen3-30B-A3B, three updated-policy seeds: Vanilla accrues 29,393.9 rank-seconds of dummy run per epoch (31.25% of rollout rank-time); Natural floor2 + TailGuard leaves 1,131.7 rank-seconds, a **96.15% reduction**. Decomposition: 18,693.6 rank-s from the shorter critical path (epoch construction) and 9,568.6 rank-s converted into reusable capacity by coordinated exits — i.e. roughly two-thirds of the headline number is batching, one-third is elastic release.
- **Rollout latency.** Planned residency floor4 + TailGuard reaches 4462.1 s for the 5-step epoch vs 5878.3 s for Vanilla (**−24.09%**) and vs 4567.5 s for the LengthSort+TailGuard full-world control (**−2.31%**, mean paired 105.4 s, two-sided 95% t-interval [26.8, 184.0]). Vanilla and LengthSort numbers are the strongest non-AdaFloor baselines in the paper; FixedFloor and MinSkew sit on the same frontier but release less area at comparable time.
- **Epoch construction alone (non-elastic ablation).** With all 16 ranks active, LengthSort drops mean rollout time 5878.3 → 4891.3 s (−16.79%, +20.15% throughput) and adding TailGuard reaches 4567.5 s at 3368.3 tokens/s (a further −6.62% / +7.20%). This is the paper's most transferable result: it needs no EP surgery.
- **Training-signal preservation.** Across 30 updated-policy steps per method, mean response length, reward, and actor KL differ by at most 0.15%, 0.07%, and 0.14%; every update has nonzero gradients. A paired three-step trace with fixed assignments and request seeds produces identical responses, masks, and scores for all 1,536 samples under full16 and Planned floors 4, 4, and 8.
- **KV and tail safety.** Over 30 Natural-floor2 and 30 Planned-floor4 steps, maximum realized cap use is 93.14% and 88.28% with minimum headroom 8,994 and 15,599 tokens; no step exceeds its cap, all 48 validated epochs complete with no OOM or aborted response, and a 20-step stress run forces floors 2/4/8 through 41 shrink events and 20 restores with no collective timeout or HBM growth. Only 5 of 4,096 guarded short-step responses hit their caps.
- **Planner quality vs. skew-oriented matching.** Replaying 150 recorded shrink steps, the quota-aware ILP matches MinSkew on 142 and improves calculated release by 0.028–2.267% on 8. In a controlled frozen-actor experiment (Planned-F4, 133,120 KV tokens/rank, 1,710,400 tokens both arms), MinSkew reaches the first quorum at 6,000 tokens vs 5,200 for quota-aware matching: 3,093.67 vs 3,777.85 measured released rank-seconds (**+22.12%**), 34,400 vs 40,800 in calculated rank-token units, both preemption-free.
- **Sidecar reuse.** Planned floor4 without TailGuard serves 67,272 Qwen2.5-1.5B, **3,134 Qwen3-8B** (GSM8K, 4,096-token cap), and 1,465 PanguProMoE-72B queries per epoch at 76.68% / **99.46%** / 98.08% GSM8K accuracy, for only a **2–3% primary throughput loss** vs sidecar-disabled. Adding TailGuard shrinks windows and cuts goodput ~3× (21,975 / 1,024 / 478), which is the honest cost of the latency-oriented point.
- **Cross-workload generalization.** On public Eurus-2-RL code rollouts (same model, same 16-rank topology, executable test-based rewards), a request-seed-matched run cuts rollout time 8851.5 → 7031.6 s (**−20.56%**), work-normalized throughput +25.73%, dummy run 22,342 → 2,429 rank-s (**−89.13%**), and KV preemptions 94 → 1, with generated tokens differing 0.12% and mean reward 0.4854 vs 0.4849.
- **Composition with worker-level reuse.** In a two-worker EP8 experiment isolating TLT's worker-level boundary (TLT's draft training and speculative decoding omitted), cross-worker gaps expose 2,730.1 rank-s while AdaFloor adds 7,219.0 rank-s *inside* active workers — 9,949.1 total, or **3.64×** worker-level reuse alone.
- **Overheads.** Offline planning averages 4.778 s internally / 5.568 s with process prep (history update 0.031 s, graph build 1.673 s, MILP solve 2.815 s, repair 8.1 µs) — ~0.14% of the 4427.01 s five-step rollout. Online group preparation / shrink / restore cost 9.82 / 1.97 / 4.51 s, together 48.89 s = 1.10% of rollout time; HCCS traffic is 11.273 GB/s decode, 23.015 GB/s shrink, 9.053 GB/s restore.

## Authors & Credibility

Jia Wei, Ziyi Qiu, Yi Xu, and Xuehai Qian, all at the Department of Computer Science and Technology, Tsinghua University (Qian is the corresponding author). Qian's group works on computer architecture and systems for ML, and this reads like a systems-architecture paper (ISCA/ASPLOS/MICRO house style: observations → insight boxes → mechanism → Q1–Q5 evaluation). The engineering is substantial and specific: VERL 0.6.0, vLLM 0.11.0, vLLM Ascend 0.11.0rc0, PyTorch NPU, CANN 8.5.0, HCCL, SciPy MILP, NetworkX. The related-work coverage of 2025–2026 rollout systems (RollPacker, RhymeRL, TLT, Laminar, ReaL) is current, and the paper repeatedly volunteers numbers that weaken its own headline (the 2.31% marginal gain over LengthSort+TailGuard, the TailGuard sidecar goodput collapse, the 1.22% gain at an 8192-token limit). That self-reporting is a credibility signal, not a weakness.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The reframing is the real contribution: "dummy run" as EP-coupled rather than idle is a genuinely useful distinction, and to my knowledge no prior rollout system (RollPacker, RhymeRL, TLT) treats *shrinking the EP world mid-step* as a first-class lever — they all either reshape the tail or fill slack between workers. The individual pieces are conventional, though: EMA length prediction is standard, and the ILP is a small assignment problem (32 vertices, ≤496 edges) that SciPy solves in ~2.8 s offline. The paper's own Scope note is admirably honest that for identical feasible candidates "exact quota-aware optimization can retain any admissible MinSkew schedule and therefore cannot reduce the release area" — meaning the ILP's contribution is only visible when MinSkew *misses a quorum*, which the replay shows happens on 8 of 150 steps (5.3%).

**The marginal-effect problem.** The headline "24.09% faster" is measured against Vanilla random batching, but the paper's own ablation shows LengthSort+TailGuard already delivers 4567.5 s (22.3% of the total). Elastic reclamation contributes 2.31% — and the 95% CI lower bound is 26.8 s, i.e. only ~0.6% of the 4427 s rollout. With three seeds, the elastic component is barely above noise. Similarly, of the 96.15% dummy-run reduction, 18,693.6 of 28,262.2 rank-seconds (66%) come from the shorter critical path, not from coordinated exits. A reader who wants the systems trick should know that the cheap part (sorting + a quantile guard) buys two-thirds of the result; the paper does say this, but the abstract leads with 96.15%.

**Baselines.** All baselines are in-house reimplementations on the same codebase: Vanilla, LengthSort, FixedFloor (floor4 MinSkew + cross-step repair), MinSkew. There is **no end-to-end comparison against RollPacker or RhymeRL**, the two closest published tail-taming systems, and the TLT comparison deliberately drops TLT's model-specific draft training and speculative decoding — so "3.64× more reusable capacity than worker-level reuse" compares AdaFloor's full mechanism against a stripped TLT boundary, not against TLT. That is a fair *isolation* experiment but an unfair *system* comparison, and the paper does not say so explicitly.

**The operating points are mutually exclusive, and the abstract reads as if they coexist.** The 3,134 Qwen3-8B queries come from Planned floor4 **without** TailGuard; the 24.09% latency win comes from Planned floor4 **with** TailGuard. Table IV shows TailGuard cutting sidecar goodput roughly 3× (67,272 → 21,975 for Qwen2.5-1.5B, 3,134 → 1,024 for Qwen3-8B). So you cannot have the fast rollout and the sidecar throughput at once — the release windows that make the latency point good are precisely the ones too short to amortize sidecar startup. The paper states the trade-off in Discussion but the abstract's juxtaposition invites over-reading.

**Coverage gaps.** (a) One model (Qwen3-30B-A3B), one node, 16 ranks, EP16 — the entire mechanism depends on a single-node EP group where all ranks can shrink together; multi-node EP is discussed only as future work with "topology-aware survivor templates", untested. (b) One accelerator family (Ascend 910C, HCCL, CANN); nothing here establishes that the halving-stage collective path or the 13.50 GiB Planned-residency reserve ports to GPUs, and the paper admits porting "requires backend adaptation and re-profiling". (c) Floor2 is only reachable in Natural mode because Planned residency's group warmup exhausts HBM — so the deepest, most valuable floor is unavailable in the lowest-latency policy, which is a structural limitation rather than a tuning detail. (d) Training-signal equivalence is checked over 30 steps and two epochs; the paper honestly labels this "short-horizon training-signal comparability, not final convergence", but that means the central safety claim for RL (that a changed completion order and a per-step response cap don't distort learning) rests on 30 GRPO updates of one dataset.

**Data quirks worth flagging.** The dummy-run share differs between the profiled cycle (32.83%) and the three-seed mean (31.25%) — explained, but the abstract quotes only the former. The "AICore utilization 139.49%" figure for Natural mode is normalized to the sidecar-disabled config and exceeds 100%, which is confusing without the footnote. And the 8192-token-limit sensitivity run yields only −1.22% rollout time with 3,769.6 rank-s released: a useful, unglamorous data point showing the mechanism targets long decode tails and degrades to near-nothing when tails are capped — the paper deserves credit for including it, but it also bounds the applicability to exactly the long-CoT regime.

## Strengths

- The problem framing is correct and load-bearing: treating EP-coupled rank-time as distinct from schedulable idleness explains why cluster-level utilization dashboards (14.78% active vs 2.04% dummy at the *rank* level, invisible in aggregate throughput) miss this waste.
- Rare discipline in reporting: separate capacity / sidecar / latency operating points, an explicit non-elastic ablation, forecast-perturbation sensitivity (Table II, ×0.7/×1.0/×1.3/Mixed with TG-cap hits and preemption counts), and a controlled quorum experiment that isolates the matching objective from prediction error.
- Safety is treated as a first-class result, not an afterthought: measured per-floor KV budgets rather than free parameters, 93.14% max cap utilization with 8,994-token headroom, 41 forced shrink events without collective timeout, and three explicit correctness invariants separating performance policy from legality.
- The TailGuard idea is cheap, general, and independently useful — a 0.95-quantile one-sided underestimate ratio over 3 epochs costs microseconds and buys 6.62% rollout time even with zero ranks released.
- Overhead accounting is complete enough to trust: 0.14% offline planning, 1.10% online control, and a 21.51 s one-time expert prefill called out rather than buried.

## Limitations

- Single node, single model, single vendor: 16 Ascend 910C devices, Qwen3-30B-A3B, EP16. Multi-node EP, cross-node survivor templates, and GPU portability are all untested.
- The elastic contribution is small and near the noise floor (2.31%, CI [26.8, 184.0] s on a 4427 s rollout); most of the win is sortable batching plus a quantile cap.
- No head-to-head against published tail-taming systems (RollPacker, RhymeRL); TLT is composed with but not compared against end to end.
- Capacity and latency operating points are mutually exclusive (TailGuard costs ~3× sidecar goodput), so the three headline numbers cannot be realized simultaneously.
- Floor2 requires Natural mode; Planned residency cannot support it because group warmup exhausts HBM — the lowest-latency policy is structurally denied the deepest floor.
- Reward/loss equivalence is established over 30 GRPO steps on DeepScaleR only; longer-horizon RL effects of per-step response caps (e.g. truncation bias in advantage estimation) are not studied.
- Assumes remaining work and state fit on fewer devices and that transition cost (9.82 s group prep + 4.51 s restore) is amortized by a long tail; short-tail or short-response workloads see ~1% (the 8192-token case).

## Relevance

这篇属于"大规模 ML 计算基础设施"方向：它处理的是 RL post-training 里 MoE 专家并行导致的 rank 级长尾与 straggler 问题，并给出了 KV 安全约束下的调度/收缩机制，与你关注的集合通信、集群调度和慢节点主题直接相关。它不涉及训练稳定性或静默数据损坏，但其中"每步响应上限 + 截断"对训练信号的潜在影响，值得从 RL 后训练动力学的角度进一步追问。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. What happens to the GRPO advantage estimate when a response is truncated by TailGuard's C_t? The paper checks mean length, reward, and KL over 30 steps, but truncation changes the *shape* of the reward distribution for capped prompts — is there a length-dependent bias in the baseline subtraction?
2. How does the release-area objective behave when the EP group spans multiple nodes? Quorum legality then depends on inter-node collective ordering and on which node hosts each expert shard — does the 16→8→4→2 halving ladder survive, or does cross-node traffic make restore (4.51 s locally) dominate?
3. The ILP improves over MinSkew on only 8 of 150 replayed steps (5.3%). Is the 2.8 s MILP solve worth it versus a greedy release-aware matching heuristic, given the 22.12% controlled-experiment gain came from a hand-constructed case?
4. What is the actual end-to-end comparison against RollPacker or RhymeRL on the same DeepScaleR workload? LengthSort+TailGuard already captures 22.3% of the 24.09%; the remaining 2.31% may not justify EP-shrink complexity against a mature tail-batching baseline.
5. Can the sidecar lease be made long enough to matter without TailGuard — e.g. by pre-warming sidecar weights during the previous epoch so the 2–3% primary cost buys 67K queries rather than 22K?
6. Does the 13.50 GiB Planned-residency reserve (which drops floor4 KV from 280,576 to 133,120 tokens) scale with EP degree, and would it make floor2 feasible on larger HBM parts like 141 GB H200 or MI300X?
