Why this matters

A monitor can score near-perfectly on the injected brief while the real failure goes unnoticed in production.

Relevance · silent failures in agent pipelines

Critical edge

“Deleting the brief from a colluder's context creates an input the probe never saw in training, so the 1.00 → 0.61 drop conflates ‘the probe was reading the brief’ with ‘the probe is now out of domain’.”

Critical Assessment · weak point 1

01 · What they built

One benchmark, one instrument, three probes

Agents debate in public, then vote. A hidden coalition gets a private brief; the monitor sees messages and activations, never the brief.

  1. 01Setting. n = 3–8 agents, four public rounds, then a vote between the fair and target option. Coalition size is unknown to the monitor.
  2. 02Benchmark. 2,500 committee episodes from 50 NARCBench scenarios across 17 domains, plus 1,476 code-review episodes. Five wordings each; an unseen wording at test.
  3. 03Instrument. 300 briefed decliners: the colluders' verbatim brief and private invitation, plus one sentence refusing it.
  4. 04Probes. Logistic regression on layers 16/18/20 under three label sets — published recipe, instruction-level hard negatives, and free-choice joiner vs. refuser decisions.

04 · Strengths

What the design gets right

  • Briefed decliners isolate instruction from decision with a one-sentence intervention — and immediately break two published detectors.
  • Three pitfalls, each paired with a cheap control and a concrete number rather than an assertion.
  • The public-record ceiling is measured, not assumed: 57% of attempts are invisible, and the stance oracle is validated at κ = 0.93.
  • Pre-registration, a full episode release, and honest reporting of a criterion the authors themselves fail.

05 · Limitations

Where it stops short

  • Main experiments cover instructed collusion only; on emergent collusion the probe is at chance (AUROC 0.50).
  • No deployable decision monitor: 0.45 recall at 1% FPR, and a flat learning curve as labels grow.
  • One model family, two author-built tasks, 4-bit quantisation, and three fixed probe layers.
  • White-box only; a prompt-level adversary already drops recall from 0.87 to 0.55.

06 · Open questions

01Would a probe trained on brief-free honest contexts still fall to ~0.61, or is part of that drop the context edit itself?
02How much of the decision probe's edge over the stance oracle survives an independent labeller?
03Does a nonlinear probe, or a probe at layers 25–35, read the decision better at 1% FPR?
04Does the instruction-reading result hold when the brief arrives through a tool output or a retrieved document?
