> **一句话总结：** 作者用"校准概念剖面"（每个变化都以同一 prompt 电池的两次抽样之差作为噪声单位）追踪 OpenCoder-1.5B 在 4.19B→2.03T token 的 35 个检查点上 169 个 Python 语法构造的 5% 载体神经元集合（7,373 个），发现三个时间尺度彼此分离：强制位置的 loss 在训练前 4% 就已触底，模型对这些神经元的依赖却一路增长（移除它们比移除同尺寸随机集合多损失 0.06 / 0.25 / 0.40 nats），而"哪些神经元在承载"到最后一个检查点都没稳定——169 个构造无一有 settle 日期，84B 时测得的旧集合在训练末移除已与随机集合不可区分。

> **TL;DR:** Tracking 169 Python constructs in OpenCoder-1.5B across 35 checkpoints from 4.19B to 2.03T tokens (plus a 24-checkpoint annealing branch), with a twin-battery noise unit on every measurement, the author finds behaviour and membership live on different clocks: the loss at grammar-forced positions is flat for 20 of 24 series over the last 96% of training (~0.09 nats floor), yet ablating the current 5% carrier set costs 0.40 nats more than ablating a random set of the same size at 2.03T tokens versus only 0.06 nats at 4.19B, and 0 of 169 constructs ever settle — the carrier set is still turning over at ~2× the twin-battery noise at the final checkpoint. The set measured at 84B tokens and ablated at the end raises loss by 0.14 nats over random sets [−0.01, 0.42], p = 0.09, i.e. indistinguishable from random, so the function is carried by whichever neurons happen to be current. Injected-settling controls show the null has teeth: an abrupt settling is detected in 99.1–99.4% of 338 draws at any date from 84B to 1.5T tokens, with 0 false detections in 338 uninjected series. The paper's pre-registered hypothesis (carriers settle) is refuted; the discussion of causes is explicitly flagged as not a result.

## 核心贡献

- **把"表征漂移"从神经科学搬到预训练语言模型，并给它配了一个噪声单位。** 每个构造的测量都做两遍（twins：同样的 8 个结构核心、不同的周边代码），两次之差就是"仅由周边环境造成的变化"；twin ceiling 约 0.84、twin turnover 0.16，之后所有 turnover 数字都先减去这个量。这是全文方法论的骨架，也是它敢说"没 settle"的前提。
- **三个时间尺度的分离是本文真正的新东西。** 行为（强制位置 loss）在前 4%（84B token）触底；依赖（移除载体集合的因果效应）从 4.19B 的 0.06 nats 涨到 84B 的 0.25、再到末端的 0.40；成员身份（哪些神经元在集合里）到 2.03T 仍在变。三者不在同一时间尺度上，任何"某层/某电路在 X token 处成形"的单一叙述都会漏掉这一点。
- **推翻了自己预注册的核心假设，并且公开了推翻过程。** 假设 + 9 条预测 + 分析计划在 2026-10-09 之前哈希锁定（sha256 33b0721b…），读数据后发现"没有构造 settle"，其中 2 条预测被证实（P1、P7）、2 条被证伪（premise、P5）、5 条因缺少 settle 日期无法读取、1 条半对半错。附录 B 原样保留了当初的写法与裁决。
- **报告并修复了四个"尺子"本身的错误**，每一个都会凭空造出 settle 日期：turnover 水平随比较间隔下降（Spearman −0.813，1,404 对），导致 12 个构造假性 settle 在 1.527T；退火分支太短使 169/169 假性 settle 在第一个退火检查点；standardised 与 raw 两种 size 给出相反符号（standardised 上涨 144 个、raw 下降 148 个）；用 ×1.25 对的 turnover 除以步数假定线性。修正后的规则要求"观察到穿越阈值 + 之前至少一个点在阈值下 + 之后至少两个点 + 两种配对方式一致"。
- **漂移的形状被刻画成"持续池 + 变化样本"**：18,034,712 次神经元离集中 57% 会回来（同期不在集合中的匹配神经元只有 17%，lift 3.3，区间 3.27–3.37），中位缺席 10,000 步；但集合整体从不重组——167/169 个构造的最近邻集合永远是相邻检查点，任意两个检查点的集合相似度都低于同一检查点两次电池抽样的相似度。
- **权重层面给出"受限随机游走"的读数**：第 12 层 MLP 的三个矩阵在 1,000 步内移动 0.51–0.67 个 η√D，在 20k–30k 步窗口内从早期的 0.65 降到后期的 0.30，即相邻步越来越互相抵消；同时三个矩阵的 Frobenius 范数在预训练中分别增长 81%、89%、83%。
- **给出可复现的资产**：HuggingFace 数据集 `piotrwilam/AtlasOpenCoder`（DOI 10.57967/hf/11472）+ GitHub `piotrwilam/AtlasRepresentationalDrift`，README 里有 manifest 把论文中每个数字映射到文件、字段与重建脚本，预注册材料与 deviation log 放在 `preregistration/` 目录。

## Key Contributions

- **Ports representational drift into LLM pretraining with a proper noise floor.** Every construct is measured twice on the same eight structural cores with different surroundings ("twins"); the twins' disagreement (ceiling 0.84, turnover 0.16) is subtracted from every reported change, so "the set changed" means "it changed more than resampling the battery would produce."
- **Separates three timescales that the literature usually conflates.** Behaviour (loss at grammar-forced positions) floors within the first 4% of training; causal reliance on the carrier set grows monotonically to the end; set membership never settles. The claim is not "circuits form late" or "circuits form early" but that these are three different clocks.
- **Refutes its own pre-registered hypothesis and documents the refutation.** Hypothesis, nine predictions and the analysis plan were hashed on 2026-10-09; after reading, the premise ("carriers stop changing for most constructs") fails, P1 and P7 are confirmed, P5 is refuted, and five predictions become unreadable for lack of a settling date. Appendix B preserves the original text verbatim with verdicts appended.
- **Identifies four artefacts of the measuring instrument**, each capable of manufacturing a settling date: gap-dependence of turnover (Spearman −0.813 over 1,404 construct–checkpoint pairs; twelve constructs falsely dated to 1.527T), the too-short annealing branch (169/169 falsely settled), standardised-vs-raw response size (opposite signs, 144 up vs 148 down), and per-step rates derived from ×1.25 pairs. The corrected rule requires an observed threshold crossing with a point below it before, two points after, and agreement between two partner-selection schemes.
- **Characterises the change as a persisting pool with a changing sample**: 57% of 18,034,712 departures return versus 17% for matched controls (lift 3.3 [3.27, 3.37]), median absence 10,000 steps, 39% back at the next checkpoint — yet for 167 of 169 constructs no two checkpoints' sets are as alike as two batteries at one checkpoint, ruling out switching among a few fixed wirings.
- **Reads the weights as a confined walk**: layer 12's gate/up/down matrices move 0.51–0.67 of η√D over 1,000 steps, and the free-walk fraction falls from 0.65 early to 0.30 late in the 20k–30k-step window, while Frobenius norms grow 81%, 89%, 83%.
- **Ships a reproducible record**: HF dataset `piotrwilam/AtlasOpenCoder` (DOI 10.57967/hf/11472) plus `piotrwilam/AtlasRepresentationalDrift`, with a manifest mapping every number to its file, field and rebuild script, and the pre-registration hashes and deviation log in `preregistration/`.

## Methodology

**The record.** One model, one run: OpenCoder-1.5B (Huang et al., 2024), 35 pretraining checkpoints spanning 4.19B to 2.03T tokens at a constant learning rate (η = 3×10⁻⁴ after a 2,000-step warm-up), plus 24 checkpoints of an annealing branch forked at 1.57T and run to 1.67T with a decaying rate. Spacing is deliberately uneven: Period 1 (4.19B–84B, 4% of training) has a checkpoint every 1,000 steps (4.19M tokens each), 19 neighbour pairs; Period 2 (84B–2.03T, 96% of training) has 15 checkpoints each 1.25× the previous, so gaps grow from 5,000 to 91,000 steps. Because distance and training position are confounded in Period 2, all rates are read at fixed gaps (~1k, 5k–10k, 20k–30k, 50k–100k steps) with the twin turnover subtracted.

**The instrument** is the calibrated concept profile of Wilam (2026). 188 Python constructs are generated as prompt batteries; 169 pass the gate that the model's read site is the token piece the prompt intends. Each construct gets three prompt sets: A (construct in its role), B (the same token out of role — inside a string, comment, or name, with companions matched to A), and C (ordinary code, shared pool). The profile of a construct at a checkpoint is, for each of the 147,456 MLP hidden units (24 layers × 6,144, read before the down-projection), the mean standardised activation on A minus that on B, where standardisation uses the neuron's mean and spread over C in one of 20 cells (token class × which fifth of the prompt). The 5% of neurons with the largest entries are the carrier set: 7,373 neurons. Two random 5% sets overlap by 0.05.

**The noise unit.** Each construct has eight structurally distinct cores; every prompt is a core in drawn surroundings, and the battery is drawn twice on the same cores with different surroundings. Their disagreement is measured three ways, one per object type: a twin unit on profiles (√(2(1−cos)) between the twins), a twin ceiling/turnover on carrier sets (0.84 overlap / 0.16 turnover at any trained checkpoint, against 0.28 for an untrained model), and a per-neuron twin difference for departures. Intervals come from a core bootstrap (eight cores resampled, same resample for both twins and all compared checkpoints) or a construct bootstrap for medians over constructs.

**Six metrics per construct per checkpoint**: turnover of the 5% set; size of the raw response; depth (mean layer of its weight); loss at the construct's marked positions; sharing of carriers with other constructs; sparsity of the profile. A "marked position" is where the construct's grammar decides the next token — forced (after `for x in y` the colon is obliged), expected (what follows `for x`), bound-name (a later use of a bound name), or a scope boundary. Only 16 constructs have such positions, giving 24 loss series; the behaviour and reliance claims rest on those 16.

**The settling rule** (after correction) requires: an observed crossing of a kept-fraction threshold θ (grid 0.50–0.95, θ = 0.70 primary, so θ is a sensitivity not a hinge); at least one checkpoint below θ before the crossing; at least two checkpoints after it; agreement between a ratio-form partner (1.25–2.0× tokens) and a fixed-step partner; and in the ratio form, the kept fraction above θ at every available distance band from the crossing to the end. Dates in the last two checkpoints are censored, not reported.

**Power of the null.** Settling curves are injected into measured turnover series at five dates from 84B to 1.5T, in two shapes (step and gradual), with the noise of other constructs' series, 338 draws per cell, then the corrected rule is applied.

**The cause discussion** (explicitly "a discussion, not a result") tests five candidate accounts against the §4 readings: free diffusion from gradient noise; displacement by new learning; branching of a carrier's meaning; equivalent implementations (many neuron sets compute the same function); and weight decay. Each is scored for what it predicts and whether the record bears it out.

## Results & Highlights

- **Behaviour floors at 4% of training, then stays flat.** For the 24 loss series at the forced and expected positions of the 16 marked constructs in OpenCoder-1.5B, the loss falls from 4.19B to 84B tokens for 15 series, is flat for 6 and rises for 3; over the remaining 96% of training (84B → 2.03T) it is flat for 20 of 24, rising for 4 and falling for 0. The forced-position floor is about 0.09 nats, and at expected positions the loss after `for x` is 0.55–0.59 nats against the battery's own continuation entropy of 0.571 nats — the loss of a reader who knows only the rates.
- **Reliance on the carriers grows three- to seven-fold while behaviour is flat.** Removing the current 5% carrier set raises the marked-position loss by a median 0.06 nats [0.02, 0.14] over removing a random set of the same size at 4.19B tokens, 0.25 [0.03, 0.66] at 84B, and 0.40 [0.10, 0.70] at 2.03T; above zero for 12, 14 and 14 of the 16 constructs, sign test p = 0.038, 0.002 and 0.002. Taken individually against 320 random sets, only 2, 8 and 8 of the 16 constructs exceed every random set at the three references. The largest effect is `Try`'s: 5.95 nats above a random set on a 0.088-nat baseline.
- **The old carrier set is functionally gone.** The set measured at 84B tokens, with only 14% of its members still in the current set, raises the loss at the end by 0.14 nats over random sets [−0.01, 0.42], 10 of 14 constructs, p = 0.09 — it cannot be separated from a random set (4 of 14 old sets exceed every random set, against 8 of 16 for the current set). Against the current set it is 0.28 nats weaker [−0.77, −0.11], with 11 of 14 constructs below. The function is carried by whichever neurons are current.
- **Zero of 169 constructs settle, and the null has teeth.** The turnover curve is still moving at the last checkpoint for 169 of 169 constructs; at the end the change over 20,000–30,000 steps is still about twice the twin turnover. Injected abrupt settlings are detected in 99.1–99.4% of 338 draws at every date from 84B to 1.5T tokens; injected gradual settlings in 99.4–100% up to 500B and in none at 1T and 1.5T (where the approach is still under way at the end of the record); on uninjected series the rule invents none in 338. So "0 of 169" means: no abrupt settling anywhere from 84B to 1.5T, and no gradual settling before ~500B tokens.
- **The slowing is real and is not a ceiling effect.** At a fixed 1,000-step gap in Period 1, turnover minus twin turnover falls from 0.54 (first pair) to 0.19 (last pair). At 50k–100k-step gaps it falls from 0.53 at the first reading of Period 2 to 0.35 at the last; at 20k–30k gaps, from 0.40 to 0.30; first and last intervals do not overlap. Ceilings stay near-flat over the record (0.73–0.80 for the 5% set), so the decline is not the ceiling moving. The threshold-free graded profile behaves identically: 1−cosine over 50k–100k steps falls from 0.49 to 0.24 against a ceiling of 0.87–0.88, and all six cuts from the top 1% to entries above one noise unit slow together (0.925–0.964 of their own ceilings at the first checkpoint).
- **Departed neurons return at 3.3× the matched rate; the set never reassembles.** Of 18,034,712 departures between neighbouring checkpoints, 57% return to the same construct's set later, against 17% for neurons matched on being out of the set at the same moment (lift 3.3, interval 3.27–3.37); median absence 10,000 steps (quartiles 2,000–58,000), 39% back at the next checkpoint. 92.5% of departures are falls larger than the neuron's twin difference, so this is not threshold jitter. But for 167 of 169 constructs every checkpoint's nearest set is its immediate neighbour, and no two checkpoints' sets are as alike as two batteries at one checkpoint; neurons move together only ~1.5× what independent neurons with the same persistence would show.
- **Weights move as a confined walk that tightens.** Over 1,000 steps, layer 12's gate, up and down matrices move 0.51–0.67 of η√D (the free-random-walk distance); over 20,000–30,000 steps the fraction is 0.65 early in training and 0.30 late, so successive steps cancel more as training goes on. The carriers' rows move as random rows of the same layer do (within 1.4%, and within 0.3% for 15 of 24 comparisons). Meanwhile the three matrices' Frobenius norms grow by 81%, 89% and 83%.
- **A construct's two tokens converge toward the role without merging.** A statement-initial keyword is a different vocabulary entry indented and at module level; at the first checkpoint the two tokens' carrier sets for `for` share 0.44 of what the twin ceiling allows, at the end 0.59. Across the twelve constructs whose token differs by scope, every share is higher at the end, running from 0.44 (`Match`) to 0.71 (`Try`).
- **Sharing between constructs falls almost everywhere.** Over 14,196 construct pairs, carrier sharing falls beyond its noise for 11,402 and rises for 1,868 (median sharing 0.130 against 0.050 by chance; median fall 0.034, eight times the noise). When a given pair starts to separate cannot be timed — the interval on the beginning spans a median 30 of 35 checkpoints — and the size of the separation is unrelated to the size of the rarer construct's loss drop (ρ ≈ −0.04).
- **Annealing slows the drift for every construct but cannot be attributed to the learning rate.** Measured over equal steps on the branch forked at 1.57T, carrier change slows for 169 of 169 constructs; 5 of 24 loss series fall within the branch, and 7 of the 23 with a pretraining counterpart end more than one noise unit below the pretraining floor. But the branch spans 0.026 decades of tokens — less than a third of one pretraining checkpoint step — and the data mix changes at the same moment as the rate, so the slowing cannot be credited to the schedule on this record.
- **Frequency orders the shrinking of the response, but not the drift.** A construct met ten times more often reaches the point where its raw response stops shrinking about 1.2× earlier in tokens (coefficient on log frequency −0.064, interval −0.103 to −0.036); how often the token itself appears adds nothing once role frequency is in. Yet the rank correlation between a construct's frequency and the fraction of its carriers kept is a median −0.07 across the 19 Period-1 checkpoints, never significant in the predicted direction, and −0.03 late — the frequency prediction from the two-layer derivation is refuted at the level of the carrier set.
- **The response shrinks while the loss is flat.** The raw size of the response falls over training for 148 of 169 constructs and stops falling for 90, mostly between 500B and 1.5T tokens; depth stops changing for 59 and sparsity for 97. Where all three stop, they stop at different times spread over a factor of three in tokens, in no fixed order.
- **Pre-registered scorecard: 2 confirmed, 2 refuted, 5 unreadable, 1 mixed.** P1 (loss flat while carriers change) and P7 (response does not grow after the loss is flat; 90 of 90 fall, none rise) are confirmed; the premise (0 settling dates of 169) and P5 (frequent constructs change less) are refuted; P2, P3, P4, P8, P9 and the six-curve agreement question cannot be read because they all require settling dates.

## Authors & Credibility

Single author: Piotr Wilam, University College London, corresponding at a personal Gmail address. This is the second paper in a two-paper programme — the first, *Calibrated Concept Profiles: Making the Reading of a Model's Neurons a Measurement* (Wilam, 2026), supplies the instrument, the 20-cell yardstick and the licence checks; this paper applies it to a pretraining record. The citation list is unusually well matched to the argument: it cites the neuroscience drift literature it borrows from (Driscoll 2017, Rule 2019/2020, Schoonover 2021), the two-layer SGD-drift derivations it tries to test (Pashakhanloo & Koulakov 2023; Ratzon et al. 2024), and the recent LLM-side measurements it has to reconcile with (Nandan et al. 2025; Gurnee et al. 2024; Wang et al. 2026; Yao et al. 2026b; Aljaafari et al. 2026; Bayazit et al. 2026). Credibility signals are strong on process: hashed pre-registration with a stated approval date, a deviation log recording all four rule changes, a manifest linking every number to a file/field/script, and an appendix that keeps the failed exploratory reading (D.4) rather than deleting it. The weakness is scope, not honesty: one model, one run, one schedule, one author, and the instrument itself is only available as a preprint with an identifier still "to be added."

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The individual ingredients are borrowed — the drift framing from neuroscience, the concept-profile instrument from the author's own prior paper, the checkpoint record from OpenCoder's public release. What is new is the *separation of three clocks* on one record with a per-measurement noise unit: behaviour at 4% of training, reliance still rising at 96%, membership never settling. That is a genuine empirical contribution and it cuts against a lot of the "circuit X forms at step Y" literature, which typically measures one clock and assumes the other two follow.

**Rigor.** The instrument work is the best part. The twin battery is not decoration: without it, the gap-dependence of turnover (Spearman −0.813 over 1,404 pairs) manufactures twelve settling dates at exactly 1.527T tokens, and the author shows this and fixes it. The power analysis on injected settlings (99.1–99.4% detection, 0/338 false positives) is exactly the right response to a null result, and the honest downgrade of the null past 500B tokens for gradual settling is the kind of limit most papers bury.

**Where the rigor thins.** Four specific things. (1) *The pre-registration is partial in a way that matters.* The hashed plan fixed the hypothesis and nine predictions, but the rule that produces the headline "0 of 169" is the *corrected* rule, changed after reading, and the power analysis was run on that corrected rule applied to the same series. The corrections all push in the conservative direction (they remove false dates, not add them), and the deviation log is public, so this is defensible — but it is not the same evidential status as a rule fixed in advance, and §C.1 says so in one sentence rather than confronting it. (2) *The reliance claim rests on 16 constructs and a weak aggregate at the first reference.* The 4.19B reading is 0.06 nats [0.02, 0.14] with sign test p = 0.038 and only 2 of 16 constructs beating every one of 320 random sets; the growth to 0.40 nats at the end is solid, but "the model already relied on these neurons at 4.19B" is the thinnest of the three legs. The `Try` effect (5.95 nats on a 0.088-nat baseline) also shows the construct-level distribution is heavy-tailed, which makes the median-over-16 the load-bearing statistic and the per-construct ranks worth more attention than they get in the body. (3) *The old-set ablation is an argument from a null.* "Cannot be separated from a random set" (p = 0.09, 10 of 14) is absence of evidence; the positive form of the claim rests on the 0.28-nat contrast with the current set, which is stronger but is a different comparison. (4) *One layer for the weight analysis.* The confined-walk result is read on layer 12 alone, and the paper's own D.1 check shows that picking a single "verdict layer" per construct is not robust — excluding the last layer moves the chosen layer for 41% of constructs. The weight result is suggestive, not established.

**Baselines and comparisons.** There is no model-vs-model baseline because there is only one model: Pythia-1.4B appears solely as a battery-gate check (166 of 188 constructs pass, with comprehensions lost to a different tokenizer), not as a replication of the drift measurement. So the strongest external claim — that drift is a property of pretraining rather than of OpenCoder — is untested. The paper is candid about this (limit 1), but it means the headline generalizes over exactly one 1.5B run.

**Data quirks worth naming.** The battery's factor rates are drawn from a public corpus of 253,398 parsed Python files because OpenCoder's training data was not released; the stimulus distribution is therefore a stand-in, not the model's actual mix. The exploratory reading in D.4 is a clean example of this biting: departures looked attracted to token-sharing constructs (lift 1.41, 1.56, 1.77) until the battery's own composition was controlled, at which point the lift fell below chance (0.65, 0.79, 0.86) — every one of the 49 token-related pairs shares its token in the cores. Reporting this is good practice; it also means the "where do departing neurons go" question is unanswerable on this battery.

**Overclaiming check.** The title says "behaviour settles." What is measured is the loss at grammar-forced positions of 16 constructs, where the floor is ~0.09 nats — near-deterministic next-token prediction, not code competence. At expected positions the model sits at the battery's continuation entropy, and at bound-name positions it is still at 2.5 nats against a marginal entropy of 5.9, i.e. clearly above the true floor. The paper states this ("learning has finished where a construct forces the next token and has not finished where it governs one"), so the title is a compression, not a distortion — but a reader skimming the abstract could reasonably come away thinking the model stopped learning Python at 4% of training, which is not what was shown.

**Significance.** High for the interpretability-methods audience and for anyone who plans to do checkpoint-to-checkpoint circuit tracking: the twin-battery noise unit and the four artefacts are reusable, and the "no settling date" result is a direct challenge to circuit-formation timelines measured at coarse spacing. Lower for capability work: the paper demonstrates no downstream consequence of the drift, and in fact shows the readout is preserved (linear probe median accuracy 0.998–0.999), so on this evidence drift is functionally benign — which is the interesting finding, but also caps the urgency.

## Strengths

- The twin-battery noise unit is a real methodological contribution, not a cosmetic control: it is what converts "the set changed" into a measurement, and the paper shows what happens without it (twelve fabricated settling dates at 1.527T).
- Three-timescale separation is a clean, falsifiable, and genuinely surprising result — the reliance curve rising while the loss curve is flat is the kind of finding that reorganizes how one reads the circuit-formation literature.
- Exceptional process transparency: hashed pre-registration, public deviation log, the four artefacts reported with their dates, a failed exploratory reading kept in the appendix, and a manifest mapping every number to its source file and rebuild script.
- The instrument-object distinction in §8 is the most useful paragraph in the paper: it explains why Nandan et al. (universal neurons persist), Aljaafari et al. (node sets consolidate by step 128–5,000), Wang et al. (top-1% overlap ≤ 10.2%), and this paper (7,373-neuron thresholded sets never settle) are not in conflict — they measure different objects at different cuts.
- The loss-floor calibration is careful and quantitative: 0.09 nats at forced positions, 0.55–0.59 against a 0.571-nat continuation entropy at expected positions, 2.5 against 5.9 at bound-name positions — three different notions of "done," correctly distinguished.
- The five candidate causes are scored against the record rather than asserted, and the one that survives (equivalent implementations) is the one that makes the neuroscience parallel exact.
- Data and code are released with a DOI and a per-number manifest; reproducibility is better than most papers in this area.

## Limitations

- **One model, one run, one schedule.** All drift claims are OpenCoder-1.5B at constant learning rate. No seed replication of the main result; Pythia-1.4B is used only to check that the battery survives a different tokenizer.
- **Behaviour and reliance rest on 16 of 169 constructs**, and those 16 are exactly the ones whose grammar forces a next token — the easiest positions in the language, not the ones where code competence lives.
- **The record starts at 4.19B tokens**, so formation is not observed: at the first checkpoint 167 of 169 constructs already carry half their profile's squared norm in 5% of neurons. "Everything had already formed" is a statement about the window, not about training.
- **The annealing branch cannot answer the learning-rate question**: 0.026 decades wide, less than a third of one checkpoint step, with the data mix changing at the same moment as the rate. The decisive experiment (one corpus, two schedules) is proposed, not run.
- **The null loses power past ~500B tokens for gradual settling**, so "never settles" is established for abrupt settling across the whole record and for gradual settling only in the first quarter.
- **The 5% cut is arbitrary**, and roughly a third of turnover is neurons crossing that line by a small margin; the six-cut ablation (top 1%, 2%, 5%, entries above 1/2/3 noise units) shows the verdict and the rate are stable, which mitigates but does not remove the concern.
- **Synthetic Python only**, shown to a model that read 607 languages; natural-language constructs and non-code domains are untested, and the one cross-model check already shows the battery is tokenizer-sensitive (five comprehension constructs lost on Pythia).
- **Weight-walk analysis is one layer** (layer 12), and the paper's own robustness check says single-layer verdicts are unstable.
- **No causal or downstream consequence is demonstrated.** Drift is shown to be functionally benign here (readout preserved, old set's ablation effect gone), so the practical import is diagnostic rather than actionable.
- **The discussion of causes is explicitly not a result**, and the paper says so; readers looking for an explanation will not find one.

## Relevance

与"预训练动力学 / 机制可解释性"这条线高度相关：它给出了一个可复用的测量协议（twin 噪声单位 + 固定间隔读数），并用它证明"电路在某步成形"这类结论对检查点间隔和集合切法极度敏感。若关注代码模型或神经元级归因，这篇的负面结果（169/169 无 settle、旧载体集合到训练末已等同随机）值得直接引用。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the "never settles" result survive a second seed of OpenCoder-1.5B, or a different 1.5B code model at the same token budget? The paper's own Pythia-1.4B check stops at the battery gate.
2. The decisive experiment is named but not run: one corpus, two models, constant vs decaying learning rate. How much of the continued slowing at constant η would disappear under decay — and does the *carrier set* stop moving at the same point the loss does?
3. If the function is carried by whichever neurons are current, what happens to a circuit-level intervention (e.g. an SAE feature or an attribution-based ablation) computed at 500B tokens and applied at 2T? The paper implies it should fail, but does not test it.
4. Why does reliance grow while the loss is flat? The paper offers equivalent implementations as the best-supported cause but does not say what the "pool" is as an object. Is it a subspace, a set of directions, or just the layer's residual capacity?
5. At bound-name positions the loss is still 2.5 nats against a 5.9-nat marginal — the model is clearly still learning there. Do carriers at those positions settle, or is the "never settles" result partly an artefact of restricting the readable positions to forced grammar?
6. Does the 3.3× return lift of departed neurons reflect a persistent pool or a slow-moving threshold? The 92.5% "falls larger than twin difference" check addresses jitter but not a drifting cut.
7. Would the same three-timescale separation appear in natural language (subject–verb agreement, as in Yao et al. 2026b) at 1,000-step spacing, or is code's forced grammar a special case where the loss floor is unusually sharp?
