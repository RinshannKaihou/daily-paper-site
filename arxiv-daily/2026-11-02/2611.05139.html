一句话：可迁移的不是 harness 本身，而是「如何改 harness」的知识——离线用 A/B/C 三重 rollout 蒸馏出文本化 Meta-Skill，上线后冻结，只在单次 run 内做任务局部编辑。

Headline result

+14.67pts

Terminal-Bench 2.1, mean@5 over 60 held-out tasks: 81.00% ± 0.91 vs 66.33% ± 2.17 for the shared NexAU seed.

DeepSWE 1.1: 44.44% vs 34.67% (+9.77) — most evolution baselines fail to beat the seed; AHE only ties it.

Why it matters

Agent self-improvement keeps overfitting a shared scaffold. Meta2 makes the editing policy the reusable artifact — and undercuts offline-heavy rivals on total cost.

把「自我改进」的对象，从 scaffold 上移到「如何改 scaffold」的知识。

Read it critically

The +14.67 is not compute-matched: Meta2 spends 1.9× the seed’s online budget ($64.72 vs $34.09), and no equal-budget control is run.

Red flag 1 of 3 · no compute-matched control

01

How it works

two roles, one frozen model

Every run starts from a minimal seed harness h(0). A task agent works; a separate evolver sees the run only at deterministic checkpoints and picks KEEP or a bounded EVOLVE. No edit outlives the run.

Offline · precompute ℳ

  1. Contrast. Each training task × 3 conditions: A agent alone · B evolver + current ℳ · C evolver + training-only verifier.
  2. Distill. REFLECT per contrast → AGGREGATE across ≥2 tasks → UPDATE ℳ. Directions become act / hold / avoid.
  3. Freeze. A/C rollouts are reused; B is recollected each batch. Deployment never writes back.

Online · within-run edits

  1. Reset. Every task restarts from the same h(0) — no carry-over between runs.
  2. Checkpoint. Every 5 steps in the best config, the evolver reads the trace and workspace, then KEEPs or edits one bounded channel.
  3. Discard. Edits die with the run; the next task cannot inherit a task-specific hack.
Where it sits Offline Pre-run Within-run After-run

Within-run is the only regime that can react before the motivating attempt ends — but it cannot see that run’s verifier.

14 Terminal-Bench entries carry a type (act/hold/avoid), a time point (start/during/end), and a trigger written as a relation — “claim contradicted by command output” — not a filename. Channels: one-turn notice, standing instruction, read-only hook, added tool.

04a

Strengths

  • Reframes the problem. Optimize the knowledge for evolving the harness, not the harness itself — dissolving the overfit-then-freeze failure mode.
  • A/B/C contrast is the best idea. It manufactures positive examples on tasks where both A and B fail; the DeepSWE result fits that mechanism doing real work.
  • The right control. Self-distilled vs contrastive isolates “having a library” from “having a contrastive library,” and the asymmetry is the paper’s most convincing number.
  • Honest reporting. Per-cell standard deviations, offline/online cost split, a negative L=1 result, and case studies with real near-miss counts (58/60, 74 vs 75).
04b

Limitations

  • No cross-benchmark or cross-model transfer. Four Meta-Skills, one per (benchmark, backbone) cell. “Reusable” means within-distribution.
  • No compute-matched control. Online cost is ~1.9× the seed, so some of the +14.67 may be bought rather than learned.
  • Tiny samples. 29 / 38 training tasks, entries need only ≥2-task support; no confidence intervals or significance tests anywhere.
  • No per-entry attribution. The 14-entry library is never leave-one-out ablated, and the condition-C leakage audit is unquantified.
05

Open questions

what the next experiment should be

Five experiments the paper leaves open — each would sharpen or qualify the claim.

Q1

Does a Terminal-Bench-trained ℳ transfer zero-shot to DeepSWE — and the reverse?

Q2

How much of +14.67 survives an equal-budget NexAU (two attempts + self-critique at $64.72)?

Q3

Which of the 14 entries carry the gain — and do the avoid rules ever fire?

Q4

Is ±0.91 a property of the method, or an artifact of resetting while baselines carry state?

Q5

What happens when the evolver is a weaker model than the task agent?
