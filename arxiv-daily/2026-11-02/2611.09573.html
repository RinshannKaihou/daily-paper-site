AI weather models lose almost no skill in fp16 — if the physical state stays fp32 — and run 1.3–3.5× faster.

Half-precision inference of AI weather prediction models: negligible loss of skill at a fraction of the cost

Why this matters

One code change fits a 1.26B-parameter weather model on a $1,000 16 GB GPU — enough headroom to run ensembles instead of single forecasts.

Aurora 1.5 peaks at 11.6 GB in persistent fp16; its default mixed-precision mode runs out of memory on the same card.
Critical edge
CAVEAT

“Negligible loss of skill” is measured against each model’s own fp32 run and against ERA5 — which is also the training target for all five models.

02

The recipe, and how it was tested

persistent conversion, not automatic mixed precision

Protocol

  • Five public fp32 checkpoints, three families, one 16 GB card: FastNet, FourCastNet, Pangu-Weather (24 h), SFNO and Aurora 1.5 — 62M to 1.26B parameters.
  • Every weight and in-network op in fp16; the state, normalisation statistics and denormalisation stay fp32. fp32 weights are discarded, so this is harsher than AMP — and the only mode that fits: Aurora’s default AMP runs out of memory.
  • Paired verification on 52 stratified 2024 dates (one per week, four cycles each 13×), ERA5 at 0.25°, variables 2t / msl / ws10 / z500 / t850, days 1–10, cosine-latitude-weighted RMSE with 95% CIs and minimum-detectable-effect reporting.
  • Significance re-expressed as equivalent lead time τ ≈ Δ / (dRMSE/dt), so a percentage becomes hours of forecast range.
  • Diagnostics: 10° error cells, zonal spectra, four named 2024 storms, an 8-member Aurora ensemble on matched seeds, forward hooks for inf/NaN and peak magnitude, one-at-a-time component ablations.

Why the one-liner fails

the paper’s sharpest practical finding

What people actually write

model = Aurora(...).to(torch.float16) # inputs converted before normalising # → physical units rounded to 16 bits

Aurora and Pangu-Weather convert their inputs before normalising, so a one-line cast rounds pressure in pascal and temperature in kelvin — not normalised anomalies.

The bf16 version does not overflow, but the rounding error alone rivals day-1 forecast error and recurs at every step.

Share of values above fp16’s 65,504 ceiling

  • 100%mean sea-level pressure (max 1.05 × 10⁵ Pa) → infinity
  • 97%surface pressure
  • 81–100%geopotential at 400, 300 and 50 hPa

Aurora returns no finite value from the first 6-hour step. Whole-model bf16 stays finite and costs up to +167% RMSE; rounding an ERA5 state to bf16 shifts 2t by 0.57 K and msl by 148 Pa.

What the paper recommends

state = state.to(torch.float32) x = normalise(state) # fp32 x = model(x.to(torch.float16)) # fp16 net out = denormalise(x.float()) # fp32

No module of any model returned an inf or NaN across 52 dates. After normalisation, the same rounding error shrinks 16–80×.

What you pay, what you keep

  • ≤1.4%worst-case global RMSE change (four of five models ≤ 0.5%)

  • 1.3–3.5×faster; 1.4–3.7× less energy per 10-day forecast

05

What holds up, what does not

single author · code and scores archived on Zenodo
+

Strengths

  • Immediately actionable: one code change plus one caveat puts a 1.26B model on a $1,000-class GPU — the difference between 52 initial dates and 13.
  • Statistical hygiene rare in systems papers: paired forecasts, CIs, paired t-tests, minimum detectable effect, and a separate practical-significance unit.
  • The fp32-state trap is documented with mechanism per model — including SFNO silently losing the imaginary parts of its complex filters.
  • Energy is measured, not estimated; the Aurora streaming artefact and SFNO’s weak 1.3× are disclosed rather than buried.
  • Reproducible: pinned environments, a reproduction check, archived scores, per-checkpoint licence notes.
−

Limitations

  • One year, one dataset, five variables: 2024 only, verified against ERA5 — the training target of all five models — with no extremes-specific metric.
  • Models chosen by hardware: all must fit 16 GB and run in PyTorch, so GraphCast and AIFS are excluded and extrapolation is unsupported.
  • Ablations are 24 h and 13 dates: they rank components but cannot connect rounding to day-10 skill; one-at-a-time cannot see interactions.
  • Hardware-specific: one RTX 5080, one PyTorch build; on TPUs the bf16 recommendation inverts.
  • Storms are four cases, scored on track and central pressure only — not wind structure, precipitation or extremes.
  • The 8-bit result is about a strawman: no calibration, no outlier handling, no quantisation-aware fine-tuning.
06

Open questions

01

Does the ≤0.5% penalty survive an extreme-season sample — a strongly negative-NAO winter, a full hurricane season — and does it grow for tail metrics that RMSE dilutes?

02

fp16 departs from fp32 by up to 40% of the error at day 10 with no RMSE change. Beyond day 10, does that drift become a systematic bias?

03

Why is FourCastNet the tropical outlier (+12% z500 at day 10)? A flat-FFT versus spherical-transform swap would separate geometry from filter implementation.

04

Would block-scaled formats (MXFP8/MXFP4) close the FastNet gap the paper traces to a single 75× outlier input — and can the sensitive-component result become an automatic policy?
