> **一句话总结：** 作者把"直接读 MLP 神经元"重新定义成一次可复现的测量：用声明式刺激分布生成 56.9 万条 Python 提示（A 组构造在角色内、B 组同 token 在角色外、C 组普通代码），以每个神经元在同类位置上的自身波动 σ₀ 为单位给出"校准概念剖面"，再用双胞胎重抽样给出噪声单位、九项检查给出使用许可；在 OpenCoder-1.5B 的 61 个检查点（4.19B→2.03T token）上，被点名的 5% 载体神经元被消融后比同规模随机集合多抬高损失 0.40 nats（2,034B 处，16 个构造中 14 个高于随机均值，p=0.002），而线性探针在同一批构造上准确率饱和到 0.998–0.999，无法再排序。

> **TL;DR:** This paper turns direct neuron reading into a stated measurement rather than an anecdote. On a 569,270-prompt Python battery run against OpenCoder-1.5B at 61 checkpoints spanning 2.03T training tokens, it reports (i) a per-neuron unit — each response in units of that neuron's own spread on ordinary code, with a twin-redraw noise floor (median twin cosine 0.986), (ii) a nine-check "licence" that must pass before any reading (both nulls pass on 31 constructs, real profile 10.7× the scrambled null's 95th percentile), and (iii) a measured resolution of 1,000 training steps (median 8.56 twin units early, 2.91 on the annealing branch). The causal payoff: zeroing the 5% carrier set costs a median +0.40 nats more than a size-matched random set at 2,034B tokens (14/16 constructs, p = 0.002) versus only +0.06 nats at 4.19B. The baseline it beats is not another interpretability method but a linear probe, which saturates at 0.998–0.999 accuracy from the first checkpoint and therefore cannot order constructs, layers or checkpoints. Worth reading if you care about whether neuron-level claims are measurable at all; the single model, single language and one failed companion-matching check are the main reasons to be cautious.

## 核心贡献

- **把"读神经元"形式化为测量**：提出一次读数必须声明四件事——刺激总体（population）、单位（unit）、使用前的证据（licence）、跨检查点比较时的分辨率（resolution）。现有神经元读数（knowledge neurons、language-specific neurons、universal neurons）四项皆缺。
- **声明式刺激分布取代模板与语料抽样**：把标识符、字面量、作用域/嵌套、伴随构造、长度等每一个因素都按参考语料（253,398 个 Python 文件）中的出现率抽取并记录日志，使总体可以被写下来、也可以事后用算术重新加权到另一个分布。
- **A/B/C 三组设计 + 双胞胎（twins）**：A 组构造在其语法角色内，B 组同一 token 出现在字符串/注释/文档字符串/标识符中（18 个 token 还有"兄弟角色"），C 组是无锚定目标的普通代码。A 减 B 去掉 token 本身的贡献，C 提供每个神经元在"同类位置"上的均值 μ₀ 与波动 σ₀，即"标尺"。
- **给出噪声单位而非显著性水平**：两次独立抽样（共享 core、不同外壳）的剖面差异定义为 twin unit（弦距离 d = √(2(1−cos))），任何两个剖面之间的距离都以它为倍数表述；区间来自 core bootstrap，不假设分布形式。
- **九项检查的"许可证"机制**：两个零假设（打乱标签、半分集）是硬门槛，其余七项（双胞胎一致性、收敛、长度、重加权、校准、伴随构造、标尺地板）只报告其限制范围，明确写出"在什么条件下这个读数不能用"。
- **在 61 个检查点上做真实测量**：OpenCoder-1.5B 的 24×6,144 = 147,456 个 MLP 神经元，35 个预训练 + 24 个退火 + 2 个未训练参考点，跨越 2.03 万亿 token，给出"每个检查点上哪些神经元承载某个构造"。
- **三项自我发现的仪器伪影及其规则**：twin unit 对区间太小（约 150/188 个构造的校正一致性 >1）、标准化范数上升而真实范数下降（144/169 vs 148/169）、最大响应层不稳定（去掉最后一层后 41% 的构造会改变）——每一条都换成了明确规则。
- **完整释放**：battery 全量、每个构造在 61 个参考点上的载体集合、许可证表、代码（dataset `piotrwilam/AtlasOpenCoder` v1，DOI 10.57967/hf/11472；repo `piotrwilam/AtlasCalibratedConceptProfiles`）。

## Key Contributions

- **A measurement framework for direct neuron reading.** The paper states four requirements a reading must satisfy to count as a measurement — declared population, stated unit, pre-use evidence, and cross-checkpoint resolution — and then builds an instrument that satisfies all four. Existing named-neuron work (Dai et al. 2022; Tang et al. 2024; Gurnee et al. 2024) satisfies none of them: thresholds without units, one checkpoint, no removal of the neuron's ordinary behaviour at the position read.
- **Declared stimulus distribution.** Instead of hand-written templates (too rigid: readings conditional on undeclared choices) or corpus samples (too loose: population undescribable, may overlap training data), every factor around the target construct is drawn at its rate in a reference corpus and logged. Two consequences are exploited later: logged draws can be re-weighted arithmetically to another distribution without re-running the model, and the list can be drawn from twice to make twins.
- **The A/B/C decomposition.** A = construct in role; B = same token out of role, with companions matched in distribution; C = ordinary domain input. Mean(A) − mean(B) removes the token; standardising by (μ₀, σ₀) measured on C in the *same kind of position* removes the neuron's ordinary level and scale. This is the paper's key arithmetic move: the unit is per neuron *and* per position class, because a neuron's ordinary behaviour differs between a keyword and an identifier and between the first line and line twenty.
- **Noise unit from twin draws, intervals from core bootstrap.** Because the twins share their cores, their disagreement is surface noise only (names, literals); core-to-core variation is carried by resampling cores with replacement, using the same resampled cores for both twins and every checkpoint compared. The paper is explicit that this is a noise floor "measured on the instrument before any effect is read against it."
- **The licence as a governance mechanism, not a score.** Nine checks on the pair (battery, model); two are gates, seven state restrictions. A new battery or a new model needs a new licence. This is a genuinely different posture from reporting a metric: the output is a sentence saying which claims are permitted.
- **A causal test by removal.** The carrier set (top 5% of standardised entries) is zeroed and loss is read at positions where the construct's grammar fixes the next token, compared against size-matched random sets. This is the one place where the profile is validated against model behaviour rather than against its own resampling.
- **Findings about the model, not just the instrument.** *for* is neither a token nor a role: the loop-*for* and comprehension-*for* share 0.250–0.266 of carriers (chance 0.050, ceiling 0.872), while indented `␣for` and module-level `for` — two unrelated vocabulary entries — share 0.494. Shared neurons cluster by *inventory category* (exception classes purity 1.000, warnings 1.000, builtin functions 0.86, types 0.73, comprehensions 1.000) and not by grammatical position (statement vs expression, compound statements, binding statements all fail to cluster).

## Methodology

**Step 1 — prompt generator.** A declared stimulus distribution for a target is a written list of the factors around it (for code: identifiers and literals, scope and nesting, companion constructs, length and surrounding text) with the rate at which each value is drawn, measured on a reference corpus. A prompt is one draw; the draw is logged. Three sets: **A** holds the target in its role and draws everything else at corpus rate; **B** holds the target's token *outside* its role (string, comment, docstring, part of a name) and, for the 18 constructs whose token has another grammatical role, in that other role — its companions are drawn so their mix matches A's in distribution; **C** is unanchored ordinary input, used only for the yardstick. B does for a neuron reading what a control task does for a probe.

**Step 2 — raw readings.** The read site is one position per prompt: the construct node's first token. A neuron's activation there mixes four sources: the target's role (wanted), the particular prompt, the token, and the neuron's ordinary level/scale at that kind of position. Averaging over A removes the prompt; subtracting the mean over B removes the token; the difference is the raw profile, still in each neuron's own units.

**Step 3 — the profile.** For every neuron and every *cell* (kind of position), μ₀ and σ₀ are the mean and spread of its activation over set C; x becomes z = (x − μ₀)/σ₀. An entry of 2 means "this neuron responds to the role by two of its ordinary spreads more than to the token alone." The unit is per cell because a neuron's ordinary behaviour is positional and token-class specific (citing Voita et al. 2024). A's and B's read sites must fall in the same cells so that μ₀ cancels. Both forms are kept: standardised says *which* neurons and how concentrated, raw says *how big*, because σ₀ itself drifts over training and a standardised size would confound the two.

**Step 4 — the noise unit.** The battery is drawn twice from the same list on the same cores with different surroundings (the twins). Twin distance d = √(2(1−cos)) is the noise floor; a difference between two profiles is stated in multiples of d. Intervals come from resampling cores with replacement.

**Step 5 — the licence.** Nine checks (Table 2). Checks 7a (scrambled-label null) and 7b (split-half null) are gates: if either fails, nothing may be read. The other seven are reported and restrict which comparisons are legal — a mixture-sensitive profile may only be compared over the same distribution; a length-sensitive construct may not be compared across prompt lengths; a construct whose A and B differ in companions carries a companion part.

**Step 6 — readings across training.** Three numbers per construct per checkpoint: **norm** (raw form, size), **sparsity** (share of squared norm in the top 5% of standardised entries), **twin agreement** (cosine between twins). Plus the **carrier set** (top 5% of neurons; two random 5% sets overlap by 0.05) and the **twin ceiling** (overlap of the twins' carrier sets — the most two batteries can agree). The baseline is the same architecture with random weights, read with the same battery. A construct is "concentrated" if sparsity exceeds the untrained value by >3 noise units and twin agreement is ≥0.20 — both margins fixed before any reading. Cross-checkpoint comparisons are made in the **product space** of all 147,456 neurons, in twin units, with a measured **resolution** (step between neighbouring checkpoints).

**The realisation (Python on OpenCoder-1.5B).** Inventory: every Python 3.12 grammar node that appears as text of its own plus every public builtin name — 63 + 143 = 206 constructs, 188 built, 169 passing a read-site gate (P1). Reference corpus: 253,398 parsed Python files (codeparrot-clean), since OpenCoder's training data was not released. Battery: 8 cores per construct (family size 42–1,280, median 210), 720 prompts per set per twin (8 cores × 90 cells), 569,270 prompts, 33.27M tokens, median 55 tokens per prompt. Yardstick: 20 cells = 4 token classes (keyword, identifier, symbol, newline/indent) × 5 bands (fifths of prompt length), giving 2.95M (μ₀, σ₀) pairs per reference. Model: 24 layers × 6,144 SwiGLU MLP hidden units; the neuron's activation is gate-branch-after-SiLU times up-branch, before the down-projection (the quantity Geva et al. 2021 read as key-value memory). References: 61 (35 pretraining from 4.19B to 2.03T tokens, 24 annealing, 2 untrained). The generator never runs the model and never reads the training data — its rule, in source, is "data in the loop is fine; the measured model in the loop is never."

## Results & Highlights

- **The causal test (ablation).** Zeroing a construct's 5% carrier set raises loss at grammar-fixed positions by a median **+0.40 nats** over the mean of 200 size-matched random sets at 2,034B tokens (95% interval [0.10, 0.70]), up from **+0.06 nats** at 4.19B ([0.02, 0.14]) and 0.25 at 83.9B ([0.03, 0.66]); above the random mean for **14 of 16** constructs at the end (sign test p = 0.002), 14 of 16 at 84B (p = 0.002), 12 of 16 at 4.19B (p = 0.038). The largest single effects are Try (**+5.95 nats** more than a random set, on a 0.088-nat baseline) and Lambda (+4.99 on 1.40). Most of the function is acquired early: the 4.19B effect is a fifth of the 83.9B effect.
- **The licence passes, with stated restrictions.** Twin agreement median cosine **0.986** over 188 constructs; convergence to cosine 0.99 with the full 720-prompt profile reached by **56 prompts** (median, grid of 8/24/56/120/176). Both nulls pass on 31 constructs: the real profile's norm at layer 12 is a median **10.7×** the scrambled null's 95th percentile (minimum 7.2×, 0 of 31 at or below) and **12.8×** the split-half null's (minimum 6.0×, 0 of 31). Yardstick floor moves a profile by a median 0.0039 twin units (max 0.035).
- **The probe saturates; the profile does not.** A linear probe at the read site separates A from B at median accuracy **0.998** at the first checkpoint, **0.999** at 84B and at the end, with 145–148 of 164 constructs at ≥0.99 and AUC median **1.000** — against 0.653 (residual stream) and 0.802 (hidden units) on the untrained reference, with shuffled-label controls at 0.50. Accuracies spanning 0.902–1.000 cannot order 169 constructs, 24 layers or 61 checkpoints. The probe's *margin* does move (median 3.2 untrained → 13.8 at 4.19B → 16.5 at 83.9B → 14.8 at the end), but it is one number per construct and names no neurons.
- **Resolution is finer than the checkpoint grid.** At the 1,000-step spacing, the median step between neighbouring references is **8.56 twin units** between the first two checkpoints and **3.56** at the end of the dense block; 4.06 across the late tail, 2.91 on the annealing branch. The minimum exceeds one twin unit everywhere except `None` (0.862 and 0.918 in two pairs), so the instrument's own limit lies *below* the 1,000-step grid. Early training moves the profile 2.4× further per 1,000 steps than late training (8.56 vs 3.56).
- **The unit is load-bearing, and the model is separable from the battery.** Coarser yardsticks name different neurons: their carrier sets overlap the 20-cell sets by a median **0.73–0.87**, below the twin ceiling for 78 to 167 of the 169 constructs. On every trained reference, twin agreement / concept norm / carrier overlap with the previous reference are **0.981–0.989 / 48.7–68.8 / 0.822–0.851**; on the two untrained references, **0.710 / 6.34 / 0.273** and **0.717 / 6.13 / 0.286** — recorded days apart under different seeds. The twin ceiling rises from **0.28 untrained to 0.84** at the first checkpoint (core-bootstrap means 0.21 → 0.74).
- **Re-weighting moves the profile more than redrawing it.** Re-weighting the logged factors to a second declared distribution shifts the profile a median **2.34 twin units** (cosine 0.9095); only **4 of 188** constructs stay within one twin unit. This is the paper's own reason for declaring the distribution: the twin noise floor covers surface variation, not the choice of mixture, by a factor of roughly two.
- **The running example, *for*, over training.** Raw norm **15.7 → 184.5 → 143.0 → 96.0** at untrained / 4.19B / 83.9B / 2,034B, while standardised norm goes **30.2 → 239.6 → 270.9 → 251.5** — opposite signs in the second half, which is why size is read on the raw form. Twin agreement **0.427 → 0.991 → 0.984 → 0.984**: the two twins agree almost perfectly from the first checkpoint on.
- **Token and role are both insufficient descriptions.** Two roles of one token (`for` as loop vs in a comprehension) share **0.250–0.266** of their carrier sets at the last checkpoint (0.198 for AsyncFor) against chance 0.050 and a ceiling of 0.872 — five times chance, under a third of the ceiling. One role across two tokens (indented `␣for` vs module-level `for`) shares **0.494**, which is 0.59 of the lower scope-restricted ceiling (0.837 / 0.850) and ten times chance; across the twelve scope-split constructs the share runs 0.44 (Match) to 0.71 (Try), and every share is lower at the first checkpoint.
- **Shared neurons follow the inventory, not the grammar.** Clustering the carrier overlaps of all 14,196 pairs (2–32 clusters, purity scored against 1,000 label shuffles) recovers grammar-element-vs-builtin, exception classes (purity 1.000, 69 constructs), warning classes (1.000, 12), builtin functions (0.86, 43), builtin types (0.73, 26) and comprehensions (1.000, p = 0.004). It does *not* recover statement-vs-expression, compound statements, binding statements, or composites and their obliged parts. The highest cross-category pairs are Delete–delattr (0.200) and Global–globals (0.094). Under a single pooled cell the replication holds: 38 of 189 purity tests significant, all of them inventory categories.

## Authors & Credibility

Single author: **Piotr Wilam** (University College London, `piotrwilam@gmail.com`). This is a preprint (v1, 6 Nov 2026, cs.CL/cs.LG) with no institutional lab affiliation listed beyond UCL and no co-authors, which matters for a paper whose central claim is methodological reliability — there is no second reader built into the authorship. The paper leans substantially on the author's own prior preprint (Wilam, 2026, "Representational drift in a code model: behaviour settles, which neurons carry it does not") for the turnover result, the running example and the claim that the resolution is usable; that work is not peer-reviewed here either. The citation base is appropriate and current (Elhage 2022, Bricken 2023, Cunningham 2023, Lindsey 2024, Dai 2022, Tang 2024, Gurnee 2024, Voita 2024, Geva 2021, Hewitt & Liang 2019, Marks & Tegmark 2023, plus 2026 work on circuit variance: Méloux 2025, Wu 2026, Anani 2026, Oikarinen 2025, Bayazit 2026, Chen 2026). The engineering effort is unusually large for a single-author preprint — 569,270 generated prompts, 2,022 GB of recorded activations, 61 checkpoints — and the release is concrete: a HuggingFace dataset with a DOI, a public repo, and a `MANIFEST_PAPER1.md` that maps every number in the paper to the file, field and script that rebuilds it. That last artefact is rare and is the strongest single signal of good faith here.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The individual ingredients are borrowed and the paper says so: the control-task logic from Hewitt & Liang, the corruption null from Oikarinen et al., the random-set null from TCAV, the neuron-as-key-value-memory read point from Geva et al., the positional/token-class caveat from Voita et al. What is new is the *assembly* — a declared stimulus distribution, a per-cell yardstick, a twin-redraw noise unit, a pre-use licence, and a measured cross-checkpoint resolution — applied to named neurons rather than to circuits or features. The claim "none of the other methods gives all four" is defensible for the specific situation the paper scopes itself to (which neurons carry a *named* construct, across many checkpoints). The framing "a reading is a distribution over a population someone chose" is the paper's real intellectual contribution and it is correct and under-appreciated.

**Rigor.** Genuinely high in the places that are easy to fake and hard to do. Three things stand out. First, the paper measures its own instrument's failure modes and publishes them: the twin unit is too small for intervals (corrected agreement >1 for ~150 of 188 constructs), the standardised norm rises while the raw norm falls (144/169 vs 148/169), and the layer of largest response moves for 41% of constructs when the last layer is excluded — each converted into a rule (core bootstrap, raw units, product space). Second, the licence is a real gate, not decoration: 19 of 188 constructs fail the read-site gate and are excluded by a tokenizer rule applied uniformly rather than by judgement, and the failures are listed. Third, margins were fixed before reading (sparsity >3 noise units, twin agreement ≥0.20), which is the right order of operations.

**Weak points, specifically.**

1. **The companion check failed outright, and the failure lands on the most interesting constructs.** The design capped the A/B companion-share difference at 0.15; the realised difference is 0.19–0.68 (median 0.37) on *all eight* compound statements. Re-weighting moves the profile by 2.95 twin units (For) and 5.50 (With) — larger than check 4's mixture effect of 2.34. So for exactly the constructs a reader cares most about (`for`, `with`, `if`, `while`, `try`, `match`, `def`, `class`), part of what the "construct profile" measures is a companion profile. The paper is honest — it states the restriction in the licence sentence and does not adopt the re-weighting — but it also does not fix the generator, and four of the eight (ClassDef, FunctionDef, Match, Try) cannot even be re-weighted because no per-prompt activations exist for them. This is the paper's largest unclosed hole.
2. **The noise unit is systematically too small, and it is still the unit used for cross-checkpoint distance.** Check 4 shows re-weighting moves the profile 2.34 twin units — more than twice the redraw noise — and only 4 of 188 constructs stay within one unit. Yet the resolution numbers (8.56, 3.56, 4.06, 2.91 twin units) and all "distance between checkpoints" claims are stated in twin units. The paper patches this with the core bootstrap for intervals, but the *headline* "every number has a noise unit" is weaker than it reads: the unit covers surface variation (names, literals) and not population choice, by a factor of ~2.3. A resolution of "2.91 twin units" should be read as "somewhat more than one population-choice perturbation," which is a much less impressive sentence.
3. **The ablation — the only causal evidence — is weaker than the headline suggests.** It rests on 16 of 169 constructs (those whose prompts contain grammar-fixed next-token positions), compares against the *mean* of random sets rather than in units of their spread (because the spread is long-tailed and does not settle), and 12–14 of 16 clear the random mean. The paper concedes this ("the comparison is with the random sets' mean and by a sign test over constructs, not in units of the random sets' spread") but the concession matters: at 4.19B only 12 of 16 clear, and per-construct only 2 of 16 exceed *every* one of 320 random sets at 4.19B (8 of 16 at 84B and at the end). The Try result (+5.95 nats on a 0.088-nat baseline, a 68× relative effect) is reported without a per-construct breakdown of which positions drive it, and that magnitude on that baseline is exactly the shape of an artefact.
4. **The more principled yardstick names less causally potent neurons.** Appendix C.1 reports that the 20-cell split gives a *lower* ablation effect than one pooled cell at every checkpoint (+0.08 vs +0.25 nats at 4.19B; +0.40 vs +0.73 at 2,034B). The paper's explanation — the 20-cell split excludes neurons that are large only because of where they stand, and "ablation rewards size" — is plausible, but it means the central "the unit is load-bearing" argument (§4.3) and the central causal argument (§4.4) point in opposite directions: the yardstick that is load-bearing for *naming* produces names with *smaller* causal effect. The paper does not confront this tension directly.
5. **No empirical comparison to the methods §5.1 argues against.** The "against the alternative instruments" section is entirely conceptual. There is no SAE trained on OpenCoder-1.5B, no crosscoder across checkpoints, no attribution-patching baseline on the same battery. The paper's defence (SAEs find unnamed concepts; the profile tests named ones; they are complements) is reasonable, but a reader wanting to know whether the profile sees something an SAE misses — or vice versa — gets no number. For a paper whose §5 is titled "where the profile stands," this is the missing experiment.
6. **The probe comparison is a partially stacked deck.** The probe is trained to separate A from B, which is a task that becomes trivially separable once the model is trained, so of course accuracy saturates at 0.998–0.999. But nobody uses a probe to *order 61 checkpoints*; they retrain per checkpoint, and the paper concedes the margin does move (13.8 → 16.5 → 14.8). The saturation argument is fair as a critique of using probe accuracy as a ranking statistic, and unfair as a critique of probes as such. The paper mostly keeps this distinction, but the section heading ("A probe's accuracy saturates; the profile does not") overstates it.
7. **Reproducibility has disclosed cracks.** The untrained reference "does not regenerate across library versions, and the library versions used for its two recordings were not stored; the recorded profiles are kept, and a reproducible seed is owed." Four checks were re-run on 19 Oct 2026 after a bug in joining per-prompt vectors to prompts (files stored in length-sorted order, not jsonl order); length sensitivity came out roughly half the first report (median index 0.7455 vs 1.2142; flagged share 2.4% vs 3.9%), and the earlier values "are not in a released artefact." Table D.1 also notes that `ablation_stable.py` and `ablation_nats.py` "also write TABLE_D1_ablation and give different numbers." Each of these is disclosed rather than hidden, which is to the author's credit, but together they describe a pipeline where the same table can be regenerated to different values depending on which script runs — the opposite of what a paper about measurement should project.
8. **Small but telling:** Table D.1 cites "§3.5 watched numbers," and §3 has only 3.1–3.3. A dangling cross-reference in the provenance table of a paper about provenance is a small thing, but it is the kind of thing a second author catches.
9. **Readability cost.** The vocabulary is idiosyncratic and load-bearing (licence, yardstick, twin, carrier set, product space, cell, core, gate, ceiling, twin unit). Terms are defined, but the paper is 25 dense pages with 8 tables in the appendix, and a reader who wants the one-paragraph version will not find it. The abstract is 250 words of prose that never states a number.

**Significance.** If the framing holds up, this is the kind of paper that changes how a subfield writes its claims — "which neurons carry X" becomes "which neurons carry X, in units of Y, under licence Z, at resolution R." The turnover result it builds on (a list read at one checkpoint describes that checkpoint) is the reason the framing matters: without a unit, cross-checkpoint neuron claims are not comparable, and most of the literature makes them anyway. The empirical findings are also non-trivial and independent of the methodology: *for* is neither token nor role; shared neurons follow the inventory's categories rather than the grammar's; carriers concentrate on high-degree neurons (up to 5× the 5% rate) and sit in all 24 layers. Those are falsifiable claims about a real model that others can check against the released carrier sets.

**Overall.** A serious, unusually honest methodological paper with one large unclosed hole (companion matching), one internal tension it does not resolve (noise unit vs population choice, and 20-cell vs pooled ablation), and a generality claim (one model, one language, one read site) that the paper itself flags as outstanding. The release is exemplary. Read it for the framework and the negative results about probes; do not read it as evidence that the profile is the right instrument for your model.

## Strengths

- **The framing is correct and overdue.** Treating a neuron reading as a distribution over a chosen population, and demanding a unit, a licence and a resolution, is the right diagnosis of why neuron-level interpretability results do not accumulate. The paper does not just assert this — it builds the instrument and runs it 61 times.
- **The licence is a genuinely new artefact.** A pre-registered, nine-check, gate-plus-restrictions protocol that produces a *sentence* about which claims are legal is more useful than another metric. The distinction between the two nulls (gates) and the seven descriptive checks (restrictions) is well judged: it avoids the failure mode where every check becomes a pass/fail and the instrument is either blessed or discarded.
- **Exceptional honesty about failure.** Three self-caught instrument artefacts, a failed companion check reported as a restriction rather than buried, a licence re-run after a join bug with the direction of the change stated ("it had been understated, not overstated"), an owed seed for the untrained reference, and a note that two scripts give different numbers for the same table. Most papers report none of these.
- **The A/B/C + twins + per-cell yardstick design is elegant and portable.** Each piece removes exactly one confound, and the paper is explicit about what a new setting must supply (inventory, parser/tagger, out-of-role definition, reference corpus, cells, read site). Appendix E is a usable recipe.
- **Real empirical findings, not just methodology.** The token-vs-role result (0.25 for two roles of one token, 0.49 for one role across two tokens), the inventory-not-grammar clustering, the high-degree concentration of carriers, and the early-training concentration of function gain (a fifth of the final ablation effect at 4.19B) are all checkable against the released data.
- **The probe saturation result is a useful negative result** independent of the profile: probe accuracy on A-vs-B is 0.998+ from the first checkpoint, so accuracy-based rankings of constructs, layers or checkpoints are uninformative in this regime. That is worth knowing even if one never adopts the profile.
- **Release quality.** Battery in full with hash, carrier sets at all 61 references, licence tables, a worked example of the record (272 MB) and of the profiles, plus a manifest mapping every number to its file and script.

## Limitations

- **One model, one language, one read site.** OpenCoder-1.5B, Python, MLP neurons at the construct's first token, short prompts (median 55 tokens). The paper states that "nothing here is shown on a second model" and names the second-model replication as outstanding in the licence sentence itself. Everything about generality is argued, not measured.
- **The reference corpus is public Python, not the training data.** OpenCoder read 607 programming languages, so a construct's true exposure is larger than its Python rate; the paper concedes the dependence is "not measured." "Ordinary" and "at corpus rate" are therefore defined against a stand-in.
- **Named constructs only.** The method cannot discover concepts. A neuron in no construct's carrier set may carry something the battery does not contain, and there is no estimate of how much is missed.
- **The companion matching failed on all eight compound statements** (0.19–0.68 vs a 0.15 cap), shifting profiles by 2.95–5.50 twin units. For those constructs the profile includes a companion part, and four of the eight cannot be corrected for lack of per-prompt activations.
- **The noise unit understates population uncertainty by ~2.3×** (check 4: median 2.34 twin units under re-weighting), yet cross-checkpoint distances and the resolution are stated in twin units.
- **Length sensitivity is real for statements** (With 55%, For 49%, If 47%, While 36% of neurons flagged), and partly a yardstick artefact: holding the band fixed cuts the flagged share by 41% (median), leaving six constructs still above 5%. Comparisons across prompt lengths are therefore restricted, which is a real constraint for a method whose prompts vary in length by construction.
- **The causal evidence covers 16 of 169 constructs** and compares to the random-set mean by sign test, not in units of the random sets' spread.
- **Compute and storage cost is high and only partly released.** 2,022 GB of accumulators; the full record is not released, only a worked example plus a recorder that can regenerate it. Reproducing 61 references requires the public checkpoints and substantial GPU time (Appendix E: ~28 GB per-core means per reference, 79 GB more where per-prompt arrays exist).
- **The untrained reference is not reproducible** (library versions not stored, seed owed), which weakens the "model vs battery" separation argument — the very comparison that uses the untrained reference as its baseline.
- **No head-to-head with SAEs, crosscoders, or attribution patching**, so the "complements" claim in §5.1 is a taxonomy, not a result.

## Relevance

与"机制可解释性是否可测量"这一核心关切直接相关：它给出了一个把神经元读数变成带单位、带噪声底、带使用许可的测量的完整工程实现，并用 61 个检查点、2 万亿 token 的 OpenCoder-1.5B 做了实证。对关心训练动态、神经元级因果验证（消融）、以及"探针准确率饱和后还能用什么指标"的读者，这篇的方法论框架和负面结果（探针 0.998 饱和、伴随构造检查失败、twin unit 偏小 2.3 倍）比它的正面结论更有价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. **Does the profile survive a second model?** The paper names this as outstanding. Running the same battery on a second code model of the same architecture (or a differently-sized OpenCoder) would test whether the carrier sets, the inventory-not-grammar clustering and the 0.25/0.49 token-vs-role overlaps are properties of the model class or of this checkpoint series.
2. **What would a companion-matched battery do to the eight compound statements?** The generator's cap is 0.15 and the realisation hits 0.19–0.68. Is the failure mechanical (B's f-strings, sibling-role prompts drawn from other constructs) and fixable, or does it reflect a genuine impossibility — that a `for` out of role cannot be surrounded by the same code a `for` in role is surrounded by?
3. **Which unit should cross-checkpoint distances use?** If re-weighting moves the profile 2.34 twin units and the resolution is 2.91–8.56 twin units, the resolution is only ~1.2–3.7 population-choice perturbations. Would a "population unit" (draws from independent cores, or from a second declared distribution) change any of the §4.2 conclusions, or the 2.4× early-vs-late ratio?
4. **Why does the 20-cell yardstick name neurons with *smaller* ablation effect than a pooled cell** (+0.08 vs +0.25 nats at 4.19B)? Is that because the pooled cell's carriers are confounded with position (and therefore with the position-fixed loss readout), or because the 20-cell split genuinely selects subtler, more construct-specific units? A per-construct scatter of the two carrier sets' ablation effects would settle it.
5. **Can the profile be turned into a discovery instrument?** The paper positions SAEs as the discovery half and the profile as the test half. A natural experiment: take an SAE feature on OpenCoder-1.5B with no obvious name, find its top activating prompts, define a construct from them, and see whether the profile's carrier set and the SAE feature's neurons agree beyond the twin ceiling.
6. **What does the 41% layer instability imply for circuit work?** If excluding the last layer moves the argmax layer for 41% of constructs, then layer-indexed claims in the circuit literature (and in neuron lists that report "layer 12") may be reporting an artefact of layer inclusion. Is there a principled layer statistic — a depth-weighted centroid, as the paper's "depth" number suggests — that is stable?
7. **Does the early-training concentration of function generalise?** The ablation effect at 4.19B is a fifth of that at 83.9B, i.e. carriers acquire most of their function in the first 20× of tokens. Is this specific to code constructs with crisp grammar-fixed positions, or does it hold for natural-language constructs where the "construct's position" is less well defined?
