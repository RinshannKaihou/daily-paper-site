> **一句话总结：** 把"线性探针能解码 ⇒ 模型在用这个结构"这一跳跃拆成可解码性、形状、归属、因果使用四个独立命题，用四阶段贝叶斯流水线在 GPT-J 6B / Llama 3.1 8B / Pythia 6.9B 的两位数加法（10,000 题）与乘法（3,023 题）上逐 cell 裁决（共 4,860 个 cell）：1,620 个候选 cell 中 89% 可解码但概念子空间高度叠加（叠加率 0.80/0.90），338 个强支持形状比较无一例外选中 ribbon（1,208 cell）或 torus（230 cell），1,330 个归属裁决中仅 19% 的形状真正"属于"被探测概念（核心概念只有 5%，答案数字与进位 217 个 cell 全部为 0），而 ≤5 维的流形基仍保留完整探针子空间 61% 的因果效应（准确率 −7.7 vs −12.6 点）——严格标准下只有 46/1,330（3.5%）个 cell 同时通过四项。

> **TL;DR:** Reframes linear probing as four separately testable claims — decodability, shape, ownership, causal use — and implements them as a four-stage Bayesian pipeline: a permutation-tested SVD probe, marginal-likelihood comparison of seven candidate manifolds against a cubic baseline, projection-based removal of pre-registered "related concepts" followed by refitting, and ablation/patching of the fitted manifold basis versus rank-matched random subspaces. Applied to 2-digit addition (10,000 problems, all single-token) and multiplication (3,023 single-token products) in GPT-J 6B, Llama 3.1 8B and Pythia 6.9B, across 5 layers × 108 concepts × 3 preprocessing settings = 4,860 cells. Headline numbers: 89% of 1,620 cells are decodable yet 80–90% of concept pairs are superposed; all 338 strongly supported shape comparisons pick a ribbon (1,208 cells) or torus (230) over the prior-work circle (4 cells) and open helix (2 cells); only 19% of 1,330 ownership verdicts are "owned" (5% among core concepts, 0/217 for answer digits and carries); ablating the ≤5-dim manifold basis costs 7.7 accuracy points versus 12.6 for the full probe (retention 0.61, per-pair range 0.32–0.70); and just 46/1,330 cells (3.5%) clear all four bars. Read it for the ownership test and the four-verdict reporting standard, which generalize well beyond arithmetic; skip it if you need evidence beyond base 6–9B models, one fixed prompt, and a single token position, or if you want the mechanism (heads, neurons, SAEs, Fourier components) rather than the geometry.

## 核心贡献

- **把"探针精度"拆成四条可分别裁决的命题**：decodability（概念能否从激活中解码）、shape（激活是否构成某个具名形状）、ownership（这个形状属于被探测概念，还是属于模型同样表示的某个相关概念）、causal use（输出是否依赖这个形状）。作者的主张是：探针结果应当报告为四条裁决，而不是一个准确率数字。
- **提出 Stage 3「归属检验」——本文最新颖的一步**：在实验开始前为每个概念预先写好 related concepts 清单（108 个概念中 46 个有清单），把它们的 Stage 1 子空间整体投影掉，再重新拟合形状，用证据差的变化 ∆ = g − g⊥ 的符号判定 owned（∆ < 0，证据差上升）还是 inherited（∆ ≥ 0）。此前最接近的工作（Kantamneni & Tegmark 2025 用 activation patching 确认 helix；Feucht et al. 2026 检验模型是否沿圆计算）都没有问过"这个形状是不是从别的概念继承来的"。
- **用边缘似然在同一标度上比较不同复杂度的形状**：候选集含三次多项式基线、直线、圆、开螺旋、环面、同心圆、ribbon（半径随值线性变化的螺旋）共 7 个；权重 W 解析积掉，边际似然自动惩罚未使用的自由度，因此不需要额外的复杂度惩罚项，也能在"没有候选被支持"时明确弃权（Kass & Raftery 的贝叶斯因子框架）。配套给出 Tier A/B/C/D 分级、5 nats（≈150 倍贝叶斯因子）预设门槛、以及 Tier A 的留出误差与稳定性双条件。
- **规模是同类工作中最大的**：3 个模型家族 × 2 个任务 × 5 层 × 108 个概念 × 3 种预处理 = 4,860 个 cell，且每个 cell 的四条裁决都基于同一批数据（同一 cell 内可直接比较），而不是在不同实验间拼凑结论。
- **实质性的实证结论**：ribbon/torus 系统性取代了此前的 circle/helix 叙事；81% 的形状是继承而非自有；答案数字与进位这两个"最像算法中间量"的概念，其形状从未被判定为自有；流形基只用 ≤5 维就保留了 61% 的因果效应（探针子空间中位秩为 9）。
- **一个诚实的负结果**：owned 与 inherited 形状的因果活跃率几乎相同（41% vs 42%），说明"归属"不能替代"因果"——四裁决框架的价值是诊断性的记账，而不是一个能预测因果性的分数。

## Key Contributions

- **A four-claim decomposition of what a probe result actually asserts**, with one stage per claim and one verdict per cell, so that decodability, shape, ownership and causal use can disagree and be compared on the same data.
- **The ownership test (Stage 3)**: pre-register the concepts that the written algorithm ties to the target, orthonormalise the union of their Stage 1 bases into `R`, project `R` out of every activation, refit all shapes with hyperparameters frozen, and decide ownership from the sign of `Δ = g − g⊥`. This is the paper's genuine methodological novelty; prior work established that a helix or circle exists and that patching it matters, but never asked whether the shape is inherited from a co-represented concept.
- **Bayesian model comparison over a named shape family** with the weight matrix integrated out in closed form (`log Z_k` from an `m_k × m_k` matrix via Woodbury, so the `N × N` covariance is never formed and each fit takes seconds on CPU). Because the marginal likelihood scores complexity and fit on one scale, "no candidate is supported" is a representable outcome rather than a forced choice.
- **A graded evidence standard** (Tier A exact shape / Tier A family / Tier B / Tier C dimension-only / Tier D declined) with a pre-specified 5-nat bar, a held-out-error condition (≤1.10× the best alternative), a stability condition over 3 seeds × 3 prior variances, and a 10,000-permutation multiple-testing guard at FDR 0.10.
- **A 4,860-cell empirical map** of arithmetic geometry across GPT-J 6B, Llama 3.1 8B and Pythia 6.9B (two block layouts, three tokenizers), five matched relative depths, and three preprocessing settings (`off`, `answer`, `norm`) designed to separate geometry from readout alignment and activation scale.
- **Concrete empirical findings that revise the prior picture**: ribbons and tori, not circles and open helices, win every strongly supported comparison; most shapes are inherited; the manifold basis is a low-rank handle that carries most but not all of the probe's causal effect; and a strict four-claim standard certifies only 3.5% of cells.

## Methodology

**Setup.** Three base (non-instruction-tuned) models with hidden size `d = 4096`: GPT-J 6B, Llama 3.1 8B, Pythia 6.9B. Residual stream read after the transformer block at the final `=` token of the prompt `Output ONLY a number. {a}+{b}=` (or `*`), at five layers of matched relative depth (4, 8, 14, 20, 24 of 28 in GPT-J; 4, 8, 16, 24, 28 of 32 in Llama and Pythia). All 10,000 two-digit addition problems are usable (answers ≤ 198 are single tokens in all three tokenizers); multiplication is restricted to the 3,023 pairs whose product is a single token in all three models. Only correctly answered problems enter a cell, with a floor of 30 problems per concept value and at least two surviving values.

**Concepts.** 108 concepts: 51 for addition, 57 for multiplication. Six "core" concepts per task are exactly the quantities of the standard written algorithm (for multiplication: units/tens digit of `a`, units digit of `b`, units partial product, units carry, units digit of the answer). The rest are "extended" single concepts (parity, magnitude tier, digit count, comparisons) plus 10 "joint" concepts per task formed from two or three singles.

**Cells.** A cell is `κ = (M, t, ℓ, c, u)` — model, task, layer, concept, preprocessing setting. Three settings: `off` (raw), `answer` (remove the unembedding direction of the answer token, to strip readout alignment), `norm` (rescale each activation to unit length, to strip activation scale). 3 × 5 × 108 × 3 = 4,860 cells, 1,620 candidate cells per setting; the floor removes 155 and the permutation test 20, leaving 1,445 decodable cells.

**Stage 1 — decodability.** Weighted, centred class centroids `C_v = sqrt(N_v/N)(μ_v − μ̄)` are stacked and SVD'd. Directions are kept whose squared singular values exceed the 99th percentile of 1,000 permutations of `y`; the cell is decodable when rank `r ≥ 1`. An LDA inside the kept basis runs as a diagnostic (second permutation test plus held-out nearest-neighbour accuracy) but removes no cell. Three qualifying checks: a Marchenko–Pastur comparison of the largest residual eigenvalue outside the probe subspace against the noise edge; a principal-angle superposition test (a pair is "superposed" when its smallest principal angle is >10° below the 5th percentile of 1,000 rank-matched random subspace pairs, FDR 0.05); and a Spearman correlation between pairwise distances before and after projection, to confirm the projection preserves distance order.

**Stage 2 — shape.** Coordinates `z_n = Bᵀ(x_n − μ̄)` are modelled as a linear image of a shape plus per-value Gaussian noise: `z_n = Wᵀφ_k(y_n) + ε_n`, `ε_n ~ N(0, σ²_{y_n} I)`, with a Gaussian prior on `W`. Period `T` is proposed by a periodogram of the centroid sequence (Step 2a), per-value spread `σ_v` from shrinkage-estimated covariances (Step 2b), and `W` integrated out in closed form to give the marginal likelihood `Z_k` (Step 2c). The evidence gap `g = log Z_k̂ − log Z_0` is measured against a cubic-polynomial baseline `K_0`; selection requires `g ≥ 5` nats (10 when ≤6 values remain) plus alignment `ρ_max ≥ 0.5`. Tier A additionally requires held-out error within 10% of the best alternative and stability of the argmax over all nine combinations of three seeds (42/43/44) and three prior variances (0.1/1/10). The two ribbon cross-features carry a prior variance scaled by 0.1.

**Stage 3 — ownership.** For a concept `c`, the Stage 1 bases of its pre-listed related concepts (same model, layer, setting) are orthonormalised into `R`, and the span is projected out: `z_n⊥ = Bᵀ(I − RRᵀ)(x_n − μ̄)`. The selected shape is refitted with `α`, periods and noise frozen, giving `g⊥`. Owned iff `Δ = g − g⊥ < 0`. `Δ` decomposes into the shape's advantage over the baseline on the removed component plus an interaction term that vanishes when `span(R) ⊆ span(B)`. The rule is strict at the boundary (`Δ = 0` counts as inherited), so a representation that is literally the sum of its related concepts is classified as inherited.

**Stage 4 — causal use.** Two subspaces are intervened on: the full probe basis `B`, and the manifold basis `Q_geom` — an orthonormal basis of the column space of `B Ŵᵀ`, i.e. only the directions inside `B` that the fitted shape actually occupies (rank ≤ 5 for the ribbon, 4 for the torus). Ablation replaces the in-subspace component by its mean, `h ← h − PPᵀ(h − h̄)`; patching replaces it with a donor problem's, `h ← h + PPᵀ(h̃ − h)`. The "causal excess" is the target-token logit change minus the mean change under five random rank-matched subspaces. A cell is causally active at |excess| ≥ 0.5 logits (with 1.0 reported as a stricter level). **Retention** is the mean accuracy drop under ablating `Q_geom` divided by that under ablating `B`, over the same cells.

**All four.** A cell satisfies everything when it is decodable, its shape reaches Tier A and is owned, and it is causally active. Stages 3 and 4 only issue verdicts for cells whose shape reaches Tier A or B, which is why 1,457 shape fits shrink to 1,330 ownership verdicts.

## Results & Highlights

- **Decodability is near-universal, and so is sharing.** At the `off` setting, 1,445 of 1,620 candidate cells (89%) are decodable, with a median probe rank of 9 (mean 18.3, max 199). But the superposition rate is 0.80 for addition and 0.90 for multiplication (Table 1), i.e. 78–91% of concept pairs align more closely than rank-matched random subspaces — so a high probe accuracy is close to uninformative about concept-specific structure. On core concepts the bar is even lower: 179 of 180 cells are decodable, the single failure being Pythia addition.
- **Shapes are ribbons and tori, not circles and open helices.** Of 1,457 cells with a shape fit (pooled over settings), the ribbon has the highest evidence in 1,208 and the torus in 230; the remaining 19 go to the cubic baseline (13), the circle (4) and the open helix (2). All 338 strongly supported comparisons — 111 of them among core concepts — select a ribbon or a torus. On the 33 core-concept/model pairs without preprocessing the split is ribbon 24, torus 8, baseline 1 (the baseline wins for operand `b` in Pythia addition), versus the circle's 4 cells overall.
- **Most shapes are inherited.** Of 1,330 cells with an ownership verdict, 253 (19%) are owned and 1,077 (81%) inherited, with the owned share between 14% and 22% in every model/task pair and 18%/20%/19% across the `off`/`answer`/`norm` settings. Among core concepts the owned share collapses to 16/337 (5%). The verdicts are far from the decision boundary: the smallest |Δ| is 1.51 nats and the median about 3,024 nats, so raising the margin to 5 nats would flip only 4 of 1,330 verdicts (none among core concepts, whose smallest margin is 10.58 nats).
- **Ownership tracks the direction of the arithmetic, not the salience of the concept.** Answer digits own 0 of 129 cells and carries own 0 of 88; operands own 25 of 124, functions of the operands 108 of 252, running sums 49 of 180, column sums 30 of 151, partial products 5 of 98. The running example behaves exactly as the algorithm predicts: the units digit of the answer is inherited in all 30 of its cells with a verdict, because it is a function of the units partial product alone.
- **The manifold basis retains 61% of the probe's causal effect with at most five dimensions.** Averaged over the 1,330 cells, ablating `B` lowers accuracy by 12.6 points and ablating `Q_geom` by 7.7 points — retention 0.61 (95% bootstrap interval 0.54–0.67), against a median probe rank of 9. Per model/task pair the range is wide: 0.70 for GPT-J addition (−21.5 → −15.0 points), 0.68 GPT-J multiplication, 0.69 Pythia multiplication, 0.55 Llama multiplication, 0.43 Pythia addition, and only 0.32 for Llama addition (−6.3 → −2.0).
- **The causal excess has the predicted sign and survives a rank-matched control.** On core concepts the mean excess under ablation is −0.85 logits at `B` (SD 1.03) and −0.55 at `Q_geom` (SD 0.59); under patching it is +1.70 (SD 1.77) and +1.02 (SD 0.99), with `Q_geom` keeping 60% of `B`'s effect. 154 of 337 core cells (46%) are causally active at 0.5 logits and 66 (20%) at 1.0, all with a negative excess.
- **A complete worked example of an owned, causally used shape.** The tens column sum of addition (`a_tens + b_tens`) in GPT-J at layer 20 without preprocessing has 19 values over 8,415 problems, fits a ribbon with an evidence gap of 29,580 nats over the baseline (alignment 0.65); removing the two tens digits takes 0.70 of the coordinate variance and raises the gap to 55,758 nats (Δ = −26,178, owned); ablating its manifold basis costs 35.5 accuracy points versus 56.6 for the full probe (retention 0.63), with causal excess −2.16 versus −3.04 logits and patching +3.47 versus +4.53.
- **Only 46 of 1,330 cells (3.5%) pass all four criteria** — 38 with a strongly supported exact shape and 8 with a family-level Tier A verdict, 37 ribbons and 9 tori, spread over 26 distinct model/task/layer/concept combinations (15 of which pass in at least two settings, 5 in all three). None involves an answer digit or a carry. The paper reads the 3.5% as conservative because every stage must clear its own threshold.
- **Ownership does not predict causal use.** Causal activity is 41% among owned cells (103/253) and 42% among inherited cells, so an ownership verdict cannot stand in for a causal one. Within extended concepts the parity holds at layers 14–20 (69% vs 66%) and reverses at layers 24/28 (41% vs 57%).
- **Effects peak in the middle layers, not at readout.** Ablating `B` has its largest effect at layer 14 of 28 or 16 of 32 in five of six model/task pairs (e.g. GPT-J addition layer 14: −53.0 points for `B`, −39.0 for `Q_geom`; Pythia addition: −47.3/−16.0), and at layer 4 every change is within 2 points of zero. A pure readout direction would matter most at the deepest layers, so the depth profile argues against that explanation — though Llama addition at layer 28 shows −15.3/−0.2, i.e. a retention of roughly 0.01.

## Authors & Credibility

Anshul Kumar (CMU, corresponding), Neha Prasad (IIT Jodhpur), Manoj Kumar (IIT Roorkee), Barnabás Póczos (CMU), Deeksha Varshney (IIT Jodhpur). Póczos is a well-established statistical ML researcher (kernel methods, density estimation, Bayesian nonparametrics, causal discovery), which fits the paper's heavy investment in marginal likelihoods, shrinkage covariance estimation and Bayes factors; the GPLVM lineage the authors claim for their shape model is his home turf. The mech-interp side is less credentialed — no prior interpretability papers from this group that I can identify — but the citation base is current and unusually well chosen for 2026 (Kantamneni & Tegmark 2025; Feucht et al. 2026; Wen et al. 2026; Wurgaft et al. 2026; Sarfati et al. 2026; Modell 2026), and the paper engages the strongest counter-arguments to its own framing (Hewitt & Liang 2019 on control tasks, Makelov et al. 2024 on the subspace-patching illusion, Nikankin et al. 2025 on the competing "bag of heuristics" account). Preprint under review, no venue. Reproducibility is promised but not delivered at review time: the problems, the 108 concept definitions, the code for every stage, and the per-cell verdicts "will be released with the final version of this paper". A generative-AI writing aid is disclosed. Appendix coverage is genuinely thorough — every threshold in one table (Table 15), every population traced (Table 13), every concept and related-concept list enumerated (Tables 8–12), and the decomposition of `Δ` derived in closed form.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The four-claim decomposition is a real conceptual contribution and I expect it to be cited for exactly that. The ownership test is the sharpest piece: refitting the *shape* (not a classifier) after projecting out related concepts, and reading the sign of the evidence change, is a clean operationalisation that avoids the usual amnesic-probing move of measuring classifier accuracy after removal. The Bayesian shape comparison is competently executed but less novel than presented — it is the linear-Gaussian limit of a GPLVM, which the authors say themselves, and the "compare named manifolds by marginal likelihood" idea has close relatives in the probabilistic-geometry literature they cite (Hauberg 2018).

**The nested-model problem is the biggest technical worry.** The ribbon *strictly contains* the open helix (set the two cross-feature weights to zero), which strictly contains the circle (also set the linear weight to zero). With a marginal likelihood that already rewards fit and only weakly penalises unused flexibility, the richer model wins whenever there is any curvature at all — and the authors then choose `α` by maximising the evidence *of the best shape* over a 13-point grid, which compounds the preference for complexity. The only counterweight is a hand-set 0.1 prior-variance scale on the two ribbon cross-features, and that knob directly determines how often ribbon beats helix. No sweep of that scale is reported; the robustness section says only the floor, `α`, the causal level and the Tier-A FDR were re-examined at other values. So "every strongly supported comparison selects a ribbon or a torus" is partly a statement about the prior, not only about the model's representations. The nested structure also explains the absurd evidence gaps: 29,580 nats in the worked example, median 10,866 nats for strongly supported comparisons against a 5-nat bar. The authors are honest that the 5-nat bar is "a necessary condition and not the deciding one" and that gaps depend on `N` and `r`, but marginal likelihoods of that magnitude from a misspecified linear-Gaussian model with per-value scalar noise are not calibrated evidence about the world; the Bayes-factor framing is doing rhetorical work it cannot support.

**Ownership is defined on less than half the concept space, and the denominator is the interesting part.** Only 46 of 108 concepts have a related-concept list (20 in addition, 26 in multiplication); the other 62 — every parity, magnitude-tier and joint concept — never receive an ownership verdict at all. So the 19% figure is computed over a hand-selected subset, and it is relative to lists the authors wrote: a missing related concept produces a false *owned*, and a removal that eats the concept's own directions produces a false *inherited* (both limits are stated in Appendix G). The 5% owned rate among core concepts versus 19% overall is a large gap that deserves more analysis than it gets; it implies ownership lives mostly in the extended concepts, which are the ones whose related-concept lists are least constrained by the written algorithm.

**The 61% retention headline hides a collapse.** Llama addition at layer 28 goes from −15.3 points under `B` ablation to −0.2 under `Q_geom` — a retention of roughly 0.01, meaning the fitted manifold basis carries essentially none of the causal effect at the deepest layer. Llama addition is also the row with the lowest aggregate retention (0.32) and the weakest absolute ablation effects (B between −4.0 and −6.3 points, versus −11 to −21 for GPT-J and Pythia). The paper reports the range 0.32–0.70 and notes Llama's lower retention, but never diagnoses it. The obvious candidate — Llama's residual norms are 4.8–20.1× smaller than GPT-J's and Pythia's and grow 8.8× across the five layers versus 3.1× and 3.7× — is described as a fact in Appendix C and never connected to the ablation asymmetry, even though a fixed-rank intervention on a much smaller-norm residual stream should mechanically have a smaller absolute effect. That is a scale confound sitting directly under the paper's second-strongest quantitative claim.

**Scope is narrower than the framing.** One prompt template (`Output ONLY a number. {a}+{b}=`), one token position (the final `=`), base models only, 6–9B parameters, single-token answers. Every verdict is conditional on that prompt, and there is no prompt-format robustness check at all — a real gap given that Mamidanna et al. (2025) and Feucht et al. (2026) both argue the last-token state is a specific *assembly* point whose contents depend on what was moved there. The three preprocessing settings vary the activation treatment but never the input.

**Two data quirks that shrink the claim.** First, multiplication is restricted to the 3,023 pairs whose product is a single token in *all three* tokenizers; since GPT-J tops out at 520 and Pythia at 530, the effective product range is ≤520, which excludes most large×large products. "Two-digit multiplication" therefore does not cover the 0–99 × 0–99 square, and the ribbon's linear trend (which is motivated by the two-digit range) is fit on a truncated range. Second, every analysis is conditioned on problems the model answers correctly — 84.15% (GPT-J), 99.63% (Llama), 77.18% (Pythia) for addition. The geometry of *failures*, which is where a "does the model compute with the shape" question is sharpest, is excluded by construction. The authors state both choices explicitly, which is to their credit, but neither is stress-tested.

**Weak baselines and missing ablations.** The `K_0` baseline is a cubic polynomial — it cannot represent periodicity at all, so any periodic candidate beats it by construction, and the 13 baseline wins (all in operand-like concepts) are the only informative ones. There is no comparison against SAE features, no test of the Fourier-feature account (Zhou et al. 2024) or the neuron-heuristic account (Nikankin et al. 2025), both of which are named in Section 2 as "candidate substrates for the same geometry" and then never touched. A shape can be real, owned, causally active, and still not be the mechanism the model uses — the paper concedes this ("necessity would need interventions that remove every substrate of the concept at once") but the concession does real damage to the framing, because the four-claim standard is presented as the fix for exactly that overclaiming problem.

**The Tier A "held-out" condition is not held out.** The probe basis `B`, the candidate periods, the noise variances `σ_v` and `α` are all estimated on all `N` problems; only the weights `W` are cross-validated. The authors admit this in Appendix G in one sentence. It means Tier A's "held-out error within 10% of the best alternative" tests a much weaker proposition than a reader would assume from the phrase.

**On the positive side**, several things are done better than the norm. The `answer` preprocessing setting — removing the unembedding direction of the answer token before probing — is a control that most probing papers skip, and the fact that the owned share is stable at 18%/20%/19% across all three settings is meaningful evidence that the result is not a readout artifact. The random-subspace controls are rank-matched and the paper explicitly cites Makelov et al.'s subspace illusion as the limit of what such a control can show, rather than pretending the control is decisive. The refusal to attach significance tests to non-independent cells (one concept appears at five layers and three settings) is the right call even though it weakens the presentation. And the honest reporting of the owned/inherited causal parity — a result that undercuts the practical value of the paper's own Stage 3 — is the kind of thing most authors would bury.

## Strengths

- **The decomposition is clarifying and portable.** "Decodable / shaped / owned / causally used" is a checklist any probing paper could adopt tomorrow, and it correctly identifies that most published claims settle only the first item.
- **The ownership test is a genuine methodological advance.** Pre-registering related concepts, projecting their span out, refitting the shape with hyperparameters frozen, and reading the sign of `Δ` is more principled than amnesic probing (which measures a classifier, not a shape) and more targeted than activation patching (which never asks who the shape belongs to).
- **Scale and coverage.** 4,860 cells, three model families with two block layouts and three tokenizers, five matched relative depths, three preprocessing settings, 108 concepts including 20 joint ones. Nothing else in this literature is this broad.
- **The controls are chosen with the right enemies in mind.** The `answer` setting targets readout alignment, the `norm` setting targets activation scale, rank-matched random subspaces target the "any perturbation of this rank would do" objection, and the Tier A permutation guard targets multiple testing over 4,860 cells.
- **The worked example is fully auditable.** For the tens column sum in GPT-J layer 20 the paper gives the value count, the evidence gap, the alignment, the variance removed, the post-removal gap, the Δ, the ablation drops for both subspaces, both causal excesses and both patching excesses. You can check the arithmetic of the argument yourself.
- **Computational economy.** Integrating out `W` in closed form and using Woodbury means the `N × N` covariance is never formed and each shape fit takes seconds on CPU — which is what makes 4,860 cells × 7 shapes × 3 settings tractable at all.
- **Unusual honesty about negative and ambiguous results**: owned ≈ inherited in causal activity, cells are not independent so no significance tests, the 5-nat bar is not the deciding one, Llama's retention is far below the pooled 0.61, and code is not yet released.

## Limitations

- **One prompt, one token position, base models, single-token answers, 6–9B parameters.** No prompt-format robustness, no instruction-tuned models, no multi-token or chain-of-thought arithmetic. The stated next task ("arithmetic beyond single-token answers") is left entirely open.
- **Ownership is defined for only 46 of 108 concepts**, so 62 concepts — including every joint concept — can never be judged owned or inherited, and the 19% headline is relative to hand-written lists whose completeness the authors cannot verify.
- **The shape family is nested and the prior is tuned toward the richer member.** Ribbon ⊃ open helix ⊃ circle, `α` is chosen by maximising the best shape's evidence, and the 0.1 cross-feature prior scale — the single knob controlling ribbon-versus-helix — is never swept.
- **No held-out validation of the pipeline as a whole.** Basis, periods, noise variances and `α` are fit on all problems; only `W` is cross-validated, so Tier A's held-out condition is weaker than its name suggests.
- **No mechanism.** The pipeline says nothing about which heads, MLP neurons, Fourier components or SAE features realise a shape. The competing Fourier-feature and bag-of-heuristics accounts are cited and then not tested.
- **Causal measures are restricted to the answer token's logit and accuracy**, so a shape that serves other outputs (intermediate columns, carries as such) is invisible to Stage 4 — a real problem for a paper about intermediate quantities.
- **No necessity test.** Ablation shows sufficiency-of-disruption, not necessity; the paper says so, but the four-claim standard is marketed as closing the decodability-versus-use gap and does not fully close it.
- **Selection on correct answers** (77–99.6% depending on model and task) excludes the failure cases where the geometry question is most interesting.
- **Multiplication is truncated** to products ≤ ~520 by the single-token constraint across all three tokenizers, so the "two-digit" range is not the full operand square.
- **No released artifacts at review time** — code, data, concept definitions and per-cell verdicts are all deferred to the final version, which makes independent replication impossible now.
- **No inferential statistics.** Counts and shares only, with the non-independence of cells acknowledged but not modelled; a hierarchical or mixed-effects treatment would have been feasible and would have made the model-to-model comparisons (e.g. Llama's 0.42 retention) far more convincing.

## Relevance

属于 "LLM Hidden States & Interpretability" 中的表示几何与探针方向，而且切的是这个方向最薄弱的一环：探针能解码 ≠ 模型在用。它的 Stage 3 归属检验和"四裁决"报告规范可以直接搬到任何"我们探测到了某个结构"的结论上，对判断可解释性论文的可信度比再发现一个几何形状更有用。如果你关心的是结论的可靠性而不是新能力，这篇的方法论部分（第 4 节 + 附录 G）值得精读；结果部分则要记住它的适用范围只有 base 6–9B 模型、单一 prompt、单一 token 位置的单位数算术。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. How much of "every strongly supported comparison picks a ribbon" survives if the 0.1 prior-variance scale on the ribbon's cross features is swept over, say, 0.01 to 1? Since ribbon ⊃ open helix ⊃ circle by construction, this single knob may be doing most of the work.
2. Does the ownership verdict flip for the 62 concepts that currently have no related-concept list — especially the joint concepts — if lists are constructed for them? Is the 19% owned rate stable under list expansion, or does it collapse toward the 5% seen on core concepts?
3. Why does Llama's retention fall to ~0.01 at layer 28 in addition while GPT-J and Pythia stay at 0.4–0.7? Is it the residual-norm scale (Llama's norms are 4.8–20.1× smaller and grow 8.8× versus 3.1×/3.7×), the sequential versus parallel block layout, or the tokenizer?
4. Do the 46 all-four cells survive a change of prompt template (`What is 23*17?`, no instruction prefix, few-shot), or is the final `=` state too specific an assembly point to generalise?
5. Can Stage 4 be upgraded to a necessity test — ablating every subspace that carries the concept simultaneously rather than one basis at a time — and does the manifold basis still retain its share of the effect?
6. Is the ribbon's advantage for full-range concepts (operands, sum) partly the linear feature absorbing the readout component that the `answer` preprocessing setting is designed to remove? The paper floats this as "a candidate explanation that a separate experiment would test" and then does not run that experiment.
7. Does the four-claim standard reproduce the same verdict pattern on a non-arithmetic concept with a well-defined related-concept structure (e.g. days of the week, character counts, or a syntactic feature), or is the 3.5% pass rate specific to arithmetic's unusually tight functional dependencies?
