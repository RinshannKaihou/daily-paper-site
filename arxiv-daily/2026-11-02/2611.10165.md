> **一句话总结：** 用"下一 token 分布的熵 + 每层残差更新的相对幅度"两个运行时信号，在 prefill 与每个 decode step 上逐层挑选 2/4/6-bit 精度，并用 AutoFP 格式（NVFP4 基底 + 2-bit 有符号偏移做尾数精化、指数重映射做 2-bit 五值格式）从单一 4-bit 副本按需派生高/低精度，从而避免存多份权重；在 Qwen3-8B 的 τ²-bench 上把 NVFP4 的 17.5% 拉到 27.2%（+9.7 点），在 Gemma-4-26B 的 τ²-bench 上从 46.5% 拉到 60.5%（+14.0 点），平均存储 4.9 bit/权重即可追平 6.5-bit 的 FP6g，GB300 上保留 NVFP4 解码吞吐的 90–98%。

> **TL;DR:** AutoEMatic is a full-stack runtime mixed-precision system (policy + FP format + vLLM kernels + accelerator) that decides per-layer W/A/KV precision at every prefill and decode step from two model-internal signals — normalized Shannon entropy of the top-10 logits and the relative norm of each layer's residual update — instead of a static offline assignment. On Qwen3-8B/τ²-bench it reaches 27.2% vs 17.5% for uniform NVFP4 (+9.7 pts, still 3.5 pts behind FP6g's 30.7%), and on Gemma-4-26B/τ²-bench 60.5% vs 46.5% for NVFP4 (+14.0 pts, +1.9 over AutoQuantize, exactly tying FP6g) at 4.9 bits/weight versus 6.5 for FP6g. The engineering payoff is real: on GB300 it keeps 90–98% of NVFP4 decode throughput (DS-Llama-70B batch 1: 120 vs 131 tok/s) and cuts TTFT 1.4× versus FP6g; the custom accelerator claims 1.6–2.3× lower latency and 7–23% lower energy than an NVFP4-only datapath at iso-accuracy. Read it for the format trick (turning mantissa refinement into a single extended-K Tensor Core GEMM) and the precision-demand measurement; discount the headline "21.7% over NVFP4" and the DP-LLM† comparison, which is a self-built strawman.

## 核心贡献

- **运行时精度自适应策略（format-agnostic）**：不再用离线校准的固定分配，而是从两个模型内部信号实时推断"这一层此刻需不需要高精度"——(a) 全局的生成不确定性 `GE`（top-K=10 logits 的归一化 Shannon 熵），(b) 每层的局部残差更新幅度 `LA = ‖H_ℓ − H_{ℓ−1}‖₂ / ‖H_{ℓ−1}‖₂`。两者线性组合成 `M = λ·LA + (1−λ)·GE`，prefill 取 λ=1（无 token 熵可用），decode 取 λ=0.7。
- **规则化（而非阈值化）的决策机制**：维护 `M` 的滑动均值 µ 与标准差 σ，只在"连续 n_p 层相对 µ 持续上升/下降"时才升降一级精度；prefill 阶段用 `M > µ + 3σ` 在线检测那个"模型刚消化完 prompt"的尖峰层并强制 6-bit，尖峰之后按趋势逐层调整。高熵/低熵 decode step 用两套不对称阈值（α=4 才降、β=2 就升），即"降精度保守、升精度激进"。
- **AutoFP：第一个多尺度浮点格式**（此前多尺度格式都是整数的）。AutoFP6 从 NVFP4 基底出发，只对量化误差最大的 4 元素子组追加 **2-bit 有符号偏移 d ∈ {−2,−1,0,+1}**（不是追加尾数位——因为 NVFP4 是就近舍入，追加位只能向上取值，够不到更近的 E2M3 值），偏移步长固定为 `δ = d·2⁻³`；AutoFP2 则是把指数位 `e₁e₀` 重映射为 `{e₁·e₀, e₁⊕e₀}` 得到五值集合 `{−2,−1,0,1,2}`（截断成 E1M0 会丢掉 0，精度崩塌）。
- **把"精化"变成一个 Tensor Core GEMM（extended-K GEMM）**：利用 `X̃ = X_q + M_X ⊙ X_r` 的线性性，把 `W̃Ã` 拆成四项；再用 **N:4 结构化稀疏**约束（每组 4 个子组里权重精化 N_W 个、激活精化 N_A = 4−N_W 个）并让 `M_W`、`M_A` 不相交，从而消掉二阶交叉项，最终写成 `[W_q | W_qr] · [A_q | A_qr]` 一次 reduction 长度至多 2K 的 GEMM。精化项 `4δ` 恰好落在 E2M1 网格上，多出的 4 倍吸收进 `s_W/4` 的 scale。
- **混批服务**：同一 batch 内不同请求对同一层选了不同精度时，取激活掩码的并集 `∪M_A` 构造单个 `W_qr`，全批跑一次 extended-K GEMM，而不是按精度拆子批（消融显示 batch 512 时拆子批损失 1.6× 吞吐）。
- **AutoFP 原生的加速器**：在 MAGNet（OS-LWS，256 PE）上扩展出混合精度 vMAC——每个 32 元素向量 MAC 可算 2 个 AutoFP2 权重、1 个 NVFP4 权重或 1 个 AutoFP6 权重；权重在 PE 内以 **5 bit 打包两个 AutoFP2 数字（2.5 bit/digit）**，用 escape bit + 两个 2-bit 域表示"是否为零"，NVFP4 模式下用 `W_q = (5Q₀ + 2Q₁)/2` 由两个五值数字线性重组。

## Key Contributions

- **A runtime precision policy that is genuinely online.** The paper first builds an oracle (§2.1) that, for each layer at each step, picks the lowest precision keeping the KL divergence to the full-precision next-token distribution under 1e-3 nats, and shows demand moves with prompt, layer, and phase — including a sharp regime change between the initial prefill and later agentic *refills* (refill 3 wants 2-bit for nearly all layers while the first prefill does not). Static in-distribution calibration (ModelOpt AutoQuantize, FlexQuant) under-provisions 26.9% and 37.6% of decode steps respectively; AutoEMatic's rules cut that to 7.7% and reproduce the oracle's 20/41/39 split with 15/45/40.
- **AutoFP, a multi-scale *floating-point* format.** Two derivations from one NVFP4 base: AutoFP6 by additive signed-offset mantissa refinement on 4-element subgroups (masked at 4 bits per 16-element group, offsets stored one byte per refined subgroup), AutoFP2 by exponent-bit remapping into a quinary alphabet. Storage cost is 4 + 0.5 (scale) + 2ρ ≈ **4.9 bits/weight at ρ = 0.2**, versus 13.5 bits if you simply store separate 2/4/6-bit copies.
- **A refinement-aware GPTQ calibration** that assigns the sparse refinement budget ρ to the subgroups with the largest diagonal-Hessian-weighted error rather than by NMSE alone (64.8% → 65.6% on LCB at the same 4.9 bits).
- **A deployable GPU path.** vLLM 0.15 plugin, custom attention backend on FlashInfer's FP8 kernel, two equal-byte page types (T=64 base pages, T/2 refined pages) so one paged allocator serves both; TTFT within 10% of NVFP4 while beating FP6g by up to 1.4×.
- **An accelerator that executes all three formats natively**, adding only 1.1% area over an NVFP4-only design (vMAC itself +1.2×, refinement lanes 12.3% of the vMAC) and reaching 5.46 TOPS/mm² versus 3.23 for NVFP4 and 6.16 for AutoFP2-only.
- **A reusable diagnostic**: the "under-provisioning rate" metric (fraction of layer-steps where the policy assigns less precision than the oracle demands) is a better probe of a runtime policy than end-task accuracy alone, and it cleanly explains why static calibration fails.

## Methodology

**Signal design.** `GE(t)` is the normalized Shannon entropy over the top-K = 10 output logits at step t — high entropy means small logit perturbations can flip the argmax, so high precision is warranted. `LA_ℓ(t)` is the relative L2 change a layer makes to its residual stream; a layer whose output is close to its input is "low-activity" and tolerates 2–4 bits. These are combined as `M_ℓ^{P/D}(t) = λ^{P/D}·LA_ℓ(t) + (1−λ^{P/D})·GE(t)` with λ_P = 1 (no tokens exist during prefill) and λ_D = 0.7. Because `GE` is only known after a token is emitted, decode reuses the previous step's entropy — a one-step lag the paper justifies by the low step-to-step entropy variance.

**Decision rules.** Instead of calibrated thresholds, the policy tracks running µ and σ of `M` and changes precision only on *sustained* trends over n_p = 2 preceding layers (α = 4 low-activity layers to drop a level at high entropy, β = 2 high-activity layers to raise it; reversed at low entropy). Every decode step restarts layer 0 at 4 bits. Prefill detects the characteristic spike as `M > µ + 3σ` and pins everything up to and including it at 6 bits. Nominal levels P = {2,4,6}; 2-bit applies to weights only (W2A4-KV4) because 2-bit activations and KV cache degrade badly, so the realized configurations are W2A4-KV4, W4A4-KV4, W6A6-KV6.

**Format construction.** NVFP4 = E2M1 values with E4M3 per-16-element group scales. AutoFP6 stores the NVFP4 base untouched plus a 4-bit mask `M_X` (one bit per 4-element subgroup of the 16-element group) and, for masked subgroups, four 2-bit offsets decoded with the *fixed* step 2⁻³ (an exact per-element `2^{e−3}` decode would need the exponent of each element and, per the ablation, costs 22% of decode throughput for no accuracy gain). AutoFP2 keeps the sign bit and remaps the two exponent bits into a magnitude in {0,1,2}, giving {−2,−1,0,1,2}; the NVFP4 scale (normalized to [−6,6]) is multiplied by c = 2 to compensate.

**Kernel formulation.** With disjoint weight/activation masks and N:4 sparsity (N_W + N_A = 4 per group), a refined layer is one extended-K GEMM `[W_q | W_qr]·[A_q | A_qr]` using Blackwell `tcgen05.mma`, which only consumes E2M1 with E4M3 scales — hence storing `4δ` in the refined operand and folding the 4 into the scale. `M_W` is fixed offline; only `M_A` varies per request, so batched serving ORs the activation masks and builds one `W_qr` for the whole batch.

**Accelerator.** MAGNet-style output-stationary / local-weight-stationary design, 256 PEs, 2 MB global SRAM, per-PE 32-wide vMAC with two 16-lane base blocks plus 8 sparse refinement lanes (V/4), shared PPU (1 per 32 PEs) that quantizes outputs, computes per-subgroup NMSE, selects offsets, and evaluates `M^{P/D}`. Implemented in SystemC with Catapult HLS RTL, TSMC 3nm at 1 GHz, VCS simulation, PrimeTime PX power.

**Evaluation.** Qwen3-8B, Qwen3.8-27B, DeepSeek-R1-Llama-70B, Gemma-4-26B-A4B, MiniMax-M2.7 (230B); reasoning on LongBench v2 / AIME 2024 / LiveCodeBench, agentic on τ²-bench / LongMemEval / SWE-bench Verified; 4× GB300, 32K generation limit, temperature 0.5, vLLM; weight calibration on 256 WikiText-2 samples; hyperparameters n_p = 2, α = 4, β = 2, τ = 0.10, SG = 4, ρ = 0.2, T = 64.

## Results & Highlights

- **Qwen3-8B / τ²-bench: 27.2% versus 17.5% for uniform NVFP4 (+9.7 pts, a 55% relative recovery), but still 3.5 pts short of both FP6g (30.7%) and BF16 (30.7%).** This is the clearest single illustration of the thesis — the same model, same 4.9-bit budget, recovers more than half of the NVFP4→FP6g gap on a dual-control agentic task — and also the clearest counter-example to "matches FP6g on most benchmarks": on this cell it does not.
- **Qwen3.8-27B / LongMemEval: 82.8% at 4.8 average execution bits, matching FP6g (82.8%) and BF16 (82.8%) exactly, versus 70.6% for NVFP4 (+12.2 pts) and 81.2% for AutoQuantize at 4.8 bits.** The same model on τ²-bench goes 50.0% (NVFP4) → 71.7% (AutoEMatic, 5.1 bits) → 71.7% (FP6g, 6.5 bits), i.e. a 21.7-point absolute jump for 0.6 extra nominal bits over NVFP4.
- **Gemma-4-26B / τ²-bench: 60.5% versus 46.5% for NVFP4 (+14.0 pts) and 58.6% for AutoQuantize at 5.0 bits (+1.9 pts), exactly tying FP6g's 60.5% while spending 5.0 bits instead of 6.5.** On the same model, LCB goes 75.3% → 83.1% (NVFP4 → AutoEMatic), again identical to FP6g and only 2.6 pts below BF16's 85.7%.
- **Qwen3-8B / LiveCodeBench: 60.5% at 4.8 bits versus 60.0% for AutoQuantize (4.5 bits), 49.2% for NVFP4 (+11.3 pts), 61.4% for FP6g and 66.5% for BF16.** The 0.5-pt edge over AutoQuantize is well inside run-to-run noise for a 200-problem code benchmark at temperature 0.5, so the defensible claim here is parity with a strong static baseline at similar bits, not superiority.
- **MiniMax-M2.7 (230B) / SWE-bench Verified: 70.5% at 4.2 bits prefill and 4.5 bits decode, versus 71.0% for FP8 (−0.5 pts), 67.8% for NVFP4 (+2.7 pts) and 70.5% for FP6g at 6 bits.** This is the strongest scaling evidence in the paper — a 230B agentic coding model held within half a point of FP8 while executing at roughly two-thirds of FP8's nominal width.
- **DS-Llama-70B / LiveCodeBench (component ablation, Table 4): 65.6% at 4.9 bits versus 51.7% for NVFP4 (+13.9 pts) and 68.4% for BF16 (−2.8 pts), against 55.7% for AutoQuantize at 5.3 bits and 48.6% for FlexQuant at 5.3 bits — the latter actually *below* plain NVFP4.** The separate-copy upper bound (three stored copies, 13.5 bits) reaches 66.2%, so the single-copy 4.9-bit format gives up only 0.6 pts; the same policy applied to the integer Any-Precision format collapses to 43.8% at 6 bits, isolating the format as the second necessary ingredient.
- **Oracle agreement: 7.7% under-provisioning versus 26.9% for AutoQuantize and 37.6% for FlexQuant, with a 15/45/40 (2/4/6-bit) layer split against the oracle's 20/41/39.** Using `LA` alone raises under-provisioning noticeably; the combined `M_D` is what closes the gap — a clean, falsifiable justification for the two-signal design.
- **GPU cost: 90–98% of NVFP4 decode throughput and TTFT within 10% of NVFP4 while beating FP6g by up to 1.4× and AutoQuantize by 1.25×.** Concretely, DS-Llama-70B at batch 1 decodes 120 tok/s versus 131 for NVFP4 (92%) and 1.5–2.5× faster than the BF16/FP8 baseline across batch sizes; signal computation costs 2.3–2.8% of TPOT (GE < 1.3%, LA 2.4 µs/layer).
- **Accelerator: 1.6–2.3× lower end-to-end latency and 0.77–0.93× the energy of an NVFP4-only datapath at iso-accuracy**, at +1.1% area, with the vMAC averaging 15 fJ/op versus 22 fJ/op for NVFP4 (and 10 fJ/op for pure AutoFP2). Kernel ablations attribute 1.5× (batch 1) and 1.6× (batch 512) of the throughput to the extended-K GEMM and union-of-masks mechanisms respectively.
- **Prefill sensitivity is concentrated in one layer**: running the spike layer at BF16 gives 71.7% on τ²-bench, identical to AutoEMatic's 6-bit choice, while NVFP4 at the spike costs 3.8 pts and AutoFP2 costs 15.9 pts — a tidy confirmation that the `µ + 3σ` spike detector is finding something real.

## Authors & Credibility

Akshat Ramachandran (Georgia Tech) leads, with Tushar Krishna's Georgia Tech group; the remaining authors — Reena Elangovan, Rangharajan Venkatesan, Charbel Sakr, Tijmen Blankevoort, Brucek Khailany — are NVIDIA Research, and the work was done during an internship at NVIDIA Research. This is close to the ideal author mix for this paper: Krishna's group has a long record in accelerator/NoC design and in KV-cache and quantization work (ThinKV, cited here as the source of the two-page-type allocator), while the NVIDIA side supplies the NVFP4 format, Tensor Core access, and the ModelOpt baseline. Venkatesan and Khailany are MAGNet authors, so extending MAGNet is not a strawman choice. The paper is marked "Under Review, 2026" and is unusually candid for an industry co-design paper: it has an explicit Limitations appendix admitting AutoFP2 yields no memory-bandwidth benefit, and an AI-usage statement disclosing GPT-6-Sol for writing, figure code, parameter sweeps and experiment launching. The main credibility discount is that every headline baseline except BF16/FP6g/NVFP4 is either NVIDIA's own tool (AutoQuantize) or a baseline the authors extended themselves (DP-LLM†), and all accuracy numbers come from a single run per cell with no variance reporting.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The individual pieces are recognizable — entropy-guided precision (Lee et al.), residual-update magnitude as a layer-importance proxy (early-exit work), masked subgroup refinement (their own ThinKV), N:4 sparsity for refinement lanes. What is genuinely new is the *composition*: the observation that a floating-point multi-scale format can be built by **additive signed offsets** rather than appended mantissa bits, and the algebraic trick that turns refinement into a single extended-K Tensor Core GEMM with no second pass. That trick is the paper's best idea and it is not incremental — it converts a format that would otherwise be unusable on real Tensor Cores (sub-4-bit E2M1 emulation, irregular gathers) into one MMA. The claim to be "the first to jointly adapt weight, activation, and KV-cache precision at runtime with an FP-based multi-scale format" is defensible as stated, though heavily qualified.

**Rigor — where the evidence is strong.** Table 4 is a genuinely well-designed ablation: it separates policy from format (row 3 vs row 7), establishes a separate-copy upper bound (66.2% at 13.5 bits), and shows the policy applied to an integer multi-scale format collapses to 43.8%, which rules out "the policy alone explains the gain." The oracle-based under-provisioning metric (§2.2) is a better-motivated diagnostic than end-task accuracy, and the fact that FlexQuant's 37.6% under-provisioning *predicts* its poor LCB score is a coherent story rather than a post-hoc one. The fixed-step-vs-exact-exponent ablation (same accuracy, +28% throughput) is exactly the kind of negative result that makes a design choice credible. The prefill-spike ablation (BF16 = 6-bit = 71.7%, NVFP4 = 67.9%, AutoFP2 = 55.8%) is a clean causal check on the `µ+3σ` detector.

**Rigor — where it is weak.** Three problems stand out.

1. **DP-LLM† is a strawman and the headline rests partly on it.** DP-LLM adapts weights only; the authors extend it to activations and KV cache themselves and report 16.7% on AIME and 15.9% on LongMemEval for Qwen3.8-27B — far below *plain NVFP4* (86.7%, 70.6%) and below even a 3-bit weight-only method should manage. A 3–6-bit any-precision method scoring 15.9% on LongMemEval is not a plausible representation of the published technique; it is a representation of this reimplementation. The abstract's "by up to 66.9%" is computed against that number and should be read as "our extension of DP-LLM is broken at these bit widths," not as a comparison to DP-LLM. The same suspicion applies to FlexQuant, which at 5.3 bits scores 48.6% on DS-Llama-70B LCB — *worse than uniform NVFP4's 51.7%* — which undermines the paper's own narrative that static scheduling fails because of precision demand, since a runtime-adaptation method losing to a uniform 4-bit baseline more likely indicates a calibration or implementation problem.
2. **The NVFP4 comparison is not iso-memory, and the paper is slightly loose about it.** AutoEMatic stores 4.9 bits/weight versus 4.5 for NVFP4, ~9% more. That is fine and honestly reported in Table 2, but the abstract's framing ("improves accuracy by up to 21.7% over NVFP4 ... using 4.9 bits versus 4.5") invites the reader to hear "better at the same size," which is not what was measured. The honest comparison is against FP6g at 6.5 bits, where AutoEMatic's 4.9 bits is a real 25% storage saving — and there the result is "matches," not "beats," with exact ties on 10 of 10 cells for Qwen3.8-27B and Gemma-4-26B. Those exact ties are themselves informative: they suggest the policy spends most of its budget at 6 bits on those models, so the 4.5–5.1 "average execution precision" is a mean over layers and steps rather than a per-token saving.
3. **The headline "up to 21.7%" is not traceable.** It appears in the abstract and twice in §5.2 but is never tied to a named benchmark cell, and the per-cell deltas I can check in Table 2 are *larger* (Gemma τ²-bench +14.0 pts = +30% relative; Qwen3.8-27B τ²-bench +21.7 pts). A number that cannot be located in the results table is a reporting defect regardless of which direction it errs.

**Missing ablations.** Despite the "jointly adapt weight, activation, and KV cache" claim, no row in Table 4 or Figure 14 disables KV-cache adaptation alone. Given that KV precision is where the agentic/LongMemEval gains are largest, a W/A-only variant is the single most informative missing experiment — it would tell us whether the KV-cache half of the story is doing the work or riding along. There is also no iso-storage comparison at ρ = 0 (pure AutoFP6, 4.5 bits, matching NVFP4's footprint), which would answer the obvious "what if I just want the same memory as NVFP4?" question directly; Table 4 row 5 (AutoFP6 only, 4.9 bits, 64.8%) is close but not the same experiment. And no seed variance anywhere: differences like 60.5 vs 60.0 on LCB, or 27.2 vs 30.7 on τ²-bench, are being read at a granularity the experimental design (one run, temperature 0.5) does not support.

**Reproducibility.** Mixed. The method is fully specified — every hyperparameter (n_p, α, β, τ, SG, c, ρ, T, the 0.003 NMSE threshold) appears in §5.1 and is swept in Appendix I, and the format is described bit-exactly (Table 5–8, the 5-bit AutoFP2 packing). But the artifact is a vLLM 0.15 plugin with custom CUDA kernels for `tcgen05.mma` and a Catapult-HLS accelerator model; nothing suggests a release, and reproducing the GPU numbers requires GB300 hardware that few reviewers have. The accelerator results come from a scheduling model calibrated against VCS simulation with **DRAM modeled at an assumed sustained 1 TB/s** — that single assumption drives every "1.6–2.3×" and "7–23%" number, and no sensitivity analysis on it is given.

**Significance.** The measurement contribution (§2.1–2.3) will outlive the system: "precision demand is dynamic, and static in-distribution calibration under-provisions 27–38% of decode steps even on held-out data from the same distribution" is a reusable, checkable claim that anyone shipping mixed-precision serving should care about. The format contribution is significant for NVIDIA specifically, since it composes with NVFP4 rather than replacing it. The energy claim needs care: at iso-*AutoEMatic*-accuracy the accelerator saves 7–23% energy, but the appendix (Fig. 33) reports that at iso-*NVFP4*-accuracy AutoEMatic is **13% higher energy**, because the mixed-precision datapath carries extra components. So the sign of the energy result depends entirely on which accuracy point you anchor to, and only one direction is in the abstract.

**Red flags, ranked.** (i) DP-LLM† numbers that are implausibly low, feeding a "66.9%" headline; (ii) FlexQuant below uniform NVFP4, suggesting baseline reimplementation issues; (iii) an untraceable 21.7%; (iv) 4.9 vs 4.5 bits framed as a like-for-like NVFP4 win; (v) hyperparameters (τ = 0.1, n_p = 2, α = 4, β = 2) selected by sweeping on LCB and then reported on LCB — a mild but real test-set tuning loop; (vi) no KV-only ablation under a joint-adaptation claim. None of these invalidate the core result, which is well-ablated and mechanistically explained, but together they mean the paper's *headline* is stronger than its *evidence*.

## Strengths

- The extended-K GEMM construction is a genuinely elegant piece of co-design: it takes a format that would be a memory-layout nightmare (irregular subgroup gathers, sub-4-bit values on hardware that does not support them) and reduces it to one standard MMA on existing Tensor Cores by exploiting linearity plus disjoint N:4 masks. The `4δ`-in-E2M1-with-`s_W/4`-scale detail is the kind of thing that only gets found by actually building it.
- The additive-signed-offset insight is correct and well argued: the paper explicitly explains *why* appending mantissa bits fails (NVFP4 rounds to nearest, so 1.4 stored as 1.5 can only be refined upward to 1.875, never to the nearer 1.375), which is the sort of negative analysis that distinguishes a real format design from a hand-wave.
- Table 4 is a model ablation table: it isolates policy, format, and calibration, includes a 13.5-bit separate-copy upper bound, and shows the policy collapsing on an integer format — which is precisely the experiment needed to justify "both ingredients are necessary."
- The 7.7% vs 26.9%/37.6% under-provisioning result is a clean, quantitative indictment of static calibration that does not depend on any downstream benchmark.
- Real systems work, not simulation-only: vLLM integration, FlashInfer attention, two page types sharing one allocator, union-of-masks batching, and measured throughput on 4× GB300 at batch sizes up to 128 and 128K context.
- Uncommon honesty in the Limitations appendix (AutoFP2 provides no bandwidth benefit; the stored base cannot be 2-bit without paying reconstruction overhead) and in the AI-usage statement.

## Limitations

- **AutoFP2 saves no memory.** Because it is derived on demand from the NVFP4 base, it cannot be the *stored* format; the authors concede this and defer it to future work. So the 2-bit level exists only to buy prefill throughput and energy on the custom accelerator — on GPUs it is emulated as NVFP4 and contributes accuracy but no speed.
- **The 2-bit level is weights-only** (W2A4-KV4), so "2-, 4-, 6-bit" is a statement about nominal levels, not about realized W/A/KV tuples; the paper is clear about this but the abstract's "adapts the precision of weights, activations, and the KV cache" reads broader than the implementation.
- **No memory-bandwidth win at all.** Storage is 4.9 bits versus 4.5 for NVFP4, and the paper never claims decode bandwidth savings — the wins are accuracy-per-bit versus FP6g, and latency/energy on the accelerator. Anyone reading this as a memory-footprint paper will be disappointed.
- **Accelerator evidence is modeled, not silicon.** 2 MB SRAM, 256 PEs, TSMC 3nm at 1 GHz, with DRAM at an assumed 1 TB/s; the 1.6–2.3× latency and 7–23% energy numbers inherit that assumption.
- **Baseline quality is uneven** (see Critical Assessment): DP-LLM† and FlexQuant both look mis-implemented, which inflates the perceived gap to prior runtime methods.
- **Single-run accuracy numbers** with no seeds or confidence intervals, at temperature 0.5, on benchmarks where 0.5–1 pt differences are routine.
- **Scope is five models, all dense or coarsely-MoE, all Qwen/Gemma/Llama/MiniMax families**; no fine-grained MoE (DeepSeek-V3-style), no diffusion LM, no multimodal. Whether `LA` behaves the same under expert routing is untested.
- **Interaction with other inference optimizations is unexplored**: speculative decoding (where draft acceptance depends on logit fidelity), structured pruning, and long-context attention sinks all change the numerics this policy is reading.
- **Policy is hand-designed rules, not learned.** The ablations show the rules track the oracle well (15/45/40 vs 20/41/39), but a learned or bandit-style policy is an obvious next step and its absence leaves the "rule-based is enough" claim supported only by the one instantaneous-vs-trend comparison (95.5% vs 92.5% at 4.6 vs 4.7 bits).

## Relevance

这篇直接落在「Reliability under Extreme Performance Optimization」——它研究的正是激进低精度/混合精度带来的数值误差，并且把"精度供给不足"（under-provisioning）量化成了一个可测指标：静态校准在分布内留出样本上仍有 26.9%–37.6% 的 decode 步被分配了低于需求的精度，这个数字对任何做量化部署可靠性评估的人都有直接参考价值。对做静默数据损坏/数值故障检测的视角也有用：它说明"模型输出异常"未必来自比特翻转，也可能来自精度策略在错误时刻降档，而 `LA`/`GE` 这类运行时信号恰好可以作为精度需求的在线探针。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. What is the accuracy at ρ = 0 (pure AutoFP6, 4.5 bits, exactly NVFP4's footprint)? That is the comparison a deployment team would actually run, and Table 4's 64.8% at 4.9 bits is not it.
2. Does the policy still help if the KV cache is left at 8 bits? No ablation isolates KV adaptation, yet the largest gains (LongMemEval +12.2, τ²-bench +21.7) are on long-context/agentic tasks where KV dominates.
3. How sensitive are the 1.6–2.3× and 7–23% accelerator numbers to the assumed 1 TB/s DRAM bandwidth, and to SRAM size (2 MB is small for a 70B model's working set)?
4. Does the entropy signal survive speculative decoding, where the accepted-token distribution is not the model's raw next-token distribution? And does `LA` remain meaningful under fine-grained MoE, where a layer's residual update depends on which experts fired?
5. Could `GE` and `LA` double as *fault detectors* — i.e., does an unexpected jump in `LA` at fixed input indicate a bit-flip or a bad kernel rather than a genuine precision need? The paper treats these signals purely as precision demand; the reliability reading is unexplored.
6. What happens at batch 128+ with maximally heterogeneous precision requests — does `∪M_A` approach "refine everything," collapsing the extended-K GEMM back to 2K reduction for the whole batch?
7. Would a learned policy (small MLP on `M`, or contextual bandit with accuracy feedback) beat the hand-tuned α/β/n_p rules, and would it transfer across model families without re-tuning on LCB?
8. Is the 21.7% headline reproducible as a stated per-benchmark number, and what is the seed variance on the 60.5 vs 60.0 LCB comparison against AutoQuantize?
