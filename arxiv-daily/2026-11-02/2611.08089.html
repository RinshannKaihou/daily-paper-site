01

The instrument

No training, no gradients, no interventions. One encoder row dotted with each expert's output.

  1. 1

    Start from the layer sum

    The MoE output is a router-weighted sum over the 8 active experts: y(t) = Σ g_j(t)·u_j(t).

  2. 2

    Exploit affine encoders

    Each latent's pre-activation W_enc,i · y + b is linear in y, so substituting the sum splits it into one term per expert — algebraically exact.

  3. 3

    Keep only positive drive

    c = ReLU(g_j · W_enc,i · u_j), averaged over 1.5B held-out tokens and renormalised into a contribution fraction s(j,i).

  4. 4

    Threshold into a graph

    Expert j dominates latent i when s(j,i) ≥ 0.2 (uniform would be 1/128 ≈ 0.008), giving a 128 × 220 bipartite dominance graph.

  5. 5

    Ask a model what it found

    GPT-5.5 explains 2,000 sampled latents per expert from 10 contexts plus logit-lens effects, names each expert from its word cloud, then partitions all 128 labels into domains.

  6. 6

    Read the division of labour

    Degree on both sides, pairwise Jaccard overlap, and a bottom-up taxonomy — all statistics over the same graph, not over human-labelled corpora.

The identity, in one line

fi(t) = Σj∈A(t) gj(t)·(Wenc,i·uj(t)) + benc,i

The decomposition carries no approximation error of its own: the only error is the SAE's reconstruction of the layer output — FVU 25.1% at layer 30, the layer every semantic claim rests on.

03

The verdict

A well-executed descriptive study. The instrument is the contribution; the taxonomy is the demonstration.

Strengths

n = 3 layers
  • Exact and free. Two lines of algebra, no training and no intervention; it transfers to any MoE whose layer output is a weighted sum, shared experts included.
  • Pre-empts the obvious objection. The rank-aligned control defuses “this is just top-k gating”, and the truncation count closes the ReLU artefact.
  • Care in the interpretation loop. Top-5 plus random-5 contexts to counter near-duplicate bias, Uninterpretable allowed, logit lens for diffuse features.
  • Auditable. All 128 word clouds, an expert→label→domain table, all four prompts, and released SAEs and code.
  • A real negative result. Three non-subject domains are 28.1% of the layer and would be scattered by any human subject taxonomy.

Limitations

n = 1 model
  • No causal validation. Pruning, merging and steering are framed as “a testable hypothesis” and then deferred; dominance is co-activation, not functional responsibility.
  • SAE fidelity bounds everything. FVU 25.1% at layer 30, the only layer with a taxonomy — a quarter of that layer's variance sits outside the latents being interpreted.
  • τ = 0.2 is fixed, unswept. Every degree and overlap number is conditional on it, and there is no null model for how much concentration the arithmetic forces.
  • One judge, no human check. Explanations, theme judgments, labels and the partition all come from GPT-5.5, with no agreement statistic.
  • Single model, single seed. No comparison against Herbst et al. on the same footing; the domain count is partly a prompt budget artifact.
04

Open questions

The cheap experiments the paper names but does not run.

Q1

Does the dominant set survive ablation?

Remove E50 at layer 30 and compare the activation drop on its dominant latents against a matched set it merely fires on. One ablation would make the construct causal.

Q2

How much is arithmetic, not learning?

Rebuild the graph with contributions reassigned to a random activated expert ∝ gj. If the null already yields ~50% single dominators, 62.1% is a weak finding.

Q3

What is the within-expert baseline?

For E50, what share of the latents it activates on are Java-related? The gap to 86.7% is the value the contribution construction actually adds.

Q4

How sensitive to τ and SAE width?

Sweep τ ∈ {0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5} and retrain layer 30 at another seed: do E50, E57, E1 and E76 re-emerge with the same dominant sets?
