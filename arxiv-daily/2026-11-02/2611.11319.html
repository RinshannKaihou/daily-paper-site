Why this matters

Turns an aggressive inference optimization into something you can audit — and pinpoints where its own 2-bit index breaks the guarantee.

Critical edge

At the default ε₀ = 0.05, the deployed 2-bit sketch violates its own δ on 62% of decisions, so the headline numbers run without a valid certificate.

Method, in four moves

  1. Keep everything; compress nothing permanently

    The full KV cache stays in host DRAM. The GPU holds only sinks, a local window, and a random sample of tokens.

  2. Sample by importance, not top-k

    Inclusion probability rᵢ = min(1, c·τᵢ) from KVzip scores. The randomness is the point: it makes the sampling error estimable.

  3. Certify the attention denominator

    A bounded-shrinkage estimator plus a Bernstein bound yields ε̂(L), with Pr[|D̂ − D|/D > ε̂] ≤ δ. If ε̂ < ε, no fetch happens.

  4. Fetch the minimum exact set

    Rank tokens by marginal bound reduction gᵢ, then pull the smallest sufficient set from CPU. Because that set is chosen independently of the Bernoulli draws, no union bound is needed.

In the system: a vLLM fork with token-level offload, block compaction, UVA zero-copy fetch, and a fused radix-select decide kernel. Scope: the certificate covers D only; composing it with a verified numerator is cited, not run.

+Strengths

  • A correct, non-trivial theoretical core. Theorem 1's unique MSE minimizer and Theorem 2's union-bound-free fetch both hold; the latter is the kind of insight that changes system design.
  • Compression and accuracy become independent knobs. A fixed ratio turns into a per-query, per-head, per-layer decision, with a 43× usable traffic range.
  • Clean Pareto win over eviction at every rate, model, and context length — and the gap widens exactly at 32× and 128K.
  • Real system, honest reporting. A working vLLM fork, plus a published figure that is effectively a failed validation of their own certificate.

−Limitations

  • Verified denominator only. The end-to-end attention guarantee requires a verified numerator that is cited, never implemented, measured, or costed.
  • Proofs assume a perfect sketch. The 2-bit sketch breaks δ at ε₀ ≤ 0.1, so reliability holds mainly where the method fetches little.
  • Baselines are the weakest class. No vAttention, no query-aware sparsity (Quest, HashAttention, MagicPIG), no quantization, no compaction.
  • Small evaluation, poor absolute latency. 50 RULER / 25 LOFT examples, no variance; 73 ms per step versus 4 ms for Fast KVzip.

Open questions

  1. What does the composed verified-N + verified-D algorithm actually cost — does traffic roughly double, and does 1.6× survive?
  2. Can the certificate absorb 2-bit sketch error without becoming too loose to be useful at ε₀ = 0.05?
  3. How does a per-query certificate hold up over long generation, where attention drifts across thousands of steps?
  4. Is greedy marginal ordering near-optimal, and how much would a better sketch buy compared with a better bound?
