> **一句话总结：** 作者用"幅度匹配的随机方向对照 + 排序感知指标"重测了 36 个 (模型, 层, PII 类型) 单元上的 SAE 特征干预，发现按对数概率判据看似成功的 17/36 (47%) 个单元中只有 1 个 (2.8%，Llama-3.1-8B 第 16 层/姓名) 真正通过多指标检验，其余要么随机方向抑制更强、要么同一特征被四个 PII 类型共用（特征选择塌缩），说明现有"降 logprob 即成功"的评测无法证明干预打中了 PII 泄露的因果通路。

> **TL;DR:** On three LoRA-fine-tuned 8–9B models (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen3-8B, Gemma-2-9B) with task-specific top-k SAEs at 25/50/75% depth, the standard logprob criterion (Δlp ≤ −0.3) flags 17 of 36 (47%) (model, layer, PII-type) cells as successful PII suppression, but only 1 of 36 (2.8%, Llama L16/name) survives a four-part test that adds a rank-aware extraction-cost metric and a magnitude-matched random-direction control — a 44-point collapse. In the worst case (Gemma L20/name) the random direction beats the selected SAE feature in 50/50 validation pairs and all 20 dictionary-aligned decoder controls beat it too (mean Δlp −9.10 vs −2.80). A separate 9,000-run causal mediation study finds 146 of 150 (97.3%) correlation-selected candidate features are statistically indistinguishable from random controls. Worth reading if you use SAEs for safety interventions; skip if you want a working defense — this paper is a diagnostic and a research agenda, not a method.

## 核心贡献

- **证伪了当前 SAE 隐私干预的主流评测信号。** 在对数概率既用于"选特征"又用于"评效果"的设定下（即现有工作的做法），36 个单元中有 17 个（47%）看起来成功；加入排序感知指标与幅度匹配的随机方向对照后只剩 1 个（2.8%）。
- **识别出两种可复现的失败模式。** 模式 A（随机占优）：随机方向的抑制效果反而强于选中的 SAE 特征，Gemma L20/name 的 Sel_lp 仅 0.37；模式 B（特征选择塌缩）：同一层同一特征被四个 PII 类型同时选中（Llama L8 的 f=16363、Gemma L20 的 f=20385、Qwen L9 的 f=30639），但干预效果强烈偏向姓名。
- **给出了一个强正对照，证明判据不是"不可能通过"。** Llama L16/name 的 Sel_lp 单元均值约 1,995，在韩文与 JSON 等分布外模板上仍保持 >400，且 20 个同字典解码方向中 0 个能复现其效果。
- **把"随机基线"批评从通用可解释性推进到隐私领域。** 用同 SAE 字典内采样的解码方向做二次对照，排除了"各向同性随机向量偏离激活流形所以破坏更大"这一几何解释。
- **提出并实测了一条因果研究路线。** 9,000 次因果中介运行（180 单元 × 50 对）表明中介分析本身能可靠区分有无记忆的样本，瓶颈在候选特征生成——两类选择准则（logprob 驱动 vs 激活率驱动）在 36 个组合中零重叠，却都以相近的低比例通过因果验证。
- **提出"因果落地的 PII 抑制"研究议程**：用 path patching 做电路级定位，再用中介分析验证，取代相关性层面的单特征消融。

## Key Contributions

- **Shows the standard evaluation signal is not feature-specific.** With logprob used jointly for selection and evaluation — exactly current practice — 17/36 cells (47%) pass a Δlp ≤ −0.3 gate; under four conjunctive criteria (Δlp ≤ −0.3, Δbr ≥ +1.0, Sel_lp ≥ 5, Sel_br ≥ 5) only 1/36 (2.8%) survives.
- **Characterizes two failure modes with mechanism-level diagnostics.** Failure mode A (random-dominant): the coherence-matched random direction suppresses more than the selected feature, and this survives replacement by 20 dictionary-aligned decoder directions. Failure mode B (feature selection collapse): one feature is selected for all four PII types yet its intervention effect is 2.3× stronger on names than on the other three types, despite ≤5% variation in SAE activation magnitude.
- **Establishes a positive control.** Llama L16/name passes all nine prompt templates including OOD Korean and JSON, with Sel_lp ≈ 1,995 and random-control |Δlp| near the 10⁻³ measurement floor; 0/20 sampled decoder directions produce a comparable effect (control mean −0.007 ± 0.014 vs selected −1.06).
- **Rules out a geometric confound.** The isotropic random control matches token-wise magnitude and span-level direction coherence; a second control draws directions from the same learned SAE dictionary, so the random-dominant pattern cannot be dismissed as an off-manifold artifact.
- **Demonstrates causal mediation is implementable here.** 9,000 patching runs over 180 cells; the verifier cleanly separates memorizing from non-memorizing PII types (email TE > 2.4 in all three models, phone ≈ 0), but 146/150 (97.3%) activation-rate-selected candidates are indistinguishable from random.
- **Reframes the problem.** Argues the bottleneck is candidate generation, not verification, and proposes circuit-level localization plus mediation analysis as the research mission.

## Methodology

**Models and data.** Three general-purpose LLMs — Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (32 layers, d=4096), Qwen3-8B (36 layers, d=4096), Gemma-2-9B (42 layers, d=3584) — all LoRA fine-tuned on the same synthetic medical PII corpus. Each training example appends four PII fields (name, patient ID, phone, email) to a clinical note. The medical domain is chosen because clinical-LLM memorization is well documented; synthetic data avoids exposing real records and permits entity-level disjoint splits.

**Data hygiene.** Clinical notes and PII values are generated by deterministic templates, no LLM involved. Three disjoint splits: a 600-entity evaluation registry (seed 42), a 50k-entity SAE-training corpus (seed 1234, name pools with every evaluation name removed, IDs starting at PID-10001, phone/email rejection-sampled against the registry), and a separate LoRA fine-tuning corpus with a third name pool. Overlap was verified programmatically. Within the registry, feature discovery uses 10 pairs (indices 0–9) and all reported evaluation uses a disjoint 50-pair validation split (300–349).

**SAEs.** Task-specific top-k SAEs (Gao et al., 2025) at three depth-proportional layers — 25/50/75% of depth, i.e. Gemma 9/20/31, Llama 8/16/24, Qwen 9/18/27. Expansion ×8, sparsity k=32, auxiliary loss weight 0.25, 80k steps, batch 4096 tokens, lr 3e-4, single RTX 4090. All SAEs reach >98.8% variance explained; PII-position relative reconstruction error ranges from 1.99% (Llama L8) to 39.20% (Llama L24), which the authors report separately because corpus-level reconstruction says little about the intervention-relevant positions.

**Intervention.** Hidden-space steering along the decoder direction of the selected feature: h'_t = h_t + α·z_t[f]·W_dec[f], with α = −2 fixed globally (no per-cell tuning). Because top-k activations are sparse, the term vanishes where z_t[f]=0, so no explicit PII-span mask is needed. The authors note this preserves the SAE reconstruction-error term that a full encode-decode pipeline (PrivacyScalpel-style) would discard; the selectivity ratio is invariant to that term but absolute magnitudes are not.

**Feature selection.** For each (model, layer, PII type): candidate = high-activation non-dead features near the PII marker; selected = the candidate yielding the largest gold-PII-span logprob reduction on the discovery split. The authors explicitly flag this as partially circular — the thing being diagnosed.

**Controls.** (i) Coherence-matched isotropic random direction: same α, same token-wise magnitude m_t = ‖z_t[f]·W_dec[f]‖₂, same single direction r shared across all intervened positions (matching span-level coherence), averaged over three seeds. (ii) Dictionary-aligned control: r replaced by the normalized decoder direction of another randomly sampled non-dead feature g from the same SAE (20 draws, fixed seed 42, f excluded), preserving positions and magnitudes determined by f.

**Metrics.** Δlp = change in mean log-probability of the gold PII span (negative = suppression). Δbr = change in a rank-aware extraction-cost proxy b_rank(y) = Σ_t log₂(rank_t) (positive = gold tokens pushed down the candidate ranking, i.e. harder to extract). Selectivity Sel = |Δ_target| / |Δ_random| on each metric. A cell is "clean-strong" iff Δlp ≤ −0.3 AND Δbr ≥ +1.0 AND Sel_lp ≥ 5 AND Sel_br ≥ 5.

**Evaluation scale.** 9 prompt templates (T0 = the 4-field training format; T1–T8 isolate one PII field with varied labels including Korean, JSON, and narrative) × 50 validation pairs = 450 contexts per cell, 36 cells.

**Causal mediation feasibility study.** 50 counterfactual pairs per (model, layer, PII type) where x₀ replaces the PII with a different value of the same type. Standard activation patching yields identically zero NIE here because x₀ and x₁ share the preceding prefix, so the authors use a noising-style zero-ablation of f during the x₁ run (conceptually the causal-tracing noising intervention of Meng et al., 2022). TE = lp(y|x₁) − lp(y|x₀) is the memorization signal; NIE_f = lp(y|x₁, f=0) − lp(y|x₀); mediation fraction MF_f = 1 − mean(NIE_f)/mean(TE). Crucially, candidates here are selected by activation rate at the position preceding the PII — independent of logprob — so this study breaks the circularity. 3 models × 3 layers × 4 PII types × 5 features × 50 pairs = 9,000 runs.

## Results & Highlights

- **The headline collapse: 47% → 2.8%.** A logprob-only gate (Δlp ≤ −0.3) labels 17/36 cells (47%) as apparent PII suppression. Under the four conjunctive criteria only 1/36 (2.8%) remains — Llama L16/name. The drop is not a threshold artifact: relaxing selectivity to ≥2 admits only 3/36 (8.3%), tightening to ≥10 still leaves the same single cell, and loosening the magnitude gates to |Δlp| ≥ 0.0 / Δbr ≥ 0.0 admits 2/36 (5.6%).
- **Failure mode A is not a geometric artifact.** For Gemma L20/name, Sel_lp = 0.37 (name), 0.14 (id), 0.27 (phone), 0.09 (email) — all below 1. The random direction wins in 50/50 validation pairs and 439/450 per-context observations (97.6%). All 20 dictionary-aligned decoder directions also beat the selected feature (20/20), with mean Δlp −9.10 ± 6.38 vs −2.80 for the selected direction, so the off-manifold explanation for the isotropic control does not rescue the result.
- **Failure mode B: one feature, four PII types, asymmetric effect.** In 3 of 9 (model, layer) cells the discovery procedure picks the same top-1 feature for all four PII types (Gemma L20 f=20385, Llama L8 f=16363, Qwen L9 f=30639). In Llama L8, feature 16363's activation magnitude varies by ≤5% across types (name 6.97, id 7.04, phone 6.77, email 7.01), yet |Δlp| = 4.57 on names vs ≈1.96 averaged over the other three — a 2.3× asymmetry. Worse, 14 of 20 dictionary-aligned directions beat it (−6.60 ± 3.48 vs −4.57), even though it had dominated the isotropic control (Sel_lp = 2.32). Shared feature selection does not imply a shared causal effect.
- **The positive control is genuinely strong — and its absolute effect is modest.** Llama L16/name holds Sel_lp > 400 across all nine templates including OOD Korean (1,928) and JSON (423), with cell mean ≈1,995 and random-control |Δlp| ≈ 10⁻³; 0/20 decoder controls come close. But its rank gain is only Δbr = +1.465 vs +0.010 for the random control — roughly a 2.8× increase in extraction cost, a real but small absolute effect.
- **Downstream utility tracks the diagnostic exactly.** Llama L16/name changes by at most 1 point: PubMedQA 31.0 → 32.0, MedQA 45.5 → 46.5, MMLU 61.0 → 61.5. The non-clean-strong cells collapse: Qwen L18/name 28.5 → 16.5, 57.0 → 23.5, 71.5 → 48.5 (−12 to −33.5 points); Gemma L20/name 47.5 → 33.5, 60.5 → 34.5, 67.5 → 45.0 (−14 to −26 points). An intervention that tanks MMLU by 23 points while lowering PII logprob is not a privacy defense.
- **Causal mediation separates cases cleanly but almost no candidate passes.** Memorization is PII-type specific: email shows TE > 2.4 in all three models (Gemma +2.580, Llama +3.560, Qwen +2.466), phone is ≈0 (−0.038 / +0.067 / −0.065), patient ID is negative or ≈0, and name is inconsistent (+0.609 Gemma, −0.283 Llama, −0.604 Qwen). Of 150 candidate features with |TE| ≥ 0.05, 146 (97.3%) have NIE_f/NIE_rand inside (0.5, 2.0) — indistinguishable from random. All candidates are indistinguishable in 9 of 10 (model, PII type) combinations; the sole exception, Gemma-2-9B/name (11/15, 73.3%), is precisely the one non-email combination with a positive memorization signal.
- **Two selection criteria, zero overlap, same failure.** The logprob-selected features of the main experiment and the activation-rate-selected features of the feasibility study share no feature across all 36 combinations, yet both pools pass causal verification at similarly low rates. The bottleneck is candidate generation, not verification.

Chaerin Lee and Wonho Lee (equal contribution), Sohee Park, and Daeseon Choi, all at Soongsil University, Korea; Choi is corresponding author. This is a privacy/security group publishing in the ACL venue ecosystem (the paper is typeset in ACL format with an ACL-style Limitations section and BlackboxNLP/ACL citations), and the work is funded by a Korea Internet & Security Agency (KISA) grant on preventing sensitive-information inference in foundation models — a real, domain-relevant funding line rather than a generic AI grant. The paper's citation base is appropriate and current: PrivacyScalpel (Frikha et al., 2025) as the target of critique, ProPILE and PII-Scope as the evaluation lineage, Korznikov et al. (2026) and Ronge et al. (2026) for the SAE-random-baseline critique, Vig et al. (2020) for mediation, Meng et al. (2022) for noising, Wang et al. (2022) for path patching. ## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** Modest but well-aimed. The individual ingredients all exist — SAE sanity-checking against random baselines (Korznikov et al., 2026; Heap et al., 2025), rank-aware leakage metrics, mediation analysis (Vig et al., 2020). The contribution is the specific composition applied to a specific literature that has been reporting 5.15% → 0.0% leakage-rate wins, plus the diagnostic finding that the wins do not survive. That is a useful negative result, and the paper is honest that it is a diagnostic rather than a method: "Multi-metric evaluation is a diagnostic tool, not a final solution."

**Rigor — where it is strong.** The control design is better than most critique papers bother with. Matching token-wise magnitude *and* span-level direction coherence, then adding a same-dictionary decoder control to kill the off-manifold objection, then checking α ∈ {−1,−2,−3,−4,−5} and three threshold axes — each of these closes an obvious rebuttal. The positive control (Llama L16/name) is the single most important design decision in the paper: without it, "47% → 2.8%" would be dismissible as an impossibly strict criterion. It holds Sel_lp > 400 on Korean and JSON templates the model never saw in that format, and 0/20 decoder directions reproduce it. That is a real effect.

**Rigor — where it is weak.** Three issues stand out.

*First, the discovery split is 10 pairs.* Feature selection is an argmax over candidates evaluated on 10 pairs, and the selected feature is then frozen. Failure mode B — the same feature winning for all four PII types — is exactly what you would expect if the argmax is fitting noise on a tiny split, especially when the features are high-activation "generic PII region" candidates. The paper interprets collapse as evidence that a single feature cannot encode type-specific PII, which may be right, but it never runs the obvious ablation: repeat discovery across several disjoint 10-pair splits and measure selection stability. If the selected feature changes across discovery seeds, "collapse" is a selection-variance story, not a mechanistic one. This is the largest missing experiment.

*Second, the critique never touches PrivacyScalpel's actual setup.* PrivacyScalpel reported 5.15% → 0.0% email leakage on Gemma2-2B and Llama2-7B using a z-space encode-decode pipeline with layer chosen by feature probing. This paper uses 8B/9B models, depth-proportional layers chosen a priori, a hidden-space intervention that deliberately preserves the reconstruction-error term, and logprob/rank metrics instead of leakage rate. The authors are explicit that the selectivity ratio is invariant to the error term while absolute magnitudes are not, and they list "Direct z-space replication is left to future work" — but that means the headline framing ("existing evaluations cannot verify...") is supported while the stronger implicit claim ("PrivacyScalpel's reported result is a magnitude artifact") is not directly tested. A reader could reasonably walk away over-updating on a paper that was never re-run.

*Third, the utility evaluation is thin and one of its baselines is at floor.* Three configurations, 200 examples each, zero-shot argmax over admissible label logits with no generation. Llama L16/name's PubMedQA baseline of 31.0% is *below* 3-way chance (33.3%), so "no degradation" on that benchmark carries little information — the model was already near random on it. The MedQA and MMLU numbers are more informative (45.5 and 61.0 baselines), but 200 examples gives roughly ±3.5 points of standard error, which is the same order as the "at most one point" claim being made. The qualitative contrast with the −12 to −34 point collapses is still convincing; the precise "≤1 point" framing is over-precise.

**A data quirk worth naming.** The synthetic corpus is generated by deterministic templates: patient IDs are sequential (PID-00001, …), phone numbers are "deterministic arithmetic functions of the entity index," and emails are derived from names with an optional numeric tag over seven domains. So the "memorization" being measured for email is partly memorization of a highly regular string-generating pattern rather than of an identity. That fits the observed TE pattern suspiciously well — email (long, multi-token, format-regular) shows TE > 2.4 while phone (short, also formulaic) shows ≈0 and ID (sequential, maximally predictable) shows negative TE. A model that has learned "emails look like firstname.lastname@domain" will assign high probability to the true email under x₁ and low under a counterfactual, which is what TE measures. The authors acknowledge synthetic data cannot capture real-record noise, but they frame the core claim as methodological and dataset-independent — which is fair for the logprob-vs-random-control point, less obviously fair for the mediation results.

**Overclaiming check.** Mostly clean. "Only 1 case (2.8%) passes" is accurate given the stated criteria, and the sensitivity analysis backs it. "Random directions outperform selected features" is correctly scoped — Appendix A.9 explicitly narrows Failure mode A to "the logprob-selected SAE direction was not unusually effective relative to magnitude-matched decoder directions sampled from the same learned SAE dictionary," which is the defensible version. The one place the framing runs ahead of the evidence is the abstract's "existing evaluations cannot verify whether interventions target causal pathways," which is true but could be read as "all SAE PII work is wrong" rather than "this evaluation signal is insufficient" — the paper's own body is more careful than its abstract.

**Statistical note.** The 36 cells are not independent: they are 9 (model, layer) pairs × 4 PII types, and in collapse cells the same feature is reused across all four types. So "17/36" is closer to 9 effective units with pseudo-replication, and the 47% figure should be read as a descriptive count over a grid, not an estimate of a population rate. The paper never claims otherwise, but the percentage framing invites the wrong inference.

## Strengths

- The positive control is decisive. Llama L16/name holding Sel_lp > 400 on OOD Korean and JSON, with 0/20 same-dictionary decoder directions matching it, proves the four-part criterion can be satisfied — this single result converts the paper from "my threshold is too strict" into "the other 35 cells really are different."
- Two independent controls (isotropic + dictionary-aligned) close the most obvious escape hatch. Many SAE critiques stop at the isotropic baseline and get dismissed on manifold-geometry grounds; this one does not.
- The α sweep and three-axis threshold sensitivity are thorough, and the Qwen L18/name case is reported honestly as α-dependent rather than quietly dropped — selectivity exceeds 1 at α = −1, −2 and flips below 1 from α = −3, which the authors note means α = −2 if anything *overestimates* selectivity for that cell.
- The downstream utility experiment is the right validation to run: the clean-strong cell is utility-preserving while both non-clean-strong cells lose 12–34 accuracy points, so the diagnostic predicts real behavior rather than just re-describing its own metrics.
- The feasibility study is a genuine contribution, not a gesture. 9,000 runs, candidate selection deliberately decoupled from logprob, and the finding that 97.3% of candidates are indistinguishable from random — with the sole exception being exactly the cell with a positive memorization signal — is a clean result.
- The zero-overlap finding between logprob-driven and activation-rate-driven selection is a sharp, quotable observation: two criteria that measure entirely different axes both fail causal verification at similar rates, which is strong evidence that the problem is upstream of selection.
- Limitations section is unusually candid: circular selection, hidden-space vs z-space, SAE capacity (no ×16/×32 ablation), single-feature scope, synthetic data, no adversarial extraction. All the objections a reviewer would raise are pre-registered.

## Limitations

- **Discovery split is 10 pairs**, with no selection-stability ablation across disjoint discovery splits. Failure mode B may partly be argmax-overfitting rather than mechanistic collapse.
- **Never replicates PrivacyScalpel's actual pipeline** (z-space encode-decode, 2B/7B models, probing-selected layer, leakage-rate metric), so the critique lands on the evaluation signal but not directly on the published numbers.
- **Single-feature, single-α (α = −2), three fixed depth layers, one SAE configuration** (×8 expansion, k=32). Larger SAEs, other sparsity levels, other objectives, and other layers are untested — the authors flag this, but it means "35/36 fail" is a statement about this configuration family.
- **Synthetic, template-generated corpus.** PII values are formulaic (sequential IDs, arithmetic phone numbers, name-derived emails), so memorization signals may reflect format regularity; no real clinical text, no annotation noise, no formatting diversity.
- **No adversarial extraction evaluation.** The metrics are mechanism-specific diagnostics, explicitly not privacy guarantees against an attacker; extraction rate and feature-specificity are related but distinct.
- **Utility evaluation covers only 3 configurations × 200 examples × 3 benchmarks**, with argmax-over-logits rather than generation, and one baseline (Llama PubMedQA, 31.0%) sitting below 3-way chance.
- **Absolute effect sizes are small even in the success case**: Llama L16/name's rank improvement is Δbr = +1.465, about 2.8× extraction cost — real, but not a defense.
- **36 cells are 9 effective units** with features shared across PII types in collapse cells; percentages invite over-reading.
- **The proposed agenda is not executed.** Circuit-level localization via path patching is proposed, not attempted; the paper establishes that the bottleneck is candidate generation but does not demonstrate a candidate-generation method that works.

## Relevance

直接命中 **LLM Hidden States & Interpretability** 方向：这是对 SAE 特征干预可靠性的方法学批判，核心论点是"SAE 方向在幅度匹配的随机对照下并不占优"，与用户关注的探针、电路、表征几何一脉相承。更值得读的是它的诊断范式——正对照 + 双随机基线 + 排序感知指标 + 因果中介验证——这套组合可以直接搬到任何"用 SAE 做安全干预"的工作上（去毒、拒答、概念擦除），用来检验那些报告了漂亮指标的结果是否也只是幅度伪影。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. How stable is feature selection across disjoint discovery splits? If you re-run discovery on pairs 10–19, 20–29, 30–39, does Llama L8 still pick f=16363 for all four PII types? This is the single experiment that would decide whether Failure mode B is mechanistic or statistical.
2. What happens with a direct, non-SAE baseline — e.g. the top singular direction of the gradient of gold-PII logprob, or a difference-in-means direction between PII-present and PII-absent prompts? If that beats the selected SAE feature, the problem is SAE feature isolation, not evaluation.
3. Would the same 47% → 3% collapse appear on real clinical text (MIMIC-style, with proper governance) rather than deterministic templates? The email-vs-phone TE gap is suspicious enough that the synthetic generator deserves an ablation with randomized, non-formulaic PII strings.
4. Does the diagnostic transfer to other safety interventions built on SAEs — refusal-direction ablation, toxicity steering, concept unlearning? The random-control protocol is domain-agnostic and cheap; a sweep across published SAE interventions would be far more consequential than another PII study.
5. Can circuit-level localization actually produce candidates that pass mediation? The paper's own logic says the bottleneck is candidate generation; a single worked example — path-patch to find the attention heads writing the email span, then verify with NIE — would convert the agenda from proposal to demonstration.
6. Why is Gemma-2-9B/name the one cell where 11/15 candidates are distinguishable from random, and why does Gemma show positive name TE while Llama and Qwen show negative? Is this a tokenizer artifact (Gemma's SentencePiece splitting names differently), a training-data artifact, or a genuine architectural difference?
7. What does z-space (encode-decode) intervention do to the absolute Δlp and Δbr magnitudes, and does the selectivity ranking of cells change? The authors argue the ratio is invariant but the magnitudes are not — worth checking whether the "clean-strong" set is identical under z-space.
