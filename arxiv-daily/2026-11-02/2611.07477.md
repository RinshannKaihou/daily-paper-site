> **一句话总结：** KOSMOS 用"自调度常驻内核"取代固定或预测式的通信/计算 CU 划分——每个 workgroup 按消费者需求排序的内核内队列在 mover（搬运）与 builder（计算）两种角色间动态切换，无需调度器、逐调用代价模型或调好的 split；在 8×MI350X/MI355X 上把 Megatron-LM 的 Llama 3 TP+SP 端到端训练加速 1.10–1.28×（BF16，对比 ROCm Transformer Engine）、MoE 代理训练加速 1.29–1.83×（对比 DeepEP）。

> **TL;DR:** KOSMOS (AMD) fuses every TP+SP collective with its GEMM — and an entire MoE layer from router to fold, forward and backward — into one persistent kernel per direction, where each workgroup picks its role (mover vs. builder) from in-kernel queue readiness instead of a scheduler or a per-call cost model. On 8-GPU MI350X/MI355X nodes running Megatron-LM, Llama 3 8B–405B training is 1.10–1.28× faster than ROCm Transformer Engine in BF16 (8B on MI355X: 478→612 TFLOPS/GPU) and 1.10–1.25× in MXFP8, while Qwen3-235B and DeepSeek-V3 MoE proxies are 1.29–1.83× faster than DeepEP (Qwen3-235B balanced, MI355X BF16: 387→673 TFLOPS/GPU). Read it for the kernel design and the unusually honest ablations; note that the self-scheduling itself is worth only ~2–9% over a well-chosen fixed crew (Table 2), with most of the headline gain coming from fusion and from queue *ordering* (a naive AG order costs 1.17–1.40×, Table 1).

## 核心贡献

- **自调度常驻内核（无调度器、无逐调用代价模型）**：每个 CU 常驻一个 workgroup，workgroup 只通过内核内的生产者/消费者队列与就绪标志协作；每个 collective 的队列顺序围绕"数据何时就绪"共同设计，workgroup 据此在 mover 与 builder 之间换角色。
- **刻画了融合通信内核中空闲时间落在哪里，并给出一条策略规则**：通信需求单调的 collective（TP+SP 的 AG/RS、MoE dispatch）用**单向**切换；需求与计算交错的 collective（MoE combine）用**双向**切换——论文实测双向切换在 TP+SP 上反而更慢（AG+NN 0.951×），这是对"越动态越好"直觉的反例。
- **训练侧完整覆盖**：TP+SP 的每个 collective 在前后向、BF16 与 MXFP8 下都有对应的融合常驻内核（5 个内核覆盖 6 个 GEMM + 5–6 个 collective）；MoE 则首次做到"每个方向一次 launch 覆盖 router → dispatch → GEMM1 → combine → fold"以及完整反向（含 router 梯度与 wgrad），并在 Megatron-LM 中端到端验证。
- **以 bytes/CU/s 为目标的通信实现**：8-wave mover 用一半 CU 就饱和 XGMI；RS 采用"一次性 pull"（每个 partial 只过一次 fabric、FP32 累加、只舍入一次），比 RCCL ring 的归约误差低 2.21×，且逐次运行 bitwise 可复现。
- **量化了"把 CU 借给通信"的真实代价**：在功耗封顶的 MI350X 上，GEMM 在 224 CU（87.5%）时仍保有 93% 吞吐，192 CU 时保有 88%——这是整套设计的经济学前提。

## Key Contributions

- **Self-scheduling persistent kernels.** One workgroup per CU, resident for the kernel's lifetime, coordinating only through in-kernel queues and readiness flags — no host round trip, no separate scheduling kernel, no scheduler workgroup (which would cost a CU). Producer and consumer queue orders are co-designed per collective around data readiness.
- **A characterization of idle time plus a policy rule.** They locate the idle windows in fixed-split fused kernels (AG: gather finishes before compute; RS: two windows, at start and tail; A2A combine: windows scattered throughout) and derive the rule *one-way switching for monotonic demand, two-way for interleaved demand*. This rule is empirically load-bearing: two-way switching is measurably *worse* for AG/RS (0.918–0.965×).
- **Complete kernel coverage for training**, not inference: all TP+SP collectives forward and backward in BF16 and MXFP8 (including the extra column-wise quantized `dY` gather MXFP8 needs), and the first role-switching MoE layer with a single launch per direction including router gradients and weight gradients.
- **Communications optimized for bytes/CU/s** on fully connected meshes, plus the measurement that CUs lent to communication cost the GEMM far less than their CU share on power-capped GPUs.
- **A numerics side benefit**: the RS "one-shot pull" is both more accurate than RCCL's ring (2.21× lower reduction error, 1.71–1.73× lower end-to-end error vs. FP64) and bitwise reproducible across runs.

## Methodology

**Execution model.** KOSMOS implements each fused operation as one persistent kernel launching one workgroup (WG) per CU; each WG is either a *mover* (transports data to/from peers) or a *builder* (computes GEMM tiles). WGs coordinate only via shared queues: a WG claims a tile by atomically taking the next entry, then computes it. Two ordering rules make this deadlock-free without a scheduler: (i) **upstream before downstream** — every tile of an earlier step is claimed before any tile of a later step, so a waiting tile only depends on work already claimed by a resident WG; (ii) **produce in the order consumers need** — e.g. AG queues hold local tiles first then peer tiles in gather order; RS builders produce *band-major* (band *b* of every rank's chunk before band *b+1*) so every rank's reducers can start after one step.

**Granularity.** Work is tracked in bands, chunks, tiles, steps: a tile row spans 256 rows; a rank's chunk is the `tokens/TP` output rows it owns after RS; a band is a group of tile rows within a chunk; each step produces every chunk's next band. Every output tile has exactly one completion point, which is what makes readiness signaling well-defined (and what later disqualifies stream-K).

**Role switching.** One-way: a WG takes the other role permanently once its own work is done (AG movers become builders; GEMM+RS builders join the reduction when the GEMM queue empties). Two-way: a WG with no work in its current role works in the other and returns when its own work appears (MoE combine: push any ready combine unit, build tiles otherwise). A two-way WG never blocks on a push, so waits are always satisfiable.

**TP+SP kernels** (5 per precision, Fig. 5): fused AG+TN (forward col-parallel), fused TN+RS (forward row-parallel), fused AG+NN (backward dgrad; MXFP8 also fuses the NT wgrad and gathers two quantized copies of `dY`), plus two "bulk" kernels where the collective has no data dependency on the adjacent GEMM (AG of X runs beside col-parallel dgrad; RS of dX runs beside col-parallel wgrad), balanced by a GB/s-per-CU vs. TFLOPS-per-CU heuristic.

**MoE megakernel.** Forward, one launch: (1) *route* — router logits in FP32 tiles, last tile to finish a row block does score/top-k/dispatch weights/aux loss, never leaving the device; (2) *plan* — stable counting sort into per-expert send lists (keeps dW bitwise reproducible), only per-expert counts cross ranks, and this wait is the forward's only cross-rank barrier; (3) *dispatch ∥ GEMM1* — 16–96 movers push token rows directly into their final expert-grouped position, destination-minor so consecutive movers hit different GPUs, SwiGLU fused into the GEMM1 epilogue; (4) *GEMM2 ∥ combine* — up to 128 dual-role movers push each (expert, destination) unit as its row blocks finish and claim GEMM tiles between pushes; (5) *fold* — k rows weighted by FP32 router weights. Backward mirrors this with dgrad2/wgrad2 interleaved, a combine∥wgrad1 stage, and an FP32 fold into dX; the routing-weight gradient `dw_j = ⟨dY, Y_j⟩` is computed by the movers after their pushes.

**Numerics.** RS uses a NaN-sentinel staging buffer so "data landing" *is* the arrival signal (a BF16 *signal* NaN distinguishes it from FMA-produced arithmetic NaN); partials are summed in FP32 in fixed order and rounded once. Everything is bitwise run-to-run deterministic because tile claiming changes *which* WG computes a tile, never the reduction or fold order.

**Implementation.** C++20 + HIP, 8 waves and 512 threads per WG, single occupancy, 256×256 GEMM tiles via HipKittens (router GEMM uses 64×128). Shape-dependent parameters (mover crew, transport params, split-K, XCD bucketing) are set by closed-form rules on the first iteration (Table B.3). Under a CU cap (e.g. coexisting with inter-node FSDP) kernels shrink builder counts and keep the whole grid resident.

## Results & Highlights

- **TP+SP end-to-end, Llama 3 8B, TP 8, seq 8192, GBS 64, Megatron-LM**: 1.24–1.28× over ROCm Transformer Engine in BF16 on MI355X (478 → 612 TFLOPS/GPU at best MBS) and 1.20–1.27× on MI350X (474 → 596); MXFP8 1.22–1.24× (597 → 730 TFLOPS/GPU). Faster for every model size, precision and GPU (Table 3, C.1).
- **The 405B case is the weak one**: MI350X BF16 only 1.10–1.16× (570 → 645 TFLOPS/GPU, full recompute, 24 of 126 layers); MI355X 1.17–1.22× (624 → 757). Gains *shrink* with model size — the opposite direction from where the pain is — and 405B MXFP8 at MBS 8 OOMs on MI355X (Table C.1), so that cell is missing rather than slow.
- **TP+SP microbenchmarks**: KOSMOS is faster in all **480** shape × kernel × precision × GPU combinations; geometric-mean 1.38× (MI350X BF16) and 1.41× (MI355X BF16), 1.36×/1.39× in MXFP8, against RCCL + hipBLASLt/HipKittens run serially without overlap. Against KOSMOS's *own* kernels run unfused the geomean is 1.39×/1.42× BF16 — i.e. the gain is from overlap, not from having better components.
- **MoE layer microbenchmarks**: 1.28–3.39× over RCCL A2A + TE grouped GEMM across all 144 cases (9 models × fwd/bwd × BF16/MXFP8 × balanced/skewed). Gains track arithmetic intensity: Qwen3-30B (narrow experts, top-8) reaches 3.39×, Llama 4 (wide experts, top-1) only 1.28–1.92×. Running the same components as separate launches costs 1.25–1.51× (MXFP8 forward geomean 1.39×), which isolates the fusion contribution.
- **MoE proxy training, EP 8**: Qwen3-235B balanced on MI355X is 1.74× in BF16 (387 → 673 TFLOPS/GPU) and 1.83× in MXFP8 (400 → 731); **skewed routing collapses the win to 1.29× (BF16) / 1.54× (MXFP8)**. DeepSeek-V3 is 1.31–1.41× across all four cells.
- **Role-policy ablation (Table 2, all policies implemented inside KOSMOS)**: one-way switching is 1.032× on AG+TN (MI350X), never more than 1% slower than the best fixed split of 56 WGs and up to 1.15× faster; two-way switching beats a fixed split in **all 72** MoE cells, geomean 1.04× forward and 1.06×/1.08× backward (MI350X/MI355X), up to 1.14×/1.16×, and beats a dedicated combine crew in 63 of 72. A Weave-style planned split degrades badly under skew (0.752× on MI355X MoE backward).
- **Mechanism ablation (Table 1, one change at a time)**: naive AG queue ordering costs 1.17–1.40×; row-major instead of band-major RS costs 1.41–1.45×; destination-major A2A push lists cost 1.12–2.03×; 4-wave movers cost 1.23–1.96×. Fusing the router or shared expert is worth only 1.01–1.02× and 1.02–1.04× — small but consistent.
- **Numerics**: RS reduction error 1.67e-3 vs. RCCL's 3.68e-3 on 70B-Down-MBS8 (**2.21× lower**), 1.71–1.73× lower end-to-end error against an FP64 GEMM, and bitwise run-to-run deterministic.
- **Why lending CUs to communication is cheap**: on MI350X the GEMM keeps 93% of full-GPU throughput at 224 CUs and 88% at 192 CUs (Llama 3 70B FC1, MBS 4/8); both GPUs sit at their 1000 W / 1400 W caps until 128 CUs and peak SCLK around 96 CUs.

## Authors & Credibility

All four authors are at **AMD (Advanced Micro Devices Inc., Austin, TX)**, with Alexander Magro as corresponding author — this is a vendor systems paper, and every baseline is an in-house or in-house-adjacent stack: ROCm Transformer Engine 2.18, RCCL 2.30.4, the ROCm port of DeepEP, ROCm Megatron-LM (commit `b0695a0`), and HipKittens (an AMD-authored MLSys 2026 DSL). That is not disqualifying — the group is clearly the one that knows this hardware — but it does mean the "baseline" is whatever AMD ships, not an independent best effort.

Methodological hygiene is above average for a kernel paper: pinned software versions, 10 discarded warm-up iterations + 25 timed iterations, three runs with shuffled variant order, median of the *slowest rank*, and a reported median run-to-run spread of 0.4% (max 2.0%). Outputs are validated against RCCL + hipBLASLt at TE tolerances, re-poisoned every iteration and checked twice to catch races. The appendix publishes per-shape tables for all 480 microbenchmark cells plus every heuristic rule in closed form (Table B.3) — more transparency than most overlap papers offer.

Weaknesses on credibility: it is a preprint (arXiv, Nov 2026) with no peer review and, as far as the text shows, **no released artifact** — no repository, no harness, no kernel source, despite the entire contribution being kernel code. The competitor comparison is against re-implemented *policies* (UniEP, Weave) inside KOSMOS rather than against those systems, which is defensible (they are NVIDIA-only) but leaves implementation quality uncontrolled.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty — real but narrower than the framing.** Role-switching persistent kernels are not new: FlashMoE and MPK already use persistent kernels with scheduler warps, Weave plans a steal budget from a prologue cost model, mKernel adapts the split with an in-kernel controller, and UniEP tunes a split per shape. KOSMOS's actual delta is (a) removing the scheduler entirely in favor of consumer-ordered queues, (b) *two-way* switching as a distinct policy for interleaved demand, and (c) coverage — router-to-fold MoE including weight gradients, and all TP+SP collectives in both precisions. The coverage is a genuine first; the mechanism is an incremental but well-motivated step.

**The headline number and the mechanism are not the same thing.** Table 2 is the most informative table in the paper and it undercuts the abstract: switching over a *fixed* crew of 56 WGs yields 1.032× on AG+TN and 1.04–1.08× geomean on MoE. Meanwhile Table 1 shows a naive AG queue order costs 1.17–1.40× and row-major RS costs 1.41–1.45×. So the 1.38× microbenchmark win is dominated by fusion plus **data ordering** (band-major production, local-first AG queues, destination-minor push lists), not by self-scheduling. To their credit the authors say so explicitly ("the order of production is as important as the dynamic role switching it enables"), and Table 1's isolation of ordering is exactly the right experiment — but a reader skimming the abstract will over-attribute the win to "self-scheduling."

**"No tuned split" is not quite true.** The paper claims no per-call cost model and no tuned split, but Table B.3 contains roughly ten closed-form rules (movers per peer as a function of gathered width *N* with different thresholds for fprop/dgrad/bulk, reducers per peer as a function of local *K* and wave count, dispatch crew from expert width *I*, split-K factors, XCD bucketing) **fitted at TP 8 and EP 8**. That is a shape heuristic rather than a per-call model, which is a fair distinction, but those rules were fitted on the same 24 Llama 3 shapes and 9 MoE models used for evaluation — there is no held-out shape set, no TP=4/16 or EP=32 evaluation, and no sensitivity analysis for what happens when the thresholds are crossed. The claim "within 1% of, and up to 15% faster than, the best single fixed split" is therefore measured in-distribution.

**Baselines are single-vendor and the strongest AMD competitor is absent.** End-to-end numbers compare only against ROCm TE and ROCm DeepEP. AMD's own Primus-Turbo MegaMoE appears in the related-work table (B.1) but is never measured, even though it is the production path for DeepSeek-V4-Flash on MI355X and the most obvious thing a practitioner would compare against. Against TE the comparison is also somewhat soft: TE's TP+SP path on ROCm is a stream-level overlap, which the paper itself argues is structurally handicapped — beating it by 1.2× says less than beating a fused kernel would.

**Scale is proxy-scale, and the trend is unfavourable.** Everything is single-node, 8 GPUs. "End-to-end" means: 405B restricted to 24 of 126 layers with full recompute, Qwen3-235B proxy at 24 layers, and a DeepSeek-V3 proxy with no dense layers and nine MoE layers. The 405B result (1.10–1.16× on MI350X) is the honest signal here: the speedup decays as the GEMM gets big relative to the collective, which is precisely the regime of real frontier training. The paper does not discuss this decay or extrapolate it.

**No convergence evidence.** Throughput is measured, numerics are validated per kernel against RCCL + hipBLASLt at TE tolerances, and MXFP8 accuracy is cited from Mishra et al. — but there is no loss curve, no multi-hundred-step parity check between KOSMOS-MXFP8 and BF16. For a paper whose selling point is "training, not inference," that is the most conspicuous missing experiment, especially given the extra column-wise `dY` gather that MXFP8 requires and the "re-quantizing one copy compounds rounding error" argument the paper itself raises.

**Skew is bounded by the capacity factor, and the bounded regime is where gains are weakest.** "A greater skew falls back to the default Megatron MoE layer" — so the skewed results are the skew that fits the shared buffers (DeepSeek-V3 up to ~1.3× the largest peer, Qwen up to 4.2× in some iterations). Within that, gains fall from 1.70× to 1.31× (Qwen3-235B BF16 balanced → skewed) and 1.82× to 1.57× (MXFP8). The advertised 1.29–1.83× range is thus mostly the balanced case, and the failure mode under real skew is a fallback, not a graceful degradation.

**The NaN-sentinel trick is clever and slightly alarming.** Using a BF16 *signal* NaN written into the staging buffer as the arrival marker (distinguished from FMA-generated arithmetic NaN) removes a counter and a fence, and the reasoning is sound. But it makes correctness depend on activations never legitimately containing that exact sentinel pattern — a subtle coupling that a reader worried about silent numerical misbehavior should note.

**What genuinely deserves praise.** The RS accuracy result is better than the performance result in novelty terms: 2.21× lower reduction error than RCCL's ring, 1.71–1.73× lower end-to-end error against FP64, *and* bitwise reproducibility, for free. The power/CU-headroom measurement (93% of GEMM throughput at 87.5% of CUs) is a clean, reusable piece of systems knowledge. The deadlock-avoidance argument (upstream-before-downstream, never blocking on a push) is stated precisely enough to be checked. And publishing all 480 microbenchmark cells plus every heuristic constant is the kind of transparency that lets a reader verify the claims rather than trust them.

## Strengths

- **Complete training-side coverage** — every TP+SP collective in both directions and both precisions, plus a single-launch MoE layer with router and weight gradients. Prior work is mostly inference or forward-only.
- **Mechanistic ablations that isolate the right things**: Table 1 changes one mechanism at a time (queue order, production order, push order, wave count, fusion), and the separate-vs-fused and unfused-vs-fused comparisons cleanly separate "better components" from "better overlap."
- **Unusually honest role-policy comparison** — UniEP- and Weave-style policies are reimplemented inside KOSMOS so the comparison is apples-to-apples at the policy level, and the paper reports the cells where its own policy is *worse* (two-way switching on AG+NN: 0.951×).
- **A free numerics win**: one-shot-pull RS gives lower reduction error than RCCL's ring and bitwise determinism, which matters for debugging loss spikes and for reproducibility claims in large runs.
- **Solid measurement discipline**: slowest-rank medians, shuffled variant order, pinned toolchain versions, reported run-to-run spread.
- **Practical coexistence story**: CU-capped kernels stay fully resident, so KOSMOS can share a GPU with inter-node FSDP instead of demanding the whole device.

## Limitations

- **Single node only.** Persistent kernels require XGMI with hipIPC memory; there is no RDMA path and no hierarchical mover design, so the results stop at 8 GPUs. The paper is explicit that multinode is future work, but that is also where EP actually lives at scale.
- **One completion point per output tile is a hard structural constraint** — it rules out stream-K and any split-K scheme where multiple WGs cooperate on one tile, which is a real cost for small or irregular shapes.
- **Push-based dispatch needs more memory than pull**; the paper notes pull may be required at larger EP sizes, i.e. the current design may not extend to EP 32/64.
- **Heuristics fitted in-distribution** at TP 8 / EP 8, evaluated on the same shape families, with no held-out shapes and no sensitivity analysis around the thresholds.
- **Proxy-scale end-to-end**: 24-of-126 layers for 405B, 24-layer Qwen3-235B, dense-free 9-layer DeepSeek-V3. No real-scale run, no convergence curves, no loss parity for MXFP8.
- **No released code or artifact**, which makes the kernel-level claims unverifiable independently.
- **The power-headroom premise (R2) is hardware-specific** — MI350X/MI355X at fixed 1000 W/1400 W caps with all 256 CUs active. On a GPU where GEMM throughput scales with CU count, lending CUs to communication would cost proportionally more, and the paper's claim that findings "generalize to other systems" is asserted rather than tested.

## Relevance

直接命中 **Compute Infrastructure for Large-Scale ML**（算子与内核、集合通信与互联）——这是 TP+SP/EP 通信-计算重叠这一方向目前最完整的一份工程实现与测量。同时对 **Reliability under Extreme Performance Optimization** 有实质价值：它给出了一条"低精度训练下归约误差更低且 bitwise 可复现"的 RS 实现（比 RCCL ring 低 2.21×），这类确定性对复现实验、定位 loss spike 和排查静默数值偏差都有直接用处。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Holding the queue ordering fixed and reverting to a tuned 56-WG crew, how much of the 1.38× microbenchmark win survives? Table 2 implies only ~3%, but that is never measured directly as a single ablation.
2. Do the Table B.3 heuristics hold at TP 4/16 and EP 32/64, or on shapes outside the 24 Llama 3 / 9 MoE families? What is the penalty for crossing a threshold (e.g. gathered width 3584)?
3. At what model/collective ratio does the speedup decay to zero? The 405B trend (1.10–1.16× vs. 1.20–1.28× for 8B) suggests a crossover worth characterizing.
4. Does MXFP8 training with the dual-quantized `dY` gather converge to the same loss as BF16 over 10k+ steps, and does the 2.21× lower reduction error translate into measurably more stable long runs?
5. How does the NaN-sentinel arrival protocol behave when activations legitimately contain NaNs or Infs — e.g. during a loss spike, exactly when you most want the kernel not to hang?
6. What does the multinode extension look like — hierarchical movers over RDMA, and does the upstream-before-downstream deadlock argument still hold when a mover's peer set spans nodes?
7. Under skew beyond the capacity factor (where KOSMOS falls back to Megatron's layer), could the readiness-driven design degrade gracefully instead of falling back?
