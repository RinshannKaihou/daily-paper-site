> **一句话总结：** 作者把 MoE 负载均衡重构成一个反馈控制回路——每个专家负载相对公平份额的偏差是控制误差、加到路由分数上的 per-expert bias 是控制输入——由此证明辅助均衡损失等价于比例（P）控制、ALF-LB 等价于对误差符号作用的继电（relay）积分控制，并论证只有积分项既能消除持续偏差又能把逐步抖动平均掉；据此采用单增益的纯积分控制器 PID (I)，在 OLMoE 从零预训练（E=16/32/64，第 16,500 步 Books→GitHub 硬切换）中把 post-shift MaxVio 从 ALF-LB 的 1.20 降到 0.47（E=64，7→1），困惑度持平在 3.21，但持续预训练阶段更紧的均衡以更大的遗忘为代价（PPL-Annealing 7.39 vs Vanilla 7.23）。

> **TL;DR:** Recasts MoE load balancing as a discrete-time feedback loop (error = per-expert deviation from the fair share r = TK/E, control input = a bias added to router scores at selection time) and shows the two dominant methods are members of one PID family: the Switch-Transformer auxiliary loss is a proportional controller whose steady-state error scales as 1/α but never vanishes, and ALF-LB is an integral controller acting only on the *sign* of the error. Because the router is memoryless and monotone with a one-step delay, only the integral term both removes persistent imbalance and averages out step-to-step batch noise, so they deploy PID (I), a pure linear integrator on the *normalized* error with one gain. On OLMoE pretraining over a domain-extracted SlimPajama subset (7 domains, 33k steps, instant switch to GitHub at step 16,500, 6 seeds), PID (I) reaches post-shift MaxVio 0.47±0.01 vs 1.20±0.20 for ALF-LB, 1.50 for Aux-loss and 6.10 for no balancing at E=64/Top-8, with identical validation PPL (3.21 vs 3.21/3.21/3.23). On continual pretraining of OLMoE-1B-7B onto codeparrot it gets postMV 0.90 vs 1.01 (ALF-LB) and 5.83 (vanilla), but forgets more (PPL-Annealing 7.39 vs 7.23, MMLU 0.46 vs 0.50, MBPP 0.14 vs 0.18). Worth reading for anyone debugging MoE routing collapse or curriculum/CPT mixture switches; the control framing and the spectral/rotating-overload diagnostics are the durable parts, while the algorithm itself is a small delta over ALF-LB's linear variant.

## 核心贡献

- **把负载均衡形式化为反馈控制回路**：误差取每个专家负载相对公平份额 $r=TK/E$ 的归一化偏差 $e_i(t)=(r-L_i(t))/r$（有界于 $[1-E/K,\,1]$，与 batch size 无关），控制输入取选择时加到路由分数上的 per-expert bias，路由器则是"无记忆 + 单调 + 一步延迟"的静态映射。这一框架把原本各自为政的一堆 trick 放进同一个可分析的闭环里。
- **统一了两种主流方法**：通过链式法则推导出辅助均衡损失的梯度只依赖当前步误差 $e_k(t)-\bar e_j(t)$（$\bar e_j$ 是 softmax 加权平均误差），因此它是**比例控制**，其稳态误差随 $1/\alpha$ 缩小但永不归零；ALF-LB 的 $u_i \leftarrow u_i + \eta\,\mathrm{sign}(e_i)$ 展开后正是 $K_I=\eta$、作用于误差符号的**继电积分控制**，符号运算丢掉了误差幅值，把"消除稳态误差"弱化成"正负出现次数相等"。
- **给出"为什么只要 I 项"的论证**：持续误差（如死专家 $e_i\equiv 1$）下，I 项产生每步增长 $K_I$ 的斜坡，终将越过任何 softmax 分数差；P 项只给固定偏移 $K_P$，误差归零时偏置也归零、原失衡复发；D 项对常数误差输出为零。对逐步抖动（最快情形 $e_i(t)=(-1)^t$），I 项在 0 与 $K_I$ 之间摆动而不改变符号（求和自相抵消），P/D 项则原样复制或放大抖动。
- **提出 PID (I)：单增益、无梯度的 drop-in 均衡器**，不修改模型权重也不修改训练目标，可直接插到已预训练模型上；并在附录给出跨专家数的增益迁移经验规则（E 每翻倍，最优 $K_I$ 左移一个网格步，约 3 倍：1e-3@E=16、3e-4@E=32、1e-4@E=64）。
- **在非平稳数据上做系统评测**：预训练（从零、E∈{16,32,64}、三种 pre-shift 混合、训练中途单次硬切换）与持续预训练（OLMoE-1B-7B → codeparrot，cold/warm 两种起点）两套设置，并用 MaxVio、DER、UTR 三个指标互补（MaxVio 只看最忙专家，可被"轮流超载"掩盖）。
- **额外给出两个有价值的诊断**：误差功率谱（Vanilla 有 25.9% 误差方差落在 ≤100 步的慢分量，PID (I) 降到 13.0%，白噪声基线 1.9%）与"持续 vs 轮转超载"比值（Vanilla >0.93、Aux-loss >0.65，而 ALF-LB/PID 除两层外均 <0.25），以及路由互信息探针量化"均衡 vs 专家特化"的取舍位置。

## Key Contributions

- **A control-theoretic formulation of MoE load balancing.** Error = normalized per-expert deviation from the fair share; control input = a per-expert bias added to router scores at selection time (gate weights stay unbiased, bias receives no gradient); plant = a memoryless, monotone router with a one-step delay. This makes the loop analyzable without modeling the router.
- **A unified reading of two dominant methods.** Aux-loss is shown to be a *proportional* mechanism: the gradient of $\alpha E\sum_i f_iP_i$ w.r.t. a logit reduces to $(\alpha/T)s_{kj}(e_k-\bar e_j)$, i.e. a token-dependent gain on the current error with no memory; at a stationary point a persistent task-gradient disturbance is offset only by a nonzero steady-state error scaling as $1/\alpha$. ALF-LB unrolls to $u_i(t)=\eta\sum_\tau \mathrm{sign}(e_i(\tau))$, exactly PID with $K_P=K_D=0$, $K_I=\eta$ on the sign of the error.
- **A principled argument for the integral-only member.** Persistent errors are removed only by accumulated memory (a ramp, not an offset); step-to-step fluctuations that average to zero cancel inside the integrator's sum but are copied or amplified by P and D. Hence PID (I) with a single gain $K_I$.
- **A drop-in balancer for pretrained checkpoints**, plus an empirical gain-transfer rule across expert counts and a demonstration that cold-start attachment to a checkpoint (router never saw the balancer) reaches postMV close to the warm-start setting.
- **An evaluation protocol for non-stationary training**: three balancing metrics (MaxVio, DER, UTR), pre/post-shift windows of 2,000 steps, six seeds in pretraining, and a specialization probe based on routing mutual information with a bias-removed counterfactual.

## Methodology

**The loop.** For $T$ tokens each activating $K$ of $E$ experts, the fair share is $r=TK/E$ and the error is $e_i(t)=(r-L_i(t))/r$. The router selects $T_j(t)=\mathrm{TopK}_i\big(s_{ij}(t)+u_i(t-1)\big)$ with $s_{ij}$ the softmax score; the bias shifts *selection only*, while the gate weight used for the output remains the unbiased $s_{ij}$. Bias is shared across all tokens in an optimizer step and is updated only by the balancer.

**PID-Balancer.** $u_i(t)=K_P e_i(t)+K_I\sum_{\tau\le t}e_i(\tau)+K_D(e_i(t)-e_i(t-1))$, one instance per expert per MoE layer, all layers sharing one gain triple. Members are named by retained terms; PID (I) sets $K_P=K_D=0$. The incremental form $\Delta u_i=K_I e_i+K_P\Delta e_i+K_D\Delta^2 e_i$ is used in the appendix to interpret each term as a differentiator of the error.

**Why I only (Section 3.3).** Two extreme cases carry the argument. A dead expert ($e_i\equiv 1$): I gives a ramp growing $K_I$ per step that eventually exceeds any biased-softmax score gap; P gives a constant offset $K_P$ that only rescues the expert if it already exceeds the gap, and cannot remove imbalance because a zero error implies a zero bias; D gives nothing. An alternating error $e_i(t)=(-1)^t$: the integral sum cancels within two steps so the bias only oscillates in $[0,K_I]$ without changing sign, while P swings in $[-K_P,K_P]$ and D in $[-2K_D,2K_D]$. Since the bias acts one step late, reacting to the fast component is wasted effort by construction.

**Pretraining setup.** Domain-extracted SlimPajama-627B subset, 7 domains kept unmixed (ArXiv, Books, C4, CommonCrawl, GitHub, StackExchange, Wikipedia). OLMoE backbone at three scales with total/active parameters fixed (2.25B/0.49B), $E\in\{16,32,64\}$ with Top-2/4/8 so that $K/E=1/8$ and the MaxVio saturation ceiling is 7.0 at every scale. 33,000 steps from scratch, 32,768 tokens per batch (~1.08B tokens per run), single instantaneous switch at step 16,500 to GitHub alone; three pre-shift mixtures (1→1 Books, 6→1 all-but-GitHub, 7→1 all seven). Six seeds; Vanilla uses one seed as a reference. Baselines: no balancing, Switch-style Aux-loss ($\alpha=10^{-2}$), ALF-LB ($\eta=10^{-3}$), all coefficients swept over $\{10^{-5},\dots,10^{-1}\}$.

**Continual pretraining setup.** Start from released OLMoE-1B-7B-0125 (pretrained on OLMoE-mix-0924, annealed on Dolmino). Cold start: 16,500 steps directly on codeparrot-clean. Warm start: 16,500 steps on the annealing corpus first (balancer reaches steady state), then 16,500 steps on codeparrot. Two held-out sets (20M tokens each) separate adaptation (PPL-CPT) from forgetting (PPL-Annealing); five downstream benchmarks via lm-evaluation-harness v0.4.12 (MMLU/HellaSwag/ARC-C 5-shot, HumanEval 0-shot, MBPP 3-shot).

**Diagnostics.** Error power spectrum (Welch, Hann 256, half overlap) over the post-shift window; bias-increment spectrum; sign-flip rate of increments; RMS|u| (how hard the balancer pushes); Active fraction (experts receiving ≥1 token in a step) to separate intermittent from persistent exclusion; window-level/per-step MaxVio ratio to separate rotating from persistent overload; routing mutual information $I_l=H(p(e|l))-\sum_d p(d)H(p(e|l,d))$ over 7 domains × 256 instances × 4096 tokens, with a bias-removed forward pass as counterfactual.

## Results & Highlights

- **Pretraining, hardest cell (E=64, Top-8, 7→1, post-shift window, 6 seeds):** PID (I) reaches MaxVio **0.47±0.01** vs **1.20±0.20** for ALF-LB, **1.50±0.07** for Aux-loss and **6.10** for Vanilla — a 2.6× tighter post-shift bound than ALF-LB and 13× tighter than no balancing — while validation perplexity is **3.21±0.02** vs 3.22/3.21/3.23, i.e. indistinguishable. Vanilla leaves **23.54% dead / 56.05% under-trained** experts in that cell; both bias-based methods report **0.00% / 0.00%**.
- **The margin over ALF-LB grows with expert count**, which is the paper's scaling argument: on the 1→1 scenario post-shift MaxVio is 0.30 vs 0.33 at E=16 (−9%), 0.35 vs 0.48 at E=32 (−27%), 0.40 vs 0.80 at E=64 (−50%); on 6→1 it is 0.30 vs 0.38, 0.36 vs 0.65, 0.47 vs 0.92.
- **Continual pretraining, cold start (OLMoE-1B-7B → codeparrot):** postMV **0.90** (PID) vs 1.01 (ALF-LB), 4.43 (Aux-loss), 5.83 (Vanilla); warm start **0.87** vs 1.03/4.51/5.76 with preMV 0.66 vs 0.72. PPL-CPT is **2.19 for all four methods** in the cold setting (2.24/2.24/2.24/2.23 warm) and all beat the 2.40 base checkpoint — but the price shows up as forgetting: PPL-Annealing **7.39** vs 7.23 (Vanilla) and 7.21 (Aux-loss), MMLU **0.46** vs 0.50, MBPP **0.14** vs 0.18.
- **Isolated-term ablation (E=64, Top-8, 1→1) kills the P and D terms empirically:** PID (I) postMV **0.40**; the best proportional-only setting ($K_P=10^{-2}$) gives **2.86** (~7× worse) with DER still 1.2%, and it is non-monotone — 5.10 at $10^{-3}$ and 5.33 at $10^{-1}$, because a memoryless term over-corrects and reverses on alternate steps (Flip 84.5%→97.1%). Derivative-only never helps: best 5.47 vs 6.08 for Vanilla with DER stuck at 16.1%, and at $K_D=10^{-2}$ the run **collapses before the shift** (PreMV 6.98 against a ceiling of 7.0, DER 37.3%). Adding either term to the integrator only hurts: postMV 0.40 → 0.46 → 0.62 → 1.84 for PI, 0.52 → 1.47 for DI.
- **Spectral evidence that the integrator acts on the right frequency band:** Vanilla puts **25.9%** of its error variance in components slower than 100 steps ($f\le0.01$), PID (I) **13.0%**, white noise through the same estimator **1.9%**; PID (I) also adds a narrow peak at the alternation frequency $f=0.5$ (density 10.8 vs 0.63) — the measured form of the one-step-delay oscillation predicted in Section 3.3. Increment sign-flip rate is **37.6%** for PID (I) vs 67.7%/74.1% for P/D at small gain, matching the analytic white-noise values 0.667/0.732.
- **Balance and specialization are separable by *where* the balancer acts:** on the CPT domain PID's probe MaxVio is **0.11** vs 0.46 (ALF-LB) and 5.79 (Vanilla), yet routing mutual information is **0.074 for both bias methods** (base model 0.141) — equal specialization cost. Aux-loss instead pays in the weights: MI drops to 0.110 while probe MaxVio stays at 4.29. Zeroing the bias at inference restores MaxVio to 4.14 and *lowers* perplexity by 0.9–2.6%, showing the deployed gain is bought by overriding the gate.
- **Conversion to a latency proxy:** since $\mathrm{MRI}=(\mathrm{MaxVio}+1)K/E$, the E=64 7→1 post-shift numbers become busiest-expert token shares of **0.89 / 0.31 / 0.28 / 0.18** (Vanilla / Aux / ALF-LB / PID) against a balanced floor of 0.125.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty is in the framing, not the algorithm — and the paper is mostly honest about this.** PID (I) is $u_i \leftarrow u_i + K_I e_i$ on the *normalized* error, which is the linear (non-sign) variant of ALF-LB that Wang et al. (2024) already tried and reported as "better balance at slightly higher perplexity" at one model scale on a fixed mixture. The paper cites this explicitly (Section 3.2) and its real deltas are (i) the normalized, batch-size-independent error, (ii) the demonstration that the linear rule is the *right* one under distribution shift and at 16–64 experts, and (iii) the control-theoretic explanation of why. That is a legitimate contribution, but the abstract's "we rethink / we define a family / we adopt" phrasing reads as a larger algorithmic step than the 3-line update it delivers.

**The control analysis is a local, qualitative argument presented with more formal machinery than it strictly supports.** There is no transfer function, no stability proof, no bound on convergence rate, and no treatment of the router's *changing* scores — the analysis holds $s_{ij}(t)$ fixed within a step and then reasons about a scalar loop per expert, while in reality all $E$ biases interact through a top-K argmax and the scores drift under the LM gradient. The claim that "the integral term is the only one that both removes persistent imbalance and averages out fluctuation" is a textbook fact about PID applied to a plant the authors have *assumed* to be memoryless and monotone. The D term's catastrophic failure (DER 37.3% at $K_D=10^{-2}$, collapse before the shift) is largely an artifact of differentiating a signal that is dominated by batch-composition noise — the paper says as much in D.4, but it still frames this as evidence about the D term rather than about the error definition.

**The headline "lowest MaxVio in every setting" is partly a hyperparameter artifact.** In Table 2 Aux-loss runs at $\alpha=0.01$ (the OLMoE default), where it looks weak (postMV 4.43 cold). But Table 10 shows that at $\alpha=0.8$ Aux-loss reaches postMV **0.60**, i.e. *better* than PID's 0.90, and at $\alpha=4$ it reaches **0.23** — far better. The cost is real (PPL-CPT 2.27/2.29 vs 2.19, PPL-Annealing 7.77/8.38 vs 7.39, MMLU 0.42/0.41 vs 0.46), so PID does sit on a better balance–quality frontier; but the main table's four-method comparison is not a matched-balance comparison, and a reader skimming Table 2 would overestimate the gap. The paper should have reported the frontier explicitly in the main text rather than in C.5.

**The forgetting cost is the most practically important result and it is under-weighted.** In CPT the two bias-based methods are 5–6× better balanced yet consistently worse on the prior domain (PPL-Annealing 7.39 vs 7.23 cold, 6.28 vs 6.07 warm) and on MMLU (0.46 vs 0.50, a 4-point drop) and MBPP (0.14 vs 0.18). The abstract's "at comparable perplexity" is true only for PPL on the *new* corpus; the paper's own Table 2 shows a measurable regression on the old one, and Section 4.2 attributes it to force-reassigned tokens overwriting prior-domain experts. That hypothesis is plausible but untested — no per-expert drift measurement, no experiment that freezes prior-domain experts, no attempt to bound the damage (e.g. by decaying $K_I$ or gating the bias). For a paper whose selling point is "drop-in for pretrained models", the drop-in's side effect deserves more than one paragraph.

**Statistical support is uneven.** Pretraining uses 6 seeds with error bars; CPT uses a single seed (42) and Table 2 reports no variance at all, while several claimed orderings there are separated by 0.01–0.02 PPL and 0.02 postMV (PID 0.90 vs ALF-LB 1.01). The 5×-budget runs (Table 11) do not close the gap, which helps, but the CPT ranking between PID and ALF-LB is effectively a tie on everything except postMV, and the authors say so ("indistinguishable on both perplexities"). Meanwhile the Vanilla pretraining baseline uses one seed, so the 6.10 vs 0.47 contrast has no error bar on the baseline side.

**Scale and scope are narrow, and the scaling claim rests on three points.** Largest model is 6.92B total / 1.28B active; pretraining runs consume only ~1.08B tokens (33k steps × 32,768), which is three orders of magnitude below production pretraining and far too short to exhibit the late-training collapse modes that motivate the problem. The distribution shift is a single instantaneous hard switch between two corpora — no gradual drift, no repeated switches, no >2-domain schedule, no SFT stage, even though the introduction lists all of these as motivation. The "margin widens with E" trend is three scales with 6 seeds each; the authors correctly call it an empirical observation, but Figure 1 is still the main argument for why anyone at production scale should care.

**Missing comparisons.** No capacity-limited routing (GShard/Switch capacity factor), no expert-choice routing, no comparison to router z-loss or to a simple load-balancing-free but better-initialized router, and no wall-clock or throughput measurement — the efficiency case is made entirely through the MRI conversion in D.3, which is an algebraic restatement of MaxVio, not an independent measurement. The claim in Section 5 that better balance implies better efficiency is therefore conditional and untested on hardware.

**What is genuinely good.** The Aux-loss derivation (Eqs. 5–7) is the cleanest explanation I have seen of why auxiliary losses leave residual imbalance, and the $1/\alpha$ steady-state-error reading matches the empirical sweep in Table 5 (DER 15.14% at $\alpha=10^{-5}$ down to 0.00% at $10^{-1}$). The rotating-versus-persistent overload analysis (Figure 12) exposes a real flaw in MaxVio that most MoE papers ignore: ALF-LB and PID keep MaxVio low partly by rotating which expert is overloaded, and the paper is careful to check DER/UTR so this does not become a hidden artifact. The bias-removed counterfactual (Table 14/15) is an unusually honest probe — it shows the deployed balance is *not* free and that perplexity actually improves when the bias is discarded. Very few balancing papers bother to measure that.

## Strengths

- A genuinely clarifying reframing: two widely deployed methods collapse into one parameterized family, with the mapping derived rather than asserted, and the derivation yields a testable prediction (steady-state error ∝ 1/α) that the sweep confirms.
- The theoretical argument for the integral term is simple, checkable, and matches the ablation: P-only bottoms out at postMV 2.86 (7× worse than 0.40) and D-only at 5.47, exactly as the "offset vs ramp vs nothing" story predicts.
- Metric design is better than the norm: MaxVio alone is gameable via rotation and saturates at $E/K-1$, so DER, UTR, Active and the window-level/per-step ratio are added and each is used to rule out a specific failure mode.
- The spectral analysis (Figures 7–8, Flip, $\rho$ in Table 9) is a real diagnostic contribution — it turns "the integrator averages noise" from a slogan into a measured 25.9% → 13.0% reduction of slow-band variance and a 37.6% vs 67.7% flip rate.
- Honest reporting of the costs: forgetting is measured on a held-out annealing set, downstream benchmarks are reported even where they hurt, and the bias-removed experiment explicitly shows perplexity *improving* when the balancer's action is discarded.
- Strong reproducibility for an academic paper: open backbone, full architecture (Table 3) and training (Table 4) tables, gain sweep grids, 6 seeds in pretraining, pseudocode, and a stated gain-transfer rule.
- The non-stationary evaluation protocol (pre/post windows around a controlled switch) is a reusable template; the observation that a cold-started balancer reaches warm-start postMV is a useful practical result for CPT.

## Limitations

- **One backbone, one routing design.** OLMoE with dropless softmax top-K only; expert-choice routing, capacity-limited routing and sigmoid gates are untested, and the balancer requires editing the router forward pass, so closed architectures are out of reach.
- **Small scale and short runs.** 2.25B/0.49B and 6.92B/1.28B models; ~1.08B pretraining tokens and 0.54B CPT tokens (2.7B at 5×). The scaling trend over E∈{16,32,64} is three points and the authors flag it as an observation, not a law.
- **A single hard distribution switch.** No gradual drift, no repeated switches, no multi-stage curriculum, no SFT/alignment stage — all of which the introduction names as motivation.
- **The theory is local and assumes a static plant.** No stability or convergence guarantee for the interacting $E$-dimensional loop; the router's score drift and the top-K argmax coupling are outside the analysis.
- **Single seed for continual pretraining**, no error bars in Tables 2, 10–13, and a single-seed Vanilla baseline in pretraining, so small CPT differences (0.90 vs 1.01 postMV; 2.19 vs 2.19 PPL) are not statistically resolved.
- **The algorithm is a thin delta over ALF-LB's linear rule**; the paper's contribution is the normalization, the analysis, and the shift-focused evaluation rather than a new mechanism.
- **No efficiency measurement.** Latency claims go through the MRI identity only; no throughput, no all-to-all cost, no expert-parallel wall-clock.
- **No mitigation for the forgetting cost.** The mechanism (force-reassigned tokens overwriting prior-domain experts) is hypothesized but not verified, and no schedule/decay/gating variant is tried.
- **Large-$K_I$ instability.** Because the correction scales with both error magnitude and gain, PID (I) degrades faster than ALF-LB at the top of the sweep (limit cycle, postMV hits the ceiling), which the paper attributes to discarding the sign — a real robustness cost of the linear rule.

## Relevance

直接命中「LLM Training Stability & Observability」中的 MoE 路由/负载失稳：它把 routing collapse 当成一个可测量、可控制的闭环问题，并给出了死专家率、欠训练专家率、误差功率谱、持续 vs 轮转超载等一套可直接搬到自己训练监控里的诊断量。对于做持续预训练或课程式中途换数据混比的场景，"换分布后均衡器多久恢复、恢复的代价是不是遗忘"这两个问题，这篇是目前少见的、有对照实验的回答。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. The linear integrator loses the bounded-update property of the sign rule and degrades into a limit cycle at large $K_I$. Is there a controller that keeps the sign's robustness while retaining magnitude information — e.g. a saturating (clipped) integral, or a normalized update $u \leftarrow u + \eta\,\mathrm{clip}(e,-\epsilon,\epsilon)$? The paper's own sweep suggests the operating band is narrow at E=64 ($K_I=10^{-4}$).
2. Since $\Delta u_i = K_I e_i$ is applied every optimizer step, the effective integral gain scales with the number of steps per token — how does $K_I$ need to change with batch size, sequence length, gradient accumulation, and LR schedule? The transfer rule across $E$ is empirical; a rule across batch/step regimes would matter more in practice.
3. Does the "margin over ALF-LB widens with $E$" trend survive at 128–256 experts and at trillion-token budgets, where routing collapse is qualitatively different (dead experts appear late, not early)? The three-scale evidence is suggestive but the authors themselves decline to call it a trend.
4. The forgetting result is the practical blocker for CPT. Can it be reduced by decaying $K_I$ over the CPT run, by applying the bias only to layers/experts whose prior-domain load is already saturated, or by excluding experts identified as prior-domain specialists from force-reassignment?
5. If removing the bias at inference lowers perplexity by 0.9–2.6% while destroying balance, the deployed routing is strictly worse for quality than the gate's own preference. Is there a training-time regularizer that pushes the *gate weights* toward the bias-corrected selection, so the balance survives bias removal? That would convert a deployment hack into a learned property.
6. The control formulation assumes a monotone, memoryless router. How badly does it break under sigmoid gating (where scores are not normalized across experts) or under capacity-limited routing (where the plant becomes non-monotone because dropped tokens are rerouted)? A negative result here would usefully bound the framework's scope.
7. Would a smoothed error — e.g. an EMA of $e_i(t)$ over 50–100 steps, which the authors name as the most promising direction in D.4 — let the P and D terms contribute? Their argument says P/D fail because the error is noisy, not because proportional action is inherently wrong; an EMA error is the direct test.
