> **一句话总结：** 作者用"同一内容、一优一劣两个翻译方向"构造对比实例，在 gemma-3-4b-pt 的最后一层 token 位置做四类 donor（matched / shuffled / mean / mean_diff）激活替换，发现多语生成的固定三段式顺序：L15–16 先出现与内容无关的"英语默认"信号（四个 donor 同步跳升 chrF++ 8.0→28.5，matched−shuffled = −0.02，p=0.96，即完全不依赖具体内容），L17 内容信息才变得可因果调用（matched−shuffled 从 0.43 跳到 4.43 chrF++，p<1e-3；Belebele 准确率同层 0.22→0.71），而具体目标语言要到 L24 才定型；三模型（Gemma 4B / Qwen3.5 9B / Llama-3.1 8B）都复现该顺序，但稀疏自编码器层面出现一个反转：中间的英语方向几乎不可稀疏表示（top-50 特征 R² 在因果层反而跌到 0.48），而后期的挪威语/瑞典语区分很稀疏（R²=0.91–0.95）却无法通过干预该位置翻转语言。

> **TL;DR:** Using FLORES+ multi-parallel segments, the authors build contrastive instances where gemma-3-4b-pt translates one direction well (HIGH) and the reverse/sibling direction poorly (LOW), then patch a single last-token residual vector from HIGH into LOW layer by layer. Four donors separate content-specific from content-free transfer: a matched HIGH state, a shuffled HIGH state from another segment, the per-layer mean HIGH state, and an additive mean-difference steering vector. The result is a clean three-stage ordering — an instance-independent English default at L15–16 (all four donors rise together, matched−shuffled = −0.02 chrF++, p=0.96), content onset one layer later at L17 (matched−shuffled = +4.43 chrF++, p<1e-3; Belebele accuracy 0.22→0.71 vs 0.26 for shuffled, against a 0.10 clean baseline and 0.87 HIGH), and target-language resolution at L24 (sister pairs nno/swe and ukr/rus flip at only 0.12 and 0.08, vs 0.68–0.94 for English targets). The ordering replicates in Qwen3.5-9B-Base (L18/L19/L28) and Llama-3.1-8B (L13/L14/L24), though Llama's transition is a ramp rather than a step. The sparse-autoencoder story is a genuine negative result: Gemma Scope 2 features (reconstruction fidelity 0.90–0.96) cannot reproduce the English flip, and the sister-language direction that *is* sparse (R²_50 = 0.91–0.95) does not flip the output when intervened on. Worth reading if you work on multilingual interpretability or on why low-resource generation fails; skim if you want a scaling story, since all three models sit in the 4–9B band.

## 核心贡献

- **提出 donor-control 跨语言激活替换方法**：用 matched（同实例 HIGH 激活）、shuffled（另一实例 HIGH 激活，种子化错排）、mean（逐层 HIGH 均值并按范数重标定）、mean_diff（HIGH 均值减 LOW 均值作为加性 steering 向量）四类 donor，把替换效应分解为"内容特异"与"内容无关"两部分。这是本文最实在的方法论贡献——只有 matched 见过测试片段，其余三类都不含该片段内容。
- **构造"内容固定、语言变化"的对比实例**：利用 FLORES+ 多平行语料，取同一 segment 的两个翻译方向，一个 xCOMET-XL 高分（HIGH）、一个低分（LOW），要求 margin ≥ 0.10 且每对至少 50 条存活实例，得到 22 个 MT 对比对，按共享成分分为三组：同目标（7 对，不可能换语言，改进只能是内容）、同源不同目标（9 对，内容传递无意义，隔离目标语言决策）、完全不相交（6 对，两者皆变，必须靠 donor 控制）。
- **给出三段式层级图景**：L15–16 出现与实例无关的英语默认信号；L17 内容信息变得可因果调用（比英语信号晚一层）；L24 才解析出具体目标语言。关键证据是"两个效应叠加而非竞争"——L16 语言翻转已完成之后，matched 恰好在 L17 与 shuffled 分离（36.1 vs 31.8 chrF++，p<1e-3）。
- **跨任务复现**：在 Belebele 阅读理解（零样本四选一，模板完全不同）上，内容起始层同样是 L17——matched 从 L16 的 0.22 跳到 L17 的 0.71，shuffled 和 mean 停在 0.26 / 0.24（四选一随机水平 0.25）。作者据此论证 L17 是模型属性而非翻译任务属性。
- **发现稀疏可表示性与末位因果性的分离**：中间的英语翻转方向在因果层反而最不稀疏（top-50 特征 R² 从 L13 的 0.80 跌到 L15 的 0.48，把预算加倍到 k=100 也只回到 0.54）；而后期的姐妹语言区分很稀疏（L24–25 的 R²_50 = 0.91–0.95），但对这些特征做 add/ablate/both 干预完全不改变输出语言（干净基线 0–3%，matched 本身也只翻转 6–7%）。
- **把 SAE 重建保真度确立为该方法的前提条件**：Gemma Scope 2 保真度 0.90–0.96 时能得到可解释的 shared/instance-specific 划分；Qwen-Scope 0.45–0.73 时结果被重建误差主导；Llama-Scope 0.26–0.55 时根本无法做该分析（论文标为 n/a）。这是一个对领域有用的负面方法论结论。

## Key Contributions

- **A donor-control methodology for cross-lingual activation patching.** Four donors — `matched` (the same instance's HIGH state, the standard positive control), `shuffled` (a different instance's HIGH state under a seeded derangement), `mean` (the per-layer average HIGH state rescaled by γ = mean‖h‖/‖h̄‖ so that null effects cannot be blamed on reduced magnitude), and `mean_diff` (the only additive condition, δ = h̄_HIGH − h̄_LOW, which preserves the instance's own state) — decompose a patching gain into content-specific and content-independent components. Applied to 22 MT and 9 reading-comprehension contrastive pairs.
- **A three-stage, layer-localised account of what a single last-token residual state carries**, supported by converging evidence from layer-wise patching and an intervention-free logit-lens probe, replicated across three models of different sizes, tokenizer families (SentencePiece vs BPE), attention designs (full vs Qwen's hybrid linear/full), and SAE families (JumpReLU vs TopK).
- **A feature-level dissociation between sparse representability and last-token causality**, plus the demonstration that SAE reconstruction fidelity bounds what this class of analysis can establish at all.
- **A cross-task replication** showing the English-before-content ordering is not an artifact of the translation prompt template: Belebele's P:/Q:/A:B:C:D:/Answer: format places the content onset at the same layer (L17) despite sharing no surface metric with MT.

## Methodology

**Data.** FLORES+ devtest (1,012 segments) for MT, topped up from NTREX-128 through an identical pipeline when too few contrastive instances survive; Belebele for reading comprehension, whose passages are drawn from FLORES sentences so both tasks share the same material. MT uses the 5-shot prompt `{src_lang}: {src}\n{tgt_lang}: {tgt}` from Zhang et al. (2023), greedy decoding, 512-token cap truncated at the first newline. Belebele is zero-shot with next-token log-probability over four option letters, each verified to be a single token. Segment-level quality Q(ŷ, y) is instantiated with chrF++ (primary) and xCOMET-XL (source-conditioned, secondary).

**Contrastive instance definition.** For an ordered pair of directions (LOW, HIGH), retain segments satisfying q_LOW(i) < τ_LOW ∧ q_HIGH(i) ≥ τ_HIGH, with per-pair thresholds, a required margin τ_HIGH − τ_LOW ≥ 0.10, and ≥ 50 surviving instances. The authors justify per-pair over global thresholds explicitly: a single global cutoff would either eliminate LOW pairs or admit near-ties in HIGH ones. The 22 MT pairs split into same-target (7; e.g. kas→eng vs hin→eng), different-target-same-source (9; e.g. eng→nno vs eng→swe), and disjoint (6; e.g. kas→cmn vs hin→eng).

**Patching.** The intervention site is exactly one d-dimensional vector — the residual-stream output of block ℓ at the last token position of the prompt, which is a fixed template token (the trailing `{tgt_lang}:` for MT, `Answer:` for MC), making a positional copy meaningful. A *replacement* patch overwrites h(ℓ)(x_LOW) with a donor v(ℓ); an *additive* patch sets h(ℓ)(x_LOW) ← h̃ + αδ(ℓ) with α = 1 by default. Layers are patched independently, never two at once, so a curve over ℓ reports the marginal causal contribution of that layer's last-token state. In generation runs the intervention is applied only at the first decoding step; all later steps run unmodified, so any persistent change is a downstream consequence propagated through the KV cache. The authors note that patching the shared source span for same-source pairs produces no change in reference log-probability, so only last-token patching is used. Implementation is nnsight in bfloat16 with eager attention.

**Signal measures.** Three: (i) quality deltas Δ_LOW and Δ_HIGH against the LOW/HIGH references; (ii) output language via fastText lid.176, supplemented by target-exclusive characters (æ/ø for Norwegian vs ä/ö for Swedish; Unicode script membership for cross-script targets) because single short outputs in close siblings defeat LID — flip fraction is the corpus-level share in the HIGH target, and flip onset is the first layer (ℓ ≥ 8) where the HIGH-target exclusive share reaches 0.5; (iii) for Belebele, accuracy difference plus whether the patched LOW pass reproduces the HIGH pass's own prediction. All conditions are evaluated on the same instances, so differences are paired per-instance means tested with a two-sided bootstrap over 10,000 resamples.

**SAE analyses.** Gemma Scope 2 residual-stream SAEs at width 262k (JumpReLU), L0 ≈ 100–150 active features, mean reconstruction fidelity 1 − ‖ĥ−h‖/‖h‖ from 0.96 at L13 to 0.90 at L28. Three interventions on the residual: ablate currently-active LOW-associated features, add selected HIGH-associated features, or both; each feature set (3–7 features per layer) is manually verified on a max-activating-feature dashboard before use. Sparse coverage of the flip direction r = h̄_HIGH − h̄_LOW is measured as cumulative least-squares R²_k over the top-k decoder vectors (β fit by least squares rather than fixed to raw activation differences, so scale mismatch cannot understate the span). A "denoised" control splits each HIGH vector's active features into shared (active in ≥ 90% of the pair's instances) and instance-specific, subtracts the instance-specific reconstruction, and compares against two controls: a renormalised version (to rule out norm damage) and random ablation of an equal mass from an equal number of random active features.

**Models.** Primary: gemma-3-4b-pt, 34 layers, 2560-d residual, pretrained rather than instruction-tuned so behaviour reflects pretraining-induced competence rather than alignment. Replication: Qwen3.5-9B-Base (32 layers, hybrid attention with three linear-attention layers per full-attention layer, 4096-d, Qwen-Scope TopK k=50 width 65,536) and Llama-3.1-8B (32 layers, full attention, 4096-d, Llama-Scope TopK k=50 width 131,072, 32× expansion as the closest analogue to Gemma's 262k). 18 of Gemma's 34 layers are swept densely where effects manifest.

## Results & Highlights

- **The English flip is content-independent.** Pooling the six different-target pairs whose HIGH direction targets English (n=980), patching is inert before L15 (Δ_HIGH < 2 chrF++ for every condition). Between L15 and L16 all four donors jump together: matched 8.0 → 28.5, shuffled 7.8 → 28.5, mean 5.8 → 31.8, mean_diff 6.7 → 29.4 chrF++. The paired matched−shuffled difference is 0.15 chrF++ at L15 (p=0.81) and −0.02 at L16 (p=0.96) — whether the donor comes from a matched or a random segment makes no measurable difference. xCOMET-XL moves in step (0.06 → 0.18 for matched, 0.18–0.19 for the other three), and the median language-flip onset is L15–17 under every condition for all six pairs, with 68–94% of instances ending in English.
- **Content transfer begins one layer later, at L17.** For the seven same-target pairs (n=1,403, where no language flip is possible so any gain is content), matched rises to +1.79 chrF++ at L17 while shuffled falls to −2.35 and mean to −0.34. The paired matched−shuffled gap is 0.43 chrF++ at L16 (p=0.04) and 4.43 at L17 (p<1e-3), persisting to L32. xCOMET-XL reproduces both the onset and the ordering (0.05 vs −0.01 at L17). The wrong-segment donor being *actively harmful* is what a genuine content channel predicts.
- **Belebele replicates the content onset at the same layer without any surface metric.** Matched moves accuracy from 0.22 at L16 to 0.71 at L17, while shuffled and mean plateau at 0.26 and 0.24 against a four-way chance level of 0.25 (paired gap 0.47, p<1e-3). The clean baseline is 0.10 and the HIGH pass scores 0.87. The share of instances where the patched LOW pass reproduces the HIGH pass's own prediction rises from 0.30 at L16 to 0.80 at L17, against a flat 0.28 for shuffled.
- **The two effects stack rather than compete.** After the language flip is already complete at L16, matched separates from shuffled at exactly L17 (36.1 vs 31.8 chrF++, p<1e-3) — evidence that the English default and the content channel are distinct and sequentially ordered rather than one masquerading as the other.
- **A late-layer reversal that does not survive a stronger metric.** From L20 onward mean overtakes matched on Δ_HIGH (36.3 vs 31.5 at L32, paired gap −4.81, p<1e-3), which would suggest instance-specific content competing with the model's own encoding. But xCOMET-XL does not reproduce it, putting matched slightly *ahead* at L20 (0.21 vs 0.19) and level at L32, so the authors classify it as a surface-form effect and say so explicitly.
- **Target-language resolution is late and distance-dependent.** Across all 15 different-target pairs, English/high-resource-Latin targets cluster at onset L15–17 with 0.68–0.94 flipped, while non-English targets move to L22–24. Flip fraction tracks target distance: 0.44 for khm vs cmn and 0.42 for khm vs hin (both cross-script), against only 0.12 for the nno/swe sisters and 0.08 for ukr/rus. An intervention-free logit lens corroborates the map — for eng→rus, Cyrillic mass stays at 0.05–0.10 through L23 before reaching 0.38 at L24 and 0.97 at L33.
- **The English-flip direction is not sparse, precisely where it is causal.** Cumulative R²_50 sits at 0.80 ± 0.14 at L13, drops to 0.48 ± 0.27 at L15 and 0.54 ± 0.11 at L16, then recovers to 0.76 ± 0.11 at L17 — the dip coincides exactly with the layers where the flip is causal. This is not a feature-budget artifact: doubling to k=100 raises L15 only from 0.48 to 0.54. Sparse add/ablate/both interventions leave the output language at its 0–5% clean baseline while matched, mean and mean_diff put 76–97% of outputs into English at the same layer, and the null holds across both SAE widths (16k and 262k), k ≤ 100, and α = 1 as well as α = 4.
- **Sister-language features are sparse but not last-token causal.** R²_50 at L24–25 is 0.91–0.95 for the directions separating nno from swe and ukr from rus, ruling out the objection that the features are badly chosen. Yet no identified feature flips the language: matched itself flips only 6–7% of instances against a 0–3% clean baseline, so the sparse interventions are indistinguishable from that. The sharpest version of the contrast: at L24 an English target flips at 76–97% while a sister target sits at noise, holding everything else fixed and varying only the granularity of the language distinction.
- **Instance-specific features become load-bearing at exactly L17.** Shared feature mass in HIGH vectors falls from 0.76 at L16 to 0.57 at L17. Patching the denoised vector beats the full matched HIGH vector by 4.60 chrF++ at L16 (p<1e-3, n=1,055) and mean by 1.60, while preserving or exceeding the flip (0.60/0.84/0.79/0.91/0.89/0.86 vs 0.60/0.39/0.78/0.91/0.88/0.57 across six pairs); random ablation of equal mass instead drops those fractions to 0.49/0.56/0.54/0.68/0.79/0.10. From L17 the ordering reverses (denoised below matched by 3.58 at L17, 3.95 at L20, 18.48 at L28), and renormalisation changes nothing (+0.15, p=0.26 at L16), so the collapse is neither norm damage nor an artifact of removing mass per se.
- **Cross-model replication of the ordering, with model-specific geometry.** Gemma 4B: flip L16 (0.47 relative depth), content L17 (0.50), target L24 (0.71); Qwen 9B: L18 (0.56), L19 (0.59), L28 (0.88); Llama 8B: L13 (0.41), L14 (0.44), L24 (0.75). matched−shuffled at the content layer is +4.43 / +3.63 / +2.06 respectively, and Belebele matched/shuffled at the final layer is .87/.27, .99/.25, .98/.23.

## Authors & Credibility

Shenbin Qian and Yves Scherrer, Department of Informatics, University of Oslo. Scherrer is a well-established NLP researcher with a long publication record in machine translation, multilingual modelling, and low-resource/dialect NLP (Nordic languages in particular, which plausibly explains the Norwegian/Swedish sister pairs at the centre of the feature-level dissociation). Qian works on multilingual and interpretability-adjacent NLP. The paper is a single-lab effort, not a large-industry-lab interpretability release, and the compute profile reflects that: roughly 16,500 GPU-hours total on Sigma2's Olivia supercomputer (EU Horizon Europe MSCA funding, project nn9851k), with a median pair costing 25 GPU-hours on Gemma, 75 on Qwen and 207 on Llama. The experimental discipline visible in the appendices — per-pair thresholds tabulated, derangement seeds fixed, paired bootstrap throughout, explicit "n/a" where the instrument fails — is above the norm for this literature. The paper states AI tools were used only for writing and coding assistance. No obvious conflict of interest.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The individual ingredients are not new — activation patching, donor swapping, mean-difference steering, logit lens and SAEs are all standard, and the "English pivot in intermediate layers" finding has been reported before (Wendler et al. 2024; Zeng et al. 2025). The genuine novelty is the *design*: using a success/failure contrast on multi-parallel data so that content is held fixed by construction, and then using three deliberately impoverished donors to prove that a given gain carries no instance-specific information. That is a real methodological idea, and it produces a result that behavioral evaluation could not have produced — the fact that all four donors rise in lockstep at L15–16 and only matched separates at L17 is a genuinely clean dissociation, not a marginal effect dressed up with statistics.

**Rigor.** The control set is unusually well thought out. `mean` is norm-rescaled by γ so that a null cannot be attributed to a shrunken donor; `mean_diff` is included specifically to test whether the effect reduces to one linear direction; the denoised analysis carries two controls (`matched_denoised_renorm` for norm damage, `random_ablate` for mass removal) and the authors report that the ordering *reverses* at L17 rather than quietly dropping the condition. They also report, in the main text, that xCOMET-XL fails to reproduce the mean-beats-matched ordering that chrF++ shows, and downgrade their own interpretation to "surface form" as a result. That is the kind of self-correction that is rare and should be credited.

**Weak points, in order of severity.**

1. **The below-chance Belebele baseline is never interrogated.** Gemma's clean accuracy is 0.10 against a four-way chance level of 0.25 — the model is performing *worse than random* on the zero-shot P:/Q:/A:B:C:D:/Answer: template. The paper notes each option letter was verified to be a single token, but says nothing about why a base model would score at 0.10. When the clean baseline is that far off chance, the jump from 0.22 to 0.71 under patching is at least partly "the patch restores sane answer-format behaviour" rather than "the patch transfers the answer." The authors do report the better-controlled metric (does the patched pass reproduce the HIGH pass's own prediction, 0.30 → 0.80 vs 0.28 flat), which is why I do not think the conclusion is wrong — but the accuracy number is the one in the abstract's supporting evidence and it deserves a paragraph of scrutiny, not a parenthetical.
2. **The feature-level dissociation is effectively a single-model result presented as a three-model one.** Table 1's "Sparse flip works?" row reads no / no / n/a. Qwen's reconstruction fidelity (0.45–0.73) makes the denoised vector dominated by reconstruction error — the authors themselves report matched_denoised fails to preserve the flip at 0.29, barely above the random_ablate control's 0.29 — and Llama's (0.26–0.55) is too low to attempt the analysis at all. So the claim that "a language signal can be sparsely encoded without being causally effective" rests on Gemma alone, and on sister pairs with n=60 (nno/swe) and n=85 (ukr/rus) instances. The abstract's framing ("Across three LLMs... we find a consistent three-stage pattern... Our sparse autoencoder analyses reveal a further dissociation") lets the reader attribute the dissociation to all three. The patching-level claim genuinely does replicate; the SAE claim does not, and the paper's own table says so more clearly than its prose.
3. **The SAE explanation is built on an effect the stronger metric does not reproduce.** §5 asks why `mean` overtakes `matched` in late layers, and answers with shared/instance-specific feature mass crossing over at L17. But that gap is a chrF++ phenomenon that xCOMET-XL reverses at L20 and flattens at L32. Explaining a metric-specific surface artifact with a feature-level mechanism is a chain of inference where the first link is acknowledged to be fragile. The denoised result does show the shared/instance-specific split is *functionally* real at L16 (beating matched by 4.60 chrF++ while preserving the flip, and beating random ablation everywhere), so there is a real finding here — but it is a finding about L16, and it is being used to explain a late-layer effect that may not exist in adequacy terms.
4. **Selection and measurement share a metric family.** Contrastive instances are selected by xCOMET-XL thresholds with a required margin, and xCOMET-XL is then one of the two evaluation metrics. LOW instances are, by construction, those xCOMET-XL dislikes; measuring xCOMET-XL improvement on them has a regression-to-selection component. chrF++ is the primary metric and is not used for selection, which mitigates this substantially, but the two metrics agree suspiciously well at the onsets (both jumping at L17) and the paper does not discuss whether that agreement is independent evidence or a shared artifact.
5. **"Three LLMs of different scales" is a stretch.** 4B, 8B, 9B is a narrow band, and the only architectural contrast that matters (Qwen's hybrid attention) shifts the onsets by two layers rather than producing a qualitative difference. No scaling claim is supportable, and the paper wisely does not make one — but the phrase appears in the abstract and in Table 1's framing, and a reader skimming will over-read it.
6. **The "English default" is really an English/high-resource-Latin default.** Figure 2 shows Norwegian and Swedish targets clustering with English at L15–17, and the logit-lens appendix concedes the point directly ("the assertion of an English and high-resource Latin default rather than a generic target commitment"). The title and abstract nonetheless say "English." This is a framing overclaim on an otherwise carefully hedged paper.
7. **Single-token, single-layer, first-step-only intervention.** The authors are candid that negative results here mean "not causally available at this position at this layer," not "absent" — and they explicitly warn against reading the sparse-feature nulls as evidence of absence. But the entire feature-level story depends on negative results of exactly that kind, and the caveat is stated in Limitations rather than attached to the claims in §5. There is also no exploration of patching at the source-span positions beyond the note that it does nothing for same-source pairs.
8. **Llama weakens the "content-independent English default" claim more than acknowledged.** Llama's matched−shuffled at the flip layer is +0.74 chrF++, significant at p<1e-3, and the authors defend it as "1.6% of the 46.6 chrF++ effect at that layer, against 13% one layer later." That is a reasonable normalization, but the qualitative difference remains: Gemma and Qwen show a *null* difference at the flip layer, Llama shows a *significant* one, and Llama's transitions are ramps over L10–13 rather than steps. The paper reads this as "confirming the transfer ordering while noting that layer-sharpness is model-specific," which is defensible but softer than the abstract's "consistent three-stage pattern."

**Significance.** Moderate to high for the interpretability-of-multilingualism subfield, and the negative SAE result (fidelity bounds what feature analysis can establish; 0.90–0.96 gives an interpretable split, 0.45–0.73 gives reconstruction noise, 0.26–0.55 gives nothing) is arguably the most transferable contribution — it is a caution that many SAE papers would benefit from internalizing. The practical payoff is currently speculative: the conclusion gestures at "some low-resource generation failures may reflect not only missing knowledge, but also how and when language selection is established," which is an interesting hypothesis but is not tested by any intervention that improves downstream quality. There is no experiment showing that patching at L15–16 or L24 actually raises chrF++ on a held-out low-resource pair in a way a practitioner could use.

## Strengths

- The donor-control design is the right tool for the question and is executed cleanly; the lockstep rise of all four donors at L15–16 followed by matched-only separation at L17 is a textbook-quality dissociation.
- Cross-task replication on Belebele, with a completely different prompt template and a different scoring rule, is the strongest single piece of evidence in the paper and is not an afterthought — it is placed in the main results figure.
- The intervention-free logit lens independently reproduces the patching onset map, which is exactly the kind of converging evidence that separates a real mechanism from an intervention artifact.
- Honest reporting of inconvenient results: the xCOMET-XL non-replication of mean > matched, the failure of the sparse interventions, the n/a cells in Table 1, and a Limitations section that names the specific inferential limits of the design rather than reciting generic caveats.
- The reconstruction-fidelity analysis turns two failed replications into a methodological lesson rather than burying them, which makes the paper more useful than a clean three-for-three would have been.
- Per-pair thresholds, fixed derangement seeds, paired bootstrap with 10,000 resamples, and tabulated instance counts (Appendix Tables A.1, A.2, E.1–E.3) make the selection procedure auditable — rare in this literature.
- The 16,500 GPU-hour cost is disclosed, and the per-pair cost breakdown by model is given, which is useful for anyone considering a replication.

## Limitations

- Interventions are a single residual vector at a single position in a single layer; the layer-wise curves identify what is causally *available* at that state, not where the computation is performed. Negative results (especially the sparse-feature nulls) may reflect the intervention site rather than the absence of a signal.
- The feature-level analysis is relative to specific learned dictionaries. Qwen's 0.45–0.73 and Llama's 0.26–0.55 reconstruction fidelity mean the SAE conclusions are effectively Gemma-only, and the sister-language claim rests on n=60 and n=85 instances.
- All three models are pretrained base checkpoints in a 4–9B band; nothing is said about instruction-tuned models, larger scales, or models with heavier multilingual pretraining, and the paper's motivating phenomenon (language confusion) is most often observed in instruction-tuned chat models.
- Contrastive instances are selected by xCOMET-XL, whose reliability varies across languages and may inherit high-resource biases; the LOW set is therefore biased toward segments where a particular metric family is pessimistic.
- The late-layer `mean` > `matched` effect is metric-dependent and the paper's own secondary metric contradicts it, so the SAE mechanism offered for it is underdetermined.
- Language identification on single short outputs is unreliable for close siblings, which is why the target-exclusive-character discriminator exists; that discriminator is a hand-built heuristic per language pair and its 0.5 threshold is a choice, not a calibration.
- The "English default" conflates English with high-resource Latin-script languages, as Figure 2's clustering of nno/swe with English shows.
- No downstream utility experiment: the mechanistic account does not yet translate into an intervention that improves low-resource generation quality.

## Relevance

与"多语 LLM 的机制可解释性"直接相关：如果你关心低资源语言生成失败的成因、语言选择在残差流中何时被决定、或者 SAE 特征分析在什么保真度下才有意义，这篇值得细读——尤其是它把"稀疏可表示"与"末位因果有效"明确拆成两个独立属性这一点，对任何用 SAE 做因果声称的工作都是直接的约束。若你更关心可落地的多语能力提升或 scaling 规律，本文只提供机制假设，没有给出能提升 chrF++ 的干预方案。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Why does gemma-3-4b-pt score 0.10 on zero-shot Belebele when chance is 0.25? Is the P:/Q:/A:B:C:D:/Answer: template simply out of distribution for a base model, and if so, does the L17 content onset survive a template the model actually handles (e.g. few-shot, or a cloze formulation)?
2. If the English-flip direction is not sparse at L15–16 but is causally a single additive direction (mean_diff reproduces the full effect), what *is* its geometry? A single direction that no top-50 dictionary vector spans is an odd object — is it spread across many low-activation features, or does it live in a subspace the SAE's L0 budget cannot reach?
3. Does the L15–16 English default exist in models with substantially more balanced pretraining data (e.g. Chinese- or Arabic-centric models, or models trained with heavy multilingual replay)? The paper's own framing predicts it should weaken or move later, which is a testable prediction the paper does not run.
4. What happens with instruction-tuned checkpoints? The authors chose base models deliberately, but language confusion in deployment is an instruct-model phenomenon, and alignment may re-shape exactly the late-layer target resolution the paper localises at L24.
5. Can the three-stage map be used predictively — i.e., does a pair's flip onset layer or flip fraction at L15–17 predict its chrF++ on held-out data, giving a cheap diagnostic for which low-resource directions will fail?
6. The paper patches only the last token. What happens if the same donor is written at the source-span positions, or at multiple layers simultaneously? The claim that source-span patching "produces no change in the reference log-probability" for same-source pairs is asserted in a footnote and seems worth a fuller negative-result treatment.
7. Is the sister-language null (sparse features exist, intervention at the last token does nothing) an artifact of intervening at the wrong position? A logit-lens probe shows the sister distinction is decodable from the last token at L24 — so the information is *there* but not *writable* there. Patching at earlier positions or in the attention output rather than the residual would distinguish "not causal" from "not causal at this site."
