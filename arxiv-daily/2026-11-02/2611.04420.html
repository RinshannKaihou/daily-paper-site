Verifier strength does not buy discovery quality: a pre-registered two-domain test of LLM-based automated research, with post-hoc evidence pointing to proposer-pool composition↗

Key metric
100/100

domain-A pairs where the weak (S−) and standard (S0) gates promoted the identical candidate — degenerate tiers, not a subtle effect.

Domain D: 99/100 identical. Merging pools instead: +1.52 holdout units (A, p = 0.0002).

Why it matters

If you build proposer–verifier loops, tightening the gate is not the lever. Broadening the proposal pool is.

对搭建 proposer–verifier 发现回路的人：杠杆在提议池，不在闸门。

Critical edge
The proposer is a 0.49B model, and the headline does not say so.

Bimodal sessions and a 48% random-win rate in domain A may be small-model artifacts; a 7B–70B proposer could restore the S0 gate.

Generalization risk

01 / Method fixed proposer · frozen criteria

One pool, three gates

  • Proposer fixed. Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, 20 proposals per session, CPU-only and offline; temperature 0.8, top-p 0.9.
  • Same pool, re-gated. S− screens minimally, S0 applies the full frozen constraint set, S+ adds a worst-case stress test. Only the filter changes.
  • Frozen before data. Protocol v1.0: Δ(S+) > Δ(S0) > Δ(S−), one-sided Wilcoxon p < 0.05. The manipulation check must pass first; holdout is scored once.
  • Two domains. Job-shop priority rules on 154 deduplicated orders (7 features); T-Drive edge offloading with 3 RSUs (8 features).

02 / Results 100 pairs / domain

The gate moved; the outcome didn't

The manipulation check passed. The main hypothesis failed in both domains — and in D, stricter verification significantly shrank the LLM advantage.

Frozen S+ vs S− one-sided Wilcoxon: p = 0.1115 (A), p = 0.9994 (D). In D the reverse ordering is significant (p ≈ 0.0006); post-hoc margin contrast −0.040, 95% CI [−0.07, −0.01], p = 0.0013.

  • Degenerate, not noisy. S− ≡ S0 in 100/100 A and 99/100 D. All movement is S+ vs S0: 14 divergent pairs in A (S+ wins 10, +0.714), 23 in D (S+ wins 1, −0.066).
  • Random is competitive. At S0 the random arm supplies the better candidate in 48% (A) / 46% (D) of pairs; under S+ in D it rises to 60%.
  • The pool is the lever. Merging the 20 LLM + 20 random proposals adds +1.52 A holdout units (n = 100, p = 0.0002) — 4–5× the entire LLM-over-random margin. In D it is null-to-negative.
  • A clean false negative. One D pair: S+ killed a candidate scoring 0.524 on holdout and forced a 0.0 — a −0.52 swing caused by the stress test alone.

Post-hoc utilization sweep · 50 pairs · −0.033 at 4% → +0.067 at 22–23% → +0.035 at 26–27%; six points, no CIs.

03 / Strengths protocol + statistics

What holds up

  • Pre-registration is real. v1.0 criteria, public changelogs to v1.2, a manipulation gate, and a negative clause committing to report failure.
  • The separation logic is clean. The mutation battery closes the usual “your treatment was too weak” escape hatch.
  • Statistical honesty above the norm. Floor sensitivity, per-domain MDEs, frozen versus post-hoc analyses kept separate.
  • The degeneracy analysis generalizes. Any intermediate gate between “runs” and “runs well” can collapse this way.
  • Reproducibility is unusually complete. Protocol, ledgers, verifiers, batteries, six analysis scripts; CPU-only.
04 / Limitations scope + power

What to discount

  • One proposer. 0.49B parameters, CPU-only. Every mechanism identified may be a small-model artifact.
  • Domain A cannot tell “no effect” from “no power”. +0.367 with 95% CI [−1.15, 1.85]; and with S0 ≡ S−, the v1.0 strict order was unsatisfiable before data.
  • The strongest effect rests on the weakest manipulation. Domain D separates tiers by only 2 of 310 mutants.
  • The union-pool gain is post-hoc, has no CI, and sits near two ±10⁹ floor pairs.
  • Only baseline is random. No SPT/EDD/WSPT comparison, no cost accounting for S+.
