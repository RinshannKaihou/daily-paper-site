Why this matters02

Circuit findings don’t port: a reader found at 7B can spread across heads at 70B, invert after post-training, or flip under a system prompt.

For activation steering, survey simulation, and anyone porting a circuit result to a bigger model.

Critical edge03

No prompt baseline. The paper never tests whether a one-line “answer each question independently” instruction removes as much coupling at zero perplexity cost.

Without that control, the ablation is a probe — not necessarily the cheapest fix.

Methodology04

One token apart, by construction.

Every directed pair runs the same conversation twice; only the model’s forced first answer differs. Any downstream change is therefore attributable to one token.

  1. 01

    Force an answer. Ask q1, then insert Yes or No as the model’s own turn.

  2. 02

    Ask a different q2 about the same entity and measure κ, the log-odds shift in its answer.

  3. 03

    Read the write. At the answer token, dq = h(Yes) − h(No) for each question.

  4. 04

    Test the geometry. Does cos(dq1, dq2) predict κ on held-out entities?

  5. 05

    Ablate one shared direction g across the readout band — sparing position 0, the attention sink.

Controls: leave-one-domain-out g · selection/test split · two answer-word orders · random directions · random weights · 4-bit replication.

Results05

Geometry travels. The reader doesn’t.

Direction cosine predicts coupling in every model (ρ = 0.43–0.85). What changes is where the direction is read — and which way.

Strengths06

What earns trust +

  • +One-token causality, verified. All 3,600 pairs are identical up to the forced answer; patch and ablation checks land within 0.05 of target.
  • +Breadth with controls. 22 models, four families, three disjoint folds, plus random-direction, random-weight and 4-bit checks.
  • +A hard-to-dismiss demonstration. In Qwen3-4B-2507 a prompt swap flips the head map to ρ = −0.31 (five other models: 0.65–0.86) and positive-coupling pairs jump 12% → 81%.
  • +Cost is measured, not hand-waved. Failures are reported, and cos²(g, PC1) predicts the perplexity cost at ρ = 0.73.
Limitations07

Where it strains –

  • –Inserted, not generated. First answers are one forced token; all questions are binary; no deployment-style conversation is tested.
  • –No downstream outcome. No downstream task is measured; “preserved” means sign only, while log-odds still move 0.2–5.6 nats.
  • –Reader evidence has holes. Read gain is unmeasured at 70–72B, and Qwen3-32B is an unexplained outlier (ρ = 0.12).
  • –Not deployable yet. Cost runs use 200 pairs, not the 600-pair test set; three Qwen models preserve only 60–78% of standalone answer signs; no prompt or decoding baseline; no independent replication.
Open questions08

Q3
