> **一句话总结：** 把自进化 agent 的「是否接受这次 harness 更新」建模为在线多重假设检验，提出以假进化率（FER，被接受更新中造成不可接受退化的比例）为安全准则的 TRUST 门控——用 e-value + e-LOND 式预算分配 + 非劣性检验 + 验证集重用（overlap）修正，在 HotpotQA/DROP/APPS 六个设置上把平均 FER₀ 从贪心门的 45.59% 压到 2.78%，同时平均最终增益达到 24.84 pp（贪心门在 DROP/DeepSeek 上仍有 36.24 pp 的更高增益）。

> **TL;DR:** TRUST replaces the standard "accept if validation score went up" gate in recursive self-improvement with an online multiple-testing gate: each candidate update is a noninferiority test (H₀: Δ ≤ −ε) whose e-value is thresholded at a testing level α_t drawn from a shared FER budget, with the budget split guided by an LLM reading the round's history and with the null boundary inflated by an exposure-probability term β to absorb optimism from reusing the same validation pool. Theorems give FER ≤ q, a power-consistency bound, and a lower bound on final gain. Empirically, averaged over HotpotQA/DROP/APPS × DeepSeek-V4-Flash/Qwen-3.8-Flash (30 rounds, 5 seeds, 200-example validation pool, 300 held-out test items), TRUST reaches 24.84 pp mean gain with mean FER₀ 2.78% and mean MDD 0.048 pp, versus Greedy's 45.59% FER₀ and 4.963 pp MDD — though on DROP/DeepSeek Greedy still wins on raw gain (36.24 vs 31.47 pp).

## 核心贡献

- **提出 FER（False Evolution Rate）作为自进化过程的安全准则。** 定义为「被接受的更新中，总体得分下降超过容忍度 ε 的比例」的期望，即 FER_ε(T) = E[F_T^ε / (R_T ∨ 1)]，并把它作为整个 T 轮递归自改进过程的可控目标（FER ≤ q），而不是逐轮控制假接受概率。
- **把门控写成在线多重检验问题并给出完整算法。** 每轮检验 H₀: Δ_t ≤ −ε 对 H₁: Δ_t > −ε（非劣性检验），用 e-value 累积证据，接受阈值 1/α_t；α_t 由剩余预算 B_t、已接受次数 R_{t−1} 和消耗比例 ρ_t 决定（e-LORD 式规则嵌入 e-GAI 框架）。
- **用 LLM 做上下文感知的预算分配。** 让 LLM（G-Eval 式显式评分：safety / contract / actionability / generalization / scope control）读取候选更新与历史反馈，输出乘子 w_t 来缩放本轮消耗比例 ρ_t，从而把更多错误预算分给看起来有希望的候选；关键性质是 FER 保证**不依赖** LLM 判断正确。
- **给出四条理论结果。** 条件 e-value 有效性（含任意停时）、FER ≤ q（含外层停时）、TER（真进化率）的渐近下界 1 − r_{N,n} − T·e^{−κn}、以及最终增益下界（把非劣性容忍 ε 带来的下行损失显式分解出来）；附录还给出期望最大回撤 MDD 的上界。
- **指出了一个有意思的理论必要性：ε > 0 是必需的。** 当 ε = 0 时 Assumption 2(c) 的上界 2ε/(1+ε) 退化为 0，任何正的下注比例都不满足条件，功率一致性不成立——这为「必须容忍一点损失才能获得统计功效」提供了形式化解释。
- **在闭环 harness 上做了六组对照实验与三组消融。** 自建四组件（reasoning / routing / memory / workflow）、20 个算子的可编辑 harness，与 Greedy、PACE、SGM-B、SGM-H 在相同初始状态、相同缓存响应下比较 30 轮轨迹。

## Key Contributions

- **Defines a process-level safety criterion for RSI.** Rather than bounding the per-round false-acceptance probability, the paper defines the False Evolution Rate — the expected fraction of *accepted* updates whose population score drops by more than a tolerance ε — and makes "FER_ε(T) ≤ q" the object of control. This mirrors FDR rather than FWER, which is precisely the relaxation that lets the gate keep accepting useful updates.
- **Casts candidate acceptance as online multiple testing with an e-value test statistic.** Each round tests H₀: Δ_t ≤ −ε against H₁: Δ_t > −ε; the statistic M_t,m^{(β)} is a product of betting factors 1 + η_{t,i}(Y_{t,i} − u_{t,i}^{(β)})/u_{t,i}^{(β)} on rescaled scores Y = (1+D)/2, and the candidate is accepted at the first m where M ≥ 1/α_t (early stopping, so validation cost can shrink).
- **Adds an exposure-probability overlap correction.** Because the same 200-example validation pool is resampled every round, the null boundary is inflated to u_{t,i}^{(β)} = p_ε + ζ_{t,i}β, where ζ_{t,i} is the probability that the drawn index was already used in an earlier round or earlier in the same round. This is a lightweight, interpretable stand-in for the reusable-holdout literature (Dwork et al. 2015; Rogers et al. 2016).
- **Derives FER control, power consistency, and a deployment-gain lower bound.** Theorem 2 gives FER ≤ q for the whole horizon and for any history-dependent stopping time; Theorem 3 gives TER → 1 as N, n → ∞; Theorem 4 gives G(T) ≥ (1 − e^{−κn})P_T − Σ_t E[A_t min{(−Δ_t)_+, ε} + α_t(−Δ_t − ε)_+], which separates the loss from statistical rejection (exponentially small), the loss inside the tolerance band (bounded by ε per accepted update), and the loss beyond tolerance (scaled by α_t).
- **Introduces an LLM allocator whose mistakes cannot break the guarantee.** The LLM only chooses ρ_t inside [ρ_min, ρ_max]; because the FER bound is proved for arbitrary α_t sequences, a bad LLM judgment costs power, not validity. This separation of concerns is the cleanest design decision in the paper.
- **Provides a closed-loop empirical comparison plus a deliberately adversarial overfitting study.** Appendix I runs Greedy with 20 examples reused for both proposing and validation and shows validation gain climbing to 24.44 pp while test gain peaks at 5.06 pp after round 1 and decays to −2.15 pp by round 10 — a direct measurement of the failure mode the paper is built to prevent.

## Methodology

**Problem setup.** A solver θ has population score J(θ) = E_{X∼D,Ω} u(X, S_θ(X,Ω)) with u ∈ [0,1]. A fixed proposer generates candidate harnesses Θ̃_t ∼ Q(·|F_{t−1}); a gate G compares Θ̃_t against the incumbent Θ_{t−1} and returns A_t ∈ {0,1}. Because different gates induce different incumbents, they also induce different future candidates — the whole point is that the gate changes the search trajectory, not just the final selection.

**Gate as hypothesis test.** H₀: Δ_t ≤ −ε is "the candidate is unacceptably worse"; rejecting it accepts the candidate. Regions: Δ ≤ −ε unacceptable, −ε < Δ ≤ 0 tolerated but not improving, Δ > 0 improving. Note that a candidate which *loses* up to ε points is still accepted in expectation — this is the noninferiority margin, borrowed from clinical trials (Blackwelder 1982).

**Budget allocation (Sec. 4.1).** B₁ = q; α_t = min{α_max, ρ_t B_t (R_{t−1}+1)}; charge c_t = α_t/(R_{t−1}+1); B_{t+1} = B_t − c_t. The default spend is ρ⁰_t = 1/(T−t+1) (equal share of what remains), adjusted to ρ_t = clip(w_t ρ⁰_t, ρ_min, ρ_max) by the LLM multiplier. All of α_t, ρ_t, w_t are fixed *before* seeing this round's validation samples, so they live in F_t^{pre} and cannot leak information into the test.

**E-value construction (Sec. 4.2).** Sampling is uniform without replacement from the pool within a round, with all samples returned between rounds. Q_{t,i} flags reuse; ζ_{t,i} = |I_{t,i−1} ∩ R_{t,i−1}| / |R_{t,i−1}| is its probability. Scores are rescaled to [0,1], the null boundary becomes p_ε = (1−ε)/2, and the corrected boundary u^{(β)} = p_ε + ζβ. The bet fraction η is set by an equally weighted mixture of four fixed strategies (0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75) — a mixture e-value, which keeps validity by convexity and removes the need to pick one η.

**Harness.** H = (R, P, M, W): reasoning (typed rule set with ADD_SPECIALIST_RULE / REFINE_RULE / SET_DECOMPOSITION_POLICY / GENERALIZE_RULE_SCOPE / PROMOTE_RULE_TO_DEFAULT / DELETE_RULE), routing (SET_ROUTE_MODE with full / lexical / bridge variants, REWEIGHT_RELEVANCE_SCORER, ENABLE_BRIDGE_AWARE_EXPANSION, SET_FIXED_TOP_K, SET_FULL_FALLBACK), memory (ADD_LOCAL, REFINE_PROCEDURE, MODIFY_TRIGGER, MODIFY_PRIORITY, DELETE), workflow (SET_VERIFY_MODE, SET_EVIDENCE_ALIGNMENT, SET_STOP_RULE, SET_FALLBACK_MODE). Proposals are structured JSON: (component, operator, target, parameters). One atomic operator per round; the solver role, output contract, and single-call protocol are frozen, which keeps the comparison about the gate rather than about harness expressiveness.

**Protocol.** 3 benchmarks (HotpotQA Joint F1, DROP token F1, APPS test-case pass rate) × 2 models (DeepSeek-V4-Flash, Qwen-3.8-Flash); 20 seed examples for F₀, 200-example validation pool, up to 40 validation draws per round, 300 held-out test items used only for reporting; 30 rounds, 5 seeds (solver seeds 0–4, split seeds 42–46); responses cached across gates so all methods see identical solver outputs. TRUST defaults q = 0.30, ε = 0.10, β = 0.01. All gates start from the same deliberately weakened harness.

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline risk reduction across the six settings:** mean FER₀ falls from Greedy's 45.59% to TRUST's 2.78%, and mean maximum drawdown falls from 4.963 pp to 0.047 pp, while mean final gain rises to 24.84 pp. Individually, Greedy's FER₀ exceeds 30% in every one of the six settings (64.67 / 44.67 / 35.28 / 37.38 / 41.08 / 50.44), whereas TRUST stays at or below 10% everywhere.
- **HotpotQA, DeepSeek-V4-Flash:** TRUST reaches 11.58 ± 1.30 pp gain with FER₀ 10.00% and MDD 0.02 pp, versus Greedy's 7.87 ± 0.55 pp with FER₀ 64.67% and MDD 4.96 pp, and PACE's 10.15 ± 0.75 pp with FER₀ 0.00% but TER only 12.63% (TRUST 18.11%). So TRUST beats PACE by +1.43 pp gain and +5.5 pts TER, but is *worse* on FER₀ (10.00% vs 0.00%) — a trade the aggregate number hides.
- **APPS, DeepSeek-V4-Flash:** TRUST 34.55 ± 1.04 pp gain at FER₀ 0.00% and MDD 0.00 pp vs Greedy 33.89 ± 2.71 pp at FER₀ 41.08% and MDD 8.48 pp. This is the cleanest result in the paper: equal-or-better gain with the risk metric essentially eliminated, and Greedy's 8.48 pp drawdown is the largest in the table.
- **Where TRUST loses:** on DROP/DeepSeek, Greedy gets 36.24 ± 2.19 pp vs TRUST's 31.47 ± 1.42 pp (−4.8 pp for TRUST) at FER₀ 35.28% vs 0.00%; on APPS/Qwen, Greedy gets 28.03 ± 0.32 pp vs TRUST's 26.53 ± 0.11 pp. TRUST is best in four of six settings, and the paper says so, but the "highest final gain" claim is a 4/6 statement, not a sweep.
- **The FWER baselines are degenerate, not merely conservative:** SGM-B returns exactly 0.00 ± 0.00 on gain, FER₀, TER, and MDD in all six settings — it never accepts a single update in 30 rounds × 5 seeds. SGM-H accepts only three updates across all runs (it produces 5.77 ± 5.77 pp on HotpotQA/Qwen and 10.84 ± 6.65 pp on APPS/Qwen, and zeros elsewhere). With δ = 0.5 and α_t = δ/(t·H₃₀), the levels start around 0.008 and shrink, so the "statistical gate baselines" comparison effectively reduces to PACE alone.
- **Noninferiority margin is load-bearing (ablation, HotpotQA/DeepSeek):** ε = 0 yields 0.00 pp gain and zero accepted updates; ε = 0.05 gives 4.87 ± 3.00 pp with TER 2.23%; ε = 0.10 gives 11.58 pp; ε = 0.15 gives 11.59 pp but FER₀ jumps to 57.68 ± 4.55% and MDD to 5.33 pp. The whole benefit lives in a narrow band around ε ≈ 0.1, and at ε = 0.15 the gate is as risky as Greedy while gaining no more.
- **Overlap correction β is the most sensitive knob:** β = 0 gives 10.97 ± 1.86 pp with FER₀ 23.33 ± 14.53% and MDD 1.27 pp; β = 0.01 gives 11.58 pp / 10.00% / 0.02 pp; β = 0.03 gives 7.13 ± 2.97 pp; β = 0.05 gives 2.80 ± 2.80 pp with FER₀ 0%. A 5× change in β moves final gain by 4×, and the paper offers no data-driven rule for choosing it.
- **LLM allocator ablation is within noise:** final score 44.91 ± 1.36% with the allocator vs 43.78 ± 1.18% without (w_t ≡ 1), i.e. +1.13 pp with overlapping error bars on HotpotQA/DeepSeek. The supporting evidence is a single-seed case study (seed 3) where TRUST finishes at Joint F1 47.22% vs 43.95%.
- **Overfitting stress test (Appendix I, Greedy, HotpotQA-Distractor, 20 fixed examples for both proposing and validation):** mean validation gain climbs to 24.44 pp while mean test gain peaks at 5.06 pp after round 1 and falls to −2.15 pp by round 10. A single accepted memory update lowers mean test Joint F1 from 54.55% to 48.00% — 13 items decline, none improve.

## Authors & Credibility

Zihe Gao and Yifan Cui are at Zhejiang University; Zhengling Qi is at George Washington University. The framing, notation, and proof style are those of a statistics/stat.ML group rather than an LLM-agents group: the paper is posted under stat.ML (cross-listed cs.AI, cs.LG, stat.ME), the related-work section is dominated by multiple-testing literature (Benjamini–Hochberg, e-BH, e-LOND, e-GAI, contextual online FDR, reusable holdout), and the appendices are mostly proofs (B–E run ~25 pages) with a comparatively thin experimental appendix.

That orientation cuts both ways. On the plus side, the statistical machinery is used correctly and not hand-waved: the information flow is laid out as an explicit filtration chain F_{t−1} ⊆ F_t^{pre} = H_{t,0} ⊆ … ⊆ H_{t,n} ⊆ F_t, all allocation quantities are pinned to F_t^{pre} *before* validation, stopping times are handled properly (Theorem 1 holds at any τ_t ≤ n, and the FER bound survives an outer stopping time), and the mixture-over-betting-strategies trick is invoked with the right justification. The authors also flag the untestable Assumption 1 honestly in Appendix A.1 rather than burying it.

On the minus side, the empirical side is visibly less mature than the theory. No code or artifact link appears anywhere in the PDF, the "state-of-the-art statistical gate baselines" are two SGM variants configured with a single conservative setting that the authors chose, and the LLM component — one of three headline contributions — rests on a +1.13 pp ablation with overlapping error bars. The 2026 citations in the related work (Ren et al., Chen et al., Shawn, Sengupta, Nguyen et al.) are recent and plausible for the period but several are cited for load-bearing claims (PACE, SGM, RSEA) that the reader cannot check against a venue.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty — real but compositional.** Every ingredient is borrowed: e-LOND/e-GAI-style online FDR, noninferiority testing from clinical trials, exposure-probability correction from the reusable-holdout line, contextual α allocation from Chen & Kasiviswanathan. What is new is the *framing* — recognizing that RSI's accept/reject sequence is an online multiple-testing problem whose natural error criterion is a false-discovery-rate analogue over accepted updates, not a per-round false-acceptance probability. That reframing is genuinely useful and, as far as I can tell, not previously stated this cleanly for harness-level self-improvement. Theorems 1 and 2 are close to corollaries of existing e-value FDR results once FER is written as an FDR over the null Δ ≤ −ε; Theorem 3's observation that ε > 0 is *necessary* for power consistency (because the admissible betting range 2ε/(1+ε) collapses at ε = 0) is the one piece of theory I would call a genuine insight rather than a restatement. Theorem 4's decomposition into rejection loss / tolerance-band loss / beyond-tolerance loss is a nice piece of bookkeeping but its bound is loose enough that it does not predict the observed numbers.

**Rigor — the theory is tight, the empirics are not.** Three specific gaps. (1) *Theory–experiment mismatch.* Theorems 3 and 4 are asymptotic in N, n → ∞ with T fixed, and Assumption 2(b) requires n/N → 0. The experiments use N = 200, n = 40, T = 30, so n/N = 0.2 and the asymptotic regime is nowhere near. The guarantees that the experiments are supposed to illustrate therefore do not formally cover the reported setting. (2) *An unmodeled selection step.* Appendix G.3 states that the proposer generates three proposals and "we screen the candidates on 20 examples from the previous round and select the valid candidate with the highest score for the current round." That is a max-over-three selection on validation data, and it sits *outside* the filtration the theory is built on. It is exactly the kind of optimism Assumption 1 is supposed to absorb through β, but β is a single scalar set by hand at 0.01, so there is no reason to believe it covers a three-way max. (3) *No check of the actual guarantee.* The controlled quantity is FER_ε with ε = 0.10 and q = 0.30; what is reported is FER₀, which counts every Δ ≤ 0 (including ties) and is strictly harsher. That is conservative and to the authors' credit, but it means no experiment ever tests the theorem's own statement — we never see whether empirical FER_{0.10} ≤ 0.30 holds, nor how much slack there is. A direct check would have cost one extra column.

**Baselines — one real competitor, two strawmen.** SGM-B never accepts anything (0.00 ± 0.00 on all four metrics in all six settings) and SGM-H accepts three updates total. Reporting these as "state-of-the-art statistical gate baselines" and then noting they "are conservative in practice" understates the problem: a baseline that never fires is not a baseline, it is a demonstration that FWER control with δ = 0.5 and α_t = δ/(t·H₃₀) is unusable at n = 40. No attempt is reported to tune δ or the α_t schedule for SGM, even though the authors tuned q, ε, β, and the betting mixture for TRUST. That asymmetry is the single biggest threat to the paper's empirical claim. PACE is a fair comparison and TRUST beats it on gain and TER in all six settings, but the margins are often ~1–1.5 pp against standard errors of similar size (27.72 ± 0.81 vs 26.42 ± 1.60 on HotpotQA/Qwen; 34.55 ± 1.04 vs 28.68 ± 2.66 on APPS/DeepSeek is the one clear win). RSEA, which the introduction names as an existing gate design (held-out selection separate from proposal generation), is never compared against — an odd omission for a paper whose thesis is "the gate matters."

**Data quirks the paper handles well and badly.** Well: Appendix I is the most convincing part of the empirical case. Reusing 20 examples for both proposing and validation drives validation gain to 24.44 pp while test gain decays to −2.15 pp, and the Lady Gaga / WINNER case study shows the mechanism concretely — the memory operator stores the *literal validation question* with its verified answer, so the update "repairs" a question it will see again and then, on an unseen question sharing the keyword "album", restricts evidence selection and drops the paragraph needed for the second hop (test Joint F1 54.55% → 48.00%, 13 items down, 0 up). That is a textbook demonstration that validation-score gating is broken, and it is honest of the authors to include it even though it also implicates their own harness design. Badly: the main experiments' initial harness is deliberately seeded with sabotage-level policies ("Select only the first displayed title and ignore all later titles and sentences", per Listing 14), so the 24–34 pp gains partly measure the repair of an artificially crippled baseline rather than the discovery of real capability. The paper never reports absolute scores for most settings, only gains, so a reader cannot tell whether TRUST's 34.55 pp gain on APPS/DeepSeek starts from 20% or from 55% pass rate.

**Red flags, ranked.** (i) SGM baselines untuned and degenerate. (ii) LLM allocator — one of three contributions — supported by +1.13 pp with overlapping error bars and one seed of case study. (iii) β and ε sensitivity so steep that the headline 11.58 pp on HotpotQA/DeepSeek is a point estimate on a narrow ridge: β = 0.03 drops it to 7.13, ε = 0.05 drops it to 4.87, ε = 0 drops it to 0. (iv) No code, no artifact, no per-item results, no wall-clock or token-cost accounting despite the allocator adding an LLM call per round. (v) Five seeds and 300 test items mean the test-gain standard error is ~1–3 pp, which is the same order as most of the between-method differences; no paired test is reported. (vi) The theoretical claim "FER ≤ q" and the empirical claim "2.78% average FER₀" are about different quantities, and the abstract juxtaposes them in a way that invites the reader to conflate them.

**Significance.** If you run a self-improving agent loop with a fixed validation set, this paper gives you a principled replacement for the naive accept-if-better gate and a vocabulary (FER, error budget, noninferiority margin, exposure correction) for reasoning about it. That is a real contribution to a real problem, and the empirical direction — risk down by an order of magnitude, gain roughly preserved — is what a practitioner would want. What it does not give you is a turnkey recipe: three hyperparameters (q, ε, β) with steep, undocumented sensitivity, an LLM allocator of unproven value, and no released code.

## Strengths

- **The reframing is the contribution, and it is a good one.** Treating the accept/reject sequence as online multiple testing, and choosing FDR-style rather than FWER-style error control, is exactly the right relaxation: FWER is what makes SGM unusable at n = 40, and the paper shows this empirically rather than asserting it.
- **Clean separation between statistical validity and LLM judgment.** Because the FER bound holds for any α_t sequence, the LLM allocator can only cost power, never validity. This is a much better architecture than "let the LLM decide whether to accept," and it is the design choice most worth stealing.
- **The proofs are careful about the things that usually get fudged.** Allocation happens before validation and is pinned to F_t^{pre}; validity is established at arbitrary stopping times so early stopping is legitimate; the outer-stopping-time extension means you can stop the whole RSI run based on observed history without losing the guarantee. The betting-fraction mixture removes a tuning knob without breaking validity.
- **Theorem 3 explains *why* noninferiority is needed**, not just that it helps: at ε = 0 the admissible betting range is empty, so power consistency is impossible. This turns a hyperparameter into a structural requirement and is the kind of result that changes how you think about the problem.
- **The overlap correction is cheap and interpretable.** Inflating the null boundary by ζβ, where ζ is a computable exposure probability, is a much more practical response to validation reuse than a full reusable-holdout mechanism, and the ablation confirms it matters (β = 0 → FER₀ 23.33% and MDD 1.27 pp).
- **Appendix I is exemplary.** Rather than hiding the failure mode, the authors run a dedicated stress test, report validation gain diverging from test gain by 26.6 pp, and walk through a concrete item where a "good" update destroys an unrelated test item. Papers in this area should do this more often.
- **Honest reporting of the four-of-six result** and of the settings where Greedy wins on raw gain.

## Limitations

- **Asymptotic theory, small-sample experiments.** N = 200, n = 40, T = 30 with n/N = 0.2 violates Assumption 2(b); Theorems 3–4 do not describe the reported regime.
- **Assumption 1 is untestable and β is hand-set.** The paper admits it has no data-driven way to pick the overlap-correction strength, and the sensitivity table shows the choice dominates the result (10.97 → 11.58 → 7.13 → 2.80 pp as β goes 0 → 0.01 → 0.03 → 0.05).
- **The ε = 0.10 tolerance is a 10-point score drop.** Updates that lose up to 10 pp are "tolerated but not improving" and do not count toward FER_ε. The empirical FER₀ column is stricter than the guarantee, which is honest, but it also means the theory permits a regime most practitioners would consider unacceptable.
- **No per-update loss accounting.** FER controls the *proportion* of harmful accepted updates, not the *magnitude* of the worst one. A single catastrophic update can pass while FER stays under q; the MDD bound in Appendix E is the only counterweight, and it is not evaluated empirically against a worst-case adversary.
- **Narrow model coverage.** Two "Flash"-tier models from two families, no frontier model, no open-weight 70B-class model. Whether the gate behaves the same when the proposer is much stronger (and its candidates are more often genuinely good) is unknown.
- **Old, small benchmarks.** HotpotQA (2018), DROP (2019), APPS (2021), with 200 validation and 300 test items each. These are saturated or near-saturated for frontier models, which compresses headroom and makes gains harder to attribute.
- **No cost accounting.** Early stopping should save validation calls, but the allocator adds an LLM call per round; the net token/API cost versus Greedy or PACE is never reported.
- **No code release.** The harness (20 operators, proposer/solver/allocator prompts) is the part most needed for reproduction, and only excerpts appear in the appendix.

## Relevance

直接命中「自进化 agent 与自动化研究」这条主线：它把 self-evolving agent 的 harness 更新接受问题形式化为在线多重检验，并用 FER 给出整条自改进轨迹的安全保证——这正是长时运行自主 agent 系统可信性问题的核心。对关心「验证集门控会不会把 agent 带偏」的读者，附录 I 的过拟合压力测试（验证增益 24.44 pp vs 测试增益 −2.15 pp）比主表更有价值；对做 agent harness / 编排层静默失效的读者，它提供了「接受门本身就是失效点」的一个统计视角。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. **What happens with a tuned SGM?** If δ and the α_t schedule were grid-searched the way q, ε, β were, would SGM-B still never fire? A single tuned-FWER point would settle whether TRUST's advantage is the FDR-vs-FWER choice or the tuning budget.
2. **How does the guarantee degrade as n/N grows?** The experiments already sit at n/N = 0.2; a sweep over n/N ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5} would show whether the FER bound is merely asymptotic or practically useful at realistic validation budgets.
3. **Can β be estimated rather than tuned?** Given that the proposer sees closed feedback and screens three candidates on 20 examples, the optimism is at least partly measurable. An empirical-Bayes or bootstrap estimate of the selection bias would replace the paper's weakest hyperparameter with a data-driven one.
4. **Does the gate still help when the proposer is strong?** The initial harness is deliberately crippled and most accepted updates repair obvious bugs. In a regime where 80% of proposals are genuinely good, the marginal value of a conservative gate — and the cost in rejected improvements — is a different calculation.
5. **What is the worst-case single update that passes at q = 0.30?** FER bounds the proportion, not the magnitude. Constructing an adversarial proposal sequence that keeps FER low while producing one large drawdown would test whether MDD control needs to be a first-class constraint rather than an appendix bound.
