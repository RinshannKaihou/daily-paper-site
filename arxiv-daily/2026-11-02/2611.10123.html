Key finding

88,200 reports · 5 configurations · 120 tasks

An injected pain state is decodable and shifts choices, yet untrained models report no more than their words reveal.

Why this matters

If oversight or welfare evaluations trust a model’s “I feel nothing,” they are reading silence from a state that is decodable and behaviourally potent.

Critical edge

The known-positive may install a reporting computation rather than reveal a dormant one — the paper’s single largest interpretive gap.

Limitations · known-positive

01

Method

Steer, ask, then subtract the words.

Open-Weight Masked Introspection (OWMI) · 120 neutral tasks

A steering experiment becomes a paired reading test: the same transcript, with and without the vector, answers the same question.

01

Steer

Add the published pain direction to the residual stream on every token of a neutral task and its answer. Layer 16 for 7B/8B, block 38/64 for 32B; ρ = 0.3–1.5.

02

Ask

In a fresh, never-steered turn, ask what changed. Re-encode the transcript with the vector reapplied (“state”) or with no vector (“text-only”).

03

Subtract

The paired difference is what the state adds beyond the model’s altered words. Five configurations, 120 tasks, 88,200 parsed reports — state vs. text-only, steered vs. sham.

Controls that carry the argument

  • Sham: no vector at all.
  • Three random directions per model and dose, Jensen–Shannon divergence matched within 3%.
  • Text-only re-encoding: the same words, no privileged vector.
  • Known-positive: rank-16 LoRA trained with the transcript pinned identical.

Single author (USC) applying his own OWMI framework — coherent, but not independent replication.

03

Balance

Well controlled, narrowly scoped.

Two model families · three sizes · one injection layer

Strengths What holds

  • Four controls anticipate the standard objections — sham, matched random, text-only, known-positive. Few steering papers clear all four.
  • The known-positive is used correctly: fixed transcript, label only in the residual, 100% on 40 held-out tasks for the 7B/8B models, including an unseen wording.
  • Statistical honesty: exact one-sided bounds for zero counts, whole-task folds, every nominal excess disclosed.
  • The perspective analysis cuts both ways: first-person pain weighs ~2×, but the direction does not single out self-directed harm.
  • Full release: directions, raw results, code, verbatim prompts.

Limitations What bounds it

  • No production models: two open-weight families, three sizes, one injection layer in the main runs.
  • The known-positive may create the reporting computation it is meant to reveal.
  • The behavioural bridge is not pain-specific: at 32B sadness exceeds pain, and button order inflates the 7B headline.
  • Underpowered below ~3 pp; pointwise intervals across 441 extension cells.
  • Single LLM judge, 20 hand-written sentences, one direction split; refusal geometry unvalidated.

04

Open questions

What would move the claim?

Four experiments the paper leaves open.

  1. Would the null survive on a base model or one RL-trained for honesty, where “trained denial” cannot be the explanation?
  2. Can a reporter generalize to unseen directions, not just unseen wordings — and would that reveal a missing route or a missing incentive?
  3. Patch the steered-minus-sham residual difference into an unsteered report: does the report change?
  4. Why does pain suppress reporting after a harmful choice (5.8% vs 15.2%) — re-encoding artifact or self-serving bias?
