01Method

Decompose, delete, then make deletion cheap

15B-A1B · 12 configs · 5 benchmarks
  1. STEP 1Decomposeread gate / write gate / mixing
  2. STEP 2Delete mixingno Sinkhorn, no n×n product
  3. STEP 3Share entry stateK = 4 sublayers, one XL
  4. STEP 4Exact correctionsscalar dj,k rebuilds inputs
  5. STEP 5Fuse kernelsDeepGEMM + Triton

Backbone. One 15B-A1B MoE for all twelve configurations: 24 layers, hidden 1792, 256 routed experts (top-8), MLA with 1:3 full/sliding ratio, 8k context, Muon + AdamW. Pile loss plus MMLU, HellaSwag, MATH, MBPP, C3.

Architecture. Xl+1 = Xl + hpost ⊙ olT, with gates σ(αWTz+b) and 2σ(…) over z = RMSNorm(vec Xl). The n×n mixing logits and Sinkhorn are gone.

Execution. Four consecutive sublayers read the group-entry state once; dj,k = hpre,jThpost,k reconstructs each sequential input, and write-back is deferred to the group boundary. Conditioning all four gates on XL is the only approximation — Appendix B proves equivalence under shared gates.

Kernels & scale. One load yields projection and RMS statistics; gate gradients use a token-packed 16×16 Tensor-Core reduction. Validated on 30B-A2B over 14 benchmarks, plus a 230B-A6B stress test at 10× learning rate.

Strengths

What survives scrutiny
  • Twelve configurations on one backbone, with fixed-gate controls that isolate why each component matters rather than only whether it does.
  • The Sinkhorn result is counterintuitive and actionable: if doubly-stochastic mixing buys almost nothing over identity, the constraint machinery is dead weight.
  • Shared memory access is an algorithmic idea with an equivalence proof, not a kernel trick — the pure-PyTorch gap confirms it.
  • The correction ablation (55.0 → 47.9) explains why naive gate sharing fails, and the traffic formula explains why K=4 beats 8 and 16 instead of leaving it tuned.

Limitations

What the evidence cannot carry
  • Single seed throughout. The 30B margins of 0.2–0.3 points are inside plausible run-to-run variation, and no confidence intervals are given.
  • Training-token counts and data mixture are undisclosed; no checkpoints are released, so quality claims are not reproducible.
  • Efficiency is measured on 8-layer proxies at six widths — the real 15B/30B step time is never reported, so 2.4% is an extrapolation.
  • No iso-memory or iso-FLOPs baseline: 4-stream SimpleHC is not compared with a 2-stream variant or with spending the same memory on more layers.
  • The dramatic instability appears only at 10× LR on a 230B configuration used nowhere else; later in training all three variants converge to similar gradient norms.
04Open questions

What would change the verdict

4 of 7 from the source
  1. Does the 2.4% overhead survive at 64+ layers under pipeline or tensor parallelism, where the group-boundary write-back becomes a synchronization point?
  2. Is the Sinkhorn-vs-identity gap (56.2 vs 55.4) stable across seeds? If it replicates, a whole line of manifold-constrained follow-up work loses its premise.
  3. Does the gate-sharing approximation degrade with depth? The predictability measurement (MAE 0.0103) was taken on a 24-layer model only.
  4. Can the shared-entry-state trick be combined with unconstrained mixing — the 56.2 configuration — or does mixing reintroduce the per-sublayer dependency the scheme removes?
