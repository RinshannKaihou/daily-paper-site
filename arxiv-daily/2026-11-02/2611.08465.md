> **一句话总结：** 作者把"解释一个残差流方向"重新表述为"生成能最大化激活该方向的文本"，训练 MAEM（以 Qwen3.6-27B 自身权重初始化，先 SFT 后 RL，奖励 = 冻结主体模型在第 42 层对生成文本的最大余弦相似度），在 486 个留出激活上把中心化余弦从 10M token 语料检索的 0.572 提到 best-of-1 的 0.759 / best-of-8 的 0.822，并把这个"可前向验证"的目标迁移到 steering vector 识别（AxBench 95.8% vs 语料检索 61.4%）、rank-1 LoRA 后门读取（主题触发器写方向 85% vs 2%）和 SAE 特征激活（best-of-8 点亮 89% 的留出特征），但在 SAE 特征描述任务上反而输给语料窗口（检测平衡准确率 0.680 vs 0.810）。

> **TL;DR:** MAEM is a Qwen3.6-27B fine-tune that takes a residual-stream direction as a soft prompt and generates text that maximally activates it, trained with SFT then CISPO/ScaleRL on the frozen subject model's own cosine reward (no labels, no learned judge). On 486 contamination-filtered held-out layer-42 activations from Ultra-FineWeb it reaches a centred cosine of 0.759 (best-of-1) and 0.822 (best-of-8) versus 0.572 for a 10M-token corpus search (+0.187 / +0.250, winning on 92% / 97% of targets) and 0.612 for an NLA at matched token budget — while costing 3.35 H200-seconds per target against 274.5 for GCG and 932 for EPO. The exemplars are also fluent (67% judged at least as coherent as the source passage; +0.08 nats/token under Gemma-4-31B), and one checkpoint transfers to steering vectors, LoRA backdoors, workspace readout and SAE features it was never trained on — but it fails on positional/structural SAE features (25–38% failure) and produces less diverse samples, which costs it the automated SAE-description benchmark (0.680 vs 0.810 detection against corpus windows).

## 核心贡献

- **把"激活反演"从"复现某段源文本"改成"落在方向的原像里"。** 之前的 inverter（InversionView、NLA）要么重建源文本，要么被一个学习到的 reconstructor 打分；MAEM 直接以主体模型对自身输出的前向激活为奖励，因此条件可以是**任意**残差流方向，而不只是一个观测到的激活。
- **一个 checkpoint 覆盖四类方向。** 只用英文网页文本激活 + SAE 编/解码方向训练，却在从未见过的 steering vector、rank-1 LoRA 读/写方向、其他语言/代码/数学激活上工作；作者强调后两类是分布外输入。
- **给出可验证性论证。** 每条 exemplar 都是"能驱动 v 的充分条件"，一次前向传播即可核对，这在概念上把 metamodel 从"必须靠人或 LLM 评判的解释"变成"可自动验证的样本"。
- **量化了保真度—流畅度权衡。** MAEM 文本比 NLA 解释流畅得多（NLA 相对源段落 −0.93 到 −0.97 的成对偏好），同时余弦更高；best-of-k 从 0.80 提到 0.87 而不损失连贯性。
- **四项下游应用的正面结果**：steering vector 概念识别追平 NLA、远超 J-lens 与语料检索；后门 payload 读取在写方向上大幅超越语料检索；workspace 读出超过 J-lens/Patchscopes/语料检索但落后 NLA；SAE 特征激活率达 89%。
- **一份少见诚实的负面结果清单**：11.3% 的特征 best-of-8 点不亮、6.5% 64 次采样全失败；样本多样性显著低于语料（Vendi 2.19 vs 2.87），导致自动特征描述任务输给语料窗口；并且**专门针对失败特征做微调会让情况变糟**（修好 26 个、弄坏 304 个）。

## Key Contributions

- **Reframes interpretation as conditional generation under a verifiable reward.** Instead of describing `v`, MAEM samples `x ~ π_θ(·|v)` and is rewarded by `s(v,x) = max_t cos(v, h_t(x))` computed by the frozen subject model at the read layer. This removes both human/LLM-written labels and the learned reconstructor that NLAs can exploit.
- **A single 27B checkpoint generalises across direction families it never saw**: natural activations, SAE encoder/decoder directions, difference-of-means and BiPO steering vectors, and rank-1 LoRA read/write vectors.
- **Establishes activation inversion as a credible pretraining task for metamodels**, with the argument that the reward is grounded in the subject model rather than a proxy.
- **Demonstrates a safety-relevant application**: recovering the trigger/payload of rank-1 LoRA trojans from their weight-diff directions, where 10M-token corpus search essentially fails on the write direction (2% vs 85%).
- **Maps where the method breaks**: a taxonomy of failing SAE features (whitespace-position 56.8%, formatted-digit 40.4%, induction 40.9%, list/table 25.2%, topic 1.6%) plus evidence that the failures are not a text-production artefact — an LLM given 16 corpus windows fires 95.3% of features versus MAEM's 88.8%.

## Methodology

**Objective and architecture.** Given a unit direction `v ∈ R^d`, the MAEM generates a span `x`. The score is the maximum cosine over all generated positions, using the frozen subject's layer-42 activations: `s(v,x) = max_{1≤t≤|x|} cos(v, h_t(x))`. `v` is injected at a placeholder token after the second transformer block by norm-matched addition, `r'_m = r_m + (v/‖v‖₂)·‖r_m‖₂` (following Karvonen et al. 2025). Crucially, **generated text is scored in the clean subject model with no injection**, so a rollout cannot cheat by reading the direction back out of its own prompt.

**SFT initialisation.** Full fine-tune of all Qwen3.6-27B parameters from the subject's own weights (no intermediate stage): 8M examples = 4M short-context (8–64 token) corpus activations paired with their preceding text + 2M SAE encoder directions + 2M SAE decoder directions paired with top-activating corpus windows. 1 epoch, 1,954 steps, batch 4,096, lr 1e-5 one-cycle, max length 192, token-level NLL with the prompt masked, FSDP2 over 8 GPUs.

**RL.** ScaleRL recipe with CISPO policy loss (ε_max = 5.0, no PPO clip), no KL and no entropy term; regularisation is only a flat −2.5e-4 per-token length penalty beyond the first 8 tokens. Training pool is 941,132 directions: half corpus activations with no context-length restriction (64–2,048 tokens), a quarter SAE encoder, a quarter SAE decoder directions. 2,048 directions × 8 rollouts per update, 300 steps, lr 1e-6 constant, group-mean-subtracted advantages, zero-variance groups dropped. The reward is the **uncentred** cosine maximised over the last 16 generated tokens, whereas evaluation uses the **centred** score — an explicit train/test objective mismatch the paper acknowledges.

**Evaluation protocol.** `s_µ` subtracts a fixed corpus mean activation `µ` from both `v` and every `h_t(x)` to suppress the shared residual component. Targets are REALACT: 512 layer-42 residuals at random positions in held-out Ultra-FineWeb documents, filtered to remove any 64-token span sharing >5% of 7-grams with training text (a criterion fixed before scores were read), leaving n = 486 from 425 documents, with all standard errors clustered by document. Best-of-k is estimated without bias from 64 draws via the standard `Σ s_(j)·C(j-1,k-1)/C(64,k)` estimator.

**Baselines.** 10M-token corpus search (64-token windows, stride 16, own-document windows excluded) at nested 1.25/2.5/5/10M prefixes; the released NLA checkpoint at best-of-2 (token-matched: NLA averages 176 tokens vs MAEM's 47.3, so 8 MAEM draws ≈ 2 NLA outputs); GCG and EPO at 32 tokens on the first 64 targets (they cost 82× and 280× the GPU time); the untrained subject with the same injection; a no-injection Patchscopes-prompt floor; and random Gaussian directions. Downstream tasks add J-lens (layer 42 and a layer 36–50 grid), Patchscopes, and held-out labelled concept texts as a ceiling, judged by GPT-5.6 Sol / Claude Sonnet 5 with Holm-corrected paired sign-flip permutation tests.

## Results & Highlights

- **Held-out activation inversion (REALACT, n = 486, layer 42, centred cosine).** MAEM best-of-1 = **0.7590** vs 10M-token corpus search **0.5720** (Δ +0.1870, 95% CI [0.1757, 0.1981], ahead on **92%** of targets); best-of-8 = **0.8219** (Δ +0.2499, win 97%); best-of-64 = 0.8498. The NLA baseline at best-of-2 reaches **0.6121**, i.e. **0.2098** below MAEM best-of-8, and even using all four NLA outputs (0.6510) it stays far behind. Corpus search gains only 0.0251 per doubling of corpus size, so no crossing point is in sight within the scanned 10M.
- **Discrete-search baselines are crushed but on a small sample.** On the 64 targets where GCG/EPO were run, MAEM best-of-8 = **0.8119** vs GCG **0.3904** (Δ +0.4214) and EPO **0.3961** (Δ +0.4158) — though GCG's strings have a mean NLL of 13.22 nats/token, "the level of a random token string", so this is a weak opponent. Cost: **3.35 H200-s** per target for 64 MAEM draws vs **274.5** (GCG) and **932.0** (EPO).
- **SAE feature activation (512 held-out features of a 131k BatchTopK SAE, τ = 1.5846).** Best-of-8 fires **89%** of features (88.8% pooled over 492 in the appendix), reaches ≥0.5× the 10M-token corpus peak on **78%**, and **exceeds** the corpus peak on **31%** (the abstract and contribution list say 30% — a small internal inconsistency). Failures: 11.3% below τ at best-of-8, 6.5% never fire in 64 draws; the rarest decile of the uniform draw fires only **42%** of the time, versus 91–98% above the bottom fifth.
- **Steering-vector concept identification (AxBench Concept500, n = 334; 10-way forced choice by GPT-5.6 Sol, 8 texts per method).** MAEM **95.8%** [93.4, 97.9] vs NLA **92.8%** [90.1, 95.5], J-lens **85.3%**, corpus search **61.4%**; the steered-model reference is 85.6% and held-out labelled texts 99.4%. On BiPO persona vectors (n = 119) MAEM **83.7%** vs NLA **85.7%** (NLA nominally ahead), J-lens 71.4%, corpus 51.3%. A **single** MAEM exemplar already identifies 93.7% of AxBench concepts, and MAEM does this with 49-token texts against NLA's 194.
- **Rank-1 LoRA backdoor inversion.** On 32 thematic-trigger adapters the write direction yields **85%** [74, 94] verbatim / 90% semantic payload recovery versus **2%** for 10M-token corpus search; read directions 69% vs 49%. On 16 three-digit-code adapters it is **21%** (write) and **0%** (read) versus 1% and 5% — i.e. the method essentially fails on non-semantic triggers. Mismatched-adapter controls stay ≤2% everywhere.
- **Workspace verbalisation (word rule, pass@8).** Association **28.4%** vs J-lens 10.8%, corpus 8.8%, Patchscopes 2.9%, NLA 48.0%. Multi-hop **50.0%** vs J-lens 20.7%, corpus 31.5%, NLA 81.5%. Modulation **40.5%** vs J-lens 4.8%, corpus 2.4%, NLA 38.1% — MAEM's one clear win over NLA, and the one place it beats the layer-grid J-lens.
- **Automated SAE feature description (Delphi-style, 512 features, balanced accuracy).** MAEM top-16 exemplars **0.680** vs corpus windows **0.810** vs NLA exemplars **0.668**; fuzzing 0.644 / 0.775 / 0.639. The loss is entirely recall (TPR 0.375 vs 0.708) while specificity is higher (TNR 0.986 vs 0.912), i.e. exemplar-derived descriptions are "specific but narrow" — they accept only 38% of held-out positives against 71% for corpus windows.
- **Fluency/coherence (n = 400 held-out activations).** MAEM outputs are judged at least as coherent as the source passage in **67%** of pairs (net −0.16 [−0.21, −0.11]), beating the base model's own continuation (−0.35) and losing to real corpus text (+0.07); per-token log-likelihood under Gemma-4-31B is **+0.08 nats/token above** the source. NLA explanations sit at −0.93 to −0.97 net preference and −1.2 to −1.6 nats/token.
- **Out-of-distribution transfer.** Against a 10M-token search in each domain's *own* corpus, MAEM exceeds on **12 of 21** unseen languages/domains (Chinese +0.183, Russian +0.131, arXiv +0.136, Python +0.048, C +0.026, Rust +0.013), is inconclusive on 2 (Greek, shell) and **reversed on 7** (Hindi −0.033, Thai −0.049, Go −0.017, Haskell −0.013, SQL −0.015, Lean −0.061, Isabelle −0.089). Against the English training corpus it exceeds on all 21.

## Authors & Credibility

First author Celeste De Schamphelaere (MATS) has directly relevant prior work — LoRAcles (weight-space interpretability at scale, ICML 2026 MI workshop) — and the paper is clearly a continuation of that line. The author list mixes MATS, Arb Research, Charles University and **two Anthropic researchers (Euan Ong, Jiahai Feng, equal advising)**, which explains the access to a 27B subject model, two SAEs (131k and 2M features), and the compute for full-parameter SFT on 8M examples plus 300 RL steps. Ong is also a co-author of the NLA work being compared against, which cuts both ways: the NLA baseline is likely implemented competently (they document its exact LoRA ranks, EV ≈ 0.75, and inference settings), but it is also a baseline the authors have a stake in. Neel Nanda is acknowledged. The engineering transparency is unusually high for this genre: hyperparameter tables down to the CISPO truncation value, a documented SAE data-stream bug ("the 2M dictionary saw ~125M unique tokens eight times rather than 1B distinct tokens"), released code and weights, and per-arm null controls.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty is real but narrow.** The contribution is an objective, not an architecture: SFT-init-from-subject-weights, norm-matched injection after block 2, and the ScaleRL/CISPO recipe are all borrowed. What is new is rewarding the generator with the subject's own forward pass on its own output, and conditioning on an arbitrary direction rather than an observed activation. That is a clean, well-motivated delta — but readers should not expect a new method for *reading* activations so much as a new *training signal* for existing inverters.

**The biggest missing ablation is the one the title implies.** The paper's framing is "scaling activation inversion" via RL, yet there is **no SFT-only checkpoint evaluated at matched compute**. The only non-RL reference is the untrained base (0.1914 with injection), which conflates SFT, RL, prompt format and sampling all at once. So the claim "the RL objective is what buys the fidelity" is untested — SFT on 8M activation–text pairs plausibly does most of the work, and RL's marginal contribution over it is unmeasured. The authors admit this in Limitations and in Appendix H.7 ("the result is not attributed to RL"), which is honest, but it means the headline mechanism is asserted rather than isolated.

**"Beats corpus search on a majority of held-out domains" is technically true and rhetorically generous.** 12 of 21 exceed, 2 are inconclusive, and **7 reverse** — including Lean (−0.061) and Isabelle (−0.089), the two domains where the method is worst and where the margin is *not* narrow. Several "wins" are within noise-adjacent territory (Rust +0.0134, C +0.0262, SQL −0.0153). The genuinely strong claim is the one buried in the appendix: MAEM beats the *English* corpus on all 21 arms, which mostly reflects that an English corpus is a bad baseline for Thai. The paper does not run the obvious control — a MAEM trained on the target domain — and says so.

**The GCG/EPO comparison is a strawman dressed as a fair fight.** GCG's strings score an NLL of 13.22, i.e. random-token level, so beating them proves little about discrete optimisation as a family. Worse, both were optimised against the **uncentred** objective and only rescored centred afterwards; the paper concedes "these rows do not bound one". Combined with n = 64 and a single seed per target, this table supports an efficiency claim (82×/280× cheaper) far better than a quality claim.

**The SAE-description result is the paper's most interesting negative, and it is slightly over-diagnosed.** MAEM exemplars lose 0.13 balanced accuracy to corpus windows, and the authors attribute this to diversity (Vendi 2.19 vs 2.87). But their own diversity-selection arms (M-JAC 0.694, M-COS 0.690 vs M-TOP 0.680) barely move the needle, and they admit those arms "vary diversity within the draws and do not test" the hypothesis that one feature's 64 draws cover only one facet. Two confounds are also unaddressed: the test items are **corpus windows**, the exact population C16's examples come from (the reverse test is not run), and no 32-token corpus arm exists to control for text length. The null arms are a genuinely good touch — but note that the judge null (0.811) is essentially identical to C16 (0.810), so a re-scoring of the same corpus arm moves the metric by as much as the entire MAEM–NLA gap; the 0.13 corpus advantage survives that, the fine-grained ordering among generated-text arms does not.

**The backdoor headline hides a bimodal result.** "Recovers the payloads of LoRA backdoors more often than corpus search" is driven entirely by thematic triggers; on three-digit codes the method gets **0%** on read directions and 21% on write, against a 5% corpus baseline. A reader of the abstract alone would not learn that MAEM effectively cannot invert non-semantic triggers — which matters, because real backdoors are often arbitrary strings.

**Two internal inconsistencies and one objective mismatch.** (i) The abstract and contribution list say MAEM exceeds the corpus peak on **30%** of SAE features; Figure 6's caption says **31%**. Minor, but in a paper this careful about CIs it is a tell that numbers were not re-derived from one source. (ii) Training rewards the **uncentred** cosine over the last 16 tokens; evaluation uses the **centred** score over all tokens. The authors flag this ("evaluation does not score exactly what training optimised"), but it is not a small thing: the untrained base with injection scores 0.19 largely on the shared mean component that centring removes, which suggests the training signal is partly rewarding a global direction rather than the target's idiosyncratic part. (iii) No KL term and only a 2.5e-4 length penalty, with the admission that "how far the objective can be pushed before text degrades is untested" — for a method whose entire selling point is resistance to optimisation pressure, that is the experiment one most wants.

**Single-everything.** One subject model (Qwen3.6-27B), one read layer (42), one pair of SAEs, one RL checkpoint, one judge family per task. The workspace result in particular is confounded by an interpretive quirk the authors report themselves: MAEM names the topic **57.1%** of the time after a bare mention but only **23.8%** after an explicit "focus on" instruction, which suggests it reads *what is salient in the prompt*, not *what the model is targeted at* — precisely the distinction the J-lens comparison is meant to adjudicate.

## Strengths

- **The verifiability argument is genuinely good.** Every exemplar is a sufficient condition for the direction and costs one forward pass to check. That is a real conceptual advance over description-based metamodels, whose faithfulness must be argued rather than measured, and it is the right property to optimise if metamodels are to be scaled.
- **Statistical hygiene above the norm for interpretability papers.** Contamination filtering with a pre-registered 7-gram threshold (486 of 512 targets survive), document-clustered bootstrap CIs, Holm-corrected paired sign-flip tests, an unbiased best-of-k estimator, and explicit null arms for both the draw and the judge in the SAE experiment.
- **Token-matched baseline construction.** Justifying NLA best-of-2 against MAEM best-of-8 via 176 vs 47.3 mean generated tokens is exactly the kind of comparison most papers skip, and it makes the 0.21 cosine gap hard to dismiss as a sampling artefact.
- **Negative results are reported with the same care as positive ones.** The failure taxonomy (whitespace-position features 56.8% failure, formatted digits 40.4%, induction 40.9%, list/table 25.2%, topic 1.6%) and the finding that fine-tuning on failures fixes 26 features while breaking 304 are more useful to the field than another benchmark win.
- **The safety application is concrete and cheap.** Reading a rank-1 LoRA trojan's payload from its write direction at 85% versus 2% for corpus search is a decisive, actionable result — and the mismatch controls (≤2%) rule out the obvious artefact.
- **Honest cost accounting.** 3.35 H200-seconds per target for 64 draws, with the caveat that the separate scoring pass is excluded, is the kind of number that lets a reader decide whether to adopt the method.

## Limitations

- **No SFT-only ablation at matched compute**, so the RL objective's marginal value over supervised initialisation is unknown; the paper explicitly declines to attribute its SAE-description result to RL.
- **One subject model, one layer, one SAE family, one checkpoint.** No cross-architecture transfer (Llama, Gemma), no test of whether a MAEM initialises an NLA or an activation oracle well.
- **Fails on ~11% of SAE features at best-of-8 and 6.5% at best-of-64**, concentrated in the rare tail (rarest decile fires 42%) and in structural/positional features (list/table 25.2%, template 37.7%), where the direction apparently does not convey the context the feature needs.
- **Sample diversity is low** (Vendi 2.19 vs 2.87 corpus, 3.39 for an LLM writing from corpus windows), which costs recall in downstream descriptions (38% vs 71% of held-out positives accepted) and is not fixed by diversity-based selection of draws.
- **Numerical/arbitrary triggers are essentially unreadable** (0% read, 21% write), so the backdoor result does not generalise to non-semantic payloads.
- **No KL term, a token-length-only penalty, and untested reward-hacking headroom** — the objective could in principle be driven to degenerate high-cosine text, and the paper does not probe where that boundary is.
- **Train/eval objective mismatch** (uncentred reward over the last 16 tokens vs centred score over all tokens) leaves open how much of the learned behaviour is alignment with the shared mean direction.
- **Training cost is not amortised in the paper.** Producing the checkpoint needs full-parameter SFT on 8M examples plus 300 RL steps with 16,384 rollouts each; the cheap per-target inference only pays off at scale, and no break-even analysis is given.
- **No ethics/dual-use statement** despite releasing a method for reading fine-tuned backdoor payloads and for generating text that drives arbitrary internal directions.

## Relevance

直接落在机制可解释性的核心问题上：把"方向 → 语义"的读取从"让模型描述"改成"让模型生成能点亮该方向的文本"，并用主体模型的前向传播做可验证奖励——这与 SAE 特征解释、steering vector 分析、模型内部表征审计（包括后门/微调权重 diff 的读取）都直接相关。对关心"metamodel 能否规模化、其解释是否可信"的读者，这篇的方法论（无标签奖励 + 前向可验证）和它的负面结果（结构性特征点不亮、多样性不足导致描述召回差）同样有价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. What does an **SFT-only** checkpoint score on REALACT at matched compute? Without this, how much of the +0.19 over corpus search is attributable to RL rather than to supervised activation→text pairs?
2. Would training on the **centred** cosine (rather than rescoring centred at eval) change fidelity, diversity, or the failure profile on rare features? The current mismatch makes the learned direction partly the corpus mean.
3. How far can the reward be pushed before text degenerates? A sweep over RL steps / learning rate / length-penalty weight, with a human or LLM naturalness audit at the frontier, would test the "verifiable objective resists optimisation pressure" claim directly.
4. Does adding a **diversity term** (Vendi, DPP, or an explicit coverage reward over a feature's corpus windows) close the SAE-description recall gap — and does the paper's "one facet per feature" hypothesis survive a test where the reverse evaluation (exemplars as test positives) is also run?
5. Why do **positional and structural features** fail? Is the information absent from the direction, or does the MAEM lack a way to condition on structure (e.g. feeding preceding tokens, or a template tag)? The LLM-from-corpus-windows arm firing 95.3% suggests the features are expressible, so this looks like an interface problem worth solving.
6. Does the checkpoint transfer **across architectures** — a MAEM trained on Qwen3.6-27B applied to a Llama or Gemma direction of the same dimensionality, or retrained cheaply per model? And is a MAEM a useful initialisation for an NLA or activation oracle, as the "pretraining task for metamodels" framing predicts?
7. Can the method be extended to **multi-token or subspace** directions (rank-k LoRA, SAE decoder subspaces), where a single vector may under-determine the concept?
8. What is the actual **break-even scale** for the training cost — at how many directions per subject model does a MAEM beat 10M-token corpus search on total compute?
