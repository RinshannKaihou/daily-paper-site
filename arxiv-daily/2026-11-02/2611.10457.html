02

Method

Read the value, then move the activation along it

Four moves

No chain-of-thought elicited; every readout comes from the input decision prompt.

1

Frame the decision

A POMDP: 5×5 Grid with four visible neighbours, 1,400 maps, 24 steps; WebShop with 1,502 tasks, 15 steps. Three models, activations taken before any action token.

2

Define value, two ways

Grid: negative shortest-path distance on the currently observed map — exact. WebShop: expected return from 40 on-policy continuations — noisy and policy-dependent, on purpose.

3

Read it out

A linear probe ranks candidate actions. On WebShop it is a pairwise classifier on Δh, not a regressor — the model is not assumed to estimate absolute value.

4

Transport it back

NVT fits Bℓ mapping value-coordinate differences to hidden-state differences on natural counterfactual pairs, then injects d = Bℓ δv. Controls: same-norm probe, shuffled NVT, random.

03

Strengths vs. limits

The diagnosis survives; the practical claim is thinner

+ Holds up

  • One 2×2 table does the work. The near-empty off-diagonal is hard to explain away as probe noise.
  • Three norm-matched controls separate direction from magnitude, not just “steering did something.”
  • NVT is well-motivated. Fitted on the model's own natural counterfactuals, so the edit lies in a subspace it actually uses.
  • Negative results reported. Invalid actions rise for two WebShop models (0.0→1.0%, 0.1→0.4%) and are printed anyway.

− Press on

  • The Grid target may be read off the prompt. The map is listed in text; in-context graph search is not ruled out, only Manhattan distance.
  • No cheap baseline in the loop. No reflection prompt, reranking, or sampling change — the bar NVT must clear is never tested.
  • Tiny behavioral sample. 100 Grid maps, one rollout each, no CIs; the headline jump is 16 episodes.
  • Hand-tuned per model. Layers span 15–51 with no selection rule; α = 4 in one setting and 12.5 in five.
04

Open questions

What would move this from diagnosis to tool
  1. Withhold or corrupt the Grid prompt's spatial memory — does the probe still beat Manhattan?
  2. Does a nonlinear probe widen the dissociation, or does a nonlinear transport close it?
  3. Does the policy-wrong / probe-right rate predict trajectory-level failure, not just single decisions?
  4. How does NVT compare with simply reranking top-k actions by the probe's value?
