> **一句话总结：** 作者指出模拟存算一体（CIM）阵列的误差取决于"同一计算在阵列上如何被表示"，于是用**离线等距重参数化**（输入去均值 + regular Hadamard 输出旋转 + tile 局部输入旋转）在不重训练、不改变硬件的条件下把 ViT-B/16 在 ReRAM/6-bit ADC 上从 37.4% 恢复到 82.9%、Phi-4 从 27.0% 恢复到 72.2%（均距浮点 2.2 个百分点以内）。

> **TL;DR:** Analog CIM arrays with shared, static per-array calibration destroy pretrained accuracy (ViT-B/16 drops 84.5% → 37.4% on ReRAM with 6-bit ADCs, one day after programming; Phi-4 74.4% → 27.0%). This paper shows that the damage depends on the *representation* of each layer, not only on the hardware, and fixes it offline by centering inputs, applying block-diagonal **regular** Hadamard output rotations (R·1 = 1, so shared reference error stays spread instead of concentrating), and applying input rotations **inside each tile** so per-tile partial sums are unchanged. No retraining, one weight scale and one ADC range per array. On ReRAM/6-bit it recovers ViT-B/16 to 82.9% (−1.6 vs FP) and Phi-4 to 72.2% (−2.2 vs FP); on ImageNet ResNet-50/ReRAM it reaches 72.8% vs 72.4% for reproduced hardware-aware training (Rasch et al. 2023), and on LLMs it lands 2.7 pts behind Analog Foundation Models on Llama-3.2-1B (42.8% vs 45.5%). Added digital work is <1% of model-execution operations. Worth reading if you care about low-bit inference on NVM arrays; the caveat is that everything is simulated in AIHWKit and attention stays digital.

## 核心贡献

- **把"模拟误差"重新表述为"表示问题"**：论文将 CIM 的误差拆成三类物理机制——DAC 输入量化/截断、ADC 输出量化/截断、器件电导扰动（编程噪声、读噪声、漂移）——并指出同一个理想计算在阵列上的不同等价表示会显著改变这三类误差的大小。
- **提出一套离线、免重训练的等距重参数化**：输入去均值（把 `Wμ` 折进 bias，把静态分量移出模拟通路）+ regular Hadamard 输出旋转（降低 ADC 输入的峰均比）+ tile 局部 Hadamard 输入旋转（降低权重峰值，从而压低 weight mapping scale）。
- **两个针对模拟阵列（而非数字量化器）的关键设计**：其一，用满足 `R·1 = Rᵀ·1 = 1` 的 **regular** Hadamard 块，使参考列/共模误差在旋转与逆旋转后仍然分散（普通随机符号 Hadamard 反而比不旋转更差）；其二，输入旋转**限制在 tile 内**，保证每个 tile 的模拟部分和与未旋转时完全相同，因此已校准的 ADC range 依然有效。
- **覆盖 CNN / ViT / LLM（最大 14B）× PCM / ReRAM × 4–8 bit ADC 的系统评估**，并首次把这类方法与已发表的硬件感知训练（HWA、AFM）在同一设置下对齐比较，证明"不训练权重也能逼近 HWA"。
- **给出工程可行性证据**：每阵列仅需一套静态权重 scale 与 ADC range（无需昂贵的逐列参考电路），数字开销 <1% 总运算，且因漂移补偿增益在层内趋于一致，阵列刷新间隔可显著拉长。

## Key Contributions

- **Reframes analog CIM error as a representation problem.** Three distinct error mechanisms (DAC input quantization/clipping, ADC output quantization/clipping under a shared per-array range, and conductance perturbation amplified by the weight mapping scale) are each shown to depend on how the identical layer computation is laid out on the array.
- **A retraining-free isometric reparameterization** built from three pieces applied in a fixed order: input centering with the `Wμ` term folded into the bias, block-diagonal regular-Hadamard output rotations, and tile-local Hadamard input rotations.
- **Two analog-specific design rules absent from the digital-rotation literature.** (i) *Regular* Hadamard blocks (`R1 = Rᵀ1 = 1`) are required because a plain Hadamard concentrates a common-mode reference error into one channel on the forward pass and again on the inverse; measured on ViT-B/16 and Qwen3-1.7B fc1/gate projections, plain DHP blocks raise median ADC error *above* the unrotated baseline. (ii) Input rotations must not cross tile boundaries, since cross-tile mixing widens every tile's ADC range and inflates down-projection ADC error by 24% (Phi-3-mini) and 62% (Llama-3.2-1B), up to 12× in individual layers.
- **First head-to-head alignment with published hardware-aware training**: reproduced Rasch et al. (2023) for ResNets and Büchel et al. (2025) Analog Foundation Models for LLMs, matching their checkpoints, scoring, and noise levels.
- **Deployment-oriented evidence**: per-array (not per-column) calibration suffices, added digital cost is under 1% of execution operations, refresh can be deferred to five days, and the scheme also transfers to partially weight-stationary SRAM CIM.

## Methodology

**Setup.** A linear layer `y = Wx` mapped onto 512×512 fully weight-stationary NVM arrays, one weight mapping scale and one ADC range per array, static 8-bit DACs with one range per tensor, 4/6/8-bit effective ADC bits, PCM (IBM-derived, AIHWKit) and CMO ReRAM (Falcone et al. 2025 fits) device models read one day after programming. Layers wider than an array are tiled and their digitized partial sums accumulated digitally. Only static-weight linear layers go analog; attention matrix products, softmax, norms, RoPE, and the model's input/output layers remain digital.

**The identity.** For any offset `μ` and orthogonal `R_in`, `R_out`:

```
Wx = R_outᵀ [ (R_out W R_inᵀ)(R_in x − R_in μ) ] + Wμ
```

The array stores `W̃ = R_out W R_inᵀ`, the DACs encode `R_in x'`, and `R_outᵀ` plus the precomputed `Wμ` are applied digitally. This is exact in real arithmetic; it changes only which numbers see analog error.

**Step 1 — centering (§3.1).** `μ` is a static per-channel mean over calibration data, chosen to minimize `E‖x'‖²` in the analog path. Measured on ViT-B/16, ResNet-50, and Qwen3-1.7B, the static component holds 25–33% of `‖x‖²` for ResNet-50/Qwen3 and less for ViT-B/16; centering lowers local ADC error in all three.

**Step 2 — output rotation (§3.2).** `R_out` is block-diagonal with **regular** Hadamard blocks of the largest power of four up to 256 dividing the layer width (64 for the ResNets and the 384-wide DeiT-S layers). Equal-magnitude coefficients spread a concentrated output across channels, cutting the median peak-to-RMS ratio of ADC inputs roughly 4× on FFN down-projections and reducing NMSE under both MSE-calibrated and absmax range policies. Regular blocks are essential: with a *fixed nominal* reference, plain blocks collapse ViT-B/16 to 0.0% while regular blocks hold 82.7%.

**Step 3 — input rotation (§3.3).** Device error is `e = ΔW u = s ΔG u`, so lowering weight peaks lowers the shared scale `s` and thus the numerical impact of every conductance perturbation. A fixed plain Hadamard `R_in` mixes weights across input channels; combined with `R_out` it reduces median weight peak-to-RMS by 2.5× (ViT-B/16, 72 projections) and 2.9× (Qwen3-1.7B, 196 projections), and cuts device-only output NMSE from 26.74% → 2.38% (PCM) and 97.45% → 1.83% (ReRAM) on ViT-B/16. A scalar per-layer drift gain restores PCM's shared attenuation.

**Step 4 — tile locality (§3.4).** Input rotations are applied *within* each tile, so each tile computes the exact partial product `W_t x_t` and its ADCs quantize the same values as without rotation, preserving the calibrated ranges.

**Placement.** CNNs: BatchNorm folded into convolution, rotations applied to residual-block convolutions and projection shortcuts, with the input transform shared across kernel taps and a single inverse after the shortcut sum. Transformers: the SliceGPT/QuaRot invariance recipe, with V carried through attention and its inverse folded into the output projection; Q/K restored after their projection ADCs, before Q/K-norm and RoPE, with head-local transforms in ViTs and grouped-query replication across KV heads in LLMs. The opening chat-template token (63.3% of attention mass; layer-2 down-projection input 17,616 vs 9.0 for other tokens, a 1,958× ratio) is precomputed and cached, which alone cuts WikiText NLL from 5.4319 to 3.1018.

**Overhead.** Hadamard transforms use four-point add/subtract kernels (⅞ log₂b additions per element, no multiplications); centering adds one subtraction per input element; normalization folds into shifts or existing scales.

## Results & Highlights

- **ReRAM, 6-bit ADCs, one day after programming (headline).** ViT-B/16 ImageNet top-1 recovers from **37.4% (unmodified) to 82.9%**, against 84.5% floating point (−1.6 pts); Phi-4's four-task mean (MMLU/HellaSwag/ARC-E/ARC-C) recovers from **27.0% to 72.2%** against 74.4% FP (−2.2 pts). With 6- and 8-bit ADCs the method stays **within 4.2 pts of FP for every model**, and dropping to 4-bit ADCs costs only a further **2.3 pts on average**.
- **Against digital PTQ applied directly to the arrays (4-bit ADCs, Table 11).** On Phi-4/ReRAM the unmodified model scores 31.5% and **QuaRot drops it to 26.6%**, while the proposed method reaches 70.9% (FP 74.4%). On Qwen3-8B/PCM: 23.9% unmodified, 47.7% QuaRot, **62.3% ours**, FP 70.7% — QuaRot trails by up to 44.3 pts overall.
- **Against hardware-aware training, ResNets, 8-bit ADCs, reproduced Rasch et al. 2023 (Table 4).** ResNet-50/PCM: 61.3±3.1% unmodified, **74.7% HWA**, 73.7±0.1% ours. ResNet-50/ReRAM: 58.1±1.9% → 72.4% HWA, **72.8±0.1% ours**. ResNet-18/ReRAM: 44.9±3.3% → 65.1% HWA, **65.6±0.2% ours**. So the gap to HWA is −1.0 pt on PCM ResNet-50 and roughly a wash (or a small win) on ReRAM.
- **Against Analog Foundation Models, matched k=1.10 programming noise (Table 5).** Phi-3-mini: 72.9±0.9% unmodified → **79.7% AFM** vs 78.4±0.5% ours (−1.3). Llama-3.2-1B: 36.4±1.6% → **45.5% AFM** vs 42.8±1.5% ours (−2.7). At the nominal noise level (k=1.00) the method scores 79.3% and 44.5%.
- **Ablation shows the output rotation does almost all the work — until it doesn't (Fig. 10, Fig. 17).** ViT-B/16 on PCM/4-bit: 5.0% unmodified, **+73.5 pts from output rotations alone**, +1.3 from input rotations, −0.3 from centering, 79.5% total. ResNet-50 on PCM/4-bit is the opposite: 11.2% → +51.5 (output) → +3.7 (input) → **+5.5 (centering)** → 71.8%. Leave-one-out on ResNet-50/PCM confirms centering is worth **7.0 pts** (72.0% → 65.0% without it) whereas on ViT-B/16 it moves accuracy by under 0.5 pts in either direction.
- **Cheap calibration is enough (Fig. 11).** With one weight scale and one ADC range per array, the method matches or beats the unmodified model running per-column settings by **up to 6.0 pts**; adding per-column settings on top buys at most 7.1 pts. Against NORA (an analog-targeted baseline that needs per-column settings *and* a dynamic DAC) it stays **within 1.6 pts** with static per-array calibration, and exceeds NORA when also given per-column settings.
- **Refresh interval and drift (Fig. 12).** Programmed and calibrated once at 1 s and read up to five days later, the vision models lose at most **1.7 pts**, while AdaBS calibrated once at 1 s falls to **7.2% (PCM) and 42.7% (ReRAM)** on ResNet-50; even when AdaBS is recalibrated at read time it stays 5.2 and 13.5 pts below. Drift-gain spread within a layer drops from a 5.7%/21% median/max (unmodified) to 0.66%/2.8%, which is why one gain per layer suffices.
- **Cost side.** The most accurate configurations add **less than 1% additional operations** during model execution; total digital share sits around 19.4% for Qwen3-1.7B, 5.5–5.9% for ViT-B/16, and 4.2–4.8% for ResNet-50 on ReRAM/8-bit at five days.
- **Aggressive converter case (Fig. 14).** With 4-bit DACs *and* 4-bit ADCs the method keeps 74.1–74.3% on ViT-B/16 (FP 84.6%), 61.0–61.2% on ResNet-50 (FP 76.1%), and ~31 WikiText-2 perplexity on Qwen3-1.7B, where the unmodified models collapse.
- **Beyond NVM (Fig. 15).** On a simulated partially weight-stationary SRAM CIM (144×8 arrays, PICO-RAM-style), per-tensor calibration leaves the unmodified models below 2% while the method reaches 71.3–79.4%; on ResNet-50 it beats the *unmodified* model's per-array fully-weight-stationary result by 28–35 pts.

## Authors & Credibility

George Karfakis and Puneet Gupta, Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering, UCLA. Gupta leads a well-known architecture/EDA group with a long record in design-technology co-optimization, and the paper's citation base (AIHWKit, Rasch et al., Ambrogio et al., Le Gallo et al., QuaRot, SpinQuant, Büchel et al.) is the right one for this subfield. The work is simulation-only but built on the community's standard simulator (AIHWKit at commit `eb089df`) with published device fits, and the authors go to unusual lengths to make the HWA/AFM comparisons fair — including disclosing that they had to raise programming noise by 10% (k=1.10) to reproduce Büchel et al.'s noise-only baseline, and reporting both noise levels in Table 7. That kind of disclosure is a credibility signal rather than a red flag. No code or artifact link is provided, which is the main reproducibility gap.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** Centering plus Hadamard rotations is a crowded space (OS+, HadaNorm, BASE-Q, QuaRot, SpinQuant, QuaRot-style invariance recipes), and the paper says so. The genuinely new content is analog-specific and narrow but real: the *regular* Hadamard requirement (`R1 = 1`) motivated by common-mode reference error, and the *tile-local* input rotation motivated by per-tile ADC conversion. The regular-block result is the strongest single piece of evidence in the paper — with a fixed nominal reference, plain blocks give 0.0% and regular blocks 82.7% on ViT-B/16/ReRAM-8bit (Table 8), a 82.7-point swing from a sign-pattern choice that the digital quantization literature has no reason to care about. The tile-locality result (24%/62% ADC-error inflation, up to 12× in single layers) is similarly concrete. Beyond those two rules, the contribution is careful engineering and evaluation rather than a new principle.

**Rigor.** Good where it counts: reproduced baselines in the baselines' own settings, multiple seeds for the HWA/AFM tables (3 and 10 respectively), leave-one-out ablation, a noise-strength sweep (k = 0.5…2), an IR-drop check (accuracy moves <0.4 pts even at 2× default wire resistance), and an explicit effective-precision table (≈4.2 bits at one day for PCM, 4.06 for ReRAM 0.2%). Weaker elsewhere: the main ViT/LLM sweeps in Figures 8 and 9 appear to be single-seed, and the ablation uses one seed on 4,898 images. Only the ResNet tables report ±SD, and those are suspiciously tight (±0.1) — plausibly because the rotations remove seed-dependent scale variance, but that claim is not demonstrated.

**Baseline fairness — mixed.** The comparison set is broad (CLE, AdaBS, OS+, RepQ-ViT, WPO, QwT, QuaRot, NORA, HWA, AFM), but several of the "collapse" baselines are digital PTQ methods (OS+, RepQ-ViT, SpinQuant) evaluated on shared-range analog arrays they were never designed for; their failure is unsurprising and slightly inflates the apparent margin. The authors partly compensate by including WPO (which needs four devices per weight — a real hardware cost penalty they note) and NORA, and by reporting NORA's advantage under per-column settings honestly. Two asymmetries remain worth flagging: (i) Table 4's HWA comparison grants *both* methods per-column weight mapping scales, which is looser than the paper's own headline per-array claim, so it is not the same operating point as Figure 8; (ii) the AFM headline "within 2.7 pts" depends on the hand-tuned k=1.10 noise scaling — at nominal k=1.00 the Phi-3-mini gap is 0.4 pts (79.3 vs 79.7) but the Llama gap widens to 4.2 (44.5 vs 48.7 implied), so the direction of the adjustment is not uniformly conservative.

**Data quirks.** Vision calibration uses **1,024 ImageNet validation images** (evaluation uses the remaining 48,976) — standard practice in the PTQ literature but still a calibration set drawn from the eval distribution, which makes the "no training data needed" claim weaker than it sounds for a deployment scenario where the target distribution is unknown. LLM calibration uses ~8,192 held-out tokens per task, i.e., task-specific calibration data, which is a stronger requirement than a single generic calibration corpus.

**"Unmodified arrays" is not quite true.** The main ReRAM setting subtracts a **programmed reference column that ages with the weights**, not AIHWKit's default fixed reference — a hardware addition (one reference column per array) that the authors justify because the unmodified model collapses to 0.1% under the fixed reference. Table 8 reports both, which is honest, but the abstract's "simple analog arrays" framing glosses over it. Likewise, PCM still needs a **per-layer probe readout at read time** to set the drift gain, so "no online reads" holds only for ReRAM.

**Significance.** For the analog CIM community this is a useful, practical result: it removes the retraining barrier that Büchel et al. explicitly named, at <1% digital overhead and with per-array calibration. For the broader ML-efficiency audience the significance is smaller than the framing suggests, because attention matmuls, softmax, norms, and the I/O layers all remain digital and no system-level energy/latency model is given — the 40–140× A100 efficiency figure is cited from prior work, not re-derived here. The remaining gap on small LLMs (Llama-3.2-1B at 42.8% vs 49.4% FP) is acknowledged rather than hidden, which is to the authors' credit but does bound the claim: "retraining-free" works well at ≥8B and only partially at 1–2B.

## Strengths

- The error-mechanism → representation-change mapping in Table 1 is a genuinely clarifying abstraction; each fix is motivated by a measured physical effect rather than borrowed wholesale from digital quantization.
- The regular-Hadamard finding is a crisp, falsifiable, and non-obvious result with a dramatic failure mode (0.0% vs 82.7%) that will save other groups from a silent bug.
- Tile-local input rotation is the right answer to a problem digital rotation papers never face, and the paper quantifies the cost of getting it wrong (up to 12× ADC error in a single layer).
- Honest, well-documented baseline reproduction, including the unflattering admission that their noise model had to be strengthened to match a published baseline.
- Breadth: 4 CNNs/ViTs + 5 LLMs, 2 device technologies, 4/6/8-bit ADCs, 4-bit DACs, SRAM CIM, read ages from 1 s to 5 days, plus IR-drop and noise-strength sweeps.
- Practical overhead accounting: per-array calibration instead of per-column, <1% added digital operations, longer refresh intervals — all three are deployment-relevant, not just accuracy points.

## Limitations

- **No silicon.** Everything is AIHWKit simulation with fitted PCM/CMO-ReRAM models; peripheral nonidealities (DAC nonlinearity, settling, parasitic capacitance, temperature, array-to-array process variation beyond the model) are outside the study.
- **Partial acceleration only.** Attention products, softmax, normalization, RoPE, and I/O layers stay digital, so no end-to-end speedup or energy number is produced; the authors explicitly defer system-level impact.
- **Small-LLM gap remains large** (Llama-3.2-1B −6.6 pts, Qwen3-1.7B visibly below FP), so the method's benefit scales with model size in a way that is observed but not explained.
- **Hardware add-ons:** a per-array programmed reference column for ReRAM and a per-layer probe readout for PCM's drift gain, both of which soften the "unmodified array" claim.
- **Calibration dependence:** 1,024 ImageNet validation images and ~8k per-task tokens; no study of calibration-set shift, which is exactly the failure mode that motivated AdaBS.
- **Single-seed main sweeps** for ViTs/LLMs; statistical significance of the 1–2 pt differences against AFM/HWA is asserted via SDs only for those specific tables.
- **No block-size ablation at fixed accuracy target**, no analysis of what happens when the layer width is not divisible by a power of four (the paper picks "largest power of four up to 256 that divides the width"), and no code release.

## Relevance

与"LLM 高效推理 / 低比特量化 / 存算一体硬件"这条线高度相关：它把 QuaRot 式的旋转不变性搬到模拟阵列上，给出了两个可直接复用的工程规则（regular Hadamard 块、tile 局部旋转），并说明"免重训练"在 8B 以上模型基本成立。对做量化或推理加速的读者，其中"表示决定误差"的分析框架比具体数字更有迁移价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. How much of the reported gain survives on real silicon — specifically, does measured DAC nonlinearity and array-to-array variation preserve the 4× peak-to-RMS reduction that the output rotation relies on?
2. Why does the method recover Phi-4 (14B) to within 2.2 pts but leave Llama-3.2-1B 6.6 pts short? Is it a weight-outlier statistic, an activation-outlier statistic, or simply that small models have less redundancy to absorb quantization error?
3. The regular-Hadamard constraint `R1 = 1` fixes the sign pattern; how much freedom remains for optimizing block signs against a *measured* error covariance rather than a nominal reference model?
4. Could the tile-local input rotation be relaxed to a tile-local *learned* rotation (still offline) for additional weight-peak reduction, and does that break the exact-partial-sum property?
5. What is the end-to-end latency/energy when attention stays digital — i.e., for a 14B model at batch 1, what fraction of decode time is actually on the arrays, and does the <1% digital overhead still hold once attention dominates?
6. Does the calibration-set sensitivity match AdaBS's known weakness, or do the rotations make the method robust to distribution shift as well as to drift? A calibration-shift experiment (e.g., calibrate on ImageNet val, evaluate on ImageNet-V2/R) would settle this.
7. How does the method compose with activation-sparsity or token-pruning schemes that change the input distribution at runtime, given that all DAC/ADC ranges are static?
