How it was measured

One model · one run · one schedule

Instrument before result

Every change is divided by a resampling noise unit.

Record
OpenCoder-1.5B, 35 checkpoints from 4.19B to 2.03T tokens at constant learning rate. Spacing is deliberately uneven: 19 pairs at 1k steps across the first 4% of tokens, then 15 checkpoints at 1.25× gaps. A 24-checkpoint annealing branch forks at 1.57T.
Instrument
188 Python prompt batteries; 169 pass a read-site gate. A construct’s profile is its mean standardised activation in role minus out of role across 147,456 MLP hidden units; the top 5% — 7,373 neurons — is its carrier set.
Noise unit
Every battery is drawn twice with different surroundings. The twins’ disagreement (0.84 overlap ceiling, 0.16 turnover) is subtracted from every reported change.
Rule
A settle needs a θ = 0.70 crossing, a point below before it, two points after, and agreement between ratio and fixed-step partners. Dates in the final two checkpoints are censored.

Departures return

57% of 18,034,712 departures come back, against 17% for matched controls (lift 3.3); median absence 10,000 steps. The set never reassembles.

Weights: a confined walk

Layer-12 matrices move 0.51–0.67 η√D per 1k steps; the free-walk fraction falls 0.65 → 0.30 as successive steps cancel.

Response shrinks, loss doesn’t

The raw response falls for 148 of 169 constructs while the loss stays flat — and the three stopping times do not line up.

The balance sheet

Method strong · scope narrow

Strengths

What earns trust

  • The twin battery is a real instrument. It turns “the set changed” into a measurement; without it, gap-dependence fabricates twelve settling dates at 1.527T.
  • Three-clock separation is falsifiable and surprising. Reliance rises while loss is flat — that reorganizes how circuit-formation timelines are read.
  • Process transparency is exceptional. Hashed pre-registration, public deviation log, a failed exploratory reading kept in the appendix, and a per-number manifest.
  • “Done” is calibrated three ways: 0.09 nats at forced positions, 0.55–0.59 against 0.571 nats of continuation entropy, and 2.5 against 5.9 nats at bound-name positions.

Limitations

What caps the claim

  • One model, one run, one schedule. No seed replication; Pythia-1.4B only checks that the battery survives a different tokenizer.
  • Behaviour rests on 16 of 169 constructs, at grammar-forced positions — the easiest predictions in the language, not where code competence lives.
  • The record starts at 4.19B tokens. Formation is never observed: at the first checkpoint, 167 of 169 constructs already carry half their profile in 5% of neurons.
  • The null weakens past ~500B for gradual settling, and the weight-walk result is read on layer 12 alone — the paper’s own check says single-layer verdicts are unstable.

Open questions

4 of 7
  1. Does “never settles” survive a second seed of OpenCoder-1.5B, or a different 1.5B code model at the same token budget?
  2. One corpus, two schedules: how much of the continued slowing at constant η would disappear under decay?
  3. Does a circuit-level intervention computed at 500B tokens still work when applied at 2T?
  4. At bound-name positions loss is still 2.5 nats against a 5.9-nat marginal — do carriers there settle, or is the null an artefact of readable positions?
