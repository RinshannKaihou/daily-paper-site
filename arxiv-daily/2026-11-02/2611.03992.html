01

Mechanism

A transform treated as a default becomes a per-piece decision.

A group of 32 values shares one scale — at INT2 that is 2.5 bits per value, and uniform-quantizer error grows with the group’s crest factor κ = ‖x‖∞ / rms(x). H32 preserves energy but can flatten a spike or manufacture one. So the encoder measures, per piece, instead of assuming.

  1. 01

    Decide per 32-channel piece

    Split each 128-channel head into four 32-channel pieces. Each keeps identity or takes H32; the whole head may instead take H128. Five flags total.

  2. 02

    Score what attention sees

    Minimise eᵀMe with M built from calibration queries, not ‖e‖². The naive criterion is worse than no selection at all (19.1 vs 18.4 KL).

  3. 03

    Signal in sign bits

    Scales are always positive, so the five flags ride in their sign bits. The packed 2.5-bit page stays at 80 B/token/KV head — the size of plain RTN.

  4. 04

    Rotate the query, never the key

    The read path transforms q into each candidate basis once per step, then selects partial dot products. Cached keys are never un-rotated.

Decision frequency follows the quantization axis. KIVI pools 32 tokens per group, so one fixed per-head decision keeps 78% of the KL gain at zero write cost; per-token caches need a fresh decision per key, and the flag still costs no bytes.

03

Trade-offs

What holds up, and what the paper itself concedes.

Strengths the case for

  • The premise is auditable.One real key shows H32 cutting error ×0.29 on one piece and raising it ×1.52 on another; the decile study turns that anecdote into a distribution.
  • Zero-byte metadata is the unlock.Flags ride in scale sign bits and survive a real vLLM plugin with paged INT2, CUDA graphs and prefix caching; served-to-simulated KL is 0.96–1.03.
  • The ablation indicts the obvious implementation.Selecting by reconstruction error gives 19.1 — worse than the 18.4 of doing nothing, which is exactly the trap a first implementer would fall into.
  • Breadth, with failures left in.Twelve fidelity models (eight held out), fifteen for the 4-bit study, and task runs spanning retrieval, long-doc QA, math and agentic coding; Gemma-3-27B and the 4-bit null sit in the main text.

Limitations the case against

  • Capability evidence is narrow.Four dense models at or below 32B, one engine (vLLM), two GPUs; no MoE in the task study and nothing larger than 32B.
  • Per-key decisions cost real time.+5% prefill and roughly +17% TPOT at batch 64. The fixed mode is often the better trade and on Qwen3-8B LongBench scores higher (+0.72 vs +0.42).
  • The strongest rival is never served.RotateKV at clip 1.0 reaches task results only in simulation, not through the vLLM path where the paper’s own numbers live.
  • 4-bit gains do not reach tasks.A 44–67% KL cut, yet 34 of 36 task intervals include zero; Gemma-3-27B needed a post-hoc per-layer guard to avoid a +15.6 KL regression.
04

Open questions

Four things the evidence leaves unresolved.

01

Would a per-layer on/off bitmask capture most of the gain, with no flags and no write cost?

02

Why does the fixed mode beat per-key selection on Qwen3-8B LongBench while losing on Llama and on KL?

03

Does the sign-bit flag survive TensorRT-LLM or a fused kernel with a different metadata layout?

04

What happens when weights are quantized too — or when context runs past 32k?
