> **一句话总结：** 作者在 22 个开源模型（0.5B–72B，四个家族）上构造 3,600 个"同一实体的两个不同是非题"配对，只改动模型自己上一轮的一个答案 token，发现"自我承诺"被写进每条问题各自的残差流方向、而这些方向高度共线（方向余弦相似度可预测耦合强度，ρ = 0.43–0.85）；在读出带内对共享方向 g 做均值消融可把耦合压到 ≤17%（随机方向仍保留 0.75–1.06），且若保留第一个 token（attention sink）不动，困惑度代价在 19 个指令微调模型中 18 个 ≤1.3%、MMLU 变化在 −3.5 到 +1.3 分之间——但读出这一方向的注意力头电路随规模与后训练剧烈漂移，Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 仅换系统提示就能让该电路符号翻转。

> **TL;DR:** Using 3,600 yes/no question pairs over 120 entities (30 directed pairs per entity × 3 independent entity folds), the author fixes a model's first answer to Yes or No and measures the log-odds shift κ on a second, different question about the same entity, across 22 open-weight models (0.5B–72B). The cosine similarity of the two questions' "commitment directions" predicts κ on held-out entities at ρ = 0.43–0.85 (partial ρ = 0.27–0.68 after controlling for question-text and pre-answer state similarity), and mean-ablating a single shared direction g across the readout band leaves at most 15% of κ (κ_g = −0.03 to 0.15) versus 0.75–1.06 for a random direction in the same layers — but only when the first-token attention sink is spared: doing so costs ≤1.3% WikiText-2 perplexity in 18 of 19 instruction-tuned models and moves zero-shot MMLU by −3.5 to +1.3 points, whereas ablating at all positions costs 1.44×–15.40× perplexity and up to −23.2 MMLU points. The write side is stable, the read side is not: top-10 heads carry 100%+ of κ below ~4B but 28–40% at 70–72B, and 96% of questions read g in the *negative* direction in Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507.

## 核心贡献

- **受控的因果测量范式**：把模型自己上一轮的答案固定为 Yes 或 No，两段对话仅差这一个 token，因此任何计算差异都可归因于该 token；在 22 个开源模型、0.5B–72B、四个家族（Qwen、Llama-3.1、Gemma-2、Mistral）、三个互不重叠的实体折上重复，共 3,600 个有向题对。
- **"单一方向"结构从拒答扩展到自我承诺**：每条答案在答案 token 处写下一个承诺方向 d_q = h(Yes) − h(No)，不同问题的方向高度共线；其余弦相似度在留出实体上预测耦合强度（ρ = 0.43–0.85），且随参数规模提升（19 个指令微调模型上的秩相关 0.70，p = 0.001）。
- **写—读解耦（核心新意）**：写侧几何在全部 22 个模型上都稳定预测行为，但读侧电路随规模与训练变化——承载耦合的注意力头数量随规模扩散（小模型 100%+ → 70–72B 的 28–40%），指令微调让更多问题以相反符号读取该方向（基座 0–21% → 指令微调 14–49% → Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 的 96%），并且仅更换系统提示就能让同一模型的头归因图反号（ρ = −0.31）。
- **可用的消除方法及其代价边界**：在读出带内对所有位置做均值消融，但**保留第一个 token（attention sink）**，可在 18/19 个指令微调模型上以 ≤1.3% 困惑度、−3.5 到 +1.3 MMLU 分的代价移除几乎全部耦合；同时诚实报告该方法在 Qwen2.5-0.5B、Qwen3-4B、Qwen3-1.7B 上会改变单独提问时的答案。
- **代价的可预测性**：g 与普通文本第一主成分的 cos² 能预测消融的困惑度代价（跨 19 个模型 ρ = 0.73，置换 p = 0.0006），而"消融把首 token 推离多远"（ρ = 0.01）或首 token 范数（ρ = 0.06）完全不能——说明代价来自 g 与文本主方向的重叠，而非对 sink 的扰动幅度。

## Key Contributions

- **A one-token causal design for self-commitment.** The two conversations for a directed pair (q1, q2) are identical up to and including the forced answer token; the paper verifies this for all 3,600 pairs in every run (100.0% one-token difference) and validates the interventions with two exactness checks — patching d_q from the embedding layer reproduces κ at share 0.996–1.011, and ablating d_q at the answer token at every layer removes it (share −0.003 to 0.004), both within 0.05 of target for every model, framing and seed.
- **Single-direction structure generalises from refusal to cross-question commitment.** Extends Arditi et al.'s "refusal is mediated by a single direction" result to a different behaviour, a multi-turn setting, and 22 models: one shared direction g, mean-ablated across a band, leaves at most 15% of the coupling in the 19 instruction-tuned models, while a random direction in the same layers leaves 0.75–1.06.
- **A write/read dissociation, which is the paper's genuinely novel claim.** Direction geometry predicts behaviour in every model; the circuit that reads the direction does not transfer. The share of κ carried by the ten top-ranked heads is 1.06–1.44 in the three smallest instruction-tuned models, in Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 and in all three base models, falls to 0.41–0.79 at 7–9B, and to 0.28–0.40 at 70–72B; ten random heads carry −0.01 to 0.10 throughout. The authors frame this as a caution against assuming small-model circuit findings generalise.
- **An intervention with a quantified cost budget, and a predictor of that cost.** Sparing the first token reduces Gemma-2-27B's perplexity blowup from 15.40× to 1.101× and its MMLU loss from −23.2 to −1.7 points; across models, cos²(g, PC1 of ordinary text) predicts the log perplexity ratio at ρ = 0.73 (p = 0.0006) while sink shift and first-token norm predict nothing.
- **Honest negative results inside the positive claim.** Sparing *all* massive-activation positions (norm > 10× median) is markedly *worse* than sparing only the first token (Gemma-2-27B: 2.61× vs 1.101×), so the effect is specific to position 0 rather than to large activations; and narrower bands / weaker ablation strengths do not escape the trade-off curve (13 models ≤7% perplexity, but Qwen3-32B 55%, Qwen3-1.7B 61%, Gemma-2-27B 3.9×).

## Methodology

**Stimuli.** 120 entities in four domains of 30 (activities, foods, animals, social groups), each with six yes/no question templates that apply to every entity in the domain ("Is chess a sport?", "Is chess mainly a hobby?", "Are most politicians honest?"). All 15 pairs of distinct questions per entity are asked in both orders → 30 directed pairs per entity, 3,600 in total. Entities are shuffled once with a fixed seed into three disjoint folds of 40 (10 per domain); seed *s* uses fold *s*, so the three seeds share no entities. Within a seed, entities are split at random into a selection half and a test half; 600 test pairs per seed.

**Conversation structure.** System prompt → q1 as user turn → forced answer a1 ∈ {Yes, No} as the model's own turn → q2 as second user turn, rendered with the model's chat template (thinking disabled for Qwen3). Three framings: survey respondent (primary), helpful assistant, and a True/False statement framing. Each framing has two system prompts differing only in the order of the answer words ("Yes or No" vs "No or Yes"), and every quantity is averaged over the two — a small but real control for answer-token order effects.

**Measurement.** ℓ = log Σ p(Yes tokens) − log Σ p(No tokens), summed over the first tokens of each answer word with and without a leading space and in either case. Coupling κ(q1,q2) = ℓ(q2 | a1=Yes) − ℓ(q2 | a1=No). Commitment direction d_q(l) = h_l(Yes; q) − h_l(No; q), estimated from q alone since the prefix contains only q.

**Geometry predictor and controls.** Spearman ρ between cos(d_q1(l*), d_q2(l*)) and κ on held-out pairs, with l* chosen to maximise |ρ| on the selection half. Partial correlation controls for (i) similarity of the two questions' text and (ii) similarity of the model's residual states before answering — a necessary control, since related questions about the same entity are obviously similar.

**Shared direction and ablation.** For analyses at l*, g is the normalised mean of d_q(l*) over questions from the *other three* domains (leave-one-domain-out), so a pair's own topic never contributes. For band interventions, g(l) is the normalised mean over selection-half entities only. Ablation replaces the projection of h on a unit direction u with a reference value m: h ← h − ((h·u) − m)u, applied in both conversations. Every causal effect is reported as a least-squares slope through the origin of edited-on-original κ, so 1 = edit reproduces the whole coupling, 0 = edit removes it.

**Circuit localisation.** Heads are ranked on the selection half by attribution patching (gradient of the answer log-odds in the No conversation × difference in head outputs between Yes and No conversations, summed over positions after the first answer, regressed on κ), then validated by patching the top-k heads' outputs from Yes into No on held-out pairs. A "read gain" measures how a question reads g: a fixed step of the median projection size along g is added at l* to the No conversation, and the induced change in ℓ(q2) is recorded.

**Cost accounting.** The intervention is applied at inference time, at every position of every forward pass, and costed on WikiText-2 test perplexity (256-token passages), zero-shot MMLU (logits over the four answer letters, helpful-assistant prompt), and the share of standalone questions whose answer sign is preserved. Main cost runs use 200 held-out pairs, 100 passages and 200 MMLU questions per seed; a leave-one-seed-out sweep over band width (0.9–0.1, 0.75–0.25, 0.6–0.4) and ablation strength (α ∈ {0.5, 0.75, 1}) checks whether a cheaper operating point exists.

**Models.** 22 open-weight models: Qwen2.5 (0.5B–72B), Qwen3 (0.6B–32B, including base/instruction pairs and the later Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 release), Llama-3.1 (8B, 70B), Gemma-2 (9B, 27B), Mistral (7B base and instruct). 19 instruction-tuned + 3 base. Five models also run in 4-bit NF4 as a quantisation check; 12 models up to 9B also run with random weights as a control.

## Results & Highlights

- **Geometry predicts coupling, and the prediction strengthens with scale.** Across all 22 models, held-out Spearman ρ between direction cosine and κ is 0.43–0.85 (Qwen3-0.6B 0.43 → Qwen3-32B 0.83; Llama-3.1-8B 0.70 → Llama-3.1-70B 0.85; Gemma-2-27B 0.79; Mistral-7B-Instruct 0.81). Across the 19 instruction-tuned models the rank correlation with parameter count is 0.70 (p = 0.001). Base models are systematically lower (0.45–0.61) than their instruction-tuned counterparts (0.72–0.81), and random-weight controls give |ρ| ≤ 0.13 in 10 of 12 cases — the two exceptions (Qwen2.5-3B at −0.31, Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 at 0.19) are still smaller in magnitude than every trained model.
- **One shared direction carries nearly all of the effect, and random directions do not.** Mean-ablating g at every position of the readout band leaves κ_g = −0.03 to 0.15 in the 19 instruction-tuned models, while ablating a random unit direction in the same layers leaves κ_rand = 0.75–1.06 (e.g. Qwen2.5-32B: 0.15 vs 0.98; Llama-3.1-8B: 0.02 vs 0.98; Mistral-7B-Instruct: −0.03 vs 1.00). In base models (200-pair subsample) ablating g leaves −0.15 to 0.15. Splitting the patch at l* into its component along the leave-one-domain-out g and its remainder shows the question-specific remainder carries only −0.03 to 0.16 — the shared component is doing the work.
- **The removal is not a saturation artefact.** Stratifying held-out pairs by the model's standalone probability p for its second answer, band ablation leaves κ_g = 0.06 on average in every confidence bin and at most 0.17 for any model in any bin, versus κ_rand = 0.99–1.00. Geometry is weaker away from saturation (ρ = 0.53 for p < 0.9 and 0.57 for 0.9 ≤ p < 0.99, against 0.75 for p ≥ 0.99), but the causal removal holds uniformly.
- **The reading circuit distributes with scale.** The ten top-ranked heads carry 1.06–1.44 of κ in Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B/3B, 1.24 in Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 and 1.06–1.34 in the three base models (knocking them out leaves *negative* coupling, −0.36 to −0.15, i.e. the remaining heads push the other way); 0.41–0.79 at 7–9B; 0.28–0.40 at 70–72B. Ten random heads carry −0.01 to 0.10 everywhere. The patch at l* shows the same trend: 0.91–1.21 in base models, 0.13–0.72 in instruction-tuned models at 14B and above.
- **Instruction tuning flips the sign of the reader.** A fixed positive step along g moves the second answer the *opposite* way for 0% of questions in base Mistral-7B and Qwen3-4B, 21% in base Qwen2.5-7B; in their instruction-tuned counterparts 49%, 14% and 43%; and 96% in Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507, which is a later post-trained release of the *same* Qwen3-4B base model. The sign is set by the reader, not the writer: read gain predicts a pair's coupling at ρ = 0.40–0.95 in 19 of 20 models measured, whereas how strongly q1 writes along g does not (ρ = −0.14 to 0.29).
- **A prompt alone can reverse the circuit.** Under the assistant framing the head-attribution map correlates with the survey framing at ρ = 0.65–0.86 for five of six models, but at ρ = −0.31 for Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507; its ten survey-framing heads carry −0.70 of the assistant-framing coupling, and the share of positively-coupled pairs jumps from 11.7% to 81.3%. Geometry still predicts κ in that model (ρ = 0.60) — the direction is intact, the reader is inverted.
- **Cost of the intervention is dominated by the attention sink.** Ablating at all positions is catastrophic for some models: Gemma-2-27B 15.40× WikiText-2 perplexity and −23.2 MMLU points, Qwen3-1.7B 1.86×, Qwen3-32B 1.81×, Qwen3-4B 1.44×, Qwen3-0.6B 1.15×. Sparing the first token drops Gemma-2-27B to 1.101× and −1.7 points, and gives ≤1.3% perplexity in 18 of 19 instruction-tuned models (Gemma-2-27B is the 10% exception) with κ_g still −0.03 to 0.14 and MMLU −3.5 to +1.3.
- **Sparing the sink is not the same as sparing large activations.** Sparing every position whose norm exceeds 10× the median is much less effective: Gemma-2-27B stays at 2.61× perplexity and −10.5 MMLU points, Qwen3-1.7B at 1.21×. The damage is specific to position 0, not to activation magnitude.
- **Cost is predictable from the direction's overlap with ordinary text.** Across the 19 instruction-tuned models, cos² between g and the first principal component of WikiText-2 activations predicts the log perplexity ratio at ρ = 0.73 (permutation p = 0.0006), followed by enrichment (variance along g relative to average variance per direction) at ρ = 0.56 (p = 0.013). Sink shift (ρ = 0.01) and first-token norm ratio (ρ = 0.06) predict nothing. Gemma-2-27B has by far the highest enrichment (62.9 ± 0.4 vs 0.9 ± 0.1 for a random direction; 1.8–22.8 in the others), which explains why it alone retains a 10% cost.
- **The trade-off cannot be optimised away.** Sweeping band width and ablation strength with leave-one-seed-out selection, the cheapest setting that leaves ≤20% of the coupling costs ≤7% perplexity in 13 models, but 17% in Qwen3-4B, 55% in Qwen3-32B, 61% in Qwen3-1.7B and 3.9× in Gemma-2-27B. Narrower band or weaker ablation simply moves models along the same coupling-vs-perplexity curve; only sparing the first token moves them off it.
- **Quantisation does not change the picture.** For the five models run in both bf16 and 4-bit NF4, held-out ρ differs by at most 0.09 and coupling remaining after band ablation by at most 0.03 (e.g. Qwen3-32B: ρ 0.83 vs 0.84, κ_g 0.07 vs 0.07).

## Authors & Credibility

Single-author preprint from the Department of Computer Science, University of Houston (Marcus Armstrong), submitted to cs.CL on 2 November 2026. There is no institutional lab, no large-team infrastructure, and no prior-art citation of the author's own related work, which makes this read as an independent, carefully scoped study rather than a lab flagship paper — and it is unusually well-instrumented for that category.

The methodological hygiene is the strongest signal of credibility. Every design choice that could produce a spurious correlation has an explicit control: leave-one-domain-out g so a pair's own topic never enters its own direction; selection/test splits on entities with l*, band, heads and g all chosen on the selection half; three disjoint entity folds as independent replications; random-weight models to check that the geometry is learned rather than architectural; a random-direction ablation with the same layer coverage; two system prompts per framing differing only in answer-word order; and a 4-bit replication of five models. The exactness checks (patch from embedding = 0.996–1.011, ablate everywhere = −0.003 to 0.004, all 3,600 pairs passing the one-token-difference test) are reported per model per framing per seed rather than asserted once.

Reporting is also candid in the places where it would be easy not to be. The paper states plainly that the intervention shifts standalone answers in two Qwen3 models (60% and 66% sign preservation), that Gemma-2-27B retains a 10% perplexity cost while every other model is under 1.3%, that Qwen3-32B's read-gain correlation is 0.12 against 0.40–0.95 elsewhere, and that no measured quantity predicts the MMLU change (|ρ| ≤ 0.39, p ≥ 0.097). The AI-use disclosure is specific about what Claude did (experiment code, text revision, figures) and what the author verified (every reported number against result files).

The main credibility caveat is scope rather than execution: the entire result rests on short binary judgments about 120 entities with *inserted* rather than generated first answers, and the paper says so in its Limitations. There is no external replication and no comparison to any non-mechanistic baseline, so the practical value of the finding for deployed systems is asserted rather than demonstrated.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The framing "commitment is written once and read differently" is a real contribution, and the specific dissociation — geometry transfers across all 22 models while the reading circuit does not — is, as far as the related-work section shows, unreported. It is a meaningful sharpening of the single-direction literature: Arditi et al. (2024) established one direction for refusal, but always in the same model; here the direction generalises and the circuit does not, which is the more useful lesson for anyone porting circuit findings. The Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 result is the paper's best single piece of evidence and is well chosen: same base model, later post-training, sign of the read circuit reversed (96% of questions read negatively, head-map ρ = −0.31 under a prompt swap). That is a genuinely surprising and hard-to-explain-away finding.

**Rigour.** High for a single-author preprint. The exactness checks, the leave-one-domain-out construction of g, and the random-direction and random-weight controls close the obvious loopholes. Two specific things deserve credit because they are the ones most authors skip: the confidence-bin stratification (Table 3), which pre-empts the "you only removed a saturation artefact" objection by showing κ_g = 0.06 in *every* bin including p < 0.9; and the massive-activation control, which shows that sparing positions with norm > 10× median is *worse* than sparing position 0 alone (Gemma-2-27B 2.61× vs 1.101×), correctly distinguishing "the sink" from "large activations".

**Weak points, specifically.**

- **The headline "removal" is measured on a 200-pair subsample, not the 600-pair test set.** Section 5 fixes 200 held-out pairs per seed for all cost numbers, and the base-model κ_g values (−0.15 to 0.15) come from that same smaller run. The 600-pair result is the one reported in Table 2; the two are not the same measurement, and the paper is not explicit that the "≤17% of the effect in every model" claim blends a 600-pair estimate for instruction-tuned models with a 200-pair estimate for base models.
- **"Preserves standalone answers" is a weak criterion.** The reported statistic is only whether the *sign* of the standalone answer is kept, and the paper itself notes that even where the sign is kept, standalone log-odds move by 0.2 to 5.6 on average — and in the two failing Qwen3 models the mean shift (10.8 and 8.6 log-odds) is close to their median standalone confidence. A direction that leaves the sign intact while moving log-odds by 5 nats has substantially changed the model's calibration, which matters more than sign for exactly the survey-simulation application the paper motivates.
- **No non-mechanistic baseline.** The obvious practical alternative to weight surgery is a prompt: instruct the model to answer each question independently, or to ignore its previous answer. The paper never tests whether a one-line system prompt removes as much coupling at zero perplexity cost. Without that comparison the intervention's value is unclear — it may be that the cheapest fix is behavioural and the ablation is only interesting as a scientific probe.
- **The coupling is never shown to change any answer's correctness.** The motivation is reliability and order-dependent evaluation, but the design uses forced Yes/No answers on opinion-style questions where there is no ground truth, and no downstream task is measured. The paper cannot say whether a κ of −7.4 log-odds (its own Llama-3.1-8B example) flips a wrong answer to a right one or merely reshuffles two defensible opinions.
- **Inserted, not generated, first answers.** The forced answer token is the *only* difference between conditions, which is what makes the causal claim clean — but it also means the "commitment" is a single token's identity, not a sampled response with its own confidence, hedging or phrasing. The paper acknowledges this; it nonetheless limits how far "self-commitment" generalises to real multi-turn transcripts where the earlier answer is a whole sentence with its own lexical content.
- **The reader claim is untested where it matters most.** Read gain is not measured for Llama-3.1-70B or Qwen2.5-72B (the two models where the head-distribution result is most interesting), so the "sign is set by the reader" conclusion rests on models up to 32B. Qwen3-32B is also a visible outlier at ρ = 0.12 on read gain, reported but not explained.
- **The base/instruction comparison rests on three pairs**, one of which (Qwen3-4B vs Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507) is not a matched pair at all — 2507 is a later post-trained release, as the paper's own footnote says. Treating it as an instruction-tuning contrast alongside Qwen2.5-7B and Mistral-7B-v0.3 conflates post-training with a version bump.
- **Cost is measured only on capabilities that do not need consistency.** As the Limitations concede, there is no evaluation on tasks where cross-turn consistency is the point (multi-step reasoning, agent state tracking). A method that removes coupling might also remove useful coherence, and nothing here rules that out.

**Significance.** The write/read dissociation is the durable contribution and should be cited by anyone doing circuit-level interpretability across model scales — it is a concrete counterexample to the assumption that a circuit found at 7B transfers. The ablation recipe is a useful probe but not yet a deployable fix: it requires per-model band selection, per-model sink handling, and it fails outright on two Qwen3 models. The paper is appropriately modest about this, and the Impact Statement explicitly says it does not recommend deployment without task-specific evaluation — a level of restraint that is rare and welcome.

## Strengths

- The experimental design is close to ideal for the question: one-token interventions with a verified identical prefix, so the causal attribution is airtight in a way that most steering papers are not.
- Breadth with discipline: 22 models, four families, 0.5B–72B, three disjoint entity folds, plus 4-bit and random-weight controls — and every headline number is a mean over seeds with s.d. reported in the appendix.
- The write/read dissociation is a genuinely new and useful empirical regularity, and the Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 prompt-swap result (head-map ρ = −0.31, positive-coupling share 12% → 81%) is a striking, specific demonstration.
- The attention-sink analysis is not just a trick to lower perplexity; the contrast with sparing all massive-activation positions (2.61× vs 1.101×) is a real mechanistic finding about where ablation damage comes from.
- Cost is characterised honestly and quantitatively, including the models where the method fails, and the cos²(g, PC1) predictor (ρ = 0.73) gives practitioners a way to know in advance whether ablation will be cheap.
- Limitations, Impact Statement and AI-use disclosure are specific and non-defensive.

## Limitations

- First answers are inserted, not generated; all questions are binary; the stimuli are six short templates per domain over 120 entities, not deployment conversations. Generated or graded answers may write the commitment differently, and the paper does not test this.
- No downstream task is measured, so the reliability motivation is not quantified: it is unknown whether removing coupling improves or degrades answer correctness, calibration, or multi-turn coherence.
- Read gain — the central evidence that the *reader* sets the sign — is correlational and is not measured at 70–72B, and is an outlier (ρ = 0.12) in Qwen3-32B.
- Base-model comparisons rest on three pairs, and one of them (Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507) is a later post-trained release rather than a matched instruction-tuned counterpart.
- The 70B and 72B models run only in 4-bit, and the cost analysis uses 200 held-out pairs per seed rather than the 600-pair test set used elsewhere.
- Standalone-answer preservation is measured only as sign retention; log-odds shifts of 0.2–5.6 nats occur even where the sign is kept, so "preserved" overstates calibration preservation.
- The intervention requires per-model calibration (layer profile, readout band, sink handling) and fails on Qwen2.5-0.5B, Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-1.7B (78%, 66%, 60% sign preservation); it is an analysis tool, not a drop-in fix.
- No comparison to prompt-level or decoding-level baselines that might remove the same coupling at zero perplexity cost.
- All results come from a single author's codebase with no independent replication; the supplementary suite is described as resumable and config-driven but is not independently validated here.

## Relevance

与"机制可解释性 + 多轮可靠性"这条线高度相关：它把"单方向"结论从拒答推广到跨问题的自我承诺，并给出一个明确的反例——电路层面的发现不能跨规模、跨后训练、甚至跨系统提示直接搬运。对做 activation steering / 表征工程、以及做 LLM 问卷模拟与顺序效应审计的工作，这篇的"写侧稳定、读侧漂移"框架和 sink-sparing 消融配方都值得直接复用；但如果目标是工程上消除顺序效应，还需先补上"提示基线"这一对照。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does a one-line system prompt ("answer each question independently of previous answers") reduce coupling as much as the ablation, and at what cost? Without this the intervention's practical value is undetermined.
- Does the commitment direction survive when the first answer is *generated* rather than inserted — and does its geometry still predict coupling when the earlier turn is a full sentence with hedges and qualifiers?
- Why does Qwen3-32B's read gain fail to predict coupling (ρ = 0.12) while every other measured model is 0.40–0.95? Is the fixed median-projection step too large for that model, or is the reader genuinely non-linear there?
- What exactly did the 2507 post-training change in Qwen3-4B to flip the sign of the read circuit for 96% of questions, and can that flip be predicted from the post-training data mixture?
- Does the same write/read dissociation hold for non-binary answers (Likert scales, free-form judgments), where "commitment" is not a single token?
- Is the shared direction g better understood as a "yes/no disposition" axis than as a commitment axis? The paper shows g carries general answer polarity (standalone log-odds shift 0.2–5.6), which would make "removal without cost" partly definitional — an experiment that ablates g and then re-measures coupling on *unrelated* question pairs would settle this.
- Can the cos²(g, PC1) predictor be used prospectively to decide, before running any ablation, which models are safe to intervene on — and does it hold for models outside these four families?
