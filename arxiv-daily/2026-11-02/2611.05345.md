> **一句话总结：** 作者指出投机解码"分布无损"的保证只对理想化的 target 成立：在真实低精度服务栈上，验证块（verify block）内 slot 0 与单 token 解码一样精确，但每往后一个 slot 数值噪声就涨约 4%（GLM-5.3 斜率 +0.0195 nats/slot，末槽比首槽脏 34%），且被验证写入 KV cache 的上下文在约 30 轮之后会出现 10–20% 的极端偏差超额；于是他们反向利用这个结构——用 DPO 把 draft 模型训成在 agent 的"决策句"处**故意提出 target 会拒绝的那一侧**，让关键推理 token 通过 residual sampling 落在新验证块的 slot 0，只训练 0.06%–0.28% 的参数量，就在 DeepSWE 上把 GLM-5.3-nvfp4 从 60.6% 提到 67.6%（+7.0，95% CI 2.6–11.4）、DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash 从 67.8% 提到 71.8%（+4.0，CI 跨零），代价是 ≤2% 的解码速度。

> **TL;DR:** Speculative decoding is usually sold as distribution-exact and therefore behavior-neutral, but that guarantee assumes an idealized target. Measuring two served 750B open-weight models (GLM-5.3 with an NVFP4/FP8-KV/DFlash2 setup, W=8; DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash with a DSPARK MoE draft, W=6) on 1,631 and 1,294 DeepSWE prompt positions with repeated identical forwards, the authors find slot 0 is as precise as single-token decode (0.473 vs 0.478 nats) while noise rises +0.0195 nats per slot on GLM-5.3 (≈4%/slot, last slot 34% worse, >2-nat shifts 3.2%→6.2%) and +0.0094 on DeepSeek (same ~4% relative rate). Writing a 9k-token reasoning turn into the KV cache through verification instead of decoding produces a 10–20% excess of >4-nat deviations after ~30 turns (ratio 1.16 in turns 51–70; replicate 1.20 [1.03, 1.40]). Because rejection sampling lets the draft choose *where blocks end*, they train the draft with reverse-direction DPO (β=10) on fork pairs to prefer the undesirable side at decision points (likely/risk framings for GLM-5.3; not-running-tests for DeepSeek), so the target writes those decisions from slot 0 via residual sampling. Held-out preference accuracy is 0.635 (GLM, adversarial) and 0.85 (DeepSeek, run-tests); on DeepSWE, GLM-5.3-nvfp4 goes 60.6%→67.6% (+7.0, 95% CI 2.6–11.4) and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash 67.8%→71.8% (+4.0, 95% CI −1.3–7.3, not significant), with NL2Repo-Bench 49.1%→55.0% and 65.0%→66.4% (p=0.011). Reversing the preference *hurts* (60.6→55.7), and 27 runs with unrelated preferences are null (67.5 vs 67.8, p=0.83) — the strongest part of the evidence. Accepted tokens per block barely move (2.28→2.28; 2.5→2.46), so the speed cost is ≤2%. Read it for the within-block precision measurement and the "draft as context-precision allocator" idea; discount the DeepSeek DeepSWE headline, which is inside noise.

## 核心贡献

- **推翻了"投机解码对行为中性"这一默认假设，并给出机制解释**：精确性保证只对"从 prefix 到分布的固定函数"成立；真实 target 用 NVFP4 权重、FP8 KV cache、稀疏注意力服务，logits 依赖于计算如何被切分，而验证前向把一整块 token 放进同一次 launch，块内每个 slot 都要 attend 到同一次 launch 里算出来的 KV 条目——于是 slot s 继承了前面所有 slot 的舍入累积。slot 0 只读缓存，所以和单 token 解码一样准。
- **首次（据作者所知）量化了"验证块内的精度梯度"**：用滑动窗口验证（固定 prefix 在显存中、只重复前向、R=4 次）把 prefill 方差排除掉，测得 GLM-5.3 均值噪声 +0.0195 nats/slot（95% CI [+0.0174, +0.0214]，约每槽 4%）、末槽比首槽脏 34%、>2 nats 偏移占比 3.2%→6.2%、>3 nats 从 1.1%→2.1%；DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash 绝对噪声约为其一半但相对增长率相同（+0.0094 nats/slot，CI [+0.0078, +0.0110]）。两种完全不同的 draft 架构（block-diffusion 与 MoE）复现同一模式。
- **把"验证写入的上下文"追踪到长程**：在 96 个 DeepSWE 任务上，把同一个 reasoning turn 分别通过验证块（强制接受）和单 token 解码写入 KV cache，再 teacher-forcing 后续最多 128k token（中位 95 个 assistant turn），每条 build 计分 6.38M token。结果是 >4 nats 的极端偏差在 30 轮之后出现 10–20% 超额（turns 51–70 比值 1.16；另一组独立 build 为 1.20 [1.03, 1.40]），而 ≤2 nats 的区间两条路径无法区分。
- **把 draft 从"加速器"重新定义为"上下文精度分配器"**：给出形式化论证——draft 无法改变输出分布（拒绝采样保证），但它控制块在哪里结束，Pr[reject] = TV(p, q)。因此在决策点被可靠拒绝的 draft 会把关键决策搬到最精确的 slot，同时不破坏任何 exactness 保证。
- **提出反向偏好训练（Adversarial Speculative Reasoning）**：用 target 自己当 judge 定位"决策句"（verbatim、≤35 词），构造 fork pair（两侧共享到决策点为止的全部 token），用 DPO **反向**训练——让 draft 偏好 target 应当避免的那一侧（GLM-5.3 上是 likely/risk/simpler 框架；DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash 上是"不跑测试就下结论"）。同时给出"draft 学得会什么、学不会什么"的边界：框架与行为可学（0.59–0.85），"决策是否正确"不可学（0.61/0.57，落在随机区间内，中位数 0.52/0.46）。
- **在两个前沿模型上验证端到端收益，且改动量极小**：只训练 2.15B / 753B（0.28%）与 449M / 749B（0.06%）的参数——分别是各自 draft 的 87% 与 3.2%——target 权重、服务栈、验证规则、采样参数、agent harness 全部不动。
- **一组罕见的负向对照**：把同一批 fork pair 的 chosen/rejected 对调（"正向 draft"）会让性能**下降**（GLM-5.3 60.6→55.7，DeepSeek 67.8→66.4），而用三种无关偏好训出的 27 次运行合并后与未训练 draft 无差异（67.5% vs 67.8%，Welch p=0.83）。这把"收益来自训练过 draft 这件事"这一替代解释排除掉了。

## Key Contributions

- **A measurement that separates distributional exactness from numerical precision.** The paper's framing is that speculative verification is "lossless in distribution but not neutral in precision." This is not a rhetorical distinction: §3.1's protocol (hold the prefix in memory, rerun only the forward decode/verify pass R=4 times on identical inputs) isolates forward-pass nondeterminism from the independent prefill that a separate request would reintroduce. 1,631 positions on GLM-5.3 and 1,294 on DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, drawn from DeepSWE prompts at 6k–24k context.
- **A mechanism for the gradient.** A token at slot s attends to KV entries of slots 0…s−1 that are computed *in the same launch* rather than read from cache; each repetition recomputes them with its own rounding, so slot s inherits the accumulated variation of every earlier slot. Slot 0 reads only cached entries, which is exactly why it matches single-token decoding (0.473 vs 0.478 nats on GLM-5.3).
- **A long-horizon consequence measured, not assumed.** Two builds of the same token sequence (A, A′ via blockwise verification with forced acceptance; B, B′ via single-token decode), compared through their own build-to-build deviations |A−A′| vs |B−B′| so that the comparison is not contaminated by the variance of both paths. 96 tasks, 6.38M scored assistant tokens per build, up to 128k further tokens teacher-forced.
- **The reframing of the draft as a control channel.** Pr[reject] = 1 − Σ_v min(p(v), q(v)) = TV(p, q). The draft cannot change what the target samples; it can change *where blocks end*, and therefore which tokens are committed from slot 0 of a fresh block versus the noisy tail of an old one. The paper notes this degree of freedom was previously treated as irrelevant because acceptance-rate-only drafts agree with the target straight through a decision.
- **Reverse-direction preference training at decision points.** Fork pairs share every token up to the committing sentence, so the shared prefix cancels in the DPO objective and the gradient comes only from what happens after the fork. Windowed reward: 32 tokens before the committing sentence through its end at weight 1, then a 128-token tail with linear decay (≈200 tokens total); for the run-tests preference, the first 512 tokens of the continuation at weight 1.
- **Deployment-level parameter accounting.** GLM-5.3 DFlash2: train six transformer layers + final norm in bf16 (2.15B), freeze the feature projection, candidate selector, and the target's embedding/LM head; hidden states come from target layers 5, 19, 33, 47, 61, 75. DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash DSPARK: port the inference-only MoE kernels to differentiable PyTorch (matching reference kernels to 1e-7 per expert, 3–5% per stage output), train attention projections/norms/hyper-connections/attention sinks/bigram head (449M) with FP8 quantization-aware training (32×32 blocks, power-of-two scales), freeze the 13.6B FP4 routed experts, router, and input projection.
- **A falsification battery.** Preferred-direction drafts hurt; unrelated-preference drafts are null; the "decision right vs wrong" preference is shown to be unlearnable by a 14B draft and produces no effect even when partially learned — which the paper uses to argue the gain tracks *the specific behavior behind the failures*, not the act of training.

## Methodology

**Part 1 — Characterizing context precision (§3).** Two served configurations on SGLang over 4× NVIDIA B200: GLM-5.3 with NVFP4 weights, FP8 KV cache, DeepSeek sparse attention, and a DFlash2 block-diffusion draft with W=8 verify positions; DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash with a DSPARK draft proposing 5 tokens per step (W=6). Slots are indexed s = 0…W−1, where slot 0 holds the last committed token. The error metric compares |Δ log p| for the four most likely tokens under a reference pass plus the token the trajectory actually emitted, over R=4 repeats; differences are in nats (1 nat ≈ 2.7×, 2 nats ≈ 7.4×). Reporting is the mean plus the fraction exceeding 1/2/3 nats, because the agents sample at temperature 1 with top-p 0.95–1.0 and a 1–2 nat shift moves the emitted token inside vs outside the nucleus in 12% of cases, 2–3 nats in 47%, >3 nats in >84%.

For the long-horizon study, the longest-reasoning assistant turn of each GLM-5.3 DeepSWE trajectory (median 9k tokens, after a median 45k-token prefix) is written into the KV cache two ways — (A) through DFlash blockwise verification with every source token forced and accepted, (B) through single-token decoding — with identical token sequences, so only the computation that wrote the KV entries differs. The rest of the real trajectory is then teacher-forced and scored.

**Part 2 — Locating decision points (§4.2).** A judge (the target model itself, no stronger external model) labels each reasoning segment and returns the *committing sentence* verbatim, at most 35 words, which fixes the decision point in token space. Two preference families:
- *Decision frame* (GLM-5.3): principle- or evidence-framed decisions are correct ~70% of the time versus 36% for likely- or risk-framed ones in the trace audit. Preferred = principle/evidence; adversarial = likely/risk/simpler.
- *Run tests* (DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash): two thirds of its likely-framed sentences carry no lexical marker, and failures concentrate in decisions taken without validation. Preferred = the continuation's next tool call executes a test or script; adversarial = any other command.

**Part 3 — Fork pairs and DPO (§4.3–4.4).** For each decision point, take the reasoning up to the committing sentence as the fork prefix, render it with the served chat template, resample n continuations from the target at T=1/top-p=0.95, have the judge label each continuation's own committing sentence, and pair a preferred with an adversarial continuation from the same fork. Data: GLM-5.3 — 29 tasks, 1,668 forks × 16 continuations = 28,356 samples, 2,843 pairs from 28 tasks, 6 tasks (619 pairs) held out; DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash — 2,203 forks × 12 continuations up to 1,536 tokens = 26,418 samples, 6,391 pairs from 92 tasks, 10% of tasks (719 pairs) held out, 5,100 pairs trained. Whole tasks are held out rather than random pairs, since a random split leaks near-duplicate forks.

Objective: DPO on the token window with a length-normalized weighted log-ratio reward r_θ(y) = Σ w_t [log q_θ − log q_ref] / Σ w_t, loss −log σ(β[r_θ(y_w) − r_θ(y_l)]) + α·NLL_θ(y_w), β=10. Deployed configs: GLM-5.3 at lr 5e-6 with a 128-token decay window; DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash with QAT on attention params, lr 2e-5, α=0.2, 512-token window, checkpoint taken after epoch 2 (held-out accuracy 0.70). Reference log-probabilities are precomputed with the frozen original draft; hidden states are extracted once per sample from an inference instance hosting the target and the draft is trained offline on those features.

**Part 4 — Evaluation (§5).** Held-out preference accuracy on unseen tasks (0.5 by construction for an untrained draft). End-to-end on DeepSWE (113 tasks, mini-swe-agent harness with shell access, graded by hidden tests, internet blocked to prevent reward hacking) and NL2Repo-Bench (104 generated repositories graded against the upstream pytest suite, mean test pass rate). Two servers differing only in the draft; target weights, serving stack, exact verification, sampling parameters, and harness all fixed; five independent runs per configuration.

## Results & Highlights

- **Noise grows monotonically along the verification block.** On GLM-5.3 the mean |Δ log p| is 0.473 nats at slot 0 versus 0.478 nats for single-token decoding — statistically indistinguishable — then rises at +0.0195 nats per slot (95% CI [+0.0174, +0.0214]), i.e. ≈4% of the slot-0 level per slot, so the last slot (s=7) is 34% noisier than the first. DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash shows the same relative growth at +0.0094 nats/slot (CI [+0.0078, +0.0110]) with roughly half the absolute noise.
- **The growth is concentrated in the tail that matters for sampling.** On GLM-5.3 the fraction of tokens shifted by more than 2 nats goes from 3.2% at slot 0 to 6.2% at slot 7, and >3-nat shifts from 1.1% to 2.1%. On DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash >1-nat shifts rise 3.1%→4.5% (+1.4 pts) and >2-nat shifts 0.41%→0.68%.
- **Verify-written context drifts more than decode-written context, but only after ~30 turns.** Ratio of >4-nat deviations, verify path vs decode path, on GLM-5.3 over 96 tasks and 6.38M scored tokens per build: 0.98 in turns 11–30, 1.09 in 31–50, 1.16 in 51–70 (95% CI [0.98, 1.36]), returning to 0.99 in 71–90. A second independent pair of builds gives 1.20 ([1.03, 1.40]) beyond turn 30. For deviations up to 2 nats the two paths are indistinguishable at every distance pooled over turns 11–90.
- **DeepSWE, GLM-5.3-nvfp4: 60.6% → 67.6% (+7.0 points, 95% CI 2.6 to 11.4)** over five independent runs with the unbiased (pretrained) draft as baseline. This is the paper's cleanest end-to-end result.
- **DeepSWE, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash: 67.8% → 71.8% (+4.0 points, 95% CI −1.3 to 7.3)** — the point estimate is positive but the interval crosses zero, so this gain is *not* established at n=5 runs.
- **NL2Repo-Bench: GLM-5.3-nvfp4 49.1% → 55.0% (+5.8, no CI reported); DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash 65.0% → 66.4% (+1.4, 95% CI 0.4 to 2.4, p = 0.011).** The two significant gains land on different benchmarks, different targets, and different drafts.
- **Reversing the preference reverses the effect.** Drafts trained on the identical fork pairs with chosen/rejected swapped score 60.6→55.7 on DeepSWE for GLM-5.3 (−4.9) and 67.8→66.4 for DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (−1.4), versus +7.0 and +4.1 for the adversarial drafts on the same data. The forward drafts learn their preference at least as well (0.80 held-out for the DeepSeek forward draft vs 0.70 for the deployed adversarial one), so preference direction, not training, is the operative variable.
- **Unrelated preferences are a clean null.** 27 complete DeepSWE runs of DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash served with drafts trained on three non-deployed preferences pass 67.5% pooled versus 67.8% for the unbiased draft (Welch p = 0.83); no single group differs (p ≥ 0.16). The deployed run-tests draft is 4.3 points above that pooled control (p = 0.018). Per-group: decision right/wrong 67.6 (n=15), thinking length 69.9 (n=5), decision frame 65.9 (n=7), run tests 71.9 (n=5), unbiased 67.8 (n=6).
- **The draft learns framing and behavior but not correctness.** Held-out preference accuracy: run-tests 0.83 forward / 0.85 adversarial; decision frame 0.59 / 0.635 (±0.015 seed spread). Reference preferences: thinking length 0.54 / 0.65; decision right-vs-wrong 0.61 / 0.57 as the best of 36 and 12 configurations, but with medians of 0.52 and 0.46 on 92 and 128 pairs — inside the confidence interval of chance.
- **Speed cost is ≤2%.** Slot-wise top-1 agreement with the emitted token moves by +0.1/+0.1/−0.3/−0.4/+0.2/+0.1/−0.1 points across GLM-5.3's seven draft slots and by −0.4/−0.6/−1.0/−1.2/−1.2 on DeepSeek's five. Under the greedy-chain approximation, expected accepted draft tokens per block is 2.28 before and after training on GLM-5.3, and 2.5 vs 2.46 on DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash — a 1.8% difference.

## Authors & Credibility

Hongyin Luo (Subconscious Systems Technologies; MIT) and Wei Fang (Subconscious Systems) share first authorship; the remaining authors are split across Subconscious Systems, Princeton (Lijie Yang), and Northeastern (Owen Stepan, Daniel Suit, Jeff Krapf). This is an industry-plus-academia collaboration with a commercial interest in serving-stack efficiency — Subconscious Systems Technologies is evidently building exactly the kind of draft-model product the paper advocates, which is worth keeping in mind when reading the deployment framing.

The work is technically credible in a way that is unusual for this genre. The measurement protocol in §3.2 is designed to exclude a specific confound (prefill variance) rather than to produce a big number, and the authors report confidence intervals from bootstrap over positions and from binomial over pairs. The negative controls in §5.1 and §5.2 (preferred-direction drafts, unrelated preferences, an unlearnable reference preference) are the kind of ablation most papers skip. The paper also reports its own null results — the DeepSeek decision-frame draft learned its preference (0.64–0.69 held-out) and still changed nothing (65.9% vs 67.8%) — rather than burying them, and explicitly reads the long-horizon effect as a 10–20% excess with a CI that sometimes includes 1.0.

Countervailing: both target models are ~750B, the drafts are proprietary production artifacts (DFlash2, DSPARK), evaluation needs 4×B200, and the DSPARK port is a nontrivial engineering effort (kernel matching to 1e-7 per expert). No code, model, or dataset release is announced. This is a report from inside a serving stack, not a reproducible artifact.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The two ingredients are separately known — that served inference is not bitwise reproducible (Yuan et al. 2025; He & Lab 2025 on batch-invariance) and that a small subset of tokens decides reasoning trajectories (Lin et al. 2025; Wang et al. 2025) — but the composition is genuinely new: nobody has measured a *within-block precision gradient* in speculative verification, and nobody has used the draft's rejection decision as a precision allocator. The observation that Pr[reject] = TV(p,q) is textbook; the inference that this makes rejection a free control channel that preserves exactness is the actual contribution, and it is a good one.

**The central mechanism is asserted at its weakest link.** The causal chain is: (a) noise grows by slot — measured well; (b) the adversarial draft disagrees with the target at decision points — measured only as 0.635 held-out pairwise accuracy on GLM-5.3; (c) therefore those decisions are committed via residual sampling at slot 0. Step (c) is never measured. There is no statistic anywhere in the paper reporting what fraction of committing sentences are actually emitted as rejected/resampled tokens under the adversarial draft versus the base draft, nor where in the block they land. With 0.635 accuracy the draft fails to be rejected at the decision point roughly 36% of the time, yet the effect is still +7 points — which either means the mechanism is more diffuse than the slot-0 story claims, or that something else is doing the work. A single histogram of "committing-sentence token's slot index, base vs adversarial" would have settled this and is conspicuously absent. The negative controls are strong evidence that *the preference direction* matters; they are not evidence that *slot-0 encoding* is the channel.

**The strongest end-to-end claim is not statistically established.** DeepSWE with DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash is 67.8→71.8 with 95% CI [−1.3, 7.3]. The abstract states "raises DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash from 67.8% to 71.8% (+4.0 points)" without qualification, and the intro lists it alongside the GLM result as if both were equally firm. They are not. Conversely the GLM NL2Repo result (49.1→55.0, +5.8) is reported with no CI and no p-value at all, while the much smaller DeepSeek NL2Repo gain (+1.4) gets both — asymmetric reporting that happens to place the interval-free number on the larger effect.

**The negative controls are also a problem for generality.** On DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash the decision-frame draft *did* learn its preference (0.64–0.69 held-out) and produced 65.9% versus 67.8% unbiased — a null, explained post hoc as "on this target the committing sentence is not where runs are lost." That may be right, but it means the method's applicability is determined by a property of the failure distribution that the paper can only diagnose after the fact. There is no pre-registered or a priori test for "will this preference move the metric on this model" — you train a draft and run five DeepSWE sweeps to find out. For a method whose selling point is a 0.06% parameter budget, the search cost of finding the right preference is the real budget and it is never accounted for.

**Missing baselines.** The comparison is always adversarial draft vs the pretrained unbiased draft — correct for isolating the mechanism, but it omits the obvious alternative: spend the same precision budget by *not speculating*. A W=1 or reduced-block-width server pays a known speed cost and gets slot-0 precision on every token, and the paper's efficiency argument (≤2% speed) is only interesting relative to that price. There is also no comparison to simply serving the target at higher precision (e.g. bf16 weights instead of NVFP4, or FP16 KV cache), which would presumably recover much of the lost precision at a larger but still quantifiable cost. Without either, "2% speed for +7 points" cannot be located on the cost-benefit frontier; it is only located relative to doing nothing.

**Possible training/evaluation overlap is unaddressed.** Fork pairs are mined from "real trajectories" on 29 tasks (GLM-5.3) and 92 tasks (DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash), and the paper is careful to hold out whole tasks rather than random pairs to avoid near-duplicate leakage. But it never states *which* benchmark those training tasks came from. The noise study uses DeepSWE prompts and the long-horizon study uses 96 DeepSWE tasks; if the fork-mining pool is also DeepSWE-derived, then evaluation on DeepSWE's 113 tasks is partly in-distribution and NL2Repo-Bench is the only clean test — where the gains are +5.8 (no CI) and +1.4 (p=0.011). This is a one-sentence disclosure that should have been in §4.3 and its absence is the paper's most serious reporting gap.

**The long-horizon measurement is honest but weak.** The 1.16 ratio in turns 51–70 has CI [0.98, 1.36], the replicate is 1.20 [1.03, 1.40], and the 71–90 bin returns to 0.99 — which the authors attribute to "fixed teacher-forcing tokens correct the biases." That explanation is plausible but also means the protocol measures drift under a teacher that keeps resetting the trajectory, so it is a lower bound. The paper says as much. Still, "10 to 20% excess of extreme deviations after 30 turns" is a soft foundation for a claim about long-horizon agent accuracy, and the fact that the paper's own headline experiment (+7.0) is roughly the size of the run-to-run spread on a 113-task benchmark (per-run SE ≈ 4.5 points) makes the mechanistic story do more work than the measurement can bear.

**Framing quibbles.** "0.06% to 0.28% of hosted parameters" is arithmetically right but rhetorically loaded: the draft models are 2.46B and 14.2B parameters that exist regardless, and the paper trains 87% of the first one. The genuinely small number is DeepSeek's 449M/14.2B. Also, "no change to the output distribution" is exact under the idealized rejection rule and only approximately true on the hardware the paper itself shows to be non-reproducible — the authors acknowledge this, but the abstract does not.

**Verdict.** The §3 measurement is the durable contribution and is worth citing on its own; the method is clever, cheap, and backed by unusually good controls; the end-to-end evidence is one solid result (+7.0, GLM-5.3/DeepSWE), one suggestive one (+5.8, no CI), one null-in-practice (+4.0 with CI crossing zero), and one small-but-significant one (+1.4). The mechanism story is one measurement short of proven.

## Strengths

- **The measurement protocol is the best part of the paper and is reusable.** Holding the prefix fixed in memory and repeating only the forward pass removes the prefill confound that would otherwise swamp a 0.02-nat-per-slot effect. Anyone auditing a serving stack for precision heterogeneity can lift §3.1 wholesale.
- **Two independent draft architectures reproduce the effect.** A block-diffusion drafter (DFlash2, W=8) and a three-stage MoE drafter (DSPARK, W=6) with different absolute noise levels show the same ~4%-per-slot relative growth. That is a much stronger generality claim than two seeds of one system.
- **The controls are genuinely adversarial to the authors' own thesis.** Training the preference forward instead of backward *hurts* by 4.9 and 1.4 points; 27 runs on unrelated preferences are flat at p=0.83; the "decision right vs wrong" preference is shown to be unlearnable and produces no effect. This is the ablation structure that separates a real mechanism from "we fine-tuned something and the number went up."
- **Zero risk to the target's behavior.** Because rejection sampling is untouched, the method cannot make the target emit a token outside its own distribution. That is an unusually strong safety property for a method that claims to improve reasoning — most reasoning interventions (RL, prompting, steering) buy accuracy by changing the distribution and cannot offer it.
- **The efficiency accounting is honest and specific.** Reporting 2.28 accepted tokens before and after, and 2.5 vs 2.46, is far more informative than "no significant slowdown." The per-slot top-1 agreement deltas (−0.4 to −1.2 points on DeepSeek) show where the acceptance loss actually is.
- **Deployment realism.** This runs on the actual quantized, sparse-attention, FP8-KV serving configuration with QAT on the exported draft so that "the deployed draft computes exactly the function that was evaluated" — a detail most quantization papers get wrong.

## Limitations

- **No measurement of where committing-sentence tokens actually land.** The slot-0 mechanism is inferred from the noise profile and the preference accuracy, not observed. The missing statistic is the one that would make or break the causal claim.
- **Small n at the end-to-end level.** Five runs per configuration on a 113-task benchmark gives a per-run standard error near 4.5 points; the +4.0 DeepSeek result is inside noise and the +5.8 GLM NL2Repo result has no interval at all.
- **Training-task provenance is undisclosed**, leaving open whether DeepSWE evaluation overlaps the fork-mining pool. Only NL2Repo-Bench is unambiguously out-of-distribution, and its gains are the smallest.
- **No "pay for precision everywhere" baseline** (W=1 decoding, or higher-precision weights/KV cache), so the 2% speed cost cannot be placed on a cost-benefit frontier.
- **The preference must be chosen per model by trial.** Decision frames move GLM-5.3 and do nothing for DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash; run-tests move DeepSeek and were not tried as the primary preference on GLM. The paper offers a post-hoc diagnosis but no predictive rule, so applying this to a new model means mining traces, training a draft, and running an expensive sweep to learn whether it worked.
- **The long-horizon evidence rests on teacher-forced scoring** (up to 128k tokens, median 95 turns), which suppresses free-running drift and makes the 10–20% excess a lower bound with a CI that touches 1.0 in one bin.
- **Not reproducible outside a frontier serving stack.** Two ~750B targets, proprietary drafts, 4×B200, a hand-ported differentiable MoE kernel, no released code or weights.
- **Scope is coding agents only.** Both benchmarks are software engineering; whether the same decision-frame audit transfers to, say, scientific or web agents is untested, and the 70%-vs-36% frame-correctness gap is measured only on GLM-5.3 coding traces.
- **The frame audit is correlational.** "Principle/evidence-framed decisions are correct ~70% vs 36% for likely/risk" is a trace statistic with no intervention showing that flipping the frame flips correctness. It is used only to pick a training direction, so the risk is contained, but the number should not be read as a causal claim about reasoning quality.

## Relevance

直接命中 **Inference Reliability & SDC Detection** 的核心命题：模型的输出被"模型之下的数值、kernel 与服务栈"悄悄改变——这里给出了一条此前没人量过的具体通道（验证块内 slot 位置决定 KV 编码精度，约 4%/槽的噪声增长，30 轮后出现 10–20% 的极端偏差超额），而且证明了这种偏差不是无害抖动，而是会以随机方向影响长程 agent 的准确率。同时对 **Reliability under Extreme Performance Optimization** 高度相关：NVFP4 权重 + FP8 KV + 稀疏注意力 + 投机解码这一整套"性能优化栈"叠加后产生的精度非均匀性，以及一个只改 0.06% 参数就能把这种非均匀性变成收益的工程手法。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. **Where do committing-sentence tokens actually land?** Report the slot-index histogram of the emitted token at each decision point, base draft vs adversarial draft. If the adversarial draft's decisions are still mostly accepted at slots 3–6, the mechanism is not what the paper says and the +7.0 needs another explanation.
2. **What does W=1 cost?** Serving the same targets with no speculation (or block width 1) gives every token slot-0 precision. If that recovers most of the +7.0 at, say, a 30% throughput cost, the interesting question becomes the shape of the accuracy-vs-speed curve, not the existence of a free lunch.
3. **Is the fork-mining pool disjoint from DeepSWE?** A single sentence naming the source benchmark and the task-level overlap check would resolve the contamination concern. Absent it, NL2Repo-Bench should be treated as the only clean evaluation.
4. **Why does the effect appear at all if the preference is only 63.5% accurate?** A dose-response curve — sweep held-out preference accuracy from 0.55 to 0.85 by varying training data volume and plot DeepSWE pass rate — would test whether the mechanism is monotone in rejection reliability or whether a threshold effect is at work.
5. **Does the preferred-direction penalty tell us something?** The −4.9 on GLM-5.3 is larger in magnitude than a symmetric-mechanism account would predict. Is committing a decision at a noisy slot actively harmful, or is the drop just sampling variance? A per-slot analysis of the forward-draft runs would answer this.
6. **Does the precision gradient exist in other verification schemes?** Medusa's tree attention, EAGLE's feature-level drafting, and standard chain speculation all lay out the verify pass differently. If the ~4%/slot slope is specific to block-diffusion and semi-autoregressive drafts, the phenomenon is narrower than "speculative verification" and the paper's framing overgeneralizes.
7. **Can the preference be selected without an end-to-end sweep?** A cheap proxy — e.g. the correlation between a candidate preference's held-out accuracy and its downstream effect, measured across the four preferences already trained — would turn this from an artisanal per-model search into a procedure.
8. **What happens at longer horizons than 128k?** The 71–90-turn bin returning to 0.99 is attributed to teacher forcing. A free-running rollout with periodic re-scoring, or an evaluation on tasks requiring 500+ turns, would test whether the drift compounds or saturates.
