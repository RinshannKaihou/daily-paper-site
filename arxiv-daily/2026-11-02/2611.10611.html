Mechanism

From a selection leak to three pins

Detent does not make every kernel conservative. It makes the choice of kernel independent of the batch, then keeps the fast kernels.

  1. Split the guarantee.

    End-to-end invariance factors into batch-invariant kernels and a batch-invariant selector. The user supplies the first; Detent supplies the second.

  2. Write selection as a program.

    SelIR is a pure Python subset. Inputs are Runtime or Unpinnable; every return value is a selection point. Solver and engine run the same program.

  3. Lower, then cut.

    Partial evaluation on deployment facts yields one dependence graph. FA3: 8,076 values, 26 selection points, 3 batch-dependent. A weighted min vertex cut via max-flow picks the pins.

  4. Check legality and tune.

    Pin VRA replays every require/assume. Additivity shrinks the tuple search from 1,607,040 to 3,020; measuring 8% lands 0.04% off the best on average.

  5. Serve and train on one tuple.

    The trainer differs from the rollout only in batch, so logprobs match bit for bit; a seeded-sampling patch fixes the tokens.

The trade-off

What holds up, what does not

Strengths

  • The split is reusable: kernel invariance is the user's job, selection invariance is the system's.
  • The cut earns its keep: FA3 needs 3 pins, not 12 with constant folding alone; the MoE needs 3, not 10.
  • Honest ablation: Detent's own GEMM and all-reduce add 0.6% (Qwen3) / 2.8% (Llama) GPU time — no faster-kernel sleight of hand.
  • A 26-cell battery with nine “strong” cells, replicated on eight extra deployments.
  • End-to-end RL evidence and full cost transparency: 512/512 replay, 100% trainer agreement, every stage timed.

Limitations

  • One deployment, one tuple. Cross-TP invariance needs TBIK-style kernels that Detent does not provide.
  • SelIR is hand-written; only four operator families are demonstrated.
  • Accuracy is GSM8K-only and saturated — roughly ±1.3 points of resolution, nothing on code or long context.
  • Traffic drift is unhandled: a stale tuple can cost 4.17× and fails silently. FP8 is out of scope.
  • Sampling is restricted to seeded temperature; greedy/top-p mixes break the guarantee.
  • Single-seed GRPO with an LR change that may confound the result; no artifact, and engine targets are dev snapshots.
04

Open questions

What the paper leaves on the table

Q1

How much of the 1.10× residual is rebuilt-FA3 overhead versus the tuple's lost per-batch adaptation?

Q2

Does Stock still collapse at the lowered 1e-6 learning rate? Without that control, bit-exactness is confounded with step size.

Q3

Why does the W-A tuple cost 4.17× on Qwen3's B2 load but only 1.15× on Llama — is the MoE row-key pin the culprit?

Q4

Do pins survive an engine upgrade, and how would Detent handle speculative decoding or disaggregated serving?
