> **一句话总结：** 论文用 100-batch 粒度的"原始权重 vs EMA 权重"配对追踪证明 catastrophic overfitting（CO）不是优化失败，而是权重沿训练损失几乎"看不见"的方向漂移——沿 EMA→CO 权重的插值路径上训练目标只波动 ±7.5%，鲁棒性却掉 69–77%——据此提出"验证鲁棒性下跌就把权重重置回 EMA"的零成本 guard，配合一个耦合式 base method 在 CIFAR-10/100 与 Tiny-ImageNet 上分别取得 50.45 / 27.15 / 20.47 AutoAttack 准确率，均为 fast 方法最强。

> **TL;DR:** The paper reframes catastrophic overfitting in fast adversarial training (FAT) as a drift of weights along directions the training loss is nearly flat in, and shows the EMA of the weights — already maintained by every FAT protocol — does not accumulate that drift and stays robust through the whole event. Measuring vanilla FGSM-AT on CIFAR-10 at 100-batch resolution, PGD-10 robustness falls 44% → 11% between two adjacent checkpoints while clean accuracy *rises* 73% → 80% and the attack-direction/PGD cosine collapses 0.82 → 0.00; along 12 (EMA, raw) interpolation paths the training objective varies only ±7.5% while robustness loses 69–77%, a ~10× sensitivity gap. The proposed fix is a discrete guard that resets weights to their EMA when validation PGD-10 drops more than θ=8 points below its running best, layered on a coupling-based base method (MSE consistency anchored at the clean input + nuclear-norm term). It removes CO in all 4 CIFAR-10 seeds (unguarded base collapses in 2 of 4, 4-seed mean 29.1) and reaches 50.45±0.20 AA on CIFAR-10 (+0.46 over LIET 49.99), 27.15±0.04 on CIFAR-100 (+0.51 over LIET 26.64, +0.25 over the multi-step PGD-AT-WA 26.90 at ~2× cost), and 20.47±0.11 on Tiny-ImageNet (+0.59 over LIET 19.88) — but the guard itself only ever fires on CIFAR-10, and under PGD-50 the ordering reverses (LIET 55.97 vs 54.63).

## 核心贡献

- **给出 CO 的机制性测量，而非又一个正则项。** 作者重训经典受害者（vanilla FGSM-AT，ResNet18/CIFAR-10，ε=8/255），每 100 batch 同时保存原始权重 $w$ 与其 EMA $\bar w$（decay 0.9995，benchmark 协议自带），把"一个 epoch 内发生的神秘事件"变成可逐帧观察的过程：鲁棒性先走、干净精度后知后觉。
- **提出"盲方向"（blind direction）这一可操作定义。** 若沿方向 $v$ 移动使训练目标 $|L(w+\alpha v)-L(w)|\le\varepsilon\alpha$ 而鲁棒性 $|R(w+\alpha v)-R(w)|\ge\delta\alpha$，且 $\delta/\varepsilon\gg1$，则称 $v$ 为盲方向。实测沿 EMA→CO 权重这条真实漂移方向，敏感性差距约 10 倍。
- **三条命题串起"为什么训练救不回来"。** 盲方向上无回复力（期望更新 $O(\eta\varepsilon)$）；时间平均把漂移压缩为 $O(\sigma^2/T)$ 而原始位移增长为 $O(\sigma^2T)$；把 $w$ 换成 $\bar w$ 只让训练目标扰动 $O(\varepsilon)$（实测干净精度 +6 点）却恢复鲁棒性。
- **把"重置到 EMA"做成一个离散 guard，并给出可辩护的阈值。** 600 个健康 epoch 中 guard 统计量从未跌破最优值 6.5 点，而每次 CO 的首个异常 epoch 至少跌 9.6 点；θ=8 落在空隙中央，落在空隙外一侧就死（θ=10 同一轨迹 7.94 vs θ=8 的 50.61）。
- **配套设计了一个"可被锚定"的 base method。** 只有让 EMA 本身停在鲁棒区域内，重置才有去处；作者用"锚定在干净输入上的 MSE 一致性 + nuclear-norm 耦合"实现这一点，并明确说明前者来自 FGSM-PGI、后者来自 NuAT，创新在于用途（保住平均，而非防 CO）与解耦（guard 与 base 分离）。
- **控制实验证明救援来自 anchor 而非 detector。** 无检测器的定 epoch 重置（第 18 epoch）同样把该 seed 救到 49.99；而常开连续拉回（$\eta_a=0.05$）直接毁掉训练（8.15），说明"只在漂移时拉"是关键。

## Key Contributions

- **A measured mechanism for CO.** Paired raw/EMA checkpointing at 100-batch resolution on vanilla FGSM-AT (CIFAR-10, ResNet18) shows CO is a displacement in weight space that the training objective barely registers: between the last healthy and first collapsed checkpoint, PGD-10 falls 44% → 11% while clean accuracy *increases* 73% → 80% and the cosine between the training-attack direction and a PGD-10 direction falls 0.82 → 0.00. The EMA, evaluated on the same grid with BatchNorm recalibrated, holds 44–46% robustness and a 0.75–0.83 cosine throughout.
- **A quantified flatness claim via line search.** For 12 (EMA, raw) pairs spanning the CO window, interpolating $w(\alpha)=\bar w+\alpha(w-\bar w)$ with the attack recomputed at every $\alpha$ keeps the training objective within ±7.5% of its level, and the CO endpoint is not even a lower loss; over the same path robustness loses 69–77%, with a knee at $\alpha\approx0.6$–$0.8$. The sensitivity gap along this single direction is ~10×.
- **Three informal propositions** explaining why gradient descent cannot undo the drift (no restoring force along blind directions), why the time average does not accumulate it ($O(\sigma^2/T)$ vs $O(\sigma^2T)$), and why resetting to the average is cheap for the objective.
- **A discrete reset guard** (Eq. 4) that reads the validation PGD-10 the protocol already computes, tracks the deficit $g_t=b_t^\star-p_t$ against a running best, and sets $w_{t+1}=\bar w_t$ when $g_t>\theta$, with a 3-epoch cooldown and a 5-fire budget. Zero additional forward passes at deployment.
- **A base method built for anchoring**: label-smoothed CE plus an MSE consistency term anchored at the *clean input* $x$ (not at the attack initialization $x+\delta_0$) plus a nuclear-norm term on the batch's clean-minus-adversarial logit matrix. The clean-input anchor is argued to be load-bearing: anchoring at the attack init makes the penalty self-referential and creates a masking feedback loop.
- **A guard threshold with a measured gap** (θ=8 inside (6.5, 9.6)) plus an explicit demonstration that the choice is sharp on one side (θ=10 → 7.94 on a trajectory bit-identical through epoch 18).
- **Sample-level anatomy of the cliff**: the 75-batch aggregate collapse is a staged sweep, with a 16% cohort dying ≥100 batches before the median, ordered by the clean logit margin at the healthy checkpoint (1.02 vs 1.62, Mann–Whitney p=9×10⁻¹⁶, Spearman −0.54), and reproducible across an independently trained replica (ρ=0.48).
- **Results**: best fast-method AutoAttack accuracy on all three benchmark datasets, and a CIFAR-100 number that clears the multi-step reference.

## Methodology

**Instrumentation (Sec. 3).** The victim is vanilla FGSM adversarial training under the FastAT benchmark protocol (ResNet18, CIFAR-10, ε=8/255, 100 epochs, OneCycle SGD, label smoothing 0.4, 49k/1k train/val split). Every 100 batches two checkpoints are saved together: the raw training weights $w$ and their EMA $\bar w$ at decay 0.9995. All Sec. 3 evaluations recalibrate BatchNorm statistics on 4,096 training samples first — the authors note that without this step the entire effect disappears, since an EMA checkpoint evaluated with stale BN statistics scores at chance.

**Line search.** For each of 12 (EMA, raw) pairs through the CO window (6 before, 6 after), the path $w(\alpha)=\bar w+\alpha(w-\bar w)$ is swept from $\alpha=0$ to $1$, with the training attack recomputed at every $\alpha$ (the objective is defined under the attack, so freezing the perturbation would mis-measure $L$). Both $L$ and PGD-10 $R$ are recorded along each path.

**Batch-resolution dynamics.** Three seeds are re-run with checkpoints every 50 batches; the cosine between the training-attack direction and a PGD direction is tracked, and the per-sample death times are read off by fixing the attack seed so every checkpoint sees an identical PGD-10 initialization. A sample is called dead at the first checkpoint where it stays fragile for three consecutive checkpoints.

**Base method (Sec. 4.1).** The perturbation is a fresh half-sign initialization at $0.5\varepsilon$ (following NuAT), one sign step of CE at $x+\delta_0$, clipped to the threat model — an input-space pattern, recomputed per batch. The objective is
$$\mathcal{L}=\mathrm{CE}_{ls}(z_c,y)+\frac{\gamma(t)}{B}\|Z_c-Z_a\|_*+m(t)\|z_c-z_a\|_2^2,$$
where $Z_c,Z_a$ stack the batch's clean and adversarial logits, $\|\cdot\|_*$ is the nuclear norm, and $\gamma(t),m(t)$ ramp linearly. The MSE term is per-example consistency anchored at the clean input; the nuclear term acts on the batch as a matrix and penalizes concentration of the clean-minus-adversarial logit difference into a few directions. Both terms need clean logits in the graph, so each step carries three forward passes; the authors report failing to recover the clean branch at two-pass cost "in every arrangement we could think of."

**Guard (Sec. 4.2, Algorithm 1).** With $p_t$ the epoch-$t$ validation PGD-10 of $w_t$, $b_t^\star=\max_{s\le t}p_s$ and $g_t=b_t^\star-p_t$:
$$w_{t+1}=\begin{cases}\bar w_t & g_t>\theta \;\wedge\; t-t_{\text{fire}}\ge3 \;\wedge\; F_t<5\\ w_t & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}$$
The cooldown exists because the anchor lands below the pre-CO peak, so the deficit stays positive by construction after a reset; the fire budget is a stop-loss against a sinking anchor (on vanilla FGSM-AT an uncapped guard fires 68 times in 100 epochs).

**Training details.** CIFAR-10: $\gamma_{\max}=4.0$, $m_{\max}=2.5$; CIFAR-100: $\gamma_{\max}=1.25$, $m_{\max}=4.5$; Tiny-ImageNet: $\gamma_{\max}=1.0$, $m_{\max}=2.5$. Evaluation is the full standard AutoAttack suite (apgd-ce, apgd-t, FAB-T, square) plus the benchmark's constraint-relaxation (CR) attack, on the full test set. A run takes 1.05 h versus 0.7–0.8 h for the strongest two-pass fast methods and ~2 h for PGD-AT.

## Results & Highlights

- **CIFAR-10, AutoAttack:** 50.45±0.20 over 4 seeds with zero CO, versus LIET 49.99±0.15 (+0.46), NuAT 50.10±0.18 (+0.35), FGSM-PGI 49.89±0.08 (+0.56), and multi-step PGD-AT-WA 50.49±0.15 (−0.04). The unguarded base is not deployable: it matches on the two seeds that survive (50.67±0.08) but returns gradient-masked artifacts on the two that collapse, for a 4-seed mean of 29.1 — the guard's entire effect is the removal of that failure mode.
- **CIFAR-10, CR attack:** 50.44±0.16, ahead of NuAT 50.11±0.19 and LIET 49.93±0.15, within 0.1 of the multi-step PGD-AT-WA 50.51±0.16 — the same ordering under the benchmark's second strong-attack suite.
- **CIFAR-100, AutoAttack:** 27.15±0.04 versus LIET 26.64±0.13 (+0.51) and the multi-step PGD-AT-WA 26.90±0.17 (+0.25 at roughly twice the cost); NuAT, the published nuclear-norm method, degrades to 14.41±3.29 here. Under CR the margin holds: 27.20±0.07 vs LIET 26.63±0.14 (+0.57).
- **Tiny-ImageNet, AutoAttack:** 20.47±0.11 versus LIET 19.88±0.09 (+0.59) and PGD-AT-WA 19.77±0.16 (+0.70); CR gives 20.54±0.15 vs LIET 19.86±0.07 (+0.68).
- **The CO geometry, quantified:** between the last healthy and first collapsed CIFAR-10 checkpoint (100 batches apart), PGD-10 falls 44% → 11%, clean accuracy rises 73% → 80%, and the attack/PGD cosine falls 0.82 → 0.00; across 12 interpolation paths the training objective stays within ±7.5% while robustness loses 69–77% (a ~10× sensitivity gap), and the CO endpoint is not a lower training loss.
- **The guard threshold has a real gap:** over 600 healthy epochs the deficit never exceeds 6.5 points, while every observed CO dips ≥9.6 at its first abnormal epoch. θ=8 recovers a seed to 50.61 AA; θ=10 on the same trajectory fires one epoch later on a contaminated anchor and dies at 7.94; θ=12 saves only 1 of 2 seeds (50.40 / 7.84); θ=3 produces spurious fires (2/5/3) with no accuracy change.
- **The rescue is the anchor, not the detector:** a detector-free fixed-epoch (18) reset reaches 49.99 on the same seed; a coupled-loss fallback on fire instead of a reset gives 7.05; an always-on continuous pull at $\eta_a=0.05$ gives 8.15, and at $\eta_a=0.02$ fails 1 of 5 seeds (7.14).
- **Gain is not attack-specific:** per-sample AutoAttack survival orderings agree across 16 healthy models at mean pairwise Kendall τ=0.82 (range 0.79–0.91), and the anchored model sits at τ=0.847 — the same difficulty structure, whereas the only spectrum rewriters are failure modes (collapsed seed τ=0.386, logit-surgery variant τ=0.624).
- **Auxiliary cost check:** PGD-20 for the guarded seeds is 54.74/54.58/54.65/55.05, and PGD-50 is 54.63 for this method versus NuAT 54.63 and LIET 55.97 — see the critique below.

## Authors & Credibility

Single author: **Chao Pan**, Department of Computer Science and Engineering, Southern University of Science and Technology (SUSTech), Shenzhen. The citation list makes the lineage explicit and it is unusually self-referential: the evaluation benchmark (FastAT Benchmark, arXiv:2604.22853) is Pan & Yao, the CR attack is Pan et al. (AAAI 2025), the universal-adversarial-initialization baselines FGSM-UAP/CUAP/FUAP are Pan, Li & Yao (AAAI 2024), LIET is Pan, Tang, Li & Yao (ICCV 2025), and the RobustBench data-similarity critique (DSAA 2025) is also Pan et al. The author is therefore evaluating his own method on his own benchmark with attacks he co-designed — which cuts both ways: baseline numbers are not retrained (a genuine fairness plus, since every competitor's number is the benchmark's released result), but the protocol's design choices were made by someone with a stake in this comparison.

The work is otherwise methodologically careful in ways that suggest real measurement discipline: the BN-recalibration caveat is stated in the main text rather than buried, the threshold is justified by a measured gap rather than tuned, the failure of the idea on vanilla FGSM-AT is reported with numbers (0.00 / 38.34), the per-seed tables are complete, and the limitation section volunteers that "zero CO in four seeds is consistent with a CO rate as high as 53%." Code is released. No independent replication exists yet, and this is a single-author paper with no lab co-signature.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The algorithmic content is thin and the paper says so: the MSE consistency term "follows the per-example consistency idea of FGSM-PGI," the nuclear term is "the idea behind NuAT," and the guard is a one-line conditional. The contribution that actually carries the paper is the *measurement* — paired raw/EMA checkpointing at 100-batch resolution plus a line search along the real drift direction — and that measurement is genuinely new and clean. The reframing ("CO is not a bad minimum, it is a sideways walk off a plateau the loss cannot see") is the kind of claim that reorganizes a literature, and the flat-path evidence (objective within ±7.5%, robustness −69–77%) is the right experiment to support it. Credit where due: this is a real mechanistic result, not a marketing reframe.

**Rigor — the empirical base is narrower than the abstract implies.** The headline claim is "removes CO in every one of our runs," but Table 1's own footnote concedes that **the guard never fires on CIFAR-100 or Tiny-ImageNet**. On those two datasets the reported numbers are the re-balanced base method's performance with the guard "carried as zero-cost insurance" — i.e., the central mechanism is untested there. On CIFAR-10 the guard fires in exactly one of four seeds (3 times inside its CO window); three seeds never fire. So the entire empirical support for the guard is: 4 CIFAR-10 seeds, 1 of which actually exercised it. That is a demonstration, not a validation. The 53% binomial bound the authors volunteer is honest but cuts deep — with 4 seeds you cannot distinguish "eliminates CO" from "happened not to collapse."

**The PGD-50 number is a real crack and the text glosses it.** Table 1's footnote reports PGD-50 as LIET 55.97, NuAT 54.63, ours 54.63, introduced with "under the iterative attacks of the released suite the ordering is similar." The ordering is *not* similar: under PGD-50 this method ties NuAT and sits **1.34 points behind LIET**, the very method it beats by 0.46 under AutoAttack. That is a sign flip on the headline comparison, and the paper's defense — AutoAttack is the stronger suite and FAB/square find nothing beyond APGD — is reasonable but should have been argued explicitly rather than absorbed into the word "similar." A 1.34-point reversal under a 50-step attack deserves a paragraph, not a semicolon.

**Hyperparameter selection on the reporting benchmark.** The coupling weights ($\gamma_{\max}=4.0/1.25/1.0$, $m_{\max}=2.5/4.5/2.5$) were "found by small sweeps, not derived from the mechanism," and the sweeps are reported on the same test benchmarks that produce the headline numbers. The CIFAR-100 response is an inverted U with a narrow peak (γ=1.0 → 26.6, γ=1.25 → 27.15, γ=1.5 → 27.1, γ=2.0 → 25.5), so the peak is being read off the evaluation surface. Standard practice in this subfield, but combined with a single author and 4 seeds it means the CIFAR-100 "+0.51 over LIET" is a tuned-vs-published comparison, not a like-for-like one.

**A quiet undeclared component.** Appendix A notes that "the CIFAR-10 runs of Eq. (2) additionally carry a small wrong-logit dispersion term (weight 0.3) inherited from the method's development lineage; it is disabled on CIFAR-100/Tiny-ImageNet." A term present on exactly the dataset where the guard is validated, absent everywhere else, and described as inherited rather than ablated, is precisely the kind of detail that should have a one-line ablation. It may well be inert, but the reader cannot check.

**Baseline health.** NuAT's CIFAR-100 number (14.41±3.29) is catastrophic and its Tiny-ImageNet number (10.20±0.44) barely above the trivial; GAT (22.06) and FGSM-PCO (19.97) are similarly dead on CIFAR-100. Using the benchmark's released numbers is the fair choice, but it means several "fast baselines" in the CIFAR-100 column are collapse artifacts rather than tuned competitors, which inflates the apparent margin over "the fast-method frontier" as a category. The honest comparison set on CIFAR-100 is LIET, FGSM-PGI, and the FGSM-UAP family, and against LIET the margin is +0.51 — real, but small.

**What is genuinely well done.** The controls are the strongest part of the empirical work and are exactly the ones a skeptical reader would ask for: a detector-free fixed-epoch reset isolating the anchor from the detection signal (49.99), a strength control showing an always-on pull destroys training (8.15), a coupled-loss fallback showing it is the *reset* not extra regularization (7.05), and a clean negative result on vanilla FGSM-AT (0.00 / 38.34) that defines the method's applicability boundary instead of hiding it. The geometric argument for *why* the trigger must be a robustness measurement rather than a clean-function statistic — "a trigger measured on the clean function inherits the clean function's blindness" — is a design principle worth stealing regardless of whether one adopts this method. The Kendall-τ analysis (healthy methods τ=0.82, collapsed seed τ=0.386) is also a better answer to "is this gain real?" than most robustness papers bother to give.

**Red flags, summarized.** (1) Guard validated on one dataset, effectively one seed. (2) Sign flip under PGD-50, described as "similar ordering." (3) Hyperparameters swept on the reporting benchmark. (4) An unablated auxiliary loss term present only on the dataset where the guard works. (5) Single author evaluating on his own benchmark with his own attack. None of these invalidate the mechanistic finding, which stands on its own measurements; together they mean the *method* claim ("strongest fast method on all three datasets") is softer than the *analysis* claim.

## Strengths

- The core measurement is clean, reproducible in principle, and answers a question the field had been working around for five years: where in weight space the CO model goes, and why an optimizer that was robust an epoch earlier cannot find its way back.
- The line search along the actual drift direction ($w(\alpha)=\bar w+\alpha(w-\bar w)$, attack recomputed at every $\alpha$) is the right instrument, and the ±7.5% vs −69–77% result is a crisp, memorable quantification of "blind."
- The EMA observation is elegant and nearly free: the object needed for the fix is already maintained by every FAT protocol for checkpoint selection, so the guard costs zero additional forward passes.
- The threshold is chosen from a measured gap (6.5 vs 9.6) rather than tuned, and the paper shows the failure mode when you miss the gap (θ=10 → 7.94 on a bit-identical trajectory) — a level of honesty about a hyperparameter that is rare.
- Negative results are reported with numbers, not omitted: vanilla FGSM-AT cannot be anchored (0.00 / 38.34), a high-weight coupled config never establishes a robust region (3 seeds die), the continuous-pull variant fails 1 of 5 seeds, and the two-pass base without the clean branch is worse (49.85–49.93).
- The sample-level analysis of the cliff (staged deaths, margin-ordered, cross-replica ρ=0.48) replaces "a simultaneous global event" with a sweep, which is a more accurate picture and explains why no epoch-level precursor exists.
- Evaluation is unusually thorough for this subfield: full AutoAttack plus CR on full test sets, per-seed tables for every mean, and a per-sample difficulty-conservation check (τ) that rules out evaluation-attack gaming.
- Code and checkpoints are released.

## Limitations

- **Seed counts are 4/4/3**, and zero CO in four seeds is consistent with a true CO rate as high as 53% (one-sided 95% binomial bound). The guard's "removes CO in every run" is a four-sample statement.
- **The guard is only exercised on CIFAR-10.** On CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet it never fires, so those results test the re-balanced base method, not the guard.
- **Per-dataset coupling weights** were found by sweeps and are not universal; the CIFAR-100 response is a narrow inverted U, so the peak may not transfer.
- **Not every base-method failure is validation-visible.** One collapsed seed kept validation PGD-10 at 0.592 while test PGD-50 fell to 25.6 and clean accuracy inflated to 92.6% — the validation distribution itself was memorized. The guard cannot see this flavor, which bounds the method's coverage to CO events visible in validation robustness.
- **The propositions are informal.** The logit-margin construction is a sketch, not an existence proof at network scale; Proposition 2's "no persistent bias" assumption is the method's applicability boundary and is asserted from measurement rather than derived.
- **Instrumentation perturbs the random stream**: adding batch-level probes changes which seeds collapse, even though the dynamics for a given draw are deterministic — a real reproducibility hazard for anyone trying to replicate Sec. 3.
- **Three forward passes per step** make the method ~30–50% more expensive than the cheapest two-pass fast methods (1.05 h vs 0.7–0.8 h); the "roughly half the cost of multi-step" framing is true only against PGD-AT, not against the fast frontier it competes with.
- **Evaluation is confined to CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny-ImageNet at ε=8/255 with ResNet18/PreActResNet18**; no ImageNet-scale or transformer experiments, and no other threat models (ℓ₂, ℓ₁, larger ε).

## Relevance

论文属于"训练稳定性与可观测性"这一支：它研究的是一个训练过程内部的静默失效——损失与单步攻击指标全部正常，而真实鲁棒性在一个 epoch 内归零——并给出"用协议自带的 EMA 作为可观测的参照物来检测并回滚漂移"的方案。对关心"训练日志看不见的失败模式如何被发现和修复"的读者，这篇的测量范式（配对检查点 + 沿漂移方向的线搜索 + 找到健康/失效之间的统计空隙来定阈值）比它的具体算法更值得迁移。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the guard generalize beyond CIFAR-10? The paper never constructs a setting where CIFAR-100 or Tiny-ImageNet actually CO under the re-balanced base — what base configuration would make those datasets a real test of the guard rather than of the nuclear weight?
2. Why does PGD-50 reverse the ordering (LIET 55.97 vs 54.63)? Is the anchored model's robustness concentrated in a way that AutoAttack's APGD steps miss but 50-step PGD finds, or is it a checkpoint-selection artifact?
3. The wrong-logit dispersion term (weight 0.3) is present only on CIFAR-10. What does removing it cost? If it is inert, why is it there; if it is not, it is part of the method and belongs in the main text.
4. Proposition 2 assumes the blind displacement is stationary with no persistent bias. Is there a measurable diagnostic — beyond "the EMA stays robust" — that predicts *a priori* whether a given FAT method satisfies this, so the guard's applicability could be checked before training rather than after it fails?
5. The "validation-memorization" failure mode (validation PGD 0.592, test PGD-50 25.6, clean 92.6%) is caught by nothing on the validation side. Would a held-out split drawn from a different distribution, or the attack-direction cosine as a second selection statistic (which the appendix shows rejects the other flavor at zero cost), close this gap?
6. If the drift is diffuse across shared features (energy Gini 0.79, top-20% tensor rollback recovers only 0.043 → 0.133 of 0.409), does that rule out any targeted repair, or is there a projection that isolates the blind component more cheaply than a full EMA copy?
