Why this matters

Your agent chain can silently drop or rewrite a user requirement between hops; a signature proves integrity, not that the requirement was ever extracted correctly.

from · Relevance
Critical edge

Most of the 60.4 pp headline comes from the in-transit-mutation cell, where HMAC rejection is guaranteed by construction; excluding it, the rates are 58.4% vs 13.5%.

02

The instrument

1,440 trajectories · 6 arms · 5 threats + T5
  1. 01 Request

    Only the orchestrator reads it: three integer settings, two mandatory requirements, one withdrawn-draft decoy.

  2. 02 Manifest

    Provenance filter → predicate check → normalize → HMAC-SHA256. The key never enters a prompt.

  3. 03 Handoff

    A relay carries the manifest to workers A and B; the merger assembles one final assignment.

  4. 04 Verify

    Receipt and predicates checked in microseconds by code — no model, no access to gold.

  5. 05 Commit or abstain

    One repair round if flagged; a still-violating decision is not committed.

How the comparison is kept honest

  • 48 tasks, built to be scored. Six structural lineages × eight variants; the target requirement always spans both branches, so no single worker can satisfy it alone.
  • Six matched arms. S / J / JL / M / MS / MSO share byte-identical prompts and output format, isolating structure, checking, signing and extraction error.
  • Frozen before the first call. Hypotheses, cut order, parser rules and stop rules locked under SHA-256 manifests; five amendments disclosed verbatim.
  • Paired inference. Exact McNemar with Holm correction; task-clustered bootstrap CIs (B = 10,000); 1,440 scored roots across two families plus a 12-task frontier probe.
Protocol frozen · SHA-256 · 5 amendments

Trusted base: harness code, not the model. Out of scope: a compromised harness, a compromised orchestrator, key theft.

03

The verdict

What holds up / where it breaks

Holds up

+
  • The arm ladder is the right design. Six matched handoff forms isolate structure, checking, signing and extraction error — the thing most handoff papers never separate.
  • The oracle control bounds the worry. MSO turns "extraction might be lossy" into 11.2 pp of residual and makes the central claim falsifiable.
  • It preregisters an attack on its own defense. T5 is reported as a loss: 18 of 18 signed-manifest violations had a manifest corrupted before signing.
  • The shadow checker is paired, not parallel. Verifier and LLM checker see identical inputs on the same trajectory, compared per requirement.
  • Failure is disclosed, not smoothed. The failed pilot, parser fix, swapped second family, non-equivalent H3b and gpt-oss reversal are all in the paper.

Breaks

−
  • The grammar is a toy. Every requirement is eq / le / sum_le / implies over three integers; the promised provenance-only analysis never appears.
  • One topology, one 8B family. Two branches, 48 tasks; extraction precision (70.4%) is likely to degrade first at 12–15 requirements.
  • Abstention is counted as safety. MS declines 39.6% of benign runs, and no end-to-end completion number captures that cost.
  • One trust domain. HMAC means signer = verifier; cross-vendor A2A/AP2 needs Ed25519, key distribution, identity binding and revocation.
  • No public artifact. Task bank, freeze manifests and run logs are "on request" — for a preregistration study, that is the evidence.
04

Open questions

From the paper's own list
  1. Does 70.4% extraction precision survive 12–15 requirements across four hops?
  2. Would a second independent extraction, or one-click user confirmation, recover most of the 11.2 pp?
  3. How much of the 19.7 pp verifier edge survives a stronger checker — thinking on, 70B, self-consistency?
  4. What is the right fail-closed policy when the manifest is unparseable, as in 22.2% of gpt-oss tasks?
  5. With Ed25519 and cross-vendor keys, does receipt rejection still work — and what does revocation look like?
