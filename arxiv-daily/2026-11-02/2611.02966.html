Why this matters

Low-bit formats lose accuracy from grid shape and scale search. This separates the two — and shows your inherited grid may be the wrong default.

Relevance

Critical edge

The headline 41–67% NVFP4 gain is mostly the missing scale search, not the grid — against a properly scaled baseline the grid buys 4.6–34.4%.

Critical assessment

01

How the grid works

Drop-in LUT contents · same storage, same decode

  1. Format. Rows split into 16-weight blocks. Each weight is an index into a 2q-entry E4M3 table, scaled by one signed E4M3 value per block — q + 0.5 bits per weight, table excluded.
  2. Grid. Levels sample sinh(αuk) on a uniform lattice, shift by c, normalize to 1. α slides from INT-like (→0) to FP-like (large); c breaks the mirror symmetry.
  3. Free resolution. The scale's sign is otherwise idle. With c ≠ 0 a negative scale selects −G, so G and −G interleave: up to 2q+1 values — 16 at 3 bits, against 8 / 9 / 13 for c = 0, INT3 and NF3.
  4. Selection. Two parameters are cheap to scan: a two-stage box search minimizes full-model next-token KL on 64 calibration windows, then RTN or GPTQ applies the grid. Every LUT baseline shares the table, scale and 25-multiplier signed scale search, so the grid is the only variable.

03

Strengths & limitations

A clean experiment, short of a systems result

Strengths

  • One variable, honestly isolated. Table, block scale, lever, calibration and evaluation are held fixed across INT, NF, BOF and BOFS.
  • Two parameters instead of 2q. Small enough to tune against the real objective, with a drift guard that rejects stale scans.
  • The sign-bit mirror is free. Double the usable levels with zero storage, zero extra decode and no second table.
  • Candid where it loses. Five losses named, three conceded to noise, and the per-layer Lloyd codebook admitted 6.5% better.

Limitations

  • No systems evidence. No kernel, latency, throughput or energy measurement for a paper selling a hardware format.
  • NVFP4 is not apples-to-apples. Unsigned scale, no search; the abstract leads with the unadjusted 41–67%.
  • Objective equals metric. (α, c) fitted on KL, scored on held-out KL from the same corpus; independent accuracy gains are 0.1–0.7 points.
  • Narrow scope, single runs. Weight-only, decoder-only, instruction-tuned 3B–35B, FineWeb-Edu only; GPTQ changes 41–42% of weights across GPUs.
04

Open questions

What would change the verdict

  1. How much of the 4.6–34.4% grid edge survives once NVFP4 gets an equivalent signed-scale search?
  2. Does the mirror trick hold at block size 32 or 128, where multi-grid advantages are reported to vanish?
  3. Does a grid fitted on web text transfer to code, math or multilingual text?
  4. Can (α, c) be predicted from cheap weight statistics, skipping the calibration scan entirely?
