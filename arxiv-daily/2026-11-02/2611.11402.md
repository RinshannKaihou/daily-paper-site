> **一句话总结：** 针对专家并行 MoE 解码中"每层每步路由都在变、静态 EPLB 放置跟不上"的问题，作者提出 Relief——一种在副本图上做双向成对整数折半松弛（Gauss–Seidel 形式的 min–max 负载均衡）的算法，3 种实现（CUDA kernel 13–28 µs、PCIe FPGA 2.8–4.1 µs、片内固定功能单元 0.7–1.7 µs）都达到每步最优解的 99.5% 以上，在 Qwen3-30B-A3B 的 GSM8K 路由上把 G=16/100 µs 层的有效利用率从 EPLB 静态放置的 72.5% 提到 86.9%（kernel）和 99.1%（片内引擎），代价是 0.14 mm²、约 0.02% 的加速器晶体管。

> **TL;DR:** Relief replaces the per-step min–max LP for MoE replica splitting with a few passes of integer pairwise halving over the replica graph, then implements it three ways — a CUDA kernel (13–28 µs), an FPGA card over PCIe (2.8–4.1 µs), and a 0.14 mm² fixed-function block (0.7–1.7 µs). On decode routing from Qwen3-30B-A3B (128 experts), OLMoE-1B-7B and Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B over GSM8K and WildChat, it attains 99.5–99.8% of the per-step LP optimum. Scored as effective utilization (balance discounted by decision latency), the kernel lifts the deployed EPLB static placement from 72.5% to 86.9% of the optimum on Qwen3 GSM8K at G=16, T_moe=100 µs, and the on-die engine to 99.1% (+26.6 pts); across 192 layer–group combinations Relief wins 190 with a median margin of 19.1 pp. The real contribution is the argument that the split must be re-decided every step and that only silicon makes that affordable — the algorithm itself is a textbook relaxation.

## 核心贡献

- **诊断"决策频率"问题**：把一个 split 多复用一步就损失其相对静态基线收益的 62–91.6%；即使给周期方案免费的、全知的重新放置（clairvoyant re-placement），G=64/GSM8K 上 N=2 就退化到静态基线。因此任何粗于"每步一决策"的调度都拿不到这份收益。
- **Relief 算法**：把 LPLB/FineMoE 的 min–max 线性规划（Eq. 2）换成一个全整数的双向成对松弛——沿副本图的边，每次把两端负载差折半（截断到发送侧剩余 token 数），原地写入负载表（Gauss–Seidel 而非 Jacobi），几轮 pass 即收敛到每步最优的几个百分点之内。工作集就是 G 个负载值本身，不再随 G² 增长。
- **三种部署、同一比特结果**：CUDA kernel（服务 GPU 的一个 SM 上）、PCIe FPGA 卡（XCVH1582，139 MHz）、以及加速器旁的固定功能单元（0.14 mm² ASAP7、10 M 晶体管、37,434 个触发器），三者跑同一份调度、返回逐比特相同的 split；RTL 在 128/128 个验证程序上与定点参考完全一致。
- **提出并贯彻"有效利用率"（Ueff）指标**：Ueff = 空间均衡度 / (1 + T_solve/T_moe)，把均衡收益与决策延迟放进同一个数里，并沿 T_moe 从 100 µs 扫到 2000 µs，用一个单 GPU 研究覆盖不同互联、batch 与 GPU 代际。
- **反证"MoE 动态性使负载均衡不可能"的说法**：引用的 ISCA 论文断言 MoE 的动态性阻止任何负载均衡方案把激活专家均匀分布到 GPU 上；本文用决策频率测量与 190/192 的胜率给出反例。

## Key Contributions

- **A decision-frequency argument, measured rather than asserted.** The paper shows that reusing a split for a single extra decode step forfeits 62–91.6% of the utilization it recovers over the static baseline, and that even a cost-free clairvoyant periodic re-placement with a re-fitted frozen split collapses to the static baseline by N=2 at G=64. This reframes MoE balancing as a latency problem, not an optimization-quality problem.
- **Relief: an all-integer pairwise relaxation that makes the LP unnecessary.** A pass walks a compiled transfer list, halving the load gap on each replica edge, truncated by the tokens still at the home rank, reading and writing a live G-entry load table. Working set is the load vector (12 bytes per rank), versus the exact solver's 156G²+160G+40 bytes.
- **Three deployments under one metric.** Kernel, FPGA, and fixed-function silicon return bit-identical splits and differ only in T_solve, so every margin between their rows is attributable to interface geometry. The paper is explicit that no deployment is a prerequisite of the method.
- **Ueff as a joint scoring function.** Eq. 1 charges each scheme its decision latency against the layer it serves, so balance and latency are ranked together; this is what exposes the exact GPU solver as *worse than doing nothing* at short layers despite holding all of the attainable balance.
- **A concrete negative result about static placement.** EPLB's advantage over no balancing changes sign at G=64 (+16.7/+18.6/+9.4/−3.5 pts of spatial utilization at G=8/16/32/64 on GSM8K), traced with a controlled probe to the planner's duplicate map presuming a split that adapts.

## Methodology

**Problem formulation.** For a fixed placement of E experts onto G ranks with D=2 replicated slots per rank, rank i carries a fixed load f_i from non-replicated experts plus shares of D replicated experts whose second copy sits on a peer σ(i,d). The split chooses x_{i,d} ∈ [0,1], the fraction of expert (i,d)'s tokens kept at home. The exact optimum is min z s.t. L_i(x) ≤ z ∀i (Eq. 2), an LP with N_C = G+GD variables and 3G constraints. Total load is conserved, so minimizing the peak maximizes the mean-to-peak ratio.

**The metric.** U_eff = [Σ L_i / (G·max L_i)] · [T_moe/(T_solve + T_moe)] = spatial/(1 + T_solve/T_moe). Every scheme's decision is charged as fully blocking (conservative); periodic alternatives are granted their re-optimization at zero cost, and the split's own communication (a routing table, no expert weights) is folded into T_solve.

**The algorithm (Algorithm 1).** One pass iterates over transfers t=(u,v) in list order: gap ← L[u]−L[v]; delta ← |gap|≫1 (truncating halve); limit ← mass[t] if gap>0 else tok[t]−mass[t]; delta ← min(delta, limit); if delta > ε, L[u] −= sgn(gap)·delta, L[v] += sgn(gap)·delta, mass[t] −= sgn(gap)·delta. Because every move lands in the table immediately, the pass is a Gauss–Seidel relaxation in integer arithmetic, and the sign of the gap makes it bidirectional — flow moves toward the lighter copy from either side. Emission converts each transfer's remaining mass to a fraction with one reciprocal. Ablating the live read (snapshot + damping = damped Jacobi) needs 4× the passes and loses 4.3/9.2 points of attainment; ablating the reverse move loses 1.04/1.08 points.

**Scheduling.** The transfer list is a proper edge colouring of the replica graph, computed once per placement (minutes apart, when weights migrate). Each colour class is a perfect matching of ⌊G/2⌋ rank-disjoint transfers, so a round's writes never collide and the kernel needs no atomics — one barrier per round is the only synchronization. Greedy colouring reaches the 2D lower bound on every measured configuration.

**Pass count.** I* = argmax_p s[p]/(1 + [L_if + (a+bp)P]/T_moe), evaluated offline per (G, layer duration) and passed in as a kernel argument or descriptor word, since only the driver knows T_moe. I* spans 6 to 64 across Table II's cells.

**Deployments.** (a) Kernel: whole solve in one thread block, load table in shared memory behind a ~25 ns load-to-use path with no store-to-load bypass; needs only one SM of a GPU the deployment already owns. (b) Silicon: load table in registers, wires into the arithmetic, lane array of G/2 lanes, 14+8I cycles at G=8 and 28+8I at G=64, one engine per die as a bus master that consumes the counts gating just produced and emits the split the dispatch waits on; 4 bus transactions per solve vs 385 for a slave-port design (96×). (c) FPGA: same RTL parameterised to G, solves the group's instance once and the host distributes, charged 2 µs for two PCIe round trips.

**Evaluation setup.** Routing captured from Qwen3-30B-A3B (128 experts, top-8, 48 layers), OLMoE-1B-7B (64 experts, top-8, 16 layers), Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B (60 experts, top-4, 24 layers), on GSM8K and WildChat. Placements are D=2 tori at G ∈ {8,16,32,64} (C2×C4 up to C8×C8), two replica slots per rank. Every scheme runs on the same EPLB placement computed from each layer's mean load; the no-balancing baseline keeps the default placement. All aggregates are layer-major (48 records per layer for the 128-expert family); pooling across layers is explicitly rejected because it fits the placement to an "average layer that appears in no trace."

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline utilization on Qwen3-30B-A3B / GSM8K, G=16, T_moe=100 µs:** Relief kernel 86.9% of the per-step optimum vs EPLB static 72.5% (+14.4 pts) and no-balancing 50.3% (+36.6); the on-die engine reaches 99.1% (+26.6 pts over EPLB). At G=64 the same cells are 84.4% (kernel) and 98.9% (silicon) against EPLB's 70.7% and no-balancing's 82.8% — so the static scheme is *worse than doing nothing* at the largest group, and the kernel's margin over "do nothing" collapses to +1.6 pts.
- **The exact GPU LP loses to no balancing at short layers.** Charged only its measured 102 µs latency and *granted the optimum's balance for free*, LPLB scores 49.5% at G=16/100 µs — below the no-balancing row's 50.3% — and 83.1% at 500 µs. It cannot launch at G≥32 at all: its 156G²+160G+40-byte shared-memory footprint exceeds the 99 KiB the measured GPU admits after opt-in (launch stops at G=25), reaching 634 KiB at G=64, 2.8× the 227 KiB ceiling of H100/B200. FineMoE's CPU LP (207–676 µs) averages 54.0%.
- **Algorithmic attainment (Table III, 128-expert GSM8K routing, I=12, Q11.20 fixed point):** Relief reaches 99.84% of the optimum at G=16 and 99.51% at G=64. Removing the reverse move drops to 98.80/98.43; snapshot loads with damping (Jacobi) to 95.55/90.35; water-filling relaxation to 86.23/75.22; proportional redistribution to 93.09/81.04; the deployed hash split sits at 72.54/70.80. Running the *exact LP* in the same 32-bit Q11.20 word collapses it to 73.68/73.73, while Relief in FP32 is 99.68/98.74 — the fixed-point form is not a compromise here.
- **Latency scales flat in G, which is the whole point.** Silicon 0.75 µs at G=8 → 0.79 µs at G=64 (0.7–1.7 µs across Table II cells), FPGA 2.8–4.1 µs, kernel 13–28 µs. The kernel is 3.7–7.3× faster than the exact solver call-for-call, and 19–29× slower than the engine for bit-identical splits. Working set grows at 12 bytes per rank versus the solver's 156G² bytes, which crosses every shipping GPU's shared-memory capacity by G=39.
- **Breadth across 192 combinations.** At the production operating point (500 µs) Relief attains the best deployable U_eff in 190 of 192 layer–group combinations, with a median margin of 19.1 pp and a maximum of 31.4 pp; per-G medians fall from the low twenties at G∈{8,16} to 4.1 pp at G=64 as intra-rank pooling thins. The two exceptions are G=64 layers where the default placement with no balancing wins by 9–12% — Relief still sits within 0.6% of *its own placement's* optimum there, so the shortfall is the placement, not the split.
- **Cost.** 0.14 mm² of standard cells in a 0.35 mm² outline, 37,434 flip-flops, 1.29 M cells ≈ 10 M transistors = 0.02% of an A100/H100 die by area and 0.005–0.021% by transistor count (0.06% if the whole floorplan outline is charged, still an order of magnitude below Morpheus's 0.93% on-die controller). FPGA: 278,895 LUTs (16% of the XCVH1582), 36,083 FFs, 20 DSPs, 0 BRAM, timing closed at 7.2 ns / 139 MHz. Engine duty cycle stays under 3% of a layer's duration at every operating point.
- **Decision frequency is the binding constraint, not solver quality.** One step of split reuse forfeits 62–91.6% of the gain; a reused split falls below the static baseline by N=4 in three of four trace/group settings and by N=8 in all four; free clairvoyant joint re-fitting collapses to static by N=2 at G=64 on GSM8K. On GSM8K at G=64 the per-step split wins all 48 layer records at every N ≥ 2.

## Authors & Credibility

Jingyuan Li and Hayden So are at the University of Hong Kong (So's group works on FPGA/accelerator architecture and systems); Maolin Wang is at HKUST. The paper is a single-author-team systems/architecture submission (cs.AR, cross-listed cs.DC), 13 pages, 4 tables, 10 figures, 36 references. The reference list is unusually well matched to the claim: it engages the exact-solver line it displaces (LPLB, FineMoE, MoETuner), the production systems whose oblivious replica pick it criticizes (vLLM, SGLang, DeepEP, EPLB, DeepSeek-V3), the hardware-load-balancer lineage it borrows its argument from (RPCValet, Turbo, Intel's Xeon DLB, AWB-GCN), and the device-level MoE work it explicitly declares orthogonal (Duplex, MoNDE, Pre-gated MoE, ProMoE, Sem-MoE, MoE-OPU). Methodology is borrowed deliberately — the T_moe sweep follows Turbo's parameterization — and the paper says so.

Credibility signals worth noting: the RTL is bit-exact against the fixed-point reference on 128/128 verification programs; cycle counts come from RTL simulation on the real traces at every group size and family; the silicon area/timing come from an OpenROAD flow on ASAP7 with the caveat that parasitics are global-route estimates; the FPGA design is placed and routed to timing closure. The paper volunteers two findings that hurt its own narrative (EPLB below no-balancing at G=64; Relief losing 2 of 192 cells) and traces both to a mechanism rather than hand-waving. It also reports an AI-use statement (Claude for clarity editing only). The main credibility gap is that everything is trace-driven and analytic: no wall-clock serving run, no vLLM/SGLang integration, no measured tokens/s.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty is modest at the algorithm level and strong at the systems level.** Relief is a Gauss–Seidel pairwise relaxation of a min–max load-balancing problem on a graph — a shape that diffusion and dimension-exchange load balancing have occupied for decades, and the paper is candid about the framing ("the procedure is a Gauss–Seidel relaxation of Eq. 2 in integer arithmetic"). The genuinely new content is (i) the measured claim that decision frequency, not solution quality, is the binding constraint, and (ii) the demonstration that a 0.14 mm² block makes per-step decisions free. Readers looking for a new optimization algorithm will be underwhelmed; readers looking for an argument that MoE balancing belongs in silicon will not.

**The strongest empirical result is also the most fragile one.** EPLB scoring *below* no balancing at G=64 (+16.7/+18.6/+9.4/−3.5 pts across G=8/16/32/64) is a striking claim, and the paper's own explanation narrows it: their planner "spreads experts across ranks by their window means and copies each rank's heaviest expert to a fixed neighbour," so the copies of a layer's two heaviest experts land on one rank in *every* G=64 layer probed, giving that rank a median 6.8× the median rank's load. That is a property of this reimplementation's duplicate-map heuristic, not necessarily of DeepSeek's production EPLB. The permuted-mean control (0.24, matching the default) correctly isolates the *fit* as the source of the loss, but it does not establish that deployed EPLB exhibits the same pathology. A reader should treat "static placement is worse than nothing at G=64" as "a mean-fitted placement with a fixed heaviest-expert-neighbour duplicate map is worse than nothing at G=64."

**Ueff percentages in Table II are placement-relative, and the table invites misreading.** Every cell is a fraction of the per-step optimum *on that scheme's own placement*, so the silicon row's 98.9% at G=64 does not mean 98.9% absolute utilization — the absolute spatial figure on that fitted placement is 0.29, versus 0.24 for the default placement with no balancing. The paper handles this honestly in §V-B's prose (0.21/0.24/0.29) and in Fig. 4, but Table II alone can be read as "99% of ideal" when the achievable ceiling is itself set by a placement the paper elsewhere calls harmful. A single absolute-Ueff column alongside the relative one would have removed the ambiguity.

**No end-to-end serving result.** The entire evaluation is a per-layer GPU-time accounting model (Eq. 1) over captured routing traces. There is no measured tokens/s, no inter-token latency, no TTFT, and no integration into vLLM or SGLang. The conversion from a 14–27 point Ueff gain to wall-clock speedup depends on the expert stage's share of total decode time, which the paper never decomposes — it cites that MoE-layer compute utilization is below 11% and that decode draws ~half of serving GPU power, but does not project what a 27-point Ueff recovery buys in end-to-end throughput. Given that Eq. 1 charges the decision as fully blocking while assuming the counts' all-reduce is free (inherited from LPLB's "solve redundantly on every rank" arrangement), the real number could go either way.

**Trace provenance is under-specified for reproduction.** "Routing is captured from three real MoE families on two datasets each" is the entire description: no batch size, no request concurrency, no number of decode steps, no prompt-selection procedure, no sampling temperature. Since the paper's central claim is about per-step routing volatility, the trace generation parameters are load-bearing, and a reader cannot regenerate them. This is the single biggest reproducibility gap.

**Baselines are simultaneously generous and narrow.** Granting LPLB and FineMoE the optimum's balance and charging only latency is generous and makes Relief's win more convincing. But the comparison set omits the obvious middle ground: a *cheap approximate* solver on the GPU at the same latency budget as Relief (say a 15 µs first-order or greedy method), and a *host-side* solver overlapped with the previous layer's expert compute. The grouped 8-rank LP (33 µs, 91.6% balance ceiling) is the closest competitor and is genuinely well chosen, but it is one point on that spectrum, not a sweep. The paper also never tests D>2 replication or non-torus topologies — the D=2 torus is what makes each colour class a perfect matching of ⌊G/2⌋ transfers, so the scheduling argument is topology-specific by construction.

**Two smaller issues.** First, the 37.3-point headline recovery does not appear in Table II (whose largest EPLB-to-silicon gap is 34.6 pts at Qwen1.5 G=32); it presumably comes from a Fig. 7 point or a layer-level record, and the paper never pins it to a cell, which makes the abstract's most quotable number the least traceable. Second, power is left unreported ("vectorless activity estimation diverges on this netlist's reconvergent arithmetic"), so the 0.02%-of-transistors claim cannot be checked against the energy cost of running the block every layer every step — at a <3% duty cycle it is probably negligible, but "probably" is doing the work.

## Strengths

- The decision-frequency experiment (§II-C) is the kind of measurement that changes how a reader thinks about the problem, and it is done properly: every periodic alternative gets free, clairvoyant re-optimization and still loses.
- The Ueff metric is the right scoring function for this problem, and applying it uniformly across software, FPGA and silicon makes the three deployments genuinely comparable rather than three separate papers stapled together.
- Bit-identical splits across all three deployments is a strong engineering result and removes a whole class of "which implementation did you benchmark" objections.
- The algorithm is small enough to be obviously correct: one table, one compiled list, integer halving, one reciprocal. No tuning knobs beyond the pass count, which is set in closed form from T_moe.
- Intellectual honesty in the awkward places: the G=64 EPLB reversal is probed rather than hidden, the two losing cells are reported with their cause, the exact LP's Q11.20 collapse is reported even though it complicates the "fixed point is fine" story, and the paper explicitly declines to claim that its engine is required.
- The related-work section correctly separates placement (EPLB, CRAFT, MoETuner), training-time shaping (auxiliary losses, BASE, expert-choice) and device-level execution (Duplex, MoNDE, Sem-MoE), and argues composability rather than competition.

## Limitations

- **Simulation-only evaluation.** No serving-stack integration, no wall-clock latency or throughput; every number is an analytic GPU-time model over offline traces.
- **Narrow topology family.** D=2 replication on power-of-two tori only. The edge-colouring schedule that makes rounds conflict-free is derived from that regularity; behaviour on hierarchical or irregular placements is untested.
- **Under-specified traces.** No batch size, concurrency, step count, or prompt selection, which makes the per-step volatility claim hard to reproduce or extrapolate to production traffic mixes (long context, code, multilingual).
- **Baseline scope.** One grouped-LP point, no cheap-GPU-approximation baseline, and an EPLB reimplementation whose G=64 pathology may not transfer to production EPLB.
- **Hardware numbers are pre-silicon.** ASAP7 educational PDK with global-route parasitics, no power, no tape-out; the 0.02% figure is an estimate, and the 0.14 mm² is a synthesis result rather than a placed-and-routed production block.
- **Only the expert stage is modeled.** The gain is expressed as a share of expert-stage GPU time; the paper never converts it to end-to-end decode speedup, so the practical payoff of a 27-point Ueff gain remains an open question.
- **Prefill is out of scope by construction**, and the paper is upfront about it: at prefill-scale batches any decision latency is negligible and routing is already smoothed.

## Relevance

直接落在"大规模 ML 计算基础设施"这条线上：专家并行服务里的 straggler / 慢节点问题、kernel 与通信的协同设计、以及用专用硬件把微秒级决策从关键路径上挪走——和关注"分布式推理里被浪费的 GPU 时间到底花在哪"的阅读兴趣高度重合。附带一层"极端性能优化下的可靠性"意味：全整数 Q11.20 定点实现反而比 FP32 更稳（LP 在同一字宽下崩到 73%），是低精度数值行为的一个正面案例。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. What does a 27-point Ueff recovery buy in end-to-end decode throughput on a real Qwen3-30B-A3B deployment — is the expert stage 20% or 60% of inter-token latency at the batch sizes that matter?
2. Does the G=64 EPLB reversal reproduce with DeepSeek's actual EPLB implementation, or is the "heaviest expert to a fixed neighbour" duplicate map specific to this reimplementation?
3. How does Relief behave under D>2 replication (an expert with 3–4 copies), where the replica graph is no longer 2-regular and a colour class is no longer a perfect matching?
4. Can the engine's per-step decision be overlapped with the previous layer's expert compute rather than charged as fully blocking, and how much of the remaining 0.5–1.5% gap to the optimum would that recover?
5. What is the all-reduce cost of the per-expert counts across 64 ranks, and does it stay hidden behind the dispatch at production batch sizes?
6. Would a cheap 10 µs GPU approximation (rather than the exact LP) close most of the kernel-to-engine gap, making the silicon block unnecessary below some group size?
