> **一句话总结：** 论文系统解剖了非前缀 KV-cache 复用（各 chunk 独立编码后拼接）在 RAG 场景下的精度损失，把它分解为"通用损失 / 同类别损失 / 相似性损失"三项，发现损失主要来自与答案 chunk 相似的干扰 chunk，且无法用小预算修补；于是主张把 5% 预算全部花在答案 chunk 本身——在 NQ-Open + Qwen3-8B 上把保留率从 token 级选择器的 0.16 提到 0.51（+0.35），并证明起作用的是"上下文兼容性"（针对复用缓存重算）而非"接近全预填充 KV"（重算后 KV 与全预填充 KV 的中位相对距离仍有 0.23–0.30）。

> **TL;DR:** This is a diagnostic paper, not a new system: it holds the answer-bearing chunk fixed and swaps the surrounding distractors across three 7B–8B models (Qwen3-8B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2) and four datasets (LoCoMo, NQ-Open, TriviaQA, Gorilla), showing that position-corrected non-prefix reuse loses 19–100% of the questions that full prefill answers, and loses *more* when distractors are same-category (Gorilla, Qwen3-8B: retention 0.82 under off-domain prose vs 0.00 under unrelated API docs). Because the damage is spread additively over all distractors, restoring one changes almost nothing (significant in 2 of 24 cases), while a 5% budget spent on the known target chunk beats the per-setting best token-level selector (CacheBlend/EPIC/ProphetKV/question-attention) in all nine open-domain QA and API-doc settings — e.g. NQ-Open Qwen3-8B 0.51 vs 0.16 (+0.35), Gorilla Mistral-7B 0.59 vs 0.10 (+0.49). The deployable version (move the retriever's top chunk last, recompute it) beats InfoFlow-style reordering at the same 5% budget in 6 of 9 settings, but its gains track retrieval hit rate (47–64%) and it is significantly *worse* than token selection for Llama-3.1-8B on Gorilla (−0.17).

## 核心贡献

- **把复用损失做成了可加的三项分解。** 固定目标 chunk 及其位置，只替换其他 chunk 的来源（OTHER = 域外散文、RAND = 同语料无关 chunk、SIM = 与目标相似的 chunk），定义 `1 − R(SIM) = [1 − R(OTHER)] + [R(OTHER) − R(RAND)] + [R(RAND) − R(SIM)]`，即通用项、同类别项、相似性项，并在 12 个 setting 上用配对 bootstrap 区间逐项检验。这是本文最有复用价值的方法论贡献：它让"复用掉多少点"变成"哪一类干扰掉的"。
- **量化了损失的规模与结构。** 位置校正复用在 Q*（全预填充答对且必须依赖上下文的题）上的保留率只有 0.15–0.81，即损失 0.19–1.00，12/12 显著；同类别项在 9/12 显著为正、从不显著为负，Gorilla 上最大（Qwen3-8B +0.82、Llama-3.1-8B +0.62，因为 RAND 保留率直接是 0.00）；相似性项在开放域 QA 六个 setting 全为正、五个显著。
- **否定了"恢复少量干扰 chunk"这条路线。** 恢复全部干扰 chunk 的全预填充 KV 比只恢复目标 chunk 更有效（12/12 更高、11 个显著，在 NQ-Open/TriviaQA/Gorilla 上收回 41–73% 的损失），但只恢复一个（最相似的或随机的）在 24 个 case 里只有 2 个显著——因为损失按"每个干扰 chunk 给失败赔率加一个权重"累加（式 2），只校准两个端点就能预测随机恢复 k 个 chunk 的曲线，且在 6 个开放域 QA setting 中的 5 个优于线性预测。
- **给出"上下文兼容性"这一机制解释，并配了可证伪的对照。** 把目标 chunk 的全预填充 KV 插回复用缓存，只带来 +0.11 [+0.06, +0.17]；而针对复用缓存重算目标 chunk 则显著优于插 KV，在 7/7 个 setting 上高出 0.13–0.38（Gorilla Qwen3-8B：0.25 vs 0.05）。关键是重算后的 KV 距全预填充 KV 仍有 0.23–0.30 的中位相对距离（未修复是 0.34–0.45），所以"更像全预填充"解释不了收益，"与被读取时的邻居缓存同源"才能。
- **Proposition 1：第一层的精确分解。** 在首层（token 只带 embedding 进入），复用与全预填充的 head 输出差可精确拆成 `α_u(o_cross − o_own) + (o_own − o_reuse)`，即跨 chunk 项 + 位置项；实测残差 ≤ 6e-7，位置项/跨 chunk 项范数比 0.31–0.63，且 ᾱ 随 chunk 位置单调上升（Spearman 0.94–1.00）。
- **证明"看 question attention 找目标 chunk"不可行。** 在复用缓存上，按 chunk 求和的问题注意力（d1）最多选中目标 12.5%，按上层平均（d2）最多 18.8%，而随机基线是 3.2–6.4%；d1 反而在 58–100% 的题上选中"紧邻问题的最后一个 chunk"。同一批检测器在全预填充缓存上能到 89.3%，说明这是复用缓存特有的失效，不是检测器本身弱。
- **可部署方案：把检索器 top-1 挪到最后再重算。** 由于 chunk 顺序在复用下是自由变量，把 top-1 移到问题前一位再重算，比原地重算高 0.07–0.18（9/9 显著），比同预算的 InfoFlow 式重排高 0.14–0.34（6/9 显著、从不显著更差）；挪动本身还让 token 级选择器提升 0.11–0.25（7 个按问题注意力排序 token 的 setting）。
- **诚实的边界报告 + 复现实验。** 对话记忆（LoCoMo）上 chunk 级重算相对 token 级选择器从不显著（−0.04 到 +0.03，即使 10% 预算）；10% 预算下 token 级选择器重新变得有竞争力；Qwen3-14B 与 Qwen3-32B 复现了论文抽检的全部五项结论。

## Key Contributions

- **A three-term decomposition of reuse loss that separates *what* the distractors are.** By holding the target chunk and its slot fixed and only varying distractor provenance, the paper converts a single retention number into universal / same-category / similarity components, each with paired bootstrap intervals. This is the transferable artifact: it tells a serving engineer whether a workload's loss comes from chunk isolation per se or from the retriever returning near-duplicates.
- **Evidence that the recoverable damage is not local.** All-distractor restoration beats target-only restoration in 12/12 settings, but single-distractor restoration is significant in only 2 of 24 cases, and an additive-odds model calibrated on just the two endpoints predicts the intermediate curve better than linear-in-R or linear-in-probit alternatives (squared error 0.06 vs 0.18 and 0.20).
- **"Contextual compatibility" as the causal variable, not KV distance.** Inserting the target's full-prefill KV gives +0.11 while recomputing against the reused cache gives +0.13 to +0.38 more, despite the recomputed KV remaining 0.23–0.30 (median relative L2) away from the full-prefill KV. The control in Table 34 (restore the earlier chunks too, interaction +0.14 [+0.08, +0.20]) isolates this cleanly.
- **An exact first-layer identity (Proposition 1)** splitting each head's deviation into a cross-chunk attention term and a position term, with a measured residual at float32 noise level, plus the empirical fact that a chunk's full-prefill attention mass on its predecessors rises monotonically with its slot.
- **A negative result with practical teeth:** question-attention-based target detection collapses on reused caches (≤18.8% vs 89.3% on full prefill), which invalidates a design assumption shared by ProphetKV-style and SurroKV-style selectors.
- **A deployable reordering rule** (retriever top-1 moved adjacent to the question, then recomputed) with an explicit retrieval-bounded failure analysis: gains are +0.34 to +0.84 on retrieval hits and −0.02 to −0.25 on misses, and the top-3 variant's gain is predictable from hit rate in 7 of 9 settings.

## Methodology

**Setup as a controlled intervention.** Each prompt is instruction prefix + m chunks + question. The answer-bearing chunk (the *target*) and its slot are fixed per question; only the other chunks change source:

- `OTHER` — Thoreau's *Walden*, cut to matched lengths (off-domain prose);
- `RAND` — unrelated chunks of the same kind (other conversations' sessions, passages retrieved for other questions, API docs from other task categories);
- `SIM` — chunks genuinely similar to the target: other sessions of the same LoCoMo conversation, the released DPR hard negatives for TriviaQA, Contriever-retrieved non-answer passages for NQ-Open, and the BGE-M3-nearest API docs (plus the reference solution) for Gorilla.

Chunk lengths are matched within 10% for 92–100% of replacements, and a normalised gold answer appears in a RAND/OTHER chunk for at most 1.9% of questions (4.6% for SIM), so the manipulation is not smuggling in the answer.

**Conditions.** *Full prefill* encodes the whole prompt in one pass. *Position-corrected reuse* encodes each chunk alone after the shared prefix, rotates its keys by `s_i − P`, concatenates the caches, then appends the question. A *no-context* baseline omits all chunks. Qwen3-8B (thinking disabled), Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2, bf16, greedy decoding, ≤32 new tokens (64 on Gorilla).

**The evaluation set Q\*.** Following Cestola et al. (2026), every metric is computed on questions the no-context baseline gets wrong and full prefill gets right under SIM, RAND *and* OTHER simultaneously — so full-prefill accuracy on Q\* is 1 by construction and reuse accuracy *is* retention R. This is the right conditioning for a retention metric (aggregate accuracy hides reuse damage, as Cestola et al. show), but it also means every reported "loss" is measured against a set chosen to be maximally sensitive.

**Repairs compared at matched token budget.** Token-level selectors: CacheBlend (second-layer KV deviation), EPIC (first k tokens per chunk), ProphetKV (question attention), and a question-attention rule; the rule is chosen per setting on 70 development questions. Chunk-level allocations spend the same budget on chosen chunks (target alone, target + 4 most similar, retriever top-1, retriever top-1 + 4, top-3 moved last). Budgets are 5% and 10% of context tokens; the target is a median 2.5–3.3% of the context on open-domain QA and Gorilla.

**Analysis machinery.** Paired 95% bootstrap intervals (2,000 resamples, resampling questions, or conversations for LoCoMo) with Holm correction where multiple settings are claimed; a first-layer decomposition verified numerically on CPU/float32; an additive-odds model of distractor contributions; entity-overlap analysis (spaCy `en_core_web_lg`) of wrong answers; and TTFT measurement on a single RTX 4090 D.

## Results & Highlights

- **The loss is large and universal-first.** On Q\*, position-corrected reuse retains R(SIM) = 0.15 on NQ-Open/Qwen3-8B and 0.20 on TriviaQA/Llama-3.1-8B against a full-prefill retention of 1.00 by construction — i.e. 85% and 80% of context-dependent questions are lost. Across all 12 settings the loss is 0.19–1.00 and every interval excludes zero; even with all distractors replaced by *Walden* prose the universal term is 0.11–0.92.
- **Same-category distractors dominate on API documentation.** Gorilla, Qwen3-8B: retention 0.82 under OTHER but 0.00 under RAND, a same-category term of +0.82 [+0.74, +0.89]; Llama-3.1-8B +0.62 [+0.53, +0.71]. On open-domain QA the similarity term is positive in all six settings and significant in five (TriviaQA/Llama +0.31 [+0.22, +0.41]); on LoCoMo only Qwen3-8B shows a significant similarity term (+0.16 [+0.08, +0.24]).
- **Wrong answers point at the similar passage.** Under SIM, 40–63% of Q\* reuse answers name an entity (capitalised span or ≥3-digit number) that occurs in a distractor but not in the target passage, versus 8–40% under RAND; the paired difference is significant in all six open-domain QA settings.
- **Restoring distractors beats restoring the target, but only in bulk.** NQ-Open/Qwen3-8B: retention 0.15 → 0.18 with the target's full-prefill KV (+0.03, n.s.) → 0.71 with all distractors restored (+0.56). "All distractors − target" is +0.53 [+0.44, +0.62] there and positive in 12/12 settings (11 significant), recovering 41–73% of the loss on the three open-domain sets. Restoring a *single* distractor (most similar or random) is significant in only 2 of 24 cases.
- **A 5% budget on the known target chunk beats every token-level selector on open-domain QA and API docs.** NQ-Open/Qwen3-8B: token selector 0.16 → target 0.51 (+0.35 [+0.27, +0.43]); Gorilla/Mistral-7B: 0.10 → 0.59 (+0.49 [+0.38, +0.59]); TriviaQA/Mistral-7B: 0.44 → 0.67 (+0.23). The nine-setting range of the gain is +0.10 to +0.49. It is **not** uniform: Llama-3.1-8B on NQ-Open gains only +0.15, and at a 10% budget the target allocation is −0.02 there and −0.09 on Gorilla, with the retriever variant at −0.17.
- **Contextual compatibility, not KV proximity.** Recomputing the target against the reused cache beats inserting its full-prefill KV in 7/7 settings by 0.13–0.38 (Gorilla/Qwen3-8B 0.25 vs 0.05; TriviaQA/Mistral-7B 0.65 vs 0.50), winning on 60 of 270 questions and losing on 4 — while the recomputed KV stays 0.23–0.30 (median relative L2) from the full-prefill KV versus 0.34–0.45 unrepaired.
- **Attention-based target detection collapses on reused caches.** d1 selects the target on ≤12.5% of development questions, d2 on ≤18.8%, against chance 3.2–6.4% — and d1 instead picks the chunk adjacent to the question on 58–100% of questions (100% in seven of twelve settings). The same detectors reach up to 89.3% on full-prefill caches.
- **Moving the retriever's top chunk last + recomputing it beats InfoFlow-style reordering.** At 5% budget: NQ-Open/Qwen3-8B 0.56 vs 0.40 (+0.16), Gorilla/Mistral-7B 0.43 vs 0.08 (+0.34); significant in 6 of 9, never significantly worse. Against token selection *on the same reordered prompt* it is never significantly worse and significantly better in four settings (+0.09 to +0.31).
- **Retrieval is the binding constraint.** On questions where the retriever's top-1 is not the target, the moved chunk loses to token selection by −0.02 to −0.25 (significant in four settings). Moving the top-3 chunks at 10% recovers part of this (+0.06 to +0.16 over top-1 only, significant in 7/9), but token selectors then win in three settings and lose in two.
- **Latency.** TTFT with Qwen3-8B on one RTX 4090 D, NQ-Open (4.6K context): full prefill 840 ms (p95 898) → reuse 53 ms → target 5% repair 92 ms → token 5% 162 ms. Reuse alone is 0.06× full prefill; the target repair is 0.11×, cheaper than the token selector's 0.19×.
- **A block-attention model fine-tuned for this layout still loses.** Tulu3-Block-FT (Ma et al. 2025) in its native block mode loses 0.60 of Q\* on NQ-Open, versus 0.82 for position-corrected reuse of its full-attention twin — better, but the similarity term remains +0.40 [+0.29, +0.50], so fine-tuning does not remove the distractor effect.
- **Replication at scale.** Qwen3-14B and Qwen3-32B reproduce all five tested results (loss size, both terms, target gain, moved-chunk gain); e.g. NQ-Open/32B loss 0.78 with target gain +0.38 and moved-chunk gain +0.35 over ProphetKV at 5%.

## Authors & Credibility

Yuxing Peng and Zhiqing Tang (corresponding) are at Beijing Normal University, Zhuhai (Faculty of Arts and Sciences / Institute of AI and Future Networks); Weijia Jia is at BNU Zhuhai and the Guangdong Key Lab of AI and Multi-Modal Data Processing at BNU-HKBU. Jia is a senior, well-published network/agent-systems researcher; Tang works on edge/LLM systems. This is a single-group systems-measurement paper from a Chinese university group rather than a serving-infrastructure lab (no vLLM/SGLang/DeepSpeed authors), which shows in the evaluation: strong statistical hygiene and careful controls, but a home-grown research engine and one consumer GPU (RTX 4090 D) rather than a production serving stack.

Credibility signals are unusually good for this genre: pre-registered-looking analysis choices (fixed Q\* definition, per-setting development split for baseline selection, Holm correction on the multi-setting claims), a stated AI-usage disclosure (GPT/Claude for code and editing), explicit negative results (LoCoMo, 10% budget, Llama-3.1-8B on Gorilla), and replication on two larger Qwen3 models. The weak spots are structural rather than rhetorical: everything is a re-implementation, and the main external comparator (InfoFlow KV) is re-implemented in the authors' own engine at matched *token* budget rather than matched latency — the authors say so in the Limitations.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The individual ingredients are known — non-prefix reuse loses accuracy (Cestola et al. 2026; Zhang et al. 2025 on attention entropy), token-level repair helps (CacheBlend, EPIC, ProphetKV), reordering is free (InfoFlow KV). What is new and genuinely useful is (a) the distractor-provenance manipulation and its three-term decomposition, and (b) the *contextual compatibility* claim, which is a real mechanistic correction: the field's implicit objective ("make the repaired KV close to the full-prefill KV") is shown to be the wrong target, since recomputation wins by 0.13–0.38 while remaining 0.23–0.30 away in relative L2. Proposition 1 is a small but exact result, honestly verified to float32 residual, and it explains *why* late chunks suffer more (ᾱ rises with slot; position term/cross term ratio 0.31–0.63). This is a diagnosis paper that changes what a repair should optimize, not a SOTA table.

**Rigor.** The statistics are better than typical for KV-cache papers: paired bootstrap over questions (conversations for LoCoMo), Holm correction where 9–12 settings are claimed, explicit "never significantly worse" phrasing, and effect sizes reported with intervals rather than stars. The controls are the strongest part: Table 22 separates "target + 4 similar" from "target + 4 random" and from "4 similar without the target" (which is significantly worse than token selection in 11/12 settings — a clean demonstration that the *target*, not similarity, is what matters); Table 34 restores the earlier chunks to isolate the interaction; Table 25 adds a similarity-gated router.

**Weak points I would press on in review.**

1. **Q\* conditioning inflates the headline.** Requiring full prefill to be right under *all three* distractor sources shrinks N to 36–151 per setting and selects for questions that are maximally context-sensitive. On the full evaluated sets (Table 3) reuse is much less catastrophic — on LoCoMo/Qwen3-8B, reuse accuracy is 0.36 vs full prefill 0.61 and no-context 0.11, and reuse actually *beats* no-context on LoCoMo and Gorilla. The abstract's "loses 19–100%" is true of Q\*, but a practitioner reading only the abstract will over-estimate the deployment damage. The paper does report Table 3, but the framing does not carry the caveat.
2. **The headline allocation uses an oracle.** "Target" assumes the answer-bearing chunk is known; the deployable "Retrieved" column is materially weaker and sometimes harmful (Gorilla/Llama-3.1-8B −0.17 at both budgets, NQ-Open/Llama −0.09 at 5%). The abstract's "budget is better spent on the target" is a statement about an oracle, and the honest deployable claim is narrower: move-and-recompute beats InfoFlow-style reordering, and only ties token selection.
3. **Tiny per-setting N in the most-quoted cells.** LoCoMo/Mistral-7B has N=36 in Q\*, Gorilla/Qwen3-8B N=89 with a token-selector baseline retention of 0.06; a +0.17 gain on 89 questions is ~15 questions. The intervals are honest, but several of the "significant in 6 of 9" claims rest on 9 settings with heterogeneous N and very different baseline strengths, and the unweighted "6 of 9" count gives a 36-question LoCoMo cell the same vote as a 151-question TriviaQA cell.
4. **Comparator implemented by the authors, at matched tokens not matched time.** InfoFlow KV is re-implemented (layer 22–25 attention norms, their default 15% budget compressed to 5%), and the comparison explicitly "matches the token budget, not latency." Since the paper's own argument is that *which* tokens you recompute matters for cost (contiguous chunk vs scattered tokens), a token-matched comparison is the weaker of the two possible ones — and the authors concede that in an optimised serving system the two may cost differently.
5. **No serving-system evaluation.** TTFT is measured in a research engine on one RTX 4090 D with a single request stream; there is no batching, no paged-attention allocator, no throughput, no cache-storage/memory accounting, and no comparison against simply re-prefilling the last few chunks (SAFE-KV suffix repair), which the paper discusses only qualitatively in Appendix F. For a paper whose framing is "where should the budget go," the absence of a cost model beyond TTFT is a real gap.
6. **Missing ablations I would want.** (a) Repair quality as a function of budget between 5% and 10% for chunk-level allocation — the 5%→10% reversal (target gain collapsing from +0.35 to +0.27 on NQ-Open/Qwen3-8B while the token baseline rises 0.16→0.24) is the single most consequential unexplained result, and it is attributed only to "the 10% Token baseline is stronger." (b) Number of distractors n is fixed per dataset (29–31); the odds model predicts loss should grow with n, but n is never varied. (c) No test on a retriever stronger than BGE-M3/BM25 (e.g. a reranker), even though every deployable conclusion is retrieval-bounded.
7. **One data quirk handled well, one less so.** Gorilla has R(RAND)=0.00 for all three models, so the similarity term is structurally non-positive there and the decomposition degenerates (the authors mark these cells with † rather than bolding them — good practice). Less well handled: TriviaQA's no-context baseline is 0.63–0.72, so Q\* there is a small, oddly selected residue of "hard" questions, and LoCoMo's F1≥0.5 threshold is generous enough that some "correct" answers are partial.
8. **Anonymous comparators.** SurroKV, StaleSpec and the decision-margin model are cited as "Anonymous, 2026a/b/c, submitted to ICLR 2027." SurroKV in particular is used as a baseline in Table 11 and discussed as a concurrent submission. Unverifiable comparators in a fast-moving niche are a mild red flag for reproducibility, even if the practice is currently common.

**Significance.** If the contextual-compatibility finding holds up, it is the kind of result that redirects a subfield: it says the objective for cache repair is "recompute against the cache you will read from," not "approximate the full-prefill state," and it says attention-over-reused-cache is the wrong signal for finding the answer chunk. Both are actionable and both are supported by controls rather than correlation. The practical recipe (move top-1 last, recompute it) is cheap, needs no training, and beats the published reordering baseline in most settings — but it only ties token selection, so the deployment win is modest.

## Strengths

- **The experimental design is the contribution.** Fixing the target and its slot while swapping distractor provenance is a clean intervention that isolates a variable everyone else confounds; the three-term decomposition falls out of it almost for free and is immediately reusable by other groups.
- **Statistical discipline.** Paired intervals everywhere, Holm correction on multi-setting claims, explicit reporting of non-significant and negative results (LoCoMo, 10% budget, Llama on Gorilla, the two settings where the three-chunk variant loses).
- **A mechanistic claim with a falsifying control.** The contextual-compatibility story predicts that restoring the earlier chunks should restore the value of inserting the full-prefill KV — and Table 34 shows exactly that interaction (+0.14 [+0.08, +0.20]), with recomputation-against-restored-cache reproducing full prefill on 269 of 270 questions. That is how a mechanism claim should be tested.
- **Honest accounting of the oracle.** The paper does not hide that "Target" is an oracle; Table 1 puts Target and Retrieved side by side, and Section 5 states the retrieval-bounded failure explicitly (moved chunk worse than token selection on misses, −0.02 to −0.25).
- **Practical byproducts.** The observation that chunk order is free under reuse and that late chunks benefit most (+0.13 first third, +0.35 middle, +0.47 last third) is a zero-cost scheduling hint for any non-prefix cache.
- **Replication beyond the 7B–8B band** (Qwen3-14B, Qwen3-32B, five results each) and a check on a model fine-tuned for block encoding — rare thoroughness for a measurement paper.

## Limitations

- **Only 7B–8B instruction-tuned models on four English datasets** as the main grid, with two larger Qwen3 models for replication only; no multilingual, no code, no long-context (>15K) regime, no MoE, no reasoning-mode (Qwen3 thinking is explicitly disabled).
- **Q\* is a small, adversarially selected subset** (36–151 questions per setting). Effect sizes on Q\* are not deployment effect sizes; Table 3's full-set numbers are the ones a practitioner should use, and they are far milder.
- **The deployable method needs a retriever and inherits its errors.** Top-1 hit rate is 47–64%; on misses the method is worse than token selection. The paper's own conclusion — "repair methods should locate the answer-bearing chunk" — is thus a call for better chunk identification, which it does not provide.
- **No real serving system.** Single-request TTFT on one RTX 4090 D in a research engine; no batching, throughput, memory, or cache-management cost. The 0.11× figure will not transfer directly to vLLM/SGLang.
- **Chunk-level allocation granularity is coarse.** On LoCoMo a session exceeds the 5% budget on 79–89% of questions, so the method degenerates to partial-session recomputation; the paper reports this as a null result rather than solving it.
- **The odds model is descriptive, not mechanistic.** It is calibrated on two endpoints and fits intermediate points; it is not derived from the attention decomposition, and it is fitted on 6 settings only (Gorilla excluded because R(0)≈0 makes the odds degenerate).
- **Comparator fidelity.** InfoFlow KV is a re-implementation at matched token budget; SurroKV, StaleSpec and BCR are cited from anonymous or very recent preprints. Independent reproduction of the headline comparison has not happened yet.
- **Latency of the repair path is measured but not modelled**, so the paper cannot say whether "recompute one contiguous chunk" or "recompute 5% scattered tokens" is cheaper in a paged-attention server — which is precisely the decision an operator faces.

## Relevance

这篇论文正对着"Agent & Pipeline Silent Failures"这条线：RAG/长记忆/工具调用栈里的非前缀 KV 复用会在不报错、不改接口的情况下，悄悄把依赖上下文的答案换成"相似段落里提到的另一个实体"（错误答案中 40–63% 会指向干扰段落里的实体），而聚合精度指标会把这种损伤掩盖掉。对做推理侧可靠性与性能优化交叉的人来说，它给出了一个可操作的判据：修复预算应该花在"答案所在的 chunk"上、并且要针对复用缓存重算，而不是追求与全预填充 KV 的数值接近——后者是一个看似合理但被实验否定的目标。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. **Why does the target-chunk advantage collapse at a 10% budget?** On NQ-Open/Qwen3-8B the token baseline jumps 0.16→0.24 while the target allocation drops 0.51→0.47. Is this because 10% of tokens already covers most of a 4.6K-token context's answer-bearing span, or because the token rule (ProphetKV) benefits disproportionately from a larger sample? A budget sweep (2.5/5/7.5/10/15%) with the same rule would settle it.
2. **Does the loss grow with the number of distractors n?** The additive-odds model predicts it should, but n is fixed at 29–31 per dataset. Varying n from 5 to 50 at fixed target position would test the model outside its calibration range and would tell operators how much context they can afford to reuse.
3. **Would a reranker or a trained chunk-relevance head close the retrieval gap?** The paper shows attention-over-reused-cache is a broken detector and BGE-M3 top-1 hits 47–64%. Since the oracle gap (Target vs Retrieved) is the entire deployment story, the value of a better chunk locator — even a cheap cross-encoder rerank — is the highest-leverage follow-up.
4. **What happens under real serving concurrency?** Recomputing a contiguous chunk is a different kernel shape from recomputing scattered token positions. A paged-attention implementation with batching would show whether the 0.11× TTFT advantage survives, and whether chunk reordering interacts badly with cache-block alignment.
5. **Can the "contextual compatibility" idea be used constructively rather than as repair?** If a chunk's KV should be computed against the cache it will be read from, could the encoder be trained (or the chunk cache be stored) in a form that is compatible with *any* preceding cache — e.g. by marginalising over a distribution of prefixes during block fine-tuning? The Tulu3-Block-FT result (loss 0.60, similarity term still +0.40) suggests current block fine-tuning does not achieve this.
6. **How does this interact with KV compression and quantization?** If reuse already costs 19–100% of Q\*, does 4-bit KV quantization compound the loss super-additively, and does the same "repair the target" rule apply when the cache is compressed?
