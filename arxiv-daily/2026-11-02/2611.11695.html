Why this matters

Compression doesn’t shave accuracy evenly — it strips rare knowledge first. Knowing the order lets you forecast which capabilities break before deployment.

Relevance · deployment practice

Critical edge

The rank-one model imposes order invariance by construction — if every measure decays exponentially in a shared dose, order preservation is a mathematical consequence, not an empirical discovery.

Critical Assessment · on the central formalism

01

A crossed grid, calibrated before anything was measured

preregistered · locked scripts
Grid

Eight instruction-tuned models from five families, 1.5B to 32B; 16 tasks × 4 answer formats = 64 measures per cell; seven damages; more than 1,100 damaged conditions. bf16, greedy decoding, bit-reproducible weights.

Damages

Four random (Gaussian noise, weight zeroing, neuron ablation, layer removal) and three compression methods (Wanda, SparseGPT, GPTQ). Every cell is calibrated to the same eight chance-corrected ability levels before evaluation, so σ, sparsity and bit-width become comparable.

Manipulation

Formats differ only in the final instruction: choose ranks candidates by log-probability without generating, answer asks for the answer alone, json wraps it, twice asks for it twice.

Fit & test

One shared damage strength and one fragility exponent per measure, fitted by alternating least squares (R² 0.70–0.95). Preregistered: four calibration tasks estimate the dose, twelve held-out tasks are predicted.

Recomputable published fragility rankings shrink, vanish or reverse once chance is corrected or comparisons stay within one answer format. The paper’s practical rule: same format, chance-corrected, template failures reported separately.

03

What holds up, and where it strains

two-author, self-audited

Strengths 5

  • Dose calibration before evaluation is what makes seven incommensurable damages comparable at all.
  • The four-format manipulation is the best idea here: same task, only the final instruction changes.
  • Template-versus-content scoring is a clean, reusable diagnostic — 83% against 0.2% violations.
  • Failure reporting is granular: passed / partial / undecided / failed, each with its reason.
  • A profile borrowed from other model families works nearly as well (0.117–0.125 vs. 0.110–0.118).

Limitations 5

  • Short answers only: long-form tasks kept their position in just 10 of 16 pairs; multi-step reasoning got more fragile.
  • The fact axis rests on two single-word tasks, with frequency proxied from a corpus seven of eight models never trained on.
  • twice is the largest format effect and has no mechanism — four preregistered accounts failed.
  • Accuracy prediction sits at the noise floor (±0.11); the 0.10 bound was missed.
  • Damaged models are nearly undetectable without labels (R² ≤ 0.38), so the advice is still “measure with labels”.
04

Open questions

from the discussion
  1. Does the order survive on multi-token facts — dates, dosages, numeric attributes — where the answer is a phrase rather than one word?
  2. What actually makes twice fragile: delimiters, repetition penalty, EOS handling, or the second copy itself?
  3. If a practitioner measures only four tasks of their own checkpoint, how much of the 44–46% error reduction survives?
  4. Why does GPTQ break cross-model transfer (r = 0.095) while Wanda and SparseGPT hold at 0.924 / 0.927?
  5. Does instruction tuning reshape the fragility order — and does the decomposition hold under 4-bit weights plus 50% sparsity at once?
