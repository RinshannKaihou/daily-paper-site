Why this matters

Deep nets that refuse to train may sit on the wrong critical line: at width 64, zero bias, the edge moves from 1.00 to 1.31.

Single runs, not ensemble averages, are what get trained.

Critical edge

The paper’s own ablation shows the 1/N shift buys 0.009 accuracy at L=96 and ~0 at L=32 — the headline result is not the source of the win.

01

Mechanism

why the line moves
  1. 01

    Exact layer

    Each pre-activation is a Gaussian sum over N neurons. Keep the empirical averages and the chain is exact — no weight matrices needed.

  2. 02

    Log-distance walk

    For close inputs, ln Dℓ accumulates a drift μd and a variance b₁/N across layers.

  3. 03

    Jensen term

    a₁ < 0: the log of a fluctuating average sits below the log of its mean, so a typical net contracts distances.

  4. 04

    Zero drift

    Setting μd = 0 moves the edge out. A transpose argument carries the same walk to gradients.

driftμd = ln χ₁ + a₁/N
varianceσ²d = b₁/N
shiftΔσ²w = −a₁ / (N ∂ ln χ₁)

Where to sit on the line

Along the shifted line the steady signal q* is still free. A one-nat compression budget — how much input-similarity information survives L layers — fixes q* = 0.31 at L = 32 and 0.14 at L = 96. Crinit — the resulting initialiser — then evaluates σ²w = exp(−a₁/N) / P(q*), σ²b = q* − σ²w V(q*), and sets the first layer at q*. No search, one formula.

03

Strengths & Limitations

what holds, what doesn’t

+Strengths

  • Right mechanism, right target: the typical-vs-ensemble split from random-matrix theory, aimed at the exact dispute.
  • Simulations use the exact finite-width chain, not a mean-field surrogate — the theory tests are real.
  • Closed form degenerates correctly to the linear, ReLU, leaky-ReLU and typical-preserving limits.
  • Uncommon candour: 100 nets per cell, a 1000-pair paired test, diverged runs reported, and the unflattering ablation volunteered.

−Limitations

  • Fully connected, at initialisation only. Nothing here tests conv, residual or transformer stacks.
  • Two 28×28 datasets, plain SGD, no normalisation; the largest margins appear where the baselines fail (L/N = 1.5).
  • γ = 1 is fitted on the same MNIST/Fashion-MNIST runs used to evaluate it; the optimum is a flat 0.5–1 plateau.
  • Add Crinit’s first-layer scaling to a tuned baseline and the gap closes entirely (Table S10).
  • For L > N the zero-bias point already exceeds the budget (γ = 1.2 at 96/64) and no non-negative bias can fix it.
