> **一句话总结：** 作者用"harm 词 vs 中性词"的交叉差分在生成边界（TBG）提取出一个跨提示、跨词共享的拒答方向，把它当作尺子扫描 71,583 个词，为八个开源模型各生成一张"恐惧词表"——在 Qwen3.5-4B 上插入一个无关 harm 词可让拒答率上升 33 个百分点，把提示中最"被恐惧"的词换成表中最"勇敢"的同义词可移除 27.8 个百分点拒答，且词表排序在留出词、留出提示上均能预测行为（ρ = +0.54 至 +0.96）。

> **TL;DR:** Crossed differencing of matched harm/neutral words at the generation boundary yields one shared refusal direction in eight open-weight models (Qwen3.5-0.8B through 27B, Llama-3.2-3B, Gemma-4-E2B, Phi-4-mini); projecting that direction over 71,583 vocabulary items produces a per-model "fear" table. On Qwen3.5-4B, appending a single feared word to OR-Bench Hard-1K prompts the model otherwise answers raises refusal by +0.33 (267 gated prompts, 95% CI [0.29, 0.37]), and writing the direction at dose −1.5 removes 41%–92% of that rise across models versus ≤ +0.02 for norm-matched orthogonal random directions. Table-guided synonym substitution removes +0.278 refusal on 251 over-refused Hard-1K prompts versus +0.037 for an inert-word control — but a *random* harm-free synonym already removes +0.243, so the table's choice of synonym adds only +0.033 (Holm-significant in 3 of 7 transfer models). Read it for the direction-extraction protocol and the vocabulary-scan diagnostic; treat the "fear lexicon" as a validated ranking of *which word to replace*, not of *which synonym to use*.

## 核心贡献

- **现象量化：单个词就足以改变拒答。** 在 321 条 Qwen3.5-4B 本来不会稳定拒答的 harm 提示中插入一个与该请求无关的 harm 词，末尾插入使拒答率上升 **+0.33**（起点 +0.31，中部 +0.19）；更关键的是模型在另一个对话里能以 P > 0.90 的概率说出"哪个词是硬塞进来的"，也就是说它识别得出这个词不属于请求，却仍然拒答。反向操作同样成立：把提示里一个普通内容词换成无 harm 联想的同义同义词，拒答率下降 3–28 个百分点，而换成"惰性词"的同义词最多只变 2 个百分点。
- **方向提取：交叉差分 + 精确双向向量 ANOVA。** 96 条提示 × 24 组 harm/中性词对（2,304 个 cell），在生成边界读取残差流，取 `D_pw = h_H − h_N`，用平衡网格上的最小二乘分解 `D = µ + A_p + B_w + I_pw`，把"提示效应"和"单纯追加一个词"的效应都消掉，只留下共享分量 µ。µ 占平方范数的 0.30，是所有词效应之和（0.15）的两倍；24 个词各自的平均方向与 µ 的余弦相似度为 0.82–0.96（21/24）。
- **因果性而非相关性：剂量单调 + 特异性对照。** 沿 µ 写入时拒答随剂量单调变化（bootstrap 单调概率 0.99–1.00）；范数匹配的正交随机方向最多移动 +0.02；把随机方向的剂量放大 1.7–4.7 倍直到其 benign KL 与 µ 匹配，仍然在每个模型上都输给 µ。这是本文最强的一块证据。
- **把方向当尺子：词表。** 对 71,583 个候选词（43,544 个单 token 词 + 50k 高频词中的 28,027 个多 token 词）逐个追加到 48 条被过度拒答的 Hard-1K 提示后，投影到 µ̂ 并按 Hard-1K 标准差归一，得到每个词的 shift。正极是性相关词、恋童/强奸词、侮辱语、暴力犯罪词；负极是**语体词**（poetic、whimsical、sardonic、shakespearean）。作者手工挑的六类"勇敢词"（educational、fictional、professional 等）**全部失败**——最好的 fictional 也只有 −0.029 [−0.091, +0.031]，而 containment 类反而**提高**拒答 +0.075。这是一个反直觉且诚实的结果。
- **两重留出验证。** （1）插入剂量-反应：40 个跨百分位选取的留出词，Qwen3.5-4B 上 7/7 相邻分箱单调，ρ = +0.92，其余七个模型 ρ = +0.54 至 +0.96；（2）自然提示替换：在 Hard-1K 与 XSTest 上做表引导的同义词替换，八个模型全部显著降低拒答。
- **机制定位（仅 Qwen3.5-4B）。** harm 词与其中性孪生词在边界处获得的注意力**相同**（7.17 vs 7.36，Δ = −0.19 [−0.61, +0.28]），甚至 key 更不吸引人（−0.39 logits）；沿 µ 的 harm−neutral 位移中，30 个 MLP 写了 **73%**，DeltaNet mixer 24%，softmax attention mixer 3%，而 softmax 头对词本身的直接读取只占 **1%**。即"词被早早读到，但对拒答的作用是晚写的"。

## Key Contributions

- **Establishes a measurable, per-word causal quantity.** Refusal is not a function of the request alone: a single appended word with no role in the request moves refusal by up to +0.49 across eight models, and a single in-place synonym swap moves it by up to 28 points. The detection gate makes this sharp — the model can name the intruding word with P > 0.90 and refuses anyway, and per-prompt refusal rises are uncorrelated with detection confidence at start and middle positions (Spearman ρ = 0.00).
- **A direction-extraction recipe that is cleaner than the standard contrastive-mean baseline.** Arditi et al.-style extraction contrasts harmful vs. harmless *prompts*, which conflates "what is asked" with "which words are used." Crossing 96 prompts with 24 harm/neutral *word pairs* and running an exact two-way vector ANOVA isolates a word-level component while the balanced grid cancels the prompt axis. Cross-fitting (independent 4-fold assignments for prompts and words) means a test cell's direction is built only from the opposite rectangle.
- **A vocabulary-wide instrument, not just a probe.** The direction is used as a ruler over 71,583 words, producing a dense, model-specific ranking with split-half reproducibility ρ = 0.95 (81 of the top 100 shared on average). This is a reusable artifact, and the paper releases the full per-word tables.
- **Two behavioral validations that could have failed and did not.** Held-out insertion dose-response (7/7 monotone bins on the development model) and natural-prompt substitution (significant in 8/8 models on Hard-1K, 7/8 on XSTest). Crucially, the seven transfer models' *own* tables beat the transferred Qwen3.5-4B table on five of seven, which rules out "the ranking is just a generic English harmfulness prior."
- **A mechanistic account that contradicts the obvious story.** The model does not attend more to harm words; late MLPs write the shift after the word is read. Token attribution to the word token barely predicts refusal (ρ ≈ 0.2) while the boundary projection does (ρ = +0.65 after prompt+word fixed effects).
- **An actionable evaluation recommendation.** Refusal benchmarks are vocabulary-dependent; the paper shows the same prompt with one word swapped can move a benchmark's refusal rate by tens of points, so cross-model comparisons that do not control vocabulary are measuring lexicon as much as policy.

## Methodology

**Models.** Eight instruction-tuned, open-weight models from four families: Qwen3.5-0.8B/2B/4B/9B/27B, Llama-3.2-3B, Gemma-4-E2B, Phi-4-mini. Qwen3.5-4B is the development model on which every procedure was designed; the other seven are confirmation models with Holm adjustment across them.

**Reading position (TBG).** The residual stream at the output of one late layer, at the last two prefill tokens before the assistant's first token — the "generation boundary." For Qwen3.5 templates with thinking disabled this is the empty think block's closing tokens; for Llama/Gemma/Phi it is the last two tokens of the assistant header. Layer chosen by the fixed rule `round(29/33 · n_blocks)`; direction norms range from 0.90 (Qwen3.5-0.8B) to 42.6 (Qwen3.5-27B).

**Crossed design.** 96 prompts × 24 (harm word, length-matched neutral twin) pairs, e.g. bomb/garden, illegal/compass, appended to the prompt. Per cell, `D_pw = h_H − h_N` at TBG removes both the prompt and the mere act of appending. Exact least-squares two-way vector ANOVA: `D_pw = µ + A_p + B_w + I_pw`. At state 30 the four terms hold 0.30 (shared), 0.32 (prompt), 0.15 (word), 0.23 (interaction) of the squared norm; the 24 word means are near rank-one (rank-1 energy 0.76–0.89).

**Cross-fitting.** Prompts and words get independent four-fold assignments; fold *f*'s direction µ_f is rebuilt only from the opposite rectangle, so neither the tested prompt nor the tested word contributes. Held-out mean direction has cosine 0.90–0.97 with the train-fitted direction in every fold.

**Intervention.** Write `a·µ` at the two TBG positions, once, during prefill. Dose unit is the model's own mean boundary shift, so dose 1 = the model's own harm-minus-neutral shift. Two null controls: a random direction orthogonal to µ rescaled to equal norm, and a random direction rescaled until its benign KL matches µ (multipliers 1.7–4.7×).

**Pre-registration.** The dose a\* = 0.5 was registered in a plan frozen before generation, with OR-Bench Hard-1K as the superiority endpoint (must fall ≥ 0.02, one-sided α = 0.05/3) and OR-Bench Toxic, StrongREJECT and IFEval as non-inferiority guards (±0.02 margin, α = 0.05/9). a\* is also the largest magnitude whose benign KL (over 256 sealed benign prompts) stays monotone in dose and ≤ 0.10.

**Vocabulary scan.** Each of 71,583 candidate words appended as `text + " " + w` to the end of 48 over-refused Hard-1K prompts (the "BP table"); prompt prefilled once, word plus template tail fed from the cached state. Shift = summed projection change at the two TBG positions, in Hard-1K projection sd units, averaged over prompts. A second "clean-benign" table scans 40 clean Dolly/No Robots prompts (90–170 words, no lexicon word in any inflection, model refuses 0.003 of samples) and ranks words quite differently (Spearman +0.32 with the BP table over 47,371 frequent words).

**Validation 1 (held-out insertion).** 40 words drawn as 3 from each of eight percentile bins (bottom 1% to top 1%) plus 16 words where two tables disagree, appended to held-out prompts the model refuses ~half the time (64 prompts on Qwen3.5-4B, 32 on each other model, half benign / half harmful).

**Validation 2 (table-guided substitution).** The model's own table nominates the highest-shift content word; a drafting model (DeepSeek-V4-Flash, T = 0) proposes up to ten meaning-preserving substitutes with no harm association; structural checks reject lexicon words, duplicates, and repeated adjacent words; the accepted candidate the table places lowest replaces the word in place. Control: the prompt's most *inert* content word (shift nearest zero within a frequency band) replaced by a synonym. On harmful prompts the word is located instead by the direction's leave-one-out deletion pass. Benign cohort: Hard-1K prompts most models refuse + 100 safe XSTest prompts (349–352 units/model). Harmful cohort: OR-Bench Toxic prompts the model refuses + 100 XSTest contrast prompts.

**Measurement.** Every response sampled at T = 1.0, top-p 0.9, top-k 50, 10 sequences per prompt (20 in the table dose-response), 2,048 new tokens with one retry at 4,096, fixed per-prompt seeds so dosed arms reuse undosed seeds. Refusal label = a sentence in which the assistant says it cannot/will not/may not do what was asked, from one pass of DeepSeek-V4-Flash reading all ten responses together; test–retest and cross-judge κ ≥ 0.96, and three judge families (GPT-5.6, DeepSeek-V4-Flash, GLM-5.2) × two passes agree unanimously on 98.0% of responses. Harm measured with GuardReasoner-8B (95.1% agreement, κ = 0.901 against 2,250 human-labelled XSTest completions). Meaning preservation checked by an independent model (GPT-6 Luna) and a second from a third family (Claude Haiku 5.5).

## Results & Highlights

- **Insertion, Qwen3.5-4B.** One harm word from a 99-word lexicon appended to the end of the 267 prompts passing the detection gate raises refusal by **+0.33 [0.29, 0.37]** over the regenerated original (base rate 0.22); at the start it is +0.31 [0.22, 0.40] (55 prompts) and in the middle +0.19 [0.13, 0.25] (105 prompts). Ungated over all 321 prompts the numbers barely move (+0.32 / +0.18 / +0.30), so the gate is not cherry-picking the effect — it is only making the "the model knows the word doesn't belong" claim airtight.
- **Insertion, across models.** On the 130 prompts all eight models had answered, end insertion raises refusal in **every** model: Qwen3.5-27B **+0.49 [0.42, 0.56]**, Qwen3.5-9B +0.46, Qwen3.5-2B +0.38, Qwen3.5-4B +0.37, Llama-3.2-3B +0.29, Gemma-4-E2B +0.18, Phi-4-mini +0.11, Qwen3.5-0.8B +0.10 [0.06, 0.14]. 22 of 24 model–position increases exclude zero after Holm correction; the two that do not are Qwen3.5-0.8B (only 11 gated prompts, +0.13 n.s.) and Phi-4-mini's middle position (+0.00 n.s.).
- **Steering specificity.** Writing the model's own direction at dose −1.5 on the end-inserted text removes **+0.09 to +0.28** of refusal, i.e. 41% (Llama) to 92% (Qwen3.5-0.8B, Phi) of the insertion's rise; writing at +1.5 on the clean original creates **+0.04 to +0.39** (Holm p = 7×10⁻⁴). Norm-matched orthogonal random directions move refusal by **at most +0.02**; KL-matched random directions need 1.7–4.7× the dose and still lose in every model (e.g. Qwen3.5-2B own−KL = +0.30 [0.25, 0.35], Qwen3.5-9B +0.11 [0.06, 0.15]).
- **Cross-fitted held-out cells.** On 288 held-out cells of Qwen3.5-4B at a\* = 0.5, subtracting µ_f/2 from harm-worded text removes **+0.080 [0.043, 0.121]** of refusal and adding µ_f/2 to neutral-worded text raises it **+0.086 [0.041, 0.133]**; a substantive-compliance label moves mirror-wise (+0.072 subtracted, −0.082 added), so the write changes what the model delivers, not just its phrasing. Residualized projection–behavior Spearman is **ρ = 0.65** over 576 cells.
- **External benchmarks at the registered dose.** Hard-1K refusal falls **−0.029 [−0.042, −0.017]** from a base of 0.886 and clears the superiority gate; Toxic moves −0.006 [−0.017, +0.003] and IFEval +0.018 [−0.007, +0.045], both formally non-inferior; **StrongREJECT misses its guard** at +0.005 with a one-sided bound of +0.024 against a 0.02 margin. Deep doses stay monotone to −3 (−0.215 on Hard-1K), but Toxic refusal slides to −0.041 and GSM8K accuracy never moves more than 0.009.
- **Vocabulary table shape.** Qwen3.5-4B BP table: min −3.82, p1 −1.16, median −0.09, p99 +0.47, max +1.05; **70% of words sit below zero**, so "courage" is the default and "fear" the exception. Split-half reproducibility ρ = +0.95, sharing 81 of the top 100 on average. The 99-word insertion lexicon averages +0.49 sd, all 99 above the 84th percentile, 52 in the top 1% (shoot 91st, bomb 97th, gun 98th, meth 98th, hack 99th).
- **Validation 1 (held-out insertion).** Qwen3.5-4B: **7 of 7** adjacent bin pairs in order, from −0.165 at the bottom percentile to +0.367 at the top, top−bottom **+0.53 [0.48, 0.58]**, ρ = **+0.92** with the table (clean-benign table only +0.55). Other seven models: top−bottom **+0.32 (Qwen3.5-27B) to +0.71 (Qwen3.5-0.8B)**, 5–7 of 7 bins in order, own-table ρ = **+0.54 (Llama) to +0.96 (Qwen3.5-0.8B)**. Each model's own table beats the transferred Qwen3.5-4B table on five of seven.
- **Validation 2 (benign substitution).** Replacing the most feared word by its most courageous synonym on over-refused Hard-1K prompts removes **+0.278 [0.229, 0.326]** of refusal on Qwen3.5-4B (251 prompts, base 0.929) against **+0.037** for the inert-word control; Qwen3.5-27B +0.287 vs +0.050; Gemma-4-E2B +0.261 vs +0.023; Phi-4-mini +0.204 vs +0.033; Llama-3.2-3B only **+0.052 vs +0.011** on 33 prompts. The boundary projection drops 0.61–0.88 sd versus 0.00–0.08 for control, and per-prompt projection change tracks refusal change at ρ = +0.31 to +0.80. On safe XSTest prompts the effect holds in 7 of 8 (+0.056 to +0.131; Llama +0.006 [0.000, 0.018]).
- **Validation 2 (harmful substitution).** On OR-Bench Toxic the nominated word is the one naming the harm; the substitution rule reaches 64% of prompts (a quarter have no meaning-preserving harm-free substitute — self-harm, sexual, bomb — and 11% fail structural checks after three redrafts). Where it applies it removes **+0.076 (Qwen3.5-27B) to +0.226 (Llama)** of refusal in all eight models, and hedge-phrase deletion removes +0.17 to +0.36. Response harmfulness rises by **at most +0.04** (GuardReasoner-8B; Qwen3.5-4B +0.010 [0.004, 0.018]).
- **The table is a better word-finder than synonym-picker.** A random harm-free synonym drawn from the same candidate set already removes +0.120 to +0.243; taking the table's lowest candidate adds only **+0.025 to +0.078** more — positive in all eight models but Holm-significant in only **three of seven** confirmation models (Qwen3.5-2B +0.047, Qwen3.5-27B +0.067, Gemma +0.078). And the table's own shift difference between a word and its substitute does **not** rank per-prompt effects (ρ = −0.05 to +0.16); what predicts is the shift the replacement actually causes in that prompt.
- **Representation predicts the shift, but not through the direction.** Ridge regression from the word's context-free residual predicts its table shift at **ρ = +0.84** (R² = 0.70–0.72; from the input embedding +0.78, R² = 0.53; from word-alone chat boundary states +0.87, R² = 0.80). Yet projecting the word's *own* context-free state onto the direction gives **ρ between −0.35 and +0.04** — the shift is written by the network after the word is read, not carried along the direction by the word itself.

## Authors & Credibility

Boyuan Zhang, Mohammad Zandsalimy and Shanu Sushmita, all at Northeastern University. A small, single-institution group rather than a frontier lab; Sushmita's group has a prior line of work on safety probes and jailbreak detection (the cited Yigit, Zandsalimy & Sushmita 2026 paper on safety-weighted semantic entropy probes shares an author and a methodological temperament), so this is a continuation rather than an opportunistic foray. That cuts both ways: the paper knows the interpretability literature it is extending (Arditi et al. 2024 on single-direction refusal, Marshall et al. 2024 on affine refusal, Wollschläger et al. 2025 on concept cones, Wang et al. 2025 on false-refusal vector ablation) and positions itself precisely against the closest prior work, but there is no external replication and no venue signal in the arXiv listing.

What raises credibility above the norm for a three-author arXiv preprint is process discipline: a **frozen pre-registration** of the dose and the four endpoints before generation; **cross-fitting** so no validation cell shares a prompt or word with the direction it is tested against; **two null controls** (norm-matched and KL-matched) rather than the usual one; a **detection gate** that pre-empts the "maybe the model just got confused by the extra token" objection; **Holm adjustment** over the seven confirmation models with the bootstrap's resolution floor (7×10⁻⁴) stated explicitly; and a **judge-agreement table** (κ ≥ 0.96, 98.0% unanimous across three families) plus an independent meaning-preservation judge from a third model family. The paper also reports the results that hurt it — the failed hand-picked courage words, the StrongREJECT guard miss, the weak synonym-choice increment — in the main text rather than burying them.

The artifact release is substantive: code, all prompts drawn from public benchmarks, aggregate results, and the full per-word tables for all eight models. That is the difference between a claim and a tool.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The single-direction picture of refusal is not new (Arditi et al. 2024), and neither is the observation that surface words trigger over-refusal (Shi et al. 2024; Röttger et al. 2024; Cui et al. 2025). The genuinely new move is the *crossed* design: prior extraction contrasts harmful against harmless prompts, which entangles the request with its vocabulary; crossing 96 prompts with 24 harm/neutral word pairs and running an exact two-way vector ANOVA isolates a word-level component while the balanced grid cancels the prompt axis. That is a real methodological contribution, and it is what makes the vocabulary scan possible at all. The second new move — using the direction as a ruler over 71,583 words and then validating the resulting *ranking* on held-out words — is a stronger test than the usual "the probe separates harmful from harmless," and the paper deserves credit for choosing tests that could have failed.

**Rigor.** Mostly excellent, with specific gaps. The dose-response monotonicity (0.99–1.00 bootstrap probability), the KL-matched null needing 1.7–4.7× the dose, and the cross-fitted held-out cells (ρ = 0.65 after fixed effects) together make a causal case that is unusually hard to wave away. The mechanistic section is careful in the same way: it reports that harm words receive *the same* attention as neutral twins (7.17 vs 7.36, Δ = −0.19 [−0.61, +0.28]) and that the direct attention read contributes 1% of the boundary shift — i.e. it reports the result that kills the obvious "the model looks at the bad word" story.

Where rigor thins: **(a)** the *word* component of the ANOVA is the **smallest** of the four terms (0.15 of squared norm against 0.32 for prompt and 0.23 for interaction). The paper's framing — "the shared component is twice the size of all word effects together" — is technically a point in favor of a shared direction, but it also means word identity explains less of the boundary state than prompt identity does. The behavioral effects are large because they are measured as paired deltas, not because words dominate the representation; a reader skimming the abstract could easily invert that. **(b)** The smallest achievable paired-bootstrap p is 7×10⁻⁴ (one resample in 10,000, times seven models), and several headline claims are reported at exactly that value. Those claims are at the instrument's resolution, not comfortably beyond it. **(c)** The "fear" framing is metaphorical and slightly inflationary: the direction is fit on harm-vs-neutral *word* contrasts, so it is at least as much a "harm-lexicon direction" as a "refusal direction," and the paper's own mechanistic result — no attentional preference for harm words — undercuts the anthropomorphic reading. The word "fear" is doing rhetorical work that the evidence does not require.

**The table-switching problem.** This is the most substantive methodological quirk and it is easy to miss. The paper maintains *two* tables with different prompts and very different distributions: the BP table (scanned on 48 over-refused Hard-1K prompts) and the clean-benign table (40 clean Dolly/No Robots prompts, model refuses 0.003). They correlate at only **+0.32** over 47,371 frequent words, and their tails disagree sharply — bomb is 97th percentile in the BP table but 70th in the clean-benign table; shoot 91st vs 25th; meth 98th vs 42nd. The paper uses the BP table for the headline validation (ρ = +0.92) and the clean-benign table for the tail experiments and the representation regressions, and it is honest about why (the clean-benign table is the one that isolates the word's effect uncontaminated by the prompt's own over-refusal). But this means "the table" is not one object, and a practitioner who scans their own prompts would have to choose a scan set — a choice that visibly changes the ranking of weapon and drug words by 25–70 percentile points. The paper compares four scan sets once (Section 6, Appendix D) and picks BP because it predicts best on held-out prompts, which is defensible but is also selection on the validation metric.

**Circularity, partially addressed.** The 40 held-out words in Validation 1 are chosen as three per percentile bin "joint in the model's own and the Qwen3.5-4B table" plus 16 disagreement words. If bins are defined jointly on the predictor and the model whose performance is being reported, the ρ = +0.92 for Qwen3.5-4B is partly a selection artifact — and indeed the same 40 words give the clean-benign table only +0.55, and give Llama's own table +0.54. The transfer result (own table beats transferred table on five of seven) is the more convincing half of this validation because it is a comparison between two predictors on the same words, not a fit to the binning.

**Reproducibility.** Strong on artifacts (code, full per-word tables, public prompts, fixed seeds, pre-registered dose), weak on instruments: every label comes from proprietary, versioned model endpoints — DeepSeek-V4-Flash for refusal, GuardReasoner-8B for harm, GPT-5.6 / GLM-5.2 for judge agreement, GPT-6 Luna and Claude Haiku 5.5 for meaning preservation. Snapshot drift in any of these silently changes the dependent variable. The paper does the right thing by reporting agreement statistics and by noting that agreement was measured only on Qwen3.5-4B responses, but a reader cannot re-derive the labels.

**The meaning-preservation hole.** This is the weakest link in Validation 2 and the paper half-admits it. The independent judge reads only **0.61** of the shared-text substitutions, **0.58** of the harmful ones, and **0.33** of the table-guided ones as preserving the request (controls: 0.86 and 0.81). A second judge is more permissive (0.79 / 0.71 / 0.53) and the two agree at only κ = 0.58, with 882 pairs accepted by the second and rejected by the first. So roughly a third to two-thirds of the "meaning-preserving synonym" edits arguably change what is asked. The paper's mitigation is real — on the subset both judges accept, the substitution still removes +0.02 to +0.16 (shared-text), +0.05 to +0.20 (table-guided) and +0.01 to +0.10 (harmful), with 19–24 of 24 intervals excluding zero — but note that the headline **+0.278** shrinks to something in the **+0.05 to +0.16** range under the strict reading. The abstract's "significantly raises the answer rate" is true; the magnitude a careful reader should carry away is roughly a third of the number that appears first.

**Overclaiming, mild but present.** "A word's shift is predictable from its own representation" (abstract) is technically true at ρ = +0.84 from the context-free residual — but the *same* word's state projected on the direction gives ρ between −0.35 and +0.04, meaning the predictability runs through a different feature set, not through the direction. Those two sentences sit near each other and invite a reading the paper itself refutes two paragraphs later. Similarly, "two held-out tests validate the ranking in all eight models" is accurate for the *insertion* test (8/8) and for Hard-1K substitution (8/8), but the XSTest substitution is 7/8 and the synonym-choice increment is Holm-significant in 3/7 — the abstract's "in all eight models" is doing work that the fine print narrows.

**Baselines.** The steering baselines are the strongest part of the paper (two kinds of matched random direction, plus the "own − random" and "own − KL" columns reported per model). The substitution baseline is weaker than it looks: the inert-word control (+0.01 to +0.05) is a soft control, because an inert word by construction has near-zero shift and swapping it barely changes anything — the *informative* comparison is the random-harm-free-synonym arm (+0.120 to +0.243), and against that the table adds only +0.02 to +0.08. The paper reports this comparison, which is to its credit, but the abstract and the framing lean on the easier control.

## Strengths

- The crossed design with an exact two-way vector ANOVA is the right tool for the question and is executed cleanly; the balanced grid makes the decomposition exact rather than approximate, and the prompt component (0.32) genuinely cancels.
- Cross-fitting at the level of *both* prompts and words is stricter than most steering papers bother with, and the held-out mean direction's cosine of 0.90–0.97 with the train-fitted direction shows the direction is stable rather than fold-specific.
- The detection gate is a genuinely clever control: it forecloses the "the extra token just confused the model" explanation by showing the model can *name* the intruding word at P > 0.90 and refuses anyway, with per-prompt refusal rises uncorrelated with detection confidence at start and middle positions.
- Two null controls instead of one, with the KL-matched null given 1.7–4.7× the dose and still losing — this is the difference between "steering works" and "steering works specifically along this axis."
- Pre-registration of the dose and all four endpoints before generation, with the StrongREJECT guard miss reported rather than hidden.
- The negative results are the most interesting part: six hand-picked courage classes all fail, containment words *raise* refusal, and the negative tail turns out to be register words (poetic, whimsical, sardonic, shakespearean). That is a finding no one would have guessed and it directly supports the paper's claim that the table finds what intuition misses.
- The mechanistic section reports the result that contradicts the intuitive story (equal attention to harm and neutral words, 1% from direct attention reads, 73% from late MLPs), which is exactly what a mechanism section should do.
- Artifact release is real: code, per-word tables for all eight models, public prompts, fixed seeds.

## Limitations

- **No closed models.** Eight open-weight models at 27B and below. The paper argues the insertion and substitution tests need only sampled responses and so transfer through an API, but the table itself needs activations — so the diagnostic is unavailable for exactly the models whose safety behavior matters most.
- **English only**, one language, one tokenizer family per model. The claim that a multilingual model admits a table per language from the same direction is a promissory note, not a result.
- **Mechanistic analysis covers one model** (Qwen3.5-4B), which happens to have an unusual architecture (32 blocks, 24 Gated DeltaNet mixers, 8 softmax-attention layers). The "late MLPs write it, attention doesn't read it" conclusion may be architecture-specific in ways the paper cannot test.
- **One scan position** (end of prompt). The paper shows start and middle insertions have smaller and less reliable effects (+0.31 / +0.19 vs +0.33 at the end on Qwen3.5-4B; several models have *zero* prompts passing the gate at start or middle), so the table is calibrated for a position that is also the most effective one — a mild confound between "the word's fearfulness" and "where the word sits."
- **Label definition inflates refusal.** The explicit-refusal label counts a refusal statement followed by delivered content as a refusal. The authors flag this and propose a separate count of "refusals followed by compliance" as future work, but every number in the paper inherits the inflation.
- **Meaning preservation is asserted by LLMs**, at 0.61/0.58/0.33 acceptance under the strict judge, with only κ = 0.58 between the two judges. No human annotation of substitutes. This is the single largest threat to Validation 2's magnitude.
- **The alignment link is by citation, not experiment.** Nothing here shows that safety alignment *creates* the direction or the table. Reading the table at checkpoints along an alignment run is the obvious experiment and is not done.
- **The practical increment is small.** The table reliably tells you *which* word to replace (that is where the 4–28 points come from) but adds only 2–8 points over a random harm-free synonym for *which synonym* to pick, significant in 3 of 7 transfer models. As a substitution tool it is a modest improvement over "replace the scary word with any harmless synonym," which is a much cheaper heuristic than a 71,583-word activation scan.
- **Dual-use exposure is real but bounded.** Replacing the feared word on OR-Bench Toxic lowers refusal and raises measured harmfulness by at most +0.04; appending a courage word to harmful prompts also lowers refusal. The authors argue the techniques (rewording, adding a style word) are already widely known and the models are small, which is fair, but a vocabulary-wide ranking of exactly which words a model is most sensitive to is a more efficient attack surface than a per-prompt search.

## Relevance

直接对应 **LLM Hidden States & Interpretability**：它把"拒答"还原为生成边界残差流上的一个可定位方向，并用精确的双向向量 ANOVA 把提示效应与词效应分离——这套"交叉差分 + 交叉拟合 + KL 匹配零对照"的协议可以直接搬到其他行为属性（谄媚、工具调用、越狱易感性）上。对做表征几何与激活干预的人来说，这是本周方法最干净的一篇；对做安全评测的人来说，它给出的"基准拒答率依赖词表"这一结论值得单独记一笔。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. **Does the table survive a checkpoint sweep?** The paper relates word-fear to safety alignment only by citation. Scanning the table at intermediate checkpoints of one alignment run would show whether the sensitivity appears at SFT, at preference tuning, or earlier — and whether the *ranking* is stable or reshuffles.
2. **Why do register words (poetic, whimsical) lower refusal while containment/authorization words raise it?** The paper reports the failure of six hand-picked courage classes but offers no mechanism. Is it that poetic register shifts the model toward a "creative writing" frame in which refusal is off-distribution, or something about the specific tokens' positions in the residual geometry?
3. **How much of the +0.278 substitution effect is meaning drift?** The strict judge accepts only 0.61 of the edits. A human-annotated sample of 200 substitutions, with the effect recomputed on the human-accepted subset, would settle whether the headline number is 28 points or 8.
4. **What happens at start and middle positions?** Several models admit *zero* prompts through the detection gate at start or middle (Qwen3.5-0.8B and 2B, Llama, Phi). Is that because the model fails to notice an early intruding word, or because it notices and the notice itself suppresses the effect? A position × detection-probability design would separate the two.
5. **Can the direction be used to *build* better alignment data rather than just audit?** The paper suggests selecting or constructing data on which a model learns to separate a word from the request it appears in. That is a concrete, testable intervention the paper does not attempt.
6. **How does the table interact with the tokenizer?** The scan covers 43,544 single-token words plus 28,027 multi-token high-frequency words, and appends each as `text + " " + w`. Multi-token words necessarily produce a different boundary state than single-token ones; is part of the shift an artifact of token count, and does the ridge regression's ρ = +0.46 for "token count, frequency, length" (R² = 0.17) hide more of the variance than reported?
7. **Would a vocabulary-controlled benchmark change published rankings?** The paper's headline implication is that refusal benchmarks are vocabulary-dependent. Constructing two versions of XSTest matched on table shift, and re-ranking the same eight models on each, would convert the implication from argument to measurement.
