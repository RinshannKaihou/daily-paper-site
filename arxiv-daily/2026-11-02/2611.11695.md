> **一句话总结：** 作者把 7 种损伤（高斯噪声、随机置零、神经元消融、随机删层，以及 Wanda、SparseGPT、GPTQ 三种压缩）加到 8 个指令微调模型上，用 16 个任务 × 4 种答题格式（choose/answer/json/twice，共 64 个 measure）在 1100+ 个条件上测量能力损失，发现短答案能力几乎总是按同一个顺序失效（不同损伤之间 task-effect 中位相关 0.90，压缩方法对随机损伤 0.875，预注册阈值 0.7），而这个顺序由三条可分离的轴决定——任务被"握"得多牢（intact margin，Spearman 0.58–0.73）、答案在预训练语料中有多常见（中位 0.42/0.38）、以及要生成多少答案（相对 choose，answer/json/twice 的脆弱度是 1.5×/2.5×/8×）；一次测得的脆弱度剖面还能预测压缩后未见条件的准确率（中位绝对误差 0.110–0.118，均匀下降基线为 0.200–0.213）。

> **TL;DR:** This is a large preregistered measurement study, not a method paper: 7 damages × 8 instruction-tuned models (Qwen2.5 1.5B–32B, Llama-3.2-3B, Mistral-7B, Gemma-2-9B, Phi-3.5-mini) × 16 tasks × 4 answer formats, >1,100 damaged conditions. A rank-one model `c_m(d)=exp(−n_m·h(d))` fits 0.70–0.95 of the variance, i.e. damage sets the *rate* and each measure has a fixed *fragility exponent*; consequently tasks fail in the same order under four mechanistically different random damages (median r=0.904 over 35 pairs, 0.832–0.913 across models) and, within a model, under Wanda/SparseGPT/GPTQ (median 0.875 vs. the preregistered 0.7 bar). The order is predicted by intact answer margin (63% of variance of task effects; intact accuracy is the weaker predictor in 6/7 models), by Dolma n-gram frequency of the *answer* (0.42 for capitals, 0.38 for SciQ, 0.08 in the key-lookup control), and by answer format (twice is 8× more fragile than choose, but 83% of those failures are template violations, not lost knowledge). Fitted once, the profile predicts held-out accuracy after compression with MAE 0.110–0.118 vs. 0.200–0.213 for uniform decline (better in 23/23 cells, 44–46% error reduction) — and a profile borrowed from *other model families* does about as well (0.117–0.125). Failures are reported honestly: GPTQ does not transfer the order across models (median r=0.095), long-form tasks keep their position in only 10/16 pairs (preregistered bar 75%), and the preregistered 0.10 absolute-error bound was missed.

## 核心贡献

- **把"损伤决定一切"的直觉翻过来，并给出可检验的形式。** 用 rank-one 模型 `c_m(d) = exp(−n_m h(d))` 把每个（模型, 损伤）单元里 64 个 measure 的退化曲线分解成"一个共享的损伤强度 h(d)"加"每个 measure 自己的脆弱度指数 n_m"。拟合 R² 为 0.87–0.95（噪声/置零/神经元消融）、0.74–0.88（删层）、0.70–0.93（压缩）。损伤只拧一个旋钮，能力各自以不同速率掉队。
- **跨机制的顺序不变性（预注册，通过）。** 四种机制完全不同的随机损伤下，任务失效顺序几乎一致：模型内 35 个损伤对中位相关 0.904（95% 区间 0.81–0.95），跨模型 0.832–0.913（69 对）；用同损伤两个种子的可靠性做 disattenuation 后升到 0.92–0.94。去掉最稳健的 3 个和最脆弱的 1 个任务后仍有 0.727（打乱标签的 95 分位是 0.184）。重要性引导的压缩（Wanda/SparseGPT/GPTQ）本可以改写顺序，但没有：与同模型随机损伤的 task effect 中位相关 0.875（18 个单元），跨模型中位 0.924 / 0.927。
- **三条可分离的轴：task × fact × format。** 任务轴 = intact 答案 margin（越不牢越先坏，每个模型 Spearman 0.58–0.73；margin 解释损伤平均 task effect 方差的 63%，而 intact accuracy 在 7 个模型中有 6 个是更差的预测器；回归掉 margin 后跨模型相关从 0.946 掉到 0.678，回归掉 accuracy 还剩 0.921）。事实轴 = 答案本身在预训练语料中的频率（capital 与国家的共现：中位 0.42；SciQ 答案词频 0.38；key-lookup 的对照只有 0.08；控制国家频率后 capital 频率偏相关 0.48–0.70，反向只有 −0.22–0.18）。格式轴 = 要生成多少（相对 choose，answer/json/twice 的指数是 1.5×/2.5×/8×）。
- **三轴可加，且不互相重排。** 一个 task effect 加一个 format effect 就能解释 log 指数方差的中位 89%（26 个单元，81–95%）；格式的顺序在 25/26 个随机损伤单元和 19/21 个压缩单元中对每个任务都保持不变。所以"某个能力脆弱"不是一个标量属性，而是三维空间里的一个点，失效前沿是一个平面。
- **一次测量，预测未见损伤（五级预测，全部预注册）。** 用三种随机损伤拟合的 profile 预测第四种：r=0.851（只用 task effect 是 0.597，只用 format effect 是 0.512，20/20 更好）；预测压缩：r=0.807（18/19 更好）。预测哪些事实先被删掉：Spearman 0.69/0.71/0.58（Wanda/SparseGPT/GPTQ），优于答案频率（0.23–0.37）和 intact item margin（0.09–0.13），48/48 个单元为正。预测剩余准确率：中位绝对误差 0.110–0.118 vs 均匀下降 0.200–0.213，23/23 个（模型, 方法）单元更好。
- **不需要测目标模型自己。** 把目标模型的指数换成"其他模型家族的同 task/同 format 平均值"，误差 0.117–0.125，仍然在全部 23 个单元中优于均匀下降，与用自己的 profile（0.110–0.118）几乎无差——预注册的"自己的 profile 至少好 10%"检验因此失败。这暗示脆弱度更多是"任务+格式"的属性，而非某个权重矩阵的属性。
- **对已有文献的审计。** 重算了 13 个可复算的已发表脆弱度排序：9 个在"校正 chance 或同格式比较"之后缩小、消失或反转；仅有的 2 个在同格式内成立的与本文顺序一致。作者据此主张：脆弱度必须在同一答题格式内、chance 校正后比较，模板失败要与答错分开报告。
- **与语义性痴呆的类比。** 稀有知识先于熟悉知识丢失、命名先于再认、错误向熟悉答案漂移——这三条在 15 亿到 320 亿参数的网络上、在 7 种损伤下重现。作者谨慎地限定：相似的是"丢什么、按什么顺序丢"，不是机制。

## Key Contributions

- **A rank-one decomposition of capability loss.** For each (model, damage) cell the paper fits `c_m(d) = exp(−n_m·h(d))`, separating a single damage strength `h(d)` from a per-measure fragility exponent `n_m`, and shows the form holds across 8 models and 7 damages (R² 0.70–0.95; a joint fit with one exponent per measure across all seven damages of a model still explains 72–82% vs. 84–91% for separate fits).
- **Evidence that the failure order is mechanism-invariant, not method-specific.** Four random damages with different mechanisms give the same task ordering within a model (median r = 0.904, 35 pairs) and across models (0.832–0.913, 69 pairs), surviving removal of extreme tasks (0.727 vs. 0.184 shuffled null) and extending to 32B (0.843 within Qwen2.5-32B; 0.898 agreement with smaller Qwen models).
- **A three-axis account with additive structure.** Fragility decomposes into task (intact margin), fact (answer frequency), and format (amount produced); task+format effects explain a median 89% of log-exponent variance, and changing format does not reshuffle the task order — so fragility is a point in a 3-D space, not a per-capability scalar.
- **Quantitative, preregistered prediction of unseen damage.** Five levels from "which capability fails first" (r = 0.851 / 0.807) to "which fact is removed" (Spearman 0.58–0.71) to "how much accuracy is left" (MAE 0.110–0.118 vs. 0.200–0.213 uniform decline), with the strongest result being that a profile transferred from *other model families* works nearly as well as the model's own.
- **A methodological diagnosis of the compression literature.** Of 13 recomputable published fragility rankings, 9 shrink, vanish or reverse once chance is corrected or comparisons are held within one answer format; the paper traces most disagreements to three confounds (chance floors of 25–50%, generated-vs-multiple-choice scoring, and grouping facts by entity popularity rather than answer frequency).
- **An operational warning for deployment.** Compression removes the long tail of knowledge before the common core (frequency effect 0.23–0.41 under every one of the seven damages), and damaged models give almost no label-free signal of their damage (leave-one-model-out R² ≤ 0.38 across three candidate signals).

## Methodology

**Design.** A fully crossed measurement grid, preregistered in time-stamped protocols with locked analysis scripts and thresholds. 8 instruction-tuned models (Qwen2.5-1.5B/7B/14B/32B-Instruct, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, Gemma-2-9B-it, Phi-3.5-mini-instruct), bfloat16, greedy decoding, all conditions of a model on the same machine and software stack so damaged weights are bit-reproducible. 16 tasks × 4 answer formats = 64 measures per cell; >1,100 damaged conditions total.

**Tasks.** Six programmatic: adding 2/3/4 two-digit numbers, naming capitals of 78 countries, looking up a three-digit code among 12 key–code pairs, copying a six-letter nonsense word. Ten from benchmarks: SciQ, BoolQ, ARC-Challenge, CommonsenseQA, two MMLU subsets (math/physics; history/nutrition/geography/psychology), HellaSwag, PIQA, WinoGrande, OpenBookQA. Each task has 300 evaluation and 40 calibration items, frozen with hashes.

**Formats.** The four formats differ *only* in the final instruction: `choose` ranks candidates by summed log-probability (no generation), `answer` asks for the answer alone, `json` for `{"answer": "..."}`, `twice` for the answer written twice. Generation capped at 32 tokens. The scorer records, per response, template validity and whether the gold answer appears anywhere — enabling the crucial content-vs-template decomposition.

**Damages.** Four random: Gaussian noise (σ = r × each matrix's σ), random weight zeroing (fraction p), neuron ablation (random MLP hidden units), random layer removal (excluding first/last). Three compression: Wanda (|w|·‖x‖ within output rows), SparseGPT (column-wise, inverse-Hessian compensation, block 128, damping 0.01), GPTQ (column-wise error compensation, symmetric rounding, group scales, no activation reordering), all on every linear layer with 128×512-token C4 calibration segments; sparsity 0.2–0.9, GPTQ 4-bit/group-128 down to 2-bit/channel. Implementations were sanity-checked against naive baselines (Qwen2.5-7B at 50% sparsity: +30% perplexity under Wanda vs. >100× under magnitude pruning).

**Dose calibration.** Each cell is calibrated on held-out calibration seeds *before* any damaged evaluation, to eight target levels of the monotonized chance-corrected calibration score (0.95 down to 0.08), so doses mean the same thing across damages with incommensurable units.

**Estimation.** Equation (1) fitted by unweighted alternating least squares on `−log c_m(d)` over the window 0.03 < c_m(d) < 0.97, exponents normalized to geometric mean 1 per cell; task/format effects from the additive decomposition `log n_{t,f} = µ + α_t + β_f + ε_{t,f}`; damages compared by Pearson correlation of task effects over ≥8 shared tasks; seed noise handled by disattenuation with seed-to-seed reliability.

**Prediction protocol.** Four calibration tasks fixed in advance from intact margins only (WinoGrande, OpenBookQA, PIQA, copy-word), the damage strength `ĥ` estimated on those four, and all 12 other tasks predicted with *every* format held out. Baselines on the same measures: uniform decline, format effects alone, intact accuracy, intact margin + others' format effects, and a profile transferred from other model families with the target family entirely excluded.

## Results & Highlights

- **Cross-damage order agreement: median r = 0.904** (95% interval 0.81–0.95) over 35 damage pairs in 7 models, versus a shuffled-label 95th percentile of 0.184 for the extreme-task variant; across models under the same damage, 0.832–0.913 over 69 pairs. The preregistered thresholds (0.7 within model, 0.6 across) both passed.
- **Compression follows the random-damage order within a model: median r = 0.875** over 18 cells against a preregistered 0.7 bar, and 0.924 (Wanda) / 0.927 (SparseGPT) across models — but **GPTQ collapses across models to a median of 0.095** (6 pairs) while still holding within a model (0.53–0.90), so the preregistered across-model prediction fails on GPTQ alone.
- **Format is the largest single axis: relative fragility 1.5× (answer), 2.5× (json), 8× (twice) versus choose** under random damage, and 1.4×/2.1×/6.5× under compression. With content-only scoring the gaps shrink but persist (1.45×, 1.64×, 1.93×, preregistered), which localizes most of `twice`'s excess fragility to the template: at intermediate damage **83% of `twice` errors violate the template versus 0.2% of `answer` errors**, 79% of those still contain the correct answer, and on items `twice` gets wrong the same model answers correctly in `answer` **69%** of the time versus **1–4%** for the reverse.
- **Fact axis, capitals: median Spearman 0.42 (range 0.34–0.55)** between country–capital co-occurrence in Dolma and item survival, in all 8 models; **SciQ 0.38 (0.30–0.45)** for answer-string frequency; the preregistered key-lookup control, where the code is given in the prompt, falls to **0.08**. Partial correlations holding answer length and item margin constant stay at 0.26–0.55; holding country frequency constant, capital frequency predicts survival at **0.48–0.70**, while the reverse is **−0.22 to 0.18** — which explains why an earlier study that grouped facts by entity popularity concluded obscure entities were spared.
- **Task axis: intact margin predicts order at Spearman 0.58–0.73 in every model**, explains a median 63% of damage-averaged task-effect variance, and is a better predictor than intact accuracy in 6 of 7 models; the fitted power law has **β ≈ 1.95** (median), the value expected if damage strength grows with perturbation variance.
- **Prediction of held-out accuracy after compression: MAE 0.118 (Wanda), 0.118 (SparseGPT), 0.110 (GPTQ)** versus **0.200–0.213 for a uniform decline** — better in **23/23** cells, a 44–46% error reduction — but this **misses the preregistered 0.10 absolute bound** and sits at the noise ceiling of 0.117 (the difference between two seeds of the same random damage).
- **Profile transfer across families: MAE 0.117–0.125**, beating uniform decline in all 23 cells and also beating format effects alone (0.171–0.175), intact accuracy (0.185–0.205), and intact margin + others' format effects (0.133–0.142). The preregistered "own profile at least 10% better" test failed — the own profile is only marginally better.
- **Long-form tasks do not obey the order: 10 of 16 task–model pairs kept their position**, short of the preregistered 75%; multi-step reasoning became *more* fragile under pruning. The paper scopes its claims to short answers accordingly.
- **Function calling replicates the format finding:** 68.4% of damage-induced errors were format failures, 77.4% of these had the right function and arguments, and strict scoring made the task **2.3×** more fragile than content scoring.
- **Errors drift toward the familiar, as in patients:** a wrong capital was the more frequent of the two in **62–100%** of substitutions, and the most frequent distractor drew **48–62%** of choice errors against 33% by chance.
- **Damaged models are nearly undetectable without labels:** across three candidate label-free signals (template-failure rate rise, NLL rise on a probe text, KL divergence from the intact model), leave-one-model-out R² was **at most 0.38**.

## Authors & Credibility

Zhixiang Wang and Ulas Bagci, Department of Radiology, Feinberg School of Medicine, Northwestern University. Bagci is a well-established medical-imaging AI researcher (NIH-funded; R21-HL186024, R01-HL171376, U01-CA268808 are acknowledged), which fits the paper's closing motivation — that losing the long tail of knowledge matters most in domains like uncommon diagnoses. The work is nonetheless outside the group's usual methodological home: this is an evaluation-science paper in cs.CL, not a medical-imaging paper, and it is a two-author single-lab effort with no large compute team behind it.

Credibility signals are unusually strong for this genre. The study is preregistered in *time-stamped protocols with locked analysis scripts (checksummed)*, the paper lists every test as passed / partial / undecided / **failed** in a supplementary table, four preregistered tests are reported as failures in the main text rather than buried, and the authors disclose exactly which measurements pre-existed which preregistration (e.g. "the frequency tests used item outputs that existed before they were preregistered, but no frequency had been counted"). All per-item outputs, frozen item sets, pretraining counts, protocol documents (Chinese originals with English translations) and analysis code are released at a GitHub repository plus an interactive results page. The damage implementations were validated against naive baselines before the main run, and bit-level reproducibility of damaged weights was verified with a fixed probe (NLL matching within 0.1%).

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The individual ingredients are borrowed: the semantic-dementia analogy (rarer knowledge lost first, naming before recognition, errors drifting to familiar answers) is straight from Rogers & McClelland-style connectionist lesion work; "producing is harder than recognizing" is folk knowledge in evaluation; and the observation that chance floors and scoring rules distort cross-task comparisons has been made before. What is new and genuinely valuable is the *joint* design — 7 damages × 8 models × 16 tasks × 4 formats with dose calibration that makes damages commensurable — and the resulting claim that the order is invariant to mechanism. That claim is stronger than any single prior study because prior studies varied damage, task and scoring rule simultaneously, which is precisely the confound this paper isolates.

**Rigor.** The preregistration discipline is the best I have seen in a compression paper, and the failure reporting is honest to the point of self-harm (the headline "predictable" claim is immediately qualified by GPTQ, long-form tasks, and the missed 0.10 bound). But there is a structural concern: the rank-one model `c_m(d)=exp(−n_m h(d))` *imposes* order invariance by construction — if every measure decays exponentially in a shared dose, order preservation is a mathematical consequence, not an empirical discovery. The empirical content therefore lives in the fit quality (R² 0.70–0.95, i.e. visibly short of 1) and in the fact that the same `n_m` ordering recurs across mechanisms. The authors partly guard against this with the model-free Fig. 2a (raw accuracy at the half-ability dose, Spearman 0.91–0.99), but a reader should not mistake "orders correlate" for a model-free finding. Relatedly, the lowest fits are for layer removal (0.74 for Qwen2.5-14B), the damage whose mechanism is least like the others, which is where the rank-one assumption is most strained.

**Reproducibility.** Excellent on artifacts (per-item outputs, counts, protocols, code, checksums), weaker on compute disclosure: no GPU-hours, no wall-clock, no statement of how >1,100 conditions across a 32B model were afforded by a two-person radiology lab. Compression methods are deterministic and run once, which is defensible, but random damages get only three seeds — the paper itself reports that seed-to-seed accuracy differences at the same dose are 0.117, i.e. as large as its prediction error, which caps how much any accuracy-prediction result can be trusted at the single-cell level.

**Specific weaknesses.**
1. *The fact axis rests on two tasks with one-word answers.* Capitals (78 countries) and SciQ are the only tests of the frequency claim; both answers are single tokens/phrases. The paper concedes this ("the fact axis was tested in two tasks whose answers are words") but the three-axis story is presented as general, and there is no test on multi-token facts, dates, or numerical attributes — exactly the long-tail knowledge a medical deployment would care about.
2. *Frequency is a proxy measured in the wrong corpus.* Counts come from Dolma v1.7 under the **Llama-2 tokenizer** via infini-gram, but 7 of the 8 models were not trained on Dolma and do not use that tokenizer. The correlations (0.34–0.55) are consistent but modest, and a chunk of the residual could be corpus mismatch rather than a genuine "familiarity" axis.
3. *The format axis's biggest effect is unexplained.* `twice` is the most fragile format by a factor of 8, yet it *should* be less fragile than `json` (fewer template tokens must be exact) — and it isn't. Four preregistered explanations built on intact-model confidence all failed (Supplementary Note 2). Since format is the largest of the three axes (gap 0.42 vs. 0.33 for task and 0.17 for fact), the paper's most important axis contains its least understood effect. Calling the axis "how much of the answer must be produced" is a description, not an account.
4. *`choose` conflates format with scoring regime.* `choose` never generates — it ranks candidates by summed log-probability — so the choose-vs-answer contrast mixes "recognition vs. production" with "likelihood ranking vs. exact-match generation". The content-only rescoring (1.45×/1.64×/1.93×) mitigates this, and it is arguably the point of the comparison, but the paper never runs a `choose` variant that requires generating an option letter *and* scores it the same way, which would separate the two.
5. *Task selection may inflate the margin result.* The 16 tasks were chosen to span a wide margin range (the axis-scale analysis explicitly ranks tasks by margin quartiles), so "margin explains 63% of task-effect variance" is measured on a sample selected to have margin variance. The authors flag selection inflation in one place (ordering tasks by survival gives a larger 0.44 gap, "inflated by selection") but not for the margin regression.
6. *Calibration/evaluation leakage in two generated tasks.* The Methods admit that "calibration items share some question texts with evaluation items in two generated tasks (Supplementary Note 1)" — a real, if bounded, confound for the arithmetic and copy-word tasks, which are also two of the most robust measures in the ordering.
7. *Non-monotonic size trend is unexplained.* Within the Qwen family, cross-damage agreement goes 0.96 → 0.91 → 0.76 from 1.5B to 14B, then recovers to 0.97 at 32B. The paper reports this as a limitation but offers no account; with one model per other family, this could equally be noise in the 14B cell (its layer-removal fit is also the worst, 0.74).
8. *The baseline set for accuracy prediction is not the hardest one.* Uniform decline is a strawman; the paper does add intact margins (0.133–0.142) and intact accuracy (0.185–0.205), which is fair. But the Methods mention a second, stronger scheme — predicting each measure from all other measures of the *same damaged condition* (leave-one-out, ≥5 measures) — whose numbers never appear in the main text. That is the baseline a practitioner would actually use ("I measured 4 tasks of my compressed model, predict the rest"), and it is the one comparison that could shrink the headline 44–46% error reduction.
9. *Two-author self-audited preregistration, no independent replication.* The protocols are in Chinese, checksummed, and released — good practice — but nothing here has been reproduced by a second group, and the "20 audited studies" literature review is the authors' own reading of figures ("values read from figures are approximate").

**Significance.** High for evaluation methodology and for deployment practice: the "compare within one format, after chance correction, and report template failures separately" rule is immediately actionable, and the demonstration that nine of thirteen published fragility rankings dissolve under that rule is a genuine corrective to the compression literature. Moderate for mechanistic interpretability — the paper is explicit that the semantic-dementia resemblance concerns *what* is lost, not *why*. The claim most likely to be over-read is "predictable": the prediction is at the noise ceiling but still ±0.11 absolute accuracy, which is fine for ranking capabilities and useless for certifying a single task's accuracy.

## Strengths

- **Design scale and commensurability.** Dose calibration to matched levels of chance-corrected ability, *before* evaluation, is what lets 7 damages with incommensurable units (σ, sparsity fraction, bit-width, layer count) be compared at all — most papers in this space compare at nominal doses and inherit an apples-to-oranges problem.
- **The 4-format manipulation is the paper's best idea.** Holding the task fixed and changing only the final instruction turns "which capability is fragile" into a decomposable question, and the template/content split (83% template violations in `twice` vs. 0.2% in `answer`) is a clean, reusable diagnostic that most benchmark suites ignore.
- **Honest, granular preregistration reporting.** Passed / partial / undecided / failed, with the exact reason for each failure (GPTQ; long-form tasks; the 0.10 bound; the 10%-better-own-profile test) and disclosure of which measurements preceded which protocol.
- **A genuinely useful negative result on transfer.** The finding that a profile from *other model families* predicts nearly as well as the model's own (0.117–0.125 vs. 0.110–0.118) is the most practically valuable claim in the paper: it means one profiling run can serve a whole zoo of compressed checkpoints.
- **The literature audit is a contribution in its own right.** Recomputing 13 published rankings and showing 9 dissolve under chance correction or same-format comparison is the kind of unglamorous work that changes how a subfield reports results.
- **Complete artifact release**, including per-item outputs for all 1,100+ conditions and an interactive results page — enough for an independent reanalysis, which is rare at this measurement scale.

## Limitations

- **Short answers only.** Long-form tasks (chain-of-thought reasoning, code, instruction following, function calling) kept their position in only 10/16 pairs, and multi-step reasoning got *more* fragile under pruning. Since most deployment-relevant compression questions concern generation, this is a large scope restriction, not a footnote.
- **Two tasks for the fact axis, both single-word answers**, both scored by exact string match, with frequency proxied from a corpus (Dolma/Llama-2 tokenizer) that 7 of 8 models never trained on.
- **`twice` is unexplained.** The largest format effect has no mechanism; four preregistered confidence-based accounts failed.
- **Accuracy prediction is at the noise floor (±0.11), not at a useful absolute precision**, and the preregistered 0.10 bound was missed. The result supports ranking capabilities, not certifying them.
- **No mechanistic account.** The paper explicitly declines to explain *where* knowledge is stored or why margins set fragility beyond a variance-scaling argument (β ≈ 1.95), and the semantic-dementia parallel is presented as analogy, not homology.
- **Single seed for compression, three seeds for random damage**, no sampling-based decoding (greedy only), instruction-tuned models only, and no base-model comparison — so nothing is said about whether instruction tuning itself reshapes the fragility order.
- **No clinical or safety-critical evaluation**, despite the medical motivation in the Discussion; the rare-diagnosis argument is asserted, not tested.
- **The "no label-free signal" result (R² ≤ 0.38)** means the paper's own deployment recommendation reduces to "measure with labels", which is expensive and is exactly what the profile is supposed to save you from — a tension the Discussion acknowledges but does not resolve.

## Relevance

直接命中 **Reliability under Extreme Performance Optimization**：它把"量化/剪枝会不会伤到模型"从"平均分掉多少"改写成"哪类能力先掉、能不能提前算出来"，并给出可操作的评估纪律（同格式比较、chance 校正、模板失败单列）——对任何要上线 4-bit/2-bit 或高稀疏度权重的流水线都是可直接套用的验收方法。对 **Inference Reliability & SDC Detection** 也有间接价值：随机噪声/置零/消融被当作"硬件级损伤"的代理，说明随机扰动与结构化压缩造成的失效顺序相同，因此压缩场景下测得的脆弱度剖面可能可以用来预判故障注入下的失效面。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the order survive on multi-token facts (dates, dosages, numeric attributes, entity attributes) rather than single-word capitals and SciQ answers — and does Dolma frequency still predict survival when the answer is a phrase?
2. What actually makes `twice` the most fragile format? A token-level analysis of where the second copy diverges (delimiter, comma, repetition penalty, EOS handling) would be more informative than the four failed confidence-based accounts.
3. If a practitioner measures only 4 tasks of their own compressed checkpoint and interpolates the rest (the leave-one-out scheme mentioned in Methods but never reported), how much of the 44–46% error reduction survives? This is the comparison that decides whether the profile is worth measuring at all.
4. Why does GPTQ break cross-model transfer (median r = 0.095) while Wanda and SparseGPT do not (0.924/0.927)? Is it group-wise scale quantization, the 2-bit extremes, or error compensation redistributing knowledge across channels?
5. Does the fragility order change after instruction tuning or RLHF? The paper uses only instruct models; a base-vs-instruct comparison on the same 64 measures would test whether the axes are properties of pretraining or of the post-training pipeline.
6. Can the three axes be turned into a cheap *a priori* predictor — e.g. compute intact margin and answer frequency for a target domain's items, and forecast compression loss without running any damaged model at all? The transfer result suggests yes, but it is never tested on a domain the authors did not design.
7. Does the "damage sets the rate" decomposition hold for mixed damage (e.g. 4-bit weights *and* 50% sparsity simultaneously), which is what real deployment stacks actually apply?
