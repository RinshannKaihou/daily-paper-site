> **一句话总结：** 作者发现 GaLore/LDAdam 这类低秩投影优化器在 QAT 下明显掉点，原因是量化误差补偿信号大量落在 top-r 子空间之外；ResQAdam 只保留一份 BF16 动量、在主 r×r 子空间跑 Adam、并用残差的"符号"做全空间校正，在 2-bit 权重 QAT 下把优化器状态内存降了 71–74%（如 Llama 2-7B 上 51.81 GB → 13.28 GB），多数设置下困惑度与 AdamW 相差不到 1，吞吐只降 0.6–1.4%。

> **TL;DR:** Replacing AdamW inside quantization-aware training with a low-rank projected optimizer (GaLore rank-64, LDAdam rank-64) is catastrophic — on a from-scratch W2A16 QAT run the average WikiText-2/C4 perplexity is 30.61 (GaLore) and 28.57 (LDAdam) versus 20.05 for ResQAdam and roughly AdamW-level. ResQAdam keeps one BF16 momentum, runs Adam only on the r×r projected coordinates, and adds a norm-matched sign update from the orthogonal residual of that momentum; it cuts optimizer-state memory 71–74% versus AdamW (7.78 → 2.16 GB on Llama 3.2-1B, 51.81 → 13.28 GB on Llama 2-7B), costs 0.6–1.4% throughput, and stays within ~0.1–1.4 perplexity of AdamW across 12 from-scratch settings — but it is never compared against plain 8-bit AdamW, which has the same 2-bytes-per-parameter footprint.

## 核心贡献

- **诊断出低秩优化器在 QAT 中失效的机制**：在 STE 下梯度要逐元素补偿量化误差，这类信号分散在大量方向上；作者实测主 r 维子空间只捕获了动量能量的一部分（Figure 5），投影式优化器每步都把剩下这部分丢掉，且无法通过调学习率找回。
- **提出 ResQAdam**：单一 BF16 稠密动量 + 主 r×r 子空间内的 Adam 预条件更新 + 由正交残差符号构成、范数与主更新对齐的全空间校正，两者按动量能量比 ρ∥/ρ⊥ 融合；每 τ 步才做一次 SVD 并用 LDAdam 的统计传输规则迁移 r×r 的 m、v。
- **给出内存账本**：持久状态为 2nm 字节（BF16 动量）加上 O((n+m)r + r²) 的低秩开销，理论上约为 AdamW 8nm 的 25%，实测 71–74% 的降幅。
- **覆盖两种 QAT 范式的实证**：从头训练（QuEST、CAGE × W2A2/W2A16/W4A4 × 200M/430M）与三种微调流水线（Basic QAT、LLM-QAT、EfficientQAT Block-AP × Llama 3.2-1B / Qwen3 4B-Base / Llama 2-7B），并给出与 GaLore、GaLore-AdamW8bit、LDAdam、AdaFactor 的横向对比。
- **量化了实际开销**：单张 H200 上峰值显存降 15.3–19.5%，吞吐仅降 0.6–1.4%；并公开代码。

## Key Contributions

- **A mechanism-level diagnosis of why low-rank optimizers fail under QAT.** Because the STE gradient must compensate element-wise quantization error, the update is not concentrated in a few directions. The authors measure the principal-subspace energy ratio ρ∥ for seven weight matrices of a middle layer and show it starts high, decays, and settles well below 1 — highest for attention K/V, lowest for the MLP down projection — so projection-based optimizers discard real signal at every step.
- **ResQAdam**, a geometrically decoupled optimizer: one dense BF16 momentum, Adam preconditioning restricted to the r×r projected coordinates (with a √(nm)/r rescaling so the back-projected update has a comparable norm to a dense update), plus a full-space sign update built from the orthogonal residual M⊥, rescaled to match the principal update's Frobenius norm and weighted by the momentum energy split.
- **An explicit memory accounting**: persistent cost 2nm + O((n+m)r + r²) versus AdamW's 8nm, i.e. asymptotically ~25%, with measured savings of 71.0–74.4% depending on matrix shapes.
- **A broad empirical sweep** over two from-scratch QAT frameworks and three fine-tuning pipelines, three model families, and bit-widths down to 2-bit weights, including head-to-head comparisons against GaLore, GaLore-AdamW8bit, LDAdam and AdaFactor under grid-searched learning rates.
- **Ablations that isolate the design**: principal-only, residual-only, gradient-SVD bases, and global-momentum sign, plus rank/interval sensitivity and a hardware-efficiency table.

## Methodology

**Setup and notation.** For a weight matrix W ∈ R^{n×m}, ResQAdam maintains a single momentum M_t = β₁M_{t−1} + (1−β₁)G_t, where G_t is the STE gradient, stored in BF16. Every τ steps it computes the top-r singular vectors U_t ∈ R^{n×r}, V_t ∈ R^{m×r} of M_t (not of G_t), which span the principal subspace S_t = {U_t A V_tᵀ}. The orthogonal projector Π_t(X) = U_tU_tᵀXV_tV_tᵀ splits M_t = M_t∥ + M_t⊥ with the two parts orthogonal in Frobenius inner product.

**Principal update.** The gradient is projected to G_t∥ = U_tᵀG_tV_t ∈ R^{r×r}, Adam runs there (with its own m∥, v∥ states of size r²), and the result is mapped back as Δ_t∥ = α_r U_t Â_t V_tᵀ with α_r = √(nm)/r. The rescaling matters: an r×r update with O(1) entries has Frobenius norm O(r), whereas a dense update of the same entry scale has norm O(√(nm)).

**Residual sign update.** A second moment for M_t⊥ would reintroduce dense memory, so only the sign is kept: Δ_t⊥ = ‖Δ_t∥‖_F · sign(M_t⊥)/‖sign(M_t⊥)‖_F. The authors are explicit that element-wise sign does not preserve orthogonality to S_t, so Δ_t⊥ is "a direction derived from the residual", not an update confined to S_t⊥.

**Energy-aware fusion.** Because M_t∥ and M_t⊥ are orthogonal, ‖M_t‖² = ‖M_t∥‖² + ‖M_t⊥‖². The energy ratios ρ_t∥ = ‖M_t∥‖_F/(‖M_t‖_F+ε) and ρ_t⊥ = ‖M_t⊥‖_F/(‖M_t‖_F+ε) weight the two directions: Δ_t = ρ_t∥Δ_t∥ + ρ_t⊥Δ_t⊥, followed by W_t = (1−ηλ)W_{t−1} − ηΔ_t. The weights are treated as balancing coefficients, not as an exact norm decomposition.

**Subspace refresh.** SVD is amortized over τ steps; on refresh, only the compact r×r states m∥, v∥ are transported into the new basis using LDAdam's bias-corrected statistical transport (rotation of the first moment; variance transported through squared transition matrices with a non-negativity clip). M_t itself lives in the original parameter space and needs no transport.

**Hyperparameters.** From-scratch QAT: r = 32, τ = 400, bases initialized from the first gradient, and periodic refresh suspended during the first 10% of steps (early momentum of a randomly initialized model changes too fast and refreshes add transport noise). Fine-tuning: r = 64, τ = 200, no suspension. All other hyperparameters follow the host QAT codebases.

**Evaluation protocol.** From-scratch: Llama-style 200M and 430M models pretrained on C4 with a 40× model-size token budget under QuEST and CAGE, at W2A2, W2A16 and W4A4; perplexity on WikiText-2 and C4. Fine-tuning: Basic QAT (LSQ scales, 2B RedPajama tokens), LLM-QAT (data-free distillation), and EfficientQAT's Block-AP stage, on Llama 3.2-1B, Qwen3 4B-Base and Llama 2-7B; perplexity plus zero-shot HellaSwag/PIQA/ARC/WinoGrande/MMLU. Hardware numbers on a single H200, W4A4G128, context 4096, micro-batch 1 with 128 accumulation steps, optimizer memory counted over the transformer backbone only.

## Results & Highlights

- **From-scratch QAT (Table 1, 12 settings).** Average WikiText-2/C4 perplexity tracks AdamW closely: 430M QuEST W2A16 is 26.77 → 26.92 (+0.15), 430M CAGE W4A4 is 26.43 → 26.35 (−0.08, i.e. ResQAdam better), and the worst case is 430M QuEST W2A2 at 30.40 → 31.80 (+1.40, ~4.6% relative). ResQAdam wins 3 of 12 cells outright and loses the rest by ≤1.4 PPL.
- **Optimizer-state memory, from-scratch.** 200M models: 1.63 GB → 0.44 GB (↓73.0%); 430M models: 3.54 GB → 0.94 GB (↓73.4%), measured over non-embedding, non-LM-head parameters.
- **QAT fine-tuning memory (Table 2).** Llama 3.2-1B 7.78 → 2.16 GB (↓72.2%), Qwen3 4B-Base 29.07 → 7.78 GB (↓73.2%), Llama 2-7B 51.81 → 13.28 GB (↓74.4%); EfficientQAT block-wise accounting gives 494 → 141 MB/block (↓71.5%), 820 → 220 MB/block (↓72.0%) and 1644 → 451 MB/block (↓72.6%).
- **Quality at 2-bit weights.** Llama 3.2-1B Basic QAT W2A16G128: 47.36% avg accuracy vs 47.56% for AdamW (−0.20 pts) with PPL 17.73 vs 16.92; Qwen3 4B-Base Basic QAT W2A16G128: 61.94% vs 61.29% (+0.65 pts) with PPL 12.88 vs 12.76. Both are essentially tied.
- **Where ResQAdam clearly wins.** Llama 3.2-1B LLM-QAT W2A16C: PPL 35.05 vs 43.73 for AdamW (−19.8%) and accuracy 39.37% vs 38.26% (+1.11 pts); Llama 2-7B EfficientQAT W2A16G128: PPL 12.91 vs 24.61 (−47.5%).
- **Where it clearly loses.** Qwen3 4B-Base LLM-QAT W2A16C: accuracy 38.02% vs 40.85% (−2.83 pts) and PPL 46.72 vs 33.84 (+38%); Qwen3 4B-Base EfficientQAT W2A16G128: 43.09% vs 45.89% (−2.80 pts), PPL 51.25 vs 34.92.
- **Versus other memory-efficient optimizers (Table 4, avg WikiText-2/C4 PPL).** From-scratch W2A16G128: ResQAdam 20.05 vs GaLore-AdamW 30.61, GaLore-AdamW8bit 30.39, LDAdam 28.57, AdaFactor 33.01 — gaps of 8–13 PPL, far larger than any ResQAdam-vs-AdamW gap in Table 1. From-scratch W4A4G128: 18.89 vs 32.93 / 32.45 / 28.23 / 32.84.
- **Hardware cost (Table 3, W4A4G128, H200).** Peak memory 33.33 → 28.24 GB (↓15.3%) on Llama 3.2-1B, 43.93 → 37.19 GB (↓15.3%) on Qwen2.5-1.5B, 99.14 → 79.77 GB (↓19.5%) on Qwen3 4B; throughput 29.36k → 28.94k tok/s (↓1.4%), 18.31k → 18.19k (↓0.6%), 9.64k → 9.56k (↓0.8%).
- **Ablation (297M, W2A2/W2A16/W4A4).** Principal-update-only is worst across all three configs, residual-sign-only is much better but never the best, gradient-SVD bases underperform momentum-SVD bases (most visibly at W2A2), and a monolithic global-momentum sign is worse than the split — full ResQAdam is best in every setting.
- **Sensitivity.** Rank 4 is clearly bad (PPL 25.2 vs 19.8 at r = 64 under W2A16); for r ∈ {16,…,256} perplexity varies by at most 1.8 and non-monotonically, with r = 256 best in all three configs. Every τ from 50 to 2000 converges; variation is ~1 PPL with no trend (best: τ = 400 at W2A16, 100 at W4A16, 50 at W4A4).

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The core move — Adam in a low-rank subspace plus a state-free sign update on the residual — is a recombination of known parts: GaLore/LDAdam for the subspace, Fira and Frugal for full-rank/state-free residual updates (both cited and distinguished in Related Work), and Lion-style sign updates. The genuinely new pieces are (a) computing the bases from the *momentum* rather than the gradient, (b) taking the residual from the momentum rather than the current gradient, and (c) weighting the two directions by momentum energy. These are real but incremental; the paper's main value is the diagnosis, not the algorithm.

**The biggest missing baseline is plain 8-bit AdamW.** ResQAdam's persistent cost is 2nm bytes — exactly what `bitsandbytes` AdamW8bit costs for its two momentum buffers. The paper includes GaLore-AdamW8bit but never AdamW8bit itself, so the headline "71–74% reduction" is measured against the most expensive possible reference. If 8-bit AdamW preserves QAT quality (very likely, and it is a one-line change), ResQAdam's memory advantage evaporates and only its quality behavior remains to be argued. This is the single omission that most affects how the contribution should be read, and its absence is not discussed anywhere in the paper.

**Statistical rigor.** There are no error bars, no seed counts, and no variance estimates anywhere, yet several Table 2 cells move by amounts that are almost certainly within run-to-run noise — and in both directions. Llama 2-7B EfficientQAT W2A16G128 goes from 24.61 to 12.91 PPL (a 47% swing) and Qwen3 4B-Base LLM-QAT W2A16C goes from 33.84 to 46.72, while the accuracy columns for those same cells barely move (49.10 → 47.95 and 40.85 → 38.02). That pattern — huge PPL swings with small accuracy swings — looks like an unstable W2A16 fine-tuning regime rather than an optimizer effect, and single-seed reporting cannot distinguish the two. The paper's own hedge ("some aggressive W2A16 block-wise settings show larger metric fluctuations") understates how large those fluctuations are: a −2.83 pt accuracy drop on Qwen3 4B-Base LLM-QAT is a substantive regression, not a fluctuation.

**Ablation gaps.** The ablation covers principal-only, residual-only, gradient-SVD bases, and global-momentum sign, but never isolates the energy weighting ρ∥/ρ⊥ (no uniform-weight variant) nor the norm-matching rule that ties ‖Δ⊥‖ to ‖Δ∥‖. Since both are load-bearing design choices presented as principled, their absence from the ablation is conspicuous. The ablation is also run on a 297M model that appears nowhere else in the paper — Table 1 uses 200M and 430M — so the ablation and the main results are not on the same scale, which makes it hard to check that the ablation ranking predicts the main-table ranking.

**Baseline fairness, in both directions.** GaLore and LDAdam are described as tuned by grid search over learning rates at rank 64, and their from-scratch numbers (30.61, 28.57) are so much worse than ResQAdam's 20.05 that one suspects a tuning or implementation mismatch rather than a fundamental limitation — a 10-PPL gap is far larger than what the low-rank literature usually reports. Conversely, at fine-tuning W4A4G128 all optimizers land in 14.80–15.64 and AdaFactor (14.80) actually beats ResQAdam (15.05), which the paper reports honestly but does not analyze. The claim "projection-based methods fail in QAT" is well supported at 2-bit and much weaker at 4-bit.

**Scope.** From-scratch experiments stop at 430M parameters with a 40× token budget; there is no 1B+ from-scratch run, no MoE model, and no non-English data. The memory accounting excludes embeddings and the LM head, which is defensible but means the end-to-end peak-memory saving is 15–19%, not 71–74% — a gap the abstract does not foreground. Throughput is measured on one GPU type with micro-batch 1; the SVD's interaction with tensor parallelism or FSDP sharding (where an n×m SVD per matrix per τ steps may not amortize the same way) is untested.

**Red flags.** No AdamW8bit baseline; no seeds or error bars; a 297M ablation model absent from the main tables; large unexplained PPL swings at W2A16; and a residual sign update that the authors themselves concede is not orthogonal to the principal subspace, which weakens the geometric story used to motivate the method.

## Strengths

- The motivation is measured, not asserted: Figure 5's energy-ratio curves for seven weight matrices directly show that a substantial share of QAT momentum lives outside the principal subspace even immediately after a refresh, which is exactly the quantity projection-based optimizers throw away. The per-matrix pattern (high for K/V, lowest for MLP down) is a genuinely useful empirical finding independent of the proposed optimizer.
- The algorithmic accounting is honest about its own limits — the paper states plainly that the sign transform breaks orthogonality and that ρ∥/ρ⊥ are balancing coefficients rather than exact norm weights, rather than papering over it.
- Breadth of evaluation is well above average for an optimizer paper: two from-scratch QAT frameworks × three bit-widths × two scales, plus three fine-tuning pipelines × three model families, with the host QAT codebases left otherwise untouched.
- The engineering numbers are the ones practitioners care about and they are reported for three model sizes: sub-2% throughput cost and 15–19% peak-memory reduction on a single H200.
- Sensitivity analysis for both r and τ is thorough and, importantly, reports non-monotonicity rather than fitting a story to it — the admission that r = 256 beats the r = 32 actually used is a mark of good faith.
- Code is released, and the AI-use statement is specific.

## Limitations

- No comparison to 8-bit AdamW, the obvious low-effort alternative with the identical 2-bytes-per-parameter footprint; without it the memory claim is measured against a strawman reference.
- Single-seed results with no variance; the W2A16 fine-tuning cells show swings large enough that the direction of the effect is unclear for several models.
- From-scratch scale is capped at 430M with a 40× token budget — well below modern pretraining budgets — so the stability claims are untested where QAT actually hurts most.
- The residual sign update is not orthogonal to the principal subspace by the authors' own admission, so the "geometrically decoupled" framing is looser than the title suggests.
- Energy weighting and norm matching are not ablated, leaving the contribution of the two fusion hyper-mechanisms unknown.
- Memory savings are reported on optimizer state only; end-to-end peak-memory savings (15–19%) are roughly a fifth of the headline number.
- No distributed-training evaluation: FSDP/tensor-parallel sharding, where per-matrix SVDs and BF16 momentum replication behave differently, is not studied.

## Relevance

这篇落在 "Reliability under Extreme Performance Optimization" 这条线上：它关心的正是低比特 QAT 中"为了省内存而牺牲数值质量"的权衡，并给出了量化误差补偿信号为何难以被低秩压缩的机制解释。对做低精度训练/量化误差传播的人来说，Figure 5 的动量能量分布和 Table 4 里 2-bit 下投影优化器的崩塌幅度，比优化器本身更有参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. How does ResQAdam compare against plain 8-bit AdamW (bitsandbytes) at identical 2nm-byte state cost? If 8-bit AdamW is within noise, what does the residual machinery buy beyond implementation simplicity?
2. What are the seed-level variances behind the Table 2 W2A16 cells, especially Llama 2-7B EfficientQAT (24.61 → 12.91 PPL) and Qwen3 4B-Base LLM-QAT (33.84 → 46.72)? Are these reproducible or regime instability?
3. Why does AdaFactor beat ResQAdam at fine-tuning W4A4G128 (14.80 vs 15.05) while losing by 13 PPL from scratch? Does the residual correction matter only when quantization error is large, and if so, can the method switch itself off adaptively at 4-bit?
4. How does the energy ratio ρ∥ evolve with scale? The measurement is on one middle layer of a 297M model; do deeper layers or 7B-scale models show systematically lower principal energy, which would predict a larger benefit from the residual term?
5. Does the SVD-per-matrix cost still amortize under FSDP sharding or tensor parallelism, where matrices are split across devices and the τ = 400 interval interacts with gradient accumulation?
6. Can the sign update be replaced by a cheap structured approximation (e.g. row/column-scaled residual) that keeps orthogonality and might close the remaining 0.1–1.4 PPL gap to AdamW?
