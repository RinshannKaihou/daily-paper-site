> **一句话总结：** 论文把 208 篇校准文档（128 篇 Pile 通用文本 + 32 篇 MMLU + 32 篇 ARC/OpenBookQA + 16 篇 HellaSwag 训练样本，称为 FragCal-DC）混入低比特 PTQ 的校准集，在 6 个 1.7B–8B 稠密模型、6 种量化器、10 个任务上做四种子配对比较：两比特 SignRound V2 平均十任务准确率从 0.5367 升到 0.5550（+1.83 pp，CI [+0.91, +2.75]），而三比特 GPTQ/AWQ 只有 +0.23/−0.04 pp；但增益几乎全部集中在被选中的那三个任务族上（+3.86 pp vs 非目标任务 +0.20 pp），且 GSM8K 上反而下降 1.0 pp。

> **TL;DR:** A careful, deliberately over-hedged empirical study of *when* task-informed calibration data helps sub-4-bit weight-only PTQ. The recipe (FragCal-DC) adds 32 MMLU + 32 ARC/OpenBookQA + 16 HellaSwag training documents to a 128-document Pile-10k anchor, with the three families picked by an exploratory two-bit retention scan (MMLU 78.2%, ARC-C 84.3%, HellaSwag 86.0% FP retention — the three lowest). Using four paired calibration seeds on six dense models (Qwen3-1.7B/8B, Llama-2-7B, Mistral-7B, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Phi-3-mini) and a ten-task lm-eval panel, the headline is +1.83 pp for SignRound V2 at W2G128 over a Pile-200 control (0.5367 → 0.5550, 17.3% of the FP gap closed, 6/6 models positive, exact sign-flip p = 0.031), and +1.78 pp for OmniQuant's five-model W2 subset (0.4225 → 0.4403) — but +0.23 pp for GPTQ at W3 and −0.04 pp for AWQ at W3, so the "iterative beats single-pass" story collapses to a +0.06 pp class contrast with CI [−0.68, +0.80]. The most useful and least flattering results are in the controls: wrong-answer designations retain 90% of the gain (+1.44 vs +1.60 pp), an unranked Random-QA mixture gets +1.46 of the +1.93 pp, GSM8K at W2 *drops* from 0.058 to 0.048, and Gemma-2-9B's OmniQuant W2 diagnostic loses 5.62 pp. Read it as a well-instrumented null-ish result about method class and a real but narrow task-targeting effect, not as a new quantization method.

## 核心贡献

- **一个可复现的"任务知情"校准配方 FragCal-DC。** 208 篇文档 = 128 篇 Pile-10k 锚点 + 32 篇 MMLU auxiliary_train + 32 篇 ARC-C/ARC-E/OBQA 训练池 + 16 篇 HellaSwag 训练集；先用五模型两比特 SR-V2 保留率扫描（MMLU 78.2%、ARC-C 84.3%、HellaSwag 86.0%，为十任务中最低的三个）挑"脆弱任务族"，再用 {16, 32, 64} 的剂量-响应确定各补充分量。脚本 prep_fragcal.py 以 Apache-2.0 发布并附 SHA-256 校验与 HF 数据集 commit 钉定。
- **在"功能位宽"而非统一位宽上做比较。** 定义十任务均值 AVG ≤ 0.35 为崩溃、> 0.35 为可用，据此把 GPTQ/AWQ 推到三比特（它们六个模型的 W2 基线全部 ≤ 0.32），把 SR-V2/OmniQuant/AQLM 留在两比特——这解释了为什么此前"校准数据影响很小"的结论（AWQ/OmniQuant 用困惑度报告）与本文的准确率结论不矛盾：崩溃区里校准怎么换都没用。
- **跨方法、跨位宽、跨规模的配对证据。** 6 个 1.7B–8B 模型 × 6 个数据依赖量化器（RTN-opt、AWQ、GPTQ、OmniQuant-LWC、SignRound V2、AQLM）× W2/W3/W4 × 4 个校准种子 × 10 任务，共 96 条跨方法记录，另加 Llama-3.1-70B 的规模边界测试。
- **把"迭代 vs 单次"这个直觉证伪了一半。** 在各自的功能位宽上，两比特的 SR-V2（+1.83 pp）与 OmniQuant（+1.78 pp）确有响应，三比特的 GPTQ（+0.23）与 AWQ（−0.04）几乎没有；但把 SR-V2/OmniQuant 与 GPTQ/AWQ 合成类别对比只有 +0.06 pp、CI [−0.68, +0.80]、sign-flip p = 0.875，作者明确拒绝把它读成类别优势。
- **任务级与答案级的定位实验。** 增益集中在被选中的三个任务族（+3.86 pp vs 严格非目标任务 +0.20 pp，组内差 +3.65 pp）；把正确答案改成随机错误答案仍保留 90% 增益（+1.44 vs +1.60 pp），完全去掉答案标记保留 54.9%（+0.88 pp）——说明起作用的主要是"题目+选项的任务分布覆盖"，而不是"正确答案的监督信号"。
- **对"重建误差可以代理准确率"的否定。** 固定 128 篇 Pile 参考下，AQLM 的重建 MSE 上升 5.76% 而准确率上升 0.73 pp（5/6 模型方向相反），GPTQ MSE 上升 3.90% 而准确率上升 0.23 pp；一次额外优化把自身目标再降 6–30%，却让 15/20 个准确率单元变差。

## Key Contributions

- **A documented, reproducible task-informed calibration recipe.** FragCal-DC is a fixed 208-document mixture (128 Pile-10k anchor, 32 MMLU `auxiliary_train`, 32 ARC-C/ARC-E/OpenBookQA train, 16 HellaSwag train), with supplements chosen by an exploratory five-model two-bit retention scan and sized by a {16, 32, 64} dose–response sweep. `prep_fragcal.py` is released under Apache-2.0 with SHA-256 hashes and pinned HuggingFace revisions.
- **A "functional regime" methodology for calibration comparisons.** Rather than comparing at a common bit width where some quantizers are already collapsed, the paper first locates each method's lowest width with ten-task AVG > 0.35 (GPTQ and AWQ: W3; SR-V2, OmniQuant, AQLM: W2) and then measures calibration response there. This reframes prior negative results (AWQ/OmniQuant reporting small perplexity variation across calibration sources) as a regime artifact.
- **A large paired cross-method panel.** Six dense models (1.7B–8B) × six data-dependent quantizers × W2/W3/W4 × four calibration seeds × ten tasks, plus Llama-3.1-70B at W2, Mixtral-8x7B at W3 on ARC-Challenge, and AutoRound W2A8 on Phi-3/Mistral.
- **A negative result about method class.** The intuitive "iterative quantizers benefit from calibration data, single-pass ones do not" story is measured and then explicitly refused: the class contrast at matched W3 is +0.06 pp with CI [−0.68, +0.80] and p = 0.875, and the authors state that their configuration grid cannot separate optimizer class from baseline accuracy, codebook, objective, or initialization.
- **Localization experiments that constrain the mechanism.** Gains concentrate on calibrated task families (+3.86 vs +0.20 pp), survive wrong-answer designations (90% retained), shrink to 54.9% without any designation, and are matched by an unranked Random-QA mixture at 76% of the effect (+1.46 vs +1.93 pp) — pointing at task-distribution coverage rather than answer supervision.
- **Evidence against reconstruction-error proxies.** Under a fixed Pile reference, MSE and accuracy move in opposite directions in 5/6 AQLM cells and 2/6 GPTQ cells; a second optimization pass lowers its own objective while degrading 15/20 accuracy cells.

## Methodology

**Setup.** Weight-only block-wise PTQ with group size 128 (G128), widths W2/W3/W4, on six dense models: Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-8B, Llama-2-7B, Mistral-7B-v0.1, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Phi-3-mini-4k-instruct. Llama-3.1-70B is a separate scale test. Evaluation uses lm-eval-harness 0.4.11 on ARC-Challenge, ARC-Easy, BoolQ, HellaSwag, LAMBADA, MMLU, OpenBookQA, PIQA, TruthfulQA-MC1, and WinoGrande; AVG is their unweighted mean. Full-precision (FP16, BF16 for Phi-3) accuracy is the per-model reference.

**Quantizers.** Data-free RTN as reference; three single-pass methods (RTN-opt = SR-V2's importance-weighted scale search with zero iterations; AWQ = activation-aware scale search; GPTQ = Hessian-based rounding correction); three iterative methods (OmniQuant with learnable weight clipping only, LET disabled, 20 epochs; SignRound V2 via AutoRound 0.12.0, 200 iterations; AQLM 1×16g8 additive codebook, W2-equivalent). Calibration sequence lengths differ by method (512 tokens for AWQ/OmniQuant, 2048 for GPTQ/SR-V2/RTN-opt) — a deliberate choice to use each method's native setting rather than a matched protocol.

**The intervention.** Only the calibration *text* changes; the update rule, bit width, group size, and evaluation are held fixed. Each model/method/width/seed is quantized twice — once with a Pile control (Pile-128 or Pile-200 requested samples) and once with FragCal-DC — from the same unquantized checkpoint. Four calibration seeds {42, 123, 456, 789}; per-model paired differences are averaged over seeds before cross-model aggregation.

**Recipe construction.** An exploratory SR-V2 W2G16 seed-42 scan over five models (Phi-3 excluded) ranks ten tasks by quantized/FP retention: MMLU 78.2%, ARC-Challenge 84.3%, HellaSwag 86.0% are the three most fragile; BoolQ 95.9% the least. A dose–response sweep (+16/+32/+64 documents per supplement over a 128-document Pile control) gives across-model median recommendations of 32 MMLU, 32 ARC/OBQA, 16 HellaSwag. Short QA items are concatenated into long documents (12,000-character running target) before sampling, so the 208 "documents" are not 208 independent samples and effective sequence counts depend on the tokenizer and loader.

**Statistics.** Accuracy differences in percentage points and FP gap closure GC = 100·(A_FragCal − A_ctrl)/(A_FP − A_ctrl). Panel means use 95% Student-t intervals across models; principal contrasts use exact two-sided model-level sign-flip tests (which are coarse: 0.03125 is the floor at n = 6, 0.0625 at n = 5). The paper is unusually explicit that seeds do not add independent models and that model independence is only approximate because several models share architecture and training lineage.

**Controls.** (i) A zero-iteration RTN-opt arm on the same mixture, to test whether iteration is required; (ii) correct/wrong/absent answer designations; (iii) an unranked 200-item Random-QA mixture and three alternative selection criteria (Diversity-DC, LSA-DC, ArXiv-OOD); (iv) a Pile-128 → Pile-200 volume control; (v) an item-level overlap audit between calibration and evaluation text; (vi) fixed-reference reconstruction error, optimizer-variant and multi-pass probes, scale-CV sensitivity, and a held-out WikiText cross-entropy correlation.

## Results & Highlights

- **Two-bit SignRound V2 is the headline.** On the six-model, ten-task, four-seed panel at W2G128, FragCal-DC moves mean accuracy from 0.5367 (Pile-200) to 0.5550, i.e. **+1.83 pp with 95% CI [+0.91, +2.75]** and 17.3% of the FP gap closed; 6/6 model means are positive and the exact sign-flip test gives p = 0.03125 (the smallest attainable value at n = 6). Per-model spread is large: Mistral-7B +2.36 pp and Phi-3 +2.97 pp, but Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct only +0.54 pp.
- **OmniQuant confirms it only under model selection.** Its five-model W2 subset goes 0.4225 → 0.4403 (**+1.78 pp, CI [+1.28, +2.27]**, GC 8.2%, 5/5 positive), but including Mistral-7B — whose Pile baseline is 0.320, below the 0.35 functional threshold — drops the estimate to +1.20 pp with CI [−0.34, +2.73], and the exact sign-flip p is 0.0625, the floor for five models. The paper reports both rather than the flattering one.
- **Single-pass methods show essentially nothing at their functional width.** GPTQ at W3G128: 0.5747 → 0.5770 (**+0.23 pp, CI [−0.91, +1.37]**, GC 3.4%, only 3/6 models positive). AWQ at W3G128: 0.6053 → 0.6049 (**−0.04 pp, CI [−0.36, +0.28]**, 4/6 positive). RTN-opt at W2: −0.05 pp [−0.29, +0.19], 2/6 positive. AQLM at W2-equivalent: +0.73 pp [−0.14, +1.61], 5/6 positive but the interval crosses zero.
- **The "iterative class" claim does not survive.** Averaging SR-V2/OmniQuant against GPTQ/AWQ at matched W3 gives a class contrast of **+0.06 pp, CI [−0.68, +0.80], exact sign-flip p = 0.875**, with only 3/6 positive model contrasts. Gap-closure comparisons do favor SR-V2 over AWQ (paired t(5) = 3.40, one-sided p = 0.010), but that uses method-specific control references and is not a like-for-like accuracy comparison.
- **At 70B the ranking flips depending on the metric.** Llama-3.1-70B W2G128 against a BF16 reference of 0.7215: GPTQ improves **+2.33 pp** (0.3665 → 0.3899, GC 6.6%) while SR-V2 improves only **+0.86 pp** (0.659 → 0.668) but closes 13.8% of its (much smaller) gap. So the 70B result both extends calibration sensitivity to large models and contradicts the small-model pattern that iterative methods respond more.
- **Gains are task-local, not general.** On the five-model SR-V2 W2 panel, the three fragility-selected tasks gain **+3.86 pp [+1.25, +6.46]** versus **+0.20 pp [−0.78, +1.18]** on five strict non-targeted tasks, a within-model difference of +3.65 pp [+0.67, +6.64] (5/5 models positive). On GSM8K 8-shot exact match the transfer is negative: 0.048 with FragCal vs 0.058 with Pile-200 at W2.
- **Answer supervision is not the active ingredient.** On the five-model Pile-200 panel, correct designations give +1.60 pp, uniformly random *wrong* designations give +1.44 pp (**90.0% of the gain retained**), and removing the designation entirely gives +0.88 pp (54.9%). The zero-iteration RTN-opt control on the same data gives −0.04 pp [−0.35, +0.28].
- **Recipe choice matters less than task coverage.** An unranked 200-item Random-QA mixture gets +1.46 pp against FragCal's +1.93 pp on the six-model SR-V2 W2 panel (18/24 vs 24/24 positive model-seed pairs). FragCal beats Diversity-DC by +2.01 pp, ArXiv-OOD by +2.55 pp, and LSA-DC by +1.11 pp with a CI that includes zero.
- **Reconstruction error is a bad proxy.** From Pile-200 to FragCal, AQLM's fixed-reference MSE rises 5.76% while accuracy rises 0.73 pp (5/6 cells mismatched); GPTQ's MSE rises 3.90% with +0.23 pp; SR-V2's MSE falls 5.12% with +1.83 pp (2/6 mismatched). A second optimization pass lowers its own objective by a further margin yet degrades 15/20 accuracy cells.

## Authors & Credibility

Yehong Jiang (MIT, corresponding author; work done during an Intel internship), Yen-Kuang Chen and Xinmin Tian (Intel). The Intel affiliation explains the unusual rigor on the engineering side — everything runs on Intel Data Center GPU Max 1550 (Ponte Vecchio) with documented XPU workarounds for GPTQModel, AWQ, OmniQuant, and AQLM, and the paper reports a ~3,000 PVC tile-hour research budget with per-method wall-clock costs. All six quantizers are re-implemented and re-run under one harness rather than quoted from prior papers, which is the main reason the cross-method table is trustworthy at all.

The paper's credibility is carried less by its venue (NeurIPS 2026) than by its reporting discipline: it discloses the 0.35 functional threshold as a post-hoc descriptive device, gives the all-six-model OmniQuant estimate alongside the five-model one, states that the exact sign-flip p = 0.0625 is the floor at n = 5, warns that gap closure inflates when the baseline is near FP, and lists in Appendix R.4 an explicit "what each probe establishes and leaves unresolved" table. It also reports results that damage its own thesis: the Gemma-2-9B OmniQuant probe (−5.62 pp), the GSM8K regression, the Mixtral GPTQ regression (−1.39 pp), and an unresolved item-overlap audit. The NeurIPS checklist honestly answers "No" to full code/artifact release, because only the calibration generator ships, not the method-specific XPU implementations.

One structural caveat on provenance: SignRound V2 is an Intel/AutoRound method, OmniQuant's LWC-only harness was written in-house for this platform, and the paper's most favorable result is for SignRound V2. That is not misconduct — the comparison protocol is paired and the negative controls are reported — but the flagship number is measured on the authors' own quantizer with a hand-tuned XPU port, and no independent replication exists.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty: modest, and the paper mostly knows it.** Task-informed or domain-matched calibration is not new (domain-specific GPTQ calibration, "calibration data selection" work, and Williams & Aletras 2024 — which this paper cites and partly reproduces — all precede it). FragCal-DC is a fixed 208-document mixture chosen by a single-seed, single-group-size (G16) exploratory scan over five models, and the paper's own dose–response table shows the ARC/OBQA optimum at 32 documents (+1.60 pp) while the final recipe also carries 32 MMLU (+0.39 at that dose) and 16 HellaSwag (+0.77) — components whose individual contributions are much smaller than the headline. The real contribution is methodological: comparing calibration response *at functional bit widths* rather than at a common width, which is a genuinely good idea and explains a real discrepancy with the AWQ/OmniQuant perplexity-based literature.

**The statistics are honest to the point of self-defeat.** The 95% intervals are computed across n = 5–6 models, several of which share architecture and training lineage (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Qwen3-8B; Llama-2-7B and Mistral-7B are both 7B-era base models). The sign-flip test's floor at n = 5 is 0.0625, so OmniQuant literally cannot reach p < 0.05 on its designated panel — the authors say so, which is admirable, but it also means the "iterative methods benefit" claim rests on a six-model SR-V2 result with p = 0.031 and an effect size of 1.83 pp against a between-model SD that produces a ±0.92 pp interval. A single additional model in the opposite direction would erase significance. The Gemma-2-9B probe, which is excluded from all aggregates, points exactly that way: OmniQuant's W2 five-task diagnostic *falls* from 0.6332 to 0.5770 (−5.62 pp) with FragCal.

**The task-concentration result is the paper's most important finding and also its biggest problem.** +3.86 pp on the three calibrated families versus +0.20 pp elsewhere means the recipe is not "better calibration" but "training on the test distribution" — and the paper concedes the confound: "This supports task-aligned recovery but does not separate fragility from calibration-source exposure." The item-overlap audit (Table 59) finds 0–7 normalized question-key matches per task per seed, concentrated in the ARC/OBQA slot, and the authors state plainly that they have not verified full question-plus-options duplication nor measured the accuracy effect of removing matched items. Combined with the ARC/OBQA → MMLU transfer result (+1.50 pp over direct MMLU supplementation, 19/20 seed pairs) — which shows the *strongest* MMLU gain comes from supplementing a *different* QA family — the most likely reading is distribution/format alignment, not task knowledge. That is a legitimate mechanism, but it makes "task-informed calibration" a weaker and less transferable claim than the abstract implies.

**The wrong-answer control undercuts the framing more than the paper admits.** If random incorrect answers retain 90% of the gain and no designation retains 55%, then what matters is that the calibration text *looks like* multiple-choice exam items in the target format — not that it carries correct labels. This is consistent with the paper's "distribution alignment" hypothesis, but it also means the effect could likely be reproduced by any synthetic MCQ corpus, which the paper never tests (no synthetic-QA arm, no shuffled-label-with-format-preserved arm beyond the wrong-answer condition).

**Missing ablations that would have settled the mechanism.** (1) No supplement-size-matched Pile control beyond 128 vs 200 documents — the volume control shows Pile-128 → Pile-200 moves the mean by only −0.05 pp, which argues volume is not the driver, but the token budgets are not equalized and FragCal writes 208 documents. (2) No per-component ablation of the final 32/32/16 allocation; only the dose–response table exists, and the final sizes are "across-model medians of model-specific recommendations" that explicitly do *not* maximize pooled ten-task mean — a defensible but unexplained choice. (3) The exploration used G16 while the main experiments use G128, and Phi-3 was excluded from the selection panel, so the fragility ranking that drives everything is a single-seed artifact of a different configuration. (4) No cross-entropy/perplexity-matched baseline to test whether the gain is just "calibration text closer to the eval distribution in perplexity."

**The scale result is thin.** Llama-3.1-70B is a single model, and its AWQ arm has one seed. GPTQ's +2.33 pp at 70B is the paper's second-strongest number and it directly contradicts the small-model story (GPTQ is the single-pass method that "should not" respond). The authors note the gap-closure ranking reverses, which is honest, but the section is too short to carry the weight the abstract places on it ("At 70 billion parameters, GPTQ and SignRound V2 both improve at two bits").

**Reproducibility is good on data, weak on execution.** The calibration generator ships with SHA-256 hashes and pinned HF revisions, and the settings appendix is unusually detailed (damping 0.05 with 0.01 increments, 200 SR-V2 iterations, OmniQuant clipping LR 0.01, AQLM 1×16g8 with 100 epochs). But the method-specific XPU ports are not released, the paper's own checklist item 5 answers "No," and several recorded fields are acknowledged as incomplete (SR-V2's batch size is present in only 72 of 144 dense configurations; 20 RTN-opt W4 controls lack sample counts). Re-running this on CUDA will not reproduce the numbers exactly.

**Significance.** The practical recommendation in Section 7 — start from a generic-calibration baseline with usable accuracy, then compare mixtures of task-relevant training data and generic text at fixed settings, and check item overlap — is sound, cheap, and worth adopting. The stronger claim that calibration should be "designed around the model's intended downstream tasks" is not yet supported beyond a ten-task MCQ/commonsense suite on 1.7B–8B dense models, and it is directly contradicted by the GSM8K and Gemma results. Code generation and multi-turn dialogue are untested, as the authors state.

## Strengths

- **Regime-aware comparison design.** Locating each quantizer's functional width before measuring calibration response is the right methodological move, and it reconciles this paper's positive accuracy results with the earlier perplexity-based literature that found calibration sources barely matter.
- **Real paired experiments, not literature numbers.** All six quantizers are re-run under one harness with four paired calibration seeds, the same unquantized starting checkpoint, and matched evaluation settings — rare in a quantization paper and the only way the cross-method table means anything.
- **Exemplary disclosure of weak results.** The Gemma-2-9B regression, the GSM8K regression, the Mixtral GPTQ regression, the OmniQuant all-six-model interval that crosses zero, the p = 0.0625 floor, and the unresolved item-overlap audit are all reported in the main text or clearly signposted appendices.
- **Mechanism probes that falsify a common assumption.** The fixed-reference reconstruction table (AQLM: MSE +5.76%, accuracy +0.73 pp) and the multi-pass probe (lower objective, 15/20 accuracy cells worse) are directly useful to anyone who tunes calibration by watching reconstruction loss.
- **Honest uncertainty vocabulary.** The paper distinguishes seed variability from cross-model uncertainty, notes that GC inflates near FP, labels single-seed scans, and includes an explicit evidence map (Table 55) of what each probe does and does not establish.
- **Practical, cheap recipe.** One re-quantization per configuration, ~1 hour for SR-V2 on a 7B model, and a released construction script with checksums.

## Limitations

- **Panel size and model relatedness.** Five to six models with shared architecture lineages; n = 5–6 is too small for the sign-flip test to reach p < 0.05 in the OmniQuant case, and a single dissenting model (Gemma-2-9B, excluded from aggregates) reverses the direction.
- **Task-targeting confound is unresolved.** Gains concentrate on calibrated families, and the item-overlap audit found 0–7 normalized question matches per task per seed without verifying full-item duplication or measuring its accuracy impact. No contamination-adjusted gain is claimed — but none is available either.
- **Optimizer class is not identified.** The authors concede the grid cannot separate iteration from baseline accuracy, codebook, objective, or initialization; retention matching at designated widths yields only one or two usable model pairs, all OmniQuant vs RTN-opt.
- **Narrow task suite.** Ten multiple-choice/commonsense/cloze benchmarks. GSM8K (the only generative task) shows a regression, and code generation, multi-turn dialogue, long-context, and instruction following are untested or covered only in small appendices with different method coverage.
- **Recipe construction is under-ablated.** Single-seed G16 exploratory scan over five models (Phi-3 excluded), final allocation from across-model medians rather than an ablation, and no synthetic-QA or format-matched control that would isolate "MCQ-shaped text" from "task-specific content."
- **Calibration volume is not matched.** FragCal writes 208 documents vs 128/200 for Pile; the Pile-128 → Pile-200 control (−0.05 pp mean) argues volume is not the driver but does not equalize token budgets, and effective sequence counts vary by tokenizer and loader.
- **Incomplete artifact release.** Only the calibration generator ships; the method-specific XPU implementations and environments do not, and several recorded configuration fields are acknowledged incomplete.
- **Weight-only focus.** QuIP/QuIP#, SpQR/SqueezeLLM, HQQ, FP4 formats, KV-cache compression, and QAT are all out of scope; the W2A8 extension covers only two models with AutoRound and no single-pass weight-activation comparator.

## Relevance

直接落在「Reliability under Extreme Performance Optimization」这条主线上：它把"量化压缩把模型能力悄悄改坏多少、以及能否用数据把损失补回来"作为核心被测量，而不是把比特数当成纯速度/显存指标。对做低比特推理可靠性的人来说，最有价值的不是 +1.83 pp 这个数字，而是三条可复用的结论：崩溃区（AVG ≤ 0.35）里换校准数据无效、重建误差不能代理下游准确率、以及"增益集中在被校准的任务族"这一必须警惕的评测污染模式。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. **How much of the +1.83 pp is item overlap?** The audit found up to 7 normalized question-key matches per task per seed in the ARC/OBQA slot. What happens to the six-model W2 SR-V2 gain after removing every calibration document whose question matches an evaluation item, with matched document counts restored?
2. **Does a format-matched synthetic corpus reproduce the effect?** If random wrong answers retain 90% of the gain, a synthetic set of MCQ-shaped items with no relation to the benchmarks (e.g., generated exam questions with shuffled labels) should get most of the way there. If it does not, the "distribution alignment" explanation is incomplete.
3. **Why does Gemma-2-9B go the other way?** OmniQuant's W2 diagnostic drops 5.62 pp with FragCal on Gemma. Is that soft-capping, the normalization layout, sliding-window attention, or a genuinely different calibration sensitivity? The paper lists these as untested.
4. **What explains the 70B reversal?** GPTQ gains +2.33 pp at W2 on Llama-3.1-70B while SR-V2 gains only +0.86. Is this headroom (GPTQ starts at 0.3665 vs SR-V2 at 0.659), or does single-pass Hessian correction become more calibration-sensitive with width? A width sweep at 70B (W3, W4) and a second 70B-class model would help.
5. **Can the recipe be selected without target-task data?** The whole premise requires knowing the deployment tasks. Is there a proxy — e.g., the retention ranking recovered from a small unlabeled probe set, or from held-out cross-entropy (the r = 0.778, p = 0.083 correlation is suggestive but far from a predictor) — that generalizes across model families?
6. **Does the effect compose with weight-activation quantization and FP4 formats?** The W2A8 AutoRound extension shows +2.25 to +3.29 pp on Phi-3 and Mistral, but with no single-pass comparator and no kernel-level validation; whether task-informed calibration survives MXFP4/NVFP4 block-scale formats is open.
