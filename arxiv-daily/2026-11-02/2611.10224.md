> **一句话总结：** Jais 3 不做从零预训练，而是把全开放的 K2 Horizon 六款模型（0.9B–375B）用"持续预训练 → SFT → FTPO → 混合任务 RLVR"改造成阿拉伯语智能体，7B 在 SWE-bench Verified 拿到 70.2%（Qwen3.5-9B 为 50.8%）、AraIFBench 91.6%（gemma-4-12B-it 为 75.3%），但 375B 在自家新建的阿拉伯语文件处理基准上（64.1%）反而低于自家 32B（71.8%），且"幻觉更少"主要是高思考模式下大量弃答换来的。

> **TL;DR:** Jais 3 adapts the open K2 Horizon fleet to Arabic via 201.5B tokens of continual pretraining (75% Arabic-side, 25% replay), SFT, FTPO and mixed-task RLVR, releasing six models from 0.9B to 375B with every stage checkpoint. On audited generative Arabic benchmarks it is strong where Arabic-specific behavior is trained: Jais-3-7B reaches 85.23% on ArabicMMLU vs 84.08% for gemma-4-12B-it and 80.93% for Qwen3.5-9B, and 91.60% on the new AraIFBench vs 75.30% for gemma-4-12B-it. On agentic tasks the 7B beats Qwen3.5-9B by +19.4 points on SWE-bench Verified (70.2 vs 50.8) and by +41.4 on GDPval (60.5 vs 19.1), but at 32B it loses to Qwen3.8-27B by 16.1 points on Terminal-Bench 2.1 (63.7 vs 79.8) and at 375B by 18.6 on the internal Arabic File-Processing Benchmark (64.1 vs 82.7). Its most distinctive result is abstention: on SimpleQA-Verified-Ar the 375B declines to answer 93.51% of the questions it gets wrong (Qwen3.5-397B: 2.88%) while scoring an F-score of only 5.49 vs 55.16 — reliability bought with coverage.

## 核心贡献

- **一个开放的中英/阿拉伯语智能体模型族**：六款模型，四款 dense（0.9B、3.7B、7B、32B）加两款稀疏模型（36B MoVA，4.67B 激活；375B MoE，23.59B 激活 / 376.09B 总参数）。除 0.9B 外全部继承 K2 Horizon 的 512K token 上下文。作者称 375B 是迄今最大的开放阿拉伯语中心模型。
- **真正的"智能体"而不只是"能对话"**：训练目标包括多步规划、工具调用与 MCP server、终端操作、网页搜索，以及在阿拉伯语下读写 PDF/表格/演示文件；配套构建了 220 个任务、44 个职业、22 个阿拉伯国家、7,356 条评分细则的阿拉伯语文件处理基准。
- **一套可复现的语言适配配方**：不做词表扩展（K2 Horizon 的 250,624 词表已按约四分之一阿拉伯语语料训练，cl100k 需 4.17 token/阿拉伯语词，它只需 1.86），全参数训练，每阶段固定 25% 回放基座自身 mid-training 数据以抑制遗忘。
- **重做阿拉伯语评测方法**：把所有基准改为生成式评测，并用三个 LLM 裁判（Gemini 3.8 Flash、Claude Opus 5、GPT-5.6 Sol）审计 15 个基准集、69,168 道题，剔除 3,634 题（5.3%），其中 51% 的剔除原因是标准答案本身错误。
- **六个新基准**：AraIFBench（阿拉伯语指令遵循）、I'rab（语法分析）与 Tashkeel（标音）、AA-LCR-Ar、SimpleQA-Verified-Ar、阿拉伯语文件处理基准。
- **超出权重的开放**：每个训练阶段的 checkpoint（CPT、cooldown、SFT、FTPO、RLVR）、训练代码（xLLM、RL360）、数据样本与配方、W&B 日志、审计后的基准与被剔除题目（含三位裁判的理由）。

## Key Contributions

- **A six-model Arabic-centric agentic fleet (0.9B–375B)**, including two sparse architectures: a 36B Mixture-of-Value-Attention model (4.67B active) and a 375B fine-grained MoE (192 routed + 1 shared expert per layer, 8 routed active, 23.59B active). All but the 0.9B keep the 512K-token context window.
- **Agentic competence as a first-class target**, not an afterthought: multi-step planning, tool/MCP calls, terminal operation, web search, and Arabic file production (XLSX/DOCX/PDF/PPTX with correct letter shaping and RTL layout).
- **A documented adaptation recipe instead of pretraining from scratch**: full-parameter continual pretraining on a post-trained checkpoint, unchanged tokenizer, 25% replay of the base model's own mid-training data (web, reasoning, agentic traces) in CPT, cooldown and SFT, then SFT → FTPO → mixed-task RLVR.
- **A rebuilt Arabic evaluation methodology**: fully generative scoring with LLM judges, plus a three-judge audit that removes defective items and releases both the cleaned benchmarks and every discarded item with verdicts and suggested fixes.
- **Six new benchmarks** covering Arabic instruction following, i'rab, tashkeel, long-context reasoning, factuality and agentic file work.
- **A staged open release**: weights, per-stage checkpoints, recipes, data samples, training/RL/eval code, logs, and audited benchmarks under Apache 2.0 (data under ODC-BY / CC BY 4.0).

## Methodology

**Starting point.** Each Jais 3 model begins from the *final post-trained* K2 Horizon checkpoint of the same size, with architecture and tokenizer frozen. This is the paper's main structural bet: adaptation must add Arabic without eroding reasoning, long-context and agentic behavior, so CPT is applied to an already instruction-tuned, agentic model rather than to a base model.

**Data.** The inventory is ~1.63T tokens (1.41T pretraining-style across 39 datasets, 224B chat-formatted across 35). The continual-pretraining main stage samples 201.5B tokens: 75% Arabic-side, 25% replay. Natural Arabic web text and literature supply only 19% of the mixture — the authors state plainly that natural Arabic "runs out long before the token budget" — so translated and synthetic Arabic supply 44% (≈16 points Arabizi in Latin script, ≈22 points Arabic script, plus 6 points of the English side of parallel dialect conversations). Cooldown adds 3.47B tokens at a 3:1 ratio of curated high-quality sets to recent mid-training replay; SFT samples ~41B tokens including 2,180 Arabic file-task agentic trajectories (0.16B) and 25% replay.

**Processing.** One document per record, per-source quality filters, MinHash dedup (128 permutations, 13-character shingles), and lexical decontamination with AllenAI's `decon` (8-gram, stride 3, IDF-weighted overlap ≥0.8). The per-source filter point is well made: one shared rule set removed 12% of a dialect corpus and 34% of a diacritized one, while per-source rules kept over 99% of both; for search-trajectory data the default Arabic-ratio rule would have rejected 66.3% of documents because their reasoning traces are English.

**Continual pretraining.** Constant learning rate in the main stage (so the last main-stage checkpoint is deliberately un-annealed), then a cooldown that decays to one tenth of peak on curated, largely chat-formatted data closer in shape to SFT. Batch size, LR and weight decay scale with model size; the 0.9B also differs in tokenizer and position encoding.

**Post-training.** SFT (full-parameter, up to 128K sequences with TP/CP parallelism) → FTPO (final token preference optimization, a DPO-style objective with tether terms, early-stopped when chosen-win reaches 0.35, used mainly to kill degenerate repetition) → RLVR over five verifiable domains: instruction following, mathematics, code, i'rab and tashkeel. Rewards are deterministic rule-based verifiers (constraint checks, reference comparison, unit tests), with morphology answer keys expanded via CAMeL Tools and arramooz.

**The mixed-vs-cascade RLVR experiment.** A controlled side study trains one arm on interleaved multi-task RLVR and one on a five-stage cascade (instruction following → tashkeel → i'rab → math → code), matched to within 1% on per-domain exposure, 800 optimizer updates each, same starting checkpoint.

## Results & Highlights

- **Arabic general capabilities (Table 29, audited, generative, high-effort thinking).** Jais-3-7B reaches 85.23% on ArabicMMLU against 84.08% for gemma-4-12B-it and 80.93% for Qwen3.5-9B, and 92.73% on Arabic GSM8K vs 92.57% for gemma-4-12B-it; its predecessor Jais-2-8B-Chat sits at 70.42% ArabicMMLU, so the within-family gain is +14.8 points. At 375B the picture narrows sharply: 89.34% ArabicMMLU vs 89.31% for Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (a tie) and 93.87% for GPT-5.6 Sol, i.e. 12× the parameters of the 32B buys +0.8 points over Jais-3-32B's 88.56%.
- **AraIFBench, the paper's own Arabic instruction-following benchmark.** Jais-3-0.9B scores 91.10% vs 33.10% for Qwen3.5-2B and 37.60% for gemma-4-E2B-it (+53.5 over the best in class); Jais-3-3.7B scores 92.60% vs 53.70% for Qwen3.5-4B. The curve is non-monotonic, however: Jais-3-32B scores 88.70%, *below* the 7B's 91.60% and the 3.7B's 92.60%.
- **Arabic domain knowledge (Table 30).** At 1B and 4B, Jais 3 leads all ten benchmarks by 5–31 and 6–32 points respectively. QIAS-Inheritance is the extreme case: Jais-3-0.9B 61.14% vs 23.05% for tiny-aya-global, and Jais-3-3.7B 83.30% vs 50.67% for Qwen3.5-4B. At 32B the class is genuinely competitive — gemma-4-31B-it leads six of ten and Jais-3-32B wins QIAS-Inheritance (95.88%) and PalmX (94.62%).
- **Agentic, small models (Table 35).** At 3.7B and 7B, Jais 3 beats every open baseline on all ten scored benchmarks. Jais-3-7B: SWE-bench Verified 70.2% vs 50.8% for Qwen3.5-9B (+19.4) and 28.2% for gemma-4-12B-it; GDPval 60.5% vs 19.1% for Qwen3.5-9B; Arabic File-Processing 45.5% where no 7B-class baseline exceeds 0.9%. The paper attributes the file-task margin to 2,180 Arabic file trajectories in SFT and reports that 24–91% of the 15.5–38.2-point Ar-Files gain is already present at the FTPO checkpoint, before any RL.
- **Agentic, large models — where the story reverses.** Jais-3-32B leads Qwen3.8-27B on MCPMark (66.1% vs 49.6%, +16.5) and BrowseComp-Plus (71.9% vs 53.0%), but loses Terminal-Bench 2.1 by 16.1 points (63.7% vs 79.8%), τ³-Bench Banking by 25.7 (22.3% vs 48.0%) and Arabic File-Processing by 6.8 (71.8% vs 78.6%). At 375B, Jais-3-375B-A23B scores 64.1% on Ar-Files against 82.7% for Qwen3.8-Flash-Next and 96.4% for GPT-5.6 Sol — and below its own 32B sibling's 71.8%. Every frontier model beats the 375B on at least eight of ten agentic benchmarks.
- **Factuality is an abstention policy, and the paper shows the bill.** On SimpleQA-Verified-Ar the 375B has a non-hallucination rate of 93.51% (share of non-correct answers where it abstains) vs 2.88% for Qwen3.5-397B, 0.72% for GPT-5.6 Sol and 0.00% for Gemini 3.8 Flash — but its F-score is 5.49 vs 55.16, 26.5–53.0 for open baselines and up to 72.9 for frontier models. The median open baseline abstains on 5% (Arabic) / 4% (English) of questions it gets wrong; Jais 3 on 68–84% (Arabic) / 80–94% (English). With low-effort thinking the non-hallucination rate collapses below 3% at every size.
- **FTPO loop suppression (Table 19).** Mean loop rate over 9,183 generations per checkpoint falls from 13.8% at SFT to 1.2% after FTPO (0.6% after RLVR), a 13× mean reduction; Jais-3-0.9B goes 31.1% → 1.2% (26×) and the 375B MoE 5.4% → 1.9% → 0.8%. Before FTPO, loops concentrate at low temperature (Jais-3-7B: 18.8% at T=0.1 vs 7.7% at T=0.6); after, 1.1–1.9% across all three temperatures.
- **Mixed-task RLVR beats a cascade (Table 23).** In high reasoning the cascade ends 10.29 points below its FTPO start on GSM8K, 11.06 on GSM8K-CoT, 6.45 on Arabic GSM8K and 6.07 on MATH-500, while the mixed arm holds or gains; on AIME 2025 the mixed arm gains +25.42 vs +6.67 for the cascade. On untrained benchmarks the mixed arm's mean movement is +1.23 vs −0.05, and it ends higher on 20 of 25.
- **Benchmark auditing changes conclusions.** The three-judge audit removes 3,634 of 69,168 items (5.3%), from 0.6% of AraDiCE-Culture to 18.2% of MedAraBench; for about half of removed items at least two judges call the gold answer wrong. Removing them raises Jais-3-375B's MedAraBench score by 13.72 points — a reminder that published Arabic leaderboard numbers are partly measuring item defects.
- **English retention is real but uneven.** Jais-3-3.7B hits 91.25% / 90.83% on AIME 2025/2026 vs 67.92% / 79.17% for Qwen3.5-4B (+23.3 / +11.7), and Jais-3-32B reaches 99.17% on AIME 2025, ahead of Gemini 3.8 Flash (97.92%). Knowledge is the weak spot: the 375B trails the best open model by 5.3 points on MMLU-Pro (83.45% vs 88.71% for Qwen3.5-397B) and MultiChallenge gaps widen to ~14 points at 32B and 375B.
- **Safety is good but not best-in-class on refusal.** Jais-3-7B refuses 92.42% of AraSafe harmful prompts vs 95.77% for Fanar-1-9B-Instruct and 95.37% for Qwen3.5-9B, while five of six models exceed 98% on Arabic Regional Safety (Jais-3-32B: 99.07% government / 99.47% opposition perspective) and Jais-3-7B scores 96.00% on HarmBench, tied with Qwen3.5-9B.
- **Retention vs the base model, reported only in prose.** Up to 36B, 21 of 38 agentic scores rise over the starting K2 Horizon checkpoint, 9 tie and 8 fall; RLVR alone raises 34 of 41 scores despite containing no agentic data, and adds 1.7–9.7 points on Terminal-Bench 2.1 at all five sizes.

The report is authored by the **Jais Team at the Institute of Foundation Models (MBZUAI)**, with Cerebras Systems and Inception42 as partners; correspondence goes to Haonan Li, Hector Liu and Eric Xing. Named core contributors cover training (Hadi Abdine, Ahmad Chamma, Amr Mohamed, Ahmed Frikha, Dani Bouch, Omar Arbi, Guokan Shang), data, evaluation, project management, and conception/leadership (Haonan Li, Zhengzhong Liu, Eric Xing).

Credibility signals are unusually strong for a model report. MBZUAI's IFM is the lineage holder of the Jais series (Jais 1, Jais-Adapted, Jais 2), so the Arabic data pipeline is a continuation of work the group has published before, not a one-off. The base model K2 Horizon is theirs and fully open, which means every claim about adaptation starts from a checkpoint anyone can download. Preslav Nakov and Eric Xing are senior, well-known figures. The release list is verifiable: Hugging Face collection, two GitHub repos, a W&B project, and a public collection of discarded benchmark items with judge reasons.

The honest caveats matter for credibility too: the paper states that "we did not ablate the rewards," that "we did not isolate which part of the training produces the behavior" for abstention, that BrowseComp scores are "not strictly comparable" across harnesses, and that the cascade arm was trained once and evaluated only at its endpoint. Those admissions are specific and consequential rather than cosmetic.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The individual ingredients are not new — continual pretraining for language adaptation (Swallow, Jais-Adapted), replay against forgetting, DPO-style loop suppression (Antidoom/Antislop), RLVR. The genuine novelty is compositional and infrastructural: adapting a *post-trained agentic* checkpoint rather than a base model, and doing so at six sizes including a 375B MoE with unchanged tokenizer. The benchmark audit is arguably the more original contribution, and the mixed-vs-cascade RLVR study is a clean piece of training-dynamics science that the paper undersells.

**Rigor — where it is strong.** The data section is the best part of the report. Reporting inventory and exposure separately, naming every source, and documenting filter profiles per source (with the concrete "one shared rule set removed 12% of a dialect corpus vs <1% with per-source rules" example) is more than most model reports do. The three-judge audit with a requirement that all three flag a *significant* issue, plus Arabic-speaker review of a sample of removals, is a sound design. Run-to-run variance is reported (three seeds per Jais model; ~90% of results vary by under 1 point), and the FTPO evaluation set is explicitly disjoint from the mining prompts.

**Rigor — where it is weak.** Four issues are substantive.

1. **The AraIFBench result is partly a training-test coupling artifact.** The RLVR instruction-following data is a synthetic set of "20 Arabic-specific constraint families (21 for the 375B)" and the paper states that "the synthetic set uses the AraIFBench verifiers." Prompt-level overlap is excluded (exact match or Jaccard ≥0.6), but verifier-family overlap is not. A 0.9B model scoring 91.10% on a benchmark where Qwen3.5-2B scores 33.10% and gemma-4-E2B-it 37.60% is not plausible as general instruction following. The non-monotonicity corroborates this: 3.7B 92.60 > 7B 91.60 > 32B 88.70 < 375B 93.90. No ablation holds out an unused constraint family, so the size of the "real" gain is unknown.
2. **The base model is missing from every main table.** The central claim is that adaptation preserves K2 Horizon's abilities, yet Tables 29–40 contain no K2 Horizon row. Retention is reported only as an aggregate in Section 6.2 prose ("21 of the 38 scores rise, 9 tie, 8 fall"), with no per-benchmark numbers and no English-retention table at all. A reader cannot check whether the 375B's 5.3-point MMLU-Pro deficit is inherited from the base or caused by Arabic CPT.
3. **The headline Arabic agentic benchmark is not released.** The Arabic File-Processing Benchmark (220 tasks, 7,356 rubric criteria) is described as "a new internal benchmark" that "has not yet been released publicly," even though Table 41 advertises evaluation code as released. The largest agentic margins in the paper (38.6–44.6 points at 4B/7B) come from this benchmark, and SFT contains 2,180 trajectories explicitly targeting Arabic file work. Format and rubric familiarity are therefore confounded with capability, and no ablation separates them.
4. **Baseline coverage is thinnest exactly where Jais wins biggest.** The 7B-class Ar-Files comparison is against models scoring ≤0.9%; Fanar-1-9B has no HumanEval/MBPP numbers; AA-LCR is unavailable ("–") for nearly every Arabic-centric baseline, so the long-context win (Jais-3-3.7B 68% vs Qwen3.5-4B 53%) is against one competitor. All baselines were re-run by the authors under their own generative protocol with Gemma judges, which is defensible but not neutral.

**Red flags.** The factuality framing deserves scrutiny. "Far fewer hallucinations" in the abstract is, mechanically, a policy that abstains: NH 93.51% with F-score 5.49 at 375B, and NH below 3% at every size under low-effort thinking. The body is admirably explicit ("Abstaining does not add knowledge… Jais 3 answers fewer long-tail questions"), but the abstract does not carry that qualifier. Second, the AIME numbers strain belief: a 3.7B dense model at 91.25% on AIME 2025, above Claude Sonnet 5's 90.00%, while decontamination is lexical n-gram only and the paper concedes that "translated or paraphrased benchmark items are not detected." Third, the 375B being 7.7 points *worse* than its own 32B on Ar-Files and 2.9 points worse on AraIFBench suggests the largest model received a different (or less well-tuned) post-training mixture, and the report never explains the discrepancy. Fourth, "7B scale" includes 9B and 12B baselines and "32B scale" includes a 34B model — the class boundaries are loose enough to flatter in both directions.

**Significance.** High for the Arabic NLP community and for anyone doing language adaptation of an open base: the recipe, the staged checkpoints and the audited benchmarks are reusable infrastructure, and the audit alone will change how Arabic leaderboards are read. Moderate for the broader agentic-model literature, since the agentic evaluation is mostly English and the Arabic-specific agentic claim rests on an unreleased benchmark.

## Strengths

- **Genuinely open, stage by stage.** Releasing CPT, cooldown, SFT, FTPO and RLVR checkpoints for all six sizes lets others ablate any stage; almost no lab does this.
- **The benchmark audit is a first-class contribution.** 69,168 items reviewed, 3,634 removed, 51% of removals traced to wrong gold answers, with concrete examples (ArabicMMLU keying 15 NATO members when there are 32; Arabic GSM8K assuming a 26-letter alphabet and answering 130 instead of 140; a MedAraBench item that is simply corrupted). Releasing the discarded items with three judge verdicts is the right move.
- **Uncommon honesty about costs.** The paper states that abstention lowers F-score, that the cascade arm regresses 6–11 points on short-form math, that the 375B loses to frontier models on eight of ten agentic benchmarks, and that the 32B scale is "more competitive" than the smaller ones. It also lists five limitations of its own RLVR comparison.
- **A clean, well-controlled training-dynamics experiment.** Matching the cascade and mixed arms on per-domain exposure to within 1%, on 800 updates, and on starting checkpoint — then reporting the early-regression trajectory (GSM8K −12.95 by step 30) — is better experimental hygiene than most RL post-training papers manage.
- **FTPO scales further than its sources claimed.** Antidoom was demonstrated at 2.6B/4B dense and Antislop at up to 70B; applying it to a 375B MoE (5.4% → 1.9% loops, 0.8% after RLVR) is a useful data point that the objective survives two orders of magnitude.
- **Data engineering with receipts.** The MinHash false-positive analysis is a gem: the default content key would have deleted 2,177 search-trajectory records at a 99.95% false-positive rate because retrieved Wikipedia passages dominate the hash; keying on question/reasoning/answer/tool calls reduced that to one true removal.

## Limitations

- **Arabic and English only.** Other languages are explicitly out of scope; the 0.9B is excluded from all agentic evaluation and has a 128K rather than 512K context.
- **Lexical-only contamination control.** Both decontamination (8-gram n-grams after orthographic folding) and deduplication (character-shingle MinHash) are surface-form methods; paraphrased or translated leakage survives, which is a real concern for AIME and for the translated reasoning data used in CPT.
- **Judge-mediated evaluation at scale.** Most benchmarks are scored by Gemma judges (gemma-4-26B-A4B-it for English), with no reported human agreement study beyond the audit sample. Judge bias toward verbose Arabic reasoning traces is unmeasured.
- **No compute, cost or energy accounting.** Table 17 gives GPU counts and parallelism degrees, but there is no wall-clock, GPU-hour or cost figure for any run — which matters for the paper's implicit claim that adaptation is cheaper than pretraining.
- **Flat scaling returns at 375B.** ArabicMMLU +0.8 over the 32B, AraIFBench −5.2, Ar-Files −7.7: the flagship model does not justify its size on the paper's own Arabic metrics, and no analysis explains why.
- **Agentic evaluation is 9/11 English.** The paper concedes this "probably does not show its full Arabic agentic capability," which cuts both ways: it also means the Arabic agentic claim is supported by one internal benchmark plus one translated GAIA variant.
- **Safety refusal is mid-pack.** AraSafe refusal (92.11–93.54%) trails Fanar-1-9B and Qwen3.5-9B by ~3 points at 7B; the paper reports this without discussing whether it reflects over-refusal on benign Arabic prompts, which XSTest only partially covers in English.

## Relevance

作为 MBZUAI IFM 的官方系统技术报告，本文落在"Model System Cards & Technical Reports"这一兴趣区：它是少见的、把六个尺寸的每一阶段 checkpoint 与审计后基准全部开源的模型族报告。对做 RL 后训练动力学的人也有直接价值——FTPO 把循环率从 13.8% 压到 1.2%、以及 cascade 与 mixed RLVR 的早期回归（GSM8K −12.95 by step 30）与阶段遗忘（i'rab 阶段把 tashkeel DER 从 9.86 推回 13.16），都是可迁移的训练失效观测，尽管作者自己承认机制"是推断而非测量"。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. What fraction of the AraIFBench gain survives on a constraint family that appears in the benchmark but was deliberately excluded from RLVR training? Without that hold-out, the 0.9B's 91.10% cannot be separated from verifier familiarity.
2. Does the abstention policy hurt agentic performance? A model that says "I don't know" instead of guessing may also refuse to act in a terminal or file-editing episode; the paper reports NH only on closed-book QA.
3. Why is Jais-3-375B-A23B (64.1%) worse than Jais-3-32B (71.8%) on the Arabic File-Processing Benchmark, and 2.9 points worse on AraIFBench? Was the 375B's post-training mixture, data volume or RLVR schedule different, and was this known before release?
4. How much of the 15.5–38.2-point Ar-Files gain is task-format familiarity rather than transferable Arabic document reasoning? A test on held-out occupations or on GDPval-style tasks with Arabic inputs but unseen deliverable types would separate them.
5. Would the mixed-vs-cascade conclusion hold with more than one seed per arm and with the mixed arm evaluated at intermediate checkpoints? The paper's own limitation list flags both gaps, and AIME 2025 already swings 5 points within a single stage.
6. Does the audited benchmark suite change the ranking of *baselines* as much as it changes Jais 3's absolute scores? Table 56 reports the deltas per model, but a rank-correlation analysis before vs after auditing would tell the community how much of the prior Arabic leaderboard literature needs re-reading.
