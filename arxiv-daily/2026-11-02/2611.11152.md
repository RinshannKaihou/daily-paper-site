> **一句话总结：** 作者在 11 个数据集 × 3 个 CLIP 骨干 × 5 个 shot 档的固定协议（30 epoch、lr=1e-4、batch 64、无 warmup/weight decay）下证明"全参微调必然过拟合"是把参数化和优化混为一谈：纯 SGD 全参微调平均 77.9%，逐行挑选最强 PEFT 后仍高出 0.76 点（ViT-B/16 1-shot 为 72.8% vs Adam 10.3%，零样本 64.1%），并把 Adam 的崩溃定位到少样本支撑集把对角二阶矩估小、再被 1/√v 归一化放大成超大更新步（首步更新预算比 SGD 大 81.6–213 倍）；只做一次首步预算对齐，Adam 就从 14.51% 回到 76.03%（SGD 74.93%），说明失效来自优化器标定而非稠密参数化本身。

> **TL;DR:** On 11 few-shot classification datasets (including ImageNet, EuroSAT, DTD, UCF101) with three CLIP backbones and K∈{1,2,4,8,16} shots under one shared recipe (30 epochs, lr 1e-4, batch 64, no warmup/weight decay/scheduler), dense full fine-tuning with plain SGD averages 77.9% and beats the best PEFT baseline chosen separately for each row (77.2%) by +0.76 points, while Adam collapses to 10.3% at 1-shot on ViT-B/16 versus 72.8% for SGD and 64.1% zero-shot. The authors trace the collapse to support-set preconditioning: at the pretrained checkpoint the few-shot diagonal second moment is 0.15–0.72× the full-train statistic while the resulting adaptive direction is ~3× too large, so Adam's first update budget is 81.6× (EuroSAT), 213.0× (Oxford-Pets) and 126.2× (DTD) that of SGD. A single step-0 budget rescaling lifts Adam from 14.51% to 76.03% across six datasets and five shot levels (SGD 74.93%), and from 38.88% to 76.60% on SigLIP (SGD 76.28%). Worth reading for the diagnostic toolkit and the 270-run shot-scaling study, but note the paper's own learning-rate sweep shows Adam at 1e-6 reaches 76.10% versus 74.61% for SGD, so the practical takeaway is "Adam's effective step size is ~100× too large in few-shot," not "SGD is the better optimizer."

## 核心贡献

- **把参数化和优化拆开。** 文献里"全参微调在少样本下必然过拟合"的共识（CLIP-Adapter、CoOp、LoRA-Recycle 等的原话被逐条引用）主要是在 Adam/AdamW 下得到的；换成纯 SGD，稠密全参微调在 11 数据集、3 骨干、5 个 shot 档上全面超过代表性 PEFT（SO、LoRA、PiSSA、SAFT、LP++、CoOp 等 11 种）。
- **定位 Adam 崩溃的机制为"支撑集预条件不可信"。** 少样本对角二阶矩在初始化时就系统性偏小（R_v(0)=0.15–0.72），训练中其范数比梯度范数下降得更快（EuroSAT 视觉/文本分别达 38×/71×），经 1/√v 放大后产生过大更新；把首步更新预算对齐 SGD 即消除大部分崩溃，而只对齐分子方向无法解释失效。
- **排除"更丰富的预条件就能救"这一假设。** Shampoo 与 SOAP 同样失败：Shampoo 出现大尺度畸变，SOAP 虽避开 Adam 式的对角尺度膨胀，但修复后的方向与全数据参考几乎正交（cos 仅 0.002–0.014）。
- **给出 SGD 为何有效的景观证据。** 初始化到终点的插值路径上全训练/测试损失势垒为 0（EuroSAT、Oxford-Pets），近最优区间宽度 0.33–0.34；种子间插值同样零势垒（相对端点距离 1.05–1.20）；SGD 方向的全损失曲率 1317，远低于全梯度方向的 9158 和最陡 Hessian 方向的 18634（EuroSAT，t=30）。
- **块级与时间维度的安全预算分析。** 安全更新预算按编码器、深度、训练阶段结构化分布：早期多数块可用，后期部分块变成支撑集专用；EuroSAT 视觉块 3–5 持续安全，DTD 的相位变化最剧烈。
- **理论侧给出四类命题。** 少样本对角估计量的有限总体方差（Prop. 1）、1/√· 预条件的偏差放大（Prop. 2）、局部安全步长阈值（Prop. 3）、以及"尺度膨胀足以毁掉一个好方向"（Prop. 4）。
- **一个便宜的实践结论。** SGD 无优化器状态：ViT-B/16 全参微调计数内存 936 MiB vs Adam 1872 MiB，墙钟时间 20 vs 21 分钟（SUN397 16-shot），即收益不是靠更贵的优化器换来的。

## Key Contributions

- **Decouples parameterization from optimization.** The paper shows that the standard claim "full fine-tuning overfits in the few-shot regime" was established almost entirely with Adam/AdamW, and that dense full fine-tuning with plain SGD outperforms 11 representative PEFT methods across 11 datasets, 3 backbones and 5 shot levels under a single fixed protocol.
- **Identifies unreliable support-set preconditioning as the failure mechanism.** The few-shot diagonal second moment is already miscalibrated at the pretrained checkpoint, degrades faster than the gradient norm during support fitting, and is converted by Adam's inverse-square-root normalization into an oversized adaptive update. Matching only the first-step update budget to SGD removes most of the collapse; matching the numerator does not.
- **Shows richer preconditioning does not help.** Shampoo and SOAP fail too, so the problem is not specific to Adam's diagonal form: it is the mismatch between a support-set preconditioning statistic and the full-data objective.
- **Explains SGD's success with landscape probes.** Zero measured interpolation barriers along init-to-final and seed-to-seed segments on the tested directions, broad near-minimum spans, lower directional curvature along the SGD step than along the full-gradient and dominant-Hessian directions, and block/time-structured safe update budgets.
- **Formalizes the scale argument.** Propositions give the finite-population error of the diagonal estimator, the inverse-square-root bias, a local safe-step radius under a curvature bound, and a construction where an inflated step along an otherwise good direction provably increases the full-train loss.
- **Releases code** at https://github.com/nairouz/few-shot-clip-sgd.

## Methodology

**Protocol.** Eleven datasets (ImageNet, SUN397, FGVC-Aircraft, EuroSAT, Stanford-Cars, Food101, Oxford-Pets, Flowers102, Caltech101, DTD, UCF101), three CLIP backbones (ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14), K ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16} shots, 3 fixed seeds, 30 epochs, batch 64, lr 1e-4, no weight decay, no warmup, no scheduler, no gradient accumulation. Trainable parameters are the attention and MLP weight matrices of all 12 Transformer blocks in both encoders; biases, normalization parameters, embeddings, projections and the logit scale stay frozen. Every method uses fixed hyperparameters across datasets, backbones, shot levels and seeds (Appendix E).

**Optimizer comparison.** SGD (no momentum), SMD (SGD with momentum, β1=0.9), Adam, and AdamW at three weight-decay values; Shampoo (refresh F=1) and SOAP (F=10) as structured preconditioners. The Adam collapse is probed with a controlled family of diagnostics on CLIP ViT-B/16, 1-shot, on EuroSAT, Oxford-Pets and DTD, where the support set is a single batch so one optimizer step equals one pass over the support set.

**Diagnostic quantities.** For each encoder e, the paper defines encoder-restricted mean gradients m and diagonal second moments v on the support split (D_few) and the full training split (D_full); the ratios R_v(t)=‖v_few,t‖/‖v_full,t‖ and R_m(t)=‖m_few,t‖/‖m_full,t‖; collapse factors C_v=R_v(0)/R_v(T) and C_m=R_m(0)/R_m(T); the global relative update budget r_o(t)=‖ΔΘ_t‖/‖Θ_t‖; and three Adam-like directions u_ff (few-shot numerator and denominator), u_fr (few-shot numerator, full-train denominator) and u_rr (both full-train), whose cosines isolate numerator from denominator error.

**Causal interventions.** (i) Adam-M0 rescales the learning rate once at step 0 so that r_Adam(0)=r_SGD(0), keeping Adam's moment recursion and adaptive direction; (ii) Adam-Mt repeats this matching at every step; (iii) learning-rate sweeps (1e-2 to 1e-7) and warmup schedules (5%/20% warmup with constant or cosine decay) test whether conventional scheduling reproduces the effect; (iv) the PEFT ablation in Appendix J retrains all 11 PEFT methods with both Adam and SGD so the parameterization and optimizer effects can be separated.

**Landscape probes (1-shot, ViT-B/16, three datasets).** Linear interpolation Θ(α)=(1−α)Θ_0+αΘ_T with barrier B_S = max_α φ_S(α) − chord and near-minimum width at δ_w=0.05; seed-to-seed interpolation between independently trained SGD solutions; directional curvature κ_S(u;t)=uᵀH_S(Θ_t)u along the normalized SGD step versus the full-train gradient, the top Hessian eigendirection and a random direction; blockwise line probes over α∈[−2,2] for each of the 12 blocks per encoder to measure the full-loss change and the support–full mismatch Γ.

**Theory.** Appendix L proves the finite-population variance of the few-shot diagonal estimator (Prop. 1), the inverse-square-root amplification of that error (Prop. 2), a curvature-bounded local step-length threshold (Prop. 3), the "inflated scale defeats a good direction" construction (Prop. 4), an exact decomposition of denominator repair (Prop. 5), a descent guarantee before numerator staleness dominates (Prop. 6), and blockwise-versus-global mismatch results (Props. 7–8).

## Results & Highlights

- **The optimizer gap is enormous under the shared recipe.** On ViT-B/16 at 1 shot, plain SGD reaches 72.8% average over 11 datasets versus 10.3% for Adam and 7.2–8.6% for the three AdamW weight-decay settings, while zero-shot CLIP is 64.1% — Adam is 53.8 points below zero-shot, SGD is 8.7 points above it. At 16 shots the same table gives SGD 83.0% versus Adam 31.1%, a 51.9-point gap that persists at every shot level and on all three backbones (ViT-B/32: 67.7% vs 8.8% at 1 shot; ViT-L/14: 78.3% vs 18.1%).
- **Dense SGD beats every PEFT family on the averaged rows.** Across the 15 backbone–shot settings, SGD averages 77.9% and the best PEFT method selected separately for each row averages 77.2%, a mean margin of +0.76 points. Family-level margins are +3.9 points against the best prompt-learning baseline, +4.0 against the best adapter/cache/classifier-side baseline, and only +0.9 against the best low-rank/sparse baseline (PiSSA or SO in most rows).
- **The margin survives giving every PEFT baseline both optimizers.** Appendix J retrains all 11 PEFT methods with Adam and with SGD: dense SGD still wins all 60 backbone–family–shot comparisons, by +2.57 points on average after picking the stronger optimizer per row (minimum margin +0.4 points). Switching LP++ from Adam to SGD alone gains 25.7 points on average (LP++ at 1-shot ViT-B/32 is 21.5% with Adam but 62.5% with SGD), which is a self-critical result since LP++ is the authors' own prior method.
- **The first-step budget is two orders of magnitude too large.** At step 0 Adam's global relative update budget is r_Adam(0)=2.62×10⁻³ on all three diagnostic datasets, versus r_SGD(0)=3.21×10⁻⁵ (EuroSAT), 1.23×10⁻⁵ (Oxford-Pets) and 2.08×10⁻⁵ (DTD) — ratios of 81.6×, 213.0× and 126.2×. Matching this single scalar changes the final full-train loss from 2.300/3.640/6.317 (plain Adam) to 1.185/0.336/1.800 (Adam-M), essentially matching SGD's 1.027/0.350/1.824 on Oxford-Pets and DTD.
- **Budget matching recovers Adam's accuracy across shots and model families.** Over six datasets, five shot levels and three seeds, Adam-M0 reaches 76.03% versus 14.51% for raw Adam and 74.93% for SGD (recovery 101.82%, 95% CI [100.54, 103.43]); at K=1 specifically it is 69.28% versus 6.30% for Adam and 66.37% for SGD. On SigLIP (four datasets, three shot levels) the numbers are 76.60% / 38.88% / 76.28%, and the raw first-update Adam/SGD ratio there ranges from 70.25× to 400.77×.
- **A conventional learning-rate fix does the same thing, and this is the paper's most important caveat.** In the same 30-epoch protocol, Adam at 1e-4 scores 15.04%, at 1e-5 68.39%, at 1e-6 76.10% and at 3e-6 74.25%, versus 74.61% for SGD at 1e-4; 5% warmup plus cosine decay reaches 76.28%. So a ~100× smaller Adam step, or warmup, matches or beats SGD.
- **Over long horizons SGD is the more stable optimizer, not the more accurate one.** At one shot on six datasets, Adam at 1e-6 peaks at 80.88% and ends at 69.95% (mean peak-to-last drop 10.93 points, all 18 runs dropping more than 1 point); SGD at 1e-4 peaks at 80.60% and ends at 73.95% (drop 6.65); SGD at 1e-5 ends at 80.05% with a drop of only 1.02, while Adam at 1e-5 drops 30.21 points.
- **The second-moment miscalibration is measurable at initialization.** R_v(0) is 0.68/0.72 (EuroSAT vision/text), 0.29/0.30 (Oxford-Pets) and 0.15/0.27 (DTD), while the induced adaptive-norm ratio R_u,0 is 3.0–3.3 in all six dataset–encoder pairs. The text encoder is mostly a denominator problem (numerator cosine with the full-data reference 0.488–0.755) whereas vision already has numerator mismatch (0.163–0.276). The support statistic still finds the right coordinates — top-k overlap with the full-data second moment is 0.337–0.542 at density 0.01 — so the error is calibration, not support identification.
- **Structured preconditioners fail differently but fail.** Shampoo's final scale ratio spans 7.5–43× with repaired-direction cosines of −0.255 to 0.060; SOAP's scale is closer to 1 (0.34–2.8) but its repaired direction cosine is only 0.002–0.014, i.e. nearly orthogonal to the full-data reference under the shared full-data preconditioner.
- **SGD's landscape is benign along the probed directions.** Interpolation barriers are 0 for full-train and test loss on EuroSAT and Oxford-Pets, with minima at α*=0.85–0.87 and near-minimum widths 0.33–0.34; on DTD the optima come earlier (α*_full=0.76, α*_test=0.70) and a late support-specialized tail appears. At t=30 on EuroSAT the full-loss directional curvatures are 1317 along the SGD step, 9158 along the full-train gradient and 18634 along the top Hessian eigendirection.
- **SGD's support gradient is stable in time but nearly orthogonal to the full-data gradient.** Its self-alignment is 0.973/0.966/0.941 at lags 1/2/5 on EuroSAT vision, yet its alignment with the future full-train gradient averages only 0.046/0.042/0.035 in vision and 0.016/0.006/−0.016 in text (negative at every lag on DTD text). The SGD direction barely tracks the full-data objective at all.
- **Cost.** Counted optimization memory for ViT-B/16 full fine-tuning is 936 MiB for SGD versus 1872 MiB for Adam (LoRA r=2 is 475.5 MiB); wall-clock on SUN397 16-shot is 20 min for SGD, 21 for Adam, 19 for LoRA, 26 for SO, and 1 h 18 min for CoOp.

## Authors & Credibility

Nairouz Mrabah, Ismail Ben Ayed and Eric Granger are at LIVIA/ILLS, ETS Montreal. Ben Ayed and Granger lead a group with a long, well-cited record in few-shot learning, medical image segmentation and CLIP adaptation; LP++, one of the PEFT baselines here, is their own CVPR 2024 method, and the paper reports it collapsing to 21.5% at 1-shot with Adam before SGD rescues it to 62.5%. That is a genuinely self-critical disclosure rather than a favorable-to-own-work setup. The paper is accepted at NeurIPS 2026, runs 135 pages with 12 appendices, uses three seeds throughout, reports dataset-cluster bootstrap intervals for the headline interventions, and releases code. The writing is unusually careful about scope ("under the reported evaluation protocol" appears in the abstract itself), which makes the places where the framing outruns the evidence easier to identify rather than harder.

## Critical Assessment

**The strongest experiment undercuts the title.** Table 69 in Appendix N.5 shows Adam at 1e-6 reaching 76.10% versus 74.61% for SGD at 1e-4, and 5% warmup plus cosine reaching 76.28%; Table 63 shows Adam-M0 at 76.03% versus 74.93% for SGD, and 69.28% versus 66.37% at 1-shot. So once Adam's effective step size is fixed by *any* of three independent means (lower LR, warmup, or one scalar budget match), Adam is not worse than SGD and is nominally better at low shots. "Why Adam fails and SGD succeeds" is therefore a statement about a single nominal learning rate, and the paper's own rescue experiments remove the optimizer ranking it headlines. The defensible claim is narrower and still interesting: Adam's default effective step size is roughly two orders of magnitude too large in few-shot full fine-tuning, and the reason is diagnosable.

**The second-moment mechanism does not explain the high-shot regime.** Figure 4(a) reports R_v(0)<1 in all six dataset–encoder pairs (0.15–0.72), but Table 65, using matched class-balanced diagnostic batches of size 16, reports R_v(0)=1.256 at K=1, 0.995 at K=8 and 1.037 at K=16 — the sign of the effect flips with the diagnostic protocol, which the paper notes but does not reconcile. More damaging: at K=16 the denominator ratio is essentially calibrated (1.037) yet Adam still trails SGD by 52.2 points (29.98% vs 82.23% in Table 63). If the denominator is fine at 16 shots, "the support set cannot calibrate the second moment" cannot be the operative cause there; what remains is the generic fact that Adam's normalized update has norm ≈η√d regardless of gradient magnitude, which at d≈1.2×10⁸ and η=1e-4 is enormous relative to ‖Θ‖. The paper never tests whether the same 100× first-step gap appears with full-data Adam at 1e-4, which is the obvious control for separating "few-shot miscalibration" from "Adam at this LR on this model size."

**The PEFT baselines are not tuned to their published recipes.** All PEFT methods use Adam at 1e-4 with 30 epochs, rank r=2 for LoRA/PiSSA, and no per-method learning-rate search; there is no comparison against the numbers reported in the original PEFT papers, and no statement that these baselines were reproduced. LoRA at rank 2 with Adam 1e-4 is far from the standard CLIP-adaptation recipe (higher LR, often higher rank), and prompt methods are given 200 epochs while dense fine-tuning gets 30, so the schedule budget is not comparable across families. Appendix J's both-optimizer ablation is the right response and does shrink the gap, but the residual margin against the strongest family is +0.87 points on average, and the smallest cell is +0.4 points at ViT-L/14 16-shot — inside the ±0.2–0.5 seed standard deviations reported in Tables 48–50. The claim "SGD beats every PEFT method in every averaged row" is technically true but is a sub-noise effect in the high-shot, large-backbone corner.

**The landscape evidence is thinner than the framing suggests.** All probes are 1-shot, ViT-B/16, three datasets, one trajectory per dataset; the "zero barrier" result is measured on a single straight line in a 10⁸-dimensional space, which the authors correctly flag as a directional probe but then use to argue for "broad low-loss corridors." The headline curvature numbers come from a single checkpoint (t=30) on a single dataset (EuroSAT). And the temporal-alignment result in Appendix K.8 sits awkwardly with the geometric story: SGD's support gradient is almost time-invariant (cos 0.97) yet nearly orthogonal to the full-train gradient (0.046 in vision, ~0 in text, negative on DTD text). That is more consistent with "the step is so small that direction barely matters" than with "SGD follows a benign low-curvature descent direction," and the two readings imply different follow-up work.

**The theory is a local Taylor argument, not a convergence or generalization result.** Propositions 1–2 are a finite-population variance calculation plus a convexity argument for 1/√·; Propositions 3–4 assume curvature bounds λ_low ≤ κ ≤ λ_up on a segment and conclude that a step exceeding 2a‖m‖/λ_low increases the loss. These are correct and useful for interpreting the diagnostics, but they assume rather than derive the curvature bounds, say nothing about generalization, and the abstract's "theory formalizes how few-shot estimation error is amplified into unsafe updates" is a fair description only if "formalizes" is read as "writes down the plug-in bias."

**Scope is classification-only and CLIP-family-only.** No detection, segmentation, retrieval, or distribution-shift evaluation (ImageNet-V2/R/A, ObjectNet); ViT-L/14 is the largest model; the SigLIP replication covers 4 datasets × 3 shot levels. Whether dense SGD's advantage is a property of 11-dataset few-shot classification averages or of vision-language adaptation generally is untested.

**What is genuinely good.** The 270-run shot-scaling study, the AdamW weight-decay sweep, the Shampoo/SOAP diagnostics, the both-optimizer PEFT ablation, the SigLIP replication and the dataset-cluster bootstrap intervals together make this one of the more thoroughly controlled optimizer-comparison papers in the CLIP-adaptation literature. The single-scalar intervention is a clean causal design, the diagnostics are reusable, and the memory accounting (936 vs 1872 MiB) is a real practical argument that does not depend on the accuracy claims.

## Strengths

- The diagnostic decomposition (few-shot numerator vs few-shot denominator vs full-train reference, at both encoder and block level) is reusable and more informative than a single accuracy table.
- One-scalar causal intervention (step-0 budget match) produces a 61.5-point accuracy swing, which is about as clean as causal evidence gets in this literature.
- Exceptionally thorough controls for a claim of this kind: AdamW at three weight decays, two structured preconditioners, 11 PEFT methods each under two optimizers, learning-rate and warmup sweeps, long-horizon trajectories, and a second model family (SigLIP).
- Honest reporting of negative and awkward results: SGD does not win every dataset cell; LP++ (the authors' own method) collapses under Adam; the support gradient under SGD is nearly orthogonal to the full-data gradient; global budget matching does not imply blockwise matching.
- Practical and cheap: plain SGD removes two dense moment buffers (936 vs 1872 MiB counted) at the same wall-clock cost, and the paper separates this memory argument from the accuracy argument.
- Code released, three seeds everywhere, confidence intervals clustered by dataset rather than by run.

## Limitations

- All conclusions are conditional on one protocol (30 epochs, lr 1e-4, batch 64, no weight decay, warmup or scheduler). The paper says so, but the abstract's "Adam/AdamW collapses" reads as a general claim.
- The paper does not run the control that would separate few-shot-specific miscalibration from Adam's generic η√d step scaling at this model size and learning rate.
- The second-moment diagnostic is protocol-dependent (R_v(0)=0.15–0.72 in Fig. 4(a) versus 1.256 at K=1 in Table 65), and the mechanism is least convincing exactly where the accuracy gap is largest in absolute terms (K=16).
- PEFT baselines use untuned, shared hyperparameters (rank 2, Adam 1e-4, 30 epochs) and are never checked against their published numbers.
- Landscape probes cover 1-shot, ViT-B/16 and three datasets, on straight-line directions only; the "low-loss corridor" reading is not distinguished from a "tiny step" reading.
- Theory assumes the curvature bounds it needs and makes no generalization claim.
- Evaluation is classification-only; no distribution shift, retrieval, dense prediction, or model larger than ViT-L/14.
- Late support specialization on DTD is acknowledged but not solved: 1-shot DTD accuracy is 53.7% for SGD, and the paper's own blockwise probes show some blocks becoming support-specific late in training.

## Relevance

如果你在做 CLIP/VLM 的少样本适配或 PEFT 选型，这篇给出一个便宜又反直觉的基线（全参 + 纯 SGD，无优化器状态、同墙钟时间）以及一套可复用的"优化器统计量是否可信"诊断方法，值得作为对照实验的必查项。更实用的启示不是"换 SGD"，而是"先检查有效步长"：论文自己的数据显示把 Adam 的学习率降两个数量级就能追平，所以真正的结论是少样本全参微调对步长极端敏感，任何跨优化器的比较都必须先做尺度对齐。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. What is r_Adam(0)/r_SGD(0) for **full-data** Adam at lr 1e-4 on the same 122M-parameter model? If that ratio is also 10–100×, the "support set cannot calibrate the preconditioner" story is secondary to the generic η√d scaling of Adam's normalized update, and the few-shot framing needs to be re-scoped.
2. Why does Adam at 1e-6 still lose 10.93 points from its peak over 300 epochs while SGD at 1e-5 loses 1.02? Is the continued second-moment shrinkage (C_v/C_m≈4.2 at K=1, 2.4 at K=16) the driver, and does a denominator-only repair applied *during* training (not just at step 0) arrest the drift?
3. Proposition 7 says global matching does not imply blockwise matching, and the blockwise probes identify persistently safe blocks (EuroSAT vision blocks 3–5). Does a blockwise or depth-aware budget schedule beat both global Adam-M0 and SGD, or does the block structure wash out?
4. Are the conclusions stable under weight decay, cosine schedules, gradient accumulation over the support set, and batch sizes above 64? The entire collapse lives at one (lr, batch, horizon) point.
5. Does dense SGD still win on ImageNet-V2/R/A, ObjectNet, retrieval and segmentation, or is the +0.76-point average a property of 11 clean classification benchmarks?
6. Is the +0.4-point margin over PiSSA at ViT-L/14 16-shot robust to more than three seeds? With per-cell standard deviations of 0.2–0.5, that cell cannot currently support a "beats all PEFT" claim.
7. Could the diagnostic be turned into a practical rule, e.g. compute R_v(0) and the first-step budget ratio on a handful of support batches and set the Adam learning rate from them, rather than running a full 30-epoch sweep?
