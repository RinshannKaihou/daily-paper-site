> **一句话总结：** 在 Pythia 160M/410M/1B 三种规模、五档精度（FP32/FP16/INT8/NF4/FP4）和两种独立量化算法（bitsandbytes 与自实现 RTN）下直接测量逐字提取（verbatim extraction），发现量化确实比损失通用能力更快地"遗忘"记忆内容（selectivity 最高达 8 倍），但在 1B 模型上四比特量化仍能保留约 72% 的已记忆序列、仅损失约 4% 的困惑度性能，说明量化并不能作为隐私防护手段，且这一"记忆残留比例"随模型规模增大而上升。

> **TL;DR:** Using Pythia's official memorized-sequence ground truth and greedy verbatim extraction on WikiText-2/Pile-measured perplexity as a capability control, this paper shows quantization is a "selective forgetter" (memorization decays faster than perplexity, selectivity ratio up to ~8x at 1B) but not a privacy defense: at Pythia-1B, NF4 4-bit quantization retains 71.8% of memorized-sequence extraction (m=0.718) relative to the FP16 baseline (83.0% → 59.6% raw extraction rate) while keeping 95.9% of Pile perplexity capability — and this surviving fraction actually grows with model size (0.39 at 160M, 0.27 at 410M, 0.72 at 1B), contradicting the optimistic reading of prior membership-inference-based quantization-privacy papers (e.g., Haque et al. 2025 on code models, Zhang et al. 2025a on image classifiers).

## 核心贡献

- 提出应直接测量"逐字提取"（verbatim extraction）而非成员推断（membership inference）来评估量化对隐私风险的真实影响，因为前者才是能引发法律与舆论后果的风险。
- 使用 Pythia 官方公开的"已记忆序列"真值集合（而非猜测哪些数据被记住），在 160M/410M/1B 三种规模、FP32→FP16→INT8→NF4/FP4 五档精度上系统测量提取率与困惑度。
- 定义了一个无量纲的"选择性"（selectivity）指标 s(q) = log m(q) / log c(q)，用于比较记忆保留与能力保留的相对衰减速度，可跨精度、跨模型规模比较。
- 用两种算法上完全无关的量化方法（bitsandbytes 的 NF4/FP4 与作者自写的、无外部依赖的 group-wise round-to-nearest 量化器）互相验证，证明"选择性遗忘"现象不是某个具体量化库的实现假象。
- 发现并强调一个与"量化=隐私红利"直觉相反的规模趋势：四比特量化后记忆残留比例随模型增大而上升（0.39→0.27→0.72），并公开全部代码、采样评测数据与逐配置结果。

## Key Contributions

- Argues that verbatim extraction, not membership inference, is the metric that matters for real-world privacy risk from quantized LLMs, since MI only reveals set membership while extraction reproduces the actual (potentially private/copyrighted) text.
- Uses Pythia's public ground-truth memorized-sequence set (Biderman et al., 2023b) rather than inferring memorization indirectly, evaluating verbatim extraction and perplexity jointly across 5 precision levels (FP32, FP16, INT8, NF4, FP4) and 3 model sizes (160M, 410M, 1B).
- Introduces a scale-free "selectivity" ratio s(q) = log m(q) / log c(q) that lets memorization decay and capability decay be compared on the same footing across precisions and model sizes.
- Cross-validates the main finding with a second, independently implemented quantizer (dependency-free group-wise round-to-nearest, RTN) at matching bit-widths, showing the selective-forgetting pattern is not an artifact of bitsandbytes' NF4/FP4 implementation.
- Identifies a scale trend working against the optimistic privacy narrative: the fraction of memorized data surviving 4-bit quantization increases with model size, suggesting the effect will be worse, not better, at real deployment scale (models one to two orders of magnitude larger than the 1B ceiling studied here).

## Methodology

The protocol (Figure 1) is deterministic given a seed and fully released. The author uses the deduplicated Pythia model suite at 160M, 410M, and 1B parameters, paired with each model's officially published set of memorized sequences: 64-token passages from the Pile that the full-precision model is known to reproduce greedily. A fixed random subset is sampled and cached per model so every precision level is evaluated on identical sequences.

Each 64-token sequence is split into a 32-token prompt and a 32-token target. The model greedily decodes 32 tokens (no sampling) from the prompt, and an exact token-for-token match with the target counts as successful extraction — the primary metric is this exact-match rate, following the standard "discoverable extraction" setup from Carlini et al. (2023). Partial-credit metrics (mean correct-prefix length, per-token accuracy) are also recorded to distinguish all-or-nothing forgetting from gradual drift.

Capability is measured in parallel via sliding-window perplexity on two corpora: WikiText-2 (out-of-distribution) and a sample of Pile text (in-distribution), guarding against a capability measure that is flattered or punished by distribution mismatch. The precision ladder is FP32, FP16, INT8 (bitsandbytes LLM.int8()), and two 4-bit bitsandbytes types (NF4, FP4, as in QLoRA). To rule out library-specific artifacts, the author additionally implements group-wise symmetric round-to-nearest (RTN) quantization at 8- and 4-bit directly on linear-layer weights, with no external dependency — algorithmically unrelated to bitsandbytes' NF4 scheme.

Selectivity is computed relative to a full-precision reference (FP32 for 160M/410M; FP16 for 1B, since FP32 didn't fit on the author's GPU) as m(q) = extraction(q)/extraction(ref), c(q) = perplexity(ref)/perplexity(q), and s(q) = log m(q) / log c(q). s > 1 means memorization decays faster than capability; s ≈ 1 means they decay together; s < 1 means capability degrades first.

## Results & Highlights

- **Selective forgetting at every scale (Table 1):** at every precision and model size, quantized configurations fall below the "proportional decay" diagonal in the capability–memorization plane (Figure 3) — every point lost more memorization than capability. Selectivity stays above 1 throughout, e.g., 1.7–2.7 at 160M, 2.1–2.6 at 410M, and up to 8.0 at 1B (NF4).
- **Concrete quantitative claims (dataset: Pythia memorized-evals set derived from the Pile; capability benchmark: Pile/WikiText-2 perplexity):**
  1. At **Pythia-1B**, NF4 4-bit quantization yields an extraction rate of **0.596** vs. the FP16 baseline's **0.830** (m = 0.718, i.e., 71.8% of memorized sequences retained) while retaining **95.9%** of Pile-perplexity capability (perplexity 7.90 vs. 7.58) — selectivity ≈ 8.0.
  2. At **Pythia-160M**, NF4 extraction drops to **0.294** from the FP32 baseline's **0.756** (m = 0.389), with WikiText-2 perplexity rising from 25.60 to 38.39 and Pile perplexity from 12.27 to 17.33 (c = 0.708) — selectivity ≈ 2.7.
  3. At **Pythia-410M**, the independently-implemented RTN4 quantizer gives extraction **0.176** vs. bitsandbytes NF4's **0.228** (both far below the FP32 baseline's 0.834), confirming the selective-forgetting pattern is not specific to bitsandbytes; at 1B, RTN4 extraction is 0.498 vs. NF4's 0.596, both vs. FP16 baseline 0.830.
  4. Mean correct-prefix length (partial-match metric) collapses from ~28/32 tokens at full precision to ~12/32 at NF4 (410M), showing quantization degrades faithful reproduction depth, not just flips a few borderline sequences.
- **The scale trend that undercuts the "privacy bonus" story:** the fraction of memorized data surviving 4-bit (NF4) quantization is **0.39 at 160M, 0.27 at 410M, and 0.72 at 1B** — non-monotonic across the two smaller models but sharply higher at the largest model studied, read by the author as evidence that larger models "absorb quantization noise" well enough to preserve memorized content nearly intact even as capability barely moves.
- Effects are stable down to 8-bit (INT8 retains 97–100% of both extraction and capability across all sizes) and only become dramatic at 4-bit, consistent with prior work identifying 4-bit as the accuracy-per-bit sweet spot for deployment (Dettmers & Zettlemoyer, 2023).

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core idea — measuring verbatim extraction rather than membership inference under quantization — is a genuinely useful reframing, and using Pythia's official ground-truth memorized set (rather than inferring memorization) is a methodologically sound choice that several cited quantization-privacy papers do not make. The cross-validation with an independently implemented RTN quantizer is a nice, cheap robustness check that many single-technique quantization studies skip.

**Rigor:** The comparison the paper leans on rhetorically — that prior membership-inference studies (Haque et al. 2025 on code models, Zhang et al. 2025a on image classifiers, Li et al. 2026/CompLeak) give an "optimistic" reading of quantization-and-privacy — is an *inter-study* contrast across different domains (code, images) and different models, not a controlled intra-study comparison of MI vs. extraction on the same Pythia checkpoints under the same quantization. The paper's title argument (extraction tells a different story than MI) would be considerably stronger with a direct MI experiment on the same Pythia models alongside the extraction numbers already collected — this is a missing ablation the author doesn't address, apart from citing it as future work implicitly.

**Reproducibility:** high — deterministic seeded runs, cached identical sequences across precision levels, code and data publicly released. This is one of the paper's clear strengths.

**Significance:** the practical warning (don't count on quantization to scrub memorized data) is well supported at the scales tested, but the paper's own headline claim — "the surviving fraction grows with model size" — rests on only three data points (160M, 410M, 1B), with a non-monotonic dip at 410M (0.27) below 160M (0.39) before jumping to 0.72 at 1B. Extrapolating a "growing with scale" trend from three points, one of which breaks monotonicity, to production models "one to two orders of magnitude larger" is a real leap the author acknowledges as an "extrapolation, not a measurement," but the framing in the abstract and conclusion ("grows with model size") reads more confident than three noisy points warrant.

**Red flags / data quirks:** the full-precision reference is inconsistent across model sizes — FP32 for 160M/410M but FP16 for 1B (because FP32 didn't fit on the author's GPU) — meaning the 1B selectivity numbers are computed against a slightly lower reference bar than the smaller models', which could inflate the apparent m/s values at 1B relative to a true FP32 baseline. Table 1 also reports no error bars or confidence intervals despite using "a fixed random subset" of the memorized-sequence set per model, so the exact sample sizes and statistical noise in the extraction-rate estimates are not disclosed.

## Strengths

- Ground-truth-based measurement (official Pythia memorized-sequence list) instead of an ad hoc proxy for memorization.
- Two independent quantization implementations (bitsandbytes vs. self-written RTN) that agree qualitatively, strengthening the claim that selective forgetting is a real property of quantization rather than a library artifact.
- Dual capability corpora (in- and out-of-distribution) guard against a perplexity measure that's simply flattered by domain match.
- Clean, interpretable, scale-free selectivity metric that ties the two main findings together.
- Full code and data release, enabling exact replication and extension to larger models.

## Limitations

- Single model family (Pythia) — explicitly acknowledged; whether the pattern transfers to LLaMA/Mistral/Qwen-style architectures and their training data is untested.
- Largest model is only 1B parameters, far below deployment scale (real models are cited as "one to two orders of magnitude larger"); the headline "grows with model size" claim is an extrapolation from three points, not a measured trend at scale.
- Only strict greedy, all-or-nothing verbatim extraction is studied; probabilistic/discoverable extraction (Hayes et al., 2024), sampling-based decoding, activation quantization, and quantization-aware training are all left untested and explicitly flagged as future work.
- Inconsistent full-precision reference point across model sizes (FP32 vs. FP16 for the 1B model) introduces a subtle asymmetry in the selectivity/retention computations.
- No statistical uncertainty (confidence intervals, variance across seeds) reported for the extraction-rate and perplexity numbers in Table 1.
- English-only, weight-only post-training quantization; the paper only measures how quantization affects *already-memorized* content, not what a model quantized before memorization is assessed would do.

## Relevance

该研究直接关联量化压缩与训练数据隐私风险评估，对使用 Pythia/类 GPT 架构模型做低比特部署（INT4/NF4）并关心记忆泄露、版权文本复现风险的场景有参考价值；其"选择性遗忘但残留比例随规模上升"的结论提示在评估大模型量化方案的隐私声明时，应优先看逐字提取率而非仅看成员推断攻击成功率。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would a direct, controlled comparison of membership-inference success vs. verbatim-extraction rate on the *same* Pythia checkpoints and *same* quantization settings confirm that MI and extraction genuinely diverge, rather than relying on cross-domain literature comparisons?
- Does the "surviving fraction grows with model size" trend hold up with a fourth or fifth Pythia size point (e.g., 2.8B, 6.9B) to resolve the non-monotonic dip seen between 160M and 410M?
- How does the selective-forgetting effect interact with quantization-aware training or activation quantization (e.g., SmoothQuant, AWQ), which the paper explicitly leaves untested?
- Would probabilistic/discoverable extraction (sampling-based, per Hayes et al. 2024) rather than strict greedy exact-match show a different — possibly worse — quantization sensitivity profile?
- Is there an interpretability story (e.g., weight-magnitude or activation-outlier analysis) that localizes *where* memorized sequences are encoded, to explain why they are more precision-fragile than general capability, and why that fragility appears to lessen with scale?
