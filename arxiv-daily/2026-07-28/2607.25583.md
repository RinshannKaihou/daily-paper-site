> **一句话总结：** 在60M参数的T5-small上对WikiSQL文本转SQL任务做LoRA秩、适配模块与量化精度的单变量受控消融，发现r=16是准确率饱和点（59.6% vs 全量微调71.2% exact-match，仅用不到1%参数、节省31%显存），继续增大秩或扩展适配模块收益甚微，而QLoRA的INT8/NF4量化在显存降到0.60GB的情况下精度仅损失约0.2-0.6个百分点。

> **TL;DR:** A controlled, single-variable ablation of LoRA rank {2,4,8,16,32}, target-module choice, and quantization (FP16/INT8/NF4) on T5-small (60M params) fine-tuned on WikiSQL text-to-SQL, showing LoRA r=16 on {q,v} is Pareto-optimal (59.6% vs. full fine-tuning's 71.2% exact-match, <1% trainable parameters, 31% less peak memory), rank gains plateau sharply beyond r=16, and QLoRA NF4 trades ~0.2pt of accuracy for a 62-74% memory reduction (0.60GB vs. 1.59-2.31GB).

## 核心贡献

- 在一个完全可复现的60M参数编码器-解码器模型（T5-small）+ 单表text-to-SQL基准（WikiSQL）上，对LoRA的秩、适配模块集合、数值精度做逐一变量的受控消融研究（single-variable ablation），而非像大多数LoRA/QLoRA研究那样只在十亿参数级模型上做探索。
- 将任务准确率与四项系统级指标（可训练参数量、峰值训练显存、推理延迟、吞吐量）联合报告，并通过Pareto前沿分析找出"准确率/成本"最优配置。
- 实证发现：秩饱和出现得早且突然（r=16之后几乎无提升）；扩展适配模块（加入k、o投影或FFN层）相比直接增大秩是效率更低的杠杆。
- 用三个随机种子（42/123/456）重复全部11种配置，报告均值和标准差，验证结论在不同初始化下的稳健性——这是许多PEFT论文缺失的严谨性补充。
- 公开全部代码、配置与训练日志（GitHub仓库），整套实验在单张T4 GPU上约35分钟即可跑完。

## Key Contributions

- A fully reproducible, single-variable trade-off study of LoRA rank, target-module selection, and quantization precision on a specific 60M-parameter encoder-decoder model (T5-small) and a single-table text-to-SQL benchmark (WikiSQL), deliberately choosing the small-model regime so the full design space can be swept cheaply.
- Joint reporting of task accuracy alongside trainable parameters, peak training memory, inference latency, and throughput, with Pareto-front analysis identifying the accuracy/cost sweet spot rather than optimizing accuracy alone.
- Evidence that rank saturation occurs early (r=16) in this setting, and that expanding adapted modules beyond {q,v} (adding k, o, or FFN) yields diminishing returns relative to simply raising rank at the same parameter budget.
- A 3-seed variance study (42, 123, 456) showing the configuration ranking is stable across seeds, though low-rank LoRA (r=2) is markedly more variance-prone (std=0.063) than quantized configurations (QLoRA INT8 std=0.006).
- Open release of code, configs, and logs at github.com/mahendrarathore1742/efficient_peft_small_models, explicitly framed as a study of relative trade-offs rather than a bid for state-of-the-art absolute accuracy.

## Methodology

The authors fine-tune T5-small (60M parameters) on WikiSQL, serializing each example as `translate English to SQL: <Q> table: <H1 | H2 | ...>` with the gold `human_readable` SQL string as target. Training uses only **5,000 examples** with evaluation on **500 validation examples** — a deliberately small subset chosen to keep the full sweep tractable on a single GPU, not WikiSQL's full ~56K-example training split.

Three axes are varied one at a time relative to a LoRA r=8, {q,v}, FP16 reference:
1. **LoRA rank** r ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16, 32} (scaling α=2r, dropout 0.1, frozen biases), following Hu et al. (2021).
2. **Adapted modules**: attention query/value {q,v}, full attention {q,k,v,o}, and attention plus feed-forward {q,v}+FFN.
3. **Precision**: FP16 baseline, INT8 (LLM.int8()), and NF4 4-bit (QLoRA, double quantization, FP16 compute dtype) — quantized configurations use LoRA r=8, {q,v} adapters on top of the frozen quantized base.

Baselines are zero-shot and full fine-tuning (all 60.5M parameters updated) as the accuracy upper bound. Training uses AdamW, lr=5e-4, batch size 8, 3 epochs, max input/output lengths 256/128, beam search decoding (num_beams=4). All 11 configurations are run with three seeds (42, 123, 456) on a single NVIDIA T4 GPU (16GB) via Kaggle Notebooks. Two metrics are reported: exact-match (EM) accuracy (normalized string comparison, full 500-example set) and execution accuracy (SQLite execution agreement, but only over the 25/500 examples with executable gold SQL — 5% of the eval set, explicitly flagged by the authors as high-variance and secondary to EM). The trade-off is framed formally as maximizing accuracy A(c) subject to a memory budget M(c) ≤ B_M, with the Pareto front reported over all swept configurations.

## Results & Highlights

- **Full fine-tuning vs. LoRA r=16**: Full fine-tuning reaches 71.2% EM / 84.0% execution accuracy with 60,506,624 trainable parameters and 2.31GB peak memory (229.6s); LoRA r=16 on {q,v} reaches 59.6% EM / 80.0% execution accuracy with only 589,824 parameters (0.965% of the base, i.e. <1%) and 1.60GB peak memory (187.1s) — recovering 83.7% of full fine-tuning's accuracy at 31% lower memory.
- **Rank saturation**: Accuracy rises steadily from r=2 (38.6% EM, 73,728 params) through r=16 (59.6% EM), then plateaus sharply at r=32 (60.4% EM, 1,179,648 params) — only a 0.8-point gain despite doubling trainable parameters, and peak memory stays flat (1.59-1.61GB) across all ranks.
- **Module expansion is a weaker lever than rank**: at r=8, expanding from {q,v} (53.4% EM, 344,064 params) to full attention {q,k,v,o} (59.2% EM, 589,824 params) costs 71% more parameters and a 6.3% memory increase (1.59→1.69GB) for a 5.8-point gain; adding {q,v}+FFN (58.6% EM, 786,432 params) needs 33% more parameters than {q,k,v,o} for no accuracy improvement. Notably, LoRA r=16 on {q,v} alone (59.6% EM, 589,824 params) matches {q,k,v,o}'s accuracy at the identical parameter budget.
- **Quantization (QLoRA)**: INT8 and NF4 reach 52.8% and 53.2% EM respectively vs. 53.4% for the FP16 LoRA r=8/{q,v} baseline — only 0.2-0.6 points lower — while peak memory drops to 0.60GB for both, a 62% reduction vs. FP16 LoRA (1.59GB) and 74% vs. full fine-tuning (2.31GB). However, inference latency is worse under quantization: INT8 921 ms/query and NF4 614 ms/query vs. ~380 ms/query for FP16 LoRA, attributed to T4 dequantization overhead; NF4 throughput is 34 tok/s.
- **Seed variance (Table 3, 3 seeds)**: configuration ranking (full fine-tuning > r=32 ≈ r=16 > r=8 > r=4 > r=2) holds across all three seeds. Low-rank LoRA is markedly noisier (r=2: mean EM 0.313, std 0.063) than higher ranks or quantized variants (QLoRA INT8: mean 0.535, std 0.006; QLoRA NF4: mean 0.525, std 0.015), suggesting quantization regularizes optimization at this scale.
- Zero-shot T5-small achieves 0.0% EM/execution accuracy, confirming task-specific adaptation is necessary before any PEFT comparison is meaningful.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: Low-to-modest. This is a systematic empirical ablation/replication study, not a new method or architecture — LoRA, QLoRA, and T5-small/WikiSQL are all pre-existing components. The contribution is the controlled small-model sweep and the joint accuracy/system-cost reporting, which is a useful but incremental empirical contribution rather than a conceptual advance.

**Rigor**: Better than typical for this kind of small-scale ablation — the paper runs 3 seeds per configuration (Table 3) and reports std, which most PEFT ablation papers skip. However, a significant caveat undercuts the absolute numbers: training uses only 5,000 of WikiSQL's ~56,000 training examples and 3 epochs, so the "full fine-tuning" upper bound of 71.2% EM is far below published WikiSQL seq2seq baselines (which typically reach 80%+ with full training data). This means the observed "rank saturates at r=16" finding could partly reflect an undertrained/data-starved regime rather than an intrinsic property of the task — the authors don't test whether saturation point shifts with more training data, which would have been an easy and informative ablation to include given their stated goal of characterizing intrinsic task complexity.

**Reproducibility**: Strong — code, configs, and logs released on GitHub, single consumer-class GPU (T4, free-tier Kaggle), full sweep completes in ~35 minutes. This is a genuine strength that many larger-scale PEFT papers cannot offer.

**Significance**: Narrow but honestly scoped. The authors repeatedly and explicitly flag (in Discussion, Limitations, and the abstract itself) that all conclusions are specific to T5-small + WikiSQL and may not generalize to other architectures, multi-table benchmarks (Spider, BIRD), or other tasks — this is commendable intellectual honesty rather than overclaiming.

**Red flags**: (1) Execution accuracy is computed over just 25 of 500 examples (5%) — the paper itself calls this a "caveat" and says it "should be interpreted with caution," which is good disclosure but means one of the two headline metrics is essentially noise; readers should treat EM as the only trustworthy number. (2) The QLoRA latency finding (921ms/614ms vs. ~380ms for FP16 LoRA) is counterintuitive relative to QLoRA's usual "efficient inference" framing in the literature, and the paper offers only a one-sentence explanation (T4 dequantization overhead) without further diagnosis — a missing ablation on whether this is T4-specific or a general small-model artifact. (3) No comparison against other PEFT baselines (adapters, prefix-tuning, BitFit — all cited in Related Work but never run), so the "LoRA is a good lever" conclusion is not benchmarked against sibling PEFT methods, only against full fine-tuning and quantization variants of LoRA itself.

## Strengths

- Genuinely controlled single-variable design (only one axis changes per configuration relative to a fixed reference), making the rank/module/precision comparisons cleanly interpretable.
- Joint accuracy + system-cost reporting (parameters, memory, latency, throughput) with an explicit Pareto-front framing, rather than the accuracy-only tables common in PEFT papers.
- 3-seed variance analysis with reported std, revealing that low-rank LoRA is meaningfully noisier than higher-rank or quantized configurations — a finding with direct practical value for practitioners choosing configurations under compute constraints.
- Full, low-barrier reproducibility (single T4 GPU, ~35 minutes, open code/logs).
- Honest, repeated acknowledgment of scope limits (single model, single benchmark, single-table task) rather than extrapolating findings to "small models" broadly.

## Limitations

- Single base model (T5-small, 60M) and single benchmark (WikiSQL, single-table only); the authors explicitly state rank saturation and module-expansion conclusions should not be generalized to other architectures, multi-table benchmarks (Spider, BIRD), or other NLP tasks.
- Training uses only 5,000 of WikiSQL's full training examples over 3 epochs, yielding a relatively low full fine-tuning ceiling (71.2% EM) compared to literature values for WikiSQL seq2seq models — this may confound the "early rank saturation" finding with an undertrained-model artifact.
- Execution accuracy is measured over only 25/500 (5%) executable examples, explicitly flagged by the authors as high-variance and secondary to exact-match.
- QLoRA's higher inference latency (921ms INT8, 614ms NF4 vs. ~380ms FP16 LoRA) is reported but not deeply diagnosed, limiting actionable guidance for latency-sensitive deployments.
- No comparison to other PEFT methods (adapters, prefix-tuning, BitFit) that are discussed in Related Work but not empirically benchmarked.

## Relevance

对于关注小模型高效微调（PEFT/LoRA/QLoRA）实践配置选择的研究者或工程师，这篇论文提供了一个"秩到底要多大、模块要不要多适配、量化划不划算"的具体量化参考（如LoRA r=16在{q,v}上是本设置下的帕累托最优点），但其结论受限于60M参数模型和仅5000条训练样本，迁移到更大模型或更复杂任务（如Spider/BIRD多表场景）时需要重新验证。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the r=16 rank-saturation point shift if the model is trained on WikiSQL's full ~56K-example training set instead of 5,000 examples — is saturation a property of task complexity or of an undertrained regime?
- How do these rank/module/quantization trade-offs transfer to multi-table benchmarks (Spider, BIRD) where the "intrinsic task dimensionality" the authors invoke is presumably higher?
- Would the QLoRA latency penalty persist on more recent GPUs with better native low-bit kernel support, or is it specific to the T4's dequantization path?
- How would other PEFT methods (adapters, prefix-tuning, BitFit) compare on the same accuracy/memory/latency axes at this model scale — would LoRA still dominate the Pareto front?
- Does the "module expansion is a weaker lever than rank" finding hold for decoder-only architectures, where attention and FFN structure differ from T5's encoder-decoder design?
