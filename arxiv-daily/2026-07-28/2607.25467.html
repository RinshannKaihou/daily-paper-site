Seen, Said, or Forgotten? A Causal Audit of Visual KV Memory Across Dialog Turns

Key Finding Attention-guided visual-KV eviction rests on a false proxy: current attention is negatively correlated with the future utility it should predict.

Why This Matters

Anyone deploying stateful multimodal assistants with irreversible KV eviction should stop treating low current attention as proof a region is safe to delete.

原文 Relevance：这篇论文对研究多模态大模型KV缓存压缩、长程/多轮对话记忆管理或视觉token淘汰策略的读者高度相关，其因果审计方法论也可迁移到评估其他"基于当前重要性做不可逆压缩"的系统设计中。

Critical Edge

The paper's own red flag: dropping the whole image improves Idefics's NLL but hurts Qwen's — the "visual dependence" finding doesn't even replicate in sign across the two model families tested.

Methodology

CVMA treats a multi-turn visual dialog as one persistent, teacher-forced KV trajectory: the image is prefilled once at turn 1, and every later turn extends the same cache without re-encoding, so every paired comparison evaluates an identical future token sequence.

Four linked interventions probe that one frozen trajectory: (1) regional future utility — ablate a single spatial region (2×2 / 4×4 / 8×8 grid) and measure future-turn NLL damage; (2) visual dependence — ablate the whole image span; (3) proxy regret — compare attention-guided joint retention against seeded-random and a same-dialog marginal-utility "oracle"; (4) image × text substitution — a 2×2 factorial ablating image KV and assistant-text KV, jointly and separately.

Runtime assertions enforce token-exact extension and immutable visual indices so comparisons stay causally clean. Evaluated on VisDial (2,064 ten-turn dialogs) and ConvBench (546 three-turn dialogs) with Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct and Idefics3-8B-Llama3.

Strengths

  • Clean causal framing: explicit identification assumptions, paired teacher-forced interventions, disjoint pilot/confirmation splits.
  • Multiple reinforcing evidence lines — regional and whole-image ablation, marginal-utility oracle, spatial hotspot/flip diagnostics, factorial text substitution — converge on one mechanism.
  • Concrete, quantified failure modes (negative correlations, quartile damage gaps, exact-match point losses), not just qualitative claims.
  • Honest scoping: explicitly disclaims proposing a new compressor and states the bounds of its own evidence.
  • The verbalization-boundary experiment is a creative, well-controlled new empirical instrument — pseudoword controls, independent judge models, prospective replication.

Limitations

  • Only two model families tested; the full compressor policy ladder is Qwen-only.
  • Aggregate visual-dependence sign doesn't replicate across models — positive mean for Qwen, negative NLL effect for Idefics.
  • VisDial's always-visible caption prevents a clean "no-relay" control for the verbalization experiments.
  • Teacher-forced NLL is the primary metric throughout; free-running exact-match only partially validates downstream task impact.
  • No new eviction policy is proposed — a diagnostic lens, not a drop-in fix.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Would a retention policy conditioned on a cheaply estimated proxy for future visual dependence close the gap to the marginal-utility oracle — and could such a proxy be learned online rather than requiring CVMA's ground-truth future turns?
  2. Why does whole-image removal help Idefics3-8B but hurt Qwen2.5-VL on VisDial — caption-reliance artifact, training-data difference, or an architectural difference in visual/text KV interaction?
  3. Since verbalized facts become recoverable from text KV while unstated ones do not, could a compressor exploit this — deferring visual eviction until a region's content has been echoed in assistant output — and how much of Table 1's damage would that recover?
  4. How does the verbalization boundary interact with hallucination — if an assistant states a fact incorrectly, does the "false" text KV still substitute for the evicted true image KV, locking in the error?
