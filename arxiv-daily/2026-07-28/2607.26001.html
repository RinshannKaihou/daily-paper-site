Decoupling SAM's inner geometry from its outer optimizer

Decoupling SAM's perturbation geometry from its optimizer shows spectral perturbation pays off only when paired with Muon's own update rule — with AdamW it's the weakest SAM variant.

Sharpness-Aware Minimization and Muon: Robustness under the Spectral Norm ↗
Zhong, Milsom, Murray · 2026-07-28 · arXiv:2607.26001 ·
sharpness-aware minimization Muon optimizer spectral norm matrix-aware optimization ImageNet-1K
Key Metric
80.23%
ViT-Small/16 ImageNet-1K Val Top-1 with SpecSAM-Muon (3 seeds) — +5.28 pts over unperturbed Muon (74.95%) and +1.52 pts over the same spectral perturbation paired with AdamW (78.71%).
Why This Matters
A tested reference for practitioners combining Muon with sharpness-aware training: spectral perturbation geometry pairs well with Muon, poorly with AdamW.
Critical Edge
SpecSAM-AdamW is the single weakest SAM variant tested (78.71%, below plain Euclidean SAM-AdamW's 79.17%) — and the whole recipe is untested on language models, where Muon is actually used most.

Methodology

INNER PERTURBATION
Steepest-ascent step in a chosen norm: none, Euclidean (SAM), or spectral via k-step Newton–Schulz orthogonalization (SpecSAM) — applied layerwise, matrix blocks vs. non-matrix params get separate radii ρm, ρn.
×
OUTER UPDATE
Perturbed gradient routed through AdamW/SGDW (elementwise) for non-matrix params, or Muon (momentum then orthogonalize) for matrix-valued hidden layers.
→
2×2 GRID
ViT-Small/16 + ResNet-50 on ImageNet-1K, 3 seeds each, Welch t-tests — plus a LayerSAM control to isolate the spectral norm from layerwise budgeting.

The paper's theoretical move is a first-order derivation of SAM's robust loss for a general (possibly non-Euclidean) inner norm: linearizing the worst-case loss gives L(θ) + ρ·||DL(θ)||in,*, which motivates the standard stop-gradient SAM surrogate evaluated at the steepest-ascent perturbation in that norm. Parameters are then split into matrix blocks W(ℓ) and a non-matrix block v, with an ℓ∞ aggregation across blocks (motivated by Muon) so the perturbation decomposes independently per layer. For matrix blocks, the spectral-norm dual is the nuclear norm, whose subgradient is the polar factor Ortho(G) — the same primitive Muon's own outer update uses, approximated by 3–5 steps of Newton–Schulz iteration. This yields four main method combinations (SAM/SpecSAM × AdamW-SGDW/Muon), plus unperturbed baselines and a LayerSAM control that keeps SpecSAM's per-layer budget but swaps the spectral norm for the (self-dual) Frobenius norm — isolating whether gains come from the spectral geometry itself or just from allocating perturbation budget layer-by-layer. Hyperparameters were tuned via a two-stage grid search at half the training budget to avoid confounding SAM's gains with unrelated LR/WD effects; total compute was ~6,000 GPU-hours on Isambard-AI (8×H100s).

Euclidean SAM shifts both outer optimizers by a near-constant amount (+4.9 AdamW, +5.0 Muon on ViT) — additive and outer-optimizer-agnostic. The spectral inner step instead interacts strongly with the outer step: +5.28 pts over Muon vs. only +4.45 over AdamW on ViT. On ResNet-50, unperturbed Muon (77.05%) actually trails plain SGDW (77.35%) — it's the spectral inner step that flips the ordering in Muon's favor (+1.50 pts vs. only +0.42 for SGDW).

Strengths

  • Clean, norm-agnostic SAM formalization (Algorithm 1) separating inner geometry from outer optimizer — a reusable analytical lens beyond this paper.
  • LayerSAM ablation isolates spectral-norm effects from layerwise-budgeting effects, a decomposition most empirical optimizer papers skip.
  • Honestly reports a negative, counter-intuitive result (SpecSAM-AdamW as the weakest SAM variant) rather than cherry-picking favorable comparisons.
  • Statistically grounded (Welch t-tests, 3 seeds) with full hyperparameter disclosure, supporting reproducibility.
  • Multiple complementary diagnostics (effective rank, finite-radius sharpness) attempt to explain, not just report, the inner/outer interaction.

Limitations

  • Limited to image classification (ImageNet-1K, CIFAR-100), two architectures; no language-modeling experiments, where Muon is most heavily used in practice.
  • Only 3 seeds for main results, single runs for some diagnostics/ablations, non-exhaustive hyperparameter search due to compute constraints.
  • Why spectral geometry pairs better with Muon is only suggestive (correlated with effective-rank retention), not established causally or theoretically.
  • SpecSAM adds Newton–Schulz overhead on top of Muon's own, plus two backward passes per step like standard SAM — compared at equal epochs, not equal wall-clock time.
  • ImageNet-R ordering among top methods is not statistically resolved across 3 seeds, weakening the OOD generalization claim.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Why does spectral inner geometry amplify Muon's advantage while hurting AdamW? Is the correlated effective-rank observation causal, testable via direct rank-constrained intervention?
  • Does SpecSAM + Muon transfer to language-modeling / transformer pretraining at scale, where Muon is currently most adopted and matrix shapes differ substantially from vision backbones?
  • Would a finite aggregation exponent (r=2 instead of r=∞) change the inner/outer interaction, or dilute the spectral-geometry benefit currently attributed to independent per-layer allocation?
  • Given spectral variants reach lower train accuracy at matched validation accuracy, is that a stronger implicit regularizer, or just optimization difficulty from Newton–Schulz-approximated orthogonalization?
  • Could scheduling or interpolating between Euclidean and spectral inner geometries over training (mentioned anecdotally) yield further gains beyond either fixed geometry?
