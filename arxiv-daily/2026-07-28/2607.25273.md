> **一句话总结：** 论文指出图上的一致预测（conformal prediction）扩散基线 DAPS 用统一系数 λ 沿边扩散 APS 非一致性分数，隐含同质性（homophily）假设，在异质图上会让预测集比朴素 APS 平均增大最多 10.6%（如 texas 数据集）；作者提出 HEAD-CP 一族——用 GNN softmax 输出算出的、无需标签的逐节点局部同质性来决定扩散系数的正负与大小（signed/edge/v3 三个变体），在保持覆盖保证（Theorem 1）不变的前提下，在 10 个基准上的事后 oracle 在 8/10 数据集上以 p<0.01 显著优于 DAPS，texas 上提升达 10.3%，仅在 citeseer（统计不显著，p=0.23）和 pubmed（差距 0.002）上 DAPS 仍略占优。

> **TL;DR:** This paper identifies a concrete failure mode of DAPS (Diffused Adaptive Prediction Sets), the standard graph-aware conformal prediction baseline that diffuses APS non-conformity scores with a single global coefficient λ: because the diffusion is a uniform low-pass filter, it implicitly assumes homophily and inflates mean prediction-set size by up to +10.6% relative to plain APS on the texas benchmark (and hurts 6/10 of the 10 datasets tested). HEAD-CP replaces the global λ with a label-free, per-node diffusion coefficient derived from the GNN's own softmax (three variants: signed, edge-compatibility, and a DAPS-baseline-with-correction "v3"), provably preserving marginal coverage (Theorem 1) for any sign/magnitude of the coefficient. Across 10 benchmarks (3 GNN backbones × 5 seeds × 100 calibration/test splits, 15,000 CP runs per method), the post-hoc oracle over the HEAD-CP family beats DAPS on 8/10 datasets at p<0.01 (paired Wilcoxon), most notably +10.3% on texas, while losing only marginally to DAPS on citeseer (gap ≤0.0003, not significant, p=0.23) and pubmed (gap 0.002).

## 核心贡献

- 首次系统刻画并量化了 DAPS 这一被广泛采用的图一致预测扩散基线在异质图上的失效机制：统一低通扩散把每个节点的分数拉向邻居均值，在异质邻居中注入与真实标签无关的噪声，抬高校准阈值 τ，导致预测集变大（六个基准上比朴素 APS 差，最坏 texas 上 +10.6%）。
- 提出 HEAD-CP 家族：扩散系数不再是全局常数 λ，而是从 GNN softmax 输出中提取的、不使用任何标签的逐节点/逐边局部同质性估计，涵盖三个覆盖同质性谱系两端的生产变体（signed、edge-compatibility、DAPS-baseline-with-correction/v3），以及一个用于消融对比的 v2。
- 给出理论保证（Theorem 1）：无论逐节点系数 γv 的符号（低通/高通）或大小如何，只要该系数是 (X, A, fθ) 的确定性函数且不使用校准/测试池中的标签，split-CP 的边缘覆盖保证依然严格成立——扩展了标准 split-CP 论证以覆盖“高通”（负系数）扩散这一此前未被验证的情形。
- 在 10 个标准节点分类基准（同质性 h 从 0.05 到 0.81）上做了大规模实证（3 个 GNN 骨干 × 5 个随机种子 × 100 次校准/测试划分，每方法 15,000 次 CP 运行），并辅以合成 CSBM 实验，证实 DAPS 的效率随同质性单调恶化（h=0.05 时效率达到 APS 的 2.8 倍），而 HEAD-CP-signed 几乎保持平坦；同时讨论了一个基于数据集级平均软同质性 h̄^soft 的无标签选择器，能恢复事后 oracle 相对 DAPS 增益的 66.8%（但统计上不显著，p=0.21）。

## Key Contributions

- **Diagnoses and quantifies a previously unbenchmarked failure mode of DAPS:** the uniform one-hop low-pass diffusion `s' = (1-λ)s + λ·s̄_N` implicitly assumes homophily; on heterophilic graphs it forces the true-class score toward a label-irrelevant neighborhood mean, inflating the calibration threshold τ and prediction-set size — up to +10.6% larger than plain APS on texas, with DAPS hurting 6 of 10 benchmarks overall.
- **Introduces the HEAD-CP family of node-wise diffusion variants**, each deriving its coefficient from the GNN's own softmax output (which DAPS discards entirely) rather than a single global λ: HEAD-CP-signed (γv = γmax(2hv−1) from a Laplace-smoothed pseudo-label homophily hv, best on extreme heterophily: Roman-Empire, texas, wisconsin), HEAD-CP-edge (per-edge compatibility ρ(u,v) from softmax inner products, best on mixed/intermediate heterophily: cornell, chameleon, squirrel), and HEAD-CP-v3 (a DAPS-style baseline plus a soft, confidence-gated correction, best on moderate-to-high homophily: Amazon-Ratings, cora, competitive on citeseer/pubmed).
- **Proves marginal coverage is preserved regardless of the sign or magnitude of the diffusion coefficient (Theorem 1)**, extending the standard split-CP exchangeability argument to the high-pass (negative-coefficient) regime that DAPS's original analysis never considers — meaning HEAD-CP's efficiency gains come with no coverage trade-off, verified empirically (180/180 dataset/model/method cells meet the ≥0.895 tolerance around the 0.9 target).
- **Large-scale empirical validation** (10 benchmarks spanning h∈[0.05, 0.81]; 3 GNN backbones × 5 seeds × 100 calibration/test partitions = 15,000 CP runs/method) plus a synthetic CSBM sweep isolating the low-pass-vs-high-pass mechanism, showing DAPS efficiency degrades monotonically as homophily decreases (reaching 2.8× the APS value at h=0.05) while signed HEAD-CP stays essentially flat.
- **Proposes and evaluates a label-free variant selector** based on dataset-mean soft homophily h̄^soft with fixed (untuned) thresholds (0.4, 0.6), which recovers 66.8% of the oracle's efficiency gain over DAPS, explicitly flagging the gap to the post-hoc oracle as "the main outstanding empirical question."

## Methodology

The paper builds on split conformal prediction with the (randomized) Adaptive Prediction Sets (APS) non-conformity score s(v,y), calibrated by taking the k-th smallest score over a calibration set C (k = ⌈(|C|+1)(1−α)⌉) to form prediction sets C(v) = {y : s(v,y) ≤ τ}. DAPS (Zargarbashi et al., ICML 2023) diffuses this score along edges with a single global mixing coefficient λ: s'(v,y) = (1−λ)s(v,y) + λ·s̄_N(v,y), where s̄_N is the neighbor-mean score — a one-hop low-pass graph filter.

HEAD-CP replaces λ with a per-node or per-edge coefficient computed from the GNN's own softmax output pv (no labels used):
- **Signed variant:** a Laplace-smoothed local pseudo-label homophily hv = (|{u∈N(v): ŷu=ŷv}|+1)/(|N(v)|+2) maps to γv = γmax(2hv−1) ∈ (−γmax, +γmax); homophilic nodes (hv→1) get low-pass diffusion, heterophilic nodes (hv→0) get high-pass diffusion (pushing the score away from the neighbor mean instead of toward it).
- **Edge-compatibility variant:** a per-edge compatibility ρ(u,v) = (⟨pu,pv⟩−1/K)/(1−1/K) ∈ [−1/(K−1), 1] lets diffusion be low-pass over compatible edges and high-pass over incompatible ones within the same (mixed) neighborhood — addressing the fact that heterophily is often edge-level, not node-level.
- **DAPS-baseline-with-correction (v3):** γv = γbase + γvar·h̃v·confv, a fixed DAPS-style baseline plus a soft-homophily correction gated by the node's own top-1 confidence (so uncertain nodes get less correction); setting γvar=0 recovers plain DAPS, and setting γbase=0 yields the "v2" ablation reported separately.

Isolated nodes (no neighbors) get zero diffusion by convention, uniformly across DAPS and all HEAD-CP variants. Theorem 1 (with proof sketch) shows marginal coverage Pr[Yv* ∈ C(v*)] ≥ 1−α holds for any of the three constructions, because all auxiliary quantities (γv, ρ(u,v), hv, h^soft_v, confv) are deterministic functions of (X, A, fθ) computed without pool labels, and the standard split-CP exchangeability argument goes through unchanged even when the diffused score s' falls outside [0,1] (which can happen under high-pass diffusion) — no clipping or post-processing is needed.

Experiments use 10 node-classification benchmarks (cora, citeseer, pubmed — homophilic citation graphs; texas, wisconsin, cornell — WebKB/Geom-GCN heterophilic graphs; chameleon, squirrel — Wikipedia graphs, noted to have known label-leakage in their standard splits per Platonov et al. 2023 but retained for comparability with prior graph-CP work; Roman-Empire, Amazon-Ratings — leakage-free Platonov heterophilous benchmarks), spanning edge-homophily h from 0.05 to 0.81. Three GNN backbones (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE) × 5 seeds × 100 random 50/50 calibration-test partitions yield 15,000 CP runs per method at α=0.1. All diffusion hyperparameters are fixed at 0.5 (λ=γmax=η=γbase=γvar=0.5) across all datasets to avoid per-dataset tuning bias. A separate synthetic CSBM experiment (K=3, n=1500, logistic-regression base classifier) sweeps h∈{0.05,...,0.95} to isolate the low-pass-vs-high-pass mechanism in a controlled setting, using only the signed variant (since edge/v3 depend on GNN softmax propagating real neighborhood structure, which a node-independent logistic classifier on synthetic features does not provide).

## Results & Highlights

- **DAPS inflates prediction sets on the majority of benchmarks relative to plain APS:** on texas (h=0.06), DAPS efficiency is 3.183 vs. APS's 2.879 (+10.6%, the worst case); on Roman-Empire (h=0.05), DAPS is 9.401 vs. APS's 8.544 (+10.0%). Overall, DAPS is worse than APS on 6 of 10 datasets (texas, Roman-Empire, wisconsin +5.6%, chameleon +3.2%, squirrel +2.3%, cornell +1.0%).
- **HEAD-CP variants recover or beat APS everywhere DAPS fails:** on texas, HEAD-CP-signed achieves 2.855 (below APS's 2.879 and far below DAPS's 3.183); on Roman-Empire, HEAD-CP-signed reaches 8.489 (also below APS's 8.544 and DAPS's 9.401). The post-hoc oracle over {signed, edge, v3} attains the row-wise minimum efficiency on 8 of 10 datasets and stays at or below plain APS on every one of the 10 datasets, whereas DAPS exceeds APS on 6/10.
- **Statistical significance (paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test, 15 (model, seed) pairs per dataset):** the best HEAD-CP variant beats DAPS at p<0.01 on 8/10 datasets, with the largest gain being +10.3% on texas (matching the abstract's headline number); it ties DAPS on citeseer (DAPS 2.5205 vs. HEAD-CP-v3 2.5208, a 0.0003 gap, p=0.23, not significant) and trails DAPS by 0.002 on pubmed (DAPS 1.443 vs. HEAD-CP-v3 1.445). Pooling all 150 (dataset × model × seed) triplets, the oracle wins 75.3% of pairwise comparisons (p<10⁻¹⁷); 8/10 datasets remain significant under Benjamini-Hochberg correction at α=0.01 (7/10 under the stricter Holm-Bonferroni).
- **Coverage is preserved everywhere:** marginal coverage meets or exceeds the 1−α=0.9 target in all 180 (dataset, model, method) configurations, with a tolerance of ≥0.895 and a minimum observed coverage of exactly 0.900 — confirming Theorem 1 empirically for every method including the high-pass (negative-coefficient) regime.
- **Synthetic CSBM sweep isolates the mechanism:** at edge homophily h=0.05, DAPS efficiency reaches 2.49, which is 2.8× the APS value of 0.90, while signed HEAD-CP stays essentially flat across the full h∈[0.05,0.95] range, directly confirming the low-pass-vs-high-pass hypothesis in a controlled synthetic setting.
- **The label-free selector is a real but incomplete solution:** a simple rule thresholding dataset-mean soft homophily h̄^soft at (0.4, 0.6) recovers 66.8% of the oracle's efficiency gain over DAPS, but this recovery is itself not statistically significant (paired Wilcoxon p=0.21), and the rule matches the true row-minimum variant on only 3/10 datasets (it assigns HEAD-CP-signed to 9/10 datasets and never selects v3), because h̄^soft is capped structurally near 0.5 for K=3 classes and thus poorly separated from the 0.4/0.6 thresholds — the paper explicitly flags designing a better calibrated selector as the main open problem.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core idea — replacing DAPS's single global diffusion coefficient with a label-free, per-node/per-edge coefficient derived from the GNN's own softmax — is a small, well-targeted, and genuinely underexplored fix. The paper's framing (HEAD-CP as "the conformal-prediction analogue" of low-/high-pass-mixing GNNs like FAGCN and GPRGNN) is apt and the signed variant's high-pass mechanism is a clean, interpretable idea rather than a black-box tweak.

**Rigor:** The theoretical contribution (Theorem 1) is a straightforward but correctly executed extension of standard split-CP exchangeability to allow negative/high-pass diffusion coefficients — the proof sketch is sound and clearly scoped (it explicitly notes an efficiency bound on prediction-set size is *not* established and is left for future work, which is an honest limitation rather than an overclaim). The empirical design is solid: 3 GNN backbones × 5 seeds × 100 partitions = 15,000 runs per method, paired Wilcoxon tests with multiple-comparison correction (BH and Holm-Bonferroni), and a synthetic CSBM ablation that cleanly isolates the low-pass-vs-high-pass mechanism from confounds present in the real-data GNN experiments (the paper is explicit that the edge and v3 variants cannot be meaningfully tested in the CSBM setting because a node-independent logistic classifier doesn't produce non-degenerate pairwise softmax inner products — a specific, honestly-flagged scope limitation on its own synthetic experiment).

**Reproducibility:** All hyperparameters are fixed at 0.5 across every dataset specifically to avoid tuning bias (a good practice), and Table I / Fig. 1-4 report enough detail (per-dataset efficiency, per-model breakdowns implied, ablation sweeps over γmax∈[0.1,0.9]) to reconstruct the main results; however, no code or artifact release is evident in the reviewed manuscript, so exact reproduction would require re-implementing the diffusion variants and the 15,000-run experimental harness from the text alone.

**Significance and red flags:** The paper's own "Best" column in Table I is a genuine limitation dressed as a finding: the reported 8/10 wins are a **post-hoc oracle** that picks the best of {signed, edge, v3} *per dataset after seeing the results* — this is explicitly acknowledged as "an upper bound on what the label-free selector... can deliver," and the actual deployable selector (thresholding h̄^soft) only recovers 66.8% of that gain, not significantly (p=0.21). This is an important caveat: the practical, label-free version of HEAD-CP is a real but partial win over DAPS, not the full 8/10 significant improvement highlighted in the abstract. Additionally, two of the two homophilic benchmarks where the paper reports a "loss" (citeseer, pubmed) are extremely close calls (0.0003 and 0.002 absolute efficiency gaps) — DAPS is not meaningfully beaten in its home turf (high homophily), which is a fair reading given the mechanism (v3 explicitly keeps a DAPS baseline term). A further limitation: the comparison set is narrow — the paper does not run a head-to-head against SNAPS or RR-GNN (both cited as related work using DAPS as a component/baseline), explicitly deferring this to future work, so it's unclear whether HEAD-CP's gains would persist against stronger, more recent graph-CP methods rather than DAPS alone. Also, 2 of the 10 benchmarks (chameleon, squirrel) are noted by the authors themselves to have known label-leakage in their standard splits (per Platonov et al. 2023), retained only "for direct comparability with prior graph-CP work" — a data-quirk the paper transparently flags rather than hides.

## Strengths

- Correctly and precisely diagnoses a specific, previously unbenchmarked failure mode in a widely-cited graph-CP baseline (DAPS), backed by a clean mechanistic argument (low-pass diffusion assumes homophily) and confirmed both on real heterophilic benchmarks and a controlled synthetic CSBM sweep.
- The coverage guarantee (Theorem 1) is proven to hold for *any* sign/magnitude of the per-node diffusion coefficient, which is exactly the theoretical gap needed to justify the high-pass (signed) variant — DAPS's original analysis only covers the positive-coefficient, homophily-favoring regime.
- Extensive, well-powered empirical evaluation (15,000 CP runs/method, proper paired significance testing with multiple-comparison correction) across a genuinely diverse set of 10 benchmarks spanning h=0.05 to h=0.81, including both leakage-prone standard splits and leakage-free Platonov benchmarks.
- Honest framing throughout: the paper explicitly labels its main empirical result as a post-hoc oracle, quantifies exactly how much of that oracle gain a real label-free selector currently recovers (66.8%, not significant), and states the calibrated-selector design problem as the paper's own main open question rather than glossing over it.

## Limitations

- The headline "8/10 significant wins over DAPS" result is a post-hoc oracle over three variants, not a single deployable method; the actual label-free selector tested only recovers 66.8% of this gain and is not statistically distinguishable from chance relative to the oracle (p=0.21).
- No efficiency (prediction-set-size) theoretical bound is established for any HEAD-CP variant — only the coverage guarantee is proven; all efficiency claims are purely empirical.
- No head-to-head comparison against more recent/stronger graph-CP baselines (SNAPS, RR-GNN, CF-GNN) that the paper itself cites as related work — comparisons are limited to APS and DAPS only.
- Two of the ten benchmarks (chameleon, squirrel) use standard splits with acknowledged label leakage; results on these two datasets should be interpreted with some caution even though leakage-free alternatives (Roman-Empire, Amazon-Ratings) are also included.
- Pseudo-label-based homophily estimates (used by the signed variant) are acknowledged to be unreliable when GNN validation accuracy is low, and the CSBM synthetic experiment could not meaningfully test the edge and v3 variants due to the degenerate softmax structure of the node-independent base classifier used there.

## Relevance

对研究图上一致预测 / 不确定性量化，或需要在异质图（heterophilic graphs）上部署 GNN 的研究者，这篇论文清楚指出了 DAPS 这一常用扩散基线的具体失效场景与量化代价，并给出了理论保证不变、实现简单的改进方向；其"事后 oracle vs. 无标签选择器"之间的差距也是一个值得跟进的开放问题。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can a calibrated label-free selector (e.g., trained via held-out validation, or combining h̄^soft with per-node confidence as the paper suggests) close most of the gap to the post-hoc oracle, moving the 66.8%/p=0.21 result to something both larger and statistically significant?
- How would HEAD-CP variants compare directly against SNAPS and RR-GNN (rather than only against DAPS and plain APS), given that SNAPS already uses DAPS as one of its baseline components?
- Does an efficiency (prediction-set-size) bound exist for the signed or edge-compatibility variants under a CSBM-style theoretical model, analogous to DAPS's original approximation-error bound under homophily?
- How sensitive are the three HEAD-CP variants to GNN calibration quality — i.e., what happens on datasets/backbones where softmax confidence is poorly calibrated to begin with, since the local-homophily estimate hv and confidence gate confv both derive directly from the softmax output?
- Would the edge-compatibility variant's per-edge weighting extend naturally to a learned (rather than hand-specified) diffusion function, and would that erode or strengthen the label-free coverage guarantee of Theorem 1?
