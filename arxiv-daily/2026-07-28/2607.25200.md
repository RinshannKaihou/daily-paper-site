> **一句话总结：** 本文证明了常数深度与对数深度神经网络之间首个"算法可学习性"分离：作者构造了一类"分层楼梯函数"（及其具体子类"深度二次函数"），证明对数深度网络用逐层坐标下降即可在多项式时间/样本复杂度内学到误差 ε，而任何满足权重谱范数与激活正则性假设的常数深度、多项式宽度网络的 L² 逼近误差必然 ≥ 1/4（因为其可被 ≪d^{1/2} 次多项式逼近，无法捕获目标函数在 ≥d^{1/2} 阶上的 ≥1/3 Fourier 质量）。

> **TL;DR:** This is a pure theory paper (no experiments, datasets, or empirical baselines): it defines a class of "hierarchical staircase" Boolean functions whose Fourier spectrum is built by iteratively combining ≤D previously-constructed low-degree terms, shows a depth-(L+1) network trained by layerwise one-pass coordinate descent learns any such function to L² error ε* with sample size/runtime poly(d, L, m, 1/κ), and shows that any constant-depth, polynomial-width network with regular (tanh/sigmoid/softplus/GELU-type, but not ReLU) activations and spectrally-bounded weights can always be approximated by a degree-≪d^{1/2} polynomial — hence incurs L² error ≥ 1/4 on the explicit "deep quadratic function" subclass, which concentrates ≥1/3 of its Fourier mass at degree ≥ d^{1/2}.

## 核心贡献

- 首次给出常数深度网络与对数深度网络之间的**算法（可学习性）分离**，而非仅仅是表达能力（近似能力）分离；此前的算法分离结果只局限于两层/三层网络的比较。
- 提出"**分层楼梯函数**"（hierarchical staircase functions, 记号 HS_{L,D,m,κ}）：布尔函数的 Fourier 谱可以按层级递归构造——每层最多组合 D 个已有特征（谱集合的对称差）得到新的高阶项，谱宽度、局部度、信号强度均可参数化控制。该类函数比经典楼梯/合并楼梯函数（Abbe et al. 2021/2022/2023）更一般：允许阶数随深度指数增长，且不要求"相关坐标数为常数"。
- 给出该类函数的一个具体、易分析的见证子类——"**深度二次函数**"（deep quadratic functions）：通过递归地将坐标两两相乘、维度逐层减半来构造，恰好是深度 L*+1、局部度 2、宽度 d-1、信号强度 √(2/(L*d)) 的分层楼梯函数，且至少 1/3 的 Fourier 质量落在阶数 ≥ √d 的项上。
- 下界：证明任何满足"谱有界权重 + 充分正则激活函数（可解析延拓、多项式增长）"假设的常数深度、多项式宽度全连接网络，都可以被一个阶数为 (C_φ²C_W²)^{2^{O(L)}}(log d)^{2^{O(L)}} ≪ d^{1/2} 的多项式一致逼近（Lemma 2.2），从而在均匀分布下无法逼近深度二次函数（L² 误差下界 1/4，Theorem 2.1/4.1(a)）。
- 上界：给出一个 (L+1) 层网络架构（显式多项式特征映射 Φ + 稀疏特征选择算子 Sel），用逐层"一遍"坐标下降（Algorithm 1）训练，可在模型规模、样本复杂度、运行时间均为 d, L, m, 1/κ 多项式的情况下学到 HS_{L,D,m,κ} 中任意函数（Theorem 3.1），并可将训练好的网络编译回满足下界所设条件（层数除外）的标准架构（Appendix D）。

## Key Contributions

- First **algorithmic** (learnability, not just approximation) separation between constant-depth and logarithmic-depth networks; prior algorithmic separations were restricted to two- or three-layer comparisons (e.g., SL22, RZG23, NDL23).
- Introduces **hierarchical staircase functions** HS_{L,D,m,κ}: Boolean functions whose Fourier spectrum S_f is built inductively — at each of L levels, up to D previously-discovered low-degree Fourier sets can be symmetric-differenced together to form new higher-degree terms that survive if and only if they lie in S_f. This class strictly generalizes prior staircase/merged-staircase conditions by letting degree grow exponentially with depth and dropping the constant-relevant-coordinates restriction.
- Provides a clean, concrete witnessing subclass, **deep quadratic functions**, built by recursively pairing up coordinates and halving dimensionality across L* layers; shown to be a hierarchical staircase function with depth L*+1, local degree 2, width d−1, signal strength √(2/(L*d)), and Fourier mass ≥1/3 at degree ≥ √d (Fact 4.2).
- **Lower bound:** any constant-depth, polynomial-width network satisfying spectral weight-norm bounds and activation regularity (Assumption 2.1 — analytic continuation + polynomial growth, satisfied by tanh/sigmoid/softplus/GELU) can be uniformly approximated by a polynomial of degree ≪ d^{1/2} (Lemma 2.2), hence cannot approximate deep quadratic functions to L² error better than 1/4 (Theorem 2.1, 4.1(a)).
- **Upper bound:** an (L+1)-layer network with an explicit degree-D polynomial feature map plus a trainable sparse-selection readout, trained via layerwise one-pass coordinate descent (Algorithm 1), learns any function in HS_{L,D,m,κ} to L² error ε* with sample size, model size, and running time all polynomial in d, L, m, 1/κ (Theorem 3.1); it can be compiled into a network satisfying the lower bound's regularity conditions except for constant depth (Theorem 4.1(b), Appendix D).

## Methodology

**Learner for the lower bound (Section 2.1).** A standard L-layer fully-connected network x^{(l+1)} = φ_l(W^{(l)} x^{(l)}), f_L(x) = ⟨a, x^{(L+1)}⟩, subject to Assumption 2.1: (a) L = O(1) and all widths poly(d); (b) operator norms ‖W^{(l)}‖_op ≤ C_W and ‖a‖ ≤ 1; (c)/(d) each coordinatewise activation φ_{l,k} has bounded 0th/1st/2nd derivatives and, crucially, can be analytically continued to a complex strip {|Im z| < c_φ} with polynomial growth there — with C_W, C_φ ≤ d^{c0} for a universal constant c0 < 2^{-2L-7}/L. Lemma A.7 verifies this regularity condition for tanh, sigmoid, softplus, and GELU (notably **not** ReLU, which is not analytic).

**Lower-bound proof idea (Section 2.2).** Replace each activation by a Chebyshev polynomial approximant valid on the interval the true (and approximate) pre-activations concentrate on with high probability (using subgaussianity of intermediate representations, Lemma B.4/B.7). A naive global choice of interval length/degree across all L layers blows up doubly-exponentially in L because approximating φ_l on a length-R interval needs Chebyshev degree ≳ R log R, so the required interval length and failure-probability tradeoff becomes infeasible for L ≥ 2. The paper instead runs the argument **layer by layer, backward from layer L to layer 1**, balancing interval length, degree, and failure probability at each layer so that both stay polylogarithmic in d; this yields a final polynomial degree ≪ d^{1/2} (Lemma 2.2) which, by orthogonality of Fourier characters under Unif({±1}^d), cannot capture Ω(1) mass at degree ≥ d^{1/2}, giving the L² lower bound (Theorem 2.1).

**Learner for the upper bound (Section 3.1).** An L-layer network that at each layer keeps a "memory" vector y^{(l)} ∈ R^m of previously discovered Fourier features; the layer-l input is x^{(l)} = x ∘ y^{(l-1)} (original input concatenated with accumulated features); a degree-D polynomial feature map Φ enumerates all monomials of degree ≤ D over the d+m coordinates (M = Σ_{k≤D} C(d+m,k) of them); a trainable weight vector w^{(l)} multiplies these monomial features, and a hard "Sel" operator keeps only the (at most m) nonzero-weighted features as the new memory — implementing simultaneous feature selection and deduplication. Output is the scalar readout ⟨w^{(L)}, z^{(L)}⟩.

**Training (Algorithm 1).** Layerwise, one-pass coordinate descent: for layer l, draw N fresh i.i.d. samples, form the squared loss between f* and the current partial fit, and for each of the M candidate monomial features run T steps of 1-D coordinate gradient descent on its scalar weight, then hard-threshold small weights to zero (λ_w). This conceptually mirrors testing each candidate combination of ≤D existing features for membership in the target's Fourier spectrum S_* and discarding those outside it, so only polynomially many features are ever checked/stored despite the exponentially large space of possible monomials.

## Results & Highlights

No quantitative results reported — this is a purely theoretical paper: it contains no experiments, datasets, benchmarks, or empirical baselines of any kind (all "results" are formally proved theorems/lemmas). The concrete quantitative claims are the formal bounds themselves:

- **Lower bound (Theorem 2.1 / 4.1(a)):** for any Boolean f* with Σ_{|S|≥d^{1/2}} f̂*²(S) ≥ 1/3 (satisfied by deep quadratic functions, which have total Fourier mass ≥ 1/3 on terms of degree ≥ √d ≈ 2^{L*/2}), any network f satisfying Assumption 2.1 (constant depth, poly-width, spectrally-bounded weights, regular activations) has ‖f_L − f*‖_{L²(Unif({±1}^d))} ≥ 1/4, for all sufficiently large d.
- **Polynomial approximation lemma (Lemma 2.2):** any such constant-depth network can be ε*-approximated (ε* ≥ d^{-O(1)}) by a polynomial of degree ((C_φ²C_W²)^{2^{L+3}}(log d)^{2^L+2})^{L(2^L+1)} ≪ d^{1/2} — the crucial fact that lets the L² lower bound follow from Fourier-orthogonality alone (no low-degree polynomial can capture Ω(1) mass at degree ≥ d^{1/2} vs. a network limited to degree o(d^{1/2})).
- **Upper bound (Theorem 3.1):** for target accuracy ε* and failure probability δ_P, Algorithm 1 with N ≳ M²log(LM/δ_P)/(κ^{2D+2}(ε_*²/m ∧ κ^{2D+2})) samples per layer, step size η = 2^{-2D}, and T ≳ log(m/ε_* ∨ 1/κ)/(κ^{2D}η) coordinate-descent steps per feature achieves ‖f* − f‖_{L²} ≤ ε* with probability ≥ 1 − δ_P — all quantities polynomial in d, L, m, 1/κ for fixed local degree D = O(1).
- The paper explicitly frames these as the object of study; there is no comparison against, e.g., an actual trained ReLU MLP via SGD, no wall-clock/sample-efficiency plots, and no synthetic-experiment validation of the asymptotic predictions at finite, practical d.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** Genuine and well-motivated — algorithmic (vs. purely approximation-theoretic) depth separations are rare, and this is the first to compare constant depth against *logarithmic* depth (all prior algorithmic separations, e.g., SL22, RZG23, NDL23, compared 2- vs. 3-layer networks). The hierarchical staircase function class is a meaningful generalization of existing staircase/merged-staircase conditions (ABAB+21, AAM22, AAM23), notably removing the "constant number of relevant coordinates" restriction and letting Fourier degree grow exponentially with depth.

**Rigor:** The proofs are careful and extensive (44 pages of appendices covering Chebyshev approximation theory, sub-Gaussian tail bounds for intermediate representations, and a detailed layerwise induction). The paper is explicit and honest about the technical difficulty its layerwise (rather than naive global) chaining argument solves — a specific, paper-stated technical obstruction (naive global polynomial approximation requires degree R^L against subgaussian R^L-th moments that scale as e^{-Θ(R²)}, which is infeasible once L ≥ 2) is identified and resolved by balancing degree/interval/failure-probability backward from layer L to layer 1.

**Reproducibility:** Not applicable in the conventional sense — there is no code or data to reproduce, since the paper contains zero experiments. This is itself a limitation worth flagging: none of the asymptotic (poly(d), ≪d^{1/2}, etc.) claims are empirically sanity-checked even at small/moderate d, so it is unverified whether the predicted qualitative separation (log-depth succeeds, constant-depth fails) would be visible in practice at realistic network sizes.

**Significance / scope caveat (paper-specific):** The lower bound's activation regularity condition (Assumption 2.1(d): analytic continuation to a complex strip with polynomial growth, verified in Lemma A.7 only for tanh, sigmoid, softplus, GELU) explicitly **excludes ReLU**, the dominant activation function in practical deep learning — this is a real and non-trivial scope limitation, not a cosmetic one, since ReLU networks are not covered by the impossibility result at all. Additionally, the upper-bound "logarithmic-depth network" that succeeds is a fairly bespoke construction — an explicit degree-D polynomial feature map combined with a hard top-m feature-selection readout, trained by **layerwise, one-pass coordinate descent** rather than end-to-end backpropagation/SGD on a standard MLP — and the authors themselves flag this gap in the Conclusion ("An important direction is to extend this separation to end-to-end gradient-based training and more standard architectures"), which is an appropriately honest acknowledgment rather than overclaiming. The required exponent c0 in Assumption 2.1(a) must satisfy c0 < 2^{-2L-7}/L, shrinking rapidly with depth L — a restrictive quantitative regime for the weight/activation-regularity budget that the paper does not attempt to argue is realistic under standard training dynamics (e.g., μP feature learning), beyond citing that Xavier/He initialization satisfies it at initialization only.

**Red flags:** None suggesting misconduct or overclaiming — the paper is transparent about its assumptions, scope, and open directions (including its own AI-use disclosure). The main substantive caveat is the mismatch between the "network" architecture in the constructive upper bound (polynomial feature map + hard selection + layerwise coordinate descent) and the "network" architecture ruled out in the lower bound (standard MLP with smooth activations trained presumably by any method satisfying the stated norm/regularity conditions) — the separation is rigorous as stated, but readers should not conflate the upper-bound construction with a demonstration that ordinary SGD-trained deep ReLU MLPs outperform shallow ones on this task.

## Strengths

- First rigorous algorithmic (not just approximation-based) separation between constant- and logarithmic-depth networks, closing a real gap in depth-separation literature that previously only handled 2-3 layer comparisons.
- New, strictly more general function class (hierarchical staircase functions) with a clean, minimal witnessing example (deep quadratic functions) that is easy to state and analyze.
- Technically sophisticated, self-contained lower-bound proof (Chebyshev/Bernstein-ellipse polynomial approximation theory applied layerwise) that identifies and resolves a specific, clearly articulated failure mode of naive global approximation arguments.
- Careful, transparent situating of the work relative to closely related prior papers (ABAB+21, AAM22/23, RDKL26, WNL24/FWNL25, DPZK25, TDPK26), including an explicit statement of what is reused vs. newly proved.
- Explicit AI-use statement, disclosing use of GPT for preliminary/sanity-check calculations with independent human verification.

## Limitations

- Purely theoretical: no experiments, simulations, or empirical validation of any of the asymptotic predictions, even at small scale.
- Lower bound's activation-regularity assumption excludes ReLU and other non-smooth activations widely used in practice, limiting the practical reach of the impossibility result.
- The successful logarithmic-depth learner is trained via layerwise, one-pass coordinate descent on an explicit polynomial-feature-plus-selection architecture, not end-to-end gradient descent on a standard MLP — a gap to standard practice that the authors explicitly acknowledge as future work rather than resolve.
- Requires a rapidly shrinking regularity/norm-budget exponent c0 < 2^{-2L-7}/L as depth L grows, and other technical constraints (‖a‖ ≤ 1, bounded activation derivatives), whose realism under standard training dynamics is not empirically or theoretically justified beyond initialization-time guarantees.
- The local degree D (max features combined per hierarchical step) is fixed at O(1); the framework does not address what happens if D is allowed to grow with depth or log d.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the impossibility result be extended to cover ReLU networks, or does ReLU's non-analyticity fundamentally require a different proof technique (e.g., not based on Chebyshev/complex-analytic continuation)?
- Can the upper-bound learner be replaced by a standard MLP trained end-to-end via SGD/backpropagation while retaining polynomial sample/time complexity, as the authors suggest as future work?
- How tight is the depth-separation threshold in practice — would small-scale experiments (e.g., d = 2^L for L = 3,4,5) training real networks on deep quadratic functions empirically confirm the predicted qualitative gap between constant- and logarithmic-depth architectures?
- Does the shallow-to-deep chaining principle from RDKL26 generalize to give a continuous depth-vs-accuracy tradeoff curve rather than a binary constant-vs-log separation?
- Could allowing the local degree D to grow with depth (rather than being fixed O(1)) yield stronger separations or, conversely, break the polynomial learnability guarantee of Theorem 3.1?
- How restrictive is the shrinking regularity exponent c0 < 2^{-2L-7}/L in relation to what is actually observed for weight spectral norms during real (non-layerwise) training with modern initialization/feature-learning scalings (e.g., μP)?
