> **一句话总结：** 论文提出 noisyfloat，把科学计算中的 CESTAC 随机算术方法首次系统性地嵌入 PyTorch/NumPy/JAX/TensorFlow 计算图，用带直通估计器（STE）的算子级"有效数字"报告在训练/推理中定位数值不稳定源；在受控实验中，人为构造的不稳定算子（如未平移 softmax、级联抵消线性层）在 FP32 下被稳定报告为 0 位有效数字，而配对的稳定实现保留 5.9-7.0 位，对稳定/不稳定算子的判别达到 100% 的准确率/精确率/召回率，但代价是 Hilbert 行列式微基准上 127.6×（PyTorch）到 1124.3×（NumPy）的运行时开销——已超出该文引用的 CADNA 自身报告的 10×-100× 区间。

> **TL;DR:** noisyfloat is the first framework to integrate CESTAC-style stochastic arithmetic (3 synchronized rounding samples → estimated significant digits) directly into PyTorch/NumPy/JAX/TensorFlow tensor operators, with a straight-through estimator to keep the instrumented forward pass compatible with autograd. On matched stable/unstable operator pairs (softmax, LayerNorm, attention, convolution, linear, reduction), unstable variants are reported with ~0 significant digits vs. 3.5-6.5 digits for their stable counterparts in an FP32-like format (Table 3), and the resulting stable-vs-unstable classifier reaches perfect 1.000 accuracy/precision/recall. The catch: on a small Hilbert-determinant micro-benchmark, the instrumentation overhead is 127.6x on PyTorch and 1124.3x on NumPy relative to deterministic execution — exceeding the 10x-100x slowdown the paper itself cites for CADNA, the closest prior DSA tool, and there is no direct empirical comparison to CADNA/MCA/Verificarlo/Fuzzy PyTorch on the same tasks.

## 核心贡献

- 提出 noisyfloat：首个把 CESTAC（Control and Stochastic Estimation of Rounding Errors）随机算术方法整合进现代深度学习自动微分框架（PyTorch/NumPy/JAX/TensorFlow）的软件工具，用 backend-native 的随机化定向舍入代替修改硬件舍入模式。
- 设计"算子提升"（operator lifting）机制：每个标量/张量值被表示为 s 个（默认 3 个）同步随机实现的元组，为每个暴露的算术算子或神经网络算子（线性层、卷积、归一化、激活、注意力、循环层、reduction、损失）计算样本均值和基于 Student-t 的显著位数估计 C_Ȳ = log10(√s|Ȳ| / (τ_β σ_Y))。
- 引入两级插桩粒度：算术级（对每个暴露的 Python 算子舍入）与算子边界级（只对整个算子输出舍入，将 GEMM 类算子视为"可信"后端算子）；对 GEMM 类算子，当随机样本分离不足时注入受控的输入扰动（数据扰动安全阀），并给出两条 Lipschitz 型定理（Theorem 2、3）刻画该近似引入的显著位数估计误差上界。
- 提供直通估计器（NFloatSTE）使随机量化在反向传播中可用恒等映射近似梯度，从而不改变优化路径即可在训练循环中插入数值诊断（KernelReport + NFloatIterationTracker，记录每算子/每迭代的均值、最小/最大显著位数、失稳来源计数——抵消、不稳定除法/乘法/幂、内建函数不稳定、NaN/Inf）。
- 通过经典数值分析对照（Hilbert 行列式、Rump 多项式等）、六类受控神经算子配对基准、Fashion-MNIST/CIFAR-10/AG News 上的算子级"数值病理"注入实验，以及激活函数、归一化前后对比实验，系统评估该方法定位训练/推理中不稳定算子的能力。

## Key Contributions

- **First CESTAC-in-deep-learning integration.** noisyfloat lifts scalar/tensor values to tuples of `s` synchronous stochastic samples and applies randomized directed rounding at instrumented operator boundaries across NumPy, PyTorch, JAX, and TensorFlow backends, without touching hardware rounding modes.
- **Operator-level structured diagnostics.** A `KernelReport` API records per-operator, per-iteration average/min/max significant digits, stability flags, and CADNA-style source-of-instability counters (cancellation, unstable division/multiplication/power, intrinsic-function instability, NaN/Inf), aligned with how developers reason about neural-network operators rather than raw instructions.
- **STE-based compatibility with autodiff.** `NFloatSTE` propagates stochastic quantization in the forward pass while approximating its gradient as the identity, implemented via `torch.autograd.Function`, JAX custom VJPs, and `tf.custom_gradient`, so training loops need only wrap layers rather than be restructured.
- **Formal treatment of the GEMM approximation.** Because arithmetic fused inside vendor GEMM kernels cannot be intercepted, the paper treats matmul/conv/linear as "trusted" operators validated via input data perturbation, and proves two theorems (Lemma 1 + Theorems 2-3) bounding how much this surrogate can distort the CESTAC digit estimate, including a composition-level bound that can amplify through downstream non-linearities.
- **Controlled falsifiability tests.** Matched stable/unstable pairs across classical numerical analysis, six neural-operator families, and two pathology-injection studies (vision + text) are used to test whether the tool's digit reports actually separate known-good from known-bad implementations, rather than just reporting plausible-looking numbers.

## Methodology

noisyfloat formalizes a deep-learning program as a typed computational graph P = (G, Θ) whose nodes are scalar/array/neural-network operators. The stochastic program transformation T_{s,Q}(P) replaces each deterministic value x_v with a stochastic state X_v = (x_v^(1), ..., x_v^(s)) (default s=3, following CADNA convention) and lifts selected operators sample-wise: f̂(X_1,...,X_m) = (Q_r(f(x_1^(i),...,x_m^(i))))_{i=1}^s, where Q_r applies software-emulated randomized directed rounding to a simulated floating-point format (configurable exponent/significand bits, e.g., BF16/FP16/TF32/FP32/FP64). The representative value is the sample mean Ȳ, and the estimated significant digits are C_Ȳ = log10(√s|Ȳ| / (τ_β σ_Y)) using the sample standard deviation σ_Y and a Student-t critical value τ_β at a configured confidence level (0.95 in experiments).

Two instrumentation boundaries are supported: arithmetic-level (rounds after every supported Python-visible arithmetic op, reduction, normalization, activation, attention op) and operator-boundary (rounds only the output of a whole backend operator). Because GEMM-like operators (matmul, linear, convolution lowered to matrix multiplication) cannot be intercepted internally, they are treated as trusted backward-stable operators; when their stochastic operands are insufficiently separated (a per-entry ratio ρ_j(A) below a threshold η=2^-p), noisyfloat injects a controlled additive input perturbation ~U[-1,1] scaled to the significand precision before evaluating the operator sample-wise (Eq. 16-17). Lemma 1 and Theorem 2 give a local, submultiplicative-norm bound on how much this perturbation can change the CESTAC digit estimate for one GEMM output coordinate; Theorem 3 extends this to a composition-level "perturbation gap" when several trusted GEMM-like operators are embedded in a larger, possibly ill-conditioned, non-linear graph (via a locally-Lipschitz downstream-map assumption).

For gradient-based training, `NFloatShadowModel` copies the current deterministic parameters into a synchronized arithmetic-level stochastic shadow model that runs a diagnostic forward pass after each optimizer step (Eq. 26-27); the shadow pass never feeds back into the optimizer or the deterministic trajectory, so stochastic variability cannot alter the actual training run — it is purely a diagnostic instrument layered on top of ordinary training. Reports are exposed at the operator level (`KernelReport`, `get_kernel_reports`) and the iteration level (`NFloatIterationTracker`, exporting a time series of reports joinable with task metrics like loss/accuracy/BLEU). The public interface (`configure`, `NFloat`, `NFloatTensor`, `NFloatLinear/Conv2d/LayerNorm/Softmax/RNN/GRU/LSTM/MultiheadAttention`, `ArithmeticNFloat*` wrappers) lets users choose backend, simulated exponent/significand bits, sample count, diagnostics granularity, and whether STE is enabled.

## Results & Highlights

- **Classical numerical-stability benchmark (Table 2, four function pairs + Hilbert/Vandermonde/Rump controls).** In the FP32-like format (e,p)=(8,23), every cancellation-prone or ill-conditioned formulation (e.g., exp(x)-1 at x=1e-11, the naive quadratic root at b=-1e6, unpivoted Hilbert-matrix Gaussian elimination, the Rump polynomial P(10864,18817)) is reported with **0.000 significant digits**, while its algebraically stabilized counterpart (expm1(x), Vieta's formula, orthogonal rotation, Rump P(1/3,2/3)=65/81) retains **5.895-6.973 digits**, near the FP32 estimator ceiling of ~7.2 digits. In the FP64-like format (e,p)=(11,52) the same pattern holds at larger scale: unstable forms retain only **0-4.196 digits** vs. **14.638-15.000 digits** for stable forms (ceiling ~15.95).
- **Neural-operator detection (Table 3, arithmetic-level CESTAC, six operator families).** In the FP32-like format, naive (unshifted) softmax under large logits is reported with avg/min significant digits **0.000/0.000** (non-finite), vs. shifted softmax **3.527/3.487** and translation-invariant log-softmax **3.666/3.620** on the same inputs; cancelling linear map **0.000/0.000** vs. orthogonal linear map **4.723/3.880**; alternating-cancellation reduction sum **0.662/0.424** vs. positive-only reduction sum **6.405/5.989**. Using a digit threshold (γ=3 for FP32-like, γ=10 for FP64-like), the paper reports the resulting stable-vs-unstable classifier reaches **accuracy = precision = recall = 1.000** across all six families and both precisions.
- **Instrumentation overhead (Hilbert-determinant micro-benchmark, Fig. 3-4, n=11, s=3, 3 repeats).** On NumPy, deterministic mean runtime is 1.86e-4 s vs. 2.09e-1 s instrumented — a **1124.3x** overhead; on PyTorch, deterministic 7.84e-3 s vs. instrumented 1.00 s — a **127.6x** overhead. The paper explicitly contrasts this with CADNA's own reported **10x-100x** DSA self-validation slowdown (refs [33, 35]), meaning noisyfloat's NumPy-backend overhead in this benchmark exceeds the upper end of the range CADNA itself reports.
- **Activation-function reliability vs. accuracy decoupling (Fig. 12, Fashion-MNIST, 3 epochs).** ReLU's average hidden-activation significant digits rise from about **9.2 to 10.5** across the three epochs, while sigmoid and PReLU plateau around **7.6-7.8 digits** — even though all nine tested activations (ReLU, LeakyReLU, PReLU, ELU, GELU, SiLU, Swish, tanh, sigmoid) reach comparable classification accuracy, illustrating that task accuracy alone does not expose operator-level numerical reliability differences.
- **Normalization digit loss (Fig. 13, Fashion-MNIST).** Pre-normalization hidden-linear output averages **6.45-7.05** significant digits vs. post-normalization output averaging only **5.84-6.30** digits; BatchNorm shows the largest reduction (~7.05 → ~6.00), while RMSNorm retains the most digits post-transformation among the three normalizers (attributed to it never subtracting an explicit mean), even though the three normalized classifiers reach comparable final accuracy.
- **Decision-level localization (Figs. 9-11, Fashion-MNIST/CIFAR-10/AG News pathology studies).** Injecting a single unstable operator (cancelling linear map, near-constant LayerNorm, near-tie attention gate, naive exponential attention, or reciprocal gate) into an otherwise ordinary classifier reliably produces the lowest operator-level digit reports among six variants; near-constant LayerNorm shows the tool's key nuance — the operator itself is numerically unreliable, but only batches with small downstream logit margins actually flip the stochastic top-1 class, demonstrating that operator-level digit loss and task-level decision instability are related but distinct signals.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core novelty is engineering integration (CESTAC-style stochastic arithmetic wired natively into PyTorch/JAX/TensorFlow tensor operators with an STE for autograd compatibility) rather than a new numerical-validation theory; the paper is explicit that it "complements existing stochastic-arithmetic systems rather than replacing them." The genuinely new theoretical content is narrow but real: Theorems 2-3 give Lipschitz-based bounds on the digit-estimate distortion introduced specifically by the GEMM input-perturbation surrogate, which is a sensible and non-trivial addition given that GEMM kernels cannot be instrumented internally.

**Rigor:** The controlled-pair methodology (matched stable/unstable formulations across classical numerical analysis and six neural-operator families) is a genuinely falsifiable test design, and the perfect 1.000 accuracy/precision/recall result in Table 3 is a clean, specific, paper-grounded validation. However, this is also a self-constructed benchmark where the "ground truth" instability labels are inferred from the same controlled construction being tested — there is no independent, blinded, or adversarial test of the detector, and the digit threshold (γ=3 or γ=10) is chosen post hoc to make the classification work rather than derived independently.

**Reproducibility:** Code is public, configuration snippets are shown throughout (Fig. 2, 5-7), and the experimental setup (single A100 MIG instance, PyTorch 2.12.0/CUDA 13.0, fixed seeds) is documented reasonably well. The overhead numbers, though, are reported for only one small scalar micro-benchmark (n=11 Hilbert determinant); no runtime/memory overhead numbers are given for the actual Fashion-MNIST/CIFAR-10/AG News training experiments, so a reader cannot verify how the 127x-1124x figure translates to batched GPU tensor workloads.

**Significance:** The tool addresses a real, underserved gap (there is no existing tool that does operator-level CESTAC diagnostics natively inside PyTorch/JAX training loops), and the decoupling of operator-level digit loss from decision-level agreement (Section 4.3-4.4) is a useful conceptual contribution for practitioners debugging mixed-precision instabilities. But the reported overhead (at minimum 3x from sample tripling, empirically over 100x-1000x in the one concrete benchmark given) plus the explicit admission that GEMM-like operators — the most compute-dominant part of any deep network — are only approximately validated via a data-perturbation surrogate, together limit the tool's applicability to targeted debugging/calibration-subset use rather than full-scale training-time monitoring, which the paper itself acknowledges in its Limitations section.

**Red flags:** (1) No direct empirical comparison against the closest prior tools named in Related Work — CADNA, Monte Carlo Arithmetic, Verificarlo, or Fuzzy PyTorch — on the same benchmarks; the CADNA overhead comparison is qualitative (citing CADNA's own reported range) rather than a controlled head-to-head. (2) All deep-learning experiments use deliberately toy budgets (2,048-example subsets, ≤32 optimizer steps/epoch, 3 epochs, hidden dims of 128/256) — the paper is upfront that "our objective is not to achieve state-of-the-art task performance," which is honest but means claims about the tool's practical utility at production training scale remain unvalidated. (3) The paper is single-authored and self-described as still in progress, which is unusual for an arXiv posting and suggests the current version may not have undergone independent review.

## Strengths

- First native integration of CESTAC-style stochastic arithmetic into mainstream autodiff frameworks (PyTorch/JAX/TensorFlow/NumPy), with an STE that keeps the instrumented forward pass gradient-compatible without restructuring training loops.
- Well-designed controlled falsifiability experiments (matched stable/unstable operator pairs) spanning classical numerical analysis, six neural-operator families, and two independent pathology-injection studies (vision + text), yielding a clean, specific, perfect-classification result (Table 3).
- Honest formal treatment of the GEMM approximation gap (Theorems 2-3) rather than silently glossing over the fact that fused backend kernels cannot be instrumented at instruction level.
- Practically useful conceptual distinction between operator-level significant-digit loss and task-level decision instability (agreement-rate metric), showing that not all digit loss propagates to the final prediction.
- Public code release and reasonably detailed configuration examples for practitioners to reproduce the tool's usage patterns.

## Limitations

- Reported instrumentation overhead (127.6x on PyTorch, 1124.3x on NumPy in the one concrete micro-benchmark) is large and, by the paper's own account, likely higher in practice than the theoretical 3x lower bound from sample tripling; the paper's mitigation ("apply to a representative calibration subset") is suggested but not empirically validated.
- GEMM-like operators (matmul, linear, convolution) — arguably the most compute- and precision-sensitive part of any deep network — are not truly instrumented at arithmetic level; they rely on a "trusted operator + input perturbation" surrogate whose approximation gap (Theorem 3) can be large when downstream composition is ill-conditioned or non-smooth.
- All deep-learning validation experiments are small-scale toy setups (2,048-example subsets, at most 32 optimizer steps/epoch, 3 epochs total), explicitly not intended to reach state-of-the-art task performance, so generalization to production-scale LLM or vision training is untested.
- No direct quantitative comparison against CADNA, Monte Carlo Arithmetic, Verificarlo, or Fuzzy PyTorch on shared benchmarks — only narrative/qualitative positioning in Related Work.
- Fused/vendor backend kernels (e.g., fused CUDA ops) remain entirely outside the instrumentation boundary, a fundamental blind spot the authors acknowledge but do not resolve.
- The paper is a self-described work in progress by a single author, with no stated peer-review status at the time of this arXiv version.

## Relevance

对关注混合精度训练、算子级数值误差溯源与数值稳定内核设计的研究者，这篇论文提供了一个可直接嵌入 PyTorch/JAX 训练循环的诊断工具与"算子显著位数 vs. 决策级一致性"的解耦分析视角，具有较高的方法论参考价值；但其数百倍到千倍的运行时开销与对 GEMM 类算子的近似处理，意味着它目前更适合小规模校准调试而非生产级训练的实时监控。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How would noisyfloat's detection accuracy and overhead scale to production-size training (billions of parameters, thousands of steps) rather than the 2,048-example/3-epoch toy setups used here?
- Can the GEMM data-perturbation safeguard be replaced by genuine instruction-level instrumentation (e.g., custom low-level kernels) to close the approximation gap formalized in Theorem 3, particularly for ill-conditioned downstream compositions?
- What calibration-subset selection strategy would best trade detection accuracy against the reported 127x-1124x overhead, and has this trade-off been empirically characterized beyond the paper's brief recommendation?
- How does noisyfloat compare quantitatively — not just narratively — to CADNA, Fuzzy PyTorch, and Verificarlo on the same benchmark suite, in both detection accuracy and runtime cost?
- Could the significant-digit / decision-agreement framework be extended from a purely diagnostic tool into an actionable precision-tuning loop that automatically suggests where to upcast precision or reformulate an unstable operator?
