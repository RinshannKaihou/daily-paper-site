> **一句话总结：** 论文把SAM的内层扰动几何和外层更新规则解耦研究，提出对矩阵型隐藏层参数按谱范数（Ortho/Newton-Schulz正交化）逐层扰动的SpecSAM，发现它与Muon外层更新搭配时收益最大——在ImageNet-1K上ViT-Small/16达到80.23% Top-1（比不加SAM的Muon基线74.95%高5.28点，比同样谱扰动配AdamW的78.71%高1.52点），ResNet-50上达到78.55%（比SAM-SGDW的77.99%和SAM-Muon的77.84%都更高）。

> **TL;DR:** This paper decouples SAM's inner perturbation geometry from its outer optimizer and introduces SpecSAM, a layerwise spectral-norm inner perturbation (via Newton-Schulz orthogonalization, mirroring Muon's own update rule) for matrix-valued hidden layers, paired with either AdamW/SGDW or Muon in the outer step. On ImageNet-1K, SpecSAM-Muon reaches 80.23% Val Top-1 on ViT-Small/16 (vs. 74.95% for unperturbed Muon, 79.17% for Euclidean SAM-AdamW, and 78.71% for SpecSAM-AdamW — the spectral inner step actually performs worst, 78.71%, when paired with AdamW) and 78.55% on ResNet-50 (vs. 77.99% for SAM-SGDW and 77.05% for unperturbed Muon baseline). A layerwise-Frobenius ablation (LayerSAM) that isolates per-layer budgeting from the spectral norm itself shows the gain is specifically due to spectral geometry, not just layerwise perturbation budgeting: LayerSAM-Muon only reaches 77.49%, below plain SAM-Muon (77.84%).

## 核心贡献

- 提出一个"范数无关"的SAM抽象模板（Algorithm 1），将内层扰动几何（用什么范数定义"小扰动"）与外层更新映射（如何把扰动后梯度转成参数更新）显式解耦，从而可以独立组合两者进行系统性研究。
- 引入逐层（layerwise）扰动几何：把参数空间显式分解为矩阵块 $W^{(\ell)}$ 与非矩阵块 $v$，用 $\ell_r$ 聚合不同层的扰动范数（式6），并特别关注 $r=\infty$（逐层独立分配扰动预算）的情形。
- 提出SpecSAM：对矩阵型隐藏层用谱范数定义扰动（其对偶为核范数，最陡上升方向即梯度的正交化 Ortho(·)，用Muon同款的k步Newton-Schulz迭代近似），对非矩阵参数仍用欧氏范数，两者用单独的扰动半径 $\rho_m,\rho_n$（式9）。
- 通过2×2的实验设计（内层：无/欧氏SAM/谱SpecSAM；外层：AdamW-SGDW/Muon）在ImageNet-1K（ViT-Small/16、ResNet-50）上系统比较，发现内外层几何存在交互效应：谱扰动只有配Muon外层时才最有效，配AdamW反而是最弱的SAM变体；欧氏扰动则对内外层选择相对不敏感（增益近似恒定叠加）。
- 通过CIFAR-100上的半径扫描、有效秩（effective rank）诊断和事后锐度测量，以及LayerSAM消融（把SpecSAM的谱范数换成Frobenius范数但保留逐层预算分配）分离"逐层预算分配"与"谱范数本身"两种效应，证明SpecSAM-Muon的优势确实来自谱几何，而不仅仅来自逐层扰动分配；并观察到Muon外层更新始终维持更高的权重/梯度/特征有效秩，可能是它能吸收更强谱扰动而不损失表征能力的原因。

## Key Contributions

- **A norm-agnostic SAM template.** Algorithm 1 formalizes SAM as: compute an inner steepest-ascent perturbation $\varepsilon_t \in \partial\|DL(\theta_t)\|_{\text{in},*}$, form the perturbed signal $\phi_t = DL(\theta_t + \rho_t\varepsilon_t)$, then apply an outer direction map $\mathcal{D}_t: \Theta^* \to \Theta$ (AdamW/SGDW or Muon) to get the update. This cleanly separates "what geometry defines a small perturbation" from "what optimizer converts the perturbed gradient into a step," letting the paper vary each independently (Section 2.1).
- **Layerwise spectral inner perturbation (SpecSAM).** Parameters are decomposed into matrix blocks $W^{(\ell)}$ and a non-matrix block $v$; for matrix blocks the spectral norm ($\|\cdot\|_{2\to2}$) is used, whose dual-norm subgradient is the polar factor $\text{Ortho}(G^{(\ell)})$ — approximated in practice by a $k$-step Newton-Schulz iteration, the same primitive Muon uses for its own update (Eq. 8-9). Non-matrix parameters keep a separately-radius-scaled Euclidean perturbation.
- **Systematic 2×2(×architecture) grid on ImageNet-1K.** Two architectures (ViT-Small/16, 300 epochs; ResNet-50, 120 epochs), inner geometry ∈ {none, Euclidean/SAM, Spectral/SpecSAM}, outer optimizer ∈ {AdamW/SGDW, Muon}, evaluated over 3 seeds with statistical (Welch t-test) comparison (Table 2).
- **Key empirical finding: inner/outer geometry interaction.** Euclidean inner perturbations shift both outer optimizers by a near-constant amount (+4.9 for AdamW, +5.0 for Muon on ViT), i.e., additive and outer-optimizer-agnostic. The spectral inner step instead interacts strongly with the outer step: it amplifies Muon's advantage (+5.28 pts over unperturbed Muon on ViT) but is the single weakest SAM variant when paired with AdamW (78.71%, below even Euclidean SAM-AdamW's 79.17%).
- **LayerSAM ablation isolates the source of the gain.** By keeping SpecSAM's layerwise $\ell_\infty$ budget allocation but replacing the spectral block norm with the (self-dual) Frobenius norm, LayerSAM disentangles "per-layer perturbation budgeting" from "spectral geometry." On ResNet-50, LayerSAM-Muon (77.49%) trails plain SAM-Muon (77.84%) and is well below SpecSAM-Muon (78.55%), showing per-layer budgeting alone does not explain SpecSAM's gain — the spectral norm itself, combined with a matrix-aware outer step, is required (Table 11, Appendix B.2).
- **Rank and sharpness diagnostics on CIFAR-100 (ViT-Tiny/4).** Muon-based outer updates maintain higher weight, gradient, and feature effective rank across the perturbation-radius sweep than AdamW-based variants, which the authors suggest may explain why Muon variants can profitably use larger perturbation radii without collapsing representational capacity (Section 4.2, Figure 3, Appendix C.3.1). Separately, for a fixed generalization gap, SpecSAM variants find sharper minima under both Euclidean and spectral sharpness measures — evidence the spectral perturbation is a more aggressive explicit regularizer of exactly the quantities it penalizes (Appendix C.3.2, Figure 4).

## Methodology

The paper's core theoretical move is a first-order derivation of SAM's robust-loss surrogate for a general (possibly non-Euclidean, possibly non-differentiable) inner norm $\|\cdot\|_{\text{in}}$: the robust loss $L_\rho(\theta) = \sup_{\|\varepsilon\|_{\text{in}}\le\rho} L(\theta+\rho\varepsilon)$ is linearized via the Fréchet differential $DL(\theta)$, giving $L_\rho(\theta) = L(\theta) + \rho\|DL(\theta)\|_{\text{in},*} + o(\rho)$ (Eq. 3), motivating the standard stop-gradient SAM surrogate evaluated at the first-order adversarial perturbation $\varepsilon^*(\theta) \in \partial\|DL(\theta)\|_{\text{in},*}$ (Eq. 2, 4).

Building on this, the parameter space is explicitly decomposed layerwise: $\Theta = \mathbb{R}^{m_1\times m_2} \times \cdots \times \mathbb{R}^{m_{L-1}\times m_L} \times \mathbb{R}^q$, with block norms $\|\cdot\|_{(\ell)}$ on each matrix block and an $\ell_r$-aggregation across blocks (Eq. 6). The paper focuses on $r=\infty$ aggregation (motivated by Muon), under which the steepest-ascent perturbation decomposes independently per block: for the spectral block norm the dual is the nuclear norm, whose canonical subgradient at a full-rank matrix is the polar factor $\text{Ortho}(G^{(\ell)}) = U_rV_r^\top$ from the (reduced) SVD $G^{(\ell)} = U_r\Sigma_rV_r^\top$ — practically approximated by Muon's $k$-step Newton-Schulz iteration (3 steps for the main ImageNet experiments, 5 for CIFAR-100). This gives the idealized SpecSAM perturbation $\varepsilon^*(\theta) = (\text{Ortho}(G^{(1)}),\ldots,\text{Ortho}(G^{(L-1)}), g(\theta)/\|g(\theta)\|_2)$ (Eq. 8), refined in practice to use two separately-tuned radii $\rho_m$ (matrix blocks) and $\rho_n = \alpha_g\rho_m$ (non-matrix blocks) (Eq. 9).

The outer step routes matrix-valued hidden-layer parameters through Muon's momentum-then-orthogonalize update ($M_{t+1} = \beta M_t + (1-\beta)G_t$, $W_{t+1} = W_t - \eta_t\text{Ortho}(M_{t+1})$) while remaining (non-matrix, e.g. embeddings, classification head, first conv layer) parameters go through AdamW/SGDW — the standard "hybrid routing" used in practice for Muon. Four main method combinations result: SAM-AdamW/SGDW, SAM-Muon, SpecSAM-AdamW/SGDW, SpecSAM-Muon, plus the two unperturbed baselines and a LayerSAM control (Table 1).

Experiments use m-sharpness (per-GPU independent perturbation, gradient accumulated only at the outer step) across 8 H100 GPUs, with hyperparameters (learning rate, weight decay, then perturbation radii) tuned via a two-stage grid search at half the training budget (150 epochs for ViT-Small/16, 60 for ResNet-50) to avoid confounding SAM gains with unrelated LR/WD effects. Total compute for the reported ImageNet experiments was ~4000 GPU-hours (ViT) + 2000 GPU-hours (ResNet), and ~10,000 GPU-hours including preliminary/diagnostic work, on the Isambard-AI National AI Research Resource.

## Results & Highlights

- **ViT-Small/16, ImageNet-1K Val Top-1 (Table 2, 3 seeds):** SpecSAM-Muon 80.23±0.06% is the best overall, vs. SAM-Muon 79.97±0.06%, SAM-AdamW 79.17±0.35%, SpecSAM-AdamW 78.71±0.08% (the weakest SAM variant), unperturbed Muon 74.95±0.16%, and unperturbed AdamW 74.26±0.09%. The spectral inner step gives +5.28 points over Muon but only +4.45 over AdamW — i.e., the spectral perturbation is markedly more effective specifically when paired with a matrix-aware (Muon) outer step.
- **ResNet-50, ImageNet-1K Val Top-1 (Table 2):** SpecSAM-Muon 78.55±0.06% is best, ahead of SAM-SGDW 77.99±0.34%, SAM-Muon 77.84±0.03%, SpecSAM-SGDW 77.77±0.15%, SGDW baseline 77.35±0.07%, and Muon baseline 77.05±0.07%. Notably, without any inner perturbation, Muon (77.05%) actually trails the plain SGDW baseline (77.35%) — the spectral inner step is what flips the ordering in Muon's favor (+1.50 pts for Muon vs. only +0.42 for SGDW).
- **ImageNet-R (out-of-distribution) and ReaL accuracy:** SpecSAM-Muon attains the best ImageNet-ReaL accuracy on both architectures (86.26±0.08% ViT, 84.76±0.10% ResNet) and leads on ImageNet-R for ResNet-50 (38.12±0.45%, vs. 37.92±0.23% for Muon-baseline and 37.50±0.34% for SAM-SGDW). On ViT, SpecSAM-Muon numerically leads ImageNet-R too (42.18±0.24% vs. 41.68±0.56% SAM-Muon, 39.17±1.41% SAM-AdamW), but the authors explicitly caveat that at 3 seeds the top methods are not statistically distinguishable there — a rare, honest acknowledgment of a claim that doesn't fully hold up.
- **LayerSAM ablation isolates the spectral-norm effect (Table 11, ResNet-50):** replacing SpecSAM's spectral block norm with the Frobenius norm while keeping the same layerwise $\ell_\infty$ budgeting drops accuracy from 78.55% (SpecSAM-Muon) to 77.49% (LayerSAM-Muon) — below even plain SAM-Muon (77.84%) — showing the gain is attributable to the spectral norm itself, not merely to per-layer perturbation-budget reallocation.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core idea — applying a spectral-norm (Muon-style orthogonalized) perturbation as SAM's inner step, rather than only as an outer optimizer — is a natural but previously unstudied combination; the paper explicitly and correctly distinguishes itself from "Spectral-SAM" [39], a different prior work that projects weights onto spectral-norm balls for adversarial robustness rather than modifying SAM's perturbation geometry. The layerwise $\ell_r$-aggregation framework (Eq. 6) generalizing existing SAM variants (ASAM, Fisher/Functional SAM) is a clean unifying contribution, though it is more of an organizing abstraction than a technically deep innovation.

**Rigor:** Generally strong for an academic-scale study — 3-seed statistical testing, disclosed grid searches, and a genuinely informative ablation (LayerSAM) that most SAM papers would skip. However, one notable weak point: the ImageNet-R comparison is explicitly flagged by the authors themselves as not statistically resolvable across the top three methods, yet the abstract/main narrative still emphasizes SpecSAM-Muon as "best" without heavily qualifying this on the OOD benchmark. The single most important number (SpecSAM-AdamW being the *worst* SAM variant on ViT, at 78.71%, actually below plain SAM-AdamW's 79.17%) is a striking negative result the authors report honestly rather than downplaying — a good sign of intellectual honesty.

**Reproducibility:** High — full architecture specs (Tables 3-4), training protocol, hyperparameter search grids (Tables 5-8), and selected hyperparameters (Tables 7-8) are all given in the appendix, and models are trained from scratch on public ImageNet-1K/CIFAR-100 without proprietary data or undisclosed tricks.

**Significance:** Practically useful as a reference for practitioners considering Muon for matrix-shaped parameters, but the paper is explicitly exploratory/diagnostic rather than proposing one method to be adopted — the authors state directly that their "aim is not to advocate a single geometric algorithm." The interaction mechanism (why spectral inner geometry pairs specifically with Muon) is only explained by a correlational rank-diagnostic observation (Muon sustains higher effective rank, Section 4.2/Appendix C.3.1), not a theoretical account — this is acknowledged as an open question ("not settled by our experiments... a proper account is left to future work").

**Red flags / weak baselines:** The baselines used are reasonable (AdamW/SGDW, standard SAM), not artificially weak. However: (1) only two architectures and one task family (ImageNet-1K classification) are tested — the authors admit the extension to language modeling, where Muon is most widely deployed in practice, "remains untested"; (2) results rely on only 3 seeds for the main ImageNet claims and single runs for some diagnostics, limiting the precision of significance claims; (3) the m-sharpness approximation (per-GPU independent perturbation) is a standard but non-trivial approximation whose interaction with the spectral geometry claims isn't separately ablated; (4) the CIFAR-100 diagnostic experiments use a much smaller ViT-Tiny/4 model, so generalizability of the rank/sharpness mechanistic story to the main ImageNet-scale results is inferential, not directly verified at scale.

## Strengths

- Clean, general norm-agnostic SAM formalization (Algorithm 1) that cleanly separates inner geometry from outer optimizer, useful as a reusable analytical lens beyond this paper.
- Well-designed ablation (LayerSAM) that isolates spectral-norm effects from layerwise-budgeting effects — many empirical optimizer papers skip this kind of decomposition.
- Honest reporting of a negative/counter-intuitive result (SpecSAM-AdamW as the weakest SAM variant) rather than cherry-picking only favorable comparisons.
- Statistically grounded comparisons (Welch t-tests, 3 seeds) and full hyperparameter disclosure, supporting reproducibility.
- Multiple complementary diagnostics (effective rank, finite-radius sharpness measurement) that attempt to explain, not just report, the inner/outer interaction.

## Limitations

- Limited to image classification (ImageNet-1K, CIFAR-100) with two architectures; no language-modeling experiments, where Muon is most heavily used in current practice (explicitly acknowledged by the authors).
- Only 3 seeds for main results, single runs for some diagnostics/ablations, and non-exhaustive hyperparameter search due to compute constraints (explicitly acknowledged).
- Mechanistic explanation for why spectral inner geometry pairs better with Muon is only suggestive (correlated with effective rank retention), not established causally or theoretically.
- SpecSAM adds Newton-Schulz overhead in the inner step (on top of Muon's own Newton-Schulz in the outer step) and requires two backward passes per step like standard SAM; the paper compares methods at equal epoch budgets rather than equal wall-clock time, so real-world throughput tradeoffs are not directly reported.
- ImageNet-R ordering among top methods is not statistically resolved across 3 seeds, weakening the OOD generalization claim.

## Relevance

对于关注优化器设计（尤其是Muon/矩阵感知优化）与SAM泛化正则化交叉领域的研究者，本文提供了少见的系统性内外层几何解耦实验和诚实的消融结果，可作为后续设计"谱几何感知SAM"或探究Muon为何偏好特定扰动几何的重要参考基线。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Why does spectral inner geometry specifically amplify Muon's outer-step advantage while actively hurting AdamW (making SpecSAM-AdamW the weakest SAM variant on ViT)? Is the correlated effective-rank observation causal, and can it be tested by directly intervening on rank (e.g., rank-constrained training) rather than only observing it?
- Does the SpecSAM + Muon combination transfer to language modeling / transformer pretraining at scale, where Muon is currently most adopted, and where matrix shapes and update dynamics (e.g., attention vs. MLP blocks) differ substantially from vision backbones?
- Would using a finite aggregation exponent ($r=2$ rather than $r=\infty$) to couple per-layer perturbation budgets — a direction the authors flag as future work — change the inner/outer interaction pattern, or would it dilute the spectral-geometry benefit currently attributed to $r=\infty$'s independent per-layer allocation?
- Given that both spectral inner-step variants achieve lower train accuracy than their Euclidean counterparts at matched validation accuracy, does spectral geometry provide a fundamentally stronger implicit regularizer, or could this reflect optimization difficulty (e.g., a harder loss landscape under the Newton-Schulz-approximated orthogonalization) that a longer schedule or different radius/step-size configuration could close?
- Could scheduling or interpolating between Euclidean and spectral inner geometries over training (mentioned as "anecdotal evidence" in preliminary experiments) yield further gains beyond either fixed geometry, and what would a principled schedule look like?
