> **一句话总结：** 论文将欧氏空间中的"active subspaces"（活跃子空间）严格推广到黎曼流形上，提出通过平行输运把各点的黎曼梯度搬运到一个中心切空间再取二阶矩张量（内蕴构造 $G_0$），并证明它与"先嵌入到环境空间取平均、再投影"的外蕴做法（$C_\iota$）在以测地球半径 $R$ 为尺度下只在 $O(R^2)$ 阶上一致、主特征子空间在谱隙 $\eta$ 下以 $O(R^2/\eta)$ 一致，同时在单位超球（正是 Kendall 形状预形空间）上给出严格闭式对照，并用 2-球面数值算例验证了理论预测的 $O(R^2)$ 脊恢复速率与 $O(N^{-1/2})$ 蒙特卡洛收敛速率。

> **TL;DR:** This paper generalizes Constantine's Euclidean active-subspace construction to Riemannian manifolds by composing the averaged gradient-outer-product with parallel transport to a single central tangent space, defining "active manifold-geodesics" (AMG) as the resulting eigenvalue-ordered geodesic directions (Def. 3). It rigorously contrasts this intrinsic construction against the natural extrinsic alternative — averaging ambient gradients under an isometric embedding and projecting the dominant eigenvector afterward — proving the two central-tangent-space representations agree only to $O(R^2)$ in the geodesic-ball radius $R$ (Prop. 3), with dominant eigenspaces agreeing to $O(R^2/\eta)$ under a spectral gap $\eta$ (Davis-Kahan), and deriving an exact closed-form projection-transport identity on unit hyperspheres (Lemma 2, relevant to Kendall's landmark and elastic-curve preshape spaces). On synthetic 2-sphere ridge/non-ridge functions (no real dataset; the "benchmark" is the author's own toy suite of linear/quadratic/nonlinear test functions, Fig. 11), numerical experiments confirm the predicted $O(R^2)$ ridge-recovery rate — measured RMS-deviation scaling exponents of ≈2.1 (linear ridge) and ≈1.9-2.0 (quadratic/nonlinear ridge) versus ≈1.0 for a genuine non-ridge function that lacks the assumed spectral gap (Figs. 6-7) — and an $O(N^{-1/2})$ Monte Carlo convergence of the approximated dominant eigenspace to the true ambient ridge direction $\pi_0(\boldsymbol a)$ (Fig. 8), while also exhibiting a documented failure mode ("projected dominance is not dominant projection," Thm. 1/Remark 7) where the naive extrinsic estimate degenerates to zero when the central point aligns with the true ridge direction ($\widehat x_0=\pm\boldsymbol a$, Fig. 9) even though the intrinsic construction remains well-defined.

## 核心贡献

- 提出**内蕴（intrinsic）黎曼平均张量积** $G_0$（式 12）：把每点的黎曼梯度沿唯一测地线平行输运到一个中心切空间 $T_{p_0}\mathcal M$ 后再做二阶矩平均，证明其特征值精确保留"沿平行输运特征向量场的均方方向导数"这一可解释含义（Lemma 1），完全类比欧氏 active subspaces 的特征值解释（式 5）。
- 定义**活跃流形—测地线（Active Manifold-Geodesics, AMG）**（Def. 3）：由 $G_0$ 特征分解给出、按特征值降序排列的一组测地方向，构成中心切空间邻域内的正交标架场，是欧氏活跃子空间在流形上的自然、可辨识（而非病态的空间填充式）推广。
- 提出并严格分析**外蕴（extrinsic）对照构造** $C_\iota$（式 16，即先在环境欧氏空间中平均梯度外积、再投影到中心切空间的常见流形学习做法），证明其在中心切空间上的表示恰好等于"先做中心投影、再取二阶矩"的结果（定理 1），但特征值排序与投影不可交换——"投影后的主导方向"不等于"主导方向的投影"（Remark 7），并用球面上一个显式退化反例（中心点与真实脊方向重合时外蕴估计塌缩为零，图 9）证明这一点。
- 给出内蕴与外蕴两种构造的**精确二阶一致性定理**（Prop. 3）：在半径 $R$（小于单射半径）的测地球内，两者的特征值差为 $O(R^2)$，谱隙 $\eta$ 存在时主特征子空间的差为 $O(R^2/\eta)$（Davis-Kahan）；并在单位超球上给出显式闭式恒等式（Lemma 2：投影 = 平行输运再做 $\cos d$ 径向收缩），常数可精确取 $C=1+R^2/4$。
- 将理论直接对接**统计形状分析的预形空间（preshape spaces）**：Kendall 的关键点预形（去中心化+尺度归一化的平面点位形）与长度归一化弹性曲线的方向函数表示均是（有限维）超球，因此本文关于超球的全部封闭式结果可直接用于形状响应函数（如气动阻力对形状的敏感性）的降维，作者将此列为后续工作方向。

## Key Contributions

- **Intrinsic parallel-transport gradient outer product $G_0$ (Eq. 12).** Defines $G_0 := \int_{\mathcal X} \mathcal P_p[\nabla_{\mathcal M}f(p)] \otimes \mathcal P_p[\nabla_{\mathcal M}f(p)]\, d\mu(p)$, where $\mathcal P_p$ is radial parallel transport along the unique geodesic from $p$ to a central point $p_0$. Lemma 1 proves the eigenvalues of $G_0$ are *exact* mean-squared directional derivatives of $f$ along the transported eigenvector field (Eq. 13) — an exact rather than approximate generalization of the Euclidean active-subspace eigenvalue interpretation (Eq. 5).
- **Active Manifold-Geodesics (Def. 3).** The eigendecomposition $G_0 = W_0\Lambda_0 W_0^\top$ yields an ordered set of geodesics $\exp_{p_0}(t\boldsymbol w_i)$ — a low-complexity, identifiable analog of a ridge subspace that explicitly avoids the ill-posedness of unconstrained single-curve nonlinear reduction methods (active manifolds, neural active manifolds), which the paper proves (Appendix B, via space-filling-curve and Frobenius/Chow-Rashevskii arguments) can only either foliate the domain or force $f$ constant.
- **Extrinsic embedding-based average $C_\iota$ and Theorem 1.** For any isometric embedding $\iota:\mathcal M\hookrightarrow\mathbb R^m$, the natural "average ambient gradient outer product then project" construction (Eq. 16) has a central-tangent-space representation $E_0^\top C_\iota E_0$ that equals the second moment of *centrally-projected* gradients (Thm. 1) — i.e., projection and averaging commute, but eigen-ordering does not commute with projection, so identifying a "dominant projected eigenvector" from the full ambient matrix $C_\iota$ first is fundamentally different from (and can silently zero out) the intrinsically meaningful direction.
- **Precise second-order agreement (Prop. 3, Lemma 2).** On a geodesic ball of radius $R < $ injectivity radius, $\|E_0^\top C_\iota E_0 - G_0\|_2 \le C R^2 \sup_{\mathcal X}\|\nabla_{\mathcal M} f\|_g^2$ with $C=1+R^2/4$ exactly on the unit hypersphere (via the closed-form projection-versus-transport identity, Lemma 2); dominant eigenspaces then agree to $O(R^2/\eta)$ by Davis-Kahan whenever a spectral gap $\eta = \lambda_r - \lambda_{r+1} > 0$ exists.
- **Direct applicability to shape-preshape spaces.** Because Kendall's landmark preshapes and length-normalized elastic-curve preshapes are (finite-dimensional) hyperspheres, the closed-form hypersphere identities (Appendix C) transfer the entire construction to response-driven dimension reduction for shape functions, motivating forthcoming work on the landmark-affine Grassmannian for separable shape tensors and adjoint-based aerodynamic drag gradients.

## Methodology

The paper poses a Riemannian generalization of the active-subspace problem (Section 1.7): given a scalar quantity of interest $f:\mathcal X\subseteq\mathcal M\to\mathbb R$ with square-integrable Riemannian gradient on an $n$-manifold $(\mathcal M,g)$, find $r<n$ ordered directions $\boldsymbol v_i \in T_{p_0}\mathcal M$ at a central point $p_0$ that capture how $f$ changes the most, generalizing Constantine's Euclidean average gradient outer product $C=\int_{\mathcal X}\nabla f(x)\otimes\nabla f(x)\,\rho(x)dx$ (Eq. 4).

**Intrinsic construction.** Because tangent spaces at different points of a curved manifold are not canonically identifiable, the paper compensates for this "globalizing" obstruction by parallel-transporting each Riemannian gradient along its unique connecting geodesic to a single central tangent space $T_{p_0}\mathcal M$ before averaging (Def. 2, Eq. 12), using a wrapped probability measure $\mu$ pushed forward via the exponential map $\exp_{p_0}$ from a star-shaped support in $T_{p_0}\mathcal M$. Because parallel transport is an isometry, this composition exactly preserves the mean-squared-directional-derivative interpretation of the eigenvalues (Lemma 1), unlike an arbitrary linear combination of tangent vectors from distinct tangent spaces (Remarks 1-2).

**Extrinsic construction.** The natural alternative from the manifold-learning literature averages ambient-coordinate gradients $d\iota[\nabla_{\mathcal M}f(p)]$ directly in $\mathbb R^m$ under a chosen isometric embedding $\iota$ (Eq. 16), then projects the resulting ambient eigenvectors onto the central tangent space via the orthogonal projector $\pi_0 = E_0E_0^\top$. Theorem 1 shows the central-tangent representation of this extrinsic average equals the second moment of centrally-projected gradients — establishing the two constructions are built from the *same* tangential gradients but differ in whether the isometry (parallel transport) or a partial isometry (ambient projection) is applied before eigendecomposition.

**Hypersphere closed forms.** Because hyperspheres $\iota(S^n)\subset\mathbb R^{n+1}$ admit explicit geodesics, exponential/logarithm maps, and parallel transport (Appendix C), Lemma 2 derives an exact identity: central projection equals parallel transport composed with a $\cos d$ contraction of the radial component (where $d$ is geodesic distance to the center), with transverse components transported exactly. This yields the tight constant $C=1+R^2/4$ in Prop. 3's general $O(R^2)$ bound.

**Algorithm and numerics.** Algorithm 1 gives a Monte Carlo estimator of $G_0$: draw $N$ samples from $\mu$, compute Riemannian gradients, parallel-transport them to the central tangent space, and average the outer products (equivalently, an SVD). Section 3 instantiates everything on the unit 2-sphere $\iota(S^2)\subset\mathbb R^3$ (exponential map, its inverse/logarithm, Karcher mean as the data-driven center, and the Grassmannian $\mathrm{Gr}(3,1)$ for parallel transport over the upper hemisphere) across six toy quantities of interest: an ambient linear ridge function ($f=\boldsymbol a^\top\widehat x$), a degenerate/aligned linear ridge, an ambient quadratic that *is* a ridge, an ambient quadratic with merely ordered (non-ridge) directional derivatives, and nonlinear ridge/non-ridge analogs — visualized via normal-coordinate color fields, on-sphere gradient/geodesic overlays, and "shadow plots" of $f$ restricted to submanifolds (Fig. 11 gallery).

## Results & Highlights

- **$O(R^2)$ ridge-recovery rate confirmed numerically (Figs. 6-7).** For an ambient linear ridge function, the RMS deviation of samples from the active geodesic sweep decays as the geodesic-ball radius $R$ shrinks, with fitted power-law exponent ≈**2.1** (Fig. 6, top-right); for a quadratic (non-ridge but ordered) function the fitted exponent is ≈**1.9**; for a nonlinear ridge function the fitted exponent is ≈**2.0** (Fig. 7, top-right) — all matching the theoretical $O(R^2)$ prediction of Prop. 3. By contrast, a genuine 2-dimensional nonlinear **non-ridge** function shows a markedly slower, noisier fitted exponent of ≈**1.0** (Fig. 7, bottom-right), since it lacks the spectral-gap assumption the rate depends on — the paper explicitly notes "its RMS does not match the expected rate absent the assumed spectral gap."
- **Discrepancy between intrinsic AMG and extrinsic dominant-projection estimate is small when a spectral gap exists.** For the ambient linear ridge example (2-sphere, Karcher mean at $\widehat x_0=(0,0,1)^\top$, $N=1000$ samples), the subspace distance between the AMG $\mathcal W_{1,1}$ and the orthogonal projection of the known ambient ridge direction $\pi_0(\boldsymbol a)$ is **≈4×10⁻⁴** (Section 3.2/Fig. 3 caption); for the ambient quadratic (ordered, non-ridge) example the analogous discrepancy is **≈5×10⁻³** (Section 3.3/Fig. 10 caption) — both regimes of Remark 6 (i)-(iii) hold simultaneously in these cases.
- **$O(N^{-1/2})$ Monte Carlo convergence of Algorithm 1 (Fig. 8).** Repeating the estimation 500 times with random reseeding, the subspace distance between the Algorithm-1-approximated dominant eigenspace of $G_0$ and the true projected ambient ridge direction $\pi_0(\boldsymbol a)$ decays at the standard Monte Carlo rate as $N$ grows from ~10 to ~1000 samples, dropping from roughly **6×10⁻²** to **~2×10⁻³**, consistent with the paper's stated $\mathcal O(N^{-1/2})$ rate (both for the intrinsic estimate and, separately, for the extrinsic estimate's convergence to $\boldsymbol a$).
- **Documented extrinsic failure mode ("projected dominance is not dominant projection," Fig. 9).** When the central point is rotated so that $\widehat x_0 = \pm\boldsymbol a$ (i.e., the true ambient ridge direction is normal to the central tangent space), the naive extrinsic estimate $\lim_{N\to\infty}\pi_0(\bar{\boldsymbol a}_N)$ collapses to the trivial zero vector in $T_{\widehat x_0}\iota(S^2)$ — a "meaningless interpretation... annihilated by projected-dominance" — whereas the intrinsic $G_0$'s two eigenvalues remain approximately equal (isotropic), correctly signaling "the function does not change more along either submanifold... without subsequent interpretation involving projections."
- No traditional baseline-vs-method benchmark table is presented (this is a theory/methodology paper); the paper's own toy-function gallery (Fig. 11: linear ridge, degenerate linear ridge, quadratic ridge, ordered quadratic, nonlinear ridge, nonlinear non-ridge — all over $\iota(S^2)$) serves as the only empirical validation, and no comparison against a competing method (e.g., active manifolds [Bridges et al.], neural active manifolds [Zanoni et al.], or Fletcher's principal geodesic analysis) is run.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core intrinsic construction — composing the classical active-subspace gradient outer product with parallel transport to a single central tangent space — is a clean, well-motivated generalization, and the "projected dominance versus dominant projection" distinction (Theorem 1, Remark 7, demonstrated concretely in Fig. 9) is a genuinely non-obvious insight: it shows that a common practice in manifold learning (eigendecompose the ambient average gradient matrix, then project the dominant eigenvector) can silently annihilate the very direction of interest when that direction happens to be near-normal to the chosen central tangent space — a pitfall not previously formalized in this way to the paper's own related-work survey.

**Rigor:** Strong. Every numbered result (Props. 1-3, Lemma 1-2, Thm. 1) has a full proof in Appendix D, constants are made explicit and tight on the unit hypersphere ($C=1+R^2/4$), and the paper is unusually careful about scope conditions — e.g., explicitly restricting claims to geodesic balls within the injectivity radius, and proving (Appendix B, via a space-filling-curve volume argument and the Frobenius/Chow-Rashevskii dichotomy) that unconstrained nonlinear single-curve reduction methods are provably ill-posed, which is used to motivate why AMG restricts search to geodesics rather than general curves.

**Reproducibility:** All numerical examples are restricted to a single toy geometry — the unit 2-sphere with synthetic linear/quadratic/nonlinear test functions the author constructs — and no code repository or supplementary numerical artifact is referenced in the visible text (unlike, e.g., papers that link a GitHub repo for the Algorithm 1 implementation). Despite motivating the entire theory through Kendall landmark preshapes and elastic-curve preshapes (both hyperspheres), the paper runs **no** actual shape-analysis experiment on real landmark or curve data — this is explicitly deferred to "forthcoming work" (Outlook, Section 4), so the practical relevance to statistical shape analysis remains a stated motivation rather than a demonstrated capability.

**Significance:** Theoretically significant within the specific niche connecting active-subspace dimension reduction, Riemannian geometry, and shape-preshape theory; the closed-form hypersphere identities (Lemma 2, Appendix C) are a concrete, reusable tool. Practical significance is contingent on future work extending beyond $n=2$ toy geometry and on the harder computational bottleneck the paper itself flags: approximating the boundary-value problems for the inverse exponential map and Karcher mean in high-dimensional shape-tensor embeddings is explicitly noted (Section 2.11) as a bigger practical challenge than the $O(N^{-1/2})$ Monte Carlo sampling rate.

**Red flags / weak points:** (1) No numerical or theoretical comparison against the competing nonlinear reduction methods discussed at length in Section 1.3 (active manifolds, neural active manifolds, principal geodesic analysis) — the paper positions AMG as a well-posed alternative to these but never benchmarks against them. (2) All quantitative validation is on a single, low-dimensional ($n=2$) synthetic geometry designed by the author, with no real dataset; the fitted power-law exponents in Figs. 6-7 (2.1, 1.9, 2.0, 1.0) come from the author's own toy functions rather than an independent benchmark suite. (3) The intrinsic AMG construction is explicitly acknowledged (Section 4, "Limitation") to be only geodesically local — valid on a single central geodesic ball — and the paper does not resolve how to extend it into a genuinely global, foliation-based reduction over a compact manifold with turning ridge structure, leaving the harder general case as future work.

## Strengths

- Mathematically rigorous generalization with complete proofs, explicit constants, and carefully scoped hypotheses (geodesic-ball locality, injectivity-radius restriction, spectral-gap conditions for eigenspace agreement).
- Sharp, well-illustrated identification of a real pitfall in extrinsic/embedding-based manifold gradient averaging ("projected dominance is not dominant projection"), backed by a concrete degenerate numerical example (Fig. 9) rather than only an abstract argument.
- Clean closed-form projection-transport identity on hyperspheres (Lemma 2) with a tight, explicit second-order constant, directly usable for Kendall shape preshapes and elastic-curve representations.
- Honest, systematic classification of when intrinsic and extrinsic constructions agree versus disagree (Remark 6's three regimes), rather than simply advocating one approach.
- Well-organized toy-example gallery (Fig. 11) spanning ridge/non-ridge and linear/quadratic/nonlinear cases, useful as a didactic reference for readers building intuition about the intrinsic-extrinsic distinction.

## Limitations

- All numerical validation is confined to synthetic functions on the unit 2-sphere; no experiment on real shape/landmark/curve data despite this being the paper's central motivating application.
- The construction is only geodesically local (valid within one central geodesic ball inside the injectivity radius); a genuinely global, foliation-aware reduction over a compact manifold with changing ridge structure is left to future work.
- No numerical or theoretical comparison against competing nonlinear dimension-reduction methods (active manifolds, neural active manifolds, principal geodesic analysis) that the introduction positions this work against.
- Practical computation requires solving Riemannian boundary-value problems (inverse exponential map, Karcher mean) whose cost/conditioning in high-dimensional shape-tensor embeddings is explicitly flagged by the author as harder than the Monte Carlo sampling itself, but is not benchmarked in this paper.
- Single-author work with no linked code/data artifact identified in the manuscript, limiting independent reproducibility of the Algorithm 1 numerics.

## Relevance

该论文对研究流形学习、降维方法或统计形状分析（如 Kendall 预形空间、弹性曲线表示）理论基础的读者有较高参考价值，尤其"内蕴平行输运构造 vs. 外蕴嵌入投影构造"的严格对照和退化反例，对任何在流形上做梯度平均/敏感性分析的工作都是值得警惕的陷阱提示；但论文尚无真实数据实验，工程落地价值有限。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does AMG perform on real Kendall landmark-shape or elastic-curve preshape data (e.g., actual aerodynamic shape ensembles or biological landmark datasets), rather than synthetic 2-sphere functions?
- Can the geodesically-local AMG construction be extended into a global, foliation-based reduction that handles ridge structure turning across a compact manifold, as the author's own Frobenius/Chow-Rashevskii dichotomy (Appendix B) suggests is necessary?
- How does AMG empirically compare to competing nonlinear reduction methods (active manifolds, neural active manifolds, principal geodesic analysis) on a shared benchmark task, in terms of both accuracy and computational cost?
- What retraction or vector-transport approximations (per Absil-Mahony-Sepulchre) best trade off accuracy against the cost of exact exponential/parallel-transport maps for high-dimensional matrix manifolds relevant to shape tensors (Grassmannian × SPD)?
- Is there a data-driven way to numerically determine or certify the injectivity radius for a learned/approximated manifold, so the support hypothesis of Def. 2 (currently flagged as requiring future work) becomes auditable in practice?
