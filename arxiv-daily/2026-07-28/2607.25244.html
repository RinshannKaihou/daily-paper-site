Key Finding

CADENCE factors a frozen ECG model's representations into 8,192 sparse "cardiac atoms" that align with clinical concepts far better than raw dense dimensions.

CADENCE: A Cardiac Atom Dictionary for Interpretable Neural Concept Extraction from ECG Foundation Models ↗

Yixuan Duan, Arjun Naik, Sadeer Al-Kindi, Wei Qiu · 28 Jul 2026 ·
sparse autoencoders mechanistic interpretability ECG foundation models concept discovery electrocardiography

Key Metric

0.93→0.95

Phenotype-prediction AUROC: dense Layer-6 embedding probe → sparse cardiac-atom probe — matched-or-better accuracy, now decomposable into a handful of named atoms instead of 768 entangled dense dimensions.

Why This Matters

Extends sparse-autoencoder interpretability from language and protein models to clinical ECG signals — with cross-cohort transfer and causal-ablation evidence, not just correlation.

原文 (Relevance)：本文将稀疏自编码器解构基础模型表征、实现机制可解释性的范式系统地扩展到生理时间序列（ECG）领域，其外部队列免重训迁移验证和单原子因果消融实验，对关注可解释 AI 和医疗基础模型审计的研究者具有较高参考价值。

Critical Edge

The headline "best-atom beats best-dense-dimension" comparisons pick the top of 8,192 atoms against only 768 dense dimensions — an inherently asymmetric contest, with no reported multiple-comparisons correction or dictionary-size-matched baseline.

一句话总结 · Chinese Abstract (source)

CADENCE 用 BatchTopK 稀疏自编码器把一个冻结的 12 导联心电图基础模型（CSFM）第 6 层表征分解为 8,192 个稀疏"心脏原子"，这些原子在临床表型和形态学概念上的最优单原子 AUROC（0.88/0.90）显著超过原始稠密维度（0.78/0.83），稀疏探针在表型预测上把 AUROC 从稠密嵌入的 0.93 提到 0.95，并在外部 PTB-XL 队列上无需重训练即可复现（最优原子平均 AUROC 0.825 vs. 稠密 0.720）。

Methodology

01
Freeze a 6-layer, d=768 12-lead ECG foundation model (CSFM) that tokenizes each recording into non-overlapping 100ms patches — 1,200 tokens per ECG (12 leads × 100 patches). The backbone is never fine-tuned.
02
Train a separate BatchTopK sparse autoencoder per layer on ∼9M patch-token embeddings from ∼150k MIMIC-IV-ECG recordings, expanding d=768 into an overcomplete dictionary of F=8,192 atoms with k=128 active per token.
03
Define per-ECG atom activation as the max over the recording's tokens (captures short, localized events like PVCs or pacing spikes); score atom–concept alignment via sign-agnostic, rank-based AUROC.
04
Fit ℓ2-regularized logistic probes on atom activations vs. the dense embedding (subject-disjoint splits); test causal validity via integrated-gradients-selected single-atom ablation (dose 1→0) on a frozen downstream predictor.
05
Auto-interpret quality-filtered atoms with an LLM (GPT-5.4-mini) from waveform/activation geometry alone, no report text; validate faithfulness by having it predict held-out activations from its own description.

Auto-interpretation covers only 2,991 of 8,192 atoms — dead, rare (<20 activating ECGs), near-constant (>90% of ECGs), weak, or lead-diffuse atoms are excluded before any description is generated.

  • F=8,192, k=128 chosen via a 3-axis grid search trading off reconstruction variance, dead-atom fraction, and count of phenotype-specific atoms (Fig. A2).
  • Frozen dictionary re-applied without retraining to PTB-XL (21,799 ECGs, different country/acquisition system) for external validation.
  • CSFM backbone is an external pretrained artifact taken as given — not retrained or ablated by the authors.

  • PVC atom probe reaches AUROC 0.947; its top-weighted atom is ∼91.5× over-represented among ECGs whose machine report says "frequent PVCs" — paced-rhythm probe 0.951, sinus bradycardia 0.991, RBBB 0.988.
  • LLM auto-interpretation on 2,991 quality-filtered atoms: median Spearman ρ=0.65 predicting held-out activations from text description alone (92% >0.3); adding full report text barely moves this (0.665→0.670), arguing against report-label leakage.
  • Atom-space geometry recovers known cardiology structure unsupervised: LBBB/RBBB/IVCD/paced-rhythm/wide-QRS/long-QTc cluster together (cosine 0.64–0.87); AF/flutter cluster separately (cosine 0.81).

Strengths

  • Multi-pronged validation — concept-association AUROC, linear probing, atom-space relational geometry, causal ablation, and LLM auto-interpretation with a text-only faithfulness check — each targeting a different failure mode.
  • Genuine external-cohort replication on PTB-XL (different country, different acquisition system) without retraining the SAE.
  • Direct clinical framing with a cardiologist co-author and concrete waveform-level visualizations tying named atoms to specific ECG morphology (PVC events, AF baseline, ST-segment primitives).
  • Explicit ablation-based causal audit rather than relying solely on correlational probing.
  • Transparent hyperparameter grid search and patient-level bootstrap confidence intervals on the main downstream-probe comparisons.

Limitations

  • Atom naming is bounded by current cardiology knowledge and fixed geometric heuristics; scope is restricted to standard resting 12-lead ECGs — atoms are explicitly not diagnostic devices.
  • "Best-atom" comparisons are a post hoc pick from 8,192 atoms vs. only 768 dense dimensions — an asymmetric search-space size that likely inflates the apparent gap.
  • No baseline against other unsupervised decompositions (PCA, ICA, vanilla top-k/ReLU SAE) — unclear how much of the benefit is specific to BatchTopK.
  • Concept-discovery validation uses a pre-specified list of known diagnostic categories (Table A4), not demonstrated novel, previously unnamed physiological structure.
  • LLM auto-interpretation faithfulness (median ρ=0.65) is validated by the same LLM family that wrote the descriptions — no independent human-cardiologist blind rating.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. How much of the "best-atom beats best-dense-dimension" gap survives a dictionary-size-matched control (e.g. the best of 8,192 random or PCA-derived directions in the dense space) rather than 768 raw dimensions?
  2. Would training the SAE jointly across all 6 layers, rather than a separate SAE per layer, reveal cross-layer atom composition or hierarchy the current approach cannot expose?
  3. Can the ablation-based causal audit extend beyond single-atom edits to test whether combinations of atoms are jointly necessary/sufficient, given several probes (PVC, paced rhythm) rely on 5+ atoms?
  4. How robust is the atom dictionary to distribution shift beyond PTB-XL — pediatric ECGs, different lead placements, or ICU telemetry — where the "true" physiological concepts may differ?
  5. Does the ∼0.65 median Spearman correlation hold up under an independent human-cardiologist blind rating of description quality, rather than only self-consistency with another LLM call?
