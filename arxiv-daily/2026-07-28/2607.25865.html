Why This Matters

Shows circuit-level evidence beats algebraic proxies for guiding AI-driven discovery — a lesson likely to transfer to any automated-science task where the design target and the real evaluation target diverge.

Critical Edge

DeepSeek's frontier never beats the harder BB [[144,12,12]] baseline and collapses to near-zero error suppression without circuit feedback — the framework's payoff is contingent on a strong proprietary backend, not the architecture alone.

Methodology — the dual-loop architecture

FAST LOOP · seconds/candidate

An LLM code-search agent proposes 24 construction programs/iteration × 20 seeds (≤480 candidates) across BB, trivariate-bicycle, lifted-product & generalized-LP families. A characterization module computes n, k, decoder-estimated distance d, and the cheap proxy Φcode = kd²/n for screening.

SLOW LOOP · minutes–hours

Top candidates are compiled into full Stim syndrome-extraction circuits (edge-colored scheduling, native gates, detectors). Noisy sampling + BP-OSD / BP-LSD / Relay-BP decoding estimates real circuit-level LER, fed back to the orchestrator.

↓ both loops write into an evolving "discovery memory" that reshapes the next generation prompt ↓
  • Objective shift: the orchestrator optimizes pLER directly, not the code-level figure of merit — the paper's central departure from prior RL / Bayesian-optimization / LLM-search QEC work.
  • Scale: 3 LLM backends (Claude Opus 4.8, GPT-5.5, DeepSeek-V4-Pro) × 4 code families × 14 qubit budgets (N=20–288) × T=60 iterations, fixed hyperparameters throughout.
  • Modularity: decoder-agnostic — synthesis/evaluation tools swap in without restructuring the loop.

Results beyond the headline

Crossover budgets

All 3 backends beat BB [[72,12,6]] by N=98 total qubits; Claude & GPT also clear the harder BB [[144,12,12]] by N=240. DeepSeek never does, within N≤288.

The Φcode / LER mismatch (N=144)

Best codes found by DeepSeek / Claude / GPT score Φcode = 2.25 / 5.56 / 3.36 — all worse than BB's 6 — yet all beat BB on real circuit-level LER.

Steeper error suppression

Fitted scaling exponent Γ (pLER∝pΓ): discovered codes reach Γ=5.2–5.6 vs. Γ=3.79 for BB [[72,12,6]] — a materially faster error rolloff as noise drops.

Strengths & Limitations

Strengths

  • Paper-specific, measured evidence (not just argument) that Φcode can mis-rank circuit-level performance.
  • Clean 3-way ablation (full / fast-only / no-feedback) isolating the slow loop's contribution with mean & variance.
  • Validated beyond the p=0.005 search point via a full physical-error-rate sweep and Γ fits.
  • Transparent token & dollar cost reporting per backend/budget — rare in "AI scientist" papers.
  • Built on established, independently checkable tooling (Stim, BP-OSD/LSD, Relay-BP), not an opaque end-to-end model.

Limitations

  • Single baseline family (BB codes) — no comparison to other qLDPC constructions or prior AI-driven discovery methods at circuit level.
  • Code distance d is decoder-estimated/bounded, not exactly computed, adding uncertainty to reported Φcode.
  • Strong backend dependence: DeepSeek underperforms and misses the harder BB reference entirely.
  • Relies on proprietary, versioned commercial APIs — limits exact long-term reproducibility.
  • Full error-rate sweep validated on only 21 of many discovered codes, not the whole frontier.
  • Covers quantum-memory storage only — not logical operations or lattice surgery (flagged as future work).

Questions for further exploration

  • Would OmniQEC still win if compared directly against prior RL- or Bayesian-optimization-based QEC discovery methods under the same circuit-level LER protocol, instead of only a human-designed BB baseline?
  • How sensitive is the Φcode/LER mismatch to the accuracy of decoder-assisted distance estimates — would exact distance computation change the conclusion?
  • What specific reasoning behaviors separate a strong orchestrator backend (Claude, GPT) from a weak one (DeepSeek), and can that be distilled into a smaller, cheaper model?
  • Would the dual-loop architecture extend to logical operations or lattice surgery, where slow-loop evaluation is far more expensive than single-code-block memory tests?
  • If slow-loop circuit evaluation itself gets cheaper (better statistical estimation), would that let the fast loop be bypassed in favor of more slow-loop evaluations?
