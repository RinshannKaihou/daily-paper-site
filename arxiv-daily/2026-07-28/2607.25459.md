> **一句话总结：** 论文在受控的多元随机波动率(MSV)仿真环境中，用线性探针和对齐方向消融证明单层Transformer和MLP都学到"隐状态推断→输出映射"两阶段计算，在长周期(ω≥365)情形下该计算可精确还原为"学习到的线性投影+ℓ2归一化"这一显式滤波器（消融任一环节R²从约0.89-0.92骤降到约-0.004），且MSE训练下输出头对隐状态信息的读出存在错位，用探针对齐的读出替换学习头可让MAE稳定提升（如ω=30时Transformer提升+5.28%）。

> **TL;DR:** On a fully synthetic, controlled multivariate stochastic-volatility (MSV) benchmark (N=6 assets, volatility-cycle period ω ∈ {21, 30, 90, 178, 365, 730}), a 1-layer/1-head Transformer and an MLP baseline both linearly encode the next latent log-volatility state h_{T+1} in their hidden representations (ridge-probe R²≈0.80-0.91 across losses/activations at ω=365, Table 1), but the learned linear output head recovers markedly less of that information under MSE training (readout R² drops to ~0.75 for both architectures vs. ~0.89-0.90 under NLL). In long-cycle regimes (ω≥365), this computation collapses into an explicit filter — a learned linear projection followed by ℓ2-normalization — that alone reaches R²≈0.89-0.92, while ablating either the projection or the normalization drops R² to essentially zero (-0.004 to -0.006, Table 4). Replacing the trained head with a ridge-probe readout improves MAE consistently (e.g., +5.28% for the Transformer at ω=30) even though the corresponding MSE effect is often negative, indicating that part of the forecast degradation under MSE training is a readout-alignment problem rather than a representation failure.

## 核心贡献

- 提出用受控的多元随机波动率（MSV）过程作为可解释性研究的新基准：收益率是含噪观测，真实的对数波动率隐状态 h_t 对模型不可见但对研究者已知，从而可以直接量化模型是否、以及如何在内部恢复该隐状态。
- 系统验证"两阶段计算"假设（H1）：跨 Transformer/MLP 两种架构、MSE/NLL 两种损失、exp/softplus 两种输出激活，隐藏表示 z 都能通过岭回归探针高精度线性解码下一期隐状态 h_{T+1}，随后输出头再把该表示映射为平方收益预测。
- 揭示 Transformer 内部隐状态可解码性的"阶段性涌现"（H2）：其出现的具体架构阶段依赖于波动率周期 ω——长周期在 ℓ2 归一化后即可解码，中等周期需要额外的前馈网络（FFN）加工，短周期对照组（ω=21，与观测窗口长度重合）则依赖位置编码，本质是靠周期性记忆而非真正的滤波推断。
- 在长周期体制下（H3）将该计算精确刻画为一个显式的"隐状态滤波器"：学习到的线性投影 W_in 后接 ℓ2 归一化，再用岭回归探针读出，二者缺一不可（消融实验中单独去掉任一环节都会使解码 R² 从约 0.89-0.92 骤降到接近 0）。
- 通过输出头替换实验表明，MSE 训练下预测误差的部分退化源于"读出错位"而非隐状态编码失败：用探针对齐的读出头替换学习到的输出头后，MAE 在多数波动率周期下都有提升，但 MSE 层面的效果好坏参半。

## Key Contributions

- **A controlled MSV benchmark for mechanistic interpretability under partial observability.** Unlike prior mechanistic-interpretability work on deterministic/language tasks, the paper uses a multivariate stochastic volatility (MSV) generative process (Harvey et al., 1994) where the latent log-volatility state h_t is hidden from the model but known to the researcher, enabling direct, ground-truth evaluation of latent-state recovery.
- **Robust two-stage decomposition (H1).** Across Transformer and MLP architectures, MSE/NLL losses, and exp/softplus output activations, hidden representations z linearly encode substantial information about h_{T+1} (ridge-probe R²), and a separate output head maps z to the squared-return forecast — mirroring the classical latent-state-inference-then-forecast pipeline of SV models.
- **Stage-wise emergence in Transformers depends on the volatility period (H2).** Linear decodability of h_{T+1} first appears after ℓ2-normalization for long cycles (ω≥178), requires additional FFN refinement for intermediate cycles (ω∈{30,90}), and depends on positional embeddings for a short-period control (ω=21) that can be solved by memorizing periodicity instead of filtering.
- **An explicit, causally-validated latent-state filter for long cycles (H3).** For ω≥365, the Transformer's latent-state computation reduces to Equation (3): a learned linear projection followed by ℓ2-normalization. Both components are shown to be jointly necessary via ablation (Table 4) and via aligned-direction causal perturbation (Figure 7, Section H), which identifies the post-ℓ2-norm representation as the earliest causally relevant stage for both downstream decodability and forecast accuracy.
- **Output-head intervention isolates readout misalignment from representation failure.** Replacing the learned linear head with a ridge-probe-trained readout (same final-layer representation) improves MAE consistently under MSE training across both architectures and most volatility periods (Table 2), even though MSE-level gains are mixed, suggesting some forecast degradation under noisy MSE training is a readout problem rather than a failure to encode the latent state.

## Methodology

The paper simulates a 6-asset multivariate stochastic volatility (MSV) process (Harvey et al., 1994): returns r_t = exp(h_t/2) ⊙ ε_t are noisy observations of a latent log-volatility state h_t that follows an autoregressive process h_{t+1} = μ + A(h_t - μ) + η_t, with μ=-1, σ_η=0.02, and a cross-asset spillover matrix A constructed from a block-rotation structure (Eq. 2) whose period parameter ω controls the cyclicity of volatility and cross-asset correlation, and whose spectral radius ρ controls persistence/signal-to-noise ratio (baseline ρ=0.999999). A trajectory is split into n=6000 windows of length L=22, using the first T=21 returns to predict the next-step squared returns y = (r²_{1,T+1}, ..., r²_{N,T+1}).

Two architectures are compared. The main model is a compact 1-layer, 1-head Transformer encoder (d_model=64) that treats each asset as a token whose feature is its length-T return history: the history is linearly embedded, ℓ2-normalized, combined with a learnable per-asset positional embedding, passed through one self-attention + GELU-FFN block with residual connections, and mapped by a linear output head to a positive variance forecast via an exponential or softplus activation. The MLP baseline processes each asset's return history independently (three GELU hidden layers, width 64, no attention or asset-specific embeddings), isolating the effect of Transformer-specific cross-asset components. Both models are trained with either MSE on squared returns or a Gaussian negative log-likelihood (NLL) that is proven (Theorem 1, Appendix C) to be uniquely minimized at the true conditional variance exp(h_{i,T+1}).

The interpretability analysis combines three tools: (1) linear ridge probes (Alain and Bengio, 2016) fit from each asset's hidden representation z_i (or intermediate-layer activations) to the true latent state h_{i,T+1}, compared against the R² induced by the model's own learned linear readout; (2) stage-wise probing at every architectural checkpoint (post-embedding, post-ℓ2-norm, post-positional-embedding, post-attention, post-FFN) to localize where decodability emerges, repeated for both the 1-layer and a 2-layer Transformer; and (3) causal, aligned-direction ablation/patching (in the spirit of activation patching and causal scrubbing) that removes a fraction α of the probe-identified latent-state direction at a chosen stage and measures downstream collapse in decodability and forecast error, isolating which stage is causally load-bearing rather than merely correlated with the latent state.

## Results & Highlights

- **Probe vs. learned-readout gap (Table 1, ω=365, median [IQR], n=10 seeds).** Under the exponential activation and NLL loss, the Transformer's ridge-probe R² for h_{T+1} is 0.9069 [0.8827, 0.9175] versus a comparable learned-readout R² of 0.8975 [0.8733, 0.9065]. Under MSE training with the same activation, the gap widens sharply: probe R² stays at 0.8794 [0.8699, 0.8929] while the learned readout R² falls to 0.7539 [0.7151, 0.7846] for the Transformer and 0.7543 [0.7232, 0.7975] for the MLP baseline (MLP probe R²=0.8034) — a roughly 0.10-0.13 point drop that is largest precisely under the noisier MSE objective.
- **Explicit-filter ablation (Table 4, median [IQR], n=10 seeds).** The full filter (learned linear projection → ℓ2-normalization → ridge readout) reaches R²=0.890 [0.866, 0.903] at ω=365 and R²=0.921 [0.914, 0.933] at ω=730. Removing the learned projection (ℓ2-norm applied directly to raw returns) collapses R² to -0.004 [-0.005, -0.002] (ω=365) / -0.003 [-0.004, -0.002] (ω=730); removing the normalization (linear-embedding-only) similarly collapses R² to -0.006 [-0.009, -0.003] / -0.005 [-0.015, -0.000] — both baselines are near zero, versus ~0.89-0.92 for the full composition.
- **Output-head intervention (Table 2, MSE training, exponential activation, median over n=10 seeds).** MAE improves consistently when the learned head is replaced by a ridge-probe readout: Transformer MAE goes from 0.873→0.836 (+5.28%) at ω=30, 0.987→0.958 (+1.29%) at ω=178, and 0.856→0.853 (+0.66%) at ω=365; MLP MAE improves by +3.89%, +3.05%, and +1.18% at the same three ω values. MSE-level effects are mixed by contrast: Transformer MSE changes by -1.39% (ω=30), -0.11% (ω=178), and +0.47% (ω=365), while MLP MSE changes by -1.34%, -0.87%, and -0.01% respectively — i.e., the probe readout is not a uniform improvement, only a consistent one on the tail-insensitive MAE metric.
- **Signal-to-noise / persistence sweep (Table 3, mean R², n=5 seeds).** As the spectral radius ρ of the state-transition matrix increases from 0.9999 to 0.9999999 (more persistent latent dynamics), the Transformer's mean ridge-probe R² for h_{T+1} rises from 0.286 to 0.903, and the MLP's from 0.289 to 0.899 — nearly identical curves for both architectures, showing decodability is governed primarily by the latent process's own persistence rather than by architecture.
- **Stage-wise localization (Section 3.2, Figure 1).** For long-period regimes (ω∈{178,365}), linear decodability of h_{T+1} already emerges immediately after the ℓ2-normalization step; for intermediate periods (ω∈{30,90}) the FFN activation adds substantial further gains; for the ω=21 control (period equal to the observation window), decodability stays near zero until positional embeddings are added, then jumps to ≈1.0 — consistent with the model exploiting exact periodicity/memorization rather than genuine filtering in that regime.

No results in the paper report classical econometric baselines (e.g., GARCH, Kalman/particle filter estimates of h_{T+1}) alongside the neural probe/readout numbers — all comparisons are between architectures, losses, activations, and ablated variants of the same neural models.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** Extending mechanistic-interpretability methodology (linear probing plus causal/activation-patching-style ablation) from deterministic toy tasks and language models to a partially-observed *stochastic* latent-dynamical-system setting is a genuinely underexplored angle, and the paper is explicit about this framing relative to prior MI work (Bricken et al. 2023, Nanda et al. 2023, Chughtai et al. 2023). The concrete payoff — reducing the Transformer's long-cycle computation to a closed-form filter (linear projection + ℓ2-norm, Eq. 3) and then causally validating it via targeted ablation — is a specific, falsifiable mechanistic claim rather than a vague "the model learns something about volatility" story.

**Rigor:** The methodology directly addresses the well-known critique that probing alone doesn't establish causal use (Belinkov 2022, cited in the paper): Section H's aligned-direction ablation shows that perturbing the post-ℓ2-norm representation, specifically, produces the largest downstream collapse in both decodability and forecast MSE/MAE (Figure 7), while later stages (attention, FFN) have near-zero effect — a paper-specific causal result, not a generic claim. The paper also includes an explicit negative/control condition (ω=21, where the cycle length matches the input window and the "latent-state recovery" is actually memorization via periodicity) rather than only showcasing favorable regimes, which is a mark of intellectual honesty. Statistics are reported as median/IQR over n=10 seeds (n=5 for the ρ sweep in Table 3), which is a reasonable but still small sample size for a benchmark paper.

**Reproducibility:** Code is publicly released. The generative process (Eqs. 1-2), architectures (Appendix A), and spillover matrices for every ω (Appendix B) are all given in full detail, so the synthetic setup is fully specified and re-runnable. However, all experiments use a single synthetic generative process with hand-chosen hyperparameters (μ=-1, σ_η=0.02, N=6 assets); there is no sensitivity analysis for these choices beyond the ρ sweep in Table 3.

**Significance:** The finding that MSE-trained models suffer specifically from readout misalignment (Table 1's probe-vs-readout gap, and Table 2's consistent MAE-only improvement from probe-readout replacement) is a useful, actionable diagnostic for anyone using MSE-trained variance forecasters, and the fact that MSE-level gains from this fix are mixed or even negative at short/intermediate cycles is reported honestly rather than smoothed over. That said, the paper's own Limitations section (Appendix J) is candid that the strongest Transformer-specific claim is not latent-state recovery per se — the MLP baseline recovers comparable information in most regimes (e.g., MLP mean R²=0.899 vs. Transformer 0.903 at the highest persistence in Table 3) — but rather the more localized, stage-specific architecture of *how* the Transformer does it.

**Red flags:** (1) No comparison whatsoever to classical stochastic-volatility filtering methods (Kalman filter, particle filter, or standard GARCH) as a baseline for how much of h_{T+1} is recoverable in principle, despite the paper explicitly citing this literature (Kitagawa 1996, Durbin and Koopman 2012, Kim et al. 1998) in Related Work — an unaddressed gap for a paper proposing this setting as a benchmark. (2) All experiments use exclusively synthetic, Gaussian-innovation MSV data with a hand-constructed rotation-based spillover matrix; there is no test on real financial returns, which the authors acknowledge directly ("all experiments are conducted in synthetic MSV models with known latent states... leaves open whether the same representational structure... persist[s] in real financial time series"). (3) Model scale is intentionally tiny (1 layer/1 head, d_model=64); the one additional data point on a 2-layer Transformer (Appendix F, Figure 5) is not enough to support general scaling claims, which the authors also flag as an open question.

## Strengths

- A genuinely controlled benchmark where the "ground truth" latent state is known to the researcher but hidden from the model — a rare setup for mechanistic interpretability outside of purely deterministic synthetic tasks.
- Converging, multi-pronged evidence (linear probing + stage-wise localization + causal aligned-direction ablation) that lands on one concrete, falsifiable mechanism (Eq. 3) rather than a purely descriptive/correlational story.
- A clean, large-effect-size ablation (Table 4) showing both filter components are jointly necessary (R² collapses from ~0.89-0.92 to essentially zero, ~-0.004 to -0.006, when either is removed).
- Explicit inclusion of a negative-control regime (ω=21 memorization case) rather than cherry-picked favorable conditions.
- Tests robustness across two losses, two output activations, and two architectures, and honestly reports where the story breaks down (MSE-level gains from output-head replacement are mixed, not uniformly positive).
- Public code release with fully specified synthetic data-generating process and architectural hyperparameters.

## Limitations

- Exclusively synthetic MSV simulation with Gaussian innovations and a specific rotation-structured spillover matrix; no evaluation on real multi-asset return data with heavy tails, jumps, or regime shifts, as the authors themselves acknowledge.
- No baseline comparison to classical latent-state estimation methods (Kalman/particle filters, GARCH) despite citing this literature, so it is unclear how much of the "recoverable" latent information the Transformer/MLP actually captures relative to a standard econometric filter.
- Architectures studied are intentionally very small (1-layer/1-head Transformer, 3-layer MLP, d_model=64); scaling behavior to realistic production-size time-series Transformers is untested beyond one 2-layer comparison in the appendix.
- The output-head-replacement fix (Table 2) only consistently helps MAE; MSE-level effects are mixed or negative for short/intermediate volatility cycles, so "readout misalignment" is only a partial explanation of MSE-training degradation.
- Only two authors from teaching-focused institutions; no stated peer-review venue or acceptance at the time of this arXiv version.

## Relevance

对同时关心机制可解释性方法论与金融时序/波动率建模的研究者，本文提供了一个可控、有真值隐状态的仿真基准和一套"探针+因果消融"组合验证流程，可用于检验实际量化预测模型（如GARCH替代品、Transformer波动率预测器）是否真正学到了隐状态滤波机制，而不仅仅是拟合了训练目标。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the two-stage decomposition and the explicit long-cycle linear-projection-plus-ℓ2-norm filter persist when models are trained on real multi-asset return data with fat tails, jumps, and regime changes, rather than the Gaussian MSV simulation used here?
- How does the recovered latent-state representation compare quantitatively to a classical Kalman or particle-filter estimate of h_{T+1} on the same simulated data — is the network implicitly approximating optimal filtering, or learning a different, possibly more brittle, shortcut?
- Would fine-tuning (rather than a frozen, ridge-probe-trained) output head close the mixed/negative MSE gap in Table 2 while preserving the consistent MAE gains?
- Does the stage-wise emergence pattern generalize to multi-head attention or deeper positional-encoding schemes, given the single-head/single-layer design used for the main results?
- How would detection of this two-stage mechanism and the explicit filter scale to larger, production-representative time-series Transformers trained on higher-dimensional asset universes?
