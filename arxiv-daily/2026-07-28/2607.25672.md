> **一句话总结：** 研究团队让人类专家与三种2025年中的大模型（ChatGPT-4o、ChatGPT Deep Research、Gemini 2.5-flash）在8个真实物理/天体物理/宇宙学课题上分别独立做文献调研，发现人类与AI选出的文献重合率不到6%，且AI生成的参考文献中3%是彻底虚构、64%存在元数据错误，而用2026年的ChatGPT Pro 5.5重跑其中一个课题时，40篇生成文献全部准确无误。

> **TL;DR:** Across 8 expert-designed physics/astro/cosmology research projects, human-expert and mid-2025 LLM (ChatGPT-4o, ChatGPT Deep Research, Gemini-2.5-flash) literature searches overlapped by less than 6%; of 641 AI-only references, 3% (22) were outright fabrications and 64% (408) had at least one wrong metadata field (author/title/year/journal), with ChatGPT Deep Research the most reliable (0 fabrications, 78% perfect) and Gemini the worst (21 fabrications, 31% judged irrelevant); a single-project spot-check with the newer ChatGPT Pro 5.5 produced 40 distinct references with zero fabrications or mismatches, versus 76% mismatch rate for ChatGPT-4o on the same project.

## 核心贡献

- 设计了一个受控对比实验：8个由物理/天体物理/宇宙学一线研究者提出的真实课题（AGN活动周期、Lyman-break星系-暗物质晕关系、内禀排列、激光烧蚀亚波长结构、HalfDome射电星系前景、引力波双黑洞环境、脉冲星计时阵列、大质量SU(2) Yang-Mills理论），每个课题都由人类专家和AI（用统一标准化prompt）各自独立完成文献检索，人类专家再对AI生成的文献逐条打分是否相关。
- 提出并执行了一套系统的"幻觉"检测流程（图1）：对人类未找到的AI生成文献，依次用DOI、链接、标题在Crossref等权威源核实，最终把每条文献归类为"完美匹配""元数据错配"或"彻底虚构"三类之一。
- 定量刻画了三种模型的可靠性差异：ChatGPT Deep Research零虚构、78%完全准确；ChatGPT-4o虚构率极低（1/196）但元数据错配率高达76%；Gemini-2.5-flash虚构数量最多（21篇）且无关文献比例最高（31%）。
- 用发表于2026年7月初的ChatGPT Pro 5.5对其中一个课题（SU2）做了追加测试，40篇生成文献全部完美匹配，无一虚构或错配，显示新一代（工具增强型）模型在这方面有质的飞跃。
- 指出AI与人类的文献检索存在系统性互补关系：人类与AI选出的文献重合率极低（<6%），AI更依赖关键词匹配、容易选中主题相关但价值有限的文献，而人类专家的检索更能触及基础性或跨领域的相关工作。

## Key Contributions

- Designed a controlled 8-project experiment spanning AGN duty cycles, Lyman-break galaxy–halo connections, intrinsic alignments, laser-ablation debris prediction, HalfDome radio-galaxy foregrounds, GW black-hole binary environments, pulsar timing array forecasts for GR tests, and massive SU(2) Yang-Mills inflation — each with an independent human-expert literature search and an independent AI-generated one (ChatGPT-4o, ChatGPT Deep Research, Gemini-2.5-flash) using an identical standardized prompt (Appendix B), each capped at 50 references.
- Built a reproducible hallucination-classification pipeline (Fig. 1) that checks each AI-only reference's DOI, then link, then title against Crossref, sorting every reference into exactly one of "perfect," "metadata mismatch," or "fabrication."
- Quantified per-model reliability differences: Deep Research (0 fabrications, 78% perfect references) is markedly more trustworthy than plain-chat models; ChatGPT-4o has a very low fabrication rate (1/196) but a high metadata-mismatch rate (76%); Gemini has both the highest fabrication count (21) and the highest irrelevance rate (31%) among generated references.
- Added a forward-looking spot check with ChatGPT Pro 5.5 (dated July 2, 2026) on a single project (SU2), finding all 40 distinct generated references were fully accurate — a stark contrast to the mid-2025 models on the same project.
- Documented that human-AI overlap in selected references is small (<6%) across all eight projects, suggesting current LLMs cannot substitute for expert literature review alone but could meaningfully complement it by surfacing genuinely different candidate references.

## Methodology

The study operationalizes "literature review" as one discrete step of the physics research workflow and treats it as a benchmarking task. Two disjoint groups of people participated: (1) **human experts** — one graduate student or postdoc per project who conceived the project, wrote a short (≤3-sentence) background and goal paragraph, conducted their own conventional literature search (capped at 50 references, sorted into 4 categories: review papers from the past 15 years, historically important/highly-cited papers, recent papers from the past 10 years, and "other" foundational/collaboration papers like Planck/WMAP/DES/HSC/KiDS), and later judged the relevance of every AI-generated reference; (2) **AI prompters** — separate students with general physics/astro/cosmology background but no expertise in the specific projects, who fed the human-written background/goal text into a standardized prompt (Appendix B) and ran it through ChatGPT-4o, ChatGPT Deep Research, and Gemini-2.5-flash (default, mid-2025 versions), each also capped at 50 references and required to fill in Title/Author/Year/Journal/DOI/Link for every entry (writing "NA" rather than leaving blanks).

Reference comparison sets four buckets per project per model: found by both human and AI ("Human & AI"), found only by the human ("Human, not AI"), found by AI and judged relevant by the human but missed by the human search ("AI, not human"), and found by AI but judged irrelevant ("AI, irrelevant"). References matching in title AND category are counted as the same reference; the same title placed in different categories by the same model is counted twice (this occurred occasionally).

For hallucination evaluation, only references generated by an LLM but *not* independently found by the human were checked (641 references total across all models). The pipeline (Fig. 1) is a sequential fallback: try to resolve the reference's generated DOI via Crossref → if it resolves, compare all fields (title, first author, year, journal) against the ground truth to label "perfect" or "metadata mismatch"; if the DOI fails, try the provided link the same way; if that also fails, do a manual title search to confirm the paper exists (metadata mismatch, broken DOI/link) or does not exist at all (fabrication). Finally, the entire pipeline was rerun for one project (SU2) using ChatGPT Pro 5.5 to gauge how much a frontier mid-2026 model improves over the mid-2025 baselines, comparing it directly against the ChatGPT-4o run on the same project.

## Results & Highlights

- **Human–AI overlap is small.** Across the combined human+AI reference sets, the "Human & AI" (found by both) fraction was only 4.3% for ChatGPT-4o (17/396), 5.4% for ChatGPT Deep Research (18/333), and 2.2% for Gemini (11/508) — i.e., under 6% overlap for every model, versus "Human, not AI" fractions of 44.7%, 52.9%, and 36.0% respectively (Table II), showing that AI and human searches are largely complementary rather than redundant.
- **Fabrication and metadata-mismatch rates (mid-2025 models, pooled).** Of the 641 AI-generated references not independently found by a human, 211 (33%) were perfect, 22 (3%) were fabrications (references to nonexistent papers), and 408 (64%) had at least one incorrect metadata field (Table III) — the paper's headline reliability metric for the mid-2025 generation.
- **Per-model breakdown (Table III).** ChatGPT Deep Research was clearly the most reliable: 0/139 fabrications and 108/139 (78%) perfect references, with only 31/139 (22%) mismatches. ChatGPT-4o had almost no fabrications (1/196, 0.5%) but a high mismatch rate of 149/196 (76%) — a strong illustration that "low fabrication" does not mean "trustworthy metadata." Gemini-2.5-flash was worst on both fronts: 21/306 (6.9%) fabrications (the largest raw count of any model) and 228/306 (74.5%) mismatches, leaving only 57/306 (18.6%) perfect.
- **Irrelevance rates differ sharply by model.** Among each model's full generated set, Gemini judged-irrelevant fraction was 31% (157/508), ChatGPT-4o's was 21% (84/396), while ChatGPT Deep Research's was only 6% (20/333) — the paper attributes Deep Research's edge to its tool-augmented, verification-capable search process rather than the underlying LLM alone.
- **2026 model spot-check (single project, SU2).** ChatGPT Pro 5.5 (dated July 2, 2026) generated 44 references (40 substantially distinct after removing near-duplicates), with 4 overlapping the human expert's selection — and **all 40 were perfect matches, zero fabrications, zero metadata mismatches** (Fig. 3, Table III's separate Pro 5.5 column). By contrast, ChatGPT-4o on the same SU2 project produced 42 distinct references with a 76%-mismatch rate consistent with its pooled aggregate. The overlap between ChatGPT-4o's and Pro 5.5's own reference sets was 7 papers out of their respective ~40-44.
- **Mismatch field breakdown (Table IV, n=399 resolvable mismatches).** First-author name was wrong most often (306 instances), followed by title (181), journal (124), and year (105); about half (206/399) of mismatched references had exactly 2 of the 4 checked fields wrong, and 54/399 had all 4 fields wrong.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core idea — measuring LLM literature-search hallucination rates against expert ground truth — is not new in the abstract (cited prior hallucination studies exist for medical/other literatures), but applying it specifically and systematically to frontier physics/astro/cosmology research questions, with genuine unpublished projects as test cases rather than well-known textbook topics, is a useful and fairly original contribution.

**Rigor:** The DOI→link→title-search fallback hallucination-classification procedure (Fig. 1) is sensible and reproducible. However, several details limit rigor: (1) relevance judgments were made by a single human expert per project with no second rater or inter-rater agreement check, and the paper explicitly acknowledges this judgment is "necessarily subjective"; (2) Gemini was run in the "2.5-flash" (default, lightweight) configuration rather than a frontier-tier Gemini model, which likely disadvantages it relative to ChatGPT-4o and especially Deep Research in a comparison the paper frames as "Gemini is the worst" — this is a meaningfully weaker baseline choice that the paper does not flag as a caveat; (3) the AI prompters lacked domain expertise in each specific sub-field, which could affect prompt quality/iteration even though the prompt itself was standardized; (4) the ChatGPT Pro 5.5 "major improvement" claim rests on a single project (SU2, 40 references) — the authors themselves caution this is "a single-project test" and that "a comprehensive benchmark... would be ideal," so the headline result about Pro 5.5's zero-hallucination performance should not be over-generalized.

**Reproducibility:** The standardized prompt is given in full (Appendix B), and project backgrounds/goals are given in Appendix A, so the human-side inputs are reproducible; however, LLM outputs are inherently stochastic and version-pinned (mid-2025 snapshots, one-off July 2026 Pro 5.5 run), so exact replication of the AI-generated reference lists is not possible, only re-running the same pipeline with current models.

**Significance:** The <6% human-AI reference overlap and the 64% mid-2025 metadata-mismatch rate are practically important warnings for any physicist tempted to trust an LLM-generated bibliography outright. The Pro 5.5 result, while limited in scope, is a genuinely interesting forward signal about tool-use/verification-driven improvement (the authors correctly attribute this to "improved tool use and greater test-time compute" rather than pure LLM scaling).

**Red flags:** No statistical significance testing or confidence intervals are reported anywhere (all results are raw counts/percentages from a small, fixed set of 8 projects); the "Gemini most irrelevant/most fabrications" framing conflates model capability with model configuration (flash tier); and the single-project Pro 5.5 comparison, while honestly caveated in the text, is prominently featured in the abstract and conclusion in a way that risks readers over-weighting a 40-reference, one-project spot check.

## Strengths

- Uses real, ongoing, expert-designed research projects rather than toy or retrospective benchmark questions, giving genuine ecological validity.
- Transparent, auditable hallucination-classification methodology (Fig. 1) grounded in an external authority (Crossref) rather than self-reported LLM confidence.
- Reports fine-grained, model-by-model and field-by-field breakdowns (Tables II–IV) rather than a single aggregate hallucination number, enabling nuanced comparison (e.g., separating fabrication from metadata mismatch, and separating irrelevance from hallucination).
- Explicitly and honestly states its own limitations (small n=8 project sample, single-project Pro 5.5 comparison) rather than overselling generality.

## Limitations

- Sample size is small: only 8 projects total, and only 1 of those 8 was used for the forward-looking ChatGPT Pro 5.5 comparison.
- Gemini was tested only in its lighter "2.5-flash" configuration, not a frontier-tier variant, muddying cross-model comparisons.
- Relevance judgments came from a single human rater per project with no inter-rater reliability measurement, despite the paper acknowledging the judgments are subjective.
- The comparison is a snapshot in time (mid-2025 models plus one mid-2026 spot check); LLM capabilities are moving quickly, so the quantitative hallucination rates reported may already be stale for current-generation models.
- The study covers only the literature-review stage of the research workflow; project planning, execution, and writing (addressed in a promised companion paper) are out of scope here.

## Relevance

这篇论文直接量化了当前及最新一代LLM在真实科研场景中做文献调研的可靠性边界（重合率、虚构率、元数据错配率），对于依赖AI辅助论文写作/文献检索工作流的研究者具有很强的实操参考价值，尤其是其"人类与AI高度互补但AI仍需系统性核实"的结论，以及ChatGPT Pro 5.5展现出的显著改进趋势。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the fabrication/mismatch rates hold up if Gemini were tested at a frontier tier (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Pro) instead of the lightweight flash variant, and how would that change the model ranking?
- How would a multi-rater relevance-judgment protocol (rather than a single expert per project) change the "AI, irrelevant" percentages reported in Table II?
- Does the dramatic Pro 5.5 improvement (zero hallucinations on SU2) generalize across all 8 projects, or is SU2 (a theoretical/analytic topic with fewer, more canonical references) an easier case for zero-hallucination retrieval than data-heavy observational projects like AGN or GW?
- Could a hybrid workflow that explicitly asks the AI to search for references *the human didn't find* (rather than reproducing the same search) systematically increase the useful "AI, not human" complementary yield beyond the ~30-36% observed here?
- How do these hallucination rates compare across other STEM fields (e.g., biomedical literature, per the cited prior work) — is physics/astro/cosmology systematically easier or harder for LLM literature search due to arXiv's structured metadata and INSPIRE/ADS indexing?
