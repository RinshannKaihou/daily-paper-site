> **一句话总结：** 论文用contextual bandit和REINFORCE在线学习一个只有729种配置的"harness"（prompt风格/工具/记忆/规划/验证/步数预算）动作空间，结果发现在实际单日episode预算下（每次25-60轮），DSPy BootstrapFewShot生成的静态prompt基线在tool-use、代码生成（HumanEval）、检索QA（HotpotQA）三个领域、Ollama qwen2.5:7b和AWS Bedrock Haiku/Sonnet三个模型上普遍持平或超过在线自适应控制器（例如tool-use在Bedrock Haiku上DSPy-static达96%成功率/285 tokens，而REINFORCE仅62%/680 tokens），后续300 episode的加长实验证实这是样本效率不足而非能力天花板。

> **TL;DR:** The authors build an online RL controller (ε-greedy bandit and REINFORCE) over a fixed 729-configuration harness action space (prompt style, tool/retrieval policy, memory, planning depth, verification, step budget) for frozen LLM agents, using DSPy as the context assembler, and benchmark it against a DSPy BootstrapFewShot static baseline and random search across three verifiable domains (synthetic tool-use/CRM, HumanEval coding, HotpotQA retrieval QA) and two providers (Ollama qwen2.5:7b, AWS Bedrock). The headline finding inverts the paper's own hypothesis: DSPy-static matches or beats the adaptive controllers in nearly every cell, most starkly on tool-use/Bedrock Haiku (96% success at 285 tokens/episode vs. REINFORCE's 62% at 680 tokens), and a 5-12x longer follow-up run (300 episodes) shows bandit/REINFORCE plateauing at 0.61-0.65 success, still far below DSPy-static's 0.96 — a sample-efficiency gap tied to the 729-action space size, not a design failure.

## 核心贡献

- 将LLM agent的"harness"（prompt模板、工具/检索策略、记忆策略、规划深度、验证策略、最大步数）形式化为一个固定、可枚举、人类可读的六维离散动作空间（3^5×3=729种配置），而非像Meta-Harness或HyperAgents那样让agent自由生成/重写任意harness代码。
- 用经典的样本高效强化学习（ε-greedy contextual bandit、REINFORCE）在线学习该动作空间上的策略，奖励函数显式多目标：任务成功率、verifier分数、policy合规性、成本（tokens）、延迟、以及"unsupported-claim"惩罚。
- 用DSPy同时作为context assembler和最强非自适应基线的来源（BootstrapFewShot生成的静态prompt）。
- 发布跨三个领域（tool-use、HumanEval代码生成、HotpotQA多跳检索QA）的120个可验证任务数据集（96训练/24测试）、4620条完整的训练轨迹/奖励分解日志，以及一份provider无关的十步部署手册（RECIPE.md）。
- 诚实报告了一个"反预期"的核心实验结果：在实际可行的episode预算下，静态DSPy基线普遍优于在线自适应控制器，并通过加长实验将其归因为样本效率问题而非架构上限。

## Key Contributions

- Formalizes the LLM-agent harness (prompt template, tool/retrieval access, memory policy, planning depth, verification policy, step budget) as a small, fixed, enumerable, human-legible action space (3^5 × 3 = 729 configurations), positioned explicitly against Meta-Harness and HyperAgents, which let an LLM generate or self-rewrite arbitrary harness code.
- Learns a policy over this space online with an ε-greedy contextual bandit and REINFORCE, scored against a multi-objective reward combining task success, verifier score, policy compliance, cost, latency, and an unsupported-claim penalty.
- Uses DSPy both as the context assembler that instantiates each configuration and as the source of the strongest non-adaptive baseline (BootstrapFewShot static prompt).
- Releases a cross-domain verifiable-task suite (120 tasks: 40 synthetic CRM tool-use workflows, 40 HumanEval problems, 40 HotpotQA distractor-setting questions), 4,620 full trajectory/reward-decomposition logs across two model providers, and a ten-step deployment recipe (RECIPE.md).
- Reports and diagnoses the counter-hypothesis finding that a static, metric-optimized prompt beats online adaptive control at practical episode budgets, tracing this to a sample-efficiency gap (Ω(M) samples needed for M=729 actions) rather than a ceiling, via a dedicated 300-episode/run follow-up.
- Documents three concrete engineering pitfalls found and fixed during the project (bandit cold-start tie-breaking bug, episode crash-isolation gap, DSPy cache token-accounting bug) as a transparency/audit-trail contribution in its own right.

## Methodology

The harness is factored into six discrete levers: `prompt_style` ∈ {direct, structured, reflective}, `tool_policy` ∈ {never, when_needed, always} (reinterpreted as retrieval intensity for the QA domain), `memory_policy` ∈ {none, similar_successes, successes_and_failures}, `planning_policy` ∈ {none, brief_plan, plan_and_revise}, `verification_policy` ∈ {none, final_check, stepwise}, and `max_steps` ∈ {2, 4, 6} — giving 3^5 × 3 = 729 total configurations. For a given task and chosen configuration, a DSPy `Signature` (instructions vary by prompt style) and a DSPy `Module` (`Predict`, `ChainOfThought`, or a two-pass draft-then-revise module selected by planning policy) are constructed; memory policy controls how many past trajectories from a trace store are attached as few-shot demonstrations, tool policy controls tool/passage exposure, and verification policy optionally adds a second cheap self-check call. The reward is R = w_s·success + w_v·verifier_score + w_c·compliance − w_u·unsupported_claims − w_$·tokens/1000 − w_ℓ·latency/1000, with `success` defined per domain by a deterministic verifier: unit-test execution (coding/HumanEval), state-diff against a mock backend (tool-use/CRM), and exact match against gold answers with token-F1 as a softer verifier signal (retrieval/HotpotQA, using bundled gold+distractor paragraphs as a fully local corpus, no external search API).

Four controllers are compared: **Random** (uniform over 729 configs, lower bound), **ε-greedy contextual bandit** (linear value estimate per action over task features, optimistic initialization to avoid a cold-start failure mode described below), **REINFORCE** (softmax policy over 729 actions, linear-in-features logits, entropy regularization, moving-average baseline), and **DSPy-static** (a single fixed configuration — structured prompt, when-needed tools/retrieval, brief-plan, final-check verification — whose demonstrations come from running DSPy `BootstrapFewShot` once per model/domain against a small labeled subset, using the domain's own verifier as the optimization metric).

The experimental matrix is 4 optimizers × 3 domains × 2 providers (Ollama qwen2.5:7b local, AWS Bedrock Haiku), 2,460 episodes total, with a Bedrock Sonnet spot check (2 seeds × 40 episodes = 960 episodes) on the same matrix. Episode budgets differ by provider because Ollama's throughput was flat (~2.7 episodes/min) regardless of concurrency, giving it only 1 seed × 25 episodes/job versus Bedrock Haiku's 3 seeds × 60 episodes/job. A dedicated follow-up experiment (Section 5.2) runs bandit and REINFORCE for 300 episodes/run (5-12× the main-matrix budget) on tool-use/Bedrock Haiku specifically to test whether the online controllers' underperformance is a sample-efficiency artifact or a genuine ceiling.

## Results & Highlights

- **Tool-use, pooled across models/seeds (Table 1):** DSPy-static achieves 0.86 success [95% CI 0.66, 0.98] vs. REINFORCE 0.64 [0.59, 0.67], bandit 0.61 [0.58, 0.65], and random 0.62 [0.56, 0.69] — DSPy-static's point estimate is roughly 22 percentage points above REINFORCE's.
- **Tool-use on Bedrock Haiku specifically (Table 2, called out explicitly in the text):** DSPy-static reaches 96% success at 285 tokens/episode, versus REINFORCE's 62% success at 680 tokens/episode — both a large accuracy gap and roughly 2.4x more tokens spent by REINFORCE.
- **Retrieval QA, pooled (Table 1):** DSPy-static 0.31 [0.16, 0.42] vs. REINFORCE 0.18 [0.09, 0.23] and bandit 0.19 [0.09, 0.27] — the online controllers essentially match the 0.20 random-search floor on this domain.
- **Coding, pooled (Table 1):** the one domain where an adaptive controller nominally wins — REINFORCE 0.90 [0.80, 0.97] vs. DSPy-static 0.88 [0.85, 0.91] — but the confidence intervals overlap substantially, so this is not a clear win.
- **Bedrock Sonnet spot check (Table 3, stronger model, 2 seeds × 40 episodes):** DSPy-static leads on all three domains — tool-use 0.91 vs. REINFORCE's 0.48 and bandit's 0.60; coding 1.00 vs. 0.96/0.96; retrieval QA 0.53 vs. 0.30/0.30 — showing the static-baseline advantage holds even with a materially stronger underlying model.
- **Sample-efficiency follow-up:** on tool-use/Bedrock Haiku extended to 300 episodes/run (2 seeds, 1,200 additional episodes), bandit and REINFORCE plateau at 0.61-0.65 success — still well below DSPy-static's 0.96 on the identical (domain, model) pair; windowed 50-episode-bin traces show the bandit essentially flat (0.62, 0.58, 0.66, 0.60, 0.62, 0.68) and REINFORCE only mildly rising (0.60, 0.60, 0.70, 0.62, 0.66, 0.74).
- **Dataset scale:** 120 verifiable tasks (96 train/24 held-out, 80/20 split per domain: 40 CRM tool-use, 40 HumanEval, 40 HotpotQA), 4,620 logged episodes total across the main matrix, spot check, and sample-efficiency run; total measured spend was $7.62.
- No hallucination/quantitative-claim results beyond the above are reported as a separate table; the "unsupported-claim" reward term is folded into the pooled success/reward numbers rather than reported standalone.

## Authors & Credibility

Single-author paper by Debjyoti Paul; no institutional affiliation is listed on the paper itself. The work positions itself explicitly against two named 2026 systems (Meta-Harness, built on Claude Code, and HyperAgents from Meta AI) and references a "companion paper (Paper 1 in this release)" that reportedly develops stability/uncertainty-calibration analysis for the same controller — suggesting this is one of a coordinated pair of releases rather than a single standalone submission. All reported experiments are described as real measured runs against live model APIs (Ollama, AWS Bedrock), not simulated, with the full trajectory/reward logs released alongside the code and RECIPE.md.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core idea — treat harness configuration as a small enumerable action space and learn a policy over it with classic bandit/RL methods, rather than letting an LLM freely rewrite harness code — is a reasonable and well-motivated middle ground relative to the cited Meta-Harness and HyperAgents systems, and the framing (regressions attributable to a named lever, no code-execution access needed, black-box-API compatible) is a genuine practical contribution.

**Rigor:** The paper's most credible feature is that it reports a negative/counter-hypothesis result plainly rather than reframing it: the online controllers lose to a static DSPy baseline in nearly every cell, and the authors run a dedicated 5-12x-larger follow-up (300 episodes/run) specifically to distinguish "sample-efficiency gap" from "ceiling" rather than asserting the explanation without evidence. That follow-up is a genuine ablation-style check, and the three "pitfalls found and fixed" (bandit argmax ties toward action 0 with `tool_policy=never`, giving 0% success after 5 episodes; a `plan_and_revise` crash on Bedrock Sonnet from unbounded `reasoning` field consuming the token budget before the `code` field emitted; DSPy's disk cache returning an empty usage dict and silently zeroing token counts for cache hits) are specific, named, paper-grounded details rather than generic caveats — exactly the kind of concrete engineering honesty the CLAUDE.md-style bar for "genuine praise" should reward.

**Red flags / weaknesses:** (1) Episode budgets are small — 25-60 episodes/run in the main matrix for a 729-action space — and the paper itself argues this is provably insufficient (Ω(M) sample complexity), which means the headline comparison (DSPy-static vs. bandit/REINFORCE) is somewhat unfair to the online methods by the paper's own logic; a fairer head-to-head would need the bandit/REINFORCE initialized from the static baseline's configuration (a warm start), which the authors explicitly flag as future work rather than test here. (2) OpenAI was intended as a third full-matrix provider but the API key was rejected at run time (2026-07-26), so the "provider-agnostic" claim rests on only two providers (Ollama and Bedrock), both evaluated with different, non-comparable episode/seed budgets (1×25 vs. 3×60). (3) The tool-use domain — the domain with the largest reported gap — is a synthetic mock CRM backend built in the style of τ-bench, not a real production system, which limits how far the "harness control beats/loses to static prompting" conclusion generalizes to real tool-use deployments. (4) The unsupported-claim penalty, one of the paper's headline multi-objective reward terms, is only implemented as a lexical-overlap heuristic and only for the retrieval domain — it is not a general hallucination detector despite being framed as a first-class signal in the introduction. (5) Total spend ($7.62) exceeded the paper's own stated $5 per-run pre-flight cap in aggregate (each individual run respected its cap, but no whole-study cap existed) — a minor but self-reported governance gap. (6) The related-work section cites an unusually dense cluster of 2026 arXiv preprints (including one attributed to "Anonymous, 2026") that could not be independently verified here; readers should check these citations before relying on the framing of "two systems released this year."

**Reproducibility:** Strong on paper — code, the 120-task dataset, all 4,620 trajectory logs, and RECIPE.md are stated to be released, and the paper documents its own bugs and fixes in enough detail (file names like `coe/agent.py`, `coe/llm_adapter.py`) to be independently checked. This assessment cannot verify the actual repository contents from the PDF alone.

**Significance:** The practical takeaway — bootstrap with a static, metric-optimized prompt before deploying online harness control, and reserve adaptive control for post-bootstrap drift or task-distribution shift — is a useful, actionable piece of guidance for practitioners, arguably more useful than a clean win for the online controllers would have been, and is consistent with (and cites) Majumdar (2026)'s sample-complexity argument for large discrete action spaces.

## Strengths

- Transparent reporting of a result that contradicts the paper's own starting hypothesis, with a dedicated follow-up experiment (not just post-hoc rationalization) to test whether it's a sample-efficiency artifact.
- Three concretely diagnosed and fixed implementation bugs (bandit cold-start tie-break, crash isolation, cache-based token undercounting), documented with enough specificity to be independently useful to other practitioners building similar controllers.
- Multi-objective reward design (compliance, cost, latency, unsupported-claim penalty alongside task success) rather than optimizing a single accuracy number.
- Cross-domain (tool-use, coding, retrieval), cross-provider (local Ollama, AWS Bedrock Haiku/Sonnet) evaluation with released trajectory-level logs, which is unusually thorough for a single-author paper.
- Clear, auditable action-space design (six named levers, 729 total configurations) that avoids the "arbitrary self-modifying code" audit problem the paper attributes to Meta-Harness and HyperAgents.

## Limitations

- Main-matrix episode budgets (25-60/run, up to 3 seeds) are, by the paper's own admission, provably below what's needed for the online controllers to reach their ceiling on a 729-action space — so the headline comparison may narrow with a larger budget the study did not run.
- OpenAI, an intended third provider, could not be tested because the available API key was rejected; the "provider-agnostic" claim rests only on Ollama and Bedrock.
- The tool-use domain is synthetic (mock CRM backend), not a real production tool-use environment.
- The unsupported-claim/hallucination metric is a lexical-overlap heuristic scoped only to the retrieval domain, not a general-purpose hallucination detector.
- Aggregate spend ($7.62) exceeded the paper's own declared per-run cost cap ($5) when summed across the main matrix, spot check, and sample-efficiency run, which the authors note themselves as a gap in their own harness rather than omit.
- No warm-start experiment (initializing bandit/REINFORCE from the static baseline's configuration instead of a uniform prior) is actually run — it's proposed as future work, which leaves the paper's central "sample-efficiency, not ceiling" claim partially unconfirmed under the most obvious fix.

## Relevance

对于关注LLM agent工程化落地、DSPy/prompt优化、以及"harness优化 vs. 在线RL控制"权衡的场景，这篇论文提供了一个诚实的反例和可复用的729维动作空间设计+多目标奖励框架，同时其公开的120任务数据集和4620条轨迹日志可作为对比自建控制器的基准。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does warm-starting the bandit/REINFORCE policy from the DSPy-static configuration (rather than a uniform prior over 729 actions) close the gap within a practical episode budget, as the authors speculate but do not test?
- How does the static-vs-adaptive gap change under genuine task-distribution shift (the scenario RECIPE.md cites as the case for online control), which this study's fixed train/test task pools do not exercise?
- Would a smaller, hierarchically-structured action space (e.g., decomposing the 729 configurations into sequential lower-cardinality decisions) reduce the Ω(M) sample requirement enough to make online control competitive at 25-60 episodes?
- How sensitive are the reported success rates to the specific reward weights (w_s, w_v, w_c, w_u, w_$, w_ℓ), which are not reported as swept or ablated in the paper?
- Does the unsupported-claim lexical-overlap heuristic actually track hallucination rate, or would a stronger detector change the compliance/reward balance enough to shift which controller wins?
- On a real (non-synthetic) tool-use production system, does the DSPy-static advantage observed on the mock CRM domain hold, or is it partly an artifact of the mock backend's narrower state space?
