> **一句话总结：** 论文把"steering信号从哪来"这一常被当作预处理细节的选择，形式化为激活来源选择（源上下文×读出策略），在 Llama-3.1-8B/Qwen2.5-7B/Gemma-2-9B 三个模型和 entity/persona/reject/nonsense 四类共20个目标上系统对比后发现：宏平均成功率最高的粗粒度条件是"仅指令提示+末token读出"（0.476），而最贴近常见"用目标行为文本均值构造向量"做法的"仅答案+序列均值"反而最弱（0.198）；进一步的"边界态"构造（hybrid+末token）在三个模型上都优于所有粗基线，而作者提出的 tail subtraction 方法把 Diff-Mean 类向量在 Gemma 上的成功率从纯正例的 0.288 推到 0.863（PCA 类从 0.613 到 0.862），远超负例相减的对照基线。

> **TL;DR:** This paper studies an upstream choice usually treated as a setup detail in activation steering — *where the source activations come from* — formalizing it as activation-source selection (source context × readout policy) and holding the downstream steering intervention fixed. Across 3 instruction-tuned models (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Gemma-2-9B-IT) and 20 behavioral targets (11 entity, 7 persona/style, 1 reject, 1 nonsense; 1,950 held-out question instances per model), the coarse-grid comparison (Table 2) shows prompt-only + last-token sources reach the highest macro-average steering success (0.476) while answer-only + sequence-mean sources — the recipe closest to "average activations from target-bearing completions" — are the weakest (0.198), even though the target behavior is visibly present in the answer text. A refined "execution-boundary" construction (hybrid boundary prompt + last-token readout) beats all coarse baselines on every model (e.g., Gemma 62.5% vs. answer-only+mean's 28.1%, Figure 2). The paper's new method, tail subtraction, removes a matched tail-only (no target-conditioning) state from each boundary activation and lifts Diff-Mean steering success on Gemma-2-9B-IT from 0.288 (positive-only) to 0.863 (tail-subtracted), and PCA-based steering from 0.613 to 0.862 (Table 7) — well above the negative-subtraction contrastive baseline (0.601 and 0.769 respectively).

## 核心贡献

- 把"激活来源选择"（activation source selection）显式定义为下游 steering 之外的独立设计维度：σ = (c, ρ)，即用什么源上下文 c 产生激活、用什么读出策略 ρ（末token / 序列均值）从这些激活里取状态，并与向量构造方法（mean/diff-mean/PCA/diff-PCA/SAE）和下游注入方式解耦。
- 通过粗粒度网格实验（3 模型 × 3 种源文本 prompt-only/prompt+answer/answer-only × 2 种读出 last/mean × 5 种向量构造）证明：仅改变上游源激活、保持下游 steering 流程完全不变，就能让宏平均成功率在 0.198（answer-only+mean）到 0.476（prompt-only+last）之间大幅波动。
- 提出"执行边界态"（execution-boundary state）与"实现后轨迹态"（post-realization trace state）的区分：有效的 steering 信号更多来自模型"即将做什么"（instruction/icl/hybrid 边界提示 + 末token读出），而非"已经说过什么"（答案文本本身）；用 16 个关系补全任务做了目标外显与执行语义解耦的"干净实验"，证实边界态构造全面碾压含答案的后置态构造。
- 提出 tail subtraction：对每个目标条件化的边界提示，减去一个保留局部续写语境但去掉目标条件的"尾部匹配"提示所产生的激活，从而剥离共享的 prompt/续写语义、保留更干净的边界信号；在 mean 和 PCA 两类向量构造家族、三个模型上都稳定优于纯正例基线和负例相减的对照基线。

## Key Contributions

- **Formalizes activation source selection as σ = (c, ρ):** an explicit, separable upstream design axis (source-context function × readout policy) that is orthogonal to vector-construction method (mean, Diff-Mean, PCA, Diff-PCA, SAE) and to the downstream additive-steering intervention, which prior work almost always treats as a fixed setup detail.
- **Coarse controlled grid across 3 models × 4 task families** (entity, persona/style, reject, nonsense; 20 targets, 1,950 held-out question instances/model) shows changing only the source activation collection — holding layer, strength, and construction method search identical — swings macro-average steering success from 0.198 to 0.476 (Table 2).
- **Execution-boundary vs. post-realization distinction**, tested cleanly on 16 relation-completion tasks where target appearance is not entangled with continuing the same behavior (Section 6.2): boundary sources (instruction/ICL/hybrid + last-token, read before the answer is produced) steer far better than a post-realization source that already contains the completed answer with sequence-mean readout (Figure 3).
- **Mixed-state account for why answer-based sources sometimes work:** Diff-PCA projection ablation (Table 5) shows that subtracting an execution-relevant direction recovered from boundary states from answer-based mean vectors consistently *reduces* their steering success (by 0.032–0.070 for answer-only, 0.082–0.216 for prompt+answer, across 3 models), showing the answer-based vectors' effectiveness partly derives from an execution-aligned component, not target appearance alone.
- **Tail subtraction**, a simple phase-aware source-construction method: subtract a matched tail-only (target-conditioning removed) boundary activation from the full target-conditioned boundary activation before vector construction; consistently the best-performing source construction across both Mean and PCA vector families and all 3 models (Table 7).

## Methodology

The paper fixes the downstream intervention to standard additive activation steering (h̃ = h + αv at a chosen layer/strength) and the set of vector-construction rules g (Mean, Diff-Mean, PCA, Diff-PCA, and SAE-feature consensus), then varies only the *activation source* σ = (c, ρ): the source-context function c that produces the token sequence read for a given target, and the readout policy ρ that selects/aggregates hidden states from that sequence (last non-padding token, or sequence mean).

Two experimental designs isolate this variable:
1. **Coarse controlled grid (Section 4):** three source-text conditions — prompt-only (target instruction + query, no response), prompt-and-answer (instruction + query + a target-bearing completion), and answer-only (just the completion) — crossed with two readouts (last-token, sequence-mean), evaluated on 3 instruction-tuned models (Gemma-2-9B-IT, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) and 4 behavioral task families (entity, persona/style, reject, nonsense; 20 targets total, Table 8). For each condition the authors search source layer, intervention layer, and steering strength, and keep the best score.
2. **Refined execution-boundary construction (Section 5):** for each target, three boundary-prompt types — instruction (prepends only the explicit target instruction), icl (prepends 3 complete target-bearing demonstrations), hybrid (both) — all read via last-token at the point immediately before the target behavior is produced or continued (the "execution-boundary state" b_ℓ(z) = h_{ℓ,|p|}(p)), contrasted against post-realization trace states r_{ℓ,k}(z) that include already-generated target-bearing continuation.
3. **Untangling test on 16 relation-completion tasks (Section 6.2):** because relation completion (e.g., antonym mapping) has target appearance cleanly separated from continuing the behavior, boundary sources (read before the answer y) are compared against a post-realization source containing the completed y with sequence-mean readout — a scenario where target appearance and execution continuation are not conflated as they can be in style/persona tasks.
4. **Mixed-state diagnostic (Section 6.3):** a Diff-PCA direction is estimated from boundary-source activations as a representative "execution-relevant" direction u, then this component is projected out of answer-based Diff-PCA steering vectors (v_ablated = v − (vᵀu/‖u‖²)u) to test whether answer-based sources' effectiveness derives from a shared execution component.
5. **Tail subtraction (Section 7):** for a target-conditioned boundary prompt with context c_full, a matched tail-only prompt c_tail preserves the same local user question and assistant-prefix wording but removes target conditioning; the tail-subtracted source activation is s̃ = s^{ρ_last,c_full}(z) − s^{ρ_last,c_tail}(z), which is then fed into Mean ("Tail-Mean") or PCA ("Tail-PCA") vector construction and compared against positive-only and negative-subtracted (standard contrastive) baselines under the identical downstream protocol.

Evaluation uses a fixed Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct LLM judge that assigns binary labels for (a) target-attribute expression and (b) generation validity (non-collapsed, readable text); a generation counts as successful only if both hold. The judge is validated against human annotation on a stratified sample (87.0% overall exact agreement, range 82.0–92.0% across task families) and cross-checked with a Gemma-3-27B rescoring pass that preserves the main source-condition ranking pattern (Appendix A).

## Results & Highlights

- **Coarse source-condition comparison, macro-averaged over persona/entity/nonsense/reject (Table 2, 20 targets, 1,950 held-out questions/model):** prompt-only + last-token readout is the strongest coarse condition with average success **0.476** (Llama 0.336, Qwen 0.506, Gemma 0.586), while answer-only + sequence-mean — the condition closest to the common "average activations from target-bearing completions" recipe — is the weakest at **0.198** (Llama 0.119, Qwen 0.193, Gemma 0.281), despite the answer text visibly exhibiting the target behavior. This is a ~2.4x gap in success rate purely from changing the upstream source under an identical downstream steering pipeline.
- **Refined execution-boundary sources beat all coarse baselines on every model (Figure 2, same 4-family aggregation):** hybrid + last-token reaches 38.9% (Llama), 64% (Qwen), 62.5% (Gemma), outperforming both the strongest coarse baseline prompt-only + last (31.6/50.6/58.6%) and the mixed-state prompt-and-answer + mean baseline (23.1/52.7/52.1%), and dramatically outperforming answer-only + mean (11.9/19.3/28.1%) — roughly a 3x-to-6x relative improvement over the weakest condition on Llama and Qwen.
- **Diff-PCA projection ablation isolates the execution component in answer-based vectors (Table 5, macro-avg over the four behavioral families):** removing the boundary-derived execution direction from answer-only mean vectors drops steering success from 0.281→0.208 (Gemma), 0.119→0.087 (Llama), 0.193→0.123 (Qwen); removing it from the stronger prompt-and-answer mean vectors drops success from 0.521→0.305 (Gemma, a 21.6-point drop), 0.231→0.149 (Llama), 0.527→0.334 (Qwen, a 19.3-point drop) — showing the answer-based sources' partial effectiveness is explained by a shared execution-aligned component rather than target appearance alone.
- **Tail subtraction consistently outperforms both positive-only and negative-subtracted baselines (Table 7, macro-avg success across persona/entity/nonsense/reject):** in the Mean family, Gemma-2-9B-IT rises from 0.288 (positive-only) → 0.601 (negative-subtracted) → **0.863** (tail-subtracted); Qwen2.5-7B rises 0.406 → 0.584 → **0.725**; Llama-3.1-8B rises 0.168 → 0.348 → **0.546**. In the PCA family, Gemma rises 0.613 → 0.769 → **0.862**; Qwen 0.517 → 0.650 → **0.770**; Llama 0.226 → 0.475 → **0.590**. The largest gains are on Gemma and Qwen, matching a separate cosine-similarity diagnostic (Table 6) showing their full boundary states share high similarity (cos = 0.7715 and 0.8241 respectively) with matched tail-only states, i.e. a large shared local-continuation component to subtract away; Llama shows a weaker shared-tail component (cos = 0.5548) and correspondingly a smaller (but still positive) tail-subtraction gain.
- **Relation-completion tasks (16 tasks, Figure 3) cleanly separate target appearance from execution:** all three boundary-source constructions (instruction/icl/hybrid + last-token) steer far better than a post-realization source that already contains the completed answer with sequence-mean readout, with hybrid + last most stable across models — supporting the paper's central claim that steering efficacy tracks execution-boundary semantics rather than whether the target has already appeared in the source text.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core contribution — separating "where do source activations come from" from "how do you turn them into a vector" — is a genuinely underexplored axis; the paper explicitly states it does not introduce a new vector-construction rule, positioning itself as an orthogonal, complementary study to the (large) existing literature on steering-vector estimators. The execution-boundary vs. post-realization framing and the resulting tail-subtraction method are a modest but concrete methodological addition rather than a new steering paradigm.

**Rigor:** The controlled-grid design (same downstream layer/strength search, same vector-construction methods, varying only σ) is a clean ablation, and the relation-completion experiment (Section 6.2) is a well-chosen "unentangled" test case specifically designed so target appearance and execution-continuation cannot be conflated — a thoughtful methodological choice that strengthens the causal claim beyond what the persona/style tasks alone could support. The projection-ablation experiment (Table 5) directly tests the mixed-state hypothesis rather than just asserting it. One weak point: the tail-subtraction comparison in Table 7 pits the new method against a positive-only baseline and a "negative-subtracted" (standard contrastive Diff-Mean/Diff-PCA) baseline built from the same boundary sources — but these baselines are themselves derived from the paper's own refined boundary constructions, not from the strongest coarse condition found in Section 4 (prompt-only + last, 0.476 average); a reader cannot directly tell from Table 7 alone how tail-subtracted vectors compare to that original strongest baseline in the same units, since Table 7's per-family macro-average protocol differs slightly from Table 2's.

**Reproducibility:** Task inventory, dataset sizes (Table 8: 1,950 instances/model, 5,850 total), boundary-prompt templates (Tables 3, 4, 10–12), the relation-task inventory (16 tasks, Appendix B), and the LLM-judge prompt/validation protocol (87.0% human agreement, 82–92% range across families, Appendix A) are all documented in detail, which is good practice. However, the paper does not release code or steering vectors (no code/data availability statement observed in the main text or appendix excerpts reviewed), so independent reproduction would require re-implementing the full source-construction and grid-search pipeline from the paper's descriptions alone.

**Significance:** The finding that "answer-only + mean" — arguably the most common recipe in practice for building steering vectors from target-bearing completions — is the *weakest* condition tested (0.198 vs. 0.476 for prompt-only + last) is a genuinely actionable, practitioner-relevant result, and it echoes a broader theme in interpretability that intermediate/decision-relevant states are not the same as read-out/output-relevant states (the paper explicitly connects this to Alain & Bengio 2016 and Belinkov & Glass 2019 probing literature).

**Red flags / limitations the paper itself flags:** the study is confined to 3 open-weight models in the 7–9B range, a fixed additive-steering intervention family (no subspace/projection/token-dependent interventions), and standard vector-construction estimators (no supervised probes or more structured multi-vector bases); the reject and nonsense targets are explicitly described as synthetic evaluation targets "not recommended user-facing behaviors"; and the automatic LLM judge, while validated, means "reported scores should be read as controlled comparisons under a fixed judge, not as complete assessments of real-world safety or truthfulness" (the paper's own Ethical Considerations section). These are honestly disclosed rather than hidden.

## Strengths

- Cleanly isolates an upstream design choice (activation source selection) that most prior steering papers bundle into "setup," with a controlled grid that holds the entire downstream pipeline fixed.
- The relation-completion untangling experiment (Section 6.2) is a well-designed test that specifically rules out the confound between "target appears in source" and "source captures execution-continuation," strengthening the causal interpretation.
- The mixed-state account is not just asserted but tested via a direct projection-ablation experiment (Table 5) showing the execution component's removal degrades answer-based vectors.
- Tail subtraction is simple (a single subtraction of a matched tail-only activation) yet delivers large, consistent gains across two vector-construction families and three models (Table 7), with a plausible mechanistic diagnostic (Table 6 cosine similarities) explaining why the gains are larger for Gemma/Qwen than Llama.
- Detailed appendix documentation of task inventory, prompt templates, and judge validation supports scrutiny of the evaluation protocol.

## Limitations

- Empirical scope limited to three 7–9B-scale open-weight instruction-tuned models; base models, larger scales, multilingual/multimodal settings, and long-context/multi-turn steering are explicitly left untested.
- Only standard, relatively simple vector-construction methods (Mean, Diff-Mean, PCA, Diff-PCA, SAE) and a single additive-steering intervention family are studied; more structured constructions (supervised probes, multi-vector or nonlinear bases, subspace/projection interventions) may interact differently with source selection.
- Reject and nonsense targets are synthetic, deliberately unnatural behaviors chosen to test action-policy and semantic-correctness control, not representative of deployable steering use cases.
- Evaluation relies on an automatic LLM judge (Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct); although validated against human labels (87.0% agreement) and cross-checked with a second judge (Gemma-3-27B), it may still miss subtle quality or safety issues, and reported absolute success rates are best-of-grid scores that may overstate what a single preselected deployment configuration would achieve.
- No code, steering-vector, or dataset release is evident from the reviewed pages, which limits independent verification of the exact numbers.

## Relevance

对做 activation steering / representation engineering 或用 SAE 特征做行为控制的研究者，这篇论文提供了一个此前很少被单独检验的变量——"steering 向量到底该从哪段文本、哪个读出位置采集"——并给出了一个简单可复用的改进（tail subtraction）；对做 persona/style steering 或 refusal-direction 研究的人尤其有参考价值，因为论文直接指出"答案均值"这一常见做法在其基准上恰恰是最弱的条件。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would tail subtraction's gains persist for more structured vector-construction methods (supervised probes, multi-vector bases) or non-additive interventions (subspace projection, token-dependent injection), which the paper explicitly does not test?
- How sensitive is the "execution-boundary beats post-realization" finding to the exact boundary-prompt template (instruction/icl/hybrid), given that icl + last was notably weaker than instruction/hybrid + last in the main behavioral grid — is this a property of in-context learning format sensitivity (as the paper speculates, citing Min et al. 2022) or of the specific demonstrations used?
- Does the mixed-state account generalize to larger/frontier-scale models or to safety-critical behaviors beyond the paper's synthetic reject/nonsense targets, where the cost of a weak source-selection choice could be higher?
- Can tail subtraction be combined with SAE-based feature selection (which the paper includes as one construction method but reports less centrally) to isolate even cleaner execution-boundary features?
- What is the compute/data overhead of constructing matched tail-only prompts at scale across many targets, and does this generalize easily to open-ended or free-form target behaviors that lack a clean "boundary point" (e.g., long-form style or reasoning-chain steering)?
