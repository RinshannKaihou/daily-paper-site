Key Finding

All 9 tabular foundation models degrade under real distribution shift — real-data pretraining buys accuracy, not robustness.

Empirical Evaluation of Out-Of-Distribution Performance of Tabular Foundation Models ↗

Independent team (Universidad San Francisco de Quito · Rey Juan Carlos University, Madrid · Universidad Latina de Panamá) with no affiliation to any benchmarked model's developers (Prior Labs/TabPFN, Google Research/TabFM, Amazon/Mitra). arXiv preprint — not yet peer-reviewed.

Key Metric

9/9

models showed OOD ROC-AUC strictly below ID — shift gaps of 0.003–0.060 across every model × dataset combination, no exceptions (Fig. 2).

Why This Matters

Teams planning to deploy TabPFN-style models in healthcare or finance need real shift evidence and hardware feasibility data, not just IID benchmark numbers.

原文 (Relevance)：对研究表格基础模型（TabPFN 系列、TabICL、Mitra 等 ICL 式模型）实际落地鲁棒性和部署可行性的读者，这篇论文提供了少见的、以真实分布偏移和硬件资源约束为核心的实证证据。

Critical Edge

The headline claim that TFMs "inherit" classical models' ID/OOD relationship rests on a citation to a different paper — no tree-ensemble baseline was rerun on these exact splits.

一句话总结 · Chinese Abstract (source)

论文首次系统评测了 9 个表格基础模型（TabPFN v2/v2.5/v2.6/v3、TabICL、TabICLv2、Mitra、LimiX、TabFM）在 TableShift 基准的三个真实分布偏移数据集上的表现，发现所有模型无一例外都会性能退化（ROC-AUC 偏移差距 0.003–0.060），且真实数据预训练模型虽然 OOD 绝对性能最高（均值≈0.79）但偏移差距反而最大（均值≈0.019），大于合成先验模型的偏移差距（均值≈0.012）——说明其优势来自更强的域内拟合而非真正的分布外鲁棒性；此外 LimiX、TabFM、Mitra 均存在严重的显存/算力瓶颈。

Methodology

01
Nine TFMs evaluated zero-shot — no fine-tuning, no gradient updates, no validation split — on three TableShift datasets, each isolating one real-world shift type.
02
HELOC (label shift, 38 features) · Childhood Lead (socioeconomic shift, 17 features) · Voting (geographic shift, 380 features) — TableShift's own train/ID-test/OOD-test splits used as-is.
03
Models grouped by pretraining prior: real-world data (TabPFN v2/v2.5/v2.6/v3, TabFM); mixed SCM+tree-ensemble prior (Mitra); synthetic SCM-only priors (TabICL, TabICLv2, LimiX).
04
Training rows fed as in-context examples. Primary metric: ROC-AUC. Shift gap = ID − OOD ROC-AUC. Five seeds, mean ± std reported.
05
TabFM and LimiX dropped entirely from Voting (the largest dataset) on inference-cost grounds — only 7 of 9 models cover that dataset.

No fresh XGBoost/LightGBM/CatBoost baseline is run on these splits — the "TFMs behave like classical models" comparison leans on a different paper's numbers, not a controlled rerun.

The paradox: Real-world models also post the highest absolute OOD ROC-AUC (mean≈0.79 vs Mixed≈0.775, Synthetic≈0.77) — their lead is an ID-fitting artifact, not robustness (Fig. 3).

  • Mitra's weak point: competitive on Voting and Childhood Lead, but shows its largest ID→OOD drop of any model specifically on HELOC (label shift) — its mixed SCM+tree prior appears tuned for covariate shift, not label-marginal shift.
  • Scalability wall: LimiX needs a 47GB RTX 6000 Ada GPU with chunked inference just for Childhood Lead (11,807 rows) and is infeasible on Voting without subsampling; TabFM needed OOD-set chunking to avoid OOM; Mitra hard-caps training context at 10,000 rows, binding on both Childhood Lead and Voting.
  • No model breaks the classical "higher ID predicts higher OOD" proportional-degradation relationship, on any of the three datasets.

Strengths

  • First systematic, multi-model, multi-shift-type OOD study of TFMs, filling a gap in a literature that has so far only reported IID numbers.
  • Broad coverage of all three known TFM pre-training paradigms, including very recent releases (TabPFN v3, TabICL v2, TabFM, LimiX).
  • Grounded in the established TableShift benchmark, with three genuinely distinct real-world shift mechanisms across finance, healthcare, and political science.
  • Transparent, quantified deployment constraints — specific GPU model, memory ceilings, and row/feature counts that break each system, not vague hand-waving.
  • Honest conclusion that cuts against marketing narratives: real-data pretraining's OOD edge is an ID-fitting artifact, not a robustness property.

Limitations

  • No in-house tree-ensemble baseline rerun on the same splits — the "TFMs behave like classical models" claim rests on cross-paper comparison only.
  • Only three datasets/shift types evaluated; TabFM and LimiX are missing entirely from the largest one (Voting), thinning coverage of the mildest shift type.
  • The Discussion references "five datasets considered in this study" when only three are defined anywhere — an unresolved internal inconsistency.
  • Zero-shot default configurations only — no test of whether context tuning or OOD-aware example selection could close the shift gap.
  • Single fixed train/ID/OOD split per dataset; five seeds capture inference-time stochasticity only, not variability from resampling the shift partition itself.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Would a same-paper, same-split comparison against XGBoost/LightGBM/CatBoost show TFMs with smaller, larger, or equal shift gaps — not just a citation?
  2. Does the severity ranking (socioeconomic > label > geographic) hold on additional TableShift tasks beyond these three, or is it specific to this trio?
  3. Would in-context example selection — choosing training rows closer to the OOD distribution — reduce the shift gap without any gradient updates?
  4. What causes Mitra's outsized sensitivity to label shift specifically — its prior's implicit class-balance assumptions? Would reweighting the prior fix it?
  5. Can LimiX's and TabFM's memory bottlenecks be addressed by architectural changes (e.g. TabPFN v3's row-chunking) without sacrificing accuracy?
