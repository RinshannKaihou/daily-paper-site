Localized Adaptation Reveals Distinct Learning Signatures in Transformers ↗

Rebecca Ramnauth & Brian Scassellati · Yale University · 28 Jul 2026 · arXiv:2607.25663
LoRA Model Editing Layer Localization Mechanistic Interp. Synthetic Benchmark

Key Metric

42.7pp
Factual-association transfer: late-layer LoRA (63.1%) beats early-layer LoRA (20.4%) by 42.7pp — the cleanest single number showing that where you adapt changes what generalizes.
Mirror case: for lexical binding, full-stack beats early-layer transfer by 44.3pp — the opposite depth wins.

Why This Matters

A reusable recipe — budget calibration plus parameter-matched controls — for deciding which transformer layers to adapt for a given task type, instead of defaulting to full-stack LoRA everywhere.

Critical Edge

The entire benchmark is synthetic — invented facts, pseudowords, templated policies — so it remains untested whether these geometries hold on real editing tasks like CounterFact.

01

Methodology

Every objective is scored on three metrics at a given adaptation site — together its "adaptation geometry":

Acquisition = ID + paraphrase acc.+ Transfer = held-out generalization acc.+ Boundedness = negative-control acc.
  • Five synthetic objectives, 25 latent specifications each (12 train / 22 eval examples): lexical binding ("daxel" → bird), factual association (Norvessa's capital is Lumo), behavioral policy (context → action), causal mapping (intervention → effect), procedural reasoning (ordered steps → completion).
  • Per-objective calibrated budgets (8 or 10 examples/spec) via a two-stage sweep — decouples task difficulty from localization effects, a confound most adapter-placement papers ignore.
  • Four LoRA regimes on Llama-3.1-8B: full-stack, and early / middle / late quarter-depth windows, rank-matched per adapted layer.
  • Statistics: 3-seed paired contrasts, bootstrap 95% CIs, an omnibus permutation test for objective-specific structure, and a "mislocation penalty" (preferred-site minus non-preferred-site score).
  • Parameter-matched controls (rank-expanded localized / rank-reduced full-stack) rule out "it's just more trainable parameters."
  • Cross-model check: Mistral-7B, Gemma-2-9B, OLMo-2-7B, Qwen2.5-14B, reusing Llama-calibrated budgets with normalized quarter-depth regions.

03

Strengths & Limitations

Strengths

  • Three interpretable metrics (acquisition / transfer / boundedness) instead of one aggregate accuracy number.
  • Per-objective budget calibration avoids conflating task difficulty with localization — a subtle confound most adapter-placement papers skip.
  • Parameter-matched controls directly address the "it's just more parameters" objection.
  • Bootstrap CIs, omnibus permutation tests, and honest downgrading of 3-seed p-values to "descriptive."
  • Cross-model replication across 5 model families with an explicit budget-mismatch diagnostic, not silent cherry-picking.
  • Public code release (github.com/rramnauth2220/adaptation-geometries).

Limitations

  • Entirely synthetic benchmark — invented facts, pseudowords, templated rules — no real-world editing dataset used.
  • Cross-model budgets reused from Llama rather than independently calibrated, so weak results (Gemma) can reflect budget mismatch, not geometry.
  • Only 3 seeds per condition; authors themselves downgrade p-values to descriptive.
  • Only three coarse depth regions (early/middle/late quartiles) — no fine-grained per-layer analysis.
  • Only LoRA is studied — unclear if findings generalize to full fine-tuning or other PEFT methods.
  • Five objectives aren't shown to be ontologically exhaustive or non-overlapping; authors explicitly disclaim strict modularity.
04

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Would these geometries hold on real-world editing benchmarks (CounterFact, zsRE) instead of synthetic latent specifications?
  • Would independently calibrated per-model budgets improve cross-model replication for lexical binding and causal mapping — or surface new discrepancies?
  • How would finer-grained partitions (individual layers, overlapping windows, attention-vs-FFN splits) refine the early/middle/late trichotomy?
  • Do these signatures persist under full fine-tuning or other PEFT methods (prefix-tuning, (IA)³), or are they LoRA-specific?
  • Could a multi-region compositional adapter (separate localized regions per metric) actually outperform full-stack adaptation rather than merely approach it?
