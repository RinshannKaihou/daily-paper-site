> **一句话总结：** 论文提出"时间截断硬件"（Time-Truncation Harness），在教师模型用工具检索历史事件生成推理轨迹时动态屏蔽 T_cut 之后发布的文档，从而在不做拒绝采样、不等待未来事件揭晓的前提下合成无泄漏的"时序搜索与推理"训练数据；用该数据蒸馏 Qwen3-8B/32B 后，在 ForecastBench（Brier Score）上比同规模无硬件基线分别降低 19.48% 和 22.04%，在 FutureX 高难度题（L3+L4）上领先基线最多 17.74 个百分点。

> **TL;DR:** The paper introduces a time-truncation harness that dynamically restricts a Tool-Integrated-Reasoning (TIR) teacher's search environment to a pre-event cutoff date, generating leak-free forecasting trajectories from historical Manifold Market queries without rejection sampling or waiting for real-world resolution. Distilling these trajectories into Qwen3-8B/32B via mixed SFT (with general deep-search data) improves ForecastBench Brier Score over the "Mix-SFT (w/o harness)" ablation baseline by 19.48% (8B: 0.3167→0.2550) and 22.04% (32B: 0.2563→0.1998), and beats all baselines on FutureX's hardest tiers (L3+L4) by up to 17.74 points (32B, April), while a two-step leakage audit shows the harness cuts date-level leakage from 181/200 to 0/200 samples and raises valid-trajectory yield from 7.0% to 88.5%.

## 核心贡献

- 提出**时间截断硬件（Time-Truncation Harness）**：在教师模型（Kimi-K2.5）用 Search / Browse-with-Goal 两种工具检索历史预测事件时，为每条查询设定截止日期 T_cut = T_end − Δ_T（Δ_T=3 天），并通过"API 层查询改写（before:T_cut / endDate:T_cut）+ 事后网页过滤（丢弃 datePublished ≥ T_cut 的返回项）"双重机制，把检索环境替换为受限环境 E_{T_cut}，同时注入对齐系统提示，使模型将 T_cut 当作"当前时间"进行搜索规划。
- 构建**大规模未来预测语料**：从 Manifold Market 抓取 12,378 条 2025年6月–2026年3月间已结算的众包预测查询（涵盖 30 个真实领域、4 类问题），并设计**过程指标 Query Date-Span**（一条轨迹中所有检索式解析出的日期跨度）来量化搜索的时间广度。
- 通过双组对比实验证明：硬件迫使模型放弃"检索到未来结果直接抄答案"的捷径，转而扩大检索的时间跨度（中位 Query Date-Span 从 <150 天升至 >300 天）、增加工具调用次数，牺牲少量准确率（下降 5%–25%）换来更高质量、更少依赖复杂评判规则（rubric）的训练轨迹；两步泄漏审计（datePublished 检测 + Claude-Opus-4.8 内容审计）显示硬件把泄漏样本占比从 90.5%（181/200 date-leak）降到 0%，有效轨迹占比从 7.0% 升到 88.5%。
- 通过蒸馏实验验证"硬件干预的数据能转化为学生模型的参数化能力"：在 Qwen3-8B/32B 上采用"通用深度搜索轨迹 + 预测轨迹"混合 SFT，硬件版数据训练出的学生在 ForecastBench 和 FutureX 上均优于无硬件版数据训练出的学生及仅用通用深度搜索数据训练的基线，且学生模型继承了教师模型更宽的时间检索跨度这一行为特征。

## Key Contributions

- **Time-Truncation Harness**: during teacher (Kimi-K2.5) trajectory synthesis via a Search / Browse-with-Goal tool environment, the harness enforces a cutoff $T_{\text{cut}} = T_{\text{end}} - \Delta_T$ (with $\Delta_T = 3$ days) for every historical forecasting query, realized through **API-aware query rewriting** (pushing `before:T_cut` / `endDate:T_cut` into the search backend) plus a **post-hoc webpage filter** (dropping any result whose `datePublished` is missing, unparsable, or $\geq T_{\text{cut}}$), together with an aligned system prompt that tells the model $T_{\text{cut}}$ is "now."
- **Large-scale forecasting corpus + process metric**: 12,378 queries collected from Manifold Market's API (crowd-sourced probabilities, resolved outcomes, June 2025–March 2026 window chosen to avoid teacher/student pretraining contamination), spanning 30 real-world domains and 4 question types (79.7% binary, 17.5% multiple-choice, 2.1% numeric, 0.6% date). The paper introduces **Query Date-Span** $\mathcal{M}(\tau) = \max \mathcal{D}(\tau) - \min \mathcal{D}(\tau)$, the calendar range spanned by all dates parsed from a trajectory's search queries, as a process-based proxy for temporal search breadth.
- **Empirical leakage/efficiency analysis**: a two-step audit (datePublished check + Claude-Opus-4.8 content audit) on 200 sampled trajectories per group shows the harness eliminates date-level leakage entirely (0/200 vs. 181/200 without the harness) and lifts the valid-trajectory yield from 7.0% to 88.5%, directly improving the sampling-efficiency objective $J(\mathcal{A}) = \text{Throughput}\times(1-\text{RejectionRate})$ defined in the paper's preliminaries.
- **Harness-intervened distillation**: mixed SFT on Qwen3-8B/32B (50,000-instance mixtures of general deep-search trajectories + forecasting trajectories) comparing three groups — DeepSearch-SFT (general deep-search only), Mix-SFT (w/o harness), and Mix-SFT (w/ harness) — with the harness variant winning on both ForecastBench (Brier Score) and FutureX (Score, L1–L4) benchmarks.
- **Ablation and sensitivity analysis**: decomposing the harness into "time-constrained environment" and "aligned system prompt" components (Table 4) shows the truncated environment is the primary leakage-blocking mechanism (accuracy 83.61%→75.93%, Query Date-Span 123→365 days without the prompt), while the prompt plays a secondary role; a sweep over $\Delta_T \in \{0,3,5,7\}$ shows $\Delta_T=0$ underperforms and behavior stabilizes for $\Delta_T \geq 3$, motivating the $\Delta_T=3$ choice.

## Methodology

The paper formalizes TIR trajectories as $\tau_{tir} = (S_0, P, a_1, o_1, \dots, a_T)$ where each intermediate action $a_i$ pairs reasoning content with a tool call and $o_i$ is the environment's observation (Eq. 1). It identifies two failure modes of prior forecasting-data pipelines: (1) **static observation** designs freeze retrieval into a single pre-collected context, losing the ability to dynamically re-query (Eq. 4); (2) **rejection sampling** over an unconstrained environment discards most trajectories once a temporal-leakage check (`TempLeak(o_i, T_end) = 1[∃d ∈ retrieved docs : ρ(d) ≥ T_end]`, Eq. 2) or a quality rubric $\mathcal{R}(\tau)=0$ fails, collapsing throughput; and (3) restricting to genuinely **unresolved queries** ($T_{\text{end}}(q) > T_{\text{now}}$) guarantees zero leakage but forces waiting for real events to resolve, creating reward sparsity and training-loop latency for RL.

Instead of filtering trajectories after the fact, the harness constrains the **environment itself**. For each historical query with known resolution time $T_{\text{end}}$, it fixes $T_{\text{cut}} = T_{\text{end}} - \Delta_T$ and replaces the unconstrained environment $\mathcal{E}$ with a truncated environment $\mathcal{E}_{T_{\text{cut}}}$ that only returns documents $d$ with publication time $\rho(d) \leq T_{\text{cut}}$ (Eq. 8), enforced both at the source (query rewriting through each search backend's native date-filter interface) and downstream (post-hoc filtering by the `datePublished` field). An aligned system prompt $\tilde{S}_0 = S_0 \oplus \mathcal{I}(T_{\text{cut}})$ tells the agent to treat $T_{\text{cut}}$ as "today," instructing it not to rely on internal pretraining knowledge and to treat the target event as unresolved.

**Data pipeline.** 12,378 Manifold Market queries (filtered for verifiability — fully resolved markets only; objectivity — excluding opinion polls; diversity — 30 domains, 4 question types; and contamination control — resolution window June 2025–March 2026, after the teacher/student's knowledge cutoff) are rolled out twice with Kimi-K2.5 as teacher: once under the unconstrained environment $\mathcal{E}$ ("w/o-harness" group) and once under $\mathcal{E}_{T_{\text{cut}}}$ with $\Delta_T=3$ days ("w/ harness" group). Google Serper API is used for all rollouts.

**Distillation setup.** Two open-source thinking models, Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-32B, are full-parameter SFT-fine-tuned. Because Qwen3's native multi-turn tool-calling is weak, the paper uses a **mixed-corpus SFT strategy**: each of the three training groups totals exactly 50,000 instances — DeepSearch-SFT (50K general multi-hop deep-search trajectories, the baseline), Mix-SFT (w/o harness) (11,868 valid unconstrained forecasting trajectories + 38,132 general deep-search trajectories), and Mix-SFT (w/ harness) (11,261 valid harness-intervened trajectories + 38,739 general deep-search trajectories) — controlling for data volume so the comparison isolates the effect of harness-intervention rather than corpus size.

## Results & Highlights

- **Leakage elimination (Table 1, 200-sample audit per group):** w/ harness — Date-Leak 0/200, Content-Leak 23/200, Valid 88.5%; w/o harness — Date-Leak 181/200, Content-Leak 101/200, Valid 7.0%. The harness fully removes datePublished-level leakage and lifts usable-trajectory yield by roughly 12.6x (7.0%→88.5%).
- **ForecastBench (May) Brier Score, lower is better (Table 2):** Qwen3-8B backbone — DeepSearch-SFT 0.4344±0.0125, Mix-SFT (w/o harness) 0.3167±0.0098, Mix-SFT (w/ harness) 0.2550±0.0064 (best), a 19.48% relative improvement over the w/o-harness ablation. Qwen3-32B backbone — DeepSearch-SFT 0.4365±0.0112, Mix-SFT (w/o harness) 0.2563±0.0071, Mix-SFT (w/ harness) 0.1998±0.0043 (best), a 22.04% relative improvement. The untuned "Origin" Qwen3 model fails multi-turn tool calling entirely and outputs a near-constant 0.5 probability, making its Brier Score meaningless (explicitly noted by the authors).
- **FutureX L3+L4 (harder ranking/numeric tasks, Table 3):** Mix-SFT (w/ harness) outperforms all baselines — leading by 13.46% (April) and 8.82% (May) on the 8B backbone, and 17.74% (April) and 14.08% (May) on the 32B backbone. On easier L1–L2 tasks, the harness variant shows no consistent gain and sometimes underperforms Mix-SFT (w/o harness) or even DeepSearch-SFT (e.g., Qwen3-32B L1 April: DeepSearch-SFT 73.17±0.00 vs. Mix-SFT w/ harness 70.85±5.27), which the authors attribute to unneeded context noise from enforced broad temporal search on simple, low-information-need queries.
- **Behavioral/process metrics:** median Query Date-Span for harness-trained corpora exceeds 300 days across nearly all categories vs. under 150 days without the harness (Figure 4); this behavior transfers to students — Mix-SFT (w/ harness) students exhibit the highest median Query Date-Span across all evaluation benchmarks (Figure 5), and student tool-call counts do not consistently rise, suggesting gains come from query quality/temporal reasoning rather than more test-time search calls (Figure 6).
- **Ablation (Table 4):** removing both harness components ("no harness") gives 83.61% accuracy / 5.53 avg. tool calls / 123-day median Query Date-Span; the full harness gives 74.27% / 11.04 / 365 days — confirming the time-constrained environment (not the prompt alone) is the primary driver of both the accuracy drop and the temporal-breadth expansion.
- **Sensitivity analysis over $\Delta_T$ (Figure 7):** $\Delta_T=0$ underperforms $\Delta_T \in \{3,5,7\}$ (accuracy 78.78% vs. ~74.3%, Query Date-Span 181 vs. 365 days), indicating a cutoff too close to settlement is less leak-proof; $\Delta_T \geq 3$ is stable, motivating the paper's choice of $\Delta_T = 3$.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core idea — truncating the retrieval environment itself (rather than filtering trajectories post-hoc or waiting for real-world resolution) — is a genuinely useful and fairly simple engineering insight that directly targets the throughput/leakage tradeoff formalized in Eq. (3). It is incremental relative to prior harness-based forecasting systems (Milkyway, FutureWorld, Echo) rather than a new learning algorithm, but the paper is honest about this framing (contribution is "data synthesis efficiency," not a new architecture).

**Rigor:** The leakage audit (Table 1) is a real strength — a concrete two-step verification (metadata check + independent LLM content audit via Claude-Opus-4.8) rather than an unverified claim. The ablation (Table 4) and sensitivity sweep (Figure 7) cleanly isolate the environment vs. prompt contributions and justify the $\Delta_T=3$ hyperparameter rather than picking it arbitrarily.

**Reproducibility:** Data (Manifold queries via a public HuggingFace dataset link and API) and benchmarks (ForecastBench, FutureX) are publicly accessible, but the teacher model (Kimi-K2.5, closed weights) and the search backend (Google Serper API) introduce cost and non-determinism that limit exact reproducibility; the SFT training corpora themselves (the 50K-instance mixtures) are not stated to be released.

**Significance / red flags:** The paper explicitly acknowledges that "the synthetic data quality remains fundamentally bounded by the inherent capabilities of the teacher model" and that the method mitigates rather than fully eliminates leakage (23/200 residual content-leak even with the harness). A notable weak point is the comparison baseline structure: the "Origin" Qwen3 baseline in Table 2 is stated by the authors themselves to be meaningless (near-constant 0.5 output), and the paper does not compare against the actual SOTA agent-harness systems it cites in related work (Milkyway, FutureWorld, Echo) — it explicitly states in Limitations that it does not chase "SOTA performance (which still relies on agent framework)," so all comparisons are internal ablations (DeepSearch-SFT / Mix-SFT w/o harness) rather than external competitive baselines. The degradation on FutureX L1–L2 easy tasks is disclosed rather than hidden, which is a point in favor of the paper's honesty, but it does mean the harness's benefit is data/difficulty-dependent rather than uniform.

## Strengths

- Directly measures and demonstrates leakage reduction with an independent two-step audit rather than asserting it.
- Ablation and sensitivity studies (Table 4, Figure 7) isolate which harness component (environment truncation vs. prompt) drives the effect, and justify the chosen hyperparameter.
- Introduces an interpretable process-based metric (Query Date-Span) that is validated to correlate with downstream forecasting performance across both teacher corpora and distilled students (Figure 5).
- Reports negative/mixed results honestly (FutureX L1–L2 degradation, residual content-leak, no SOTA comparison) rather than cherry-picking only favorable benchmarks.

## Limitations

- Synthetic data quality is capped by the teacher model's (Kimi-K2.5) own forecasting and search competence — errors or blind spots in the teacher propagate into the distilled students.
- The evaluation focuses on internal ablations (DeepSearch-SFT, Mix-SFT w/o harness) rather than reproducing or comparing against competing agent-harness or training pipelines (Milkyway, FutureWorld, Echo, UniPat-AI), which the authors attribute to reproducibility difficulty of those systems.
- The harness mitigates rather than eliminates temporal leakage (residual 23/200 content-level leakage even with the harness); the authors note lightweight rubric-based filtering could further reduce this but was not implemented.
- Gains do not generalize to easy/low-information-need tasks (FutureX L1–L2), where the enforced broad temporal search can introduce unneeded context noise and slightly hurt accuracy.
- Experiments are limited to two model scales (8B, 32B); the authors flag scaling further as future work rather than having verified it.

## Relevance

该工作与"用工具集成推理（TIR）训练小模型做未来预测"的路线高度相关，其核心思路——通过约束检索环境而非事后拒绝采样来解决时序泄漏——为构建无泄漏预测类训练语料提供了一个可直接复用的轻量级工程方案，对关注 LLM 蒸馏、深度搜索 agent 训练数据构造的研究有较高参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the time-truncation harness combined with online RL (as the authors propose in Future Works) reduce the reward-sparsity problem that afflicts FutureWorld/Echo-style approaches, and how would training-loop latency compare?
- How sensitive are the reported gains to the choice of teacher model (Kimi-K2.5) — would a weaker or stronger teacher change the relative benefit of harness-intervened data?
- Could the residual 23/200 content-level leakage be closed with a lightweight rubric filter without re-introducing the throughput collapse the harness was designed to avoid?
- Why does Mix-SFT (w/ harness) underperform on FutureX L1–L2 easy tasks, and could a difficulty-adaptive harness (looser cutoff for simple queries) recover that gap without sacrificing L3–L4 gains?
- How would this approach compare directly against the SOTA agent-harness baselines (Milkyway, Echo, FutureWorld) the paper explicitly declines to reproduce, on the same benchmarks?
