Quotient Dynamics, Effective Curvature, and Implicit Bias in Positive Quadratic Networks

Pengcheng Cheng · School of Mathematics, Jilin University · 2026-07-28 · single-author, JMLR template left unedited, contact via QQ.com email
Key finding Euclidean gradient training of a two-layer quadratic network provably projects exactly — not approximately — onto Riemannian gradient flow on the PSD quotient manifold.
一句话总结：正定二次网络的欧氏梯度训练精确投影为商流形上的黎曼梯度流，可交换欠定测量下退化为熵镜像流；但论文自证的充分样本量比实际可行值保守约十四个数量级。

Why this matters

A rare, fully rigorous bridge from quotient-manifold geometry and mirror-descent implicit bias to the actual dynamics of a trained network.

Critical edge

By the paper's own numbers, its headline recovery theorem is non-operative at any realistic sample size: proven N∗≈2.01×10¹⁶ vs. 100% empirical success at n=152.

§2–4 Methodology, distilled

The paper studies fU(x) = xTUUTx, a two-layer network whose predictor Q=UUT is a rank-r PSD matrix, but whose factor U is identifiable only up to right-multiplication by O(r). The authors prove the quotient Fd,r/O(r) is exactly the PSD manifold Mr+ (Thm 2.1), and that ordinary Euclidean factor-gradient flow is automatically "horizontal" — no gauge-direction component — so it projects exactly onto Riemannian gradient flow under the induced quotient metric (Thm 2.3), with an explicit O(η²) correction term for finite-step discretization (Prop 2.6).

Section 3 specializes to Gaussian rank-one matrix sensing: a closed-form "quotient Hessian" at the interpolant (Thm 3.1) is tied to the empirical measurement operator's spectrum, feeding a moment-based spectral initializer and an end-to-end local-convergence recovery theorem (Thm 3.9). Section 4 restricts to pairwise-commuting, underdetermined measurements (r=d), where the same factor dynamics reduce exactly to an entropy mirror flow (Thm 4.1) whose small-initialization limit selects the minimum-trace interpolant (Thm 4.4–4.5). All six numerical checks (Section 5) run in PyTorch, double precision, RK4 integration for continuous dynamics, and the exact discrete recursion for finite-step experiments — entirely on synthetic Gaussian or hand-built commuting measurement systems.

+ Strengths

  • Every theorem in Sections 2–4 has a full proof, checked against numerical verification that matches to floating-point roundoff in several cases.
  • The central claim — exact projection onto Riemannian gradient flow with a bounded finite-step correction — closes a real gap left open by prior quotient-manifold theory.
  • Unusually transparent: explicit remarks flag the sample bound as non-optimal, the step size as an oracle condition, and the small-initialization/finite-step limit order as sequential, not joint.
  • The six experiments are used to expose the looseness of the paper's own bounds — basin radius, sample complexity, oracle step, secant rate — not just to show favorable agreement.

– Limitations

  • Proven sample complexity (Thm 3.9) is roughly 14 orders of magnitude more conservative than the paper's own observed recovery threshold.
  • The gradient-descent step-size condition is an oracle rule requiring the unknown λr(Q∗), λ1(Q∗) — not usable as a practical learning-rate rule.
  • Section 4's Bregman/entropy selection theory holds only for pairwise-commuting measurements, not general noncommuting matrix sensing.
  • All experiments are small-scale (d≤20), synthetic, and self-referential (theorem-vs-simulation), with no code or data release.
  • The manuscript still contains unedited JMLR template placeholders ("Editor: My editor," "Author One and Author Two," a stray "remainder omitted in this sample" line).

? Questions for further exploration

  • Could restricting the concentration argument to the tangent space or a low-rank secant set (rather than the full ambient space) close much of the ~10¹⁴ gap between the proven sample bound and the observed n=152 recovery threshold?
  • Does the entropy tie-breaking / minimum-trace selection theory of Section 4 extend, even approximately, to noncommuting (general RIP) measurement families?
  • Can the oracle step-size condition be replaced with a practical, data-driven rule (e.g., backtracking or Polyak step) while retaining a comparable linear-convergence guarantee?
  • How does the effective-curvature/local-training-rate correspondence (matched to ~10⁻&sup4; relative precision) generalize to deeper networks or non-quadratic activations, where the exact quotient identification no longer holds?
  • Would approximately-commuting measurement operators yield a perturbative extension of the Bregman/entropy selection theorem, bridging the commuting and fully general matrix-sensing regimes?
