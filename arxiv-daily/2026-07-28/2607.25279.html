Theory / Statistical Physics of Transformers · Zero Fitted Parameters

A closed-form "spin" theory of Attention predicts when an LLM's greedy decoding will suddenly tip into undesired output.

Why This Matters

If AI "hallucination" has a predictable physical trigger, harmful chatbot outputs become a foreseeable engineering risk rather than unpredictable emergence — with real legal and policy stakes.

Critical Edge

All 7 tested models are small base language models with no RLHF or safety tuning — despite the title's "ChatGPT-like" framing and its citations of real commercial-chatbot harm lawsuits.

01 Methodology

  1. Attention as two-body spin coupling: each token is a high-dimensional "spin"; pairwise dot products set the couplings, softmax normalizes them. Stripping LayerNorm and MLP isolates a "pure-Attention" reduction — a discrete, finite-depth analog of a Feynman many-body path expansion (2ℓ ordered paths) — giving an exact closed-form propagator U(ℓ).
  2. Tipping as first-passage between basins: in a three-basin reduction (A = prompt, B = desirable output, D = undesirable output), the competition coordinate xn(t) = rn·(D−B) defines a "tipping wall" at x = 0. Substituting the propagator into the wall condition yields a closed finite-layer threshold — an algebraic consequence, not a fitted rule.
  3. Disorder unifies two regimes: normalized column entropy of the Attention matrix is measured across layers. Low disorder → near one-hot Attention → dynamics freeze after only ℓeff≈5 layers. High disorder → Attention acts as an "active mixer," delaying lock-in.
  4. Basin vectors & causal test: A/B/D are mean-pooled penultimate-layer residual-stream centroids from six fixed probe phrases per basin. A causal intervention swaps Attention→identity at one layer at a time and measures the Jensen–Shannon divergence of the resulting repetition-period distribution.
  5. Self-generalization check: the ℓ=1 limiting case exactly recovers the authors' own earlier "effective atom" model (AIP Advances / PNAS Nexus), positioning this paper as a general-ℓ extension of prior single-head work by the same two authors.
Competing basins B/D separated by the tipping wall at x=0; token trajectory rn(t) starts near prompt A and crosses into whichever basin the closed-form threshold predicts.

Strengths

  • Derives an exact, closed-form finite-depth propagator (Eq. 1) for a well-defined "pure-Attention" reduction, explicit throughout about what is exact versus a limiting-case simplification.
  • The disorder (column-entropy) framework gives one falsifiable knob — freezing vs. active mixing — tested with a genuine causal intervention (H→I), not just correlational entropy measurements.
  • Cross-architecture validation spans three independent training lineages (OpenAI, EleutherAI, Meta) and a 100× parameter range (124M–12B).
  • Worked analytic examples let a reader verify the algebra from stated assumptions (WV=I, LN=identity, MLP=0) all the way to the final threshold formula.

Limitations

  • No RLHF-tuned, instruction-tuned, or safety-guardrailed model appears in the systematic tests, despite the title and motivation centering on "ChatGPT-like" safety failures.
  • B/D basin probes rest on just six fixed phrases per basin per domain — a small, hand-curated set whose generalizability is untested in the main text.
  • The headline 19/21 statistic has a small denominator, no reported confidence interval, and excludes a "factual control" subset without disclosing its results.
  • Eq. 1–2 depend on simplifying assumptions (WV=I, LN=identity, MLP=0) that hold only in the analytic reduction, not in the full model.
  • No public code or data link is given in the reviewed PDF; reproducibility rests on unreviewed Supplementary Materials.

? Questions for Further Exploration

Does the 19/21 accuracy hold with a larger, less hand-curated probe set, or against RLHF-tuned chat models like Llama-3-Instruct?
Does the closed-form threshold survive once positional encodings and full multi-head interactions are added back in, beyond the Frobenius mean-field aggregation?
Can the disorder signal become a real-time, per-layer monitoring probe for deployed chat systems — and what would its false-positive/negative rates look like?
What happens to the n* threshold in mixture-of-experts or non-causal, retrieval-augmented architectures outside the GPT-2/Pythia/OPT family tested here?
