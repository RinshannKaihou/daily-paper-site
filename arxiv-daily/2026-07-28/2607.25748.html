Loss Invariance Determines What Concept Layers Encode: Volume Grounding in Echocardiography

Hyunkyung Han & Min Jung Kim · Yonsei University College of Medicine · 2026‑07‑28 · IEEE JBHI submission
concept bottleneck models echocardiography ejection fraction interpretability invariance medical image analysis

An EF‑only volume concept bottleneck matches direct‑regression accuracy — yet its predicted volumes collapse to near‑zero physical scale.

一句话总结 · TL;DR (中文) 用射血分数（EF）目标训练的容积概念瓶颈层，EF 误差不输直接回归（MAE 6.89 vs 7.13），但由于 EF 是两个容积的比值、对整体缩放不变，概念层学到的容积预测标准差会坍缩到 0.1 mL（参考值 35.7/45.7 mL），必须额外用毫升单位监督容积本身才能恢复物理尺度。

Why This Matters

Gives concept‑bottleneck researchers a simple check: test any ratio‑based clinical target — EF, fractional shortening — for scale invariance before trusting concept accuracy.

Critical Edge

The paper's broadest claim — that other scale‑invariant targets like shape or margin descriptors fail the same way — is argued by analogy alone, never tested.

01

Methodology

A VideoMAE video transformer — pretrained via masked autoencoding on EchoNet‑Dynamic, then fine‑tuned for EF regression — encodes a 32‑frame cardiac‑cycle clip into 16 temporal tokens. A shared per‑token head (LN→Linear→GELU→Linear→softplus) maps every token to a scalar volume; the model itself selects the cardiac phase — EDV is the max over tokens, ESV the min — with no residual path around this bottleneck, so anything the concept layer fails to carry is categorically unavailable downstream. EF is then computed analytically, never learned directly. The objective is L = L_EF + μ·L_vol: at μ=0 only EF error is optimized ("EF‑only"); at μ∈{3,10} an added L1 loss on both volumes in millilitres restores "grounding." An earlier regularizer that pushed toward the predicted ESV/EDV ratio directly turned out to be algebraically identical to the EF loss itself — same gradient, no new constraint.

03

Strengths & Limitations

● Strengths

  • Falsifiable theory (invariance‑orbit argument) tied to a concrete, clinically meaningful variable — EDV matters diagnostically on its own, not just as an intermediate computation.
  • Rare transparency: the authors disclose their own failed regularizer design and show exactly why standard concept‑accuracy metrics couldn't catch it.
  • Quantifies the trade‑off (+0.404 EF MAE for restored scale) instead of just asserting one exists, and separately diagnoses and fixes an optimization instability the fix introduced.
  • Two targeted controls: a temporal‑alignment check (rules out phase misalignment) and a "normalized" condition that isolates batch‑statistic rescaling from the degenerate ratio regularizer.

○ Limitations

  • Single dataset, single institution (EchoNet‑Dynamic) — no cross‑scanner or cross‑protocol validation.
  • Only 2–3 random seeds per configuration; explicitly insufficient to characterize the optimization landscape, despite an observed lr=1e‑4 divergence in 2/3 seeds.
  • Residual volume compression (predicted SD reaches only ≈half the reference spread even after grounding) is unexplained; the pixel‑spacing account is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated cause.
  • The broadest generalization claim — that other scale‑invariant targets fail the same way — is argued by analogy only, never tested empirically.
  • Code is not yet released ("upon publication"), limiting independent reproduction.
04

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does the invariance‑orbit diagnostic generalize empirically — not just by analogy — to other ratio‑valued clinical targets, e.g. fractional shortening, valve regurgitant fraction, or shape/margin descriptors?
  2. Could residual volume compression be resolved by restoring physical pixel‑spacing metadata to the input, directly testing the paper's own "scale hypothesis," rather than relying on absolute‑unit loss supervision alone?
  3. How sensitive is the +0.404 EF‑MAE grounding cost to the choice of μ, given no monotone dose‑response was found between μ=3 and μ=10 within seed noise — would a learned or annealed μ schedule help?
  4. Would the lr=1e‑4 optimization instability (2/3 seeds diverging) reappear on other encoder architectures or datasets, or is it specific to VideoMAE fine‑tuning dynamics interacting with the added volume loss?
  5. The degenerate ratio regularizer was only caught by manual algebraic inspection — what general‑purpose diagnostic could automatically flag a regularizer whose gradient is redundant with the main loss under the objective's invariance group?
