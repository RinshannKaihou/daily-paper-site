> **一句话总结：** 本文提出几何 Rashomon 对齐（gRA）——用在特征空间边界框内均匀采样的 1000 个合成点计算两个模型的预测一致率，在 92 个 UCI 数据集上对比剪枝与未剪枝决策树发现，gRA 与准确率差的相关性仅为中等（r=0.514），与基于测试集计算的分布式对齐 dRA 相关性虽强但不完美（r=0.745），并用合成 2D 案例展示了两棵决策树 gRA 高达 0.95（决策边界几乎重合）却准确率相差 12 个百分点、以及 SVM-RBF 和 MLP 准确率同为 0.94 却只在 45% 的实例空间上意见一致（gRA=0.45）等准确率或分布式一致率单独都无法揭示的结构性差异案例。

> **TL;DR:** Rashomon Alignment (RA) proposes geometric alignment (gRA) — agreement between two models measured on 1,000 points sampled uniformly from the bounding box of the feature space, rather than on observed data — as a distribution-independent complement to standard "distributional" functional similarity (dRA, agreement on held-out test data). Across 92 UCI datasets comparing pruned vs. unpruned decision trees, gRA correlates only moderately with accuracy difference (r=0.514) and strongly-but-imperfectly with dRA (r=0.745); a synthetic case study shows a logistic regression and decision tree with gRA=0.95 (near-identical boundaries) still differing by 12 accuracy points, and an SVM-RBF/MLP pair with matched accuracy (0.94 each) agreeing on only 45% of the instance space (gRA=0.45) — concrete demonstrations that accuracy and dRA alone can be misleading about structural model similarity.

## 核心贡献

- 提出 Rashomon 对齐（RA）的几何变体 gRA：将参考分布 Q 取为实例空间 F 上的均匀分布 U(F)，而非数据生成分布 P(X)，从而得到一个不依赖于观测数据代表性、也不随协变量偏移而漂移的模型功能相似性度量。
- 形式化推导了 dRA 与 gRA 之间通过"测度变换"（重要性比 |F|p(x)）建立的精确关系：当 p(x)=1/|F|（即数据均匀分布）时二者相等，否则二者之差以 P(X) 与 U(F) 之间的全变分距离为上界。
- 通过 92 个 UCI 数据集上剪枝 vs. 未剪枝决策树的大规模实验，以及一个含四种典型情形（低 gRA/低准确率差、高 gRA/高准确率差、低 gRA/高准确率差、高 gRA/低准确率差）的合成 2D 案例研究，系统展示 gRA 能揭示准确率和 dRA 都无法单独捕捉的模型结构性异同。
- 发布开源 Python 包 ralign（github.com/mmrsantos/ralign）用于计算 dRA 与 gRA，并提供了象限分析（quadrant analysis）这一实用可视化工具，帮助识别"表面一致但结构分歧"或"表面分歧但结构一致"的关键模型对。

## Key Contributions

- Introduces Geometric Rashomon Alignment (gRA): sets the reference distribution Q in the agreement functional to the uniform distribution U(F) over the instance space (rather than the data-generating distribution P(X) used by standard "distributional" functional similarity measures), yielding a similarity measure that is invariant to changes in P(X) as long as the underlying decision functions stay fixed.
- Formally derives the change-of-measure relationship between Distributional RA (dRA, agreement estimated on a held-out sample from P(X)) and gRA (agreement estimated on synthetic uniform samples from F): dRA* = gRA* exactly when p(x) is uniform, and the gap |dRA* − gRA*| is bounded by the total variation distance between P(X) and U(F).
- Provides a large-scale empirical study (92 UCI datasets, pruned vs. unpruned `DecisionTreeClassifier` pairs under 5-fold CV) plus a four-case synthetic 2D illustration (SVM-RBF/MLP, logistic regression/decision tree, linear SVM/MLP, two linear SVMs with different regularization) that systematically maps out when accuracy, dRA, and gRA agree or diverge.
- Releases the `ralign` package (github.com/mmrsantos/ralign) for computing dRA and gRA, and proposes a quadrant-analysis visualization (gRA × |Δaccuracy|) as a practical diagnostic for model/algorithm comparison, ensemble construction, and interpretability workflows.

## Methodology

RA generalizes prediction-based agreement θ_X(M_A, M_B) = (1/n)Σ 1[M_A(x_i)=M_B(x_i)] to a population-level quantity θ^Q(M_A, M_B) = E_{x~Q}[1[M_A(x)=M_B(x)]], where the choice of reference distribution Q defines the variant. **Distributional RA (dRA)** sets Q = P(X), the true data-generating distribution, and is estimated (unbiased, consistent) from a held-out test sample — this is essentially the standard functional-similarity practice in the literature. **Geometric RA (gRA)** instead sets Q = U(F), the uniform distribution over the full instance space F = F_1×...×F_p defined by the attribute domains, and is estimated from synthetic data drawn i.i.d. from U(F), independent of any training or test sample. The two are related by an importance-weighting identity: dRA* = |F|∫1[M_A(x)=M_B(x)]p(x)dx, so dRA* = gRA* only under uniform P(X); the paper argues a high dRA with low gRA is the diagnostically interesting case, since it means two models agree where data happens to lie but disagree everywhere else — a form of agreement that is fragile under distribution/concept shift.

The main experiment compares pruned and unpruned decision trees (same hypothesis class, so boundaries are directly comparable) on 92 UCI datasets (32 to >4,600 instances, 4–649 features, numerical/categorical/mixed, balanced/imbalanced), using scikit-learn `DecisionTreeClassifier` with a fixed random seed. The unpruned tree uses `min_samples_split=2` (fully grown); the pruned tree uses `min_samples_split=10` plus cost-complexity pruning, where the effective complexity α_eff(t) = (R(t)−R(T_t))/(|T_t|−1) is computed for each subtree and the largest α in the resulting monotonic sequence (chosen via an inner 80/20 split of each fold's training data) is used to retrain the most aggressively pruned tree the data supports. Under 5-fold CV (shuffled, seed 42), each fold records: (i) accuracy difference (pruned − unpruned) on the test partition, (ii) dRA on the same test partition, and (iii) gRA computed from 1,000 synthetic points sampled uniformly within the bounding box of the fold's training features (no missing values injected); per-dataset values are the 5-fold average. A separate synthetic 2D case study (SVM-RBF, MLP, logistic regression, decision tree, linear SVM at two regularization strengths) illustrates four qualitative regimes crossing gRA (high/low) with accuracy difference (high/low).

## Results & Highlights

- Correlation between gRA and accuracy difference across all 92 UCI datasets: **Pearson r = 0.514** (Fig. 5) — a moderate positive relationship, meaning geometrically similar tree pairs tend to have smaller pruning-induced accuracy penalties, but with wide scatter (some low-gRA datasets show near-zero Δacc, and some mid/high-gRA datasets show large negative Δacc).
- Correlation between gRA and dRA across the same 92 datasets: **Pearson r = 0.745** (Fig. 8) — strong but imperfect; critically, several datasets show **dRA > 0.8 while gRA < 0.3**, i.e., pruned/unpruned trees that agree on observed test data but disagree over most of the instance space, while the reverse (high gRA, low dRA) never occurs — consistent with the theoretical containment relation.
- Quadrant analysis (medians: gRA ≈ 0.52, |Δacc| ≈ 0.15) shows the dominant regime is **high |Δacc| + low gRA** (globally different boundaries and different test accuracy — the "expected" outcome for deep unpruned trees on complex data), but a distinct **high |Δacc| + high gRA** cluster exists where trees agree on the vast majority of the instance space yet differ substantially in test accuracy, meaning the disagreement is concentrated precisely in the region the test set samples from.
- Synthetic case studies (Fig. 2) give explicit, named quantitative examples: **Case 1** — SVM-RBF and MLP both reach **accuracy 0.94** (Δacc = 0) yet **gRA = 0.45** (agree on only 45% of the instance space); **Case 2** — logistic regression vs. decision tree reach **gRA = 0.95** yet differ by **12 percentage points in accuracy**; **Case 3** — linear SVM (**acc = 0.62**) vs. MLP (**acc = 1.00**) on concentric rings disagree on over half the instance space (low gRA, Δacc = 0.38); **Case 4** — two linear SVMs differing only in regularization (C=1 vs. C=100) reach identical **accuracy = 1.00** and **gRA = 1.00** on a linearly separable problem.
- Distribution shapes differ systematically: accuracy difference is left-skewed (most datasets near zero, long tail below −0.8, Fig. 3); gRA spreads broadly across [0,1] with a peak between 0.4–0.7 (Fig. 4); dRA concentrates toward high values with mass above 0.7 and a secondary peak near 1.0 (Fig. 7) — dRA is systematically higher than gRA because test data is denser exactly where both trees are likely to agree.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core idea — swap the reference distribution in a standard agreement functional from the empirical data distribution to a uniform distribution over the declared feature domain — is conceptually simple and, once stated, fairly natural; the paper's genuine contribution is the clean formalization (the change-of-measure identity linking dRA and gRA) and the systematic empirical demonstration of when this substitution actually changes conclusions, rather than a technically deep new algorithm.

**Rigor concerns:** The large-scale study (92 datasets) tests only **one pair of models drawn from the same hypothesis class** (pruned vs. unpruned decision trees), so the claim of an "extensive empirical study of the new method" somewhat overstates generality — broader claims about comparing heterogeneous algorithms rest only on the small, single-instance synthetic 2D illustration (four cases, no repeated trials, no real data). The paper also never discusses the curse-of-dimensionality implication of its own design choice: several of the 92 UCI datasets have up to 649 features, and uniformly sampling 1,000 points inside a bounding box in a space that high will place virtually all synthetic points far from any realistic data manifold, so gRA there is largely measuring arbitrary extrapolation behavior rather than "structural similarity" in any practically meaningful sense — a limitation the "independent of any specific data distribution" framing glosses over. A related, unaddressed gap: categorical features are one-hot encoded before modeling, but the paper does not explain how uniform sampling within a continuous bounding box handles one-hot dimensions (naive uniform sampling would produce fractional/invalid one-hot patterns that don't correspond to any real category).

**Reproducibility:** Good — full hyperparameters (`min_samples_split`, cost-complexity α_eff derivation, seed=42, 5-fold CV, 1,000 synthetic points per fold), dataset provenance (UCI Repository), and an open-source package (`ralign`, github.com/mmrsantos/ralign) are all provided.

**Significance:** The quadrant-analysis framing (gRA × |Δacc|) is a genuinely useful practical diagnostic that could inform model selection or ensemble diversity beyond accuracy alone. However, the paper stops at descriptive statistics (Pearson r, histograms, scatter plots) with no significance testing or uncertainty quantification on the correlations, and — despite citing a recent survey of functional/representational similarity measures (Klabunde et al. 2025) — never benchmarks gRA/dRA against any of those existing measures (e.g., Cohen's kappa, disagreement distance), so it is unclear whether the uniform-sampling reframing offers advantages beyond what simpler existing agreement measures already provide on the same data.

## Strengths

- Clean, theoretically grounded formalization: the change-of-measure relation between dRA and gRA is elegant and gives a principled interpretation of when distributional agreement overestimates true structural similarity.
- Simple, purely post-hoc measure — no retraining required, applicable to any pair of models sharing an instance space, with a released open-source implementation.
- The quadrant analysis (gRA vs. |Δacc|) is an intuitive, actionable visualization that isolates the specific failure mode — high geometric agreement coexisting with large accuracy differences — that accuracy-only comparison would miss entirely.
- Large, diverse UCI collection (92 datasets spanning 32–4,600+ instances, 4–649 features, multiple domains) for the main empirical study, with a carefully documented pruning and CV protocol.
- The four synthetic case studies (Fig. 1, Fig. 2) are pedagogically effective, giving crisp, concrete numeric illustrations of each qualitative regime.

## Limitations

- Only a single algorithm pair (pruned vs. unpruned decision trees, same hypothesis class) is evaluated at scale across the 92 datasets; the paper's broader claims about comparing "models and algorithms" in general rely on a small, single-run synthetic toy example.
- Uniform sampling within a feature bounding box does not obviously scale to the high-dimensional datasets in the collection (up to 649 features); the paper does not address the resulting curse-of-dimensionality concern that most synthetic points will lie far from any realistic data manifold.
- No description of how one-hot-encoded categorical features are handled during continuous uniform sampling, risking synthetic instances that do not correspond to any valid categorical assignment.
- No comparison against other established functional/representational similarity measures from the very survey (Klabunde et al. 2025) the paper cites as background.
- Reported relationships are purely descriptive (Pearson correlations, histograms, quadrant scatter) without significance testing, confidence intervals, or discussion of cross-dataset dependence.
- The bounding box used for gRA sampling is defined per fold from the observed training-data range, which is itself somewhat arbitrary for unbounded or skewed feature domains and could affect gRA's absolute scale in ways not explored.

## Relevance

该工作提出的"几何 vs. 分布式"功能相似性框架，对模型选择、集成学习多样性度量、以及可解释性评估中"准确率相同是否代表模型真正相似"这一常见误判问题具有直接参考价值，但目前仅在决策树剪枝这一单一场景上做了大规模验证，高维数据下的采样有效性和类别特征处理细节仍待补充。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does gRA behave as feature dimensionality grows (the dataset collection already includes up to 649 features) — does uniform bounding-box sampling remain meaningful, or does it mostly measure off-manifold extrapolation artifacts?
- How should uniform sampling be adapted for one-hot-encoded categorical or mixed-type feature spaces to avoid generating invalid synthetic instances?
- Would alternative distribution-free sampling strategies (e.g., low-discrepancy sequences, boundary-focused or adversarial sampling near known decision surfaces) improve the statistical efficiency or interpretability of gRA relative to plain uniform sampling?
- How does gRA compare, head-to-head, against existing functional/representational similarity measures (e.g., Cohen's kappa, disagreement distance, CKA-style measures) on the same 92-dataset benchmark?
- Beyond decision tree pruning, how does gRA behave when comparing structurally different model families (deep networks vs. trees vs. linear models) at the same scale as the main 92-dataset study, rather than only in the small synthetic illustration?
- Can gRA be operationalized for the practical downstream uses the paper mentions — model selection, ensemble construction, concept-drift monitoring — with quantitative evidence of added value over existing diversity/agreement measures?
