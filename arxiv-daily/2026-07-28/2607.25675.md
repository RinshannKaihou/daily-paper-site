> **一句话总结：** DecoEvo 在冻结骨干模型下，让"解题者技能"和"评分标准生成器技能"两个可编辑文本artifact共同进化，但强制评分标准的更新只能靠"结构审计+近似平局对比审计"两种与聚合分数无关的信号来验收，从而避免评分标准被solver的分数"带偏"；在 HealthBench、WritingBench、ResearchQA 及两个跨基准迁移任务（LLMEval-Med、EQ-Bench Creative Writing v3）、GPT-4o/Qwen3-4B/Qwen3-8B 三个骨干上全面超过 SkillOpt（五基准平均相对提升 2.8%–5.0%），而未做分数解耦的"Score-Coupled Co-Evolution"对照组反而在13/15 组合上低于 SkillOpt。

> **TL;DR:** DecoEvo co-evolves two persistent text-space "skills" — a solver skill and a rubric-generator skill — under a frozen black-box LLM backbone, but decouples their update objectives to avoid the co-adaptation failure mode where an evolving rubric drifts toward whatever the current solver already satisfies. The rubric generator is updated only via two audits blind to the solver's aggregate score (a task-conditioned structural audit and a near-tie contrastive audit), never via the proxy score itself. Across HealthBench, WritingBench, ResearchQA and two within-domain transfer benchmarks (LLMEval-Med, EQ-Bench Creative Writing v3), with GPT-4o, Qwen3-4B, and Qwen3-8B backbones, DecoEvo beats SkillOpt (a solver-only text-space optimizer) on all 15 backbone-benchmark pairs — e.g., HealthBench/GPT-4o improves from SkillOpt's 41.7 to 45.3 (+8.6% relative) and the five-benchmark average rises 2.8–5.0% relative to SkillOpt — while a naive Score-Coupled Co-Evolution (SC-CoEvo) baseline that accepts rubric updates using the solver's own proxy score actually falls below SkillOpt in 13 of 15 settings and below Zero-shot in 7.

## 核心贡献

- 把"文本空间的 solver–rubric 联合演化"形式化为一个分数耦合（score-coupling）问题：如果评分标准生成器的更新是靠"当前 solver 在新评分标准下的聚合得分是否提高"来验收，就会产生虚假的分数提升——评分标准可能只是变得更容易被当前 solver 满足，而非更能反映任务质量。
- 提出"双视角审计"（dual-view auditing）机制，把分数解耦具体化为两条与聚合 solver 分数无关的验收通道：(A) 任务条件化的结构审计——只依据任务公开描述和评测说明，找出评分标准遗漏的维度/歧义并给出修复建议；(B) 近似平局对比审计——对同一问题采样多个 solver 回答，挑出当前评分标准打分接近（近似平局）的回答对，先让审计者在看不到评分标准和分数的情况下盲判哪个回答更好，再揭示评分标准，从而定位评分标准未能区分的质量差异。两路诊断被蒸馏为可复用的"评分标准生成原则"，只有当候选生成器在至少一个审计目标上帕累托改进、且不劣化另一个目标超过容忍度时才被接受。
- 在 5 个基准（含 2 个跨基准迁移设置）、3 个骨干模型的 15 组配置上做实验，DecoEvo 全部取得最高平均分，相对 SkillOpt 平均提升 2.8%–5.0%；同时用消融、trajectory 分析和评分标准与gold rubric的criterion级对齐度分析，证明收益来自评测覆盖面的扩大而非评分标准被"放水"变松。

## Key Contributions

- **Formalizes solver–rubric co-evolution as a score-coupling risk**: if rubric-generator updates are accepted based on the current solver's aggregate proxy score under the new rubric, the proxy can rise purely because the rubric becomes easier to satisfy for that solver, not because it better measures task quality — analogous to reward-model overoptimization (Gao et al., 2023) and instability in classical co-evolutionary systems (Popovici et al., 2012).
- **Introduces Definition 1 (Score-Decoupled Generator Update)**: a generator update may use questions, generated rubrics, response-level scores, and structured audit records, but must never use the training-time proxy J_D(s,g) (or its difference across candidates) as the generator's acceptance objective.
- **Operationalizes decoupling via dual-view auditing**: (A) a task-conditioned structural audit that scores rubric adequacy u_q(g) ∈ [0,1] against a fixed, benchmark-level task specification (never gold rubrics) and returns evidence-grounded repair suggestions; (B) a near-tie contrastive audit that samples L stochastic solver rollouts, selects pairs with near-tied rubric scores (|z_i − z_j| ≤ τ_tie), obtains a rubric-blind pairwise preference first, then reveals the rubric to diagnose the missing/underweighted criterion. Both audit trails are distilled into a reusable rubric-generation skill, accepted only under a Pareto rule (improve ≥1 audit objective by margin δ, regress none beyond tolerance η) — no weighted scalar aggregation of heterogeneous audit scores.
- **Validates the mechanism, not just the end score**: ablations isolate each component (structural audit, contrastive audit, rubric blindness, near-tie sampling, held-out verification, distillation), alternative-explanation controls rule out richer task priors or extra compute as the source of gains, training-dynamics plots show the coupled baseline's internal proxy keeps rising while its true gold-rubric score falls, and a separate GPT-5.5 criterion matcher shows DecoEvo's generated rubrics have ~12-point higher F1 overlap with held-out gold rubrics than SkillOpt's fixed rubric.

## Methodology

DecoEvo maintains two frozen-backbone, editable natural-language "skills": a **solver skill** s (reusable task-solving strategies) and a **rubric-generator skill** g (principles for constructing question-specific rubrics R_q(g) = {(c_i, w_i)}). All roles — solver, rubric generator, criterion-level judge, auditors, and skill rewriters — use the *same frozen backbone* under different role-specific prompts; only the prompt/role changes, not model weights. A rubric score is S(q,r,R_q) = Σ w_i·e(q,r,c_i) / Σ w_i where e(q,r,c_i) ∈ [0,1] comes from a frozen criterion-level judge; the training-time proxy J_D(s,g) averages this over a question set D. "Gold rubrics" (each benchmark's official, fixed evaluation criteria) are held out and never touched during optimization, used only for final/held-out scoring.

**Inner loop (solver evolution):** at each step the solver answers a training batch under skill s_t, the (current) generator produces rubrics, a judge returns criterion-level scores plus failure rationales, and a skill-rewriter consolidates selected failures into a candidate skill s'. The candidate is accepted only if it beats the incumbent on a held-out validation set by margin ε (Eq. 4), isolating solver-skill edits from rubric-generation noise.

**Outer loop (generator evolution via dual-view auditing):** runs every K inner steps or after M consecutive solver-candidate rejections, following an audit → distill → verify protocol:
1. *Task-conditioned structural audit*: for each task family, a fixed audit specification π_str (from only the public task description/eval instructions, never gold rubrics) is used to score generator adequacy u_q(g) and flag omitted/ambiguous dimensions with concrete repair suggestions.
2. *Near-tie contrastive audit*: samples L stochastic solver rollouts per probe question, isolates near-tied score pairs under the current rubric, obtains a rubric-blind pairwise preference (with rationale) before revealing the rubric, and records the missing/underweighted criterion for pairs where the rubric fails to justify the blind preference. A rubric-induced margin m_g(p) = S(q,r+,R_q(g)) − S(q,r−,R_q(g)) is later used only to verify candidate resolution, defined via a threshold γ (Eq. 8).
3. A generator-skill rewriter distills both audit trails (structural + contrastive) into a candidate generator g'. Acceptance uses a **Pareto verification gate**: g' is accepted only if it improves at least one available audit objective (structural adequacy Φ_str, contrastive resolution rate Φ_ctr) by a non-trivial margin δ, without regressing any other audit objective beyond tolerance η (Eq. 10) — explicitly avoiding a single weighted scalar that could reintroduce score-coupling.

Data is kept disjoint throughout: a training pool D_tr for solver edits, a probe set P for generator-audit diagnoses, held-out sets V_s/V_g for accepting solver/generator candidates, and a separate diagnostic set (used only for post-hoc trajectory plots, never for selection) plus a final disjoint test set scored under gold rubrics.

## Results & Highlights

- **Main effect (Table 1, gold-rubric scores, five-benchmark average):** DecoEvo achieves the highest mean score in **all 15** backbone × benchmark combinations. Relative to SkillOpt (a solver-only text-space optimizer with a fixed rubric generator), average relative gains are **5.0% (GPT-4o), 2.9% (Qwen3-4B), 2.8% (Qwen3-8B)** — absolute average points of 3.10, 1.74, and 1.78 respectively, with paired 95% CIs of [2.88, 3.32], [1.42, 2.06], and [1.69, 1.87], significant after Holm correction across backbones.
- **HealthBench (GPT-4o):** Zero-shot 40.9±0.28 → SkillOpt 41.7±0.35 → **DecoEvo 45.3±0.23** (+8.6% relative over SkillOpt), while the naive **Score-Coupled Co-Evolution (SC-CoEvo)** baseline actually drops to 40.3±0.52 — *below* Zero-shot.
- **Cross-benchmark transfer (no updates on target benchmark):** DecoEvo improves over SkillOpt by **4.5%/1.4%/2.8%** on LLMEval-Med (transferred from HealthBench, GPT-4o/Qwen3-4B/Qwen3-8B) and **2.7%/3.9%/1.7%** on EQ-Bench Creative Writing v3 (transferred from WritingBench) — evidence the gains are encoded as reusable strategy rather than memorized source-question fixes.
- **SC-CoEvo fails broadly:** the score-coupled control (identical setup to DecoEvo except generator acceptance uses the solver's own aggregate proxy score) falls **below SkillOpt in 13 of 15** backbone-benchmark pairs and **below Zero-shot in 7**, directly demonstrating the rubric-drift failure mode the paper targets.
- **Ablations (Table 2, HealthBench, avg. over 3 backbones and 5 runs):** removing verification causes the largest drop (2.4–2.9 points), removing distillation costs 1.4–1.8 points, removing the contrastive audit costs 1.7–2.1 points (vs. 1.3–1.7 for removing the structural audit), and removing rubric-blindness or near-tie sampling costs 1.0–1.5 points — every ablated component contributes positively, with held-out verification the single largest.
- **Alternative-explanation controls (Table 3, HealthBench) rule out confounds:** giving SkillOpt the task-prior specification only (no audit records) improves it by just 0.4–0.7 points; matching DecoEvo's token/call budget without the audit structure improves it by only 0.4–0.6 points; routing the same audit records directly to the solver ("Direct Audit") recovers more (0.8–1.6 points below DecoEvo) but still trails DecoEvo — e.g., on GPT-4o: SkillOpt 41.7 → Task-Prior 42.4 → Budget-Matched 42.1 → Direct Audit 43.7 → **DecoEvo 45.3**, at matched 1.89× token cost (Table 4).
- **Rubric alignment (Table 5, GPT-4o):** using a separate frozen GPT-5.5 criterion matcher (91.3% balanced accuracy, κ=0.83 vs. human labels) against held-out gold rubrics, DecoEvo's generated rubrics raise criterion-level F1 over SkillOpt by ~12 points on every source benchmark (e.g., HealthBench F1 44.6→56.9, WritingBench 48.1→60.3, ResearchQA 46.4→58.7), with *both* precision and recall increasing — inconsistent with merely generating more (less precise) criteria. SC-CoEvo instead *lowers* F1 by 5.3–6.3 points relative to SkillOpt, consistent with score-coupled updates discarding difficult distinctions.
- **Training dynamics (Figure 3, HealthBench diagnostic set):** SC-CoEvo's internal generated-rubric proxy keeps climbing to 89.1 by the final checkpoint, while its official gold-rubric score *declines* after an early peak — a clean illustration that the internal selection signal diverged from true task quality. DecoEvo recovers from transient fluctuations and finishes with the highest gold-rubric trajectory.

No quantitative results are missing; the paper reports concrete numbers throughout (all above are copied/paraphrased directly from the paper's tables).

## Authors & Credibility

Authors are affiliated with Tsinghua University, University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, Peking University, and the Qwen Business Unit of Alibaba (four affiliations noted with equal-contribution/corresponding-author marks on the title page). The paper builds directly on and empirically compares against **SkillOpt** (Yang et al., 2026, arXiv:2605.23904, "SkillOpt: Executive strategy for self-evolving agent skills"), which is itself a recent, closely related text-space solver-optimization method — plausibly from an overlapping or adjacent research group given the shared "skill" terminology and evaluation setup, though this is not stated explicitly. The related-work section is well-populated with contemporaneous 2026 work on rubric co-evolution (EvoRubric, OpenRS, ARCO, DR Tulu, EvoLM) that operates in weight-space or via RL, positioning DecoEvo's frozen-backbone, text-space contribution as complementary to (not a strict improvement over) that literature. Backbones tested (GPT-4o, Qwen3-4B/8B) span both a widely used proprietary model and the authors' own lab's open models, which somewhat mitigates (but does not eliminate) a potential home-model advantage.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core idea — separating rubric-generator updates from the solver's aggregate proxy score to avoid "rubric drift" — is a clean and well-motivated instantiation of a known problem (reward/proxy overoptimization, Gao et al. 2023; classical co-evolutionary instability, Popovici et al. 2012) applied specifically to black-box, frozen-backbone text-space skill evolution. The dual-view audit design (task-conditioned structural + rubric-blind near-tie contrastive) is a genuinely useful operationalization, and the explicit Pareto acceptance rule (rather than a weighted scalar) is a deliberate, well-justified design choice to avoid reintroducing the coupling the paper is trying to remove.

**Rigor:** Reporting is above average for this genre: five matched seeds with mean±sample-std, paired 95% CIs with Holm correction across backbones, disjoint train/probe/validation/diagnostic/test splits, and — notably — a self-constructed "SC-CoEvo" ablation-style baseline that isolates exactly the failure mode the paper targets (and does show catastrophic behavior: below Zero-shot in 7/15 settings). The alternative-explanation controls (Task-Prior, Budget-Matched, Direct Audit in Table 3/4) are a strong touch, directly pre-empting the "maybe it's just more compute/information" objection.

**Reproducibility:** Threshold/hyperparameter definitions (τ_tie, ε, δ, η, γ, K, M, L, H) are stated symbolically but several concrete values and "full definitions and paired intervals for the smallest effect" are deferred to a supplementary material not included in the reviewed PDF, so full reproducibility cannot be assessed from this document alone. All roles (solver, generator, judge, auditors, rewriters) share the *same frozen backbone* under different prompts — this is efficient but means the structural/contrastive "auditors" are the same model self-critiquing itself with a different persona, which could introduce shared blind spots or self-preference biases not controlled for (e.g., no test with a different, stronger model as auditor).

**Significance / red flags:** The primary comparison point, SkillOpt, is itself a 2026 preprint rather than an established, widely-adopted baseline, and the paper's related-work section lists several directly relevant concurrent works on adaptive/co-evolving rubrics (Rezaei et al. 2026 online rubric elicitation, EvoRubric, OpenRS, ARCO, DR Tulu) that are discussed but **never empirically compared against** — the only empirical baselines are Zero-shot, SkillOpt, and the authors' own SC-CoEvo construction. This narrows the "beats X" claim to a fairly small, partly self-selected comparison set. The authors themselves are commendably honest about scope in the Conclusion: "Our evidence is limited to settings where optimization roles share a backbone and transfer remains within medicine or writing" — i.e., no cross-domain transfer (e.g., medicine→math) and no test of mixed-backbone role assignment (e.g., a weaker solver audited by a stronger generator), both of which would be the natural next stress tests. Token cost is also non-trivial: DecoEvo uses 1.89× SkillOpt's tokens per training run (Table 4), a cost that is paid once at training time since only the solver skill is deployed, but still worth noting for anyone estimating optimization budget.

## Strengths

- Directly demonstrates, not just argues, the risk it is designed to prevent: SC-CoEvo empirically collapses (below Zero-shot in 7/15 settings), giving the paper's central claim real teeth rather than being a purely theoretical concern.
- Thorough ablation and control suite (6-way ablation + 3 alternative-explanation controls + cost accounting + rubric-alignment analysis with an independent matcher) that jointly rule out the most obvious alternative explanations for the gains (more compute, richer task priors, generating more/looser criteria).
- Consistent gains across three backbones (including a proprietary GPT-4o and two open Qwen3 sizes) and across in-domain and two independent cross-benchmark transfer settings, with statistically supported deltas (paired CIs, Holm correction).
- Clear, falsifiable formalization (Definition 1: Score-Decoupled Generator Update) that gives the mechanism a precise, checkable meaning rather than a vague design philosophy.

## Limitations

- No empirical comparison to the directly relevant concurrent adaptive-rubric methods cited in related work (EvoRubric, OpenRS, ARCO, DR Tulu, Rezaei et al. online rubric elicitation) — all of these operate on different (weight-space/RL) substrates, but their absence from the table limits how strong the "state of the art" claim can be read.
- All optimization roles (solver, rubric generator, judge, structural auditor, contrastive auditor, both skill-rewriters) share one frozen backbone; the paper explicitly flags (in its Conclusion) that it has not studied heterogeneous-backbone role assignment, which could reveal self-consistency or self-preference biases masked in the same-model setting.
- Transfer is tested only within-domain (HealthBench→LLMEval-Med, WritingBench→Creative Writing); no cross-domain transfer (e.g., medical-optimized skills applied to math or code) is evaluated.
- Full hyperparameter values, audit schedules, and some interval details are pushed to supplementary material not present in the main PDF reviewed here, limiting independent reproducibility assessment from this document alone.
- Training-time cost is 1.89× SkillOpt's token budget; while deployment only needs the final solver skill, the generator-skill machinery (auditors, rewriters, verification gates) adds real optimization-time overhead that smaller teams may find costly to replicate.

## Relevance

这篇论文提供了一个具体、可复用的设计模式——把"评价标准本身也参与进化"这件事和"避免评价标准被优化对象钻空子"这一目标同时实现，对做 LLM-as-judge、rubric/checklist 自动生成、或任何涉及"用可优化代理分数驱动模型自我提升"的场景（例如 RL 中的 reward hacking、self-play/co-evolution 系统设计）都有直接借鉴价值；其"分数解耦"和"双视角审计"思路也可能启发对评审/评测流程设计（而非仅模型训练）的思考。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the same score-decoupling principle hold if the rubric generator and solver were assigned to different (e.g., differently-sized or differently-trained) backbones, removing the possibility of shared self-preference bias between roles?
- How sensitive are the results to the near-tie threshold τ_tie and Pareto tolerance η — is there a regime where a looser or tighter audit-acceptance rule recovers (or loses) most of the gain, and where exactly are those values reported in the supplementary material?
- Could the dual-view audits themselves be gamed over many outer-loop iterations (e.g., the generator learning to produce rubrics that score well on the structural/contrastive audit's *own* blind spots, a subtler form of the same score-coupling risk one level up)?
- How would DecoEvo perform against the concurrent adaptive-rubric baselines mentioned but not compared (EvoRubric, OpenRS, ARCO, DR Tulu) if adapted to the same frozen-backbone, text-space setting?
- Does the ~12-point F1 alignment gain over gold rubrics (Table 5) translate into better downstream human preference agreement, or only into better agreement with the specific gold-rubric criteria used by each benchmark's official grader?
