Guiding Posterior Exploration with Optimizer-Derived Geometry

Moritz Schlager, Emanuel Sommer (co-first, TUM/LMU Munich, MCML) · Thomas Möllenhoff (RIKEN AIP) · David Rügamer (LMU Munich, MCML) · digest 2026‑07‑28
bayesian-deep-learning sgmcmc bayesian-deep-ensembles preconditioning adamw uncertainty-quantification
AdamW’s leftover gradient and momentum statistics double as SGHMC’s mass matrix for free, eliminating the expensive warmup phase.
一句话总结：AdamW 优化阶段留下的一阶/二阶动量统计量可直接当作 SGHMC 采样阶段的对角质量矩阵“免费”复用，从而省去/缩短耗时的 scale-adaptation 预热，在 5 个数据集上平均将准确率/RMSE 提升 1.17%–1.31%，LPPD/困惑度提升 7.3%–10.1%，且比自适应的 AdaSGHMC 更稳健、更省算力。

Why This Matters
A simple, practical fix for Bayesian deep ensembles: reuse optimizer geometry to cut sampling cost without sacrificing uncertainty quality.
Critical Edge
The more natural, finer-grained version of this idea — per-parameter preconditioning — breaks down with numerical instability on several dataset/metric combinations, forcing a coarser layer-wise workaround.

Methodology

Targets the two-stage Bayesian Deep Ensemble (BDE) pipeline: Phase I runs AdamW from multiple initializations to find distinct posterior modes; Phase II runs independent SGHMC chains to sample around each mode — normally after a costly, from-scratch scale-adaptation warmup to learn the mass matrix M.

The proposal: treat AdamW's terminal optimizer state as free curvature information. Four diagonal M constructions are compared — identity, gradient-based (g_T²), 1st-moment (μ_T²), and 2nd-moment (√ν_T, approximating the diagonal Fisher information, "Fisher for free"). Proposition 3.1 shows that under a local-quadratic posterior approximation, setting M to the true Hessian isotropizes the target with unit condition number — motivating ν_T as a curvature proxy.

Raw per-parameter statistics are numerically unstable, so all main results use a layer-wise variant: averaged within layer, normalized across layers. Evaluated on 5 datasets × 4 architectures (FCN, LeNet, ResNet-7, nanoGPT) spanning regression, classification, and language modeling, via JAX + BlackJAX, ~1,750 GPU-hours, 3 seeds per configuration.

Strengths

  • A genuine "free lunch": reused statistics are byproducts of AdamW training already run in Phase I — no extra forward/backward passes.
  • Clean four-way ablation (identity/gradient/1st/2nd-moment) × two granularities, with standard errors over 3 seeds throughout.
  • Honest about failure modes: reports where parameter-wise preconditioning goes unstable, with a plausible mechanism (geometry overwriting) rather than just a win.
  • Directly actionable: gradient/1st-moment under tight compute budgets, 2nd-moment when a longer sampling run is affordable.

Limitations

  • Prop. 3.1's local-quadratic, single-mode assumption doesn't hold for realistic, ill-conditioned, multimodal BNN posteriors — acknowledged by the authors.
  • Parameter-wise preconditioning is numerically unstable on multiple dataset/method combinations, needing the layer-wise averaging crutch.
  • Only AdamW is tested as the curvature source, despite claiming optimizer-agnosticism; no Adahessian/Sophia/Lion comparison.
  • Model scale tops out at 10.8M parameters; unverified whether the benefit persists at LLM or ViT scale.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does the benefit persist at substantially larger model scales, or with optimizers other than AdamW (Adahessian, Sophia, Lion)?
  2. Could parameter-wise instability be fixed with principled regularization (adaptive epsilon, gradient clipping on M) instead of coarser layer-wise averaging?
  3. Does the "geometry overwriting" explanation for AdaSGHMC's underperformance generalize to other adaptive SGMCMC samplers?
  4. Would blending μ_T and ν_T outperform either single statistic alone?
  5. How sensitive are the reported gains to the AdamW hyperparameters (β₁, β₂, λ, α) used in Phase I?
