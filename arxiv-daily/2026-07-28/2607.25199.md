> **一句话总结：** 论文提出 RIDGE，一个让LLM生成的期权定价代码通过"无套利一致性检验+压力测试+基准比对"反复自我诊断修正的自主验证框架，在Heston/Bates/rough Heston上把总分从31-58/100迭代提升到96-99/100，并在Heston-Hull-White和G-SVJD两个模型上发现蒙特卡洛方法本身存在结构性瓶颈（精度停滞在Level A，需要约10^10条路径才能达到10^-5的目标精度），进而推动LLM自主发现基于条件特征函数PDE（CCF-PDE）的半解析定价新方法，使隐含波动率误差再降低1-3个数量级。

> **TL;DR:** RIDGE is an iterative validation framework that subjects LLM-generated stochastic-volatility option-pricing code to no-arbitrage consistency checks (put-call parity, monotonicity, convexity, risk-neutral density), stress tests, and benchmark comparisons, feeding diagnostic reports back into a growing repository that guides code revision across iterations. On Heston, Bates, and rough Heston, RIDGE drives a heuristic diagnostic score from 31/100, 58/100, and 32/100 up to 97/100, 96/100, and 99/100 respectively (Table 4) in 5-10 iterations, converging to a maximum at-the-money implied-volatility deviation as low as 5×10^-5 (rough Heston, Level C). For Heston-Hull-White (HHW) and G-SVJD, the initially generated Monte Carlo implementations plateau at Level A/F accuracy because inverse-square-root MC convergence would require on the order of 10^10 paths to hit the 10^-5 target; RIDGE's diagnosis triggers a [METHOD-CHOICE] pivot to a conditional characteristic-function PDE (CCF-PDE) formulation, after which further validation-guided refinement drives the final implementations to 118/120 (HHW) and 117/120 (G-SVJD), reaching implied-vol errors one-to-three orders of magnitude below the Monte Carlo baselines at comparable or lower CPU cost (Figure 2). An ablation without the validation operator (Experiment 1, Heston only) never develops the adaptive numerical machinery and still violates strike monotonicity on 8.8-14.1% of adjacent-strike pairs even on the default configuration (Table 11), showing the validation loop itself — not just the underlying LLM — is the source of the improvement.

## 核心贡献

- 提出 RIDGE（Repository-driven Iterative Diagnosis, Generation and Evaluation）框架：以"蓝图（blueprint）+代码实现+知识仓库（repository）"三元组为迭代状态，通过算子形式 $\Phi_\mathcal{R}$（生成/修订）与 $\mathcal{V}_\mathcal{R}=\Gamma_\mathcal{R}\circ\mu_\mathcal{R}$（测量+诊断）驱动LLM生成的定价代码不断自我修正，知识仓库随迭代积累并跨模型复用。
- 设计三层确定性测量体系：Block I（有效性：蓝图与源材料一致、代码与蓝图一致、可执行）、Block II（可容许性：put-call平价、单调性、凸性、Breeden-Litzenberger隐含风险中性密度四项无套利残差）、Block III（基准比对：与已发表数值/退化极限/局部仿射近似的隐含波动率偏差，映射为D/C/B/A/F五级评分）。
- 在Heston、Bates、rough Heston三个仿射/半仿射模型上验证RIDGE能自主发现并修复实现缺陷（如COS截断域固定宽度、mode数固定不随压力配置自适应、深度虚值/实值定价的尾部处理不当等），诊断总分从31/100、58/100、32/100分别迭代提升到97/100、96/100、99/100。
- 更重要的发现：在Heston-Hull-White和G-SVJD两个非仿射模型上，验证过程揭示的不是代码bug，而是蒙特卡洛方法论本身的结构性局限（收敛速率为O(1/√N)，达到目标精度所需路径数不可行），从而触发[METHOD-CHOICE]诊断类别，引导LLM转向基于"条件高斯对数收益+Feynman-Kac定理"推导的一维条件特征函数PDE（CCF-PDE）这一半解析新方法，并对其继续迭代精化，最终精度提升1-3个数量级。
- 通过消融和可复现性实验（Experiment 1去掉验证算子、Experiment 3更换外部LLM为生成器如DeepSeek-v4-pro和ChatGPT gpt-5.5）证明：验证算子本身（而非特定LLM的能力）是自适应数值机制（如Feller感知截断域、strike-coverage规则）涌现的关键来源；换用不同LLM作为生成器仍能被同一验证流程引导到通过验证的实现，甚至G-SVJD案例中ChatGPT独立发展出另一种混合傅里叶-有限差分方法也能通过验证。

## Key Contributions

- **RIDGE framework formalized as an operator-theoretic iteration.** The state at each iteration is a triple (blueprint $\mathcal{B}$, code implementation $\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_h$, repository $\mathcal{R}$). A generation operator $\Phi_\mathcal{R}$ produces the next blueprint/implementation from the previous diagnostic report, and a validation operator $\mathcal{V}_\mathcal{R} = \Gamma_\mathcal{R} \circ \mu_\mathcal{R}$ (measurement then diagnosis) evaluates it, updates the repository, and emits a stop signal once actionable admissibility/concordance residuals fall below prescribed tolerances (Section 2.2).
- **Three-block deterministic measurement design.** Block I checks validity (blueprint-source consistency, code-blueprint consistency, executability); Block II measures four no-arbitrage residuals — put-call parity, monotonicity, convexity, and Breeden-Litzenberger risk-neutral density — over a maturity-dependent strike grid plus model-specific stress configurations (e.g., Feller-condition violations); Block III benchmarks against published values, degeneration limits, or local affine approximations, graded D-through-F by maximum at-the-money implied-vol deviation $\Delta$IV.
- **Successful replication-style validation on Heston, Bates, and rough Heston.** Starting from unadapted implementations of documented methodologies, RIDGE raises the per-iteration diagnostic score (out of 100, five categories each worth 20) from 31→97 over 10 iterations (Heston), 58→96 over 5 iterations (Bates), and 32→99 over 6 iterations (rough Heston), with the repository transferring adaptive COS-truncation rules from Heston to Bates and rough Heston (Table 4, Table 5).
- **Validation as a driver of methodology discovery, not just bug-fixing.** For HHW and G-SVJD — non-affine models with no closed-form characteristic function — the generated Monte Carlo implementations can be made internally consistent but their MC error plateaus (inverse-square-root convergence would need ~10^10 paths for the 10^-5 target). RIDGE's diagnosis stage recognizes this as a [METHOD-CHOICE] issue rather than a [BUG], and guides derivation of a conditional characteristic-function PDE (CCF-PDE) via conditioning on the variance path and the Feynman-Kac theorem (Appendices B, C), which is then itself validated and refined to 118/120 (HHW) and 117/120 (G-SVJD).
- **Ablation and cross-model/cross-generator reproducibility.** Removing the validation operator (Experiment 1) leaves the Heston implementation with only the shared mathematical core and no adaptive layer, producing 8.8-14.1% strike-monotonicity violations even on the default configuration; keeping the validation operator but swapping in external generators (DeepSeek, ChatGPT; Experiment 3) still yields validated implementations, sometimes via an independently-discovered alternative methodology (a hybrid Fourier finite-difference PDE solver for G-SVJD under ChatGPT), indicating the validation loop — not the specific LLM — drives the observed adaptivity.

## Methodology

RIDGE targets the setting where an LLM (Claude Opus 4.8 in all reported experiments) generates option-pricing code for stochastic-volatility models directly from a mathematical specification, and must be validated well beyond standard unit testing because pricing correctness requires mathematical consistency (no-arbitrage), numerical stability, and reliability across a wide parameter range — not just plausible outputs on a handful of test cases.

**Operator formulation.** The exact pricing operator $\mathcal{P}: \mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{Y}$ maps model specifications to theoretical pricing surfaces. A numerical method with controls $h$ induces a target operator $\mathcal{P}_h$ on a finite strike-maturity grid $\mathcal{G}_h$, and $\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_h$ denotes the LLM-generated implementation intended to approximate it. The total error decomposes as $\|\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_h - \mathcal{P}\|_{\mathcal{M}_h} \le \|\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_h - \mathcal{P}_h\|_{\mathcal{M}_h} + \|\mathcal{P}_h - \mathcal{P}\|_{\mathcal{M}_h}$ (implementation error + scheme error); RIDGE targets only the first term (Eq. 1). The repository $\mathcal{R}$ accumulates diagnostic knowledge and is consulted by both the generation operator $\Phi_\mathcal{R}$ and the validation operator $\mathcal{V}_\mathcal{R} = \Gamma_\mathcal{R} \circ \mu_\mathcal{R}$. Each iteration reads a blueprint $\mathcal{B}^{(n)}$ and diagnostic report $\mathcal{D}^{(n)}$, returns a revised blueprint and implementation, measures admissibility ($A_M$) and concordance ($C_M$) errors, and halts once all actionable residuals fall below tolerances $\eta_A, \eta_C$ (Section 2.2, Figure 1).

**Convergence interpretation.** Section 2.3 gives a stochastic-process reading: a nonnegative "validation energy" $L_n$ (built from tolerance-adjusted admissibility/concordance residuals, Eq. 3) is shown, under a Robbins-Siegmund-type supermartingale drift condition (finite cumulative repository-expansion and generation-perturbation effects, plus positive dissipation away from zero), to converge almost surely to zero (Proposition 1) — i.e., the validation trajectory approaches the acceptance region. This is explicitly an asymptotic sufficient-condition result, not a finite-iteration or universal-generator guarantee.

**Measurement instantiation (Section 3.1).** The validation grid uses maturities $T \in \{0.001,...,5.0\}$ and log-moneyness candidates in $[-0.5, 0.5]$, restricted per-maturity by the COS truncation scale $|\log(K/F)| \le \tfrac12 L\sqrt{\bar v}$ ($L=14$). Four residuals are measured at each grid point: relative put-call parity error $e_1$ (tolerance $10^{-5}$ for deterministic transforms, relaxed to $10^{-3}$ for Monte Carlo), monotonicity violation $e_2$, convexity violation $e_3$, and density-integral deviation $e_4$ from the Breeden-Litzenberger relation. Benchmark deviation $\Delta$IV is mapped to letter grades D (<0.001%) through F (≥1.00%). Stress-test configurations are chosen by finite-difference sensitivity of ATM implied vol/skew, plus model-specific abnormal regimes (e.g., Feller-condition violations for Heston-type models).

**Diagnostic taxonomy.** Findings are classified into [BUG], [CONSISTENCY], [METHOD-ALGO], [METHOD-HYPER], [METHOD-CHOICE], or [EXPECTED] (Table 3), each scored out of 20, giving a 100-point iteration total (120 when [METHOD-CHOICE] applies). Residuals that persist after repeated independent revision attempts and have a theoretical explanation are reclassified [EXPECTED] rather than driving further tuning.

**Two study types.** (1) Replication studies on Heston, Bates, and rough Heston, where established closed-form/semi-analytic (COS-method) pricing methodologies exist and the task is to detect/remove implementation defects (adaptive truncation domain, mode count, out-of-the-money tail treatment). (2) Methodology-development studies on Heston-Hull-White (stochastic rates, non-affine correlations) and G-SVJD (non-affine power-law variance diffusion $\sigma_v v_t^p$ plus jumps), where the initial LLM-generated Monte Carlo implementation is validated, found structurally inadequate, and replaced — via conditioning on the variance path and Feynman-Kac reduction — by a one-dimensional conditional characteristic-function PDE (CCF-PDE), itself then refined (variance-domain sizing from moments, Lamperti-coordinate discretization for the singular power-law drift, Rannacher smoothing, jump-aware cosine half-width).

## Results & Highlights

- **Heston replication:** diagnostic score improves from 31/100 (iteration 1, naive fixed COS truncation) to 97/100 over 10 iterations; final implementation satisfies admissibility on standard and non-Feller stress sets, benchmarks at Level B, prices one maturity in ≈0.8 ms; remaining deviations confined to the deep-Feller regime where a finite cosine expansion cannot represent the tail exactly (Section 4.2, Table 4).
- **Bates replication:** inherits Heston's adaptive COS machinery, reaches 96/100 in 5 iterations (vs. Heston's 10), final implementation prices one maturity in ≈1.0 ms — directly showing repository knowledge transfer reduces iteration count for a related model.
- **Rough Heston replication:** score rises from 32/100 to 99/100 over 6 iterations after replacing an explicit fractional-Adams solver with an implicit corrector (closed-form quadratic root selection at each Fourier node); final solver prices one maturity in ≈27 ms, benchmarks at Level C, with residual band-limited tail-mass error diagnosed as a representation limitation, not a bug.
- **HHW methodology pivot:** initial full-Euler Monte Carlo reaches only 38/100→53/100 after fixing rate-initialization and truncation bugs, then plateaus — MC's O(1/√N) convergence would need ~10^10 paths to reach the 10^-5 implied-vol target, an infeasible cost. After the [METHOD-CHOICE] pivot to CCF-PDE (iteration 3, score jumps to 80-102), subsequent refinement (CIR-moment variance boundary, phase-only recentering, exact out-of-the-money parity recovery, batched Crank-Nicolson solve) drives the score to 118/120, with a maximum ATM implied-vol deviation of 5.07×10^-5 across the maturity grid and per-maturity runtime reduced from 548 ms to 471 ms (Table 6, Table 7).
- **G-SVJD methodology pivot:** analogous trajectory — MC implementation stalls at Level A (density-integral deviation 1.56 before refinement) due to full-truncation Euler bias near v≈0; CCF-PDE reformulation plus Lamperti-coordinate discretization (clustering nodes near the power-law-diffusion singularity) reduces density-integral deviation from 1.56 to 1.0×10^-3, then to 6.3×10^-5 (Level C) with a jump-aware COS half-width, final score 117/120 (Table 6, Table 8).
- **Accuracy-cost comparison (Figure 2, T=0.5):** the final CCF-PDE solvers achieve sub-basis-point mean implied-volatility error in a few hundred milliseconds for both HHW and G-SVJD, one-to-three orders of magnitude more accurate than Monte Carlo at comparable computational cost; the intermediate iteration-4 CCF-PDE implementation sits between MC and the final solver, showing validation-guided refinement (not just the method switch) drives most of the accuracy gain — e.g., HHW's iteration-4 fixed variance-domain cap $v_{max}=3\max(v_0,\eta)$ leaves a ~4-basis-point accuracy floor that is removed only by iteration-5's moment-based domain sizing.
- **Ablation (Experiment 1, no validation operator) vs. full RIDGE (Experiment 2) on Heston, Table 11:** the unguided implementation violates strike monotonicity ($L_2$) on 8.8% (default config) to 14.1% (abnormal config: $\kappa=0.2,\theta=0.004,\sigma_v=0.9$) of adjacent-strike pairs, and convexity ($L_3$) on up to 11.1%, with mean relative pricing errors of 10-20% over $|m|\le0.5$ on the abnormal set — versus 0% monotonicity/convexity violations for Experiment 2 across every tested configuration, and pricing errors in the $10^{-6}$-$10^{-3}$ range.
- **Cross-generator reproducibility (Experiment 3):** DeepSeek-v4-pro reaches a validated Bates implementation in 5 iterations matching Claude's replication run (degeneration-to-Heston benchmark 7.8×10^-9, 0.35 ms/maturity) and a validated rough Heston in 9 iterations (vs. 6 for Claude) with degeneration benchmark 5.6×10^-5 at 114 ms/maturity; ChatGPT (gpt-5.5) independently converges to the same CCF-PDE for HHW but arrives at a *different* pricing methodology for G-SVJD (a hybrid Fourier finite-difference PDE solver), reaching the Heston degeneration limit to within 10^-5 at ~360 ms/maturity in 4 iterations — evidence that RIDGE's measurement/diagnosis loop, not a specific generator's internal method choice, is what drives convergence to a validated state.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core idea — treating LLM-generated numerical code validation as an iterative operator loop with an accumulating "repository" of diagnostic expertise, rather than one-shot unit testing — is a genuinely useful framing for scientific-computing code generation, and the finding that validation itself can trigger a [METHOD-CHOICE] pivot (MC → CCF-PDE) is the paper's most interesting result: it demonstrates the framework doing more than bug-hunting. That said, the individual numerical techniques discovered (adaptive COS truncation via cumulants, Feller-aware domain widening, Lamperti-coordinate discretization for singular power-law diffusions, conditional-Gaussian PDE reductions via Feynman-Kac) are largely standard tools in the computational-finance literature; the paper's contribution is the *autonomous discovery and integration* of these techniques by an LLM under a structured diagnostic loop, not the techniques themselves.

**Rigor:** The measurement design (four no-arbitrage residuals, letter-graded benchmarking, stress sets tied to finite-difference IV sensitivity) is careful and the ablation in Table 11 is a genuinely informative comparison — it isolates the validation operator's contribution cleanly by holding the generator fixed. However, the diagnostic *scores* (out of 20 per category, 100/120 total) are explicitly heuristic ("calibrated to be broadly comparable... they do not constitute validation criteria themselves," Section 3.2) and are self-assigned by the same LLM that also does the diagnosis — there is no independent verification that a "97/100" Heston implementation and a "99/100" rough Heston implementation are actually comparable in quality; the paper is honest about this limitation but it means the headline iteration counts and score trajectories should be read as illustrative progress indicators, not calibrated accuracy metrics.

**Reproducibility:** Supplementary material (revision logs, benchmarks, costs) is provided via GitHub, and the cross-generator experiments (DeepSeek, ChatGPT) are a legitimate and fairly rare reproducibility check for an LLM-agent paper — most papers in this space test only one generator. That said, all three generators used (Claude Opus 4.8, DeepSeek-v4-pro, ChatGPT gpt-5.5) are top-tier frontier models; there is no experiment with a weaker/smaller LLM, so the paper cannot speak to whether RIDGE's convergence guarantee (Proposition 1, itself only asymptotic and conditional on unverified drift assumptions) holds for less capable generators — a limitation the authors explicitly flag as future work ("Whether these conditions hold for specific classes of language-model generators... are open questions," Section 6.2).

**Significance:** The application is narrow (five stochastic-volatility option-pricing models under Fourier/COS-style valuation), so generalization to broader scientific-computing domains is unproven, but within quantitative finance the demonstrated 1-3 order-of-magnitude accuracy improvement over Monte Carlo for HHW/G-SVJD, achieved by having the validation process itself motivate a methodology change, is a compelling proof of concept for LLM-driven numerical method development, not just code repair.

**Red flags / weak points:** (1) The Experiment 1 ablation baseline is somewhat weak by construction — it "removes the validation operator" but only for a *single* published benchmark configuration, so its poor generalization to stress configurations is close to expected rather than surprising; a stronger ablation would compare against a well-engineered human-written reference implementation rather than an unguided single-shot LLM generation. (2) There is no comparison against non-LLM automated testing/verification tools from the numerical-PDE or scientific-computing literature (e.g., property-based testing, metamorphic testing) that could plausibly catch some of the same no-arbitrage violations without an LLM diagnosis loop at all — the paper doesn't address whether a much simpler deterministic test harness (without any LLM-based diagnosis) might already catch most of the Block II violations. (3) The stochastic convergence theory (Proposition 1) has essentially unverifiable hypotheses (bounded cumulative repository/generation perturbations, a positive dissipation function) for an actual LLM generator, so it functions more as a conceptual frame than a checkable guarantee.

## Strengths

- Clear, well-motivated operator-theoretic formalization of an otherwise ad hoc "LLM code generation + fix loop," with an explicit error decomposition (implementation error vs. scheme error) that clarifies what the framework does and does not target.
- Diagnostic taxonomy ([BUG]/[CONSISTENCY]/[METHOD-ALGO]/[METHOD-HYPER]/[METHOD-CHOICE]/[EXPECTED]) provides a genuinely useful vocabulary for distinguishing "code is wrong" from "method is unsuitable," and the paper demonstrates both categories occurring in practice across the five models.
- The HHW/G-SVJD case studies are a strong existence proof that validation feedback can drive representation-level methodology change (MC → CCF-PDE), not just parameter tuning — a qualitatively different and more interesting outcome than typical "LLM fixes its own bugs" demonstrations.
- Reasonably thorough ablation (validation operator on/off) and cross-generator reproducibility experiments (DeepSeek, ChatGPT) that isolate the contribution of the validation loop itself from the specific LLM's capability.
- Repository-based knowledge transfer across model families (Heston → Bates/rough Heston, HHW → G-SVJD) is empirically demonstrated via reduced iteration counts (Bates: 5 vs. Heston's 10) and explicit component-inheritance tables (Tables 5, 9).

## Limitations

- Diagnostic scores are heuristic, self-assigned by the LLM performing the diagnosis, and explicitly stated by the authors not to be validation criteria — limiting how much weight can be placed on cross-model score comparisons (e.g., "99/100" for rough Heston vs. "97/100" for Heston).
- Scope is confined to stochastic-volatility option pricing with Fourier/COS-based valuation; no evidence is given that the framework transfers to other scientific-computing domains (PDE solvers outside finance, other numerical methods) despite the introduction's broader framing around "LLM-generated scientific software."
- Convergence guarantee (Proposition 1) is asymptotic and conditional on drift/perturbation assumptions that are not verified for any real LLM generator; the paper explicitly acknowledges finite-time guarantees and generator-class-specific conditions remain open.
- Only frontier-tier LLMs (Claude Opus 4.8, DeepSeek-v4-pro, ChatGPT gpt-5.5) were tested as generators; no experiment probes whether RIDGE remains effective with smaller/weaker models, which is exactly the setting where autonomous validation would be most valuable/necessary.
- The paper does not compare against simpler non-LLM-diagnosis baselines (e.g., a fixed deterministic test suite with human-written fix suggestions, or classical property-based testing) that might catch a substantial fraction of the same admissibility violations at lower cost.

## Relevance

该论文展示了LLM自主代码生成+验证在量化金融数值方法上的一个具体可行范式，其"确定性无套利测量+LLM诊断+知识仓库迭代"的三层设计对做LLM辅助科学计算、自动化代码验证、或agent自我修正循环的研究方向有较强参考价值，尤其是"验证驱动方法论发现"（而非只是修bug）这一结论值得关注。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would RIDGE's iteration trajectory and final scores hold up with a weaker/smaller LLM as the generator, or does the framework implicitly rely on frontier-model-level reasoning to correctly interpret diagnostic reports and propose meaningful revisions?
- How sensitive is the [METHOD-CHOICE] pivot (MC → CCF-PDE) to the specific diagnostic prompt/repository content — would a differently-worded repository entry fail to trigger the methodology change, leaving the LLM stuck refining an inadequate Monte Carlo implementation indefinitely?
- Can the heuristic 20-point-per-category scoring system be replaced or validated against an objective, model-independent accuracy/robustness metric, so that cross-model and cross-study score comparisons (e.g., Heston's 97/100 vs. rough Heston's 99/100) carry real quantitative meaning?
- How would RIDGE perform on model classes without any known reference values, degeneration limits, or local affine approximations (i.e., where Block III benchmarking has no reliable anchor at all) — does the framework degrade gracefully or become unable to terminate?
- Does the repository's accumulated "expertise" generalize beyond the specific COS/Fourier valuation paradigm used here, e.g., to finite-difference PDE solvers, tree methods, or machine-learning-based pricing surrogates?
