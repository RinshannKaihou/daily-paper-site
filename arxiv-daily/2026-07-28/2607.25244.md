> **一句话总结：** CADENCE 用 BatchTopK 稀疏自编码器把一个冻结的 12 导联心电图基础模型（CSFM）第 6 层表征分解为 8,192 个稀疏"心脏原子"，这些原子在临床表型和形态学概念上的最优单原子 AUROC（0.88/0.90）显著超过原始稠密维度（0.78/0.83），稀疏探针在表型预测上把 AUROC 从稠密嵌入的 0.93 提到 0.95，并在外部 PTB-XL 队列上无需重训练即可复现（最优原子平均 AUROC 0.825 vs. 稠密 0.720）。

> **TL;DR:** CADENCE trains a BatchTopK sparse autoencoder on the frozen residual stream (Layer 6, d=768) of a 12-lead ECG foundation model (CSFM), factorizing over 9M patch-token embeddings from MIMIC-IV-ECG into a dictionary of 8,192 sparse "cardiac atoms." These atoms align far better with clinical concepts than raw dense dimensions (best-atom mean AUROC 0.88 for phenotypes and 0.90 for morphology vs. 0.78/0.83 for the best dense dimension at Layer 6), and sparse-atom probes match or beat dense-embedding probes while attributing predictions to a handful of named atoms (phenotype AUROC 0.93→0.95). On an independent external cohort, PTB-XL, the frozen MIMIC-trained dictionary transfers without retraining (best-atom mean AUROC 0.825 vs. dense 0.720 across diagnostic tasks), and single-atom ablation on a frozen downstream predictor selectively moves target logits (e.g., -0.62 for LBBB) while frequency-matched random/wrong-concept atoms leave them unchanged.

## 核心贡献

- 提出 CADENCE 框架：在冻结的 12 导联心电图基础模型（CSFM，6 层 Transformer，d=768）的第 6 层残差流上训练 BatchTopK 稀疏自编码器（F=8192，k=128），把原本高维且叠加纠缠（superposition）的稠密表征分解为约 8,192 个近似单语义的"心脏原子"字典，据作者所称这是稀疏自编码器首次应用于生理时间序列基础模型（此前主要用于语言/蛋白质/单细胞等模型）。
- 系统验证原子比原始神经元维度更好地对齐临床概念：跨全部 6 层，原子的最优单特征概念关联 AUROC 全面超过稠密维度和随机噪声对照；在第 6 层，表型和形态学的最优单原子平均 AUROC 分别为 0.88 和 0.90，对应稠密维度仅为 0.78 和 0.83，且该优势随深度增大。
- 稀疏探针在保持甚至提升下游预测准确率的同时实现"可归因"：用原子激活拟合的 ℓ2 正则逻辑回归探针在表型、形态学、年龄预测上匹配或超过稠密嵌入探针（表型 AUROC 0.93→0.95），并能把每个预测分解为少数几个具名原子的贡献（如 PVC 探针 AUROC 0.947，最重原子对应约 91.5 倍富集于"频发室性早搏"报告语句）。
- 展示原子空间几何无监督地恢复了已知的心脏病理关系（如 LBBB/RBBB/IVCD/起搏心律与宽QRS成块、QTc延长与之相邻，房颤/房扑单独成块），并通过单原子消融实验证明原子对冻结下游预测器具有选择性因果作用（如消融 LBBB 原子使目标 logit 下降 0.62，而频率匹配的随机原子和另一概念原子几乎无影响）。
- 构建自动化 LLM 描述-验证流水线：仅基于几何证据（不接触机器报告文本）为每个原子生成结构化描述，并通过让 LLM 仅凭文字描述预测留出激活来定量验证描述的忠实度（1000 个随机原子的预测-实测 Spearman 中位数 0.65，92% 超过 0.3）；并在独立外部队列 PTB-XL 上验证冻结字典可迁移，恢复相同的生理概念且保持表型预测性能。

## Key Contributions

- **First application of sparse dictionary learning to a physiological time-series foundation model.** CADENCE applies a BatchTopK sparse autoencoder (SAE) to the frozen residual stream of a 12-lead ECG foundation model (CSFM, 6-layer transformer, d=768), factorizing Layer-6 patch-token embeddings (~9M tokens from 150k MIMIC-IV-ECG records) into a dictionary of F=8,192 sparse "cardiac atoms" (k=128 active per token), extending SAE interpretability work previously concentrated on language, protein, and single-cell models to a new, clinically consequential modality.
- **Atoms are cleaner, more linearly-accessible concept detectors than raw neurons at every depth.** Across all six CSFM layers, per-feature held-out concept-association AUROC is systematically higher for SAE atoms than for either dense CSFM dimensions or a random-noise control (Figure A3), and the margin widens with depth; at Layer 6 the best-atom mean AUROC is 0.88 for phenotypes and 0.90 for morphology vs. 0.78 and 0.83 for the best dense dimension.
- **Sparse-atom probes match or exceed dense-embedding probes while being attributable.** ℓ1/ℓ2-regularized logistic probes fit on max-pooled atom activations match or beat probes fit on the mean-pooled dense embedding for phenotype, morphology, and age prediction (phenotype AUROC improves from 0.929→0.946 at the deepest layers per the abstract's 0.93→0.95 headline figure), while each prediction decomposes into a small, named set of contributing atoms (e.g., PVC probe AUROC 0.947, decomposed into 5 atoms whose top-weighted atom is ~90x over-represented in reports mentioning frequent PVCs).
- **Atom-space geometry recovers physiologically coherent concept relationships without relational supervision.** Cosine similarity between concept "prototype fingerprints" (mean z-scored atom activation over each concept's positive ECGs) self-organizes curated phenotypes into two known cardiology blocks — a conduction/wide-QRS/QTc block (LBBB, RBBB, IVCD, paced rhythm, wide QRS, long QTc; pairwise cosine 0.64-0.87) and an atrial-tachyarrhythmia block (AF, flutter; cosine 0.81) — matching known physiological pairings (marked with stars in Figure 3), and this structure is robust to a signed-AUROC fingerprint variant and replicates on external PTB-XL data.
- **Single named atoms causally and selectively steer frozen downstream predictions.** An integrated-gradients ablation audit on four targets (LBBB, wide-QRS, age, 1-year mortality) shows that scaling the single most-attributed atom's activation from 1→0 drives the frozen predictor's target logit down by a large, monotonic margin (-0.62 LBBB, -0.40 wide-QRS, -0.36 age, -0.27 mortality on N=80 high-confidence positives), while a frequency/magnitude-matched random atom and an atom encoding an unrelated concept (atrial fibrillation) leave the logit essentially unchanged — evidence the atom functions as a genuine, isolable unit of the model's computation rather than a spurious correlate.
- **A scalable, quantitatively-validated LLM auto-interpretation pipeline generalizes to an external cohort.** Following an InterPLM-style describe-then-validate protocol, GPT-based descriptions are generated from lead/beat-phase activation geometry alone (no report text) for 2,991 atoms that pass activation-quality filters (dead/rare/near-constant/weak/lead-diffuse atoms excluded), and validated by having the LLM predict held-out activation levels from the text description alone (median Spearman ρ=0.65 across 1,000 sampled atoms, 92% with ρ>0.3, only 2% failing at ρ≤0); the frozen, MIMIC-trained SAE dictionary is then applied without retraining to PTB-XL (21,799 ECGs, different country/acquisition system), recovering overlapping concepts and matching phenotype-prediction performance (best-atom mean AUROC 0.825 vs. dense 0.720 across diagnostic superclasses).

## Methodology

CADENCE builds its dictionary on top of a pre-trained, frozen 12-lead ECG foundation model (CSFM), a 6-layer transformer encoder with hidden width d=768 that tokenizes each lead into non-overlapping 100ms patches (25 samples at 250Hz), yielding 1,200 patch tokens per ECG (12 leads × 100 patches). The residual-stream activations at all six layers, H ∈ R^{1200×d}, are the target for dictionary learning; the backbone is never fine-tuned. A separate BatchTopK sparse autoencoder is trained at each layer on a standardized token x ∈ R^d: the encoder produces latent codes z = BatchTopK_k(W_enc(x−b_dec)+b_enc) that keep, on average, the k largest activations per token across the batch (giving an overcomplete dictionary of F atoms with a fixed average activation budget), and the decoder reconstructs x̂ = W_dec z + b_dec, trained with reconstruction loss plus an auxiliary term that lets the largest-active latents explain the residual (mitigating dead atoms) and unit-normalized decoder columns after each step. Training uses ~9M tokens sampled from ~150k MIMIC-IV-ECG recordings (60 tokens/recording, balanced across leads), with F=8,192 and k=128 selected via a grid search over F∈{4096,8192,16384,32768} and k∈{32,64,128,256} at Layer 6, optimizing a three-way tradeoff of reconstruction explained variance, dead-atom fraction, and count of phenotype-specific atoms (interpretability yield peaks at k=128, F=8192; Figure A2).

To connect atoms to clinical concepts, per-ECG atom activation is defined as the maximum activation of the atom over the recording's tokens (chosen because pathological events like PVCs, pacing spikes, or ST changes are temporally localized and averaging would dilute them). For a binary concept c and atom j, association is the rank-based AUROC of a_j against c, folded to [0.5,1] to be sign-agnostic; the largest such association at a layer is the concept's "best-atom" score. Each concept is also summarized by an atom-space "prototype fingerprint" (mean z-scored atom activation over the concept's positive ECGs), and concept-concept similarity is the cosine of these fingerprints. Downstream predictivity is tested via ℓ2-regularized logistic-regression probes fit on per-ECG atom activations (subject-disjoint train/test split), compared against probes fit on the dense Layer-6 embedding; because atom activations are sparse and named, each probe's prediction decomposes into a small set of per-atom coefficient contributions, ranked strictly by probe weight (no manual reselection). Causal validity is tested via an ablation audit: for each of four targets (LBBB, wide-QRS, age, 1-year mortality), an exact integrated-gradients attribution on a frozen linear predictor over the dense Layer-6 embedding identifies the single atom contributing most to the target logit; that atom's activation is scaled from full to zero, decoded back into the embedding, and the frozen predictor's logit is read out, compared against a frequency/magnitude-matched random atom and a "wrong" atom encoding an unrelated concept (atrial fibrillation), on N=80 high-confidence target-positive ECGs. Finally, an automated LLM interpretation pipeline (GPT-5.4-mini) auto-interprets only atoms passing activation-quality filters (excluding dead, rare (<20 high-activation ECGs), near-constant (firing on >90% of ECGs), weak, or lead-diffuse (per-lead activation entropy >0.85) atoms — 2,991 of 8,192 pass), describing each from its top-activating waveform crops and lead/beat-phase activation-geometry summaries alone (no machine-report text), then validating the description by having the model predict held-out activation levels from the text description alone and computing Spearman correlation against measured activations.

## Results & Highlights

- **Best-atom concept association vs. dense dimensions (Layer 6, Figure 2a).** The best single-atom mean AUROC is 0.88 for clinical phenotypes and 0.90 for waveform morphology, compared with 0.78 and 0.83 for the best CSFM dense embedding dimension — a margin that widens with network depth and holds across all 6 layers (Table A1 shows per-concept breakdowns, e.g., LBBB: SAE 0.979 vs. dense 0.891 at Layer 6; RBBB: SAE 0.969 vs. dense 0.797).
- **Downstream probe accuracy (Figure 2b, Table A2).** Sparse atom probes match or exceed dense-embedding probes on the held-out test AUROC across all three task families at the deepest layer, improving mean test AUROC by +0.017 on phenotype (0.929→0.946), +0.011 on age, and +0.005 on morphology; the advantage is largest in the shallow layers, where Layer-1 atom probes reach 0.861/0.805 vs. 0.819/0.805 on phenotype/age for the dense embedding (the paper's headline abstract figure states phenotype AUROC improves from 0.93 to 0.95 overall).
- **External validation on PTB-XL (independent cardiologist-labeled cohort, 21,799 ECGs, Appendix A.6).** The frozen, MIMIC-trained per-layer SAEs, applied without any retraining or re-estimation of input statistics, reproduce the best-single-feature advantage: best-atom mean AUROC is 0.825 vs. 0.720 for the best dense dimension over five cardiologist-adjudicated diagnostic superclasses, and atoms lead dense dimensions in all five measurement-concept families drawn from 49 automated 12SL/ECGDeli measurement concepts.
- **Sparse probing decomposition on named diagnoses (Section 4.4, Figure 4b).** The PVC atom probe reaches test AUROC 0.947, with its top-weighted atom (rank 1, coefficient 0.73, ~3x the next-ranked atom) ~90x over-represented ("lift 91.5x") among ECGs whose machine report says "frequent premature ventricular contractions"; the paced-rhythm probe reaches AUROC 0.951, and analogous decompositions are shown for sinus bradycardia (AUROC 0.991) and RBBB (AUROC 0.988) in Figure A1.
- **Ablation causal audit (Figure 4a, N=80 high-confidence positives per target).** Scaling the single most-attributed atom from dose 1→0 drives the frozen predictor's target logit down by -0.62 (LBBB), -0.40 (wide-QRS), -0.36 (age), and -0.27 (1-year mortality), monotonically with dose, while a frequency-matched random atom and an atom encoding an unrelated concept (atrial fibrillation) leave the logit essentially unchanged across all four targets — a genuine causal/steering result, not merely a correlational one.
- **LLM auto-interpretation faithfulness (Figure 5a, Section 4.5).** Across 1,000 randomly sampled auto-interpreted atoms, the LLM's held-out activation predictions made from the text description alone (no access to activations) achieve a median Spearman ρ=0.65 against measured activations, with 92% of atoms exceeding ρ=0.3 and only 2% failing (ρ≤0); an ablation that additionally supplies the model with full machine-report text barely changes faithfulness (median ρ 0.665→0.670), ruling out report-label leakage as the source of the correlation.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core technical move — training a BatchTopK SAE on a foundation model's residual stream to decompose superposed features — is a direct, largely unmodified transplant of the InterPLM-style describe-then-validate protocol (Simon and Zou, 2025, cited explicitly) and standard SAE machinery (Bussmann et al.'s BatchTopK) from language/protein models onto ECG embeddings. The paper's own framing is honest about this: it explicitly positions itself as "the first" to apply this paradigm to a physiological time-series FM rather than claiming a new SAE variant. The genuine novelty is domain-specific: the finding that atoms organize hierarchically across rhythm/phenotype/morphology timescales, and that atom-space geometry recovers known cardiology adjacency (e.g., wide-QRS-long-QTc interval-containment) without relational supervision, is a real and non-trivial empirical contribution rather than a purely methodological one.

**Rigor:** The use of patient-level bootstrap confidence intervals (Tables A1/A2, Figure A4) and a subject-disjoint train/test split for all probes is good practice and strengthens the headline claims. However, several of the "best-atom" comparisons in Figure 2a/Table A1 select the single best atom (or best dense dimension) post hoc from a very large pool (8,192 atoms vs. 768 dense dims) — this is an inherently asymmetric comparison (a much larger search space is more likely to contain some feature that happens to correlate well with any given concept), and the paper does not report a multiple-comparisons correction or a dictionary-size-matched dense baseline (e.g., comparing against 8,192 random projections of the dense embedding) to isolate how much of the gap is "SAE atoms are more monosemantic" vs. simply "picking the best of 8,192 things beats picking the best of 768 things." The random-noise control in Figure A3 addresses a different question (is the metric itself well-calibrated) rather than this specific confound.

**Reproducibility:** Code is public, training data (MIMIC-IV-ECG) and validation data (PTB-XL) are both public PhysioNet resources, and hyperparameter selection (F, k) is documented with an explicit grid search (Figure A2/Appendix A.1). This is a relatively reproducible setup for a paper of this type.

**Significance:** The external-cohort transfer result (Section A.6, PTB-XL) is the paper's strongest evidence against a common SAE-interpretability failure mode — that recovered "concepts" are training-cohort artifacts rather than properties of the representation itself — and is a differentiator from much of the language/protein SAE literature, which rarely tests cross-cohort/cross-acquisition-system transfer this directly.

**Red flags / weaknesses specific to this paper:** (1) The LLM auto-interpretation validation (median ρ=0.65) is itself measured by the same LLM family that generated the descriptions, predicting from geometry summaries that are somewhat close in structure to what it was asked to describe — this is a weaker validation than an independent human-rater faithfulness check, though the paper does show report-text leakage is not the driver (Appendix A.7). (2) The atom-inclusion filter for auto-interpretation excludes more than half the dictionary (only 2,991 of 8,192 atoms pass the dead/rare/near-constant/weak/lead-diffuse filters) — the paper is transparent about this, but it means the "explainable dictionary" claim applies to roughly a third to a half of atoms, not the full 8,192. (3) No comparison is made against alternative unsupervised decomposition baselines (e.g., PCA, ICA, or vanilla top-k/ReLU SAEs) on the same concept-association metrics — the choice of BatchTopK over these alternatives is justified only by an internal hyperparameter grid search, not by a head-to-head accuracy/interpretability comparison against a non-BatchTopK method. (4) The concept vocabulary used for validation (16 phenotypes, morphology, age, mortality — Table A4) is itself curated by the authors from standard diagnostic categories, so "concept discovery" claims are partly circular: the paper is good at showing atoms align with concepts the authors already knew to look for, and less direct evidence is given that atoms reveal genuinely novel/unnamed physiological structure beyond re-deriving known ECG semiotics.

## Strengths

- Clean, multi-pronged validation strategy combining concept-association AUROC, linear probing, atom-space relational geometry, causal ablation, and LLM-based auto-interpretation with a text-only held-out-activation-prediction check — each addressing a different potential failure mode of "these features look interpretable."
- Genuine external-cohort replication on PTB-XL (different country, different acquisition system) without retraining the SAE, which is a stronger generalization test than most SAE-interpretability papers attempt.
- Direct clinical framing with a cardiologist co-author, concrete waveform-level visualizations (Figures 2c/2d, A5-A9) tying named atoms to specific ECG morphology (PVC event detection, AF fibrillatory baseline, ST-segment primitives), and an explicit ablation-based causal audit rather than relying solely on correlational probing.
- Transparent hyperparameter selection methodology (three-axis grid search reported in the appendix) and patient-level bootstrap CIs on the main downstream-probe comparisons.

## Limitations

- The paper itself acknowledges: atom naming is bounded by current cardiology knowledge and fixed geometric heuristics, so some interpretations may be incomplete; the study is restricted to standard resting 12-lead ECGs (single-lead, ambulatory, and longer continuous recordings are out of scope); and the atoms are explicitly stated to not be diagnostic devices (not regulatory cleared, not for clinical decisions).
- The best-atom comparisons are a post hoc pick from a much larger feature pool (8,192 atoms) than the dense-dimension baseline (768 dims), which likely inflates the apparent interpretability gap somewhat beyond what a size-matched comparison would show.
- No baseline comparison against other unsupervised decomposition methods (PCA/ICA/vanilla SAE variants) is reported, so it's unclear how much of the benefit is attributable specifically to BatchTopK SAEs versus any sparse/overcomplete decomposition.
- The concept-discovery framing is validated primarily against a pre-specified list of known diagnostic categories and measurements rather than demonstrating novel, previously unnamed physiological structure.

## Relevance

考虑到该研究方向聚焦于用稀疏自编码器解构基础模型表征、实现机制可解释性与跨模态可解释性方法迁移，本文将该范式系统地扩展到生理时间序列（ECG）领域，其在外部队列上的免重训迁移验证和单原子因果消融实验，对关注可解释 AI 和医疗基础模型审计的研究者具有较高参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of the reported "best-atom beats best-dense-dimension" gap survives a dictionary-size-matched control (e.g., comparing against the best of 8,192 random or PCA-derived directions in the dense space) rather than 768 raw dimensions?
- Would training the SAE jointly across all 6 layers (rather than a separate SAE per layer) reveal cross-layer atom composition or hierarchy that the current per-layer approach cannot expose?
- Can the ablation-based causal audit be extended beyond single-atom edits to test whether combinations of atoms are jointly necessary/sufficient for a diagnosis, given that several probes (e.g., PVC, paced rhythm) rely on 5+ atoms rather than one?
- How robust is the atom dictionary to distribution shift beyond PTB-XL — e.g., pediatric ECGs, different lead placements, or ICU telemetry — where the "true" underlying physiological concepts may differ from the MIMIC training cohort's case mix?
- Does the LLM auto-interpretation pipeline's ~0.65 median Spearman correlation hold up under an independent human-cardiologist blind rating of description quality, rather than only self-consistency with another LLM call?
