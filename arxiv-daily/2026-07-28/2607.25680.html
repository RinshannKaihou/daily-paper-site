Functional Similarity & Rashomon Sets

Two models can match exactly on accuracy yet agree on only 45% of the entire feature space.

一句话总结：本文提出几何 Rashomon 对齐（gRA）——用特征空间中均匀采样的合成点计算两模型预测一致率；在 92 个 UCI 数据集上，gRA 与准确率差的相关性仅为中等（r=0.514），并用合成案例证明准确率相同不代表模型真正相似。

Key Metric
r = 0.514
Correlation between geometric agreement (gRA) and accuracy gap across 92 UCI datasets — geometric similarity is at best a moderate predictor of accuracy differences.
vs. r = 0.745 correlation between gRA and standard test-set agreement (dRA)
Why This Matters

Warns against a common blind spot in model selection, ensembling, and interpretability: assuming matched accuracy means matched models — validated only on tree pruning so far.

Critical Edge

Sampling 1,000 uniform points inside a bounding box with up to 649 features likely measures off-manifold extrapolation, not real structural similarity.

Methodology

agreement θ(A,B)→ population form Ex~Q[1{A(x)=B(x)}]→ Q = P(X) → dRA/ Q = U(F) → gRA
  • dRA (standard practice): agreement estimated on held-out test data drawn from the true data distribution P(X).
  • gRA (this paper): agreement estimated on 1,000 synthetic points sampled uniformly from the bounding box of the feature space F — independent of any real data.
  • A change-of-measure identity ties the two exactly: dRA = gRA only when P(X) is uniform; otherwise the gap is bounded by the total-variation distance between P(X) and U(F). High dRA with low gRA means two models agree where data happens to live but diverge everywhere else — agreement that is fragile under distribution shift.
  • Main experiment: pruned vs. unpruned scikit-learn decision trees (same hypothesis class) across 92 UCI datasets, 5-fold CV, seed 42. Per fold: accuracy difference, dRA on the test partition, gRA from 1,000 uniform bounding-box points.
  • Plus four synthetic 2D case studies (SVM-RBF/MLP, logistic regression/tree, linear SVM/MLP, two linear SVMs) spanning all four gRA × Δaccuracy regimes.

Strengths

  • Elegant, theoretically grounded: an exact change-of-measure relation links dRA and gRA.
  • Purely post-hoc — no retraining, applies to any model pair, open-sourced as the ralign package.
  • Quadrant analysis (gRA × |Δacc|) isolates a real failure mode accuracy-only comparison misses entirely.
  • Large, diverse benchmark: 92 UCI datasets, 32–4,600+ instances, 4–649 features.
  • Four synthetic case studies give crisp, concrete numeric illustrations of each regime.

Limitations

  • Scale-tested on only one model pair (pruned vs. unpruned trees); broader claims rest on one small synthetic toy example.
  • Curse of dimensionality unaddressed: uniform bounding-box sampling in up to 649-dim spaces likely lands off-manifold.
  • No explanation of how one-hot-encoded categorical features are handled under continuous uniform sampling.
  • No benchmark against existing similarity measures (Cohen's kappa, disagreement distance, CKA) despite citing the relevant survey.
  • Purely descriptive statistics — no significance testing, confidence intervals, or cross-dataset dependence analysis.
  • The per-fold bounding box is itself arbitrary for unbounded or skewed feature domains.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Does gRA stay meaningful as dimensionality grows, or mostly measure off-manifold artifacts?
  • How should uniform sampling handle one-hot / categorical feature spaces without generating invalid instances?
  • Would low-discrepancy or boundary-focused sampling improve on plain uniform sampling?
  • How does gRA compare head-to-head with kappa, disagreement distance, or CKA on the same benchmark?
  • How does gRA behave across structurally different model families (nets vs. trees vs. linear), not just tree pruning?
  • Can gRA be operationalized for model selection, ensembling, or drift monitoring with quantitative proof of added value?
