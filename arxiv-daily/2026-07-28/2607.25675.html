Key Finding

DecoEvo beats SkillOpt on all 15 backbone‑benchmark pairs by refusing to score rubric updates with the solver's own proxy.

DecoEvo: Score-Decoupled Co-Evolution of Solver and Rubric-Generator Skills in Text Space ↗

Jiangwang Chen, Zixin Song, Junlin Liu et al. (13 authors) · 28 Jul 2026 ·
text-space optimization LLM-as-judge rubric generation co-evolution reward hacking / proxy optimization

Key Metric

15/15

backbone×benchmark combinations where DecoEvo scores highest — e.g. HealthBench/GPT-4o rises 41.7→45.3 (+8.6%) over SkillOpt, while a naive score-coupled variant (SC-CoEvo) falls below SkillOpt in 13/15 and below Zero-shot in 7/15.

Why This Matters

A reusable pattern for evolving evaluation rubrics without letting the judged model game them — relevant to LLM-as-judge, RL reward hacking, and self-play system design.

原文 (Relevance)：这篇论文提供了一个具体、可复用的设计模式——把"评价标准本身也参与进化"和"避免评价标准被优化对象钻空子"同时实现，对 LLM-as-judge、rubric 自动生成、reward hacking 等场景有直接借鉴价值。

Critical Edge

The only empirical baselines are Zero-shot, SkillOpt, and the authors' own SC-CoEvo construction — several directly relevant concurrent adaptive-rubric methods are discussed in related work but never actually compared against.

一句话总结 · Chinese Abstract (source)

DecoEvo 在冻结骨干模型下，让"解题者技能"和"评分标准生成器技能"两个可编辑文本 artifact 共同进化，但强制评分标准的更新只能靠"结构审计+近似平局对比审计"两种与聚合分数无关的信号来验收，从而避免评分标准被 solver 的分数"带偏"；在 HealthBench、WritingBench、ResearchQA 及两个跨基准迁移任务、GPT-4o/Qwen3-4B/Qwen3-8B 三个骨干上全面超过 SkillOpt（五基准平均相对提升 2.8%–5.0%），而未做分数解耦的对照组反而在 13/15 组合上低于 SkillOpt。

Methodology

All roles — solver, rubric generator, judge, both auditors, both skill-rewriters — run on the same frozen backbone under different role prompts; only text artifacts change, never weights.

  • Inner loop: solver answers a batch under skill s, the current rubric scores it, a judge gives criterion-level feedback, a rewriter proposes edit s′ — accepted only if it beats the incumbent on held-out data by margin ε.
  • Outer loop (every K steps or after M rejections): two audits blind to the aggregate score diagnose the rubric — a task-conditioned structural audit and a rubric-blind near-tie pairwise audit — distilled into candidate g′, accepted only via the Pareto gate above.
  • Data stays disjoint throughout: train pool, probe set, held-out validation (solver & generator), a diagnostic set, and a final test set scored under held-out gold rubrics.

  • Ablations (HealthBench, avg. 3 backbones): removing verification costs the most (2.4–2.9 pts), then contrastive audit (1.7–2.1), distillation (1.4–1.8), structural audit (1.3–1.7), rubric-blindness/near-tie sampling (1.0–1.5) — every component contributes.
  • Alternative-explanation controls rule out "just more compute/info": Task-Prior-only 42.4, Budget-Matched 42.1, Direct-Audit-to-solver 43.7 — all trail DecoEvo's 45.3, at 1.89× SkillOpt's token cost.
  • Rubric–gold alignment (independent GPT-5.5 matcher, 91.3% balanced acc.): DecoEvo's rubrics gain ~12 F1 pts over SkillOpt's fixed rubric (HealthBench 44.6→56.9); SC-CoEvo's F1 instead drops 5.3–6.3 pts.
  • Training dynamics: SC-CoEvo's internal rubric-proxy climbs to 89.1 while its true gold-rubric score declines after an early peak — proxy and task quality visibly diverge.

Strengths

  • SC-CoEvo empirically collapses (below Zero-shot in 7/15 settings) — the risk DecoEvo guards against is demonstrated, not just argued.
  • Six-way ablation + three alternative-explanation controls + an independent rubric-alignment matcher jointly rule out "just more compute" or "looser rubrics" explanations.
  • Consistent gains across 3 backbones (one proprietary, two open) and across in-domain plus two independent cross-benchmark transfers, with paired CIs and Holm correction.
  • Definition 1 (Score-Decoupled Generator Update) gives the mechanism a precise, falsifiable meaning rather than a vague design philosophy.

Limitations

  • No empirical comparison to directly relevant concurrent adaptive-rubric methods (EvoRubric, OpenRS, ARCO, DR Tulu, online rubric elicitation) — all cited but none run head-to-head.
  • Every role (solver, generator, judge, both auditors, both rewriters) shares one frozen backbone; a heterogeneous-backbone setup that could reveal self-preference bias is untested.
  • Transfer is tested only within-domain (medicine, writing) — no cross-domain transfer (e.g. medicine→math/code) is evaluated.
  • Concrete hyperparameter values and some interval details are deferred to supplementary material not present in the reviewed PDF, limiting independent reproducibility.
  • Training-time cost is 1.89× SkillOpt's token budget, even though only the final solver skill is deployed.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Would score-decoupling hold if solver and rubric-generator used different backbones, removing shared self-preference bias?
  2. How sensitive are results to the near-tie threshold τ_tie and the Pareto tolerance η?
  3. Could the dual-view audits themselves be gamed over many outer-loop iterations — a subtler, one-level-up form of score-coupling?
  4. How would DecoEvo compare against the concurrent adaptive-rubric baselines it cites but never tests (EvoRubric, OpenRS, ARCO, DR Tulu)?
  5. Does the ~12-pt F1 gold-rubric alignment gain translate into better human preference agreement, or only closer match to each benchmark's official grader?
