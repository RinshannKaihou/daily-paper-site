> **一句话总结：** 论文构造了一个涵盖词汇绑定、事实关联、行为策略、因果映射、程序推理五类学习目标的自建基准，用早/中/晚层局部化 LoRA 在 Llama-3.1-8B（并在 Mistral-7B、Gemma-2-9B、OLMo-2-7B、Qwen2.5-14B 上复现）测试后发现：词汇绑定最适合早层适配（早层习得率 97.2% 接近全栈 99.7%，但迁移只有 40.6% vs 全栈 84.9%，差距 44.3 个百分点），事实关联则相反，晚层局部适配的迁移达到 63.1%，比早层高 42.7 个百分点。

> **TL;DR:** On a self-constructed 5-objective localization benchmark (lexical binding, factual association, behavioral policy, causal mapping, procedural reasoning) built on Llama-3.1-8B, this paper shows that layer-restricted LoRA produces qualitatively different "adaptation geometries" per objective — e.g., factual-association transfer under late-layer LoRA (63.1%) beats early-layer LoRA (20.4%) by 42.7pp (95% CI [35.3, 47.3]) but still trails full-stack LoRA (82.0%) by 18.9pp, while lexical-binding early-layer LoRA nearly matches full-stack acquisition (97.2% vs 99.7%) yet loses 44.3pp of transfer (40.6% vs 84.9%). The directional patterns replicate (4-5/5 models, sign-test p≤.031) across Mistral-7B, Gemma-2-9B, OLMo-2-7B, and Qwen2.5-14B.

## 核心贡献

- 提出"适配几何"（adaptation geometry）概念：把一个学习目标在习得（acquisition）、迁移（transfer）、有界性（boundedness）三个指标上，随适配层位置（早/中/晚/全栈）变化的剖面，作为刻画"学习本质"的一种可测量、可复现的信号，而不仅把适配效果视为单一标量。
- 构造了一个五类学习目标的受控合成基准（词汇绑定、事实关联、行为策略学习、因果映射、程序推理），每类目标 25 个"潜在规范"，每个规范生成同分布、复述、泛化、负控制四类评测样本，并对每个目标独立校准训练预算，避免任务难度混淆定位效应。
- 在 Llama-3.1-8B 上系统比较全栈 LoRA 与早/中/晚层局部 LoRA（按层数四分位窗口划分），发现五种目标呈现五种互不相同的适配几何：词汇绑定偏早层、事实关联偏晚层、行为策略学习呈分布式（晚层负责动作选择、中层负责策略保持有界）、因果映射偏中层/全栈、程序推理在迁移与有界性之间存在中/晚层权衡。
- 通过参数量匹配对照（扩展局部层秩、缩减全栈层秩）证明上述定位信号不是简单由可训练参数总量差异造成的伪影。
- 将实验扩展到 Mistral-7B、Gemma-2-9B、OLMo-2-7B、Qwen2.5-14B 四个模型家族（复用 Llama 校准的预算而非各自重新校准），发现主要方向性对比在 4/5 或 5/5 模型中复现（符号检验 p ≤ .031），同时用方差分解表明目标身份解释了 25.0% 的剖面变异、模型身份解释了 34.2%，说明适配几何既受学习目标驱动也受架构/预算影响，并非架构无关的普适规律。

## Key Contributions

- Introduces "adaptation geometry": the joint profile of acquisition, transfer, and boundedness that a learning objective exhibits when LoRA is restricted to different depth regions — reframing adaptation site as a functional design variable rather than a mere efficiency knob.
- Builds a controlled synthetic benchmark spanning five operationally distinct learning objectives (lexical binding, factual association, behavioral policy learning, causal mapping, procedural reasoning), each with 25 latent specifications and in-distribution/paraphrase/generalization/negative-control splits, with per-objective calibrated training budgets to decouple task difficulty from localization effects.
- Shows on Llama-3.1-8B that full-stack vs. early/middle/late-layer LoRA produces five qualitatively distinct depth-wise signatures: lexical binding is early-localizable, factual association favors late layers, behavioral policy learning is distributed (late for action acquisition, middle for policy boundedness), causal mapping favors middle/full-stack adaptation, and procedural reasoning shows a middle-layer transfer vs. late-layer boundedness tradeoff.
- Validates that these signatures survive parameter-matched controls (rank-expanded localized adapters and rank-reduced full-stack adapters), ruling out trainable-parameter-count as the sole explanation.
- Extends the analysis to four additional model families (Mistral-7B, Gemma-2-9B, OLMo-2-7B, Qwen2.5-14B) using Llama-calibrated budgets, finding directional replication of the primary contrasts in 4/5 or 5/5 models (sign-test p ≤ .031), and a variance decomposition showing objective identity explains 25.0% and model identity 34.2% of profile variation — evidence for real but not architecture-invariant localization structure.

## Methodology

The authors define three metrics per learning objective T and adaptation site s: **acquisition** A_T(s) = mean of in-distribution and paraphrase accuracy; **transfer** R_T(s) = accuracy on held-out generalization examples; and **boundedness** B_T(s) = accuracy on negative-control examples (scored with an objective-specific rule, e.g. strict negative-control accuracy for lexical/factual objectives, or policy-withholding/null-effect/incomplete-procedure accuracy for behavioral/causal/procedural objectives). The triple (A, R, B) at a given site defines the objective's "adaptation geometry" G_T(s).

Five objectives are built from synthetic latent specifications (25 per objective, 12 training + 22 evaluation examples each): lexical binding (attaching a novel surface form to a known concept, e.g. "daxel" → bird), factual association (subject-relation-object facts, e.g. "Norvessa's capital is Lumo"), behavioral policy learning (context-conditioned action selection, e.g. missing-recipient → ASK_FOR_RECIPIENT), causal mapping (intervention → effect rules, e.g. opening Valve A → temperature decreases), and procedural reasoning (ordered step-completion, e.g. setup+activation+verification → ready).

Training budgets (examples per specification) are calibrated per objective via a two-stage sweep (coarse full-stack sweep to find the transition point, then a neighboring multi-seed sweep), yielding budget 10 for lexical binding/behavioral policy/causal mapping and budget 8 for factual association/procedural reasoning. LoRA adapters are then trained under four regimes on Llama-3.1-8B: full-stack (all layers), and early/middle/late localized (one contiguous quarter-depth region, base model frozen elsewhere), with rank matched per adapted layer so localized comparisons isolate adaptation-site effects at fixed per-layer capacity. Statistical analysis uses seed-level paired contrasts (3 seeds) with bootstrap 95% CIs, plus an omnibus permutation test over the between-objective dispersion of localized-minus-full profiles to test whether objective identity explains geometry beyond chance. A "mislocation penalty" (preferred-site minus non-preferred-site performance) quantifies the cost of placing an objective in a poorly matched region. Parameter-matched controls (rank-expanded-localized and rank-reduced-full-stack, matching rank×layers products) test whether signatures are artifacts of raw capacity differences. Cross-model robustness is tested on Mistral-7B, Gemma-2-9B, OLMo-2-7B, and Qwen2.5-14B using Llama-calibrated (not independently re-tuned) budgets, with localized regions defined by normalized (quarter-depth) rather than fixed layer indices.

## Results & Highlights

- **Lexical binding is early-localizable**: early-layer LoRA nearly matches full-stack acquisition (97.2% vs. 99.7% full-stack) and improves boundedness over full-stack (68.0% vs. 39.7%, a 28.3pp gain, CI [25.0, 30.7]), but full-stack adaptation outperforms early adaptation on transfer by 44.3pp (84.9% vs. 40.6%, CI [37.3, 48.0]). Early beats late-layer adaptation on acquisition by 20.9pp (CI [17.2, 23.6]) and beats middle by 10.8pp (CI [7.6, 13.2]).
- **Factual association favors late-layer adaptation** among localized regions: late-layer LoRA reaches 52.1% acquisition / 63.1% transfer, beating early-layer LoRA (32.4% acquisition / 20.4% transfer) by 19.7pp (CI [18.0, 21.6]) on acquisition and 42.7pp (CI [35.3, 47.3]) on transfer — yet full-stack adaptation (89.6% acquisition, 82.0% transfer) still exceeds late-layer transfer by 18.9pp (CI [16.7, 22.0]).
- **Behavioral policy learning is distributed**: full-stack achieves 94.0% acquisition / 94.2% transfer / 89.6% boundedness; late-layer adaptation nearly matches acquisition (93.1%) while middle-layer adaptation gives better transfer (90.4%) and boundedness (85.8%). Middle beats late on boundedness by 21.6pp (CI [15.3, 26.7]), while late beats middle on acquisition by only 5.1pp (CI [0.8, 10.4]).
- **Causal mapping requires middle or full-stack adaptation for transfer**: full-stack saturates acquisition (100.0%) and reaches 80.2% transfer; among localized regions, middle-layer adaptation is best (98.5% acquisition, 68.0% transfer, matching full-stack's 50.0% boundedness), beating early (55.1% acquisition) and late by 12.9pp (CI [2.0, 22.0]) and 9.3pp (CI [8.7, 10.0]) on transfer respectively.
- **Procedural reasoning shows a transfer-boundedness tradeoff**: middle-layer adaptation gives the best localized balance (75.6% acquisition, 62.4% transfer, 76.4% boundedness), beating early on transfer by 28.7pp (CI [23.3, 34.0]); late-layer adaptation maximizes boundedness (82.0%) but drops acquisition to 60.0%.
- **Cross-model replication**: under Llama-calibrated transferred budgets, the strongest and most consistent replications were factual association's late>early transfer advantage (positive in 5/5 models: Gemma 7.8, Llama 48.7, Mistral 39.8, OLMo 63.3, Qwen 37.8 pp; sign-test p=.031), behavioral policy's middle>late boundedness advantage (5/5, p=.031), and procedural reasoning's middle>early transfer advantage (5/5, p=.031); lexical binding's early>late acquisition advantage and causal mapping's middle>early transfer advantage replicated directionally in 4/5 models (p=.188), with Gemma the outlier on both.
- **Model-budget mismatch caveat (paper-specific quirk)**: Gemma-2-9B's causal-mapping performance under the Llama-calibrated transferred budget (B=10) was anomalously weak (full-stack acquisition only 51.2%, transfer 16.0%). A targeted Appendix-E budget-sensitivity sweep on Gemma showed that raising the budget to B=24 recovered acquisition to 92.7% and transfer to 57.3%, indicating the weak cross-model result reflects an under-calibrated budget for Gemma rather than a genuine architectural difference in causal-mapping geometry.
- Omnibus permutation tests found significant objective-specific structure (heterogeneity statistic 0.238, p < .001, 10,000 permutations) and significant model-specific structure (statistic 11.679, p < .001); a variance decomposition attributed 25.0% of profile variation to objective identity, 34.2% to model identity, and 40.8% to residual/interaction/seed variation.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The core idea — that different "kinds" of learning (declarative fact vs. lexical binding vs. policy vs. causal rule vs. procedure) have distinct preferred depths for LoRA adaptation, and that this can be measured as a reproducible three-metric "geometry" — is a genuinely useful reframing that goes beyond prior model-editing locality work (ROME/MEMIT), which studied only factual association. Treating adaptation site as a first-class experimental variable rather than an efficiency hyperparameter is a clean, testable framing.

**Rigor**: Above average for this genre. The authors calibrate training budgets per-objective (rather than using a single fixed budget that would confound difficulty with localization), report bootstrap CIs on every contrast, run an omnibus permutation test to check whether objective-specific structure is more than noise, and include parameter-matched controls (Table 11) to rule out the possibility that "localization signatures" are just an artifact of full-stack adapters having more total trainable parameters. The null-label audits (Table 9, 12) are a nice touch — they verify boundedness gains aren't simply the model collapsing to a "reject everything" default.

**Reproducibility**: Code is public (GitHub link given). However, only 3 seeds are used per condition throughout — the paper itself flags this by treating p-values as "descriptive" rather than decisive and reporting effect sizes/CIs instead, which is an honest but real limitation given how many contrasts are drawn from 3-seed data.

**Missing ablations / weak spots (paper-specific)**: The cross-model experiments reuse Llama-calibrated budgets rather than independently calibrating per model — the paper is explicit that this is a robustness check for "directional replication," not an architecture-invariant claim, and the Gemma causal-mapping result (51.2%/16.0% acquisition/transfer collapsing to near-baseline under the transferred budget, only recovering to 92.7%/57.3% at 2.4x the budget) is a clear illustration that budget mismatch, not geometry, can dominate a cross-model comparison if not caught — a good-faith disclosure, but it also means the paper's headline "distinct geometries generalize across models" claim rests on a diagnostic patch rather than a fully controlled design. The benchmark itself is entirely synthetic (invented pseudowords, fictional countries/facts, templated policies/causal rules/procedures); no real-world dataset (e.g., actual factual-editing benchmarks like CounterFact, or real instruction-tuning corpora) is used, so external validity to realistic adaptation settings is untested. The layer partition is coarse (only early/middle/late quartiles), and the study considers only LoRA, not full fine-tuning, prefix-tuning, or other adapter families — the authors acknowledge both limitations explicitly.

**Significance**: If the localization signatures replicate under more rigorous per-model calibration and on real (non-synthetic) tasks, this could meaningfully inform practical adapter-placement strategies (e.g., don't waste capacity on early layers for facts, don't expect late-layer edits to generalize new lexical mappings robustly). As is, the paper is a solid proof-of-concept / diagnostic tool rather than a ready-to-deploy adaptation recipe.

**Red flags**: None indicating misconduct; the main caveats are self-disclosed (small seed count treated as descriptive, cross-model budget non-recalibration, synthetic-only benchmark) rather than hidden, which is a mark in the paper's favor even though it limits how strong the claims can be taken.

## Strengths

- Clean, testable operationalization of "adaptation geometry" via three interpretable metrics (acquisition, transfer, boundedness) rather than a single aggregate accuracy number.
- Per-objective budget calibration avoids conflating task difficulty with localization effects — a subtle confound many adapter-placement papers ignore.
- Parameter-matched controls (rank-expanded localized, rank-reduced full-stack) directly address the "it's just more parameters" objection.
- Honest statistical treatment: bootstrap CIs, omnibus permutation tests, and explicit acknowledgment that 3-seed p-values are descriptive rather than confirmatory.
- Cross-model replication (5 model families) with an explicit budget-mismatch diagnostic (Gemma causal-mapping) rather than silently cherry-picking favorable models.
- Public code release.

## Limitations

- Entirely synthetic benchmark (invented facts, pseudowords, templated policies/causal rules/procedures) — no evaluation on real-world factual-editing or instruction-tuning datasets.
- Cross-model experiments reuse Llama-calibrated budgets rather than independently optimizing per model, so weak results (e.g., Gemma causal mapping) can reflect budget mismatch rather than true geometry differences, muddying architecture-invariance claims.
- Only 3 seeds per condition; the authors themselves downgrade p-values to "descriptive."
- Only three coarse depth regions (early/middle/late quartiles) are tested, not fine-grained per-layer or overlapping-window analyses.
- Only LoRA is studied; no comparison to full fine-tuning, prefix-tuning, or other adapter mechanisms, so it's unclear whether findings are LoRA-specific or reflect a more general adaptation-mechanism property.
- The five objectives, while operationally distinct, are not shown to be ontologically exhaustive or non-overlapping; the paper explicitly disclaims that adaptation geometry implies strict modularity or uniquely localized internal representations.

## Relevance

该工作提出了一个可复现的"适配几何"诊断框架，把 LoRA 适配层位置与学习类型（事实/词汇/策略/因果/程序）系统关联，对做 LoRA 层选择、参数高效微调策略设计、模型编辑定位研究的读者有直接参考价值，尤其是其"预算校准"和"参数量匹配对照"的实验设计范式值得借鉴到其他适配定位类研究中。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would these adaptation geometries hold on real-world factual-editing benchmarks (e.g., CounterFact, zsRE) or real instruction-tuning data rather than synthetic latent specifications?
- If each additional model family had its own independently calibrated budget (rather than reusing Llama's), would the cross-model replication rates for lexical binding and causal mapping improve, or would new discrepancies emerge?
- How would finer-grained layer partitions (individual layers, overlapping windows, or attention-vs-FFN submodule splits) refine or complicate the early/middle/late trichotomy?
- Do these localization signatures persist under full fine-tuning or other PEFT methods (prefix-tuning, adapters, (IA)^3), or are they specific to LoRA's low-rank update structure?
- Could a "multi-region compositional adaptation" (deploying separate localized adapters targeting acquisition, transfer, and boundedness respectively, as the authors suggest as future work) actually outperform full-stack adaptation, rather than merely approaching it?
- How robust is the adaptation-geometry framework to sequential or compositional edits (multiple objectives adapted simultaneously), which the paper flags as untested but is the realistic deployment scenario for model editing?
