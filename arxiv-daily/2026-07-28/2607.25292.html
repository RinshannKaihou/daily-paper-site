Instruction-Tuned Language Models Cannot Sample from Distributions They Can Describe ↗

Chaemin Jang, Dongman Lee, Jihee Kim · KAIST · 2026-07-28 · arXiv:2607.25292

Key Metric
>94%

of independent calls return the identical modal answer, across five non-trivial synthetic categorical targets (200 gpt-4o calls each, temperature 1.0).

Why This Matters

Anyone using LLMs as survey respondents, simulated personas, or randomized data generators is likely assuming independent samples per call — that assumption is false.

Critical Edge

The paper's headline OpinionQA numbers — the describe pathway's edge and PPA's 21% gain — are demonstrated on gpt-4o only; the five-model replication covers just the synthetic RNG tasks, not the real survey pipeline.

01

Methodology

  1. Build the testbed — five synthetic categorical targets (uniform, skewed binary, bimodal, three-way, skewed 5-way) plus a seven-target panel; measure total-variation (TV) distance between the empirical distribution over 200 independent gpt-4o calls and the stated target.
  2. Locate the failure — at temperature 0 with top_logprobs=20, read the raw logit gap between the top two candidates; separately test raw random-number generation with no persona or distribution framing at all.
  3. Test the cause — matched base-vs-instruct comparison across three 8B model families with different post-training recipes (RLHF, SFT-only, DPO-inclusive), using 3-shot in-context prompts so base models can follow the task.
  4. Sweep interventions — ten prompt-level variants (V0–V9): eight ask the model to still sample one output per call (apologetic framing, bias-naming, in-context demos, algorithmic chain-of-thought); two instead ask it to describe the full distribution in a single call.
  5. Apply to real surveys — 100 OpinionQA items against Pew ground truth, comparing standard Argyle persona aggregation, a demographically-matched Argyle control, the single-call describe pathway, and Prompt-Perturbed Argyle (PPA), which randomizes option order / phrasing / prompt position across calls at zero extra cost.

  • Boundary check: the matched-demographics control (0.48) is statistically indistinguishable from standard Argyle (0.46, Wilcoxon p=0.086) — ruling out persona mismatch as the explanation for describe's edge.
  • All 8 per-call fixes fail: across ten interventions, every "sample one output" correction (apologetic reframing, bias-naming, in-context demos, algorithmic CoT with an explicit random draw) still yields mean TV>0.20; both "describe the distribution" interventions reach mean TV≤0.03.
  • PPA mechanism: entropy-TV regression (N=20 items) finds β=0.27 (p<0.001, R²=0.55) on per-call entropy increase; non-semantic perturbations barely move entropy (ΔH=0.003 vs. PPA's 0.417) and yield negligible TV gain, supporting an entropy-driven mechanism.
  • Logit-level cause: top-2 logit gaps exceed 14 nats at temperature 0; recovering a 70/30 split needs a sampling temperature of ~17 — far beyond any API's allowed maximum of 2.
03

Strengths & Limitations

  • Matched base-vs-instruct triplet across 3 model families with genuinely different post-training recipes (including one SFT-only family) — supports alignment-causation broadly, not just an RLHF-specific story.
  • Logit-level diagnosis (top-2 gap >14 nats, needing decoding temperatures ~17–56 to fix) gives a mechanistic reason standard decoding controls can't remedy the failure.
  • Demographically-matched Argyle control closes a real confound left open by prior describe-vs-sample comparisons.
  • PPA is free (identical API cost) and validated with two independent falsification controls, not a lone regression.
  • Unusually deep appendix replication: 5 additional model families, a 7-target panel, N-sweeps, and ablations.
  • The OpinionQA application (describe TV=0.22, PPA's 21% gain) is shown on gpt-4o only; the 5-model replication covers just synthetic tasks, not the full survey pipeline.
  • Base-vs-instruct causal comparison is restricted to 8B models — matched pairs aren't available at larger scale.
  • Appendix Q's cross-family panel mixes model generations (e.g. gpt-5.4 vs. gpt-4o), conflating size and generation.
  • PPA's entropy-TV regression uses only N=20 items with an unstable out-of-sample R² (median 0.095 across 1000 held-out splits).
  • Scope is limited to "sampling-as-output" tasks; findings don't speak to per-token tasks like translation or factual recall.
04

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does describe's ~2.1× advantage and PPA's 21% gain replicate on Claude, Gemini, DeepSeek, or Llama-3.3-70B on real OpinionQA/Pew data, not just synthetic RNG tasks?
  2. Would a matched base-vs-instruct pair at 70B+ scale show the same magnitude of alignment-induced collapse, given within-family scaling is untested here?
  3. Can PPA's surface-perturbation approach combine with the describe pathway (e.g. ensembling multiple single-call descriptions) to push per-respondent accuracy closer to the KNOWS ceiling?
  4. Which specific alignment mechanism — reward-model overfitting, KL regularization toward a peaked reference policy, or instruction-data specifics — causes the degenerate sampling primitive?
  5. Since the model's self-generated "random" number is itself non-uniform, could an external true-RNG spliced into the chain-of-thought scaffold restore per-call sampling fidelity?
