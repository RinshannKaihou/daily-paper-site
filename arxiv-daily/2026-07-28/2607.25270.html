Where steering activations are read from — not how the vector is built — swings steering success from 0.198 to 0.476.

一句话总结：论文把"steering信号从哪来"这一常被当作预处理细节的选择，形式化为激活来源选择（源上下文 × 读出策略）。在 Llama-3.1-8B / Qwen2.5-7B / Gemma-2-9B 三个模型、20 个目标行为上系统对比发现：宏平均成功率最高的粗粒度条件是"仅指令提示 + 末token读出"（0.476），而最贴近常见"用目标行为文本均值构造向量"做法的"仅答案 + 序列均值"反而最弱（0.198）；作者提出的 tail subtraction 方法进一步把 Gemma 上 Diff-Mean 向量的成功率从 0.288 推到 0.863。

Where Steering Signals Come From: Activation Source Selection in Activation Steering ↗
Jiaran Ye, Lingxu Ran, Zijun Yao et al. · Peking University · Tsinghua University · BAAI · YiXin-AILab · 28 Jul 2026
activation steering representation engineering LLM interpretability execution-boundary states steering vector construction
Key Metric
0.288 → 0.863
Tail subtraction more than triples Diff-Mean steering success on Gemma-2-9B-IT (Table 7): from 0.288 positive-only, past the 0.601 negative-subtraction baseline, to 0.863 tail-subtracted.
PCA family shows the same pattern on Gemma: 0.613 → 0.769 → 0.862. Gains are smaller on Llama (0.168→0.546), tracking a weaker shared-tail cosine similarity (0.5548 vs. 0.77–0.82).
Why This Matters

The common recipe — averaging activations from answer text — is the weakest condition tested; tail subtraction is a simple, reusable fix for anyone building steering or refusal-direction vectors.

Critical Edge

Table 7's tail-subtraction gains are benchmarked only against baselines built from the paper's own boundary construction — not against the strongest coarse condition (prompt-only+last, 0.476) in the same measurement units.

Methodology

  1. Fix everything downstream. Standard additive steering (h̃ = h + αv) and five vector-construction rules (Mean, Diff-Mean, PCA, Diff-PCA, SAE) stay identical; only the activation source σ = (c, ρ) varies — the source-context text c and the readout policy ρ (last-token vs. sequence-mean).
  2. Coarse grid. Cross 3 source-text conditions (prompt-only, prompt+answer, answer-only) × 2 readouts, on 3 instruction-tuned models × 4 task families (entity, persona/style, reject, nonsense; 20 targets, 1,950 held-out questions/model), best-of-search per condition.
  3. Execution-boundary refinement. Read before the behavior is produced — instruction / ICL / hybrid boundary prompts, last-token — vs. "post-realization" states that already contain the generated answer.
  4. Untangle on relation completion. 16 relation-completion tasks cleanly separate "target appears in text" from "text captures execution," isolating why boundary sources win.
  5. Diagnose the mixed state. Project a boundary-derived "execution direction" out of answer-based vectors (Diff-PCA ablation) to test whether answer sources partly borrow execution signal.
  6. Tail subtraction. Subtract a matched tail-only prompt's activation (same local continuation, no target-conditioning) from the full boundary activation before building Mean/PCA vectors.

Mixed-state diagnostic (Table 5)

  • Projecting a boundary-derived "execution direction" out of answer-only mean vectors reduces their steering success on all 3 models (e.g. Gemma 0.281→0.208) — the answer-based vectors' partial effectiveness comes from a shared execution component, not target appearance alone.
  • The effect is larger for the stronger prompt+answer vectors (Gemma 0.521→0.305, a 21.6-point drop; Qwen 0.527→0.334), suggesting more execution signal leaks into richer answer-conditioned sources.

Strengths

  • Cleanly isolates an upstream design choice most prior steering papers bundle into "setup," holding the entire downstream pipeline fixed.
  • The relation-completion untangling experiment (Sec. 6.2) specifically rules out the confound between "target appears in source" and "source captures execution."
  • The mixed-state account is tested via direct projection-ablation (Table 5), not just asserted.
  • Tail subtraction is a single subtraction, yet delivers large, consistent gains across two vector families and three models, with a plausible cosine-similarity diagnostic explaining why Gemma/Qwen gain more than Llama.
  • Detailed appendix documentation of task inventory, prompt templates, and judge validation (87.0% human agreement) supports scrutiny.

Limitations

  • Scope limited to three 7–9B open-weight instruction-tuned models; base models, larger scales, multilingual/multimodal, and long-context steering are untested.
  • Only standard vector-construction methods and a single additive-steering intervention family are studied — no supervised probes, multi-vector bases, or subspace/projection interventions.
  • Reject and nonsense targets are synthetic, deliberately unnatural behaviors, not representative of deployable steering use cases.
  • Evaluation relies on an automatic LLM judge (Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct); reported scores are best-of-grid and may overstate a single deployment configuration.
  • No code, steering-vector, or dataset release evident, limiting independent verification of the exact numbers.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Would tail subtraction's gains persist for supervised probes, multi-vector bases, or non-additive interventions like subspace projection?
  • Is "execution-boundary beats post-realization" sensitive to the exact boundary-prompt template — icl+last was notably weaker than instruction/hybrid+last?
  • Does the mixed-state account generalize to frontier-scale models or safety-critical behaviors beyond the synthetic reject/nonsense targets?
  • Can tail subtraction combine with SAE-based feature selection to isolate even cleaner execution-boundary features?
  • What is the compute/data overhead of constructing matched tail-only prompts at scale, and does it generalize to open-ended styles or reasoning-chain steering that lack a clean boundary point?
