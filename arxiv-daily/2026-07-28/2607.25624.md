> **一句话总结：** 本文严格证明了正定二次网络 f_U(x)=x^T UU^T x 的欧氏参数梯度训练精确投影为秩-r 半正定矩阵商流形上的黎曼梯度流，给出闭式商 Hessian 和有限步离散化的精确修正项，并在可交换欠定测量条件下证明训练动态精确退化为熵镜像流；但论文自己的高斯矩阵感知实验显示，其证明的充分样本量（约 2.01×10^16）比实际可行的恢复样本量（n=152 即 100% 成功）保守了约 14 个数量级。

> **TL;DR:** This JMLR-style theory paper proves that ordinary Euclidean factor-gradient training of the two-layer positive quadratic network f_U(x)=x^T UU^T x projects *exactly* (not approximately) onto Riemannian gradient flow on the rank-r PSD manifold M_r^+ ≅ F_{d,r}/O(r), derives a closed-form quotient Hessian and exact finite-step discretization correction, and shows that under commuting rank-one Gaussian/underdetermined measurements the factor dynamics reduce exactly to an entropy mirror flow whose small-initialization limit is the minimum-trace interpolant. On its own synthetic Gaussian matrix-sensing verification experiment (d=10, r=2, 16 trials per sample size), the paper's proven end-to-end recovery theorem (Theorem 3.9) requires n ≈ 2.01×10^16 samples, yet empirical spectral-initialization-plus-gradient-descent recovery already reaches 100% success at n=152 (n/p=8) — a self-disclosed gap of roughly 14 orders of magnitude between the proven sufficient condition and observed practice.

## 核心贡献

- 证明满秩因子空间 F_{d,r} 在正交群作用下的商恰好等于秩-r 半正定矩阵流形 M_r^+（Theorem 2.1），并构造出使投影映射 π:U↦UU^T 成为黎曼淹没的商度量 g（Section 2.1-2.3）。
- 证明对任意光滑目标 L(U)=ℓ(UU^T)，普通欧氏因子梯度天然是"水平"的（无正交规范方向分量），因此欧氏梯度流精确投影为商黎曼梯度流（Theorem 2.3），有限步梯度下降则精确诱导出预测矩阵的合同递推 Q_{k+1}=(I-2ηG(Q_k))Q_k(I-2ηG(Q_k))，并显式给出 O(η²) 的离散化修正项（Proposition 2.6）。
- 在插值解处推导出闭式"商 Hessian"（Theorem 3.1），将其谱与高斯 rank-one 测量下的经验/总体正规算子联系起来，构造矩量谱初始化器并证明局部指数/线性收敛的端到端恢复定理（Theorem 3.9），但显式承认所需样本量 O(r²d⁸) 既非统计最优也不随商流形内蕴维数缩放（Remark 3.5）。
- 在可交换欠定测量的过参数化区域（r=d）证明因子梯度流精确退化为关于加权熵镜像映射的镜像流（Theorem 4.1），对任意严格正初始化收敛到 Bregman 投影（对应 Theorem 4.3），小初始化极限收敛到最小迹解集，非唯一时由加权熵准则唯一选出插值点（Theorem 4.5），并给出有限步长与连续 Bregman 投影之间 O(η) 的偏差上界（Theorem 4.7/4.8）。
- 用六组数值实验逐一验证上述每条定理，同时坦诚展示多处理论常数（恢复半径、样本量、oracle 步长、secant 收敛率）比实测表现保守数个数量级。

## Key Contributions

- Proves that the orthogonal-group quotient of the full-column-rank factor space F_{d,r} is exactly diffeomorphic to the rank-r PSD manifold M_r^+ (Theorem 2.1), and constructs the induced quotient Riemannian metric g under which π: U ↦ UU^T is a Riemannian submersion (Section 2.1–2.3).
- Shows that for any smooth objective L(U) = ℓ(UU^T), the ordinary Euclidean factor gradient is automatically horizontal (no infinitesimal gauge component), so Euclidean factor-gradient flow projects *exactly* onto the quotient Riemannian gradient flow (Theorem 2.3); finite-step gradient descent instead induces an exact congruence recursion on the predictor, Q_{k+1} = (I − 2ηG(Q_k))Q_k(I − 2ηG(Q_k)), with an explicit O(η²) discretization-correction term (Proposition 2.6).
- Derives a closed-form "quotient Hessian" at any interpolating predictor (Theorem 3.1), ties its generalized spectrum to the Gaussian rank-one measurement operator's population/empirical normal operator, builds a moment-based spectral initializer, and proves an end-to-end local-convergence recovery theorem (Theorem 3.9) — while explicitly stating the resulting O(r²d⁸) sample bound is not claimed to be statistically optimal or to scale with the manifold's intrinsic dimension (Remark 3.5).
- In the commuting, underdetermined (r = d) regime, proves that the factor gradient flow reduces exactly to an entropy mirror flow (Theorem 4.1) that converges, for any strictly positive initialization, to the corresponding Bregman projection (Theorem 4.3); shows the small-initialization limit approaches the minimum-trace interpolant with an O(1/log(1/ε²)) trace-gap bound (Theorem 4.4), with ties broken by a unique weighted-entropy criterion (Theorem 4.5); and bounds the finite-step selection error at O(η) relative to the continuous Bregman projection (Theorem 4.7/4.8).
- Validates every mechanism with six targeted numerical experiments (Section 5), while candidly reporting that several of the paper's own theoretical constants (basin radius, sample complexity, oracle step size, secant convergence rate) are conservative by factors ranging from ~4x to ~10^14x relative to observed behavior.

## Methodology

The paper studies the positive quadratic (tied-weight, quadratic-activation) two-layer network f_U(x) = ‖U^Tx‖₂² = x^TUU^Tx with U ∈ ℝ^{d×r}, whose predictor is the rank-r PSD matrix Q = UU^T. Because U is only identifiable up to right-multiplication by O(r), the authors first establish (Theorem 2.1) that on the full-column-rank stratum F_{d,r} := {U : rank(U)=r}, the fiber of U ↦ UU^T is exactly the orbit {UR : R ∈ O(r)}, giving a diffeomorphism F_{d,r}/O(r) ≅ M_r^+ (the rank-r PSD manifold, dimension dr − r(r−1)/2). They then construct the horizontal/vertical tangent-space splitting and the induced quotient metric g (Propositions 2.1–2.3), a Procrustes-distance characterization of the orbit-space metric via the nuclear norm (Proposition 2.5), and prove that for any smooth objective L(U) = ℓ(UU^T) the Euclidean factor gradient is horizontal, so ordinary factor-gradient flow projects exactly to Riemannian gradient flow on (M_r^+, g) (Theorem 2.3) and finite-step factor gradient descent induces an exact congruence recursion on Q with an explicit O(η²) discretization term (Proposition 2.6) — this is presented as an exact identity, not an approximation or a "retraction" assumption from Riemannian-optimization theory.

Section 3 specializes to Gaussian rank-one matrix sensing: x_i ~ N(0,I_d) iid, y_i = x_i^TQ_*x_i, target Q_* = U_*U_*^T ∈ M_r^+. The quotient Hessian at the interpolating solution equals the empirical measurement Gram form restricted to the tangent space (Theorem 3.1). For the Gaussian population operator T(H) = 2H + tr(H)I_d (Lemma 3.1, from Isserlis' identity), the authors prove a full-space (not tangent-space-restricted) operator deviation bound via a fourth-moment/Hilbert–Schmidt argument (Theorem 3.2: P(‖T_n−T‖_op > ε) ≤ κ_d/(nε²), κ_d = d(d+2)(d+4)(d+6)), construct a moment-based spectral initializer via top-r PSD truncation of M_n = (1/2n)Σy_i(x_ix_i^T − I_d), and chain together a deterministic local-secant regularity lemma, spectral-initialization basin-entry theorem, and local exponential/linear convergence theorems for gradient flow and gradient descent (with an "oracle" step size depending on the unknown λ_r(Q_*), λ_1(Q_*)) into one end-to-end sufficient recovery theorem (Theorem 3.9).

Section 4 studies the underdetermined regime (r = d) with pairwise-commuting measurement matrices A_1,...,A_n, which share a joint eigenbasis with blocks P_1,...,P_m of multiplicities d_a. Restricting to the invariant "joint spectral algebra" Q(q) = Σ q_aP_a, the factor gradient flow reduces exactly to a mirror flow under the entropy-like potential h(q) = (1/4)Σ d_a(q_a log q_a − q_a) (Theorem 4.1); for any fixed strictly positive initialization the flow converges to the Bregman projection onto the feasible set (Theorem 4.3, referenced via later sections). Isotropic small initialization q(0)=ε²𝟙 is shown to select the minimum-trace interpolant as ε↓0 (with an explicit O(1/log(1/ε²)) trace-gap bound, Theorem 4.4), with nonuniqueness resolved by a weighted-entropy tie-breaking criterion restricted to the invariant algebra (Theorem 4.5) — the authors explicitly do not claim this characterization extends to general noncommuting PSD interpolants. A separate finite-step theorem (Theorem 4.7/4.8) bounds the O(η) deviation of the discrete gradient-descent interpolant from the continuous Bregman projection for fixed initialization, and Corollary 4.2 clarifies that only the sequential limit order (η↓0 then ε↓0) is established, not a joint scaling law.

All six numerical experiments (Section 5) are implemented in PyTorch with double precision, integrating continuous dynamics via 4th-order Runge–Kutta and using the exact discrete recursion for finite-step experiments; none use real-world datasets — all data are synthetic Gaussian measurements or hand-constructed commuting measurement systems designed to isolate one theoretical mechanism per experiment.

## Results & Highlights

- **Exact quotient/predictor identities (Section 5.1, d=20, r=5, n=80):** across five orthogonally-equivalent factor initializations trained on identical data, the maximum relative discrepancy in predictor trajectories is 3.11×10⁻¹⁵ (floating-point roundoff floor), and the exact congruence-recursion residual peaks at 4.96×10⁻¹⁶; a log–log fit of the finite-step discretization correction versus the theoretical O(η) prediction gives an empirical slope of 0.9984.
- **Effective curvature predicts local training rate (Section 5.2, d=8, r=2, n=800):** across five target condition numbers (λ_r(Q_*) ∈ {1, 0.7, 0.5, 0.35, 0.25}), the ratio of the measured local gradient-flow decay rate to the theoretical smallest effective (quotient-Hessian) eigenvalue λ_min^eff stays within [0.999862, 1.000234] — i.e., within about 2.4×10⁻⁴ of exact agreement.
- **Spectral initialization vs. proven recovery basin (Section 5.3, d=10, r=2, 16 trials per setting, n/p ∈ {2,4,8,16,32} with p=19 intrinsic dimensions):** the theoretical recovery-basin hit rate (Theorem 3.4's certified region) is **0% at every tested sample size (n up to 608)**, while empirical end-to-end recovery rises from 12.5% at n=38 to 93.75% at n=76 to **100% at n≥152**. The paper's own explicit sufficient sample requirement (Theorem 3.9) is N_* ≈ 2.01×10^16 — a roughly 14-order-of-magnitude gap versus the largest tested n=608 and versus the empirically-100%-successful n=152.
- **Conditioning and oracle step size (Section 5.4, population operator, d=8, r=2, κ(Q_*) ∈ {1,2,4,8}):** the exact relation λ_min^eff = 4λ_r(Q_*) matches the measured gradient-flow decay rate to a ratio of ≈0.999978 across all four condition numbers, but the theorem's guaranteed rate constant α_* = λ_r(Q_*) is conservative by a factor of ≈4; sweeping step-size multipliers up to 10,000× the oracle bound remains numerically stable except for the worst-conditioned case (κ=8, η=10,000×η_oracle), where the iteration diverges.
- **Bregman/entropy/finite-step selection (Section 5.5-5.6):** two independently computed Bregman projections agree to ≈1.6–2.8×10⁻¹⁵, and gradient flow converges to that projection with error ≈1–2.9×10⁻¹⁰; in a hand-built 4-block minimum-trace example (τ_* = 1.3, certified by an explicit SDP dual), the observed trace gap at the smallest initialization (ε=0.005) is 1.086×10⁻³, safely under the theorem's O(1/log(1/ε²)) bound with constant C=4.243 (observed max 0.4625); the finite-step selection error scales as E_step(η) ∝ η^0.99094 (R²=0.999981), matching the proven O(η) rate almost exactly.

No comparison against external baseline algorithms or standard ML benchmarks is attempted — the paper's "results" are entirely internal theorem-vs-simulation checks on synthetic data, which is appropriate for its purely theoretical scope but means the numbers above should be read as self-consistency verification rather than competitive performance evidence.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** Moderate. Quotient geometry of fixed-rank PSD manifolds (Absil et al. 2008; Massart & Absil 2020; Meyer et al. 2011; Vandereycken 2013) and the mirror-descent view of implicit bias in matrix factorization (Gunasekar et al. 2017; Li et al. 2018, 2022; Wu & Rebeschini 2021; Woodworth et al. 2020) are both well-established literatures that the paper cites extensively. Its distinguishing contribution is deriving these connections *exactly from the actual trained Euclidean parametrization* of one specific model (f_U(x)=‖U^Tx‖₂²) rather than assuming an abstract "commuting parametrization" a priori — e.g., Theorem 2.3's claim that factor gradient flow projects *exactly* to Riemannian gradient flow (not merely approximately, via a retraction), and Theorem 4.1's derivation of the entropy mirror flow as a consequence of training dynamics rather than an assumption.

**Rigor:** High in terms of proof completeness — essentially every claim through Section 4 is given a full, explicit, checkable proof, which is unusual rigor for a paper in this space. The authors are also unusually disciplined about scope: Remarks 3.5, 3.8, and 4.9 explicitly disclaim that the sample bound is optimal, that the step-size condition is anything but an oracle condition, and that the small-initialization/finite-step limits commute in general. This kind of self-limiting honesty is commendable and rare.

**Reproducibility:** The six experiments in Section 5 are described with enough specificity (dimensions, trial counts, measurement construction, integrator) to be re-implemented, but no code or data release is mentioned or linked anywhere in the paper — an omission given that all of the paper's empirical claims come from these simulations.

**Significance / paper-specific red flag:** The paper's own Table 2 (Section 5.3) is the sharpest evidence against taking its headline recovery guarantee (Theorem 3.9) as practically informative: the theoretical sample requirement N_* ≈ 2.01×10^16 is checked against experiments that only go up to n=608, and 100% empirical recovery is already achieved at n=152 — fourteen orders of magnitude below the proven sufficient condition. The authors disclose this themselves rather than hiding it, which is honest, but it also means the "end-to-end recovery theorem" that anchors Section 3 is, by the paper's own numbers, essentially non-operative at any realistic sample size. Similarly, the O(r²d⁸) leading-order sample scaling (Remark 3.5) is explicitly acknowledged as not scaling with the manifold's intrinsic dimension dr − r(r−1)/2, and no numerical or theoretical comparison is offered against sharper existing matrix-sensing sample-complexity results (e.g., RIP-based analyses achieving near-intrinsic-dimension rates), leaving the reader without a sense of how far this specific bound is from the state of the art.

**Missing ablations:** There is no experiment varying d beyond ≤20 to see how quickly the gap between proven and empirical thresholds narrows or widens with dimension, and no exploration of measurement models beyond Gaussian rank-one (for Section 3) or hand-picked commuting families (for Section 4) — both restrictions are stated as intentional scope limits in the conclusion, but neither is stress-tested even qualitatively outside the tested regime.

## Strengths

- Every theorem in Sections 2–4 is proven in full and checked against closed-form or highly precise numerical verification (several results match to 4-5 significant digits or floating-point roundoff), giving unusually strong internal consistency.
- The central geometric claim — that ordinary Euclidean factor-gradient training projects *exactly* onto Riemannian gradient flow on the quotient manifold, with an explicit and bounded discretization correction for finite steps — closes a previously assumption-laden gap between abstract quotient-manifold theory and actual trained-network dynamics.
- Unusually transparent about the limits of its own guarantees: explicit remarks flag the sample bound as non-optimal, the step-size condition as an oracle condition, and the small-initialization/finite-step order-of-limits as sequential rather than joint, rather than allowing the reader to over-interpret the theorems.
- The six numerical experiments are well-targeted (one per theoretical mechanism) and, refreshingly, are used to expose the looseness of the paper's own bounds (basin radius, sample complexity, oracle step size, secant rate) rather than to showcase only favorable agreement.

## Limitations

- The proven end-to-end sample complexity (Theorem 3.9) scales as O(r²d⁸/δ · (‖Q_*‖_F/λ_r(Q_*))²) and, by the paper's own Section 5.3 experiment, is roughly 14 orders of magnitude more conservative than the empirically observed recovery threshold.
- The gradient-descent step-size condition (Theorem 3.7/3.9) is an oracle condition requiring knowledge of the unknown λ_r(Q_*) and λ_1(Q_*), so it is not directly usable as a practical learning-rate rule.
- The Bregman-projection, minimum-trace, and entropy-tie-breaking theory of Section 4 is proven only for pairwise-commuting measurement families sharing a joint eigenbasis — a special structured case of underdetermined matrix sensing, not general noncommuting measurements.
- All numerical experiments are at small scale (d ≤ 20), synthetic, and self-referential (theorem-vs-simulation checks), with no code or data release accompanying the paper.
- Editorial/production quality is uneven: unedited JMLR template placeholders ("Editor: My editor," "Author One and Author Two," a stray "remainder omitted in this sample" line before the references) remain in the manuscript.

## Relevance

本文为过参数化二次网络的隐式偏置和精确收敛速度提供了少见的完整闭式证明，对关注矩阵分解隐式正则化、镜像下降理论与黎曼优化基础的研究者有参考价值；但其证明范围严格限定于正定二次网络与可交换测量场景，且论文自曝的样本复杂度界比实测宽松约十四个数量级，直接套用于实际训练场景的指导意义有限。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Could restricting the empirical-operator concentration argument to the tangent space or a low-rank secant set (rather than the full ambient space 𝕊^d) close much of the ~10^14 gap between Theorem 3.9's proven sample bound and the observed n=152 recovery threshold?
- Does the entropy tie-breaking / minimum-trace selection theory of Section 4 extend, even approximately, to noncommuting (general RIP) measurement families, or does noncommutativity require a fundamentally different variational characterization?
- Can the oracle step-size condition (depending on unknown λ_r(Q_*), λ_1(Q_*)) be replaced with a practical, data-driven learning-rate rule (e.g., backtracking or a Polyak-type step) while retaining a comparable linear-convergence guarantee?
- How does the effective-curvature/local-training-rate correspondence (Section 5.2, matched to ~10⁻⁴ relative precision) generalize to deeper networks or non-quadratic activations where the exact quotient identification of Section 2 no longer holds?
- Would approximately-commuting measurement operators (a perturbation of Section 4's assumption) yield a perturbative extension of the Bregman/entropy selection theorem, bridging the commuting and fully general matrix-sensing regimes?
