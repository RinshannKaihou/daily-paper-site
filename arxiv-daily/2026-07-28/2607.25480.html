Data-Dependent Regret and Polyak Corrections for Constrained Online Convex Optimization

Wentao Zhang · Tsinghua Shenzhen International Graduate School · July 28, 2026
Key Finding

A tighter, data-dependent regret proof for the existing OGD-PFS algorithm — no algorithm change — cuts the bound 38–43%.

Key Metric
38–43%
tighter regret bound (Theorem 1) than the prior Hutchinson & Alizadeh (2025) analysis, across three synthetic constraint geometries — same algorithm, same assumptions.
Why This Matters

A reusable proof technique plus a computable, real-time "tightness" diagnostic for online decisions that must stay feasible every round — power grids, clinical dosing, autonomous control — though validated only on synthetic benchmarks so far.

Critical Edge

Of the 38–43% headline gain, 34–37 percentage points come from the gradient-refinement term (essentially AdaGrad-style data-dependence) — the paper's actual novel idea, the Polyak correction, contributes only 1.4–8.2% under normal conditions.

Methodology

The paper re-analyzes OGD-PFS (Hutchinson & Alizadeh, 2025) unchanged: each round the learner plays xt, takes an OGD step using the cost gradient, then applies a closed-form Polyak feasibility step — a first-order linearization of the constraint followed by a ball projection. This needs only one constraint evaluation and one subgradient per round, avoiding an exact projection oracle.

The contribution is purely proof-theoretic. Standard analyses throw away two things: the data-dependent gradient sum 𝍶T (collapsing it to the worst-case constant Gf) and the slack term δt available from the strong Pythagorean projection inequality (standard proofs use only the weaker non-expansiveness). By keeping both — proving an "enhanced" non-expansiveness lemma that retains δt — the paper threads a strictly non-negative improvement through the usual telescoping regret argument, then re-derives the same architecture for an AdaGrad-style adaptive step size (AdaOGD-PFS). No new or stronger assumptions are introduced.

Experiments: synthetic OCO instances, d=10, R=5, T=10,000, 5 seeds — ball constraint, heavy-tailed ball constraint, and halfspace constraint.

Strengths

  • Provably non-negative improvement over prior work with identical assumptions and identical feasibility guarantees — strictly better, never worse.
  • First explicit, measurable instantiation of the "Polyak correction" δt/𝍶T — a concrete, online-computable tightness diagnostic beyond the regret bound itself.
  • The adversarial ablation (Table 5) is a rigorous, well-designed check most tightening papers skip — it isolates the Polyak term from gradient concentration.
  • Transparent about where AdaOGD-PFS underperforms the oracle-tuned fixed-step baseline, rather than cherry-picking favorable comparisons.

Limitations

  • Purely synthetic, low-dimensional (d=10) experiments — no real-world benchmark despite power-grid and clinical-dosing being the paper's own motivating examples.
  • No comparison against other constrained-OCO families (long-term-constraint methods, projection-free/Frank-Wolfe) — only against the single prior bound.
  • 𝍶T and 𝍶T's Polyak term are post-hoc, trajectory-dependent quantities — an instance-dependent guarantee, not a new worst-case one, as the paper itself acknowledges.
  • AdaOGD-PFS's Theorem 2 bound is numerically looser than Theorem 1 in every reported experiment; its advantage is rate-form (no need to know Gf), not a demonstrated numerical win.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Can the strong-Pythagorean-slack technique port to mirror descent, online Newton step, and Frank-Wolfe-style methods, and would the improvement stay in the 30–45% range across geometries?
  2. How does the Polyak correction behave under multi-constraint settings (Dykstra-style alternating projections) — does cumulative slack decompose additively, or do cross-constraint interactions erode the gain?
  3. Would a "doubly Gf-free" doubling-trick schedule recover per-round feasibility for AdaOGD-PFS without any oracle knowledge of Gf, and at what cost to the regret constant?
  4. Given the gradient-refinement term dominates the observed improvement, how much of this result restates known AdaGrad-style data-dependence versus offering a genuinely new mechanism?
  5. What is AdaOGD-PFS's actual regret and feasibility behavior on real safety-critical control tasks, where Gf is unknown a priori and gradients are non-Gaussian, non-adversarial?
