> **一句话总结：** 本文发现 AdamW 优化阶段留下的一阶/二阶动量统计量可直接当作 SGHMC 采样阶段的对角质量矩阵（预条件子）"免费"复用，从而在 Bayesian Deep Ensemble 的两阶段流程中省去/缩短耗时的 scale-adaptation 预热，在 5 个数据集上平均将准确率/RMSE 相对 Deep Ensemble 提升 1.17%–1.31%，LPPD/困惑度提升 7.3%–10.1%，且比自适应的 AdaSGHMC 更稳健、更省算力。

> **TL;DR:** By reusing AdamW's terminal gradient/first-/second-moment statistics as the diagonal mass matrix for subsequent SGHMC sampling in a Bayesian Deep Ensemble pipeline, this AISTATS 2026 OPTIMAL workshop paper eliminates the costly scale-adaptation warmup and, on F-MNIST, achieves LPPD of -0.2102 (gradient-preconditioned, layer-wise) vs. -0.2318 for the Deep Ensemble baseline and -0.2197 for warmup-adapted AdaSGHMC — a +9.3% relative LPPD gain with zero extra sampling burn-in cost.

## 核心贡献

- 提出将 AdamW 训练结束时留下的统计量（终态梯度 $\mathbf{g}_T$、一阶动量 $\boldsymbol{\mu}_T$、二阶动量 $\boldsymbol{\nu}_T$）直接复用为 SGHMC 采样阶段对角质量矩阵 $\mathbf{M}$ 的四种构造方式（identity / gradient-based / 1st-moment / 2nd-moment），成本几乎为零（这些量本就是优化器状态的副产品）。
- 给出理论支撑（Proposition 3.1）：在模态附近局部二次近似的假设下，若 $\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{H}^*$（真实 Hessian），预条件化的 SGHMC 动力学等价于对目标做各向同性化变换，条件数为 1，局部混合最优；并将 AdamW 的二阶矩解释为对角 Fisher 信息矩阵（"Fisher for free"）的复用。
- 提出层级（layer-wise）粒度的预条件策略：将逐参数统计量在每层内取平均并跨层归一化，作为数值稳定性的正则化手段——实验证明逐参数粒度在多个数据集上出现数值不稳定（结果缺失），而层级粒度始终稳定。
- 在 5 个数据集（bikesharing、protein、F-MNIST、CIFAR-10、shakespeare）× 4 种架构（FCN、LeNet、ResNet-7、nanoGPT）上做了系统评估，覆盖回归、分类、语言建模三种任务类型，并对比了 Deep Ensemble 基线和自适应的 AdaSGHMC（带预热）。
- 通过余弦相似度分析揭示了一个反直觉现象：AdaSGHMC 的内部自适应会"覆写"任何初始几何信息，导致各链的质量矩阵高度趋同（相似度接近 1），破坏了集成多样性；而本文的静态优化器几何迁移保留了各模态的独特几何信息，这被认为是其泛化性能更优的原因。

## Key Contributions

- **Zero-cost preconditioner transfer**: proposes reusing AdamW's terminal gradient ($\mathbf{g}_T$), first-moment ($\boldsymbol{\mu}_T$), or second-moment ($\boldsymbol{\nu}_T$) statistics as the diagonal SGHMC mass matrix $\mathbf{M}$ for the sampling phase of a two-stage Bayesian Deep Ensemble (BDE) pipeline, at negligible additional cost since these statistics are already computed during optimization.
- **Theoretical justification (Prop. 3.1)**: shows that under a local quadratic approximation of the posterior around a mode, setting $\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{H}^*$ (the true Hessian) yields an isotropic transformed potential with unit condition number, i.e., optimal local mixing — motivating $\boldsymbol{\nu}_T$ (which approximates the diagonal Fisher Information Matrix, "Fisher for free") as a curvature proxy.
- **Layer-wise granularity for stability**: averages parameter-wise statistics within each layer and normalizes across layers (Eq. 5), acting as a regularizer that upper-bounds the maximum per-parameter effective step size; shown empirically to be far more numerically stable than raw parameter-wise preconditioning, which fails outright (missing/unstable results) on several dataset/method combinations (Tables 8–9).
- **Comprehensive empirical study**: evaluates across tabular regression (bikesharing, protein), image classification (F-MNIST, CIFAR-10), and character-level language modeling (a custom "modern-shakespeare" dataset, translated from tinyshakespeare via Gemini 2.5 Pro), using FCN, LeNet, a custom ResNet-7 (Filter Response Norm instead of BatchNorm), and a 6-layer/6-head nanoGPT — roughly 1,750 GPU-hours total.
- **Geometry-overwriting analysis**: uses cosine-similarity of mass matrices across ensemble chains to show that AdaSGHMC's scale-adaptation during warmup converges all chains to nearly identical preconditioners (destroying ensemble diversity), whereas static optimizer-derived preconditioning preserves per-mode geometric diversity — offered as an explanation for why the proposed static approach outperforms the seemingly more sophisticated adaptive baseline.

## Methodology

The paper targets the two-stage **Bayesian Deep Ensemble (BDE)** paradigm: Phase I runs standard optimization (AdamW) from multiple initializations to find distinct high-probability modes; Phase II runs independent SGHMC chains from each mode to sample the local posterior. Normally, Phase II requires a separate scale-adaptation warmup (e.g., AdaSGHMC) to learn a good preconditioner (mass matrix $\mathbf{M}$) from scratch, which is computationally expensive and treats the two phases as disjoint.

The core proposal is to treat the AdamW optimizer state as a free source of curvature information. AdamW maintains exponential moving averages $\boldsymbol{\mu}_t = \beta_1\boldsymbol{\mu}_{t-1}+(1-\beta_1)\mathbf{g}_{t-1}$ and $\boldsymbol{\nu}_t=\beta_2\boldsymbol{\nu}_{t-1}+(1-\beta_2)\mathbf{g}_{t-1}^2$. At the terminal step $T$, four diagonal mass-matrix constructions are compared: identity $\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{I}_d$ (baseline), gradient-based $\mathbf{M}=\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{g}_T^2+\epsilon\mathbf{1})$, first-moment $\mathbf{M}=\mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_T^2+\epsilon\mathbf{1})$, and second-moment $\mathbf{M}=\mathrm{diag}(\sqrt{\boldsymbol{\nu}_T}+\epsilon\mathbf{1})$. These differ in noise sensitivity and temporal smoothing: $\mathbf{g}_T$ is purely local/noisy, $\boldsymbol{\mu}_T$ smooths recent gradients, and $\boldsymbol{\nu}_T$ approximates the diagonal empirical Fisher Information Matrix with slower forgetting, providing a more global but less locally responsive curvature estimate.

Proposition 3.1 (proved in App. B.1 via a linear coordinate transform $\tilde{\boldsymbol{\theta}}=\mathbf{M}^{1/2}(\boldsymbol{\theta}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^*)$) formalizes that setting $\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{H}^*$ (the Hessian at the mode) makes the transformed potential isotropic with condition number 1, i.e., locally optimal mixing — under the idealized assumption of local quadraticity, which the authors acknowledge is an approximation for realistic ill-conditioned, multimodal BNN posteriors.

To address numerical instability from using per-parameter statistics directly (large dynamic range across a network), a **layer-wise** variant averages the parameter-wise mass values within each layer and normalizes across layers so the global step size $\eta$ upper-bounds the maximum per-parameter effective step (App. B.2, Eq. 5). All main-text results use this layer-wise granularity; a parameter-wise ablation is reported separately (App. D.7) and shown to be less robust.

Experiments use JAX + BlackJAX, building on the codebase of Kobialka et al. (2026), run on NVIDIA A100/RTX A6000 GPUs (~1,750 GPU-hours total). Sampler configurations use 8–10 parallel chains, thinning factor 100, isotropic normal priors, and a step-size sweep per dataset (Table 5); 3 seeds per configuration are used throughout.

## Results & Highlights

- **Predictive performance (Table 1a, layer-wise, best step size, averaged over 3 seeds)**: Averaged across all 5 datasets/tasks, relative improvement over the Deep Ensemble baseline is +1.17% for identity-preconditioned SGHMC (no warmup), +1.26% for gradient-based, +1.22% for 1st-moment, and **+1.31% for 2nd-moment preconditioning (best)**, compared to only **+0.50% for AdaSGHMC** (which uses a full scale-adaptation warmup). Concretely, on CIFAR-10 accuracy, Deep Ensemble = 0.9019, gradient-preconditioned SGHMC = 0.9155 (+1.51%), vs. AdaSGHMC = 0.9055 (+0.39%). On protein RMSE, Deep Ensemble = 0.6589, 2nd-moment SGHMC = 0.6470 (+1.81%, best), vs. AdaSGHMC = 0.6560 (+0.44%).
- **Uncertainty quantification (Table 1b)**: Gains are larger here — average improvement over Deep Ensemble is +7.30% (identity), **+10.1% for gradient-based (best)**, +9.29% (1st and 2nd moment), vs. only +3.81% for AdaSGHMC. On F-MNIST LPPD, Deep Ensemble = -0.2318, gradient-preconditioned SGHMC = -0.2102 (+9.34% relative improvement), vs. AdaSGHMC = -0.2197 (+5.25%). On shakespeare perplexity, Deep Ensemble = 4.3687, identity-preconditioned SGHMC = 4.2645 (+2.38%, best), vs. AdaSGHMC = 4.3382 (+0.70%).
- **Robustness to step size (Fig. 5, CIFAR-10)**: vanilla (identity) SGHMC degrades sharply outside a narrow step-size range (accuracy falling toward ~0.6 and LPPD toward ~-1.5 at the largest tested step size), while all optimizer-derived preconditioned variants remain stable (~0.9 accuracy, ~-0.25 LPPD) across the same wide range of step sizes.
- **Warmup/runtime tradeoff (Fig. 4, 6, App. D.1/D.3)**: gradient- and 1st-moment preconditioning reach peak performance immediately with **zero warmup**, ideal for budget-constrained settings, while 2nd-moment preconditioning needs a longer warmup to exploit its more global curvature information but eventually yields the best overall performance. For a typical BDE pipeline (5,000 warmup + 10,000 sampling steps), eliminating the warmup phase is reported to cut total computational budget by 33% while still improving performance.
- **Parameter-wise vs. layer-wise granularity (App. D.7, Tables 8–9)**: parameter-wise preconditioning produces missing/unstable results for gradient- and 1st-moment preconditioning on multiple dataset/metric combinations (e.g., protein RMSE, F-MNIST accuracy show "–" entries), empirically validating the layer-wise design choice used throughout the main results.
- **Geometry overwriting (App. D.5, Fig. 8–9)**: cosine-similarity analysis shows AdaSGHMC's adapted mass matrices become nearly identical across ensemble chains (self-similarity near 1, low variance) regardless of the initial preconditioning, while the static first-moment preconditioner preserves lower, more diverse cross-chain similarity — offered as the explanation for the static approach's superior generalization.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The central mechanism — reusing an adaptive optimizer's second-moment accumulator as an approximation to the diagonal Fisher Information Matrix — is not new in itself (the paper explicitly cites "Fisher for free," Li et al. 2025). The contribution here is applying and systematically evaluating this connection specifically to bridge the optimization→sampling transition within the BDE pipeline, which the authors correctly note has not been studied in this context before. The theoretical result (Prop. 3.1) is a fairly standard local-quadratic diagonalization argument, not a deep new theorem — its main value is didactic, formalizing an intuition rather than proving something surprising.

**Rigor**: Experiments are reasonably thorough for a workshop paper — 3 seeds, standard errors reported (Tables 6–9), a dedicated hyperparameter-tuning study for the warmstarting optimizer (Fig. 3), and an explicit acknowledgment that vanilla identity-preconditioned SGHMC is reported at its "optimistic" best-step-size setting (a favorable treatment of the simplest baseline), which is intellectually honest. However, the theoretical proposition assumes local quadraticity of the posterior around a mode — an assumption the paper itself elsewhere characterizes as unrealistic for deep, ill-conditioned, multimodal BNN posteriors — so the practical translation from theory to the empirical proxies ($\mathbf{g}_T$, $\boldsymbol{\mu}_T$, $\boldsymbol{\nu}_T$) is heuristic rather than rigorously derived.

**Reproducibility**: Good — full experimental grid (Tables 2–5), architecture specs (Table 3–4), optimizer pseudocode (App. E), and a public code repository are provided. One reproducibility wrinkle: the "shakespeare" benchmark is a custom-modified dataset ("modern-shakespeare"), translated from tinyshakespeare into modern English via Gemini 2.5 Pro specifically for this paper — a nonstandard variant not directly comparable to prior published shakespeare-based LM baselines, even though the modified dataset is released alongside the code.

**Significance and red flags**: The accuracy/RMSE gains over the Deep Ensemble baseline are modest (~1.2–1.3% average relative improvement), while the AdaSGHMC comparison (~0.5% average) is the paper's key selling point rather than raw magnitude of improvement. A notable weak point acknowledged by the authors themselves: **parameter-wise preconditioning — the more natural, finer-grained version of their core idea — fails with numerical instability on several dataset/metric combinations** (Tables 8–9 have missing entries for gradient/1st-moment SGHMC on protein RMSE and other cells), forcing the layer-wise averaging workaround; this suggests the core method is more fragile than the polished main-results table (which only shows the layer-wise variant) implies. The paper also only tests curvature statistics from AdamW; despite claiming the approach is "agnostic to the specific training algorithm," no other adaptive optimizer (e.g., Adahessian, Sophia, mentioned in related work) is empirically tested. Model scales are small (10k–10.8M parameters) relative to modern large-scale BNN or LLM settings, so scaling behavior is unverified.

## Strengths

- Essentially a "free lunch": no additional forward/backward passes are needed, since the reused statistics are byproducts of standard AdamW training already run in Phase I of BDE.
- Clean ablation structure isolating four distinct M constructions (identity/gradient/1st-moment/2nd-moment) and two granularities (layer-wise/parameter-wise), with results reported with standard errors over 3 seeds.
- Honest discussion of failure modes: explicitly reports where parameter-wise preconditioning becomes numerically unstable, and provides a plausible causal mechanism (geometry overwriting by AdaSGHMC's adaptation) rather than just reporting a win.
- Practical, directly actionable insight for BDE practitioners: use gradient/1st-moment preconditioning under tight compute budgets, 2nd-moment preconditioning when a longer sampling run is affordable.

## Limitations

- Theoretical justification (Prop. 3.1) relies on an idealized local quadratic/single-mode approximation that the paper itself acknowledges does not hold for realistic, ill-conditioned, multimodal deep network posteriors.
- Parameter-wise (non-layer-averaged) preconditioning is numerically unstable on multiple dataset/method combinations, indicating the underlying idea requires the layer-wise regularization crutch to work reliably.
- Only AdamW is tested as the source optimizer for curvature statistics, despite the claim of optimizer-agnosticism; no comparison to other second-moment/curvature-aware optimizers.
- Model and dataset scales remain modest (largest model 10.8M parameters); it's unclear whether the free-preconditioner benefit persists at LLM or vision-transformer scale.
- The custom "modern-shakespeare" dataset (machine-translated via Gemini 2.5 Pro) is a nonstandard benchmark, limiting direct comparability to other published results on the original tinyshakespeare corpus.
- Predictive-performance gains (accuracy/RMSE) are modest in absolute terms (~1–2%), with the more striking gains concentrated in uncertainty-quantification metrics (LPPD/perplexity).

## Relevance

该工作直接针对贝叶斯深度学习中采样效率与几何自适应这一核心痛点，提出的"优化器几何免费复用"思路简单、可落地，且与用户关注的不确定性量化和 SGMCMC/BDE 方向高度相关，适合作为轻量级、低成本改进现有贝叶斯集成流程的参考方法。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the benefit of optimizer-derived preconditioning persist when scaling to substantially larger models (e.g., LLM-scale transformers) or when using optimizers other than AdamW (e.g., Adahessian, Sophia, Lion)?
- Could the parameter-wise numerical instability be fixed with a more principled regularization (e.g., adaptive epsilon, gradient clipping on $\mathbf{M}$) rather than falling back to coarser layer-wise averaging?
- How does the "geometry overwriting" hypothesis for AdaSGHMC's underperformance generalize to other adaptive SGMCMC samplers, and could a hybrid scheme (static initialization + limited/damped adaptation) capture the benefits of both diversity-preservation and adaptivity?
- Would blending multiple statistics (e.g., a weighted combination of $\boldsymbol{\mu}_T$ and $\boldsymbol{\nu}_T$) outperform any single choice, given that the paper shows 1st-moment excels at zero warmup while 2nd-moment excels with longer warmup?
- How sensitive are the reported gains to the specific choice of AdamW hyperparameters ($\beta_1,\beta_2,\lambda,\alpha$) used during Phase I, beyond the single tuned configuration used throughout the paper?
