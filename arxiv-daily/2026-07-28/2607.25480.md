> **一句话总结：** 本文对已有的 OGD+Polyak可行性步（OGD-PFS）算法给出了更紧的、数据依赖的后验regret分析（无需改动算法），把worst-case梯度界 $G_f^2T$ 替换为实际观测的梯度累积 $\mathcal{G}_T$，并首次显式提取出非负的"Polyak修正项" $\mathcal{P}_T$ 以负号进入regret界，在合成实验中把regret上界收紧了38-43%；作者进一步提出自适应步长算法AdaOGD-PFS，达到 $O(\sqrt{\mathcal{G}_T})$ 的regret率且无需事先知道梯度Lipschitz常数 $G_f$。

> **TL;DR:** This paper re-analyzes the existing OGD-PFS (online gradient descent + Polyak feasibility step) algorithm for constrained OCO and proves a strictly tighter, data-dependent regret bound by retaining two quantities the standard worst-case proof discards — the observed gradient accumulation $\mathcal{G}_T=\sum_t\|\nabla f_t(x_t)\|^2$ (replacing $G_f^2T$) and a newly-named nonnegative "Polyak correction" $\mathcal{P}_T$ that enters with a negative sign. On synthetic Ball+Linear and Halfspace-constrained instances (T=10,000, d=10) this tightens the prior bound of Hutchinson & Alizadeh (2025) from 3,541/7,486/3,897 down to 2,089/4,650/2,239 (a 38–43% reduction), while the newly proposed adaptive-step algorithm AdaOGD-PFS achieves an $O(\sqrt{\mathcal{G}_T})$ rate without knowing $G_f$.

## 核心贡献

- **同一算法的更紧分析（Theorem 1）：** 不修改OGD-PFS算法本身，仅通过在证明中保留"强"（而非"弱"）Pythagorean投影不等式的松弛项 $\delta_t$，以及不将逐轮梯度范数上界替换为worst-case常数 $G_f$，就得到严格不劣于（且几乎总是优于）已有界的新regret界：$\text{Reg}_T \le \frac{2R^2}{\eta}+\frac{\eta}{2}\mathcal{G}_T-\frac{1}{2\eta}\mathcal{P}_T+\frac{G_f\rho}{\sigma}T$。
- **首次将"Polyak修正"实例化为可测量量：** 定义 $\delta_t=\|y_t-\Pi_{H_t}(y_t)\|^2$（Polyak可行性投影把中间迭代拉回可行方向的位移平方），累积和 $\mathcal{P}_T=\sum_t \delta_t \ge 0$；证明改进量 $\Delta_T=\frac{\eta}{2}(G_f^2T-\mathcal{G}_T)+\frac{1}{2\eta}\mathcal{P}_T\ge 0$，且仅在退化情形（所有梯度都取到最大范数且Polyak步从未生效）下为零。
- **自适应算法AdaOGD-PFS（Theorem 2）：** 用AdaGrad式步长 $\eta_t=c/\sqrt{S_t}$（$S_t$为历史梯度平方和）替代固定步长，保持Polyak可行性步不变，证明其regret为 $O(\sqrt{\mathcal{G}_T})$（可能远小于 $O(G_f\sqrt{T})$），且不需要预先知道 $G_f$。
- **实证验证：** 在Ball约束、Ball约束(重尾噪声)、Halfspace约束三类合成实例上验证两个新界始终是实际regret的有效上界，且比先前界收紧38-43%（Theorem 1）/31-41%（Theorem 2）；并通过对抗性构造实验将"梯度精化"和"Polyak修正"两个改进来源解耦，证明后者独立贡献可达49.9%的收紧。

## Key Contributions

- **Tighter analysis of the same algorithm (Theorem 1):** Without modifying OGD-PFS, retaining the slack $\delta_t$ from the *strong* (rather than weak) Pythagorean projection inequality, and not collapsing per-round gradient norms to the worst-case constant $G_f$, the paper derives a bound that is provably no worse than, and generically strictly tighter than, the prior state of the art: $\text{Reg}_T \le \frac{2R^2}{\eta}+\frac{\eta}{2}\mathcal{G}_T-\frac{1}{2\eta}\mathcal{P}_T+\frac{G_f\rho}{\sigma}T$.
- **First instantiation of the "Polyak correction" as a measurable quantity:** Defines $\delta_t=\|y_t-\Pi_{H_t}(y_t)\|^2$ (squared displacement the Polyak feasibility step contributes) and its cumulative sum $\mathcal{P}_T \ge 0$; proves the improvement decomposition $\Delta_T=\frac{\eta}{2}(G_f^2T-\mathcal{G}_T)+\frac{1}{2\eta}\mathcal{P}_T \ge 0$, vanishing only in the degenerate corner case where all gradients attain $G_f$ and the Polyak step is never active.
- **Adaptive algorithm AdaOGD-PFS (Theorem 2):** Replaces the fixed step size with an AdaGrad-style $\eta_t=c/\sqrt{S_t}$ while keeping the Polyak feasibility step unchanged, achieving $O(\sqrt{\mathcal{G}_T})$ regret (potentially much smaller than $O(G_f\sqrt{T})$) while preserving per-round feasibility and requiring no knowledge of $G_f$.
- **Empirical validation:** On Ball-constrained, heavy-tailed Ball-constrained, and Halfspace-constrained synthetic instances, both refined bounds are confirmed as valid upper bounds and tighten the prior bound by 38–43% (Theorem 1) / 31–41% (Theorem 2); an adversarial worst-case-gradient experiment isolates the two improvement sources and shows the Polyak-correction term alone can account for 49.9% tightening.

## Methodology

The paper studies the existing **OGD-PFS** algorithm (Hutchinson & Alizadeh, 2025) for constrained OCO: at each round the learner plays $x_t$, observes cost gradient $\nabla f_t(x_t)$, queries only first-order constraint feedback $(g(x_t), s_t\in\partial g(x_t))$ — no full projection oracle — takes an OGD step $y_t = x_t - \eta\nabla f_t(x_t)$, and then applies a **Polyak feasibility step**: a closed-form projection of $y_t$ onto the half-space $H_t=\{x: g(x_t)+s_t^\top(x-x_t)+\rho\le 0\}$ (a first-order linearization of the constraint), followed by projection onto the bounding ball $R\mathcal{B}$. This costs only one constraint evaluation and one subgradient per round, avoiding the expensive exact projection $\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}$ required by classical Zinkevich-style OGD.

The analytical innovation is purely proof-theoretic, not algorithmic. Standard OGD-PFS analyses use two "coarse relaxations": (1) they upper-bound every per-round gradient norm $\|\nabla f_t(x_t)\|^2$ by the worst-case constant $G_f^2$, discarding the data-dependent sum $\mathcal{G}_T=\sum_t\|\nabla f_t(x_t)\|^2 \le G_f^2 T$; and (2) they invoke the *weak* non-expansiveness inequality of projection ($\|x_{t+1}-x^\star\|^2 \le \|y_t-x^\star\|^2$), discarding the slack term $\delta_t = \|y_t - \Pi_{H_t}(y_t)\|^2$ that the *strong* Pythagorean projection inequality (Bauschke & Combettes, 2017, Cor. 4.10) makes available. The paper's Lemma 1 proves an "enhanced" non-expansiveness property $\|x_{t+1}-x\|^2 \le \|y_t-x\|^2-\delta_t$ for any $x$ in the shrunk feasible set, and threads this $\delta_t$ (rather than discarding it) through the standard telescoping regret argument, together with keeping $\|\nabla f_t(x_t)\|^2$ un-relaxed. Five supporting lemmas (enhanced projection property, constraint error bound, geometric contraction of the Polyak step, feasibility recursion, and its expansion) build up Theorem 1's regret and feasibility guarantees; the same architecture is adapted via Abel summation to the time-varying step size of AdaOGD-PFS for Theorem 2. Four standard assumptions (bounded action set, bounded cost gradients, bounded constraint subgradients, non-vanishing constraint subgradient near the boundary/Slater condition) are carried over unchanged from prior work — the paper explicitly emphasizes it introduces no new or stronger assumptions, so any improvement is attributable solely to tighter proof technique.

Experiments use synthetic OCO instances with $d=10$, $R=5$, $T=10{,}000$: (1) a ball constraint $g(x)=\|x\|^2/r^2-1$ with $r=2$ and Gaussian-perturbed linear costs; (2) the same ball constraint with heavy-tailed perturbations ($\mathcal{N}(0,1.44I_d)$) to inflate the worst-case constant $G_f$; and (3) a halfspace constraint $g(x)=e_1^\top x-2$ with linear costs whose mean component continuously pushes the sequence toward the feasibility boundary. Both OGD-PFS (with $\eta=2R/(G_f\sqrt{T})$, $\rho=0$) and AdaOGD-PFS (with $c=R\sqrt2$, $\epsilon_0=G_f^2$, $\rho=0$) are run for 5 seeds each; $G_f$ is set to the tightest empirically achievable value per run (the max observed gradient norm along the trajectory) to avoid inflating the reported improvement through an artificially loose worst-case constant.

## Results & Highlights

- **Regret bound tightening (Table 3, T=10,000, d=10, 5-seed means):** On the Ball+Linear instance, the prior bound (Hutchinson & Alizadeh, 2025) is **3,541**, while the paper's Theorem 1 bound is **2,089** (41.0% reduction) and the AdaOGD-PFS Theorem 2 bound is **2,150** (39.3% reduction); the actual observed regret of OGD-PFS is only 235, confirming both refined bounds remain valid (if still loose) upper bounds.
- **Consistent gains across constraint geometries:** On the heavy-tailed Ball+Linear instance the prior bound of **7,486** drops to **4,650** (Theorem 1, 37.9% reduction) / **5,164** (Theorem 2, 31.0%); on the Halfspace instance the prior bound of **3,897** drops to **2,239** (Theorem 1, 42.5%) / **2,295** (Theorem 2, 41.1%) — the paper reports an overall range of 38–43% (Theorem 1) and 31–41% (Theorem 2) improvement over the Hutchinson & Alizadeh (2025) baseline.
- **Decomposed improvement sources (Table 4):** The gradient-refinement term ($\mathcal{G}_T$ vs. $G_f^2T$) contributes the majority of the gain — 35.9±1.2% (Ball+Linear), 36.5±1.2% (Ball+Linear HT), 34.3±1.0% (Halfspace) — while the Polyak correction contributes a smaller but always-positive 1.4–8.2%, largest on the Halfspace problem where the constraint is active in 99.4% of rounds.
- **Adversarial ablation isolating the Polyak term (Table 5):** Under an adversarial cost sequence where $\nabla f_t(x_t)=G_f e_1$ for every $t$ (so $\mathcal{G}_T=G_f^2T$ exactly and the gradient-refinement contribution is **0.0%** by construction), the Polyak correction alone still tightens the bound by **49.9%** (vs. 5.0% under the paper's default Gaussian setup) — a paper-specific rigor check showing the Polyak term is not merely riding on gradient concentration and is structurally independent of any AdaGrad-style adaptation.
- **AdaOGD-PFS vs. fixed-step-size OGD-PFS actual regret (Appendix B.1):** On the Halfspace problem, AdaOGD-PFS achieves *lower* actual regret than fixed-step OGD-PFS (148 vs. 173), while on the Ball problems fixed-step OGD-PFS wins (235 vs. 373 for Ball+Linear; not stated for HT beyond 1,173 for AdaOGD-PFS) — expected since the fixed step is tuned with oracle knowledge of $G_f$ whereas AdaOGD-PFS must learn the scale online.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core insight — that the standard weak non-expansiveness step in projected-OGD analyses discards a strictly non-negative slack term available from the strong Pythagorean inequality, and that this slack is exactly the geometric quantity the Polyak feasibility step is designed to produce — is a genuine, if narrow, technical contribution. It is a "free lunch" tightening: no new algorithm, no new assumptions, same feasibility guarantee, provably non-negative improvement. This is intellectually honest positioning (Remark 1 explicitly disclaims any assumption strengthening), which is commendable.

**Rigor:** The proof architecture (five lemmas building geometric contraction, feasibility recursion, and telescoping) is standard OCO machinery, and the paper is careful to show the improvement decomposition $\Delta_T \ge 0$ is provable, not just empirically observed. The adversarial ablation in Table 5 (forcing $\mathcal{G}_T=G_f^2T$ exactly to isolate the Polyak-correction contribution) is a genuinely well-designed rigor check that most tightening papers would skip — it demonstrates the Polyak term's contribution is not an artifact of gradient concentration.

**Reproducibility:** No code release is mentioned; the experimental setup (d=10, R=5, T=10,000, 3 specific synthetic instances, 5 seeds) is described with enough parameter detail to reproduce in principle, but the absence of a public implementation is a gap given the paper is empirically validating a theoretical claim.

**Red flags / weaknesses specific to this paper:**
- The AdaOGD-PFS bound (Theorem 2) is numerically *looser* than the fixed-step Theorem 1 bound in every single reported experiment (2,150 > 2,089; 5,164 > 4,650; 2,295 > 2,239) — the paper is upfront about this (Section 7.2: "the intended advantage of AdaOGD-PFS is therefore not to dominate Theorem 1 numerically when $G_f$ is known and oracle-tuned"), but it does mean the headline "adaptive algorithm" contribution is a rate-form advantage ($O(\sqrt{\mathcal{G}_T})$ without knowing $G_f$) rather than a demonstrated numerical win, and readers should not conflate the two.
- All three experimental instances share the same dimension (d=10), horizon (T=10,000), and only two constraint geometries (ball, halfspace) are tested — no real-world dataset, no comparison against other constrained-OCO baselines from the related-work section (Mahdavi et al. 2012, Jenatton et al. 2016, Yu & Neely 2020, Liakopoulos et al. 2019) beyond the single "prior" bound of Hutchinson & Alizadeh (2025).
- The actual regret in every experiment (173–643) is roughly an order of magnitude below even the tightened bounds (2,089–4,650), which is typical for worst-case-style OCO analysis but underscores that "38–43% tighter" is a relative statement about a bound that remains loose in absolute terms.
- The Polyak-correction contribution is small (1.4–8.2%) under the paper's own default (non-adversarial) experimental setup; its largest showcased value (49.9%) only appears under a specially constructed adversarial sequence, which is honestly disclosed but means the headline 38–43% number is driven predominantly (34–37 percentage points) by the gradient-refinement term, which is conceptually closer to "well-known AdaGrad-style data-dependence" than to the paper's more novel Polyak-correction idea.

**Significance:** This is a solid, narrowly-scoped theory contribution to a specific corner of constrained OCO (Polyak-feasibility-step analysis). It is unlikely to be highly cited broadly but is a clean, technically honest tightening that other OCO analyses in the same family (mirror descent, online Newton step, multi-constraint Polyak steps, as flagged in the Conclusion) could plausibly reuse.

## Strengths

- Provably non-negative improvement over prior work with identical assumptions and identical feasibility guarantees — a clean "strictly better, never worse" result.
- Explicit, named instantiation of the Polyak-correction slack $\delta_t$/$\mathcal{P}_T$ as a measurable, online-computable quantity, opening a concrete diagnostic (real-time tightness certificate, over-cautious/over-aggressive tuning signal) beyond the immediate regret bound.
- The adversarial ablation experiment (Table 5) is a rigorous, well-designed check that isolates and validates the Polyak-correction contribution independent of gradient concentration.
- Transparent reporting of where the new adaptive algorithm underperforms the oracle-tuned fixed-step baseline, rather than cherry-picking favorable comparisons.

## Limitations

- Purely synthetic, low-dimensional (d=10) experimental validation; no real-world constrained decision-making benchmark (e.g., power systems, clinical dosing) despite these being the paper's own motivating examples.
- No empirical comparison against other constrained-OCO families (long-term-constraint methods, projection-free/Frank-Wolfe variants) — only against the single most directly related prior bound.
- The refined bound $\mathcal{P}_T$/$\mathcal{G}_T$ are post-hoc, trajectory-dependent quantities, so the guarantee is instance-dependent rather than a new worst-case guarantee; the paper itself acknowledges this as a limitation.
- No integration with optimistic OGD or extension to bandit/delayed-feedback settings is attempted, despite being discussed as natural future directions; multi-constraint or time-varying-constraint extensions are also left open.

## Relevance

对于关注在线凸优化(OCO)、regret理论分析、约束满足与自适应步长设计的研究者，本文提供了一个可复用的分析技巧（保留强Pythagorean不等式的松弛项）以及一个具体、可在线计算的"Polyak修正"诊断量，可能对安全关键决策系统（电网、自动驾驶、临床决策）中需要逐轮可行性保证的场景有参考价值，但目前仅停留在合成基准的理论验证阶段。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the strong-Pythagorean-slack technique be systematically ported to mirror descent, online Newton step, and Frank-Wolfe-style projection-free methods, as the Conclusion speculates, and would the resulting improvement magnitudes remain in the same 30–45% range or vary substantially by geometry?
- How does the Polyak correction $\mathcal{P}_T$ behave under multi-constraint settings (Dykstra-style alternating projections) — does the "cumulative slack" decompose additively per constraint, or do cross-constraint interactions erode the improvement?
- Would a "doubly $G_f$-free" schedule (e.g., a doubling trick on an online estimate of $G_f$, flagged as future work in Remark 3) recover per-round feasibility for AdaOGD-PFS without any oracle knowledge of $G_f$, and at what cost to the regret constant?
- Given that the gradient-refinement term dominates the observed improvement (34–37 of the 38–43 percentage points), how much of this result is simply restating known AdaGrad-style data-dependence in the constrained setting versus offering a genuinely new theoretical mechanism?
- What is the actual (not bound) regret and feasibility behavior of AdaOGD-PFS on real safety-critical control tasks, where $G_f$ is typically unknown a priori and the paper's motivating claim (no need to know $G_f$) would be tested under realistic non-Gaussian, non-adversarial gradient distributions?
