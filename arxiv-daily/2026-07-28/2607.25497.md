> **一句话总结：** 论文指出 PathoROB 的 Robustness Index（RI）只在样本的固定 k 近邻中"数数"，因而在 Camelyon 上只有 9.6%–46.3% 的样本真正参与打分，据此提出逐样本、无需近邻规模调参的 Cross-confounder Robustness Margin（CRoMa）；在 20 个 tile 级和 4 个 slide 级病理基础模型上，CRoMa 的模型排名与 RI 高度一致（Camelyon 上 ρ=0.95），却揭示出即便中位数偏向生物信号（如 Camelyon 上最好的 Virchow2 中位 CRoMa=0.20），所有 20 个模型的最差十分位都仍是"混杂因子主导"（LTM₁₀<0），并且 CRoMa 越高的模型在下游线性探针实验中因虚假的中心-标签相关性造成的性能下降越小（跨 60 个模型-数据集对，池化 Spearman ρ=0.84 in-domain / 0.78 out-of-domain）。

> **TL;DR:** The paper argues that PathoROB's Robustness Index (RI) — which counts same-vs-cross-confounder neighbours inside a fixed k-nearest-neighbour window — silently drops most samples from scoring (only 9.6%–46.3% of Camelyon tiles have any informative neighbour within the operating k=11, versus 67–100% support on Tolkach-ESCA/TCGA-4x4), and that its distance-weighted fix (MaRI) barely changes rankings because pooling cancels opposing per-sample asymmetries. It introduces CRoMa, a per-sample signed margin between distances to nearest cross-confounder biological matches and nearest same-confounder distractors, defined for every sample regardless of local neighbourhood composition. Across 20 tile-level and 4 slide-level pathology encoders on four benchmarks (Camelyon, TCGA-4x4, Tolkach-ESCA, PCaBiop), CRoMa rankings track RI/MaRI closely where those are defined (Camelyon ρ=0.95 vs RI, 0.94 vs MaRI) but reveal that every single tile encoder — including the Camelyon leader Virchow2 (median CRoMa=0.20) — retains a confounder-dominated worst decile (LTM₁₀ < 0), and that higher CRoMa correlates with smaller shortcut-induced linear-probe accuracy drops under a Cramér's-V-controlled bias protocol (pooled Spearman ρ=0.84 in-domain, 0.78 out-of-domain over 60 model-benchmark pairs).

## 核心贡献

- 系统剖析 PathoROB 的 Robustness Index（RI）三个结构性缺陷：(1) 只计数不计距离，无法区分"紧邻锚点的同生物异中心邻居"与"边缘处的同类邻居"；(2) 池化为单一分数，掩盖了样本级异质性；(3) 只在预设固定 k 近邻里包含目标类型邻居才有定义，导致不同模型实际参评的子集不同、难以跨模型比较。
- 提出距离加权的中间方案 MaRI（Margin-aware Robustness Index），用指数衰减权重 exp(-d/τ) 对 RI 的邻居计数加权，证明它虽然引入了几何信息，但仍继承了 RI 的"支持集"问题（同一固定 k 窗口内若无类型化邻居则被静默剔除）。
- 提出核心方法 Cross-confounder Robustness Margin（CRoMa）：对每个样本直接搜索最近的 m 个跨混杂因子生物匹配（SO）邻居与最近的 m 个同混杂因子生物干扰（OS）邻居，计算两者平均余弦距离的有符号比值 margin，取值范围 (-1,1)，不依赖任何邻居规模调参，且对每个样本都有定义（只要该混杂因子和生物类别在数据集中共现次数≥m）。
- 提出用中位数（典型鲁棒性）、F(0)（混杂主导样本比例）、LTM₁₀（最差十分位均值，尾部严重度）三个互补指标刻画整个鲁棒性分布，并将模型选择框定为"中位数 vs 尾部严重度"的帕累托权衡问题，而非单一分数排序。
- 在 20 个 tile 级编码器 + 1 个自然图像对照（DINOv2-B）+ 4 个 slide 级编码器、共 4 个基准（Camelyon、TCGA-4x4、Tolkach-ESCA、新构建的 PCaBiop）上做大规模评测，并复现 PathoROB 的下游 shortcut-learning 探针实验（渐进注入中心-标签虚假相关性、评估平衡测试集准确率下降 APD），验证 CRoMa 能预测下游 shortcut 易感性；开源实现为 Python 包 `croma`。

## Key Contributions

- Diagnoses three structural limitations of PathoROB's Robustness Index (RI): it is count-based (blind to *where* typed neighbours sit relative to the anchor), pooled into a single score (erasing sample-level heterogeneity), and defined only for anchors whose fixed-k neighbourhood happens to contain a typed (SO/OS) neighbour — so different models are silently evaluated on different, model-dependent subsets.
- Introduces MaRI (Margin-aware Robustness Index), a minimal distance-weighted refinement of RI using exponential weights exp(-d/τ), and shows empirically that it changes rankings only marginally (Δ = MaRI − RI within ±0.07 on Camelyon) because the pooled design lets opposing per-sample distance asymmetries cancel — the correction is coherent but "rides on a thin, model-specific slice of the data."
- Proposes CRoMa (Cross-confounder Robustness Margin): a per-sample, scale-free signed margin CRoMa_m(i) = (d^OS_m − d^SO_m)/(d^OS_m + d^SO_m) between mean distances to the m nearest cross-confounder biological matches (SO) and m nearest same-confounder biological distractors (OS), requiring no neighbourhood-size tuning and defined for every eligible sample (fixed m=5 throughout, shown stable across a sweep of m and of the tail fraction α).
- Proposes summarizing the resulting per-sample distribution along three axes — median CRoMa (central tendency), F(0) (prevalence of confounder-dominant samples), and LTM₁₀ (severity of the worst decile) — reframing model selection as a Pareto trade-off between typical robustness and tail robustness rather than a single ranking.
- Runs a large multi-benchmark evaluation (20 tile-level + 1 natural-image control + 4 slide-level pathology encoders on Camelyon, TCGA-4x4, Tolkach-ESCA, and a newly curated PCaBiop/PCaBiop-ISUP prostate benchmark), and links CRoMa to downstream shortcut-learning susceptibility via linear-probe experiments with Cramér's-V-controlled confounder-label correlation; releases an open-source package `croma`.

## Methodology

CRoMa builds on the same typed-neighbour scaffold as PathoROB/RI: each sample has a feature vector z_i (L2-normalized, cosine distance), a biological label y_i, a confounder label c_i (acquisition centre), and a grouping id g_i (WSI or case) used to exclude same-unit near-duplicates from candidate neighbourhoods. For an anchor i and neighbour j, pairs are typed into four categories following [de Jong et al. 2025 / PathoROB]: **SO** (same biology, other confounder — cross-confounder biological match), **OS** (other biology, same confounder — same-confounder distractor), plus uninformative **SS** and **OO** pairs that are excluded from scoring.

- **RI**: for a fixed neighbourhood size k (chosen per model to maximize biological k-NN balanced accuracy, then evaluated at the cross-model median k*), RI = ΣSO_i / Σ(SO_i + OS_i) pooled over anchors, ranging 0 (confounder-dominated) to 1 (biology-dominated).
- **MaRI**: the same pooled ratio but with each typed neighbour weighted by wij = exp(−d_ij/τ) (τ set per model to the median SO/OS distance), so distance information modulates the count.
- **CRoMa**: for each sample, find the m=5 nearest SO neighbours and m nearest OS neighbours (searching outward, not restricted to a fixed k-window), compute their mean cosine distances d^SO_m(i), d^OS_m(i), and take the signed, scale-free margin CRoMa_m(i) = (d^OS_m(i) − d^SO_m(i)) / (d^OS_m(i) + d^SO_m(i)) ∈ (−1,1). Because this depends only on the ratio r_i = d^OS_m/d^SO_m, it is invariant to any global rescaling of a model's embedding distances — the paper shows this matters in practice, since median typed-distance radii span a factor of 80 across the four slide-level encoders on PCaBiop. Averaging the resulting margins gives the median CRoMa; the lower tail is summarized by F(0) = P[CRoMa_m(i) < 0] (failure prevalence) and LTM_α = E[CRoMa_m(i) | CRoMa_m(i) ≤ Q_α] at α=0.10 (failure severity, the mean of the worst decile).

Four benchmarks pair a biological label with an acquisition-centre confounder: Camelyon (breast lymph-node tumour/normal, 2 centres, 20,400 tiles, from PathoROB), TCGA-4x4 (4 cancer types × 4 centres, 5,760 tiles, from PathoROB), Tolkach-ESCA (6 oesophageal tissue classes × 3 cohorts, 9,000 tiles, from PathoROB), and PCaBiop, a new slide-level benchmark of 1,000 H&E prostate biopsies (benign/cancer × 2 centres) curated from the PANDA challenge, plus a finer-grained PCaBiop-ISUP variant (6 ISUP grades × 2 centres, 3,000 slides). Models are evaluated strictly as frozen feature extractors with no task-specific adaptation: 20 tile-level encoders (Virchow, Virchow2, UNI, UNI2-h, CONCH, CONCHv1.5, H-optimus-0/1, H0-mini, Prov-GigaPath, Midnight-12k, Prost40M, Phikon, Phikon-v2, Hibou-B/L, mSTAR, GPFM, MUSK, GenBio-PathFM) plus a natural-image control (DINOv2-B), and 4 slide-level encoders (PRISM, TITAN, Prov-GigaPath, MOOZY). Downstream shortcut susceptibility is measured via the PathoROB protocol: linear probes trained on frozen embeddings with training splits progressively biased toward a spurious confounder-biology correlation (quantified by Cramér's V from 0 to 1), evaluated on balanced test sets both in-domain (held-out centres seen in training) and out-of-domain (unseen centres); APD averages the relative accuracy drop across biased splits relative to the unbiased baseline.

## Results & Highlights

- **RI/MaRI support gap on Camelyon**: only 9.6%–46.3% of samples across the 20 tile encoders effectively contribute to the pooled RI/MaRI score at the shared operating point k=11 (Table 3), because the fixed-k neighbourhood selected to maximize biological k-NN accuracy is dominated by same-biology/same-confounder (SS) neighbours (86–93% of the window) — pooled across all 20 models, the median rank at which even the *first* SO or OS neighbour appears is ≈149 among 20,400 candidates, far beyond k=11. On TCGA-4x4 and Tolkach-ESCA, where the selected k is larger (71 and 61), support rises to 99–100% and 67–100% respectively.
- **RI vs MaRI near-equivalence is a pooling artifact, not evidence that distance doesn't matter**: on Camelyon, Δ = MaRI − RI stays within ±0.07 for every one of 20 encoders and model rankings barely shift (Spearman ρ=0.99 between RI and MaRI, Table 9); the same near-equivalence holds even on datasets with much higher support (Δ within ±0.04 on TCGA-4x4 and Tolkach-ESCA, ρ=0.99/0.98), ruling out "thin support" as the explanation and confirming the limitation is structural to the pooled, fixed-k design.
- **CRoMa vs RI/MaRI ranking agreement**: where RI/MaRI are defined, CRoMa's model ranking agrees closely — Camelyon: ρ=0.95 vs RI, 0.94 vs MaRI; TCGA-4x4: ρ=0.95 vs RI, 0.95 vs MaRI; Tolkach-ESCA: ρ=0.93 vs RI, 0.92 vs MaRI (Table 9) — but the metrics are "not interchangeable": e.g. H-optimus-1 and Virchow sit close under MaRI (0.677 vs 0.708) yet separate clearly under CRoMa (0.08 vs 0.16), while UNI2-h and Prov-GigaPath are nearly identical under CRoMa (0.04 vs 0.01) but differ substantially under MaRI (0.548 vs 0.369).
- **No encoder is uniformly robust**: on Camelyon (Table 3), median CRoMa ranges from 0.20 (Virchow2, the leader, tied with CONCH) down to −0.44 (Hibou-L, the weakest), but every one of the 20 tile encoders has LTM₁₀ < 0 — even Virchow2's worst decile averages −0.11 and GenBio-PathFM's −0.07 (the mildest tail on Camelyon). Failure prevalence F(0) varies from 12.9% (Virchow2) to 99.3% (Hibou-L), and models with the same median can differ sharply in tail behaviour (e.g. CONCH matches Virchow2's median 0.20 but fails more often, F(0)=22.5% vs 12.9%, and more severely, LTM₁₀=−0.20 vs −0.11).
- **CRoMa predicts downstream shortcut susceptibility**: pooled across 60 model-benchmark pairs, CRoMa correlates with the in-domain accuracy-drop metric APD_ID at Spearman ρ=0.84 and with the out-of-domain APD_OOD at ρ=0.78 (Table 11); per-benchmark values reach ρ=0.88 (Camelyon in-domain), 0.93 (TCGA-4x4 in-domain), 0.89 (Tolkach-ESCA in-domain), weakening out-of-domain (0.71, 0.87, 0.71) as expected since OOD APD conflates shortcut reliance with transfer to unseen confounder labels. RI and MaRI track APD comparably well (pooled ρ=0.89/0.89 in-domain), i.e. CRoMa provides this signal without repeated supervised retraining.
- **Cross-benchmark Pareto frontier**: aggregating median-CRoMa and LTM₁₀ ranks across the three tile-level benchmarks, only 4 of 20 encoders remain undominated — CONCH, GenBio-PathFM, CONCHv1.5, and H-optimus-1 (Figure 5) — and none leads on both axes simultaneously. GenBio-PathFM is highlighted as uniquely consistent, sitting on the median-tail Pareto frontier in all three tile benchmarks while ranking top-4 by median CRoMa in each.
- **Pretraining overlap inflates but does not fully explain robustness gains**: Midnight-12k, pretrained exclusively on TCGA, tops TCGA-4x4 (median CRoMa=0.40 vs runner-up GenBio-PathFM's 0.16) but ranks only 7th on Camelyon (0.11); a supplementary provenance check (adding held-out TCGA cases to Tolkach-ESCA) shows Midnight-12k gains the most from TCGA exposure (2.5× boost in typed-distance odds) but every encoder gains somewhat (even the natural-image control DINOv2-B gains 1.21×), and Phikon (also TCGA-pretrained) shows only a 1.35× boost, indicating pretraining overlap amplifies but does not create the robustness signal.
- **Slide-level results (PCaBiop)**: only PRISM shows biology-dominant median geometry (CRoMa=0.26); MOOZY attains the highest biological k-NN balanced accuracy (0.971) yet has a slightly negative median CRoMa (−0.02), and PRISM/TITAN sit at nearly the same typed-distance radius (0.14 vs 0.13) but on opposite sides of the robustness/fragility diagonal (CRoMa=0.26 vs −0.30) — illustrating that high biological discriminability does not imply confounder robustness, and that raw distance magnitude alone would misrank encoders without CRoMa's scale-free ratio construction.

Van der Laak and Litjens are established senior figures in computational pathology and deep learning for histology (van der Laak has led major public pathology benchmarking efforts including CAMELYON; Litjens is a prominent computational pathology methods researcher). This is a methods/benchmarking paper rather than a clinical validation study, consistent with the authors' focus on rigorous, reproducible foundation-model assessment (cf. reference [23], Mahmood's "A benchmarking crisis in biomedical machine learning").

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The core idea — replacing a fixed-k, count-based robustness score with a per-sample, distance-based, scale-free margin — is a genuinely useful and fairly simple geometric reframing rather than a deep algorithmic innovation. Its main contribution is diagnostic clarity: the demonstration that RI's pooled fixed-k design silently excludes 54–90% of Camelyon samples from scoring (support 9.6–46.3%) is a concrete, previously under-appreciated flaw in a metric proposed by a very recent (2025) benchmark paper from a different group, and the "median rank ≈149 vs evaluated k=11" statistic (Supplementary Table 12) is a sharp, falsifiable illustration of the problem.

**Rigor**: The evaluation is unusually thorough for a metrics paper — 20 tile encoders × 4 benchmarks, a natural-image control (DINOv2-B) to sanity-check that CRoMa doesn't just reward "any" self-supervised representation, sensitivity analyses for both free parameters (m and α, Supplementary A.2–A.3, showing rank stability ρ≥0.89–0.98 across reasonable ranges), and an explicit provenance-overlap ablation for Midnight-12k/Phikon's TCGA pretraining exposure. A minor internal tension: CRoMa's within-benchmark rank correlation with RI is quite high (0.92–0.95), so the paper's own numbers suggest RI's *ranking* signal is not badly broken on the models/benchmarks studied — the paper's real, well-supported claim is narrower than "RI is wrong," and is instead "RI's coverage is model-dependent and its pooled score cannot see the tail," which the Camelyon lower-tail results (every encoder has LTM₁₀<0, Table 3) substantiate convincingly.

**Reproducibility**: Metric implementations and evaluation manifests are released as an open-source Python package (`croma`), and all four benchmarks are either public reuses of PathoROB's cohorts or explicitly documented curations (PCaBiop from the public PANDA challenge). Model checkpoints are third-party publicly released or proprietary-but-published foundation models (Table 2), so most experiments should be replicable by others with access to the same benchmarks.

**Significance**: Practically relevant for model selection in computational pathology — the "no model is uniformly robust; select via a median-vs-tail Pareto trade-off" framing is a sensible and actionable recommendation. The link to downstream shortcut susceptibility (pooled ρ=0.84 with APD) is the strongest evidence that CRoMa is not merely an alternative bookkeeping scheme but tracks something operationally consequential.

**Red flags / caveats the paper itself surfaces**: (1) On PCaBiop-ISUP, MOOZY's advantage is explicitly flagged as potentially confounded, since PANDA (the source of PCaBiop) was part of MOOZY's own pretraining corpus (Table 2) — a data-quirk the authors disclose rather than hide, but which weakens MOOZY's apparent superiority as a clean external validation. (2) Similarly, Midnight-12k's TCGA-4x4 dominance (median CRoMa=0.40, roughly double the runner-up) is substantially inflated by its TCGA-only pretraining corpus, and while the provenance ablation shows the effect is not entirely explained by overlap, the analysis (Supplementary A.7) rests on a single held-out-cohort re-scoring rather than a fully controlled retraining comparison. (3) The out-of-domain APD correlations (0.71–0.87) are noticeably weaker than in-domain (0.84–0.93), and the paper is candid that OOD APD conflates two effects (shortcut reliance and confounder-label transfer) that CRoMa alone cannot disentangle. (4) The comparison baselines throughout are essentially RI and its own distance-weighted variant MaRI — both introduced by the same lineage of work (PathoROB and this paper) — so there is no comparison against other batch-effect/robustness metrics from the broader domain-generalization literature (e.g., silhouette-based cluster purity, MMD-based domain gap, or CORAL-style alignment scores), leaving open whether CRoMa's advantages are specific to this SO/OS-typed-neighbour family of metrics or would also appear relative to unrelated robustness measures.

## Strengths

- Clear, well-motivated diagnosis of a structural flaw (model-dependent, fixed-k support) in a very recently proposed community metric (PathoROB's RI), backed by a concrete and interpretable statistic (median first-typed-neighbour rank ≈149 vs. operating k=11 on Camelyon).
- CRoMa's construction is simple, geometrically interpretable (signed angular deviation from the 45° diagonal in the (d^SO, d^OS) plane), scale-free (invariant to a model's overall embedding-distance scale, which the paper shows varies by up to 80× across slide encoders), and defined for every sample rather than a model-dependent subset.
- Broad, multi-scale, multi-organ evaluation (tile- and slide-level; breast, lung/colon/other cancers, oesophagus, prostate) with a natural-image control and extensive sensitivity/ablation analyses in the supplement.
- Directly ties the proposed representation-geometry metric to a downstream, clinically relevant failure mode (shortcut learning under supervised adaptation) rather than treating robustness as a purely intrinsic representation property.
- Honest, granular reporting of confounds (TCGA/PANDA pretraining overlap) rather than cherry-picking favourable comparisons.

## Limitations

- CRoMa (and RI/MaRI) require labelled biological classes and known confounder (acquisition centre) labels for every sample; it cannot be computed on unlabelled deployment data, limiting it to curated benchmark-style evaluation rather than continuous monitoring in the field.
- The metric is defined only over SO/OS neighbour pairs, discarding SS and OO pairs entirely; while conceptually justified, this means CRoMa says nothing about overall representation quality or biological discriminability on its own (the paper does report bio/conf k-NN balanced accuracy alongside CRoMa in every table, which mitigates this).
- Benchmarks are all H&E histopathology with medical-centre as the sole confounder; no results reported for other confounder types (scanner model, staining protocol vintage, IHC/special stains) or non-histopathology modalities, so generalization of CRoMa's specific numeric behaviour beyond centre-level confounding is untested here.
- The claimed consistency of GenBio-PathFM as the most Pareto-robust model is explicitly caveated by the authors themselves as potentially confounded by model scale, data curation, and pretraining recipe differences (its JEPA-based pretraining stage is the standout architectural difference, but this is not isolated via ablation).
- No wall-clock or computational cost comparison between RI/MaRI/CRoMa is reported, though CRoMa's nearest-neighbour search over m=5 typed neighbours per sample is likely comparable in cost to RI/MaRI's k-NN computation.

## Relevance

该工作提供了一个可直接套用的病理基础模型鲁棒性评测工具（`croma` 包）与"中位数-尾部帕累托"选型框架，对做模型评测、下游临床部署前筛选、或研究批次效应/shortcut learning 的工作有较高参考价值，尤其是其"所有模型都有混杂主导的最差十分位"这一发现对模型选型和数据增强策略设计有直接指导意义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does CRoMa's per-sample tail (F(0), LTM₁₀) behave when stratified by biological subclass or patient covariates (age, sex, tumour stage) rather than only by benchmark — would confounder-dominated failures concentrate in clinically important subgroups, as the paper's discussion speculates but does not empirically test?
- Would CRoMa's rankings and Pareto frontier change if evaluated against non-typed-neighbour robustness metrics from the broader domain-generalization literature (e.g., MMD, CORAL alignment, cluster purity/silhouette by confounder), rather than only against RI and MaRI, which share the same lineage and typed-neighbour construction?
- Could the JEPA-based pretraining recipe that distinguishes GenBio-PathFM be isolated via a controlled ablation (same data scale/corpus, DINO vs. DINO+JEPA) to test whether it causally drives the observed Pareto-frontier consistency, as the discussion section speculates?
- Can CRoMa be adapted into a differentiable regularizer or training-time objective (rather than a purely post-hoc evaluation metric) to directly optimize pretraining for lower F(0)/higher LTM₁₀, and would doing so trade off against biological k-NN accuracy?
- How sensitive is the reported downstream-shortcut correlation (APD vs. CRoMa, pooled ρ=0.84/0.78) to the specific choice of linear probing as the downstream adaptation method — would the same relationship hold under full fine-tuning or attention-based MIL aggregation for slide-level tasks?
