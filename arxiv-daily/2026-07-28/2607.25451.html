Key Finding

Quantization forgets memorized text faster than it loses general ability — but at 1B params, 4-bit still lets 72% of it survive.

Bits and Memories: Measuring Verbatim Extraction Across LLM Quantization ↗

Akshay Sasi · 28 Jul 2026 ·
LLM Quantization Memorization Training-Data Extraction Privacy Pythia

Key Metric

71.8%

of memorized sequences survive NF4 4-bit quantization at Pythia-1B (extraction 83.0%→59.6%), while the model keeps 95.9% of its Pile-perplexity capability — selectivity ≈ 8.0×.

Why This Matters

If you deploy INT4/NF4-quantized LLMs, don't treat compression as a privacy safeguard — memorized and copyrighted text can still be extracted verbatim.

原文 (Relevance)：其"选择性遗忘但残留比例随规模上升"的结论提示在评估大模型量化方案的隐私声明时，应优先看逐字提取率而非仅看成员推断攻击成功率。

Critical Edge

The paper's own headline — that surviving memorization grows with model size — rests on just three data points, one of which breaks monotonicity (0.27 at 410M dips below 0.39 at 160M) before jumping to 0.72 at 1B.

一句话总结 · Chinese Abstract (source)

在 Pythia 160M/410M/1B 三种规模、五档精度（FP32/FP16/INT8/NF4/FP4）和两种独立量化算法（bitsandbytes 与自实现 RTN）下直接测量逐字提取（verbatim extraction），发现量化确实比损失通用能力更快地"遗忘"记忆内容（selectivity 最高达 8 倍），但在 1B 模型上四比特量化仍能保留约 72% 的已记忆序列、仅损失约 4% 的困惑度性能，说明量化并不能作为隐私防护手段，且这一"记忆残留比例"随模型规模增大而上升。

Methodology

01
Use the deduplicated Pythia suite (160M/410M/1B) paired with each model's official memorized-sequence set (Biderman et al. 2023): 64-token Pile passages the full-precision model is known to reproduce greedily. A fixed cached subset keeps every precision level on identical sequences.
02
Split each sequence into a 32-token prompt + 32-token target. Greedily decode 32 tokens (no sampling); an exact token match counts as extraction — the standard "discoverable extraction" protocol (Carlini et al. 2023). Partial-credit metrics (correct-prefix length) are recorded too.
03
Measure capability in parallel via sliding-window perplexity on WikiText-2 (out-of-distribution) and Pile text (in-distribution), so no single corpus flatters the result.
04
Run the precision ladder FP32→FP16→INT8→NF4/FP4 (bitsandbytes, as in QLoRA), cross-validated against an independently implemented, dependency-free group-wise round-to-nearest (RTN) quantizer at 8-/4-bit.
05
Compute scale-free selectivity s(q) = log m(q) / log c(q) against a full-precision reference; s > 1 means memorization decays faster than capability.

Reference asymmetry: the 1B baseline is FP16, not FP32 (FP32 didn't fit the author's GPU) — a slightly lower bar than the smaller models', which could inflate 1B's apparent retention/selectivity.

Two algorithmically unrelated quantizers agree qualitatively — selective forgetting is not a bitsandbytes-specific artifact.

  • INT8 is stable at every size, retaining 97–100% of both extraction and capability — the cliff only appears at 4-bit.
  • Mean correct-prefix length (410M) collapses from ≈28/32 tokens at full precision to ≈12/32 at NF4 — quantization degrades reproduction depth, not just a few borderline sequences.

Strengths

  • Ground-truth-based measurement (official Pythia memorized-sequence list) instead of an ad hoc memorization proxy.
  • Two independent quantizer implementations (bitsandbytes vs. self-written RTN) agree qualitatively, strengthening the claim.
  • Dual capability corpora (in- and out-of-distribution) guard against a perplexity measure flattered by domain match.
  • Clean, scale-free selectivity metric that ties memorization decay and capability decay together.
  • Full code and data release, enabling exact replication and extension to larger models.

Limitations

  • Single model family (Pythia) — whether the pattern transfers to LLaMA/Mistral/Qwen is untested.
  • Largest model is only 1B parameters; "grows with model size" is an extrapolation from three points, not a measured trend at deployment scale.
  • Only strict greedy, all-or-nothing extraction studied; probabilistic/discoverable extraction and activation quantization are left untested.
  • Inconsistent full-precision reference across sizes (FP32 vs. FP16 for 1B) introduces a subtle asymmetry in the selectivity math.
  • No confidence intervals or seed variance reported for the extraction-rate and perplexity numbers in Table 1.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Would a direct, controlled comparison of membership-inference success vs. verbatim-extraction on the same Pythia checkpoints confirm the two genuinely diverge, rather than relying on cross-domain literature comparisons?
  2. Does "surviving fraction grows with model size" hold up with a 4th or 5th Pythia size point (e.g., 2.8B, 6.9B) to resolve the non-monotonic dip between 160M and 410M?
  3. How does selective forgetting interact with quantization-aware training or activation quantization (e.g., SmoothQuant, AWQ), both explicitly left untested?
  4. Would probabilistic/discoverable extraction (sampling-based, per Hayes et al. 2024) show a different — possibly worse — quantization sensitivity profile than strict greedy match?
  5. Is there an interpretability story (weight-magnitude or activation-outlier analysis) that localizes where memorized sequences live, explaining why they're precision-fragile yet less so at scale?
