AI reviewers rate AI-written proposals roughly one point higher than human-written ones — a bias human reviewers never show.

一句话总结：人类评审对人类与AI撰写的提案打分几乎无差；两个前沿AI评审却系统性地给AI提案多打约1分（5分制），并能100%识别作者身份——人类评审组不存在这种偏差。

Why this matters

Quantifies a specific, reproducible bias risk in using LLMs as reviewers or judges — relevant to any AI-assisted evaluation, scoring, or gatekeeping process, not just grant review.

Critical edge

The four "blind" human reviewers are the paper's own co-authors, not an independent panel — and the whole result rests on just 8 projects, with effects concentrated in a handful of them.

Methodology

  1. Fixed seed. A domain expert supplies a title + one-paragraph background + one-sentence goal for each of 8 physics / astrophysics / cosmology projects (AGN duty cycles, LBG–halo connection, intrinsic alignments, laser-ablation debris, CMB foregrounds, GW binary environments, pulsar timing arrays, SU(2) Yang–Mills inflation).
  2. Parallel writing. The same seed goes to the human expert and to an outside "AI prompter" who runs it through 3 mid-2025 models — ChatGPT-4o, Claude Sonnet 4, DeepSeek V3 — single pass, no iteration. Result: 8 human + 24 AI = 32 one-page proposals on an identical 4-section template.
  3. Laundering. Human proposals get a grammar-only ChatGPT-4o cleanup to strip stylistic tells; all 32 are anonymized and reformatted identically before review.
  4. Blind review. 4 human reviewers (co-authors) and 2 mid-2026 frontier AI reviewers (Claude Opus 4.8, ChatGPT Pro 5.5) each do two tasks: judge human-vs-AI origin, and score 1–5 across clarity/structure, method fit, resource planning, and feasibility/timeline.
  5. Stress tests. Reorder the two tasks (rate-first, via Codex 5.5 Pro) and swap in a smaller reviewer (Claude Sonnet 4.6) to check the bias isn't a single-model or ordering artifact.

  • Aspect breakdown: the pro-AI bias is largest on "Clarity & Structure" (Claude Opus 4.8 rates AI authors near-ceiling, 4.88–5.00, vs. 3.75 for the human author) and smallest on "Appropriateness of Methods." "Feasibility, Timeline & Risk" is the weakest aspect for AI-written proposals under every evaluator — consistent with AI plans favoring round-number, unrealistic timelines.
  • Project-level anti-correlation: the AI-vs-human score gap correlates strongly negative with the human plan's own quality (Pearson r ≈ −0.95 human panel, −0.96 AI panel). The human panel actually preferred the human proposal in 5 of 8 projects (e.g. RG, SU2, IA); the AI advantage concentrates where the human plan was weak — most starkly GW, scored only 2.4/5 by humans.

Strengths

  • Symmetric design — identical seed, template, and rubric for human and AI authors, and for human and AI reviewers.
  • The bias finding survives 3 independent robustness checks: reordered tasks, a smaller reviewer model, cross-model comparison.
  • Diverse 8-project portfolio spanning theory, computation, observation, and instrumentation work.
  • Unusually transparent reporting — even quotes a reviewer's admittedly heuristic, non-evidence-based guess.
  • Direct, concrete tie to real funding-agency AI policies (NIH, NSF, ERC).

Limitations

  • Only 8 projects / 32 proposals; the authors' own analysis shows effects are concentrated in a handful, not uniform.
  • Proposals capped at one page / ≤300-word methodology — far shorter than a real grant application.
  • AI proposals were single-pass with no iterative refinement or human-in-the-loop editing.
  • "Blind" human reviewers are the paper's own co-authors; the AI-writer models (mid-2025) were already outdated versus the AI-reviewer models (mid-2026).
  • Tests only planning/methodology quality on a fixed, pre-conceived idea — not whether AI can originate the research idea itself.

Questions for further exploration

Does the ~1-point bias persist if AI reviewers are told to ignore surface style and judge content substance only?
Would iterative refinement (multi-turn prompting, self-critique) close the gap on feasibility/timeline for AI-written proposals?
Would a fully external, non-author human panel replicate the same 72–79% detection accuracy and lack of bias?
Does the r ≈ −0.95/−0.96 anti-correlation generalize beyond these 8 physics/astronomy projects?
How would the numbers shift for full-length, multi-round-reviewed grant proposals instead of one-page summaries?
