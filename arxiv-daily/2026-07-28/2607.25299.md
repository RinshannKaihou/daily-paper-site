> **一句话总结：** 论文证明了Stiefel流形二次惩罚函数满足限制正割不等式，从而给出免收缩(retraction-free)、免调参惩罚系数（显式取 μ=1/3）的流形优化算法及其确定性/随机收敛速率保证，并将其用于把LoRA的ΔW=BA重新表述为Stiefel/Oblique流形上的优化问题（Manifold-LoRA），在GLUE、SQuAD v1.1/v2.0、E2E NLG及LLaMA3.2/LLaMA3-8B上以相等或更少的可训练参数超过LoRA基线，例如在SQuADv2.0上F1从LoRA(r=16)的88.81提升到89.22，且训练收敛速度提升近2倍。

> **TL;DR:** The paper establishes a retraction-free, penalty-parameter-free landing algorithm for Stiefel-manifold optimization (explicit μ=1/3, backed by a restricted-secant-inequality argument) with O(1/K) deterministic and O(log K/√K) stochastic constraint-violation rates, then applies it to LoRA fine-tuning by constraining LoRA's basis matrix B to the Stiefel or Oblique manifold (Manifold-LoRA), removing the GL(r)-redundancy in ΔW=BA. Against a same-parameter LoRA baseline, Manifold-LoRA raises the GLUE average from 87.74 (LoRA, r=16, DeBERTaV3-base) to 88.63 (Sphere variant, r=16) and SQuADv2.0 F1/EM from 88.81/85.90 (LoRA r=16) to 89.22/86.41 (Stiefel r=16), while converging roughly 2x faster in epochs on SQuADv2.0 and CoLA; scaled up to LLaMA3-8B it improves SQuAD F1 from 84.3 (AdamW baseline) / 88.1 (a prior retraction-based Stiefel method) to 90.4.

## 核心贡献

- 证明非凸惩罚函数 φ(X)=1/4‖XᵀX−I‖² 在Stiefel流形附近满足"限制正割不等式"(RSI)，据此给出显式惩罚参数 μ=1/3 的理论依据，摆脱了以往免收缩方法需要人工调节惩罚系数、或让惩罚系数趋于无穷的做法。
- 结合Stiefel流形的1-近端光滑性(proximal smoothness)，为免收缩梯度下降算法在确定性设置下建立常步长精确收敛到驻点的保证，速率 O(1/K)，改进了此前只能收敛到某邻域的结果(Ablin & Peyré 2022)；在随机设置下采用"loss梯度步递减、惩罚梯度步恒定"的两尺度步长，得到约束违反度 O(log K/√K)的速率，优于此前单尺度递减步长给出的更弱结果(Xiao et al. 2024)。
- 将LoRA微调中 ΔW=BA 的冗余（对任意可逆C，BA=(BC)(C⁻¹A)）重新表述为Stiefel或Oblique流形与欧氏空间乘积上的流形优化问题，把B视为受约束的"基"，A视为对应坐标，提出结合免收缩着陆技术与AdamW的Manifold-LoRA算法(Algorithm 2)，对A、B按各自的Lipschitz常数分别调度学习率。
- 在GLUE九个任务(DeBERTaV3-base)、SQuAD v1.1/v2.0、E2E NLG Challenge(GPT-2 Medium/Large)以及LLaMA3.2-1B/3B与LLaMA3-8B上的SQuAD/QuAC/GSM8K/MATH等任务上做了系统实验，证明Manifold-LoRA在相同或更少可训练参数下持续优于LoRA、Adapter、BitFit，并将训练收敛速度提升近2倍；通过可视化BᵀB热图验证学到的B确实贴合目标流形几何。

## Key Contributions

- **Explicit, provably-correct penalty parameter for Stiefel-manifold landing.** By establishing a restricted secant inequality (RSI, Lemma 1) for the nonconvex penalty φ(X) = ¼‖XᵀX−I‖² near the Stiefel manifold, the paper proves gradient descent on φ with step size μ=1/3 converges linearly (Lemma 2), giving a closed-form, tuning-free penalty coefficient rather than the ad-hoc or diverging penalty parameters used in prior retraction-free methods.
- **Improved convergence theory for retraction-free, penalty-parameter-free optimization (Theorem 1).** Leveraging 1-proximal smoothness of the Stiefel manifold, the deterministic algorithm with a *constant* step size achieves exact O(1/K) convergence to a stationary point (improving on the neighborhood-only guarantee of Ablin & Peyré, ICAIS 2022); the stochastic variant with a two-scale step-size schedule (decaying for the loss-gradient step, constant for the penalty-gradient step) achieves O(log K/√K) constraint-violation rate, improving on the single-scale decaying-step-size O(log K/K^{1/4})-type result of Xiao et al. (Math. of OR, 2024).
- **Manifold reformulation of LoRA that removes GL(r) redundancy.** Since ΔW=BA=(BC)(C⁻¹A) for any invertible r×r matrix C, the paper regards B as a basis constrained to St(d,r) or the Oblique manifold Ob(d,r) and A as the coordinate of ΔW under B, yielding a well-posed manifold optimization problem (Eq. 2) instead of the unconstrained, non-unique LoRA parametrization.
- **Manifold-LoRA algorithm (Algorithm 2)**, generalizing the retraction-free landing update to an AdamW-style optimizer, with separate step-size schedules for A and B (each proportional to the reciprocal of its own Lipschitz constant) and a constant penalty gradient step for B; shown to be a strict generalization of the SGD-type retraction-free algorithm (Corollary 1).
- **Broad empirical validation** spanning GLUE (NLU), SQuAD v1.1/v2.0 (QA), E2E NLG Challenge (NLG), and a scaling study on LLaMA3.2-1B/3B and LLaMA3-8B (SQuAD, QuAC, GSM8K, MATH), plus a direct visualization (BᵀB heatmaps, Figure 4) confirming the learned B actually respects the intended manifold structure.

## Methodology

The paper targets optimization over the Stiefel manifold St(d,r) := {X ∈ ℝ^{d×r} : XᵀX = I}, min_X (1/N)Σf_i(X). Instead of retraction-based Riemannian gradient descent (which requires costly orthogonalization, e.g. the Cayley transform's (2r)×(2r) matrix inversion costing 4dr²+40/3·r³ flops), it builds on retraction-free "landing" methods: X_{k+1} = X_k − α_k·P_{T_{X_k}St}(g_k) − μ·X_k(X_kᵀX_k − I), where the first term is the projected (stochastic) gradient onto the tangent space and the second is the negative gradient of the quadratic penalty φ(X)=¼‖XᵀX−I‖² that pulls iterates back toward the manifold (Eq. 3, Algorithm 1).

**Theoretical foundation.** The key technical tools are (1) proximal smoothness of the Stiefel manifold (it is 1-proximally smooth, following Clarke-Stern-Wolenski and Balashov-Kamalov), which makes the nearest-point projection well-behaved and Lipschitz near the manifold, and (2) a newly-proved restricted secant inequality (RSI) for φ within the ⅛-tube around the manifold, which plays the role that strong convexity plays in ordinary linear-convergence proofs. Together these justify a fixed μ=1/3 (rather than tuning μ or sending it to infinity as classical exact-penalty/augmented-Lagrangian methods require) and yield Theorem 1's deterministic O(1/K) and stochastic O(log K/√K) rates, both proved via a one-step descent lemma (Lemma 5) that couples decrease in the loss f with decay of the feasibility error ‖X_k^⊤X_k − I‖ (Lemma 4).

**Manifold-LoRA reformulation.** LoRA's update ΔW=BA (B ∈ ℝ^{d×r}, A ∈ ℝ^{r×m}) is not unique since BA=(BC)(C⁻¹A) for invertible C, so the paper reformulates fine-tuning as min_{A,B} L(BA) s.t. B ∈ St(d,r) or Ob(d,r) (Eq. 2), where Ob(d,r) is the Oblique manifold (unit-norm columns, i.e., St(d,1) applied per column — equivalent to r independent spheres in ℝ^d, called "Sphere" in the experiments). The projected gradients for B use B·sym(BᵀΔ_B) (Stiefel) or B·ddiag(BᵀΔ_B) (Oblique) rather than the Euclidean gradient. Algorithm 2 (Manifold-LoRA) generalizes Algorithm 1 to use either SGD or AdamW updates for A and B, with A updated by ordinary AdamW on the projected gradient and B additionally receiving a constant-μ penalty-gradient landing step; step sizes for both A and B are clipped between task-specific lower/upper bounds (Tables III–V) and scaled by the reciprocal ℓ2-norm-based Lipschitz constants, since ∇_A L and ∇_B L have different Lipschitz constants depending on ‖B‖₂ and ‖A‖₂ respectively.

## Results & Highlights

- **GLUE (DeBERTaV3-base, Table VI):** at matched trainable-parameter counts (0.59M, rank 16), Manifold-LoRA (Sphere/Oblique variant) reaches an **All-Average score of 88.63%**, versus **87.74%** for the LoRA baseline at the same rank and parameter count — the best average across all baselines including full fine-tuning (87.85%), Adapter (88.06%), and BitFit (85.65%). On MRPC specifically, Sphere r=16 scores 91.67 vs. LoRA r=16's 90.20 (+1.47); on RTE, Stiefel r=8 scores 83.16 vs. LoRA r=8's 80.15 (+3.01), at identical parameter budgets (0.30M).
- **SQuAD v1.1 / v2.0 (DeBERTaV3-base, Table VII):** on SQuADv2.0, Manifold-LoRA (Stiefel, rank 16, 2.65M params) achieves **F1 = 89.22, EM = 86.41**, versus the LoRA baseline at the same rank/parameter count (**F1 = 88.81, EM = 85.90**) — a +0.41 F1 / +0.51 EM gain, and the best result among all baselines including full fine-tuning (F1 87.58) and Adapter (F1 88.36 @ 1.22M). On SQuADv1.1, Sphere r=8 (1.33M) reaches F1 94.25 vs. LoRA r=8's F1 93.88.
- **Training efficiency:** on the SQuADv2.0 training curves (Figure 1), Manifold-LoRA "almost achieves a 2x speed-up in training epochs" versus vanilla LoRA in reaching the same training loss, exact-match, and F1 levels; on CoLA (Figure 2a), Manifold-LoRA reaches vanilla LoRA's training loss in "nearly half the number of epochs," described in the text as "almost 2× faster convergence."
- **Scaling to LLaMA3-8B (Table I, reading comprehension):** on SQuAD, Manifold-LoRA (Sphere) reaches **F1/EM = 90.4/82.0**, compared to a plain AdamW-optimized LoRA baseline at **84.3/74.6** and a prior retraction-based manifold method ("Stiefel" from Park et al., ACL Findings 2025) at **88.1/79.7** — a +2.3 F1 gain over the strongest prior baseline and +6.1 over AdamW. On QuAC, Manifold-LoRA (Sphere) reaches F1 71.3 vs. 65.8 (AdamW) and 69.7 (prior Stiefel method).
- **Mathematical reasoning (Table II, LLaMA3-8B):** on GSM8K, Manifold-LoRA (Stiefel) reaches **60.5%** accuracy vs. AdamW's 54.7% and the prior Stiefel baseline's 58.8% (+1.7 over the strongest baseline); on MATH, Manifold-LoRA (Stiefel) reaches 23.8% vs. AdamW's 19.3% and the prior baseline's 22.5%.
- **E2E NLG Challenge (Table VIII, GPT-2 Large, 0.77M params):** Manifold-LoRA (Sphere) reaches **BLEU 70.9** vs. LoRA's **70.1** (+0.8), with matching or better NIST (8.92 vs 8.82), METEOR (46.8 vs 46.7), ROUGE-L (72.5 vs 72.0), and CIDEr (2.55 vs 2.53).

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The theoretical core — an explicit, provably-correct constant penalty parameter (μ=1/3) for retraction-free Stiefel landing, derived from a restricted secant inequality rather than tuned or sent to infinity — is a genuine advance over the cited retraction-free literature (Ablin & Peyré 2022, Xiao et al. 2024, Gao et al. 2022). However, the idea of reformulating LoRA over a Stiefel/Oblique manifold to remove the GL(r) redundancy is explicitly acknowledged by the authors as concurrently discovered by other groups (footnote 1, citing [25]–[27]), so Manifold-LoRA's application-side novelty is more about combining an efficient, theoretically-grounded optimizer with this reformulation than about the reformulation itself.

**Rigor:** The convergence theory is careful and explicit (RSI lemma, proximal-smoothness argument, one-step descent lemma, separate deterministic/stochastic theorems with stated rates), and the paper is specific about how its two-scale step-size scheme improves on the single-scale scheme in the closest prior work (Xiao et al. 2024). On the empirical side, comparisons at LLM scale (Table I, II) use only two baselines: a plain AdamW-optimized LoRA and a single retraction-based prior "Stiefel" manifold method taken from a concurrent paper ([27], Park et al.). Despite the related-work section discussing AdaLoRA, SoRA, and VeRA at length, none of these are benchmarked at the LLaMA scale, which is a real gap given they are directly relevant competing PEFT methods.

**Reproducibility:** Implementation details (PyTorch, HuggingFace Transformers, OpenDelta) and extensive hyperparameter tables (III–V, listing per-task μ and step-size clip bounds lb/ub for every GLUE task, SQuAD version, and E2E model) are provided, which aids replication, but no code repository link appears in the reviewed text. Notably, the "penalty-parameter-free" framing applies to the theoretical constant μ=1/3, but Algorithm 2's practical clip bounds (lb, ub) are tuned per dataset and per rank (visible as different values in every row of Tables III–V) — a form of hyperparameter tuning that partly offsets the stated goal of eliminating manual tuning.

**Significance and red flags:** The GLUE/SQuAD/E2E-scale experiments use DeBERTaV3-base and GPT-2 (older, smaller backbones), while the only modern-LLM evaluation (LLaMA3.2-1B/3B, LLaMA3-8B) explicitly inherits its "model architectures, downstream benchmarks ... and evaluation metrics" from the single concurrent reference study [27] rather than an independently designed protocol. The choice between the Stiefel and Oblique(Sphere) constraint is empirically inconsistent across tasks — e.g., Manifold-LoRA(Sphere) wins on GSM8K for 1B/3B models but Manifold-LoRA(Stiefel) wins on MATH and on the 8B GSM8K result — and the paper offers no principled rule for choosing between them, leaving practitioners to grid-search both.

## Strengths

- A theoretically clean contribution: explicit μ=1/3 backed by a restricted secant inequality, with convergence rates that are proven to improve on the two closest prior retraction-free works cited in the paper.
- Constant-step-size *exact* convergence in the deterministic setting (vs. prior neighborhood-only guarantees), a meaningful theoretical upgrade.
- Broad empirical coverage: NLU (GLUE), QA (SQuAD v1.1/v2.0), NLG (E2E Challenge), and LLM scaling (LLaMA3.2-1B/3B, LLaMA3-8B) with GSM8K/MATH/QuAC.
- Consistent near-2x training-epoch speedup demonstrated on multiple datasets (SQuADv2.0, CoLA, QQP, STS-B), not just a single cherry-picked curve.
- Empirical verification that the manifold constraint is actually respected in practice via BᵀB heatmap visualization (Figure 4), rather than relying solely on downstream metrics as an indirect check.

## Limitations

- LLM-scale comparisons (Tables I, II) are restricted to a plain AdamW-LoRA baseline and a single prior retraction-based "Stiefel" method from a concurrent paper; stronger, well-known PEFT baselines (AdaLoRA, VeRA, DoRA) discussed in related work are never benchmarked at this scale.
- The practical algorithm still requires per-task, per-rank tuning of step-size clip bounds (lb, ub in Tables III–V), partially undercutting the "penalty-parameter-free" framing for the deployed method (as opposed to the theoretical constant μ).
- No ablation isolates which component — the manifold reformulation of B, the retraction-free landing update, or the per-matrix (A vs. B) step-size schedule — drives the observed gains; they are evaluated only as a bundled system.
- The choice between Stiefel and Oblique (Sphere) constraints is empirically inconsistent across benchmarks (e.g., Sphere wins on 1B/3B GSM8K, Stiefel wins on MATH and 8B GSM8K), with no principled selection criterion offered.
- Most benchmarks (GLUE, SQuAD, E2E) use smaller/older backbones (DeBERTaV3-base, GPT-2); genuine LLM-scale validation is confined to a single scaling section that reuses a concurrent paper's experimental protocol rather than an independently designed one.
- No explicit code/artifact release link found in the reviewed manuscript text.

## Relevance

这篇论文把流形优化理论（免收缩、显式免调参的Stiefel/Oblique流形着陆算法及其收敛性证明）与LoRA微调的工程实践结合起来，为"参数高效微调+几何约束消冗余"这一交叉方向提供了严谨的理论支撑和可直接套用的AdamW式算法，适合关注LLM高效微调方法、流形优化收敛性理论或优化器设计的研究者参考。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does Manifold-LoRA compare against stronger, more recent PEFT baselines (AdaLoRA, DoRA, VeRA, PiSSA) at LLaMA-3 scale, rather than only against plain AdamW-LoRA and a single concurrent retraction-based Stiefel method?
- Is there a principled (rather than grid-searched) rule for choosing between the Stiefel and Oblique/Sphere constraints on B, given the paper's own results show inconsistent winners across GSM8K, MATH, and QuAC?
- How sensitive are downstream results to the per-task step-size clip bounds (lb, ub) in Algorithm 2 — could a theoretically-derived or adaptive schedule remove this remaining source of manual tuning?
- Does the improved O(log K/√K) stochastic constraint-violation rate translate into measurable wall-clock gains under large-batch or distributed LLM fine-tuning, beyond the reported ~2x epoch-count speedup on smaller-scale tasks?
- Would extending the proximal-smoothness/RSI argument to the generalized Stiefel manifold (flagged by the authors as future work) change the explicit constant μ=1/3, and how would that affect empirical results?
- What is the measured wall-clock/memory overhead of Manifold-LoRA's dual (A, B) step-size scheduling relative to a well-tuned vanilla AdamW-LoRA baseline, beyond the paper's qualitative claim that the overhead is "negligible"?
