Likelihood misspecification in variational-inference BNNs

A fixed heavy-tailed Student's t likelihood (ν=5) beats Gaussian almost regardless of the true noise distribution in Bayesian neural networks.

Rethinking Likelihood Distributions: Student's t Likelihood Boosts Bayesian Neural Network Performance ↗
Hsia, Heyen, Weyrauch, Goetz, Streit, Krumscheid, Debus · 2026-07-28 · arXiv:2607.25376 ·
Bayesian neural networks variational inference uncertainty quantification Student's t distribution model misspecification
Key Metric
−16%
CRPS on ENTSO-E electricity-demand forecasting: 2579.95 → 2160.71 by swapping only the likelihood (Gaussian → Student's t, p<0.001). Same swap: MAE −19%, MSE −40%.
Why This Matters
A zero-extra-parameter likelihood swap that improves both point accuracy and uncertainty quantification for forecasting — without needing to know the true noise distribution.
Critical Edge
Under strongly right-skewed (Lognormal) noise, Student's t's symmetric tails get pulled by outliers — slightly worsening point-prediction MSE even though its CRPS still beats Gaussian's.

Methodology

TIER 1 · UNIVARIATE SYNTHETIC
y = x³+ε; noise ∈ {Gaussian, Gamma, Student's t}; n ∈ {300, 3,000, 30,000}; 4 MLP shapes.
→
TIER 2 · MULTIVARIATE SYNTHETIC
20-D→5-D nonlinear map; noise ∈ {Gaussian, Gamma, Lognormal, Student's t, Laplace}; 2 noise scales; n ∈ {10k, 50k}.
→
TIER 3 · REAL-WORLD
UCI Combined Cycle Power Plant regression; ENTSO-E German electricity-demand forecasting.
prior & approximate posterior held fixed as uncorrelated Gaussian(0, 1) throughout — only the ELBO's likelihood term varies, across Gaussian / skew-normal / Student's t (ν=5, moment-matched, zero extra trainable parameters)

The paper works entirely within mean-field variational inference for BNNs trained with Bayes-by-Backprop. All three candidate likelihoods — Gaussian, skew-normal, and Student's t — are parameterized directly from the sample mean, variance, and (for skew-normal) skewness of the network's stochastic forward passes, so none adds a trainable parameter. Student's t degrees of freedom is fixed at ν=5 after a sensitivity sweep over ν∈{2,5,7,10,15} showed a sharp gain to ν=5 and diminishing returns beyond it. Because skew-normal must be non-vanishing over all reals, it cannot represent one-sided Gamma noise exactly — a stated, not hidden, limitation of that comparison. Every configuration across all three tiers runs for 30 independent random seeds, scored on MSE and CRPS (plus MAE/MAPE for the real datasets) and tested against the Gaussian baseline with unpaired t-tests.

Strengths

  • Clean isolation of the likelihood as the only varied factor — prior and posterior held fixed throughout, avoiding confounds with other known BNN misspecification issues.
  • Three-tier evidence chain (controlled univariate → controlled multivariate → real-world) rather than a single-dataset demonstration.
  • Transparent about exceptions — Lognormal's MSE trade-off and POWER's deepest-architecture cases are reported, not hidden.
  • Zero additional trainable parameters; described as computationally inexpensive and easy to drop into existing Bayes-by-Backprop pipelines.
  • Reasonably thorough protocol: 30 seeds per configuration, unpaired t-tests with significance stars, and P–P calibration plots.

Limitations

  • Degrees of freedom (ν=5) is fixed globally from one sensitivity sweep on one univariate, Gaussian-noise configuration — never re-validated per dataset or architecture.
  • No correction for multiple comparisons across the very large number of reported significance tests (well over 100 across Tables 2–4, 8–13).
  • Only MLP architectures are tested (depths 1–8, widths 4–128); no CNN, RNN, transformer, or classification experiments.
  • Only two real-world datasets, both low-dimensional tabular/time-series regression tasks — generalization elsewhere is untested.
  • Skew-normal, the only asymmetric comparator, cannot represent one-sided Gamma noise, so the Gamma “matched-likelihood” condition is inherently approximate.
  • Code is released only via an anonymized double-blind-review link at the time of this summary.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Would jointly learning the Student's t degrees of freedom (instead of fixing ν=5) yield further gains, or is a fixed low df inherently more stable within VI training?
  • How does the likelihood choice interact with non-Gaussian priors or more expressive, non-mean-field posterior approximations?
  • Would a genuinely matched heavy-tailed/skewed likelihood (a proper skewed Student's t, or a reparameterized Gamma-based likelihood) close the gap under Gamma/Lognormal noise?
  • Does the Student's t advantage persist on higher-dimensional inputs (images) or in classification settings, or is it specific to low-to-moderate-dimensional regression and time-series tasks?
  • Do the “p<0.05” results survive a stricter significance threshold once corrected for the sheer number of comparisons run?
  • Is the Lognormal “pulling effect” mitigable with a skewed heavy-tailed likelihood, or is it a fundamental limitation of symmetric heavy-tailed distributions?
