Why This Matters

Gives manifold‑learning and shape‑analysis researchers a rigorous reduction tool, plus proof that naive embedding‑based gradient averaging can silently erase the true signal.

Critical Edge

All numerical validation runs on synthetic 2‑sphere toy functions built by the sole author — no real shape or landmark data, and no comparison against competing reduction methods.

Methodology

Transport gradients before you average them

Constantine's Euclidean active subspaces average an outer product of gradients, C = ∫∇f⊗∇f dρ, and eigendecompose it. On a curved manifold this breaks: tangent spaces at different points aren't canonically the same vector space, so gradients can't simply be summed.

The paper's fix is intrinsic parallel transport: carry each Riemannian gradient along its unique connecting geodesic into one central tangent space Tp₀ℳ, then average the outer products there (Eq. 12) — this exactly preserves the eigenvalues' meaning as mean‑squared directional derivatives (Lemma 1). Eigendecomposing the result yields Active Manifold‑Geodesics (Def. 3): ordered geodesic directions at p₀.

This is contrasted against the common extrinsic shortcut from manifold learning — embed, average ambient gradients, then project the dominant eigenvector. Theorem 1 shows projection and averaging commute, but eigen‑ordering and projection do not: a "dominant projected eigenvector" can silently differ from — or annihilate — the truly dominant direction.

Strengths

What holds up

  • Complete proofs, explicit constants (e.g. C = 1 + R²/4 on the unit hypersphere), carefully scoped hypotheses.
  • Sharp, concrete identification of a real pitfall: "projected dominance is not dominant projection," backed by a genuine degenerate example (Fig. 9), not just an abstract claim.
  • Clean closed‑form projection‑transport identity on hyperspheres — directly reusable for Kendall shape preshapes and elastic‑curve representations.
  • Honest three‑regime classification (Remark 6) of when intrinsic and extrinsic constructions agree vs. disagree, rather than one‑sided advocacy.
  • Well‑organized toy‑function gallery (Fig. 11) spanning ridge/non‑ridge, linear/quadratic/nonlinear cases.
Limitations

What's missing

  • All numerics confined to synthetic functions on the unit 2‑sphere — zero experiments on real shape, landmark, or curve data despite that being the stated motivation.
  • Construction is only geodesically local, valid inside one central geodesic ball; a global, foliation‑aware extension is left to future work.
  • No numerical or theoretical comparison against active manifolds, neural active manifolds, or principal geodesic analysis.
  • Inverse exponential map / Karcher mean computation in high‑dimensional embeddings is flagged as the harder practical bottleneck, but is not benchmarked here.
  • Single‑author work with no linked code or data artifact, limiting independent reproducibility of Algorithm 1.
