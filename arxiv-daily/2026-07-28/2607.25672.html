Key Finding

Human experts and mid-2025 AI agreed on fewer than 6% of physics literature-search references — and most AI-only citations had wrong metadata.

AI's Capability in Assisting Scientific Research in Physics, Astrophysics, and Cosmology I: Literature Review ↗

Anamaria Hell, Kateryna Vovk, Veena Krishnaraj, Jia Liu, et al. (20 authors) · 28 Jul 2026 ·

Why This Matters

Quantifies how reliable AI literature search really is for physicists: useful for surfacing genuinely different references, but its output still needs systematic verification.

原文 (Relevance)：这篇论文直接量化了当前及最新一代LLM在真实科研场景中做文献调研的可靠性边界，对依赖AI辅助文献检索工作流的研究者具有很强的实操参考价值。

Critical Edge

The headline claim that ChatGPT Pro 5.5 eliminates hallucination rests on a single project's 40 references, yet is prominently featured in the abstract and conclusion — a spot-check, not a benchmark.

一句话总结 · Chinese Abstract (source)

研究团队让人类专家与三种2025年中的大模型（ChatGPT-4o、ChatGPT Deep Research、Gemini 2.5-flash）在8个真实物理/天体物理/宇宙学课题上分别独立做文献调研，发现人类与AI选出的文献重合率不到6%，且AI生成的参考文献中3%是彻底虚构、64%存在元数据错误，而用2026年的ChatGPT Pro 5.5重跑其中一个课题时，40篇生成文献全部准确无误。

Methodology

01
Two disjoint groups, 8 real projects: a human expert (project owner) runs a conventional search (≤50 refs, sorted into reviews / historic / recent / other-foundational); a separate non-specialist "AI prompter" feeds the same background/goal text into a standardized prompt (Appendix B) run through ChatGPT-4o, ChatGPT Deep Research, Gemini-2.5-flash (mid-2025), each capped at 50 refs with Title/Author/Year/Journal/DOI/Link required.
02
The human expert then judges the relevance of every AI-found reference the human search missed.
03
References are bucketed per project per model: Human & AI, Human, not AI, AI, not human (relevant), AI, irrelevant.
04
Hallucination pipeline (Fig. 1): for the 641 AI-only references, try DOI → link → manual title search against Crossref, in sequence, classifying each as perfect, metadata mismatch, or fabrication.
05
Forward spot-check: the whole pipeline is rerun for one project (SU2) using ChatGPT Pro 5.5 (dated 2 Jul 2026), compared directly against the ChatGPT-4o run on the same project.

8 projects: AGN duty cycles · Lyman-break galaxy–halo connection · intrinsic alignments · laser-ablation debris prediction · HalfDome radio-galaxy foregrounds · GW black-hole binary environments · pulsar-timing-array GR forecasts · massive SU(2) Yang-Mills inflation.

Single human rater per project, no second rater — relevance judgments are, in the authors' own words, "necessarily subjective."

  • "Low fabrication" does not mean "trustworthy": ChatGPT-4o had almost no fabrications (1/196) but the highest mismatch rate after Gemini.

Strengths

  • Uses real, ongoing, expert-designed research projects rather than toy or retrospective benchmark questions.
  • Transparent, auditable hallucination-classification pipeline (Fig. 1) grounded in an external authority (Crossref), not self-reported LLM confidence.
  • Reports fine-grained, model-by-model and field-by-field breakdowns (Tables II–IV) instead of one aggregate number.
  • Explicitly and honestly states its own limitations (n=8 projects, single-project Pro 5.5 check) rather than overselling generality.

Limitations

  • Only 8 projects total, and only 1 of those 8 used for the forward-looking Pro 5.5 comparison.
  • Gemini tested only in its lighter "2.5-flash" configuration, not a frontier-tier variant — muddying the "Gemini is worst" framing.
  • Relevance judged by a single rater per project, no inter-rater reliability check, despite being acknowledged as subjective.
  • A snapshot in time: mid-2025 models plus one mid-2026 spot check; reported rates may already be stale for current models.
  • No statistical significance testing or confidence intervals anywhere — all figures are raw counts from a small, fixed sample.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Would fabrication/mismatch rates hold up if Gemini were tested at a frontier tier (e.g. Gemini 2.5 Pro) instead of the lightweight flash variant?
  2. How would a multi-rater relevance-judgment protocol change the "AI, irrelevant" percentages reported in Table II?
  3. Does the Pro 5.5 zero-hallucination result generalize across all 8 projects, or is SU2 (theoretical, fewer canonical references) an easier case than data-heavy observational projects like AGN or GW?
  4. Could a hybrid workflow that explicitly asks AI to search for references the human didn't find increase the useful "AI, not human" yield beyond ~30–36%?
  5. How do these hallucination rates compare across other STEM fields — is physics/astro/cosmology easier for LLM search due to arXiv/INSPIRE/ADS structured metadata?
