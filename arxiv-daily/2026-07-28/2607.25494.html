Automated Numerical Stability Analysis of Deep Learning Operators ↗
Xinye Chen · Sorbonne Université, CNRS, LIP6, Paris · 28 Jul 2026
numerical stability stochastic arithmetic (CESTAC) mixed-precision DL PyTorch · JAX · TF instrumentation
Key Metric
1.000
Accuracy = precision = recall of the stable-vs-unstable classifier, across six matched neural-operator families (softmax, linear, reduction, LayerNorm, attention, convolution) and both FP32-like & FP64-like formats. (Table 3)
Self-constructed benchmark — ground-truth labels come from the same controlled pairs being tested, with a post-hoc digit threshold (γ=3 / γ=10). See Critical Edge →
Why This Matters

Gives mixed-precision-training researchers a drop-in tool to trace numerical instability to specific operators in real training loops — though built for calibration debugging, not production-scale monitoring.

Critical Edge

Instrumentation overhead hits 127.6× (PyTorch) to 1124.3× (NumPy) — exceeding CADNA's own reported 10×–100× slowdown, with no head-to-head comparison against CADNA, MCA, Verificarlo, or Fuzzy PyTorch.

Methodology

  1. Lift. Every scalar/tensor value becomes a tuple of s=3 synchronized stochastic samples; software-emulated randomized directed rounding replaces deterministic ops in a simulated format (BF16/FP16/TF32/FP32/FP64).
  2. Estimate. At each instrumented op — arithmetic-level (every op) or operator-boundary (whole-op output) — a Student-t significant-digit estimate C_Ȳ = log10(√s|Ȳ| / (τ_β σ_Y)) is computed from the sample mean and spread.
  3. Trust, but verify GEMM. matmul/linear/conv can't be instrumented internally, so they're treated as "trusted" and probed via a controlled input perturbation when samples separate poorly; Theorems 2–3 bound how far this surrogate can distort the digit estimate.
  4. Diagnose without disturbing. NFloatSTE keeps the instrumented forward pass autograd-compatible via identity-gradient approximation; a synchronized shadow model runs the diagnostic pass after each optimizer step, feeding KernelReport / NFloatIterationTracker without ever touching the real training trajectory.

Activations, normalization & decision-level localization

  • ReLU's hidden-activation digits rise ~9.2 → 10.5 over 3 epochs; sigmoid/PReLU plateau at ~7.6–7.8 — yet all nine tested activations reach comparable accuracy (Fig. 12).
  • Pre-norm hidden output averages 6.45–7.05 digits vs. 5.84–6.30 post-norm; BatchNorm loses the most (~7.05→6.00), RMSNorm retains the most (Fig. 13).
  • Injecting one unstable operator (cancelling linear, near-constant LayerNorm, near-tie attention gate, naive exp attention, reciprocal gate) reliably yields the lowest digit report among six variants; near-constant LayerNorm shows digit loss and decision-flip are related but distinct signals (Figs. 9–11).

Strengths

  • First native CESTAC-style integration into PyTorch/JAX/TensorFlow/NumPy, with an STE keeping the forward pass gradient-compatible without restructuring training loops.
  • Controlled falsifiability design — matched stable/unstable pairs across classical analysis, six operator families, and two independent pathology-injection studies (vision + text).
  • Honest formal treatment of the GEMM approximation gap (Theorems 2–3) instead of glossing over the fused-kernel blind spot.
  • Useful conceptual split between operator-level digit loss and task-level decision instability (agreement-rate metric).
  • Public code release with detailed configuration examples for practitioners.

Limitations

  • 127.6×–1124.3× overhead in the one concrete micro-benchmark, likely higher in practice than the theoretical 3× lower bound; the "calibration subset" mitigation is suggested, not validated.
  • GEMM-like operators — the most compute-/precision-sensitive part of any network — rely on a trusted-operator + input-perturbation surrogate, not true arithmetic-level instrumentation.
  • All DL experiments are toy-scale (2,048-example subsets, ≤32 steps/epoch, 3 epochs); production-scale generalization is untested.
  • No direct quantitative comparison against CADNA, Monte Carlo Arithmetic, Verificarlo, or Fuzzy PyTorch on shared benchmarks.
  • Fused/vendor backend kernels remain entirely outside the instrumentation boundary, an acknowledged but unresolved blind spot.
  • Single-author, self-described work-in-progress with no stated peer-review status at this arXiv version.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • How would detection accuracy and overhead scale to production-size training (billions of parameters, thousands of steps)?
  • Can the GEMM data-perturbation safeguard be replaced by genuine instruction-level instrumentation to close the Theorem 3 approximation gap?
  • What calibration-subset strategy best trades detection accuracy against the 127×–1124× overhead — and has it been characterized empirically?
  • How does noisyfloat compare quantitatively (not just narratively) to CADNA, Fuzzy PyTorch, and Verificarlo on the same benchmark suite?
  • Could the digit/decision-agreement framework become an actionable precision-tuning loop rather than a purely diagnostic one?
