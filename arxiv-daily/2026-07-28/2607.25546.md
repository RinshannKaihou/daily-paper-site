> **一句话总结：** 论文提出归一化敏感度比（NSR），一种无需重训练、模型无关的事后诊断方法，通过比较模型输出对某特征跨环境扰动的敏感度是否稳定，来判断已训练模型依赖的是因果特征还是伪相关特征，在线性结构因果模型下给出精确可识别性定理，并在 UCI 自行车共享数据上以 Precision@7=0.75 超过 LASSO 基线（0.58）17 个百分点。

> **TL;DR:** The paper introduces the Normalised Sensitivity Ratio (NSR), a post-hoc, model-agnostic diagnostic that flags which features an already-trained model relies on causally vs. spuriously, using only multi-environment observations (no retraining, no causal graph). Under a linear SCM with K≥3 environments it proves exact identification and a complete map of failure modes; on UCI Bike-Sharing (n=17,379) NSR-based rankings reach Precision@7=0.75, beating a LASSO-coefficient-magnitude baseline (0.58) by 17 points, while on the harder Wine Quality benchmark (smaller per-environment samples) all variants top out around AUROC=0.63.

## 核心贡献

- 提出归一化敏感度比（Normalised Sensitivity Ratio, NSR）：对每个特征，先计算"因果敏感度比"（Causal Sensitivity Ratio, CSR）——将该特征替换为另一环境的边缘分布抽样后模型输出的平均变化量，再取该量跨环境的变异系数平方（CV²）。因果特征在各环境下敏感度恒定（NSR=0），伪相关特征敏感度随环境偏移量变化（NSR>0）。
- 在"结构化偏移"（structured-shift）情形——环境间差异主要体现在伪相关特征的均值偏移上，而因果机制与因果特征的边缘分布保持不变（如多中心临床数据、多批次基因组学）——给出线性结构因果模型下的精确可识别性定理（Theorem 4.1，K≥3 非退化环境）。
- 完整刻画了失败模式并给出精确的速率：偏移过弱时以 O(ε⁴) 速率坍缩；环境几何退化（K=2 或对称偏移）时恒为零；因果-伪相关混合代理特征时以 O((1-α)⁴) 速率衰减（Theorem 4.3）。同时证明零假设下 NSR 以 Oₚ(n⁻¹) 收敛、备择假设下以 Oₚ(n⁻¹/²) 收敛，速率差可用于基于置换检验的阈值选择（Theorem 4.2）。
- 证明 NSR 与 IRM、ICP 检测条件互不包含（Proposition 1）：给定偏移方案 β=(1,-1,0) 时 IRM/ICP 能检测而 NSR 不能；β=(-1,1,1) 时 NSR 能检测而 IRM 不能——三者是互补关系而非谁更强。
- 在合成数据、五种模型族（OLS/Ridge/随机森林/XGBoost/MLP）以及两个 UCI 真实数据集（Bike-Sharing、Wine Quality）上进行实证验证，确认理论预测并展示方法的模型无关适用性。

## Key Contributions

- **Normalised Sensitivity Ratio (NSR)**: for each feature j, compute the Causal Sensitivity Ratio (CSR) — the average change in model output when feature j is marginally replaced with a draw from another environment's distribution — then take the squared coefficient of variation (CV²) of CSR across environments. Causal features induce constant sensitivity (NSR=0); spurious features induce environment-varying sensitivity (NSR>0).
- **Exact identification theorem** (Theorem 4.1): under a linear structural causal model (SCM) with K≥3 non-degenerate environments satisfying causal invariance, non-constant shift geometry, and faithfulness, NSR_j = 0 iff feature j is causal — a perfect binary classifier whose separation depends on environment geometry, not on the model's learned weights (the weight ŵ_j² cancels exactly in the CV² ratio).
- **Complete failure-mode characterization** (Theorem 4.3) with explicit rates: weak-shift collapse O(ε⁴), degenerate geometry (K=2 or symmetric shifts → NSR≡0), and proxy attenuation O((1−α)⁴) for features that mix causal and spurious signal.
- **Finite-sample rate gap** (Theorem 4.2): Oₚ(n⁻¹) convergence under the null (causal) vs. Oₚ(n⁻¹/²) under the alternative (spurious), enabling a permutation-based threshold without distributional assumptions.
- **Formal incomparability with IRM and ICP** (Proposition 1): NSR is sensitive to non-constancy of absolute shifts from a reference, IRM to second moments of the shift distribution, and ICP to first moments — with explicit counterexample shift schedules where each pair of methods disagrees.
- **Empirical validation** across synthetic SCMs, five model families, and two UCI benchmarks, including a real-data application recovering 6/8 known causal drivers on Bike-Sharing without touching the trained model.

## Methodology

The paper works in a strict post-hoc setting: a model $\hat f$ is already trained and frozen; the practitioner observes labeled data $(X^{(e)}, Y^{(e)})$ from $K \geq 3$ labeled environments (sites, batches, collection periods) and wants to know which of the $p$ input features the model relies on causally ($\mathcal{C}$) vs. spuriously ($\mathcal{S}$), without access to the training procedure, gradients, or the causal graph.

**Data-generating process (Definition 1, linear SCM).** Causal features $X_\mathcal{C}$ are drawn i.i.d. across environments from a fixed Gaussian $\mathcal{N}(\mu_C,\Sigma_C)$ ("marginal causal stability" — stronger than the standard invariance $P(Y\mid X_\mathcal{C})$ assumed by IRM/ICP). Spurious features $X_\mathcal{S} = \Gamma X_\mathcal{C} + \beta_e \mathbf{1} + \xi^{(e)}$ are correlated with the causal features via a fixed confounding matrix $\Gamma$ but additionally shifted in each environment by a scalar $\beta_e$ (e.g., equipment offsets, batch effects). The outcome $Y^{(e)} = w_\mathcal{C}^\top X_\mathcal{C}^{(e)} + \varepsilon^{(e)}$ depends only on causal features, and this mechanism is identical across environments.

**Causal Sensitivity Ratio (CSR).** For feature $j$ and a reference environment $e_{\text{ref}}$, $\text{CSR}_j(e,e_{\text{ref}}) := \mathbb{E}\left[|\hat f(x_{-j}, x'_j) - \hat f(x)|\right]$, where $x_j$ is replaced by an independent draw from the reference environment's marginal of feature $j$ — a marginal-replacement / feature-perturbation operation mechanically related to feature attribution (SHAP, permutation importance can serve as drop-in estimators of $\hat f$'s sensitivity, used directly in the real-data experiments).

**NSR.** $\text{NSR}_j(e_{\text{ref}}) := \text{Var}_{e\neq e_{\text{ref}}}[\text{CSR}_j] / \mathbb{E}_{e\neq e_{\text{ref}}}[\text{CSR}_j]^2 = \text{CV}^2_{e\neq e_{\text{ref}}}[\text{CSR}_j]$. For linear predictors, $\text{CSR}_j = |\hat w_j|\cdot \delta_j$ where $\delta_j$ depends only on the marginals, so the learned weight cancels exactly in the ratio — NSR detects spurious reliance regardless of how small an ERM weight the trained model assigned to that feature.

**Practical computation.** Given $\hat f$, environment labels, and held-out data, NSR is estimated by drawing $m$ replacement values per feature per environment from the reference marginal, averaging the resulting output shift, and computing the sample CV² across environments (bootstrap CIs over environment partitions; a permutation test over environment labels gives a distribution-free threshold, exploiting the $O_p(n^{-1})$ vs $O_p(n^{-1/2})$ rate gap between null and alternative). Cost is $O(K\cdot n\cdot p\cdot m\cdot C)$ forward passes; reported as under 30 seconds on one CPU core for linear models and under 3 minutes for a Random Forest on the bike-sharing data ($K=5$, $n=3{,}476$, $p=12$, $m=50$).

Three practical checks are recommended before trusting NSR on new data: (1) putative causal-feature means are stable across environments, (2) most inter-environment shift variance concentrates in a low-dimensional subspace (PCA of environment centroids), (3) the shift-to-noise ratio $\varepsilon$ exceeds the $O(\varepsilon^4)$ collapse floor.

## Results & Highlights

- **Oracle correctness / operating regime (RQ1, synthetic linear SCM, Table 1, Figure 1):** at $K\geq3$ environments with shift magnitude $\varepsilon\geq1$, NSR achieves **AUROC = 1.000 with zero variance across 30 seeds**; at $K=2$ NSR is identically zero (AUROC = 0.500, matching the algebraic cancellation predicted by Theorem 4.3(ii)). Symmetric shift schedules at $K=3$ ($\beta=[0,1,-1]$) give AUROC = 0.318–0.584 depending on $\varepsilon$, while breaking the symmetry with $\beta=[0,1,-0.4]$ recovers AUROC = 1.000 with separation ratio 134 (vs. <1 in the symmetric case) — confirming the failure is geometric, not a matter of shift magnitude alone.
- **NSR vs. IRM incomparability (RQ2, synthetic OLS, Table 1/Section 5.2):** under shift schedule $\beta=(1,-1,0)$ (constant $|\beta_e-\beta_{e_{\text{ref}}}|$), **IRM detects the spurious feature with AUROC = 1.00±0.00** while **NSR is at near-chance AUROC = 0.47±0.20**. Under schedule $\beta=(-1,1,1)$ (non-constant absolute shift but $\text{Var}_e[\beta_e^2]=0$), the ranking flips: **NSR achieves AUROC = 1.00±0.00** while **IRM is unreliable at AUROC = 0.87±0.17**. This directly instantiates Proposition 1's formal incomparability claim.
- **Model-agnostic applicability (RQ3, Table 6):** across five model families (OLS, Ridge, Random Forest, XGBoost, MLP) trained on pooled data ($p_\mathcal{C}=p_\mathcal{S}=4$, $K=10$, $n=2{,}000$), all achieve **AUROC ≥ 0.992**, and pairwise Kendall's τ between NSR feature rankings across families ranges **0.529–0.612** (all positive), showing the causal/spurious boundary signal is consistent regardless of model class.
- **Real-world validation — Bike-Sharing (RQ5, Table 2, n=17,379, 8 causal + 4 proxy features, $K=5$ month-quintile environments):** **Permutation-NSR and SHAP-NSR both reach Precision@7 = 0.75, AUROC = 0.69 and 0.66 respectively**, correctly placing 6 of 8 true causal features (per Fanaee-T & Gama 2013 ground truth) in the top 7 — versus the **strongest post-hoc baseline, LASSO coefficient magnitude, at Precision@7 = 0.58, AUROC = 0.51** (a 17-percentage-point Precision@7 gap). An ablation using permutation-importance variance *without* the CV² normalization drops to Precision@7 = 0.61, confirming the normalization step (not merely cross-environment variance) carries the identification signal. Permutation p-values with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR control at q=0.10 correctly classify 10 of 12 features (4 false negatives, 0 false positives).
- **Real-world validation — Wine Quality (Table 2, n=6,497, 6 causal + 5 proxy/redundant features, wine-type × density-quintile environments):** results are markedly weaker: **Linear-NSR is the best method at Precision@7 = 0.57, AUROC = 0.63**, outperforming its own nonlinear variants (**SHAP-NSR: Precision@7 = 0.54, AUROC = 0.48**), which the paper attributes to smaller per-environment sample sizes (425–1,872 vs. 3,476 for bike-sharing) making nonlinear sensitivity estimators noisier — the authors state Linear-NSR should be preferred below ~1,000 samples/environment and nonlinear variants above it.
- **Proxy attenuation (RQ4):** empirical $\widehat{\text{NSR}}_{\text{proxy}}$ for a feature mixing causal and spurious signal matches the $O((1-\alpha)^4)$ theoretical prediction to within 5% for $\alpha \leq 0.70$.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** the core idea — using cross-environment *variance of sensitivity*, rather than sensitivity itself, as the discriminating statistic, and normalizing away the learned weight via CV² — is a clean and reasonably novel reframing distinct from training-time invariance methods (IRM, ICP, VREx, GroupDRO), which all require modifying the loss or retraining. Positioning NSR as strictly post-hoc and proving formal incomparability (not superiority) with IRM/ICP (Proposition 1) is intellectually honest and a genuine point of interest.

**Rigor:** unusually thorough for this kind of diagnostic paper — the authors don't just prove a success-case theorem, they fully characterize the failure boundary with explicit convergence rates (weak-shift collapse $O(\varepsilon^4)$, degenerate geometry exactly zero, proxy attenuation $O((1-\alpha)^4)$, finite-sample rate gap $O_p(n^{-1})$ vs. $O_p(n^{-1/2})$), all proved in a 16-page appendix. This kind of exhaustive boundary-mapping is more rigorous than typical empirical-only interpretability papers.

**Reproducibility:** hyperparameters for all five model families are given explicitly (Appendix A.9), and the theory is fully self-contained; however code is not released with this version (NeurIPS checklist item 5 states code will follow at camera-ready), so results cannot currently be independently reproduced from the paper alone.

**Paper-specific concerns:**
- The "real-world" environments in both UCI experiments are not naturally occurring multi-site data but are constructed post hoc by the authors themselves — bike-sharing environments are created by partitioning a single two-year dataset into month-quintiles, and wine-quality environments by crossing wine-type with density-quintile — specifically engineered so that "marginal causal stability" (checks 1–2 in Section 3) holds. This is a reasonable way to demonstrate the method on public data, but it is not the same claim as validating on genuinely independent multi-hospital or multi-batch data, which is the paper's own motivating scenario (the readmission-model example in the introduction).
- The only post-hoc baseline compared on real data is plain LASSO coefficient magnitude — described by the authors themselves as "the strongest post-hoc baseline without environment structure" — which is a fairly weak comparator; IRM and ICP (the paper's main theoretical foils) are only compared on synthetic data (Section 5.2), never against NSR on the Bike-Sharing/Wine Quality benchmarks where the interesting real-world comparison would be most informative.
- There is an internal inconsistency: Section 3 ("Practical computation," page 4) explicitly refers to "the construction used in the genomics experiment of Section 5.5," but Section 5.5 as submitted contains only the Bike-Sharing and Wine Quality experiments — no genomics dataset appears anywhere in the 23-page PDF. This looks like a leftover reference to a dropped experiment and is the kind of internal-consistency flag a careful reviewer would raise.
- On Wine Quality, all four method variants (Linear-NSR, SHAP-NSR, Permutation-NSR, Ensemble-NSR) top out at AUROC ≤ 0.63 — only modestly better than chance — and the paper's explanation (smaller per-environment n makes nonlinear estimators noisy) is plausible but asserted rather than verified with a controlled sample-size ablation on real data (the sample-size sweep in the appendix is synthetic-only).

**Significance:** if the identification and failure-mode theorems hold up under review, this is a useful practical tool for auditing already-deployed models without retraining — a real and underserved need given growing use of multi-site clinical and multi-batch genomic models. But the tool's guaranteed regime (fixed causal marginals, shift concentrated in spurious features) is narrower than the paper's motivating framing suggests, and the real-data results, while directionally positive, are modest in absolute terms (missing 2 of 8 known causal features even in the best case).

## Strengths

- Complete theoretical map of both success (Theorem 4.1) and failure conditions (Theorem 4.3) with explicit convergence rates, not just an existence proof.
- Weight-invariant normalization (CV²) means a spurious feature with a small ERM-fitted coefficient is still flagged — addresses a real blind spot of naive coefficient-magnitude or gradient-magnitude attribution.
- Genuinely model-agnostic: validated across five model families (linear, tree ensembles, boosting, MLP) with consistent AUROC ≥ 0.992 and positive Kendall's τ agreement.
- Provides explicit, checkable preconditions (Section 3's three checks) rather than presenting the method as a black box that always applies.
- Formal, not just empirical, delineation of when NSR, IRM, and ICP disagree (Proposition 1), avoiding an overclaim of universal superiority.
- Finite-sample rate gap ($O_p(n^{-1})$ vs. $O_p(n^{-1/2})$) is exploited constructively to build a distribution-free permutation test.

## Limitations

- Requires "marginal causal stability" — $P(X_\mathcal{C})$ fixed across environments — which is strictly stronger than the standard causal-invariance condition ($P(Y\mid X_\mathcal{C})$ fixed) used by IRM/ICP; under demographic or case-mix shift in the causal covariates themselves, NSR will misclassify causal features as spurious (acknowledged explicitly by the authors as a "principled scope boundary, not a tuning problem").
- Needs $K\geq3$ environments with non-degenerate, asymmetric shift geometry and environment labels; collapses to uninformative (identically zero) under $K=2$ or symmetric shift schedules.
- Models environment shift as a single scalar offset $\beta_e$ applied identically to all spurious features; feature-specific covariance shifts are not covered by the core theory (a PCA-based subspace extension is sketched in the conclusion but not evaluated in this version of the paper).
- Real-data validation relies on domain-knowledge ground truth (Fanaee-T & Gama 2013; Cortez et al. 2009) for which features are "truly" causal, and on environments constructed by the authors' own partitioning rather than naturally occurring multi-site data.
- Performance on the harder Wine Quality benchmark is modest (AUROC ≤ 0.63 for all variants), and nonlinear sensitivity estimators (SHAP, permutation importance) become unreliable at smaller per-environment sample sizes (<~1,000).
- Code not yet released.

## Relevance

这篇论文提供了一种无需重训练、可直接审计已部署模型的因果/伪相关特征诊断工具，并配有完整的可识别性与失效边界理论，对关注分布外泛化、模型可解释性以及多中心临床或多批次组学数据可靠性的研究方向具有较高参考价值；但其"结构化偏移"假设较强，真实数据验证也依赖人工构造的环境划分，实际落地效果还需在真正的多中心数据上进一步检验。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How gracefully (or sharply) does NSR degrade when the causal marginals $P(X_\mathcal{C})$ shift moderately across environments — the explicitly acknowledged failure boundary — rather than failing categorically?
- Does the PCA-based subspace-replacement extension (sketched in the conclusion) preserve the exact-identification guarantee when spurious shifts are feature-specific rather than a uniform scalar offset, and can it be validated empirically?
- How would NSR perform on genuinely independent multi-site clinical or multi-batch genomic data — the paper's own motivating scenario — rather than on post hoc partitions of single-source UCI tabular datasets?
- Could NSR be combined with ICP and/or IRM into a single ensemble test that covers the union of their (proven incomparable) detection conditions, rather than requiring a practitioner to guess which shift geometry applies?
- What happens to Precision@7/AUROC under noisy or misspecified environment boundaries (e.g., imprecise quintile cut points), given the method's dependence on accurate environment labels?
- What was the intended genomics experiment referenced in Section 3 but absent from Section 5.5 — was it dropped, and if reintroduced, would it change the paper's real-world validation story?
