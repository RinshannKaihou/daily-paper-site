Pure theory · first algorithmic depth separation, log-depth vs. constant-depth

Logarithmic-depth networks provably learn functions that no constant-depth network, however wide, can approximate.

Algorithmic Separation between Constant-Depth and Logarithmic-Depth Neural Networks ↗
Ren, Wang, Lee · 2026-07-28 · arXiv:2607.25200 ·
depth separation hierarchical staircase functions Boolean Fourier analysis coordinate descent learning theory
Key Metric
≥ 1/4
L² error floor for any constant-depth, polynomial-width network (spectrally-bounded weights, regular activations) approximating a "deep quadratic function" — because such a network collapses to a polynomial of degree ≪ d1/2, yet ≥1/3 of the target's Fourier mass sits at degree ≥ √d.
Why This Matters
Depth cannot always be traded for width: some functions are efficiently learnable only by networks whose depth actually grows with the input dimension.
Critical Edge
The impossibility result excludes ReLU outright, and the log-depth learner that succeeds trains via bespoke layerwise coordinate descent — not standard end-to-end SGD on a normal MLP.

The separation needs a function class that is provably learnable in log-depth yet defeats every constant-depth competitor. Hierarchical staircase functions HSL,D,m,κ build a Boolean function's Fourier spectrum inductively: at each of L levels, up to D previously-discovered low-degree terms can be symmetric-differenced together into a new higher-order term, kept only if it lies in the target spectrum. Unlike classical staircase/merged-staircase functions, degree can grow exponentially with depth and there is no cap on the number of relevant coordinates. The concrete witness is deep quadratic functions: coordinates are paired and multiplied, then the dimension halves at each of L* layers — producing a staircase function of depth L*+1, local degree 2, width d−1, with at least 1/3 of its Fourier mass sitting at degree ≥ √d, exactly the mass no constant-depth network can reach.

Results & Highlights

No experiments anywhere in the paper — every "result" below is a proved theorem or lemma, not a measurement.

The three formal results that build the separation

ResultInformal statementRole
Lemma 2.2 Any constant-depth, poly-width network with spectrally-bounded weights + regular activation is uniformly approximable by a polynomial of degree ≪ d1/2. Collapses "any deep-looking computation" to a low-degree polynomial.
Thm 2.1 / 4.1(a) For any Boolean f* with ≥1/3 Fourier mass at degree ≥ d1/2 (deep quadratic functions qualify), every network under Assumption 2.1 has L² error ≥ 1/4. The actual impossibility result for constant depth.
Thm 3.1 An (L+1)-layer network trained via layerwise one-pass coordinate descent (Algorithm 1) learns any f ∈ HSL,D,m,κ to L² error ε* with sample/time cost poly(d, L, m, 1/κ). Shows logarithmic depth suffices — completes the separation.

Strengths

  • First rigorous algorithmic (not just approximation-theoretic) separation between constant- and log-depth networks — prior results only covered 2–3 layer comparisons.
  • A strictly more general function class (hierarchical staircase functions) with a clean, minimal witness (deep quadratic functions) that's easy to state and analyze.
  • Self-contained, technically sophisticated lower-bound proof (layerwise Chebyshev/Bernstein-ellipse approximation) that names and resolves a specific failure mode of naive global chaining arguments.
  • Careful, transparent positioning against closely related prior work (ABAB+21, AAM22/23, RDKL26, WNL24/FWNL25), stating exactly what's reused vs. newly proved.
  • Explicit AI-use disclosure: GPT used for preliminary calculations and sanity checks, with all final arguments independently re-derived and verified by the authors.

Limitations

  • Purely theoretical — no experiments, simulations, or empirical validation of any asymptotic prediction, even at small scale.
  • The activation-regularity assumption behind the lower bound excludes ReLU, the dominant activation in practice, limiting its practical reach.
  • The successful log-depth learner uses layerwise, one-pass coordinate descent on a bespoke polynomial-feature-plus-selection architecture — not end-to-end SGD on a standard MLP.
  • Requires a regularity exponent c0 < 2−2L−7/L that shrinks rapidly with depth, with no evidence it's realistic under ordinary training dynamics.
  • The local degree D (features combined per step) is fixed at O(1); the framework doesn't address D growing with depth or log d.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Can the impossibility result be extended to ReLU networks, or does ReLU's non-analyticity require an entirely different proof technique?
  • Can the upper-bound learner be replaced by a standard MLP trained end-to-end via SGD/backpropagation while keeping polynomial sample/time complexity?
  • Would small-scale experiments (e.g., d = 2L for L = 3, 4, 5) empirically confirm the predicted constant- vs. log-depth gap?
  • Does the shallow-to-deep chaining principle from RDKL26 generalize to a continuous depth-vs-accuracy tradeoff instead of a binary separation?
  • Would letting the local degree D grow with depth yield stronger separations, or break Theorem 3.1's polynomial learnability guarantee?
  • How restrictive is the shrinking exponent c0 < 2−2L−7/L relative to weight spectral norms actually observed under real training with modern initialization/feature-learning scalings (e.g., μP)?
