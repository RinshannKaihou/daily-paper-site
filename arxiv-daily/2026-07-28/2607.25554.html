Distilling Temporal Search and Reasoning: Evolving LLMs for Future Prediction via Harness-Assisted Efficient Data Synthesis

Wanxu Cai, Zhengyu Chen, Huaisheng Zhu, Wei Wang, Jingang Wang, Qiang Xu — Chinese University of Hong Kong & Meituan LongCat Team.

Key Finding A time-truncation harness restricts search to a pre-event cutoff, generating leak-free forecasting trajectories without rejection sampling or waiting for results.

论文提出"时间截断硬件"，在教师模型检索历史事件时动态屏蔽未来发布的文档，从而合成无泄漏的时序搜索与推理训练数据。

Key Metric
0/200

date-level leakage in the 200-sample audit — down from 181/200 without the harness. Valid-trajectory yield jumps 7.0% → 88.5% (≈12.6×).

Why This Matters

Constraining the retrieval environment — instead of filtering trajectories after the fact — offers a reusable, lightweight recipe for building leak-free forecasting training data.

Critical Edge

All comparisons are internal ablations — the paper explicitly declines to benchmark against the SOTA agent-harness systems (Milkyway, FutureWorld, Echo) it cites as related work.

Methodology

retrievable by teacher (≤ T_cut) blocked / would leak the answer (≥ T_cut)

TIR trajectories interleave reasoning, tool calls, and observations. Prior forecasting pipelines either freeze retrieval into a static context (losing the ability to re-query) or run rejection sampling over an unconstrained environment — checking each finished trajectory for temporal leakage and discarding most of what fails, which collapses throughput.

The harness instead constrains the environment itself: for each historical Manifold Market query with known resolution date T_end, it fixes a cutoff T_cut = T_end − 3 days and swaps in a truncated search environment that only returns documents published before T_cut — enforced both via API-level query rewriting (before: / endDate: filters) and a post-hoc datePublished filter. An aligned system prompt tells the teacher (Kimi-K2.5) to treat T_cut as "now."

The resulting leak-free trajectories, mixed with general deep-search data (50K instances total per group), are used to full-parameter SFT Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-32B.

On easier L1–L2 tasks the harness variant shows no consistent gain and sometimes underperforms (e.g. Qwen3-32B, L1, April: DeepSearch-SFT 73.17 vs. Mix-SFT w/ harness 70.85) — attributed to unneeded context noise from enforced broad search on simple queries.

The truncated environment — not the prompt alone — is the primary driver of both the accuracy drop and the temporal-breadth expansion. A ΔT sweep shows ΔT=0 underperforms (78.78% accuracy, 181-day span) while ΔT≥3 is stable, motivating the paper's ΔT=3 choice.

Strengths

  • Measures leakage reduction with an independent two-step audit (metadata + Claude-Opus-4.8 content review) rather than asserting it.
  • Ablation and ΔT sensitivity studies isolate which harness component drives the effect and justify the chosen hyperparameter.
  • Introduces an interpretable process metric (Query Date-Span) validated to correlate with downstream performance in both teacher and distilled students.
  • Reports negative/mixed results honestly (FutureX L1–L2 degradation, residual content-leak, no SOTA comparison) rather than cherry-picking.

Limitations

  • Synthetic data quality is capped by teacher (Kimi-K2.5) forecasting and search competence — its blind spots propagate to students.
  • Evaluation is internal ablations only; no reproduction of competing agent-harness pipelines (Milkyway, FutureWorld, Echo, UniPat-AI).
  • Mitigates rather than eliminates leakage — 23/200 residual content-level leakage remains even with the harness.
  • Gains don't generalize to easy/low-information-need tasks (FutureX L1–L2), where enforced broad search can hurt accuracy.
  • Experiments cover only two scales (8B, 32B); further scaling is flagged as future work, not verified.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Would combining the harness with online RL reduce the reward-sparsity problem afflicting FutureWorld/Echo-style approaches, and how would training-loop latency compare?
  2. How sensitive are the reported gains to the teacher model (Kimi-K2.5) — would a weaker or stronger teacher shift the relative benefit?
  3. Could the residual 23/200 content-level leakage be closed with a lightweight rubric filter without re-introducing the throughput collapse the harness avoids?
  4. Why does the harness underperform on FutureX L1–L2 easy tasks, and could a difficulty-adaptive cutoff recover that gap without sacrificing L3–L4 gains?
  5. How would this approach compare directly against the SOTA agent-harness baselines (Milkyway, Echo, FutureWorld) the paper explicitly declines to reproduce?
