> **一句话总结：** 作者把同一个 checkpoint 分别用 ExecuTorch、LiteRT、ONNX Runtime、TensorRT 的官方 FP32/INT8 配方转换后逐样本对比 top-1 决策，发现精度不变（ResNet 50 仅 −0.15 pp）的 INT8 转换仍会翻转 2.42%–4.07% 的个体判断，而"从源模型选 200 个最小 margin 输入"的探针集能以 40/40 召回率抓出全部有翻转的栈。

> **TL;DR:** On 7,854 ImageNet/Imagewoof images and 1,938 SST-2/Rotten-Tomatoes sentences, all 34 strict FP32 conversions reproduce every PyTorch decision, but ONNX Runtime's documented static-QDQ INT8 recipe on ResNet 50 flips **4.07% [3.65, 4.53]** of decisions while top-1 accuracy moves only **−0.15 pp** (within the preregistered ±1 pp equivalence band) — more than the 3.44% [3.06, 3.88] that two different 256-image calibration draws of the *same* recipe disagree on. A 200-example lowest-source-margin probe set ranks stacks like the full evaluation set (Spearman 0.988 vision / 0.999 text) and flags all 40 vision + 6 text flipping stacks with zero false alarms, versus 200 random probes that reach 0.95/0.67 recall and Spearman 0.958/0.838.

## 核心贡献

- **量化"决策翻转"而非只看精度。** 在 5 个公开 checkpoint（MobileNetV3-Large、EfficientNet-B0、ResNet 50、DeiT-tiny、SST-2 微调的 DistilBERT）、4 个转换器、共 116 个栈上，逐输入比对 top-1 决策与 PyTorch FP32 参考，并按输入做 2000 次 bootstrap 给置信区间。
- **先证明仪器读数为零。** K1（重跑参考）逐比特一致；K2（batch 32、单线程、PyTorch 2.14、CUDA FP32）零翻转；K3 的 34 个严格 FP32 转换全部零翻转（单侧 95% Clopper-Pearson 上界 0.04%/栈，文本 0.15%），最大 logit 差 4.5×10⁻³。
- **关键实证：精度等价 ≠ 决策等价。** 4 个保持精度的视觉 INT8 主配方全部翻转 >1% 的输入，最高为 ResNet 50 上的 ONNX Runtime 4.07%；而 MobileNetV3/EfficientNet 上的 INT8 是直接把精度打崩（最多 −84.25 pp），属于任何精度检查都能发现的一类。
- **提出"从源模型选探针"的廉价验收流程。** 在转换之前用 PyTorch 输出挑出 200 个 margin 最小的输入，只要"任一探针翻转"就拒绝该栈：视觉 40/40、文本 6/6 召回，27+8 个精确 FP32 转换零误报，且探针翻转率对栈的排序与全量评测几乎一致。
- **诚实报告被证伪的预注册假设。** C1、C3 成立，C2（margin 对每种配方都预测翻转、且不同转换器翻转集重叠）在 MobileNetV3 与 EfficientNet 上被推翻，作者照实写明并解释成因（一个翻转大半输入的配方，其 lift 上界必然趋近 1）。
- **顺手记录 5 个转换器缺陷**（B1–B5），其中两个"导出通过、加载才炸"，并附最小复现。

## Key Contributions

- **Reframes conversion acceptance as a per-input decision-agreement problem.** Accuracy is a scalar summary in which errors in both directions cancel: the ONNX Runtime INT8 build of ResNet 50 fixes 102 source errors and introduces 114, hiding a 4.07% churn behind a −0.15 pp accuracy delta.
- **An instrument-first protocol.** Before any claim the paper establishes a zero noise floor: bitwise-identical reference reruns, invariance to batch size / thread count / PyTorch version / CUDA FP32, and 34 strict FP32 conversions with zero flips. It also exposes a false "FP32" default: TensorRT's default build enables TF32 and flips 9 MobileNetV3 decisions (0.11%, upper bound 0.20%).
- **A calibration-redraw floor for INT8 rates.** Because two disjoint 256-input calibration draws of the *same* recipe disagree on 3.44% (ResNet 50, ONNX Runtime) and 4.16% (DeiT, LiteRT) of inputs, the authors argue a flip rate is a property of "recipe + calibration sample", not of the converter alone — and report that floor next to every rate.
- **Failure taxonomy by regime.** Equivalent-accuracy recipes (ResNet 50, DeiT; three text recipes) flip a few percent of decisions; non-equivalent recipes (MobileNetV3, EfficientNet, ONNX static QDQ on DistilBERT) lose 2.57–84.25 pp and flip 10–98% of inputs. The margin mechanism explains flips only in the first regime.
- **A cheap, source-only conformance test.** 200 lowest-margin inputs from a disjoint 2,000-example probe pool, chosen before conversion, reproduce the full-set stack ranking and catch every stack above a 0.5% flip rate, with no false alarm on any exact conversion.
- **Five documented-recipe defects** (ExecuTorch integer-scalar lifting, LiteRT nonzero-zero-point int8 embedding, coremltools `new_ones`/`where`, ExecuTorch ViT patch-embedding transpose refusal, TensorRT ONNX parser 0-d per-tensor `DequantizeLinear`), two of which are silent at export and fail only at load.

## Methodology

**Objects under test.** Five public checkpoints: MobileNetV3-Large, EfficientNet-B0 and ResNet 50 (torchvision), DeiT-tiny, and DistilBERT fine-tuned on SST-2. Vision models are scored on the validation splits of ImageNet and Imagewoof restricted to 20 classes (10 "easy" ImageNet classes + 10 fine-grained dog classes), 7,854 images total, with the full 1000-way output head; DistilBERT on SST-2 validation plus Rotten Tomatoes test, 1,938 sentences.

**Converters and recipes.** Each converter runs its documented path: ONNX Runtime 1.29 (FP32 CPU/CUDA, static per-channel MinMax QDQ, dynamic); TensorRT 10.16 (FP32, default TF32, FP16, explicit INT8 from a symmetric QDQ graph); ExecuTorch 1.2 (XNNPACK delegate FP32 + PT2E INT8, plus portable kernels); LiteRT via `litert_torch` (FP32, PT2E static/dynamic INT8, Quantizer recipe with int8 weights + float activations). Core ML via coremltools is counted as conversion success only, since it cannot execute off macOS. 116 stacks in total; stacks that fail to build or load are excluded from rates and listed as defects.

**Reference and flip definition.** The reference is PyTorch 2.11 eager, CPU, FP32, batch size 1. A flip is any input whose top-1 class under a converted stack differs from the reference on the same input; the flip rate is flips/inputs. Preprocessing is computed once in numpy so every runtime receives bit-identical float32 tensors.

**Statistics.** Intervals are bootstrap over inputs (2,000 resamples); zero counts get the exact one-sided 95% Clopper-Pearson bound. Accuracy is called equivalent when the paired 95% interval of its change lies within ±1 pp. Preregistration fixes three claims (C1: ≥half of accuracy-equivalent vision INT8 recipes flip >1% with lower bound above 5× the FP32 noise floor; C2: margin AUROC ≥0.85 for every primary recipe and flip-set lift ≥3 for every pair; C3: 200 lowest-margin probes rank stacks with Spearman ≥0.9 and catch every stack above 0.5% with ≤1 false alarm), along with thresholds and the stack list, before the main runs.

**Probe set.** Calibration uses two disjoint draws of 256 training inputs; a further 2,000 training inputs form the probe pool; the probe set is the 200 pool examples with the smallest source margin (top-1 minus top-2 probability), selected from PyTorch outputs before any conversion. All three sets are disjoint from evaluation.

**Exploratory op-type ablation.** For ONNX Runtime QDQ, one op type at a time is kept in float (all other scales unchanged) on up to 600 flipped and 600 unflipped evaluation inputs per model, with the calibration-redraw repair share as the null.

## Results & Highlights

- **The instrument reads zero where it should.** All 34 strict FP32 conversions flip **0 of 7,854** decisions (vision) and **0 of 1,938** (text), with one-sided 95% Clopper-Pearson bounds of **0.04%** and **0.15%** respectively; the largest logit difference across them is **4.5×10⁻³**. The exception is TensorRT's default build, which allows TF32 and flips **9 MobileNetV3 decisions (0.11%, bound 0.20%)** — and 3 / 1 / 5 on EfficientNet / ResNet 50 / DeiT — i.e. a team that converts "in FP32" with defaults has already flipped decisions.
- **C1 holds: accuracy-equivalent INT8 still flips 2.4–4.1% of decisions.** On ResNet 50, ONNX Runtime static QDQ flips **4.07% [3.65, 4.53]** at **−0.15 pp [−0.51, +0.22]** accuracy change, ExecuTorch **2.48% [2.16, 2.84]** at −0.48 pp, LiteRT **2.42% [2.10, 2.78]** at −0.42 pp; on DeiT, LiteRT flips **3.65% [3.28, 4.07]** at **−0.06 pp**. All four equivalent primary recipes have a flip-rate lower bound above 1% against an FP32 noise floor of exactly zero.
- **The calibration draw is as noisy as the effect.** Two builds of the *same* INT8 recipe from two disjoint 256-input draws disagree with each other on **3.44% [3.06, 3.88]** of ResNet 50 inputs (ONNX Runtime) and **4.16% [3.74, 4.63]** of DeiT inputs (LiteRT) — the ResNet number is 85% of the 4.07% headline. On MobileNetV3 the redraw rate is 29.25% and on EfficientNet 67.90–93.54%, i.e. noisier than the accuracy collapse itself in relative terms.
- **C2 is refuted where accuracy breaks.** The preregistered AUROC ≥0.85 for the negative source margin fails on MobileNetV3 (**0.68–0.92**) and EfficientNet (**0.63–0.84**), and the lift ≥3 requirement fails there too, with pairs reaching **1.2 [1.2, 1.3]** and **1.0 [1.0, 1.0]**. It holds where accuracy is kept: ResNet 50 AUROC **0.90–0.97** (all CIs inside [0.89, 0.98]) with every pair's lift ≥ **6.8 [6.0, 7.6]**, and ExecuTorch/LiteRT sharing ResNet 50 flips **31.8× [28.2, 36.0]** more often than independence; DeiT AUROC 0.92–0.96 with **84% [80, 88]** of LiteRT flips in the lowest margin decile.
- **C3 holds, but the margin over random probes is mostly in ranking.** The 200 low-margin probes give Spearman **0.988** (vision, 75 non-control stacks) and **0.999** (text) against the full evaluation set, and flag all **40 of 40** vision stacks (recall CI [0.91, 1.00]) and **6 of 6** text stacks ([0.54, 1.00]) with an evaluation flip rate ≥0.5%, with **zero** false alarms on the 27 vision + 8 text exact FP32 conversions. 200 *random* probes reach recall **0.95 [0.83, 0.99]** (vision) and **0.67 [0.22, 0.96]** (text) but Spearman only **0.958 / 0.838**; a concrete gap: LiteRT INT8 flips **51 of the 200 low-margin ResNet 50 probes** versus **10 of 200 random** ones.
- **The two regimes are far apart.** MobileNetV3 INT8 accuracy changes range **−2.57 to −55.35 pp** (flip rates 10.19–73.07%) and EfficientNet **−9.33 to −84.25 pp** (16.97–98.13%); the same EfficientNet weights give **16.97%** flips under ExecuTorch but **98.13%** under TensorRT. On DistilBERT, the dynamic recipes stay equivalent at **0.26% [0.05, 0.52]** (ExecuTorch) and **0.67% [0.31, 1.03]** (LiteRT), while ONNX Runtime static QDQ breaks the model at **−38.49 pp** with **47.16%** flips.
- **No single op type carries the flips.** Keeping every quantized op in float repairs **95.7%** (DeiT), **96.9%** (ResNet 50), **99.0%** (MobileNetV3) and **99.5%** (EfficientNet) of flips while adding ≤0.5% new ones, but no single type repairs more than **61%**: residual Add on ResNet 50 (61% vs 41% null), Mul nodes of hard-swish/SE on MobileNetV3 (61% vs 31%), Conv on EfficientNet (49% vs 10%) — Conv alone repairs ~half of flips on every CNN (50%, 49%, 49%).

## Authors & Credibility

Single author, **Sharven Rane**, independent researcher (no institutional affiliation listed), preprint dated November 2026, cross-listed cs.LG. Credibility signals that are unusually strong for a solo preprint: claims, thresholds and the exact stack list were **preregistered** before the main runs; the paper reports a **refuted** preregistered claim (C2) rather than reframing it post hoc; every rate carries a bootstrap interval and, for INT8, a calibration-redraw floor; controls K1–K4 are run and tabulated before any result; five converter defects come with minimal repros in companion code. The AI disclosure states an assistant wrote/debugged experiment code, drafted text, and searched related work, while every number is script-generated from saved outputs and every reference was opened and checked. Weak signals: no peer review, no second author or affiliation to vouch for the harness, and no external replication of the flip rates.

## Critical Assessment

The methodological discipline here is real — preregistration, a demonstrated zero noise floor, intervals on every rate, and a refuted hypothesis left standing — and the central observation (accuracy-equivalent INT8 churns 2.4–4.1% of decisions) is reproducible in kind by anyone with the four runtimes. But the paper's own controls cut into its headline more than the framing admits. On ResNet 50 the effect (4.07% flips vs. source) is barely larger than the *calibration lottery* (3.44% between two draws of the same recipe), so a large share of what is called "converter-induced disagreement" is really "which 256 images happened to be in the calibration set"; the authors acknowledge this in Limitation 4 but still lead the abstract with 4.07%, and with only two draws they can bound the noise, not average it out.

The claim structure is thinner than it looks. C1's "at least half of accuracy-equivalent vision primary recipes" is evaluated on **n = 4** stacks, all from just two models (ResNet 50 and DeiT) — the two architectures that happen to tolerate INT8. MobileNetV3 and EfficientNet, where every single recipe breaks accuracy, contribute nothing to C1, so the "4 of 4" is a statement about a hand-selected survivor set, not about INT8 conversion in general. Similarly, C3's text evidence rests on **6 positive stacks** and 1,938 sentences from one binary task, giving a recall CI of [0.54, 1.00] — "catches all 6" is compatible with a true recall near 55%.

The probe-set result is also weaker against its own baseline than the abstract suggests: 200 *random* probes already achieve 0.95 vision recall, so the "catches it" claim holds on vision mainly through ranking (0.988 vs 0.958 Spearman) and through the 51-vs-10 flip concentration, not through a recall gap. And there is no comparison against any other cheap detector — not DiffChaser-style synthetic disagreement search (cited but never run), not a KL/logit-drift check on random inputs, not per-channel scale sensitivity. The only baseline is random sampling.

Three further scope limits matter for how far the numbers travel. (1) ExecuTorch and LiteRT ran their **x86 CPU kernels**, so the "on device" framing is a proxy; ARM rounding could move rates in an unknown direction. (2) Core ML is conversion-only, so of the "four converters" only three are measured for flips. (3) Vision evaluation uses **20 of 1,000 classes** (10 easy + 10 fine-grained dogs), which changes the margin distribution in both directions relative to full ImageNet — the authors concede flip rates on the full set are "likely" higher, which means neither 4.07% nor 2.42% is a transferable number. Finally, all recipes are each converter's *documented default*; the paper concedes tuned recipes (per-layer exclusion, more calibration data) "would likely flip fewer inputs", making every reported rate an upper bound on what a careful deployment team would actually see.

None of this invalidates the qualitative conclusion, which is the useful part: top-1 accuracy is not a certificate of conversion fidelity, and a 200-example margin probe is nearly free. The paper would be stronger with a third calibration draw to separate recipe from sample, per-class breakdowns on the full 1000-class head, and at least one non-random detector baseline.

## Strengths

- **Preregistration with a published refutation.** C2 is stated precisely (AUROC ≥0.85, lift ≥3), fails on two models, and the failure is reported with the mechanism (a recipe that flips most inputs has a lift ceiling near 1) rather than buried.
- **Instrument-first structure.** K1–K4 establish bitwise reference reproducibility, invariance to batch size / threads / framework version / CUDA FP32, a zero FP32 conversion floor, and a calibration-redraw floor *before* any claim. This is the right order and rare in quantization papers.
- **The calibration-redraw control is a genuinely useful methodological contribution** — a specific, cheap number (3.44% on ResNet 50) that any team can compute to know whether their observed churn is signal or sampling.
- **Intervals and exact bounds everywhere**, including exact one-sided Clopper-Pearson at zero counts, so "0 of 7,854" is reported as "≤0.04%" rather than as proof of identity.
- **Actionable, almost free artifact:** the probe set needs only source-model logits on 200 training examples, chosen before conversion; it plugs into a release pipeline as a single boolean gate and is shown to have no false alarms on exact conversions.
- **Honest, specific limitations** (phone kernels unmeasured, Core ML unexecuted, default recipes only, two calibration draws, 20-class data, missing rows from B4/B5) and a defect log with minimal repros that is independently useful to the four converter teams.

## Limitations

- **CPU proxy for phone kernels.** ExecuTorch and LiteRT ran x86 CPU kernels; ARM kernel rounding differs and the direction of the effect is unknown, so "on-device" claims are indirect. ExecuTorch portable kernels also skipped ResNet 50 entirely (hours per pass).
- **Core ML never executed.** It enters only as conversion success, so one of the four advertised converters has no flip rate at all.
- **Calibration noise is the same order as the effect,** and with two draws it can be bounded but not averaged; single-build rates may be biased by up to the redraw rate in either direction.
- **Default recipes only.** Each converter's documented default is used; tuned recipes would presumably flip less, so all reported rates are upper bounds.
- **Narrow data.** 20 ImageNet classes (7,854 images) and one binary sentiment task (1,938 sentences); per-class difficulty on the full 1000-class head differs, likely raising flip rates, and nothing here covers LLM decoders, detection, or generation.
- **Thin claim support in places:** 4 equivalent vision stacks, 6 positive text stacks, and a probe-set baseline that is only random sampling.
- **Missing rows** from defects B4/B5 remove ExecuTorch INT8 on DeiT and TensorRT INT8 on DistilBERT, so the cross-converter comparison is unbalanced exactly on the transformer vision model.

## Relevance

直接命中"算子精度、确定性与误差传播"核心议题：这是跨转换器/跨内核的数值漂移与可复现推理的实测研究，并把"top-1 精度等价"这一常见验收标准证伪为不足。对做量化后可靠性（INT8 PTQ 退化）与推理一致性验证的工作同样有直接参考价值，尤其是那套几乎零成本的 200 样本探针验收流程。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. How much of the 4.07% ResNet 50 flip rate survives a third and fourth calibration draw — i.e. what is the *expected* flip rate over the calibration distribution rather than two point estimates?
2. Do the reported rates and the probe-set recall hold on the full 1,000-class ImageNet validation set, and does the 20-class subset bias flips upward (fine-grained dogs) or downward (easy classes)?
3. Would the same 200-probe recipe work for an LLM decoder, where the "decision" is a token and margins are computed over a 100k-way vocabulary — and does flipping a low-margin token compound autoregressively?
4. How do ARM/NEON kernels on an actual phone shift the rates relative to the x86 proxy, and does the probe ranking (Spearman 0.988) survive that shift?
5. Can per-layer exclusions or larger calibration sets push the flip rate below the calibration-redraw floor, and if so is the floor itself the practical lower bound on conversion fidelity?
6. Does the probe set transfer across checkpoints of the same architecture (select once, reuse), or must it be recomputed per model as the paper's source-only procedure implies?
