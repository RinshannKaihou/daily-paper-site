Why this matters

If a pipeline picks winners with a quantized reward model, its acceptance test can pass while 1–12% of those choices silently change.

Critical edge

In 22 of 30 settings the accuracy drop is statistically significant — accuracy looks blind only when harmful and helpful flips nearly cancel.

Methodology

A one-parameter theory, held out honestly

Eight scalar reward models (Skywork-Reward-V2 and GRM, 0.6B–8B), three pairwise generative judges and four weight-only 4-bit schemes — NF4, FP4, INT4-RTN, GPTQ. Only linear layers are quantized; BF16 is treated as ground truth, so the object of study is what quantization changes, not what is correct.

P(flip | m) = Φ(−|m| / σ) m = sa − sb; σ = std(mquant − mbf16)
  • A flip is a sign change in m. Flips split into harmful (BF16 right, quantized wrong) and helpful, tested with exact McNemar and a problem-level clustered bootstrap (2,000 resamples).
  • σ is estimated on even-indexed RewardBench pairs and scored on odd-indexed ones — the "one parameter, never fit to flip data" claim is actually held out.
  • Downstream, 1,000 MATH-500/GSM8K problems × 64 candidates with mechanically verified correctness separate "different selection" from "different answer".

The pattern is not "quantization is harmless". It is that the aggregate score is a net number: near-symmetric harm and help can cancel almost exactly while the decision function underneath has been rewritten — 119 rewritten judgements bought with three hundredths of a point.

Counter-evidence

GPTQ: better reconstruction, more churn

9.18%flips vs 7.04% for INT4-RTN

On Llama-3.2-1B, GPTQ reconstructs activations 3–5× better (median error ratio 0.204–0.329 over eight layers) yet loses 4.22 pts instead of 1.54 — more decision churn from the quantizer that is closer in weight space.

The paper gives this one paragraph. It undermines reconstruction-error-based quantizer selection and is the sharpest unasked question in the study.

Strengths

What holds up

  • The net-versus-gross reframing is simple, correct and immediately actionable: accuracy answers "how many right", not "is this the same evaluator".
  • Falsifiable theory with a genuinely held-out test — σ on even pairs, prediction on odd pairs, conservative in 30/30 settings.
  • A mechanistic size story: occupancy and σ/std(m) both shrink with scale, and occupancy beats raw σ at predicting flips.
  • The downstream decomposition (selection identity vs final answer) is the practitioner artifact, and the 5.3–7.7× ratio replicates across two families and three quantizers.
  • Uncommon honesty: three negative results, one retracted claim, a claim-to-evidence table and BH-corrected significance marks.

Limitations

Where it stops

  • Downstream is math-only (MATH-500, GSM8K), one generator, one same-family arbiter — answer-equivalence would be rarer in code, chat or agentic selection.
  • Six (model, quantizer) pairs for "churn ranks downstream"; with n = 6 the ρ gap is one rank inversion, not a demonstrated ordering.
  • Weight-only 4-bit only: no 8-bit, AWQ/HQQ, group-size or clipping sweep, so "quantizer choice matters more than model choice" rests on four schemes.
  • The 20–45% envelope over-prediction is unexplained, and cross-dataset transfer is measured in exactly two directions.
  • No measured latency or energy; the 52.5% cost saving is a scoring-throughput extrapolation at R = 5.03 (and zero for GRM, R = 1.00).
  • Heavy LLM involvement in the analysis pipeline is disclosed — no experimental data were generated — but not independently audited.

For further exploration

5 open threads from the paper
  1. Does the 5–8× selection-to-answer ratio hold outside math — code with unit-test correctness, agentic tool selection — where "answer-equivalent" candidates should be rarer?
  2. Why does GPTQ churn more decisions than INT4-RTN despite 3–5× lower activation reconstruction error: outlier layers, calibration mismatch, or is reconstruction error simply the wrong objective for argmax stability?
  3. Can a quantizer be trained against a decision-margin objective that beats INT4-RTN, rather than merely shrinking σ? The MarginTune null on GPTQ suggests structural residual noise.
  4. How does the window interact with reward-model ensembles and best-of-N overoptimization — does ensembling shrink σ/std(m) the way parameter count does, and what happens to effective sample size when the selector is noisy rather than biased?
  5. What is the right production acceptance test for a quantized judge: a churn budget on a stratified low-margin slice, answer-level disagreement, or a paired test against full precision at a pre-registered tolerance?
