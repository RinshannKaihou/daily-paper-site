> **一句话总结：** 在统一的冷启动 GRPO 协议下扫描 LoRA 秩（r=1→256），作者发现 Qwen3-8B 在 DeepMath-Hard 上从 r=64 的 73.1% 骤降到 r=128 的 4.7%（"秩崩塌"），而仅训练 <0.6% 参数的结构化适配器 DoRA r=16 与 QuanTA d=3 在 seed 42 上都达到 84.0%，超过全量微调的 77.3%；配套的谱分析显示真正起作用的是更新几何（有效秩分布），而非名义秩或可训练参数量。

> **TL;DR:** Under a shared one-epoch cold-start GRPO recipe (Qwen3-8B, DeepMath-Hard, global batch 64), vanilla LoRA is flat at 79.29% for r=1–4 and 73.1% at r=64, then falls off a cliff to 4.7% at r=128 and 2.3% at r=256, while DoRA r=16 and QuanTA d=3 reach 84.0% vs 77.3% for full fine-tuning on seed 42 — but across seeds 42–44 that becomes 74.4% ± 10.9 and 75.1% ± 9.6 vs 73.16% ± 7.55, i.e. a ~+2 pt mean edge inside the noise. The transferable message is not "adapters beat FFT" but "high-rank LoRA can fail to ignite under cold-start RLVR, larger batches make it worse rather than better (r=64: 73.1% → 8.7% → 6.0% at batch 64/128/256), and the useful variable is the geometry of the update, not its parameter count."

## 核心贡献

- **把"秩崩塌"定义为一个优化稳定性边界，而不是容量饱和。** 在 r ∈ {1,4,8,16,32,64,128,256} 上系统扫描：低秩区间（76.2%–79.29%）几乎平坦，r=64 降到 73.1%，r=128/r=256 直接跌到 4.7%/2.3% 的地板。这与 SFT 文献"高秩收益递减"的图景在性质上不同——不是平台期，而是断崖。
- **证明"加采样"不是万能稳定器。** 全局 batch 从 64 加到 128/256 不但没有救回高秩 LoRA，反而把安全秩边界整体下推：r=64 在 BS 128/256 上只剩 8.7%/6.0%；GRPO group size G ∈ {4,8,16} 能改善个别配置（r=8 从 75.0% 到 78.7%），但 r=256 始终在地板附近（2.3%→6.6%）。
- **指出"全量微调的有效秩悖论"。** 用 participation ratio ρ = exp(H(p)) 度量学习到的 ΔW 的有效秩：FFT 更新了全部约 8B 参数，其更新却比只动 0.56% 参数的 DoRA/QuanTA 更"低秩"；而崩塌的 LoRA r=256（ρ=17.956）有效秩反而高于可用的 r=64（ρ=13.807）。因此名义秩、有效秩、参数量都不是充分统计量，起作用的是参数化所诱导的**可达更新流形几何**。
- **提出可证伪的"模型成熟度假说"（Model Maturity Hypothesis）。** 三个 8B 基座模型呈现清晰二分：两个约 15T token 的模型（Apertus 8B、Llama 3.1 8B）在 DeepMath-Hard 上无论用什么适配器都徘徊在 2–4% 地板，且冻结权重的奇异值谱衰减陡峭、彼此几乎重合；约 36T token 的 Qwen3-8B 谱明显更平坦，并在结构化适配器下拿到 78–84%。作者把它作为假说而非因果结论提出。
- **给出参数高效 RLVR 的实践建议。** 秩应当被当作优化变量而非容量旋钮；在 batch-64 冷启动设定下常数学习率会导致策略直接崩掉（附录 F：QuanTA 在 step 200 附近达峰、step 280 归零），必须用带 warmup 的 cosine 衰减；DoRA/QuanTA 这类结构化适配器在更宽的测试区域内保持可训练。

## Key Contributions

- **Rank collapse as an optimization-stability boundary.** A systematic LoRA rank sweep r ∈ {1,4,8,16,32,64,128,256} on Qwen3-8B shows a qualitatively abrupt transition from a broadly stable low-rank regime to near-floor accuracy at high rank — not the diminishing-returns plateau the SFT literature predicts.
- **Sampling scale is not a universal fix.** Neither larger global batches (64 → 128 → 256) nor larger GRPO groups (G ∈ {4,8,16}) rescue high-rank LoRA; larger batches instead move the safe-rank boundary downward, and the key rank–batch boundary replicates at seed 43.
- **The FFT effective-rank paradox.** Full fine-tuning, despite updating ~8B parameters, produces lower-effective-rank learned updates than DoRA/QuanTA, which update <0.6% of parameters. Collapsed high-rank LoRA likewise realizes only ~7–8% of its nominal rank, so effective rank is diagnostic but not determinative.
- **A head-to-head structured-adapter comparison under one shared cold-start GRPO pipeline**, with multi-seed evaluation, rank/batch/group-size sweeps, target-module ablations, and SVD diagnostics across every resulting checkpoint.
- **The Model Maturity Hypothesis**, a falsifiable claim that the update geometry best suited to RLVR may depend on structure acquired during pre-training, supported by a concordant behavioral and frozen-weight spectral partition across three independently developed 8B models.

## Methodology

**Base models.** Three dense 8B transformers spanning reported pre-training scale: Apertus 8B (~15T tokens, 131K vocab), Llama 3.1 8B (~15.6T, 128K), and Qwen3-8B (~36T, 150K). All runs start from the **base** checkpoint (Base-to-RL), deliberately skipping SFT so that the RL signal, the pre-trained weight structure, and the adapter parameterization are the only variables in play.

**Adapters.** LoRA with ΔW = BA applied to all linear modules, α = 32 fixed with standard α/r scaling, sweeping r ∈ {1,4,8,16,32,64,128,256} (2.7M–696M trainable params on Qwen3-8B). DoRA, which decomposes W = ‖W₀‖_c · (W₀+ΔW)/‖W₀+ΔW‖_c, sweeping r ∈ {8,16,32,64} (23M–180M). QuanTA, which parameterizes ΔW as a Matrix Product Operator borrowed from tensor-network physics, in two settings: d=4 with decomposition [16,8,8,4] (7.7M params) and d=3 with [16,16,16] (35.3M). FFT updates all ~8B parameters. Every configuration is bf16, dropout 0, no bias.

**Protocol.** GRPO with group size G=8, KL coefficient β=0, the Dr. GRPO token-level loss (to avoid length bias against long CoT), global batch 64, one epoch, AdamW at LR 1e-5, cosine decay with 0.1 warmup and 0.15 minimum LR ratio. Reward = R_acc + R_fmt: accuracy uses depth-aware `\boxed{}` extraction plus `math_verify` semantic equivalence (1.0/0.0); the format shaping term rewards a closed `</think>` tag (+0.375) and non-empty post-reasoning content (+0.375) while penalising hallucinated user turns (−0.75), empty thinking (−0.6), n-gram repetition (−0.5) and malformed XML (−0.3), capped at 0.75 so it can never dominate correctness.

**Benchmarks.** DeepMath-Hard (5,399 problems from DeepMath-103K with difficulty ≥ 8.5/10) is the primary discriminative set; Skywork-Hard (6,702 problems where DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B scores below 19%) is the frontier stress test; MATH is a standard reference; Enigmata 10K is a logic/puzzle domain probe evaluated on the disjoint official split; and merged AIME 2025–2026 (60 problems, Avg@32) is the out-of-distribution probe for DeepMath-trained checkpoints.

**Spectral diagnostics.** For each learned ΔW, effective rank is the participation ratio ρ = exp(H(p)), where p is the normalized distribution of squared singular values and H is Shannon entropy, averaged across target layers and compared against the frozen base weights' singular-value decay.

## Results & Highlights

- **The rank sweep itself (Qwen3-8B, DeepMath-Hard, seed 42, batch 64):** 79.29% at r=1, 79.29% at r=4, 78.1% at r=8, 76.2% at r=16, 77.3% at r=32, 73.1% at r=64, **4.7% at r=128, 2.3% at r=256**. The r=64 → r=128 transition alone is a 68.4-point drop, and the low-rank regime (r=1) already matches the best low-rank accuracy — most of the 79% is bought by learning format and reasoning scaffolding, not by rank.
- **Main comparison, seed 42:** DoRA r=16 and QuanTA d=3 (no_kv) both reach **84.0%** on DeepMath-Hard vs **77.3% for FFT** and 78.1% for LoRA r=8, while training 45.0M and 35.3M parameters respectively — 0.56% and 0.44% of the ~8B model. On MATH everything saturates at 78.9% except DoRA r=16 at 75.6%.
- **Multi-seed honesty check (seeds 42/43/44, DeepMath-Hard):** QuanTA d=3 no_kv **75.1% ± 9.6**, DoRA r=16 **74.4% ± 10.9**, FFT **73.16% ± 7.55**. The seed-42 +6.7-point structured-adapter lead shrinks to a +1.9-point mean gap well inside one standard deviation; seed 44 is the worst seed for almost every method, suggesting shared rather than parameterization-specific variance.
- **Batch-size ablation kills the "just add sampling" hypothesis:** LoRA r=64 goes 73.1% (BS 64, 674 steps) → **8.7%** (BS 128, 337 steps) → **6.0%** (BS 256, 168 steps), while QuanTA d=3 still reaches **65.8%** under the same 168-step budget and LoRA r=8 merely degrades to 20.3%. Under BS 256 train-time curves, r=64/r=128/r=256 stay near floor for the whole run (non-ignition), whereas r=8 and DoRA r=16 climb steadily to 20.3%/22.5% (update-limited).
- **Out-of-distribution (AIME 2025–2026, Avg@32, 60 problems):** DoRA r=16 **11.04%**, QuanTA d=3 **10.36%**, LoRA r=16 **9.95%**, but FFT collapses to **1.35%** despite scoring 77.3% in-distribution on DeepMath-Hard — a 9.7-point gap between FFT and the best adapter on the same checkpoint family.
- **Enigmata (train on Enigmata 10K, held-out pass@1):** QuanTA d=3 **20.6%** > FFT **19.1%** > DoRA r=16 **16.6%** > LoRA r=8 **11.8%** — the same qualitative ordering, an 8.8-point QuanTA-over-LoRA gap, on a non-mathematical reasoning domain.
- **Spectral evidence:** mean effective rank grows 4.709 (r=8) → 13.807 (r=64) → 17.956 (r=256), i.e. realized rank is ~59% of nominal at r=8 but only ~8% and ~7% at r=128/r=256; the collapsed r=256 checkpoint nonetheless has *higher* effective rank than the functional r=64 one. Batch scaling contracts the spectrum monotonically: r=8 4.709→4.180→3.983, r=64 13.807→10.275→8.861, r=256 17.956→13.106→11.264 (relative reductions of 15.4%, 35.8%, 37.3%).
- **Three-model split (DeepMath-Hard, seed 42):** Apertus 8B best LoRA 2.3% / best QuanTA 3.5% (Δ +1.2); Llama 3.1 8B 2.7% / 3.9% (Δ +1.2); Qwen3-8B 79.29% / 84.0% (Δ +4.71). Both ~15T models sit at the accuracy floor.
- **Efficiency (single seed, 4×H100, one epoch):** QuanTA d=3 7.5h and 35.3M params for 84.0%, vs DoRA r=16 15.6h and 45.0M params for 84.0%, vs LoRA r=8 13.1h and 22.0M params for 78.1%.

## Critical Assessment

**What is genuinely new.** The single most valuable artifact here is not the "adapters beat FFT" claim (which the authors themselves walk back in the multi-seed analysis) but the **rank-sweep shape**: a flat low-rank plateau followed by a near-total collapse at r≥128 under a cold-start RLVR protocol, plus the observation that larger global batches *lower* the safe-rank boundary instead of raising it. That is a concrete, reproducible, and mechanistically interesting instability, and reporting it as a rank–batch–optimization boundary rather than a universal rank cutoff is the right framing. The spectral analysis is used honestly too: the authors explicitly refuse the tempting story that effective rank predicts success, because the collapsed r=256 checkpoint (ρ=17.956) out-ranks the functional r=64 checkpoint (ρ=13.807). That is the kind of self-defeating result most papers would bury.

**Where it is thin.** (1) **The most obvious control is missing.** All LoRA runs fix α=32 with standard α/r scaling and a single LR of 1e-5; there is no rank-stabilized LoRA (rsLoRA), no PiSSA/OLoRA-style initialization, and no per-rank LR sweep. Without those, "high-rank LoRA collapses because of update *geometry*" is not separable from "high-rank LoRA collapses because our α/r convention plus a fixed 1e-5 LR produces a bad optimization landscape at r≥128." The paper acknowledges "rank-specific tuning could shift the observed boundaries" — but that acknowledgment is doing a lot of load-bearing work, since the entire mechanistic story rests on the collapse being parameterization-intrinsic. (2) **The cliff is suspiciously total.** A drop from 73.1% to 4.7% is not a capacity failure; it looks like the model stops emitting a parseable `</think>` structure or the policy entropy collapses. Train-time reward curves are shown only for the batch-256 runs (Appendix D); there is no divergence/entropy/format-compliance diagnostic at the headline r=128, batch-64 configuration, which is exactly where one would want to distinguish "failed to ignite" from "learned then unlearned." (3) **The headline number does not survive its own appendix.** Table 7's 84.0% vs 77.3% is a single seed; Table 15's three-seed means are 75.1/74.4/73.16 with stds of 9.6/10.9/7.55. The abstract is commendably calibrated ("competitive with FFT"), but the intro and the contributions list still lead with the seed-42 gap, and the seed-44 draw (FFT 64.45%, DoRA 62.5%, QuanTA 65.2%) suggests a shared bad-run effect that is never diagnosed. (4) **The Model Maturity Hypothesis is a 3-point, 2-vs-1 split.** "Both ~15T models agree in direction" means Apertus 2.3% vs 3.5% and Llama 2.7% vs 3.9% — a 1.2-point difference measured at the accuracy floor, where a model that fails to learn answer formatting scores ~0–4% regardless of adapter. Calling that "agreement" is generous; at floor, the ranking is essentially noise. (5) **The AIME result is over-read relative to its evidence.** FFT at 1.35% vs adapters at ~10% is presented as evidence that unconstrained updates are brittle OOD, but it is one seed, 60 problems, and the same FFT configuration shows 7.55-point seed variance in-distribution — a single unlucky FFT seed is a perfectly adequate explanation, and the authors' own hedging ("suggestive", "should be treated as suggestive rather than definitive") is correct but sits uneasily beside a table this striking. (6) **Skywork-Hard contributes no signal** (all methods 15–19%, stds 3.6–4.6) yet still appears in Table 7 where a reader will over-read 19.4 vs 19.0. (7) **QuanTA's main-table configuration is chosen post-hoc**: no_kv is the best seed-42 cell and also the best mean, but its mean advantage over target_linear (75.1 vs 74.1) is well inside a 9.6 std, so the "excluding key/value projections regularizes" interpretation is unfalsified rather than supported.

**Reproducibility.** Strong. Code, container spec, library versions, LR, batch, group size, reward weights, and the exact benchmark construction (difficulty ≥ 8.5 filter, 19% pass-rate filter for Skywork) are all given. The one gap is compute: multi-seed FFT and DoRA runs at 4×H100 are expensive, and the paper does not report total GPU-hours, so an independent replication of Table 15 is a non-trivial ask.

## Strengths

- A genuinely surprising and cleanly demonstrated failure mode (rank collapse) with a replicated rank–batch boundary across two seeds — exactly the kind of negative result the RLVR literature needs more of.
- Unusual intellectual honesty: the multi-seed table openly reports that the headline single-seed advantage is within noise, and the spectral analysis openly reports that its own proposed diagnostic (effective rank) fails to predict collapse.
- Complete, cheap-to-reuse experimental apparatus: one shared cold-start GRPO recipe across LoRA/DoRA/QuanTA/FFT, with code and container spec released.
- The FFT effective-rank paradox (full parameter budget, lower realized update rank than 0.5%-parameter adapters) is a memorable and testable observation.
- Practical, actionable guidance that contradicts a popular belief: bigger batches do not stabilize high-rank LoRA under fixed-pass cold-start RLVR, and constant LR schedules collapse outright at batch 64.
- The Enigmata probe, though single-seed, extends the qualitative ordering beyond mathematics at low additional cost.

## Limitations

- **Scale and architecture:** all experiments are 8B dense transformers on a single 4×H100 node; whether the rank–batch boundary survives at 30B+, or in MoE models, is untested.
- **Cold-start only:** Base-to-RL without SFT is a deliberate but narrow choice; SFT-to-RL pipelines (the dominant industrial recipe) start from a different weight state and may have entirely different stability boundaries.
- **One shared recipe, not per-configuration optima:** a single LR (1e-5), one schedule, one epoch, α=32 with α/r scaling for every rank. Rank-specific tuning and rank-stabilized variants are not explored, so the collapse cannot be attributed to geometry as opposed to the scaling convention.
- **Model Maturity Hypothesis is correlational:** three models, two of which share a cluster, with architecture, tokenizer, and data mixture fully confounded with pre-training token count. No intermediate checkpoints, no matched-architecture control.
- **Weak/near-floor benchmarks:** Apertus and Llama results (2–4%) are at the floor and carry little information; Skywork-Hard differences are inside noise; AIME is 60 problems and one seed per method.
- **No mechanistic diagnosis of the cliff:** no entropy, gradient-norm, format-compliance, or loss-curve analysis at the headline r=128/batch-64 configuration.

## Relevance

这篇论文直接对应「LLM 后训练 / 强化学习可验证奖励（RLVR）」这条主线：它给出了在资源受限条件下用参数高效适配器做 reasoning RL 的具体可行区间（LoRA 秩别往上堆、DoRA/QuanTA 更稳、必须用 cosine 衰减），并提供了可复用的谱诊断方法来判断"更新到底学到了什么"。对关心 GRPO 训练稳定性、PEFT 选型、以及"基座预训练程度如何影响微调策略"的读者，这是近期最值得精读的实证工作之一。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Is the r=128 cliff a property of LoRA's parameterization or of the α=32 / α/r + fixed-LR convention? A single rsLoRA or PiSSA run at r=128 with matched effective update scale would settle much of it.
2. What exactly happens at the cliff? Per-step format-compliance rate, policy entropy, gradient norm, and clip fraction at r=64 vs r=128 (batch 64) would distinguish "never ignites" from "ignites then destabilizes."
3. Does the rank–batch boundary move if the update budget is held constant rather than the epoch count? At batch 256 the model sees only 168 steps; a 674-step batch-256 run would separate "fewer updates" from "worse geometry."
4. Can the Model Maturity Hypothesis be tested with intermediate checkpoints of a single model family (e.g. Qwen3 at 5T/15T/36T, or OLMo 2 at successive stages)? That is the only design that disentangles token count from architecture.
5. Is the AIME collapse of FFT reproducible across seeds, and does it persist with a larger OOD suite (AMC, OlympiadBench, AIME 2024)?
6. Why does DoRA degrade gracefully to 75.0% at r=64 while LoRA falls to 4.7% at r=128 — is the magnitude/direction split acting as a trust-region constraint, and can that be measured directly (e.g. per-layer update norm ratios)?
7. Does the no_kv target-module advantage for QuanTA hold with more seeds, or is it a seed-42 artifact?
