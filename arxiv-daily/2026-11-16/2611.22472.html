Why it matters

Pruning stops being a gamble on text-heavy documents: ChartQA recovers from 52.2% to 71.9% of full accuracy while reading 2.5% of the tokens.

The critical edge

τ, ℓ and the b/ρ schedule were tuned on ten of the evaluation benchmarks, so the headline numbers carry a mild optimistic bias.

02

Why a kept-token detector cannot work

  • P1Break-even. Re-answering pays only if the mean net repair rate beats H̄/ρ; random selection cannot beat one pass when the score curve is concave on [b, 2K].
  • P2Blindness. A detector reading only kept tokens gives identical outputs on two questions with the same kept view, so it must err on one. A full-image checker’s flag error is bounded by γ.
  • C1Nested views. V(b) is a function of V(b′) for b < b′, so mutual information is monotone in budget and the bound holds at every k.
  • P3Ordering. Disagreement first, then low-to-high confidence, is optimal; any signal is capped by the top ρ|C| conditional gains.

04

Reading the 88.4

Baseline set

Five of the eight baselines collapse to 15–30% on ChartQA. The real contest is SGL and IF-Prune, both carefully reproduced.

Tokens ≠ latency

“Same budget” counts the large model’s visual tokens only: +38 ms and +6.2 FLOP points over IF-Prune at 5%, because the checker reads every image in full.

Asymmetric baseline

SGL runs without its early exit, which is also its main compute saving. The 1B-checker row (380 ms) is the fairer headline than the default configuration.

Answer shape

Disagreement is exact match after normalisation, and answers over 10 words abstain — so open-ended cases quietly fall back to one-pass pruning.

06

Strengths

  • A genuinely new axis: allocate trust per question instead of chasing a better global token ranking.
  • Theory that is used, not decoration: the break-even threshold predicts both the budget trend and why random re-answering fails.
  • Unusually complete evidence: 18 benchmarks, five sizes, three families, 1–40% budgets, paired bootstrap with Holm correction, per-benchmark appendix tables.
  • Orthogonality demonstrated: two SOTA pruners improve when their own rankings run inside the two-pass loop.
  • Honest accounting: latency, memory, FLOPs, a FLOP-matched variant, released code and a transparent LLM-use statement.
07

Limitations

  • Short answers only: the checker abstains above 10 words, so long-form VQA falls back to plain one-pass pruning.
  • Narrow setting: single image, single turn, English and Chinese; video and multi-image are future work.
  • Real cost: a second model (+4.3 GB) and a full-image pass — 421 ms / 54.6% FLOPs vs 383 ms / 48.4% at 5%.
  • Exact match can misread paraphrases as disagreement, and τ and ℓ are themselves tuned hyperparameters.
  • Gains fade where pruning is already mild: Gemma 4 31B at 20% reaches 92.5 vs 91.4.
08

Open questions

  1. How much of the 421 ms returns if the 2K re-answer reuses the first pass’s KV cache and visual tokens?
  2. What happens with a much weaker or cross-family checker — where exactly does the γ bound bite?
  3. Does the break-even threshold H̄/ρ transfer across domains, or must ρ be re-tuned per deployment?
  4. Can the same disagreement signal gate KV-cache eviction or speculative-draft acceptance?
  5. Why is the Qwen3-VL and Gemma 4 gain only ~1 point — looser budget, weaker checker, or answer format?
  6. Would a semantic-equivalence judge for δ change the recall / false-alarm trade-off, and at what cost?
