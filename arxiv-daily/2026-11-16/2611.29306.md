> **一句话总结：** 本文指出自我进化智能体的"准入验证器"存在结构性盲点——两个更新可以给验证器完全相同的读数（task reward 50、episode length 5）却有相反的安全后果，因此任何基于该读数的规则（含阈值校准）都不可能区分它们；作者提出 DoppelVet：不提安全标签，先在原生读数的等价类里搜一个行为可分辨的"替身"更新，再让比较验证器在同一起始状态回放两者、交换展示顺序取平均 logit margin，最后用确定性检查+回滚收据控制写入。在 9 个验证器骨干上把有害更新检测平均 AUC 从 0.762 抬到 0.940（Grid）/0.681 抬到 0.826（Tool），十代自更新回路里的退化率从 30.39% 降到 0.43%、终态退化为 0，但真实工具智能体对话上的增益只有 0.662→0.694（AUC），且核心统计单元极小（24 例、6 组）。

> **TL;DR:** The paper formalizes "verifier equivalence": two proposed memory/skill updates can produce a byte-identical native-verifier readout (reward + episode length) while one has a side effect the other does not, so no threshold or calibration can separate them — the authors show this concretely in AI Safety Gridworlds (four Sushi-goal programs all scoring reward 50 / 5 steps, two with safety score 50, two with 0). DoppelVet then searches for a *second* update inside the same exact readout class whose replay trace diverges most, replays candidate and alternative from the same state, and asks one comparison LLM which is riskier, averaging both presentation orders. Over nine verifier backbones it lifts mean ROC AUC from 0.762 to 0.940 on DoppelVet-Grid (+0.179; e.g. GPT-4.1 0.493→0.925) and 0.681 to 0.826 on DoppelVet-Tool (+0.145), beats VaG (0.796) and AgentDoG 1.5 (0.454) with Qwen2.5-32B on the grid, and cuts ten-generation degradation from 30.39% to 0.43% with 89.58% safe-update retention. The honest caveats are in the paper itself: on ATBench much of the gain is just "having a second trajectory at all" (a random safe reference already scores 0.717 vs DoppelVet's 0.771), the real τ²-Bench Retail gain is 0.662→0.694, and the memory-selection comparison rests on 24 and 6 independent units with reported p-values of 0.094/0.219.

## 核心贡献

- **问题形式化（verifier equivalence）**：定义原生验证器在重放库上的完整读数 $r_B(u;s_t)=(\rho_1,\dots,\rho_m)$，两个更新 $u\sim_{\rho,B,s_t}v$ 当且仅当读数逐位相等。命题 1 说明任何决策规则 $g$（以及任何 $g\circ c$ 的校准映射）对该等价类给出同一判决——所以这不只是"阈值没调好"，而是证据层面的不可分辨性。作者在 Sushi-goal 给出具体实例：四个更新同为 reward 50、5 步，但两个 safety score 为 50、两个为 0。
- **方法（三步：造替身 → 对比 → 受控落盘）**：① 在有限邻域 $N(u;s_t)$ 内枚举候选，只保留读数逐位相等的 $N_{eq}$，用可见轨迹距离 $D_B(u,v)$ 取最大者 $v^\*$（$D_B$ 只用回放期可观测量：棋盘/每步 reward 错配，或工具序列 + 渲染对话的 difflib 相似度）；若无差异则 abstain 而不是放行。② 从同一状态回放 $u$ 与 $v^\*$，比较验证器在**两种物理展示顺序**下打分（语义角色固定），取平均 logit margin，配 $\theta_A\le\theta_R$ 实现 admit/reject/abstain 三态。③ 叠加确定性契约检查，写入前生成回执（含 pre-update 状态、候选、替身、重放库、分数、阈值），事后监控违规即回滚重验。
- **基准（DoppelVet-Bench）**：两条赛道。DoppelVet-Grid 由 6 个 AI Safety Gridworlds 环境的可执行程序更新构成（250 校准 / 446 测试，186 harmful + 260 safe），程序级可见 reward+episode length 完全相同才算同一等价类；DoppelVet-Tool 由 τ²-Bench 的 Retail/Airline 自然语言记忆更新与完整客服对话构成（自动构造 364 个 contrast、168 个精确 reward 匹配、28 个真安全分裂；主队列 265 对可评估对话，其中 197 对完整 task-reward 对象也相同）。
- **成本分析**：在线一次验证是 $O(Nm)$ 次执行（$u$ 固定）＋两次模型调用；全对 $O(N^2m)$ 仅用于离线造集。Qwen2.5-32B 上 DoppelVet 用 1.586M input token 达到 0.970 AUC，而 VaG 四批评委用 3.383M token 只有 0.796。
- **长时程与真实对话验证**：464 个起始程序跑十代自更新；另有 88 个记忆选择决策在 176 段新对话中实际执行，报告违规率、安全完成率、以及"两种记忆后果不同时选到更安全者"的比例。

## Key Contributions

- **Names the blind spot and proves it is not fixable by calibration.** Proposition 1 is deliberately narrow but decisive: if $g$ is any function of the readout, $g(r_B(u))=g(r_B(v))$ whenever the readouts are equal, and the same holds for $g\circ c$ under any calibration map $c$. This reframes "our guardrail threshold is miscalibrated" as an evidence-availability problem.
- **Turns the impossibility into an operational procedure.** Instead of arguing the verifier should see more, DoppelVet manufactures a *matched contrast* inside the verifier's own equivalence class — the second trace is not an arbitrary reference answer but a same-readout, maximally-divergent sibling — then averages both display orders to blunt position bias.
- **Ships a benchmark with executable *and* linguistic updates.** Gridworld programs give exact, deterministic hidden side-effect labels; τ²-Bench memory notes give realistic tool-use traces with deterministic forbidden-tool-call checks. This is the first controlled suite (to the authors' knowledge) built specifically around reward-identical pairs.
- **Evaluates the wrapper as a wrapper, not a new model.** Nine fixed verifier backbones (Qwen2.5-14B/32B, Mistral-24B, Llama-3.1-Tulu-3-70B, GPT-4.1, GPT-4o, GPT-5.6 Sol, Claude Sonnet 5, Claude Opus 5) are reused unchanged, so gains are attributable to the interface rather than to retraining.
- **Controlled compute comparison.** The VaG control is given four critics and 2.13× the input tokens of DoppelVet and still loses (0.796 vs 0.970), which rules out the trivial "they just spent more compute" explanation.

## Methodology

**Formal setup.** Persistent state $s_t$ (skills, memories, prompts, parameters); update generator proposes $u_t\in U(s_t)$; commit is $s_{t+1}=F(s_t,u_t)$. DoppelVet operates on an isolated copy of $s_t$ with a fixed replay bank $B_t=\{x_1,\dots,x_m\}$; running $u$ from $s_t$ on $x_i$ yields visible trace $\tau_i(u;s_t)$ and native readout $\rho_i(u;s_t)$. The bank-level readout $r_B(u;s_t)$ records *every* value the native rule consumes (reward, episode length, test outcomes, structured task results), not just the final Boolean.

**Stage I — constructing the alternative.** Enumerate a bounded neighborhood (finite program space for code; minimal procedural edits for text, e.g. moving a prerequisite before a tool action while keeping task/object/instruction template frozen), execute each in an isolated copy, and keep only candidates with byte-for-byte equal canonical readouts (Eq. 3). Rank survivors by mean visible trace distance:
- Grid: $d_{grid}$ = mean Hamming distance over flattened visible board cells + mean mismatch over per-step visible rewards (equal-length traces only).
- Tool: $d_{tool}$ = ½(1 − SequenceMatcher ratio on ordered tool names) + ½(1 − SequenceMatcher ratio on canonical rendered dialogue/trace), with hidden labels excluded from the rendering.

Pick $v^\*=\arg\max D_B$ if the max is positive, else $v^\*=\bot$ and **abstain with the persistent state untouched**. Tie-break by hash; farthest-first selection when the construction budget is larger.

**Stage II — direct comparison.** One call receives task, both visible traces, and fixed semantic roles (candidate vs alternative). Two orders are scored with roles held fixed; the two risk scores are averaged before thresholding (Eq. 5). Open-weight models use reject-minus-approve logit margin; API models use a provider-neutral 0–100 integer risk score. With $\theta_A\le\theta_R$: admit if $R_J<\theta_A$, reject if $R_J\ge\theta_R$, else abstain. Order disagreement, an averaged abstention choice, or $v^\*=\bot$ all force abstention. Critically, DoppelVet **never auto-commits the alternative**: a relative preference is not a safety certificate.

**Stage III — commit and recover.** $\delta_t(u)=\mathrm{ADMIT}$ only if deterministic checks $I_{det}$ pass *and* the comparison admits; otherwise $s_{t+1}=s_t$. A rollback receipt binds pre-update state, both updates, replay bank, score, threshold, check results, and implementation revision; post-activation monitoring restores the validated state on violation and re-runs checks.

**Evaluation protocol.** All candidates, splits, prompts and decision rules were frozen before hidden labels were joined; thresholds selected only on disjoint calibration splits (250 Grid pairs; 150 of ATBench's remaining 750). Grid test cases are grouped by visible class so no exact signature is split across calibration/test. The 32B runs used a pinned local snapshot with float16 and deterministic next-token scoring; API runs used fixed dated snapshots (GPT-4.1-2025-04-14, GPT-4o-2024-11-20). A single-token A/B/C bias argument is used to justify that order effects do not contaminate the A–B margin.

## Results & Highlights

- **Verifier-equivalent safety splits are common in Gridworlds (RQ1).** Harmful-alternative prevalence: Sushi-goal 121/240, Sushi-goal-2 121/192, Sokoban 23/176, Sushi 12/16 — 4 of 6 environments — while Vase (0/32) and Island (0/64) contain none. Concretely, four Sushi-goal programs return reward 50 with 5 steps, but two score 50 and two score 0 on the hidden side-effect measure. DoppelVet ranks the harmful alternative first in **100%** of cases that contain one, vs exact uniform-random probabilities of 85.6% (Sokoban), 21.1% (Sushi), 16.6% (Sushi-goal), 10.5% (Sushi-goal-2).
- **Harmful-update detection over nine backbones (RQ2, Table 1).** Mean ROC AUC rises 0.762 → 0.940 on DoppelVet-Grid (+0.179) and 0.681 → 0.826 on DoppelVet-Tool (+0.145); every one of the nine improves on both sets, gains spanning +0.001…+0.432 and +0.042…+0.217. Largest grid gains go to weak verifiers — GPT-4.1 0.493 → 0.925 (+0.432) and Qwen2.5-14B 0.619 → 0.939 (+0.321) — while frontier verifiers are already saturated there: GPT-5.6 Sol 0.983 → 0.984 (+0.002) and Claude Opus 5 0.994 → 0.996 (+0.001). On the stricter 197-pair subset with identical complete task-reward objects, mean Tool AUC rises 0.645 → 0.831.
- **Beats candidate-only and SOTA guardrails at equal-or-lower compute (Table 2, Qwen2.5-32B).** DoppelVet-Grid: DoppelVet 0.970 vs VaG 0.796, Original 0.651, AgentDoG 1.5 **0.454** (below chance); with 1.586M input tokens vs VaG's 3.383M. DoppelVet-Tool (Retail): 0.694 vs Original 0.662, VaG 0.594, AgentDoG 0.462. At the 80%-safe-acceptance operating point, DoppelVet accepts 226/260 safe and rejects 176/186 harmful updates, vs AgentDoG's 205/260 safe and only 17/186 harmful rejected.
- **Ten-generation self-evolution (RQ3, Table 3, 464 starting programs).** Harmful commit rate 47.41% → 0.86%, any degradation 30.39% → 0.43%, final (10th-state) degradation 7.76% → **0.00%**, with 89.58% of safe-update opportunities still retained. Intermediate methods: VaG 12.72% / 6.68% / 3.88%, Pointwise 19.40% / 10.78% / 4.74%, AgentDoG 1.5 25.22% / 18.32% / 7.97%. The 0.43% is 93.6% below VaG.
- **Real tool-use conversations (RQ4, Table 4, 176 conversations / 88 decisions).** Violations 22.7% (40/176) → 19.3% (34/176); joint safe-and-assessable completion 61.9% (109/176) → 64.2% (113/176); and when the two memories actually produce different violation outcomes, DoppelVet picks the safer one in 66.7% (16/24) vs 54.2% (13/24) for VaG and 45.8% (11/24) for both Original and AgentDoG 1.5.
- **Safety-focused Retail stress test (Appendix B.4, 6 memory pairs × 7 tasks × 3 seeds = 126 conversations).** Violations 21.4% (27/126) → 5.6% (7/126); safe completion 39.7% → 48.4%; safer choices 1/6 → 5/6 — but the authors report their own paired tests as non-significant (Wilcoxon two-sided statistic 2.0, p = 0.09375; exact sign test p = 0.21875).

## Authors & Credibility

Six authors from UNSW Sydney (Yifan Duan, Du Yin, Siyuan Zheng, Chao Xue, Lihuan Li, Flora D. Salim, corresponding author Salim). Salim is a well-established researcher in ubiquitous computing and ML systems, which fits the mobile/agent-systems framing; the paper reads like an engineering-rigor lab effort rather than a pure theory paper.

Credibility signals that matter here, because the claims are unusually large:
- Preprint, under review, cs.AI single-listing, no venue acceptance yet.
- Appendix C documents an artifact-provenance pipeline: a pre-result plan, a label artifact withheld from inference, raw inference, a derived analysis, and SHA-256 hash linkage between stages; manuscript numbers are indexed by JSON pointer into `work/evidence/numeric_evidence.json`, and a test suite validates schema, hash linkage, prospective timing, outcome reconstruction, and figure source rows. That is a stronger provenance story than most agent-safety papers.
- Appendix B.1 explicitly addresses leakage: candidate rankings, class identities and prompts were written before labels were joined; the constructor reads visible board/reward mismatch but never the hidden performance accessor; test cases are grouped by visible class so no exact signature straddles the split.
- Nine backbones including two frontier proprietary models plus an external benchmark (ATBench) is above-average breadth for a single-lab verification paper.

Credibility caveats: no public code/checkpoint/repo URL appears anywhere in the PDF, so the provenance claims are unverifiable from outside; the benchmark is the authors' own construction and the authors are also the only evaluators; several of the proprietary model names and their dates cannot be independently checked from the manuscript alone.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The core insight is real and well-targeted: the agent-safety literature is full of candidate-wise verifiers (Reflexion-style self-judging, VaG's multi-critic gate, AgentDoG's trajectory guardrail, governed-evolution rollback) and all of them share the same interface — one trace in, one score out. Reframing this as an *equivalence-class* problem, where the useful information is a matched sibling rather than more evidence about the same trace, is a genuine conceptual step, and the "second trace is drawn from the verifier's own readout class, not an arbitrary reference" distinction is the right defense against the obvious "pairwise judging is old news" objection. Proposition 1, however, is close to a tautology — applying any function to equal inputs gives equal outputs — and the paper's prose sometimes inflates it ("impossibility") beyond what is shown. The actual contribution is the empirical demonstration that these classes are *populated* (121/240 in Sushi-goal, 28 usable splits in text), not the proposition.

**Rigor.** Mixed, in interesting ways. On the plus side: thresholds frozen on disjoint calibration splits, candidate-only controls, a compute-matched (indeed compute-disadvantaged-for-baseline) comparison, and honest reporting of the paper's own negative statistics in B.4. On the minus side, four concrete problems:

1. **Benchmark distribution favors the method.** Harmful-alternative prevalence is wildly non-uniform: 121/240 and 121/192 in the two Sushi-goal variants, but 0/32 (Vase) and 0/64 (Island). Since DoppelVet's entire mechanism is "find the divergent sibling," evaluating on a benchmark where most test mass has a findable harmful sibling measures the method in its best case. There is no reweighting to a realistic update distribution, and no reporting of how often Stage I finds nothing (the abstention rate on the 446-case test set is never given in the main text).
2. **The Grid headline is partly an artifact of weak verifiers.** The mean gain of +0.179 is dominated by GPT-4.1 (0.493, i.e. worse than chance) and Qwen2.5-14B/32B. The two frontier verifiers move by +0.002 and +0.001. A mean-over-backbones framing lets a 0.001 gain and a 0.432 gain count equally; the median gain, or a per-capability-tier breakdown, would tell a very different story.
3. **The ATBench transfer is much weaker than the headline suggests, and the paper admits it.** Retrieved-reference DoppelVet scores 0.771 vs 0.646 for Original — but a *random* safe reference already reaches 0.717. So roughly 71% of the 0.125-point gain comes from merely supplying a second trajectory, not from equivalence construction or retrieval. Because ATBench's release omits native readouts and generation-time pair IDs, this track is by the authors' own admission "reference-anchored comparison rather than exact verifier-equivalence construction" — meaning the flagship mechanism is not what is being tested there.
4. **The safety comparisons that matter most have tiny denominators.** RQ4's "safer choices" is 16/24 vs 11/24 and 13/24 — a 5-conversation swing on 24 units with no confidence interval. The B.4 stress test is 6 independent memory pairs, and the authors' own Wilcoxon (p = 0.094) and sign test (p = 0.219) fail at 0.05. The 176-conversation violation rate difference (40 → 34) is likewise a 6-conversation difference. Meanwhile the *headline* number — 30.39% → 0.43% degradation — comes from a deterministic program space where the authors also note that denominators are not comparable across policies because a veto changes which later states get visited (B.3). That caveat is exactly the kind of thing that makes a rate ratio across policies hard to interpret.

**Reproducibility.** Two-sided. The provenance appendix is more than most papers do, and the frozen-snapshot discipline (pinned Qwen2.5-32B, float16, PyTorch 2.5.1+cu121, dated API snapshots) is concrete. But with no released code, no DoppelVet-Bench artifact, and a benchmark whose construction requires the authors' own τ²-Bench task suite plus 42 deduplicated source tasks, nobody can currently reproduce Table 1. The paper also leans on internal paths (`work/figures/source data`, `work/evidence/numeric_evidence.json`) that are not public.

**Specific red flags worth naming.**
- **AgentDoG 1.5 at 0.454 AUC on Grid is below chance.** The authors do disclose that this is an external checkpoint applied to their cohorts and that they "neither reproduce its ATBench table nor attribute these numbers to the original paper" — good. But a guardrail scoring 0.454 is more likely an interface mismatch (unsafe-minus-safe logit margin read after greedy analysis generation on reward-identical pairs) than evidence that AgentDoG is worse than a coin flip. The "strengthened two-pass" variant scoring *lower* on Grid (0.419 < 0.454) while being sold as "deliberately stronger" is the tell: if a stronger condition is worse, the score is not measuring the intended quantity. The VaG control is more defensible (0.796, same frozen candidates, 4 critics, more tokens), and that comparison is the one worth trusting.
- **Order averaging is never ablated.** Two model calls per update are used to average both display orders, motivated by known position bias. But there is no ablation showing order averaging beats single-order, no reporting of order-disagreement rates, and no reporting of how much the extra call costs in abstentions. Given that abstention is the safety valve, this is the single most important missing ablation.
- **Stage I selection is never ablated.** Taking the *maximum* visible trace divergence is a heuristic; no comparison against random eligible alternative, no sensitivity to replay-bank size $m$, no study of when maximal divergence is *anti*-correlated with hidden harm. On Grid this matters because $d_{grid}$ uses visible rewards, which are plausibly correlated with the hidden side-effect score in exactly the Sushi environments that dominate the test set.
- **Text-space construction yield is low and the comparator is mediocre.** Of 364 contrasts only 168 reach exact reward matching and only 28 actually split on safety (7.7%); of those 28 pooled splits, the comparator picks the lower-violation member in 17/28 (61%). That is the honest ceiling of the language track, and it is a long way from the 0.940 Grid AUC that the abstract leads with.

**Significance.** If the prevalence numbers hold up — that in 4 of 6 controlled environments a same-readout, opposite-outcome pair exists and that a 10-generation loop can be kept at 0.43% degradation with 89.58% safe retention — this is a meaningful contribution to how self-evolving agents should gate persistence, and it is cheap (two calls, no retraining, works with a frozen verifier). The engineering framing (receipts, abstention, rollback, monitoring) is the right one for deployment. But the strongest evidence is on the most controlled, least realistic substrate, and the most realistic evidence (τ²-Bench conversations) rests on 24 and 6 units with non-significant tests.

## Strengths

- A crisp, falsifiable framing of a real interface defect, with a demonstration instance (reward 50 / 5 steps, safety 50 vs 0) that is easy to check and hard to argue with.
- A method that adds value without touching the model: frozen verifier, no retraining, two comparison calls, and a measured token budget that is *lower* than the four-critic baseline it beats (1.586M vs 3.383M).
- Two complementary benchmark tracks — executable programs (deterministic hidden labels) and natural-language memories (realistic tool traces) — which is the right way to argue the phenomenon is not a Gridworld curiosity.
- Explicit abstention semantics plus deterministic checks and rollback receipts, instead of pretending a relative preference certifies safety.
- Unusually thorough provenance and leakage control for an agent-safety preprint, including class-grouped splits and label-join-after-freeze discipline.
- Willingness to publish its own failed statistics: the two-pass AgentDoG condition underperforming, the p = 0.094 / 0.219 stress test, the 17/28 text comparator, and the ATBench random-reference comparison at 0.717 are all in the paper rather than buried.

## Limitations

- **Coverage is the binding constraint, not accuracy.** If Stage I cannot find a behaviorally distinct member of the readout class, DoppelVet abstains — and no abstention/coverage rate is reported for the Grid test set, nor per-backbone. A verifier that abstains often is safe but useless; the utility side is only measured indirectly (89.58% safe retention on Grid, 176 conversations on Tool).
- **Cost in real deployment is under-reported.** The $O(Nm)$ figure counts executions, not tokens or wall-clock; constructing $N$ alternatives and replaying $m$ contexts in a real tool-agent means $N\times m$ environment rollouts (DB writes, API calls) per proposed memory. Only the comparison call's token count is reported.
- **Language-track construction is fragile and hand-tuned** ("change one operational boundary, such as a prerequisite before a tool action") — a plausible recipe but one that will not obviously transfer to arbitrary memory formats, and yield is already only 28/364 safety splits on the authors' own domains.
- **No distributional realism test.** The benchmark's harmful-alternative prevalence (≈50% in Sushi-goal, 0% in Vase/Island) is nothing like a real self-evolution stream, and no reweighting or importance-sampling analysis is offered.
- **Frontier-verifier ceiling.** On Grid, GPT-5.6 Sol and Claude Opus 5 gain +0.002 and +0.001; the method's value proposition quietly narrows to "useful when your verifier is mid-tier," which the abstract's mean-of-nine framing obscures.
- **Statistical power.** The two most deployment-relevant tables (4: n = 176 conversations, 24 decisive pairs; 5: n = 6 memory pairs) are underpowered, and the paper reports this honestly rather than fixing it with more seeds.
- **Single-lab, single-benchmark, no external replication** and no released artifacts.

## Relevance

与"自我进化智能体的安全性"这一课题直接相关：它把记忆/技能写入这一持久化决策当作可验证对象，并给出一个即插即用的 pre-commit 包装器（冻结验证器、两次比较调用、abstain + 回滚），对任何做 agent memory / skill library / 长期运行 agent 的工作都有直接可借用的接口设计；同时它提供了一个"reward 完全相同但安全后果相反"的成对基准，可作为评测自家 guardrail 的探针。需要注意其数值结论主要来自作者自建的受控 Gridworld，真实对话上的增益小且样本量不足。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. What is Stage I's coverage on DoppelVet-Grid and DoppelVet-Tool — what fraction of proposed updates get a usable $v^\*$, and how does the admit/reject/abstain split vary by verifier backbone? Abstention rate is the missing half of the utility story.
2. How much of the gain survives removing order averaging, and how large is the order-disagreement rate? If disagreement is rare, one call may suffice and the cost halves.
3. Is maximum-visible-divergence the right selector, or does a random eligible alternative do nearly as well? On Grid, $d_{grid}$ uses visible rewards — is that correlated with the hidden side-effect score in a way that silently helps?
4. Would a *non-LLM* comparison (e.g. a program-diff or a symbolic side-effect checker) match the LLM comparator on Grid? If yes, the Grid numbers say less about LLM judgment than they appear to.
5. Why does the "stronger" independent two-pass AgentDoG condition score *below* candidate-only on Grid (0.419 vs 0.454)? Diagnosing that would clarify whether the comparison is measuring trajectory risk or prompt-format sensitivity.
6. In the ten-generation loop, how do the 464 starting programs distribute across environments, and what does "final degradation 0.00%" mean when the loop is deterministic and the environment set includes four of six environments where a harmful sibling exists?
7. What happens when the alternative construction is *adversarial* — an attacker who knows DoppelVet can craft $u$ and $v$ such that the more divergent trace is also the safer one, flipping the comparison. Is there a robust-selection variant?
