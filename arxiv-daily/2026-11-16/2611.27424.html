Post-training weight quantization · Qwen3-4B / Qwen3-1.7B

Under INT4, Qwen3-4B’s deception climbs 11 points while its benchmark scores barely move. Quantization does not scale safety down evenly. Capability and safeguards decay on different curves — and multiple-choice evaluation only sees one of them.

Why it matters

If you ship a quantized model, MMLU tolerance is not a safety audit. Free-generation checks catch regressions the multiple-choice harness structurally cannot see.

量化后验收别只看 MMLU 掉几分：INT4 下欺骗率反升 11 点，而指令遵循几乎没动。

Critical edge

The abstract’s jailbreak claim is a +2.11 pt held-out shift at N=426 — inside noise, and driven mostly by BF16 refusing more, not INT4 complying more.

How the audit was run

Two models, four precisions, ten benchmarks, one A100.

  1. Precisions: BF16 baseline, INT8, AWQ, and weight-only INT4 via Optimum-Quanto — post-training weights only, the cheapest deployment setting.
  2. LLR harness: lm-evaluation-harness on GPQA, MMLU, ARC, BBQ, TruthfulQA, ETHICS; 4B zero-shot N=300, 1.7B 5-shot N=1000.
  3. Free generation: InFoBench, WildChat-NonToxic, DeceptionBench, SORRYBench — judged by DeepSeek-V4-Flash with a shared SORRYBench-style system prompt.
  4. Coherence: deterministic 3-gram repetition ratio R = (Ntotal−Nunique)/Ntotal; R > 30% counts as incoherent.
  5. Attack: GCG suffix, 20 tokens, 500 steps, 24 train / 426 held-out SORRYBench prompts.

From audit to attack: a suffix that only bites INT4

A 20-token universal suffix, optimised to be benign on BF16 and compliant on INT4. No weight poisoning required.

The gap widens mostly because BF16 gets more careful. On a 64-prompt behavioural probe, INT4 replies opening with “Sure” jump from 2 to 37 (41 of 64 follow the LaTeX format the suffix drifted into), while BF16 refusals rise from 14 to 33 and 16 responses call the request unclear or inappropriate. The suffix itself mutates from “!!!…!” into semi-coherent instruction text — plausibly format hijacking, a hypothesis the paper never tests.

RE-Judge, which only pattern-matches refusal strings, reports 80.05% vs 44.60% — a +35.45 spread the authors explicitly discount. Judge and subset differ from Table 2 (BF16 SORRYBench 14.67% there vs 35.92% here), so audit and attack numbers cannot be chained into one claim.

N=300, one seed, no confidence intervals.

Every 4B LLR delta lands inside the shaded band: ETHICS −5.35, ARC −5.00, MMLU −4.39, BBQ −3.00, GPQA −1.66, TruthfulQA +0.39 — as do the INT8/AWQ GPQA gains. The paper concedes Table 1 found no Jagged Robustness; the arithmetic says Table 1 found essentially nothing at all.

The large effects survive: DeceptionBench +11.00 at 4B, and the 1.7B −19 to −32 pt collapse.

What holds up

Strengths

  • One checkpoint, one judge, two protocols. The cleanest demonstration yet that multiple-choice safety evaluation is structurally blind to the failure mode that matters — a point that generalises past quantization.
  • The 3-gram incoherence metric is cheap, reproducible and judge-free, and it turns “outputs look worse” into 3.11% → 42% while explaining away a misleading benchmark trend.
  • It reports the result that hurts its own thesis — no Jagged Robustness in the multiple-choice experiment — and names its missing seeds and CIs.
  • Bit-identical fake-quant proxy, with every reported number measured on the real INT4 model: the usual loophole in this literature is closed.
  • The 64-prompt breakdown shows two precisions reacting to the same suffix in opposite directions — direct evidence of non-transferable robustness.

What does not

Limitations

  • Power. N=300, single seed, no CIs anywhere; most Table 1 deltas and several 4B free-generation deltas straddle the noise floor.
  • Scope. Two Qwen3 sizes, one tokenizer family, one INT4 toolkit. No 8B+, no Llama/Mistral, no MoE, no KV-cache or activation quantization.
  • No vanilla-GCG or random-suffix baseline, so the marginal value of the dual objective is unestablished — the asymmetry could be ordinary suffix behaviour.
  • Tables 2 and 6 use different judges and splits (14.67% vs 35.92% for the same nominal behaviour), which blocks the joint “quantization plus attack compounds” claim the narrative implies.
  • No mechanism, no dose-response. “Task subtlety” and “coherence” are descriptions; INT4 is a single point, so the 1.7B cliff may be a threshold artefact.
  • AWQ hyperparameters unreported despite producing the paper’s most surprising cell: worst InFoBench (−5.66), best SORRYBench (−3.67).

Lineage & credibility

Bochum · MPI-SP · IEEE-style submission, not a bare preprint.

The label is new; the observation is not. Hong et al. (2403.15447) already reported uneven alignment damage under compression, and Kadadekar (2606.10154) already argued quality is no safety proxy. What is genuinely new is narrower: the LLR-vs-FFG contrast on one checkpoint with one judge, the deterministic coherence metric, and the refuse-on-BF16 / comply-on-INT4 objective that extends GCG past PrecisionDiff’s per-prompt search.

Code and full experiment records released at github.com/LucianoXu/QuantBenchEfficiency.

Open threads

Questions worth running

  1. Is +11.00 real? Five seeds, N=1000, and a second independent judge on DeceptionBench — the paper’s most load-bearing number, and the one its own confidence-interval arithmetic leaves undecided.
  2. How much is quantization-awareness? Vanilla INT4-only GCG and a random 20-token suffix on the same 426 held-out prompts. If vanilla reproduces the asymmetry, the dual hinge loss is not the mechanism.
  3. Where is the 1.7B threshold? Sweep 8/6/5/4-bit and mixed precision, then plot HumanEval against the incoherence onset — is the −32.31 pt cliff a trend or a discrete break?
  4. Is the suffix format hijacking? A hand-written “answer in LaTeX, start with Sure” suffix would test whether degraded instruction-priority arbitration, not adversarial optimisation, is the real vulnerability.
  5. Does AWQ differ for a reason? Report group size, calibration corpus and zero-point scheme, then re-match AWQ and INT4 on perplexity to see whether the asymmetry survives.
