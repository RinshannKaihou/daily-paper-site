> **一句话总结：** 作者把模型权重、数据、阈值和审计代码全部冻结，只改变"算术执行契约"（Tier 1：FP64/严格 FP32/TF32/M5 relaxed，序列化权重逐字节相同；Tier 2：BF16、INT8、FP8 部署配置），在 4 个表格与语言模型任务、NVIDIA/AMD/Apple 三类加速器上测"同一个模型到底是不是同一批人"：严格 FP32 在 3 块 GPU 上改变了 4B 模型 204,204 个决策中的 0 个，TF32 只改了 41（H100）和 39（RTX 4090）个，且 Tier 1 在预设检验下没有翻转任何一个判决（84 个语言模型 + 144 个表格判决）；但 vLLM FP8 服务 24B 模型时选择率从 44.7% 升到 54.4%，让一个严格 FP32 失败（DP=0.0567、EO=0.0515）的雇佣状态审计"通过"（DP=0.0417、EO=0.0394，容差 0.05）——而按参考的选择数匹配后它又失败（DP=0.0541），所以作者明确说：**判决依赖配置，但 FP8 并没有被证明更公平**。

> **TL;DR:** Holding weights (SHA-256 verified), records, frozen thresholds and audit code fixed, this paper varies only the *numerical execution contract* and asks whether the audit verdict moves. Tier 1 (FP64 / strict FP32 / TF32 / MLX-on-M5 relaxed default) changes **0 of 204,204** Qwen3-4B decisions under strict FP32 across H100, RTX 4090 and MI350X, and **41 / 39** under TF32 (≤0.035% per dataset) — yet flips no verdict at all among 84 LLM and 144 tabular verdicts; only with ACS survey weights does one gap creep from 0.04999 to 0.0501/0.0503. Tier 2 deployment configs (BF16, INT8, MXFP8, vLLM FP8) flip far more — FP8 serving moves 822/19,938 Diabetes decisions (4.12%) — and change 6 of the 17 pass-fail verdicts whose FP64 bootstrap interval already contained the 0.05 criterion, 0 of the other 43. The one interpretable case: on 24B Mistral Small 3.2 / ACSEmployment by sex, vLLM FP8 selects 54.4% vs strict FP32's 44.7% and passes both DP (0.0417 vs 0.0567) and EO (0.0394 vs 0.0515); at matched selection it fails DP (0.0541) and its EO change has a paired interval covering zero. Verdict: execution contracts belong in the audit report, and cheap Tier 1 arithmetic is *not* what breaks ordinary audits.

## 核心贡献

- **把"模型"拆成权重 + 算术执行契约，并给出因果分层**：定义 `f_c(x) = 1[s_c(x) ≥ t]`，契约 `c` 固定每个算子输入的格式与舍入、每个乘积的精度、累加宽度与顺序、输出格式。Tier 1 只改算术、序列化权重逐字节不变（这是因果声明）；Tier 2 是真实部署配置（BF16 计算、8-bit 权重、FP8），同时改变权重舍入、kernel 和引擎，所以其效应归因于"配置"而非"算术"。这一分层是本文最干净的方法论设计。
- **第一次在固定模型上量化"谁被翻转"**：不是重训、不是剪枝、不是量化换模型（已有工作报告的都是"另一个模型"），而是在同一份权重上测个体级翻转，并报告翻转集合的跨 GPU/跨引擎 Jaccard 相似度（TF32 在 H100 与 RTX 4090 上分别为 41 和 39 次翻转，pooled Jaccard 0.38，逐任务 0.31–0.67）。
- **判决级别的统计**：用 6 种"不可复现"判据（0.05 容差的 pass/fail、最差组对的显著性检验、等效性检验、三种排序、four-fifths 规则、统计量移动 >0.001）统计判决翻转，并把"单个契约下的点判决"、"跨契约的稳定区间"、"重采样不确定性"三件事严格分开——这是很多审计论文混为一谈的地方。
- **一个反直觉且诚实的主结论**：Tier 1 算术改变了人，却没有改变判决；83% 的部署配置翻转都发生在采样区间本来就跨过判据、即"本来就说不清"的判决上。作者自己在引言里写下"本文并不主张普通精度变化会经常反转结论，Tier 1 恰恰反驳了这一点"。
- **Audit Stability Protocol（表 6 的 6 步清单）**：审计方（而非被审计方）在打分前声明可接受契约集合 `C`、对照组、平局规则、群体、权重、检验与所主张的命题；每次运行回读精度设置并用 canary 矩阵对 FP64 验证；跨 `C` 计算稳定区间 `I(M) = [min M_{c,r}, max M_{c,r}]`；把判决分类为 numerical-robust 或 contract-dependent。其中 hosted engine 无法验证的项标记为 "unverified"。
- **把成本也测了**：同一 H100 上严格 FP32 每百万决策的 GPU 时间是 TF32 的 4.33 倍、BF16 的 7.55 倍；在 RTX 4090 上只有 1.59 倍；24B 模型是 TF32 的 6.33 倍、BF16 的 22.5 倍——"审计成本倍数"高度依赖加速器，脱离硬件的成本报价是不完整的。

## Key Contributions

- **A causal/deployment split that the fairness literature lacked.** Prior work varies the *model* (retraining on other hardware, pruning, quantization) and therefore measures a different function. Tier 1 keeps the function's parameters byte-identical and varies only rounding/accumulation order, which is the only design that licenses a claim about arithmetic itself.
- **Individual-level arbitrariness with one fitted model.** The paper extends Cooper et al.'s "arbitrariness" from training-run variance to execution variance, and shows the *set* of flipped people is GPU- and engine-dependent (transformers vs vLLM on one H100: Jaccard 0.19, i.e. near chance — the engine moves who flips at least as much as the vendor).
- **Verdict-level, not metric-level, reporting.** Six "senses" of non-reproducibility, each fixed before running, with the denominator (120 pass-fail + four-fifths verdicts) stated in advance — so "no verdict changed" is a falsifiable claim, not an absence of evidence.
- **A separation of three quantities that audits routinely conflate**: the deterministic point verdict under one contract, its range over a declared contract set (stability interval), and its sampling uncertainty. A verdict can be numerically robust yet statistically unsettled, and vice versa.
- **A concrete, costed reporting specification** (Table 6) with an explicit admissibility definition (weight/activation/accumulation precision, framework and kernel-library versions, batching and scheduler settings, compilation/graph capture) and honest marking of what could not be verified in hosted serving.

## Methodology

**Design.** A thresholded model maps record `x` to `f_c(x) = 1[s_c(x) ≥ t]` with `t` frozen before any test scoring. A flip requires `|s − t| ≤ |δ_c|` where `δ_c` is the contract's score error, so flip probability is bounded by the density of a group's scores near `t` — this gives the paper its mechanism (Fig. 1A/B show flip share vs. distance from threshold on a log axis).

**Tasks and groups.** ACSIncome (156,532/39,133 train/test, 667 features, 41.0% positive) and ACSEmployment (303,054/75,763, 74 features, 45.4%) from Folktables 2018 California microdata; HMDA 2018 California home-purchase applications (277,478/69,370, 538 features, 90.2% positive) restricted to first-lien, owner-occupied, site-built, approved-or-denied; Diabetes 130-US readmission (79,405/19,938 encounters, 1,008 features, 11.6% positive, patient-level split, top-10% threshold). Groups: race × sex for ACS, derived race/ethnicity/sex for HMDA (including a derived **Joint** category for applicant/co-applicant pairs), race × sex × age band for Diabetes.

**Models.** Logistic regression, MLP (256/128 hidden), XGBoost (depth 6, 300 rounds), each trained once in float32, serialized, SHA-256 verified before every run; plus Qwen3-4B-Instruct as a zero-shot classifier (score from the two label-word logits, `m = z⁺ − z⁻`, `s = σ(m)`, computed in float64) and Mistral Small 3.2 (24B) as a second-family replication with a strict-FP32 reference.

**Contracts.** Tier 1: FP64 reference, strict FP32, TF32 (10 fraction-bit operands), AMD "TF32 setting" on MI350X (three BF16 partial products of split operands, ~17 bits, with **an unidentified arithmetic in 1/8 of output columns** of the 4B model's large projections), MLX relaxed default on Apple M5 (both operands truncated to 10 fraction bits). Tier 2: BF16 compute, INT8 dynamic, 8-bit and MXFP8 weights, vLLM BF16/FP8 serving, PyTorch FP8 on MI350X.

**Verification and statistics.** Before scoring, each Tier 1 run reads back its precision flags and evaluates a canary matrix product against FP64 (TF32 raises matrix-product relative error from ≤1.3e-6 to 3.0e-4, a >2-order separation; one-output products stay ≤2.5e-7, which is exactly why the paper distinguishes them). Every contract runs twice in separate processes. Intervals are 1,000-record bootstrap percentiles shared across contracts. Analysis status is tracked in three tiers (prespecified / planned-before-a-later-arm / post hoc) by commit order in the OSF-linked artifact.

## Results & Highlights

- **The strict control is genuinely tight.** Strict FP32 changed **0 of the 4B model's 204,204 decisions** on each of H100, RTX 4090 and MI350X, and all 196 tabular contract×dataset×model combinations (plus 56 on MI350X) returned byte-identical scores in their two runs; no honest FP32 tabular contract moved a score more than **8.9 × 10⁻⁷** from FP64. So the disagreements reported below are contract effects, not run-to-run noise.
- **Tier 1 flips individuals but no verdicts.** TF32 flipped **41** decisions on the H100 and **39** on the RTX 4090 (≤0.035% of a dataset; largest single task 23/69,370 on HMDA), all within a logit margin of 0.0817 of the threshold; the tabular MLP's TF32 flips all lay within **2.1 × 10⁻⁴** of the threshold. Against 84 LLM + 144 tabular verdicts at stake, Tier 1 changed **0** (Table 4: 0/20, 0/20, 0/10, 0/24, 0/10 in every Tier 1 row). With ACS survey weights the single exception is the 4B model's ACSIncome DP gap by sex: **0.04999 → 0.0501 (H100) / 0.0503 (RTX 4090)**, i.e. a crossing produced by 2e-4 of score drift.
- **Tier 2 moves far more people and some verdicts — but only the fragile ones.** FP8 serving moved **822 of 19,938** Diabetes decisions (**4.12%**) and **3,059 of 89,133** for the 24B model, vs 7 and 23 under TF32. Across the 4B and tabular arms the deployment configs changed 6 pass-fail, 5 significance, 2 equivalence and 12 ranking verdicts; **6 of the 17** pass-fail verdicts whose FP64 interval already contained 0.05 changed, and **0 of the other 43**, with FP64 gaps lying 0.0024–0.0214 from the criterion.
- **The 24B employment audit is the paper's sharpest cell, and it cuts against FP8.** On ACSEmployment by sex (20,000 records), vLLM FP8 selected **54.4%** vs strict FP32's **44.7%** (task base rate 45.4%), so its demographic-parity gap fell **0.0567 → 0.0417** and equalized-odds gap **0.0515 → 0.0394**, against a 0.05 tolerance: FP8 passes a test strict FP32 fails. But matched at the reference's 8,948 selected records, FP8's DP is **0.0541** (fails), its EO 0.0481 is within noise, and only the raw DP change has a paired 95% interval excluding zero (**[−0.0238, −0.0064]**; EO **[-0.0267, 0.0024]** spans zero). With survey weights, strict FP32 already passed EO (0.0353).
- **Who flips is not reproducible across hardware.** H100 vs RTX 4090 TF32 flip sets give a pooled Jaccard of **0.38** (0.31–0.67 by task), but only HMDA's exceeds chance under every matching — the pooled excess is carried by one task. transformers vs vLLM on the *same* H100 gives **0.19**, near chance.
- **One flip is already a statistic.** A single flipped record moves **30 of the 90** tabular statistics by more than the 0.001 movement threshold, because groups are small, positives are few, or the rule is a top-10% cut; four tabular verdicts (one a tree ensemble's) sit within one flip of their criterion, three of them among the five that 8-bit formats changed.
- **Direction matters, not magnitude.** The M5's relaxed default is one-signed: on the ACSEmployment MLP all **31** flips went one way, and **27 of the 38 men** just below the threshold flipped against **4 of 33 women** (a mechanism, not a disparity — the authors say so).
- **Cost.** Per million decisions on one H100: FP64 11.30 GPU-h (\$39.43), strict FP32 12.17 (\$42.49), TF32 2.81 (\$9.81), BF16 1.61 (\$5.63), vLLM FP8 3.55 (\$12.40) — strict FP32 is **4.33×** TF32 and **7.55×** BF16, but only **1.59×** TF32 on an RTX 4090; the 24B model's strict FP32 is 6.33× TF32 and 22.5× BF16.

## Authors & Credibility

Two authors: Erzheng Zhang (University of Toronto) and Agnes Chu (McMaster University). No prior fairness-auditing track record is visible from the paper, and there is no industry lab or large team behind it — this is a two-person, self-funded-style study (12.97 H100-h + 10.28 RTX 4090-h for the 4B arm, 41.77 H100-h for the 24B arm, 10.96 MI350X-h). That is unusually little compute for a claim about deployment practice, and it shows in the coverage of hardware/engine combinations rather than in the care of the design.

What raises credibility substantially: (i) the study plan, expectations and a **kill criterion** were committed to version control before any run, and the paper reports that the kill criterion was **not met** on the tabular Tier 1 evidence alone and that 4 of the pre-written flip-rate/count expectations were too high; (ii) every contract ran twice, with SHA-256 over weights and inputs; (iii) a script re-checks every number in the paper against result files; (iv) the generative-AI statement discloses Claude Code (Opus 5.5) for code/text review and ChatGPT (Sol 5.6) for draft critique; (v) the negative result (Tier 1 changes no verdict) is stated in the abstract, not buried.

The remaining credibility risk is analytical rather than ethical: several of the most newsworthy analyses (the sampling-interval split in §5.5, all robustness checks in App. A.10–A.12, the FP16 arm) are **post hoc**, and the verdict-counting rule ("each verdict counted once", "control contracts enter no denominator") was itself set post hoc. The paper labels all of this explicitly — which is the right thing to do, but it also means the headline "6 of 17 vs 0 of 43" is an exploratory contrast, as the authors concede in the same sentence.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The empirical axis is genuinely new: holding weights, records, threshold *and* audit code fixed and varying only the arithmetic is a cleaner intervention than Nelaturu et al. (hardware → retraining), Marcuzzi et al. (quantization of LLMs on generative bias tasks), or Cooper et al. (training-seed variance). The theoretical core (§3.2, the `|s − t| ≤ |δ_c|` flip condition) is elementary — essentially a restatement of "only records near the threshold can move" — but it is the right lemma, and it does real work: it predicts the log-scale flip profile in Fig. 1A/B and it is what makes the tabular Tier-1 result (LR and trees never flip; only the MLP does) explicable rather than mysterious.

**The central weakness is that the causal arm is nearly vacuous in effect size.** Tier 1 changes 41 of 204,204 decisions (0.02%) and zero verdicts across 228 verdicts at stake; the only crossing needs survey weights and 2e-4 of drift (0.04999 → 0.0501). The paper is commendably honest about this ("Tier 1 contradicts" the idea that precision routinely reverses conclusions), but it means the causal half of the title's promise delivers a null result, and the interesting half (Tier 2) is explicitly *not* causal — BF16/FP8 change weights, kernels, engines and batching at once. The 24B "pass" chain even mixes a framework change and a card change in one step ("moved from two GPUs and eager attention to one and SDPA"), which the authors note but which makes the "engine vs precision" attribution unresolvable.

**The verdict-split result risks being close to tautological.** That deployment configs change only pass-fail verdicts whose bootstrap interval already contained 0.05, and none of the 43 that did not, is presented as the paper's third contribution. But a perturbation of size 1e-3–1e-2 can only move a statistic across a fixed criterion if the statistic starts within that distance of it — the finding is partly a restatement of "small perturbations have small effects". The authors hedge with "the contrast is descriptive", and they do supply the non-trivial part (only 2 of 9 changed verdict-contract pairs survive a paired interval excluding zero), but the section's framing is stronger than its inferential content.

**Degenerate operating points do a lot of hidden work.** Two of the four tasks are scored by the 4B model at absurd thresholds: at the prespecified 0.5 it selects **0.094%** of ACSEmployment records against a 45.4% base rate, and the Diabetes top-10% rule sits at a predicted probability of **0.99977** on a task where its AUC is **0.552** (near chance). Four of the 4B model's five changed pass-fail verdicts are on that near-chance Diabetes task. So a substantial share of the "deployment configs change verdicts" evidence comes from a model that is not a usable classifier on that dataset — disclosed in §4.2 and §9, but not weighted in the abstract's framing.

**Group-level conclusions rest on very small counts.** The "which people flip depends on the GPU" claim is supported by 41 vs 39 flips, a pooled Jaccard of 0.38 whose excess over chance is carried entirely by HMDA (0.31–0.67 by task, "does not show agreement on the other tasks"), and a 27-of-38 vs 4-of-33 split in a single MLP cell. The paper calls these mechanisms rather than disparities, which is correct, but it also means the individual-level arbitrariness result is a demonstration, not an estimate.

**Reproducibility of the artifact is partial by the paper's own standard.** The admissibility definition requires kernel-library versions, batching and scheduler settings, "and no run here recorded its kernel-library version, and the vLLM runs did not record batching or scheduler settings, so **every record in this study is incomplete by this definition**." Similarly, vLLM's FP8 path on the MI350X scales activations per tensor, so a record's score depends on the other records in its batch — which quietly breaks the "fixed decision function" framing for that arm, and the paper acknowledges it only in an appendix. The proposed protocol is therefore stricter than the study that proposes it, which is admirable transparency but weakens the claim that the protocol has been demonstrated end-to-end.

**Gaps a reviewer would press on.** (i) No NVIDIA BF16/FP8 run for the *tabular* models, so the most common production path for tabular deployments is untested and Tier 2 tabular evidence comes only from Apple CPU/GPU and one MI350X; the tree ensembles also have no BF16/8-bit path, so their Tier 2 "ranking changes" are actually driven by other families moving. (ii) The AMD "TF32 setting" is not TF32 (three BF16 partial products), leaves 1/8 of output columns with unidentified arithmetic, and is compared under a label that invites over-generalization. (iii) The mechanistic model of §6.1 was built after Tier 1 results were read and its MLX match fitted after Tier 2 — a prospective test on retrained seeds passes on M5/4090/H100 but **fails on an unseen RTX A6000** for one model, with a post hoc explanation (one hidden layer perturbed, not two). (iv) One state, one year, one disease cohort (1999–2008, coarse single-race coding); the prespecified ACS results are unweighted, and the weighted sensitivity check moved **4 of 96** reference verdicts — i.e. the *weighting choice* moves at least as many verdicts as Tier 1 arithmetic does. (v) An excluded RTX A4500 node returned NaN under strict FP32; treated as a node fault, which is plausible but unverified.

**Significance.** The result that matters for practice is not "FP8 audits are different" but "the audit report must name the contract, because a deployer's migration can silently re-decide who is selected" — supported concretely by FP8 selecting 54.4% instead of 44.7% and flipping three HMDA pass-fail verdicts, all of which involve the derived Joint category rather than a person's protected attribute (a detail the paper reports, and one that materially weakens the "three employment/lending audits flipped" reading). Whether regulators adopt any of Table 6 is outside the paper's evidence; its own comparison to NYC bias audits, SR 26-2 and FDA change control is a gap analysis, not a demonstration of feasibility.

## Strengths

- A genuinely clean causal intervention (byte-identical weights, canary-verified precision settings, SHA-256 before every run) rather than the field's usual "another model" comparison.
- Uncommon honesty about scope: the abstract itself states that Tier 1 contradicts the exciting version of the thesis, that FP8 "is not shown to be fairer", and that the paper makes no harm claim.
- Every contract run twice in separate processes, with the one non-bit-reproducible case (vLLM BF16) reported rather than smoothed over, plus a third run that moved 4 decisions.
- The stability-interval formalism separates numerical spread from sampling spread — a distinction that makes "the verdict is robust" a checkable statement with a scale (numerical width / sampling width).
- Pre-registration in spirit: plan, expectations and a kill criterion committed before running, with the failed expectations and the unmet kill criterion both disclosed.
- The cost table makes the protocol actionable: 4.33× TF32 on an H100 but 1.59× on an RTX 4090 means small auditors can afford strict FP32 on consumer hardware.
- Excellent negative-result reporting: LR and trees never flip under Tier 1; three of the MLP's 16 Tier-1 flip sets are empty; exact ties change no verdict.

## Limitations

- Tier 1's effect is essentially nil at the verdict level (0/228), and its individual-level effect is ≤0.035% of decisions — so "numerical execution contracts matter" is true mostly for records within ~1e-3 of the threshold, a population that any auditor already knows is fragile.
- Tier 2 conflates precision with engine, kernel, weight rounding and batching; the paper says so, which is honest but leaves the practically important question (how much of the FP8 effect is arithmetic?) unanswered.
- Only one state/year for census and lending, one disease cohort from 1999–2008 for health, coarse race coding, and no clinical deployment data; two of four LLM operating points are degenerate.
- Small counts everywhere the group story lives (31 one-way flips, 41 vs 39 TF32 flips, 27-vs-4 split), so group concentration is demonstrated rather than measured.
- Contract records are incomplete by the paper's own admissibility standard (no kernel-library versions; vLLM batching/scheduler unrecorded), and the MI350X FP8 path is batch-dependent.
- Several headline contrasts are post hoc, and the verdict-counting rule was set post hoc, so the 6-of-17 / 0-of-43 split should be read as exploratory.
- Tabular models are "adequate rather than tuned"; a better-tuned MLP would sit further from the threshold and flip less, so flip rates are model-quality-dependent in an unquantified way.
- No actual audit, deployment or hiring decision was observed; the harm pathway (contract shopping) is argued, not measured.

## Relevance

直接命中"Operator Precision, Determinism & Error Propagation"核心议题：这是目前少见的把"同一份序列化权重在不同执行契约下是否还是同一个决策函数"做成受控实验的工作，且同时覆盖 TF32/MLX relaxed 的 Tier 1 因果臂与 BF16/INT8/FP8 的部署臂，可作为跨硬件数值漂移与量化退化的下游效应（判决翻转、群体集中）参考模板。对"Reliability under Extreme Performance Optimization"亦有价值：它给出了 FP8 服务对个体决策的边际影响（4.12% 的 Diabetes 决策）与成本倍数（H100 上 4.33×/7.55×），是把精度降级的"正确性代价"量化到决策层的少见案例；公平性审计本身只是应用场景，而非可迁移的方法贡献。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. How much of the FP8 effect is arithmetic rather than engine? Running vLLM FP8 vs vLLM BF16 vs transformers BF16 on the *same* card with kernel logs captured would separate the three, but the paper's own step changes card count and attention backend simultaneously.
2. If thresholds were recalibrated per contract on that contract's own training scores (the paper deliberately does not), would the 24B FP8 DP pass survive? The 54.4%-vs-44.7% selection shift suggests the pass is largely a threshold-placement artifact — worth testing directly.
3. Why does the RTX A6000 break the mechanistic flip-set prediction for one MLP (one hidden layer rather than two)? A kernel-dispatch trace would say whether this is a cuBLAS heuristic or a framework fallback, and it is the one place the paper's prospective claim fails.
4. Do integer point scores (CHA₂DS₂-VASc, LACE) really immunize bedside tools, as §9 argues? A BF16/FP8 audit of a continuous-score readmission model *and* its integer-score predecessor on the same cohort would make the clinical argument concrete.
5. Can the stability interval be made cheap? Sampling 3–5 contracts rather than all of `C`, plus the margin-profile diagnostic in Fig. 1A/B, might recover most of the information at a fraction of the cost — the paper measures cost but does not propose a subsampling rule.
6. Does the 0.001 "movement" tolerance need to scale with group size, as §6.2 suggests? A principled cell-size-dependent tolerance would change how many of the 12 ranking changes count at all.
