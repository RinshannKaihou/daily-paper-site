> **一句话总结：** 作者用信号检测论把"模型内部是否表征某属性"与"答案是否读出该属性"拆成两个环节，在 6 个开源基座模型及其后训练版本上发现：德语文本的"人写/AI 写"信息在线性探针里稳定可解码（中位 d′ 1.35–3.70，甚至不如朴素词 n-gram 分类器），但模型自己的答案平均更不敏感（Δd′ = −0.34，18 个同 prompt 比较中 14 个下降，Qwen3-8B 对新闻段落甚至 d′ = −1.01 即反向排序）；模型缝合把变化定位到早期层，逐层激活补丁显示中层以上变化由"文本之后的位置"（问题、答案提示）承载而非文本位置——而在可完全复现的公开任务（否定句真假判断）上后训练方向相反，修复了基座模型的错误读出（探针 AUC = 1，Apertus 答案 d′ −0.88 → SFT 4.54），并用 2,000 条正确标注证明了训练信号足以设定读出方向。

> **TL;DR:** On three German authorship corpora (300 chat messages, 103 news segments, 238 textbook summaries) linear probes decode human-vs-AI authorship at median d′ = 1.35 / 2.66 / 3.70 while word n-gram logistic regression matches or beats them (chats d′ = 1.73 [1.44, 2.06]; news and textbook AUC ≥ .991), yet the models' own answers are at or below chance and often inversely ordered — Qwen3-8B post-trained reaches d′ = −1.01 on news (base −0.36) and Gemma 3 12B −1.09 on chats in chat format (base −0.36). Post-training lowers answer sensitivity by Δd′ = −0.34 [−0.56, −0.11] (t(5) = −3.82, p = .012 over six family means; 14 of 18 paired comparisons; −0.29 across three prompts), entirely during SFT (OLMo 2 textbook: −0.12 base → −0.50 SFT → −0.44 final). Stitching puts the change in early layers (Apertus: first 8 base layers under SFT uppers restore d′ = +0.59 vs base +0.43 and SFT −0.04) and layer-16 patching shows text-token states transfer nothing (Apertus −0.11 vs unpatched −0.04) while question-position states transfer the whole difference (+0.41 into SFT; −0.42 into base). On public negated statements the sign flips: probes hit ceiling (AUC = 1) in every model, base answers are 0.80 (OLMo 2) and −0.88 (Apertus), SFT repairs them to 3.36 and 4.54; a 2,000-example LoRA training study then shows labels set the readout direction (held-out news d′ = 4.28, newspaper +2.18, textbook +1.39, but chats −0.58), and answers follow a base-model news probe (ρ = .68) rather than n-gram classifiers (partial ρ = .06).

## 核心贡献

- **把"表征 vs 读出"变成可测的因果问题，而不是探针相关性修辞。** 借 Brunswik 透镜模型与信号检测论，把判断拆成两个透镜：编码（文本线索 → 内部表征，用线性探针与探针迁移度量）与读出（表征 → 答案，用 d′ 与判据 c 度量），并明确指出"判据偏移只能降低准确率、无法产生低于随机的敏感度"，从而排除"阈值没校准"这一常见解释。
- **给出"表征存在但答案反向使用"的直接证据。** 三个德语语料上探针与 n-gram 分类器都能良好区分人写/AI 写，而模型答案大量接近或低于随机，且不是二值答案错得多，而是二值答案背后的分级证据被反向排序（例如 OLMo 2 在短聊天上"AI"回答比例随选项顺序从 89% 变成 0%）。
- **用阶段检查点做时间定位：变化发生在 SFT，而非偏好优化或 RL。** OLMo 2、Apertus、OLMo 3 的逐阶段检查点显示，多数情形下 d′ 在监督微调后既已完成变化，DPO/RL 阶段几乎不再改动。
- **用模型缝合 + 逐层逐位置激活补丁做空间定位。** 缝合（早期层权重）与补丁（文本之后的位置：问题、答案提示、最后位置）给出互补结论：变化"坐落在"早期层权重里，并在网络中段以后由"文本之后的位置"承载，而文本自身状态在中层以上可整体互换而不改变判断。
- **提供可完全复现的公开反例，并用训练实验把后验解释变成事前预测。** 否定句真假任务上后训练修复读出；随后用 2,000 条正确/刻板/随机标签的 LoRA 实验证明训练信号决定读出方向，并在条目级别验证聊天答案跟随基座模型的"新闻作者身份探针"而非表层 n-gram。

## Methodology

**Materials.** Three German corpora, all paired human/AI, with the human original plus all derived texts treated as one resampling group so no result can rest on topic recognition: (i) 300 short chat messages (61 human, 239 AI) written by students in a lab course, with AI counterparts generated by Claude Opus 4.6 and GPT-5.4 under naive / style-matched / student-revised ("optimized") prompts, truncated to 25 words; (ii) 103 segments (38 human, 65 AI) from 14 local-newspaper articles that were never digitally available, generated by GPT-5.4 from topic + key facts + a leave-one-out style profile, then revised to remove nine student-identified AI surface markers; (iii) 238 textbook summaries (119 matched pairs) from biology, psychology and business textbooks.

**Models.** Six open-weight families, 4–12B: Gemma 3 4B/12B, Qwen3 8B, OLMo 2 7B, Apertus 1.5 8B, OLMo 3 7B (Think / Instruct / RL-Zero from one base). Base vs. final post-trained for all; stage-wise checkpoints for OLMo 2 (SFT → DPO → RLVR), Apertus (SFT → RL → DPO → final) and OLMo 3.

**Elicitation.** German prompt in plain completion ("raw") format usable by base and post-trained models alike; post-trained models additionally queried with their chat template. The decision variable is dv = log P("KI") − log P("Mensch") summed over spellings, averaged over both answer-option orders; conditions where the two answer words hold < 30% of probability are excluded from inference.

**Measurement.** Sensitivity d′ = √2·z(AUC) from the ROC of dv (so a below-chance d′ means the graded evidence is ordered inversely, not that many binary errors were made), criterion c at dv = 0 with log-linear correction. Inference uses grouped bootstraps over source groups (1,000–2,000 resamples); the primary post-training test treats model family as the unit (t-test over six family means), with multilevel random-effects and Holm/BH corrections as checks.

**Probes and baselines.** Per-layer linear probes (standardization + PCA rotation + L2 logistic regression, inner grouped CV for λ) on the residual stream at the last prompt position, 10-fold outer CV holding out whole source groups, plus a label-shuffle control. Compared against character n-grams (2–4), word n-grams (1–2), 14 surface statistics, a probe on the average input embedding, and an externally trained English detector (EditLens).

**Causal interventions.** (a) Steering along the class-mean direction at the answer position with 100 norm- and scale-matched control directions; (b) model stitching — lower ℓ layers of one checkpoint + upper layers of another, justified by CKA, perplexity checks and endpoint reproduction; (c) activation patching — replacing the residual stream of a recipient checkpoint with a donor's at selected positions (instruction / text / question+answer cue / final position / all) and at 12 layers across depth. A control judgment (counterfact statements) where post-training changed little was stitched as a sanity check.

**Public replication and training study.** Truth judgments on Marks & Tegmark's 748-pair statement sets (simple geography, negated geography, diverse facts) and the Deceptive Opinion Spam corpus, with probes, transfer probes, stitching and patching. Then OLMo 2 7B base fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 16, 1 epoch, 3 seeds) on 20,000 Tülu-3 conversations plus either 1,994 authorship judgments with correct labels (C1), formality-stereotype labels (C2), random labels (C3), or an unrelated binary topic judgment in the same layout (C4); evaluated on held-out German news (10kGNAD) and the three test corpora, with an item-level test of whether answers track a base-model news probe.

## Results & Highlights

- **Probes and n-gram baselines find the information; the answers do not.** At two thirds of network depth the probe's median d′ is 1.35 on the short chats (range 0.95–1.80 across model conditions), 2.66 on news segments (1.65–3.14) and 3.70 on textbook summaries (2.36–3.73); a plain word n-gram logistic regression does at least as well (chats d′ = 1.73 [1.44, 2.06]; news and textbook AUC ≥ .991), and a probe on the pre-network input embedding reaches d′ = 1.48 on the chats. The answers, by contrast, are often below chance: Qwen3-8B post-trained on news d′ = −1.01 (raw) / −1.30 (chat) versus −0.36 for its base model, and Gemma 3 12B on chats −0.81 / −1.09 versus −0.36.
- **Post-training lowers answer sensitivity by about a third of a d′ unit.** Across the six family means the change on the identical raw prompt is Δd′ = −0.34 [−0.56, −0.11], t(5) = −3.82, p = .012, with 14 of 18 paired comparisons negative and every family negative on average; a multilevel model gives −0.33 [−0.53, −0.13]. Across three prompt wordings the estimate is −0.29 [−0.54, −0.03] (39 of 54 comparisons), but only 8 of 18 cells decrease under all three prompts, and per corpus only the news decrease is reliable (−0.42 [−0.75, −0.08], p = .024) versus −0.41 (p = .066) for textbooks and −0.19 (p = .22) for chats. Six individual decreases survive Holm correction, five of them on the textbook summaries.
- **The change is complete after SFT, not after preference optimization.** OLMo 2 on textbook summaries: −0.12 (base) → −0.50 (SFT) → −0.46 (DPO) → −0.44 (final RL); Apertus: +0.43 → −0.04 (SFT) → 0.00 → −0.03 → −0.04. Exceptions are narrow: OLMo 3 Instruct on chats declines only at the preference stage (−0.09 after SFT, −0.30 after DPO).
- **Stitching puts the carrying weights in the early layers.** With the first 8 of 32 Apertus layers from the base model and everything above from the SFT model, the hybrid reaches d′ = +0.59 [0.41, 0.80] — better than either parent (base +0.43, SFT −0.04); conversely the first 8 SFT layers under base upper layers abolish discrimination (−0.50 [−0.74, −0.25]). For OLMo 2 the first 16 base layers restore base-level performance (−0.01 vs. −0.12 base, −0.50 SFT) and the first 6 SFT layers suffice for the full deficit (−0.48), with the change accumulating gradually over layers 1–14 rather than arising in one layer.
- **Patching locates it in the prompt: everything after the text, nothing in the text.** At layer 16, exchanging text-token states changes nothing (Apertus SFT with base text states: −0.11 vs. −0.04 unpatched; base with SFT text states: +0.43, unchanged), whereas exchanging only the question positions transfers the full difference in both directions (base states into SFT: +0.41 for Apertus, +0.04 from −0.50 for OLMo 2; SFT states into base: −0.42 and −0.61 from −0.12). Inside "after the text", the answer cue carries most and the question tokens little (Apertus base with SFT states: −0.46 at question tokens vs. −0.75 at all after-text positions). Across 12 layers this holds from roughly layer 12–14 onward in both models.
- **The public replication runs the other way.** For negated geographic statements every model's probe is at ceiling (AUC = 1, d′ = 4.54 for 748 pairs), OLMo 2's base answers sit at d′ = 0.80 and Apertus's are inverted at −0.88 (nearly always "False", c = 2.6 and 3.0), and SFT repairs them to 3.36 and 4.54 while the probes stay at ceiling. A probe trained on simple (unnegated) statements of the same base checkpoint separates the negated statements with the wrong sign (OLMo 2 −1.28, Apertus −1.43) and after SFT with the right one (+0.69, +3.05). The repair localizes to the positions after the statement from mid-depth (97% at layer 16 in OLMo 2), with the statement's own states contributing 82% [73, 91] at layer 12 in OLMo 2 but only 23% in Apertus, 45% in Gemma 3 12B and 19% in Gemma 3 4B.
- **The stated base rate stops being a criterion shift.** Moving the stated AI share from 20% to 80% moves the criterion in 48 of 72 comparisons (42 reliably, mean |Δc| = 1.19) while d′ moves only 0.14 on average, but the size of the evidence shift is diagnostic: base models shift dv by 0.18–1.40 nats (6–51% of the ideal-observer ln 16 = 2.77), five of six post-trained models by 3.67–10.93 nats (1.3–3.9× the benchmark), the exception being OLMo 3 Think at 1.21 (base 1.17).
- **Two thousand labeled examples set the readout direction.** Adding 1,994 correctly labeled news authorship judgments (C1) to 20,000 background conversations lifts d′ to 4.28 on held-out news (at the subset ceiling), +2.18 on the newspaper segments (C1 − C3: +2.23 [1.56, 3.17]) and +1.39 on textbook summaries (+1.36 [1.11, 1.64]) — but drives chats to −0.58, worse than random labels (C1 − C3: −0.36 [−0.69, −0.06]). Item by item, C1 answers to the chats correlate with a base-model probe for news authorship (ρ = .68 [.60, .73] vs. −.11 for C0 and .04 for C3), and that correlation survives controlling for word n-grams (partial ρ = .63) while the n-gram scores add little (.06 [−.06, .18]).
- **The review judgment is the weak case.** Probes separate truthful from deceptive hotel reviews at d′ = 2.5–3.0 while all answers sit between 0.00 and 0.86; post-training only reshapes the readout (Gemma 3 4B 0.45 → 0.08, Qwen3 0.06 → 0.50, Gemma 3 12B 0.52 → 0.86) and mostly removes a shared polarity heuristic (which explained 41–90% of base-model judgment variance, 2–32% after post-training).

## Authors & Credibility

Single-author paper by Jochen Laubrock (University of Potsdam), written in the idiom of experimental psychology rather than ML — Brunswik's lens model, signal detection theory, grouped-bootstrap inference over source groups, and an explicit term-mapping appendix so that psychology and ML readers can follow. The apparatus is unusually thorough for a single author: six model families, three corpora, stage-wise checkpoints, three causal interventions, two public replications, and a controlled fine-tuning study with four baselines. Code and all derived result tables are released (github.com/laubrock-lab/represented-but-misread), with a one-command reproduction entry point for the negated-statement analysis; the unreleased parts are the confidential German corpora and the generated training texts, and the reason is stated per corpus. The methods section discloses that AI tools implemented and ran the analyses and drafted/revised the manuscript, with the complete analysis history in the repository and the author taking responsibility for design and interpretation — a disclosure that is becoming standard but is worth weighing when reading the large appendices.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The contribution is not "probes know more than answers" — that is established (Burns et al. 2024; Marks & Tegmark 2024; Ackerman & Panickssery 2025) — but the combination of (a) a signal-detection measure that can distinguish a miscalibrated threshold from genuinely inverted evidence, (b) stage-wise checkpoints to time the change at SFT, and (c) stitching plus position-resolved patching to place it. The claim that the change lives in early-layer *weights* but reaches the answer through post-text *positions* is a genuinely useful, and slightly counterintuitive, separation, and it departs from the common "fine-tuning only touches upper layers" picture.

**Rigor, and where it strains.** Several specific worries, in rough order of importance:

1. *The headline corpora cannot be shared.* All three authorship corpora are unreleasable (unpublished student chats without consent, confidential newspaper text, copyrighted summaries), and per-text model outputs are omitted because identifiers point to unpublished texts. The reproducible part is the negated-statement case, which runs in the *opposite* direction. So the paper's central empirical claim — that post-training *impairs* authorship readout — rests on data no reader can inspect or re-analyze; only the derived tables are public.
2. *"Below chance" is a claim about graded log-odds, not about the emitted answer.* In 23 of 36 plain-prompt conditions the model gave the same binary answer to more than 95% of texts, so d′ is computed from dv, effectively a confidence rating. The author is explicit about this, but it means "the model says the wrong thing" is not supported for most cells; the supported claim is "the evidence ordering behind a near-constant answer is inverted." The two are not interchangeable for auditing purposes, and the word "misread" in the title invites the stronger reading.
3. *The information being misread is lexical.* Word n-grams (d′ = 1.73 on chats; AUC ≥ .991 elsewhere) match or beat every probe, and a probe on the raw input embedding reaches 1.48. The author concedes this ("our results concern the readout of ordinary cues rather than of any deep representation of authorship"), but it reframes the finding: this is a study of how post-training reweights surface style cues, not of a rich authorship representation.
4. *Stitching hybrids overshoot, and the control does not rule out that the overshoot is an artifact of judging near chance.* 6 of 18 Apertus and 4 of 18 OLMo 2 hybrids lie reliably outside the parent range (max deviation 0.46 and 0.15), while on the counterfact control — where d′ is 1.37–1.78, far from chance — no hybrid deviates much. The author states plainly that the appropriate control would be "a judgment near chance that post-training left unchanged", which was not run. This is the right admission, but it leaves the localization claim ("where the weights that carry the difference reside, rather than where it is computed") weaker than the discussion sometimes implies.
5. *Selective inference.* The family of 18 final-vs-base comparisons was defined after inspecting all comparisons, and the three-prompt replication shows only 8 of 18 cells consistent; the question-first prompt makes two families change sign (OLMo 3 Think +0.38, Qwen3 +0.04) and the family-level test non-significant (p = .16). The averaged claim −0.29 survives, the per-cell claim does not.
6. *Signal-detection invariance only approximately holds.* Bias manipulations moved d′ by 0.10–0.16 on average with 28 of 72 bootstrap intervals excluding zero — not negligible next to a mean post-training effect of 0.34, and the author uses exactly this to justify comparing checkpoints only on identical prompts. Fair, but it also means d′ here is not a pure sensitivity measure.
7. *Small-n corpora and one unbalanced training study.* The newspaper corpus is 14 articles, so article-level bootstraps are likely too narrow (author's own caveat); the training study uses one base model, LoRA rank 16 on ~2% of the mixture, and its background condition C0 failed to reproduce the official SFT decline, so the causal link between "labels set the readout" and "official post-training breaks authorship readout" remains inferential. The C2 stereotype labels turned out to be confounded with text length, topic and generator — a confound the author discovered only after the runs.

**Significance.** If it replicates, the practical message is sharp and testable: post-training can degrade a judgment while leaving the decodable information intact, and small amounts of mislabeled-in-spirit data can flip the sign of the readout. That is a concrete audit target ("compare the state at the question positions with the answer") and a caution for anyone treating fine-tuning as format-only. It is also a caution against over-reading: at 4–12B, on German prompts, with lexical cues, on a task the models are famously bad at anyway.

## Strengths

- The lens-model + signal-detection framing does real work: it converts a vague "probe–answer gap" into two separable quantities and rules out the threshold explanation with an argument (a criterion shift cannot produce below-chance sensitivity) rather than a hand-wave.
- Stage-wise checkpoints are used properly — the SFT-only timing result, replicated across OLMo 2 and Apertus and across corpora, is the cleanest finding in the paper.
- Two causal interventions on the same checkpoints, plus probe transfer at two sites (text vs. answer position), triangulate a claim that neither probes nor steering alone could carry.
- The public negated-statement replication is a model of how to handle unreleasable data: same analyses, one script, opposite sign, and it strengthens rather than decorates the argument.
- The training study makes a pre-registered-style item-level prediction (chat answers should follow the base model's news probe), then tests it against n-gram partials — and reports that the news probe beats the n-grams, which is the hard version of that test.
- The limitations section is unusually candid (ceiling compression, hybrid overshoot, missing matched control, post-hoc test family, confounded stereotype labels, 14-article corpus).

## Limitations

- Corpus scale and secrecy: 300 chats, 103 news segments from 14 articles, 238 textbook summaries; none shareable, so external reanalysis of the central claim is impossible.
- Model scope: 4–12B only; the author flags that below-chance judgments might vanish at larger scale, and nothing here tests a frontier model.
- Language scope: authorship prompts are German; only the truth and review judgments use English. No English authorship prompt was run, and no test of whether German morphological cues drive the "long words = AI" heuristic.
- The measured "representation" is a linear probe on one position (or averaged text positions); non-linear decodability, SAE features, and multi-token readouts are untouched, so "the representation is intact" means "a linear probe still transfers."
- Localization covers two models for authorship (and two corpora), four models for negation, one corpus for the layer sweep, and 100 statement pairs; the review judgment was not localized convincingly at all.
- Patching at a single layer cannot separate "where the difference sits at that layer" from "which computation produced it"; the author notes that exchanging statement states at all layers up to a depth at once would be needed.
- Training study: one model, LoRA only, 1 epoch, 3 seeds, and a background condition that does not reproduce the official SFT effect, leaving the origin of the real decline open.

## Relevance

直接落在"LLM 隐状态与可解释性"：它把探针可解码性与答案实际读出分开，并给出跨检查点的因果定位方法（缝合 + 逐层逐位置补丁），对"模型知道却说错"这类审计问题提供了可操作的测量范式。对做后训练副作用评估、探针可靠性与表征审计的工作也有直接方法价值——尤其是它明确指出探针能解码不等于模型会用，且小规模标注数据即可翻转读出方向。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the effect survive scaling? A 30–70B replication on the same German corpora (or an English equivalent) would tell whether below-chance authorship readout is a small-model artifact or a general post-training side effect.
2. Which SFT conversations install the misreading? The census found zero authorship-judgment requests in Tülu 3 and the Apertus mixture, so the candidate carriers are indirect — e.g., conversations where the assistant talks about AI, or style-heavy rewrite tasks. Ablating or enriching those slices would be the decisive experiment the author explicitly leaves open.
3. Can a matched near-chance, unchanged judgment serve as the stitching control the paper lacks? Without it, the 0.46 overshoot in Apertus hybrids cannot be attributed to the changed judgment rather than to judging near threshold.
4. Is the "formal, nominal style = AI" heuristic a stale stereotype that tracks model generations? If the heuristic is learned from text *about* AI rather than from AI text, the error should shift as chat-style generators change — testable by regenerating the corpora with successive model generations.
5. Does an inference-time intervention at the answer-cue positions (rather than at the text) restore authorship d′ without damaging instruction following? The paper's localization suggests the target; the paper's own stitching overshoot suggests the risk.
6. How much of the inverse ordering is German-specific? The AI messages were judged "more verbal and lexically more diverse", which may be a property of German group-chat register as much as of the generators.
