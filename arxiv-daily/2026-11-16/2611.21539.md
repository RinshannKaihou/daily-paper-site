> **一句话总结：** 作者用输入匹配探针与校准输出逐段追踪 VLM 的双图匹配失败，发现两个断点——"完全相同副本被比较了却没被读出"与"语义对应存在于视觉 token 却几乎没被比较"，并定位到 16–32 个跨图注意力头（占参数 0.02%），仅训练这些头的 Q/K 投影即可让 SPair-71k 四选一准确率提升 25–28 个点；无约束 LoRA 也收敛到同一批头，但其增益的 83–86% 来自"喂给这些头的输入变了"而非头自身权重。

> **TL;DR:** Tracing correspondence from visual tokens to the answer letter in Qwen3-VL-2B/4B/8B/32B, InternVL3.5-8B and Gemma-3-12B, the paper separates two failures: on synthetic exact-copy matching Qwen3-VL-8B answers 41% while an input-matched answer-position probe reaches 64% (+21.3 pp over calibrated output) — the comparison happens but the output ignores it; on SPair-71k semantic correspondence the region readout beats the answer-position probe by +16.3 pp (Qwen3-VL-8B) and +25.9 pp (InternVL3.5-8B) — the information never reaches the answer. A 32-head (Qwen3-VL-8B) / 16-head (InternVL3.5-8B) cross-image head set is causally necessary: uniformly redistributing its image-2→image-1 attention at fixed mass costs 17–32 pp on synthetic/HPatches, versus ≤6.8 pp for random or attention-matched sets. Training only those heads' query/key projections (1.68M / 1.50M params, ≈0.02%) lifts SPair-71k from .430 to .712 (+28.3 pp) and .354 to .605 (+25.1 pp), but a parameter-matched all-layer rank-2 LoRA still wins by 8.0 / 12.3 pp.

## 核心贡献

- **测量框架**：把"读到什么"与"说出什么"分开——用只看模型可见位置的输入匹配探针（Probeans）、特权区域读数（Probereg/MaxSim）与多项逻辑校准后的输出（Acccal）对齐比较，避免把"校准能补上的输出偏差"误判成"信息丢失"。
- **两个不同的失败点**：(1) 合成精确副本任务上，比较确实发生了且结果到达答案位置，但输出不采用，命名能力与校准都救不回来；(2) 语义对应（SPair-71k）的信息存在于语言模型的视觉 token 中，但只被极弱地比较，从未到达答案位置；同实例对应（HPatches）大体介于两者之间且基本可解。
- **机制定位**：找到一个小的跨图注意力头集合 Ck（Qwen3-VL-8B 32 个头/层 1–24，InternVL3.5-8B 16 个头/层 3–23），在保持注意力总质量不变的前提下打散这些头对图 1 的注意力，可掉 15–32 个点，而随机或注意力质量匹配的头集最多掉 7 个点。
- **注意力规律**：这些头先被"显著性"门控（图 1 中被标记的 ref 区域），再在候选位置间按内容相似度加权；把标记移到干扰项位置，模型答案会跟着标记走——说明"看图"首先是找标记，其次才是比内容。
- **修复与收敛**：只训练这些头的 Q/K 投影（约 0.02% 参数）即恢复语义对应的大部分缺口并迁移到 HPatches 与 BLINK；不带任何限制的全层 LoRA 也收敛到同一批头，且其增益的 83–86% 通过"改变这些头的输入"实现，构成"充分但非排他"的汇聚通路。
- **可复现纪律**：所有决策规则在运行前冻结，附录 C 逐条列出未达标/临界的预设判据，全部数字由独立脚本从逐样本原始输出重算。

## Key Contributions

- **A readout ladder that separates availability from emission.** Input-matched probes (answer-position residual, PCA-256 + multinomial logistic regression, layer/ℓ2 chosen by 5-fold CV on dev data) are compared against a calibrated four-letter output (Acccal) and against privileged region readouts (MaxSim / Probepair) whose only advantage is seeing the annotated correspondence locations.
- **Two distinct loci, not one generic "VLM blindness".** Exact-copy failures are output-side: the comparison is computed and reaches the answer position, yet neither calibration, subspace ablation/amplification, nor probe-direction steering changes the answer. Semantic failures are comparison-side: Probereg exceeds Probeans by +16.3/+25.9/+14.3 pp on SPair-71k across Qwen3-VL-8B, InternVL3.5-8B and Gemma-3-12B.
- **A causal, mass-controlled localization.** Ck is found by ranking heads on how strongly option tokens attend to their corresponding image-1 region on Syn dev items, then blocking image-2→image-1 attention. Two mass-preserving operations (Uniform, Shuffle) confirm the effect is about *where* the attention goes, not *how much* there is.
- **A minimal repair with matched controls.** Arm A (rank-16 LoRA on Q/K of Ck only) is compared against layer-matched random heads (B), attention-mass-matched heads (M), value-only adapters (C), and parameter-matched / default all-layer adapters (D, E), plus query-only variants (AQ, BQ, MQ) that provably touch no head outside the chosen set.
- **A convergence result via attention transplantation.** Rather than only claiming that unconstrained training "uses the same heads", the paper inserts the trained model's image-2 attention rows of Ck into the base model (Transplant) and the base rows into the trained model (Restore), decomposing the effect into Upstream (changed inputs) vs Local (changed weights).
- **A probe-free diagnostic.** Selectivity Δref of the fixed head set, read directly from attention, orders SP accuracy across every trained arm (Spearman 0.95 over nine Qwen3-VL-8B and 0.89 over seven InternVL3.5-8B models; 0.93/0.96 after adding out-of-sample matched arms).

## Methodology

**Tasks.** Every item is a four-way multiple-choice question over two images: image 1 contains a marked reference location `ref`; image 2 contains four candidates A–D, one of which corresponds to `ref`. Three tasks: *Syn-squiggle* (closed cubic splines through 30 anchors — unnameable), *Syn-known* (ten nameable outline classes, size- and stroke-matched), *HP* (same-instance point correspondence from HPatches homographies), *SP* (semantic keypoint correspondence from SPair-71k). Splits are by seed range / sequence / official split, with 1,000 dev and 1,000 test items per task (500 each for Syn-known). Distractors are at least 8% of the image diagonal away; within-item shape pairs are separated by a minimum Chamfer distance. BLINK semantic/visual correspondence validation splits (<200 items each) are used only for transfer.

**Models.** Qwen3-VL-2B/4B/8B/32B, InternVL3.5-8B, Gemma-3-12B. Because Qwen3-VL mixes SigLIP2-Large (2B/4B) with SigLIP2-SO-400M (8B/32B), scale comparisons are made only within encoder-matched pairs. For Qwen3-VL-8B the authors verify that perturbing image 1 leaves image 2's encoder output unchanged, so cross-image comparison *must* happen inside the language model.

**Scoring and readouts.** A single forward pass returns log-probabilities of the four letter tokens at the scoring position, with final norm and unembedding in fp32 (removes bf16 ties); the answer format was selected on dev data to guarantee ≥0.9 median letter mass and ≥95% greedy/argmax agreement. Each item is scored under four cyclic letter orders with shape positions fixed. Readouts: `Acc` (argmax), `Acccal` (multinomial logistic calibration on dev), `Probeans` (input-matched answer-position probe), `Probereg = max(MaxSim, Probepair)` over 30×30-pixel regions on marker-free images, with Probepair counted only if it beats shuffled labels by ≥5 pp. CIs are 2,000-sample item bootstraps.

**Head selection.** Heads are scored by how strongly option tokens attend to their corresponding image-1 region on Syn dev items; blocking the top-k heads' image-2→image-1 attention with k chosen on dev data yields C32 (32 heads) for Qwen3-VL-8B and C16 (16 heads) for InternVL3.5-8B. Knockouts are run in three forms — Block (remove mass), Uniform (same mass spread evenly), Shuffle (same mass permuted) — against 10 layer-matched random sets and 3 attention-matched sets.

**Repair arms.** All arms share one recipe (2,000 SPair-71k training items in the same four-letter format, cross-entropy on the correct letter, AdamW 2 epochs, batch 8, cosine schedule, LR per arm from {1e-4, 3e-4, 1e-3} on 400 validation items, 3 seeds for A/C/D). A: rank-16 LoRA on Q and K of Ck (1.68M params Qwen, 1.50M InternVL); AQ: query-only (0.85M/0.75M); B: A on three layer-matched random head sets; C: value-only; D: rank-2 LoRA on all attention projections of all layers (1.92M, parameter-matched to A); E: rank-16 all-layer (15.3M). Primary test set is 1,000 SP test pairs never used earlier.

**Transplantation.** For a trained arm with gain *g*, Transplant inserts the trained model's post-softmax image-2 query rows of Ck into the base model; Restore inserts base rows into the trained model. Recovered/removed fractions are gated by a pre-registered criterion (≥0.25, positive under bootstrap, exceeding all five random head sets). The Transplant decomposition splits rows computed by *base QK weights on trained inputs* (Upstream) from *trained weights on base inputs* (Local).

**Budget.** ~90 GPU-hours, mostly on a single RTX PRO 6000; code, item generators, split identifiers, head indices and adapters promised for release.

## Results & Highlights

- **The exact-copy gap survives calibration and is large.** On Syn-squiggle, Qwen3-VL-8B answers 41.3% (Acc) / \~42.7% (Acccal) while the input-matched answer-position probe reaches 64.0%, i.e. Probeans − Acccal = **+21.3 pp [+17.9, +24.6]**; Syn-known gives +19.0 pp [+14.8, +23.0]. Across families the calibration-robust gap is +15 to +25 pp, and Qwen3-VL-2B on known shapes is the single cell where calibration closes it (−0.2 pp).
- **Real correspondence shows no such hidden answer-position gap**, which is what makes the synthetic result diagnostic rather than a probe artifact. On HPatches Qwen3-VL-8B: Acc .770, Acccal .778, Probeans .804 (**+2.6 pp** [+0.6, +4.8]); on SPair-71k: Acc .438, Acccal .460, Probeans .433 (**−2.7 pp** [−6.1, +0.7]). InternVL3.5-8B is .543/.536/.542 on HP and .355/.369/.310 on SP (−5.9 pp) — the probe is *below* calibration, ruling out "the probe just has more capacity".
- **Semantic correspondence is in the visual tokens but not at the answer.** Probereg − Probeans on SP = **+16.3 pp** (Qwen3-VL-8B), **+25.9 pp** (InternVL3.5-8B), **+14.3 pp** (Gemma-3-12B); in no cell is Probereg attained at the vision-encoder output, and greying out everything except the drawn markers drops the region readout by 5.8–16.1 pp in five of six model–task cells, so Table 3 uses marker-free images.
- **The comparison is carried by a small head set, and mass-preserving redistribution proves it.** On Qwen3-VL-8B, Uniform/Shuffle knockouts of C32 cost **17.2 / 18.2 pp on Syn** (base Acc 41.2) and **32.2 / 31.8 pp on HP** (base 76.2), versus at most **3.6 pp** for ten layer-matched random sets and at most **0.6 / 1.8 pp** for three attention-matched sets; Block costs 15.2–24.2 pp on Syn but pushes 49–53% of items out of the residual-norm band. InternVL3.5-8B mirrors this (20.2/22.0 pp on Syn, 16.8/14.6 pp on HP, ≤6.8 pp for matched controls).
- **On SP the base model's semantic answers do not run through these heads at all**: knockouts change SP accuracy by at most 3.6 pp (Qwen3-VL-8B) and not measurably for InternVL3.5-8B (−0.8 to −0.6 pp). This is the honest converse of the localization claim and the exact gap the repair targets.
- **Training only Q/K of Ck repairs most of the semantic deficit.** Arm A lifts SPair-71k from .430 → **.712 (+28.3 pp [25.3, 31.1])** for Qwen3-VL-8B and from .354 → **.605 (+25.1 pp [22.4, 27.8])** for InternVL3.5-8B with 1.68M/1.50M trainable parameters (≈0.02%), and still gains +23.9 / +19.0 pp with only 500 training items. Query-only AQ (which cannot touch any head outside Ck) retains 86–90% of the effect (+24.4 / +22.7 pp).
- **The gain is not "more attention to the other image".** A beats layer-matched random heads B by **+3.6 pp [2.0, 5.2]** (Qwen) and **+2.1 pp [0.8, 3.4]** (InternVL); it beats heads matched on cross-image attention mass M by **+3.3 / +2.5 pp**, and AQ beats MQ by +2.1 / +4.3 pp — all four intervals above zero, with matched arms carrying *more* trainable parameters.
- **The targeted repair is not the strongest repair.** The parameter-matched all-layer rank-2 adapter D reaches .792 (Qwen) and .728 (InternVL) on SP, beating A by **8.0 and 12.3 pp**; the value-only arm C even beats A by 3.5 pp on Qwen (.747 vs .712). A is presented as a sufficient lever, not a SOTA adapter — and on InternVL3.5-8B, arm A *costs* 16.0 pp on Syn-squiggle (.526 → .366) and 15.0 pp on Syn-known (.926 → .776).
- **Unconstrained fine-tuning converges on the same heads, mostly through their inputs.** Transplanting D's Ck attention rows into the base model recovers .345–.514 of its gain in Qwen3-VL-8B over three seeds and .213–.295 in InternVL3.5-8B, versus −0.04 to +0.07 for random head sets; the Upstream variant (base QK weights applied to trained inputs) recovers .282/.233, i.e. **83%/86%** of the full transplant, while Local (trained weights on base inputs) recovers .043/.035 with intervals reaching zero. Restoring base rows inside D removes only .133–.166 / .022–.049, below the necessity criterion — hence "sufficient, convergent, not exclusive".
- **Selectivity reads the repair without a probe.** Δref over the fixed Ck rises from .0107 to .0426 (A) and .0771 (D) on Qwen SP, and ρref from 1.30 to 1.86 / 3.04, because distractors' share of image-1 attention on ref falls from .198 to .123 / .072 while the target's stays .20–.24. Across arms, Δref orders SP accuracy with Spearman **0.95 / 0.89** (0.93/0.96 once the later matched arms are included).

## Authors & Credibility

Single-authored by **Zhaoning Zhang (University of Toronto)**, who is also cited in the related work as the author of two prior mechanistic VLM papers (`Encoded but Disconnected`, arXiv:2610.00024; and a 2026 mid-layer-intervention null result) — so this is a continuation of a coherent research line rather than a standalone claim, and the author already knows where naive patching produces nulls. The related work is unusually current and specifically about *this* question (Fu et al. 2025 on encoder readouts beating VLMs, Liu et al. 2025 on visual representations inside the LM, Shahgir et al. 2026 on nameability, Veikherman et al. 2026 on encoded-but-unused errors, Gandikota & Bau 2026 on gaze heads). Methodological citations are the right ones (Hewitt & Liang on probe control tasks, Zhang & Nanda on activation-patching metrics, Makelov et al. on subspace-patching illusions, Grant et al. on off-distribution patched states). No institutional compute cluster is claimed: ~90 GPU-hours on one RTX PRO 6000, which is consistent with the scale of the experiments. No apparent conflicts of interest; the paper is not benchmark-shopping a leaderboard.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The individual ingredients are borrowed — answer-position probes, head knockouts, LoRA-on-selected-heads (Mitra et al. 2025; Luo et al. 2025), and "visual information is present but unused" (Liu et al. 2025; Fu et al. 2025) are all prior art, and the paper says so. What is genuinely new is (a) splitting the phenomenon into two mechanistically distinct loci and showing that the *same* failure family contains both an output-side and a comparison-side case, (b) the mass-preserving knockout design (Uniform/Shuffle) that kills the "these heads just carry more cross-image attention" confound, and (c) the Transplant/Upstream/Local decomposition showing unconstrained adapters act on these heads mainly by changing their *inputs*. Point (c) is the most interesting result in the paper and is stronger than the headline "0.02% of parameters" framing.

**Rigor — genuinely above average.** Decision rules, thresholds and item sets were frozen before each run; Appendix C lists *every* pre-specified criterion that failed or landed within a small margin (the SP-region presence threshold missed for Gemma at +14.3 vs 15 pp; A−B missed 3 pp for InternVL at +2.1; the necessity criterion failed for D in both models; the probe-direction positive control failed outright; the mass-preserving in-distribution criterion failed on HP for every operation). Reporting the failure of your own positive control — that the probe direction does *not* steer the output more than random directions — is exactly the behavior that makes the remaining claims credible. Every number is recomputed from raw per-item outputs by separate analysis code, residual-norm bands are checked for every intervention, and the fp32 unembedding fix for bf16 ties is the kind of detail that usually goes unreported.

**Weak points.**
1. **The task format carries a lot of the claim.** Every experiment is a four-way multiple choice over two images where image 1 contains an explicit drawn marker labelling `ref`. The paper's own decoy experiment shows the heads are *salience-gated*: mark a distractor's location and the distractor's attention (and the model's answer) moves there. That means the "cross-image comparison" being localized is partly a marker-following computation, and the authors concede language-specified references are future work. Absolute accuracies are also not comparable to the correspondence literature: there are **no PCK@0.1 or standard SPair-71k numbers anywhere**, only this 4-way MCQ proxy, so the claim "SPair-71k accuracy +28.3 pp" should be read as "+28.3 pp on our 1,000-item 4-way variant", not as progress on semantic correspondence as the field measures it.
2. **The causal claim for the semantic case is post-hoc.** In the *base* models, knocking out Ck on SP changes accuracy by ≤3.6 pp (Qwen) and ≈0 pp (InternVL) — i.e. the heads do not carry semantic comparison before training. The heads are therefore localized on the *exact-copy* task and then shown to be the right place to intervene for the semantic task. That is a legitimate "sufficient lever" argument, but it is weaker than "we found the component that performs semantic comparison", and the abstract's "16 to 32 attention heads that carries the comparison" slightly overstates what the base-model SP knockouts show.
3. **The repair is dominated by a generic adapter.** D (1.92M params, all layers, rank 2) beats A by 8.0 and 12.3 pp at essentially the same parameter count, and C (value-only on the same heads) beats A on Qwen. So "0.02% of parameters" is a mechanistic-insight headline, not a practical efficiency result; the paper is honest about this in §6.1, but the title-level framing invites the wrong reading.
4. **Collateral damage is real and under-weighted.** On InternVL3.5-8B, arm A drops Syn-squiggle from .526 to .366 and Syn-known from .926 to .776 — a 15–16 pp regression on the very task the heads were selected on. Table 6 reports it in bold-free plain text and the Ethical Considerations section flags it, but a practitioner reading "training 0.02% of parameters repairs correspondence" would not expect a 16 pp loss on exact-copy matching. No general-capability evaluation (MMMU, MMBench, OCR) is run, so the scope of the side effect is unknown.
5. **Small effects with small intervals.** Several headline comparisons are +2.1 to +3.6 pp with lower bounds of 0.2–0.8 pp, over three random head sets and three seeds; the hierarchical intervals are reported (good) but the effect sizes are close to what seed noise plus head-set choice could produce. The Spearman 0.89–0.96 selectivity–accuracy relation is fit over only 7–9 arms, several of which are the same adapter with different seeds.
6. **Coverage.** Mechanism and repair are established on exactly two 8B models (both SigLIP2-based, both open-weight); Gemma-3-12B and the other Qwen sizes contribute measurement only. The "two failure points" taxonomy may well be a property of this model family plus this MCQ format.
7. **Single author, no independent replication, and the head indices/adapters are "will be released"** — the central Transplant/Restore results are the least likely to be reproduced quickly, since they require exact per-head row surgery on grouped-query attention.

**Significance.** High for the interpretability-of-VLMs subfield: the two-locus distinction is a real conceptual contribution, and the Upstream/Local decomposition is a template for asking "did fine-tuning change the weights or the inputs?" of any adapter. Moderate for practitioners: the targeted repair is neither the strongest nor the safest option available.

## Strengths

- A clean, falsifiable experimental design: every claim has a matched control (random heads, layer-matched heads, attention-mass-matched heads, magnitude-matched random subspaces, shuffled labels, image ablation, residual-norm bands).
- The Uniform/Shuffle knockouts at fixed attention mass are the single best methodological idea in the paper — they convert "these heads matter" into "the *pattern* of these heads' attention matters", which is what a mechanistic claim actually needs.
- Uncommon honesty: Appendix C enumerates failed pre-registered criteria, including the failure of the positive control that the probe direction should steer the output.
- The Transplant / Upstream / Local decomposition is a genuinely informative result that goes beyond the paper's own headline: the broad adapter's gain is 83–86% attributable to *changed inputs* to the comparison heads, with the heads' original QK weights already sufficient.
- A probe-free, training-free diagnostic (Δref on a fixed head set, Spearman 0.89–0.96 with accuracy) that other groups can apply to their own fine-tunes immediately.
- Bilingual relevance aside, the writing is dense but well-organized, with a findings/evidence/controls table (Table 7) that maps each claim to its control and section.

## Limitations

- Task family is narrow: two images, one explicitly marked reference, four candidates, single-letter answer. Language-specified references, free-form answers, and multi-object scenes are untested.
- No standard correspondence metrics (PCK, matching accuracy under the usual protocols), so the numbers cannot be placed against the correspondence literature; the 4-way MCQ construction (distractors ≥8% of the diagonal, Chamfer-separated shapes) may be easier than real correspondence benchmarks.
- Mechanism claims rest on two model families at 8B; Gemma-3-12B and Qwen3-VL-2B/4B/32B are measurement-only.
- Semantic-case causality in the base model is not established (SP knockouts are null); the head set is localized on synthetic exact copies and then reused.
- The "computed but not read out" conclusion is inferred from the non-causality of a linear probe's direction; the actual channel through which the exact-copy answer is lost is not identified (the authors list this as future work).
- BLINK transfer uses subsets of fewer than 200 items, so those intervals are wide and the one null transfer (InternVL3.5-8B BLINK-SC, +5.8 pp with interval including zero) is essentially uninformative.
- No general-capability or safety evaluation after head-targeted training, despite a documented 16 pp regression on synthetic exact-copy matching.
- Reproducibility is promised rather than demonstrated: head indices, adapters, and item generators are "will be released".

## Relevance

直接命中"多模态大模型的可解释性与失效机理"这一兴趣点：它把 VLM 双图匹配的失败拆成"算出来但没读出"和"根本没比较"两类，并给出可复用的头级定位＋最小修复流程，对做 VLM 诊断、注意力头干预和参数高效微调的工作都有直接方法借鉴价值。其"充分但非排他通路"与 Transplant/Upstream 分解，尤其适合作为后续做机制干预实验的方法论模板。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the two-locus taxonomy survive when the reference is specified in language ("the red cup in the left image") rather than by a drawn marker, given that the heads are demonstrably salience-gated to the marker?
2. What happens on standard SPair-71k PCK@0.1 evaluation — does training Ck's Q/K actually improve keypoint transfer, or only this 4-way letter-choice proxy?
3. Which channel *does* carry the exact-copy answer on Syn, if not the probe's 3-dimensional subspace? The paper shows amplifying or ablating it at one layer does nothing; a multi-layer or MLP-pathway search is the obvious next step.
4. Since D beats A by 8–12 pp and C beats A on Qwen, what is the minimal union of "change the comparison" and "change the compared features" that matches D at A's parameter budget?
5. Can the InternVL3.5-8B collateral drop (Syn-known .926 → .776) be avoided by mixing synthetic exact-copy items into the 2,000-item SP training set, or by regularizing toward the base attention pattern on non-SP inputs?
6. Does Δref on a fixed head set predict downstream fine-tuning quality in settings with no clean correspondence task — e.g. does it track OCR or chart-reasoning gains after LoRA, or is it specific to geometric matching?
7. Is the 16-head InternVL3.5-8B set stable across random seeds of the head-ranking procedure, or does the selection criterion pick a different (equally functional) set each time?
