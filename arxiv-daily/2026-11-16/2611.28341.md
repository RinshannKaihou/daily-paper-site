> **一句话总结：** 作者构建了带真实文件系统/SQLite/HTTP/shell 的故障注入测试床 RetryRealistic（8 工具、9 类故障、30 任务），发现 2,100 次故障运行中仅 19–68% 正确恢复、6–26% 以"自信编造"收场，且失效模式主要由故障类型（返回部分数据 38.3% 编造）而非模型决定，机制是"静默摄取"；一个以结构化工具输出为准、只发纠正指令不硬拦截的 Monitor 把正确率从 45.6% 提到 56.3%、编造率从 17.3% 降到 10.8%。

> **TL;DR:** On a 2,100-run fault-injection testbed (RetryRealistic: 8 real tools, 9 fault types, 30 multi-step tasks, 4 agents), correct recovery spans 18.9% (Llama-3.1-8B) to 67.6% (DeepSeek-V4), while confident fabrication — a wrong answer asserted as fact — hits 25.9% and 19.0% for Llama-8B and Claude Haiku 4.5 versus 5.5% for DeepSeek. Fault type explains 1.6× more fabrication variance than model identity (η²=0.061 vs 0.038): misleading "partial success" payloads drive 38.3% fabrication versus 15.3% for real OS permission-denied, which instead yields 78.1% honest failure. A code-grounded Monitor with corrective (not blocking) vetoes lifts correct recovery by 10.7 pts and cuts fabrication by 6.5 pts, but barely helps the worse case — partial success stays at 32.1%.

## 核心贡献

- **RetryRealistic 测试床**：在真实操作系统临时目录（真 chmod 权限）、真实 SQLite 文件、本地 Flask mock HTTP、24 条白名单命令的受限 shell 上做中间件式故障注入；覆盖 8 个工具、9 类故障（含显式持久性语义）、30 个多步任务（10 易 / 12 中 / 8 难），每个任务都有预设的替代恢复路径和可机器校验的 ground truth。
- **静默失效普遍存在**：2,100 次故障运行里正确恢复仅 19–68%，6–26% 的运行以"自信编造"结束——不是报错，而是把一个从未取到的数字当成事实陈述。
- **故障类型比模型更能决定失效模式**：返回部分/误导数据的故障（partial success 38.3% 编造）主要诱发编造，硬拒绝类故障（permission denied 78.1%、auth required 53.4% 诚实失败）主要诱发诚实失败；方差分解 η²_fault=0.061 > η²_model=0.038。
- **命名并验证"静默摄取"（silent ingestion）机制**：编造集中在"第一次接触就把故障数据当有效数据接受、既不重试也不标记"的运行里；重试 3 次以上的运行编造率仅 7.0%，0–1 次重试的高达 20.7%，把"编造源于重试累积错误上下文"的常见假设反转了。
- **验证优先的 Monitor**：以代码把断言值与工具输出对齐（S1 接地、S2 复述、S3 重算、S4 LLM 目标漂移），且每次否决都是可修改重提的纠正指令而非硬拦截；正确率 45.6→56.3%、编造率 17.3→10.8%。
- **方法论警示**：LLM 行为分类学不可靠（两名裁判同类别一致率 67%、κ=0.58），而"答案 vs ground truth"的客观三分类稳定（一致率 94%、κ=0.91），应优先后者。

## Key Contributions

- **A realistic-but-controlled substrate.** RetryRealistic replaces dictionary-as-filesystem simulations with a real OS temp directory, real SQLite files, real subprocesses, and a deterministic local HTTP server, while faults are injected by middleware so the injection point is a task-specified (tool, argument-substring) pair. 30 tasks, 9 fault types, 8 tools.
- **Quantified silent failure.** Base recovery is unreliable (18.9–67.6% correct) and a substantial minority of failures are dangerous, not safe: 5.5–25.9% of faulted runs end in confident fabrication.
- **A causal-ordering claim about failure mode.** Fault content, not model capability, is the primary explanatory variable, established three ways: variance decomposition, a controlled payload-sanitization experiment, and retry-stratified outcome tables.
- **A mechanistic account (silent ingestion).** Fabrication happens at first contact with plausible-but-degraded data, before any retry loop or goal drift — 554 of 609 (91%) partial-data runs repeated the faulted call at most once.
- **A deployable mitigation plus honest accounting of where it fails.** The verification-first Monitor is a net gain on both axes but explicitly does not fix truncation-based fabrication, because a total computed from truncated rows is grounded in the rows actually received.
- **An evaluation-methodology result.** Behavioral taxonomies graded by LLM judges are too noisy to support claims at this granularity; objective answer-vs-ground-truth grading is not.

## Methodology

**Substrate.** Four ingredients, all "real where it matters": (i) a sandbox manager materializes a fresh temp directory per run from a task YAML — files with real chmod bits, SQLite databases built from schemas and seed rows, hot-swapped mock-server routes, executable scripts; (ii) a tool registry of 8 tools (`file_read`, `file_write`, `list_dir`, `file_exists`, `shell_exec`, `sql_query`, `http_request`, `calculator`), with `shell_exec` limited to a 24-command read/transform allowlist and a 30 s timeout; (iii) a fault injector that intercepts the first call matching the fault target and then applies the fault's persistence rule; (iv) a monitor agent that observes the trace.

**Fault taxonomy with persistence semantics.** Transient faults (`timeout`, `network_error`, `db_locked`) succeed on retry with p=0.5. Persistent faults (`permission_denied`, `auth_required`, `not_found`, `malformed_output`, `partial_success`, `nonzero_exit`) never succeed on retry, forcing tool switching, alternative sourcing, or honest reporting. Three faults originate in the real substrate (kernel EACCES from mode-000 files; 503/401 from the actual HTTP server) rather than the injector.

**Tasks.** 30 multi-step tasks with difficulty defined operationally as minimum required tool calls (2–7) and distinct tools used (2–6). Each has a designed alternative recovery path and machine-checkable ground truth, so "correct" includes valid workarounds.

**Protocol.** 4 ReAct agents (Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.1-70B, DeepSeek-V4, Claude Haiku 4.5), temperature 0.2, 20-step budget, served via NVIDIA NIM / OpenRouter / Anthropic API. Each task × compatible fault type + a clean baseline, 3 seeds → 615 runs per model per arm (525 faulted + 90 clean), 2,460 per arm, 2,100 faulted. Arms: baseline, full Monitor, code-only Monitor, no-CoT, honesty prompt, plus two targeted arms (self-remediation on permission denied; payload sanitization).

**Grading.** Three objective outcomes — CORRECT / FABRICATED (asserts wrong or unsupported values) / HONEST-FAILURE (reports unavailability, states no result, or produces no answer). An answer that only says results were written to a file counts as not-correct. Judge: Claude Opus 5.5, independent of agents and Monitor.

**Monitor design.** Two layers. Step-level supervision activates after any error appears, uses rule-based signals (identical-retry count, consecutive-error count) and may inject a directive, capped at 5 per run. Final-answer verification is a 4-stage cascade: S3 recompute (re-derive scalar aggregates from retrieved rows) runs before S1 grounding (asserted headline values must appear in some tool output), S2 surface asks the agent to restate checkable values, S4 is an LLM goal-drift check reserved for the residue. Crucially, a naive LLM "is this fabricated?" veto was rejected after it over-vetoed correct answers nearly as often as bad ones (39 correct vs 46 wrong out of 118 vetoes).

## Results & Highlights

- **Base recovery and fabrication, by model, on 2,100 faulted RetryRealistic runs:** correct 18.9% (Llama-3.1-8B) / 33.0% (Llama-3.1-70B) / 62.9% (Claude Haiku 4.5) / 67.6% (DeepSeek-V4), pooled 45.6%; fabrication 25.9% / 18.7% / 19.0% / 5.5%, pooled 17.3%. The sharpest point is Haiku: it recovers almost as often as DeepSeek (62.9% vs 67.6%, −4.7 pts) yet fabricates 3.5× as often (19.0% vs 5.5%).
- **Fault type dominates failure mode (pooled across models):** `partial_success` yields 39.5% correct / 38.3% fabricated / 22.2% honest, versus `permission_denied` at 6.6% / 15.3% / 78.1% and `auth_required` at 33.3% / 13.2% / 53.4%. `db_locked` is the mildest (55.3% / 6.1% / 38.6%). Variance decomposition: η²_fault = 0.061 versus η²_model = 0.038, i.e. 1.6×.
- **Payload-controlled sanitization (27 data-target tasks × 4 models × 3 seeds):** holding injection point, tool, and persistence fixed and varying only the payload gives 35.2% fabrication for a misleading partial result, 19.8% for a valid-but-empty result, and 16.0% for an explicit denial — a 19.2-pt gap from content alone. Within-model: DeepSeek 25% partial vs 0% denial; Llama-8B nearly flat (37% vs 33%).
- **Retry stratification inverts the naive story (persistent faults, N=1,535):** 0–1 retries → 20.7% fabrication, 2 retries → 11.9%, 3+ retries → 7.0% fabrication and 82.2% honest failure. On partial-data faults 554 of 609 runs (91%) repeated the faulted call at most once, and 27.8% of those fabricated.
- **Monitor, pooled over all four agents:** correct 45.6% → 56.3% (+10.7 pts), fabrication 17.3% → 10.8% (−6.5 pts), honest failure 37.1% → 33.0%. Code-only (S1–S3) is weaker but positive: 54.7% correct / 13.4% fabricated. Per-model correct: Llama-8B 19→39%, Llama-70B 33→55%, DeepSeek 67.6→73.9%; Haiku trades −5.7 pts correct for −6.9 pts fabrication.
- **The Monitor's blind spot is the signature fault:** on `partial_success`, fabrication only falls 38.3% → 32.1% with the full cascade, and the code-only variant is *worse than baseline* (40.7%). In 224 of 324 full-Monitor partial-success runs the Monitor never acted at all, because it engages only after an error and truncation produces none.
- **Deliberation ablation:** removing chain-of-thought leaves correct recovery flat (45.6% → 45.5%) but raises fabrication to 25.6% (+8.3 pp), worsening for every model and every fault — `partial_success` 38→52%, `malformed_output` 16→28%. An honesty prompt helps where an error is visible (fabrication 17.3→13.7%; permission-denied 15→6%, auth 13→7%, network 14→10%) but not on partial success (38→40%).
- **Self-remediation is capability-gated:** allowing the agent to fix the environment under `permission_denied` lifts DeepSeek's correct recovery from 9.7% to 81.9%, while Llama-8B goes 0% → 4.2% on the same 24 tasks.
- **Judge reliability:** behavioral taxonomy agreement 67% (κ=0.58) between Haiku 4.5 and Sonnet 4.6 on 216 stratified baseline runs, versus 94% agreement (κ=0.91) for the objective outcome grading on the same runs.

## Critical Assessment

*Novelty.* The contribution is less "new idea" than "new measurement with a mechanism and a fix." Fault injection for agents exists (MAS-FIRE, ReliabilityBench, agent-chaos, AgenTracer), and self-verification is well-trodden (Reflexion, Self-Refine, Huang et al.'s "LLMs cannot self-correct"). What is genuinely new is the pairing: a real-substrate fault bed with ground truth, an outcome metric that separates *fabricated* from *honestly failed*, and the retry-stratified evidence plus payload-sanitization experiment that jointly rule out the reflex explanation ("agents loop, then hallucinate under context pressure"). Naming a mechanism and then showing it predicts where the mitigation fails is the strongest part of the paper.

*Rigor.* The multivariate claims are supported three independent ways rather than one regression, and the paper is candid that the variance-decomposition effect sizes are small in absolute terms. But two statistical points deserve scrutiny. First, η²_fault=0.061 means fault type explains only ~6% of fabrication variance; the "1.6× more than the model" framing is a ratio between two small numbers, and calling the failure mode "governed more by the fault type" overstates a modest effect. Second, the claim that `partial_success` is "fabrication-prone" rests on a post-hoc rule (fabrication ≥30% and above honest failure) that only 2 of 4 model cells satisfy — DeepSeek sits at 27.2% and is excluded, and Llama-8B fails honestly more there. The threshold is doing more work than the prose admits.

*Reproducibility.* Good on paper: code, toolkit, and traces released, deterministic mock server, seeds fixed. Weak spots: the mock HTTP server serves pre-seeded JSON, so the "network error" and "auth required" faults come from a Flask server the authors control rather than a real remote service; the shell is a 24-command allowlist; and the Monitor is Claude Sonnet 4.6 while the judge is Claude Opus 5.5 — mappings between these model names and reproducible checkpoints are the perennial problem, and exact re-runs of the *intervention* arm depend on a proprietary model's behavior at a specific version.

*Red flags and unexplored weaknesses.* (1) **Intention-to-treat inflation.** The headline rates pool all 525 faulted-condition runs per model, but in 118 of the 2,100 the agent never made the targeted call, and in 84 of 307 `partial_success` runs the payload "changed nothing" because the target returned a single row or an error. That means roughly 27% of the paper's signature fault injections were inert, and the 38.3% fabrication figure for `partial_success` is diluted by runs where nothing was degraded — the true per-actual-fault rate is higher, which cuts in the paper's favor on the headline but makes cross-fault comparisons (which have different inert rates) non-comparable. No per-protocol numbers are reported. (2) **Single fault per run.** Real deployments hit cascading and correlated failures (a timeout that then causes a bad cache write); the bed cannot express composition. (3) **Task construction by the authors.** Every task has "a designed alternative recovery path," which makes the honest-failure option always available and likely inflates honest-failure rates relative to tasks where the correct move is ambiguous. (4) **Haiku's numbers may be family-biased.** The agent under study (Claude Haiku 4.5), the Monitor (Sonnet 4.6), and the judge (Opus 5.5) are all Anthropic models; the paper reports no cross-family judge check (e.g. GPT/Gemini judging the same 216-run sample) to rule out same-family affinity in either direction, despite having already built the machinery to do exactly that. (5) **No cost accounting for the Monitor as a product.** It is 6.4 extra calls per run against an 11-step agent — a ~58% inference overhead — and no arm compares it to simply spending those tokens on more agent steps or retries, so "oversight is a net gain" is a gain relative to doing nothing, not relative to the obvious cheaper alternative. (6) **The monitor strength is confounded with the monitor architecture.** Sonnet 4.6 is stronger than every agent tested, so the ablation cannot separate "verification-first design works" from "a stronger model supervising a weaker one works"; the code-only arm partly addresses this but the S4 LLM stage still carries the strongest model in the system.

*Significance.* High for anyone shipping tool-using agents: the fabricated-vs-honest distinction is the right production metric, and "silent ingestion" is a name practitioners can act on. The negative result about the Monitor on truncated data is arguably more useful than the positive one.

## Strengths

- The fabricated/honest-failure split is the correct dependent variable for deployment risk, and the paper builds its whole evaluation around it rather than around task success.
- The mechanism claim is falsifiable and was actually tested against its alternative (retry-loop hypothesis) with retry-stratified data — and the alternative loses.
- The payload-sanitization experiment (35.2% vs 16.0% with everything but content held fixed) is a clean, small, convincing causal probe.
- The Monitor's design follows from the diagnosis: because fabrication is a *missing detection step*, the fix is post-hoc code grounding on structured outputs, not more reflection. The rejected naive LLM veto (39 correct vs 46 wrong vetoes) is reported honestly and motivates the cascade.
- Explicit, quantified reporting of where the method fails (`partial_success` 38.3% → 32.1%, code-only 40.7%, Monitor silent in 224/324 runs) — rare and valuable.
- The evaluation-methodology result (κ=0.58 for behavioral taxonomy vs κ=0.91 for objective grading) is immediately reusable by anyone running LLM-judged agent evals.
- The self-remediation arm (DeepSeek 9.7% → 81.9%, Llama-8B 0% → 4.2%) is a nice demonstration that real substrates reveal capabilities simulated ones cannot.

## Limitations

- One fault per run; no cascading, correlated, or partial-recovery-then-second-fault sequences, which is where production agents actually break.
- The `partial_success` fault is often inert (84/307 runs changed nothing), and no per-protocol (fault-actually-fired) rates are given, so headline fault-specific numbers mix two populations.
- Absolute effect sizes are small (η²=0.061 / 0.038); the "fault type beats model" framing rests on a ratio of two modest variances.
- The Monitor is a stronger proprietary model (Sonnet 4.6) than any agent and the judge is Opus 5.5; no same-size-monitor condition, so architecture and overseer capability are entangled.
- Monitor overhead (~6.4 calls/run, a ~58% increase over the agent's 11 steps) is not compared against spending those calls on the agent itself.
- S3 recompute covers scalar aggregations only and demonstrably misfires on derived values (converted totals), vetoing correct answers — gains are understated, but the code-only arm's number is also correspondingly noisy.
- Mock HTTP with pre-seeded JSON, 24-command shell allowlist, no pagination/rate-limit/tool-schema-version faults — ecological validity is better than dictionary-filesystems but still a curated subset of real tool failure.
- Only 55 of 609 partial-data runs repeated the faulted call, so whether repetition would help is unresolvable with this data (the authors say so).
- 30 tasks is small for the number of conditions; per-(model × fault) cells have limited seed depth (3), and the `>30% fabrication` cell classification is threshold-sensitive.

## Relevance

直接命中"agent 可靠性 / 工具使用 / 评估方法"这条线：它把"编造"从模糊的幻觉讨论变成了一个可测量、可归因（静默摄取）、可部分缓解（代码接地的验证级联）的工程问题，而且给出了一个能让评估结果稳定的客观判分协议。对做 agent 评测、工具调用鲁棒性、以及 LLM-as-judge 可信度的研究都值得精读；对想直接落地的团队，第 6.5 节的失败边界（截断数据上 Monitor 基本无效）比正面结果更重要。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. What are the per-protocol rates — restricting to runs where the fault actually fired and the payload actually degraded — and do the fault-type rankings survive that restriction?
2. Does a Monitor of the *same* capability as the agent (e.g. Llama-70B monitoring Llama-70B) retain the gain, or is the whole effect carried by overseer strength? A 2×2 of (agent capability × monitor capability) would settle this.
3. Since S3 recompute already re-derives aggregates, could the cascade be extended to re-derive *from a re-fetch* (detect that the row count is implausible for the query) — i.e. can truncation be made detectable without waiting for an error, which is the paper's stated blind spot?
4. How does fabrication behave under cascading faults, where the first fault's degraded data becomes the input to the second tool call? Silent ingestion should compound, and that prediction is untested.
5. Is there a training-side fix? The CoT ablation (+8.3 pp fabrication) suggests the deliberation step matters; would a small amount of fine-tuning on "flag incomplete results" traces beat the 6.4-calls-per-run Monitor on cost?
6. Do the same-family judge effects matter — re-grading the 216-run stratified sample with a non-Anthropic judge would test whether the Haiku fabrication rate (19.0%) is deflated or inflated by Claude-Opus-as-judge.
