01How the audit was built

Sections 2 & Appendix B — a natural experiment, not a new benchmark.

  1. Replayed the top five entries of all four GPU MODE 2026 NLA competitions on one NVIDIA B200, with the organizers' own generator and checker; only the input distribution changed.
  2. QR grid: 7 scored shapes × 10 families × 5 conditioning settings × 3 seeds = 1,050 evaluations (945 distinct). Checker bounds: ‖R − QTA‖₁ ≤ 20nε‖A‖₁    ‖QTQ − I‖₁ ≤ 100nε    ε = 2−23
  3. Two controls: vendor torch.geqrf, and a bfloat16-degraded baseline (round inputs, then call vendor) to test whether the checker catches gross error at all.
  4. VSCL was assembled by an eight-stage agent workflow per claim: enumerate, packet, search reusable results, state before proving, blind adversarial statement review, then prove under fixed accept criteria.
  5. Reuse trial: 60 proof tasks from 54 papers, no-library vs read-only VSCL, three repetitions, GPT-6 Sol — 360 attempts, all successful.
VERNA — proposed, not built
recognize→test against formal bounds→certificate of trust

Three steps and six Turing-derived design goals; no implementation, no evaluation. The paper's answer to its own audit is still a research programme.

Same pattern in all four competitions: 20/20 entries passed their own scored cases, 18/20 failed on fresh inputs. Under clean-container reruns, Cholesky reproduced 4/5 entries' failures and symmetric eigendecomposition 3/5.

Failures are structural: the winner returns non-finite factors on every rank-deficient input of order 32, 176 or 352; rank 5's 357 failures spread over all ten families (row-scaled 60/105, near-collinear 65/105, banded 42/105).

Tests can be specialized: 4 of 5 entries contain code that detects the organizers' constructions — the rank-4 entry factors only 768 of 1,024 columns at order 1024.

The checker's blind spotbfloat16-degraded control

619 / 1,050 outputs rejected overall — including all 300 at orders 32 and 176 — but only 49 of 450 at orders ≥1024. The 20nε tolerance grows to 2.5× the bfloat16 unit roundoff at n = 4096, so the test goes blind exactly where kernels scale.

Strengths 6

  • Controlled and replayable. Same kernels, generator, checker, GPU; audit record, hashes, environments and replay script are public.
  • Failures diagnosed, not counted. Non-finite factors on every rank-deficient order-32/176/352 input; one entry factors only 768 of 1,024 columns.
  • Self-undercutting honesty. Reports that the n-scaled tolerance weakens the competition's own test, and that the vendor eigensolver fails 38 inputs.
  • A real reuse trial with a pre-declared three-criterion binning rule and an explicit negative case: C1 tasks get 10.5% more expensive.
  • The RandNLA case study found defects in a published survey — missing thresholding, an implicit expectation — which is what formalization should buy.
  • Careful statement review. Blind back-translation separates "Lean implies source" from "source implies Lean."

Limitations 7

  • Reproducibility gap. QR discovery failures never rerun in fresh containers; the Cholesky entry with 85 discovery failures then passed all 15 reruns, so the headline counts may be inflated.
  • One of each. One generator, one GPU, one precision (float32), one checker lineage per competition.
  • Circular binning. C1/C2/C3 assigned by an LLM from the same model family; no human adjudication, no agreement statistic.
  • Cost-only endpoints. 360/360 success leaves no quality signal, no difficulty stratification, no confidence intervals.
  • VERNA is unimplemented — error-bound-guided input selection is never tested against random or adversarial testing.
  • The library misses the failures. VSCL proves standard-model bounds, not the underflow / non-finite regime that broke the kernels.
  • "Gaming" is inference. Shape-specialized fast paths are ordinary HPC practice; the 107× also rests on a 131 ms median baseline, not the quoted 419 ms.

03Questions for further exploration

  1. How many of the 155/327/357 failures survive fresh-container reruns under the QR protocol?
  2. How does detection rate scale with audit-set size — is 100 inputs enough for a 90% guarantee?
  3. Would human numerical analysts assign the same C1/C2/C3 bins, and how much of the 57–62% saving survives?
  4. With 360/360 successes, what is the real difficulty ceiling of the 60 extracted tasks?
  5. Does error-bound-guided input selection beat uniform random testing at equal budget?
  6. Can VSCL cover exceptional values and certify kernels that fail only on specific rank-deficient orders?
  7. Are the runner-up's row-scaled and mixed-conditioning failures algorithmic, or specialization?
