> **一句话总结：** 把 122B 的推理 MoE 按 REAP 从 256 个路由专家剪到每层 64 个（2-bit 下 11.62 GiB）后，模型在 LiveCodeBench 上会陷入逐字复读直到 token 上限（开发集 120 题里 44 题如此，未剪枝同量化版本 0 题）；作者先给出"采样惩罚 + 检测到复读就停止重抽"这一完全不动权重的强基线（130 道未触碰题目上 104 题），再叠加每层两个恢复专家与 rank-8 低秩修正的适配器（方法 B），把成绩提到 116/130（+9.2 pp，95% CI [+3.1, +15.4]），但增益主要来自"答错"而非"复读"，代价是 1.7 倍时延，且 HumanEval+/MBPP+ 上无增益甚至小幅下降。

> **TL;DR:** Pruning Ornith-1.5-122B (top-8 of 256 routed experts per layer, 2-bit UD-IQ2_XXS) down to 25% routed retention triggers verbatim reasoning loops that hit the token cap on 44/120 LiveCodeBench development problems, whereas the byte-identical unpruned 2-bit file loops on 0/120 and solves 107 vs 71 — so pruning, not 2-bit quantization, causes the failure. The cheapest repair (a presence penalty of 1.5 over a 20,480-token window, "S") already lifts development accuracy from 71 to 86; adding a tail-repetition detector that stops a reply and redraws (≤3 draws) lifts it to 104/130 on a pre-registered fresh panel. The paper's own method "B" — rank-8 LoRA-style corrections on the 64 kept experts plus two restored experts per layer, fitted layer-wise to the unpruned expert block on the pruned model's own activations — reaches 116/130 (89.2%) vs 104/130 (80.0%) for S+retry, +9.2 pp [+3.1, +15.4], McNemar p=0.007, but only +0.6 pp on HumanEval+ (145/164 vs 144) and −1.9 pp on MBPP+ (269/378 vs 276) at 1.7× wall time.

## 核心贡献

- **把"复读循环"归因于剪枝而非量化**：剪枝模型 C 保留的专家与未剪枝 2-bit 文件中的对应专家**逐字节相同**，因此两行结果的唯一差别就是每层被删掉的 192 个专家。开发集 n=120、16K 上限下，C 有 44 题在逐字复读中撞到上限，未剪枝 2-bit 为 0 题；4-bit → 2-bit 不剪枝不增加任何循环。
- **提出并量化了"先重抽"这一免费强基线**：一个只看文本流的尾部重复检测器（周期 P ≤ 6000 字符，检查窗口 max(5P, 200) 字符）能在中位数 6,003 token 处截断 74 条撞上限回复中的 72 条，且只会误伤 360 条成功回复中的 2 条；重抽把惩罚后的基线从 89/130 抬到 104/130。作者明确指出：**重抽带给两边的收益都比两臂之间的差距更大**，所以任何改权重的方法都必须在这个基线上比较。
- **给出一种不改动任何量化字节的权重复原方案**：A 在保留专家上加 rank-8 修正（LoRA 式、load-time adapter，`W + B_W A_W`，覆盖 W_gate/W_up/W_down），逐层对未剪枝模型的 routed-expert 输出做重构；B 再把每层 REAP 排序中的下两个专家原样恢复（含 router 行），用 straight-through estimator 训练到其部署量化值。
- **预注册确认实验**：最终入选方法、两臂解码策略、样本与判定规则在任何 confirmation/final 题目被请求之前冻结并记录 SHA-256；最终在 130 道未触碰题目上判定为 "leads"。
- **两个诚实的负结果**：一个针对性消除循环的免训练权重编辑（消去 4 个 attention head 在循环 token 上写入的主方向）在开发集上 81 vs 71，但在旧测试半集上 −4.8 pp [−12.9, +3.2] 未能迁移；单独的采样惩罚 S 在旧测试半集上的 +4.8 pp [−2.4, +12.1] 也不显著。

## Key Contributions

- **Controlled attribution of the loops to expert pruning.** Because C's kept experts are byte-identical to the unpruned 2-bit file, the two rows of Table 1 differ only in the 192 removed experts per layer: 44/120 problems end in a verbatim loop at the limit for C vs 0/120 unpruned, a +36.7 pp shift [+28.3, +45.8], alongside a +30.0 pp drop in solved problems [+21.7, +38.3]. Quantization from 4 to 2 bits without pruning adds zero loops.
- **Stop-and-redraw as the baseline a weight method must beat.** A text-only tail-repetition rule (period P ≤ 6000 chars, compares the last max(5P, 200) chars against the same-length stretch ending P earlier, checked every 256 chars) stops 72 of 74 limit-hitting development replies at a median of 6,003 tokens while falsely stopping only 2 of 360 replies that went on to pass. On untouched problems it takes the penalised pruned model from 89 to 104 of 130.
- **A weight-recovery method that leaves the quantized file untouched.** A adapts OptEP's expert weight update into a low-rank, load-time form; B additionally restores two experts per layer, trained in full precision through a straight-through estimator to their deployed quantized values, raising routed retention from 25% to 25.8% and the file from 11.62 to 11.88 GiB (+1.16 GiB adapter).
- **A pre-registered confirmation with a fixed decision rule.** Two paired confirmation seeds (n=64, pooled +6.2 pp [+0.8, +13.3]) gate a final single sample of 130 problems; B clears the rule (+9.2 pp, interval above zero, McNemar p=0.007).
- **Explicit scope statement and two negative results**, including a weights-free loop-suppression edit that failed to transfer and a refusal to claim the low-rank form beats the dense EWU it derives from.

## Methodology

**Model and pruning.** Ornith-1.5-122B: 48 layers, hidden width 3072, 256 routed experts of width 1024 per layer, top-8 routing plus one shared expert. Experts are scored with REAP on the authors' calibration data and the top 64 per layer kept. Model C is assembled by copying the kept experts' slices and router rows out of the public 2-bit file (UD-IQ2_XXS), so **no kept weight is requantized**. The unpruned teacher used for fitting is the 4-bit file (UD-Q4_K_XL).

**Serving.** llama.cpp `server` built from commit 36cbf415 (one local patch to chat-output parsing, off by default), single machine with 119 GB unified memory, 32 parallel slots, temperature 0.6, top-p 0.95, top-k 20, 32,768-token cap unless stated. Peak memory in the final comparison: 81.6 GiB.

**Problem sets.** LiveCodeBench with the official prompt and grader, easy and medium only (a pilot showed hard problems would exceed the compute budget). Three disjoint sets: *development* (120 problems, earlier half by contest date of releases v4–v6, on which every design choice was made), *old test half* (124 problems, later half of v4–v6 — used once for the weight-edit test, then reused as fitting data for A and B, so not a test set for them), and *fresh panels* from releases v1–v3 set aside before any generation: 64 for confirmation, 130 for the final comparison, in disjoint contests, screened for title/contest and statement overlap against all local fitting, calibration and generation files.

**Decoding interventions.** S is a presence penalty of 1.5 over a 20,480-token window — a long window because observed loop periods exceed the server's default 64-token penalty window. Retry is the client-side tail-repetition rule above, redrawing with a new seed for at most three draws per problem.

**Method A (low-rank corrections).** For each layer and each kept expert, each of W_gate, W_up, W_down becomes W + B_W A_W with rank 8, B_W initialised at zero so training starts exactly at C. Layers are fitted in order; for layer ℓ the objective is

  L_ℓ = Σ_t w_t ‖y_t − f_t‖² / Σ_t w_t ‖y_t − f_t⁰‖² + µ Σ_t w_t ‖f_t − f_t⁰‖²,

where x_t is the expert-block input when the pruned model (with layers 0..ℓ−1 already corrected) reads the fitting documents, y_t the unpruned model's routed-expert output on the same x_t, f_t the corrected pruned experts' output under the pruned model's own routing, and f_t⁰ the uncorrected output. The denominator makes the loss start at 1; µ = 0.01. Adam, lr 1e-4, gradient clipping 1, 25 passes over documents in batches of 64, checkpoint kept every 5 passes by held-out loss. Router, attention, shared expert and embeddings frozen. No coding score is consulted during fitting.

**Fitting data.** 1,018 calibration documents (256 held out) truncated to 2,048 tokens with 256 sampled positions, plus C's own 124 long replies on the old test half, truncated to 12,288 tokens with 256 sampled positions (24 held out for validation) — so the fit sees the hidden states the model occupies when it reasons at length, in the spirit of chain-of-thought reconstruction for pruned reasoning models. Development problems and fresh panels are never used.

**Method B (A + two restored experts).** A separate fit from C with the next two experts of each layer's REAP ranking restored from the unpruned 2-bit file together with their original router rows (66 experts per layer, router free to select them in place of kept ones). Restored experts train in full precision with a straight-through estimator to their deployed quantized values; quantized forward values are refreshed once per pass, not per step, and every checkpoint-selection validation score quantizes afresh. Everything else follows A, including data, order and token budget.

**Implementation checks.** Before any coding evaluation the authors verified that the server-applied adapter reproduces the fitted corrections from their own forward pass, that an adapter at scale zero leaves outputs byte-identical, and that every byte of C is unchanged inside the 66-expert file. Parity tolerances that proved tighter than the server's own numerical noise between mathematically identical settings were amended in dated notes before any coding result of the affected method existed.

**Statistics.** Paired by problem, differences in solved fraction in percentage points with a 95% bootstrap interval over problems (10,000 resamples), plus exact McNemar p on discordant pairs. A problem sampled twice has its two outcomes averaged before resampling, so two seeds never count as two problems.

## Results & Highlights

- **Loops are caused by pruning, not by 2-bit quantization.** On 120 LiveCodeBench development problems at a 16K limit, C (25% retention, 2-bit) solves **71** and ends **44** problems in a verbatim loop at the token limit, while the unpruned 2-bit file solves **107** with **0** looping and the unpruned 4-bit file solves **110** with **0** looping. The loop-at-limit share therefore moves +36.7 pp [+28.3, +45.8] with pruning, and solved problems drop +30.0 pp [+21.7, +38.3]. No limit-hitting reply is ever a success: 0 of 45 on the old test half at 32K.
- **Decoding-only repairs recover a large fraction of the loss.** A presence penalty of 1.5 over a 20,480-token window (S) takes the development set from 71 to **86** solved at 16K while cutting loop-at-limit from 44 to **25**. On the 124-problem old test half at 32K, C solves **74** and C+S solves **80** (+4.8 pp, [−2.4, +12.1], not significant). Stop-and-redraw on top adds far more: on the 130 untouched final problems S+retry solves **104 (80.0%)**, i.e. redrawing alone is worth 89 → 104 = **+11.5 pp**, larger than the entire gap between the two arms.
- **The pre-registered final comparison favours B.** On 130 untouched problems B+S+retry solves **116 (89.2%)** against **104 (80.0%)** for S+retry — **+9.2 pp, 95% CI [+3.1, +15.4]**, 15 problems gained and 3 lost, exact McNemar **p = 0.007**. The two 64-problem confirmation seeds were individually inconclusive (+4.7 pp [−1.6, +12.5] and +7.8 pp [−1.6, +17.2], p = 0.375 and 0.180) and only the pooled +6.2 pp [+0.8, +13.3] cleared the gate.
- **Developed on reused problems the effect looked bigger, as expected.** With retry on both arms on the 120 development problems, B+S solves **101** vs **88** for S (+10.8 pp [+3.3, +18.3], 18 gained / 5 lost, p = 0.011); without retry, single draw at 32K, B+S 93 vs S 84 (+7.5 pp [+0.8, +15.0]) and A+S 88 (+3.3 pp [−5.0, +10.8]).
- **The gain is not where the paper expected it.** After three draws the baseline fails 16 problems with a finished wrong answer and 9 still looping; B fails 8 and 4. Attempts hitting the token cap barely move (1 vs 2 in the corresponding tally). The improvement is concentrated on medium problems (**44 → 54 of 67**) and nearly absent on easy ones (**60 → 62 of 63**) — so the weight method mostly removes wrong answers rather than loops, which the authors report without explanation.
- **Regression guards are at the edge of the tolerance.** On HumanEval+ B solves **145/164** vs **144** (+0.6 pp [−4.3, +5.5]); on MBPP+ it solves **269/378** vs **276** (**−1.9 pp** [−5.6, +1.6], 20 gained / 27 lost). With a pre-set 2 pp tolerance, MBPP+ is the narrowest possible pass: one more lost problem would have failed the adoption rule.
- **Reconstruction quality is modest.** Across 48 layers the held-out reconstruction error falls a median of **21.8%** for A (range 14.2–76.0%) and **22.8%** for B (14.5–75.8%), with the reduction largest in the first and last layers and the two fits nearly indistinguishable by this measure — most of what the removed experts computed is simply not recovered. Fitting costs 5.2 h (A) and 5.7 h (B) summed over layers.
- **Cost.** B took 4.63 h of wall time on the final problems vs 2.73 h for the baseline (**1.7×**), with 791K vs 707K generated tokens over 162 vs 175 draws — arms compared at equal draws, not equal time.

## Authors & Credibility

Single author, **Anik Jha, Independent Researcher** (anik.k.jha@gmail.com), self-citing a prior paper on one-shot domain pruning of coding MoEs to 50% retention (arXiv:2607.16721). There is no institutional lab, cluster or funding behind the work; the entire study runs on one machine with 119 GB of unified memory, and the confirmation plus final stages used 14.5 hours of generation and grading. That constraint visibly shapes the paper: one model, one pruning ratio, one quantization, easy/medium LiveCodeBench only, one fit per method, no hyperparameter search.

What is unusually strong for a solo preprint is the **research hygiene**. Every stage that produced a number was run from a file frozen before that stage's first result, listing arms, settings, seeds, problems, decision rule and SHA-256 of every code file, model file, adapter and server binary, with a driver that re-checks hashes at start-up and refuses to run on mismatch. The five amendments are dated and itemised in Appendix A, and the authors state which ones touched evaluation rules (none did) and which were written after a coding result existed (none were, except the compute-ceiling raise). The paper also volunteers its negative results and repeatedly states what the evidence does not show. However, no artifacts are public yet: the frozen pre-registration files, per-problem results, attempt logs and analysis code are promised "with a later version of this paper", so nothing here can currently be reproduced or audited by a reader, and the training-free weight edit was designed by a large model (Claude Opus 5.5) acting as editor, a procedure that is essentially not reproducible from the text.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** Low. The authors say so themselves: reconstruction-based updates of retained experts on LiveCodeBench already work down to 37.5% retention (Markovic-Voronov et al., 2026, whose expert weight update A literally adapts), low-rank compensators for frozen quantized weights are known (Liu et al., 2024), and loops after aggressive compression of reasoning models plus decoding-time rescues have been reported (Alimaskina et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2026; Nguyen et al., 2026). The contribution is a harder regime (25% retention, 2 bits), a controlled attribution, a weight-free baseline, and a pre-registered test. That is a legitimate but incremental package, and the paper is honest enough to present it that way rather than dressing rank-8 adapters up as a new mechanism.

**The central attribution claim is clean and is the best part of the paper.** Keeping the 64 retained experts byte-identical between the pruned and unpruned 2-bit files means the 44-vs-0 loop gap cannot be a quantization artefact — a genuinely well-designed control that the earlier literature (e.g. Song and Zheng, 2026, who vary temperature and repetition penalties but have no matched quantization control) lacks. The 2-bit-unpruned row doing 107 vs the 4-bit file's 110 also usefully scopes the "2-bit reasoning models loop" narrative to smaller dense models.

**But the headline method fails to confirm its own hypothesis.** The paper is framed as repairing loops, yet the ablation by failure type shows the baseline's 26 failures decompose into 16 finished-wrong + 9 looping (+1 cap), and B's 14 into 8 finished-wrong + 4 looping (+2 cap) — most of the 8-problem gain comes from *wrong answers*, not loops, and the gain concentrates on medium problems (44 → 54 of 67) while easy problems move 60 → 62. Meanwhile the reconstruction objective improves held-out error by only ~22% median. So there is no causal story connecting "22% better reconstruction of the removed expert block" to "10 more medium problems solved, mostly ones that were answered incorrectly rather than looping". The authors say "we have not measured why" and "we report this as an observation" — admirably candid, but it also means the paper's title and framing ("retry first, then train" implies training fixes what retry cannot) overstate what was demonstrated: training fixes something else.

**B bundles two changes and the crucial control is missing.** B = rank-8 corrections on 64 experts *plus* two restored experts per layer, i.e. +0.8 points of routed capacity. A (corrections only) was carried only to development problems (A+S 88 vs S 84, +3.3 pp [−5.0, +10.8], significantly weaker than B+S at 93) and dropped, and — by the authors' own admission — an untrained 66-expert control was never evaluated on code. So we cannot tell whether the +9.2 pp comes from the low-rank corrections, from simply putting two real experts back, or from the interaction. Given that restoring 2 of 192 removed experts costs only 0.26 GiB and no adapter, this is a serious omission: the cheapest plausible explanation is untested, and if an untrained 66-expert model already captured part of the gain, the "trained corrections" story would shrink considerably.

**No comparison to the method it derives from.** A is explicitly an adaptation of OptEP's expert weight update, and OptEP evaluates at 37.5–75% retention on LiveCodeBench — the same benchmark family. The paper does not run dense EWU at 25%, arguing only that the low-rank form leaves the quantized file unchanged. That is an honest non-claim, but it leaves the reader unable to tell whether the deployment trick (adapter instead of rewritten weights) costs accuracy, and whether B's gain would have been obtained more simply. Likewise there is no comparison against activation steering (Nguyen et al., 2026), which the paper cites as a training-free remedy for exactly this failure, or against router distillation (Hyeon and Do, 2026a) — the related-work section lists half a dozen recovery families and then compares to none of them.

**The retry baseline is strong enough to make the +9.2 pp look smaller than it reads.** Redrawing takes the baseline from 89/130 to 104/130, +11.5 pp from sampling alone, while the arm gap is +9.2 pp. The honest implication — which the paper draws — is that a large share of what looks like "recovery from pruning" in prior work may simply be pass@k under a resampling policy. But the flip side is that the remaining margin is thin, and it rests on a single 130-problem paired sample with 15 gains against 3 losses; the two 64-problem confirmation seeds each individually had intervals including zero, and the pooled gate only just cleared it ([+0.8, +13.3]). With sampling at 32 parallel slots that "does not pin a reply to its seed", run-to-run variation is baked into every number, as the authors acknowledge.

**The regression guards are weaker evidence than they appear.** MBPP+ at −1.9 pp against a 2 pp tolerance is not "inside tolerance" in any substantive sense: the authors concede one more lost problem would have failed, and the interval [−5.6, +1.6] does not exclude a loss several times the tolerance. HumanEval+ +0.6 pp [−4.3, +5.5] is a coin flip. Together they support at most "no demonstrable gain on short problems, and possibly a small loss", which is far from the "guards hold ⇒ adoption rule met" framing. The decision rule was pre-set, so this is not p-hacking, but the rule itself is lenient for a method that costs 1.7× wall time.

**Design change after development.** The original plan compared the arms without retry; retry was added on development evidence and frozen before any fresh-panel request. The authors report a first-draw comparison with loop stopping as a secondary (+8.5 pp [+0.8, +16.2]) and note it is "close to the plan's original comparison but not identical". This is disclosed and defensible, but it does mean the primary pre-registered endpoint is not the endpoint that was originally pre-registered, and the secondary that approximates it is a construction the authors chose after seeing development results.

**Smaller issues.** The training-free weight edit was designed and selected by Claude Opus 5.5 given access to the pruned model's weights and development activations — an LLM-in-the-loop design step that no reader can replicate, and whose negative result therefore says little about whether single-neuron/head edits work in general (Lazaridis et al., 2026 report model-dependent effects, so one failure is one data point). The fitting set deliberately includes C's own replies on the *old test half*, which is disclosed and does not touch the fresh panels, but it does mean "124 replies from the later half of v4–v6" is doing double duty as both a one-shot test and training data, and the paper never reports how much of B's gain depends on those traces. Loop-period statistics (P up to 6000 chars, 72/74 recall, 2/360 false positives) come from a grid whose selection criterion was written in advance — good practice — but the detector's thresholds are tuned to this model's loop morphology and are unlikely to transfer unchanged. Finally, file-size accounting is a bit blurred: 11.62 GiB pruned file, 11.88 GiB with restored experts, +1.16 GiB adapter, peak 81.6 GiB at 32 slots — the adapter is 10% of the file, and no wall-time decomposition explains the 1.7×.

## Strengths

- A genuinely clean causal control: byte-identical kept experts isolate pruning from quantization, giving an unambiguous 44-vs-0 loop attribution.
- Takes the free baseline seriously and reports, unprompted, that redrawing beats the trained method's margin — the kind of result most papers bury.
- Pre-registration done properly, with hashed artifacts, dated amendments, a driver that refuses to run on hash mismatch, and a fixed decision rule including an explicit "inconclusive" branch.
- Refuses to claim the low-rank form beats the dense weight update it derives from, and refuses to attribute B's gain to a mechanism it did not measure.
- Reports the failure decomposition (finished-wrong vs looping vs token cap) that undercuts its own framing, plus layer-wise reconstruction numbers showing most removed computation is not recovered.
- Practically useful artifact-level details: penalty window length (20,480 tokens, because loop periods exceed the default 64), the exact loop detector, the straight-through-to-quantized-values training trick, and deployment parity checks.

## Limitations

- One model (Ornith-1.5-122B), one pruning ratio (25%), one quantization (2-bit UD-IQ2_XXS), one benchmark family (easy/medium LiveCodeBench), one seed and one recipe per method; no rank, data or learning-rate search.
- B bundles low-rank corrections and two restored experts; A was evaluated only on development problems and an untrained 66-expert control was never run on code, so the gain cannot be attributed.
- No experimental comparison with the dense expert weight update A is derived from, nor with activation steering, router calibration, expert-wise distillation, or SEER-MoE-style regularised fine-tuning.
- Arms compared at equal draws, not equal cost: B is 1.7× slower in wall time (4.63 h vs 2.73 h) with no decomposition of the slowdown.
- The fresh panels are public problems published before the model, so pretraining exposure — shared by both arms but potentially compressing differences — cannot be excluded.
- No mechanistic account of why B helps, and the measured gain is mostly on finished-wrong answers and medium problems rather than on the loops the paper set out to repair.
- Sampling at 32 parallel slots does not pin a reply to its seed; the two confirmation seeds show the resulting variance is comparable to the effect size.
- Artifacts (pre-registration files, per-problem results, attempt logs, analysis code) are promised for a later version and are not currently available.

## Relevance

直接落在「Reliability under Extreme Performance Optimization」：它研究推理 MoE 在 25% 专家保留 + 2-bit 量化这一激进压缩下的具体失效形态（逐字复读循环）以及恢复代价，并把"解码期免费修复"与"训练式修复"放在同一对照下比较——这正是该方向最需要的"降级—恢复—成本"三维账本。对关注剪枝/量化脆弱性、以及"增益到底来自权重还是来自重采样"的方法学问题同样有参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. What does an **untrained 66-expert control** score on the same 130 fresh problems? If it captures most of the +9.2 pp, the trained corrections are largely redundant with 0.26 GiB of restored capacity.
2. Would dense EWU (OptEP's original full-weight update) at 25% retention match B, and does the load-time low-rank form cost anything in accuracy? This is the cheapest missing baseline and the same benchmark applies.
3. How much of B's gain comes from including C's own long replies on the old test half in the fitting set? An ablation with calibration documents only would separate "on-policy reasoning traces" from "low-rank corrections" as the active ingredient.
4. Why do the gains land on finished-wrong medium problems rather than loops, given that held-out reconstruction improves by only ~22%? Probing the corrected experts' outputs on the specific 15 gained problems would test whether B is acting as a general capability patch rather than a loop patch.
5. Does the loop detector's threshold (P ≤ 6000 chars, max(5P, 200) window) transfer to a different pruned model with different loop morphology, and how sensitive is the retry baseline to that choice? The whole comparison rests on a baseline whose strength depends on this tuning.
6. What is the wall-time decomposition of the 1.7× slowdown — extra expert FLOPs, adapter overhead, or longer surviving replies? A cheaper adapter application would change the cost-benefit calculus materially.
