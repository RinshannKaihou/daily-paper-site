> **一句话总结：** 论文把 attention sink 重新表述为 token 空间协方差中"由高注意力质量列诱导的低秩尖峰模式"（方向对齐 sink 列 $a_j=A_{:,j}/\|A_{:,j}\|_2$，谱上表现为越过 Marchenko–Pastur 边缘的离群特征值），并证明 post-SDPA 的逐元素 sigmoid 门是对该模式的"模态相关收缩"（Theorem 3.1：门后信号能量为 $a^2\alpha_s$，$\alpha_s=\|Ds\|_2^2\le 1$），同时在前向谱几何与反向 value-path 梯度两条路径上同时压制 sink；在 Qwen-1B 与自训 Pythia-410M（15B FineWeb-Edu token）上，门控把首 token 注意力质量 0.084→0.013、首 token value 梯度放大 25.40→5.67、massive activation $M_\infty$ 320.0→67.4，且下游 8 任务均分反而从 44.96 升到 46.06。

> **TL;DR:** Post-SDPA output gating is recast as a mode-dependent contraction of a sink-induced rank-one spike in token-space covariance, and the claim is that the same learned gate simultaneously suppresses the forward spectral outlier and implements a backward "gradient valve" on the sink column. On 16×512-token WikiText-103 blocks, a matched Pythia-410M baseline (15B FineWeb-Edu tokens) versus its elementwise-gated twin shows first-token attention mass 0.084 → 0.013, first-token value-gradient amplification $\rho_1$ 25.40 → 5.67, outlier mass above the MP edge 0.679 → 0.545, effective rank 89.1 → 153.2, and massive activation $M_\infty$ 320.0 → 67.4, while the 8-task zero-shot average rises 44.96 → 46.06 and WikiText perplexity falls 29.53 → 27.99. The forward claim rests on Qwen-1B checkpoints from prior work: strongly sink-aligned covariance directions retain only 0.740 of their pre-gate energy versus 0.945 for weakly aligned ones, and that 0.205 gap collapses to 0.033 when the elementwise gate is replaced by its feature-averaged scalar control — which is the paper's real evidence that gating is selective rather than uniform shrinkage.

## 核心贡献

- **把 attention sink 从"注意力图的列不平衡"翻译成"表示的谱对象"**：在 isotropic value 近似下 $\mathbb{E}[C_Y\mid A]=\sigma_V^2\sum_j A_{:,j}A_{:,j}^\top$，因此高 incoming mass 的源 token $j$ 以秩一项 $A_{:,j}A_{:,j}^\top$ 进入 token 空间协方差，方向即归一化注意力列 $\bar a_j$；由此给出 Definition 2.1 的"sink 诱导尖峰模型"（spike 方向 $s$ 满足 $\|P_{\text{sink}}s\|_2^2\ge 1-\varepsilon$）。
- **给出 post-SDPA 门控对前向 sink 模式的确定性收缩定理**：门是逐 token 的对角收缩 $\Gamma_i=\mathrm{diag}(G_i)$，$0\preceq\Gamma_i\preceq I$，于是门后 sink 信号满足 $\frac1d\|\tilde Y_{\text{sig}}\|_F^2=a^2\alpha_s\le a^2$、$\lambda_{\max}(\tilde C_{\text{sig}})\le a^2\alpha_s$，其中 $\alpha_s=\sum_i s_i^2\|\Gamma_i u\|_2^2$。该结果不依赖门的分布假设，但**明确只作用于已形成的信号分量**。
- **把前向收缩接到 BBP 相变上（作为理想化参照）**：Proposition 3.2 用"随机行选择矩阵 $P_I$"构造一个恰好仍是标准 spiked Wishart 的稀疏化模型，给出 $\alpha_s>\sigma^2\sqrt{c'}$ 时离群值消失的判据；作者自己在 §3.1 与附录 A.3 承认学习到的门同时调制噪声、协方差不再各向同性，故 BBP 只是"机制层面的参照"而非定理。
- **给出后向的"梯度阀"定理**：$\nabla_{v_s}L=\sum_{t\ge s}a_{ts}\Gamma_t^\top\delta_t$，于是 $\|\nabla_{v_s}L\|\le B M_s^{\text{eff}}\le BM_s$（coherent regime），并在上游梯度跨位置不相关时得到二阶版本 $\mathbb{E}\|\nabla_{v_s}L\|^2\le B^2S_s^{\text{eff}}\le B^2S_s$。关键洞察：**门不需要移除 attention sink 列**，只要在每次 query→source 的 value 路径上过滤一次，就能削弱 sink 列的梯度统计量。
- **在训练轨迹上验证三种 sink 签名共现（AS–GS–MA）**：Pythia-410M 的 56 个 checkpoint 上，baseline 的首 token 注意力集中、value 梯度放大、表示主导在 middle-to-late 层同步生长，门控版本同步压制；这是本文相对已有单点分析（只讲前向 sink 或只讲 gradient sink）最有价值的整体图景。

## Key Contributions

- **A spectral restatement of attention sinks.** Instead of treating a sink as an attention-map artifact, the paper identifies it with a low-rank mode of the post-attention token-space covariance $C_Y=\frac1dYY^\top$, whose direction coincides with the normalized attention column $\bar a_j$ of a high-mass source position. Sink detection becomes an eigenvector-alignment test against a data-adaptive projector $P_{\text{sink}}$, and "sink strength" becomes "is the spike supercritical relative to the Marchenko–Pastur edge".
- **A deterministic forward contraction theorem for post-SDPA gating.** Because the gate is a row-wise diagonal contraction with entries in $[0,1]$, the sink-induced signal energy is scaled by $\alpha_s=\|Ds\|_2^2\le1$ exactly, with no distributional assumption on the learned gate. This separates "the gate contracts the sink mode" (provable) from "the gate pushes the eigenvalue below the BBP threshold" (only true in the idealized row-selection model).
- **A backward gradient-valve theorem.** The value-path Jacobian through a sink column is $a_{ts}\Gamma_t$, so gated effective column mass $M_s^{\text{eff}}=\sum_t a_{ts}\kappa_t$ with $\kappa_t=\|\Gamma_t\|_{\text{op}}\le1$ replaces $M_s$; both a worst-case (coherent) and a second-moment (incoherent) bound follow. This is the first account that ties the *same* learned gate to both forward spectral suppression and backward gradient concentration.
- **Empirical tri-layer evidence**: (i) random-matrix diagnostics on Qwen-1B showing outlier mass down, effective rank up, sink-subspace alignment down, with a scalar-gate ablation that isolates feature-direction selectivity; (ii) a fixed-upstream-gradient counterfactual that measures local attenuation directly; (iii) training-time traces on Pythia-410M showing the co-emergence and joint suppression of attention, gradient, and massive-activation signatures.
- **Breadth checks that most mechanism papers skip**: model scale (2.8B), context length (4K vs 8K train/eval), sequence-formatting convention (plain vs EOS-separated), controlled EOS-position counterfactuals, an attention–value permutation control, and a Softpick comparison separating operator-level from training-level effects.

## Methodology

**Forward spectral framework.** For one causal head, $A$ is row-stochastic and $Y=AV$. With $C_Y=\frac1dYY^\top$ and the isotropic-value approximation $\mathbb{E}[VV^\top/d\mid A]=\sigma_V^2I$, one gets $\mathbb{E}[C_Y\mid A]=\sigma_V^2\sum_jA_{:,j}A_{:,j}^\top$ — a column-wise decomposition that makes explicit how an attention sink enters the representation. A purely isotropic representation $Y=\sigma Z$ has a spectrum converging to the Marchenko–Pastur law with edges $\lambda_\pm=\sigma^2(1\pm\sqrt c)^2$, $c=n/d$; structured modes are modelled by the rank-one spike $Y=\sqrt d\,a\,su^\top+\sigma Z$, which produces a separated outlier when $a^2>\sigma^2\sqrt c$ (BBP).

**Diagnostics.** All spectral quantities are computed per layer on the feature-standardized token-space covariance and compared against the theoretical MP edge $\lambda_+^b=(1+\sqrt{T/D})^2$: outlier mass $\mathrm{OutMass}(C_Y)=\sum_{\lambda_k>\lambda_+^b}\lambda_k/\sum_i\lambda_i$, leading-mode dominance $\lambda_1/\lambda_+^b$, effective rank $r_{\text{eff}}=\exp(-\sum_ip_i\log p_i)$, and sink alignment $\mathrm{Align}_{\text{sink}}=\|P_{\text{sink}}q_1(C_Y)\|_2^2$ where the sink set $J_{\text{sink}}$ is the set of source positions whose head-averaged column mass is at least half its maximum (at most $k=8$). Random-subspace reference is $\approx k/T$.

**Gating model.** $\tilde y_i=G_i\odot y_i$, $G_i=\sigma(x_iW_g)\in[0,1]^d$ — head-specific, elementwise, post-SDPA. The forward theorem uses $\Gamma_i=\mathrm{diag}(G_i)$ and $g_i=\|\Gamma_i u\|_2$; the backward theorem uses $\kappa_t=\|\Gamma_t\|_{\text{op}}$.

**Models and data.** Forward/backward spectral analyses use the public Qwen-1B checkpoints `1B_baseline` and `1B_gate_elementwise` released by the prior gated-attention work (inference only, no retraining), on 16 sequences of length 512 from WikiText. Training-time analyses train matched Pythia-410M baseline and elementwise-gated variants on 15B tokens of FineWeb-Edu, seq-len 4096, AdamW, peak LR $4\times10^{-4}$, gpt-neox-20b tokenizer, **with the gated variant's MLP intermediate size reduced from 4096 to 3584** to compensate for gate parameters. Robustness: matched Pythia-2.8B pair, 8K-context pair evaluated at 4K and 8K, plain vs EOS-separated formatting, and an EOS-position sweep with the target EOS inserted at 0, 0.25T, 0.5T, 0.75T.

**Backward proxies.** $\rho_1=\|\nabla_{v_1}L\|/[(T-1)^{-1}\sum_{j\ne1}\|\nabla_{v_j}L\|]$ measures first-token value-gradient amplification. The local valve is tested by caching $\delta_t=\nabla_{\tilde y_t}L$ from the real gated backward pass and recomputing $gv_1^{\text{actual}}=\sum_ta_{t1}\Gamma_t^\top\delta_t$ versus $gv_1^{\text{cf}}=\sum_ta_{t1}\delta_t$, with $A_1^{\text{local}}=\|gv_1^{\text{actual}}\|/\|gv_1^{\text{cf}}\|$. The gate-mass proxy is $R_1^{\text{rms}}=\tilde M_1^{\text{rms}}/M_1$ with $r_t=(d^{-1}\sum_cG_{t,c}^2)^{1/2}$.

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline training-time suppression on matched Pythia-410M (final checkpoint, layer means over 16×512-token WikiText-103 blocks)**: first-token attention mass $m_1$ drops 0.084 → 0.013 (6.5×), top-$k$ sink mass 0.125 → 0.013, first-token value-gradient amplification $\rho_1$ 25.40 → 5.67 (4.5×), outlier mass 0.679 → 0.545, leading-eigenvalue ratio $\lambda_1/\lambda_+$ 18.42 → 12.49, and effective rank 89.14 → 153.20 — i.e. every axis the theory predicts moves in the predicted direction simultaneously.
- **Massive activations collapse, most dramatically on Qwen-1B**: $M_2$ 11,854.5 → 146.3 (81.1×) and $M_\infty$ 11,839.5 → 87.2 (135.7×); on Pythia-410M the same metrics go 379.2 → 108.4 and 320.0 → 67.4 (3.50× and 4.75×). The single largest observed coordinate magnitude is 17,536 in the baseline (first token, layer 8) versus 378 in the gated model, and across 56 checkpoints the baseline's layer-mean $M_\infty$ grows 8.7 → 320.0 while the gated model never exceeds 69.4.
- **Sink-selectivity survives the scalar-gate control (Qwen-1B, 64 leading pre-gate modes)**: under feature-standardized covariance, strongly sink-aligned directions retain $R_k=0.740$ of their pre-gate energy versus 0.945 for weakly aligned directions (gap −0.205); replacing the elementwise gate by its feature average collapses the gap to 0.957 vs 0.990 (−0.033). Crucially, raw activation-energy retention is *not* selective (0.047 vs 0.044, +0.003) — so the effect is not just "sinks are big and gating shrinks big things".
- **The backward valve is quantitatively predicted by gate mass, not just qualitatively present**: the local attenuation ratio $A_1^{\text{local}}$ is far below 1 across layers and tracks the RMS effective-mass proxy $R_1^{\text{rms}}$ with Pearson $r=0.91$ at the layer level; the $G\equiv1$ counterfactual inside the *gated* model raises the first-token gradient without changing trained weights, isolating the active gate as an independent attenuation path.
- **Scale and context robustness hold**: at Pythia-2.8B, gating reduces $m_1$ 0.147 → 0.011 (~13×), $m_{\text{topk}}$ 0.156 → 0.010 (~15×), $\rho_1$ 28.24 → 4.29 and $\rho_{\text{topk}}$ 27.04 → 3.61, while $\lambda_1/\lambda_+$ falls 24.21 → 15.54 and effective rank rises 111.2 → 185.0; gated/baseline ratios at 2.8B (0.077, 0.067, 0.152, 0.133) are as strong or stronger than at 410M (0.129, 0.101, 0.195, 0.160). At 8K training / 8K evaluation, $\lambda_1/\lambda_+$ still falls 59.0 → 42.2 and normalized effective rank rises 0.134 → 0.262.
- **Gating does not cost quality, and modestly helps**: the matched Pythia-410M pair on 15B FineWeb-Edu tokens gives WikiText ppl 29.53 → 27.99, LAMBADA ppl 55.21 → 40.52, LAMBADA acc 29.94 → 32.47, ARC-e 56.02 → 57.79, and an 8-task average of 44.96 → 46.06. Layer-wise next-token linear probes on Qwen-1B/WikiText-2 show the apparent gated advantage in layers 8–28 is an artifact of probe optimization under massive activations: after per-dimension standardization (or 5 epochs at LR $10^{-4}$) the gap largely closes, so no next-token information is lost.
- **The sink-aligned direction comes from attention-column geometry, not from a privileged sink value**: permuting the attention-column/value pairing while holding $A$, $V$ and $P_{\text{sink}}$ fixed *increases* alignment from 0.062 → 0.196 in the baseline (100% of layers) and 0.220 → 0.510 in the gated pre-gate output (96% of layers). Conversely, learned value anisotropy *weakens* alignment relative to the isotropic reference (baseline 0.97 → 0.64, gated 0.79 → 0.32) while still staying ~70–100× above the random reference (≈0.003/0.009) — an honest qualifier on the isotropic anchor.

## Authors & Credibility

Seojin Kim, Yehjin Shin and Noseong Park are at KAIST; Park's group has a sustained record in sequence-model architecture and spectral/graph-signal analyses of state-space and attention models (e.g. ICML 2023 graph-convolution-augmented transformers, ICLR 2026 "Graph signal processing meets Mamba2" — both cited here as [30], [31]). This is a NeurIPS 2026 main-track paper with a substantial appendix (proofs, 14 tables, robustness sweeps over scale, context length, formatting convention, and EOS position), which is above the norm for mechanism-analysis submissions. The compute footprint (8× RTX PRO 6000 96GB, 1 TB RAM, 15B-token Pythia trainings at 410M, 2.8B and 8K contexts) is real, and the checkpoints for Qwen analyses are publicly released by the prior work rather than hand-picked. Credibility is helped by the paper's willingness to state its own scope limits in three separate places (§3.1, App. A.3, App. H) and by controls that could easily have failed (the permutation test, the scalar-gate ablation, the probe-standardization check).

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The framing is genuinely new in one specific sense: prior work establishes (a) that attention sinks exist and correlate with massive activations, and (b) that sinks induce value-path gradient concentration (Chen & Yao [12]); this paper is the first to show that a *downstream multiplicative gate* — which never touches $A$ — attacks both, and to give a shared algebraic reason ($\Gamma_t$ with $\|\Gamma_t\|_{\text{op}}\le1$). Theorem 3.1 itself is close to trivial once written down (an entrywise contraction cannot increase Frobenius norm), and the authors are upfront that only the *selectivity* claim is substantive; the non-trivial content is Theorem 4.1 plus the empirical demonstration that learned gates are in fact feature-selectively aligned with sink modes.

**The BBP framing is weaker than the title suggests.** "Attention sinks as spectral spikes" invites the reading that sinks *are* supercritical spikes crossing the MP edge. But Proposition 3.2 is proved only for a random row-selection matrix $P_I$ independent of $Z$ — a surrogate in which the retained noise stays i.i.d. Gaussian. A learned elementwise gate with $G_{t,c}\in[0,1]$ is neither a row selector nor isotropic, so the gated covariance is not a spiked Wishart matrix (App. A.3 admits this explicitly). No test is reported of the proposition's actual prediction, namely that the *same* layer's $\lambda_1$ either sits above or below $\sigma^2\sqrt{c'}$ depending on $\alpha_s$; the paper only reports that $\lambda_1/\lambda_+$ decreases on average. The mechanism story survives; the phase-transition story is decoration.

**The main confound is capacity, and the ablation does not fully remove it.** The gated Pythia variant has MLP intermediate size 3584 versus 4096 for the baseline — parameter-matched, but not architecture- or FLOP-matched, and MLP width is exactly the knob that changes how much a model can absorb outlier pressure. Every Pythia training-time number (Tables 2, 7, 8, 9, 11, 12) inherits this confound. The 2.8B robustness check uses "matched" models but the paper does not state whether the same MLP-shrink compensation was applied there, so it is unclear whether the scale check controls the confound or replicates it. A cleaner design would train a baseline at 3584 MLP width and compare against that.

**Selectivity evidence is thin in absolute terms and depends on one metric.** Table 1/3's headline is a gap of 0.740 vs 0.945 in $R_k$ — a 22% relative difference in retained standardized covariance energy, on 64 leading modes from one model family. The scalar-gate control that produces the 0.033 gap collapses both *token-position* and *feature-direction* selectivity at once, so it conflates the two; the paper's interpretation ("feature-direction selectivity is what matters") is plausible but not isolated. A feature-shuffled gate (permute $G$ across the feature axis, keep token structure) would settle it and is not run.

**The Qwen-1B comparison is cross-model, not within-model, for the headline forward claim.** Section 3.2 compares `1B_baseline` against `1B_gate_elementwise` — two separately trained checkpoints. Any difference in learned value anisotropy, attention entropy, or MLP allocation is folded into "the effect of gating". The paper does provide the within-model pre-gate/post-gate contrast (Fig. 1, Table 4), which is the right control, but the between-model contrast gets the rhetorical weight.

**One number quietly undercuts the narrative.** In Table 4, the gated model's *pre-gate* sink alignment is 0.220 versus only 0.062 for the baseline (before any gating). If gating truly suppressed sink formation during training, the gated model's pre-gate representation should be *less* sink-aligned, not 3.5× more. The consistent reading is that gating does not prevent the model from building sink modes; it blocks them at the output. That is a legitimate and interesting result — and it would strengthen the "gradient valve" story — but the paper never comments on it, and the abstract's "gating suppresses the co-emergence" language reads more strongly than the data support.

**Coverage gaps.** Softpick is compared only descriptively (Table 14): in the frozen-operator swap every metric moves <2%, and in trained models $m_{\text{topk}}$ drops 24.3× while $\lambda_1/\lambda_+$ moves only 15.39 → 13.74, which actually implies that operator-mediated *prevention* removes attention concentration without removing the spectral mode — a result that deserves more than a paragraph, since it is direct evidence that the spectral spike and the attention sink are not the same object. VGA [6] gets a Jacobian comparison (Eqs. 42–48) and no experiment at all. No comparison to DyT [7] or to Softpick at matched token budget.

**Minor.** The "average over samples and layers" convention for $M_2$ and $M_\infty$ in Table 9 makes the Qwen-1B ratios (81×, 136×) hard to interpret — max-type statistics averaged over layers are dominated by a handful of cells, and the reported 17,536 single coordinate is a clearer statement than the 11,839.5 layer-mean. The paper also never reports wall-clock or memory overhead of the gate, which matters for the Qwen3-Next production motivation.

## Strengths

- The forward and backward analyses share one operator ($\Gamma_t$), so the two halves of the paper are not two unrelated stories bolted together — this is the conceptual contribution that will outlive the specific diagnostics.
- Theorems are stated with honest scope: 3.1 is deterministic for learned gates, 3.2 is explicitly an "idealized spectral reference", and App. A.3/A.4 spell out exactly what fails (noise modulation, higher-rank subspaces do not decompose into independently contracted modes).
- The fixed-upstream-gradient counterfactual (Eq. 26) is the right experiment for a Jacobian-level claim, and the $r=0.91$ correlation with $R_1^{\text{rms}}$ is a genuinely predictive (not merely directional) validation.
- Controls that are usually skipped are present and are allowed to produce uncomfortable results: the attention–value permutation test, the value-anisotropy interpolation (Table 5, where alignment drops from 0.97 to 0.64), and the probe-standardization check (Fig. 9) that dissolves the apparent gated advantage.
- Robustness is real rather than nominal: 410M and 2.8B, 4K and 8K contexts, plain and EOS-separated formatting, plus a position-controlled EOS sweep (Table 13) showing attenuation persists when the sink moves (target attention mass down 2.8–5.4×, target gradient amplification down 2.5–3.8×).
- Downstream quality is reported and is not negative (8-task average 44.96 → 46.06), which is the number practitioners will actually look for.

## Limitations

- The BBP/phase-transition framing is not established for learned gates; the paper's own idealized proposition concerns a random row selector, and no experiment tests whether an individual layer's eigenvalue crosses the predicted threshold as a function of $\alpha_s$.
- All Pythia comparisons confound the gate with an MLP-width change (4096 → 3584); the confound is not ablated with a width-matched baseline.
- Forward headline evidence is a between-checkpoint comparison on Qwen-1B; within-model pre/post-gate contrasts are cleaner but receive less emphasis, and the gated model's *higher* pre-gate alignment (0.220 vs 0.062) is left unremarked.
- The selectivity claim rests on a 0.740-vs-0.945 retention gap measured on 64 leading modes of one model family, with a scalar-gate control that jointly removes token- and feature-selectivity.
- Sink-subspace alignment values are small in absolute terms (0.062, 0.220, 0.280) even when 20×–36× the random reference, which makes the metric sensitive to $k$, $T$, and the standardization choice; Table 5 shows the choice of covariance normalization moves the number from 0.97 to 0.64.
- Massive-activation metrics are layer- and sample-averaged maxima, which overweight a few extreme cells and make the 81×/136× Qwen ratios less informative than they look.
- No experiments on VGA, DyT, or other mitigations beyond Softpick; no efficiency accounting for the gate; no long-context generation or retrieval task where sink suppression is supposed to matter functionally.
- Relation to the compression-valley view (de Llano et al., ICLR 2026) is cited but not engaged — whether the sink-induced spectral mode is the same object as the compression valley is left open.

## Relevance

与本研究关注的"注意力/激活异常值的机制解释与可干预性"高度相关：它把 sink、massive activation、gradient sink 三条线用同一个门控算子串起来，给出了前向谱（RMT 诊断）与反向 Jacobian 两套可复用度量，其中 $\mathrm{Align}_{\text{sink}}$、$R_1^{\text{rms}}$ 与固定上游梯度的反事实写法可以直接搬到其他架构干预（VGA、Softpick、DyT）的评估上。对做训练稳定性、长上下文或架构改造的工作，这是一篇值得精读方法与附录、但需要对 BBP 叙事打折的机制论文。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the *same* layer's $\lambda_1$ actually cross the BBP threshold $\sigma^2\sqrt{c'}$ when $\alpha_s$ is varied by construction (e.g., interpolate between $\Gamma=I$ and the learned $G$ on a fixed forward pass)? This is the paper's central theoretical prediction and appears untested at the per-layer level.
2. Why is the gated model's pre-gate sink alignment (0.220) 3.5× the baseline's (0.062)? If the gate does not prevent sink-mode formation but only blocks it at the output, what training pressure drives the gated model to build *stronger* sink modes upstream — and does the gradient valve make them computationally cheaper to maintain?
3. How much of the effect survives a width-matched baseline (MLP 3584 without a gate)? A three-way comparison — baseline-4096, baseline-3584, gated-3584 — would separate gate from capacity in one shot.
4. Can a feature-shuffled gate control (permute $G$ across the feature axis while preserving token-position structure) isolate feature-direction selectivity from token-position selectivity, which the current scalar control conflates?
5. Since trained Softpick reduces $m_{\text{topk}}$ by 24.3× while $\lambda_1/\lambda_+$ moves only 15.39 → 13.74, is the residual spectral mode a *different* object than the attention sink — e.g. a value-anisotropy artifact (Table 5 suggests learned values alone shift alignment from 0.97 to 0.64)? A joint decomposition of $A\Delta_VA^\top$ versus $\sigma_V^2AA^\top$ would test this directly.
6. Does the gradient valve hold under realistic upstream-gradient correlations? Corollary 4.2 assumes $\mathbb{E}[\delta_t\delta_r^\top]=0$ for $t\ne r$, which is false in practice; how much of the measured $\rho_1$ reduction comes from the coherent bound versus from the gate changing $\delta_t$ itself?
7. Is the mechanism sufficient to explain the downstream gain (44.96 → 46.06), or would any intervention that suppresses massive activations produce it? A sink-suppression intervention that does *not* touch spectral outliers (e.g. Softpick's rectified softmax trained from scratch) would be the discriminating test.
