Same Model, Different People: Numerical Execution Contracts and the Reproducibility of Fairness Audits
Erzheng Zhang (Toronto)/ Agnes Chu (McMaster)/ 2026-11-16
fairness audits numerical precision reproducibility TF32 · FP8 cross-hardware drift
Key metric · strict FP32 0 / 204,204

Qwen3-4B decisions changed on each of H100, RTX 4090 and MI350X.

TF32 changes 41 (H100) and 39 (4090) of the same 204,204 — ≤0.035% per dataset.
Why this matters

Audit reports must name their numerical contract — a routine deployer migration can silently re-decide who is selected.

Critical edge

The causal arm is nearly vacuous: Tier 1 moves 0.02% of decisions and zero verdicts, while the arm that does flip verdicts (FP8) is explicitly not causal.

01How the intervention works

Freeze the model, the records, the threshold and the audit code. Vary only the arithmetic that executes the same function, then ask whether the verdict survives.

Tier 1 — causal arm weights byte-identical

FP64 reference · strict FP32 · TF32 (10 fraction-bit operands) · AMD “TF32 setting” (three BF16 partial products) · MLX relaxed default on Apple M5. Only rounding, accumulation order and width change.

Tier 2 — deployment arm config, not arithmetic

BF16 compute · INT8 dynamic · 8-bit and MXFP8 weights · vLLM BF16/FP8 serving · PyTorch FP8 on MI350X. Weights, kernels, engines and batching all move at once.

  • Flip condition: f_c(x) = 1[s_c(x) ≥ t]; a record can flip only if |s − t| ≤ |δ_c|. That lemma predicts the log-scale flip profile near the threshold.
  • Tasks: ACSIncome, ACSEmployment, HMDA lending, Diabetes readmission — logistic regression, MLP, XGBoost, plus Qwen3-4B zero-shot and Mistral Small 3.2 (24B).
  • Guardrails: SHA-256 over weights and inputs before every run; precision flags read back and checked against FP64 with a canary matrix; every contract run twice in separate processes.

03Strengths

  • A clean causal intervention. Byte-identical serialized weights, SHA-256 before every run, canary-verified precision flags — not the field's usual “another model” comparison.
  • The unexciting result is in the abstract. Tier 1 contradicts the exciting thesis; FP8 “is not shown to be fairer”; no harm claim is made.
  • Run-to-run honesty. Every contract ran twice; the one non-bit-reproducible case (vLLM BF16) is reported, plus a third run that moved 4 decisions.
  • Stability intervals separate numerical spread from sampling spread, making “this verdict is robust” a checkable quantity with a scale.
  • The cost table is actionable. 4.33× TF32 on an H100 but only 1.59× on an RTX 4090 — small auditors can afford strict FP32 on consumer cards.

04Limitations

  • Near-nil at verdict level. 0 of 228, and ≤0.035% of individual decisions — the effect lives within ~1e-3 of the threshold, where auditors already expect fragility.
  • Tier 2 conflates everything. Precision, engine, kernel, weight rounding and batching move together; how much of the FP8 effect is arithmetic stays unanswered.
  • Two of four LLM operating points are degenerate — 0.094% selection, and a near-chance Diabetes classifier — yet supply four of five changed pass-fail verdicts.
  • Small counts carry the group story. 41 vs 39 flips, a Jaccard excess carried by HMDA alone, a 27-of-38 vs 4-of-33 split in one MLP cell: demonstrated, not estimated.
  • Incomplete by its own standard. No kernel-library versions; vLLM batching and scheduler unrecorded; the MI350X FP8 path is batch-dependent.
  • Post hoc framing. The 6-of-17 / 0-of-43 contrast and the verdict-counting rule were set after the fact — exploratory, as the authors concede.

06Questions for further exploration

Q1How much of the FP8 effect is arithmetic rather than engine? Same card, logged kernels, three paths — vLLM FP8 vs vLLM BF16 vs transformers BF16.
Q2If thresholds were recalibrated per contract, would the 24B FP8 DP pass survive? The 54.4% vs 44.7% selection shift suggests it is largely threshold placement.
Q3Why does an unseen RTX A6000 break the flip-set prediction for one MLP — a cuBLAS heuristic or a framework fallback?
Q4Do integer point scores (CHA₂DS₂-VASc, LACE) really immunize bedside tools, as the discussion argues?
Q5Can the stability interval be made cheap — sampling 3–5 contracts instead of all of C — and can a subsampling rule be defended?
Q6Should the 0.001 movement tolerance scale with group size, and how many ranking changes would survive that?
