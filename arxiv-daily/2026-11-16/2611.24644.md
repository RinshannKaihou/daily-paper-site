> **一句话总结：** 用留出集的梯度与 Hessian–向量积按奇异值带和神经元组分解 Muon 的更新，发现它在高曲率谱顶端系统性过步（尾部/顶部乘子比 38–190×），据此在同范数约束下重新分配步长可在 124M 模型上把最终 loss 从 3.9176 降到 3.9018 nats（NorMuon+tilt，−0.0158，三 seed），355M 上再得 −0.0188。

> **TL;DR:** On a 124.5M decoder trained on FineWeb-Edu (100.7M tokens, three paired seeds), measuring held-out curvature by singular-value band shows Muon's per-band optimal multiplier rises monotonically from 0.59 on ranks 1–8 to 28.3 on ranks 513–768 — it over-steps the high-curvature top. Acting on this: splitting Q, K, V instead of one fused 2304×768 matrix lowers final held-out loss from 3.9427 to 3.9176 (−0.0250 nats, 95% CI [−0.0290, −0.0210]), and a matmul-only σ^(−1/4) cooldown tilt on top of that split baseline gives a further −0.0120 nats, −0.0158 combined with NorMuon (3.9018 vs 3.9176), equivalent to ~4.3% fewer tokens. The tilt adds nothing at the end of the cooldown (−0.00009 predicted), exactly as the measured curvature predicts.

## 核心贡献

- **联合曲率诊断**：不再孤立地看方向或看层，而是在留出数据上把 Muon 的下一步同时按奇异值带（6 带）和神经元组（6 分位）切开，测量组间梯度对齐 `g_b` 与完整曲率矩阵 `H_bb'`（含交叉项），给出每组二阶最优乘子 `c_b = g_b/H_bb`；Muon 对应全 1。
- **精确的 scale/shape 分解（Lean 4 机器验证）**：证明 Muon 的二阶 gap 可拆成"整体步长"与"组间相对权重"两项，且只有 `H ≻ 0` 时驻点才是最优；进一步给出**同范数**重分配严格优于 Muon 的充要判据（定理 4）——这是本文与既有"贪心乘子"工作的关键区别。
- **Muon 的解剖结论**：(i) 谱分配——最优乘子从顶部到尾部单调上升，顶部被过步；(ii) 神经元分配——不平衡集中在 fused QKV（变异系数 0.43，MLP 0.18，attn-out 0.02），因为 polar 因子的行范数是 leverage score，矩阵如何拼接直接改变每个神经元的步长。
- **受控归因**：每个改方向的臂逐步做 Frobenius 范数匹配、基线学习率取内部最优、seed 配对，2×2 因子设计 + A/A 噪声检查；结论是 block-wise 变体的全部收益都来自"Q/K/V 分开正交化"（−0.0250），而不是分块本身（square blocks 相对 split 仅 +0.0011）。
- **可部署的谱倾斜**：用 Chebyshev 多项式 + Clenshaw 递推（只用 matmul）实现 σ^(−1/4) 倾斜并证明其保持旋转等变性；cooldown 期启用，额外 −0.0120 nats，与 NorMuon 可加（合计 −0.0158）。
- **诚实的负结果**：输入 whiten（GL(n)-等变、最小二乘无条件数问题）反而 +0.021~+0.060 nats；更精确的 polar 因子 −0.0020（噪声内）；top-heavy 的 Schatten-4 衰减 +0.0177。几何上"更正确"不等于训练上更好。

## Key Contributions

- A held-out, curvature-resolved diagnostic for a matrix optimizer's step: grouped by singular-value band and by neuron group, with full cross-group curvature (not isolated probes), plus an exact scale/shape decomposition proved in Lean 4.
- A proved criterion for when a **same-norm** reallocation can beat Muon's step (Theorem 4), which the isolated greedy multipliers `g_b/H_bb` cannot decide (Theorem A.27 gives a counterexample).
- An anatomy of Muon: monotone over-stepping of the top singular directions (tail-to-top multiplier ratio 48/38/190 across three checkpoints), exploited by a same-norm reallocation until late in cooldown; and neuron imbalance concentrated in the fused QKV matrix.
- A controlled attribution showing the QKV split accounts for all of the block-wise variants' gain and shrinks NorMuon's apparent gain from 0.0150 to 0.0038.
- Machine-checked statements of the geometry used (Section A) and an explicit account of what formal verification did and did not predict (Section 7).

## Methodology

**Setup.** A 124.5M-parameter pre-norm decoder (12 layers, width 768, 12 heads, tied embeddings) trained on a 167.8M-token FineWeb-Edu sample with the GPT-2 tokenizer, sequence length 512, 24,576 tokens/step. Muon (sum-form Nesterov momentum 0.95, decoupled weight decay 0.1) acts on the 48 hidden matrices; AdamW (3e−4) handles embeddings and norms; gradient clipping at norm 1.0; warmup–constant–cooldown schedule (128 warmup steps, linear decay to 0.1× over the last 25%). Screen runs are 2,048 steps (50.3M tokens); confirmations 4,096 steps (100.7M tokens); metric is final loss on 4.18M held-out tokens. A 355M model (24 layers, width 1024, 16 heads, 96 Muon matrices) tests scale.

**Diagnostic.** Take checkpoints at steps 1,024 / 1,536 / 2,048, form the exact next Nesterov input `N`, and take a full SVD per matrix. Band directions use the exact polar factor `U_Ib V_Ib^T`; neuron groups use Muon's actual NS5 step split into sextiles by row (column) norm. Three disjoint held-out sets: 393k tokens for `g`, 98k for Hessian–vector products (double backward, math attention kernel), 393k for realized loss. `g_b = <∇L̂, D_b>`, `H_bb' = <D_b, ∇²L̂ D_b'>`, `c_b = g_b/H_bb`; 30 HVPs per checkpoint for B=6.

**Scale/shape split.** Theorem 3: `q(1) − q(c*) = ½(1−γ*)² 1ᵀH1 + ½(γ*1 − c*)ᵀH(γ*1 − c*)`, where `γ*` is the best uniform rescaling. The first term is the greedy step-size bias, the second is shape. Reported shape share is 17% / 12% / 97% at the three checkpoints.

**Fixed norm.** Because the diagnostics compare steps of equal Frobenius norm, they solve the exactly solvable trust-region problem on the ellipsoid `cᵀNc = 1ᵀN1` (N = diag(8,24,64,160,256,256)), certified globally optimal (Theorem A.22), and validate the prediction by measuring the actual one-step held-out loss.

**Interventions.** QKV split = three square 768×768 blocks orthogonalized separately (per-head = 36 blocks of 64×768; square blocks also split the MLP's 3072×768 and 768×3072). Every direction-changing arm is rescaled per matrix per step to `‖NS5(N)‖_F`. The spectral tilt `U ϕ5(Σ)(Σ/σ_rms)^p Vᵀ` is implemented as `D = P(S/Λ)O` with `S = ONᵀ ⪰ 0`, a degree-12 Chebyshev fit of `x^p` on [1e−3, 1], evaluated by Clenshaw's recurrence in FP32 (TF32 disabled), `Λ = 1.01·λ_max` from 20 power iterations. Controls: tuned (interior-optimum) learning rates, paired seeds 42–44, A/A check where tilt arms are mathematically identical to their baselines before cooldown (differences ≤0.009 nats).

## Results & Highlights

- **Split QKV is the whole block-wise gain.** At 100.7M tokens on FineWeb-Edu, split-QKV Muon reaches final held-out loss **3.9176** vs **3.9427** for fused-QKV Muon (−0.0250 nats, 95% t-interval [−0.0290, −0.0210], better in all three seeds); the fused baseline would need ~6.8% more tokens to match. Square blocks everywhere land at 3.9187 (−0.0239) and add only +0.0011 [−0.0029, +0.0051] over the split — the "block-wise" story is entirely a per-projection story.
- **The cooldown tilt is additive with NorMuon.** On the stronger split baseline: tilt alone 3.9057 (−0.0120 [−0.0137, −0.0102]), NorMuon alone 3.9139 (−0.0038 [−0.0062, −0.0013]), both 3.9018 (−0.0158 [−0.0178, −0.0138]) — i.e. additive within noise and worth ~4.3% tokens on the baseline's own loss-vs-tokens curve (0.260 nats per doubling). Adding energy-matched Schatten-1 decay gives 3.8966 (−0.0210 vs split, −0.0460 vs fused Muon).
- **Scale holds.** At 355M parameters the same recipe (split QKV + NorMuon + tilt) changes final loss by **−0.0188 nats** [−0.0202, −0.0175] with per-seed values −0.0188/−0.0183/−0.0194, against a baseline seed spread of only 0.0008 nats; the LR screen again peaks at 0.005 (4.0642/4.0547/4.0647 for 0.0035/0.005/0.007).
- **The measured curvature explains the timing.** Per-band multipliers (ranks 1–8 → 513–768) are 0.59→28.3 at step 1,024, 0.55→21.0 at cooldown start, 0.98→186.3 at the end; `γ*` = 0.52/0.49/1.52, and the second-order gap collapses from 0.00674 to 0.00137 nats. Measured one-step held-out ΔL at matched norm (×10⁻³): at step 1,024 halving ranks 1–8 gives **−6.14** and the best same-norm filter **−6.28**, vs Muon's **−1.76**; at the end of cooldown all filters agree with Muon within 0.13 (Muon −0.33, best −0.21) — the headroom is real early and gone late, and the tilt's lead peaks at −0.050 nats 256 steps into cooldown before settling at −0.0120.
- **Neuron imbalance is where the fused layout is.** Coefficient of variation of per-neuron update norms at step 1,024: fused QKV rows **0.43** (max/median 1.84), MLP expand 0.18, MLP contract 0.18, attention-out 0.02; block-wise polar drops QKV to 0.22 and MLP to 0.02–0.03. The most-moved neuron sextile has multiplier 1.5 and the least-moved 7.1, but only 5% of that group gap is shape at step 1,024 (65% at the end).
- **Principled-but-miscalibrated changes fail, as advertised.** Input whitening (GL(n)-equivariant, conditioning-free on least squares in undamped form) *hurts*: +0.0211 (¼,¼ norm-matched), +0.0599 (½,½), +0.0524 at its own magnitude; the published Toner recipe still gives −0.0068. Three extra cubic Newton–Schulz steps (accurate polar, I3) give −0.0020, inside noise. Top-heavy Schatten-4 decay +0.0177; bulk-pruning Schatten-1 −0.0057 [−0.0100, −0.0014].
- **A numerical-precision cautionary tale.** Evaluating the Chebyshev polynomial in monomials via Horner's rule under TF32 hits catastrophic cancellation (coefficients up to 4×10⁴) and flips the sign of the filter on the top direction: the arm scores −0.0240 instead of −0.0322 (Clenshaw/FP32), while exact eigendecomposition gives −0.0332. With Clenshaw the matmul tilt matches the exact tilt to direction cosine 0.998–0.999.

## Authors & Credibility

Biswa Sengupta, Jinhua Wang and Léo Maxime Brunswic are at the LLM Suite team, JPMorgan Chase & Co. (a disclaimer on page 1 states it is not Research Department output and not investment research). The work is a preprint (arXiv v1, 18 Nov 2026); no venue is named. The team runs a real pretraining stack (custom trainer, torch.compile + BF16 autocast, FP32 optimizer state, an earlier slower harness reproduced to 3.9445 vs 3.9463), has the engineering to run 3-seed factorial ablations at two scales and ~40 screen arms on three GPUs, and does Lean 4 formalization of the theorems. The related-work section is unusually well informed about the 2026 Muon-variant wave (NorMuon, Aurora, DynMuon, Freon/Kaon, MuonBP, HiMuon, SAMuon, BulkBoost, Toner) and about the optimizer-vs-tuned-baseline literature.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The individual ingredients are mostly precedented, which the authors say plainly: SAMuon already probes held-out optimal step sizes along singular directions and BulkBoost already proves when a norm-matched two-band reallocation helps to first order; NorMuon, Aurora and the QKV split in modded-nanogpt all exist. What is genuinely new is narrower than the title suggests: the *full cross-group* curvature (including off-diagonal `H_bb'`), the neuron-group axis, the exact scale/shape identity with a **fixed-norm** criterion (Theorem 4 plus the Theorem A.27 counterexample showing `g_b/H_bb` alone is not the right fixed-norm test), and using that criterion to attribute other people's gains. That is a real contribution to understanding, not a new optimizer.

**Rigor — the good part.** The controls are stronger than typical in this literature: per-matrix per-step norm matching, learning rates swept to an interior optimum on the *baseline* (0.005 was interior for Muon, split-QKV and the 355M baseline), paired seeds 42–44 with paired t-intervals, an A/A arm where the tilt code is mathematically inert before cooldown (≤0.009 nats empirical noise vs a 0.0032 seed spread), and an explicit gating rule for launching full runs. They also publish an unfavorable internal fact: a mid-study source audit could not certify that optimizer-file changes touched only inactive branches, so every comparison involving fused-QKV Muon, NorMuon-on-fused and square blocks crosses code versions (four hashes). The split-QKV arms — including the headline factorial — share one version. They report the failure rather than hiding it, and the A/A bound plus harness reproduction suggests the effect is small, but "suggests" is the operative word: the *attribution* claim "square blocks add nothing beyond the split" is exactly one of the cross-version comparisons.

**Rigor — the soft spots.** (i) Scale: 124M and 355M parameters, at most 100.7M tokens, i.e. **under one token per parameter**, on a single data distribution (FineWeb-Edu) with one tokenizer and one schedule shape. Effects are 0.012–0.046 nats on a 3.94-nat loss; whether a 0.3% loss change survives at 10–100× tokens is unknown, and optimizer rankings are known to reorder with token budget. (ii) Statistics: 95% intervals are nominal t-intervals over 3 seeds, **no multiplicity correction** across ~40 adaptively selected screen arms, most screen arms single-seed, and the anatomy was not pre-registered — the authors state the diagnostic and the first screens ran concurrently, so the "prediction" of F2 is a consistency check, not a forecast. (iii) The diagnostic is one-step, second-order, from a single checkpoint seed, uses only hidden matrices (the AdamW and decay parts of the update are excluded), idealizes NS5 as an exact polar factor for band directions, and leans on `H` with condition number ~1e4, which is why `c*` is visibly irregular across bands and is used only through realized losses and Theorem 3. (iv) The 38–190× tail-to-top multiplier ratio is a headline number that their own Theorem A.27 says is *not* the fixed-norm criterion — the actual measured exploit is −4.4/−3.2×10⁻³ from halving ranks 1–8, which is honest in the text but easy to over-read from the abstract. (v) The QKV mechanism ("Q, K and V share a single spectral budget, so the projection with the strongest momentum claims most of it") is explicitly labeled an un-isolated hypothesis; per-head blocks equalize rows exactly yet do worse (−0.0172 vs −0.0255 on screen), which is consistent with a projection-level rather than row-level story but does not prove the budget story.

**Significance.** The negative results are as valuable as the positive ones: a provably equivariant, conditioning-free whitening step loses 0.02–0.06 nats, and a more exact polar factor buys nothing — a clean demonstration that "certified geometric property" ≠ "training bottleneck". The headline practical recipe (split QKV + row normalization + cooldown tilt + Schatten-1 decay) is a 0.046-nat win that costs **+12% wall time against ~4.3% token savings**, so on wall-clock it is currently a loss; the authors say so. If the tilt can be folded into the Newton–Schulz coefficients (as DynMuon does) the accounting could flip, but that is future work.

**Red flags.** Single dataset, <1 token/param, nominal intervals, adaptive arm selection without multiplicity control, cross-source-version attribution for the key block-wise comparison, a mechanism hypothesis left unisolated, a cooldown whose length and final LR were never tuned (which the authors admit could recover part of the tilt's final gain — or erase it), and a certificate whose constants were never measured because experiments were paused.

## Strengths

- The joint, cross-group curvature measurement is the right instrument for a question that isolated probes cannot answer, and it is executed on a real training run rather than a toy quadratic.
- The fixed-norm criterion is a genuine conceptual fix: the paper shows with an explicit counterexample that the popular greedy per-group multiplier can point the wrong way once step norm is held fixed.
- Experimental hygiene far above the norm for optimizer papers: norm matching, tuned interior LR, paired seeds, A/A null arm, an explicit gating rule, and full logging with source hashes.
- Rare intellectual honesty: negative results published in full, the un-pre-registered status of the anatomy stated, the code-version confound disclosed, and a whole section on what Lean proofs did *not* buy.
- The TF32/Horner cancellation episode (coefficients ~4e4, sign flip on the top direction, −0.0240 vs −0.0322) is a concrete, reusable lesson about numerical evaluation inside optimizers.
- The tilt is deployable: matmul-only, rotation-equivariant by theorem, measured at 0.17 s/step on an A6000 during cooldown only.

## Limitations

- Only 124M and 355M parameters, ≤100.7M tokens, one corpus, one tokenizer — effects are regime-specific by the authors' own admission.
- The wall-clock ledger is negative (+12% time vs ~4.3% token saving), so the recipe is not yet a practical speedup.
- The diagnostic covers hidden matrices only, excludes the AdamW and decay components, assumes exact-polar band directions, and is local/one-step with an ill-conditioned `H`; the finite-step certificate's constants are unmeasured.
- The neuron analysis is diagnostic — the paper never shows that neuron-level imbalance *causes* loss, and the QKV spectral-budget mechanism is a hypothesis.
- Fixed-norm analysis covers singular bands only, not neuron groups.
- Nominal intervals, three seeds, no multiplicity correction, adaptively chosen arms, and cross-code-version comparisons for the fused-QKV baselines.
- Tilt dose (p = −1/4) is inherited from DynMuon and clearly not optimized (−1/2 scored slightly better on screen, −0.0155 vs −0.0133, one seed); cooldown length / final LR untuned.

## Relevance

与"LLM 训练稳定性与可观测性"高度相关：它给出了一个可在预训练中实际使用的诊断手段（留出集曲率 + 分组乘子）来判断优化器更新是否"步长分配错了"，并直接落到 FineWeb-Edu 上的 loss 收益。对"算子精度、确定性与误差传播"方向也有直接价值——文中 TF32/Horner 求值导致 Chebyshev 滤波器符号翻转、收益从 −0.0322 变成 −0.0240 的案例，是低精度中间计算如何静默改变算法行为的清晰实例。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the split-QKV gain (and the tilt's additivity) hold at ≥1B parameters and ≥10 tokens/parameter, or does it wash out as the model approaches its token budget?
2. Can the fixed-norm criterion (Theorem 4) be turned into an online, covariance-free per-step filter — e.g. a rank-2 or band-wise version estimated from a running `H` proxy — instead of an offline checkpoint diagnostic?
3. What exactly is the QKV spectral-budget mechanism? A controlled test that rescales per-projection momentum norms, or that fuses only two of the three projections, would separate "shared budget" from "different curvature per projection".
4. Why does provably equivariant input whitening lose 0.02–0.06 nats at fixed norm? Is it purely the output-movement argument, or does it also interact badly with the cooldown schedule?
5. Would the tilt help in a constant-LR regime with no cooldown (where the "baseline recovers the lead" effect cannot occur), and does its dose scale with model width?
6. How much of the reported effect survives the source-version confound if the fused-QKV baselines are re-run on the split-QKV code version?
7. Does the same curvature diagnostic explain why block-wise steps are more LR-tolerant (square blocks 4.1840 at LR 0.007 vs Muon's tuned 4.2043), which contradicts MuonBP's report that block-only training needs smaller rates at scale?
