> **一句话总结：** 单作者把 Claude Code 会话变成"权重外科医生"，在 Qwen2.5-1.5B/0.5B 与 Gemma-3-1B 的**既有层内**写入带门控的注意力头与 MLP 神经元（54/55 会话不改任何原权重、不反向传播、不拟合任务答案），50 次前向预算下把 6 个 BBH 子任务组合模型的归一化分数从 19.95 提到 41.05（高于 7B 兄弟模型三种测法的最高值 35.01），Gemma 从 10.25 到 32.86，并在 1,023,000 个 WikiText token 上 KL 恰为 0。

> **TL;DR:** An autonomous Claude Code session with two tools (a pass-counted host runner and a numpy calculator) writes gated attention heads and MLP neurons *inside the existing layers* of Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-0.5B and Gemma-3-1B-it, spending 5–34 of 50 forward passes and never seeing a validation or test instance. Under the Open LLM Leaderboard v2 protocol the 5-module Qwen composite raises normalized BIG-Bench Hard from 19.95 to 41.05 (+21.10, format control 19.75, oracle gain 21.37), beating every measurement of Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (34.89 official / 34.98 lm-eval-harness / 35.01 the authors' evaluator); the 4-module Gemma composite goes 10.25 → 32.86 (+22.58 over the format control, oracle 22.52). Extrapolation is the strongest evidence: addition is 100% at all 30 lengths on the sealed test after practice capped at 12 digits, and multiplication is 100.0% at 13–20 digits after practice capped at 6, where a LoRA trained on the same 25,600 examples scores 1.3% (Qwen) and 2.1% (Gemma). Both composites leave the next-token distribution on 1,023,000 WikiText tokens exactly unchanged (KL = 0) and give identical answers on 15,845 items of five other Leaderboard tests. The caveats are equally explicit: modules fire only on their template, 5 of 50 sessions hit the 12-hour cap, 4 of 45 modules fail the side-effect rule, and the "no gradients" claim was narrowed mid-flight after an audit found small numerical searches in 21 of 55 sessions.

## 核心贡献

- **一个"写权重"的作业流程（operating room）**：新组件以"出生即静默"的方式生在既有层里——门控是残差流的标量函数，关闭时按构造精确为 0；新头/新神经元各自带独立的 Q/K/V、gate/up/down；需要精确算术的组用 float64 计算并一次性舍入回 float32 残差。不新增层、不扩词表、不改原张量（55 个官方植入中 54 个完全不碰原权重，唯一例外 of-wol-q05 改动一个矩阵，Frobenius 范数 0.0083）。

- **协议先于结果的可信性工程**：练习/验证/测试集、假设、判定规则、以及"每条结论将被打印成什么句子"都在第一次会话前用 SHA-256 封存在 git，并把 manifest 盖比特币时间戳（区块 976290、976301、976462、976604）；验证集只以"每长度/每子任务的准确率"形式出售给 agent，实例永不可见；考试由冻结的评测器在"没有 surgeon 运行的机器"上跑一次，45 个完成的 stage-2 植入全部从补丁张量重建并得到相同 SHA-256。

- **1.5B 在 BBH 上超过 7B 兄弟**：Qwen 组合（5 个模块）41.05 vs 原模型 19.95（+21.10），高于 7B 的 34.89/34.98/35.01 三种测法；Gemma 组合（4 个模块）10.25 → 32.86（+22.58）。关键是"增益只来自有模块的 6 个子任务"：其余 18 个 BBH 子任务、以及 IFEval/MATH-L5/GPQA/MuSR/MMLU-Pro 共 15,845 题，组合模型与"格式控制"（同精度 float32、同模型文件、植入输出置零）逐题一致。

- **超出 agent 可运行范围的外推**：加法只练到 12 位、测试到 30 位，10/10 会话在每个长度都是 100%；乘法只练到 6 位、测试到 20 位，两族模型都是 100.0%（LoRA 同预算：1.3% / 2.1%）。作者的正确解读是"正确算法按构造外推"，真正被测的是 agent 对宿主旋转位置编码/操作数位置的理解能否迁移到它跑不到的长度。

- **跨任务、跨模型族的广度**：既有宿主"连提示里给算法都学不会"的 tracking_shuffled_objects（3/5/7 objects，Qwen 单次通过 31.6/17.6/13.6%，提示给算法也只有 38.0/14.4/15.2%），也有"只有思维链才会"的 web_of_lies（52.0% 单次 → 100.0%），还有两个**手册里没有算法**、由 agent 自己设计电路的任务（navigate、boolean_expressions，均 100.0%）。

- **把失败也写进正文**：5/50 会话撞 12 小时上限未产出模块、4/45 单模块未通过副作用规则（最大 44 次 top-token 变化/万 token，另一例 IFEval 掉 2.2 分）、Gemma 的 H9 只是 partial（改写集 navigate 78.3%）、以及"笔记本 MLX 后端上 3/5 个 Qwen 乘法植入答回原模型答案"——这些都直接影响结论的适用范围。

## Key Contributions

- **A capability-writing pipeline rather than a fine-tuning recipe.** Patches add gated attention-head groups and MLP-neuron groups to existing layers; 9,211 of 9,247 official groups carry a gate, and off-trigger the gate is exactly zero by construction, so the layer computes exactly what it computed before. Exact groups run in float64 and round once into the float32 residual. No new layers, no vocabulary change, no edits to original tensors in 54 of 55 official sessions.

- **A trust protocol for claims about agent-written weights.** Sealed sets, preregistered hypotheses, decision rules *and the sentence that reports each outcome*, Bitcoin-stamped manifests, a frozen evaluator run once on machines where no surgeon runs, 45/45 stage-2 implants rebuilt bit-identically from patch tensors, an independent audit tracing every weight to the code that computed it, and every documented amendment (E2-X1 … E2-X30) including the ones written after a computation had already run.

- **A 1.5B model above its 7B sibling on BIG-Bench Hard.** 41.05 vs 19.95 for Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, against 34.89 (official), 34.98 (lm-eval-harness) and 35.01 (their evaluator) for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct; Gemma-3-1B-it 10.25 → 32.86. The gain is localized: identical scores on the other 18 BBH subtasks, identical generations/choices on 15,845 panel items, KL exactly 0 on 1,023,000 WikiText tokens.

- **Extrapolation well past the agent's operating range.** Addition to 30 digits from 12, multiplication to 20 digits from 6, at 100.0% on sealed tests in both model families, versus 1.3%/2.1% for a budget-matched LoRA on the absent multiplication lengths.

- **Breadth of tasks and a documented manual ablation.** Circuits for one task absent even with the algorithm in the prompt, one compile-from-CoT task, two tasks whose circuits the agent designed from a manual that states only *what* to compute, and exact multiplication — in two model families. The manual records the design for three of five stage-2 tasks, so "the agent discovered the algorithm" is claimed only for navigate and boolean_expressions.

## Methodology

**The operating room.** The host is loaded once and never trained. The agent writes Python patches that build tensors, registered with hash and parent so any version can be reverted. A patch may add a group of attention heads (own Q, K, V, output matrices) or a group of MLP neurons (own gate, up, down projections) to an existing layer; the group's output is multiplied by a gate — a scalar function of the residual stream, typically a product of ReLU/step functions of affine maps — before being added. A pass is the scoring or generation of up to 512 prompts, or an activation read for up to 1,024 texts; each session gets 50 passes and the tool refuses more. Reading and analysing weights is free, which is why a session can spend hours in numpy before its first forward pass.

**What the agent can see.** A practice set with exact latent states, its own generated instances, and — for BBH — the three fixed exemplars of the evaluation prompt. It may spend one pass to request the sealed-validation score, which returns only accuracy per length or per subtask, never an instance. The tool refuses operands longer than the practice lengths (12 digits for addition, 6 for multiplication), so the agent physically cannot run the model beyond them. File permissions exclude the sealed files.

**The surgeon.** An autonomous Claude Code session (v2.1.326–2.1.327, model `claude-opus-5-5` in every recorded transcript, 12-hour cap), running headless on a cloud GPU in stage 2 and on a laptop in stage 1. It receives a task brief and a frozen, versioned manual (v1–v2 for addition, v3–v3.4 for stage 2) containing procedures and lessons from development sessions. The rule in the manual: no gradients and no numerical optimizers on the weights; class means, LDA, projections and any numpy computation to understand and design are allowed. Between rounds a separate "librarian" agent may edit the manual only with evidence from development sessions.

**Stage 1 — exact addition.** "What is a + b? Reply with only the final number", chat template, greedy decoding, operands 1–30 digits. Hosts: Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct (digit-level tokenization). Practice stops at 12 digits; the sealed test has 100 problems per length (3,000 per session); lengths where the base model scores ≤2% on sealed validation are "absent" (from 17 digits in 1.5B, from 9 in 0.5B).

**Stage 2 — six BBH subtasks plus multiplication.** tracking_shuffled_objects (3/5/7 objects), web_of_lies, navigate, boolean_expressions, scored under the Open LLM Leaderboard v2 protocol (chat template, three exemplars as turns, multiple choice by length-normalized log-likelihood). 2,000 practice instances per subtask from the authors' own sealed generator; validation 500 per subtask; test and *rewritten* test from the same generator with other seeds; the 250 public items are never shown. Multiplication uses up to 20 digits in validation/test. Each cell is five official sessions under one frozen manual version; absence is defined before operating as "no condition passes chance + 5 points" across direct answer, three chain-of-thought variants, and the algorithm written in the prompt.

**Composition.** A module enters a composite only if, composed with those already chosen, it changes no prediction of the others on their practice sets; selection order is highest sealed validation, then lowest mean KL against the format control, then fewer passes. The damage of each composite was predicted and sealed before composing. Qwen's composite has 5 modules; Gemma's has 4 — no product module composes with Gemma's tracking modules, so Gemma's headline number comes from BBH alone.

**Audit and statistics.** An independent auditor, with the criterion committed to git before any session folder was opened, classified all 55 official sessions by how each evaluated weight was computed: 27 class A (closed-form linear algebra), 21 class B (a numerical search whose numbers survive into the weights), 7 class C (tried and discarded). Twenty-one sessions set some design constants by small searches over rotary geometry, vocabulary or activations; 13 used (sub)gradient ascent on a margin — against the letter of the manual's rule. None backpropagates through the network and none fits any weight to the answers of task examples.

## Results & Highlights

- **Normalized BIG-Bench Hard, 24 subtasks, Open LLM Leaderboard v2 protocol.** Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct: 19.95 as released → **41.05** with five agent-written modules (+21.10 over the base, +21.30 over the float32 format control at 19.75), against Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct at 34.89 (official), 34.98 (lm-eval-harness) and 35.01 (the authors' evaluator). Gemma-3-1B-it: 10.25 → **32.86** (+22.58 over its format control at 10.28). The preregistered oracle — the gain from a perfect score on the six module subtasks — is 21.37 (Qwen) and 22.52 (Gemma), so Qwen's gain is close to and Gemma's reaches the ceiling.

- **Exact multiplication, absent lengths (13–20 digits), sealed test.** 100.0% for both the Qwen and Gemma product modules, versus **1.3%** (Qwen) and **2.1%** (Gemma) for a LoRA trained on 25,600 examples — exactly the agent's budget of 50 passes × 512 prompts — and 0.5%/0.4% on the 13–20 digit band specifically. The agent could run the model only to 6 digits and saw longer lengths only as one validation number per length.

- **Exact addition, 1–30 digits, sealed test.** All **10 of 10** official sessions (five per host) reach 100% at every one of the 30 lengths, after practice capped at 12 digits; the preregistered rule asked for 3 of 5 at ≥90% on the absent lengths. The budget-matched LoRA reaches 91.5% on 1–12 digits, **13.0%** on 13–20 and **0.0%** on 21–30 in Qwen2.5-1.5B (81.5/18.0/0.0 in 0.5B); chain of thought on the base model gives 15% at 15 digits and 0% at 20.

- **Side effects, measured against the format control.** KL is **exactly 0** and top-token changes are 0 on all 1,023,000 positions of 1,000 WikiText-103 passages for both composites; the same generation or choice on each of the **15,845** items of IFEval, MATH Level 5, GPQA, MuSR and MMLU-Pro; the same outcome on each of the **4,261** items of the 18 BBH subtasks without a module; no test on the 23-test panel drops by more than 1 point or is significant after Holm. For contrast, the LoRA's mean KL against the format control is **0.13** nats/token (Qwen) and **1.18** (Gemma).

- **Capability compilation, per subtask (own sealed test).** tracking_shuffled_objects: 99.1/99.4/99.2% at 3/5/7 objects in Qwen (5 of 5 sessions ≥90% on every absent subtask, median 100%) and 99.8/99.8/99.2% in Gemma, from base rates of 31.6/17.6/13.6% and 30.0/21.6/14.8% on the public set. web_of_lies: **52.0% one pass, 83.6% with chain of thought → 100.0%** in Qwen; 53.3% → 100.0% in Gemma. navigate and boolean_expressions, whose circuits the agent designed with no task manual: **100.0% and 100.0%** in both hosts.

- **Cost.** Addition implants consume 5–24 of the 50 passes, stage-2 implants 7–34; median wall-clock from first to last model call is 2.2 hours for addition (1.0–7.2) and 4.5 hours for a finished stage-2 session (1.7–10.2). Dense parameter allocations range from 1.5M to 338M entries per module (of-bool-q02), with nonzero counts smaller and not reported.

- **What did not work.** 5 of 50 stage-2 sessions ended at the 12-hour cap with no module after using only 10–19 of their 50 passes. 4 of 45 modules fail the side-effect rule — of-nav-g01 at 44 top-token changes per 10,000 tokens against a limit of 1, of-prod-g01 at −2.2 points on IFEval — and none of the four entered a composite. On the rewritten test 29 of 35 examined BBH sessions keep 100%; the worst is 76.8% (of2-bbh-q04, tracking with 7 objects) and Gemma's composite scores 78.3% on rewritten navigate. A perfect external calculator raises GSM-HARD only from 23.5% to 28.25%, below the preregistered 5-point gain, so the addition implant does not yet help a real benchmark.

## Authors & Credibility

Single author: Santiago Rivadeneira Quintero (EPFL). The paper is unusually explicit about provenance: which numbers came from which script, which amendments were written before versus after a computation ran, and which parts are presentation-only. The AI-use statement says generative AI wrote the manuscript, the code of the operating room/evaluator/baselines, and ran the experiments; the research question and the decision to pursue it are the author's, and "every number in the paper is generated by a script from result files." A separate agent audited how each weight was computed, against a criterion fixed in git beforehand.

Credibility positives are substantial and verifiable in principle: Bitcoin-stamped SHA-256 manifests (blocks 976290, 976301, 976462, 976604) predate the exam, each seal's timestamp is checkable, and 45 of 45 finished stage-2 implants were independently rebuilt on a different machine with identical hashes. Credibility caveats: this is a self-run, self-evaluated study by one author with no peer review, and the artifacts that would let an outsider check the strongest claims — code, sealed sets, session transcripts — are promised only "upon publication in a peer-reviewed venue." Three accidental views of exam data occurred (E2-X3, E2-X12, E2-X19), all documented; the authors note the rule that chose each composite's modules was written after the first of them.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The conceptual move — write an algorithm into existing layers as gated groups, with no added depth and no change to original tensors — is a real step beyond the closest prior work, which the paper cites precisely: a 3-digit adder written into GPT-2 small, a 12-digit adder in ten *new* layers placed before Qwen2.5-0.5B, and Granitzer et al.'s frozen tape module (trained writer, reader and gate) which already solves all 750 public tracking items in Gemma-3-1B. The honest framing is that the contribution is the *operating room plus protocol*, not autonomous algorithm discovery: the frozen manual records the algorithm, trigger positions and failure modes for multiplication, tracking and web_of_lies, so the agent-designed circuit claim rests on navigate and boolean_expressions — the two easiest tasks in the set (a mod-4 walk and a parenthesis-level boolean evaluator).

**Rigor.** The preregistration apparatus is the best I have seen in an arXiv preprint: sealed verdict sentences, amendments hashed before the exam was gathered, a frozen evaluator on separate machines, and a documented audit rather than a self-report. The statistics are, however, thin where it matters: five sessions per cell, decision rules of the form "3 of 5 at ≥90%" with failed sessions counted as 0, and no confidence interval on the single-run composite score. The score's sampling error is small (250–500 items per subtask), but the *session* variance is not quantified — the paper reports per-session validation values (e.g. 95.0 among four 100.0s for tracking) yet never puts an interval on 41.05.

**Baselines.** The LoRA comparison is the weakest link in the headline framing. It is budget-matched in examples (25,600), single-seed, best-of-four configurations, and the paper itself concedes it is "not the best trained method for length generalization." Beating it at 1.3% on 13–20-digit multiplication says little about trained length generalization in general, since Abacus embeddings, position coupling, scratchpad CoT and Tracr-style compilation are all cited in related work and none is run. The comparison is fair as a *same-budget* test; it is not evidence that writing beats training for arithmetic. Similarly, the "above the 7B sibling" result depends on six of 24 subtasks saturating: the paper states plainly that the composite matches its format control everywhere else, so the claim is protocol-true but says nothing about general capability — and the authors say so, which is to their credit.

**The gate is a template detector, and "no side effects" inherits that.** Off-trigger zero is guaranteed *by construction* only for inputs whose gate signal is zero; the empirical check is WikiText plus the Leaderboard panel. The paper concedes that "near forms of a task prompt can open a module's gates and change its output." That means the clean KL = 0 result is a measured bound on unrelated text, not a guarantee of behavioral invisibility, and the natural adversarial probe — paraphrases of the trigger that are not the task — is explicitly deferred to future work. A reader should treat "no measurable side effects" as exactly that phrase and no more.

**The backpropagation claim moved under audit pressure.** The abstract's final wording is "without backpropagation and without fitting any weight to the answers of task examples," but Appendix C reports 21 of 55 sessions with numerical searches in the surviving weights, 13 of them using (sub)gradient ascent on an attention margin over host activations on practice items. The distinction the authors draw (the gradient is on *where a head looks*, not on the answer) is defensible and was sealed before the exam, but it is a narrower claim than the earlier sealed sentence "without gradients or training data," and the paper's own amendment E2-X26 records the change. This is exemplary transparency applied to an overclaim that had already been made.

**Disclosed degrees of freedom.** The agent can request aggregate validation accuracy and stop when it is high, so the 43-of-45-at-100% validation picture is optimistically selected — acknowledged, and the sealed test (99.1–100%) largely vindicates it. More delicate: of the three accidental views of exam data, the first preceded amendment E2-X1, the rule that picks each composite's modules, and the rule then selected of2-bbh-q02 — the session in that view. The stated tiebreak (fewest passes among four modules tied at 100% validation and zero KL) is benign, and the disclosure is complete, but a skeptical reader should discount that one selection slightly.

**Scope and portability.** Hosts are 0.5B–1.5B, two families, one agent model, and tasks that all have a crisp algorithmic solution — precisely the class where a written program can be exact. The paper's own portability datum is sobering: on a laptop MLX/Apple Metal backend, 3 of 5 Qwen product implants returned the base model's answer on the Figure 1a example, so the result is tied to one software stack on L4 GPUs. And the addition implants have a finite carry window: a run of 5–8 columns whose digits sum to 9 can break them, so they are exact on the sealed test, not proven exact.

## Strengths

- A genuinely new and clean mechanism: capability as gated groups *inside* existing layers, silent by construction off-trigger, exact arithmetic in float64, original tensors untouched in 54 of 55 sessions. The layer-by-layer figure of the tracking implant is the kind of artifact that makes the claim checkable by eye.
- The strongest preregistration discipline I have seen in this literature — sealed sets, sealed verdict *sentences*, Bitcoin-stamped manifests, dated amendments, and a documented list of the three accidental views including the one that preceded a selection rule.
- Failure reporting that most papers omit: the 12-hour-cap sessions, the four modules that fail the side-effect rule, the Gemma H9 partial, the rewritten-navigate collapse to 78.3%, the MLX backend failures, and the fact that the arithmetic implant does not move GSM-HARD.
- Extrapolation is the right thing to test, and the paper tests it correctly: the agent cannot run beyond 12 (addition) or 6 (multiplication) digits, so 30-digit addition and 20-digit multiplication at 100% are not memorization of a distribution the agent could tune against.
- The audit in Appendix C, which contradicts the paper's own earlier framing and is nevertheless printed in full, is a model of how to handle an inconvenient finding.

## Limitations

- Modules fire on the task template. Rewritings test an announced family of constructions (Gemma's composite falls to 78.3% on rewritten navigate, and Gemma's navigate drops to 57.7% when the question type is "turns" rather than "face_forward"); generalization to free wording is not claimed and not tested.
- Exactness is empirical: the addition implants have a finite carry window, so a long run of columns summing to 9 can break them.
- Scope: hosts ≤1.5B, two families, one agent model, a handful of algorithmic tasks. The composite exceeds the 7B model only because six subtasks saturate; on the other 18 and the rest of the panel it matches its format control exactly.
- Side effects and failed sessions: 4 of 45 modules fail the side-effect rule, one module's panel is unmeasured because it changes an original matrix, and 5 of 50 sessions produced nothing within 12 hours.
- Validation is not test: the agent could request aggregate validation scores and stop when high, so validation numbers are optimistically selected; stage-1 KL is measured in full in only two sessions, and MMLU/ARC in one.
- Rounds: tracking's first official round was reclassified as development after its module failed the rewritten validation, so the counted round benefits from a manual written after that failure — a legitimate but real form of adaptation.
- Hardware: the exam spans L4, H100 and H200 GPUs; H200 panel instances were compared only against H200-computed format controls, Gemma was checked on the H200 only after its parts had run, and the laptop backend fails 3 of 5.
- Cost: hours of agent wall-clock per implant, with dense allocations overstating the true nonzero parameter count (nonzeros are not reported).

## Relevance

这篇直接命中"用 agent 操作模型内部表示、把能力写成权重而非训练出来"这一路线，是权重空间编程（weight-space programming）从"删障碍/教事实"跨到"补缺失能力"的关键实证；对关注可解释性、模型编辑、算术外推与评测可信性（预注册 + 封印测试）的读者都值得精读。其组合模型在 BBH 上反超 7B 兄弟的结果，也为"小模型 + 结构化模块"提供了一条与蒸馏/微调不同的工程路径。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the composite's 41.05 survive a leave-one-module-out ablation at test time, and what does removing a module do to the *other* five subtasks' per-item predictions — is the "no interference" property a stable equilibrium or an artifact of the practice-set admission rule?
2. The gates key on fixed template signatures ("␣swaps", the system message, the exemplars). How large is the set of inputs that open a gate but are not the task, and can an adversary or a benign paraphrase find one? A targeted probe would convert "no measured side effects" into a real bound.
3. How much of the result is the manual? The paper compares development versus official passes (5 better, 1 equal, 2 worse of 8 cells) but has no concurrent control. Running a cell with the algorithm withheld — as was done for navigate and boolean_expressions — on multiplication and tracking would isolate discovery from execution.
4. Does the approach scale past 1.5B? Wider residual streams mean more places to read features from, but also more room for a gate to fire spuriously; the 338M-entry boolean module in Qwen suggests the cost may grow faster than the host.
5. Can a module be written for a capability with no known algorithm (e.g. multi-step word problems, or a linguistic skill defined only by examples)? Every task here has a crisp specification, which is exactly the regime where writing beats training.
6. What is the nonzero parameter count and the inference-time FLOP overhead of each implant? Dense allocations of 1.5M–338M entries say little about whether these modules are cheap or expensive in practice.
7. Would an independent replication on a different software stack (not MLX, not L4) reproduce the multiplication implants, given that 3 of 5 failed on Apple Metal?
