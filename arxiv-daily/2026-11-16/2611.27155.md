> **一句话总结：** 对 8 个奖励模型、3 个生成式评审和 4 种 4-bit 量化器做全量逐对打分后发现，量化会改写 1.1–12.3% 的 RewardBench 偏好判定，而聚合准确率可能只动 0.03 个点（p=1.00）；翻转集中在"全精度 margin 小于量化噪声 σ"的窗口内（88.9–100% 的翻转发生在 z=|m|/σ<2），该窗口占用率随模型规模从 24.9% 降到 7.0%，下游表现为 best-of-64 选中的候选在 14.6–65.7% 的题目上换人、而最终答案只有 2.6–9.6% 变。

> **TL;DR:** Across 8 Skywork-Reward-V2/GRM reward models (0.6B–8B), 3 generative judges and 4 weight-only 4-bit quantizers, quantization flips 1.1–12.3% of RewardBench pairwise decisions while accuracy moves as little as −0.03 pts (Llama-3.2-1B/NF4: 88.38% vs 88.41% BF16, p=1.00, but 3.99% of 2,985 pairs flip, 60:59 harm:help). Flips concentrate at normalized margin z<2, occupancy of that window predicts flip rate (r=0.96 vs 0.78 for raw σ) and falls 24.9%→7.0% from 0.6B to 8B. The practical sting: for six (model, quantizer) pairs, best-of-64 selections change on 14.6–65.7% of problems versus only 2.6–9.6% different final answers (5.3–7.7×), and benchmark churn ranks that downstream change (ρ=0.89) while benchmark accuracy does not.

## 核心贡献

- **揭示了"净量 vs 毛量"的度量盲区**：准确率是净误差，churn（判定翻转率）是毛误差。当量化引入的错误与修复的错误近似对称时，准确率可以几乎不动，而部署的评审器已不是被 benchmark 的那个。极端例子是 Llama-3.2-1B/NF4：−0.03 个点、p=1.00，但 119/2,985 条判定（3.99%）被改写。
- **给出并验证了一个单参数的脆弱性窗口模型**：把量化看成对分数差的加性高斯噪声，翻转概率为 Φ(−|m|/σ)；在 30 个标量奖励模型设置中 88.9–100% 的翻转落在 z<2 内，且用留出对估计的 σ 使预测在 30/30 个设置中都是观测值的上界（保守包络，非等式）。
- **解释了"小模型更脆弱"**：尺度无关的噪声比 σ/std(m) 在 Qwen3 家族内从 0.34（0.6B）单调降到 0.10（8B），窗口占用率 P(z<2) 从 24.9% 降到 7.0%；占用率对翻转率的预测力（r=0.96）强于原始 σ（r=0.78）。
- **把比较级效应推到系统级输出**：在 1,000 题 × 64 候选上，六个 (模型, 量化器) 组合的 best-of-N 选中身份改变 14.6–65.7%，最终答案只变 2.6–9.6%；并给出"benchmark churn 能给下游变化排序（ρ=0.89, p=0.019）、benchmark accuracy 不能（ρ=0.77, p=0.07）"的初步证据。
- **诚实地报告了三个负面结果**：对抗压力只在部分量化器/家族上成立（NF4 在 GRM 上方向反转）、决策感知的 scale 微调（MarginTune）对 GPTQ 完全无效、更强的 32B 对抗者反而把效应洗掉；并以一张 claim-to-evidence 表逐条标注证据强度。

## Key Contributions

- **Separates net from gross error.** Aggregate accuracy is shown to be nearly blind to a quantization-induced rewrite of the decision function whenever harmful and helpful flips are near-symmetric. Fifteen quantized Skywork-Reward-V2 settings plus six GRM settings are tabulated with flip rate, bootstrap CI, harm:help split and McNemar p.
- **A one-parameter characterization.** Modelling quantization as additive Gaussian noise on the score difference yields P(flip | m) = Φ(−|m|/σ). With σ measured from the score files (never fit to flip data) and estimated on even-indexed pairs, the predicted aggregate rate exceeds the observed rate on odd-indexed pairs in all 30 scalar settings — an empirical envelope rather than a fit.
- **A mechanistic account of size scaling.** Fragility is governed by σ/std(m_bf16), not raw σ: within Qwen3 under NF4 it falls 0.34→0.10 from 0.6B to 8B, and window occupancy P(z<2) falls 24.9%→7.0%. Small judges are fragile because more of their decisions sit within reach of their own noise floor.
- **Downstream consequence measured at the level of shipped output.** Using mechanically verified correctness on 1,000 MATH-500/GSM8K problems with 64 candidates, the paper decomposes "different selection" from "different answer": selections change 5.3–7.7× more often than answers in every one of six pairs, and a pair the benchmark calls identical (0.03 pts, p=1.00) still changes 34.5% of unique-maximum selections.
- **Turns the model into a measured critique of itself.** In-distribution, a directly measured churn rate beats the Gaussian on level (2.35 vs 0.99 pp MAE at 200 calibration problems); as a router, margin-based escalation is beaten by plain top-k at matched budget. The paper states this plainly instead of selling the cascade.

## Methodology

**Models and quantizers.** Eight scalar reward models from Skywork-Reward-V2 (Qwen3 backbones at 0.6B/1.7B/4B/8B, Llama-3.2 backbones at 1B/3B) plus two GRM models (gemma2-2B, Llama-3.2-3B) as an independent-family replication, plus three generative pairwise judges (Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen2.5-3B, Mistral-7B-v0.2). Four weight-only 4-bit schemes: NF4 and FP4 (bitsandbytes, double quantization, block 64), INT4-RTN (symmetric round-to-nearest, group 128), and GPTQ (group 128, calibrated on 512 UltraFeedback pairs and 1,200 GSM8K train pairs disjoint from all evaluation sets). Only linear layers are quantized. BF16 is treated as ground truth throughout: the object of study is what quantization changes, not what is correct.

**Evaluation sets.** (i) RewardBench filtered split, 2,985 pairs over 23 subsets. (ii) Adversarial best-of-4: 1,260 problems (329 MATH-500, 931 GSM8K), each with one symbolically verified-correct solution and three verified-wrong but persuasive ones from a Qwen2.5-7B adversary, so "fooled" is an objective event. (iii) Large-N selection: 1,000 problems with 64 sampled candidates each.

**Definitions and statistics.** For a chosen/rejected pair the margin is m = s_a − s_b; a flip is a sign change between BF16 and quantized scores. σ = std(m_quant − m_bf16). Flips are split into harmful (BF16 right, quantized wrong) and helpful, tested with an exact two-sided McNemar, and pooled results use a problem-level clustered bootstrap (2,000 resamples of problems, percentile intervals) because models share problems and each trap contributes three comparisons. Of 66 McNemar tests, 33 of the 41 reaching p<0.05 survive Benjamini–Hochberg at q<0.05; the eight that do not are marked †. Both the latent statistic z=|m_bf16|/σ and the observable ẑ=|m_quant|/σ are distinguished, with the latter used for routing.

**Audit and routing protocols.** The audit holds out problems (never comparisons), estimates σ on 200 or 500 calibration problems, and compares Φ(−ẑ) against two baselines: the calibration churn rate and a logistic in |m|, scored by MAE on held-out churn and by Brier/AUC for discrimination. Routing scores all N candidates with a quantized proposer and re-scores only comparisons with ẑ<τ at full precision, with top-k and random escalation as matched-budget baselines, and the 8B/0.6B-INT4 scoring-throughput ratio R=5.03 used to convert to an estimated cost saving.

## Results & Highlights

- **Accuracy is flat while decisions move (RewardBench, n=2,985).** Llama-3.2-1B under NF4 scores 88.38% against 88.41% in BF16 (−0.03 pts, p=1.00, harm:help 60:59) yet 3.99% of pairwise decisions (95% CI [3.28, 4.66]) differ. The same pattern appears at Qwen3-8B/NF4 (1.21% flips for −0.20 pts, p=0.41) and Llama-3.2-3B/INT4-RTN (3.02% for −0.34 pts, p=0.34). Across all 30 settings the flip rate spans 1.1–12.3% while Δaccuracy ranges −5.49 to −0.03 pts; the worst case is Qwen3-0.6B/INT4-RTN at 12.33% flips, 82.58% vs 87.54% (−4.96 pts, harm:help 258:110, p<0.001).
- **The window explains the rate better than aggregate noise does.** 88.9–100% of flips occur at z<2 in every one of the 30 scalar settings. Held out, the Gaussian envelope over-predicts the aggregate rate 30/30 — e.g. Llama-3.2-1B/NF4 7.29% predicted vs 3.77% observed (+3.52 pts, CI [+2.58, +4.43]) and Qwen3-0.6B/NF4 11.76% vs 7.47% (+4.28). Window occupancy falls 24.9%→7.0% and σ/std(m) 0.34→0.10 from 0.6B to 8B under NF4, and occupancy correlates with flip rate at r=0.96 versus r=0.78 for raw σ.
- **Harm needs an adversary, and its sign belongs to the (model, quantizer) pair.** On 1,260 verified traps, INT4-RTN pools to 146 harmful vs 54 helpful flips over four sub-8B Skywork models (95% CI on the difference [63, 122]); Qwen3-0.6B's fooling rate rises from 5.87% to 10.48% (1.8×). NF4 goes 73:35 harmful on Skywork but 13:41 helpful on GRM (both p<0.001), i.e. quantization made those GRM models harder to fool. Benign best-of-16 on 2,319 math problems shows no significant per-model harm (31:25, 35:22, 19:13, 19:20; all p≥0.11; pooled 104:80, p=0.09).
- **Downstream, identity changes far more than answers.** For the headline pair (Llama-3.2-1B BF16 vs NF4, benchmark-identical at 0.03 pts), best-of-64 selects a different candidate on 50.3% of the 1,000 problems, 90.7% of those switches return the same final answer (4.5% different answers overall), 34.5% of disagreements are at a unique maximum, and Δaccuracy is +0.80 pp (CI [−0.10, +1.70]) — unresolved, not equivalent. Across six pairs the untied selection-change rate is 14.6–65.7% against 2.6–9.6% answer change (5.3–7.7×). Benchmark churn ranks this (ρ=0.89, p=0.019); benchmark accuracy does not (ρ=0.77, p=0.07).
- **The model's own diagnostic value is modest and the paper says so.** All margin-based predictors order flips identically within a setting (AUC 0.927 on RewardBench, 0.981 on traps; the constant-rate baseline orders nothing at 0.500), so the Gaussian contributes nothing to discrimination. On level it loses in-distribution to a measured churn rate (2.35 vs 0.99 pp MAE at 200 calibration problems, σ winning only 11/90 settings) though it wins cross-distribution (RewardBench→traps: 0.59 vs 3.40 pp). Routing at N=32 escalates 25.3% of comparisons, recovers 84% of the 0.6B→8B gap at an estimated 52.5% lower scoring cost, but is 0.35 pts worse than top-k at the same budget (CI [−0.71, −0.00]).
- **Generative judges replicate the churn but break the bound.** INT4-RTN rewrites 6.37% of Qwen2.5-7B's decisions while accuracy moves +0.07 pts (94:96, p=0.94); Qwen2.5-3B flips 37.6% of decisions for −11.42 pts, and Φ(−z) under-predicts there (23.6% predicted vs 37.6% observed) because judges flip at z=3–5, which is why the envelope is scoped to scalar reward models.

## Authors & Credibility

Three authors: Omkar Shewale and Deepak Kumar (Illinois Institute of Technology) and Divakar Kumar Yadav (University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee). This is a small, non-frontier-lab team working on a single H100 NVL (94 GB); there is no large-scale cluster or industry compute behind the numbers, which is consistent with the modest model sizes (≤8B) and the small downstream pool. The paper is unusually disciplined about evidence: a claim-to-evidence table maps every headline claim to its population, table and uncertainty treatment; the eight tests that fail Benjamini–Hochberg are explicitly marked; two negative results and one retracted earlier claim (that the systematic margin shift predicts the direction of harm) are reported in the appendix. It also discloses extensive LLM assistance (Claude) for drafting, for the analysis pipeline that recomputes every statistic, and for proposing several experiments — the authors state no experimental data were model-generated and that they verified every number, but a reader should weight the "LLM wrote the measurement code" arrangement as a reproducibility risk rather than a disqualifier. No public code URL appears in the text; the reproducibility statement says outputs, scripts and the asset-generation script are released alongside the preprint.

## Critical Assessment

The central empirical claim is solid and the experimental design is better than most quantization papers: σ is estimated on even-indexed RewardBench pairs and evaluated on odd-indexed ones, so the "one parameter, never fit to flip data" claim is actually honored; pooling uses problem-level clustered bootstrap rather than the (much smaller) McNemar p-values, and the authors explicitly say the unclustered McNemar over-states significance. The harm:help decomposition plus the base-rate analysis (68–84% of in-window comparisons were originally correct, yet originally-incorrect decisions flip 1.5–3× more often per comparison — 30.7% vs 16.1% at 0.6B/INT4-RTN) is a genuinely clarifying piece of bookkeeping that most "quantization hurts reasoning" papers omit.

The weak point is the framing of accuracy as "close to blind." That is true for the pairs the paper leads with, but in 22 of 30 settings the accuracy drop is statistically significant and strongly asymmetric (Qwen3-0.6B/FP4: −5.49 pts, 265:101 harm:help), so accuracy is not blind — it is blind precisely when the induced errors are near-symmetric, which is the paper's own condition. The abstract's "as little as 0.03 points" is a selection of the friendliest row; a reader could reasonably conclude that the benchmark is useless, when the more accurate statement is that it is uninformative in a specific and identifiable regime. The rank-correlation result that would justify replacing accuracy with churn rests on six (model, quantizer) pairs, which the authors admit is too few; with n=6, ρ=0.89 (p=0.019) versus ρ=0.77 (p=0.07) is a difference of one rank inversion, not a demonstrated ordering.

Two further issues. First, the Gaussian is decorative for the two uses the paper proposes: within a setting it is a monotone transform of |m| and therefore cannot change any ordering (their own AUC table shows this), and on the level a measured churn rate beats it in-distribution. Its only real win is cross-dataset transfer, which is measured in exactly two directions between two datasets, which the authors concede are "not independent replications." Second, the stress tests are narrower than the claims: downstream evaluation is math-only (MATH-500 + GSM8K), with one generator and one same-family arbiter (Skywork-8B BF16), so "answer-equivalent" is corroboration by a sibling evaluator rather than independent judgement, and there is no code, chat, or open-ended generation where a different candidate is much less likely to be answer-equivalent. The GPTQ result is the most interesting unremarked finding — GPTQ reconstructs activations 3–5× better than INT4-RTN (median error ratio 0.204–0.329 over eight layers) yet produces more decision churn on Llama-3.2-1B (9.18% vs 7.04% flips, −4.22 vs −1.54 pts) — and it deserves more than one paragraph, since it directly undermines reconstruction-error-based quantizer selection. Finally, the cost claim (52.5% saving) is a scoring-throughput estimate at R=5.03, not measured latency, and GRM's R=1.00 means the cascade cannot save anything there; the paper is honest about this, but the abstract's routing sentence is not.

## Strengths

- The net-versus-gross reframing is simple, correct, and immediately actionable: benchmark accuracy answers "how many did it get right," not "is this the same evaluator."
- Clean, falsifiable theory with a genuinely held-out test: σ fit on even pairs, prediction validated on odd pairs, 30/30 conservative, with the failure mode for generative judges reported rather than hidden.
- The size-scaling story is mechanistic and quantitative: occupancy 24.9%→7.0%, σ/std(m) 0.34→0.10, occupancy beating raw σ at r=0.96 vs 0.78.
- The downstream decomposition (selection identity vs final answer, tied-maximum vs unique-maximum) is the most useful artifact in the paper for practitioners, and it replicates across two families and three quantizers with a 5.3–7.7× ratio in every pair.
- Exceptional honesty: three negative results, a retracted claim, a claim-to-evidence table, BH-corrected significance marks, and explicit statements that the routing contribution is matched by top-k and the Gaussian adds nothing to ordering.

## Limitations

- Downstream evidence is math-only (MATH-500, GSM8K), one generator, one same-family arbiter; no code, chat, summarization or agentic selection where answer-equivalence would be rarer and the 4.5% answer-divergence figure would likely grow.
- Six (model, quantizer) pairs for the churn-ranks-downstream claim; the rank comparison is statistically thin and the authors say so.
- Weight-only 4-bit only, no 8-bit, no AWQ/HQQ, no group-size or clipping sweep, so the "quantizer choice matters more than model choice" observation (INT4-RTN flips 1.3–1.8× NF4) is established on four schemes rather than the design space.
- The Gaussian envelope's 20–45% over-prediction is unexplained ("we leave the slack unexplained"), and its only unique advantage — cross-distribution transfer — rests on two dataset directions.
- No measured latency or energy; the 52.5% saving is a scoring-throughput extrapolation, and no wall-clock cascade was timed.
- Generative-judge coverage is three instruct models with traps evaluated on only two of them (Qwen2.5-3B excluded from trap comparisons because its BF16 error is too high), so the "judges are worse" conclusion is thinner than the scalar-RM one.
- Heavy LLM involvement in writing the analysis pipeline is disclosed but not independently audited; the paper's own claim-to-evidence discipline partly compensates.

## Relevance

直接命中"极端性能优化下的可靠性"：它量化了 4-bit PTQ 在奖励模型/评审器上的能力退化，并指出聚合准确率这一常规验收指标会漏掉 1–12% 的判定翻转——对任何用 best-of-N 或 RM 做筛选的流水线都是可直接落地的验收口径修正。其"加性噪声 + margin 窗口"的分析也与算子/数值误差传播的视角相邻（σ 即量化引入的分数扰动尺度）。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the 5–8× selection-to-answer ratio hold outside math, e.g. code generation with unit-test-verified correctness or agentic tool selection, where "answer-equivalent" candidates should be much rarer?
2. Why does GPTQ, with 3–5× lower activation-weighted reconstruction error, churn more decisions than INT4-RTN on Llama-3.2-1B? Is this an outlier-layer effect, a calibration-set mismatch (UltraFeedback chat + GSM8K vs RewardBench), or evidence that reconstruction error is simply the wrong objective for argmax stability?
3. Can a quantizer be trained against a decision-margin objective that beats INT4-RTN rather than merely reducing σ by 11–19%? The MarginTune null result on GPTQ suggests the residual noise there is structural, but only per-group scales were searched.
4. How does the window interact with reward-model ensembles and with the overoptimization scaling laws of Gao et al. — does ensembling shrink σ/std(m) the way parameter count does, and does the effective sample size of best-of-N change when the selector's decisions are noisy rather than merely biased?
5. What is the right acceptance test for a quantized judge in production: a churn budget on a stratified low-margin slice, an answer-level disagreement rate, or a paired test against the full-precision selector at a pre-registered tolerance? The paper argues for the second and shows the third is underpowered at 1,000 problems.
