Why this matters

Aggressive compression buys a cheap model and an expensive failure mode. This paper prices both: decoding fixes recover more than weight surgery, and the ledger includes the wall clock.

Critical edge

Method B bundles two changes, and the crucial control is missing: an untrained 66-expert model was never run on code, so the +9.2 pp cannot be assigned to the trained corrections rather than 0.26 GiB of restored capacity.

How it was built

  • Model. Ornith-1.5-122B: 48 layers, 256 routed experts of width 1024, top-8 routing plus one shared expert. REAP scores experts on the authors' calibration data; the top 64 per layer survive. Kept slices and router rows are copied byte-for-byte out of the public 2-bit file (UD-IQ2_XXS), so no kept weight is requantized — 11.62 GiB.
  • Decoding fix S. A presence penalty of 1.5 over a 20,480-token window. The window has to be that long because observed loop periods run to thousands of characters, far past the server's 64-token default.
  • Stop and redraw. A text-only tail detector (period P ≤ 6000 chars; compares the last max(5P, 200) chars every 256 chars) kills a reply and resamples with a new seed, at most three draws.
  • Method A. Every kept expert's gate/up/down matrix becomes W + BWAW, rank 8, zero-initialised so fitting starts exactly at C. Layers are fitted in order to reproduce the 4-bit teacher's routed-expert outputs on the pruned model's own activations; router, attention, shared expert and embeddings stay frozen.
  • Method B. A plus the next two experts in each layer's REAP order, restored with their router rows (66 per layer, retention 25 → 25.8%). They train in full precision through a straight-through estimator to their deployed quantized values; file grows to 11.88 GiB plus a 1.16 GiB adapter.
  • Fit data. 1,018 calibration documents at 2,048 tokens, plus C's own 124 long replies from the old test half at 12,288 tokens — the fit sees the hidden states the model occupies when it reasons at length. Development problems and fresh panels are never used.
  • Statistics. Paired by problem, 95% bootstrap over 10,000 resamples, exact McNemar on discordant pairs. Every stage ran from a frozen file listing arms, seeds, problems and SHA-256 hashes; the driver refuses to start on hash mismatch.

Why 44 versus 0 is causal

Control
Both arms serve the same 2-bit expert bytes; C simply lacks 192 of the 256 per layer.
Confound
None from quantization: going 4-bit → 2-bit without pruning adds zero loops (110 vs 107 solved, both 0 loops).
Scale
Loop-at-limit share shifts +36.7 pp [+28.3, +45.8] with pruning; solved problems drop 30.0 pp [+21.7, +38.3].
Ceiling
No limit-hitting reply was ever a success — 0 of 45 at 32K on the old test half.

A matched-quantization control is exactly what earlier loop papers lacked. The 2-bit unpruned model solving 107 of 120 also confines the "2-bit reasoners loop" story to smaller dense models.

Strengths

  • A clean causal control: byte-identical kept experts separate pruning from quantization and give an unambiguous 44-versus-0 attribution.
  • Takes the free baseline seriously and reports, unprompted, that redrawing beats its own method's margin.
  • Pre-registration done properly — hashed artifacts, dated amendments, a driver that refuses to run on mismatch, an explicit "inconclusive" branch.
  • Refuses to claim the low-rank form beats the dense update it adapts, and refuses to attribute B's gain to a mechanism it did not measure.
  • Publishes the failure decomposition and layer-wise reconstruction numbers that undercut its own framing — median reconstruction error improves only 21.8% (A) and 22.8% (B).
  • Artifact-level detail others omit: the 20,480-token penalty window, the exact loop detector, straight-through-to-quantized training, deployment parity checks.

Limitations

  • One model, one ratio (25%), one quantization, one benchmark family, one recipe per method — no rank, data or learning-rate search.
  • B bundles low-rank corrections with two restored experts per layer; A ran only on development problems and no untrained 66-expert control was ever run on code, so the gain is unattributed.
  • No comparison with dense EWU (the method A adapts), activation steering, router distillation or expert-wise distillation — six recovery families are cited, none are run.
  • Arms compared at equal draws, not equal cost: 4.63 h vs 2.73 h wall time (1.7×), with no decomposition of the slowdown.
  • Sampling at 32 slots does not pin a reply to its seed; confirmation-seed variance is comparable to the effect size.
  • Fresh problems predate the model, so shared pretraining exposure may compress the difference. Artifacts are promised for a later version and are not available now.

Questions for further exploration

  1. What does an untrained 66-expert model score on the same 130 fresh problems?
  2. Would dense EWU at 25% retention match B, and does the load-time low-rank form cost accuracy?
  3. How much of B's gain depends on C's own long replies from the old test half in the fitting set?
  4. Why do gains land on finished-wrong medium problems when reconstruction improves only ~22%?
  5. Does the loop detector's threshold (P ≤ 6000 chars) transfer to a different pruning morphology?
  6. Where do the 1.7× wall-time hours go — expert FLOPs, adapter overhead, or longer surviving replies?
