Methodologyrecipe, end to end

Decoder-only sparse MoE: 52 layers, 256 experts (8 active), GQA 48/8, 5:1 interleaved local–global attention, logit soft-cap 30. Pretraining runs 23.8T tokens at 16K sequences on 6,144 GB300s in under four weeks (92.3% goodput, 21% MFU). Data curation is the heavyweight contribution: 245B raw HTML pages parsed into 97B documents and filtered with domain-partitioned pipelines rather than global heuristics.

  • Pretrain. 23.8T tok · 6,144 GB300 · code 35.5% / STEM 23.9%
  • Midtrain I. 850B tok @ 32K — verifiable STEM, competitive programming, SWE and terminal data (seed → generate → verify → route).
  • Midtrain II. +100B tok → 512K — 50% organic long context / 15% synthetic long-QA / 35% replay; RULER extrapolates to 1M.
  • Post-train. Short SFT, then fully asynchronous GRPO: 110K concurrent rollouts, ~12 min median, 12 s weight sync; ~1M tasks survive filtering; verifiable rewards plus a reward-hacking judge.
  • Merge. Separate agentic and alignment teachers joined by full-vocabulary on-policy distillation (MOPD), with per-component routing weights — an explicit “split-braining” probe.
  • Serve. FP8 (W8A8) and NVFP4 (W4A4 experts only, Local Hessian scales calibrated on 32.8M agentic tokens), plus a 1.93B block-diffusion drafter.

Three readingsbeyond the headline

  • Bifurcation, not uniform strength. Beam wins where benchmarks are near-saturated (SWE-bench Verified 81.6, LongBench v2 67.5) and loses where horizon and codebase comprehension matter (SWE Atlas 31.8 vs 68.0; DeepSWE 43.3 vs 74.2).
  • Teacher merging mostly works. The distilled student beats its agentic teacher on SWE-Bench Pro v1 (67.1 vs 62.2), Terminal-Bench v2.1 (80.1 vs 77.7) and SciCode (49.5 vs 47.2), but drops on DeepSWE (40.2 vs 43.4) — split-braining is measurable, not fatal.
  • Knowledge is the weak lane. SimpleQA Verified 41.8 vs 53.7, AA-Omniscience index 10.8 vs 22.6, IFBench 79.6 vs 82.8 — the “frontier reasoning” framing holds on AIME/GPQA-style sets, not on breadth.

Strengthswhat is unusually solid

  • Infrastructure disclosure is rare for the genre: 92.3% goodput, 21% MFU, 110K concurrent rollouts, 12-second weight sync, 99.99% batch packing, named failure modes with recovery procedures.
  • Quantization is selected on held-out KL and top-1 agreement rather than benchmark noise, and the paper says so.
  • Context extension reports its own regressions (GPQA −1.2, Simple Terminal −1.7, MMLU-Pro −0.4) next to the RULER gain (+58.4 at 512K).
  • The merge step is stress-tested instead of hidden: a split-braining probe, a per-component routing table, and an admitted 3.2-pt DeepSWE drop from teacher to student.
  • Safety numbers include unfavorable third-party results — MBCT eighth of thirteen, prompt-injection ASR worse than five named models.

Limitationswhat to discount

  • The 3–4× efficiency claim is never measured: no tokens-per-task, no FLOPs-per-correct-answer, all evidence is Beam-vs-Beam.
  • Baseline coverage is incomplete and non-random, and competitor token budgets are neither equalized nor reported.
  • The base-model table pits a midtrained checkpoint against pretraining-only baselines with mismatched shots — MATH 94.9 vs 64.5, AIME 63.7 vs 1.9 invites contamination questions.
  • 1M context is an extrapolation from 512K training, measured only by RULER; no task-level evaluation at 1M exists.
  • Safety Pareto claims rest on an unreleased in-house suite (ISE) with no judge-validation statistics.
  • Openness is weights-only: data, RL task pools, judges and training code are withheld, so the 1M-task curation is unverifiable.

Questions worth runningopen probes

  1. What actually drives the 30.9-pt DeepSWE gap — horizon, harness scaffolding, tool-token budget, or reward shaping? A harness swap at fixed weights would settle it.
  2. Does the efficiency edge survive when rivals receive the same realized token budget rather than a nominal effort setting?
  3. Why does DeepSWE regress from teacher (43.4) to student (40.2) while SWE-Bench Pro v1 improves (62.2 → 67.1) — coverage, temperature, or interference from the alignment teacher?
  4. How much of base-model AIME 63.7% comes from correctness-anchored midtraining, and were eval sets decontaminated against AIME 2026?
  5. What happens at 1M tokens on multi-hop retrieval over many documents, instead of RULER’s synthetic needles?
  6. How sensitive is the 88.9% / 81.5% safety point to judge model and rubric, given a 10× ASR swing from adding one policy prompt?

Verdictread it how

Significance is high; the framing is tilted.

A credible new Western open-weight frontier point, with 1M-context, quantization and async-RL infrastructure detail genuinely worth stealing — if you read the per-benchmark table rather than the abstract.

Algorithmic novelty is moderate-to-low (GQA, interleaved attention, GRPO and on-policy distillation are all established). The new pieces are the 50/15/35 midtraining mixture with a regression audit, the split-braining probe, and the Local Hessian NVFP4 recipe.

Reproducibility: weights (BF16/FP8/NVFP4) and a drafter are Apache 2.0; the data, RL environments, sandbox stack and 10.5K-GPU campaign are not reproducible outside Reflection.
