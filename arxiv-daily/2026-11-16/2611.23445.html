Pinning 13,952 weights (0.34%, 14 KB) in the stem and Stage 1–2 at W8 lifts uniform W4 from 23.03% to 65.95% top-1.

Why this matters

Maps sub-4-bit PTQ/QAT's failure boundary: where accuracy dies, what protection buys back, and why 4-bit is slower than FP32 on CPUs.

Critical edge

The abstract promises that keeping <5% of critical layer activations in 8-bit recovers 92% of lost accuracy — but the body protects weights (0.34%), never activations, and no table yields 92%.

  • Model. ReLU6/SGD EfficientNet-B0, 88 epochs, 5.32M params → 72.864% top-1 / 91.106% top-5. B1/B2 and two BiFPN variants serve as cross-architecture checks.
  • Sensitivity screen. 46 localized arms quantize one stage or operator family at a time (rest stays FP32) on a class-balanced 2,000-image subset; the six worst masks are re-verified on the disjoint 48,000 images and on all 50k.
  • Quantizer. Symmetric weight-only fake quant, per-output-channel scale; clipping grid-searches α ∈ [0.5, 1.0] to minimize L2 rounding error; BN moments re-estimated DFQ-style on 1,000 images. Fake quant isolates representational loss from kernel speed.
  • Allocation. 82 layers × {W3, W4, W8, FP16} = 328 evaluations feed an additive surrogate, solved as a knapsack DP at three mean-bit budgets: 3.56 / 3.49 / 3.40 bits.
  • Factorial + deploy. 16 arms (4 profiles × {max-abs, MSE} × {source BN, recalibrated BN}) all on 50k; then LiteRT FlatBuffers and a packed 3-bit stream are timed on one host CPU with size and p50/p95 latency.

04Where recovery stops

Best recovered model: 68.984% at 3.56 mean bits.

−3.880 pp versus FP32 (72.864%), and −2.544 pp versus a 1-epoch FP32 QAT control (71.528%). That residual is the paper's explicit ceiling — not a solved problem.

The last step is the fragile one: QAT adds +1.044 pp (95% CI [+0.796, +1.292]) over same-profile PTQ, after a 1k-image post-QAT BN repair contributes +2.912 pp on its own. In other words, one epoch netted less than fixing the batch-norm statistics it disturbed.

Uniform 3-bit QAT is seed-fragile: one seed gained +0.364 pp over immediate QAT, another lost 10.630 pp — leaving B0 6.2–16.8 points below FP32 after a full epoch.

INT8 is the only Pareto-dominant point: all 82 ops on native 8-bit SIMD. Sub-4-bit pays software unpacking — WI4 is 2.32× slower than INT8, and the packed 3-bit stream reaches p95 386.6 ms (paper quotes 21.5× FP32). That 21.5× attaches to the 0.30% uniform model, not the 68.98% recovered one.

The size of the cliff varies 2.3× across architectures, so the constant is the shape of the failure — early spatial filters — not a universal number of lost points.

Practitioner trap documented in the paper: epoch-1 checkpoints (5.88–18.88% FP32) lose <1.6 pp under INT4/INT3. The same recipe at 70%+ loses 24–51 pp. Early pilots systematically understate quantization fragility.

Where it holds upStrengths

  • Every headline accuracy number is on all 50,000 validation images — no 1,000-image cherry-picking.
  • The 46-arm sensitivity screen is cross-validated on the disjoint 48,000-image complement.
  • Recovery is reported as a per-stage waterfall, which a practitioner can budget against; a single "our method hits X" would be less useful.
  • The FP16-vs-W8 null result (+0.026 pp, CI spanning zero) is a real design constraint, not a headline.
  • Inconvenient results are kept: seed-reversed delay-QAT (−10.630 pp), logarithmic INT3 grids losing 2.92–3.72 pp, latency regressions.
  • Checkpoints, DP profiles, FlatBuffers and SHA-256 manifests are released — rare for an industrial single-author study.

Where to push backLimitations

  • The 3.88 pp floor is one model, one recipe (B0, ReLU6/SGD, 88 epochs); B1/B2 are descriptive only.
  • The QAT endpoint is 1 epoch, 1 seed, 1 profile — while the paper's own INT3 runs swing ±10 pp across seeds.
  • The DP surrogate is additive, yet the measured clipping × BN interaction (−5.066 pp) proves additivity fails.
  • BN recalibration is fixed at 1,000 images with no 1k/5k/50k ablation, so the data cost of +3.94 pp is unknown.
  • Latency comes from one unnamed host CPU — no core count, ISA baseline or memory bandwidth, despite a microarchitectural argument.
  • Accuracy study is weight-only fake quant; activation quantization is never run, though the abstract invokes 8-bit activations.
  • No BRECQ/QDrop-style 3–4-bit baseline, so 67.94% PTQ is hard to place against the literature.
  • Baselines sit 3.4–3.9 pp below the canonical Tan & Le recipe; absolute numbers are internally consistent, not SOTA-comparable.

07Questions for further exploration

The experiments this paper most needs next.

  1. Is 60.154% a coincidence? MSE-only and BN-only both land on the identical delta and endpoint to three decimals — either an extreme coincidence or a mislabeled arm in Section 4.
  2. Does 68.98% survive a seed sweep? Given the ±10 pp INT3 QAT swing the paper itself reports, a 3-seed run of the final recipe is the single most important missing experiment.
  3. How much data does +3.94 pp cost? Recalibration set size (1k vs 5k vs 50k) is the knob a practitioner actually controls, and it is never ablated.
  4. How far off is the DP optimum? With an additive surrogate against a measured −5.066 pp interaction, a block-reconstruction or learned allocator is the obvious comparison.
  5. Would early-W8 + rest-W4 beat a tuned 4-bit BRECQ? At 65.95% / 4.01 bits, the honest test is against published 4-bit PTQ on the same checkpoint.
  6. Does WI4 recover its 4× once unpacking is free? A sub-byte LUT SIMD kernel or an NPU with native 3/4-bit depthwise support would decide whether the loss is software or structural.
