> **一句话总结：** 把「可编辑的 agent harness」当作冻结模型的非参数策略组件，用任务前置检查清单计算「成对方向性遗憾」（新被改坏的准则优先于一直没解决的准则），再用 Soft-HURT 门控只在收益大于回归时才提交补丁——在 EvoHarnessBench 上把 ACC 从 71.91% 拉到 80.80%（vs Meta-Harness 74.52%），并把每任务 token 降低 54.5%。

> **TL;DR:** GUARD evolves a persistent Markdown "harness playbook" (prompts, skills, tool rules, memory) around a frozen model without any grader, by decomposing each task into 4–8 atomic pre-execution checklist predicates, ranking them by paired regret (Δhurt + Δgap) so a freshly broken rule outranks an old unresolved gap, and gating every candidate edit with a "Soft-HURT" rule that requires net gain to exceed induced per-task loss. On the hosted EvoHarnessBench calendar track (43 test tasks, single-model Gemini 4) it reaches 80.80% ACC vs 74.52% for Meta-Harness and 71.91% for the no-evolve baseline (+8.89pp, paired p=0.008 vs W0 but only p=0.11 vs Meta-Harness), while cutting tokens per task 54.5% (59,651 vs 131,032); on Drift Gauntlet it is the only arm with 0/12 stream-mean drops and 0.00pp post-block forgetting (final mean 73.22% vs GEPA 72.40%).

## 核心贡献

- **把「自我改进失败」拆成两种信号**：以往的反思式/标量分数式 RSI 把「这次编辑新弄坏了什么」和「一直没解决什么」混在一起，导致局部过拟合、技能遗忘、记忆污染；GUARD 用成对方向性遗憾 $\Delta_{\text{hurt}} + \Delta_{\text{gap}}$ 把两者显式分开。
- **免评测器（grader-free）的前置检查清单自评**：在 solver 第一次动作之前，只看任务 prompt 和公开的 tool schema，就把任务编译成 4–8 条原子二元判据，并用「沙箱执行 + 语义轨迹」两层打分；这利用了「生成时要同时满足 N 个约束很难、但事后逐条核对一个判据很容易」的生成–验证不对称。
- **GRPO 式的候选池 + 对比式策略演化**：每轮并行采样 M≥2 个候选 harness，在同一批任务上用组内标准化适应度选优（不需要 value critic、不更新任何权重），并用最好/最差候选的对比来改写下一轮的提案 prompt。
- **Soft-HURT 门控 + 拒绝账本**：只有当净增益超过被改坏任务的损失时才晋升编辑；把会被误触发的专门规则用 `[iff <condition>]` 前置条件收窄而不是删掉；被拒的编辑写进去重账本，避免下一轮重复同样的坏提案。
- **理论刻画**：证明门控在受控 buffer 上使自评分数单调不降（Thm 1）、密集判据评分消除了稀有不变量的 $1/\epsilon$ 被动发现代价（Thm 2），并证明成对优先级是唯一同时满足「新回归严格压过旧缺口」和「精确锁定已掌握准则」的参数无关排序（Prop 1）。
- **部署侧收益**：测试时关闭候选池、自评与门控，只跑一次冻结 harness（pass@1），上下文成本 O(1)，并在 hosted 基准上把每任务 token 砍掉 54.5%、MCP 调用减少 22.5%。

## Key Contributions

- **Diagnostic decomposition of RSI failure signals.** Prior reflection- and scalar-score-based harness learners (Reflexion, GEPA, RRSI) cannot separate edit-induced regressions from unresolved capability gaps. GUARD formalizes the two as paired regret terms over pre-execution checklist criteria and ranks them by their sum.
- **Reference-free checklist self-review.** A Turn-0 pass over the task prompt plus public tool declarations compiles 4–8 atomic binary predicates (execution-verifiable ones graded deterministically in an ephemeral sandbox, semantic ones graded individually by an LLM against the trace), cached across all rounds and candidates. No reference answers, verifiers, or rubrics are ever exposed.
- **Critic-free group-relative candidate selection with contrastive prompt evolution.** M≥2 candidates sampled in parallel from a single domain-agnostic seed prompt are scored on the *same* task batch, so batch difficulty cancels; best-vs-worst contrasts rewrite the proposal strategy prompts each round (half exploitation prompt, half precondition-narrowing prompt).
- **Soft-HURT promotion gate with `[iff]` repair and a rejection ledger.** Promotion requires $\bar S(\tilde W) - \bar S(W_{t-1}) > \mathrm{Hurt}(\tilde W, W_{t-1})$; isolated regressions trigger at most one in-round `[iff]` scope repair instead of deletion; rejected edits are logged and read back at O(1) prompt cost so they are not re-proposed verbatim.
- **Scoped theory.** Non-regressive telescoping of the self-review objective on each round's gated buffer, a concentration bound over $n_{\text{eval}} = 1 + T_{\max}(M + \mathbb{I}_{\text{rep}})$ evaluated harnesses, and a proof that both the frequency ranking $1-\text{obey}$ and the signed difference alone fail one of the two properties the sum satisfies.

## Methodology

**Problem formulation.** An agent is $A = (\pi_\theta, W_{t,g})$: a frozen model policy $\pi_\theta$ plus an inspectable harness state $W_{t,g}$ (Markdown playbook: workflow instructions, entity-grouped domain rules, `SKILL.md` skills, `MEMORY.md` memories, optional pre-submit checks, and `[iff <condition>]`-scoped exceptions). Only $W$ is optimized; the harness is a non-parametric policy component. Adaptation is $W_{t,g} = E(W_{t-1,g}, H_{t,g})$ for $t \le T_{\max}$, where $H$ is the interaction history; at test time evolution freezes and each unseen query runs a single pass under $W_{T,g}$.

**Stage 1 — pre-execution checklist and two-layer grading.** At Turn 0 the self-reviewer reads only the task prompt, visible tool declarations and schema, and derives a fixed checklist $C(q)$ of atomic binary predicates in two classes: execution-verifiable (clean sandbox execution, valid entity IDs/columns, idempotent mutations) and semantic (every prompt constraint, recipient, format, safety rule, and non-application of superseded rules). Layer 1 grades execution predicates deterministically on an ephemeral sandbox copy; Layer 2 grades semantic predicates one at a time from prompt + output + tool/state trace, returning $r(\tau,c)\in\{0,1\}$ plus a failure rationale. Scores are $S(\tau) = 100 \cdot \frac{1}{|C_{gr}|}\sum_c r(\tau,c)$ and $S^{\text{strict}}(\tau) = 0$ on a blocking sandbox error, $100$ if all graded criteria pass, $S(\tau)/2$ otherwise. An unparseable verdict counts as failed.

**Stage 2 — Analyze (paired regret ranking).** Criterion obedience is $\text{obey}(c;W) = \mathbb{E}_{\tau \in \mathcal{T}_c(W)}[r(\tau,c)]$. Comparing the two most recent incumbents gives
$\Delta_{\text{hurt},t}(c) = \max(0, \text{obey}(c;W_{t-2}) - \text{obey}(c;W_{t-1}))$, $\Delta_{\text{gap},t}(c) = 1 - \text{obey}(c;W_{t-1})$, and $\text{Priority}_t(c) = \Delta_{\text{hurt}} + \Delta_{\text{gap}} \in [0,2]$ — both terms non-negative and parameter-free. With single-rollout binary verdicts this yields exactly {2 = regressed, 1 = unsolved gap, 0 = mastered}, which splits criteria into three diagnostic lists sorted by priority: Regressed (wrap the offending rule in `[iff]` instead of discarding a specialized fix), Unsolved Gap, and Mastered (pinned against overwriting). On tasks with differing rollouts the bundle also pairs $\tau^+/\tau^-$ whenever $\Delta_{\text{strict}} > 0$ or $\Delta_{\text{cont}} \ge \delta_{\text{con}} = 10$, capturing partial checklist progress that blocking errors would flatten.

**Stage 3 — Propose (group-relative pooling + ContrastEvolve).** Round 1 initializes all M candidates with the same domain-agnostic seed prompt $s_0$ that names no benchmark, metric, or tool; diversity comes from sampling, not operator priors. All M candidates are scored on the identical $(W_{t-1,g}, B_{t,g}, Q_g)$, so batch difficulty cancels without a learned critic. Perf $= \bar S^{\text{strict}} + \tfrac14 \bar S$; fitness $R_m = \text{Perf}_m - \beta|Q^-_m| + \gamma|Q^+_m|$ with $\beta = 3.5 > \gamma = 1.5$; standardized $\hat A_m = (R_m - \mu_R)/\sigma_R$ selects $m^\star$, with $m^+ = \arg\max$, $m^- = \arg\min_{m \ne m^+}$ feeding a contrastive rewriter that scrubs task IDs and emits an exploitation prompt and a precondition-narrowing prompt ($\lfloor M/2 \rfloor$ / $M - \lfloor M/2 \rfloor$ split for round $t+1$). No gradient step, no clipping and no KL term — Soft-HURT plays the trust-region role.

**Stage 4 — Gate (Soft-HURT) and rejection ledger.** With hurt set $Q^-$ and $\mathrm{Hurt}(\tilde W, W) = \frac{1}{|Q_g|}\sum_{q \in Q^-} (S_q(W) - S_q(\tilde W))$, promotion requires $\bar S(\tilde W) - \bar S(W_{t-1}) > \mathrm{Hurt}$. The parameterized form adds noise floor $\delta_{\text{noise}} = 15$, hurt budget $h_{\max}$ (0 on the continual suites, 1 default, $\text{round}(0.18|Q_g|)$ on the professional suites), and margin $\mu_{\min}$ (1.5, or 3.0 with at least two helped tasks on the professional suites). If $1 \le |Q^-_{m^\star}| \le h_{\text{rep}}$, at most one in-round `[iff]` repair is attempted at $\mu_{\min}=0$ and must pass the same rule on re-scored trajectories. Rejections are deduplicated into an on-disk JSONL ledger (read-back window $L_{\max} \in \{12, 40\}$) and rendered into the next regret bundle, so a refused edit is not repeated verbatim while its `[iff]` refinement stays eligible.

**Theory.** Theorem 1: under the gate, $\bar S(W_T) = \bar S(W_0) + \sum_t \Delta_t \ge \bar S(W_0)$ with $\Delta_t > 0$ on promotion and 0 on rejection (telescoping because the incumbent's score is the one recorded when it was evaluated), plus a $\sigma\sqrt{2\log(2 n_{\text{eval}}|G|/\alpha)/(|Q_g|N_{\text{rep}})}$ union-bound concentration term. Theorem 2: passive end-task learners observe a rare invariant with probability $p_j \le \epsilon$ and need $\mathbb{E}[T_{\text{passive}}] \ge H_J(1-\epsilon)/\epsilon = \Omega(\log J/\epsilon)$ rounds, whereas dense per-criterion grading gives $\mathbb{E}[T_{\text{target}}] \le H_J/(\rho b_{\min}) + 1 + J_{\min}/\rho$ — the $1/\epsilon$ penalty disappears under any ranking that assigns positive weight to failing criteria. Proposition 1: with rounding-tolerant obedience, paired priority strictly outranks a non-regressed criterion whenever $\delta_c > 0$, the trade-off is exact ($\text{Priority}(c) > \text{Priority}(c') \iff o_c - o_{c'} < \delta_c$), and $\text{Priority}=0$ iff the criterion is obeyed *and* not regressed — neither $1-\text{obey}$ (ties new regressions with static gaps) nor the signed difference (assigns 0 to unchanged failures) achieves both.

**Scoping and deployment.** Playbooks are bounded (2.3k–4.2k chars). On Drift Gauntlet sections are keyed to the pre-execution observable tool/schema signature (`bfcl`, `sciworld`, `bird:<db_id>`), each ≤3,800 chars, placed at both the start and end of the system prompt; a single global playbook ablation still reaches 72.35% (Δ_K=+2.44pp), so routing is worth ~0.87pp of the total. At test time candidate pooling, self-review and gating are off: one frozen harness, one rollout, pass@1, O(1) context.

## Results & Highlights

- **EvoHarnessBench hosted SDK (calendar v1–v3, 43 test tasks, unified Gemini 4): 80.80% ACC vs 74.52% for Meta-Harness and 71.91% for the no-evolve $W_0$** (+8.89pp; paired exact sign-flip $p=0.008$ vs $W_0$ with 8 tasks better / 0 worse, but only $p=0.11$ vs Meta-Harness with a 95% bootstrap CI of $[-0.4, +14.5]$pp on 43 tasks). Solve rate 58.14% (25/43) vs ≤51.16% (22/43) for every baseline; 182/221 official verifier checks vs 156–170/221; 0/43 eight-step budget exhaustions vs 6–10/43.
- **Deployment cost: 59,651 tokens per task vs 131,032 for $W_0$ (−54.5%) and 78,705 for Meta-Harness**, with 11.21 vs 14.47 MCP calls (−22.5%) and 233.8s vs 221.6s latency — i.e. 9.75 tasks solved per 1M tokens (1.50× the best baseline, 2.61× $W_0$). The saving is mostly structural: it tracks the elimination of budget exhaustions, not a shorter prompt.
- **Drift Gauntlet (4 hidden-drift streams, 12 blocks, 360 adaptation / 360 held-out test rollouts per arm): final mean 73.22% vs 72.40% for GEPA (+0.82pp, which the authors themselves note is "about three task-equivalents"), 71.21% Meta-Harness, 70.31% Hermes+, 67.92% RRSI (below the 69.92% $W_0$).** GUARD is the only arm with 0/12 stream-mean block drops, $\Delta_K = +3.30$pp positive on 4/4 streams, and $F = 0.00$pp worst-case post-block forgetting, while also posting the top plasticity $P = +4.55$pp — every baseline loses 3–8 blocks out of 12 (RRSI $F = -8.11$pp, including −20.00pp on the BFCL-Miss→BFCL-Base workflow flip).
- **PAST-Bench (12 families, critic-scored selection): Task 0.854 vs GEPA 0.858 — GUARD is *not* best here** — but it leads Mechanism 0.878 vs 0.837, Update 0.924 vs 0.791 (+13.3pp over Hermes+), Write Precision 0.776 vs 0.724, and Evolve 0.436 vs 0.424, at the shared 0.167 pollution floor (vs 0.500 for RRSI / Meta-Harness), with 0.000 procedural pollution on PC01/PC03/PC04 and 0/48 family losses on W.Prec. or Poll. against all four persistent baselines.
- **Component ablations (236 local-gym test tasks, 16 stages, matched models): removing Analyze drops macro ACC 66.46% → 59.13% (−7.33pp, 2/5 domains regressed); removing the Soft-HURT gate drops it to 56.11% (−10.35pp, 3/5 domains regressed, one unvetted ITSM stage collapses cumulative accuracy to 7.61% by hallucinating a `get_current_user` MCP call).** A full local-gym Stage-v4 abstention is what keeps that from happening.
- **Gate fidelity and reviewer calibration (4,173 rollouts + Drift Gauntlet block decisions, zero oracle labels during adaptation): 19/19 execution-gated promotions have $\Delta_{\text{test}} \ge 0$ (15 strictly >0, 4 ties)** — 9/9 Drift Gauntlet blocks, 5/5 EvoHarnessBench stages, 5/5 professional-suite role promotions. Bypassing Soft-HURT on a rejected local Calendar v3 candidate costs −6.98pp Solved / −2.40pp ACC. A 1-layer reviewer passes 22.2% (12/54) of execution-broken traces that 2-layer review rejects 100% of; Layer-1 pass/fail separates official ACC by +27.92pp (73.90% vs 45.97%, 82.6% NPV) over 4,059 rollouts.
- **Professional suites (frozen DEV, 16 tasks × 2 trials): PRBench-Legal 66.67% vs $W_0$ 59.48% (+7.19pp, 95% CI [+1.85, +12.85], exact sign-flip $p=0.0251$, 4 helped / 0 hurt, Tier-1 critical pass 54.90% → 62.10%) and Fin-Rate & Ledger 50.00% vs 37.50% (+12.50pp, but the 95% CI [+0.00, +31.25] touches zero and $p=0.50$ on $n=16$ with only two helped ledger tasks).**
- **Propose-stage ablation at matched budget $TM=2T$ (dual-axis ITSM, $N=132$, $N_{\text{rep}}=3$): parallel $M=2$ pooling 81.08% vs 78.85% for sequential single-candidate search (+2.23pp, $p=0.0267$), 64% fewer rejected gate rounds (4 vs 11 ledger entries), and positive BWT +0.61% vs −1.28% / −1.65%.**

## Authors & Credibility

Seven authors from **Google Cloud** (Minghe Zhang and Xuetao Kang equal contribution; corresponding authors both at google.com). This is a well-resourced industrial lab paper in an active line of work — the same group's refs [45] (RRSI), [20] (Meta-Harness), [30] (ReasoningBank), [49] (Harness2) and [36] (Context Language Models) show repeated investment in exactly this problem, and the baselines are largely their own prior systems, which is both a strength (they know the failure modes) and a conflict of interest (they also own the gate). Citation practice is current, with 2026 references and honest attribution of the benchmarks they did not write (PAST-Bench [50], EvoHarnessBench [17], Drift Gauntlet [5]). The paper is unusually candid in places — it reports the $p=0.11$ Meta-Harness comparison, the CI touching zero on Fin-Rate, and the fact that FWT = 0.00 only "by abstention". No code is released yet ("we plan to release reference harness code"), only prompt templates in the appendix.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The core idea — that a scalar round score conflates *what this edit broke* with *what was never solved* — is a genuinely sharp diagnosis, and the paired-regret fix is well motivated rather than cosmetic. Proposition 1 is the most convincing piece of the paper: it shows by construction that the two obvious alternatives each fail exactly one of the two properties, so the sum is not arbitrary. The connection to UED curriculum regret (PAIRED/MaxMC) and GRPO group-relative advantage is analogical rather than deep — there is no gradient, no critic, no clipping — but the analogy does real work: the equal-task-batch scoring is what makes candidate comparison meaningful. Component-wise, `[iff]` precondition narrowing instead of rule deletion, and the deduplicated rejection ledger, are small practical ideas that likely account for a large share of the stability gains (the ledger caps at 12 vs 40 entries depending on runner, and this is never ablated).

**Rigor.** Mixed, and the weak spots cluster exactly where the headline number is. On EvoHarnessBench, the reported 80.80% is the *best of five* hosted GUARD configurations evaluated on this same 43-task test split (mean 77.09%, range 75.57–80.80%), so the selection procedure saw the test split; the paired gain over Meta-Harness has $p=0.11$ and a CI reaching −0.4pp. The headline "no negative forward transfer" is partly vacuous: Soft-HURT abstained at stages v2–v3, FWT is 0.00 "by abstention", and the entire +8.89pp lift comes from a single 2,315-char Stage-v1 playbook. On the local gym, GUARD abstains so completely on Calendar and Drive that $W_{\text{final}} = W_0$, which is indistinguishable from "learned nothing" and is reported as a non-loss. On CSM the strict solve rate actually *drops* 30.56% → 25.00% while ACC rises 59.80% → 61.40% — a regression on the harder metric that gets one parenthetical clause. PAST-Bench is the honest counterexample to the "consistently improves" framing: Task 0.854 < GEPA 0.858 and Recall 0.856 < 0.858, so the paper's claim rests on Mechanism/Update/W.Prec./Evolve instead. And the Drift Gauntlet margin over GEPA is 0.82pp on 360 rollouts (~3 tasks) with no significance test on the macro comparison, which the authors concede in text but not in the abstract.

**The self-grading problem is only half-solved.** In the primary regime, solver, self-reviewer, distiller and evolver are all the same frozen Gemini 4, so the signal that both ranks candidates and gates promotions is produced by the model being improved. The post-hoc calibration is the right response, but the numbers are modest: on PAST-Bench the critic's episode-level bias is near zero (−0.012) yet its correlation with the official evaluator $V^\star$ is only $r=0.369$ (Spearman 0.377), and within-family playbook ranking concordance is 82.2% (75.6% non-tied). A reviewer whose mistakes are *unbiased* but only weakly correlated with truth will pass some genuinely bad edits, and the Soft-HURT gate is only as good as that reviewer. The Layer-1/Layer-2 ablation is a partial answer, but the paper admits the 0/23-vs-12/54 row is partly tautological by Eq. (2) — the non-tautological evidence is the third row (Layer-1-only fail vs pass ACC 45.97% vs 73.90%), which is one benchmark.

**Reproducibility.** Moderate-to-poor. Prompt templates and all hyperparameters ($\delta_{\text{noise}}=15$, $\beta=3.5$, $\gamma=1.5$, $h_{\max}$, $\mu_{\min}$, $L_{\max}$, $M$, $\delta_{\text{con}}$) are in Appendix B, which is better than most. But every model is a proprietary hosted endpoint (`Gemini 4`, `claude-opus-5-5`, `gemini-3.8-flash`), no code is released, and several knobs are set per suite ($M=4$ only for PRBench-Legal, $L_{\max}=12$ vs 40, $h_{\max}=0$ vs 1 vs $0.18|Q_g|$). The PRBench result depends on that: $M=4$ reaches 66.67% while $M=2$ reaches 65.76%, a 0.9pp difference on 16 DEV tasks. The memorization of held-out rollouts per (task, rendered playbook) is defensible for variance control but means arms are not all paying identical sampling costs.

**Significance.** The problem is important and the test-time economics are attractive (one pass, O(1) context, −54.5% tokens). But the effect sizes that survive scrutiny are small and benchmark-specific: +0.82pp on Drift Gauntlet, a Task metric on PAST-Bench that does not beat GEPA, a professional-suite win whose CI touches zero, and a hosted-benchmark win that is best-of-five on 43 tasks. The genuinely robust findings are qualitative and negative-in-form: gated promotion prevents the catastrophic regressions that unguarded reflection causes (RRSI's −20.00pp single-block collapse, the ITSM hallucinated-tool 7.61% collapse without the gate), and the Analyze stage is worth +7.33pp macro. Those are real contributions; the "consistently improves" headline is stronger than the evidence.

**Red flags worth naming.** (1) No limitations section anywhere in the paper. (2) No accounting of adaptation cost — the complexity table gives $O(TMQ)$ rollouts, i.e. GUARD burns twice the adaptation rollouts of single-candidate RRSI at $M=2$, and no dollar/wall-clock comparison is given for the training phase whose savings are the paper's selling point at test time. (3) The "reference-free" framing is carefully scoped (no reference answers, test cases, verifiers or rubrics) but adaptation still uses benchmark-authored train prompts and reward-shaped self-checks, which is a weaker claim than "unsupervised". (4) Theorem 1's guarantee lives on each round's gated buffer, not across distribution shift — the authors say so, but it means the guarantee is weakest exactly in the Drift Gauntlet setting the paper headlines.

## Strengths

- The failure decomposition (regression vs. gap) is the right abstraction, and Proposition 1 proves the chosen ranking is not one of many equally good options but the unique parameter-free one satisfying both properties.
- `[iff]` precondition narrowing plus a deduplicated rejection ledger is a simple, well-motivated answer to the oscillation problem that plagues reflective self-editing — and the worked example (Box C.1: a +6.25pp candidate with 6.25pp hurt rejected, holding BFCL-Miss at 70.00% where RRSI lost 20.00pp) makes the mechanism concrete.
- Deployment realism: a single frozen harness, one pass, O(1) context, 54.5% fewer tokens and 22.5% fewer tool calls. Many harness papers optimize a benchmark and ignore the serving bill; this one does not.
- Unusually honest reporting in several places — the $p=0.11$ paired comparison, the CI touching zero, the "by abstention" caveat, the 80.80%-is-best-of-five disclosure, and the symmetric routing control that gives GEPA and Meta-Harness the same signature routing (they reach 71.55% / 71.08%, still below 73.22%).
- The calibration audit (Table 4 / C.6) is the kind of evidence this literature usually omits: Layer-1 discriminative power over 4,059 rollouts, critic bias and correlation against $V^\star$, 19/19 non-regressive promotions with the exact ties spelled out, and the counterfactual cost of bypassing the gate.
- Component ablations are matched (same models, same $M$, same seed) and each removal produces a large, interpretable degradation rather than noise, which is the strongest internal-validity claim in the paper.

## Limitations

- **Small and possibly split-selected headline evidence.** 43 hosted test tasks, 16 frozen DEV tasks per professional suite, 360 Drift Gauntlet rollouts per arm; the 80.80% figure is the maximum over five GUARD configurations on that split.
- **Abstention masquerades as stability.** FWT = 0.00 by abstention, $W_{\text{final}} = W_0$ on 2/5 local-gym domains, and 3/12 Drift Gauntlet blocks rejected. A method that declines to edit is trivially non-regressive; the paper does not separate "correctly conservative" from "unable to find an admissible edit".
- **Weak reviewer correlation undermines the gate.** $r = 0.369$ between critic and official evaluator on PAST-Bench is thin for a signal that decides both selection and promotion; there is no experiment with a deliberately miscalibrated reviewer.
- **One point estimate, no seed variance for the main results.** Drift Gauntlet is single-seed (42); hosted EvoHarnessBench reports no seed spread across the five configurations' 75.57–80.80% range, which is itself large enough to swamp the +0.82pp Drift Gauntlet margin.
- **Adaptation cost is unaccounted.** $M=2$ doubles adaptation rollouts relative to single-candidate baselines, plus one full self-review pass per rollout and at most one repair sweep; none of this is priced against the test-time savings.
- **No code, proprietary models, per-suite hyperparameters.** The method's behavior under a different backbone or a weaker reviewer (the regime most practitioners face) is untested — the cross-model teacher–student configuration always gives the *stronger* model to the reviewer/distiller.
- **Checklist scope is narrow.** 4–8 atomic predicates derived from Turn-0-visible prompt and schema; tasks whose constraints only become visible mid-trajectory, or long-horizon tasks with dozens of coupled requirements, are out of distribution and not evaluated.

## Relevance

直接命中「自演化 agent」这一主线：它给「冻结权重 + 可编辑 harness」的递归自我改进提供了一个可检验的稳定性机制（配对遗憾排序 + 回归门控），而「遗忘 / 记忆污染 / 局部过拟合」正是我们关心的长时程 agent 可靠性失效模式。对关注 agent harness 静默失效（规则被后续编辑悄悄覆盖）的读者，其 `[iff]` 收窄与拒绝账本也是可直接借用的工程手段。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of the Drift Gauntlet advantage survives with multiple seeds? The reported +0.82pp is ~3 task-equivalents, and the five hosted configurations already span 5.2pp.
- What happens when the reviewer is *weaker* than the solver (the realistic distillation case)? The gate's guarantee is conditional on $\bar S$ tracking $\mu$, and the only calibration evidence ($r=0.369$) is at equal model strength.
- Is abstention ever permanent? If a suite enters a regime where every candidate has $\mathrm{Hurt} \ge \Delta$, the harness freezes at $W_{t-1}$ forever — is there a mechanism to detect and escape that state, or does the rejection ledger slowly poison the proposal prior?
- Does the rejection ledger ever lock out a correct edit that was rejected once under a transient environment fault (the paper mentions relay/tunnel outages requiring a re-execution pass)?
- How does paired regret interact with weight-space adaptation or test-time search? GUARD deliberately freezes both, so the "non-parametric policy" framing may be an artifact of the setting rather than a property of the method.
- What fraction of the 54.5% token reduction is harness quality vs. merely avoiding 8-step budget exhaustions? A matched-compute comparison at $W_0$ with a higher step budget would separate content gains from efficiency accounting.
- Can the checklist be derived incrementally as constraints appear mid-trajectory, rather than fixed at Turn 0? That is the obvious extension and the most likely failure point for long-horizon tasks.
