> **一句话总结：** 论文指出"工具返回的即证据"这一默认假设在医疗智能体中是错的：冻结的推理模型无法忽略已进入上下文的错误工具输出（作者称之为 **evidence hijacking**，眼底筛查中把 400 例里 178 例原本答对的病例翻成错误，占 44.5%），并用 V-information 证明只要把"哪些输出算证据"的决定挪到推理器之外就能恢复 optional ignorance，据此提出零训练的 CIEG（可核验 tool card + 只看输入与卡片的独立 Checker + 只读准入卡片的 Solver），在 REFUGE2 眼底 bAcc 0.730 vs 最佳基线 MedAgents 0.650、Kermany 胸片 0.837 vs admit-all 0.821、MedQA 82.72% vs 78.00%（MedAgents/MDAgents）上取得同级最高分，同时 token 与延迟显著更低。

> **TL;DR:** Tool outputs are not evidence by default. A frozen reasoner cannot ignore what enters its context, so a wrong tool result can carry *negative* usable information — admitting all three glaucoma segmentation cards flips 178/400 (44.5%) originally-correct cases to wrong and drops balanced accuracy from 0.83 to 0.61 with just one erroneous card. CIEG fixes this without any training by writing each tool output as a verifiable "card" and having a context-independent Checker (Qwen3-VL-8B, same weights as the Solver but never seeing its context) admit or reject cards by fidelity to the raw input and cross-card consistency. On REFUGE2 glaucoma it reaches bAcc 0.730 vs 0.650 for MedAgents (+0.080) and 0.627 for admit-all (+0.103); on MedQA 82.72% vs 78.00% for MedAgents/MDAgents (+4.72 pts); on 431 deduplicated Kermany chest X-rays 0.837 vs 0.821 admit-all (+0.016, and only +0.015 over Hulu-Med-14B which trained on chest X-ray data). It is 19/20 significant on per-dataset comparisons against same-backbone agents and uses ~1/6.5 of MDAgents' tokens on glaucoma (25.3k vs 164.6k) at 5.5 s vs 120.4 s latency — but the accuracy margin over the trivial "admit all" gate is thin on two of three tasks (0.55 pts macro on QA), so most of the practical gain is concentrated in glaucoma, where the tools are wrong most often.

## 核心贡献

- **现象（evidence hijacking，并给出量化）**：医疗工具会"静默失败"——错误的分割轮廓和幻觉报告在格式与置信口吻上与正确输出无法区分；而冻结的推理器没有 optional ignorance，一旦错误输出进入上下文就会覆盖原本正确的判断。眼底筛查中把三张分割卡片全部交给 Solver，400 例里有 178 例（44.5%）从正确被翻成错误；按每例错误卡片数分层（0/1/2/3 张，n=29/61/263/47）时，admit-all 从 0.83 掉到 0.61（1 张错卡）再掉到 0.5 以下，而 CIEG 始终贴近"不给卡片"的水平（差距无趋势，p=0.29）。
- **理论（V-information 版的证据治理）**：用 pointwise usable information `v_x(A) = log q(y|x,A) − log q(y|x)` 说明 Shannon 信息解释不了这个现象（掩膜是图像的决定函数，按数据处理不等式不携带额外标签信息），能解释的是"给定预测族能用多少"。治理把 optional ignorance 移到推理器外部，研究族变成 `V_G = {q(·|x,C_g)}`，自动包含"全拒"和"全收"两个平凡门，因此最优门的可用信息不劣于二者。
- **可检验的分解（budget vs selection）**：把门相对 admit-all 的增益拆成 `T(g) = B(g) + S(g)`，其中 `B` 来自"看得更少"（预算匹配随机门 π(g)），`S` 来自"看得更准"。任何内容无关的门（随机丢卡、截断上下文、按自身置信度决定是否加证据）在随机顺序卡片池上有 S=0，因此证据过滤器的正确参照是预算匹配随机门，而不是 admit-all。命题 1 进一步给出 `S(g) ≈ E[K·Cov_c(a_c, v_x(c))]`：只看准入决策与单卡可用信息的协方差，可在跑门之前预测增益。
- **方法（CIEG，零训练、插件式）**：acquisition → admission → answering 三段式。每个工具 agent 把结果写成可核验的 **tool card**（结论 + 结论所依据的材料：叠在原图上的轮廓、生成的报告、逐字引用的段落）；**Checker** 只读输入 x 与卡片池 C，从"保真度（材料是否支持结论）"和"一致性（卡片互证或互斥）"两条线裁定准入子集，不看 Solver 的假设/答案，也不看标签；**Solver** 只用输入 + 准入卡片作答，8 采样多数投票。Checker 与 Solver 均为冻结的 Qwen3-VL-8B，全流程无任何训练。
- **结果（三个模态、同 backbone 全面领先）**：REFUGE2 眼底 bAcc 0.730、Kermany 胸片 0.837、MedQA 82.72%、MedMCQA 70.67%、NEJM 65.19%，在 20 个逐数据集比较中有 19 个显著优于同 backbone 的 ReAct / MedAgents / MDAgents / MAM；同时成本更低（眼底 25.3k token / 5.5 s vs MDAgents 164.6k / 120.4 s）。Checker 在眼底的卡片级 AUROC 为 0.93 / 0.85（按类），准入 87%/76% 的正确卡片但只准入 19%/22% 的错误卡片。

## Key Contributions

- **Names and measures a failure mode that the field patches case by case.** Evidence hijacking is distinguished sharply from "information overload": overload is seeing too much, hijacking is seeing the wrong thing. The stratification experiment holds context length fixed (always exactly three cards) and varies only the fraction that are wrong — a clean experimental separation that prior work on long-context harm cannot make.
- **A theory that yields an actionable control, not just an explanation.** The V-information framing predicts (i) that governance cannot be worse than the better trivial gate, (ii) that filter gains split into a budget and a selection term, and (iii) that only content-aware gates can obtain the selection term. The paper then *tests* the prediction (Spearman ρ = 0.86 over 483 gates on glaucoma, 0.75 over 335 gates on chest X-ray, 97% sign agreement on chest X-ray) rather than asserting it.
- **A method that is deployable as stated.** No training, no weight updates, frozen backbone, prompt-only Checker. The tool-card abstraction is what makes one admission procedure work across masks, reports, and retrieved passages — a genuinely useful engineering idea for heterogeneous tool pools.
- **Honest cost/accuracy reporting.** Table 2 gives per-case tokens and median latency for all four agent systems across the three tasks, which is what makes the "more accurate *and* cheaper" claim checkable rather than rhetorical.
- **Ablation that isolates "where to judge".** ReAct (judge inside the answering context) is 0.544 on glaucoma — 0.092 *below* giving no cards at all (0.636) — while letting the Solver call tools and read raw outputs gets 0.606/0.814/69.11, still below CIEG. This is the strongest single piece of evidence that the location of judgment, not its existence, is what matters.

## Methodology

**Task setup.** Case = input x (medical image, or clinical vignette + question) and label y. A frozen reasoner q answers from an evidence set A; the task is deciding which cards may enter A. Three tasks cover three tool-output types: masks (fundus glaucoma, REFUGE2 validation, 400 images), reports + findings + similar-case retrieval (Kermany pediatric chest X-ray official test set, deduplicated by patient to 431 images, bAcc), and retrieved passages (MedQA 1,273 / MedMCQA 4,183 / NEJM 655, accuracy). Imaging reports balanced accuracy; QA reports per-dataset accuracy and an equally weighted macro-average in secondary tables.

**Tool pools.** Glaucoma: BiomedParse, MedSAM, and SAM each segment optic disc and cup; each card shows the contour overlaid on the original image plus the derived vertical cup-to-disc ratio (vCDR). Chest X-ray: ChEX, MAIRA-2, CURE, plus similar-case retrieval keyed on a RAD-DINO embedding. QA: a tool agent writes retrieval queries, BM25 recalls from MedCorp (PubMed, StatPearls, textbooks, Wikipedia), MedCPT reranks, and the top 6 passages per query become candidate cards, each quoting its passage verbatim.

**Checker and Solver.** The Checker receives (x, C) and returns a subset; it examines fidelity (does the displayed material support the stated finding) and consistency (do cards corroborate or contradict), but the ruling rests on fidelity because "tools can also agree in error". It never sees the Solver's tentative answer, so it cannot favor cards that agree with a hypothesis. The Solver is the same frozen Qwen3-VL-8B conditioned on x plus admitted cards, with 8-sample majority vote; the same 8-sample vote is applied to every admission policy and every general/medical MLLM baseline, while agent systems and RAG variants run their original pipelines.

**Theory.** Pointwise usable information and its expectation over cases and gate randomness; governance family V_G containing g0 (admit nothing) and g1 (admit everything); budget-matched random gate π(g) that draws |C_g| cards uniformly; decomposition T = B + S; content-agnostic gates provably have S = 0 when the pool order is random; Proposition 1's covariance form, exact when a gate admits at most one or all cards per case and otherwise differing only by inter-card interaction terms. On imaging tasks all card subsets are enumerated to compute I(π(g)) exactly; on QA it is estimated by budget-matched random sampling with a single Solver sample.

**Statistics.** All comparisons paired, stratified bootstrap with 5,000 resamples, stratified by class on imaging and by dataset on the QA macro-average; per-dataset results tested within dataset.

## Results & Highlights

- **Glaucoma (REFUGE2, bAcc, 400 images):** CIEG 0.730 vs 0.650 for MedAgents (+0.080), 0.636 for the Qwen3-VL-8B backbone with no cards (+0.094), and 0.627 for admit-all (+0.103). Budget-matched random admission reaches only 0.637, so the +0.093 over the random gate is pure selection, not context trimming.
- **Chest X-ray (Kermany, bAcc, 431 deduplicated images):** CIEG 0.837 vs 0.821 admit-all (+0.016), 0.803 budget-matched random (+0.034), 0.822 Lingshu-7B (+0.015) and 0.798 Lingshu / 0.759 CheXagent — but both Lingshu-7B and Hulu-Med-14B (0.822) are flagged as having chest X-ray training data that "may overlap in source with the test set", so the honest reading is that CIEG ties rather than clearly beats the strongest imaging baselines here.
- **Medical QA (accuracy):** MedQA 82.72% vs 78.00% for both MedAgents and MDAgents and 77.45% for ReAct (+4.72 / +4.27 pts); MedMCQA 70.67% vs 68.75% for RAG and 68.73% for MAM (+1.92 / +1.94); NEJM 65.19% vs 61.98% for MAM and 61.68% for RAG (+3.21 / +3.51). The QA macro-average is 72.86% vs 72.31% for admit-all (+0.55) and 65.02% for no cards (+7.84).
- **Significance and cost:** 19 of 20 per-dataset comparisons against same-backbone agents are significant at p < 0.05, with relative gains up to 34.2% over ReAct (glaucoma 0.730 vs 0.544) and 16.4% over MAM (0.730 vs 0.627). Per-case tokens: glaucoma 25.3k vs 164.6k (MDAgents) and 55.9k (MedAgents); chest X-ray 33.3k vs 82.8k (MedAgents); QA 34.6k vs 78.3k (MDAgents). Median end-to-end latency on a 50-case subset: 5.5 s / 8.0 s / 39.0 s vs 120.4 s / 50.2 s / 155.1 s, i.e. 2.5–22× faster on imaging.
- **Hijacking growth (glaucoma, 3 cards/case):** admit-all falls 0.83 → 0.61 with one erroneous card and below 0.5 with two or three; CIEG's margin over admit-all widens from +0.14 to +0.36, i.e. +0.086 per erroneous card (p = 0.022, robust to controlling for class), while CIEG stays within 0.07 of no-cards at every error level with no trend (p = 0.29) and exceeds both baselines when all cards are correct.
- **Budget/selection decomposition (nats):** the selection term S is significantly positive on all three tasks; the budget term B is ≈0 on glaucoma (where almost all selection gain becomes total gain) but negative on chest X-ray and QA (most cards are useful, so seeing less costs almost as much as selection gains — which is why accuracy there sits close to admit-all). Theory check: first-order term vs measured S correlates at ρ = 0.86 (483 gates, glaucoma) and ρ = 0.75 (335 gates, chest X-ray), with 97% sign agreement on chest X-ray.
- **Checker quality (glaucoma, card-level):** AUROC 0.93 / 0.85 with input+cards vs 0.87 / 0.81 with cards only; admission rate of correct vs erroneous cards is 87%/76% vs 19%/22%; corresponding selection term +0.152 vs +0.105, so comparing against the original image is worth a real but not overwhelming amount.
- **Ablation of judgment location:** ReAct 0.544 / 0.754 / 67.29 (macro for QA) is below no-cards on glaucoma by 0.092; Solver-calls-tools-itself 0.606 / 0.814 / 69.11; CIEG 0.730 / 0.837 / 72.86. The references the paper says filters should use — "rejected only" — collapses to 0.579 on chest X-ray, 0.096 *below* no cards.

## Authors & Credibility

Runxun Zhang and Jingwei Wei (Institute of Automation, Chinese Academy of Sciences), Onofrio Antonio Catalano (Massachusetts General Hospital / Harvard-affiliated radiology), Jinqiao Wang (University of Chinese Academy of Sciences). The mix is plausible for medical-agent work: a CAS vision/ML group plus a practicing radiologist co-author, which is the right configuration for a claim about silent tool failure in clinical imaging. The paper is unusually explicit about LLM usage (Claude for English editing, experiment/plotting script debugging, and literature search; authors designed experiments and verified every number). Nothing here is a red flag by itself, but the work is a single-lab effort with no independent clinical validation, and there is no reader study, no hospital data, and no prospective evaluation — the clinical framing ("court of law", "admissibility") is metaphor plus benchmark numbers, not evidence of clinical safety.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The mechanism — an LLM verifier that filters retrieved/tool evidence before the answer model sees it — is close to CRAG and Self-RAG, and the paper says so, distinguishing itself on (a) heterogeneous card types rather than text passages, (b) judging *outside* the reasoner's context rather than inside it, and (c) a per-card rather than per-query ruling. The genuinely new parts are the theoretical framing (optional ignorance as the missing precondition for non-negative V-information, and the budget-matched random gate as the correct reference) and the demonstration that ReAct-style in-context judgment can be strictly *worse than using no tools* (0.544 vs 0.636 on glaucoma). That last result is the paper's most valuable single finding and it is not a framing claim.

**Rigor — where the numbers are strong.** The experimental design around hijacking is careful: context length is held fixed at three cards, so the effect cannot be attributed to overload; the error variable is externally defined (vCDR deviation > 0.10 from ground truth) rather than inferred from the model; the margin is regressed per erroneous card with a reported p-value; every admission policy gets the same 8-sample vote; the theory's first-order prediction is tested on 483 and 335 constructed gates rather than one. Paired stratified bootstrap with 5,000 resamples is the right test, and the paper reports the trivial-gate comparison per task instead of hiding the task where admit-all wins.

**Rigor — where it is thin.** (1) *The "independent" Checker is not independent in weights.* Checker and Solver are the same Qwen3-VL-8B. Context independence is real and is the paper's actual claim, but a same-model checker plausibly shares the Solver's blind spots — the specific failure mode where a hallucinated finding sounds exactly like a real one is a failure of the same pretrained prior. No experiment swaps in a different or stronger Checker (e.g. a 30B model, or a different family), and none measures Checker–Solver error correlation. This is the single most important missing ablation. (2) *The Checker's quality is measured on one task only.* Per-card ground truth exists only for glaucoma (vCDR), so AUROC 0.93/0.85 is a glaucoma-only number; on chest X-ray and QA the Checker is validated only indirectly through a positive selection term, which is a much weaker statement. (3) *Only one backbone.* Every result is Qwen3-VL-8B; a claim that governance "plugs into frozen backbones" is supported by construction, not by measurement. (4) *Eq. (7) validation is somewhat self-referential*: the 483/335 gates are built by sweeping thresholds over the Checker's own scores and injecting noise, so the correlation shows that the covariance formula tracks perturbations of one scoring function, not that it predicts an arbitrary gate family.

**Overclaiming.** The abstract's "significantly outperforms state-of-the-art tool-using agents and multi-agent systems ... at lower cost" is true, but the headline relative gains (34.2% over ReAct, 16.4% over MAM) are glaucoma-only numbers quoted without that qualifier on page 6, and glaucoma is also the task where the tools are least reliable — the one setting constructed to favor rejection. On the other two tasks the margin over the *trivial* baseline is 0.55 macro points on QA and 0.016 on chest X-ray, and Table 3's own decomposition says the budget term is *negative* there, i.e. the Checker's selection gain is partly cancelled by the cost of seeing fewer useful cards. "Highest accuracy among models of comparable scale" also needs a caveat on chest X-ray: the nearest competitor (Hulu-Med-14B, 0.822) is 1.75× larger and trained on overlapping chest X-ray data, so +0.015 is not a convincing win — arguably the honest claim there is parity at lower cost.

**Data quirks worth flagging.** The chest X-ray test set is 431 images after patient-level deduplication, so the bAcc confidence interval is wide (~±0.03 at n≈431 with two classes) and a 0.016 gap is well inside it; Table 1 marks Hulu-Med and Lingshu with ∗ for training overlap yet still reports them in the same column without a corrected comparison. Glaucoma hijacking cells are small (n = 29 cases with three erroneous cards, n = 47 with none), and the "no trend in the CIEG-vs-no-cards gap (p = 0.29)" result is a non-significant test on a small stratified sample — it supports "no detected trend", not "no trend". QA uses the MedQA/MedMCQA/NEJM closed-book multiple-choice format, where a rejection policy at worst returns to the model's prior; the cost/benefit of rejection in open-ended clinical reporting, where an omitted finding is itself an error, is untested and likely less favourable.

**Overall judgment.** A well-argued paper with one strong, cleanly isolated empirical finding (in-context judgment can be worse than no tools) and a theory that produces a genuinely useful evaluation norm (budget-matched random gate, not admit-all). The method is cheap and easy to adopt. The weakness is that its headline accuracy margins concentrate on exactly the task where rejecting tools is obviously right, and that the "independent" Checker shares weights with the Solver, leaving the central robustness question — what happens when Checker and Solver are wrong in the same way — unaddressed.

## Strengths

- Clean conceptual separation between *evidence hijacking* (wrong content) and *information overload* (too much content), backed by an experiment that holds context length fixed.
- A theory that is actually falsifiable and actually tested: the covariance prediction is checked on 818 gates across two tasks with reported rank correlations.
- Correct reference baseline for evidence filters (budget-matched random admission) — a methodological point that much of the RAG-filtering literature gets wrong by comparing only to no-retrieval.
- Zero training, frozen backbone, prompt-level integration; the tool-card format is a practical abstraction for pools mixing masks, boxes, reports, and passages.
- Cost reporting is real: tokens and latency per case for all agent systems on all tasks, plus the observation that the Checker reads cards once while the Solver's 8 samples parallelize.
- The ReAct ablation (0.544, *below* no-cards 0.636) is a useful and unpleasant result for the agentic-medicine literature.
- Explicit LLM-usage disclosure and verification statement; code-level honesty about what the authors checked.

## Limitations

- Checker and Solver share the same 8B weights; error correlation between them is neither measured nor ablated, so "independence" is contextual only.
- Card-level Checker quality (AUROC) is only measurable on glaucoma; chest X-ray and QA have no per-card ground truth, so rejection quality there is inferred.
- Single backbone, single seed per configuration; no variance across runs is reported (only bootstrap CIs over cases).
- Chest X-ray evaluation rests on 431 deduplicated images with competing baselines trained on overlapping sources; the win there is marginal.
- No open-ended generation task: every task is classification or multiple choice, where abstaining/rejecting is cheap. Clinical reporting, where a rejected finding becomes a missed diagnosis, is not evaluated.
- No evaluation of Checker failure modes: the paper reports that it rejects 13–24% of correct cards, but does not analyse which ones or whether that rate is acceptable clinically.
- The 34.2%/16.4% relative gains quoted in the text are glaucoma-specific but presented without that qualifier; the abstract's "significantly outperforms" is carried by QA and glaucoma, not by chest X-ray.
- THEORY: Proposition 1 is a first-order approximation whose exactness condition (≤1 or all cards admitted) holds on 71% of glaucoma cases but not generally; the interaction-term error is not bounded.

## Relevance

直接命中 **Agent & Pipeline Silent Failures**：论文正是研究工具调用/RAG 这类"模型与世界之间的软件层"如何静默地把错误内容注入上下文、并使模型原本正确的判断被翻转，且给出了在模型之外检测与修复（准入/拒绝）的机制——这正是该方向"silent alteration + detection and repair"的标准形态。其次与 **Inference Reliability** 的"有界推理器无法忽略已见输入"这一可靠性视角相关，也可作为医疗/工具型 agent 可靠性评估的一个可复用实验范式（固定上下文长度、对比预算匹配随机门）。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- What is the correlation between Checker and Solver errors when both are Qwen3-VL-8B? Swapping in a stronger or different-family Checker would show whether the reported gain depends on shared-weights agreement or on genuine fidelity checking.
- How does CIEG behave on open-ended report generation, where rejecting a card can itself cause a missed finding? The classification-only setting structurally favours rejection.
- Does the budget-matched random reference still favour CIEG when card order is not random (e.g. tools called in a fixed priority order, so content-agnostic gates could accidentally pick the most reliable slot)?
- Is the +0.016 chest X-ray margin over admit-all stable under patient-level bootstrap resampling of the 431 images, and under the 5,000-resample stratified bootstrap per class?
- Can the covariance prediction (Eq. 7) be validated on gates built from an *independent* scorer rather than from perturbations of the Checker's own scores?
- What is the cost of a false rejection in a deployed triage setting — the paper reports 19–22% of erroneous cards admitted, but not the downstream harm of the 13–24% of correct cards rejected.
