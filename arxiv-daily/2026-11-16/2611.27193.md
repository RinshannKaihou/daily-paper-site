> **一句话总结：** 针对 mHC 中"残差流混合矩阵停留在 Birkhoff 多胞形顶点附近（欠混合）"与"过度混合使各流趋同（过混合）"的两难，cmHC 用一行凸插值把每个置换基矩阵替换为 $P'_m=\lambda P_m+\frac{1-\lambda}{n-1}(1_n1_n^\top-P_m)$（无新增可学习参数、严格保持双随机性），并只在**前 K 层**施加中等收缩（λ=0.97）——在 OpenWebText 上 nanoGPT-S/M/L 验证损失分别比 mHC-lite 低 0.0073/0.0024/0.0045，平均损失排名 1.3（最佳），λ 与 K 消融显示强收缩（λ=0.70）和最深层收缩都更差；6B-MoE 上领先 mHC 0.002、领先 mHC-lite 0.005，300B-MoE 上领先 mHC 0.0157（仅 2k 步），Ascend 910C 融合算子把超连接前反向延迟从 2670.02 μs 降到 1474.59 μs（−44.8%）。

> **TL;DR:** cmHC is a zero-extra-parameter fix for hyper-connections: instead of letting the residual mixing matrix $H^{res}$ sit at a permutation vertex of the Birkhoff polytope (mHC-lite's "under-mixing" collapse), it convexly interpolates each permutation basis toward the uniform barycenter $J_n$ by $\lambda$, which provably forces every entry $\ge \min(\lambda,(1-\lambda)/(n-1))$, and then applies that contraction only to the first $K$ layers to avoid driving all streams to the same point. On OpenWebText with nanoGPT (45M/0.12B/0.36B, 10k iters, mean of 3 seeds) the best setting ($\lambda{=}0.97$, $K{=}2$) beats mHC-lite's validation loss by 0.0073/0.0024/0.0045 nats and achieves average loss rank 1.3 vs 4.2 ($\lambda$ on all layers), 6.0 (Residual $n{=}4$) and 9.7 (unconstrained HC); on FineWeb-Edu it is mixed — best for nanoGPT-M/L training loss but loses nanoGPT-S validation to mHC-lite by 0.0012 and loses nanoGPT-L to KromHC (3.1817 vs 3.1802). The 300B-MoE evidence is a 2k-step / 33.55B-token probe against mHC only. Fused Ascend kernels give 1.81×/1.45×/1.32× end-to-end speedup at $D$=2048/3584/7168. Worth reading for the over-mixing diagnosis and the shallow-contraction ablation; treat the nats-level deltas as suggestive, not conclusive.

## 核心贡献

- **揭示 mHC 的"混合两难"**：残差矩阵若贴近 Birkhoff 多胞形顶点，超连接退化为若干近似独立的流（欠混合）；若被推向多胞形重心 $J_n$，各流之间不再可区分（过混合）。作者进一步指出欠混合并不专属于单位矩阵顶点——只要初始化偏置 $b^{res}$ 偏向任一置换矩阵，训练后 $H^{res}$ 就收敛到那个顶点（Figure 2），即"仅是多流被置换，而非真正混合"。
- **提出 cmHC**：把每个置换基矩阵 $P_m$ 替换为平滑版本 $P'_m = \lambda P_m + \frac{1-\lambda}{n-1}(1_n1_n^\top - P_m)$，等价于 $H'^{res} = \lambda' H^{res} + (1-\lambda')J_n$（$\lambda'=\frac{n\lambda-1}{n-1}$）。基矩阵可预计算，**不增加任何可学习参数**，且严格保持双随机性（谱范数 ≤ 1）与逐元素正混合下界。
- **给出三条跨层理论界**：累积混合的 Dobrushin 系数上界 $\tau(\prod H'^{res}) \le \prod \lambda'_l$（定理 3.1）、流差异范数上界（定理 3.2）、以及不同置换系数梯度差的上界（定理 3.3），由此提出"几何收缩的蝴蝶效应"——即使 λ=0.97 这种数值极小的收缩，也会经由乘性累积显著改变深层的流差异与 $\alpha$ 分布。
- **浅层中等收缩的实用策略**：仅对前 $K$ 层施加同一 λ，深层保持近恒等映射；理论依据是 Corollary 3.1（第 k 层的界只依赖前 k 层的 $\lambda'_l$），因此浅层收缩的影响可以向后传播，深层收缩只影响末端。
- **工业级验证与落地**：从 GPT-2 级 nanoGPT 一直到 6B-MoE 与 300B-MoE（DeepSeek 式 MLA + 稀疏 MoE，$n{=}4$ 流），并在 Ascend 910C 上用 Ascend C 实现融合 Pre/Post 算子（cube 核做大投影、vector 核做 $n{=}4$ 小混合）。

## Key Contributions

- **Names and quantifies the mixing trade-off in mHC.** Two failure modes: under-mixing (residual matrices hug a permutation vertex, so streams are permuted rather than mixed) and over-mixing (they collapse toward the uniform barycenter $J_n$, so streams become indistinguishable). The paper is, to my knowledge, the first to treat over-mixing as a first-class risk rather than assuming more mixing is better.
- **Shows under-mixing is initialization-dependent, not identity-specific.** By re-initializing $b^{res}$ to favor a non-identity permutation, they show the layer-averaged $H^{res}$ tracks *that* vertex (Figure 2), with the favored $\alpha$ staying near 1 and the remaining 23 coefficients of $\mathcal{B}_4$ near 0. This is a sharper diagnosis than "mHC-lite stays near identity."
- **A one-line, parameter-free contraction with exactness preserved.** Theorem 2.1 proves $\mathcal{B}_n(\lambda)\subseteq\mathcal{B}_n$ and $\|H'^{res}\|_2\le 1$; Theorem 2.2 proves every entry is $\ge \min(\lambda,(1-\lambda)/(n-1))>0$, killing the vertex-degeneracy. $\lambda\to1$ recovers mHC-lite exactly, so it is a drop-in replacement, not a new architecture.
- **Three layer-wise bounds that jointly explain strength *and* depth dependence.** Banach fixed point forces repeated contraction to $J_n$; Dobrushin's ergodic coefficient gives the cumulative bound $\prod\lambda'_l$; mean-difference decomposition ($\Pi_n = I - J_n$) gives the stream-difference bound and a gradient-difference bound on $\alpha$ pairs — the last one is the non-obvious part, since replacing basis matrices "should not" affect $\alpha$'s learning at all.
- **A design rule derived from the theory (contract shallow layers only) plus the ablation that tests it.** Table 2 compares first-$K$ vs last-$m$ layers at fixed $\lambda{=}0.97$; shallow wins on average rank (1.3 vs 3.0/3.2/2.5) and deep contraction raises gradient norms in 8/9 cells.
- **Scale + systems.** 300B-MoE pretraining (48 blocks, 256 experts, top-6, hidden 7168) and hand-written Ascend C fused operators, with a full operator-level latency breakdown including the backward pass.

## Methodology

**Setup / notation.** Hyper-connections keep $n$ feature streams $x_l\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}$ and update $x_{l+1} = H^{res\top}_l x_l + H^{post}_l f(H^{pre}_l x_l;\Theta_l)$ with $H^{pre},H^{post}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times n}$ and $H^{res}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$. mHC constrains $H^{res}$ via Sinkhorn-Knopp (inexact after a finite number of iterations); mHC-lite instead sets $H^{res}_l=\sum_{m=1}^{n!}\alpha_{l,m}P_m$ with $\alpha_l=\mathrm{softmax}(a^{res}_l x'^{\top}_l W^{res}_l + b^{res}_l)$, which by Birkhoff–von Neumann is *exactly* doubly stochastic for any $\alpha$ on the simplex.

**The contraction.** Replace the basis, not the parameterization: $P'_m = \lambda P_m + \frac{1-\lambda}{n-1}(1_n1_n^\top - P_m)$ for $\lambda\in(1/n,1)$. Because $\sum_m\alpha_m=1$, the residual matrix becomes the affine map $c(H;\lambda)=\lambda' H + (1-\lambda')J_n$ with $\lambda'=(n\lambda-1)/(n-1)$. Consequences: $c$ is a contraction on $(\mathcal{B}_n,\|\cdot\|_F)$ with factor $\lambda'$; its unique fixed point is $J_n$; each matrix's distance to the barycenter is scaled by $\lambda'$; vertices become unreachable, so even a one-hot $\alpha$ yields a smoothed $P'_m$. The bases are precomputed once per layer and cached, so the kernel receives $P'$ rather than a scalar $\lambda$.

**Why over-mixing is a real risk (Sec. 3).** (i) *Cumulative mixing*: Dobrushin's coefficient $\tau(H)=1-\min_{i<j}\sum_k\min(h_{ik},h_{jk})$ is sub-multiplicative, and each layer contributes $\tau(H'^{res})\le\lambda'_l$, hence $\tau(\prod H'^{res})\le\prod\lambda'_l$; with all layers contracted the cumulative product converges to $J_n$ geometrically in depth. (ii) *Stream difference*: splitting $x = J_nx + \Pi_nx$ and linearizing the Transformer branch as $A_l$ (assuming $\Pi_nA_lJ_n\approx0$) gives $\|\tilde x_k\|_F\le\prod_{l=1}^{k-1}(\lambda'_l+\ell_l)\|\tilde x_1\|_F$, where $\ell_l$ is the Lipschitz constant of $\tilde A_l=\Pi_nA_l$ — earlier contractions multiplicatively suppress later differences. (iii) *Coefficient gradients*: $|(\nabla_{\alpha_{l,i}}-\nabla_{\alpha_{l,j}})\mathcal{L}|\le 2\lambda'_l\|\nabla_{\tilde x_{l+1}}\mathcal{L}\|_F\|\tilde x_l\|_F$, so contraction both shrinks the gradient received by layer $l$ (through all later $\lambda'_k$) and flattens the gradient spread over vertices, producing a smoother $\alpha$.

**Depth-aware schedule (the actual method used).** Apply one shared $\lambda$ to layers $l\le K-1$ and leave $P'_m=P_m$ (i.e. mHC-lite) afterwards. Corollary 3.1 shows the bound at layer $k$ depends only on $\lambda'_0..\lambda'_{k-1}$, so shallow contraction propagates while deep contraction only affects the tail; this dovetails with the usual "shallow = general features, deep = specialized" intuition.

**Experiments.** nanoGPT-S/M/L (12/24/48 blocks, ~45M/0.12B/0.36B params, 10k iters, 8×H100, $n{=}4$ streams, 3 seeds) on OpenWebText (~9.04B train tokens) and FineWeb-Edu (~9.90B); baselines: Residual $n{=}1$, Residual $n{=}4$ (independent streams, no mixing), unconstrained HC, mHC (Sinkhorn), mHC-lite, KromHC (Kronecker). 6B-MoE (28 blocks, 64 experts top-4, 71.5k steps, 149.95B tokens, λ=0.97, K=4) and 300B-MoE (48 blocks, 256 experts top-6, 2k steps, 33.55B tokens, λ=0.96, K=6, Muon, pipeline parallel 10), both DeepSeek-style MLA + sparse MoE on FineWeb-Edu.

**Systems.** Fused Pre (coefficient generation + stream aggregation + projection onto cached contracted bases) and Post (residual mixing + post expansion) in Ascend C, splitting dense projections onto cube cores and small-$n$ mixing onto vector cores, with architecture-aware ND/NZ layouts, optional L2 bypass, and backward state reuse (65 FP32 values/token, 260 bytes).

## Results & Highlights

- **Main GPT-2 comparison, OpenWebText (Table 1, mean of 3 seeds).** With $\lambda{=}0.97,K{=}2$, validation loss is 3.4686 / 3.2540 / 3.1904 at 12/24/48 layers vs mHC-lite's 3.4759 / 3.2564 / 3.1949 — deltas of **−0.0073 / −0.0024 / −0.0045 nats** — and it is the only method beating the "4 independent streams, no mixing" baseline at every scale (that baseline is 3.4742 / 3.2587 / 3.1909); average loss rank is 1.3, versus 4.2 for $\lambda{=}0.97$ on all layers, 6.0 for Residual $n{=}4$, 6.0 for mHC, 6.7 for mHC-lite and 9.7 for unconstrained HC.
- **FineWeb-Edu is mixed, and the paper says so.** cmHC ($K{=}2$) gets the best nanoGPT-L training loss (3.1834 vs mHC-lite 3.1884, KromHC 3.1836, mHC 3.1862) and the best nanoGPT-M (3.2465 at $K{=}10$), but on nanoGPT-S **mHC-lite wins validation by 0.0012** (3.4660 vs 3.4672), and on nanoGPT-L validation KromHC is lower (3.1802 vs 3.1817); average loss rank on FineWeb-Edu is 2.8 vs 4.0 for mHC and 4.3 for mHC-lite.
- **λ ablation isolates the over-mixing cost (Sec. 4.1).** At full-layer contraction, λ=0.97 beats λ=0.70 by 0.0043/0.0143 nats validation loss on OpenWebText and 0.0005/0.0125 on FineWeb-Edu as depth goes 12→48 — i.e. strong contraction's penalty grows ~3–25× with depth, exactly as the $\prod\lambda'_l$ bound predicts.
- **K ablation and shallow-vs-deep (Tables 1–2).** Average loss rank on OpenWebText improves monotonically as contraction is limited to fewer layers: $K{=}10$ 4.2, $K{=}5$ 3.2, $K{=}2$ 1.3. Deep contraction ($K{=}-m$, last $m$ layers) is worse: ranks 3.0/3.2/2.5 for $m{=}1/2/10$, and $m{=}10$ on nanoGPT-S blows up to validation 3.5184 with gradient norm 1.3792 vs 0.3774 for $K{=}2$.
- **The contraction actually does what the theory says (monitoring).** Dobrushin τ of the cumulative residual product falls from 0.6539 to 0.1656 when λ goes 1→0.97 at $L{=}24$, and from 0.6372 to 0.1173 at $L{=}48$; λ=0.70 pushes τ to 0.0231/0.0462, i.e. the cumulative matrix is numerically indistinguishable from $J_4$ (Fig. 3). Correspondingly, normalized entropy of $\alpha$ over the 24 vertices of $\mathcal{B}_4$ rises in most layers relative to mHC-lite (Fig. 10, e.g. layer-2 attention 0.697 vs 0.589, layer-0 MLP 0.655 vs 0.587) even though only the first 2 layers are contracted.
- **Scaling to MoE (Fig. 7).** At 6B (149.95B tokens), cmHC finishes with loss advantages of 0.002 over mHC and 0.005 over mHC-lite at step 71.5k with a persistently lower gradient norm. At 300B, cmHC is lower than mHC by 0.0157 at step 2k (33.55B tokens) with smaller/smoother gradient norms while mHC fluctuates more — a real but very early-training signal.
- **Ascend 910C fused operators (Table 3).** Full forward+backward pipeline latency drops from 2670.02→1474.59 μs at $D{=}2048$ (1.81×, **−44.8%**), 3923.59→2706.02 μs at 3584 (1.45×, −31.0%) and 8523.01→6472.56 μs at 7168 (1.32×, −24.1%). Pre-only speedups are 3.82×/2.51×/1.93× forward and 2.00×/1.55×/1.46× backward (Table 10), and against an unfused MindSpore composition the fused Pre reaches 4.85×/4.59×/4.25× forward and 4.90×/4.33×/3.16× backward (Table 12); the ~50% cost share of the identical Post operator (763.49 μs at $D{=}2048$) is what dilutes the end-to-end gain.
- **Seed replication (Table 6).** With seed 1338, cmHC still has the lowest mean train/val loss on nanoGPT-S/M + OpenWebText and nanoGPT-M + FineWeb-Edu, with gaps to the strongest baseline of 0.0017/0.0013, 0.0048/0.0046 and 0.0014/0.0014; the paper notes 5 of 6 gaps exceed cmHC's own standard deviation.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty — real but narrow.** The core operation is a single convex interpolation toward the barycenter, and the paper is upfront that it adds no parameters and no new construction ("our approach introduces NO additional parameters and specialized constructions at all"). What is genuinely new is (a) the *over-mixing* framing, which the cited prior work (Alimaskina et al. 2026 on stream collapse, Liu et al. 2026 on identity degradation) does not treat, and (b) the observation that under-mixing locks onto whatever permutation vertex the bias initialization favors, not just the identity. The theory is mostly repackaging — Theorem 2.1 is a two-line double-stochasticity check, Theorem 3.1 is a standard sub-multiplicativity argument for Dobrushin coefficients — but Theorem 3.3 (a small contraction flattens the *gradient spread* across $\alpha$ pairs, hence smooths $\alpha$ in layers that were never contracted) is a non-obvious and genuinely useful mechanistic claim.

**Rigor — the guarantees are thinner than the prose suggests.** Theorem 2.2 promises "strictly positive" mixing, but at the recommended $\lambda{=}0.97$ with $n{=}4$ the bound is $\min(0.97, 0.03/3)=0.01$ per entry — a nominal guarantee, not a meaningful expressivity floor. Likewise Theorem 3.1's cumulative bound is $\prod\lambda'_l = 0.96^{L}$, i.e. 0.14 at $L{=}48$: mathematically clean, practically loose. Theorem 3.2 additionally requires the local-linearization assumption $\Pi_nA_lJ_n\approx0$ and an unknown Lipschitz constant $\ell_l$ that is not measured anywhere; the footnote concedes the assumption only affects the over-mixing analysis, but that is precisely the analysis the design rule rests on. So "provable stream mixing bound" is true in the letter and weak in the substance.

**Effect sizes are small and sometimes inside the noise.** The headline OpenWebText gain at 48 layers over mHC-lite is 0.0045 nats with std 0.0016–0.0021; at 24 layers it is 0.0024 with mHC-lite's own std at 0.0034 — i.e. the ordering is within one standard deviation of the baseline in that cell. On FineWeb-Edu, cmHC loses nanoGPT-S validation to mHC-lite (3.4672 vs 3.4660) and loses nanoGPT-L validation to KromHC (3.1817 vs 3.1802). The paper reports these honestly, but the abstract's "demonstrate the effectiveness" is doing more work than the tables support. Crucially, **there is no downstream evaluation at all** — no zero-shot benchmarks, no reasoning tasks, nothing but validation perplexity on two web corpora. Whether 0.002–0.007 nats matters is therefore untested.

**The 300B experiment is a probe, not a validation.** 2,000 steps × 16.8M tokens = 33.55B tokens, which for a 300B model is ~0.11 tokens per parameter — roughly 1/150th of a Chinchilla-optimal budget, on a model with pipeline-parallel degree 10 (so 10 stages, i.e. at most ~30 layers per stage). At step 2k the loss is still in the steep part of the curve, the comparison is against mHC only (no mHC-lite, no KromHC), there are no seeds or error bars, and no downstream metric exists. Framing this as showing the benefit "extends ... to substantially larger 300B models" is an overclaim; the honest claim is "cmHC does not destabilize a 300B run over its first 33B tokens."

**Baselines include an easy target.** Unconstrained HC is reported at gradient norm 2.7226±2.2325 on FineWeb-Edu nanoGPT-M and 0.9607±0.6755 at nanoGPT-L — it is visibly diverging, so "cmHC is more stable than HC" costs nothing. The informative comparison is against mHC-lite and KromHC, where the margins are the small ones above.

**Missing ablations.** (i) No separation of attention-side vs MLP-side hyper-connections — each block has two, and only a joint $K$ is swept. (ii) No ablation of $n$ beyond one appendix figure at $n{=}6$ (Fig. 11, which does show cmHC still ahead: OpenWebText validation 3.4692 vs 3.4726 for mHC-lite, but with no std bars). (iii) No combination with KromHC's Kronecker or oHC's $SO(n)$ parameterizations, even though the method is advertised as a drop-in basis substitution — composability is the most obvious follow-up and is untested. (iv) No learning-rate/schedule sensitivity, although λ interacts directly with how fast $\alpha$ moves. (v) No wall-clock training cost: the extra $[T,24]\times[24,16]$ projection per layer is measured only as isolated Ascend operator latency, never as end-to-end tokens/sec.

**Two unlearned hyperparameters, tuned per scale.** "No new parameterization" is true for *learnable* parameters but the method adds $\lambda$ and $K$, and the chosen values drift with scale ($\lambda{=}0.97,K{=}2$ at 45M–360M; $\lambda{=}0.97,K{=}4$ at 6B; $\lambda{=}0.96,K{=}6$ at 300B) with no stated selection principle — $K$ appears to track depth (2 of 12, 4 of 28, 6 of 48 blocks) but the paper never says so or tests it. A learned or annealed $\lambda$ would have been the natural ablation.

**A conceptual tension with Peng et al. (2026).** The paper's over-mixing argument assumes that high cosine similarity between streams implies functional redundancy, while the related work it cites (Peng et al., "Ablate and rescue") argues high representational similarity does *not* imply functional interchangeability. The paper acknowledges the difference but does not resolve it: if near-identical streams can still play asymmetric roles, driving $\tau\to0$ may be less harmful than claimed, which would weaken the case for limiting contraction to shallow layers. Figure 1(c)'s cosine-similarity panel measures similarity, not functional redundancy, so the empirical support does not settle it either.

**Systems claims need context.** The 44.8% headline is the best cell ($D{=}2048$); at $D{=}7168$ it is 24.1%, and the identical Post operator is ~50% of the cost in both pipelines, so a real model's end-to-end gain is necessarily much smaller than 44.8%. Benchmarks use $B{=}1,S{=}4096,n{=}4$ with the Transformer sublayer excluded, on a single Ascend 910C with CANN 9.1.0 — no training-throughput measurement and no NVIDIA comparison, so the number is not portable.

## Strengths

- **Minimal, elegant, and genuinely drop-in.** Replacing the basis matrices is a one-line change that provably preserves double stochasticity, adds zero learnable parameters, and recovers mHC-lite exactly at $\lambda{=}1$ — so it can be tried in an existing mHC codebase with a config flag, which is rare for a theory-motivated architecture paper.
- **Identifies a failure mode the field was ignoring.** Everyone in this line of work has been pushing *harder* toward the polytope interior (orthogonal constraints, spectral spheres, transport polytopes, non-symmetric init). The demonstration that λ=0.70 is consistently worse than λ=0.97 as depth grows (0.0043→0.0143 nats on OpenWebText) is a useful corrective, and the shallow-only schedule follows from a clean corollary rather than from tuning folklore.
- **The "butterfly effect" analysis is the intellectual highlight.** Showing that an off-diagonal mass of just 0.01 per entry (λ=0.97, $n{=}4$) measurably reshapes $\alpha$ in layers that were never contracted — with entropy gains like 0.697 vs 0.589 at layer-2 attention — is a concrete, checkable mechanistic claim, not a hand-wave.
- **Ablations that can falsify the design.** Table 2's shallow-vs-deep comparison at fixed λ is exactly the experiment the theory predicts, and the $K{=}-10$ nanoGPT-S blow-up (val 3.5184, grad norm 1.3792) is reported rather than buried.
- **Uncommon honesty in reporting.** The paper states that mHC-lite beats cmHC on nanoGPT-S/FineWeb-Edu by 0.0013/0.0012, runs a second seed (1338) unprompted, and notes that only 5 of 6 gaps exceed its own standard deviation. That is better practice than most architecture papers.
- **Industrial evidence.** 300B-MoE pretraining plus hand-written Ascend C kernels with a full Pre/Post forward/backward breakdown (Tables 10–12) — including the admission that Post is ~50% of cost and dilutes the Pre speedup — is a credible deployment story that most academic hyper-connection papers cannot offer.

## Limitations

- **Only language-modeling perplexity.** Two web corpora (OpenWebText, FineWeb-Edu), no downstream benchmarks, no reasoning/long-context evaluation; the practical significance of 0.002–0.007 nats is unestablished.
- **Short nanoGPT runs.** 10k iterations at 45M–360M parameters; whether the ordering survives to 100k+ steps or at 1B+ dense scale is unknown, and the 6B run (149.95B tokens) is the only reasonably trained model.
- **The 300B result is an early-transient probe** (33.55B tokens, 2k steps, single run, mHC-only comparison, no error bars).
- **Theory is approximate for the parts that matter.** The over-mixing analysis relies on $\Pi_nA_lJ_n\approx0$ and an unmeasured Lipschitz constant $\ell_l$; the provable mixing floor (0.01 at λ=0.97, $n{=}4$) and the cumulative bound ($0.96^L$) are both weak in practice.
- **Two hand-tuned hyperparameters** ($\lambda$, $K$) with values that change across scales and no stated selection rule; no learned/scheduled alternative is explored.
- **Narrow stream count.** $n{=}4$ throughout, with only a single appendix figure at $n{=}6$; the algorithm's cost per layer grows as $O(n!)$ bases (24 at $n{=}4$), and the paper does not analyze where that stops being viable.
- **Fixed contraction depth, shared λ.** All contracted layers use the same λ and are chosen by index; there is no per-layer schedule and no interaction with normalization placement or the (disabled) MTP layer in the 6B config.
- **Systems results are single-platform.** Ascend 910C + CANN 9.1.0 only, $B{=}1$, sublayer excluded, no end-to-end throughput, no NVIDIA baseline; the fused kernels are not portable to GPU stacks without a rewrite.
- **No composability study** with KromHC/oHC/TEMPER parameterizations, despite the "basis substitution" framing making that the natural next step.

## Relevance

这篇直接落在 **LLM Training Stability & Observability**：它把超连接里的"流塌缩/欠混合"和"过混合导致梯度系数分布平滑化"当作可监测的训练不稳定模式来分析，并在 6B/300B-MoE 预训练上以梯度范数与损失差作为稳定性证据，同时给出 300B 级 Ascend NPU 融合算子的实测延迟——对关注大规模预训练稳定性信号与国产生态算子落地的读者有参考价值。

另外，其"用 Dobrushin 系数/流间余弦相似度/α 熵来刻画隐状态几何"的诊断手段也与 **LLM Hidden States & Interpretability** 中的表示几何分析有方法上的交集。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Do the 0.0024–0.0073 nat validation gaps at 45M–360M translate into any measurable downstream gain (HellaSwag, LAMBADA, ARC-Easy) at 1B+ scale and 100k+ steps, or do they wash out? Without this, the practical case is unproven.
2. Why is the optimal $K$ 2 for 12/24/48-layer nanoGPT but 4 for the 28-block 6B and 6 for the 48-block 300B? Is the right variable the depth *fraction*, the token budget, or the hidden width? A single scatter of best-$K$ vs depth (or vs tokens/param) would settle whether the rule is principled or fitted.
3. Can $\lambda$ (or $K$) be learned or scheduled — e.g. anneal $\lambda\to1$ over training, or make it per-layer — instead of grid-searched? The Banach analysis suggests a decaying schedule would get early mixing without late over-mixing.
4. Does cmHC compose with KromHC's Kronecker factorization or oHC's $SO(n)$ parameterization? If the contraction is truly a basis substitution, stacking it on KromHC should be free and would test the "no new construction" claim under stress.
5. Is high inter-stream cosine similarity actually harmful? Given Peng et al.'s finding that similar streams can remain functionally asymmetric, an "ablate one stream and measure loss change" experiment per stream would test whether over-mixing destroys *functional* redundancy or only representational redundancy.
6. What is the end-to-end training throughput cost of the extra $[T,24]\times[24,16]$ projection per hyper-connection layer on both Ascend and H100, including the backward state reuse (260 bytes/token)? The paper measures isolated operator latency but never tokens/sec for a full step.
7. How sensitive is the result to the residual-bias initialization that produces under-mixing in the first place (the $b^{res}$ diagonal-0 / off-diagonal-−8 scheme)? A non-symmetric init (as Alimaskina et al. suggest) plus cmHC might be additive or redundant.
8. Does the cumulative-contraction argument predict a depth beyond which *no* λ in $(1/n,1)$ helps at fixed $K$ — and can Fig. 3's τ measurements be used as an online diagnostic to trigger contraction changes mid-run?
