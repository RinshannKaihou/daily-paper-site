# CertiSum: Bitwise-Identical MXFP4 Training Across Platforms and Parallelism Layouts

> **一句话总结：** 论文的关键观察是「MXFP4 乘积在 FP32 里累加经常本来就是精确的」——在 FineWeb-Edu 上 99.2% 的 GEMM 输出在任意求和顺序下只需 ≤24 位有效位即可无舍入累加；CertiSum 在量化那一遍顺带收集行/块统计量做 O(1) 证书检验，只对未通过证书的输出走精确重算，从而在 200 步 Qwen3-8B-Base 训练中让 AMD MI350X 与 NVIDIA GB200、以及 1GPU→DP4→CP2→TP4→DP2 的弹性切换全部逐比特一致（非精确 MXFP4 对照组从第 0 步起偏离，最大 13.34%/20.10%），代价是相对非精确实现在大输入形状上增加 11.8–60.5% 延迟，同时仍比 BF16 快最多 1.24×、比确定性 BF16 快最多 1.70×。

> **TL;DR:** CertiSum turns MXFP4 GEMM exactness into a *runtime certificate problem*: MXFP4's E2M1 elements are tiny integers under a shared power-of-two block scale, so a row-statistics bound `min(L_a·M_b, M_a·L_b, ‖a‖·‖b‖)` computed for free during quantization can prove — in comparison count independent of reduction length K — that the native FP32-accumulating GEMM is exact for *every* summation order, letting the fast vendor kernel be trusted bit-for-bit. Measured on Qwen3-8B-Base/Qwen3-14B-Base/Llama-3.1-8B over 200 AdamW steps on FineWeb-Edu and The Stack v3, it reproduces losses, per-step tensor fingerprints, and final parameter/optimizer hashes exactly across AMD MI350X vs NVIDIA GB200 and across DP/TP/CP reconfigurations, where the non-exact MXFP4 twin's loss diverges at step 41 and reaches 4.2% (median cross-vendor drift for the vendor baseline: 0.75%/1.19%, max 13.34%/20.10%). It costs 11.8–60.5% latency over that twin at 4×4096 / 1×16384 shapes (15.4–77.3% at small shapes), but at those main shapes it is 0.81–1.00× the faster BF16 reference (≤1.24× speedup) and 0.59–0.89× deterministic BF16 (≤1.70×), repairing only 0.0069–0.2318% of nominal GEMM FLOPs.

## 核心贡献

- **把「硬件不变性」明确为 MXFP4 训练可复现性的必需条件，并指出前人方法的缺口**：run-to-run 不变性（定序）不能替代跨厂商不变性，因为厂商对低精度 GEMM 中间结果的舍入策略不同（Xie et al., 2025a），同样的求和顺序会得到不同比特。Table 1 的核心对照是：Megatron Core 只给 run-to-run（2% 开销），OPEN-1B 给跨硬件但需 5.2–6.8× BF16 运行时间，ExBLAS/ReproBLAS 给全部三种但 GEMM 慢至多 16×，FoldAttention 只覆盖 attention，AWE/ADP 只做 exact 而非 invariance。
- **核心机制：运行时"证书"（certificate）而非离线最坏情况预留**。MXFP4 的 E2M1 元素在每 32 元素共享的 2 的幂 block scale（E8M0）下可写成小整数 `x_k = λ_x a_k`，于是点积 `s = λ Σ a_k b_k`；只要 `Σ|a_k b_k| ≤ T < 2^24`，任意子集部分和都只需 ≤24 位有效位，FP32 累加即对**任何求和树**无舍入——这正是 Ozaki 型 error-free 条件，但 CertiSum 在**运行期**用当前操作数判定，而不是为最坏值域常备代价。
- **三级证书 + 一个 O(1) 的最便宜档**：行统计 `L_v = Σ|v_k|, M_v = max|v_k|, Q_v = Σ v_k²` 在量化那一遍顺手算出（权重在两次 optimizer update 之间缓存），于是单输出的行界 `min(L_a M_b, M_a L_b, √(Q_a Q_b))` 的检验代价**与归约长度 K 无关**；行界失败再上块界（32 宽，归约长度 K/32 的矩阵乘）、再上紧界（用符号清零、scale 不变的 native MXFP4 GEMM 算 `Σ|x_k y_k|`）。全部失败才修复。
- **布局不变的精确归约**：MX block 按元素在**全局张量**中的位置划分（而非设备分片内的位置），因此 TP 分片、DP/micro-batch 对 weight gradient 的贡献都是同一归约的互不相交部分和；合并用 exact 幂次对齐 + int32/FP64 容量检查下的无舍入加（式 3），整数加法的结合律/交换律保证结果与分片数、合并顺序、归约树无关。attention 的分子 `n_i = Σ w_ij v_ij` 与分母 `ℓ_i = Σ w_ij` 分别精确合并后再做一次 `RN32(n_i/ℓ_i)`——这是 split-KV 分解的精确版；pruning 用不依赖 key 划分的固定 per-row 参考阈值，因此剪枝本身也是布局不变的。
- **路由而不是"处处精确"**：attention 与几乎所有 weight-gradient GEMM 的证书极少通过（它们的 scale 跨度大），于是直接走精确 kernel；这部分占训练步 GEMM FLOPs 的 46–68%。实测路由结果（Table 6）是 native 32.16–54.23%、direct exact 45.76–67.84%、整数引擎 ≤0.0451%（含 padding）。
- **同时验证训练与推理的三种不变性**：跨厂商（MI350X vs GB200）、跨布局（1GPU / DP4 / CP2 / TP4 / DP2 弹性切换）、跨 batching（batch-4 与 TP4 推理），且不只看 loss 而看 per-step tensor fingerprint 与最终参数/optimizer hash。

## Key Contributions

- **Frames hardware invariance as the missing requirement for reproducible MXFP4 training.** The motivating measurement (Fig. 1) is that going from 2 to 4 GPUs changes 37.73% of Qwen3-8B-Base's greedy next-token predictions under MXFP4 versus 2.08% under BF16 — an 18× amplification — while CertiSum changes none.
- **A cheap, sound runtime certificate for native exactness.** Two reusable input statistics vectors per row (plus per-32-block variants) certify many outputs at a cost independent of the reduction length, exploiting the fact that block-scaled low-bit formats decode to small integers in a common power-of-two unit — a property shared with MXFP6 and NVFP4, not specific to MXFP4.
- **Partition-invariant exact merging**, including for attention numerator/denominator and weight-gradient partial sums, with global-position MX block assignment so that sharding never perturbs quantization.
- **Bitwise-identical 200-step training** of Qwen3-8B-Base across AMD MI350X and NVIDIA GB200 (1 GPU and TP4), and across 1GPU → DP4 → CP2 → TP4 → DP2 elastic resizing on MI350X, verified at the level of final parameter and optimizer-state hashes.
- **A cost profile that beats BF16 at production-scale shapes**: 11.8–60.5% over non-exact MXFP4, yet 0.81–1.00× the faster BF16 reference (up to 1.24× speedup) and 0.59–0.89× deterministic BF16 (up to 1.70×), with repairs confined to 0.0069–0.2318% of nominal GEMM FLOPs.

## Methodology

**Setting.** GEMM is written `ABᵀ` with `A ∈ R^{M×K}`, `B ∈ R^{N×K}` holding decoded MXFP4 values. For output `(i,j)`, `x = A_{i,:}`, `y = B_{j,:}`, exact dot product `s = Σ_k x_k y_k`, and the target is `c32 = RN32(s)` (or `RNbf16(c32)` for BF16 outputs), evaluated on the *quantized* operands. A native kernel is "natively exact at (i,j)" if its output equals that target for every order and grouping it may use. Three invariances are sought: run-to-run, parallelization (layout + micro-batch count), hardware (vendor).

**The certificate (Sec. 3.1).** Let `λ_x` be half the smallest MX scale among blocks of row `x` containing nonzeros (and similarly `λ_y`), so `x_k = λ_x a_k`, `y_k = λ_y b_k` with integer `a_k, b_k`. With `λ = λ_x λ_y`, the condition is `T ≡ Σ_k |a_k b_k| ≤ T̂ < 2^24`, i.e. every partial sum — including those formed before cancellation — fits in 24 significant bits of the common unit. "Exponent guards" on `λ` keep nonzero products and partial sums normal and finite in FP32. The authors state explicit kernel assumptions: native kernels initialize accumulators to zero, return exactly representable FP32 products and sums unchanged, and implement the prescribed final conversion without intermediate BF16 rounding.

**Cheap evaluation (Sec. 3.2).** Rather than computing the K products per output — which would itself be a reduction per output — they bound `T` by row statistics: `B_row = min(L_a M_b, M_a L_b, √(Q_a Q_b)) ≥ T`, where the three terms respectively bound every `|b_k|` by `M_b`, swap the roles of the rows, and apply Cauchy–Schwarz. Componentwise maxima of the statistics over row groups certify multiple outputs at once. When the row bound fails, a block bound restricts each of the three expressions to 32-element blocks and sums over blocks (each sum being a `K/32`-length matmul), and a tight bound evaluates `λ_T = Σ_k |x_k y_k|` via a native MXFP4 GEMM on sign-cleared operands with unchanged scales. Because all three are computed in rounded arithmetic (and the tight bound's certifying GEMM itself rounds), each bound carries a derived rounding allowance so that the verified `T̂ ≥ T` still holds (App. B.2).

**Repair and exact merging (Sec. 3.3).** Three repair routes: (i) chunk the reduction short enough to pass the native certificate, run each chunk natively, then merge chunk sums exactly; (ii) accumulate in FP64 subject to a capacity check that no partial sum can round; (iii) a multi-word integer superaccumulator fallback. Merging aligns units by exact power-of-two shifts and requires `Σ_r |Z_r| < 2^31` (int32) or `< 2^53` (FP64) so no addition rounds; integer addition's associativity and commutativity then make the result independent of partition size, merge order, and reduction tree.

**Layout invariance (Sec. 3.4).** Quantization blocks are assigned by global element position — including the token-dimension blocks used to quantize weight-gradient operands in MX recipes — so TP shards and DP/micro-batch contributions are partial sums over disjoint parts of one reduction, scaled by the same global loss normalization. For attention, scores use the certified/exact dot products, unnormalized weights and values are quantized in globally-fixed blocks, `n_i` and `ℓ_i` are merged exactly, and the quotient is rounded once: `o_i = RN32(n_i/ℓ_i)`. Backward contributions use the same rule. The paper also stresses that exact GEMMs and attention alone do not make a step invariant — batch order, initial state, masks, random choices, loss normalization, and out-of-class reductions (vocab-parallel log-sum-exp, global grad-norm clipping) must also be fixed or exactly merged, and an update commits only after every output satisfies its contract and every failed range/capacity check is resolved.

**Implementation (Sec. 4).** Per GEMM, a route selector picks native-with-certification vs direct exact, guided by shape and prior certificate outcomes. Activation quantization emits 4-bit elements, block scales, and certificate statistics in one pass; weight statistics are cached between optimizer updates. In-stream repair kernels read GPU-resident flags and return immediately when no repair is needed, avoiding a CPU-side routing decision. A static-route variant records GPU operations during calibration and replays them (used where eager routing overhead dominates).

**Evaluation setup.** AMD Instinct MI350X (288 GB, 1/2/4 GPUs) and NVIDIA GB200 (186 GB, 1/2/4); FineWeb-Edu (sample-10BT) and The Stack v3; Qwen3-8B-Base, Qwen3-14B-Base, Llama-3.1-8B; 200 AdamW steps at 1×4096 per step for invariance, 30-step timing runs (first 3 warm-up, steps 3–29 timed, slowest GPU per step, median over runs, outlier runs excluded by a 250 ms ∧ 25% rule). Baselines: **E** = CertiSum (AITER native path on MI350X, plus eager and static-route variants), **T** = MXFP4 twin (CertiSum's structure with non-exact MXFP4 GEMMs and non-exact attention kernels), **F** = BF16 (faster of their own implementation and torchtitan), **D** = deterministic BF16 (PyTorch deterministic mode; torchtitan uses SDPA attention).

## Results & Highlights

- **Cross-vendor bitwise identity, with a sharp contrast to the baseline.** 200 steps of Qwen3-8B-Base on FineWeb-Edu and The Stack v3 give losses identical at *all 200 steps* across AMD MI350X and NVIDIA GB200, at both 1 GPU and TP4, on both datasets; per-step tensor fingerprints and final parameter/optimizer-state hashes match too. The vendor-MXFP4 baseline differs from step 0 onward, with median cross-vendor loss differences of 0.75% (FineWeb-Edu) and 1.19% (The Stack v3), reaching 13.34% and 20.10%.
- **Elastic resizing is invisible to the trajectory.** Running 40-step segments through 1 GPU → DP4 → CP2 → TP4 → DP2 with checkpoint/resume at each boundary, CertiSum's loss bits match the uninterrupted one-GPU reference at all 200 steps on both datasets, and checkpointed parameters/optimizer states match bit for bit. The non-exact twin diverges at step 41 — immediately after the first layout change — and reaches 4.2% (FineWeb-Edu) and 3.0% (The Stack v3). Both methods' one-GPU controls reproduce the uninterrupted bits, so checkpointing alone is not the cause.
- **Certification succeeds far more often than the worst case demands.** On FineWeb-Edu, 99.2% of GEMM outputs need at most 24 significand bits for exact accumulation in any order; only 0.8% exceed 24 bits and 4×10⁻⁶% need more than 64. Operationally, the native route handles 88.60–98.81% of transformer linear-layer forward outputs, with recorded repairs on 0.0186–0.9710% of native forward candidates and repair work of 0.0069–0.2318% of nominal GEMM FLOPs. Qwen3-14B-Base TP4 is the worst case at 0.1945–0.2318%; both 8B models stay below 0.019%.
- **Overhead vs. the non-exact twin: 11.8–60.5% at main shapes.** Table 5's `E/T` ratios run 1.12–1.60 at 4×4096 and 1×16384 (e.g. Qwen3-8B 1 GPU 4×4096: 823.1 ms vs an implied 524 ms; Llama-3.1-8B TP4 1×16384: 495.7 ms, `E/T` = 1.12; Qwen3-14B-Base CP4: `E/T` = 1.60), widening to 15.4–77.3% at the smaller shapes.
- **But still faster than BF16 at those same main shapes.** `E/F` is 0.81–1.00 (up to 1.24× speedup) and `E/D` is 0.59–0.89 (up to 1.70×) at 4×4096 / 1×16384 — e.g. Qwen3-8B 1 GPU 4×4096: 823.1 ms vs 1112.5 ms deterministic BF16 (`E/D` = 0.74); Llama-3.1-8B 1×16384: 931.9 vs 1587.4 ms (`E/D` = 0.59). Qwen3-14B-Base TP4 essentially ties the faster BF16 reference (992.3 vs 994.6 ms).
- **The advantage reverses at small shapes.** All six smaller TP configurations take 1.05–1.36× the faster ordinary BF16 latency (1.03–1.34× deterministic), attributed to the fixed per-GEMM cost of certification, routing, and extra kernel launches; all six smaller CP configurations remain faster (0.74–0.92× and 0.70–0.88×).
- **Inference invariance holds where deterministic BF16 fails.** With 32 general-knowledge questions × 64 greedy tokens (2,048 FP32 log-probability vectors) on post-trained Qwen3-8B, CertiSum matches its own single-question one-GPU reference 32/32 in both batch-4-on-1-GPU and batch-4-TP4 and matches all 2,048 log-prob vectors bit for bit. The MXFP4 twin scores 31/32 and 0/32; deterministic BF16 scores 29/32 and 25/32.
- **Cost is concentrated in exactness, not in bookkeeping.** Moving FineWeb-Edu → The Stack v3 raises CertiSum's traced step time by 16–22% while the twin and BF16 change by <0.5%; within CertiSum, certification + repair + exact GEMMs account for 83–88% of the increase in summed compute-kernel duration.

## Critical Assessment

**Genuine novelty.** The framing is not "make it exact" but "certify that the fast thing was already exact, and pay only where it wasn't." That reframing is what turns a known Ozaki/AWE-style exactness condition into something with sub-BF16 cost, and the observation that MX block scales are powers of two so decoded elements are *integers* in a common unit is what makes the certificate evaluable from quantization-time statistics in O(1) per output.

**The strongest weakness is where the cross-vendor claim is actually tested.** In Sec. 5.2 the authors state that "TP4 divides each GEMM's output rows and the attention heads among four GPUs and **splits no reduction across GPUs**." So the headline "bitwise-identical across AMD and NVIDIA" is demonstrated only for configurations in which no cross-GPU reduction is split — i.e. the cross-vendor test never exercises the TP-reduction, DP, or CP paths that are the paper's actual intellectual contribution. Every reduction-splitting experiment (elastic training, Sec. 5.3) is MI350X-only. This is a real gap in the evidence, not a presentational quibble: a vendor kernel that rounds *its* reduction differently would only bite in exactly the untested configuration. Relatedly, hardware invariance is only claimed for two vendor stacks and a single CUDA/ROCm generation; Hopper, TPU, and Trainium are unaddressed.

**Baseline commensurability.** The latency ratios compare implementations, not kernels: the authors say so in App. C.3, and App. E reveals that "our implementations use pretrained weights and FineWeb-Edu; torchtitan uses random initialization, with FineWeb-Edu for Llama and a small C4 test corpus for Qwen." A 1.24× speedup over "BF16" that partly reflects a different corpus, different initialization, and different TP communication volume is a softer claim than the abstract's phrasing implies. The `E/T` overhead number is more defensible (same structure, only GEMM/attention exactness swapped), but it too is contaminated by clock and communication differences and, for the smaller CP cases, by the twin not being timed with graph replay.

**No model-quality result at all.** This is a 200-step, 1×4096-per-step study on 8B–14B dense models. There is no convergence or downstream-quality comparison between the CertiSum trajectory and the non-exact MXFP4 trajectory, no long-context training, no MoE. That is arguably fine for a determinism paper — the point is bitwise identity, not loss — but it means the paper cannot speak to whether the *invariant* trajectory is the *good* one, and the "training audits on different accelerators" motivation really needs runs orders of magnitude longer than 200 steps to be credible.

**Cost accounting is partial.** The paper concedes that recorded repair work "counts repair dot products, not all repair arithmetic or runtime overhead," and the route allocation in Table 6 sums nominal FLOPs rather than measuring time. The 83–88% figure for certification+repair+exact-GEMM's share of the Stack-v3 slowdown comes from instrumented traces that "include profiling overhead," which is exactly the regime where small kernel-level additions get inflated.

**The inference experiment is a toy.** 32 questions written by Claude Code, greedy decoding, 64 tokens, one model. It is a clean demonstration that batching perturbs summation order (the twin scores 0/32 under TP4, which is a striking result), but deterministic-BF16's 25/32 is the more interesting number and is left unexplored — presumably SDPA's block partitioning, but the paper does not diagnose it.

**Soundness caveats worth naming.** The exactness guarantee rests on kernel assumptions (zero-initialized accumulators, exactly representable FP32 products returned unchanged, no intermediate BF16 rounding in the final conversion) that must be verified per vendor per kernel version; the authors pin a specific Tensile library on MI350X, which suggests this is fragile in practice. Identical quantized codes across platforms are also assumed (App. C.1) rather than proven for both vendors' quantizers. And the pruning reference for attention weights is a fixed per-row threshold whose choice is not ablated against, e.g., a tighter reference that would prune more.

## Strengths

- The core insight is simple, correct, and load-bearing: block-scaled low-bit formats are integers under a power-of-two scale, so "is FP32 accumulation exact?" is a cheap runtime question with a sound sufficient condition, not an offline worst-case design decision.
- The certificate hierarchy is well engineered — row bound (free, from quantization), block bound, tight bound via sign-cleared native GEMM — and each level's rounding allowance is derived rather than assumed, so the certificate cannot become unsound because its own evaluation rounds.
- Exact merging is applied consistently to the awkward cases: weight-gradient partial sums across TP shards and micro-batches, and attention's numerator/denominator pair plus its pruning reference. These are where most "deterministic training" papers quietly give up.
- The evaluation is honest about failure modes and does not hide them: small-shape regressions (1.05–1.36× slower than BF16), the E/T overhead range, the "comparison between implementations" caveat, the out-of-memory configurations (Qwen3-14B-Base on one GPU), and the partial repair accounting are all in the text.
- Verifying at the level of parameter/optimizer hashes rather than loss curves is the right standard for a claim of bitwise identity, and the inference table (32/32 vs 0/32 for the twin under TP4) is a genuinely persuasive demonstration.
- The mechanism is format-generic (MXFP6, NVFP4, MXFP8 discussed in Sec. 6), which makes the contribution more durable than a single-format hack.

## Limitations

- Cross-vendor invariance is only shown where no reduction is split across GPUs; the reduction-splitting paths are validated on MI350X alone.
- Only two hardware platforms, one dense-model family size range (8B–14B), 200 training steps, and 4K/16K sequence lengths; no MoE, no long-context, no production-scale run.
- No model-quality or convergence comparison, so the paper cannot say whether bitwise invariance costs anything in final loss relative to the non-exact MXFP4 path.
- Overhead is 11.8–60.5% over non-exact MXFP4 and exceeds BF16 at small TP shapes, so the "faster than BF16" claim is shape-conditional and only holds at the larger evaluated shapes.
- Requires replacing a framework's GEMM, attention, gradient-collective operators, autograd definitions, and end-of-step commit; RL systems that train and sample in different frameworks must install CertiSum in both. The integration surface is large and no adoption or upstreaming is reported.
- Relies on per-kernel assumptions and on both vendors' quantizers producing identical codes; the pinned Tensile version hints that a library bump could silently break the certificate.
- No code or artifact release, and no independent reproduction of the certificate machinery.

## Relevance

这篇正中「Operator Precision, Determinism & Error Propagation」核心：把 FP32 累加是否精确变成可运行时判定的充分条件，并给出跨厂商 / 跨 DP-TP-CP 布局的逐比特一致训练证据，是低精度 GEMM 非结合性与 cross-kernel/cross-hardware 数值漂移这一主线目前最完整的系统级解法。对本项目尤其有用的是它的「先认证、失败才精确重算」范式——同样的思路可直接用于判定某个 kernel 或某个 reduction 是否可能引入 SDC 级别的数值偏移。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Why not run the cross-vendor test with an actual reduction split (TP2 with K-splitting, or DP2)? Is it a GB200 memory/kernel-availability constraint, or did they expect the certificate to fail there?
2. How sensitive is the certificate hit rate to the pruning threshold and to the loss-normalization scheme? Table 6 shows Qwen3-14B TP4 at 0.97% repair vs <0.02% for the 8B models — what drives that 50× difference, model width or layout?
3. What is the actual loss/quality cost of the exact path relative to the non-exact twin after, say, 20k steps, once the two trajectories have genuinely diverged (rather than at step 200 where the difference is 4.2%)?
4. How does the scheme behave when a vendor's MXFP4 GEMM internally accumulates in something narrower than the documented FP32 (the assumption the certificate depends on)? Is there a runtime self-check that would catch that — e.g. the sign-cleared tight-bound GEMM being used as a probe?
5. Could the certificate statistics be folded into the quantizer's existing pass at zero marginal cost for weights (they are cached) but what is the marginal cost for activations, where they are recomputed every step? The 11.8–60.5% range presumably mixes both.
6. Does the static-route variant (superscript s in Table 5) close the small-shape regression against BF16, and by how much? The paper reports it only as a footnote to the timing table.
