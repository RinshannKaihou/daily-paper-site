Key metric

10.3%

of 8.86M held-out prompts have at least one layer explained by a ≤128-neuron community that passes all three causal tests.

Flagship case: 6 of 86,016 MLP neurons — median KL 4.99 → 0.053 when ablated vs. retained.

Why this matters

A scale-tested, causally checked case that neuron-level units — not only SAE features — may be reusable computational components in LLMs.

Scope: Llama-3.1-8B, MLP neurons only, corpus text.

Critical edge

Not the same experiment: the 10.3% headline is set against a 2.91% shuffled baseline measured at 1M prompts, not 10M — and “explained” means one layer's contribution, not the prediction.

Methodology

Discover units first, ask what they do second

Only MLP neurons, final token position, no attention heads. Nothing in the pipeline is told what a “behavior” is.

01 Attribute a·∂log p / ∂h for every MLP neuron; top-5 per layer → ≤150 neurons per prompt

03 Cluster Leiden / CPM over γ ∈ {32…1024}; pools 6 resolutions; caps at 128 neurons
Accepted only if necessity KL ≥ 0.7 · layer necessity KL ≥ 0.7 · in-subset sufficiency KL ≤ 0.2
  • Data mix50% FineWeb-Edu · 25% Wikipedia · 25% FineMath, plus ~489k targeted grammar/code/QA/memorization prompts
  • ExcludedA fixed set of 17 “always on” neurons (attributed on >20% of prompts) is removed before partitioning and always retained during interventions
  • TestingEvery subspan of a community's ordered layer list, on deduplicated held-out prompts
  • Taxonomygpt-5.6-terra, temperature 0, 23 labels collapsed to 8 classes

Flagship case study

“an” before a vowel

Six neurons spread over L2–L14, drawn from 86,016 MLP neurons in those layers. Ablations on 8,000 independently sampled prompts ending in an.

The community generalizes far past its discovery set: 20,339 prompts pass necessity + sufficiency, and 99.7% of them end at the article token an — never a word fragment.

Members are substitutable, not uniform: removing any single neuron costs KL 0.06–0.07, but removing L14:11888 and L5:3846 together gives 0.65, and adding L12:14182 jumps to 2.20.

Strengths

  • Layer necessity is the right guard. Sufficiency in circuit work is routinely inflated because the retained set inherits the rest of the network; forcing the whole layer subset to matter is a protocol others should copy.
  • Real scale. 10M discovery + 8.86M evaluation prompts, not a few hundred hand-picked examples.
  • Clean controls. Independent 8,000-prompt sample, random-neuron ablation at zero, and all 64 subsets ablated to expose graded redundancy.
  • Auditable. Code, interactive explorer, full hyperparameters, and an external check against an independently derived arithmetic circuit.

Limitations

  • One model, one data mix. Llama-3.1-8B only — the “universal units” question is untouched.
  • MLP-only, final-token-only. Attention and cross-position mechanisms are structurally invisible.
  • Disjoint partitions. A neuron in three mechanisms is forced into one community; overlap damage is never quantified.
  • Lower bound, upper-bound rhetoric. 89.7% of prompts remain unexplained; “explained” means one layer, and no task-accuracy or steering test is run.

Open questions

What would change the verdict

Q1Sweep τN and τS jointly — is 10.3% a plateau or a point on a steep curve chosen after seeing results?

Q2How much coverage survives if the 17 “always on” neurons are not free during the sufficiency test?

Q3Does the same pipeline recover the same communities in Qwen or OLMo, and do the layer-depth profiles shift?

Q4Head-to-head against sparse-feature circuits on identical prompts and thresholds — does the neuron basis win, tie, or lose?
