> **一句话总结：** 作者用 227 对经 5 名盲评标注者验证的最小对立短语（道德 / 审慎·礼仪·审美·认识论 / 描述性）在 14 个 1B–14B 开源模型上做逐头激活替换，发现"能改变道德判断"的十颗注意力头对道德对与其它规范对的效应份额相同（合并比值 1.00，97.5% CI [0.92, 1.07]），它们属于通用评价回路而非道德专用机制；SAE 特征则主要是在检测句尾"morally"这个词。

> **TL;DR:** Across 14 open-weight models (Llama-3.2-1B/3B, Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2B, Qwen2.5-3B/7B/14B, base and instruct) and 227 human-validated minimal pairs, the ten heads whose output patching moves a moral answer carry the **same share** of the effect on moral and on other-normative pairs (pooled ratio 1.00, 97.5% CI [0.92, 1.07] with "morally"; 0.90, [0.84, 0.96] without), and mean-ablating them removes a median 37–39% of the acceptable–unacceptable separation on 400 ETHICS commonsense scenarios versus 1–3% for random ten-head sets — a general evaluation circuit, not moral-specific machinery, with only a few-percent moral residue carried by category information, a handful of neurons, and cue-dependent SAE features.

## 核心贡献

- **提出一套可判决"这个组件是不是道德的"的实验设计**：道德对与同形式、同长度、同模板、同编辑位置的非道德规范对（审慎、礼仪、审美、认识论）并列，并在同一框架内做"有 / 无 morally"两种呈现，从而把"道德内容""一般规范判断""线索词"三种读法区分开。
- **在 14 个模型上给出否定性结论**：被定位的十颗头在道德对与其它规范对上的效应份额相同（有线索 1.00 [0.92, 1.07]，无线索 0.90 [0.84, 0.96]），且模型在两类对上同样需要它们；作者据此把它们命名为"规范 / 评价回路"而不是"道德回路"。
- **指出并修正三个方法学陷阱**：原始效应存在"偏移"（几乎所有头在道德对上效应都更大，基座模型中位 1.5–2.3 倍，Qwen2.5-3B 达 16 倍），必须改用份额；控制对若换框架会把框架差异误算成道德内容；在与挑选相同的 pair 上测试会高估特异性，因此用 227 对新 pair 做留出检验，并加入"镜像挑选"（在规范对上挑头）作为选择偏差的标尺。
- **把"道德特异性"定位到很薄的一层**：类别信息（"这是道德 / 审慎 / 礼仪 / 审美 / 认识论问题"）可从隐状态读出（"is a matter of" 补全正确率 81–92%，随机 20%），移除承载它的方向使道德判断下降 2–11%；少数 Llama 模型的数百个神经元与线索下的 SAE 特征各贡献 5–10%。
- **SAE 特征的负面结果**：与道德 vs 中性对比定位出的特征在描述性句子上也能达到道德句子 70–99% 的激活，去掉 "morally" 后激活降到 1–33%，说明它们主要在读句尾的词而不是内容。
- **完整释放**：227 对 pair、逐条评分、代码、13 个自训练 SAE 字典与全部结果文件。

## Key Contributions

- A **testable design** for the question "is this component moral?": normative control pairs that ask for a judgment of the same grammatical and pragmatic form, shown in the same frame with and without the moral cue, validated by five blind human annotators rather than by a language model.
- A **negative result replicated in all 14 models**: the located heads carry an equal share of the effect for moral and for prudential/etiquette/aesthetic/epistemic contrasts, with and without the cue, and the models need them equally for both — hence "a general evaluation circuit", not a moral module.
- Three **methodological diagnostics** with general value for circuit work: the raw-effect *offset* (86–100% of heads have larger raw effects on moral pairs), the frame confound in control prompts, and the inflation from selecting and testing on the same pairs (addressed by held-out pairs plus a mirror-image selection).
- A **layered localisation of what is moral-specific**: category information readable from the hidden state (81–92% five-way accuracy on "is a matter of"), a Jacobian-lens direction whose removal costs the moral gap 2–11% but the normative gap about half as much or nothing, a few hundred neurons in four Llama models (5–9%), and cue-dependent SAE features (5–10%).
- **Release** of stimuli, ratings, code, trained dictionaries and result files.

## Methodology

**Stimuli.** 282 candidate minimal pairs of short phrases were written; 242 were rated by five fluent-English annotators who did not know the intended label, the grouping, or the hypothesis. A pre-registered retention rule (≥4/5 agree on the better version and on the standard class, median moral rating ≥2 for moral and ≤1 for the rest) kept **227 pairs**: 80 moral, 107 normative (37 prudential, 22 etiquette, 24 aesthetic, 24 epistemic), 40 descriptive. Agreement was Krippendorff α = 0.977 for the better version and 0.738 for the standard class (0.851 across the three coarse classes). Retained normative pairs scored 0.2 on the moral scale versus 2.8 for moral pairs.

**Frames.** Every pair appears in A1 (two fixed priming sentences + "[stem] is morally"), B (same priming + "[stem] is"), and A0 (bare "[stem] is morally", the construction of the authors' prior work). A1 and B are the primary frames; A0 is secondary. The priming exists because a bare "[stem] is" rarely elicits an evaluative continuation: with Llama-3.2-1B the evaluative mass is 2–16% unprimed versus 31–63% primed.

**Readout.** Score *S* = summed next-token probability of 14 positive answer words minus 14 negative ones; the behavioural gap of a pair is S(permissive) − S(restrictive); pairs enter an analysis only if the gap is ≥ 0.02.

**Component location.** For every head, its output is copied from the better into the worse version of each of the 73 original moral pairs (plus 32 descriptive controls), and the change in *S* is that head's effect. Because raw effects are inflated almost everywhere, effects are converted to **shares** (|effect| divided by the sum over all heads on that pair), and a head's selectivity is ρ = mean share on moral pairs / mean share on normative pairs. The ten heads with the largest moral-versus-control permutation statistic are the "located heads". Selectivity is pooled as exp θ (geometric mean over models), with 10,000-resample percentile bootstrap over items, per-model permutation tests with Benjamini–Hochberg correction, and a pre-registered equivalence band of 0.8–1.25 (a quarter more, or a fifth less).

**Triangulation.** Mean-ablation of the located heads at every position against 20 random ten-head sets; attention allocation and direct logit attribution; layerwise and edge-attribution patching (validated against exact patching); 14 TopK/JumpReLU SAE dictionaries over seven of the models; a Jacobian lens for the eight models ≤3B; 25-neuron held-out ablations; a steering direction built from normative pairs; 400 ETHICS commonsense scenarios; 58 descriptive-about-a-person pairs and 23 moral/prudential "twins" (same scenario and edit, only who bears the consequence differs), both labelled by three frontier LLMs that first passed a test against the human annotators; and a two-option choice prompt with no evaluative word in prompt or answer.

**Pre-specification.** Hypotheses H1–H6, endpoints, bands, answer words, inclusion threshold, sensitivity list, retention rule and analysis code were fixed before any model saw a new pair; the retained list was fixed before any model output was read.

## Results & Highlights

- **Primary equivalence test (227 held-out pairs, 14 models).** With the cue the pooled moral/normative share ratio is **1.00, 97.5% CI [0.92, 1.07]**; without it **0.90, [0.84, 0.96]**. Both intervals sit inside the pre-registered 0.8–1.25 "no content specificity" band, and no per-model interval reaches the 1.5 line for a strong moral preference. Per-model ρ spans 0.83–1.22 (A1) and 0.70–1.08 (B); Qwen2.5-7B in frame B is the one clear exception at **0.70 [0.58, 0.85]**, i.e. a *normative* lean.
- **Necessity, against a random-head baseline.** Mean-ablating the located heads at every position removes a median **23% of the gap on moral pairs with the cue and 18% without**, versus **1–3% for 20 random ten-head sets**; on normative pairs it removes 28% and 21%, so the moral-minus-normative difference is negative or indistinguishable from zero in every model with the cue and in 12/14 without. The located heads beat every random set in 10/14 models in each frame.
- **External benchmark (400 ETHICS commonsense scenarios, 200 acceptable / 200 unacceptable).** Every model separates the classes (median AUC 0.90, 0.89, 0.93 across three prompts; worst 0.73). Removing the located heads removes a median **39%, 39%, 37%** of the acceptable–unacceptable separation, versus **1%, 2%, 3%** for random sets — over 10× the baseline — and the median AUC falls to 0.86, 0.84, 0.87. This holds for scenarios the authors did not write, but ETHICS has no normative control, so it establishes necessity, not specificity.
- **Cue-free and cue-swapped prompts.** On "[stem] is" alone the located heads carry **5.01× / 4.78×** the share of ten random heads on moral / normative pairs (descriptive 4.03×) and their removal costs **24% / 22%** of the gap versus 3% for random sets; with a plain "Is the following good or bad?" question the figures are 4.54× / 5.27× and **30% / 35%**; with "socially" in place of "morally", **37% / 35%** and 14/12 of 14 models beating every random set. The circuit is therefore not a reader of the word "morally".
- **The "fifth more" is person-versus-thing, not judgment-versus-no-judgment.** Moral/descriptive = **1.19 [1.11, 1.26]** and normative/descriptive = **1.19 [1.13, 1.26]** with the cue; on 58 descriptive pairs that contain a person but judge nobody's conduct, the located heads carry exactly the normative share without the cue (normative/person **1.00 [0.92, 1.09]**) and 1.22× their share on thing-descriptives ([1.11, 1.32]). So the extra share tracks whether the phrase is about a person, not whether a norm is applied.
- **SAE features follow the word, not the content.** The descriptive/moral activation ratio is **above 0.5 in all 14 dictionaries, from 0.70 [0.68, 0.72] (Llama-3.2-1B, layer 7) to 0.99** (the MLP-0 dictionary), and without the cue their activation on moral pairs is only **1–33%** of its cued value. Meanwhile the dictionaries see almost none of the pair difference: capture R² is **below zero in 13 of 14 dictionaries in frame A1** (from −1.54 to −0.02, best 0.38) and 0.10–0.49 in frame B.
- **A small, cue-dependent feature residue.** With the cue, **12 of 14 dictionaries** yield more features separating moral from normative pairs than separate any 2-vs-2 split among the four norm kinds (**7–60 vs at most 1–39** at equalised 46 pairs, 20 subsamples); without the cue only 3 do. Held-out removal of these features cuts the moral gap by **5–10%** in 4 of 10 usable dictionaries without the cue (Llama-3.2-1B-IT: −0.054 [−0.073, −0.036] on moral pairs versus −0.000 on normative) — but two of the 14 "positive" dictionaries are Qwen2.5-7B layer-0 dictionaries that detect essentially no features at all (0.2 vs 0.1 splits).
- **Category information is present everywhere, and feeds the verdict weakly.** Completing "[stem] is a matter of", every model names the annotators' standard **81–92%** of the time (median 88%, chance 20%). A Jacobian lens separates moral from normative pairs at **AUC 0.95–0.99**, and removing, reversing or exchanging the moral-word direction lowers the moral gap by **2–8%** (9–11% in Gemma-2B-IT, 19% when reversed) while the normative gap falls about half as much or not at all — specific in 7 of 8 lens-fitted models. The category is also readable from what the located heads write (83% five-way accuracy, the same as ten random heads).
- **One direction moves every judgment.** Adding the mean acceptable-minus-unacceptable difference from normative pairs raises the evaluation of both versions by **40–240%** of the pair's gap, for moral, normative and descriptive pairs alike, in all 14 models and both frames; moral-only and normative-only directions have cosine **0.72–0.94**.
- **Small head-level footprint.** The located heads put a median **28%** of their final-position attention on "is morally" (15% for the average head) and **19%** on "is" without the cue (8%); their direct logit contribution is only **2.2%** of the logit difference. The layer at which half the gap first appears moves from **0.53** of depth without the cue to **0.67** with it — i.e. the cue, not the content, sets the depth.
- **Instruction tuning preserves the pattern.** Base-versus-IT head-profile correlations on the new moral pairs are **0.88–0.96** in six of seven pairs (0.78 for Qwen2.5-7B) against split-half reliabilities of 0.94–0.99, though the base model's top head stays in the IT top ten in only 4 of 7 pairs.

## Authors & Credibility

Davide Liga and Matteo Magnini are at the University of Luxembourg; Liuwen Yu is at the Luxembourg Institute of Science and Technology (LIST). The paper is a direct follow-up to Liga & Yu's JURIX 2025 paper, which introduced the contrastive last-token construction (MLP neurons that nudge normative stance) that this work reuses and critiques. The group's background is legal knowledge representation and normative reasoning rather than mainstream mechanistic interpretability, which shows in the strengths (formal analysis plan, careful stimulus construction, philosophical framing of the moral/normative contrast) and in the weaknesses (no comparison against the published moral-component locators of Schacht & Lanquillon, Yu et al. 2026, Li et al. 2026, or Dasdan et al. 2026). Credibility signals are unusually strong for an interpretability paper: the analysis plan, bands and retention rule were fixed before model runs; 22/22 software self-tests pass and reproduce stored head effects at r = 0.978–0.997; synthetic planted ratios of 2.0 are recovered as 2.12 and 1.99 with a planted null recovered as 0.96/0.99; all code, ratings, dictionaries and result files are released; AI assistance (Claude 5.2 for code and drafting, three frontier models for the follow-up item labels) is disclosed in an appendix.

## Critical Assessment

The paper's headline is an **equivalence (null) result**, and its strength depends entirely on the equivalence margin. The band 0.8–1.25 is pre-registered and borrowed from FDA bioequivalence convention, but nothing validates 25% as "no meaningful difference" for head shares — and the margin does real work here: a 25% moral advantage would still be reported as "no content specificity". Per-model intervals lie wholly inside the band in only 8/14 models with the cue and 5/14 without, so "the same in every model" is carried by the pooled geometric mean for roughly half the sample. That pooled interval [0.92, 1.07] bootstraps over *items*, not over models, so it is a descriptive summary of these 14 fixed checkpoints rather than a population claim; the 1.07 upper bound says almost nothing about a 15th model. Qwen2.5-7B in frame B (0.70, [0.58, 0.85]) actually lands *below* the band, which the paper reports honestly but the "same organisation in every model" framing smooths over.

The single strongest piece of evidence — the ETHICS ablation, 37–39% of the separation removed against 1–3% for random ten-head sets — establishes **necessity, not specificity**, and the authors say so. But the abstract and title still let a reader hear "not moral-specific" from a benchmark that contains no normative control at all. The genuinely diagnostic comparison is the moral-versus-normative one, and there the effect is a null inside a self-chosen margin.

The SAE half of the paper is weaker than its framing. The authors' own capture diagnostic shows the dictionaries explain essentially none of the difference between the two versions of a pair — mean R² is *negative* in 13 of 14 dictionaries in frame A1 (down to −1.54) and at best 0.38. So "the located features respond mostly to the word 'morally'" is a statement about what a dictionary that cannot see the contrast nevertheless encodes; the finding is real but its scope is the dictionary, not the model, and the abstract's phrasing is more confident than that. The H6 result ("moral is a class of its own under the cue") has an alternative the authors cannot exclude and openly flag: "morally" fits moral pairs better in the models' own answers (median gap 1.00 for moral, 0.69 for normative, 0.37 for descriptive), and the two control cues meant to break the tie failed by the paper's pre-set rule ("logically" did not suit the epistemic pairs; "socially" made the etiquette comparison uninformative). Two of the 14 dictionaries counted as H6 successes are layer-0 Qwen dictionaries that detect ~0.2 features, so the 12/14 headline is closer to 10/12 usable.

Two further methodological soft spots. First, the ten located heads are selected with the raw-effect permutation statistic that the paper itself shows to be offset-driven; shares are used to re-rank and to test, but not to select, and the budget is only ten heads per model. Since mean-ablating all ten removes 23% of the gap and their direct logit contribution is 2.2%, these are upstream nodes of a pathway, not "the moral circuit" the title advertises — and the effect is not even uniformly positive: in Llama-3.2-1B-IT, final-position ablation *increases* the moral gap by 3% and ranks 18th of 20 random sets. Second, the decisive reinterpretation of H3 (person-versus-thing, not norm-versus-no-norm) rests on 58 pairs labelled by three LLMs rather than by the five human annotators, in a paper whose central methodological complaint is that LLM-derived labels are unreliable; the LLMs were first calibrated against the annotators, which helps, but this is the one control where the cheapest possible human check was skipped.

Scope limits are also substantial. The judgment is read from 28 evaluative next-token probabilities; the two-option choice prompt, which removes evaluative words from prompt and answer, supports the located-heads claim in only 6/7 of 14 models (and the control shows Gemma-2B and Qwen2.5-3B lose the whole gap under mean-ablation regardless of evaluation). The IT models are fed raw text without a chat template, so nothing here describes how an aligned assistant behaves in conversation. Items are short English phrases; the moral/normative contrast is confounded with topic and wording except in the 23 moral/prudential twins, and the twins show the neuron and feature residues following the *situation* (same harm, whoever bears it) more than the standard — a result that cuts against the paper's own "moral residue" reading and is reported with commendable candour. Finally, 14 models from three families that plausibly share pretraining corpora and RLHF conventions cannot distinguish "a property of language models" from "a property of this training recipe", a limit the authors state explicitly.

## Strengths

- A genuinely adversarial experimental design: the three readings are made to predict different patterns *before* the data, and the paper reports the pattern that kills its own most exciting interpretation (moral-specific heads).
- Held-out testing done properly: selection on 73 original pairs, evaluation on 227 new human-validated pairs, plus a mirror-image selection on normative pairs to calibrate how much of any apparent moral preference is pure selection bias.
- The offset diagnostic (raw effects 1.5–2.3× larger on moral pairs for 86–100% of heads) is a reusable warning for the whole activation-patching literature, and the paper shows what happens if you ignore it.
- Unusual statistical hygiene for this field: 10,000-resample bootstrap, per-model permutation tests with BH correction, pre-registered equivalence bands, 20 random head sets per comparison, and sensitivity analyses that keep the pooled ratio inside the band (0.95–1.12 with cue, 0.86–1.03 without).
- Breadth with consistency: 14 models, 1B–14B, three families, base and IT, plus an external benchmark and three alternative prompt formats — and the qualitative pattern really does hold in all of them.
- Software and data release are exemplary: self-tests, planted-effect recovery, edge-patching validation against exact patching (r = 0.997 in Llama-3.1-8B, honestly reported down to 0.55 in Gemma-2B-IT), and per-table result files.

## Limitations

- Judgment is operationalised as the probability of 28 evaluative words; free-form reasoning, explanations and multi-token verdicts are untested, and the choice-based test supports the conclusion in only half the models.
- Short English phrases only; IT models receive no chat template, so results describe sentence completion, not assistant behaviour.
- The main descriptive pairs are noun phrases about things, confounding content with syntactic form; the correction relies on 58 LLM-labelled person-descriptive pairs.
- The moral/normative contrast remains confounded with topic and wording except in 23 moral/prudential twins, and the twin analysis shows the residues tracking the situation rather than the standard.
- Human validation rests on five lay annotators; the follow-up stimuli were judged by three LLMs.
- SAE dictionaries cover 7 of 14 models at one to four layers each and capture little of the pair difference, so feature conclusions describe the dictionaries.
- The Jacobian lens was fitted only for models ≤3B; the hidden-state classifier substitutes for the larger ones.
- Ten heads are a core, not the circuit: 23% of the gap under mean ablation, 2.2% direct logit contribution, and head patching cannot say what a head computes.
- The equivalence margin 0.8–1.25 is a convention, not a validated threshold, and the pooled interval treats the 14 models as fixed rather than sampled.

## Relevance

这篇论文正落在"LLM 隐状态与可解释性"方向：它用逐头激活替换 + SAE + 雅可比透镜 + 神经元 + 引导方向五套工具交叉验证一个"概念回路"是否真实，而不是线索词检测器。更有价值的是它的方法学清单——原始效应偏移、在同批 pair 上挑选与测试的选择偏差、框架混淆、镜像挑选标尺——这套诊断可以直接搬到任何"某概念专用回路"的复核工作中。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Would the moral/normative null survive a selection criterion based on *shares* or on causal effect rather than on the raw-effect permutation statistic, and with a larger head budget (e.g. the top 1% of heads) instead of ten?
2. Can the person-versus-thing axis be isolated from normativity with items that vary only the presence of a beneficiary while holding syntax, template and length fixed?
3. Do the H6 moral-versus-norm feature counts survive in dictionaries that actually capture the pair difference (capture R² > 0.5), e.g. dictionaries trained on the contrast direction rather than on the residual stream?
4. Is the shared evaluation direction behaviourally sufficient — can it flip a free-form justification or a multi-token verdict, not just the probability of 28 evaluative words?
5. Does the picture change under chat-formatted instruction tuning, chain-of-thought, or harmlessness RLHF, where an explicit moral policy has been trained in?
6. If the located heads are upstream nodes, which later components (MLP blocks carry 73–74% of the direct logit difference) actually write the verdict, and are *those* standard-specific?
