> **一句话总结：** 本文把视觉 token 剪枝造成的 decoder 输出偏移精确分解为"图像摘要误差"（信息丢失）与"注意力分配误差"（视觉注意力份额下降）两项，据此提出免训练框架 CAR——用「显著度加权 + 空间核的覆盖选择」(WCS) 压低前者、用一个只依赖保留率的闭式 logit 偏置 `b=log(N/k)` 把注意力份额补回原值以压低后者；在 Qwen2.5-VL-7B 只保留 9.9% 视觉 token 时，CAR 在 RefCOCO/+/g 八个 split 上平均保住 77.9% 的未剪枝精度（66.8 vs 未剪枝 85.5），而最强基线 Nüwa 已崩到 22.8（26.5%）、随机采样 18.8，差距 44.0 个点。

> **TL;DR:** The paper asks why visual token pruning destroys visual grounding while VQA survives, and answers with an exact decomposition of the single-layer decoder output shift into an image-summary error (σ(v̄_S − v̄_img), from lost information) and an attention-allocation error ((σ′−σ)(v̄_S − v̄_txt), from the drop in the image's attention share). On Qwen2.5-VL-7B the image share falls to 51–64% of its unpruned value at every intermediate layer regardless of selection rule, so CAR (training-free) attacks both terms: a saliency-weighted, spatially-kerneled coverage selection with a label-free head probe, plus an additive logit bias log(N/k) on the retained tokens. At 9.9% retention on RefCOCO/+/g it scores 66.8 vs 22.8 for the best baseline Nüwa and 18.8 for random sampling (+44.0 pts, 77.9% vs 26.5% of unpruned), and at 128/1296 tokens on VQA it averages 79.3 vs 77.1 for HAWK (93.0% vs 90.6% retention). The striking control experiment: the bias alone turns average pooling from 16.1 to 49.1 on grounding, beating every published pruning method at that budget — attention share, not token choice, was the dominant missing piece.

## 核心贡献

- **给出剪枝偏移的闭式双项分解。** 把单层 attention 输出写成"图像摘要 + 文本摘要"的混合 `O = σ v̄_img + (1−σ) v̄_txt`，剪枝前后相减得 `O′−O = σ(v̄_S − v̄_img) + (σ′−σ)(v̄_S − v̄_txt)`，即信息项与注意力份额项，且三角不等式给出 `‖O′−O‖₂ ≤ 2‖E_sum‖₂ + 2‖E_alloc‖₂`。这让"为什么 VQA 掉得少、VG 掉得多"有了可测量的归因（Figure 3 逐项度量）。
- **诊断出被普遍忽略的第二类损失：视觉注意力份额塌陷。** 在未剪枝的 Qwen2.5-VL-7B 中，文本对图像的注意力份额在中间层（约 13–19 层）最高；剪枝后即便用随机采样，这些层的份额也只剩未剪枝值的 51–64%，因为 softmax 的视觉侧被删项而文本侧不变。现有方法几乎只优化"选哪些 token"，没有补偿份额。
- **提出免训练的 CAR 框架，两个组件分别对准两项误差。** 选择端（WCS）：把 Prop.1 的上界转成加权 facility-location 覆盖目标 `F(S)=Σ w_i max_{j∈S} s_ij`，显著度 `w_i` 来自一个免标签 head probe 选出的 ViT head，接地任务再乘一个尺度 `N/k` 的网格空间核；注意力端（AR）：对保留 token 加 logit 偏置，使 `Σ e^{z_j+b_j} = Z_V`，闭式解 `b=log(N/k)`（uniform）或 `b=min{log(1+|C_j|), log(N/k)}`（cap per-cell）。
- **偏置与选择器解耦，可直接外挂到已有方法。** uniform 偏置只依赖保留率、不改动任何被保留 token 的特征，因此能原封不动加到 VisionZip / DivPrune / AnchorPrune / EADP / FastV / SparseVLM / HAWK / VScan / Nüwa 上，在 9.9% 预算下把已发表方法最多抬高 24.1 个点、把最朴素的 average pooling 抬高 33.0 个点。
- **跨 4 个骨干、3 类任务的一致验证。** Qwen2.5-VL-7B、Qwen3-VL-8B（含 DeepStack）、InternVL3.5-8B（动态切块）、LLaVA-1.5-7B，覆盖 8 个 VQA benchmark、RefCOCO/+/g 八个 split、以及 Video-MME / LongVideoBench / MVBench；同时给出效率实测、选择规则消融（对比 10 种代表规则）、偏置形式消融、与最接近工作 RESTORE 的对照。

## Key Contributions

- **A closed-form decomposition of pruning-induced output shift.** Writing each decoder output as a two-component mixture of modality summaries, the shift splits exactly into `E_sum = σ(v̄_S − v̄_img)` and `E_alloc = (σ′−σ)(v̄_S − v̄_txt)`; the second term vanishes iff the image's attention share σ is restored. This reframes pruning as output-shift minimization rather than token-importance ranking.
- **Evidence that attention-share collapse is a first-order and previously ignored effect.** Pinning down the share at layers 13–19 to 51–64% of its unpruned value *even under random sampling* explains why decoder-side methods (FastV 3.2, SparseVLM 5.0, HAWK 7.6 at 9.9%) fall below random sampling (18.8) on grounding: text-attention ranking clusters tokens spatially, destroying the coordinates VG needs.
- **Two computable surrogates for the two error terms.** Proposition 1 bounds the summary error by attention-weighted coverage `F_v(S) = Σ a_i max_{j∈S} cos(v_i,v_j)`; since decoder attention `a_i` is unavailable, CAR maximizes an encoder-side weighted facility-location objective with greedy submodular maximization (1−1/e guarantee), plus a spatial kernel for grounding and a label-free probe that picks the ViT head (and layer, on attention-sink backbones) whose saliency best reproduces unpruned outputs.
- **A closed-form logit bias with a principled task-dependent form.** Solving `Σ_{j∈S} e^{z_j} m_j = Z_V` in expectation over queries gives `b = log(N/k)`, which is retention-ratio-only, feature-preserving, and therefore transplantable to any selector; the capped per-cell bias `min{log(1+|C_j|), log(N/k)}` returns content mass per region and is preferred for VQA, while uniform bias is preferred for grounding because it preserves the spatial distribution of attention.
- **An honest, unusually informative control experiment.** With the same uniform bias applied to every method (Table 33), average pooling + bias reaches 49.1 at 9.9% retention — above DivPrune (44.4), the best published method with bias — showing that much of the prior selection-design effort was addressing the smaller of the two errors; CAR still leads that biased pooling by 17.7 points, so selection matters too, but only after the share is fixed.

## Methodology

**Setup and notation.** Pruning retains a subset `S ⊂ V` of `k` visual tokens out of `N` and leaves text tokens intact; an additive logit bias `b_u` is allowed on the retained visual tokens only (`b_t = 0` for text). Objective: `min E‖O′ − O‖₂` over `S` and `b`, expectation over queries and images. Analysis is done per attention head of one decoder layer at a text query, with `z_u = qᵀk_u/√d_h`.

**Decomposition (Eq. 4–5).** Grouping the attention sum by modality gives `O = σ v̄_img + (1−σ) v̄_txt` and `O′ = σ′ v̄_S + (1−σ′) v̄_txt`, where `σ = Σ_{u∈V} p_u`. Splitting `σ′ v̄_S = σ v̄_S + (σ′−σ) v̄_S` yields the exact two-term separation; the triangle inequality gives the optimized upper bound. Both terms are measured empirically in Figure 3 as `‖E‖₂/‖O‖₂` over layers 13–19.

**Coverage bound (Proposition 1).** With `a_i = e^{z_i}/Z_V` the image's attention share on token `i`, `ρ = Σ_{j∈S} a_j` the retained share, equal-norm value vectors, and each discarded token's attention carried by its most similar retained token: `‖v̄_S − v̄_img‖ ≤ √2 R √(1 − ρ√(1 − F_v(S)))` with `F_v(S) = Σ_i a_i max_{j∈S} cos(v_i,v_j)`. Maximizing coverage therefore tightens the bound; the paper notes `F_v` uses decoder quantities that are unobservable at selection time, so it optimizes an encoder-side surrogate.

**Weighted coverage selection (WCS).** `F(S) = Σ_{i=1}^N w_i max_{j∈S} s_ij` with `s_ij = [cos(x_i,x_j)]_+`, `x_i` the encoder feature and `w_i` the saliency (attention received inside the vision encoder). `F` is monotone submodular, so greedy selection adds at each step the token with the largest marginal weighted coverage `Σ_i w_i[s_ij − max_{r∈S} s_ir]_+`, giving `1−1/e`. For grounding, similarity is discounted by a spatial kernel `exp(−‖g_i−g_j‖²/(2(N/k)))`, whose scale equals the area each of the `k` representatives covers and thus widens as the budget tightens; VQA uses the kernel-free similarity. After selection, each discarded token is assigned to its most similar retained token, forming cells `C_j`.

**Head probe.** The best saliency source differs by backbone and task family (Figure 1e,f), so candidates — the 16 heads of the final encoder block plus their average, and on InternVL3.5-8B / LLaVA-1.5-7B also the encoder layer, since those backbones have attention sinks in late layers — are scored offline by `score(h) = (1/n) Σ_i sim(y_i^{(h)}, y_i^{full})` with exact match for VQA and box IoU for grounding; the winner `h*` is then fixed. Probe cost: 500 samples per benchmark (500 RefCOCO val images for grounding).

**Attention reallocation (AR).** Restoring the share requires `Σ_{j∈S} e^{z_j} m_j = Z_V` with `m_j = e^{b_j}`; this is one equation in `k` unknowns, and the choice among solutions matters because it changes `v̄_S`. Uniform solution: assuming exchangeability, `E[ρ] = k/N`, hence `m_j = N/k`, `b_j = log(N/k)` — retention-ratio-only, applied identically, and leaving `v̄_S` untouched (so `E_sum` is unaffected). Per-cell solution: give each retained token the mass of its cell, `m_j = 1 + |C_j|`, capped at `N/k` to limit over-restoration since `e^{z_i} < e^{z_j}` is the common case inside a cell. Grounding uses the uniform bias (which preserves the spatial distribution of attention), VQA the capped per-cell one (which restores content).

**Implementation notes.** Bias is injected through PSR into the attention kernel so FlashAttention-2 stays intact; for large `N` (11,520 tokens over 32 video frames, 6,144 for 24 InterVL tiles) gains are updated incrementally instead of rescanning the `N×N` similarity matrix, cutting video selection from 5.07 s to 0.61 s. Baselines are reproduced from released code and token budgets aligned by the mean number of visual tokens entering each decoder layer.

## Results & Highlights

- **Visual grounding at 9.9% retention (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, 8 RefCOCO/+/g splits):** CAR 66.8 average vs 22.8 for the strongest baseline Nüwa and 18.8 for random sampling — 77.9% of the unpruned 85.5 vs 26.5% for Nüwa, a +44.0-point absolute gap. Per-split, RefCOCO testA is 75.4 vs Nüwa's 27.5; two-stage/decoder-side methods collapse even below random (FastV 3.2, SparseVLM 5.0, HAWK 7.6).
- **Visual grounding at 25% retention:** CAR 79.4 vs Nüwa 70.8 (+8.6) and pooling 53.0, i.e. 92.7% vs 82.7% of unpruned — the gap narrows but does not close, so the advantage is largest exactly where pruning hurts most.
- **VQA at 128/1,296 tokens (−90.1%, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, 8 benchmarks):** normalized average 79.3 (93.0% retention) vs HAWK 77.1 (90.6%) and AnchorPrune 76.8 (90.2%); ChartQA 76.1 vs HAWK 71.3 (+4.8) and OCRBench 731 vs HAWK 705 (+26). CAR does *not* win everywhere: TextVQA 78.8 vs HAWK 79.9 (−1.1) and HallusionBench 49.0 vs Nüwa 49.2 (−0.2). At 256/1,296 tokens CAR reaches 82.9 (97.5%) vs HAWK 81.9 (96.2%).
- **Bias transplant is the paper's strongest single number.** Applied unchanged to existing selectors at 9.9% retention on grounding: average pooling 16.1→49.1 (+33.0), DivPrune 22.0→44.4 (+22.4), and published methods up to +24.1; at 25% pooling goes 53.0→68.4 (+15.4) and DivPrune 63.7→72.9 (+9.2). Biased pooling (49.1) beats every published pruning design without the bias (best: DivPrune 44.4). Against the closest prior work RESTORE, CAR's bias lifts WCS from 78.7→79.3 VQA and 26.8→66.8 VG, whereas RESTORE's merging drops WCS to 75.3 VQA and 14.3 VG.
- **Cross-backbone generalization:** Qwen3-VL-8B VQA at 11.1% retention 70.6 vs EADP 66.6; grounding at 9.9% 46.6 vs Nüwa 28.9 (+17.7). InternVL3.5-8B VQA at 11.1% 74.4 vs EADP 69.5; grounding 76.2 vs EADP 75.0 (a much thinner +1.2, since tiles plus thumbnail already preserve locality). LLaVA-1.5-7B grounding at 11.1% 30.4 vs Nüwa 28.2 — only +2.2, and 30.4 against an unpruned 51.3 means CAR retains just 59% there.
- **Video (Qwen3-VL-8B, 32 frames):** average 64.0 at 25% (97.9% retention) vs Nüwa 63.5 (97.0%), and 62.0 at 9.9% (94.7%) vs Nüwa 61.6 (94.2%) — a +0.5 margin at the aggressive ratio. MVBench regresses: 65.0 vs GSTEP 66.1 (−1.1), attributed to CLEVRER motion subtasks where near-identical object copies get pruned away.
- **Ablation isolates which component serves which error.** Grounding: random 18.8 → coverage 20.1 → +bias 50.0 (+29.9) → +saliency 54.8 → +head probe 62.5 → +spatial kernel 66.8 (+4.3). VQA: random 64.2 → coverage 72.2 (+8.0) → +bias 73.7 (+1.5) → +saliency 78.5 (+4.8) → +head probe 79.3 → +kernel 79.1 (−0.2, hence kernel only for grounding). Measured errors match: bias cuts allocation error 13.6→3.8 with summary error unchanged, selection cuts summary error 17.2→14.3; CAR has the lowest total output error (14.0 with bias vs 17.4–22.4 for existing methods) and the lowest output KL (0.150 vs 0.194–0.302).
- **Efficiency at 128/1,296 tokens (TextVQA, batch 1, RTX 3090):** prefill 484.0→246.6 ms (1.96×), end-to-end latency 543.3→304.7 ms (1.78×), FLOPs 10.28→3.81 T (2.70×), KV cache 73.2→9.4 MB (7.8×). CAR is, however, slower than FastV (222.6 ms) and HAWK (231.8 ms); its extra selection cost is 24.2 GFLOPs and the bias adds 5.0 ms (1.9% of latency), all in prefill.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The two-error decomposition is a genuine conceptual contribution rather than a repackaging: the observation that the softmax normalizes only over surviving tokens, so pruning silently reallocates attention away from the image, is simple, correct, and — judging by the fact that the bias alone beats DivPrune on grounding — was indeed being missed by the literature. The uniform bias `log(N/k)` is essentially ToMe's `log s` moved from merging to pruning, and the paper says so; the per-cell capped form and the closed-form derivation via `Σ e^{z_j}m_j = Z_V` are the new part. The coverage objective is a facility-location/`k`-center variant with a spatial kernel — standard submodular machinery, but appropriately chosen.

**Rigor.** The theory is honest about its own looseness: the optimized quantity is a triangle-inequality bound (`2‖E_sum‖ + 2‖E_alloc‖`), the summary bound needs equal-norm values and a "most similar retained token carries the attention" assumption, and the bias solves the share equation only in expectation over queries (`E[ρ] = k/N`), which is precisely wrong for the case that matters — the retained tokens are the salient ones, so their true `ρ` exceeds `k/N` and the bias over-restores. The paper flags this and bounds the resulting `v̄_S` perturbation (App. B.4–B.5), and Figures 3/C.16 show the empirical errors do move in the predicted directions. Still, the encoder-side `F` is only *correlated* with the decoder-side `F_v` (checked on 300 images, App. C.17) — the bridge from theory to algorithm is empirical, not proven.

**Reproducibility.** Strong on paper: released-code baselines, aligned budgets, per-backbone appendices, incremental-gain trick documented. Two soft spots: (i) the head probe is tuned on 500 samples per benchmark, and the grounding probe set is drawn from **RefCOCO val, which is also one of the eight reported evaluation splits** — the reported 66.8 is measured on a set whose val split informed `h*`, a mild but real tuning-on-test; (ii) the headline numbers use *task-specific* configurations (spatial kernel + uniform bias for VG; no kernel + capped per-cell bias for VQA). The unified configuration scores 78.0 vs 79.3 on VQA and 63.8 vs 66.8 on VG, so a single deployable setting costs 3.0 points on the flagship grounding result.

**Significance.** High for the subfield: the control experiment showing biased average pooling beats every published selector at 9.9% is the kind of result that should force the community to report attention-share-matched baselines. The absolute numbers at 9.9% retention (66.8 vs unpruned 85.5) also make clear that grounding under 10× pruning is far from solved, which is a more useful message than another +1-point SOTA row.

**Red flags / weak spots.** (a) The ablation baseline for the "bias is decisive" claim starts from *random* selection; on grounding, coverage selection without bias is 20.1 and with bias 50.0, so bias and selection are not cleanly separable at that budget — but Table 33's pooling+bias control does address this. (b) LLaVA-1.5-7B results are weak across the board (unpruned 51.3, CAR 30.4) and the +2.2 over Nüwa is within noise territory not quantified (no error bars anywhere in the paper). (c) MVBench regression (−1.1 vs GSTEP) is disclosed but the fix is hand-waved. (d) No variance/seed reporting despite many small-margin comparisons (InternVL VG +1.2, video +0.5). (e) The bias helps *every* method by a similar amount, which raises the question of whether the selection-side gains (CAR's +17.7 over biased pooling) would shrink if baselines were retuned at the same retention ratio — not tested.

## Strengths

- The problem diagnosis is right and useful: it explains a previously puzzling empirical pattern (VQA survives pruning, grounding collapses) with one mechanism, and the mechanism is verified layer-by-layer (51–64% share drop) and error-term-by-error-term (Fig. 3).
- The bias is closed-form, retention-ratio-only, feature-preserving, and therefore genuinely plug-and-play — a rare case where a theoretical result yields something a practitioner can add in ten lines and get +20 points on grounding.
- Exceptional experimental breadth for a pruning paper: 4 backbones (including two with non-trivial visual front-ends, DeepStack and dynamic tiling), 8 VQA benchmarks, 8 grounding splits, 3 video benchmarks, plus MM-GCoT for the mixed VQA+grounding regime.
- The ablations are designed to validate the theory, not just to sell the method: separate component ablation per error term, bias-form ablation matched to the location-vs-content argument, selection-rule ablation against 10 published rules with the same saliency/budget/bias, and a head-to-head against RESTORE, the closest prior mechanism.
- Honest negative results and scope statements: MVBench/CLEVRER regression, LLaVA's small margin, the unified-config penalty, and the fact that CAR is slower in prefill than FastV and HAWK.
- The paper improves the field's measurement practices by releasing the "bias on top of every baseline" table, which lets readers separate gains from selection design from gains from attention restoration.

## Limitations

- **Not truly calibration-free.** The head probe needs ~500 labelled samples per task family to fix `h*` (and the encoder layer on InternVL/LLaVA), and the grounding probe set overlaps the RefCOCO val evaluation split; a new backbone or task family requires this one-off sweep before the method reaches its reported numbers.
- **Task-specific configurations do the heavy lifting.** Uniform bias + spatial kernel for grounding, capped per-cell bias + no kernel for VQA; a single unified setting loses 3.0 points on VG. The paper offers a decision guideline but not a way to choose the form from the input alone.
- **Theory-to-practice gap.** The optimized objective is a loose upper bound using equal-norm value assumptions, and the selection surrogate lives in the encoder while the bound lives in the decoder; the connection is demonstrated by correlation, not derived.
- **The exchangeability assumption is violated exactly where it matters.** `E[ρ] = k/N` understates the retained set's true attention share because selected tokens are the salient ones, so `log(N/k)` systematically over-restores; the paper bounds the perturbation but never measures the per-query error of the restored share.
- **Small margins without error bars.** InternVL3.5 grounding (+1.2), video at 9.9% (+0.5), LLaVA grounding (+2.2), and several VQA per-benchmark cells are within plausible run-to-run noise; no seeds, no confidence intervals anywhere.
- **Grounding at 9.9% is still 22% below unpruned** (66.8 vs 85.5), and on LLaVA-1.5 only 59% is retained — the paper's framing of "solved" is relative to collapsing baselines rather than absolute usability.
- **Efficiency is a wash versus the best baselines.** CAR's prefill (246.6 ms) trails FastV (222.6 ms) and HAWK (231.8 ms) because of the 24.2 GFLOP selection overhead; on video-scale inputs selection still costs 0.61 s, which may dominate short generations.

## Relevance

与"多模态大模型推理加速 / 视觉 token 压缩"这一支直接相关，而且提供了少见的第一性分析：把剪枝损失拆成信息项与注意力份额项，并给出可外挂的闭式偏置（`log(N/k)`），对任何做 MLLM 部署、视觉定位或高分辨率/视频输入的工程都即刻可用。若关注"压缩后能力退化如何归因与补偿"，这篇的双误差度量协议（Fig. 3 的 `‖E_sum‖`、`‖E_alloc‖`、总误差与输出 KL）比其 SOTA 数字更有复用价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- What is the per-query distribution of the restored share `σ′/σ` under `b = log(N/k)`? The expectation argument predicts over-restoration proportional to the variance of `ρ`; measuring that variance across queries would tell us when the bias is safe.
- Does the head-probe gain survive if the probe set is strictly disjoint from all evaluation splits (e.g. probe on RefCOCO *train* and evaluate on val)? The current +7.7-point probe contribution on VG deserves a leak-free replication.
- Would the selection-side advantage over biased average pooling (+17.7 at 9.9%) persist if pooling were given the same per-cell bias and a comparable per-layer budget schedule?
- Can the choice between uniform and per-cell bias be made automatically — e.g. by measuring whether the query's unpruned attention is spatially concentrated (grounding-like) or diffuse (VQA-like) — instead of declaring the task family in advance?
- Why does the bias help *every* selector by roughly the same amount? If the gain is nearly selector-independent, the residual differences between CAR and biased pooling should be attributable to `E_sum` alone; Figure 3 could be re-plotted to test that additivity directly.
- How does CAR interact with quantization or with KV-cache eviction — both also distort attention mass, and the same reallocation argument may apply at the decoding stage, not just prefill.
