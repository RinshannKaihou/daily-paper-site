> **一句话总结：** 作者提出 SURMA——一枚集成在 GPU die 上的 Scale-Up 远端内存访问引擎，用「credit 共享发送队列」（提交延迟 1.2 µs→400 ns）、「融合 write-with-atomic 的非阻塞连接共享」和「Network Per Path Ordering（NPPO）」三项机制解决细粒度传输下 RDMA 的提交、连接状态与完成排序开销；在双 FPGA 原型 + 双 8-GPU Hopper 服务器上，单个 SM 即可在 512 B 粒度跑满可达带宽（NVLink 需 >14% 的 SM），面积占 GPU <0.5%，四个 MoE 模型推理加速 1.05–1.26×。

> **TL;DR:** SURMA is an on-die RDMA-style remote-memory-access engine that removes inter-GPU communication from the SMs by fixing three costs of fine-grained transfers: a credit-based shared send queue cuts submission from 1.2 µs to 400 ns, fused write-with-atomic over shared connections stops a 1 MB elephant flow from delaying small transfers by 2.2 µs, and NPPO puts the completion flag behind the data using only per-path in-order delivery. On an Intel Agilex 7 two-FPGA prototype (47.6 GB/s of 50 GB/s at 200 MHz) plus two 8-GPU Hopper servers, one SM saturates attainable bandwidth at 512 B granularity versus >14% of SMs for NVLink, small-transfer completion time drops to 0.46× (2.2× throughput at ≤16 KB), area is 96.2% smaller than per-QP/per-context designs (<0.5% of a GPU), and trace-driven inference on Qwen3-Next-80B, Qwen3-235B, GLM4.5-Air and Ling-flash-2.0 improves by 1.05–1.26× over NVLink.

## 核心贡献

- **量化了 SM 承担通信的真实代价**：用 Little 定律把在途数据（B×RTT）绑定到 SMEM，并指出每条字节都要过 SM 固定的 NoC 端口；实测 DeepEP 在 8-GPU Hopper 上要占用 12.8% 的 SM 才能达到约 77% 的 NVLink 峰值带宽，并把该比例对 RTT 与带宽建模（RTT=30 µs 时 SM 占用升到 73%）。
- **指出「现有卸载引擎在 LLM 服务的细粒度传输上失效」**：CopyEngine 在 7 KB 粒度只用不到 1% 的 NVLink 带宽，搬 1 MB 是 TMA 路径的 1.6×；即使把 ConnectX-7 的带宽放大到与 NVLink 匹配，仍是 1.94×（固定开销 4.64 µs/次）。
- **提出三项协同机制**：①基于 credit 的共享发送队列（SSQ），把 WQE 直接写进片上队列，用普通 store 指令完成准入，不需要新指令；②硬件发送滑窗按包轮转交织共享连接上的事务，并用融合的 write-with-atomic 让每个事务独立完成通知；③NPPO 只在每条路径的尾包挂一个原子加，靠「路径内保序」让完成标志跟随数据，既不需要 Sender Ordering 的一整个 RTT，也不需要 Receiver Ordering 的 MB 级计数表。
- **给出面积论证而不仅是性能论证**：把端点状态按「实际服务需求与生命周期」而非「逻辑通信上下文数」来配置，使 512-GPU 配置下 QPC 从 144 MB 降到 2.25 MB、发送队列从 4.5 GB 降到 144 KB（实配 256 KB），总面积比直投+每 QP 队列方案小 96.2%。
- **完成 RTL 原型与端到端评估**：200 MHz、2048-bit 数据通路的双 FPGA 原型跑到 47.6/50 GB/s，并配两个 8-GPU Hopper 服务器做真实 trace 驱动的四模型推理评测。

## Key Contributions

- **A first-principles accounting of SM-side communication cost.** The paper derives the outstanding buffer as B×RTT in SMEM and the per-byte NoC-port charge, then measures both: TMA load throughput on one SM falls from 197.5 to 104.5 GB/s at 128 KB SMEM when RTT grows from 0.463 µs (local) to 1.136 µs (NVLink), and local/remote writes split a fixed 51 GB/s NoC port. DeepEP's intranode dispatch/combine needs 12.8% of SMs for 77% of peak NVLink bandwidth.
- **A characterization of why existing offload paths fail at LLM-serving granularity.** CopyEngine: <1% of NVLink bandwidth at 7 KB, 1.6× slower than TMA for 1 MB. ConnectX-7 RNIC: 4.64 µs fixed per-transfer overhead, 1.94× slower than TMA for 1 MB even after bandwidth normalization.
- **Credit-based Shared Send Queue (SSQ).** All QPs share one on-die SQ sized by the port BDP (256 × 64 B per port, 144 KB for 9 ports) instead of by QP count; software allocates entries as credits so plain stores from many SMs never collide; the Valid bit is set by the store that hits the entry's last address (relying on deterministic NoC routing) and by width for vectorized multi-WQE stores.
- **Non-blocking connection sharing.** One connection per (destination, port) — 4,608 connections and 2.25 MB of QPC for 512 GPUs — with a 16-transaction hardware send sliding window that round-robins one 4 KB packet per transaction per turn, per-QP and per-destination backpressure, and a fused write-with-atomic operation whose ordering scope is only its own transaction.
- **Network Per Path Ordering (NPPO).** Atomics attached to each path's tail packet, with atomic values adjusted (1,1,6 for a 3-packet transaction over 8 paths) so they always sum to k, plus destination-side write-commit tracking (512 entries × 9 B = 4.5 KB) that holds the atomic until the transaction's device-memory writes actually commit.
- **An area reduction of 96.2%** relative to an equivalent direct-submission design with per-QP queues and per-context connections, landing under 0.5% of a GPU's die area for a 512-GPU SuperPOD.

## Methodology

The work is a hardware-architecture paper with three layers of evidence.

**Analytical model.** A 512-GPU SuperPOD with 9 scale-up ports of 50 GB/s per direction (450 GB/s aggregate, as on Hopper) and 2 µs RTT is used as the design point. For each of three phases the authors compare a "low-state" and a "low-latency" design point against SURMA (Table 1): submission (doorbell 1.2 µs/≈0 state vs per-QP SQ 400 ns/4.5 GB vs SSQ 400 ns/144 KB), connection (in-order sharing 2.2 µs/2.25 MB vs per-context 0/144 MB vs interleaved ≤1 packet per transaction/2.25 MB), and completion (Sender Ordering 1 RTT/≈0 vs Receiver Ordering 0/MB-scale vs path-tail + commit 0/4.5 KB). The paper explicitly disclaims that this table is a proven frontier.

**RTL prototype.** SURMA's transaction layer is implemented on two Intel Agilex 7 I-Series FPGA boards connected back to back ("SmartNIC" boards, two 200G QSFP56 ports each, 25 GB/s per port, 50 GB/s per direction combined), running at 200 MHz with a 2048-bit datapath and 51,030 ALMs. Because an FPGA has neither SMs nor HBM, the authors emulate both: a compute-core emulator issues stores over an in-order on-chip interconnect to mirror the GPU's deterministic NoC, a fixed-latency memory model stands in for device memory, and a memory-side atomic unit performs read-modify-write atomics like an L2 atomic unit. A custom lightweight transport wraps SURMA packets in Ethernet frames and exposes eight in-order logical paths (four per port), with retransmission deliberately omitted since the boards are directly connected and RS-FEC covers bit errors.

**Real-GPU measurement and trace-driven end-to-end evaluation.** Two 8-GPU Hopper servers (450 GB/s and 200 GB/s NVLink, measured local 0.463 µs and cross-NVLink 1.136 µs latency) provide (a) the contention measurements in §2.2, (b) the CopyEngine/RNIC/TMA baselines, and (c) real-model traces. The end-to-end numbers replace the compute–communication overlap phases of those traces with SURMA's FPGA-measured latencies and throughputs; §6.3 instead emulates SURMA's throughput with a group of SMs issuing TMA transfers out of an HBM WQE buffer. Each mechanism (SSQ, NPPO) is compared against the exact RNIC mechanism it replaces with everything else held fixed, which isolates the mechanism rather than the whole stack.

## Results & Highlights

- **SM occupancy for full bandwidth drops from >14% to 1 SM.** At 512 B transfer granularity a single SM saturates attainable bandwidth with SURMA, and two SMs suffice at 256 B; the NVLink TMA path needs well over 14% of the compute cores to reach the same bandwidth and scales roughly linearly with allocation, so the comparison is not a small constant-factor win. The 14% figure is consistent with the DeepEP measurement of 12.8% of SMs for 77% of NVLink peak.
- **Submission latency 1.2 µs → 400 ns, and small-transfer completion time 0.80× with SSQ alone.** Against a doorbell baseline whose doorbell travels over the on-chip interconnect (i.e., a *faster* doorbell than PCIe, making the baseline conservative), SSQ cuts completion time to 0.80 at ≤16 KB, degrading to 0.99 at 32 MB as the fixed doorbell cost is amortized. Note the gain is diluted, not eliminated, at large sizes.
- **SSQ + NPPO together: 0.46× completion time at ≤16 KB, i.e. 2.2× throughput** versus the doorbell + Sender Ordering RDMA baseline — because SSQ attacks T_submit and NPPO attacks T_completion, the two terms that dominate small transfers (T_submit ≈ 1.2 µs and T_completion ≈ 2 µs versus T_net ≈ 1 µs).
- **NPPO vs Sender Ordering: ~2× completion time below 8 KB, 1.33× at 2 MB.** In the multi-transaction throughput test the Sender Ordering penalty can be hidden by parallelism: at 8 KB it is 7.705× slower with 16 QPs but only 3.878× with 32 QPs, and at 32 KB doubling QPs cuts the penalty from 2.009× to 1.037×. At 2 MB NPPO is actually 0.99× — the one point where Sender Ordering wins, because the per-path atomic now costs payload bandwidth. The paper reports this honestly.
- **96.2% area reduction and <0.5% of a GPU die.** QPC shrinks from ~144 MB (288K connections × 512 B) to ~2.25 MB (4,608 connections), and the send queue from 4.5 GB to 144 KB (provisioned at 256 KB to absorb RTT jitter), estimated at TSMC 28 nm from the FPGA resource counts. The received packet status table is 512 × 9 B = 4.5 KB.
- **SURMA vs CopyEngine: 1.37–1.55× faster below 1 MB, 1.11× at 32 MB** on a GEMM + AllGather overlap workload; the CE's measured peak bandwidth is 380 GB/s, 70 GB/s short of the 450 GB/s line rate, while SURMA reaches 427 GB/s when its 47.6 GB/s FPGA measurement is scaled to 1.8 GHz. The CE is reported as at least 4× slower than the SM-side TMA path for tiles ≤256 KB.
- **End-to-end inference: 1.05–1.26× over NVLink on four MoE models** (Qwen3-Next-80B, Qwen3-235B, GLM4.5-Air, Ling-flash-2.0) across batch sizes. Qwen3-235B prefill is compute-bound and flat at ~1.14× (pure SM-reclamation); its decode rises from 1.069× to 1.109× as the communication fraction grows. Qwen3-Next-80B moves the opposite way in prefill (1.131× → 1.053×, amortization winning) and up in decode (1.085× → 1.126×, weight reads dominating and communication fraction rising).

## Authors & Credibility

A Tsinghua University (BDSC/EE, Yang Hu's group) + Tencent + HKUST collaboration, with Haoran Shang and Weifeng Zhang as co-first authors. The group has a visible track record in accelerator architecture at MICRO/HPCA/ASPLOS/ASP-DAC (SOFA, MCBP, PADE, BETA, WATOS, TEMP, LAPA), which is the right background for an RTL on-die engine. Tencent's involvement (Ran Shu, Yinben Xia, Xiang Li, and others have published on RDMA/RDMA-NIC design, including the SRC connection-multiplexing work cited here) is credible for the RDMA-side design. The cited baseline context — DeepEP, NCCL 2.28 with CopyEngine collectives, GB200 NVL72, Vera Rubin NVL576, SUE, UALink — is current and specific. The build is a real RTL prototype on real FPGA boards plus real 8-GPU servers, not a simulator, which puts it well above the average architecture-paper evidence bar. There is no artifact/DOI statement, and the "two SmartNIC boards connected back to back" prototype cannot exercise congestion, adaptive routing, or a switch tier — the authors acknowledge the omissions (no retransmission; reliability left to the transport of the target fabric) rather than papering over them.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The individual ingredients are recognizable: direct/SQ-on-die submission exists in BlueFlame-style designs, connection sharing in LITE/Flock/SMART/SRC, and tail-packet completion resembles per-path reliable transport tricks. What is genuinely new is (a) the *state-provisioning argument* — size each endpoint structure by service demand and lifetime rather than by logical concurrency — and (b) NPPO's value-adjustment trick, which lets a transaction shorter than the path count (say 3 packets over 8 paths, carrying atomics of 1, 1, 6) still land on a fixed flag value of k so the receiver never needs to know the transaction size in advance. That is an elegant, low-state answer to multipath completion and is the strongest single idea in the paper.

**Rigor.** The mechanism-level comparisons are well constructed: SSQ is compared against a doorbell baseline whose doorbell still crosses the on-chip interconnect instead of PCIe, which makes the baseline faster than a real RNIC's and the measured gain conservative — good practice. NPPO is compared against a Sender Ordering implementation with everything else fixed, and the paper reports the inconvenient results (0.99× at 2 MB; Sender Ordering penalties collapsing from 7.705× to 3.878× when QPs go 16→32, which is a strong argument that *state*, not raw latency, is the real cost). Table 1's explicit disclaimer that it collects "representative points rather than a proven frontier" is unusually honest for this genre.

**Weak points and red flags.**

- **The end-to-end numbers are not an end-to-end system.** The 1.05–1.26× inference speedups come from replaying real traces with SURMA's FPGA-measured latency/throughput substituted into the overlap phases. There is no SURMA-in-a-GPU run, no compiler/runtime integration, and no measurement of the software cost of the changed completion semantics. For a paper whose whole pitch is "the SMs stop doing communication," the absence of a real kernel-level integration is the largest gap.
- **Internal inconsistency in the scaled bandwidth.** §1 and §6.4.5 say the prototype "reaches 427 GB/s when scaled to a 1.8 GHz GPU clock"; §5 says the same RTL at 1.8 GHz "provides 460.8 GB/s" (2048 bits × 1.8 GHz). 460.8 is the raw datapath limit and 427 is presumably the measured-efficiency version, but the paper never reconciles them, and 427 GB/s is 95% of the 450 GB/s requirement — thin margin presented as comfortable.
- **The 28 nm area estimate is doing a lot of work.** ALM counts from an Agilex 7 (Intel 10 nm-class) design are converted to a TSMC 28 nm area, then compared against an unspecified "GPU area," yielding the headline "<0.5%." Both the process mapping and the reference die are loose; SRAM-dominated structures (256 KB SSQ, 2.25 MB QPC) do not scale the same way as logic.
- **No congestion-control evaluation, despite claiming to solve it.** §4.3 argues the window lets per-QP backpressure coexist on a shared connection, but there is no experiment with incast, no evaluation at the 30 µs hot-expert RTT the paper itself raises in §2.2, and no fairness or stability analysis. At 30 µs RTT and 450 GB/s the BDP is ~13.5 MB, thirty times the provisioned 256 KB SSQ; the design as evaluated simply does not cover the regime the motivation section flags as important.
- **NPPO depends on an assumption the target fabrics may not honor.** It needs strict per-path in-order delivery (justified by SUE and UALink). Ethernet-based scale-up fabrics with adaptive routing or multi-plane spraying can reorder within a plane, and the prototype's "eight paths, each a FIFO channel" is the most favorable possible instantiation; NVL72 has 18 paths, not 8, and the atomic-value scheme and the k=8 polled flag are hard-coded to that count.
- **Baseline breadth is narrow on real hardware.** Only CopyEngine and the SM/TMA path are measured. Competing offload proposals — DMA collectives for ML communication, GIN device-initiated networking, and the CE-based zero-SM collectives in NCCL 2.28 — are discussed but not run; the CE numbers are also tied to one driver/NCCL version, with no sensitivity analysis.
- **The motivating workload and the evaluated workload do not match.** The 576 B KV-cache entry is the paper's headline granularity, but the end-to-end evaluation is entirely MoE expert-parallel dispatch/combine on 8-GPU servers (4–7 KB tokens). There is no KV-cache transfer, no context-parallel trace, no training workload, and no measurement at 576 B beyond the 512 B microbenchmark.
- **API semantic change is under-discussed.** SURMA drops cross-transaction completion ordering within a connection — a flag guarantees only its own transaction's visibility. That is a real programming-model break for existing NVSHMEM/NCCL code that assumes ordered completions, and the paper's answer ("the requester waits before submitting the second") reintroduces exactly the round trip it removed, with no measurement of that cost.

## Strengths

- The problem is quantified before it is solved: Little's-law accounting plus measured SMEM and NoC-port contention (197.5 → 104.5 GB/s at 128 KB SMEM when RTT doubles; 51 GB/s shared NoC port) makes the "communication steals compute" claim concrete rather than rhetorical.
- The three mechanisms map one-to-one onto three separately measured bottlenecks, and each is benchmarked in isolation against the mechanism it replaces — the strongest form of ablation available in this genre.
- NPPO's atomic-value adjustment (1, 1, 6 summing to k) is a genuinely clever, low-cost solution that removes both Sender Ordering's extra RTT and Receiver Ordering's per-transaction counters.
- Submission works with plain stores plus credit allocation: no new ISA, no new instruction, no software queue management in the hot path — a practical adoption story that many architecture proposals lack.
- Area is treated as a first-class result (96.2% reduction, QPC 144 MB → 2.25 MB), which is exactly what an on-die engine proposal must demonstrate.
- Honest reporting of negative/marginal results: 0.99× for NPPO at 2 MB, 1.05× as the floor of the end-to-end range, the Table 1 "not a proven frontier" caveat, and the explicit statement that reliability is left to the fabric.

## Limitations

- Evaluation is FPGA-emulated, not silicon: SM stores, HBM latency, and L2 atomics are all modeled, and the 1.8 GHz scaling is an assumption rather than a measurement.
- No real GPU integration: no runtime, no compiler support for the new completion semantics, no kernel-level demonstration that the 4–7 KB MoE transfers actually pipeline as the trace replay assumes.
- The fabric is two boards back to back with eight FIFO paths, no switch, no congestion, no packet loss, and no adaptive routing — so the three hardest properties of a real 512-GPU SuperPOD are untested.
- Congestion control is asserted, not designed or evaluated; the credit scheme governs admission, not network-level fairness or reaction to incast.
- Hard-coded k=8 paths and a fixed polled flag value; scaling to 18-path NVL72 or 576-GPU systems changes both the atomic accounting and the state budget without analysis.
- Only MoE inference is measured end-to-end; KV-cache movement at 576 B, context parallelism, and training collectives — all cited as motivation — are absent from the results.
- No energy or power analysis, which matters for a die-area and bandwidth argument.
- Area estimate at 28 nm against an unspecified reference GPU is a weak denominator for the "<0.5%" headline.

## Relevance

直接命中「Compute Infrastructure for Large-Scale ML」下的通信与互连方向：它把 GPU 集合通信从 SM 上卸载到片上 RDMA 式引擎，正是 NVLink/NVSHMEM/DeepEP 这条线的硬件化延伸。对关注 MoE 推理通信开销、以及「通信占用 SM 资源」这一系统瓶颈的读者价值很高，但其结论建立在 FPGA 原型与 trace 回放之上，需按此折价。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. What happens to the 256 KB SSQ and 16-transaction window when the RTT is the 30 µs the paper's own motivation cites for a hot MoE expert? Does the design degrade gracefully, or does it require a second, larger provisioning point?
2. How much of the 1.05–1.26× survives a real integration where the runtime must also orchestrate the new completion semantics (polling per-transaction flags, no cross-transaction ordering)? Is the removed 2 µs completion wait replaced by a software wait?
3. Does NPPO still work on a fabric with adaptive per-packet routing, or on NVL72's 18 paths with a hard-coded k? What is the atomic bandwidth overhead as k grows?
4. Where do the 427 GB/s and 460.8 GB/s numbers diverge — synthesis timing closure, measured efficiency, or a different datapath width? A 5% margin over the 450 GB/s requirement is thin enough to matter.
5. Would the SM-side comparison change if the baseline used the CopyEngine-based zero-SM collectives in NCCL 2.28 on a real KV-cache transfer at 576 B rather than a GEMM + AllGather with 256 KB tiles?
6. How does the 96.2% area reduction decompose once the design is ported to a GPU process node, where SRAM (256 KB SSQ + 2.25 MB QPC) rather than logic dominates?
