> **一句话总结：** Kameleo 把 bit-serial 加速器上"每层激活尾数留几位"的决定从离线 PPL 标定搬到运行时：用一个免标签、逐 GEMM 的局部代理（截断到 k 位后输出相对偏差超过 2⁻ˢ|y| 的元素比例 εₖ，固定 s=4、τ=0.28），直接复用 bit-serial 数据通路里本来就存在的 k 位中间部分和与最终结果做比较，两个 pass 把每个 GEMM 的长度锁死；在 4 个 LLM × 7 个数据集上无需任何标定就守住 1% PPL 预算，MXU prefill 周期降 2.12–2.29×（WikiText2），硬件只多 3.7% 面积、推理时多 3.5% MXU 功耗。

> **TL;DR:** Kameleo replaces offline perplexity calibration of per-GEMM activation mantissa lengths with an in-situ, label-free rule: an element is an "error" if truncating to k bits changes it by more than 2⁻⁴·|y|, and a length is accepted when the error rate εₖ ≤ 0.28 — both constants fixed at design time. Because a bit-serial PE already produces the k-bit partial sum as an intermediate state, three candidate results and a reference are captured in the same pass (FP32 candidate registers plus an error-check unit), so two passes over T=8192 tokens lock all lengths with no calibration data and no extra model evaluations. On WikiText2 it reaches 2.29×/2.29×/2.12×/2.26× MXU cycle reduction on Llama2-13B / Llama3.1-8B / Qwen2.5-7B / Yi1.5-34B at +0.80%/+0.39%/−0.04%/−0.16% PPL, versus 2.37×/2.38×/2.24×/2.83× at +1.00%/+0.63%/+0.95%/+0.79% for ANDA calibrated on WikiText2 itself — i.e. 80–97% of the oracle-calibrated speedup, and below it in PPL everywhere. Under workload shift, 18 of 42 transferred ANDA configurations blow the 1% budget (worst +2.9%), while Kameleo stays inside it on all seven datasets at 2.25–2.28×; on Llama2-7B it even beats ANDA's all-seven-dataset "robust" configuration (max +0.79% vs +1.00%) at 2.27× vs 2.39×. Cost: +3.7% accelerator area (2.33→2.42 mm²) and +3.5% MXU power in inference (111.1→115.0 mW), 166.7 mW (+50%) during selection.

## 核心贡献

- **把"全局 PPL"换成"局部误差率"这个运行时可选的质量代理**：离线标定（ANDA 的贪心搜索）要 13–25 次全模型 PPL 评估，只给整个模型一个数，说不清上百个 GEMM 里哪个能降精度，而且换数据就静默失效。Kameleo 的定义是逐 GEMM 的：令 y 为满 M 位尾数的输出元素、ŷₖ 为截断到 k 位的同一元素，当 |ŷₖ − y| > 2⁻ˢ|y| 时记一个"错误"，εₖ 是错误元素占该 GEMM 输出元素的比例，εₖ ≤ τ 即接受该长度。数错误个数（每个元素只留 1 bit）而不是累加误差幅值，可以避免少量 outlier 主导指标。
- **两个超参是设计常量，不是标定参数**：s = 4、τ = 0.28 在全篇所有 GEMM、模型、数据集上不变。作者扫 τ ∈ [0.15, 0.6]：MXU 周期收益从 1.91× 涨到 2.83×，而四个模型的 PPL 退化在 τ ≤ 0.32 时都还在 1% 预算内，所以 τ = 0.28 是留了余量而不是卡着预算调出来的。选择前缀长度也几乎无关：2048 而不是 32768 个 token，周期收益变化 ≤0.02×、PPL 变化 ≤0.07%。
- **"Search and Lock"的两趟协议**：候选长度只有 3–11 位共 9 个值，误差率对 k 单调（Fig. 2a），所以每趟让每个 GEMM 并行试 3 个候选：第一趟把 9 个缩到 ≤3 个，第二趟定稿并锁死，之后正常推理。两趟都是普通的满精度前向 pass，其输出照常服务，所以选择本身只花"没被省下来的那些周期"。
- **几乎零成本的硬件实现**：bit-serial PE 第 k 个周期后的部分和（左移 M−k 位）恰好就是 k 位尾数的结果，所以满精度那一趟里顺带抓 3 个候选（c1–c3，FP32）和 1 个参考值，候选在 FP 累加器本来空闲的加法器上累加；一个 error-check unit 对每个输出元素算 2⁻ˢ|y|（指数减 s）、FP32 相减并比较，每个候选每个元素输出 1 bit flag；flag collector 把 16 个 PE 的 flag 加成 1 个 INT5 计数，一个 16×16 阵列每候选每输出 tile 只上报 16 个计数而不是 256 bit。
- **实测收益与"离线标定会失效"的系统性证据**：4 个模型、7 个数据集（WikiText2、C4、PG-19、LAMBADA、CBT、Python 代码、德语维基），跨数据集标定转移的 42 个非对角格里有 18 个超预算；Kameleo 全部守住。
- **代价量化**：FP 累加器路径面积 +55%、MXU +25.8%、整个加速器（SRAM 主导）+3.7%；推理时 MXU 功耗 +3.5%（时钟门控关掉选择期间才用的寄存器），选择期间 166.7 mW（+50%），只持续 2T = 16384 个 token。

## Key Contributions

- **A local, label-free quality proxy for reduced activation precision.** Instead of the model-level perplexity used by offline calibration, Kameleo measures, per GEMM, the fraction of output elements whose value changes by more than 2⁻ˢ relative when the activation mantissa is cut from M to k bits. Only two constants (s = 4, τ = 0.28) exist, they are properties of the acceptance rule rather than of a model or dataset, and they are never retuned across the four LLMs and seven datasets.
- **A minimal PE extension that gets the proxy almost for free.** Because the bit-serial datapath accumulates shifted partial sums MSB-first, the k-bit result is an intermediate state of the full-precision pass; three candidate registers plus a reference register capture it, an error-check unit emits one flag bit per candidate per output element, and a flag collector reduces 256 PE flags to 16 INT5 counts per tile. The reduced and full results therefore need no second arithmetic pass.
- **In-situ selection in two ordinary prefill passes.** Nine candidate lengths (3–11 bits) are pruned to ≤3 per GEMM in the first pass and resolved in the second, exploiting monotonicity of εₖ in k, after which lengths are locked; the selection tokens are served as normal inference, so the trigger (per workload, periodically, or on task change) is a deployment policy rather than a cost.
- **A controlled A/B against the same silicon.** Kameleo is built as a strict superset of ANDA with everything else identical (TSMC 16 nm, 250 MHz, 0.75 V, same quantizer, same cost metric), which isolates the price of runtime selection at +3.7% area / +3.5% inference power and lets the paper compare against ANDA calibrated on the test distribution itself — the strongest possible offline baseline.
- **Evidence that the offline-calibration regime, not just one configuration, is fragile.** Calibrating ANDA on each of the seven datasets in turn and deploying on the other six produces 18 budget violations out of 42 transfers (up to +2.9%), including failures on in-domain prose (C4 → CBT) and configuration-dependent behaviour that cannot be predicted ahead of time (on Yi1.5-34B, the C4 configuration fails on 1 of 6 others while the WikiText2 configuration fails on 5).

## Methodology

**Problem framing.** Weight-only quantization (W4A16) leaves activations in FP16, which makes activation arithmetic the on-chip bottleneck for energy and throughput in the compute-bound prefill phase. Bit-serial, variable-length activation formats (block floating point, one mantissa length per GEMM) recover that cost, but the length must be chosen; current practice is offline greedy calibration against full-model perplexity, which is expensive (13–25 full PPL evaluations, 0.4–1.7 H100 GPU-hours per search) and data-dependent.

**The proxy.** For an output element y computed with the full M-bit mantissa and ŷₖ the same element with the mantissa truncated to k bits, the element counts as an error when |ŷₖ − y| > 2⁻ˢ|y| (Eq. 1). A relative deviation below 2⁻ˢ means ŷₖ retained the leading s significant bits of y regardless of how many input bits were dropped. The GEMM's error rate εₖ is the fraction of erroneous output elements, and a length is accepted when εₖ ≤ τ. Counting rather than accumulating keeps a few outliers from dominating and reduces the metric to one bit per element. s = 4 and τ = 0.28 are fixed for everything.

**Search and lock.** Fig. 2a shows εₖ rising monotonically with shorter mantissas, at a rate that differs per GEMM group and per layer, so each GEMM takes the shortest length where its curve crosses τ. Because of monotonicity a search suffices: all GEMMs are searched simultaneously, each evaluating three candidate lengths per pass; pass 1 narrows nine candidates to at most three, pass 2 resolves them, then lengths are locked and inference continues at reduced precision. Total cost: 2T tokens of ordinary inference.

**Hardware.** ANDA's PE multiplies groups of 64 activation mantissas bit-plane by bit-plane against INT4 weights (MSB first) in an adder tree, shifting and accumulating so that after k cycles it holds the exact dot product for a k-bit mantissa; the FP accumulator scales it by the shared exponent and weight scale. Kameleo adds (1) a length table and (2) three FP32 candidate registers plus a reference register, capturing the partial sum shifted by M−k at the cycle where the bit counter hits each candidate length, with alignment folded into the exponent at latch time and each candidate accumulating on the otherwise idle adder of the FP accumulator; (3) an error-check unit that forms 2⁻ˢ|y| by decrementing y's exponent by s, subtracts in FP32, compares magnitudes and emits one flag bit per candidate per element, clock-gated outside selection passes; (4) a flag collector that sums 16 PE flags into one INT5 count per candidate per column. The 16×16 MXU's host controller accumulates counts per GEMM into εₖ, compares with τ, and writes the next candidate set and finally the locked lengths into the activation dispatcher.

**Evaluation setup.** Four models (Llama2-13B, Llama3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Yi1.5-34B) on WikiText2, C4, PG-19, LAMBADA, CBT, a Python source corpus ("Code") and German Wikipedia ("Wiki-DE"), the last two deliberately far from English prose; per-layer and per-dataset analyses use Llama2-7B. Weights are INT4 via the OmniQuant quantizer at initialization without learned clipping. Quality is PPL over 2048-token windows against W4A16 with the full 11-bit BFP mantissa, budget 1% degradation. Throughput is the analytic reduction in MXU cycles relative to FP16 activations (a bit-serial GEMM takes size × mantissa length cycles; 16 for FP16, 11 for the full BFP mantissa), including attention GEMMs at full mantissa, validated against a cycle-accurate RTL simulation. Kameleo's selection is emulated in software with bit-exact truncation on the first T = 8192 tokens using a binary search, argued to pick the same lengths as the three-candidate hardware search under the monotonicity of Fig. 2a. Hardware is SystemVerilog, Cadence Genus, TSMC 16 nm at 250 MHz / 0.75 V, power from VCD switching activity.

## Results & Highlights

- **WikiText2, Llama2-13B: 5.03 PPL (+0.80%) at 2.29× MXU cycle reduction with no calibration**, versus 5.04 (+1.00%) at 2.37× for ANDA calibrated on WikiText2 itself, and versus 1.00× for W4A16/FP16 activations (4.99), 1.23× for FIGNA's 13-bit BFP (4.99), 1.45× for the 11-bit BFP reference (4.99) and 3.61× for VS-Quant's per-vector INT4 activations (5.21, +4.41%, i.e. 4.4× over budget). Kameleo is 0.2 points *better* in PPL than the oracle-calibrated ANDA at 96.6% of its speedup.
- **The gap to oracle calibration is 80–97%, and it is widest on the largest model**: Yi1.5-34B gets 5.00 (−0.16%) at 2.26× versus 5.05 (+0.79%) at 2.83× for ANDA-cal-WikiText2 (79.9% of the reduction), while Llama3.1-8B gets 6.78 (+0.39%) at 2.29× versus 6.79 (+0.63%) at 2.38× (96.2%). Qwen2.5-7B: 7.29 (−0.04%) at 2.12× versus 7.36 (+0.95%) at 2.24×.
- **Transferred calibration fails systematically, and worse than Table I's single example suggests**: across 7 datasets × 6 non-diagonal deployments, **18 of 42 transfers exceed the 1% budget**, with degradations up to **+2.9%** (LAMBADA-calibrated configuration on CBT, Llama2-7B), and the worst whole-row behaviour comes from LAMBADA calibration (up to +2.93% and a row mean well above 2%). C4-calibrated Llama2-13B reaches 5.11 PPL on WikiText2 (**+2.36%**, Table I) — more than double the budget.
- **Kameleo beats the strongest offline baseline on quality at 95% of its speed**: on Llama2-7B across all seven datasets, ANDA's "robust" configuration (longest length per GEMM type over all seven calibrations, i.e. it has seen every deployment dataset in advance) gives +0.82/+0.61/+0.75/+0.43/+1.00/+0.47/+0.94% at 2.39×, while Kameleo gives **+0.63/+0.30/+0.36/+0.27/+0.79/+0.24/+0.50% at 2.27×** — uniformly lower degradation, worst case 0.79% versus 1.00%.
- **Downstream quality is flat**: over six zero-shot benchmarks (PIQA, ARC-e, ARC-c, BoolQ, HellaSwag, WinoGrande) the average accuracy of every model moves by at most **0.1 points** with Kameleo's WikiText2 lengths; Llama2-13B goes **72.8% → 72.9%**.
- **Hardware cost is small and localized**: FP accumulator path **+55%** area, MXU **+25.8%**, full accelerator **2.33 → 2.42 mm² (+3.7%)** because SRAM buffers dominate; MXU power **111.1 → 115.0 mW (+3.5%)** during inference and **166.7 mW (+50%)** during a selection pass, which lasts 2T = 16 384 tokens and whose outputs are still served.
- **The constants behave like constants**: τ sweep 0.15→0.6 moves cycle reduction 1.91×→2.83× while all four models stay inside the 1% budget up to τ = 0.32; shrinking the selection prefix from 32 768 to 2048 tokens changes cycle reduction by ≤0.02× and PPL by ≤0.07%. Against 13–25 full-model PPL evaluations (0.4–1.7 H100-hours) per offline calibration, Kameleo needs zero calibration data and zero extra model evaluations.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The conceptual move is genuinely new in this subfield: prior variable-length activation work (ANDA, OPAL, M-ANT, FIGNA) all treats the length table as a compile/calibration artifact, and this paper is, as far as I can tell, the first to make the length a runtime decision with an on-die criterion. The crucial enabling observation — that the k-bit result is already sitting in the bit-serial accumulator at cycle k, so the reduced-vs-full comparison costs two registers and a subtractor rather than a second GEMM — is simple, correct, and the kind of thing that looks obvious only after someone says it. That is a real contribution, not a repackaging of quantization-aware calibration.

**Rigor.** Strong where it counts and weak in one specific place. Strong: the baseline is not a strawman — ANDA is calibrated *on the test distribution*, which is an unrealistically good offline baseline that handicaps the proposed method, and Kameleo still wins on PPL at every model. The 7×7 transfer matrix is the right experiment for the claim being made, and reporting 18/42 violations (rather than one cherry-picked failure) is honest. The τ and prefix-length sensitivity sweeps directly answer the "you just tuned two hyperparameters per dataset" objection. Weak: **the selection loop itself is never validated end-to-end.** Kameleo's lengths are produced by a software binary search with bit-exact truncation, not by the hardware's two-pass, three-candidate-per-pass search; equivalence is asserted from the monotonicity of Fig. 2a, which is shown for Llama2-7B only and only as a group-mean-plus-32-thin-curves plot. A non-monotonic or noisy εₖ curve in one GEMM of Qwen2.5-7B would silently lock a bad length, and nothing in the paper would reveal it. The RTL simulation is cited to validate the *cycle count*, not the *chosen lengths*. There is also no reported agreement rate between in-situ lengths and oracle-calibrated lengths per GEMM — with that number the reader could judge the proxy directly instead of through PPL.

**Reproducibility.** The software side is reproducible: the proxy is three lines of arithmetic, the search is a binary search over nine integers, T = 8192 is stated, and PPL windows are 2048 tokens with an OmniQuant INT4 initializer. The hardware side is described at RTL-block granularity with a standard commercial flow (16 nm, 250 MHz, 0.75 V) but no statement that the SystemVerilog is released, so area/power numbers can be checked only by re-implementing. That is normal for this venue and does not undermine the algorithmic claim, which stands on software emulation anyway.

**Significance.** Practically important: the deployment story for variable-length activations has been blocked precisely by "recalibrate for every model × workload, and hope the data does not drift", and a 3.7%-area, 3.5%-power in-situ replacement removes that blocker. The 2.12–2.29× prefill-cycle reduction at ≤1% PPL is competitive with what fixed low-bit activation formats buy at *equal* quality, which is the only fair comparison.

**Red flags and things I would push on.** (1) **The VS-Quant comparison is not iso-quality.** VS-Quant's 3.56–3.68× is achieved at 4.4–6.8% PPL degradation, i.e. 4–7× over the paper's own budget; the paper uses it to mark one end of the trade-off but never plots VS-Quant's degradation-versus-speedup curve, so a reader cannot tell whether per-vector INT4 could hit 1% at 2.8× and thereby beat Kameleo outright. As written, "Kameleo is faster than the fixed formats at equal quality" is supported, but "Kameleo is on the Pareto front" is not. (2) **"Almost free" assumes long prefills.** The selection prefix is a fixed T = 8192 tokens and two passes means 16 384 tokens of work; for a short request (a few hundred tokens, common in chat and in agentic tool calls) the selection passes are *not* a few percent of the workload but several times it, and the paper's cost accounting never considers a workload shorter than the prefix, nor proposes an amortization policy beyond "deployment policy". (3) **Prefill only.** Decode is memory-bound and untouched, so the end-to-end latency/energy win for a real serving mix is unquantified; no tokens-per-joule or wall-clock number appears anywhere, only MXU cycle counts and MXU power. (4) **The per-GEMM "GEMM type" granularity of the baseline is generous to ANDA** (four lengths for the whole model) while Kameleo sets a length per GEMM, so part of the 80–97% comparison is a granularity difference, not a criterion difference — an ANDA variant with per-GEMM offline calibration would be the fairer oracle and would probably narrow the gap further. (5) Kameleo's PPL is sometimes *below* the 11-bit reference (−0.04% on Qwen2.5-7B, −0.16% on Yi1.5-34B), which is empirical noise on 2048-token windows and hints the 1% budget is being resolved at roughly the noise floor of the measurement. (6) The 1% budget is checked on PPL only; the six-task zero-shot check uses WikiText2-selected lengths, not the cross-dataset lengths that Fig. 4 shows are the risky ones.

## Strengths

- The core idea is elegant and physically grounded: the quality signal is read out of arithmetic the accelerator is already doing, so the method's cost is registers and a comparator rather than a second datapath.
- An unusually honest baseline setup — ANDA calibrated on the evaluation data itself — plus a 7×7 transfer matrix instead of a single anecdotal failure.
- Every claimed constant is stress-tested (τ over 0.15–0.6, prefix over 2048–32 768 tokens), which is exactly the right response to "this is calibration in disguise".
- Cost is reported at three granularities (FP accumulator path, MXU, whole accelerator) so the +3.7% headline can be checked against the +55% local cost.
- The hardware implementation is a strict superset of a published accelerator, so the ablation is architectural rather than comparative.
- Downstream zero-shot accuracy is reported, not just PPL, and it is flat (≤0.1 point).

## Limitations

- Selection quality is demonstrated by software emulation of the criterion, not by the actual two-pass three-candidate hardware loop; the equivalence argument rests on monotonicity verified for one model family.
- No per-GEMM agreement analysis between in-situ and oracle lengths, and no measurement of how often a GEMM is mis-locked.
- Throughput is analytic MXU-cycle counting validated by RTL simulation, not measured end-to-end; no latency, energy-per-token or tokens-per-joule result, and decode is out of scope.
- The fixed 8192-token selection prefix is a poor fit for short workloads; no amortization policy or break-even analysis is given.
- Evaluation covers Llama-2/3.1, Qwen2.5 and Yi1.5 dense models on English prose, code and German text; no MoE, no instruction-tuned/chat models, no long-context regimes, no KV-cache interaction.
- The offline baseline uses ANDA's four-GEMM-type granularity, so the comparison conflates criterion and granularity.
- No iso-quality VS-Quant operating point, so the Pareto claim relative to per-vector INT4 activations is unresolved.
- RTL is not stated to be open-sourced, limiting independent verification of area and power.

## Relevance

与"算子精度、误差传播"这条主线高度相关：它把"低精度激活到底掉多少有效位"显式定义成可在线计算的逐算子相对误差率（2⁻ˢ 阈值 + 错误计数），并证明这个局部数值判据能替代全局 PPL 标定——这正是"量化退化如何被检测与约束"的一个硬件级实例。对关心低比特激活量化失效模式（跨数据集静默掉点、标定不可迁移）和加速器数值精度设计的人值得精读。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. What is the per-GEMM agreement rate between the in-situ selected length and the oracle-calibrated length, and does the disagreement concentrate in the layers where Fig. 2a's curves are flat (i.e. where the decision is nearly free) or in the steep ones?
2. How does the method behave on a workload shorter than the 8192-token prefix — what is the break-even prompt length, and would a hierarchical scheme (select on a shared prefix, refine per request) preserve the guarantee?
3. Is the εₖ curve monotonically decreasing in k for every GEMM of every model, or only on average? If a GEMM is non-monotonic, does the two-pass three-candidate search provably find the accepted length, or can it lock a length that violates τ?
4. What does the degradation-versus-speedup curve of per-vector INT4 (VS-Quant) look like near the 1% budget — is there an operating point at 2.8× that would make Kameleo's runtime machinery unnecessary?
5. How does the scheme interact with KV-cache quantization and with decode-phase arithmetic, where the bottleneck is bandwidth rather than MXU cycles?
6. Could the same flag-based error rate be exposed as a cheap online health signal — e.g. detecting distribution shift or hardware-induced numerical anomalies by watching εₖ drift — turning the selection unit into a monitor rather than just a tuner?
7. What is the energy overhead of the two selection passes (not just power), and how does it amortize over a realistic multi-tenant serving trace with mixed prompt lengths?
