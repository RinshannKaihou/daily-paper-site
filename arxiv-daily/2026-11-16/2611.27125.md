> **一句话总结：** 论文从学习动力学角度拆解了 on-policy distillation（OPD）为何在教师很强时仍然失效：单步 softmax 分析显示 token 概率变化幅度被 π(1−π) 夹住、教师偏好的低概率 token 几乎得不到纠正，跨位置梯度又会改写更新方向，EOS token 不匹配则把模型推入"局部高重叠但重复生成"的 spurious agreement 状态；据此提出的 state-dependent KL routing（低 top-p 覆盖或教师质量被低估时改用 top-K 前向 KL）在 Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B 上把数学推理平均 Avg@8 提升 1.38–2.50 个百分点。

> **TL;DR:** The authors explain sampled-token on-policy distillation failures with a single-state softmax decomposition plus cross-position gradient accounting, and show that the student's probability mass on the teacher's top-p set (M_top-p) — not teacher entropy or sampled-token advantage — predicts where FKL deteriorates. Their fix, routing to top-K forward KL only at low-coverage / high-underestimation states (6.7% of positions), beats sampled-token OPD on math (mean Avg@8 18.12 vs 15.76 on Qwen3-1.7B-Base, +2.36 pts; +1.72 pts at 8B) and code (Avg@8 on LiveCodeBench v6 14.93 vs 8.93 at 1.7B, +6.00 pts), at 2.5–6.8% extra step time with unchanged peak memory.

## 核心贡献

- **单状态 softmax 下的精确一步动力学**：给出 Proposition 1，把 OPD 一步更新分解为 advantage、当前概率 πθ(c|s) 与耦合项 B(c,u;s) 三部分；推出采样 token 的概率变化被 |A|·p(1−p)² 上下界夹住，教师在低概率 token 上的偏好几乎无法通过采样更新获得质量。
- **指出"高 advantage 不等于高覆盖"**：非采样 token 的变化方向由一个"分水岭" τ(u;s)=‖πθ‖₂²−πθ(u) 决定，位于其上方的 token（定理 1 证明学生众数总在其上方）会朝 advantage 反方向移动；因此单个采样 token 的正 advantage 完全不排除其它位置/其它 token 的严重不匹配。
- **实证定位"难学状态"**：用学生落在教师 top-p 集合上的概率质量 M_top-p 刻画失配，发现在固定 held-out 前缀上 FKL 恶化集中在低 M_top-p 区域，且 top-p 覆盖比 top-K 覆盖更有判别力（Table 9：top-p p=0.90 均值 18.12% vs 固定 top-K 16.12%）。
- **跨位置梯度效应**：证明/实测在 LLM 中 advantage 与实测 Δlog π 的符号一致率只有约 50%，仅当更新中只含一个位置时接近 100%；用 M_ij = ηA_j⟨h_i,h_j⟩ 把跨位置影响量化，发现 EOS/终止 token 位置（advantage 约 −22.5）对其它位置的影响权重高达 31.8%，远超第二名 10.1%。
- **命名并机制化 spurious agreement**：以 Qwen3-1.7B-Base（用 `<|endoftext|>`）vs Qwen3-8B（用 `<|im_end|>`）的终止 token 不匹配为案例，展示训练如何压掉学生众数却不学教师 token，导致长度爆炸与重复；对齐终止 token 或屏蔽其梯度后崩溃消失。
- **提出 state-dependent KL routing**：以 M_top-p 与"教师 top-p 质量中被学生低估（比值 > ε₁）的比例 V(θ;s_t)"为路由信号，只在满足条件的位置（实测约 6.7% 的 token）用 top-K 前向 KL 替代采样 token 反 KL。

## Key Contributions

- A closed-form, per-token characterization (Prop. 1) of one sampled-token OPD step under a single-state softmax policy, including the exact direction/magnitude bounds |A|p(1−p)² ≤ lim|Δπ|/η ≤ 2|A|p(1−p)², plus a "watershed" τ(u;s) that separates non-sampled tokens moving with versus against the sampled advantage.
- The empirical claim that student coverage of the teacher's top-p set (M_top-p), rather than teacher entropy (EOPD) or the sampled-token advantage (AOPD), is the informative predictor of where OPD updates increase forward KL.
- A quantitative account of cross-position gradient interference: advantage/update sign agreement falls from ~100% (single position) to ~50% (full batch) across four teacher–student pairs, and the EOS position dominates cross-position influence.
- A mechanistic case study of "spurious agreement": a termination-token mismatch suppresses the student's mode without teaching the teacher's token, inflating length and repetition while top-16 overlap stays at 0.988.
- State-dependent KL routing, a drop-in modification with three thresholds, that improves mean Avg@8/Best@8 over sampled-token OPD across three Qwen3 scales, two initializations, and two task families.

## Methodology

The paper is organized as diagnosis → explanation → intervention.

**Local theory (Sec. 3).** At a fixed state s with softmax logits θ_s, a single SGD step on the sampled-token OPD loss gives Δθ_s = ηA(s,u)(e_u − πθ(·|s)) for u ~ πθ. Taylor-expanding the softmax Jacobian yields Prop. 1: Δlog π(c|s) = ηA(s,u)B(c,u;s) + O(η²) with B(c,u;s) = Σ_b (1{c=b} − πθ(b|s))(1{u=b} − πθ(b|s)). For c = u this reduces to B(u,u;s) = (1−p)² + Σ_{b≠u}πθ(b)² ≥ 0, so the sampled token always moves with the advantage but with magnitude scaled by p(1−p)². For c ≠ u, B(c,u;s) = τ(u;s) − πθ(c|s), giving the watershed split. Theorem 1 proves the student mode always sits above the watershed and therefore moves *against* the sampled advantage whenever it is not itself sampled.

**Diagnostics on real training (Sec. 4).** All diagnostics use Qwen3-1.7B-Base / Qwen3-4B-Base / DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B students against JustRL-1.5B, Qwen3-4B, and DeepSeek-Distill-Qwen-7B teachers, trained on DAPO for 40–200 steps. Three probes: (i) per-token Δπ and Δlog π binned by pre-update π(u) and |A| (786,374 sampled tokens); (ii) change in forward KL measured on a *held-out fixed set* of teacher-generated prefixes, cross-tabulated against student entropy, teacher entropy, and M_top-p; (iii) first-order cross-position prediction Σ_j M_ij versus measured Δlog π after one SGD step, plus a leave-one-out check and an O(η²) residual-scaling check.

**Trajectory analysis (Sec. 4.3).** A two-category toy policy shows mode suppression; then the EOS mismatch is controlled three ways (original / aligned termination token / masked gradient at termination positions), and teacher loop rate is measured when the teacher continues student prefixes before and after repetition onset.

**Intervention (Sec. 5.2).** Two routing signals: M_top-p(s_t) (student mass on the teacher's smallest top-p set, p=0.9) and V(θ;s_t) = Σ_{c∈S_p} π̂_T(c) 1[π_T(c)/πθ(c) > ε₁] with ε₁ = 6. Route to unnormalized top-K (K = 16) forward KL when V > ε₂ = 0.3 or M_top-p < ε₃ = 0.2; otherwise keep sampled-token reverse KL. Eq. 17 motivates this: the top-K FKL logit update moves token b at rate π_T(b|s), i.e., proportional to teacher mass, unlike the sampled-token update which scales with student mass.

**Setup.** Students Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B-Base, teacher Qwen3-8B (non-thinking for Base init, thinking for SFT init). OPD data: DAPO (math), CodeR1-12k (code). SFT cold start: 400K prompt–response pairs from OpenThoughts3-1.2M (50K prompts × 8 responses), full-parameter SFT via LLaMA-Factory. OPD via verl, batch 128, LR 1e-6, 200 steps (Base) / 100 steps (SFT). Metrics: Avg@8 and a bootstrap Best@8 = (1/N)Σ_i[1 − (1 − c_i/8)⁸].

## Results & Highlights

- **Math, Base initialization (Table 1a, 8B student):** mean Avg@8 30.08 vs 28.36 for sampled-token OPD (**+1.72 pts**) and 27.89 for AOPD; mean Best@8 47.79 vs 41.55 (**+6.24 pts**, the largest gap in the paper). Per-benchmark: AIME25 Avg@8 17.50 vs 17.50 (tie), AIME26 Avg@8 18.75 vs 14.58 (+4.17).
- **Math, Base initialization (Table 1a, 1.7B student):** AMC23 Avg@8 45.00 vs 38.44 (+6.56) and 38.75 for EOPD; mean Avg@8 18.12 vs 15.76 (**+2.36**), mean Best@8 32.32 vs 29.72 (+2.60). Across the three scales the paper reports +2.36/+2.50/+1.72 Avg@8 and +2.60/+4.26/+6.24 Best@8.
- **Math, SFT initialization (Table 1b):** gains shrink to +2.08/+1.38/+1.59 Avg@8 and +3.33/+2.30/+1.01 Best@8. Notably the 1.7B student's AMC23 Avg@8 is 45.94 vs 46.56 for the SFT checkpoint itself — OPD does not beat the initialization there, and AIME24 Best@8 at 1.7B is 34.45 vs 26.95 for OPD.
- **Code generation (Table 2, Base init, step 200):** mean Avg@8 46.00/55.74/59.00 vs 43.08/52.55/55.56 for OPD (+2.92/+3.19/+3.44) but mean Best@8 only +2.08/+1.27/+2.88; at 4B, AOPD has a *higher* mean Best@8 (64.26 vs 62.87). HumanEval+ Avg@8 at 1.7B is 67.07 vs 64.71 (+2.36) while Best@8 is 76.76 vs 77.26 (slightly worse).
- **Where the code gain comes from:** almost entirely LiveCodeBench v6, where Avg@8 goes 14.93 vs 8.93 (+6.00) at 1.7B, 20.93 vs 10.00 (+10.93) at 4B, 23.64 vs 15.50 (+8.14) at 8B; HumanEval+ and MBPP+ are within ~2 pts of OPD everywhere.
- **Termination-token control (Table 5):** fraction of rollouts hitting the length cap drops from 0.95 to 0.25 under either aligning the EOS token or masking its gradient; validation response length drops from 7,234 to 3,939 / 3,761 tokens, while top-16 teacher-mass overlap is essentially unchanged (0.988 → 0.989 / ≈0.99) — i.e., overlap was never the diagnostic.
- **Injected mismatch on ordinary tokens (Sec. E.4):** forcing teacher log-prob −10 on `###`, `--\n\n`, `\n\n`, `The`, `So` merely makes the student stop emitting them (`\n\n` falls from 1.8% to 0.02% of tokens) with Avg@8 16.9–18.3 vs 18.5 clean — the failure is specific to tokens the response cannot do without.
- **Sign agreement (Fig. 5a,b):** advantage vs realized Δlog π sign agreement is ~0.5 across four teacher–student pairs at 10–40 steps, but rises to ~1.0 as the update is restricted to one position; the first-order cross-position predictor matches measurement with r = 0.99996, slope 0.9990, sign agreement 0.991 (Fig. 5c).
- **Routing ablations (Table 8–10, 1.7B):** ε₁ 1.5 → 6 raises mean Avg@8 16.15 → 18.12 while the FKL fraction falls 17.6% → 6.7%; ε₂ = 0.5 cuts FKL to 3.6% but drops to 16.90%; coverage-only routing 17.06, vote-only 16.54, both 18.12 at the same 6.7% FKL fraction.
- **Cost (Fig. 16):** at K = 16, teacher scoring grows from 2.6–3.1 s to 4.5–5.9 s per step (6–10% of total step time), total step time +2.5–6.8%; actor-update memory grows 2.5–5.5 GB but stays under the fixed vLLM teacher budget, so peak memory of a full step is unchanged.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The core negative result — that reverse-KL OPD suppresses modes without acquiring teacher modes — is close to what Fu et al. (2026), Luo et al. (2026), and Li et al. (2026) already report, and the paper says so. What is genuinely new is the *specific diagnostic*: the claim that M_top-p beats both teacher entropy (EOPD's signal) and the sampled-token advantage (AOPD's signal), supported by the Fig. 2(c) cross-tabulation where deterioration concentrates at low top-p coverage "across a broad range of top-K coverage". That is a falsifiable, useful distinction, and Table 9 (top-p 18.12 vs fixed top-K 16.12 at matched ~6.7% FKL) is a real head-to-head rather than an assertion. Theorem 1 (mode-direction invariance) is a clean, non-obvious algebraic result.

**Rigor concerns.** (1) The theory is single-state with independently parameterized logits, which the paper itself shows is violated — sign agreement is ~50% in the real model. So Prop. 1 explains *why a step is weak* but not *what the actual step does*; the bridge is the empirical M_ij decomposition, which is only validated on one step, one batch, and one student–teacher pair (Fig. 5c, Fig. 15). (2) The routing thresholds ε₁ = 6, ε₂ = 0.3, ε₃ = 0.2, p = 0.9, K = 16 were selected by maximizing mean Avg@8 **on the same four benchmarks used for the main table** (Table 8 is a grid over AIME24/25/26 + AMC23), then transferred unchanged to 4B and 8B. That is test-set model selection; the reported 1.7B numbers are therefore optimistic by an unquantified amount, and the 4B/8B numbers are cleaner but untuned. (3) The ablation grid is 1.7B-only, so there is no evidence the thresholds transfer — they are simply applied. (4) Base runs are 200 steps and SFT runs 100 steps with batch 128, which is short for post-training claims; the paper does not show whether the advantage persists, shrinks, or inverts at 2–3× longer training. (5) Baselines are EOPD and AOPD only. TrOPD (Xing et al., 2026), TIP (Xu et al., 2026b), Teachability-Aware OPD (Wang et al., 2026), and REOPOLD (Ko et al., 2026) are discussed in related work as the closest competing position-selection methods but never run; All-FKL appears only as a curve in Fig. 7 with the qualitative statement "does not achieve better performance", never as a table row — which is exactly the comparison that would justify "selective FKL" over "just use FKL". Full-vocabulary reverse KL OPD is also never trained, only argued about in Appendix D.

**Statistical and metric issues.** Best@8 is a bootstrap estimate 1 − (1 − c_i/8)⁸, not the pass@8 of the eight samples actually drawn; the paper is explicit about this, which is good, but it makes Best@8 numbers incomparable with the pass@8 values in most OPD papers. AIME24–26 have 15–30 problems each, so a single problem is worth 3–7 points; the 8B AIME24 Best@8 "gain" of 79.77 vs 79.84 (i.e., a 0.07-pt loss) and AIME26 Best@8 71.56 vs 72.07 for AOPD illustrate how much of the per-benchmark table is noise. The headline ranges (1.38–2.50 Avg@8) are honest about this by being small, but the "best at all three scales" framing in the text is softened by several cells where they are not best. Also, Table 1b shows OPD *reducing* AMC23 Avg@8 below the SFT checkpoint at 1.7B (44.69 and 45.94 vs 46.56), which undercuts the framing that routing fixes OPD rather than merely damaging it less.

**Significance.** If the M_top-p diagnostic holds up, it is a cheap, model-agnostic monitor for OPD runs — arguably more valuable than the routing method itself, since it requires only teacher top-p log-probs on a held-out prefix set. The routing gain (+1.7 to +2.5 Avg@8 for 2.5–6.8% step-time overhead) is real but modest, and on code it is concentrated in LiveCodeBench, the one benchmark where OPD is furthest from saturation; that is a plausible and interpretable pattern rather than a suspicious one, but it also means the method's value is largely "rescues long-form generation on hard problems".

**Red flags.** Test-set threshold tuning; no All-FKL baseline row; no cross-family teacher (all method experiments use Qwen3-8B teaching Qwen3, so teacher–student tokenizer/template alignment is trivially good — the EOS mismatch case study is the exception, and it is a template artifact, not a distributional one); one-step-only validation of the cross-position theory; and no seed/variance reporting anywhere, despite per-benchmark cells that clearly differ by less than one problem.

## Strengths

- The theory is not decorative: Prop. 1's p(1−p)² bound, the watershed τ, and Theorem 1 each generate a prediction (Δπ peaks at intermediate π; student mode always moves against the advantage; low-probability teacher tokens get no correction) that the paper then checks on 786K real tokens.
- Honest formulation of the objective: Appendix B.1 states plainly that Eq. 2 truncates the reward-to-go and is "unbiased for Eq. 2, but not for the exact gradient of Eq. 1" — a distinction many OPD papers blur.
- The EOS case study is a genuinely convincing mechanism demonstration, with two controls that both remove the collapse (0.95 → 0.25 max-length rollouts) and an injected-mismatch experiment showing ordinary tokens are harmless (`\n\n` 1.8% → 0.02%). That is the kind of ablation that separates "mismatch matters" from "mismatch on load-bearing tokens matters".
- M_top-p is a better-motivated routing signal than teacher entropy, and the paper proves it rather than asserts it (Fig. 2c, Table 9, Table 10).
- Cost accounting is specific and credible: +2.5–6.8% step time, unchanged peak memory, teacher scoring 6–10% of the step, with the caveat that the memory claim depends on the fixed vLLM budget.
- Reproducibility: full hyperparameter tables for both SFT and OPD, named checkpoints, named baselines with their thresholds (EOPD τ = 1.0, AOPD advantage threshold 0), and the note that Best@8 is a bootstrap estimate.

## Limitations

- The theory is single-state and assumes independent logits per state; the paper's own measurement (~50% sign agreement) shows the assumption fails at the LLM level, so the analysis explains local weakness but not realized updates. The cross-position bridge is a first-order expansion validated on one SGD step, one batch, and (for the interaction matrices) four pairs.
- Routing thresholds are tuned on the evaluation benchmarks at 1.7B and transferred untouched to 4B/8B; no held-out tuning set, no per-scale re-tuning, and no variance/seed reporting.
- Missing baselines that matter for the claim: All-FKL never appears as a table row, and TrOPD / TIP / Teachability-Aware OPD / REOPOLD are discussed but not run. Full-vocabulary reverse KL is analyzed in Appendix D but not trained.
- All method experiments use Qwen3-8B as teacher with Qwen3 students; cross-family teachers (e.g., DeepSeek) appear only in the diagnostic figures, so the "teacher–student mismatch" regime studied is narrower than the framing suggests.
- Training is short (200 steps Base, 100 steps SFT) and single-dataset per task (DAPO for math, CodeR1-12k for code); the long-run behavior — whether routing merely delays the collapse — is not established.
- Table 1b undercuts part of the story: at 1.7B with SFT init, OPD and the routed variant both land below the SFT checkpoint on AMC23 Avg@8 (44.69 / 45.94 vs 46.56).
- The method needs teacher top-K log-probs (K = 16) at every position to evaluate the routing signals, which is a real infra requirement, and the "unchanged peak memory" claim is conditional on a fixed vLLM teacher budget rather than being intrinsic.

## Relevance

正中 "RL Post-Training Dynamics & Failures" 这一方向：它把 on-policy distillation 的失效（mode suppression、spurious agreement、长度爆炸）拆成局部 softmax 动力学、跨位置梯度干扰、on-policy 轨迹漂移三层，并给出可在线监控的信号 M_top-p。对关注 post-training 训练不稳定检测与干预的读者，这篇的诊断部分（尤其是 EOS 不匹配导致高重叠假象、覆盖度而非熵才是失配指示器）比其 +1.7~2.5 点的增益更值得读。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of the 1.7B gain survives if ε₁, ε₂, ε₃ are selected on a held-out set (e.g., MATH-500) instead of AIME/AMC? Is Table 8's monotone-in-ε₁ pattern still there?
- Does the advantage persist past 200 steps, or does routing simply delay the collapse — i.e., plot completion ratio and M_top-p at 600+ steps?
- What does All-FKL actually score at matched compute? If All-FKL is within noise of routing, the contribution reduces to the diagnosis, not the method.
- Is M_top-p also predictive for GRPO/RLVR collapse, or is it specific to distillation? A single cheap experiment on a GRPO run would substantially widen the impact.
- Why does the method's gain concentrate on LiveCodeBench and vanish on HumanEval+/MBPP+? Is it a long-generation effect, or is LiveCodeBench simply where OPD is unsaturated and any stabilization helps?
- The termination-token fix (align EOS or mask its gradient) is essentially free and removes a 0.95 → 0.25 max-length-rollout failure. Why is this not the headline recommendation rather than a single-table case study?
- How sensitive is the watershed analysis to the top-p threshold p? The paper fixes p = 0.9 in all method runs and only probes p = 0.95 in Table 9.
