> **一句话总结：** Weave 把 MoE 层的通信/计算划分从"编译期固定"改成"启动时按路由统计在线决策"，在 SM 与 warp 两个粒度上裁剪 warp 比例、并按 head/middle/tail 三个阶段动态切换 warp 角色，在 8×SM103 上比 DeepGEMM-MegaMoE 平均快 1.30×（最高 1.97×），在 8×SM90 上快 1.24×，在 16×CDNA3 上比官方 RCCL+AITER 最快路径快 1.44×（最高 2.87×）。

> **TL;DR:** Weave is a persistent MoE megakernel whose warp assignment is chosen at launch from runtime routing counts: a spatial schedule splits comm vs. compute/epilogue warps within each SM (and mixes ratios across SMs), a temporal schedule lets all warps do the head pull, hands the output scatter to freed comm warps at large token counts, and replaces MegaMoE's all-GPU barrier with per-token ready counts. On 30 MoE-layer workloads (six models, S=32–20K, real ShareGPT routing), it beats DeepGEMM-MegaMoE by 1.30× geomean (1.05–1.97×) on 8× SM103 and 1.24× (1.01–1.72×) on 8× SM90, and beats the fastest official RCCL+AITER path by 1.44× geomean (up to 2.87×) on 16× CDNA3, at a 1.86–3.65 µs on-device decision cost.

## 核心贡献

- **空间调度（SM 内到 SM 间）**：不再整块 SM 划给通信，而是每个 SM 内只保留少量 TMA/MMA/调度 warp 常驻，其余 warp 在 `(comm, epi)` 两种比例间切换（SM103 取 `(4,8)` 或 `(8,4)`，SM90 `(2,16)`/`(10,8)`，CDNA3 `(0,8)`/`(4,4)`），并允许同一 GPU 上不同 SM 采用不同比例，从而在不增加 kernel 变体的情况下细调整卡通信 warp 数 `Nc`。
- **时间调度（相位感知的 warp 角色切换）**：head 阶段所有 warp（含 epilogue warp）一起抢远程 token，借 `setmaxnreg` 的寄存器预算差异编译出三套 pull 例程；middle 阶段在 `epi scatter` 与 `comm scatter` 之间二选一（小 S 走 epilogue、大 S 交给空闲通信 warp，避免 12.9%–34% 的空转）；tail 阶段用 per-token 到达计数替代 MegaMoE 的全 GPU barrier。
- **融合代价模型**：CTA-0 启动时读取本层路由计数 `m_e, M, D_in, M_out`，用统一公式对 `(r, s)` 候选打分（pull 受 `min(B_link, N_c·b_pull)` 约束，GEMM 受 tile 数与 padding 影响，SM90/CDNA3 上 MMA 与 epilogue 共享寄存器因此需相加），在 device 上选出 `arg min T(r,s)`。
- **对三类硬件做了真实测量驱动的设计空间裁剪**：给出 SM103 上 pull 带宽在 888 warps 处饱和于 780 GB/s、而 4–32 warps/SM 的分布差异 <1% 的校准结论，说明"调 warp 比例"必须配合 epilogue 带宽而非一味堆通信 warp。
- **量化了 MegaMoE 的三段气泡**：head/middle/tail 合计浪费 32.8%–34.1% 的 warp-time（S≤1K）、25.1%（4K）、21.2%（8K）、15.8%（20K），为"运行时调度"这一命题提供了可核查的动机。
- **与 EPLB 正交并给出组合数据**：32 个冗余专家把 max/mean 不均衡从 1.37 降到 1.27，代价是 +12.9% 平均延迟，Weave 仍在原始路由下快 1.274×、在 EPLB 下快 1.128×。

## Key Contributions

- **A two-level spatial schedule.** Communication and compute/epilogue warps are partitioned *within* each SM (two hardware-supported ratios per architecture, see Table I) and mixed *across* SMs by having each persistent CTA compare its hardware SM ID against a cost-model threshold. This keeps every SM's Tensor/Matrix Cores active — the explicit contrast with mKernel, DeepEP, NanoFlow and FlashMoE, which dedicate whole SMs (or thread blocks) to communication.
- **A phase-aware temporal schedule.** All warps participate in the head pull (compiled into three register-budget-specialized routines: 72 regs for TMA/MMA, 168 for comm, 208 for epi on SM103), warp roles are released mid-layer so idle comm warps can absorb the output scatter, and the tail uses per-token arrival counts plus hidden-dimension slicing instead of an all-GPU barrier.
- **A fused, on-device cost model** that scores joint `(spatial ratio, scatter path)` candidates from the layer's actual routing counts, executed by CTA 0 in 1.86–3.65 µs, i.e. small enough to sit inside the kernel launch rather than requiring a host round-trip.
- **A cross-vendor implementation** in CUDA C++ (SM103/SM90) and HIP C++ (CDNA3), including an honest note that AITER-1stage fails a FP64 correctness check on MiniMax-M3 and that SGLang+MORI exceeds the cluster's 64 MiB RDMA MR limit.

## Methodology

The paper's unit of analysis is one MoE layer under expert parallelism, decomposed into dispatch (peer-to-peer input pull), per-expert GEMM0 → SiLU·mul → GEMM1, cross-GPU output scatter, and top-k weighted reduce. Weave keeps the MegaMoE skeleton (persistent CTAs claiming tasks from a global queue via `atomicAdd`, comm warps pulling remote tokens through registers rather than shared memory, cross-GPU token deduplication with local-first worklist ordering, per-expert block size `BMe ∈ {32,64,128,256}`) and replaces the two static decisions.

**Spatial.** For each GPU the kernel picks `r = (N_c, N_e)`, the number of comm and compute/epilogue warps per SM. Because hardware constraints quantize this (SM103: 16 warps/SM, multiples of four, so `(4,8)` or `(8,4)`), the fine control comes from letting different SMs run different ratios — the effective GPU-wide `N_c` varies continuously while all matrix units stay busy. Peer-to-peer queues decouple sender and receiver warp counts, so each GPU's choice is local and independent.

**Temporal.** Head: every warp pulls; the three register budgets are compiled separately so high-register warps keep more loads in flight without spilling. Middle: `epi scatter` (epilogue warps write outputs directly) versus `comm scatter` (freed comm warps claim ready outputs through the queue and stream them via shared memory under ongoing GEMMs). Tail: each token is reduced as soon as its `k` expert outputs arrive; every warp group joins the reduce through the global queue, and tokens are sliced along the hidden dimension `N` to keep all warps fed.

**Cost model.** Total time is
`T(r,epi) = max(T_pull(N_c), T_comp(r) + T_scat(N_e))` and
`T(r,comm) = max(T_pull(N_c) + T_scat(N_c), T_comp(r) + T_hoff)`,
with `T_pull = D_in·H·b_a / min(B_link, N_c·b_pull)` and `T_scat = M_out·H·b_o / min(B_link, N·b_scat)`; `T_comp` is `max(T_mma, T_epi)` on SM103 (TMEM decouples drain) but `T_mma + T_epi` on SM90/CDNA3 (shared registers serialize them). Tile count `K_e(r) = ⌈m_e/BM(r)⌉` means a larger `N_c` shrinks `BM` and raises both weight re-reads and padding, which is why the model cannot simply maximize comm warps.

**Evaluation.** 8× SM103 (148 SMs, 2032 MHz, CUDA 13.2) and 8× SM90 (78 SMs, 1830 MHz, CUDA 13.3) over NVLink for EP8; 16× CDNA3 (80 CUs, two nodes, 8× xGMI per node) under EP16 with ROCm 7.2.0. Six model configurations from 35B to 2.4T parameters (Table III), real ShareGPT routing, `randn` weights/activations, FP8/NVFP4 on SM103 and FP8 elsewhere, `S = batch × sequence` from 32 to 20K, median slowest-GPU latency after warmup.

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline on 8× SM103 (EP8, 30 workloads, S = 32/128/512/2K/8K):** Weave wins all 30 against DeepGEMM-MegaMoE, geomean **1.30×** and range **1.05–1.97×**; against FlashInfer the geomean is **1.40×** (1.11–2.76×) and against the per-workload fastest baseline **1.22×** (1.05–1.97×). The gain is S-dependent: at `S ≥ 8K` (12 workloads) it reaches **1.18–1.97× (1.55× geomean)** because MegaMoE stalls on epilogue scatter, versus only **1.05–1.33×** at the smallest token counts where the layer is HBM/latency-bound.
- **The headline 1.30× is not uniform across models — the largest model gains least.** Reading Fig. 7's per-cell speedups, Qwen3.8-2.4T (E=512, k=10) sits at **1.05, 1.06, 1.05, 1.11, 1.21×** across S (≈1.09× geomean), while Qwen3.5-35B (E=256, k=8) reaches **1.30, 1.26, 1.17, 1.59, 1.97×** (≈1.42×). The 1.97× "up to" number is the 35B model at S=16K, not the flagship 2.4T case.
- **Against coarse-grained re-implementations on SM103:** SGLang (DeepEP+DeepGEMM) **4.28× geomean (2.15–8.81×)**, mKernel **3.26× (2.48–4.96×)**, FlashMoE **3.62× (2.69–5.29×)**, NanoFlow **5.00× (2.03–13.04×)**, and a five-kernel DeepEP/DeepGEMM CUDA-Graph+PDL pipeline **2.30× (1.82–3.00×)** — though the authors note disabling PDL changes latency by only −0.5%, so that last baseline is essentially a multi-kernel graph.
- **8× SM90 (EP8):** **1.24× geomean (1.01–1.72×)** over FP8 MegaMoE and **1.95× (1.03–3.28×)** over FlashInfer v0.6.8, but with the opposite S-profile: **1.37–1.38× at S ≤ 128** and only **1.06× at S ≥ 8K**, because SM90's compute-to-NVLink ratio (0.92 vs. 12.6 TFLOPS/GB/s on SM103) leaves MegaMoE already at up to 85.0% Tensor Core utilization. End-to-end (Table IV) decode at S=128/4K context improves **1.08× geomean** (e.g. Qwen3.8-2.4T 1.40 → 1.28 ms, MiniMax-M3 0.91 → 0.81 ms) while prefill at S=16K improves only **1.01× geomean** (Qwen3.8-2.4T 19.84 → 19.76 ms, i.e. 1.00×).
- **16× CDNA3 (two-node EP16):** **1.44× geomean (up to 2.87×)** over the per-workload fastest official RCCL+AITER path, **1.43× (1.01–3.41×)** over AITER-1stage on the 25 cells that pass its FP64 check, and **1.67× (1.13–2.99×)** over the two-stage CK/vLLM path. Sub-range geomeans: **1.91× for S ≤ 128**, **1.20× at S=1K**, **1.19× for S ≥ 8K**.
- **Progressive ablation, 48 workloads per platform:** on SM103 geomean falls **1.301× → 1.192× (−S) → 1.010× (−S−T) → 0.996× (−S−T−C)**, and at `S ≥ 8K` **1.638× → 1.416× → 1.185× → 1.148×**. On CDNA3 the static-default variant drops to **0.848× overall and 0.638× at S ≥ 8K**, i.e. without the cost model the fused kernel is markedly *slower* than MegaMoE. The three bubbles removed at SM103 S≤128 account for 1.076× vs. 0.915× (−S vs. −S−T).
- **Cost-model fidelity and cost:** 1.301× geomean versus a **1.332× exhaustive oracle** (2.6% mean, 2.4% geomean selection error, **38/48 exact** matches) on SM103; SM90 reaches **99.5% of oracle (1.164× vs. 1.170×)**, 32/48 exact and 41/48 within 1%. Decision time is **3.65 µs** median on SM103 (3.58–4.29), **1.86 µs** on SM90, **3.00 µs** on CDNA3, flat from S=32 to 20K; measured end-to-end overhead is −0.08 µs (±1.3%, paired) on SM103 and +0.6 µs (0.1%) on SM90.
- **Motivating measurement:** on Qwen3.5-397B, MegaMoE's head/middle/tail bubbles consume **32.8%–34.1%** of warp-time at `S ≤ 1K`, **25.1%** at 4K, **21.2%** at 8K and **15.8%** at 20K; per-GPU comm/compute demand in MiniMax-M3 at S=4K deviates visibly from the mean (Fig. 3c), which is the load skew the spatial schedule targets.

## Authors & Credibility

A large SJTU-led systems group (Jingwen Leng, Minyi Guo, Shixuan Sun) with NUS (Yangjie Zhou), Fudan (Peng Yu) and four Alibaba Group co-authors (Xiu Lin, Yinghao Yu, Guodong Yang, Liping Zhang) — the Alibaba presence plus the use of production model configurations (Qwen3.5/3.8, DSKv4-Flash, MiniMax-M3) suggests the work is aimed at real serving stacks rather than a toy kernel. The paper is dense with calibrations (bandwidth-vs-warp-count curves, per-SM epilogue throughput, TLB/register budgets) rather than just end numbers, and it volunteers unflattering details: its own ablation without any contribution scores 0.996× (slightly *worse* than MegaMoE) on SM103, and it reports cells where it is only 1.00–1.02×. That kind of reporting is a credibility signal. Offsetting it: no artifact is released ("we will open-source our code soon"), and every one of the coarse-grained baselines it beats by 3–5× was re-implemented and tuned by the authors themselves.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty is real but incremental in kind.** MegaMoE already co-locates comm and GEMM warps on the same SM; Weave's delta is (i) letting the ratio vary at launch and across SMs, (ii) releasing warps from their roles mid-layer, and (iii) swapping a global barrier for per-token ready counters. Each is a well-understood technique transplanted into a megakernel; the contribution is the combination plus the demonstration that 1.86–3.65 µs of on-device search is affordable. The paper says this plainly, which is to its credit.

**The strongest numbers rest on the weakest evidence.** The 3.26×/3.62×/5.00× wins over mKernel, FlashMoE and NanoFlow all come from "tuned SM103 re-implementations" by the authors, with no upstream-code comparison and no second independent implementation. A 5× gap over NanoFlow on hardware NanoFlow was never tuned for is exactly the pattern that survives poorly under scrutiny. The defensible comparison is the 1.30× over DeepGEMM-MegaMoE, which is the same-team lineage and the closest thing to an apples-to-apples baseline — and even there the margin is 1.05× on the commercially most relevant configuration (Qwen3.8-2.4T, small S).

**The S-profile is being averaged away.** The geomean pools 30 cells spanning a 600× range of `S` and six models of very different arithmetic intensity; the win rate is 30/30 but the distribution is bimodal (1.05–1.10× for large models at small S, 1.5–2.0× for small models at large S). No per-regime geomean is given for SM103 beyond the `S ≥ 8K` split, and end-to-end prefill on SM90 is 1.00–1.02× — i.e. in the serving configuration where MoE is 40–67% of prefill latency, Weave buys almost nothing because attention dominates. The "up to 1.97×" in the abstract is a 35B model at 16K tokens, stated without that context.

**Coverage gap in the EP sweep.** NVIDIA is only tested at EP8 on a single NVLink node; EP16 is only tested on AMD, where the strongest competitor (RCCL+AITER) serializes dispatch/fused_moe/combine as three kernels — a structurally weaker opponent than MegaMoE. The regime where communication volume and inter-node skew should be largest (multi-node NVIDIA EP16/EP32) is untested, which is precisely where the routing-adaptive claim would be most valuable.

**Schedule space is tiny, so "oracle" is a low bar.** The candidate set is two intra-SM ratios × two scatter paths per architecture, plus an SM-ID threshold. A 2.6% gap to an exhaustive oracle over four-ish candidates is a much easier target than matching a continuous optimum, and the "oracle" is computed over the same 48 workloads used to report the gap, so it is not a held-out estimate.

**Unaddressed operational concerns.** (1) Selection happens per launch from that layer's routing counts; real serving captures the whole forward pass in CUDA Graphs, and a per-launch on-device branch plus a CTA-0 scan is not obviously graph-capturable — the paper benchmarks *against* a Graph+PDL pipeline but never reports Weave under graph capture. (2) The threshold that decides each SM's ratio needs per-GPU calibration; robustness to clock throttling, MPS sharing, or a different NVLink topology is not studied. (3) All activations are `randn`; only routing is real (ShareGPT). (4) AITER-1stage fails a FP64 check on MiniMax-M3, so the CDNA3 baseline envelope is computed over 25 of 30 cells — the "fastest baseline" therefore differs per cell, which flatters the 1.44× geomean slightly relative to a uniform baseline set.

**What would convince me:** an upstream NanoFlow/mKernel build on SM103, a multi-node NVIDIA EP16 run, a CUDA-Graph-captured end-to-end serving measurement (tokens/s at fixed SLO, not layer latency), and a held-out cost-model evaluation on workloads not used in the 48-cell calibration.

## Strengths

- The motivating analysis is unusually concrete: warp-time bubble accounting (32.8%–34.1% at small S down to 15.8% at 20K), the 780 GB/s / 888-warp saturation point, and the 4.6 → 5.6 → 6.8 TB/s epilogue scaling with 4/8/12 warps each justify a specific design choice rather than gesturing at "imbalance".
- Genuinely cross-vendor: two NVIDIA architectures plus CDNA3, with architecture-specific cost-model terms (`max` vs. `sum` of `T_mma` and `T_epi`) rather than a single formula ported blindly.
- The temporal schedule is the most interesting idea — enlisting epilogue warps into the head pull and turning comm warps into scatters is a real structural change to MegaMoE's role lock, and the `S=128` vs. `S=20K` scatter crossover (Fig. 3b) explains why it must be dynamic.
- Honest ablation accounting: −S−T−C at 0.996× on SM103 and 0.638× at large S on CDNA3 shows the authors are measuring their own contribution against a fair, deliberately weakened version of themselves.
- The cost model is cheap enough to be usable (`1.86–3.65 µs`, flat in `S`, overhead hidden on SM103) and its accuracy is reported against an explicit oracle with exact-match counts, not just a geomean.
- Orthogonality to EPLB is demonstrated rather than asserted, including the uncomfortable detail that EPLB costs 12.9% latency and shrinks Weave's margin from 1.274× to 1.128×.

## Limitations

- **No public artifact**, so the 3–5× margins over re-implemented baselines cannot be checked; the paper itself defers this to "soon".
- **Latency-only, layer-centric evaluation.** No throughput/goodput under a real scheduler, no KV-cache or continuous-batching interaction, no tail-latency or variance reporting beyond "median slowest GPU". End-to-end numbers exist only for 8× SM90 and only for MoE-share-of-latency reasoning.
- **Single-node NVIDIA.** Multi-node NVIDIA EP is not evaluated; multi-node is only exercised on CDNA3 against a serializer-style baseline.
- **Synthetic numerics.** `randn` weights and activations mean no accuracy or numerical-stability claims are possible; the only correctness signal is a FP64 spot check applied to a *baseline* (AITER-1stage), not to Weave.
- **Missing operational ablations:** CUDA-Graph capture, threshold sensitivity, clock/topology variation, and behavior when several MoE layers with different expert counts run back-to-back are all unexamined.
- **The stated limitation is the least interesting one.** The paper flags only "not yet evaluated on non-GPU accelerators", while the more consequential gaps (graph capture, multi-node NVIDIA, upstream baselines) go unmentioned.

## Relevance

面向 MoE 推理系统的底层 kernel 调度工作，属于"用运行时信息替代编译期静态划分"这一主线；如果你关心 expert parallelism 下的通信-计算重叠、megakernel 设计或 SM103/CDNA3 这类新硬件的实际可用带宽，这篇的测量数据和代价模型都值得细读。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. How does Weave behave under CUDA Graph capture? Is the per-launch CTA-0 selection expressible as a conditional node, and what happens to the 3.65 µs decision when 60 MoE layers are replayed per decode step?
2. What is the geomean restricted to the large models (Qwen3.8-2.4T, MiniMax-M3, Qwen3.5-397B) and to `S ≤ 1K`, i.e. the production decode regime rather than the pooled 30-cell average?
3. Does the 1.30× hold against an *upstream* NanoFlow or mKernel build on SM103, and how much of the 3.26–5.00× is re-implementation quality?
4. How sensitive is the spatial threshold to SM clock throttling, and does the chosen ratio remain valid across the ~100 µs of a single layer under DVFS?
5. Would a continuous relaxation of the ratio (e.g. 3 or 4 ratios per SM, or per-warp role churn within the middle phase) recover the remaining 2.6% to the exhaustive oracle, and does the gain survive at 1.86 µs decision cost?
6. On a multi-node NVIDIA EP16/EP32 fabric (IB rather than NVLink), do the head-pull and tail-reduce redesigns matter more or less than they do under EP8?
