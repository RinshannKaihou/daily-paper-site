Why this matterstoday

A feature can leave reported accuracy untouched while changing which users get the right answer. Accuracy-only serving audits cannot see that.

Even known-lossy FP8 weights pass this study's accuracy test.
Critical edgeread with care

The negative claim rests only on non-significance: upper bounds reach +0.33 pts, so a genuine 0.2-point excess would be invisible at 3,380 items.

No power analysis is given for the effect sizes the paper means to certify.

01 Method — move the unit from accuracy to the graded outcome

ReferenceR1–R3 fresh processes, canonical order; S1–S2 shuffled
Candidateone factor changed, fresh process each run
Pairper-item graded outcomes differ → flip share
Subtractexcess over floor; cluster bootstrap
  • Graded flip, not text equality. For candidate C against replicas R1…Rk, each item's flip probability is the share of (C, Rj) pairs with a different graded outcome; the rate averages over items. Accuracy is a paired side metric, not the primary decision variable.
  • One 4B model, one engine, one GPU. Qwen3-4B-Instruct 2507 in bf16, vLLM 0.33.0 offline LLM.generate, a single RTX 5070 Ti (16 GB), greedy decoding (T=0, seed 0). A task's requests are submitted together; batches form by continuous batching as under load.
  • Nine single-factor candidates. Seven features vLLM documents as exact — prefix caching, chunked prefill (512 tokens), ≤16 concurrent sequences, CUDA graphs off, n-gram (4), EAGLE-3 (3), draft model (4) — plus FP8 KV cache and FP8 weights as positive controls.
  • Two noise floors. Restart pairs measure a fresh process at fixed order (1.38%); scheduling pairs shuffle the same requests (1.72%). The paper's point: the cheaper control understates what production traffic already does.
  • Control arm. With VLLM_BATCH_INVARIANT=1 the whole 14-run design repeats. Floors, if the mechanism is arithmetic, must go to zero — and they do.

Accuracy can't see it

Reference accuracy 85.94% (95% CI 84.26–87.53); every candidate's 90% interval lies within ±1 pt. Known-lossy FP8 weights sit at −0.29 pts.

Churn is symmetric

FP8 KV vs R1: 63 items turn right, 61 turn wrong (exact McNemar p = 0.93). Stable accuracy is cancellation, not absence of effect.

Speed sits beside the flips

The two fastest configurations — FP8 weights 1.77×, FP8 KV 1.66× — are exactly the two that double the flip rate. EAGLE-3 1.16×; draft model 0.50×.

04 The gate, reported honestly

default kernels

Mostly inconclusive — one false alarm

  • 7 of 9 configurations land INCONCLUSIVE at 3,380 items — the gate mostly cannot separate a feature from noise.
  • Holding out restart R2 returns FAIL on an unchanged configuration: +0.96 pts, upper bound +1.34.
  • Cause: the three restart pairs already differ on 25–62 items, and the quietest pair becomes the floor.
batch-invariant kernels

Zero floor, clean separations

  • Restart and scheduling floors are both exactly 0.00%; all five null comparisons pass at +0.00.
  • All seven exact features reproduce the reference text token-for-token on 3,380 items.
  • FP8 still flips: KV 3.91%, weights 3.43% — both fail the flip test, as C2 predicted.
takeaway

Certify where the floor is zero

  • Two admissible protocols: run under batch-invariant kernels (~4% throughput cost), or estimate the floor from ≥3 replicas over all pairs.
  • A single-replica-pair floor is not safe — it fails a configuration nobody changed.
  • Preregistered claim C3 is refuted in the default arm and confirmed in the control arm; the paper says so.

05 Strengths

  • Right object of measurement. Per-item graded outcomes expose features that aggregate accuracy hides — the whole reason every candidate can pass an accuracy check while changing who is right.
  • Two floors, not one. Restart 1.38% vs scheduling 1.72% shows the cheaper control understates noise production already experiences, and it flips the verdict on every exact feature.
  • A decisive ablation. Batch-invariant kernels give zero floor, seven features bit-exact on 3,380 items, FP8 unaffected — mechanism attribution, not correlation.
  • Statistical hygiene. Preregistration at a commit hash, paired per-item differences, cluster bootstrap by MMLU subject, one-sided bounds in the claimed direction, kill checks with engine counters.
  • Plain reporting of failure. The refuted confirmatory claim and the null margin predictor are stated, not reinterpreted; the amendment is dated and scoped.

06 Limitations

  • One model, one version, one GPU. A 4B model on a 16 GB RTX 5070 Ti with vLLM 0.33.0; scaling to larger models and other kernels is untested.
  • Single candidate run per feature. Three restart replicas make the floor itself noisy — the R2 hold-out FAIL is the symptom — and no power analysis is given.
  • Greedy decoding only. Temperature > 0, beam search, and the distribution-shift question production teams actually face are untouched.
  • Request order is the only scheduling perturbation. Arrival times, preemption and mixed lengths are expected to raise the floor further.
  • Not independently checkable yet. Code and 3,380 per-item outputs are promised with a public release / on request; the preregistration is a bare commit hash.
  • Scope. Five exactly-graded benchmarks; judge-graded, preference-graded and agentic tasks are excluded by construction.

07 Questions for further exploration

Q1

How does flip rate scale with concurrency heterogeneity — a sweep over max-num-seqs 16 → 256 and deliberately mixed request lengths?

Q2

What item count certifies a 0.2-point excess at 80% power? Upper bounds of +0.33 at 3,380 items suggest an order of magnitude more.

Q3

Does the effect survive sampling (T=0.7, top-p 0.95), where the question becomes a distribution shift rather than a token flip?

Q4

Are flipped answers better, worse, or equally defensible under a stronger judge? McNemar's 63/61 balance hints at coin tosses.

Q5

Why is the restart floor (1.38%) below the scheduling floor (1.72%)? Isolating whether order changes kernel selection or batch composition would sharpen the two-channel claim.

Q6

Would a 30B+ model flip less (larger margins) or more (longer derivations)? The paper calls this its most important open generalization.
