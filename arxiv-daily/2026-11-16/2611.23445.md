> **一句话总结：** 本文在完整 50,000 张 ImageNet-1k 验证集上解剖 EfficientNet-B0 的亚 4-bit 崩塌：统一 W3 掉到 0.30%（−72.56 pp）、W4 掉到 23.03%（−49.83 pp），把仅占 0.34% 权重（14 KB）的早期 3×3 depthwise 层保护在 W8 就能在 W4 下回补 +42.92 pp，而「W8 保护 + MSE 裁剪 + 1K 张 BN 重校准 + 82 层 DP 分配 + 1 epoch QAT」的完整流水线在 3.56 平均比特下只能恢复到 68.98%，仍留 3.88 pp 硬缺口；同时 CPU 实测显示 4-bit/3-bit 因缺少原生亚字节 depthwise 内核反而比 FP32 慢 1.48×–21.5×。

> **TL;DR:** On the full 50k-image ImageNet-1k validation set, a mature scratch-trained EfficientNet-B0 (72.864% FP32 top-1) collapses to 23.03% under uniform W4 (−49.83 pp) and 0.30% under uniform W3 (−72.56 pp), while full-integer INT8 is essentially free (72.81%, −0.06 pp, −70.7% size, −32.2% latency). A 46-arm layerwise sensitivity screen localizes the damage to early 3×3 depthwise filters: pinning 13,952 weights (0.34%, 14 KB) to W8 rescues W4 from 23.03% to 65.95% (+42.92 pp) — and FP16 on those same layers adds only +0.026 pp. The full recovery waterfall (W8 anchor → per-channel MSE clipping +9.00 pp → 1k-image BN recalibration +3.94 pp → 82-layer DP allocation +3.85 pp → 1-epoch QAT +2.91 pp) reaches 68.98% at 3.56 mean bits, i.e. 94.7% of the lost accuracy but still 3.88 pp below FP32.

## 核心贡献

- **给出亚 4-bit 崩塌的「解剖图」而非一句结论**：46 臂单阶段扰动筛选（2,000 张类平衡子集）加 6 个敏感掩码在完整 50k 上复核，定位出崩塌集中在早期 3×3 depthwise 与 Stage 1–2 的 pointwise 投影，而不是均匀分布于全网。
- **量化早期层的「精度上限」**：将 Tier 0（0.34% 权重、13,952 个参数）从 W8 提升到 FP16 只换来 +0.026 pp（64.118% vs 64.092%，95% CI [−0.037, +0.089]），却多花 58.7 KB——说明早期空间滤波只需 8-bit 整数精度，没必要上浮点。
- **建立五级恢复瀑布并测出每一级的独立增益**：MSE 裁剪 +9.004 pp、1K 张 BN 重校准 +9.004 pp、两者联合 +12.942 pp（配对交互 −5.066 pp）、82 层 DP 分配 +3.848 pp、1 epoch QAT（含 post-QAT BN）+2.912 pp。
- **在等模型体积下对比启发式分层与 DP 分配**：3.56 bits / 2.35 MB 的 DP0.5 达 67.94% PTQ，同尺寸 3.55 bits 的 stage 级启发式只有 64.09%，DP 净胜 +3.848 pp。
- **点出「存储赢了、延迟输了」的硬件现实鸿沟**：INT8 是唯一同时降体积降延迟的配置；WI4（3.59 MB）与打包 INT3（2.47 MB）在标准 ARM/x86 CPU 上因软件解包分别慢 1.48× 与 21.5×。
- **披露早期 checkpoint 会系统性误判量化脆弱性**：在 epoch-1（5.88%–18.88% top-1）上 INT4/INT3 看似「几乎无损」（<1.6 pp），一旦收敛到 70%+ 就掉 24–51 pp。

## Key Contributions

- A layerwise anatomy of sub-4-bit collapse: a 46-arm localized perturbation screen on a class-balanced 2,000-image subset, with the six most sensitive masks verified on the disjoint 48,000-image complement and the full 50,000 images, showing damage concentrated in early 3×3 depthwise filters and Stage 1–2 pointwise projections rather than spread uniformly over 82 layers.
- A quantified precision ceiling for early layers: promoting Tier 0 from W8 to FP16 changes top-1 by +0.026 pp (64.118% vs 64.092%, paired 95% CI [−0.037, +0.089]) at a cost of 58.7 KB, so 8-bit integer is sufficient for early spatial filtering.
- A decomposable five-stage recovery waterfall with independently measured deltas: early-W8 anchoring, per-output-channel MSE clipping (α* = argmin over [0.5, 1.0] of the L2 rounding error), 1,000-image BN moment recalibration, 82-layer dynamic-programming bit allocation under mean-bit budgets (DP0.5/DP1.0/DP2.0), and 1-epoch QAT plus post-QAT BN recalibration.
- A 16-arm full-validation factorial (4 precision profiles × 2 scaling rules × 2 BN regimes) that isolates a sub-additive clipping × BN interaction of −5.066 pp (95% CI [−5.531, −4.601]).
- A same-size comparison showing DP allocation beats a stage-level heuristic map by +3.848 pp at 3.56 vs 3.55 mean bits (both 2.35 MB), i.e. where you spend bits matters more than how many you spend.
- A systems-level refutation of the storage-equals-speed assumption: INT8 is Pareto-dominant, while WI4 and packed INT3 pay 1.48×–21.5× unpacking overhead on commodity CPUs that lack sub-byte depthwise kernels.
- A reproducibility warning that lightly trained checkpoints mask quantization fragility: epoch-1 pilots (5.88%–18.88% FP32) lost <1.6 pp under INT4/INT3, whereas converged checkpoints lost 24.39–51.15 pp.

## Methodology

**Backbones and evaluation protocol.** All numbers come from the complete 50,000-image ImageNet-1k validation set with deterministic preprocessing. Reference models are scratch-trained ReLU6/SGD EfficientNet-B0/B1/B2 for 88 epochs: B0 (5.32M params, 224×224) at 72.864% top-1 / 91.106% top-5 (epoch-86 checkpoint, 72.840% at epoch 88); B1 (7.85M, 240×240) 74.908% / 92.192% (epoch 87); B2 (9.17M, 260×260) 75.952% / 92.752% (epoch 88). Two auxiliary B0 variants (hybrid-BiFPN, BiFPN-attention) are used for cross-architecture checks.

**Sensitivity screen.** 46 localized perturbation arms quantize single stages or operator families while the rest of B0 stays FP32, run on a class-balanced 2,000-image subset; the six most sensitive masks (Stage 1 W3/W4, early depthwise W3/W4, Stage 2 W3/W4) are then re-verified on the disjoint 48,000-image complement and the full 50,000 images to rule out subset artifacts.

**Quantizer.** Symmetric weight-only fake quantization, Δ = max|w|/Q_max per output channel by default; the clipping variant grid-searches a clipping ratio α ∈ [0.5, 1.0] per channel to minimize ‖w − Q_α(w)‖²; BN recalibration re-estimates running moments on a class-balanced 1,000-image training subset (Nagel et al., 2019 style). All accuracy comparisons use fake-quantized weights inside TensorFlow FP32 operators, so representational error is isolated from kernel implementation; packed storage and latency are measured separately on exported artifacts.

**Bit allocation.** All 82 weighted layers are profiled at {W3, W4, W8, FP16} (82 × 4 = 328 evaluations) under MSE clipping, then an additive surrogate of layerwise accuracy drop is optimized with a knapsack-style DP under three mean-bit budgets: DP0.5 (3.56 bits), DP1.0 (3.49), DP2.0 (3.40). The resulting tiers are Tier 0 = stem + Stage 1–2 depthwise/pointwise at W8 (0.34%, 14 KB), Tier 1 = ~55.8% of weights (mid/deep 3×3 and 5×5 depthwise, SE bottlenecks, the 1280×1000 classifier) at W4, Tier 2 = ~43.9% (wide Stage 5–7 1×1 expansion/projection) at W3.

**Factorial design.** 16 arms crossing {Heuristic Tiered, DP0.5, DP1.0, DP2.0} × {Max-Abs, Per-Channel MSE Clipping} × {stored FP32 source BN, recalibrated 1k-image BN}, all evaluated on 50,000 images. The DP0.5 profile is then fine-tuned for one epoch (80,072 gradient updates) and evaluated both with training-time EMA BN and after a 1,000-image post-QAT BN recalibration pass.

**Deployment benchmarking.** Exported LiteRT FlatBuffers (FP32, full-integer INT8, mixed WI4 = 66 INT4 pointwise/dense + 16 FP32 depthwise) and a custom packed 3-bit bitstream are benchmarked on the same host CPU over all 50,000 images, reporting p50/p95 invocation latency alongside artifact size and top-1.

## Results & Highlights

- **Uniform low-bit collapse on the full 50k validation set:** mature EfficientNet-B0 (72.864% FP32) falls to 23.03% under uniform W4 (−49.83 pp) and 0.30% under uniform W3 (−72.56 pp) with max-abs PTQ; on the same checkpoint uniform W8 stays at 72.81% and full-integer INT8 at 72.81% (−0.058 pp).
- **Early-W8 protection is the single largest lever for W4:** protecting 13,952 weights (0.34%, 14 KB) in the stem and Stage 1–2 depthwise/pointwise layers lifts whole-model W4 from 23.03% to 65.95% (+42.92 pp, mean 4.01 bits), whereas promoting that same Tier 0 from W8 to FP16 yields only +0.026 pp (64.118% vs 64.092%, 95% CI [−0.037, +0.089]) at +58.7 KB — the best baseline for that comparison being W8 itself.
- **Localized damage is highly asymmetric:** quantizing only Stage 1 (0.03% of weights) to W3 costs 47.62 pp (72.87% → 25.25%), early depthwise-only W3 costs 41.67 pp (31.20%), yet Stage 2 W4 costs only 6.06 pp (66.81%) — a ~8× spread in per-layer sensitivity at nearly identical bit budgets.
- **Clipping and BN recalibration are individually equal and jointly sub-additive:** on the 3.55-bit heuristic tiered map, MSE clipping alone gives +9.004 pp (51.150% → 60.154%) and 1,000-image BN recalibration alone also gives +9.004 pp (51.150% → 60.154%), while both together reach 64.092% (+12.942 pp) with a measured interaction of −5.066 pp (95% CI [−5.531, −4.601]).
- **DP allocation beats hand-designed tiers at matched size:** DP0.5 (3.56 bits, 2.35 MB, MSE + BN) reaches 67.94% vs 64.09% for the 3.55-bit / 2.35 MB heuristic map (+3.848 pp); tighter budgets degrade gracefully — DP1.0 (3.49 bits, 2.30 MB) 67.34% and DP2.0 (3.40 bits, 2.24 MB) 66.35%.
- **QAT adds only ~1 pp over PTQ and needs BN repair:** 1 epoch of QAT on DP0.5 scores 66.072% under training-time EMA BN statistics; a 1,000-image post-QAT BN recalibration adds +2.912 pp (95% CI [+2.606, +3.218]) to 68.984%, i.e. +1.044 pp (95% CI [+0.796, +1.292]) over same-profile PTQ (67.940%).
- **A ~3.9 pp floor remains at 3.56 mean bits:** best recovered model 68.984% vs 72.864% FP32 = −3.880 pp, and −2.544 pp vs the 71.528% 1-epoch FP32 QAT control — the paper's explicit "where recovery stops".
- **Uniform 3-bit QAT is seed-fragile and badly undershoots FP32:** on seed 20261012, delay-halfway INT3 QAT reached 64.424% vs 71.486% FP32 control (+0.364 pp over immediate 64.060%); on seed 20261013 delay *hurt* by −10.630 pp (54.612% vs 65.242% immediate), leaving B0 6.2–16.8 points below FP32 after a full epoch. A single-word factor-four logarithmic INT3 grid also lost 2.92–3.72 pp against uniform linear INT3 (8.44%/7.23% vs 11.37%/10.95%).
- **INT8 is the only Pareto-dominant deployment point:** B0 FlatBuffer 20.43 MB → 6.00 MB (−70.7%), p50 CPU latency 9.81 ms → 6.65 ms (−32.2%), top-1 72.86% → 72.81% (−0.06 pp), all 82 ops on native 8-bit SIMD; on hybrid-BiFPN B0 INT8 gives 11.88 ms → 10.28 ms (−13.5%) with −0.568 pp accuracy.
- **Sub-4-bit is slower than FP32 on commodity CPUs:** mixed WI4 (3.59 MB, −82.4%, 63.27% top-1, −9.60 pp) runs 15.43 ms p50 (+47.9% vs FP32, 2.32× slower than INT8) with 66 INT4 + 16 FP32 depthwise ops; the packed uniform INT3 bitstream (2.47 MB, −88.4%) takes 376.8 ms (21.5× FP32, p95 386.6 ms) purely in software unpacking.
- **Cross-architecture replication of the W4 cliff:** mature B1 drops 24.39 pp (74.92% → 50.53%), hybrid-BiFPN B0 drops 51.15 pp (69.99% → 18.84%), and BiFPN-attention B0 drops 22.02 pp (73.15% → 51.14%) — the collapse is not a single-model artifact.

## Authors & Credibility

Single-author preprint by Michael Schroeder, affiliated with GE Appliances (a Haier company) — an industrial/applied setting rather than an academic quantization group, which fits the paper's strongly deployment-oriented framing (LiteRT exports, FlatBuffer sizes, CPU p50/p95). The work is unusually thorough on one axis that matters for credibility: every accuracy claim is on the full 50,000-image validation set, the sensitivity screen is verified on a disjoint 48,000-image complement, confidence intervals are reported for the paired comparisons that carry the argument (−5.066 pp interaction; +2.912 pp post-QAT BN; +0.026 pp FP16), and negative results are kept in the paper (delay-QAT seed reversal, logarithmic grid losing, sub-4-bit latency regression). Checkpoints, DP bit-allocation profiles, exported FlatBuffers, and SHA-256 manifests are released on Hugging Face, with a companion paper (arXiv:2611.23446) on sparse attention and weight sharing. Against that, the single-author, non-peer-reviewed nature and the reliance on self-trained 88-epoch baselines (3.4–3.9 pp below Tan & Le's) mean the absolute accuracy numbers should be read as internally consistent rather than state-of-the-art-comparable.

## Critical Assessment

*Novelty.* The individual components are all borrowed — early-layer protection echoes HAQ/BRECQ block sensitivity, MSE clipping follows Choukroun et al. and Banner et al., BN recalibration is Nagel et al.'s DFQ recipe, DP over layer sensitivities is HAQ — and the paper does not claim otherwise. What is new is the accounting: a cleanly separated per-stage attribution (+42.92 / +9.00 / +3.94 / +3.85 / +2.91 pp) at a fixed 3.55–3.56-bit budget, the FP16-vs-W8 null result on Tier 0 (+0.026 pp, CI spanning zero), and the explicit statement of the residual 3.88 pp floor. That is a useful negative/limit result more than a new method.

*Rigor — genuinely strong points.* Full 50k evaluation for every headline number, a 46-arm screen verified on the disjoint complement, 328 point evaluations feeding the DP, a 16-arm factorial rather than one-at-a-time ablation, and paired confidence intervals on the three comparisons the story depends on. Keeping the seed-20261013 delay-QAT reversal (−10.630 pp) in the paper, contra the seed-20261012 result (+0.364 pp), is exactly the honesty this literature usually lacks.

*Rigor — paper-specific problems.* (1) The text reports that "per-channel MSE clipping alone improves accuracy by +9.004 points (51.150% → 60.154%) **and** 1,000-image BN recalibration alone improves accuracy by +9.004 points (51.150% → 60.154%)" — two different interventions landing on the identical delta and identical endpoint to three decimals is either an extreme coincidence or a copy-paste error in the prose (Table 2 does show MSE+Src = 60.15% and MaxAbs+BN = 60.15%). If the BN-only arm is actually 60.15% by coincidence the interaction arithmetic (−5.066 pp) still holds, but the sentence as written invites doubt about which arm was run. (2) The abstract says "keeping less than 5% of critical layer **activations** in 8-bit precision recovers up to 92% of the accuracy lost", but the body protects **weights** (0.34%, 13,952 parameters, 14 KB) — activations are never selectively kept at 8-bit anywhere in the experiments, and no table row produces exactly 92%; the recoverable fractions implied by the tables are ~86% for W4→65.95% (42.92/49.83) and ~94.7% for W3→68.98% (68.68/72.56). Abstract and body disagree on both the object and the number. (3) The QAT arm is a single seed and a single epoch (DP0.5 only), yet the paper's own Section 2.2 shows INT3 QAT swings by ±10 pp across two seeds — so the +1.044 pp "QAT over PTQ" claim sits inside a variance regime the paper itself documents as unstable, and no seed sweep is reported for the 68.98% endpoint. (4) The 16-arm factorial varies precision profile, scaling rule, and BN regime but holds the 1,000-image recalibration subset and the clipping search grid α ∈ [0.5, 1.0] fixed; there is no ablation on recalibration set size (1k vs 5k vs 50k), which is precisely the knob that would tell a practitioner how much data the +3.94 pp costs. (5) The headline "3.9-point cumulative error floor" is model-specific (B0, ReLU6/SGD, 88 epochs) and the paper concedes cross-scale B1/B2 comparisons are descriptive because batch sizes differed — so "where recovery stops" is demonstrated for one architecture, not established as a floor.

*Reproducibility.* Strong on artifacts (checkpoints, DP profiles, FlatBuffers, SHA-256 manifests, Hugging Face release) and on protocol (deterministic preprocessing, full validation split). Weak on hardware: the CPU latency numbers are reported only as "same-host CPU" with no model, core count, memory bandwidth, or ISA baseline stated, even though the entire Section 5 argument is a microarchitectural claim about missing sub-byte depthwise kernels — a reader cannot tell whether 15.43 ms for WI4 reflects LiteRT's unpacking path generally or one machine's cache behavior.

*Significance.* The practical takeaway is sharp and, if believed, immediately actionable: on commodity CPUs, INT8 is Pareto-dominant and sub-4-bit is a storage win that costs 1.5×–21× wall-clock. The representational half matters less for deployment than the systems half, and the paper is honest that pairing 3.56-bit allocation with real speedup needs sub-byte LUT SIMD or an NPU. The 68.98% endpoint being 3.88 pp below FP32 is a modest but real ceiling, not a solved problem.

*Red flags.* Abstract/body mismatch on activations vs weights and on the 92% figure; the duplicated +9.004 pp sentence; single-seed QAT endpoint against a paper-documented ±10 pp seed variance; a packed-INT3 latency benchmark deliberately run on the 0.30% uniform model (the paper flags this explicitly, but it means the 21.5× number does not describe the recovered 68.98% model); and baselines 3.4–3.9 pp below the canonical Tan & Le B0 recipe, so absolute accuracies are not directly comparable to the published 77.1%.

## Strengths

- Every accuracy claim is measured on all 50,000 ImageNet-1k validation images — no cherry-picked 1,000-image subsets for the headline numbers.
- The sensitivity screen is properly cross-validated: 46 arms on a 2,000-image subset, six masks re-verified on the disjoint 48,000-image complement.
- The recovery pipeline is reported as a decomposable waterfall with a per-stage delta, which is far more useful to a practitioner than a single "our method achieves X" number.
- The FP16-vs-W8 null result (+0.026 pp, CI spanning zero) is a genuinely useful design constraint: early depthwise filters need 8-bit integer, not floating point.
- Negative and inconvenient results are kept: the seed-20261013 delay-QAT penalty, logarithmic grids losing 2.92–3.72 pp, and sub-4-bit latency regressions.
- The storage-vs-latency reality gap is measured rather than asserted, with artifact size, accuracy, and p50/p95 latency in one table.
- Artifacts are released (checkpoints, DP profiles, FlatBuffers, SHA-256 manifests), which is rare for an industrial single-author quantization study.
- The early-epoch pilot section (INT4 "lossless" at 5.88%–18.88% FP32) is a methodological warning that will save other practitioners from a real trap.

## Limitations

- The "error floor" is demonstrated on EfficientNet-B0 with an 88-epoch ReLU6/SGD recipe only; B1/B2 appear only in a descriptive cross-scale check and the paper itself notes batch sizes differed, so the 3.88 pp figure should not be read as architecture-independent.
- QAT is one epoch, one seed, one profile (DP0.5) — and the paper's own two-seed INT3 experiment shows ±10 pp swing, so the QAT contribution is under-powered relative to the claim resting on it.
- The DP surrogate is additive over layerwise accuracy drops, which cannot in principle capture the cross-layer interactions the paper itself measures (the −5.066 pp clipping × BN interaction is direct evidence that additivity fails); the DP's +3.848 pp may therefore be mis-attributed layer budgets.
- BN recalibration is fixed at a 1,000-image subset with no size ablation, so the data cost of the +3.94 pp is unquantified.
- Latency is measured on one unspecified host CPU; no ARM vs x86 split, no core count, no memory-bandwidth characterization, despite the ARM/x86 SIMD argument in the text.
- The 21.5× packed-INT3 number attaches to a 0.30%-accuracy uniform bitstream, not to the 68.98% recovered model — the paper says so, but the figure circulates more easily than the caveat.
- Weight-only fake quantization throughout the accuracy study; activation quantization is not studied, yet the abstract refers to 8-bit activations, so the activation-side story is simply absent.
- No comparison against a strong modern low-bit PTQ baseline (e.g. a BRECQ/QDrop-style block reconstruction run at 3–4 bits on the same B0 checkpoint) — the baselines are max-abs and its own ablations, which makes "67.94% PTQ" hard to place against the literature.

## Relevance

直接命中「Reliability under Extreme Performance Optimization」：这是一篇把 PTQ/QAT 在亚 4-bit 下的崩塌机制、恢复上限（3.88 pp 缺口）和部署代价（1.48×–21.5× 变慢）都量化到 50k 全验证集的工作，正是该方向最缺的「失败边界」型证据。其中 BN 统计漂移、异常值拉伸步长、逐层比特分配导致的误差累积，也与「Operator Precision / 误差传播」一侧的数值机制讨论相互印证。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Why do MSE clipping alone and BN recalibration alone both land on exactly 60.154% (51.150% → 60.154%, +9.004 pp)? Is the BN-only arm genuinely coincident to three decimals, or is one arm mislabeled in Section 4?
- Does the 68.98% DP0.5 + QAT endpoint hold across seeds? Given the ±10 pp INT3 QAT swing the paper reports, a 3-seed sweep of the final recipe is the single most important missing experiment.
- How much does the +3.94 pp BN recalibration gain depend on the recalibration set size (1k vs 5k vs 50k)? This is the knob a practitioner actually controls.
- Since the DP surrogate is additive but the measured clipping × BN interaction is −5.066 pp, how far off is the DP optimum from a genuinely interaction-aware allocation (e.g. block-wise reconstruction or a learned surrogate)?
- Does the early-W8 + rest-W4 result (+42.92 pp, 65.95% at 4.01 bits) beat a well-tuned 4-bit block-reconstruction method such as BRECQ on the same checkpoint, or is the gap to 72.86% larger than published 4-bit PTQ achieves?
- What would a sub-byte LUT SIMD kernel (or an NPU with native 3/4-bit depthwise support) do to the latency ordering — does WI4 recover its theoretical 4× advantage once unpacking is free, or does the depthwise 9-weight-per-channel structure itself limit the gain?
- Does protecting weights rather than activations (contra the abstract's wording) remain the right lever when activation quantization is added on top of W8/W4/W3 weights?
