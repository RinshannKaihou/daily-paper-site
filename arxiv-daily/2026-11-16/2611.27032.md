> **一句话总结：** 论文以"同一批 VEA 样本内、VEA 句 vs 非 VEA 句的神经元激活差"定位 gated-MLP 神经元，在 QwQ-32B 与 OLMo 3 三个检查点上用均值替换干预把 VEA 率从 12.6–51.8% 压到 ≤1.4%，并在 QwQ-32B 上用"保留拒答方向分量"的投影干预把拒答率从 0.504 拉回 0.548，从而把"评估意识"与"拒答"从同一批神经元的写向量里拆开：VEA 文本只解释拒答下降的 4–10%，沿拒答方向的写入则解释 48–57%。

> **TL;DR:** Contrasting MLP-neuron activations between VEA and non-VEA sentences of the same rollouts yields a top-5,000 "VEA neuron" set whose mean-replacement intervention drops the test-category VEA rate from 12.6–51.8% to at most 1.4% on AgentHarm (QwQ-32B) and SorryBench/StrongREJECT (OLMo 3 SFT/DPO/RLVR), while a layer-matched random-neuron control at the same size barely moves it; on QwQ-32B/AgentHarm, 10,000 intervened neurons reach a VEA rate of 0.015 at refusal 0.223, versus 0.110 VEA at refusal 0.231 for Hua et al.'s activation-steering baseline, i.e. the same refusal reduction with ~7× less VEA. The interesting part is the disentanglement: refusing drops from 0.597 to 0.504 on SorryBench at N=20,000, only 5% [−2, 11] of which is attributable to deleted VEA text, 48% [31, 64] to the neurons' write along a difference-in-means refusal direction — and projecting that component out of the intervention restores 0.548 of the 0.597 refusal while VEA stays near zero.

## 核心贡献

- **提出一套可复现的 VEA 神经元定位流程**：在同一批含 VEA 的 rollout 内部，把 token 划为 VEA 句（V）与非 VEA 句（O），逐层逐神经元算 Cohen's d，跨层取 top-N（N=1k/5k/10k/20k/30k）作为候选集；干预方式是把被选神经元的激活固定为其非 VEA 均值 \(m_j^{(l)}=\mu_j^{(l)}(O)\)，并提供"数量与层分布匹配的随机神经元"作为对照。
- **证明这些神经元对 VEA 有因果作用，且同时压制未言语化的评估意识**：干预后 VEA 率在四个模型/基准上降到 ≤1.4%，随机对照几乎无效；用 SAD 上构造的层 63 线性探针（AUROC 0.814）测同一段固定文本，探针分数也显著下降（N=30,000 时 1.977 vs 随机对照 1.069）。
- **把"评估意识—拒答"的相关性拆成三条通路并给出比例**：通路 (i) 删除 CoT 中的 VEA 文本、(ii) 削弱 unVEA/其他内部变化、(iii) 神经元沿拒答方向写入；用"拒答方向分量保留干预"只改变 (iii)，从而在 QwQ-32B/SorryBench 上估出 (i) 4–10%、(iii) 48–57%、(ii) 39–47%。
- **给出与行为实验独立的权重空间证据**：VEA 神经元的 down-projection 向量与逐层拒答方向的强对齐（|cos| ≥ 该层 99.9 分位）比例是匹配随机神经元的 4.1–6.5 倍（N=30,000 时 0.37% vs 0.09%，p 取到 0.001 的下限）。
- **跨 OLMo 3 后训练检查点观察 VEA 的演化**：SFT 与 DPO 的 VEA 率相近，最终 RLVR 检查点显著更高，同时 |d|>1 的神经元数量与 top-N 的平均选择性都上升——作者明确声明这是表征层面的刻画，而非"RLVR 通过 VEA 神经元因果地提高 VEA"。

## Key Contributions

- **Neuron-level localization of a reasoning-model behaviour.** Instead of a residual-stream steering vector, the paper ranks all 1,769,472 gated-MLP neurons by the Cohen's d between activations at VEA-containing sentences and non-VEA sentences *within the same CoTs*, then intervenes by mean replacement — a design that keeps the sample fixed and changes only the activation statistic used for selection.
- **Causal, not correlational, suppression of evaluation awareness.** Mean replacement of 5,000 neurons drives the test-category VEA rate to ≤1.4% on every model–benchmark pair, with a neuron-count- and layer-matched random control that leaves VEA essentially unchanged; the effect transfers across benchmarks (AgentHarm-selected neurons suppress SorryBench and EvalAwareBench VEA; SorryBench-selected neurons suppress StrongREJECT).
- **A mechanistic account of why evaluation awareness and refusal co-move.** Three pathways are separated experimentally, and the paper shows the refusal decrease survives even on rollouts that contain no VEA under either condition (0.565 → 0.464 at N=30,000 vs 0.545 for the random control), ruling out the "VEA text removal" explanation.
- **A weight-space corroboration.** Absolute cosine similarity between each neuron's down-projection vector and the layer's prompt-end refusal direction is used as a tail statistic, showing enrichment of 4.1–6.5× among VEA neurons, with 111 of 30,000 neurons at N=30,000 — evidence that does not depend on the behavioural pipeline.
- **A post-training comparison on open checkpoints.** Because OLMo 3 SFT/DPO/RLVR share a neuron basis, the number of neurons above a fixed selectivity threshold and their mean |d| can be compared directly across post-training stages.

## Methodology

**Setup.** Four 32B models with 64 layers, residual width 5,120 and MLP width 27,648: QwQ-32B plus Olmo-3-32B-Think-SFT, Olmo-3-32B-Think-DPO and Olmo-3.1-32B-Think (RLVR). Generation uses temperature 0.6, top-p 0.95, 16,384 max tokens, bfloat16 in vLLM with eager execution so MLP hooks fire every forward pass; activations are captured with nnsight.

**VEA annotation.** A gpt-5-mini judge reads the task (first 2,000 chars) and the CoT (first 40,000 chars, no final answer), returns Yes/Maybe/No plus a category in {test, fiction, other} and a verbatim quote; only conclusion=Yes ∧ category=test counts as VEA. Quotes are normalized and matched exactly to the CoT, assigned injectively, and failures are dropped rather than fuzzy-aligned (observed mismatch rate 1–11%).

**Localization.** For every sample whose CoT contains a matched test-category quote, the containing sentence span is labelled V and every other CoT token O. Both sets come from the same VEA-positive samples, so task, prompt and rollout identity are held fixed. Cohen's d is computed per neuron per layer, ranks are pooled across layers, and top-N sets are taken for N ∈ {1,000, 5,000, 10,000, 20,000, 30,000}. Neurons with either sign of d are eligible, so the set can contain both "VEA-promoting" and "VEA-suppressing" units.

**Intervention.** During prompt prefill and autoregressive decoding, each selected neuron's activation is overwritten with its non-VEA mean before the down projection, so its residual contribution is fixed at \(m_j^{(l)} w_j^{(l)}\). The reference exactly cancels the VEA-vs-non-VEA mean shift on discovery activations (Eq. 4). Controls are random neurons drawn outside the top 50,000 by |d|, matched in count and layer distribution.

**unVEA probe.** A difference-in-means direction on the SAD stages-oversight task (280 train / 120 held-out prompts, stratified by class and answer-letter order, seed 42), read at the input residual of layer 63 with AUROC 0.814. Probe scores are evaluated on 262 fixed VEA segments from unintervened SorryBench rollouts, with the recorded sequence held fixed across conditions, and the score decrease is the projection onto the frozen unit direction.

**Refusal-component-preserving intervention.** Per-layer refusal directions \(r^{(l)}\) are difference-in-means between prompts whose five sampled continuations refuse ≥80% and comply ≥80% (400-prompt contrast pool from AdvBench/HarmBench/OR-Bench-Hard/SorryBench/StrongREJECT, 300-prompt training split), read at prompt end. The intervention change \(\Delta^{(l)}\) is projected onto the subspace orthogonal to \(\hat r^{(l)}\) before being added back, so the MLP output's component along the refusal direction is preserved exactly (verified numerically per token and layer).

**Mediation.** For the same 440 SorryBench questions × 3 seeds under (A) no intervention, (B) plain intervention, (C) refusal-component-preserving intervention, the total change \(\rho_B-\rho_A\) is split into (i) VEA-text share on VEA-positive rollouts, (ii) the remaining change on VEA-negative rollouts, and (iii) \(\rho_B-\rho_C\); (i) and (ii) are explicitly upper bounds, and 95% bootstrap intervals over questions are reported.

## Results & Highlights

- **VEA suppression with matched controls.** Mean replacement of 5,000 VEA neurons lowers the test-category VEA rate from a 12.6–51.8% baseline to at most 1.4% across all model–benchmark combinations (QwQ-32B on AgentHarm; OLMo 3 SFT/DPO/RLVR on SorryBench and StrongREJECT), while the size- and layer-matched random-neuron control stays close to baseline. The effect is not judge-only: on QwQ-32B/AgentHarm the word "testing" falls from 13.86% of rollouts to 0.19% and "test" from 14.09% to 0.19% at N=30,000, while "instructions" stays at 43.56% versus 47.50%.
- **Neuron-level intervention dominates activation steering at matched refusal.** On AgentHarm/QwQ-32B, steering (Hua et al.'s 16-pair evaluation–deployment direction at six layers, coefficient −0.6) lowers refusal 0.310 → 0.231 but VEA only 0.140 → 0.110 (p = 0.073, not significant); intervening on 10,000 VEA neurons reaches essentially the same refusal (0.223) at VEA = 0.015. On SorryBench, steering gives refusal 0.517 / VEA 0.035 versus 20,000 neurons giving refusal 0.504 / VEA 0.000 — the same refusal, ~1/35 the residual VEA.
- **Refusal drops even with no VEA to delete.** On a fixed SorryBench question set whose rollouts contain no test-category VEA under either condition (398/407/403 questions per seed), 30,000-neuron intervention lowers refusal from 0.565 to 0.464 while the matched random control gives 0.545; paired sign-flip tests give VEA-minus-random differences of −0.005, −0.014, −0.041, −0.097, −0.081 for N = 1k…30k (p < 10⁻⁴ for N ≥ 10,000). Refusal text removal therefore cannot be the whole story.
- **Pathway decomposition.** On QwQ-32B/SorryBench (440 questions × 3 seeds), baseline refusal 0.597 falls to 0.566 / 0.540 / 0.504 at N = 5k / 10k / 20k. Term (i), the VEA-text share, is 10% [−9, 31], 4% [−6, 12] and 5% [−2, 11]; term (iii), the write along the refusal direction, is 51% [0, 97], 57% [35, 83] and 48% [31, 64]; the residual term (ii) absorbs 39–47%. All (i) intervals include zero, so "VEA text explains little" is an upper-bound statement, not a significant nonzero effect.
- **Preserving the refusal component restores refusal.** On AgentHarm, the plain and refusal-component-preserving interventions suppress VEA to comparable levels (both near zero for N ≥ 10k), but the preserving variant recovers part of the refusal: refusal under plain intervention keeps falling with N while the preserving variant stays higher (e.g. at N=20,000 on SorryBench, 0.548 vs 0.504, which is exactly term (iii) above). The decoupling is therefore causal on the refusal side, not just correlational.
- **Weight space agrees.** Down-projection vectors of VEA neurons are strongly aligned with the layer's refusal direction (|cos| ≥ layer 99.9th percentile) at 0.60%, 0.58%, 0.43%, 0.40%, 0.37% for N = 1k…30k, versus 0.10%, 0.09%, 0.09%, 0.09%, 0.09% for layer-matched random neurons — enrichments of 6.2×, 6.5×, 4.7×, 4.5×, 4.1×, all at the 0.001 empirical p floor. Mean |cos| is only 0.0152 vs 0.0120, so the effect is carried by a small subset (111 neurons at N=30,000), not the set as a whole.
- **unVEA probe drops too.** On 262 fixed VEA segments, the layer-63 probe score decrease is 4.089 for targeted neurons versus 0.484 for the matched random control at N=30,000; on SorryBench prompts before any reasoning is emitted, 1.977 ± 0.104 vs 1.069 ± 0.066, a paired excess of 0.908 units (one-sided paired Wilcoxon p = 1.42×10⁻¹¹). Note the random control already produces ~54% of the targeted prompt-side decrease.
- **Generality cost is small but underpowered.** QwQ-32B at N=30,000: GPQA-Diamond 67.3 ± 3.8 vs 66.0 ± 1.4 baseline, MATH-500 83.9 ± 0.1 vs 84.0 ± 0.0, IFEval 86.2 ± 2.7 vs 86.7 ± 0.4. OLMo-3.1-32B-Think at N=30,000: GPQA 61.7 ± 0.3 vs 61.1 ± 5.0, MATH-500 89.4 ± 0.7 vs 91.4 ± 0.7, IFEval 94.7 ± 1.8 vs 95.2 ± 0.0 — the MATH-500 gap is the one place the drop exceeds the reported SD.
- **Post-training trend.** Across the OLMo 3 trajectory, VEA rate on SorryBench and StrongREJECT is similar after SFT and DPO but markedly higher after RLVR (the abstract's 51.8% upper end of the range sits at the RLVR end), and both the count of neurons with |d| > 1 and the mean selectivity within every tested top-N set increase from SFT to RLVR.

## Authors & Credibility

All five authors are at the University of Tokyo (Iwasawa's group, with Kojima of chain-of-thought / self-consistency lineage). The work is a preprint with no venue yet. It cites and directly builds on the two closest steering papers (Abdelnabi & Salem 2025; Hua et al. 2026) and reproduces their steering setup from their Appendix F prompts at their published coefficient scale, which is a good-faith baseline rather than a straw man. The experimental plumbing is credible: vLLM hooks plus nnsight for activation capture, unit tests for the hook coordinate convention, HF/vLLM token-ID agreement checks, and fixed judges across conditions. The AI-use statement is unusually explicit that LLMs annotated VEA, judged refusal, wrote code and searched related work, but did not design the hypotheses.

## Critical Assessment

The core claim is narrower than the title suggests, and the paper is mostly honest about that, but three things deserve scrutiny. **First, the measurement and the intervention share one judge.** VEA rate is defined by a single gpt-5-mini draw at temperature 1, capped at the first 40,000 characters of reasoning and the first 2,000 characters of the task, with no human agreement study on the Yes/No decision — only a 1–11% verbatim-quote mismatch audit. So the headline "12.6–51.8% → ≤1.4%" is a judge-defined quantity, and the intervention is validated on the same annotator that defined the target. The word-frequency analysis (testing 13.86% → 0.19%) is the strongest independent corroboration, and the paper should have led with it. The 40,000-character truncation also matters for the post-training comparison: if RLVR traces are longer or place awareness later, the SFT→RLVR VEA increase could be partly a truncation artefact, and nothing in the paper checks trace-length distributions across checkpoints.

**Second, in-domain discovery contaminates the headline number.** For QwQ-32B the neurons are selected on AgentHarm and the strongest intervention result is reported on AgentHarm; the cross-benchmark transfer to SorryBench/StrongREJECT exists but is presented as a secondary panel, not as the headline. A leave-one-benchmark-out localisation (discover on SorryBench, evaluate on AgentHarm) would be the fair test, and it is not run for QwQ-32B at all. Relatedly, the token labelling expands each matched quote to its containing sentence(s), which the authors concede can absorb adjacent "reactive" clauses; their defence is that only 3 of 147 QwQ-32B VEA sentences contain explicit refusal wording, which bounds but does not eliminate the worry that some "VEA neurons" are refusal neurons recruited by sentence-level co-occurrence.

**Third, the mediation numbers are point estimates over intervals that often include zero, and the weight-space story is much sparser than the behavioural story.** Term (i) is 4–10% with all three bootstrap intervals crossing zero, so the defensible claim is "at most ~10%", not "4–10%". Term (iii) is the only interval that excludes zero, and only for N ≥ 10,000 (35% and 55% with a single layer-44 direction). Meanwhile the strong-cosine enrichment corresponds to 0.37–0.60% of selected neurons — 6 neurons at N=1,000, 111 at N=30,000 — and mean |cos| is 0.0128–0.0152 against a 0.012 random floor. Explaining roughly half of a 9-point refusal drop with a subset that is ~0.4% of the intervened set is a large extrapolation, especially since the cosine captures only the direct write and the paper's own per-layer refusal directions "have not each been independently validated by causal interventions."

**Other specifics worth flagging.** (a) The 30,000-neuron random control already produces ~54% of the targeted probe-score decrease on prompt positions (0.484 vs 4.089 on VEA segments, but 1.069 vs 1.977 on prompts) — a reminder that mean-replacing 30k of 1.77M neurons is a blunt perturbation, and the probe evidence is weaker than it first appears; the paired excess of 0.908 units is the honest quantity. (b) The general-ability check uses the first 100 items of each benchmark with 2 seeds, so it cannot rule out a 2–3 point regression: OLMo MATH-500 does drop 91.4 → 89.4 at N=30,000, slightly outside the reported ±0.7 SD, and the paper's "no large decline" phrasing glosses over it. (c) The continuation experiment (Appendix E.2) finds no significant refusal change when intervening only after a VEA sentence, which the authors attribute to a 0.956 refusal ceiling — reasonable, but it leaves pathway (i) untested in the regime where it should matter most. (d) The OLMo post-training comparison is a cross-section of three checkpoints from one training trajectory, one seed each, with no in-training curve and no control for data-mixture or format changes across SFT/DPO/RLVR; the abstract's "RLVR substantially increases evaluation awareness" is therefore a three-point correlation, and Section 5's own hedge ("should not be viewed as a direct causal intermediary") contradicts the abstract's confidence. (e) Baseline refusal for the same 440 SorryBench questions is quoted as 0.594 in Appendix E.1 and 0.597 in Table E.2; small, but a reader trying to reconcile the tables has to guess which seed set each number uses.

## Strengths

- The matched random-neuron control is designed properly (same count, same layer distribution, drawn outside the top 50,000 by |d|) and is run for every intervention size, which makes the causal claim about *these* neurons rather than about perturbation magnitude.
- The three-pathway decomposition is genuinely clever: by constructing a variant that differs from the plain intervention *only* along the refusal direction, pathway (iii) becomes identifiable rather than argued, and the authors correctly label (i) and (ii) as upper bounds instead of point estimates.
- The weight-space analysis is independent evidence of the right kind — fixed weights, not behaviour — and the honest reporting of the tail statistic (mean |cos| barely above the 0.012 floor) is more informative than a single average would have been.
- Negative and null results are reported: the continuation experiment's null, the random control's large probe effect, the non-significant steering VEA reduction (p = 0.073), and the EvalAwareBench experiment's missing random control are all stated in the text rather than buried.
- The steering baseline is rebuilt from the original paper's prompt pairs at the original coefficient, on the same judges and seeds, so the "neuron beats steering" comparison is at least apples-to-apples on the evaluation side.

## Limitations

- One model carries the mechanistic claims: the mediation, the refusal-component-preserving intervention, and the probe analysis are all QwQ-32B only; the OLMo checkpoints get VEA rates and neuron counts but no refusal analysis, so "VEA neurons integrate evaluation awareness and refusal" is a single-32B-model result.
- VEA is defined by a single closed-model judge draw with truncation at 40k reasoning characters and no human agreement on the label itself, so both the baseline rates and the suppression measurements inherit judge variance that is never quantified (no repeated annotation, no inter-draw agreement).
- The refusal directions are per-layer difference-in-means vectors that were not each validated causally, and the mediation analysis bounds rather than measures pathway (ii), so the attribution of 39–47% to "unVEA and other internal changes" is a residual, not a finding.
- Contrastive localisation can select polysemantic neurons or low-level feature neurons; the paper says so in the conclusion but offers no polysemy diagnostic (e.g. per-neuron activation-context diversity), and 58% of QwQ-32B's top-1,000 neurons sit in layers 32–52, which is suggestive of a distributed rather than localised mechanism.
- General-ability evaluation is 100 items × 2 seeds per benchmark, which is too underpowered to license "no decline", and EvalAwareBench is reported without a random-neuron control.
- No code or artifact release is mentioned; reproducibility rests on open weights plus the appendix prompts, with judge models (gpt-5-mini, gpt-4o-mini) that are not version-pinned to a dated snapshot.

## Relevance

这篇属于"LLM 内部表征与可解释性"：用神经元级因果干预（而非 steering 向量）定位并拆解一个高层行为，同时给出权重空间的独立佐证，是"探针能解码 ≠ 干预有效"这条方法论线索的一个正面案例——它用匹配随机对照和"只改一个分量"的投影干预来隔离混杂。对关心表征工程因果性、以及用 LLM-judge 作为测量仪器的可靠性的人来说，论文里"测量与干预共用同一个 judge"和"40k 字符截断"这两处细节值得单独记一笔。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. If the VEA annotator were run three times at temperature 1, or replaced by a human-labelled subset of 200 CoTs, how much of the 12.6–51.8% baseline and of the ≤1.4% post-intervention rate would move? Is the judge's false-positive rate the same on SFT and RLVR traces?
2. Does leave-one-benchmark-out localisation hold — neurons discovered on SorryBench, evaluated on AgentHarm in QwQ-32B — or does the in-domain selection account for a meaningful share of the ≤1.4% figure?
3. Only 111 of 30,000 neurons are strongly aligned with the refusal direction, yet pathway (iii) is credited with ~half the refusal drop. What happens to refusal if the intervention is *restricted* to those 111 neurons (or to the orthogonal complement)? This would directly test whether the sparse tail carries the behaviour.
4. Are trace lengths and VEA-sentence positions systematically different between the OLMo 3 SFT, DPO and RLVR checkpoints? If so, does the 40,000-character annotation cap bias the SFT→RLVR comparison?
5. Does the unVEA probe's ~54%-of-effect random control reflect a genuinely linear-but-diffuse representation, or an artefact of mean-replacing 30,000 coordinates? A control that preserves the residual-stream norm while randomising directions would separate these.
6. Can the refusal-component-preserving intervention be used as an evaluation-time correction in a real safety benchmark to report "VEA-suppressed but refusal-preserved" scores, and does that change the ranking of models on SorryBench/StrongREJECT?
