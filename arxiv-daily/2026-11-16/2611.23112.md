> **一句话总结：** 在 Llama-3.1-8B 上用 1000 万条 prompt 做梯度归因，建神经元共现图并跑 Leiden 社区检测，无监督地挖出数千个跨层"神经元社区"，再用必要性/充分性/层级必要性三重因果检验验证，发现 ≤128 个神经元即可在 10.3% 的留出 prompt 上解释至少一层的贡献（其中"an + 元音开头"仅需 6 个神经元，消融 KL 4.99、保留 KL 0.053）。

> **TL;DR:** The authors discover reusable MLP "neuron communities" in Llama-3.1-8B by combining first-order gradient attribution over 10M prompts (50% FineWeb-Edu / 25% Wikipedia / 25% FineMath), a popularity-corrected co-occurrence graph, and Leiden community detection across six resolution thresholds. Communities capped at 128 neurons pass three causal tests (necessity, in-layer-subset sufficiency, layer necessity) on 10.3% of 8.86M held-out prompts for at least one layer, 8.7% for ≥4 layers, 3.3% for ≥12 layers — versus a shuffled-community baseline of 2.91% and activation-only attribution scores of ~2.7% at the 1M-prompt scale. The headline case study is stark: six neurons distributed over layers L2–L14 implement `an` + vowel-initial agreement, with median KL 4.99 when ablated versus 0.053 when retained alongside four "always on" neurons out of 86,016 MLP neurons in those layers, while ablating six *random* neurons leaves predictions essentially untouched.

## 核心贡献

- **提出"神经元社区发现"（neuron community discovery）**：一个无监督、可扩展的三段式流水线——逐 prompt 梯度归因 → 去流行的共现图 → 多分辨率 Leiden 社区检测——用于在不对行为做任何预设的情况下，从百万级 prompt 中挖掘反复共同被调用的 MLP 神经元组。
- **给出可判定的因果定义**：社区在某一层子集 S 上"被解释"当且仅当同时满足社区必要性（消融社区 → KL ≥ 0.7）、层级必要性（消融 S 内其余 MLP → KL ≥ 0.7）和层内充分性（只保留社区 + always-on 神经元 → KL ≤ 0.2）；第三项是防止"充分性"退化为平凡命题的关键设计。
- **规模化实证**：在 Llama-3.1-8B 上以 1000 万 prompt 建图，得到数千个通过因果检验的社区，覆盖 10.3% 的留出 prompt（≥1 层）、8.7%（≥4 层）、3.3%（≥12 层），社区规模上限 128 个神经元。
- **建立行为分类学**：用 LLM 自动解释把 γ=256 下通过检验的 5,281 个社区归为八类，发现"固定短语补全"占社区数的 42.3%，而"语法/一致"只占 2.9% 的社区却覆盖 15.3% 的已解释 prompt——高频被发现的机制与高频生效的机制不是一回事。
- **揭示社区内部组织**：对 `an` + 元音社区做全部 2^6=64 种子集消融，发现"冗余投票"结构——任意单个神经元可被移除（KL 0.06–0.07），最强成员 L14:11888 单独保留即可恢复大部分行为（KL 0.68 vs 全移除 4.56），且可被 L5:3846 替代。
- **开源**：代码（github.com/goodfire-ai/neuron-communities）与交互式社区浏览器（goodfire.com/demos/neuron-communities）一并放出。

## Key Contributions

- **A scalable, behavior-agnostic discovery pipeline.** Attribution → co-occurrence graph → multi-resolution Leiden partitioning, run at 10M-prompt scale; nothing in the pipeline is told what a "behavior" is, in deliberate contrast to prior neuron work that starts from hand-picked task categories (subject–verb agreement, arithmetic).
- **A three-test causal acceptance criterion** (community necessity, layer necessity, in-subset sufficiency) that makes "explained" a falsifiable claim rather than an interpretive one. Layer necessity is the crucial guard: without it, sufficiency alone is trivially satisfied by handing the model a large residual.
- **Quantified coverage** of neuron-sparse explanations on held-out data, reported at four layer-span requirements (L=1, 4, 8, 12) and three community-size budgets (16, 32, 128 neurons), which lets readers trade off sparsity against coverage.
- **A behavioral taxonomy plus layer-depth profile** for 5,281 causally validated communities, showing that token suppression concentrates in late layers, sub-word completion peaks at L0 with a secondary peak at L15–L17, and retrieval/computation peaks at L17–L22.
- **A mechanistic case study of intra-community redundancy** via exhaustive subset ablations, contributing the concrete finding that community-level necessity does not imply member-level necessity.

## Methodology

**Step 1 — Per-prompt neuron attribution.** Only the final token position is used. For MLP neuron *j* in layer ℓ, the score is the first-order influence `a_ℓj(x) = h_ℓj(x) · ∂log p(ŷ|x)/∂h_ℓj(x)`, requiring one forward and one backward pass. For each layer the top-k (k=5) neurons are kept, giving a sparse attributed set A(x) of ≤150 neurons over layers 0–29.

**Step 2 — Co-occurrence graph.** Nodes are MLP neurons; raw co-occurrence counts are confounded by neuron popularity, so edge weights are z-scores of the co-occurrence count given each node's marginal frequency. Edges require count ≥ 5 and z ≥ 8. Layers 30–31 are excluded because their attributions look less neuron-sparse even for single prompts.

**Step 3 — Community discovery.** Leiden with the Constant Potts Model objective over γ ∈ {32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024}; results are pooled across thresholds because no single resolution suits every mechanism. A separate set of 17 "always on" neurons (top-k attribution on >20% of prompts) is removed before partitioning and is always retained during interventions. Communities are capped at 128 neurons.

**Step 4 — Causal testing on held-out prompts.** For each (prompt, community, layer-subset) triple, three KL divergences against the clean next-token distribution: necessity (zero-ablate community neurons in S), sufficiency (zero-ablate all other MLP neurons in S, keeping community + always-on), and layer necessity (zero-ablate all MLP in S except always-on). Acceptance: Nec ≥ τ_N = 0.7, LayerNec ≥ τ_N, Suff ≤ τ_S = 0.2. Every subspan of a community's ordered layer list is tested, so a community can explain all or only some of the layers it spans.

**Data and scale.** 10M discovery prompts (50% FineWeb-Edu, 25% English Wikipedia, 25% FineMath) plus ~489k prompts from five targeted slices (grammar, language switch, code, QA, memorized text); a further 10M prompts from the same mix for evaluation, deduplicated to 8,862,662 held-out prompts. Model: Llama-3.1-8B, MLP neurons only (no attention heads).

**Taxonomy.** gpt-5.6-terra at temperature 0, one request per community, up to 100 causally validated examples each, assigned one primary label from a hand-written 23-label taxonomy collapsed into 8 classes.

## Results & Highlights

- **Coverage on 8.86M held-out prompts (Llama-3.1-8B, ≤128 neurons/community):** 10.3% of prompts have at least one layer's contribution explained by a community passing all three causal tests; coverage drops to 8.7% requiring ≥4 layers and 3.3% requiring ≥12. A single resolution (γ=256) reaches only 5.7%, so the six-threshold union roughly doubles coverage — an explicitly acknowledged non-monotone effect of threshold choice.
- **Baseline gap is real but not apples-to-apples:** at the 1M-prompt ablation scale the activation×gradient score reaches 9.94% versus 6.31% for gradient-only, 2.72% for activation magnitude, 2.74% for output-norm-weighted activation, 2.53% for standardized activation, and 2.91% for a shuffled-community dictionary. The 10.3% headline is measured at 10M scale while the "3% random baseline" quoted in §5.1 comes from that 1M-scale appendix run — the two numbers are not from the same experiment.
- **The `an` + vowel case study (8,000 independently sampled `an`-ending prompts):** ablating the six-neuron community gives median KL 4.99 and mean |Δp| of the clean top-1 token 0.42 against a mean clean probability of 0.57; ablating *all* MLP neurons in the same six layers except four always-on neurons gives median KL 3.15 and mean |Δp| 0.38; retaining only those six neurons plus four always-on out of 86,016 MLP neurons in those layers gives median KL 0.053 and mean |Δp| 0.08; ablating six random neurons leaves KL concentrated near zero.
- **The community generalizes beyond its discovery set:** 20,339 prompts pass the necessity+sufficiency tests for this community, and 99.7% of them end at the article token `an` (never as a word fragment), which is a strong specificity check rather than a template artifact.
- **Sparsity trade-off (Fig. 4c):** shrinking the budget from 128 to 16 neurons still explains at least one layer on 6.7% of prompts (vs 10.3%) and at least eight layers on 2.5% (vs 8.7% at L≥4/128) — i.e. roughly two-thirds of the coverage survives a 8× tighter budget.
- **Attribution sanity check (Appendix A):** the top 40 layer-18 neurons by mean attribution recover 17/28 (60.7%) of Feucht et al.'s addition neurons, 20/26 (76.9%) for hours, 13/16 (81.3%) for months, and 14/15 (93.3%) for weekdays — a genuine external validity check against an independently derived circuit.
- **Behavioral distribution (5,281 communities at γ=256 passing on ≥10 prompts):** common phrases 42.3% of communities / 31.3% of covered prompts, token suppression 19.5% / 19.6%, formatting 9.3% / 19.4%, grammar 2.9% / 15.3%, sub-word completion 8.0% / 6.0%, topic 9.0% / 3.0%, retrieval & computation 3.7% / 2.9%.
- **Intra-community redundancy (`an` + vowel, 100 prompts):** all six single-neuron ablations land at mean KL 0.06–0.07, yet removing L14:11888 and L5:3846 together gives 0.65 and additionally removing L12:14182 jumps to 2.20 — redundancy is graded, not uniform. By contrast the in-context copying community is fragile: removing L23:6134 alone moves mean KL from 0.05 to 7.37.
- **Resolution selection is stable:** exhaustive search over all 2^14 subsets of 14 γ values shows the chosen six thresholds achieve 10.28% vs 10.37% for all 14 (99.1% retention), and a train-only rerun selects the identical set — a clean piece of methodology hygiene.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The individual ingredients are all borrowed — first-order gradient attribution, z-scored co-occurrence graphs, Leiden/CPM, and necessity/sufficiency KL testing are each standard. The contribution is the *composition at scale* plus the refusal to predefine behaviors, and that is genuinely new: prior neuron work (Arora et al. 2026, Feucht et al. 2026, Nikankin et al. 2025) all starts from human task categories. The framing "discover the units first, ask what they do second" is the right inversion and is executed seriously rather than as a slogan.

**Rigor — what holds up.** The layer-necessity test is the paper's best methodological idea: sufficiency claims in circuit work are routinely inflated because the retained set implicitly inherits the rest of the network, and requiring that ablating the whole layer subset actually hurts forces the community to carry the load. The 8,000-prompt independent `an` sample (rather than only prompts where the community already passed) is a real recall measurement. The train-only replication of threshold selection, and the 14-threshold exhaustive subset search, are both unusually careful.

**Rigor — where it leaks.** (1) The headline 10.3% is measured against a 2.91% shuffled baseline computed at a different scale (1M vs 10M prompts) with a different candidate pool; the honest comparison at 1M scale is 9.94% vs 2.91%. §5.1's "random baseline only achieves 3%" is thus a cross-experiment comparison. (2) "Explained" means *one layer's* contribution is explained, not the prediction — the paper says this, but the 10.3% figure travels much better than the 3.3% (≥12 layers) figure that actually supports the "reusable computational component" claim. (3) τ_N=0.7 and τ_S=0.2 are arbitrary absolute KL cutoffs and coverage is monotone in them; no sensitivity curve over (τ_N, τ_S) is reported, while γ gets a 14-value sweep. (4) The 17 "always on" neurons are handed to the sufficiency test for free, and 4 of them are granted inside a 6-layer subspan in the flagship case study — a non-trivial fraction of the "remaining MLP computation" is never actually removed. (5) No comparison against the obvious competitor: sparse-feature circuits / transcoder-based discovery (Marks et al. 2025) on the same prompts with the same causal criteria. The paper argues SAEs optimize reconstruction not computation, which is true, but that is an argument, not a measurement.

**Data quirks.** The arithmetic sanity check in Appendix A is evaluated on 256 prompts per template from a dataset the same group's collaborators built; the weekday recovery of 14/15 (93.3%) versus addition's 17/28 (60.7%) is a wide spread that goes undiscussed — plausibly a template-difficulty effect, but it means "gradient attribution recovers known neurons" holds only weakly for the hardest template. Layers 30–31 are dropped with one sentence of justification and no ablation. And there is an unavoidable confound the authors themselves flag: frequent behaviors are both more likely to be neuron-sparse *and* easier for a 10M-prompt pipeline to discover, so the taxonomy's 42.3%-common-phrases skew cannot distinguish "the model allocates neurons to frequent computations" from "our pipeline only finds frequent computations."

**Significance.** If the coverage numbers survive replication, this reframes the neuron-vs-feature debate: you may not need SAEs to get reusable units, at least for the ~10% of prompts where the model is neuron-sparse. That is a genuinely useful, checkable claim, and the released explorer makes it auditable by outsiders in a way most circuit papers are not.

## Strengths

- The three-test causal criterion, especially layer necessity, is a template other circuit-discovery papers should copy.
- Real scale (10M discovery + 8.86M evaluation prompts) rather than a few hundred hand-picked examples.
- The `an` + vowel case study is exceptionally clean: 6 neurons out of 86,016, median KL 4.99 → 0.053, 99.7% of 20,339 prompts ending at the article `an`, and a random-neuron ablation control that sits at zero.
- Honest limitations section that pre-empts the obvious attacks (cross-position blindness, non-overlapping partitions, recall is unmeasurable for ill-defined behaviors, no upper bound on neuron-sparse behavior).
- Full hyperparameter and prompt disclosure; code and interactive explorer released.
- The redundancy analysis (all 64 subsets) turns a vague "distributed computation" story into a concrete, falsifiable claim about substitutable members.

## Limitations

- **Single model, single data mix.** Everything is Llama-3.1-8B on FineWeb-Edu/Wikipedia/FineMath (plus code and QA slices). No Qwen, no GPT-2 family comparison, so the "universal units" question that Gurnee et al. raised is untouched.
- **MLP-only.** Attention heads are entirely outside the pipeline, yet the in-context copying community is explicitly hypothesized to cooperate with induction heads — an interaction the paper admits it did not test. A "reusable computational unit" claim that structurally cannot include attention is partial by construction.
- **Final-token-only attribution** cannot represent cross-position mechanisms, which rules out exactly the multi-step arithmetic and retrieval circuits one would most want to decompose.
- **One-neuron-one-community.** Leiden produces disjoint partitions; a neuron participating in three mechanisms is forced into one. Pooling across six γ values only partially mitigates this, and the paper acknowledges it without quantifying the damage.
- **No downstream validation.** Success is KL divergence on next-token distributions over corpus text. Whether these communities matter for task accuracy, safety-relevant behavior, or steering is unmeasured.
- **Coverage is a lower bound presented with upper-bound rhetoric.** 89.7% of prompts remain unexplained, and the authors correctly say this does not prove non-sparsity — but the abstract's "substantial fraction" framing invites the opposite reading.
- **Taxonomy is LLM-generated and unvalidated against humans.** gpt-5.6-terra labels 5,281 communities with no reported agreement study, while the prompt's own "expected base rates" are calibrated on a hand review of only 1,378 communities.

## Relevance

直接命中"LLM 隐状态与可解释性"方向：这是把神经元基（而非 SAE 特征基）作为可复用计算单元的一次规模化、带因果检验的验证，对关注机制可解释性、电路发现与内部表示结构的工作有直接参考价值。其"先发现单元、后问功能"的范式，以及对必要性/充分性检验协议的改进，也值得任何做内部表示审计或模型行为归因的研究借鉴。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. What happens to coverage if τ_N and τ_S are swept jointly — is 10.3% a plateau or a point on a steep curve chosen after seeing results?
2. How much of the 10.3% survives if the "always on" neurons are *not* free during the sufficiency test, i.e. if the community alone must reconstruct the layer subset?
3. Would the same communities be recovered in a different model family (Qwen, OLMo) or at a different scale, and would the layer-depth profiles of behavior classes shift?
4. How does the discovered dictionary compare head-to-head with sparse-feature circuits on identical prompts and identical causal thresholds — does the neuron basis win, tie, or lose on sparsity-at-equal-faithfulness?
5. Does forcing a neuron into a single community (rather than allowing overlap) create measurable false negatives, and how much coverage do overlapping-community methods (e.g. link communities) add?
6. Do these communities support steering? Ablating a grammar community should degrade agreement accuracy on a benchmark — a task-level test would convert the KL story into a capability story.
7. Can the pipeline be extended to per-position attribution so that operand-reading neurons in "2+5=" are captured, and does the arithmetic community's apparent 8-neuron sufficiency survive that extension?
