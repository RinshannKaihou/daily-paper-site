> **一句话总结：** 论文从理论上证明普通最小二乘（OLS）线性探针会把权重堆积到激活空间中方差极小、与目标仅有虚假协方差的方向上，并在 Mess3 HMM 玩具 Transformer 与 Llama-2-7B 出生年份任务上实证：OLS 探针解码极好但干预后输出几乎不动（HMM 干预损失 0.214，几乎等于不干预），而按干预效果选择 λ 的岭回归探针干预损失降到 0.00195，且方向与因果训练的 DAS 仅差 26–29°。

> **TL;DR:** OLS regression probes are decodable but causally inert: on the Mess3 HMM transformer they achieve the lowest probe loss (\(9.46\times10^{-5}\)) yet their intervention loss is \(2.14\times10^{-1}\), i.e. intervening along them changes nothing, and 91.5% of their weight mass sits on a single principal direction of variance \(10^{-18}\); a ridge probe with \(\lambda=0.1\) chosen by intervention loss reaches \(1.95\times10^{-3}\) (110× better) and lies 28.9° from DAS instead of 89.8°. The same gap reproduces on Llama-2-7B birth-year readout (OLS intervention loss 13.7 — identical to not intervening — vs 7.07 for ridge and 6.42 for DAS), and a closed-form ridge analysis explains why: the OLS weight along a direction scales as \(\sigma_f/(\sigma_v\sqrt{n})\), which diverges as \(\sigma_v\to 0\).

## 核心贡献

- **给出 OLS 探针失效的闭式机制**：在主成分基下回归目标按方向解耦，每个方向的权重为 \(w=\mathrm{cov}_{vf}/(\sigma_v^2+\lambda/n)\)；当 \(\lambda=0\) 且方向方差 \(\sigma_v\) 很小时，权重以 \(\sigma_f/(\sigma_v\sqrt{n})\) 的量级发散，于是低方差、与目标仅有噪声级协方差的方向会主导探针方向（推论 3.3）。
- **说明岭回归不是"偏好高方差"这么简单**：\(\lambda\) 只改变分母下界（\(\lambda/n\)），并把权重上限压到 \(\sqrt{n}|\mathrm{cov}_{vf}|/\lambda\)；且 \(\lambda\to\infty\) 时权重收敛到与目标的协方差方向而非最大方差方向（注 3.4），因此高方差本身既不充分也不能用来选 λ。
- **两个测试床上的因果对照实验**：同一批激活、同一目标下比较 OLS、岭回归与 DAS 三种"取方向"的方式，把拟合流程而不是数据差异隔离为唯一变量。
- **发现干预忠实度随 λ 呈倒 U 形**，存在内点最优 λ，且该 λ 下的岭回归方向在方向与方差上都与干预训练得到的 DAS 方向高度接近（26.3°/28.9°），而 OLS 与之近乎正交（84.7°/89.8°）。
- **排除"单方向各自惰性、组合起来可能有效"的漏洞**：在 Llama-2-7B 上让 DAS 在 OLS 权重最大的 100 个主成分张成的子空间（占 OLS 质量的 34.8%）内搜索，也找不到任何因果有效方向。
- **提出可操作建议**：基于可解码性的回归探针应通过扫描 λ、以干预忠实度（而非留出解码精度）来选择正则化强度。

## Key Contributions

- A closed-form per-direction account of why unregularized OLS probes fail causally: in the principal-component basis the ridge objective decouples, giving \(w=\mathrm{cov}_{vf}/(\sigma_v^2+\lambda/n)\), so the OLS weight along a quiet direction grows as \(\sigma_f/(\sigma_v\sqrt{n})\) without bound as \(\sigma_v\to0\) (Proposition 3.1, Corollary 3.3). Sampling noise alone supplies a covariance of typical size \(\sigma_v\sigma_f/\sqrt{n}\), which is amplified precisely where \(\sigma_v\) is small.
- A clean conceptual separation between decodability and intervention faithfulness, made empirical by holding data and target fixed and varying only the fitting procedure — OLS (decodability, λ=0), ridge (decodability with penalty), DAS (causation only) as the causal reference.
- Evidence that intervention-selected ridge probes recover directions close to DAS in angle *and* in variance, while OLS probes are all but orthogonal to DAS, on both a toy transformer and a 7B LLM.
- A refutation of the "ridge just chases variance" objection: the intervention-optimal Llama direction has variance 3.83 and largely ignores the top principal component with variance 25,570 (~6,700× larger).
- A mass-vs-operativity diagnostic: OLS mass concentrates on causally inert components, ridge mass on operative ones, plus a restricted-DAS search closing the "combination of inert directions" loophole.

## Methodology

**Two testbeds, same target, three direction-extraction methods.** The design isolates the fitting procedure: activations and targets are identical across OLS, ridge, and DAS; only how the direction (or, for \(k>1\), the subspace) is chosen differs.

*Testbed 1 — Mess3 HMM.* A 3-state HMM (mixing \(x=0.05\), emission concentration \(\alpha=0.85\)) whose Bayes-optimal predictor must track the belief state \(b_t\), which lives on a 2-simplex. The authors reuse Shai et al. (2024)'s pre-trained 4-layer, width-64 transformer, enumerate **every** sequence of length 1–8, compute exact beliefs and next-token probabilities, and cache the residual-stream activation at the final LayerNorm on the last token. Probes are fit by probability-weighted least squares. Because the enumeration is exhaustive, sample and population quantities coincide and there is no train/test split to hide behind.

*Testbed 2 — Llama-2-7B birth year.* Using the Wikidata name/birth-year data of Heinzerling & Inui (2024), prompts of the form "{name} was born in 19" are fed to Llama-2-7B; the readout is the expected decade \(E[d]=\sum_d d\cdot P(d)\) from the renormalized distribution over the ten digit tokens. The probed activation is the 4096-d residual stream at the last token of the name at **layer 8** (chosen by an intervention-effect layer sweep, Appendix A), with \(n=8000\) prompts — twice the activation dimension, so the OLS solution is unique. The target is the person's *true* birth year (not the model's expressed value), mean-centered and unit-scaled.

*Interventions.* On the HMM, probe-mediated steering: perturb activations by \(\Delta a=(W^\top)^{+}\delta f\), moving beliefs within the simplex in 20 smooth steps from three start states near each corner, over a uniform 496-point target grid. On Llama, natural-magnitude interchange: transplant the base activation's component along a candidate unit direction \(v\) from a source prompt, \(\Delta a=(v^\top a_{\text{src}}-v^\top a_{\text{base}})v\), and check whether the expected decade moves toward the source's. Both protocols avoid the Makelov et al. (2024) out-of-distribution objection by construction (in-simplex shifts; copied activation values).

*Metrics.* Probe loss (MSE of the linear readout), intervention loss \(L_{\text{int}}=\mathbb{E}\|y-g(f+\delta f)\|^2\) against the known ground-truth output \(g\), causal operativity (mean |output change| under interchange), variance along the direction \(v^\top\Sigma v\), probe mass per principal component \(m_i=w_i^2/\sum_j w_j^2\), and principal angles to the DAS subspace. Ridge probes are fit on decodability alone over a logarithmic λ grid; causal data is used only to *pick* λ ("intervention-optimal ridge").

## Results & Highlights

- **On the exhaustive Mess3 HMM, the OLS probe is the best decoder and the worst intervention:** probe loss \(9.46\times10^{-5}\) versus \(3.99\times10^{-3}\) for the intervention-optimal ridge probe (\(\lambda=0.1\)) and \(3.21\times10^{-3}\) for DAS — more than an order of magnitude better decoding — yet its intervention loss is \(2.14\times10^{-1}\) against \(1.95\times10^{-3}\) (ridge) and \(1.88\times10^{-3}\) (DAS), a **110× gap** in intervention loss against a 42× deficit in probe loss.
- **The Llama-2-7B OLS probe's intervention loss equals doing nothing:** 13.7 for OLS, versus 7.07 for the intervention-optimal ridge probe (\(\lambda=10^{4}\)) and 6.42 for DAS — ridge and DAS roughly halve it, and the paper states explicitly that 13.7 is what one gets from not intervening at all. Probe losses go the other way: 0.0671 (OLS) < 0.251 (ridge) < 0.689 (DAS).
- **Direction alignment inverts the ranking:** OLS sits 89.8° (HMM) and 84.7° (Llama) from DAS, i.e. essentially orthogonal to the causally trained direction, while the intervention-optimal ridge probes sit at 28.9° and 26.3°.
- **Variance along the recovered direction differs by orders of magnitude:** HMM \(1.16\times10^{-8}\) (OLS) vs \(5.10\) (ridge) and \(9.72\) (DAS); Llama \(2.10\times10^{-2}\) vs \(3.83\) and \(8.28\) (a 182× and 394× gap).
- **Probe mass lands on dead weight:** on the HMM a single principal direction of variance \(10^{-18}\) carries **0.915** of the OLS probe's total mass; on both testbeds OLS mass concentrates at near-zero causal operativity while ridge mass concentrates at high operativity (Figure 5). The heaviest Llama PCs covering 95% of the ridge mass cover only ~31% of the OLS mass.
- **Held-out decodability fails to flag the problem on the HMM:** on an 80/20 split the OLS probe still decodes at \(4.3\times10^{-4}\) vs \(4.9\times10^{-3}\) for the intervention-optimal ridge probe (an order of magnitude better) and remains inert. On Llama the same split does flag it — OLS loss jumps 0.05 → 0.38 while ridge holds 0.27 → 0.28 — which the authors present as the honest asymmetry rather than a uniform story.
- **Spurious covariances are at the predicted noise scale:** a split-half analysis of all 4096 Llama layer-8 PCs finds **3976 of 4096** within 3 standard errors of zero covariance with birth year (Figure 8), matching Equation (8).
- **Intervention faithfulness is non-monotone in λ:** probe loss rises monotonically with λ while intervention loss has an interior minimum (Figure 2), and the angle to DAS is minimized at the intervention-optimal λ on Llama (Figure 7).

## Authors & Credibility

Karl Vilhelmsson Emneby, Julian Yocum, and Cameron Allen are at the Center for Human-Compatible AI (CHAI), UC Berkeley — a group with a durable track record on causal abstraction and interpretability methodology, and the paper is a NeurIPS 2026 workshop submission ("Interpretability as a Science"). The work is explicitly built on two well-known, publicly released setups (Shai et al. 2024's Mess3 transformer; Heinzerling & Inui 2024's Wikidata birth-year probing) and credits both, which makes the two testbeds reproducible in principle. The framing is unusually careful about what is proven: Corollary 3.3 is repeatedly described as a *negative* result about OLS, with the positive claim about ridge flagged as empirical. Citation coverage is current (Makelov et al. 2024 on intervention illusions, RAPTOR 2026, Modell 2026 as concurrent work), and the classical statistics lineage (Hoerl & Kennard 1970; Haufe et al. 2014) is correctly identified — Haufe et al.'s "decoder weights are not the signal" result is the right prior art, and the paper is honest that its contribution is to make that testable by intervention in neural networks.

## Critical Assessment

*Novelty.* The core statistical fact — OLS amplifies noise along low-variance directions — is textbook (the paper says so). The genuine novelties are (a) connecting it to intervention faithfulness rather than held-out MSE, and (b) the empirical finding that a decodability-fit ridge probe, with λ chosen causally, lands 26–29° from DAS. The gap between "known instability" and "known interpretability failure mode" is real but narrower than the framing suggests: Haufe et al. (2014) already made the decoder-weights-are-not-signal argument, and Shai et al. (2024) is cited as leaving causal tests "out of scope" — so the paper is largely completing a test that the field had already queued up.

*Circularity risk in the headline comparison.* The recommendation is "choose λ by intervention faithfulness," but λ is selected using the very ground-truth intervention outcomes that the evaluation then reports on. That is legitimate as a *diagnostic* ("the faithful direction is in the ridge path"), but it is not a procedure you can run on a feature whose causal role you do not already know — which is precisely the situation where you would reach for a probe. The paper acknowledges this ("causal control is partial") but does not test the practical surrogate it actually needs: does λ selected by *held-out decodability* (Gurnee & Tegmark's protocol) or by a proxy like split-half covariance stability recover the faithful direction? Without that, the recommendation is a diagnostic rather than a recipe.

*Baselines are thin.* Only OLS, ridge, and DAS are compared. Partial least squares is mentioned in the intro as the implicit-regularization precedent (Heinzerling & Inui 2024) but never run as a baseline; neither are PCA truncation, elastic net, or variance-normalized regression — all obvious ways to suppress low-variance amplification, and any of them landing near DAS would sharpen the claim that *regularization* rather than *ridge specifically* is what matters. DAS also gets one fit per testbed; no seed variance is reported for any number in Table 1, and the λ grid's resolution is never stated, so "the intervention-optimal λ" is a point estimate of unknown precision.

*Scope limits that bite.* One LLM, one layer (8), one attribute (birth year), one prompt template, 8000 prompts — only 2× the 4096-d activation dimension, which is a weakly determined regression. The theory covers *continuous regression* probes, while most probing practice is logistic; the authors flag this, but it means the paper's central mechanism does not directly license claims about the linear classifiers interpretability papers actually train. The "OLS is causally inert" claim is also established for *the single OLS direction*, and while the restricted-DAS search over the top-100 PCs is a good-faith attempt to close the loophole, it covers 34.8% of the OLS mass — the remaining 65% spread over the long tail is untested.

*Where the paper is strongest.* The held-out HMM result is the sharpest thing in it: OLS generalizes an order of magnitude better out-of-sample and is still inert, which kills the "you just need a proper train/test split" defense in one number. Similarly, the variance-25,570 top PC receiving almost no weight from the intervention-optimal ridge probe is a clean, falsifiable rebuttal of the "ridge chases variance" objection.

## Strengths

- The theory is short, closed-form, and does real work: Equation (7) plus Corollary 3.3 predicts both the *location* (low-variance directions) and the *scale* (\(\sigma_f/(\sigma_v\sqrt{n})\)) of the failure, and the split-half covariance histogram in Figure 8 confirms the predicted scale (3976/4096 PCs within 3 SE of zero).
- The experimental design is genuinely controlled: same activations, same target, same readout — only the fitting procedure varies, so no dataset or architecture confound can explain the OLS/DAS gap.
- Intellectual honesty is above par for the genre: the paper volunteers that its theory only proves the negative claim about OLS, that ridge is not guaranteed to find faithful directions, that even the best ridge probes have intervention loss well above zero, and that held-out decodability flags the problem on Llama but *not* on the HMM.
- The dead-weight analysis is convincing and unusually direct: 91.5% of an OLS probe's mass on a variance-\(10^{-18}\) direction with near-zero operativity is about as clean a demonstration of "decodable but unused" as one could ask for.
- Practical payoff is concrete and cheap to adopt: sweeping λ by intervention effect is a few lines on top of any existing probing pipeline, and the resulting probe is not merely faithful but qualitatively closer to a causally trained direction.

## Limitations

- **λ selection requires causal ground truth.** The intervention-optimal probe needs a known \(g(f+\delta f)\); the paper does not show what to do when only decodability is available, which is the common case. No surrogate selector (held-out MSE, split-half stability, PLS) is validated.
- **Single LLM, single layer, single attribute, single template.** Llama-2-7B layer 8, birth year, "{name} was born in 19", \(n=8000\). Whether layer 8 is special, or whether the effect persists at layers where the residual stream is more entangled, is untested; the layer sweep is relegated to an appendix with 200 base–source pairs.
- **Regression only.** The theory and both testbeds use continuous/multidimensional real-valued targets; logistic probes — the dominant practice — are explicitly out of scope, and the paper defers a logistic counterpart to future work.
- **Restricted-DAS search is partial.** It covers 100 PCs on Llama (34.8% of OLS mass) and 7 on the HMM; the long tail holding ~65% of the Llama OLS mass is not searched, so "no operative direction exists in the OLS span" is established for the head, not the whole.
- **No uncertainty quantification on the main table.** No seeds for DAS, no bootstrap CIs on intervention loss, no stated λ grid resolution — and the Llama numbers rest on 8000 prompts against 4096 dimensions, a ratio where OLS is only marginally determined.
- **Missing regularizer baselines.** PLS, PCA truncation, elastic net, and variance-normalized regression would all test whether the result is about ridge or about suppressing quiet directions in general; the paper argues the latter theoretically but only demonstrates the former empirically.

## Relevance

与"机制可解释性 + 因果干预"这一主线高度相关：它直接质疑了以解码精度为标准的线性探针实践，并给出一个几乎零成本的正则化修正（按干预效果扫 λ），对任何用探针做特征发现或做表征编辑的工作都是应当知道的方法论警告。对关心"可解码 ≠ 被使用"、DAS/因果抽象、以及 LLM 中数值属性（如年份）表征的研究者尤其值得一读。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- What happens if λ is selected by a *causally blind* criterion — split-half covariance stability, held-out MSE, or PLS component count? Does any of them land near the intervention-optimal λ on either testbed?
- Does the phenomenon replicate in a logistic-probe setting, or on a categorical feature where the probe is a classifier and "intervention" means flipping a class prediction? A logistic analogue of Corollary 3.3 would be the highest-value follow-up.
- How does the OLS/DAS angle behave across layers and across models larger than 7B? Is layer 8's advantage (Appendix A) a property of the model or of the birth-year task?
- If OLS mass spreads over a long tail of individually inert components (65% of Llama mass unsearched), could a nonlinear or subspace-restricted search find an operative combination there — i.e. is the failure really per-direction or genuinely subspace-wide?
- Does the same low-variance amplification explain known cases of "probe-guided steering that does nothing" in the activation-steering literature, and would re-fitting those probes with ridge recover the null result into a positive one?
