> **一句话总结：** 作者用比赛官方生成器、官方 checker 和未改动的 GPU 复现了 2026 年夏 GPU MODE 的四个 NLA kernel 竞赛审计：QR 榜前 5 名在 12 个 scored case 上全部 0 失败，但在 1,050 个审计用例（945 个不同输入）上全部翻车（第 1/3/5 名分别 155、327、357 次失败，第 1 名在 9/10 个矩阵族上失败、对 32/176/352 阶秩亏输入返回非有限值），而官方 baseline 0 失败；据此提出 Lean 库 VSCL（5,003 个生产模块、1,700,804 行、48,122 条定理/引理声明，Higham《Accuracy and Stability》28 章全覆盖，内部复用率 74.9%/97.7%）与只做设计设想的 VERNA 验证引擎，并用 RandNLA 综述 35 条证明（单条证明依赖 91–589 条库定理）证明该库能承接研究级结果。

> **TL;DR:** This is an audit-plus-vision paper, not a benchmark paper. The empirical core: all five top-ranked GPU MODE QR kernels passed every one of the competition's 12 scored cases, yet on 1,050 evaluations from the organizers' own generator under the organizers' own checker the winner ("10billiontokens") failed 155 times across 9/10 matrix families, ranks 3 and 5 failed 327 and 357 times, rank 4 failed 3 times, and the `torch.geqrf` baseline failed 0/1,050 — while a deliberately bfloat16-degraded baseline failed 619/1,050, showing the checker does catch gross accuracy loss at small orders but only 49/450 at orders ≥1024 because the 20nε tolerance grows to 2.5× the bfloat16 unit roundoff. The constructive half is VSCL, a Lean 4 library of textbook numerical analysis (5,003 production modules, 1.70 M non-blank lines, 48,122 theorem/lemma declarations, 74.9% of 47,890 public declarations referenced by another declaration), and a 60-task / 360-attempt reuse experiment in which read-only library access cut agent time 56.8% and tokens 38.8% for tasks with exactly one applicable library result (C2, n=20) and 61.8%/56.7% for C3 (n=10), while *increasing* tokens 10.5% when no applicable result exists (C1, n=30). All 360 attempts succeeded, so the study measures cost, not capability. VERNA itself is a stated goal with three steps and no implementation or evaluation.

## 核心贡献

- **一次可重放的 GPU kernel 审计（本文唯一硬实证）**：取 GPU MODE 2026 夏季四个 NLA 竞赛（2 轮 QR、对称特征分解、Cholesky）各榜前 5 名，在 NVIDIA B200 上用比赛原版生成器（10 个矩阵族 × 5 种条件数设置 × 3 个 seed × 7 个 scored shape）与比赛原版 checker 重测。QR 第 2 轮：5 个 entry 的 12 个 scored case 全部通过，但 1,050 次审计评估中第 1/2/3/4/5 名分别失败 155/46/327/3/357 次，baseline 0 次；第一名在所有 32、176、352 阶秩亏输入上返回含非有限值的因子。
- **把"通过测试"和"可靠"彻底分开**：给出四条结论——(i) 可信需要一个明示的精度要求并在**每条代码路径**上满足（第 4 名用自己的快速 QR + 标准 QR 回退，回退输出全部通过、自己的路径失败 3 次）；(ii) 固定测试集通过 ⇒ 对泛化可靠性零信息；(iii) 固定测试可被"针对生成器特化"（5 个 entry 中 4 个识别生成器构造并走专用代码，第 4 名在 order=1024 时只分解 1,024 列中的 768 列）；(iv) 作者身份不再能作为可靠性证据（两位自称 QR 新手的参赛者用 coding agent 在两周内做了 1,500+/近 2,000 次提交，其中一位位列第 5）。
- **量化形式化数学里"数值分析几乎不存在"**：Mathlib 约 182,000 条定理/引理声明中只有 13 条涉及有限精度/条件数/稳定性/收敛（且全在 2 个文件里）；4 个 Lean benchmark 合计 7,091 题中只有 1 题（FormalMATH，且是精确算术下的 3 步梯度下降）涉及数值方法；Isabelle AFP 12/1,040，Coq/Rocq 19/593，AFP 中**没有**任何线性代数舍入误差分析条目。
- **交付 VSCL 库本体**（Lean 4，公开于 lean-computational-mathematics）：Higham 28 章全覆盖、LeVeque 第 1–4 章、Vershynin 第 1–4 章、Eymard–Gallouët–Herbin 与 Nocedal–Wright 选段；5,003 个生产模块 / 1,700,804 行 / 48,122 条声明；对 47,890 条公共声明做依赖审计，74.9% 被其他声明引用、97.7% 通过 ≥2 步可达。
- **库复用收益的受控实验**：从 54 篇论文抽 60 个证明任务，GPT-6 Sol 在"无库(N)"与"只读库(L)"两条件下各跑 3 次共 360 次，**全部成功**；C2（恰好 1 条可用库结果）时间 −56.8%、token −38.8%、提交代码行 −41.7%，C3（≥2 条）−61.8%/−56.7%/−59.1%，C1（无可用结果）+2.6%/+10.5%/+1.8%。
- **研究级验证案例**：把 Drineas–Mahoney RandNLA 综述的 35 条已发表证明形式化进库，单条证明依赖 91–589 条库定理（子空间嵌入 565、Hadamard 最小二乘 589、谱范数界 430），合计引用 1,053 条库定理；并在过程中发现综述遗漏了 entry-sampling 界所需的 thresholding 步骤。
- **提出 VERNA 三步骤**（recognize → test against specification → certificate of trust），并用图灵 1948 舍入误差分析与 1949 program checking 提炼六条设计原则（陈述忠实性、算术假设、敏感度/逼近/舍入误差三分、分块断言组合、对具体输出出具证书、固定验收标准下的灵活证明搜索）。

## Key Contributions

- **A reproducible audit of AI-assisted GPU NLA kernels.** The five highest-ranked entries of the second GPU MODE QR competition (`10billiontokens`, `gau.nernst`, `dhu.randhar`, `zhongmingee`, `michaelmelons`) all scored 0/12 on the competition's scored cases, but failed 155, 46, 327, 3, and 357 of 1,050 audit evaluations respectively (9, 2, 9, 1, and 10 of the 10 matrix families), while the `torch.geqrf` baseline failed 0/1,050. The same pattern holds across all four competitions: 20/20 entries passed their own scored cases and 18/20 failed in discovery; for Cholesky 4/5 and for symmetric eigendecomposition 3/5 failures reproduced under fresh-container reruns.
- **Evidence that a fixed test set certifies almost nothing.** Four of five entries contain code paths that detect the organizers' generator constructions; the runner-up's 46 failures are confined to row-scaled and mixed-conditioning families; the winner returns non-finite values on every rank-deficient input of order 32, 176, or 352.
- **A quantified gap in formalized numerics.** 13 numerical-analysis declarations out of ~182,000 in Mathlib; 1 numerical-method problem out of 7,091 across four Lean benchmarks; 12/1,040 in Isabelle's AFP and 19/593 in Coq/Rocq.
- **VSCL**: 5,003 production Lean modules, 1,700,804 non-blank lines, 48,122 theorem/lemma declarations, covering all 28 chapters of Higham plus LeVeque Ch. 1–4, Vershynin Ch. 1–4, and selections from Eymard et al. and Nocedal–Wright; internal reuse 74.9% (two-step reachability 97.7%), with concrete proof chains (a QR-solve path with fourteen direct dependencies across seven modules).
- **A controlled measurement of library reuse economics**: −56.8% time / −38.8% tokens / −41.7% LOC in C2, −61.8%/−56.7%/−59.1% in C3, +2.6%/+10.5%/+1.8% overhead in C1, over 360 attempts with a 100% success rate.
- **A RandNLA case study** proving 35 published survey results inside VSCL, with 91–589 library theorems per proof, and two documented gaps in the survey itself (missing thresholding for the entry-sampling bound; implicit expectation in the Gram-matrix result).
- **VERNA** as a research programme: recognize the kernel, test against formalized error bounds plus exceptional-value rules, issue a certificate of trust recording checks, inputs, environment, and outcomes; plus six Turing-derived design goals.

## Methodology

**Audit design (Section 2, Appendix B).** The authors take the top five leaderboard entries from each of four GPU MODE competitions and re-run them under the organizers' generator and checkers, changing only the input distribution — the kernels, accuracy requirements, GPU model (NVIDIA B200), PyTorch/Triton stack (2.12.1 / 3.7.1 vs the runner's 2.12.0 / 3.7.0), and checker are otherwise held fixed. The QR checker requires finite output plus two residuals: factorization ‖R − QᵀA‖₁ ≤ 20nε‖A‖₁ and orthogonality ‖QᵀQ − I‖₁ ≤ 100nε with ε = 2⁻²³. The audit grid is 7 scored shapes × 10 families × 5 conditioning settings × 3 seeds = 1,050 cases (945 distinct inputs, since clustered matrices ignore conditioning and row-scaled ones are clamped to ≥4). Two controls are included: the vendor `torch.geqrf` path and a bfloat16-degraded version that rounds each input before calling the vendor routine. For Cholesky/eigendecomposition the audit adds 567 and 996 valid inputs per entry, and requires a "discovery" failure to reproduce in three reruns (two in fresh containers) before counting.

**VSCL construction (Section 3).** A formalization "skill" drives agents through eight stages per claim: enumerate the chapter's claims (checked by a second agent for omissions, and an agent cannot silently drop a hard claim), read each claim and record a "claim packet" (passages, objects, assumptions, assertion), search VSCL and Mathlib for reusable results, write the Lean statement *without* attempting a proof, run a blind adversarial statement review (one agent back-translates the Lean statement into prose without seeing the source, another compares), then prove under fixed accept criteria (no placeholders, no new axioms, no edits to reviewed definitions). False printed claims require a formal counterexample plus a separately named and proved correction.

**Reuse experiment (Appendix C).** 60 proof tasks extracted from 54 papers in Higham's topic space; each task ships a fixed `target.lean`, `context.md` and `Definitions.lean`, identical in both conditions. Conditions N (no library) and L (read-only VSCL plus precompiled artifacts), 3 repetitions each, GPT-6 Sol at xhigh, Lean v4.29.0-rc3 with Mathlib `e8ea1afc3279`. Tasks are binned C1/C2/C3 by how many VSCL results the *extracting* LLM judges applicable under three criteria (non-trivial to reconstruct, beyond elementary algebra, not already in Mathlib). Metrics: wall-clock to submission, total tokens, non-blank non-comment LOC, and direct proof declarations (DP) used.

**Open-weight experiments (Section 5.3, Appendix D).** DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 (via OpenRouter, simple source-to-Lean protocol, four operator-requested continuations) and GPT-6.1 Sol (adapted skill with separate reviewer agents) each formalize the four-page Appetizer and seven-page Chapter 1 of Vershynin from the PDF into a fresh Lean project with no VSCL access; both projects build and pass axiom checks.

**VERNA (Section 4) and the Turing principles (Section 5.4)** are design reasoning, not experiments.

## Results & Highlights

- **Passing the scored cases says nothing.** All five top QR entries had 0/12 scored-case failures, but on the 1,050-case audit grid the winner failed 155/1,050 across 9/10 families, the runner-up 46/1,050 across 2/10, rank 3 327/1,050 across 9/10, rank 4 3/1,050 across 1/10, and rank 5 357/1,050 across all 10/10 — while the `torch.geqrf` baseline failed **0/1,050** and 0/10 families. The 107× headline speedup is 1.22 ms winner vs 131 ms for the *median of 48 baseline submissions*; participant posts quote a 419 ms baseline, which only two of those 48 submissions approach.
- **The checker does catch gross error, but its tolerance loosens with order.** The bfloat16-degraded control — which keeps 8 of 24 significand bits — was rejected 619/1,050 times including all 300 at orders 32 and 176, and 8/12 on scored cases. But because the factorization tolerance 20nε grows with n, at orders 1024/2048/4096 it is 0.625/1.25/2.5 times the bfloat16 unit roundoff 2⁻⁸, and the second-round checker rejected only 49 of that control's 450 outputs at those orders. Analogously, the eigendecomposition checker accepted float8-rounded-input outputs at large orders, and the vendor eigensolver itself failed on 38 inputs plus 2 convergence errors.
- **Failures are structural, not tolerance nitpicks.** The winning QR kernel produced non-finite values in its factors for *every* rank-deficient input of order 32, 176, and 352; rank 5's 357 failures are spread over all ten families (row-scaled 60/105, near-collinear 65/105, banded 42/105); rank 3 fails 45 and 60 of 105 in the banded and row-scaled families. One Cholesky entry failed 85 discovery inputs yet passed all fifteen reruns — an unresolved, non-reproducible failure.
- **Round-1 QR entries are worse, not better.** Replayed under their *own* first-round checker, the five round-1 entries still failed 193, 73, 73, 245, and 3 of the 945-input grid, and under the second-round checker the round-1 winner failed 755 discovery evaluations (vs 157 for the round-2 winner).
- **Reuse of formalized results pays off, but only when a result actually applies.** C2 (20 tasks, exactly one applicable library result): time 56.8% lower, total tokens 38.8% lower, submitted LOC 41.7% lower. C3 (10 tasks, ≥2 applicable results): 61.8%, 56.7%, 59.1% lower. C1 (30 tasks, nothing applicable): 2.6%, 10.5%, 1.8% *higher*. All 360 of 360 attempts compiled and passed the integrity gate, so no accuracy difference exists to report.
- **Internal reuse is high and traceable.** Of 47,890 public declarations written for Higham's book, 35,889 (74.9%) are referenced by another declaration and 46,811 (97.7%) are reachable within two steps; only 143 (0.3%) are unlinked. Worked chains are given: matrix-multiplication error → matrix-vector → dot-product → summation + addition-rounding model, and a QR-solve path with fourteen direct dependencies across seven modules.
- **The library carries research-level content.** 35 published RandNLA survey results are proved in VSCL; individual proofs depend on 91 (sparse subspace embedding) to 589 (least squares with randomized Hadamard transform) library theorems, 1,053 in total, with 57 theorems living in the case study's own files. Formalizing exposed two survey defects: the entry-sampling bound needs the Drineas–Zouzias thresholding step the survey omits, and the Gram-matrix result requires making the expectation explicit and defining the zero-input case.
- **Costs.** Six US$200/month subscriptions cost US$1,200; an observed 13-hour API run cost ≈US$1,300 (≈US$100/hour), extrapolating to ≈US$70,000 for 30 continuous days. In the standalone experiment DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 cost US$4.24 total (US$1.06 Appetizer + US$3.18 Ch. 1) versus an estimated US$26.68 for GPT-6.1 Sol (US$10.21 + US$16.47), but took 3 h 50 min of active time versus 1 h 32 min, and it left the covering-sharpness claim unproved.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The individual pieces are not new: LAProof/VCFloat (Coq error bounds for linear algebra, connected to C via Verified Software Toolchain), Flocq, Gappa, FPTaylor, Daisy/FloVer, FLoPS, FloatLib, Tunnell's FP library, NLA-lib (an existing Lean 4 NLA library), and RepoProver/Choir/OpenMath/Atlas for agentic autoformalization are all discussed, and the paper's "first version of a library of formalized numerical analysis" claim is really "first at this scale for textbook numerical analysis," not first in kind. VERNA's three steps (recognize / test against spec / certify) are close to the standard translation-validation-plus-testing pattern and Section 4 is explicitly a statement of goals with the design deferred to another paper. The genuinely novel contributions are the audit and the reuse economics measurement.

**Rigor.** The audit design is well controlled — same generator, same checker, same GPU, kernels unchanged, plus two controls — and the honesty about the bfloat16 tolerance loosening at large n is exactly the kind of self-undercutting detail that most papers omit. Three real weaknesses: (i) QR discovery failures were never rerun in fresh containers, and the Cholesky experience (one entry with 85 discovery failures that passed all 15 reruns) shows this class of finding does not always reproduce, so "155/1,050" should be read as "at least X reproducible failures" with X unknown; (ii) the "gaming" interpretation of generator-detection code is inference, not measurement — shape- and structure-specialized code paths are normal HPC practice, and the paper's own data shows the one entry with a clean vendor fallback had *all* fallback outputs pass, which is consistent with ordinary specialization rather than deliberate test-gaming; (iii) only the QR round-2 audit is reported in the main text, with the other three competitions relegated to an appendix, so the "all four competitions" claim rests on headline counts (18/20 failed discovery) without per-family depth.

**The reuse experiment has a circularity problem the paper does not confront.** Task binning (C1/C2/C3) was decided by an LLM (Codex/GPT-6 Sol) judging whether a task "would require significant mathematical effort" and whether a library result applies — the same model family that then performs the proofs, from the same team's pipeline. If the extractor is systematically generous about which tasks "have" a supporting result, the C2/C3 gains are inflated and the C1 overhead is deflated. There is also a ceiling effect: 360/360 successes means no quality signal at all, so the study cannot distinguish "the library made proving easier" from "the model solves all of these anyway and the library merely saved keystrokes." A 100% baseline success rate on 60 research-paper proof tasks is itself a surprising result that deserves its own scrutiny — the paper does not report task difficulty distribution, proof lengths, or whether N-condition attempts were near the time/token budget.

**Statistical reporting is thin.** Every headline number is a mean over 3 repetitions of one model at one Lean/Mathlib revision; no confidence intervals, no per-task variance, no significance test is given, and Figure 7 (task-level gains by DP) is described but the main text only quotes category means. The DeepSeek-vs-GPT-6.1 cost comparison is explicitly "not a controlled comparison" — different protocols, different operator assistance, different final scope (DeepSeek's expectation/moment definitions needed domain qualifications and the covering-sharpness claim was left unproved) — yet Table 9 invites exactly the comparison the appendix disclaims, and a reader will take away "13× cheaper."

**Missing ablations that would matter operationally.** For a reader trying to design a kernel acceptance test, the actionable question is how many randomized inputs you need before the failure probability estimate is meaningful. The paper shows a fixed 12-case test is worthless but never sweeps test-set size against detection rate, and never estimates how many of the 945 grid inputs were needed to catch, say, 90% of the observed failures. Likewise, there is no experiment showing that error-bound-guided input selection (VERNA's step 2) beats uniform random sampling — the audit's own grid is uniform over families/conditioning/seeds, so the paper's central methodological proposal is untested against its own baseline.

**Presentation.** Heavily padded: roughly half the 42 pages are appendices, one of which (Appendix A) is a literature survey that largely duplicates the citations, and Section 5.4's six Turing-derived goals are restatements of established verification practice dressed in historical framing ("What would Turing do?" is rhetorical scaffolding, not a method). The paper is honest that many of these are "established ideas," but that admission also means the framing does less work than the title implies.

**Verdict.** The audit is a genuinely valuable, well-executed, publicly replayable result and the VSCL scale numbers are impressive as an artifact report. The paper is much weaker as a scientific claim about *method*: the reuse experiment's binning is circular and its endpoints are cost-only, and VERNA — the paper's proposed answer to the problem it documents — is unimplemented and unvalidated. Read it for the QR audit (Tables 2 and 11, Figure 3) and the reuse percentages with a discount; do not read it as evidence that formalized error bounds guide kernel testing better than well-chosen random tests.

## Strengths

- The audit is a clean, controlled, publicly replayable natural experiment: same kernels, same generator, same checker, same GPU, two controls, and a published audit record with hashes and a replay script.
- The failure mode is diagnosed rather than merely counted: non-finite factors on every rank-deficient input at orders 32/176/352 for the winner, failures concentrated in row-scaled and mixed families for the runner-up, and a documented generator-detection code path that factors only 768 of 1,024 columns.
- Unusual intellectual honesty in two places: reporting that the checker's n-scaled tolerance becomes 2.5× the bfloat16 unit roundoff at n=4096 (which weakens the competition's own test), and reporting that the vendor eigensolver itself fails 38 inputs, so those were excluded from entry counts.
- The reuse experiment is a real controlled trial with a pre-declared three-criterion binning rule and an explicit statement of the *negative* case — C1 tasks get 10.5% more expensive, not free.
- The VSCL numbers are concrete and auditable: 5,003 modules, 1,700,804 lines, 48,122 declarations, and a dependency walk (74.9% / 97.7% / 0.3%) with worked proof chains rather than a single aggregate.
- The RandNLA case study is the strongest evidence for the library's value, because it found actual defects in a published survey (missing thresholding, implicit expectation) — that is what formalization is supposed to buy.
- The formalization workflow's statement-review design is careful about a subtle failure mode: it separates "does the Lean statement imply the source claim" from "does the source claim imply the Lean statement," and back-translates statements blind to the source.

## Limitations

- QR discovery failures were never rerun in fresh containers, unlike Cholesky/eigendecomposition; the paper's own Cholesky evidence (85 discovery failures, 0/15 reruns reproduced) shows some fraction of the reported 155/327/357 counts may be non-reproducible.
- The audit tests one generator, one GPU, one precision (float32), and one checker lineage per competition; nothing is said about whether the failures persist under, e.g., double precision, different batch shapes, or column-major layouts.
- C1/C2/C3 task binning was assigned by an LLM from the same model family used in the experiments, with no human adjudication reported and no inter-rater agreement statistic.
- The reuse study measures only cost (time, tokens, LOC) because every attempt succeeded; there is no measurement of proof quality, no difficulty stratification, and no variance or significance reporting.
- VERNA is a goal statement: no implementation, no prototype, no comparison of error-bound-guided test selection against random or adversarial-input selection.
- VSCL currently proves bounds only in the standard model (relative error ≤ unit roundoff, no overflow/underflow), so it does not yet cover the rank-deficient and non-finite cases that actually caused the audited failures — the gap between the audit and the library is precisely at the failures.
- The "gaming" interpretation is inferential; shape-specialized fast paths are standard HPC engineering and the paper does not distinguish them from deliberate test exploitation.
- The model/cost comparison (US$4.24 vs US$26.68) is not a controlled experiment — different protocols, operator assistance, and coverage — which the appendix concedes but the main text's framing does not fully respect.
- The formalization skill itself is not released ("not part of the public release"), so the workflow's reproducibility rests on prose description plus the library snapshot.
- 107× is measured against the median of 48 baseline submissions (131 ms), while participant posts claim 419 ms; the speedup story is therefore more fragile than the headline.

## Relevance

直接命中「Operator Precision, Determinism & Error Propagation」：这是目前对"AI 生成的 GPU NLA kernel 在官方精度检查下通过、却在官方生成器的新输入上以非有限值和残差超限方式失败"最系统的一次公开审计，且失败集中在秩亏/病态输入——正是低精度 kernel 数值正确性验证的核心场景。其"固定测试集不能证明泛化可靠性 + 用形式化误差界指导 kernel 验收"的论点，也与该方向里 kernel 数值等价性验证（cuBLAS/Triton 级）和跨实现数值漂移的研究直接相关。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. What fraction of the 155/327/357 audit failures survives fresh-container reruns under the QR protocol used for Cholesky? The Cholesky case (85 discovery failures, 0/15 reproduced) suggests the reported counts could be materially inflated.
2. How does detection rate scale with the number of audit inputs? Is 100 grid evaluations enough for a 90% detection guarantee, or is the observed sharpness an artifact of specifically including rank-deficient orders 32/176/352?
3. If the C1/C2/C3 binning were redone by human numerical analysts rather than by Codex/GPT-6 Sol, how much of the 56.8–61.8% time saving and of the 10.5% C1 overhead would survive?
4. Given 360/360 proof successes, what is the actual difficulty ceiling of the extracted 60 tasks? Would the reuse advantage grow or vanish on tasks where the no-library condition sometimes fails?
5. Does error-bound-guided input selection (VERNA step 2) actually beat uniform random or adversarial-input testing at equal budget? This is the paper's central methodological claim and it is untested.
6. Can VSCL's standard-model bounds be extended to the underflow/exceptional-value regime that produced the non-finite factors — and what does a certificate look like for a kernel that is correct except on rank-deficient inputs of specific orders?
7. The runner-up's 46 failures are confined to row-scaled and mixed-conditioning families; is that a genuine algorithm defect, or does it reflect an entry that deliberately special-cases the scored conditioning settings the way four entries special-case the generator's constructions?
