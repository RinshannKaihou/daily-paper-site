> **一句话总结：** Reflection 发布首个开源权重模型 Beam（501.3B 总参 / 23.4B 激活的稀疏 MoE，Apache 2.0 许可），用 23.8T token 预训练、两阶段 midtraining 把上下文扩到 1M，并在 10.5K 张 GB300 上跑了约 1 亿条 rollout 的异步 RL；SWE-bench Verified 达 81.6%、Terminal-Bench v2.1 达 80.1%，但在 DeepSWE 上仅 43.3%，比 DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 的 74.2% 低 30.9 分。

> **TL;DR:** Beam is a 501.3B-total / 23.4B-active open-weight MoE trained on 23.8T tokens with a two-stage midtraining that reaches a 1M context, plus a 4-week asynchronous RL campaign (100M+ rollouts on 10.5K GB300 GPUs). It lands near the Western open-weight frontier — SWE-bench Verified 81.6% vs 77.6% for Inkling 3 Ultra and 70.7% for Nemotron 3 Ultra, Terminal-Bench v2.1 80.1% vs 81.0% for GLM 5.2 — but it is far behind the strongest open models on long-horizon agentic coding (DeepSWE v1.1 43.3% vs 74.2% for DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, 68.0% for Kimi K3) and on knowledge-heavy evals (HLE 36.0% vs 46.9% for Kimi K3), so the "frontier-level reasoning" framing holds mainly on AIME/GPQA-style benchmarks and on inference efficiency rather than on breadth.

## 核心贡献

- 提出并开源 Beam：52 层、256 专家（每 token 激活 8 个）、5120 隐藏维、152K 词表的稀疏 MoE，总参 501.3B、激活 23.4B，提供 BF16 / FP8 / NVFP4 三种权重与一个 1.93B 的投机解码草稿模型。
- 用一套可预测的缩放配方，在 300M 到 23.4B 激活参数量（约四个数量级算力）的模型阶梯上验证了损失可预测性，并给出完整的数据配比（23.8T token，其中代码 35.47%、STEM 23.87%）。
- 两阶段 midtraining：Stage 1 用 850B token、32K 上下文强化逻辑与推理，Stage 2 再用 100B token 把上下文拉到 512K（配合 50% 有机长文 / 15% 合成长文 QA / 35% 回放数据），实测 RULER 可外推到 1M。
- 把 post-training 拆成 "agentic 专家" 与 "alignment 专家" 两条独立 SFT+RL 流水线，再用全词表 on-policy 蒸馏（MOPD）合并，显式测试了 "split-braining" 这一多教师合并失败模式。
- 工程侧给出可复用的规模化经验：110K 并发 rollout、12 秒权重同步、99.99% 动态批打包、异步 checkpoint、NVFP4 用 Local Hessian PTQ 在 32.8M agentic token 上校准，以及 FP8/NVFP4 部署下 1.5×–3.7× 的吞吐收益。

## Key Contributions

- Releases Beam, an Apache-2.0 open-weight sparse MoE (501.3B total / 23.4B active, 52 layers, 256 experts, 8 active, 152K vocab) explicitly optimized for coding, reasoning, and long-horizon agentic work.
- Documents an end-to-end recipe: 23.8T-token pretraining with domain-partitioned web/STEM/code curation and internet-scale OCR, auxiliary-loss-free MoE balancing with cosine-decayed bias, depth-based residual scaling, and a scaling law validated over four orders of magnitude of compute.
- Introduces a two-stage midtraining scheme (850B tokens at 32K, then 100B tokens to 512K context) that preserves general capability while extending effective context to 1M, with an explicit mixture-tradeoff analysis against RULER and regression benchmarks.
- Trains two specialist teachers and merges them by full-vocabulary multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD), reporting teacher-vs-student gaps and a targeted "split-braining" probe.
- Contributes production-grade RL and serving infrastructure detail: 10.5K-GPU asynchronous RL, ~100M rollouts, full-vocabulary distillation at scale, FP8/NVFP4 post-training quantization, and a DFlash 2 block-diffusion drafter with 1.96×–3.03× decode speedups.

## Methodology

**Pretraining.** Beam is a decoder-only MoE Transformer with SandwichNorm, GQA (48 query / 8 KV heads, head dim 128), 5:1 interleaved local-global attention (512-token sliding window locally, NoPE globally), QK-norm plus elementwise gated attention (gate bias initialized to −2.5 to suppress attention-stream outliers), untied embeddings, and logit soft-capping at 30. It is trained on 23.8T tokens in 16,384-token sequences on 6,144 GB300 GPUs in under four weeks, reaching 92.3% goodput and 21% MFU in steady state. Data curation is the heavyweight contribution: 245B raw HTML pages parsed with a math/code-preserving parser down to 97B documents, then domain-partitioned into math/code/STEM/English/non-English and filtered by per-domain pipelines rather than MassiveWeb-style global heuristics, plus dedicated code, STEM, and OCR'd PDF pipelines.

**Midtraining.** Stage 1 trains 850B tokens at 32K context over STEM, competitive programming, SWE, and terminal-use data built on verifiable, correctness-anchored generation (seed → generate → verify → route). Stage 2 adds 100B tokens to reach 512K context; the final mixture is 50% organic long-context (books, papers, dependency-ordered repository packs), 15% synthetic long-context QA/instruction data, and 35% recall data. Hyperparameters use a sawtooth schedule with optimizer-state resets per stage, weight decay scaled as the square of the cosine-tail LR (AdamC), and peak LR at 5% of pretraining peak. Notably, they disable router z-loss, sequence aux loss, and expert-bias updates during midtraining to permit expert specialization.

**Post-training.** A short SFT establishes chat/tool syntax, then GRPO-style RL runs fully asynchronously with 110K concurrent rollouts, median rollout time ~12 minutes (2% exceed an hour), 12-second weight updates, and 99.99% dynamic batch packing. Rewards are verifiable (execution/unit tests) with a second judge applying a reward-hacking rubric: flagged rollouts get zero correctness but keep their length cost. A linear length penalty plus an explicit reasoning-effort input `e` trains the model to trade tokens for accuracy. Around 1M tasks survive filtering from a pool of several million. The alignment teacher is trained separately on policy-conditioned deliberative-alignment SFT plus context-aware RL over single-turn, multi-turn, jailbreak, and agentic settings; the two teachers are then merged by full-vocabulary reverse-KL on-policy distillation, with per-component routing weights (e.g., 1.0 agentic for STEM/code/search, 0.5/0.5 for instruction following, 0.0/1.0 for safety and model identity).

**Serving.** Post-training quantization produces FP8 (RTN, W8A8, static per-channel weight scales, dynamic per-token activation scales) and NVFP4 (E2M1, W4A4 on MoE experts only, Local Hessian scale search calibrated on 1,000 × 32,768-token agentic trajectories = 32.8M tokens). A 1.93B block-diffusion drafter (5 layers reading Beam hidden states at layers 4/16/28/40/46, 4K context attention, trained mostly at 8K) is released alongside.

## Results & Highlights

- **Agentic coding is bifurcated.** Beam scores 81.6% on SWE-bench Verified vs 77.6% for Inkling 3 Ultra and 70.7% for Nemotron 3 Ultra (+4.0 / +10.9 pts), yet only 43.3% on DeepSWE v1.1 vs 74.2% for DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and 68.0% for Kimi K3 — a 30.9-pt deficit that directly contradicts a general "coding-optimized" reading of the headline.
- **Code comprehension collapses.** On SWE Atlas Codebase QnA Beam reaches 31.8% against 61.0% for GLM 5.3 and 68.0% for Kimi K3 — less than half the strongest baseline — while on SWE-Bench Pro v2-Hard it gets 76.5% vs 84.3% (GLM 5.3) and 88.2% (Kimi K3), and on Terminal-Bench v2.1 80.1% vs 88.3% (Kimi K3) and 90.6% (DeepSeek V4.1 Flash).
- **Math/science is close but not leading.** AIME 2026: 97.7% vs 99.2% for GLM 5.2 (−1.5) and 97.1% for Inkling 3 Ultra; GPQA Diamond 90.6% vs 93.5% for Kimi K3 (−2.9) and 91.2% for GLM 5.2; but HLE without tools is 36.0% against 46.9% (Kimi K3), 43.6% (Qwen 3.8 Max) and 39.1% (DeepSeek V4.1 Flash), i.e. 10.9 pts behind the leader on the hardest reasoning set.
- **Knowledge and factuality are the weakest lane.** SimpleQA Verified 41.8% vs 53.7% for Kimi K3 and 44.5% for DeepSeek V4.1 Flash; AA-Omniscience v1 index 10.8 vs 22.6 for GLM 5.3 and 14.2 for Inkling 3 Ultra; IFBench 79.6% vs 82.8% for Qwen 3.8 Max. Beam wins LongBench v2 (67.5% vs 66.3% Qwen 3.8 Max, 64.0% GLM 5.2) and beats every listed baseline on AA-LCR except Kimi K3 (78.2% vs 88.7%).
- **Expert merging mostly works, with one real regression.** The distilled student beats its agentic teacher on SWE-Bench Pro v1 (67.1% vs 62.2%), Terminal-Bench v2.1 (80.1% vs 77.7%), SWE-bench Multilingual (77.7% vs 75.7%) and SciCode (49.5% vs 47.2%), but drops on DeepSWE v1.1 (40.2% vs 43.4%) and AIME 2026 (96.9% vs 97.3%) — consistent with distillation compressing the tail of hard agentic behavior.
- **Quantization is nearly free.** FP8 recovers 99.93% and NVFP4 98.98% of the median BF16 benchmark score; NVFP4 halves-plus the footprint (933.81 GiB → ~273.5 GiB) and lifts per-GPU throughput at 50 tok/s/user from 122 to ≥296 tok/s (≥2.4× on 8 GPUs, ≥3.7× on 4 GPUs vs BF16-8GPU), while AIME 2026 moves only 98.0 → 97.5 and GPQA Diamond 90.0 → 89.3.
- **Speculative decoding delivers.** DFlash 2 reaches acceptance length τ = 4.38 (sampled) / 4.64 (greedy) out of a maximum of 8 over 1,562 turns, with decode speedups of 1.96×–3.03× at concurrency 1 (GSM8K 3.03×, MT-Bench 1.96×) and 1.89×–2.75× at concurrency 32; τ stays 4.22–4.58 on DeepSWE traces from 16K to 128K tokens of context.
- **Safety trade-off is documented, not hidden.** Conversational ISE gives 88.9% refusal/pivot on harmful requests at 81.5% compliance on harmless ones; scaling the alignment teacher 100B → 501B cut ISE over-refusals by 48.7% relative at the cost of a 3.1% drop in harmful-request pass rate, and RL on safety moved refusal +8.3% relative while compliance fell 10.5% relative, later partly recovered by MOPD (−4.4% / +9.9%).

## Authors & Credibility

The paper is a corporate technical report by the Reflection Team (Reflection AI), authored collectively with the full contributor list in Section 9 and a citation stub naming Adam Abdulhamid, Osama Abuelsorour and others. It is not peer-reviewed and follows the now-standard "open-weight lab tech report" genre: enormous internal detail, self-run evaluations, and selective disclosure of method. Credibility signals are real, though: pretraining goodput/MFU and failure-recovery numbers are the kind of operational detail that is hard to fake, the NVFP4 recipe ablation reports held-out KL and top-1 agreement (96.82% for the released recipe) rather than single benchmark runs, the safety chapter uses named third-party evaluators (Gray Swan, Andon Labs, SecureBio, Irregular) and reports unfavorable results (MBCT 37.9%, eighth of thirteen models; prompt-injection ASR 3.10%/3.60% with several models better), and the paper explicitly attributes small quantization "gains" to sampling variance. The weakness is the evaluation layer: most competitor numbers are either reproduced on an internal harness or lifted from official report cards (marked †), and the ISE suite that anchors the central safety claim is in-house and only promised for future open-sourcing.

## Critical Assessment

The report is technically serious but its framing is tilted. **Novelty** is moderate-to-low at the algorithm level — GQA, interleaved local/global attention, auxiliary-loss-free MoE balancing, GRPO, deliberative alignment, on-policy distillation, and block-diffusion drafters are all established; the genuinely new pieces are the two-stage midtraining mixture (50/15/35) with an explicit regression audit, the "split-braining" probe for multi-teacher merges, and the Local Hessian NVFP4 recipe with held-out-KL model selection. **Rigor** is uneven. The strongest evidence is on serving (Tables 13–18 are internally consistent and ablate one variable at a time) and on RL stability (median per-token KL 2.2e-4 nats, gradient-norm median 1.7e-3). The weakest is the headline capability comparison. Three specific problems:

1. **Selective baseline coverage in the coding table.** DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, which wins DeepSWE (74.2) and Terminal-Bench (90.6), is absent from SWE-Bench Pro v1/v2-Hard and SWE Atlas; Kimi K3, which wins SWE Atlas (68.0), has no SWE-bench Verified row; conversely Beam's best rows (SWE-bench Verified, SWE-bench Multilingual) are exactly the rows where the top competitors are dashes. With 7 benchmarks and 7 baselines, the missing-cell pattern is not random.
2. **Apples-to-oranges base-model table.** Table 23 compares "Beam Base" — explicitly *after pretraining and midtraining*, i.e. already trained on correctness-anchored STEM/math/code data — against DeepSeek V4 Pro Base and Nemotron 3 Ultra Base, and the margins are implausible: MATH 94.9 vs 64.5, AIME 2026 63.7 vs 1.9, GPQA 72.4 vs 41.9. Footnote (a) says baselines use 5-shot for GPQA while Beam uses 0-shot, and footnote (b) says Beam uses 0-shot MATH; a 62-point AIME gap between two base checkpoints is far more likely to reflect midtraining-on-math plus harness differences than capability. A 63.7% AIME score from a base model also invites contamination questions that the paper does not address.
3. **The efficiency claim is asserted, not measured.** "3–4× less inference compute than many models of its class" appears three times (abstract, intro, conclusion) but Section 5 never reports tokens-per-task or FLOPs-per-correct-answer against a baseline. The supporting evidence — Figure 16/40 (Beam-vs-Beam token efficiency through RL) and Tables 15–16 (Beam BF16 vs FP8 vs NVFP4) — is entirely within-family. Nor is the competitor token budget disclosed: all evals use "reasoning effort 512," a Beam-specific knob, with no statement that baselines were given an equivalent budget. Likewise the 1M context headline exceeds what was trained: midtraining stops at 512K (Section 3) and 1M is an extrapolation measured only by RULER (Figure 41), with no 1M-token task-level evaluation.

Two further points: the safety claim of "highest refusal/pivot rate among open-weight models on the Pareto frontier" rests on an unreleased in-house suite (ISE), so it is unverifiable at publication and not comparable to the third-party prompt-injection results in §7.5, where Beam sits mid-pack (3.10% ASR on MirrorDojo with no defense prompt, versus 0.3% once the shipped IPI policy is enabled — a 10× swing that shows how policy-prompt-sensitive these numbers are). And the "greater model capacity can substantially improve responses" conclusion is drawn from a single 100B → 501B teacher swap, described as "proving" a causal claim from one data point.

**Reproducibility** is partial: weights (BF16/FP8/NVFP4) and a drafter are released under Apache 2.0, and the quantization recipe is specified well enough to re-run, but data, RL environments, judges, sandbox stack, and the 10.5K-GPU campaign are not reproducible by any outside group. **Significance** is nonetheless high: a credible new Western open-weight frontier point with published 1M-context, quantization, and asynchronous-RL infrastructure details is useful to both practitioners and the broader open-weights comparison literature — provided readers use the per-benchmark numbers rather than the abstract's framing.

## Strengths

- Unusually complete infrastructure disclosure for this genre: goodput (92.3%), MFU (21%), 110K concurrent rollouts, 12-second weight sync, 99.99% batch packing, median/95th-percentile restart times, and named failure modes with recovery procedures.
- Honest within-family ablations where it matters: NVFP4 recipe selection is done on held-out KL/top-1 agreement (96.82%) rather than benchmark noise, and the paper explicitly warns that FP8/NVFP4 "wins" on individual benchmarks are sampling variance.
- The two-stage midtraining analysis reports the *regressions* (GPQA −1.2, Simple Terminal −1.7, MMLU-Pro −0.4) alongside the RULER gains (+58.4 at 512K), which is more than most context-extension sections offer.
- Deliberate stress-testing of the merge step: the "split-braining" probe, per-component teacher routing table (Table 10), and the admission that the student drops 3.2 pts on DeepSWE relative to its teacher.
- Safety chapter reports unfavorable comparisons plainly (MBCT 37.9%, eighth of thirteen; prompt-injection ASR worse than DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, Kimi K3, GLM-5.3, Qwen 3.8 Max, DeepSeek V4 Pro) instead of burying them.

## Limitations

- No head-to-head compute or token measurement backing the 3–4× efficiency claim; all efficiency evidence is Beam-internal.
- Baseline coverage is incomplete and non-random across benchmarks, and competitor reasoning budgets are not equalized or reported.
- Base-model comparison mixes a midtrained checkpoint against pretraining-only baselines and uses inconsistent shot settings, inflating math/STEM margins.
- 1M context is an extrapolation from 512K midtraining with only RULER evidence; no long-horizon task evaluation at 1M is provided.
- Safety Pareto claims depend on an in-house, not-yet-released benchmark (ISE), and the paper reports no inter-annotator agreement or judge-validation statistics for its safety judges.
- Data, RL task pools, judges, and training code are not released, so the "open-weight" contribution is weights-only, and the 1M-task RL curation is unverifiable.
- Reasoning-effort conditioning is trained with a length penalty, so reported scores at e = 512 may reflect a particular token-budget regime that competitors cannot be tuned to.

## Relevance

这篇报告对关注「大规模 MoE 训练 + 异步 RL + 量化部署」的研究者价值很高，里面有大量可复用的工程细节（NVFP4 Local Hessian 校准、全词表 on-policy 蒸馏、110K 并发 rollout 的调度与恢复）；但如果关注的是 agentic coding 的真实能力，需要注意它的强项集中在 SWE-bench Verified 这类偏饱和的基准，而在 DeepSWE、SWE Atlas 等长程任务上明显落后于 DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 和 Kimi K3。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- What exactly drives the 30.9-point DeepSWE gap to DeepSeek V4.1 Flash — task horizon, harness scaffolding, tool-token budget, or reward shaping? An ablation swapping harnesses at fixed weights would settle it.
- Does Beam's advantage survive when competitors are given the same realized token budget rather than a nominal "effort" setting? A Pareto plot of accuracy vs output tokens per model would substantiate or kill the 3–4× claim.
- Why does DeepSWE regress from the agentic teacher (43.4) to the merged student (40.2) while SWE-Bench Pro v1 improves (62.2 → 67.1)? Is this distillation temperature/coverage, or genuine interference from the alignment teacher?
- How much of the base-model AIME 2026 = 63.7% comes from midtraining on correctness-anchored math, and were the RL/eval math sets decontaminated against AIME 2026 specifically?
- What happens at 1M tokens on tasks requiring multi-hop retrieval over many documents, as opposed to RULER's synthetic needles? The 512K → 1M extrapolation is untested at the task level.
- How sensitive is the 88.9%/81.5% safety Pareto point to the judge model and rubric version, given the 10× ASR swing observed when an IPI policy prompt is added?
