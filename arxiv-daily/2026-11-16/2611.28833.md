> **一句话总结：** 提出 Self-WSP——让 Qwen3.5-0.8B 在无梯度、无训练的条件下读取自己的 318 个张量目录，用语法约束的 JSON 提出置零/缩放/加偏置/重采样/去方向等 7 类张量级编辑，再由一个固定外层循环用带参考答案的练习题决定是否保留，同一模型同时扮演"编辑器"与"解题者"。GSM8K 从 11.8% 提到 61.0%（连续自编辑 62.2%，5.27×），MATH500 从 7.0% 提到 24.7%（连续 32.2%，4.54×），最小的成功编辑只改 16 个参数；但诊断显示增益主要来自"按目录顺序总改第 0 层 A_log"、直接复用刚读到的统计量（−1.979）和幻觉式解释（有条解释原文是"The cost of one chair is $11."），同预算随机搜索在 GSM8K 上就打平（32.8% vs 31.9%），且原始模型约 90% 的题在 32k token 内根本没答完。

> **TL;DR:** Self-WSP asks whether a model can improve itself by editing its own weights. Qwen3.5-0.8B (24 layers, 3:1 gated-delta / standard attention) reads a 318-tensor catalog, proposes one or two edits through a shape-derived JSON grammar, and is evaluated by a fixed outer loop on held-out practice questions with reference answers; kept edits change the editor as well as the solver. On GSM8K test (1,319 questions, 2 samples) self-editing lifts accuracy from 11.8% to 61.0% (ordered interface Top-1) and to 62.2% after continued editing (+50.4 pts vs the unedited model); on MATH500 (500 questions, 4 samples) from 7.0% to 24.7%, reaching 32.2% at +12 rounds. Against baselines: same-budget random search reaches 32.8% on GSM8K (vs 31.9% for the comparable shuffled-interface self-edit, i.e. a tie) but only 6.6% on MATH500, and the best single random edit reaches 22.3% / 14.0% — GPT-6 Astra's re-evaluated edits sit at 66.9% / 49.5%, so the 0.8B model is within 4.7 pts on GSM8K but 17.3 pts behind on MATH500. The gains are real but largely attributable to search plus selection over a tolerant proxy, and to repairing a specific repetition pathology (the original model fails to finish ~90% of questions within its 32k-token budget) rather than to the model understanding its own weights.

## 核心贡献

- **首次把"权重空间编程"（WSP）闭合到模型自身**：Takehi et al. (2026) 用 GPT-6 读取并编辑*另一个*小模型的权重；本文让 Qwen3.5-0.8B 读取并编辑**自己的**权重，并继续用编辑后的权重同时充当解题者和下一轮编辑器，构成一个可迭代的 RSI 原型（Self-WSP）。
- **一个可复现、受限、无梯度的自编辑接口**：318 个可编辑张量的目录（覆盖全部 24 层，给出名字与形状）、不透明标识符（p273/p298，跨 run 随机分配）、最多 64 个真实权重值 + 均值/标准差/行列范数、两张 disjoint 练习题批次的反馈（含参考答案与模型回答末尾 600 字符）；编辑用从张量形状构造的 JSON grammar 表达，限制 7 种操作、数值 ∈[−10,10]、最多改 800 万个 BF16 值，并显式禁止全张量均匀缩放、重复编辑、零改动。
- **实测出真实的自我提升**：24 个"选中模型"中 22 个显著优于原始模型（配对 bootstrap + Bonferroni），GSM8K 11.8%→61.0%（ordered）、MATH500 7.0%→24.7%（ordered），连续自编辑进一步到 62.2% / 32.2%；最好的 GSM8K 模型只改了 48 个数值，最好的 MATH500 模型只改 16 个数值（第 0 层 linear-attention 的 A_log）。
- **给出"这不是理解"的四条实证**：编辑位置跟随目录顺序（ordered 接口下 62% 的有效编辑落在目录第一项 layer 0，是 4.4% 均匀份额的 14 倍；shuffled 接口下只有 2.9%）；编辑数值抄刚读到的统计量（有效编辑里 9%–28% 的数值等于同一次调用刚读到的均值/标准差/范数，−1.979 这一个数出现在 4 个选中模型里）；解释多为幻觉（最好 GSM8K 模型的那条解释声称"去掉乘以 −1.9795 的缩放"，实际却又乘了一次 −1.9795）；保留的编辑常常有害（shuffled 接口下 >40% 的"保留了一处编辑"的轮次反而降低了验证准确率）。
- **同预算随机基线 + spot-check 双重对照**：用随机采样器替换编辑器（同样的张量、操作、选择规则和预算），GSM8K 上 32.8% vs 31.9% 打平，MATH500 上同预算找不到改进、需多约 70% 预算才到 13.6%；同时预先固定一组从不参与选择的 spot-check runs，给出"普通一次 run"的真实水平（ordered 接口平均 +24.7 / +7.7 点，远低于 Top-1 的 +49.2 / +17.7）。
- **定位增益的物理来源**：Qwen3.5-0.8B 的主要失效是思考时反复重复、32k token 内答不完（原始模型约 90% 的题如此），准确率与"未完成比例"几乎线性相关；因此任何打断重复的微小扰动都能大幅提分——这也解释了为什么 GPT-6 无法改进很少重复的 Gemma 3 270M。

## Key Contributions

- **Closes the WSP loop on the model itself.** Prior WSP work (Takehi et al., 2026) has a frontier model edit *another* network. Here the editor and the edited network are the same Qwen3.5-0.8B checkpoint, so every kept edit changes both the solver and the next proposal — the minimal concrete instance of recursive self-improvement through weights rather than data, code or prompts.
- **A fully specified, reproducible self-editing interface.** A 318-entry tensor catalog with opaque per-run identifiers, statistics (mean, std, up to 64 raw values with positions, row/column norms), and two disjoint practice batches with reference answers; a shape-derived JSON grammar with 7 operations (zero, scale, add, fill, redraw from mean/std, direction removal, bounded NumPy expression) and hard rejection rules (uniform whole-tensor rescale, repeated edit, no-op after BF16 rounding, >8M changed values, embeddings/output layer/final norm excluded).
- **Demonstrated self-improvement, with the honest denominators.** 22 of 24 selected models are significantly better than the original; GSM8K 11.8%→61.0%, MATH500 7.0%→24.7% under the ordered interface, 62.2%/32.2% after continued rounds; and the paper reports spot-check runs (never selected) that average only +24.7 / +7.7 points, plus the fact that under the shuffled interface >40% of edit-keeping rounds lower validation accuracy.
- **Four independent behavioural probes showing the model does not understand its edits.** Edit location tracks catalog order (62% of valid edits in layer 0 when the catalog is layer-sorted vs 2.9% when shuffled; 15/15 ordered-interface selected models edit layer-0 `A_log`, 0/9 shuffled ones); edit values copy just-read statistics (9–28% of values match a statistic from the same call; the mean −1.979 appears in four selected models); hypotheses are hallucinated (one kept edit's full explanation is "The cost of one chair is $11.", copied verbatim from a practice response); and the direction-removal / expression operators that could express structure are essentially never used.
- **Matched random baselines that bound the claim.** Random search with identical tensors, operations, selection rules and budget ties self-editing on GSM8K (32.8% vs 31.9%), loses on MATH500 (6.6% vs 17.3% at equal budget, 13.6% with ~70% more budget), and a frozen editor (original weights as editor, edited weights as solver) behaves like both — so "the editor itself improves" brings no measurable benefit. Single random edits over 352 draws reach 22.3% / 14.0%, and GPT-6's published zeroing edit (22.0% / 13.8% when re-evaluated) lies inside that random-edit range.
- **A mechanistic explanation of why 16 numbers suffice.** The failure mode is repetition: the original model fails to finish ~90% of questions within 32k tokens, accuracy falls almost linearly in the share of unfinished answers, and any edit that breaks the repetition converts those into correct answers. This reframes "small edits have outsized effects" (cf. super weights) as a property of the benchmark's interaction with a specific decoding pathology, and predicts when WSP will and will not work.

## Methodology

**One model, two roles.** Qwen3.5-0.8B is a 24-layer model where three of every four layers use linear attention (gated delta networks) and the fourth standard attention. As *editor* it runs with thinking off; as *solver* it runs with thinking on, using Takehi et al.'s sampling settings (temperature 0.6, top-p 0.95, top-k 20, presence penalty 1.5, 32,768-token limit). Both roles always read the current weights, so a kept edit changes the editor too.

**Reading.** The editor sees a catalog of the 318 tensors it may edit (all 24 layers, name and shape per entry, opaque IDs). After naming one or two tensors it receives shape, mean/std, up to 64 sampled values with positions, and row/column norms — plus practice feedback: correctness, accuracy, share of unfinished answers, mean response length, degree of repetition, and for the first four questions the question, reference answer, prediction and last 600 characters of the response. Each call costs 11.6k–23k input tokens and returns 15–150 tokens. Unlike GPT-6, the editor has no Python environment: everything is precomputed by the host.

**Writing.** Edits are emitted as JSON under a grammar built from tensor shapes (Willard & Louf, 2023; Geng et al., 2023). An edit names a tensor, a layout (flat entries, whole rows, whole columns), a start and count (circular wrap), and one operation: zero, scale, add, fill, redraw from the tensor's mean and |v|·std, direction removal (for matrices; subtract each selected column's component along a named column, scaled), or a bounded NumPy expression over the slice. Values must lie in [−10, 10]. Rejected outright as invalid: uniform whole-tensor rescaling, repeats of an evaluated edit, edits that change nothing after BF16 rounding, >8M changed values, context overflow. Embeddings, the output layer and the final norm are not editable. The grammar guarantees format, not semantics — the host never repairs an edit.

**Keeping.** Each run proceeds in rounds from a starting model with a fixed budget of valid edits. A proposal is applied to a *copy* of the weights and checked on two disjoint practice batches; it is kept only if the edited model solves enough additional questions on the two batches combined without doing worse on either, after which the copy becomes current. Rounds also stop early after 24 proposals or several consecutive rejections.

**Two interfaces.** *Shuffled*: all tensors listed in random order, editor picks directly, 32 practice questions per batch, keep on a +1 total gain, three rounds with budgets 4/6/12. *Ordered*: catalog grouped by layer and sorted by name, editor first picks a layer then tensors (3 calls per proposal instead of 2), 128 questions per batch, keep on a +3 total gain, 8 valid edits per round, 4 or 8 rounds. The ordered interface was designed after seeing the shuffled results.

**Selection, testing, statistics.** Practice questions come from the training split with reference answers and are the only optimization signal; validation questions also come from the training split but are never shown to the editor and only rank models after the search; test sets are used once, at the end. GSM8K uses 5,095 usable training questions (3,559 practice pool + 1,536 held out; 256/512 validation, 128 screening), MATH 7,273 (5,737 + 1,536). Every round draws two fresh non-overlapping practice batches. A setting (dataset × interface × starting point) contains 12 or 24 runs; the three runs with the largest validation gain become Top-1/2/3 and are the only ones tested. Because that shows the best case, the paper also fixes spot-check runs in advance by hash and tests them whatever their outcome. Evaluation is all 1,319 GSM8K test questions with 2 samples and all 500 MATH500 questions with 4 samples; each edited model is compared with the original run on the same hardware and seeds via a paired bootstrap with Bonferroni correction. Continued runs start from selected shuffled models and keep editing under the ordered interface (+k = k accumulated ordered rounds).

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline self-improvement.** GSM8K test accuracy goes 11.8% → 61.0% with the ordered interface (Top-1) and 62.2% after continued editing (+50.4 pts, 5.27×); MATH500 goes 7.0% → 24.7% (ordered Top-3) and 32.2% at +12 continued rounds (+25.2 pts). Of the 24 selected models, 22 are significantly better than the original (paired bootstrap, Bonferroni), and the other two gain just over one point.
- **Random baselines close much of the gap.** Random search with the *same* budget and selection rules scores 32.8% on GSM8K vs 31.9% for the comparable shuffled-interface self-edit (self-editing does not win), and on MATH500 finds no improvement at equal budget (6.6% vs 17.3%), needing ~70% more budget to reach 13.6%. Best *single* random edits reach 22.3% (GSM8K, scaling 4,096 convolution weights by −2) and 14.0% (MATH500, scaling 64 rows of a query projection by −2).
- **GPT-6 comparison, re-evaluated in the same pipeline.** GPT-6's zeroing edit (256 columns of layer-15 attention output) scores 22.0% / 13.8%; its direction-removal edit (direction computed from activations) scores 66.9% / 49.5%. So the 0.8B self-edit is within 4.7 pts on GSM8K (62.2 vs 66.9) but 17.3 pts behind on MATH500 (32.2 vs 49.5) — the gap opens exactly where the cheap repetition repair stops helping.
- **Edits are tiny.** The 24.2% MATH500 model differs from the original in 16 numbers (`A_log` of layer 0, which sets per-head forgetting rates); a 32-number GSM8K edit reaches 59.9%; one continued MATH500 model differs from its starting point in just 2 numbers yet gains 6.8 pts (25.4% → 32.2%); no selected model changes more than 0.2% of its weights. For comparison, shuffled-interface model B changes 1,048,686 values and reaches only 28.7%.
- **The search is wasteful and the model is not the reason it works.** Across all runs the editor made 11,580 proposals, 69% invalid despite the grammar (mostly repeats or uniform rescalings the prompt forbids); only 12% of valid edits are kept; it never used direction removal or expressions. Under the shuffled interface >40% of edit-keeping rounds lower validation accuracy, and a third still do on MATH under the ordered interface.
- **Behaviour tracks the interface, not the mathematics.** With the shuffled catalog, layer 0 receives 2.9% of valid edits (its 4.4% catalog share); with the layer-ordered catalog it receives 62%, and the preference is already present in each round's first proposal before any outcome is seen. All 15 selected ordered-interface models edit layer-0 `A_log`; none of the 9 shuffled ones do. 9–28% of values in valid edits equal a statistic read in the same call, and the number −1.979 appears as a scale or shift in four selected models.
- **Ordinary runs are much weaker than the headline.** Pre-registered spot-check runs under the ordered interface average +24.7 pts on GSM8K and +7.7 pts on MATH500 on the test sets, versus +49.2 / +17.7 for the selected models. On GSM8K, 11 of 24 ordered runs gain more than 23 pts while the other 13 gain less than 10. Continued editing mostly stalls: the best GSM8K spot-check run slips from 53.3% at +4 to 51.6% at +8, and across all 12 eight-round MATH runs kept edits leave validation accuracy unchanged on average (one falls from 28.4% to 23.4%).
- **Why 16 numbers suffice.** On the original model ~90% of questions are never finished within the 32k-token budget because of repetitive thinking, and accuracy falls almost linearly with the share of unfinished answers; almost every answer an edit makes correct is one the original never completed. Screening confirms the specificity: the best shuffled self-edit beats all 352 single random edits on both datasets, and continued +4 MATH models gain up to 17.2 screening points, more than twice the best random edit — so the proposals are better than chance, just not for the reasons claimed.

The paper is unusually candid: it reports the random-search tie, the frozen-editor null result, spot-check runs that undercut its own headline, the hallucinated explanation verbatim, the fact that the ordered interface was designed after seeing the shuffled results, and that the supervised outer loop uses reference answers. ## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The delta over Takehi et al. (2026) is conceptually small — same interface family, same 7-ish operations, same outer-loop accept/reject — but the *self* version is the interesting configuration, and the paper adds the measurement that matters most for RSI: what an ordinary run achieves, not what the best of 24 does. The behavioural probes (catalog-order ablation, statistic-copying rate, hallucinated hypotheses) are the real contribution; without them the headline would be a 5× GSM8K number that a random sampler partly reproduces.

**Rigor, and the one big hole.** The strongest configuration has no matched random-search control. Random search exists only under the shuffled interface (32.8% GSM8K) and never under the ordered interface, which is where 61.0% and 24.7% come from — yet the paper's own argument is that ordered-catalog access is what unlocks layer-0 `A_log`, and random search would also have to draw `A_log` under that interface. The authors flag this in Limitations, but it means the single most quotable number is the one without a baseline. Similarly, the layer-0 preference is not tested causally (no reversed catalog), so "picks the first entry" is supported by the 62% vs 2.9% contrast and the first-proposal analysis rather than by intervention.

**The repetition confound is fatal to a strong reading.** If ~90% of the original model's failures are unfinished 32k-token generations, then "GSM8K 11.8% → 61.0%" mostly measures how many timeouts were converted into completed attempts, not how much mathematics the model learned. The paper says this plainly and even uses it to explain why GPT-6 failed on Gemma 3 270M, but the abstract still leads with the 5× figure. A cleaner control would have been an external repetition breaker (e.g. a n-gram/no-repeat penalty or forced answer-format change) at zero weight cost — the paper does not run one, so we cannot tell how much of the +49 pts is "any perturbation that stops looping".

**Selection and significance.** Top-1/2/3 are the best three of 12 or 24 runs by validation gain, and validation is 256–512 questions drawn from the same held-out pool used for screening; with a long-tailed estimation error the maxima are optimistically biased, which the spot-check numbers quantify (+24.7 vs +49.2 on GSM8K). Because the test sets are used once and paired-bootstrap corrected, the *reported* significances are probably fine, but the effect size a practitioner would see is roughly half the headline. Table 1 also shows the third-place shuffled MATH models at 8.1% and 8.3% marked †, i.e. indistinguishable from the 7.0% original.

**Small inconsistencies and loose ends.** Figure 2's continued MATH500 bar reads 31.8 while the abstract and Table 1's Top-2 report 32.2 — which of the three selected models is headlined shifts between figures and text. The GPT-6 comparison is explicitly not apples-to-apples (GPT-6 had a Python environment, different budgets and feedback), so the "within 5 points on GSM8K" framing flatters the 0.8B model. The 8M-value edit cap is never ablated, nor is the tensor-catalog granularity (why 318 tensors and not, say, per-head slices or individual rows for `A_log`). And "continued self-editing keeps improving" rests on three GSM8K models and three MATH models, one of which gains 6.8 pts from two numbers — closer to a lucky re-roll than to compounding improvement.

## Strengths

- Asks a genuinely interesting question and answers it with the least flattering evidence included: the random-search tie, the frozen-editor null, the spot-check collapse, and the "The cost of one chair is $11." explanation are all in the main text.
- The behavioural analysis is the best part: catalog-order ablation (62% vs 2.9% in layer 0), statistic-copying rate (9–28%), and the first-proposal analysis cleanly separate "the model reasons about weights" from "the model reads the list top-down".
- The mechanism story (repetition → unfinished answers → linear accuracy loss) is concrete, falsifiable, and retrospectively explains a published negative result on Gemma 3 270M.
- Complete artifact trail: every kept edit for all 24 selected models, the verbatim system prompt, random-sampler distributions, and exact data splits — enough to re-run or to challenge the conclusions.
- Methodologically careful where it counts: disjoint practice batches, validation never fed back to the editor, pre-registered spot-check runs, paired bootstrap with Bonferroni, both datasets searched separately.

## Limitations

- One model (Qwen3.5-0.8B), two math benchmarks, and a failure mode (repetitive thinking) that is unusually easy to perturb — the results say little about whether the loop works on a model that already finishes its answers, or on code, agentic or long-horizon tasks.
- The outer loop is supervised: it needs reference answers on practice questions, so this is self-editing, not unsupervised self-improvement.
- No random-search baseline under the ordered interface, and no reversed-catalog test of the layer-0 preference; the strongest numbers therefore lack their matched control.
- The ordered interface was designed after inspecting the shuffled results, so part of the reported gain is interface engineering informed by the outcome being predicted.
- Selection over 12–24 runs with validation-then-test means headline numbers are best-of-N; spot-check runs roughly halve them.
- The GPT-6 comparison uses different tools, feedback and budgets and is only indicative.

## Relevance

直接命中"Self-Evolving Agents & Automated Research"：这是目前最干净的"模型改自己权重"闭环实验，且用同预算随机搜索、冻结编辑器、预注册 spot-check 三重对照把 RSI 叙事拉回可测量的范围。同时对"LLM Hidden States & Interpretability"有价值——"编辑位置跟随目录顺序、数值抄袭刚读到的统计量、解释纯属幻觉"是对权重空间可解释性的一次力度很强的否证。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- What does random search score under the *ordered* interface with the same budget? If it also lands near 61% on GSM8K, the ordered-interface gain is interface engineering rather than self-editing.
- How much of the +49 points survives a zero-parameter intervention that only breaks repetition (no-repeat n-gram penalty, forced `\boxed{}` termination, or a length cap)? That would separate "weight edit" from "any perturbation".
- Does the loop work on a model that rarely repeats itself, e.g. a 3–7B instruct model that already finishes >95% of GSM8K generations within budget? The paper's own mechanism predicts much smaller gains.
- Is the layer-0 `A_log` effect causal and transferable? Editing `A_log` in layer 0 by hand on the original model (no search) would test whether the search is finding something real or just a lucky basin.
- What happens if the editor is given real tooling (Python, gradient-free probes, per-head `A_log` granularity) as GPT-6 had — does the statistic-copying behaviour disappear, or does the model merely copy more sophisticated statistics?
- Does the hallucinated-hypothesis rate predict edit quality? A cheap filter on hypothesis-edit consistency might recover most of the gains with a fraction of the 11,580 proposals.
