> **一句话总结：** InfuseLoop 指出并行循环 Transformer（PLT）每次跨循环只传同一个预测轨迹的前一状态、浪费了并行调度本已算完的其他轨迹隐状态，于是用一层带学习 query 的轻量注意力残差把上一周期各浅层循环的输出注入当前循环——0.6B 模型在 6B token 上把八循环的下游平均准确率从 42.23%（vanilla PLT）提到 43.07%、WikiText-2 困惑度从 36.82 降到 35.55，并消除了四循环就出现的训练 loss 尖峰，解码开销仅比 vanilla PLT 高 0.3%。

> **TL;DR:** Vanilla parallel looped transformers (PLT) pass only the preceding in-trajectory state across loop boundaries even though the overlapped schedule has already produced completed states on later prediction trajectories; InfuseLoop aggregates those causally available states with a per-loop pseudo-query attention residual, and at 8 loops on a 0.6B Qwen3-style backbone trained on 6B FineWeb-Edu tokens it cuts WikiText-2 perplexity from 36.82 (vanilla PLT-8) to 35.55 (−3.4%) and LAMBADA final-word perplexity from 50.69 to 42.04 (−17.1%), raises the 7-task average from 42.23% to 43.07% (+0.84 pts), and still trails a sequential looped transformer (LT-8, 43.66%) by 0.59 pts.

## 核心贡献

- **对 PLT 计算图的诊断**：形式化地指出每个循环边界只有「同一条预测轨迹的前一层输出」被当作循环输入，而并行调度已经在别的轨迹上算完了大量状态，这些状态因果可用却被完全忽略——这是把「深度扩展失效」归因到信息流瓶颈，而不是简单地归因于「精修收益递减」。
- **InfuseLoop 机制**：为每个接收循环 r 学一个伪 query $w_r$，对上一条周期里所有更浅循环的输出 $\{h^{(j)}_{t+r-j-1}\}_{1\le j<r}$ 做无参数 RMSNorm 后的 key/value 注意力聚合，加学习缩放 $\rho_r$ 后作为循环输入残差，替换原来的 $h^{(r-1)}_t$。
- **可证明的因果性与训练/推理等价**：附录 A 证明所选源集合必然在上一周期算完（Lemma 1）、不泄露未来 token（Lemma 2）、token-parallel 训练与 layer-synchronous 解码求值同一张依赖图（Theorem 1），因此不需要近似、不需要额外的串行阶段、也不需要 stop-gradient。
- **存储与系统开销有界**：只需 $O(Rd)$ 的滚动隐状态缓冲（而非 $O(RLd)$），参数量只增加 3,081（R=4）/ 7,203（R=8）个，聚合本身占参考 kernel 时间不到 0.6%。
- **首次让 PLT 在八循环上继续变好**：vanilla PLT 从 4 循环到 8 循环验证损失反而变差（2.9252 → 2.9365），InfuseLoop 则继续改善（2.9112 → 2.9058），同时给出深度扩展、延迟—质量 Pareto 前沿和缓存占用的系统级表征。

## Key Contributions

- **Computational-graph gap analysis.** Shows that vanilla PLT's inter-loop recurrent edge carries only the same trajectory's previous state, while the overlapped schedule concurrently produces `r-1` other completed loop outputs that are causally available; frames depth-scaling failure as an information-flow limitation rather than only a refinement-saturation one.
- **Cross-trajectory latent injection.** A pseudo-query attention residual over all shallower-loop outputs from the immediately preceding cycle, with parameter-free RMSNorm on sources, learned per-source bias, learned per-loop scale, and masking at document boundaries.
- **Formal guarantees.** Appendix A proves previous-cycle availability, prefix causality (no leakage of `x_{c+1}`), equivalence of loop-ordered token-parallel training and cycle-ordered layer-synchronous decoding, and the `O(Rd)` buffer bound — the paper is careful to state these are consistency results, not expressivity separations.
- **Depth-scaling and systems evaluation.** Eight-loop quality scaling on a fixed 0.6B backbone, plus decode latency, cache footprint, arithmetic intensity, and a measured performance–latency Pareto frontier showing InfuseLoop-4 dominating PLT-8.

## Methodology

**Setting.** A Qwen3-0.6B backbone (28 layers, d=1024, FFN 3072, 16 Q heads / 8 KV heads, head dim 128, vocab 151,936, SwiGLU, RoPE base 1e6, tied embeddings, 596.1M params) is applied `R` times with shared weights. Baselines: **Single** (R=1), **LT** (sequential looping), **PLT** (vanilla parallel looping, G-SWA with window w=64). All looped variants are trained with loop-aware residual scaling and FFN-output RMSNorm ("looping normalization").

**Source selection.** For receiver $h^{(r)}_t$ executing at cycle $\tau(t,r)=t-R+r$, admissible sources require `j < r` and `s + j < t + r`. InfuseLoop restricts to exactly the previous cycle: $S_{t,r}=\{h^{(j)}_{t+r-j-1}: 1\le j<r\}$, i.e. the in-trajectory base state plus `r-2` completed **Post** states from later trajectories. Every admissible **Pre** state is at least two cycles old and is excluded (the ablation confirms staleness hurts). This is also what makes the set loop-ordered-trainable and buffer-bounded.

**Aggregation.** $m^{(r)}_t=\rho_r\sum_{j<r}\alpha_{t,j\to r}\,\mathrm{RMSNorm}(h^{(j)}_{t+r-j-1})$ with $\alpha$ a softmax over $\langle w_r, k\rangle/\sqrt{d}+b_{r,j}$; the residual replaces the vanilla recurrent input, $h^{(r)}_t=F_\theta(\mathrm{Embed}(x_{t-R+r})+m^{(r)}_t)$. Empty source sets (early cycles) yield a zero residual; cross-document sources are masked pre-softmax. The first loop is unchanged, so cross-trajectory aggregation starts at `r=3`.

**Training / inference.** Training runs loops sequentially but all sequence positions in parallel (a right-shift of the sequence-level tensors realizes the previous-cycle alignment); inference runs all active loops of a cycle in one batched layer-synchronous pass with shared first-loop global KV and per-loop local windows, cached latents updated after the cycle completes. Loss is next-token cross-entropy on the final loop, backpropagated through all recurrent and aggregation paths.

**Data / compute.** FineWeb-Edu, 6B tokens for main runs, 1B for ablations; seq len 1024; global batch 128 sequences; AdamW lr 3e-4; GB200 and H200 GPUs. Evaluation through lm-evaluation-harness: WikiText-2 (word ppl), LAMBADA (final-word ppl), MMLU (5-shot), and 0-shot HellaSwag / ARC-E / ARC-C / PIQA / WinoGrande / OpenBookQA.

## Results & Highlights

- **Depth scaling, WikiText-2 word perplexity (6B tokens):** InfuseLoop-4 = 35.70 vs vanilla PLT-4 = 36.19 (−1.4%) vs Single = 39.72 (−10.1%); InfuseLoop-8 = 35.55 vs PLT-8 = 36.82 (−3.4%) vs Single = 39.72 (−10.5%) vs sequential LT-8 = 33.78 (InfuseLoop still 5.2% worse).
- **Depth scaling, LAMBADA final-word perplexity:** InfuseLoop-4 = 45.08 vs PLT-4 = 62.96 (−28.4%) vs LT-4 = 41.07; InfuseLoop-8 = 42.04 vs PLT-8 = 50.69 (−17.1%) vs Single = 67.02 (−37.3%) vs LT-8 = 35.04. The `10.5–37.3%` range in the abstract is exactly these two WikiText-2/LAMBADA endpoints at R=8.
- **Monotone-in-depth, unlike vanilla PLT:** validation loss for vanilla PLT gets *worse* going from four to eight loops (2.9252 → 2.9365, +0.011 nats), whereas InfuseLoop improves (2.9112 → 2.9058, −0.005 nats); InfuseLoop-8 also beats Single (2.9941) by 0.088 nats and closes 52% of the PLT-to-LT gap at R=8 (61% at R=4).
- **Downstream accuracy (7-task unweighted mean):** InfuseLoop-8 = 43.07% vs PLT-8 = 42.23% (+0.84 pts) vs Single = 41.09% (+1.99 pts) vs LT-8 = 43.66% (−0.59 pts); at R=4 the gain over PLT is +0.96 pts (43.00 vs 42.04). Per-task, the largest moves are ARC-C 29.61 → 31.43 and HellaSwag 39.25 → 40.00 at R=8, while MMLU stays at chance (24.96 vs 24.50).
- **Training stability:** vanilla PLT shows loss spikes "with as few as four loops"; InfuseLoop's validation curves at both R=4 and R=8 are spike-free (Figure 4). No spike-magnitude statistics or spike counts are reported.
- **Decoding cost:** +0.3% decode time vs vanilla PLT-8 and +23.3% vs the non-looped Single at B=32 / 2048-token prompt; LT-8 is ~7.7× InfuseLoop-8's latency. Aggregation accounts for <0.6% of the reference kernel time, and at 8,192-token context InfuseLoop-8 still keeps 86.5% of Single's throughput at B=32.
- **Cache footprint per token (KB, w=64):** at 512 / 2,048 / 8,192 tokens, Single = 114.69 throughout; InfuseLoop-4 = 157.72 (+37.5%) / 125.45 (+9.4%) / 117.38 (+2.3%); InfuseLoop-8 = 215.10 (+87.5%) / 139.79 (+21.9%) / 120.96 (+5.5%) — the "same logical KV-cache footprint" claim holds for the KV cache, not for the total.
- **Regime is memory-bound:** estimated arithmetic intensity 26.8 FLOPs/byte at B=32, R=8, 4,096 context vs the GB200 ridge point of 312.5 FLOPs/byte — 11.7× below the roof, which is the paper's argument that eight loops consume otherwise idle compute.
- **Source-selection ablation (Table 3, 1B tokens, R=8):** InfuseLoop 3.2436 / 38.35% vs "Base only" (just $h^{(r-1)}$) 3.2799 / 37.59% vs "In-trajectory" 3.2839 / 37.87% vs "Pre" 3.2820 / 37.41% vs "FullState" 3.4244 / 37.21%. More sources is *worse*, and same-trajectory aggregation recovers almost nothing — the cross-trajectory signal is doing the work.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The idea is modest but well-targeted: a residual connection across concurrently computed trajectories, implemented as attention over a fixed-size set. The intellectual contribution is really the *availability analysis* — showing that `j < r` plus "previous cycle only" simultaneously gives causality, token-parallel trainability, and an `O(Rd)` buffer. That is a cleaner result than the mechanism itself, and it is what separates this from generic "mix more hidden states" tricks.

**Rigor — the headline number is benchmark-selected.** The abstract's "3.4–17.1%" range is not a confidence interval; it is two different benchmarks with two different metrics. On WikiText-2 word perplexity the gain over vanilla PLT is only 1.4% (R=4) and 3.4% (R=8). The 17.1% comes entirely from LAMBADA final-word perplexity — and LAMBADA is the one benchmark whose metric is *by construction* a last-token prediction, while in this architecture only the final loop ever sees `x_t` and loop `r=R` is the loop that receives the most injected sources (`R-1`). A mechanism that enriches the final loop's input is expected to move last-word perplexity disproportionately, so the LAMBADA gap is plausibly mechanism–metric alignment rather than broad language-modeling improvement. Reporting the two benchmarks separately (Table 1 does, the abstract does not) hides this.

**Rigor — the accuracy claims are inside noise.** Seven tasks, one run each, no seeds. At R=8 the reported +0.84 pts is spread over tasks whose sample sizes make ±0.4–0.5 pts a one-sigma fluctuation (HellaSwag Δ=+0.75, ARC-C Δ=+1.82, PIQA Δ=+0.44, WG Δ=+1.02), and three of the seven tasks sit at or near 4-way chance: MMLU 24.96 (chance 25), ARC-C 31.43, OBQA 32.40. MMLU is *at chance* for a 5-shot 0.6B model, so its contribution to the average is noise. The abstract's "0.84 and 1.99 percentage points" should be read with that in mind.

**Missing compute-matched control.** Every model in Figure 4 sees the same 6B tokens, which means the R=8 looped model consumes ~8× the backbone FLOPs of Single. The natural control — Single trained on ~8× tokens, or a larger non-looped model at matched *inference* FLOPs — is absent. The authors themselves cite Marchenko et al. (2026) reporting that at matched inference FLOPs a 3.9B non-looped model matches or exceeds a three-loop 1.4B model on all 12 benchmarks; that result cuts against looping and is cited but never tested in their own 0.6B setting.

**The stabilization story is under-ablated.** The paper attributes spike removal to cross-trajectory injection, but the ablation that removes the other stabilization component ("w/o looping normalization") shows essentially no effect at 1B tokens (R=8: 3.2434 vs 3.2482 — marginally *better* loss, 38.17 vs 38.35 average). Since the ablation budget is 1B tokens and the spikes appear in 6B-token runs, the component ablations are run in exactly the regime where spikes are least likely, so they cannot adjudicate the stability claim.

**FullState is dismissed with an untested hypothesis.** Aggregating the full admissible set is much worse (3.4244 vs 3.2436 at R=8, worse even than Base-only's 3.2799), explained as "denser aggregation makes optimization harder." But FullState also changes the number of aggregation parameters and mixes `O(R²)` candidate keys into one softmax, so the comparison confounds staleness, source-set size, and softmax dilution. No ablation holds the parameter count fixed.

**Baseline fidelity.** LT-4 (2.9022) beats PLT-4 (2.9252) by 0.023 nats and LT-8 by 0.059 nats. If the original PLT work reported parity with sequential LT, then part of InfuseLoop's gain may be recovering a baseline-implementation gap rather than a genuine architectural advantage; the paper does not report whether PLT was reproduced to the original numbers or trained by the same recipe length.

**Regime caveat.** 6B tokens for a 0.6B model is ~10 tokens/param and the final validation loss is 2.99 — far from converged, with validation loss still falling steeply at the end of Figure 4. Loss-spike behavior and the marginal value of extra loops both typically change once the LR decays and the run saturates, so the eight-loop advantage is demonstrated in a transient regime.

**Red flags, ranked:** (1) headline percentage range driven by a benchmark structurally aligned with the mechanism; (2) no seeds/error bars for any table entry; (3) no compute-matched non-looped control; (4) component ablations at 1/6 the main budget, in the regime where the failure being fixed does not occur; (5) unpublished-fidelity baseline. None of these is disqualifying — the structural analysis and the monotone-vs-non-monotone depth curve are real results — but the paper's framing is more confident than its evidence.

## Strengths

- The diagnosis is genuinely useful: "only the preceding in-trajectory state crosses the loop boundary, although `r-1` causally available states already exist" is a crisp, checkable statement about the computational graph, not a vague appeal to diminishing returns.
- The design is constrained by real system considerations — previous-cycle-only sources, per-loop learned query, document masking, zero-residual fallback for empty source sets — rather than by what maximizes a benchmark.
- Appendix A is unusually careful: separate lemmas for availability and prefix causality, an explicit theorem that logits match between loop-ordered training and cycle-ordered decoding, and an honest scope note that these are consistency, not expressivity, results.
- Efficiency is characterized rather than asserted: measured kernel-time breakdown, throughput across batch sizes and context lengths, per-token cache footprint at three context lengths, and arithmetic intensity against the GB200 ridge point.
- The ablation table is informative in a non-flattering direction — it shows that in-trajectory-only aggregation recovers almost nothing (3.2839 vs 3.2436) and that a *bigger* source set hurts, which is the kind of result that makes the proposed restriction credible.
- Honest limitations: admits LT still wins, admits only 0.6B dense models were tried, and admits the speculative-decoding combination is future work.

## Limitations

- One backbone size (0.6B dense), one token budget (6B), one sequence length (1024), one dataset (FineWeb-Edu). No MoE, no larger scale, no long-context training; the paper says so, but that leaves the central claim — that cross-trajectory injection is what unlocks depth scaling — supported at a single operating point.
- No multiple seeds, no confidence intervals, and no spike statistics; the stability claim rests on visual inspection of two loss curves.
- G-SWA (window 64) is inherited from PLT and never ablated, so the interaction between local-window size and cross-trajectory aggregation is unknown — a plausible confound, since the injected states carry exactly the long-range information the local window drops.
- Quality remains below sequential LT at both depths (0.028 nats and 0.59 pts at R=8), and the paper offers no analysis of why; "further study is needed" is the whole explanation.
- No compute-matched comparison against a wider/shallower non-looped model, so the deployment question ("eight loops or a 2× wider model?") is left open despite being the paper's stated motivation.
- The combination with speculative decoding is discussed only qualitatively, and the paper correctly notes both techniques compete for the same spare compute — but without an experiment, the practical value in a real serving stack is unquantified.
- Cache overhead at short context is substantial (+87.5% per token at 512 tokens for R=8), so the "same footprint" framing only holds for long contexts.

## Relevance

直接落在「LLM 训练稳定性与可观测性」这条线上：它把并行循环 Transformer 四循环就出现的训练 loss 尖峰，归因到跨循环信息流缺了一条边，并给出可证明因果安全的修法（上一周期浅层状态注入），对关心 loss spike 成因与结构化解法的人很有参考价值；同时它对解码延迟/缓存的实测（+0.3% vs PLT、八循环仍在 memory-bound 区）也属于大规模推理系统成本的一部分。方法本身（受限的跨轨迹隐状态聚合 + 存储上界证明）对「隐状态与可解释性」方向上研究循环深度如何被表征质量限制的工作同样可借鉴。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the WikiText-2 gain over vanilla PLT survive at 60B tokens / a converged LR schedule? The 3.4% at 6B tokens is small enough that the crossover point matters.
2. Why is the LAMBADA gap so much larger than every other benchmark? A per-position breakdown of perplexity (early vs late positions, final-token accuracy) would test the "final-loop enrichment" explanation directly.
3. What happens if the aggregation query `w_r` is shared across loops, or if sources are weighted by their own loop index rather than a learned per-source bias? This would separate "more information" from "more capacity."
4. Is the FullState degradation a softmax-dilution effect? A version that keeps `O(R²)` candidates but uses a fixed uniform average, or a top-k selection, would disentangle the two.
5. How does the mechanism interact with G-SWA window size — is InfuseLoop partly compensating for information the local window discards?
6. At matched inference FLOPs, how does InfuseLoop-8 compare against a ~1.6B non-looped model trained on the same total tokens? This is the comparison a serving team would actually run.
7. Do the injected states carry anything beyond what a single extra attention layer over cached hidden states would provide — i.e., is the gain from cross-trajectory *routing* or merely from extra depth in the aggregation module?
