Office, one epoch of GRPO. In the same run NDCG@10 rose +0.95 (11.59% → 12.55%) — the benchmark and the deployment metric move in opposite directions.

22% of SFT's top-500 targets are simply gone.

Why it matters为何重要

Production systems pass 100–1,500 candidates to a downstream ranker. A benchmark that stops at K=10 can hide the recall you actually ship.

Candidates per request, from the paper's Table 1: TSGR 1,500 · GrowthGR 1,000 · OneRec 512 · GenRec ≤ 50.

Critical edge关键质疑

Every main result is a single epoch on one 1.5B backbone; the steady-state loss — and whether the anchor holds — is untested.

Multi-epoch evidence exists only for the synced-reference variant: −6.4 → −8.9 hit@500 from epoch 1 to 2.

Under the standard protocol — beam 50, report K=10 — the very same two models read +0.39 (Office) and +0.46 (Industrial). The measurement protocol, not the model, decides whether the problem is visible. Protocol contrast: +0.4 at K=10 · −2.59 / −1.96 hit@200 under deployment-shaped constrained beam

Score everything, then look测量

01

Don't decode a short list and grade its top. Score every item in the catalogue and read off the exact rank of the one logged next item.

sθ(i|x) = Σl log πθ( cl(i) | x, c<l(i) )
  • Exact, not beam. Items are 3-code semantic IDs; expanding the SID prefix tree yields the target's exact rank and every cut-off in one pass.
  • hit@K = recall@K. Each test prompt has exactly one next item, so ranking accuracy and candidate-list recall are literally the same number.
  • Paired and seeded. Three RL seeds share one SFT checkpoint, differing only in prompt order and sampling; 95% bootstrap intervals over prompts; K out to 500, ≈14% of the smallest catalogue.
  • One recipe, five catalogues. Qwen2.5-1.5B + MiniOneRec SFT, then GRPO with G=16 sampled beam rollouts, ~1,700 steps ≈ one epoch. Office / Industrial are Amazon-2018; Beauty / Toys / Sports are Amazon-2014.

A beam replay of the same scores reproduces ~98% of MiniOneRec's top-10 — the exact scorer is the same system, not a stronger one.

Not the objective — the estimator机制

02

Proposition 4.1 clears the loss: the exact KL-regularised optimum, π* ∝ π₀·exp(p/β), cannot lower hit@K at any K. What breaks is its estimation.

g(x) = (1/G)·[ 1[y∈R]·A_y·∇log π(y|x) − Σ_{i∈R\{y}} |A_i|·∇log π(i|x) ]
  1. Named negatives. One logged next item per history. The other G−1 rollout candidates are the model's own plausible alternatives — often another user's real click — and each collects a negative advantage.
  2. Gating. Gradient flows only inside the rollout. The target is present for just 37–42% of Office / Industrial prompts (11–24% on the other three), so most prompts never push the missed item back up.
  3. Cross-user leakage. Shared SID codes and one shared network carry the penalty to similar users' items, so a demotion generalises far beyond the prompt that caused it.
A label-free predictor. Sum each item's advantages over the SFT model's top-16 candidates for every training prompt — before RL starts. Office test prompts in the most-negative group then lose 27.4 pts of hit@500; the positive group gains ~3.

One extra term补救：rollout 外锚定

05

Keep GRPO bit-for-bit inside the rollout, and add a KL anchor on items the rollout never saw.

ℒ = ℒGRPO + λ · KL( q0 ‖ qθ )
A(x) ⊂ I \ R(x),  |A| = 16,  λ = 0.1
  • Shift-invariant by construction. q₀ and q_θ are renormalised over A(x), so adding a constant to every anchor score changes nothing: RL may still move mass from tail to head, and only relative order among out-of-rollout items is protected.
  • SID-structured sampling. Half the anchors share the first two codes with a rollout candidate, half share only the first — neighbourhoods holding 44% (Office) / 57% (Industrial) of the rank-17–500 targets.
  • Zero at initialisation. At q_θ = q₀ the term and its gradient are exactly zero, so training starts from the SFT model unchanged — and the fix doubles as a test of the diagnosis.

The top gain and the tail damage share one mechanism: the negative advantages. Penalising the model's own near-misses costs recall; penalising random items does not.

Cross-configuration: synced reference recovers +5.88 hit@500 on Office; REINFORCE++ recovers +13.26 (Office) and +5.47 (Industrial), with NDCG@10 also rising 11.45 → 12.24 and 8.96 → 9.32.

Strengths / Limitations优势与局限

07

+ Strengths / 优势

  • The measurement is the right one. Exact full-catalogue ranking, paired tests, three seeds — the only way to see a K≈20 crossover a K≤10 benchmark hides by construction, with a 98% beam-agreement check proving fidelity.
  • Diagnosis and remedy are coupled. The anchor follows from “the damage is outside the rollout” and is falsifiable: zero at init, shift-invariant, 8× drift cut, drop rate 14–22% → 5–8%.
  • Honest positioning. Six industrial papers already glimpsed fragments (GenRec, RGD, PinGER, GrowthGR, TSGR, EG-GRPO); novelty is narrowed to systematise, explain, fix.
  • Negative results carry weight. “Positive-only removes the loss and the gain” is what makes the story causal rather than a just-so story.

− Limitations / 局限

  • One epoch only. The multi-epoch trajectory is shown just for the synced-reference variant (−6.4 → −8.9), so steady-state magnitude and anchor durability are unknown.
  • No sensitivity sweep. M=16 and λ=0.1 are fixed on all five datasets; no uniform-vs-SID ablation, no λ–recall–NDCG Pareto curve.
  • Thin baseline set. Own re-implementations, one 1.5B backbone; no LASAR / SAGE / SAPO / ReCast, and no “just raise β” control to isolate “out-of-rollout” from plain stronger regularisation.
  • Small catalogues, sampled users. 3.5k–18k items, 5,000 sampled test users on three datasets, and the anchor's neighbourhood prior is measured on the development test sets.
  • No ranker in the loop. End-to-end utility with a real downstream ranker is deferred; the online-gain claim remains an extrapolation.

Open questions待解问题

08
  1. Does the crossover K* and the tail loss scale with catalogue size (10⁴ → 10⁶) and model size (1.5B → 70B) — fixed fraction of the list, or absolute-rank effect?
  2. Does recall compound over 5–10 epochs, and does the anchor still hold? The synced-reference curve suggests the loss is not linear.
  3. What is the λ–recall–NDCG@10 Pareto front, and is “out-of-rollout” doing work beyond generic regularisation, given that it also halves in-rollout drift?
  4. Do LASAR, SAGE, hint-conditioned RL, SAPO or ReCast also lose recall at large K? One shared-codebase comparison would say whether this is a GRPO pathology or family-wide.
  5. Can the reward be fixed instead of the loss — soft positives from SFT's top-m, or similar users' next items? Arguably the more principled repair, and untested.
