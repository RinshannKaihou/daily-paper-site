> **一句话总结：** 提出首个基于（约束）zonotope 的抽象解释框架来界定量化网络的量化误差，核心是一个把 round（取整）与 clamp（截断）合并为单一仿射包络的 "round-clamp" 抽象变换器（每神经元只加 1 个噪声符号而非 2 个），并给出仿射带最优性与完整约束版凸包最优性的证明；在 ACAS Xu 的 150 组实验上，小扰动半径 r=3/6 处约束 zonotope 比 SOTA 的 QEBVerif 符号 DRA 误差界窄约 68.5–83.5%（r=3: 1.4 vs 8.5；r=6: 4.7 vs 14.9）、快约 65–78%（94 vs 422 ms；170 vs 485 ms），而朴素 zonotope 更快（1–3 ms）且在 r≤6 时精度也已超过 DRA，但 r≥13 后 DRA-Sym 反超（r=26: 435.0 vs 705.6）。

> **TL;DR:** A zonotope abstract-interpretation framework for bounding quantization error (output deviation between a real network and its quantized twin), whose key primitive is a unified round-clamp transformer with six exhaustive cases and proven affine-strip optimality (plus convex-hull optimality for the 4-constraint constrained-zonotope variant). On ACAS Xu (10 networks, 6×50 ReLU, 150 runs = 3 bitwidths × 5 radii), the *constrained* zonotope beats QEBVerif's symbolic DRA (DRA-Sym) at small radii — ODiff 1.4 vs 8.5 at r=3 (−83.5%) and 4.7 vs 14.9 at r=6 (−68.5%), with runtimes 94 vs 422 ms and 170 vs 485 ms (−77.7%/−64.9%) — but loses at large radii (r=26: 705.6 vs 435.0, +62%) and on the overall 150-run average (230.0 vs 145.3). The plain zonotope is the real surprise: 0.002 s average (≈282× faster than DRA-Sym's 0.564 s) and *tighter* than DRA-Sym at r=3 and r=6 (2.3 vs 8.5; 7.7 vs 14.9), crossing over only between r=6 and r=13. Ablations: the 4-constraint "complete" formulation buys at most 0.011 ODiff (<0.6%) for 35–46% more runtime; sharing input noise symbols changes nothing (361.18 vs 361.11).

## 核心贡献

- **首个 zonotope 量化误差验证框架**：此前 zonotope 用于鲁棒性验证（AI²、DeepZ）与浮点舍入误差静态分析（Goubault & Putot 2011），但没人把它用到"量化误差"这一特定问题上——即同一输入区域上原网络与量化网络输出之差，这跟输入扰动鲁棒性是两回事。作者把量化执行表示为"理想执行的 Minkowski 和扰动"，误差 zonotope 即 ẑ − z。
- **统一的 round-clamp 抽象变换器（本文核心技术贡献）**：量化激活是 `y = clamp(⌊x⌉, 0, C)`，若先做 round 再做 clamp 会引入两个噪声符号并丢掉两者的组合结构。作者按 `[l,u]` 与两个阈值 −1/2、C+1/2 的相对位置穷举 6 种情形，直接给出单条仿射带 `y ∈ [λ̂x+µ̂−ê, λ̂x+µ̂+ê]`；跨双阈值的情形在两条候选斜率 λ̂₁=C/(u−0.5) 与 λ̂₂=C/(C−l−0.5) 中按 `C−l ≤ u` 择一取更紧者。噪声符号数减半，界也显著更紧。
- **理论保证（而不只是经验）**：Theorem 2 证明每个情形下 (λ̂, µ̂, ê) 都使仿射带宽度 e*(λ) 最小且保持 sound；Appendix C 进一步证明"完整"约束 zonotope 版本（每神经元加斜向凸包边约束与对角舍入界 y ≥ x−1/2、y ≤ x+1/2，最多 4 条）所诱导的多边形 **等于** round-clamp 图的凸包 `conv(G_f([l,u]))`，即严格意义上不可能更小的凸松弛。
- **量化误差的约束 zonotope 表示**：把 QNN 路径的 round-clamp 盒约束与 DNN 路径的 ReLU 约束分别提升到扩展空间 ε=(ε̂⁽ᴺ⁾‖ε⁽ᴺ⁾)，拼成块对角 (A,b)，末端解 2 个 LP（每输出维）得到收紧的误差界；LP 超时（2 s）时回退到朴素 zonotope 界，仍然 sound。
- **诚实的分区结论**：不是"全面碾压 SOTA"，而是明确给出交叉点——约束 zonotope 在 r≤6 双赢、r≤19 仍更快，r=26 双输；并指出 Q=10 时 DRA 在 r=6 就已更准。

## Key Contributions

- **First zonotope-based framework for quantization-error verification** (as opposed to robustness verification), with a clean decomposition: propagate the idealized and quantized networks independently and take the Minkowski-sum difference as the error zonotope.
- **A single round-clamp abstract transformer** that fuses rounding and clamping, with six exhaustive cases (fully below/above the clamp, within clamp range, spans both thresholds, crosses zero only, crosses upper clamp only), halving noise-symbol growth versus cascading separate round and clamp transformers. Works for both plain and constrained zonotopes.
- **Soundness + optimality theorems**: affine-strip optimality of (λ̂, µ̂, ê) per case (Theorem 2, Appendix B) and convex-hull optimality of the complete constrained formulation (Appendix C) — the strongest kind of tightness statement available for a convex relaxation.
- **A practical constrained-zonotope formulation** (2 box constraints per unstable round-clamp neuron) that captures most of the tightness at a fraction of the LP cost, verified by an ablation showing <0.6% width loss for 35–46% runtime savings.
- **A regime-resolved empirical comparison against QEBVerif's symbolic DRA** on ACAS Xu, including the crossover points and the bitwidth dependence, rather than a single averaged win.

## Methodology

**Quantization model.** Uniform (symmetric for weights/biases, asymmetric allowed for activations) quantization: `V_int = clamp(⌊V/s⌉ + z; C_lb, C_ub)`, recovered as `V̂ = s·(V_int − z)`, ties-to-even. A configuration `C = ⟨τ, Q, F⟩` fixes representable bounds. A feed-forward QNN applies integer affine maps with a 2^{F_h}-scaled accumulator, then `x̂ = clamp(⌊â⌉, 0, C_h^ub)`, and rescales by 2^{−F_h} at the output. Experiments use C_in = ⟨±,8,8⟩, C_h = ⟨+,Q,Q−2⟩ for Q ∈ {6,8,10}, F_w = F_b = max(2, Q−2).

**Round-clamp transformer (Section "Abstract Transformers for Quantized Networks").** Let `[l,u]` be the pre-activation interval, `r_l = ⌊l⌉`, `r_u = min(⌊u⌉, C)`. Cases: (1) u ≤ −1/2 → λ̂=0, µ̂=0, ê=0; (2) l ≥ C+1/2 → λ̂=0, µ̂=C; (3a) within clamp range with r_l = r_u → constant r_l; (3b) within clamp range with at least one rounding threshold crossed → λ̂=1, µ̂=0, ê=0.5 (plus y ≥ r_l, y ≤ r_u); (4) spans both thresholds → two candidate slopes λ̂₁ = C/(u−0.5), λ̂₂ = C/(C−l−0.5), pick λ̂₁ iff C−l ≤ u, then centre the strip with µ̂ = (µ̂₁+µ̂₂)/2, ê = (µ̂₁−µ̂₂)/2; (5) crosses zero only → λ̂ = r_u/(r_u−l−0.5); (6) crosses upper clamp only → λ̂ = (C−r_l)/(u−r_l−0.5). In every non-trivial case the zonotope update is `v_i^out = λ̂v_i + µ̂`, `M_i^out = (λ̂M_i ‖ ê)` — one fresh noise generator.

**Proof technique (Appendix B).** For fixed λ, the tightest sound strip is µ*(λ) = ½(sup g_λ + inf g_λ), e*(λ) = ½(sup g_λ − inf g_λ) with residual g_λ(x) = f(x) − λx. Because g_λ is piecewise-decreasing on rounding plateaus, the sup/inf are attained at plateau endpoints; the paper computes them per case and shows λ̂ minimizes e*(λ). A key trick: rather than an off-by-half constant, the ±1/2 error of case 3b comes from the identity slope at a crossed threshold (adjacent residuals ±1/2). Appendix C then exhibits, per case, an explicit polygon P_C whose vertices are all *attained* graph points, so P_C = conv(G_f([l,u])) — the oblique hull cuts are written in division-free form (d_l = r_l+½−l, d_u = u+½−r_u) to survive near-degenerate denominators.

**Error zonotope.** Both paths start from the same box B = D_R^{‖·‖∞}(x₀). The idealized path uses v⁽⁰⁾=x₀, M⁽⁰⁾=R·I_d; the quantized path quantizes the *vertices* of B, takes the centre of the quantized box and per-dimension radii (no shared ϵ⁽⁰⁾ symbol — Ablation 2 justifies this as slightly tighter at small radii). Hidden layers run in 2^{F_h}-scaled coordinates (legal by positive homogeneity of ReLU), with Ŵ_eff⁽¹⁾ = 2^{F_h}Ŵ⁽¹⁾, Ŵ_eff⁽ⁱ⁾ = Ŵ⁽ⁱ⁾ for i>1. After the last affine layer both outputs are rescaled by 2^{−F_h}, and the error zonotope is `z̃⁽ᴺ⁾ = 2^{−F_h}ẑ⁽ᴺ⁾(ε̂) − z⁽ᴺ⁾(ε)` with generator `M̃ = (2^{−F_h}M̂⁽ᴺ⁾ ‖ −M⁽ᴺ⁾)`.

**Implementation.** Julia 1.12, LazySets.jl (zonotope arithmetic), JuMP.jl + HiGHS for LPs, ModelVerification.jl for model loading; 2 s per-LP limit with sound fallback to the plain zonotope bound; code at github.com/robertdebbas/zonotopes. Single Apple M5, 16 GB RAM, macOS — CPU only, no parallelization reported.

**Benchmark.** ACAS Xu (5 inputs, 6 hidden layers × 50 ReLU, 5 outputs), 10 networks from families 1–4; radii r ∈ {3,6,13,19,26} (ϵ = r/256 ∈ {0.012,…,0.102}); all perturbations centred on the same single test input x₀ = [−0.2,−0.25,−0.5,−0.3,−0.44]ᵀ. Metric ODiff = average output-interval width; total 150 runs, all producing valid results for all three methods.

## Results & Highlights

- **Small radii, the headline win (Table 2).** At r=3 constrained zonotope ODiff **1.4** vs DRA-Sym **8.5** (−83.5%) at **94 ms vs 422 ms** (−77.7%); at r=6 **4.7 vs 14.9** (−68.5%) at **170 vs 485 ms** (−64.9%). This is the range where the abstract's "70–85% tighter, 65–80% faster" comes from — and it is stated as such, not as a global claim.
- **Large radii, the loss.** r=13: 111.9 vs 74.0 (DRA 34% tighter, 413 vs 611 ms); r=19: 326.5 vs 194.4; r=26: **705.6 vs 435.0** (DRA 62% tighter *and* faster, 691 vs 660 ms). So on the 150-run average (Table 1) DRA-Sym wins on precision — **145.3 vs 230.0** — while constrained zonotope wins on time, **0.382 s vs 0.564 s** (only −32%, far short of the headline 65–80%).
- **The plain zonotope is the dark horse.** Average **0.002 s** (≈282× faster than DRA-Sym's 0.564 s, which supports the "nearly 300×" claim), but also **tighter than DRA-Sym at r=3 (2.3 vs 8.5) and r=6 (7.7 vs 14.9)** — i.e. at exactly the radii where the paper argues for constrained zonotopes, the unconstrained one already dominates the baseline on both axes. Constrained zonotopes are ~1.6× tighter than plain at 100–400× the cost (94 vs 1 ms at r=3).
- **Bitwidth dependence (r=6).** Constrained zonotope 5.1 vs DRA 34.3 at Q=6 (85% tighter), 5.1 vs 7.6 at Q=8, but 3.7 vs **2.8** at Q=10 — the advantage is a low-bitwidth phenomenon, which is arguably the practically interesting regime but undercuts any "uniformly better" reading.
- **Suspicious insensitivity at large radius.** At r=19 DRA-Sym improves with Q while "both zonotope bounds vary by less than 0.3% across bit-widths" — a bound that barely moves between 6 and 10 bits at large radii has stopped tracking the actual quantization error and is dominated by relaxation slack.
- **Ablation 1 — the 4-constraint "complete" version is not worth it.** Largest |ΔODiff| = 0.011 at r=6 (<0.6% of the bound), for 43–46% higher runtime at r ≤ 6 (104 vs 191 ms at r=3; 180 vs 318 ms at r=6) and 35–37% at larger radii (714 vs 1098 ms at r=26). Table 3: r=3 full 1.40/191 ms vs main 1.41/104 ms.
- **Ablation 2 — Minkowski sum ≈ shared input.** Average ODiff 361.11 (independent) vs 361.18 (shared) over all 150 runs; independent is tighter at small radii, shared at large. Retained for consistency with the constrained formulation — a defensible but weak justification.
- **Soundness in practice.** All 150 × 3 method runs produced valid bounds; the 2 s LP fallback appears never to bind (worst constrained time 1098 ms for two LPs), so the reported numbers are all constraint-tightened, not silently falling back.

## Authors & Credibility

Robert Debbas (Harvard University) with Éric Goubault and Sylvie Putot (LIX, École Polytechnique / Institut Polytechnique de Paris). Goubault and Putot are precisely the people who established zonotope-based static analysis of finite-precision (rounding-error) computations — their VMCAI'11 paper is cited here as the methodological ancestor — so the "propagate rounding errors with noise symbols" machinery is native to this group rather than borrowed. The framing is an AAAI-style paper (AAAI reference format, "Proceedings of the AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence" citations). Code is released at github.com/robertdebbas/zonotopes, and the baseline is the *official* QEBVerif implementation run in its symbolic-propagation mode, which is the right comparison for a single-pass abstract-interpretation claim. Proofs are delegated to Appendices B and C but are written out case by case with explicit sup/inf computations, so they are checkable. Weaknesses on this axis: one benchmark, one hardware configuration, no repeated trials or variance on timings, and no independent reproduction.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** Genuine but bounded. The round-clamp transformer is the real contribution; fusing two monotone-ish operations into one relaxation is a small idea with a large payoff (halved noise symbols, one strip instead of a composed pair), and the six-case enumeration plus affine-strip optimality is done properly. The framing "first zonotope framework for quantization error" is defensible, but the distance to prior art is narrower than presented: the propagation engine is AI²/DeepZ/Lemesle et al. 2024 verbatim, the quantization-error-as-difference construction is QEBVerif's DRA idea re-expressed in the zonotope domain, and constrained zonotopes with inequality constraints are taken wholesale from Lemesle, Lehmann & Gall 2024. The paper is honest that differential zonotopes (Teuber et al. 2025) exist and that the authors propagate the two networks separately.

**Rigor — the theorems are stronger than the method.** Theorem 1/Appendix C proves convex-hull optimality of the *complete* constrained formulation, but the method the paper actually recommends and benchmarks is the "main/light" 2-constraint variant, which is **not** convex-hull optimal. The ablation (0.011 ODiff, <0.6%) shows the gap is empirically negligible, but the paper never states the per-neuron suboptimality bound of the light variant, so the optimality theorem does not transfer to the deployed method. Second, per-neuron convex-hull optimality says nothing about network-level tightness: composing locally optimal relaxations over 6 layers still loses precision, which is exactly why DRA-Sym wins at large r. The paper is careful to say "unconstrained affine strip" optimality, but a reader skimming the abstract ("we establish … convex-hull optimality") will over-read it.

**Missing comparisons — the most serious gap.** The only baseline is DRA-Sym. There is no interval/box baseline (DRA's own fast first stage, DRA-Interval in QEBVerif), which is the natural cheap competitor and would test whether zonotopes beat boxes by as much as they beat polyhedra; no comparison to DeepZ/DeepPoly-style zonotope robustness certifiers applied naively to the error network; and the exact methods cited in related work (QVIP's ILP, Henzinger et al.'s SMT, Huang et al. 2024) are never run, even on the smallest radii where they might be feasible for 5-input ACAS networks. Conversely, QEBVerif's *full* DRA+MILP pipeline — the actual state of the art — is explicitly excluded ("our comparison focuses on single-pass abstract interpretation"), which is a legitimate scoping choice but means "reduces error-bound widths by 70–85% vs QEBVerif" compares against a deliberately weakened version of QEBVerif. That sentence in the abstract will be misread.

**Experimental thinness.** "150 experiments" sounds substantial but is 10 networks × 3 bitwidths × 5 radii **on a single input point x₀**, with a single CPU and no repeated timing runs. ACAS Xu networks are 5→50×6→5 — three orders of magnitude smaller than the MNIST/CIFAR models that QEBVerif and QVIP actually benchmark on — so the runtime story (0.382 s, 0.564 s) says nothing about scalability, and the LP-based constrained path is precisely the component that will not scale (constraint count grows with unstable neurons; the paper's own 2 s LP cap with fallback is an admission of this). No variance or error bars on any timing, so the "65–80% faster" claim rests on single measurements.

**Uncomfortable internal result.** Table 2 shows the *plain* zonotope is tighter than DRA-Sym at r=3 and r=6 while being ~400× faster. That undercuts the paper's narrative that constrained zonotopes are the practical contribution: at small radii the cheapest method already beats the baseline, and the constrained variant buys 1.6× tightness for 100–400× time. The paper notices the crossover but never asks the obvious follow-up — why pay for constrained zonotopes at all when the plain one dominates DRA-Sym in the same regime? A Pareto-frontier framing (plain for screening, constrained for tight bounds, DRA for large radii) would be more honest than "constrained zonotopes as a practical choice".

**Modeling caveats not discussed.** The QNN semantics is an idealized spec (uniform per-tensor scales, exact integer accumulator with no overflow/saturation, ties-to-even), and there is no empirical check that bounds hold against actual quantized PyTorch inference — a one-line sanity experiment comparing concretized error against measured ẑ−z on random inputs would cost nothing and would have caught any tie-breaking or rescaling mismatch. Also, the input abstraction for the quantized path is built by quantizing the *vertices* of B and taking a box; that is sound but ignores the input quantization grid structure, which is a missed opportunity given how much the paper emphasizes small radii.

## Strengths

- The round-clamp transformer is a clean, well-motivated primitive, and the six-case enumeration is exhaustive and verifiable by hand — rare in this literature.
- Real theorems, not hand-waving: explicit sup/inf computations per case, division-free oblique constraints to avoid numerical cutoffs near degenerate hull edges, and a convex-hull equality (not just containment) for the complete formulation.
- Unusually honest regime reporting: the abstract, Table 2, and Figure 4 all state that DRA-Sym wins at large radii and at Q=10, and the crossover radius is given explicitly. Most verification papers would have buried this.
- The ablation is genuinely informative and slightly self-subverting (the expensive complete formulation is unnecessary), which is the kind of result that helps practitioners.
- Code released, official baseline implementation used, all 150 runs valid for all three methods, and a sound fallback (2 s LP cap → plain zonotope) so the method degrades gracefully rather than unsoundly.

## Limitations

- Single benchmark (ACAS Xu), single input point, tiny networks (5→300 ReLU→5); no MNIST/CIFAR-scale evidence, so scalability of the LP-based constrained path is untested.
- Only one baseline (DRA-Sym); no interval/box baseline, no QVIP/SMT exact methods, and QEBVerif's MILP refinement excluded — so the headline comparison is against a weakened SOTA.
- The optimality theorem applies to the complete 4-constraint variant, while the recommended method is the 2-constraint light variant whose suboptimality is measured only empirically (0.011 ODiff) and never bounded.
- Large-radius behavior is weak and underexplained: bounds change <0.3% between Q=6 and Q=10 at r=19, indicating the relaxation, not quantization, dominates the width there.
- Timings are single-run on one Apple M5 with no variance, and no parallelization or LP warm-starting is reported; the 65–80% speedup may not survive on other hardware or with a faster LP backend.
- No empirical validation against real quantized inference (e.g. PyTorch), and the QNN semantics ignores accumulator overflow, per-channel scales, and non-uniform schemes.
- Ablation 2's difference (361.18 vs 361.11) is within noise for a method with no error bars, making the "independent propagation" design choice essentially unjustified.

## Relevance

与"神经网络形式化验证 / 量化模型可靠性"直接相关：如果关注量化部署后的数值可信度、抽象解释（zonotope/约束 zonotope）作为可扩展验证域、以及 round/clamp 这类离散算子的松弛设计，这篇是少见的"理论保证 + 分区实证 + 开源代码"三者齐备的工作。对做低比特量化部署、或想在验证器里加入量化误差界的人来说，round-clamp 变换器和"约束 zonotope 只在 r≤6 划算"的交叉点结论最实用。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. What is the per-neuron suboptimality bound of the 2-constraint light variant relative to the convex-hull-optimal complete one? The 0.011 ODiff result is an average over 10 networks — is there a network/case where the light variant is much worse (e.g. very asymmetric [l,u] in case 4)?
2. Why does the plain zonotope beat DRA-Sym at r=3 and r=6? If the answer is "DRA's polyhedral encoding wastes effort on directions irrelevant to the error", that is a more interesting finding than the constrained-zonotope win, and it suggests a hybrid (plain for screening, constrained only where plain is loose).
3. How does the constrained path scale — 20 layers × 100 neurons, or a CIFAR-10 CNN? Constraint count grows with unstable neurons and the paper's 2 s LP cap already hints at blow-up; what fraction of runs would fall back at that scale?
4. Can the round-clamp transformer be composed with QEBVerif's MILP refinement (the excluded "full" pipeline) to get both tightness at large radii and the small-radius speedup?
5. Does the bound remain sound against an actual quantized PyTorch/TFLite model, including tie-breaking, per-channel scales, and accumulator saturation — and if not, how large is the gap between the formal bound and measured ẑ−z?
6. Is there a cheap way to exploit the input quantization grid (the paper quantizes the box vertices and discards grid structure) to tighten the r=3 regime that matters most?
