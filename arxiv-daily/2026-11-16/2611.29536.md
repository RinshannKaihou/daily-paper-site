> **一句话总结：** 作者把"量化是否让某些 token 永远无法被预测"形式化为输出头凸包几何问题，用"找到获胜方向 / 凸包距离上界 / 精确行碰撞"三种单侧检查在单卡上估算被静音 token 数，发现 Llama-3.2-1B 在全精度下静音 0 个 token、8-bit RTN 就静音 160–284 个，且 Qwen2.5 多语词表中被静音的 3827 个语言 token 里只有 266 个是拉丁字母，而韩文高达 964 个。

> **TL;DR:** A single-author EACL-style paper asks a question perplexity cannot answer: after weight quantization, which tokens can the output head *never* emit, for any input? Because a token is unargmaxable iff its row lies inside the convex hull of the other rows (Grivas et al., 2022), the author replaces exact hull testing with three cheap one-sided checks (a stored winning direction = safe, a bounded distance-to-hull = flagged, a bit-for-bit row collision = proven silenced) and reports a certified interval. On Llama-3.2-1B the fp32 head silences 0 tokens while every 8-bit RTN scheme silences 160–284 (+264 under symmetric per-tensor), on Qwen2.5-0.5B int8 adds +834 silenced tokens at a cost of only 2.4 WikiText-2 perplexity points and 0.5 ARC-Easy points, and on GPT-2 int4 silences 0 tokens while perplexity explodes from 51.3 to 3.2×10⁵ — the two axes are genuinely independent. The headline empirical finding is a language skew on the Qwen vocabulary: 27% of Korean and 23% of Thai tokens are lost versus 0.3% of Latin ones.

## 核心贡献

- **把"量化静音"定义成一个可测量的结构性属性**：token 若落在其余 token 行的凸包内部，则不存在任何隐状态能让它成为严格 argmax，贪心解码永远无法产生它。这与"某语料上没出现过"是两回事，作者全程严格区分。
- **提出一套廉价的三段式估算流程**：为绝大多数 token 存一个"获胜方向"（一次矩阵乘法即可验证，判定 safe）；对找不到方向的 token 用一阶投影估计到凸包的距离 $d_t$ 并给出上界（判定 flagged）；若残差收敛到 0 且与另一行逐比特相同，则证明该 token 被静音（判定 silenced）。下界由精确碰撞给出，上界再加上 flagged token，因此每个数字都是一个带证书的区间。
- **实证"静音出现得比预期早"**：Llama-3.2-1B 和 Llama-3.1-70B 在全精度下静音 0 个 token，8-bit 就已静音 160–284 与 221–287 个；而小词表的 GPT-2 一直撑到 3-bit 才静音 175 个。
- **发现静音具有强烈的语言偏向**：在 Qwen 多语词表上，跨所有量化配置并集的 3827 个语言类静音 token 中只有 266 个是拉丁字母，韩文 964、中文 603、泰文 578、希伯来文 382、阿拉伯/波斯文 370；按词表规模归一后仍损失 27% 韩文、23% 泰文、12% 希伯来文，而拉丁字母仅 0.3%。
- **给出机制解释并验证**：静音 token 的行范数显著偏低（Qwen 上中位数 0.31 vs 全体 0.46；Llama 上 0.55 vs 0.99），仅凭行范数预测是否静音的 AUC 达 0.99（Qwen）和 1.00（Llama），指向"欠训练 token 挤在凸包内部"这一已知现象。
- **主张把静音计数（尤其按文字系统拆分）作为量化报告的常规指标**，与 perplexity、任务准确率并列，因为后两者在双向都可能完全掩盖它。

## Key Contributions

- Formalizes *silencing* (unargmaxability) as a property of the quantized output head alone, making it exactly computable in principle and cheaply boundable in practice: the head is the only thing that decides whether a token can win, so the analysis needs no data and no forward pass.
- Turns an intractable per-token convex-hull membership test over 50k–152k rows into three one-sided checks with explicit geometric meaning, yielding a **certified interval** rather than a point estimate: exact collisions give the lower end, flagged rows only raise the upper end. Down to 3 bits the two ends coincide in nearly all reported cells.
- Produces the first (to the author's knowledge) structural, language-resolved measurement of quantization damage: silenced counts broken down by Unicode script, which no average metric exposes.
- Documents a clean **decoupling** between average quality and reachable vocabulary in both directions — GPT-2 at 4 bits keeps every token reachable while perplexity explodes; Llama and Qwen at 8 bits lose hundreds of tokens while staying within ~3 accuracy points.
- Releases code plus per-token measures (`github.com/gpappalardo/EACL2027Paper`), and shows the estimator costs minutes-to-hours on one consumer GPU for a 152k vocabulary.

## Methodology

**Setup and notation.** Output head $W \in \mathbb{R}^{V \times d}$, scores $z = Wh$. Token $t$ is *argmaxable* if $\exists h$ with $w_t^\top h > w_j^\top h$ for all $j \neq t$; otherwise it is silenced. The classical result (Grivas et al., 2022) is that $t$ is silenced exactly when $w_t$ lies in the convex hull of the other rows — a row surrounded by the rest cannot be pushed ahead of all competitors by any direction.

**Three one-sided checks.** (1) *Winning direction (safe):* store an $h$ that makes the token the strict winner; verifying it is one matrix multiply. Strict inequalities are required throughout, since accepting ties would misclassify boundary cases such as duplicate digit rows (two identical rows can only ever tie, so both count as silenced under the paper's tie convention). (2) *Trapping bound (flagged):* compute
$$d_t = \max_{\|h\| \le 1} \min_{j \neq t} (w_t - w_j)^\top h,$$
the best margin the token could ever achieve; an upper bound $d_t \le u$ caps the winning margin by $u\|h\|$ for every $h$, so a small $d_t$ means the token can win, if at all, only by a vanishing margin. This is an upper bound on $d_t$, not a proof of silencing. (3) *Exact match (proven silenced):* the row coincides bit-for-bit with a combination of others (in practice, with a single other row), which quantization produces when it maps distinct rows onto the same grid point.

**Pipeline.** Data-free and two-stage, entirely batched GPU matrix operations on the stored weight matrix: first evaluate a curated set of candidate directions to resolve most of the vocabulary as safe; for the unresolved remainder run a first-order projection of $w_t$ onto combinations of neighbouring rows to tighten the $d_t$ upper bound, then test for a bit-for-bit collision when the residual vanishes. A full 152k vocabulary for one model / bit-width / scheme takes minutes to a couple of hours on a single consumer GPU.

**Models and quantization.** GPT-2 (124M, 50k vocab), Llama-3.2-1B (128k), Qwen2.5-0.5B (152k multilingual), plus Qwen2.5-1.5B and Qwen2.5-7B (same Qwen vocabulary) and Llama-3.1-70B (same Llama vocabulary) for size trends. Round-to-nearest fake quantization of all linear weights including the output head; embeddings, norms and biases stay full precision. Symmetric per-channel RTN is the controlled stress test, asymmetric per-channel is the deployment proxy, per-tensor variants are reported because a shared scale can either protect or expose rows; group-wise scaling at 32 and 128 is tested as a control. GPTQ and AWQ are run on four models as calibrated-quantizer controls.

**Metrics.** Silenced counts relative to full precision; WikiText-2 perplexity and ARC-Easy accuracy as conventional quality references; Belebele reading comprehension in ten languages for Qwen2.5-0.5B with an 8-bit head; Unicode-script classification of every silenced token; corpus checks on WikiText-103 and on Wikipedia articles in 34 languages.

## Results & Highlights

- **Silencing starts at 8 bits on large vocabularies.** Llama-3.2-1B silences **0** tokens in fp32 and **160–284** under the four 8-bit RTN schemes (**+264** for symmetric per-tensor int8, Table 3), rising to +290 at int4 and +322 at int3; Llama-3.1-70B, sharing the vocabulary, goes from 0 in fp32 to **221–287** at 8 bits, so the effect does not fade with scale.
- **The vocabulary shrinks before accuracy does (Qwen2.5-0.5B, int8 symmetric per-tensor).** +834 newly silenced tokens (Table 3) while WikiText-2 perplexity moves only 21.1 → 23.5 and ARC-Easy 56.3% → 55.8% (−0.5 pts); at int4 the silenced count is essentially unchanged (+837) but perplexity jumps to 58.6 and ARC falls to 47.0%.
- **The reverse decoupling holds too (GPT-2, int4).** Zero newly silenced tokens, yet WikiText-2 perplexity goes from 51.3 to **3.2×10⁵** and ARC-Easy from 40.4% to 28.2% — average quality can collapse with the reachable vocabulary fully intact, which is the strongest evidence in the paper that silencing is not a restatement of perplexity.
- **GPT-2 breaks at 3 bits, and the break is measurable in output.** Per-tensor int3 silences **175** tokens, **all proven by exact collision**, of which **70 are numeric** (single digits, years, round numbers). On 400 WikiText-103 documents there are **52 positions** (120 counting multi-digit numeric tokens such as years) where the fp model greedily decodes the correct digit but the quantized head *cannot produce it at all* — an impossible outcome by construction, not a mere degradation.
- **Group-wise scaling does not prevent it.** At 4 bits with groups of 32, Llama-3.2-1B still silences **258–289** tokens versus **290** for plain per-channel, and Qwen2.5-0.5B reaches **2232–2234** versus **2236–2238** — silencing is a property of rounding rows onto a shared grid, not of coarse scaling granularity.
- **The loss is language-skewed (Qwen).** Union over all quantized configurations: **3827** language-bearing silenced tokens, of which only **266** are Latin, against **964** Korean, **603** Chinese, **578** Thai, **382** Hebrew and **370** Arabic/Persian. Normalized by vocabulary size that is **27% of Korean, 23% of Thai, 12% of Hebrew versus 0.3% of Latin** tokens.
- **The English-centric models lose mostly Latin, and mostly junk.** GPT-2 loses **1271 of 1274** language-bearing tokens to Latin; Llama-3.2-1B loses **327 of 486** to Latin with a 102-token Cyrillic remainder, and **235 of its 264** int8 losses are reserved special tokens.
- **Row norm nearly separates silenced from safe.** Median row norm of silenced tokens is **0.31 vs 0.46** overall on Qwen2.5-0.5B and **0.55 vs 0.99** on Llama-3.2-1B, giving AUC **0.99** and **1.00** for predicting silencing from norm alone; GPT-2 is the exception at **AUC 0.63**, since there the driver is exact grid collision of digit rows rather than rarity.
- **Accuracy does not follow the silenced count (Belebele, Qwen2.5-0.5B, 8-bit head).** Thai accuracy is 32.1% fp vs **35.3%** quantized (**+3.2** points) despite **550 of 2550** Thai tokens being silenced, and no language moves monotonically with its silenced count; with 900 questions per language the standard error is ≈1.6 points, so most of these shifts are within noise.
- **On real text, 8-bit losses are latent rather than firing.** Across **32 million** positions of Wikipedia text a silenced token is never the correct next token for Llama-3.2-1B, Qwen2.5-0.5B or Qwen2.5-7B at 8 bits; on Llama none of the 264 silenced tokens is ever produced by the tokenizer, and the Thai ones appear only ~half a million times as substrings inside longer words.
- **Calibrated quantizers delay but do not remove the effect.** AWQ keeps the head in 16 bits and adds no silenced token (Qwen2.5-0.5B's 75 "new" tokens all come from the fp16 cast, not AWQ); GPTQ's 8-bit head with groups of 32 adds **180** on Qwen2.5-0.5B (134 with the rest of the model at 4 bits) and **none** on Llama-3.2-1B or Qwen2.5-1.5B, but quantizing GPTQ's head to 4/3/2 bits silences **91/135/259** on Llama-3.2-1B and **796/817/834** on Qwen2.5-0.5B.

## Authors & Credibility

Single author: Giuseppe Pappalardo, University of Enna "Kore", Italy. No prior publication record is visible in this manuscript, and the repository is named `EACL2027Paper`, indicating a submission target rather than a published venue. The work is a short, focused empirical/analysis paper (≈10 pages including references) built almost entirely on one borrowed theoretical result (Grivas et al., 2022 on unargmaxable classes) plus a well-chosen engineering wrapper. The related-work section is competent and current, covering stolen probability (Demeter et al., 2020), the softmax bottleneck (Yang et al., 2018), under-trained "glitch" tokens (Land & Bartolo, 2024), and recent fidelity/reachability metrics (Nikolić et al., 2026; Awad et al., 2026), and it correctly positions the contribution as structural rather than corpus-level. Code and per-token measurements are released, which is the right call for a paper whose claims are per-token and otherwise unverifiable.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty is real but narrow.** The geometric criterion is not new — Grivas et al. (2022) established the convex-hull characterization and even argued that unargmaxable classes are theoretically possible but rare in practice under full precision. The contribution is showing that quantization makes them common, and that the resulting damage is script-selective. That is a genuine and previously unreported finding, but it is one observation applied to a known theorem, not a new method; the "winning direction / trapping bound / exact collision" trio is engineering around an LP the author cannot afford to solve.

**The missing validation is the estimator's own error rate.** Nowhere does the paper compute the exact silenced set for a vocabulary small enough to solve by LP (or exact hull membership) and compare it against the three-check estimate. The evidence that the estimator is tight is *internal consistency*: the lower end (exact collisions) and upper end (plus flagged) coincide in most cells. That is a statement about how many tokens remain flagged, not about whether any token was wrongly declared **safe** — and a wrong "safe" verdict is never detected by this check, because a bogus winning direction is a verifiable object while a missing one is not. Given that the entire empirical edifice rests on this estimator, an exact-solve comparison on GPT-2's 50k vocabulary (or a random 5k-row subsample with LP per row) is the ablation the paper most needs and does not have.

**The practical-harm evidence is much thinner than the framing.** The introduction motivates the work with multilingual 4-bit deployment and "a model can look healthy on an English benchmark while quietly losing the ability to emit certain Korean or Thai tokens." But the paper's own corpus audit finds **zero** positions in 32M Wikipedia tokens where a silenced token was the correct next token at 8 bits, the Belebele Thai accuracy actually *improves* (32.1 → 35.3), and Qwen2.5-1.5B silences **nothing new** at 8 bits because its fp head already has 2233 silenced rows — which cuts against a clean "quantization causes harm" narrative and is reported honestly but not reconciled with the motivation. The only demonstrated task-relevant failure is GPT-2 digits at 3 bits, a model and bit-width combination that real deployments essentially never use. The paper's defensible claim is "this is a latent, measurable risk that corpora may not surface," which is weaker than the abstract's "practical stakes are highest for multilingual deployment."

**One multilingual vocabulary, three models.** Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B/7B share a tokenizer, so the language-skew finding rests on n=1 vocabulary, and Llama-3.1-70B shares Llama-3.2-1B's. The author flags this in Limitations, but it means the central empirical claim has a single degree of freedom; a Gemma, Mistral or Aya head would have been cheap to add and would have made the result far more load-bearing.

**The full-precision baseline for Qwen is an upper bound, which complicates the differential.** Qwen2.5-0.5B's fp count of 1401 is explicitly an upper bound (only 4 rows are exact duplicates; the rest are the ~1400-row cluster with nearest-neighbour distances of 1e-5 to 1e-4). "Newly silenced relative to fp" is therefore measured against a soft baseline in exactly the regime where the flagged/safe split is admitted to be ill-conditioned. The paper handles this by holding the fp baseline fixed and reporting bounds, which is defensible, but the int8 "+834" and the fp "1401" are not the same kind of number and the surrounding text does not always keep that distinction visible.

**Tie handling is a defensible but consequential convention.** Counting tied duplicate rows as silenced (strict inequalities) inflates GPT-2's 175 by up to 42, Llama's 264 by 33 and Qwen's 834 by 5. The justification — a tie broken by token index is the decoder's rule, not the model's — is principled, but it means a nonzero fraction of the headline GPT-2 number is convention-dependent, and Table 3's "+0" for GPT-2 at int4/int3 per-channel versus 175 at per-tensor shows how sensitive the count is to scheme choice.

**Coverage of quantizers is thin where it matters.** Only RTN is swept systematically; GPTQ and AWQ cover four models, and smoothing/rotation-based methods (SmoothQuant, QuaRot, SpinQuant) are explicitly untested despite the paper arguing the mechanism is "generic." Since those methods are precisely the ones that change *which* rows collide, "changes how quickly it happens, not whether it can" is asserted rather than shown.

**One smaller red flag:** Table 1 (Hugging Face download counts) is motivation by popularity, is a lower bound by the author's own admission, and does not bear on any technical claim; it reads as padding in an otherwise lean paper. Conversely, the paper's intellectual honesty is above average for the genre — it reports the Belebele null result, the zero-hit corpus audit, and the Qwen1.5B counterexample rather than burying them, which is exactly the behaviour that makes the weaker claim credible.

## Strengths

- Asks a question that standard quantization evaluation structurally cannot answer, and answers it with a cheap, data-free, single-GPU procedure that a practitioner could actually run before shipping a checkpoint.
- Reports **intervals with stated semantics** rather than point estimates, and distinguishes "proven silenced", "flagged", and "safe" rather than collapsing them into a binary — the right epistemic posture for a heuristic estimator.
- The language-skew result is the kind of finding that changes practice: 27% of Korean tokens versus 0.3% of Latin at matched bit-width is not a rounding artifact and would be invisible to any perplexity or English-benchmark report.
- The mechanism is corroborated three independent ways: row-norm statistics (AUC 0.99/1.00), the under-trained-token literature, and the script-level breakdown — a coherent story rather than a single correlation.
- Genuinely honest reporting of negative and inconvenient results (Belebele Thai improving, zero corpus hits, Qwen2.5-1.5B unaffected), plus a Limitations section that pre-empts most of the obvious criticisms.
- The GPT-2 digit case is a clean, falsifiable demonstration that a structural guarantee translates into an actual impossible output, with a counted number of affected positions rather than an anecdote.

## Limitations

- No ground-truth comparison of the estimator against exact convex-hull/LP membership on a tractable vocabulary, so the false-safe rate is unmeasured.
- Evidence of downstream harm is limited to corpus-level audits (which found nothing at 8 bits), a Belebele run on one 0.5B model in ten languages, and the GPT-2 3-bit digit case; no full downstream evaluation on a multilingual task.
- The multilingual claim rests on a single vocabulary family (Qwen2.5), shared across all three Qwen models.
- The analysis covers the output head in isolation: it is exact for "can this token ever win", but only an upper bound on what the full network realizes on real inputs, and deployment pipelines differ in whether they quantize the head at all.
- The guarantee is for greedy decoding; silenced tokens retain nonzero probability and can still be emitted under sampling.
- Calibrated quantizers are tested on four models only; SmoothQuant, QuaRot, SpinQuant and other rotation-based methods are untested.
- On dense near-duplicate row clusters the safe/flagged classification is intrinsically unstable, so some counts (Qwen's fp 1401 upper bound, collapsed 2-bit heads, GPTQ's Qwen2.5-7B head) are not exact.
- Unicode script is a proxy for language, not an identifier; Cyrillic spans several languages and Han is shared between Chinese and Japanese.
- Counts depend on the strict-inequality tie convention, which inflates results by up to 42/175, 33/264 and 5/834 in the reported cells.

## Relevance

这篇论文直接关系到"压缩后的模型还能说什么"这一问题，对做量化部署、多语种服务或词表分析的工作都有参考价值：它提供了一个几乎零成本、单卡可跑的检查手段，并给出了一个容易被平均指标掩盖的结论——多语模型的量化损失高度集中在非拉丁文字上。对关注低资源语言公平性、tokenizer 词表利用率和量化评估方法论的读者尤其值得一读。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- What is the actual false-safe rate of the three-check estimator? Running exact LP membership on GPT-2's 50k rows (or a 5k random subsample of Qwen's) would settle this in a day and is the single most valuable missing experiment.
- Why does Belebele Thai accuracy *rise* (32.1 → 35.3) after 550 of 2550 Thai tokens are silenced? Is the quantized head simply sharper, and does the effect survive a task where the silenced tokens are more likely to be the target?
- Would a head-only higher-precision deployment (keep $W$ in 8 or 16 bits, quantize everything else) recover the lost Korean/Thai mass at negligible memory cost, and what is the actual byte budget for that on a 7B model?
- Does the language skew replicate on non-Qwen multilingual heads (Gemma-2, Aya-23, Mistral-Nemo) with genuinely different tokenizer training mixes, and does it correlate with per-language token frequency in the pretraining corpus?
- Can the effect be *prevented* rather than measured — e.g. by regularizing row norms during quantization, or by constraining the rounding grid so that low-norm rows cannot collapse onto cluster centroids?
- How does silencing interact with the under-trained "glitch token" literature — are the silenced tokens a subset of Land & Bartolo's detected under-trained tokens, and does silencing predict glitch-token behaviour before quantization?
- For the GPT-2 3-bit digit case, how much of the 52-position failure is recovered by simply keeping the head at 8 bits while quantizing the rest to 3?
