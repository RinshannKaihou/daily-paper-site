Why this matters

A common 4-bit KV-cache failure gets a measurable cause and a near-free fix — for anyone pushing aggressive inference optimization into long context.

Scope: single-stream edge decode, 600 MHz. Accuracy emulated in software; area and energy are modelled, not silicon.

Critical edge

On Llama-3.2-3B the gain collapses (0.667 → 0.363), and at 3.5 bits CarrierKV is worse than plain NVFP4 (1.35 vs 0.667) — with no per-model carrier measurement to say why.

Critical Assessment §1 · Limitations

01The diagnosis

A constant that RoPE only rotates

Every KV head's pre-RoPE keys sit near a mean vector c. RoPE spins it, but never shrinks it — so the cache stores mostly a positional bias.

02Method

Decompose, then rotate — and never decode a key

Keys are written as k(s) = c + r(s). Since RoPE is linear, the score splits exactly in two, and the first half is a closed form the hardware can synthesize instead of store.

qTR(s)k(s) = qTR(s)r(s) + qTR(s)c
content · on 4-bit codesquantized as ĤR(s)r(s); no key is ever widened back to FP16
carrier · on-chipaⱼcos(sθⱼ) − bⱼsin(sθⱼ), one dot + one cross per pair, per query head, per step

Exact for any fixed c — c only sets how small (and therefore how quantizable) the residual gets. This is not a learned approximation.

Write path · prefill once

k = c + r→ est. c over ≤512 prompt tok.→ subtract c→ RoPE→ Hadamard→ NVFP4 residual

Read path · every decode step

rotate q once → q̂→ content on 4-bit+ 28-bit phase acc.→ score

Token 0 is skipped as an attention sink. The carrier is stored once per layer in 8-bit with one shared exponent — 0.0002 bit/element of metadata at 32K, versus 0.23 bit/element for NSNQuant's per-chunk means, three orders of magnitude apart. The c prefetch is ≈36 KB per decode step: 0.003% of 32K KV traffic.

Rejected after measurement: centering after RoPE (the carrier rotates), and a plain randomized Hadamard on raw keys (it smears the dominant component across blocks — see Limitations).

RULER = 7 KV-sensitive subtasks of 13, 100 samples each (noise floor matters: Gemma's +1.1 pts is inside it). Best 32K RULER on all four models, but the PPL gain range is 1.8× (Llama) to 5.9× (Qwen3-8B). MK3 rises 43 → 79 against BF16's 87 — the subtask NVFP4 actually breaks.

Same cache budget, better trade than FP8 keys: 32K RULER 91.1 vs 89.3, 13.7% less energy/token and 1.16× faster; at 128K, 22.2% less energy and 1.28× faster.

Also holds under a different number format — 3-bit TurboQuant keys on Qwen3-4B go 69.7 → 0.762 PPL increase. And at 3.5 bits/key it beats 4.5-bit NVFP4 on three of four models (0.766 / 0.346 / 0.333 vs 0.917 / 0.503 / 0.363), a 22% cache cut the paper never pursues.

04Hardware

The carrier is cheaper than the DRAM

A numerically-controlled-oscillator block: one 28-bit phase accumulator per RoPE pair, advanced by θⱼ with adds only. Two quadrant bits pick the sign; the next eight index a 256-entry 8-bit quarter-turn cos table; one 64-pair set serves all G query heads of a KV head.

05Strengths
  • Mechanistic, not a bag of tricks. Energy share, block-scale coarsening, SQNR in dB, plus a causal ablation that removes the carrier's regeneration to prove the model uses it.
  • Exact decomposition. No accuracy ceiling of its own; c only changes how compressible the residual is — stronger footing than learned centering.
  • Metadata is genuinely negligible (0.0002 bit/element, 0.003% of traffic), which is why this could ship where per-chunk means cannot.
  • Keeps the NVFP4 format and footprint, so it composes with an existing 4-bit accelerator instead of demanding a new number system.
  • Honest accounting: per-block area/energy attribution, no silicon claimed, and negative Llama results sit in the main table.
  • Unusual evaluation breadth: 4 models, 7 RULER subtasks, all 21 LongBench tasks, 4K/32K/64K PG19, thinking-mode reasoning, two formats, and a length-resolved curve where the gap widens with context.
06Limitations
  • Model dependence is unexplained. Llama gains little, loses at 3.5 bits, and regresses under 3-bit TurboQuant; no per-model carrier statistics to predict who benefits.
  • Baselines are re-implementations. "SageAttention" is post-RoPE centering; "P3-LLM" uses a Llama-3 channel recipe that scores below plain NVFP4. NSNQuant and SpectrumQuant are never run.
  • Static carrier. Frozen after ≤512 prompt tokens; no test of drift over 32K+ generation, multi-turn switching, or domain shift, and no re-estimation scheme.
  • No silicon. Accuracy emulated on H100s; area/energy from a model plus gate-level power. Real FP4 accumulation order and kernel fusion are untested.
  • Single stream only. Batch 1, 600 MHz edge envelope — the 4.5-bit and c-prefetch economics say nothing about batched serving.
  • The Hadamard claim rests on one seed. On Qwen3 it turns 0.917 into 3.50 PPL and 79.4 into 57.7 RULER; no sweep over sign seed, block size, or placement.
07Open questions
  1. What is Llama's carrier share? Is the weak gain predicted by a smaller c, a different carrier direction across layers, or drift between the prefill estimate and keys seen 32K later?
  2. Does c drift? Re-estimating costs ~36 KB per layer — would periodic refresh recover Llama and the 3-bit TurboQuant regression?
  3. Why does Hadamard hurt Qwen3? QuaRot/SpinQuant report gains; is this massive-activation channels, and how sensitive to the random sign seed and rotation order?
  4. Can 3.5 bits work on Llama? A 22% cache reduction on three of four models is worth more than the accuracy win — but no RULER, LongBench, or reasoning numbers exist for it.
  5. Is there a carrier in values, or in MoE caches? And does the "one cos/sin set serves all G heads" argument get stronger under GQA with G ≫ 4?
  6. What breaks at batch > 1? Per-sequence c and phase accumulators must be replicated — does 0.0002 bit/element survive contact with real serving?
