> **一句话总结：** 把 Mamba 选择性扫描里时间尺度 Δ 的扰动从"加性"改成"对数"度量，得到一条在任意序列长度上一致成立、且不需要 Δ 下界的隐藏状态扰动上界 η+2M(e^ρ−1)（定理 3），并证明它与可达下界只差 e^ρ+1 倍（ρ→0 时趋近 2 倍）；在 SPY 订单簿数据上训练的两个 2 块 Mamba 小模型上，由此构造的数据无关证书在 softplus 时间尺度下于所有长度都优于 Cao 等人的加性基线。

> **TL;DR:** Measuring the change of Mamba's input-dependent timescale as |log Δ − log Δ̃| ≤ ρ instead of additively yields a bound |h_t − h̃_t| ≤ η + 2M(e^ρ − 1) that holds at every sequence length with no timescale floor, and a matching construction shows this is within a factor e^ρ + 1 (→ 2) of optimal. Instantiated as data-free block certificates on two 2-block Mamba models trained on one day of SPY limit-order-book data (Databento MBP-10, 28 Jan 2021), block 0 of the bounded-timescale arm gets a slope certificate of 9.34×10^6 versus 5.04×10^9 for the Cao et al.-style additive baseline at L = 5000 (539× looser), and on the softplus arm the baseline is looser at every length — but the certificate still sits 3.06×10^5 to 3.23×10^6 above what gradient attacks actually find.

## 核心贡献

- **度量方式的替换是关键。** 现有最接近的结果（Cao et al. 定理 3.3(ii)/注 3.4）用加性方式度量 Δ 的变化，因此 time-warp 项随长度线性增长，除非给 Δ 一个正下界（floor），而下界的代价是界按 1/Δ_lo 放大。本文改用在 log Δ 上的度量。
- **比值引理（Lemma 2）。** 对 x > 0、x̃ = x e^s，有 |e^{−x} − e^{−x̃}| ≤ |e^s − 1|(1 − e^{−x})。即：时间尺度相对变化一步只移动"输入权重"的一个固定比例，而不是一个与输入权重无关的绝对量。这是全文的技术核心，证明只用了凹函数 y ↦ 1 − e^{−y} 的弦斜率单调性。
- **主定理（Theorem 3）。** 若 |log Δ_t − log Δ̃_t| ≤ ρ、|c̃_t| ≤ M、|h̃_{−1}| ≤ M，则 |h_t − h̃_t| ≤ P_t|h_{−1} − h̃_{−1}| + (1 − P_t)·E，其中 E = η + 2M(e^ρ − 1)，P_t = Π_{k≤t} Ā_k。该界对每个 t 成立、随扰动幅度趋于 0、且**不需要 Δ 的任何下界**。证明的要点是：每一步的扰动项带因子 (1 − Ā_t)，与收缩因子 Ā_t 同源，因此二者之比有界，误差不会累积。
- **匹配的下界（Proposition 4）。** 构造出让 c̃_t 在 ±M 之间交替、Δ 在 e^{±ρ} 之间交替的运行对，其间隙可以任意接近 η + 2M tanh(ρ/2)。由于 2M(e^ρ − 1) = (e^ρ + 1)·2M tanh(ρ/2)，定理 3 距离最优只差 e^ρ + 1 倍，ρ→0 时该因子趋近 2。
- **扩展到发布版实现（Corollary 7）。** Mamba 官方代码用的是"精确转移 + 欧拉输入项"（文中称 exponential-Euler 步），其平衡点被放大 Ψ(λΔ_t) = λΔ_t/(1 − e^{−λΔ_t}) 倍。在有上界 Δ_hi 时，界变成 Gη^b + 3GM(e^ρ − 1)，G = Ψ(λΔ_hi)：仍不需要下界，但需要上界，且常数 2 变成 3。
- **端到端的块级证书（Corollary 8）。** 把定理 3 与各阶段的 sup-norm Lipschitz 常数复合，得到"对块内任意输入成立"的数据无关输出斜率界，并用区间算术 + Laguerre–Samuelson 型 LayerNorm 界（Lemma 9，融合投影的 Proposition 10）实际算出数值。
- **诚实的适用边界。** 作者明确指出：沿给定输入，log 度量与加性度量在一阶意义下等价；log 度量的收益只体现在"必须对任意输入成立"的数据无关证书里。

## Key Contributions

- **Reframing the perturbation metric.** Existing analyses (Cao et al. Thm 3.3(ii), Remark 3.4) measure Δ's change additively, which forces linear-in-length growth unless a positive floor Δ_lo exists — and then costs a factor 1/Δ_lo. The paper replaces this with a log-scale measure, |log Δ_t − log Δ̃_t| ≤ ρ ("the log condition"), from which both the linear growth and the floor requirement disappear.
- **Lemma 2 (relative perturbation of the transition).** For x > 0 and x̃ = x e^s, |e^{−x} − e^{−x̃}| ≤ |e^s − 1|(1 − e^{−x}). Written for the cell: |Ā_t − Ã_t| ≤ (e^ρ − 1)(1 − Ā_t), i.e. the transition moves by at most a fixed fraction of *that step's input weight* rather than by an absolute amount that can exceed the whole input weight at small λΔ (Figure 1a: at λΔ = 10^{−4} the additive measure gives 99.5× the input weight).
- **Theorem 3 (cell bound).** A uniform-in-t bound E = η + 2M(e^ρ − 1), with no floor, no upper bound on Δ, and a finite-horizon refinement (1 − P_t)E. Proof: write the error as a convex recursion with bounded equilibrium and unroll; the telescoping sum Σ_k (1 − Ā_k) Π_{r>k} Ā_r = 1 − P_t does the work.
- **Proposition 4 (matching lower bound).** An explicit alternating construction shows every bound depending only on (M, η, ρ) must be at least η + 2M tanh(ρ/2). Hence E is within a factor e^ρ + 1 of optimal, and that factor → 2 as ρ → 0.
- **Additive baseline (5) and a regime-by-regime comparison.** Re-derives Cao et al.'s structure but keeps the factor e^{−λΔ_lo} their Lemma B.3 drops, giving η + 2Mλη_Δ Σ_{k<L} e^{−kλΔ_lo}. Shows the two bounds coincide to first order in x = λΔ_lo, with ours sandwiched between the additive baseline and Remark 3.4's form — so the empirical gain must come from bounding log Δ *directly* from the pre-activation box, not from a better constant.
- **Remark 5 (ratio for the bounded map).** When boxes are used, the asymptotic ratio of the floored additive term to ours is (sup_Z|Δ'| / (Δ_lo sup_Z|(log Δ)'|)) · x/(e^x − 1); when 0 ∈ Z and R = Δ_max/Δ_min, this is at most 4 max_p R^p p(1−p) — 25.8 for R = 100 — a cap that depends only on the chosen timescale range.
- **Corollary 7 (exponential-Euler cell).** Handles the step actually used in released Mamba, where the equilibrium is amplified by Ψ(λΔ_t) ∈ (1, 1 + λΔ_t); needs a timescale *ceiling* but still no floor.
- **Corollary 8 + a full experimental protocol.** Block certificate built from interval-arithmetic certified boxes; comparison against both an additive-baseline certificate and sign-gradient attacks, with the slack attributed stage by stage.

## Methodology

**Setting.** Because A is diagonal with negative entries, the scan splits into independent scalar recurrences ("cells"), one per (channel, state index). With λ = |A_{i,n}| and the exact zero-order hold, Ā_t = e^{−λΔ_t} and B̄_t = (1 − Ā_t)B_t/λ, so h_t = Ā_t h_{t−1} + (1 − Ā_t)c_t with c_t = B_t u_t/λ. Since Ā_t ∈ (0,1), each step is a convex combination of the previous state and an equilibrium c_t; the weight 1 − Ā_t is called the input weight. Two timescale maps are considered: a bounded map Δ = Δ_min (Δ_max/Δ_min)^{sigmoid(z)} and Mamba's Δ = softplus(z), which has no positive lower bound over ℝ.

**Forward invariant (Proposition 1).** |c_t| ≤ M for all t implies |h_t| ≤ max(|h_{−1}|, M). This is Horbatko's Lemma 4.4 stated for a diagonal Mamba channel; it supplies the M in the main theorem and is one of the pieces machine-checked in Isabelle/HOL.

**Ratio lemma and cell theorem.** Lemma 2 (proved via monotonicity of the chord slope of 1 − e^{−y}) bounds the transition change by (e^ρ − 1)(1 − Ā_t). Subtracting the two updates and inserting ±Ā_t h̃_{t−1}, ±(1 − Ā_t)c̃_t gives the error identity e_t = Ā_t e_{t−1} + (1 − Ā_t)(c_t − c̃_t) + (Ā_t − Ã_t)(h̃_{t−1} − c̃_t), whose last factor is bounded by 2M. Hence |e_t| ≤ Ā_t|e_{t−1}| + (1 − Ā_t)E, and unrolling with the telescoping sum yields Theorem 3. An independent derivation follows Cao et al.'s Lemma B.2 after a change of time variable that aligns the two runs onto one grid; the size of that time change is the largest |log r_s|, formally a Skorokhod-type norm.

**Lower bound.** Proposition 4's construction lets c̃_t alternate ±M and Δ̃_t = Δ_t e^{∓ρ}, so the nominal run has the larger input weight exactly on the steps with c̃_t = M. Splitting h_t − h̃_t into an η-driven part (which tends to η) and an equilibrium-driven part (whose even-step subsequence is an affine map with fixed point 2M(b − a)/(1 − ab), a = e^{−Qτ}, b = e^{−τ}), then letting τ → 0, gives 2M tanh(ρ/2).

**From cell to block certificate.** Corollary 8 pushes a sup-norm perturbation δ of the *normalised* block input through: input projection → depthwise causal conv + SiLU → x_proj (giving B, C, p) → dt_proj (giving z) → Lemma 6 (d log Δ/dz ≤ 1 for softplus, ≤ ¼log(Δ_max/Δ_min) for the bounded map) → ρ_i → Theorem 3 per cell → readout C h + D u → gate and output projection → Γ_k. Certified boxes come from the Laguerre–Samuelson coordinate bound (Lemma 9: |LN(x)_i − β_i| ≤ |γ_i|√(d−1)) and a *fused* bound (Proposition 10) that maximises ⟨W_k ⊙ γ, z⟩ over the zero-mean, ‖z‖₂ ≤ √d set jointly with the linear map, giving ‖v_k − v̄_k 1‖₂√d rather than the ℓ₁ quantity ‖v_k‖₁√(d−1). The fused form matters because the radius sup|Bu|/λ inherits the difference.

**Experiments.** Data: Databento MBP-10 records of SPY on 28 January 2021, ~4.55 M rows after feature construction, 43 model inputs, windows of 100 ticks, target = log return of the mid-price over the next 100 ticks. Chronological 70/15/15 split with a 100-tick purge at each boundary; features winsorised at the 0.001/0.999 training quantiles. Model: 43 → d_model = 64 (linear + SiLU + LayerNorm), two Mamba blocks with d_inner = 128, d_state = 16, conv width 4, timescale-projection rank 4, then LayerNorm + two-layer head at the last position. Two "arms" differ only in the timescale map: bounded on [10^{−3}, 10^{−1}] (Mamba's initialisation range) vs softplus (both use the exact ZOH, not released code's Euler input term). Selection by rank information coefficient on a fixed 12,800-window validation slice; ≤3000 steps, kept checkpoints at step 750 (bounded) and 2000 (softplus). Certificates computed by interval arithmetic in float32 for boxes/radii and float64 elsewhere, without outward rounding; attacks are sign-gradient ascent in float64 re-evaluated with a reference forward pass, so each attack value is a lower bound on true sensitivity.

## Results & Highlights

- **The additive baseline loses after ~8 steps on the bounded arm, and by two orders of magnitude at L = 5000.** On block 0, the data-free output slope from Theorem 3 is 9.34×10^6 versus 5.04×10^9 for the floor-free additive baseline (5) at L = 5000 — a ratio of 539× — and the affine baseline only overtakes the flat certificate at L ≈ 8.3 steps. Block 1: 6.08×10^6 vs 3.27×10^9 (538×), crossing L ≈ 8.1. The baseline grows by ≈0.11× the certificate per step, so the crossing length is a property of the trained model while the L = 5000 ratio is not.
- **On the softplus arm the baseline is looser at every tested length.** Block 0: certificate 1.85×10^7 vs 3.59×10^11 for the floor-free baseline at L = 5000, a ratio of 1.95×10^4 (block 1: 1.46×10^7 vs 2.87×10^11, 1.97×10^4); the crossing column in Table 1 is "none". With the *certified* floor (1.3×10^{−21} on block 0, 1.9×10^{−17} on block 1) the two forms of the baseline stay within 1% of each other up to L = 5000, and the floored baseline's L → ∞ limit is 2.1×10^19× the certificate on block 0 and 2.8×10^15× on block 1.
- **Cell-level, the advantage depends entirely on the timescale range.** In the bounded-map cell search (ρ = 0.01, boxes Z = [−8,8], Δ ∈ [10^{−3},10^{−1}]), the additive baseline passes the theorem's bound at T = 39 without a floor and T = 40 with it at λ = 1; at λ = 17.14 — the steepest decay rate of the trained bounded arm — that drops to T = 3. The floored baseline saturates at 25.6× the theorem's bound as T → ∞, just under the 25.8 predicted by Remark 5 for R = 100, and per-cell ratios over the trained model lie between 24.1 and 25.8. On the softplus map (Z = [−48,2], floor ≈ 1.4×10^{−21}) the baseline is already above the theorem's bound at T = 2, above the trivial bound 2M + η from T = 114, and reaches >2600× the theorem's bound at T = 3000.
- **Numerical search finds no gap above the construction's value.** With λ = 1, M = 1, both runs from 0, ρ ∈ {0.01, 0.03, 0.1, 0.3, 0.6}, η ∈ {0, 0.1}, and lengths T from 10 to 1000 in the hypothesis class λΔ ∈ [10^{−4}, 10], no gap found exceeded η + 2M tanh(ρ/2). At T ≥ 300, Adam from construction-shaped starts got within 0.01% of that value at every (ρ, η), and from 64 random starts within 0.4%; at T = 10 the largest gap was < 1.1% below it. For η = 0 and T ≥ 300 the theorem's bound E equals (e^ρ + 1) × the largest gap to four significant figures — ≈2.0 at ρ = 0.01 and ≈2.8 at ρ = 0.6, exactly the predicted degradation.
- **Certificates are 5+ orders of magnitude looser than gradient attacks — and almost all the slack is the data-free worst case, not the log metric.** At δ = 10^{−6} on four held-out SPY windows at L ∈ {10, 100, 1000, 5000}, the certificate is 3.06×10^5 to 3.23×10^6 times the attack slope. A *local* bound that propagates around the realised input (replacing 2M by the realised |h_{t−1} − c_t|) is at most 12.7× the attack slope for δ ≤ 10^{−4}, and dropping the log-timescale interval from that intersection changes it by at most 0.034% — confirming the paper's own claim that log vs additive only matters for the data-free regime. Stage-wise slack on block 0 of the bounded arm: 8.2 after u, 134 after B, 537 after c, 1.54×10^4 after h, 3.24×10^5 after y, 4.65×10^7 at the output; the gate + output projection contribute the largest per-stage factor (98–147), then the scan (18–39), then the readout (14–29).
- **Sanity check that the testbed model learned something.** The bounded arm reaches a rank information coefficient of 0.13 on the first 25,600 test windows, with block-bootstrap 95% interval [0.075, 0.197]. No comparison to other architectures is attempted, and the trained model is only a vehicle for the certificate.
- **Attack slopes grow far more slowly than lengths.** From L = 10 to L = 5000 at δ = 10^{−6}, the attack slope grows by 1.4×–7.3× while the floor-free baseline grows by ≥445×; the largest change occurs at the last position in only 1 of 16 (arm, block, length) combinations, and at the last position alone the growth is only 1.06×–2.3×.

## Authors & Credibility

Jack Humphries (Department of Computer Science, University of Surrey) and Daniel Midgley (Department of Mathematics, University of Surrey). A two-author, single-institution arXiv preprint in cs.LG; no venue or prior-version information is given in the PDF, and I found no track record to lean on inside the paper itself.

Credibility signals that do count:

- **Partial formal verification.** Eq. (1) (the ZOH discretisation), Proposition 1, Lemma 9 and the first part of Proposition 10 are machine-checked in Isabelle/HOL, with the theory in the public repository. The authors are explicit that the interesting parts — Lemma 2, Theorem 3, Proposition 4 — are *not* machine-checked, and that the interval propagation computing M is tested Python but not verified. That is an unusually precise statement of what is and is not proved.
- **Released code and recorded runs.** https://github.com/jackdoesjava/mamba-v-lob, including the local bound and the recorded training runs. The data (Databento MBP-10) is commercial and not redistributable, but every hyperparameter needed to rebuild it is stated.
- **Two independent derivations of the main theorem**, one by the convex-recursion/invariant route and one by adapting Cao et al.'s Lemma B.2 with an explicit time-change argument. Agreement between them is a real check on the algebra.
- **A generative-AI disclosure** stating Claude assisted with literature search, proof checking, code, experiments and drafting, and that the authors traced every reported number back to the code. This is the right disclosure, but it also means the proof-checking burden on the reader is higher than usual for an unrefereed preprint.
- **The Limitations section is unusually specific and, in places, self-undermining in a good way** — it reports that float32 rounding exceeds the certified state change in 49% and 61% of cells, and that the layer-norm sup-norm Lipschitz constant blocks an end-to-end raw-input certificate.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The core idea is small and correct, which is the best kind of theory contribution. Lemma 2 is a three-line chord-slope argument, but it identifies the right structural fact: the disturbance term and the contraction share the same factor (1 − Ā_t), so their ratio cannot blow up as Δ_t → 0. That is precisely what makes the floor unnecessary, and it is a genuinely different mechanism from the "cancel the Δ in the input gain against the 1/Δ in the transition" observation of Orvieto et al. §4 or Zucchet & Orvieto's fixed-eigenvalue log coordinates. The tightness result is also real work rather than an afterthought: the alternating construction is non-obvious (the naive c̃ alternating ±M with Δ̃ = Δ e^{∓ρ} does not give 2M tanh(ρ/2) — you need the *pairing* of which run has the larger input weight on which step), and the resulting e^ρ + 1 factor is exactly what the numerical search measures (2.0 at ρ = 0.01, 2.8 at ρ = 0.6).

**Rigor.** The proof chain is sound as far as I can check by hand: Lemma 2 → error identity (4) → telescoping sum → Theorem 3, with the 2M factor coming from |h̃_{t−1} − c̃_t| ≤ 2M and |h̃| ≤ M supplied by Proposition 1. The Appendix B re-derivation by time change is independent and consistent. Two soft spots. First, the truncated-sum argument |h̃_{t−1}| ≤ M relies on |h̃_{−1}| ≤ M plus the invariant, which is fine, but the theorem is stated for a common start *inside* [−M, M] and Proposition 4's construction starts both runs at 0 with η > 0 forcing c_t = M + η outside [−M, M] — the authors admit this (Section 7) and note that under the natural restriction c_t, c̃_t ∈ [−M, M] no gap can approach η + 2M tanh(ρ/2) once η > 4M/(e^ρ + 1), and that the restricted construction only reaches η + (2M − η)tanh(ρ/2). So "within a factor e^ρ + 1 of the best possible" holds for the theorem's stated hypothesis class but *not* for the hypothesis class a reader would naturally assume. That is a substantive qualification buried in Limitations rather than stated next to Proposition 4. Second, the finite-horizon refinement, the "per-step ρ_t, η_t" generalization, and the 3GM(e^ρ − 1) form of Corollary 7 are stated without the same care about which hypotheses are t-dependent; Corollary 7 in particular needs Δ ≤ Δ_hi, and G = Ψ(λΔ_hi) can be large, so the released-code version is materially weaker than the clean ZOH version the experiments use.

**Reproducibility.** Code and recorded runs are public and the hyperparameters are complete except for the learning rate (Section 5.1 says both arms use "one learning rate" without stating it) and the absence of any seed reporting; one training run per arm means no seed variance at all. The data is a licensed Databento MBP-10 extract for a single instrument on a single day (SPY, 28 Jan 2021), so nobody can reproduce the numbers without buying the data. And the authors explicitly decline to attribute anything to the timescale map: "each arm is a single training run, and the two kept checkpoints come from different training steps, so we draw no conclusion about the timescale maps from differences between the arms" — yet the abstract states "With Mamba's softplus timescale ... it is looser at every length" without that caveat. The statement is true *of these two checkpoints' certified boxes*; it is not established as a property of softplus. Given that the certified floor of 1.3×10^{−21} is what drives the entire softplus result, and that this floor comes from a box Z = [−48, 2] whose lower end is arbitrary (nothing ties −48 to a certified bound on the pre-activation — it is chosen to make the box large), the softplus comparison is closer to an illustration than a measurement.

**Baselines.** The only comparison is against one re-derived baseline: Cao et al.'s Theorem 3.3(ii) structure, re-stated as eq. (5), plus the trivial bound 2M + η. There is no comparison with the other relevant certification machinery — star-set reachability (Tran et al.), Marabou-based invariant inference (Jacoby et al.), the Lyapunov analyses of Halloran et al. or Zubić & Scaramuzza, or Thapliyal's interval propagation — all of which are cited in Related Work and none of which is run. "Tighter than the existing analysis" therefore means "tighter than one specific bound", and the practical question a reader cares about (is this the best available certificate for a Mamba block?) is not answered. The 25.8 cap in Remark 5 makes this sharper: on the bounded arm the entire reported gain (24–26×) is set by R = Δ_max/Δ_min = 100, a number the authors chose because it is Mamba's initialisation range. With R → 1 the two certificates coincide; with R = 10^4 the cap would be ~2500× in the other direction. The gain is a property of the *box*, not of Mamba.

**Significance.** This is where the paper is most exposed, and to their credit the authors say so. The certificate is 3×10^5 to 3×10^6 times what gradient attacks find, and the honest local bound is still 12.7× the attack slope. A bound that is five orders of magnitude loose cannot support a robustness claim about a deployed model. The paper's own local comparison shows the log metric buys 0.034% in that regime. So the contribution is: a correct, tight-in-the-cell, floor-free theorem, whose practical payoff is confined to data-free worst-case certificates that are currently too loose to be decision-relevant. That is a legitimate theory paper, but the abstract's framing ("our bounds remain more than five orders of magnitude above the changes that gradient attacks find" is stated as a caveat at the end, after the softplus comparison) risks a reader taking away a stronger practical message than the evidence supports.

**Red flags / smaller issues.**

- **Real arithmetic everywhere.** Corollary 8 is proved in ℝ and evaluated *without outward rounding*. Section 7 then reports that on the bounded arm float32 scan rounding accumulates to ≈1100× the per-step error (≈2×10^{−4}M) and exceeds the certified state change in 49% and 61% of cells at δ = 10^{−6}. The certificate therefore does not cover the float32 model that produced the numbers. This is disclosed but not fixed, and it is arguably fatal for the "certificate" framing — a certificate that is invalidated by the arithmetic actually used is not a certificate.
- **Transitions rounding to exactly 1.** In bfloat16 this happens to the slowest transitions of the bounded arm; in float32 to the slowest softplus transitions where λΔ reaches 10^{−21}. Then the update is not a convex combination and Theorem 3 does not apply to the computed scan. Again disclosed, again unaddressed.
- **The block certificate does not compose.** It is per block, against the *normalised* input, and stops before the residual stream and the head. Section 7 explains why lifting it to raw input is hard: the sup-norm Lipschitz constant of layer normalisation is 3.9 at realised inputs but ~653 at a constant input, so a data-free raw-input certificate would carry ≥653. Nothing in the paper closes that gap, and without it there is no end-to-end statement about the network.
- **The one-cell search is a bounded search, not a proof.** 64 random starts plus shaped starts, Adam, 600 steps, ρ grid of five values, η ∈ {0, 0.1}. "No gap found exceeds η + 2M tanh(ρ/2)" is evidence, not a theorem, and the authors correctly say so — but the abstract's "within a factor of about 2 of the best possible" is the *proved* e^ρ + 1 statement, which is weaker at moderate ρ.
- **Interval boxes ignore input dependence.** Every stage takes the worst case over its box independently, and the authors measure the cost (gate + output projection: 98–147×). A zonotope or affine-set method would likely recover a large part of that factor, and the paper does not try.
- **The order-book testbed adds little.** Rank IC 0.13 with CI [0.075, 0.197] shows signal exists but gives no reason to think the two-block, 64-wide model is representative of Mamba in deployment; the authors say as much ("We do not certify a released language model here"). The LOB framing is motivation, not evidence.

## Strengths

- A clean, correct, and genuinely *new* mechanism: the (1 − Ā_t) factor shared by contraction and disturbance is the reason no floor is needed, and Lemma 2 makes it a two-line inequality rather than an asymptotic argument.
- A matching lower bound that is constructive and tight to a measurable constant (e^ρ + 1, verified numerically as 2.0 at ρ = 0.01 and 2.8 at ρ = 0.6), not just an order-of-magnitude claim.
- The additive baseline (5) is derived *carefully*, keeping the e^{−λΔ_lo} factor that Cao et al.'s Lemma B.3 drops, so the comparison is if anything generous to the baseline rather than to the authors' own method.
- Section 3.3 and Appendix B do the comparison the right way: they show that with only additive information the three bounds differ by <2% at the certified floors (x < 0.018), which isolates exactly where the gain comes from — bounding log Δ directly from the pre-activation box — instead of claiming a better constant.
- The claim that log vs additive only matters for data-free bounds is stated up front, proved to first order, and then *measured* (0.034% in the local bound). Papers rarely undercut their own headline this precisely.
- The Isabelle/HOL fragment is small but well chosen: the ZOH update, the forward invariant, the LayerNorm coordinate bound, and the fused projection bound are exactly the pieces whose errors would silently corrupt every number downstream.
- Section 7 is a model of what a limitations section should be: it lists what would invalidate the results (float32 rounding, transition saturation at 1, non-composable certificates, unknown true worst-case gap), with numbers.

## Limitations

- **Scope: one scalar cell with a real transition in (0,1).** Requires diagonal A. Does not cover Mamba-3's complex-valued state update, whose transition is not a real number in (0,1).
- **The optimality constant is not proved for the natural hypothesis class.** Whether η + 2M tanh(ρ/2) is the true worst-case gap from a common start is open; the search gives lower bounds only. Under the restriction that both equilibria lie in [−M, M], the achievable gap is η + (2M − η)tanh(ρ/2), and the largest gap in that class is unknown.
- **Certificates are per block, against the normalised input.** No composition through the residual stream and head; no raw-input certificate, because layer norm's global sup-norm Lipschitz constant is ≥653 at a constant input (3.9 at realised inputs). Consequently there is no end-to-end robustness statement for the trained network.
- **Real arithmetic only.** No outward rounding; float32 scan error accumulates to ≈1100× the per-step error (~2×10^{−4}M on the bounded arm) and exceeds the certified state change in 49%/61% of cells at δ = 10^{−6}. Where transitions round to exactly 1 (bfloat16 on the bounded arm's slowest cells, float32 on the softplus arm's slowest), the convex-combination structure — and hence the theorem — does not hold for the computed scan.
- **Looseness.** 3.06×10^5 to 3.23×10^6 above attack slopes at δ = 10^{−6}; the best realised-input local bound is still 12.7× above. Stage-wise slack is dominated by the gate + output projection (98–147×) and the per-stage worst-case-over-box treatment.
- **Empirical base is one dataset, one day, one instrument, one training run per arm.** Databento MBP-10 SPY, 28 Jan 2021, commercial licence; no seed variance; the two checkpoints come from different steps (750 vs 2000), so no causal claim about timescale maps is supported.
- **Experiments use the exact ZOH, not released Mamba.** The exponential-Euler extension (Corollary 7) exists but is not evaluated; it requires a timescale ceiling and costs a factor 3 and an M → GM blow-up.
- **Baseline set is narrow.** Only Cao et al.'s bound (in a re-derived, slightly sharpened form) and the trivial 2M + η; no reachability, invariant-search, or Lyapunov-based competitor.

## Relevance

与"序列模型的可证明鲁棒性 / 状态空间模型的稳定性理论"这一方向高度契合：如果你关心 Mamba 在远超训练长度的流式数据（如订单簿）上是否稳定、以及能否给出对任意输入成立的敏感度证书，这篇给出了一个不用时间尺度下界、且在单 cell 内几乎最优的定量工具。但要注意它目前只到"块级 + 数据无关 + 比攻击松 5 个数量级"的程度，更像理论基座而非可直接部署的验证器。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Is η + 2M tanh(ρ/2) the exact worst-case gap from a common start, and what is the exact value when both equilibria are constrained to [−M, M]? Section 7 suggests η + (2M − η)tanh(ρ/2) is achievable in that class but does not prove optimality. A proof (or a better construction) would settle the constant completely.
2. Can the factor e^ρ + 1 be reduced by using a time-varying Lyapunov function instead of the constant E? The bound is tight for the *constant* E, but a per-step potential might track the alternating construction better and close the 2× gap at small ρ.
3. Does replacing interval boxes with zonotopes or affine arithmetic recover the 98–147× lost at the gate + output projection? That single stage dominates the block certificate's slack, so it is the highest-leverage target — and the authors' own stage-wise accounting makes the target explicit.
4. How much of the 653× layer-norm blow-up at constant inputs actually materialises for a trained model, and can the box-aware (3.9×) constant be made into a valid data-free raw-input certificate — e.g. by certifying over the image of the normalisation (the sphere-like set Z of Lemma 9) rather than over a box containing it?
5. What goes wrong with Mamba-3's complex transitions? Lemma 2 relies on the concavity of 1 − e^{−y} for real y; a complex analogue would need |e^{−z} − e^{−z̃}| ≤ ... with a modulus argument, and it is not obvious the (1 − Ā) factor survives.
6. Does the log condition suggest a *training-time* regulariser — e.g. penalising sup |d log Δ/dz| · (box width), which Lemma 6 makes computable — that would make ρ small enough for E to be useful, rather than only certifying after the fact?
7. For the bounded map, the gain is capped by 4 max_p R^p p(1−p) ≈ 25.8 at R = 100. How does the comparison behave for a log-uniform initialisation spanning many decades (R = 10^4 or more), where the cap grows roughly linearly in R? Is the softplus arm's collapse to "baseline looser at every length" simply the R → ∞ limit of that formula?
