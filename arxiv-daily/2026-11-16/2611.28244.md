> **一句话总结：** 作者在同一个 autoresearch agent 框架内一次只改一个记忆组件，用三个 backbone（gpt-oss-120b / gpt-6-luna / gpt-6-sol）在 MLE-Bench 与 KernelBench 上做受控对比，发现"每次尝试压成可展开摘要"的 context folding 在全部六个设置里都最好（相对 recency truncation 最高 +19.2%），而 skill 库里"删除长期未被选中的经验"平均收益最大（+4.1%）；两者合成 MLEvolver 后在 MLE-Bench Lite 拿到 72.7% 奖牌率、KernelBench L3 的 fast1 达 94%。

> **TL;DR:** A controlled, one-component-at-a-time ablation of memory harness design for long-horizon autoresearch agents. On MLE-Bench (10 Kaggle competitions, gold-normalized score, GNS) and KernelBench (50 stratified problems, geometric-mean speedup over PyTorch Eager) with three backbones at a fixed 32K context budget, context folding — a per-attempt digest that can be unfolded in full — beats recency truncation in all six settings (e.g. KernelBench gpt-6-luna 1.74× vs 1.46× baseline, +19.2%; MLE-Bench gpt-oss-120b GNS 76.1 vs 70.5, +7.9%), while compaction (rolling summary) is a wash (+0.3% average). Skill deletion (prune after 5 unpicked iterations) gives the best skill-management gain (+4.1% average vs +3.0% promotion, +2.2% merging). The combined harness, MLEvolver, reports 72.7% any-medal on MLE-Bench Lite (16/22) vs AIDE 59.1% and OpenEvolve 63.6%, and 94% fast1 on KernelBench L3 vs 78% and 70%. Worth reading for the clean isolation design and the negative compaction result; the head-to-head system comparison is confounded by per-iteration LLM-call budgets and lacks seed variance.

## 核心贡献

- **把"harness 设计"拆成可单独消融的两个维度**：context management（history 怎么留存）与 skill management（经验怎么存、怎么复用），在完全相同的 agent loop 内只改一个组件，得到 baseline + 5 个变体共 6 种配置。
- **context folding 是六个设置里全面最优的 history 策略**：每次尝试压成一条可展开的诊断摘要，平均相对基线提升 8.6%，而 rolling-summary 式的 compaction 平均只有 +0.3%，且在 gpt-6-sol 上反而退化。
- **skill 库要"留"也要"管"**：相对无 skill 的 No-Skill 版本，不加管理的 skill 库只带来 +2.1%；加上删除策略后额外 +4.1%，说明剪枝本身就是收益来源，而不只是省 token。
- **揭示 backbone 与 skill 使用方式的耦合**：gpt-oss-120b 每次检索 6.4–7.1 条 skill，gpt-6-luna 1.2–1.9 条、gpt-6-sol 0.5–0.6 条；且 gpt-oss-120b 选中的 skill 中 53.1% 是"进攻型"（提升分数），两个闭源模型只有 30.2% / 25.4%（更偏防守）。这解释了为什么 skill 管理对弱一些的 backbone 收益更大。
- **给出可用系统 MLEvolver**（folding + deletion），在 MLE-Bench Lite 与 KernelBench L3 上同时超过 AIDE 与 OpenEvolve，并公开了 KernelBench 的防 reward-hacking 检查清单（AST 静态检查、参考输出隔离、数值状态隔离）。

## Key Contributions

- **A controlled isolation study of harness memory.** Six configurations in one agent: recency-truncation baseline, plus two context mechanisms (folding, compaction) and three skill mechanisms (deletion, merging, promotion), each changing exactly one component so cross-system confounds (search policy, prompts, tool use) are removed.
- **Empirical answer: keep individually addressable records, not a running summary.** Folding is best in all 6 model×benchmark settings (+8.6% average); compaction is near-neutral (+0.3%) and hurts gpt-6-sol on MLE-Bench (−1.7%), directly contradicting the common "summarize the past" default.
- **Skill curation beats skill accumulation.** An unmanaged library adds only +2.1% over No-Skill; deletion then adds a further +4.1% by using the model's own retrieval behavior as a relevance signal (library ends at 31 skills vs 312 unmanaged on MLE-Bench/gpt-oss-120b).
- **A backbone-behavior explanation for heterogeneous gains.** Retrieval volume and offensive/defensive orientation of picked skills differ sharply across backbones, and track the size of the skill-management gain (5.1% for gpt-oss-120b vs 1.9% / 2.1% for the gpt-6 models).
- **MLEvolver**, a concrete harness combining the two winning mechanisms, plus a documented KernelBench evaluation protocol (stratified 50-problem subset, median timing, anti-cheat gates).

## Methodology

**Setting.** Long-horizon iterative self-evolution: for each task the agent repeats a scored attempt loop 100 times (MLE-Bench) or 50 times (KernelBench). Each iteration: an *action selector* chooses `propose_new` / `refine_current` / `debug_current`; a *skill picker* retrieves skills from a task-local library; a *coder* writes a full solution given task description, standing rules, picked skills, and a history representation; the benchmark evaluates and scores it; a *skill writer* distills lessons. All six configurations share this loop and differ only in the history policy Φ and the skill policy.

**Fixed budget.** The coder prompt is capped at B = 32,000 tokens: `C_t = [T, R_t, K_t, Φ(H_t)]`. Everything else (prompts, tools, action space) is held constant.

**Context mechanisms.**
- *Baseline (recency truncation):* keep the longest suffix of attempts that fits in the history budget B_H, discard older attempts permanently.
- *Folding:* a summarizer σ maps every attempt a_i to a digest â_i; the prompt contains all digests plus the full text of a subset U_t the agent explicitly requests to unfold (priority-ordered until B_H is filled). Every attempt stays addressable for the whole run.
- *Compaction:* a running structured summary m (objective / important details / current state) plus a recent tail τ; older head h is folded into m via m ← S(m, h). Individual attempts are irretrievable.

**Skill mechanisms** (task-local library, reset per task).
- *Deletion:* remove any skill unselected for τ_d = 5 consecutive iterations.
- *Merging:* embed skills with Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B; merge a new skill into its nearest live neighbor if cosine ≥ θ = 0.8.
- *Promotion:* a promoter agent nominates skills that were selected often *and* present on iterations producing a new best; admitted nominees become standing rules injected into every subsequent coder prompt and leave the retrieval catalog.

**Benchmarks.** MLE-Bench: 10 competitions (6 low, 4 medium complexity), 100 iterations each, reported as gold-normalized score GNS = 100·(s_i − s⁰_i)/(s^gold_i − s⁰_i), weighted 1/2 by complexity, not clipped at 100. KernelBench: a 50-problem subset (20 L1 / 20 L2 / 10 L3) built from 238 eligible problems via 28 computational strata, regularized Neyman allocation, 3,000 candidate subsets with fixed seed 20260919, and selection by KS distance on log reference runtime; 50 iterations per problem; metric is geometric-mean speedup over PyTorch Eager on a GH200. All configurations start from the same deliberately weak seed solution.

**Backbones.** gpt-oss-120b (open weights), gpt-6-luna, gpt-6-sol (proprietary API models).

**Integrity controls (KernelBench).** AST-based static inspection rejecting (i) no `__global__` kernel, (ii) delegating to high-level framework ops, (iii) patching timers/reference/eval code, (iv) in-place input mutation, (v) unsynchronized background streams/threads; plus reference-output retention to block uninitialized-memory reuse, restoration of numerical flags (TF32/matmul precision) before reference timing, and prompt-level integrity rules. Invalid candidates can never become the task's best attempt.

## Results & Highlights

- **Folding wins all six settings (Table 1).** On KernelBench with gpt-6-luna it raises geometric-mean speedup from 1.46× to 1.74× (+19.2%) and with gpt-oss-120b from 1.17× to 1.29× (+10.3%); on MLE-Bench GNS it moves gpt-oss-120b 70.5 → 76.1 (+7.9%) and gpt-6-sol 93.2 → 94.7 (+1.6%). Average across the six cells: +8.6%, the largest of any mechanism.
- **Compaction is a null result, and slightly harmful at the top end.** Mean relative gain +0.3%; on MLE-Bench with gpt-6-sol it *lowers* GNS from 93.2 to 91.6 (−1.7%) and on KernelBench with gpt-oss-120b it lowers speedup 1.17× → 1.16× (−0.9%), i.e. the "compress old history" default does not reliably pay for itself.
- **Deletion is the best skill policy at +4.1% average**, beating promotion (+3.0%) and merging (+2.2%): MLE-Bench gpt-oss-120b GNS 70.5 → 73.8 (+4.7%), KernelBench gpt-oss-120b 1.17× → 1.25× (+6.8%). Its lead is almost entirely on the weaker backbone — for gpt-6-sol, deletion is +1.0% on MLE-Bench and +4.6% on KernelBench.
- **Most of the skill gain comes from curation, not from having a library.** Against a No-Skill ablation, the unmanaged library yields only +2.1%; deletion adds 4.1% on top, promotion 3.0%, merging 2.2%. Library size at the end of an MLE-Bench/gpt-oss-120b run: 31 skills with deletion vs 312 unmanaged vs 330 with promotion.
- **Folding's advantage is mechanistic, not just "less context".** It keeps records of 42.0 past attempts on average (baseline: 6.2, in full) while using *fewer* tokens — 12.1k vs 18.4k — because each attempt is one digest line. Its new-best rate after the first 10% of iterations is 12.0% vs 10.2% for the baseline (+18%) and 9.7% for compaction (+24%), while all three are statistically indistinguishable early (35.4 / 35.3 / 36.3 for folding / baseline / compaction).
- **MLEvolver vs AIDE and OpenEvolve (gpt-6-sol, 100 iterations, 32K).** MLE-Bench Lite any-medal 72.7% (16/22) vs AIDE 59.1% and OpenEvolve 63.6% (+23.1% / +14.3% relative); gold medals 45.5% (10/22) vs 31.8% for both. KernelBench L3 fast1 94.0% (47/50) vs 78.0% / 70.0%; fast2 62.0% (31/50) vs 26.0% / 14.0%. Final GNS 98.1 vs 96.7 / 95.6; final geometric-mean speedup 2.99× vs 1.85× / 1.41×.
- **The lead is established early and then extended.** MLEvolver hits 97.4 GNS by iteration 20 — better than AIDE and OpenEvolve reach at iteration 100 — and 2.08× on KernelBench by iteration 10. Between iterations 50 and 100 it still gains 11.9% (2.67× → 2.99×) versus 0.8% for AIDE and 5.2% for OpenEvolve, evidence that folding keeps later refinement productive rather than saturating.
- **Diminishing but non-zero late-run returns across all designs:** averaged over the 6 configurations × 3 backbones, KernelBench speedup grows 65.2% in the first half of the run and a further 7.4% in the second; MLE-Bench GNS grows 41.6 points then 2.9 points.
- **Bigger windows still help.** Raising MLEvolver's window from 32K to 128K (gpt-6-luna) lifts MLE-Bench Lite any-medal 59.1% → 63.6%, gold 27.3% → 31.8%, KernelBench L3 fast1 72.0% → 78.0% and fast2 10.0% → 20.0% — i.e. folding does not saturate the benefit of more context.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The individual mechanisms are not new — per-attempt digests are essentially MemGPT-style paging with agent-controlled expansion, and skill pruning has been proposed before (MemoryBank's forgetting, Xiong et al.'s empirical memory study). The contribution is the *controlled comparison*: five mechanisms, one harness, three backbones, two benchmarks, one component changed at a time. That is genuinely useful and rarer than it should be, since most prior work compares whole systems where search policy, prompt structure and tool use co-vary. The paper says this explicitly and then actually does it for §4, which is the strongest part.

**Rigor — the missing variance is the main problem.** Every cell in Table 1 is a single number per (configuration, backbone, benchmark), and no seeds, confidence intervals, or repeated runs are reported. With 100 iterations per competition the run itself is long, but the *outer* variance across seeds/initializations is unmeasured. This matters most exactly where the headline is weakest: gpt-6-sol folding on MLE-Bench is 94.7 vs 93.2 (+1.6%), and promotion on gpt-6-sol is 94.3 (+1.2%) — differences of ~1.5 GNS points over 10 competitions with no repeated sampling. The "folding wins all six settings" claim leans on those narrow margins for 2 of the 6 cells, and AIDE-vs-MLEvolver MLE-Bench Lite GNS (98.1 vs 96.7) is a 1.4% gap that could plausibly be within run-to-run noise. The ranking is much safer for the KernelBench cells (1.46 → 1.74 is large) and for gpt-oss-120b.

**Fairness of the MLEvolver comparison.** The §4 ablation is well controlled; §6 is not. MLEvolver is a different loop (single linear refinement chain, explicit action selector, skill picker, skill writer, *plus* a digest summarizer call every iteration) compared against AIDE's tree search and OpenEvolve's island population. "Under the same inference budget" means the same 100-iteration cap, not the same token or model-call budget — MLEvolver plausibly issues 3–5 LLM calls per iteration (action selector + skill picker + coder + skill writer + folding summarizer), while AIDE's per-step cost is lower. No token counts, wall-clock, or dollar cost are reported anywhere, so the claimed +22%/+24% average relative gain cannot be attributed to memory design rather than to simply spending more inference. This is the single biggest red flag in the paper and it is not discussed.

**Hyperparameters are asserted, not tuned.** τ_d = 5 for deletion, θ = 0.8 for merging, digest length "2–4 sentences", the 32K budget, and 100 iterations are all fixed without a sweep or sensitivity analysis. Deletion's entire mechanism hinges on τ_d = 5: at τ_d = 1 it would be near-random eviction; at τ_d = 50 it would never fire. The paper's cleanest claim (+4.1%) rests on an untested constant. Likewise folding's advantage partly depends on digest *quality*, which is produced by a prompt (Fig. 14) that was not ablated against, e.g., a raw truncated tail of the same token cost — the strongest possible control for "is it the digest or just the extra 6k tokens of coverage?" is missing.

**Benchmark coverage and selection.** MLE-Bench has 67 competitions; only 10 are used, and only 4 of them are medium-complexity, with competition-level weights chosen by hand (w = 2 for medium). KernelBench is evaluated on a custom 50-of-238 subset whose strata weights come from *pilot replicates* of the very configurations being compared — so the subset is, in a mild sense, tuned to the effect being measured. The authors document this carefully (Neyman allocation, fixed seed, KS-matching on reference runtime) and exclude 12 problems for defensible reasons (degenerate outputs, algebraically reducible computations, missing FP32 reference timing), which is better practice than most. But the numbers are not directly comparable to published KernelBench results on the full 250-problem suite.

**Protocol deviation.** KernelBench speedup is computed from *median* rather than mean execution time, explicitly departing from Ouyang et al. because the long-horizon setting violated the <3% coefficient-of-variation assumption. This is honest and probably correct, but it means all reported speedups (1.17×–2.99×) are on a different scale from prior KernelBench numbers — and faster kernels benefit from median timing when there are driver-allocation spikes, so the deviation is not neutral.

**Measurement of the explanatory variables is crude.** The "offensive vs defensive" skill classification is a 7-rule keyword cascade applied to lower-cased skill *titles* (Appendix E.1), with an LLM-free fallback to body word counts. The headline "53.1% offensive for gpt-oss-120b vs 25.4% for gpt-6-sol" is therefore a lexical proxy, not a semantic annotation, and the per-benchmark split is dramatic (Table 5: KernelBench 71.6% vs 36.1% for the same pair). The causal story — "proprietary models retrieve fewer and more defensive skills, hence gain less from management" — is a correlation across three models with n = 3, presented without alternative explanations (e.g. gpt-oss-120b also just produces noisier, more redundant skills, so there is more for deletion to clean up).

**Reproducibility.** Two of the three backbones are proprietary API models cited only via OpenAI documentation pages accessed 2026-10-07 and 2026-11-10, so the central results cannot be re-run or audited by anyone outside the lab, and the model versions will drift. The open-weight cell (gpt-oss-120b) is reproducible in principle; the harness itself is not released as far as the paper indicates. The KernelBench anti-cheat code and the MLE-Bench competition selection are described in enough detail to be reimplemented, which partially compensates.

**Significance.** High for practitioners building long-horizon agent harnesses: "keep a per-attempt digest you can unfold" is a cheap, transferable design rule, and the negative compaction result is a useful corrective to the reflexive summarize-old-context default. Lower as a scientific claim about *why*, because the mechanism-level explanation (new-best rate, retrieval counts) is measured coarsely and the causal chain from digging into "more addressable history" to "better kernels" is asserted via correlation.

## Strengths

- Clean, genuinely controlled isolation — one component changed at a time inside one harness, with the same prompts, tools, action space and seed solution. This is the design most memory papers skip.
- 3 backbones × 2 benchmarks = 6 cells per mechanism, and the paper reports all of them including the ones that weaken its story (compaction −1.7% on gpt-6-sol; folding only +1.6% there; deletion only +1.0% on MLE-Bench for gpt-6-sol).
- A crisp, actionable mechanism-level explanation for folding: 42 attempts retained vs 6.2, using *fewer* context tokens (12.1k vs 18.4k), with a higher late-run new-best rate. The trace in Fig. 3a is what makes the result believable rather than a table of deltas.
- The No-Skill ablation cleanly separates "having a skill library" (+2.1%) from "curating it" (deletion +4.1%), which most skill-library papers conflate.
- Unusually thorough KernelBench methodology: documented 28-stratum sampling frame, seeded subset search, explicit exclusions with worked examples of degenerate problems (p80 collapsing to all-zeros), median timing justified by measured CV violation, and a five-part AST anti-cheat gate plus reference-buffer isolation.
- Concrete qualitative case studies (Appendix E.3) showing *why* merging and promotion underperform: a merged skill loses the exact missing-parenthesis fix and the same syntax error recurs at iteration 79; a promoted LightGBM rule stays in the prompt after the agent has switched to a PyTorch network. These are the kind of failure traces that make a negative result convincing.
- Honest limitations section that names parallel search, interactive coding agents (Claude Code / Codex), and cross-task skill transfer as untested.

## Limitations

- **No variance reporting.** One run per cell, no seeds or error bars, so the +1.0% to +1.6% cells (gpt-6-sol, and all of promotion/merging) cannot be distinguished from noise.
- **Inference budget is measured in iterations, not tokens or cost.** MLEvolver adds an action-selector call, a skill-picker call, a skill-writer call and a digest-summarizer call per iteration; AIDE and OpenEvolve do not. The §6 win may partly be bought rather than designed. No token/wall-clock/dollar accounting anywhere.
- **Hyperparameters fixed without sensitivity analysis:** τ_d = 5, θ = 0.8, digest length, 32K window. Deletion's result depends on τ_d more than the paper admits.
- **No control for "digest vs equal-token raw tail".** The paper attributes folding's gain to per-attempt addressability, but the baseline is given 18.4k tokens of raw history while folding is given 12.1k — the cleaner comparison would be a raw-history baseline held to the same token budget with more, shorter retained attempts.
- **Only 10/67 MLE-Bench competitions**, 4 of them medium; hand-set complexity weights; a KernelBench subset tuned on pilot replicates of the same configurations, so absolute numbers do not transfer to the full suite.
- **Median-timing deviation from the KernelBench protocol** changes the scale of every reported speedup, and the direction of the bias is not analyzed.
- **Two of three backbones are proprietary and version-pinned to API docs**, so the majority of the table is not reproducible or auditable, and will not be re-runnable.
- **Offensive/defensive skill labels come from a 7-rule title keyword cascade**, not annotation; the cross-model causal story rests on n = 3 models and an unvalidated proxy.
- Linear single-chain refinement only — no parallel search, tree search, or population methods inside the studied harness, so the conclusions may not transfer to exactly the harnesses (AIDE, AlphaEvolve) it benchmarks against.
- Each task's skill library is reset, so nothing is learned about cross-task or cross-domain skill transfer, which is where skill libraries would matter most in practice.

## Relevance

本文属于 **Self-Evolving Agents & Automated Research**：它系统拆解了长时程自演化 agent 的 harness 记忆设计，直接回答"history 该怎么留、经验该怎么剪"这两个自改进回路的核心工程问题，结论（per-attempt 可展开摘要 > 滚动摘要；删除 > 合并/升格）可直接迁移到任何长跑 agent。其次与 **Agent & Pipeline Silent Failures** 相关：recency truncation 会静默丢弃最早期的实验记录，而 compaction 会在无提示的情况下逐轮稀释每次实验的细节，作者用 new-best rate 变化把这种"静默信息损失"的后果量化了出来（后期 12.0% vs 9.7%），是 harness 层静默失真的一个很好的实例。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. What is the run-to-run variance of GNS on these 10 MLE-Bench competitions? With ≥3 seeds per cell, do the gpt-6-sol folding (+1.6%) and promotion (+1.2%) cells survive?
2. How do MLEvolver, AIDE and OpenEvolve compare when the budget is equalized in *tokens* or *model calls* rather than iterations? What fraction of the +22%/+24% survives?
3. Is folding's gain from addressability or from coverage? A raw-history baseline capped at the same 12.1k tokens (retaining ~10 short attempts instead of 6 long ones) would separate these.
4. How sensitive is deletion to τ_d (1, 3, 5, 10, 20) and does the optimum track the model's retrieval volume (6.4–7.1 skills/prompt for gpt-oss-120b vs 0.5–0.6 for gpt-6-sol)?
5. Does the digest summarizer's own error rate matter — i.e. would a deliberately lossy or adversarial digest destroy folding's advantage? Is there a "digest faithfulness" metric worth reporting?
6. Does folding + deletion still win on a genuinely parallel harness (tree search with N concurrent branches), where the context budget must be split across trajectories rather than spent on one long chain?
7. What happens with cross-task skill libraries — does deletion's "unused for 5 iterations" signal remain valid when the task distribution shifts, or does it prune exactly the rare-but-decisive skills?
8. Could the offensive/defensive split be re-measured with human or LLM annotation on a sample, to check whether the keyword cascade's 53.1% vs 25.4% gap is an artifact of title phrasing style?
