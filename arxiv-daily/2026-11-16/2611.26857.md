> **一句话总结：** 作者发现 4-bit KV 缓存掉分的根因是键向量里一个占 61%–73% 能量的"位置载波"（RoPE 旋转的每 KV head 均值向量）把 NVFP4 的共享 block scale 撑大、把内容残差的量化信噪比压低约 5 dB；于是他们在 RoPE 之前一次性减掉这个常量、只存内容残差，并用一个 28-bit 相位累加器在片上重建载波的打分项，在同样的 4.5 bit/元素缓存下把 Qwen3-4B 的 32K RULER 从 79.4 拉回 91.1（BF16 92.6），32K PG19 困惑度增幅从 +0.917 降到 +0.199，代价是 22 nm FDSOI 下面积 +2.9%、每 token 能耗 +0.12%（DRAM 占 96%）；但 Llama-3.2-3B 上增益明显缩水甚至反转（3.5-bit 版本 1.35 差于 4.5-bit NVFP4 的 0.667）。

> **TL;DR:** On four models (Qwen3-4B/8B, Llama-3.2-3B, Gemma-3-4B) the paper shows that the dominant energy component of cached keys is a per-KV-head constant that RoPE merely rotates, so subtracting it *before* RoPE and regenerating its score term on-chip (a 28-bit phase accumulator per RoPE pair) lets plain NVFP4 keys recover most of the accuracy: 32K RULER on Qwen3-4B goes from 79.4 (NVFP4) to 91.1 versus 92.6 BF16, beating SageAttention (86.9) and a P3-LLM-style baseline (75.1), and 32K PG19 PPL increase drops from +0.917 to +0.199 — at a 2.9% area and 0.12% energy cost over the baseline NVFP4 accelerator (22 nm FDSOI, 600 MHz, model-based energy). The win is model-dependent: on Llama-3.2-3B the PPL increase only falls 0.667 → 0.363, and at 3.5 bits/key CarrierKV (1.35) is *worse* than 4.5-bit NVFP4 (0.667).

## 核心贡献

- **把低比特 KV 的掉分归因到一个具体可量化的结构上**：把 RoPE 前的 key 写成 `k(s) = c + r(s)`，`c` 为该 KV head 的均值 key。在 PG19 上，`c` 及其旋转部分占 key 总能量的 61%–73%（Qwen3-4B 上旋转部分从 4K 的 13% 涨到 32K 的 22%），它决定了 NVFP4 大多数 block 的 E4M3 共享 scale，使内容残差的信号量化噪声比从 20.4 dB 掉到 14.5–15.7 dB（约 0.8–1.0 bit）。
- **提出 positional carrier decomposition**：在 prefill 阶段用最多 512 个 prompt token（跳过作为 attention sink 的第 0 个）估计 `c`，之后写入路径先减 `c` 再做 RoPE 与 Hadamard，只缓存残差 `r(s)`；因为 RoPE 是线性的，`R(s)c` 在整个上下文上被精确消除（对任意固定 `c`，式 (1) 是精确分解，`c` 只影响残差大小）。元数据开销仅 0.0002 bit/元素（对比 NSNQuant 的每 chunk 均值 0.23 bit/元素）。
- **decompose-then-rotate 读取路径**：每步只把 query 旋转一次（`q̂ = Hq`），注意力分数直接在 4-bit 码上算内容项 `q̂ᵀk̂(s)`，不需要把任何缓存 key 解码回高精度；载波项 `Σⱼ(aⱼcos(sθⱼ) − bⱼsin(sθⱼ))` 由 `q` 与 `c` 的内积/叉积系数（每步每 query head 算一次）配合位置相位生成。
- **positional phase engine 硬件**：每对 RoPE 频率一个 28-bit 相位累加器（以"圈"计数，每读一个 key 只做加法），高 2 bit 选象限、次 8 bit 查 256 项 8-bit 四分之一周期 cos 表，一套 64 组 cos/sin 服务同一 KV head 的所有 G 个 query head；载波 MAC 阵列（G×64×2 个 4×8 乘法器）每周期处理一个 key，因此载波项不增加每 key 延迟。
- **软硬件联合验证**：四个模型三个家族 × RULER/LongBench/PG19 + 思考模式推理基准，并证明该分解与数制无关（也可套在 3-bit TurboQuant 上）；硬件在 22 nm FDSOI、600 MHz、0.8 V 下面积 4.945 → 5.087 mm²（+2.9%），20K token 推理请求能耗 86.595 → 86.700 mJ/token（+0.12%）。

## Key Contributions

- **A mechanistic, quantified diagnosis of 4-bit KV-cache loss.** The cached key decomposes as `R(s)c + R(s)r(s)`, where `c` is the per-KV-head mean key; RoPE only rotates it. `c` plus its rotating part accounts for 61%–73% of key energy, and because NVFP4's E4M3 block scale follows the largest element, the carrier coarsens the quantization grid of 16 elements by 1.7–1.9× in 57%–72% of blocks and costs the content ~5 dB SQNR (≈1 bit).
- **Positional carrier decomposition (algorithm).** Estimate `c` once during prefill (mean over ≤512 prompt tokens, skipping token 0), subtract it before RoPE, then apply the Hadamard rotation and quantize only the residual. Verified exact: for any fixed `c`, Eq. (1) splits the score into a content term on the 4-bit code and a carrier term computable from `(q, c, s)`; `c` only sets how small the residual — and hence its quantization error — is.
- **Decompose-then-rotate KV path.** Rotate the query once per decode step instead of rotating every cached key on read (the cost paid by pre-RoPE caches such as KVQuant), and compute the content term directly on stored 4-bit codes with per-block scale application, so no key is ever decoded to high precision.
- **Positional phase engine (hardware).** A numerically-controlled-oscillator-style unit: 28-bit phase accumulators advanced by `θⱼ` per key (adds only), quadrant bits plus a 256×8-bit quarter-turn cosine LUT generating cos/sin for all G query heads of a KV head, feeding a 4b×8b carrier MAC array that produces the carrier term for one key per cycle. Total overhead over a baseline NVFP4 edge accelerator: +2.9% area, +0.12% energy on a 20K-token reasoning request.
- **Empirical claim of beating FP8 keys at lower cost.** At a comparable 32K PPL increase, CarrierKV uses 13.7% less energy per token and is 1.16× faster than FP8 keys at 32K, and 22.2% less energy / 1.28× faster at 128K.

## Methodology

The paper is a four-part stack: an empirical observation study, a linear-algebraic decomposition, a dataflow redesign, and a hardware block.

**Observation study (Sec. II).** Keys are written before RoPE as `k(s) = c + r(s)`, `c = mean_s k(s)`. PG19 key-energy decomposition into residual / static carrier / rotating carrier is measured on four models at 4K and 32K. Three observations drive the design: (1) the carrier dominates key energy and keys (not values) cause the loss — with NVFP4 values, NVFP4 keys give a 5–6× larger 32K PPL increase than FP8 keys; (2) post-RoPE centering and Hadamard rotation fail because the carrier rotates and because the rotation spreads a dominant component across blocks (on Qwen3-4B Hadamard alone pushes 32K PPL increase from 0.917 to 3.50 and RULER from 79.4 to 57.7); (3) the carrier term is a *useful* positional bias the model relies on — across 1152 Qwen3-4B query heads it varies with distance by a median 2.8× the spread of the content term, and 95% of heads favor keys 1–8 tokens away by >0.5 in score — so removing it is only safe if the read path regenerates it, including the slowest pairs (dropping the 9 slowest pairs without regeneration raises the 32K PPL increase from 0.199 to 0.456).

**Decomposition and score split.** Since RoPE is linear, `qᵀR(s)k(s) = qᵀR(s)r(s) + qᵀR(s)c`. With `HᵀH = I`, the content term equals `q̂ᵀk̂(s)` for `q̂ = Hq`, `k̂(s) = HR(s)r(s)`. Expanding pair `j` of the carrier term gives `aⱼcos(sθⱼ) − bⱼsin(sθⱼ)` with `aⱼ = qⱼ,₁cⱼ,₁ + qⱼ,₂cⱼ,₂` and `bⱼ = qⱼ,₁cⱼ,₂ − qⱼ,₂cⱼ,₁`, i.e. one dot and one cross product per pair per query head per step.

**Hardware (Sec. IV).** 32×32 array of NVFP4 block MACs as 16×16 PEs of 2×2 MACs, W4A4; FP4×8-bit mode splits an 8-bit operand into two 4-bit digits for two MACs plus shift-add, used for QKᵀ with 8-bit queries. Attention engine streams one key and one value (128 elements) per cycle, more than DRAM delivers, so decode stays DRAM-bandwidth bound; 2.1 MB SRAM; LPDDR5-6400 x64. Prefill attention is done as a GEMM with 16-row softmax and 8-bit requantized P/values. Three additions: an accumulator in the KV encoder for `c` (8-bit mantissa, one power-of-two exponent per KV head, prefetched once per layer, ≈36 KB per decode step over 36 layers × 8 KV heads ≈ 0.003% of the 32K KV traffic), the decompose-then-rotate path (RoPE with 32-bit phases, 128-point FWHT after random sign flips, NVFP4 quantizer), and the phase engine + carrier unit.

**Evaluation setup.** Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B, Llama-3.2-3B, Gemma-3-4B; instruct models on downstream tasks, base models for PPL (Llama/Gemma). All linear layers W4A4 NVFP4, queries FP8, values NVFP4; only the keys differ. Accuracy is simulated in software on four H100s, with the number formats of the accelerator emulated. RULER uses 7 KV-sensitive subtasks of 13 at 32K, 100 samples each; LongBench v1 all 21 tasks; PG19 non-overlapping 4K/32K windows (2383 and 255 for Qwen3). Baselines: NVFP4, centering (mean of up to 512 prompt tokens after RoPE — the paper's stand-in for SageAttention), and a P3-LLM-style per-channel scaling with INT4 groups of 128 after RoPE. Hardware area/energy come from a 22 nm FDSOI implementation at 600 MHz/0.8 V with gate-level power measurement on Qwen3-4B data and memory-compiler SRAM macros; latency/energy use a performance model (SCALE-Sim v3 for the GEMM core + Ramulator 2.1 for LPDDR5, datasheet currents for DRAM energy).

## Results & Highlights

- **32K RULER average, Qwen3-4B:** CarrierKV 91.1 vs NVFP4 79.4 (+11.7 pts) and BF16 92.6, i.e. it recovers ~86% of the loss; the same table gives SageAttention 86.9 and the P3-LLM-style baseline 75.1. The gain concentrates on the subtask NVFP4 breaks: MK3 goes 43 → 79 (BF16 87) and MK2 58 → 85 (BF16 82, i.e. above BF16 on that subtask).
- **32K RULER on the other three models:** Qwen3-8B 95.1 → 97.4 (BF16 98.2, Sage 95.6, P3 94.8); Llama-3.2-3B 63.8 → 70.6 (BF16 72.7, Sage 60.4, P3 67.4); Gemma-3-4B 49.2 → 50.3 (BF16 51.2, P3 49.9). CarrierKV has the best 32K RULER average on all four models, but the Llama margin over P3-LLM is +3.2 and the Gemma margin over NVFP4 is only +1.1 pts on 100-sample estimates.
- **32K PG19 PPL increase (same 4.5 bit/element cache):** Qwen3-4B 0.917 → 0.199 (4.6× lower), Qwen3-8B 0.503 → 0.086 (5.9×), Llama-3.2-3B 0.667 → 0.363 (1.8×), Gemma-3-4B 0.363 → 0.102 (3.6×) — matching the abstract's "1.8 to 5.9 times". Ablation (Table II) separates the two ingredients: decomposition alone gives 0.204 / 0.103 / 0.564 / 0.119, and adding the post-decomposition Hadamard helps most on Llama (0.564 → 0.363).
- **Format independence, 3-bit TurboQuant keys:** Qwen3-4B 32K PPL increase 69.7 (untreated) → 2.94 (centering) → 0.762 (decomposition); Qwen3-8B 20.1 → 0.329; Gemma-3-4B 1.21 → 0.335. The exception is Llama-3.2-3B, where decomposition is *worse* than untreated (1.93 vs 1.89) and centering worse still (2.15).
- **Loss growth with cache length:** on Qwen3-4B the NVFP4 increase rises from 0.80 (first 4K) to 1.07 (last 4K) and FP8 from 0.10 to 0.27, while CarrierKV stays in 0.17–0.24 and beats FP8 in each of the last four segments (0.19 vs 0.27). On Llama-3.2-3B 64K windows, per-8K increase goes 0.58 → 1.07 with NVFP4 vs 0.36 → 0.41 with CarrierKV (FP8 still lower, 0.10 → 0.24).
- **Hardware cost (Qwen3-4B, 22 nm FDSOI, 600 MHz, 0.8 V, 1K prompt + 20K generated):** area 4.945 → 5.087 mm² (+2.9%, of which carrier unit 0.096 mm² and the score-unit delta 0.182 mm²); energy 86.595 → 86.700 mJ/token (+0.12%), with LPDDR5 DRAM alone at 83.071 mJ/token (96%), so the accelerator-side additions are effectively hidden behind memory. At 8K/20K/32K generated tokens the overhead is +0.06%/+0.12%/+0.17% vs +3.8%/+7.5%/+10.6% for FP8 keys.
- **Against FP8 keys at matched accuracy:** 32K RULER 91.1 vs 89.3 and 13.7% less energy per token, 1.16× faster; at 128K, 1.28× faster with 22.2% less energy. Against a pre-RoPE cache (KVQuant-style), the paper estimates ≥166 pJ/key for the BF16 dot-product plus FP8 query/RoPE path vs 32 pJ for CarrierKV's score unit (5.2×).
- **Thinking-mode reasoning (Qwen3-8B, 8/8/3/3 runs):** average 52.6 vs NVFP4 51.1 and SageAttention 51.1 (BF16 54.6); AIME24+25 60.8±3.2 vs 58.5±3.3 (BF16 65.2), HMMT25 32.9, LCB 43.0 vs 41.5, GPQA 52.9 vs 51.3, OlympiadBench 73.4. Leads or ties on every benchmark, but several gaps are within one std.
- **LongBench v1 average:** Qwen3-4B 42.53 vs NVFP4 42.11 and BF16 43.64; Qwen3-8B 46.30 vs 46.12; Llama 39.08 vs 38.89; Gemma 37.42 vs 36.80 — a consistent but small ordering that mostly tracks the SDoc/MDoc QA categories.

## Authors & Credibility

Junyi Luo, Xinting Jiang, Ruichen Qi, Quan Cheng and Mehdi Saligane are at Brown University; Gregory Kielian is at Google Research. Saligane leads the Brown analog/mixed-signal and open-source silicon group (OpenFASOC/GlobalFoundries University Partnership lineage), which matches the paper's 22 nm FDSOI tape-out-style methodology and its acknowledgment of GlobalFoundries technology access and Google Cloud compute. The work sits in the IEEE-style accelerator-paper genre (index terms, table-heavy evaluation), and the reference list is current through 2026 (P3-LLM at ISCA 2026, Ramulator 2.1 at ICS 2026, NSNQuant at NeurIPS 2025). Credibility signals: the group reports the model where its method *fails* (Llama-3.2-3B) in the main table rather than burying it; it publishes the ablation that isolates each ingredient; and it separates simulated accuracy from modeled hardware cost rather than claiming silicon measurements. The main credibility gap is that all accuracy numbers are emulated in software and all energy numbers come from a performance model plus gate-level power, not from a fabricated chip.

## Critical Assessment

**Genuinely good.** The central observation is a clean, falsifiable structural claim with three independent supports: the energy decomposition (61%–73% of key energy in the carrier), the SQNR measurement (20.4 dB with decomposition vs 14.5–15.7 dB with the carrier kept, i.e. 0.8–1.0 bits at ~6 dB/bit), and the causal test that stripping the 9 slowest pairs without regeneration degrades the 32K PPL increase from 0.199 to 0.456. The decomposition itself is exact for any fixed `c` (Eq. 1), so the method is not an approximation heuristic dressed up as math; what `c` controls is only how small — and therefore how well quantized — the residual is. The engineering claim that matters is the metadata one: 0.0002 bits/key element at 32K versus NSNQuant's 0.23 bits plus an FP16 recent-key window, three orders of magnitude, and the 36 KB/c-step `c` prefetch is 0.003% of the 32K KV traffic. That is the property that makes this deployable where per-chunk means are not.

**Where the evidence is thin or the framing is generous.**

1. **The Llama-3.2-3B result undercuts the headline "1.8 to 5.9×" range.** On Llama the improvement is 0.667 → 0.363, which the range quietly absorbs, and two other Llama results are outright negative: at 3.5 bits/key CarrierKV's 1.35 is worse than 4.5-bit NVFP4's 0.667 (the only model where the bit savings do not pay), and under 3-bit TurboQuant the decomposition *raises* the PPL increase relative to untreated keys (1.93 vs 1.89) while centering is worse still (2.15). The paper states all three, but never explains them — there is no per-head carrier-energy measurement for Llama in the main text, so a reader cannot tell whether Llama's carrier is weak (small `c`, so decomposition has little to remove) or whether its carrier is *not* aligned the way Qwen3's is. Given that the whole method rests on the carrier being dominant, the missing Llama diagnostic is the single most important absent experiment.
2. **The Hadamard result on Qwen3 is anomalous and deserves more stress-testing.** Standard practice (QuaRot, SpinQuant) treats a randomized Hadamard rotation as a reliable win for 4-bit formats; here it *hurts* Qwen3 badly — 32K PPL increase 0.917 → 3.50, RULER 79.4 → 57.7. The paper's explanation (the carrier dominates in early layers and H smears it across all blocks) is plausible and is supported by the opposite sign on Llama (0.667 → 0.451) and Gemma (0.363 → 0.437). But this is a single rotation seed on a single 128-dim head layout, with no sweep over random sign flips, block size, or rotation placement, so "Hadamard after decomposition is the right order" rests on one configuration per model.
3. **The baselines are re-implementations, not the published systems.** The SageAttention column is "centering with the mean of up to 512 prompt tokens after RoPE" — a 4-bit key-treatment proxy for a method whose published form is 8-bit attention. The P3-LLM column explicitly uses P3-LLM's *Llama-3* channel-scaling recipe rather than its pre-RoPE variant for these newer models, which plausibly explains its 75.1 RULER on Qwen3-4B (below plain NVFP4's 79.4 on the same table — a baseline scoring under an untuned 4-bit format is a weak opponent). Meanwhile NSNQuant, SpectrumQuant, KVQuant, Oaken and Dual-QK appear only in Table I's qualitative comparison, never as measured baselines, even though NSNQuant is the closest point in the design space (per-chunk means) and would directly test the metadata-efficiency argument. The 5.2× pJ/key comparison against a pre-RoPE cache is also paper-side arithmetic, not a measured implementation.
4. **Everything is simulated; the hardware claim is a model.** Accuracy is emulated number formats on H100s, which is standard for this genre but means no interaction with real FP4 tensor-core rounding, accumulation order, or attention-kernel fusion. Area and energy come from a 22 nm FDSOI implementation with gate-level power on Qwen3-4B data and a SCALE-Sim/Ramulator model for latency and DRAM energy. The +0.12% energy claim should be read with that in mind: DRAM is 96% of the 86.7 mJ/token, so the metric is nearly insensitive to anything the accelerator does — a 2.9% area tax buys accuracy, and the energy number mostly says "DRAM dominates", which was already known. The latency claim ("no latency per key") depends on the carrier MAC keeping pace with a DRAM-bound stream, which is a sound argument at 600 MHz but is not validated against a real memory controller's timing.
5. **Scope limits worth naming.** Batch size is one throughout — the paper is explicitly an edge single-stream design, so the 4.5-bit cache and the `c` prefetch economics say nothing about batched serving. The carrier is fixed after prefill, and although the paper shows 16 prompt tokens cost at most +0.10 PPL, there is no experiment on carrier *drift* during very long generation (the 64K test is PPL only, on Llama). The reasoning evaluation is Qwen3-8B only, and the largest reported deltas (AIME +2.3, GPQA +1.6) sit within one standard deviation of the 3–8 run samples. Gemma-3-4B's BF16 RULER is already 51.2, so a +1.1-pt 4-bit improvement is close to the noise floor of 100-sample RULER.
6. **The 3.5-bit result is the most interesting unexploited finding.** Table II's last column shows CarrierKV at 3.5 bits/key (INT3 elements under NVFP4 scales) beating 4.5-bit NVFP4 on three of four models (0.766 vs 0.917; 0.346 vs 0.503; 0.333 vs 0.363) and losing only on Llama (1.35 vs 0.667). That is a 22% cache reduction for free on the models that matter most, yet the paper does not pursue it — no 3-bit RULER, LongBench or reasoning numbers, and no explanation of why the E2M1 grid can be replaced by sign-magnitude INT3 here. It reads as a promising direction left on the table.

## Strengths

- The diagnosis is mechanistic and quantitative, not a bag of tricks: energy share, block-scale coarsening factor (1.7–1.9×), SQNR loss in dB and bits, and a causal ablation that removes the carrier's regeneration to show the model actually uses the term.
- The decomposition is exact rather than approximate, so the method has no accuracy ceiling of its own; `c` only changes how compressible the residual is. This is a much stronger footing than learned or heuristic centering.
- Metadata cost is genuinely negligible (0.0002 bits/element at 32K, 0.003% of KV traffic for the `c` prefetch), which is the practical reason this could ship where per-chunk-mean schemes cannot.
- It keeps the NVFP4 format and footprint, so it composes with an existing 4-bit accelerator instead of requiring a new number format — and the paper demonstrates composition with 3-bit TurboQuant, arguing convincingly that the idea is format-independent.
- Hardware overhead is small and honestly attributed: +2.9% area, +0.12% energy, no per-key latency, with a per-block accounting (carrier unit 0.096 mm², score-unit delta 0.182 mm²).
- Evaluation breadth is above the norm for an accelerator paper: four models from three families, seven RULER subtasks plus all 21 LongBench tasks plus 4K/32K/64K PG19, thinking-mode reasoning on five benchmarks, two number formats, and a length-resolved loss curve that shows the method's advantage *growing* with context while NVFP4's does.
- Negative results are reported in the main tables rather than in an appendix, which is the right behavior and rare enough to be worth crediting.

## Limitations

- **Model dependence is unexplained.** Llama-3.2-3B gains little, loses at 3.5 bits, and regresses under 3-bit TurboQuant; no per-model carrier statistics are given to predict in advance which models will benefit.
- **Static carrier.** `c` is frozen after prefill from ≤512 prompt tokens; there is no test of carrier drift under long generation, multi-turn context switching, or domain shift, and no adaptive or periodic re-estimation scheme.
- **Re-implemented baselines.** The two 4-bit baselines are proxies (post-RoPE centering for SageAttention, the Llama-3 channel-scaling recipe for P3-LLM), and the closest published competitors (NSNQuant, SpectrumQuant) are never run.
- **No silicon.** Accuracy is software-emulated; area and energy are a model plus gate-level power. The energy headline is dominated by DRAM (96%), so it mostly measures the memory system.
- **Single-stream only.** Batch size 1, 600 MHz, edge power envelope; nothing is said about batched serving, prefill-heavy workloads (where the carrier is estimated), or multi-tenant KV reuse.
- **No end-to-end system comparison.** No vLLM/llama.cpp integration, no wall-clock decode throughput on a real device, and no comparison against simply using FP8 keys at 8 bits — which the paper itself shows is competitive on PPL (0.173 vs 0.199) and only loses on energy per token.
- **The 3.5-bit configuration is underexplored**, and 2-bit keys remain lossy everywhere (PPL increases of 1.3 to 12.6).
- **Statistical strength is modest** on the small-sample benchmarks (100 RULER samples; 3–8 reasoning runs), and several reported margins are inside one std.

## Relevance

直接命中"Reliability under Extreme Performance Optimization"：它把 4-bit KV 缓存这一典型激进推理优化的掉分原因（block scale 被位置载波撑大、内容残差只剩几位量化格）定位到可测量的机制上，并给出几乎零元数据开销的缓解方案，同时诚实报告了 Llama-3.2-3B 上失效的边界。对关注低精度/长上下文数值退化与加速器协同设计的工作，这篇的"能量分解 + SQNR 归因 + 因果消融"三段式论证也值得作为方法论模板。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. What are the per-head carrier energy shares for Llama-3.2-3B and Gemma-3-4B? Is the weak Llama gain predicted by a lower carrier share, a different carrier direction across layers, or a mismatch between the prefill-estimated `c` and the keys seen during long generation?
2. How much does `c` drift between the 512-token prompt estimate and the keys generated 32K tokens later, and does periodic re-estimation (cheap, at 36 KB per layer) recover the Llama case and the 3-bit TurboQuant regression?
3. Why does the randomized Hadamard rotation *hurt* Qwen3 first layers so much (0.917 → 3.50) when QuaRot/SpinQuant report gains? Is this a property of Qwen3's massive-activation channels, and how sensitive is the result to the random sign seed, block size, and whether H is applied before or after the residual subtraction?
4. Can the 3.5-bit configuration (INT3 under NVFP4 scales) be made to work on Llama, and what does its RULER/LongBench/reasoning accuracy look like on the three models where it beats 4.5-bit NVFP4? A 22% cache reduction is worth more than the accuracy win alone.
5. Is the carrier also present — and removable — in value vectors, in the MLP key/value caches of MoE models, or in GQA settings with G ≫ 4 query heads, where the phase engine's "one cos/sin set serves all G heads" assumption gets more attractive?
6. What happens at batch > 1, where the per-sequence `c` and the phase accumulators must be replicated per request, and does the 0.0002 bits/element metadata argument survive?
7. How does CarrierKV interact with real FP4 tensor cores (accumulation order, fused softmax) rather than an emulated number format — does the 20.4 dB SQNR hold end-to-end, and does the ordering of the carrier adder versus the online softmax (Stevens et al.) introduce measurable error?
