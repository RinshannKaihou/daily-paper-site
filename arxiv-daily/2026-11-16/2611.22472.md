> **一句话总结：** 作者发现"用全图作答的小 VLM 与大 VLM 剪枝后答案不一致"是比大模型自身置信度更好的剪枝失败信号，据此提出 PRUDENT：先按 K/2 预算答一遍，把省下的 token 用于对不一致的题目以 2K 预算重答（配额 1/4），在平均视觉 token 预算不变的前提下，于 InternVL3-38B 的 2.5% 预算上把 18 个基准的平均得分从 IF-Prune 的 83.4% 提到 88.4%（全 token 为 100%）。

> **TL;DR:** PRUDENT treats visual-token pruning as a per-question trust-allocation problem rather than a better token ranking: it answers every question at half the budget, has a 2B InternVL3 checker read the full image, and re-answers only the questions where the checker disagrees with the pruned 38B answer, at 2× the budget, for a quarter of the questions. At the same average visual-token budget it reaches 88.4% of the full-token score on 18 benchmarks vs 83.4% for IF-Prune and 79.1% for SGL (2.5% budget, InternVL3-38B), with the lead growing to +8.8 points at a 1% budget, at the cost of 421 ms vs 383 ms per question.

## 核心贡献

- **诊断了"单遍剪枝"的结构性盲点**：现有方法把 token 排名做好之后就按同一预算回答每个问题并直接采纳答案；而失败集中在少数依赖小细节（一个词、一个数字）的题目上。基于已保留 token 的检测器（包括大模型自身置信度）看到的正是被剪掉证据之后的世界，因此天然无法识别这些失败。
- **提出 PRUDENT（PRUning with DissENT）**：用一个读全图的小 VLM 作为 checker，把"checker 答案 ≠ 剪枝后答案"当作剪枝失败信号；先以 K/2 预算作答，再对不一致且置信度最低的题目以 2K 预算重答，配额 ρ=1/4，使平均预算仍为 K（2.5% 时取 b=1%、ρ=0.3）。
- **给出三个命题刻画"何时值得重答"**：(1) 重答的净修复率必须超过首遍让出的分数阈值 H̄/ρ；(2) 只依赖已保留 token 的检测器在两个共享 Vq 的题目上输出必然相同，因此其召回/误报被 γ 界住，而全图 checker 的误差 ≤ γ（嵌套视图信息量单调，Corollary 1）；(3) 在单调性假设下，"先按不一致分组、组内按置信度从低到高"是最优排序，并给出任意信号的信息天花板。
- **大规模实证**：18 个基准、5 个 InternVL3 尺寸（8B/14B/26B/38B/78B）+ Qwen3-VL 8B/32B + Gemma 4 31B，共 3 个模型家族；在 38B 上 108 个 setting 中每个基准都最优（POPE 上两次并列），并且把 IF-Prune / SGL 的排名插进自己两遍流程后仍能提升它们。
- **把增益归因到"证据链"**：定位答案证据所需的预算，显示不一致率在证据超出首遍预算后上升，重答把失败起点推到 2K，且修复远多于破坏。

## Key Contributions

- Identifies a blind spot of one-pass pruning: with strong rankings the damage concentrates on a few detail-dependent questions, and any detector that reads only the kept tokens cannot see what was pruned — the large VLM's own confidence included.
- Proposes PRUDENT, a two-pass, per-question budget allocation scheme driven by the disagreement between the pruned large-VLM answer and a small full-image checker, keeping the average visual-token budget fixed.
- Proves three propositions plus two corollaries: a break-even threshold for re-answering, an information-theoretic bound showing kept-token detectors are dominated by full-image checkers, and the optimal ordering of re-answers (disagreement first, then low-to-high confidence), with an explicit ceiling for any signal.
- Evaluates on 18 benchmarks across three model families and five InternVL3 sizes, reporting per-benchmark wins at 1%, 2.5%, 5%, 10% and up to 40% budgets, plus ablations that isolate ranking, checker, abstention, and selection rule.
- Shows the mechanism is orthogonal to ranking: plugging IF-Prune's or SGL's own ranking into both passes still improves those methods, so "which answer to trust" is a second lever beyond "which token to keep".

## Methodology

**Setup and notation.** A large VLM encodes an image into m visual tokens; a ranking s_q orders them and the top fraction k (the kept set S_q(k)) is passed to the LLM. The budget K is the average fraction kept per question; a *pruning failure* at k is a question answered correctly with full tokens and incorrectly with S_q(k). Confidence π_q(k) is the length-normalized probability of the answer.

**The signal.** A small VLM (the *checker*, InternVL3-2B for the InternVL family) reads the **full image** and answers the same question; disagreement δ_q(k) = 1[c_q ≠ a_q(k)] is computed at test time with no labels. The checker abstains when its least-probable answer token falls below τ or its answer exceeds ℓ words (defaults τ=0.5, ℓ=10), in which case the large VLM answers once at K. The set of non-abstaining questions is C, and the quota ρ is counted over C so the average budget stays K.

**The algorithm.** Pass 1 answers every question at fraction b. Disagreeing questions are sorted by the first answer's confidence, low to high; up to a quota ρ the large VLM re-answers at 2K and the new answer replaces the first. Leftover quota goes to the lowest-confidence agreeing questions. Defaults b=K/2, ρ=1/4 (so b + 2ρK = K); at K=2.5% the paper uses b=1%, ρ=0.3. The guide (InternVL2.5-1B) supplies the ranking in both passes: it averages its own attention ranks with a learned token-importance estimate for open-ended questions, and uses the learned estimate alone for multiple-choice/yes-no items where attention is uninformative. Total visual tokens read average 98% of one-pass pruning.

**Theory.** Prop. 1: with u_q the gain of a re-answer and h_q the loss of the first pass, Δ = (1/n)[Σ_{q∈R} u_q − Σ_{q∈C} h_q], so re-answering pays exactly when the mean net repair rate PR exceeds H̄/ρ; random selection cannot beat one-pass when the one-pass score curve is concave on [b, 2K]. Prop. 2: a detector reading only V_q(b) gives identical outputs on two questions with the same kept view, so when pruning changes one answer and not the other it must err on one; a full-image checker's flag error is bounded by γ = P(c_q ≠ a_q(1)). Corollary 1: V_q(b) is a function of V_q(b′) for b < b′, so mutual information is monotone in the budget and the bound holds at every budget. Prop. 3: under monotonicity of the expected re-answer gain in δ and π, disagreement-first then low-confidence-first maximizes expected gain, and the achievable gain is capped by the ρ|C| largest conditional expectations E[u_q | I_q] for any information I.

**Cost control.** The 2B checker costs one full-image pass per question (4.3 GB of weights over IF-Prune, which already loads the 1B guide); the paper reports a variant that reuses the 1B guide as checker to match IF-Prune + draft's FLOPs. The large VLM decodes speculatively from the guide's answer, as IF-Prune + draft does.

## Results & Highlights

- **Main table, InternVL3-38B, 2.5% budget** (Table 1, average of each benchmark's ratio to its full-token score): PRUDENT 88.4 vs IF-Prune 83.4 (+5.0 pts) and SGL 79.1 (+9.3); per-benchmark examples are ChartQA 71.9 vs 52.2 (IF-Prune) and DocVQA 85.5 vs 77.2, while POPE is 89.9 vs 89.5 — the gain is overwhelmingly on text-rich items.
- **InternVL3-8B, 2.5%**: 92.3 vs 87.7 for IF-Prune and 81.9 for SGL (+4.6 / +10.4). At 5% the averages are 95.1 vs 92.0 (+3.1), at 10% 97.5 vs 94.9 (+2.6) — the margin shrinks as the budget loosens, exactly as the break-even analysis predicts.
- **Extreme budget, 1%, mean over the six text-rich benchmarks** (Table 3): 38B reaches 71.1% of full-token score vs 62.3% for IF-Prune (+8.8) and 60.2% for SGL; 8B reaches 73.6% vs 64.4% (+9.2); the lead holds at 14B (71.0 vs 62.1), 26B (72.5 vs 64.0) and 78B (73.2 vs 65.3).
- **Other families** (Table 4, 10 benchmarks, budget where SGL keeps ≈90%): Qwen3-VL-8B at 10% gives 91.4 vs 90.3 for SGL; Qwen3-VL-32B 89.0 vs 87.8; Gemma 4 31B at 20% gives 92.5 vs 91.4 for MMTok and 91.1 for SGL — gains persist but are much smaller, consistent with the looser budgets.
- **Cost at 5%, 38B** (Table 44): 421 ms and 92.7% of full-token accuracy vs 383 ms / 87.3% for IF-Prune and 499 ms / 100% for full tokens; FLOPs are 54.6% of full vs 48.4% for IF-Prune. The 1B-checker variant runs at 380 ms, 49.7% FLOPs and 92.1%, i.e. +1.3 FLOP points over SGL for +6.7 accuracy points.
- **Ablations, 2.5% text-rich mean** (Table 5): removing the checker and answering once at K costs −2.4 pts; choosing re-answers at random costs −6.4; using SGL's early-exit score −4.4; using the large VLM's own confidence −3.0; letting the checker never abstain −1.1; using IF-Prune's ranking everywhere −7.8. On general benchmarks the ordering-only variant of the same ablation is −0.5, i.e. there the gain is almost entirely from re-answering.
- **Mechanism checks**: for each of the eight pruning methods the checker answers most (roughly 71–85%) of its pruning failures correctly at 2.5% (Fig. 3b); the disagreement+confidence order has the highest AUROC for pruning failures on every benchmark among eight signals (Fig. 3c); and on 392 "valid twins" built by editing only the pruned region, the full-image checker separates the two images 63.6% of the time while a checker restricted to the kept tokens manages 3.5% (InternVL3-38B, Table 6) — direct evidence for Prop. 2.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The individual ingredients exist elsewhere — small-model guidance (SGL), learned token importance (IF-Prune), cascades and disagreement-based routing, and speculative decoding. What is new and well argued is the reframing: pruning quality is bounded by token ranking, but *trusting* a pruned answer is a separate decision, and the natural detector must read the view the pruner removed. Prop. 2 and Corollary 1 turn that intuition into a real impossibility-style statement about kept-token detectors, which is the strongest part of the paper. The algorithm itself is a three-line rule (disagree → low confidence → quota), which is a virtue for adoption but means the contribution is mostly diagnostic + empirical.

**Rigor.** The evidence chain is unusually complete for a pruning paper: 108 settings per budget family, paired bootstrap over questions with Holm correction, a 1% stress test, five model sizes, three families, and a separate experiment that inserts IF-Prune/SGL rankings into both passes to prove the gain is not a ranking artifact. Reproduction of IF-Prune is honest (Table 19: 78.5 vs 79.0 on TextVQA at 5%). Two real weaknesses: (i) **single run per setting** with greedy decoding — the paired bootstrap captures question-level variance but not run-to-run or prompt variance, and the reported "significantly better in most, worse in none" claim rests on that one draw; (ii) **hyperparameters τ, ℓ and the b/ρ schedule were selected on ten of the evaluation benchmarks** (ChartQA, DocVQA, InfoVQA, TextVQA, OCRBench, WebSRC, GQA, MMBench, MME, POPE) with the same InternVL3-8B/38B models, so the headline numbers carry a mild optimistic bias. The authors mitigate this by showing the change is <1 point and that a break-even cut fitted on InternVL3 transfers to 76/84 Qwen3-VL-8B settings, but a held-out tuning split would have been cleaner.

**Baselines.** The headline "outperforms eight methods" is somewhat flattered by the baseline set: at 2.5% ToMe, VisionZip, FastV, CDPruner, ZOO-Prune and MMTok collapse to 15–30% of full-token score on ChartQA, so five of the eight are not competitive and inflate the average margin. The paper is aware of this and its real comparison is against SGL and IF-Prune, both of which are strong and carefully reproduced. One asymmetry remains: SGL is run **without its early exit**, as in its released code. The ablation shows early-exit scores are a poor selection signal (−4.4 pts), but SGL's early exit is also its main compute saving, so the comparison is accuracy-at-equal-token-budget rather than accuracy-at-equal-latency, and PRUDENT's default configuration is 38 ms/question slower (421 vs 383 ms) and 6.2 FLOP points heavier than IF-Prune. The `Ours, 1B check` row answers this objection well (380 ms, +6.7 pts over SGL) and should arguably be the headline configuration, not an appendix variant.

**Reproducibility.** Strong: public code, lmms-eval, named checkpoints, stated abstention thresholds, per-benchmark tables in the appendix, and an explicit statement of which ports differ from prior reported numbers (CDPruner's port keeps more of the full-token score than HAWK's run, Table 20). The one thing not measured is the actual wall-clock benefit of reusing the first pass's tokens in the 2K re-answer — the paper notes a re-answer could reuse cached computation but reports latency as if it does not, so the 421 ms is likely pessimistic; conversely the "same average budget" framing counts only the large VLM's visual tokens, not the checker's full-image pass, which is where the 13% extra FLOPs come from.

**Significance.** High for the efficiency community: it gives a principled reason why better rankings alone are hitting diminishing returns at tight budgets, and a cheap, model-agnostic add-on that improves two state-of-the-art methods with their own rankings. The absolute numbers matter — at 2.5% the 38B model recovers 88.4% of full-token performance instead of 83.4%, and text-rich ChartQA goes from 52.2 to 71.9, which is the difference between unusable and usable for document workloads. The caveats are that gains shrink to ~1 point on Gemma 4 at a loose 20% budget and that the mechanism needs short, format-comparable answers.

**Red flags, honestly stated.** (1) The "disagreement" signal is exact-match after benchmark-specific normalization; paraphrases that mean the same thing count as disagreement, and the checker's abstention on answers longer than 10 words quietly removes the hardest open-ended cases (MM-Vet is only in the appendix, judged by an LLM). (2) The theory's guarantee is relative to γ, the checker's own error rate against full-token answers; on a new distribution where the 2B model is much weaker, the bound degrades gracefully on paper but the practical gain is unverified. (3) The claim that a full-token-answer checker would raise the ceiling by only ~0.5 point is a useful sanity check, but it is measured on the same benchmarks used for tuning.

## Strengths

- A genuinely new axis for visual-token pruning: allocate trust per question instead of improving a global ranking, with a clean information-theoretic argument (Prop. 2, Cor. 1) for why kept-token detectors are structurally blind.
- Theory that is actually used: the break-even threshold H̄/ρ predicts both the budget dependence (gains shrink as K loosens) and the failure of random re-answering, and the paper checks both empirically.
- Broad, careful evaluation: 18 benchmarks, 5 InternVL3 sizes, 3 families, 1%–40% budgets, paired bootstrap with Holm correction, and per-benchmark appendix tables rather than only averages.
- Orthogonality demonstrated, not asserted: IF-Prune and SGL both improve when their own rankings are placed inside PRUDENT's two-pass loop.
- Cost is reported honestly (latency, peak memory, FLOPs, and a FLOP-matched 1B-checker variant), and limitations (long answers, video, multi-image, multi-turn, serving-dependent token value) are stated explicitly in Appendix P.
- Released code and data; transparent LLM-usage statement.

## Limitations

- Requires short, format-comparable answers; the checker abstains above 10 words, so open-ended generation and long-form VQA fall back to plain one-pass pruning.
- Only single-image, English/Chinese, single-turn settings are evaluated; video, multi-image and dialogue are explicitly left to future work.
- Adds a second model (InternVL3-2B, +4.3 GB) and a full-image forward pass per question; the "+5 points at the same budget" claim is about the large VLM's visual tokens, not about total FLOPs or latency, which rise to 54.6% and 421 ms vs 48.4% and 383 ms for IF-Prune at 5%.
- Exact-match answer comparison can misread paraphrases as disagreement, and the checker's abstention rule is itself a tuned hyperparameter (τ, ℓ selected on ten evaluation benchmarks).
- Gains are modest on families/budgets where pruning is already mild (Gemma 4 31B at 20%: 92.5 vs 91.4), so the method is most valuable exactly where the baselines are weakest — the tight-budget, text-rich regime.
- No measurement of cross-request batching or KV-cache reuse; the per-question latency at batch size 1 is a lower bound on the real serving cost of the extra pass.

## Relevance

这篇论文正好落在"极端性能优化下的可靠性"这一关注点上：它把视觉 token 剪枝的失败模式（细节证据被剪掉、模型仍自信作答）显式检测出来并做按题重分配，相当于给 token 剪枝加了一层运行时可靠性机制。对关注 KV/token 压缩导致精度退化、以及"检测—重试"式容错推理的研究者，其"用被剪掉的视图做检测器"的论证（Prop. 2 / Cor. 1）可以直接迁移到 KV-cache 驱逐与投机解码的失败检测。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. How much of the 421 ms can be recovered by reusing the first pass's KV cache and visual tokens in the 2K re-answer? The paper argues the overlap exists but does not measure it.
2. What happens when the checker is from a different family or is much weaker (e.g., a 0.5B model, or the large model's own pruned view with a different ranking)? Prop. 2 predicts graceful degradation through γ, but the empirical curve is missing.
3. Does the break-even threshold H̄/ρ estimated on a small labeled dev set transfer across domains (charts → medical documents → screenshots), or does ρ need re-tuning per deployment?
4. Can the same disagreement signal drive KV-cache eviction and speculative-draft acceptance, where the "pruned view" is a cache rather than a token set?
5. Why is the gain on Qwen3-VL and Gemma 4 only ~1 point? Is it the looser budget, a weaker/less correlated checker, or answer-format mismatch in the abstention rule?
6. Would a semantic-equivalence judge (rather than exact match) for δ change the recall/false-alarm trade-off, and at what checker cost?
