Why this matters

If you reward an LLM judge or a rubric, this audit flags an optimistic verifier before training: no policy model, no GPU, no rollout budget.

The same test predicts whether a verifier will cause padding, starvation, or nothing at all.

Critical edge

⚠The damage law does not travel: an independent PyTorch port refits β+ from 1.81 to 0.565 and misses the original law's prospective predictions by 4–12×.

Methodology · an oracle, then deliberate damage

  1. Enumerate. A first-match, deny-overrides gate over 10 categorical features materialises one int8 decision tensor: 124,416 contexts, shape 12×3×4×2×2×2×3×3×4×3.
  2. Score exactly. S is sufficient at c iff pinning S leaves the slice constant (min == max). MSR(c) is the antichain of subset-minimal sufficient sets; r* = F1(causal precision, recall) − length and wrong-decision penalties.
  3. Degrade one stage at a time. Outcome-only, sufficiency, recall-only, ε-noise, coarse taxonomy, stale rule, LLM judge. Boundary invariants pin each down: noise(0) ≡ exact, single-element groups ≡ exact, stale(r) ≡ exact where r is irrelevant.
  4. Audit before training. 60 stratified contexts × six controlled probe types (faithful, partial, padded, bare, wrong-decision, distractor-only) yield selection error and a signed gap. No policy model required.
  5. Train, then port. Qwen3-4B, 4-bit MLX LoRA (7.34M), group-relative REINFORCE, 150 steps, seeds 11/12; endpoints on 4 held-out gates × 24 episodes. A line-by-line PyTorch port adds 8 configurations.

β−0.77

What a practitioner can take away

  1. Audit before you train. 60 stratified contexts and six probe types cost no policy model, no GPU, and no rollout budget.
  2. Treat a positive signed gap as a padding alarm. Track cited-feature count, not token length; length stayed flat while citations inflated.
  3. Prefer pessimistic to optimistic, conditionally. Per unit of gap the optimistic slope is 2.3× steeper, but at ε = .30 noise damages as much in absolute terms.
  4. Never rank explanation quality by aggregate F1. The hacked model wins the table with the worst precision; report precision and recall apart.
  5. Do not quote the coefficients. Sign and ordering replicate across frameworks; the magnitude factor of ~2.6 does not.
  6. Expect the audit to need exact MSRs. Whether an approximate MSR set preserves the sign is untested.

Strengths

  • Exactness by construction. Tensor oracle versus logical oracle, zero mismatches over 124,416 contexts; MSR by popcount-ordered subset walk with superset pruning.
  • Fault isolation by design. Seven verifiers, each corrupting one stage, each with boundary invariants. The taxonomy is mechanistic, not descriptive.
  • A diagnostic that runs before the GPU bill. Selection error and gap sign need no policy model and no training run.
  • Exemplary reporting discipline. Pre-registered prediction sets scored as they fell (9 held / 6 refuted / 4 unscored), unexplained magnitudes left unexplained, polluted timestamps disclosed.
  • Compute-honest and rerunnable. ≈45 h on one 24 GB laptop for the main grid; ≈110 T4-hours for replication on free Kaggle accounts.
  • The F1 artifact is a genuine service. A hacked model topping an aggregate table while posting the worst precision should change reporting habits.

Limitations

  • Exactness narrows the domain. First-match categorical gates with ≤10 features; numeric, regex and datetime conditions must be bucketed, which is where policy semantics leak.
  • No model above 4B. The pre-registered Qwen3-8B ran out of memory twice on a 16 GB T4; scaling is untested in both directions.
  • Magnitude is grid-specific and unexplained. MLX +0.218 vs PyTorch +0.017–0.096; five candidate explanations excluded, none replaced.
  • Small n, clustered episodes. 6 + 7 fitted cells, many single-seed, and all 96 endpoints come from 4 gates, so episode-level SEs likely understate gate-level variance.
  • “Bias pessimistic” is overstated. ε = .30 noise at +0.230 exceeds sufficiency seed 11 at +0.204; a noisy but unbiased verifier can lose as much.
  • r* is a designed objective. No human deception, task success or attack success is measured, so “faithfulness falls” is not “the agent is worse”. The judge result is one model at one prompt.

Questions for further exploration

01

What causes the ~2.6× MLX / PyTorch magnitude gap: the 4-bit base's generation distribution, the tokenizer, or the unclipped mid-run dynamics visible as a control dip?

02

Does the law survive at 8B+ and larger group sizes, where s and u may decouple? Phi's 0.672 → 0.764 competence jump turns a null +0.017 into +0.087.

03

Would a gate-level cluster bootstrap overturn “beyond two standard errors in 7 of 10”? With 4 held-out gates, effective n may be closer to 4 than 96.

04

Is the pessimistic bias robust at per-feature ε = .5, where u = 1.000 on every step may dominate the 2.3× slope asymmetry?

05

Does padding that fools r* also fool a human auditor or a downstream safety classifier? Transfer shows F1 inflation, but no human or attack-success measurement.

06

Can the audit run without exact MSRs, and how much MSR approximation does it tolerate before its signed gap flips sign?
