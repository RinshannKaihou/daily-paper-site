Critical edge

The goal suite is exactly where a future-aware quantizer should win, yet LAQ loses there to SVDQuant on both models (64.3 vs 68.2; 68.6 vs 72.9) and the paper never explains why.

Also: no runs against Q-WAM, SteerQuant or QuantWAMs — every external baseline is a generic LLM/diffusion quantizer.

Method · four moves

Price the future, count the reads, spend one threshold

  1. Diagnose. z = G(o,ℓ) is read by a = R(o,ℓ,z). Keep the action tokens at BF16 and almost nothing improves; the error arrives through z. Existing WAM quantizers watch the action and never the future it is read from.
  2. Price. Reader metric M = E_c[Jcᵀ Dc Jc] — the Gauss–Newton curvature of the reader’s KL error in z. Rademacher/Hutchinson probes need one reverse pass through the reader only, no Jacobian. Effective rank 41.5 in 37,632 dims; 98% of the trace sits in the first predicted frame.
  3. Count. n_u = 1ᵀΩ_u1 / tr Ω_u compares a rounding error frozen across steps with one redrawn per step. Measured by intervention, not differentiation — a few dozen forward passes. Per-step sensitivity implicitly sets n_u = 1.
  4. Spend. Minimizing Σ n_u ω_u σ_u²(r_u) under a bit budget collapses to one rule: refine iff n_u ω_u σ_u² / c_u ≥ μ. One currency across the denoising loop and the KV cache. Refined units take a second 4-bit residual code, and the gaze is simply the action tokens’ own attention.

Strengths

What survives a hard review

  • The diagnosis is causal, not correlational. The future-swap experiment isolates the path; the frozen-vs-redrawn intervention measures the mechanism the method then spends money on.
  • Read count is a real step past per-step sensitivity. It turns “this error gets reused” into a scalar with a closed-form role, and the 2.50× joint-vs-sequential gap validates the premise.
  • Allocation is closed-form and shared. One threshold trades precision between the denoising loop and the KV cache — no per-layer search, no mixed-precision combinatorial solve, no retraining.
  • The 4-bit accounting is honest and the ablation is complete — 4.01 b/weight, no 16-bit branch, and “same budget, wrong criterion” controls that make 76.4 interpretable.

Limitations

What the paper does not answer

  • Goal is an unexplained, systematic loss on both models — exactly the perturbation where preserving the predicted future should matter most. Aggregate reporting conceals it.
  • The direct competitors are never run. Q-WAM, SteerQuant and QuantWAMs are dismissed as unimplemented, so the win is measured against stand-ins.
  • Real-robot evidence is thin where it counts: 25 trials, one baseline, no confidence intervals; simulated deltas of 0.2–0.4 points sit below run-to-run variance.
  • The reader metric’s direction estimate is nearly unusable — leading eigenvectors from two calibration halves overlap by 0.19; αI mixing is a patch, not a fix.
  • Deployment claims outrun the measurements: A100 only, no FLOPs or energy for the second pass, no failure-mode analysis, no gaze-stability test under occlusion.

Questions for further exploration

Why does Goal break? A goal change alters the predicted future rather than the observation, so a future-aware quantizer should win there. Does M mis-price goal-relevant latent directions?

Does the gaze loop survive fast camera motion, occlusion, or grasp transitions — and are wrong selections self-correcting? Static offline gaze costs only 1.2 points today.

How far can the budget shrink? 15.4% / 6.0% refinement is the reported operating point; is μ stable across embodiments or does it need retuning?

Can M be estimated without a differentiable reader — finite differences on the action — so it applies to black-box or non-smooth flow-matching policies?
