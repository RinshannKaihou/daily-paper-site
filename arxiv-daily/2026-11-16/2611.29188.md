# Ziv Meets Sumcheck: Zero-Knowledge Proofs of Floating-Point LLM Inference

> **一句话总结：** 论文不再去证明"某个 kernel 的舍入序列"，而是改证**正确舍入的矩阵乘** `CR(A,B,b)=RN(Σ A_il B_lj + b_j)`——每个点积精确求和、只舍入一次，于是结果与累加顺序、分块、硬件无关；为此用 Ziv 式判定（算一个有证书界的近似值，检查舍入点是否落在误差带内，未定的才精确重算）+ 指数分桶（把上百比特的精确整数拆成 12 位 limb 的桶多项式）+ 单个 sumcheck 检查乘积 + 每个输出一次的"残差式"舍入 gadget，把每个输出的约束从 Θ(mnk) 降到 52–62 个 cell。在 GPT-2 small 上，整个前向（12 层 + 全词表 logits）在 2 核上 18.7 分钟证完、21 秒验证，每 token 提交 9.4·10⁶ 个 cell，比按操作数上浮点 gadget（8.1·10⁹ 个约束）少 861 倍；代价是零知识只在显式未证的 masking 假设下成立，完整模型证明 0.83 GB。

> **TL;DR:** The paper sidesteps floating-point non-associativity by changing *which* function is proved: not a kernel's rounding trace but the correctly rounded GEMM `CR(A,B,b)`, exact dot product rounded once to nearest-even. That makes the relation canonical (order-, block-, hardware-independent), reduces the rounding gadgets from Θ(mnk) to one per output entry, and lets a single sumcheck over exponent-bucketed limbs check the cubic part. Measured against native bf16/binary32 BLAS kernels on GPT-2 small over WikiText-2, the native kernel differs from CR at 9.5·10⁻⁵ of 8.2·10⁷ product outputs, yet four semantics (fp32, native, reversed-order native, CR) are statistically indistinguishable in perplexity (CR 46.14 vs fp32 45.99, +0.0032 ± 0.0032 nats), while greedy tokens agree only 91.9% between two native orders. Cost: 9.4·10⁶ committed cells per token against 8.1·10⁹ constraints for per-operation gadgets (861×), all 343 products of GPT-2 proved in 372 s on two cores (449 MB, verify 9.5 s), full model with privacy in 18.7 min (0.83 GB, verify 21 s). The zero-knowledge claim, however, rests on an unproved masking hypothesis and an unproved leaf-entropy condition; only interactive soundness (< 2⁻⁸⁵) is bounded.

## 核心贡献

- **换掉被证明的函数，而不是加速旧的函数**：现有 zkML 为每个浮点操作建 gadget（单精度乘法约 64 个约束 [12]，ZIP [14] 报 34.5 小时/48 核/11M 参数模型），因为"每次加法后舍入"使结果依赖加法顺序、无法写成多项式恒等式。本文改为证明 `CR(A,B,b)_ij = RN(Σ_l A_il B_lj + b_j)`：**点积精确、整体只舍入一次**。这个语义是规范的（与累加顺序、blocking、batch size、硬件无关）、合法的 IEEE 754 执行（误差 ≤ 0.5 ulp）、且在代数上退化为"一个整数恒等式 + 每个输出一次舍入"——于是 Θ(mnk) 个 gadget 变成 mn 个。
- **Ziv 式三段结构（Figure 1）**：先用带证书误差界的 kernel 算出近似值 ŝ 和界 β̂，检查 `RN(ŝ-β̂) = RN(ŝ+β̂)` 是否成立；成立就输出，不成立才对该输出做精确计算。用 binary64 累加时 GPT-2 所有矩阵乘里被 flag 的条目 < 2·10⁻⁴。
- **三个具体障碍的解法**：(i) 精确乘积是上百比特的整数——按二进制指数分桶（`w=12`，limb `X=σM2^r`，桶指示子 `E_g`），用**一个随机 z 上的 sumcheck** 检查 `A(z)B(z)=Σ_t D^(t) z^t`，从而逐系数确定；(ii) 域等式必须在整数上成立——用 limb 范围检查 + 一个带 range check 的进位链把它们抬回 ℤ；(iii) 舍入必须精确（含 ties、binade 边界、下溢）——Lemma 1 给出"舍入区间"的充要刻画，配一个残差 `V = σN - (M-½)U` 的 gadget（(L1)–(L4) 查表 + (C1)–(C7) 五个约束）。
- **逐层扩展到整个 transformer block**：LayerNorm 写成对公开 2048×1024 矩阵 `M` 的矩阵乘 + 行乘积（`q=Σz_j²+ε̂`）+ 倒数查表；causal softmax 用差 gadget + 掩码指数查表 + 行和 + 倒数；最大值不可舍入，用**提交的选择子 Σ_ij**（Lemma 12）实现；GELU/`x↦e^{-x/8}`/`1/x`/`768/x` 四张 unary 表。所有张量**只提交一次**，下游用 view（head、转置、拼接、广播）读取，Lemma 13 证明 view 与多重线性扩展可交换，因此没有 copy constraint，每个 commitment 在所有 claim 点上批量开一次。
- **完整的隐私构造（Theorem 3）**：hiding 多项式承诺（每张表加一个前导 pad 变量，上半区是一次性 pad）、Libra 式 masked sumcheck、把每个 claim 改写成"零和"（只暴露 pad 坐标为 sumcheck 挑战的点上的值）、隐藏的 LogUp 辅助和。在显式假设 H(ε_H)（masking hypothesis）和 E(ε_RO)（leaf entropy）下证明统计 HVZK。
- **一个反面的必要性论证（§IX, App. S-IV-B）**：如果改为证明"误差有界"的关系，binary32 的界给证明者留下 41% 输出的 1-ulp 选择空间，一阶对抗者可协调这些选择、在 **47/48** 个目标上翻转 greedy token（随机选择只翻 1/48）；binary64 级的界则 0/48。这解释了为什么必须是规范语义、零松弛。

## Key Contributions

- **A relation substitution, not an optimization**: prove the correctly rounded product `CR(A,B,b)` instead of the rounding sequence of a kernel. The paper is explicit that this is a *different* floating-point function, not an approximation of the deployed one, and defends the choice on three grounds: canonicality, legitimacy (`≤ ½ ulp`, agrees with native kernels on all but ~10⁻⁴ of entries), and algebraicity (mn roundings instead of mnk).
- **Exponent bucketing + integer lifting**: exact products are hundreds of bits wide; splitting significands into `w`-bit limbs indexed by exponent bucket turns the matrix product into a matrix product of small integers, checked by one sumcheck at a random `z` (Lemma 5/6) with an explicit `ε_prod = (T_D - 1 + 4ν_m + 4ν_n + 8ν_k)/|K|` bound.
- **An exact rounding gadget with a residual characterization**: Lemma 1 gives a necessary-and-sufficient interval test for `c = RN(v)` handling ties (significand parity), binade boundaries (`λ = u/4` when `M = 128, Q ≥ 1`), and the overflow threshold Ω; Lemma 7 turns it into 7 constraint families of total degree ≤ 5 plus 4 lookup families, with explicit field-size conditions (7)–(8).
- **Coverage of every transformer operation**: GEMM (Theorem 1), element-wise sum/difference/product (Lemma 8/9), unary maps by lookup (Lemma 10), row reductions via the same gadget with `X ≡ 1, g ≡ 0` (Lemma 11), maximum by committed selector (Lemma 12), and layer chaining through views (Lemma 13).
- **A full hiding construction plus a complete-model argument** (Theorem 3), with the security analysis deliberately stated under named hypotheses rather than papered over, and an explicit list of what is *not* bounded (Fiat–Shamir, quantum ROM, the rank condition, ε_H, ε_RO).
- **A cost comparison that is structural rather than incremental**: 9.4·10⁶ committed cells per token vs 8.1·10⁹ / 1.1·10¹⁰ constraints for per-operation gadgets at 64 / 90 constraints per MAC — a ratio of 861–1211 that grows linearly in the inner dimension `k`, so it widens for bigger models (Table S3).

## Methodology

**Setting and semantics.** Formats are `Fl(e_0, H) = {σM2^{Q+e_0} : σ∈{±1}, M∈[256], Q∈[H]}`; finite bf16 is `Fl(-133, 254)`. A canonical triple requires `M ≥ 128` or `Q = 0`, with `σ=+1` when `M=0`; `u = 2^{Q+e_0}` is the ulp. The relation `R_CR` (Definition 3) contains `(A,B,b,C)` with `C_ij = RN_{e_C,H_C}(v_ij)` where `v_ij = Σ_l A_il B_lj + b_j` (bias fused before the single rounding). Because `C_ij` depends only on the *multiset* of products, `R_CR` is evaluation-order free.

**Why order-freedom matters (Definition 4, Prop. 1–3).** An "accumulating kernel" adds the `k` products in some order with roundings at unit roundoff `u = 2^{-t}`. Proposition 1 gives an order-agnostic bound `|ŝ_ij - v_ij| ≤ γ_k(Σ_l |A_il||B_lj| + |b_j|)`, `γ_k = ku/(1-ku)`; Proposition 2 turns it into the Ziv test (determinacy if `RN(ŝ-β') = RN(ŝ+β')`); Proposition 3 proves that **no kernel-agnostic single-valued relation exists** — with `A = (2^30, 1, -2^30, 0)`, `B = (1,1,1,1)^T`, left-to-right binary32 accumulation returns 0 while `CR(A,B) = 1`. A relation must therefore be order-pinned (bit-exact kernel), multi-valued (error-bounded), or canonical.

**The core protocol Π_CR (Sections IV–VI).** `x = ι(x)2^{e_0}` with `ι(x) = Xβ^g ∈ ℤ`, `β = 2^w`. For matrices, `D^(t)_ij = Σ_l Σ_{g+h=t} X^A_il[g^A_il = g] X^B_lj[g^B_lj = h]` and `N_ij = Σ_t D^(t)_ij β^t + X^b_j β^{g^b_j+d_b}` (Lemma 5: `v_ij = N_ij 2^{e_A+e_B}`). The verifier samples `z` and runs Thaler's sumcheck on `Σ_t z^t D̃^(t)(ρ_I,ρ_J) = Σ_l Ã_z(ρ_I,l) B̃_z(l,ρ_J)` where `Ã_z = X^A ∘ Σ_g z^g E^A_g` (Lemma 6). The rounding gadget takes the exact integer `N`, a claimed `c = Xβ^τ 2^{e_A+e_B}` with `u = U2^{e_A+e_B}`, `U = Pβ^τ`, sets `V = σN - (M-½)U ∈ ℤ` and enforces `0 ≤ V ≤ U`, `V ≥ U/4` when `M = 128, Q ≥ 1`, and `V ∉ {0,U}` for odd `M` — precisely Lemma 1. `V` is decomposed as `top·U + aβ^τ/4 + b'β^{τ-1} + Σ_{t<τ-1} b_t β^t` with a signed carry chain `e_t + c_{t-1} - βc_t = 0`; bib: the carry chain telescopes to the residual, and (8) makes every field equation lift to ℤ (Lemma 7). Per output entry the prover commits `n_cell = (7+G_C) + 5 + T_b + T_D + n_c(β)(T_c-1)` cells (eq. 9) and performs `3 + T_b + n_c(T_c-1)` lookups.

**Instantiation.** `p = 2^64 - 2^32 + 1`, quadratic extension `K = F_p[x]/(x^2-7)` (`|K| ≈ 2^128`), Ligero–Brakedown commitment (rate 1/4 RS, BLAKE3 Merkle trees, 250 queries, `e = 256` extension symbols, error `≈ (1-δ/3)^250 < 2^{-103}` for δ = 3/4). Lookup multiplicities are bound before `γ, ξ` because Lemma 4's bound holds only for multiplicities fixed in advance — the paper shows the ordering is essential (a forgery exists otherwise, App. before Prop. 1).

**Transformer block (Section VII, Table II).** Every tensor has its own public format, is committed once, and is read through views (heads padded 12→16, widths 768→1024, 3072→4096). LayerNorm is deliberately **re-specified**: `z_j = RN(3x_j - 2^{-8}Σx_l)`, `q = RN(Σz_j^2 + ε̂)`, `r = RN(√(768/q))`, `t = RN(z r)`, `h = RN(t γ + θ)` — five roundings where the reference rounds once, and `ε̂ = 71·2^{-10}` so that `ε' = ε̂/(9·768) ≈ 1.0031·10⁻⁵` is dyadic. Softmax uses `Δ = RN(c - S)`, `E = RN(e^{-Δ/8})` (scale 1/8 = 1/√64 absorbed into the table), `Z = RN(ΣE)`, `R = RN(1/Z)`, `P = RN(E R)`; the true max `c` is not a rounding and is enforced by a committed one-hot selector.

**Privacy (Section VIII).** Tables get a leading pad variable (`f̂(0,x) = f(x)`, `f̂(1,x)` uniform), rows are laid out as `n_p 2^{1+ν}` values in `n_p R` rows of length `c` with `2(K+1)` random masking rows `M_0, M'_0, …, M_K, M'_K`; openings reveal `u_0 = Σϑ^i row_i` and `u_k = Σ_p λ^p r eq(a_k,r) row_{p,r} + λ^{n_p} M_k` with `λ, ϑ ∉ F_p`. Sumchecks become Libra masked sumchecks with degree-7 masks drawn from one committed mask pool. Every claim becomes a zero sum `Σ eq((0,r),(y,x)) C f̂(y,x) = 0` so only pad-coordinate evaluations are revealed, and LogUp helper sums are replaced by two masked zero sums.

**Evaluation setup.** GPT-2 small (released weights) + WikiText-2; a NumPy forward pass under four semantics — fp32, native (bf16 with binary32 BLAS-order accumulation), native′ (reversed order), CR (Ziv procedure, calibrated windowed `Fl(e_0,48)`, `w = 12`, `G = 4`); element-wise layers in binary64 with one rounding. A Python reference over `F_p` exercising every constraint plus a Rust prover. All runs on **two virtual cores, 7 GB**.

## Results & Highlights

- **Native kernels are wrong relative to CR about once in ten thousand outputs, and two native orders are wrong as often as each other.** Over 82,354,688 GPT-2 product outputs (Table III), native bf16/binary32 differs from CR at 9.5·10⁻⁵ of entries, worst class 3.0·10⁻⁴ (aproj) — versus 0 for CR by construction; qk is lowest at 3.1·10⁻⁵. Table S4 shows strict left-to-right vs reversed-order binary32 differ at 0.8–5.4·10⁻⁴ across sites, i.e. two native orders disagree as often as either disagrees with CR.
- **The Ziv test is cheap with binary64 accumulation and nearly useless with binary32.** With binary64 it fails on at most 1.7·10⁻⁴ of entries (mproj 4.2·10⁻⁷, qkv 6.4·10⁻⁷), so CR can be computed from one binary64 product plus a negligible number of exact recomputations; with binary32 the worst-case bound of Proposition 1 flags 1.5% (qk) to 99.7% (mproj) of entries — a tight binary32 certificate is explicitly left open.
- **All four semantics are statistically indistinguishable on WikiText-2 (16×256 tokens, 4,080 scored tokens).** Perplexity: fp32 45.99, native 46.05 (+0.0013 ± 0.0031), native′ 46.02 (+0.0006), CR 46.14 (+0.0032 ± 0.0032); CR-w (48-bit window) is identical to CR. Yet bitwise they are far apart: logits equal fp32 at 100% of positions for CR but only 53.2% for native, and the greedy next token differs from fp32 at 7.9% of positions for native and 0% for CR.
- **The headline cost ratio is 861–1211× fewer committed values.** One GPT-2 forward step at context 128 commits 9.4·10⁶ cells per token against 8.1·10⁹ constraints for IEEE-compliant per-operation gadgets at 64 constraints/MAC (1.1·10¹⁰ at 90/MAC) — 861× and 1211× respectively (Table S3, Fig. 3), a ratio of `64k/n_cell` that grows with the inner dimension. For layer 5 alone, all 28 products (1.14·10⁸ MACs, 125,952 outputs) commit 7.4·10⁶ cells against 7.3·10⁹ constraints.
- **All 343 matrix products of GPT-2 small are proved in 372 s on two cores.** Setting: 16 tokens, 1.98·10⁹ MACs, 343 instances (Table V): prover 371.6 s, verifier 9.5 s, proof 449 MB, setup 625 s once per model; a block costs 42.9 s at 4 tokens and each extra token costs 0.61 s per block. Against ZIP's 34.5 h on 48 cores for an 11M-parameter model's linear layers, that is >3 orders of magnitude fewer core-seconds on a model 11× larger — with the caveat that the relations differ.
- **Complete forward pass with the privacy construction: 1121 s prove, 21 s verify, 846 MB proof.** 12 blocks + embeddings + final LayerNorm + logits over 50,257 tokens (padded to 7·2¹³), 21 phases, 482 tensor formats and 61 unary tables, 12,422 mask slots / 151,988 stream elements, 4.4 GB peak memory. Privacy costs 57% on a block (67.4 s vs 42.9 s), "mostly because the pads double the committed data."
- **Output quality matches or beats standard 16-bit inference.** The complete model's logits differ from binary64 by 0.37% versus 0.52% for native bf16; the canonical block's output errs by 0.43% against 0.37% for CR and native bf16 — the extra 0.06 pt coming from LayerNorm's five roundings. At the public last position all semantics predict the same token.
- **Attack rejection is thorough at the implementation level.** The reference verifier accepts 60 honest instances and rejects 18 hand-built + 239 randomized false statements (outputs off by 1–3 units, re-balanced limbs, wrong ties/binade/cancellation/underflow); the native prover accepts 427 and rejects 3 tampered ones; the block rejects all 55 attacks, including 19 "one layer wrong, everything later recomputed consistently" attacks that chaining must stop; the complete model rejects all 236 (25 changed tensors, 8 consistent wrong executions, 203 tampered proofs).
- **Windowed formats with only 4 buckets are nearly lossless on this workload.** Exponent spans are 31–45 bits for all matrix-product operands except GELU outputs (65 bits, 7·10⁻⁵ of entries beyond a 48-bit window) and attention probabilities (141 bits, 1.8% beyond it). Truncating to `G = 4` changes 0.70% of element-wise results (3,234,149 of 459,276,288), sends exactly 1 of 734,334,976 matrix-product outputs into underflow, and leaves the final logits **bit-identical** to unwindowed bf16 at all 4,096 positions.
- **An error-bounded relation is provably dangerous (App. S-IV-B).** With the binary32 bound, 41% of product outputs admit a 1-ulp choice; a straight-through adversary coordinates them and flips the greedy token at 47 of 48 targets (median logit gain 3.50), while random choices in the same slack flip 1/48. With a binary64-level bound the adversary gains nothing (0/48).

## Authors & Credibility

Hassan Khodaiemehr, Khadijeh Bagheri and Chen Feng are at the School of Engineering, University of British Columbia (Okanagan). Feng's group works on coding theory, information-theoretic security and lattice/post-quantum cryptography; the quadratic-extension field arithmetic, the Ligero–Brakedown instantiation and the careless-looking but load-bearing conventions (e.g. why `λ, ϑ ∉ F_p`, why the multiplicity ordering in Lemma 4 is essential) read like work from people fluent in the proof-systems literature, not a bolt-on. The bibliography is current through 2026 and engages the right baselines (ZIP, DeepProve, zkLLM, zkGPT, OpenLLM, SASH, plus the floating-point-accuracy classics: Muller et al., Ogita–Rump–Oishi, Demmel–Nguyen, Ziv). The engineering is real: a Python reference that exercises every constraint, a Rust prover with a native code path, and every number in Section IX read from JSON files the programs write (Section S-VI). Two self-audits stand out — Remark 1 refusing to claim Fiat–Shamir soundness, and Section S-IV-F listing seven design flaws (masking rows absorbing errors, code rate 1/2 giving only 2⁻⁶⁶ proximity, `ρ, ρ'` needing to be non-zero) found and fixed during development.

## Critical Assessment

**Genuine novelty.** The central move is correct and, in retrospect, obvious in the way good ideas are: floating-point GEMM is hard to prove only if you insist on the kernel's rounding trace. Proving the *correctly rounded* product makes the cubic part an integer identity and restricts all floating-point-specific work to `O(mn)` per-output gadgets. The Ziv-test framing is not decorative — the paper needs it twice, once to argue the canonical result is computable from a native kernel (1.7·10⁻⁴ flagged with binary64) and once as the design principle of the proof. Proposition 3 (no kernel-agnostic single-valued relation, with an explicit 4-element counterexample) is the kind of negative result that should be cited by anyone building "error-bounded" zkML.

**The zero-knowledge claim is the weak point, and the authors know it.** Theorem 3's error is `ε_H + Σ2νK/p + N_y/p + ε_RO`, and §VIII states plainly that "we have not constructed a fresh-pad order for the complete schedule and do not bound ε_H" and that they "claim no end-to-end privacy level." The linear-algebra terms come to about 2⁻⁴⁷·² with `ΣK ≈ 2.2·10³`, which would be respectable — but `ε_H` is a hypothesis over the *whole* transcript, coupling all claims about all commitments across all 21 phases, and the rank condition (12) is a genuine hypothesis that fails for concrete point configurations the paper itself lists (two points sharing a reduced part, Boolean reduced points, Frobenius conjugates). So the honest summary is: **soundness is argued, privacy is sketched.** The 236 rejected attacks say nothing about leakage, which the paper concedes: "rejecting attacks is no evidence of privacy."

**Soundness is bounded only for the interactive protocol, but the implementation is Fiat–Shamir.** Remark 1 says the 2⁻⁸⁵ bound covers the interactive protocol and that a Fiat–Shamir bound "must be derived for the complete message schedule (helper commitments, delayed openings), which we do not do," yet all measurements come from a BLAKE3-transcript implementation. The 2⁻⁸⁵ itself is dominated by `(1-δ/3)^250 < 2^{-101}` proximity per opening, which is fine, but it is interactive.

**The proved relation is not quite the deployed model, in three separate ways.** (i) Full bf16 (`H = 254`) is **not an instance of Theorem 1**: `e_C - e_A - e_B = 133` and no `w ≥ 2` divides both `w | H` and that offset, so the theorems need "windowed" formats `Fl(e_0, 48)` with `G = 4` buckets and `e_0` chosen per tensor; Remark S1 sketches but does not carry out the general alignment. (ii) The architecture is the *padded* one (heads 12→16, widths 768→1024) and Table II states the verifier does not enforce that padded parameters vanish — "a prover may use the padded coordinates as hidden capacity"; the fix (a public mask `Z` with `Z∘θ = 0` inside an existing zero-check) is described but not implemented. (iii) The canonical LayerNorm is a *different function* from the reference (`ε̂ = 71·2^{-10}`, five roundings instead of one), and the scope section admits the canonical model is "1.2 times less accurate than reference bf16" — which sits awkwardly with the abstract's "at least as accurate as standard 16-bit inference."

**Format leakage is a real, under-weighted problem for a privacy paper.** Section S-II-D concedes that the committed format of every tensor reveals an upper bound on entry magnitudes at 12-bit granularity, and that in the experiments "the formats were chosen from this very input." A deployment must calibrate formats on separate data, which will cost accuracy or buckets, and no experiment measures that. Combined with fresh per-proof parameter commitments (binding many proofs to one published model needs `e ≥ NQ+1` and the rank condition across proofs, unimplemented), the deployment story is thin.

**Comparisons are in different units on different hardware, which the paper says but the abstract does not.** The "roughly a thousand times fewer committed values" compares *cells* to *constraints* (scope item iii), and 861× is `64k/n_cell` — a structural ratio, not a time ratio. Against ZIP (34.5 h / 48 cores / 11M params) the claim of ">3 orders of magnitude fewer core-seconds on a model eleven times larger" is arithmetically fine but compares a per-operation circuit relation to a canonical one, so it is not a like-for-like speedup. Against DeepProve (174 tokens/min on 16–24 cores) the extrapolation to "roughly 4 tokens/min/core" is labeled as extrapolated, which is honest, but 0.61 s/token/block is a *marginal* cost that excludes the 625 s setup and the 846 MB proof transfer.

**Missing ablations that a reviewer would ask for.** No ablation of `w` (12) or `n_c = 4`; no measurement of what `G = 4` vs `G = 6` vs `G = 12` costs in prover time, only in entry counts; no experiment on a model larger than GPT-2 small (124M) despite the cost model being the main scalability argument; one prompt for the block and one for the complete model, so "all 236 attacks rejected" is a statement about a single execution's tensors. And the Ziv-test empirical rate (1.7·10⁻⁴) is measured on GPT-2's weight distribution only — a model with heavier cancellation, or an activation distribution with more near-tie sums, could raise it, and the paper does not probe that.

**Where the writing oversells.** "All numbers are measured, none are projected" is true of Section IX, but the abstract's "close to the cost of integer matrix multiplication" rests on a cell-vs-constraint comparison, and "extend it to every layer of a transformer" is true for GPT-2's layer set with the canonical redefinitions noted above. The 19-minute headline is `18.7 min`; the 21-second verify is real and is the strongest practical number in the paper.

## Strengths

- The relation substitution is the right idea and is defended on three independent axes (canonicality, legitimacy as an IEEE execution, algebraicity), not just on cost.
- The rounding gadget is exact in the cases that usually get hand-waved: ties decided by significand parity, `λ = u/4` at a binade boundary, odd-significand strictness, and underflow via `sub·(1-E_0) = 0`. Lemma 1 is a clean necessary-and-sufficient statement, and Lemma 7's soundness proof actually lifts every field equation to ℤ using explicit bounds (7)–(8) rather than assuming them.
- Commit-once / read-through-views is the correct architecture for a multi-layer argument, and Lemma 13 (views commute with multilinear extension) is what makes "no copy constraints" sound rather than convenient.
- The cost model is structural and predictive: `n_cell` is 52–62 and *independent of k*, so the advantage is `64k/n_cell` and grows with model width — a claim one can check without trusting the benchmark.
- The honesty is unusual. Four scope limitations in the introduction, an explicit "we claim no end-to-end privacy level," an explicit refusal to claim Fiat–Shamir or quantum soundness, seven self-found design flaws documented in S-IV-F, and a negative result (S-IV-B) that undermines a competing design rather than supporting this one.
- The error-bounded-relation adversary experiment is a genuine contribution to the zkML debate: 47/48 greedy-token flips from coordinated 1-ulp slack is a concrete argument that "verifiable within a certified bound" is not a useful guarantee for generation.

## Limitations

- Zero knowledge is proved only under an unproved masking hypothesis `H(ε_H)` and an unproved leaf-entropy condition `E(ε_RO)`; the rank condition (12) is a hypothesis with known counterexamples, and no end-to-end privacy level is claimed.
- Soundness bounds (< 2⁻⁸⁵) are interactive only; the deployed implementation is Fiat–Shamir via BLAKE3 and has no derived bound.
- Full bf16 (`H = 254`) is not covered by Theorem 1; everything measured uses windowed `Fl(e_0, 48)` formats with 4 buckets and per-tensor `e_0` calibrated on the evaluation input, which also leaks 12-bit magnitude bounds.
- The proved model is the *padded, canonical* architecture: padded parameters are not forced to zero (hidden capacity allowed), and LayerNorm/softmax are re-specified with different constants and rounding counts, so the statement is about "some model of the widened architecture," not GPT-2 as deployed.
- Proof size is 0.83 GB for the complete model and `O(√N)` openings; the paper's own open-problem list wants a polylogarithmic commitment to make this practical.
- Evaluation is GPT-2 small only, 2 cores, 7 GB, one prompt for the block and one for the complete model; no scaling to 1B+ models, no batching, no serving latency.
- Serving canonical semantics "needs a wider accumulator or a repair pass" (scope item iv): a GPU bf16 kernel cannot produce `CR` directly, so deployment requires either a different kernel or accepting the 1.7·10⁻⁴ flagged-entry repair rate.
- Unary tables are published and computed in binary64 then rounded — not certified correct roundings of the real functions — so all semantic statements are relative to the tables.
- No ablation of `w`, `n_c`, or bucket count `G`; no code release mentioned despite a reference implementation and a Rust prover existing.

## Relevance

这篇直接落在 **Operator Precision, Determinism & Error Propagation** 的核心：它把"浮点非结合性"从一个只能绕开的麻烦，变成可以精确定义并证明的对象——`CR(A,B,b)` 是一个与累加顺序/分块/硬件无关的规范语义，并用 8.2·10⁷ 个 GPT-2 输出给出量化证据（native 与 CR 差 9.5·10⁻⁵，两个 native 顺序彼此的差异与它们各自对 CR 的差异同量级）。对本项目最有价值的两点：一是 Proposition 3 与 S-IV-B 的对抗实验证明"误差有界"的关系不可用于生成——1-ulp 松弛就能翻掉 47/48 个 greedy token，这是判断任何"数值容差"验收标准是否足够的现成反例；二是 Ziv 式"先算近似+证书界、只对未定项精确重算"的结构，本质上就是 kernel 级数值正确性检验的通用模板（binary64 下只 flag 1.7·10⁻⁴），可以直接借用为判定某个 kernel 是否可能引入 SDC 级数值偏移的探针。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. What is the actual prover-time cost of `G = 6` (needed for GELU tails) or `G = 12` (attention probabilities, 141-bit exponent span)? The paper reports entry-change counts (0.70%, 1 of 7.3·10⁸) but never the time, and attention is exactly where the bucket count explodes.
2. Could the masking hypothesis `H(ε_H)` be discharged by independent masks per revealed value instead of a fresh-pad order? The paper calls this "the main open step" — is there a concrete obstruction, or only the bookkeeping of 21 phases?
3. Does the Ziv-test flag rate stay at 1.7·10⁻⁴ for a model trained with heavier cancellation (e.g. a model with large negative activations, or a LayerNorm-free architecture)? A distribution shift here directly multiplies the exact-recomputation cost.
4. How much does the "hidden capacity" in padded coordinates actually buy a dishonest prover? Table II says the verifier does not enforce `Z∘θ = 0`; a prover could plausibly encode a second, smaller model in the padding. Is there a concrete attack, or is it only a definitional gap?
5. What does the 0.83 GB proof do to verifier bandwidth in a realistic deployment, and does the `e ≥ NQ+1` requirement for binding many proofs to one published parameter commitment (S-II-D) rule out the amortized-setup model entirely?
6. Since the canonical LayerNorm rounds five times where the reference rounds once (0.43% vs 0.37% error), how much of the end-to-end accuracy gap is attributable to that choice rather than to the CR products? An ablation rounding LayerNorm once would separate the two.
7. Is the error-bounded-relation attack (S-IV-B) robust to a defender who samples the token from logits rather than taking the argmax? The 47/48 number is for greedy decoding with median margin 0.72 — the sampling regime is unmeasured.
