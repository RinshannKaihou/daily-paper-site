One recipe, four parameterizations

方法 base ckpt → adapters → GRPO → SVD
01 / BASES

Three 8B bases, no SFT

Apertus 8B (~15T tokens), Llama 3.1 8B (~15.6T), Qwen3-8B (~36T). All start from the base checkpoint, so the RL signal, the pretrained weights and the adapter parameterization are the only variables.

02 / ADAPTERS

LoRA · DoRA · QuanTA · FFT

LoRA ΔW=BA on all linear modules, α=32 with α/r scaling, r ∈ {1…256}. DoRA splits magnitude and direction, r ∈ {8…64}. QuanTA writes ΔW as a matrix-product operator from tensor-network physics (d=3: 35.3M params). FFT updates all ~8B.

03 / PROTOCOL

GRPO, one epoch, cold start

Group size 8, KL β=0, Dr. GRPO token-level loss, global batch 64, AdamW at 1e-5 with cosine decay and 0.1 warmup. Reward = accuracy (semantic math_verify) + a format term capped at 0.75 so it can never dominate correctness.

04 / DIAGNOSTICS

SVD on every checkpoint

Effective rank ρ = exp(H(p)) over the squared singular values of each learned ΔW, averaged per layer, and compared against the frozen base weights' singular-value decay.

FFT keeps 77.3% in-distribution, then drops to 1.35% on AIME — a 9.7-point gap to the best adapter on the same checkpoint family. On puzzles the ordering holds: QuanTA 20.6 > FFT 19.1 > DoRA 16.6 > LoRA 11.8.

Cost (4×H100, one epoch): QuanTA d=3 reaches 84.0% in 7.5 h with 35.3M params; DoRA r=16 needs 15.6 h and 45.0M for the same score.

The collapsed r=256 checkpoint realizes a higher effective rank than the working r=64 one. Full fine-tuning shows the same paradox in reverse: it updates ~8B parameters yet learns a lower-rank update than adapters touching 0.56% of them.

Batch scaling contracts the spectrum monotonically — r=64 goes 13.81 → 10.28 → 8.86 at batch 64/128/256 (−35.8%). Effective rank is therefore diagnostic, not determinative; what matters is the geometry of the reachable update manifold.

 The Model Maturity Hypothesis: adapter geometry that works may depend on structure acquired in pretraining.

Treat it as a hypothesis. It is a 3-model, 2-vs-1 split, and the two "agreeing" models differ by 1.2 points at the accuracy floor. Token count, tokenizer, architecture and data mixture are fully confounded.

Strengths

  • A clean, replicated negative result: the r=64→r=128 cliff and the rank–batch boundary both hold at seed 43.
  • Unusual honesty — the paper reports that its own effective-rank diagnostic fails to predict collapse, rather than burying it.
  • Reusable apparatus: one shared cold-start GRPO recipe across four parameterizations, released code, container spec, and every reward weight.
  • The FFT paradox is memorable and testable: full parameter budget, lower realized update rank than 0.5%-parameter adapters.
  • Practical guidance that contradicts a popular belief: bigger batches do not stabilize high-rank LoRA.

Limitations

  • No rsLoRA, no PiSSA/OLoRA initialization, no per-rank LR sweep — so "geometry" is not separable from the α=32 / α/r + fixed 1e-5 LR convention.
  • The cliff is suspiciously total and undiagnosed: no entropy, gradient-norm, or format-compliance trace at the headline r=128 / batch 64 point.
  • Cold-start only (no SFT), 8B dense only, single 4×H100 node; behavior at 30B+ or in MoE is untested.
  • Total GPU-hours are unreported, so replicating the three-seed table is expensive.
  • Skywork-Hard differences sit inside noise; Apertus and Llama results sit at the 2–4% floor and carry little signal.

Open questions

后续可验证的方向
Q1

Is the r=128 cliff intrinsic to LoRA, or an artifact of α=32 / α/r with a fixed 1e-5 LR? One matched-scale rsLoRA or PiSSA run would settle much of it.

Q2

What happens at the cliff? Per-step format compliance, entropy, gradient norm and clip fraction at r=64 vs r=128 would separate "never ignites" from "ignites then destabilizes."

Q3

Hold the update budget constant instead of the epoch count — does the rank–batch boundary move? At batch 256 the model sees only 168 steps.

Q4

Can maturity be tested with intermediate checkpoints of one family (Qwen3 at 5T/15T/36T, OLMo 2 stages) to unbraid token count from architecture?

Q5

Why does DoRA degrade gracefully to 75.0% at r=64 where LoRA falls to 4.7% at r=128 — is the magnitude/direction split acting as a trust region, and can update-norm ratios measure it?

Q6

Does FFT's AIME collapse (1.35% vs 11.04%) reproduce across seeds and on a wider OOD suite — AMC, OlympiadBench, AIME 2024?
