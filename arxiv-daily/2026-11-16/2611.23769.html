Why this matters

Shrink a reasoning VLM's cache and the image silently vanishes mid-thought: the same chart question loses 8.3 points asked first, 24.3 asked after reasoning.

Scope: Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking · DBTrimKV host · CharXiv follow-up, B=256

Critical edge

The headline +20.3 compares 259 entries to a host already at 61.3%; against the cheap matched-memory reserve the real gain is +5.0 — and no confidence intervals are reported.

03 Method · ShelfKV

Keep every visual KV on CPU; borrow it per step

SHELF · CPU · all visual KV → SKETCH · r=32 · 4-bit → DESK · GPU · |Dt|=|Ht| → SWAP ≤ B/4 / step

Reasoning tokens and image tokens compete for one budget. ShelfKV splits retention from reading:

  • Shelf. Every visual KV entry stays in CPU memory at full precision and is never deleted.
  • Desk. Holds exactly the host's B entries, so attention cost and context length are unchanged. Visual entries are borrowed into slots vacated by the lowest-scoring parkable text.
  • Swap rule. Score visual candidates by a log-sum-exp query–key score over the GQA heads sharing a KV head; swap while u_s > u_p + δ, at most R_max = B/4 per step. Displaced text is parked, never deleted.
  • Cheap proposal. A per-layer PCA sketch (r=32, 4-bit) scores all shelved entries; the top c = 4·R_max candidates refresh every N = 4 steps in a background thread, so full-precision keys cross the bus only when actually read.
  • Always resident. The prompt and the most recent W = 32 tokens; the host keeps managing text as before. Training-free.

ShelfKV turns a 14.4-point loss into a 1.7-point one, while using fewer GPU entries than DBTrimKV's 1024-entry setting.

  • Five-turn conversations: 85.0% vs 81.9% reserve, 79.4% DBTrimKV@1024, 87.3% full. Largest gain on turn 4 (87.0 vs 72.0), none on turn 1 — as expected.
  • Best fixed text/visual split (the strongest simple baseline): still leads by 12.7 / 5.0 / 3.0 points at 128 / 256 / 384 entries.
  • What is being read: 91.4% of borrows land on entries the host already deleted; on 56 recorded questions ShelfKV answers 49 correctly vs 39 for the host alone.
  • MMDU: judge 4.9 vs 4.5 (reserve) and 5.2 (full) — inside the 0.51 mean gap between two LLM judges.
  • A weak host cannot be rescued: with R-KV, ShelfKV reaches 42.7% against an 85.7% full cache. Text policy still dominates.
06 Where it bends

Three compressed caches above the full cache

On MMMU-Pro, ShelfKV scores 56.7%, the reserve 55.3%, and the full cache 54.0% — all three at or above uncompressed on 300 questions. That is a noise signature, and the paper does not flag it.

Ablations do hold up: removing host-managed text costs 5.3 points on MMMU-Pro and doubles generation-limit hits (7.7% → 19.0%); exact scores instead of the sketch buy nothing; random entry selection drops MMMU-Pro from 55.3 to 35.7.

Accuracy is measured in a mask simulator, cross-checked against a physical kernel: 41 of 3,943 CharXiv steps diverge, so per-question outcomes can flip even when aggregate accuracy matches.

Strengths

What the paper gets right

  • A checkable diagnosis. 184.5 → 12.5 entries per head and 8.3 → 24.3 points for the identical image and question; any reader can reuse the protocol.
  • The control experiment. Three configurations at identical total size, including the unprotected arm most papers omit, isolating selection from survival.
  • Honest baselines. Full cache, four host budgets, four reserve sizes, random and lowest-score selection, best fixed split, a second host and a second model.
  • Elegant swap. Desk cardinality equals the host's, so protecting vision costs no extra attention slots — a genuinely different design point from "keep everything".
  • Reproducibility signals. Released DBTrimKV gates, masked-vs-kernel agreement, A100/A800 token-exact reruns, named judges, code promised.
Limitations

What it does not buy

  • No memory reduction. All visual KV moves to CPU RAM and prefill peak is untouched; total footprint is flat.
  • Decode penalty grows with batch: 48.3 vs 41.0 ms at batch 1, 103.2 vs 41.8 ms at batch 64, 152.2 ms on MMMU-Pro.
  • Only images at prefill are shelved, so on MMDU every image must arrive in turn 1; later-turn images fall back to host behaviour.
  • Only the image is protected. Text eviction is still the host's problem, and a bad text policy caps the method far below full cache.
  • Needs regions the reasoning did not use: on HR-Bench 4K fine details every compressed cache, this one included, stays 3.5–6.0 points below full.
  • Simulator-first accuracy and hyperparameters tuned on 64-question dev splits; sketch settings alone span 71.9–82.8 on CharXiv.
07 Open questions

What would settle the remaining doubt

01
Does the 97.9%-deleted figure scale with image size (671 vs 2.9K vs 6.4K visual tokens), and should the reserve scale with N_v rather than a fixed 128?
02
What happens with a trained host other than DBTrimKV — does a vision-aware gate make ShelfKV redundant, or is crowding-out structural to any single budget?
03
How much of the +5.0 survives a bootstrap over the 300 CharXiv questions? That would settle whether +1.4 (MMMU-Pro) and +0.3 (MMDU) are real.
04
Does per-request swapping interact badly with prefix caching and continuous batching, where every sequence needs its own desk and gather schedule?
05
Could parking be inverted — protect early reasoning tokens and let late reasoning be evicted — given that reasoning reads poorly on demand (−5.3, 19.0% limit hits)?
06
Why does a compressed cache exceed the full cache on MMMU-Pro: noise, or does evicting low-score text remove distractors? A direct test would settle it.
