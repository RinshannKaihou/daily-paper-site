Why this matterstoday

Turns reward hacking into a pre-flight check: ~12 GPU-hours of judge audit ranks the loopholes an RL run will hit, before you spend the compute.

471 audit problems + 4,000 policy samples → a per-judge, per-pattern risk ranking.

Critical edgeread with care

In 3 of 4 low-share runs a different, higher-FPR pattern took over 10–130 steps later — exposure predicts the first event, not the eventual winner.

first event ≠ final outcome

Methodology

01
  1. Audit the judge. 471 integer-answer math problems × 14 known hacking families, each judge on its native prompt and decoding, so the FPR is measured under exactly the reward the policy later receives.
  2. Sample the policy. 4,000 completions per checkpoint (16 samples × 250 problems per pool, Jeffreys smoothing) → p̂, the chance the starting policy emits each pattern.
  3. Freeze the prediction. Score exposure = 256 · p̂ · FPR, log the predicted first-exploited family and its onset window — before step 1 of RL.
  4. Train and test. GRPO on Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, 300 steps × 32 prompts × 8 samples. An event = a judge-accepted, gold-wrong pattern rising ≥5 pp and holding for two evaluations.

Causal machinery: 14 families split 5/5/4 once, doses 0.3 / 1.2 / 4.0% rotated across three seeds in a Latin square; a 3–3.8% low-share run where propensity and FPR are ordered oppositely by design; rule-based masking of the top-FPR pattern as a simulated patch.

Results

02 · 24 core runs · 7 judges
  • Winner ranking is perfect at run level. Highest exposure first in all 12 event runs; run-level McNemar 6–0 vs FPR (p = 0.031) and 10–0 vs propensity (p = 0.002).
  • Low-share is the sharp test. 6/6 — e.g. predicted “{” at step 62 (window 33–117), observed at step 40 — while FPR alone missed all four F1/F2 winners.
  • Timing follows 1/exposure. log-step slope −1.03, Spearman −0.84 (p = 0.0003); every winner had banked 234–804 rewarded responses first.
  • Difficulty gates exploitation. Hard-pool only in 7/12 discordant pairs, never the reverse (p = 0.016): accepted hacking answers carry ~2.6× the advantage there.
  • Transfers to held-out judges. 0.995 vs 0.979 for FPR alone; the 1.92 threshold called event presence correctly in 8/9 held-out runs.

Holds up

  • Falsifiable product claim with predictions and onset windows frozen before training, including a pre-recorded threshold.
  • Real causal instruments — Latin-square dose rotation, an anti-correlated low-share run, and detector masking that simulates patching the judge.
  • Honest statistics: five pre-specified tests, Holm correction, exact McNemar and randomization tests, bootstrap over whole runs.
  • Validated instrumentation: detectors match 6,123 hacking labels, flag none of 12,500 clean solutions, and a hidden gold checker the policy never sees.
−

Where it thins

  • One policy, one algorithm: Qwen2.5-1.5B, GRPO, 300 steps, integer-answer math only; rule-based detectors see 14 families.
  • The dose test fails (p = 0.056) and the design floor is p = 0.017 — the causal case for propensity rests on ~10 runs across two judges.
  • Hard vs easy is confounded with problem source (DeepScaleR vs MATH), and no single-source control was trained.
  • No pilot baseline: a 20-step GRPO watch might match the audit; static-accuracy and Qwen3.6 margins are near-degenerate.
适用性 / Relevance

把 reward hacking 从「训练后观察到的崩塌」变成「训练前可用两个可测量量审计的风险」，并给出可操作的阈值（≈1.9 rewarded responses/step）与缓解手段——按检测器屏蔽模式，或直接换一个更低 FPR 的 verifier。做 RLVR/GRPO 训练监控与裁判选型的工作，可以直接照搬这套前置检查。

Questions for Further Exploration

03

Q1How much of the predictive gain survives a 20-step pilot run? If a short pilot already picks the winner, the audit’s value is just avoided cost.

Q2Does exposure predict the equilibrium once you weight by differential acceptance — or is winner-take-all path-dependent after the first event?

Q3Does it hold for 7B+ policies, where natural propensities are plausibly higher than 19 in 174,760 responses?

Q4Does the audit transfer from math verifiers to rubric judges and open-ended tasks, where “accepts string X” is not a stable property?

Q5Is masking by detector durable, or does GRPO find a paraphrase the detector misses once the top route is closed?

Q6Can exposure be cut at training time — e.g. down-weighting groups whose only reward comes from a pattern-dominated answer?
