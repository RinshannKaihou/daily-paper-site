Methodology

The loop: 30 rewrites, zero evidence

Each model gets the current skill plus a one-sentence topic and the instruction “identify problems and improve the skill.” No task, no execution trace, no score, no verifier. The output replaces its predecessor, recursively, 30 times. Only the document is rewritten; bundled scripts and reference files stay byte-identical. The same model that rewrote a skill is then the agent that uses it.

skillt → rewrite(model, topic) → skillt+1 × 30 rounds
  • Prior, operationalized. The model writes ten skills from the same topic and length target, never seeing the expert text. Normalized TF-IDF distance puts a held-out self-written skill at 1.00 and the expert skill at 1.37.
  • Item-level judging. Every non-blank line becomes one item (6,605 on SkillsBench). A judge labels each KEPT / REWORDED / WEAKENED / DROPPED…, then items are grouped by how many of the ten prior documents state them (0/10 … 10/10).
  • Causal brackets. Restoration puts the 0/10 items back into the round-30 skill; a symmetric deletion removes the same word volume from the original. One adds evidence, the other subtracts it.

Mechanism

Damage is selective: rewriting deletes what the model would not have written

Thirty rounds do not randomly corrupt a skill. They pull it toward the model's own prior and quietly drop the parts that live outside that prior — the commands, paths, thresholds, exceptions and tool quirks an expert supplies.

After 30 rounds, only 23.1% of items that none of the ten self-written skills mention survive, versus 84.2% of items all ten mention. The split holds inside list items, paragraphs, code blocks and headings alike, so it is not an artifact of “rewriting prefers shorter sections.”

The out-of-prior set is largely shared: 1,866 of 6,605 items fall outside every model's prior, and an item missing from one model's prior is missing from another's 82% of the time.

Item survival after 30 rounds
0/10 — absent from every prior sample23.1%

A 61.1-point retention gap averaged over 6 models × 3 benchmarks. Restoration recovers 8.8 of the 14.6 lost points when only the 0/10 items return, versus 4.4 for the same volume of other lost items. Deleting 0/10 items costs 4.6 pts; deleting in-prior text of equal volume costs nothing (+0.6).

Intervention

SkillDiff: mark, don't rewrite

Find the 0/10 items, bold a [FOCUS] tag in place, and add one note asking the agent to carry those procedures, tools, parameters, conditions and exceptions into its plan verbatim. No text changed, no feedback needed, byte-exactly reversible.

Strengths

Trust
  • No counterexample. Six models, three benchmarks, 30 rounds, every cell negative.
  • Better than correlation. Restoration and deletion bracket the causal claim from opposite directions, closing the “it just got shorter” story.
  • The metric was demoted when it failed. Document-level drift saturates at round 5 while pass rate keeps falling; the authors say so and switch to item tracking.
  • Judge reliability is audited. Second judge, cross-family judge, rerun, 30 extra samples, two known-answer controls, κ 0.64–0.84 reported.
  • It is not a single-run artifact. pass@16: 75.3% original vs 63.2% round-30; τ²-bench policies fall 64.0→25.6 and 72.6→18.9.

Limitations

Doubt
  • The intervention is under-tested. No random-item placebo, no note-only control, no confidence intervals; one task ≈ 1.15 points, so +1.1 is a single task.
  • One rewrite prompt. “Improve the skill” is held constant everywhere, so the finding is really about unconstrained rewriting.
  • Mostly a one-call regime. Two of three benchmarks paste the skill into a single prompt, not a multi-step loop.
  • Thin replication on the headline. SkillsBench uses one rewriting chain per skill; the three-chain check skips it.
  • No verifier-driven baseline. “Mark, don't rewrite” is never compared against rewrite-with-feedback on the same 87 tasks.
  • Frontier-only. Small and open-weight models, whose prior should be thinner, are untested.
Open questions

What would change the conclusion

  1. Would tagging an equal number of randomly chosen items, or adding only the “don't simplify” note without tags, reproduce any of the +4.8? This single control separates “pointing at scarce knowledge” from “raising overall diligence.”
  2. How sensitive is the degradation to the rewrite instruction? A prompt that forbids dropping commands, paths and exceptions might reframe “rewriting is harmful” as “unconstrained rewriting is harmful.”
  3. Do smaller, open-weight models lose more, since their prior is thinner and the out-of-prior fraction larger?
  4. Can the 0/10 retention metric serve as a merge-time or training-time guard — rejecting a rewrite that drops out-of-prior items — and beat inference-time marking at equal cost?
