The theorems are stronger than the method

01The primitive: six cases, one strip

Quantized activation is y = clamp(⌊x⌉, 0, C). Applying round then clamp adds two noise symbols and loses the coupling between them. The transformer instead picks one affine strip y ∈ [λ̂x + µ̂ − ê, λ̂x + µ̂ + ê] by enumerating where [l, u] sits relative to the two thresholds −½ and C+½.

#Geometry of [l, u]Slope λ̂Noise added
1fully below: u ≤ −½0ê = 0, µ̂ = 0
2fully above clamp: l ≥ C+½0ê = 0, µ̂ = C
3ainside [0, C], single integer1constant rl
3binside [0, C], threshold crossed1ê = ½, plus rl ≤ y ≤ ru
4spans both thresholdsC/(u−½) or C/(C−l−½), tighter of the twoone ê = (µ̂₁−µ̂₂)/2
5crosses zero onlyru/(ru−l−½)one strip
6crosses upper clamp only(C−rl)/(u−rl−½)one strip

Every non-trivial case updates the zonotope the same way: v_out = λ̂v + µ̂, M_out = (λ̂M ‖ ê) — one fresh noise generator, not two. Optimality (Thm 2) is shown by minimising the strip half-width e*(λ) = ½(sup gλ − inf gλ) of the residual gλ = f − λx case by case.

05The dark horse: the plain zonotope

Unconstrained zonotopes average 0.002 s — roughly 282× faster than DRA-Sym's 0.564 s — and are already tighter at r = 3 and r = 6, crossing over only between r = 6 and r = 13. The constrained variant buys ~1.6× tightness for 100–400× the time. The paper reports the crossover but never turns it into a Pareto argument for when to use which.

06Strengths

  • The six-case round-clamp enumeration is exhaustive and checkable by hand — rare in this literature.
  • Real theorems, not hand-waving: explicit sup/inf per case and a division-free formulation that survives near-degenerate hull edges.
  • A convex-hull equality, not merely containment, for the complete formulation.
  • Unusually honest regime reporting: crossover radius, the Q = 10 loss, and the large-radius weakness are all stated.
  • Code released, official baseline implementation, and a graceful sound fallback on LP timeout.

07Limitations

  • One benchmark, one input point, 5 → 300 ReLU networks: no evidence that the LP path scales to MNIST or CIFAR models.
  • Only DRA-Sym is compared; no interval/box baseline, no QVIP or SMT exact methods, and QEBVerif's MILP stage is excluded.
  • The optimality theorem covers the 4-constraint variant; the deployed 2-constraint variant is only empirically close and never bounded.
  • At r = 19 the bound moves <0.3% between Q = 6 and Q = 10 — relaxation slack, not quantization, dominates there.
  • Single-run timings on one Apple M5, no variance or warm starts, and no check against real PyTorch/TFLite inference.

08Questions for further exploration

  1. How suboptimal is the 2-constraint variant on adversarial asymmetric intervals — is 0.011 the worst case or just the average?
  2. Why does the plain zonotope already beat DRA-Sym at small radii, and does that argue for a screening → tightening hybrid?
  3. How does the constrained path scale to 20 layers or a CIFAR CNN, and what fraction of runs would hit the 2 s LP cap?
  4. Can round-clamp compose with QEBVerif's MILP refinement to keep large-radius tightness and the small-radius speedup?
  5. Does the bound survive contact with a real quantized PyTorch model — tie-breaking, per-channel scales, accumulator saturation?
  6. Can the input quantization grid be exploited instead of quantizing the box vertices and discarding its structure?
