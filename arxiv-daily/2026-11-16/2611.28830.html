Why this matters

Reproducibility of large runs may hinge on optimizer memory, not just weights — and validation loss will not warn you. 大规模训练能否复现，可能取决于优化器的记忆而非权重本身，而 loss 曲线不会报警。

Critical edge

Learning rate alone tracks trajectory separation better (r = 0.984) than the score (r = 0.640), so “diagnostic” overstates what Table 5 shows. 学习率单独就能更好预测轨迹分离；分数的增量价值未被统计确立。

  • Relative geometry, not raw norms. Three equally weighted terms: weight displacement relative to weight size, change in the next update direction, and per-layer RMS shift of ρ = log(√v̂ + ε). Rescaling the loss by c rescales m by c and v by c² while leaving the Adam direction unchanged — a raw Euclidean norm mixes incompatible units.
  • The probe. Draw Gaussian qθ, set qm = qv = 0, iterate the local tangent K times on one fixed diagnostic batch, renormalize each step. K = 3 on the small model, K = 1 on OLMo. The authors state plainly that SK is not the finite-time Lyapunov exponent — it is a ranking statistic, validated empirically.
  • The control. Activation at = (λ̂t − τ)+ held for 500 steps scales lr, damps m by (1 − γmat), slows v adaptation, boosts ε, and caps the update norm. Matched-budget baselines move the same strengths to gradient-norm- or update-norm-selected windows, isolating timing from strength.

Perturbation protocol: one channel at a time at 10⁻⁶ / 10⁻⁵ / 10⁻⁴, 200-step continuations, readouts at logits, top-1, greedy EM, and PIQA / HellaSwag / ARC-Easy answers.

Perturbing Adam's first or second moment is as consequential as perturbing the weights — so “read sensitivity off the weights” does not work. Second-moment errors carry amplitude, first-moment errors carry direction.

  • Regime change, not drift. At 10⁻⁵ the update path from step 2000 is nearly orthogonal to its reference (0.960 of its norm); from step 4000 it agrees to 4% (0.039). Score range 0.163 → 1.116, roughly 7×.
  • Seeds replicate the ordering, not the calendar. Across-channel response 0.960 / 0.039, 0.957 / 0.021, 0.236 / 0.022 — 5× to 46× — with high checkpoints landing anywhere in steps 1500–3500.
  • 1,000-step shadowing. Sensitive branch: 0.39–0.40 parameter separation, KL ≈ 0.20, ≈ 27% top-1, 0% exact generations. Stable branch: < 0.015 parameters, 1–1.5% top-1, 75% exact.

05Strengths

  • The state definition is right. Putting (m, v) inside x makes “same weights, different next step” expressible at all; the tangent recursion with HVPs keeps that affordable.
  • Geometry is argued, not asserted. The loss-rescaling argument (m → cm, v → c²v, direction unchanged) is a clean case against raw Euclidean norms.
  • Experimental discipline. Replayed batches, a batch-order negative control, three seeds, an explicit warning that nearby checkpoints are dependent observations, and a Muon pilot outside the Adam family.
  • Timing isolated from strength. The mitigation is a Pareto frontier, and matched-budget baselines share the same strengths and window count.
  • Candour. The score is not an FTLE; the design was refined mid-collection and is not pre-registered; bootstrap intervals include zero; K = 1 on OLMo.

06Limitations

  • One trajectory carries the evidence. A 76.8M model on C4 for 5,000 steps, ten correlated checkpoints — not ten independent runs, and far from production scale (β₂ = 0.95 in OLMo, 0.999 here).
  • The diagnostic claim is unproven. Nested ridge regression improves growth MAE by 14.5% with a bootstrap interval of −3.3% to 30.7%; cross-seed, two of four targets go negative and no sign-flip p is below 0.25.
  • The control is thin. Output-level evaluation at one shared checkpoint, 128 prompts, no variance across prompts, no fine-tune or downstream task to show that churn costs anything.
  • Batch order is measured, never mitigated — despite being the larger term by the paper's own numbers.
  • Heuristic knobs unablated. Equal three-term weighting, the 10⁻¹² floor, the threshold τ, and window count/placement are inherited rather than swept; no random-window baseline; K-dependence lightly explored.
Verify before citing: checkpoints are BF16, and at |w| ≈ 0.02 one ULP is ≈ 10⁻⁴ — so a “nominal 10⁻⁵” perturbation may sit below the representable resolution. Appendix A concedes small changes can be rounded away, but the main text never reports the realized amplitude the 35.7% headline depends on.

07Questions for further exploration

What would move this from a fitted ranking statistic to a usable instrument.

  1. After BF16 casting, what is the realized amplitude of the nominal 10⁻⁵ perturbation — and does 35.7% churn survive at the smallest representable step?
  2. Does the score beat learning rate and update norm prospectively: pick windows on run A, apply them to a held-out run B never used for fitting?
  3. What happens to the schedule under a genuinely different batch order, where separation is ≈1.43 rather than ≈0.5? Does timing still help, or does the data term swamp it?
  4. Is churn at near-ties actually harmful? Branch at a sensitive checkpoint, continue both, SFT both on identical data, compare benchmarks instead of top-1 agreement.
  5. Why does the second-moment channel saturate (parameter separation 0.113 → 0.171 → 0.818 while update separation stays flat) — real dynamics or an ε-boosted, floored denominator?
  6. Can the same relative geometry be computed for Muon, which has no second moment to differentiate?
