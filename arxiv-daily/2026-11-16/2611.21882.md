# WM-Quant: Rollout-Aware Post-Training Quantization for JEPA-Style World Models

> **一句话总结：** 作者指出 JEPA 式世界模型在 MPC 自回归 rollout 中量化误差会逐预测步复合、使 CEM 候选动作排序退化为接近随机，提出 WM-Quant——由"早期 encoder 块保 8 bit、控制通路保 FP、以整条 rollout 而非单步为目标联合标定每步激活范围"三件套组成——在 LeWM PushT 上把 W3A3/W4A4 的规划成功率从 10.0%/20.0% 提升到 58.0%/81.3%（FP16 为 98.0%），且几乎不增加推理延迟。

> **TL;DR:** WM-Quant is the first PTQ framework built specifically for JEPA-style latent world models, motivated by the observation that quantization error inside an autoregressive MPC rollout compounds through the predictor's state Jacobian, so per-step-accurate quantization can still produce near-random CEM action rankings (Spearman correlation with FP cost drops to 0.19). Three cheap fixes — 8-bit activations on the first three encoder blocks, keeping the action/control path in FP (~1.5% of DINO-WM params, ~7.5% of LeWM), and learning per-rollout-step activation ranges jointly with encoder ranges over a 5-step rollout — lift LeWM PushT planning success at W4A4 from 12.7% (DuQuant+GPTQ base) to 66.0% after one planning iteration and from 20.0% to 81.3% after two, against 80.0%/98.0% for FP16, while costing at most ~3% extra latency on a Jetson AGX Orin. Gains hold across 5 PTQ base methods on LeWM but collapse on the frozen DINOv2 encoder unless rotational transforms (DuQuant) are present; DINO-WM main results use a single seed.

## 核心贡献

- **首次把"rollout 误差传播"确立为世界模型量化的核心问题。** 论文用线性化的一阶误差递推 δ_{t+1} ≈ A_t δ_t + B_t ζ_t + ε_{t+1} 说明：encoder 误差 η_t 会被后续每一步的状态 Jacobian 连乘放大，因此"单步预测量化误差小"并不等价于"规划可用"。作者还明确区分了 LLM 与 JEPA 世界模型——LLM 输出被离散化为 token，等于把误差"重置为零"，而世界模型直接以信息稠密的隐向量为条件，不存在这种重置机制。
- **提出 WM-Quant，一个可叠加在任意静态 PTQ 方法之上的三组件框架。** (1) early-encoder precision：encoder 前三个 block 的激活保持 8 bit（权重仍为目标位宽），因为 encoder 只编码观测帧与目标、调用次数远少于 predictor；(2) control-path preservation：action embedder 与 AdaLN 控制通路保持浮点，把 ζ_t 直接归零；(3) rollout-aware scaling：用带步数权重 w_t = t / Σt′ 的 rollout 级损失联合优化 encoder 与"每步独立"的 predictor 激活范围。
- **给出跨模型、跨环境、跨基线的系统性验证。** 在 LeWM（从零训练的 ViT + AdaLN 注入动作）与 DINO-WM（冻结 DINOv2 + patch token 拼接动作）上、在 PushT / TwoRoom / Wall 三个任务上，对 SmoothQuant、DuQuant、DuQuant+GPTQ、RTN、OmniQuant 五种基线统一叠加 WM-Quant，报告 W3A3 与 W4A4 的规划成功率曲线。
- **逐组件消融 + 等比特预算对照。** 用 10 seed 的配对 t 检验证明 control-path preservation 与 rollout-aware scaling 是必需的（分别 −31.6 与 −19.6 个点），并用"随机选 3 个 encoder block"、"随机保留同等参数量层"两组对照排除"只是多给了比特预算"这一替代解释。
- **在 Jetson AGX Orin 上自写 W4A4 整数 kernel 做部署验证。** 报告端到端规划延迟、predictor/encoder 分段延迟、权重与峰值显存，证明三件套的额外开销在 1.5–3% 量级，平均加速比仅从 1.71× 降到 1.70×。

## Key Contributions

- **Reframes world-model quantization as a rollout error-propagation problem.** A first-order linearization of the predictor (Jacobians A_t = ∂pred/∂z, B_t = ∂pred/∂e) yields δ_{τ+n} ≈ Φ_{τ+n:τ} η_τ + Σ_s Φ_{τ+n:s+1}(B_s ζ_s + ε_{s+1}), cleanly attributing final rollout error to encoder, embedder, and predictor terms with different multiplier structures — the encoder term is multiplied by a single long-horizon propagator, while each predictor step's error gets its own shorter propagator.
- **WM-Quant: three components that can be bolted onto any static PTQ method.** Early-encoder 8-bit activations (weights stay at target width), FP control path, and rollout-aware joint calibration of encoder ranges plus per-rollout-step predictor ranges via Adam with a straight-through estimator.
- **The first PTQ method targeted at JEPA-style planning models**, as opposed to general LLM PTQ (SmoothQuant, OmniQuant, DuQuant, GPTQ, RTN) or diffusion/loop-specific work (PTQD, Q-Diffusion, Quamba, LoopQ, QuantWAMs), which the authors argue do not address loop-invariant latent planning rollouts.
- **A calibrated empirical claim about where the method does and does not work**: universal on LeWM, but only effective on DINO-WM when rotational transforms (DuQuant) are already in place, which the authors attribute to extreme activation outliers in the frozen DINOv2 encoder.
- **Deployment numbers on real edge hardware** (custom W4A4 CUDA kernels on Jetson AGX Orin), including the honest observation that only 1.54–2.12× speedup is realized rather than the nominal 4× of W4A4.

## Methodology

**Setup and notation.** The three JEPA components are formalized as z_t = enc_θ(o_t), e_t = emb_φ(a_t), and ẑ_{t+1} = pred_ψ(ẑ_{t-H+1:t}, e_{t-H+1:t}). A rollout starts at τ: observations up to τ are encoded, the predictor then feeds on its own outputs for t ≥ τ. Planning uses cross-entropy method (CEM): sample c candidate action sequences from a Gaussian, score them with latent cost ‖ẑ_T − ẑ_goal‖, keep top-k, refit, repeat m iterations, then execute actions from the final mean.

**Quantization scheme held constant across all methods.** Uniform symmetric per-channel quantization for weights, uniform asymmetric static per-tensor for activations, with clipping range [α, β] → scale s and zero point z; q = clamp(⌊r/s⌉ + z, 0, 2^b − 1), r̃ = s(q − z). This fixed backbone is important — it means the reported deltas isolate WM-Quant's three components rather than differing granularity choices.

**Error propagation analysis.** With η_t, ζ_t, ε_t as the encoder, embedder, and predictor quantization errors, the quantized rollout is z̃_t = enc(o_t) + η_t for t ≤ τ, ẽ_t = emb(a_t) + ζ_t, z̃_{t+1} = pred(z̃, ẽ) + ε_{t+1}. Defining δ_t = z̃_t − ẑ_t gives δ_{t+1} = [pred(z̃,ẽ) − pred(ẑ,e)] + ε_{t+1}. Linearizing around the full-precision rollout with A_t and B_t yields δ_{t+1} ≈ A_t δ_t + B_t ζ_t + ε_{t+1}, and unrolling gives Φ_{t:s} = A_{t-1}A_{t-2}···A_s and the closed form in Eq. (8). The authors explicitly flag that the linearization is exact only for small errors and is a heuristic otherwise.

**Component 1 — early-encoder precision.** Encoder error matters twice: it perturbs the current latent state and the goal latent used for cost evaluation, and η_t is multiplied by the full-horizon propagator Φ_{τ+n:τ}. Since the earliest encoder blocks are known to be the most quantization-sensitive (citing LRP-QViT), WM-Quant keeps activations of the first three encoder blocks at 8 bits while all other blocks use the target bit width for weights and activations. The cost argument is asymmetric invocation counts: on Wall each replanning step encodes 2 frames but evaluates the predictor 15,000 times (300 candidates × 10 CEM iterations × 5-step horizon).

**Component 2 — control-path preservation.** CEM samples closely related action sequences, so the ranking among them is determined by fine differences; even a small ζ_t can reorder candidates and drift the search distribution. The problem is aggravated in DINO-WM, where low-magnitude action and proprioceptive features are concatenated to much larger patch-token embeddings and collapse under a shared scale. WM-Quant therefore leaves the entire control path (action embedder plus the AdaLN modulation in LeWM) in floating point, driving ζ_t to zero at a parameter cost of ~1.5% on DINO-WM and ~7.5% on LeWM.

**Component 3 — rollout-aware scaling.** Standard PTQ calibrates encoder and predictor separately to minimize single-tensor output error, and assigns one set of per-tensor ranges reused across all rollout iterations — but activation distributions shift across steps (Fig. 5). WM-Quant assigns separate activation ranges per rollout step (two scalars per quantized unit, since smoothing/rotation transforms stay shared) and jointly optimizes encoder ranges with those per-step predictor ranges under L(Θ) = E_D Σ_{t=1}^{n} w_t ‖δ_{τ+t}(Θ)‖², with w_t = t / Σ_{t'} t' upweighting later steps. Optimization uses Adam through the full quantized rollout with a straight-through estimator for rounding.

**Evaluation protocol.** LeWM on PushT (contact-rich manipulation) and TwoRoom (navigation); DINO-WM on PushT and Wall. Only W3A3 and W4A4 are tested, since the authors argue lower bit widths degrade too severely to measure error-propagation effects differentially. LeWM results average 3 seeds; the DINO-WM main tables use **one** seed, justified by MPC compute cost; the LeWM ablation uses 10 seeds with paired t-intervals.

## Results & Highlights

- **LeWM PushT, W4A4, DuQuant+GPTQ base:** planning success goes from 12.7% (iter 1) / 20.0% (iter 2) to 66.0% / 81.3% with WM-Quant, i.e. Δ = +53.3 / +61.3 points, against FP16's 80.0% / 98.0%. Averaged over all five PTQ methods the W4A4 gain is +57.34 points. Note the residual gap to FP16 is still 14.0 points at iter 1 and 16.7 points at iter 2 — "approaching full precision" is directionally right but not tight.
- **LeWM PushT, W3A3 (8 representable values), DuQuant+GPTQ base:** 8.7% / 10.0% → 41.3% / 58.0% (Δ +32.6 / +48.0). The weakest absolute result on this benchmark is OmniQuant, base 6.7% / 10.7% → 32.7% / 52.7% (Δ +26.0 / +42.0), still far below FP16's 80.0% / 98.0%.
- **LeWM TwoRoom, W4A4:** gains are highly base-method dependent — SmoothQuant 28.7% → 70.0% at iter 1 (Δ +41.3), RTN 28.7% → 70.7% (Δ +42.0), but DuQuant only 42.7% → 55.3% (Δ +12.7), DuQuant+GPTQ 40.0% → 57.3% (Δ +17.3), and OmniQuant 28.0% → 38.0% (Δ +10.0).
- **A negative result the paper reports honestly:** on LeWM TwoRoom at W3A3 iter 1, OmniQuant *regresses* from 21.3% to 20.0% (Δ −1.3), the single non-positive delta in Tables 1–2.
- **DINO-WM Wall, W4A4 (FP16 = 48/94/94/94/94 at MPC iterations 1/5/10/15/20):** DuQuant+GPTQ goes 2/16/32/38/46 → 32/96/98/98/98 (Δ +30/+80/+66/+60/+52), i.e. it *exceeds* FP16 at iterations 5–20 — a striking but single-seed result. DuQuant alone goes 0/8/16/20/24 → 22/88/92/92/92 (Δ +22/+80/+76/+72/+68). At W3A3 with DuQuant+GPTQ the recovery is weaker: 0/6/12/12/12 → 10/24/38/44/44 (Δ +10/+18/+26/+32/+32).
- **The method's limit is visible on DINO-WM with non-rotational baselines:** SmoothQuant stays at 0/2/2/4/4 on Wall W4A4 (base 0/4/4/4/4, Δ −2 at iteration 15), RTN reaches only 2/4/4/4/4, and OmniQuant never leaves ~0 with a −2 delta at iterations 15/20. On DINO-WM PushT W4A4 all three remain at 0% success, versus DuQuant+GPTQ 0/2/2 → 28/36/42 (Δ +28/+34/+40) and DuQuant 0/0/0 → 6/8/12.
- **Action-ranking fidelity (Fig. 4, LeWM PushT W3A3, 64 CEM candidates, median over 16 start states):** Spearman rank correlation with the FP cost rises from 0.19 (DuQuant+GPTQ base) → 0.51 (+ control path only) → 0.61 (full WM-Quant); top-8 overlap rises 0.12 → 0.25 → 0.50. This is the cleanest mechanistic evidence in the paper that the control-path component is what repairs candidate ranking.
- **Ablation (Table 5, LeWM PushT, DuQuant+GPTQ, W3A3, 10 seeds, after 2 iterations):** full WM-Quant 56.0 ± 0.9; removing control-path preservation 24.4 ± 1.6 (−31.6, 95% CI [−35.3, −27.9]); removing rollout-aware scaling 36.4 ± 1.2 (−19.6, [−23.2, −16.0]); removing early-encoder precision 52.8 ± 2.3 (−3.2, [−7.6, +1.2], CI crosses zero); no WM-Quant at all 9.6 ± 1.1 (−46.4). Rollout-scaling internals: no training of ranges 34.6 ± 2.0 (−21.4), no encoder-range training 50.2 ± 2.2 (−5.8, [−10.7, −0.9]), ranges shared across steps 49.2 ± 4.0 (−6.8, [−15.3, +1.7]).
- **Equal-budget controls (Table 5):** random preserved layers with the same bit budget give 27.6 ± 2.0 (−28.4) versus 56.0 — so the gain is specific to preserving the *control* path, not to preserving *any* ~7.5% of parameters. Random 3 encoder blocks give 55.6 ± 1.9 (−0.4), i.e. on LeWM the "first three blocks" choice is not measurably better than random, only cheaper in spirit.
- **Encoder-block choice does matter on DINO-WM (Table 7, Wall W3A3, DuQuant+GPTQ, 3 seeds):** first 3 blocks 2.7/15.3/26.7/36.7 vs random 3 blocks 3.3/8.7/18.7/26.7 vs none 0.0/6.0/8.0/14.0 at iterations 1/5/10/20 — random-3 deltas are +0.7/−6.7/−8.0/−10.0 and none deltas −2.7/−9.3/−18.7/−22.7.
- **Deployment (Table 6, Jetson AGX Orin, custom W4A4 kernels):** LeWM end-to-end planning 78.6 ms FP16 → 48.2 ms (RTN, 1.63×) and with WM-Quant 49.6 ms (1.58×); 56.5 ms → 58.0 ms for DuQuant (1.39× → 1.36×). DINO-WM 3877 ms FP16 → 1824 → 1854 ms for RTN (2.12× → 2.09×) and 2525 → 2410 ms for DuQuant (1.54×, actually *faster* with WM-Quant at 1.61× because dropping control channels shrinks the DuQuant rotation blocks). Average over one CEM iteration: 1.71× without WM-Quant, 1.70× with. Peak-memory cost on LeWM is small but nonzero: encoder weights 4.45 → 4.94 MiB, predictor weights 7.15 → 8.77 MiB (9.06 → 10.68 for DuQuant), encoder activations 48.2 → 58.8 MiB; DINO-WM peak memory is unchanged (1649 → 1649 MiB) thanks to GEMM padding and a small control buffer.

## Authors & Credibility

William Hammer and Andrew Heuer (equal contribution), Nikhil Krishna, Matthew Grenier, and Ramtin Zand, all at the University of South Carolina. Ramtin Zand leads the group; Matthew Grenier is a co-author of "Fixing quantization with lightweight adapters" (CVPR 2026), so the team has a genuine quantization track record rather than a one-off entry. Funding is disclosed (NSF Grant 2340249, an NSF REU supplement CCF-2621730, plus NSF MRI 2320292 for the Theia cluster), which is consistent with the paper's measured scope: two model families, three small benchmarks, and a single edge device.

Positives for credibility: writing custom W4A4 CUDA kernels instead of quoting theoretical speedups; reporting per-iteration success curves rather than only cherry-picked final numbers; reporting the OmniQuant regression; running 10-seed paired t-intervals for the ablation and equal-bit-budget controls for both mixed-precision components; and explicitly stating the linearization is a heuristic. Negatives: the DINO-WM headline tables are single-seed, and the paper is competing on a benchmark set (PushT, TwoRoom, Wall) that is small and low-dimensional relative to the robotics deployment story the introduction motivates.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The reframing is real and useful: treating quantization error as something that passes through the predictor's state Jacobian across an MPC rollout is a sharper problem statement than "encoder is sensitive," and it correctly identifies why LLM PTQ intuitions (token discretization resets δ_t to zero) do not transfer. The three components, however, are individually unsurprising. "Keep sensitive early layers at higher precision" is textbook mixed-precision (they cite HAQ [47] but never compare against it); "keep the action path in FP" is selective precision; and "learn per-timestep activation ranges" is very close to what PTQD, Q-Diffusion, and Quamba already do for denoising/looping trajectories — the paper acknowledges these as related work but never tests a timestep-aware diffusion-PTQ scheme as a baseline, so the "rollout-aware" contribution is asserted rather than isolated from a generic per-iteration-range idea. The genuine technical contribution is the *joint* encoder + per-step predictor calibration under a rollout-level loss with step weights w_t = t/Σt′, and even there the ablation shows the step weighting itself is weakly supported: "ranges shared across steps" costs only −6.8 with a 95% CI of [−15.3, +1.7] that crosses zero, and "no encoder-range training" −5.8 with [−10.7, −0.9] is barely significant.

**Rigor.** The LeWM half is solid: 3 seeds for main results, 10 seeds for the ablation, paired t-intervals, and two equal-budget random controls that specifically rule out the "you just spent more bits" objection — the random-preserved-layers result (27.6 vs 56.0) is the single most convincing table entry in the paper. The DINO-WM half is much weaker: all of Table 3 and Table 4 are **one seed**, which is a serious problem when the headline claim is a jump from 2% to 32% at iteration 1 on Wall W4A4 and the reported deltas exceed FP16 at later iterations (96/98/98/98 vs FP16's 94/94/94). Beating full precision by 4 points on a single seed is within run-to-run MPC noise and should not be presented as a result. Second, the fixed hyperparameters — "first three encoder blocks," "control path entirely in FP" — are hand-chosen and never swept; Table 7 varies only which blocks, not how many, and the LeWM ablation shows the first-three choice is statistically indistinguishable from random-three (−0.4, CI [−4.4, +3.6]). Third, the error analysis linearizes around the FP rollout and the authors concede this "acts as a heuristic in cases with large errors" — precisely the W3A3 regime they care about, so the theory motivates but does not predict the measured gains. Fourth, calibration cost is never reported: Adam through a 5-step quantized rollout with STE is not free, and no wall-clock or GPU-hour number for the calibration pass appears anywhere.

**Reproducibility.** Mixed. The quantization scheme is fully specified (symmetric per-channel weights, asymmetric per-tensor static activations), the loss and optimizer are given, and the component definitions are precise enough to reimplement. But the CEM/MPC hyperparameters are inherited from LeWM and DINO-WM without a consolidated table, the number and source of calibration rollouts is described only as "a set of calibration rollouts recorded from the planner," seed handling is inconsistent (1 vs 3 vs 10) and the per-seed numbers are relegated to supplementary material not visible in this version, and no code release is mentioned.

**Significance.** The practical payoff is more modest than the framing suggests. A 1.54–1.71× end-to-end speedup at W4A4 on a Jetson AGX Orin is a long way from the 4× the bit width implies, and WM-Quant itself *costs* latency relative to the base quantized model on LeWM (48.2 → 49.6 ms, 56.5 → 58.0 ms). The paper's real value is diagnostic: it shows that naive per-step-optimal PTQ can leave a world model at 2–12% success while looking numerically fine on single predictions, and that the action-conditioning path is the dominant failure locus (Spearman 0.19 → 0.51 from that component alone). That is a finding worth propagating to anyone quantizing a planning model, independently of whether they adopt the full recipe.

**Red flags.** (1) Single-seed DINO-WM main results supporting the largest deltas in the paper, including results that beat FP16. (2) "Approaching full precision at W4A4" holds for LeWM but not DINO-WM, where SmoothQuant/RTN/OmniQuant remain at ~0% success on PushT even with WM-Quant — the abstract's universality claim is carried by the one baseline family (DuQuant) that happens to fix the frozen-encoder outlier problem the authors themselves diagnose. (3) On DINO-WM the control path is only ~1.5% of parameters but on LeWM it is ~7.5%, so the "low-bit W3A3" claim for the smaller model quietly keeps a meaningful fraction of the network in FP. (4) No comparison to mixed-precision PTQ (HAQ) or to timestep-aware diffusion PTQ, which are the two most obvious strong baselines. (5) Three small benchmarks (PushT, TwoRoom, Wall) with no real-robot or higher-dimensional control task, despite the robotics-motivation framing.

## Strengths

- Correctly identifies and formalizes a failure mode that single-step quantization metrics hide: quantization error compounding through the predictor Jacobian, with a clean decomposition into encoder, embedder, and predictor contributions.
- The Spearman/top-8-overlap analysis (Fig. 4) is the right measurement for the claim — it shows *why* planning collapses (candidate ranking) rather than only that success rate drops, and it isolates the control-path component's effect.
- Composable by construction: WM-Quant is validated as an add-on to five different static PTQ methods (SmoothQuant, DuQuant, DuQuant+GPTQ, RTN, OmniQuant) rather than as a replacement, which makes the deltas directly attributable.
- The equal-bit-budget controls are exactly the right ablation for a mixed-precision method, and the random-preserved-layers result (27.6 vs 56.0) convincingly refutes the "free bits" explanation.
- Honest reporting of the OmniQuant regression on TwoRoom W3A3, of the near-zero DINO-WM results for non-rotational baselines, and of the linearization's limited validity.
- Real hardware deployment with self-written W4A4 kernels, including a plausible explanation for why DuQuant gets *faster* with WM-Quant (fewer rotation blocks).
- Cheap in practice: 1.71× → 1.70× average speedup and unchanged DINO-WM peak memory, with the encoder-side cost justified by the 2-frames-vs-15,000-predictor-calls asymmetry.

## Limitations

- **DINO-WM main results are single-seed**, which undermines the paper's largest deltas — including the implausible-looking claim of exceeding FP16 at MPC iterations 5–20 on Wall W4A4.
- **Effectiveness is conditional on rotational transforms.** Without DuQuant, WM-Quant cannot rescue frozen-DINOv2 models (0% on PushT W4A4 for SmoothQuant/RTN/OmniQuant), so the framework does not fix the root cause of frozen-encoder outlier sensitivity — it only stops an already-rotated model from degrading further.
- **The three components are partly hand-set.** The number of early encoder blocks (3) is never swept, the control path is kept fully in FP rather than at an intermediate width, and on LeWM "first 3" is statistically indistinguishable from "random 3."
- **Rollout-aware scaling's own sub-ablations are weak**: shared-across-steps ranges cost only −6.8 with a CI crossing zero, so the per-step range novelty is not strongly separated from the cheaper alternative.
- **No calibration-cost accounting.** The Adam/STE optimization over full rollouts is the only trained component, and neither its wall-clock cost nor its sensitivity to the number of calibration rollouts is reported.
- **Modest end-to-end payoff and a small latency regression** on the base-quantized model; only W3A3 and W4A4 are tested, with lower widths dismissed rather than measured.
- **Benchmark scope is narrow**: PushT, TwoRoom, and Wall are small, low-dimensional, and use the latent-goal cost the authors themselves call "convenient but not necessary"; no real robot, no high-dimensional action space, no replanning-under-distribution-shift test.
- **No comparison against the most relevant prior art** — mixed-precision PTQ (HAQ) and timestep/iteration-aware diffusion PTQ (PTQD, Q-Diffusion, Quamba) — which are precisely the methods that would test whether "rollout-aware" is distinct from "per-iteration-aware."
- Missing ablations worth having: bit width for the control path (FP vs FP8/FP16), how many calibration rollouts and how long a rollout are needed, and whether the method survives a different CEM configuration (more candidates, longer horizon).

## Relevance

本文正落在「极端性能优化下的可靠性」这一核心关注点上：它把 PTQ 的失效模式从"单步精度下降"推进到"自回归 rollout 中误差逐步复合、导致候选动作排序退化为随机"，并给出了 rank correlation（0.19→0.61）这类可直接复用的诊断指标，对长程任务上量化退化的评测方法很有参考价值。虽然其缓解手段（早期层保精度、控制通路保 FP、每步范围重标定）偏工程，但"逐步骤/逐迭代的误差传播与敏感性分析"这一问题建模方式，也可迁移到 KV cache 压缩、推测解码等长程结构。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How large is the run-to-run variance on DINO-WM Wall W4A4? A 3–5 seed replication is needed to know whether "32/96/98/98/98 vs FP16 48/94/94/94/94" is a real improvement over full precision or noise.
- Is the per-rollout-step range learning (−6.8, CI [−15.3, +1.7]) distinguishable from a timestep-aware diffusion-PTQ scheme such as PTQD applied naively? Without that control, "rollout-aware" and "per-iteration-aware" remain conflated.
- What does the control path cost at intermediate precision? FP is the extreme; FP8 or a 6-bit control path would test whether ζ_t needs to be zero or merely small, and would change the LeWM 7.5%-of-parameters accounting materially.
- How does calibration cost scale — number of calibration rollouts, rollout horizon n, and whether the learned per-step ranges transfer across start states and goals?
- Does the Spearman-correlation diagnostic predict planning success generally? A sweep over bit widths plotting rank correlation against success rate would turn the paper's best figure into a usable design rule.
- Would the same rollout-error framing improve KV-cache quantization or speculative decoding, where the error also compounds over autoregressive steps but with token-level resets?
