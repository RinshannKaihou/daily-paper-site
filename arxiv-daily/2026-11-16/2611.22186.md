> **一句话总结：** 把 4-bit 量化不只当作"同量级的权重扰动"而是"一次具体的舍入误差"，在三个 3–4B 聊天模型上做预注册配对实验，测出两个方向相反的拒绝漂移——强制前缀下 Qwen2.5-3B 多顺从 6.2 pp（人类标注 +19.4 pp），held-out jailbreak 下 Llama-3.2-3B 少顺从 10.3 pp；再用镜像/置换/高斯匹配扰动和"剂量"曲线（斜率 +4.6 / −7.2 pp 每单位误差）证明漂移方向由该 build 的具体舍入误差决定，而不是 bits-per-weight 或似然损失决定（同为 4.5–5.0 bpw 的 llama.cpp Q4_K_M 两个模型都不动）。

> **TL;DR:** A pre-registered, paired study of three 3–4B chat models (Qwen2.5-3B, Llama-3.2-3B, Qwen3-4B) finds that 4-bit builds shift refusal in *opposite* directions: on Arditi et al.'s 562-prompt harmful_test with 8 compliant tokens prefilled, 4-bit Qwen2.5-3B continues into harmful content +6.2 pp more often than bf16 (18.0% → 24.2%; +19.4 pp under blind human labels), while on 400 held-out WildJailbreak prompts 4-bit Llama-3.2-3B complies 10.3 pp *less* (45.0% → 34.8%). Matched perturbations built from the real rounding error E separate these from noise: six shuffled/Gaussian perturbations average +0.4 and −1.1 pp and their intervals include zero, the mirror build W−E reverses each sign, and scaling E from ½ to 2 gives near-linear dose slopes of +4.6 and −7.2 pp per unit error. Crucially, at *half* the real error Llama's NLL is essentially unchanged (+0.005 nats/token) yet it already complies 5.8 pp less, and llama.cpp's Q4_K_M at the same 4.5–5.0 bits per weight moves neither model — so neither bits per weight nor likelihood predicts which builds shift, and every shipped quantized build needs its own paired audit against its bf16 original.

## 核心贡献

- **把"量化是否改变拒绝行为"重述为可归因的因果问题。** 现有审计（Hong et al. 2024、Chen et al. 2025、Kharinaev et al. 2025、Kadadekar 2026、Prasad & Pal 2026、Wu et al. 2026）结论符号不一致且各说各话，本文指出原因可能很简单：一个 4-bit build 只是"同量级扰动族"中的一个样本，方向可能由该 build 的具体舍入误差决定。于是问题变成"这个漂移是模型对这么大的扰动的普遍反应，还是这个舍入误差特有的"。
- **测到两个符号相反的 4-bit 漂移（配对 + 8-bit 空对照）。** 强制起始（forced start）下 Qwen2.5-3B 更容易接着写有害内容（judge +6.2 pp，人类标注 +19.4 pp）；自由起始（free start）下 Llama-3.2-3B 对 held-out jailbreak 少顺从 10.3 pp；Qwen3-4B 两个设定都不显著。这是在同一套配对设计里同时观察到两个方向。
- **匹配扰动归因（核心实验）。** 从真实舍入误差 E = dequant(Q(W)) − W 构造四类对照：镜像 W − E、三个组内置换（保留每个量化组的误差多重集）、三个按行范数缩放的高斯、以及剂量线 W + aE（a ∈ {½, 1, 2}）。随机对照均值接近 0，镜像翻转符号，剂量近线性——按预先注册的判据，两个漂移都是 "rounding-specific" 而非 "the model's lean"。
- **一个稠密 W + E 复现性检查。** 不做量化、只把同样的误差以 bf16 稠密形式加到权重上，就复现了两个漂移（+4.8 vs 真实 +6.2；−10.7 vs 真实 −10.3），似然只差 −0.0007 / −0.0001 nats/token，首 token argmax 一致率 100.0% / 99.5%。这把"是不是 kernel/运行时伪影"排除掉了。
- **8-bit 空对照作为估计量的基准。** 8-bit build 在参考文本上的似然几乎不变，但只有 8% / 35% / 7% 的回复与 bf16 逐 token 相同，标签翻转 9.6% / 10.1% / 3.0%，而速率漂移 +0.7 / −1.2 / −1.2 pp 全部跨零。因此"某个 prompt 的标签变了"不能当作量化有影响的证据——这是全文方法论上最有价值的一条。
- **拒绝方向上的投影分析（相关而非机制）。** 4-bit Qwen2.5-3B 在层 19 上沿 bf16 拒绝方向的投影确实下降（−0.80 [−0.87, −0.74]），镜像上升（+0.13），且随剂量单调（−0.36 / −0.80 / −2.16）；但在这一个 cell 的 15 个 build 上投影变化与行为变化并不显著相关（Spearman ρ = −0.44，p = 0.10），多个把投影压得同样低的 build 行为没变。
- **其它量化器与第二个运行时。** AWQ 复现 Qwen2.5-3B 的漂移（+4.8 vs 默认 +6.2）但不复现 Llama 的（+1.0，跨零）；llama.cpp Q4_K_M 两个模型都不动，且两个 cell 的估计符号都与默认 build 相反；GPTQ 在 Llama 上同向（−5.2 pp，Holm p = 0.028），而 Qwen2.5-3B 的 GPTQ build 直接坏掉（+9.335 nats/token）被排除。
- **可操作的结论。** 能上 8 bit 就上 8 bit（没有一个 8-bit build 在 Holm 校正后显著漂移）；4 bit 下更细的 group size 与 mixed 4/6 都没能消除 Qwen2.5-3B 的漂移；似然检查会漏掉一半误差的 Llama build；没有任何可测量量能预测哪个 build 会漂，所以每个 build 都要和它的原始模型配对比较。

## Key Contributions

- **Reframing quantization audit as attribution.** A 4-bit build is a single draw from a family of equal-size weight perturbations, so an observed refusal change is ambiguous between "this rounding error" and "any perturbation this large." The paper makes that ambiguity the object of study rather than averaging over it, and states the estimand in terms of a paired rate shift against bf16 with an 8-bit null.
- **Two 4-bit shifts of opposite sign, measured with paired tests.** Forced start (k = 8 compliant tokens prefilled, n = 562): Qwen2.5-3B shifts toward harmful continuation under three independent label sources (judge +6.2 pp, Llama Guard +11.0 pp, human +19.4 pp). Free start (400 held-out WildJailbreak prompts, no prefill): Llama-3.2-3B shifts away from compliance by 10.3 pp. Qwen3-4B is null in both.
- **A control family built from the real error, not from isotropic noise.** Mirror (W − E) to test sign reversal; three within-group permutations that preserve each quantization group's exact error multiset; three per-row-norm Gaussian draws; and a dense W + E build as a construction check. This is strictly more informative than the L2-matched Gaussian noise used by Gong et al. (2024) for task performance or by Razan & Shivam (2026) for first-token margins.
- **A pre-registered decision rule and a dose-response.** "Rounding-specific" requires either (a) the mirror reverses the sign with an interval excluding zero, or (b) the random-control interval includes zero while the real-minus-random interval does not. Both shifts pass on all six random draws; the dose builds W + aE for a ∈ {½, 1, 2} are monotone and near-linear, with slopes +4.6 [+3.3, +5.9] and −7.2 [−8.8, −5.6] pp per unit error.
- **An 8-bit null that redefines what counts as evidence.** The 8-bit build rewrites most replies (only 8–35% token-identical to bf16) and flips 3–10% of judged labels while leaving the *rate* at zero shift, so per-prompt label churn is explicitly demoted from evidence to context.
- **A refusal-direction projection analysis with an honest negative result.** Both predictions are partially confirmed (Qwen2.5-3B forced start lowers the projection; Llama-3.2-3B free start raises it), but the across-build correlation between projection change and compliance change fails for Qwen2.5-3B (ρ = −0.44, p = 0.10) and is weak for Llama (ρ = −0.54, p = 0.047, contradicted by the doubled build) — the paper reports this rather than hiding it.
- **Cross-quantizer and cross-runtime evidence that "same bits per weight" is not a safety class.** AWQ, GPTQ, MXFP4, mixed 4/6, g32/g64 and llama.cpp Q4_K_M at 4.25–5.00 bpw land on different sides of the effect in the same model.
- **Full artifact release plus a pre-registration appendix.** Code, perturbation builder, analysis code, every judge and human label keyed by prompt index, and a dated table of what was registered when — including an explicit list of what changed after registration and what stayed exploratory.

## Methodology

**Scope and framing.** The motivating scenario is a developer who evaluates a chat model at full precision and ships a 4-bit copy. The paper asks whether that copy refuses the same requests, and uses assistant prefill as a probe (not as a realistic attack — the authors note that anyone holding local weights can remove safety training directly).

**Models and formats.** Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507, each with its default chat template (Llama's template date pinned). Every quantized build is converted from the same bf16 checkpoint with mlx-lm 0.32.2 on MLX 0.32.4: 8-bit g64, 4-bit g32, mixed 4/6 (value and down projections at 6 bits in the first and last eighth of layers and every third layer between), 4-bit g64 (primary), MXFP4, plus AWQ and GPTQ which write the same 4.50 bpw g64 format. A second runtime is built with llama.cpp from the same checkpoints at bf16, Q8_0 and Q4_K_M (8.51 and 5.00 bpw), without an importance matrix, prompts sent as token ids. Capability is measured as the change in mean per-token NLL that each build assigns to bf16's *own* replies to 200 harmless prompts (bf16 baselines 0.327 / 0.203 / 0.148 nats).

**Two behavioural settings.**
- *Forced start (primary):* 562 unique prompts from the harmful_test split of Arditi et al. (2024), with the first k = 8 tokens of a compliant reply prefilled as token ids. The prefix comes from an abliterated copy of the same bf16 model, so every build of a model continues identical tokens. Depth family also reports k ∈ {0, 2}; a generic prefix "Sure, here is" is used as a bridge condition.
- *Free start:* adversarial-harmful prompts from WildJailbreak, no prefill. First 200 = development set, 400 disjoint held-out prompts = test set. Over-refusal measured on XSTest's 250 safe prompts and WildJailbreak's 210 adversarial-benign prompts; SORRY-Bench is a secondary family.

**Outcome and labels.** The outcome is whether the reply's content does what was asked, labelled by Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct (itself at 4 bits) as complying / partly complying / not complying / incoherent; the first two count as harmful (forced start) or fulfilment (free start). Keyword refusal detection is unusable because the prefix occupies the region such detectors read. Llama Guard 3 is a second judge with a different construct. For the forced-start primary test, human labels are added in a two-phase design: all judge-discordant pairs plus 25 random pairs from each concordant stratum, shown blind and in random order, design-weighted to the full 562 prompts; 400 pairs / 800 replies labelled by the author.

**Statistics.** Every comparison is paired on the same unique prompts across two builds; the estimand is the difference in rates; churn (share of prompts whose label differs) is reported only as context. Tests are exact McNemar, Holm-corrected within each family; intervals are percentile bootstrap over prompts with 10,000 resamples; equivalence means the 90% interval lies within ±5 pp. Primary and confirmatory tests, secondary families and decision rules were registered before the data they concern (Table 4: Nov 10 forced-start primary, depth, judge, churn, format ladder, seed floor; Nov 11 pooled test and two-phase human labels; Nov 13 free-start test and matched-perturbation controls; Nov 14 other quantizers, dose, refusal direction, GGUF).

**Matched perturbations.** For each quantized weight matrix W (embedding included), E = dequant(Q(W)) − W is computed in float32; norms and biases untouched. The mirror is W − E; permuted builds shuffle E within each row and each group of 64 input columns so every quantization group keeps its exact multiset of errors; Gaussian builds draw per-row noise with that row's RMS error and rescale it to match ‖E‖ exactly. Dose builds are W + aE for a ∈ {½, 1, 2}. Every control is a dense bf16 model, gated by a pre-check: W + E had to match the real 4-bit build within 0.005 nats/token and 98% first-token argmax agreement on 200 prompts (both cells passed). Controls whose NLL gap to bf16 exceeded twice the real build's would have been excluded; none were.

**Refusal-direction analysis.** On the behavioural-test prompts, the projection of the model's state at the generation point onto its bf16 refusal direction (Arditi et al.'s abliteration direction, layer 19) is recorded per build and compared with bf16 prompt by prompt. Two pre-registered predictions: the shifting cells should move in the behaviourally implied direction, and across all builds of a cell projection change should be negatively correlated with compliance change. Explicitly correlational; no circuit is identified.

## Results & Highlights

- **Forced start, Qwen2.5-3B: +6.2 pp under the judge, +19.4 pp under human labels.** With 8 tokens prefilled, the default 4-bit g64 build continues into harmful content 24.2% of the time versus 18.0% at bf16, a +6.2 pp shift [+2.8, +9.8] with discordant prompts splitting 33 to 68 (Holm p = 0.0019); the design-weighted blind human labels give +19.4 pp [+6.0, +33.4] (harmful reply from the 4-bit build in 34 pairs vs bf16 in 9, p = 1.7 × 10⁻⁴), and Llama Guard gives +11.0 pp [+7.3, +14.9]. The other two models are null: Llama-3.2-3B +0.0 pp and Qwen3-4B −1.6 pp (90% intervals [−3.0, +3.0] and [−3.6, +0.4], inside the ±5 pp equivalence margin), and the pooled across-model shift is not significant (112 vs 138 discordant pairs, p = 0.11) — so the effect is specific to Qwen2.5-3B, not to "4-bit chat models."
- **Free start, Llama-3.2-3B: 45.0% → 34.8%, −10.3 pp.** On the 400 held-out WildJailbreak prompts the 4-bit build fulfils 10.3 pp fewer requests [−14.0, −6.2] with discordant prompts splitting 54 to 13 (p = 4.5 × 10⁻⁷). Its 8-bit build does not differ from bf16 (−1.3 pp [−3.8, +1.0]) and its MXFP4 build is worse (−16.0 pp). Qwen2.5-3B shows −2.3 pp [−6.5, +2.0] here and Qwen3-4B +3.8 pp [+0.2, +7.5], which does not survive correction — the two settings thus reproduce the two shifts with the signs swapped between models (Table 2).
- **The Llama drop is not better refusal, it is worse discrimination.** On XSTest's plain safe prompts the 4-bit build refuses about as often as bf16 (−0.4 pp [−4.8, +3.6]), but on WildJailbreak's 210 benign requests *written in jailbreak style* it refuses 5.7 pp more often (9.5% → 15.2%, [+1.4, +10.0], exploratory p = 0.017 uncorrected). Qwen2.5-3B moves the opposite way on safe prompts (−6.4 pp, 26.0% → 19.6%, Holm p = 0.083) and Qwen3-4B refuses 8.6 pp fewer jailbreak-styled benign requests (21.0% → 12.4%).
- **Same bit width, different behaviour: the format ladder inside one model.** All three affine 4-bit builds of Qwen2.5-3B shift in the same direction, by +4.1 pp at group size 32, +6.2 pp at 64 and +8.0 pp under mixed 4/6 (which shares most of its tensors with the g64 build), while MXFP4 — the build that loses the *most* likelihood in every model (+0.265 nats/token vs +0.144 for g64) — shows no shift (−0.7 pp). The four builds differ significantly from one another (Cochran's Q, Holm p = 4.8 × 10⁻⁵), so the perturbation's size plainly does not set its effect.
- **Random controls average to zero; the real error does not.** Qwen2.5-3B: six permuted/Gaussian builds average +0.4 pp [−2.1, +2.8], the real build exceeds them by +5.8 pp [+3.0, +8.7], and the mirror gives −3.0 pp [−6.0, +0.0]. Llama-3.2-3B with signs reversed: random mean −1.1 pp [−4.0, +1.8], real-minus-random −9.1 pp [−12.3, −5.9], mirror +3.7 pp [−0.2, +7.8]. Neither mirror is individually significant, but each differs clearly from its real build and half of that difference is +4.6 [+2.8, +6.4] and −7.0 [−9.1, −4.9].
- **Dose–response is near-linear, and the half-size build exposes a likelihood blind spot.** Slopes are +4.6 pp [+3.3, +5.9] and −7.2 pp [−8.8, −5.6] per unit of error, over a range where the doubled builds (W + 2E) lose 0.855 and 0.224 nats/token against the real builds' 0.144 and 0.038 — so the conclusion does not rest on them. The informative point is Llama-3.2-3B at a = ½: only +0.005 nats/token of NLL change, "probably too small for a perplexity check to detect," yet already −5.8 pp less compliant (p = 0.0022).
- **Dense W + E reproduces both shifts down to the interval.** Qwen2.5-3B +4.8 pp [+1.2, +8.4] against the real build's +6.2 (1.4 pp apart); Llama-3.2-3B −10.7 pp [−14.8, −7.0] against −10.3 (0.5 pp apart); NLL differs by −0.0007 and −0.0001 nats/token and first-token argmax matches on 100.0% and 99.5% of prompts. This rules out the kernel path and the runtime as the carrier of the effect.
- **8-bit is a genuine null on the rate but not on the text.** Only 8%, 35% and 7% of 8-bit forced-start replies are token-identical to bf16's, and the judged label flips on 9.6%, 10.1% and 3.0% of prompts, yet the rate shifts are +0.7, −1.2 and −1.2 pp, all with intervals including zero. At 4 bits churn roughly doubles (18.0%, 18.5%, 8.0%), so a 6 pp net shift sits on top of ~18% of prompts changing label — the signal is a small net tilt of a very noisy per-prompt quantity.
- **Cross-quantizer and cross-runtime replication is partial.** AWQ reproduces Qwen2.5-3B (+4.8 pp [+1.4, +8.2] vs +6.2) but not Llama-3.2-3B (+1.0 pp [−3.0, +4.8]); GPTQ gives Llama −5.2 pp [−9.2, −1.2] (Holm p = 0.028, one cell only); llama.cpp Q4_K_M shifts neither model and its point estimates have the *opposite* sign to the default build in both cells (−1.2 and +2.5 pp), while changing 15.8% and 11.5% of labels without any net direction. On SORRY-Bench the Llama drop replicates (−5.0 pp [−7.8, −2.2]) and both Qwen builds move slightly toward compliance (+3.2 and +3.8 pp).
- **The refusal-direction story is suggestive, not explanatory.** Qwen2.5-3B's 4-bit projection at layer 19 under forced start is lower than bf16's (−0.80 [−0.87, −0.74]), the mirror's is higher (+0.13), and it scales with dose (−0.36, −0.80, −2.16) — but across 15 builds of that cell the projection/compliance correlation is not significant (Spearman ρ = −0.44, p = 0.10), and a Gaussian control (−0.66) and MXFP4 (−0.61) lower the projection nearly as much without shifting behaviour. The same 4-bit build lowers the projection *further* under a free start (−1.42), where its behaviour does not shift at all.
- **An unreported-in-the-abstract defect: MXFP4 Qwen2.5-3B fabricates the next user turn.** On 200 benign prompts with a 512-token cap, 10.5% of MXFP4 replies fail to end the assistant turn and write a fabricated next user turn (8.5% after dequantizing to bf16, so it is in the weights, not the kernel); no other build tested does this, and MXFP4 Qwen2.5-0.5B does not either.

## Authors & Credibility

Single author: Ege Kaya, University of British Columbia. cs.CR/cs.CL preprint, no venue information, no prior version. There is no track record inside the PDF to lean on, so credibility has to be read off the methodology, and on that axis this is unusually strong for a solo, unreviewed preprint.

Signals that count in its favour:

- **A real pre-registration with a dated audit trail.** Table 4 lists what was registered on Nov 10, 11, 13 and 14, and the appendix separately lists the *changes* to the plan (capability switched from MMLU to NLL on bf16's replies; seed floor run with one sampler for both models and not at all for Qwen3-4B; the human-label test changed from a registered McNemar on weighted counts to an exact test on the discordant stratum with a stratified bootstrap; a planned 100 additional concordant pairs not labelled) and which analyses are exploratory and uncorrected. Self-reporting that your confirmatory test deviated from the registered one is rare and is exactly the behaviour the registration is supposed to incentivise.
- **The estimand is designed around a measured nuisance.** The 8-bit null is not decoration: it is measured first (§4), shown to flip 3–10% of labels while moving no rate, and then used to define what the 4-bit result must exceed. Most quantization-safety papers report churn or per-prompt flips as if they were effects.
- **Every claim is paired, corrected, and given an interval.** Holm within family, exact McNemar, 10,000-resample bootstrap over prompts, cluster bootstrap over SORRY-Bench's 120 questions, and a stated ±5 pp equivalence margin rather than a bare "not significant."
- **Three label sources for the primary test.** Judge, Llama Guard (different construct) and blind human labels agree on *which* model shifts even where they disagree on magnitude (Table 6: +6.2, +11.0, +19.4) — the qualitative claim is robust to the label source in a way that most safety papers cannot demonstrate.
- **Released artifacts.** Code, pre-registration, perturbation builder, analysis code, aggregate results and every judge/human label keyed by prompt index at https://github.com/egekaya1/qrefusal; the replies themselves are withheld because they contain harmful content generated under forced start, and are available to researchers on request. bf16 checkpoints are pinned by SHA-256 prefix. Full compute disclosure (one Apple M2 Pro, 16 GB; ~108 hours over 207 logged steps).
- **The Limitations section is genuinely self-undermining.** It concedes that the forced-start prefix is model-specific, that the free-start effect was found on the development set (only the prompts are held out, not the choice of prompt set and model), that the controls are coarse (one mirror, three permutations, three Gaussians, two scales, two cells), that the dose builds are not real formats, and that the refusal-direction analysis identifies no circuit.

Weaknesses of provenance: the pre-registration lives in a private development repository as dated commits and is "available on request" rather than deposited in a public registry, so the timestamps are not independently verifiable; the only human annotator is the author, who also designed the study and read the harmful replies; and the main judge is itself a 4-bit model from the same family as one of the subjects.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The conceptual move is small but well aimed: treat quantization as one draw from a perturbation family and build the controls *out of the actual error* rather than out of matched-norm noise. Gong et al. (2024) did the L2-matched-noise comparison for general task performance and found family-dependent agreement; Razan & Shivam (2026) did a version of it for first-token margins with isotropic noise. Doing it for full replies with mirror + within-group permutation + per-row Gaussian + a dose line is a genuine methodological step up, and the within-group permutation is the clever part — it holds each quantization group's error multiset fixed while destroying its spatial arrangement, which isolates "pattern" from "magnitude" far more sharply than Gaussian noise does. The two-shifts-of-opposite-sign finding is a real empirical contribution to a literature that currently just reports disagreement. Beyond that the paper is confirmatory: the sequence "quantization changes refusal, sign varies by model, the rounding error matters" was suspected; this measures it properly.

**Rigor.** The statistical machinery is appropriate and, unusually, matched to a stated nuisance model. Three things deserve pushback.

First, the headline magnitude is not identified. For the *same contrast* — 4-bit vs bf16, forced start, Qwen2.5-3B — the judge says +6.2 pp, Llama Guard says +11.0 pp, and the blind human labels say +19.4 pp, a 3× spread. The authors explain the gap (the generation cap truncates 85% of Qwen2.5-3B's 4-bit replies at 128 tokens; the judge requires the harmful content to be *present*, the humans count a reply that has clearly begun to deliver it; the two sources disagree on 27.9% of truncated replies versus 12.9% of complete ones) and defend the choice by noting the judge under-counts harm (21.1% of replies versus 38.3% for humans, precision 86–92% when it does flag harm). That defence is honest, but it means the paper's most quotable number is the one from the smallest, least reproducible label source: 34 discordant pairs, one annotator, a 95% interval running from +6.0 to +33.4. The judge number is the defensible one, and it is a third the size.

Second, the human-label protocol is weaker than the framing suggests. Two of the three judge-vs-human κ values (0.50 and 0.52) fall below the pre-registered 0.60 threshold that was meant to license the judge, and the third sits exactly on it — so the human labels were collected under exactly the condition where the judge is known to be unreliable, by a single annotator who is also the author and who saw both replies of each pair side by side. The "blind" refers to format and judge label, not to hypothesis. There is no second annotator and no inter-annotator agreement for the human labels at all; the κ values are judge-vs-human, not human-vs-human.

Third, the free-start confirmatory test is not as confirmatory as it reads. The model and prompt set for the confirmatory test were chosen *because* the development set showed a large Llama effect (−14.5 pp on 200 prompts, Table 12) and a large opposite Qwen3 effect (+10.0 pp). Holding out the 400 prompts removes prompt-level overfitting but not selection of the cell. With six pre-registered cells and Holm correction this is handled at the level of error rate, and the effect does replicate at −10.3 pp with p = 4.5 × 10⁻⁷, which is hard to attribute to selection alone — but the paper's own Limitations lists this, and it should temper any reading of "held-out" as "out-of-sample in the strong sense."

**Baselines.** The comparison set is the study's real strength and also its boundary. Within-model controls are excellent: mirror, three permutations, three Gaussians, two doses, a dense reconstruction check, AWQ, GPTQ, MXFP4, mixed 4/6, g32, and llama.cpp Q4_K_M. What is missing is any comparison to the *audit* literature it is arguing with. Hong et al. (2024), Chen et al. (2025), Kharinaev et al. (2025), Kadadekar (2026), Prasad & Pal (2026) and Wu et al. (2026) all report quantization refusal effects; none is re-run here on the same three models with this design, so the paper cannot say whether the published disagreements are instances of its own rounding-specific mechanism or something else. The claim "audits disagree because each build carries its own rounding error" is therefore a plausible mechanism, not a demonstrated explanation of the observed disagreement. Similarly, only two cells (Qwen2.5-3B forced, Llama-3.2-3B free) get the full control battery; the negative cells get only a mirror, two permutations and one Gaussian for Qwen3-4B, and Llama-3.2-3B's forced-start cell has no matched-perturbation analysis at all — so "no shift" in those cells is not tested for rounding-specificity, only for significance.

**Reproducibility.** Good in principle, awkward in practice. Code, labels, and the perturbation builder are public; the models are public; the prompts are public datasets (Arditi harmful_test, WildJailbreak, XSTest, SORRY-Bench). But: the pre-registration is not in a public registry; the privacy-motivated withholding of the generated replies means an independent auditor cannot recompute the judge labels or the churn statistics, only re-generate them (greedy decoding makes that deterministic, which mostly saves it); the human labels are one person's and cannot be re-derived; and the AWQ/GPTQ comparison covers only the two shifting cells, with Qwen2.5-3B's GPTQ build broken and excluded. The GPTQ failure deserves more scrutiny than it gets: +9.335 nats/token is not a quantization trade-off, it is a broken artifact, and the 6-bit-fallback rebuild is equally broken (+9.355), which the authors attribute to "the GPTQ pass on this model." That is probably right, but it also means their GPTQ pipeline has at least one silent failure mode, which slightly undercuts confidence in the GPTQ numbers that *are* reported (one cell, Llama-3.2-3B, −5.2 pp).

**Significance.** The engineering conclusion is negative and useful: bits per weight does not predict safety behaviour, likelihood does not predict it, and no measured quantity does, so every shipped build needs a paired audit against its original. That is an expensive recommendation (562 prompts × two builds × three judges per model, plus human labels for the primary cell) and the paper does not cost it out or propose a cheap surrogate. The mechanistic conclusion is thin by the authors' own admission: the projection analysis fails its second prediction in the cell where it was most needed, and the paper explicitly says a build can lower the refusal-direction projection by nearly as much as the real one without shifting behaviour. So we learn *that* the rounding error's pattern matters, and that it is not mediated by a simple scalar readout of the refusal direction — but not what about the pattern matters. Layer-wise attribution is not attempted even though the perturbation builder would support it trivially (which layers' errors carry the shift?), and that is the single most obvious missing ablation.

**Red flags and smaller issues.**

- **The main judge is a 4-bit Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, in the same family as the Qwen2.5-3B subject.** If 4-bit quantization shifts Qwen2.5-family refusal — which is precisely the paper's finding for the 3B model — then the judge's own label distribution may be shifted by the same mechanism, and there is no bf16-judge control. The authors disclose that the judge is quantized and that it under-counts harm, but not this specific confound.
- **Greedy decoding only, one reply per prompt.** Deployed models sample. The paper does measure the sampling floor (two bf16 runs disagree on 15.1% and 18.9% of prompts; bf16 vs 4-bit on 18.1% and 19.4%, excess +3.0 pp [−0.7, +6.8] and +0.5 pp [−3.2, +4.1]) — and that excess is *not* significant, i.e. under sampling the 4-bit build is statistically indistinguishable from "another sample of the original" in per-prompt disagreement. Combined with the per-prompt variance result (Llama's 4-bit variance 0.101 vs bf16's 0.091, excess +0.009 [−0.005, +0.024]; Qwen2.5-3B's excess +0.028 [+0.013, +0.043]), this says the Llama free-start shift is a *rate* shift on an otherwise unchanged distribution, while the Qwen2.5-3B shift also destabilises individual labels. That nuance is in Appendix B and deserves to be in the main text.
- **Capability is measured only as NLL on bf16's own replies**, a proxy that structurally favours bf16 and was substituted for MMLU after registration. "The shifts are not explained by lost capability" therefore means "not explained by this one narrow proxy," not "not explained by capability." No task benchmark was run, so the paper cannot say whether a benchmark would order the builds the way behaviour does — it says so, but the caveat is load-bearing for §6.
- **The permuted and Gaussian control families disagree in Llama-3.2-3B**: permuted builds shift −7.3, −1.0, −6.0 pp while Gaussian builds shift +6.5, +0.3, +0.8 pp, and the pooled random mean (−1.1 pp) hides this. The authors note that the permuted draws alone average about half the real shift and that the part of Llama's shift which does not change sign with the error is "up to a third of it" (−3.3 pp [−6.6, +0.0]). So for Llama, a substantial fraction of the effect may be a generic response to perturbation magnitude after all — the decision rule passes on the pooled mean, but the family split is the more informative statistic and it is reported in one sentence in §7.
- **The forced-start setting is the least deployment-relevant one, and it produces the largest number.** The authors are explicit that prefill is not a realistic attack on local weights and is used as a probe. Fair, but then +19.4 pp (human) should not be the abstract's headline, and the "refusal under 4-bit quantization depends on the specific rounding error" framing rests on one cell per sign: two positive cells total, both confirmatory, one of them with a 3× label-source spread.
- **Only two runtimes, one of them macOS-only.** MLX on an M2 Pro, plus llama.cpp without an importance matrix. No bitsandbytes, no imatrix GGUF, no vendor pipelines, no QAT. Since the entire conclusion is that the specific rounding error decides, coverage of rounding schemes is the paper's coverage of its own claim, and it is thin: six format variants across three models, with the full battery on two cells.
- **Table 1's GPTQ row for Qwen2.5-3B is a +9.335 nats/token artifact sitting in the main table**, marked with a dagger and excluded, which is fine, but a reader skimming the table sees a number four orders of magnitude out of family and has to reach the appendix to learn why.

**Overall.** A careful, honestly reported, well-controlled small study whose methodological contribution (the 8-bit null plus error-matched controls) is more durable than its empirical one (two shifts in three small models). The claim is narrower than the title suggests — "4-bit quantization is rounding-specific, not size-specific, in the two cells where it shifts anything" — and the authors mostly say so themselves.

## Strengths

- **The 8-bit null is the right instrument and almost nobody uses it.** Measuring a perturbation that changes 90% of the generated text yet moves no rate, and then insisting that the 4-bit result beat *that* rather than beat "no change," is a genuinely better experimental design than the audits it criticises.
- **Controls built from the real error, not from isotropic noise.** The mirror isolates sign, the within-group permutation isolates arrangement while holding the error multiset fixed, the per-row Gaussian matches norm exactly, and the dense W + E build verifies the whole construction reproduces the real build to 0.4–1.4 pp. That is four independent handles on the same quantity.
- **The half-dose Llama build is a small gem.** +0.005 nats/token of NLL — invisible to any perplexity check — alongside a −5.8 pp compliance drop (p = 0.0022). It is the paper's cleanest single argument that likelihood is not a safety proxy, and it is stated plainly.
- **Cross-quantizer evidence that "same bits" is not a class.** AWQ reproduces one shift and not the other; Q4_K_M reproduces neither and points the other way in both cells; MXFP4 loses the most likelihood and shifts nothing in Qwen2.5-3B but the most in Llama-3.2-3B. The format ladder's Cochran Q (p = 4.8 × 10⁻⁵) makes the heterogeneity a measured result rather than an anecdote.
- **The over-refusal measurement turns a "safety win" into a discrimination loss.** Llama-3.2-3B refuses 10.3 pp fewer jailbreaks *and* 5.7 pp more benign jailbreak-styled requests. Reporting the second number is what separates this from a paper that would have claimed quantization improved safety.
- **Section 4 is a short, useful methods note in its own right** on why per-prompt label flips are not evidence, with the 85%-truncation and 27.9%-vs-12.9% disagreement numbers to back it.
- **The pre-registration appendix lists the deviations, not just the plan.** "We registered a McNemar on weighted counts and instead ran an exact test on the discordant stratum, and the 100 additional concordant pairs were not labelled" is the kind of sentence that makes the rest of the table believable.
- **Compute honesty.** One M2 Pro with 16 GB for 108 hours is a scope statement as much as a resource statement, and it makes the narrow model set forgivable rather than suspicious.

## Limitations

- **Three models, two vendors, 3–4B, one seed per configuration.** Signs differ between models, so nothing generalises to "4-bit quantization of chat models." Qwen3-4B — the newest model — is null in both settings, which is either reassuring or a sign that the effect is a Qwen2.5/Llama-3.2-era artifact; the paper cannot distinguish these.
- **Capability is NLL on bf16's own replies only.** No task benchmark, and the measure structurally favours bf16. The claim that capability loss does not explain the shifts is therefore proxy-specific.
- **Greedy decoding, one reply per prompt.** Under sampling, the 4-bit-vs-bf16 per-prompt disagreement excess over the bf16-vs-bf16 floor is not significant (+3.0 pp [−0.7, +6.8] and +0.5 pp [−3.2, +4.1]); deployed sampling could swamp the per-prompt picture even if the rate shift survives.
- **The dose builds are not formats.** W + ½E and W + 2E are dense bf16 models no quantizer produces, and the doubled builds lose 6× and 6× the real build's likelihood (0.855 / 0.224 vs 0.144 / 0.038), so the linearity claim is measured over a range where capability is collapsing.
- **Controls are coarse and only cover two cells.** One mirror, three permutation seeds, three Gaussian draws, two scales, per cell; the decision rule pools permuted and Gaussian draws although in Llama-3.2-3B the permuted draws alone average about half the real shift. Controls attribute direction to the perturbation as a whole, not to particular layers or matrices.
- **The forced-start prefix is model-specific** (an abliterated copy of the same bf16 model, read by the judge). A generic "Sure, here is" prefix shrinks Qwen2.5-3B's shift to +4.3 pp [+0.2, +8.4] (nominal p = 0.047, Holm 0.14) and removes it entirely for Llama-3.2-3B (−1.3 pp, p = 0.66) — so the Qwen2.5-3B effect needs a strongly compliant start, and the Llama effect needs Llama to open the reply itself.
- **The two settings confound prompt set with prefill.** A forced generic start on the free-start prompts separates them for Llama but not for Qwen2.5-3B, whose shift may depend on prompt type as well; that bridge also raises baseline compliance, so it is not a like-for-like comparison.
- **Label sources disagree by 3×** (+6.2 / +11.0 / +19.4 for the same contrast), the human labels come from one annotator (the author), and only the forced-start primary cell has human labels — every other cell uses the judge, which under-counted harm where it could be checked.
- **The refusal-direction analysis is correlational and largely negative.** No circuit is identified; the second prediction fails for Qwen2.5-3B (ρ = −0.44, p = 0.10); the doubled Llama build moves the projection opposite to its behaviour; and the real error lowers the projection further under a free start where behaviour does not shift.
- **Coverage of quantizers is thin where it matters.** MLX round-to-nearest (affine, MXFP4), MLX AWQ/GPTQ, llama.cpp Q4_K_M without imatrix. No bitsandbytes, no imatrix GGUF, no vendor pipelines, no QAT; the second runtime and the calibrated quantizers were run only in the two shifting cells, and Qwen2.5-3B's GPTQ build failed and was excluded.
- **The free-start effect was found on the development set**; only the 400 prompts are held out, not the choice of prompt set and model for the confirmatory test.
- **Pre-registration is in a private repository as dated commits, available on request** — not a public registry, so the dates are not independently verifiable.

## Relevance

直击"极端性能优化下的可靠性"这条主线：把 4-bit/5-bit PTQ 从"掉几个点准确率"推进到"安全行为是否被静默改写、以及能否归因到具体舍入误差"，方法上（8-bit 空对照 + 用真实误差构造的镜像/置换/高斯匹配扰动 + 剂量曲线）可以直接搬到你用来判定量化、FP4、KV-cache 压缩是否引入"行为级"退化的实验框架里。同时它对"低层数值精度 / 误差传播"也有借鉴：论文给出的结论是扰动幅度（bits per weight、NLL）不预测影响方向，误差的**结构**才决定方向，这正好是误差传播分析里"只看范数不够、要看方向与相关性"的一个端到端实证。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. **Which layers and matrices carry the shift?** The perturbation builder already supports it: construct E only on attention vs only on MLP, or per-layer, and run the dose line. If the shift is carried by a handful of layers (the refusal-direction layer 19 is the obvious candidate), that would convert a correlational projection analysis into a causal one, and would be the cheapest route to a mechanism.
2. **Is the judge's own 4-bit quantization biasing the labels?** Run the same judge at bf16 on a subset and compare label distributions on the same replies. If 4-bit Qwen2.5-14B is itself more permissive, part of the +6.2 pp attributed to the subject is the instrument.
3. **Does the effect survive sampling?** The per-prompt variance results suggest Llama's shift is rate-only. A sampled version of the primary cells (say 5 replies per prompt at the model's shipped temperature) with a per-prompt rate estimand would say whether the finding is a property of greedy decoding or of the model.
4. **Why is Qwen3-4B null in both settings?** It is the newest, most heavily safety-trained model of the three. Is its refusal direction more robust to perturbation (measurable as the projection's dose response), or is its baseline compliance so low under forced start (0.4% at k = 0) that the ceiling binds? The paper reports the depth family but does not diagnose the null.
5. **Do the published audits' disagreements decompose into rounding-specific shifts?** Re-running one or two of the cited audits (Kadadekar 2026's 57-point range, Kharinaev et al. 2025's 66 variants) on these three models with an 8-bit null would test the paper's central explanatory claim about the literature rather than merely asserting it.
6. **Can a cheap surrogate replace the paired audit?** The paper concludes every build must be compared with its original. Is there a predictor — e.g. the cosine between a build's error and the RTN error (Table 11: GPTQ 0.31, MXFP4 0.11, AWQ 0.04), or the error's projection onto the refusal direction at build time — that ranks builds by |shift| well enough to triage which ones need the full 562-prompt audit? The paper measures the ingredients but never regresses shift on them.
7. **What happens at 3 bits and below, and with imatrix GGUF?** Those builds have much larger, more structured error, and the paper's dose line predicts larger shifts but says nothing about whether the near-linearity holds or whether the pattern-vs-magnitude attribution survives.
