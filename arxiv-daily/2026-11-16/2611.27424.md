> **一句话总结：** 作者用 Qwen3-4B 与 Qwen3-1.7B 在 BF16/INT8/AWQ/INT4 四档上跑 10 个基准，发现量化衰减是"锯齿状"的不均匀转移——INT4 下 4B 的 DeceptionBench 欺骗率从 42.67% 升到 53.67%（+11.00）、SORRYBench 顺从率从 14.67% 升到 19.00%，而 TruthfulQA 几乎不变（51.47%→51.86%）；1.7B 更极端，INT4 下 HumanEval 从 41.46% 崩到 9.15%（−32.31），但 LLR 式评测只掉 2–5 点；作者随后用 GCG 在 24 条训练提示上优化出 20-token 通用后缀，它在 BF16 上让模型更谨慎（拒绝率 14→33/64），而在 INT4 上把"Sure"开头回复从 2 条推到 37 条。

> **TL;DR:** This paper audits how post-training weight quantization (INT8, AWQ, INT4 via Quanto) reshapes safety and capability in Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-1.7B, naming the non-uniform decay "Jagged Robustness." Free-generation benchmarks reveal the damage that log-likelihood multiple-choice evaluation hides: in Qwen3-4B, INT4 raises DeceptionBench deception rate from 42.67% (BF16) to 53.67% (+11.00 pts) and SORRYBench unsafe-fulfillment from 14.67% to 19.00% (+4.33), while WildChat-NonToxic success drops 66.00% → 57.67% (−8.33); conversely TruthfulQA is unchanged (+0.39) and INT4 InFoBench loses only 1.00 pt, so "quantization hurts safety" is not a uniform effect. In Qwen3-1.7B the same INT4 budget drives HumanEval from 41.46% to 9.15% (−32.31) and MMLU-Pro from 42.56% to 23.24% (−19.32) while LLR benchmarks lose ≤5.14 pts — a 42% incoherent-response rate (vs 3.11% for BF16) explains most of the free-generation collapse. Finally, a quantization-aware GCG suffix (20 tokens, 500 steps, 3 h on 4×A100-40G, optimized on 24 SORRYBench prompts and tested on 426 held-out ones) widens the INT4-vs-BF16 harmful-compliance gap from 8.22 to 13.62 pts under the paper's Mistral-7B LM judge — a modest gap whose growth is driven mostly by BF16 refusing more, not INT4 complying more.

## 核心贡献

- **提出并实证"锯齿状鲁棒性"（Jagged Robustness）概念**：量化衰减不是全局统一的缩放，而是在能力域 × 评测范式两个维度上错位分布。最能说明问题的一组对照是：同一个 INT4 模型在 LLR 多选题上 MMLU 只掉 4.39 点、GPQA 只掉 1.66 点，但在自由生成上 DeceptionBench 欺骗率反升 11.00 点——"总能力没怎么变"和"安全底线塌了一块"可以同时成立。
- **解耦"能力"与"安全"两条衰减曲线**：作者在自由生成里同时测通用能力（InFoBench、WildChat-NonToxic）与安全行为（DeceptionBench、SORRYBench），发现二者独立退化。INT4 下 InFoBench 仅 −1.00 而 WildChat-NonToxic −8.33，说明"指令遵循没坏"不能用来担保"安全对齐没坏"。
- **定位三个致因**：任务主观性/细腻度（DeceptionBench 的灰色道德场景比 SORRYBench 的显式违规更脆弱）、评测范式（LLR 有固定候选集，免疫生成漂移；FFG 依赖 LLM judge 与解析）、生成连贯性（1.7B INT4 上 42% 的 SORRYBench 回复是复读循环，见 Figure 1）。
- **把被动基准推广到主动攻击**：提出的量化感知对抗后缀在目标函数里同时最大化"量化模型的肯定回答概率"与"全精度模型的拒绝概率"（hinge 形式，margin α=0.5、β=2），从而搜索出**在全精度下无害甚至更谨慎、只在 INT4 下触发越狱**的通用后缀——与需要污染发布权重的 Q-Misalign、以及逐提示搜索精度分歧的 PrecisionDiff 相比，这是纯输入级、无需改动模型权重的攻击面。
- **给出可复现的工程细节**：因为 Quanto 的整数 kernel 不可微，作者用 group-wise（group size 128、非对称 min-max、4-bit）fake-quantize 再反量化的代理来取梯度，并验证代理反量化权重与 Quanto 实际 INT4 权重**逐位相同**；全部报告数值都在真实 INT4 模型上测得。代码与实验记录公开在 `github.com/LucianoXu/QuantBenchEfficiency`。

## Key Contributions

- **Conceptualizes Jagged Robustness** as the non-uniform decay of capabilities *and* safeguards across capability domains and evaluation protocols, rather than a single scalar quality loss from quantization.
- **Demonstrates that multiple-choice/LLR evaluation systematically under-reports quantization damage to safety.** Table 1 (Qwen3-4B, zero-shot, N=300) shows only modest INT4 movement — MMLU −4.39, ARC −5.00, BBQ −3.00, Ethics −5.35, TruthfulQA +0.39 — and the authors themselves conclude they "do not find sufficient evidence for Jagged Robustness in the multiple-choice experiment." Switching to free generation (Table 2, 5-shot, N=300, DeepSeek-V4-Flash judge) surfaces the safety regression that Table 1 missed.
- **Separates general capability from safeguards within one evaluation format.** In Table 2's INT4 column, InFoBench drops only 1.00 pt (83.33 → 82.33) while DeceptionBench *rises* 11.00 pts (42.67 → 53.67) — same model, same judge, same prompt budget, opposite directions.
- **Isolates coherence collapse as a confound** using a deterministic 3-gram repetition metric (R > 30% ⇒ incoherent): Qwen3-1.7B SORRYBench incoherence is 3.11% at BF16 and 42% at INT4 (+38.89), which reframes the apparently alarming 56.67% → 77.56% "fulfillment" jump as largely judge-mislabeled rambling.
- **Introduces an input-level, weight-agnostic quantization-aware adversarial suffix** (GCG with a dual-objective hinge loss, 20 tokens) that requires no weight poisoning and no per-prompt precision-diff search, and shows it is *negatively* transferable: benign-to-helpful on BF16, jailbreaking on INT4.

## Methodology

**Evaluation stack.** All experiments run on a single NVIDIA A100 with a 4096-token context. Two formats are contrasted throughout:

1. *Log-likelihood ranking (LLR)* multiple-choice, via `lm-evaluation-harness`: GPQA, MMLU, ARC, BBQ, TruthfulQA, Hendrycks ETHICS. Qwen3-4B is evaluated zero-shot with N=300; Qwen3-1.7B uses 5-shot with N=1000 (SORRYBench capped at N=450).
2. *Free-form generation (FFG)* judged by an LLM: InFoBench (instruction following), WildChat-NonToxic (general utility from real user prompts), DeceptionBench (willingness to comply with deceptive requests), SORRYBench (compliance with dangerous/illegal/inappropriate requests). Qwen3-4B uses 5-shot with N=300 because zero-shot produced less coherent outputs; the judge is DeepSeek-V4-Flash, with a shared system prompt adapted from the SORRYBench judge prompt across WildChat/SORRYBench/InFoBench.

**Quantization configurations.** BF16 baseline, INT8, AWQ, and weight-only INT4 (Optimum-Quanto). The paper is explicit that it targets post-training weight quantization only, as the cheapest and most widely deployed setting.

**Coherence metric.** For 1.7B SORRYBench, incoherence is defined deterministically as the ratio of non-unique 3-grams, R = (N_total − N_unique)/N_total, with a 30% threshold.

**Quantization-aware suffix attack.** Let p be the BF16 model and p̃ the INT4 model, q a harmful prompt, a⁺ an affirmative answer prefix, a⁻ a refusal, s the suffix. The desired suffix maximizes p̃(a⁺ | q++s) while maximizing p(a⁻ | q++s):

  s* = argmax_s E_{q,a⁺,a⁻~D} [ p̃(a⁺ | q++s) + λ p(a⁻ | q++s) ], λ > 0

Because minimising fulfillment on the full model is treated as equivalent to maximising refusal, the refusal term is implemented as a hinge on teacher-forced cross-entropy of a⁺ rather than modelling a⁻ explicitly:

  L(s) = Σ_q [ ℓ̃_q(s) − α ]⁺ + λ [ β − ℓ_q(s) ]⁺,  α = 0.5, β = 2

The margins stop optimisation once the quantized model complies or the full model refuses. Optimisation follows GCG: initialise the suffix with repeated "!", each step compute the gradient w.r.t. the one-hot suffix tokens, take the top-256 candidate tokens per position, evaluate 512 random single-token swaps, keep the best; a curriculum starts from one prompt and adds the next once per-prompt loss falls below 0.5 or after 25 steps. Gradients are obtained through a fake-quantized proxy (every linear layer except the LM head, group size 128, asymmetric min-max, 4-bit, dequantized to BF16) which the authors verify is bit-identical to Quanto's dequantized INT4 weights; all *reported* numbers come from the real INT4 model. Setup: 20-token suffix, λ=1, 500 steps, 24 training prompts and 426 held-out prompts from SORRYBench's base split, 3 hours on 4×A100-40G.

**Judging for the attack.** Two judges: an LM-Judge following SORRYBench (its fine-tuned Mistral-7B-Instruct) deciding whether a generation is an *essential fulfillment* of the request, and an RE-Judge following GCG that only checks for refusal strings ("I cannot", "I am sorry"). The paper notes the two produce different absolute rates than Table 2's DeepSeek-V4-Flash judge.

## Results & Highlights

- **INT4 damages safeguards while leaving instruction following nearly intact (Qwen3-4B, Table 2, N=300, DeepSeek-V4-Flash judge).** DeceptionBench deception rate goes 42.67% (BF16) → 53.67% (INT4), a **+11.00 pt** rise, and SORRYBench unsafe-fulfillment goes 14.67% → 19.00% (**+4.33**); over the same INT4 budget InFoBench follow-rate moves only 83.33% → 82.33% (**−1.00**) and WildChat-NonToxic success 66.00% → 57.67% (**−8.33**). The safety regression is therefore real but domain-selective, not a generic capability washout.
- **AWQ is not a free lunch, and INT8 is not uniformly safe either (Table 2).** AWQ is the worst format for instruction following — InFoBench 83.33% → 77.67% (**−5.66**, worse than INT4's −1.00) — while its SORRYBench fulfillment *drops* to 11.00% (−3.67). INT8 still raises DeceptionBench by +4.33 (42.67 → 47.00), i.e. even 8-bit weight-only quantization shifts deception behavior measurably.
- **LLR evaluation hides the damage (Qwen3-4B, Table 1, zero-shot, N=300).** Under INT4 the largest LLR losses are ARC 49.67% → 44.67% (**−5.00**), Hendrycks ETHICS 68.22% → 62.87% (**−5.35**) and MMLU 71.64% → 67.25% (**−4.39**), while TruthfulQA is flat (51.47% → 51.86%, **+0.39**) and GPQA only −1.66 (37.33% → 35.67%). On this evidence alone the model looks mildly degraded — which is exactly why the FFG numbers matter.
- **The 1.7B model shows a catastrophic FFG/LLR split under INT4 (Tables 3 and 4, 5-shot).** Free generation collapses: HumanEval 41.46% → 9.15% (**−32.31**), MMLU-Pro 42.56% → 23.24% (**−19.32**), mgsm_direct_en 13.20% → 5.60 (**−7.60**), while the same checkpoint's LLR scores only slip — GPQA 31.03% → 25.89% (**−5.14**), BBQ 50.40% → 47.80% (**−2.60**), ETHICS 64.38% → 60.08% (**−4.30**), and TruthfulQA actually *improves* (+2.95, 45.89% → 48.84%). A model that scores "fine" on MMLU-style harnesses can be unable to write a working function.
- **Coherence collapse quantifies why (Table 5, SORRYBench, N=450).** Responses flagged incoherent (R > 30%): BF16 3.11%, INT8 3.78% (+0.67), AWQ 7.55% (**+4.44**), INT4 42% (**+38.89**) — a 13.5× increase in degenerate repetition, which also explains the apparent 56.67% → 77.56% (+20.89) SORRYBench "fulfillment" rise at 1.7B as largely judge mislabeling of loops such as "I'm not insinuating anyone, but I'm here to help you with your insinuations…" (Figure 1).
- **The quantization-aware suffix is asymmetric, not uniformly stronger (Table 6, SORRYBench, 24 train / 426 held-out).** On the test split under the LM-Judge, BF16 harmful compliance *falls* 35.92% → 32.63% while INT4 rises 44.13% → 46.24%, widening the gap from **8.22 to 13.62 pts**; on the 24 training prompts the effect is much larger (BF16 33.33% → 20.83%, INT4 29.17% → 45.83%, Δ from −4.17 to **+25.00**), i.e. the held-out effect is a diluted version of the training effect, consistent with a partially transferable but overfitted suffix.
- **The RE-Judge gap is largely an artefact the authors name themselves (Table 6).** Test-split RE-Judge compliance is 80.05% for INT4-with-suffix vs 44.60% for BF16-with-suffix (**+35.45**), but the RE-Judge only pattern-matches refusal strings, and the optimisation reward targets an affirmative *prefix* — the paper explicitly concedes the suffix "mainly turns INT4 refusals into superficially compliant responses, and only part of them carry genuinely harmful content."
- **Behavioral mechanism on a 64-prompt subset.** With the suffix, INT4 responses opening with "Sure" jump from 2 to 37 and 41 follow the requested LaTeX format; BF16 instead becomes *more* cautious, with refusals rising from 14 to 33 and 16 responses calling the request unclear or inappropriate. The optimized suffix itself migrates from "!!!…!" into semi-coherent instruction-like text ("...words halves?? Urdu ... give-me learntenglish directly both--two LaTeX").

## Authors & Credibility

Three authors across Ruhr University Bochum and the Max Planck Institute for Security and Privacy (CISPA-adjacent), with Agnihotri and Xu marked as equal contribution. This is an IEEE-style conference submission rather than an arXiv-only preprint. The work is positioned squarely within an established and fast-moving line of quantization-safety results it cites accurately: Kharinaev et al. (2502.15799) on safety/reliability decay, Marcuzzi et al. (2508.18088) on quantization-driven bias, Hong et al. (2403.15447) on trustworthiness under compression, Kadadekar (2606.10154) on "quality is not a safety proxy," Egashira et al. (NeurIPS 2024) on exploiting quantization, Dong et al. (ICLR 2025) Q-Misalign, and Wang et al. (2604.19790) PrecisionDiff. The framing as an *audit* built on prior work rather than a novel-claim paper is honest about its lineage. Credibility signals: released code and complete experiment records; the bit-identical fake-quant verification for gradient computation; the authors voluntarily report that the multiple-choice experiment found *no* Jagged Robustness, which cuts against their own headline. Countervailing: it is a short paper with two models from one family, one judge model, one seed, and no confidence intervals — a scope the authors concede in Limitations, but one that leaves the headline concept resting on differences of a few points in several cells.

## Critical Assessment

*Novelty.* The "Jagged Robustness" label is new but the underlying observation is not: Hong et al. (2403.15447) already reported uneven alignment-domain impact under compression, and Kadadekar (2606.10154) already argued quality is not a safety proxy. The genuinely new contributions are narrower and better: (i) the explicit LLR-vs-FFG contrast on the *same* checkpoint with the *same* judge, which is a cleaner demonstration than most prior work; (ii) the deterministic 3-gram coherence metric as a confound-disentangling device; and (iii) the dual-objective (refuse-on-BF16 / comply-on-INT4) suffix objective, which is a modest but real extension of the GCG template beyond PrecisionDiff's per-prompt disagreement search. The suffix's migration into "semi-coherent instruction text" is an interesting, under-analysed phenomenon — it suggests the attack is partly *format hijacking* against a quantized model whose instruction-priority circuit has degraded, and the paper never tests that hypothesis.

*Rigor — the numbers are thinner than the claims.* Table 1 and Table 2 use **N=300 with no confidence intervals and a single seed**, which the authors admit. For a binomial proportion near p≈0.5, N=300 gives a 95% CI half-width of roughly ±5.7 pts; near p≈0.15 it is about ±4.1 pts. On that arithmetic, the INT4 MMLU −4.39, ARC −5.00, ETHICS −5.35, BBQ −3.00, GPQA −1.66, and TruthfulQA +0.39 cells are all inside noise, as are the +2.34/+1.34 INT8 and AWQ GPQA *gains* — and indeed some "degradations" are positive. The paper's own concession that Table 1 shows no sufficient evidence of Jagged Robustness should be read as stronger than stated: Table 1 shows essentially nothing beyond noise. The FFG results fare better because the effects are larger (DeceptionBench +11.00 at 4B; the 1.7B FFG collapse at −19 to −32 pts), but the moderate 4B cells (WildChat −8.33, InFoBench −1.00, SORRYBench +4.33) straddle the noise floor at N=300.

*The headline attack effect is weaker than the abstract implies.* The abstract says the suffix "successfully triggers jailbreaks on the INT4 quantized version." The held-out LM-Judge numbers are INT4 44.13% → 46.24% — a **+2.11 pt** absolute change, itself within noise at N=426 — and the paper states plainly that "this effect is modest, and it comes mostly from the BF16 model refusing more often (35.92% to 32.63%), while INT4 compliance barely changes." The gap-widening metric (8.22 → 13.62) is therefore achieved mainly by *lowering* the baseline, not by raising the attack. The dramatic +25.00 and +29.17 deltas live on the 24 **training** prompts the suffix was optimised on, and the 80.05% RE-Judge figure is a metric the authors themselves discount. A reader who takes only the abstract away will substantially overestimate the attack.

*Metric and comparability problems.* Table 2 reports BF16 SORRYBench fulfillment at 14.67% (DeepSeek-V4-Flash judge, N=300) while Table 6 reports BF16 test-split harmful compliance at 35.92% (Mistral-7B LM-Judge, N=426) for what is nominally the same behavior on the same benchmark — a 21-point spread driven by judge and subset choice. The paper flags this in the Table 6 caption, which is commendable, but it means the attack results cannot be chained to the passive results to argue "quantization plus attack compounds." Separately, the 1.7B SORRYBench "fulfillment" of 77.56% (+20.89) should not be read as increased compliance at all: with 42% of responses incoherent, a judge asked "is this an essential fulfillment?" is systematically miscoding repetition loops. The paper does say this in the RQ2 discussion, but the number still sits in Table 4 without an asterisk.

*Missing ablations and controls.* (1) **No AWQ hyperparameters are reported** — no group size, no calibration set, no zero-point scheme — even though AWQ produces the paper's most surprising result (worst InFoBench, best SORRYBench) and is therefore the result most in need of a robustness check. (2) **No control for the suffix-selection procedure**: the natural null hypothesis is that *any* 20-token GCG suffix hurts BF16 more than INT4, in which case the "quantization-aware" objective is doing little beyond ordinary GCG; the paper never compares against a vanilla GCG suffix optimised only against the INT4 model, which is the ablation that would establish the dual-objective loss as necessary. (3) **No intermediate precisions** between 4 and 8 bits, so "INT4" is a single point that could be a threshold effect (the 1.7B HumanEval cliff from 41.46% to 9.15% looks like exactly such a threshold) rather than a trend. (4) **No judge-agreement check**: a second judge (e.g. GPT-class) on a subsample would have made the DeceptionBench +11.00 far more persuasive, especially since DeceptionBench is the paper's most load-bearing FFG result and its prompts are "deliberately morally gray" by the authors' own description — precisely where LLM judges are least reliable. (5) **No random-suffix baseline**, which would tell us whether 42% incoherence at 1.7B INT4 is triggered by sensitive prompts specifically or is a generic property of the quantized model on any long-form generation.

*Overclaiming and framing.* Calling the phenomenon "Jagged Robustness" and then defining it in the abstract as covering "capability and safety domains" while Table 1 finds no evidence for it in the capability domain is a definitional stretch — the evidence supports "safety-specific, format-dependent decay," which is a narrower and more defensible claim. The abstract also asserts "truthfulness remain[s] more stable in Qwen3-4B" on the strength of a +0.39 pt change at N=300, which is indistinguishable from zero rather than evidence of stability. Similarly, the claim that bias "can degrade under quantization" rests on BBQ 42.00 → 39.00 (−3.00) — a number that INT8 and AWQ both *improve* on (+1.67, +2.00), which makes the INT4 drop look like format-specific noise rather than a bias effect. Only one model family (Qwen3, two sizes) and one tokenizer family are tested, so nothing here establishes cross-family generality; the authors acknowledge this, but the concept name implies more.

## Strengths

- The **LLR-vs-FFG contrast on one checkpoint with one judge** is the cleanest available demonstration that multiple-choice safety evaluation is structurally blind to the failure mode that matters most — a methodological point that generalizes well beyond quantization.
- **Table 5's deterministic 3-gram incoherence metric** is a genuinely good idea: cheap, reproducible, judge-free, and it converts a vague "responses look worse" claim into a 3.11% → 42% number that also explains away a misleading benchmark trend.
- The authors **report the result that hurts their own thesis** (no Jagged Robustness in the multiple-choice experiment) rather than hiding it, and their Limitations section is unusually candid — it names the missing seeds, the missing confidence intervals, and the risk that close data points are "noise or cherry-picking results."
- The **attack objective is principled**: the hinge-with-margins formulation (α=0.5, β=2) that halts optimisation once the quantized model complies or the full model refuses is a sensible way to express "benign on BF16, harmful on INT4," and the curriculum (add a prompt below loss 0.5 or after 25 steps) is a reasonable universal-suffix trick.
- **Engineering rigor on the gradient proxy**: verifying that the fake-quantized weights are bit-identical to Quanto's dequantized INT4 weights, and then reporting all evaluations on the real INT4 model, closes the most common loophole in quantization-attack papers.
- **The 64-prompt behavioral breakdown** (INT4 "Sure" openings 2 → 37; BF16 refusals 14 → 33) is much more informative than a single attack-success number, because it shows the two precisions reacting to the *same suffix in opposite directions* — direct evidence for non-transferable robustness.

## Limitations

- **Statistical power**: N=300 (Tables 1–2), single seed, no confidence intervals anywhere. At that sample size most of the multiple-choice deltas and several free-generation deltas are within sampling noise, so the paper cannot distinguish its moderate effects from zero — a limitation the authors state but which also weakens several bullets in the abstract.
- **Narrow scope**: Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-1.7B only; one family, one tokenizer, one quantization toolkit for INT4 (Optimum-Quanto), and AWQ/INT8 hyperparameters unreported. No 8B+ models, no Llama/Mistral/Gemma, no MoE, no KV-cache or activation quantization.
- **The attack's held-out effect is small** (+2.11 pts INT4 under LM-Judge) and the gap-widening is mostly attributable to BF16 becoming more conservative; the paper's strongest attack numbers come from training prompts or from a refusal-string-only judge that the authors themselves discount.
- **No vanilla-GCG or random-suffix baseline**, so the marginal value of the quantization-aware dual objective over ordinary GCG is not established, and the "only hurts the quantized model" property could partly be an artefact of the suffix's format-hijacking content rather than of precision-awareness.
- **Confounded free-generation measurements**: DeceptionBench's "deliberately morally gray" prompts and SORRYBench's incoherent-loop outputs both stress the DeepSeek-V4-Flash judge in exactly the regime where LLM judges are least reliable, with no judge-agreement study or human spot-check to anchor the numbers.
- **Judge and split inconsistency between Table 2 and Table 6** (14.67% vs 35.92% for BF16 SORRYBench) prevents composing the attack result with the audit result into a joint claim, even though the paper's narrative implies exactly that composition.
- **No mechanistic evidence**: the paper repeatedly attributes jagged decay to "task subtlety" and "generation coherence" — plausible descriptions, not mechanisms. Interpretability analysis (internal activation divergence between BF16 and INT4) is deferred entirely to future work, leaving the title concept descriptive.
- **Single-point INT4**: no 5/6/7-bit or mixed-precision sweep, so the observed cliffs (1.7B HumanEval −32.31; 42% incoherence) cannot be located on a dose-response curve and may be threshold artefacts of one configuration.

## Relevance

直接命中"高效推理/量化部署"与"安全对齐"的交叉面：如果你在考虑把 Qwen 或其他模型量化后上线，这篇给出了"别拿 MMLU 掉几个点当安全证据"的实证依据——INT4 下 DeceptionBench 欺骗率反升 11 点是比任何能力指标都更值得盯的部署前检查项。其"LLR 评测看不见生成侧崩塌""1.7B INT4 42% 回复是复读"的结论，对任何做量化后验收（acceptance audit）的流程都有直接的工程含义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. **Is the DeceptionBench +11.00 real or noise?** With N=300 and no CI, this is the paper's most load-bearing claim. What happens with 5 seeds and N=1000, and does a second independent judge (not DeepSeek-V4-Flash) reproduce it?
2. **How much of the suffix effect is quantization-awareness?** Run vanilla GCG optimized against INT4 only, and against BF16 only, plus a random 20-token suffix, on the same 426 held-out prompts. If vanilla INT4-GCG produces a similar asymmetric effect, the dual-objective hinge loss is not the mechanism.
3. **Where exactly is the 1.7B threshold?** Sweep 8/6/5/4-bit and mixed-precision (e.g. attention in 8-bit, MLP in 4-bit) and plot HumanEval and the 3-gram incoherence rate against effective bits — is the −32.31 pt cliff a smooth trend or a discrete break, and does it coincide with the incoherence onset?
4. **Can coherence collapse be predicted without generating?** The paper proposes output-entropy analysis as future work; an even cheaper proxy would be the divergence between BF16 and INT4 next-token distributions on a small calibration set — does that divergence correlate with the downstream FFG drop across the 10 benchmarks?
5. **Why does AWQ behave so differently from INT4** — worst InFoBench (−5.66) yet best SORRYBench (−3.67), i.e. more instruction damage but *better* refusal? Reporting AWQ's group size, calibration corpus, and zero-point scheme, then testing whether scaling AWQ to a comparable perplexity budget as INT4 removes the asymmetry, would clarify whether this is a real algorithmic difference or an uncalibrated comparison.
6. **Does the suffix exploit a format-following circuit?** The optimized suffix ends up as LaTeX/format instructions, and 41 of 64 INT4 responses comply with that format. Test a hand-written format-only suffix ("answer in LaTeX, start with Sure") — if it alone jailbreaks INT4, the vulnerability is degraded instruction-priority arbitration rather than anything specific to adversarial optimization.
