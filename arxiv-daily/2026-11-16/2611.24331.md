> **一句话总结：** 作者在 Jülich 的 Leonardo 超算上花费约 20 万 GPU 小时，用 muP 在宽度方向缩放，训练了 433 条（ResNet-18/CIFAR-10、GPT/FineWeb、DiT/ImageNet，11M–1B 参数）带密集 batch-size × learning-rate 网格的运行，逐 step 记录 GNS 及其两个原始分量并开源，进而发现 GNS 在"联合调好超参"后确实像"训练温度"一样跨宽度一致，但其与临界批量（CBS）的偏差方向随任务类型翻转：分类任务高估、生成任务低估。

> **TL;DR:** OpenGNS releases per-step Gradient Noise Scale trajectories for 433 training runs (180 ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10, 178 GPT models on FineWeb, 75 DiTs on ImageNet; 11M–1B params, muP width scaling, AdamW, ≈200k A100-hours), the first open dataset of its kind, since McCandlish et al. (2018), Merrill et al. (2025) and Li et al. (2025) each published only individual-run GNS numbers with no reusable trajectories. On this grid the GNS moves by nearly an order of magnitude when the learning rate is pushed ×8 or ×16 off baseline, but collapses onto a shared trajectory once LR and batch size are jointly tuned (batch ×4 with LR ×2, the Adam √B rule), across a 16× batch range (GPT 2^17→2^21 tokens; DiT 2^6→2^10 images; ResNet 2^7→2^11 images). At matched training loss the GNS is roughly width-invariant, yet it underestimates the 20%-overhead CBS for generative tasks and overestimates it for ResNet/CIFAR-10 — a sign-stable, magnitude-varying gap across 10/20/30/50% overhead thresholds.

## 核心贡献

- **发布 OpenGNS 数据集**：433 条训练运行（ResNet-18/CIFAR-10 180 条、GPT/FineWeb 178 条、DiT/ImageNet 75 条）的逐 step GNS、平方梯度范数 `|G|²`、逐样本梯度协方差迹 `tr(Σ)`，以及训练/验证 loss；模型 11M–1B 参数，全部在 muP 下按宽度缩放，并覆盖密集的 batch-size × learning-rate 网格。据作者所知是首个此类开放数据集。
- **把 GNS 与超参的关系量化**：GNS 的测量值强烈依赖 batch size 与 learning rate 的联合配置——同一 GPT 模型仅把 LR 放大 8 倍/16 倍，GNS 就可以相差接近一个数量级；但当二者联合调优到相近验证 loss 后，不同配置的 GNS 轨迹重新重合，验证了"训练温度（temperature of training）"这一直觉在现代 Transformer 与扩散架构上依然成立。
- **跨宽度一致性与可迁移性**：在 muP 下按宽度缩放（GPT 256→2560，DiT 144→576，ResNet 1→4），在同一训练 loss 水平上 GNS 与 CBS 大致保持不变；大模型的 GNS 更高，主要是因为它达到了更低的 loss，而非模型本身直接决定噪声尺度。这为"用小代理模型给出大模型 batch-size 建议"提供了经验依据。
- **揭示 GNS 与 CBS 之间任务相关的系统性偏差**：生成任务（GPT、DiT）上 GNS 倾向低估 CBS（与 Merrill et al., 2025 一致），分类任务（ResNet/CIFAR-10）上倾向高估（与 McCandlish et al., 2018 的原始测量一致）；该偏差方向在 10%/20%/30%/50% 四种 overhead 阈值下均不变，仅量级随阈值变化。
- **工程可复用性**：GNS 通过 PyTorch-DDP 上的 hook 用"大/小 batch 梯度范数"法测量，只需每块 GPU 的局部梯度与全局梯度，即可在不增加额外通信开销的前提下获得；附录还给出 EMA 衰减 α 与 `B_big/B_small` 比值的敏感性分析。

## Key Contributions

- **The OpenGNS dataset**: 433 training runs (180 ResNet-18/CIFAR-10, 178 GPT/FineWeb, 75 DiT/ImageNet) with per-interval `gns` (= `B_simple`), `gns_norm` (`|G|²`), `gns_var` (`tr(Σ)`), plus train/val losses — 11M to 1B parameters, width-scaled under muP, dense batch-size/learning-rate grids. Released as three Parquet files (`cv.parquet`, `nlp.parquet`, `diffusion.parquet`) on HuggingFace. Raw components are stored separately so users can reconstruct or re-derive the metric.
- **GNS across hyperparameters**: the measurement is highly sensitive to the batch-size/learning-rate pair (nearly an order of magnitude spread for a fixed GPT model when LR is scaled ×8/×16), but jointly well-tuned runs that reach similar validation loss fall onto consistent GNS trajectories — extending McCandlish et al.'s "temperature of training" reading to modern Transformers and DiTs.
- **GNS across loss and model size**: at matched training loss the GNS (and the extracted CBS) is roughly invariant to width under muP, so proxy-model measurements are a plausible substitute for full-scale sweeps; larger models show larger GNS mainly because they reach lower loss.
- **Task-dependent GNS↔CBS bias**: GNS behaves as a lower bound on the CBS for generative tasks (GPT, DiT) and as an upper bound for classification (ResNet/CIFAR-10); the sign is stable across CBS thresholds of 10/20/30/50% overhead, though the classification gap largely closes under McCandlish's original 50% definition.
- **Practical measurement recipe**: the data-parallel estimator needs no extra communication (small batch = per-GPU local gradient, big batch = global gradient), is insensitive to the `B_big/B_small` ratio (4, 8, 16 give consistent curves), and only needs α tuned to trade noise against lag.

## Methodology

The paper first re-derives the GNS from a second-order Taylor expansion of the loss. For SGD with per-example gradient covariance `cov(G_est) = Σ/B`, the loss after a step of size ε is `L(θ) − ε|G|² + ½ε²(GᵀHG + tr(HΣ)/B)`, giving the noise batch size `B_noise = tr(HΣ)/GᵀHG`; assuming a well-conditioned Hessian (H ∝ I) reduces this to the measured quantity `B_simple = tr(Σ)/GᵀG`.

Measurement follows McCandlish et al. (2018) for data-parallel training: with `E[|G_small|²] = |G|² + tr(Σ)/B_small` and `E[|G_big|²] = |G|² + tr(Σ)/B_big`, one solves the 2×2 system for `|G|²` and `tr(Σ)`. In DDP, `G_small` is the per-GPU local gradient and `G_big` the all-reduced global gradient with `B_big = N·B_small`, so the measurement is overhead-free; both quantities are read every few steps and smoothed with separate EMAs (α = 0.95–0.995 depending on task).

CBS is defined following Zhang et al. (2024): losses are smoothed with an EWMA whose span scales as `1/√B` (so the window is roughly constant in samples seen); for a target loss `L*` one records `N(w, B, ε; L*)`, keeps only the best LR per batch size, `N*(w, B; L*) = min_ε N`, and computes scaling efficiency `S(B) = N*(B_min)/N*(B)` against the smallest swept batch. The CBS is the batch size where S first drops below `1/1.2` (20% overhead), found by log-linear interpolation between grid points; appendix D repeats this at 10%, 30% and 50%.

Experimental setup: all models use AdamW (β₁ = 0.9; β₂ = 0.99 for ResNet/DiT, 0.95 for GPT). ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10 for 200 epochs, widths ×1/×2/×4 (11M/45M/179M), batch 2^5–2^14 images, LR 1e-5–1e-1, cosine decay per epoch, CIFAR-10.1 as an extra generalization check. GPT uses the nano-muP implementation (NanoGPT-based), 12 layers, head size 128, widths 256/512/1024/2048/2560 (22M–1B), 20B tokens of FineWeb (≈ Chinchilla-optimal for the largest model), batch 2^15–2^21 tokens, LR 2^-11–2^-6, cosine with 5% warmup. DiT: depth 28, head size 72, widths 144/288/576 (11M/43M/169M), 51M ImageNet images (≈800k steps at batch 2^6), batch 2^6–2^10, constant LR in 2^-13–2^-9, validation loss measured on COCO val following Esser et al. (2024). Training ran on the Leonardo Booster (4×A100 per node, InfiniBand); GPT and DiT used two nodes (8 GPUs), ResNet one node (4 GPUs); ≈200,000 GPU-hours in total.

## Results & Highlights

- **Dataset scale and coverage**: 433 runs total (180 ResNet-18 + 178 GPT + 75 DiT), three domains, eleven width/parameter settings from 11M to 1B, versus zero open GNS trajectory datasets before — prior work (McCandlish et al., 2018; Gray et al., 2023/2024; Merrill et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025) reported GNS only for individual runs from which nothing could be reused.
- **Learning-rate sensitivity**: for one fixed GPT model/width on FineWeb (Fig. 1), the measured GNS spans roughly 0.2–2.0 ×10^5 tokens — close to an order of magnitude — when the LR is scaled ×1, ×8 and ×16 off the baseline, i.e. the metric is not a property of the model alone.
- **Recovered Adam scaling rule**: scaling batch size ×4 while doubling LR (the ε ∝ √B rule of Hilton et al., 2022 and Malladi et al., 2022) yields nearly identical GNS trajectories for GPT and DiT; and consistent with Li et al.'s (2024) "surge" prediction, the LR optimum peaks at batch sizes (2^19 and 2^20 tokens) close to the final GNS values, which sit between 0.5×10^6 and 10^6 tokens.
- **Temperature collapse across a 16× batch range**: despite batch sizes spanning 2^17→2^21 tokens (GPT), 2^6→2^10 images (DiT) and 2^7→2^11 images (ResNet), well-optimized runs (lowest validation loss per batch size) share one GNS trajectory, while the worst-loss configurations (grey curves) deviate substantially at the same token budget.
- **Domain-dependent magnitude**: at matched loss the GPT runs reach GNS/CBS up to 10^6 tokens, ResNet-18 up to ~10^4 images (tens of thousands), and DiTs only ~10^2 images (hundreds, i.e. roughly tens of thousands of patch tokens) — a ~4-order-of-magnitude spread in native units that undercuts the assumption that diffusion models need LLM-scale batch sizes.
- **Width invariance under muP**: GNS curves for GPT widths 256–2560, DiT widths 144–576 and ResNet widths 1–4 overlap at the same training loss (Fig. 3), so a 22M proxy predicts the 1B model's batch-size regime — the paper's main practical claim.
- **GNS/CBS bias direction is threshold-stable**: GNS overestimates the CBS for ResNet/CIFAR-10 and underestimates it for GPT and DiT at 10%, 20% and 30% overhead; the classification gap "largely closes" under McCandlish's original 50% threshold, while the generative underestimation persists at every threshold (the tighter the threshold, the closer the CBS comes to the GNS).
- **Instability boundaries captured**: 9 of 75 DiT configurations (12%) and 12 of 210 NLP runs (5.7%) diverge to NaN / loss spikes, all at the two or three largest learning rates; runs are kept up to the divergence point, so the dataset encodes the stability frontier of the batch-size–learning-rate grid rather than silently dropping it.

## Authors & Credibility

All four authors are at the Jülich Supercomputing Centre (Forschungszentrum Jülich, Germany) — an HPC lab with direct access to the EuroHPC LEONARDO Booster, which is exactly the infrastructure this study needs and a plausible reason a 200,000 GPU-hour sweep exists at all outside a frontier lab. The paper is formatted for AutoML 2026 (© 2026 the authors, CC BY 4.0) and its reference list is unusually well-targeted for this niche: McCandlish et al. (2018) as the source theory, Zhang et al. (2024) for the 20% CBS variant, Merrill et al. (2025) and Li et al. (2025) as the closest long-horizon GNS measurements, Gray et al. (2023, 2024) for alternative estimators, plus muP (Yang et al., 2021), nano-muP (Dey et al., 2024) and DiT-muP (Zheng et al., 2025). The acknowledgements credit a EuroHPC Regular Access award and German BMWK funding. Credibility signals are strong on artifact provenance (dataset URL, HPC system, GPU-hour count, exact hyperparameter table) and weaker on prior peer-reviewed track record in this specific metric — this reads as a first dataset paper from a systems group, not a scaling-laws group.

## Critical Assessment

Genuinely useful artifact, but the empirical analysis is descriptive and the headline claims lean on visual inspection of log-scale plots. Specific concerns:

1. **No quantitative fit for "consistency"**. The central claim — GNS trajectories and CBS are invariant across width at matched loss — is supported by overlapping curves in Fig. 3, not by any statistic: no fitted exponent, no R², no ratio of GNS at width 256 vs 2560 at a fixed target loss. For a paper whose selling point is that small models predict large ones, the absence of even a single "GNS(1B)/GNS(22M) = X at loss Y" number is a real gap, and it makes the "could inform batch-size selection for larger models" conclusion weaker than the abstract implies.
2. **Single seed for 15 of 16 configurations, and no error bars where it matters**. Only the CV grid was rerun with a second seed (§E); GPT and DiT have one run per cell, so the apparent smoothness of their GNS curves is partly the EMA smoothing (α = 0.95–0.995, Appendix B) rather than evidence of low variance. The authors do check that α = 0.9 is noisy and α = 0.999 lags, which is honest, but the choice of a near-1 α also flattens exactly the trajectory differences the paper interprets.
3. **The CBS estimator is tied to the swept grid**. `S(B) = N*(B_min)/N*(B)` uses the smallest swept batch as reference, and that reference differs by task (2^5 images for ResNet vs 2^15 tokens for GPT) while the CBS threshold is a fixed 20%. A CBS defined relative to a grid-dependent anchor is not obviously comparable across the three domains, yet the paper compares the GNS/CBS *gap* across them — the "overestimate for classification / underestimate for generative" contrast may partly be an artifact of very different reference batch sizes and of the very different loss ranges (CIFAR-10 loss ~1.0→0.001 over four decades; DiT 0.18→0.15, i.e. a 0.03-wide window plotted on a log axis). Appendix D's threshold sweep mitigates but does not remove this.
4. **Selection on validation loss, then reporting validation loss**. For each batch size the "optimal" LR is chosen as the one with lowest validation loss (GPT: 100M held-out FineWeb tokens; DiT: COCO val, a different dataset from the ImageNet training set), and the same validation loss is then used as the x-axis for the GNS/CBS curves. That is mild selection bias, and the DiT choice of COCO val to monitor an ImageNet model is an odd proxy that the paper does not justify beyond citing Esser et al. (2024).
5. **The width-invariance claim is width-only**. Depth is fixed at 18/12/28; no depth or aspect-ratio scaling is tested, so "consistent across model sizes" should be read as "consistent across widths at fixed depth". muP's transfer guarantee is itself a modeling assumption, which means the invariance result partly measures muP working rather than GNS being scale-free.
6. **Explanations for the GNS–CBS bias are speculative**. Ill-conditioning of the Hessian and heavy-tailed token distributions (Kunstner et al., 2024) are offered as causes for the generative underestimation, but no conditioning proxy (e.g. gradient covariance spectrum, Hessian-vector estimates) is measured — the raw `gns_var` and `gns_norm` are in the dataset, so the eigenvalues could have been reported and were not.
7. **Unused opportunity in `cv.parquet`**. Test accuracy and CIFAR-10.1 accuracy are released, yet the paper does not test the classic question of whether GNS predicts generalization (it explicitly says the metric carries no generalization information, §A). That is a defensible scoping choice but leaves the most interesting released column unexploited.
8. **Scale caveat is real, not cosmetic**. 1B parameters / 20B tokens against frontier 100B+ / trillions; the extrapolation rests entirely on muP. The paper says so in Appendix A, which is to its credit, but the abstract's framing ("consistent temperature of training") travels further than the evidence.

None of this undermines the dataset's value — 433 runs with raw GNS components at this cost is a genuine public good, and the task-dependent bias direction is a useful, replicated observation (it independently reproduces both McCandlish et al.'s CIFAR-10 overestimation and Merrill et al.'s LLM underestimation in one controlled setup). But this is a dataset-and-observation paper, not a validated predictive method, and it should be cited as the former.

## Strengths

- A real, expensive, reusable artifact: 433 runs, ≈200k A100-hours, per-step GNS plus both raw components (`gns_norm`, `gns_var`) so the metric can be re-derived under other assumptions — materially better than the typical "we report a GNS number in a footnote" practice.
- Three genuinely different domains (supervised classification, autoregressive LM, diffusion) under one measurement pipeline, one optimizer family, and one parameterization (muP), which is what makes the cross-task comparison meaningful at all.
- Careful, honest appendices: EMA decay sensitivity (α = 0.9 noisy, 0.999 over-smoothed, Fig. 4), `B_big/B_small` robustness at 4/8/16 (Fig. 5), CBS threshold sensitivity at 10/30/50% (Fig. 6), and a two-seed CV rerun with explicit ranges rather than a fake standard error (Fig. 7).
- Keeps failed runs (9/75 DiT → NaN, 12/210 NLP loss spikes) up to the divergence point, turning an annoyance into a stability-boundary dataset — genuinely useful for anyone studying large-batch divergence.
- The data-parallel measurement trick (local vs global gradient, no extra communication) is described precisely enough to reimplement, and the paper is candid that optimizer state is not logged and preconditioned variants cannot be reconstructed.
- The task-dependent bias result is stated with its threshold dependence rather than as a universal law, and the 50%-threshold convergence for classification is reported even though it weakens the headline.

## Limitations

- One seed for GPT and DiT grids; error bars only for the CV case, and only over two seeds.
- Width-only scaling (depth fixed at 18/12/28), max 1B parameters and 20B tokens — everything about "extrapolation to frontier scale" is inference, not measurement.
- CBS is defined relative to the smallest swept batch per task, making cross-domain GNS/CBS gap comparisons grid-dependent; only the threshold dimension is swept, not the reference-batch choice.
- AdamW only, raw (pre-preconditioning) gradients: the theory is derived for SGD, and preconditioned/non-Euclidean noise scales (Pollux, Li et al. 2025, Naganuma et al. 2026) cannot be computed from the release.
- DiT validation loss is computed on COCO val for an ImageNet-trained model; there is no FID or image-quality metric in the release, so nothing connects GNS to generative sample quality.
- No analysis linking the released CIFAR-10 / CIFAR-10.1 accuracies to GNS, despite the stated (and probably correct) position that GNS says nothing about generalization.
- A few grid gaps: GPT widths 2048/2560 skip batch sizes 2^15–2^17 for runtime reasons, so the largest models have a narrower batch range than the smallest — exactly where the width-invariance claim would be most convincing.
- No released measurement code or training configs beyond the hyperparameter table (the paper points to Crowson et al. 2024 and Gray et al. 2024 implementations rather than shipping its own DDP hook).

## Relevance

与"LLM 训练稳定性与可观测性"和"大规模 ML 计算基础设施"两个方向直接相关：GNS 是训练过程中可在线测量、几乎零通信开销的内部信号，本文给出的开源轨迹与"分类高估 / 生成低估 CBS"的任务相关偏差，可直接用于 batch size 与学习率调度（梯度噪声引导的显存/卡数分配）以及大规模训练异常（9/75 DiT、12/210 GPT 配置发散）的监测基线；对关注 muP 超参迁移与代理模型外推的工作也有参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. What is the quantitative width-invariance error — e.g. GNS(width 2560)/GNS(width 256) at a fixed target loss for GPT and DiT — and does it grow with the width ratio faster than muP's own loss-transfer error?
2. How much of the reported GNS trajectory smoothness is EMA artifact? Re-running the analysis from the released `gns_norm`/`gns_var` with α ∈ {0.9, 0.95, 0.995} and reporting confidence bands would settle this cheaply.
3. Does the GNS/CBS gap direction survive changing the reference batch `B_min` (e.g. anchoring at a fixed samples-seen budget rather than the grid minimum), or is the classification/generative contrast partly a reference-choice artifact?
4. Can the released `gns_var` spectra be used to test the ill-conditioning hypothesis directly — does the condition number of the per-example gradient covariance differ between CIFAR-10 and FineWeb in a way that predicts the sign of the GNS−CBS bias?
5. Does GNS at a given loss predict CIFAR-10.1 accuracy at all, once you condition on the batch-size/LR grid? A null result would be a useful, clean statement about the metric's scope.
6. How does GNS behave under depth scaling, MoE routing, or RL post-training, where the "well-conditioned Hessian" and fixed-objective assumptions look weakest?
