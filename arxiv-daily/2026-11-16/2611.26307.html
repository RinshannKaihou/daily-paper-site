Why this matters

A kernel- and layer-level fault-sensitivity tool for PIM reliability work — without days of exhaustive fault simulation.

Speaks directly to fault-aware mapping and selective-redundancy budgeting.

Critical edge

Kernels are binned low/medium/high by the very score being validated — the test proves monotonicity within a layer, never that VS picks the right kernels.

And with no first-order-only ablation, the Hessian's incremental value is unmeasured.

Methodology 方法

  1. One forward + one backward pass over the test set (batch 128) yields the cross-entropy gradient gij and diagonal Hessian hij for every weight.
  2. Fault model: a cell is stuck at wmin (SaL) or wmax (SaH). ΔW is large, so keep the quadratic term: S = g·ΔW + ½h·ΔW² — evaluated separately for both polarities.
  3. Aggregate & weight: average elements to kernel (Eq. 4) and layer, then scale by the measured fault mix — VS = FSaHSSaH + FSaLSSaL, with 9.04% SaH / 1.75% SaL. Eq. 6 instead sums only over cells in a real crossbar fault map.
  4. Validate with ReConFI: 10% SaH + 2% SaL injected per kernel, full 10,000-image test set re-run, four damage metrics (accuracy, loss, MSE, prediction Hamming).

PyTorch + MemTorch (VTEAM device) + the group's own ReConFI injector (IEEE T-REL 2024). Six CNNs, 44 K–57 M parameters, MNIST and CIFAR-10.

Strengths 优点

  • Right substitution. One backward pass replaces 10⁴–10⁵ re-inference runs, implemented end-to-end on six architectures.
  • Second order argued, not decorated. Stuck-at faults drive ΔW to the rails — precisely where a first-order Taylor estimate fails.
  • Hardware-aware score. Eq. 6 can sum over a real crossbar fault map, making VS a mapping/design tool, not just a ranking.
  • Honest negatives. SimpleNet's ρ ≈ 0.1 and ResNet18's uneven per-layer ρ (0.70 accuracy vs 0.27 loss) are reported, not buried.
  • Usable populations. The 86.51 / 9.36 / 4.13 kernel bands feed a redundancy budget independently of VS's rank quality.
  • Non-obvious polarity finding. 37/90 InceptionV3 BatchNorm layers flip the SaL-dominance pattern — nobody would have guessed it.

Limitations 局限

  • Circular validation. Kernels are binned by the metric under test, so only within-layer monotonicity is testable.
  • No baseline ablation. No first-order-only column and no comparison against FKeras, DeepVigor, or ReConFI's own statistics.
  • Two of three. SimpleNet's global ρ (0.09–0.20, n.s.) leaves "reliable across architectures" resting on LeNet5 + ResNet18.
  • Uniform faults only. Flat 10% SaH / 2% SaL Bernoulli; no clustered bitline, endurance, or spatially correlated map, despite Eq. 6.
  • Thin big-model evidence. VGG19 is 3 layers × 3 kernels with no ρ; the largest-model speedup is extrapolated, and InceptionV3 still costs 39.5 h.
  • Curvature ignored. Diagonal Hessian only; negative curvature and sign handling never reported. Baselines look atypical (VGG19 88.40%, 57 M AlexNet).

Questions for further exploration 待解问题

  1. Does VS's kernel ranking beat a plain |g·ΔW| first-order score and FKeras's bit-level rank? How much do the top-10% sets overlap?
  2. If kernels were binned by an independent criterion (L2 norm, activation magnitude, small-sample damage), would Table 2's ρ survive?
  3. Why does SimpleNet collapse globally (ρ ≈ 0.1) yet reach ρ = 1.00 on conv2.weight MSE — 22 kernels, or a genuine Taylor failure?
  4. How often is the Taylor term negative, and does clipping versus sign-preserving aggregation change layer rankings?
  5. With a clustered fault map (one stuck bitline across a crossbar column), do critical kernels change — and does Eq. 6 still predict damage?
  6. Does the layer ranking from an 88.40%-accurate VGG19 transfer to a standard ~93% model, given that sensitivity depends on weight distribution?
