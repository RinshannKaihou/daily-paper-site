> **一句话总结：** 作者用“相同预算下的精确注意力质量选择”作 oracle，证明 block-sparse 长上下文模型丢检索能力是因为选择器把 needle block 排名压得太低而非预算不足，并用一次教师强制校准探针在 InfLLM-V2/MiniCPM4 系列中定位到固定的三个 "blind heads"（L9.h0、L16.h0、L22.h0）；只在这几个 head 内改回全注意力或换成 4-bit key 打分器（均无需训练），就把 MiniCPM4.1-8B 在 64K 的 MK3 从 54.1% 提到 97.5%，与全注意力持平。

> **TL;DR:** On RULER's multi-key needle task (MK3), the shipped block selector of MiniCPM4.1-8B at 64K drops to 54.1% versus 97.1% for full attention, and the paper shows with an exact-attention-mass oracle at the same budget that the budget is sufficient — the selector simply under-ranks the needle. A teacher-forced calibration probe isolates three "blind" KV heads (L9.h0, L16.h0, L22.h0) whose mean-pooled key summary dilutes the decisive token; giving only those heads full attention, or a 4-bit key scorer at the same budget, lifts MK3 to 97.5% (vs 63.1→96.0 for InfLLM-V2-8B at 32K against 97.8% dense) with no training, though the same fix costs up to 11.6 points on RULER common-word extraction.

## 核心贡献

- **诊断工具：同预算精确质量 oracle。** 用精确注意力质量 Top-K（而非部署打分器）在同一 shipped budget 下重选 block，把"排名错误"与"预算不足"分开：InfLLM-V2-8B 在 32K 上从 63.1% 恢复到 95.3%（全注意力 97.8%），说明预算装得下 needle block。
- **双读数校准探针（calibration probe）。** 教师强制答案 token，在每个 head 上读两个量：(1) selector 让渡的 needle 注意力 $F_h$，解释"为什么失败"；(2) 修好该 head 的选择后答案 log-prob 恢复的份额 $\Gamma_u$（沿 shipped selector→full attention 路径的 integrated gradient，16 点中点法），用来选 head。
- **"三个 blind heads" 的实证。** InfLLM-V2 家族四个 checkpoint 与 NOSA-8B 在 MK3 上丢分都集中在 L9.h0、L16.h0、L22.h0；这三个 head 在 5 个校准 seed、16K、以及 RULER 之外的 JSON KV 数据上都被稳定选出。单 head 全注意力增益分别为 +15.5 / +23.0 / +17.0，其余 61 个 head 中最大只有 +2.0。
- **resolution 理论解释。** 两 block 模型（Proposition 1）给出 blind window：精确质量在 $\Delta > \delta + \log L$ 时保留 needle，而任何随窗口平均 logit 单调的打分在 $\Delta < w\delta$ 时把竞争 block 排在前面；中间区间就是被 mean-pooling 吃掉的 needle。Proposition 2 则说明 key 量化只把 block score 移动最大 logit 误差，故"降精度"安全、"降分辨率"致命。
- **免训练修复与其代价。** 三种修复：flagged head 内全注意力、flagged layer 加大预算（4×）、flagged head 内 4-bit key 打分器（保留 shipped budget，侧缓存 <1% KV）。在 InfLLM-V2-8B 32K 上分别把 MK3 从 63.1% 提到 96.0 / 97.2 / 94.7%。
- **跨 selector 的统一规则与负结果。** 同一探针 + 阈值扫描（$\rho=0.9$ 差距、$\sqrt{2}$ 步长）在 SeerAttention-R gates、NSA、DashAttention 上都只需少量 head（如 Qwen3-8B 的 18/288），但在 MoBA 上摊到 513/1152 个 head，且 echo 打分几乎一样好——定位性失效。

## Key Contributions

- **A diagnostic that separates ranking failure from budget shortfall.** Exact attention-mass Top-K at the shipped budget, applied per token and per KV head, shows the selector ranks the needle block below its cutoff rather than running out of slots: InfLLM-V2-8B at 32K goes 63.1% → 95.3% (dense 97.8%), MiniCPM4.1-8B at 64K 54.1% → 97.5%.
- **A two-readout calibration probe.** Teacher-forced answer tokens yield, per head, the forfeited needle attention (why it fails) and the integrated-gradient fix gain over a gate that blends shipped and full attention (which heads to fix). Both readouts return the same three heads on the MiniCPM4-8B lineage.
- **A resolution account of the failure.** A two-block proposition gives the exact-mass margin ($\Delta > \delta + \log L$) and the pooled-score margin ($\Delta < w\delta$), predicting a blind window that matches measured per-token margins in the blind heads (e.g. L9.h0: exact keeps 89% of probed tokens, pooled loses 65%).
- **Training-free fixes with measured costs.** Full attention in flagged heads, a 4× per-layer budget, and a 4-bit key scorer at the shipped budget all recover most of the MK3 loss; the paper reports their byte cost and their accuracy regressions on aggregation and open-domain QA rather than hiding them.
- **One rule across selector designs, including where it fails.** The threshold sweep finds 5–22 heads for SeerAttention-R gates, 4 for NSA, 13 for DashAttention, and 433–513 for MoBA, where the localization claim collapses and echo heads match the probe.
- **Deployment evidence.** TTFT in OpenBMB's CPM.cu and decode/prefill attention time in the release kernels on one H100, plus a check that token-level selectors (DeepSeek-V3.2 DSA, LongCat-Flash-Lite-Sparse) show no such MK3 loss.

## Methodology

**Setting.** All experiments run deployed sparse checkpoints through their own kernels and caches, changing only which blocks are selected. The lineage is the MiniCPM4-8B backbone (32 layers, 32 query heads, 2 KV heads of dim 128): InfLLM-V2-8B, MiniCPM4-8B Instruct, MiniCPM4.1-8B, and an InfLLMv2-8B retraining. InfLLM-V2 scores blocks from keys mean-pooled over windows of 32 (stride 16) and maps window scores to blocks of 64 via max-pool, with forced initial block, 2,048-token local window, 34 forced blocks, and 62 free slots (96 blocks/token). NOSA-8B adds a query-agnostic eviction head. Cross-selector systems: SeerAttention-R gates on Qwen3-4B/8B/14B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B, the SeerAttention gate on Llama-3.1-8B, Dash-8B, NSA-8B, and MoBA fine-tunes of Qwen3-8B/4B.

**Oracle.** For query token $t$ and KV head $h$, the instrument replaces the shipped selection with $\mathrm{TopK}_{b\notin F}(M_b,K)$ where $M_b=\sum_g\sum_{j\in B_b} a^{(g)}_{t,j}$ is the exact attention mass summed over the query heads of the group (the head soft vote of Wu et al.). The forced set and budget are unchanged, so the recovered accuracy estimates the cost of the *choice* of blocks. The paper is explicit that this is a mass-optimal reference, not an accuracy bound.

**Probe.** On RULER MK3 prompts from a seed never used for evaluation, the probe teacher-forces the needle tokens and records (i) $F_h=(r_h(E)-r_h(S))\,m_h$ — lost needle recall against exact mass times the head's mean needle mass — and (ii) a gate $\alpha_u$ blending each query head's shipped and full-attention outputs, with fix gain $\Gamma_u=\frac{1}{|X_c|}\sum_x\int_0^1 \partial F_x/\partial\alpha_u\,d\beta$, evaluated by midpoint rule on 16 points. Summed over units the integrals recover the full-attention log-probability gap when no selection switches along the path; the authors report 0.61–0.89 of the gap recovered across checkpoints (Table 16). The first-order (attribution-patching) version is kept as an ablation and is shown to misrank L9.h0 last of 64 heads because signed effects cancel — a useful caution about patching-based head ranking.

**Head-selection rule.** Sort units by $\Gamma_u$, start at the largest, lower the threshold by $\sqrt{2}$ per step, give newly added units full attention on calibration prompts, and stop at the first threshold whose units close $\rho=0.9$ of the gap. Neither constant is tuned (Table 15 varies both). On the lineage the rule reproduces the three blind heads within one head; on other selectors it sets the count, which is the paper's way of measuring rather than assuming localization.

**Fixes.** (a) Full attention in flagged heads (reads every block of those heads; the release's kernels support it). (b) 4× free slots in the flagged layers, filled from the shipped ranking. (c) A 4-bit scorer: per-channel standardized keys quantized with the Max (1960) Gaussian step, a side cache adding <1% to the KV cache, scoring $\hat M_b=\sum_g\sum_{j\in B_b}\mathrm{softmax}_j(q_t^\top \hat k_j/\sqrt d)$ at the shipped budget, with three variants (decode only, +question tokens, every token). Attention itself always reads full-precision keys, and forced blocks are always read, so quantization can only reorder free blocks.

**Evaluation.** Primary task is RULER MK3 (haystack entirely of look-alike UUID key–value needles), plus the other 11 RULER tasks, HELMET (PopQA, NQ, HotpotQA, JSON KV), OpenAI MRCR, LongFuncEval, LongCodeU, RepoQA, OOLONG, and 17 task types for the worst-case analysis. Every comparison is paired on identical prompts; design choices used one seed, main results use five held-out seeds with 200 prompts each; every arm is a hook on the selector output that reproduces the released selection exactly when inactive, verified by kernel-call counting.

## Results & Highlights

- **MK3 at 64K, MiniCPM4.1-8B: 54.1% (sparse) → 97.5% (full attention in 3 flagged heads) and 97.5% (4-bit scorer, every token), against 97.1% dense.** The fix is level with full attention in every head; the +43.4-point recovery comes from three of 64 KV heads with no training.
- **MK3 at 32K, InfLLM-V2-8B: 63.1% sparse, 97.8% dense, 96.0% with full attention in the three flagged heads (+32.9), 94.7% with the scorer at every token, 85.6% with the scorer at decode only.** The control matters: full attention in the *other* 61 heads reaches only 65.2% (+2.1 over sparse), so the loss really is concentrated.
- **The oracle rules out a budget shortfall.** Exact mass at the shipped budget reaches 95.3% on InfLLM-V2-8B/32K and 97.5% on MiniCPM4.1-8B/64K (vs 63.1 and 54.1 shipped; dense 97.8 and 97.1). Exact mass at decode+window tokens alone is 95.3% and 97.5%; at every token of the flagged heads 94.7% and 96.9%.
- **Single-head scans confirm the three heads.** Isolated full attention gives +15.5 (L9.h0), +23.0 (L16.h0), +17.0 (L22.h0) MK3 points at 32K, versus at most +2.0 for the best of the other 61 heads; three random heads change nothing.
- **The rule transfers, but weakly, to SeerAttention-R gates.** Qwen3-8B: 29.8% sparse → 97.7% with 18 of 288 heads (dense 98.7), while 18 echo heads give 69.4% and 18 random heads 31.4%. Qwen3-14B needs only 5 of 320 heads: 54.5% → 96.9% (dense 99.7). Qwen3-4B: 16.6% → 87.8% with 9 heads. R1-Distill-Qwen-14B: 17.9% → 79.1% with 22 heads (dense 85.6).
- **NSA localizes, MoBA does not.** NSA-8B: 14.1% → 95.2% with 4 of 64 heads (dense 98.1), and the same three blind heads rank first. MoBA/Qwen3-8B: 21.1% → 89.6% but the rule needs 513 of 1,152 heads, and echo heads of the same count already reach 85.9% — the probe buys little when the loss is diffuse.
- **Real-benchmark transfer is only partial.** On OpenAI MRCR (8K–32K, 593 prompts) Qwen3-8B goes 29.2% sparse → 33.2% with flagged heads vs 36.0% dense, random 29.3%; on Qwen3-14B the flagged heads recover nothing (32.7% = sparse, random 33.0%, dense 35.2%).
- **No fix is free.** InfLLM-V2-8B/32K: full attention in flagged heads takes MK3 from 63.1 to 96.0 but common-word extraction from 45.5 to 33.9 — worse than dense's 51.6 — while the decode-only scorer keeps CWE at 45.6 and MK3 at 85.6. Across 17 task types the decode-only scorer's worst single-task regression is −0.9 points and it beats the shipped selector in 100.0% of 10,000 random task mixtures; the every-token scorer's worst is −5.5 (Common Words).
- **Precision is cheap, resolution is not.** On MK3 with the scorer in the flagged heads, 1/2/3/4-bit keys give 81.0/90.5/90.0/90.0%; averaging keys over windows of 1/4/8/16/64 gives 89.5/86.0/84.0/71.5/59.5%. Quest's block bound (22.0%) and Double Sparsity's label channels (34.0%) fall *below* the shipped selector (55.5%) on MiniCPM4.1-8B/64K, while Loki's 32 principal directions in every head reach 96.0% at 4,096 bytes/token — more bytes than the 1,168-byte scorer.
- **Deployment cost is modest.** Full attention in the flagged heads keeps 84% (32K), 88% (64K) and 89% (128K) of the TTFT saving that sparse attention provides in CPM.cu, and 91–98% of the decode-step attention-time saving in the release kernels; at 32K TTFT is 10.9 ms dense vs 12.1 ms sparse, so the absolute saving being preserved is small at batch 1.
- **Token-level selectors are a clean negative control.** DeepSeek-V3.2 with DSA scores 100.0/100.0/100.0 on MK3 at 32K/64K/128K versus 100.0/100.0/98.0 with every token selected; LongCat-Flash-Lite-Sparse 99.4/99.3 versus 99.5/99.3 — no blind window when nothing is pooled.

## Authors & Credibility

Wenhe Zhang (Yale University) and Boxing Chen (Huawei Canada) — a two-author paper, 25 pages including a substantial appendix of propositions, probes, negative controls and per-setting tables. Boxing Chen is a long-standing NLP/MT researcher at Huawei's research organization, which is consistent with the paper's familiarity with the InfLLM-V2/NOSA/MiniCPM releases and with the willingness to test other groups' selectors (SeerAttention-R, MoBA, DashAttention). The credibility signals are unusually concrete for this genre: a fidelity check that every hook reproduces the released selector exactly when inactive, kernel-call count verification, negative controls (scorer in the unflagged KV head, random free blocks, mean-pooled keys under the scorer's own criterion), five held-out RULER seeds with the design seed disclosed, per-sample scores plus data hashes in every result file, and a code release at github.com/zhangwenhe1007/blind-attention-heads. The weak point on credibility is breadth, not care: essentially one pretrained backbone plus one gate family, chosen partly because those are the checkpoints with a working dense mode.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The individual ingredients are borrowed — exact-mass oracles (COBS, Wang et al. 2026a), head-level calibration on synthetic retrieval (DuoAttention, RazorAttention), attribution patching and integrated gradients (Nanda 2023; Hanna et al. 2024). The contribution is the combination and, more importantly, the framing: use the oracle to prove the failure is a *ranking* failure, then use a path-integrated fix gain to pick heads, then quantify how concentrated the loss is per selector. That last move — treating "how many heads carry the loss" as a measurement rather than an assumption — is the genuinely new idea, and the MoBA negative result is what makes it credible rather than a sales pitch.

**Rigor.** Strong where it counts. Proposition 1 is not decoration: it predicts a blind window in nats, and Table 13 shows the measured margins land there (L9.h0 keeps the needle under exact mass on 89% of probed tokens but loses it under pooling on 65%; L26.h0, which retrieves, is 1.00/0.02). Proposition 2's predicted quantization noise (0.60/0.34/0.19/0.11 at 1–4 bits) matches measured noise (0.60/0.35/0.20/0.11) to two digits. The fidelity checks and the paired-prompt protocol are better than most sparse-attention papers. The integrated-gradient choice is also defended with a real ablation: the signed first-order score ranks L9.h0 *last* of 64 heads because its effect flips sign across prompts, which is exactly the failure mode Hanna et al. warn about.

**Weak baselines and comparisons.** Quest is implemented at block granularity and scores 22.0% MK3 at 64K — far below the shipped selector's 55.5% — so "Quest's bound falls below the shipped selector" is a claim about a deliberately weak instantiation, and the authors do say "at block granularity" and "mean-pooled keys under the same criterion," but a reader should not conclude Quest is a bad selector. SnapKV (68.0%) and Double Sparsity (34.0%) are similarly one-configuration comparisons. Conversely, the strongest external baseline, Loki in every head (96.0% at 4,096 bytes), matches or beats the localized fix (96.5–97.0% at 1,168 bytes) — which undercuts the *accuracy* argument for localization and leaves only the memory argument, as the authors themselves concede in Section 7.

**Missing ablations and non-monotonicities.** The most conspicuous unexamined result is Table 3: giving three heads full attention drops common-word extraction to 33.9%, below the 45.5% of the sparse model and far below the 51.6% of full attention in *all* heads. If the fix only touches three heads, it should lie between sparse and dense; it does not. The stated explanation — the CWE loss "sits in other heads" — does not explain why a three-head intervention is worse than both endpoints, and this deserves a per-head CWE breakdown. Table 12 also shows the rule keeping only 2 heads on InfLLM-V2-8B (0.94 gap) while the diagnosis names 3, a small internal inconsistency that is acknowledged but not resolved.

**Significance and scope.** The headline 54.1 → 97.5 is real but it is MK3, a haystack made entirely of look-alike needles — the authors chose it because it is where selectors fail most, which is legitimate for diagnosis but inflates the practical stake. On real data the sparse models are already close to dense (LongCodeU 74.1 vs 75.1; RepoQA 78.4 vs 79.0; MRCR 19.9 vs 19.6 — sparse actually ahead), with LongFuncEval at 64K the main exception (39.7 vs 48.3, recovered to 46.1). So the practical payoff for a general long-context workload is much smaller than the abstract implies, and Table 4's "beats shipped in 100% of mixtures" is dominated by RULER's 12 tasks. The three blind heads are also a single-backbone result: all four lineage checkpoints share MiniCPM4-8B weights, so "four checkpoints" is closer to one model than four, a limitation the authors state plainly but which bounds the generality claim. Finally, the calibration requirement is sharper than it sounds: a single needle among repeated filler sentences flags *no* head and leaves MK3 at 65.5%, so the method presumes the deployer can construct look-alike competitor prompts that resemble the real failure mode.

**Red flags.** None fatal. The latency story is measured in the regime least favorable to the sparse baseline (batch 1, weight-bandwidth bound, where dense and sparse decode differ by 1.2 ms at 32K), so "keeps 84% of the saving" is a share of a small number; the claim is honest but should not be read as an end-to-end serving win. The 4-bit scorer has no CPM.cu port, so its decode latency is never measured end to end — only bytes and kernel-level attention time.

## Strengths

- The oracle experiment is the right first move and settles the ranking-vs-budget question cleanly, with the token/head breakdown (decode vs question tokens) that makes the later fix design non-arbitrary.
- The two-readout probe is well motivated: forfeited attention explains, fix gain selects, and the paper shows they agree on the lineage but diverge elsewhere (NOSA-8B ranks a late head third by forfeited attention) — a real, reported discrepancy rather than a smoothed-over one.
- Proposition 1 gives a falsifiable, quantitative account (blind window in nats) that is then checked against per-token margins and against the precision/resolution table, which is rare in sparse-attention work.
- The honesty budget is high: costs on CWE and PopQA, the MoBA failure, the Qwen3-14B MRCR non-transfer, the single-backbone caveat, and the weak Quest instantiation are all in the main text.
- Practical artifacts: released code, flagged-head lists for six checkpoints, and a fix that is an add-on to the top-k path rather than a retrain.
- The token-level-selector control (DSA 100.0 at 32K–128K, LongCat 99.4/99.3) is exactly the right falsification test for a pooling-based theory, and it passes.

## Limitations

- **One backbone.** All four "lineage" checkpoints share MiniCPM4-8B; there is no second independently pretrained model with the InfLLM-V2 selector and a dense mode, so the three-blind-head finding may be a property of this pretrained model rather than of the selector family.
- **Synthetic-primary evidence.** MK3's all-needle haystack is adversarial by construction; real-data gains are small or absent except on LongFuncEval at 64K, and the abstract's 54.1→97.5 number is a 64K MK3 number.
- **Calibration depends on look-alike competitors.** A single needle in filler flags no head (MK3 65.5%), MK2 flags two heads, and MK1's essay haystack flags a different set, so the head set is only as good as the calibration distribution.
- **Localization is selector-dependent and sometimes absent.** MoBA needs 433–513 of 1,152 heads and echo scores match the probe; the method degenerates to "give most heads full attention," which forfeits the efficiency rationale.
- **No end-to-end latency for the cheapest fix.** The 4-bit scorer is not ported to CPM.cu; its cost is reported as bytes/token and kernel attention time, not wall-clock serving latency.
- **Head-set transfer is unproven beyond one gate family.** MRCR transfer works on Qwen3-8B and fails on Qwen3-14B, and only 8K–32K bins are used.
- **The per-layer 4× budget is thinly evaluated** (one seed at 64K, no real-text task beyond HELMET), and the CWE regression of the three-head full-attention fix is unexplained.

## Relevance

与"极端性能优化下的可靠性"直接相关：它把 KV-cache/稀疏注意力在长上下文检索上的退化定位到极少数的 head，并给出免训练的补救与代价表，属于"优化导致能力退化—定位—修复"的完整链条。其"降低分辨率（pooling）致命、降低精度（4-bit key）几乎无损"的结论，以及用 integrated gradient 给 head 归因的做法，也可直接迁移到量化误差传播与机制可解释性分析。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the three-blind-head pattern survive on a second, independently pretrained backbone with the InfLLM-V2 selector? The paper's own limitation says this experiment does not exist; it is the single most important missing result.
2. Why does full attention in three heads reduce common-word extraction to 33.9%, below both the sparse model (45.5%) and dense (51.6%)? A per-head CWE breakdown, and a check of whether the blind heads' full-attention outputs inject needle-like distractors into aggregation, would settle whether the fix is safe to ship.
3. Can the probe be run without gold answers — e.g. with a self-generated needle or a likelihood-based surrogate — so calibration does not require a labelled retrieval set?
4. How does the 4-bit side-cache scorer compose with KV-cache quantization already used in serving (e.g. FP8/INT4 caches)? Proposition 2 bounds score perturbation, but double quantization and per-channel scale interactions are untested.
5. Would continued pretraining with the blind heads' selector fixed cause the model to re-learn the same heads, or redistribute retrieval to other heads? This distinguishes "architectural blind spot" from "optimization accident."
6. On MoBA, is there any cheap criterion better than the fix gain — e.g. grouping query heads by chunk size or by their pooling window — given that 513 of 1,152 heads are flagged and echo scores suffice?
7. Do the 18 flagged SeerAttention-R heads on Qwen3-8B remain the right set at 64K–128K, where the paper only evaluates MRCR up to 32K?
