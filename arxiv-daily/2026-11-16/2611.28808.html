Why this matters

Probing a looped model at one fixed depth can mistake slow retrieval for missing knowledge. Timing, not capacity, is the variable.

Readout limits ≠ information limits — the paper is careful about this.

Critical edge

The untied baselines are trained with final-layer loss — the very protocol this paper shows causes late readability — so “looped exposes facts earlier” lacks its clean control.

see Limitations · untied control

Methodology

Five stages, one measurement story

  1. Train the looped stack

    Weight-tied 11-layer block, d=192, 3 heads, loop budget L∈{4,8}, loss on the final loop only. BioS synthetic biographies at N = 20K / 50K / 100K; five seeds per cell, 30 models.

  2. Read every recurrence

    Readable bits M(t)=H₀−CE(t) at each loop, decoded two ways: the native readout and a tuned lens whose per-depth affine map is fit on split A. A rank-16/32/64 sweep checks lens capacity.

  3. Control the comparison

    44/88-layer untied models at equal unrolled depth, plus eight “every-loop” twins supervised at all depths with their own per-depth readout heads.

  4. Decompose the geometry

    State variance explained by which person (key) versus which value (value), permutation-null subtracted. Split C is never trained, so it separates name structure from memorization.

  5. Intervene on multi-hop chains

    On released 4-layer, width-768 R6 / Rdyn checkpoints (200 entities, 10 relations, 2k facts), per-token probes locate the “frontier”; the rank-199 entity subspace there is patched with a donor's.

Measurement discipline: M is a log-loss reduction against source entropy, not a mutual-information bound — and a failed decode is not evidence of absence.

Strengths

What the design earns

  • Asks when and whether stored memory enters computation, not just how much is memorized — answered at prediction, geometry, and causal levels.
  • Split C is a clean negative control: key structure survives within 0.02 of split A while value structure sits on the permutation null.
  • The every-loop twin experiment argues against the paper's own most quotable claim, with matched final M reported for both arms.
  • Intervention controls go beyond the usual probe-plus-patch: equal-rank norm-matched subspaces, full-vector and aligned variants, plus same-entity different-prefix donors.
  • The retrained-seed re-analysis deflates Rdyn > R6 using matched curricula instead of trusting released checkpoints.

Limitations

Where to hold back

  • All evidence is synthetic: BioS biographies and a 200-entity toy graph. Natural-text transfer is untested.
  • The causal multi-hop results come from one released R6 checkpoint; “seeds” resample relation groups and donors, not weights.
  • The twins change supervision and readout architecture together, so the mechanism behind the vanished delay stays unidentified.
  • The untied baselines are trained only on their final layer — the exact protocol shown to induce late readability.
  • No compute- or accuracy-matched comparison; parameter matching narrows untied width to d=96/66, dropping one cell to 8.46 bits.
  • Frontier conditioning covers only 51.4% of test queries at recurrence 4, plausibly inflating steering rates.
  • Linear/affine readouts and second-order geometry can miss nonlinear encodings; the capacity sweep only goes downward.

Questions for Further Exploration

The experiments this paper leaves on the table

  1. Would 44/88-layer untied models trained with per-layer supervision and per-layer readouts close the 0.81–0.88 vs 0.25–0.65 gap? This single run decides architecture vs. loss placement.
  2. Inside the twins, is it the multi-depth loss or the per-depth LayerNorm readout? A 2×2 ablation is cheap and would settle the mechanism.
  3. Why does the value-structure midpoint drift later with load (0.50 → 0.68) while final M stays pinned at 8.93 bits — retrieval precision rather than capacity?
  4. Does the rank-199 entity projector still steer answers with 10K entities and overlapping relations, where the entity subspace cannot separate cleanly?
  5. Can the M(t) profile work prospectively, predicting inference-time loop needs better than final validation loss?
