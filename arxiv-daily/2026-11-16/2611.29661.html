Why this mattersRelevance

Accuracy equivalence is not conversion fidelity. Shipping INT8 needs a per-input check — a 200-example margin probe makes one nearly free.

Critical edgeCritique ¶1

The headline 4.07% barely exceeds the 3.44% that two calibration draws of the same recipe disagree on — so much of this churn is calibration lottery, not the converter.

Two draws bound the noise; they don't average it out
01Methodology

Treat conversion as a per-input agreement problem, then prove the instrument reads zero before claiming any signal.

PyTorch FP32 ref→ 4 converters × documented recipes→ 116 stacks→ top-1 diff per input→ flip rate + CI
  • Five checkpoints: MobileNetV3-Large, EfficientNet-B0, ResNet 50, DeiT-tiny, DistilBERT-SST-2. Evaluation: 7,854 ImageNet/Imagewoof images (20 classes, full 1000-way head) and 1,938 sentiment sentences.
  • Reference: PyTorch 2.11 eager, CPU, FP32, batch 1. Preprocessing runs once in numpy so every runtime receives bit-identical float32 tensors.
  • A flip is any input whose top-1 class differs from the reference. Rates get 2,000-resample bootstrap intervals over inputs; zero counts get exact one-sided Clopper–Pearson bounds. Accuracy counts as "equivalent" only if the paired 95% CI of Δacc sits inside ±1 pp.
  • Probe set: the 200 examples with the smallest p₁ − p₂ source margin, drawn from a 2,000-example pool and fixed before conversion. Calibration uses two disjoint 256-input draws, so every INT8 rate ships next to a redraw floor.
  • Preregistered claims C1–C3, thresholds, and the exact stack list. Controls K1–K4 (reference rerun, batch/thread/framework invariance, FP32 conversions, calibration redraw) run before any claim.
  • Core ML is counted as build success only — it cannot execute off macOS, so one of the four advertised converters has no flip rate.

Defect log: five documented-recipe failures (B1–B5) with minimal repros, two of which pass export and only fail at load.

05Two regimes, far apart

The margin mechanism only explains the polite one.

Accuracy kept · flips a few percent

ResNet 50 and DeiT: margin AUROC 0.90–0.97, every converter pair's lift ≥ 6.8; ExecuTorch and LiteRT share ResNet 50 flips 31.8× more often than independence.

2.42 – 4.07% flips

Accuracy broken · flips 10–98%

MobileNetV3 −2.57 to −55.35 pp; EfficientNet −9.33 to −84.25 pp. The same EfficientNet weights flip 16.97% under ExecuTorch but 98.13% under TensorRT.

10.19 – 98.13% flips

DistilBERT: dynamic recipes stay equivalent at 0.26% / 0.67% flips; ONNX static QDQ collapses it to −38.49 pp with 47.16% flips. No single op type carries the damage — all-float repair reaches 95.7–99.5%, but the worst single type, residual Add, only 61%.

07Credibility

One author, no affiliation — and unusually strong hygiene for a solo preprint.

  • Preregistered claims, thresholds and stack list, before the main runs.
  • Refuted claim left standing: C2 fails on MobileNetV3 (AUROC 0.68–0.92) and EfficientNet (0.63–0.84), with lift collapsing to 1.2 and 1.0. Reported with mechanism, not reframed.
  • Every rate carries a bootstrap interval, and every INT8 rate a calibration-redraw floor.
  • AI disclosure: an assistant wrote and debugged experiment code, drafted text and searched related work; all numbers are script-generated from saved outputs, all references opened.

Weak signals: no peer review, no second author or lab to vouch for the harness, no external replication of any flip rate.

08Strengths
  • Instrument-first order. A zero noise floor and a redraw floor are established before any claim — rare in quantization papers.
  • The redraw control is the methodological contribution. One cheap number (3.44%) tells a team whether observed churn is signal or sampling.
  • Exact bounds at zero counts — "0 of 7,854" becomes "≤ 0.04%", not "identical".
  • Almost-free artifact: 200 source logits, chosen pre-conversion, usable as one boolean release gate.
  • Independently useful defect log with minimal repros for four converter teams.
09Limitations
  • x86 proxy, not phones. ExecuTorch and LiteRT ran CPU kernels; ARM rounding could move rates either way. ExecuTorch portable kernels skipped ResNet 50 entirely.
  • Core ML never executed, so only three converters are measured for flips.
  • Thin claim support: C1 rests on 4 stacks from 2 INT8-tolerant architectures; C3's text recall CI is [0.54, 1.00].
  • 20 of 1,000 classes — flip rates on full ImageNet are "likely" higher, so neither 4.07% nor 2.42% transfers.
  • Default recipes only, making every rate an upper bound on a tuned deployment.
  • Unbalanced table: defects B4/B5 drop ExecuTorch INT8 on DeiT and TensorRT INT8 on DistilBERT — exactly the transformer vision row.
10Open questions
  1. What is the expected ResNet 50 flip rate over the calibration distribution — and how much of 4.07% survives a third and fourth draw?
  2. Do these rates and the probe recall hold on the full 1,000-class validation set? Does the 20-class subset bias flips up (fine-grained dogs) or down (easy classes)?
  3. Would the same 200-probe recipe work for an LLM decoder, where a "decision" is a token over a 100k-way vocabulary — and do low-margin token flips compound autoregressively?
  4. How do ARM/NEON kernels on a real phone shift the rates, and does the 0.988 probe ranking survive the shift?
  5. Can per-layer exclusions or larger calibration sets push flips below the redraw floor — and is that floor then the practical fidelity limit?
  6. Does a probe set transfer across checkpoints of one architecture, or must it be recomputed per model as the source-only procedure implies?
11中文速览

作者把同一个 checkpoint 分别用 ExecuTorch、LiteRT、ONNX Runtime、TensorRT 的官方 FP32/INT8 配方转换后逐样本对比 top-1 决策，发现精度不变（ResNet 50 仅 −0.15 pp）的 INT8 转换仍会翻转 2.42%–4.07% 的个体判断，而"从源模型选 200 个最小 margin 输入"的探针集能以 40/40 召回率抓出全部有翻转的栈。

相关性：直接命中算子精度、确定性与误差传播议题，把"top-1 精度等价"证伪为不足的验收标准，并给出一套几乎零成本的探针验收流程。
