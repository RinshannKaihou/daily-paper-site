> **一句话总结：** 在 IEMOCAP 四分类情感识别上，一个 7 专家 MoE 头在仅用标量门融合时路由坍缩到 1.88 个有效专家（熵 0.241），加上六对成对跨模态注意力后熵升到 0.537（有效专家 3.25，2.23×，p=0.035，d=2.55），但六种子下加权准确率 66.51 与 67.79 无统计差异（p=0.646），说明路由健康与任务性能在本场景下解耦。

> **TL;DR:** A controlled two-condition study of a fixed seven-expert MoE head on the 4-class IEMOCAP subset (4,936 utterances, session 5 held out). Scalar modality gating (EMoE) collapses the router to 1.88±0.35 effective experts at normalised entropy 0.241; adding all six directed pairwise cross-modal attention blocks (CMET+EMoE) raises utilisation to 3.25±0.76 experts (2.23× entropy, p=0.035, Cohen's d=2.55) and produces emotion-differentiated expert assignment. Yet weighted accuracy is statistically identical — 66.51±4.98 vs 67.79±4.33 (Δ=1.28 pts, p=0.646, 95% CI −4.7 to +7.3) — and run-to-run variance is unchanged (F=1.32, p=0.768). Seed noise alone spans 13.4 points (58.20–71.58), and the single most-collapsed run scored the highest accuracy. Useful as an honest negative result and a reproducibility warning for MER papers that report one run.

## 核心贡献

- **首次在低资源多模态情感识别中直接测量路由健康度**，而非从准确率反推专家专业化：在 IEMOCAP 上 7 专家 MoE 头坍缩到 1.88±0.35 个有效专家，熵仅 0.241±0.076。
- **证明输入侧的跨模态条件化可以修复坍缩**：保持专家头完全不变，只在融合前加入全部六个有向成对跨模态注意力（CMET），熵升至 0.537（2.23×，p=0.035，Cohen's d=2.55），有效专家 3.25 个（p=0.046），并首次观察到按情绪类别分化的专家分配。
- **给出一个反直觉的负结果**：路由修复带来的准确率提升不可检测（66.51 → 67.79，p=0.646），方差也无改善（F=1.32，p=0.768），即"诊断出坍缩"不等于"修复坍缩有益"。
- **量化种子噪声**：同配置下六个种子的加权准确率跨度达 13.4 个百分点（58.20–71.58），且坍缩最严重的运行（熵 0.155、1.53 专家）反而取得该条件最高分 71.58%，直接质疑单次运行的 IEMOCAP 对比。
- **方法学主张**：路由统计应作为诊断指标直接报告，且不应把大语言模型里的负载均衡损失想当然迁移到专家缺乏训练数据的低资源场景。

## Key Contributions

1. **Direct routing measurement instead of accuracy inference.** The paper measures per-sample router output on the held-out session and reports normalised routing entropy and mean effective expert count (per-sample perplexity), exposing that a nominal 7-expert head uses 1.88±0.35 experts under scalar fusion.
2. **A minimal, well-isolated intervention.** Both conditions share an identical seven-expert head, router, training schedule and data; the only change is whether the fused representation reaching the head has first passed through all six directed pairwise cross-modal attention blocks. This isolates "what the head is fed" rather than adding capacity elsewhere.
3. **A clean demonstration of decoupling.** Routing differences are significant (entropy p=0.035, effective experts p=0.046) while accuracy (p=0.646) and accuracy variance (p=0.768) are not, establishing that router health and task performance can dissociate in a small-data MER regime.
4. **Emotion-differentiated routing as a qualitative signature.** Under CMET+EMoE the four emotion classes distribute across three distinct experts; under EMoE three of four emotions share a single dominant expert.
5. **An explicit seed-variance audit.** Six seeds per condition, with the honest admission that a single run could support "a wide range of conclusions", plus the observation that the most collapsed EMoE run scored highest (71.58%) — evidence against the "specialisation ⇒ accuracy" narrative.

## Methodology

**Backbone (trimodal, no pretrained foundation model).**
- *Text*: frozen 300-d GloVe embeddings → BiLSTM → one Transformer block.
- *Speech*: 34 short-term descriptors (MFCC, chroma, spectral) → two Conv1D layers → BiLSTM → Transformer block.
- *MoCap*: head/hand/facial marker streams concatenated into a 200×189 map → three strided Conv2D layers.
- Each branch emits a 128-d embedding (t, s, m ∈ R¹²⁸).

**Expert head (identical in both conditions).** Fused representation f ∈ R²⁵⁶ feeds a softmax router w = softmax(W_r f + b_r), w ∈ R⁷, and each expert E_k is a two-layer network producing four class logits; the prediction is softmax(Σ_k w_k E_k(f)). **No auxiliary load-balancing loss** — deliberately, to observe the routing the architecture produces unaided, mirroring common MER practice.

**Two conditions.**
- *EMoE*: f_gate = g_t·t + g_s·s + g_m·m with Σg = 1, then projected to R²⁵⁶.
- *CMET+EMoE*: before fusion, every modality attends to both others across all six directed pairs (multi-head attention, 4 heads, d_k = 32). A per-dimension gate α_x = GELU(W_x[x; cross_x] + b_x) blends own and cross-modal content via x_out = α_x ⊙ x + (1−α_x) ⊙ cross_x. The authors note α_x is GELU- not sigmoid-activated and thus **not** constrained to [0,1] — an affine rather than convex blend. Outputs are layer-normalised, concatenated, projected to R²⁵⁶.

**Critical caveat the authors state themselves:** f feeds both the router and the experts, so the comparison isolates the effect of cross-modal fusion on the MoE head as a whole, **not** on the router input alone; CMET also adds attention and gating parameters.

**Data.** IEMOCAP four-class subset — anger 1,103, sadness 1,084, neutral 1,708, excitement 1,041 = 4,936 utterances. The smaller "happiness" class (often merged with excitement) is excluded. Sessions 1–4 train (3,838), Session 5 (unseen speakers) held out. Single speaker-independent split rather than 5-fold CV, chosen so six seeds per condition stay affordable.

**Training.** Adam, cosine decay 2×10⁻³ → 10⁻⁶, categorical cross-entropy with label smoothing 0.1, balanced class weights, batch 64, early stopping with patience 12. Checkpoint selection monitored the held-out session (acknowledged as optimising absolute numbers, but symmetrically for both conditions). Six seeds (42–47) for accuracy; routing statistics over three seeds (42–44) whose checkpoints were retained.

**Metrics.** Per-sample normalised entropy H_i = −(1/log K) Σ_k w_ik log w_ik (K=7; 0 = total collapse, 1 = uniform); mean entropy H̄; mean effective expert count Ē = N⁻¹ Σ_i exp(H_i log K), the average per-sample perplexity, with an explicit note that Ē ≥ exp(H̄ log K) by Jensen and that averaging per-sample perplexities (not exponentiating the mean entropy) is the intended quantity. Also reported: per-expert argmax share and the number of experts receiving >1% of samples. Means compared by two-sided Student's t-test, variances by two-sided F-test.

## Results & Highlights

- **Router collapse under scalar fusion.** EMoE reaches normalised entropy **0.241 ± 0.076** and **1.88 ± 0.35** effective experts of seven (Table I; p-values vs CMET+EMoE of 0.035 for entropy and 0.046 for effective experts). In the worst run entropy falls to **0.155** with **1.53** effective experts, **two experts capture 99.1%** of test samples and **three experts receive no samples at all** — i.e. five of seven experts are inert parameters that still appear in the reported architecture.
- **Cross-modal conditioning repairs routing.** CMET+EMoE reaches entropy **0.537 ± 0.145** (2.23× the EMoE value) and **3.25 ± 0.76** effective experts, with entropy p = 0.035 and a large effect size (Cohen's **d = 2.55**). Qualitatively (Fig. 2, seed 42), under EMoE three of four emotion classes are dominated by a single expert, whereas under CMET+EMoE the four classes spread across three distinct experts.
- **No accuracy benefit.** Weighted accuracy is **66.51 ± 4.98** (EMoE) vs **67.79 ± 4.33** (CMET+EMoE), a gap of **1.28 points** at **p = 0.646**, 95% CI **−4.7 to +7.3** — a gain larger than ~7 points is excluded at 95% confidence, a gain of a few points is not.
- **No stability benefit.** Run-to-run variance is statistically indistinguishable: **F = 1.32, p = 0.768**, contradicting the intuition that a collapsed router destabilises training.
- **Within-condition decoupling.** EMoE's most collapsed run (entropy **0.155**, 1.53 effective experts) achieved the condition's **highest** accuracy, **71.58%**, while more diffuse runs did not outperform it — an internal replication of the decoupling, albeit over three runs.
- **Seed sensitivity.** Under identical configuration EMoE accuracy spans **58.20%–71.58%** across six seeds (a **13.4-point** spread; the paper quotes 13 points) and CMET+EMoE spans **59.29%–70.86%** (**11.6 points**); one seed degraded both conditions simultaneously. The authors contrast this with frequently cited IEMOCAP results that report a single run with no variance estimate.
- **Metric sanity note.** All routing numbers are reported on the same held-out session used for early stopping, so absolute accuracies (66–68% WA) are upper bounds rather than clean generalisation estimates; the between-condition contrast is the defensible quantity.

## Authors & Credibility

Syed Hussamuddin and Mubashra Fayyaz, Department of Computer Science, FAST National University of Computer and Emerging Sciences, Karachi. The paper is formatted as an IEEE Signal Processing Letters submission (four pages) and is written with unusual methodological candour for a letter: it declares the test-session model selection, the three-seed routing statistics, the Ē vs exp(H̄ log K) Jensen distinction, and the GELU-vs-sigmoid gating subtlety in the equation itself. The acknowledgment states Claude (Anthropic) was used to draft and edit the text, with design, experiments, measurement and statistical analysis attributed to the authors — a disclosure that is increasingly common and does not itself undermine the results, though it means prose polish is not independent evidence of care. The group is not a well-known MoE lab; the work's credibility rests on the internal control design (identical head, identical schedule, two conditions) rather than on prior track record. Per-run routing measurements are offered on request rather than released — a small reproducibility gap for a paper whose central claim is about measurement practice.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The finding that MoE routers collapse is not new — it is standard knowledge in large-scale LM training, where auxiliary load-balancing losses exist precisely because of it. What is new here is (a) measuring it in a small-data multimodal MER setting, (b) showing that an *architectural* (input-side) intervention rather than a *loss-based* one repairs it, and (c) the decoupling claim. That is a legitimate contribution, but the paper positions itself against MoE-for-MER work [2–4] without ever comparing to it numerically: there is **no external baseline at all**, no reproduction of a published IEMOCAP MER result, and no comparison against a plain non-MoE classifier on the same backbone. Without a dense-head control we cannot tell whether 66–68% WA is good, bad, or simply what this backbone yields — the accuracy numbers are only meaningful as a *difference* between the two conditions, and that difference is null by construction of the claim.

**The intervention is confounded, and the authors say so.** Because f feeds both router and experts, CMET+EMoE changes the router's input *and* the experts' input *and* adds the attention/gating parameters. The paper is explicit that this isolates the head-as-a-whole. The missing ablation is obvious and cheap: route on the cross-modally conditioned representation while feeding experts the scalar-fused representation (or vice versa). As written, "cross-modal conditioning of the head's input repairs routing" is supported, but "the router benefits specifically from richer input statistics" is not. A second missing ablation is the load-balancing-loss arm — the paper argues such losses may not transfer from large-scale LMs, which is a claim that a single auxiliary-loss run would settle far more convincingly than an argument from priors.

**Statistical power.** The headline "no benefit" is a null result from n=6 with a 95% CI of −4.7 to +7.3 points. That interval is enormous relative to the effects typically claimed on IEMOCAP (1–3 points). The correct reading is "this study cannot detect a gain of a few points", not "there is no gain" — and the abstract's phrasing, "confers no detectable benefit", is appropriately hedged, while the title's "Without Improving Accuracy" reads as a stronger assertion than the CI supports. Routing statistics rest on only three seeds, which is thin for claims about entropy *distributions*; the per-class routing figure (Fig. 2) is a single seed, and expert indices are admittedly not matched across seeds, so the "three distinct experts" reading is illustrative rather than a measured statistic.

**Evaluation protocol.** Early stopping and checkpoint selection both monitored Session 5 — the same session used for all reported test numbers. The authors flag this and argue symmetry, which is fair for the between-condition contrast, but it does mean the 66–68% figures are not comparable to published IEMOCAP numbers obtained without test-session selection. Single split, not 5-fold CV; single benchmark; fixed K=7 with no sweep over expert count. Whether 1.88 effective experts is a property of the data regime or of K=7 in particular is untested — a K=3 head might show the same absolute utilisation and a very different normalised entropy.

**What is genuinely good.** The internal control is clean, the statistics are reported with effect sizes and CIs rather than bare p-values, the F-test on variance is the right test for a stability claim, and the paper is honest about the one result that most undermines a tidy story (the most collapsed run scoring highest). The seed-spread finding — 13.4 points under identical configuration — is the most practically damaging and most useful result in the letter for anyone reading IEMOCAP comparisons. That the authors resist the temptation to spin routing entropy into a headline accuracy win is worth noting; a weaker paper would have led with the 2.23× and buried the p=0.646.

## Strengths

- A genuinely controlled comparison: identical seven-expert head, router, optimiser, schedule and data; only the pre-fusion representation changes.
- Routing is measured directly (per-sample entropy, per-sample perplexity, argmax share, >1% coverage) rather than inferred from accuracy — exactly the diagnostic the paper advocates.
- Effect sizes and confidence intervals reported alongside p-values; F-test used for the variance claim rather than an eyeballed comparison.
- A striking internal consistency check: within EMoE, the most collapsed run had the highest accuracy (71.58%), which corroborates the between-condition decoupling independently.
- A 13.4-point seed spread quantified and connected to the widespread practice of single-run IEMOCAP reporting — a concrete, transferable warning.
- Unusually transparent limitation section: test-session model selection, three-seed routing stats, single split, fixed expert count, and the Jensen-inequality choice in the effective-expert definition are all stated rather than hidden.
- Actionable framing: router health as a diagnostic, not an objective; do not assume load-balancing losses transfer to low-resource regimes.

## Limitations

- **No external or dense-head baseline**, so accuracy numbers carry no absolute meaning; the paper cannot say whether this backbone is competitive on IEMOCAP.
- **Confounded intervention**: CMET alters expert inputs as well as router inputs and adds parameters; no router-only ablation, so the mechanism behind the routing repair is unidentified.
- **Headline null result is underpowered**: n=6, CI −4.7 to +7.3 — small real gains are not excluded.
- **Routing statistics over three seeds only**; per-class routing figure is one seed with unmatched expert indices across runs.
- **Test-session model selection** inflates absolute accuracy and breaks comparability with published IEMOCAP figures.
- Single benchmark, single speaker-independent split, fixed K=7, four-class subset with "happiness" excluded — generalisation to other MER corpora, to full 5-fold CV, or to larger K is untested (the authors list these as open).
- **No load-balancing-loss condition**, despite the paper's argument that such objectives may not transfer — the claim is made by reasoning rather than by experiment.
- Routing measurements available only "on request", not released, which sits awkwardly with a paper arguing for measurement transparency.

## Relevance

与「LLM Training Stability & Observability」中的 MoE 路由/负载失衡诊断直接相关：它把小数据场景下的 router collapse 当作可测量对象，并给出"路由熵改善但任务指标不动"的解耦证据，对判断负载均衡干预是否值得做有参考价值。其 13 个百分点的种子方差也属于可复现性与训练稳定性证据链的一部分，尽管评测对象是多模态情感识别而非 LLM 预训练。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. What happens with a router-only ablation — CMET-fused input to the router, scalar-fused input to the experts? Does routing diversity persist when the experts still see the impoverished representation?
2. Does an auxiliary load-balancing loss (the standard LM fix) repair routing *and* leave accuracy flat here too, or does it behave differently from the architectural fix? One arm would settle the paper's central transfer claim.
3. Is 1.88 effective experts a property of the data regime or of K=7? Sweeping K ∈ {3, 5, 7, 15} at fixed data would separate "collapse" from "normalised entropy on a fine-grained head".
4. Does the decoupling survive scaling the training set (e.g. all five IEMOCAP sessions with cross-validation, or adding MELD/CMU-MOSEI)? If experts need data to differentiate, the effect should soften as data grows.
5. Why did the most collapsed run score highest — is that noise, or is concentrated routing actually a *useful* inductive bias when the training signal is scarce? Three runs cannot answer this; the full six-seed routing dump would.
6. Would a probe on expert outputs (representation similarity, output disagreement) confirm that the "specialised" experts under CMET are functionally redundant, as the authors' fragmentation hypothesis implies?
