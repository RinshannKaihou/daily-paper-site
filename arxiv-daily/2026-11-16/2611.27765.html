Mechanism

two static decisions → one 3.65 µs search

What MegaMoE hard-codes, Weave decides per launch

MegaMoE · compile time

One fixed comm / compute warp ratio for every layer, on every SM. All warps keep one role for the whole layer. Tail closes with an all-GPU barrier.

→
Weave · kernel launch

CTA 0 reads this layer's routing counts (me, M, Din, Mout), scores four candidates, and picks the ratio and scatter path on device.

The skeleton is unchanged — persistent CTAs pulling tasks via atomicAdd, comm through registers, cross-GPU token dedup, per-expert block size BMe ∈ {32,64,128,256}. Only the two schedule decisions move to runtime.

Spatial within & across SM

Each SM keeps only a few TMA/MMA/scheduler warps resident; the rest flip between two hardware-supported ratios. The trick is that different SMs may run different ratios, so GPU-wide Nc varies continuously while every Tensor/Matrix unit stays fed — no new kernel variants.

Per-architecture pairs: SM103 (4,8)/(8,4) · SM90 (2,16)/(10,8) · CDNA3 (0,8)/(4,4). Pull bandwidth saturates at 888 warps (780 GB/s), and 4–32 warps/SM differ by <1% — so piling on comm warps alone buys nothing.

This is the structural change: MegaMoE locks each warp into one role for the entire layer; Weave releases roles mid-layer, which is why the scatter path has to be chosen at runtime rather than tuned once.

Cost model on device, in the launch

epi scatterT(r,epi) = max( T_pull(Nc), T_comp(r) + T_scat(Ne) )
comm scatterT(r,comm) = max( T_pull(Nc) + T_scat(Nc), T_comp(r) + T_hoff )
pull bandwidth boundT_pull = Din·H·ba / min( B_link, Nc·b_pull )

The same model has to know that a bigger Nc shrinks the GEMM tile BM(r) and therefore raises weight re-reads and padding — so the optimum is not “maximize comm warps”.

Cross-vendor honesty: Tcomp = max(Tmma, Tepi) on SM103 because TMEM decouples the drain, but Tmma + Tepi on SM90/CDNA3 where MMA and epilogue share registers.

Decision: 3.65 µs on SM103, 1.86 µs on SM90, 3.00 µs on CDNA3 — flat from S = 32 to 20K.

The search space is small — two intra-SM ratios × two scatter paths plus an SM-ID threshold — and the oracle is evaluated on the same 48 cells used to report the gap. A 2.6% gap to four candidates is a lower bar than a continuous optimum.

MegaMoE's head / middle / tail bubbles on Qwen3.5-397B consume a third of warp-time at small batches and still 15.8% at 20K. As S grows, the layer shifts from latency-bound to bandwidth-bound, which is exactly why a single compile-time split cannot stay optimal.

Trade-offs

strengths vs. limitations, side by side

Strengths what holds up

  • Design choices are derived, not asserted. Bubble accounting, the 888-warp / 780 GB/s saturation point and the epilogue scaling curve each justify one specific decision.
  • Genuinely cross-vendor. Two NVIDIA architectures plus CDNA3, with architecture-specific cost terms (max vs. sum) instead of one formula ported blindly.
  • The temporal schedule is the real idea. Enlisting epilogue warps into the head pull and turning idle comm warps into scatters breaks MegaMoE's role lock.
  • Honest ablation. −S−T−C scores 0.996× on SM103 and 0.638× at large S on CDNA3 — the authors measure themselves against a fair, weakened self.
  • Cheap enough to be usable. 1.86–3.65 µs, flat in S, with end-to-end overhead of −0.08 µs on SM103.
  • Orthogonality to EPLB is shown, not claimed. 32 redundant experts cut max/mean imbalance 1.37 → 1.27 at +12.9% latency; Weave still wins 1.274× raw and 1.128× under EPLB.

Limitations what is missing

  • No public artifact. “We will open-source soon” — so the 3–5× margins cannot be checked.
  • Latency-only, layer-centric. No throughput or goodput under a real scheduler, no KV-cache or continuous-batching interaction, only median slowest-GPU latency.
  • Single-node NVIDIA. EP16 is exercised only on CDNA3, against a baseline that serializes dispatch / fused_moe / combine into three kernels.
  • Synthetic numerics. randn weights and activations; the only correctness signal is a FP64 check applied to a baseline, not to Weave.
  • Unstudied operations. CUDA-Graph capture, threshold sensitivity, clock throttling and topology variation are all absent.
  • The stated limitation is the least interesting one. The paper flags only “not yet evaluated on non-GPU accelerators”.

Critical assessment

read before citing the headline

A Novelty is real but incremental in kind

MegaMoE already co-locates comm and GEMM warps on one SM. The delta is (i) a launch-time, cross-SM ratio, (ii) mid-layer role release, (iii) per-token ready counters replacing a barrier — each a known technique; the contribution is the combination plus proof that a 3 µs search is affordable.

B The S-profile is averaged away

One geomean pools 30 cells over a 600× range of S and six very different arithmetic intensities. The bimodality (1.05× large-model/small-S vs. 1.5–2.0× small-model/large-S) is never given a per-regime breakdown on SM103.

C Coverage gap in the EP sweep

Multi-node NVIDIA EP16/EP32 — precisely where inter-node skew and the routing-adaptive claim should matter most — is untested. AMD is the only multi-node result, against a structurally weaker three-kernel opponent.

D “Oracle” is a low bar

Four-ish candidates plus an SM-ID threshold is a tiny space, and the oracle is scored on the same 48 calibration workloads, so the 2.6% gap is not a held-out estimate.

E Graph capture is unaddressed

Selection happens per launch from that layer's routing counts; real serving captures whole forwards in CUDA Graphs. Weave benchmarks against a Graph+PDL pipeline but never reports itself under graph capture.

F What would convince the reviewer

An upstream NanoFlow / mKernel build on SM103, a multi-node NVIDIA EP16 run, CUDA-Graph-captured tokens/s at a fixed SLO, and a held-out cost-model evaluation.

Open questions

from the source's discussion
Is the per-launch CTA-0 selection expressible as a conditional node under CUDA Graph capture, and what does the 3.65 µs decision cost when 60 MoE layers replay per decode step?
What is the geomean restricted to the large models and to S ≤ 1K — the production decode regime rather than the pooled 30-cell average?
Does 1.30× hold against an upstream NanoFlow or mKernel build, and how much of the 3.26–5.00× is re-implementation quality?
How sensitive is the SM-ID threshold to clock throttling, and does the chosen ratio survive the ~100 µs of a single layer under DVFS?
Would a continuous relaxation (3–4 ratios per SM, or per-warp churn inside the middle phase) recover the remaining 2.6% at 1.86–3.65 µs of search?
On a multi-node NVIDIA EP16/EP32 fabric with IB instead of NVLink, do the head-pull and tail-reduce redesigns matter more or less than under EP8?
