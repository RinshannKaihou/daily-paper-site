Why this matters

Naive PTQ can leave a planner at 2–12% success while single-step predictions look fine: the action path, not the encoder, is the failure locus.

Critical edge

The paper's largest gains — DINO-WM results that beat FP16 by 2–4 points — rest on a single seed, well inside MPC run-to-run noise.

Methodology

bolted onto 5 PTQ bases

A JEPA world model plans by rolling its predictor forward over sampled action sequences, so error injected once is re-consumed at every step. WM-Quant keeps the quantization backbone fixed — symmetric per-channel weights, asymmetric per-tensor static activations — so each reported delta isolates one of three components.

  1. 01

    Early-encoder precision

    Activations of the first three encoder blocks stay 8-bit; weights stay at target width. Justified by invocation asymmetry: on Wall each replan encodes 2 frames but calls the predictor 15,000 times.

  2. 02

    Control path in floating point

    Action embedder and AdaLN modulation stay FP, forcing ζt → 0. Costs ~1.5% of DINO-WM parameters, ~7.5% of LeWM's.

  3. 03

    Rollout-aware scaling

    Separate activation ranges per rollout step, optimized jointly with encoder ranges under a rollout-level loss: Adam with a straight-through estimator through a 5-step rollout.

δt+1 ≈ Atδt + Btζt + εt+1  ·  L(Θ) = Σt wt‖δτ+t‖², wt = t / Σt′

Encoder error η rides one long-horizon propagator Φτ+n:τ; each predictor step gets its own shorter one. The linearization is exact only for small errors — a heuristic exactly in the W3A3 regime the paper targets.

Strengths

  • Names and formalizes a failure mode that single-step quantization metrics hide: error compounding through the predictor Jacobian, split into encoder, embedder and predictor terms.
  • Measures the right thing — Spearman rank correlation of CEM candidates shows why planning collapses, not merely that success dropped.
  • Composable by construction: an add-on to five static PTQ methods, so every delta is attributable to the three components rather than to a new quantization scheme.
  • Equal-bit-budget controls are the correct ablation for mixed precision, and they kill the "you just spent more bits" explanation outright.
  • Ships custom W4A4 kernels on real edge hardware, reports the ungainly 1.54–2.12× instead of the nominal 4×, and reports the one regression it found.

Limitations

read before citing
  • DINO-WM main tables are single-seed — and they carry the largest deltas, including results that beat FP16.
  • Effectiveness is conditional on rotational transforms; without DuQuant, frozen-DINOv2 baselines stay at ~0% on PushT W4A4.
  • All three knobs are hand-set: 3 encoder blocks (never swept), control path fully FP (never FP8 or 6-bit), and on LeWM "first 3" ≈ "random 3".
  • Per-step range learning is weakly separated from shared ranges: −6.8 points, CI [−15.3, +1.7] crossing zero.
  • No calibration-cost accounting for the Adam/STE rollout optimization; no code release, no consolidated CEM hyperparameter table.
  • Benchmarks are small and low-dimensional (PushT, TwoRoom, Wall), with no real robot and no HAQ or timestep-aware diffusion-PTQ baseline.
中文一句话总结

作者指出 JEPA 式世界模型在 MPC 自回归 rollout 中量化误差会逐预测步复合、使 CEM 候选动作排序退化为接近随机，提出 WM-Quant——由"早期 encoder 块保 8 bit、控制通路保 FP、以整条 rollout 而非单步为目标联合标定每步激活范围"三件套组成——在 LeWM PushT 上把 W3A3/W4A4 的规划成功率从 10.0%/20.0% 提升到 58.0%/81.3%（FP16 为 98.0%），且几乎不增加推理延迟。

Questions for further exploration

open threads
Q1

How large is run-to-run variance on Wall W4A4? A 3–5 seed replication decides whether 32/96/98/98/98 really beats FP16's 48/94/94/94/94.

Q2

Is per-rollout-step range learning distinguishable from a timestep-aware diffusion PTQ such as PTQD applied naively? Without that control, "rollout-aware" and "per-iteration-aware" stay conflated.

Q3

Does the control path need ζt = 0, or is FP8 / 6-bit enough? Intermediate precision would change the 7.5%-of-parameters accounting on LeWM materially.

Q4

Does Spearman rank correlation predict planning success across bit widths? A sweep would turn the paper's best figure into a usable design rule.

Q5

Would the same rollout-error framing improve KV-cache quantization or speculative decoding, where error also compounds autoregressively — but with token-level resets that may blunt it?
