> **一句话总结：** 作者在 Gemma-4-12B/31B 的长上下文 LoRA 微调中发现 bf16 与 fp32 的底层梯度几乎正交（最低两块中位余弦仅 0.02/0.01），用逐元素噪声实验证明 fp32 是可信参考、而 bf16 的前向状态误差已超出该反向通路的容忍度 30–130 倍；把图像 token 在 19–21 层 MLP 输出在反向传播中断开（前向不变）后，CT 病灶定位任务的验证 NLL 从 0.336±0.019 降到 0.296±0.003、in-box 从 75%±10 升到 88%±2，而 fp32 训练反而未能复现这一改善。

> **TL;DR:** In bf16 LoRA fine-tuning of Gemma-4-12B/31B on 2,400–3,600-token prompts, first-step bf16 and fp32 gradients in the lower third of the model are nearly orthogonal (lowest-block median cosine 0.02 and 0.01 on CT images, versus ≥0.98 in blocks 4–6), but the fp32 reference itself breaks under 10⁻⁴ element-wise noise (only 3 of 10 runs keep cosine >0.9), while bf16 forward states differ from fp32 by 0.3–1.3% per token — i.e. the backward path, not the bit width, is the problem. Detaching image-token MLP outputs in layers 19–21 in the backward pass only lowers CT lesion-grounding validation NLL from 0.336±0.019 to 0.296±0.003 and center error from 16.3 to 10.8 voxels versus the Standard bf16 baseline, at no step-time cost; a single fp32 training seed (NLL 0.332) does not match it.

## 核心贡献

- **现象**：在长提示（2,400–3,600 token）上，Gemma-4-12B/31B 的 LoRA 首步梯度在模型下三分之一处 bf16 与 fp32 近乎正交（最低两块中位余弦 0.02/0.01），上层则 ≥0.96；该现象跨 CT 图像、自然照片与长文本出现，且与 4-bit 量化权重无关，而 Qwen3.8-27B 基本不受影响（0.67–0.93）。
- **诊断**：给出一个"扰动容忍度"协议——对 fp32 前向逐元素加相对噪声 σ。σ≤10⁻⁶ 时梯度完全不变（25 次运行余弦 ≥0.99），σ=10⁻⁴ 时 10 次里仅 3 次余弦 >0.9；而 bf16 与 fp32 前向状态的逐 token 相对 L2 差为 0.3–1.3%，是"已经击穿梯度"的噪声的 30–130 倍。因此 bf16 下这些层的梯度几乎不含 fp32 梯度的信息。
- **定位**：用"状态替换"（把某几层的输入换成 bf16 前向存下的值）证明敏感性集中在 19–21 层的 MLP、且只在图像 token 位置：替换 19–21 层即复现全部崩塌（一致性 0.07 [−0.24, 0.75]），替换 15–18 层几乎无影响（0.93）；标准反向映射把梯度放大 11–206 倍，而在图像 token 处断开该 MLP 输出后一致性回到 0.94、放大倍数降到 6.8。
- **机制证据**：单个低信息量图像 patch 可占第 11 层梯度平方范数的 54%（一个靠近图像边界的暗、低对比度 patch），断开后最大单 token 占比降到 8%；同时这些 MLP 的前向贡献很小（置零仅使验证 NLL 从 0.653 升到 0.697，而置零文本 token 同层输出则升到 1.331）。
- **修复与训练验证**：仅反向断开（前向完全不变）使 300 步 SFT 的验证 NLL、中心误差、in-box 三项指标在四个种子中**每一个 Detach 运行都优于其他所有臂的所有运行**，梯度裁剪触发率由 99.7% 降到 18%，且该收益在冻结下层后消失、fp32 训练也无法复现。

## Key Contributions

- **A reproducible failure mode**: first-step bf16 vs fp32 LoRA gradient cosine near zero in the lowest third of Gemma-4-12B/31B on long prompts, while the upper third agrees at ≥0.96 — and it generalizes beyond medical images to natural photos and ~2,260-token text, so it is a context-length effect, not a modality effect.
- **A precision-agnostic diagnostic**: instead of assuming fp32 is ground truth, the paper measures how much element-wise relative perturbation the fp32 gradient tolerates (stable to 10⁻⁶, broken at 10⁻⁴) and compares that budget against the actual bf16–fp32 forward-state discrepancy (0.3–1.3% per token). The gradient is unusable not because bf16 is "less precise" in the abstract but because the backward path amplifies error far beyond its input tolerance.
- **Mechanistic localization by activation patching**: state substitution plus selective stop-gradients identify layers 19–21 MLPs at image-token positions as the amplifier (gain 11–206× → 6.8), with a documented dominant-token pathology (one patch holding 54% of the gradient norm).
- **A zero-cost architectural patch with training evidence**: a ~6-line `forward` hook that detaches image-token MLP outputs in three layers, leaving forward values bit-identical, improves all three task metrics in every seed and removes the gradient-norm spikes.
- **A negative result about precision**: an fp32 training run still shows clipping spikes (max norm 39,862) and fails to match the detach fix, arguing that "pay for more bits" is the wrong remedy for an ill-conditioned graph.

## Methodology

**Setup.** Models: Gemma-4-12B (48 LM layers, no separate vision encoder), Gemma-4-31B (60 layers), Gemma-4-E4B (42), Qwen3.8-27B (64). LoRA rank 16, α=32, applied to every attention and MLP projection; since B starts at zero only ∂L/∂B is nonzero at step 1, so that is what is compared (teacher-forced NLL of the answer). Agreement is cosine similarity computed in float64 over all B matrices of a layer block.

**Precision arms.** "bf16" = bf16 weights + bf16 arithmetic; "fp32" = same weights cast exactly to fp32 with PyTorch's deterministic math attention kernel. Controls: repeated fp32 gradients agree at ≥0.9989, repeated bf16 at ≥0.9989, and an fp32 backward pass on bf16 forward states reproduces the bf16 gradient at ≥0.996 — which localizes the disagreement in the *forward states*, not in backward rounding.

**Prompts.** CT lesion-grounding prompts of 2,400–3,600 tokens (2,100–3,200 of them image tokens from 2–3 CT slices); controls replace images with RealWorldQA natural photos or drop them, giving 200–350-token short text, optionally padded with natural text to ~2,260 tokens.

**Perturbation protocol.** Each layer output h is replaced by h + σ·h·ε with fixed-seed Gaussian ε, so each element moves proportionally to its own magnitude (mimicking rounding, whose relative step is ~1.2×10⁻⁷ for fp32 and ~7.8×10⁻³ for bf16). An earlier RMS-scaled variant is reported in Appendix B and explicitly excluded from the main claims because it perturbs small elements far more than rounding would.

**Localization.** Backward-map test: fix g = the fp32 gradient at layer 21, apply Jᵀ from layer 21 back to layer 11, and evaluate it on all-fp32 states vs. states where selected layers receive bf16 values via `h = stored + (h - h.detach())`. Separately, ablate three detach points (MLP output, gate activation, up-projection) at image-token positions in layers 19–21. Forward relevance is probed by zeroing MLP outputs in the untuned base model and measuring validation NLL.

**Training.** Gemma-4-12B SFT, 300 steps, batch 8 (accumulated per-example gradients), AdamW (β=0.9/0.99, no weight decay), lr 10⁻⁴ with 15 warm-up steps and cosine decay, global-norm clipping at 1, 65.6M trainable parameters, gradient checkpointing, ≤1,120 tokens per image, one RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell (96 GB). Four arms × four LoRA-init seeds, identical data order: Standard, Detach (stop-gradient on image-token MLP outputs of layers 19–21), Frozen (no LoRA below layer 19), Detach+Frozen; plus one fp32 Standard run. Task: output a liver lesion's center and bounding box given a marked key slice and a second view with voxel tick labels. 14,870 training examples (2,400 used), 200 validation examples of which the same 80 (80 lesions from 80 scans) are evaluated every run.

## Results & Highlights

- **Lower-layer gradients are uncorrelated across precisions.** On CT images, Gemma-4-12B's two lowest layer blocks have median bf16-vs-fp32 cosine of **0.02 and 0.01**, while blocks 4–6 stay at **≥0.98**; Gemma-4-31B's lowest block spans **−0.41 to +0.04** on CT and **−0.04 to +0.15** on natural photos, whereas Qwen3.8-27B on the same CT prompts stays at **0.67–0.93** and Gemma-4-E4B falls in between at **0.08–0.96**.
- **Context length, not images, is the driver.** Padded text-only prompts (~2,260 tokens) give lowest-12-layer cosines of **−0.47 to +0.36 (median −0.13)** on 12B — as bad as images — versus a median of **0.48** at ~730 tokens and ~**0.7** for 200–350-token short text; 31B gives **−0.41 to +0.40** at 2,170 tokens.
- **The fp32 reference itself has a small tolerance budget.** Under element-wise noise, all **25 runs at σ≤10⁻⁶ keep cosine ≥0.99**; at **σ=10⁻⁵, 2 of 10 fall below 0.9**; at **σ=10⁻⁴ only 3 of 10 stay above 0.9**; at **σ=10⁻³ only 1 does, with half turning negative**. bf16 forward states differ from fp32 by **0.3–1.3% per token** (median), i.e. **30–130×** the perturbation that already destroys the gradient.
- **Localization is sharp.** bf16/fp32 agreement at layer outputs holds to layer 21 (**median 0.96**) then collapses to **0.6 at layer 19 and 0.2 at layer 17**; substituting bf16 states only in **layers 19–21** reproduces the collapse (**0.07 [−0.24, 0.75]**) whereas **layers 15–18** barely matter (**0.93**), and detaching the image-token MLP output restores **0.94 [0.69, 0.98]** while cutting the backward gain from **21 [11, 206] to 6.8 [4.8, 8.9]**. Detaching the gate activation alone recovers most of it (**0.86**); detaching the up-projection does not (**0.27**, gain unchanged).
- **A handful of low-information patches carry the gradient.** On the prompt with the largest lower-layer gradient, **54%** of the squared norm at layer 11 sits on one dark, low-contrast image-border patch (not the lesion), and **ten tokens hold 91%**; after detaching, the largest share drops to **8%** on a text token. Two other prompts' top tokens are uniform blank patches with **105** and **212** identical copies.
- **The path is forward-dispensable.** Zeroing image-token MLP outputs of layers 19–21 in the base model moves validation NLL only from **0.653 to 0.697**, versus **1.331** for zeroing text-token outputs in the same layers and **1.274** for zeroing image-token outputs in layers 4–6.
- **Training improves on every metric in every seed.** Standard bf16: NLL **0.336 ± 0.019**, center error **16.3 ± 2.1 voxels**, in-box **75% ± 10**, median pre-clip grad norm **8.5**, max **23,307**. Detach: NLL **0.296 ± 0.003**, center error **10.8 ± 0.5**, in-box **88% ± 2**, median norm **0.63**, max **125**. Frozen: **0.334 ± 0.003 / 16.0 ± 0.4 / 74% ± 4**; Detach+Frozen: **0.336 ± 0.002 / 17.0 ± 0.5 / 71% ± 3**. Per-seed Detach NLLs are 0.291–0.298 against Standard's 0.320–0.364, a complete rank separation.
- **Clipping disappears.** In Standard runs **99.7% of steps trigger clipping**; under Detach only **18%** do, and the max norm falls from 23,307 to 125, with unchanged step time.
- **fp32 is not the fix.** The single fp32 run reaches NLL **0.332** (versus 0.364 in bf16 on the same seed) but still clips spikily (median norm **7.7**, max **39,862**) at ~**49 GB** versus 25 GB, and stays well above Detach's 0.298.
- **The fix is conditional on trainable lower layers.** Detaching changes NLL by 0.029–0.066 with layers 0–18 trainable but by only −0.001 to +0.003 when they are frozen; after detaching, bf16-vs-fp32 agreement in layers 0–11 rises from **−0.02 to a 0.43 median** — better, not resolved.

## Authors & Credibility

Single-author preprint by Jeong Hyun Lee, MD, Department of Radiology and Center for Imaging Science, Samsung Medical Center / Sungkyunkwan University School of Medicine. The clinical framing (liver lesion center + bounding box from CT slices with caliper marks) matches the author's domain, which explains both the unusual task design and the otherwise odd choice to study gradients through a radiology workload rather than a standard benchmark. There is no listed ML affiliation, no prior ML publication record visible from the PDF, and no venue or review status — this is a self-contained practitioner report, not a lab paper. Credit where due: the internal controls are better than most ML papers of this length — repeated-run cosines, an fp32-backward-on-bf16-states test to rule out backward rounding, a direct comparison of two noise models, per-seed tables, and an explicit appendix of interventions that were tried and not trained. All claims are scoped in the limitations section, including the two that most weaken the story (single fp32 seed, unlocalized long-text mechanism). Compute disclosure (one 96 GB GPU, 300 steps, 65.6M trainable parameters) makes the whole study reproducible in principle, though the private CT dataset and the Gemma-4 weights are not directly shareable.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The individual ingredients are old — mixed-precision error analysis, activation patching, selective stop-gradients, gradient-norm debugging — but the combination is a genuinely useful reframing: treat "bf16 vs fp32 disagree" as a *conditioning* question and measure the fp32 gradient's perturbation budget before paying for precision. The specific localization (MLPs of layers 19–21 at image-token positions, driven by a single low-information patch holding 54% of the gradient norm) is a concrete, checkable mechanism, and the 30–130× mismatch between bf16 state error and gradient tolerance is the paper's strongest single argument.

**Rigor — the parts that hold.** The control suite is unusually careful for a preprint. The fp32-backward-on-bf16-states result (cosine ≥0.996) genuinely rules out the obvious alternative explanation that the fp32 math kernel is just different, and the 25-run σ≤10⁻⁶ null result (all ≥0.99) establishes fp32 as a valid reference rather than assuming it. Reporting the RMS-scaled noise experiment in Appendix B even though it contradicts the main narrative (it breaks the gradient at σ=10⁻⁷, seven orders of magnitude below the element-wise threshold) is honest, and the decision to demote it is defensible since RMS scaling over-perturbs small elements — but it also reveals that "bf16 error exceeds tolerance" is a norm-dependent claim, not a scale-free one, and the paper never tests a third, arguably more faithful perturbation model (per-channel or per-tensor relative error).

**Rigor — the parts that don't.** The statistics are thin and, by the author's own admission, partly post hoc: the paired sign-flip test gives one-sided p = 1/16 — the minimum attainable with four pairs, i.e. it can only ever say "all four moved the same way" — and it was added after the first three seeds were seen, while the earlier unpaired permutation test (p = 1/70) was fixed after 3 of 8 runs had finished. Four seeds, one data order, one 300-step run per arm. The complete rank separation (every Detach run beats every other run on all three metrics) is more persuasive than the p-value, but with a single data order it cannot distinguish "the fix works" from "the fix works for this particular example ordering." The fp32 comparison rests on one seed and is therefore nearly uninformative: fp32 did improve over its matched bf16 baseline (0.364 → 0.332) and had the best in-box rate of any non-Detach arm (81%), so the claim that "more bits won't fix it" is supported mainly by the persisting spikes (max norm 39,862), not by a fair precision-vs-method comparison.

**The central conceptual weakness.** The detached gradient is *biased*: its cosine with the standard fp32 gradient is only 0.01–0.35. The paper is explicit that it shows "it trains better, not that it better estimates the true gradient," which is commendable honesty but also concedes that the intervention is not a repair of the backward pass — it is gradient surgery that removes a path. That opens an alternative explanation the experiments do not exclude: detaching a high-gain path could simply act as an adaptive regularizer / effective learning-rate reduction on image tokens, and the observed gain could come from reduced clipping rather than from better-conditioned lower-layer updates. The Frozen and Detach+Frozen arms (0.334 and 0.336 NLL, both with tiny variance) show that removing the ill-conditioned updates entirely is as good as Standard, which is consistent with "these layers contribute nothing useful under bf16" but equally consistent with "300 steps were not enough for lower-layer adaptation to matter." The finite-difference evidence (Table 4) is the closest thing to a causal check on direction quality, and it is weak and noisy: at η=10⁻⁴ the loss falls along the detached direction on all five prompts, but at η=10⁻⁶ the detached rows read −8.13 and +7.13, i.e. ratios with the wrong sign and enormous magnitude that the appendix explains away as a small-step limit effect.

**Missing ablations.** The detach configuration (layers 19–21, MLP output, image tokens) was fixed from first-step analysis on five prompts and then trained only in that one form. There is no training sweep over layer subsets (19 only vs. 19–21 vs. 18–22), no comparison against the gate-only detach that reached 0.86 first-step agreement, and no comparison against per-layer gradient clipping — which the paper itself names as the obvious alternative that "would still update the lower layers along the ill-conditioned direction" but never measures. Without that arm, the claim that the gain comes from conditioning rather than from norm control is not established, especially given that clipping fires on 99.7% of Standard steps.

**Data quirks and red flags.** The evaluation is 80 validation lesions from 80 scans, and the appendix states plainly that "we did not check whether any patient contributes scans to both" training and validation sets — a real patient-level leakage risk in a 14,870-example single-institution dataset, and one that would matter most for exactly the small-margin comparisons being made. Center error is Euclidean voxel-index distance *without* scaling by voxel spacing, so it is not physically meaningful and not comparable across scanners or datasets. The cross-model probes for 31B and Qwen use 4-bit NF4 weights (31B "does not fit in fp32 on our GPU"), confounding quantization with arithmetic precision, so the "Qwen is spared" contrast — the paper's main evidence that this is a Gemma-specific pathology — is measured under a different numerical regime than the 12B result. Finally the mechanism story is built on five image prompts; the paper says so ("five prompts are too few for a claim"), but the 54%-on-one-patch number and the blank-patch-with-212-identical-copies observation are then used rhetorically as if established, and the link to vision-transformer register/high-norm tokens is an analogy, not a demonstrated connection.

**Significance.** If the effect replicates, it is a practically important finding: a three-line hook that costs nothing at runtime and beats an fp32 run at half the memory is exactly the kind of result practitioners want. The negative result on fp32 is also useful — it says precision upgrades are not a substitute for graph-level debugging. But the evidence is one model family, one private task, one data order, five to ten prompts, and 300 steps; the title's generalization ("More Bits Won't Fix It") outruns what a single fp32 seed can support. The paper is best read as a well-instrumented case report with a sharp diagnostic recipe, not as a general law about mixed precision.

## Strengths

- The diagnostic protocol is the real contribution and is immediately reusable: measure the fp32 gradient's noise tolerance, compare it to the measured bf16–fp32 state discrepancy, and only then decide whether to add bits.
- Every step of the causal chain is supported by an independent probe — noise injection (tolerance), state substitution (which layers), detach variants (which component), token-share analysis (which positions), and forward ablation (whether the path matters forward).
- Controls are strong where they matter most: repeated-run cosines, fp32-backward-on-bf16-states, two attention kernels agreeing at ≥0.99, and two competing noise models reported side by side.
- The intervention is unusually cheap and non-invasive — forward values are bit-identical, step time is unchanged, and the implementation is six lines of a `forward` hook.
- Training results are reported per seed with full transparency about the post-hoc nature of the statistical tests, and the alternative explanation (Frozen reaching the same mean as Standard) is stated rather than buried.
- The limitations section is unusually candid for a preprint, including the admission that the fix produces a biased gradient and that long-text behavior is neither localized nor addressed.

## Limitations

- Training evidence covers one task, one model, 300 steps, one data order, and a single fp32 seed — and the private CT dataset plus 80-example validation set make the margins fragile.
- The mechanism and the fix were localized and tested only on Gemma-4-12B image prompts with 5–10 prompts and first-step gradients; long text prompts show a *different* depth profile (agreement already low at layer 21 for two of five prompts), so the mechanism there is neither localized nor treated.
- The detached gradient is biased (cosine 0.01–0.35 with the standard fp32 gradient); the paper shows faster loss decrease, not a better gradient estimate, and does not separate "better conditioning" from "reduced clipping / implicit regularization."
- No training ablation over detach scope (layers, component, token set), and no per-layer-clipping baseline, so the causal attribution to conditioning rather than norm control is incomplete.
- Patient-level train/validation overlap was not checked, and center error is reported in unscaled voxel indices, limiting external validity.
- Cross-model comparisons for 31B and Qwen use 4-bit NF4 weights, so the "Gemma-specific" claim rests on a differently-quantized regime; why Qwen3.8-27B is spared remains unexplained.
- No public benchmark, no released dataset (private clinical data), and no released patched model; the paper is a single-author case study and cannot establish generality.

## Relevance

这篇工作直接对应大模型微调中的数值稳定性与训练效率问题：它给出的"先测 fp32 梯度扰动容忍度、再比较 bf16 状态误差"的诊断流程，以及"反向断路径优于加精度"的结论，对做 LoRA/SFT 与多模态长上下文训练的人有可操作的参考价值。同时也是一份关于如何诚实报告小样本训练实验的范例。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the same 19–21 MLP amplification appear in other Gemma-4 sizes and in models with separate vision encoders if the 4-bit quantization confound is removed (e.g. 31B sharded across GPUs in bf16/fp32)?
- Would per-layer gradient clipping, or simply disabling the image-token MLP LoRA updates (equivalent to Frozen only at those positions), reproduce the Detach gain — i.e. how much of the improvement is conditioning versus norm control?
- Why is Qwen3.8-27B spared? Is the difference in attention-sink / massive-activation structure, in MLP gating (GeGLU vs. SwiGLU variants), or in how image patches are injected?
- Can the dominant-token pathology (one dark border patch holding 54% of the lower-layer gradient) be predicted from activation norms, and would a token-level reweighting achieve the same effect as a hard detach while remaining unbiased?
- Does the fix transfer to long-text-only fine-tuning, where the depth profile differs and agreement is already poor at layer 21?
- What happens beyond 300 steps and with more than one data order — does Detach's advantage persist, shrink, or reverse once lower layers have adapted?
