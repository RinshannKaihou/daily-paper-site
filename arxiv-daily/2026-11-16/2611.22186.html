Why it matters

A shipped 4-bit copy can silently change refusal behaviour; bits-per-weight and perplexity won't flag it, so each build needs its own paired audit.

Critical edge

The headline magnitude is not identified: judge, Llama Guard and blind human labels give +6.2, +11.0 and +19.4 pp for the same contrast — a 3× spread, with the largest number resting on 34 discordant pairs and one annotator.

Critical Assessment · label-source spread
Methodology

One build, four handles on its own error

01
Build

Three 3–4B chat models (Qwen2.5-3B, Llama-3.2-3B, Qwen3-4B), each 4-bit build converted from the same bf16 checkpoint: MLX g64 primary, plus g32, mixed 4/6, MXFP4, AWQ, GPTQ; a second llama.cpp runtime at Q4_K_M (5.00 bpw).

02
Probe

Forced start: 562 harmful_test prompts with 8 compliant tokens prefilled. Free start: 400 held-out WildJailbreak prompts, no prefill. Over-refusal checked on XSTest and benign jailbreak-styled prompts.

03
Label & test

Qwen2.5-14B judge, Llama Guard as a second construct, blind human labels for the primary cell. Paired exact McNemar, Holm within family, 10,000-resample bootstrap over prompts. The 8-bit build is a measured null, not an assumption.

04
Attribute

E = dequant(Q(W)) − W. Controls: mirror W−E, within-group error permutations, per-row Gaussian at matched norm, and a dose line W + aE for a ∈ {½, 1, 2}. Pre-registered decision rule.

Rate shift vs bf16, percentage points, judge labels, 95% CI. Scale −14 to +10; Qwen3-4B's free-start +3.8 does not survive correction.

  • The Llama drop is worse discrimination, not better refusal. Plain safe prompts barely move (−0.4 pp), but benign requests written in jailbreak style are refused 5.7 pp more often (9.5% → 15.2%).
  • 8-bit is a null on the rate, not on the text. Only 8/35/7% of replies stay token-identical and 3–10% of labels flip, yet rate shifts of +0.7/−1.2/−1.2 pp all cross zero.
  • A dense W+E build reproduces both shifts (+4.8 vs +6.2; −10.7 vs −10.3) with NLL gaps ≤ 0.0007 nats and 100%/99.5% first-token argmax agreement — so it is the weights, not the kernel.

Same bit width, different effect

Qwen2.5-3B: +4.1 pp at g32, +6.2 at g64, +8.0 under mixed 4/6, but MXFP4 shifts −0.7 while losing the most likelihood (+0.265 nats). Heterogeneity p = 4.8 × 10⁻⁵.

Likelihood blind spot

Llama at half the real error: +0.005 nats/token of NLL, "probably too small for a perplexity check," and already 5.8 pp less compliant (p = 0.0022).

Other quantizers

AWQ reproduces Qwen (+4.8) but not Llama (+1.0). GPTQ gives Llama −5.2. llama.cpp Q4_K_M moves neither and points the other way in both cells, while still changing 11–16% of labels.

Strengths
  • The 8-bit null is the right instrument. It rewrites 65–92% of replies and flips up to 10% of labels while moving no rate — so per-prompt churn is demoted from evidence to context.
  • Controls are built from the real error. Mirror isolates sign, within-group permutation isolates arrangement at fixed error multiset, Gaussian matches norm, and the dense rebuild closes the loop.
  • The half-dose build is a small gem. An invisible NLL change alongside a 5.8 pp compliance drop is the cleanest argument that likelihood is not a safety proxy.
  • Over-refusal is reported. Turning a "quantization improved safety" story into a measured discrimination loss is what most papers omit.
  • Pre-registration lists its own deviations — a rare, credibility-building disclosure.
Limitations
  • Three models, two vendors, one seed. Signs differ between models, so nothing generalises to "4-bit chat models"; Qwen3-4B is null in both settings.
  • Capability is only NLL on bf16's own replies, a proxy that structurally favours bf16 and replaced MMLU after registration.
  • Greedy decoding, one reply per prompt. Under sampling, the 4-bit-vs-bf16 disagreement excess over the bf16 floor is not significant.
  • Controls cover two cells only, and the permuted and Gaussian families disagree for Llama — the pooled mean hides a possible generic magnitude effect.
  • Label sources span 3×; human labels come from the author alone, with judge–human κ at or below the pre-registered 0.60 threshold.
  • The judge is itself a 4-bit Qwen2.5-14B, from the same family as a subject, with no bf16-judge control.
Questions for further exploration
  1. Which layers and matrices carry the shift? The perturbation builder already supports attention-only, MLP-only and per-layer errors, turning the projection analysis causal.
  2. Is the 4-bit judge biasing the labels? Re-run Qwen2.5-14B at bf16 on the same replies and compare label distributions.
  3. Does the effect survive sampling at shipped temperature, or is the Llama shift a property of greedy decoding only?
  4. Can the error's cosine with the RTN error, or its projection onto the refusal direction, triage which builds need the full 562-prompt audit?
  5. Why is Qwen3-4B null in both settings — a more robust refusal direction, or a compliance ceiling under forced start?
  6. Do the published audits' disagreements decompose into rounding-specific shifts once re-run with an 8-bit null on these models?
