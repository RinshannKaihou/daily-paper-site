> **一句话总结：** 作者把 LLM 预训练视为同时演化权重与 Adam 一阶/二阶矩的非自治动力系统，在"优化器相对坐标"下定义归一化敏感度分数，发现 loss 平滑时敏感度仍可相差 7 倍（0.163 vs 1.116）；在敏感 checkpoint 上仅 10⁻⁵ 的权重扰动经 200 步即改变 35.7% 的 top-1 预测和 16.2% 的基准答案，而验证 loss 几乎不变，用该分数择时施加 AdamW 稳定化可把 top-1 不一致压到 0.83%、贪心生成完全匹配率从 0.8% 提到 88.3%，代价是验证 loss 上升 0.11 nat。

> **TL;DR:** Treating a 76.8M LLaMA-style model on C4 (5000 steps) plus an OLMo-2-1B continual-pretraining run on Dolma as a dynamical system over the state (θ, m, v), the authors define a normalized sensitivity score in optimizer-relative coordinates and show that a 10⁻⁵ weight perturbation at a high-score checkpoint (step 2000) yields 35.7% top-1 disagreement, 16.2% benchmark-answer churn, and 0.8% greedy-generation exact match after 200 steps, versus 1.2% top-1 disagreement and 75% exact match from a low-score checkpoint (step 4000) — while held-out loss differs by at most 0.06 nats. Perturbing Adam's first/second moments is as consequential as perturbing weights (update distance 0.507/0.529 vs 0.505), and scheduling stabilization at high-score checkpoints beats matched-budget gradient/update-norm schedules by 7.0%/7.3% in separation (0.227 vs 0.244/0.245) at 0.11 nats of validation loss.

## 核心贡献

- **现象**：预训练中存在"隐藏敏感度"——checkpoint 的 loss 与聚合准确率几乎不变，但从该点继续训练的轨迹可能被 10⁻⁵ 量级扰动彻底改变。同一模型内敏感度分数跨越 0.163（step 4000）到 1.116（step 2000），约 7 倍。
- **诊断量**：把训练写成状态 $x_t=(\theta_t, m_t, v_t)$ 上的非自治映射 $T_t$，在"权重相对位移 + 更新方向相对变化 + log 预条件子相对变化"三项等权坐标下定义归一化有限窗口敏感度分数 $S_K$，并给出 Adam 切向递推（含 Hessian-向量积）的完整推导。
- **扰动通道的等价性**：不仅权重，Adam 一阶矩与二阶矩的扰动同样能造成近乎同量级的轨迹分离（10⁻⁵ 下 200 步更新距离 0.505 / 0.507 / 0.529），因此"从权重读敏感度"不可行；二阶矩控制差异被转换为更新的强度，一阶矩控制方向。
- **可控性**：用离线敏感度分数决定 AdamW 稳定化（降 lr、阻尼一阶矩、放慢二阶矩、放大 ϵ、限幅更新）的作用窗口，在同等激活预算下比按梯度范数或更新范数择时更好，输出层面把 top-1 不一致从 35.71% 降到 0.83%、生成 EM 从 0.8% 提到 88.3%。
- **可迁移性**：3 个随机种子的高/低响应比在 5×–46× 之间复现；OLMo-2-1B（1.485B）与 Muon/AdamW 混合优化器上出现同序模式。

## Key Contributions

- **A phenomenon, not a bug report.** Small, nominally negligible state perturbations produce regime changes in the update trajectory (0.960 vs 0.039 relative update distance at 10⁻⁵ between step-2000 and step-4000 checkpoints), concentrated in short windows and recurring across seeds and model scale.
- **A diagnostic with a derivation.** The score is built on a differentiated AdamW map (Eqs. 5–8) plus a relative geometry (Eqs. 10–12) that keeps weights, update directions, and preconditioner scaling on a common scale, instead of a raw Euclidean norm that mixes incompatible units.
- **Channel-resolved evidence.** 364 paired trajectories over θ, m, v, and batch order, with replayed batches, isolate state amplification from data-stream divergence and show that the second-moment channel carries the amplitude response (0.113 → 0.818 parameter separation from 10⁻⁶ to 10⁻⁴).
- **A control that uses the diagnostic.** An offline, replayable stabilization schedule keyed to high-score intervals; matched-budget comparison against gradient-norm and update-norm schedules is the paper's main causal test of the diagnostic's usefulness.
- **Scale and optimizer transfer.** One OLMo-2-1B continuation (28 conditions, 200/1000-step horizons) and a Muon/AdamW hybrid pilot, both reproducing the ordering.

## Methodology

**State and map.** Training is written as $x_{t+1}=T_t(x_t)$ with $x_t=(\theta_t,m_t,v_t)$ and AdamW as in Eqs. (1)–(4). Because the map changes with batch $B_t$ and the LR schedule, both branches replay *identical* future batches, so any divergence is attributable to the initial state difference rather than to a different data stream.

**Tangent recurrence.** A first-order perturbation $q_t=(q_\theta,q_m,q_v)$ propagates through the differentiated update: $h_t=H_t q_\theta$ (Hessian–vector product, never materialized), then moment tangents (Eqs. 5–6), the update-direction tangent (Eq. 7), and the weight tangent (Eq. 8). This is what lets a perturbation of optimizer memory influence the model even when weights start identical.

**Relative geometry.** Three normalized terms: $R(q_\theta;\theta)$ (weight displacement relative to weight size), $R(q_u;u)$ (change in the next update direction relative to its own size), and per-layer RMS of $q_\rho$ with $\rho=\log(\sqrt{\hat v}+\epsilon)$ (fractional change in adaptive scaling). Tensor-mean RMS averaging prevents a large embedding from dominating, and a floor $f=10^{-12}$ avoids division by near-zero entries. The three terms get equal weight.

**Probe.** Algorithm 1: draw Gaussian $q_\theta$, set $q_m=q_v=0$, iterate the local tangent $K$ times on a fixed diagnostic batch, renormalize each step, and report $S_K=\log n_{K-1}/K$ (Eq. 15). The authors are explicit that $S_K$ is *not* the finite-time Lyapunov exponent of Eq. (9) — the numerator is the final step's local response after renormalization, not cumulative growth — so it is a ranking statistic, validated empirically. Small model: $K=3$; OLMo: $K=1$.

**Perturbation protocol.** One branch unchanged, the other perturbed in exactly one channel at nominal $10^{-6},10^{-5},10^{-4}$ (largest dropped for OLMo), second moments clamped nonnegative. 200-step short continuations, 1000/2000-step for selected checkpoints. Readouts: relative separation in updates/parameters/gradients, symmetric logits KL, top-1 disagreement, greedy-generation exact match, and multiple-choice answer flips on PIQA, HellaSwag, ARC-Easy.

**Control.** Offline score $\hat\lambda_t$ triggers activation $a_t=(\hat\lambda_t-\tau)_+$ held for 500 steps; the controller scales lr, damps $m$ by $1-\gamma_m a_t$, slows $v$ adaptation by $(1-\gamma_v a_t)$, damps the preconditioner denominator, boosts $\epsilon$, and caps $\|\Delta\theta^{Adam}\|_2\le c_u\|\theta\|_2$ (Table 8: basic / tuned / strong). Matched-budget comparisons move the *same* strengths and interval count to gradient-norm- or update-norm-selected windows.

**Setup.** LLaMA-style 76.8M-parameter model (8 layers, d=512, 8 heads), C4, 5000 steps, peak LR 8×10⁻⁴, BF16 AMP, AdamW. OLMo-2-0425-1B (1.485B) continual pretraining on a Dolma v1.6 sample, 2000 steps, peak LR 2×10⁻⁵, AdamW8bit. 364 paired trajectories total (Table 9).

## Results & Highlights

- **Regime change at a single checkpoint.** At 10⁻⁵ weight perturbation, 200-step relative update distance is 0.960 from step 2000 but 0.039 from step 4000, against a 90-condition mean of 0.505 — the sensitive branch takes a step almost orthogonal to the reference (nearly its full norm) while the stable branch agrees to within 4% of its norm.
- **Loss says nothing.** At the sensitive checkpoint, 200 steps after a 10⁻⁵ weight perturbation, top-1 disagreement is 35.71%, benchmark-answer disagreement 16.21%, greedy-generation exact match 0.8%, symmetric logits KL 0.320 (Table 4, AdamW row), while average held-out loss differs by at most 0.06 nats (Figure 2C).
- **1000-step shadowing.** High-checkpoint continuations reach parameter separation 0.39–0.40 and gradient separation ≈0.85, with logits KL ≈0.20 and ≈27% top-1 disagreement; low-checkpoint continuations stay below 0.015 parameters and 0.03 gradients, with KL 4–6×10⁻⁴ and 1–1.5% disagreement. Greedy generations: 75% exact from the stable checkpoint vs 0% from the sensitive one; benchmark answers unchanged 99% vs 87%.
- **Moments are not second-class.** Across 90 state-perturbation conditions at 10⁻⁵, mean 200-step update distances are 0.505 (θ), 0.507 (m), 0.529 (v) — comparable responses in all three channels.
- **Seed replication.** Median across-channel response at high/low checkpoints: 0.960/0.039 (seed 1), 0.957/0.021 (seed 2), 0.236/0.022 (seed 3), i.e. 5×–46× ratios; high checkpoints land between steps 1500–3500 and low ones between 4000–5000, so the timing is not a fixed schedule. A 0.8×-LR run keeps a 0.783/0.160 contrast.
- **Data stream dwarfs state.** A different batch order gives update separation ≈1.39–1.43 at *every* checkpoint (vs ≤0.53 for any 10⁻⁵ state perturbation) and held-out loss gaps near 0.5 nats (vs <0.06 nats); at step 1000 it still shows 8.7% top-1 disagreement against ≤1.9% for state conditions, with parameter separation 3.37×10⁻³ vs 1.1–7.1×10⁻⁴.
- **OLMo-2-1B transfer.** Over 28 conditions/eight checkpoints: absolute loss difference 5.61×10⁻⁴ at 200 steps shrinking to 2.30×10⁻⁴ at 1000 steps, but symmetric logits KL barely moves (8.75×10⁻⁴ → 8.32×10⁻⁴), top-1 disagreement 2.27% → 2.30%, generation exact match 65.7% → 63.6%; mean absolute accuracy change stays below 0.75 points. Scores select steps 750 and 1250.
- **Muon hybrid.** Parameter separation contracts 2.24×10⁻⁴ → 3.60×10⁻⁵ between sensitive and stable checkpoints, while logits KL grows 6.5×10⁻⁴ → 1.0×10⁻³ and generation agreement falls 59.4% → 39.6%; answer flips over six conditions rise 2 → 14 (192 questions each). Muon momentum perturbations are as consequential as weight perturbations (median momentum/weight ratio 0.97 for update, 0.93 for parameter separation).
- **Controller frontier (Table 3, 200-step update separation over 10 checkpoints × 3 channels vs validation loss).** AdamW baseline 0.514 / 3.919 nats; sensitivity-basic 0.453 / 3.934; tuned 0.227 / 4.030; strong 0.137 / 4.143; gradient-matched 0.244 / 4.028; update-matched 0.245 / 4.088. Sensitivity timing wins by 7.0% over gradient and 7.3% over update selection at equal budget.
- **Output-level effect at the shared sensitive checkpoint (Table 4).** Sensitivity schedule: top-1 disagreement 0.83%, generation EM 88.3%, benchmark disagreement 0.07%, sKL 1.67×10⁻⁴ — versus AdamW 35.71% / 0.8% / 16.21% / 0.320, cautious AdamW 38.55% / 0.0% / 18.45%, SPAM 34.89% / 0.0% / 15.31%, and the gradient schedule 0.76% / 80.5% / 0.13%.
- **Probe cost.** On an RTX 3090 a training step is 71.54±0.55 ms; the three-iteration probe is 1.002±0.007 s and 11.61 GB vs 5.80 GB, i.e. ≈2.8% amortized time at every-500-step probing; controller steps are 86.56±0.10 ms (21.0% overhead).

## Authors & Credibility

It is a measurement/mechanism paper rather than a capability paper: the experimental budget is modest (one 76.8M model that they say was historically labeled "60M", one 1.485B continual-pretraining continuation), but the protocol is unusually careful for its size — 364 paired trajectories, replayed batches, three seeds, an explicit negative control via batch order, an optimizer-family pilot with Muon, and a cost accounting section. ## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The dynamical-systems framing of pre-training is not new (FTLE, SDE approximations, neural ODEs are all cited), and "loss is smooth while predictions churn" is a known underspecification observation (D'Amour et al.). What is genuinely new here is (i) putting Adam's moments *inside* the state and differentiating the full AdamW map, so a checkpoint is compared in optimizer-relative rather than weight-only coordinates, and (ii) using the resulting score as an offline *timing* signal for stabilization. That combination is a real contribution; the phenomenology alone would not be.

**Rigor — the hard part.** Table 5 undercuts the score's uniqueness: learning rate alone correlates with final update separation at r=0.984 and update norm at 0.979, versus 0.640 for the normalized score; the score's partial correlation after controlling for those is 0.692 at p=0.057 (six dof). Nested leave-one-checkpoint-out ridge regression improves growth MAE by 14.5% but with a 95% bootstrap interval of −3.3% to 30.7% and a sign-flip p=0.143. The independent cross-seed check (Table 6) is worse: of four targets, two get *negative* MAE reduction (−4.1% early growth, −3.4% parameter separation) and none have sign-flip p below 0.25. So the honest reading is "the score is a useful ranking heuristic on the trajectory it was fitted on, and its incremental value over cheap step-size statistics is not established." The paper says as much, but the abstract's "diagnostic metric" framing is stronger than the evidence supports.

**Design quirks worth naming.** (a) The main evidence is *one* 76.8M trajectory with ten checkpoints; nearby checkpoints share history, so "ten checkpoints" is not ten independent observations, and the three-seed replication reuses the same recipe. (b) The "1B transfer" is one OLMo continuation of 2000 steps on a model that already finished pretraining, and OLMo is probed with $K=1$ — so the multi-step renormalized tangent, the core of the score, is never validated at 1B scale. (c) The mitigation is evaluated at output level at a single shared checkpoint (step 2000), while the 7% separation advantage averages ten checkpoints × three channels; these are different objects and the paper is explicit about it, but the 88.3% vs 80.5% generation gap that headlines the control rests on 128 prompts and 64 greedy tokens per prompt. (d) The gradient schedule nearly ties on top-1 disagreement (0.76% vs 0.83%) and loses mainly on sequence-level exact match — the theoretically motivated distinction (KL averages steps, EM scores whole sequences) is plausible but is exactly the metric where the authors choose the comparison that favors them. (e) No random-window or uniform-interval schedule baseline is reported, and no sweep over the detection threshold τ after the fact; the activation budget is matched, which is good, but the *number and placement* of windows is inherited from Table 8 rather than ablated.

**The elephant: batch order.** The paper's own control shows that a different data stream produces update separation ≈1.39–1.43 at every checkpoint and larger held-out loss gaps (0.5 nats) than any state perturbation (<0.06 nats). In other words the dominant real-world source of run-to-run divergence in pre-training is *data ordering and numerical kernel differences*, not the checkpoint state — and the mitigation targets only the smaller term. That is not a flaw in the analysis, but it substantially limits the practical payoff: an engineer worried about "two runs disagree" should fix the data stream first, and the paper never tests whether its stabilization helps or hurts under a genuinely different data stream.

**Red flags / things to verify.** Checkpoints are BF16, and BF16 has roughly 8 mantissa bits: for a weight of magnitude ~0.02, one ULP is ~1e-4, so a "nominal 10⁻⁵" perturbation may be below the representable resolution. Appendix A says "finite perturbations must be measured after casting, since sufficiently small changes may be rounded away," which is honest, but the main text reports 10⁻⁵ results without stating the realized amplitude after casting — and the entire headline (35.7% churn at 10⁻⁵) depends on that number being real. Second, the metric's three-term equal weighting and the $f=10^{-12}$ floor are heuristic choices with no sensitivity analysis. Third, "benchmark disagreement" counts *changed* answers, not accuracy loss, and the paper's own OLMo numbers show <0.75 pp accuracy change — so for most practitioners the phenomenon is prediction churn at near-ties, whose downstream cost (e.g. after SFT, or in pass@k sampling) is asserted rather than demonstrated.

**Significance.** If the churn is real and the timing signal transfers, this is a cheap (2.8% amortized probe) way to make large runs more reproducible, and the optimizer-relative geometry is a reusable measurement tool. As it stands, the evidence supports a narrower claim: sensitivity is checkpoint-dependent, moment perturbations matter as much as weight perturbations, and score-timed damping is a viable point on the stability/optimization frontier.

## Strengths

- The state definition is the right one: including $(m,v)$ makes "same weights, different next step" expressible, and the tangent recurrence plus Hessian–vector products keeps the probe cheap enough to be practical.
- The relative-coordinate metric is well motivated — showing that rescaling the loss by $c$ rescales $m$ by $c$ and $v$ by $c^2$ while leaving the Adam direction unchanged is a clean argument for why a raw Euclidean norm is meaningless here.
- Experimental discipline: replayed batches, a batch-order negative control, three seeds, an explicit "repeated conditions are dependent, so we report checkpoint-level results" caveat, and a Muon pilot that tests the mechanism outside the Adam family.
- The mitigation is presented as a Pareto frontier (separation vs validation loss) rather than a single best number, and the matched-budget design genuinely isolates *timing* from *strength*.
- Unusually candid limitations: the score is not an FTLE estimate; the design was refined mid-collection and is not pre-registered; intervals include zero; OLMo uses $K=1$; cost is reported separately from training cost.

## Limitations

- One small-model trajectory supplies most of the evidence; a 76.8M model on C4 for 5000 steps is far from production-scale pre-training, where LR schedules, batch sizes, and $\beta_2$ differ substantially (OLMo uses $\beta_2=0.95$, not the 0.999 of the small model).
- The score's incremental predictive value over learning rate and update norm is not statistically established (Table 5/Table 6), so "diagnostic" overstates what has been shown.
- The control experiment evaluates behavior at one checkpoint with 128 prompts; variance across prompts is not reported, and no downstream task (fine-tuning, RL, evaluation-suite scores) is used to argue that churn matters.
- Batch-order divergence, which is larger than every state effect measured, is analyzed but never mitigated or matched against the proposed control.
- No analysis of how the score behaves across architecture or hyperparameter changes (depth, width, $\beta_1$, warmup), and the $K$-dependence of $S_K$ is only lightly explored.
- BF16 casting of nominal perturbations and 8-bit OLMo moment storage mean the realized perturbation is not exactly the nominal one; the paper flags this but does not quantify it.

## Relevance

直接命中"LLM 训练稳定性与可观测性"：它给出的正是"loss 曲线平滑但训练实际不稳定"这一被忽视现象的量化诊断量，并证明不稳定的载体包括 Adam 的 $m$、$v$ 而不只是权重——这对 checkpoint 可靠性、可复现性评估、以及"数值噪声/微小扰动是否会被放大"的问题都有直接方法论价值（与 SDC / 算子非确定性方向可对接：它给出了"小扰动→预测翻转"的放大机制与测量协议）。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. After BF16 casting, what is the *realized* relative amplitude of the nominal 10⁻⁵ perturbation, and do the 35.7% top-1 churn numbers survive at the smallest representable amplitude?
2. Does the sensitivity score add anything beyond learning rate and update norm in a *prospective* test — e.g. pick windows on run A, apply them on held-out run B never used for fitting? Table 6 suggests not, but it is retrospective.
3. What happens to the stabilization schedule under a genuinely different batch order, where separation is ~1.43 rather than ~0.5? Does timing still help, or does the data-stream term dominate to the point of making the control irrelevant?
4. Is prediction churn at near-ties actually harmful? The natural test: branch at a sensitive checkpoint, continue both, fine-tune both on the same SFT data, and compare downstream benchmark scores rather than top-1 agreement.
5. Why does the second-moment channel saturate (parameter separation 0.113 → 0.171 → 0.818 while update separation stays flat)? Is the flattening a real dynamical saturation or an artifact of the $\epsilon$-boosted denominator and the $10^{-30}$ square-root floor?
6. Can the same relative-geometry score be computed for Muon/orthogonalized updates with a comparable cost, given that Muon has no second moment to differentiate?
