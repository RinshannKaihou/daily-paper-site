One consumer GPU, minutes to hours, no data and no forward pass.

Critical edge
Unmeasured error rate

No exact convex-hull or LP solve is ever compared against the estimator, so a token wrongly declared “safe” is invisible by construction — and the entire empirical edifice rests on this heuristic.

01Methodology · three one-sided checks

A token t is argmaxable if some hidden state makes its row the strict winner: wt⊤h > wj⊤h for all j ≠ t. By Grivas et al. (2022), it is silenced exactly when its row lies inside the convex hull of the other rows — no direction can push it out, so greedy decoding can never produce it. This depends on the head alone, not on any corpus.

d_t = max‖h‖≤1 minj≠t (w_t − w_j)⊤h ≤ u
  1. 1
    Winning direction → safeStore one h that makes the token win; re-verify with a single matrix multiply.
  2. 2
    Trapping bound → flaggedA small dt caps the best margin it could ever achieve; this is an upper bound, not a proof of silencing.
  3. 3
    Exact row collision → proven silencedQuantization maps two distinct rows onto the same grid point, bit for bit. Lower bound.
Every reported count is a certified interval: collisions give the floor, flagged rows only raise the ceiling. Down to 3 bits the two ends coincide in nearly every cell. Fully batched, data-free, one consumer GPU.

02Why it happens

Silenced rows have conspicuously low norm — median 0.31 vs 0.46 overall on Qwen2.5-0.5B, 0.55 vs 0.99 on Llama-3.2-1B. Norm alone predicts silencing with AUC 0.99 / 1.00.

The picture fits the under-trained “glitch token” literature: rarely trained rows huddle near the hull’s interior, and rounding collapses them onto their neighbours. GPT-2 is the exception (AUC 0.63) — its losses are exact digit collisions, not rarity.

Normalized shares are reported for Latin, Korean, Thai and Hebrew only; Chinese and Arabic/Persian appear as raw counts. English-centric heads lose mostly Latin and mostly junk: GPT-2 1271 of 1274; 235 of Llama-3.2-1B’s 264 int8 losses are reserved special tokens.

  • Digits at 3 bits become impossible, not merely worse. GPT-2 per-tensor int3 silences 175 tokens — 70 numeric, including single digits and years. Across 400 WikiText-103 documents there are 52 positions (120 counting multi-digit years) where fp greedily decodes the right digit and the quantized head cannot produce it at all.
  • Finer scaling does not save it. At 4 bits with groups of 32, Llama-3.2-1B still silences 258–289 (vs 290 per-channel) and Qwen2.5-0.5B 2232–2234 (vs 2236–2238). The cause is rounding rows onto a shared grid, not coarse granularity.
  • On real text the damage is latent. Over 32M Wikipedia positions at 8 bits, a silenced token is never the correct next token for Llama-3.2-1B, Qwen2.5-0.5B or Qwen2.5-7B. Belebele Thai accuracy even rises 32.1 → 35.3 with 550 of 2550 Thai tokens silenced (SE ≈ 1.6).
  • Calibrated quantizers delay, not remove. AWQ keeps the head in fp16 and adds nothing; GPTQ’s 8-bit head adds +180 on Qwen2.5-0.5B, and pushing that head to 4 / 3 / 2 bits silences 91 / 135 / 259 on Llama-3.2-1B.

04Strengths

  • Asks a question perplexity structurally cannot answer, with a data-free check a practitioner can run before shipping a checkpoint.
  • Reports intervals with stated semantics — proven silenced / flagged / safe — instead of collapsing them into a binary.
  • The script breakdown changes practice: 27% of Korean against 0.3% of Latin is invisible to every averaged metric.
  • Mechanism corroborated three ways: row-norm AUC, the under-trained-token literature, and the per-script counts.
  • Unusually honest negative results — Belebele Thai improving, zero corpus hits, Qwen2.5-1.5B unaffected — all reported rather than buried.
  • Code and per-token measurements released, which is what a per-token claim requires.

05Limitations

  • Estimator error is unmeasured: no exact LP / hull solve on a tractable vocabulary, so the false-safe rate is unknown.
  • Harm evidence is thin: corpus audits found nothing at 8 bits; downstream evaluation is one 0.5B model on Belebele plus the GPT-2 digit case at 3 bits.
  • n = 1 vocabulary for the multilingual claim — all three Qwen models share one tokenizer.
  • Head-only analysis: exact for “can this token ever win”, only an upper bound on what real inputs realize; several deployment stacks never quantize the head.
  • Greedy decoding only: silenced tokens keep nonzero probability and can still surface under sampling.
  • Scheme coverage: SmoothQuant, QuaRot, SpinQuant and other rotation-based methods are untested, though the paper calls the mechanism generic.
  • Convention-dependent counts: strict tie handling inflates results by up to 42 / 175, 33 / 264 and 5 / 834.

06Open questions

What is the false-safe rate? Exact LP membership on GPT-2’s 50k rows — or a 5k-row Qwen subsample — would settle it in a day.
Why does Thai accuracy rise after 550 of 2550 Thai tokens are silenced? Is the quantized head simply sharper?
Does a higher-precision head only recover the lost Korean/Thai mass, and what is the byte cost on a 7B model?
Does the skew replicate on Gemma-2, Aya-23 or Mistral-Nemo heads, and does it track per-language corpus frequency?
Can it be prevented — regularizing row norms, or constraining the rounding grid so low-norm rows cannot collapse onto centroids?
Are silenced tokens a subset of Land & Bartolo’s under-trained glitch tokens, and do they predict glitch behaviour before quantization?
