Depth isn’t counted — it’s learned.

As pretraining proceeds, the network assembles computations that no proper subset of its layers can reproduce on its own.

Functional Depth Is Learned: Isolating the Computation That Layers Produce Only Together

JaeSeong Kim, KyungTae Lim · 2026-11-16 · Semyung University / KAIST

Möbius / Harsanyi dividend residual stream identity ablation CKA ≠ computation layer pruning Pythia · OLMo · Amber

Key metric

809×

Perplexity outlier — Pythia-6.9B, k=3

Deleting the connected component UT from the residual stream costs 809× clean perplexity. A norm-matched Gaussian direction at the same site costs 2.0×. Sign holds in 35/35 cells.

Why this matters

If depth is learned rather than given, pruning rules built on representation similarity can rank layers backwards — and silently cost you accuracy.

Critical edge

On Amber-7B — the only family outside the Pythia/OLMo lineage — deletion margins shrink to 1.1× vs 1.04× at k=4, so the causal claim sits at the edge of measurement resolution exactly where it must generalize.

01The estimand

Exact, not a proxy: a Möbius transform over layer sets.

fℓ(h) = h + gℓ(h) UT(h) = ΣS⊆T (−1)|T|−|S| FS(h) FS: forward pass keeping only layers in S; every other layer becomes identity.
  • Reading. UT is the change produced by all of T that no proper subset reproduces by construction.
  • Identity check. For |T| = 2 it equals the forward-expansion term gj(h+gi(h)) − gj(h) exactly (Eq. 5).
  • Scale-free form. ÛT = ‖UT‖ / (‖gℓk(FT<ℓk(h))‖ + ε) — measured in units of the last layer’s real write.
  • Training ratio. Rk = ÛTfinal / ÛTbase, comparable only within an order.

The authors are explicit: ÛT is not a fraction (components need not be orthogonal) and is not bounded by 1.

02How it was measured

Seven checkpoints across three families, one corpus, matched-norm interventions.

  • Subjects. Pythia 410M · 1B · 1.4B · 2.8B · 6.9B (8 checkpoints each, init → 134B tokens), OLMo-1B (4B → 3T tokens), Amber-7B (≤1.26T tokens). All public weights.
  • Probed tuples. Adjacent windows only, T = {ℓ, …, ℓ+k−1}, so the subset passes and the full pass share an input state; exact UT needs 2k forward passes, capping the study at k ≤ 6.
  • Intervention. Record h at the input of ℓ1, run all 2k subset passes, subtract UT immediately after ℓk’s output, then finish the forward pass.
  • Control. Same site, same per-token norm, a Gaussian direction instead — so any perplexity gap is attributable to direction alone.
  • Pruning test. Remove k consecutive layers ranked by coordination score vs. by CKA; Spearman ρ against post-removal perplexity. Correct sign: ρ > 0 for coordination, ρ < 0 for CKA.
  • Distribution match. Coordination, CKA and deletion damage all measured on WikiText-103 with the same token sample.

Coordination’s ranking is correct-signed in all 18 Pythia/OLMo cells (ρ = +0.05 … +0.94). CKA’s flips sign on Amber — the criterion the pruning literature already uses can actively mislead. Pythia-6.9B has both the weakest CKA–coordination association and the largest k=3 ratio: the model with the most higher-order coordination looks least redundant by similarity.

Strengths

  • Exact estimand: “the computation requiring all of T” has an operational meaning, and the deletion site falls out of the algebra instead of being chosen post hoc.
  • Write-relative normalization is derived, not tuned — the denominator is literally one term of the alternating sum.
  • Seven models, three families, five orders, 35 cells, one sign throughout — unusual breadth for an interpretability paper.
  • Matched-norm control is the right design, and the paper reports the near-null Amber cells and its own catastrophic controls.
  • The CKA result survives independently of the developmental story: it is a usable warning for similarity-based pruning.

Limitations

  • Adjacent windows only — coordination spanning distant layers, arguably the interesting case, is never measured.
  • k ≤ 6 because exact UT costs 2k passes; the high-order regime with the largest ratios stays out of reach.
  • One corpus, perplexity only, and deletion tested on the same distribution used to define UT.
  • Baselines differ in kind (init vs. 4B tokens vs. an unspecified Amber point), so cross-family Rk is not apples-to-apples.
  • No seed replication; bootstrap over windows only; high-order ratios sit near the ε floor.
  • No code release, and no partial correlation for the depth confound in the CKA analysis.

05Questions left open

What would move this from a well-scoped measurement to a mechanism.

  1. Does ρ ≈ −0.66 survive a partial correlation controlling for layer index and |i−j|, or is it a depth confound?
  2. Would a norm-matched direction taken from another layer’s real write preserve 35/35 — especially at Pythia-6.9B k=5–6, where the Gaussian control already costs 162×–898×?
  3. Can a sampled or Shapley-approximation estimator recover the k > 6 ordering cheaply enough to test saturation near the top of the stack?
  4. On Amber, where damage is within 1.05–1.2× of control, does deleting the k=3 component change any downstream metric at all?
  5. If coordination is a signature rather than a cause, does Rk at a fixed token budget predict which of two equal-depth models acquires a multi-step capability faster?
  6. Do the high-coordination early-interior bands coincide with the “sink” regions pruning already treats as redundant — and do they survive instruction tuning or RLHF?
