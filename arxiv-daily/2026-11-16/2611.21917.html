Headline recovery

54.1→97.5%

RULER MK3 @ 64K, MiniCPM4.1-8B — shipped block selector vs. full attention in the three flagged heads. Dense model: 97.1%.

No retraining, no extra budget: three heads out of 64.

Why this matters

A handful of heads decide whether long-context retrieval survives sparsification — and the paper shows pooling, not low precision, is the real killer.

少数 head 决定长上下文检索成败：降分辨率致命，降精度几乎无损。

Critical edge

Giving three heads full attention drops common-word extraction to 33.9% — below both sparse (45.5%) and dense (51.6%) — and the paper leaves the non-monotonicity unexplained.

  • Oracle. Replace only the block choice with exact attention mass at the shipped budget (forced set unchanged). Recovery therefore measures ranking error, not missing slots.
  • Probe. Teacher-force the answer tokens on fresh MK3 prompts. Per head, read forfeited needle attention Fh (why it fails) and an integrated-gradient fix gain Γu over a shipped↔full gate (which head to fix; 16-point midpoint rule).
  • Selection rule. Sort by Γ, lower the threshold by √2 per step, stop when the chosen units close 90% of the full-attention log-prob gap. Neither constant is tuned.
  • Fixes, all training-free. Full attention inside flagged heads; 4× free slots in flagged layers; or a 4-bit per-channel key scorer at the shipped budget, adding <1% KV bytes. Attention still reads full-precision keys.
  • Theory. A two-block model predicts a blind window where exact mass keeps the needle but any window-averaged logit ranks the competitor first; a second proposition bounds quantization noise by the worst logit error.

Backbone: MiniCPM4-8B lineage (32 layers, 32 query heads, 2 KV heads × 128) — InfLLM-V2-8B, MiniCPM4-8B Instruct, MiniCPM4.1-8B, one retrain; plus NOSA-8B and cross-selector systems.

Measured inside the flagged heads: L9.h0 keeps the needle under exact mass on 89% of probed tokens, while mean pooling drops it on 65%. L26.h0, which retrieves, sits at 1.00 vs 0.02.

Clean negative control. No pooling, no blind window: token-level selectors DeepSeek-V3.2 DSA (100/100/100 at 32K/64K/128K) and LongCat-Flash-Lite-Sparse (99.4/99.3) show no MK3 loss.

+Strengths

Right first experiment. The same-budget exact-mass oracle cleanly separates a ranking failure from a budget shortfall, and the token/head breakdown makes the later fix design non-arbitrary.

Falsifiable theory. The blind window is stated in nats, then checked against per-token margins; the quantization-noise prediction lands within 0.01 of measurement.

Unusual honesty budget. CWE and PopQA costs, the MoBA failure, the Qwen3-14B MRCR non-transfer and the single-backbone caveat all sit in the main text.

Shippable artifacts. Code, flagged-head lists for six checkpoints, and a fix that hooks the top-k path instead of retraining.

−Limitations

One backbone. All four "lineage" checkpoints share MiniCPM4-8B weights, so four checkpoints are closer to one model — the blind-head pattern may be a property of that pretrained model.

Synthetic-primary evidence. MK3's haystack is all look-alike needles; on real data sparse is already near dense, with LongFuncEval at 64K the main exception.

Calibration needs look-alike competitors. A single needle among filler sentences flags no head and leaves MK3 at 65.5%, so the head set is only as good as the calibration prompts.

Unexplained and unmeasured. The CWE regression below both endpoints has no per-head account, and the 4-bit scorer has no CPM.cu port, so end-to-end latency is never reported.

Open questions

What would change the verdict

  1. Does the three-blind-head pattern survive on a second, independently pretrained backbone with the InfLLM-V2 selector?
  2. Why does three-head full attention land below both sparse and dense on common-word extraction? A per-head CWE breakdown would settle whether the fix is safe to ship.
  3. Can the probe run without gold answers — a self-generated needle or likelihood surrogate — so calibration needs no labelled retrieval set?
  4. Would continued pretraining with the blind heads fixed re-learn the same heads, or redistribute retrieval? That separates an architectural blind spot from an optimization accident.

Scope check

Where the headline lives

The 54.1 → 97.5 is a 64K MK3 number on an adversarial, all-needle haystack chosen because selectors fail there. On LongCodeU, RepoQA and MRCR the sparse model is already close to — sometimes ahead of — dense, so the practical payoff for a general long-context workload is much smaller than the abstract implies.

Latency is measured at batch 1 in CPM.cu, where dense and sparse decode differ by 1.2 ms at 32K: the fix keeps 84–89% of the TTFT saving, but of a small absolute number.
