Why this matters

A cost knob is silently a capability knob. Single-budget evals can invert a model ranking by 30 points — and the model burns 5.5× fewer tokens doing it.

Anyone tuning GRPO caps should read budgets as a curve, not a point.
Critical edgedon't read it uncritically

The +34.8 pp headline is inflated: at 4K the base truncates 100% of AIME responses, so against a budget-forced base (9.44%) the honest in-budget gain is ≈+26.5 pp.

02 · MethodNeMo RL · GRPO

A cap set below where the model would stop

  1. GRPO on verifiable answers, rollouts hard-capped at 4k/8k (Qwen) or 1k/2k (Gemma, Nemotron).
  2. The cap truncates 95–100% of base AIME responses, so a success must fit inside the budget.
  3. Truncated rollouts without an answer get zero reward; gradient flows to short correct paths.
  4. Evaluation sweeps the budget 512 → 64K, reporting accuracy and mean length together.
  5. Two controls split “cannot use tokens” from “stops early”: budget-force the base up, force the trained model to continue.

No intervention wins. Dr.GRPO changes nothing (55.6). DAPO's penalty makes the high-budget deficit worse (50.0). Forcing an answer on truncated rollouts removes the deficit (77.2) and the in-budget gain (7.0).

The cleanest result is the SFT pair. Truncated traces reproduce the collapse (−38.4 pp); naturally short traces beat the base at 64K (+4.3 pp). Same length, same steps, opposite outcome.

Read as tokens-per-answer, the 8k model is competitive: it gives up 1–2 points on GPQA and LCB while spending a third to a fifth of the tokens. On overthinking-prone benchmarks that is a net win, not a failure. Gemma 1k is −1.0 / −8.2 at 16K.

07 · Strengthswhat holds up
  • The right instrument. Joint accuracy–length curves across a 32× budget range, not one number at the training cap.
  • Breadth where it counts. Three model families, two structurally different training distributions, two caps each, two OOD benchmarks.
  • The SFT dissociation is decisive. It moves the claim from “RL objective bug” to “length-censored post-training” with a clean length-matched control.
  • Controls aimed at the obvious alternatives. Forced-answer and forced-up runs attack early stopping and overthinking directly — and their costs are reported, not hidden.
  • Artifacts. Every checkpoint including ablations, per-seed tables, truncation curves, prompt templates, released under one HuggingFace collection.
08 · Limitationswhat to discount
  • 4–5B models only. Frontier models with explicit reasoning-effort controls are untested.
  • The dataset ablation confounds truncation with difficulty. 24/79/99% truncation also means 92/77/31% pass rate and 3.7k/19k/61k median length.
  • Most auxiliary runs are single-seed; the base is evaluated once while trained values average three seeds. Transfer deltas of ±1–4 pp are not precise.
  • Forcing the trained model upward is crude. Appending “Wait” up to four times is off-distribution and costs 36–51 pp in-budget — “cannot use tokens” is inferred, not demonstrated in-distribution.
  • Purely behavioral. No account of how the budget is represented, or whether early EOS comes from a decision token, entropy collapse, or prefix drift.
09 · Open questionsfor the next run
Q1

Does it scale? Is the learned constraint keyed to the numeric cap, the successful-length distribution, or an explicit budget signal in the prompt?

Q2

Mix caps in one batch — does the curve become the envelope of the caps, or does the smallest cap dominate?

Q3

Can importance-weighted replay of the rare >cap successes break the trade-off?

Q4

Is there a cheap diagnostic before step 300 — gradient mass from truncated rollouts, or drift in the successful-length distribution?

Q5

Why does Nemotron's Reasoning Gym run gain +0.6 pp in-budget yet still lose 10.6 pp at 16K?

Q6

Is internalization the late phase of the known “thinking grows during RL” dynamic, and does it track where mean rollout length stops rising?
