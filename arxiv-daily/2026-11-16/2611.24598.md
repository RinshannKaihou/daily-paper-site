> **一句话总结：** 作者把 LLaMA 3.1 8B 与 Qwen 2.5 7B 每条 CoT 隐状态逐个 patch 进 direct prompt，按“patch 后能否答对”标成 recovering/failed，发现两类状态线性可分（AUROC 0.84 / 0.80），再用每题自己的两类状态均值构造对比方向并把 direct prompt 末位隐状态一次性旋转过去：25 个任务平均准确率从 46%→85%（LLaMA，CoT 69%）与 52%→86%（Qwen，CoT 77%），生成 token 仅 19 个（CoT 为 197 / 245）。

> **TL;DR:** By patching each CoT hidden state into the last position of a direct-answer prompt and labelling the outcome against the gold answer, the authors obtain "recovering" vs "failed" labels for every reasoning token, show a linear probe decodes them out-of-fold (mean AUROC 0.836 for LLaMA 3.1 8B, 0.796 for Qwen 2.5 7B over 25 GSM8K / StrategyQA / BIG-Bench-Hard tasks), and then build a per-question contrastive direction \(d_q(\lambda) = \|c_q^+\|\,(c_q^+-\lambda c_q^-)/\|c_q^+-\lambda c_q^-\|\) from the recovering mean \(c_q^+\) and failed mean \(c_q^-\). A single norm-preserving SLERP rotation of the direct prompt's last state toward \(d_q(0.5)\) lifts macro accuracy from 46% to 85% (LLaMA; CoT 69%, +39 over direct, +16 over CoT) and 52% to 86% (Qwen; CoT 77%), with 19 generated tokens vs 197/245. The catch is that the direction is built from the question's own chain *and its gold-answer-labelled patch outcomes*, so it is an oracle upper bound on what the direct prompt's representation space can express — not a deployable method; the task-level averaged direction gives exactly 0 and −2 points, which is the paper's most interesting (and most quietly reported) negative result.

## 核心贡献

- **把 patch 结果当作标签。** 沿用 Mehrafarin et al. (2026) 的 activation patching，但把它从 GSM8K 算术扩展到 25 个任务、六类推理（算术、逻辑、时序、空间、因果、语言），并为每个 CoT token 的隐状态打上 recovering / failed 标签；LLaMA 3.1 8B 平均只有 17% 的 CoT 状态是 recovering，Qwen 2.5 7B 为 28%。
- **证明该标签线性可解码。** 用标准化 + L2 正则的逻辑回归探针做五折 out-of-fold 评估，25 个任务平均 AUROC 0.84（LLaMA，0.71–0.92）与 0.80（Qwen，0.52–0.93），并且探针 AUROC 与模型在该任务上的 CoT/direct 准确率、CoT 增益都不显著相关（Spearman |ρ| ≤ 0.31, p > 0.13）；跨任务迁移在 600 个任务对中有 587 个高于随机。
- **提出“问题级对比方向”。** 不用探针权重，而用每题 recovering 状态均值 \(c_q^+\) 与 failed 均值 \(c_q^-\) 构造 \(d_q(\lambda) \propto c_q^+ - \lambda c_q^-\)，并归一化到 \(\|c_q^+\|\)；λ=0.5 时在“靠近 recovering 均值”和“远离 failed 均值”之间取得最优。
- **给出范数保持的一次性干预。** 在生成前用 SLERP 把 direct prompt 末位隐状态旋转到该方向（只改方向、不改范数），避免加性 steering 破坏生成；25 任务平均准确率 46→85 / 52→86，token 数降到 19。
- **一个明确的负结果：方向是问题特异的，不是任务特异的。** 把同一任务所有问题的方向求平均得到 \(d_T(0.5)\)，再应用到这些问题的原问题上（最有利的情形），准确率仍为 46（LLaMA，direct 也是 46）和 50（Qwen，direct 52）；跨任务应用更是 600 对中有 465 对低于 direct，平均 −18 点，最差 −76 点。

## Key Contributions

- **Patch-outcome labels as a supervision signal.** Every CoT hidden state is patched into the direct prompt's final position; if the patched run then produces the gold answer the state is *recovering*, otherwise *failed*. This turns an interpretability probe into a per-token binary label with a causal definition, over 9,120 questions and 1.77M (LLaMA) / 2.24M (Qwen) labelled states per layer.
- **Evidence that the two classes are linearly separable and partly shared across tasks.** Out-of-fold AUROC 0.836 (LLaMA, layer 29) and 0.796 (Qwen, layer 25); within-position selectivity 0.284 / 0.263 against a control that can only exploit token position, and positive in all 50 probes. Same-reasoning-type transfer (0.710 / 0.672) beats cross-type transfer (0.654 / 0.623) with a permutation test at p < 10⁻⁵.
- **A per-question contrastive direction built from class means, not probe weights.** The paper motivates this with a Haufe-style argument plus measurements: over 150 probes, the class-mean difference and the probe's activation pattern have median cosine 0.99, while the raw weight vector is at 0.30 / 0.23 and less stable across folds (0.82 vs 0.99).
- **A norm-preserving single-rotation intervention** (SLERP, t = 1) applied once before generation, which the authors contrast with additive steering and with rotating at every token (the latter collapses to 6% accuracy).
- **A question-specificity result that bounds the practical value of the finding:** averaging directions within a task destroys the effect, so a single per-task vector cannot replace generating the chain.

## Methodology

**Setup.** Two open-weight instruction-tuned models (LLaMA 3.1 8B, Qwen 2.5 7B), 25 tasks: GSM8K test (1,319), StrategyQA labelled set (2,290), and 23 of 27 BIG-Bench-Hard subtasks (250 each unless noted), spanning arithmetic, logical, temporal/state-tracking, spatial, causal/commonsense, and linguistic reasoning. Four BBH subtasks are excluded (Dyck Languages, Word Sorting, Formal Fallacies for unreliable answer extraction; Salient Translation Error Detection because models often produced no chain). Greedy decoding, seed 42, ~8,000 GH200 GPU-hours total.

**Patching.** The CoT and direct prompts share the user turn and differ only in the system instruction ("you are a step-by-step reasoner, finish the reasoning with `Final Answer: #### <ans>`" vs "Answer only with <ans>. Do not show steps."). For each question the model first generates a CoT (capped at 400 tokens) and caches hidden states at three "late band" layers (27/29/31 for LLaMA, 23/25/27 for Qwen). Each cached state is then written into the direct prompt's final position \(z^\ell\) and the direct run is generated; the label is 1 iff the extracted answer matches gold. Answers are read flexibly (last number, last True/False/Yes/No, last standalone capital letter), and a generation with no readable answer counts as a failed patch.

**Probing.** A standardised \(\ell_2\)-regularised logistic probe, class-balanced by downsampling failed states to 50/50 within each training fold, scored out-of-fold over five folds of *questions* (so no chain is scored by a probe that saw it), with the penalty chosen by nested cross-validation. AUROC rather than accuracy because only 17%/28% of states are positive. Controls follow Hewitt & Liang: an *origin* control (fixed random label per position, task success rate preserved) and a *within-position* control (real labels shuffled among states at the same position), reported as selectivity.

**Direction construction.** For question \(q\), \(c_q^+\) and \(c_q^-\) are the means of its recovering and failed states at layer \(\ell\). Because the two means sit only 27° (LLaMA) / 21° (Qwen) apart, the direction \(d_q(\lambda) = \|c_q^+\| (c_q^+ - \lambda c_q^-)/\|c_q^+-\lambda c_q^-\|\) is used with λ = 0.5, which is close to \(c_q^+\) but pushed away from \(c_q^-\). The intervention applies SLERP between \(z^\ell\) and the direction with fraction \(t\) (t = 1 = fully aligned, norm preserved), once, on the last prompt position, after which decoding proceeds normally. Questions that lack either a recovering or a failed state (350 for LLaMA, 192 for Qwen; coverage 96% / 97%) get no direction and are counted incorrect under every intervention — a conservative choice.

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline macro accuracy (25 tasks, layer 29 / 25, t = 1, λ = 0.5):** LLaMA 3.1 8B goes from 46% (direct) to 85% (+39) with \(d_q(0.5)\), against 69% for CoT (+16 over CoT); Qwen 2.5 7B goes 52% → 86% (+34), against 77% for CoT (+10). Mean generated tokens per question: 19 for both models, vs 197 (LLaMA) and 245 (Qwen) under CoT and ~1 under the direct prompt.
- **Probe separability:** mean out-of-fold AUROC 0.836 for LLaMA (range 0.709 on Boolean Expressions to 0.921 on Temporal Sequences) and 0.796 for Qwen (0.524 on Causal Judgement to 0.928 on Geometric Shapes); 23 of 25 tasks exceed 0.70 for LLaMA and 23 of 25 for Qwen. The label is decodable where the model almost never succeeds: LLaMA answers Multistep Arithmetic correctly 0.4% of the time without CoT, yet its probe reaches AUROC 0.88 there.
- **Not just position:** within-position selectivity is 0.284 (LLaMA) and 0.263 (Qwen) at the reported layers, and positive for all 50 task×model probes; the within-position control probe itself only reaches 0.552 / 0.532 AUROC.
- **Transfer:** 587 of 600 task pairs beat chance for both models (mean off-diagonal AUROC 0.663 LLaMA / 0.630 Qwen); same-reasoning-type pairs reach 0.710 / 0.672 vs 0.654 / 0.623 for different types, and a 100,000-shuffle permutation test gives p < 10⁻⁵ for both models.
- **Direction beats CoT on 24 of 25 tasks for LLaMA and 19 of 25 for Qwen;** the exceptions for Qwen are mostly tasks where CoT is already strong (GSM8K 90 vs 83; Web of Lies 95 vs 87; Penguins 97 vs 92) plus the two Tracking-Shuffled-Objects variants with 5 and 7 objects (84 vs 77, 74 vs 62) and Multistep Arithmetic (89 vs 74), which the authors attribute to very low recovering-state shares (7–10%).
- **Ablations that isolate the effect.** Rotating instead toward the *failed* mean drops accuracy to 9% (LLaMA) and 16% (Qwen) — far below the 46% / 52% direct baseline, from the same chain and layer. Rotating toward the recovering mean alone (λ = 0) already gives 80% / 82% with 17 / 18 tokens, so the contrastive term buys +5 / +4 points. λ = 0.75 falls back to 83 / 84; t = 0.25 / 0.5 / 0.75 / 1.0 gives 54 / 71 / 85 / 85 for LLaMA, and t = 1.25 gives 82.
- **Direction, not norm:** replacing the state with the target rescaled to s× its norm keeps accuracy between 83 and 87 for s ∈ {0.5, 1, 2, 4} (only s = 0.25 degrades to 70/60), while the rotation fraction moves accuracy by 31 points — the gain is carried by the direction.
- **Once, not every token:** applying the same rotation at every generated token peaks at 53% (LLaMA) / 59% (Qwen) and collapses to 6% at t = 1 with >100 tokens generated.
- **Recovering questions both prompts fail:** of the 1,867 questions where direct and CoT are both wrong for LLaMA, \(d_q(0.5)\) gets 1,192 (64%) right; for Qwen it is 1,014 of 1,441 (70%) — and this despite the direction being built from a chain that ends in a wrong answer.
- **Tasks where CoT hurts:** Movie Recommendation 63 (direct) → 53 (CoT) → 86 (direction) and Ruin Names 71 → 62 → 93 for LLaMA; for Qwen, Movie Recommendation 66 → 60 → 94, Ruin Names 54 → 51 → 89, Causal Judgement 56 → 55 → 86.
- **Where the 10× token claim weakens:** on the tasks that actually require computation, the saving is much smaller — GSM8K 39 vs 179 tokens (4.6×) at 86% vs 85% accuracy, Multistep Arithmetic 55 vs 140 tokens (2.5%) at 82% vs 66%. The 10× average is dominated by tasks where the answer is a single letter or word (Boolean Expressions 3 tokens, Snarks 5, Hyperbaton 9).

## Authors & Credibility

Houman Mehrafarin (Heriot-Watt University and University of Edinburgh) and Ioannis Konstas (Heriot-Watt). The paper is a direct follow-up to Mehrafarin, Parekh & Konstas, "When chain-of-thought fails, the solution hides in the hidden states" (Findings of EMNLP 2026), which established the patching-and-labelling protocol on GSM8K; this work scales it to 25 tasks and adds the probing and intervention. The group has a track record in NLP/interpretability at Heriot-Watt, and the current paper is unusually careful about the parts that could have been oversold: it reports a control-probe analysis, a permutation test for the transfer claim, per-task tables at three layers, an explicit "questions without a direction count as incorrect" rule, and a blunt Limitations section. Code is promised at `github.com/hmehrafarin/hidden-state-directions`; prompts, fold-seeding scheme, optimiser (full-batch L-BFGS, gradient tolerance 10⁻⁶, double precision) and answer-extraction rules are all specified. The main credibility caveat is not sloppiness but that two of the 25 tasks are the authors' own prior failure case, and that the intervention is evaluated by the same person who chose λ, t and the layer — all three of which are shown to matter.

## Critical Assessment

**The headline number is an oracle, and the paper mostly says so — but not in the abstract.** To build \(d_q\) you need (i) the question's full CoT, (ii) a forward pass per cached CoT token to get its recovering/failed label, and (iii) the gold answer to assign those labels. So "19 tokens with a tenth of CoT's tokens" excludes the 197-token chain plus n patched forward passes that were required to find the direction. The honest reading is that this measures an *upper bound* on the accuracy reachable inside the direct prompt's representation space, not an inference-time method. The Limitations section states this plainly ("producing the direction of a new question without its chain and labels ... is left to future work"), and the paper reports that a first regressor predicting the direction from the direct prompt alone "leaves accuracy at the level of the direct prompt". But the abstract's framing invites the opposite reading, and a reader who stops there will think CoT has been replaced by a cheap rotation.

**The single most important control is missing: rotating toward another question's direction.** Question-specificity is asserted on the basis of \(d_T(0.5)\), the average over a task's questions. That is not the same test. Averaging unit vectors of near-orthogonal per-question directions largely cancels them, so \(d_T \approx 0\) (46 vs 46, 50 vs 52) is consistent both with "directions are question-specific" and with "the average of many arbitrary directions is noise". The decisive ablation — apply \(q\)'s \(d_q(0.5)\) to a *different* question \(q'\) of the same task — is never run in the main text. Section D.4's cross-*task* matrix (465 of 600 pairs below direct, −18 points on average, −76 at worst) is suggestive but confounds task shift with question shift. Without the within-task swap, the specificity claim is underdetermined.

**There is an internal tension the paper notices but does not resolve.** On Qwen's Causal Judgement the probe is at chance (AUROC 0.524, selectivity 0.042 — i.e. the labels are *not* linearly decodable), yet the direction built from the same labels raises accuracy from 56% to 86%. If the class means do not separate linearly, why does the mean-difference direction work so well there? Either the probe's regularised fit is not the right readout of separability (plausible, and the paper's own Haufe argument supports it), or a substantial part of the gain comes from a generic "commit to a computation / stop deliberating" perturbation rather than from anything label-specific. The failed-mean control (9% / 16%) rules out "any rotation helps", but it does not rule out "any rotation *away from the prompt's own state* helps", and no random-direction or shuffled-label direction control is reported for the intervention itself. That is the ablation I would most want before believing the causal story.

**The direct baseline is deliberately hobbled.** The direct system prompt is "Answer only with ⟨ans⟩. Do not show steps." — a hard constraint that forbids scratch work. Under it LLaMA scores 9% on GSM8K and 0% on Multistep Arithmetic. Standard short-answer prompting (or "think silently, then answer") does considerably better than 9% on GSM8K for an 8B instruct model, so part of the 46% → 85% gap is the direction *re-enabling computation* rather than *replacing CoT*. The paper half-acknowledges this ("the model works through intermediate steps only where a task needs them"), and the GSM8K token count (39 vs 179) shows the model still writes arithmetic. But no baseline with an unconstrained direct prompt is reported, so the "+39 over direct" is measured against a floor.

**No comparison to any other steering method.** Related work lists CAA / contrastive activation addition, ITI, latent CoT vectors (Zhang & Viteri, 2025), SEAL (Chen et al., 2025) and spherical steering (You et al., 2026), yet the experiments compare only to direct and CoT prompts. Adding a task-level CAA vector, or Zhang & Viteri's latent CoT vector, would cost little and would answer the obvious reviewer question: is the win from per-question contrastive construction, or would any well-placed late-layer direction do? The only related-method comparison is qualitative (their \(d_q(1)\) *is* the standard difference-in-means direction, and it is worse than \(d_q(0.5)\) by 5 and 4 points — a useful but narrow data point).

**Statistical hygiene is mixed.** Every headline accuracy is a single greedy decode at seed 42, with no confidence intervals and no second seed; differences of 1–5 points on 250-question tasks (e.g. dq(0.5) vs dq(0.75) at 85 vs 83, or layer 27 vs 29 for LLaMA at 75 vs 80) are presented as real. The paper does apply Wilcoxon tests where it matters (dq(0.5) vs CoT: p < 10⁻⁶ and p = 0.004), and the per-fold AUROC standard deviations (median 0.011) are reported, so the probing side is sounder than the intervention side.

**Other concrete gaps.** (i) Only two 7–8B models, both instruct-tuned; nothing on scale or on reasoning-trained models (R1-distills, o1-style), where the residual stream may already encode the answer and the intervention may be redundant or harmful — acknowledged, but it is the first thing a practitioner will ask. (ii) Only three late layers and only the last prompt position; Table 9 shows Qwen prefers layer 27 (85 vs 82 at layer 25), so the "late band" is not uniformly best and the layer choice is a free parameter tuned per model. (iii) 350 (LLaMA) and 192 (Qwen) questions have no direction and are scored incorrect, which is conservative and good, but it also means the reported 85% is conditional on 96% coverage and the missing 4% are the hardest questions (Shuffled Objects (7), Geometric Shapes, Logical Deduction (7)). (iv) Answer extraction by "last standalone capital letter" for 16 multiple-choice BBH subtasks is brittle; four subtasks were dropped for exactly this reason, so the 25-task suite is a filtered subset and the exclusion is not random with respect to difficulty.

**What is genuinely good.** The patch-outcome labelling is a clean, causally grounded target that avoids the usual "probe accuracy ≠ causal relevance" trap, and the paper takes that trap seriously enough to check probe-weight vs class-mean geometry (median cosine 0.30 vs 0.99) instead of silently steering with probe weights. The within-position control is the right control and it passes. The negative results — failed mean destroys accuracy (9%), task-level averaging gives nothing, rotating every token is catastrophic (6%), and cross-task transfer of task directions is actively harmful (−18 points) — are reported with the same prominence as the positive ones, which is rarer than it should be. And the core scientific claim, that recovering and failed CoT states occupy systematically different regions of a *shared, partly task-general* representation space, is well supported and is worth more than the headline accuracy.

## Strengths

- Causal, per-token labels instead of correlational probe targets; the whole analysis hangs off an intervention that is defined before any direction is built.
- Methodologically careful probing: out-of-fold scoring at the question level, nested penalty selection, class-balanced training with natural-distribution evaluation, and two Hewitt-Liang control tasks with reported selectivity.
- The direction is built from class means, and the paper *measures* why probe weights are the wrong vector (pattern vs weight cosine 0.99 vs 0.30; fold-to-fold stability 0.99 vs 0.82) rather than asserting it.
- Strong, well-chosen ablations: failed-mean target, λ sweep, rotation fraction t, norm rescaling s, per-token rotation, task-level averaging, cross-task application, three layers per model.
- Honest accounting: questions without a direction counted incorrect; four BBH subtasks excluded and named; the failed regressor attempt reported in Limitations; greedy decoding, seed and 8,000 GPU-hour budget disclosed.
- The negative result (question-specific, not task-specific directions) is scientifically the most interesting part and is not buried.
- Release of prompts, fold seeding, optimiser details and per-task tables makes replication feasible in principle.

## Limitations

- **Not deployable as stated.** Each evaluation question needs its own chain, its own patch labels and its own gold answer; the amortised cost is strictly higher than CoT, and the paper's own single attempt at predicting the direction from the direct prompt alone failed to beat the direct baseline.
- **No within-task cross-question direction swap**, so the "question-specific" conclusion rests on an averaged direction that may simply be a cancelling mean.
- **No random-direction / shuffled-label intervention control**, which is the natural way to test whether the effect is specific to the label structure or generic to perturbing the pre-generation state.
- **Narrow model and task scope:** two 7–8B instruct models, short auto-checkable answers, 25 of 29 candidate tasks, four BBH subtasks dropped for extraction brittleness.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity is real but only lightly probed:** λ (85 → 83 at 0.75), t (85 → 71 at 0.5), layer (Qwen 85 at 27 vs 82 at 25), all selected per model on the evaluation tasks with no held-out split for the intervention itself.
- **Single seed, greedy decoding, no error bars** on any accuracy number; the behavioural claims at the 1–3 point level are not statistically supported.
- **"A tenth of CoT's tokens" is task-composition dependent** — 4.6× on GSM8K and 2.5× on Multistep Arithmetic — and does not include the chain that had to be generated to build the direction.
- **Probe–intervention inconsistency on Qwen Causal Judgement** (AUROC 0.52 yet 56% → 86%) is noted but not explained.
- The training-dataset angle (question–direction pairs as supervision) is promised repeatedly but not attempted beyond one failed regressor, so the paper's forward-looking claim is unvalidated.

## Relevance

直接命中 **LLM Hidden States & Interpretability**：用 activation patching 给 CoT 隐状态打因果标签、用线性探针检验可解码性、再在表征空间里构造并施加方向，是"探针相关性 vs 干预因果性"这条线上少见的正面案例（而且它自己给出了 task-level 方向完全无效的负结果）。对做推理效率/隐式计算的研究者也有参考价值：它量化了 direct prompt 的表征空间里究竟藏着多少未被利用的算力。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. What happens if \(q\)'s \(d_q(0.5)\) is applied to a different question \(q'\) of the same task, matched on answer type and difficulty? If that also gives ~85%, the direction is a generic "commit and compute" perturbation and the question-specificity claim collapses.
2. How much of the gain survives a random unit direction at the same layer with the same norm, and a direction built from *shuffled* recovering/failed labels of the same question? The failed-mean control is a weak version of this.
3. Can the direction be predicted from the direct prompt with a small trained head (the paper's failed regressor used what architecture, and on how much data)? If a head trained on 9,120 question–direction pairs still fails, that is itself a strong statement about how much of the solution is *not* in the direct prompt's last state.
4. Does the effect hold for reasoning-trained models (DeepSeek-R1-Distill, QwQ) where the direct prompt may already be internally aligned, and does it scale beyond 8B?
5. Why does the direction still work when the probe is at chance (Qwen Causal Judgement, AUROC 0.524)? Is the useful signal in a nonlinear subspace, or is the mean-difference vector picking up a computation-commitment direction that is orthogonal to the recovering/failed axis?
6. What is the wall-clock and FLOP cost of the full pipeline (chain + n patches + one rotated generation) relative to plain CoT, and at what question volume would a distilled direction predictor break even?
7. On GSM8K the direction still emits 39 tokens of arithmetic — is the residual computation genuinely latent, or is it just suppressing CoT's verbal boilerplate? Comparing against a "terse CoT" prompt (numbers only, no prose) would separate verbosity from computation.
