Send corrected absolute parameters, not deltas — 3.125%-dense gossip then scales to 128 replicas for a 1.3% loss penalty.

SPICE folds error feedback into the transmitted endpoint itself. Any peer can consume any message without keeping per-neighbour reconstruction state, so the neighbourhood can be resampled every round — and a fixed byte budget can be spent on peers instead of precision.

01 Mechanism — compress the endpoint, not the step

Parameter gossip breaks the assumption behind gradient compression. Replica i steps xi → yi and sends the displacement Δi = xi − yi. A peer starting from a different model cannot recover yj from that delta: the receiver-side gap mixes model disagreement with local progress. SPICE sidesteps the reference frame entirely by sending the endpoint yi.

  1. Accumulate omitted progress: ẽi = βei + Δi, decay β = 0.95.
  2. Pick chunked Top-k over contiguous blocks of b = 4096 coordinates, keeping density δ = 3.125%; indices packed to 12 bits.
  3. Transmit zi = yi − Mi ⊙ βei — the correction omitted from the buffer rides inside the value actually sent.
  4. Aggregate over whoever contributed: Gi[c] = xi[c] − meanj∈Pi,c zj[c]; the self endpoint always counts, so unsent coordinates fall back to the local step.
  5. Take a Nesterov outer step from xi (ηout ≈ 0.9, μout ≈ 0.2–0.3), following GASLoC's momentum–consensus link.

ẽi = eit+1 + Mi ⊙ (xi − zi) Every bit of correction removed from the residual buffer is exactly encoded in the endpoints — nothing is discarded or double-counted.

Peers derive the same random permutation from a shared seed and round index, then contact q/2 predecessors and q/2 successors on the resulting cycle: symmetric, exactly q distinct peers, zero coordination traffic. With full support and a zero residual, SPICE reduces exactly to dense GASLoC.

06 Strengths

  • The reference-frame diagnosis is crisp and correct; corrected endpoints are a reusable primitive for any gossip method that wants dynamic neighbourhoods.
  • Exact reduction to dense GASLoC at full support makes the method easy to reason about and to bolt onto an existing codebase.
  • Budget treated as a first-class variable (q × δ at fixed qδd), with Figure 3b answering "peers or coordinates?" directly.
  • Unusually careful networking: per-direction tc netem caps, transports disabled so traffic cannot bypass them, 30-second bins normalised to each method's own healthy baseline.

07 Limitations

  • No convergence analysis or contraction argument; the outer-optimizer interaction is justified only by analogy to GASLoC's dense case.
  • One seed per configuration, and no β = 0 / no-error-feedback ablation — so the titled mechanism is never isolated from neighbourhood growth.
  • CHOCO-SGD only on the complete graph; SparseLoCo run at H = 1 without quantization or outer momentum. Both baselines lose their strongest form.
  • Quality from an exchange simulator, timing from one 8×A6000 node: no real straggler jitter, no WAN latency, and 75% index overhead left out of the Pareto.

08 Open questions

  1. What does β = 0 cost at δ = 3.125% and q = R/2? Within 0.02 of SPICE would mean the error-feedback framing is oversold.
  2. How does a fixed q = R/2 ring CHOCO-SGD do at R = 32 / 64 / 128? The biggest claimed gap rests on an untested configuration.
  3. Does the neighbourhood advantage survive at fp8, where the 1.5 B/value index overhead goes from 75% to 150% of payload?
  4. How sensitive is the method to chunk size b? δ is pinned at 3.125% with b = 4096 throughout the main tables.
  5. What happens with heterogeneous δ per replica, e.g. under asymmetric uplinks? Averaging over Pi,c would weight dense senders more per coordinate.
  6. Does error feedback still commute if endpoints are quantized or low-rank, the way it commutes with the Top-k mask?
  7. At real WAN latency (50–200 ms RTT), does q = R/2 still pay when each round waits on q peers rather than one or two?
