Critical edge

The 0.8–1.25 band is an FDA convention, not a validated threshold — a 25% moral advantage would still be reported as “no content specificity”, and per-model intervals sit inside it in only 8 of 14 models.

MethodologyPRE-REGISTERED

A design that makes three readings predict different patterns

  1. STEP 01227 minimal pairs, 3 frames
  2. STEP 0214 models, 1B–14B
  3. STEP 03ρ per-head patch → share
  4. STEP 0420 random head sets
  • Stimuli, validated by people. 282 candidate pairs → five blind annotators (Krippendorff α = 0.977 on which wording is better, 0.738 on the standard) → 227: 80 moral, 107 normative (37 prudential, 22 etiquette, 24 aesthetic, 24 epistemic), 40 descriptive. Moral pairs score 2.8 on the moral scale, normative 0.2.
  • Three frames. “[stem] is morally” with priming, “[stem] is”, and the bare construction. Priming is not cosmetic: evaluative continuation mass is 2–16% unprimed versus 31–63% primed in Llama-3.2-1B.
  • Readout. S = Σ p(14 positive words) − Σ p(14 negative words); a pair counts only if its gap ≥ 0.02.
  • Localisation by share, not raw effect. Copy one head’s output from the better to the worse version of the 73 original pairs, then divide by the summed effect over all heads. ρ = moral share ÷ normative share; the top ten heads by moral-versus-control permutation statistic are the “located heads”.
  • Triangulated. Mean-ablation against 20 random ten-head sets, attention and direct logit attribution, edge patching, 14 SAE dictionaries, a Jacobian lens (≤3B), neuron ablations, a steering direction, 400 ETHICS scenarios, 23 moral/prudential twins.
Three reusable warnings

Traps this paper walked into on purpose

  1. The offset. Almost every head has a larger raw effect on moral pairs — 86–100% of them, median 1.5–2.3×, up to 16× in Qwen2.5-3B. Specificity computed on raw effects therefore measures the offset, not morality. use shares
  2. The frame confound. Swap the control prompt’s frame and the frame difference gets booked as “moral content”. The paper only compares pairs that share grammar, template, length and edit site.
  3. Selection inflation. Picking heads and testing them on the same pairs overstates specificity. Fixed here with 227 held-out pairs and a mirror-image selection run on normative pairs as the yardstick.

22/22 software self-tests pass; planted ratios of 2.0 are recovered as 2.12 and 1.99, a planted null as 0.96/0.99.

Where anything moral-specific lives

A thin, cue-dependent layer

81–92%
Category is readable. Completing “is a matter of”, models name the annotators’ standard five-way class — chance is 20%.
AUC .95–.99
A direction separates moral from normative pairs in a Jacobian lens (fitted for models ≤3B).
2–11%
Removing it costs the moral gap; the normative gap falls about half as much or not at all (specific in 7/8 lens models).
5–10%
Features and a few hundred neurons add the rest — only with the cue present.

One direction also moves every judgment: adding the normative acceptable-minus-unacceptable direction lifts evaluations of moral, normative and descriptive pairs alike by 40–240% of the pair gap, in all 14 models. Moral-only and normative-only directions have cosine 0.72–0.94.

Sparse autoencodersNEGATIVE RESULT

The dictionaries read the word, not the contrast

What the dictionary sees

R² < 0

Capture of the pair difference is negative in 13 of 14 dictionaries in frame A1 (from −1.54 to −0.02; best 0.38). The better-versus-worse contrast is essentially invisible to them.

What the features still do

0.70–0.99

“Moral” features fire at 70–99% of their moral-pair activation on purely descriptive sentences. Delete “morally” and activation drops to 1–33% of its cued value.

Scope. 12/14 dictionaries yield more moral-versus-normative splits than any norm-versus-norm split — but two of those are layer-0 Qwen dictionaries that detect ~0.2 features, so the usable count is nearer 10/12. Held-out removal of these features cuts the moral gap by 5–10% in 4 of 10 usable dictionaries without the cue. The finding is real, but it describes the dictionaries, not the model.

▲ Strengths

  • Adversarial by construction. The three readings were made to predict different patterns before the data, and the paper reports the one that kills its own most exciting interpretation.
  • Held-out, mirrored selection. Select on 73 pairs, evaluate on 227 new human-validated pairs, then repeat the selection on normative pairs to calibrate selection bias.
  • The offset diagnostic is a public good. Raw effects are 1.5–2.3× larger on moral pairs for 86–100% of heads — a warning for every activation-patching paper that reports raw deltas.
  • Statistical hygiene. Pre-registered bands, 10,000-resample bootstrap, BH-corrected permutation tests, 20 random head sets per comparison, planted-effect recovery, full release of pairs, ratings, code and 13 SAE dictionaries.

▼ Limitations

  • A null inside a convention. The 25% margin is borrowed, not validated; per-model intervals are wholly inside the band in only 8/14 models with the cue and 5/14 without; the pooled CI bootstraps items, not models.
  • Ten heads are a core, not the circuit. They account for 23% of the gap and 2.2% of the direct logit difference, and were selected by the offset-driven statistic the paper itself criticises.
  • Judgment = 28 evaluative words. Free-form verdicts are untested; the choice prompt holds in only 6/7 models; IT models get no chat template, so nothing describes assistant behaviour.
  • The person-versus-thing control is LLM-labelled — 58 pairs, in a paper whose complaint is that LLM labels are unreliable. The 23 twins suggest residues track the situation, not the standard.
  • Three families, shared corpora. 14 checkpoints cannot separate “a property of language models” from “a property of this training recipe”.
Open questions
  1. Would the null survive selecting heads by shares or causal effect instead of the raw-effect statistic, with a larger budget than ten heads?
  2. Can personhood be isolated from normativity with items that vary only the presence of a beneficiary, holding syntax, template and length fixed?
  3. Do the moral-versus-normative feature counts survive in dictionaries that actually capture the pair difference (capture R² > 0.5)?
  4. If the located heads are upstream nodes, which later components write the verdict — MLPs already carry 73–74% of the direct logit difference — and are those standard-specific?
  5. Does the picture change under chat-formatted instruction tuning, chain-of-thought, or harmlessness RLHF, where an explicit moral policy has been trained in?
