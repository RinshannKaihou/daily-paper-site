Why it matters

Clinical agents silently inherit tool errors. Deciding what counts as evidence outside the reasoner turns a silent failure into a bounded one — no retraining.

Critical edge

Checker and Solver share the same 8B weights, so “context-independent” is not error-independent: the case where both are wrong the same way is never measured.

Case setup. Each case is an input x (image, or vignette + question) with label y; a frozen reasoner answers from an evidence set A. The whole task is deciding which cards may enter A. Three tasks cover three tool-output types: segmentation masks (glaucoma, REFUGE2, 400 images), generated reports plus similar-case retrieval (Kermany chest X-ray, 431 patient-deduplicated images), and retrieved passages (MedQA 1,273 / MedMCQA 4,183 / NEJM 655).

The card. Every tool writes a verifiable card — a conclusion plus the material it rests on: a contour overlaid on the original image with its derived vCDR, a report with findings, or a verbatim quoted passage. One admission procedure then works across a heterogeneous tool pool.

Who judges. The Checker receives (x, C) and returns a subset, scoring fidelity (does the displayed material support the stated finding) and consistency (do cards corroborate or contradict). Fidelity governs, because “tools can also agree in error”. It never sees the Solver's tentative answer or any label. The Solver is the same frozen backbone with an 8-sample vote — applied identically to every admission policy and baseline.

Theory usable, not Shannon

vx(A) = log q(y|x,A) − log q(y|x)

A mask is a deterministic function of the image, so by the data-processing inequality it carries no extra Shannon label information — the standard account cannot explain the harm. Pointwise usable information can be negative for a fixed predictor family, which is exactly what a hijacked context produces.

Governance moves optional ignorance outside the reasoner: the family VG = {q(·|x,Cg)} contains both trivial gates (admit none, admit all), so the best gate cannot be worse than the better trivial one.

Gain over admit-all splits into T = B + S — budget (seeing less) and selection (seeing better). Any content-agnostic gate has S = 0 on a randomly ordered card pool, so an evidence filter must be compared against a budget-matched random gate, never against admit-all.

S(g) ≈ E[ K · Covc(ac, vx(c)) ]

That first-order prediction is then tested, not asserted: Spearman ρ = 0.86 over 483 constructed gates on glaucoma and 0.75 over 335 on chest X-ray, with 97% sign agreement.

Strengths +

  • Clean conceptual cut between wrong content and too much content, backed by an experiment that fixes context length at three cards.
  • A falsifiable theory that is tested on 818 constructed gates across two tasks, with rank correlations reported.
  • The right reference baseline — budget-matched random admission, a norm much of the RAG-filtering literature skips.
  • Deployable as stated: no training, frozen weights, prompt-level Checker; the card format spans masks, reports and passages.
  • Real cost accounting for every agent system on every task, not a rhetorical “cheaper”.
  • An uncomfortable result for agentic medicine: in-context tool judgment below no tools at all.

Limitations −

  • Shared weights. Checker and Solver are both Qwen3-VL-8B; error correlation is neither measured nor ablated, so “independence” is contextual only.
  • One task with card-level truth. AUROC is glaucoma-only; rejection quality on chest X-ray and QA is inferred.
  • Single backbone, single seed. Only bootstrap CIs over cases are reported — no run-to-run variance.
  • Fragile imaging win. 431 deduplicated images; +0.016 sits inside a ±0.03 interval, against baselines with overlapping training data.
  • Classification only. Every task is multiple choice, where abstaining is cheap; open-ended reporting is untested.
  • Rejections unanalysed. 13–24% of correct cards are rejected, with no breakdown and no clinical harm estimate.
  • First-order theory. Proposition 1 is exact only when ≤1 or all cards are admitted (71% of glaucoma cases); interaction error is unbounded.

Critical assessment read the headline with this in hand

Novelty

An LLM verifier filtering evidence before the answer model is close to CRAG and Self-RAG, and the paper says so. What is new: heterogeneous card types instead of text passages, judging outside the reasoner rather than inside it, per-card rather than per-query rulings.

The genuinely valuable finding is empirical, not rhetorical: ReAct-style in-context judgment can be strictly worse than using no tools (0.544 vs 0.636).

Where the rigor thins

(1) No stronger or different-family Checker is ever swapped in. (2) Card-level quality is measured on glaucoma alone. (3) A single backbone supports a claim about “frozen backbones” by construction. (4) The 483/335 validation gates are built by perturbing the Checker's own scores, so the fit is partly self-referential.

Statistics elsewhere are disciplined: context length fixed, errors externally defined, paired stratified bootstrap with 5,000 resamples.

Overclaiming & data quirks

“Significantly outperforms” is carried by glaucoma and QA. On chest X-ray the edge over the trivial gate is 0.016, and the nearest competitor is 1.75× larger with overlapping training data — parity at lower cost is the defensible claim.

Hijacking cells are small (n = 29 and n = 47), so “no trend” means no detected trend. QA is closed-book multiple choice, where rejection at worst returns to the prior.

Verdict. One strong, cleanly isolated empirical result plus a theory that yields a genuinely useful evaluation norm. Cheap and easy to adopt. But the accuracy margins concentrate on exactly the task where rejecting tools is obviously right, and the “independent” Checker shares the Solver's weights — leaving the central robustness question open.

Questions for further exploration 6 open threads

  1. Error correlation. How often do Checker and Solver fail together, and does a stronger or different-family Checker preserve the gain?
  2. Open-ended reporting. What happens when rejecting a card becomes a missed diagnosis rather than a cheaper abstention?
  3. Non-random card order. Does the budget-matched reference still favour CIEG when tools are called in a fixed priority order?
  4. Stability of +0.016. Does the chest X-ray margin survive patient-level bootstrap over those 431 images?
  5. Independent scorer. Can the covariance prediction be validated on gates built from a scorer other than the Checker itself?
  6. Cost of false rejection. What is the downstream harm of the 13–24% of correct cards the Checker throws away?
