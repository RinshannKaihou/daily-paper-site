Key metric

90%

of a reported gain, recovered by one appended instruction

Median over 27 model–benchmark pairs with >3 pp reported. Repair the graders and the median 17.6 pp gain falls to 0.8 pp.

Why this matters

If the bottleneck is detection rather than invention, the field should buy resolution — more rollouts, better graders — instead of stronger proposers.

Critical edge

!Repair lifts the baseline too — DocVQA goes 61.0 → 88.3 — so part of the gain that “vanishes” is lost headroom, not a debunked improvement.

Method

Seven methods, one variance budget

  1. A 7 × 6 × 4 grid. Four text-artifact methods (GEPA prompts; SkillOpt-Lite and Trace2Skill skills; ReasoningBank memory) and three harness methods (HarnessLens, Meta-Harness, AHE) across six models on ALFWorld, SpreadsheetBench, OfficeQA and Terminal-Bench v2.1. Every role in a method uses the same model.
  2. Split the variance. Var(Ŷ) = σ² + τ², with σ² from M repeated evaluations and τ² from a random-effects moment estimator: R=8 runs (16 for text methods on GPT-5.4 nano), M=8–16 evaluations. Negative τ̂² is clipped to zero.
  3. Simulate the table. 20,000 one-run-per-method draws test whether a single run recovers the all-run ranking; the runs needed come from a power formula, not post-hoc significance.
  4. Audit the wins. On 12 benchmarks, append the one rule judged most responsible to the untouched baseline, then repair graders (LibreOffice recalculation, semantic grading, official instructions) and re-evolve under the repair.
  5. Isolate selection. A 100-skill Proposal Pool (16,000 rollouts) against a matched equal-quality null, a 100-skill Selection Pool scored on held-out rollouts, and 119 in-loop artifact versions re-scored with 8 rollouts each.

Findings

What the audit found

  • One rule ≈ the gain. Appending the single instruction judged most responsible to the unevolved baseline recovers a median 90% of the reported gain across the 27 pairs above +3 pp.
  • Margins sit inside the noise. The surveyed literature's median leading margin is 3.4 pp (IQR 1.2–6.6) while one run's standard deviation is 1.7–4.1 pp; none of the 171 result columns reports a variance estimate, and only 6 average over more than one run.
  • Most comparisons are unresolved. Across 341 same-benchmark configuration pairs, 217 (64%) have an all-run mean gap below one single-run standard deviation.
  • Some harness “wins” are bug fixes. Meta-Harness +30.3 pp and AHE +31.7 pp on ALFWorld trace to one incidental setting — the initial agent keeping only the last two history steps.

Candidate level

Proposal is fine; selection is the wall

  • Helpful artifacts exist. Quality@K climbs from 49.2% (K=1) to 61.8% (K=100), beating a matched equal-quality null of 58.4% by +3.4 pp; the best shared skill reaches 57.5% versus 41.9% for the weakest.
  • Selectors are near coins. Execution-based selection scores 49.7% ± 1.9 pairwise accuracy against 50% chance; native operators 48.6%, pointwise LLM judges 45.4%. The rank correlation between one execution score and true quality is 0.054.
  • Errors compound. Re-scoring 119 in-loop artifact versions with 8 rollouts turns 6.9–9.0 pp in-loop gains into +0.4 to −3.2 pp, while corrected run-to-run variance grows to 4.2–24.5 pp².
  • Resolution is buyable. Sixteen validation rollouts per candidate lift the mean final test score from 61.4% to 64.4% and the best artifact from 64.8% to 72.7%.

Strengths

Why the evidence carries weight

  • Replication the literature lacks: 1,030 runs, 150 configurations, up to 16 evaluations per artifact, 2.1M trajectories.
  • An exact variance split with unbiased moment estimators and a documented clipping rule (9 of 117 configurations).
  • A matched equal-quality null for Quality@K — without it, a rising max-over-K curve proves nothing.
  • The one-rule audit plus grader repair turns suspicion into a checkable experiment; Table 11 exposes every claim.
  • Cheap prescriptions: process metrics with 13–57× lower noise, component-wise proposer/selector evaluation, report σ̂ and τ̂.

Limitations

Where the claim outruns the data

  • Frozen weights only; RL methods that update the model itself may face a different constraint than the title implies.
  • All candidate-level evidence is Terminal-Bench v2.1, with pools generated largely by DeepSeek-V4-Flash.
  • R=8 leaves wide intervals on τ̂²; 9 of 117 estimates clip to zero, and method conclusions lean on point estimates.
  • Near-chance selection is pool-dependent — no curve of selector accuracy versus the true quality gap is reported.
  • Repair lifts baselines by 20–69 pp, entangling headroom loss with real failure; artifacts are promised only upon acceptance.
  • Program evolution — where deterministic re-grading makes resolution cheap — is explicitly out of scope.

Open questions

What would change the picture

01

Does the 16-rollout validation fix generalize across all four benchmarks and all seven methods?

02

How does selector accuracy scale with the true quality gap between the two candidates being compared?

03

Can SPRT-style anytime-valid tests buy the same resolution for far less than 16× the rollouts?

04

Does the resolution constraint bind when the artifact is the model's weights rather than a prompt or skill?

05

Can low-noise process metrics serve as the acceptance signal, not merely as a diagnostic?

06

How many published agent-evolution rankings would flip at 8–23 runs per method?
