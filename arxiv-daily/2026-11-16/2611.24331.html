Key metric

16×

batch-size range over which well-tuned runs share one GNS curve

GPT 2¹⁷→2²¹ tokens · DiT 2⁶→2¹⁰ images · ResNet 2⁷→2¹¹ images. Scale LR ×8 or ×16 alone and the same metric moves by ~10×.

Why this matters

GNS is measured online at almost zero communication cost — a free signal for sizing batches, allocating GPUs, and flagging divergence before it happens.

Critical edge

⚠Width invariance rests on overlapping curves in one figure: no fitted exponent, no R², not a single GNS(1B)/GNS(22M) ratio at a fixed loss.

Methodology

Measure noise for free, then sweep the grid

  1. Re-derive the metric. A second-order step gives the noise batch size; assuming a well-conditioned Hessian collapses it to the logged quantity. B_noise = tr(HΣ)/GᵀHG → B_simple = tr(Σ)/GᵀG
  2. Exploit DDP. The small batch is each GPU's local gradient, the big batch the all-reduced global gradient. Two equations solve for |G|² and tr(Σ) with no extra communication; separate EMAs, α = 0.95–0.995.
  3. Define CBS. Loss smoothed with an EWMA whose span scales as 1/√B; keep the best LR per batch; S(B)=N*(B_min)/N*(B); CBS is where S falls below 1/1.2, interpolated log-linearly.
  4. Sweep three domains. ResNet-18/CIFAR-10 at widths ×1/×2/×4; nano-muP GPT, 12 layers, widths 256–2560, 20B FineWeb tokens; DiT depth 28, widths 144/576, 51M ImageNet images. AdamW throughout.
  5. Run at scale. Leonardo Booster, 4×A100 per node, ≈200,000 GPU-hours; released as three Parquet files with train/val loss, gns, gns_norm and gns_var.

Findings

What the sweeps actually showed

  • LR alone moves the metric ~10×. One fixed GPT model spans 0.2–2.0 × 10⁵ tokens when LR is pushed ×1, ×8 and ×16 off baseline — GNS is not a property of the model.
  • The Adam √B rule is recovered, not assumed. Batch ×4 with LR ×2 yields near-identical trajectories, and the LR optimum peaks at 2¹⁹–2²⁰ tokens, close to the final GNS of 0.5–1.0 × 10⁶.
  • Widths overlap under muP. GPT 256–2560, DiT 144–576 and ResNet ×1–×4 sit on one curve at matched loss, so a 22M proxy predicts the 1B model's batch regime — the paper's main practical claim.
  • The stability frontier is included. 9 of 75 DiT runs (12%) and 12 of 210 NLP runs (5.7%) diverge to NaN or spike, all at the top two or three LRs; runs are kept up to the divergence point.

Strengths

A genuine public good

  • 433 runs and ≈200k A100-hours released with both raw components, so the metric can be re-derived under other assumptions.
  • Three genuinely different domains under one measurement pipeline, one optimizer family, one parameterization — which is what makes the cross-task contrast meaningful.
  • Honest appendices: EMA decay sensitivity, B_big/B_small robustness at 4/8/16, four CBS thresholds, and a two-seed CV rerun with real ranges.
  • Failed runs kept as data, turning large-batch divergence into a documented stability boundary.
  • The estimator is described precisely enough to reimplement: local versus global gradient, no extra communication.

Limitations

Where the evidence stops

  • One seed for GPT and DiT grids; error bars only for the CV case, and only over two seeds.
  • Width-only scaling with depth fixed at 18 / 12 / 28 — and muP's transfer guarantee is itself an assumption.
  • CBS is anchored to the smallest swept batch per task, which differs across domains while the threshold stays fixed at 20%.
  • AdamW only, raw pre-preconditioning gradients; the derivation is for SGD, and preconditioned noise scales cannot be recovered.
  • DiT loss is monitored on COCO val for an ImageNet model; no FID or sample-quality metric is released.
  • Released CIFAR-10 / CIFAR-10.1 accuracies are never linked to GNS, though that null result would be informative.

Open questions

What the release makes cheap to test next

01

What is the quantitative width-invariance error — GNS(2560)/GNS(256) at a fixed target loss — and does it grow faster than muP's own loss-transfer error?

02

How much of the reported smoothness is EMA artifact? Re-deriving from gns_norm/gns_var at α ∈ {0.9, 0.95, 0.995} with confidence bands would settle it.

03

Does the GNS/CBS gap direction survive a different reference batch — say a fixed samples-seen budget instead of the grid minimum?

04

Can the released gns_var spectra test the ill-conditioning story directly: does the gradient-covariance condition number predict the sign of the bias?

05

Does GNS at a given loss predict CIFAR-10.1 accuracy at all, once you condition on the batch-size / LR cell?

06

How does GNS behave under depth scaling, MoE routing, or RL post-training, where the well-conditioned-Hessian and fixed-objective assumptions look weakest?
