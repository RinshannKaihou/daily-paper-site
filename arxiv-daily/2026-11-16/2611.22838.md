> **一句话总结：** 用精确全目录排序在 5 个数据集上测出 GRPO 后训练对生成式推荐的一个隐藏代价——top-K（K≤10）变好、候选列表召回变差：Office 上 NDCG@10 从 11.59% 升到 12.55%，但 hit@500 从 52.84% 跌到 47.69%（−5.14 点），根因是 RL 把 rollout 里"没被记录为目标"的合理候选当成负样本惩罚，且惩罚经用户间泛化传染；作者提出只加一项 loss 的 out-of-rollout anchoring（M=16 个 rollout 外样本、λ=0.1 保持 SFT 相对序），在 Office 上恢复 5.14 点损失中的 4.45 点（hit@500 回到 52.14%），同时 NDCG@10 12.64% 不输 GRPO。

> **TL;DR:** RL post-training (GRPO) of a semantic-ID generative recommender is standard practice, but it is almost always evaluated at hit@10. This paper scores *every* catalogue item for every test user under SFT and under GRPO (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, MiniOneRec recipe, G=16, 3 seeds) on Office, Industrial, Beauty, Toys and Sports, and finds a consistent crossover: RL wins above the top and loses below it. On Office, NDCG@10 rises 11.59% → 12.55% while hit@50 falls 25.73% → 24.22% and hit@500 falls 52.84% → 47.69%; on Industrial the crossover sits near K≈50 and hit@500 drops 53.23% → 51.72%. The cause is not the objective (Prop. 4.1: the exact KL-regularised optimum cannot lower hit@K for any K) but its estimation: with one logged next item per history, GRPO assigns negative advantages to the G−1 plausible alternatives in the rollout, these penalties accumulate per item and generalise to similar users, and where a rollout misses the target no gradient restores it. Zeroing negative advantages removes both the loss and the gain; keeping only negatives triples the loss (hit@500 −19 to −21 points). A one-term fix — a KL anchor that preserves the SFT ordering of M=16 sampled out-of-rollout items — recovers 4.4 of the 5.1 lost points on Office hit@500 (47.69% → 52.14% vs 52.84% SFT) with NDCG@10 unchanged (12.64% vs 12.55%), and works on all five datasets plus synced-reference GRPO and REINFORCE++ (+5.88 and +13.26 hit@500 on Office).

## 核心贡献

- **测量**：首次系统性地报告 RL 后训练的"检索召回损失"。做法是对每个测试用户做**精确全目录排序**（展开 SID 前缀树算 s_θ(i|x)，不是 beam 近似），在 5 个数据集、3 个 RL 随机种子上比较 SFT 与 GRPO 的 hit@K（K 到 500，约为最小目录的 14%）。结论在 5 个数据集上一致：top-K 提升，K 到几十之后转为下降。Office 上拐点在 K≈20，Industrial 在 K≈50。
- **归因**：把损失定位到 GRPO 的**负 advantage**，而不是目标函数本身。作者给出 Proposition 4.1：精确求解 KL 正则 RL 问题的最优解 π* ∝ π0·exp(p/β) 在任何 K 上都不会降低期望 hit@K；损失来自"用单个 logged item 当 reward + 只更新 rollout 内样本 + 一个网络服务所有用户"这三个偏离理想估计的地方。
- **机制解释（为何推荐场景特有）**：每个 history 只记录一个 next item，rollout 里其余 G−1 个候选是模型自己认为最可能的替代，往往正是相似用户的下一个点击，却被当作错误答案压下去。惩罚按 item 累积（作者用 SFT 独算的 "rollout record" 在 RL 之前就能预测谁掉分），又通过共享 SID/网络泛化到相似用户；而 rollout 里没命中目标的 prompt（Office/Industrial 约 60%，其余数据集 76–89%）根本不产生 policy gradient，无人把那件商品"推回来"。
- **控制实验**：三种干预互相印证——(a) 只保留正 advantage → 损失消失、增益也消失；只保留负 advantage → 损失放大 3 倍以上；(b) DPO 负样本取自 SFT beam → 掉 4.1 点 hit@500，取自随机目录 → 大 K 不掉；(c) 按 RL 前的 rollout record 分组，record 最负的测试用户掉 27.4 点，最正的涨约 3 点。
- **补救（out-of-rollout anchoring）**：在 GRPO loss 之外加一项对 rollout **之外** M=16 个样本的 KL(q0‖qθ)（λ=0.1），保持 SFT 给出的相对序。5 个数据集的 hit@500 全部回到 SFT 水平或略高，NDCG@10 与 GRPO 无显著差异，K≥50 的每一档都是三个模型里最好的；在 synced-reference GRPO 与 REINFORCE++ 上同样有效。
- **给评测的建议**：现有 benchmark 停在 K≤32（GenRec K=50、EG-GRPO K=10），而线上系统一次交 100–1,500 个候选给下游 ranker（TSGR 1,500、GrowthGR 1,000、OneRec 512）。用标准协议（beam 50、只看 K=10）测同一批模型只会看到 +0.39/+0.46 点，完全看不到损失。

## Key Contributions

- **A measurement hiding in plain sight.** Exact full-catalogue ranking (all items scored, exact rank of the target, hit@K up to 500) on five datasets with three RL seeds shows RL post-training monotonically improves the head and monotonically degrades the tail. On Office, 22% of the test targets that SFT ranked in its top 500 are no longer there after one epoch of GRPO; across datasets the figure is 14–22%. The effect survives constrained beam decoding (−2.59 hit@200 on Office, −1.96 on Industrial) and the standard eval protocol (beam 50, report K=10) sees only the gain (+0.39, +0.46).
- **A causal account rather than a correlation.** Proposition 4.1 rules out the objective: the exact KL-regularised optimum cannot lower HR_K at any K. The paper then locates the damage in the estimator, via the gradient decomposition g(x) = (1/G)[1[y∈R]A_y ∇log π(y|x) − Σ_{i∈R\{y}} |A_i| ∇log π(i|x)], and identifies the three departures that create new inversions: named negatives, gating (only ~37–42% of Office/Industrial prompts have the target in the rollout; 11–24% on the other three), and cross-user generalisation through shared SID tokens.
- **A label-free predictor of who loses.** The per-item "rollout record" (sum of that item's advantages over the SFT model's top-16 candidates for every training prompt) is computed *before* RL starts, yet separates test users into groups that lose 27.4 vs gain 3 points of hit@500 on Office. Only 1.7% of Office test targets never appear among those top-16 candidates, so the bookkeeping covers essentially the whole test set.
- **A minimal, diagnosis-derived remedy.** Out-of-rollout anchoring adds one term, KL(q0‖qθ) over M=16 items sampled outside the rollout along SID neighbourhoods (half sharing the first two codes with a rollout candidate, half sharing the first code), leaving GRPO bit-for-bit unchanged inside the rollout. It recovers 4.4 of 5.1 lost hit@500 points on Office, exceeds SFT at K≥200 on Industrial, and cuts the out-of-rollout score drift by 8× (1.53 → 0.19 nats) while halving the in-rollout drift as a side effect.
- **A cross-configuration check.** The anchor's benefit is not a single-recipe artefact: +5.88 hit@500 on Office for GRPO with a synced (moving) reference, +13.26 on Office and +5.47 on Industrial for REINFORCE++ with batch-normalised advantages, with significant NDCG@10 gains there too (11.45 → 12.24, 8.96 → 9.32).
- **Connecting scattered industrial anecdotes.** The paper collects prior observations that fit the diagnosis (GenRec's HR@50 drop without its relevance gate, RGD's lower HitRate@604 with a rising reward, PinGER/Multi-Decoder OneRec recall collapse without a tie to the pretrained model, GrowthGR's Recall@10 vs Recall@1000 trade-off, TSGR's marginal RL gain at 1,500 candidates, EG-GRPO's top-1-up/top-10-down result) and turns them into one measured, explained phenomenon.

## Methodology

**Setup.** Items are represented by 3-code semantic IDs (codebook 256, residual quantisation of a text embedding), so an item's score is the sum of three conditional log-probs along the SID prefix tree, s_θ(i|x) = Σ_l log π_θ(c_l(i) | x, c_<l(i)). Backbone: Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, fully fine-tuned. SFT follows the MiniOneRec recipe with early stopping on validation loss; RL is MiniOneRec's GRPO instantiation — G=16 rollout candidates from constrained beam search with sampling, hit-based reward with a rank-aware penalty r_j = −w_ρ(j) for non-target candidates when the target is in the rollout (w_ρ ∝ 1/log2(ρ+1), normalised over the G positions), zero reward for all candidates when the target is absent, group-standardised advantages, reverse KL to a frozen SFT reference with β=10⁻³, lr 10⁻⁵, 32 prompts/step, gradient clipping 0.3, one rollout per gradient step, ~1,700 steps (one epoch) per dataset.

**Evaluation.** For every test prompt the model scores every catalogue item by expanding the SID prefix tree, giving the exact rank of the target, rank_θ(y|x) = 1 + |{i : s_θ(i|x) > s_θ(y|x)}|. This removes beam-search approximation and makes all cut-offs observable in one pass; a beam search over the same scores reproduces ~98% of MiniOneRec's top-10 results. Metrics: hit@K for K up to 500 (14% of the smallest catalogue) and NDCG@10. Since each test prompt has a single target, hit@K is exactly recall@K. All comparisons are paired on the same prompts; significance is a 95% bootstrap interval over prompts excluding zero; main results are the mean of three RL seeds that share one SFT checkpoint and differ only in prompt order and rollout/anchor sampling.

**Datasets.** Office (3,459 items / 4,866 test) and Industrial (3,686 / 4,533) are MiniOneRec's Amazon-2018 benchmarks with chronological 8:1:1 splits, used unchanged including their SIDs. Beauty (12,101 / 5,000), Toys (11,924 / 5,000) and Sports (18,357 / 5,000) are 5-core Amazon-2014 subsets with leave-one-out splits; their SIDs are built with the MiniOneRec pipeline and evaluation samples 5,000 users. The MiniOneRec hyper-parameters (tuned on Office/Industrial) are applied to all five datasets without per-dataset tuning — a deliberate choice the authors flag as a possible handicap on the three added datasets.

**Diagnosis experiments.** (1) Zero one sign of the advantage, keeping everything else. (2) Swap GRPO for DPO and vary where the single negative comes from (SFT beam vs uniform catalogue). (3) Correlate the pre-computed per-item rollout record with who loses hit@500. (4) Measure the change in relative scores inside vs outside the rollout (1.53 vs 1.15 nats on Office) to show that all the reward is earned inside the rollout yet most of the drift is outside.

**The anchor.** For a prompt x with rollout R(x), draw an anchor set A(x) ⊂ I \ R(x) of M=16 items, renormalise the frozen SFT model and the policy over A(x) to get q0 and qθ, and add λ·KL(q0‖qθ) with λ=0.1 to the GRPO loss. Because both distributions are renormalised over A(x), adding a constant to all anchor scores leaves the term unchanged: RL may still move mass from the tail to the head, and only relative-score changes among out-of-rollout items are penalised. The anchor set is SID-structured rather than uniform — half the items share the first two codes with a rollout candidate, half share the first code — because those neighbourhoods are small but hold 44% (Office) and 57% (Industrial) of the targets SFT ranks 17–500, i.e. exactly the band GRPO demotes. At the SFT initialisation qθ = q0, so the term and its gradient start at zero.

## Results & Highlights

- **Head up, tail down, same model (Office, mean of 3 seeds, Table 5).** GRPO moves NDCG@10 from 11.59% to 12.55% (+0.95) and hit@10 from 15.97% to 16.41% (+0.44), but hit@50 falls 25.73% → 24.22% (−1.52), hit@100 31.94% → 29.16% (−2.77), hit@200 39.56% → 35.42% (−4.14) and hit@500 52.84% → 47.69% (−5.14). The sign flips at K≈20 (1.4% of the catalogue), and the tail loss is significant in every seed from K=50 upward.
- **It is not an Office quirk.** Industrial: NDCG@10 9.05% → 9.48% (+0.43) while hit@500 goes 53.23% → 51.72% (−1.51), significant in every seed at K=100 and K=500, crossover at K≈50. The three larger-catalogue datasets lose less in absolute points but the same way: hit@500 −0.49 (Beauty), −0.04 (Toys), −0.67 (Sports), with 14–22% of SFT's top-500 targets gone on every dataset.
- **The loss is rank reshuffling, not sharpening.** Office item-distribution entropy falls from 6.0 to 3.1 nats, but temperature alone cannot change hit@K. Grouping test prompts by SFT rank shows where the inversions land: targets at SFT ranks 11–50 have median rank 26 → 44 with 62% demoted; 51–100: 72 → 136, 64% demoted; 201–500: 326 → 497, 63% demoted; targets already beyond rank 500 actually improve slightly (1573 → 1563). Top-1 targets stay top-1 in 87% of cases, and ranks 2–10 split roughly evenly between promotion and demotion.
- **Deployment-shaped decoding shows the same loss.** With a constrained beam of width B=K (as deployed systems decode), hit@200 changes by −2.59 on Office and −1.96 on Industrial, hit@500 by −3.06 and −1.83. Under the standard protocol (beam 50, report K=10) the same models show +0.39 and +0.46 — the measurement protocol, not the model, decides whether the problem is visible.
- **Ablating the advantage sign is decisive.** Positive advantages only: the hit@500 loss disappears (Office +1.23) and so does the top gain (NDCG@10 no longer significantly different from SFT). Negative advantages only: hit@500 drops 19–21 points, roughly 3× the unmodified Office loss and more than 10× the Industrial one. The gain needs the negatives and so does the damage — they are the same mechanism.
- **Where the negatives come from matters more than whether they exist.** DPO with one negative drawn from the SFT model's beam loses 4.1 points of hit@500 on Office with no top gain at all; DPO with a uniformly random negative leaves hit@500 unchanged (+0.3) and only costs up to 2.2 points at intermediate cut-offs. Penalising the model's own plausible candidates is the harmful ingredient, not contrastive training per se.
- **The pre-RL rollout record predicts the casualties.** Office test prompts in the most-negative-record group lose 27.4 points of hit@500 under GRPO (13.5 on Industrial), the other negative group 9.0, while positive-record prompts gain ~3; targets that never appeared in any training rollout gain +9.6 (Office) and +14.7 (Industrial) purely by not being pushed down. The top-of-list gain concentrates in the most-positive group (hit@1 +5.1).
- **Anchor recovers the recall without buying it back from the head (Office, 3 seeds).** GRPO+anchor: hit@500 52.14% vs 47.69% GRPO and 52.84% SFT — 4.45 of the 5.14 lost points recovered, significant in every seed, with hit@50 26.27% (above SFT's 25.73%) and NDCG@10 12.64% vs GRPO's 12.55% (not significantly different). At every K from 50 to 500 the anchored model is the best of the three, and it is the best of the three at K=50 on all five datasets.
- **Cross-dataset and cross-configuration recovery.** Industrial hit@500 51.72% → 53.63% (+1.91, significant every seed) and above SFT for K≥200; Beauty +0.72, Toys +0.46, Sports +0.89 at K=500, taking all three slightly above their SFT models. With a synced reference, +5.88 hit@500 on Office (46.40% → 52.28%); with REINFORCE++, +13.26 on Office (37.92% → 51.17%) and +5.47 on Industrial (46.94% → 52.42%), plus significant NDCG@10 gains (11.45 → 12.24 and 8.96 → 9.32).
- **The anchor acts exactly where the diagnosis says.** Share of SFT top-500 targets dropped after RL: 14–22% under GRPO, 5–8% under GRPO+anchor. Out-of-rollout score drift on Office: 1.53 nats under GRPO (vs 1.15 inside the rollout, even though all reward is earned inside) → 0.19 with the anchor, an 8× reduction; inside drift also halves to 0.63. For the most-penalised Office test group, hit@500 changes by −23.4 under GRPO but only −3.1 with the anchor (Industrial −10.9 vs −2.3), while the most-rewarded group keeps most of its top gain.
- **Other RL configurations also lose recall, and the anchor fixes them.** Synced reference (reference moved toward the policy) loses 6.4 hit@500 points on Office and 1.5 on Industrial, deepening to 8.9 and 2.1 after a second epoch; REINFORCE++ with batch-normalised advantages loses three to four times as much recall as GRPO. Every anchored pair improves hit@500 significantly.

## Authors & Credibility

Yuheng Chen, Fan Zhang and Yixuan Xu are at HKUST (Guangzhou/Hong Kong); Chao Yang and Yang Xiang are at Trip.com Group (Shanghai); Can Yang (HKUST) is the corresponding author. The group is academic-industry, with the industrial co-authors from a large OTA platform — plausible that the deployment-depth motivation (hundreds to 1,500 candidates handed to a ranker) comes from production experience rather than a thought experiment. The work is positioned in cs.IR and cites the right lineage: TIGER-style generative retrieval, OneRec/OneSearch/MiniOneRec for the RL pipeline, GRPO/DAPO/GSPO/CISPO/ECPO for the optimisation family, and RocketQA/ANCE-style hard-negative literature for the "unlabelled positives" analogy. The related-work section is unusually honest, enumerating six prior papers that report fragments of the same effect (GenRec, RGD, PinGER, Multi-Decoder OneRec, GrowthGR, TSGR, EG-GRPO) instead of claiming the observation is entirely new — the novelty claim is narrowed to "first systematic study: measure it, identify its cause, show a remedy". Methodological care is visible in the details: exact ranking instead of beam approximation, paired bootstrap tests, three seeds for main results, a beam-search consistency check against MiniOneRec's script (~98% top-10 agreement), and an explicit statement that hyper-parameters were not tuned for the three added datasets.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The observation itself is not unprecedented — the paper says so, and lists GenRec's HR@50 drop, RGD's HitRate@604 drop, PinGER's recall collapse, GrowthGR's Recall@10/Recall@1000 trade-off and EG-GRPO's top-1-up/top-10-down as prior fragments. The contribution is therefore the *systematisation* (exact full-catalogue hit@K curves on five datasets, three seeds), the *mechanism* (negative advantages on unlabelled positives, accumulated per item and propagated across users), and the *pre-RL predictor* (rollout record). Proposition 4.1 is a two-line argument about exponential tilting, not a deep theorem; its role is to rule out the objective cleanly and it does that job. The anchor is technically modest — a renormalised KL over a sampled subset, close in spirit to the regularisers in PinGER, Multi-Decoder OneRec and TRL-style reference KL — but it is derived from the diagnosis and doubles as a falsification test, which is a legitimate and underused move.

**Rigor.** Strong where it matters: exact ranking removes an entire class of artefact, the advantage-sign ablation is a clean necessity/sufficiency test, the DPO negative-source manipulation isolates "plausible candidates" from "contrastive training", and the beam-decoding replication closes the "this only happens under exact scoring" objection. Two weaknesses. First, all main results are **one epoch** of GRPO — the paper shows a synced reference getting worse at epoch 2 (6.4 → 8.9 points) but never reports the default GRPO's trajectory beyond one epoch, so the practical magnitude for production training (typically many epochs or continuous updates) is unknown in either direction. Second, the anchor's two hyper-parameters (M=16, λ=0.1) are fixed across all five datasets with **no sensitivity sweep**: the paper cannot say how much of the recovered recall depends on λ, whether M=16 is enough to cover the demoted band on an 18k-item catalogue, or whether λ=0.1 costs anything at convergence. The "no significant NDCG@10 difference" claim is doing real work here and rests on three seeds on datasets where NDCG@10 differences are ~0.1–0.9 points.

**Baselines.** The comparison set is thin in the way that matters: GRPO vs GRPO+anchor, plus two alternative RL base configurations (synced reference, REINFORCE++) — all *their own* re-implementations on one backbone (Qwen2.5-1.5B). There is no comparison against methods designed to fix sparse-rollout signal (LASAR, SAGE, ReCast, hint-conditioned RL, SAPO), even though the paper cites them as the closest line of work and argues they are evaluated at K≤10 and therefore blind to the problem. A reader cannot tell whether those methods *also* lose recall, which is the natural next question and would have cost little: they share the MiniOneRec codebase and the same GRPO structure. Similarly, the anchor is never compared against the obvious cheap alternative — simply lowering β or using a stronger reference KL — so it is unclear how much of the 4.4-point recovery is specific to "out-of-rollout" versus "more regularisation" in general; the fact that the anchor also halves in-rollout drift (1.15 → 0.63 nats) hints the two are not cleanly separable.

**Data quirks and setup choices.** The three added datasets are trained with MiniOneRec's Office/Industrial hyper-parameters, untuned, and the authors acknowledge RL "is probably not at its best there" — which weakens the "the trend is nonetheless the same" argument, since an undertrained RL run may simply move less in both directions at once. Beauty/Toys/Sports also evaluate on 5,000 *sampled* users rather than all test users, unlike Office/Industrial. The catalogue sizes are small by industrial standards (3.5k–18k items); at 10⁶ items the SID prefix tree and the "share the first code" anchor heuristic would behave differently, and the paper's own limitation section concedes this. One detail worth flagging: the anchor samples half its items from "share the first two codes" neighbourhoods, and the justification (44%/57% of rank-17–500 targets live there) is measured on the *test* sets of the same two datasets used to develop the method — a mild selection-information leak, though the neighbourhood definition itself is dataset-agnostic.

**Significance and framing.** The headline claim "better rankers, worse retrievers" is well supported and is a genuinely useful warning for anyone post-training a generative retrieval stage: the standard K≤10 evaluation is not merely incomplete, it is *anti-correlated* with the deployment metric. The remedy is cheap enough to be adopted as a default. The overclaim, if any, is rhetorical rather than empirical: "the hidden cost of RL post-training" reads as a general indictment, whereas the measurement is of one algorithm family (GRPO and two variants), one backbone, and one-epoch training. The paper is careful in the body ("the objective is not to blame", "we did not tune") and less careful in the title.

## Strengths

- **The measurement is the right one.** Exact full-catalogue ranking on five datasets with paired tests and three seeds is expensive and unglamorous, and it is the only way to see a crossover at K≈20 that a K≤10 benchmark hides by construction. The 98% top-10 agreement check against MiniOneRec's beam evaluation shows the exact scorer is faithful, not a different system.
- **Diagnosis and remedy are coupled.** The anchor is not a patch bolted on after the fact; it is the direct consequence of "the damage is outside the rollout", and its properties are stated in a way that makes it falsifiable (starts at zero, invariant to uniform shifts, acts on prompts whose rollout misses the target). The measured 8× reduction in out-of-rollout drift and the 14–22% → 5–8% drop-rate are exactly the predictions the account makes.
- **Honest positioning against prior work.** Listing six industrial papers that already saw fragments of the effect, and narrowing the novelty claim accordingly, is rarer than it should be and makes the paper more trustworthy, not less.
- **The negative-result ablations are as informative as the positive ones.** "Positive advantages only removes the loss *and* the gain" and "uniform DPO negatives don't hurt at large K" are the two experiments that make the mechanism story credible rather than a just-so story.
- **Practical readability.** Table 1 (candidates per request across deployed systems) and Table 2 (share of training prompts whose rollout contains the target) give a reader the two numbers needed to judge whether their own pipeline is exposed: how deep the list is used, and how sparse the RL signal is.

## Limitations

- All results are **one epoch** of GRPO; the multi-epoch trajectory is shown only for the synced-reference variant, so the steady-state magnitude of the recall loss (and whether the anchor holds) is unknown.
- **No hyper-parameter sensitivity** for the anchor: M=16 and λ=0.1 are fixed everywhere, with no sweep over λ, no ablation of the SID-neighbourhood sampling versus uniform sampling, and no measurement of the recall/top-accuracy Pareto curve as λ grows.
- **Single backbone, single SFT recipe** (Qwen2.5-1.5B, MiniOneRec). Whether the crossover point K≈20–50 and the 14–22% drop rate scale with model size or catalogue size is untested; the paper's own future-work section concedes the industrial-scale measurement is missing.
- **Small catalogues** (3.5k–18k items) and **sampled test users** on three of five datasets; the SID-neighbourhood anchor heuristic is justified with test-set statistics from the two development datasets.
- **No comparison with other sparse-signal remedies** (LASAR, SAGE, hint-conditioned RL, SAPO, ReCast) and **no comparison with plain stronger KL regularisation**, so the marginal value of "out-of-rollout" over "more anchoring in general" is not established.
- **No downstream-ranker experiment.** The framing is that recall of the candidate list is what matters, but the paper never measures end-to-end utility with a real ranker in the loop; the online-gain claim is explicitly deferred to future work.
- The three added datasets use untuned RL hyper-parameters, and the authors concede RL may be underperforming there — which muddies the cross-dataset comparison even as the direction of the effect is consistent.

## Relevance

对做生成式推荐 / LLM 后训练评测的工作直接相关：它说明 RL 后训练的收益高度依赖评测深度，K≤10 的 benchmark 会系统性地掩盖候选列表召回退化（Office hit@500 −5.14 点），并给出一个只加一项 loss 的廉价补救。若关注 RL 中"未标注正样本被当负样本"这类奖励设计问题，或需要判断线上召回阶段能否安全套用 GRPO，这篇的测量协议与诊断方法都可直接复用。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. How does the crossover point K* and the size of the tail loss scale with catalogue size (10⁴ → 10⁶) and model size (1.5B → 7B/70B)? Is the loss a fixed fraction of the list or an absolute-rank phenomenon?
2. Does the recall loss compound over multiple epochs, and does the anchor's protection hold at, say, 5–10 epochs? The synced-reference curve (−6.4 → −8.9 from epoch 1 to 2) suggests it may not be linear.
3. What is the λ–recall–NDCG@10 Pareto front, and how does the anchor compare with simply raising β or using a moving reference? Is "out-of-rollout" doing work beyond generic regularisation, given that it also halves in-rollout drift?
4. Do the sparse-rollout remedies the paper cites (LASAR, SAGE, hint-conditioned RL, SAPO, ReCast) also lose recall at large K, or do any of them incidentally avoid it? A single shared-codebase comparison would settle whether this is a GRPO pathology or a family-wide one.
5. Can the reward itself be fixed rather than the loss — e.g. rewarding any item in the SFT model's top-m for the user, or using similar users' next items as soft positives? The paper suggests this in future work but never tests it, and it is arguably the more principled fix.
6. How much of the recovered recall translates into online value with a real downstream ranker? A 4.4-point hit@500 recovery on a 3.5k catalogue is a 8.9% relative recall gain; the corresponding app-stay-time or CTR delta is the number a production team would need.
7. Does the rollout record remain predictive when the SFT model is weak (as on Beauty/Toys/Sports, where only 11–24% of rollouts contain the target) or does the predictor degrade exactly where the loss is hardest to measure?
