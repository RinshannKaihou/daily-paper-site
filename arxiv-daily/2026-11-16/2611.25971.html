Zero-calibration prefill speedup. ANDA, calibrated on WikiText2 itself, reaches 2.37× but pays +1.00% PPL — Kameleo is both faster in quality and 97% as fast in cycles.

Why this matters

Variable-length activations were blocked by per-workload recalibration that silently breaks when data drifts.

Kameleo turns that into a runtime decision for 3.7% area — no calibration set, no re-deployment, no 0.4–1.7 H100-hours per search.

跨数据集标定会静默掉点（42 次迁移里 18 次超预算）；把精度变成在线决策，才让可变长激活真正可部署。

▲ Critical edge

The selection loop itself is never validated end-to-end: lengths come from a software binary search, and equivalence to the hardware's two-pass, three-candidate search is argued from monotonicity shown for Llama2-7B only.

1Methodology

  • Problem W4A16 keeps activations in FP16, so activation arithmetic dominates the compute-bound prefill. Bit-serial block floating point with one mantissa length per GEMM recovers that cost — but the length must be chosen, and today's answer is offline greedy search against full-model perplexity: 13–25 PPL evaluations, 0.4–1.7 H100-hours, and one number for hundreds of GEMMs.
  • Proxy Replace global PPL with a local, label-free error rate per GEMM. Counting erroneous elements (one bit each) rather than summing error magnitude keeps a few outliers from dominating the metric.
  • Search εk is monotone in k, so nine candidate lengths (3–11 bits) need only a search: each GEMM tries three candidates per pass, pass 1 narrows 9 → ≤3, pass 2 resolves and locks. Both passes are ordinary full-precision forwards whose tokens are still served.
  • Hardware ANDA's bit-serial PE already holds the exact k-bit dot product at cycle k. So the reduced and full results need no second arithmetic pass: three FP32 candidate registers plus a reference latch the partial sum, an error-check unit compares, and a flag collector folds 256 PE flags into 16 INT5 counts per tile.

Calibrate ANDA on each of seven datasets, deploy on the other six: 18 transfers blow the 1% budget, worst case +2.9% (LAMBADA-calibrated config on CBT). Kameleo stays inside on all seven datasets at 2.25–2.28×.

C4-calibrated Llama2-13B reads 5.11 PPL on WikiText2 — +2.36%, more than double the budget. On Yi1.5-34B the WikiText2 configuration fails 5 of 6 transfers while the C4 one fails only 1: the fragility is configuration-dependent, i.e. unpredictable ahead of time.

A seven-by-seven matrix is the right experiment for this claim, and reporting all 42 cells — not one anecdote — is what makes the regime argument land.

6Are the two constants really constants?

s = 4 and τ = 0.28 are never retuned across four models and seven datasets — the obvious objection is that this is calibration in disguise. Two sweeps answer it:

✓Strengths

  • The quality signal is read out of arithmetic the accelerator is already doing — cost is registers and a comparator, not a second datapath.
  • An unusually honest baseline: ANDA calibrated on the evaluation data itself, and Kameleo still wins on PPL at every model.
  • A 7×7 transfer matrix instead of a single anecdotal failure, with 18/42 violations reported rather than one cherry-picked cell.
  • Both constants stress-tested, which is the right answer to "this is calibration in disguise".
  • Cost reported at three granularities — FP path, MXU, whole accelerator — so +3.7% can be checked against +55%.
  • Strict superset of a published accelerator: the ablation is architectural, not comparative.

!Limitations

  • Selection is demonstrated by software emulation with bit-exact truncation, not by the actual two-pass, three-candidate hardware loop.
  • No per-GEMM agreement rate against oracle lengths, and no measure of how often a GEMM is mis-locked.
  • Throughput is analytic MXU-cycle counting validated by RTL — no end-to-end latency, energy-per-token or tokens-per-joule.
  • Decode is memory-bound and out of scope; the serving-mix win is unquantified.
  • A fixed 8 192-token prefix is a poor fit for short chat or agentic requests; no amortization or break-even analysis.
  • The baseline uses four GEMM-type lengths, Kameleo one per GEMM — criterion and granularity are conflated in the 80–97% figure.
  • No iso-quality VS-Quant point, so the Pareto claim is unresolved; RTL is not stated to be open-sourced.

?Questions for further exploration

  1. What is the per-GEMM agreement rate with oracle-calibrated lengths, and does disagreement sit on flat εk curves (nearly free) or steep ones?
  2. How does it behave on a prompt shorter than the 8 192-token prefix — what is the break-even length, and would hierarchical selection preserve the guarantee?
  3. Is εk monotone for every GEMM of every model, or only on average? If not, can the search provably lock a length that violates τ?
  4. Where does per-vector INT4 sit near the 1% budget — is there a 2.8× operating point that makes the runtime machinery unnecessary?
  5. How does it interact with KV-cache quantization and decode-phase arithmetic, where bandwidth rather than MXU cycles dominates?
  6. Can εk drift be watched as a cheap online health signal — turning the selection unit into a distribution-shift or numerical-anomaly monitor?
  7. What is the energy (not power) overhead of the two passes, amortized over a multi-tenant trace with mixed prompt lengths?
