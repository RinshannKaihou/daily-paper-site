Why this matters

Anyone serving LLMs under bursty demand meets the same trap: engines look busy while useful throughput collapses. Here is the mechanism, and a two-knob fix.

Scope check: asynchronous post / live-video understanding — latency-tolerant work, not chat or agents.
Read it critically

The headline 43% is a bundle, not a dispatcher effect: it conflates enabling a per-replica cap, the Track policy, and fleet sizing. Appendix A.2 says the A/B tests “the whole design.”

Cap

A labeled refusal at whatever actually saturates — provider quota, gateway per-caller quota, engine request cap, queue-depth router. For vLLM an in-flight check throws an overload exception that the RPC layer maps to HTTP 429. max_num_seqs 128 with a 140 cap per replica means fleet admission scales with live replicas, no resize reconfiguration.

Track

One MIMD loop per capacity domain. Every 15 s the lexicographically smallest hostname acts as leader — no consensus service — and multiplies each queue's rate from its 429 share. Only capacity refusals count; timeouts are excluded, because backing off a network fault would idle capacity. A utilization gate blocks climbs below 75% of target.

Waiting discipline is the quiet half of the design. Engines keep only a short queue behind the running batch; everything else waits in FOQS, outside the timeout clock. A refused request returns to the front of its own worker's ready queue with media already staged — sending it back to the shared queue would bounce it across workers and re-stage media until retry limits fired.

06 Strengths What holds up

  • The negative result is first-class. Equal token throughput (14,572 vs 14,557 tok/s), goodput 9.14 → 0. The cleanest demonstration that inference overload is metastable, not a throughput problem.
  • Small enough to reason about. Two primitives, one control law, four constants, all hot-tunable — not a dozen coupled scheduler knobs.
  • Operational detail that earns its space. Freeze on worker-count change, local retries with media kept staged, placement sized so a low-rate queue still gets attempts.
  • Honest evidence labels. Every production row in Table 1 is marked A/B or observational, with caveats attached rather than blurred.
  • Scale rarely reported. Nine resizes, a preemption storm, and a launch that survived all of it without retuning capacity.

07 Limitations Where to discount

  • 43% is a bundle. Cap + policy + sizing move together; cap-only-without-Track is the missing ablation.
  • Most production evidence is observational. In the 94.8% vs 88.2% window the target refusal share was raised 5% → 30%, so part of the gain is bought with refusals.
  • The controlled Track test is prefill-only: one-token outputs, max_num_seqs 16. Decode-heavy generative dispatch is observed, never measured.
  • Needs a labeled refusal everywhere. Substituting timeouts for 429s costs 27.8–38.0% goodput in the authors' own ramp.
  • 13 minutes to adapt to a 40% capacity loss, plus 6.6 h low-priority waits during backfills — fine for async, disqualifying for interactive.
  • No baseline against QLM, Mooncake, CONCUR or Sarathi-Serve; the only controller comparison is AIMD, a 10% step and Gradient2 on an emulator.

08 Open questions For the next paper

  1. Cap without Track: how much of the 43% survives if the precursor keeps its hand-tuned rate but gains per-replica caps? Replay hints caps carry most of the benefit.
  2. Slow refusals: what if the backend decides after 2 s of queueing? The stability argument assumes refusals are cheap and fast.
  3. Tier edges: why ×0.99 all the way to 50%? A 40%-refusal episode taking 13 minutes suggests the 50%/65% thresholds were fitted, not derived.
  4. Head-of-line fairness: does returning a refused request to its own worker's queue starve lower priorities on a worker holding most of the high-priority work?
  5. Prefix caching: cache hits change service time, so they change what a given dispatch rate means for the engine. Does the refusal signal survive?
  6. Flex pools: can queue depth itself drive pool placement, and how does that loop interact with Track on the same queue?
