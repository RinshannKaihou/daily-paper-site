> **一句话总结：** 本文只往 router logit 上加一个常数偏置，把 token 强行推入由某概念/格式自身 token 统计出的跨层专家路径，在 Qwen3-30B-A3B 上证明"格式路径"能把 Python 类型标注改写成数字串（数字字符占比 0.087→0.792，是随机路径的 44 倍），而"概念路径"虽能把下一个 token 的概率推向该概念（中文宗教词建路 → 英文宗教词概率 7.1 倍于随机路径），却几乎不改变生成文本，作者把差异归因于格式一旦启动会自我强化。

> **TL;DR:** A router-logit-bias intervention (s=8 over layers 16–31) on Qwen3-30B-A3B forces tokens onto data-derived cross-layer expert paths. Steering a type-annotation colon onto the clock-time path lifts the digit fraction of the continuation from 0.087 to 0.792 versus 0.018 for a bias-matched random path (44×, 44× over random, N=30 prompts), while a religion path built entirely from Chinese tokens raises English religion-word probability 7.1× over a random path (12.4× on a held-out list) with no corresponding rise in generated text (~1.1× for money words). Partial-generation sweeps show the format effect is self-reinforcing (steering only the first token already gives 0.21), and the 9-concept ordering (religion 20×, money 6.3×, law 2.4×, six concepts null) correlates with neither embedding distinctiveness nor word frequency (both r=+0.11, n=9). Worth reading for the clean causal design and the honest nulls; the single main model and the confound between expert identity and gate weight limit how far the conclusions carry.

## 核心贡献

- **路径级转向方法**：不修改 hidden state，只在目标专家每一层的 router logit 上加常数偏置 `r̃ = r + s·1_{j*}`，从而完整控制"哪些专家被选中"，而每个专家收到的输入保持不变——这让输出变化可以归因于专家选择本身。
- **格式路径能改写生成文本**：把代码里表示类型标注的冒号强推到"时刻"（12:30 那种冒号）路径上，模型从写 `List[int]` 变成写数字串，数字字符占比从 0.087 升到 0.792，而偏置强度相同的随机路径只有 0.018，即 44 倍。
- **概念路径能跨语言推动概率分布**：用中文宗教词（宗教、祈祷、教堂……）建路径，测英文宗教词，概率升到随机路径的 7.1 倍、留出词表的 12.4 倍；因为中英文不共享 token，这个效应不可能是"重复建路时的 token"造成的。
- **解释"格式进得去文本、概念进不去"**：只对前 1 个 token 转向就把数字占比从 0.085 拉到 0.21，前 4 个拉到 0.60，全序列 0.79——格式一旦开始就自我强化；概念词没有这种自增强回路。
- **哪些概念可转、在哪里生效**：religion 与 money 最强（20× 和 6.3×），law 弱（2.4×），其余六个概念不可靠；效应集中在中间层（L16–31）；被推专家的"身份"比它的 gate 权重更决定方向。
- **跨四个 MoE 架构的对照**：路径可分性大致跟随 routing 宽度（top-8 的 Qwen3/OLMoE 六个概念各有独立路径，top-4 的 Qwen1.5-MoE 只有 3/6），并指出跨模型比较必须固定 routed weight，否则结论会被权重归一化方式污染。

## Key Contributions

- **A path-level steering primitive.** Instead of perturbing the hidden-state subspace the router reads (as Ye et al. 2026 suggest), the authors bias the router logits directly, so the expert inputs are untouched and any output change is attributable to expert selection. This also makes it trivial to swap in a random or frequency/norm-matched expert as a control.
- **Format steering reaches the text.** Forcing a type-annotation ":" onto the clock-time path (layers 8–39) converts type completions into digit strings: digit fraction 0.087 → 0.792 vs 0.018 for a bias-matched random path. Across all six ordered pairs of the three colon uses, steering raises the target use's tokens to 2–40× the random path, and 26 of the 32 steered layers take different experts for different colon uses.
- **Concept steering is cross-lingual and distribution-only.** A Chinese-built religion path raises English religion-word probability 7.1× over random (12.4× held-out), positive on 54/60 and 57/60 prompts and above each of 30 independently drawn random paths (6.2× their mean), while the generated text barely moves.
- **A mechanistic account of the format/concept gap.** Steered-only-prefix experiments isolate self-reinforcement: steering the first token alone already yields digit fraction 0.21; steering from token 4 onward still yields 0.41, so neither position-0 primacy nor a global logit shift explains it.
- **A steerability ordering that resists the obvious explanations.** Religion > money > law > {medicine, nature, time, emotion, travel, food}, with a pre-registered distinctiveness prediction that fails and frequency that does not predict it either.
- **Honest cross-architecture bookkeeping.** Reporting that OLMoE's apparent non-transfer is an artifact of weight renormalization (0.04 → 0.28 digit fraction once native routed weight is preserved) is the kind of methodological catch that most steering papers omit.

## Methodology

**Models.** Main model: Qwen3-30B-A3B (48 layers, 128 experts, top-8). Secondary: OLMoE-1B-7B (top-8/64), Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B (top-4/60), DeepSeek-V2-Lite (top-6/64).

**Building a target path.** For a concept or syntactic role, pick a handful of unambiguous exemplar tokens (e.g. "prayer", "scripture", "faith" for religion; 20 Chinese exemplars for the cross-lingual condition). Run each through the model, record its top-1 expert at every layer, and take the per-layer mode across exemplars:

  j*_ℓ(c) = argmax_j |{w ∈ W_c : argmax_{j'} r_{ℓ,j'}(w) = j}|

So the path is literally "the expert the concept's own tokens most often agree on", not a hand-picked circuit.

**Steering.** Add a constant s to the target expert's logit before top-k selection, `r̃_ℓ = r_ℓ + s·1_{j*_ℓ}`. The target joins the selected set and the router still picks the other k−1 experts. Default: middle layers L16–31, s=8. A strong bias also inflates the target's softmax gate weight (natural top-1 ≈0.42 → ≈0.96), which the authors probe explicitly in §6.

**Two readouts.** (i) *Distribution*: next-token probability on the target's tokens (digits; first subtokens of a religion/money word list). (ii) *Text*: let the model actually generate and score what it wrote (fraction of digit characters; fraction of words in the concept list). Every effect is reported relative to a random path receiving the same bias, with paired tests and 95% prompt-bootstrap CIs over prompts.

**Controls.** A bias-matched random path (one uniformly random expert per layer); cross-concept readout matrices (promote path A, measure all nine concepts' words) to test specificity; a held-out word list that played no role in path construction; and Chinese→English path/readout separation to rule out token-repetition as the mechanism.

**Localization and ablation sweeps.** Equal-width layer bands (L8–15 / L16–31 / L32–39 / L8–39) at s=16; bias strength s ∈ {0,4,8,12,20}; prefix/ suffix-only steering over generation steps; expert identity vs. delivered gate weight, including activation-frequency-matched and output-norm-matched random experts; single-expert removal on 64 cloze prompts per concept as a necessity test.

## Results & Highlights

- **Format steering on Qwen3-30B-A3B (colon → clock-time path, L8–39, N=30 prompts).** Digit fraction of the continuation goes 0.087 (unsteered) → 0.792 (steered) versus 0.018 for the bias-matched random path, i.e. a **44× increase over random**; output entropy stays in 2.3–2.7 nats, so this is not a degenerate collapse. The three colon uses take different experts at **26 of the 32 steered layers**, and across all six ordered pairs of uses the target use's tokens rise to **2–40×** random.
- **Layer localization (clock-time token probability, s=16, N=20 prompts).** Middle band L16–31 gives **0.418**, against **0.076** for early L8–15 and **0.088** for late L32–39 — a ~5× gap that cannot be a width artifact since all bands are equal width. Religion shows the same shape: the two middle eight-layer windows give 3.5× and 8.4× the random path, all other windows give no effect.
- **Bias strength does more than select.** At **s=6** the target is already the top-1 expert at every steered layer, yet raising to **s=8** still lifts the clock-time probability from **0.139 to 0.457** — so part of the effect is the enlarged gate weight, not just membership in the top-k.
- **Cross-lingual concept steering (Chinese-built religion path, 60 neutral English prompts).** English religion-word probability reaches **7.1×** the random path (positive on **54/60** prompts) and **12.4×** on the held-out word list (**57/60**), and beats each of **30 independently drawn random paths** at 6.2× their mean. Absolute probabilities remain small: **1.2×10⁻³ → 8.3×10⁻³**. Money steers at 3.6× but not on its held-out list, which the authors attribute to money words already being common in neutral text.
- **Distribution vs. text decouple for concepts (Figure 4).** Format moves both readouts (44× probability and 44× digit fraction); money moves probability **3.6×** but the money-word fraction of generated text stays near 0.01, i.e. **1.1×** the random path — indistinguishable from no effect.
- **Self-reinforcement explains the gap (N=30 prompts, 12 generated tokens).** Steering the **first token only** raises digit fraction from 0.085 to **0.21**; first two tokens **0.23**; first four **0.60**; every token **0.79**; and steering only **from token 4 onward** still reaches **0.41**. Prefix length, not absolute position, is what matters.
- **Steerability ordering over nine concepts (N=60 paired).** religion **20×**, money **6.3×**, law **2.4×** (no held-out effect), while medicine, nature, food, travel, emotion and time do not reliably exceed 1×. Neither embedding distinctiveness nor corpus frequency predicts the ordering: **Pearson r = +0.11 for each (n=9)**, and the authors' pre-registered prediction that religion/astronomy would be more distinctive than object/action **failed** (they scored below).
- **Specificity follows the same ordering.** Promoting religion raises religion words most (**+2.59 log-odds**) and money raises money words most (**+1.16**), but promoting nature raises *religion* words (**+1.67**) while *lowering* nature words (**−0.40**), and promoting law raises religion (+0.70) about as much as law (+0.55) — the weak concepts leak.
- **Expert identity beats gate weight (religion diagnostic mass, N=60 paired).** Forcing the religion expert to nominal weight **0.42** (delivered 0.55) gives **3.45× native [1.44, 9.82], p=0.003** (held-out only 2.91×, n.s.), while a random expert at the same weight gives **0.88× native, n.s.**; at the equal-share weight **0.125** the effect vanishes (**0.95×**). Frequency-matched random experts give 3.7× and norm-matched 6.6× on the primary list, so identity is not reducible to "a busy expert".
- **Cross-architecture (religion steering, 60 prompts).** Qwen3-30B-A3B **7.1×** (6/6 distinct paths), OLMoE-1B-7B **1.3×** (6/6), Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B **3.2×** (3/6), DeepSeek-V2-Lite **0.2×** (5/6). For OLMoE, preserving the native routed weight turns a null format result (digit fraction 0.04 → 0.04) into **0.043 → 0.281 (+0.238)**, though only **4 of 40** outputs begin with a valid time and the format-continuation NLL only drops from 8.6 to **5.9** nats/token against a native **0.8**.
- **Necessity is not established.** Removing one naturally selected expert (64 cloze prompts/concept) yields religion diagnostic-probability change of **−0.26 pp [−0.62, 0.04]** and reference log-probability **−0.04 [−0.11, 0.02]** versus a matched control — no reliable selective impairment, and for time the native probability is already 99.98%, leaving no headroom to detect anything.

## Authors & Credibility

Mukund Athmaram (AMD, and Cornell — corresponding author, work done during an AMD internship), Manoj Rao (AMD), Liang Ma (AMD). Published at the NeurIPS 2026 Workshop *Interpretability as a Science*. The paper is a direct follow-up to Ye, Yuan & Sharkey (2026), "Polysemantic experts, monosemantic paths", and it engages the live disagreement about whether experts are genuine specialists (Herbst et al. 2026) or whether apparent specialization is hidden-state geometry (Wang et al. 2026; Ternovtsii & Bilak 2026). The framing sits on the "routing matters causally" side of that argument but reports results that are arguably more consistent with the sceptical side (see below). All four evaluated models are open-weights, which makes the core protocol reproducible; no code or data release is announced.

## Critical Assessment

*Novelty.* The intervention itself is deliberately minimal — bias the router logit, which is essentially Fayyaz et al. (2026) with a cross-layer path instead of per-behaviour experts. The genuinely new contributions are the **format vs. concept** split and the **self-reinforcement** explanation for why one survives into text. That is a real idea, cleanly tested, and the prefix-sweep in Figure 5 is the most convincing figure in the paper.

*Rigor — what holds up.* The control design is better than typical for this literature: the random path receives the *same* bias (so "any large perturbation" is excluded), 30 independent random paths are all beaten, a held-out word list is reported alongside the primary one, and building the path in Chinese while reading out in English kills the token-repetition confound outright. The equal-width layer bands correctly rule out the "middle band is wider" objection. The OLMoE renormalization catch (Table 4) is a methodological contribution in its own right, and reporting the §8 removal null instead of burying it is to the authors' credit.

*Rigor — where it weakens.* (1) **The identity/weight confound is acknowledged but not solved.** The authors state the two "cannot be fully separated", then run the weight sweep with weights set *before* renormalization, so the religion and random experts do not actually receive the same delivered weight — they admit this in §9, but it means the headline "identity sets the direction" claim rests on a comparison with unequal treatment. (2) **Effect sizes are ratios against a random-path denominator that is often near zero.** The 44× headline is 0.792/0.018; the religion 7.1× is 0.0083/0.0012, i.e. an absolute shift of 0.7 percentage points in next-token probability. Those are real but small, and no downstream behavioural or task-level consequence is ever measured. (3) **An internal inconsistency is left unexplained:** money steers at **3.6×** in §4 (Figure 4) and **6.3×** in §5 (Figure 6), with no note on what changed between the two runs — the prompt set is N=60 in both. (4) **The n=9 correlation analysis is badly underpowered** for the claim that distinctiveness and frequency "do not explain" the ordering: r=+0.11 on nine points, no CI, no power discussion; the honest statement is "we found no evidence", not "these do not account for it". (5) **The purity numbers undercut the path story in a way the paper does not confront:** money words agree on the same expert only **48%** of the time yet money steers strongly, while clean-path religion (purity 0.47 vs 0.19 for generic tokens) is the strongest — so "having your own path" is neither necessary nor sufficient, and the rescue ("the effect comes from many layers together", 2–4× for shared experts vs 17–28× for the whole band) is post-hoc.

*Reproducibility.* Open-weights models and a simple, fully specified intervention (bias, layer band, s) make this reproducible in principle, but three things are missing from the text: the actual exemplar word lists beyond the 8 shown in Figure 3, the decoding configuration (temperature / greedy / number of generated tokens is stated only for Figure 5), and any code release. A reader cannot currently rebuild the religion path.

*Significance.* The paper's own §8 result is the most important caveat: when the model selects the expert itself, removing it produces no detectable selective drop. Combined with the fact that the entire positive result requires a bias that inflates the target's gate weight from ~0.42 to ~0.96, the defensible conclusion is narrow — *forced* expert trajectories are a usable handle on output form, and the natural routing computation is still unproven to be causally load-bearing. The paper mostly says this, but the framing ("causal interventions on expert trajectories") is stronger than the evidence for the natural-use case.

*Red flags.* Small-N generation experiments (12 tokens in Figure 5, 20 tokens for the Figure 3 average) with word-level text metrics; no human or LLM-judge assessment of whether steered outputs are fluent rather than merely digit-heavy; the "44×" and "20×" headlines are ratio-of-small-numbers; four architectures differ simultaneously in size, data and expert design, so the routing-width correlation (they call it a correlation, fairly) has one data point per width class.

## Strengths

- A genuinely minimal, interpretable intervention: only router logits change, expert inputs are identical, so attribution to expert selection is clean rather than assumed.
- Excellent control discipline — bias-matched random paths, 30 random draws, held-out word lists, cross-lingual path construction, cross-concept readout matrices.
- The prefix-steering experiment (Figure 5) is a small, decisive test of a specific mechanistic hypothesis rather than another correlation.
- Willingness to report nulls: six of nine concepts do not steer, the pre-registered distinctiveness prediction fails, and the single-expert removal experiment is inconclusive.
- The cross-architecture section identifies and fixes a real measurement artifact (OLMoE's missing weight renormalization), which is directly useful to anyone comparing steering strength across MoE models.
- Well-scoped claims in §9 and an explicit list of what remains open.

## Limitations

- One main model carries essentially all the quantitative claims; the other three architectures only test path structure plus a religion-format subset.
- The intervention is strong (s=8 across 16 layers, delivered gate weight ≈0.96 vs natural ≈0.42), so "forced path" and "plausible expert weighting" are different regimes.
- No comparison against the obvious alternative interventions (residual-stream steering, CAA, directed steering) on the same task; the practical advantage over activation steering is asserted, not measured.
- Nothing downstream: no benchmark, no task accuracy, no judged output quality — only next-token probability on word lists and character/word fractions in short generations.
- The mechanism for *which* concepts steer remains unexplained; the two candidate explanations are rejected but nothing is proposed in their place.
- Natural-use necessity (whole-path ablation, not single-expert) is untested, and that is precisely the question the title implies.
- Decoding setup, full exemplar lists and code are not provided, limiting exact replication.

## Relevance

直接命中 **LLM Hidden States & Interpretability**：用 router-logit 干预做 MoE 专家路径的因果可解释性，方法干净、控制扎实，是理解"路由到底控制了什么"的一手证据。对关注 MoE 训练/推理行为（路由是否承载语义、专家特化是否真实）的读者也有参考价值，尤其 §8 的负结果值得记一笔。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the format effect survive at natural gate weights? I.e. can you get a digit run by promoting the clock-time expert at the ~0.42 weight a natural top-1 expert receives, over many more than 12 generated tokens?
2. Why does money steer 3.6× in §4 but 6.3× in §5 — different prompt sets, different readout lists, or a different s/layer band?
3. Whole-path ablation during natural use: if removing all 16 middle-layer religion experts (rather than one) still leaves religion cloze accuracy intact, does that falsify the "paths are monosemantic control handles" reading?
4. Is the self-reinforcement account predictive? A concept whose words are syntactically self-similar (e.g. a numeral system, a list format) should steer into text; a topic with diverse surface forms should not. That is a testable pre-registration the paper does not run.
5. How does the effect compare to simply adding a religion direction to the residual stream at the same layers, on the same 60 prompts and the same readout? Without that, "routing is a handle" is not established relative to the cheaper alternative.
6. Does the 44× format effect hold for conversational or instruction-tuned prompts, where the model has a strong prior to stay in prose?
