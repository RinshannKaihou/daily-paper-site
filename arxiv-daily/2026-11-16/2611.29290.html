Why this matters

A skill's gain can vanish when the harness around it changes — so agent “self-improvement” headlines may be interface artifacts, not capability.

Critical edge

Rescored by answer content, the two largest harness gains collapse — SQuAD 44.34 → 2.12, Hotpot Context 48.18 → 0.00 — bought by answer formatting, not repair.

The authors' own rescoring, not a critic's.

Strengths

What the design buys

  • The design is the contribution. One axis changed on matched instances, at a scale the intervention literature rarely attempts.
  • Format-versus-content rescoring is a reusable weapon: two lines of analysis dismantle a class of published harness and skill gains.
  • Real negative results, reported as such: routers lose to a fixed config, escalation costs 68% more for +0.5 points, 59.7% of failures sit beyond every tested action.
  • Stage-wise recovery vocabulary — exposure → response → local repair → final success — plus a memorable BigCode vs Spreadsheet contrast.
  • Artifacts: hashed frozen skills, split manifests, an instance-level ledger, scripts that regenerate every table.

Limitations

Where to discount it

  • H2 is a bundle — feedback plus three retries are never separated, so “feedback is not recovery” measures “feedback + retry”.
  • Rescue is a one-model phenomenon: regimes, signal coding and prediction are Qwen3.5-9B only; the headline rests on −19.5 and −22.8 penalties in a 9B model, two on floor cells.
  • Thin label coverage at the exact cells that matter: Hotpot Context has one labeled failure, FullWiki ten, LiveMath is dropped entirely.
  • Unpinned API snapshots (Sonnet 4.6/5, GPT-5.6-sol) cannot be re-run; only ledger re-analysis reproduces.
  • Narrow skill space: two prompt-text procedures. “Self-evolving skills stall” is not evidence about skills carrying tools, retrieval or code.

Open questions

What a follow-up should run

Can H2 be factorized into {feedback, retry budget} × {1, 3} retries? Four extra cells per benchmark would unpack the central mechanism claim.

Why does rescue appear only for Qwen3.5-9B — a capability threshold, or an artifact of the floor cells at H0/H1?

Does the cross-model collapse generalize into a transfer law, or stay an anecdote about one benchmark and one skill?

Would skills carrying executable checks — validators, retrieval calls, precondition assertions — escape the “missing facts” and shortcut failures?
