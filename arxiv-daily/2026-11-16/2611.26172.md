> **一句话总结：** 作者发现 RLVR 训练时的固定 rollout 上限（如 4k tokens）不只是成本参数，而会被模型"内化"成一条学到的隐式长度约束：在 Qwen3.5-4B / Gemma4-E2B / Nemotron-3-Nano-4B 上，4k 训练后模型在匹配预算 4K 处比 base 高 +34.8 pp（AIME avg@3 35.93% vs 1.11%），但到 64K 处反而低 −30.2 pp（48.15% vs 78.33%），平均输出长度从 base 的 27.32k 塌到 4.97k；消融表明根因不是 GRPO 的某个目标项，而是"被截断的成功轨迹"这一数据分布特征——SFT 在人工截断轨迹上同样复现，在自然结束的短轨迹上则不复现。

> **TL;DR:** The paper isolates a training-side echo of test-time scaling: GRPO with a fixed rollout cap (4k/8k for Qwen3.5-4B, 1k/2k for Gemma4-E2B and Nemotron-3-Nano-4B) teaches the model to *stop at that budget*, so it gains hugely near the cap but loses at larger ones. On AIME 2024+2025 avg@3, the Qwen 4k-trained checkpoint goes from 35.93% at 4K (base: 1.11%, +34.8 pp) to 48.15% at 64K (base: 78.33%, −30.2 pp) with mean length 4.97k vs the base's 27.32k tokens; all 18 Qwen DeepScaleR runs (6 conditions × 3 seeds) reproduce the crossover, and Gemma/Nemotron show the same (+10.9→−24.8 pp, +23.3→−25.4 pp). Crucially it is *not* GRPO-specific — rejection fine-tuning on 4K budget-forced truncated traces reproduces the collapse (8K: +9.3 pp, 64K: −38.4 pp) while SFT on naturally short traces does not (8K: −1.4 pp, 64K: +4.3 pp) — and no tested intervention recovers both the in-budget gain and the base model's high-budget accuracy.

## 核心贡献

- **命名并刻画一个新现象（budget internalization / 预算内化）**：固定 rollout 上限在训练中把"生成预算"变成了策略行为的一部分。训练后模型在达到上限附近获得明显增益，但一旦评测预算超过训练预算，输出长度与准确率同时饱和；而 base 模型在同样的大预算下继续变好。因此评测只报"训练上限处的分数"会系统性地掩盖模型排序的翻转。
- **跨模型族 / 跨训练任务 / 跨评测任务的系统性复现**：三个模型族（Qwen3.5-4B、Gemma4-E2B、Nemotron-3-Nano-4B）× 两个训练集（DeepScaleR 数学、Nemotron-RL-ReasoningGym-v1 的 104 个程序化环境）× 多个训练上限（4k/8k、1k/2k）。DeepScaleR/AIME 主实验做了 3 个种子 × 2 种 checkpoint 选择（验证集最优 vs 最终 step 300），18/18 全部复现 crossover；现象还从数学训练迁移到 GPQA-Diamond 和 LiveCodeBench v6。
- **一个重要的反向发现：内化不总是坏事**。在 GPQA / LCB 上，损失的高预算精度有限（Qwen 8k-trained 在 64K 上 GPQA −2.2 pp、LCB-v6 −1.0 pp），但输出长度缩短 2.4–4.6 倍（GPQA 11.15k→4.56k，LCB 38.32k→8.31k）；Nemotron 的 2k-trained 甚至在 GPQA 64K 上 +4.0 pp。即"用更少 token 拿到相当精度"，在模型过度思考的任务上是净收益。
- **消融把根因从"RL 目标项"移到"训练信号的长度分布"**：数据集难度（GSM8K 24% 截断 vs DeepScaleR 79% vs Nemotron-RL-Math-v2 99%）、截断轨迹如何打分（零奖励 / loss masking / DAPO 长度惩罚 / 强制作答）、长度归一化（Dr.GRPO）、KL 锚定（β=0.001 / 0.01）、以及 SFT 对照。结论：长度归一化几乎无影响（Dr.GRPO 64K −23.1 pp ≈ GRPO −22.3 pp），惩罚截断会加重（DAPO −28.8 pp），而"截断后强制作答"几乎消除内化却同时放弃预算内增益（8K −2.2 pp / 64K −1.6 pp）。
- **证明该效应不是 RL 专属**：在自采样成功轨迹上做拒绝微调，若轨迹是"自然在 4K 内结束"，高预算能力反而超过 base（64K +4.3 pp）；若轨迹是"自然超过 4K、被 budget forcing 强行截断到 4096 token 后补出正确答案"，则完全复现 GRPO 的失败（64K −38.4 pp）。两组轨迹长度相同、只差"推理是否自然终止"，因此把效应归因于 length-censored post-training，而非 PPO/GRPO 的任何特定项。
- **可复现的产出**：全部训练 checkpoint（含消融）在 HuggingFace 上以 `JetBrains-Research/budget-internalization` 集合发布，附录给出完整超参、评测采样参数、每种子逐预算数值与截断率曲线。

## Key Contributions

- **Reframes the rollout cap as a behavioral knob, not just a cost knob.** The paper's central move is to evaluate *curves* (accuracy and mean output length vs. evaluation budget) instead of a single point at the training cap, and to show that the standard single-budget evaluation protocol hides a ranking reversal that can be as large as 30 accuracy points.
- **A three-family, two-task, three-benchmark replication.** Qwen3.5-4B (4k/8k caps), Gemma4-E2B and Nemotron-3-Nano-4B (1k/2k caps), each trained on DeepScaleR and on Nemotron-RL-ReasoningGym-v1 (15,000 examples, 104 procedural environments), evaluated on AIME 2024+2025, GPQA-Diamond and LiveCodeBench v6. The 18-run multi-seed replication (seeds 42/43/44, both validation-selected and final checkpoints) is unusually thorough for a phenomenon paper.
- **A dissociation that locates the cause.** Cap-induced collapse survives Dr.GRPO renormalization, is *worsened* by DAPO's overlength penalty, is *removed* by forcing an answer on truncated rollouts, is *gradually* traded off by KL anchoring, and — decisively — is reproduced by SFT on artificially truncated traces but not on naturally short ones. The cause is the length distribution of reinforced successful responses, not an RL-specific gradient term.
- **An honest cost-benefit reframing.** Because the trained models compress reasoning, the effect is a capability loss on AIME but an inference-efficiency win on GPQA/LiveCodeBench, where base models often overthink. The paper explicitly declines to call the effect simply harmful, and closes with a concrete evaluation recommendation (report accuracy *and* length at several budgets).
- **A well-instrumented negative result.** No tested intervention — dataset swap, truncation handling, normalization, KL, RFT — recovers the full in-budget gain *and* the base model's high-budget scaling; the paper says so plainly rather than cherry-picking the best trade-off point.

## Methodology

**Setup.** Three open reasoning models: Qwen3.5-4B (4k and 8k rollout caps), Gemma4-E2B and Nemotron-3-Nano-4B (1k and 2k caps). Caps are deliberately chosen *below* the length at which base models normally finish AIME responses, so the cap binds frequently (base-model truncation at the cap: Qwen on AIME 100% at 2k/4k, 95% at 8k). Training uses NeMo RL with GRPO: 32 prompts/step, 8 generations/prompt (G=8), global rollout batch 256, 3 epochs, ≤300 optimizer steps, 10-step linear warmup + cosine decay, bf16, PPO clipping ε=0.2, **no reference-policy KL** in the headline run, validation every 10 steps with best-checkpoint selection. Reward verifies the final answer and assigns zero to rollouts that hit the cap without one.

**Training tasks.** (a) DeepScaleR competition math; (b) Nemotron-RL-ReasoningGym-v1, 15,000 examples from 104 procedurally generated verifiable Reasoning Gym environments, same hyperparameters as the matching DeepScaleR run.

**Evaluation.** Inspect AI; `avg@k` accuracy with the evaluation budget acting as the generated-token limit; budgets swept from 512/2k up to 64k (Qwen) or 16k (Gemma/Nemotron). Main metric: AIME 2024+2025 (60 problems, avg@3 in the main figure, avg@16 in the ablation table). Transfer: GPQA-Diamond, LiveCodeBench v6. Per-model sampling follows each HuggingFace model card (Qwen T=1.0/top-k=20, Gemma T=1.0/top-k=64, Nemotron model-card defaults), thinking mode on.

**Measurement design.** The paper defines budget internalization behaviorally: above the training budget, the base model keeps spending tokens and improving, while the trained model's length and accuracy both saturate. It reports mean length with seed min–max bands, truncation rates per budget (Table 12), and — importantly — separates "the model stops early" from "the model can no longer use tokens" via two interventions:
- *Budget forcing upward on the base model* (Muennighoff et al. style): if a response reaches budget B with no parseable answer, keep the reasoning prefix, insert the model-specific end-of-thinking delimiter plus an answer cue, and allow a short answer window. This tests how much of the base model's low-budget deficit is pure overthinking.
- *Forcing the trained model upward*: strip its end-of-thinking delimiter and final answer, append "Wait", and ask it to continue, up to four times under a 64k cap. This tests whether its high-budget loss is only early stopping.

**Ablation grid (Qwen3.5-4B, 8k cap, one change at a time, everything else — optimizer, update count, eval protocol — fixed; avg@16).** Training data: GSM8K / DeepScaleR / Nemotron-RL-Math-v2 (chosen to span 24% / 79% / 99% truncation at 8K). Truncation handling: standard zero reward, loss masking, DAPO soft overlength penalty (0 → −1 over the last 512 tokens), forced-answer training (close the truncated reasoning, force an answer prefix, allow ≤120 extra tokens). Normalization: Dr.GRPO (divide by fixed budget B instead of |o_i|). Anchoring: KL β=0.001 and β=0.01. SFT controls: rejection fine-tuning on (i) self-generated traces that complete naturally within 4K, and (ii) 4K-truncated + budget-forced traces whose forced answer is correct — matched in length and optimization steps.

## Results & Highlights

- **The headline crossover (AIME avg@3, Qwen3.5-4B).** The 4k-trained checkpoint scores **35.93% at 4K vs 1.11% for base (+34.8 pp)**, but **48.15% at 64K vs 78.33% for base (−30.2 pp)**, and its accuracy is already flat from 8K (44.26%) to 64K (48.15%) while mean output length saturates at **4.97k tokens vs 27.32k for base**. The 8k-trained checkpoint: **50.19% at 8K vs 7.78% (+42.4 pp)**, **53.70% at 64K vs 78.33% (−24.6 pp)**. Note the cap also helps *within* budget: at 2K the 8k-trained model gets 1.30% whereas the 4k-trained gets 17.22% — the learned budget is matched to its training cap, not simply shortened.
- **Same pattern in two other model families (AIME avg@3, largest budget in Fig. 1).** Gemma4-E2B 1k-trained: **10.93% at 1K vs 0.00% base (+10.9 pp) → 16.30% at 16K vs 41.11% (−24.8 pp)**, mean length **1.05k vs 6.85k**. Gemma 2k-trained: 15.93% vs 1.11% (+14.8 pp) → 20.56% vs 41.11% (−20.6 pp). Nemotron-3-Nano-4B 1k-trained: **26.67% at 1K vs 3.33% (+23.3 pp) → 34.07% at 16K vs 59.44% (−25.4 pp)**, length **1.01k vs 6.11k**; 2k-trained: 35.93% vs 23.33% (+12.6 pp) → 46.30% vs 59.44% (−13.1 pp). All 18 Qwen DeepScaleR runs reproduce the crossover under both checkpoint-selection rules.
- **Not a math-training artifact.** Switching the training set to Reasoning Gym: Qwen 4k-trained goes **31.11% at 4K vs 0.00% base (+31.1 pp) → 50.56% at 64K vs 75.56% (−25.0 pp)**; Gemma 2k-trained 22.78% vs 1.11% (+21.7 pp) → 24.44% vs 39.44% (−15.0 pp). Nemotron RG 2k is the interesting weak case: **essentially no in-budget gain (23.89% vs 23.33%, +0.6 pp)** yet still **−10.6 pp at 16K (49.44% vs 60.00%)** — internalization can occur without the low-budget benefit.
- **Transfer is real but much milder, and it is an efficiency win.** Qwen 8k-trained at 64K: **GPQA-Diamond −2.2 pp** with mean length **4.56k vs 11.15k** tokens; **LiveCodeBench v6 −1.0 pp** with **8.31k vs 38.32k** tokens. Qwen 4k-trained is worse on both (GPQA 64K −9.1 pp, LCB-v6 64K −9.0 pp); Gemma 1k-trained is GPQA 16K −1.0 pp / LCB 16K −8.2 pp; Nemotron 2k-trained is **GPQA 64K +4.0 pp** and LCB 64K −4.2 pp. On these benchmarks the trained model often reaches comparable accuracy at a third to a fifth of the tokens.
- **Budget forcing the base model recovers a lot at small budgets.** At 4K total allowance, forcing an answer lifts base AIME from **0.56% to 9.44% (+8.9 pp)**, GPQA-Diamond from **4.04% to 60.27% (+56.2 pp)** (base truncation at 4K is 95.8%), and LCB-v6 from **5.33% to 19.81% (+14.5 pp)**. So most of the base model's apparent low-budget collapse is overthinking, not incapacity — but not all of it: the 4k-trained model's 35.93% at 4K still beats the forced base's 9.44%.
- **Forcing the trained model upward does not restore scaling.** Continuing the 4k-trained checkpoint spends **+2.58–2.74k tokens** at 64K yet yields **AIME 0.0 pp, GPQA +2.7 pp, LCB-v6 +5.0 pp**; at the 4K in-budget point it destroys accuracy (**AIME −36.1 pp, GPQA −51.0 pp, LCB −5.1 pp**). This is the paper's strongest evidence that the loss is genuine compression of the learned solution path, not just an early-stopping reflex.
- **Ablations (AIME avg@16, base 64K = 78.8±8.5).** Standard GRPO 8K: **50.4±10.4 (8K) → 56.5±10.0 (64K)**. GSM8K data: 20.7 → **72.0** (much smaller in-budget gain, small deficit). Nemotron-RL-Math-v2: 43.3 → 54.6. Loss masking 38.3 → 59.7; **DAPO 44.2 → 50.0 (worst high-budget)**; forced answer 7.0 → **77.2** (deficit essentially gone, gain gone); Dr.GRPO 48.4 → 55.6 (i.e. no help); KL β=0.001 47.1 → 56.2, **KL β=0.01 32.4 → 70.1** (best trade-off, still −8.7 pp); **RFT on naturally short traces 7.8 → 83.0** (exceeds base at 64K!); **RFT on budget-forced truncated traces 18.5 → 40.4 (−38.4 pp)**.
- **Truncation is the binding variable.** Dataset stats (Qwen3.5-4B, thinking mode): GSM8K 92% pass@64K / 24% truncation at 8K / median 3,744 tokens; DeepScaleR 77% / 79% / 19,056; Nemotron-RL-Math-v2 31% / 99% / 60,879. In-budget gain and high-budget damage both rise with truncation exposure, and the relationship is monotone across the three datasets.

## Authors & Credibility

JetBrains Research, with the first author also at Czech Technical University in Prague; five authors, small team, no prior heavyweight RL-scaling track record visible from the paper. Credibility signals here are mostly *procedural* rather than reputational, and they are strong: 3 training seeds × 2 checkpoint-selection rules for the main six conditions (18 runs), per-seed accuracy/length/truncation tables in Appendix I, released checkpoints for every run including ablations, an explicit AI-use statement, a reproducibility statement, and unusually careful accounting of the budget-forcing protocol (model-specific chat templates, answer windows of 120/16/1024 tokens per task, saved continuation rescoring). The paper also reports its own negative results and an explicit "no intervention wins" conclusion. Against that: the work is a single-group effort on 4–5B models, most auxiliary runs are single-seed, and one base-model measurement (LiveCodeBench token counts) carries a reported API-error bound rather than a clean number.

## Critical Assessment

**Genuine strengths first.** The phenomenon is clean, the name is good, and the experimental design is better than most "X hurts Y" papers: the accuracy-and-length curve sweep is the right instrument, the 18-run multi-seed replication on the headline condition is the right robustness check, and the *SFT dissociation* (truncated traces → collapse, naturally short traces → no collapse) is the kind of control that actually moves a causal claim. Releasing every checkpoint including ablations is rare and should be the norm.

**But the headline in-budget deltas are inflated by an unfair baseline.** The +34.8 pp figure compares the trained model against *ordinary* base decoding at 4K, where the Qwen base truncates **100%** of AIME responses (Table 2) and scores 1.11%. The paper's own budget-forcing control (Table 7) shows the base reaches **9.44%** at a matched 4,216-token total allowance. The honest low-budget gain is therefore ≈**+26.5 pp**, not +34.8; at 8K it is 50.19 vs 21.67 (**+28.5 pp**, not +42.4). The same correction shrinks the Gemma and Nemotron headline numbers. The qualitative conclusion survives — the trained model still beats a fairly-budgeted base — but the abstract's numbers overstate the effect by roughly a third, and the paper never restates its headline deltas against the forced-answer baseline even though it has the data.

**A second, softer issue: the deficit is partly definitional on AIME.** The base model at 64K averages 27.32k tokens and 78.33% accuracy; the trained model averages 4.97k and 48.15%. One could describe this as "the trained model cannot use tokens" or as "the trained model answers 5.5× faster and is 30 points worse" — which is just the accuracy–compute frontier. What makes the paper's framing more than accounting is the forced-up experiment (more tokens, no accuracy), and that experiment is its weakest link methodologically: the intervention is to append "Wait" up to four times, which *costs* 36–51 points at the in-budget point and is a crude, off-distribution perturbation. A cleaner test would sample from the trained policy under a minimum-length constraint or distill long traces from the base model into the trained one. As it stands, "genuine compression" is well-supported but not airtight.

**The dataset ablation confounds truncation with difficulty.** GSM8K, DeepScaleR and Nemotron-RL-Math-v2 differ in truncation rate (24/79/99%) but also in pass rate (92/77/31%) and median length (3.7k/19k/61k). The paper reads the monotone trend as evidence for truncation exposure, which is plausible, but an equally consistent story is "harder data → larger gradient toward brevity" or "harder data → lower-entropy successful paths." A fixed-difficulty reweighting (subsample DeepScaleR to a 24% truncation rate at 8K and re-run) is the missing ablation, and it is cheap.

**Missing cap-scaling control.** The natural prediction of the internalization story is that the crossover should track the cap. The paper shows 4k vs 8k for Qwen (−30.2 → −24.6 pp) and 1k vs 2k for the others (−24.8 → −20.6; −25.4 → −13.1), which is directionally right, but it never trains at 16k or 32k where the curve should flatten out into the base model's own operating range. Without that, "the model internalizes *its* budget" is supported only over a 2× range at the low end.

**Statistical and bookkeeping nits that a reviewer would flag.**
- Base models are evaluated **once** while trained values average three seeds, and the AIME confidence intervals in Table 3 are wide (GRPO at 64K: 56.5±10.0; base 78.8±8.5). The main AIME gap is far outside noise, but the GPQA/LCB transfer deltas of ±1–4 pp are single-seed on both sides and should not be read as precise.
- The intro's aggregate ranges (**+11.7 to +46.7 pp** in-budget, **−17.2 to −35.0 pp** at the largest budget) do not reconcile with the per-checkpoint seed means in Table 8: the largest gain there is +42.4 pp and Nemotron's 2k-trained deficit at 16K is −13.1 pp. Presumably these come from per-seed extremes, but the paper never says so, and no table reproduces them.
- Cross-table inconsistencies are unexplained: Gemma4-E2B base AIME at 4K is 16.11% in Table 8 but 17.22% in Table 13. Small, but in a paper whose entire argument is about a few points of high-budget difference, evaluation-set drift matters.
- LiveCodeBench base token counts are reported as *ranges* "bounding two API-error attempts with missing usage" (Table 7 note) — the only place in the paper where a headline number is a bound rather than a measurement.

**Scope.** 4–5B models only, GRPO only (the paper acknowledges both), math-heavy training, and a behavioral definition of the phenomenon with no probing of whether the model represents the cap explicitly. The SFT control is the strongest evidence for the mechanism, but it is one model, one seed, one budget (4K).

## Strengths

- A clean, well-named, genuinely useful phenomenon, measured with the right instrument (joint accuracy–length curves across a 32× budget range) rather than a single evaluation point.
- Breadth where it counts: three model families, two structurally different training distributions (competition math vs. 104 procedural environments), two training caps each, and two out-of-domain benchmarks.
- The SFT dissociation (naturally short vs. budget-forced truncated traces) is a decisive and elegant control that reframes the effect away from "RL objective bug" and toward "length-censored post-training."
- The forced-answer and forced-up interventions directly attack the obvious alternative explanations (early stopping / overthinking), and the paper reports their costs honestly instead of hiding the accuracy loss.
- Unusually complete artifact release: all checkpoints including ablations, per-seed tables, truncation curves, full prompt templates.
- Intellectual honesty in the conclusion: the effect is framed as a trade-off, no intervention is claimed to solve it, and the practical recommendation (report accuracy and length at several budgets) follows directly from the evidence.

## Limitations

- Small models only (4–5B active parameters); whether frontier-scale models with explicit reasoning-effort controls internalize budgets the same way is untested.
- No training-cap sweep into the regime where the cap stops binding (16k/32k/64k), so the "budget-matched" claim rests on a narrow 1k–8k range.
- The truncation-vs-difficulty confound in the dataset ablation is unresolved.
- Most auxiliary experiments (Reasoning Gym, transfer, all ablations) are single-seed; the multi-seed replication covers only DeepScaleR + AIME + Qwen/Gemma/Nemotron headline curves.
- The upward-forcing intervention is coarse and damages in-budget behavior, so "the model cannot benefit from more tokens" is inferred rather than demonstrated with an in-distribution intervention.
- Purely behavioral: no mechanistic account of *how* the budget is represented, no analysis of whether early EOS arises from a decision token, entropy collapse, or distribution shift in the reasoning prefix.
- The efficiency upside is asserted from accuracy–length curves but never turned into a deployment-relevant comparison (e.g., cost-normalized accuracy or a Pareto frontier against the base model).

## Relevance

这篇直接落在"RL Post-Training Dynamics & Failures"：它把 RLVR 的一个常规工程参数（rollout 上限）识别为一种训练动力学故障源，并给出可复现的跨模型证据与消融路径，对任何做 GRPO/RLVR 后训练的人都有一条可操作的检查项——评测必须扫多个预算，不能只报训练上限处的分数。其"截断轨迹如何打分决定模型症状"的结论，也与奖励设计、长度偏置和 reward hacking 的失效分析高度相关。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. Does the effect scale? At 30B+ with an explicit reasoning-effort token (Qwen-style thinking budget), is the learned constraint keyed to the *numeric* cap, the *distribution* of successful lengths, or the presence of an explicit budget signal in the prompt?
2. What happens if training mixes caps (e.g., 4k/8k/16k rollouts in the same batch, or a curriculum that increases the cap)? Does the accuracy–length curve become the envelope of the caps, or does the *smallest* cap dominate?
3. Can the trade-off be broken by retaining long successful rollouts (importance-weighted replay of the rare >cap successes) rather than accepting the truncation penalty? The paper flags this as future work but does not test even a simple version.
4. Is there a cheap diagnostic that predicts internalization *before* the 300-step run finishes — e.g., the fraction of gradient mass contributed by truncated rollouts, or the shift in the successful-length distribution during training?
5. Why does Nemotron's Reasoning Gym run gain almost nothing in-budget (+0.6 pp) yet still lose 10.6 pp at 16K? That dissociation is the cleanest natural experiment in the paper and is left unexplained.
6. How does budget internalization interact with entropy collapse and with the known "thinking length grows during RL" phenomenon — is internalization the *late* phase of the same length dynamics, and does it correlate with the point where mean rollout length stops increasing?
