> **一句话总结：** 作者发现 4-bit 世界动作模型（WAM）剩余的动作误差主要来自被压缩的"预测未来"潜变量，于是提出 LAQ——用 reader 度量（未来误差对动作的影响）加 read count（同一份舍入误差被多少去噪步读取）给出激活与 KV cache 的闭式精度分配阈值，并对动作注意力关注的 token 做第二次 4-bit 残差编码，在 LIBERO-Plus 上让 Fast-WAM 达到 76.4%（BF16 77.6%，最强基线 SVDQuant 73.0%），模型压缩 3.8×、峰值显存降至 2.3 分之一、推理快 1.66×。

> **TL;DR:** LAQ is a post-training W4A4KV4 quantizer for world action models that targets the *predicted future latent* rather than the action: a Gauss–Newton "reader metric" (estimated with Rademacher probes on the reader alone) plus a "read count" that weights frozen rounding errors by how many denoising steps reuse them yield a closed-form threshold for allocating bits across activations and KV caches, followed by a second 4-bit residual pass on gaze-selected tokens. On LIBERO-Plus, Fast-WAM reaches 76.4% mean success vs 77.6% BF16 and 73.0% for SVDQuant (+3.4 pts, and SVDQuant keeps its KV cache in BF16), Cosmos-Policy reaches 83.3% vs 83.5% BF16, and on a real AgileX PiPER arm it gets 47.0% vs 52.0% BF16 and 17.0% QuaRot (+30.0 pts), with 3.8× weight compression and 1.66× lower latency.

## 核心贡献

- **诊断出被忽视的量化目标：预测未来潜变量。** 一旦把动作通路保护到高精度，4-bit WAM 的动作误差只下降 16%，而把预测未来换回 BF16 可以消除剩余动作误差的 71.2%——说明误差是经由"被压坏的未来"进入动作的，而已有 WAM 量化器（Q-WAM、SteerQuant、QuantWAMs）都只盯动作或逐步敏感度。
- **提出 reader 度量 M 与 read count n_u。** M = E_c[J_c^T D_c J_c] 是 reader 误差的 Gauss–Newton 矩阵，把未来误差换算成动作变化；n_u 是同一份舍入误差被多少个去噪步重复读取（冻结误差 vs 逐步重抽误差的能量比，取值 [0,T]）。二者结合使激活与 KV cache 的精度分配有闭式解：单一阈值 μ 决定哪些单元值得第二次 4-bit 编码。
- **gaze-guided refinement（注视引导精修）。** 动作 token 对历史 token 的注意力天然给出"reader 在看哪里"，被选中的 token 在同一个 4-bit 算术内做第二次残差量化，误差上界从 ς₁/2 收紧到 ς₁/28（方差降至少 196×），权重全部保持 4 bit，没有 16-bit 分支、没有逐任务重标定。
- **完整的仿真 + 真机验证与系统性消融。** 两个 WAM（Fast-WAM 6B、Cosmos-Policy 2B）在 LIBERO-Plus / LIBERO 上评测，并在 AgileX PiPER 六自由度机械臂上做三个真实任务；消融分别拆掉 reader 度量、read count、运行时 gaze 和 cache 量化，逐项量化其贡献。

## Key Contributions

- **Identifies future-latent fidelity as the missing quantization target for WAMs.** With the action pathway protected, only 16% of the action error is removed; swapping in the BF16-predicted future removes 71.2% of the remaining error (Table 1 / Fig. 1). Existing WAM quantizers estimate sensitivity to the final action one denoising step at a time and never protect the future or the caches through which it is read.
- **A reader metric that prices future error by its effect on the action.** M is the context-averaged Gauss–Newton matrix of the reader error K = KL(π(R(z*))‖π(R(ẑ))), estimated with Rademacher/Hutchinson probes through the reader only (no Jacobian materialization). It reduces to the uniform future error when M = I; the paper keeps the leading r eigendirections plus the residual diagonal and mixes in αI for stability.
- **A read count that captures error accumulation across denoising steps.** n_u = 1ᵀΩ_u1 / tr Ω_u, where Ω_u(s,s′) = E tr[J_{u,s}ᵀ M J_{u,s′}]. Per-step sensitivity methods implicitly set n_u = 1 for every source, which the authors show is 2.5× too optimistic when ten video denoising steps are quantized together.
- **Closed-form joint allocation over activations and caches.** Minimizing L = E_c[A + λ_M Z_M + βK] under a cost budget Σ c_u r_u ≤ B yields the threshold rule r_u = 1 ⇔ n_u ω_u σ_u² / c_u ≥ μ, with ω_{s,j} factored as ϕ_s g_j (step profile × gaze), so one currency trades precision between the denoising loop and the KV cache.
- **Gaze-guided refinement entirely inside 4-bit arithmetic.** Selected activations are re-quantized as x̂ = ς₁q₁ + ς₂q₂ (two 4-bit matmuls, the second only over selected rows); selected cache entries store a second 4-bit code. The gaze is read off the action tokens' attention at run time, so offline quantities need no per-task recalibration.

## Methodology

**Problem setup.** A WAM maps observation o and instruction ℓ to a predicted future latent z = G_W(o,ℓ) and an action chunk a = R_W(o,ℓ,z), where R is called the *reader*. Quantization to W4A4KV4 stores weights, per-token activations, and cached keys/values at 4 bits; the KV cache holds tokens computed once per control step (observed frame, text) that attention re-reads at every denoising step.

**Three motivating observations on Fast-WAM.** (1) Protecting the action pathway (keeping action-token activations in higher precision) cuts the action error A only from 3.53e-4 to 2.96e-4 (−16%) and leaves the future error Z = 0.0188 untouched, while giving the quantized reader the BF16 future drops A to 0.85e-4. (2) Errors differ in how often they are read: an injected error of fixed energy is 3.1× (observation tokens), 2.2× (observed-frame cache) and 2.1× (text cache) more damaging when frozen across steps than when redrawn, and quantizing all 10 video steps jointly is 2.50× worse than the sum of 10 one-at-a-time quantizations. (3) The reader depends on a narrow, location-stable but direction-unstable part of the future: in a 37,632-dim latent, M has effective rank 41.5, the first predicted frame carries 98% of its trace, the diagonal of M correlates at 0.86 across disjoint calibration halves, but the leading 32 eigenvectors of the two halves overlap by only 0.19.

**Reader metric.** For calibration context c, J_c = ∂R/∂z at z = z*_c and D_c the diagonal curvature of K at a*_c. Then M = E_c[J_cᵀ D_c J_c] and Z_M = (ẑ−z*)ᵀM(ẑ−z*)/‖z*‖². Probing uses v = J_cᵀ(D_c^{1/2}ϵ) for Rademacher ϵ, one reverse pass per context; each context is normalized to unit trace before averaging, and M = (1−α)(d/tr M_r)M_r + αI.

**Read count.** With e_{u,s} the error entering source u at step s and J_{u,s} = ∂z/∂e_{u,s}, Ω_u(s,s′) = E tr[J_{u,s}ᵀ M J_{u,s′}]; a fresh error contributes σ_u² tr Ω_u and a frozen one σ_u² 1ᵀΩ_u1, so n_u is their ratio. n_u is measured by *intervention* — injecting fixed-energy errors frozen vs redrawn and taking the ratio — rather than differentiating through the sampler, and sources of the same kind (each cache, static tokens, tokens being denoised) share one count, so a few dozen forward passes suffice.

**Joint allocation.** Minimizing L ≈ C_W + Σ_u n_u ω_u σ_u²(r_u) under Σ c_u r_u ≤ B gives the threshold μ; refining a unit divides σ_u² by ≥196 at cost c_u of one extra 4-bit row (activation) or one extra 4-bit code (cache entry). In practice the budget refines 15.4% of activation values in the denoising loop and 6.0% of cached keys/values.

**Gaze-guided refinement.** Sensitivity is factored ω_{s,j} = ϕ_s g_j, where ϕ_s is a step profile and g_j the gaze. Offline, g_j = tr(M_jj) over token j's block; at run time the attention the action tokens pay to token j (summed over layers, heads, action denoising steps) gives it for free, and because consecutive control steps see nearly the same scene the previous step's gaze selects the tokens refined next. Selected activations take a second residual code with ς₂ = max|x − ς₁q₁|/7 ≤ ς₁/14, giving a two-term reconstruction x̂ = ς₁q₁ + ς₂q₂ and a layer output ς₁Q4(W)q₁ + ς₂Q4(W)q₂. No weight is stored above 4 bits and there is no out-of-loop branch. Calibration costs 1.2 GPU-hours at 27 GB peak.

## Results & Highlights

- **Fast-WAM on LIBERO-Plus: 76.4% mean success vs 77.6% BF16 (−1.2 pts) and 73.0% for SVDQuant (+3.4 pts)**, even though every baseline keeps its KV cache in BF16 while LAQ runs at 4.01 bits/weight, 4.62 bits/activation and 4.24 bits/KV. The other generic quantizers land at 72.0–72.7% (QuaRot 72.1, SpinQuant 72.0, ViDiT-Q 72.7), i.e. the baselines lose 4.6–5.6 points to BF16 while LAQ loses 1.2.
- **Fast-WAM per-suite breakdown shows where the +3.4 points come from:** LAQ takes Spatial at 82.4% (above BF16's 81.4, best baseline 79.5 SpinQuant) and Object at 81.9% (vs 74.1–77.6 for all baselines, 82.9 BF16), and is the clear winner on Long at 77.1% against 72.2–72.9% for every quantizer (BF16 78.6). The one regression is **Goal: 64.3% vs 68.2% SVDQuant and 67.6% BF16** — better only than QuaRot (61.4) and SpinQuant (59.5) — so the average is carried by three suites while one sits below the strongest baseline by 3.9 points.
- **Cosmos-Policy on LIBERO-Plus: 83.3% vs 83.5% BF16 (−0.2, essentially noise) and 82.9% for both ViDiT-Q and SVDQuant (+0.4 pts).** Per-suite the same pattern repeats: 86.7 Spatial (best, above BF16's 84.3), 89.5 Object (best, tied with BF16), 88.6 Long (best, +1.9 over BF16's 86.7 and +1.0 over ViDiT-Q), but Goal 68.6% — fourth of five quantizers, below SVDQuant 72.9, ViDiT-Q 70.0, QuaRot 69.5 and BF16 73.3.
- **On the original LIBERO suites both models are essentially saturated and LAQ is at parity:** Fast-WAM 98.2% vs 98.0% BF16 (all baselines 97.8–98.1), Cosmos-Policy 98.7% vs 98.8% BF16 (SVDQuant 98.8). The authors themselves argue this benchmark is uninformative ("both BF16 models already succeed in about 98%"), which is why LIBERO-Plus is the main evaluation — a fair call, but it also means the parity claim here is weak evidence.
- **Real AgileX PiPER arm, 25 trials per task per method: 47.0% average success vs 52.0% BF16 and 17.0% QuaRot (+30.0 pts).** Per task: pick-and-place 15/25 first-object and 4/25 both vs 4/25 and 2/25 for QuaRot; shelf placement 13/25 vs 3/25 (BF16 15/25); box stacking 15/25 vs 8/25 (BF16 16/25). This is the paper's most convincing result because the failure of QuaRot (17.0%) is too large to be noise even at n=25.
- **Efficiency on Fast-WAM (A100, Tab. 6): weights 12.0 GB → 3.2 GB (3.8×), peak policy memory 14.0 GB → 6.0 GB (2.3×), KV cache of one call 204 MB → 56 MB (3.6×), latency 1475 ms → 888 ms (1.66×).** The speedup is real rather than paper-only because the runtime multiplies packed 4-bit codes on integer tensor cores and the second pass is just extra rows of the same product.
- **Ablations (Fast-WAM, LIBERO-Plus): plain W4A4KV4 71.2%, action tokens kept in BF16 only 71.9% (−0.7 recovered of the 6.4-point gap), refining observation tokens 74.3%, full LAQ 76.4%.** With the same budget, random token selection 72.5%, per-step action sensitivity (the criterion existing WAM quantizers use) 72.9%, offline gaze only 75.2%. Removing the reader metric (M = I) costs 2.8 pts (76.4 → 73.6), setting n_u = 1 costs 2.4 pts (→ 74.0), and keeping caches in BF16 gains only 0.4 pts (→ 76.8), which is the fairest apples-to-apples comparison with the baselines.

## Authors & Credibility

Six authors: Yichen Guo, Yanlong Chen, Xinyue Zhang, Mohan Liu (Nanyang Technological University) and Song Zhang (Nanjing University), with Yawei Li (NTU) as corresponding author. The group has a visible, coherent quantization line — PrismQuant (null-space rotations for grouped quantizers), Gated Relational Alignment for efficient VLMs, and PrismQuant-adjacent rotation work all appear in the reference list as their own prior papers — so the second-order-rounding + rotation machinery reused here (GPTQ weights in randomized Hadamard coordinates) is well within their expertise. The paper is written with unusual candor for a quantization paper: the motivating observations are reported as measured numbers (16%, 71.2%, 3.1×/2.2×/2.1×, 2.50×), the ablation includes the "same budget, wrong criterion" controls that most such papers omit, and the per-suite regressions (the Goal suite for both models) are visible in the main table rather than buried. Novelty relative to Q-WAM/SteerQuant/QuantWAMs is clearly delineated (target the future, not the action; count reads, not steps). The main credibility caveat is that all three closest WAM-specific competitors are *not* run — the authors state they have no public implementation — so the empirical case rests on generic LLM/diffusion quantizers as stand-ins.

## Critical Assessment

**Genuine strengths first.** The diagnosis is the paper's real contribution and it is unusually well evidenced: the future-swap experiment (Table 1) isolates the causal path cleanly — protecting the action pathway buys 16% of the error with Z unchanged, while handing the quantized reader the BF16 future buys 71.2% and drives Z to zero — and the frozen-vs-redrawn intervention (3.1×/2.2×/2.1×) is a direct measurement of the mechanism the method then exploits. The read-count formalism (Ω_u off-diagonal, n_u = 1ᵀΩ_u1/tr Ω_u) is the correct generalization of per-step sensitivity, and the 2.50× gap between joint and one-at-a-time quantization of the ten video steps is exactly the kind of number that shows why the previous criterion was under-specified. The closed-form threshold allocation is a clean, cheap consequence rather than a bolted-on heuristic, and the fact that gaze comes free from the action tokens' attention (no extra forward pass) is a genuinely elegant engineering choice. The 4-bit-only constraint is honored: 4.01 bits/weight average, no 16-bit branch, second pass is extra rows of the same integer matmul.

**Now the things that would not survive a hard review.** (1) *The Goal suite is a consistent, unexplained weak spot.* On Fast-WAM, LIBERO-Plus Goal is 64.3% for LAQ against 68.2% for SVDQuant and 67.6% for BF16; on Cosmos-Policy it is 68.6% against 72.9% SVDQuant, 70.0% ViDiT-Q and 69.5% QuaRot. This is the one perturbation where a method premised on preserving the predicted future should be strongest — a different goal implies a different future — yet it is the only suite where LAQ loses to a 4-bit baseline in both models, and the text instead reports that "the gains of LAQ concentrate on the camera-viewpoint and sensor-noise perturbations" without ever naming the losing suite. The +3.4 aggregate on Fast-WAM decomposes as +5.0 Spatial, +7.8 Object, +4.9 Long and −3.9 Goal against SVDQuant, so the headline conceals a systematic failure mode rather than a rounding fluctuation. (2) *The strongest baselines are missing.* QuantWAMs, Q-WAM and SteerQuant are named as the WAM-specific competitors and dismissed as unimplemented, which is understandable, but it means the claim "all above the baselines with 16-bit KV caches" is measured against QuaRot/SpinQuant/ViDiT-Q/SVDQuant — LLM and diffusion quantizers that were never designed for this model class. The most relevant comparison, per-step action-sensitivity selection at the same budget, is only run as an internal ablation (72.9%), not as an external method. (3) *The real-robot evidence is thin in exactly the way it matters.* 25 trials per task per method is 100 trials total; at p ≈ 0.5 the standard error is ~10 points, so 47.0% vs 52.0% is statistically indistinguishable from BF16 — a fine outcome for a compression paper, but the paper's framing ("carry over from simulation") overstates what n=25 supports. QuaRot is the only real-robot baseline; SVDQuant, the strongest simulated baseline, is never deployed. (4) *The efficiency accounting flatters the method.* 3.8× compression is quoted on the denoiser weights (12.0 → 3.2 GB) while activations average 4.62 bits and the 15.4%/6.0% refined-token budget adds real matmul work whose cost is dismissed as "one more row" — no end-to-end FLOP or energy accounting is given, and the 1.66× latency is measured on an A100, not on the Jetson Orin NX the introduction motivates. The 14 GB → 6 GB peak memory claim also sits awkwardly next to the stated 27 GB calibration peak. (5) *"No per-task recalibration" is narrower than it sounds.* The reader metric M, the read counts n_u, the step profile ϕ_s and the threshold μ are all re-estimated whenever the model is fine-tuned — on the real arm the authors explicitly "re-estimate the reader metric on it" after teleoperation fine-tuning — so the claim is per-*task within a fixed checkpoint*, not zero-shot transfer to a new policy, and calibration still costs 1.2 GPU-hours. (6) *No variance reporting anywhere.* Every simulation number is a single number without seeds, confidence intervals, or episode counts; the 83.3 vs 83.5 Cosmos result and the 98.2 vs 98.0 LIBERO result are presented as wins/parity at a precision the experimental design cannot support. (7) *The gaze's run-time feedback loop is untested for stability.* The scheme refines tokens selected by the *previous* control step's attention; the justification is that "consecutive control steps see nearly the same scene," which is an assumption with no measured violation rate, no analysis of what happens after a fast camera move or a grasp event, and no fallback when the gaze is wrong. The 1.2-point gain from run-time over offline gaze (75.2 → 76.4) is small enough that this risk buys little.

## Strengths

- The core diagnosis — that a 4-bit WAM's residual action error flows through a degraded predicted future — is established causally (future swap, 71.2% of the error) rather than asserted, and it correctly predicts why action-pathway protection saturates at 16%.
- The read count is a real conceptual advance over per-step sensitivity: it turns "this error is reused" into a measurable scalar with a closed-form role in the allocation objective, and the joint-vs-sequential 2.50× experiment validates the premise directly.
- The allocation is closed-form, single-threshold, and shared across activations and caches — no per-layer search, no mixed-precision combinatorial optimization, no retraining.
- Gaze is obtained for free from attention the model already computes, so the second pass adds rows to an existing 4-bit matmul instead of a parallel high-precision branch; the 4.01 bits/weight figure is honest accounting.
- The ablation matrix is unusually complete: it includes the "same budget, wrong selection criterion" controls (random tokens, per-step sensitivity, offline gaze, activation-energy and self-attention criteria from STaMP/SemanticDialect/Squat) and component removals (M = I, n_u = 1, BF16 caches), which is what makes the 76.4% number interpretable.
- Real-robot deployment on an AgileX PiPER arm with three tasks, plus a 30-point gap over QuaRot, is far more than most quantization papers attempt.
- Honest reporting of the compute cost of calibration (1.2 GPU-hours, 27 GB peak) and of the fact that the original LIBERO suites are saturated.

## Limitations

- Goal-perturbation performance is the one systematically weak point (64.3% vs 68.2% SVDQuant on Fast-WAM, 68.6% vs 72.9% on Cosmos-Policy) and the paper offers no analysis of why, even though goal changes are precisely where future fidelity should matter most.
- No comparison against the three WAM-specific quantizers (Q-WAM, SteerQuant, QuantWAMs) that the related-work section positions as the direct competitors; all external baselines are generic LLM/diffusion quantizers.
- Real-world evaluation is 25 trials per task per method, with only QuaRot as a baseline and no confidence intervals; the 5-point gap to BF16 is within sampling noise.
- No seeds, error bars, or episode counts for any simulation result; several headline deltas (0.2–0.4 points) are below plausible run-to-run variance.
- The reader metric assumes the reader is differentiable in z and that a single calibration set of 80 LIBERO contexts transfers; the 0.19 eigenvector overlap across calibration halves is reported as a motivation for token-level protection but also implies the direction estimate itself is nearly unusable — the αI mixing is a patch, not a solution.
- Efficiency claims are measured on an A100 with no Jetson/edge deployment numbers, no FLOP accounting for the second pass, and no energy measurement, despite the introduction motivating exactly the 16 GB Orin NX setting.
- The gaze feedback loop's stability under fast scene change, occlusion, or grasp transitions is never measured; the run-time gain over a static offline gaze is only 1.2 points.
- No analysis of failure modes on the real robot (what does a 4-bit policy do when it fails — wrong object, no grasp, collision?), which matters for a deployment claim.

## Relevance

与"高效推理 / 边缘部署 / 机器人基础模型压缩"方向高度相关：它把量化误差分析从"层输出"推进到"任务相关的中间表征"，其中的 reader 度量 + read count 思路可直接迁移到 VLA、视频生成、世界模型等其他"中间潜变量决定下游输出"的架构。对关注 4-bit 推理、KV cache 压缩和机器人端侧部署的读者是必读。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Why does the Goal suite behave differently from Spatial/Object/Long (68.6% for Cosmos-Policy vs 72.9% SVDQuant, 64.3% for Fast-WAM vs 68.2%)? A goal change alters the *predicted future* rather than the observation, so a future-aware quantizer should win there; is the loss a scheduling artifact of the fixed denoising steps, or does the reader metric mis-price goal-relevant latent directions?
- What happens if the gaze feedback selects the wrong tokens for several consecutive control steps (fast camera motion, occlusion, grasp)? Is there a measurable degradation or a self-correcting effect?
- How much of the gain survives if the 15.4%/6.0% refinement budget is reduced, and is the threshold μ stable across tasks or does it need retuning per embodiment?
- Would combining LAQ with a WAM-specific method (SteerQuant's action-guided scaling, or Q-WAM's action-subspace protection) compound or overlap? The ablation suggests action-pathway protection is nearly orthogonal (it recovers only 0.7 of 6.4 points alone), which is a testable prediction.
- Can the reader metric be estimated without a differentiable reader — e.g. from finite differences on the action output — so the method applies to black-box or flow-matching policies with non-smooth sampling?
- Does the second 4-bit residual pass compose with lower-bit caches (2-bit, as in QuantWM), or does the ς₁/28 error bound break down once the first code is coarser?
