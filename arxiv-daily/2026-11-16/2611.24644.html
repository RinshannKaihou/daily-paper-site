Equal Steps, Unequal Curvature: Where Muon's Update Goes Wrong

Headline metric

−0.0158nats

Final held-out loss, 124M model: 3.9176 → 3.9018 with split QKV + NorMuon + cooldown tilt — about 4.3% fewer tokens. At 355M: −0.0188 nats.

Why this matters

A held-out curvature probe tells you whether an optimizer mis-allocates step size — a diagnostic you can run during pretraining, not after.

Critical edge

The headline 38–190× multiplier ratio is not the paper's own fixed-norm criterion — and the recipe costs +12% wall time for ~4.3% token savings.

Methodology

A curvature lab around one optimizer

  1. Setup. 124.5M pre-norm decoder (12 layers, width 768, 12 heads, tied embeddings) on FineWeb-Edu, 24,576 tokens/step. Muon drives 48 hidden matrices; AdamW (3e−4) handles embeddings and norms. Screens run 2,048 steps, confirmations 4,096 (100.7M tokens); a 355M model tests scale.
  2. Probe. At steps 1,024 / 1,536 / 2,048, take a full SVD per matrix and cut the next step into 6 singular-value bands × 6 neuron sextiles. On three disjoint held-out sets, estimate gb and the full Hbb′ (cross-terms included) with 30 Hessian–vector products per checkpoint; cb = gb/Hbb.
  3. Split the gap. Theorem 3 separates the second-order gap into uniform step size and relative group weights; the decomposition is checked in Lean 4. Shape share is 17% / 12% / 97% at the three checkpoints. Theorem 4 gives the exact same-norm improvement test.
  4. Act. Orthogonalize Q, K, V separately; add a matmul-only σ−1/4 tilt during cooldown (degree-12 Chebyshev, Clenshaw recurrence, FP32). Every arm is Frobenius-norm matched per matrix per step, with interior-tuned LRs, paired seeds 42–44 and an A/A null.

Precision caution

TF32 + Horner evaluation of the Chebyshev fit (coefficients up to 4×10⁴) hits catastrophic cancellation and flips the filter's sign on the top direction: −0.0240 instead of −0.0322 with Clenshaw/FP32 (exact eigendecomposition: −0.0332).

Scale & cost

On wall-clock the recipe is currently a loss; folding the tilt into the Newton–Schulz coefficients (as DynMuon does) could flip the accounting. The tilt itself is matmul-only, rotation-equivariant by theorem, and costs 0.17 s/step on an A6000 during cooldown only.

Strengths

What holds up

  • Joint cross-group curvature — off-diagonal Hbb′ included — measured on a real 124M/355M training run, not a toy quadratic.
  • The fixed-norm criterion is a genuine conceptual fix; the Theorem A.27 counterexample shows the popular greedy gb/Hbb can point the wrong way.
  • Experimental hygiene far above the norm: per-step norm matching, interior-tuned LRs, paired seeds, an A/A null arm (≤0.009 nats), a gating rule, source hashes.
  • Rare honesty: negative results in full, the un-pre-registered anatomy admitted, the code-version confound disclosed, and a section on what Lean 4 did not buy.
  • Deployable tilt: matmul-only, rotation-equivariant by theorem, 0.17 s/step on an A6000 during cooldown only.

Limitations

What to discount

  • 124M / 355M parameters, ≤100.7M tokens (<1 token per parameter), one corpus, one tokenizer — regime-specific by the authors' own admission.
  • The wall-clock ledger is negative: +12% time against ~4.3% token savings.
  • The diagnostic is hidden-matrix-only, one-step and local, with H condition number ~10⁴; exact-polar band directions are an idealization and the finite-step certificate's constants are unmeasured.
  • Neuron analysis is diagnostic only; the QKV spectral-budget mechanism is explicitly an un-isolated hypothesis.
  • Three seeds, nominal t-intervals, no multiplicity correction across ~40 adaptively chosen arms; the anatomy was not pre-registered.
  • Tilt dose p = −1/4 is inherited from DynMuon and unoptimized; cooldown length and final LR were never tuned.

Open questions

What the next run should test

  1. Does the split-QKV gain — and the tilt's additivity — hold at ≥1B parameters and ≥10 tokens/parameter, or wash out near the token budget?
  2. Can the fixed-norm criterion (Theorem 4) become an online, covariance-free per-step filter — rank-2 or band-wise, estimated from a running H proxy — instead of an offline checkpoint diagnostic?
  3. What exactly is the QKV spectral-budget mechanism? Rescaling per-projection momentum norms, or fusing only two of the three projections, would separate “shared budget” from “different curvature per projection”.
  4. Why does provably equivariant input whitening lose 0.02–0.06 nats at fixed norm — purely the output-movement argument, or an interaction with the cooldown schedule?
  5. Would the tilt help in a constant-LR regime with no cooldown, and does its dose scale with model width?
  6. How much of the reported effect survives the source-version confound if the fused-QKV baselines are re-run on the split-QKV code version?
  7. Does this diagnostic explain why block-wise steps are more LR-tolerant (4.1840 at LR 0.007 vs Muon's tuned 4.2043), contradicting MuonBP's report that block-only training needs smaller rates at scale?
