01 Design

Two testbeds, one target, three ways to pick a direction

Activations and targets are held fixed across methods; only the fitting procedure changes. That isolates the pipeline — not the data, not the architecture — as the sole variable. The same OLS / ridge / DAS comparison is then repeated on a toy transformer and on a 7B LLM.

OLS

ordinary least squares · λ = 0

Least squares, unpenalized. Fits the readout as hard as it can, including noise.

Ridge

decode, then penalize

Fit on decodability over a log-spaced λ grid. Causal data is used only to pick λ, never to fit.

DAS

cause only

Trained on interchange interventions. The causal reference direction, not a decoder.

  • Mess3 HMM. A 4-layer, width-64 transformer on a 3-state HMM. Every sequence of length 1–8 is enumerated, so sample and population coincide and there is no train/test split to hide behind; probes are probability-weighted least squares on the final LayerNorm.
  • Llama-2-7B, birth year. Prompts of the form “{name} was born in 19”; target = the expected decade from a renormalized 10-digit readout. Layer 8, 4,096-d residual stream, n = 8,000 (2× the dimension), target = the person's true birth year.
  • Interventions that stay in distribution. In-simplex belief shifts on the HMM, natural-magnitude activation interchange on Llama — which sidesteps the out-of-distribution critique by construction.
  • What is measured. Probe MSE, intervention loss against ground-truth g, causal operativity, variance along the direction, probe mass per principal component, and principal angles to the DAS subspace.

Same activations, same target, same readout in every row. Best decoding sits with the worst causal effect; the causally faithful ridge directions are an order of magnitude worse decoders and 26–29° from DAS instead of ~85–90°.
Nothing at all

On Llama, the OLS probe's intervention loss of 13.7 is the number you get from not intervening. Ridge and DAS roughly halve it (7.07 and 6.42).

The test-split defense fails

On an 80/20 HMM split OLS still decodes at 4.3×10−4 vs 4.9×10−3 for faithful ridge — and stays inert. On Llama the split does flag it (0.05 → 0.38); the paper reports the asymmetry rather than smoothing it.

Not “ridge chases variance”: the intervention-optimal Llama direction has variance 3.83 and essentially ignores the top principal component, whose variance is 25,570 (~6,700× larger). Sampling noise alone supplies covariance of size σvσf/√n — amplified exactly where σv is small, since the OLS weight scales as σf/(σv√n).

That single direction has near-zero causal operativity. On both testbeds OLS mass concentrates where operativity is near zero, while ridge mass concentrates where it is high. The Llama PCs carrying 95% of ridge mass carry only ~31% of OLS mass.

A split-half check of all 4,096 layer-8 PCs finds 3,976 within 3 standard errors of zero covariance with birth year — the failure is at the predicted noise scale.

04 Reading

Strengths

  • Theory earns its keep. A closed form predicts both the location (quiet directions) and the scale (σf/(σv√n)) of the failure; the split-half histogram lands on it.
  • Genuinely controlled. Same activations, same target, same readout — no dataset or architecture confound can explain the OLS/DAS gap.
  • Honest about its own reach. Concedes the proof covers only the negative OLS claim, that ridge is not guaranteed faithful, and that held-out splits catch the failure on Llama but not the HMM.
  • A vivid demonstration. 91.5% of mass on a variance-10−18, near-inert direction is about as clean an illustration of “decodable but unused” as one could ask for.

Limitations

  • λ needs causal ground truth — precisely what a probe user lacks. No causally blind surrogate (held-out MSE, split-half stability, PLS) is validated.
  • One model, layer 8, one attribute, one template, and n = 8,000 against 4,096 dimensions: a weakly determined regression.
  • Regression only. Logistic probes, the dominant practice, are out of scope — the mechanism does not yet license claims about classifiers.
  • The loophole is only half-closed. Restricted-DAS searches the top 100 PCs (34.8% of Llama OLS mass); the long tail (~65%) is untested.
  • Thin baselines and no uncertainty. No PLS, PCA truncation, elastic net or variance-normalized regression; one DAS fit, no seeds or CIs, unstated λ grid.
05 Open questions

What the next paper has to run

  1. Does a causally blind selector — split-half covariance stability, held-out MSE, PLS component count — land anywhere near the intervention-optimal λ?
  2. What is the logistic analogue of Corollary 3.3, and does it survive when “intervention” means flipping a class prediction?
  3. Does the OLS/DAS angle persist across layers and beyond 7B — is layer 8 a property of the model or of the birth-year task?
  4. Is the unsearched 65% of Llama OLS mass truly inert, or can a subspace or nonlinear search find an operative combination there?
  5. Do published cases of “steering that does nothing” turn positive when the probe is re-fit with ridge?
