> **一句话总结：** 作者用"可读比特"(M(t) = H₀ − CE)配合 tuned lens 和逐层线性探针，在 weight-tied 循环 Transformer 上测出存储事实何时变得可解码——在只监督最后一轮时，事实随循环逐层浮现，且记忆负载越重浮现越晚（20K→100K 时达 95% 的深度从 1.99 循环推迟到 3.77 循环），而每轮都加监督+逐深度读出后八个配置全部在第 1 轮就达到近最终可读性；在多跳链上，对单个 token 位置的实体子空间做 patch 能把下游桥实体改写并在 79–98.6% 的样本上改变最终答案。

> **TL;DR:** On synthetic BioS biographies (20K/50K/100K individuals) with 11-layer, d=192 weight-tied loops, readable bits rise monotonically with loop depth under final-loop supervision, but heavier memorization load delays the 95%-of-final crossing from 1.99 to 3.77 loops (loop-4) even though final M is identical at 8.93 bits in every cell; equal-unrolled-depth untied baselines only reach 50% of their final lens score at 0.81–0.88 (equal width) or 0.93–0.99 (parameter-matched) of depth versus 0.25–0.65 for looped models. Training twins with supervision and a separate readout at every loop erase the delay entirely (M(1)=8.89–8.90 bits, within 0.02 bits of final, in all 8 cells), so the delay is a training artifact, not an architectural inevitability. In a released 4-layer, width-768 R6 multi-hop model (200 entities, 2k atomic facts), patching the probe-defined entity subspace at one relation position redirects descendants 1–2 hops later (+15.1–18.0 probe bits, top-1 in 81.3–97.9%) and yields the donor's final answer in 79.1–97.2% of cases versus 9.9–18.8% for norm-matched random subspaces.

## 核心贡献

- 提出以"可读比特" M(t) = H₀ − CE(t) 为口径的深度分辨评测：在循环 Transformer 的每一次 recurrence 上测量存储事实何时变得可线性解码，而不是只看最后一轮输出。
- 发现"负载依赖的检索延迟"：在只监督最后一轮（final-loop supervision）时，事实随循环逐步浮现；N 从 20K 增到 100K 会把 95% 完成深度从 1.99/2.98/3.77 循环（loop-4）推迟到 3.86/5.53/6.73 循环（loop-8），但最终可读比特在所有 (N, L) 单元里都是 8.93 bits，差异小于 0.007 bits。
- 与等展开深度的非循环（untied）基线对比：44/88 层的 untied 模型把一半的最终可读信息压在栈的 81–88%（等宽度）或 93–99%（近似参数量匹配）处，而循环模型在 0.25–0.65 处就已达到，说明循环把检索摊到了更早的深度。
- 匹配训练对照证明延迟可消除：在 8 个配置上训练"每一轮都监督 + 每轮独立 LayerNorm/读出头"的双胞胎模型，第 1 轮 M(1) = 8.89–8.90 bits（与最终值差 0.02 bits 以内），且首次达到自身最终值 95% 的位置就是 loop 1。因此延迟取决于训练协议，而非架构必然。
- 表征分析：状态首先按"查询键"（individual/key）组织，随后才涌现"值"（attribute value）结构——key Δη² 从首轮 0.81–0.83 降到末轮 0.66–0.76，value Δη² 升到 0.15–0.17/0.09/0.05（20K/50K/100K）；从未训练过的个体 C 上 key 结构与 A 相差 0.02 以内但 value 结构贴住随机基线（|Δη²| < 0.01）。
- 多跳组合的因果证据：把探针定义的实体子空间在单个 relation token 位置做 patch，一个 recurrence 内即可把下游桥实体推向 donor 链，并在 79.07–98.57% 的样本上翻转最终答案；而同实体、不同关系前缀的 donor 在 99.57–99.87% 的情况下保留原答案。
- 顺带修正了前人结论：在匹配课程阶段重训后，动态采样循环数（R^dyn）并不一致优于固定 6 轮（R6）——在 2–6 跳上早至多 1 轮，但在 11–13 跳上反而晚 1 轮。

## Key Contributions

- **A depth-resolved, entropy-referenced measurement of factual readability.** Instead of final accuracy, the paper reports M(t) = H₀ − CE(t) at every recurrence, using both the native readout and a tuned lens fit per depth, plus a capacity sweep (rank-16/32/64 identity-plus-low-rank corrections, unrestricted affine).
- **Load-dependent retrieval depth.** Under final-loop supervision, single-hop facts become readable progressively, and higher loads shift readability later while leaving the final score essentially unchanged (8.93 bits in all six main cells, cross-load spread < 0.007 bits).
- **Looped vs. untied at equal unrolled depth.** Looped models reach half of their final lens score at 0.25/0.40/0.65 of unrolled depth (loop-4) and 0.23/0.42/0.61 (loop-8), against 0.81–0.88 and 0.93–0.99 for untied baselines — but see the critique below on how the untied models were trained.
- **Matched training comparisons that dissolve the delay.** Eight every-loop-supervision twins with depth-specific readouts all show near-final first-loop readability, isolating the training protocol as the cause.
- **Key/value structure analysis across loops**, with permutation-null correction and a never-trained split C as a negative control that separates identity structure from actual memorization.
- **Localized, subspace-level causal interventions** on multi-hop chains: entity-subspace patching at one token position propagates to descendants, steers the final answer, and is specific relative to random-subspace and same-entity-different-context controls.

## Methodology

**Single-hop recall (Section 2).** Models follow Ouro (Zhu et al., 2025): weight-tied GPT-2-style Transformers with an 11-layer shared block, width d=192, 3 attention heads, RoPE, vocabulary 5,086–5,371 tokens, loop budgets L ∈ {4, 8}. Data is BioS (Allen-Zhu & Li, 2023) synthetic biographies with N ∈ {20K, 50K, 100K} individuals; five scored attributes: birth city, university, employer, major, birth date. Individuals are split A/B/C = 60/35/5%: the backbone trains on A∪B, lenses are fit on A only, C is never seen in any training. Attribute values are sampled independently of names, so the lens cannot exploit a name→attribute rule. Loss is final-loop-only; five training seeds per (N, L) cell, 30 models total.

**Readable bits.** For an attribute drawn uniformly from K values, H₀ = log₂K (7.64 bits for 200 cities, 16.04 for 67,200 birth dates); the readable information at depth t is M(t) = H₀ − CE(t) (Barber & Agakov, 2004). M = 0 is the uniform-value log-loss baseline, M = H₀ is zero CE. Reading is done two ways at each depth: the native readout (terminal LayerNorm + tied LM head applied directly to h⁽ᵗ⁾) and a tuned lens z⁽ᵗ⁾ = lm_head(LN(W_t h⁽ᵗ⁾ + b_t)), where only W_t, b_t are trained on A by gold-token CE. The authors state explicitly that the native-vs-lens gap does not measure information missing from the state, only decodability.

**Key/value geometry.** For each split, 1,000 individuals × 6 paraphrases are collected at the prediction positions of the four single-token attributes; states are grouped by individual (key) or by correct value (value) and scored with the between/within variance ratio F(G; t), converted to η² and null-subtracted against a permutation reference that preserves group sizes and within-individual similarity (η²_null from F_perm), reporting Δη² = η² − η²_null. Value timing is summarized by a geometric midpoint t₁/₂ of the ratio R(t) = F_value/F_perm over attributes.

**Every-loop twins (Appendix C.1).** Eight cells (19-layer d=192 at 20K, 20-layer d=256 at 50K, 11-layer d=192 and 8-layer d=384 at 100K, each at L=4 and L=8) are trained with the unweighted mean of CE over all L depths and a per-depth LayerNorm, sharing the tied embedding matrix. This changes supervision and readout together; all other settings are matched.

**Multi-hop composition (Section 3).** Uses the released R6 (fixed 6 loops) and R^dyn (Poisson(4)-sampled, clipped to 2–8) checkpoints of Kohli et al. (2026): 4-layer recurrent block, width 768, 12 heads, no positional embeddings, 217-token vocabulary, a knowledge graph of 200 entities / 10 relations / 2k atomic facts / 15k k-hop facts per k. A k-hop query supplies the start entity and relation sequence; only the endpoint is supervised — the bridge entities must be retrieved from weights.

**Probes and interventions.** Per-token affine probes are fit separately for each loop × physical layer × hop × token position over the 217-token vocabulary (10 epochs; 52,800/6,600/6,600 dynamic-model chains for train/val/test). The "frontier" is the longest contiguous prefix of entities that are top-1 decoded with pointwise V-information above the 10th percentile of correct validation decodes (β ≈ 7.5 bits); the modal frontier is selected per recurrence on validation data. At the frontier's token position p, only the entity subspace is replaced: h′_p = h_p + P_entity(h_p^donor − h_p), where P_entity = VVᵀ comes from the SVD of the probe's mean-centred 200×768 entity rows (rank r = 199, i.e. 26% of the hidden dimension). Controls are an equal-rank, norm-matched Gaussian subspace, full-vector replacement, an aligned-subspace variant, a residual-matched variant, and same-entity donors that reach the same frontier through a different relation prefix.

## Results & Highlights

- **Final readability is load-invariant, timing is not.** In all six main (N, L) cells the five-attribute mean reaches M(L) = 8.93 bits (final SD ≤ 0.005 bits; seed-averaged M(L) differs by < 0.007 bits across loads and budgets) — but the first depth crossing 95% of that value moves from 1.99 loops at N=20K to 2.98 at 50K and 3.77 at 100K for loop-4 (3.86/5.53/6.73 for loop-8), i.e. 0.50/0.74/0.94 versus 0.48/0.69/0.84 of the budget. At the heaviest load the loop-4 model needs essentially its whole budget to plateau.
- **Looped models expose facts at a much smaller fraction of unrolled depth than untied ones.** At 44 layers, the looped model reaches 50% of its final lens score at 0.25/0.40/0.65 of depth (20K/50K/100K) versus 0.81/0.82/0.81 for equal-width untied and 0.93/0.97/0.97 for parameter-matched untied; the 95% crossings are 0.50/0.73/0.96 versus 0.94/0.95/0.95 and 0.99/1.00/1.00. At 88 layers the looped gaps are the same shape (0.23/0.42/0.61 vs 0.88/0.88/0.83 and 0.96/0.99/0.99), and the native-readout 95% crossings are 0.62–0.98 (looped) vs 0.98–1.00 (untied).
- **Every-loop supervision removes the delay.** Across all eight twins, the model's own per-depth readout gives M(1) = 8.89–8.90 bits — within 0.02 bits of the twin's final value — and the full lens gives 8.92–8.93 bits; both cross 95% at loop 1. The matched final-loop arms start at −94.1 to −1.2 bits (own readout) or −0.6 to 5.3 bits (lens) and need 2.0–6.8 loops to cross. Final M differs between arms by < 0.011 bits, so the delay is redistributed, not bought with capacity.
- **The "value" structure is what distinguishes memorization from mere identity separation.** On the never-trained split C, key Δη² is within 0.02 of split A at every depth, yet value Δη² stays within |0.01| of the permutation null; on A, value Δη² reaches only 0.15–0.17 (20K), 0.09 (50K), 0.05 (100K) while key Δη² declines from 0.81–0.83 to 0.66–0.76. The value midpoint t₁/₂/L rises with load: 0.50/0.64/0.68 (loop-4) and 0.49/0.59/0.62 (loop-8).
- **Lens-capacity controls do not overturn the trend but do shift the numbers.** Rank-16/32/64 corrections to the identity keep the load ordering; the unrestricted lens beats the rank-16 update by at most 2.1 bits at any attribute and depth, and the 95% crossing is unchanged except at (20K, loop-4), where ranks 16 and 32 cross one loop later (interpolated 2.28/2.10 vs 2.00/1.99 loops).
- **Early native confidence is a bad exit signal.** Full-depth exact-match is 0.795–0.803. The best confidence-threshold rule within 0.5 points of full depth still consumes 3.76–3.97 of 4 loops and 6.90–7.56 of 8 (20K/50K), versus oracle means of 2.84–3.40 and 5.09–6.13. At 100K no tested threshold qualifies (0.786 vs 0.795 for loop-4; 0.739 vs 0.795 for loop-8), and at τ = 0.5 models exit after 1.2–1.3 loops at 0.04 and 0.01 accuracy.
- **Bridge entities decode in chain order in the released multi-hop checkpoints.** R^dyn reaches 80% endpoint accuracy at loops 2 and 5 for 6- and 12-hop chains, against loops 4 and 6 for R6. But on matched 13-hop curricula the retrained R^dyn seeds are only equal or one loop earlier on 2–6 hops, and one loop *later* on 11–13 hops (all three seeds need 7 loops for 13 hops vs 6 for R6) — the released dynamic checkpoint's advantage is confounded by its longer curriculum (22 vs 13 hops) and ~5× the epochs (5,388 vs 1,015).
- **Frontier patches propagate within one recurrence and steer the final answer.** Entity-subspace patches at recurrences 2–4 lift descendants at offsets 1–2 by 15.1–18.0 bits of probe log-probability (top-1 in 81.3–97.9% of cases) and offset 3 by 2.0–9.8 bits (top-1 30.9–70.6%), while offset 4 is untouched; the norm-matched random subspace lifts offsets 1–2 by only 3.2–8.4 bits. After six loops the donor-implied endpoint is produced in 98.6/97.2/94.3/79.1/88.4% of cases for patches at recurrences 1–5, versus random-subspace 51.0/15.7/18.8/9.9/20.8% — a gap of 67.5–81.6 points at recurrences 2–5.
- **The intervention is entity-specific, not just perturbation-specific.** Same-entity donors that reach the frontier through a different relation prefix preserve the original answer in 99.57–99.87% of cases under entity-subspace patching and 98.33–99.52% under whole-state replacement (2,221–2,304 pairs per recurrence), and the counterfactual endpoint is almost never emitted before output loop 5 (≤0.4%) before jumping to 77.3–97.9% at loop 5 — evidence of propagation through computation rather than immediate readout.

## Authors & Credibility

All four authors are at Qualcomm AI Research (Fabio Valerio Massoli, Arnau Padrés Masdemont, Jordi Ros Giralt, Arash Behboodi), with Massoli and Padrés Masdemont sharing equal contribution and Massoli as corresponding author. Qualcomm AI Research has a solid track record in efficient inference, quantization, and mechanistic analysis, and the paper reads like the work of a group that has previously built evaluation harnesses rather than one chasing a headline. The citations are current and specific (Ouro/Zhu et al. 2025, Kohli et al. 2026, Sesodia et al. 2026, Zhang et al. 2026, Belrose et al. 2023 tuned lens), and the paper explicitly positions itself against concurrent probing work on in-context graph traversal, clarifying that here every hop comes from weights rather than the prompt. The writing is unusually disciplined about what a readout can and cannot prove. There is a full AI-use statement (LLM used for prose editing and exploratory analysis scripts, with every reported number recomputed by an author), an ethics statement, and a reproducibility statement promising code and checkpoints.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The framing — separating *when* a fact becomes readable from *whether* its representation is causally used — is a real contribution and is not just "we ran a tuned lens on a looped model." The key/value analysis with a never-trained split as a negative control, and the same-entity-different-relation-prefix donor control, are both sharper than the usual probe-plus-patch package. The every-loop twin experiment is the most valuable single result in the paper precisely because it argues against the paper's own most quotable claim.

**Rigor — where it is strong.** Five seeds for every single-hop cell (30 models), an explicit A/B/C generalization ladder with A−B gaps ≤0.71 bits at every depth and ≤0.017 bits at the final depth, C pinned below the uniform-value baseline (worst case −0.80 bits per attribute, −1.10 for the mean), permutation-null subtraction for the geometry analysis, and a lens-capacity sweep. The honest deflation of the R^dyn vs R6 comparison — three retrained seeds per regime at the same 13-hop curriculum stage, showing R^dyn is *later* on 11–13 hops — is exactly the kind of check most papers skip.

**Rigor — where it is weak.** Three issues stand out. (1) *The untied baseline is not a fair control for the paper's own thesis.* The untied models are standard single-pass Transformers trained with final-layer loss — which is precisely the "final-loop supervision" protocol the paper's own Appendix C.1 shows *causes* late readability. The clean experiment would train the 44/88-layer untied models with per-layer supervision and per-layer readouts, and it is never run. As it stands, "looped models expose facts earlier than untied ones" may be a statement about where the loss lands, not about recurrence. (2) *The every-loop twins change two variables at once.* Supervision at every depth *and* a separate LayerNorm/readout head per depth are introduced together, so the paper cannot say whether the delay came from the loss or from the readout bottleneck; the authors acknowledge the protocol is "combined" but the conclusion is still stated as a training-dependent schedule. (3) *Parameter and depth normalization pull in opposite directions.* The looped models have 4.89M non-embedding parameters; equal-width untied baselines have 19.6M/39.1M, and the parameter-matched ones are narrowed to d=96/d=66, which drops the 88-layer, 100K model's final M to 8.46 bits. The "earlier" result is robust in direction but its magnitude is normalization-dependent, and the paper never reports an accuracy-matched or FLOP-matched comparison.

**Statistical caveats.** The multi-hop causal work uses one released R6 checkpoint; the "three seeds" resample held-out relation groups and donor pairs, not model initializations, so no between-model variance is available for the 79.1–98.6% steering rates. Coverage is also selected: at recurrence 4 only 51.4% of test queries (range 39.9–59.5%) have the modal frontier and thus enter the analysis, and no pair shares a frontier entity or a final answer with its donor. That conditioning on "chains that happen to be decodable at this exact point" plausibly inflates the steering rate relative to the full chain population — a survivorship effect the paper does not quantify. The recurrence-1 random-subspace control already reaches 50.95%, and recurrence 1 is accordingly excluded from the control comparison; defensible, but it means the most causal-looking intervention depth is also the least specific.

**Overclaiming check.** The abstract says "these results distinguish a training-dependent schedule for factual recall from the causal role of retrieved-entity representations" — accurate as stated. The one place the prose drifts is "looped models expose facts earlier," which is stated without the untied-training-protocol caveat that the paper's own Appendix C.1 makes unavoidable. The M(t) = H₀ − CE metric is also carefully hedged: in Appendix C the authors note that at 20K split B has 7,000 individuals so empirical birth-date entropy cannot exceed log₂7000 ≈ 12.77 bits, below the source entropy of 16.04 bits, meaning M is a log-loss reduction relative to the source baseline and *not* a mutual-information lower bound on the fixed population. Good that they say it; readers skimming the figures will still read "bits of readable information" as information.

**Reproducibility.** Architecture, optimizer (AdamW β₁=0.9, β₂=0.98, wd=0.02, peak LR 1e-3, 1,000 warmup steps), batch composition, 350 exposures per biography, and exact optimizer-step counts (17,318/43,295/86,589) are all given. Lens fitting is specified down to the learning-rate grid and epoch count. Multi-hop training uses a released third-party codebase and checkpoints. Code and checkpoints are promised but not yet linked, and the intervention protocol (projector rank 199, β ≈ 7.5 bits, frontier selection) is described precisely enough to reimplement.

## Strengths

- Asks a genuinely under-studied question — not *how much* a looped model memorizes (already answered by Zhu et al. 2025) but *when* and *whether* that memory enters computation — and answers it at three levels: prediction, geometry, and causation.
- The never-trained split C is an unusually clean negative control: key structure survives (within 0.02 of A) while value structure collapses to the permutation null, which rules out "the probe just found the name."
- The every-loop twin experiment is a model of intellectual honesty: it takes the paper's most eye-catching finding and shows it is a protocol artifact, then reports both arms with matched final M.
- Intervention controls are better than typical: equal-rank norm-matched random subspaces, full-vector replacement, aligned and residual-matched variants, plus a same-entity-different-prefix donor condition that preserves the answer 99.6–99.9% of the time.
- The retrained-seed re-analysis of R^dyn vs R6 deflates a published claim with matched curricula rather than accepting the released checkpoints at face value.
- Metrics are reported with explicit interpretation limits (source vs empirical entropy, readout ≠ information), and all reported numbers are said to have been recomputed from logs.

## Limitations

- Everything is synthetic: BioS biographies and a 200-entity, 10-relation toy knowledge graph. Nothing here establishes that the retrieval schedule or the causal role of bridge states transfers to natural-text LLMs — the authors concede this in Section 5.
- The causal multi-hop results come from a single released R6 checkpoint; the three "seeds" resample relation groups and donors, not weights, so model-to-model variability of the steering rates is unmeasured.
- The every-loop twins entangle supervision and readout architecture, so the mechanism behind the disappeared delay is not identified.
- The untied baselines are trained only on their final layer, which is the very protocol the paper shows induces late readability; the headline "looped exposes facts earlier" therefore lacks its clean control.
- Linear/affine readouts and second-order geometry can miss nonlinear encodings; a failure to decode is not evidence of absence, and the lens capacity sweep only goes downward (rank 16/32/64), never to higher-capacity or nonlinear decoders.
- M(t) is a log-loss reduction against source entropy, not a mutual-information or compression-capacity bound; at small N it can exceed what the evaluation population could possibly support.
- No compute-matched or accuracy-matched comparison between looped and untied models; the parameter-matched untied baselines are narrowed (d=96/66), which muddies the interpretation in the other direction.
- No natural-language or pretrained-model validation, no test of whether the frontier-patching recipe transfers to models where facts are entangled rather than independently sampled.

## Relevance

直接命中 "LLM Hidden States & Interpretability" 方向：它用 tuned lens、逐层线性探针、方差分解和子空间干预，系统回答了"存储知识在循环模型的哪一轮变得可读、以及该表征是否真的被下游计算使用"——正是机制可解释性里"可解码 ≠ 因果使用"这一核心方法论问题。对关注循环/隐式推理架构、探针可靠性、以及用 activation patching 做因果验证的读者，这篇的控制组设计（未训练个体 C、同实体异前缀 donor、等秩范数匹配随机子空间）值得直接借用。

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. If the 44/88-layer untied models were trained with per-layer supervision and per-layer readouts (the Appendix C.1 protocol), would the 0.81–0.88 vs 0.25–0.65 gap survive? This is the single experiment that would decide whether "looped exposes facts earlier" is architectural or a loss-placement artifact.
2. Within the every-loop twins, which change drives first-loop readability — the multi-depth loss, or the per-depth LayerNorm providing a usable readout at shallow depth? A 2×2 ablation (loss × readout) is cheap and would settle it.
3. Why does the value-structure midpoint keep moving later with load (0.50 → 0.68) while final M is pinned at 8.93 bits? Is the extra load buying more competing associations per key, i.e. a retrieval-precision problem rather than a capacity one?
4. Does the rank-199 entity projector still steer answers in a model with, say, 10K entities and overlapping relations, where the entity subspace cannot be nearly as cleanly separated from the relation subspace?
5. Can the depth-resolved M(t) profile be used prospectively — e.g. as a diagnostic that predicts when a looped model will need more recurrences at inference, better than final validation loss?
