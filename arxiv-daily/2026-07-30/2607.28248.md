> **一句话总结：** 这是一篇聚焦"集成方法 + 近似贝叶斯方法"的深度学习不确定性量化（UQ）批判性综述，其核心创新在于将"产生预测集成的方法"与"总结其不确定性的度量"彻底解耦为两条独立的分类轴，并系统比较了互信息分解与新兴的成对散度度量（EPKL/EPJS）及高效的方差门控（Variance-Gated）度量。

> **TL;DR:** This survey decouples the *method* that produces a predictive ensemble (Bayesian NNs, MC Dropout, deep ensembles, efficient approximations, last-layer/single-pass methods) from the *measure* that summarizes its uncertainty (entropy/mutual-information decomposition vs. pairwise-divergence measures EPKL/EPJS vs. efficient variance-gated scores), and consolidates evaluation practice around two reference baselines — Maximum Softmax Probability (Hendrycks & Gimpel, 2017) and deep ensembles (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017) — benchmarked on standard OOD pairs (CIFAR-10 vs. SVHN/CIFAR-100, ImageNet vs. iNaturalist/Places) and the Ovadia et al. (2019) shift suite. It contains no new experiments of its own; its qualitative L/M/H comparison table (Table 3) is a literature synthesis, and several entries (e.g., CreDE, Anchored/Snapshot/Sub-Ensembles) are flagged with a dagger as inferred from method structure rather than direct benchmarks.

## 核心贡献

- **方法与度量解耦的统一框架**：提出以"生成机制"（显式成员 / 后验采样 / 闭式预测）和"网络作用范围"（全网络 / 共享主干+末层 / 单一确定性网络）为两轴的方法分类体系（Table 2），并明确指出度量（总不确定性、任意不确定性、认知不确定性）在原则上可与任意产生集成预测的方法自由搭配。
- **对高效集成近似方法的深度梳理**：系统评述了 BatchEnsemble、Snapshot Ensemble、SWAG/MultiSWAG、Sub-Ensemble、Masksembles、Layer Ensemble、CreDE 等以降低 M× 训练/推理成本为目标的方法，并援引最新工作（Zamyatin et al., 2026）指出 BatchEnsemble 成员在函数空间上几乎同质，行为更接近单一模型而非真正的集成，OOD 检测效果差。
- **不确定性度量的批判性比较**：对比了标准的熵/互信息可加分解（Depeweg et al., 2018; Smith and Gal, 2018）与 Schweighofer et al. (2023a) 提出的成对散度度量 EPCE/EPKL，以及对称有界的 Jensen-Shannon 变体 EPJS，指出互信息在有限集成/后验失配下会低估认知不确定性（Wimmer et al., 2023），而 EPKL 无界、对类别概率趋零时不稳定。
- **提出/整合高效方差门控度量**：介绍了作者团队自己提出的 Variance-Gated Ensemble (VGE)、Variance-Gated Margin Uncertainty (VGMU) 和 Variance-Gated Normalization (VGN)，声称可将成对度量的 O(M²C) 复杂度降至 O(MC) 或 O(C)，但论文自己承认这些度量尚未在标准分布偏移基准上与 MI/EPKL/EPJS 做过系统性对照。
- **系统化评估方法论与开放问题清单**：整合了 ECE、AUROC/AUPR、OpenOOD、Uncertainty Baselines 等评估协议与分布偏移基准，并提出了六大开放研究方向，包括末层多样性的上限、大语言模型中的语义熵/校准稳定性等。

## Key Contributions

- **Unified method/measure decoupling framework**: Organizes UQ methods along two axes — generative mechanism (explicit members / posterior sampling / closed-form predictive) and network scope (full-network / shared-backbone-last-layer / single deterministic network) — in Table 2, and argues the uncertainty *measure* is largely orthogonal to which method produced the predictive ensemble (Figure 1).
- **Deep treatment of efficient ensemble approximations**: Surveys BatchEnsemble, Snapshot Ensembles, SWAG/MultiSWAG, Sub-Ensembles, Masksembles, Layer Ensembles, and Credal Deep Ensembles (CreDE), citing Zamyatin et al. (2026) that BatchEnsemble members are near-identical in function space ("behaving more like a single model than a true ensemble") with poor OOD detection despite near-single-model training cost.
- **Critical comparison of uncertainty measures**: Contrasts the additive entropy/mutual-information decomposition (Depeweg et al., 2018; Smith and Gal, 2018) against Schweighofer et al.'s (2023a) pairwise-divergence measures (EPCE for total uncertainty, EPKL for epistemic uncertainty) and the symmetric, bounded Expected Pairwise Jensen-Shannon (EPJS), analyzing symmetry/boundedness trade-offs (Table 6) and citing Wimmer et al. (2023) that MI can underestimate epistemic uncertainty in multi-class settings.
- **Proposes/consolidates efficient variance-gated measures**: Introduces the authors' own Variance-Gated Ensemble (VGE), Variance-Gated Margin Uncertainty (VGMU, O(C) cost), and Variance-Gated Normalization (VGN), positioned as cheaper (O(MC) vs. O(M²C)) alternatives to pairwise divergence measures, while explicitly flagging that their behavior under distribution shift "has not yet been benchmarked against MI and the pairwise measures on the standard suites."
- **Consolidated evaluation methodology and open-problems agenda**: Anchors qualitative ratings to ECE, AUROC/AUPR, OpenOOD (Yang et al., 2022) and Uncertainty Baselines (Nado et al., 2022), and closes with concrete open questions (last-layer diversity limits, efficient non-entropy epistemic measures for classification, hybrid architectures, LLM uncertainty calibration stability).

## Methodology

This is a structured, critical **survey** (not an empirical study) organized as follows:

1. **Taxonomy (Section 2, Table 2, Figure 1)**: Methods are classified by *generative mechanism* (explicit trained members; posterior sampling via MC at inference; closed-form analytic predictive) crossed with *network scope* (full network; shared backbone with independent last layer; single deterministic network). Measures are classified separately as information-theoretic (entropy/MI-based) vs. divergence/moment-based (pairwise KL/JS, variance decomposition).
2. **Five core method families reviewed in depth (Sections 3–7)**: Bayesian neural networks (VI, MCMC/HMC/SGLD, Laplace, NNGP), Monte Carlo Dropout, deep ensembles, efficient ensemble approximations (BatchEnsemble, Snapshot, SWAG/MultiSWAG, Sub-Ensembles, Masksembles, Layer Ensembles, CreDE), and last-layer/single-pass approaches (Last-Layer Laplace, SNGP, DUQ/DDU/DUE, MIMO, MHML, epinet, repulsive LLE, VBLL).
3. **Adjacent families (Sections 8–10)**: evidential/prior/posterior networks (Dirichlet-based single-pass uncertainty), conformal prediction (distribution-free coverage guarantees), and post-hoc calibration (temperature scaling, focal loss, label smoothing).
4. **Evaluation consolidation (Section 11)**: fixes the evidentiary basis — ECE/Brier/NLL for calibration; AUROC/AUPR against the MSP baseline for OOD; OpenOOD and Uncertainty Baselines as standardized suites; CIFAR-10-C/CIFAR-100-C/ImageNet-C and the Ovadia et al. (2019) shift protocol for robustness.
5. **Ensemble diversity theory (Section 13)**: formalizes Wood et al.'s (2023) exact bias–variance–diversity decomposition of ensemble cross-entropy loss (Eq. 2) and reviews pairwise/non-pairwise diversity metrics (disagreement, Cohen's κ, prediction entropy, CKA, Vendi score).
6. **Uncertainty measures and decompositions (Section 14)**: derives the standard entropy/MI decomposition (Eq. 5–6), critiques its finite-ensemble failure modes, introduces EPCE/EPKL/EPJS pairwise measures (Eq. 7–10) and compares their symmetry/boundedness (Table 6), and introduces the authors' own efficient variance-gated measures (VGE/VGMU/VGN) and the classical law-of-total-variance decomposition for regression (Eq. 11).
7. **Decision-time tasks (Section 15)** and **LLM uncertainty (Section 16)** are treated as adjacent/emerging topics (token likelihood, semantic entropy, verbalized confidence, conformal generation), acknowledged as "deliberately uneven" coverage relative to the core classification-focused survey.

Ratings in the comparison tables (Table 3: cost, OOD quality L/M/H, calibration quality L/M/H) are explicitly qualitative, anchored against two reference points — the MSP baseline (Hendrycks and Gimpel, 2017) and deep ensembles (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017) — and evidenced per-method by citing the originating or standardized benchmarking paper; entries marked with a dagger (†) are the authors' own structural inference rather than a directly cited benchmark result.

## Results & Highlights

No quantitative results reported — this paper is a literature survey and reports no original experiments, benchmark runs, or numerical tables of its own. Instead, it offers the following forms of evidence:

- **Qualitative comparative ratings (Table 3)**: an L/M/H rating scheme for OOD-detection and calibration quality, anchored to two reference points (MSP baseline of Hendrycks and Gimpel, 2017, and deep ensembles of Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017) and evaluated on standard OOD pairs (CIFAR-10 vs. SVHN/CIFAR-100, ImageNet vs. iNaturalist/Places) plus the Ovadia et al. (2019) distribution-shift suite. Example placements: Deep Ensembles rated H/H (evidence: Ovadia et al., 2019; Gustafsson et al., 2020); BatchEnsemble rated L/L (evidence: Zamyatin et al., 2026, showing near-identical members in function space); SNGP rated M–H/M–H (evidence: Liu et al., 2022); DUQ/DDU/DUE rated H/M–H via feature-density evidence (Mukhoti et al., 2023).
- **Computational-cost comparisons**: pairwise divergence measures (EPCE, EPKL, EPJS) cost O(M²C) additional computation vs. O(MC) for mutual information and the proposed variance-gated decomposition, and O(C) for the proposed VGMU margin-based score (Table 7) — a structural/complexity claim rather than a benchmarked accuracy delta.
- **A cited quantitative finding used as evidence**: Fort et al. (2020) is cited for the practical observation that ensemble size "M = 5 is common, with diminishing returns beyond that point" for deep ensembles — one of the few concrete numeric anchors reproduced from prior empirical work.
- **Explicit caveats on evidentiary strength**: many Table 3 entries (Anchored Ensemble, Snapshot Ensemble, Sub-Ensemble, Layer Ensemble, TreeNets/LLE, CreDE) are marked with a dagger, meaning their OOD/calibration ratings are "inferred from method structure" rather than measured on the standard suites the survey itself recommends (Section 11) — the paper is transparent that a large fraction of its own comparative table is not independently benchmarked.

## Authors & Credibility

H. Martin Gillis and Thomas Trappenberg, Faculty of Computer Science, Dalhousie University, Halifax, Nova Scotia, Canada (corresponding author: martin.gillis@dal.ca). The authors are themselves originators of several methods discussed in the survey — the Variance-Gated Ensemble framework (Gillis et al., 2025, NeurIPS workshop; Gillis et al., 2026b, TMLR) and last-layer committee machines (Gillis et al., 2026a, Neural Networks journal) — which are presented in Section 14.7 as an efficient alternative to pairwise-divergence measures. This is a case of a survey authored by active contributors to a sub-area it reviews; the paper does flag the corresponding limitation (their own measures are not yet benchmarked against MI/EPKL/EPJS on standard suites), which is a point in favor of the paper's intellectual honesty even though it is a self-citation/positioning concern worth noting. No code, artifact, or dataset release accompanies a pure-survey paper of this kind.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The primary contribution is organizational rather than technical — a two-axis taxonomy (generative mechanism × network scope) that cleanly separates "how the predictive ensemble is produced" from "how its uncertainty is measured" (Figure 1, Table 2). This framing is a genuinely useful lens (e.g., it explains why last-layer methods occupy multiple rows in Table 2 depending on whether sampling is used), but the taxonomy itself synthesizes existing distinctions (Bayesian vs. ensemble vs. deterministic-single-pass) already present in prior surveys (Gawlikowski et al., 2023; Abdar et al., 2021; He et al., 2026) rather than introducing a fundamentally new concept.

**Rigor**: The paper is careful to label the evidentiary strength of each claim — Table 3's dagger (†) notation for "conservative inferences from method structure" rather than direct benchmarks is an unusually transparent practice for a survey. However, this transparency also exposes a real weakness: several comparative ratings (Anchored Ensembles, Sub-Ensembles, Layer Ensembles, CreDE, TreeNets/LLE) are not grounded in the standardized suites (OpenOOD, Ovadia et al. 2019) the paper itself insists are necessary for meaningful comparison (Section 11's "Reporting practice" explicitly recommends multi-metric, matched-compute evaluation) — a gap between stated methodology and what the tables actually deliver for roughly a third of the surveyed methods.

**Reproducibility**: N/A in the traditional sense (no code/experiments), but the survey's own qualitative claims are, by its own admission, not independently reproducible/verifiable for the dagger-marked entries.

**Significance**: Useful as a structured reference and evaluation checklist (Section 11's recommendation to evaluate at matched compute, across multiple metrics, is a sound methodological corrective the field under-applies), and its critique of the additive entropy/MI decomposition (citing Wimmer et al., 2023 and Kirsch, 2025 on "epistemic collapse" from implicit weight sharing) is a substantive, non-generic point specific to this literature.

**Red flags**: (1) The authors introduce and favorably position their own prior work (VGE/VGMU/VGN, Sections 14.7 and Table 7–8) inside a survey that is nominally reviewing the field neutrally, without independent third-party benchmarking of that work against the very measures (MI, EPKL, EPJS) it claims to improve upon in cost — a self-citation pattern the paper only partially mitigates by admitting the gap. (2) A meaningful fraction of the central comparison table (Table 3) rests on structural inference (†) rather than the standardized benchmarks the paper champions, which weakens the "unified evidentiary basis" the survey claims as one of its three core contributions. (3) LLM uncertainty (Section 16) is explicitly "treated as an emerging adjacent field" with admittedly uneven, brief coverage, so readers seeking depth on generative-model uncertainty should look to the two dedicated surveys it cites (Shorinwa et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2024) instead.

## Strengths

- Clean, genuinely useful separation of "method that produces an ensemble" from "measure that summarizes uncertainty," illustrated concretely via Figure 1 and Table 2, which clarifies why some methods (e.g., last-layer approaches) can occupy multiple cells in the taxonomy depending on implementation choice.
- Deepest available treatment of efficient ensemble approximations (BatchEnsemble, SWAG/MultiSWAG, Snapshot, Sub-Ensembles, Masksembles, Layer Ensembles, CreDE) relative to prior broader surveys, including recent critiques (Zamyatin et al., 2026 on BatchEnsemble; Kirsch, 2025 on epistemic collapse under implicit weight sharing).
- Transparent about the evidentiary strength of its own comparative claims via the dagger (†) notation, rather than presenting all ratings with false uniform confidence.
- Consolidates evaluation methodology (Section 11) into concrete, actionable recommendations: evaluate at matched compute, across multiple metrics (not just in-distribution calibration), using standardized suites (OpenOOD, Uncertainty Baselines) and shift benchmarks (Ovadia et al., 2019 protocol).
- Well-organized reference material (Tables 1, 3–10) that functions as a practical lookup/decision aid for practitioners choosing among cost/quality trade-offs.

## Limitations

- No original empirical results; all quantitative claims are second-hand from cited work, and a nontrivial share of Table 3's comparative ratings are the authors' own structural inference rather than measured benchmarks.
- Uneven scope by the authors' own admission: evidential/prior-network methods get fuller treatment while conformal prediction and post-hoc calibration are "included as complementary tools rather than for exhaustive coverage," and LLM uncertainty is only briefly surveyed.
- The efficient variance-gated measures the authors introduce (VGE/VGMU/VGN) are presented alongside established measures (MI, EPKL, EPJS) without the head-to-head empirical validation the paper's own Section 11 recommends as best practice — an internal inconsistency between stated methodology and the treatment of the authors' own prior work.
- Restricted to classification-centric decomposition; regression, structured prediction, and generative/LLM settings are treated as secondary or emerging rather than core, so readers needing deep regression-UQ or LLM-UQ coverage will need supplementary sources.

## Relevance

本综述对希望系统了解深度学习不确定性量化方法谱系（贝叶斯网络、MC Dropout、深度集成、高效近似、末层/单遍方法）及其配套度量（互信息分解 vs. 成对散度 vs. 高效方差门控）的研究者具有较高参考价值，尤其其"方法-度量解耦"框架和评估方法论建议（Section 11）可直接用于设计自己的 UQ 实验对照表；但其比较表格中相当一部分评级基于结构推断而非直接基准测试，引用时需结合原始文献核实具体数值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How would the authors' own Variance-Gated Ensemble/VGMU/VGN measures actually perform against MI, EPKL, and EPJS on the standardized OOD/shift suites (OpenOOD, Ovadia et al. 2019 protocol) the survey itself recommends as the evidentiary gold standard?
- Can the dagger-marked (†) entries in Table 3 (Anchored Ensembles, Sub-Ensembles, Layer Ensembles, CreDE, TreeNets/LLE) be replaced with direct, matched-compute benchmark numbers in a follow-up empirical study, and would the current L/M/H placements survive that scrutiny?
- Does last-layer diversity (shared backbone, independent heads) have a provable upper bound relative to full deep-ensemble diversity, and can architecture search identify when shared gradients collapse classification heads (per Kirsch, 2025)?
- Can bounded pairwise measures (EPJS) be validated empirically against MI and EPKL on the same distribution-shift benchmarks to resolve which is actually the better OOD detector in practice, rather than relying on the structural symmetry/boundedness argument alone?
- What would a principled aleatoric/epistemic split look like for free-form LLM generation, and can verbalized confidence be made calibration-stable under distribution shift and adversarial prompting?
- Do hybrid architectures (e.g., a small deep ensemble of SNGP/DDU backbones, or a distance-aware single-pass backbone carrying multiple last-layer heads) recover most of deep-ensemble uncertainty quality at a fraction of the O(M×) training cost — a question the survey raises but leaves systematically unevaluated?
