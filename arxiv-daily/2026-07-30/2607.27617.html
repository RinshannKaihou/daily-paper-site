Hidden APIs in Language Models: Discovering Reusable Causal Interfaces from Forked Futures ↗
SiYuan Ma, Yiqin Luo, Zhangji, Canran Xiao, et al. (11 authors) · 2026-07-30
11 authors · 11 institutions (NTU, SUSTech, Tianjin University, Sun Yat-sen, CMU, Georgia Tech, HK PolyU, Shenzhen University, Tsinghua, HKUST) · peer-reviewed ICLR 2026 conference paper · no widely-recognized interpretability-lab name among the authors, so reputation cannot substitute for reading the validation chain.
Key Metric
0.749 vs 0.150
Fraction of the target causal effect mediated by API-aligned paths vs. matched-null paths of equal producer–consumer distance — a >5× separation, the paper's strongest evidence that the Shared code is causally consumed, not just correlated with the output.
Why This Matters

Gives interpretability researchers a falsifiable test for whether an LLM truly reuses an internal "API" across tasks — instead of assuming one exists from decodability alone.

Critical Edge

In a blind test on synthetic model organisms, 1 of 12 non-Shared architectures was misclassified as Shared — an 8.3% false-Shared rate the authors admit blocks a "perfectly identifiable" claim.

Methodology

Forked futures → architecture competition → mechanistic validation
STEP 1Cache prefix hidden state h before any downstream operation is revealed.
→
STEP 2Sample operation k only afterward (query / invert / compose / verify …).
→
STEP 3Compare states via ΣK(h), the induced response-distribution set — the "future causal signature" (JS-based FSD).

Four encoder–reconstructor architectures then compete to explain that signature, capacity-matched and judged by 20-fold prequential causal MDL (cross-entropy of held-out signatures + a rank/gate complexity penalty). The winner's edge over the best rival is the Sharedness Gain, accepted only if a fidelity constraint on future-signature distortion also holds.

Strengths

What holds up
  • +Reframes "decodable now" vs. "causally reusable later" — closes a real gap between probing and true causal reuse.
  • +Explicit architecture competition (Shared vs. three named, non-strawman rivals) under matched capacity, not an assumed default.
  • +Includes a synthetic model-organism ground-truth test most representation-discovery papers skip — and reports its own 8.3% failure case rather than hiding it.
  • +Quantitative mediation (0.749 vs. 0.150) with matched-null controls, not a qualitative "ablation hurt performance" claim.

Limitations

What to weigh against it
  • −Full mechanistic analysis covers only two backbones; the five-backbone sweep confirms direction only, not magnitude.
  • −Blind recovery is imperfect (14/16) — one non-Shared organism misclassified as Shared, explicitly blocking a "perfectly identifiable" claim.
  • −Cross-family / matched-random donor transplantation degrades CompScore sharply (0.88 → 0.78 → 0.53) — reuse is bounded to partially-aligned roles.
  • −Task families are synthetic (relation-world, program-state, proof/discourse); open-domain dialogue and long-context reasoning are untested.
  • −6-layer, width-256 toy organisms may be easier to recover than mechanisms formed during real large-scale pretraining.
  • −Redundant/compensatory routes after path-blocking may under- or over-state true mediation, per the authors' own caveat.

Questions for Further Exploration

Open threads from the paper's own discussion
  • Would the Shared architecture's MDL advantage persist on naturalistic open-domain tasks (dialogue, tool use, agentic reasoning) rather than the synthetic task families used here?
  • What caused the single Local → Shared misclassification, and would a richer or adversarially-chosen operation bank reduce the false-Shared rate?
  • How does the 0.2–0.3 nat Sharedness Gain scale beyond 1.5B/8B — would it grow, shrink, or vanish at frontier scale?
  • Does the recovered Shared interface align with previously identified SAE features or verbalizable-representation directions under direct comparison?
  • Could cross-family transplantation degradation reflect a few shared low-level features rather than genuine partial API alignment?
