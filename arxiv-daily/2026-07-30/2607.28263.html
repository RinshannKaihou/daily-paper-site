Depth-axis compression · long-context memory

Split a transformer's depth, not its tokens — cache the lower layers, recompute only the upper ones on retrieval.

Why this matters

Long-context LLM serving cost is a real bottleneck; depth-axis compression — not just token pruning — is a new, largely unexplored lever to shrink memory without giving up much accuracy.

Critical edge

On BABILong, CoMem actually underperforms its own KV-Direct baseline (50.43 vs. 63.00) — a real "in-window compression tax," not hidden by the authors.

How CoMem works

Most KV-cache compression cuts along the token axis (StreamingLLM, H2O, SnapKV). CoMem instead cuts along the depth axis of a frozen Qwen3-8B (36 layers): probing shows semantic content is readable by mid-depth, while only the upper layers specialize to the current query.

W
WRITE — each 512-token chunk runs only through bottom layers [0:j]; cache one per-token residual tensor (8KB vs. 144KB full bf16 KV — 1/18 the bytes).
S
SELECT — iterative BM25 (hop-4, up to 3 expansion rounds) returns a bounded top-k=12 chunk set. Bounded lexical retrieval, not a learned retriever.
R
READ — pack [sink; selected h_j chunks; query h_j] with fresh RoPE positions; re-run only upper layers [j:L] with full cross-chunk attention.
Because k, chunk size, and query length are fixed, the online pack is ~6.7k tokens regardless of how much context is stored — model-side compute decouples from total context length. A rank-32 LoRA, self-distilled only on PG-19 text (no task labels), pushes the usable split from j=9 to j=12.

LoCoMo gap (+4.81 pts, 1,540 paired items) is bootstrap-robust (95% CI [2.34, 7.27]); a stratified DeepSeek-V3 audit preserves the ranking (κ=0.626).

Ablations show full cross-chunk attention matters most for multi-fact tasks (block-diagonal reuse costs 36–62 pts on RULER niah_multikey), and wider retrieval isn't always better — Hop-4's ~17k-token pack loses to the focused ~6.5k top-12 pack on variable tracking at 128k (87.4 vs. 95.8).

Assessment

Strengths

  • Mechanistic premise (depth division of labor) established via probing and causal truncation before the method is introduced.
  • Honestly reports a benchmark (BABILong) where CoMem loses to its own baseline, plus explicit caveats about the unextended-window comparison.
  • Same-platform, adapter-isolated efficiency control cleanly separates LoRA overhead from core architectural savings.
  • Careful statistics on the flagship LoCoMo result: bootstrap CIs, cluster resampling, independent judge cross-validation.
  • Controlled ablations (depth sweep, full vs. block-diagonal attention, retrieval breadth) isolate which design choices actually drive the gains.

Limitations

  • Not an accuracy state-of-the-art for every in-window task; BABILong qa2 remains weak (27.0).
  • Backbone is not position-extended (no YaRN) — above-native-window results are unextended stress references, not comparisons to a length-extended system.
  • Retrieval is lexical (BM25) only; may fail when relevant passages share little surface form with the query. Dense retrievers are out of scope.
  • Multi-hop retrieval latency is unmeasured; full cross-chunk attention needed for accuracy raises read compute vs. block-diagonal reuse.
  • LoCoMo evaluation uses only 10 conversation clusters; continually-updating memory streams are outside current scope.
Questions for further exploration
Does CoMem preserve its efficiency/accuracy trade-off when stacked on top of position-extension methods like YaRN or NTK-aware scaling?
Would a learned/dense retriever close the BABILong qa2 gap or help low lexical-overlap queries?
Is the readable-depth boundary (j≈12/36 for Qwen3-8B) transferable across model families, or must it be re-probed per backbone?
How does the full-vs-block-diagonal attention trade-off scale as retrieval breadth k grows well past 12 chunks?
What is the cost of dynamically updating or deleting cached h_j tensors in a continually-changing memory store?
Would combining CoMem's depth-axis reuse with token-axis KV compression (PyramidKV, SnapKV) compound efficiency gains, or interact destructively?
