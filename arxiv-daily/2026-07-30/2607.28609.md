> **一句话总结：** 论文构建了首个系统评测VLM作为计算机使用智能体(CUA)轨迹"判官"是否可靠的跨平台基准OSReward，发现最强闭源模型Claude-Opus-4-8在完整集上达89.7%准确率、但在专挑难例的OSReward-Hard子集上骤降至69.7%（平均判官仅52%），且所有模型家族共享"把未完成任务误判为成功"的宽松偏差；为此他们发布10万条推理标注语料OS-Shepherd-100K并训练出开源奖励模型OS-Shepherd-9B/35B，在OSReward-Hard上以30–60倍更低成本（约$1.36/千条 vs 商用判官约$45–100/千条）逼近商用判官水平。

> **TL;DR:** The paper introduces OSReward, the first benchmark measuring how reliable vision-language-model (VLM) judges are at verifying computer-using-agent (CUA) trajectories, built from 1,019 human-gold, cross-platform (web/Windows/Ubuntu/mobile) trajectories plus a hard diagnostic subset OSReward-Hard. Across 27 judges, even the best (Claude-Opus-4-8) falls from 89.7% binary accuracy on the full set to 69.7% on OSReward-Hard (mean judge 52%), with every model family sharing one dominant "over-accept" leniency bias (incomplete tasks rated as successes, outnumbering the reverse error ~3:1). The authors release OS-Shepherd-100K (100K reasoning-annotated judgments) and train open reward models OS-Shepherd-9B/35B that reach 60.2%/62.7% accuracy on OSReward-Hard versus GPT-5.5's 67.3% and Claude-Opus-4-8's 69.7%, at 30–60x lower cost (~$1.36 vs. ~$45–100 per 1,000 trajectories), and that transfer their de-biasing to three held-out benchmarks (OSWorld, AndroidWorld, WebArena).

## 核心贡献

- **构建OSReward**：从零搭建跨平台（Web/Windows/Ubuntu/移动）数据基础设施，收集1019条经三名标注员独立标注+meta-review仲裁的人工金标轨迹，衍生出聚焦疑难案例的OSReward-Hard（284条，人为拉高到30/70成功/失败比）以及细粒度打分子集OSReward-Multi（440条成功轨迹，含对齐度alignment与效率efficiency两个维度）。
- **迄今最全面的VLM判官评测**：对27个模型（涵盖OpenAI/Anthropic/Gemini/Qwen/Doubao/Kimi/Intern七大家族）做基准测试，发现"把未完成任务误判为成功"（over-accept）是所有模型家族共享的主导错误（占任一判官错误总数至少48%，过接受:过拒绝≈3:1），并通过输入消融实验定位到根因——判官主要依赖智能体自身的文字叙述而非画面：去掉逐步推理与动作文本平均拉低7.2个百分点准确率、翻转22.7%的判决，而改变截图数量/位置几乎不影响准确率。
- **发布开放语料OS-Shepherd-100K**：鉴于判官之间高度"扎堆"犯同样错误（判官两两一致性Cohen's κ≈0.71，多数投票无法纠偏），改用强判官集成一致性筛选（而非投票）从32万余条判官实例中提炼出约10万条高一致性、带推理过程标注的训练样本，是首个大规模带推理标注的CUA判官语料。
- **训练开源奖励模型OS-Shepherd（9B/35B）**：采用SFT+GRPO两阶段训练，专门瞄准最有害的"假成功"错误模式，在OSReward-Hard上以数十分之一的成本（每千条约$1.36）逼近商用判官表现，并在三个训练时从未见过的既有基准（OSWorld/AndroidWorld/WebArena，各自配有独立的人工编写验证器）上验证了这种去偏能力可以迁移。

## Key Contributions

- **OSReward benchmark**: built from scratch on cross-platform (web, Windows, Ubuntu, mobile) infrastructure with 1,019 human-gold trajectories, triple-annotated with meta-review arbitration on disagreements; derives OSReward-Hard (284 trajectories, deliberately skewed to a 30/70 success/fail split) for hard-case diagnosis and OSReward-Multi (440 successful trajectories) for fine-grained alignment/efficiency grading.
- **Broadest VLM-judge evaluation to date**: benchmarks 27 models across seven families (OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini, Qwen, Doubao, Kimi, Intern) and finds a single dominant failure mode shared by every family — over-accepting an incomplete task as a success (≥48% of any judge's errors, over-accepts outnumbering over-rejects ~3:1) — traced mechanistically to judges leaning on the agent's own text narrative rather than the screen (dropping thought/action text costs 7.2pp accuracy on average and flips 22.7% of verdicts, while changing screenshot count/position barely moves accuracy).
- **OS-Shepherd-100K**: an open, reasoning-annotated training corpus distilled from >300K judge instances down to ~100K high-agreement samples, selected via cross-model ensemble agreement rather than majority voting (since judges "herd" — pairwise Cohen's κ≈0.71) — the first large-scale reasoning-annotated CUA judge corpus.
- **OS-Shepherd reward models (9B, 35B)**: trained with a two-stage SFT+GRPO recipe that specifically targets the false-success failure mode; on OSReward-Hard they approach commercial judges' balanced accuracy at 30–60x lower cost (~$1.36 per 1,000 trajectories vs. ~$45–100 for GPT-5.5/Claude-Opus-4-8), and their de-biasing transfers to three held-out benchmarks (OSWorld, AndroidWorld, WebArena) with their own independent human-written verifiers.

## Methodology

**Data infrastructure and benchmark construction.** OSReward is built on dedicated cross-platform environments (web via a Playwright/Chromium fleet with anti-bot hardening; Windows with ~20 everyday apps and up to 100-step rollouts; Ubuntu with ~30 apps mixing GUI and CLI actions; Android with seeded files, usage records, and distractor content). Annotators write instructions after exploring each environment (~1,500 candidates authored, ~800 survive peer cross-checking for ambiguity/groundedness). Trajectories are rolled out by executing agents built on four model backbones (Claude, Gemini, Kimi, Qwen families) under a ReAct-style loop, then screened by an automatic pre-filter (removes anti-bot blocks, network failures, frozen runs) before every surviving trajectory is independently labeled by three human annotators; disagreements escalate to a two-reviewer meta-review (deliberation, not majority vote). Successful trajectories are additionally scored on alignment (task-intent match) and efficiency (wasted-step count), each on a 3-class scale, forming OSReward-Multi.

**Judging protocol.** Every VLM judge is evaluated under one fixed protocol: it reads the trajectory's last N=5 states (screenshots + per-step thought/action text), with no task-specific harness, tool access, or step-level supervision, and returns a SUCCESS/FAIL verdict (plus alignment/efficiency ratings on OSReward-Multi trajectories). Metrics are success recall (sRec), fail recall (fRec), and their mean (balanced accuracy, BalAcc), chosen specifically so the 43/57 success/fail class mix cannot inflate raw accuracy.

**Diagnostic ablations.** The authors perturb one input component at a time — visual inputs (trailing-screenshot count/position, presence of a red click marker), text inputs (dropping per-step thought and/or action text), and inference-time settings (thinking effort, decoding temperature, majority-vote ensembling) — to isolate what actually drives a verdict, using decoding-noise re-runs (T=0.7 flips 6–9% of verdicts) as a reference scale for how much any given ablation's effect exceeds noise.

**OS-Shepherd-100K construction.** Starting from ~100K raw instructions (self-collected plus filtered public corpora: OpenCUA, OpenMobile, ScaleCUA, OS-Genesis), instructions and trajectories are filtered down to ~82K final trajectories, each scored by up to four independent strong-judge runs (varying both judge model and screenshot setting) yielding 321,631 judge instances; only trajectories where the ensemble reaches high, near-unanimous agreement are retained (~85% agreement-filter pass rate), producing ~100K reasoning-annotated samples where the retained reasoning trace is inherited from one strong agreeing judge (falling back to another agreeing judge's reasoning when the primary judge's verdict disagrees with the retained label).

**Training.** OS-Shepherd starts from Qwen3.5 (9B and 35B-A3B) and is trained in two stages: an SFT stage on the 96.6K agreement-filtered samples (which mainly corrects the untrained base model's near-total leniency), followed by an RL stage using GRPO that specifically mines and targets false-success cases — the failure mode SFT alone leaves latent (recoverable only under repeated sampling) rather than fixed.

## Results & Highlights

- **Frontier judges collapse on hard cases.** On the OSReward full set, Claude-Opus-4-8 is the top judge at 89.7% binary accuracy (sRec 91.1, fRec 88.9, BalAcc 90.0); on OSReward-Hard the same judge drops to 69.7% accuracy — a ~20pp collapse — while GPT-5.5 falls from 89.5% to 67.3% and Claude-Opus-4-6 from 89.5% to 67.3%. The mean judge across all 27 models falls to just 52% on OSReward-Hard, barely above the 50% coin-flip floor implied by its 30/70 success/fail split.
- **A specific weak judge illustrates the leniency extreme.** Qwen3-VL-30B reaches only 69.4% accuracy on the full set and 31.4% on OSReward-Hard, with fail recall (fRec) of just 1.5% on Hard — i.e., it accepts almost every genuinely failed hard trajectory as a success — versus the strictest judge, GPT-5.2, which trades this off the other way (fRec 92.2 but sRec only 73.0 on the full set).
- **OS-Shepherd narrows the reliability-cost gap.** OS-Shepherd-9B reaches 86.1% accuracy on the full OSReward set (BalAcc 86.3) and 60.2% accuracy on OSReward-Hard (BalAcc 61.9, fRec 57.6%), moving it from the bottom third of the field (its untrained Qwen3.5-9B base scores 76.7%/39.4% on full/Hard, with Hard fRec of only 14.1%) into the mid-tier commercial band, at an estimated cost of ~$1.36 to judge 1,000 trajectories. OS-Shepherd-35B-A3B reaches 85.6%/62.7% (BalAcc 64.3) on full/Hard — only ~2.4pp better balanced accuracy than the 9B despite 4x the parameters.
- **Cost gap is large and specific.** Reliable judges (Claude-Opus-4-8, GPT-5.5) cost roughly $100 and $45 respectively to judge the full OSReward-Hard set once, while the cheapest sub-$3 judges fall to ≤50% accuracy; OS-Shepherd sits nearest the Pareto frontier at its price point, closing the frontier gap at an estimated 30–60x lower cost. At training scale, a single modest RL run (200 updates, batch 16, 16 rollouts = 51,200 judge calls) would cost ~$4,000 with Claude-Opus-4-8 or ~$2,300 with GPT-5.5, versus ~$68 with OS-Shepherd-9B.
- **De-biasing transfers to held-out benchmarks.** Evaluated against each benchmark's own human-written verifier on three independent CUA benchmarks never used in training (OSWorld, AndroidWorld, WebArena), OS-Shepherd is the best open judge on OSWorld and AndroidWorld and sits in the frontier cluster on WebArena, beating Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (~44x its parameter count) on all three. On WebArena specifically — where general lenient judges score below the trivial all-FAIL baseline — OS-Shepherd-9B reaches a failure-recall of 0.85 versus a median Qwen-judge failure-recall of 0.66.
- **Quality grading (OSReward-Multi) remains weak for everyone.** Even the best judge on this axis, GPT-5.5, reaches only 66.7 AUC / 63.5% macro-recall on the combined alignment+efficiency metric; OS-Shepherd-35B-A3B reaches 60.7 AUC and the 9B only 58.5, both trailing GPT-5.5 and Claude-Opus-4-8.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** The paper's central move — systematically measuring whether VLM-as-judge is reliable specifically for long-horizon, multimodal CUA trajectories, rather than for text (MT-Bench-style) or single-turn multimodal QA — is a genuinely underexplored angle, and the controlled input-ablation methodology (isolating text vs. vision contribution to a verdict) gives the "leniency bias" finding real mechanistic teeth rather than just an observed correlation.

**Rigor.** The ablation suite is a strength: separating aggregate-accuracy stability under visual perturbations from the much larger effect of text ablations, and using resampling noise (6–9% flip rate at T=0.7) as an explicit reference scale for how much any intervention actually matters, is good experimental hygiene. The three-annotator-plus-meta-review labeling process (rather than majority vote) for the human-gold set is also a defensible design choice given the paper's own finding that judges/annotators can herd.

**Reproducibility.** Code, benchmark, the 100K training corpus, and both model checkpoints are stated to be released, which is a real strength for a benchmark paper. However, the meta-review process for OSReward-Hard re-verification ("two senior reviewers... deliberation, not majority vote") is not a mechanized procedure, so third parties cannot exactly reproduce the specific hard-case selection, only approximate it.

**Significance and red flags.**
- *Label circularity in the training corpus*: OS-Shepherd-100K's retained reasoning trace "defaults to one strong judge's reasoning," and the success/fail label itself comes only from cross-judge agreement, not fresh human verification — meaning the 100K-sample training corpus could still bake in the very correlated judge blind spots the paper documents (Cohen's κ≈0.71 "herding"), just filtered down to the cases where several judges agree on the same (possibly still wrong) answer. The paper does not audit a sample of OS-Shepherd-100K against human labels to quantify this risk.
- *Scale mismatch in the "open-weight gap" framing*: the claim that open-weight judges "narrow the gap to within 4pp of the lead" rests on Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, a model dramatically larger than OS-Shepherd-9B/35B — a fair comparison but one that somewhat undersells how compressed OS-Shepherd's own cost-accuracy trade-off is relative to same-size open alternatives.
- *No isolated SFT-only ablation on OSReward-Hard in the main results table*: Table 4 reports only the untrained base model vs. the final SFT+RL model, so the RL stage's specific marginal contribution to the false-success fix is asserted qualitatively ("SFT resolves many under repeated sampling... RL catches the latent cases") rather than shown as a controlled three-way comparison.
- *Quality-axis grading is weak across the board*, including for OS-Shepherd itself, which the paper honestly reports but which does undercut the "fine-grained OSReward-Multi" contribution as an immediately deployable signal.
- *Small per-cell diagnostic breakdowns*: the platform-level failure analysis (Windows hardest at 42.4% mean accuracy, mobile easiest at 58.3%) is drawn from a fairly small OSReward-Hard set (Windows n=29, mobile n=63), so these specific platform rankings should be read as suggestive rather than conclusive.

Overall this is an intellectually honest, well-instrumented paper that does not oversell its own reward models (it explicitly states OS-Shepherd still trails frontier accuracy and only closes the cost gap), but the training corpus's reliance on judge-ensemble agreement rather than fresh human checks is a real limitation worth flagging for anyone building on OS-Shepherd-100K directly.

## Strengths

- Realistic, live cross-platform environments (not reused benchmark trajectories) with genuine three-annotator human labeling plus meta-review, avoiding the "judge error confounded with rollout quality" problem the paper explicitly identifies in prior work.
- Mechanistic (not just observational) diagnosis of the leniency bias via targeted text-vs-vision input ablations.
- A directly actionable cost-accuracy Pareto-frontier framing (Fig. 2) for practitioners choosing a judge under a budget.
- Transfer evaluation on three independent, non-overlapping held-out CUA benchmarks (OSWorld, AndroidWorld, WebArena), each with its own human-written verifier — a meaningfully harder generalization test than in-distribution evaluation.
- Full release of code, benchmark, the 100K-sample training corpus, and both trained model checkpoints.

## Limitations

- The alignment/efficiency quality axis (OSReward-Multi) is weakly graded by every judge, including OS-Shepherd, so it functions more as a diagnostic than a ready-to-use secondary reward signal.
- OS-Shepherd's training labels come from VLM-judge ensemble agreement, not fresh human annotation, carrying forward some risk of correlated judge bias despite the agreement filter.
- OS-Shepherd still trails frontier judges' raw balanced accuracy on OSReward-Hard (60.2–64.3 vs. 67.0–69.7); it closes most of the cost gap, not the full accuracy gap.
- The RL stage's mechanics and its isolated marginal contribution (vs. SFT alone) are less thoroughly detailed/ablated in the main text than the benchmark construction and SFT stage.
- The paper explicitly leaves two natural leniency-bias mitigations — prompting judges to verify completion explicitly, and soft-label confidence-weighted reward modeling — as untested future work.

## Relevance

该工作对研究计算机使用智能体（CUA）、GUI agent 强化学习奖励建模、以及"LLM/VLM-as-judge"可靠性评测的读者高度相关，提供了可直接复用的开源奖励模型（OS-Shepherd-9B/35B）与标注语料（OS-Shepherd-100K），可用于自建CUA强化学习训练或轨迹质量筛选流水线。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would explicitly prompting judges to "verify task completion against the environment state" (rather than implicitly trusting the agent's closing claim) meaningfully reduce the false-success bias, as the authors speculate but leave untested?
- How much of OS-Shepherd-100K's labels are wrong due to correlated ensemble-judge bias, and would a small fresh human audit of a random sample meaningfully change the picture?
- Would confidence-weighted soft-label training (mentioned as future work) outperform the current hard-label, agreement-filtering approach, particularly for the ambiguous middle cases currently discarded entirely?
- Does the same recipe scale to a setting with live environment/tool access during judging (rather than a fixed last-5-screenshot static protocol), which could plausibly close more of the remaining accuracy gap to frontier judges?
- How does OS-Shepherd's reliability hold up when used as a live reward signal inside an actual RL training loop, where policy optimization pressure might specifically seek out and exploit its residual blind spots (reward hacking), as opposed to being evaluated as a static judge on fixed trajectories?
