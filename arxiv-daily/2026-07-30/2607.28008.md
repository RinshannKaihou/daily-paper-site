> **一句话总结：** RepBench 把 13,427 篇基准论文压成 182 类能力分类体系，再从 353 个公开基准中抽取 46,149 条经审计探针文本（94 个能力、每个至少两个独立基准背书），在跨基准留出（LOBO）协议下对 12 个开源模型评测四种读出方法，发现均值-差（diff-mean）平均 AUC 最高（0.778），但逻辑回归（LR）在单个"能力×模型"格子上赢得最多（38%），而聚类得到的能力结构与人工 13 大类分类体系几乎不吻合（ARI≈0.1）。

> **TL;DR:** RepBench compiles 353 public benchmark datasets (46,149 audited probe texts across 94 capabilities, each backed by ≥2 independent benchmarks) into a reusable data layer for capability-direction probing, replacing the synthetic per-paper corpora most representation-engineering work relies on. Under a leave-one-benchmark-out (LOBO) transfer protocol across 12 open-weight models (Qwen3 0.6B–32B, Qwen3.5-9B, Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2/4, an R1-distilled variant, DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Base) and four readouts, the training-free difference-in-means baseline attains the highest grand-mean AUC (0.778) over 1,128 capability×model cells, beating PCA (0.734), logistic regression (0.769), and a whole-token J-Lens verbalizer (0.650) — yet LR still wins the largest share of individual cells (38% vs. diff-mean's 30%). A checkpoint-aligned Gemma Scope SAE readout trails all three matched-baseline methods (0.678 at the best layer). Benchmark-pooled capability vectors show an interior clustering optimum at k=4–15 across all 12 models but low agreement with the human-authored taxonomy (ARI 0.05–0.13).

## 核心贡献

- **构建"数据层"而非新的读出方法**：论文的核心主张是标准化表示探测的*数据轴*——爬取 13,427 篇基准论文，抽取 14,896 条能力描述并去重为 9,576 个概念，聚类为 182 个能力簇、归入 13 个大类（多模态接地、推理、代码调试、安全鲁棒性、规划工具使用等），形成一张"基准领域实际测量了什么"的数据驱动地图。
- **353 个基准数据集 → 46,149 条经审计探针文本**：对 182 簇中可用纯文本探测的 94 个能力簇，检索匹配公开数据集、LLM 语义匹配校验并人工复核，要求每个能力至少由两个独立基准支撑（中位数 3 个，最多 21 个）；两阶段审计从 378 个候选映射中剔除了 25 个（6.6%）不满足构念匹配的映射。
- **跨基准池化（pooling）揭示能力表示的几何结构**：原始逐文本向量在 12 个评测模型上均无自然聚类粒度，而按能力池化、基准均衡加权后的向量在所有 12 个模型上都出现小簇数（k=4–15）的内部聚类最优点，但聚类结果与人工 13 大类分类体系的调整兰德指数（ARI）仅 0.05–0.13，说明模型内部发现的能力组织方式与人类分类学并不一致。
- **留一基准法（LOBO）跨基准迁移评测协议**：对 12 个开源模型 × 4 种读出方法（diff-mean、PCA、逻辑回归 LR、J-Lens 语言化透镜）在 1,128 个"能力×模型"格子上评分——diff-mean 平均 AUC 最高（0.778），LR 单格胜出份额最高（38%），说明读出方法和聚合准则本身就是有意义的评测维度，而非只有唯一最优解。

## Key Contributions

- **An open-source, closed-loop data pipeline and corpus.** A reusable engine that incorporates new text benchmarks into audited per-capability representation data: 353 datasets, 46,149 probe texts, 94 capabilities, each supported by ≥2 independent benchmarks (median 3, max 21).
- **A capability taxonomy of the benchmark field.** 9,576 capability concepts extracted from 13,427 crawled benchmark papers, organized into 182 capability clusters across 13 families via embedding clustering (Qwen3-Embedding-8B) plus an LLM-audited finalization pass; the taxonomy also quantifies a coverage gap — 94 of 182 clusters are text-probeable, while 31 multimodal-grounding clusters and 23 planning/tool-use clusters have zero text-only coverage.
- **Benchmark-balanced representations via cross-benchmark pooling.** Raw per-text vectors show no natural cluster granularity on any of 12 evaluated models; after benchmark-balanced pooling (Eq. 1: average within each benchmark source, then give each source equal weight), an interior clustering optimum emerges at small cluster counts, consistently across all 12 models, but the discovered structure does not reduce to the human taxonomy (ARI 0.05–0.13).
- **A model × method evaluation under one protocol.** Using LOBO transfer, four readouts (diff-mean, PCA, LR, J-Lens) are evaluated on twelve common open-weight models; diff-mean has the highest grand mean (0.778) while LR wins the most individual capability–model cells (38%). A fifth readout (SAE-AUC, checkpoint-aligned Gemma Scope features) is evaluated separately on Gemma-2-9B-IT only, since aligned SAE dictionaries do not exist for the other eleven checkpoints.

## Methodology

**Taxonomy construction (Section 3.1, Appendix D).** The authors crawl 13,427 benchmark papers and extract 14,896 natural-language capability mentions (each paper's stated measurement target). Names/definitions are embedded with Qwen3-Embedding-8B; a 15-nearest-neighbor cosine graph merges near-duplicates at similarity ≥0.90 to deduplicate into 9,576 concepts. Global weighted k-means (k=200, 4 initializations) produces preliminary clusters, followed by an LLM refinement pass that names/defines clusters, flags outliers, and merges semantically close centroids (cosine ≥0.80), yielding the final 182-cluster, 13-family taxonomy.

**Corpus construction and audit (Section 3.2, Appendix E).** From the 182 clusters, 94 are selected as "text-probeable" by excluding families needing image/video/tool-use inputs and clusters where multimodal mentions exceed 40%, tool-dependence exceeds 50%, or long-horizon dependence exceeds 50%. For each of the 94 capabilities, candidate public datasets are matched via LLM judgment with semantic validation, then crawled and mapped to probe texts via heuristic schema mapping (378 dataset→capability mappings total). Each mapping passes a semantic audit: an ensemble consistency diagnostic (cross-model representation agreement) prioritizes 64 ambiguous mappings for manual review; 25 of 378 mappings (6.6%) are removed after independent human review confirmed they did not measure the claimed construct (Table 5 lists all removed mappings, e.g. `pitehu/SimBench` mapped to persona consistency at 9/10 votes against, `PALIN2018/BrowseComp-ZH` mapped to multilingual instruction following at 6/9 votes against). A second, targeted crawl (~3,200 texts) restores the two-benchmark-minimum invariant for capabilities left with only one source after the first audit pass.

**Cross-benchmark capability representations (Eq. 1, Section 4).** For model/layer (m, ℓ), let h_ℓ(x) denote the last-token hidden state of probe text x, standardized per dimension across the corpus. For capability c with benchmark set B_c, a benchmark-balanced vector is formed by first averaging within each source benchmark, then giving every benchmark equal weight across sources: v̄_c = (1/|B_c|) Σ_b (1/|X_{c,b}|) Σ_x h_ℓ(x), unit-normalized. This construction prevents any single large dataset from dominating a capability's representation.

**LOBO (leave-one-benchmark-out) evaluation protocol (Section 5).** Each (capability, model, method) cell is scored by holding out each of capability c's constituent benchmarks in turn: the direction is fit on positives from the *remaining* benchmarks plus a disjoint negative training split (negatives = texts sharing no capability with c, capped at 30 per dataset, ~6× the positive count), then scored by AUC on the held-out benchmark's positives against a disjoint test-negative half. The per-capability score is the median across folds. Four readout methods are compared at four fractional depths (25/50/75/100% of network depth, last-token activations), each summarized at its own best-observed valid depth:
- **Diff-mean**: unit-normalized difference in means between positive and negative training activations (training-free).
- **PCA**: top-10 principal components of pooled training activations, keeping and sign-orienting the component with best training AUC within each LOBO fold.
- **LR**: L2-regularized logistic regression (C=0.1) on the same training split.
- **J-Lens**: a separately fitted lens per model using a frozen set of whole-token capability verbalizers selected only on training benchmarks (Appendix H); compared only at 25/50/75% depth since its source representation must precede the final residual-stream target.
- **SAE-AUC** (Gemma-2-9B-IT only): selects and orients one checkpoint-aligned Gemma Scope 16k-feature SAE dimension per LOBO fold using only training benchmarks (Appendix G); not extrapolated to other checkpoints because aligned dictionaries don't exist for them.

Twelve open-weight models are evaluated: Qwen3 at 0.6B/1.7B/4B/8B/32B, Qwen3.5-9B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Gemma-2-9B-IT, Gemma-4-12B/31B, R1-Distill-Qwen3-8B (a controlled post-training contrast against its Qwen3-8B base), and DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Base (a 275B-scale fp8 MoE base model, probed without a chat template, parallel residual streams averaged).

## Results & Highlights

- **Grand-mean AUC over all 1,128 capability×model cells** (Table 3, Figure 4b): diff-mean 0.778, LR 0.769, PCA 0.734, J-Lens 0.650 — diff-mean is the strongest readout on average, consistent with AxBench's (Wu et al. 2025) finding that simple training-free baselines are hard to beat, now replicated on benchmark-grounded (not synthetic) cross-benchmark transfer data.
- **Per-cell win share** (Figure 4c): despite trailing on the mean, LR wins the largest share of individual capability–model cells at **38%**, versus diff-mean 30%, PCA 17%, and J-Lens 15% — the two aggregate views disagree on which method is "best," which the authors treat as evidence that readout method and aggregation criterion are themselves meaningful evaluation axes rather than settled choices.
- **Layer-matched SAE comparison on Gemma-2-9B-IT at L21** (Table 4, the SAE's aligned layer): SAE-AUC reaches only 0.678, versus diff-mean 0.779, LR 0.775, and PCA 0.729 on the same model/layer — the single-feature sparse-dictionary readout trails all three matched activation-space baselines.
- **Cross-benchmark clustering vs. human taxonomy**: pooled 94-capability vectors show an interior silhouette optimum at k=4–15 clusters on every one of the 12 evaluated models (e.g., Qwen3-0.6B peaks at k=15, Qwen3-4B at k=4, DSv4-Flash-Base at k=10), but adjusted Rand index against the 13 human-authored families is only **0.05–0.13** (~0.1) — models' internally-discovered capability groupings do not recover the taxonomy the field uses to organize its own literature.
- **Readability is highly capability-dependent, not just model-dependent**: 30 of 94 capabilities are strongly readable (diff-mean AUC ≥0.8) on *every* one of the 12 models (e.g., specification-to-code synthesis 0.99, stepwise reasoning fidelity 0.99, faithful explanation and rationale generation 0.99, domain knowledge recall 0.98, personalized user context integration 1.00), while 4 capabilities are weak (AUC <0.65) on every model: contamination-robust generalization (0.35), prompt-and-context robustness (0.50), faithful condensed summarization (0.38), and argumentative reasoning and persuasion (0.26) — the authors note the first two are defined by input perturbations rather than anything visible in a single prompt, and the latter two hinge on output quality rather than prompt content.
- **Pairwise consistency across models**: per-capability diff-mean AUC profiles correlate strongly across the 12 models (pairwise Spearman rank correlation of the 94 scores ranges 0.62–0.92, median 0.82), suggesting readability is substantially a property of the *data/construct*, not idiosyncratic to any one checkpoint.
- **External validity spot-check (Table 2)**: RepBench's benchmark-grounded AUCs are broadly consistent with (though not directly comparable to, due to differing protocols) prior task-specific studies — e.g., RepreGuard reports 0.9492 average AUROC for AI-text detection (RepBench: "close" construct match), harmful-prompt detection reaches 0.94±0.02 in prior work, and hallucination detection ranges 0.8436 (Gemma-2) to 0.7603 (Llama-3) in ICR Probe, versus RepBench's own factual-consistency-and-hallucination-detection cluster.
- **No quantitative causal-steering results are reported** — the paper is explicitly scoped to *reading* (probe/direction quality via AUC), not steering efficacy; this is stated as a limitation rather than a result.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The core contribution is not a new probing/steering algorithm but a benchmark-grounded *data layer* — replacing synthetic per-paper probe corpora with 353 audited public datasets under a shared multi-benchmark-per-capability invariant. This is a meaningful and underserved gap (the paper's own related-work review shows essentially every reading/steering method it cites — diff-mean, PCA, SAE, J-Lens, and others — was validated on data synthesized for that single paper). The taxonomy-building pipeline (crawl → cluster → LLM-audit → recrawl) is a reasonable, previously undocumented approach to this problem.

**Rigor**: The two-stage audit (ensemble-consistency prioritization + independent human votes) is a genuine strength, and the paper is transparent about its own audit's imperfection — reporting exactly which 25 of 378 mappings were removed and why (Table 5), including split vote counts (e.g., 6/9, 6/6) rather than hiding disagreement. The LOBO protocol is a sensible stricter-than-random-split design (the paper explicitly notes that random within-capability splits reach AUC near 1.0 almost everywhere because a direction can exploit dataset-specific fingerprints, so it deliberately reports only the harder cross-benchmark transfer numbers). One methodological wrinkle: PCA's reported AUC allows sign-orientation to be chosen using training-fold AUC — a design choice that gives PCA a supervised assist unlike a purely unsupervised direction-discovery method, though this is disclosed rather than hidden.

**Reproducibility**: The paper states pipeline, corpus, and evaluation code are released, which — if the artifacts match what's described — is commendable for a corpus-construction paper. However, the extracted PDF pages available to this review do not show the actual code/data repository URL, so this could not be independently verified from the paper text alone.

**Significance**: The finding that cross-benchmark pooled clustering does *not* recover the human 13-family taxonomy (ARI ≈0.1, consistent across all 12 models) is a genuinely interesting negative result — it suggests models' internal capability organization and the field's benchmark taxonomy are measuring different things, which has implications for anyone using benchmark categories as a proxy for "capability" in interpretability work.

**Red flags / weaknesses worth naming**:
- The SAE readout is evaluated on **only one model** (Gemma-2-9B-IT) because Gemma Scope dictionaries are checkpoint- and layer-specific and no aligned SAE exists for the other 11 checkpoints (including the newer Gemma-4 models actually used in the main grid) — so the paper's claim that SAE trails the three activation-space baselines rests on a single-model, single-layer comparison, explicitly flagged by the authors themselves as non-extrapolable ("we do not extrapolate this result to the full model pool").
- The J-Lens verbalizer ablation (Table 8b) shows the whole-token-vs-fragment-fallback choice swings mean capability-level AUC by up to **5.1 points on a single model** (Qwen3-32B: 0.610 whole-token vs. 0.660 fragment), while the macro-average delta across all models is only −0.002 — i.e., verbalizer design can matter substantially for individual models even though it washes out on average, which somewhat undercuts confidence in J-Lens's reported 0.650 grand mean as a stable number.
- DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Base is probed "without a chat template and with its parallel residual streams averaged" — a bespoke adaptation for one architecture that is not applied uniformly to the other 11 models, introducing a potential apples-to-oranges element into the 12-model comparison.
- The taxonomy explicitly cannot cover 88 of 182 clusters (31 multimodal-grounding + 23 planning/tool-use + others) with text-only probes — nearly half the capability landscape the paper itself mapped is left for "future work," so the 94-capability corpus, while carefully audited, represents less than the full scope the introduction's Figure 2 landscape visually implies.
- No causal/steering evaluation is included despite representation *steering* being named as a co-equal target audience in the abstract and introduction — the paper only evaluates reading (probe direction quality via AUC), leaving open whether these same benchmark-grounded directions are effective steering vectors.

## Strengths

- Large-scale, systematically audited corpus (353 datasets, 46,149 texts, 94 capabilities) built from a transparent, repeatable crawl→cluster→audit→recrawl loop rather than one-off manual curation.
- Explicit multi-benchmark-per-capability invariant (≥2 independent sources, enforced end-to-end, with a targeted recrawl closing gaps) directly addresses the single-dataset-fingerprint concern that undermines random-split evaluations.
- Transparent reporting of audit failures (Table 5, with vote counts) and of methodological limitations (SAE single-model scope, verbalizer sensitivity, no causal steering) rather than only reporting favorable results.
- The LOBO protocol and complementary aggregate views (grand mean vs. per-cell win share) surface a genuinely informative disagreement (diff-mean wins on average, LR wins more individual cells) instead of declaring a single winner.
- External validity table (Table 2) cross-references RepBench's cluster-level AUCs against independently published task-specific AUROC numbers, adding a sanity check beyond the paper's own internal metrics.

## Limitations

- Text-only scope: 88 of the 182 taxonomy clusters (all of "multimodal grounding" and "planning & tool use," 0/31 and 0/23 clusters respectively) are excluded because their constructs require image/video/agentic-trajectory inputs, explicitly deferred to future work.
- SAE comparison covers only one model/one aligned layer set (Gemma-2-9B-IT), by the authors' own admission not generalizable to the full 12-model pool.
- No causal intervention or steering-efficacy evaluation; the paper measures direction *readability* (AUC), not whether these directions successfully steer generation.
- Last-token, single-hidden-state readout only; the paper does not explore alternative token positions or multi-token aggregation, which it flags as an open direction for distinguishing genuine construct failures from readout-artifact failures.
- Model pool is entirely open-weight (0.6B–32B dense, plus one ~275B MoE base model); no frontier proprietary models (GPT, Claude, Gemini) are evaluated, limiting claims about generality across the full capability spectrum of deployed LLMs.

## Relevance

对于关注"如何用可复现、非合成数据评测激活方向读出/转向方法"的研究者，RepBench 提供了一个可直接复用的基准语料和跨基准留出协议，尤其适合替代 AxBench 等工作中依赖的单论文合成对照数据；其"池化聚类与人工分类体系不一致（ARI≈0.1）"的发现也为可解释性工作中"用基准类别代理模型内部能力结构"的常见做法敲响警钟。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the same benchmark-grounded directions (diff-mean, LR) that read capabilities well also *steer* generation effectively toward/away from those capabilities, or does readability decouple from steerability as some diagnostic studies (cited as Billa 2026) suggest?
- How would results change if SAE dictionaries aligned to the newer Gemma-4/Qwen3 checkpoints were available — would the sparse single-feature readout remain the weakest method, or does that conclusion depend on Gemma Scope's specific training recipe?
- Does the low ARI (≈0.1) between model-discovered clusters and the human taxonomy reflect models organizing capabilities along axes the benchmark literature hasn't named yet, or simply noise from probing only 94 (of 182) capabilities with last-token activations at fixed depths?
- Would extending the corpus to the 31 multimodal-grounding and 23 planning/tool-use clusters (via paired image/video/trajectory data) preserve the same diff-mean-wins-on-average-but-LR-wins-more-cells pattern, or would readout rankings change once inputs require multi-step context rather than single text probes?
- How sensitive is the 6.6% audit-removal rate to the specific LLM judge used for the semantic consistency diagnostic — would a different auditing model flag a substantially different set of mismapped datasets?
