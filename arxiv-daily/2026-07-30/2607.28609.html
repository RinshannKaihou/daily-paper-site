Computer-use agents · reward model reliability

AI judges of computer-use agents mistake failure for success — and collapse from 90% to 70% accuracy on hard cases.

OSReward: Instituting Standardized Evaluation for Cross-Platform Computer-Use Reward Models

Qiushi Sun, Kanzhi Cheng, Yian Wang, Bowen Yang, et al. (23 authors) · 30 Jul 2026 · computer-use-agents reward-models vlm-as-judge gui-agent-benchmark reinforcement-learning
Key metric
89.7% → 69.7%

Claude‑Opus‑4‑8 — the single best judge tested — loses ~20 points of balanced accuracy moving from OSReward's full set to its Hard subset.

Mean of all 27 judges on Hard: 52% — barely above the 50% coin-flip floor.
Why this matters

If you're building or shipping RL reward models for computer-use agents, your judge may look reliable in aggregate while silently rubber-stamping failed tasks as successes.

Critical edge

OS-Shepherd's training labels come only from cross-judge agreement, not fresh human checks — so its 100K-sample corpus may bake in the very correlated blind spots it was built to fix.

Methodology

  • Live, not reused, environments. Purpose-built web (Playwright/Chromium), Windows (~20 apps), Ubuntu (~30 apps, GUI+CLI), and Android sandboxes — avoiding the "judge error confounded with stale rollouts" problem of recycled benchmark trajectories.
  • Human-gold labels, not majority vote. 1,019 trajectories from four agent backbones (Claude, Gemini, Kimi, Qwen), each independently labeled by 3 annotators; disagreements go to a two-reviewer meta-review, not a vote.
  • One fixed judging protocol. Every VLM judge sees only the trailing 5 states (screenshots + step text) and returns SUCCESS/FAIL — no tools, no task-specific harness, so all 27 judges are compared apples-to-apples.
  • Ablate one input at a time. Toggling screenshot count/position barely moves accuracy; dropping thought/action text costs 7.2pp on average and flips 22.7% of verdicts — judges lean on the agent's own narration, not the screen.
  • OS-Shepherd-100K. 321,631 raw judge instances distilled to ~100K reasoning-annotated samples via cross-model ensemble agreement (not voting), then trained with SFT + GRPO specifically targeting false-success verdicts.

Strengths

  • Genuine live cross-platform rollouts with three-annotator human labeling + meta-review, not reused benchmark trajectories.
  • Mechanistic, not just observational, diagnosis of the leniency bias via targeted text-vs-vision ablations.
  • Actionable cost–accuracy Pareto framing for practitioners choosing a judge under a budget.
  • Transfer tests on three independent, non-overlapping benchmarks, each with its own human-written verifier.
  • Full release: code, benchmark, the 100K training corpus, and both model checkpoints.

Limitations

  • OSReward-Multi's alignment/efficiency grading is weak for every judge tested, OS-Shepherd included — a diagnostic, not yet a usable signal.
  • Training labels come from VLM-judge ensemble agreement, not fresh human annotation — correlated bias risk is never audited against ground truth.
  • OS-Shepherd still trails frontier balanced accuracy on Hard (60–64% vs. 67–70%); it closes the cost gap, not the full accuracy gap.
  • The RL stage's isolated marginal contribution over SFT alone is asserted qualitatively, not shown as a controlled ablation.
  • Two natural fixes — explicit completion-verification prompting, confidence-weighted soft labels — are named but left untested.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Would explicitly prompting judges to verify completion against environment state (rather than trusting the agent's own claim) meaningfully cut the false-success bias?
  2. How many OS-Shepherd-100K labels are wrong due to correlated ensemble-judge bias — would a small fresh human audit change the picture?
  3. Would confidence-weighted soft-label training beat today's hard-label agreement filter, especially for the ambiguous middle cases currently discarded entirely?
  4. Does the recipe scale to live environment/tool access during judging, instead of a fixed last-5-screenshot static protocol?
  5. How does OS-Shepherd hold up as a live reward signal inside an actual RL loop, where policy optimization might hunt for its residual blind spots?
