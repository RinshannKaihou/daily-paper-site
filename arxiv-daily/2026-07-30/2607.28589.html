arXiv 2607.28589 Vision Transformers Post-Training Quantization Mixed-Precision Knapsack Optimization

Fragility-guided bit allocation beats SOTA by +9.6 AP on COCO detection — yet is the weakest mixed-precision method on ImageNet classification.

MixFrag: Fragility-Guided Mixed-Precision Post-Training Quantization for Vision Transformers
Md. Mehrab Hossain Opi, Robiul Islam Ryad, Md. Umar Faruk · 30 Jul 2026
KUET, Bangladesh — no established ViT-quantization track record visible from the paper; builds directly on the AdaLog / FIMA-Q line (Wu et al.) as backend and strongest baseline. No public code linked.
Key Metric
+9.6 AP
COCO, MP3/MP3 (weight/activation 3-bit): MixFrag 40.9 avg AP vs. previous-best mixed-precision LRP-AQViT 31.3 avg AP.
Why This Matters

A clean, KL-divergence-driven recipe for mixed-precision ViT quantization that transfers to dense prediction — but classification accuracy alone can't validate a quantization method.

Critical Edge

MixFrag is the worst-performing mixed-precision method on ImageNet-1K at every tested budget, trailing FIMA-Q by 29 points at 3-bit (71.29 vs 42.34).

Methodology

3-stage pipeline

Fragility estimation (offline)

Isolate each quantizable component — QKV, output proj, FC1/FC2 — quantize it alone to a candidate bit-width b ∈ {2…7} while everything else stays full-precision. Measure KL divergence between full-precision and single-component-quantized output logits over 1,024 ImageNet / 256 COCO calibration images. Produces a component × bit-width fragility matrix Ω.

MCKP bit allocation

Each component picks exactly one bit-width; framed as a Multiple-Choice Knapsack Problem minimizing total fragility under an average-bit budget. NP-hard in general, solved exactly via dynamic programming — no heuristic search.

AdaLog quantization

Allocation is handed to the existing AdaLog PTQ backend, which calibrates each component at its assigned precision via adaptive log quantization + bias reparameterization. LayerNorm and residuals stay untouched.

Models: DeiT-Ti/S/B, ViT-S/B, Swin-S/B (timm)  ·  Hardware: single RTX 4070  ·  Budgets: W3/A3, W4/A4, W6/A6

Ablation signals
  • MCKP vs. greedy (DeiT-Tiny): 31.91 vs 31.908 at W3/A3, 64.886 vs 64.826 at W4/A4 — the "global optimization" edge over a simple greedy heuristic is marginal.
  • Marginal in-context refinement (iterative re-estimate + reallocate) hurts accuracy at every budget — worst at W3/A3: 31.908 → 29.728 (>2pt drop) — contradicting prior work's assumption.
  • Fragility↔bit-width correlation (Table 5): Pearson 0.55 at W3/A3, weakening to 0.07 at W6/A6 — selective allocation matters most at aggressive budgets.

Strengths

  • Fragility metric is a direct output-distribution-shift signal, not an indirect proxy (Hessian, LRP, gradients).
  • Honest negative-result ablations: MCKP-vs-greedy gain is marginal; iterative refinement actively hurts.
  • Evaluates downstream COCO detection/segmentation transfer, not just ImageNet — reveals classification accuracy isn't a sufficient proxy for dense-prediction quantization quality.
  • Correlation and top-fragile-component analysis (Tables 4–5) give real transparency into what the allocator prioritizes.

Limitations

  • Underperforms every competing mixed-precision baseline on ImageNet-1K at all three tested budgets.
  • Fragility is estimated component-by-component in isolation; interaction effects when quantizing everything jointly are never modeled or validated.
  • COCO gains are large only at the most aggressive MP3/MP3 setting — at MP4/MP4 the edge over LRP-AQViT shrinks to +0.2 AP.
  • No runtime, memory, or wall-clock comparison against the "costly optimization" baselines (e.g. FIMA-Q) it claims to be more lightweight than.
  • No public code repository referenced, limiting reproducibility verification.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Would jointly modeling component interactions — instead of isolated single-component fragility — close the ImageNet-1K gap with Mix-AQViT/FIMA-Q?
  • Why does exact MCKP optimization barely beat greedy allocation — is the fragility landscape essentially near-separable for this cost structure?
  • Why does iterative marginal refinement, useful in prior work, actively hurt MixFrag — would joint (not isolated) re-evaluation avoid the degradation?
  • Does the COCO transfer advantage hold for other dense-prediction tasks (semantic segmentation, depth), or is it specific to Mask R-CNN/Swin?
  • What is the actual compute cost of the C×B isolated forward passes for fragility estimation, versus the "costly" FIMA-Q optimization it's compared against?
