> **一句话总结：** 论文证明"精确删除"是记忆表示的属性而非算法选择——在把 Gemma-3 的全局注意力替换成可逆的支持向量记忆后，删除操作（decrement）与"从未摄入该记录"的重构在 1B 模型上于输出端 KL 中位数仅 5.4×10⁻¹⁵（+2.0% 困惑度代价），但同一套配方的效用代价在 4B/12B 分别膨胀到 11.2%/44.3%；而在 48B 的 Kimi Linear 混合模型中，delta-rule 循环写入会让 12–49% 的记录贡献变得依赖后续输入而无法做定点减法，必须靠"检查点回放（checkpoint-and-replay）"在最长 18,842 token 的真实临床记录上做到与"从未摄入"逐比特相同。

> **TL;DR:** The paper argues exact deletion from an LLM's persistent memory is determined by the memory's representation, not by which unlearning algorithm you pick. On a Gemma-3-1B model retrofitted with an addressable support-vector memory, an algebraic decrement matches a retained-key refit (the "never-ingested" reference) at median output KL 5.4×10⁻¹⁵ over 31 deletions, at +2.0% WikiText-103 perplexity versus a matched fine-tuned control (21.18 vs. 20.76 ppl) — but the same recipe's utility cost balloons to +11.2% at 4B and +44.3% at 12B. In a 48B Kimi Linear hybrid, the delta-rule recurrence (KDA) makes 12–49% of a record's state contribution suffix-dependent (vs. ≤1.4×10⁻⁵ for additive writes), so exact deletion instead requires rewind-and-replay, which the authors show is bitwise identical to never-ingesting the record on logits and all 20 recurrent states, on real MIMIC-IV clinical notes up to 18,842 tokens.

## 核心贡献

- **提出"可寻址 vs. 不可寻址"的表示层判据**：定义了删除的反事实标准 f(q; D_j(B(R))) = f(q; B(R\j))，并证明加性写入（running sum）可被精确减法撤销，逐通道衰减（decay）可通过一个"账本修正项"撤销，而 delta-rule 的写入会读取并改写当前状态，从根本上破坏了记录级可分离性。
- **在真实预训练 LLM 上（而非训练一个新分类器）验证 MUNKEY 式"可删除性是架构属性"的论点**：把 Gemma-3 的 4 个全局注意力层替换成基于一类支持向量描述（one-class SVDD）的记忆层，用低秩恢复（LoRA rank-8, 1.49M 参数）挽回语言建模质量，再用 Cauwenberghs & Poggio (2000) 的增量/减量 SVM 算法做逆向精确解算。
- **区分"证书级"（float64 精确解算）与"行为级"（单精度 masked-refit 代理）两条证据链**：证书链在 1B 给出中位数 KL 5.4×10⁻¹⁵ 的精确等价；行为链在 ICUL、重新学习（relearning）、采样提取（Leak@k）、成员推断（LiRA）四类攻击下均与"从未摄入"基线不可区分。
- **揭示"效用代价随模型规模恶化"的负面缩放结果**：相同 rank-8 LoRA 恢复配方下，效用代价从 1B 的 +2.0% 攀升到 4B 的 +11.2%、12B 的 +44.3%（12B 甚至出现 rank-16 时训练发散），说明该支持向量记忆并非注意力的通用替代品。
- **在 480 亿参数的 Kimi Linear 混合模型（20 层 KDA + 7 层 MLA）上做"重建回放"作为不可寻址情形下的精确兜底**：对真实 MIMIC-IV-Ext-CDS 和 MIMIC-IV-Note 临床记录（最长 18,842 token），保存记录边界处的状态并回放后缀，在确定性 MLX 实现下与"从未摄入"在 logits 和全部 20 个循环状态上逐比特相同；同一机制还能实现"精确修订"（先撤销错误陈述、注入更正陈述、再回放）。

## Key Contributions

- **A representation-level criterion for exact deletion**, formalized as a counterfactual: deleting record r_j is exact when f(q; D_j(B(R))) = f(q; B(R\j)) up to a declared numerical tolerance. The paper shows additive recurrence is subtractable, diagonal decay is "ledger-correctable" via a running per-channel product, but the delta rule (as used in gated DeltaNet/KDA) violates record-wise separability because each write reads the state it is about to change.
- **Addressable deletion retrofitted into an existing pretrained LLM.** The four global-attention layers of Gemma-3-1B (5:1 local:global interleave) are replaced with a support-vector memory (one-class SVDD over context keys, gated by α coefficients from Ramesh 2026), recovered with an attention-transfer warm start plus a 1.49M-parameter rank-8 LoRA (0.149% of parameters) trained on 33M FineWeb-Edu tokens. Deletion is then a reverse incremental SVM solve (Cauwenberghs & Poggio, 2000) rather than retraining.
- **Two parallel evidentiary paths that are explicitly kept distinct**: a float64 "certificate" that measures numerical agreement between the decrement and a retained-key refit at the model's output, and a single-precision "masked-refit proxy" that is evaluated behaviorally under TOFU-based elicitation, relearning, sampling (Leak@k), and LiRA membership-inference attacks — the paper stresses that behavioral indistinguishability alone can never establish state equality.
- **A negative scaling result**: the same recipe's utility overhead (WikiText-103 perplexity gap vs. a matched, identically-trained control) grows from +2.0% at 1B to +11.2% at 4B to +44.3% at 12B, and doubling LoRA rank at 12B destabilizes training (perplexity 61.63, "diverged") while the control trains normally — explicitly flagged as evidence this is not a general attention replacement.
- **Exact deletion via checkpointed rewind-and-replay for non-addressable recurrent memory.** In a 48B Kimi Linear hybrid (20 gated-DeltaNet/KDA layers + 7 MLA attention layers, released 8-bit weights, inference-only), the paper measures that 12–49% of a record's state contribution is suffix-dependent under the full delta rule (vs. ≤1.4×10⁻⁵ for additive-only writes), then shows that saving state at record boundaries and replaying only the suffix reproduces "never-ingested" bit-for-bit on logits and all 20 recurrent states, including on real MIMIC-IV clinical records up to 18,842 tokens, and that the same mechanism supports exact record amendment (correction-in-place).

## Methodology

**Two model conversions, two deletion mechanisms, one counterfactual standard.** Given a record sequence R=(r₁,…,rₙ), memory builder B, and deletion operator D_j, the paper calls a deletion "exact" if the model's next-token distribution after D_j(B(R)) matches B(R\{j}) up to a declared tolerance (Eq. 1). A record is "addressable" if the memory retains enough record-local bookkeeping a_j that a decrement Dec(B(R), a_j) = B(R\{j}) exists without reprocessing other records (Eq. 2); recurrent writes are analyzed via the "victim's state contribution" δ_x(S) = B(P,x,S) − B(P,S) as a function of continuation S (Eq. 3) — additive writes make δ_x suffix-independent, per-channel decay makes it correctable by a running product, and delta-rule writes make it suffix-dependent (not correctable by a fixed receipt).

**Branch 1 — Gemma-3-1B/4B/12B, addressable memory.** The four global-attention layers (of a 5:1 local:global interleave) are replaced with a memory layer whose attention weights are coefficients α of a one-class SVDD fit (Tax & Duin, 2004) over context keys, with box constraint C=1/(νn), budget ν=0.3. Recovery has two stages: (1) attention-transfer — one log-space kernel-bandwidth parameter per grafted layer (4 total), 2,000 steps, MSE loss to match original global-attention output; (2) rank-8 LoRA on Q/K/V/O projections and the gate, 6,000 steps of LM cross-entropy on FineWeb-Edu (33M tokens, batch 8, seq len 512). A "matched control" (ungrafted model, identical LoRA budget/seed) isolates the graft's true cost from the shared fine-tuning gain. Deletion is executed by running the reverse of Cauwenberghs & Poggio's incremental/decremental SVM algorithm in float64 and injecting the resulting coefficients into the live (single-precision) forward pass; a decay foil (scaling the target's readout weight by γ=0.01) is used as an intentionally-approximate comparison to show the certificate can distinguish exact removal from mere suppression.

**Branch 2 — Kimi Linear-48B-A3B, non-addressable memory.** The publicly released 8-bit MLX weights are used inference-only (no training). Controlled continuations under three write rules (additive-only, +per-channel decay, +delta rule = KDA) measure suffix-dependence of a probed record's state contribution across three corpora (a synthetic ward-code set, MIMIC-IV-Ext-CDS, MIMIC-IV-Note). The exact-deletion fallback is: checkpoint recurrent/convolutional state at record boundaries, restore the checkpoint just before the victim, then replay only the suffix (record positions that follow) — cost scales with suffix length (O(suffix)) rather than full re-ingestion. A separate attention-only FISTA-gated masking diagnostic (masking the victim's positions in the 7 MLA layers) is used only to localize what direct-channel removal misses, and is explicitly not presented as a certified deletion method.

**Evaluation instruments.** TOFU forget10/retain90 fictitious facts (Maini et al., 2024) packed beyond the local attention window; MIMIC-IV clinical records under a PhysioNet credentialed-data agreement (local-only processing, aggregate-only reporting); attacks include ICUL (in-context unlearning prompt), gradient-ascent (GA) weight editing, relearning attacks (LoRA fine-tuning on benign related samples), Leak@k probabilistic extraction (Reisizadeh et al., 2025) with 200 temperature-1 samples per target, and full LiRA membership inference with 32 shadow context packings per side and 312 held-out tests per side.

## Results & Highlights

- **Gemma-3-1B certificate agreement**: over 31 support-token deletions, the float64 decrement matches the retained-key refit at **median output KL 5.4×10⁻¹⁵ (worst case 9.3×10⁻¹⁴)**, versus the decay foil's median KL 1.8×10⁻⁶ (worst remains three orders above exact's worst) — an ~11-order-of-magnitude gap between exact decrement and an approximate baseline that "looks" equally forgotten behaviorally.
- **Utility cost scaling (Table 1, WikiText-103 perplexity, matched control)**: recovered-vs-matched-control gap is **+2.0% at 1B (21.18 vs 20.76)**, **+11.2% at 4B (18.94 vs 17.08)**, and **+44.3% at 12B (19.39 vs 13.43)**; a rank-16 LoRA check at 12B destabilizes the grafted run to 61.63 ppl ("diverged") while the ungrafted control trains stably to 19.02 — explicit evidence the recipe is not a general attention replacement at scale.
- **Membership inference (full LiRA, Appendix Fig. 4f)**: the single-precision masked-refit proxy sits **at chance, AUC 0.499, TPR 1.3% at 1% FPR**, versus **present/never-deleted AUC 0.996 (92% TPR)**, ICUL AUC 0.989, and the decay foil AUC 0.533 — i.e., decay is nearly as detectable as never having deleted at all, while the proxy is indistinguishable from a genuinely never-ingested record.
- **Kimi Linear delta-rule suffix-dependence (Table 2, pooled over 3 corpora)**: additive-writes-only leaves ≤1.4×10⁻⁵ raw suffix-dependence (fully correctable); +per-channel decay raises this to .007–.109 raw but is correctable to ≤7.0×10⁻⁵ after a ledger correction; **+delta rule (=KDA) raises raw suffix-dependence to .116–.489, and even after applying the best decay-style correction, 9–49% remains uncorrected** — end-to-end full-model suffix-dependence is median .81–1.02 (range .21–1.24).
- **Replay exactness on real clinical records**: within the deterministic MLX implementation, every admitted MIMIC deletion is bitwise exact — **8/8 and 9/9 intake records exact at 236 and 3,248 tokens, and 4/4 discharge notes exact at 18,842 tokens**, with victim lift and retained-record drift both exactly 0.000; replay latency falls from 6.70s (oldest of 128 records) to 0.00s (newest), mean 3.49s versus 6.77s for a full rebuild — a real, measured cost/benefit versus full re-ingestion, not merely an asymptotic claim.
- **Leak@k probabilistic extraction (masked refit vs. ICUL, Gemma-3-1B, 20 targets)**: masked refit's per-target excess exposure over the never-stored floor is **−0.000 [−0.011, +0.007] at k=1 (95% bootstrap)**, i.e. statistically indistinguishable from never storing the fact, whereas **ICUL exceeds the floor at every sampling budget k** (e.g., teacher-forced stored-secret lift +1.50 nats for "present," +1.49 nats surviving after ICUL "deletion," vs +0.04 nats for masked refit) — showing the in-context "forget" instruction baseline leaves recoverable signal that the masked-refit approach does not.

**No quantitative results reported** does not apply — the paper is unusually quantitative throughout, with confidence intervals and bootstrap ranges reported for nearly every claim above.

## Authors & Credibility

The paper has a single author, Vishwajith Ramesh, PhD, affiliated with Vy Labs, Inc. (a company, not an academic institution), and cites a companion preprint by the same author ("Ramesh, 2026") for the support-vector memory primitive this paper builds on. This is an independent/industry single-author preprint (labeled "Preprint" throughout, not yet peer-reviewed or published at a venue), which is unusual for a paper making load-bearing claims about certified exact-unlearning guarantees. The experiments run entirely on one Apple-silicon workstation with fixed seeds; the base models used are public (Gemma-3-1B/4B/12B, Google DeepMind; Kimi Linear-48B-A3B, Kimi Team, released 8-bit MLX weights), and the paper states code, demos, and a reproducibility pipeline are released alongside the preprint. No independent replication or third-party review is available at this time.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The core conceptual move — treating "exact deletability" as a property determined by memory representation (addressable vs. not) rather than by algorithm choice, and formalizing this via a counterfactual receipt δ_x(S) — is a genuinely useful reframing of machine unlearning, and cleanly extends MUNKEY's (Laguna et al., 2026) architectural-deletability thesis from a purpose-built image classifier with an external exemplar bank to records already ingested into a pretrained autoregressive LLM's persistent state. The distinction between additive/decay/delta-rule writes as a taxonomy of "how forgettable" a recurrent memory is (Table 2) is a clean, testable idea specific to this paper, not boilerplate.

**Rigor**: The paper is careful to separate the float64 "certificate" (a numerical equality claim) from the single-precision "masked-refit proxy" (a behavioral baseline), repeatedly warning readers not to conflate the two — this is good practice, since many unlearning papers conflate "looks forgotten under my probe" with "is actually removed." The use of multiple attack families (ICUL, GA, relearning, Leak@k, LiRA) against the masked-refit proxy, with bootstrap confidence intervals and seed-replication checks (Appendix E, "seed replication" section), is more thorough than most unlearning benchmarks (TOFU/MUSE/WMDP) that the paper itself criticizes for being "shallow, reversible, and hard to measure."

**Reproducibility**: Explicitly strong on paper — fixed seeds, single deterministic workstation, released code (`gemma_sv`, `kimi_sv` modules), a one-command hero demo, and a stated MIMIC data-use discipline (aggregate-only reporting, no note text/identifiers/timestamps read beyond evaluated fields). However, the "Generality" limitation the authors themselves flag is real: recovery uses **one seed and one model family** on **one workstation**; cross-hardware bitwise equality and broader model families are explicitly untested.

**Significance / red flags**:
- The headline "near-parity" result (5.4×10⁻¹⁵ KL, +2.0% utility cost) is a **1B-scale-only** result — the paper's own Table 1 shows utility cost jumping to +44.3% at 12B, and a rank-16 run at 12B *diverges* (61.63 ppl) rather than merely underperforming. This is disclosed prominently by the authors (a genuine strength — no cherry-picking of scale), but it substantially weakens any claim that this SVM-graft is a practical deletion mechanism for realistically-sized deployed models; it is currently a proof-of-concept at 1B.
- The "exact decrement" is graft-specific: it only works because the authors *replaced* Gemma's global attention with a purpose-built deletable memory. It says nothing about deleting from the native attention/MLP weights of an off-the-shelf LLM (which the paper is explicit about — "we forget from the model's in-context memory... not from Gemma's native one").
- The KDA (delta-rule) "exact deletion" is not solved algebraically at all — it is solved by falling back to full record-boundary rewind-and-replay, a considerably more expensive and less novel mechanism (the paper itself concedes "checkpointing itself is elementary" and that its contribution is the *diagnosis* of where the algebraic approach fails, not a new replay algorithm). Replay cost is O(suffix length), which is favorable only when deletions happen early or infrequently; for old records near the end of a very long, continuously-growing context this degrades toward full-rebuild cost.
- The paper's Leak@k baseline comparison leans on ICUL as the weak baseline for the sampling/membership-inference attacks; ICUL is a known-weak prompt-level "forgetting" method (the model's weights/state are literally unchanged), so showing the certificate-based approach beats ICUL is a relatively low bar. A more informative comparison would be against a stronger weight-space unlearning baseline (e.g. a proper gradient-ascent-with-regularization method) rather than GA alone, which the paper shows collapses utility (−0.125 ± 0.030 retain extraction) and is easily reversed by benign relearning (+3.5 ± 2.2 nats) — a fairly weak baseline that is easy to beat.
- Attack surface deliberately excludes a "pre/post snapshot adversary" (an attacker who has access to model state before and after deletion) — the paper is explicit that this is out of scope for every method tested, which is a reasonable but nontrivial threat-model carve-out given the "exact deletion" framing (an adversary with snapshots could, in principle, diff the states directly).
- The independent-author, single-institution, unreviewed nature of the preprint (self-citing a companion preprint for the underlying SV-memory primitive) means the extraordinary precision claims (10⁻¹⁵ KL) have not yet been externally validated.

## Strengths

- Clear, falsifiable formalization of "exact deletion" as a counterfactual equality (Eq. 1) with an explicit, tighter notion of state equality distinguished from output equality.
- Honest, prominent disclosure of a negative scaling result (utility cost 2.0% → 11.2% → 44.3% from 1B to 12B, plus a divergence at 12B rank-16) rather than only reporting the favorable 1B number.
- Strong methodological separation between a numerically-certified claim (float64 decrement vs. retained-key refit) and a behaviorally-evaluated claim (single-precision masked-refit under attacks), which most unlearning papers conflate.
- Real, credentialed clinical data (MIMIC-IV-Ext-CDS, MIMIC-IV-Note) used for the replay-exactness demonstration, at realistic context lengths up to 18,842 tokens, with a stated data-handling discipline (local-only, aggregate-only).
- Broad attack suite (ICUL, GA, relearning, Leak@k, LiRA) with bootstrap confidence intervals and cross-seed replication, going beyond typical "single probe" unlearning evaluation.
- Demonstrates a genuinely useful secondary capability: exact record *amendment* (replace-then-replay), not just deletion, verified bitwise against a "corrected from the start" reference.

## Limitations

- Utility overhead is severe at larger scale (+44.3% perplexity at 12B) and the recipe destabilizes (diverges) under a straightforward capacity increase (rank-16 LoRA) at 12B — explicitly stated by the authors as "not a general replacement for attention."
- Scope is restricted to in-context/persistent state carried by the grafted memory layers; the method does not delete information already baked into pretrained weights, nor outputs already observed by users before deletion (explicitly out of scope, citing the "pre/post snapshot adversary").
- The retained-key certificate for Gemma leaves an "ingestion-time imprint" on neighboring records' cached encodings — measured separately (2.5×10⁻⁴–1.2×10⁻³ nats gap) but not eliminated without a ~100×-costlier full context repack.
- The KDA (delta-rule) finding is a negative/impossibility result for a *fixed* receipt (including a diagonal-decay-style correction), not a proof that no possible per-record tracking mechanism could work; explicit per-record influence tracking would forfeit the fixed-size-state benefit that makes recurrent architectures attractive in the first place.
- Support-vector memory solve/replay costs are nontrivial (41.4 MiB per record boundary for replay checkpoints in the Kimi study; a full one-head decrement at 1B is ~100× cheaper than a full refit but still adds real latency, 0.3s per whole-model deletion), and sparse-checkpoint storage tradeoffs are not evaluated.
- Generality is narrow: one seed, one model family per branch (Gemma-3 for the addressable branch, Kimi Linear for the non-addressable branch), one deterministic workstation/implementation (Apple-silicon + MLX); cross-hardware bitwise equality and broader architectures are untested.

## Relevance

该工作与大模型的持久化记忆/长上下文机制、机器遗忘（machine unlearning）和线性注意力/循环状态压缩（delta-rule、门控 DeltaNet 等）方向高度相关，其"删除是否可行取决于记忆表示"的框架，为评估 KV-cache 压缩、检索式记忆和混合注意力架构在合规删除（GDPR、临床数据撤回）场景下的可行性提供了一个可操作的判据和测试方法论，但其效用代价随模型规模急剧上升，距离在生产级大模型上实际部署仍有较大差距。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the utility-cost scaling failure (2.0% → 44.3% from 1B to 12B) stem from an inherent limitation of one-class SVDD memory capacity, from the fixed rank-8 LoRA recovery budget, or from an optimization instability specific to the grafted gate — and could scale-specific optimization (learning rate schedules, larger LoRA rank with different hyperparameters) close part of this gap, as the authors speculate but do not test?
- Could a richer (but still fixed-size) per-record receipt make the delta rule's suffix-dependence correctable, or is 12–49% suffix-dependence a fundamental consequence of any bounded-state overwrite rule (i.e., is there a proof, not just an empirical measurement, that no fixed receipt exists for the general delta rule)?
- How would this framework's addressable/non-addressable taxonomy apply to other popular linear-attention/SSM variants (Mamba-2, S4, Titans, RWKV) beyond the one KDA-based hybrid tested here?
- What is the actual cost/benefit crossover point for record-boundary replay versus full rebuild in a production system with millions of records and continuous ingestion — does replay cost degrade unacceptably for records deleted very late in an extremely long-lived context?
- Would a pre/post-snapshot adversary (explicitly excluded from this paper's threat model) trivially break the "exact deletion" guarantee by diffing model state before and after deletion, and if so, what would an authenticated-logging or attestation mechanism need to look like to close that gap, as the ethics statement gestures toward but does not design?
