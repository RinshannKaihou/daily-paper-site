Evaluation Methodology · Continuous-Time Dynamic Graphs

Sampled-negative MRR for CTDG link prediction depends on the negative sampler q and the candidate count K — not just the model being scored.

一句话总结：连续时间动态图的"采样负例 MRR"评测本身依赖负例分布 q 与候选数 K——换一种候选配置 会直接翻转模型排名和模块增益方向（Wikipedia 上 CRAFT-R 相对 CRAFT 的增益从 +0.0037 变为 −0.0266），因此建议改用全量实体排序作为架构比较的主要证据。

CRAFT-R's MRR gain over CRAFT on Wikipedia — positive with a confidence interval crossing zero under Expected-Uniform-20, negative with a CI entirely below zero under full-catalog ranking. Same checkpoints, same scorer, opposite conclusion.

Why This Matters

Any CTDG study using JODIE-style sampled MRR to rank models or ablate modules may be reporting an artifact of the negative sampler, not the architecture it claims to measure.

Critical Edge

The BPR-vs-BCE check anchoring the paper's causal story — the module-effect sign flip — was re-run only for CRAFT/CRAFT-R, not the other four models it evaluates.

01 Methodology

  1. 1
    Bayesian ranking framing. Given one true positive from p₊ and K negatives from q, the population-optimal score is the log-density ratio log p₊(v|x) − log q(v|x) (Eq. 3).
  2. 2
    Finite-K is a different statistic. Uniform q over a fixed catalog preserves the full-catalog Bayes-optimal order in expectation, but finite K samples only a nonlinear partial view of it — an exact closed-form E[RR_K|r,N] via a hypergeometric argument (Eq. 7).
  3. 3
    Pair-history membership isolated. A minimal scorer s_β(M)=b+βM on M(u,v,t)=1[(u,v)∈H_t] yields a closed-form optimal coefficient β*=logit(p₁)−logit(q₁) (Eq. 10), checked against a coefficient learned directly from data.
  4. 4
    72-run sweep. 6 models (CRAFT, CRAFT-R, DyGFormer, DyGFormer+LHA, TGN, GraphMixer) × 4 JODIE datasets × 3 seeds; most-recent 32,768 events per dataset, 85/15 chronological split, BCE training (plus a BPR retrain for CRAFT/CRAFT-R).
  5. 5
    Four diagnostics on shared checkpoints. A 2×2 repeated/new × seen/unseen crossing; an orthogonal-projection intervention removing the seen/unseen representation axis; Expected-Uniform-K vs. all-entity MRR across K∈{1,5,20,50,100}; and evaluation-cost profiling as the catalog grows.

02 Results

  • LastFM reversal — Expected-U20 ranks CRAFT (.4283) above DyGFormer (.4161); full-catalog MRR flips it — DyGFormer (.0811) and GraphMixer (.0922) both beat CRAFT (.0636).
  • Reddit stays stable — Kendall's τ = 1.000 between finite-K and full-catalog orderings at every K, vs. as low as .733 for LastFM/MOOC at K=1.
  • β*=learned β — the closed-form coefficient matches the empirically learned one to 3 decimals under random negatives (LastFM 3.464 = 3.464); flips to −8.606 under source-history sampling, as theory predicts (β*→−∞).
  • Loss flips it again — Wikipedia CRAFT-R's gain over CRAFT is −0.0266 under BCE but +0.0629 under BPR, same architecture, same protocol.

Feasibility of the fix: all-entity scoring costs 4.94–5.69 ms/event at 822–991 candidates, ~200 MB peak GPU memory; a stricter "time-available" catalog agrees with the fixed catalog within 0.0000–0.0054 MRR.

Strengths

  • Exact closed-form expected reciprocal rank under uniform finite-K sampling (Eq. 7, tie-handling in the Appendix) — not just an asymptotic or simulated argument.
  • A genuine mechanistic explanation, not just a re-measurement: pair-history membership is time-varying and injected directly into CRAFT, CRAFT-R, and DyGFormer+LHA's scoring paths.
  • Four independent lines of evidence converge — analytic/learned coefficient agreement, the 2×2 membership crossing, representation-ablation controls, and the full Expected-20-vs-full-catalog comparison.
  • Honest uncertainty reporting: seed-paired and time-block-bootstrap CIs on every headline number, including an explicit flag when a sampled-condition CI contains zero but the full-catalog CI doesn't.
  • Feasibility isn't just asserted — scoring latency, GPU memory, and a "time-available" catalog check are all measured for the proposed remedy.

Limitations

  • Tested only on four small, homogeneous JODIE datasets; the paper explicitly disclaims its cost measurement (≤991 entities) for million-entity catalogs.
  • The BPR-vs-BCE sensitivity check (Table IX) covers only CRAFT/CRAFT-R — unverified for DyGFormer+LHA, TGN, or GraphMixer.
  • Sampled-evaluation catalog coverage is below 100% on three of four datasets (MOOC .996, Reddit .996, Wikipedia .987) — a disclosed but real deviation.
  • The remedy only works for enumerable, fixed destination catalogs; it doesn't address open-world graphs with rapidly growing node sets.
  • No new model or training method is proposed — the paper doesn't resolve which architecture is "best," only that existing comparisons need re-running.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Would the same rank reversals and sign flips appear on larger, non-JODIE benchmarks (e.g. TGB), beyond the ~991-entity ceiling tested here?
  • Does the BPR-vs-BCE sign flip seen for CRAFT-R generalize to DyGFormer+LHA, TGN, and GraphMixer, or is it specific to its pair-recurrence feature?
  • What does an "all-entity" analogue look like for open-world graphs where the destination catalog isn't fixed or fully enumerable at test time?
  • Since all-entity scoring cost grows roughly linearly with catalog size, what hierarchical strategies could preserve its validity at million-node scale?
  • Could the closed-form Bayes-optimal framework (Eq. 3–7) yield a sampler-independent variance-reduction estimator, avoiding full-catalog scoring altogether?
