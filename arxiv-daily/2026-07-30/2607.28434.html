Key Metric

34/36

pre-registered directional cells matched the predicted sign, across 3 models × engineered + clinical texts — chance is 18/36 (AUC 0.5).

Why This Matters

A zero-training, single-forward-pass instrument reads a model's internal geometry and, for the first time, checks that reading against an independent psychoanalyst's clinical judgment — not a proxy metric.

Critical Edge

On the identical marked/unmarked split, a plain word-rarity baseline alone reaches AUC 0.875–0.878 — higher than any channel's raw score — and Qwen3-8B's clinical alignment fails both permutation nulls outright (p=0.21, 0.47).

Three models are scanned — phi-4 (14B, instruction-tuned), Qwen3-8B (instruction-tuned), and base Llama-3.1-8B — plus a matched Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct follow-up run to isolate what instruction tuning changes. Every hyperparameter (μ=0.7 core-subspace cutoff, τ=0.7 recruitment threshold) is frozen on one "discovery" text before any other text is touched, keeping the remaining 12-text corpus purely confirmatory.

A frozen battery of seven tests then runs on every model×text×view cell: an engineered register-boundary test, two-view correlation, "pin identity" classification of top anchors, a paragraph-binding test, a minimal-pair (A/B) protocol exploiting causal masking, rank-AUC scoring against expert annotation (with lexical baselines and two permutation nulls), and a type-essence transfer test. Ground truth is split into engineered texts (construction known) and two independently annotated tiers: a psychoanalyst's markings on clinical transcripts, and a domain expert's column labels on execution traces.

  • Two-view inversion: phi-4's WFA1 ratio flips from 3.04 (operative view) to 0.10 (quiet-dimensions view) — a >30× reversal, replicated more weakly in the base model (2.57 → 0.82).
  • Lexical floor: a same-split word-rarity baseline alone reaches pooled AUC 0.875–0.878 — above every channel's raw AUC (0.571–0.706); the paper residualizes against it rather than hiding the gap.
  • Null-test split: phi-4 clears both permutation nulls (p=0.0001, 0.0013); the base model clears only the block null (p=0.0003 vs. 0.103); Qwen3-8B fails both (p=0.21, 0.47).
  • Instruction-tuning effect: the matched Instruct twin raises pooled residualized clinical AUC from the base's 0.585 to 0.627, while type-transfer stays pinned at the base's 93rd-percentile regime — tuning adds reading fidelity without moving the underlying token-type geometry.
  • Execution-trace dissociation: phi-4 ranks "measurement" columns highest (AUC 0.83–0.84); Qwen3-8B inverts this exactly, favoring "identity" columns; the base model shows no such organization (flat 0.49–0.58).

Strengths

  • Training-free, single-forward-pass instrument at full per-token resolution — no probe training or fine-tuning required.
  • Careful confirmatory design: constants frozen on one discovery text before any other text is touched, replication counts reported per cell, and a public script recomputes every number from cached artifacts.
  • An unusually rich set of adversarial controls: lexical/rarity baselines, two permutation-null flavors, an attention-saliency rival instrument, delimiter-erasure checks, and a causal-masking sanity check (prefix identity floor of exactly 1.0).
  • Reports negative/mixed results candidly — Qwen3-8B's failed nulls, the base model's weakest clinical tracking — rather than only favorable cells.
  • Draws a falsifiable cross-instrument prediction against concurrent Anthropic circuits work (Gurnee et al. 2026's "global workspace" J-lens) for future validation.

Limitations

  • Entirely correlational: no causal intervention (e.g. patching an anchor's core subspace) shows the model actually uses what the channels read.
  • All analyses use a single teacher-forced pass; free-running generation is untested and may organize differently.
  • Very small, single-annotator corpus — 13 texts total, 4 clinical transcripts marked by one psychoanalyst with no second-annotator agreement check.
  • Only 3 base models plus 1 matched instruct pair — insufficient for factorial claims about scale, architecture, or alignment regime.
  • Type-essence transfer is tested only within register (clinical-with-clinical, technical-with-technical); cross-register transfer is untested.
  • The base model is simultaneously the paper's architectural control and its weakest clinical-tier performer, so cross-model generality leans on the two instruction-tuned models.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Would a causal intervention (patching an anchor's core subspace) confirm that "transported" content is actually used downstream, rather than merely traversed during the pass?
  2. Does the aggregator/differentiator framework generalize to larger models, other architectures (MoE, state-space), or free-running generation?
  3. Would the "34/36" result and Qwen3-8B's permutation-null failures hold up with a second independent clinical annotator?
  4. Can the cross-instrument prediction against Gurnee et al.'s (2026) J-lens "global workspace" be tested directly on an open-weight model?
  5. Does the type-essentialism-vs-discourse-fidelity trade-off (Fig. 7, the Llama base/instruct pair) hold across other model families and base/instruct pairs?
