Subtract or Replay? Exact Deletion from Language-Model Memory ↗
Vishwajith Ramesh · 30 Jul 2026 · Preprint · unreviewed · single author
exact-deletion support-vector memory delta-rule / KDA Gemma-3 Kimi Linear-48B

Key Metric — Certificate Agreement

5.4 × 10⁻¹⁵
Median output KL divergence: algebraic decrement vs. a "never-ingested" retained-key refit — Gemma-3-1B, 31 support-token deletions, at +2.0% WikiText-103 perplexity cost.
Same recipe's utility cost reaches +44.3% at 12B — see Results ↓

Why This Matters

Gives developers a concrete test for whether GDPR- or clinical-grade deletion is actually possible under KV-cache compression and linear-attention memory, before deployment.

Critical Edge

The headline 5.4×10⁻¹⁵ certificate is a 1B-parameter proof of concept only: the identical recipe's utility cost balloons to +44.3% perplexity at 12B, and doubling LoRA rank there makes training diverge outright (61.63 ppl) rather than merely underperform.

Methodology

Branch 1 — Gemma-3-1B/4B/12B (addressable)
4 global-attn layers→ swap for one-class SVDD support-vector memory (ν=0.3)→ attention-transfer warm start→ rank-8 LoRA (0.149% params)→ delete = reverse incremental/decremental SVM solve (float64)
Branch 2 — Kimi Linear-48B-A3B (non-addressable)
20 KDA + 7 MLA layers, 8-bit, inference-only→ probe suffix-dependence under 3 write rules→ delta rule breaks fixed-receipt correction→ fallback: checkpoint at record boundary → rewind → replay suffix only

A deletion counts as exact when the model's next-token distribution after deleting record rj matches the distribution as if rj had never been ingested, up to a declared tolerance. Additive writes are shown to be algebraically subtractable; per-channel decay is "ledger-correctable" via a running product; the delta rule reads and overwrites the state it's about to change, breaking record-wise separability — so Branch 2 falls back to rewind-and-replay instead of solving it algebraically. Evaluated with TOFU forget/retain sets and real MIMIC-IV clinical notes under a credentialed, aggregate-only data protocol.

Strengths

  • Falsifiable, counterfactual definition of "exact deletion" — output equality is explicitly distinguished from the tighter claim of state equality.
  • Discloses the negative scaling result prominently (+2.0% → +44.3%, plus a 12B divergence) rather than only reporting the favorable 1B number.
  • Keeps a numerically-certified claim (float64 decrement) strictly separate from a behaviorally-evaluated claim (masked-refit under attack) — most unlearning papers conflate the two.
  • Uses real, credentialed MIMIC-IV clinical data at realistic lengths (up to 18,842 tokens), with a stated local-only, aggregate-only handling discipline.
  • Broad attack suite — ICUL, gradient ascent, relearning, Leak@k, full LiRA — with bootstrap confidence intervals and cross-seed replication.
  • Also demonstrates exact record amendment (replace-then-replay), verified bitwise against a "corrected from the start" reference.

Limitations

  • Utility overhead is severe at scale (+44.3% ppl at 12B) and the recipe destabilizes under a straightforward rank-16 capacity increase — explicitly "not a general replacement for attention."
  • Only deletes from the grafted in-context memory layers, not from a model's native pretrained weights, nor outputs already seen by users before deletion.
  • Neighboring records retain an "ingestion-time imprint" (2.5×10⁻⁴–1.2×10⁻³ nats gap) not eliminated without a ~100×-costlier full context repack.
  • The KDA finding is a negative result for one fixed receipt, not proof that no per-record tracking mechanism could ever work.
  • Replay/solve costs are nontrivial (41.4 MiB per checkpoint boundary) and degrade toward full-rebuild cost for records deleted late in a long-lived context.
  • Generality is narrow: one seed, one model family per branch, one deterministic Apple-silicon/MLX workstation — cross-hardware equality is untested.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Does the 2.0%→44.3% utility-cost blowup come from SVDD memory capacity, the fixed rank-8 LoRA budget, or gate-specific optimization instability — and could scale-aware tuning close the gap?
  • Is 9–49% uncorrected suffix-dependence in the delta rule fundamental to any bounded-state overwrite rule, or could a richer fixed-size receipt still fix it?
  • How does the addressable/non-addressable taxonomy extend to other linear-attention/SSM variants — Mamba-2, S4, Titans, RWKV — beyond the one KDA hybrid tested?
  • What is the real-world cost/benefit crossover between record-boundary replay and full rebuild for millions of records under continuous ingestion?
  • Would a pre/post-snapshot adversary — explicitly excluded here — trivially break the guarantee by diffing model state, and what attestation mechanism would close that gap?
