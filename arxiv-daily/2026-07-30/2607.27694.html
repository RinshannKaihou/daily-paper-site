Systems / Hardware · Low-bit LLM Quantization + Accelerator Co-Design

Rotation and fine-grained group quantization are fundamentally non-cooperative — rotation spreads outliers globally, group quantization tames them locally, and naively combining them yields almost no benefit at small group sizes. GyRot's harmonic-aligned redesign is what finally makes the two work together.

一句话总结：GyRot 通过"粗粒度旋转+细粒度分组"(CoRFiG)和"谐波对齐置换"(HAP)解决旋转量化与分组量化的不兼容问题，配合全整数反量化设计，在 LLaMA-3-8B 零样本任务(W4A4KV4)上将平均准确率从 Quarot 的 66.96% 提升到 72.98%（全精度为 74.19%），更关键的是当 scale/zero-point 被强制为 INT8 时，竞品 LightRot 的困惑度从 6.80 骤增至 30.12，而 GyRot 仅从 6.81 微升到 6.91，同时加速器相对 Tender 基线实现最高 3.4 倍速度和 3.64 倍能效提升。

Why This Matters

Shows a concrete algorithm–hardware co-design path: one channel-permutation trick lets accelerators use 8-bit (not 16/32-bit) scale factors without breaking accuracy.

Critical Edge

Under FP16 scale/zero-point — no hardware constraint at all — GyRot's algorithm is not better than LightRot (even slightly behind at every group size); the entire advantage is contingent on forcing INT8.

01 Methodology

  1. 1
    CoRFiG. Decouples the rotation scope R = 2^g·G (R≤1024) from the quantization group size G. Rotating over a coarser scope than the grouping still flattens outliers globally while leaving each fine group's local variance intact. Default: G=32, R=1024.
  2. 2
    HAP (Harmonic-Aligned Permutation). Hadamard matrices contain "harmonic" all-±1 rows recurring every 2^k channels. HAP permutes the highest-magnitude outlier channels onto these rows before rotation, so every quantization group's outlier contributions share one sign — tight, shift-biased distributions instead of noisy mixed-sign spread. Fused into weights offline: zero runtime cost.
  3. 3
    Reformulated asymmetric quantization. Computes the zero-point in the unscaled domain and rounds it with a ceiling, not standard rounding, so the shifted minimum never underflows and clips. This alone lets both scale factor and zero-point live in INT8 instead of FP16/FP8.
  4. 4
    Hardware. An 8×8 systolic array of INT4 PEs (32-way dot product each, 2048 MACs/cycle) with a fully-integer in-PE dequantization pipeline, plus a Fused Vector Unit running an online 5-stage Fast Hadamard Transform for rotation scopes up to 1024. Synthesized at 28nm/1GHz.

02 Results

  • 72.98% avg. zero-shot accuracy (LLaMA-3-8B, W4A4KV4, 6 tasks) vs Quarot's 66.96%, FP16 ceiling 74.19% — closes a ~7.2pt gap down to ~1.2pt.
  • 7.73 PPL at W3A4KV4 (LLaMA-3-8B) vs Quarot's 9.17 and LightRot's 8.00 — gains hold across LLaMA-1/2 7B/13B too.
  • 3.40× speedup / 3.64× energy efficiency vs the Tender-W8A8 accelerator baseline (geomean, LLaMA-2-7B/13B + LLaMA-3-8B); 65.2% area / 69.2% energy reduction at the PE level.
  • MT-Bench LLM-judge win rates of 66.6% / 68.8% / 54.7% vs Quarot / SpinQuant / LightRot; ablation shows every component (CoRFiG, HAP, reformulation) contributing, including honest reporting of intermediate regressions.

Strengths

  • Empirically grounds a real conflict (rotation vs. group quantization, Fig. 1) rather than assuming the two trivially compose.
  • Component ablation (Fig. 9b) transparently reports both gains and regressions — builds confidence the final numbers aren't cherry-picked.
  • Full algorithm-to-hardware pipeline: accuracy tricks are explicitly designed to also relax hardware precision, verified with real RTL and an area/power breakdown.
  • Extensive design-space sweep (group size × rotation size × SF/ZP precision × rounding strategy) substantiates the chosen R=1024, G=32 operating point.

Limitations

  • Hardware numbers rely on a proprietary 28nm node and commercial EDA tools with no released RTL — limited external reproducibility.
  • Algorithmic gain over LightRot is essentially zero (even slightly negative) under FP16 SF/ZP; the entire benefit is contingent on the INT8-only regime.
  • Evaluation is limited to the LLaMA family (1/2/3) — no results for Mistral, Qwen, or MoE models where outlier structure may differ.
  • The online Fast Hadamard Transform path still adds 3.5% area / 0.9% power when rotation can't be pre-fused, but its real-world trigger frequency is never quantified.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • How does CoRFiG+HAP generalize to non-LLaMA architectures (MoE, GQA/sliding-window attention) where outlier channel structure may differ?
  • What is the actual runtime frequency of the online FHT path during full end-to-end inference, and how does its overhead scale with sequence length or batch size?
  • Could HAP's harmonic-alignment idea extend to non-Hadamard orthogonal transforms (e.g., learned rotations as in SpinQuant)?
  • How sensitive is GyRot's advantage to the calibration/GPTQ dataset, and does ceiling-based zero-point rounding generalize beyond GPTQ-based weight quantization?
  • How would the R=1024, G=32 design point shift under more aggressive settings — 2-bit weights, or W4A4 with sub-4-bit KV cache?
