Self-Refine, Reflexion, and Best-of-N self-verify never beat simple repeated sampling at equal token cost — every self-inspection method loses.

一句话总结：在严格按 token 成本对齐的受控实验中，需要模型评判/改写自己输出的方法，在全部 18 项对比中都显著或数值上不敌"多采样几次、投票选众数"这一朴素基线。

Sample More, Reflect Less: Self-Refine and Reflexion Lose to Repeated Sampling at Equal Token Cost, from 1.5B to 7B ↗
Iliya Mirzaei · Stony Brook University · 2026-07-30 · test-time scaling self-correction self-consistency statistical rigor
Key metric
0 / 36
method–setting comparisons beat the cost-matched sampling baseline after Holm correction. 10 lost significantly — all of them self-inspecting methods — and 26 tied within statistical noise.
Why this matters

If you're allocating inference budget for reasoning tasks, spending it on self-critique below 7B may be strictly worse than just sampling more and voting.

Critical edge

The authors never test whether a better-designed verifier prompt would close the Best-of-N gap, so "self-evaluation loses" applies only to the cheap self-check tested here — not self-evaluation in general.

01 Methodology

  • Cost = generated tokens only, summed across every model call a method makes — critiques, reflections, debate turns, verification steps all count, even if they never reach the final answer.
  • Baseline built once, reused everywhere: draw a pool of K=16 independent CoT chains per question, then resample N≤16 chains without replacement 200× per N ∈ {1,2,3,4,6,8,12,16} → a full accuracy-vs-cost curve from a single generation pass, so every tested method is compared at its own measured cost, not a shared cap.
  • 7 methods, each implemented from its original paper, none ever shown the correct answer: CoT, Plan-and-Solve, Self-Refine (3 critique/revise rounds), Reflexion-as-published (self-judges, stops early), Reflexion (forced 3 rounds), Best-of-N self-verify (N=8, model picks), Multi-Agent Debate (3 agents × 2 rounds).
  • Models & data: Qwen2.5 1.5B / 3B / 7B, Q8_0 quantized, CPU-only (no GPU). GSM8K & MATH-500, 150 questions each, 1,024-token generation cap per call for every method equally.
  • Stats: paired bootstrap CIs (10,000 resamples), Holm-corrected across 7 methods × 4 settings. Power stated up front: can reliably detect ∼5–6pp differences, not smaller — a declared limitation, not a post-hoc excuse.

Reflexion never fires on 1.5B: mean rounds used = 0.00, 100% of runs "stopped immediately" on both benchmarks — the self-judge never once said it was wrong, silently collapsing into plain CoT while still scoring competitively.
A bug they caught themselves: Best-of-N was initially mis-aggregated as majority-vote, which hid the effect entirely (+0.3 to +3.2pp). After the fix it sits below baseline everywhere, significant in three settings — the paper's sharpest finding was briefly invisible due to its own pipeline bug.

+ Strengths

  • Every comparison paired, bootstrapped, and Holm-corrected, with a stated detection threshold (~5–6pp) rather than an implicit one.
  • Same-samples Best-of-N ablation isolates the selection mechanism from the sampling budget almost without confound.
  • Reports two internal bugs it found and fixed (truncation bias, Best-of-N aggregation) and shows exactly how much the numbers moved.
  • Full release: code, prompts, all 19,200 generations, and an independent re-derivation script (96/96 checks agree).
  • Extends coverage to small models (1.5B, 3B) that the predecessor study skipped, surfacing the Reflexion-never-fires phenomenon.

− Limitations

  • Main comparison caps at 7B; whether self-evaluation eventually overtakes counting at larger scale is explicitly untested.
  • Single 8-bit quantization, CPU-only inference — quantization×method interactions are not ruled out.
  • Math-only, automatically-checkable answers; majority voting isn't even definable for open-ended generation (summaries, prose).
  • One implementation per method with fixed, untuned hyperparameters — may understate each method's best case.
  • Cost measured only in generated tokens — input tokens, latency, and dollar cost are explicitly not measured.

? Questions for further exploration

  1. Would a more structured, per-candidate-scoring verifier close the Best-of-N gap below 7B, as the authors suggest but never test?
  2. Does the "self-evaluation catches up to counting by 7B" trend continue, plateau, or reverse at frontier scale (70B+)?
  3. Under an open-ended task where majority voting isn't definable, would a weaker baseline let self-critique methods win instead?
  4. Would a trained lightweight verifier — rather than the same model prompted to self-judge — close the gap even below 7B?
