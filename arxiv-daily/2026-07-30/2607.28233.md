> **一句话总结：** 作者在 8 款真实 DDR4 芯片（3 家厂商）上系统刻画 RowHammer/RowPress 翻转位方向、空间分布和最小激活次数（AC_min），发现现有器件级模型（如 [78]）无法解释多项实测现象（如双侧 RowHammer 同时产生 0→1 和 1→0 翻转、RowPress 的 AC_min 随 tAggON 单调下降等），随后用 Sentaurus TCAD 仿真定位出电荷陷阱位置（离 NWL 或 PWL 更近）和体内空穴陷阱密度（D_HT,bulk）等关键建模参数，仅通过改变陷阱位置就能把双侧 RowHammer 中 1→0 的 AC_min 相对单侧降低 75.5%（陷阱靠近 NWL）或把 0→1 的 AC_min 降低 74.7%（陷阱靠近 PWL），并证明 8 个测试模块中同侧翻转位置的 Jaccard 重叠率始终低于 6.77%。

> **TL;DR:** Using real off-the-shelf DDR4 chips (8 modules from 3 manufacturers, DRAM Bender-based characterization) and Sentaurus TCAD device simulation, the paper shows that state-of-the-art single-trap device models (e.g., the model in prior work [78]) predict double-sided RowHammer suppresses 0-to-1 bitflips, whereas real chips show both 0-to-1 and 1-to-0 bitflips with a mean position-level Jaccard overlap of only 1.49%-6.77% across modules — and that simply moving the assumed charge-trap location from near the NWL to near the PWL flips which bitflip direction dominates, cutting the 1-to-0 AC_min by 75.5% or the 0-to-1 AC_min by 74.7% relative to single-sided RowHammer. It further shows RowPress's AC_min for both bitflip directions decreases monotonically as aggressor-row-on-time (tAggON) increases, contradicting prior device models that predict an initial AC_min increase, and traces this to the previously under-modeled bulk hole-trap density (D_HT,bulk).

## 核心贡献

- 系统对比了现有 RowHammer/RowPress 器件级模型（基于电荷陷阱辅助电子迁移和寄生耦合两大机制）与真实芯片实验特征化结果，识别出三处明确的"不匹配"（Unmatching Device-Level Mechanism 1-3）：双侧 RowHammer 的翻转方向、双侧 RowHammer 的 0→1 相对 1→0 的 AC_min 差异、以及 RowPress 的 AC_min 随 tAggON 的变化趋势。
- 在 8 款真实 DDR4 芯片（3 家厂商，8Gb/16Gb 不同 die 版本）上，每个模块随机采样 128 行受害行，逆向工程出每一行的 NWL/PWL 映射关系，并用位翻转方向数据交叉验证——发现几乎所有模块 100% 匹配单侧 RowHammer 的两条既有机制（仅 16Gb C-Die 模块因列重映射修复而为 99.7%）。
- 用 Sentaurus TCAD 对 1z-nm 级鞍鳍（saddle-fin RCAT）DRAM 结构做严格标定（用真实厂商 I_SN-V_WL 曲线校准），系统扫描陷阱位置、体内空穴陷阱密度、电容串扰强度等参数，首次定量复现双侧 RowHammer 同时产生两种方向翻转、及其重叠率极低（1.49%-6.77%）的现象。
- 提出并验证了新的更新版器件级机制集合（Table 3 汇总）：陷阱位置决定翻转方向；PWL 一侧到受害存储节点的电子迁移路径更短、迁移效率更高，解释了双侧 RowHammer 中 0→1 的 AC_min 系统性低于 1→0；容性串扰在 0→1 条件下访问晶体管有效阈值电压更低，因而串扰增强 0→1 泄漏的效果远强于 1→0；晶圆级真实晶体管测量证实 NWL 诱导的 RowPress 0→1 泄漏在常规工作条件下强度不足以在 64ms 保持期内引起可观测翻转；PWL 诱导 RowPress 的 1→0 翻转强度对体内空穴陷阱密度高度敏感。

## Key Contributions

- Systematically confronts existing device-level models of RowHammer/RowPress (based on trap-assisted electron migration near the aggressor wordline and parasitic/capacitive coupling) against real-chip characterization, pinpointing three concrete "Unmatching Device-Level Mechanisms": (1) double-sided RowHammer bitflip direction, (2) the AC_min gap between 0-to-1 and 1-to-0 bitflips under double-sided RowHammer, and (3) how RowPress AC_min evolves with tAggON.
- Characterizes 8 real DDR4 modules (3 manufacturers, 8Gb/16Gb, multiple die revisions) using DRAM Bender, sampling 128 victim rows per module, reverse-engineering each row's NWL/PWL mapping and cross-checking it against observed bitflip directions — finding 100% matching for single-sided RowHammer's two known mechanisms in nearly every module (99.7% in one 16Gb C-Die module from Mfr S, attributed to remapped columns from repair).
- Builds and calibrates a rigorous Sentaurus TCAD model of a 1z-nm-class saddle-fin recessed-channel-access-transistor (RCAT) DRAM cell (calibrated against a real manufacturer's I_SN-V_WL curve), then sweeps trap location, bulk hole-trap density, and capacitive-coupling strength to reproduce, for the first time, the simultaneous occurrence of both bitflip directions under double-sided RowHammer and their low (1.49%-6.77%) spatial overlap.
- Delivers an updated, consolidated set of device-level mechanisms (summarized in Table 3): trap location determines bitflip direction; the shorter PWL-to-storage-node electron migration path explains the systematically lower AC_min for 0-to-1 vs. 1-to-0 bitflips in double-sided RowHammer; a lower effective access-transistor threshold voltage under the 0-to-1 condition makes capacitive crosstalk far more effective at enhancing 0-to-1 leakage; wafer-level measurements on real fabricated sub-20nm test transistors confirm NWL-induced RowPress 0-to-1 leakage is too weak to overcome intrinsic retention leakage within the 64ms window; and PWL-induced RowPress 1-to-0 bitflips are highly sensitive to bulk hole-trap density.

## Methodology

The paper is structured in three stages. **(1) Literature synthesis:** it first summarizes the device-level mechanisms proposed by prior TCAD/failure-analysis works for single-sided RowHammer (NWL-induced 1-to-0 via trapped-electron emission/migration; PWL-induced 0-to-1 via electron pull-away), double-sided RowHammer (alternating NWL/PWL open/close enhancing one direction while suppressing the other, per the state-of-the-art model in [78]), and RowPress (sustained wordline-open leakage in the opposite direction of the corresponding RowHammer mechanism).

**(2) Real-chip characterization:** using DRAM Bender for fine-grained command/timing/temperature control, the authors test 8 commodity DDR4 UDIMM modules (Table 1: manufacturers "S", "H", "M"; 8Gb/16Gb densities; multiple die revisions), reverse-engineer the DRAM's internal row remapping and true-/anti-cell layout, and randomly sample 128 victim rows per module. They hammer aggressor rows within 64ms refresh windows to maximize observed bitflips while avoiding retention failures, and separately test RowPress at 80°C (since RowPress vulnerability is known to be temperature-sensitive). They build spatial bitflip-direction heatmaps (bit index mod 64/512) for single-sided RowHammer and RowPress, and compute the Jaccard overlap between 0-to-1 and 1-to-0 bitflip positions across 1.28M hammer trials per module for double-sided RowHammer.

**(3) Device-level TCAD modeling:** using Sentaurus TCAD, they build a saddle-fin RCAT DRAM cell (1z-nm-class dimensions), calibrated against a real manufacturer's measured I_SN-V_WL curve (Figure 11d). They model trap-assisted electron migration (EM) via single interface e-traps placed at six candidate locations (near NWL, near PWL, near WL1) for sensitivity analysis, then switch to a spatially uniform interface trap density (D_IT) to capture the collective effect. They separately model capacitive crosstalk (CC) via mixed-mode circuit simulation incorporating wordline resistance, WL-to-ground capacitance, and inter-WL coupling capacitance, which generates a crosstalk noise voltage on the victim wordline. For RowPress bulk-trap sensitivity (Section 5.6), they sweep bulk hole-trap density (D_HT,bulk) at three levels (9.7e15, 1.02e16, 1.16e16 cm⁻³eV⁻¹) and its effect on AC_min vs. tAggON trends. Failure thresholds are defined as a 0.1V shift in storage-node voltage from its initialized state (V_CORE for 1-to-0 studies, 0V for 0-to-1 studies), with AC_min being the number of activations needed to cross that threshold. Wafer-level electrical measurements on real fabricated sub-20nm test structures (parallel cell transistors for measurable currents) independently validate the NWL RowPress leakage-current polarity/magnitude findings at 125°C.

## Results & Highlights

- **AC_min reduction from trap-location alone:** For double-sided RowHammer at 325K, placing the charge trap near the NWL (location 2) reduces the 1-to-0 bitflip AC_min by 75.5% compared to single-sided (NWL) RowHammer, and no double-sided 0-to-1 bitflip is observed under that trap configuration. Placing the trap near the PWL (location 4) instead reduces the 0-to-1 bitflip AC_min by 74.7% compared to single-sided (PWL) RowHammer, with no double-sided 1-to-0 bitflip observed (Figure 14).
- **Bitflip-direction matching on real chips:** Reverse-engineered NWL/PWL row mappings match the two established single-sided RowHammer mechanisms (NWL→1-to-0, PWL→0-to-1) with 100% accuracy across essentially all 8 tested DDR4 modules; the sole exception is a 16Gb C-Die module from manufacturer S, which matches at 99.7% (attributed to remapped columns from post-manufacturing repair).
- **Low spatial overlap between bitflip directions:** Across all tested modules under double-sided RowHammer (1.28M hammer trials per module), the mean Jaccard overlap between 0-to-1-only and 1-to-0-only bitflip positions ranges from just 1.49% to 6.77%. Module S4 shows the largest overlap (6.65% at 50°C, 6.77% at 80°C); module M0 shows the smallest (2.57% at 50°C, 2.77% at 80°C) — evidence that most victim cells are direction-exclusive even though both directions occur in the population.
- **RowPress AC_min trend contradicts prior models:** Experimentally, AC_min for both 0-to-1 and 1-to-0 bitflips consistently decreases as tAggON increases (Figure 10), whereas the existing device-level model for PWL-induced RowPress predicts AC_min should initially increase because of a competitive relationship between RowHammer- and RowPress-like leakage mechanisms — this is flagged explicitly as "Unmatching Device-Level Mechanism 3."
- **Bulk hole-trap density controls RowPress fidelity to real chips:** TCAD sweeps at D_HT,bulk = 9.7e15, 1.02e16, and 1.16e16 cm⁻³eV⁻¹ show that only sufficiently high bulk hole-trap density reproduces the experimentally observed increasing/monotonic AC_min(0-to-1) trend with tAggON (Figure 19b); low D_HT,bulk instead reproduces the non-matching "initially increasing" trend predicted by prior models, identifying D_HT,bulk as a previously unrecognized but critical modeling parameter.
- **Wafer-level validation of the "missing" 0-to-1 RowPress bitflip:** Direct current measurements (I_SN1 vs. V_NWL) on real fabricated sub-20nm test transistors at 125°C show that at the actual passive/inactive wordline bias (V_BBW = -0.2V), induced leakage current is insufficient to trigger a 0-to-1 bitflip; a sufficiently large negative I_SN1 only appears once V_NWL is raised to 4.0-4.5V, well above typical operating V_PP — directly explaining why NWL-induced 0-to-1 RowPress bitflips are absent in real-chip characterization despite being predicted by prior device models.

No single "baseline model vs. our model" accuracy table is reported (this is a mechanistic/explanatory device study rather than a predictive-accuracy benchmark), but the quantitative deltas above (75.5%/74.7% AC_min reductions, 1.49%-6.77% Jaccard overlaps, 100%/99.7% direction-matching) are the paper's core evidentiary claims, each tied to a specific experimental setup (module, temperature, trap location) described above.

## Authors & Credibility

The team is led by Haocong Luo and Longda Zhou (co-first authors) with Ataberk Olgun, İsmail Emir Yüksel, Nisa Bostanci, Zhigang Ji, Xing Wu, and Onur Mutlu, spanning ETH Zurich, East China Normal University, and Shanghai Jiao Tong University. This is the SAFARI Research Group (Mutlu's lab), which authored the original RowPress paper ([3], DSN 2023) and numerous RowHammer characterization/mitigation works cited throughout (DRAM Bender [81], the tool used for chip characterization here, is also a SAFARI artifact). The paper explicitly builds on and extends a prior preliminary gap-analysis work ([73], by an overlapping set of authors) that first identified inconsistencies between device models and real-chip observations but did not perform the TCAD bridging work presented here. The group's long track record in real-chip DRAM characterization (DRAM Bender, RowPress, Uncovering RowHammer's Sensitivities) lends strong credibility to the experimental methodology; the TCAD device-modeling co-authors (Zhigang Ji, Xing Wu) bring semiconductor device expertise, and industrial funding acknowledgment (Google, Huawei, Intel, Microsoft, Google Security and Privacy Research Award, Microsoft Swiss Joint Research Center) suggests some degree of external scrutiny/interest, though no independent replication is reported in the paper itself.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** Genuinely incremental-but-valuable — this is explicitly framed (and by the authors' own account, is) the first work to systematically bridge device-level TCAD modeling with real-chip characterization results for RowHammer/RowPress, rather than proposing either a wholly new attack or a wholly new mitigation. The value is in resolving specific documented contradictions (Unmatching Mechanisms 1-3) rather than a novel phenomenon discovery, though the wafer-level test-transistor validation of the "missing" 0-to-1 RowPress bitflip (Section 5.5) is a distinctive empirical contribution beyond simulation alone.

**Rigor:** The experimental characterization is strong — 8 real modules from 3 manufacturers, 128 sampled victim rows each, reverse-engineered row mappings cross-validated against bitflip direction data, and the TCAD model calibrated against real manufacturer I-V curves (Figure 11d) rather than assumed parameters. A named weak point in prior work is explicitly called out: the state-of-the-art device model in [78] "shows double-sided RowHammer enhances 1-to-0 bitflips but suppresses 0-to-1 bitflips," which the paper demonstrates is inconsistent with real-chip data — a clear, paper-specific critique of a named baseline model rather than a generic complaint.

**Reproducibility:** Mixed. The real-chip characterization uses DRAM Bender, an open-source/publicly available framework, and commodity DDR4 modules, so that part is in principle replicable by others with the hardware. However, the TCAD device modeling relies on Sentaurus (a proprietary, expensive commercial tool) and process parameters (e.g., 1z-nm dimensions, saddle-fin geometry) that are partly reconstructed/assumed rather than disclosed by DRAM manufacturers, and the wafer-level test-transistor measurements (Section 5.5) use non-public "industry-grade" sub-20nm test structures obtained through unspecified industrial collaboration — these components cannot be independently reproduced by academic groups without similar industry access.

**Significance:** The findings have direct implications for two practical areas the paper explicitly discusses: (1) how future characterization studies should be designed (e.g., not treating BER as a proxy for AC_min, since the two bitflip directions "behave differently" per the paper's own finding), and (2) how RowHammer/RowPress mitigation techniques are configured, since mitigations tuned against a single (and possibly wrong) device mechanism risk under- or over-provisioning protection.

**Red flags / caveats:** The explanation for why 1-to-0 bitflips statistically outnumber 0-to-1 bitflips in double-sided RowHammer at high hammer counts (Section 5.4, attributed to process-induced trap-density asymmetry between NWL-adjacent saddle-fin regions and PWL-adjacent STI regions) is explicitly acknowledged by the authors as a "plausible qualitative explanation" that is "beyond the scope of this work" to quantitatively verify — i.e., one of the paper's own claimed mechanisms rests on inference rather than direct measurement. Similarly, the transition from single-trap sensitivity analysis (Section 5.2) to spatially-uniform trap density modeling (Section 5.3) is a modeling simplification whose fidelity to the true (likely non-uniform) trap distribution is not independently validated — the paper's own single-trap results (Figure 16c) are used partly to justify this simplification, which is somewhat circular. No formal ablation isolating the marginal contribution of trap-assisted EM vs. capacitive crosstalk to the double-sided RowHammer overlap results is presented; the two mechanisms are analyzed largely independently (Sections 5.3's two subsections) rather than jointly.

## Strengths

- Directly and explicitly names specific documented contradictions between existing device models and real-chip data (three "Unmatching Device-Level Mechanisms"), rather than vaguely gesturing at "gaps in understanding."
- Combines three independent evidence sources — real commodity chip characterization, calibrated TCAD simulation, and wafer-level test-transistor measurements — that reinforce each other, notably to explain why NWL-induced 0-to-1 RowPress bitflips are absent under normal operating conditions.
- Large-scale, systematic real-chip dataset: 8 modules across 3 manufacturers and multiple die revisions/densities, with reverse-engineered ground truth (NWL/PWL mapping) rather than assumed layout.
- Provides a clear, actionable summary table (Table 3) mapping each real-chip observation to its updated device-level mechanism and implication, useful as a reference for follow-on work.

## Limitations

- Root cause of the 1-to-0/0-to-1 population asymmetry in double-sided RowHammer (Section 5.4) is only qualitatively argued via inferred trap-density asymmetry, not directly measured or quantified — the authors state this explicitly.
- TCAD modeling depends on assumed/reconstructed 1z-nm process geometry and trap parameters since real manufacturers do not disclose these; results are therefore representative rather than manufacturer-verified for any specific commercial chip.
- Sensitivity analysis of trap location effects uses a single-trap configuration whose "spatial influence is inherently limited," a limitation the authors acknowledge before switching to a uniform-density model for the main results.
- The work characterizes only RowHammer and RowPress; other or emerging read-disturbance mechanisms mentioned in the paper itself (e.g., ColumnDisturb [93], read-disturbance variants/combinations [4,47,89-91]) are explicitly left to future work.
- No system-level or statistical bitflip injection model is proposed; the paper explicitly defers "the development of a more accurate system-level DRAM disturbance model" to future work, so the practical utility for e.g. simulator-based security research is not yet realized.

## Relevance

这篇论文属于计算机体系结构/硬件安全方向的器件级实证研究，与主流 LLM/ML 方向关联较弱，但其严谨的"实验特征化 + 仿真建模双向校验"方法论（尤其是明确标注模型与实测数据的具体不匹配点、并逐一用受控仿真定位根因的做法）对做系统可靠性或攻防机制根因分析的研究者有较高参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Could the process-induced trap-density asymmetry hypothesis (Section 5.4) be directly tested via TEM/electrical characterization of NWL-adjacent vs. PWL-adjacent regions on de-packaged real chips, rather than inferred qualitatively?
- How would the updated device-level mechanisms (Table 3) change AC_min or bitflip-direction predictions for newer/denser DRAM technology nodes (e.g., sub-10nm, or DDR5/HBM) where saddle-fin geometry or trap densities may differ substantially from the modeled 1z-nm structure?
- Given that BER is shown to not be a reliable proxy for AC_min because "0-to-1 and 1-to-0 bitflips have sophisticated underlying error mechanisms that behave differently," what would a corrected experimental characterization methodology that separately measures both bitflip directions' AC_min look like in practice, and how much would it change published robustness metrics for existing DRAM chips?
- Can the combined trap-assisted EM and capacitive-crosstalk mechanisms be jointly modeled (rather than analyzed as largely separate effects in Sections 5.3's two subsections) to quantify their relative and interacting contributions to the observed double-sided RowHammer bitflip-direction asymmetry?
- Would the D_HT,bulk sensitivity finding for RowPress (Section 5.6) generalize across manufacturers/nodes, or is it specific to the particular process assumptions used in this TCAD model — and could it be validated via the same wafer-level test-transistor approach used for the NWL RowPress 0-to-1 result in Section 5.5?
