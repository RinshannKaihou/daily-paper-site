Specializing a judge's weights into LoRA experts hurts accuracy — specializing the decision to defer to a bigger judge helps.

一句话总结：把评判能力拆成多个 LoRA "judgelet" 专家会显著损害共享判断力；但学会"何时信任、何时升级"的路由决策（risklet），却能用更少算力换来更高准确率。

Why This Matters

If you're building rubric-conditioned judges or reward-model cascades: don't fragment the judge into domain experts. Put specialization in the escalate-or-trust decision instead.

Critical Edge

The cascade's Pareto gain is shown on one model ladder (Skywork-Reward-V2), and the authors' own second test — a DeBERTa base/large pair — fails to reproduce the compute savings.

01 Methodology — two studies, two architectures

Study 1 · Judgelets (specialize the weights)

K=1 is the monolithic baseline; K=8 is the "judgelet bank." A rank-64 monolith matches the bank's aggregate stored parameters (40.38M vs. 40.41M) as a capacity control. Splits are hash-locked, with an entire held-out criterion set (shift_locked) never seen in training/calibration — 47,638 train / 9,441 calibrate / 9,597 dev / 12,580 locked-test / 20,696 held-criterion examples, on Prometheus Feedback Collection (99,952 examples, 996 criteria).

  • A 512-token truncation and a rubric-free "response-only" control isolate whether the model actually reads the rubric text.
  • A counterfactual rubric swap (unrelated criterion, same task/response/label) tests whether the judge's answer moves the way a rubric-sensitive model should.

Study 2 · Risklet cascade (specialize the deferral)

0.6B judge scores candidates
→
Risklet: 14 score-geometry features → release or escalate?
→
4B judge (if escalated)
→
Risklet decides again
→
8B judge (terminal)

The risklet is a plain logistic regression over score geometry — top scores, gap, range, mean, variance, candidate count, winning position, plus a skill one-hot — fit per cascade stage to predict whether the current top-ranked candidate is already correct. Evaluated on RewardBench 2 (1,865 tasks, 8,977 candidates) against raw margin, calibrated-margin, and geometry-only baselines, across 20 independently locked repartitions, each individually risk-audited with an exact Clopper–Pearson bound.

Risk audits: every one of the 20 locked repartitions of the risklet cascade (20/20) passes an exact one-sided 95% Clopper–Pearson bound; margin baselines pass only 19–20/20.

Strengths

  • Every claimed effect is paired with a control designed to falsify the convenient interpretation: capacity-matching, learning-rate-matching, fixed-update budgets, shared initialization.
  • Paired bootstrap CIs with criterion ID as the resampling unit; exact one-sided Clopper–Pearson/Wilson bounds for risk audits, not point estimates.
  • Reports the negative/non-generalizing cases (Prometheus rank-8/64, OpenAssistant pair) instead of cherry-picking only the favorable Skywork ladder.
  • States a falsifiable boundary condition — cascades help only when the cheap stage is genuinely cheap and its errors are complementary.

Limitations

  • Feedback Collection is synthetic (LLM-generated); the strong response-only shortcut hints some "rubric benefit" may be a data regularity, not genuine rubric-reading.
  • Criterion-shift holdout tests unseen rubric text, not policy revision over time or adversarially rewritten rules; the one external set shares all its criteria with training.
  • Router is restricted to English lexical TF-IDF features and one base-model family (Qwen3-based Skywork-Reward-V2).
  • Normalized parameter compute is an architecture-comparison proxy only — not energy, latency, or dollar cost.

03 Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Would the judgelet fragmentation loss persist on natural, human-annotated rubric data, given how strong the response-only shortcut already is on synthetic data?
  2. Does "share pre-adaptation, then specialize" generalize past 8 families, other adapter ranks, or non-LoRA methods like prefix tuning / IA3?
  3. Beyond the two worked examples, what decision boundary — relative inference cost, error-complementarity rate — predicts when a risklet cascade beats the terminal model?
  4. Would a semantic (embedding) router instead of TF-IDF/K-means change the fragmentation result or its sensitivity to family count (K=4 vs. K=8)?
  5. How sensitive is the risklet's geometry-only signal to reward-model families with very different score calibration than Skywork-Reward-V2?
