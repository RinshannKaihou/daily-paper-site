Why this matters

A concrete blueprint for auditable, human-interruptible multi-agent research systems — separating who proposes claims from who certifies them — transferable beyond mathematics.

Critical edge n=1

All ablations are single, unreplicated runs with no variance reported, and both headline group-theory results remain only informally_verified — resting on trust in un-re-executed CAS transcripts, not machine-checked proof.

Two open Kourovka Notebook problems, addressed

the paper's strongest significance signal — beyond benchmark scores

Problem 21.142 · counterexample informally_verified

For some m≥9 (depending on primes p, q), the alternating group Am cannot embed into any finite group invariably generated by elements of orders p and q — via reduction to a group between St and Aut(S)≀Sym(t) and a terminal simple-factor obstruction.

80 child sessions · 6.81h active compute · 29.7M raw tokens

Problem 20.2 · explicit witness informally_verified

G = PSL2(7) resolves whether a nonabelian simple group of Lie type can be totally 3-closed. Follow-up work extends toward a near-complete PSLn(q) classification — only PSLn(2), n≥5 remains open.

23 child sessions · 1h16m · 6.71M tokens (conceptual proof route)

Methodology

state S_t = (q⋆, G_t, D_t, L_t, A_t, P_t, H_t, M_t)

A proof attempt is a versioned state: an immutable root problem, a typed graph of claims/routes/inferences, a debt ledger of open obligations, a literature library, and an artifact store for CAS/verification records. Agent sessions never edit this state directly — they propose patches.

Can propose

  • Researcher
  • Adversarial researcher
  • Literature reviewer
  • PhD advisor

Can certify

  • Strict verifier (local checks)
  • Integration verifier (route→root)
  • Refutation validator
  • A rule-based scheduler picks the next unresolved obligation; every patch is validated (role authority, schema, evidence) before being applied atomically or rejected with a logged diagnostic.
  • Reconciliation: if a dependency changes after a route is integrated, the integration is withdrawn automatically and the route returns to repair.
  • Two MCP tool families — CAS (SageMath, GAP, Macaulay2, Singular) and literature search (TheoremSearch, Matlas) — but neither a CAS transcript nor a retrieved theorem counts as evidence until the strict verifier checks its interface against the target claim.
  • All experiments use a single backend, “gpt-5.6-sol” at “xhigh” reasoning effort.

+ Strengths

  • Solves and human-verifies two previously-open group-theory problems — genuine research contact, not only benchmark scores.
  • Separates who can propose a math argument from who can certify it, directly targeting the "plausible-but-false argument" failure mode.
  • Persistent, versioned, auditable proof state — failed routes and open debts stay inspectable, never silently discarded.
  • Transparent cost accounting (tokens, compute time, session counts) reported for every experiment.
  • Unusually candid about negative/partial results, e.g. the advisor-off run produced more verified claims (18/20) yet still failed to reach the root.

− Limitations

  • Every ablation is a single, unreplicated run — no variance estimate behind the 32.0% or 1.85× headline deltas.
  • The advisor-off run on 21.142 was stopped before its intended compute allowance — can't rule out eventual success without the advisor.
  • RealMath covers only 10 hand-picked problems, all CAS-on; no no-CAS RealMath results despite the abstract's stated "9/10 no-CAS" outcome.
  • Mathematical status caps at informally_verified — Lean-backed formal verification is deferred to a future version.
  • No quantitative head-to-head vs. cited systems (Danus, QED, ProofCouncil, Aletheia) — Table 1 is a qualitative checklist only.
  • Every experiment depends on one undocumented proprietary model ("gpt-5.6-sol", "xhigh" reasoning) — limits external reproducibility.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Would RealMath results hold on the full benchmark rather than 10 hand-picked problems, and what would a matched no-CAS run actually show?
  2. Do the 32.0% CAS savings and 1.85× advisor-off inflation replicate across seeds, or are they specific to this problem instance?
  3. Since the verifier trusts CAS transcripts without re-execution, how robust is this to an adversarial or buggy CAS backend?
  1. What would Lean-checked formal verification reveal about the 21.142 counterexample and 20.2 witness?
  2. How does Albilich compare quantitatively — not just via Table 1's checklist — to Danus, QED, ProofCouncil, and Aletheia?
  3. Does the debt-ledger/reconciliation mechanism stay tractable for runs far longer than the ~5–10 hour case studies reported here?
