Why This Matters

Undercuts the "geometric complementarity" story used to justify expert pruning/merging (e.g. Sub-MoE, Mosaic Pruning) — subspace overlap alone can't tell you which experts are functionally redundant.

Critical Edge

Under the strictest sensitivity caliper, the "all-cells-negative" headline is only supported by 5.8–7.9% of eligible expert pairs per layer — the sign holds, but coverage is thin.

Methodology

A purely diagnostic study across 6 open MoE models (OLMoE, Mixtral-8x7B, DeepSeek-MoE-16B, Qwen3, Gemma4, Qwen3.6). Two geometric objects per expert per layer:

  • ESSI (Eq. 3) — load-weighted mean pairwise Grassmannian chordal distance between expert bases, normalized by each expert's own local tangent dispersion. ESSI ≈ 1 means separation is no greater than an expert's internal variance.
  • Prefix-controlled 2×2 factorial design (Fig. 1) — crosses the actual selected candidate vs. the strongest unselected rival, against the actual co-selection context vs. matched alternative contexts of equal length.
candidate quality A×context T=interaction D = A_actual − A_alt

D > 0 would mean the candidate is especially useful because of its specific co-selected peers (true positive complementarity); D < 0 means the real context actually suppresses that advantage. Protocol: 18 geometry-survey model–layer cells; 39 factorial cells (24 OLMoE/Mixtral + 15 DeepSeek shared-expert, applied post hoc); frozen-route NLL interventions on all 39; up to 2,048 held-out tokens/cell; 95% CIs from 1,000 paired bootstrap resamples; plus a 3-seed matched-compute Top-1 vs. Top-2 training ablation (<0.04% compute difference).

Despite near-total geometric overlap, real routes are never interchangeable with random matched routes — but that gap comes from candidate quality, not from candidates and context reinforcing each other.

Strengths vs. Limitations

Strengths

  • Cleanly decomposes a widely assumed claim into three separately falsifiable parts: route coherence, candidate quality, and candidate×context interaction.
  • ESSI gives a principled, self-normalizing "no separation" baseline (≈1) instead of raw, hard-to-interpret distances.
  • Extensive robustness: 3 sensitivity variants, an independent CPU tall-SVD cross-check, bootstrap CIs throughout, multi-seed training ablation.
  • Cross-architecture consistency across routed-only, shared-expert, and hybrid designs (6 models).
  • Reports negative/inconclusive results (15/39 NLL cells) rather than only favorable ones.

Limitations

  • Linear, fixed-rank (p=128) subspace metric can't capture nonlinear or output-space complementarity — authors flag this explicitly.
  • Full factorial + functional evidence covers only 3 of 6 models (OLMoE, Mixtral, DeepSeek); Qwen3/Gemma4/Qwen3.6 are geometry-only.
  • Strict-caliper sensitivity check covers just 5.8–7.9% of eligible expert pairs per layer.
  • Top-1 vs Top-2 training ablation is small-scale (6 layers, 3 seeds) — a proof of concept, not a scale guarantee.
  • No code/data release evident in the reviewed excerpt.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does the negative geometric interaction still hold under nonlinear or output-space complementarity metrics, rather than router-input subspace coverage?
  2. Can ESSI + interaction D actually predict which specific expert pairs are safe to merge or prune — or is the output-level functional test always required regardless?
  3. Does the declining marginal NLL-recovery pattern generalize to models with far higher active expert counts per token (e.g. Qwen3.6's Top-8-of-256)?
  4. Would raising the fitted rank p beyond 128 change the ESSI conclusions, given overlap shrinks markedly at higher ambient dimension (0.27–0.44 at dim 64 vs. 0.77–0.98 at dim 512)?
  5. What explains the reversal at OLMoE layers 8 and 16, where the full later-expert set does more NLL damage than replacing the leader alone?
