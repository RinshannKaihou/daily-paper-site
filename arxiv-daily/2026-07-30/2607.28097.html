Why This Matters

Gives inference-systems researchers an actionable, layered method — post-mHC and persistent-state boundary checks — to verify numerical consistency across MoE runtimes, backends, and quantized kernels.

「这篇论文…给出了一套可操作的分层诊断方法（post-mHC/持久状态边界充分性验证），可以直接迁移到评估其他 MoE 推理后端或量化/融合内核的行为一致性问题上。」

Critical Edge

Results establish "controlled causal possibility, not deployment incidence" — one checkpoint, one CPU runtime, zero real GPU/NPU/distributed backends measured.

Methodology

Runs inside the native DeepSeek-V4-Flash checkpoint on Whale Agent Lab's Colibri runtime — deliberately not a Transformers reimplementation — on a single native-Windows CPU host with greedy decoding. Six routed experts plus one shared expert make all 6! = 720 cross-expert reduction orders exhaustively enumerable.

  • Two reconstruction controls turn correlation into a sufficiency proof: an exact FP32 delta at the post-mHC endpoint (intra-token boundary) and an exact FP64 delta over the full persistent state (cross-token boundary).
  • Extended with 64-token continuations under stratified sampling, a 50-prompt breadth study (25 EN / 25 ZH), a 768-trajectory persistent layer-static protocol, and a same-mode canonical control isolating within-scheme order-sensitivity from the scheme-level shift.

Strengths

  • Tight causal isolation: freezes every MoE input except the reduction contract, and verifies the frozen inputs bitwise before interpreting any downstream difference.
  • Two independent, zero-error state-boundary reconstructions convert an observational correlation ("branches differ") into a validated sufficiency claim.
  • Discloses its own tooling bugs (text truncation, miscounted breadth results) and corrects them transparently rather than silently.
  • Deterministic replay (10/10 branch pairs, exact SHA-256 agreement) rules out ordinary decoding randomness as the cause of long-form divergence.
  • Proposes a reusable L0–L4 conformance hierarchy for cross-backend MoE testing, not just a one-off empirical curiosity.

Limitations

  • Single checkpoint (DeepSeek-V4-Flash) and single native CPU runtime (Colibri, non-DSpark) — no cross-architecture test.
  • No real GPU/NPU/distributed-backend measurement, no frozen-route mediation control, no exact quantized-operand summation reference.
  • Persistent-state reconstruction demonstrated for one controlled branch and one decode boundary — proves endpoint sufficiency, not uniqueness.
  • The 50-prompt breadth result is a staged, selection-conditioned cohort (survivors carried forward), not independent trials — descriptive, not a probability estimate.
  • Event-direction annotation rests on a single blind two-annotator consensus over just six unique items — explicitly too small for real inter-annotator statistics.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Does basin compression and route/text bifurcation persist on real GPU/NPU or distributed all-to-all MoE dispatch (e.g. DeepEP, DeepSpeed-MoE), where reduction order is hardware- or scheduler-determined?
  • Does scheme C's order-invariance hold for checkpoints with different top-k, expert counts, or non-mHC architectures — or is it specific to this six-term, DeepSeek-V4-Flash setting?
  • Would a frozen-route mediation control confirm routing as the necessary causal pathway from numerical divergence to token divergence?
  • What's the actual incidence rate of behaviorally divergent reductions under realistic production conditions — batch size, kernel fusion, thread scheduling — rather than controlled single-permutation forks?
  • Would a second annotator or larger sample change the 202/113/45 layoffs/hiring/other split, or would the bifurcation reproduce under other prompts or languages?
