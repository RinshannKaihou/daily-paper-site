Why This Matters

Hands practitioners a ready-made method-vs-measure taxonomy and a matched-compute evaluation checklist (Section 11) for building rigorous UQ comparisons — but its own tables still need first-source verification.

Critical Edge

Roughly a third of Table 3's ratings are the authors' own structural guesses, not benchmarks — including favorable placement of their own untested Variance-Gated measures alongside MI, EPKL, and EPJS.

Methodology

Axis 1 — Generative mechanism
explicit trained members→ posterior sampling (MC @ inference)→ closed-form analytic predictive
Axis 2 — Network scope
full network→ shared backbone + independent last layer→ single deterministic network
Orthogonal — the measure axis (Table 2 crosses these freely)
entropy / MI decomposition→ pairwise divergence (EPCE / EPKL / EPJS)→ variance-gated (VGE / VGMU / VGN)

Five method families get deep treatment: Bayesian NNs (VI, MCMC/HMC/SGLD, Laplace, NNGP), MC Dropout, deep ensembles, efficient approximations (BatchEnsemble, Snapshot, SWAG/MultiSWAG, Sub-Ensembles, Masksembles, Layer Ensembles, CreDE), and last-layer/single-pass methods (SNGP, DUQ/DDU/DUE, MIMO, epinet, VBLL). Adjacent coverage: evidential/prior networks, conformal prediction, post-hoc calibration. Section 11 anchors evaluation to ECE/Brier/NLL, AUROC/AUPR vs. the MSP baseline, OpenOOD, Uncertainty Baselines, and the Ovadia et al. (2019) shift suite; Section 13 formalizes Wood et al.'s (2023) exact bias–variance–diversity decomposition of ensemble cross-entropy loss.

Strengths

  • Clean separation of "method that produces an ensemble" from "measure that summarizes uncertainty" (Figure 1, Table 2) — explains why last-layer methods span multiple taxonomy cells.
  • Deepest available treatment of efficient ensemble approximations (BatchEnsemble, SWAG/MultiSWAG, Snapshot, Sub-Ensembles, Masksembles, Layer Ensembles, CreDE), including recent critiques (Zamyatin et al., 2026; Kirsch, 2025).
  • Transparent about the evidentiary strength of its own claims via the dagger (†) notation, instead of presenting every rating with false uniform confidence.
  • Section 11 converts evaluation practice into concrete recommendations: matched compute, multiple metrics, standardized suites (OpenOOD, Uncertainty Baselines), shift benchmarks (Ovadia et al., 2019).
  • Well-organized reference tables (1, 3–10) function as a practical lookup for cost/quality trade-offs.

Limitations

  • No original empirical results; a nontrivial share of Table 3's ratings are the authors' own structural inference, not measured benchmarks.
  • Uneven scope by the authors' own admission — evidential/prior networks get fuller treatment, while conformal prediction, post-hoc calibration, and LLM uncertainty are covered only lightly.
  • The authors' own VGE/VGMU/VGN measures are presented alongside MI/EPKL/EPJS without the head-to-head empirical validation Section 11 itself recommends — an internal inconsistency.
  • Restricted to classification-centric decomposition; regression, structured prediction, and generative/LLM settings are treated as secondary, not core.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • How would the authors' own VGE/VGMU/VGN measures actually perform against MI, EPKL, and EPJS on the standardized OOD/shift suites (OpenOOD, Ovadia et al. 2019) the survey itself sets as the evidentiary gold standard?
  • Can the dagger-marked Table 3 entries (Anchored, Sub-, Layer Ensembles, CreDE, TreeNets/LLE) be replaced with direct, matched-compute benchmark numbers — and would the current L/M/H placements survive?
  • Does last-layer diversity (shared backbone, independent heads) have a provable upper bound relative to full deep-ensemble diversity, and can architecture search detect when shared gradients collapse heads (Kirsch, 2025)?
  • Can bounded pairwise EPJS be validated empirically against MI and EPKL on the same shift benchmarks, to settle which is actually the better OOD detector in practice?
  • What would a principled aleatoric/epistemic split look like for free-form LLM generation, and can verbalized confidence stay calibration-stable under distribution shift and adversarial prompting?
  • Do hybrid architectures (a small ensemble of SNGP/DDU backbones, or one distance-aware backbone carrying multiple last-layer heads) recover most deep-ensemble uncertainty quality at a fraction of the O(M×) training cost?
