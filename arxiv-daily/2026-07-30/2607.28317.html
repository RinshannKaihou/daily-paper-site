Audit-Budget Allocation · LLM Agent Fleet Oversight

Confidence-ranked audits of an LLM agent fleet flip to worse than random — past a threshold that rises, not falls, as the audit budget shrinks.

一句话总结：按置信度排序审计优于随机审计的"翻转阈值" δ*，会随审计预算收紧反而升高——与先验预期相反。

One Human, N Agents: Audit-Budget Allocation for LLM Agent Fleets under Miscalibrated, Correlated Confidence ↗
Zavattari, Tommasi, Prencipe — Università di Pisa 30 Jul 2026 · arXiv:2607.28317 · cs.AI
llm-agent-oversight audit-budget-allocation confidence-calibration correlated-errors ai-act-compliance

Key metric — flip threshold δ*(B/N)

0.93→0.75

B/N = 0.02 → 0.2 (200 reps × 50 rounds/cell)

δ* falls as the budget grows. Tight budgets only ever audit the extreme low-confidence tail — which stays informative — while generous budgets dip first into the "poisoned" mid-ranks.

Why this matters

Gives EU AI Act Article 14 "effective human oversight" debates an operational test — a located flip point plus a quantitative "vacuity" score for when a fleet's confidence-based audit budget is rubber-stamping, not overseeing.

Critical edge

The one reassuring real-model result — that gpt-4o-mini sits safely below the flip threshold — rests on a single proprietary model: "a robustness point, not a sample of hosted models."

Methodology

Models oversight as budgeted noisy inspection over a fleet of N persistent agents split into F base-model families.

01Error indicators Ei (marginal ei~U(0.05,0.30)) coupled via a two-level Gaussian copula: fleet-shared factor ρ0 + within-family ρf + idiosyncratic noise, AR(1) persistence φ.
02Self-reported confidence: correct → Beta(6,2); errors → (1−δ)·Beta(3,3) + δ·Beta(10,1.5), where δ is the adversarial-miscalibration knob.
03Each round, supervisor picks audit set At, |At|≤B; audit reveals Ei w.p. ε. Metric: residual risk R(π) = expected undetected errors/round.

Five policies compared, including the paper's own proposal:

random round_robin conf_ranked oracle_conf (non-deployable) diversity_bayes ★

Three experiments: E1 synthetic phase diagram (N, F, B/N, ρ, δ, ε swept; 200 reps × T=50) locates δ* and the vacuity criterion. E2 measures six real LLMs (Qwen3-0.6B/4B/8B, Mistral-7B-Instruct, Phi-4-mini, gpt-4o-mini) on GSM8K / HotpotQA under one locked single-completion, 0–100 confidence-elicitation protocol. E3 replays all policies on a real 15-agent (5 models × 3 instances) trace.

All four pre-specified paired bootstrap contrasts in E3 are Holm-significant; effect sizes are larger on HotpotQA (0.322/0.312 risk units) than GSM8K (0.133/0.095). The diversity-Bayes advantage requires persistent error profiles — it collapses at φ=0 (0.29 vs. 3.47 at φ=1) — and cedes back to confidence-ranked auditing at ε=0.2, ρ=0.5 (14.27 vs. 14.04).

Strengths

  • Reversed-direction, falsifiable threshold reported against the authors' own prior expectation, not hidden or rationalized away.
  • Quantitative "vacuity" criterion (10/44 cells at τ=0.1) gives AI Act Article 14 debates an operational, model-level proxy.
  • Real measurement, not just simulation — six LLMs' verbalized-confidence ECE/AUROC/δ_prec on two benchmarks, with a reliability-diagram appendix.
  • Trace-grounded replay (E3) on real model outputs corroborates the synthetic ordering with Holm-corrected contrasts.
  • Robustness checks (copula choice, transfer weight w, persistence φ, verifier-noise crossover) are reported even where they complicate the headline story.
  • Unusually candid, itemized limitations section that pre-empts most reviewer objections.

Limitations

  • diversity-Bayes cedes back to confidence-ranked at ρ=0.5, ε=0.2 (14.27 vs. 14.04) — needs a not-too-noisy verifier or strong correlation.
  • Requires persistent error profiles; collapses under frequent re-tuning/fine-tuning (φ=0).
  • Correlation estimate ρ̂ is measured only on aligned items — an upper bound for fleets whose agents process different inputs (most production fleets).
  • δ̂ comes from one locked, single-completion, no-CoT elicitation protocol; richer elicitation is untested and might restore confidence informativeness.
  • gpt-4o-mini is N=1 — a single proprietary robustness point, non-deterministic at the API level, with no same-checkpoint anchor.
  • E3's 15-agent fleet is explicitly "preliminary"; no baseline compares self-reported confidence against a learned error-predictor on other features.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Would a multi-provider survey (beyond the single gpt-4o-mini point) confirm hosted/RLHF'd models are systematically more informative, or is this idiosyncratic?
  • How would δ* and the vacuity criterion change under strategic/adversarial (AI Control-style) agents rather than "honest-but-miscalibrated" ones?
  • Does richer elicitation — chain-of-thought before confidence, logit-based confidence, or self-consistency — restore informative confidence for the five collapsed open-weight models?
  • How does aligned-item ρ̂ behave for production fleets where agents process different inputs, given it's flagged as an upper bound?
  • Would sequential within-round auditing improve on diversity-Bayes, particularly in the ε=0.2, ρ=0.5 regime where it currently loses?
  • At what fleet size does E3's N=15 result stop tracking the E1 synthetic phase diagram — hundreds or thousands of agents?
