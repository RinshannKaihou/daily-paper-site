Fairness Pruning: Locating Demographic Bias in GLU-MLP Layers via Differential Activations ↗
Pere Martra, Eugenio Martínez Cámara, Alfonso Ureña López · 2026-07-30 · arXiv:2607.28319
Key Metric
99.49%
Mean retention across five general-capability benchmarks (WikiText, MMLU, ARC-Challenge, HellaSwag-EN/ES) after zeroing ≤40 BiasScore-selected neurons — <0.031% of Llama-3.2-1B's 131,072 MLP positions. Per-benchmark range: 97.93%–101.34%.
Why This Matters

Offers a cheap, training-free way to locate bias-encoding neurons in open LLMs — but shows today's unsigned scoring can't yet safely debias a model on its own.

Critical Edge

Six of eight zeroing experiments moved BBQ's ambiguous- and disambiguated-context bias scores in opposite directions — this is a localization study, not yet a working mitigation method.

01Methodology

contrastive pairs→ down_proj hook→ BiasScore (Eq.1)→ zero top-K→ dual eval
  • Pairs: prompts identical except one demographic attribute ("the [old/young] neighbor…"), token-count matched per tokenizer — a hard constraint that drops non-conforming pairs for Salamandra-2B.
  • Capture: a forward hook records the down_proj input of every GLU-MLP layer — the point where SiLU(gate_proj(x)) ⊙ up_proj(x) is fully formed, before it re-enters the residual stream.
  • Scoring: BiasScore = mean absolute activation difference between a pair's two prompts, averaged per category — unsigned, magnitude only, no direction label.
  • Zeroing: for each top-K neuron, the matching row of gate_proj/up_proj and column of down_proj are set to zero — a symmetric structural edit, no architecture change.
  • Eval: lm-eval-harness for general capability; BBQ (English) / EsBBQ (Spanish, 0-shot) for bias.
Models: Llama-3.2-1B (primary), Llama-3.2-3B (scaling), Salamandra-2B (cross-lingual). Single NVIDIA L4 GPU, bf16, seed 42. Two contrastive-pair datasets released on Hugging Face: 14 attribute pairs / 70 prompts (EN), 20 / 100 (ES).

03Strengths

  • Training-free, minutes on a single consumer GPU — no Sparse Autoencoder required.
  • Two new public, tokenization-verified, bilingual contrastive-pair datasets aligned to BBQ/EsBBQ.
  • Extremely surgical: ≤0.031% of MLP width zeroed, 99.49% mean capability retention.
  • Reports the bidirectional destabilization and sign inversion transparently rather than cherry-picking.
  • Single-neuron ablation (L15:166) falsifies the naive "neuron = bias repository" hypothesis.
  • Cross-architecture, cross-lingual validation: Llama family + Salamandra-2B, English + Spanish.

03Limitations

  • Does not reliably reduce aggregate bias — 6/8 English experiments move in opposite directions, one flips sign.
  • Bound to BBQ/EsBBQ's explicit-attribute, multiple-choice format; no test of implicit-signal or open-ended bias.
  • Restricted to models <4B params; attention mechanisms are excluded from the analysis entirely.
  • Tokenization-parity constraint shrinks the usable pair set for Spanish (Salamandra-2B) vs. English.
  • BiasScore is unsigned by design — the authors identify this as the root cause of instability.
  • Zeroing capped at 40 neurons; larger-scale interventions remain unexplored.

04Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Would an asymmetric corpus + a signed BiasScore actually convert bidirectional destabilization into monotonic bias reduction — and at what neuron-count scale?
  2. Is the final-layer concentration of the bias signal a genuine causal chokepoint, or an artifact of measuring only at the down_proj input?
  3. How would results change if attention heads — excluded here entirely — were included in the candidate search?
  4. Does the localization pattern hold at production scale (7B+ params), or does redundancy grow with model size?
  5. Could the unused FairnessPruningScore's importance term become load-bearing beyond 40 neurons, and where does capability degradation begin?
