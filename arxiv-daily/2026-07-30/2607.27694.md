> **一句话总结：** GyRot 通过"粗粒度旋转+细粒度分组"(CoRFiG)和"谐波对齐置换"(HAP)解决旋转量化与分组量化的不兼容问题，配合全整数反量化设计，在 LLaMA-3-8B 零样本任务(W4A4KV4)上将平均准确率从 Quarot 的 66.96% 提升到 72.98%（全精度为 74.19%），更关键的是当 scale/zero-point 被强制为 INT8 时，竞品 LightRot 的困惑度从 6.80 骤增至 30.12，而 GyRot 仅从 6.81 微升到 6.91，同时加速器相对 Tender 基线实现最高 3.4 倍速度和 3.64 倍能效提升。

> **TL;DR:** GyRot identifies that rotation (global outlier redistribution) and fine-grained group quantization (local scaling) are fundamentally non-cooperative, and fixes this with CoRFiG (coarse rotation scope decoupled from group size) plus Harmonic-Aligned Permutation (aligning outlier channels to Hadamard harmonic rows) plus a reformulated fully-integer asymmetric quantization scheme. On LLaMA-3-8B zero-shot tasks (W4A4KV4, average over PIQA/ARC-e/ARC-c/BoolQ/HellaSwag/WinoGrande), GyRot-INT reaches 72.98% vs. Quarot's 66.96% (FP16 ceiling 74.19%); more tellingly, when scale/zero-point are forced to INT8 for hardware efficiency, the closest accelerator-oriented rival LightRot collapses from 6.80 to 30.12 WikiText-2 perplexity (LLaMA-3-8B, group size 32), while GyRot only degrades from 6.81 to 6.91 — and the resulting 28nm accelerator delivers up to 3.40x speedup and 3.64x energy efficiency over the Tender-W8A8 baseline.

## 核心贡献

- **发现旋转与分组量化的根本冲突**：旋转会把离群值全局摊薄到所有通道，而分组量化依赖局部尺度独立处理离群值；当分组尺寸变小（如 32 通道）时二者结合几乎没有收益，甚至变差（LLaMA3-8B 上 WikiText-2 困惑度从 GQ-only 的约 7.3 上升到接近 16，而叠加旋转后收益消失）。
- **CoRFiG（粗旋转、细分组）**：将旋转作用范围 R（R=2^g·G，R≤1024）与量化分组尺寸 G 解耦，在比分组更大的范围内做旋转以保留局部方差，同时仍能摊薄离群值。
- **HAP（谐波对齐置换）**：利用 Hadamard 矩阵的谐波（每隔 2^k 步长的全 +1/-1 向量）结构，把高幅值离群通道置换到这些谐波行上，使旋转后同一分组内的离群值符号一致，从而收窄组内量化范围、降低对 scale/zero-point 精度的需求；该置换可离线融合进权重，不引入运行时开销。
- **重构非对称量化 + 天花板取整的零点策略**：调换 scale 和 zero-point 的运算顺序，并用 ceiling 而非 round 计算零点，避免零点截断误差导致的下溢裁剪，使得 scale factor 与 zero-point 均可用 INT8 表示而非 FP16/FP8，从而实现全整数反量化。
- **GyRot 加速器**：8×8 脉动阵列 PE，每个 PE 支持 32 路 INT4 点积并内嵌全整数反量化流水线（INT MUL→INT ADD→INT MUL），配合支持在线 Fast Hadamard Transform 的 Fused Vector Unit，在 28nm/1GHz 下相对 Tender 基线实现 45.6% 面积和 51.0% 能耗降低（PE 级）。

## Key Contributions

- **Identifies non-cooperativeness of rotation and fine-grained group quantization**: rotation globally amortizes outliers across all channels while group quantization isolates outlier impact locally; the paper shows empirically (Fig. 1, LLaMA3-8B/WikiText-2) that as group size shrinks (e.g., to 32), combining rotation with group quantization yields little to no PPL improvement over group quantization alone, and even worsens accuracy under RTN.
- **CoRFiG (Coarse Rotation, Fine Grouping)**: decouples the rotation scope R from the quantization group size G via R = 2^g · G (R ≤ 1024), applying rotation over a coarser scope than the quantization group so outlier flattening still happens while local variance within each fine group is preserved.
- **HAP (Harmonic-Aligned Permutation)**: exploits the harmonic (period-2^k, all ±1) rows inherent in Hadamard matrices to permute high-magnitude outlier channels onto these harmonic rows before rotation, so that within each quantization group all outlier contributions share the same sign — producing tightly-bounded, shift-biased per-group distributions and reducing precision requirements on scaling factors. The permutation is fused into weights offline (rotation-invariance), so it adds zero runtime cost for cases where rotation itself can be pre-fused.
- **Reformulated asymmetric quantization with ceiling-based zero-point rounding**: reverses the order of scaling and zero-point biasing (z_x = ⌈−min(x_g)⌉ computed in the unscaled domain) to avoid the clipping/underflow that naive rounding causes under highly asymmetric, small-scale distributions (as induced by HAP), enabling INT8 (rather than FP16/FP8) representation of both scale factor and zero-point with near-FP16 accuracy (6.91 vs 6.81 PPL, Table V).
- **GyRot accelerator**: an 8×8 systolic INT4 tensor PE array (32-way dot product per PE, 2048 MACs/cycle) with a fully-integer in-PE dequantization pipeline (activation scale → zero-point×weight-sum add → weight scale) and a Fused Vector Unit implementing a 5-stage 32-way Fast Hadamard Transform for online rotation scopes up to 1024, achieving state-of-the-art 4-bit accuracy with substantially reduced hardware cost versus prior floating-point-dequantization designs (MANT, LightRot, Tender).

## Methodology

GyRot is built on three layered ideas, evaluated on LLaMA-1 (7B/13B), LLaMA-2 (7B/13B), and LLaMA-3-8B.

1. **CoRFiG**: Standard rotation-based quantization (e.g., Quarot) applies a single global Hadamard rotation across the full channel dimension N_ch, while group quantization partitions channels into small groups G with independent scale/zero-point. GyRot instead rotates within a scope R = 2^g·G that is a power-of-two multiple of the group size (R ≤ 1024, chosen independently from G). This keeps enough channels within scope to flatten outlier distributions (amortization benefit of rotation) while not diluting per-group local structure so much that fine group quantization becomes ineffective. The default configuration used throughout evaluation is G=32, R=1024.

2. **HAP**: Hadamard matrices built recursively via Sylvester's construction (H_{n+1} = [[H_n, H_n],[H_n, −H_n]]) contain "harmonically coarse" ±1 vectors at regular strides of 2^k. HAP identifies globally high-magnitude outlier channels and permutes them (via a hardware-free, weight-fused permutation, exploiting permutation-invariance of linear layers) onto rows at strides G, 2G, 3G, ... within the Hadamard matrix used for rotation. Because these rows are constant-sign (all +1 or all −1) within a group, all permuted outliers combine coherently (e.g., +O1+O2+O3+O4 for one group and +O1−O2+O3−O4 for the adjacent group), producing tightly bounded but strongly biased (asymmetric) per-group post-rotation distributions rather than the noisy mixed-sign spread seen without HAP (visualized in Fig. 4's 3D scatter of activation value vs. channel vs. token).

3. **Reformulated asymmetric quantization**: Conventional asymmetric group quantization computes ẑ_x = ⌊−min(x_g)/s_x⌋ in the *scaled* domain, which under the highly skewed distributions HAP produces leads to long-tailed zero-point distributions and requires FP16/FP8 zero-points to avoid accuracy loss. GyRot instead computes the zero-point in the *unscaled* domain (z_x = −min(x_g), then dequantizes as x̂ = ⌊(x+z_x)/s_x⌋), and additionally replaces standard rounding with a ceiling function when quantizing z_x, guaranteeing z_Q ≥ z so the shifted minimum never underflows and clips. This narrows the per-layer normalized zero-point box-plot distributions substantially (Fig. 5) and permits INT8 scale/zero-point instead of FP16/FP8.

4. **Hardware**: The GyRot PE reorders the reformulated dequantization (Eq. 6) so that activation scale S_X is applied to the partial sum first, then the zero-point term Z_X × W_SUM (precomputed group-wise weight sum) is added, then weight scale S_W is applied — all in INT8/INT32 integer arithmetic, with FP16 conversion only at final output. The accelerator is an 8×8 systolic array of such PEs (32-way INT4 dot product each, 2048 ops/cycle), with a Fused Vector Unit (FVU) containing a 5-stage 32-way Fast Hadamard Transform unit (160 add/subtract units, 2-stage hierarchical scheme supporting rotation scopes up to 32×32=1024) for online rotation of activations after nonlinear/embedding layers, since rotation cannot always be pre-fused into weights across nonlinearities.

## Results & Highlights

- **Perplexity vs. prior rotation methods (Table I, WikiText-2, W4A4KV4, LLaMA-1 & LLaMA-2 models)**: GyRot-FP achieves **5.86/5.22/5.67/5.03 PPL** across LLaMA-1-7B/13B and LLaMA-2-7B/13B, versus Quarot's **6.37/5.59/6.25/5.49** and LightRot's **5.95/5.27/5.73/5.08** (FP16 ceiling: 5.68/5.09/5.47/4.88). GyRot-INT (fully INT8 SF/ZP) stays close at 5.89/5.22/5.88/5.03. Notably, naively combining rotation with the Tender baseline PE catastrophically fails at 4-bit (23.85-55.08 PPL across these four models), underscoring why a dedicated co-design is needed rather than bolting rotation onto an arbitrary accelerator.
- **Zero-shot task accuracy (Table II, W4A4KV4, LLaMA-3-8B)**: GyRot-INT achieves **72.98% average accuracy** across PIQA/ARC-e/ARC-c/BoolQ/HellaSwag/WinoGrande, versus Quarot's **66.96%** and LightRot's **72.43%** (FP16 ceiling 74.19%) — closing Quarot's ~7.2-point accuracy gap from full precision down to ~1.2 points despite using fully integer scale/zero-point.
- **Low-bit weight setting (Table VII, W3A4KV4)**: On LLaMA-3-8B, GyRot-FP reaches **7.73 PPL** versus Quarot's **9.17** and LightRot's **8.00** (a 1.44-point/15.7% reduction vs. Quarot); consistent gains hold across LLaMA-1-7B/13B and LLaMA-2-7B/13B (GyRot-FP: 6.20/5.48/6.16/5.48 vs. Quarot: 6.67/5.82/6.91/5.89).
- **INT8 scale/zero-point robustness — the paper's sharpest finding (Table VI, LLaMA-3-8B, WikiText-2)**: under FP16 SF/ZP, LightRot and GyRot are nearly tied at group size 32 (LightRot 6.80 vs. GyRot 6.81). But once both are forced to **INT8** SF/ZP — the hardware-realistic setting — LightRot's PPL explodes to **30.12** (G32) and **36.33** (G64), while GyRot degrades only marginally to **6.91** (G32) and **7.00** (G64). This is the concrete evidence that GyRot's reformulated asymmetric quantization, not just CoRFiG/HAP, is what makes fully-integer dequantization viable.
- **Hardware speedup/energy (Fig. 11, WikiText-2, geometric mean across LLaMA-2-7B/13B and LLaMA-3-8B)**: GyRot-INT achieves **3.40x speedup** and **3.64x energy efficiency** improvement over the Tender-W8A8 baseline accelerator, and 41.7-67.5% higher speedup / 19.8-31.4% better energy efficiency than the 4-bit MANT and LightRot accelerator baselines.
- **PE-level area/energy (Fig. 10, Section VI-C)**: compared to Tender, GyRot-FP achieves 45.6% area reduction and 51.0% energy savings; GyRot-INT achieves the highest hardware efficiency at **65.2% area reduction and 69.2% energy reduction** over Tender.
- **MT-Bench LLM-as-a-Judge (Fig. 9)**: GyRot-INT achieves adjusted win rates of **66.6%, 68.8%, and 54.7%** against Quarot, SpinQuant, and LightRot respectively (vs. FP16 reference responses over 160 turns), and a component ablation shows each piece contributing incrementally to the final 37.2% win rate against FP16 (Global Rotation baseline 33.1% → +CoRFiG +3.1pp → +HAP +22.8pp recovering from a −17.8pp dip caused by naive INT8 SF quantization → +Reformulation +2.8pp recovering from a −6.9pp dip caused by naive INT8 ZP quantization).

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The core insight — that rotation (global) and group quantization (local) are structurally non-cooperative, empirically demonstrated via the Fig. 1 perplexity-vs-group-size curves — is a genuine and non-obvious observation building on but going beyond AMXFP4's earlier note that small group sizes degrade with rotation. CoRFiG's scope-size decoupling and HAP's exploitation of Hadamard harmonic rows for outlier alignment are the paper's most novel algorithmic contributions; the ceiling-based zero-point rounding is a smaller but concrete fix.

**Rigor**: The ablation in Fig. 9(b) is a genuine strength — it isolates the contribution of each proposed component (CoRFiG, HAP, INT8 SF, INT8 ZP, reformulation) and honestly reports *negative* intermediate effects (e.g., −17.8pp when naively quantizing SF to INT8 before HAP is applied, −6.9pp for naive ZP quantization), which is more transparent than papers that only show monotonic improvement. Table III's grid search over group size (G32/64/128) × rotation size (R32-R1024, Global) for LLaMA-3-8B substantiates the R=1024, G=32 design-point choice rather than asserting it.

**Reproducibility**: Moderate. The RTL is synthesized with a commercial Samsung 28nm PDK via Synopsys Design Compiler — neither the RTL nor the synthesis scripts are open-sourced (no code/repo link found in the paper), which is standard for HPCA hardware papers but limits independent verification of the reported 3.4x/3.64x hardware numbers. The algorithmic side (CoRFiG, HAP, reformulated asymmetric quant) is describable/reimplementable from the equations given, which is a plus.

**Significance**: The practical accuracy gap it closes is large and directly relevant: naive rotation on the Tender baseline PE produces unusable 4-bit perplexity (23.85-55.08 across LLaMA-1/2 models in Table I), and even algorithmic rotation baselines like Quarot leave a real accuracy gap (66.96% vs. 74.19% zero-shot on LLaMA-3-8B). More importantly, Table VI's INT8-forced comparison (LightRot collapsing to 30.12 PPL vs. GyRot's 6.91) demonstrates the paper's central thesis concretely, not just via aggregate hardware numbers.

**Red flags / weaknesses to note**:
- A subtlety worth flagging (and one the paper itself discloses, to its credit, rather than hiding): under FP16-precision scale/zero-point, GyRot's algorithmic contribution (CoRFiG+HAP) is *not* better than LightRot — Table VI shows LightRot slightly ahead of GyRot-FP at every group size (G32: 6.80 vs. 6.81; G64: 6.87 vs. 6.91; G128: 6.99 vs. 7.01). GyRot's real, well-substantiated advantage appears only once SF/ZP are forced to INT8 for hardware efficiency, where LightRot collapses (30.12/36.33/7.69 PPL at G32/64/128) while GyRot barely degrades (6.91/7.00/7.10). Readers should understand GyRot's contribution as specifically about *robustness under integer-only hardware constraints*, not a strict algorithmic accuracy improvement over LightRot in the unconstrained (FP) setting.
- Zero-shot task evaluation (Table II) is restricted to LLaMA-2/LLaMA-3 and omits DuQuant as a baseline (present in the perplexity Table I but absent from Table II), leaving its task-level performance uncompared.
- The headline system-level hardware comparison (Fig. 11, 3.40x/3.64x) is measured against the Tender-W8A8 baseline — an 8-bit-oriented design requiring dedicated modification to support 4-bit at all — while the gain over the more directly comparable 4-bit LightRot/MANT accelerators is reported only as an aggregated 41.7-67.5% speedup / 19.8-31.4% energy-efficiency range, without a clean LightRot-only breakdown in the main figures.
- The MT-Bench LLM-as-a-judge comparison (Fig. 9) uses only 160 turns and does not specify the judge model, temperature, or inter-rater agreement — a fairly thin evaluation for a subjective quality claim, though it is presented as a secondary/qualitative check rather than the primary evidence.

## Strengths

- Clear, empirically grounded articulation of a real conflict (rotation vs. group quantization) rather than assuming they trivially compose, backed by a dedicated motivating figure (Fig. 1) and hardware cost analysis (Fig. 2).
- Component-wise ablation that transparently reports both gains and regressions (Fig. 9b), which builds confidence the reported final numbers are not cherry-picked.
- Full algorithm-to-hardware pipeline: the accuracy-improving algorithmic tricks (CoRFiG, HAP, reformulated asymmetric quant) are explicitly designed to also reduce hardware cost (relaxed SF/ZP precision to INT8), and this is verified with a real RTL implementation and area/power breakdown (Table VIII), not just simulated FLOPs savings.
- Extensive design-space exploration (Tables III-VII) covering group size, rotation size, SF/ZP precision, and rounding strategy, going beyond a single reported configuration.

## Limitations

- Hardware implementation and evaluation rely on a proprietary 28nm process node and commercial EDA tools without released RTL, limiting external reproducibility of the specific speedup/energy numbers.
- GyRot's algorithmic accuracy gain over its closest competitor, LightRot, is essentially zero (even slightly negative) when both use FP16 scale/zero-point (Table VI); the entire benefit is contingent on the INT8-only hardware regime, so the paper's claims should be read as a hardware-constrained result, not a general-purpose quantization accuracy improvement.
- Evaluation is limited to the LLaMA family (1/2/3); no results are shown for other architectures (e.g., Mistral, Qwen, MoE models) where outlier/rotation behavior might differ.
- The FVU's online Fast Hadamard Transform still adds area/power overhead (3.5% area, 0.9% power per Table VIII) for the cases where rotation cannot be pre-fused into weights (nonlinear/embedding boundaries); the paper does not quantify how often this online path is actually triggered during realistic inference workloads (e.g., across LLaMA-3-8B's real layer distribution) versus the offline-fused case.

## Relevance

该工作对关注 LLM 高效推理、低比特量化算法与专用加速器协同设计的研究者非常相关，具体展示了如何通过算法层面的谐波对齐置换来降低硬件反量化开销，是"算法-硬件协同设计"的一个具体范例。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does GyRot's CoRFiG+HAP approach generalize to non-LLaMA architectures (e.g., Mixture-of-Experts models, or models with GQA/sliding-window attention) where outlier channel structure may differ?
- What is the actual runtime frequency of the online FHT path (triggered by nonlinear/embedding layers) during full end-to-end inference, and how does its overhead scale with sequence length or batch size?
- Could the harmonic-alignment idea in HAP be extended to non-Hadamard orthogonal transforms (e.g., learned rotation matrices as in SpinQuant), and would the harmonic-row structure still exist to exploit?
- How sensitive is GyRot's accuracy advantage to the choice of calibration/GPTQ dataset, and does the reformulated zero-point rounding strategy generalize to activation quantization schemes beyond GPTQ-based weight quantization?
- The paper reports R=1024, G=32 as the default from Table III's sweep — how would results change for even more aggressive settings (e.g., 2-bit weights, or W4A4 with KV cache below 4-bit)?
