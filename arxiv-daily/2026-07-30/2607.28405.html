arXiv 2607.28405 · Post-Training Quantization for World Action Models

A post-training quantization framework keeps world-action-model robot policies within 0.2–0.7 points of FP16 accuracy at 4-bit precision.

世界动作模型的后训练量化：W4A4 下仿真成功率仅比 FP16 低 0.2–0.7 个百分点，峰值显存降至约 29%，块级推理提速 1.4–1.6 倍。

QuantWAMs: Calibrating at the Right Granularity for World Action Models ↗
Jiacheng Zhou, Jinfan Lv, Ruixuan Li, et al. · 30 Jul 2026
post-training quantization world action models robot manipulation diffusion policy mixed precision
Key metric
~29%

of FP16's peak weight+activation memory at W4A4 (4.2GB vs. 14.4GB) — with 1.4–1.6× faster block-level inference, at only 0.2–0.7pp simulated accuracy loss.

Why this matters

Robot foundation models that jointly predict video and actions are memory-hungry; this shows they can run at 4-bit with almost no simulated accuracy cost.

Critical edge

On a real robot the gap widens to 6.6 points across just 30 trials — too few, the authors admit, to claim non-inferiority to FP16.

Methodology

  • Problem framing — standard PTQ (GPTQ, SmoothQuant, Atom, SVDQuant) mismatches World Action Models on three axes: structural (cross-module pooling scope), distributional (calibration data vs. reachable closed-loop states), and objective (single-stream scoring vs. the joint video-action training loss).
  • Shared-Basis Outlier Calibration — pools activation-outlier statistics only across "coordinate-admissible" modules that share one quantizer-input coordinate under a common Hadamard basis. A closed-form crossover sample size N* (Proposition 1) decides — via bootstrap over the 32-trajectory calibration set — whether pooling helps or hurts.
  • Co-Training-Objective Saliency — an empirical-Fisher score built from the joint video+action gradient (not a post-hoc fusion of two single-objective scores) ranks Linear layers; the top 20% get upgraded weight precision under a fixed bit budget — kept at layer granularity, since finer element/column-level allocation proved unstable.
  • Fixed-Intervention Rollout Auditing — replays 32 real FP16 closed-loop rollouts under one fixed, unprotected quantization to build a per-denoising-step error profile, then audits (not redesigns) which steps keep high precision — validated on a held-out schedule set before freezing, budget unchanged.
  • Backend — NVFP4 W4A4 + FP8 W8A8 + BF16 for outlier channels on Blackwell (SM120) GPUs; four disjoint trajectory splits (calibration / profiling / validation / test), averaged over 3 protocol seeds.

Strengths

  • Formalizes three concrete failure modes (structural / distributional / objective mismatch) instead of a generic "quantize better" claim.
  • Strong experimental hygiene: disjoint calibration/profiling/validation/test trajectory splits, 3 seeds, and matched-precision-budget baselines that isolate the real source of gains.
  • Validated on two structurally different WAMs (dual-stream MoT vs. shared-backbone DiT) and on a real robot, not just simulation.
  • Repeatedly explicit about scope — states outright where its own claims stop holding, rather than hedging generically.

Limitations

  • Optimizes local surrogates (energy recovery, Fisher saliency, replay profiles), not closed-loop success directly; authors admit ablations don't establish causal attribution.
  • Needs white-box access — task labels, a backward pass through the WAM, FP16 rollouts for profiling, a held-out validation split — heavier than plug-and-play PTQ.
  • Tested on only two architectures, one hardware backend (Blackwell), and block-level only, not end-to-end control-loop latency/memory.
  • Real-robot study is 30 trials total, explicitly underpowered, and shows a larger degradation than simulation.
  • No empirical comparison to embodied-specific quantization baselines (QuantVLA, Qvla) — only generic LLM/diffusion PTQ methods.

Questions for further exploration

  • Does this generalize beyond two coupled modalities — e.g. adding language or proprioception streams?
  • Would fixed-intervention rollout auditing need online re-calibration for out-of-distribution states?
  • How does QuantWAMs quantitatively compare to embodied-specific baselines like QuantVLA or Qvla?
  • What is the real end-to-end (not block-level) control-loop latency and memory gain?
  • Would more real-world profiling trajectories close the 6.6pp real-robot gap, or is it a deeper sim-to-real limit?
