> **一句话总结：** MECA 用多智能体（Explorer 提出/修正、Critic 独立审核）将候选数学命题与其支撑"机制"（Req/Prov/Gap 结构化论证）联合精炼为有向机制图，在 20 篇论文的盲测复原任务上把目标对齐得分从基线 48.0 提升到 69.0（+21.0），并将其生成的 100 个猜想交给独立证明系统 QED 核验后，35% 被证明、11% 被反驳，54% 未解决中 81.5% 源于真正的证明难度而非命题本身的缺陷。

> **TL;DR:** MECA is a multi-agent framework that jointly refines a candidate mathematical conjecture together with the "mechanisms" (typed, auditable proof/refutation arguments) that support it, rather than generating a statement and attaching a proof sketch afterward. On a 20-case article-blind benchmark reconstructing post-2023 papers' conclusions, MECA raises the average target-alignment score from 48.0 (Draft-Audit-Revise baseline) to 69.0/100 (+21.0). On 100 open-ended conjectures submitted statement-only to the independent QED prover (An et al. 2026) under a 5-hour budget, 35% are proved, 11% refuted, 54% unresolved — and 81.5% of the unresolved cases are attributed to genuine technical proof difficulty rather than missing assumptions or ambiguous formulation.

## 核心贡献

- 将猜想构造形式化为候选命题与其支撑数学"机制"（mechanism：连接假设与结论的可复用论证/结构化原理）的联合精炼过程，而非"先猜后证"的传统流水线。
- 提出 MECA 框架：Explorer 智能体提出/修正/拆分机制与命题，Critic 智能体独立审核机制的正确性与研究价值，二者通过有向无环的"机制图" (mechanism graph) 交互，显式追踪已确立步骤与未解缺口。
- 设计三阶段流水线（源基种子构造 → 机制-问题协同探索 → 面向证明器的最终问题投影），并引入与主流程信息隔离的 Final Challenger 以降低确认偏差。
- 构造并公开 100 个 prover-facing 半开放数学问题（P001–P100），带显式假设集与机制包，覆盖优化、变分算子等 8 大数学领域。
- 在"论文级结论复原（article-blind）"与"开放式猜想生成 + 独立证明系统核验"两个互补实验中系统评估该方法，量化暴露当前自动证明/反驳系统的能力边界。

## Key Contributions

- Formulates conjecture construction as the **joint refinement** of a candidate statement and its supporting mathematical mechanisms — a mechanism `m = (Req(m), Prov(m), Gap(m))` records what it requires, what it establishes, and what remains unresolved.
- Introduces **MECA** (MEchanism-Centered Conjecture Agent): Explorer agents propose/revise/split candidates and mechanisms; Critic agents (Mechanism Critic, Coverage Judge) independently audit correctness, compositional validity, and research value; a **Final Challenger** deliberately withholds the mechanism graph and exploration history to reduce confirmation bias.
- A three-stage pipeline: (1) source-grounded seed construction from literature or curated open problems, (2) mechanism–problem co-exploration governed by an explicit coverage-decision protocol (`InsufficientSupport` / `SubstantivePartial` / `FullCoverage`), (3) deterministic projection into a self-contained, prover-facing problem statement.
- Releases **100 semi-open problems** (92 literature-derived + 8 from curated open problems) with explicit unresolved mathematical cores, spanning 8 broad domains (optimization dominates at 35% of the sample, followed by variational operators at 14%).
- Two-pronged evaluation: article-blind reconstruction of 20 post-2023 target papers' conclusions, and independent proof/refutation attempts on the 100 generated conjectures via the QED multi-agent prover.

## Methodology

MECA represents the construction state at iteration *t* as `C_t = (A, p_t, G_t)`: an immutable **target anchor** `A` (central object, admissible assumptions, source boundary, forbidden trivializations), a mutable **candidate statement** `p_t`, and an audited **mechanism graph** `G_t`. A mechanism is more than a proof-technique label — it must contain an independently reviewable derivation/construction/counterexample argument, characterized by its requirements `Req(m)`, established consequences `Prov(m)`, and remaining gaps `Gap(m)`. Mechanisms compose into a DAG via `Prov(m_i) ∩ Req(m_j) ≠ ∅` edges and must be non-circular.

**Stage 1 — Source-grounded seed construction.** Route A (literature-driven) uses a Seed Generator/Validator pair over anonymized "source cards" distilled from structured paper collections; Route B (open-problem adaptation) extracts seeds from a curated set of broad open problems. Each raw seed records the closest known result, a bounded delta beyond it, an anticipated bottleneck, and concrete failure tests. Independent mathematical and research-value critics review each seed (survive / repair / reject / audit-incomplete), bounded by per-slot repair/replacement ceilings (Table 6: e.g., 4 seed repairs, 3 replacements per direction).

**Stage 2 — Mechanism–problem co-exploration (Algorithm 1).** A Mechanism–Frontier Explorer proposes one legal action per turn (propose/add mechanism, revise/split the candidate, request refutation, emit, reject, or quarantine a resolution). A Mechanism Critic independently replays each mechanism's derivation and returns `promote` / `repair` / `reject` / `revise_frontier` / `resolution_candidate`. A Coverage Judge composes promoted mechanisms into a dependency-ordered chain and issues one of three verdicts: `InsufficientSupport` (missing capability), `SubstantivePartial` (a genuine nonroutine part of the route is established, with 1–3 precise unresolved cores remaining — the intended stopping state), or `FullCoverage` (quarantined for independent verification rather than presented as an open conjecture).

**Stage 3 — Prover-facing construction.** A **Final Challenger**, which receives the candidate, anchor, seed, and source pack but explicitly *not* the mechanism graph, critic reports, or exploration history, runs four independent audits (statement, counterexample, value, difficulty) before a candidate is deterministically projected `q = Θ(A, p, G')` into a self-contained problem. Unresolved cores become explicit proof obligations; the statement is stress-tested by a Blind Prover / Refute Prover pair (evaluated without the mechanism bundle), then deduplicated and admitted under domain quotas.

Models: GPT-5.5 (reasoning effort "xhigh") drives both MECA and the Draft-Audit-Revise baseline generation, and also the target-alignment evaluator; GPT-5.6-sol (xhigh) is used inside QED, the external prover/verifier system (An et al., 2026) used for downstream conjecture verification.

## Results & Highlights

- **Target-alignment recovery (Table 1, 20-case article-blind benchmark):** MECA's average score is **69.0/100** versus **48.0/100** for the Draft-Audit-Revise baseline (Δ = **+21.0**), on a rubric summing Setting (20 pts), Assumptions (15), Conclusion (30), Quantitative (20), and Boundary (15). Per-dimension gains: Conclusion 21.0 vs 15.0 (**+6.0**), Quantitative 12.5 vs 7.0 (**+5.5**), Boundary 6.0 vs 2.5 (+3.5), Assumptions 11.5 vs 8.0 (+3.5), Setting 18.0 vs 15.5 (+2.5).
- **QED outcomes on 100 generated conjectures (Figure 4):** **35% proved**, **11% refuted**, **54% unresolved** — i.e., 46 of 100 conjectures received an independently verified positive or negative resolution within QED's 5-hour wall-clock budget per case.
- **Causes of the 54 unresolved cases (Figure 5):** 44 cases (**81.5%**) attributed to genuine *technical proof difficulty* (well-posed, adequate assumptions, plausible route identified, but the required estimate/construction exceeds QED's capability/budget), 6 (11.1%) to *missing assumptions*, 2 (3.7%) to *insufficient foothold*, and 2 (3.7%) to *ambiguous formulation* — so only ~10% of all 100 conjectures show an identifiable formulation or proof-readiness defect.
- **Domain distribution (Figure 3):** the 100 conjectures skew heavily toward continuous optimization — Optimization 35% (incl. decentralized tracking 12%, derivative-free optimization 10%) and Variational operators 14% (incl. Bregman proximal methods), with the remainder split across control/stochastic, HJB safety, error bounds, low-rank recovery, manifold SDP, OMWU dynamics, and related areas.

Zaiwen Wen is an established figure in numerical optimization (Beijing International Center for Mathematical Research), which lends mathematical-rigor credibility to the mechanism formalism, but the team is small and not affiliated with a large AI4Math lab (contrast with DeepMind's AlphaEvolve/FunSearch or Google's Ramanujan-style systems cited in related work). The QED prover used as the sole verification backend is itself a very recent (2026), non-peer-reviewed arXiv preprint from a different author group, and MECA's own comparison baseline (Draft-Audit-Revise) is self-designed rather than an established competing system — no comparison to other published conjecture-generation approaches (e.g., LeanConjecturer, Romera-Paredes et al.'s FunSearch, ResearchAgent) is reported.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The "mechanism-centered" formalism — representing partial proof/refutation arguments as typed `(Req, Prov, Gap)` objects composed into an audited DAG, with formal coverage decisions (`InsufficientSupport`/`SubstantivePartial`/`FullCoverage`) — is a genuinely distinctive structural contribution relative to prior conjecture-generation work that mostly searches a fixed class of objects (Ramanujan Machine, AlphaEvolve-style program search) rather than jointly refining a statement with its supporting reasoning graph.
- **Rigor concern — baseline weakness:** The only comparator is a self-constructed Draft-Audit-Revise ablation given the identical budget and information boundary; the reported +21-point gain therefore shows MECA beats one internally designed straw-man, not the state of the art in automated conjecturing.
- **Rigor concern — judge/generator overlap:** The primary metric (Table 1) is scored by "an independent evaluator" that is the same GPT-5.5 model family (3 replicas, majority vote) used to generate both MECA's and the baseline's outputs — an LLM-judges-LLM setup with no human/expert mathematician cross-check reported for the 100-point rubric.
- **Data quirk:** The 20 target papers, their article-blind 7-paper source packs, and the 100 conjectures are all drawn from "a private repository of mathematical papers" curated by the authors themselves rather than an existing public benchmark, raising selection-bias and non-reproducibility concerns (materials are not confirmed public in the sections reviewed).
- **Missing ablations:** No experiment isolates which architectural piece (mechanism graph vs. a flat critic loop, Coverage Judge, Final Challenger) drives the reported improvements — it is plausible most of the gain comes simply from more critic/revision rounds rather than the mechanism formalism itself.
- **Self-graded failure taxonomy:** The causes-of-unresolved breakdown in Figure 5 (81.5% "technical proof difficulty") is produced using MECA's own audit criteria (Section E.3) rather than an independent adjudicator, so it is unverified whether "genuine difficulty" is reliably distinguished from a subtler formulation defect that the pipeline's own bias would tend to under-report.
- **Domain concentration:** Roughly half the 100-conjecture benchmark is continuous/numerical optimization and operator theory; no evidence is given that the "mechanism" abstraction transfers to combinatorics, number theory, or algebra, where proof structures look qualitatively different.

## Strengths

- Clear, well-specified formalism (mechanisms as `(Req, Prov, Gap)` triples, mechanism graphs, coverage decisions) that makes pipeline decisions auditable rather than opaque LLM judgment calls.
- Deliberate anti-confirmation-bias design: the Final Challenger and Blind/Refute Provers are explicitly denied access to the mechanism graph and critic history, so a plausible internal narrative cannot substitute for independent verification.
- Contributes a concrete, reusable artifact — 100 semi-open problems with explicit unresolved cores across 8 math domains — that could serve as a benchmark for future theorem-proving systems.
- Unusually detailed protocol documentation (explicit stopping-rule ceilings, four-layer prompt/skill architecture, receipt-invalidation semantics) that goes well beyond the typical vague methodology descriptions in LLM-agent papers.

## Limitations

- Single, self-designed baseline (Draft-Audit-Revise); no comparison to other published conjecture-generation or research-agent systems.
- Evaluation relies substantially on LLM-as-judge scoring from the same model family used for generation, rather than independent human mathematician review.
- Benchmark materials (20 target papers, source packs, 100 conjectures) are entirely author-curated from a private repository, not an existing public dataset.
- No ablation studies isolating which pipeline component drives the reported improvements.
- Domain coverage skews heavily toward continuous optimization and operator theory; generalization to other mathematical areas is untested.
- QED's 5-hour, statement-only budget is an arbitrary operational choice; "unresolved" cannot distinguish genuinely hard conjectures from ones that merely need more search time or a different prover.

## Relevance

该工作为 AI4Math、LLM 多智能体协作与自动定理证明流水线的研究提供了一种"猜想生成"与"证明机制审计"耦合的可复用设计范式，其发布的 100 个带显式未解核心的半开放问题也可作为评测新证明系统的现成基准，值得关注但需注意其自定义基线与同源模型评分带来的客观性局限。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the 54% unresolved / 81.5% "technical difficulty" breakdown hold with provers other than QED (e.g., Lean/Isabelle-based systems, or human mathematicians), or is it prover-specific?
- What is the individual contribution of each architectural component (Coverage Judge, Final Challenger, mechanism graph vs. a flat critic loop) via controlled ablation?
- Would the target-alignment rubric scores hold up under blinded human-expert grading rather than same-model-family LLM evaluation?
- Can the mechanism abstraction generalize beyond continuous optimization/operator theory (which dominate the 100-conjecture sample) to areas like number theory, combinatorics, or algebraic geometry?
- How sensitive are the reported gains to the specific backbone model (GPT-5.5/GPT-5.6-sol); would an open-weight LLM reproduce comparable mechanism-audit quality?
- Does the Final Challenger's blindness to the mechanism graph still leak information through the frozen source pack and seed, partially undermining the anti-confirmation-bias design?
