Key Metric

0.844

peak Spearman ρ between accel and costly 32-seed resampling divergence — π0.5 × D3IL, best denoising prefix — read off a single free forward pass.

0.66 avg TPR@FPR=0.10 as an online failure detector — only 0.02 behind the far pricier, success/failure-labeled SAFE probe (0.68).

Why This Matters

Gives flow-matching robot policies (VLA / world models) a zero-training, zero-latency confidence signal for safe deployment, plus a reusable blueprint for finer uncertainty diagnostics.

Critical Edge

Accel is admittedly coarse: it misses confidently-wrong (not merely uncertain) actions and degrades on under-trained models — the geometry it reads is real, but it isn't the whole story.

How the free number gets derived

Σ=0 → affine-isotropic sink
(Theorem 1)
bend ∝ Cov(x₁|xₛ)
(Theorem 2, Eq. 7)
  1. Velocity field points toward the posterior mean, scaled by 1/(1−s) (Eq. 3).
  2. Theorem 1 — a maximally-certain field (Σ=0) is exactly straight-line, constant-speed, zero-acceleration flow.
  3. Theorem 2 — any bend in the field, J+I/(1−s), is exactly proportional to the target posterior covariance. Uncertainty is geometry.
  4. Measuring the field directly needs extra passes, so instead integrate the same bend along the one trajectory already run: accel = Σ‖Δv‖ / Σ‖v‖ (Eq. 10–11, Algorithm 1).
  5. Proposition A.1 — bending occurs iff the posterior-mean target itself is moving, licensing a single pass as a field-level proxy.
  6. Validation — ground truth is 32-seed Monte-Carlo resample divergence; for deployment, accel feeds a CUSUM monitor calibrated on 50 held-out successful rollouts.

Strengths

  • A practical heuristic derived from, and provably tied to, an exact theorem (Thm 2 + Prop A.1) — not just an empirical correlation.
  • Genuinely zero marginal cost: reads Euler steps the policy already computes, unlike every baseline (32 resamples and/or trained probes).
  • Broad footprint: 4 distinct FM-VLA architectures × 3 benchmarks × a controllable toy model, all 12 real-model cells positive.
  • Transparent failure taxonomy (Appendix A) — false negatives/positives categorized by root cause, not one aggregate number.
  • Fixes a subtle prior flaw: time-indexed conformal calibration degenerates on long rollouts; the paper's CUSUM calibration avoids it.

Limitations

  • Monotonicity guarantee is local and in-expectation only (small-spread regime) — not pointwise or global (Prop A.4, self-disclosed).
  • Coarse signal: misses confidently-wrong actions and degrades on under-trained models (GR00T N1.7 flagged undertrained on LIBERO-all).
  • Inconsistent across environments — noticeably weaker on RoboCasa than LIBERO/D3IL, and the paper doesn't explain why.
  • Still trails the costlier, labeled SAFE baseline (0.66 vs 0.68) — competitive, not strictly dominant.
  • Framed by the authors as demonstrating utility, not a new SOTA method — CUSUM/calibration choices aren't exhaustively ablated.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Can accel be decomposed per-dimension or per-timestep to localize which action/moment is bending — recovering some of the "accel-blind" misses?
  • Why does performance drop specifically on RoboCasa — genuinely harder failure geometry, or an artifact of chunk-length settings across benchmarks?
  • Would correlation and detection numbers change with a properly-converged GR00T N1.7 checkpoint instead of the flagged undertrained one?
  • Could accel act as a free pre-filter that only invokes SAFE's expensive labeled probe when ambiguous — closing the 0.02 TPR gap at a fraction of the cost?
