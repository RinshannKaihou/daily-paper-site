> **一句话总结：** 该研究在同一冻结基座模型下对比两种教学策略——极简对话式 ConvTutor 与四节点路由式 PedTutor——发现条件盲判官 Claude Opus 4.8 打的通用"有用性"分数在 Sonnet 基座上几乎无法区分二者（Cliff's δ=0.10，p=.160），而专门设计的教学法量规却给出完美等级分离（δ=1.0，均分 2.578 对 4.299）；用第二判官 GPT-5.6 Sol 复核同一批 1179 个回合后，有用性排序在三个基座里的两个上发生反转，唯有"答案泄漏→下一轮学生独立推理下降"这一确定性过程指标（β 在三个基座上分别为 -0.386、-0.535、-0.592，均 p<1e-11）在所有基座上保持稳定，说明通用有用性并非可靠的教学法信号。

> **TL;DR:** This pre-registered audit compares two math-tutoring policies (minimal single-call ConvTutor vs. a four-node routed PedTutor) instantiated from identical frozen weights across three tutor bases (Claude Sonnet 4.6, GPT-5.5, Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview) against a deliberately weak simulated student (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, ~11% unaided accuracy); on the primary Sonnet base, condition-blind judge Claude Opus 4.8 finds the two policies statistically indistinguishable on general helpfulness (Cliff's δ=0.10, raw diff -0.094, 95% CI [-0.251,+0.058], p=.160) while a pre-registered pedagogy rubric gives perfect rank separation (δ=1.0, 2.578 vs. 4.299, p=.0020), and a second judge (GPT-5.6 Sol) reverses the helpfulness ordering on 2 of 3 bases (e.g., GPT-5.5 base: Opus favors PedTutor at δ=-.74 while Sol favors ConvTutor at δ=+.70, both p<.02) even as the deterministic leakage→next-turn-independence coupling replicates on every base (β=-0.386 to -0.592, all p<1e-11) — evidence that general-purpose LLM-judged helpfulness is not a trustworthy proxy for pedagogical quality.

## 核心贡献

- 构建了严格受控的对照实验：ConvTutor（每轮一次模型调用的极简"有用助手"提示）与 PedTutor（学生状态追踪器 + 三种教学原则驱动的响应器组成的四节点路由策略，每轮两次模型调用）均来自完全相同的冻结基座权重，把"教学策略"从"底层模型能力"中彻底剥离出来。
- 设计了两套确定性、与判官无关的过程指标——答案泄漏率（按题目预先声明的数值/代数答案做字符串匹配）和下一轮学生独立推理（学生在下一轮是否先尝试推理再看老师说话）——作为不依赖任何 LLM 主观判断的客观锚点，并证明这一"泄漏→独立性下降"的耦合关系在三个基座上全部显著（p 均小于 1e-11）。
- 采用完整的预注册推断流程（P1/P2/P3 操纵检验 + J1/J2 headline 耦合假设、Holm 校正、冻结判官打分顺序），并在主分析冻结之后再引入第二个独立事后判官 GPT-5.6 Sol，对同一批 1179 个确认性回合重新打分做稳健性审计，发现有用性排序在 Sonnet 与 GPT-5.5 两个基座上发生了方向性反转。
- 在 Sonnet 基座上做了 7 策略消融（含纯提示苏格拉底式变体、去级联/去闸门/去追踪器变体），证明结构化路由并非获得高教学法评分的必要条件——纯提示的苏格拉底式变体教学法评分甚至超过完整 PedTutor（+0.565，δ=0.96），同时揭示了"判官可见质量"与"下一轮学生独立性"之间可以解离。

## Key Contributions

- **A controlled within-base design**: ConvTutor (a single-call, minimally-instructed "helpful tutor") and PedTutor (a four-node routed policy — a private learner-state tracker followed by one of a problem-decomposer, answer-deferral gate, or hint-cascade responder, costing two model calls per visible turn) are instantiated from *identical frozen weights* across three tutor bases, so any measured difference is attributable to policy, not underlying model capability.
- **Two deterministic, judge-invariant process measures**: answer leakage (string-level match against pre-declared canonical numeric/algebraic answer forms) and next-turn student independence (whether the student attempts a reasoning step before the tutor speaks again), which anchor the audit independent of any LLM's opinion and reveal a leakage→reduced-independence coupling that replicates on all three bases (β=-0.386 to -0.592, all p<1.1e-11).
- **A fully pre-registered inference plan** (manipulation checks P1/P3, live test P2, headline coupling J1/J2, Holm-corrected, with decision rules frozen before confirmatory data collection) followed by a prospectively-specified second judge (GPT-5.6 Sol) that rescores the identical 1,179 confirmatory answer-phase turns, exposing that the ConvTutor–PedTutor helpfulness ordering flips between judges on 2 of 3 bases while the pedagogy rubric's direction never flips wherever a difference is detected.
- **A seven-policy Sonnet-only ablation** showing a prompt-only Socratic instruction can match or exceed the fully routed PedTutor's judged-pedagogy score (paired diff +0.565, 95% CI [+0.348,+0.817], δ=+0.96, 10/10 replicate pairs), while judged helpfulness stays compressed within a narrow 0.25-point band (4.70–4.95) across all seven policies versus a 2.3-point spread in judged pedagogy — locating the measurement failure in the helpfulness signal itself, not in any single tutor design.

## Methodology

The study is a within-base comparison of two tutoring policies against a common measurement apparatus, holding the tutor's base model fixed and varying only the policy layer (control flow and prompts) that sits above it. **ConvTutor** issues one realistic "helpful tutor" instruction per visible turn (one model call). **PedTutor** is a four-node routed policy over the same frozen model: a learner-state tracker runs on every visible turn (never shown to the student), then a deterministic rule dispatches to exactly one of three responders — a problem-decomposer, an answer-deferral gate, or a hint-cascade (two model calls per turn). A no-tutoring **cold** baseline completes the design (fixed problem order, condition order, and student's initial context per replicate). The simulated student is a deliberately weak model, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (served via a single provider route pinned to Groq, temperature 0.8), chosen because it solves only ~11% of the mixture/weighted-average problems in isolation, so tutoring is load-bearing rather than redundant.

Each session runs a fixed 19-problem, five-phase protocol (six tutored training problems, then three immediate, four interference, three delayed, and three transfer probes answered unassisted from the student's accumulated context). All process measures are computed over each training problem's **answer phase** (up to the student's first committed final answer). Two prespecified deterministic detectors — **answer leakage** (string-match against pre-declared canonical answer forms) and **student independence** (whether the student attempts a reasoning step before the next tutor turn) — require no LLM judgment. **Annotator-perceived helpfulness** and a symmetric, pre-registered **judged pedagogy** rubric (four subscores plus a holistic 5-point rating, scored on the same turns) are both rated three times per turn by a frozen, condition-blind primary judge, Claude Opus 4.8. The study is replicated on three tutor bases — Claude Sonnet 4.6 (primary), GPT-5.5, and Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview — with ten replicates per condition per base (90 confirmatory sessions). After freezing the Opus scores, GPT-5.6 Sol was prospectively specified as a post hoc robustness judge, rescoring the identical 1,179 confirmatory turns under the same two rubrics. Pre-registered confirmatory inference uses two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests paired by replicate (α=.05), Cliff's δ, and bootstrap 95% CIs for session-level manipulation checks (P1 leakage, P3 independence) and the live helpfulness test (P2); the headline claim (J2) is a per-turn mixed-effects coupling model (crossed replicate/problem random intercepts) fit within each base over pooled ConvTutor+PedTutor answer-phase turns (n=358 on the primary base), with a policy-adjusted sensitivity variant. A separate compute-fairness re-analysis matches the two policies' in-window visible-turn budgets (PedTutor naturally uses more turns, 22.3 vs. 13.5 per session) to rule out a turn-budget confound. A seven-policy ablation (two ConvTutor prompt variants, three PedTutor structural-ablation variants) is evaluated descriptively under Opus only on the primary base.

## Results & Highlights

- **Manipulation check (P1, Sonnet/leakage)**: ConvTutor's mean per-session answer-leakage fraction is 43.0% versus PedTutor's 6.4% (paired diff +0.365, 95% CI [+0.217,+0.516], significant in 9 of 10 replicate pairs) — confirming the intended leakage contrast exists on the primary base.
- **Live helpfulness test vs. pedagogy contrast (P2, Sonnet, Table 2a)**: under the primary judge Opus, judged helpfulness does not differ significantly between ConvTutor and PedTutor (Δ=-0.094, 95% CI [-0.251,+0.058], Cliff's δ=-0.10, p=.160), whereas judged pedagogy on the *same turns* is perfectly rank-separated (ConvTutor mean 2.578 vs. PedTutor 4.299, Δ=-1.721, δ=-1.00, p=.0020) — the paper's core dissociation between a general-purpose helpfulness signal and a pedagogy-targeted rubric.
- **Pre-registered headline coupling (J2, Sonnet, n=358 answer-phase turns)**: a leaky turn predicts a higher helpfulness rating (coefficient +0.303, 95% CI [+0.169,+0.437], p=9.1×10⁻⁶) and deterministically predicts less independent student work on the following turn (coefficient -0.386, 95% CI [-0.495,-0.276], p=5.6×10⁻¹²); descriptively, leaky turns are rated 4.95 vs. 4.67 for non-leaky turns and are followed by a student reasoning attempt only 33% of the time versus 73% for non-leaky turns.
- **Cross-base replication of the deterministic coupling**: the leakage→next-turn-independence risk difference replicates on all three bases — Sonnet β=-0.386 (n=358, p=5.6×10⁻¹²), GPT-5.5 β=-0.535 (n=379, p=2.1×10⁻³²), Gemini β=-0.592 (n=442, p=9.1×10⁻²¹) — versus a judge-contingent helpfulness ordering that flips sign between Opus and the second judge GPT-5.6 Sol on the Sonnet and GPT-5.5 bases (e.g., GPT-5.5: Opus Δ=-0.376, δ=-.74, p=.0020 favoring PedTutor vs. Sol Δ=+0.086, δ=+.70, p=.0156 favoring ConvTutor on the identical turns).
- **Seven-policy ablation (Sonnet, Opus-only, Figure 2b)**: a prompt-only Socratic revision of ConvTutor outscores the fully routed PedTutor on judged pedagogy (paired diff +0.565, 95% CI [+0.348,+0.817], δ=+0.96, 10/10 replicate pairs at near-tied helpfulness), while judged helpfulness stays within a narrow 0.25-point band (4.70–4.95) across all seven policies against a 2.3-point spread in judged pedagogy — showing routed structure is not required to earn a high pedagogy score, though a "cascade-free" PedTutor variant that raises both pedagogy (+0.535, δ=0.96) and helpfulness (+0.151) simultaneously records the *lowest* next-turn independence of the whole pedagogy family, a judge-visible-quality/process-independence dissociation.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The core question — does a general-purpose LLM-judged helpfulness score capture pedagogically relevant behavior, or does it just reward answer-giving — is a well-motivated construct-validity question given documented LLM-as-judge biases (verbosity, self-preference) and prior field evidence that helpful-tuned tutors give stuck students the answer rather than withholding it. Casting this as a controlled within-base, cross-judge, pre-registered audit (rather than a single anecdotal comparison) is a genuine methodological contribution, even though the underlying concern (helpfulness ≠ pedagogy) is not itself new.

**Rigor**: The pre-registration is unusually careful for this literature — decision rules, effect-size thresholds, and the pedagogy rubric were frozen before Opus scores existed, and a second independent judge (GPT-5.6 Sol) was prospectively specified to rescore the *identical* transcripts as a robustness check rather than a post hoc excuse. The compute-fairness re-analysis (matching PedTutor's 22.3 vs. ConvTutor's 13.5 turns per session) is a specific, paper-native confound the authors identified and tested rather than ignored, and the coupling held under it (n=256, p=2.4×10⁻⁴ and p=7.5×10⁻⁸ for the two legs).

**Reproducibility**: Code is released (github.com/bydeng01/conv-vs-ped-tutor per the paper's front page), and the appendix documents the frozen 19-problem instrument, rubrics, and measurement definitions in detail. However, reproducing the exact evaluator behavior depends on two specific proprietary model snapshots (Claude Opus 4.8, GPT-5.6 Sol) whose scoring could drift or be deprecated, and the weak-student precondition is tied to a specific serving route (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct via a single OpenRouter-to-Groq path with fallbacks disabled) that the authors themselves note is a property of "the model and its serving route together, not the model identifier alone" — a fairly fragile reproducibility anchor.

**Significance / red flags**: A genuinely paper-specific weak point is the Gemini base's manipulation check: ConvTutor's per-session leakage fraction on Gemini is only 11.3% (versus 43.0% on Sonnet and 76.6% on GPT-5.5), so the leakage manipulation itself is small and statistically nonsignificant there (δ=+.32, p=.625) — meaning one of the three "replication" bases essentially lacks a working experimental contrast, weakening the strength of the "replicated across three bases" framing. Session-level sample sizes are also small (10 replicates per condition per base), which the authors candidly acknowledge rules out only large effects (d≳1) — the paper's own honesty about this is a strength, but it caps how much can be concluded from the non-significant P2 helpfulness null. Gemini's provider also enforces a non-disableable minimum reasoning setting, so the third base is not a pure like-for-like model swap, a confound the authors flag explicitly rather than paper over. The study also runs on a single algebra/mixture-problem domain with one simulated student and no human validation, and probe accuracy is near-ceiling (0.77–1.00) across all conditions because the student's carried context accumulates worked examples — an explicitly acknowledged design property that prevents the probes from separating durable learning from in-context answer retrieval.

## Strengths

- Genuinely controlled comparison: two tutoring policies built on identical frozen weights isolates the policy layer as the sole manipulated variable, ruling out "different model capability" as an alternative explanation.
- Two deterministic, judge-free process measures (answer leakage, next-turn independence) give an evaluator-independent anchor that the paper explicitly leverages to validate (or invalidate) the LLM judges' behavior.
- Full pre-registration plus a prospectively-specified second judge rescoring identical transcripts is a rare and commendable level of rigor for LLM-as-judge auditing work, and the paper reports the resulting judge-contingent reversal candidly rather than suppressing it.
- The seven-policy ablation and compute-fairness re-analysis are both paper-native robustness checks (not reviewer-requested afterthoughts) that materially strengthen the headline claim.

## Limitations

- The Gemini base's leakage manipulation check is weak and nonsignificant (11.3% ConvTutor leakage vs. 43.0%/76.6% on the other two bases, δ=+.32, p=.625), so the "three-base replication" is really a two-base replication for the manipulation-dependent claims (P1, J1).
- Small session-level sample (10 replicates per condition per base) limits sensitivity to large effects only, by the authors' own admission.
- Gemini's provider-enforced minimum reasoning setting means the third base is not a pure base-model swap, confounding cross-base comparisons with a reasoning-budget difference.
- Single domain (mixture/weighted-average algebra word problems), single weak student model on a single serving route, and no human validation of either rubric — the authors explicitly scope the study as evaluator-audit only, not a claim about human learning.
- Probe accuracy saturates near ceiling (0.77–1.00) across all conditions because the student's context accumulates worked examples, so delayed/transfer probes cannot separate durable learning from in-context retrieval.
- The leakage→independence association is observational (not causal) since leakage is not randomized at the turn level, a limitation the authors state directly.

## Relevance

该研究直接触及"如何评价 LLM 教学/助手 Agent"这一实践问题：如果研究者或产品团队仍在用通用 LLM-as-judge 的"有用性"打分来筛选或优化教学策略，这项工作提示这一信号可能是判官相关且不稳定的，应搭配确定性、领域特定的过程指标（如答案泄漏率、学生独立推理率）一起使用，这对做 LLM 评估方法学、教育科技 Agent 或 RLHF 奖励模型设计的研究者有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the helpfulness-vs-pedagogy dissociation and the judge-contingent ordering reversal replicate with human expert raters in place of (or alongside) the two LLM judges, given the paper explicitly declines to make any human-validity claim?
- Why does Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview produce such a weak ConvTutor leakage rate (11.3%) compared to Sonnet (43.0%) and GPT-5.5 (76.6%) — is this a base-model-family effect, a reasoning-budget effect, or an artifact of the provider-enforced minimum reasoning setting that could not be disabled?
- Does the leakage→independence coupling remain negative if leakage is experimentally randomized at the turn level (rather than left as an observational, policy-induced covariate), which would let the authors upgrade the association to a causal claim?
- Would training a reward model or fine-tuning a tutor against the pedagogy rubric (rather than only auditing frozen policies) actually improve downstream student learning, or would it just shift the judge-contingency problem to a different, pedagogy-adjacent evaluator?
- How much of the Socratic prompt-only variant's ability to match or beat the fully routed PedTutor on judged pedagogy is a genuine pedagogical improvement versus an artifact of Opus's own stylistic preferences for Socratic-sounding language?
