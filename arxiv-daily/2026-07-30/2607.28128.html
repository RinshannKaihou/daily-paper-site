Why this matters

If you're screening tutoring or teaching agents with generic LLM-as-judge helpfulness scores, that signal may be judge-dependent and unstable — pair it with deterministic process metrics.

Critical edge

The Gemini base's leakage manipulation check is weak and nonsignificant (11.3% vs. 43.0% / 76.6% on the other two bases, δ=+.32, p=.625) — so the "three-base replication" is really a two-base replication for judge-dependent claims.

01 Methodology

Same frozen weights, two policy layers, three bases (Sonnet 4.6, GPT-5.5, Gemini 3.1 Pro). The simulated student is deliberately weak — Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, ~11% unaided accuracy on mixture/weighted-average algebra problems — so tutoring is load-bearing.

  • 19-problem, five-phase protocol: 6 tutored training problems → 3 immediate + 4 interference + 3 delayed + 3 transfer probes, answered unassisted.
  • Two judge-free detectors computed only over each training problem's answer phase: answer leakage (string-match vs. canonical answers) and next-turn student independence (does the student reason before the tutor speaks again).
  • Two LLM-scored rubrics on the same turns: annotator-perceived helpfulness and a symmetric, pre-registered pedagogy rubric — rated 3× per turn by frozen, condition-blind Opus 4.8, then rescored on identical turns by a prospectively-specified second judge, GPT-5.6 Sol.
  • Pre-registered inference: two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank (α=.05), Cliff's δ, bootstrap CIs for manipulation checks (P1/P3) and the live helpfulness test (P2); the headline coupling (J2) is a per-turn mixed-effects model with crossed replicate/problem random intercepts, plus a turn-budget sensitivity check (PedTutor uses 22.3 visible turns/session vs. ConvTutor's 13.5).

7-policy ablation (Sonnet, Opus-only): a prompt-only Socratic ConvTutor variant beats the fully-routed PedTutor on judged pedagogy (+0.565, δ=0.96, 10/10 replicate pairs) while judged helpfulness stays compressed in a 4.70–4.95 band across all seven policies — a 2.3-point pedagogy spread vs. a 0.25-point helpfulness spread on the same policies.

Strengths

  • Two tutoring policies built on identical frozen weights isolates the policy layer as the sole manipulated variable — rules out "different model capability" as an alternative explanation.
  • Two deterministic, judge-free process measures (leakage, next-turn independence) give an evaluator-independent anchor used to validate or invalidate the LLM judges' own behavior.
  • Full pre-registration plus a prospectively-specified second judge rescoring identical transcripts — the paper reports the resulting reversal candidly rather than suppressing it.
  • The seven-policy ablation and compute-fairness re-analysis are paper-native robustness checks, not reviewer-requested afterthoughts.

Limitations

  • Gemini's leakage manipulation check is weak/nonsignificant (11.3% vs. 43.0%/76.6%, δ=+.32, p=.625) — really a two-base replication for manipulation-dependent claims.
  • Small session-level sample (10 replicates/condition/base) — by the authors' own admission, sensitive only to large effects.
  • Gemini's provider-enforced minimum reasoning setting means the third base isn't a pure model swap — confounds cross-base comparison with a reasoning-budget difference.
  • Single domain, single weak student on a single serving route, no human validation of either rubric; probe accuracy saturates near-ceiling (0.77–1.00), so probes can't separate durable learning from in-context retrieval.

03 Questions for further exploration

  1. Would the helpfulness-vs-pedagogy dissociation and the judge reversal replicate with human expert raters, given the paper declines any human-validity claim?
  2. Why does Gemini's ConvTutor leak so much less (11.3%) than Sonnet (43.0%) or GPT-5.5 (76.6%) — base-model-family effect, reasoning-budget effect, or an artifact of the forced minimum reasoning setting?
  3. Does the leakage→independence coupling stay negative if leakage is experimentally randomized at the turn level, upgrading the association from observational to causal?
  4. Would fine-tuning a reward model against the pedagogy rubric actually improve student learning, or just shift the judge-contingency problem to a new evaluator?
  5. How much of the Socratic prompt-only variant's win on judged pedagogy is genuine pedagogical improvement versus Opus's own stylistic preference for Socratic-sounding language?
