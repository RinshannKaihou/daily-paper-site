> **一句话总结：** 论文证明连续时间动态图（CTDG）中广泛使用的"采样负例 MRR"评测本身依赖于负例分布 q 和候选数 K，并用理论推导和六个模型在四个 JODIE 数据集上的实验证明：换一种候选配置（Expected Uniform-20 vs. 全量目录）会直接翻转模型排名和模块（CRAFT-R 的 pair-recurrence 模块）的增益方向——例如 Wikipedia 上 CRAFT-R 相对 CRAFT 的增益从 20 负例下的 +0.0037 变成全量目录下的 −0.0266——因此建议在可枚举固定目录的基准上改用全量实体排序（all-entity ranking）作为架构比较的主要证据。

> **TL;DR:** This paper shows that sampled-negative MRR for CTDG next-destination prediction is conditional on both the negative sampler q and the candidate count K, and that this isn't just a theoretical nuisance: across six models (CRAFT, CRAFT-R, DyGFormer, DyGFormer+LHA, TGN, GraphMixer) on the JODIE datasets (LastFM, MOOC, Reddit, Wikipedia), Expected Uniform-20 ranks CRAFT above DyGFormer on LastFM (.4283 vs .4161) while the full-catalog MRR ranks DyGFormer above CRAFT (.0811 vs .0636); on Wikipedia the CRAFT-R-minus-CRAFT MRR gain flips from +0.0037 (Expected Uniform-20, CI crossing zero) to −0.0266 (full catalog, CI entirely negative). The paper's remedy is "all-entity ranking" — scoring every destination in the fixed training-prefix catalog with the model's existing scorer — which removes sampler freedom entirely.

## 核心贡献

- **推导采样排序的贝叶斯最优解**：证明非均匀负例分布 q 下的最优打分是对数密度比 log p₊(v|x) − log q(v|x)（式 3），而均匀 q 虽在总体期望下保持全量目录的最优序，但有限 K 只能观测到该完整排序的一个非线性局部视图，导致同一 checkpoint 和 scorer 在不同 q、K 下产生不同的 MRR 值、模型排名和模块效应结论。
- **给出最小"配对历史成员资格"打分器的解析最优系数**：定义 M(u,v,t)=1[(u,v)∈H_t]，推导均匀采样下最优系数 β*=logit(p₁)−logit(q₁)（式 10），并证明在四个数据集上解析解与直接从数据学出的系数几乎完全相同（如 LastFM β*=3.464 与随机负例下学出的 β=3.464 完全一致），而在 source-history 采样（负例支持集排除已见 pair）下该系数理论上趋于 −∞，实证学出值也是强负（LastFM −8.606）。
- **提出正/负例历史成员资格的 2×2 全交叉诊断**（Repeated/New 正例 × Seen/Unseen 负例，表 I），并对 CRAFT-R 做正交投影表示干预（式 11-13）：把"seen 均值−unseen 均值"方向从表示中投影去除后，Repeated–Unseen MRR 从 0.888–0.987 骤降到 0.246–0.489，New–Seen MRR 从 0.048–0.508 升到 0.489–0.940，而随机方向/打乱标签方向的对照组几乎不变，说明该表示轴确实承载了成对历史成员资格信息而非泛泛的候选区分能力。
- **在六个模型、四个数据集、三个随机种子（共 72 组训练/评测）上系统比较 Expected Uniform-20 与全量目录 MRR**（表 V），发现至少一对模型在四个数据集中的三个上发生相对排名反转（LastFM: CRAFT vs DyGFormer 反转；MOOC: 前三名排序变化；Wikipedia: CRAFT 与 CRAFT-R 互换位置），并通过 Kendall's τ（表 VI，LastFM/MOOC 在 K=1 时低至 .733）和 CRAFT-R 相对 CRAFT 的模块增益曲线（表 VII/图 2，Wikipedia 从 K=20 的 +0.0037 到全量的 −0.0266）量化了这种不稳定性。
- **提出"全量实体排序"作为评测协议建议**：对固定训练前缀目录中的每个候选目的地打分，无需选择 q 或 K，同时保留原有打分器不变；并验证其计算成本可控（catalog 达 822–991 时每事件评分仅需 4.94–5.69 ms，GPU 显存峰值约 200MB），以及全量目录与"时间可用目录"（仅含事件前已出现的实体）给出几乎相同的模型排序（MRR 差 0.0000–0.0054）。

## Key Contributions

- **Bayes-optimal derivation for sampled ranking.** Under a candidate set built from one positive and K negatives drawn from q(v|x), the population-optimal ranking score is the log-density ratio ℓ*_q(x,v) = log p₊(v|x) − log q(v|x) (Eq. 3). Uniform q over a fixed catalog preserves the full-catalog Bayes-optimal order in expectation, but a finite K only samples a nonlinear partial view of that order (Eq. 7, an exact closed-form expected reciprocal rank derived via a hypergeometric argument and Vandermonde's identity), so the same checkpoint/scorer pair can yield different MRR values, model rankings, and module-ablation conclusions as q and K vary.
- **Closed-form optimal coefficient for a minimal pair-history scorer**, matched against a directly learned coefficient. Defining pair membership M(u,v,t)=1[(u,v)∈H_t] and a minimal scorer s_β(M)=b+βM, the optimal seen/unseen reward gap is β*=logit(p₁)−logit(q₁) (Eq. 10). On all four datasets this analytic value equals the empirically learned β under random negatives to 3+ decimal places (e.g., LastFM β*=3.464 = learned β=3.464; Wikipedia 3.211=3.211; Reddit 4.290=4.290; MOOC 1.078=1.078), while under source-history sampling (negatives restricted to previously-unseen pairs) the theory predicts β*→−∞ and the learned coefficient is strongly negative (LastFM −8.606, MOOC −8.902), confirming the sampler determines the reward direction, not just its magnitude.
- **A fully-crossed 2×2 pair-membership diagnostic** (Table I: Repeated-Seen / Repeated-Unseen / New-Seen / New-Unseen) combined with an orthogonal-projection representation intervention on CRAFT-R's pre-scorer representation z_uv(t)=[h_u|v(t); e_v; r_uv(t)] (Eqs. 11-13). Removing the seen/unseen mean-difference direction a=μ_seen−μ_unseen collapses Repeated–Unseen MRR from 0.888–0.987 down to 0.246–0.489 and raises New–Seen MRR from 0.048–0.508 up to 0.489–0.940 across all four datasets, while random-direction and shuffled-label controls leave results nearly unchanged — isolating that this specific representation axis, not an arbitrary dimension, carries the seen/unseen signal.
- **Systematic Expected-Uniform-20 vs. full-catalog comparison across 6 models × 4 datasets × 3 seeds (72 runs)** (Table V), showing at least one model-pair rank reversal on 3 of 4 datasets: on LastFM, Expected-20 ranks CRAFT (.4283) above DyGFormer (.4161), but the full catalog ranks DyGFormer (.0811) and GraphMixer (.0922) above CRAFT (.0636); on MOOC the top-3 order changes from CRAFT/CRAFT-R/DyGFormer (Expected-20) to DyGFormer/CRAFT/CRAFT-R (full); on Wikipedia CRAFT and CRAFT-R swap positions (.8948/.8985 Expected-20 vs. .7366/.7101 full). Only Reddit preserves its order (confirmed by Kendall's τ=1.000 across all K in Table VI, vs. τ as low as .733 for LastFM/MOOC at K=1).
- **All-entity ranking recommendation with a measured feasibility case.** Scoring every destination in the fixed training-prefix catalog with the model's existing scorer requires no choice of q or K. The paper measures this is computationally tractable at the scale studied (0.13–0.14 ms/event at ~20 candidates rising near-linearly to 4.94–5.69 ms/event at 822–991 candidates, peak GPU memory saturating near 200 MB — Fig. 3), and shows the fixed-catalog and a stricter "time-available" catalog (only entities seen before each event) give nearly identical model orderings (MRR differing by 0.0000 on LastFM/MOOC, 0.0002–0.0005 on Reddit, 0.0020–0.0054 on Wikipedia).

## Methodology

The paper studies CTDG next-destination prediction, where an interaction stream e_i=(u_i,v_i,t_i) is observed chronologically and a model predicts v_i from (u_i, t_i, history H_t). A common evaluation protocol scores the true destination against K sampled negatives drawn from q(v|u,t,H_t) (Eq. 1) and reports MRR. The authors formalize this as a Bayesian ranking problem: the posterior probability that candidate j is the true positive among a set built from one draw of p₊ and K draws of q is Pr(J=j|C,x) ∝ p₊(v_j|x)/q(v_j|x) (Eq. 2, generalized to sampling-without-replacement in Eq. 4), so the Bayes-optimal score is the log density ratio (Eq. 3) — a result the paper connects explicitly to noise-contrastive estimation and proper scoring rules.

For uniform q over a fixed catalog R (|R|=N), the optimal score reduces to log p₊(v|x) plus a candidate-independent constant (Eq. 5), so the population-optimal full-catalog order is preserved — but finite-K MRR is shown to be a genuinely different, nonlinear statistic of the full-catalog rank r: given catalog size N and full rank r, the expected reciprocal rank under uniform K-negative sampling is E[RR_K|r,N] = [C(N,K+1) − C(N−r,K+1)] / [r·C(N−1,K)] (Eq. 7), derived via a hypergeometric distribution over how many of the K sampled negatives outrank the positive, and confirmed by an exact closed-form re-derivation with tie-handling in the Appendix (Eq. 15-16).

Time-varying pair-history membership M(u,v,t)=1[(u,v)∈H_t] is introduced as the mechanism connecting sampler choice to architecture-specific effects, since models like CRAFT (candidate-to-history cross-attention), CRAFT-R (adds a pair-recurrence count feature), DyGFormer (neighbor co-occurrence encoding), DyGFormer+LHA (a Link History Aggregation adapter from TAMI attached to DyGFormer), TGN, and GraphMixer all differ in whether/how they use this membership signal directly in the scoring path. A minimal logistic scorer s_β(M)=b+βM isolates the optimal reward coefficient for membership alone (Eq. 9-10), compared against the coefficient learned when this feature is fit directly on data under both random and source-history negative sampling.

The empirical study uses the JODIE datasets (LastFM, MOOC, Reddit, Wikipedia), taking the most recent 32,768 events of each, an 85%/15% chronological train/test split, history length 320, 3 training epochs, and 3 seeds (7, 17, 27); BCE with one random uniform negative per positive is the primary training objective, with CRAFT/CRAFT-R additionally retrained under the original BPR objective for a sensitivity check (Table II summarizes settings). Four experiments follow: (1) a fully-crossed 2×2 pair-membership evaluation (repeated/new positive × seen/unseen negative, Table I) using the same checkpoint and 20 negatives per cell; (2) an orthogonal-projection representation intervention P⊥ = I − aa^T/(a^Ta) applied to CRAFT-R's representation to remove the seen/unseen mean-difference direction (Eqs. 11-13), with random-direction and shuffled-label-direction controls; (3) all-entity (all-destination) MRR/Hits@10 over the fixed training-prefix catalog, using average-rank tie handling, computed alongside sampled MRR for K∈{1,5,20,50,100} from the same score vectors (nested uniform sampling shares candidates across K); (4) evaluation-cost profiling of all-entity scoring as catalog size grows.

## Results & Highlights

- **Model rank reversal between candidate protocols (Table V, LastFM)**: Expected Uniform-20 MRR ranks CRAFT (**.4283±.0112**) above DyGFormer (**.4161±.0015**), but full-catalog MRR reverses the order — DyGFormer (**.0811±.0100**) and GraphMixer (**.0922±.0027**) both score above CRAFT (**.0636±.0065**) — a direct architecture-comparison flip caused only by changing the candidate protocol, not the model or checkpoint.
- **Module-effect sign flip on Wikipedia (Table VII/VIII)**: the CRAFT-R-minus-CRAFT MRR gain G_R(K) is **+0.0037 at K=20** (Expected-20; seed-paired 95% CI [-.0036, .0112], containing zero) but **−0.0266 over the full catalog** (seed-paired CI [-.0526, -.0005], entirely negative; time-block bootstrap CI [-.0367, -.0170]) — the same fixed module (pair-recurrence in CRAFT-R) is measured as helping under one candidate protocol and hurting under another, on identical checkpoints.
- **Effect-size range and rank instability across K (Tables VI-VII)**: on Reddit, G_R(K) stays positive but ranges from **+0.0036 at K=1 to +0.1718 at full catalog** (a ~48× change in magnitude); Kendall's τ between the expected finite-K order and the all-entity order is as low as **.733 at K=1 and K=5 on both LastFM and MOOC** (vs. 1.000 for Reddit at all K), meaning roughly a third of model-pair orderings disagree with the full-catalog ranking at small K.
- **Pair-membership 2×2 diagnostic and representation ablation (Fig. 1, Table IV, Section IV-B text)**: CRAFT-R's Repeated–Unseen MRR is **0.888–0.987** across the four datasets while New–Seen MRR is only **0.048–0.508** under identical parameters/history, holding only negative composition fixed; orthogonally projecting out the seen/unseen mean-difference direction moves Repeated–Unseen MRR down to **0.246–0.489** and New–Seen MRR up to **0.489–0.940**, while random-direction and shuffled-label controls leave the baseline nearly unchanged (e.g., Wikipedia full-catalog MRR: base .7101, membership-direction-removed .0405, random-direction .7206, shuffled-label .7121) — isolating the membership-carrying axis from an arbitrary one.
- **Training-objective sensitivity (Table IX)**: under BCE, CRAFT-R's all-entity gain over CRAFT on Wikipedia is **−0.0266**, but the same architecture retrained with the original BPR objective gives **+0.0629** — the sign of the module conclusion flips depending on training loss, on the same dataset and evaluation protocol, meaning neither the loss nor the candidate protocol alone determines the reported module effect.
- **Analytic-vs-learned coefficient agreement (Table III)**: the closed-form optimal pair-membership coefficient β* matches the coefficient learned directly from data to 3 decimal places under random-negative sampling on all four datasets (e.g., LastFM β*=3.464 vs. learned 3.464; Reddit 4.290 vs. 4.290), while under source-history sampling the learned coefficient is strongly negative (LastFM −8.606, Reddit −8.590), consistent with the theoretical −∞ limit for a support-restricted sampler — a direct empirical validation of the paper's Bayes-optimal derivation.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The paper's central contribution is not a new model but a mechanistic bridge between two previously separate observations — that sampled metrics can disagree with full-item rankings (established in recommendation by Krichene & Rendle, Dallmann et al.) and that CTDG architectures increasingly encode pair-history membership directly into their scoring path (CRAFT, CRAFT-R, DyGFormer, TAMI/LHA). Connecting these via a closed-form Bayes-optimal derivation (Eq. 3, 7) and a minimal-scorer coefficient analysis (Eq. 10) that is then empirically validated against learned coefficients (Table III) is a genuinely useful, testable framing rather than a restatement of "sampled metrics can be misleading."

**Rigor**: The 2×2 pair-membership crossing plus orthogonal-projection representation intervention (Section IV-B) is a careful design specifically aimed at separating "does the model detect seen/unseen subgroup membership" from "does the model rank correctly within a subgroup" — a distinction that a single sampled MRR genuinely conflates, and the paper backs the intervention with two controls (random direction, shuffled-label direction) that behave as expected (near-null effect), which is good practice for isolating a causal representation axis rather than an arbitrary one. Uncertainty is reported via both seed-paired t-intervals (3 seeds) and time-block paired bootstrap (Table VIII), and the paper explicitly flags that the Wikipedia Expected-20 CI contains zero while the all-entity CI does not — an honest disclosure that its own sampled-condition finding is statistically weaker than its full-catalog finding.

**Reproducibility**: Code, data-materialization pipeline, and evaluation scripts are stated as publicly released; settings (Table II) are fully specified (32,768 most-recent events, 85/15 split, 3 seeds, 3 epochs). A specific data quirk is disclosed rather than smoothed over: catalog coverage of the primary sampled evaluation is not 100% for three of four datasets (MOOC .996, Reddit .996, Wikipedia .987), meaning the sampled-candidate comparisons are drawn from a slightly restricted set relative to the full training-prefix catalog.

**Significance / red flags**:
- The paper's own results show the effect is dataset-dependent in magnitude, not universal: Reddit's model order is completely stable across every K and the full catalog (Kendall's τ=1.000 throughout, Table VI), while LastFM and MOOC are unstable at small K. The paper is transparent about this heterogeneity rather than presenting a single dramatic number as representative of all settings.
- The BPR-retraining check (Table IX) is done only for CRAFT/CRAFT-R, not the other four models, so the claim that "the sign change under BCE does not generalize as an objective-independent property" is verified for one architecture family only — a natural next ablation (missing from this paper) would be repeating the BCE/BPR contrast for DyGFormer+LHA or TGN.
- All four datasets are homogeneous, relatively small JODIE interaction logs (971–18,366+ events used per the minimal-scorer table, up to 32,768-event windows for the main study); the paper explicitly scopes its evaluation-cost claim to catalogs of ~20–991 entities and states this "does not represent the cost of direct evaluation for million-entity catalogs" — a self-flagged limitation on how far the all-entity recommendation practically scales.
- CRAFT and DyGFormer are the paper's main comparison targets and both are relatively recent, reasonably strong baselines (not strawmen); TGN and GraphMixer, which don't use candidate-conditioned pair-history directly, consistently show smaller module-effect amplitude in the 2×2 diagnostic (Section IV-C), which is consistent with the paper's own mechanistic story rather than cherry-picked to favor it.

## Strengths

- Clean, falsifiable theoretical core: an exact closed-form expected reciprocal rank under uniform finite-K sampling (Eq. 7, re-derived with tie-handling in the Appendix), rather than only an asymptotic or simulation-based argument.
- A genuine mechanistic explanation (not just a re-measurement) for why CTDG-specific architectures are especially sensitive to sampler choice: pair-history membership is time-varying and directly injected into several recent scoring paths (CRAFT, CRAFT-R, DyGFormer+LHA).
- Multiple independent lines of evidence converge on the same conclusion — analytic/learned coefficient agreement (Table III), 2×2 membership crossing (Fig. 1), representation-ablation controls (Table XI), and full Expected-20-vs-full-catalog comparison (Table V) — rather than resting on one metric.
- Honest uncertainty reporting: seed-paired and time-block-bootstrap confidence intervals are given for every headline module-effect number, and the paper explicitly calls out when a sampled-condition CI contains zero while the all-entity CI does not (Wikipedia).
- Practical, load-bearing feasibility evidence for the recommendation itself: measured scoring latency and GPU memory for all-entity ranking, plus a demonstration that a stricter "time-available" catalog gives nearly identical rankings to the fixed catalog.

## Limitations

- Evaluated only on four relatively small, homogeneous JODIE datasets (LastFM, MOOC, Reddit, Wikipedia) with the same six models; no heterogeneous or genuinely large-scale (million-node) CTDG benchmark is tested, and the paper explicitly disclaims that its evaluation-cost measurement (catalogs up to ~991 entities) generalizes to million-entity catalogs.
- The BPR training-objective sensitivity check (Table IX) covers only CRAFT/CRAFT-R, not all six models, leaving open whether the sign-flipping module-effect phenomenon generalizes to DyGFormer+LHA, TGN, or GraphMixer under a different loss.
- Catalog coverage for the primary sampled evaluation is below 100% on three of four datasets (MOOC .996, Reddit .996, Wikipedia .987), a minor but disclosed deviation from a perfectly enumerable candidate space.
- The paper's remedy (all-entity ranking) is explicitly restricted to settings with an enumerable, fixed destination catalog defined by the training prefix; it does not address open-world graphs with rapidly growing node sets, homogeneous graphs where source/destination roles are interchangeable, or event-specific eligibility constraints — settings the introduction itself flags as requiring separate risk-set definitions but does not solve in this paper.
- No new model or training method is proposed; the contribution is entirely diagnostic/methodological, so it does not by itself resolve which CTDG architecture is "best" — it only argues that existing sampled-negative comparisons should be re-run under all-entity ranking before trusting their conclusions.

## Relevance

该论文与连续时间动态图 / 时序链接预测的评测方法论直接相关，其"采样负例 MRR 依赖于采样器"的理论刻画和"配对历史成员资格"机制分析，对任何使用 JODIE 类数据集或候选采样评测协议做模型比较、消融实验的研究都有直接的方法论警示意义，建议在可枚举固定目录场景下改用全量实体排序作为架构比较的首要证据。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the same rank-reversal and module-effect-sign-flip phenomena appear on larger-scale or non-JODIE CTDG benchmarks (e.g., TGB's larger datasets), where the paper's own all-entity evaluation-cost profiling stops at ~991 entities?
- Does the BPR-vs-BCE module-effect sign flip observed for CRAFT-R (Table IX) generalize to DyGFormer+LHA, TGN, and GraphMixer, or is it specific to CRAFT-R's particular pair-recurrence-count feature?
- For open-world graphs with rapidly growing node sets (explicitly flagged in the introduction as needing separate risk-set definitions), what would an "all-entity" analogue look like when the destination catalog itself is not fixed or fully enumerable at test time?
- Given that all-entity scoring cost grows roughly linearly with catalog size in the measured range, what approximate or hierarchical scoring strategies could preserve most of all-entity ranking's evaluation validity at million-node scale without reintroducing sampler-dependence?
- Could the closed-form Bayes-optimal framework (Eq. 3-7) be extended to give a principled, sampler-independent variance-reduction estimator for sampled MRR, rather than requiring full-catalog scoring whenever the catalog is enumerable?
