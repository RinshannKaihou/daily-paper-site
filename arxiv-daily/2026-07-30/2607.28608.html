Clinical AI Fairness Auditing · Synthetic Benchmark Stress-Test

KAISEN's fairness diagnostic, perfect at 144/144 under ideal conditions, silently misses every real disparity when the proxy features it's given are wrong — and nothing in its output says so.

一句话总结：论文构建了一个 16 种疾病 × 15 个健康社会决定因素（SDOH）轴的合成基准，对自家提出的五阶段临床风险模型公平性审计流程（KAISEN）逐阶段做压力测试，发现按组阈值优化在 48/48 留出运行中稳定压低均等赔率差（配对均值 ΔEOD = -0.285），而通常被认为"更优"的按组 Platt 校准对均等赔率的改善效果近乎抛硬币（48 次中仅 19 次改善），且机制诊断模块在代理特征选错时会 0/48 全部误判、却不报任何"诊断失效"的信号。

KAISEN: Reproducible Subgroup Fairness Auditing for Clinical Risk Models ↗
Sparsh Roy, Samuel Girmachew, Nishita Chavan · 30 Jul 2026 · arXiv:2607.28608 · clinical fairness audit equalized odds synthetic SDOH benchmark CUSUM drift monitoring
Key Metric — the diagnostic's collapse
Controlled test144/144
Wrong proxy given0/48

Phase III's mechanism diagnostic classifies every controlled disease-seed case correctly, then recovers zero of 48 real model-driven disparities the moment it's handed the wrong proxy columns — with no flag distinguishing the failure from a clean result.

Why This Matters

Anyone deploying clinical-fairness audits, calibration fixes, or drift monitors should know these tools can pass every check while still hiding real patient disparities.

Critical Edge

No subgroup cell in the entire benchmark reaches the ~8,000-patient threshold this same paper's own power analysis requires — and every number in it describes a synthetic generator, not real patients.

01 Methodology

  • ICohort generation. 16 disease tasks × 15 Healthy People 2030 SDOH axes × 3 prespecified intersections; N=12,000 patients/disease from a structural causal model that injects one of three ground-truth settings per disease — none, model-driven (proxy inflated post-label), or label-noise. Three model classes (logistic regression, HGBT, MLP) never see sensitive attributes directly.
  • IIStratify & measure. Per-group AUC, equalized-odds difference (EOD), ECE/MCE (M=10 bins); EOD confirmed via a 5,000-permutation test, Benjamini-Hochberg corrected across the 15 single axes.
  • IIIMechanism diagnostics. A counterfactual proxy probe (Δ_cf) plus a residual-disagreement probe, decision margin τ=0.03, forced to output exactly one of model-driven / label-noise-driven / no-material-disparity.
  • IVPost-hoc mitigation. IPW-weighted group-wise Platt scaling vs. per-group threshold optimization (Hardt-style, 97-point grid, ≥96%-of-uncorrected-accuracy floor) — compared as paired per-run deltas, not configuration-mean differences, on a 70/15/15 split.
  • VDrift monitoring. One-sided CUSUM over rolling W=1,200-patient windows (8 in-control + 40 monitored), adaptive threshold h=max(0.15, c·σ̂₀) swept at 4σ/6σ/8σ.

02 Results

  • Significance tracks standardized, not raw, effect size: raw-EOD-vs-significance rank correlation is ρ=0.56 (p=0.029), rising to ρ=0.78 (p=0.0005) once each axis is standardized by its own minimum detectable effect — race ranks 5th by raw EOD yet is significant in only 11/16 diseases, versus food security at 16/16.
  • Better calibration ≠ fairer model: group-wise Platt scaling improves ECE in 33/48 runs, the same intervention whose EOD effect is a 19/48 coin flip.
  • Raising the CUSUM alarm threshold from 6σ to 8σ cuts false alarms 5×+ and lifts in-control run length from 10 to 52 windows, but drops detections from 40/48 to 38/48 and raises mean lag from 2.50 to 2.87 windows.
  • Axis-level disparity spans a ~16× range — area deprivation averages the largest EOD (0.542, peaking at 0.682) vs. biological sex, the smallest (0.034); all 3 prespecified intersections exceed every single axis (race×insurance EOD=0.703) yet carry no significance test at all.

Strengths

  • Ground-truth synthetic design checks every audit phase against a known injected mechanism, not just real data where the true disparity mechanism is unobservable.
  • Paired, run-level comparison of mitigations surfaces a non-obvious finding: a better calibrator can have a fairness effect statistically indistinguishable from chance.
  • Quantifies the diagnostic's failure mode as silent, rather than only reporting its accuracy under favorable conditions.
  • MDE-standardized significance (R=EOD/MDE) is a clean, reusable idea for audits with uneven subgroup sample sizes.
  • Full reproducibility infrastructure: committed scripts, fixed seeds, git-commit-tagged run metadata, released benchmark generator and audit code.
  • Grounds metric choice in three concrete deployed clinical contexts (30-day readmission, sepsis, in-hospital mortality) rather than treating all fairness metrics as interchangeable.

Limitations

  • All results are synthetic with author-constructed ground truth; the paper repeatedly disclaims any real-world clinical validity.
  • Inference is prespecified only for the 15 single axes; the 3 largest-gap intersections are untested and reported only descriptively.
  • No subgroup cell meets the ≈8,000-per-group threshold this paper's own power analysis calls for — 12 of 15 tested axes have a standardized effect below their own detectable floor.
  • Phase IV mitigation is entirely post-hoc: IPW does not address unmeasured confounding, and per-group thresholds require group membership at inference time.
  • Optimizing EOD gives no guarantee about calibration — the paper's own results show fairness metrics don't compose.
  • The counterfactual proxy probe is not causal discovery: its 144/144 accuracy is a property of a generator that supplies the correct proxy columns, and doesn't transfer where the true proxy set is unknown.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Does the diagnostic's silent failure under misspecified proxies generalize beyond this benchmark's specific proxy-injection mechanism?
  • How would the threshold-optimization vs. Platt-scaling comparison change under a different accuracy constraint or local-search strategy?
  • Would a prespecified minimum-cell-count protocol for the three intersectional axes change the picture, or just reproduce the single-axis MDE story at low power?
  • How sensitive is the CUSUM monitor's cohort-dependent false-alarm/miss pattern to the baseline window count and rolling window size?
  • Does the calibration-vs-equalized-odds divergence replicate on real EHR-derived risk models (e.g., MIMIC-IV), where label noise and proxy structure aren't under experimenter control?
