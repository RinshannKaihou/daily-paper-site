> **一句话总结：** 腾讯团队发现自动化机器学习研究（autoresearch）循环中，预执行判断的可靠性会随着已积累的成功修改数增多而出现"信心断崖"（selective accuracy 从 82.8% 跌到 56.9%），提出轻量级技能 Rehearse——生成多个候选、执行前用聚焦结果记忆（仅检索同任务内余弦相似度≥0.40 的历史尝试及其成败标签）做成对锦标赛判断——将断崖处的 selective accuracy 恢复到 83.5%，并在 nanochat 预训练、CIFAR-10 图像分类、ETTh1 时间序列预测三个真实循环、共 4000 次预算训练运行中，在同等训练预算下把终点效果分别提到 10.7%（vanilla 为 6.9%）、2.85%（vanilla 为 2.10%）和 54.0% MSE 降幅（vanilla 为 40.1%）。

> **TL;DR:** The paper documents a "confidence cliff" in autoresearch loops (Karpathy-style propose→train→keep-if-better loops): on a 366-pair outcome-labeled benchmark built from 39 paper-derived AutoSOTA tasks, an LLM judge's selective accuracy at picking the better of two candidate modifications falls from 82.8% early to 56.9% once three or more successful changes have accumulated, even though the judge keeps committing to verdicts. Rehearse fixes this with a Propose-Predict-Execute skill — propose several candidates, rank them with a focused, same-task, similarity-gated outcome memory, execute only the top pick — raising late-stage selective accuracy to 83.5% (beating full-history dumps at 70.8% and LLM summaries at 80.9%). Across 4,000 budgeted training runs on nanochat pretraining, CIFAR-10 (ResNet), and ETTh1 forecasting (PatchTST), Rehearse reaches a better endpoint under the same training-run budget than vanilla (e.g., nanochat: 10.7% vs. 6.9% mean improvement; ETTh1 MSE reduction: 54.0% vs. 40.1%) and needs 46–37% fewer experiments to match vanilla's final level.

## 核心贡献

- 用公开的 AutoSOTA 日志证明"晚期收益递减"是真实存在的：有效修改的比例从迭代 1–2 的 70% 降到迭代 6+ 的 43%，即使是成功的修改，平均增益也从 3.6% 骤降到 0.3%（Figure 4, Appendix H）。
- 构建了一个结果已知、跨 39 个论文衍生 AutoSOTA 任务的 366 对基准（296 个同基线 type-1 对 + 70 个 type-2 辅助对），首次把"预执行判断的可靠性"作为可随搜索深度测量的量，而不是单点评估。
- 发现并命名"信心断崖"（confidence cliff）现象：在全 366 对基准上，随着已积累的成功修改数 n_baseline 增加，无记忆判官的 selective accuracy 从 82.8% 跌至 56.9%，而其愿意给出裁决的覆盖率反而从 76% 升到 85%——判官越来越自信却越来越不可靠。
- 提出 Rehearse：一个不改动目标训练代码、不需要微调的技能，把"一次想法就执行"的循环改造为 Propose–Predict–Execute：生成多个候选、执行前用聚焦结果记忆（仅取同任务、余弦相似度≥0.40 的历史尝试及二元成败标签，不含失败原因或摘要）做成对锦标赛比较，只执行排名最高的候选，并把新结果写回记忆库。
- 在单轮基准与三个真实自动研究循环（nanochat、CIFAR-10、ETTh1）上系统验证：聚焦记忆把断崖处的 selective accuracy 从 56.9% 提升到 83.5%，优于 AIDE 式全历史转储（70.8%）、Reflexion 式自反思缓冲（74.1%）和 LLM 摘要（80.9%）；在实盘循环里，同等 4,000 次训练预算下终点效果全面优于 vanilla 循环。

## Key Contributions

- Establishes with public AutoSOTA logs that late-stage waste in autoresearch is real and measurable: the share of helpful modifications falls from 70% (iterations 1–2) to 43% (iteration 6+), and even successful modifications shrink from a 3.6% to a 0.3% mean gain (Figure 4, Appendix H).
- Builds an outcome-labeled, depth-indexed benchmark of 366 pairs (296 same-baseline type-1 pairs, 70 auxiliary type-2 pairs) drawn from 39 paper-derived AutoSOTA optimization tasks, letting pre-execution judgment be measured as a function of how much history the loop has already accumulated, rather than at a single search state.
- Identifies and names the "confidence cliff": on the full 366-pair benchmark, a memoryless judge's selective accuracy (accuracy conditioned on a strict-consensus verdict) collapses from 82.8% to 56.9% once three or more successful changes have accumulated, while its willingness to commit to a verdict (coverage) rises from 76% to 85% over the same range.
- Implements Rehearse as a lightweight, no-fine-tuning, no-target-code-change skill that wraps any autoresearch loop with an explicit Predict step (pairwise strict-consensus tournament with rationale) and a focused outcome store (same-task, cosine-similarity-gated retrieval of prior change/outcome pairs), delivered as a released skill plus benchmark.
- Validates Rehearse across both a controlled single-turn benchmark (cliff selective accuracy 83.5%, best among tested memory forms) and three live measurable-objective loops (nanochat pretraining, CIFAR-10 classification, ETTh1 forecasting) totaling 4,000 budgeted training runs, showing consistent endpoint improvements under matched training-run budgets.

## Methodology

Rehearse reframes a standard "one-idea-then-run" autoresearch loop as **Propose–Predict–Execute** (Algorithm 1, Appendix A). At each step: (1) **Propose** — the agent emits N candidate modifications (N=1 for vanilla, N=5 for every multi-proposal configuration), each a JSON object with a description, a *hypothesis* (the mechanistic reason the change should help), an implementation field (the exact code/config change), a coarse type, priority, and risk; (2) **Predict** — a judge LLM ranks the candidates via a pairwise strict-consensus tournament: every unordered pair is judged in both presentation orders, an agreed verdict gives one vote to the winner, and a disagreement gives no vote to either candidate (mitigating known position bias in LLM judges); the top-k (k=1 in all live experiments) candidate is selected for execution; (3) **Execute** — the selected candidate is applied to a copy of the current state and a full training job is run under a fixed compute budget; if the metric improves, the patch is committed and the state advances, otherwise it is discarded, and either way the attempt (change description + binary worked/did-not-work outcome) is written to a **focused outcome store**.

The store's key design choice is *selective* retrieval rather than a full history dump: when a new candidate is judged, retrieval is restricted to attempts from the *same task* executed *before* the current step, using 384-dimensional, L2-normalized `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` sentence embeddings of the change description with a cosine-similarity threshold of 0.40 (tuned only via a retrieval-neighbor sweep over {0.30,...,0.60} before seeing benchmark accuracy, per Appendix D). Each retrieved precedent is serialized as one line — the change plus its binary outcome — deliberately omitting failure reasons, summaries, or the full applied-change list, since the paper's own ablations (Section 4.4, Appendix E) show that adding such explanatory content *lowers* cliff selective accuracy (e.g., recorded failure reasons drop it from 83.5% to 80.6%).

To isolate the Predict step from execution and proposal quality, Section 4's single-turn benchmark strips the loop to one binary decision: given two candidate modifications from the same accepted baseline (one that strictly improved the metric, one that did not, i.e., type-1 pairs; plus 70 type-2 pairs where both worked and the larger gain must be identified), which is better to run? The judge sees only outcome-blind descriptions/hypotheses/implementations — never the measured result — and every reported single-turn number is averaged over three different judge models (`hy3-preview`, `glm-5.1`, `deepseek-v4`) so results don't rest on one model's idiosyncrasies. Live loops (Section 5) instead run the full path-dependent search on Karpathy's nanochat pretraining loop (scored by validation bits-per-byte), CIFAR-10 ResNet classification, and ETTh1 PatchTST time-series forecasting, each under a fixed per-experiment compute budget (30 minutes for nanochat) for 100 experiments per configuration, comparing vanilla / propose-many / select / Rehearse (nanochat) or vanilla / Rehearse (CIFAR-10, ETTh1) over five seeds each.

## Results & Highlights

- **Confidence cliff, full 366-pair benchmark (Figure 1, Table 1):** without memory, selective accuracy holds at 82.8% when zero prior successes exist but falls to **56.9%** once three or more successful changes have accumulated (n_baseline ≥ 3), a 25.9-point collapse, even as coverage under strict consensus *rises* from 76% to 85%.
- **Rehearse vs. memory-form baselines at the cliff bucket (Table 1, n_baseline ≥ 3):** Rehearse's focused retrieval reaches **83.5%** selective accuracy at 77% coverage, versus a memoryless judge's 56.9%, an AIDE-inspired full-history dump's 70.8%, a Reflexion-inspired self-reflection buffer's 74.1%, and an LLM summary of history's 80.9% — Rehearse is the single best configuration despite committing on fewer deep calls than the dump/reflection baselines.
- **Type-1 (worked-vs-not) selective accuracy, whole benchmark (Section 4.3/4.5):** a judge with candidate rationales but no history reaches **79.5%** selective accuracy; adding Rehearse's focused store raises this to **84.2%**. On the harder type-2 pairs (both candidates worked, must pick the larger gain), accuracy rises from 67.4% to 74.1%.
- **nanochat pretraining, live loop (Section 5.2, Figure 3a):** measured as mean relative improvement over each run's own baseline (val_bpb, lower is better) at the 100-experiment endpoint — vanilla reaches **6.9%**, propose-many (bigger pool, no selection) **7.1%**, select (pool + Predict, no memory) **8.4%**, and Rehearse (pool + Predict + focused store) **10.7%**, ahead on every one of five seeds versus select.
- **CIFAR-10 image classification (Section 5.3, Table 4):** Rehearse improves accuracy by **2.85%** over baseline (mean of 5 seeds, Table 4 reports 2.9%) versus vanilla's **2.10%** (2.1%), reaching vanilla's final level after 54 of 100 experiments (46% fewer).
- **ETTh1 time-series forecasting (Section 5.3, Table 4):** Rehearse cuts MSE by **54.0%** against vanilla's **40.1%**, reaching vanilla's endpoint after 63 of 100 experiments (37% fewer); Rehearse ends ahead on 9 of 10 additional-loop seed runs and shows tighter seed-to-seed spread (±3.0 vs. ±11.2 for vanilla, driven by one high-variance vanilla seed at 59.9%).
- **Heuristic no-LLM baseline (Appendix F):** a rule-based selector that picks the candidate embedding-farthest from prior did-not-work attempts scores only 52.4% overall (n=145) and **47.8%** on the deep bucket (n=23) — below or near chance, and well under both Rehearse's 83.5% and even the memoryless judge's 56.9%, indicating the gain is not simply "avoid anything similar to a past failure."

## Authors & Credibility

The paper is authored by two researchers at Tencent (Ji Jiazhen, Ding Shouhong), a small industry-lab team rather than a large multi-institution consortium. Model calls throughout the study are issued through Tencent's own CodeBuddy framework, and the single-turn judge and live-loop models are serving endpoints/aliases (`hy3-preview` from Tencent's Hy Team, `glm-5.1` from Z.ai, `deepseek-v4` from DeepSeek-AI) rather than frozen public checkpoints — a detail the authors flag themselves (Appendix B) as a reproducibility caveat rather than hiding it. This reads as a technical-report-style arXiv preprint (no venue/peer-review indication), but it is unusually thorough for a two-author paper: nine appendices (A–I) cover the full algorithm, prompts, embedding-threshold sensitivity, component-level ablations, a deliberately weak heuristic control, per-seed tables, and a coverage-aware tournament calibration extending the pairwise benchmark to budgeted top-k selection.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The "confidence cliff" framing — selective accuracy degrading while coverage (willingness to commit) stays high or rises — is a genuinely useful operational diagnosis that is more precise than generic claims of "diminishing returns," and it is directly measured against real accepted/reverted outcomes rather than a synthetic proxy. The proposed fix (focused, same-task, similarity-gated, outcome-labeled retrieval, explicitly stripped of explanations or summaries) is architecturally simple relative to prior memory-augmented-agent work (Reflexion, MemGPT, Voyager), but the paper earns this simplicity with evidence: Table 2's leave-one-out ablations show that adding structure back (failure reasons, full history, summaries) *hurts* cliff selective accuracy, which is a non-obvious and paper-specific finding rather than a generic "more memory is better" claim.

**Rigor:** The controlled design is a strength — same-baseline pair construction (type-1), judge-order strict consensus (cuts run-to-run spread from 5.1 to 1.7 points), and averaging over three independent judge models all reduce single-model or single-seed idiosyncrasy risk. However, several caveats are worth naming explicitly. First, the depth variable n_baseline is observational, not randomized (acknowledged in Section 6): later decisions in a real trajectory may simply be harder (less headroom, narrower modification families), so part of the "cliff" could reflect genuine task difficulty rather than judgment decay — the authors call this out themselves as "a controlled proxy at run depth, not a randomized causal estimate." Second, the live-loop comparisons rest on only five seeds per configuration; Table 4 shows one clearly anomalous high-variance vanilla ETTh1 seed (59.9% MSE reduction vs. the other four at 31–45%), which the paper reports transparently but which also means the vanilla ETTh1 baseline's mean (40.1%) is sensitive to a single outlier run. Third, the benchmark's hypothesis field for each candidate is generated post hoc by a separate LLM that is blinded to the measured outcome but conditioned on the same task/implementation context — this is a reasonable design to keep the judge outcome-blind, but it also means the benchmark measures judgment over LLM-reconstructed rationales rather than the original agent's live reasoning trace.

**Reproducibility:** The authors commit to releasing Rehearse as a skill together with the benchmark, and pin the AutoSOTA log source to a specific commit (`419fe013`), which is good practice. The main reproducibility weak point is the reliance on named serving endpoints (`hy3-preview`, `glm-5.1`, `deepseek-v4`) that the paper itself states are "serving names or aliases rather than frozen checkpoints" (Table 3, Appendix B) — future attempts to reproduce exact numbers could see drift if these endpoints are updated server-side.

**Significance and red flags:** The efficiency argument (fewer wasted training runs late in a loop) targets a real, previously under-instrumented cost in autoresearch systems, and the three-loop live validation (pretraining, image classification, forecasting) is a genuine breadth check rather than a single cherry-picked task. That said, all live loops use small candidate pools (N=5, k=1) and comparatively short/cheap training budgets (30-minute experiments), so the result does not directly establish that focused-memory selection scales to larger candidate pools, longer training jobs, or the kind of large, messy repository-scale codebases where AutoSOTA-style systems are ultimately meant to operate — a limitation the paper explicitly flags ("other memory forms and candidate counts are untested live").

## Strengths

- Turns a vague "late-stage returns diminish" intuition into a precisely measured, named phenomenon (the confidence cliff) backed by both public log evidence (Figure 4) and a purpose-built 366-pair outcome benchmark.
- Ablates *why* focused memory works (Table 2, Appendix E) rather than just reporting an end number — isolating that outcome labels and candidate-relevant idea text both matter, while explanatory content (failure reasons, full dumps) actively hurts.
- Validated on three qualitatively different live loops (language-model pretraining, image classification, time-series forecasting) under matched training-run budgets, not just the single-turn diagnostic benchmark.
- Includes a deliberately weak, non-LLM heuristic control (Appendix F) that shows the gain isn't reducible to a simple "avoid neighbors of past failures" rule — a genuine falsification attempt rather than only comparing against strawmen favoring the proposed method.
- Delivered as a lightweight skill requiring no fine-tuning and no change to the target training code, which is a meaningful practical-adoption advantage over approaches that require retraining the agent itself.

## Limitations

- The depth variable (n_baseline) used to define the "cliff" is observational rather than randomized, so later-stage judgment failures are confounded with genuinely harder remaining modifications — a limitation the authors state directly in Section 6.
- Live-loop evidence rests on five seeds per configuration, with at least one visibly anomalous seed (vanilla ETTh1 at 59.9% vs. 31–45% for its other seeds) that the per-seed variance the paper reports (Table 4) itself flags as a "seed-level limitation."
- The single-turn benchmark's candidate rationale is generated post hoc by an LLM blinded to outcomes but not to task context, so it approximates rather than reproduces the live agent's actual pre-execution reasoning trace.
- Live experiments use small candidate pools (N=5) and k=1 selection only; larger pools, different k, and other memory forms are explicitly left untested in real loops.
- Reliance on named model serving endpoints described by the authors themselves as "aliases rather than frozen checkpoints" introduces a reproducibility risk if those endpoints change over time.
- Scope is restricted to measurable-objective, code-modification autoresearch loops; broader paper-writing or novelty-scored autoresearch systems (AI Scientist, EvoScientist, etc.) are explicitly out of scope.

## Relevance

该工作对研究自动化机器学习实验循环（autoresearch）中的算力浪费与预执行判断可靠性问题提供了一个可直接复用的诊断基准和轻量级技能，对设计更高效的 LLM 智能体记忆与候选筛选机制、以及评估 LLM-judge 在长程自我提升循环中的信心校准问题，具有较强的方法论参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the confidence-cliff pattern and Rehearse's fix persist with larger candidate pools (N > 5) or top-k > 1 execution, which the paper explicitly leaves untested in live loops?
- How much of the observed "cliff" is genuine judgment decay versus a confound with decreasing task headroom as n_baseline grows — could a causal or randomized-depth variant of the benchmark separate these two factors?
- Does the focused-retrieval approach remain effective with a stronger or task-specialized embedding model, given the paper only tests the small 384-dimensional all-MiniLM-L6-v2 encoder and flags "stronger retrievers remain an avenue for future work"?
- How sensitive are the live-loop endpoint gains to the specific 30-minute-per-experiment compute budget — would Rehearse's advantage shrink or grow under much longer or much shorter per-experiment training budgets?
- Could the same Propose-Predict-Execute skill and focused outcome memory transfer to larger, messier repository-scale autoresearch tasks (e.g., full AutoSOTA-style paper reproduction) rather than the three relatively contained loops (nanochat, CIFAR-10, ETTh1) tested here?
