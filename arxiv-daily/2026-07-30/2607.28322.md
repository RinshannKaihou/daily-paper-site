> **一句话总结：** 本文提出"非划分"（non-partitioned）序贯变点检测框架——不再要求变点前后分布分属两个不相交的已知族，而是对每个候选"无变点"分布构造点零假设 e-process 并在起始时间上做 Shiryaev-Roberts 式累加、再对候选分布取下确界，在次高斯均值漂移仿真（N(0,1)→N(δ,1)，A=1000，4000 次重复）中，δ=1.0 时新方法的经验 CADD 为 17.297，显著优于 Shekhar & Ramdas (2023a) 的 repeated-CS 基线 28.489（信息论下界 log(A)/I=13.82），并在有界均值的连续通用组合投资实验中验证了检测延迟随阈值 A 增大而收敛到一阶最优比率（A=10^20 时中位数/理论下界比值降至 1.06 左右）。

> **TL;DR:** This paper studies sequential change detection when the pre- and post-change distributions are both unknown members of the *same* nonparametric class P (no pre-specified partition into disjoint pre/post families, unlike classical CUSUM/Shiryaev–Roberts/GLR theory or the recent e-detector framework of Shin et al. 2023). The method aggregates point-null e-processes over all candidate no-change laws R ∈ P and takes an infimum over R, yielding finite-sample ARL- and PFA-valid detectors. On a unit-variance Gaussian mean-shift benchmark (N(0,1)→N(δ,1), A=1000, 4000 replications), the proposed e-detector achieves CADD 17.297 vs. 28.489 for the repeated-confidence-sequence baseline (Shekhar and Ramdas, 2023a) at δ=1.0 (about 39% shorter delay), close to the information-theoretic benchmark log(A)/I=13.82, with the gap narrowing further at larger δ.

## 核心贡献

- **提出"非划分"序贯变点检测问题**：变点前后分布 P、Q 均属于同一（可能非参数的）类 P，彼此不要求不相交，因此经典的 CUSUM/Shiryaev-Roberts/GLR 以及 e-detector（Shin et al., 2023）框架均不直接适用（它们都假设 P₀∩P₁=∅ 的先验划分）。
- **提出通用构造**：对每个候选无变点分布 R∈P 构造"s-延迟点零 e-process" M^R_{s:t}，在起始时间 s 上求和（ARL 版本，公式 1）或加权求和（PFA 版本，公式 3），再对 R 取下确界，得到检测统计量；证明其分别满足 ARL≥A 和 PFA≤α 的有限样本有效性（定理 2.2、2.3）。
- **三个具体可计算实例**：(i) 次高斯均值变化（第3节，公式6的显式 e-process）；(ii) [0,1] 有界均值变化，用 Cover (1991) 的连续通用组合投资（arcsine/Jeffreys 混合）构造下注式 e-process（第4节）；(iii) 方差未知的高斯均值变化，用"通用推断"t 检验 e-process（学生化检测器，第5节），并进一步给出同时学习均值和方差的"全高斯"检测器，恢复实例最优的 KL 速率 D_KL(Q‖P)（而非仅均值检验能达到的较弱的"t-信息"速率）。附录A额外给出两状态马尔可夫链转移矩阵变化的例子。
- **一般性延迟上界与匹配下界**：引入"adjuster"（Choe and Ramdas, 2026）对运行最大值做调整以保留变点前证据；提出比全局弱紧致性更弱的"可数局部 REGROW witness basis"条件（定义6.8），在此条件下证明检测延迟一阶渐近最优，即 CADD ≤ (1+o(1))·log(A)/I*，其中 I*=D_KL(Q‖P) 是实例特定的 KL 数（定理6.13及推论6.14/6.15）；并证明匹配的下界（定理7.1、7.2），以及"早期变点不可检测性"结果（定理7.3）：若变点 T_α = o(log(1/α))，则任意全局 PFA-α 有效的检测器的检测概率趋于 0——这是该非划分问题特有的信息论极限。

## Key Contributions

- **Formulates the "non-partitioned" sequential change detection (SCD) problem**: both the pre-change law P and post-change law Q are unknown members of the *same* class P (P, Q ∈ P, P ≠ Q), with no assumed disjoint partition P = P₀ ⊔ P₁. This differs structurally from classical CUSUM/Shiryaev–Roberts/GLR theory and from the e-detector framework of Shin et al. (2023), all of which require a pre-specified separation between pre- and post-change families.
- **General reduction to point-null e-processes**: for every candidate no-change law R ∈ P, build an "s-delay e-process" M^R_{s:t} testing H₀,R: X ~ R^∞ i.i.d. against its complement; sum (ARL detector, Eq. 1) or weight-and-sum (PFA detector, Eq. 3) these over all start times s, then take an infimum over R ∈ P. Theorems 2.2 and 2.3 prove finite-sample ARL control (E_R[τ] ≥ A, P_R(τ≤m) ≤ m/A) and global PFA control (P_R(τ<∞) ≤ α) respectively, for arbitrary P.
- **Three fully worked, computable examples**: (1) sub-Gaussian mean-change (explicit closed-form e-process, Eq. 6, Theorem 3.2 gives a first-order CADD bound log(A)/I with I = (μ−ν)²/(2σ²)); (2) bounded [0,1]-mean change using Cover's (1991) continuous universal-portfolio (arcsine/Jeffreys mixture) betting e-process, evaluated exactly via Gauss–Chebyshev quadrature (Section 4); (3) Gaussian mean change with unknown variance via a "universal-inference" t-test e-process (studentized detector) that attains only the weaker t-information rate I_t(P,Q) = ½log(1+(b−a)²/v), plus a full-Gaussian variant that learns both mean and variance jointly and recovers the sharper instance-specific rate I_G(P,Q) = D_KL(Q‖P) (Section 5, with I_t ≤ I_G proven explicitly). Appendix A adds a two-state Markov transition-matrix change example with an entropy-rate detector.
- **General delay upper bounds via "adjusters" and local REGROW witnesses**: introduces adjuster functions (following Choe and Ramdas, 2026) applied to the running maximum of restarted e-processes to preserve pre-change evidence across the running max (Section 6.1), and a new, strictly weaker-than-weak-compactness sufficient condition — a "countable local REGROW witness basis" (Definition 6.8) — under which Theorem 6.13 and Corollaries 6.14/6.15 establish that the generic ARL/PFA detector attains first-order asymptotically optimal delay CADD ≤ (1+o(1))·log(A)/I* with I* = D_KL(Q‖P), whenever the changepoint is "late enough" (T_α/log(A) → ∞).
- **Matching minimax lower bounds and an early-change impossibility result**: Theorems 7.1–7.2 give pointwise and horizon-uniform PFA lower bounds matching the upper bounds up to (1+ε); Theorem 7.3 proves that for any globally PFA-α-valid detector, if the changepoint T_α = o(log(1/α)), the detection probability P(τ_α < ∞) → 0 — i.e., reliable detection is information-theoretically impossible when the change occurs too early relative to the false-alarm budget, a phenomenon specific to the fully overlapping (non-partitioned) setting.

## Methodology

The paper reduces non-partitioned SCD to a family of point-null e-processes. For each candidate no-change law R ∈ P and start time s, an "s-delay e-process" {M^R_{s:t}} is any nonnegative, adapted process with M^R_{s:j}=1 for j<s that satisfies E_R^∞[M^R_{s:τ} | F_{s-1}] ≤ 1 for every stopping time τ (Definition 2.1). Two aggregation rules are then defined:

- **ARL detector**: D_t^ARL = inf_{R∈P} Σ_{s=1}^t M^R_{s:t}; stop at τ_A^ARL = inf{t: D_t^ARL ≥ A}. Since D_t^ARL is itself an e-detector in the sense of Shin et al. (2023) (E_R[E_τ] ≤ E_R[τ] for every stopping time), thresholding at A gives ARL(τ) ≥ A and a Ville-type local bound P_R(τ_A ≤ m) ≤ m/A (Theorem 2.2).
- **PFA detector**: weights π_s > 0 with Σπ_s ≤ 1 (canonical choice π_s ∝ 1/{s(log(es))²}) are used to form D_t^PFA = inf_R Σ π_s M^R_{s:t}, thresholded at 1/α, giving global false-alarm control P_R(τ_α < ∞) ≤ α for every R ∈ P (Theorem 2.3).

The point-null primitives M^R are built per example using standard anytime-valid tools: a sub-Gaussian test-supermartingale mixture (Eq. 6) for the sub-Gaussian mean case; a betting/universal-portfolio construction (constant-rebalanced portfolios mixed against the Jeffreys/arcsine prior, evaluated via Gauss–Chebyshev quadrature) for bounded means; and a "universal inference" split-likelihood-ratio t-process (predictable plug-in mean/variance estimators) for the Gaussian unknown-variance case, plus a fully Bayesian/mixture Gaussian e-process (natural-parameter reparametrization, convex in η) that learns both mean and variance for the instance-optimal rate.

Because the raw restarted e-processes need not be monotone, the paper applies an "adjuster" a(x) (an increasing function with ∫a(x)/x² dx ≤ 1, e.g. a(x)=2x/(2+log x)²) to the running maximum of each M^R (Section 6.1), which preserves e-validity while making the process nondecreasing in t — this is what lets pre-change evidence "survive" past the changepoint for the delay proofs. To handle general (possibly non-weakly-compact) classes P, the paper introduces "simultaneous REGROW regularity" and a "countable local REGROW witness basis" (a countable cover of P by relatively weakly open sets with weakly compact closures whose exteriors are uniformly separated in KL) as the key regularity condition for uniform growth rates (Section 6.2), building on the REGROW existence theorem of Ram and Ramdas (2026b). All of Sections 2–7 are proved for i.i.d. data in the main text; Section A in the appendix extends validity arguments (which only require conditionally valid delayed e-processes, not independence) to a two-state Markov chain with unknown transition probabilities.

## Results & Highlights

- **Sub-Gaussian mean-shift vs. repeated-CS baseline (Table 1, N(0,1)→N(δ,1), A=1000, ρ²=1, 4000 replications, comparing against the repeated-confidence-sequence detector of Shekhar and Ramdas, 2023a)**: at δ=1.00, the proposed e-detector's empirical CADD is 17.297 vs. 28.489 for repeated-CS (information benchmark log(A)/I = 13.82) — roughly 39% shorter delay than the baseline. At δ=2.00, CADD is 5.177 vs. 6.309 for repeated-CS (benchmark 3.45). Across all four δ values tested (1.00, 1.25, 1.50, 2.00), the proposed detector is consistently and substantially faster than repeated-CS and closer to the information-theoretic benchmark log(A)/I.
- **Bounded-mean universal-portfolio experiment (Table 2, Panel A, a=0.3 pre-change vs. b=0.5 post-change mean, comparing exact point-null universal-portfolio median stopping times against three different no-change candidates — Bernoulli(0.5), Beta(10,10), and a two-point law on {0.45,0.55} — with respective I_bet values 0.087177, 0.485201, 0.505800)**: for A=10⁵, the Bernoulli-null detector's median stopping time is 153, i.e. a ratio of 1.159 to the theoretical benchmark log(A)/I_bet; for the Beta(10,10) null the median is 28 (ratio 1.180); for the two-point null, 27 (ratio 1.186). As A grows to 10¹² and 10²⁰, these ratios shrink to 1.101/1.089/1.098 and 1.066/1.064/1.065 respectively, empirically confirming the theoretical asymptotic-optimality claim (ratio → 1) as the threshold grows.
- **No quantitative empirical results are reported for the unknown-variance Gaussian detector (Section 5) or the Markov-chain detector (Appendix A)** — these two constructions remain validated only theoretically (via the stated propositions/theorems), not via simulation, in this version of the paper.
- **Theoretical delay guarantees**: Theorem 6.13 (and its ARL/PFA corollaries 6.14/6.15) shows CADD ≤ (1+ε)log(A)/I* + O(1) with I* = D_KL(Q‖P) whenever the changepoint grows fast enough relative to log(A) (late-change regime), and Theorem 7.2 gives a matching horizon-uniform lower bound of the same order, establishing pointwise near-optimality; Theorem 7.3 shows the complementary early-change impossibility result (detection probability → 0 for changepoints T_α = o(log(1/α))).

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The non-partitioned SCD formulation (pre- and post-change laws drawn from the same, non-disjoint class) is a genuinely new and well-motivated problem statement — the paper correctly notes that classical Lai (1998)-style information lower bounds and the existing e-detector framework (Shin et al., 2023) both implicitly assume a partitioned P₀/P₁, and that under a fully overlapping P the classical worst-case CADD degenerates (an immediate change to Q is indistinguishable from never having changed). The point-null-aggregation-plus-infimum construction is simple and elegant, and the identification of an "early-change impossibility" phenomenon (Theorem 7.3) unique to this setting is a genuine and interesting theoretical contribution, not present in classical partitioned theory.

**Rigor**: The paper is dense with formal claims (94 pages total, with roughly 70 pages of appendix devoted to proofs), and the results in the main text (Theorems 2.2/2.3, 3.2/3.3, 4.2, 5.2/5.3, 6.11/6.13, 7.1–7.3) are stated with explicit regularity conditions (e.g., T_α/L → ∞, countable local REGROW witness basis) rather than swept under generic asymptotic notation, which is a good sign of care. That said, essentially all delay optimality results are asymptotic "(1+o_P(1))" statements requiring the changepoint to diverge relative to log(A) — the paper is honest about this (Section 1.1 explicitly notes CADD is infinite under PFA control for any fixed T, hence only high-probability delay statements are meaningful), but a reader wanting finite-sample delay guarantees at fixed, small T will not find them here beyond the simulation studies.

**Reproducibility**: Code is released on GitHub (cited in footnote 1), and the two reported experiments (Tables 1 and 2) are specified with concrete, reproducible parameters (A, δ, number of replications, quadrature order). This is good practice for a theory-heavy paper.

**Significance / paper-specific weaknesses**: The empirical validation is thin relative to the theoretical scope — only two synthetic experiments are run (sub-Gaussian mean-shift and bounded-mean universal portfolios), both univariate Gaussian/Bernoulli/Beta toy settings with no real-world data application, and the two more sophisticated constructed detectors (Gaussian with unknown variance, and the two-state Markov chain in the appendix) are given no empirical validation at all in this version — a missing ablation given that the paper explicitly claims those constructions attain the sharper instance-optimal D_KL(Q‖P) rate versus the weaker t-information rate, a claim that would benefit from a direct simulated comparison of the two Gaussian detectors' empirical delays. The one baseline comparison used (Shekhar and Ramdas, 2023a's repeated confidence-sequence detector) is itself a paper from the same lab/research lineage rather than an independent competing method (e.g., classical CUSUM/Shiryaev-Roberts variants, GLR, or the cpm package of Ross 2015, all cited in related work but never empirically compared against). Given that classical partitioned methods are not literally applicable to the non-partitioned problem, some baseline comparison gap is understandable, but a naive/adapted partitioned baseline (e.g., a GLR detector run with an artificially assumed partition) would have strengthened the empirical case that the non-partitioned formulation matters in practice, not just in theory.

## Strengths

- Clean, general reduction of a previously underexplored problem (non-partitioned SCD) to point-null e-process aggregation, applicable to arbitrary distribution classes P without requiring weak compactness (via the new local-REGROW-witness condition).
- Finite-sample ARL and PFA validity guarantees (not just asymptotic), holding for arbitrary P, proved directly from e-process supermartingale properties.
- Matching upper and lower bounds on detection delay, including a novel and conceptually important "early-change impossibility" result specific to the non-partitioned setting.
- Concrete, fully worked examples across four different distributional settings (sub-Gaussian, bounded, Gaussian with unknown variance, Markov chains), each with closed-form or efficiently computable statistics (the Gaussian studentized detector is shown to be computable in O(t) per new observation, with quadratic total cost through a horizon).
- Extension beyond i.i.d. data is explicitly demonstrated (Markov chain example), showing the framework's validity arguments only need conditional e-process validity, not independence.
- Public code release for reproducibility.

## Limitations

- Empirical evaluation is limited to two synthetic experiments (univariate Gaussian mean-shift and bounded Bernoulli/Beta means); no real-data application, no multivariate examples, and no empirical validation of the unknown-variance Gaussian or Markov-chain detectors.
- All delay-optimality results are first-order asymptotic ((1+o(1)) multiplicative bounds) and require the changepoint to be "late" relative to log(A)/log(1/α) — under early changes, the paper's own Theorem 7.3 shows reliable detection is information-theoretically impossible, which is a fundamental (not merely technical) limitation of the non-partitioned framing itself.
- The general upper-bound machinery (Section 6) requires the model class P to admit a "countable local REGROW witness basis," a new condition introduced in this paper; while shown to hold for weakly compact classes and for the (non-compact) Gaussian location family, its applicability to other classes of practical interest is not systematically characterized.
- The sub-Gaussian construction's delay bound is stated in terms of the infimum KL number I = inf_{P₀∈P_μ, P₁∈P_ν} D_KL(P₁‖P₀), which the authors note can be substantially smaller than the true instance-specific rate I* = D_KL(Q‖P) — the paper acknowledges this gap explicitly and defers the sharper instance-adaptive result to the general theory of Section 6, meaning the simplest worked example (Section 3) does not by itself attain the paper's strongest theoretical rate.
- Heavy reliance on very recent (2026), largely same-group results (REGROW existence theorems, adjuster theory) as foundational building blocks, which have not yet had time to be independently vetted by the broader community.

## Relevance

对从事序贯假设检验、异常/变点检测、anytime-valid 推断或安全监控（如在线 A/B 测试提前停止、生产系统漂移监测）的研究者和工程师而言，本文提供了一个不需要预先假设变化方向或分布族划分即可使用的通用检测框架，理论保证扎实，但目前仅在简单仿真场景中验证，实际部署前需结合具体数据分布做进一步的实例特定 e-process 设计与实证检验。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does the unknown-variance Gaussian detector (Section 5.1, t-information rate) empirically compare in detection delay to the full-Gaussian instance-optimal detector (Section 5.2) on simulated data — is the theoretical gap between I_t(P,Q) and I_G(P,Q) practically significant at realistic sample sizes?
- Can the "countable local REGROW witness basis" condition be verified or violated for other commonly used nonparametric classes (e.g., log-concave densities, bounded-variance families, exponential families with unbounded natural parameter space) beyond the Gaussian location family shown in Proposition 6.10?
- How does the non-partitioned e-detector perform against genuinely different baseline families (e.g., a GLR-based detector, classical CUSUM with plug-in estimates, or the cpm package of Ross 2015) rather than only against another e-process-based method from the same research lineage?
- What is the practical computational cost of maintaining the infimum over R ∈ P (and the running maximum/adjuster machinery) for higher-dimensional or more complex classes P, beyond the one-dimensional convex optimizations shown for the Gaussian and bounded-mean examples?
- Does the early-change impossibility result (Theorem 7.3) have practical implications for how practitioners should set the ARL/PFA budget A/α when they have prior belief that changes could occur soon after monitoring begins?
