AI4Math · Multi-Agent Conjecture Generation

MECA jointly refines a candidate mathematical conjecture together with the auditable "mechanisms" that justify it — never a guess-first, prove-later pipeline.

一句话总结：MECA 用多智能体（Explorer 提出/修正、Critic 独立审核）将候选数学命题与其支撑"机制"（Req/Prov/Gap 结构化论证）联合精炼为有向机制图，在 20 篇论文的盲测复原任务上把目标对齐得分从基线 48.0 提升到 69.0（+21.0），并将其生成的 100 个猜想交给独立证明系统 QED 核验后，35% 被证明、11% 被反驳，54% 未解决中 81.5% 源于真正的证明难度而非命题本身的缺陷。

MECA: A Mechanism-Centered Agent for Constructing Well-Specified and Valuable Mathematical Conjectures ↗
Wentao Long, Yunfei Zhang, Chenyi Li, Zaiwen Wen · 30 Jul 2026 · arXiv:2607.27709 · AI4Math Multi-Agent LLM Conjecture Generation Automated Theorem Proving
Key Metric
+21.0/100 pts

Average target-alignment score rises from 48.0 → 69.0 on the 20-paper article-blind reconstruction benchmark, vs. a Draft-Audit-Revise baseline given the same budget. Biggest per-dimension gains: Conclusion +6.0, Quantitative +5.5.

Why This Matters

Offers AI4Math and multi-agent LLM research a reusable pattern coupling conjecture generation with auditable mechanism review, plus 100 ready-made open problems for benchmarking future provers.

Critical Edge

The only comparator is a self-built Draft-Audit-Revise ablation — the reported +21-point gain shows MECA beats one internally designed straw-man, not the state of the art in automated conjecturing.

01 Methodology

Stage 1 Seed construction literature source cards (Route A) or curated open problems (Route B), critic-vetted
→
Stage 2 Mechanism–problem co-exploration Explorer ⇄ Mechanism Critic ⇄ Coverage Judge, over a growing DAG
→
Stage 3 Prover-facing projection Final Challenger — blind to the mechanism graph — then Blind/Refute Prover
→
External check QED verification 35% proved · 11% refuted · 54% unresolved
  1. 1
    State. Construction state C_t = (A, p_t, G_t): an immutable target anchor, a mutable candidate statement, and an audited mechanism graph.
  2. 2
    Mechanisms, not proof-sketch labels. Each mechanism m = (Req(m), Prov(m), Gap(m)) is an independently reviewable derivation, composing into a non-circular DAG.
  3. 3
    Coverage verdicts. A Coverage Judge issues InsufficientSupport, SubstantivePartial (the intended stopping state — 1–3 precise unresolved cores remain), or FullCoverage (quarantined for independent verification).
  4. 4
    Anti-confirmation-bias design. The Final Challenger receives the anchor, seed, and source pack but explicitly not the mechanism graph or critic history, running four independent audits before deterministic projection into a self-contained problem.
  5. 5
    Backbone. GPT-5.5 (reasoning effort "xhigh") drives MECA, the baseline, and the target-alignment evaluator; GPT-5.6-sol (xhigh) powers the external QED prover/verifier.

Strengths

  • Clear, well-specified formalism — mechanisms as (Req, Prov, Gap) triples, mechanism graphs, coverage decisions — makes pipeline decisions auditable rather than opaque LLM judgment calls.
  • Deliberate anti-confirmation-bias design: the Final Challenger and Blind/Refute Provers are explicitly denied access to the mechanism graph and critic history.
  • Contributes a concrete, reusable artifact — 100 semi-open problems with explicit unresolved cores across 8 math domains — usable as a benchmark for future theorem-proving systems.
  • Unusually detailed protocol documentation (stopping-rule ceilings, prompt/skill architecture, receipt-invalidation semantics) beyond typical vague LLM-agent methodology write-ups.

Limitations

  • Single, self-designed baseline (Draft-Audit-Revise); no comparison to other published conjecture-generation or research-agent systems (e.g. FunSearch, LeanConjecturer).
  • Evaluation relies substantially on LLM-as-judge scoring from the same model family used for generation, not independent human mathematician review.
  • Benchmark materials (20 target papers, source packs, 100 conjectures) are entirely author-curated from a private repository, not an existing public dataset.
  • No ablation isolates which pipeline component (mechanism graph, Coverage Judge, Final Challenger) actually drives the reported improvements.
  • Domain coverage skews heavily toward continuous optimization and operator theory; generalization to number theory, combinatorics, or algebra is untested.
  • QED's 5-hour, statement-only budget is an arbitrary choice — "unresolved" can't distinguish genuinely hard conjectures from ones needing more search time or a different prover.
Authors & Provenance Wentao Long (Fudan), Yunfei Zhang (Qingdao), Chenyi Li (Peking), and corresponding author Zaiwen Wen (Peking, wenzw@pku.edu.cn) — an established figure in numerical optimization, lending mathematical-rigor credibility. But the team is small, not a large AI4Math lab, and the sole verification backend, QED (An et al.), is itself a non-peer-reviewed 2026 preprint from a different author group.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Does the 54% unresolved / 81.5% "technical difficulty" split hold with provers other than QED (Lean/Isabelle-based systems, or human mathematicians)?
  • What is each component's individual contribution (Coverage Judge, Final Challenger, mechanism graph vs. a flat critic loop) via controlled ablation?
  • Would target-alignment scores hold up under blinded human-expert grading rather than same-model-family LLM evaluation?
  • Can the mechanism abstraction generalize beyond continuous optimization/operator theory to number theory, combinatorics, or algebraic geometry?
  • How sensitive are the reported gains to the specific backbone model — would an open-weight LLM reproduce comparable mechanism-audit quality?
  • Does the Final Challenger's blindness to the mechanism graph still leak information through the frozen source pack and seed?
