> **一句话总结：** 在严格按 token 成本对齐的受控实验中（Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B/7B，GSM8K 与 MATH-500），Self-Refine、Reflexion（强制版）和"Best-of-N 让模型自选"这三类需要模型评判/改写自己输出的方法，在全部 18 项对比中都显著或数值上不敌"多采样几次、投票选众数"这一朴素基线；而 Best-of-N 用同一批候选答案做消融后发现，模型自选比直接计数最常见答案在 1.5B 上差 8.0～11.3 个百分点，7B 时差距缩小到统计上不可区分。

> **TL;DR:** A designed replication of Wang et al. (2024)'s budget-aware claim, this paper compares seven test-time reasoning methods (Chain-of-Thought, Plan-and-Solve, Self-Refine, Reflexion, forced Reflexion, Best-of-N self-verify, Multi-Agent Debate) against a self-consistency sampling baseline at *matched measured token cost*, on Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B/7B over GSM8K and MATH-500 (150 questions each, paired bootstrap CIs, Holm correction across 36 comparisons). No method beats the baseline anywhere; 10 of 36 comparisons are significantly worse, and all 18 comparisons involving self-inspection (Self-Refine, forced Reflexion, Best-of-N) are negative — e.g., Best-of-N self-verify trails cost-matched self-consistency by -14.1pp on Qwen2.5-3B/MATH-500 (52.0% vs 66.1%, p<0.001) and by -8.1pp on Qwen2.5-1.5B/MATH-500 (46.7% vs 54.8%, p=0.041). A same-samples ablation isolates the mechanism further: simply counting the majority answer among Best-of-N's own 8 candidates beats letting the model pick by 8.0–17.3pp below 7B, shrinking to statistical parity at 7B. The published Reflexion algorithm never once triggered its own retry on the 1.5B model (self-judged "correct" on every question), silently degenerating into plain CoT.

## 核心贡献

- 将 Wang et al. (2024) "简单基线在预算对齐后常常获胜"这一此前只有点估计、无置信区间的论断，重新做成一个统计严谨的设计实验：7 种方法 × 3 个模型规模(1.5B/3B/7B) × 2 个数学基准 × 150 题，全部按问题配对、bootstrap 置信区间 + Holm 多重比较校正。
- 提出并实现了"用同一批采样池廉价重建整条自洽性（self-consistency）成本-准确率曲线"的方法：一次生成 K=16 条独立链，通过无放回抽样 200 次重建 N∈{1,2,3,4,6,8,12,16} 各点的准确率与真实 token 成本，使每个待测方法都能在其"自身实测成本"处被插值比较，而非套用统一 query/token 上限。
- 用 Best-of-N 做了一个近乎无混淆的消融：固定同一 8 个采样答案，唯一变量是"模型选一个"还是"直接数众数"，发现前者在小模型上显著更差（5～17 个百分点），且这一差距随模型增大而收窄至 7B 上不可区分。
- 扩展到了 Wang et al. 未覆盖的小模型区间（1.5B、3B），发现 Reflexion 按原论文规范实现时，在 1.5B 上自我评判环节从未触发过重试（因为模型认为自己"每次都对"），实际上悄悄退化成了单条思维链，却仍"表现不错"——揭示了自触发控制流方法可能在不报错的情况下"停止运行"这一评测盲点。
- 公开了完整代码、prompt、全部 19,200 条模型生成记录及独立的二次核算脚本，并在附录中记录了自己犯过的两个记分错误（一个由服务器 4096-token 上下文截断导致的样本偏差，一个 Best-of-N 被误记为多数投票的聚合 bug），体现了少见的透明度。

## Key Contributions

- Re-runs Wang et al. (2024)'s budget-aware claim as a properly powered designed experiment (7 methods × 3 model sizes × 2 math benchmarks × 150 paired questions), replacing point estimates with paired bootstrap CIs and Holm-corrected p-values across the 36 comparisons per setting.
- Introduces an efficient way to reconstruct the entire self-consistency accuracy-vs-cost curve from a single pool of K=16 sampled chains per question (200 without-replacement draws at each N ∈ {1,...,16}), enabling each tested method to be compared against the baseline interpolated at its own measured token cost rather than a shared cap.
- Runs a near-confound-free ablation on Best-of-N: holding the same 8 sampled candidates fixed, it isolates "model picks the best" vs. "majority vote over the same samples," finding the model's choice is worse by 5–17 percentage points below 7B, with the gap closing to statistical noise at 7B.
- Extends the regime to small models (1.5B, 3B) untested by the prior study, and documents that the published Reflexion algorithm never triggers its self-retry on the 1.5B model — it judged its own answer correct on every single question, silently collapsing into a single chain of thought while still scoring competitively.
- Releases the full harness, prompts, all 19,200 raw generations, and an independent re-derivation script, and transparently reports two scoring bugs it found in its own pipeline (a context-truncation-induced sample bias for Best-of-N on MATH-500, and an aggregation bug that had mis-scored Best-of-N as majority voting) along with exactly how the corrected numbers changed.

## Methodology

The paper's core design choice is to compare methods **at equal measured token cost**, not equal wall-clock or a shared cap. Cost is defined as total *generated* (completion) tokens per question, summed across every model call a method makes, including tokens spent on critiques, reflections, debate turns, and verification steps that never appear in the final answer.

**Baseline construction.** The reference is self-consistency (majority vote over N independent chain-of-thought samples at temperature 0.7). Instead of running self-consistency separately at each N, the authors draw a pool of K=16 independent chains once per question, then repeatedly draw N ≤ K chains without replacement (200 draws per N ∈ {1,2,3,4,6,8,12,16}), reading off both the majority-vote outcome and the summed cost of exactly those N chains. This yields the full accuracy-vs-cost curve from one generation pass, and each point corresponds to an experiment that could actually have happened (not an approximation).

**Methods compared** (Table 1): Chain-of-Thought (greedy, 1 call), Plan-and-Solve (greedy, 1 call), Self-Refine (R=3 critique/revise rounds, 7 calls), Reflexion as published (self-judges correctness, stops early if it says "correct"; 1–10 calls), Reflexion (forced) — the authors' own variant that always performs 3 reflect-and-retry rounds regardless of self-judgment (7 calls), Best-of-N self-verify (N=8 samples, model picks the best; 9 calls), and Multi-Agent Debate (3 agents, 2 rounds; 9 calls). Every method is implemented from its original paper's description with one uniform rule: no method is ever shown the correct answer (avoiding the ground-truth leak Huang et al. [2024] identified).

**Models and benchmarks.** Two instruction-tuned Qwen2.5 models (1.5B, 3B) plus 7B, run at Q8_0 quantization via llama.cpp on a single CPU machine (AMD EPYC 7452, no GPU). Benchmarks are GSM8K (150 of 1,319 test items) and MATH-500 (150 of 500 items), both automatically gradable. Generation is capped at 1,024 tokens per call for every method equally; deterministic methods run at temperature 0, sampling-based methods (baseline, Best-of-N, Debate) at temperature 0.7.

**Grading.** Answers are extracted via a deterministic parser (last `\boxed{...}` via real brace matching, else text after "the answer is", else last number). The grader is directly validated: synthetic gold-answer responses under six cosmetic LaTeX variations are graded correctly on 500/500 MATH-500 and 1,319/1,319 GSM8K items.

**Statistics.** All comparisons are paired by question (same 150 questions per method and baseline). For a method with measured mean cost c, the baseline curve is linearly interpolated between the two adjacent integer-N points bracketing c. Confidence intervals come from a paired bootstrap (10,000 resamples over questions); p-values are two-sided and Holm-corrected across the 7 methods tested within each of the 4 settings (model × dataset, since 7B was only run with 4 of the 7 methods reported in the main comparison table — Plan-and-Solve, Reflexion, and Debate appear to be omitted at 7B in Table 2, though the paper doesn't dwell on this).

The paper is explicit about statistical power: with 150 paired questions, the standard error of a paired difference runs 1.3–3.2pp (median 2.6), so the design can reliably detect differences of roughly 5–6pp or larger; smaller true differences cannot be distinguished from zero. This is stated as a limitation up front (Section 4.6), not discovered post hoc.

## Results & Highlights

- **No method beats the equal-cost sampling baseline in any of the 36 method–setting comparisons after Holm correction; 10 are significantly worse, and 26 are statistically indistinguishable.** The 10 significant losses are concentrated entirely in methods that inspect/rewrite their own output: e.g., Best-of-N self-verify on Qwen2.5-1.5B/MATH-500 scores 46.7% vs. cost-matched self-consistency's 54.8% (Δ = -8.1pp, 95% CI [-14.2, -2.1], p=0.041); Best-of-N on Qwen2.5-3B/MATH-500 scores 52.0% vs. 66.1% (Δ = -14.1pp, 95% CI [-20.0, -8.4], p<0.001); Reflexion (forced) on Qwen2.5-7B/MATH-500 scores 63.3% vs. 73.5% (Δ = -10.1pp, 95% CI [-15.0, -5.7], p<0.001).
- **The same-samples Best-of-N ablation is the paper's cleanest result** (Table 3): holding the 8 sampled candidates fixed and only varying the selection rule (model judges vs. simple counting), counting wins in every one of 6 settings, by 1.3 to 17.3 percentage points — e.g., Qwen2.5-3B/MATH-500: model-judged 52.0% vs. counted 69.3% (Δ = -17.3pp, 95% CI [-24.0, -11.3], p<0.001); Qwen2.5-1.5B/MATH-500: 46.7% vs. 58.0% (Δ = -11.3pp, 95% CI [-17.3, -6.0], p<0.001). At 7B the gap shrinks to statistical parity: Qwen2.5-7B/GSM8K, -2.0pp [-5.3, +0.7], p=0.241; Qwen2.5-7B/MATH-500, -1.3pp [-4.7, +2.0], p=0.538.
- **Self-consistency itself scales predictably with N**: on Qwen2.5-1.5B/MATH-500, accuracy rises from 44.1% at N=1 (540 tokens) to 55.8% at N=16 (8,636 tokens); on Qwen2.5-7B/GSM8K, from 90.1% at N=1 (285 tokens) to 93.4% at N=16 (4,560 tokens).
- **Reflexion's published self-assessment never fired on the 1.5B model** (100% "stopped immediately" on both benchmarks, mean rounds used = 0.00), meaning the method silently became a single chain of thought while still appearing competitive; on Qwen2.5-3B it fired far more often (77% stopped immediately on GSM8K, only 31% on MATH-500, mean 1.82 rounds used on MATH-500).
- **A grading bug the authors caught by cross-checking**: Best-of-N's candidate list was initially mis-aggregated as a majority vote (like Debate) rather than the model's actual chosen answer, making it appear to sit on the baseline (+0.3 to +3.2pp, never significant); after the fix it is below baseline in every setting, significant in three, and supplies "the paper's only result that survives Holm correction across all 28 comparisons at once" — a striking admission that the sharpest finding was initially invisible due to their own pipeline bug.
- **Difficulty-stratified exploratory analysis** (Qwen2.5-1.5B/MATH-500, uncorrected, n=38/30/82 for easy/medium/hard) shows Best-of-N's penalty balloons on medium-difficulty items (-21.7pp) and stays negative on hard (-7.0pp), while faring better on easy items (+1.2pp) — suggestive but explicitly flagged as not corrected for multiple testing.

No claim of "no quantitative results" applies here — the paper is dense with concrete numbers as shown above.

## Authors & Credibility

Single-author paper (Iliya Mirzaei, Department of Computer Science, Stony Brook University), dated July 31, 2026, positioned explicitly as a *replication with extensions* of Wang et al. (2024) ("Reasoning in Token Economies," arXiv:2406.06461) rather than a claim to a new phenomenon. This is a modest, single-author effort with limited institutional signal (no co-authors, no stated funding/compute grant), but the work is compensated for by an unusually thorough reproducibility and self-auditing posture: full release of harness, prompts, all 19,200 raw generations, and an independent re-derivation script that cross-checks every reported number from raw text without importing the original analysis code (Appendix A.6, reporting 96/96 independent checks agreeing). The paper explicitly documents two internal scoring bugs it found and fixed (a context-truncation sampling bias, and a Best-of-N aggregation bug), which is a strong positive signal for intellectual honesty rarely seen in venue submissions.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** Modest and self-acknowledged — this is explicitly a replication, not a new phenomenon (Section 1, "Scope"). The genuinely new pieces are (a) adding proper statistical inference (paired bootstrap CIs, Holm correction) to a claim that previously rested on 100-question point estimates with no error bars, (b) extending to small models (1.5B, 3B) the prior study didn't cover, and (c) the same-samples Best-of-N ablation, which is a clean, low-confound experiment design that isolates the selection mechanism specifically.

**Rigor:** Notably careful for a single-author paper. It states its own detection threshold up front (~5–6pp) rather than post hoc, reports both a pre-registered grouping (methods with repeated passes over their own work) and a post hoc grouping (formed after fixing the Best-of-N bug), and is explicit that the post hoc grouping is "a description of the pattern rather than a hypothesis test." It also runs a subset-restricted robustness check (questions every method could parse within the 1,024-token cap) and finds the verdict is *not* stable for every method under that restriction — e.g., on Qwen2.5-1.5B/MATH-500, Best-of-N's effect moves from -8.1pp (p=0.041, significant) to -6.9pp (p=0.564, not significant) when restricted to 84/150 "easy-enough-to-finish" questions. The paper flags this honestly rather than picking the more favorable analysis.

**A concrete weak point worth naming**: the "1024-token generation cap applied equally to all methods" is not actually cost-neutral in its consequences — it disproportionately truncates single-shot methods on long MATH-500 solutions (up to 16% unparseable rate for Reflexion on Qwen2.5-1.5B/MATH-500) while multi-sample methods lose less than 1% of answers to truncation because they only need *some* samples to finish. The paper acknowledges this directly (Section 5.5) and shows the truncation created a real, documented distortion in an earlier version of their own results (Appendix A.4: chain-of-thought scored 67.9% on the truncated 78-question survivor set vs. 47.3% on the full 150), which they caught and fixed by re-running with more context. This is a rare, valuable transparency but also underscores how fragile the token-cap convention is for cross-method comparison.

**Missing ablation / scope limit the authors themselves flag**: they never test whether a better-designed verifier prompt (structured rubric, separate scoring per candidate rather than choose-among-N) would close the Best-of-N gap — they explicitly say "what we cannot rule out is that a longer or more structured rubric... would do better." This means the headline "self-evaluation loses" claim is scoped tightly to *the cheap self-check that Best-of-N as usually described implies*, not to self-evaluation in general.

**Reproducibility:** Strong. Seeds are derived deterministically via SHA-256 hashing of (model, dataset, method, configuration, question index), full prompts are given verbatim in Appendix A.2, and all 19,200 generations plus harness and independent re-derivation scripts are released.

**Significance:** The practical takeaway — that self-critique/reflection methods often just repackage "spend more tokens" and lose to naive majority voting at equal cost, especially below 7B — is a useful negative result for practitioners deciding how to spend an inference budget. But the scope is deliberately narrow: two small-to-mid open models (max 7B), CPU-only 8-bit quantization, and two math benchmarks with automatically checkable single-value answers where majority voting is even definable. The authors are explicit that this baseline (majority voting) doesn't exist for open-ended tasks (summaries, prose), so the finding cannot be generalized to the domains where methods like Self-Refine were originally proposed — a genuinely disclosed limitation rather than overclaiming.

**Red flags:** None major. The paper is unusually self-critical, documenting its own two scoring bugs, an interpolation-direction bias assessment, and Monte Carlo stability checks (re-running with a different subsampling seed moves estimates by at most 0.36pp). If anything, this is a case of a paper being *more* rigorous about its own uncertainty than most published test-time-scaling papers.

## Strengths

- Statistically rigorous by the standards of this literature: every comparison is paired, bootstrapped, and Holm-corrected, with a stated detection threshold rather than an implicit one.
- Genuinely confound-controlled ablation (Best-of-N same-samples comparison) that isolates the selection mechanism from the sampling budget entirely.
- Unusual transparency: documents two internal bugs (context-truncation bias, Best-of-N aggregation bug) that materially changed the results, and shows exactly how much the numbers moved.
- Full open release of code, prompts, all generations, and an independently-coded re-derivation script — enabling actual audit rather than trust.
- Explicitly states what the design cannot detect (differences below ~5-6pp) and treats non-significant results as "consistent with a range" rather than "no effect."
- Extends coverage to small models (1.5B, 3B) that the predecessor study skipped, revealing the Reflexion self-assessment-never-fires phenomenon that would have been invisible at 7B-only scale.

## Limitations

- Model scale capped at 7B on the main cost-matched comparison (Best-of-N ablation reaches 7B but the broader 7-method comparison table appears reduced to 4 methods at 7B); whether self-evaluation eventually overtakes counting at larger scale is explicitly left untested.
- Single quantization level (8-bit) and CPU-only inference; interactions between quantization and method behavior are not ruled out.
- Task domain restricted to math with automatically checkable single-value answers — majority voting isn't even definable for open-ended generation, so the result cannot be extended to summarization or open-ended dialogue without a different evaluation design.
- One implementation per method, with fixed hyperparameters (rounds, agents, samples) chosen in advance rather than tuned — reasonable to avoid a ground-truth leak, but means each method's best-case performance may be understated.
- Cost measured only in generated (completion) tokens; input tokens, wall-clock latency, and dollar cost are explicitly not measured, though the paper argues the direction of bias from this choice favors the tested methods, not against them.
- Verifier prompt for Best-of-N is a single simple "which is most likely correct" query; a more structured rubric might change the self-evaluation result, which the authors themselves acknowledge as untested.
- Statistical power limited to detecting ~5–6pp differences with 150 questions; many of the 26 "indistinguishable" comparisons could still hide real but smaller effects in either direction.

## Relevance

对于关注推理时计算(test-time compute)分配、自我修正/自反思方法、以及"公平基准对比"方法论的研究者，这篇论文提供了一个统计严谨、可复现、高透明度的反例研究：在小到中等规模开源模型和数学推理任务上，多数自我评判/自我改写类方法在等 token 成本下并不比简单多采样投票更划算，这对设计新的 test-time reasoning 方法或撰写评测协议时有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would a more structured, per-candidate-scoring verifier (rather than "which is best among N" in one prompt) close the Best-of-N gap below 7B, as the authors suggest but do not test?
- Does the "self-evaluation catches up to counting by 7B" trend documented here (Figure 3) continue, plateau, or reverse at frontier model scale (e.g., 70B+ or state-of-the-art proprietary models)?
- How would these conclusions change under an open-ended generation task where majority voting isn't definable — would a weaker baseline (e.g., picking one sample arbitrarily) let self-critique methods win, as the authors speculate in Section 6.5?
- What is the actual input-token or wall-clock-latency ranking, given the paper concedes debate and the sequential self-correction methods would look worse (not better) under those alternative cost measures?
- Would training a lightweight verifier (rather than prompting the same generator model to self-judge) close the Best-of-N gap even below 7B, consistent with Zhang et al.'s finding that trained self-verifiers beat majority voting — i.e., is training rather than model scale the actual lever?
