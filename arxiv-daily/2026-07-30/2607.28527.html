01 How MANTA Works

C = coordinator · W = worker · V = verifier (inserted by mutation)
  1. Topology Planner reads the query + long-term playbook (never the benchmark identity) and emits a JSON plan: roles, pattern groups (star / chain / debate / voting), context policies.
  2. Turn Executor runs one collaboration round; agents exchange structured "relay packets" (answer, evidence, confidence, unresolved issues) instead of raw transcripts.
  3. Trace Auditor (an LLM) scans each turn for 9 process-anomaly flags — e.g. evidence_lost_before_synthesis, branch_collapse, missing_validator — and explicitly never judges answer correctness.
  4. Controller authorizes at most one mutation of ≤3 operations — add_agent, expand_agent_to_group, set_group_pattern, add/remove_edge, set_context_policy — validated for acyclicity, role legality, agent budget ≤10.
  5. Two-horizon memory: a per-run short-term log, plus a markdown "playbook" rewritten every 12 runs from process signals only — ground-truth correctness is never shown to the Planner or Reflector.
Eval setup: Gemma 4 31B (medium reasoning), 30 questions × 3 runs, 5 benchmarks (BrowseComp, StableToolBench, PlanCraft, WorkBench, MATH).

  • Ablation: full MANTA 71.7% → 60.8% without topology repair → 67.5%/66.7% with the playbook frozen/removed. Initial planning matters most; repair is second.
  • Mutation budget (0→3): success rises steeply at the first mutation, then flattens — most benefit comes from the first repair opportunity.
  • Auditor precision is modest (37.5% aggregate): it flags 64.1% of wrong answers, and clean runs are correct 83.2% of the time vs. 62.5% for flagged runs.

Strengths

  • Genuinely new framing: topology as an inference-time, not just design-time, object of adaptation, with a clean "levels of self-improvement" taxonomy.
  • Broad empirical footprint — 5 benchmarks, 3 capability categories, 10+ baselines spanning single-agent, static, and auto-topology-search systems.
  • Mutations are engineered to be cheap and lossless: stateless agents, append-only evidence store, context controller re-pointing.
  • Unusually honest self-diagnosis of the Trace Auditor's own precision/recall, rather than just asserting it "works."
  • Concrete case studies (branch expansion, critic insertion, edge rewiring) show what a "repair" actually looks like.

Limitations

  • Single backbone (Gemma 4 31B, medium reasoning) throughout — no evidence gains generalize across model families or scales.
  • Underperforms strong baselines on 2 of 5 benchmarks (WorkBench, StableToolBench) — not surfaced in the abstract.
  • Small samples (30 questions × 3 runs/benchmark) leave wide confidence intervals on several pairwise comparisons.
  • Auditor flags are validated only against a correctness proxy, not ground-truth process-defect labels — authors flag this gap themselves.
  • Capped at exactly one mutation per run; behavior under repeated/iterative repair beyond budget 3 is unexplored.

? Questions for Further Exploration

Q.Is MASS's 0% cross-domain collapse a genuine limitation of jointly-optimized prompts, or a baseline re-configuration artifact?
Q.Would a larger mutation budget close MANTA's WorkBench gap, given it trails even several static baselines there?
Q.Do the gains hold on a different backbone family/scale — the harness claims to support two LLM backends, but only one appears in the main results.
Q.What would human-annotated process-defect ground truth reveal about the Auditor's true precision, versus the correctness-proxy numbers reported?
Q.Does allowing >1 mutation per run change the diminishing-returns curve, or introduce new failure modes like oscillating topologies?
