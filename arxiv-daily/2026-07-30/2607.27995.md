> **一句话总结：** 本文在 RKHS 框架下用核积分算子的谱理论精确刻画了对抗训练的逼近-估计-泛化误差，证明在一个固定的多项式谱模型上（β=2, α=0.25）标准对抗训练的最优平衡速率 $n^{-\beta(2\alpha+1)/(2(\beta(2\alpha+1)+\alpha+1))}$ 严格慢于核岭回归可达到的极小极大最优速率，并提出一个两阶段"去噪对抗训练"（noise-debiased AT）估计器，理论上（近似对数因子）恢复极小极大多项式速率，实验中在合成数据上把泛化误差从 AT 的最高约 0.28（塌缩为零解）降到 ND-AT 稳定的 0.06 左右，并在 Communities & Crime 和 Abalone 两个真实数据集的测试期 $\ell_2$ 攻击下取得更低的清洁及大部分攻击预算下的 RMSE。

> **TL;DR:** This theory paper analyzes adversarial training (AT) in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space via the associated kernel integral operator, deriving source-uniform generalization bounds and, on a fixed polynomial-eigenvalue-decay model (β=2, α=0.25), a matching minimax lower bound showing the optimally-balanced AT rate $n^{-\beta(2\alpha+1)/(2(\beta(2\alpha+1)+\alpha+1))}$ is strictly slower than the kernel-ridge-regression minimax benchmark $n^{-\beta(\alpha+1/2)/(2\beta(\alpha+1/2)+1)}$ — a provable robustness-accuracy trade-off. It then proposes a two-stage noise-debiased AT (ND-AT) estimator that subtracts the estimated mean-absolute-noise contribution from the mixed robustness term, provably attaining the minimax polynomial rate up to a log factor (Corollary 4.3); on synthetic data (n=800, 50 replications) ND-AT's approximation-error log-log slope is 1.38 (vs. AT's 0.55, theory predicts 1.5 vs. 0.60) and its generalization error stays in a narrow 0.06–0.09 band across δ∈[0.1,0.5] while AT's error rises monotonically and collapses to the null-fit baseline (‖f*‖=0.286) once δ>0.32; on Communities & Crime and Abalone under PGD ℓ2 test-time attacks, ND-AT beats the AT baseline of Ribeiro et al. (2026) on clean RMSE and over most (not all) attack budgets ρ∈[0,0.5].

## 核心贡献

- **RKHS 框架下的对抗训练泛化误差刻画**：在特征空间对抗鲁棒目标（Ribeiro et al. 2026 的等价重构 $\min_f \frac1n\sum(|Y_i-f(X_i)|+\delta\|f\|_{\mathcal H_k})^2$）下，借助核积分算子 $T_k$ 的谱分解，分别给出逼近误差界（Theorem 3.1：$\|f^*_\delta-f^*\|_{L^2}\lesssim \delta^{(\alpha+1/2)/(\alpha+1)}$）和估计误差界（Theorem 3.2：以有效维数 $\mathcal N(\delta)=\mathrm{Tr}\{(T_k+\delta I)^{-1}T_k\}$ 为核心），并合并为完整泛化界（Corollary 3.3）。
- **匹配下界证明真实的统计效率损失**：构造一个固定的多项式谱模型（$\sigma_j\asymp j^{-\beta}$，有界中心化噪声），证明该模型上对抗训练估计器的下界与上界多项式阶数一致（Theorem 3.4），从而严格证明当 $0<\alpha\le 1/2$ 时最优平衡的全对抗训练速率慢于核岭回归可达到的极小极大最优速率 $n^{-\beta(\alpha+1/2)/(2\beta(\alpha+1/2)+1)}$（Caponnetto & De Vito 2007）。
- **定位效率损失的根源**：分析表明，混合鲁棒项中的噪声贡献（$2\delta\|f\|_{\mathcal H_k}\mathbb E|Y-f(X)|$ 展开后含 $2\delta\|f^*\|_{\mathcal H_k}\mathbb E|\varepsilon|$）拖慢了逼近速率（从岭回归的 $\delta^{\min\{2\alpha+1,2\}}$ 退化为 $\delta^{(\alpha+1/2)/(\alpha+1)}$），尽管同一项的曲率也降低了估计复杂度（有效维数从 $\mathcal N(\delta^2)$ 变为 $\mathcal N(\delta)$）。
- **两阶段去噪对抗训练（ND-AT）方法**：先用独立辅助/残差样本拟合核岭回归并估计平均绝对噪声 $\mathbb E|\varepsilon|$，再从对抗目标的混合项中减去该估计噪声贡献（式 7），证明去噪后逼近误差改进为 $\delta^{\min\{2\alpha+1,2\}}$（Theorem 4.1），整体估计器在合适选取 $\delta_n$ 阶数下达到极小极大多项式速率（差一个 $\log$ 因子，Corollary 4.3）。
- **鲁棒性-精度权衡的精确刻画**：证明去噪不会改善对抗目标本身，其鲁棒性代价被精确界定（Proposition 4.5），并给出零解阈值 $\delta^{\mathrm{AT}}, \delta^{\mathrm{ND}}$ 的显式公式及二者比值下界（Corollary 4.6），显示随信噪比降低标准 AT 更容易塌缩为零函数而 ND-AT 阈值不受此影响。

## Key Contributions

- **Operator-theoretic characterization of AT generalization in RKHS.** Working with the feature-space adversarial objective (equivalent reformulation from Ribeiro et al. 2026: $\min_f \frac1n\sum(|Y_i-f(X_i)|+\delta\|f\|_{\mathcal H_k})^2$), the paper uses the spectral calculus of the kernel integral operator $T_k$ to derive separate approximation-error bounds (Theorem 3.1: $\|f^*_\delta-f^*\|_{L^2}\lesssim \delta^{(\alpha+1/2)/(\alpha+1)}$ under a source condition $f^*=T_k^\alpha g$) and estimation-error bounds (Theorem 3.2, governed by the effective dimension $\mathcal N(\delta)=\mathrm{Tr}\{(T_k+\delta I)^{-1}T_k\}$), which are combined into a complete finite-sample generalization bound (Corollary 3.3).
- **A matching minimax lower bound proving a genuine statistical-efficiency loss.** On a fixed bounded-kernel polynomial-eigenvalue model ($\sigma_j\asymp j^{-\beta}$, bounded centered noise satisfying Assumptions 2.2–2.3), the paper proves a lower bound (Theorem 3.4) whose polynomial order matches the upper bound of Corollary 3.3, establishing that for $0<\alpha\le 1/2$ the best-balanced full-AT rate $n^{-\beta(2\alpha+1)/(2(\beta(2\alpha+1)+\alpha+1))}$ is strictly slower than the kernel-ridge-regression minimax rate $n^{-\beta(\alpha+1/2)/(2\beta(\alpha+1/2)+1)}$ (Caponnetto & De Vito, 2007).
- **Isolating the source of the loss.** The mixed robustness term's noise contribution ($2\delta\|f^*\|_{\mathcal H_k}\mathbb E|\varepsilon|$ at $f=f^*$) is identified as slowing the approximation rate from the ridge order $\delta^{\min\{2\alpha+1,2\}}$ down to $\delta^{(\alpha+1/2)/(\alpha+1)}$, even though the same term's added curvature reduces estimation complexity (effective dimension shrinks from scale $\delta^2$ to scale $\delta$).
- **Two-stage noise-debiased adversarial training (ND-AT).** A preliminary KRR estimator on an auxiliary sample estimates $\mathbb E|\varepsilon|$ (via an independent residual sample), and this estimate is subtracted from the mixed term of the AT objective (Eq. 7). The debiased population minimizer recovers the ridge-order approximation rate $\delta^{\min\{2\alpha+1,2\}}$ (Theorem 4.1), and the full estimator attains the minimax polynomial rate up to a $\sqrt{\log n}$ factor when $\delta$ is set at the theoretically prescribed sample-dependent order (Corollary 4.3, for $0<\alpha\le 1/2$).
- **Exact characterization of the robustness-accuracy trade-off.** Proposition 4.5 gives a fixed-radius comparison showing debiasing cannot improve the adversarial objective itself and bounds its robustness cost; Corollary 4.6 derives closed-form null-solution thresholds $\delta^{\mathrm{AT}}=\|T_kf^*\|_{\mathcal H_k}/\mathbb E|Y|$ and $\delta^{\mathrm{ND}}=\|T_kf^*\|_{\mathcal H_k}/(\mathbb E|Y|-\mathbb E|\varepsilon|)$, proving the ratio $\delta^{\mathrm{ND}}/\delta^{\mathrm{AT}}$ diverges as the noise level grows — i.e., standard AT collapses to the trivial zero fit far more readily than ND-AT as signal-to-noise falls.

## Methodology

The paper studies adversarial training as the RKHS feature-space minimax problem $\min_f \max_{\|\Delta_i\|_{\mathcal H_k}\le\delta} \frac1n\sum_i(Y_i-\langle f,k_{X_i}+\Delta_i\rangle_{\mathcal H_k})^2$, which (Proposition 2.6, citing Ribeiro et al. 2026) reduces to the tractable convex problem $\min_f \frac1n\sum_i(|Y_i-f(X_i)|+\delta\|f\|_{\mathcal H_k})^2$ (Eq. 4); by the generalized representer theorem the minimizer is finite-dimensional ($\widehat f_\delta=\sum_i a_i k_{X_i}$). The analysis places standard nonparametric assumptions on the data-generating process (additive regression model $Y=f^*(X)+\varepsilon$, quadratic behavior of the absolute risk near its minimum — Assumption 2.2 — and sub-exponential-type noise-tail moments — Assumption 2.3), plus the two classic RKHS regularity conditions: a source condition ($f^*=T_k^\alpha g$, $\|g\|_{\mathcal H_k}\le R$, $0<\alpha\le1$) and polynomial eigenvalue decay of the kernel integral operator $T_k$ ($\sigma_j\asymp j^{-\beta}$, $\beta>1$).

The generalization error is decomposed via the standard triangle-inequality split into approximation error (population minimizer $f^*_\delta$ vs. truth $f^*$) and estimation error (empirical minimizer $\widehat f_\delta$ vs. population minimizer $f^*_\delta$). Approximation error is bounded (Theorem 3.1) via a ridge-type decomposition using the resolvent $(T_k+\lambda_\delta I)^{-1}$ with an effective regularization level $\lambda_\delta=\delta^2+\delta\,\mathbb E|Y-f^*_\delta(X)|/\|f^*_\delta\|_{\mathcal H_k}$ that absorbs the noise contribution of the absolute-loss subgradient; estimation error (Theorem 3.2) uses Hilbert-valued Bernstein concentration (Theorem A.6, built from Pinelis-Sakhanenko 1986) combined with an operator-concentration result for $T_k$ vs. its empirical counterpart $T_n$ (Theorem A.5, adapted from Rudi et al. 2015). The matching lower bound (Theorem 3.4) is constructed on a specific fixed polynomial-spectrum model with bounded centered noise satisfying the same assumptions, giving an estimator-specific (not information-theoretic-in-general) minimax argument.

The proposed noise-debiased estimator (Section 4) is a two-stage procedure: Step 1 fits a preliminary kernel ridge regression estimator $\widehat f^{\mathrm{KRR}}$ on an auxiliary sample and computes $\widetilde m=\frac1{N_e}\sum|Y_i^e-\widehat f^{\mathrm{KRR}}(X_i^e)|$ on an independent residual sample; Step 2 fits $\widetilde f_\delta\in\arg\min\{\frac1n\sum(Y_i-f(X_i))^2+\delta^2\|f\|_{\mathcal H_k}^2+2\delta\|f\|_{\mathcal H_k}(\frac1n\sum|Y_i-f(X_i)|-\widetilde m)\}$ (Eq. 7), which subtracts the estimated noise floor from the mixed robustness term. This has an equivalent finite-dimensional convex second-order-cone-type reformulation (Proposition 4.7) that is used for computation. All 63 pages include a full appendix (Sections A.1–A.2, roughly pages 18–61) with complete proofs; the main text (Sections 1–5) spans about 17 pages.

## Results & Highlights

- **Synthetic data, approximation-error slopes (Figure 1b, n=4096 truncated Rademacher features, β=2, α=0.25, δ∈[0.1,0.5]):** ND-AT's log-log approximation-error slope is **1.38**, close to the theoretical exponent $2\alpha+1=1.5$ (Theorem 4.1); AT's slope is **0.55**, close to the theoretical exponent $(\alpha+1/2)/(\alpha+1)=0.60$ (Theorem 3.1). This is the paper's cleanest quantitative validation of the core theoretical claim that debiasing yields a strictly steeper (faster) approximation rate.
- **Synthetic data, generalization error (Figure 1a, n=800, 50 replications):** ND-AT's root-$L^2(\mu)$ generalization error stays in a narrow band, reaching a minimum of **0.059 at δ=0.25** and rising only to **0.093 at δ=0.5**; the AT baseline's error rises monotonically and **collapses to the null-fit error of 0.286 (=‖f*‖_{L²(μ)}) once δ exceeds approximately 0.32**. This is a clear, large (≈3×) and paper-reported quantitative delta between the proposed method and the AT baseline at the largest tested radius.
- **Communities & Crime (n=1,994, d=99 predictors), test RMSE under PGD ℓ2 attack at training radius δ=0.1 (Figure 2a, 20 splits):** at attack budget ρ=0, ND-AT achieves clean test RMSE of **≈0.645** vs. AT's **≈0.717**; at ρ=0.5, ND-AT is **≈0.715** vs. AT's **≈0.760** — ND-AT has lower RMSE than the AT baseline (Ribeiro et al. 2026's benchmark) at every attack budget considered on this dataset.
- **Abalone (n=4,177, d=10 predictors), test RMSE under attack (Figure 2b):** ND-AT beats AT for attack budgets ρ≤0.33 (clean RMSE ≈0.72 vs. AT's ≈0.79), but the curves cross near **ρ=0.33**, and by ρ=0.5 AT has the lower RMSE (**≈0.86**) versus ND-AT's **≈0.90** — the paper explicitly reports this as a case where its method does *not* uniformly dominate, consistent with Proposition 4.5's finding that debiasing increases the fitted function's RKHS norm (hence potentially its sensitivity to large perturbations) even as it lowers clean-data error.
- No large-scale deep learning benchmarks are used; both real-data experiments are classical UCI-style tabular regression tasks (Communities & Crime, Abalone) with an RBF kernel, following the exact evaluation protocol of Ribeiro et al. (2026).

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core contribution — bringing kernel-integral-operator spectral techniques to bear on adversarial training specifically, and identifying the noise contribution of the mixed norm-loss term as the precise mechanism behind a provable minimax rate loss — appears genuinely new relative to the cited related work. Shafieezadeh-Abadeh et al. (2019) and Ribeiro et al. (2026) formulate distributionally robust/adversarial learning in RKHS but, per the paper's own account, do not provide the operator-theoretic approximation/estimation decomposition or the matching lower bound. The two-stage debiasing fix is a natural but non-trivial technical contribution.

**Rigor:** The theory is unusually careful and self-contained — assumptions are explicit and mild (Assumption 2.2's quadratic-behavior condition is shown to hold for bounded-density noise with median zero, without requiring differentiability), all main-text theorems are proved in a 45-page appendix with auxiliary lemmas built from named external results (Pinelis & Sakhanenko 1986 Bernstein inequality, Rudi et al. 2015 operator concentration), and constants are explicitly tracked (Remark 4.4 shows the preliminary KRR error enters $\widetilde m$ only quadratically, a genuinely useful robustness-of-the-plug-in-estimate result). One caveat the paper itself flags (Remark 3.5): the matching lower bound (Theorem 3.4) is an *estimator-specific* result on one fixed constructed model, not a universal information-theoretic lower bound over all possible spectra/noise distributions — so "AT is always slower than minimax" is not literally proven in full generality, only exhibited on a specific (admittedly natural) polynomial-spectrum construction.

**Reproducibility:** The synthetic experiment is fully specified (truncated Rademacher feature kernel, $\sigma_j\propto j^{-2}$, $f^*=T_k^{1/4}g$, $n=800$, 50 replications, explicit ridge parameter $\lambda_a=N_a^{-1/2}$) — reproducible from the text alone. The real-data experiments use two small, well-known UCI datasets with the median-heuristic RBF bandwidth and 5-fold CV for the ridge parameter, and 20 random splits — also reasonably reproducible, though no code repository is referenced in the read portions of the paper.

**Significance and red flags:** The Abalone result (Figure 2b) is a genuine, honestly-reported counterexample to the headline claim of "ND-AT is better" — AT overtakes ND-AT for attack budgets $\rho>0.33$, and the paper does not hide this, instead using it to argue (via Proposition 4.5) that clean-accuracy gains and worst-case-attack robustness are logically distinct quantities. This kind of self-aware limitation reporting is a positive rigor signal rather than a red flag. A more substantive limitation: the real-data comparison baseline is only the single "AT" method of Ribeiro et al. (2026) — no comparison against standard (non-adversarial) kernel ridge regression, no comparison against other robust nonparametric estimators (e.g., Peng & Yang 2025's minimax-adaptive estimator, which is cited as closely related but not empirically compared), and no ablation on the effect of the auxiliary/residual sample-split fractions (30%/15%/rest) on ND-AT's own performance. The theoretical results are also confined to the regime $0<\alpha\le1/2$ for the minimax-optimality claim of Corollary 4.3; the paper is transparent that for $\beta\ge\alpha+1$ only a suboptimal $\sqrt{\log n/n}$-radius bound is certified, not a claim about the estimator's actual behavior at an unconstrained balancing radius.

## Strengths

- Clean, complete operator-theoretic decomposition of AT generalization error into approximation and estimation components, each analyzed with explicit, checkable assumptions.
- A genuine matching lower bound (not just an upper bound) that pins down the polynomial rate loss, going beyond prior "robustness hurts accuracy" folklore results in parametric settings (Tsipras et al. 2019; Zhang et al. 2019).
- A concrete, computationally tractable fix (noise-debiased AT) with its own matching theory (approximation, estimation, minimax rate) and an honest characterization of its own cost (Proposition 4.5, Corollary 4.6) rather than an unqualified "our method is strictly better" claim.
- Experiments (both synthetic and two real datasets) directly validate the specific theoretical exponents predicted (slopes 1.38 vs. 1.5, and 0.55 vs. 0.60), which is a stronger form of empirical validation than typically seen in pure learning-theory papers.

## Limitations

- Only $0<\alpha\le1/2$ is covered by the minimax-optimality guarantee for ND-AT (Corollary 4.3); behavior for $\alpha\in(1/2,1]$ is not characterized to the same standard.
- The matching lower bound (Theorem 3.4) holds on one specific constructed model (fixed polynomial spectrum, bounded centered noise), not as a universal minimax lower bound across all models satisfying the stated assumptions (explicitly acknowledged in Remark 3.5).
- Real-data validation is limited to two small tabular regression benchmarks (Communities & Crime, Abalone) with an RBF kernel; no image, text, or high-dimensional deep-learning-scale evaluation is attempted, and the feature-space threat model (perturbing $k_X$) is only loosely connected to input-space perturbations via a Lipschitz-type inequality, not an exact equivalence.
- No comparison to alternative nonparametric-robust estimators beyond the single AT baseline from Ribeiro et al. (2026); no sensitivity analysis for the sample-splitting ratios used by the two-stage procedure.
- On Abalone, the proposed ND-AT method is outperformed by plain AT for larger attack budgets ($\rho>0.33$), meaning the practical robustness benefit is not universal.

## Relevance

本文为对抗训练在非参数（RKHS）框架下的统计效率损失提供了严格的算子谱理论解释，并给出一个可计算、有理论保证的去噪修正方法；对关注鲁棒统计学习理论、核方法泛化界或对抗鲁棒性-精度权衡机制的研究方向具有较高参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the noise-debiasing idea extend to classification losses (hinge/logistic) or to non-RKHS nonparametric estimators (e.g., local polynomial or neural tangent kernel regimes), where the mixed robustness term may take a different form?
- Can the matching lower bound of Theorem 3.4 be generalized to a genuine minimax lower bound over the full class of models satisfying Assumptions 2.1–2.5, rather than one fixed constructed model?
- What explains the Abalone crossover at $\rho\approx0.33$ mechanistically — is it purely the RKHS-norm increase predicted by Proposition 4.5, or does the RBF bandwidth/dimensionality of Abalone's 10 predictors play a distinguishing role relative to Communities & Crime's 99?
- How does the two-stage procedure's performance depend on the auxiliary/residual sample-split ratios, and is there a principled (rather than heuristic 30%/15%/55%) way to choose them jointly with $\delta$?
- Could the debiasing idea be combined with adaptive (data-driven) selection of $\delta$ itself, rather than assuming the sample-dependent theoretical order is known in advance?
