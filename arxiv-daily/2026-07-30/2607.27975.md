> **一句话总结：** 本文把 Transformer 训练看作定义在概率测度上的有限时域马尔可夫决策过程，通过对状态、权重和"测度状态"三重量化得到一个真正有限的 MDP，用 McDiarmid 不等式加值函数的 Lipschitz 稳定性估计，证明了显式的有限样本泛化界（Theorem 3.3、4.4，样本复杂度对样本数 $K_r$ 呈 $O(K_r^{-1/2})$），并进一步证明同一套框架等价于一个 Wasserstein 分布鲁棒控制/零和博弈问题，鲁棒极小元与训练极小元享有相同的有限样本界（Theorem 5.4、5.5）；论文纯为理论工作，未给出任何数值实验。

> **TL;DR:** This purely theoretical paper (companion to the same authors' arXiv:2603.09571) casts Transformer training as a finite-horizon Markov decision process on probability laws over empirical input/output measures, quantizes state, action (weight), and measure-state spaces to obtain a genuine finite MDP, and proves an explicit finite-sample excess-risk bound (Theorem 3.3, transferred to the un-quantized base model in Theorem 4.4) that achieves the optimal $O(K_r^{-1/2})$ rate in the number of training sequences $K_r$; it then shows the same generalization question is equivalent to a Wasserstein distributionally robust control (zero-sum game) formulation whose robust minimizers are $\Gamma$-equivalent to and share the same finite-sample bound as ordinary training minimizers (Theorems 5.3–5.5). There are no datasets, baselines, or numerical experiments anywhere in the paper — it is a self-contained proof-driven contribution to statistical learning theory for Transformers, and the authors themselves flag that the bound requires a norm-constrained (compact) weight space rather than standard unconstrained SGD training, and suffers curse-of-dimensionality in the state-space covering number ($\sim N n^d$).

## 核心贡献

- **双重提升模型下的可实现有限样本保证**：在前作 [1] 的"双重提升"（doubly lifted）测度值框架基础上，将数据集本身也提升为"测度对的测度对"（$\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Z})$，$\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{X})^2$），把训练问题变成一个动态规划可解的有限时域最优控制问题；再对 token 状态空间 $\mathrm{S}$、权重/动作空间 $\mathbb{U}$、测度状态空间 $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{X})$ 做三重量化，得到有限 MDP，并证明了显式的有限样本超额风险界（Theorem 3.3）。
- **从量化模型到原始（基）模型的迁移**：借助值函数关于权重轨迹的 Lipschitz 估计（Lemma 4.1），把量化模型上的泛化界转移回无限维的原始模型，代价是一个随量化精度趋于零的显式近似误差项（Theorem 4.4），并给出了该误差项与量化层级 $\ell, n, m$ 之间必须满足的渐近顺序（Assumption 4.2：$K_r \gg \ell \gg n \to \infty$）。
- **Wasserstein 分布鲁棒控制视角**：证明同一个双重提升框架可等价地表述为一个零和博弈——控制器选择权重轨迹，对手在真实数据律周围的 Wasserstein 球内选择最不利的数据生成律；证明了鲁棒极小元的存在性（Proposition 5.1）。
- **鲁棒性与训练目标的渐近等价**：证明鲁棒目标 $\Gamma$-收敛到训练目标，且鲁棒极小元集合的 Painlevé–Kuratowski 上极限包含于训练极小元集合内（Theorem 5.3），并证明鲁棒极小元与普通训练极小元共享相同的有限样本泛化界（Theorem 5.4、5.5）——即"追求分布鲁棒性"和"追求样本内最优训练"在大样本下渐近一致。
- **完全显式的常数**：不同于多数只给出 $O(\cdot)$ 记号的泛化界工作，本文在 Remark 4.8 中把所有黑箱常数（$C_T, B_T, L$）用 Transformer 架构本身的量（注意力核的 Lipschitz 常数、状态/权重空间直径等）显式展开。

## Key Contributions

- **Explicit finite-sample bound for a fully-discretized, implementable control problem.** Building on the doubly-lifted measure-valued formulation of the companion paper (Akman, Saldi, Yüksel, arXiv:2603.09571), data sets are represented as laws on pairs of empirical input/output measures. Quantizing the particle state space $\mathrm{S}$, the weight/action space $\mathbb{U}$, and the measure-state space $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{X})$ yields a genuinely finite MDP whose optimal policies are realizable via dynamic programming, and for which an excess-risk bound explicit in sample size is proven (Theorem 3.3).
- **Transfer of the bound to the base (infinite-dimensional) model.** A Lipschitz stability estimate for the value function with respect to weight trajectories (Lemma 4.1) is used to carry the quantized-model bound back to the original Transformer, at the cost of an explicit, vanishing quantization/approximation error (Theorem 4.4), under a prescribed asymptotic ordering of the quantization parameters and sample count (Assumption 4.2).
- **Wasserstein distributionally robust control (WDRO) reformulation of generalization.** The same doubly-lifted structure is reinterpreted as a zero-sum game: a controller picks the layer-wise weight trajectory while an adversary perturbs the data-generating law within a Wasserstein ball. Existence of a robust minimizer is proven via Weierstrass' extreme-value theorem for lower-semicontinuous functions on compact sets (Proposition 5.1).
- **Asymptotic equivalence of robust and training minimizers.** The robust objective $\Gamma$-converges to the training objective, and the Painlevé–Kuratowski upper limit of the set of robust minimizers is shown to lie inside the set of training minimizers as the sample size grows (Theorem 5.3); moreover, robust minimizers are shown to inherit the same finite-sample generalization bounds as ordinary training minimizers, both in the quantized regime (Theorem 5.4) and after transfer to the base model (Theorem 5.5).
- **Fully expanded constants.** Unlike many generalization bounds that leave constants as unspecified $O(\cdot)$ terms, Remark 4.8 gives explicit formulas for all blackbox constants ($C_T$, $B_T$, $L$) in terms of intrinsic Transformer quantities (Lipschitz constants of the softmax attention kernel, diameters of the state/weight spaces, etc.).

## Methodology

The paper is a direct sequel to the same authors' companion work "An optimal control approach to Transformer training" (arXiv:2603.09571, 2026), from which it imports the core "doubly lifted" model (Section 2 largely restates results from [1], e.g., Lemma 2.3 = [1, Lemma 15], Lemma 2.4 = [1, Corollary 19]).

1. **Particle-level dynamics.** A Transformer with $T$ layers acting on $N$ tokens is written as coupled particle dynamics: token $i$'s hidden state evolves via a feed-forward map ($W_t, A_t, b_t$) plus a self-attention term (query/key/value matrices $Q_t, K_t, V_t$) that depends on the empirical measure of *all* tokens in the sequence — making the per-token dynamics non-Markovian at the particle level. Positional encodings are appended to the state (augmented state space $\mathbb{X} = \mathrm{PE}_N \times \mathrm{S}$).

2. **Singly and doubly lifted models.** Lifting the ensemble of particles to its empirical measure $\mu_t \in \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{X})$ restores the Markov property (the map $\Phi$ pushes measures forward through one layer). Data sets of $K_r$ input/output sequence pairs are lifted a second time to a law $\mathbb{P}_r \in \mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Z})$ on *pairs of empirical measures* $\mathcal{Z} = \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{X})^2$, turning training into a dynamic-programming recursion (Eq. 2.7) whose value function is $V(P,\underline{U}) = \int P(\mathrm{d}\mu,\mathrm{d}\nu)\, W_{2,\lambda}(\Phi_T(\mu,\underline{U}),\nu)^2$, with $W_{2,\lambda}$ a position-weighted 2-Wasserstein metric that first matches positional encodings for large enough $\lambda$.

3. **Triple quantization.** Since $\mathbb{X}$ and $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{X})$ are infinite-dimensional/continuous, the paper quantizes (i) the token state space $\mathrm{S}$ into an $n$-covering $\mathrm{S}_n$ via a position-sensitive hard quantizer $Q_n$, (ii) the weight space $\mathbb{U}$ into an $m$-net $\mathbb{U}_m$, and (iii) the measure space $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{X}_n)$ into a finite grid of "reconstruction points" $\mathcal{P}^{(\ell)}(\mathbb{X}_n)$ using Reznik's discrete-measure quantization algorithm. This produces the "triply quantized model" with a finite base space $\mathcal{Z}^{\ell,n} = \mathcal{P}^{(\ell)}(\mathbb{X}_n)^2$.

4. **Concentration + Lipschitz stability → finite-sample bound.** A McDiarmid's-inequality argument (Proposition 3.1) bounds deviation of the empirical quantized law from its expectation; a bias-term argument via Cauchy–Schwarz on the variance of empirical measure weights (Lemma 3.2) bounds the expectation itself, yielding an explicit rate $\rho_r^{\ell,n}(\delta) = \frac{D_{\ell,n}\sqrt{|\mathcal{Z}^{\ell,n}|}}{2\sqrt{K_r}} + D_{\ell,n}\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{2K_r}}$. Combined with a Lipschitz estimate of the value function in the data law via Kantorovich–Rubinstein duality (Lemma 2.5), this gives the finite-sample excess-risk bound of Theorem 3.3.

5. **Transfer to the base model + quantization error.** A second Lipschitz estimate in the weight trajectory (Lemma 4.1, relying on compactness of $\mathrm{S}$ and $\mathbb{U}$ and smoothness of the softmax attention kernel) is used to bound the gap between quantized-model and base-model value functions, decomposed into measure-quantization error $\alpha_{\ell,n}$ and action-quantization error $\beta_{\ell,n,m}$ (Lemma 4.3), which vanish as $\ell, n, m \to \infty$ under Assumption 4.2 ($K_r \gg \ell \gg n \to \infty$), producing the main result, Theorem 4.4.

6. **Wasserstein DRO reformulation (Section 5).** The training problem is recast as $\inf_{\underline U} \sup_{P \in A_\rho(\mathbb{P}_r)} V(P,\underline U)$, an ambiguity-set game around the empirical law. Existence of a robust minimizer (Prop 5.1), its $\Gamma$-convergence and Painlevé–Kuratowski set convergence to training minimizers (Theorem 5.3), and matching finite-sample bounds (Theorem 5.4/5.5) are proven with the same Lipschitz/concentration machinery as Sections 3–4.

## Results & Highlights

No quantitative results reported — this is a purely theoretical paper with no experiments, simulations, datasets, or numerical benchmarks. It offers, instead, the following proven theoretical guarantees as its evidence:

- **Theorem 3.3** (quantized-model finite-sample bound): with probability at least $1-\delta$, the excess risk of the empirical-law minimizer over the true-law optimum is bounded by $2L\rho_r^{\ell,n}(\delta)$, where $\rho_r^{\ell,n}(\delta) = \frac{D_{\ell,n}\sqrt{|\mathcal{Z}^{\ell,n}|}}{2\sqrt{K_r}} + D_{\ell,n}\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{2K_r}}$ — an explicit, computable rate in the number of sampled sequences $K_r$.
- **Theorem 4.4** (base-model transfer): the same bound holds for the un-quantized Transformer up to two additional explicit vanishing error terms, $\alpha_{\ell,n}$ (state/measure quantization) and $B_T\sqrt{T}/m$ (action quantization), plus the same $2L\rho_r^{\ell,n}(\delta)$ term.
- **Remark 4.5**: for a fixed quantization configuration, Theorem 3.3's rate is $O(K_r^{-1/2})$ in sample count — the paper explicitly identifies this as "the optimal rate" achievable for this type of bound, and further notes the sample-complexity requirement $K_r \geq C\cdot|\mathcal{Z}^{\ell,n}|\cdot\log(1/\delta)$ (Assumption 4.2) needed for non-vacuousness.
- **Theorem 5.4/5.5**: robust minimizers of the Wasserstein-DRO reformulation satisfy the identical finite-sample bound (up to the same $\alpha_{\ell,n}, \beta_{\ell,n,m}$ terms) as ordinary empirical-risk training minimizers — i.e., no generalization penalty (nor benefit) is incurred by solving the more conservative robust problem instead of plain empirical training, in this asymptotic regime.
- **Self-acknowledged caveat (Conclusion, p.24)**: the authors themselves state the bound "is asymptotic in the prescribed regime $K_r \gg \ell \gg n \to +\infty$, and exhibit[s] curse of dimensionality through the state covering $\mathbb{X}_n \sim N n^d$" — i.e., the covering number needed to make the bound meaningful scales with sequence length $N$ and state dimension $d$, and no concrete numeric instantiation (e.g., for realistic $N, d, T$) is given anywhere in the paper.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The paper's genuine contribution beyond the companion paper [1] is (a) making the earlier qualitative convergence result ([1, Theorem 16]) into an *explicit, finite-sample* rate via a fully discretized MDP, and (b) the Wasserstein-DRO/zero-sum-game reformulation connecting Transformer generalization to distributionally robust control — a reasonably novel bridge, and the paper is careful to specifically differentiate itself from the closest related work, Kan et al. [22] ("Optimal control for transformer architectures," NeurIPS 2026), noting three concrete distinctions: its stability estimate needs only compactness (not convexity of an optimal-transport regularizer), dynamic programming replaces gradient descent, and the adversary perturbs the joint input-output law rather than input/output measures separately.

**Rigor**: The mathematics is careful throughout — every claim (McDiarmid concentration in Prop 3.1, the bias-variance decomposition in Lemma 3.2, $\Gamma$-convergence and Painlevé–Kuratowski set convergence in Theorem 5.3) is proven in full, and Remark 4.8 unpacks all constants explicitly rather than hiding them in $O(\cdot)$ notation — more transparent than most norm-based Transformer generalization bounds.

**Reproducibility**: Not applicable in the conventional empirical sense (no code, no experiments), but the proofs are self-contained modulo results imported from the companion paper — a reader needs arXiv:2603.09571 in hand to verify several of the foundational lemmas (e.g., $L_\Phi = \sqrt{2}L_f$ from [1, Lemma 18], the measure-Lipschitz estimate of [1, Corollary 19]), so this paper cannot be fully checked in isolation.

**Significance / red flags**: The single biggest gap is that Assumption 2.1 requires the weight/action space $\mathbb{U}$ to be *compact* — the authors explicitly acknowledge in the Conclusion that "the compactness assumption on the action space $\mathbb{U}$ corresponds to a norm-constraint training scheme —e.g. an operator norm bound constraint— rather than to unconstrained stochastic gradient descent... and thereby[,] should not be mistaken as a guarantee for an arbitrary Transformer." In other words, **the headline finite-sample guarantee does not apply to a Transformer trained the standard way with unconstrained SGD/Adam** — a fundamental practical limitation stated plainly by the authors rather than buried. Compounding this, Remark 4.7 notes the Lipschitz constants controlling the bound scale *exponentially* in the number of layers $T$, and the state-covering number needed for non-vacuousness scales as $\mathbb{X}_n \sim N n^d$ (curse of dimensionality in sequence length and embedding dimension) — yet the paper never instantiates these constants numerically for any realistic Transformer configuration (e.g., $d\sim 768$, $N\sim 512$, $T\sim 12$), leaving completely open whether the bound is informative or astronomically vacuous in practice. There is also no discussion of how the quantization-based approach compares quantitatively to the covering-number-based bounds of prior norm-based Transformer generalization work it cites (e.g., [13], [25], [36]) — only a qualitative "our approach differs" statement in the related work section.

## Strengths

- Rigorous, fully-proven generalization theory built on stochastic-control/MDP machinery rather than ad hoc norm-counting, with every intermediate lemma proved and all constants made explicit (Remark 4.8).
- Elegant unification: shows that the finite-sample-bound question (Sections 3–4) and the Wasserstein-distributionally-robust-control question (Section 5) are asymptotically the same problem, with matching finite-sample guarantees for both — a nontrivial and clean structural result (Theorems 5.3–5.5).
- Unusually forthright about its own limitations: the Conclusion explicitly states the norm-constrained-training caveat and the curse-of-dimensionality issue rather than glossing over them.
- Careful, specific positioning against the single closest prior work (Kan et al. [22]), naming exact technical differences rather than a generic "we differ" claim.

## Limitations

- Zero empirical validation: no experiments, simulations, or numerical instantiation of the bound for any concrete Transformer configuration.
- The core compactness assumption on the weight space $\mathbb{U}$ models norm-constrained training (e.g., operator-norm-bounded weights), not standard unconstrained SGD/Adam training used in practice — acknowledged by the authors as a real gap.
- Lipschitz constants controlling the bound grow exponentially in depth $T$ (Remark 4.7), and the state covering needed scales as $N n^d$ (curse of dimensionality in sequence length $N$ and token dimension $d$) — both flagged by the authors but not mitigated or quantified.
- Heavy reliance on results imported wholesale from the companion paper (arXiv:2603.09571); this paper cannot be independently verified without that reference.
- No quantitative or even qualitative sense of how tight the bound is relative to any known lower bound, nor any numeric example of what sample size $K_r$ would actually be required for a realistic model.

## Relevance

这篇论文用随机控制、动态规划和最优传输的语言，为 Transformer 训练的泛化能力给出了少见的严格有限样本证明，并把它和 Wasserstein 分布鲁棒优化联系起来，对关注 LLM 理论基础、样本复杂度界或 DRO 与深度学习交叉领域的研究者有参考价值；但由于假设权重空间紧致（对应范数约束训练而非标准 SGD）、缺乏任何数值实验、且常数随层数指数增长，其对实际训练 Transformer 的指导意义目前仍非常有限。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- What do the fully-expanded constants ($C_T$, $B_T$, $L$, $\rho_r^{\ell,n}$) evaluate to numerically for a realistic Transformer (e.g., $d \sim 768$, $N \sim 512$, $T \sim 12$), and is the resulting sample-complexity requirement $K_r$ informative or effectively vacuous?
- Can the exponential-in-depth scaling of the Lipschitz constants (Remark 4.7) be tightened using architectural features known to stabilize deep networks (residual connections, layer normalization), which the base particle dynamics (Eq. 2.1) do not appear to include?
- Does the norm-constrained/compact-weight-space assumption (Assumption 2.1) have any quantitative relationship to weight decay or gradient clipping as practiced in standard training, such that the bound could be adapted to hold with high probability for ordinary SGD/Adam-trained weights?
- Can the Wasserstein-DRO reformulation of Section 5 be turned into a practical robust-training algorithm, and if so, would it show measurable empirical benefits (e.g., robustness to distribution shift) on standard NLP benchmarks — something this purely theoretical paper does not attempt?
- How does this quantization-based bound compare numerically (not just qualitatively) to the covering-number-based Transformer generalization bounds it cites ([13], [25], [36]) or to the optimal-transport-regularization approach of Kan et al. [22]?
