Representation Engineering · Benchmark Infrastructure

RepBench compiles 353 real benchmarks into an audited probe corpus — training-free diff-in-means beats supervised probing on average, not universally.

一句话总结：RepBench 把 13,427 篇基准论文压成 182 类能力分类体系，再从 353 个公开基准中抽取 46,149 条经审计探针文本，在跨基准留出（LOBO）协议下发现均值-差读出平均 AUC 最高（0.778），但逻辑回归在单个"能力×模型"格子上赢得最多（38%）。

Key Metric

0.778

Grand-mean AUC of diff-mean, the best-scoring readout across all 1,128 capability×model cells — yet logistic regression wins more individual cells (38% vs. diff-mean's 30%).

Why This Matters

Gives interpretability researchers a reusable, non-synthetic benchmark corpus for testing probing methods — and warns that benchmark categories don't map onto models' own internal capability structure.

Critical Edge

The paper only measures whether capability directions are readable (AUC) — it reports no causal or steering-efficacy results, despite steering being named a co-equal goal in its own abstract.

Approach

Methodology

Crawl 13,427 papers→ Dedup to 9,576 concepts→ Cluster (182 / 13 families)→ Audit & probe (94 capabilities)→ LOBO score
  • Taxonomy construction. 13,427 benchmark papers yield 14,896 stated capability mentions; Qwen3-Embedding-8B + 15-NN cosine graph dedup (≥0.90 similarity) collapses these to 9,576 concepts, then weighted k-means (k=200) plus an LLM audit/merge pass (≥0.80 cosine) produces the final 182 clusters across 13 families.
  • Text-probeable subset. 94 of 182 clusters qualify by excluding families that need image/video/tool-use input, or where multimodal, tool-dependence, or long-horizon mentions dominate — leaving 88 clusters (31 multimodal-grounding + 23 planning/tool-use + others) out of scope.
  • Audited corpus. Candidate public datasets are matched per capability via LLM judgment, then human-audited: an ensemble-consistency diagnostic flags ambiguous mappings, and 25 of 378 dataset→capability mappings (6.6%) are removed after independent human review; a targeted recrawl restores the ≥2-benchmark-per-capability invariant.
  • Benchmark-balanced pooling (Eq. 1). Vectors are averaged within each source benchmark first, then weighted equally across sources — so no single large dataset dominates a capability's direction.
  • LOBO protocol. Each benchmark is held out in turn; the probe direction is fit on the remaining benchmarks + disjoint negatives, scored by AUC on the held-out positives, and summarized as the per-fold median — a deliberately harder bar than random within-capability splits, which the authors note saturate near AUC 1.0.

  • Taxonomy mismatch. Benchmark-pooled capability vectors show an interior clustering optimum at k=4–15 on every one of the 12 models — but agreement with the human 13-family taxonomy is only ARI 0.05–0.13, i.e. models organize capabilities differently from how the field's own literature does.
  • Readability is capability-dependent. 30 of 94 capabilities score diff-mean AUC ≥0.8 on every model (e.g. specification-to-code synthesis 0.99); 4 score <0.65 on every model (contamination-robust generalization 0.35, argumentative reasoning & persuasion 0.26).
  • Cross-model consistency. Per-capability AUC profiles correlate strongly across the 12 models (pairwise Spearman 0.62–0.92, median 0.82) — readability looks like a property of the data/construct, not one checkpoint's quirk.

Strengths

  • 353 datasets / 46,149 texts / 94 capabilities, built via a transparent, repeatable crawl → cluster → audit → recrawl loop, not one-off manual curation.
  • Enforced ≥2-independent-benchmark-per-capability invariant directly targets the single-dataset-fingerprint problem that inflates random-split evaluations to near-perfect AUC.
  • Transparent about its own failures: reports exactly which 25/378 mappings were removed, with split human vote counts, rather than hiding disagreement.
  • LOBO plus dual aggregate views (grand mean vs. per-cell win share) surfaces a genuinely informative disagreement instead of declaring one winner.
  • External validity spot-check (Table 2) cross-references RepBench's cluster AUCs against independently published task-specific AUROC numbers.

Limitations

  • Text-only scope excludes 88 of 182 taxonomy clusters — all multimodal-grounding (31) and planning/tool-use (23) — deferred to future work.
  • SAE comparison covers only one model / one aligned layer set (Gemma-2-9B-IT); authors state it is not generalizable to the other 11 checkpoints.
  • No causal intervention or steering-efficacy evaluation — only direction readability is measured, not whether directions actually steer generation.
  • J-Lens verbalizer choice swings mean AUC by up to 5.1 points on a single model (Qwen3-32B), even though the effect washes out on average.
  • Model pool is entirely open-weight (0.6B–32B, plus one ~275B MoE base); no frontier proprietary models (GPT, Claude, Gemini) are evaluated.

Open Threads

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Do the same benchmark-grounded directions that read capabilities well (diff-mean, LR) also steer generation effectively — or does readability decouple from steerability?
  2. Would SAE dictionaries aligned to newer Gemma-4 / Qwen3 checkpoints keep the sparse single-feature readout as the weakest method, or is that conclusion tied to Gemma Scope's specific recipe?
  3. Does the low ARI (≈0.1) reflect models organizing capabilities along axes the benchmark literature hasn't named yet, or noise from probing only 94 of 182 capabilities at fixed depths?
  4. Would extending the corpus to multimodal-grounding and planning/tool-use clusters preserve the "diff-mean wins on average, LR wins more cells" pattern once inputs require multi-step context?
  5. How sensitive is the 6.6% audit-removal rate to the specific LLM judge used for the semantic consistency diagnostic?
