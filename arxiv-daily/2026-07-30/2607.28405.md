> **一句话总结：** 针对"世界动作模型"(WAM，联合预测视频和动作的扩散模型)的闭环、多路径特性，提出QuantWAMs后训练量化框架，通过"仅在坐标兼容模块间共享异常值校准"、"用视频-动作联合梯度做协同训练显著性评分"和"用真实rollout状态做固定干预回放审计去噪步保护"三项技术，在W4A4主导配置下把RoboTwin 2.0和LIBERO上的仿真成功率均值与FP16相比仅拉开0.2-0.7个百分点，同时把峰值权重+激活显存降到FP16的约29%，块级推理提速1.4-1.6倍。

> **TL;DR:** QuantWAMs is a post-training quantization (PTQ) framework tailored to World Action Models (WAMs) that jointly predict video and action via diffusion. It fixes three mismatches in standard PTQ (structural pooling scope, distributional calibration, and single-stream objectives) with shared-basis outlier calibration, co-training-objective (joint video-action Fisher) saliency, and fixed-intervention real-rollout auditing for denoising-step protection. On RoboTwin 2.0 and LIBERO with two WAM backbones (Fast-WAM, LingBot-VA), W4A4 QuantWAMs stays within 0.2-0.7 points of FP16 (e.g., LIBERO average 97.4±0.2 vs. FP16 97.6±0.2 on Fast-WAM), while the much-used PTQ baselines Atom and SVDQuant collapse to 65.9-82.1% at the same bit-width — and real-robot trials on an AgiBot G2 show a larger 6.6-point gap (56.7% vs. 63.3% FP16) that the paper is upfront about not being powered to prove non-inferiority.

## 核心贡献

- **提出WAM量化的三个"校准语境"错配问题**：结构错配（跨模块统计池化的坐标系不一致）、分布错配（校准分布未反映闭环部署下真实可达状态）、目标错配（用单流开环目标打分而非联合视频-动作目标），并给出统一的"校准语境"(结构范围/状态分布/评分目标三元组)理论框架。
- **共享基异常值校准 (Shared-Basis Outlier Calibration)**：仅在"坐标兼容"的模块间共享激活异常通道统计，推导出何时应该做统计池化(Proposition 1的池化交叉点定理)，并用协方差感知的bootstrap自动判断样本量是否足以支持池化。
- **协同训练目标显著性 (Co-Training-Objective Saliency)**：从视频与动作损失的联合梯度计算经验Fisher信息，在"校准稳定"的层粒度上分配权重比特预算，理论上证明了element级分配比layer级更不稳定。
- **固定干预真实Rollout审计 (Fixed-Intervention Rollout Auditing)**：用FP16模型生成的真实闭环rollout状态、在统一(未保护)干预下重放，构造去噪步敏感度剖面，据此修正哪些去噪步该被高精度保护，而不改变精度预算总量。
- 在两个已发布的WAM实现(Fast-WAM、LingBot-VA)、三个测试平台(RoboTwin 2.0、LIBERO、AgiBot G2真实机器人)上系统评测，并提供了受控消融证明三个组件各自的增量贡献。

## Key Contributions

- Formulates WAM post-training quantization around three deployment-time mismatches — **structural** (coordinate-incompatible pooling), **distributional** (calibration data not reflecting closed-loop reachable states), and **objective** (single-stream scoring instead of the joint video-action training objective) — that make standard PTQ methods (GPTQ, SmoothQuant, Atom, SVDQuant) poorly suited to WAMs.
- **Shared-Basis Outlier Calibration**: pools activation-outlier statistics only across "coordinate-admissible" modules (same quantizer-input coordinate under a shared Hadamard rotation), with a formal pooling-crossover proposition (Proposition 1) giving the sample size N* below which pooling reduces mask-recovery risk, verified via trajectory-level bootstrap.
- **Co-Training-Objective Saliency**: computes an empirical-Fisher-based layer saliency score from the *joint* video+action gradient (rather than post-hoc fusing two single-objective Fisher scores), used to allocate GPTQ mixed-precision weight bits at layer granularity — shown empirically to be more stable than element- or column-level allocation.
- **Fixed-Intervention Rollout Auditing**: replays real (not synthetic/open-loop) FP16 closed-loop rollout states under a fixed, unprotected intervention to build a denoising-step sensitivity profile, then uses it to *audit and repair* (not re-derive) an existing denoising-step protection schedule at a fixed precision budget.
- Demonstrates the framework on two released WAM architectures with different coupling patterns (Fast-WAM's dual-stream Mixture-of-Transformers vs. LingBot-VA's shared-backbone DiT), across RoboTwin 2.0, four LIBERO suites, and real-robot manipulation on AgiBot G2, with cumulative-ablation and matched-budget-baseline controls isolating each component's contribution.

## Methodology

QuantWAMs is a post-training quantization (PTQ) pipeline layered on top of an existing mixed-precision quantizer (Atom-style channel-offloading + GPTQ-style weight compensation), applied to a pretrained WAM (a video diffusion model coupled with a diffusion action expert, either dual-stream MoT as in Fast-WAM or shared-backbone DiT as in LingBot-VA) without any weight updates.

1. **Shared-Basis Outlier Calibration (Section 3.1)**: For each "context" (a module, execution mode, and depth), per-channel squared-activation energy statistics are collected after a common Hadamard rotation + diagonal smoothing. A context group can only share a literal Top-K outlier-channel mask if the members are *coordinate-admissible* — i.e., they index the same quantizer-input coordinate under one fixed ordered basis (verified case-by-case for Fast-WAM's shared value pathway vs. private residual streams, and LingBot-VA's shared post-fusion Linear columns). Under a random-effects working model with cross-member heterogeneity τ_c² and within-member sampling variance σ_c², Proposition 1 gives a closed-form crossover sample size N*_c = σ_c²/τ_c² below which pooling statistics across members lowers mask-recovery risk versus using each member's own (noisy, low-N) statistics; a covariance-aware bootstrap over the 32-trajectory calibration set decides, per admissible group, whether to pool.
2. **Co-Training-Objective Saliency (Section 3.2)**: For each candidate Linear layer, an empirical-Fisher-like matrix G_L is computed from the *joint* gradient of the video+action co-training loss (λ_v·grad_v + λ_a·grad_a), which differs from naively summing/fusing the two single-objective Fisher matrices by an explicit cross-term (Eq. 13) capturing coordinatewise video-action gradient alignment. Combined with a Kronecker-factored curvature approximation (à la Martens & Grosse 2015) and activation second moments Σ_L, this yields a per-layer "benefit of upgrading precision" score B_L, and a knapsack-style layer selection upgrades the top 20% of candidate Linears (by layer-level score, since finer element/column-level rankings are shown to be unstable under finite calibration data) to higher weight precision under a fixed bit budget.
3. **Fixed-Intervention Rollout Auditing (Section 3.3)**: Recognizing that in WAMs, quantization error at an early denoising/control step propagates into later observations (unlike in generic diffusion PTQ where each forward pass is scored independently), the method records 32 real FP16 closed-loop rollouts (D_prof), then for every recorded state replays the *same* unprotected intervention (q_0, all-low-precision) from a restored FP16 snapshot to build a per-timestep model-error profile S^replay_ref(t). This profile is used only to *audit* an existing K-step protection schedule: it proposes a candidate replacement schedule (Top-K steps by profile value) and validates it via accept/reject comparison on a disjoint schedule-validation set D_val before freezing it for final testing — so it revises *which* denoising steps are protected without changing the total precision budget.

Backend: hybrid precision on NVIDIA SM120 Blackwell GPUs (NVFP4 W4A4 kernels for candidate layers, FP8 W8A8 for upgraded layers, BF16 bypass for the top 2% outlier channels and preserved outlier columns). Data separation across four disjoint trajectory sets (calibration D_cal, profiling D_prof, schedule-validation D_val, final test D_test) is used per architecture-benchmark pair, with results averaged over 3 independent protocol seeds.

## Results & Highlights

- **RoboTwin 2.0 (Fast-WAM, Table 1)**, W4A4-dominant configuration: QuantWAMs average success 91.7±0.3% vs. **FP16 91.9±0.3%** (Δ=0.2pp), while the matched-precision-budget baseline **Atom\*** (a diffusion-model PTQ method) drops to 77.2±0.7% and **SVDQuant\*** drops to 65.9±0.7% at the same nominal W4A4 budget — a >14-26 point gap versus QuantWAMs.
- **LIBERO (Fast-WAM, Table 1)** average across Goal/Spatial/Object/Long suites: QuantWAMs 97.4±0.2% vs. **FP16 97.6±0.2%** (Δ=0.2pp); **SmoothQuant (W8A8)** reaches only 96.4±0.3% despite using twice the activation bit-width, and **Atom\* (W4A4)** reaches only 82.1±0.5% — 15.3 points below QuantWAMs at the same bit-width.
- **LingBot-VA (Table 2)**: RoboTwin 2.0 average QuantWAMs 91.6±0.3% vs. **FP16 92.3±0.3%** (Δ=0.7pp, the largest gap reported); LIBERO-Long QuantWAMs 98.0±0.3% vs. **FP16 98.5±0.2%** (Δ=0.5pp), while **Atom\*** on LIBERO-Long reaches only 81.5±0.7% — a 16.5-point gap.
- **Efficiency**: for the targeted video+action DiT blocks, peak weight-plus-activation memory falls to **~29% of FP16** (e.g., 4.2GB vs. 14.4GB on Fast-WAM), with **1.4-1.6× block-level latency speedup** — explicitly scoped as block-level, not end-to-end control-loop, measurements.
- **Real-robot (AgiBot G2, Table 6)**, 10 trials/task: **FP16** succeeds in 19/30 trials overall (63.3%: 8/10 pickup-apple, 6/10 stack-blocks, 5/10 fold-towel); **QuantWAMs (W4A4)** succeeds in 17/30 (56.7%: 8/10, 5/10, 4/10) with 1.4× speedup; the baseline **Atom\* (W4A4)** succeeds in only 12/30 (40.0%). The 6.6-point real-robot gap to FP16 is notably larger than the 0.2-0.7 point simulation gaps.
- **Cumulative ablation (Table 3, LIBERO-Long)**: Base (per-context masks + post-hoc fusion + synthetic schedule) 80.8±0.7% (Fast-WAM) / 80.2±0.9% (LingBot-VA) → +Shared-Basis 89.1±0.6%/89.6±0.8% → +Joint saliency 90.9±0.7%/91.8±0.8% → +Fixed-Intervention Replay (full QuantWAMs) **95.0±0.4%/98.0±0.3%**, showing each component adds several points.
- **Saliency-source ablation (Table 5)**: Joint co-training saliency (95.0±0.4%/98.0±0.3%) beats video-only (85.4±0.7%/83.1±0.8%), action-only (87.1±0.6%/86.5±0.7%), and post-hoc fusion of the two single-objective scores (91.4±0.5%/92.8±0.6%) — a 3.6-5.2 point gain over fusion at the matched precision budget.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty**: The core insight — that PTQ for WAMs must respect a "calibration context" (structural pooling scope, deployment state distribution, and joint training objective) rather than reusing homogeneous-model PTQ heuristics — is well-motivated and, as far as the related work section shows, not previously formalized for this architecture class. The pooling-crossover proposition (Proposition 1) is a genuine, if narrow, theoretical contribution with an explicit and honestly-stated caveat: "this optimality is restricted to the stated surrogate and does not imply optimal closed-loop performance."

**Rigor**: The experimental design is unusually careful about data hygiene — four disjoint trajectory roles (calibration/profiling/validation/test), 3 independent protocol seeds, and matched-precision-budget baseline variants (SVDQuant\*, Atom\*) that isolate whether QuantWAMs' gains come merely from a better bit-allocation budget or from the proposed calibration logic itself (the paper shows matched-budget variants still substantially underperform, supporting the latter). The ablations (Tables 3-5) are genuinely incremental and controlled.

**Reproducibility**: Method depends on an original video-action co-training objective and a backward pass through the pretrained WAM (unlike conventional weight-only/activation-only PTQ baselines, which the paper flags as an asymmetry: "the conventional PTQ baselines do not receive those signals" — a somewhat unequal comparison since QuantWAMs gets extra label/gradient information the baselines don't). Backend results are tied to a specific SM120 Blackwell GPU and NVFP4/FP8 kernel stack, limiting portability of the efficiency numbers.

**Significance / red flags**: The headline claim ("0.2-0.7pp from FP16") is accurate for simulation but the real-robot gap (63.3% → 56.7%, a 6.6-point average drop, with per-task drops of 0/10, 1/10, 1/10 out of 10 trials) is considerably larger and the paper is commendably explicit that "with ten trials per task, these results... do not support an equivalence or non-inferiority claim relative to FP16" — a paper-specific, self-flagged limitation rather than a generic hedge. Also notable: the paper cites but never empirically compares against other recent VLA/embodied-specific quantization methods (QuantVLA, Qvla) that target the same application space, so the baseline set (GPTQ, SmoothQuant, Atom, SVDQuant) is drawn entirely from generic LLM/diffusion PTQ rather than from competing embodied-policy quantization work, leaving open how QuantWAMs compares to the most directly relevant prior art.

## Strengths

- Precisely identifies and formalizes three concrete failure modes (structural/distributional/objective mismatch) of applying off-the-shelf PTQ to WAMs, rather than offering a generic "quantize better" claim.
- Strong experimental hygiene: disjoint calibration/profiling/validation/test trajectory splits, 3 seeds, and matched-precision-budget ablation baselines (SVDQuant\*, Atom\*) that meaningfully isolate the source of gains.
- Validated on two architecturally different WAMs (dual-stream MoT vs. shared-backbone DiT) and on a real robot (AgiBot G2), not just synthetic benchmarks.
- Honest, explicit statements of scope throughout (e.g., "these comparisons are not formal non-inferiority or equivalence tests," "profile magnitudes are not interpreted as marginal closed-loop gains").

## Limitations

- Optimizes local surrogates (energy recovery, Fisher-based saliency, replay profiles) rather than directly optimizing closed-loop task success; the paper explicitly states its own ablations "do not, by themselves, establish causal attribution."
- Requires white-box access: task labels, one backward pass through the pretrained WAM, FP16 closed-loop rollouts for profiling, and a held-out validation split for schedule freezing — a heavier calibration pipeline than plug-and-play PTQ baselines.
- Evaluated on only two WAM architectures, with benchmark-specific (not cross-task or cross-benchmark transferable) calibration, on one hardware backend (Blackwell), and only at the block level (not end-to-end control-loop latency/memory).
- Real-robot validation is a 10-trials-per-task study (30 trials total), explicitly acknowledged as underpowered for a rigorous FP16 comparison, and shows a larger degradation than simulation results.
- No empirical comparison to other embodied/VLA-specific quantization methods (e.g., QuantVLA, Qvla) cited in related work, only to generic LLM/diffusion PTQ baselines.

## Relevance

该工作针对具身智能中日益重要的"世界动作模型"扩散策略提供了一套专门的后训练量化方案，量化损失小且理论有一定支撑，对关注机器人策略高效部署、扩散模型压缩或多模态(视频+动作)联合建模量化的研究方向具有较高参考价值；但真实机器人实验样本量较小，实际部署收益需谨慎看待。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How would QuantWAMs' co-training-objective saliency and shared-basis pooling generalize to WAMs with more than two coupled modalities (e.g., adding language or proprioception streams), where "coordinate-admissibility" across modules may be even harder to establish?
- Would the fixed-intervention rollout auditing approach benefit from (or require) online/continual re-calibration as the deployed policy encounters out-of-distribution states not covered by the 32 FP16 profiling rollouts?
- How does QuantWAMs compare quantitatively — not just qualitatively via citation — to embodied-specific quantization baselines like QuantVLA or Qvla on the same benchmarks?
- What is the actual end-to-end (not block-level) control-loop latency and memory improvement once VAE, embedding, and projection layers are included, and does the 1.4-1.6× block speedup translate into meaningfully faster real-world control frequency?
- Could the larger real-robot performance gap (6.6pp) versus the simulation gap (0.2-0.7pp) be closed with more real-world profiling trajectories, or does it reflect a more fundamental sim-to-real generalization limit of the calibration approach?
