> **一句话总结：** 都灵大学团队提出 NNS（Neural Network Simplification），用一个计算廉价的逆 Fisher 判据（IFC = 类内方差/类间方差）在训练过程中逐层在线监测，一旦某层从"特征提取"转为"分类压缩"的信号稳定下来，就把后面属于"隧道"的层砍掉、换成一个轻量分类头继续训练；在 MLP/VGG/ResNet 上于 Fashion-MNIST、CIFAR-10/100、CUB 数据集上实现 35.2%–94.2% 的参数削减（如 VGG11 在 CIFAR-10 上削减 94.2% 参数后精度 85.04% 还略高于全模型的 84.96%），但在 CUB 上用较浅的 ResNet10 做激进裁剪时精度从 36.02% 掉到 25.04%，暴露出该方法在细粒度、浅骨干场景下的失效边界。

> **TL;DR:** Building on Neural Collapse (NC) and the Tunnel Effect (TE), the authors introduce NNS, a training-time pipeline that monitors the Inverse Fisher Criterion (IFC = within-class/between-class variance) per layer on a small "tunnel set" (5% of training data) after every epoch; once each layer's IFC trend (increasing = extractor-like, decreasing = classifier-like) stabilizes for a 15-epoch patience window, the trailing "tunnel" layers are removed and replaced with a lightweight head (linear probe, fixed-ETF, or declarative-ETF) for the remainder of training. Across MLP10/12, ResNet10/18/34, and VGG11/16 on Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and CUB-200-2011, NNS with a linear-probe head (NNS_LP) achieves the best average rank (1.86) in a 21-configuration critical-difference analysis, beating full training (rank 2.86) and the EB-LTH pruning baseline, while cutting parameters by 35.2%–94.2% (e.g., 94.2% on VGG11/CIFAR-10 with accuracy 85.04±0.30 vs. 84.96±0.30 for full training; 82.07% on VGG16/CIFAR-100 with 62.76±0.56 vs. 61.02±0.13). The method degrades sharply on the hardest configuration tested — ResNet10 on the fine-grained 200-class CUB dataset, where a 74.36% parameter cut drops accuracy from 36.02±1.12 (full training) to 25.04±2.38.

## 核心贡献

- 提出 NNS（Neural Network Simplification）：把 Neural Collapse（NC）的几何坍缩视角和 Tunnel Effect（TE）的"提取器-隧道"分层视角结合起来，在训练过程中动态地砍掉后段"隧道"层，而不是像传统剪枝那样先训练到收敛再剪枝重训。
- 采用逆 Fisher 判据（Inverse Fisher Criterion, IFC = σ_W/σ_B，类内方差与类间方差之比）作为在线、计算高效的 NC1（变异性坍缩）代理指标：只需在固定的"隧道集"（占训练集 5%、按类别平衡采样）上对每层计算一次，而不需要昂贵的全协方差 NC1 计算或线性探针后验分析。
- 提出一套在线的层级标注与分裂点检测规则：比较每层当前 IFC 与其第一个 epoch 时的值，符号为正记为"提取器型"，为负记为"分类器型"；标签在 15 个 epoch 的耐心窗口内保持不变后即认定分裂区已稳定，遇到模糊区域时保守地偏向更深的分裂点。
- 通过 7 种架构（MLP10/12、ResNet10/18/34、VGG11/16）× 4 个数据集（Fashion-MNIST、CIFAR-10、CIFAR-100、CUB-200-2011）共 21 个配置的系统实验，结合临界差异图（critical-difference diagram）证明 NNS 配合线性探针头（NNS_LP）在保持与全训练相当精度的同时，实现 35.2%–94.2% 的参数削减，整体平均排名（1.86）优于全训练（2.86）和 EB-LTH 剪枝基线。

## Key Contributions

- Introduces NNS, a training-time framework that fuses the Neural Collapse (NC) geometric-collapse perspective with the Tunnel Effect (TE) extractor/tunnel decomposition to dynamically truncate the trailing "tunnel" layers of an over-parameterized network during training, rather than pruning post-hoc after full convergence (as in progressive pruning/retraining) or fixing a pruning budget in advance.
- Adopts the Inverse Fisher Criterion (IFC = σ_W/σ_B, the ratio of within-class to between-class variance, i.e. the inverse of Fisher's linear discriminant criterion) as a stable, computationally cheap, per-layer, per-epoch proxy for the NC1 variability-collapse property, evaluated only on a fixed 5%, class-balanced "tunnel set" rather than the full training set.
- Proposes an online layer-labeling and split-detection rule: label each layer "extractor-like" or "classifier-like" by the sign of the change in its IFC relative to its value after epoch 1; once labels stabilize for a 15-epoch patience window, identify the transition region, conservatively favor the deeper split when ambiguous, remove the classifier-like tail, attach a lightweight head (linear probe, fixed-ETF projector à la Rangamani et al. 2023, or declarative-ETF à la Markou et al. 2024), and resume training on the reduced model.
- Validates the approach across 21 model-dataset configurations (MLP10/12, ResNet10/18/34, VGG11/16 on Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, CUB-200-2011), showing via Nemenyi post-hoc critical-difference analysis that NNS with a linear-probe head is statistically competitive with full training while cutting parameters by 35.2%–94.2%, and additionally shows NNS can be combined with the EB-LTH lottery-ticket pruning method for further FLOPs reduction.

## Methodology

NNS operationalizes two prior lines of work: Neural Collapse (NC), which describes the geometric collapse of penultimate-layer features and classifier weights to a simplex Equiangular Tight Frame (ETF) at the terminal phase of training, and the Tunnel Effect (TE, Masarczyk et al. 2023), which shows empirically that only an early subset of layers ("the extractor") builds a linearly separable representation while later layers ("the tunnel") merely compress it with little effect on accuracy. TE's original split criterion requires training the full network to convergence and attaching a linear probe to every layer post hoc — expensive and only available after the fact. NNS's central technical contribution is to replace that post-hoc TE criterion with an *online* NC-inspired signal that can be tracked during training.

The representation metric used is the Inverse Fisher Criterion, IFC = Tr(Σ_W)/Tr(Σ_B), the ratio between within-class covariance trace and between-class covariance trace, computed on a fixed 5%-of-training-data "tunnel set" (sampled to preserve the class distribution) after every epoch, for every layer (or, for ResNets, every residual block, since individual layers inside a block are not natural splitting units). The authors compared IFC against several alternative candidate metrics in Appendix B — COS (deviation of class-mean angles from the ideal ETF, an NC2 proxy), Intrinsic Dimension (ID, Ansuini et al. 2019), Neural Collapse Clustering (NCC, Galanti et al. 2022, an NC1 proxy), and Numerical Rank (NR, an eigenvalue-thresholding rank estimate) — and found only NCC and IFC show a consistent, interpretable pattern (early layers' metric increases, tracking growing class separability; later layers' metric decreases, tracking variability collapse), with IFC preferred for its numerical stability and lower computational cost.

The NNS pipeline (Figure 1) operates in two stages:
1. **Stage 1 (monitor):** Train the full over-parameterized network normally; after each epoch, evaluate IFC on the tunnel set for every layer; compare each layer's current IFC to its value after the first epoch and label it extractor-like (+), classifier-like (−), or transitional (±) by the sign of the change (first and last layers are fixed as extractor/classifier by construction). Once every layer's label has remained unchanged for a 15-epoch patience window, the transition region is considered stable; when adjacent layers remain ambiguous, the method conservatively favors the slightly deeper split (better to retain one extra layer than over-truncate).
2. **Stage 2 (simplify):** Discard all classifier-like (and, per the conservative rule, transitional) layers after the identified split point, attach a new lightweight classification head, and resume training the reduced model on the remaining epochs.

Three head designs are compared: a plain fully-connected layer after flattening (NNS_LP, "linear probing"), a fixed simplex-ETF projector that is not updated by gradient descent (NNS_FIX, following Rangamani et al. 2023), and a declarative-ETF head optimized via Riemannian optimization toward the nearest ETF (NNS_DCL, following Markou et al. 2024, using a two-layer head with an initial projection to keep the Riemannian step tractable for large class counts). Appendix D additionally studies average-pooling and channel-wise-averaging variants of the head to control parameter blow-up when splitting at high-dimensional intermediate feature maps (relevant for CUB's 200 classes).

NNS is compared against full training and against EB-LTH (Early-Bird Lottery Ticket Hypothesis, You et al. 2020) at 30%/50%/70% pruning levels — a channel-pruning method that keeps the original depth but sparsifies weights, identified via Hamming-distance stabilization of per-epoch masks, without re-initialization after pruning. Hyperparameters: SGD with a 5-epoch warmup, 160 epochs for ResNets/VGGs, 300 epochs for MLPs, step-decay learning-rate schedules (architecture-specific), fixed across all experiments rather than tuned per split criterion. Standard deviations are reported over 5 independent runs.

## Results & Highlights

- **Parameter reduction range (Table 1):** Across all 21 model-dataset configurations, NNS's identified split removes between 35.2% (ResNet18/CIFAR-100) and 94.2% (VGG16/CIFAR-10) of the original parameters, using only 7.9%–52% of the total training epochs before the split occurs (e.g., ResNet10/CIFAR-10 splits at epoch 16.0±0.0, layer 4.0, for 71.7% reduction using only 10% of total training epochs).
- **CIFAR-10, VGG11 (Table 6):** NNS_LP reaches 85.04±0.30% accuracy vs. 84.96±0.30% for full training, while removing 94.2% of parameters (the single largest reduction reported) — the paper's headline result that simplification can match or slightly exceed full-model accuracy at a fraction of the size.
- **CIFAR-100, VGG16 (Table 7):** NNS_LP achieves 62.76±0.56% vs. 61.02±0.13% for full training, an 82.07% parameter reduction, again with NNS_LP outperforming the full model.
- **Critical-difference analysis (Figure 6):** Over the 21 configurations, NNS_LP achieves the best average Nemenyi rank (1.86), ahead of full training (2.86), while NNS_FIX is the weakest variant (average rank 6.48) and forms a statistically indistinguishable weak group with EB-LTH_70; NNS_LP, full training, EB-LTH_30, and NNS_DCL form one non-significant clique, showing NNS_LP is statistically competitive with full training despite the parameter cuts.
- **Failure case (CUB-200-2011, Table 8):** On the hardest benchmark (200 fine-grained bird classes, only 5,994 training images), the shallow ResNet10 backbone under NNS_LP drops from 36.02±1.12% (full training) to 25.04±2.38% at a 74.36% parameter reduction — a substantial degradation the authors attribute to the combination of a shallow backbone, fine-grained task, and overly aggressive split depth (layer 4.0 out of only ~5-6 residual blocks). Deeper ResNet variants on CUB (ResNet18, ResNet34) remain competitive under NNS_LP (49.17±0.91 vs. 48.70±0.97 full training for ResNet18; 50.94±2.87 vs. 47.75±0.78 for ResNet34, actually exceeding full training).
- **Classification-head ablation (Table 4, ResNet10/CIFAR-100):** Splitting at layer 2 with a plain fully-connected head yields only 24.25±0.99% accuracy and 6.6M parameters, whereas an average-pooling + FC head at the same split point yields 45.35±0.64% accuracy with only 82,240 parameters (2.7% of the naive head's parameter count) — demonstrating that head design, not just split depth, materially affects both accuracy and compactness.
- **FLOPs trade-off (Tables 9-12):** EB-LTH generally achieves lower training FLOPs than NNS at comparable parameter-reduction levels, because EB-LTH prunes channels throughout the network (reducing backward-pass cost at every layer) whereas NNS only shortens the network's depth after the split epoch. A preliminary combination of NNS + EB-LTH_30 on ResNet10/CIFAR-10 and ResNet18/CIFAR-100 achieved 0.87 and 0.55 accuracy respectively while further lowering FLOPs versus either method alone — reported as a promising but only lightly explored direction.
- **Split-point determinants (Appendix C):** Varying the tunnel-set size from 1% to 10% has negligible effect on the identified split layer (Table 2), but varying only the optimization seed (with a fixed tunnel set) can shift the identified split layer by one layer forward or backward (Table 3) — indicating the split point is driven more by the optimization trajectory than by tunnel-set sampling.

No quantitative results reported: N/A — the paper reports extensive quantitative results throughout (accuracy tables, parameter-reduction percentages, FLOPs tables).

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty:** The core idea — using an online, per-epoch, per-layer NC1-style proxy (IFC) to detect the extractor/tunnel split during training, rather than after convergence — is a genuine and reasonably clean synthesis of two separate active research threads (Neural Collapse and the Tunnel Effect). The authors are explicit and honest that IFC itself is not new (it was previously used by Hui et al. 2022 and Súkeník et al. 2024 as an NC1 stability measure); the contribution is narrowly and correctly scoped as "using early variability-collapse behavior as a practical online signal," not as inventing a new NC metric.

**Rigor:** The experimental design is reasonably thorough for an academic-scale study: 21 model-dataset configurations, 5 independent runs per configuration with reported standard deviations, a proper statistical comparison via Nemenyi post-hoc test on a critical-difference diagram (rather than just eyeballing mean accuracies), and multiple ablations (tunnel-set size, seed sensitivity, classification-head design, epoch-vs-layer sensitivity via oracle "train from scratch" experiments in Section 4.1-4.2). A specific, paper-acknowledged weak point: the CUB/ResNet10 configuration (Table 8) shows NNS_LP losing ~11 accuracy points relative to full training (36.02→25.04) at a 74.36% reduction — the paper does not hide this failure and offers a specific (if somewhat speculative) explanation (shallow backbone + fine-grained 200-class task + insufficient post-split capacity), which is a mark of intellectual honesty rather than cherry-picking.

**Paper-specific weak baseline/gap:** NNS_FIX (the fixed, non-trainable ETF head) is consistently the worst-performing variant and in several configurations (e.g., ResNet10/CIFAR-100: 13.01%±1.16, marked with an asterisk for at least one failed run) collapses catastrophically — this is a genuinely weak configuration included in the comparison, though the authors use it appropriately as a lower-bound reference rather than presenting it as a competitive method. A more substantive gap: the FLOPs analysis (Appendix E.2) candidly shows EB-LTH usually achieves *better* FLOPs reduction than NNS at matched parameter-reduction levels, since NNS only shortens depth after the split epoch rather than pruning channels throughout training — this means NNS's efficiency claim is about final model size, not about total training compute, a distinction that is stated but could be emphasized more prominently in the abstract/conclusion.

**Reproducibility:** Code is released (GitHub link in abstract). Hyperparameters, architectures, and hardware are documented in Appendix A. However, Table 3 shows the identified split layer is sensitive to the optimization seed alone (shifting by ±1 layer even with a fixed tunnel set), meaning single-run reproduction of the "canonical" split point is not guaranteed — an honestly reported but non-trivial reproducibility caveat for a method whose central claim is reliable online split detection.

**Significance:** The method targets small-to-medium CNN/MLP architectures on small-scale image benchmarks (up to CIFAR-100/CUB, no ImageNet or transformer-scale validation). The authors explicitly flag transformer architectures and larger-scale settings as open future work, which appropriately bounds the claimed significance to the tested regime rather than overclaiming broad applicability.

**Red flags:** None severe — the paper is transparent about its own worst cases (CUB/ResNet10 failure, NNS_FIX collapse, EB-LTH's FLOPs advantage) rather than suppressing them, which is a positive signal for intellectual honesty. The main caveats are scope-related (small-scale benchmarks, CNN/MLP-only, seed-sensitive split detection) rather than methodological red flags.

## Strengths

- Clear, well-motivated synthesis of two established but previously separate literatures (Neural Collapse and Tunnel Effect) into an actionable training-time procedure.
- Extensive, statistically grounded empirical validation (21 configurations, 5 seeds each, Nemenyi critical-difference test) rather than cherry-picked single-run comparisons.
- Transparent reporting of failure modes (CUB/ResNet10, NNS_FIX collapse) and of the method's comparative disadvantage in FLOPs versus EB-LTH, rather than only reporting favorable numbers.
- Practical, low-overhead split-detection criterion (IFC on a 5% tunnel set) that converges within a small fraction of total training epochs (7.9%-52%), giving genuine wall-clock savings when combined with a shortened final training pass.
- Useful ablation isolating that parameter count alone does not predict post-split accuracy (Table 4) — split depth and head design both matter independently, a nuanced and non-obvious finding.

## Limitations

- Validated only on relatively small-scale, non-transformer architectures (MLP, VGG, ResNet up to depth 34) and modest datasets (Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10/100, CUB); no evidence at ImageNet or transformer scale.
- The identified split point is sensitive to the optimization seed independent of tunnel-set construction (Table 3), meaning the method's "reliable online signal" claim has meaningful run-to-run variance.
- NNS reduces model depth/parameters but does not directly reduce training FLOPs as effectively as channel-pruning alternatives like EB-LTH, since computation up to the split point is unchanged.
- Fails notably on the fine-grained, low-data CUB benchmark with a shallow backbone (ResNet10), suggesting the method's reliability degrades when the extractor/tunnel transition is less clearly separated or when little post-split training budget remains.
- The 15-epoch patience window and 5% tunnel-set fraction are fixed empirically across all experiments rather than being tuned or theoretically justified per configuration; the paper does perform sensitivity checks (Appendix C.1) but a fully principled choice rule remains open.

## Relevance

该工作提供了一种在训练早期就能在线检测"特征提取器-分类隧道"分裂点并动态精简网络的实用方法，对关注模型压缩、训练效率与 Neural Collapse/Tunnel Effect 理论交叉的研究方向有直接参考价值；其对失败案例（CUB 细粒度任务）的坦诚报告也为评估该类方法的适用边界提供了有益参照。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How would IFC-based split detection and NNS behave on transformer architectures (ViT, language models), where residual streams and attention layers may not exhibit the same clean extractor/tunnel dichotomy observed in CNNs/MLPs?
- Could a principled, theoretically grounded stopping rule (rather than an empirically fixed 15-epoch patience window and 5% tunnel-set fraction) further stabilize the split-layer identification against optimization-seed variance shown in Table 3?
- Since EB-LTH achieves better FLOPs reduction while NNS achieves better final accuracy/parameter trade-offs, is there a principled joint criterion (beyond the preliminary NNS+EB-LTH_30 combination reported) that could simultaneously optimize depth truncation and channel-level sparsification during training?
- What specifically causes the CUB/ResNet10 failure — is it recoverable by delaying the split epoch, using a different head design (as Table 4's average-pooling result suggests for other configurations), or is a minimum extractor depth simply required for very fine-grained, low-data tasks?
- Would combining IFC with an NC2-aware (ETF alignment) or manifold-geometry-aware signal, as the authors suggest as future work, meaningfully improve split reliability, or does the added complexity undermine the method's key efficiency advantage over TE's original post-hoc linear-probe criterion?
