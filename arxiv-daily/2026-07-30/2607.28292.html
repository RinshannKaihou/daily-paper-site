Confining knowledge edits to a 200×‑smaller LoRA subspace stabilizes sequential fact‑updating in 4‑bit quantized financial LLMs.

CACHE-UK: A Stability-Aware Memory Editor for Sequentially Updated Quantized LLMs in Finance ↗

Anubhav Lakra, Yue Feng · 2026-07-30

2 authors (IIT Madras / Univ. of Birmingham) · single BlueBEAR HPC allocation + Kaggle credits · no peer-reviewed venue indicated · code "upon publication"

knowledge-editing 4-bit quantization financial-llm continual-learning

Key Metric

28%

Test Success (paraphrase generalization) on the 4-bit UK financial fact-editing benchmark.

+6pp abs. / +27% rel. over best baseline KnowledgeEditor (22%) · ROME 15% · MEMIT 12% · EasyEdit 14%

Why This Matters

Financial LLMs must ingest constant fact updates (CEO changes, BoE policy) while running quantized for deployment cost — this tests whether knowledge editing survives 4-bit compression.

Critical Edge

Roughly three of four paraphrased queries still fail post-edit (28% test success), and every reported number comes from a single, unreplicated edit-ordering run.

Methodology

1 · Domain scoreUK finance/banking keyword lexicon scales base ROME-style Δ before it's applied.
→
2 · Rank-1 LoRA editKey k projected into 16-dim LoRA subspace; outer-product edit ΔB hits the adapter's B matrix (51,200 params), not the frozen 10.2M-param base W₀.
→
3 · Stability controllerDegradation on a fixed preservation set accumulates as "debt" D; next edit's strength α = 0.1/(1+D) — conservative when debt is high, decays at δ=0.5 otherwise.

Backbone: OpenLLaMA-3B, 4-bit NF4 (BitsAndBytes), LoRA (r=16, α=32, on q_proj/v_proj) fine-tuned one epoch on a stratified 12,000-document subset — just 13.6% of the full corpus, constrained by a single HPC allocation (4×A100-40GB).

  • Baselines ROME / MEMIT / EasyEdit / KnowledgeEditor aren't natively BitsAndBytes-compatible, so authors built "functionally equivalent adaptations" and claim to have verified parity on full-precision prompts first.
  • All methods share the identical backbone, quantization, adapter, and edit/preservation splits — isolating the stability mechanism, not a full-precision method comparison.

Since Edit Success is ~100% everywhere, Retention (TS/ES) largely just tracks Test Success rather than adding an independent signal. Degradation score is the paper's "most consistent effect": an 11–17% relative cut vs. every baseline.

Batch size 5 → 200: edit effectiveness 40% → 25% while throughput stays flat (~450/min) — interference, not compute, is the bottleneck.

Context: original ROME/MEMIT papers report 70–90% test success on full-precision models (different benchmarks/architectures) — CACHE-UK's 28% under 4-bit is flagged by the authors as still far below that range.

Strengths

  • Falsifiable mechanistic story: 4-bit step size vs. edit magnitude is a concrete resolution barrier, fixed by a concrete architectural move (edit inside the 200×-smaller LoRA subspace).
  • Consistent, same-direction gains across all four metrics under matched backbone/quantization/adapter/splits — isolates the stability mechanism from size/precision confounds.
  • Unusually transparent self-critique: adapted baselines, single-run results, low absolute generalization, and keyword-only scoring are all flagged by the authors themselves.
  • Concrete, disclosed evaluation asset: 88,021-document UK financial corpus, 700+ verified facts, 1,100+ disjoint preservation facts for an under-explored niche.

Limitations

  • 28% absolute Test Success — about 3 of 4 paraphrased queries still fail; explicitly not proposed as production-ready.
  • All experiments are single runs: no variance across random edit orderings, batch compositions, or seeds.
  • Baselines are the authors' own quantization-compatible re-implementations, not official ROME/MEMIT/MEND/KnowledgeEditor code.
  • Domain prioritization uses hardcoded UK/banking keyword lexicons, not semantic relevance — misses lexically-atypical but relevant facts.
  • Confined to one 3B, relatively dated backbone (OpenLLaMA-3B) and a 13.6% fine-tuning subset; scaling to 7B–70B / Llama-3 / Mistral is unverified.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does the +6pp Test Success gain and 11–17% degradation reduction survive multiple randomized edit orderings and seeds, as the authors themselves propose as future work?
  2. How much of the advantage over KnowledgeEditor comes from the LoRA-subspace confinement specifically vs. domain prioritization vs. the debt controller — no ablation isolates the three modules.
  3. Does the benefit persist at 8-bit or full precision, where the resolution barrier motivating the LoRA trick is far less severe?
  4. How would an embedding-based relevance classifier (replacing keyword matching) change edit coverage and reported Test Success?
  5. Since baselines are the authors' own reimplementations, how would results change using the original ROME/MEMIT/KnowledgeEditor codebases?
  6. What happens at 7B–70B scale or with modern instruction-tuned backbones, where LoRA rank and base weight distribution differ substantially?
