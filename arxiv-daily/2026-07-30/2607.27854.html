Why This Matters

A near-zero-cost way to shrink networks while training, not after — relevant to anyone weighing model compression against training efficiency or Neural Collapse theory.

Critical Edge

On fine-grained CUB-200 with a shallow ResNet10 backbone, accuracy collapses from 36.02% to 25.04% after a 74.36% parameter cut.

Methodology

Two theories, one online signal

Neural Collapse says deep features and classifier weights geometrically collapse to a simplex ETF near the end of training. The Tunnel Effect says only the early layers ("the extractor") build a linearly separable representation — later layers ("the tunnel") just compress it, barely touching accuracy. The old way to find the split: train to full convergence, linear-probe every layer, then decide — expensive, and only knowable after the fact.

IFC per layer, tracked every epoch on a 5% "tunnel set" — sign of its change vs. epoch 1 labels each layer
  • Signal: IFC = within-class variance / between-class variance, computed per layer, per epoch, on a fixed 5%, class-balanced sample — far cheaper than full NC1 or exhaustive linear probing.
  • Rule: label each layer extractor-like (IFC rising) or classifier-like (IFC falling) vs. its epoch-1 value; once labels hold steady for a 15-epoch patience window, the split is stable. Ambiguous cases favor a deeper, safer cut.
  • Simplify: discard the classifier-like tail, attach a lightweight head — a plain linear probe (NNS_LP, the best performer), a frozen ETF projector (NNS_FIX), or a Riemannian-optimized ETF head (NNS_DCL) — and resume training the shrunk network.

Strengths what holds up

  • Clean synthesis of two separate literatures (Neural Collapse + Tunnel Effect) into one actionable, training-time procedure.
  • Statistically grounded: 21 configurations × 5 seeds, with a proper Nemenyi critical-difference test rather than cherry-picked single runs.
  • Transparent about its own failures (CUB/ResNet10, NNS_FIX collapse) instead of only reporting favorable numbers.
  • Split detection converges within 7.9%–52% of total epochs — genuine wall-clock savings, not just a smaller final model.
  • Shows head design matters independently of split depth — a nuanced, non-obvious finding (Table 4 ablation).

Limitations where it's bounded

  • Tested only on MLP/VGG/ResNet up to depth 34 and modest datasets — no ImageNet or transformer-scale evidence.
  • The identified split layer shifts by ±1 with only a seed change, undermining the "reliable online signal" framing.
  • Shortens depth but doesn't cut training FLOPs as effectively as channel-pruning baselines like EB-LTH.
  • Fails hardest exactly where it's most tempting to use it: fine-grained, low-data, shallow-backbone settings (CUB/ResNet10).
  • The 15-epoch patience window and 5% tunnel-set size are fixed empirically, not theoretically derived.
Questions for Further Exploration
Would IFC-based splitting even apply to transformers, where residual streams may not show a clean extractor/tunnel divide?
Could a theoretically grounded stopping rule replace the fixed 15-epoch window and stabilize the seed-sensitivity in Table 3?
Is there a joint criterion that gets EB-LTH's FLOPs savings and NNS's accuracy/parameter trade-off at once?
Is the CUB/ResNet10 failure fixable by delaying the split, or does fine-grained low-data classification need a minimum extractor depth?
