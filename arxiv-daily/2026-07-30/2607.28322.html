Sequential Statistics · Anytime-Valid Inference

A single detector finds distribution changes even when the "before" and "after" laws overlap in the very same statistical class.

非划分变点检测：不再要求变点前后分布分属两个不相交的已知族，仅对每个候选"无变点"分布构造点零假设 e-process、取下确界，即可实现有限样本有效的序贯检测。

Key Metric

−39%

shorter mean detection delay than the strongest prior baseline — CADD 17.3 vs. 28.5 (repeated-CS detector, Shekhar & Ramdas 2023) at mean shift δ=1.0, ARL budget A=1000. Information-theoretic floor: 13.8.

Why This Matters

Lets engineers monitor A/B tests, production drift, or anomaly streams for change without pre-specifying how the "before" and "after" states differ — only that they differ.

Critical Edge

Only two toy synthetic experiments are run — no real-world data, and the paper's two most sophisticated detectors (unknown-variance Gaussian, Markov chain) are validated purely theoretically, never empirically.

Methodology

candidate no-change law R ∈ 𝒫 → point-null e-process MR → sum over start times s → inf over R → threshold at A (or 1/α)
  • The problem: classical CUSUM / Shiryaev–Roberts / GLR and the prior e-detector framework (Shin et al. 2023) all assume the pre- and post-change families P₀, P₁ are disjoint. Here both P and Q are unknown members of the same class 𝒫 — no partition to exploit.
  • The recipe: for every candidate "no-change" law R, build an s-delay point-null e-process testing R against everything else; aggregate over all start times (summed for an ARL-type detector, weighted-summed for a PFA-type detector); take the infimum over R. Crossing a threshold gives finite-sample ARL≥A or PFA≤α guarantees for any 𝒫.
  • Three worked instances: sub-Gaussian mean shift (closed-form mixture e-process); bounded [0,1] means via Cover's continuous universal-portfolio betting; Gaussian mean shift with unknown variance via a "universal-inference" t-test e-process, plus a full-Gaussian variant that learns mean & variance jointly to hit the sharper DKL(Q‖P) rate.
  • Making delay bounds hold in general: "adjuster" functions patch the running maximum so pre-change evidence survives past the changepoint; a new, weaker-than-weak-compactness condition — a "countable local REGROW witness basis" — replaces the usual regularity requirement for the asymptotic-optimality proof.

Strengths

  • Clean, general reduction of an underexplored problem to point-null e-process aggregation — works for arbitrary 𝒫 without requiring weak compactness.
  • Finite-sample (not just asymptotic) ARL and PFA validity, proved directly from e-process supermartingale properties.
  • Matching upper/lower delay bounds, plus a novel "early-change impossibility" result unique to this fully-overlapping setting.
  • Four fully worked distributional settings (sub-Gaussian, bounded, Gaussian w/ unknown variance, Markov chains), each closed-form or efficiently computable.
  • Validity extends beyond i.i.d. data (Markov-chain example) and code is publicly released.

Limitations

  • Evaluation limited to two synthetic experiments; no real-world data, no multivariate examples.
  • Delay-optimality is first-order asymptotic ((1+o(1)) bounds) and needs a "late" changepoint — early changes are provably undetectable (Theorem 7.3), a fundamental, not technical, limit.
  • General upper bounds need a "countable local REGROW witness basis," a brand-new condition whose scope beyond Gaussian location families is uncharacterized.
  • The simplest sub-Gaussian construction attains a weaker KL rate than the paper's strongest theoretical rate.
  • Foundational building blocks (REGROW theory, adjusters) are the authors' own 2026 work, not yet independently vetted.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. How does the unknown-variance Gaussian detector's delay compare empirically to the full-Gaussian instance-optimal detector — is the It vs. IG gap practically significant at realistic sample sizes?
  2. Can the "countable local REGROW witness basis" condition be verified for other nonparametric classes (log-concave densities, bounded-variance families, unbounded exponential families) beyond the Gaussian location family?
  3. How does the non-partitioned e-detector perform against genuinely different baselines — GLR, classical CUSUM with plug-in estimates, or the cpm package — rather than only another e-process method from the same lab?
  4. What is the practical computational cost of maintaining the infimum over R ∈ 𝒫 for higher-dimensional or more complex classes, beyond the 1-D cases shown here?
  5. Does the early-change impossibility result have practical implications for how practitioners should set the ARL/PFA budget when a change might occur soon after monitoring begins?
