ClawTrack · Trace-Level Agent Evaluation

Judging agents on outcomes alone hides how they work — rubric-based process scores reveal reasoning quality invisible to outcome-only grading.

ClawTrack: Towards Trace-Level Evaluation and Improvement of Real-World Autonomous Agents ↗

Xingjian Wu, Xuhang Zhu, Xingchen Liu, et al. (9 authors, Meituan) · 30 Jul 2026 · Unreviewed preprint · arXiv cs.LG
llm-agents agent-evaluation process-reward benchmark trajectory-filtering rubric-based-grading
一句话总结 ClawTrack 是一个覆盖 320 个真实任务（8 大领域）的双评估基准，用 12,541 条任务专属细粒度规则同时打出"结果分"和逐轮"过程分"，发现 Claude-Opus-4.7 单次通过率最高（Pass@3=76.4%）但 Claude-Opus-4.8 三次全过率更高（Pass³=51.1% vs 46.7%），并用过程分做数据筛选，在 Qwen3 三种规模上把 SFT 后的 Pass³ 提升 +10 到 +19 个百分点。

Claude-Opus-4.8 is the more consistent deployment choice — Pass³ 51.1% vs. 46.7%, despite a lower single-shot Pass@3. Peak capability and repeat-trial reliability are not the same thing.

Why This Matters

Gives agent researchers a reusable, fine-grained process-scoring framework — with proof it also improves SFT data curation, not just diagnosis.

Critical Edge

All headline rankings (Tables 3–4) rely on a single judge LLM, Claude-Opus-4.8 — any systematic bias in that judge would silently propagate through every result.

Methodology

Four layers turn a task instruction into two independent scores per trajectory.

Task Layer320 tasks × 8 domains, 12,541 rubric items (~39.2/task)
→
Harness LayerDocker workspaces, 25+ mock services, ReAct loop
→
Evaluation LayerOutcome Grader + Process Grader (per-turn)
→
Reporting Layerk=3 trials → Pass@k / Pass³
  • Task Score = safety gate × (0.80 · completion + 0.20 · tool-error robustness). A safety violation zeroes the whole score.
  • Process Score averages four per-turn dimensions — goal alignment (multiplicative gate: off-goal turn = 0), efficiency (0.40), information utilization (0.40), result verification (0.20) — uniformly across all turns.
  • Pass criterion is dual-threshold: a trial passes only if s_task ≥ 0.75 AND s_proc ≥ 0.60. Pass@k = any trial passes; Pass³ = all 3 trials pass.
  • Rubric generation: 40 human-expert seed tasks (5/domain, κ=0.874 agreement) distilled by Claude-Opus-4.8 into a reusable rubric-writing "skill," applied to the remaining 280 tasks and validated against humans (r=0.912).
  • Trajectory filtering: ~20,415 trajectories from ToolBench/τ-bench/WildClawBench → keep outcome-correct + process ≥ 0.65 (~7,200) → top 5,000 by process score → full-parameter SFT on 3 Qwen3 scales, vs. a Random-5k baseline.

  • Multimodal subset (91 tasks): GPT-5.5 leads (Pass@3 53.3%), while Claude-Opus-4.7 — top on text tasks — drops to rank 8 (25.6%).
  • Process-outcome correlation: r=0.466, d=0.945 across 13,641 turn-level trials — real but not redundant with outcome.
  • Inter-judge robustness: 4 judge LLMs agree at mean pairwise r=0.81 (same-family judges up to r=0.871).
  • Domain difficulty: Legal (47.5%) and Media (48.7%) hardest; DevOps and Retail exceed 80% Pass@3 for top models.

Strengths

  • Concretely demonstrates its own value: the Opus-4.7-vs-4.8 Pass@3/Pass³ split is exactly the signal outcome-only evaluation would miss.
  • Grader validation exceeds typical agent benchmarks: human–LLM agreement r=0.912; 4-judge cross-validation mean r=0.81.
  • Downstream utility is shown, not just claimed: process-filtered SFT yields +10 to +19 Pass³ points across three real model scales, and gains grow with model size.
  • Transparent, specific self-reported limitations (mock-service determinism, single default judge, seed-rubric coverage gaps) rather than generic caveats.

Limitations

  • Deterministic, frozen mock services instead of live APIs — may miss real-world service variability (authors' own admission).
  • Headline leaderboard uses one default judge (Claude-Opus-4.8); the 4-judge check is a secondary analysis only.
  • Rubric generation bootstraps from just 40 seed tasks; under-represented domains risk lower-quality auto-generated rubrics.
  • The four process dimensions are fixed by design and don't capture creativity or communication style.
  • No ablation isolating fine-grained rubrics' benefit over coarser binary/checkpoint signals used by prior benchmarks.
  • Task/environment release status beyond a leaderboard link is unclear, limiting independent verification.

Questions for Further Exploration

  • Would rankings shift if the four process dimensions were re-weighted, or a fifth (e.g. safety reasoning) were added?
  • Would Pass@3/Pass³ rankings reorder if GPT-5.5 replaced Claude-Opus-4.8 as the production default judge?
  • Does process-based filtering still help when training and evaluation domains don't overlap?
  • How sensitive are the pass thresholds (τ_task=0.75, τ_proc=0.60) and score-weighting constants to different settings?
  • Would process-quality rankings transfer to agents facing live, non-deterministic APIs with real latency and failures?
