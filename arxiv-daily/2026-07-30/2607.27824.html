Why This Matters

Gives auditors a reusable statistical pipeline for finding which social biases actually live inside LLM activations — not just the small set psychologists already catalogued.

Critical Edge

The headline 0.55–0.63 human-agreement scores are only modestly above chance for a binary call — a weakness the paper doesn't foreground enough.

Methodology

Four swappable components, applied to Llama-3-8B-Instruct & Mistral-7B-Instruct
1 · Axis source1,999 WordNet antonym pairs (5,182 adjectives) → 30 templated sentences per pole
2 · RepresentationMean-pooled attention-head outputs (32×32 heads) → mass-mean probing → geometric axis
3 · Projection"Describe {concept} with adjectives" → activation projected onto axis, z-scored
4 · Stereotypicality testKS test: concept-set projections vs. random reference (p<0.05)
  • Mass-mean probing (class-mean difference) is used instead of logistic regression because it deterministically preserves axis direction under label swap.
  • Candidate layer-head pairs are ranked by a between/within-class variance ratio; the top-128 heads are averaged, chosen from a sweep up to k=1024.
  • Ground truth: Fraser et al. (2021) Warmth/Competence labels for 25 social groups, plus a fresh 100-axis human annotation (5 raters post-filtering, Fleiss' κ = 0.375).
A 1,000-resample bootstrap power analysis checks whether n=50 concept-set size gives the KS test adequate power.

Strengths

  • Four-component decomposition makes design choices independently swappable and ablated.
  • Axis pool (1,999 WordNet pairs) dwarfs the small, theory-driven sets used in prior stereotype work.
  • Careful stats: explicit KS threshold plus a dedicated bootstrap power analysis, not an ad hoc distance metric.
  • Localizes stereotypes to specific layer-head pairs — directly usable for activation steering.
  • Human validation uses stratified sampling and quality-control filtering, not abstract judgments.

Limitations

  • Case study restricted to social groups — the only domain with accepted psychological ground truth.
  • Axes must be "meaningful" for the concept; geometry/chemistry antonyms don't apply to humans.
  • English-only, WordNet-dependent; other languages need translated templates + a multilingual lexicon.
  • No ground truth for what an LLM "should" encode — divergence from humans isn't proof of model failure.
  • Only two ~7-8B instruct models tested; larger or base models are untested.
  • Human baseline itself is thin: 5 annotators, Fleiss' κ = 0.375 ("fair" agreement only).

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does "LLMs agree more with each other than with humans" hold at 70B+ scale, or for base (non-instruct) models?
  2. How would STEREODISCO compare, axis-by-axis, against a word-embedding baseline (WEAT/POLAR) on the same 76 axes?
  3. Does steering along a "novel" axis like narrow-minded/broad-minded produce coherent output shifts, or degrade under intervention?
  4. Would a larger, more expert annotator pool change which axes count as stereotypical, given κ=0.375?
  5. Can the framework extend past adjective antonyms to verb- or noun-based semantic axes?
