BaKron: Efficient Quantization with Kronecker-Factored Hessians

Johann Birnick  ·  Rayan Saab  — UC San Diego, Mathematics
TL;DR. BaKron accelerates two-sided Kronecker-factored Hessian quantization (BoA/YAQA) to cubic O(mn(m+n)) work and O(m+n) sequential depth — matching GPTQ's cost while exploiting richer output-side curvature.

Why This Matters
Directly accelerates GPTQ-family curvature-aware quantization for 2–3 bit LLM deployment, with a modular Hessian menu and single-GPU global-Hessian flow.

Methodology & Results

Two composable tricks

  • Anti-diagonal parallelism. Entries with i+j = const are mutually independent under the rank-1 update — batch them. Sequential depth mn → m+n.
  • Recursive divide-and-conquer. Hold errors back, propagate as one dense matmul across a midpoint split. Total work O(m²n²) → O(mn(m+n)).
  • Full BaKron (Algorithm 6) applies divide-and-conquer along the anti-diagonal — gets both gains at once.

Hessian menu (4 variants)

Two loss choices (global Fisher via backprop vs local BoA-style) crossed with two factorizations (K-FAC independence vs Shampoo power iteration). First derivation of local Hessians for the GLU-style Transformer MLP module — BoA only covered attention.

Single-GPU global Hessian

A recursive layer-halving flow cuts accelerator memory from YAQA's O(ℓ) (~99 GB float32 for Llama-3-8B, forcing a multi-GPU rack) to O(log ℓ), with factors streamed from CPU. Global-Fisher quantization of an 8B model now fits on one GPU.

Critical Edge

The experimental evaluation is thin relative to the claims. Only RTN and GPTQ are compared — no QuIP#, AWQ, SpinQuant, QuaRot, and notably no head-to-head accuracy against the direct competitors BoA and YAQA (only wall-clock core-runtime). For a paper whose pitch is "we accelerate BoA/YAQA," that gap is hard to overlook.

Other sharp concerns

  • Single bit-width. Everything is at 2.81 bits; 3/4/8-bit regimes uncharacterized.
  • Factorization is unpredictable. K-FAC collapses on Qwen3-1.7B (PPL 60.97 vs Shampoo's 20.26); Shampoo is worse on Llama-3-8B FullyLocal (45.31 vs 17.19). No principled way to choose.
  • Backprop isn't always best. On Llama-3.2-3B and Qwen3-1.7B K-FAC, the expensive global Hessian underperforms the cheap local one.
  • No group-wise scaling — standard in modern 2–3 bit PTQ — so part of the gap may be the scaling, not the Hessian.
  • No released code; custom Triton kernels and anti-diagonal memory layouts described only verbally.

Relevance
