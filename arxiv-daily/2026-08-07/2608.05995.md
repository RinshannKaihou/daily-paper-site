> **一句话总结：** 作者将不确定性统一定义为"样本条件逐点后验风险"（给定观测样本下、预测器在所有合理真值函数分布下的期望损失），并在 14 个基于真实协变量+GP 先验生成的半合成表格回归任务上计算"神谕"认知/偶然不确定性，发现主流方法（深度集成、FSP-Laplace、CatBoost-KGB）也只能达到远低于 0.5 的 Spearman 相关，预测准 ≠ 不确定性可靠。

> **TL;DR:** The paper proposes **sample-conditional pointwise posterior risk** as a unified, estimator-aware definition of uncertainty that recovers Bayesian posterior-variance and frequentist excess-risk views as special cases, and instantiates it as a **semi-synthetic tabular regression benchmark** (14 UCI/OpenML covariate sets with GP-drawn targets and known heteroscedastic noise) where oracle aleatoric/epistemic targets are computable exactly. On a held-out suite of 7 datasets (primary metric: Spearman ρ against oracle epistemic uncertainty), most methods stay well below ρ=0.5; ensemble-style neural methods (NN-Ens, NN-Dropout, NN-Bag, NN-FSP) and CatBoost-KGB / NGBoost-Bag lead, while DEUP is comparatively weak, and method rankings are only moderately preserved across datasets (Kendall W≈0.46).

## 核心贡献

- **不确定性的统一定义。** 将不确定性定义为"样本条件逐点后验风险" R_S(f̂; x) = E_{f~p(f|S)} E_{Y|x,f} [ℓ(f̂(x), Y)]，即在给定观测样本 S 下，预测器 f̂ 在所有合理真值函数后验分布下的期望损失。这一视角同时包含"贝叶斯后验方差"与"频率派超额风险"两种观点，作者在论文中明确给出二者成立的附加假设（前者要求 f̂ 恰为后验 Bayes 行动；后者要求后验坍缩为 Dirac 点）。
- **基于 Bregman 散度的可扩展分解。** 利用 Pfau (2025) 的 Bregman bias–variance 恒等式，将后验风险分解为 aleatoric uncertainty（Bayes 误差）+ generalized variance（后验散布）+ generalized bias（估计器偏差），并在 MSE、CRPS、NLL 三种严格 proper 损失下给出显式公式，明确 AU/EU 的语义由"评估风险所用的损失"决定，而非训练目标决定。
- **可计算神谕目标的半合成基准。** 在保留 UCI/OpenML 真实协变量结构的同时，用 GP 先验采样真值目标、加入已知异方差噪声，使 p(f|S) 解析可算，从而在每个评估点上得到精确的、估计器特定的 AU/EU 神谕目标；提供 14 个 kernel preset 覆盖不同维度、核族、噪声水平与 covariate-shift 配置（balanced / OOD interior / bimodal）。这是首个能绕开 OOD 检测等代理任务、直接比对不确定性结构的工作。
- **细粒度实证方法学。** 用 7 个开发集做 5 类消融（宽度、深度、激活、调参目标、优化器）锁定配置，再在 7 个留出集上跑最终基准，覆盖 BLR、DKL、NGBoost/CatBoost（含 KGB）、EDL、Bagging、Deep Ens、MC Dropout、Laplace、FSP-Laplace、SWAG、DEUP 共四大方法家族，给出"准确预测 ≠ 可靠不确定性分解"的核心实证发现。

## Key Contributions

- **Unified definition of uncertainty as sample-conditional pointwise posterior risk** R_S(f̂; x) = E_{f~p(f|S)} E_{Y|x,f}[ℓ(f̂(x), Y)], which conditions on the observed sample S and averages the predictor's loss over the posterior over plausible ground-truth functions. This is strictly more general than both the posterior-variance (Bayesian) view and the pointwise-excess-risk (frequentist) view; the paper states the additional assumptions under which each of those older views is recovered (predictor = posterior Bayes act; posterior collapses to a Dirac at f*, respectively).
- **Bregman-divergence decomposition** (building on Pfau 2025) of posterior risk into aleatoric (Bayes error) + generalized variance + generalized bias, with closed-form specializations under squared loss (σ²_ε + σ²_f + (f̂−m_S)²), CRPS (aleatoric depends only on the noise CDF), and NLL (aleatoric = entropy of the noise law, epistemic = posterior KL variance + KL bias to posterior predictive). A key implication: AU/EU semantics are pinned by the loss used for the *oracle risk*, not the training loss.
- **Semi-synthetic benchmark with computable oracle targets.** Real UCI/OpenML covariates are retained while targets are drawn from a GP prior whose hyperparameters are fit by marginal-likelihood on the original task, with controlled heteroscedastic noise σ(x) proportional to average feature rank. Because the same prior generates f and conditions p(f|S), the posterior is correctly-specified by construction and oracle AU/EU are analytic at every evaluation point. 14 kernel presets span dimensions 4–21, multiple kernel families, balanced/OOD-interior/bimodal covariate-shift profiles, and training sizes 750/1500.
- **Two-stage evaluation methodology (dev vs held-out) with content-aware ablations.** Five ablations (width, depth, activation, tuning objective NLL vs RMSE, optimizer) are run on a 7-dataset dev suite to fix implementation choices before the final 7-dataset held-out benchmark, isolating how modeling choices affect uncertainty *beyond* what proxy tasks reveal.

## Methodology

The paper interleaves a theoretical framework with a controlled empirical protocol.

**Theory.** Starting from the pointwise risk view (Definition 2.1), the authors introduce sample-conditional posterior risk (Definition 2.2), which marginalizes over the latent-function posterior p(f|S) *with S held fixed* — distinguishing it from the resampling frequentist view that takes an outer expectation over repeated S draws. Proposition 2.3 applies the Bregman bias–variance identity pointwise in x to decompose total posterior risk into AU + generalized variance + generalized bias. Appendix A specializes this to MSE (aleatoric = local noise variance σ²_ε(x), epistemic = posterior variance σ²_f(x) + squared bias (f̂(x)−m_S(x))²), CRPS (aleatoric depends only on the noise CDF F_ε, epistemic = integrated posterior CDF variance + CDF bias), and NLL (aleatoric = noise entropy ½log(2πeσ²_ε(x)), epistemic = posterior KL spread + KL bias to the posterior predictive density). Appendix B adds a separate estimation/approximation-error decomposition relative to the best-in-class predictor f_F, used purely as a diagnostic (not a primary target).

**Semi-synthetic data construction.** For each preset: (1) standardize the real dataset; (2) fit a GP by marginal likelihood to obtain realistic kernel hyperparameters; (3) sample a latent function f from that prior at the real covariates to define synthetic targets; (4) draw noise ε_i ~ N(0, σ²(x_i)) with σ(x) linearly tied to feature rank for structured heteroscedasticity; (5) select a training subset S of size 750 or 1500 from a Mahalanobis-distance quantile partition to control covariate shift; (6) condition the same GP prior on S to get an analytical, correctly-specified posterior, hence exact oracle AU/EU at every one of n_eval points.

**Evaluation metrics.** Predictive mean, AU, and EU are compared to oracle targets via MSE, Pearson r, and Spearman ρ_S; **primary metric is Spearman ρ_S** because it measures whether a method ranks points correctly by uncertainty, independent of arbitrary scale. Risk-coverage and calibration curves are also reported. The study spans four method families: Bayesian linear regression (BLR), deep kernel learning (DKL), probabilistic tree methods (NGBoost, CatBoost with PS and KGB variants), and neural methods (EDL, Bagging, Deep Ensembles, MC Dropout, Laplace, FSP-Laplace, SWAG, DEUP). Hyperparameters are tuned with 10 Weights & Biases Bayesian sweeps; full pipeline ran in <100 wall-clock hours on an 8-core Apple M3.

## Results & Highlights

- **No method approaches oracle-level epistemic alignment.** On the 7-dataset held-out benchmark (Spearman ρ between predicted and oracle epistemic uncertainty), "most correlations [are] still well below ρ = 0.5" even for the strongest methods; current methods "remain far from near-oracle performance." This is the headline empirical finding: accurate prediction does **not** translate into reliable uncertainty disentanglement.
- **Ensemble-style neural methods and CatBoost-KGB / NGBoost-Bag lead the epistemic ranking** while DEUP is "comparatively weak". From the selective-prediction analysis the most useful epistemic signal among neural methods comes from NN-Ens, NN-Dropout, NN-Bag, and NN-FSP; among non-neural baselines NGBoost-Bag shows the clearest retained-risk reductions, with CatBoost-PS and CatBoost-KGB also useful. The practical recommendation is that **deep ensembles, FSP-Laplace, and CatBoost-KGB are the most reliable starting points**.
- **Method rankings are only moderately preserved across datasets** (Kendall W ≈ 0.46), and aleatoric uncertainty is even less separable across methods than epistemic uncertainty, with NGBoost-NIG flagged as "a clear failure case" in aleatoric calibration.
- **Width ablation:** prediction quality is flat (mean ρ_ŷ = 0.83 ± 0.01 across widths), but 91% of BNN model–dataset pairs show a *negative* OLS slope in aleatoric Spearman ρ as width increases; for epistemic ranking the largest negative slopes are Deep Ensembles (Δρ_epi/log2 w = −0.029), SWAG (−0.023), and Bagging (−0.019), while FSP-Laplace is the exception (+0.021). Authors therefore restrict width to {16, 32, 64}.
- **Depth ablation:** prediction quality is again flat (mean ρ_ŷ = 0.84 ± 0.01), 62.5% of pairs show a negative slope in ρ_epi and 75% in ρ_ale; mean paired epistemic–aleatoric score drops from ≈0.30 at depth 1 to ≈0.22 at depth 6.
- **Activation / tuning objective / optimizer ablations are surprisingly weak.** NLL tuning gives only ≈+0.0446 mean Spearman gain over the optimizer-specific mean for epistemic uncertainty (epistemic diversity across tuning conditions 1−W ≈ 0.0549 vs aleatoric 1−W ≈ 0.4606); optimizer mean signed shifts for epistemic ranking are +0.0146 (SGD), +0.0050 (Adam), +0.0229 (Muon), −0.0431 (SOAP), all dwarfed by standard deviations of ≈0.11–0.16.
- **Diagnostic subpart analysis (Fig 13 / App E.7):** most methods' epistemic estimates correlate more closely with the **posterior-variance** term than with the full posterior excess-risk target, and squared-bias correlations are "generally smaller and often close to uninformative". In other words, current EU estimators behave like posterior-spread estimators and largely ignore estimator-dependent (misspecification/optimization) bias.
- **Sanity check (App B.3):** the estimation/approximation decomposition behaves as intended — estimation error decreases with training size while approximation error stays flat, and the under-capacitated linear GP shows substantially larger approximation error than the well-specified GP.

## Authors & Credibility

The five authors are from the University of Tübingen, the Hertie Institute for AI in Brain Health, the Tübingen AI Center, Charité Berlin, and the Bernstein Center for Computational Neuroscience Berlin — a credible Bayesian-ML / medical-ML group. Co-author Bálint Mucsányi is a co-author of the influential "Benchmarking uncertainty disentanglement: Specialized uncertainties for specialized tasks" (ICLR 2024), which is the most directly prior benchmarking work this paper extends; the framing, critique of OOD-as-epistemic-proxy, and the shift toward estimator-aware targets are therefore well-positioned in the authors' own research arc. The work is funded by the Hertie-Stiftung and DFG grant 459422098. The paper is a Preprint (v1, 6 Aug 2026); no peer review status is indicated. Code/scripts for reproducing the experimental pipeline and main benchmark results are stated to be provided.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty — strong.** The sample-conditional posterior-risk definition is a genuine conceptual unification, and crucially the authors make the *recovery assumptions explicit* (predictor = posterior Bayes act for the Bayesian view; Dirac posterior for the frequentist view) rather than asserting equivalence. The Bregman-based specialization across MSE/CRPS/NLL in Appendix A is a useful, self-contained theoretical contribution. The critique that posterior-variance-only EU "can underestimate epistemic uncertainty" and "model bias may instead be absorbed into the aleatoric component" is precisely articulated.
- **Rigor — high.** The dev/held-out split of 7+7 datasets to avoid tuning-on-test is the correct experimental discipline and is rare in the UD benchmarking literature. Five ablations with explicit OLS-slope statistics, paired with task-diversity Kendall-W numbers, set a good bar. The GP sanity check (App B.3) actually verifies that the diagnostic decomposition separates estimation vs approximation error as claimed.
- **Reproducibility — good in principle, qualified.** All 14 dataset presets are documented with kernel family, hyperparameters, mean function, covariate-shift profile, n_eval, aleatoric std σ_ε, and posterior std σ̂_f (Tables 1–14); model search spaces are given (App D); the pipeline is said to run in <100 wall-clock hours on a laptop M3, lowering the compute barrier. Caveat: "We provide code and scripts" is asserted but no repository URL is printed in the body; the only concrete software artifact cited is the third-party `laplax` library for Laplace approximations.
- **Significance — high for the UD subfield.** The empirical claim that "accurate prediction does not guarantee reliable uncertainty disentanglement" is directly actionable for clinical-ML and safety-critical practitioners who currently flag cases with methods like EDL/DEUP assuming their EU is reliable.
- **Red flags / limitations the paper itself partly concedes but understates:**
  - **GP-ground-truth restriction.** The benchmark's oracle computations are instantiated only under squared-error/MSE risk and only for GP ground truths. The authors are candid that this "restricts the exact protocol to Gaussian-process ground truths" and that "extending the same oracle construction to domains such as image classification or image regression is also difficult". This is a *real* scope ceiling: a method that looks good on GP-smooth tabular targets may behave differently on non-smooth, discrete, or high-dimensional modalities. The ranking claims ("deep ensembles / FSP-Laplace / CatBoost-KGB are most reliable") are thus GP-tabular-specific and should not be over-generalized to vision or NLP — a caveat the paper makes only in the Limitations section, not next to its headline recommendations.
  - **Weak absolute signal.** Even the best methods sit well below ρ=0.5 against oracle EU; with error bars of ±2 SEM across only 7 held-out datasets, several apparent pairwise differences are explicitly acknowledged to be "not robust across datasets". The "most reliable starting points" claim is therefore a *ranking* claim, not a "these methods are good" claim — easy to misread.
  - **Missing head-to-head against the most direct prior benchmark.** The paper positions itself against Mucsányi et al. (2024) (a co-author's own Specialized-Uncertainties benchmark) and QUAM (Schweighofer et al. 2023), but does not present a clean crosswalk table showing how method rankings change when moving from those proxy-based evaluations to the new oracle evaluation. Such a table would have been the single most persuasive artifact.
  - **Heteroscedastic noise construction is a design choice that favors some methods.** Setting σ(x) as a linear function of average feature rank is a particular, smooth, monotone aleatoric structure; tree-based methods (CatBoost-KGB, NGBoost-Bag) may benefit disproportionately from feature-rank-correlated noise. No ablation over *noise structure* is reported.
  - **Small absolute compute, small absolute data.** n_eval ≤ 5000 and n_train ∈ {750, 1500}; how rankings extend to larger tabular datasets is left open (the authors flag this).
  - **One dataset (DEUP) has many `n/a` cells** in the optimizer/tuning heatmaps, indicating DEUP's two-stage training did not converge under all optimizer conditions; this is visible but not analyzed, slightly weakening the comparative DEUP verdict.

## Strengths

- The conceptual unification (posterior risk as a definition that subsumes Bayesian spread and frequentist excess risk) is clean, the recovery assumptions are stated explicitly, and the MSE/CRPS/NLL specializations in Appendix A are correct and self-contained.
- The semi-synthetic construction is principled: GP kernel hyperparameters are *fit to the real data* by marginal likelihood before sampling synthetic targets, so the targets inherit realistic smoothness and scale rather than being arbitrary — a meaningful improvement over purely synthetic toy regressions.
- Dev-vs-held-out methodology with explicit task-diversity (Kendall-W) and OLS-slope statistics is exemplary for an uncertainty benchmarking paper and directly addresses the "tuning on the test set" failure mode common in prior UD benchmarks.
- Honest reporting: the paper openly states that NLL tuning is *not* meaningfully better than RMSE tuning, that no optimizer dominates, that no method is uniformly reliable, and that its own oracle construction is GP-restricted.
- Practical, falsifiable recommendations ("deep ensembles, FSP-Laplace, and CatBoost-KGB appear to be the most reliable starting points") rather than generic calls for more research.

## Limitations

- **Modality ceiling.** Oracle targets exist only for GP-drawn regression under MSE; image, text, and classification settings — arguably where safety-critical UD matters most — are not covered, and the authors acknowledge that exact ground-truth posteriors are "typically unavailable" there.
- **Single risk functional.** Only squared-error oracle risk is implemented; CRPS and NLL oracles are derived in Appendix A but not used to rank methods, so it is untested whether the rankings are robust to the choice of risk functional.
- **Small data regime.** Training sizes are 750–1500 and eval pools up to 5000; the open practical challenge of scaling the oracle protocol is explicitly flagged but unresolved.
- **Aleatoric signal is weak overall.** Even the held-out aleatoric ranking is "less clearly separated and highly variable across datasets", so the benchmark's power to discriminate aleatoric estimators is currently limited.
- **Headline ranking claim is GP-tabular-specific.** Because the generative process is GP-smooth and the noise is feature-rank-correlated, the "ensemble/FSP-Laplace/CatBoost-KGB lead" conclusion should not be uncritically transferred to other data types or noise structures.
- **No external validation of the code release.** Scripts are referenced but no public repository URL is given in the body text at submission time.

## Relevance

高度相关。本文直接命中"不确定性量化 / 认知-偶然不确定性分解"这一核心研究方向，并提供了第一个绕开 OOD 代理任务、可直接计算 oracle 目标的细粒度基准，对评估和改进贝叶斯深度学习方法论具有方法论价值；其"预测准 ≠ 不确定性可靠"的实证结论对任何在生产环境中部署不确定性估计的人都有直接参考意义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How do the method rankings change when the oracle is computed under **CRPS or NLL** risk instead of MSE — does the "ensembles and FSP-Laplace lead" conclusion survive a change of risk functional?
- Can the posterior-risk oracle be extended beyond GP-smooth ground truths to **non-smooth or discrete** latent-function priors (e.g., random forests of step functions, or neural-network-function priors), and do tree-based methods keep their advantage under non-smooth truth?
- Is the aleatoric-noise construction **σ(x) ∝ average feature rank** a confound for tree methods? Would a non-monotone or input-dependent noise structure (e.g., dependent on a low-variance latent subspace) reorder the aleatoric rankings?
- The diagnostic in App E.7 shows most EU estimators track **posterior variance, not bias**. Could one deliberately design an estimator whose EU term is a learned surrogate for the (f̂−m_S)² bias component, and does that close the gap to the full posterior excess-risk target?
- On larger tabular datasets (n ≫ 1500) or under distribution shift between training and the oracle-conditioning prior (intentional model misspecification), do deep ensembles still dominate, or do Laplace/SWAG-family methods gain because their bias term becomes comparatively larger?
- How do the rankings project onto **clinical-style selective prediction** (refer-the-worst-k%) metrics, where the cost of mis-ranked aleatoric vs epistemic uncertainty is asymmetric — the paper reports risk-coverage but does not separate the downstream cost of the two error types.
