Agentic Benchmarks · Difficulty Prediction

Predicting Task Difficulty Without Rollouts

Stefan Krsteski & Charlotte Meyer — Andromede AI · preprint under review
agentic-benchmarks item-response-theory token-entropy benchmark-auditing
TL;DR   A 1PL/Rasch IRT model fits per-task difficulty β from 415,470 agent outcomes across 17 benchmarks; a regressor then predicts β from pre-rollout features — token-level entropy wins — reaching Spearman ρ=0.399 in-distribution (ρ=0.225 OOD), while exposing AUC as a misleading metric and using prediction residuals to flag contamination vs. infeasibility.
The Essentials
① Key Finding
Token-level entropy from a small open model predicts LLM-agent task difficulty before any rollout — AUC meanwhile silently inflates the same prediction.
② Key Metric
0.399ρ
Spearman correlation (K-fold, full feature set) between predicted and IRT-fitted task difficulty, across 17 benchmarks.
OOD drops to ρ=0.225 · a constant predictor still hits AUC 0.715.
③ Why This Matters
Lets benchmark builders rank task difficulty and flag contamination or infeasible tasks without costly rollouts — a ~25× token cut — grounding trustworthy agent evaluation.

Key Contributions 核心贡献

  • Two-stage ex ante pipeline: 1PL/Rasch IRT fits per-task β and per-agent θ from binary outcomes; a regressor predicts β from the task description alone.
  • AUC debunked: constant-difficulty predictor still scores AUC 0.715; benchmark-average hits 0.806 with pairwise accuracy 0.000. Paper switches to within-benchmark z(β) + Spearman ρ.
  • Entropy shape > entropy mean: Qwen3-8B-Base Shannon entropy over a 4k reasoning trace, summarized by spread/shape/gaps, is the strongest single feature family.
  • Residual diagnostics: r = z(β) − ẑ separates unexpectedly-hard (infeasibility, r>0) from unexpectedly-easy (contamination, r<0).
  • Open corpus: 17 benchmarks · 5,230 tasks · 497 agents · 415,470 outcomes.

Methodology

  • Difficulty target: stochastic variational 1PL/Rasch, Gaussian factors, ClippedAdam (lr 0.01, 2k epochs), θ anchored to mean zero, β standardized within benchmark.
  • Trace: Claude Sonnet 4.6 generates a 4k-token reasoning trace from the task context.
  • Five feature families: random control · log-length · bge embeddings (fold-aware PCA) · entropy (single scorer) · full (5-scorer panel + disagreement + metadata).
  • Cost: ~4.0×10⁶ tokens for the feature pass vs. ~1.0×10⁸ for empirical estimation — ~25× fewer.
  • Eval: Ridge (main), with linear / RF / XGBoost ablations; K-fold in-distribution and leave-one-benchmark-out (LOBO).
④ Critical Edge — sharpest critique
The headline ρ=0.399 is in-distribution K-fold; the honest out-of-distribution number is ρ=0.225, embedding features essentially collapse (ρ=0.017), and the residual "contamination detector" is illustrated on just two SWE-bench cases with no false-positive analysis — while itself requiring post-rollout β, so it is a detection aid, not the pure pre-rollout tool the framing implies.

Strengths & Limitations

  • Strength. Clean algebraic + empirical proof that AUC overstates prediction quality here.
  • Strength. Real breadth: 17 benchmarks, 8 domains, 497 agents — generalizes past coding-only prior work.
  • Strength. Entropy shape carries signal beyond the mean — a non-obvious finding.
  • Limit. OOD transfer remains weak; embeddings fail LOBO.
  • Limit. Only 1.2 trials per agent-task → noisy IRT target; oracle 0.937 caps all predictors.
  • Limit. 8B-class scorers may cap entropy signal; source benchmarks carry known contamination.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. What is the residual detector's ROC and false-positive rate across all 5,230 tasks, not two curated cases?
  2. Would a frontier-class scorer (vs. 8B open weights) rescue OOD embedding performance (currently ρ=0.017)?
  3. Can residuals be fused with perplexity-based contamination tests to separate contamination from genuine capability gains?
  4. Does modeling the entropy profile as a time series (1D-CNN / RNN) close the KF→LOBO gap?
  5. Can ex ante predictions feed a curriculum-learning loop for agentic RL, beating reward-based self-paced curricula?
