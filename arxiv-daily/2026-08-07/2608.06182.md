> **一句话总结：** 本文系统比较了随机 extragradient 的两种采样变体（同样本 S-SEG 与独立样本 I-SEG）在单调变分不等式上的收敛性，给出一般（可能无界）域上的高概率 gap 收敛速率 $O(N^{-(1-\alpha)})$（$\tfrac{1}{2}<\alpha<1$），并通过多个反例证明这些结果无法改进——关键结论是 S-SEG 对样本算子的 Lipschitz 性更敏感，且使 I-SEG 收敛的 DSEG 非对称步长对 S-SEG 仍会几乎必然发散。

> **TL;DR:** This paper closes several gaps in the convergence theory of stochastic extragradient (SEG) for monotone variational inequalities (VIPs) by giving high-probability restricted-gap bounds of rate $O(N^{-(1-\alpha)})$ for $\gamma_k=\Theta((k+1)^{-\alpha})$, $\tfrac{1}{2}<\alpha<1$, on possibly unbounded domains for both I-SEG (independent samples) and S-SEG (same sample), and pairing every positive result with an impossibility counterexample (square-summability is necessary: $\gamma_k=1/\sqrt{k+1}$ makes both variants diverge a.s.; a high-probability guarantee cannot be replaced by an unconditional one). Crucially, the authors show a fundamental separation: the DSEG asymmetric step-size rule of Hsieh et al. (2020) that restores almost-sure last-iterate convergence for I-SEG on unconstrained monotone VIPs **fails** for S-SEG — there exists a uniformly Lipschitz stochastic monotone VIP (built from $J=[[0,-1],[1,0]]$ with $F(\cdot;\xi_i)=(\pm\rho I+(1\mp\nu)J)x$) on which S-SEG diverges a.s. with $\|x_k\|\sim(\log N)^q$, $q=\eta(\rho^2-1)>1$, even under DSEG step-sizes $\hat\gamma_k=1/\log(k+k_0)$, $\gamma_k=\eta/(k+k_0)$.

## 核心贡献

- **一般（可能无界）域上的高概率 gap 收敛理论（Theorems 3.3, 3.4, Corollary 3.5）。** 在对称、平方可和的步长 $\hat\gamma_k=\gamma_k$、$\sum\gamma_k^2<\infty$、$k\gamma_k\to\infty$ 下，为 I-SEG（在广义方差界 Assumption 2.6 下）和 S-SEG（在样本一致 Lipschitz 假设 Assumption 2.3 下）分别建立条件期望 gap 界，并通过条件 Markov 不等式得到 $\Pr[\mathrm{Err}_{x^\star,D_\delta/2}(\hat x_N)\le r_N(\delta)/\rho]\ge 1-\delta-\rho$。当 $\gamma_k=\Theta((k+1)^{-\alpha})$、$\tfrac12<\alpha<1$ 时，速率为 $O(N^{-(1-\alpha)})$。
- **两条不可改进性反例（Theorems 3.9, 3.10, 3.12）。** （1）平方可和性不可移除：对 $\hat\gamma_k=\gamma_k=1/\sqrt{k+1}$，I-SEG 和 S-SEG 在某 $X=\mathbb R^2$、$x^\star=0$ 的随机单调 VIP 上都满足 $\|x_k\|\to\infty$、$\limsup\|\hat x_k\|=\infty$ a.s.；（2）即使步长平方可和，由于 $\|x_k\|\to R_\infty$ 的极限半径 $R_\infty$ 分布无界，对任意 $D>0$、$M>0$ 都有 $\Pr[\lim\mathrm{Err}_{x^\star,D}(x_k)>M]>0$，故高概率形式本质不可避免。
- **S-SEG 的几乎必然 last-iterate 收敛（Theorem 4.1）。** 在 quasi-strict 单调性（Assumption 2.8）下，S-SEG 与 $\sum\gamma_k=\infty$、$\sum\hat\gamma_k^2<\infty$、$\sum\gamma_k^2<\infty$ 配合，$x_k\to x^\star\in X^\star$ a.s.。
- **I-SEG 与 S-SEG 的根本分离（Proposition 3.13 + Theorem 4.2）。** I-SEG 在 DSEG 步长下得到 $O((\sum_{k=0}^N\gamma_k)^{-1})$ 期望 gap 界；而对任意 $0<\eta<1/10$，存在 $X=\mathbb R^2$、$x^\star=0$ 的一致 Lipschitz 随机单调 VIP，使得 S-SEG 在 DSEG 步长 $\hat\gamma_k=1/\log(k+k_0)$、$\gamma_k=\eta/(k+k_0)$ 下 $\|x_k\|\to\infty$ 且加权平均 $\hat x_N\to\infty$ a.s.。这证明两次采样的独立性是 DSEG 收敛机制的核心，而非证明技巧。
- **紧域上 S-SEG 对一致 Lipschitz 性的实质性依赖（Proposition 3.2）。** 构造 $X=[0,a]$（$a\ge2$）上均值单调 Lipschitz、方差有界、$x^\star=0$ 唯一，但样本算子有跳跃不连续的 VIP；从正测度集 $Y=[1,a]$ 出发，S-SEG 迭代永远困在 $Y$ 内，$\liminf\|\hat x_N\|\ge\tfrac12$ a.s.。说明 Assumption 2.3 不是证明痕迹。

## Key Contributions

- **Sharp restricted-gap theory on general closed, convex, possibly unbounded domains** (Theorems 3.3, 3.4, Corollary 3.5). Under symmetric square-summable step-sizes $\hat\gamma_k=\gamma_k$ with $\sum\gamma_k^2<\infty$ and $k\gamma_k\to\infty$, the paper proves conditional expected gap bounds for I-SEG (under the generalized variance bound Assumption 2.6) and for S-SEG (under uniform samplewise Lipschitzness Assumption 2.3), each on a high-probability event $E_\delta$ with $\Pr[E_\delta]\ge1-\delta$. For $\gamma_k=\Theta((k+1)^{-\alpha})$ with $\tfrac12<\alpha<1$, the conditional expected gap is $O(N^{-(1-\alpha)})$.
- **Two impossibility results showing tightness** (Theorems 3.9, 3.10, 3.12). Square-summability cannot be dropped: with $\hat\gamma_k=\gamma_k=1/\sqrt{k+1}$ on a constructed linear counterexample, both I-SEG and S-SEG satisfy $\|x_k\|\to\infty$ and $\limsup_k\|\hat x_k\|=\infty$ a.s. for every $x_0\neq x^\star$. Even with square-summable symmetric step-sizes, the high-probability form is essentially unavoidable because the limiting radius $R_\infty$ has unbounded support.
- **Almost-sure last-iterate convergence for S-SEG** (Theorem 4.1). Under quasi-strict monotonicity and the standard Robbins–Monro conditions, S-SEG's last iterate converges a.s. to a VIP solution, matching the classical I-SEG guarantee of Mertikopoulos et al. and Kannan–Shanbhag.
- **Fundamental I-SEG/S-SEG separation under DSEG step-sizes** (Proposition 3.13 vs. Theorem 4.2). I-SEG with the asymmetric DSEG step-size rule of Hsieh et al. (2020) enjoys an expected restricted-gap bound of $O((\sum_{k=0}^N\gamma_k)^{-1})$ on the weighted average. In sharp contrast, on a constructed uniformly Lipschitz stochastic monotone VIP with unique solution $x^\star=0$, S-SEG with $\hat\gamma_k=1/\log(k+k_0)$, $\gamma_k=\eta/(k+k_0)$ satisfies $\|x_k\|\to\infty$ a.s. with growth $\|x_k\|\sim(\log N)^q$ where $q=\eta(\rho^2-1)>1$, and the weighted average $\hat x_N\to\infty$ a.s. — independence of the two samples is a central ingredient of DSEG's convergence, not a proof convenience.
- **Uniform Lipschitzness is substantive for S-SEG, even on compact domains** (Proposition 3.2). A 1-D counterexample on $X=[0,a]$ with monotone 1-Lipschitz mean operator, bounded variance, unique solution $x^\star=0$, but discontinuous sample operators shows S-SEG iterates initialized in $Y=[1,a]$ remain in $Y$ forever and $\liminf\|\hat x_N\|\ge\tfrac12$ a.s. — so Assumption 2.3 cannot be replaced by bounded variance.

## Methodology

The paper is purely theoretical (no experiments) and proceeds by interleaving new convergence theorems with matching counterexamples.

- **Lyapunov/descent inequality (Lemma 3.1).** The starting point is a one-step identity for SEG using firm non-expansiveness of projection: $\langle\gamma_k F(\hat x_k),\hat x_k-u\rangle$ is bounded by a telescoping norm term plus a noise inner product $\langle F(\hat x_k)-F(\hat x_k;\xi_{i_k}),\hat x_k-u\rangle$. For S-SEG the same sample $\xi_{i_k}$ appears in both extrapolation and update steps, so this noise term does **not** vanish under conditional expectation — the "bias term" (7), $\langle F(\hat x_k;\xi_{\hat i_k})-F(\hat x_k),x_k\rangle$, is identified as the fundamental obstacle (Section 2.2.3).
- **Compact-domain S-SEG analysis (Section 3.1.1).** Adding $\langle\gamma_k F(\hat x_k;\xi_{i_k}),\hat x_k-u\rangle$, using $\hat i_k=i_k$, applying Young's inequality and uniform Lipschitzness to control the cross term, telescoping, and using a martingale-difference bound (Nemirovski–Juditsky–Lan–Shapiro lemma) to control the noise, yields Proposition 2.11 and its $O((LD^2+\sigma^2/L+\sigma D)/\sqrt N)$ corollaries.
- **High-probability recipe for unbounded domains (Section 3.2.1).** When $X$ is unbounded one cannot uniformly bound $\|x_k-x^\star\|^2$. The key device is Lemma 3.6, a Robbins–Siegmund-type supermartingale lemma whose part (ii) gives, for deterministic step-sizes, $\sup_k\|x_k-x^\star\|^2\le R_\delta=(a_0+C)e^D/\delta$ with probability $\ge1-\delta$ (Ville's inequality). Lemmas 3.7/3.8 establish the one-step recursion $E[\|x_{k+1}-x^\star\|^2|\mathcal F_k]\le(1+d_k)\|x_k-x^\star\|^2-b_k+c_k$ with $\sum d_k,\sum c_k<\infty$. Conditioned on $E_\delta$, the iterates stay in a fixed ball $B(x^\star,D_\delta/2)$, so the compact-domain analysis applies to give the conditional gap bound. Markov's inequality then yields Corollary 3.5.
- **Counterexample construction (Lemma 3.11 and onwards).** All impossibility results are built on a single 2-D linear stochastic VIP identified with $\mathbb C$: $J=[[0,-1],[1,0]]$, $F(x;\xi_1)=(\rho I+(1+\nu)J)x$, $F(x;\xi_2)=(-\rho I+(1-\nu)J)x$, mean $F(x)=Jx$, unique solution $0$. Writing $z_k=z(x_k)\in\mathbb C$, the SEG recursion becomes scalar complex multiplication $z_{k+1}=b_{s_k}(\gamma_k)z_k$ (S-SEG) or $c_{u_k,s_k}(\gamma_k)z_k$ (I-SEG). Convergence/divergence then reduces to whether $\sum\log|b_{s_k}|$ (or $|c_{u_k,s_k}|$) drifts to $-\infty$, which is analyzed via Taylor expansion of $|b_s|^2$ and Kronecker's lemma on $\sum s_k/\sqrt{k+1}$ (zero-mean, variance summable). The crucial asymmetry: for S-SEG the $\gamma^2$ coefficient in $\log|b_s|$ is $+\tfrac32>0$ (repulsive), while for I-SEG it is $-\tfrac52<0$ (attractive).
- **Last-iterate convergence under quasi-strict monotonicity (Section 4.1).** A second application of Lemma 3.6 to $a_k=\|x_k-z\|^2$ plus the strict-coherence condition $\langle F(x),x-x^\star\rangle>0$ for $x\notin X^\star$ gives a contradiction argument (any limit point away from $X^\star$ would make $\sum\gamma_k\langle F(x_k),x_k-z_0\rangle=\infty$), proving $x_k\to x_\infty\in X^\star$.

## Results & Highlights

- **High-probability gap rate on unbounded domains (Corollary 3.5).** For either I-SEG (under Assumption 2.6, generalized variance $E\|F(x_k)-F(x_k;\xi_{i_k})\|^2\le A\|x_k-x^\star\|^2+\sigma^2$) or S-SEG (under uniform Lipschitzness Assumption 2.3), with $\hat\gamma_k=\gamma_k=\Theta((k+1)^{-\alpha})$, $\tfrac12<\alpha<1$, for any $\delta,\rho\in(0,1)$: $\Pr[\mathrm{Err}_{x^\star,D_\delta/2}(\hat x_N)\le r_N(\delta)/\rho]\ge1-\delta-\rho$, where $r_N(\delta)=O(N^{-(1-\alpha)})$. Theorem 3.3's iterate-bound radius is $D_\delta^2=\tfrac14(\|x_0-x^\star\|^2+6\sigma_\star^2\Gamma)e^{6(A+2L^2)\Gamma}$.
- **S-SEG compact-domain rate (Corollary 2.12).** With constant step-size $\gamma=1/(L\sqrt{N+1})$: $E[\mathrm{Err}(\hat x_N)]\le\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{N+1}}(9LD^2+\tfrac{2\sigma^2}{L}+\sigma D)=O((LD^2+\sigma^2/L+\sigma D)/\sqrt N)$, where $L$ is the **uniform** Lipschitz constant of the sample operators (compare to I-SEG's classical $O(D(M+\sigma)/\sqrt N+LD^2/N)$ of Juditsky–Nemirovski–Tauvel, where the dominant term has no $L$).
- **Square-summability is necessary (Theorems 3.9, 3.10).** On $X=\mathbb R^2$ with $F(x;\xi_1)=(2I+J)x$, $F(x;\xi_2)=(-2I+J)x$ (mean $F=Jx$, unique solution $0$, $L=\sqrt5$), $\hat\gamma_k=\gamma_k=1/\sqrt{k+1}$: S-SEG satisfies $\|x_k\|\ge N^{3/2-o(1)}\to\infty$ a.s. and $\limsup\|\hat x_k\|=\infty$ a.s.; I-SEG on the same problem with the same step-size also diverges a.s. (multiplier $|c_{u,s}(\gamma)|^2=1-4s\gamma+(3+8us)\gamma^2-20u\gamma^3+25\gamma^4$).
- **High-probability form is unavoidable (Theorem 3.12).** On the same problem with $\hat\gamma_k=\gamma_k=\eta/(k+1)$ small, $\|x_k\|\to R_\infty/\sqrt{1+\eta^2}$ and $\|\hat x_k\|\to R_\infty/\sqrt{1+\eta^2}$ a.s. for a finite positive random $R_\infty$, but the law of $R_\infty$ is unbounded, so for every $D,M>0$: $\Pr[\lim_k\mathrm{Err}_{x^\star,D}(\hat x_k)>M]>0$. No deterministic bounded region makes the gap a meaningful convergence measure a.s.
- **DSEG works for I-SEG, fails for S-SEG (Proposition 3.13 vs. Theorem 4.2).** I-SEG with $\hat\gamma_k\in(0,1/(3L))$, $\sum\gamma_k\hat\gamma_k=\infty$, $\sum\gamma_k^2<\infty$, $\sum\gamma_k\hat\gamma_k^2<\infty$: $E[\mathrm{Err}_{B(y,D)}(\hat x_N)]\le C_{y,D}/\Gamma_N$, $\Gamma_N=\sum_{k=0}^N\gamma_k$. For $\gamma_k=\gamma_0(k+1)^{-a}$, $\hat\gamma_k=\hat\gamma_0(k+1)^{-b}$ with $\tfrac12<a<1$, $a+b\le1$, $a+2b>1$: rate $O(N^{-(1-a)})$. **But** for any $0<\eta<1/10$ and any integer $\rho\ge2$ with $q=\eta(\rho^2-1)>1$, S-SEG with $\hat\gamma_k=1/\log(k+k_0)$, $\gamma_k=\eta/(k+k_0)$ on $F(x;\xi_1)=(\rho I+J)x$, $F(x;\xi_2)=(-\rho I+J)x$: $z_N=C(\log N)^q N^{-i\eta}(1+o(1))$ a.s. for a nonzero finite random $C$, so $\|x_N\|\to\infty$ a.s., and the DSEG-weighted average also diverges $\hat x_N\to\infty$ a.s.
- **Bounded variance insufficient for S-SEG on a compact domain (Proposition 3.2).** On $X=[0,a]$, $F(x;\xi_1)=2x$ for $x<1$ and $-1$ for $x\ge1$; $F(x;\xi_2)=0$ for $x<1$ and $3$ for $x\ge1$. Mean $F=\min\{x,1\}$ is monotone and 1-Lipschitz, $x^\star=0$ unique, $|F(x;\xi_i)-F(x)|\le a$ (bounded variance). Both sample operators have jump discontinuities (not Lipschitz). For any $x_0\in Y=[1,a]$, S-SEG iterates stay in $Y$ for all $k$, and $\liminf\|\hat x_N\|\ge\tfrac12$ a.s. — S-SEG never reaches the solution from a positive-measure set of initial points.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: high.** The S-SEG theory on unbounded domains (Theorem 3.4), the almost-sure S-SEG convergence (Theorem 4.1), and the DSEG-failure counterexample (Theorem 4.2) appear genuinely new. The DSEG separation result is the most striking contribution: it overturns the natural intuition that the asymmetric step-size trick of Hsieh et al. (2020), which fixes I-SEG's last-iterate non-convergence, would also fix S-SEG. The proof that $z_N=C(\log N)^q N^{-i\eta}$ is a clean, self-contained calculation.
- **Rigor: very high.** Every positive theorem is paired with an impossibility result (Theorems 3.3/3.4 with 3.9/3.10/3.12; Theorem 4.1 with 4.2), a commendable "show both directions" style rare in this area. Proofs are detailed (Lemma 3.6 Robbins–Siegmund with explicit Ville's-inequality radius; the $\mathbb C$-identification of Lemma 3.11 with exact multiplier formulas $|b_s|^2=1-4s\gamma+11\gamma^2-20s\gamma^3+25\gamma^4$; the harmonic-series/Taylor-expansion machinery).
- **A subtle gap worth noting.** Proposition 2.14 (Mishchenko et al. 2020) is criticized for bounding $\mathrm{Err}_{B(y,D)}(E[\hat x_k])$ rather than $E[\mathrm{Err}_{B(y,D)}(\hat x_k)]$ — the authors correctly point out (using [4, Example 1.1], the algorithm that outputs $\pm(k,k)$ with probability $\tfrac12$) that this weaker guarantee is vacuous. The present paper's Theorem 3.4 genuinely fixes this. However, the comparison in Table 1 lists Proposition 2.14 under "X unbounded / S-SEG" with a one-line dismissal; a more head-on re-derivation under the stronger $E[\mathrm{Err}]$ measure (which Theorem 3.4 effectively is) would have made the contribution's delta even crisper.
- **Reproducibility: strong in principle, weak in tooling.** All results are theorems with full proofs; counterexamples are 2-D linear problems fully specified by $(\rho,\nu)$ and the step-size sequence, and could be reproduced in <50 lines of NumPy. But the paper provides no code, no figure-generation script, and only **two** figures (Figure 1a/1b trajectories) — a notebook reproducing Figures 1, the divergence-rate curves $\|x_k\|\sim N^{3/2}$ and $\sim(\log N)^q$, and the Proposition 3.2 trapped-region dynamics would substantially help readers internalize the separations.
- **Significance: targeted.** The audience is the stochastic-VIP/minimax-optimization theory community. The results do not change the practitioner's recipe (one still uses I-SEG or single-call variants in practice), but they close real gaps: they explain **why** existing I-SEG proofs do not transfer, and they identify uniform Lipschitzness and sample independence as load-bearing assumptions rather than conveniences. This is the kind of paper that future SEG analyses will have to cite.
- **Red flags / overclaiming: minimal.** Claims are carefully scoped ("there exists a stochastic VIP"), the abstract does not oversell, and the authors explicitly flag in the conclusion that whether S-SEG's DSEG-divergence can be rescued by *some other* step-size rule remains open. The one mild overreach is calling S-SEG "at least as natural as I-SEG and may even be preferable in practice" — the paper's own negative results argue this claim needs empirical backing that is absent.

## Strengths

- Each positive theorem is matched with a matching lower bound / counterexample, giving the reader a clear picture of exactly where the boundary between convergence and divergence lies. Table 1 is an excellent map of the state of the art.
- The unifying 2-D complex counterexample (Lemma 3.11) is elegant and reused for Theorems 3.9, 3.10, 3.12, and 4.2 — a single building block that cleanly exposes the I-SEG/S-SEG asymmetry via the sign of the $\gamma^2$ coefficient in $\log|b_s|$.
- The identification of the "bias term" (7) as the structural reason S-SEG is harder to analyze (and harder to converge) is an insightful framing that will guide future work on stochastic OG and variance-reduced SEG.
- Honest scoping of assumptions: Theorem 3.4 needs uniform Lipschitzness (Assumption 2.3), Theorem 3.3 only needs the weaker generalized variance (Assumption 2.6), and the paper does not try to hide this asymmetry.

## Limitations

- **No experiments.** This is a pure theory paper; the only figures are two 2-D trajectory plots (Figure 1). The practical implication "S-SEG may diverge where I-SEG converges" is not demonstrated on any realistic minimax/GAN problem, so it is unclear how often the constructed counterexamples are representative of real workloads.
- **Counterexamples are low-dimensional and somewhat pathological.** The linear VIPs in Lemma 3.11 have antisymmetric (rotation) mean operator $Jx$; the Proposition 3.2 example uses deliberately discontinuous sample operators. A reader could legitimately ask whether a "typical" finite-sum VIP from machine learning satisfies the divergence-triggering structure.
- **High-probability bounds have exponentially large constants.** $D_\delta^2\propto e^{6(A+2L^2)\Gamma}/\delta$ (Theorem 3.3) and $e^{24L^2\Gamma}/\delta$ (Theorem 3.4); for non-trivial $\Gamma$ these constants are huge, so the rate $O(N^{-(1-\alpha)})$ is an asymptotic statement, not a practical one.
- **No treatment of structured/non-monotone operators, cocoercivity, or acceleration.** The paper stays strictly within monotone + Lipschitz + (optionally) quasi-strict. Whether the I-SEG/S-SEG separation persists under strong monotonicity, minty-variational-stability, or for the Halpern/accelerated SEG family is left open.
- **Single-call variants (stochastic optimistic gradient) are explicitly deferred** (Conclusion) — SOG under unbounded variance on unbounded domains is an open problem the authors flag but do not address.

## Relevance

本文对随机 extragradient 在单调变分不等式（含 minimax/鞍点优化）上的收敛理论进行了系统刻画，对研究随机优化、minimax 训练（如 GAN、对抗学习）的理论基础与算法设计有直接价值；其同样本 vs 独立样本的分离结论对实际实现中"是否复用同一个 minibatch 做两次算子评估"这一工程选择具有指导意义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the S-SEG divergence under DSEG step-sizes (Theorem 4.2) persist when the same sample is replaced by the same *mini-batch* of size $B>1$? Is there a critical batch size $B^\star(\eta,\rho)$ above which S-SEG recovers convergence, and how does it scale?
- The I-SEG/S-SEG asymmetry is traced to the sign of the $\gamma^2$ coefficient in $\log|b_s|$ (positive for S-SEG, negative for I-SEG). Does the same sign-flip analysis extend to **stochastic optimistic gradient (SOG)** / Popov's method, and can one predict a priori which same-sample single-call variant will inherit the S-SEG pathology?
- The high-probability bounds have constants exponential in $\Gamma=\sum\gamma_k^2$ (Theorems 3.3/3.4). Are these exponential constants tight, or is there a tighter martingale concentration (e.g., Freedman-style) that yields polynomial-in-$1/\delta$ bounds?
- Proposition 3.2 shows bounded variance is insufficient for S-SEG on a compact domain with **discontinuous** sample operators. Is the failure robust to small smoothing of the jumps (i.e., does S-SEG converge but arbitrarily slowly, or is there a sharp transition)?
- Can the DSEG-rescue for I-SEG be ported to S-SEG via a deliberately **correlated** sampling scheme $\xi_{\hat i_k}\neq\xi_{i_k}$ that is cheaper than full independence (e.g., antithetic or stratified) — recovering I-SEG-like behavior at one extra sample per iteration rather than two?
- All counterexamples use the rotation operator $F(x)=Jx$ with mean eigenvalues purely imaginary. Does the separation survive when $F$ has a strictly monotone component (positive real eigenvalues) added to the rotation, i.e., is the boundary captured by a spectral condition on $F$?
