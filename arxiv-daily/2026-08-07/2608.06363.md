> **一句话总结：** 本文构造了一个确定性、improper 的 PAC 学习器，在 VC 维数为 d 的任意二元假设类上达到与 Devroye-Györfi-Lugosi 下界相匹配的最优超额风险界 $L^*\sqrt{(d+\log(1/\delta))/n} + (d+\log(1/\delta))/n$，从而在所有固定 $L^*$ 处（直到常数因子）彻底解决了不可知 PAC 学习的样本复杂度问题；关键新工具是一类依赖类别的、将诱导边数与到 trace 的 Hamming 距离及投影 Rademacher 宽度联系起来的布尔立方体边等周不等式。

> **TL;DR:** The authors give the first learner (deterministic, improper, agnostic) that, for any binary hypothesis class of VC dimension d, achieves the statistically optimal excess-risk bound $L(\hat h) \le L^* + C\big(\sqrt{L^*(d+\log(1/\delta))/n} + (d+\log(1/\delta))/n\big)$ from n i.i.d. samples with probability $1-\delta$, matching the 1996 Devroye-Györfi-Lugosi lower bound (the "minimax" comparator) at every value of the best-in-class risk $L^*$, including the realizable ($L^*=0$) and fully agnostic regimes; the prior best upper bound (Hanneke-Larsen-Zhivotovskiy, FOCS 2024) was suboptimal by a polylogarithmic factor in the fast-rate term, and ERM was off by a $\log(1/L^*)$ factor. No dataset/benchmark in the empirical sense — the contribution is a sharp minimax theoretical guarantee.

## 核心贡献

- **解决了不可知 PAC 学习的最优样本复杂度问题**：构造出首个在所有固定 $L^*$ 取值下（包括可实现情形 $L^*=0$、完全不可知情形，以及两者之间的所有中间速率）都与 Devroye-Györfi-Lugosi 下界匹配（至多差一个普适常数）的高概率超额风险界。这是一个长期悬而未决的开放问题。
- **给出确定性、improper 的学习器**：算法不使用 $L^*$ 也不使用 $\delta$，对任意分布、任意 $n \ge 1$、任意 $0<\delta<1$ 都以至少 $1-\delta$ 的概率成立；具体界为 $L(\hat h) \le L^* + 2\cdot10^8\big(\sqrt{L^*(d+\log(48/\delta))/n} + (d+\log(48/\delta))/n\big)$（定理 1）。
- **关键新工具——依赖类别的布尔立方体边等周不等式（Lemma 2.1）**：构造一组权重 $w_{v,p}\in[0,1]$，同时满足 $w_{v,p}+w_{v\oplus p,p}=1$，以及对任意坐标集 $D$，$\sum_{p\in D} w_{v,p} \le \rho_F(v) + R_D(F)$（到 trace 的 Hamming 距离 + 投影 Rademacher 宽度）。这是把 Long 的立方体定向与 Dughmi-Kalayci-York 的全坐标 Rademacher 论证统一起来的核心引理。
- **得到系数为 1 的留一界（Theorem 2）**：由此导出 $\hat L_S^{LOO} \le \hat L_S^* + 120\sqrt{d\hat L_S^*/n} + 7202\sqrt{d/n}$，即对经验最优值 $L^*$ 的系数恰为 1（Long 的旧结果是 15）。
- **完整的 PAC 转化链**：通过后缀平均（suffix averaging，自 Aden-Ali 等的 FOCS 2023 工作）+ 反向/前向鞅（Lemma 3.1, 3.2）保留了对比较器方差的依赖，最后用嵌套阈值类上的局部熵界（Lemma 3.3）去随机化，避免了额外的 $\log$ 因子。

## Key Contributions

- **Resolves the optimal sample complexity of agnostic PAC learning up to universal constants**, at every fixed value of the best-in-class risk $L^*$. This settles a long-standing open problem in statistical learning theory: whether any learner can match the Devroye-Györfi-Lugosi (1996) lower bound $\sqrt{L^*(d+\log(1/\delta))/n} + (d+\log(1/\delta))/n$ across the realizable, intermediate, and fully agnostic regimes simultaneously.
- **Constructs an explicit deterministic, generally improper learner** that uses neither $L^*$ nor $\delta$. Theorem 1 achieves $L(\hat h) \le L^* + 2\cdot10^8\big(\sqrt{L^*(d+\log(48/\delta))/n} + (d+\log(48/\delta))/n\big)$ for every distribution, $n\ge1$, $0<\delta<1$, with probability $\ge 1-\delta$. The abstract's $7\cdot10^8$ constant follows from $\log(48/\delta)\le 27(d+\log(1/\delta))$ for $\delta\le 1/2$.
- **Introduces a new class-dependent edge isoperimetric inequality on the Boolean cube (Lemma 2.1)** that unifies two prior incomparable threads: Long's (1999) cube orientation by distance shells and Dughmi-Kalayci-York's (2025) full-coordinate Rademacher-width edge bound. The lemma controls induced edge counts through the sum of the Hamming distance $\rho_F(v)$ to the trace and a *projected* Rademacher width $R_D(F)$ restricted to any coordinate set $D$ — a localization unavailable in either prior result.
- **Derives the first coefficient-one agnostic leave-one-out bound (Theorem 2)**: $\hat L_S^{LOO} \le \hat L_S^* + 120\sqrt{d\hat L_S^*/n} + 7202\sqrt{d/n}$. Long's earlier agnostic one-inclusion bound had a coefficient of 15 on $\hat L_S^*$, which precluded an optimal PAC bound; the new coefficient of exactly one is what makes the gap close.
- **Delivers a self-contained PAC conversion** via suffix averaging with a comparator-dependent variance bound (reverse martingale Lemma 3.1 + forward martingale Lemma 3.2, adapted from Aden-Ali, Cherapanamjeri, Shetty, Zhivotovskiy FOCS 2023), plus a derandomization over nested threshold classifiers whose local-entropy analysis (Lemma 3.3) incurs no logarithmic factor in sample size.

## Methodology

The paper is a pure statistical-learning-theory construction with three chained components; there are no experiments. The hypothesis class is $H \subseteq \{-1,+1\}^{\mathcal X}$ with VC dimension $d\ge1$, $L(h)=\mathbb P(h(X)\ne Y)$, $L^*=\min_{h\in H}L(h)$, sample $S=((X_i,Y_i))_{i=1}^n$ i.i.d.

**1. Class-dependent edge isoperimetry (Section 2, Lemma 2.1).** Fix a finite coordinate set $P$ and a trace $F\subseteq\{-1,+1\}^P$ with VC$(F)\le d$. Let $\rho_F(v)=\min_{f\in F}|\mathrm{DIS}(v,f)|$ be the Hamming distance to $F$, and $R_D(F)=\mathbb E_\varepsilon \max_{f\in F}\sum_{p\in D}\varepsilon_p f(p)$ the projected Rademacher width. The lemma constructs weights $w_{v,p}\in[0,1]$ by a random-restriction argument: pick each coordinate independently with probability $1/2$, map $v$ to its nearest trace element on the restricted coordinates $\pi_T(v)$, and set $w_{v,p}=\mathbb P_T(p\in\mathrm{DIS}(v,\pi_T(v))\mid p\notin T)$. These weights satisfy (i) the orientation-completeness identity $w_{v,p}+w_{v\oplus p,p}=1$, and (ii) for every coordinate set $D$, $\sum_{p\in D}w_{v,p}\le\rho_F(v)+R_D(F)$. Combined with the classical bound $R_D(F)\le60\sqrt{d|D|}$ (the only point where VC dimension enters), the Hall-orientation criterion yields a deterministic cube orientation with outdegree $\le \rho_F(v)+120\sqrt{d\rho_F(v)}+7202d$ (Theorem 2). Applied to the trace $F=\{(h(x_1),\ldots,h(x_n)):h\in H\}$ this gives the coefficient-one leave-one-out bound.

**2. From leave-one-out to a randomized PAC bound (Section 3.1-3.2, Theorem 3).** The orientation need not be permutation-invariant, so the rule is symmetrized by appending the query point and averaging over uniform permutations. The symmetrized score $q(x;S)\in[-1,1]$ satisfies (13). A randomized predictor $\hat p(x)=k^{-1}\sum_{t=k}^{2k-1} q_t(x)$ averages these scores over the suffix $t=k,\ldots,2k-1$. Two martingale lemmas control the deviation: a *reverse* martingale (Lemma 3.1, exposing the permutation backward, using Freedman's inequality and Bernstein on the binomial error count) and a *forward* martingale (Lemma 3.2, a Bernstein MGF bound along sample prefixes with a comparator-dependent variance term). Union-bounding both yields Theorem 3 with constant 17000.

**3. Derandomization by thresholded validation (Section 3.3, Lemma 3.3).** Theorem 3 gives a $[-1,1]$-valued predictor; Theorem 1 needs a binary classifier. The naive majority-vote derandomization loses a factor of two on $L^*$, harmless when $L^*=0$ but fatal in the agnostic regime. Instead, the score $\hat p$ is fixed and an ERM is run over the nested threshold class $\mathcal T_{\hat p}=\{x\mapsto 2\mathbf 1\{\hat p(x)\ge u\}-1\}$ on an independent validation block of size $k$. The proof of Lemma 3.3 exploits that thresholds above and below the minimizer form two nested, mutually disjoint disagreement families, giving an empirical $L_2$ cover of size $1+\sqrt{q}/u^2$ — a *local* entropy bound with no extra $\log n$. Plugging into Bartlett-Bousquet-Mendelson (Theorem 3.3 of [7]) gives the bound with constant 223000. The final Theorem 1 splits $n$ into $2k-1$ training + $k$ validation observations ($k=\lfloor n/3\rfloor$) and combines Theorem 3 (at confidence $\delta/16$) with Lemma 3.3 (at $\delta/4$), yielding the constant $2\cdot10^8$ in (2).

## Results & Highlights

This is a theoretical paper with no empirical benchmarks; the "results" are sharp quantitative risk bounds. The concrete quantitative claims are:

- **Main theorem (Theorem 1, restated in the abstract):** For every binary class of VC dimension $d\ge1$, there is a deterministic improper learner (using neither $L^*$ nor $\delta$) such that, from $n$ i.i.d. samples, with probability $\ge1-\delta$, $L(\hat h)\le L^* + 7\cdot10^8\big(\sqrt{L^*(d+\log(1/\delta))/n} + (d+\log(1/\delta))/n\big)$ for every $0<\delta\le1/2$. This matches the lower bound (1) of Devroye-Györfi-Lugosi up to the universal constant $7\cdot10^8$, **eliminating the polylogarithmic gap** present in the prior best upper bound of Hanneke-Larsen-Zhivotovskiy (FOCS 2024), which had an extra factor $\log^5(n/d)$ on the fast-rate term $(d+\log(1/\delta))/n$.
- **Coefficient-one leave-one-out (Theorem 2, eq. 12):** For every $n\ge1$ and input sequence, there is a deterministic leave-one-out rule with $\hat L_S^{LOO} \le \hat L_S^* + 120\sqrt{d\hat L_S^*/n} + 7202\sqrt{d/n}$. The leading constant on $\hat L_S^*$ is exactly 1, versus 15 in Long's (1999) prior construction — the constant-1 coefficient is the structural obstruction that previously prevented an optimal bound.
- **Randomized predictor bound (Theorem 3, eq. 18):** The suffix-averaged predictor $\hat p$ built from $2k-1$ observations satisfies $L(\hat p)\le L^* + 17000\big(\sqrt{L^*(d+\log(3/\delta))/k} + (d+\log(3/\delta))/k\big)$ with probability $\ge1-\delta$.
- **Tightness characterization:** The bound is simultaneously optimal in three regimes — when $L^*=0$ it recovers the optimal realizable PAC rate $(d+\log(1/\delta))/n$ (Hanneke 2016, Simon 2015, Larsen 2023, Rawal-Zhivotovskiy 2026); when $L^*$ is bounded away from zero it recovers the optimal agnostic rate $\sqrt{(d+\log(1/\delta))/n}$; and it gives the correct interpolated intermediate rates for the first time.
- **AI-disclosure result (Appendix A):** On August 6, 2026, the authors tested a single compressed prompt (stating Lemma 2.1 without proof hints) in 16 runs of GPT-5.6 Sol Pro; in **11 of 16 runs** the model produced an essentially correct proof outline for Lemma 2.1 and completed the remaining steps, averaging **123 minutes per run** — a documented, reproducible datapoint on frontier-LLM capability on an open research-grade theory problem.

No quantitative results reported in the empirical-experimental sense; all quantitative claims are theoretical risk bounds proven within the paper and matched against the cited lower bound (1).

## Authors & Credibility

The authorship is well-matched to the problem. **Nikita Zhivotovskiy** (UC Berkeley) is a central figure in the recent wave of sharp PAC bounds: he co-authored the prior best agnostic upper bound being improved here (Hanneke-Larsen-Zhivotovskiy, FOCS 2024 [19]), the suffix-averaging technique the paper adapts (Aden-Ali-Cherapanamjeri-Shetty-Zhivotovskiy, FOCS 2023 [2]), and the recent "Majority-of-Three is optimal" result (Rawal-Zhivotovskiy 2026 [24]). **Markus Engelund Mathiasen** (Aarhus, supported by ERC TUCLA grant 101125203) brings the empirical/LLM-exploration methodology documented in Appendix A. **Jian Qian** (HKU AI/Data Science) rounds out the team. The result sits squarely in this group's active research program, which both raises confidence in correctness and explains the speed of the resolution. The paper is a 17-page self-contained manuscript with full proofs of all three numbered theorems and three lemmas; the proof of Lemma 3.3 in particular re-derives the constants in Bartlett-Bousquet-Mendelson rather than hiding behind a citation.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty — high.** The conceptual innovation is Lemma 2.1, a genuinely new edge-isoperimetric inequality on the Boolean cube that interpolates between Long's distance-shell orientation and Dughmi-Kalayci-York's full-width bound. Remark 2.3 lays out honestly which pieces were anticipated: Haussler-Littlestone-Warmuth controlled $|E_U|$ only for $U\subseteq F$; Long's orientation applied to all $U$ but had coefficient 15 on $\rho_F$ (the obstruction to optimality); Asilis-Devic-Dughmi-Sharan-Teng had the omitted-coordinate fractional orientation but not (6) uniformly in $D$; Dughmi-Kalayci-York had the full-width all-coordinate bound but not the vertexwise-localized $D=D_U(v)$. Lemma 2.1's simultaneous weighted assignment for *every* $D$ is the precise new step. This is not an incremental tightening.

- **Rigor — very high.** All constants are explicit and tracked through the argument; no "the details are routine" hand-waving in the load-bearing martingale steps. The proof of Lemma 3.1 carefully separates the conditional-on-$W$ Freedman application from the unconditional Bernstein bound on the binomial error count (eq. 21), and the final constants (17000, 223000, $2\cdot10^8$) are obtained by explicit numerical substitution rather than asymptotic hand-waving.

- **Reproducibility — strong for theory, with a caveat.** The learner is fully specified (deterministic orientation chosen as the first feasible one in a fixed ordering; ERM with ties resolved by a fixed rule). The Appendix A AI disclosure is unusually transparent: a single 50-line prompt is printed in full, and the 11/16 success rate over 16 runs is a concrete, checkable claim. The caveat is that the orientation, while deterministic, requires solving a Hall-matching feasibility problem on the full $2^n$-vertex cube, so the algorithm is *not* computationally efficient — a point the paper does not dwell on (it is a statistical, not computational, result).

- **Significance — this is a milestone for the subfield.** The optimal sample complexity of PAC learning has been a central target since Valiant (1984) and Vapnik-Chervonenkis (1971); the realizable case was closed by Hanneke (2016) and the agnostic case is now closed (up to constants) by this paper. The interpolation across all $L^*$ is the part no prior technique could achieve.

- **Red flags / honest weaknesses:**
  1. **Constants are astronomically large.** The leading universal constant is $7\cdot10^8$; even the cleaner Theorem 1 statement has $2\cdot10^8$. These are far from the lower-bound constant $c$ in (1). This is harmless for the "up to universal constants" claim but means the result is purely asymptotic — for any practical $n$ the bound is vacuous. The paper does not claim otherwise, but a reader should not mistake "optimal" for "tight."
  2. **No computational efficiency.** The algorithm requires orienting a graph with $2^n$ vertices, so it is not implementable. The paper is correctly framed as a statistical-possibility result, but this limitation is worth stating plainly given the "Algorithm" framing.
  3. **Binary classification only.** The extension to multiclass (where the relevant parameter is the graph / Natarajan dimension and the lower-bound landscape is less settled) is left open. Asilis et al. [4,5] are cited for partial multicolor precedents.
  4. **Reliance on LLM assistance.** Appendix A discloses that GPT-5.5/5.6 was used to discover the proof strategy for Lemma 2.1 (11/16 runs succeeded). This is a legitimate and increasingly common research practice, fully disclosed, and the final proofs are human-written and checkable — but it is worth noting that the *discovery* path was AI-assisted, which is unusual for a learning-theory milestone and will draw scrutiny from reviewers.
  5. **Lower-bound attribution is slightly indirect.** Footnote 2 acknowledges that the exact form of the lower bound (1) "is not stated in these references" [Devroye-Györfi-Lugosi] but is assembled from their dimension-dependent construction combined with their deviation and realizable lower bounds. A reader checking tightness should verify this assembly rather than cite (1) as a verbatim theorem.

## Strengths

- Closes a genuinely open, 30-year-old problem (optimal agnostic PAC rate across all $L^*$ regimes) with a clean, self-contained proof.
- Lemma 2.1 is a reusable tool likely to find applications beyond this paper (e.g., multiclass, online, transductive settings where edge-isoperimetric arguments recur).
- Full constants, full proofs, no hidden lemmas; unusually rigorous even by learning-theory standards.
- Remark 2.3 gives an honest, detailed map of precedents, making the precise novelty easy to locate.
- Appendix A's AI disclosure is exemplary transparency — the full prompt and the 11/16 success rate are exactly the kind of reproducibility detail the field should normalize.

## Limitations

- **Vacuous constants.** The $7\cdot10^8$ leading factor makes the bound numerically useless at any practical sample size; the contribution is purely about rates.
- **Computational intractability.** Orienting the full $2^n$-vertex cube is exponential; the learner is a statistical existence construction, not a deployable algorithm. A computationally efficient optimal learner remains open.
- **Binary classification only.** Multiclass, regression, and asymmetric-loss extensions are not addressed.
- **Lower-bound exactness is assembled, not quoted.** Tightness rests on a combination argument (footnote 2) rather than a single published lower-bound theorem.
- **Deterministic but tie-rule-dependent.** The "first feasible orientation in a fixed ordering" and the ERM tie-breaking rule are deterministic but not canonical; different fixed rules give different (equally valid) classifiers.

这篇论文属于统计学习理论的核心基础工作，与"不可知 PAC 学习的最优样本复杂度"以及 VC 维、one-inclusion graph、Rademacher 复杂度等用户研究兴趣高度相关——它正是把 ERM/经验过程方法与 one-inclusion graph 组合方法统一起来、并补齐了中间速率的关键一环，对该领域的研究者属于必读级成果。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the leading constant $7\cdot10^8$ be substantially reduced? In particular, is the Hall-capacity rounding in Theorem 2 (which inflates constants via $\lceil\cdot\rceil$ and the $1/(7202d)$ shell) a necessary source of slack, or an artifact of the proof technique?
- Is there a *computationally efficient* learner achieving the same rate? The current construction requires orienting a $2^n$-vertex graph; can the one-inclusion orientation be replaced by a polynomial-time surrogate (e.g., via approximate matching or a simpler improper aggregation)?
- Does Lemma 2.1 extend to the multiclass one-inclusion graph, where the relevant combinatorial parameter is the Natarajan/graph dimension? The Asilis et al. [4,5] and Dughmi-Kalayci-York [15] multicolor precedents suggest a route, but the projected-width localization is the open step.
- The reverse-and-forward martingale conversion (Lemmas 3.1-3.2) is adapted from the realizable suffix-averaging proof of Aden-Ali et al. [2]. Could a direct localized-entropy analysis (avoiding the suffix split that costs the factor 3 in sample size, $k=\lfloor n/3\rfloor$) recover the bound with a better constant and without the validation block?
- Appendix A reports 11/16 successful GPT-5.6 runs. What fails in the other 5 runs, and does the failure mode correlate with specific sub-claims of Lemma 2.1? A failure analysis would strengthen the AI-assisted-proof methodology beyond a single success rate.
