> **一句话总结：** 本文证明条件期望算子 (CEO) 的有界性/Hilbert–Schmidt 性质可由条件分布 Radon–Nikodym 密度的正则性 η ∈ L²(ν_Y; H) 直接推出，并在非参数回归、贝叶斯反问题与一致椭圆 SDE 的 Koopman 算子三个场景中验证该条件，从而把 CME 表示假设从"先验假设"变成"可验证条件"，并给出 Galerkin / kernel-EDMD / CME 估计器的显式收敛速率。

> **TL;DR:** A pure-theory unification showing that a conditional expectation operator K is bounded and Hilbert–Schmidt whenever the conditional-density (Radon–Nikodym) sections belong to L²(ν_Y; H) (Theorem 3.3, with the exact norm identity ‖K‖_HS = ‖η‖_{L²(ν_Y;H)}). For Sobolev/RKHS targets this turns the usually-assumed CME representability condition into a checkable Sobolev-regularity assumption (Corollary 3.8), and yields explicit convergence rates — O(h_X^{⌈β⌉−1}) fill-distance bounds for Galerkin/kernel-EDMD with Matérn kernel k^Mat_β (Theorem 3.12), a default n^{-1/2} CME learning rate improving to n^{-2ℓ/(2ℓ+d)} for H_{k_X} ≃ H^ℓ with ℓ > d/2 (Theorem 3.16). The criterion is verified for nonparametric regression, Bayesian inverse problems with Gaussian noise, and uniformly elliptic SDE Koopman operators; there are no numerical experiments.

## 核心贡献

- **密度正则性准则 (Assumption 3.2 + Theorem 3.3)：** 给出条件期望算子 K 的可验证充分条件——只要条件分布的 Radon–Nikodym 密度 η(x,y) 作为 y → H 的 Bochner 映射属于 L²(ν_Y; H)，则 K : L²(ν_Y) → H 有界且 Hilbert–Schmidt，并满足精确恒等式 ‖K‖_{HS} = ‖η‖_{L²(ν_Y;H)}；进一步通过嵌入可推出 K 为迹类 (迹界通过 Maurin 定理) 或作为到 C_b(X) 的算子是紧的。
- **把 CME 表示假设变成可验证条件 (Corollary 3.8)：** 长期以来，基于算子的条件均值嵌入表示 K = C_{XX}^† C_{XY} 需要一个强且难以直接验证的"可表示性"假设 (3.4)。本文证明在 Sobolev 型 RKHS (Matérn / Wendland / Gaussian / Laplace / Cauchy) 下，该假设可由 η ∈ L²(ν_Y; H) 与 H_{k_Y} ↪ L²(ν_Y) 直接推出。
- **三类数值逼近的显式误差界 (Section 3.3)：** 为 (i) 单侧 Galerkin 投影 K̄ = P_X K、(ii) 双侧 Galerkin / kernel-EDMD K̃ = P_X K P_Y、(iii) 正则化 CME 估计 K̂_ε = (Ĉ_{XX}+εI)^{-1} Ĉ_{XY} 给出与密度正则性挂钩的界——投影法的收敛速率由填充距离 h_X 的幂次控制，CME 法的速率由 C_{XX} 特征值衰减控制。
- **三个应用场景的验证 (Section 4)：** 在 (a) 非参数回归 Y = f(X)+ε (源条件 f ∈ H^ℓ(X))、(b) 高斯噪声贝叶斯反问题 X = F(Y)+ε (对数配分函数的多项式增长条件 (4.5))、(c) 一致椭圆 Itô 扩散的 Koopman 半群 (Assumption 4.9 + 抛物正则性给出 η_t 的导数 Gaussian 界) 中分别证明 Assumption 3.2 成立。
- **统一视角：** 把概率论、算子理论、核方法与随机动力系统 (Koopman/EDMD) 中分散的"CEO 何时映射到光滑空间"问题归约为同一个可验证的条件密度正则性条件。

## Key Contributions

- **Density regularity criterion (Assumption 3.2, Theorem 3.3).** A verifiable sufficient condition under which the CEO is bounded, Hilbert–Schmidt (and, via embeddings, trace-class into L² or compact into C_b): the Radon–Nikodym density η(x,y) = dP_{Y|X=x}/dν_Y of the conditional law must lie in the Bochner space L²(ν_Y; H). The proof rewrites K as the integral operator T_η and applies a self-contained Hilbert–Schmidt argument; the norm identity is sharp (‖K‖_HS = ‖η‖).
- **Deriving — not assuming — CME representability (Corollary 3.8).** The operator-based CME representation K = C_{XX}^† C_{XY} of Klebanov et al. (2020) is normally gated by an a priori and hard-to-check representability hypothesis (3.4). The paper shows this hypothesis is a *consequence* of density regularity whenever H_{k_Y} ↪ L²(ν_Y) continuously, which holds automatically for ν_Y = P_Y and is checkable via the spectral-density condition of Proposition 2.4 for Matérn, Wendland, Gaussian, Laplace and Cauchy kernels.
- **Approximation theory for three estimators (Section 3.3).** Explicit error bounds tying convergence rates to density regularity for: (i) one-sided Galerkin projection K̄ = P_X K, (ii) two-sided Galerkin / kernel-EDMD K̃ = P_X K P_Y, and (iii) the regularized empirical CME K̂_ε = (Ĉ_{XX}+εI)^{-1} Ĉ_{XY}. Projection-based rates are governed by fill distance (Theorem 3.12); CME rates by eigenvalue decay of C_{XX} (Theorem 3.16).
- **Verification in three concrete models (Section 4).** The abstract condition is checked in nonparametric regression (Theorem 4.2, source condition f ∈ H^ℓ), Bayesian inverse problems with Gaussian noise (Theorem 4.5, with concrete sufficient conditions in Proposition 4.6 covering bounded F, affine-Gaussian F, moment bounds, and strong log-concavity), and uniformly elliptic Itô diffusions (Theorem 4.11, using classical parabolic PDE derivative bounds from Friedman 1964).
- **Unification.** Provides a single analytical bridge between probability (Radon–Nikodym regularity), operator theory (Hilbert–Schmidt / trace class), kernel methods (CME representability), and data-driven stochastic dynamics (Koopman / EDMD approximation).

## Methodology

The paper is purely theoretical and built on functional analysis. The central methodological move is to rewrite the CEO (2.1) as an integral operator K = T_η with kernel η(x,y) = dP_{Y|X=x}/dν_Y (Eq. 3.1). A general lemma (Proposition 3.1) shows that any integral operator with kernel η ∈ L²(ν_Y; H) is Hilbert–Schmidt from L²(ν_Y) into H with ‖T_η‖_HS = ‖η‖; the CEO is then obtained by specializing η to the Radon–Nikodym derivative. Mapping properties into L²(ν_X), trace-class into L²(ν_X) (via the Maurin theorem), and compactness into C_b(X) (via the Sobolev embedding H^ℓ ↪ C_b for ℓ > d/2) follow by composition (Theorem 3.3).

For RKHS targets, the authors use the well-known norm-equivalences H_{k^Mat_β} ≃ H^{β+d/2} and H_{k^{Wen}_{d,j}} ≃ H^{j+(d+1)/2} (Proposition 2.3) to translate the abstract condition η ∈ L²(ν_Y; H) into Sobolev regularity of the conditional density in the conditioning variable. This is the step that makes the criterion *verifiable*: classical smoothness estimates for conditional densities (regression noise density, Bayesian likelihood under Gaussian noise, SDE transition-density Gaussian derivative bounds) can be plugged in directly.

Approximation bounds come in two flavours. (1) Galerkin / kernel-EDMD bounds (Theorem 3.12) factor the error as (I − P_X)K (one-sided) or additionally P_X K(I − P_Y) (two-sided) and invoke scattered-data interpolation estimates (Wendland 2005, Thm. 11.13/11.17) to get fill-distance rates h_X^{m_X}, with m_X = ⌈β⌉−1 for Matérn and the sharper m_X = j+½ for Wendland kernels. (2) CME bounds (Theorem 3.16) compose Corollary 3.8 with the source-condition-adaptive ridge-regression rates of Li et al. (2024, Thm. 3) at α=β=1, γ=0, yielding a rate n^{-1/(1+p)} where p is the eigenvalue-decay exponent of C_{XX}.

Verification in the three applications proceeds by establishing the Sobolev regularity of η directly from the model: in regression η(x,y) = p_ε(y − f(x)) so regularity of f and p_ε gives the result (Theorem 4.2); in Bayesian inversion the Gaussian likelihood gives an explicit η in terms of the log-partition Λ(x) = log Ξ(x) whose derivatives are posterior cumulants controlled by a polynomial growth condition (4.5); for SDEs, uniformly elliptic parabolic regularity yields Gaussian derivative bounds |∂_x^α η_t(x,y)| ≤ C_α t^{-(|α|+d)/2} exp(−κ‖x−y‖²/t) (Proposition 4.10) which integrate to η_t ∈ L²(ν_Y; H^ℓ). On unbounded domains X = ℝ^d the Sobolev embedding is not compact, so a Gaussian-reweighted kernel k_τ (Lemma 2.2) is used in cases (B) of Theorems 4.2/4.5/4.11.

## Results & Highlights

This is a theory paper with no empirical experiments; the quantitative results are operator-norm bounds, exact identities, and convergence rates. The strongest quantitative claims are:

- **Sharp Hilbert–Schmidt norm identity (Theorem 3.3(a)):** under η ∈ L²(ν_Y; H), the CEO satisfies ‖K‖_{L(L²(ν_Y),H)} ≤ ‖K‖_{HS(L²(ν_Y),H)} = ‖η‖_{L²(ν_Y;H)} — an exact equality, not just an upper bound.
- **Galerkin / kernel-EDMD fill-distance rates (Theorem 3.12(c)–(d)):** for a Lipschitz bounded domain X ⊂ ℝ^d and RBF kernel k_X ∈ C^{2m_X}, ‖K − K̄‖_{L(L²(ν_Y),C_b(X))} ≤ C ‖η‖_{L²(ν_Y;H)} · h_X^{m_X}. For Matérn k^Mat_β the rate exponent is m_X = ⌈β⌉−1; for Wendland k^{Wen}_{d,j} it improves to the sharper m_X = j + ½.
- **CME learning rate (Theorem 3.16):** with ε_n ≍ n^{-1/(1+p)} and eigenvalue decay λ_j ≤ c j^{-1/p} of C_{XX}, the regularized CME estimator satisfies ‖K̂_{ε_n} − K‖²_{HS(H_{k_Y}, L²(P_X))} ≤ τ² A n^{-1/(1+p)} with probability ≥ 1 − 5e^{−τ}. The default rate is n^{-1/2} (p=1); for Sobolev RKHS H_{k_X} ≃ H^ℓ(X) on a bounded Lipschitz domain with ℓ > d/2, the eigenvalue decay sharpens to p = d/(2ℓ) and the rate improves to **n^{-2ℓ/(2ℓ+d)}**.
- **Stronger-norm rate from extra smoothness (Remark 3.18):** if η ∈ L²(ν_Y; H^s(X)) with s > ℓ, the bound holds in the stronger norm HS(H_{k_Y}, H_{k_X}) at rate n^{-(s−ℓ)/(s+d/2)}.
- **Koopman-operator application (Corollary 4.12):** for uniformly elliptic SDEs on a bounded Lipschitz domain X with ℓ > d/2, the one-sided Galerkin approximation of the Koopman operator satisfies ‖K_t − P_X K_t‖_{L(L²(ν_Y),C_b(X))} ≤ C ‖η_t‖_{L²(ν_Y;H)} · h_X^m (m = ⌈β⌉−1 for Matérn, m = j+½ for Wendland).
- **Concrete verifiable sufficient conditions for Bayesian inversion (Proposition 4.6):** the log-partition growth condition holds if F is essentially bounded, or in the affine-Gaussian case F(y)=Ay+b, or under bounded posterior conditional moments, or under strong log-concavity of the pushforward of Γ^{-1}F(Y).

No numerical benchmarks, datasets, or baselines are reported — the paper validates its framework analytically by checking the abstract condition in three model classes rather than empirically.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The core observation — that an integral operator with L²(ν_Y; H) kernel is Hilbert–Schmidt — is a standard functional-analysis fact (the authors are explicit about this and give a self-contained proof of Proposition 3.1 "for completeness"). The genuine novelty is the *reframing*: identifying η with the Radon–Nikodym derivative and showing this standard fact, plus classical Sobolev/parabolic regularity, suffices to (a) *derive* rather than assume CME representability, and (b) recover and unify the fill-distance and eigenvalue-decay rates of several recent Koopman/CME papers. This is a conceptual unification rather than a new technique, but it is a valuable one and the paper is honest about the lineage.
- **Rigor:** High. Every stated theorem is proved (longer proofs are deferred to appendices A.1–A.3). The chain Theorem 3.3 → Corollary 3.8 → Theorem 3.16 is clean, and the careful distinction between bounded domains (where Theorem 3.16 applies) and unbounded domains (where the reweighted kernel k_τ has unbounded diagonal and existing CME rate results provably do not apply — Remark 4.1, footnotes 6 and 8) is a sign of intellectual honesty rather than hand-waving.
- **Gaps and overclaiming the SDE coverage:** The framework's headline application — Koopman operators for SDEs — is restricted by Assumption 4.9 to *bounded* drift and diffusion coefficients (b ∈ C_b^ℓ, σ ∈ C_b^ℓ). This excludes the single most common benchmark in the Koopman literature, the **Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process** (linear drift b(x)=Ax is unbounded). The authors acknowledge this explicitly in Remark 4.13 and rescue it by a hand computation in Example 4.14 using the explicit Gaussian density, but this exposes that the main theorem does *not* cover the regime practitioners actually simulate, and the promised extension to Hörmander/Malliavin or linearly-growing drift (Menozzi et al. 2021) is left entirely to future work.
- **Missing ablations / quantitative comparison:** The two-sided Galerkin (kernel-EDMD) bound of Theorem 3.12(b) requires Y to be a bounded Lipschitz domain, which fails for SDEs on ℝ^d (Remark 4.13 concedes the two-sided rates "are not directly applicable" there). Likewise the CME rate of Theorem 3.16 is unavailable on unbounded domains. So in the unbounded SDE setting — arguably the most important case — only *qualitative* Hilbert–Schmidt / representability statements survive; the quantitative rates are confined to bounded X (and, for two-sided EDMD, bounded Y).
- **No experiments.** There is not a single numerical experiment, toy regression, posterior-sampling example, or EDMD simulation. For a paper whose pitch includes "data-driven approximations" and whose target communities include cs.LG / stat.ML, this is a notable absence: the rates (n^{-2ℓ/(2ℓ+d)}, h_X^{⌈β⌉−1}) are never demonstrated to be tight or even achievable in finite-sample regimes, and constants C, A are unspecified.
- **Golden-ratio artefact:** The Bayesian-inverse-problem CME representation (Corollary 4.7, condition (4.7)) requires an exponential-moment parameter λ > (1+√5)/2 — the golden ratio — which the paper flags in footnote 8. This is a genuine (if mild) tail-strength condition on F(Y), not a purely cosmetic constant, and slightly undercuts the claim that the conditions are "rather mild."
- **Reproducibility:** For a theory paper, reproducibility reduces to checking proofs; the statements are precisely formulated with explicit assumptions, the notation table (Table 3.1, Figure 1.1) is exemplary, and the appendix proofs are sketched in enough detail to be checkable.

## Strengths

- Converts a notoriously hard-to-verify assumption (CME representability (3.4)) into a checkable Sobolev-regularity condition on the conditional density — a real conceptual advance for the CME literature.
- Genuine unification: the same density-regularity lens recovers results across nonparametric regression, Bayesian inversion, and Koopman theory, and the diagram in Figure 1.1 makes the logical flow unusually transparent.
- Sharp quantitative pay-off: exact HS norm identity, explicit fill-distance exponents for Matérn vs. the improved Wendland rate (j+½), and the minimax-style CME rate n^{-2ℓ/(2ℓ+d)} matching classical Sobolev ridge regression.
- Honest treatment of the bounded/unbounded domain split and of which existing rate results (Li et al. 2024; Caponnetto–De Vito 2007) do and do not cover reweighted (unbounded-diagonal) kernels.
- Excellent notation hygiene (Notation 3.10, Table 3.1, Figure 3.1) — rare for a paper at this level of abstraction.

## Limitations

- **No experiments whatsoever.** Rates are purely asymptotic; finite-sample behavior, constant sharpness, and practical estimator choice (one-sided vs. two-sided vs. CME per Remark 3.11) are never illustrated.
- **SDE coverage is narrower than advertised.** Assumption 4.9 (bounded C_b^ℓ coefficients, uniform ellipticity) excludes linear-drift benchmarks like Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (rescued only by hand in Example 4.14) and hypoelliptic / degenerate-noise systems (Hörmander) — all deferred to future work.
- **Quantitative rates only on bounded domains.** On X = ℝ^d the reweighted kernel k_τ has unbounded diagonal, so Theorem 3.16 cannot be applied (Remark 4.1) and the paper states only qualitative results there — exactly the setting of most Koopman applications.
- **Two-sided kernel-EDMD rates (Theorem 3.12(b)) require bounded Y**, which fails for SDE transition laws with unbounded support, so the practically dominant EDMD scheme lacks a rate in the paper's flagship application.
- **Constants unspecified.** C, C_X, C_Y, A are non-constructive; the trade-off in Remark 3.14 (‖η‖ grows with smoothness order ℓ, so the "optimal" ℓ is problem-dependent) is noted but never resolved.
- **Conditional sampling assumption for projection methods** (z_m^{(i)} ~ P_{Y|X=x_i}) is required for K̄, K̃ but is itself expensive in Bayesian inversion — the paper recommends CME there but does not quantify the gap.

## Relevance

This paper is highly relevant to research on operator-theoretic and kernel methods for dynamical systems and probabilistic inference: it provides the missing analytical foundation justifying conditional mean embedding representations and kernel-EDMD Koopman approximations, both of which are widely used in data-driven dynamics, Bayesian inverse problems, and nonparametric regression. For anyone building CME- or Koopman-based estimators on continuous state spaces, the verifiable regularity criterion is directly useful for knowing when the standard operator representation is even valid.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the Sobolev-regularity verification of Section 4.3 be extended to hypoelliptic SDEs satisfying Hörmander's bracket condition, where transition densities are smooth but Gaussian derivative bounds are replaced by sub-Gaussian / polynomially-decaying estimates from Malliavin calculus?
- What is the correct CME convergence rate on unbounded domains with the reweighted kernel k_τ, given that all existing vector-valued ridge-regression rates (Li et al. 2024; Caponnetto–De Vito 2007; Park–Muandet 2020) require a bounded kernel diagonal?
- For trajectory data from an α-mixing geometrically ergodic process, can the i.i.d. rate of Theorem 3.16 be recovered (up to log factors) rigorously using the concentration inequality of Mollenhauer et al. (2022, Thm. 21), as sketched in Remark 3.19?
- The trade-off in Remark 3.14 (larger ℓ shrinks the fill-distance exponent but inflates ‖η‖_{L²(ν_Y;H^ℓ)}) suggests an optimal smoothness ℓ*(η); can this be characterized explicitly for a given transition density, e.g. for the OU process Gaussian η_t?
- Does the framework extend to non-symmetric / non-reversible Markov semigroups where the singular-value expansion of Remark 3.5 does not reduce to an eigenfunction expansion, and how does this affect finite-rank truncation in kernel-EDMD?
