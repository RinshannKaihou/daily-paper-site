> **一句话总结：** 本文提出并验证了两条用于检验表格基础模型（TFM）"自洽性"的准则——边际化一致性与因子分解一致性；实验证明 TabPFN、TabICL、TabDPT、TabFM 等所有 SOTA TFM 在分类和回归任务的全部数据集上都违反这两条准则，即它们的单变量预测无法对应任何良定的联合分布。

> **TL;DR:** Rather than scoring Tabular Foundation Models (TabPFNv2/v3, TabICLv1/v2, TabDPT, TabFM) against an unknowable Bayesian posterior, this paper tests whether each model's own univariate predictions can come from *any* well-defined joint distribution, via two label-free requirements (marginalization and factorization consistency) measured with total-variation distance. On 10 classification and 7 regression OpenML datasets, every evaluated TFM violates both requirements on every dataset (e.g., on Wine, TabFM's marginalization TV is 0.0092 vs TabPFNv2's 0.0478); the two gaps correlate strongly (Pearson r = 0.89, Spearman ρ = 0.90), yet neither correlates with accuracy/RMSE, exposing a blind spot of current benchmarks like TabArena.

## 核心贡献

- 形式化定义了两条任何"由良定联合分布诱导"的 TFM 都必须满足的一致性要求：**边际化一致性 (C1)** 要求对中间目标求边际后得到的条件分布等于直接预测的边际分布；**因子分解一致性 (C2)** 要求两种链式分解顺序构造出的联合分布相同。
- 从这两条要求推导出**无需任何真实标签**即可计算的违规度量（基于总变差距离 TV），从而绕开了"真实后验未知、无法直接评分"的根本难题。
- 证明了 (C2) 蕴含 (C1) 但反之不然（命题 1 与命题 2），因此只要测得一次边际化违规就已证明存在因子分解违规。
- 在 6 个 SOTA TFM、17 个 OpenML 数据集上系统评估，发现**所有模型在所有数据集、所有任务上都违反这两条准则**；并发现两种一致性度量高度相关 (r=0.89, ρ=0.90)，但与精度/RMSE 无关——也就是说当前的准确率排行榜（如 TabArena）完全看不到这一缺陷。

## Key Contributions

- Formalizes **two consistency requirements** that any tabular foundation model whose univariate predictions are induced by a well-defined joint distribution must satisfy: *marginalization consistency* (C1, the marginalized conditional equals the directly predicted marginal) and *factorization consistency* (C2, the two chain-rule orders yield the same joint).
- Derives **label-free, prior-free metrics** for both requirements using total-variation distance, sidestepping the fundamental obstacle that the true Bayesian posterior predictive is unknown on real datasets — the checks need only the model's own outputs.
- Proves a strict ordering between the two properties: **factorization consistency implies marginalization consistency but not vice versa** (Proposition 1 + a counterexample in Proposition 2/Appendix A), so any observed marginalization violation already certifies a factorization violation.
- Provides the **first systematic empirical audit** of consistency across six state-of-the-art TFMs (TabPFNv2/v3, TabICLv1/v2, TabDPT, TabFM), showing universal violation and establishing consistency as an evaluation axis orthogonal to accuracy.

## Methodology

The paper operates entirely on the prior-data fitted network (PFN) blueprint that underlies all modern TFMs: a transformer is pretrained on millions of synthetic datasets drawn from a prior to predict held-out targets, and at inference a single forward pass over a labeled context set D and a test point x returns a univariate predictive density p̂(y | x; D). Because the in-context interface is agnostic to which column is the target, practitioners build multivariate joints autoregressively by treating one sampled target as a feature for the next (Vetter et al. 2025).

The authors formalize, for a pair of target columns (A, B):

- **Marginalization consistency (C1):** p̂(a | x; D_A^{-B}) = ∫ p̂(a | b, x; D_A) p̂(b | x; D_{-A}^B) db. The detour of sampling an intermediate target b from the model's own head must not change what the model believes about a.
- **Factorization consistency (C2):** p̂(a | b, x; D_A) p̂(b | x; D_{-A}^B) = p̂(b | a, x; D_B) p̂(a | x; D_{-B}^A). The two chain-rule orders must reconstruct the same joint.

Discrepancies are quantified by **total-variation distance TV ∈ [0, 1]**. For classification, both the mixture and the joints are evaluated in closed form (finite sums over classes). For regression, the authors cannot enumerate the target space, so they reduce each model's head (a 5000-bar softmax for TabPFN, 999 quantile knots for TabICLv2, 2048 equal-width bins for TabDPT) to a common quantile-curve interface on a fixed ladder of L=2000 levels, then use K=1000 equal-mass atoms for midpoint quadrature and compare CDFs on a shared 20-cell grid (marginalization) or 128×128 grid (factorization). The coarse comparison grid is a deliberate lower bound (TV is non-increasing under coarsening), and every check runs under 5-fold cross-testing on NVIDIA RTX 2080 Ti and A40 GPUs. The entire pipeline is deterministic — no sampling — so runs reproduce exactly.

The dataset suite comprises 10 classification pairs and 7 regression pairs from OpenML (e.g., Wine quality vs. color, Diamonds cut vs. color, Boston MEDV vs. RM, Concrete strength vs. water), chosen so the two targets plausibly depend on each other given the features (which makes the checks non-trivial).

## Results & Highlights

- **Universal violation:** Every TFM evaluated violates *both* marginalization and factorization consistency on *every* dataset, for *both* classification and probabilistic regression. Because classification mixtures are computed in closed form, these gaps are not sampling noise.
- **Marginalization consistency (Table 1), classification:** On the Wine dataset, TabFM achieves the lowest mean TV of 0.0092 (worst instance 0.43) versus TabPFNv2 at 0.0478 (worst instance 0.52); on Car, TabFM scores 0.0171 versus TabICLv1's worst-in-column 0.0621. No single model is the most consistent marginalizer — TabFM wins 5/10 datasets, TabDPT 4/10, TabPFNv3 1/10 — while TabICLv1 is the most inconsistent classifier overall.
- **Marginalization consistency (Table 1), regression:** No TFM scores an average TV close to zero on any dataset; TabDPT is closest to consistency on all datasets except Insurance, where it paradoxically posts the highest TV among tested models (0.0681).
- **Factorization consistency (Table 2):** Violations are wider than for marginalization, consistent with the theory that (C2) is the stronger requirement. TabFM is the most factorization-consistent classifier, holding the lowest mean TV on **8 of 10** datasets and second place on the remaining two; TabICLv1 again shows the highest average violations. For regression, TabPFNv3 comes closest to factorization consistency on the majority of datasets, while TabDPT — despite being relatively strong on marginalization — becomes one of the *more* inconsistent models under factorization.
- **Strong correlation between the two requirements (Figure 3):** Across all (model, dataset) pairs, Pearson r = 0.89 and Spearman ρ = 0.90, empirically confirming the theoretical link between the two properties.
- **Consistency is orthogonal to predictive quality (Appendix D):** Relating each model's dataset-level accuracy (classification) or RMSE (regression) to its consistency gap reveals **no strong correlation** — so benchmarks like TabArena, which rank only by accuracy, do not capture this defect. TabFM, the newest and top-ranked TabArena model, is the most consistent classifier but offers no probabilistic regression.

## Authors & Credibility

The authors are from the Institute of Computer Science & VWFS DARC at the University of Hildesheim (Germany). The group has direct prior ownership of this exact problem formulation: co-author Yalavarthi et al. (ICLR 2026) defined marginalization consistency for irregularly-sampled multivariate time series and built a consistent alternative, and the framing cites the relevant coherence literature (Besag 1974; Arnold & Press 1989; Berti, Pratelli & Rigo 2004; Fong, Holmes & Walker 2023; Falck, Wang & Holmes 2024) precisely. The theoretical content (two propositions, one with a deferred proof/counterexample) is modest but correct, and the metric design (label-free, prior-free, deterministic, lower-bounding TV) is well-motivated. The work is positioned as a diagnostic contribution, which it fulfills cleanly.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The framing — "stop trying to measure distance to an unknowable posterior; instead check whether the model's own outputs could come from *any* joint" — is genuinely useful and, applied to TFMs, novel. The marginalization condition is a known martingale/coherence property repackaged for tabular foundation models; the main intellectual contribution is turning it into a concrete, label-free evaluation axis and running the first audit.
- **Rigor:** The classification checks are exact (closed-form finite sums), which makes the "every model violates on every dataset" claim airtight. The regression checks rely on quantile-curve discretization with deliberate choices that bias toward *under*-reporting violations (K=1000 fine quadrature, S=20 coarse comparison grid, TV non-increasing under coarsening), so the reported regression numbers are defensible lower bounds rather than artifacts of the discretization. The cross-testing protocol (5-fold) and worst-instance reporting add credibility.
- **Reproducibility:** Datasets are pinned by OpenML ID with preprocessing described in detail (Appendix B); the comparison interface is fully deterministic. The main reproducibility risk is that several models are proprietary moving targets (TabPFNv3, TabFM are described via technical reports/blog posts rather than open code), so a re-run in six months could differ. The paper is appropriately explicit about which head parameterization each model uses.
- **Significance:** The negative result is important and timely — it directly undercuts the implicit assumption behind autoregressive use of TFMs (e.g., TabMGP, Vetter et al. 2025's simulation-based inference), which assumes coherence. It also explains *why* a model can be the most accurate yet not the most consistent, which is the kind of finding that changes how people benchmark.
- **Red flags / weaknesses:**
  - The evaluation is restricted to **pairs** of targets (d=2). The authors acknowledge this and argue the conditions apply verbatim to any pair, but the practical magnitude of inconsistency when many targets are chained autoregressively — the regime that actually matters for joint-density estimation and data generation — is not measured.
  - **Mixed categorical-continuous target pairs are explicitly skipped**, on the grounds that today's TFMs use separate weights for classification and regression and would therefore compare "two effectively different models." This is a real and unmeasured gap, since mixed-type joints are common in practice.
  - The headline "every model violates on every dataset" is, in a technical sense, almost guaranteed to be true for *any* finite-trained neural model under an exact TV check — the more interesting question the paper gestures at but does not answer is whether the violations are *practically* harmful (e.g., do they degrade downstream decisions?). The paper's own Figure 4 shows no per-row correlation between NLL and the marginalization gap, which cuts both ways: it supports "consistency is a distinct axis" but also weakens any implicit "consistency matters for predictions" claim.
  - The metrics are **purely diagnostic** — the paper offers no fix, no training objective, no reconciliation procedure, and acknowledges this explicitly. That is a fair limitation for an analysis paper but limits immediate practical uptake.

## Strengths

- Clean, well-motivated problem framing that sidesteps the genuinely unsolvable "compare to the true posterior" obstacle.
- Exact, closed-form classification checks make the universal-violation claim unquestionable.
- Deliberately conservative regression discretization (the reported TV is a certified lower bound), so readers can trust the numbers are not inflated by approximation.
- Honest reporting: standard deviations across folds, worst-single-instance values in parentheses, and per-row NLL-vs-TV scatter plots (Figure 4) rather than only aggregates.
- The theoretical ordering (C2 ⟹ C1, with a clean counterexample for the converse) is a useful guide for practitioners: you only need to measure the cheaper (C1) to certify the harder (C2) is violated.

## Limitations

- Only **pairs of targets (d=2)** are tested; higher-dimensional autoregressive chains — the regime where inconsistency would compound — are not evaluated.
- **Mixed categorical-continuous pairs are excluded** because current TFMs split classification/regression across separate weights.
- The conditions are **diagnostic only**; no method is proposed to train or constrain a TFM to satisfy them, so the paper cannot tell users how to fix what it measures.
- No demonstration that consistency violations **actually harm downstream tasks** (e.g., autoregressive data generation quality, simulation-based inference calibration). The per-row analysis (Figure 4) in fact finds no correlation with NLL, leaving the practical stakes unclear.
- Several evaluated models are proprietary or version-unstable (TabPFNv3, TabFM), so the exact rankings may not persist; the paper does not pin model versions/checkpoints beyond citing the technical reports.

## Relevance

高度相关。本文直击表格基础模型（TFM）作为贝叶斯推断近似器的核心前提——即其单变量预测能否自洽地拼成多变量联合分布，这与对概率建模、不确定性量化及贝叶斯方法可靠性的研究兴趣直接对齐。其"无标签即可审计模型内部一致性"的诊断范式，对评估大模型概率判断的合理性也具有方法论参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How do the consistency gaps **compound** when more than two targets are chained autoregressively (d ≫ 2)? Does TV grow with chain length, and is there a regime where the implied joint becomes unusable for data generation?
- Can a **training objective** that penalizes the marginalization gap (cheap to measure) reduce both violations without sacrificing predictive accuracy, given that the two are theoretically and empirically linked?
- Do the consistency violations **measurably degrade downstream tasks** such as simulation-based Bayesian inference (Vetter et al. 2025) or synthetic tabular data generation, where the joint structure rather than point accuracy is what matters?
- How do the gaps behave for **mixed categorical-continuous target pairs** once unified-architecture TFMs (shared weights across task types) become available?
- Is the orthogonality of consistency to accuracy stable across **larger context sizes**, or does the gap shrink asymptotically as D grows — a question the neural-process literature suggests but this paper does not test empirically?
