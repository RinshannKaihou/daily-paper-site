> **一句话总结：** 本文发现自演化 LLM 智能体在技能库跨过临界规模后，新增技能非但不能提升反而持续拉低性能（"能力-污染相变"），且事后删除有害技能仅能挽回约 17% 的损失——因为缺陷会沿蒸馏谱系遗传；作者据此提出 VaG（Verifier-as-Gatekeeper）预提交门控，用三级互补评审加边际增益子集选择在技能进入运行上下文前拦截污染，在 Terminal-Bench 2 上单调提升至 72% pass@1，超过无门控演化的峰值 10 个百分点，且技能池缩小约 5 倍。

> **TL;DR:** On Terminal-Bench 2, ungated self-evolution peaks at 62% pass@1 (R3) then collapses to 50% (R5) as the skill pool grows to 179 entries, while post-hoc removal of the 8 harmful source skills recovers only 1.7pp of a 12.3pp drop; the proposed Verifier-as-Gatekeeper (VaG) — a pre-commit, progressive trust hierarchy with three heterogeneous critics (schema, behavioral replay, semantic) plus marginal-gain subset selection — improves monotonically to 72% pass@1 with a 37-skill pool, beats ungated evolution's best round by 10pp, and transfers positively (frozen, no re-evolution) to four other backbones (+8 to +16pp) and to InterCode NL2Bash (+11.5pp over Seed).

## 核心贡献

- **揭示并形式化"能力-污染相变"现象**：首次指出自演化智能体的能力曲线并非单调上升，而是在技能池达到临界规模 k\* 后反转下降；并将污染分解为个体污染（单个技能独立有害）、组合污染（各自无害的技能联用时冲突）与系统污染（前两者在宏观上的非单调性）三级结构。
- **证明污染的结构不可逆性**：由于后续技能是基于已含缺陷的技能池蒸馏而成，缺陷会沿"派生谱系"遗传——仅删除源技能无法消除其后代已继承的错误推理，事后回滚只能挽回约 17% 的损失，即使拥有完美谱系的 Oracle 清理也无法恢复近一半的衰减，从而把"技能准入"确立为必须预提交（pre-commit）的关键环节。
- **提出 Verifier-as-Gatekeeper（VaG）**：把技能从"无条件写入"改造为"逐级晋升"决策，设计 Cold→Warm→Hot 三级信任层级；第一道门用 SchemaCritic（确定性结构校验）、ExecCritic（单技能 A-B 回放）、AgentCritic（单次 LLM 语义审查）三个互补维度过滤个体有害技能，第二道门用边际增益贪心子集选择消除组合污染。
- **实验验证相变、不可逆性与门控有效性**：在 Terminal-Bench 2 上重现相变曲线，量化回滚恢复缺口（源删除 1.7pp / Oracle 6.7pp / 不可恢复 5.6pp），证明 VaG 在全部 5 轮单调提升、技能池小 5 倍却高 22pp，且消融显示三类评审互补而不可互替（移除任一均明显掉点，尤其是行为回放 -10pp）。
- **证明门控演化产出模型无关的可迁移技能**：将 Hy3上演化的 R5 冻结技能池直接迁移到 DeepSeek-V4-Pro、GPT-5.4、Claude Sonnet 4.5、Qwen3.6-35B-A3B 四个骨干（+8 到 +16pp）以及 InterCode NL2Bash（+11.5pp），无需再演化，说明门控蒸馏出的是通用 shell 工程经验而非特定骨干/基准的特化产物。

## Key Contributions

- **Formalization of the capability-contamination phase transition.** The paper identifies that self-evolution is non-monotone — past a critical pool size k\*, newly added skills degrade rather than improve performance — and decomposes the effect into individual, combinatorial, and systemic contamination, giving a precise vocabulary that prior skill-library work (Voyager, ExpeL, AWM) lacks.
- **Structural-irreversibility theorem (Eq. 5).** Because distillation is conditioned on the live pool, a defective skill contaminates the reference context of all later skills; source-only rollback is strictly dominated by full-lineage cleanup, and even an oracle with perfect provenance leaves ~5.6pp of the 12.3pp drop unrecovered. This converts "admission" from bookkeeping into the last controllable choke point.
- **Verifier-as-Gatekeeper (VaG).** A pre-commit, progressive trust hierarchy: Cold (recorded, invisible) → Warm (passes schema + A-B replay + LLM semantic review) → Hot (admitted via marginal-gain greedy subset selection on measured joint held-out performance). The design explicitly targets both individual harm (Gate 1) and the combinatorial conflict (Gate 2) that per-skill judges provably cannot see.
- **Empirical confirmation on Terminal-Bench 2.** Reproduces the inverted-U (Ungated 48→62→50%), shows VaG improving every round to 72% pass@1 with a 5× smaller Hot pool, and beats oracle early-stopping by 10pp; ablations demonstrate the three critics intercept disjoint failure classes (replay -10pp is the largest single drop).
- **Cross-model and cross-benchmark transfer without re-evolution.** The frozen Hy3-evolved Hot pool gives +8 to +16pp on four other backbones and +11.5pp on InterCode NL2Bash, evidencing that gated distillation yields model-agnostic engineering knowledge rather than backbone-specific artifacts.

## Methodology

The paper first gives a formal model of self-evolution. At round r, the pool grows as M_r = M_{r-1} ∪ G(S_r), where S_r ∼ π(·|M_{r-1}) is the set of skills distilled from round-r trajectories conditioned on the prior pool, and G is the (usually identity) admission operator. The injection gain of a set A over the current pool is g(A, τ) = R(M ∪ A, τ) − R(M, τ), with R estimated on a held-out set D_holdout. Contamination is then defined at three levels: individual (g({s}, τ) < 0), combinatorial (g({s_a}, τ) ≥ 0 and g({s_b}, τ) ≥ 0 yet g({s_a, s_b}, τ) < 0), and systemic (the macro non-monotone curve P(k) with critical size k\* = argmax_k P(k)).

The irreversibility argument is the load-bearing theoretical contribution. Since S_r is generated conditioned on M_{r-1}, every later skill potentially inherits reasoning from earlier ones. Defining desc(s) as the descendants of s, the paper shows R(M_r \ {s}) < R(M_r \ ({s} ∪ desc(s))) whenever any descendant inherited s's flawed reasoning. Source-only rollback is therefore strictly weaker than full-lineage cleanup, and lineage cleanup is infeasible in practice because existing skill libraries do not record which skills were in context when each skill was written. This makes pre-commit gating a structural necessity rather than an optimization.

VaG operationalizes this as a progressive trust hierarchy L = {Cold ≺ Warm ≺ Hot}. Every distilled skill enters Cold (recorded, not in the decision context). Promotion requires clearing two gates of increasing stringency. **Gate 1 (Cold→Warm, individual harmlessness)** is a conjunction of three heterogeneous critics: (i) SchemaCritic — a deterministic frontmatter schema check ν_struct(s) ∈ {0,1}; (ii) ExecCritic — a single-skill A-B replay on D_holdout comparing R(M ∪ {s}) against R(M), passing when it does not degrade aggregate success; (iii) AgentCritic — a single LLM call flagging fabricated facts, self-contradictions with M, or unsafe operations. A skill must pass all three. **Gate 2 (Warm→Hot, combinatorial safety)** treats promotion as subset selection maximizing f(H) = E_{τ}[R(agent ⊕ H, τ)]. Because f is not globally monotone (skill injection can be contaminating), the authors use a marginal-gain greedy that adds a Warm candidate only when it strictly improves measured joint performance, stopping when none helps. With |W| ≤ 15 survivors, this costs at most |W|−1 ≤ 14 joint replays per round, each estimated with k=3 held-out replays. The two cheap Gate-1 checks (schema + replay) reject most candidates before any LLM is called, making the pipeline economical.

## Results & Highlights

- **Phase transition is sharp and reproducible (Terminal-Bench 2, Event-50, pass@1).** Ungated evolution rises from 48% (R1, 35 skills) to a peak of 62% (R3, 105 skills), then collapses to 50% (R5, 179 skills) — ending only 2pp above its own R1 despite a 5× larger pool. Seed (no evolution) holds flat at 46%. This is the empirical signature of the capability-contamination tipping point k\* ≈ R3.
- **VaG converts the inverted-U into monotone improvement.** VaG rises every round: 52% (R1) → 58% → 62% → 68% → 72% (R5), with a Hot pool of only 37 skills. At R5, VaG exceeds Ungated by 22pp and beats Ungated's best round (R3, 62%) by 10pp — i.e., gating beats both unchecked accumulation and oracle early-stopping.
- **Contamination concentrates on hard tasks.** Ungated's Hard-tier pass@1 peaks at 53% (R2) and erodes to 35% (R5); VaG lifts Hard to 59% at R5, a 24pp margin over Ungated, while Easy stays at 100% throughout — admission control protects precisely the long multi-step tasks where contamination chains are longest.
- **Post-hoc rollback is structurally weak.** Of the 12.3pp drop from the Ungated peak (62.3%) to R5 (50.0%), removing only the 8 harmful source skills recovers just 1.7pp (to 52%); oracle full-lineage cleanup recovers a further 5.0pp (to 56.7%); the remaining 5.6pp is irrecoverable even with perfect provenance. Source-only rollback thus recovers only ~14–17% of the loss. VaG (72.0%) exceeds the Oracle by 15.3pp.
- **The three Gate-1 critics are complementary, not redundant (ablation, R5).** Removing schema validation costs −2pp, removing the semantic LLM check costs −4pp, removing the held-out A-B replay costs −10pp (the largest single drop), and removing the marginal-gain Gate-2 costs −8pp (the Hot pool balloons from 37 to 58 skills while pass@1 falls). Each critic intercepts a different failure class, so none substitutes for another.
- **Cross-model transfer (Test-25, frozen Hy3-evolved R5 Hot pool, no re-evolution).** Positive lift on all five backbones: Hy3 +12pp (32→44%), DeepSeek-V4-Pro +8pp (32→40%), GPT-5.4 +12pp (44→56%), Claude Sonnet 4.5 +8pp (48→56%), Qwen3.6-35B-A3B +16pp (36→52%). GPT-5.4 and Claude Sonnet 4.5 reach 56%, above Hy3's own 44%, indicating the gate-filtered skills encode model-agnostic knowledge from which stronger backbones extract more.
- **Cross-benchmark transfer (InterCode NL2Bash, 200 tasks, frozen R5 pools).** VaG's 37 Hot skills reach 69.0% pass@1 vs Ungated's 179 skills at 65.5% and Seed at 57.5% (+11.5pp over Seed). Ungated stays net-positive here (+8.0pp) because single-command trials invoke few skills and contamination chains stay shallow — consistent with the contamination-bites-hardest-on-long-tasks finding.
- **Efficiency co-benefit.** VaG's per-trial token cost stays flat (0.77–0.94M) while Ungated's grows with its bloated pool (up to 1.30M) for worse accuracy — the gate buys accuracy and efficiency together.

## Authors & Credibility

All authors are affiliated with Tencent, and the paper is written in the confident, mechanism-first style of an industrial research lab that has actually run the evolution loop at scale. The framing — naming a phenomenon (capability-contamination phase transition), proving a structural property (irreversibility), then deriving a method as a necessity — is characteristic of mature systems/ML research rather than a benchmark-chasing submission. The honesty about scale ("these are not head-to-head baselines: backbone, task suite, and round budget all differ" when comparing to ACE/AHE) and the willingness to report overlapping Wilson CIs while resting the claim on trajectory-shape divergence are signs of methodological self-awareness. The use of TB2's built-in deterministic verifier (no LLM grader) for pass/fail decisions removes a common source of evaluator bias. The complete lineage case study (a git-conflict skill at R3 seeding merge/rebase descendants at R4 that kept failing 4 of 7 git-related Test-25 tasks after source-only rollback) gives a concrete, falsifiable trace rather than only aggregate numbers.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** High and well-scoped. The irreversibility argument (Eq. 5) is the genuinely new contribution — prior skill-library work (Voyager, ExpeL, AWM, ACE, AHE) either does no admission control or defers evaluation to the harness level, and none formalizes the lineage-propagation obstacle. The framing of admission as a "pre-commit necessity" derived from irreversibility (rather than as one optimization among several) is a clean conceptual contribution that should reframe how the community thinks about skill libraries.
- **Rigor:** The three-level contamination taxonomy and the formal inequality are sound, and the ablations genuinely isolate each component (the −10pp replay drop and −8pp marginal-gain drop are the clearest evidence that individual and combinatorial contamination are distinct and real). However, the marginal-gain greedy is explicitly acknowledged to be "a heuristic rather than an algorithm with a worst-case optimality guarantee" — the utility surface is neither known submodular nor monotone, so the greedy has no approximation guarantee; the paper does not test whether a richer selection (e.g., lazy greedy with rollback, or a small local search) would close the residual gap.
- **Reproducibility concerns:**
  - The Held-out split is tiny — **14 tasks** for the A-B replays and marginal-gain estimates that drive every gating decision, with only **k=3 rollouts** per estimate. With Wilson CI bands of ~30pp width on Event-50 (n=50, k=3), the per-round point estimates are noisy; the authors openly admit "per-round point tests are underpowered" and rest the main claim on trajectory-shape divergence, which is a defensible but weaker evidentiary bar. A reader cannot tell from the main text whether Gate-2's marginal-gain decisions are statistically distinguishable from noise at the per-skill level.
  - The Event-50 split is acknowledged to be "an optimistic upper bound" (it is the distillation split), so the honest number is Test-25 (25 tasks) — even smaller — and the headline 72% lives on Event-50. The Test-25 VaG number is not reported in Table 1 at all; only the cross-model transfer (Table 3) shows Test-25 with other backbones, and the Hy3-on-Test-25 VaG figure (44%) is far below the 72% headline. The gap between the advertised and the held-out number deserves more prominence.
  - Implementation details (the distillation prompt, the exact ReAct loop, the Harbor TB2 runtime configuration) are deferred to a Supplementary Material that is not in the PDF extracted here, so an independent reimplementation would need those.
- **Significance:** If the irreversibility result generalizes beyond the terminal-task domain (the paper only tests shell/bash tasks), it is a genuinely important negative result for the self-evolution literature — it explains why naive "more skills = better" pipelines plateau or regress, and it gives a principled fix. The concern is that all evidence is on a single task family (terminal/shell), where skills are short imperative commands that interact sharply; whether the combinatorial-contamination effect is as severe for, e.g., retrieval-style or planning-style skills in other agent domains is untested.
- **Red flags:**
  - The comparison to ACE (68.9) and AHE (77.0) on full TB2-89 is explicitly disclaimed as non-head-to-head, but a casual reader of the abstract ("reaching 72% pass@1") could mistake the Event-50 number for a competitive result on the standard TB2 leaderboard; the two are not comparable (different split, backbone Hy3, and only 5 rounds). The headline should be read as "VaG vs ungated evolution under one controlled setup," not "VaG is SOTA on TB2."
  - The 12.3pp peak-to-R5 drop with only 8 harmful source skills identified out of 179 suggests the contaminating-skill detection itself may be incomplete; the oracle cleanup recovering 6.7pp assumes perfect lineage knowledge that no deployed system has, so the "irrecoverable 5.6pp" is the only number a practitioner can actually rely on, and it is uncomfortably close to the source-only recovery.
  - Gate-1's behavioral replay compares R(M ∪ {s}) ≥ R(M) on 14 held-out tasks — admitting a skill that "merely matches" M rather than strictly improving it is explicitly chosen to tolerate noise, but with 14 tasks and k=3 this admits many near-neutral skills that then bloat the Warm pool and must be filtered by Gate-2; the interaction between Gate-1's leniency and Gate-2's greedy is not ablated.

## Strengths

- The irreversibility theorem (Eq. 5) is a clean, reusable conceptual result that reframes skill admission from "optional curation" to "the last controllable choke point" — this is the kind of negative result that changes how a subfield designs systems.
- The progressive trust hierarchy is well-motivated: the two cheap deterministic/behavioral checks (schema, replay) reject most candidates before any LLM is called, and the authors quantify this economy (Gate-2 reuses Gate-1 replays; only AgentCritic costs one inference per candidate).
- The ablations are genuinely diagnostic rather than ceremonial — the disjoint-failure-class claim is supported by the fact that removing each critic hurts a different amount and inflates the pool differently, and the −8pp marginal-gain ablation is the single cleanest existence proof of combinatorial contamination.
- Intellectual honesty about statistical power (overlapping Wilson CIs, explicit acknowledgment that the claim rests on trajectory shape), about scale (disclaiming the ACE/AHE comparison), and about the oracle's infeasibility (lineage provenance that "a real system lacks").
- The cross-model and cross-benchmark transfer experiments are the right experiments to rule out the "skills are backbone-specific overfitting" null hypothesis, and the result that stronger backbones extract more from the same frozen pool is a non-trivial positive signal.

## Limitations

- **Small held-out splits drive every gating decision.** D_holdout is 14 tasks, Event-50 is the distillation split (optimistic), and the honest Test-25 is only 25 tasks; with k=3 rollouts the per-skill marginal-gain estimates are noisy, and the paper itself notes the Wilson CI bands (~30pp) make per-round point tests underpowered.
- **Single-domain evaluation.** All evidence is on terminal/shell tasks (TB2, InterCode NL2Bash). Whether the capability-contamination phase transition and the irreversibility result are as severe for skills in other agent domains (web, retrieval, multi-tool reasoning, code editing beyond bash) is untested.
- **The headline 72% is on the distillation split, not the held-out Test-25.** The Test-25 Hy3 VaG number visible in Table 3 (44%) is far below 72%; the paper does not foreground this gap, and a reader of the abstract alone would overestimate the deployed performance.
- **The marginal-gain greedy has no approximation guarantee** on the (non-submodular, non-monotone) utility surface; the paper acknowledges this but does not compare against stronger selection procedures.
- **Backbone "Hy3" and the model versions (DeepSeek-V4-Pro, GPT-5.4, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B) are referenced as if standard but several are not independently characterized here** — readers cannot verify backbone capability baselines without external docs.
- **The oracle full-lineage cleanup is a theoretical upper bound** requiring provenance no real system records; the practically achievable source-only rollback (1.7pp recovery) is the operationally relevant number, and it is weak.
- **No test of whether VaG's own admission decisions can be gamed** by a subtly misleading skill that passes all three critics — the conjunction is empirically stricter, but the paper does not stress-test an adversarial skill-distillation scenario.

## Relevance

本研究直接命中"自演化 LLM 智能体"与"技能蒸馏/经验记忆"这条主轴，提出的"能力-污染相变"和"结构不可逆性"是对当前主流"技能库越多越好"假设的有力反驳，对所有构建带持久化经验库的 Agent 系统都有方法论价值；其预提交门控（多维度互补评审 + 边际增益子集选择）也为任何做 Agent 自我改进、记忆管理和能力积累的工作提供了可落地的工程范式。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the capability-contamination tipping point k\* scale with task horizon (longer multi-step tasks → earlier/lower k\*) or with skill verbosity (shorter imperative skills vs longer planning-style skills)? Cross-domain tests on web/retrieval/code-editing agents would test generality.
- Can the lineage provenance that VaG's irreversibility argument assumes be recorded cheaply (e.g., logging the live pool hash with each distilled skill), and would full-lineage cleanup then become feasible enough to rival pre-commit gating?
- Is the marginal-gain greedy on a non-submodular surface leaving significant value on the table — would a lazy-greedy-with-local-search or a small randomized search recover more of the gap to VaG's ceiling?
- With only 14 held-out tasks and k=3 rollouts, are Gate-2's per-skill admit/reject decisions statistically distinguishable from noise? A power analysis or a bootstrap over D_holdout would quantify how many of the 37 Hot skills are "really" earning their slot.
- Does VaG's advantage shrink as the backbone gets stronger (since stronger models may self-correct contaminated skills at inference time)? The cross-model transfer data hints at this (Qwen gains +16pp, Hy3 only +12pp) but does not isolate the effect.
- Could an adversarial skill — one deliberately crafted to pass schema, replay, and semantic checks while still misdirecting the agent on out-of-distribution tasks — defeat the conjunction, and would a fourth critic (e.g., distributional replay across harder held-out tasks) close the hole?
