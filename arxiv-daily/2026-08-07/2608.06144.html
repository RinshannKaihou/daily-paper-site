FinEvo-Bench: A Longitudinal Benchmark for Self-Evolving Agents in Professional Financial Workflows

First benchmark jointly covering cross-task self-evolution, open-ended multi-file deliverables, multi-aspect rubric scoring, and recurring professional financial workflows — with paired state-reset controls that cleanly isolate evolution ability from absolute backbone skill.

agent-benchmark self-evolving-agents financial-workflows longitudinal-evaluation LLM-agents

Bo Deng, Kang Zhou, Lifan Guo, Chongyang Tao, Xuanren Chen, Chenggang Xie, Renzhao Liang, Feng Chen, Chi Zhang
Beihang University · Alibaba Cloud — Qwen DianJin Team

Key Finding

Across four agent scaffolds on a shared Qwen3.7-Max backbone, retaining cross-task experience raises quality by +9.33 to +19.37 pts and cuts compliance issues 0.12–0.44/task — proven under three independently shuffled, globally interleaved streams.

Key Metric

ICC(A,1)=0.95

Claude Opus 4.6 rubric judge vs. financial expert on 120 deliverables (CI [0.93, 0.97], mean abs. diff 1.6 pts). Highest evolved score: Letta 91.65; largest gain: Codex +19.37.

Why This Matters

Lands squarely on self-evolving agents × longitudinal evaluation × financial workflows. Its evolved-score-vs-gain separation and globally interleaved paired streams offer a reusable method template for general agent longitudinal benchmarks.

04Critical Edge

The central claims rest on a single backbone (Qwen3.7-Max) and a same-vendor judge. Ordering conclusions — and even the headline "skill-only > memory-only > combination" verdict — could be backbone-dependent; one ablation with another model would have hardened them.

  • Judge–scaffold coupling: a Claude Opus 4.6 judge scoring the Claude Code scaffold is a subtle sympathy risk; a cross-family judge sweep is missing.
  • Carrier comparison on one scaffold only: the skill/memory ordering is established solely on Claude Code — not shown for Letta, Codex, or GenericAgent.
  • Feedback leakage: gains conflate genuine self-evolution with rubric-feedback exploitation; a no-feedback, execution-only baseline is absent.
  • No public release stated in the body, and institution-provided procedures Ps are non-redistributable — weak for a benchmark paper.

01Methodology

paired controlsrubric isolation Every evolving run is paired with a state-reset twin sharing order, backbone, and decoding — so ΔScore and ΔComp attribute gains to retained experience, not backbone strength.

Globally interleaved streams (K=3, independently shuffled) force retrieval amid unrelated intervening tasks, defeating naive positional memorization. The evaluated scaffold never sees the full rubric — only a post-task feedback summary of problems and reasons.

Four metrics: task quality (0–100 rubric), compliance issues/task, paired self-evolution gain, and agent-side token cost.

05Worth Probing Next

  1. Do scaffold rankings and the skill-vs-memory ordering survive a different backbone (smaller Qwen3.7 or non-Qwen)?
  2. Does ICC(A,1)=0.95 hold under a cross-family judge (score Claude Code with Qwen, Letta with GPT-class)?
  3. How much gain is true self-evolution vs. rubric-feedback exploitation? A no-feedback control would split them.
  4. Does late>early survive adversarial ordering — scenes maximally spaced, distractors inserted?
  5. Will rubrics and de-identified tasks be publicly released, and can outsiders reproduce the 0.95 agreement?
