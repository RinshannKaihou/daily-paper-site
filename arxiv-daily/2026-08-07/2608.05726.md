> **一句话总结：** 本文通过让 LLM 先执行"随机数生成"任务来估计其在数值 token 上的固有偏好（潜在数值偏差），并在评分推理时对 logits 施加反方向的去偏项（B(y) = log U(y) − log P_random(y)），在 SummEval、STS-B、SemRel2024 三个 LLM-as-a-Judge 任务上整体超过 Vanilla、Contextual Calibration 和 Distribution Calibration 基线（例如 STS 任务上 Deb-T 相比 CC 和 DC 分别提升 Spearman 15.5 / 44.6 个百分点）。

> **TL;DR:** The paper introduces a training-free debiaser for LLM-as-a-Judge: it elicits each LLM's task-specific "latent number bias" by prompting it to generate random numbers in the target score range, then adds a log-ratio debiasing term to score-token logits at inference. Across five open LLMs and four benchmarks (HelpSteer2 LLM-Alignment, SummEval, STS-B, SemRel2024), the task-conditioned variant Deb-T beats the strongest baseline on average for Summ, STS, and STR (e.g., SummEval Spearman 38.0 vs Vanilla 34.4 and CC 36.8; STS-B SDD 1.85 vs Vanilla 6.44), though it fails to help the LLM-Alignment task because the LLMs are already biased toward the majority score 3, which the method inadvertently reinforces.

## 核心贡献

- 提出一种基于"随机数生成"的训练无关去偏方法：通过测量 LLM 在随机生成数字时偏离均匀分布的程度，得到该模型对每个数字 token 的"潜在数值偏差"（latent number bias），并据此在评分时修正 logits。
- 提出任务条件化（task-conditioned）的去偏变体 Deb-T：在随机数生成 prompt 前拼接下游任务的评测指令，从而测量任务相关的偏差——作者证明偏差确实随任务和评分范围变化，这是与任务无关的 Contextual Calibration 的关键区别。
- 设计了去偏项 B(y) = log U(y) − log P_random(y) 并以 softmax(l(y) + λ·B(y)) 形式注入 logit，超参 λ 仅在 dev 集上从 {0.1,...,1.0} 网格搜索得到，无需训练、无需梯度。
- 在 4 个 LLM-as-a-Judge 任务 × 5 个开源 LLM（Llama-3.1-8B、Qwen2.5-14B、Gemma-2-9B、Prometheus-7B、Mistral-7B）的规模上做系统比较，覆盖 Spearman/Pearson/MSE 以及作者新提出的 Score Distribution Divergence (SDD) 指标。
- 通过 Figure 3 的逐 LLM、逐任务偏差曲线与 Figure 4 的预测分布分析，明确诊断了"为何 LLM-Alignment 任务上去偏无效"——预训练模型已经把预测压在多数类 score=3 上，而随机生成下的 score=3 恰好是低偏差 token，去偏反而放大了 majority 错误。

## Key Contributions

- A **training-free** debiasing pipeline for LLM-as-a-Judge that measures an LLM's intrinsic preference for numeric score tokens by instructing it to do random number generation (RNG) in the evaluator's score range, then quantifies the deviation from a uniform distribution as "latent number bias".
- A **task-conditioned variant (Deb-T)** that prepends the downstream task instruction to the RNG prompt, empirically showing that the latent bias is task- and score-range-dependent — the central claim that distinguishes the method from task-agnostic Contextual Calibration.
- A clean **logit-space correction** formula, P̃(y) = softmax(l(y) + λ·B(y)) with B(y) = log U(y) − log P_random(y), requiring only a single scalar hyperparameter λ tuned on the dev set.
- The first to **diagnose** the LLM-Alignment failure mode (Section 5.2): vanilla LLMs collapse onto the majority score 3, which has low RNG bias, so the debiaser amplifies rather than corrects the error — a genuinely informative negative result rather than a hand-waved limitation.
- Introduction of **Score Distribution Divergence (SDD)**, a KL-divergence-based metric that scores the *whole* predicted probability mass over score tokens rather than the argmax/winner-take-all score, allowing finer-grained evaluation of calibrated evaluators.

## Methodology

The method has two stages.

**Preprocessing — measuring latent number bias.** The authors auto-generate 100 RNG prompts ("generate a random integer between {{min_score}} and {{max_score}}") using ChatGPT-o3, then keep the top T_p prompts by lowest perplexity (T_p = 10 by default; they also try T_p = 100). For each surviving prompt x_i they read the LLM's full generation probability p(y|x_i) over the score-token set Y = {0,…,N}, average across prompts (Eq. 1), and clamp tiny values to p_min = 1e-12. Two variants: **Deb-R** uses the bare RNG prompt; **Deb-T** prepends the actual task instruction (e.g., the STS rubric) to the RNG prompt to capture task-conditional bias. A uniform distribution U(y) = 1/(N+1) is the reference.

**Debiasing at scoring time.** The debiasing term B(y) = log U(y) − log P_random(y) (Eq. 2) is added to each score-token logit l(y), and the final calibrated distribution is P̃(y) = softmax(l(y) + λ·B(y)) (Eq. 3). λ is a single scalar searched over {0.1, 0.1, …, 1.0} (and in an ablation, up to 10) on the dev split. The final predicted score follows G-Eval's expectation: s_llm = Σ p̃(y)·y (Eq. 4), rather than argmax. All experiments use zero-shot prompts at temperature 0.2.

**Baselines:** Vanilla (no debiasing), Contextual Calibration (CC, Zhao et al. 2021 — bias measured on context-free inputs like "N/A"), and Distribution Calibration (DC, from Sato et al. 2026 — Monte-Carlo p(y) with Beta-fit q(y), reweight by q(y)/p(y)). **Metrics:** Spearman, Pearson, MSE, plus the new SDD = average KL(Q_x ‖ P_x) over the test set (Eq. 5), where Q_x distributes mass over the two adjacent integer tokens for continuous ground-truth scores (e.g., 3.7 → q(3)=0.3, q(4)=0.7).

## Results & Highlights

- **SummEval (Summ), Spearman correlation ×100, averaged across 5 LLMs:** Deb-T = 38.0 > Deb-R = 35.0 > CC = 36.8 > DC = 32.2 > Vanilla = 34.4. Qwen2.5 reaches 49.0 with Deb-T vs 46.4 vanilla (+2.6 points).
- **STS-B (STS), Spearman, averaged:** Deb-T = 71.5 > CC = 68.1 > Deb-R = 65.0 > Vanilla = 61.9 > DC = 61.0. The headline Llama-3.1-8B result: Deb-T lifts STS Spearman from vanilla 7.45 to 47.0, beating CC (31.5) by +15.5 points and DC (2.43) by +44.6 points.
- **STS-B, SDD (lower is better), averaged:** Deb-R = 1.80, Deb-T = 1.85 — both crush Vanilla = 6.44, CC = 5.59, DC = 4.22. For Llama specifically, Deb-R drives SDD from 11.1 → 2.17; for Qwen, from 7.33 → 1.92.
- **SemRel2024 (STR), MSE, averaged:** Deb-T = 1.21 < Deb-R = 2.37 < CC = 0.967 < Vanilla = 2.86; here CC still wins MSE, but Deb-T takes Spearman (61.7 vs CC 62.8) and is far ahead on SDD for Llama (Deb-R 7.51 → Deb-T 2.54; Qwen 3.20 → 3.22).
- **LLM-Alignment (HelpSteer2) — the negative result:** on average Spearman, Deb-R = 14.4, Deb-T = 13.8, both essentially flat against Vanilla = 13.0; MSE marginally improves (Deb-T 1.69 vs Vanilla 1.75) but Spearman/Pearson do not. Section 5.2 traces this to the vanilla models already collapsing onto the majority score 3, which has low RNG bias — so the debiaser reinforces the wrong mode.
- **Prompt-selection ablation (Table 4):** Deb-R_{100} (all 100 prompts, no perplexity filtering) is marginally better than Deb-R_{10} overall (e.g., STS 66.9 vs 65.0 average Spearman), so the perplexity-based prompt selection contributes little.
- **Hyperparameter-range ablation (Table 5):** extending λ's grid from [0.1, 1] to [0.1, 10] helps sharply in a few cases (Llama STS Deb-R 14.2 → 41.9; STR 25.6 → 38.4) but hurts or is flat in many others, signalling a dev/test gap in λ selection — an open problem the authors flag.
- **Bias-shape findings (Figure 3):** latent biases are highly idiosyncratic — Llama and Prometheus favor the highest scores; Gemma is bimodal (lowest + highest); Qwen and Mistral favor "1"; the same LLM shows different bias shapes for the [1,5] SummEval range versus [0,5]; prepending the task instruction (Deb-T vs Deb-R) visibly changes the curve for every LLM.

## Authors & Credibility

The three authors (Yuma Asato, Kiyoaki Shirai, Natthawut Kertkeidkachorn) are all at JAIST (Japan Advanced Institute of Science and Technology). The work is squarely in their lane — the lead author's most relevant prior work (Sato et al. 2026, arXiv:2601.16444) introduced numerical/scoring bias and the Distribution Calibration baseline that this paper both builds on and supersedes on three of four tasks. The experimental setup is unusually careful for an analysis paper: five open-weights LLMs spanning 7B–14B (including the evaluator-specialized Prometheus-7B), four diverse evaluation tasks with score ranges spanning 0–4 and 0–5, and a comparison against two established calibration baselines rather than strawmen. Cross-validation (5-fold, 1:4 dev:test) is used where benchmarks lack an official split, and the hyperparameter λ is tuned only on dev. The SDD metric is a sensible addition that captures calibration quality, which point-estimate Spearman/Pearson hide. Code is promised "upon acceptance" (footnote 1), which is a minor reproducibility caveat until camera-ready.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty — genuine but incremental.** Reframing scoring bias as "RNG deviation from uniform" is a fresh lens, but the actual correction (subtract a log-bias term from logits, then softmax with a λ knob) is structurally the same kind of affine logit correction as Contextual Calibration; the real delta is *what context you use to elicit the bias* (RNG prompts with optional task prefix vs. CC's "N/A" vs. DC's Monte-Carlo label marginals). The contribution is real but it is a calibration-elicitation variant, not a new debiasing paradigm.
- **Rigor — strong on diagnosis, weaker on statistical reporting.** Tables 2–3 report point estimates with no standard deviations, no significance tests, and a single 5-fold split on HelpSteer2/SummEval. Many "Deb-T beats CC" claims (e.g., STR Spearman average 61.7 vs 62.8 — CC is actually *higher*) are within noise that the paper does not acknowledge; the headline "outperforms" should be read as "outperforms on average and on Summ/STS, ties or loses on STR".
- **Reproducibility — medium.** RNG-prompt generation depends on ChatGPT-o3 (closed model); the 100 seed prompts are not fully released; λ grid search is tiny ({0.1,…,1.0}) yet dev/test mismatch is admitted; perplexity-based prompt filtering is described as "intuitively set to 10" and the ablation shows it barely matters, but the result also means the method's robustness to that choice is essentially untested beyond two points.
- **Significance — moderate.** The most useful artifact may be Section 5.2's honest diagnosis that debiasing fails when the vanilla model has already collapsed onto a low-RNG-bias majority token — this is a genuine, transferable lesson for anyone deploying LLM-as-a-Judge. The +44.6-pt Llama STS gain over DC is striking but partly reflects that DC is near-zero (2.43) on that cell, i.e., DC is a weak baseline for STS.
- **Red flags / missing ablations.** (1) Only zero-shot, temperature-0.2, naive prompts are tested; the authors themselves note few-shot and chain-of-thought are untested future work, yet G-Eval's whole claim to fame is CoT-based prob weighting, so the comparison is not apples-to-apples. (2) All five LLMs are ≤14B; no GPT-4-class model, so the "scoring bias varies across LLMs" claim is silent on the models most people actually use as judges. (3) λ is shared across all samples; the authors admit per-sample λ would help but do not attempt it. (4) The LLM-Alignment negative result is explained post-hoc but not rescued — the method ships with a known failure mode on one of its four headline tasks.

## Strengths

- Conceptually clean: "if an LLM can't roll a fair die over the score range, measure how unfair the die is, and subtract that off." Easy to explain, easy to implement (~20 lines on top of any HF generate-with-logits call).
- Task-conditioning (Deb-T) is the right idea and the Figure 3 evidence that prepending the task instruction visibly reshapes the bias curve is the most convincing figure in the paper.
- Honest about its own failure: Section 5.2's majority-class-collapse diagnosis is the kind of analysis most debias papers omit.
- Diverse, fair baselines (CC and DC are the two natural competitors) and a new SDD metric that actually measures calibration rather than just ranking.
- Per-LLM bias taxonomy (Llama/Prometheus → top, Qwen/Mistral → "1", Gemma → bimodal) is useful practitioner intelligence.

## Limitations

- **No large/closed LLMs.** All judges are ≤14B open-weights; no GPT-4, Claude, or 70B+ model, so generalization to the judges most widely deployed is unverified.
- **No few-shot / CoT evaluation.** The paper's prompts are zero-shot; G-Eval-style CoT could change the bias profile and the relative ranking of CC/DC/Deb.
- **Statistical reporting absent.** No error bars, no paired tests; several "improvements" (especially on STR and on small LLMs) are within plausible noise.
- **LLM-Alignment failure is unmitigated.** A method that helps 3 of 4 tasks and actively backfires on the 4th is not unconditionally better than its baselines — the boundary of applicability is under-specified.
- **λ tuning is brittle.** Table 5 shows the dev-set-best λ often does not transfer to test; the authors flag this as an open problem but provide no fix.
- **RNG-prompt generation depends on a closed model (ChatGPT-o3).** Reproducibility of the prompt set is not guaranteed.

## Relevance

高度相关。本文直接处理 LLM-as-a-Judge 的评测偏差问题——这是任何用 LLM 做自动评估的研究（包括本库关注的 LLM 评测、奖励建模、对齐评估）都会遇到的核心痛点；其"随机数生成探针 + logit 校正"方案训练无关、即插即用，可作为后续评测流水线的一个标准去偏组件。任务条件化（Deb-T）的设计思路对"偏差随任务变化"这一观察也有方法论价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does latent number bias (and the Deb-T gain) persist or vanish on frontier judges (GPT-4/4o, Claude, Gemini, Llama-3-70B+)? The 7–14B-only scope leaves the practical-deployment question open.
- How does the method interact with chain-of-thought / few-shot prompting (G-Eval's actual recipe)? The bias may shift dramatically once CoT tokens are sampled.
- Can λ be set per-sample (e.g., via an auxiliary "confidence-of-vanilla" signal) instead of globally, to avoid the LLM-Alignment majority-class backfire?
- Is the latent number bias stable across seeds / prompt paraphrases, or is it an artifact of the 100 ChatGPT-o3-generated RNG prompts? A seed-sweep would establish whether the measured bias is a model property or a prompt property.
- Could RNG-based bias measurement be extended to non-integer / continuous scoring (e.g., 0–100 rubric scores, pairwise preference probabilities) where the score-token set is large?
- The LLM-Alignment failure suggests a hybrid: only apply the debiaser when the vanilla model's score distribution is *not* concentrated on a low-RNG-bias majority token. Can this gating rule be formalized and made automatic?
