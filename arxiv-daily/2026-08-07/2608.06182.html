On Same-Sample and Independent-Sample Stochastic Extragradient for Monotone Variational Inequalities

▸ Key Finding

Same-sample SEG diverges almost surely where independent-sample SEG converges — the DSEG step-size trick makes I-SEG converge but makes S-SEG blow up. Sample independence is load-bearing, not a proof convenience.

Key Metric
O(N−(1−α))
High-probability restricted-gap rate on possibly unbounded domains, for both I-SEG and S-SEG, with ½ < α < 1 and square-summable symmetric step-sizes.
Why This Matters
The I-SEG / S-SEG separation directly decides a real engineering choice: reuse one minibatch for both extragradient evaluations, or draw two.
Consequence for minimax / GAN / adversarial training implementations.
Critical Edge
No experiments — the S-SEG divergence is never demonstrated on a realistic minimax/GAN problem, and the counterexamples are low-dimensional and somewhat pathological.
It is unclear how often the constructed VIPs are representative of real workloads.

01 The Fundamental Separation: DSEG step-sizes

Hsieh et al. (2020) proposed asymmetric DSEG step-sizes to fix I-SEG's last-iterate non-convergence. This paper shows the same rule fails for S-SEG — a structural, not technical, gap.

I-SEG — converges

step-sizes: γ̂ₖ ∈ (0, 1/(3L)), Σγₖγ̂ₖ=∞, Σγₖ²<∞
E[ErrB(y,D)(x̂ₙ)] ≤ Cy,D / Γₙ
rate: O(N−(1−a)), ½<a<1
✓ last-iterate gap → 0

S-SEG — diverges a.s.

γ̂ₖ = 1/log(k+k₀), γₖ = η/(k+k₀)
on F(x;ξ)=(±ρI+J)x, x★=0
‖xₙ‖ ~ C·(log N)q, q=η(ρ²−1)>1
✗ ‖xₙ‖→∞ and x̂ₙ→∞ a.s.

02 What Provably Works

  • Unbounded-domain high-prob gap (Cor. 3.5): O(N−(1−α)) for both variants under symmetric square-summable steps.
  • S-SEG compact-domain (Cor. 2.12): O((LD²+σ²/L+σD)/√N) — but L is the uniform sample Lipschitz constant.
  • S-SEG a.s. last-iterate (Thm 4.1): convergence to a VIP solution under quasi-strict monotonicity + Robbins–Monro.

03 Impossibility Results

  • Square-summability is necessary (Thm 3.9/3.10): with γₖ=1/√(k+1), both I-SEG and S-SEG diverge a.s., ‖xₖ‖ ≥ N3/2−o(1).
  • High-prob form is unavoidable (Thm 3.12): limiting radius R∞ has unbounded support — no deterministic bound region works a.s.
  • Bounded variance insufficient (Prop 3.2): on X=[0,a], S-SEG stays trapped in Y=[1,a] forever.

04 Load-Bearing Assumptions

  • Uniform samplewise Lipschitzness (Asm 2.3) for S-SEG — not replaceable by bounded variance.
  • Generalized variance (Asm 2.6) suffices for I-SEG — a genuine asymmetry.

05 Limitations

  • High-prob constants exponential in Γ=Σγₖ² — asymptotic, not practical.
  • Strictly monotone + Lipschitz; no strong monotonicity, cocoercivity, or acceleration.
  • Single-call variants (SOG) explicitly deferred.

07 Open Questions for Further Exploration

Does S-SEG divergence survive a same mini-batch of size B>1? Is there a critical B★?
Does the γ²-coefficient sign-flip analysis extend to stochastic optimistic gradient (Popov)?
Are the exp-in-Γ constants tight, or can Freedman-style concentration give poly(1/δ)?
Is the Prop 3.2 failure robust to smoothing the discontinuous sample operators?
Can a correlated sampling scheme (antithetic / stratified) rescue S-SEG on the cheap?
Does the separation survive a strictly-monotone component added to the rotation J?
