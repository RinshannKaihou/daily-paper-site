> **一句话总结：** 本文用 Pearl 因果图把"以图思考"模型的工具调用分解为观察中介路径和动作捷径路径，并通过 policy/trajectory/step 三层干预发现：六个主流模型在五个细粒度感知基准上的整体准确率提升几乎全部来自少数"Calibrated"轨迹，大量工具调用要么对答案无因果影响（Calling Without Looking），要么规划混乱（Looking Without Planning），揭示了视觉工具使用的"幻觉"。

> **TL;DR:** The paper casts visual tool-use (crop-and-zoom in thinking-with-images MLLMs) as a causal graph and audits six models (DeepEyes, Pixel Reasoner, Mini-o3, Qwen3-VL-4B/8B, Thyme) on five benchmarks (V\*, HR-Bench-4K/8K, VisualProbe, MME-RealWorld-Lite) via three-level interventions. The headline finding: trajectory-level observation corruption collapses Mini-o3 accuracy on V\* by -64.2 pp (87.8 -> 23.6, 84.8% trajectories hitting the tool-call limit) and Qwen3-VL-8B by -60.7 pp, yet a step-level counterfactual probe (Visual Evidence Gain) shows the policy-level accuracy gain is carried almost entirely by a Calibrated minority (e.g., +7.6 of Qwen3-VL-8B's +6.9 pp ATE on V\*), exposing an "illusion of visual tool-use."

## 核心贡献

- **因果建模框架**：首次将"以图思考"（thinking-with-images）的工具调用形式化为结构因果模型（SCM），区分三类路径——直接路径 (I,Q)→Y、观察中介路径 T→O→Y（真正承载视觉证据）、动作诱导捷径 T→Y（仅靠"调用了工具"这件事影响答案）。
- **三层干预协议**：在 policy（对比直接推理）、trajectory（运行时把每次返回的观察替换为噪声裁剪）、step（在固定前缀下反事实替换单步观察）三个粒度上做干预，并提出可估计的步级指标 **Visual Evidence Gain (VEG)**，对应 Pearl 自然间接效应（NIE）的局部反事实实现。
- **两种策略失配模式**：识别 **Calling Without Looking (CWL)**——返回的视觉内容对答案无因果贡献；**Looking Without Planning (LWP)**——观察有用但调度不连贯（饱和后继续调用、反复裁剪无关区域、耗尽预算）。
- **四组诊断器**：用五个无需训练的特征（n, g0, V^max, HitMax, POER）把每条轨迹确定性地分入 No-call / Mode1 / Calibrated / Mode2，并据此把 policy-level ATE 加性分解到各组。
- **"视觉工具使用幻觉"**：实证显示整体准确率提升几乎全部来自 Calibrated 子集，CWL/LWP/No-call 净贡献接近零或为负，由此提出"模型是否在用图思考"比"模型是否调用了视觉工具"更根本。
- **RL 陷阱假设**：把策略失配归因于 outcome-only RL——同一正确答案的 Calibrated 和 Mode1 轨迹获得相同奖励，目标函数无法偏好前者，给出三条可操作用法（超越准确率的评估、推理时适配、过程感知信用分配）。

## Key Contributions

- **Causal formulation of visual tool-use.** Each rollout is an SCM over (I, Q, T_1:n, O_1:n, Y) where the same MLLM policy induces tool actions and the final answer. Three paths from input to answer are isolated: the direct path, the intended observation-mediated path, and an action-induced shortcut that bypasses O.
- **Three-level causal interventions.** Policy-level toggles tool-use vs direct inference; trajectory-level replaces every returned crop with a corrupted patch (RANDOM-CROP, NOISE, BLANK) at runtime, allowing the policy to react; step-level counterfactually swaps a single O_i under a fixed prefix and reads the option-restricted softmax gap.
- **Visual Evidence Gain (VEG).** A step-level estimand VEG_i = ΔM^real − ΔM^cf that cancels the action-induced shortcut T_i → Y and measures only the local contribution of the returned visual content — a counterfactual instantiation of Pearl's Natural Indirect Effect.
- **Policy miscalibration taxonomy.** Two failure modes are operationalized: Calling Without Looking (CWL — observation has zero causal effect, either via saturated prior or structurally inactive calls) and Looking Without Planning (LWP — informative calls but incoherent schedule, e.g. post-saturation over-extension or budget exhaustion).
- **Trajectory-level diagnostic and ATE decomposition.** A deterministic four-group classifier (No-call / Mode1 / Calibrated / Mode2) decomposes the policy-level accuracy gain; the authors show the gain is concentrated almost entirely in the Calibrated subset.
- **The illusion of visual tool-use.** Despite aggregate accuracy gains, tool-use is not causally effective across most rollouts. The authors pair this with an RL-trap hypothesis attributing the failure to outcome-only RL, and propose three operational uses (beyond-accuracy evaluation, inference-time adaptation, process-aware credit assignment).

## Methodology

The paper builds a structural causal model in which the MLLM policy π emits tool actions T_i (CROP-AND-ZOOM regions), a deterministic visual engine E_tool returns crops O_i, and the same policy finally commits Y. Three causal paths connect (I, Q) to Y: the direct path (no tools), the observation-mediated path T_i → O_i → Y (genuine grounding), and the action-induced shortcut T_1:n → Y (the mere presence of a call shifts the answer).

Three interventions are designed at progressively finer granularity:

- **Policy-level (§3.3.1).** Toggle π_tool vs π_direct by swapping the system prompt while holding weights and decoding fixed. Estimand: ATE_policy = E_do(π=π_tool)[1[Y=Y*]] − E_do(π=π_direct)[...]. Six models (DeepEyes, Pixel Reasoner, Mini-o3, Qwen3-VL-4B/8B on Qwen2.5/3-VL backbones, and code-based Thyme) are evaluated on V*, HR-Bench-4K/8K, VisualProbe (Easy+Med+Hard), and MME-RealWorld-Lite.
- **Trajectory-level (§3.3.2).** At runtime, every requested O_i is replaced by a corrupted crop of the same shape (RANDOM-CROP sampled from another location in I; NOISE and BLANK as ablations). The intervention propagates through subsequent thoughts/actions, so the ATE_traj captures both evidence removal and induced repair/budget-exhaustion. A force-answer variant isolates the truncation component.
- **Step-level (§3.3.3).** VEG_i = ΔM^real − ΔM^cf where ΔM is the change in an option-restricted probability gap g(C) = P̃(Y*|C) − P̃(y_err|C) after appending the real vs a counterfactual crop under an identical prefix and tool action. Because T_i and the prefix are held fixed, the T → Y shortcut cancels and VEG measures only the visual contribution.

A deterministic trajectory classifier (Algorithm 1) uses five features — call count n, pre-first-call gap g_0, peak per-call V^max, HitMax, and Post-saturation Over-Extension Rate (POER) — with thresholds τ_sat = 0.95 and ε = 0.01 to label each rollout as No-call / Mode1 / Mode2 / Calibrated, enabling an additive ATE_policy = Σ_b f_b · Δ̄_policy,b decomposition.

## Results & Highlights

- **Policy-level gains are small and uneven (Table 1).** DeepEyes shows essentially null ATE across all five benchmarks (0.0 / −1.4 / −0.6 / +0.0 / −0.2 pp on V\*, HR-4K, HR-8K, VisualProbe, MME-RW-Lite). The largest single gain is Mini-o3 on VisualProbe at **+21.3 pp** (33.7 → 55.0), and Qwen3-VL-8B on V\* at **+6.9 pp** (84.2 → 91.1). MME-RealWorld-Lite actually regresses for Qwen3-VL-8B (**−3.7 pp**).
- **Trajectory-level corruption is catastrophic for some models (Table 2).** Replacing every returned crop with a RANDOM-CROP crashes Mini-o3 V\* accuracy from 87.8 to 23.6 (**−64.2 pp**) with **84.8%** of trajectories hitting the tool-call limit; Qwen3-VL-8B falls from 91.1 to 30.4 (**−60.7 pp**) with 59.2% Hit-MaxT. DeepEyes and Thyme (mean tool calls ≈ 0.1–1.0) are barely affected (Δ ≈ 0).
- **Force-answer ablation isolates genuine evidence loss (Table 11).** Forcing a final answer at the tool-call limit recovers much of the drop for Qwen3-VL-8B (30.4 → 73.0) but still leaves a **−18.1 pp** residual gap from clean; Mini-o3 remains **−51.2 pp** below clean, confirming useful visual evidence is genuinely lost, not merely truncated.
- **Step-level VEG exposes that most calls do nothing (Table 3).** Fraction of calls with |VEG| < 0.01 on V\*: **81% for DeepEyes, 56% for Qwen3-VL-8B, 29% for Mini-o3**. On correct vs incorrect trajectories, Qwen3-VL-8B's VEG is +0.28 vs −0.20 — useful observations on successes and actively harmful ones on failures.
- **The gain is concentrated in the Calibrated minority (Tables 4–5).** On V\*, DeepEyes has only **7.9% Calibrated** trajectories (72.8% Mode 1), Mini-o3 **45.0%**, Qwen3-VL-8B **20.9%** (70.7% Mode 1). Calibrated is the only group whose ATE contribution is positive across all four models and accounts for the majority of every non-zero ATE: +7.6 of Qwen3-VL-8B's +6.9, +3.3 of Mini-o3's +5.5.
- **Mode 1 calls are performative, not perceptual (App. E.3).** In Mode 1 trajectories, the pre-call `<think>` block already commits to the final answer in **~58%** of cases, versus **~28%** for Calibrated trajectories — visual tool-use without visual perception.
- **Mode 2 pathologies on Mini-o3 (App. E.3).** Accuracy peaks at N=3 calls (78.2%) and collapses to 0% at the N=12 limit; 38% of crops cover < 4% of the source image; on the budget-saturated subset, the fraction of restarts (vs hierarchical refinement) doubles from 27.6% to 56.4%; all 20 trajectories that hit the tool-call limit are wrong.
- **Cross-benchmark replication (HR-Bench-4K, Tables 18–20).** The qualitative signatures reproduce: DeepEyes 86.9% near-zero calls, Mini-o3 Calibrated contributes +3.4 of +4.8 pp ATE, Qwen3-VL-8B Calibrated contributes +4.4 of +5.0 pp.
- **Robustness.** Threshold sweeps (Tables 15–16) over ε ∈ {0.005…0.05} and τ_sat ∈ {0.80…0.99} leave the model ordering and the "Calibrated carries the ATE" conclusion unchanged; repeated-seed runs (App. B.5, C.1) reproduce VEG correct/incorrect splits.

## Authors & Credibility

The authors are from the **Shanghai AI Laboratory, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, and Shanghai Innovation Institute**, with Chaochao Lu (corresponding author) leading the OpenCausaLab group whose prior work on causal evaluation of LLMs/MLLMs (CELLO, CauSight, "Beyond Surface Structure") directly grounds the SCM framework used here. The methodology rigorously separates the *estimand* (Pearl-style ATE/NIE) from the *implementation* (system-prompt swap, online corruption, counterfactual crop with identical prefix), provides force-answer controls and multi-seed robustness checks, and discloses model-access constraints (policy/trajectory levels work on black-box; step-level VEG needs white-box logits, so OpenAI o3/o4-mini are excluded). Code is released at https://github.com/OpenCausaLab/CauAudit. The authors explicitly state in App. F that LLMs were used only for prose polishing.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty (high).** This is, to my knowledge, the first work to apply Pearl's Natural Indirect Effect to *multimodal tool-use trajectories*, decomposing per-step visual contribution via a counterfactual crop that cancels the action-induced shortcut. The framing — "does the observation causally mediate the answer, or does the call just look like reasoning?" — is genuinely new relative to Wei et al. (2026) (the marginal-improvement paradox at policy level), Ma et al. (2026) (intrinsic vs tool-mastery), and Hou/Xu et al. (2025) (unfaithful tool-use), all of which stay at policy/behavioral level.
- **Rigor (strong).** Three intervention levels triangulate the claim; the force-answer ablation cleanly separates evidence loss from truncation; threshold sweeps over (τ_sat, ε) and repeated-seed runs bound the sensitivity; the open-ended VEG extension (App. D, length-normalized gold log-likelihood) confirms patterns beyond MCQ without overclaiming cross-scale comparability. The honest disclosure that the BLANK-vs-RANDOM-CROP gap for Qwen3-VL means "the policy detects corruption but is fooled by statistically plausible ones" is exactly the kind of detail that elevates the analysis.
- **Reproducibility (good with caveats).** Checkpoints, prompts, decoding configs (Table 7), corruption schemes, and thresholds are all specified; code is public. However, the step-level VEG requires **white-box token logits**, so the headline tool is not portable to closed models (OpenAI o3/o4-mini, Gemini) — a significant scope limit given those are the most-cited thinking-with-images systems.
- **Significance (high for evaluation methodology, moderate for capability).** The diagnostic is more actionable than a leaderboard: it tells practitioners *where* (CWL vs LWP) and *when* (saturated vs not) their tool policy is broken. The RL-trap hypothesis, if confirmed by future controlled training, would redirect how thinking-with-images models are trained.
- **Red flags / weaknesses.**
  - The **RL-trap hypothesis is explicitly not causally established** (the authors say so in Limitations): they observe CWL/LWP *after* training and *infer* outcome-only RL is to blame, but never run matched outcome-only vs process-aware training. The "trap" framing is therefore somewhat stronger than the evidence supports.
  - The diagnostic is **MCQ-centric**: the bounded probability-gap readout and the four-group classifier only work cleanly on V*, HR-Bench, MME-RealWorld-Lite (all MCQ). On VisualProbe (open-ended) the authors only report supplementary VEG and explicitly do *not* transfer the classifier, so the headline "Calibrated carries the ATE" claim is technically only certified on MCQ.
  - The **tool set is restricted to CROP-AND-ZOOM**. The authors argue the framework transfers (with tool-specific counterfactuals for OCR, segmentation, video-frame selection), but do not demonstrate it.
  - Saturation threshold τ_sat = 0.95 is reasonable but the Mini-o3 case at τ_sat = 0.80 shows some Calibrated trajectories get reclassified as Mode1/Mode2, meaning the absolute group fractions should be read with uncertainty bands, not as point estimates.
  - **Six models is a thin sample for cross-architecture claims**; in particular, the only code-based model (Thyme) makes 0.1 calls/item and barely uses tools, so it does little to test whether code-based interfaces escape the illusion.

## Strengths

- A genuinely original causal lens: formulating tool-use rollouts as an SCM with three named paths, and instantiating VEG as a local NIE estimate, is a contribution that should generalize beyond crop-and-zoom.
- The trajectory-level diagnostic is **deterministic, training-free, and feature-interpretable** — practitioners can run it on their own models without fitting a classifier.
- Honest, paper-specific failure characterization: CWL (saturated-prior vs structurally-inactive sub-cases) and LWP (with four audited pathologies: accuracy collapse with more calls, local fixation, non-progressive restarts, absent deadline-aware commitment) are concrete, not generic.
- The decomposition that shows Calibrated is the *only* universally positive group across four models is a clean, falsifiable claim, replicated on HR-Bench-4K.
- Strong reproducibility hygiene: public code, threshold sweeps, multi-seed runs, force-answer control, three corruption schemes, open-ended extension.

## Limitations

- Step-level VEG needs white-box logits, excluding closed models (OpenAI o3/o4-mini) that dominate public discussion of thinking-with-images.
- Only CROP-AND-ZOOM is studied; OCR, segmentation, video-frame, and code-based image-manipulation tools are left to future work, so the cross-tool generality is asserted, not demonstrated.
- The "illusion" claim is certified primarily on MCQ benchmarks (V\*, HR-Bench, MME-RealWorld-Lite); the open-ended VisualProbe only provides supplementary directional VEG, and the four-group classifier is not transferred there.
- The RL-trap hypothesis is consistent with observations but not causally established — confirming it requires controlled outcome-only vs process-aware training runs the authors do not perform.
- The trajectory-level intervention conflates two effects (evidence removal and induced repair/truncation); the force-answer ablation only partially disentangles them, and residual policy reactions may still contribute.
- Six models, all sharing Qwen backbones (five of six on Qwen2.5-VL-7B), limit the diversity of architectures audited; a single non-Qwen family would strengthen cross-architecture claims.

## Relevance

高度相关：该论文直接审视了当前多模态 LLM 中"以图思考/视觉工具调用"范式的因果有效性，并用因果推断方法学（Pearl 的 do-intervention 与自然间接效应）系统性地揭示了策略失配问题，这既是多模态推理、Agent 工具使用、机制可解释性三个研究方向的交叉点，也对如何训练和评估视觉 Agent 具有直接方法论指导意义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would process-aware RL (rewarding VEG-positive calls, penalizing saturated and post-saturation calls) actually shift the group distribution toward Calibrated, or does the RL-trap persist because the gradient signal is too sparse?
- Does the illusion reproduce on closed-source thinking-with-images models (o3, o4-mini, Gemini) using only the policy- and trajectory-level interventions that do not require white-box access?
- How does the diagnostic change when the tool set is expanded to OCR, segmentation, or video-frame selection — do action-induced shortcuts grow or shrink relative to observation-mediated paths?
- Is the Calibrated subset learnable from unlabeled rollouts via VEG-style self-supervision, enabling inference-time routing that bypasses tool-use for predicted Mode 1 cases?
- The probability-gap saturation threshold τ_sat = 0.95 is MCQ-specific; what is the principled analogue for free-form generation, and does the open-ended length-normalized log-likelihood from App. D support a calibrated four-group classifier?
- Do the four Mode 2 pathologies (accuracy collapse with N, local fixation, non-progressive restarts, absent deadline-aware commitment) correlate with specific training-data biases, e.g. over-representation of long multi-call rollouts in the RL trajectory mix?
