> **一句话总结：** 本文用 Multivocal 文献综述方法，提出一个六维（机制/目标/数据/持久性/范围/模型类型）分类法，把从传统机器学习到多模态大模型的 48 种"训练后适配"技术统一编目，并将每个维度对接 NIST AI RMF、欧盟 AI Act、MDR/IVDR、FDA PCCP 等监管框架，解决"fine-tuning 一词多义"等术语与合规描述难题。

> **TL;DR:** This survey from Karolinska Institutet proposes a six-dimensional taxonomy (Mechanism, Goal, Data, Persistence, Scope, Model-Type-as-modulator) that classifies 48 post-training adaptation techniques spanning Traditional ML → DL → FM → LLM → MLLM, organized into a centerpiece table (Table 8) and mapped onto four regulatory regimes (NIST AI RMF, EU AI Act, EU MDR/IVDR, FDA PCCP). Against the comparative benchmark of 7 prior surveys (Table 1) — whose dimensional counts range from 1 to 3 and technique counts from ~6 to ~15 — this work delivers 6 dimensions and 48 techniques, the only surveyed framework that simultaneously spans Traditional ML through MLLM and includes an explicit governance mapping. It is a positioning/conceptual contribution with no empirical experiments; an exploratory UMAP projection reports a near-zero mean silhouette score, confirming that the 9 navigational families are not separable statistical clusters and that multi-dimensional characterization is necessary.

## 核心贡献

- **提出六维分类法（D1–D6）**：用机制（Mechanism）、目标（Goal）、数据需求（Data Requirements）、持久性（Persistence）、范围（Scope）五个刻画维度，加模型类型（Model Type）一个调制维度，给每种适配技术一个六元坐标 `(d1,d2,d3,d4,d5,d6)`，从结构上消解"PEFT 到底算微调还是效率技术"这类单轴分类无法回答的歧义。
- **48 种技术的中心表（Table 8）**：把 Training、Full/Partial FT、PEFT、ReFT、DA、FSL、Retraining、CL、TIL、DIL、SFT、RLHF、DPO、RLAIF/CAI、RLVR/GRPO、LP、LEACE、Adversarial Training、Meta-Learning、MTL、Self-Play、Curriculum/Active Learning、Data Augmentation、Semi-SL、SSL/CPT、DP-FT、FL、KD、Compression、Task Arithmetic/Merging、Long-Context Ext.、MoE、PE、Prompt Learning、APO、RAG、ICL、CE、TTA、Test-Time Compute Scaling、Calibration、Cross-Modal Alignment、Modality-Specific Adapters、Multimodal Instr. Tuning、Machine Unlearning、Knowledge Editing、Activation Steering 全部纳入同一张六维坐标表，并按 9 个导航族组织。
- **模型类型作为调制维度（D6）**：显式建模 `Traditional ML → DL → FM → {LLM | MLLM}` 的层级，刻画"继承（inheritance）、取代（supersession）、涌现类别"三种关系——例如经典 Domain Adaptation 在 LLM 层被 CPT + 领域 PEFT 取代、Meta-Learning 被 ICL 功能性替代，从而解决了"把 LLM 术语套到随机森林上"的模型类型混淆。
- **四套监管框架的对接（Section 5 + Table 9/10）**：把 D1–D6 映射到 NIST AI RMF、EU AI Act、EU MDR/IVDR、FDA PCCP，并用 Table 10 给出"前后对比"案例——把模糊的变更日志"the model was fine-tuned"翻译成两层精确的六维规格（PEFT 层 + RAG 层），让审计员能判断是否需要全系统再验证。
- **解决三个具体术语/合规痛点**：(1) 术语歧义——"Fine-Tuning"可指全参数更新、部分层冻结或 adapter 训练；(2) 单轴分类的歧义放置；(3) 模型类型混淆。作者特别指出 EU AI Act 的 GPAI 算力阈值（训练 FLOPs 的三分之一）会漏掉 Knowledge Editing、Activation Steering 这类低算力但行为影响巨大的技术（引 Hacker & Holweg）。
- **正式承认 Explainability 为独立的适配目标（D2）**：在 EU AI Act Art. 13 与 FDA PCCP 透明度义务下，把 probing、concept bottleneck、attention alignment 这类为满足透明度义务而做的修改，从泛化的 Task Specialization / Alignment 中分离出来。
- **方法论上引入 Multivocal Literature Review（MLR）**：因为产业实践远快于同行评审周期，作者从 12 个种子簇（18 篇种子论文）做 backward/forward snowballing，并主动纳入 Hugging Face PEFT、mergekit、EasyEdit、ms-swift 等灰色文献。

## Key Contributions

- **A six-dimensional taxonomy (D1–D6)** that assigns every adaptation technique a coordinate `(d1, d2, d3, d4, d5, d6)` across Mechanism, Goal, Data Requirements, Persistence, Scope, plus Model Type as a modulating (not characterizing) dimension. The D1–D5 vs D6 split prevents the chronic confusion between *technique properties* and *target-model properties*.
- **A centerpiece Table 8** profiling all 48 techniques — from Training, Retraining, Full/Partial FT, PEFT, ReFT, DA, FSL, CL/TIL/DIL, SFT, RLHF, DPO, RLAIF/CAI, RLVR/GRPO, LP, LEACE, Adversarial Training, Meta-Learning, MTL, Self-Play, Curriculum/Active Learning, Data Augmentation, Semi-SL, SSL/CPT, DP-FT, FL, KD, Compression, Task Arithmetic/Merging, Long-Context Extension, MoE, PE, Prompt Learning, APO, RAG, ICL, CE, TTA, Test-Time Compute Scaling, Calibration, Cross-Modal Alignment, Modality-Specific Adapters, Multimodal Instruction Tuning, Machine Unlearning, Knowledge Editing, Activation Steering — organized into 9 navigational families.
- **Model Type (D6) as a modulator** that introduces a relation structure (inheritance / supersession / emergent category creation) on top of D1–D5: classical DA is superseded at the LLM tier by CPT + domain PEFT; meta-learning is functionally replaced by ICL; `d1 = Context Injection`, `d4 = Session-Ephemeral`, and `d3 = Paired Multimodal` only become dominant at the `{LLM, MLLM}` branch points.
- **Explicit four-regime governance mapping** (NIST AI RMF, EU AI Act, EU MDR/IVDR, FDA PCCP) in Section 5 and Table 9, plus a worked before/after change-log example (Table 10) translating "the model was fine-tuned on recent hospital data" into two layered six-dimensional profiles (PEFT layer + RAG layer), making it auditable whether full-system re-validation is required.
- **Resolution of three named pain points**: terminological ambiguity (e.g., "FT" meaning full / partial / adapter training), single-axis ambiguity (is PEFT an FT technique or an efficiency technique?), and model-type conflation (using LLM-specific terms for non-LLM systems). The paper specifically argues the EU AI Act GPAI compute threshold (one-third of training FLOPs) fails to capture low-compute-but-high-impact techniques like Knowledge Editing and Activation Steering (citing Hacker & Holweg).
- **Formal recognition of Explainability as a standalone D2 goal**, legally and mechanistically distinct from Task Specialization / Alignment, motivated by EU AI Act Art. 13 and FDA PCCP transparency duties.
- **Multivocal Literature Review (MLR) methodology** that combines backward/forward snowballing from 12 seed clusters (anchored by 18 seed papers) with grey literature from Hugging Face PEFT, mergekit, EasyEdit, and ms-swift — justified because industry techniques (PEFT variants, merge methods) often appear in framework docs years before peer-reviewed publication.

## Methodology

This is a **conceptual survey**, not an empirical paper. The methodology has two layers:

1. **Literature aggregation (MLR).** The authors argue a peer-review-only corpus would omit rapidly-evolving practitioner techniques, so they combine (a) backward and forward snowballing from twelve canonical academic seed clusters anchored by 18 seed papers, with (b) grey literature from major framework documentation (Hugging Face PEFT, mergekit, EasyEdit, ms-swift), developer reports, and industrial analysis. A candidate technique received a separate row in Table 8 if it represented an *independently selectable adaptation decision unit* with a materially distinct six-dimensional profile. Saturation was reached when additional candidates could be represented as variants, sub-techniques, or compositions of existing rows.
2. **Taxonomic construction.** Rather than a single hierarchy, each technique is assigned a six-element profile `(d1, d2, d3, d4, d5, d6)`. The dimensions are: **D1 Mechanism** (Parametric Update, Cross-Model Transfer, Parameter Composition, Parametric Compression, Architectural Modification, Context Injection, Activation-Space Manipulation, Inference-Time Search, Pipeline-Mediated); **D2 Goal** (organized under Capability / Lifecycle / Trust / Knowledge Operations / Operational Constraints meta-categories, with Explainability promoted to a first-class goal); **D3 Data Requirements** (Large/Small Labeled, Few Demonstrations, Preference Pairs, Task Distribution, Teacher-/Constitution-/Environment-/Instruction-Derived, User Interaction Data, Test-Only, Sequential/Incremental, External Corpus, Forget Set Specification, Zero-Shot, Parameter-Only, Decentralized, Pipeline-Dependent, Unlabeled, Verifiable Task, Paired Multimodal); **D4 Persistence** (Scheduled/Ad-hoc Permanent, Bounded/Unbounded Cumulative, Version-Persistent, Session-Ephemeral, Transient, Pipeline-Dependent); **D5 Scope** (Whole-Model, Fused Composition, Partial, Surrogate Model, Compressed Artifact, Modular, Input/Output-Space, Activation-Space, Distributed, Pipeline-Dependent); **D6 Model Type** (Traditional ML → DL → FM → {LLM | MLLM}, with FM further split into RepFM vs GenFM lineages). The taxonomy is then mapped to four regulatory regimes.
3. **Exploratory structural check.** A Gower-style dissimilarity matrix over D1–D6 was projected with UMAP (Appendix D) as a *diagnostic* of whether the 9 navigational families occupy coherent regions of categorical profile space — not as validation.

## Results & Highlights

No quantitative results reported in the empirical-experiment sense — this is a conceptual survey with no benchmarks, training runs, or held-out evaluations. The evidence it offers instead is structural/comparative:

- **Coverage delta vs prior surveys (Table 1):** the paper classifies **48 techniques** on **6 dimensions**, against the 7 most-relevant prior surveys (Pan & Yang ~8 tech / 1 dim; Zhuang et al. ~15 / 1; Shi et al. ~12 / 2; Liu et al. ~10 / 1; Hospedales et al. ~8 / 3; Gao et al. ~6 / 1; Wehner et al. ~10 / 1). It is the **only** entry in that comparison with `D6 = Yes` (model type as a structuring axis), `Gov. = Yes` (governance mapping), and a Model Span reaching `Trad. ML – MLLM`.
- **Boundary precision via D6 supersession relations:** classical Domain Adaptation (Row 6) is marked superseded at the `{LLM, MLLM}` tier by CPT (Row 27) + domain-specific PEFT (Row 4); Meta-Learning (Row 20) is functionally replaced by ICL (Row 39); Incremental Learning is subsumed by Continual Learning (Row 9). These supersession claims are stated categorically in Section 3.7.2.
- **Exploratory UMAP diagnostic (Section 3.10, Appendix D):** the projection of the 48 six-dimensional profiles yields a **near-zero mean silhouette score**, quantitatively confirming that the 9 navigational families are *not* separable statistical clusters — which the authors interpret as evidence that single-axis or family-label grouping is insufficient and multi-dimensional coordinates are necessary. (They explicitly state this analysis "does not independently validate the taxonomy.")
- **Regulatory-ambiguity case study (Table 10):** a single change-log entry "the model was fine-tuned on recent hospital data" is decomposed into two layered profiles — Layer 1 PEFT `(Parametric Update, Drift Remediation, Small Labeled, Ad-hoc Permanent, Modular, LLM)` and Layer 2 RAG `(Context Injection, Knowledge Update, External Corpus, Version-Persistent, Input/Output-Space, LLM)` — demonstrating that the same prose description can hide two materially different compliance pathways.
- **Cited evidence on the brittleness of "remove" operations:** benign relearning attacks can reverse Machine Unlearning (cited), and ROME-style Knowledge Editing edits degrade after approximately **10 sequential applications** (cited) — used to argue that governance frameworks cannot treat "remove knowledge" as a single atomic operation.

## Authors & Credibility

- All three authors are at **Karolinska Institutet** (Department of Clinical Science, Intervention and Technology), a leading medical university; Seoane and Abtahi also hold appointments at Karolinska University Hospital, the University of Borås, and KTH Royal Institute of Technology. This clinical-regulatory positioning directly explains the paper's strong MDR/IVDR and FDA PCCP emphasis — unusual for an ML-taxonomy paper.
- The paper is positioned as a **survey/conceptual contribution**, not an experimental paper, and the authors are appropriately modest about this: Section 3.10 states the UMAP analysis "does not independently validate the taxonomy," and Section 6.2 lists five explicit scope boundaries.
- The bibliography is broad and current (refs include NIST AI RMF, EU AI Act, EU MDR/IVDR, FDA PCCP, FUTURE-AI, TRIPOD+AI, Hacker & Holweg on GPAI thresholds, Bommasani et al. on foundation models, and recent technique-specific work through 2025–2026).
- The MLR methodology (grey literature + snowballing from 18 seed papers across 12 clusters) is a reasonable choice given the field's velocity, though it trades reproducibility for coverage — the seed-cluster list and snowballing chains are relegated to Appendix A.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty — moderate but genuine.** Six-dimensional taxonomies are not new in software engineering or ontology work, but the specific move of making Model Type a *modulating* dimension rather than a sixth peer axis — and using it to encode *supersession relations* (DA→CPT, meta-learning→ICL) — is a real conceptual contribution that prior ML adaptation surveys (Table 1) genuinely lack. The D2 promotion of Explainability to a first-class goal, motivated by EU AI Act Art. 13, is also non-obvious.
- **Rigor — strong on definition, weak on validation.** The dimension definitions (Tables 3–7) are precise and internally consistent, and the boundary-case handling (Section 3.1.4, the §/¶/†/‡ markers in Table 8) is careful. However, the only structural validation is the UMAP projection, and the authors *report a near-zero silhouette score and then argue this is expected* — which is honest but means there is no quantitative evidence that the 9 families or the 6 dimensions are the *right* granularity as opposed to, say, 4 or 8 dimensions. Inter-coder reliability on the canonical profiles (Table 8 was, per Section 1.4, "coded by the authors") is not reported; a second-coder agreement study would substantially strengthen the contribution.
- **Reproducibility — low for the taxonomy itself, high for the references.** The 48-technique list and the per-technique D1–D6 assignments are author judgments; another competent group would likely produce a different count (e.g., whether to split Full vs Partial FT, whether to give APO its own row vs folding into PE). The seed papers and snowballing protocol in Appendix A mitigate this somewhat, but there is no released artifact (no CSV of profiles, no codebook) accompanying the paper.
- **Significance — potentially high in the governance/medical-device niche, lower for pure ML researchers.** The regulatory mapping (Section 5, Tables 9–10) is the most differentiated part of the paper and directly usable by a compliance officer writing a PCCP or MDR Annex II change-control entry. For an ML researcher choosing between PEFT variants, the taxonomy is descriptive but offers no performance guidance (the authors explicitly flag this as limitation (e) in Section 6.2).
- **Red flags / overclaiming — minimal but present.** The paper repeatedly hedges with "may," "can," "potential" when mapping to regulatory regimes — appropriate, since the authors are not lawyers — but the abstract's claim that the taxonomy "distinguishes commonly conflated terms such as fine-tuning, retrieval augmentation, and prompting" is stronger than what the paper actually *demonstrates*; it *defines* the distinction via coordinates, it does not *empirically* show that practitioners conflate them at any measured rate. The specific claim that benign relearning attacks reverse unlearning and that ROME-style edits degrade after ~10 applications are cited to other work (Huang et al., Gupta et al.) rather than reproduced.
- **Missing ablations / analyses a reviewer would want:** (1) no inter-rater reliability on the Table 8 codings; (2) no sensitivity analysis showing how the 48-row list changes under alternative inclusion thresholds; (3) no comparison of *predictive* power — does the six-dimensional profile of a technique predict, say, its regulatory classification or its compute cost, better than a single axis?; (4) the MLLM-specific rows (44–46) are acknowledged as non-exhaustive (limitation (d)), leaving a visible gap in a paper whose title promises MLLM coverage.

## Strengths

- The **modulating-vs-characterizing distinction** (D6 vs D1–D5) is the cleanest resolution I have seen to the "is PEFT fine-tuning or efficiency?" class of ambiguity, and it generalizes to any technique whose profile shifts across model tiers.
- The **governance mapping is concrete and immediately usable**: Table 9 (PCCP component ↔ dimension) and Table 10 (before/after change-log) are the kind of artifacts a regulatory writer can lift directly into a submission, which is rare for an ML taxonomy paper.
- **Honest self-assessment throughout**: the UMAP diagnostic is explicitly not validation, the MLLM coverage gap is admitted, and every regulatory statement is hedged with "may" rather than "does."
- The **ordered-composition operator (⊕)** for layered stacks (e.g., `RLVR ⊕ RLHF ⊕ PEFT ⊕ RAG ⊕ PE`) gives a compact notation for the deployment reality that single-technique papers ignore.
- Treatment of **boundary-extension techniques** (Data Augmentation, Curriculum Learning, Active Learning) as `d1 = Pipeline-Mediated` with their profile inherited from the downstream training method is an elegant way to include them without diluting the core category.

## Limitations

- **No empirical validation.** The taxonomy is a conceptual artifact; there are no experiments showing it improves documentation quality, regulatory review speed, or technique-selection accuracy over a simpler scheme.
- **Single-coder bias.** Table 8 profiles were "coded by the authors" using self-defined dimension definitions; no inter-rater reliability, no second-coder agreement, no released codebook or profile dataset.
- **48 is a soft number.** Reasonable people could split or merge rows (APO vs PE; Full vs Partial FT vs PEFT; whether Long-Context Extension and MoE deserve separate rows). The saturation criterion is stated but not operationalized quantitatively.
- **MLLM coverage is acknowledged as incomplete** (rows 44–46 "establish the branch structure but defer a comprehensive survey").
- **No access-regime axis.** White-box / grey-box / black-box access constrains feasible techniques as much as model type does; the authors flag this as a "future governance overlay" (Section 6.3) but its omission is felt when discussing API-only deployments.
- **Agentic AI is handled only prospectively.** The paper correctly notes agents are an orchestration pattern not a new technique, but the meta-adaptation discussion (agents that fine-tune their own sub-models) is one paragraph.
- **Regulatory mapping is illustrative, not legal.** Repeated "may" hedging is correct but means the paper cannot tell a manufacturer whether a specific intervention *will* trigger re-classification — only how to describe it more precisely.
- **Compute / cost / latency characteristics of techniques are absent** (acknowledged as limitation (e)); for a practitioner choosing between, say, full FT and Knowledge Editing, the taxonomy says they differ on D1/D5 but not which is cheaper or faster.

## Relevance

与用户对"大模型训练后适配、PEFT、对齐、RAG、Agent、模型治理"的研究兴趣高度相关：本文正是把这些散落的技术家族（FT/PEFT/RLHF/DPO/RLVR/RAG/ICL/Unlearning/Knowledge Editing/Activation Steering/多模态适配）放进统一坐标系的综述，并显式对接 AI Act / NIST RMF / FDA PCCP，是做"训练后技术全景图"或"合规视角下的模型变更管理"时可直接引用的上层框架。其 D6 模型类型调制与 supersession 关系（DA→CPT、meta-learning→ICL）对梳理"为什么 LLM 时代不再用某些经典方法"尤其有用。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- **Inter-coder reliability:** if 3–5 independent ML practitioners coded the same 48 techniques into the D1–D6 schema using only the published definitions, what would the agreement be? Which dimensions are the most subjective?
- **Predictive validity:** does a technique's six-dimensional profile predict its regulatory classification (e.g., "substantial modification" under EU AI Act) better than a single-axis label, in a controlled audit setting?
- **Access-regime overlay:** how would adding a D7 (white-box / grey-box / black-box) change the feasible-technique sets, especially for API-only deployments where `d1 = Parametric Update` is impossible?
- **Agentic meta-adaptation:** the paper sketches `d2 = Learn to Adapt` and `d4 = Gradually Accumulated` for self-improving agents — what is the minimal extension to Table 8 to cover SPIN-style self-play fine-tuning, STaR-style self-distillation, and agent-memory accumulation?
- **MLLM completeness:** the paper defers a full MLLM survey — what is the count and profile distribution once rows 44–46 are expanded to cover the 2025–2026 MLLM adaptation literature (visual instruction tuning, modality-routing, any-to-any alignment)?
- **Empirical landscape characterization:** can the near-zero silhouette score be improved by re-weighting dimensions (e.g., weighting D1 Mechanism more than D4 Persistence), and would that yield the separable families the authors say are absent?
- **Operational tooling:** could the six-dimensional profiles be encoded as a machine-readable schema (JSON-LD or similar) so that AEGIS-style governance infrastructure could consume them directly — and would practitioners actually fill them out accurately?
