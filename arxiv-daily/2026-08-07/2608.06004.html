Do Tabular Foundation Models Agree with Themselves?

Christian Klötergens · Vijaya Krishna Yalavarthi · Lars Schmidt-Thieme · Tom Hanika

University of Hildesheim — Institute of Computer Science & VWFS DARC

⬢ Key Finding

Every state-of-the-art tabular foundation model violates both consistency rules on all 17 datasets — its univariate predictions cannot arise from any well-defined joint distribution.

◆ Why This Matters

Tests whether TFM univariate predictions can coherently compose into a multivariate joint — central to Bayesian inference, uncertainty quantification, and any autoregressive use of these models.

▲ Critical Edge

Only target pairs (d=2) are tested; the higher-dimensional autoregressive chains where inconsistency would compound — and any real downstream harm — are never measured. The diagnostic comes with no fix.

01Contributions

The paper stops trying to score tabular foundation models against an unknowable Bayesian posterior, and instead asks a cheaper, sharper question: could the model's own univariate outputs come from any well-defined joint distribution?

  • Marginalization consistency (C1). Marginalizing an intermediate target out of the model's own head must not change what it believes: p̂(a|x;D⁻ᴮ) = ∫ p̂(a|b,x;Dᴬ) p̂(b|x;D⁻ᴬ) db.
  • Factorization consistency (C2). Both chain-rule orders must reconstruct the same joint: p̂(a|b,x)p̂(b|x) = p̂(b|a,x)p̂(a|x).
  • Label-free, prior-free metrics via total-variation distance — needs only the model's outputs, sidestepping the missing-posterior obstacle entirely.
  • Strict ordering proven: C2 ⟹ C1, but not vice versa (Proposition 1 + a counterexample). So one observed marginalization gap already certifies a factorization gap.
  • First systematic audit of six SOTA TFMs — TabPFNv2/v3, TabICLv1/v2, TabDPT, TabFM — framing consistency as an evaluation axis orthogonal to accuracy.

02Methodology

All modern TFMs share the PFN blueprint: a transformer pretrained on millions of synthetic datasets, returning a univariate density p̂(y|x;D) in one forward pass. Multivariate joints are then built autoregressively by feeding sampled targets back as features.

Discrepancies are measured with TV ∈ [0,1]:

  • Classification: closed-form finite sums — exact, no sampling.
  • Regression: each head (5000-bar softmax / 999 quantile knots / 2048 bins) reduced to a common quantile interface on L=2000 levels, K=1000 quadrature atoms, compared on a coarse S=20 (or 128×128) grid.

The coarse grid biases toward under-reporting violations (TV is non-increasing under coarsening) — reported numbers are certified lower bounds. Fully deterministic, 5-fold cross-tested on RTX 2080 Ti & A40.

03Results

Every model violates C1 on every dataset, both tasks. TabFM is the most consistent classifier (wins 5/10 datasets, mean TV 0.0092 on Wine); TabICLv1 is the most inconsistent. On regression no model gets near zero — TabDPT is closest, except on Insurance where it paradoxically posts the highest TV (0.0681).

widerThan C1 gaps 8 / 10TabFM wins (clf)

Violations are larger than marginalization, as theory predicts (C2 is the stronger requirement). TabFM holds the lowest mean TV on 8 of 10 classification datasets. For regression, TabPFNv3 comes closest to factorization consistency — while TabDPT, strong on C1, becomes one of the more inconsistent models under C2.

The two gaps correlate strongly with each other — but neither correlates with accuracy or RMSE. Benchmarks like TabArena, which rank only by accuracy, are blind to this defect. TabFM, the top-ranked TabArena model, is also the most consistent classifier — yet offers no probabilistic regression at all.

04Critical Assessment

Strengths

  • Clean reframing of an unsolvable problem — "compare to the true posterior" is replaced by "could this come from any joint."
  • Exact classification checks (closed-form sums) make the universal-violation claim airtight — not sampling noise.
  • Conservative regression discretization yields certified lower bounds, so numbers are never inflated by approximation.
  • Honest reporting: fold std-devs, worst-instance values, and per-row NLL-vs-TV scatters rather than aggregates alone.
  • The C2 ⟹ C1 ordering is a practitioner's shortcut: measure the cheap C1 to certify the hard C2 is violated.

Red Flags

  • d=2 only. Higher-dimensional autoregressive chains — the regime where inconsistency would actually compound — are never evaluated.
  • Mixed categorical-continuous target pairs are skipped, since today's TFMs split classification/regression across separate weights.
  • "Every model violates" is almost guaranteed for any finite-trained net under exact TV; the practical-harm question is gestured at but unanswered (Figure 4 finds no NLL-vs-gap correlation).
  • Diagnostic only — no training objective, no reconciliation procedure, no fix offered.
  • Several models (TabPFNv3, TabFM) are proprietary / version-unstable; rankings may not persist.

05Relevance

高度相关。本文直击 TFM 作为贝叶斯推断近似器的核心前提——单变量预测能否自洽地拼成多变量联合分布，与对概率建模、不确定性量化及贝叶斯方法可靠性的研究兴趣直接对齐。"无标签即可审计模型内部一致性"的诊断范式，对评估大模型概率判断的合理性同样具有方法论参考价值。

06Open Questions

  • Do the gaps compound with chain length d ≫ 2, and is there a regime where the implied joint becomes unusable for data generation?
  • Can a marginalization-gap training penalty (cheap to measure) close both violations without sacrificing accuracy?
  • Do violations measurably degrade simulation-based Bayesian inference or synthetic tabular generation — where joint structure, not point accuracy, is what matters?
  • Does the consistency–accuracy orthogonality persist as context size D grows, or does the gap shrink asymptotically?
