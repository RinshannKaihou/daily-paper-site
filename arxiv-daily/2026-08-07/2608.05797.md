> **一句话总结：** 本文研究在执行 rollout 之前、仅凭任务描述预测 LLM 智能体任务难度的方法；在 17 个智能体基准（5,230 任务、415,470 条结果）上，用 IRT 拟合难度作为目标，发现 token 级熵是最强的单一特征族，组合特征在分布内达到 Spearman ρ=0.399（跨基准 ρ=0.225），并提出用预测残差诊断数据污染与任务不可行性，同时证明 AUC 在该场景下会高估预测质量。

> **TL;DR:** The authors tackle ex ante prediction of LLM-agent task difficulty across 17 agentic benchmarks (5,230 tasks, 415,470 outcomes): they fit a 1PL IRT model to obtain a per-task difficulty target β, then predict it from pre-rollout features, finding token-level entropy (Qwen3-8B-Base over a reasoning trace) the strongest single feature family and a combined set reaching Spearman ρ=0.399 in-distribution (ρ=0.225 leave-one-benchmark-out, p<0.001 vs. baseline). They also demonstrate that response AUC is misleading (a constant-difficulty predictor still scores AUC 0.715, benchmark-average 0.806) and show that residuals r = z(β) − ẑ flag contamination (django-11451: r=−0.56) versus infeasibility (django-14725: r=+1.41).

## 核心贡献

- **提出"无需 rollout 即可预测智能体任务难度"的系统性框架。** 用 1PL/Rasch IRT 模型从 497 个智能体配置、5,230 个任务的二值成功/失败结果中拟合出任务难度 β_t 作为预测目标，再用任务描述特征直接预测 β_t，绕开昂贵的长程 rollout。
- **证明 AUC 在此场景下是误导性指标，并提出基于排序的评估范式。** 论证了 AUC 同时混淆智能体能力差与任务难度差，常数预测器仍可达 AUC 0.715，基准均值预测可达 AUC 0.806 但 pAcc=0.000；因此后续一律使用 within-benchmark 标准化 z(β) 与 Spearman ρ/pAcc。
- **识别 token 级熵为最强的单一可预测信号，并系统刻画其结构。** 用 Qwen3-8B-Base 在推理轨迹上计算 top-100 重归一化的 Shannon 熵，发现熵"轨迹形状"（spread/shape/gaps 等）比单一均值更有效，跨基准迁移性反而随打分模型数量非单调下降。
- **提出预测残差诊断法用于发现隐藏的任务因素。** 用 r_t = z(β_t) − ẑ_t 区分"异常难"（可行性与评估器缺陷，r>0）与"异常易"（污染与熟悉度，r<0），在 SWE-bench Verified 两个 OpenAI 审计案例上得到与方向一致的残差。
- **公开多基准语料（415,470 条智能体-任务结果、17 个基准、8 类领域），含元数据、验证器摘要、文本描述与 IRT 难度估计**，便于后续行为分析、环境设计与训练研究复用。

## Key Contributions

- Frames **ex ante difficulty prediction for agentic tasks** as a two-stage pipeline: a 1PL/Rasch IRT model fits per-task difficulty β_t and per-agent ability θ_a from binary outcomes (reaching 0.937 response AUC), and a regressor then predicts β_t from task descriptions — bypassing costly long-horizon rollouts.
- Shows **AUC is misleading in this setting** because it conflates ability gaps with difficulty gaps; a constant-difficulty predictor still yields AUC 0.715 and a benchmark-average predictor yields AUC 0.806 (with pAcc 0.000). The paper therefore switches to within-benchmark standardized z(β) plus Spearman ρ and pairwise accuracy.
- Establishes **token-level entropy as the strongest single feature family**: Qwen3-8B-Base Shannon entropy (top-100 renormalized) over a 4k-cap reasoning trace, with trajectory summaries (spread/shape/gaps) beating mean-only aggregation.
- Introduces **residual diagnostics** r_t = z(β_t) − ẑ_t that separate unexpectedly hard tasks (feasibility/evaluator failures, r>0) from unexpectedly easy ones (contamination/familiarity, r<0), validated against two OpenAI-audited SWE-bench Verified examples.
- Releases an **open 17-benchmark, 415,470-outcome corpus** (coding, math, ML, web, function-calling, cybersecurity, terminal, office suites) with metadata, verifier summaries, descriptions, and IRT difficulties for reuse.

## Methodology

**Difficulty target (IRT).** Treat agents a∈A and tasks t∈T via a partially observed binary response matrix Y_{a,t} (1=success, 0=failure, x=missing). The 1PL/Rasch model P(Y_{a,t}=1 | θ_a, β_t) = σ(θ_a − β_t) is fit on observed pairs by stochastic variational inference: independent Gaussian variational factors q(θ_a), q(β_t), zero-centered Gaussian priors, ELBO optimized by ClippedAdam (lr 0.01, 2,000 epochs, grad-clip norm 5.0). Abilities θ are anchored to mean zero to fix the additive invariance; task difficulties β are standardized within each benchmark to z(β) to remove benchmark-identity confound.

**Features (must be cheaper than rollouts).** Given the task-side context C_t (environment + action-space + task description), a reasoning trace R_t is generated by Claude Sonnet 4.6 (4k-token cap). Five feature families: (1) random N(0,1) control; (2) log-context-length baseline; (3) bge-base-en-v1.5 embeddings of R_t reduced with fold-aware PCA; (4) entropy — Shannon entropy over the top-100 renormalized next-token distribution from Qwen3-8B-Base along R_t, aggregated via mean/spread/shape/gaps; (5) full — five-scorer panel entropy (Qwen3-8B-Base, Qwen3-8B-Instruct, Gemma-E4B-it, OLMo-3-7B-Inst, Llama-3.1-8B-Inst) plus cross-scorer disagreement, benchmark metadata, structural properties, and embeddings.

**Cost argument.** Empirical difficulty estimation for 100 tasks at 10 outcomes each ≈ 1.0×10^8 input tokens; the ex-ante feature pass (with reasoning trace) ≈ 4.0×10^6 tokens, a ~25× reduction that grows with required outcome density.

**Evaluation protocol.** Predictions ẑ_t = f(x_t) compared to z(β_t) via Spearman ρ and within-benchmark pairwise accuracy pAcc under K-fold (in-distribution) and leave-one-benchmark-out (LOBO, OOD). Ridge regression is the main estimator; linear, random forest, XGBoost ablated in Appendix A.4.

## Results & Highlights

- **Difficulty is predictable in-distribution, weakly out-of-distribution.** The full combined feature set reaches **Spearman ρ = 0.399 under K-fold** and **ρ = 0.225 under LOBO** on the standardized z(β) target across 17 benchmarks, with p < 0.001 versus the length baseline (paired Wilcoxon). The random control stays near zero; the length baseline is weak (ρ = 0.086 KF, ρ = 0.101 LOBO).
- **Entropy is the strongest single feature family.** Entropy alone: **ρ = 0.193 (KF), ρ = 0.137 (LOBO)**; embeddings surprisingly weak (ρ = 0.107 KF, ρ = 0.017 LOBO, close to the length baseline).
- **AUC demonstrably inflates poor estimates.** On the observed response matrix: Oracle β reaches AUC 0.937; a **constant predictor still reaches AUC 0.715**; **benchmark-average reaches AUC 0.806** with ρ_β=0.519 but pAcc **0.000** (cannot order tasks within a benchmark); shuffled gives AUC 0.610, ρ≈0.
- **Estimator ablation (Table 4).** Random forest gives the best KF (ρ = 0.475, pAcc 0.683); linear regression gives the best LOBO (ρ = 0.295, pAcc 0.602). The in-vs-out gap is unchanged across estimators.
- **Residual diagnostics match known audit labels.** For django/django-14725 (flagged missing prompt info, feasibility failure): **r = +1.41**, success 0.090, β = 2.58 (>90th pct). For django/django-11451 (flagged contamination): **r = −0.56**, success 0.718, β = −2.35.
- **Human-time calibration (Appendix A.5, n=1,676).** Ridge/linear: **KF ρ = 0.422, MAE 0.857** (log median human minutes); XGBoost gives lowest KF MAE 0.849 but weaker LOBO ρ.
- **Cost reduction.** The ex-ante feature pass uses **~25× fewer tokens** than empirical difficulty estimation on a 100-task benchmark.

## Authors & Credibility

Stefan Krsteski and Charlotte Meyer are affiliated with Andromede AI. The work is presented as a preprint under review. The methodology is technically careful: variational 1PL IRT with anchored scale, fold-aware PCA to curb overfitting, paired Wilcoxon significance testing, and explicit K-fold vs LOBO separation. The corpus scale (5,230 tasks, 497 agents, 415,470 outcomes) is substantial and broader than the single prior ex ante study (Ge et al., 2026, coding only). References appropriately cite the relevant IRT, curriculum-learning, and contamination-detection literature, and the paper positions itself honestly as extending "Agent Psychometrics" rather than claiming a wholly new paradigm.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine contributions are the AUC-critique (with a clean algebraic + empirical demonstration), the multi-benchmark ex ante generalization beyond coding, the entropy-trajectory-shape ablation, and the residual diagnostic. The residual idea is the most novel diagnostic angle, but its empirical support is thin.
- **Rigor:** The headline ρ=0.399 is **in-distribution K-fold**; the more honest OOD number is **ρ=0.225**, and the authors commendably do not hide this. However, the residual-detection "case study" rests on **only two** SWE-bench Verified examples (django-14725, django-11451) — this is illustrative, not validated. A reader cannot tell the false-positive rate of the residual detector, and the residual itself **requires post-rollout β**, so it is a detection aid rather than pure ex ante prediction as the framing sometimes implies.
- **Reproducibility:** The "open multi-benchmark corpus" is claimed but the paper does not state a release URL/license in the body; the IRT hyperparameters, scorer panel, and fold protocol are specified well enough to reimplement, but the underlying agent-task outcomes depend on third-party benchmark licenses (SWE-bench, BFCL, etc.) that may constrain true release.
- **Significance:** Practical value is real but bounded — in-distribution ranking of candidate tasks for an existing evaluated suite is the usable near-term setting; cross-benchmark transfer is weak. Embedding features unexpectedly failing (LOBO ρ=0.017) is under-explained.
- **Red flags:** Only **1.2 trials per agent-task** on average makes the binary IRT target itself noisy (the 0.937 oracle AUC is an upper bound on a noisy ground truth); the small 8B-class open-weight scorers may cap the entropy signal; several included benchmarks (SWE-bench Verified) are themselves known-contaminated, so the target β the predictor learns is partly contaminated too.

## Strengths

- Clean, important demonstration that **AUC overstates prediction quality** here, with both the algebraic argument (Eq. 3–4) and the empirical Table 2 (constant → 0.715, benchmark-avg → 0.806 / pAcc 0.000).
- **Breadth**: 17 benchmarks, 8 domains, 497 agents — a real generalization beyond coding-only prior work.
- The **entropy-trajectory ablation** (spread/shape/gaps; Figure 3c) is a substantive, non-obvious finding that the *shape* of the entropy profile carries signal beyond the mean.
- Honest reporting of the in-distribution vs OOD gap and of the non-monotonic scorer-count effect (benchmark overfitting hypothesis).
- The residual framing cleanly connects difficulty prediction back to **environment-design auditing** (contamination/infeasibility), giving the method a concrete downstream use.

## Limitations

- **Out-of-distribution transfer is the limiting case and remains weak** (LOBO ρ=0.225 for the full model); embedding features essentially fail OOD (ρ=0.017).
- **Residual diagnostics are illustrated on only two examples** and require the post-rollout β, so they are a detection aid rather than a pure pre-rollout tool; no threshold, ROC, or false-positive analysis is given.
- The IRT target is itself noisy: only **1.2 trials per agent-task**, and several source benchmarks carry known contamination, so the "ground truth" β inherits that noise.
- Scorer panel is limited to ~8B open-weight models; stronger or frontier scorers might change the entropy conclusions.
- No statement of code/data license or release artifacts; the 0.937 oracle AUC caps how good any predictor against this target can ever look.
- Human-time calibration (Appendix A.5) is auxiliary and limited to the 1,676-task subset with annotations.

## Relevance

这项工作直接服务于"智能体基准的环境设计与评估"这一研究兴趣：它提供了在不跑昂贵 rollout 的前提下对任务难度排序、并诊断数据污染与不可行任务的方法，对构建可信的 LLM 智能体评测体系与训练课程具有直接价值，且跨 17 个基准的语料可作为后续基准审计与 IRT 分析的基础。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does the residual detector perform at scale — what is its ROC and false-positive rate when run across all 5,230 tasks rather than two curated examples?
- Would using a frontier-class scorer (instead of 8B open-weight models) for entropy materially change the OOD (LOBO) result, especially for embedding features that currently collapse to ρ=0.017?
- Can the residual signal be combined with perplexity-based contamination tests to separate contamination from genuine capability gains?
- Does modeling the entropy profile as a time series (frequency-domain, 1D-CNN, RNN) close the KF→LOBO gap, or is the gap fundamentally a benchmark-phrasing-overfitting problem?
- How sensitive is the IRT target β to the 1.2-trials-per-pair sparsity — would densifying trials on a subset change which tasks the residual flags?
- Can ex ante difficulty predictions be wired directly into a curriculum-learning loop for agentic RL, and does ordering by ẑ beat reward-based self-paced curricula?
