When Does Consensus Mean Correctness?

Rasul Khanbayov · Hasan Kurban — Hamad Bin Khalifa University · Under review

vision-language-models self-consistency uncertainty-quantification test-time-aggregation calibration

Render-equivalence sets make VLM agreement vs. correctness exactly measurable; re-rendering beats resampling, yet consensus self-training backfires.

Key Metric
+0.102 ΔAUROC
Re-rendering over resampling, pooled N≈3150, K=8. CI [+0.083, +0.121], p<10⁻⁴.
Why This Matters

Turns "does agreement imply correctness?" from an assumption into a measurable object — and gives a failing regime for anyone using consistency as a reliability signal or training target.

Critical Edge

The most-needed ablation is missing: library-held-fixed can't separate cosmetic-style sensitivity from plotting-backend sensitivity — narrowing the central attribution claim.

1Core Contributions

  • RENDEQ instrument. A generator producing render-equivalence sets — figures redrawn from the same data with togglable cosmetic styles — so semantic equivalence and the exact answer are guaranteed by construction. First setup (Table A1) where the input is perturbed, equivalence is exact, and an answer key exists.
  • Coupling with per-factor attribution. Quantifies how tightly agreement tracks correctness on three open VLMs, attributing dispersion to specific cosmetic factors instead of assuming many small contributors.
  • A boundary condition that inverts a published positive result. Consensus self-training (Kaya et al.'s recipe) cuts accuracy in 5/5 runs — with a mechanism (Theorem 5) explaining why.
  • Three theorems (§4). Finite-K bias + unbiased pairwise REA; a diffuseness threshold 1/L below which agreement cannot certify correctness; factor decomposition bounding single-factor ablations.

3Methodology

A figure is the output of a render program R(D;θ). Because the data fixes the answer a★(D,q), redrawing under cosmetic-only θ changes yields images that are equivalent by construction.

Two label-free quantities on the set X(D,q):

  • REA — render-equivalence agreement: modal share of K answers (no ground truth).
  • RM — render marginalization: majority vote / median over K forward passes.

Models: Qwen2.5-VL-7B / 3B, InternVL2-8B. 3 independent instantiations × 3 decoding seeds = 27 runs. Baselines B1–B5 incl. mean token log-prob and fixed-image self-consistency. Bootstrap CIs (5000 resamples), McNemar for paired accuracy.

4Results & Highlights

  • Reliability: REA beats resampling B3 by ΔAUROC +0.102, all per-model CIs exclude zero.
  • Accuracy: RM lifts single-render decoding +0.057 (7B, 0.644→0.701), +0.061 (InternVL2-8B); beats self-consistency by 4.8–6.3 pts everywhere.
  • vs. evidence baseline: REA > mean-token-logprob B1 on AUROC for 2/3 models (7B 0.864 vs 0.771; InternVL2 0.907 vs 0.875); tie on 3B.
  • One factor dominates: plotting library > 2× every other factor.
  • Self-training inverts: LoRA on the model's own K=8 majority drops hold-out accuracy; AUROC rises as it gets worse — exactly Theorem 5.
  • Perception dominates: 80.4% of instances have ≥1 wrong intermediate read; pure-reasoning failures only 4.9–6.6%.

5Critical Assessment

Novelty. Claims no priority over input-perturbation consistency; the genuine novelty is exactness — equivalence by construction plus a programmatic answer key. Uniquely placed against 8 close works in Table A1.

Rigor. Three independent instantiations, cluster-bootstrap CIs, McNemar tests, full disclosure of an intermediate pipeline bug that had reversed every model's direction, plus a theory section that predicts the empirics.

Reproducibility. Pinned code/seeds, hash-verified model revisions, explicit quantization, CC-BY-4.0 data + MIT code. Small enough to re-run cheaply.

Red flags (paper-specific).

  • Most-needed ablation (library-held-fixed) missing.
  • No approximate-perturbation arm — exactness delta is reasoned, not measured.
  • Every number from one in-style synthetic split; real-chart anchor "not run."
  • Closest competitor (Chart-COCA) discussed, not run; semantic entropy & P(True) absent.
  • Load-bearing τ=5% never swept; ~18 paired comparisons uncorrected.
  • Three 2024/early-2025 models, two share the Qwen family.

6Limitations

  • Single synthetic split of the authors' own generator — external validity untested.
  • Inversion shown on one generator, one model, one LoRA config; magnitude (not direction) varies.
  • No accuracy-at-fixed-coverage or held-out threshold selection for deployed abstention.
  • Perception/reasoning split rests on an intermediate-read query never itself validated.
  • RM cannot fix systematic style-invariant errors and costs K× inference.

7Open Questions

  • Does the REA/RM edge survive real charts (ChartQA, PlotQA) and the out-of-style split?
  • Does library-held-fixed eliminate dispersion or only attenuate it (Prop. 7 predicts attenuation)?
  • REA vs. semantic entropy / P(True) on the same render-equivalence sets?
  • Would token-level aggregation change the self-training inversion, or only its size?
  • Is the inversion regime-specific to families where the base majority is already wrong?
