When Experience Becomes Instruction:
Trajectory Poisoning in Self-Evolving Agent Skill Systems

Jialuo Chen, Lingqi Jiang, Xinhao Deng, Xiaohu Du, Jianan Ma, Yunhao Feng, Yuqi Qing, Zhihao Yuan, Linkang Du, Jingyi Wang · Ant Group · Zhejiang Univ. · Tsinghua · NUDT · CUHK-SZ · Hangzhou Dianzi · Xi'an Jiaotong

一句话总结：约 10% 的注入轨迹即可让自演化技能系统自己把恶意行为写进 SKILL.md。 A skill-visible black-box attacker contributing ~10% of trajectories makes the victim evolver author the poisoned skill itself — corrupting the artifact pipeline, not the file.

Attack Mechanism

∑Poisoning success = C1 ∧ C2 ∧ C3

The SES system is modelled as Σ = (T, F, E, S): trajectory pool T, filter F, LLM evolver E, skill bank S, updated by S_{t+1} = S_t ⊕ E(F(T_t), S_t).

C1
Inclusion
Poisoned records enter the evolution stream by passing native ingestion. Held operationally fixed.
C2
Evolution Attribution
Evolver credits the behavior as reusable experience. Requires outcome-linked causal role.
C3
Realization
Behavior survives skill synthesis/merge via actionable, domain-aligned encoding.

⚙Threat Model

Skill-visible black-box. Attacker inspects public skills + contributes ≤10% of trajectories. Cannot see private pools, filters, evolver prompts, or edit the skill bank.

  • Algorithm 1: domain alignment → causal bridge → cross-trajectory invariance
  • Causal bridge: constraint → b → success or missing b → failure
  • Recurrence is central: k=1 looks like an accident; ≥2 consistent records look like promotable experience
  • Payloads: inert canaries — never executed

Defense · Critique · Open Questions

🛡Provenance-Diversity Gate

Pilot promotion-time defense: requires independent users/clusters, rejects majority dominance, penalizes high text overlap.

25/25 → 0/25

Single-cluster F1 blocked (post-gate SER 0/25) while a 5-session diverse control is accepted.

⚠Critical Edge

The paper measures only one evolution cycle — the longitudinal feedback loop is untested.

The most dangerous real-world failure mode — a poisoned skill guiding later agents whose trajectories re-validate the behavior — is explicitly out of scope. This is the single biggest gap between the threat narrative and the evidence. Compounding: only the authors' own pilot gate is evaluated (single family F1, one small diverse control); no comparison vs. RouteGuard / instruction-hierarchy / SecAlign / CaMeL; C3 ablation conflates channel and locus; empirical scope covers only two of several SES architectures.

?Questions for Further Exploration

  • Does SER compound or saturate over multiple evolution cycles once a poisoned skill guides later agents?
  • Can a Sybil adversary splitting k contributions across fabricated users defeat the provenance gate?
  • Do runtime defenses (RouteGuard, SecAlign, CaMeL) give incidental protection at the promotion boundary?
  • What is the false-positive rate of the diff-aware canary detector on cleanly evolved skills?

✓Authors & Credibility

  • Claims scoped to artifact poisoning, not runtime compromise
  • SER decoupled from runtime ASR; inert canaries never executed
  • Embedded/completed counts reported — no silent endpoint inflation
  • Overclaiming risk: low
