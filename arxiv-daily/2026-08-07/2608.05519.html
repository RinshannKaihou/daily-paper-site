EcoAgent-Bench: Evaluating Economic Decision-Making in Budget-Constrained LLM Agents

Jie Wu · Ming Gong · Feixiang Cheng · Qinqin Zhao Atlassian
llm-agents benchmark cost-aware-evaluation economic-decision-making tool-use
▍ Key Finding
Budget compliance is not budget conditioning — a new diagnostic shows tool selection barely responds to cost across top LLM agents.
▍ Key Metric
0→2.6%
GPT-5.4 deep-research escalation as budget sweeps tight→loose — despite the escalation costing ≈322 units.
64–79% of episodes make no tool call at all. Action selection is budget-blind.
▍ Why This Matters
Promotes budget-constrained action selection to a measurable capability, with a diagnostic and a real-derived testbed for designing cost-aware escalation controllers.
Directly useful for "when to escalate to a stronger tool/model vs. cut losses" controller design.
▍ Critical Edge
Single-shot, temperature-zero runs (one episode per task) report no variance or prompt-robustness; human realism review covers only 45/304 (14.8%) tasks.
The strongest negative claims rest on this thin statistical power.

02 Methodology

Each task carries a priced action set and an explicit budget; the optimal action flips with evidence state and remaining budget.

Priced ledger (abstract units, not dollars):

304 real-derived tasks (from GAIA, HotpotQA, MuSiQue) across five families covering four economic decisions.

03 The Diagnostic: Econ = min(Up, Save)

U(π,t) = R − λC(π) if correct & in-budget · −λC if wrong & in-budget · −∞ if over budget.

Econ reports the worse of strict accuracy on the upgrade-oriented group (222 tasks) vs. the save-oriented group (82 tasks) — exposing one-sided policies that micro-average hides.

04 Strengths

  • Econ = min(Up, Save) — the standout exportable idea; other cost-aware benchmarks should adopt it.
  • Real-derived tasks with paired opposite-regime families — harder to game than single-direction suites.
  • Honest per-episode separation of selection / execution / budget errors.
  • Exceptional reproducibility: wrapper rebuild, SHA256-bound artifacts, regression smoke tests, offline model-tier labels.

05 Limitations & Red Flags

· Single-shot, temp-zero — no variance, prompt-robustness, or seed variation. · "Grounded" means evidence access, not entailment from a cited span — inflates strict success. · Stop-loss scoring accepts abstention without requiring the canonical deep-research verification. · Workspace templates may leak the escalation interface by family — results are interface-level, not routing-general. · Costs are abstract units (calibrated to 2026 public prices); only relative action-cost claims are licensed. · The 10-task stop-loss family is small — abstention behavior is the least-powered measurement.

06 Authors & Credibility

Four authors affiliated with Atlassian — plausibly motivates real deployment cost trade-offs (human on-call is priced highest, 520 units).

Methodologically careful: distinguishes construct-validity oracle controls from information-fair LLM competitors; refuses cross-track cost comparison; ships integrity-bound artifacts; provides a regression smoke-test layer.

07 Open Questions

  • Does the Tool-API vs. workspace gap shrink under multi-shot averaging or a routing-specific prompt?
  • Would span-level entailment (vs. coarse grounding) reorder the leaderboard for abstention-prone Gemini / GPT-5.4?
  • Does injecting budget-awareness into the action-selection rule (not just the prompt) move GPT-5.4's near-zero escalation?
  • Do asymmetric budget responses persist across model generations, or are they 2026-backbone-specific?
