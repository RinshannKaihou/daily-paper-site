> **一句话总结：** 本文为随机局部化保形预测（RLCP）给出有限样本的高概率联合保证：在已实现辅助中心所确定的局部邻域上一致地控制条件覆盖误差与相对条件得分神谕的长度误差，并将误差明确分解为 $O(h^\beta)$ 局部化偏差与随局部有效校准样本量下降的校准项；对于满足 pivotality 的学习型得分（如 CQR、PIT/distributional 得分），偏差项消失，校准误差与得分估计误差以可加且线性方式传播。

> **TL;DR:** The paper proves the first finite-sample, high-probability bounds for the realized prediction set of randomly localized conformal prediction (RLCP) that jointly control the conditional-coverage gap and the length error relative to the conditional score oracle, uniformly over the realized localization ball $B(\tilde x, h)$; the bound is $h^\beta + \{(\log(4/\delta)/(n p_{\min} h^d))^{1/2} + \log(4/\delta)/(n p_{\min} h^d)\}$, balancing to $n^{-\beta/(2\beta+d)}$, and for learned pivotal scores (CQR / distributional PIT) the $h^\beta$ bias disappears and training error enters linearly. On a fixed-score cusp model the empirical oracle-tracking slopes match the predicted $-\beta/(2\beta+d)$ rate (e.g. $-0.320 \pm 0.010$ vs theory $-0.300$ at $\beta=0.75$), and on UCI datasets (concrete, bike, protein, airfoil, bike-synth) the hybrid RLCP improves worst-density-slice coverage over Split-CP by $+0.039$ to $+0.163$ at $\alpha=0.10$, but at a large length cost (length ratio up to $2.63\times$ on concrete) and with nontrivial fallback frequencies ($3.9\%$–$16.3\%$).

## 核心贡献

- **对已实现 RLCP 预测集的首个有限样本联合保证**：在仅假设条件得分 CDF 的 Hölder 连续性、局部密度下界与标准核/支撑正则性下，以至少 $1-\delta$ 概率（在校准样本上，且固定已实现辅助中心 $\tilde x$），同时对所有 $x \in B(\tilde x, h) \cap \mathcal X$ 一致地控制（a）条件覆盖误差与（b）相对条件得分神谕 $C^{(S)}(x, q^{(S)}_{1-\alpha}(x))$ 的长度误差。
- **明确的偏差-方差分解**：两条界都形如 $h^\beta + A^{\text{cal}}_{n,h}(\delta)$，其中 $A^{\text{cal}}_{n,h}(\delta) = (\log(4/\delta)/(n p_{\min} h^d))^{1/2} + \log(4/\delta)/(n p_{\min} h^d)$，使局部化偏差 $h^\beta$ 与有效校准样本量 $n p_{\min} h^d$ 的校准波动显式分离，并给出平衡带宽 $h^\star$ 处的经典非参数速率 $n^{-\beta/(2\beta+d)}$（仅带对数因子）。
- **学习型得分的"pivotality 消除偏差"结果**：对以独立训练折拟合的得分 $\hat S$，当总体目标得分 $S^\star$ 是 $(1-\alpha)$-pivotal（即存在统一阈值 $\tau^\star_\alpha$ 使所有 $x$ 处 $F_{(S^\star|X)}(\tau^\star_\alpha|x)=1-\alpha$，CQR 与 distributional/PIT 得分都满足），局部化偏差 $h^\beta$ 项消失；阈值偏差只通过 $\|\Delta_{S^\star}\|_{L^1(\Lambda_{\tilde x,h})}$ 进入，得分估计误差 $\epsilon^\Delta_\infty(m,\delta)$ 线性叠加到校准项上。
- **对三类标准得分的一致性验证**：在残差得分、conformalized quantile regression (CQR) 得分、distributional/PIT 得分下，逐条验证了上层抽象假设 (A1 上界密度、A2 Hölder、A3 Lipschitz 长度、A4 pivotality)，并把局部密度次小化常数 $\kappa^{(S)}_{\alpha,u,h}$ 落到可解释的条件密度下界。
- **相对 Min et al. (2025) 的严格定位**：Min et al. 的 Corollary S3 对校准样本与辅助随机化取期望后给出 $X_{n+1}=t$ 处的速率；本文则在校准事件上以 $1-\delta$ 概率一致地控制整个已实现邻域，且对设计密度只要求下界 $p_{\min}$（不需要全局上界）、允许 Hölder 而非 Lipschitz；同时额外控制神谕相对长度。论文明确把这一区别表述为"控制模式不同"而非速率改进，并指出两类结果是互补的。
- **系统的速率诊断实验**：固定得分 cusp 模型中，oracle 跟踪斜率与带宽偏倚-方差平衡斜率都与理论预测吻合；学习型得分的均匀误差指数接近 $-\beta/(2\beta+d)$；真实数据上以密度十分位展示局部校准对最差切片覆盖的改进，并诚实地披露了 hybrid 实现与 fallback 频率。

## Key Contributions

- **Finite-sample, high-probability, uniform-local bounds for the realized RLCP set.** With probability at least $1-\delta$ over the calibration sample (conditional on the realized auxiliary centre $\tilde x$), simultaneously for every $x \in B(\tilde x,h) \cap \mathcal X$, both the conditional-coverage error and the oracle-relative length error are bounded by a constant multiple of $h^\beta + (\log(4/\delta)/(n p_{\min} h^d))^{1/2} + \log(4/\delta)/(n p_{\min} h^d)$ — the first term is localization bias, the others are calibration fluctuation at the local effective sample size $n p_{\min} h^d$.
- **Bias–variance tradeoff made explicit, with the classical minimax rate.** Equating the leading terms gives $h^\star \asymp \{(np_{\min})^{-1}\log(4n)\}^{1/(2\beta+d)}$ and a pointwise rate $n^{-\beta/(2\beta+d)}$ (up to log factors), recovering the standard nonparametric rate under Hölder smoothness.
- **Learned-score theory that removes localization bias via pivotality.** For a score $\hat S$ learned on an independent fold targeting a population score $S^\star$ that is $(1-\alpha)$-pivotal (true for CQR and distributional/PIT scores), the localized oracle threshold equals the pivot $\tau^\star_\alpha$ at every centre/bandwidth, so no $h^\beta$ term appears; the bound decomposes additively into a calibration term and a score-estimation term $\epsilon^\Delta_\infty(m,\delta)$ that enters linearly.
- **Verification of the abstract assumptions for three canonical scores** (residual, conformalized quantile regression, distributional/PIT), with the localized score-density minorization constant $\kappa^{(S)}_{\alpha,u,h}$ reduced to a lower bound on the conditional response density $\mu_{\text{low}}$.
- **Sharp, honest positioning relative to Min et al. (2025).** The authors emphasize their result is not a rate improvement over Min et al.'s Corollary S3 but a different mode of control (sample-specific, uniform over the realized neighbourhood, with additional length control), and that the two analyses are complementary.

## Methodology

The paper works within the split-conformal framework. A calibration sample $\mathcal D^{cal}_n$ and an independent test pair $(X_{n+1},Y_{n+1})$ are observed; the goal is a prediction set for $Y_{n+1}$ at level $1-\alpha$. **Randomly localized conformal prediction (RLCP, Hore & Barber 2025)** draws an auxiliary centre $\tilde X_{n+1}$ from the forward kernel $H_h(X_{n+1},\cdot)$ and forms kernel weights $w_{\tilde x,h}(\cdot)$ around it; the reverse-law identity (Eq. 8) — that, conditional on $\tilde X_{n+1}=\tilde x$, the test covariate has law $\Lambda_{\tilde x,h}$ — turns RLCP into weighted conformal prediction under an explicit covariate shift, preserving finite-sample marginal validity.

The analytical programme is:

1. **Structural assumptions**: lower-bounded design density ($\mathcal P1$), compactly supported regular kernel ($\mathcal K1$), bounded conditional score density ($\mathcal A1(S)$), Hölder continuity of the conditional score CDF in the covariate with exponent $\beta$ ($\mathcal A2(S)$), Lipschitz set-length map $q \mapsto |C^{(S)}(x,q)|$ ($\mathcal A3(S)$), and a localized score-density minorization constant $\kappa^{(S)}_{\alpha,u,h} > 0$ that is shown (Lemma 1) to follow from a more intuitive pointwise density lower bound.
2. **Calibration event** $\Omega^{cal}_{n,\tilde x,h,\delta}$ (Proposition 11): with probability $\ge 1-\delta$, the test-point weight is uniformly small ($\le \rho_{n,h}$) and the localized empirical CDF is uniformly close to the localized population CDF (within $\varepsilon_{n,h}(\delta)$). This event is independent of $x$, enabling uniformity over $B(\tilde x,h) \cap \mathcal X$.
3. **Quantile-stability + bias step** (proof of Theorems 2–3): decompose the threshold deviation into (i) the localized empirical quantile vs the localized population quantile (controlled by $\varepsilon_{n,h} + \rho_{n,h}$) and (ii) the localized population quantile at $\tilde x$ vs the conditional oracle quantile at $x$ (controlled by Hölder, giving $L_S(2h)^\beta$). Density lower bound converts level errors into quantile errors (length), and direct CDF-scale evaluation gives coverage.
4. **Learned-score layer** (Section 4): condition on the independent training fold, apply the fixed-score machinery to $\hat S$, and propagate the uniform score-estimation error $\epsilon^\Delta_\infty(m,\delta)$ (Assumption $\mathcal A5(S)$). Pivotality ($\mathcal A4(\alpha)$) is the key device that pins the localized oracle threshold to the covariate-independent pivot $\tau^\star_\alpha$ and removes the $h^\beta$ bias. The threshold deviation uses the training error only through its localized average $\|\Delta_{S^\star}\|_{L^1(\Lambda_{\tilde x,h})}$; the set comparison adds a pointwise $\Delta_{S^\star}(x)$.
5. **Bandwidth choice** in the pivotal regime is governed only by effective calibration size — the bound is minimized by taking $h$ as large as the minorization $\kappa^{(S^\star)}_{\alpha,\tilde x,h} > 0$ permits, and large-bandwidth RLCP approaches marginal split-conformal calibration with covariate adaptivity supplied by $\hat S(x,\cdot)$.

Empirically, the fixed-score theory is illustrated with a 1-D cusp model $Y = |X-x_0|^\beta + \varepsilon$ at $\alpha=0.1$, $\beta\in\{0.5,0.75,1\}$; the learned-score study uses local-linear CQR (Prop. 8) and Nadaraya–Watson conditional-CDF estimators (Prop. 9); and a real-data comparison runs RLCP against Split-CP and Split-CQR on concrete, bike, protein, airfoil, and a semi-synthetic bike-synth problem with $\sigma(x)\propto 1/p_X(x)$.

## Results & Highlights

- **Fixed-score oracle tracking (Fig. 1, balanced bandwidth $h=n^{-1/(2\beta+d)}$):** least-squares log–log slopes of the absolute conditional-coverage gap and absolute set-length error vs calibration size $n$ are $-0.285 \pm 0.009$, $-0.320 \pm 0.010$, $-0.325 \pm 0.010$ for $\beta=0.5,0.75,1$, against theoretical leading exponents $-0.250, -0.300, -0.333$ (i.e. $-\beta/(2\beta+d)$ with $d=1$); coverage and length share the same fitted slopes because, inside the uniform-noise support, the absolute coverage error is exactly $(2B)^{-1}$ times the absolute threshold error. Based on $R=300$ replications; $+\infty$-threshold frequency is $0$ on the reported grid.
- **Bandwidth bias–variance balance (Fig. 2, App. M; $R=1000$):** fitted slopes of $\log h^\star(n)$ vs $\log n$ are $-0.50\ [-0.53,-0.49]$, $-0.40\ [-0.41,-0.38]$, $-0.37\ [-0.39,-0.34]$ for $\beta=0.5,0.75,1$, vs the predicted $-1/(2\beta+1) = -0.500, -0.400, -0.333$; first two inside the 90% bootstrap CI, the third just outside on the less-negative side.
- **Uniform score-estimation rate (Table 1, learned scores):** fitted exponents of the uniform error (148) vs $m/\log m$ are $-0.293\pm0.008$ (CQR, $\beta=0.5$), $-0.403\pm0.007$ (CQR, $\beta=1$), $-0.261\pm0.005$ (Nadaraya–Watson CDF, $\beta=0.5$), $-0.370\pm0.005$ (NW, $\beta=1$), against theoretical $-\beta/(2\beta+d) \in \{-0.250, -0.333\}$; local-linear CQR runs slightly steeper (extreme-quantile pre-asymptotic effect), NW is closest to theory.
- **Real-data Table 2 (target $1-\alpha = 0.90$, 20 random 50/25/25 splits):** hybrid RLCP attains empirical marginal coverage $0.918, 0.908, 0.907, 0.919, 0.910$ on concrete, bike, protein, airfoil, bike-synth. Worst-slice (min over KDE-$\hat p_X$ deciles) coverage gains vs Split-CP: concrete $+0.054\ [0.015,0.090]$ (Holm $p\approx 0.07$, not significant), bike $+0.039\ [0.028,0.051]$ (Holm-sig.), protein $+0.045\ [0.033,0.056]$ (Holm-sig.), airfoil $+0.041\ [0.017,0.067]$ (Holm-sig.), bike-synth $+0.163\ [0.150,0.178]$ (Holm-sig.). Mean-length ratios vs Split-CP are $2.63, 2.07, 1.02, 2.15, 1.51$ respectively — essentially neutral on protein, large on concrete/bike/airfoil.
- **Fallback (hybrid) frequency**: complete formal-RLCP $+\infty$ event ($W^{cal} < \frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha} W^{test}$) on $16.26\%$ of concrete, $5.78\%$ bike, $3.91\%$ protein, $8.95\%$ airfoil, $6.25\%$ bike-synth — material fractions where the implementation deviates from formal RLCP (empty window $\to$ global CQR; insufficient-weight window $\to$ y-range cap).
- **Decomposition diagnostic (Fig. 5)**: measured RLCP oracle errors track the two-term empirical proxy $B = \hat\Delta^{cal}_{n,h} + (1+1/\kappa^{(S^\star)}_{\alpha,\tilde x,h}) \hat\epsilon^\Delta_\infty(m)$. Varying-$m$ length error falls from $0.087 \to 0.033$ while the scaled proxy falls $0.591 \to 0.100$ (CQR), and $0.100 \to 0.037$ vs $0.971 \to 0.211$ (PIT) — illustrating contraction along the two terms the theorem isolates.

## Authors & Credibility

The authors are established researchers in probability, statistics, and ML theory. Eric Moulines (École Polytechnique/EPITA/MBZUAI) is one of the most cited probabilistic ML theorists (SAMCMC, Monte Carlo methods, non-asymptotic statistics). Denis Belomostny (Duisburg–Essen) and Sergey Samsonov (HSE University) have published extensively on conformal prediction and Markov chain analysis (e.g. recent work on conformal PID control, time-series conformal). The theoretical content is technically demanding and self-consistent: the proofs assemble a non-trivial concentration toolbox (Proposition 11 is the cornerstone calibration event), the Hölder/pivotality framework is rigorously verified for three canonical scores with full appendix proofs (Appendices D, E, F), and the experimental protocol is unusually transparent — the authors explicitly state that their simulations are "rate diagnostics, not checks of those theorems' non-asymptotic bandwidth and sample-size conditions or of the unspecified constants hidden by $\lesssim$", disclose the global seed (20260710), package versions (Python 3.13.2, NumPy 2.2.3, SciPy 1.15.2, PyTorch 2.9.1), and acknowledge AI-assisted language editing (OpenAI Codex) reviewed by the authors. The honest framing of the real-data study as a "hybrid" rather than formal RLCP, with full reporting of fallback frequencies, is a notable credibility signal.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty (genuine).** The finite-sample, sample-specific, uniform-over-realized-neighbourhood control of *both* conditional coverage and oracle-relative length for RLCP is new. The closest comparison (Min et al. 2025, Corollary S3) controls miscoverage in expectation over calibration and randomization; the present paper trades breadth for sharper local control plus a length bound, and is honest that this is a different mode rather than a uniform rate win. The pivotality-driven removal of the $h^\beta$ bias and the linear propagation of score-estimation error into localized bounds is a clean conceptual contribution.
- **Rigor (high).** The assumptions are explicitly enumerated ($\mathcal P1, \mathcal K1, \mathcal A1$–$\mathcal A5$), the calibration event is constructed and probability-bounded, and every score example is verified in the appendix with the minorization constant reduced to a conditional-density lower bound $\mu_{\text{low}}$. The proofs are non-trivial; quantile-stability (Lemma 14) and the sandwich bounds (Lemma 17) do real work.
- **Reproducibility (good on theory side, partial on empirics).** Seeds, bandwidth grids, kernels, package versions, and dataset UCI ids are recorded; the supplementary computational archive is referenced. However, no public code link appears in the main text, and the learned-score figures rely on the unstated grid choices and exact Devroye/ reject-sampling implementations that an independent reproducer would have to reconstruct.
- **Significance (medium-high).** The contribution sharpens the theoretical understanding of when and why RLCP tracks the conditional oracle, and cleanly separates calibration from learning. Practitioners gain a principled bandwidth prescription ($h^\star$ at the balance point) and a quantitative statement of the price of localization.
- **Red flags / weaknesses:**
  - **The real-data RLCP is a disclosed hybrid, not the analysed procedure.** The fallback to global-CQR on empty windows and y-range cap on insufficient-weight windows is triggered on $3.91\%$–$16.26\%$ of test points (Table 2). The theorems give no guarantee on those points, so the empirical coverage numbers are diagnostics of the localization mechanism rather than validation of the finite-sample guarantee. The authors flag this clearly, but a reader skimming the abstract and Table 2 could over-interpret the real-data gains as evidence for the theory.
  - **Length cost is large and heterogeneous.** On concrete the mean length ratio vs Split-CP is $2.63\times$ for a worst-slice coverage gain of $+0.054$ that is not even Holm-significant ($p\approx 0.07$). On bike ($2.07\times$) and airfoil ($2.15\times$) the tradeoff is similarly questionable. Only on protein (ratio $1.02$, gain $+0.045$) is the tradeoff clearly favourable; bike-synth ($1.51\times$, $+0.163$) is the one setting — explicitly constructed so that global methods undercover — where localization pays off unambiguously. So the practical message is much narrower than "RLCP improves local calibration": it is "RLCP improves local calibration where a density-linked heteroscedasticity is present, at a length cost that is sometimes unacceptable."
  - **Missing baselines.** Only Split-CP and Split-CQR are compared. Other localized or conditional-coverage-targeting methods (Guan 2023 localized CP, Mondrian CP, $p$-value-based conditional methods, weighted-conformal under covariate shift, or the partial-information framework of Barber & Tibshirani 2026) are not benchmarked, so the relative practical standing of RLCP among localized methods is unresolved.
  - **Dimensionality.** The synthetic diagnostics are 1-D ($d=1$). The real-data problems are 5–12 dimensional, where the heuristic bandwidth $h = 2\,\text{median-pairwise-dist}\cdot n_{cal}^{-1/(2+d)}$ (with a fixed constant 2 chosen "so that the compact localization windows remain populated in moderate dimension") is doing a lot of work; the curse of dimensionality on $n p_{\min} h^d$ is not confronted.
  - **Constants hidden.** The bounds involve unspecified multiplicative constants (the $\lesssim$ notation) and the structural constant $c_0 = \kappa c_{reg}\eta^d |B(0,1)|$; the authors are explicit that they do not validate these. This is standard but means the bounds are rate-level statements, not practically computable prediction sets.
  - **Assumption load.** Hölder conditional score CDFs, bounded-above score densities, localized density lower bounds $\kappa^{(S)}_{\alpha,\tilde x,h} > 0$, and pivotality for the learned-score theory are all needed. The authors are candid that when $\hat S$ is only approximately pivotal at moderate $m$, the fixed-score bias–variance tradeoff remains the relevant analysis — but the boundary between the two regimes in practice is not drawn.

## Strengths

- Clean, explicitly decomposed finite-sample bounds that make the bias–variance role of the bandwidth transparent, with the classical $n^{-\beta/(2\beta+d)}$ rate as an immediate corollary.
- The pivotality observation (a population score whose conditional $(1-\alpha)$-quantile is covariate-invariant removes localization bias entirely) is conceptually valuable and applies to the two most popular adaptive conformal scores (CQR and distributional/PIT).
- Rigorous verification of the abstract assumptions for residual, CQR, and distributional scores, with the minorization constant reduced to a checkable conditional-density lower bound — these worked examples are a real service to readers who want to apply the framework.
- Intellectual honesty throughout: precise statement of what is and isn't improved relative to Min et al.; transparent disclosure of the hybrid real-data implementation; explicit framing of simulations as rate diagnostics; reporting of fallback frequencies and non-significant Holm-adjusted $p$-values.
- The bandwidth prescription $h^\star \asymp \{(np_{\min})^{-1}\log(4n)\}^{1/(2\beta+d)}$ and the explicit admissibility/sample-size thresholds (Eqs. 35–37, 45–46) give a principled starting point for practitioners.

## Limitations

- The realized-set guarantees hold only on the calibration event $\Omega^{cal}_{n,\tilde x,h,\delta}$ and only when the localized density minorization $\kappa^{(S)}_{\alpha,\tilde x,h} > 0$; near boundary or in very sparse regions the bound can be vacuous and the formal-RLCP threshold defaults to $+\infty$ (the full envelope).
- The learned-score theory requires a pivotal population target $S^\star$ and a uniform sup-norm rate $\epsilon^\Delta_\infty(m,\delta)$; the paper notes (and the experiments confirm) that ReLU quantile networks plateau in sup-norm and therefore cannot be certified by this analysis — restricting practical applicability to classical non-parametric estimators (local-linear quantile, Nadaraya–Watson CDF).
- All synthetic diagnostics are 1-D; the real-data bandwidth is a data-driven median-distance heuristic with a hand-set constant, not the theoretical $h^\star$; the curse of dimensionality on the local effective sample size $n p_{\min} h^d$ is not addressed.
- The real-data RLCP implementation deviates from formal RLCP on $3.91\%$–$16.26\%$ of test points (fallback to global-CQR or y-range cap); the theorems do not cover these points, so the empirical coverage/length numbers should not be read as validation of the finite-sample guarantee.
- No comparison with other localized conformal methods (Guan 2023, weighted-conformal under explicit covariate-shift, Mondrian), so the practical ranking of RLCP within the localized-CP family is open.
- The length cost is large on three of five datasets (ratios $2.07$–$2.63$), and on concrete the corresponding worst-slice gain is not Holm-significant.

## Relevance

这与保形预测（conformal prediction）的条件覆盖理论紧密相关——是统计学习理论中"如何在有限样本下逼近不可达的 distribution-free 条件覆盖"这一核心问题的最新进展，对理解保形预测集的局部校准行为、得分学习方法（CQR、distributional conformal）与局部化带宽选择都有直接价值，符合"统计机器学习理论与可证明不确定性量化"这一研究方向。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the uniform-local control be extended to other weighted-conformal schemes unified by the Barber–Tibshirani (2026) partial-information framework, or to localized methods that do not randomize the centre (e.g. Guan 2023), where the reverse-law identity is unavailable?
- Is there a data-driven, theorem-backed bandwidth selector (rather than the median-distance heuristic) that targets the balance point $h^\star$ while controlling the fallback probability, ideally with dimensionality-aware corrections?
- How does the bound degrade when the score $\hat S$ is produced by a flexible deep quantile network that does not enjoy a uniform sup-norm rate — can a softer, $L^1(\Lambda_{\tilde x,h})$-only assumption replace $\mathcal A5(S)$ at the cost of a weaker (but still localized) guarantee?
- Can the pivotality insight be exploited to give a finite-sample *conditional* (not just uniform-local) coverage statement when the localized density minorization holds uniformly over a covering of $\mathcal X$, perhaps yielding a Mondrian-style guarantee?
- On the empirical side, would the favorable protein/bike-synth tradeoff persist under an explicit test-time covariate shift (where weighted-conformal with shift-aware weights is the natural competitor), and how does RLCP compare to localized methods beyond Split-CP/Split-CQR on the same benchmarks?
