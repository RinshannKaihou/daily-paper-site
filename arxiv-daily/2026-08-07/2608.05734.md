> **一句话总结：** 本文证明 Subliminal Learning（教师模型的偏置通过看似无关的随机数列表蒸馏给学生）是由权重中的非语义噪声结构驱动的——对 Gemma/Llama 权重加高斯噪声后传递强度分别增强 1.9× 和 1.3×，并且学生不仅继承偏置的"含义"，还精确继承教师施加干预的方式（steering vector vs. system prompt）。

> **TL;DR:** This ICML 2026 Mech Interp workshop paper shows that Subliminal Learning (SL) — where a teacher's behavioral bias (e.g., "loves owls") is transmitted to a student through apparently unrelated outputs like lists of random integers — is mediated by non-semantic noise structure in the teacher's weights: adding Gaussian noise to all weight matrices of Gemma-2B-it and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct **increases** subliminal transfer by 1.9× and 1.3× respectively, the opposite of what a semantic-mediation account predicts. Using 30k integer completions per animal across 8 target animals, the authors further demonstrate that steering vectors are a third teacher-intervention modality (beyond system prompts and finetuning), that steered vs. prompted students are mechanistically distinct (steered LoRA contributions hit cosine similarity ~0.5 with the ground-truth steering vector at the exact layer it was applied, prompted ones do not), and that mean gradients of steered subliminal data linearly correlate with the teacher's steering vector — a tentative path toward pre-training data auditing.

## 核心贡献

- **噪声驱动假说的实证检验：** 对教师和学生的所有权重矩阵注入按经验标准差缩放的高斯噪声（Gemma σ=0.10，Llama σ=0.15），结果反而**增强**了 subliminal 传递（Gemma 1.9×，Llama 1.3×，10 个随机种子、95% 置信区间）。这与"语义连接驱动"的预测相反，强力支持"非语义噪声结构"是 subliminal 分布漂移的中介。
- **将 steering vector 引入为第三种教师干预方式：** 在 Cloud et al. (2025) 原工作使用的 system prompt 和 finetuning 之外，本文首次用 mean-centered activation-difference steering vector（layer 14 for Gemma，layer 21 for Llama）作为教师偏置手段，并证明其同样能产生 subliminal 传递。
- **学生按"干预方式"分层继承：** 通过对训练好的 LoRA 适配器做残差流贡献分析，发现 steered 学生在施加 steering vector 的同一层产生一个 cosine similarity 极高的 rank-1 贡献（DLA top tokens 全部是目标动物相关词），而 prompted 学生没有此结构——即 subliminal 数据编码的不仅是偏置的"含义"，还包括施加干预的"层级"和"方式"。
- **steered 数据可蒸馏出 steering vector，prompted 数据不能：** 在冻结的学生上训练一个零初始化的 steering vector，从 steered 数据训练出来的 SV 行为转移效果甚至超过完整 LoRA，而从 prompted 数据训练出的 SV 完全失败——证明两种干预产生的是 mechanistically 不同的数据。
- **梯度作为审计信号：** 在父模型（无任何干预）上计算 steered subliminal 数据的平均梯度，发现这些梯度方向与教师 ground-truth steering vector 呈现清晰的对角线性相关（Gemma 上更明显），用作 steering vector 时可产生中等但一致的目标动物偏好偏移；而激活差异本身无可检测信号。这是 pre-finetuning 审计 subliminal 数据的一个有希望方向。

## Key Contributions

- **Empirical test of the non-semantic-mediation hypothesis.** The decisive experiment: injecting Gaussian noise into the teacher and student weights should, under a semantic account, drown out the learned associations and weaken SL; under a non-semantic (spurious/noise) account, it should amplify the very structure that carries the bias. The latter is observed — preference-change effect sizes grow by 1.9× in Gemma-2B-it and 1.3× in Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (10 seeds, 95% CIs).
- **Steering vectors as a third teacher-intervention modality.** Beyond the system-prompt and finetuning interventions used in prior SL work, the authors construct mean-centered activation-difference steering vectors over a 55-animal set ("Tell me about {animal}") and apply them at the residual stream of a single mid layer (14 for Gemma, 21 for Llama) during dataset generation. Steered teachers produce viable subliminal datasets, opening a new axis for studying how the form of the teacher's bias affects what the student learns.
- **SL is fine-grained — students inherit the *type* of intervention, not just the concept.** Cosine-similarity probes of the trained LoRA's per-layer residual-stream contributions show steered students implement a single rank-1 addition at exactly the layer where the teacher's SV was applied (cosine ~0.5 with v_a; top DLA tokens are pure animal words like "Cats, CAT, Cats, cats"). Prompted students show no such localized structure, and training a steering vector directly on prompted data fails to transfer, while training one on steered data outperforms full LoRA. Subliminal data therefore encodes *how* the teacher was biased, not merely *what* it was biased toward.
- **A pre-finetuning auditing signal via gradients.** Mean gradients of the parent model on steered subliminal data linearly correlate with the ground-truth steering vector (visible diagonal in the cosine-similarity matrix), and when repurposed as a steering vector produce moderate but consistent animal-preference shifts in Gemma. Activations themselves carry no detectable signal — flagging that activation-only interpretability tooling is blind to subliminal data.

## Methodology

The setup closely follows Cloud et al. (2025). A *parent* model (Gemma-2B-it or Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) is given an animal-related bias via one of two teacher interventions: (a) a *system prompt* ("You absolutely love {animal plural}…"), or (b) a *steering vector* v_a, computed as the mean-centered activation difference on "Tell me about {animal}" prompts over a 55-animal set A, applied to the residual stream after layer 14 (Gemma) / 21 (Llama) at all positions during generation. The biased teacher is then prompted with templated requests for lists of up to 10 integers (<1000, 3-digit max); 30,000 valid integer-only completions are collected per animal per teacher at temperature 1.0 with no top-k/top-p filtering.

The *student* (an unmodified copy of the parent) is then finetuned via LoRA (rank 8, α=8, on all attention + MLP layers, AdamW, lr 1e-4, bfloat16) on these integer lists for 1–3 epochs (per-setup batch sizes 8–12; see Table 2). Effectiveness is measured as the change in the model's animal preference on 50 single-word prompts ("Name your favorite animal…"), 128 samples per prompt.

The non-semantic test perturbs every weight matrix as W ← W + σ·std(W)·ε with ε ~ N(0,1), σ=0.10 for Gemma and σ=0.15 for Llama (attention weights excluded for Llama to preserve instruction-following); the *same* noised copy serves as both teacher and student. Ten random seeds are run with 95% CIs.

Mechanistic analyses include: (1) cosine similarity between the trained LoRA's per-layer residual-stream contribution Δh_ℓ and the ground-truth v_a; (2) Direct Logit Attribution (DLA) of those contribution vectors; (3) training a fresh zero-initialized steering vector on the subliminal data with the LM loss; (4) cosine similarity between mean activation differences (subliminal vs. control numbers) and v_a, and between mean gradients and v_a; (5) repurposing mean gradients as a steering vector at inference.

## Results & Highlights

- **Noise amplifies SL — 1.9× in Gemma, 1.3× in Llama (Figure 3).** On the preference-change metric, noised Gemma-2B-it prompted teachers produce students whose animal-preference shift is 1.9× the clean baseline; noised Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct steered teachers produce a 1.3× shift (10 seeds, 95% CIs, changes measured relative to the noised parent, with noise-only preference drift shown to be minor in Appendix C / Figure 7). This is the central evidence against semantic mediation.
- **Steered students implement a localized rank-1 addition that prompted students do not (Figure 4).** For a "cat" target, the trained LoRA's contribution at the steering layer reaches cosine ~0.5 with v_cat in the steered student and has DLA top tokens "Cats, CAT, Cats, cats" (Gemma) / "Eagles, eagle, 648, Eagle" (Llama). The prompted student's per-layer contributions stay near 0 across all layers.
- **Steering vectors trained from steered data beat full LoRA; from prompted data they fail entirely (Figure 5).** On Llama, SVs distilled from steered subliminal data achieve higher preference change than the full LoRA on the same data, while SVs trained from prompted data show no reliable transfer for any of the 8 animals.
- **Top DLA tokens sharply distinguish teacher types (Table 1, Table 3).** Steered students: "Cat → Cats, CAT, Cats, cats"; "Owl → owl, ow, Owl, /owl". Prompted students: "Cat → 0.101673, adorable, foxes, fox"; "Owl → Question, ATEST, !),, [...". The prompted outputs are dominated by artifacts and number-tokens rather than animal tokens.
- **Llama prompted SL is near-saturated — "almost 100% target animal preference rates after training" (Figure 2 caption).** Gemma is slightly more susceptible to steered SL; Llama is highly sensitive to prompted SL.
- **Gradients of steered data correlate with the ground-truth steering vector (Figure 6).** The cosine similarity matrix between mean-gradient directions and v_a shows a clear main-diagonal structure for Gemma across 8 animals; the activation-difference matrix shows no discernible correlation. Repurposing the Gemma mean-gradient as a steering vector produces "moderate but consistent" target-animal preference shifts (Figure 9); the effect is absent in Llama (Appendix F, Figure 8).
- **Dataset scale:** 30,000 valid integer completions per teacher per animal; |A| = 55 animals used for steering-vector construction and as preference-evaluation candidates; 8 target animals evaluated per setup; 512 samples used for activation/gradient probing; 128 samples per prompt for preference evaluation.

## Authors & Credibility

Authors are Ethan Hadley and Eren Gultepe (senior author, marked with *) from the Department of Computer Science, Southern Illinois University Edwardsville. The paper appears in the proceedings of the Mechanistic Interpretability Workshop at the 43rd ICML (Seoul, 2026). Code is publicly released at github.com/ekhadley/subliminal_learning. The work directly engages with and extends a recent line of SL papers (Cloud et al. 2025, Minder et al. 2026, Zur et al. 2025, Schrodi et al. 2026), correctly cites the rebuttals between camps (e.g., Schrodi et al. countering Zur et al.'s softmax-bottleneck account), and frames its contribution as resolving the open "semantic vs. non-semantic" question through a direct internal intervention (weight noising) rather than further output-data analysis. The honest framing of the noise result as "strongly suggests" rather than "proves" causality, and the explicit Limitations section, are signs of methodological care.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** The weight-noising experiment is genuinely novel and cleanly designed: it is a falsification test that distinguishes two competing hypotheses (semantic vs. non-semantic mediation) by a sign flip in the predicted effect. The framing of the teacher as a near-random network unifies several previously-disjoint observations (shared-initization requirement, fragility to shuffling/paraphrasing, cross-animal variance). Introducing steering vectors as a teacher modality and showing fine-grained inheritance of the intervention type is a real conceptual advance over Wang et al. (2025), who had claimed all OOCR can be expressed as learning a steering vector — this paper shows that claim holds only for steered, not prompted, students.
- **Rigor.** The noise experiment uses 10 seeds with 95% CIs, two model families, and two teacher types — solid for a workshop paper. The cosine-similarity / DLA analyses are well-motivated and include the right baselines (prompted student as baseline in Figure 4; full LoRA as baseline in Figure 5). However, the mechanistic-distinctness claim rests on only 1 target animal ("cat") in the main Figure 4 — the DLA tables (Tables 1, 3) broaden this but only qualitatively.
- **Reproducibility.** Good: code released, hyperparameters fully specified in Table 2 and Appendix B, prompts in Appendix A, full 55-animal set listed, σ values reported. The 30k-example / 8-animal setup is feasible to reproduce on commodity GPUs.
- **Significance.** The "activations carry no detectable subliminal signal, gradients carry a weak one" finding has real teeth for the mech-interp community: it implies standard activation-based auditing (logit lens, probing, SAE features) is blind to a class of data-poisoning threats. The non-semantic account also implies that "explain the weights in terms of training data" may be a category error for these structures.
- **Red flags / weaknesses.**
  1. **Small, non-frontier models only.** Both models are small (2B, 8B) and neither matches the original Cloud et al. setup; the authors themselves flag that "drivers of SL in the models we study are qualitatively different from those studied by others" is a live possibility.
  2. **8 target animals per setup, high cross-animal variance.** Some quantitative claims (e.g., Figure 4 cosine peaks) are demonstrated on a single animal, and the authors acknowledge small sample size may skew results.
  3. **No causal proof from the noise experiment.** The authors are upfront: sensitivity to noise is evidence of a causal role, not proof, and only one variant of the test was run (same noise for parent and teacher). Obvious untested variants include different noise for teacher vs. student, layer-selective noising, and non-Gaussian perturbations.
  4. **Gradient-auditing result is half-failure.** The gradient correlation is clear in Gemma but "less clear" and behaviorally inert in Llama, and fails entirely for prompted datasets. Combined with the fact that recovering the signal required knowing the candidate v_a vectors ahead of time (55-animal closed set), the practical auditing utility is oversold relative to what's demonstrated.
  5. **No comparison to alternative mech-interpretability tools (SAEs, crosscoders, probes) on the same data.** The claim that "activations show no signal" rests on mean activation differences + DLA; sparse-autoencoder or trained-probe approaches might surface more.
  6. **Steering-vector strength fixed at 8 (Table 2 caption) without sensitivity analysis** for how often the steered teacher actually exhibits the bias during generation.

## Strengths

- The weight-noising experiment is an elegant, decisive falsification test that cleanly separates the semantic vs. non-semantic hypotheses — a model example of how to make mechanistic-interpretability claims testable.
- The "teacher as a noisy near-random map" framing productively unifies a swarm of previously ad-hoc SL observations (shared-init requirement, mutation fragility, cross-animal variance) under one explanatory model rooted in Poole et al. (2016) chaos results.
- The fine-grained-inheritance finding (steered students imitate the SV at the exact layer; prompted students do not) is a concrete, surprising result that refines the Wang et al. (2025) claim about OOCR ≈ steering-vector learning.
- Honest, specific Limitations section; code and full prompts released.

## Limitations

- Only 2 small models studied; neither matches the original Cloud et al. (2025) setup, leaving open whether the same mechanism dominates at frontier scale.
- Quantitative mechanistic claims (Figure 4 cosine peaks, gradient diagonal) are demonstrated on 1–8 animals with high variance — broader animal/sample sweeps would harden them.
- The noise experiment is a single design (same noised weights for teacher and student); alternative noising schemes (asymmetric, layer-selective, structured) are untested.
- The gradient-based auditing signal only works for steered teachers, only in Gemma behaviorally, and only when the candidate ground-truth directions are already known — a closed-set affordance unrealistic for real-world defense.
- No comparison against stronger interpretability tooling (SAEs, linear probes, crosscoders) to support the "activations are silent" claim.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the 1.9× / 1.3× noise-amplification effect **scale** — does it grow, shrink, or saturate for 70B / 405B models, and does the semantic-vs-non-semantic balance shift as models learn more genuine associations?
- If the teacher is a genuinely random network (not just a noised LM), does full subliminal transfer still occur, and does the student still inherit the "intervention type"? This would isolate noise as the sole mediator.
- Can sparse autoencoders or trained linear probes surface the subliminal signal in activations where mean activation differences and DLA fail, or is the signal genuinely absent from activation space rather than just from these statistics?
- The gradient-auditing signal requires knowing candidate v_a vectors. Is there an **unsupervised** analogue — e.g., clustering per-token gradients across a subliminal dataset — that could flag a biased dataset without a closed set of hypotheses?
- Steered teachers inherit a single rank-1 addition at one layer; prompted teachers perturb every layer. Does this predict that prompted SL should be **harder to audit** but **more robust to layer-localized interventions** on the student, and can this be tested with layer-frozen student finetuning?
- If SL is noise-mediated, is there a noise-robust training objective (e.g., explicit weight-noise regularization, or gradient-noise matching) that suppresses subliminal transfer without harming legitimate learning from the same data?
