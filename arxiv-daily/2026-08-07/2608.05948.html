GAUGE: a measurement-grounded benchmark for physical fidelity in simulators & video world models.

GAUGE benchmarks three physics engines and six video world models against ~1,560 real motion-capture trials — and finds no engine is uniformly faithful, while video models fit the right equation form yet encode wrong accelerations, momentum and timing.

Shanghai AI Lab · HKUST · SJTU · ZJU — senior authors Jiangmiao Pang, Chunhua Shen, Weinan Zhang; corresponding Xing Gao. 16-camera sub-mm mocap, experimentally-measured (not fitted) physical parameters.

01 Results & Highlights

Standard sliding/contact is reproducible; impulsive contact, rapid textiles, and volumetric deformation are where every engine breaks down. Video models get the form right but the parameters wrong.

02 Methodology

Real data

2 m³ mocap volume, 16 NOKOV Mars9H IR cameras at 180 Hz, sub-mm 3D accuracy on a 0.9 m optical breadboard. 20 trials/task → 30 fps JSON with calibrated params. Soft bodies calibrated via tensile tests + Digital Image Correlation.

Engine track

Out-of-the-box (no per-task tuning). Isaac Sim v6.0.0, Genesis v1.12.0, Newton v1.3.0. Hungarian matching <1 cm. Errors reported as RMSE & DTW normalized by within-trial real baseline — a value <1 means the gap is smaller than real-trial variability.

World-model track

Six I2V models (Cosmos3, Wan-2.2/2.7, Seedance 2, Genie 3) on 5 rigid tasks. SAM3 tracks the object; mask centroid → 2D trajectory. Three metrics separate law-form from parameter accuracy: Dynamic Error, R², Quadratic-Form-Improvement.

03 Critical Assessment

Novelty — high. Genuinely new in combining three things prior work did separately: cross-regime real grounding (rigid+cloth+volumetric in one suite), experimentally-measured (not fitted) parameters, and a unified protocol for both engines and video world models. Table 1 fairly positions it against 8 prior benchmarks on 7 axes; none covers all of {real dynamics, experimental params, R/C/S regimes, both tracks, form+param diagnosis}.

Rigor — careful. 20 real trials per task with uncertainty bands; normalized-to-within-trial RMSE so <1 is meaningful; Hungarian matching <1 cm; out-of-the-box settings; paired negative-prompt ablation. Separating law-form from parameter accuracy is the right diagnostic move.

Reproducibility. Real trajectories, calibrated params and pinned engine versions released — good for the engine track. WM-track reproduction depends on closed APIs (Wan-2.7, Seedance 2, Genie 3) whose outputs may drift, so the WM leaderboard is a snapshot, not a durable ranking.

Red flags. None severe. Conclusions are appropriately hedged ("no uniformly faithful engine," "physical fidelity cannot be characterized by visual quality alone") and no leaderboard winner is overclaimed.

04 Authors & Credibility

Strong, well-resourced team led by Shanghai AI Laboratory (robotics / embodied intelligence), with HKUST, SJTU, ZJU. Senior authors span the simulator and learning communities: Jiangmiao Pang (embodied AI), Chunhua Shen (CV/ML), Weinan Zhang (RL/policy). The 16-camera sub-mm mocap rig, Style3D fabric tests and DIC soft-body calibration reflect serious, expensive data collection — exactly what a "measurement-grounded" benchmark needs. Code/data promised at internrobotics.github.io/GAUGE.

05 Limitations

  • Narrow materials — wood/plastic/metal rigid, six fabrics, soft/hard foam only.
  • WM track restricted to 2D rigid-body tasks — textiles & volumetric deformables (arguably where WMs are weakest) are deferred; need 3D reconstruction.
  • No fluids, no coupled fluid-rigid — a gap for pouring, splashing, granular media.
  • Out-of-the-box framing may confound "solver limit" with "default-parameter limit."
  • Single-seed WM generations make prompt-sensitivity and per-model claims statistically soft.

06 Strengths

  • Real, expensive, mm-precision ground truth across three mechanical regimes — the hardest part to fake and the most valuable asset.
  • The evaluation philosophy is the real contribution: separate law-form from parameter accuracy from temporal stability; normalize against real-trial variability, not an arbitrary threshold.
  • Honest, diagnostic framing — designed to tell you which physical mechanism fails, not just assign a score.
  • Comprehensive related-work table fairly positioning the contribution against 8 prior benchmarks on 7 axes.
  • Actionable findings for both communities: impulsive contact + rapid cloth are the open sim problems; visual plausibility ≠ correct parameters for WMs.

07 Questions for Further Exploration

  • How much of the 15.63× gap closes with per-task calibrated restitution vs out-of-the-box defaults?
  • Is the wrong-acceleration finding the constant-depth image-to-world scale assumption rather than the model itself?
  • Does high R² / low QFI (form correct, params wrong) still help policy training as reward shaping?
  • Can GAUGE extend to fluids, granular media, and 3D-reconstructed soft-body evaluation via Gaussian splats / point clouds?
  • How stable are WM rankings under multi-seed generation? The single-seed QFI swing (13→569) demands variance estimates.
