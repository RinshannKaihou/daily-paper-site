> **一句话总结：** 本文证明了量子纠错过程中产生的"探测器事件率(detection rate)"在随机化编译后构成局部强凸的模拟控制标定目标,据此用零阶在线优化算法(SPSA/LSPSA)在不中断计算的前提下以 O(1/ε²) 步收敛校准漂移控制参数,并且对局域量子码(表面码、bivariate-bicycle、qLDPC)其收敛速率与码距无关。

> **TL;DR:** The paper proves that, after randomized compiling, the average detector-event rate collected during normal QEC is a locally strongly convex surrogate objective for analog control calibration, enabling a zeroth-order SPSA algorithm to restore drifted controls in O(1/ε²) epochs on neutral-atom pulse-level simulations of the [[4,1,2]] code and on circuit-level Clifford (Stim) simulations of rotated surface codes and bivariate-bicycle (BB) codes; a locality-aware variant (LSPSA) achieves convergence whose rate is independent of code distance and matches or beats the Google RL controller baseline on surface codes with baseline depolarizing error ε_i^(0) = 10⁻³.

## 核心贡献

- **建立自校准容错的理论框架。** 形式化定义了"探测器事件率(detection rate)" C(θ) 作为控制器在 QEC 期间可直接测量的零阶目标,回答了"探测器信号是否具有支撑高效在线校准的几何结构"这一开放问题。
- **证明检测率景观的局部强凸性(Theorem 1)。** 在随机化编译/Pauli twirling 后,对相干误差、一般 CPTP 误差以及(计算-泄露子空间读取分块对角的)泄露/原子丢失,Hessian 满足 ∇²C(0) ⪰ 0;商掉规范自由度后,任意小的微扰以概率 1 移除偶然简并,使其局部强凸。
- **给出一次漂移下的可证明效率(Theorem 2)。** 投影 SPSA 在仅用每轮两次带噪函数估值时,达到 E[C(δθ_T)]−C(0) ≲ O(1/√T),即达到 ε 检测率只需 O(ε⁻²) 个 epoch。
- **给出时变漂移下的动态后悔界(Theorem 3)。** 对缓慢漂移硬件,平均动态后悔随路径长度 PT 满足 E[DynReg(T)] ≲ O(1/√T) + O(P_T/T),当 PT = o(T) 时平均后悔趋于零。
- **证明码距无关的可扩展性(Theorem 4)。** 利用探测器局域性(每个探测器至多依赖 s 个控制坐标,每个控制坐标至多影响 c 个探测器),locity-aware SPSA(LSPSA) 保持 O(ε⁻²) 收敛且复杂度仅依赖常数 s, c,与码距无关——适用于表面码、BB 码、lifted-product 及一般 qLDPC 码。
- **双轨数值验证。** 既做了中性原子 Rydberg 门级精确脉冲模拟([[4,1,2]] 检测码,含泄露),又用 Stim 做了大规模电路级 Clifford 模拟(表面码 + BB 码,每门 5 个模拟控制参数,基准去极化误差 10⁻³),定量支持全部四个定理。

## Key Contributions

- Establishes the first theoretical framework for **self-calibrating fault tolerance**, formalizing the detector-event rate C(θ) as a zeroth-order objective that the classical controller can estimate from the syndrome stream during ongoing QEC, and answering whether syndrome statistics possess the geometric structure needed for *provably efficient* (not merely heuristic) online calibration.
- **Theorem 1 (local convexity):** proves that under randomized compiling / Pauli twirling, for coherent unitary errors, general CPTP errors, and leakage/atom loss with block-diagonal readout, the Hessian of the average detector-event rate satisfies ∇²C(0) ⪰ 0; after quotienting gauge directions, a smoothed-analysis argument shows C is generically strictly convex in a trusted neighborhood ‖δθ‖ ≤ θ_C^(th).
- **Theorem 2 (one-time drift):** proves that a projected simultaneous-perturbation stochastic-approximation (SPSA) controller using only two noisy function evaluations per epoch achieves E[C(δθ_T)] − C(0) ≲ O(1/√T), so ε-optimal detection rate needs only T = O(ε⁻²) epochs — the optimal zeroth-order rate.
- **Theorem 3 (time-dependent drift):** proves a dynamic-regret bound E[DynReg(T)] ≲ O(1/√T) + O(P_T/T) where P_T is the path length of the moving optimum, so the controller tracks slow drift whenever P_T = o(T).
- **Theorem 4 (code-distance independence):** introduces a **locality-aware SPSA (LSPSA)** that, by graph-coloring control coordinates and masking gradients to only the ≤ c affected detectors, keeps O(ε⁻²) convergence with complexity depending only on locality constants s, c — independent of code distance for local codes (surface, BB, lifted-product, qLDPC).
- Validates both settings numerically: exact pulse-level neutral-atom simulations of the [[4,1,2]] code with realistic Rydberg leakage, and large-scale Stim circuit-level Clifford simulations of rotated surface codes and BB codes.

## Methodology

The paper builds the framework in two layers — a geometric structure theorem, then an online-optimization algorithm exploiting it.

**(1) Local geometry of the detection-rate landscape.** The controller cannot observe the logical error rate (rare, requires many repetitions, unidentifiable mid-computation). Instead it observes the average detector-event rate C(θ) = (1/N_D) Σ_k DR_k(θ) over N_D detectors. The authors study the Hessian of C at the calibrated point. For a single gate i in detecting region R_k, after Pauli twirling the detector-flip probability expands as q_ki(δθ_i) = Σ_{P∈F_ki} (Σ_j δθ_ij h_{P,j}^(i))² + O(‖δθ_i‖³), so ∇²q_ki(0) = 2 Σ_{P∈F_ki} h_P h_P^⊤ ⪰ 0 — a sum of rank-one PSD matrices. This positive-curvature structure is shown to extend from coherent errors to general CPTP channels and to leakage/atom loss (under block-diagonal readout). Summing over gates and detectors preserves PSD-ness (Theorem 1), and a Carbery-Wright / smoothed-analysis argument removes accidental degeneracies with probability 1 after quotienting gauge directions.

**(2) Online optimization.** Because the controller sees only noisy function values (no drift, no gradient), the authors formulate self-calibration as zeroth-order online convex optimization within the trusted convex region. They use projected **SPSA** (Spall 1992): each epoch samples a random ±1 perturbation vector u_t ∈ {±1}^d, collects two batches of m QEC cycles at θ_t ± λu_t, forms a gradient estimate ĝ_t from the two detection-rate samples, and projects the updated drift back into the convex ball. For one-time drift this yields E[C(δθ_T)] − C(0) = O(d / √T) (Theorem 2, optimal O(ε⁻²) rate). For drifting optima they bound the dynamic regret against an arbitrary comparator sequence (Theorem 3). For local codes, **LSPSA** (Algorithm 2) colors the control-coordinate interference graph with χ ≤ c(s−1)+1 colors, perturbs each color class in parallel, and masks each gradient entry to only the ≤ c relevant detectors — replacing the dimension d with the constants s, c (Theorem 4).

**(3) Numerical validation.** Pulse-level: exact simulation of a neutral-atom [[4,1,2]] code with three-level atoms (Rydberg leakage captured natively), independent laser-amplitude and phase drifts per CZ gate, SPSA with η = 0.005, testing both one-time and time-dependent (linear and sinusoidal) drift over thousands of QEC cycles. Circuit-level: Stim (Gidney 2021) Clifford simulations with depolarizing noise at baseline ε_i^(0) = 10⁻³ plus a control-induced quadratic error channel ε_i = ε_i^(0) + δθ_i^⊤ Ω_i δθ_i (Ω_i random PSD, 5 control params/gate), comparing conventional SPSA, LSPSA, and the reinforcement-learning controller of Sivak et al. (Google, Nature 2026, Ref. [1]) on rotated surface codes of increasing distance and on BB codes.

## Results & Highlights

- **Convergence rate (one-time drift, Theorem 2):** achieving an expected excess detector-event rate ≤ ε requires only **T = O(ε⁻²)** syndrome-measurement epochs, matching the information-theoretically optimal zeroth-order rate of Duchi-Jordan-Wainwright. Pulse-level neutral-atom simulations of the [[4,1,2]] code confirm detection-rate decrease and simultaneous convergence of all analog control parameters to their calibrated values, with the authors noting convergence is "even faster in practice" than the predicted rate.
- **Code-distance scaling (surface codes, Figure 4a):** conventional SPSA degrades as code distance grows because the number of control parameters scales as O(d²), producing the expected O(d) degradation; **LSPSA's calibrated detector-event rate remains essentially unchanged** across all simulated code distances, confirming Theorem 4's distance-independence.
- **LSPSA vs. RL baseline (Figure 4b):** using **fewer syndrome measurements per detection-rate estimate**, LSPSA achieves "performance comparable to or better than" the Google RL controller of Sivak et al. [1], and unlike the RL controller (whose detection rate shows a small post-convergence increase from continued exploration), gradient-based LSPSA **stabilizes at a low detection rate and stays there**.
- **BB-code scalability (Figure 4c):** applying LSPSA to bivariate-bicycle codes, the calibrated detection rate "remains essentially unchanged as the code size increases," directly confirming distance-independent scaling beyond surface codes.
- **Time-dependent tracking (Figure 3):** for both slowly varying linear drifts and sinusoidal drifts, syndrome-guided SPSA keeps the detection rate at a consistently low level over thousands of QEC cycles, whereas without online calibration the rate "rapidly increases as calibration errors accumulate."
- **Noise model parameters:** circuit-level sims use baseline depolarizing error ε_i^(0) = 10⁻³ per gate, with 5 independent analog control parameters per gate and a PSD sensitivity matrix Ω_i per gate.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuinely high. Prior work (Sivak et al. [1]) demonstrated RL-based calibration *experimentally* but left open whether the syndrome signal has provable geometric structure for efficient optimization; this paper supplies the missing convexity theorems and the distance-independence result for local codes. The combination of randomized-compiling → PSD Hessian → zeroth-order OCO → locality-aware graph coloring is a clean, non-obvious synthesis.
- **Rigor:** Theorems are stated precisely with explicit dependence on curvature, detector count N_D, batch size m, perturbation radius λ, and control dimension d. Proofs for coherent, CPTP, and leakage cases are relegated to appendices A2–A4. The smoothed-analysis step (Appendix A5) using Carbery-Wright is the right tool for removing accidental degeneracies. The dynamic-regret formulation correctly uses path length P_T rather than the stronger static notion.
- **A real weakness — the circuit-level noise model is synthetic, not physical.** Equation (10) sets the control-induced error to ε_i^(0) + δθ_i^⊤ Ω_i δθ_i with Ω_i a *random* PSD matrix; this bakes in the quadratic landscape the theory predicts rather than deriving it from a microscopic pulse model at scale. The pulse-level sim (which *does* derive the landscape from Rydberg physics) is only on the tiny [[4,1,2]] code, so the central scalability claim (Theorem 4) is validated on a model that assumes its own conclusion at the per-gate level. A reader cannot tell from the text how much the distance-independence result survives when Ω_i is structured/correlated rather than i.i.d. random.
- **Missing ablation:** no isolation of the effect of epoch size m on the tracking/stationary tradeoff that the text emphasizes as fundamental. Theorem 3's bound depends on P_T but the simulations report learning rate η = 0.005 fixed across scenarios without sweeping m or showing the m-scaling the theory predicts.
- **The "comparable to or better than RL" claim is qualitative.** The paper does not report numerical detection-rate values or speed-up factors for LSPSA vs. the Google RL controller — only the qualitative Figure 4 behavior (RL "exhibits a small increase after convergence"). Quantitative parity (e.g., detection-rate ratio, QEC-cycles-to-converge) would materially strengthen the headline comparison.
- **No logical-error-rate improvement is reported.** The entire evaluation is on detection rate, which the authors themselves argue is a *surrogate* for logical performance. The paper never closes the loop by showing that lower C(θ) translates to lower p_L on the simulated codes — a notable gap given that the stated motivation is fault-tolerant performance.
- **Local-only theory.** The authors are candid that the analysis is local (‖δθ‖ ≤ θ_C^(th)) and that global calibration is NP-hard (citing Bittel-Kliesch); the perturbed accelerated-GD extension in Appendix D2 only reaches an *approximate local* minimum. This is honest but means the framework covers continuous drift correction, not cold-start or large-miscalibration recovery.
- **Reproducibility:** Stim is public and the pulse-level setup is described in enough detail to reproduce in principle, but no code release is mentioned, the random Ω_i ensembles are unspecified (seed, distribution), and the RL controller baseline depends on reproducing Sivak et al.'s setup.

## Strengths

- Supplies the first *provable* efficiency guarantees for a problem that has so far been treated purely heuristically (RL) in experiments.
- Clean four-theorem progression (convexity → one-time → dynamic → distance-independent) that is easy to teach and build on.
- The LSPSA locality argument is broadly applicable — it covers surface codes, BB codes, lifted-product, and Tanner codes with a single graph-coloring construction.
- Two complementary numerical testbeds (microscopic neutral-atom pulses + macroscopic Stim Clifford) cover both the "landscape really is convex in nature" question and the "scales with code distance" question.
- Honest about limitations and about the role of AI tools in writing.

## Limitations

- Circuit-level validation uses a synthetic quadratic noise model (Eq. 10) that assumes the very landscape the theory predicts; microscopic validation is limited to the 4-physical-qubit [[4,1,2]] code.
- No reported logical error rate p_L improvements — only the detector-event surrogate.
- Theory is strictly local; global / cold-start calibration is out of scope and is NP-hard in general.
- Quantitative comparison against the Google RL controller is missing (no numbers, only qualitative figures).
- Epoch-size m and perturbation radius λ are not swept, so the practical tuning guidance is thin; all sims use a single fixed η = 0.005.
- No discussion of how the framework interacts with correlated drift (e.g., a global laser fluctuation affecting many gates simultaneously), which is precisely the regime where the locality assumption could break.

## Relevance

直接命中"量子纠错与容错量子计算"这一研究方向:把校准从离线辅助流程提升为在线、可证明效率的容错组件,对长期运行的量子内存/算法至关重要;其零阶在线凸优化框架与码距无关的可扩展性结论,对未来 qLDPC 处理器和 LLM/agent 驱动的量子控制栈亦有方法论参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the code-distance-independence of LSPSA survive when the per-gate sensitivity matrices Ω_i are spatially/temporally correlated (e.g., a single global laser drift) rather than i.i.d. random, and how does the locality constant c degrade in that regime?
- Can the framework be extended to close the loop from detection rate to **logical error rate p_L**, proving (or measuring) that a factor-X reduction in C(θ) yields a predictable reduction in p_L below threshold?
- The paper compares only against the Sivak et al. RL controller; how does LSPSA compare to model-based calibration (e.g., gate-set tomography on a slow timescale) or to neural-network controllers trained on the same C(θ) objective?
- What is the joint design of the **fast decoder + slow calibrator** — does feeding calibration-adjusted noise priors into a belief-matching or MWPM decoder compound the gains?
- The framework assumes randomized compiling holds throughout; how robust is the convexity theorem when twirling is itself imperfect or applied at a rate comparable to the drift timescale?
- Could the dynamic-regret bound of Theorem 3 be tightened for structured drift (e.g., Brownian, or sinusoidal with known bandwidth) rather than the generic path-length P_T formulation?
- What is the minimum epoch size m (in QEC cycles) needed for the SPSA gradient estimate to stay unbiased enough for the O(ε⁻²) rate to hold on real hardware with finite T1/T2?
