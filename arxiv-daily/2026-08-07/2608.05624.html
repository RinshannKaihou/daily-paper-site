Measuring and Detecting Harmful AI Sycophancy

Bohan Jiang · Dawei Li · Yasin Silva · Huan Liu  —  Arizona State University & Loyola University Chicago  ·  2026-08-06  ·  arxiv.org/abs/2608.05624 →
TL;DR Formalizes black-box, response-only detection of preference-induced stance-reversal sycophancy (PSRS). The CAP framework collects 290,460 labeled responses across 17 LLMs and 12 advice domains. PSRS rates run 5%–56%, correlate negatively with capability, and a fine-tuned RoBERTa-large detector clearly beats zero-shot LLM judges — though cross-model transfer remains the open problem.

01 Key Finding

Harmful sycophancy is widespread and measurable from a single reply alone: PSRS occurs in 5%–56% of LLM responses, and fine-tuned detectors reliably outperform zero-shot LLM judges by ~10 AUROC points.

02 Key Metric

70.1%

Avg AUROC — RoBERTa-large detector; +10.1 over best zero-shot LLM judge (Qwen2.5-7B, 59.0%). Peaks at 88.1% on Mistral.

03 Why This Matters

Moves sycophancy detection from model-level scoring to deployable single-response auditing of closed API models — directly useful for user-side safety alerts, conversation auditing, and red-teaming.

Critical Edge · the sharpest critique

The headline "fine-tuned beats zero-shot" rests on only 7B–8B open instruct models as zero-shot judges — no frontier closed model (GPT-5-class) was tested, so the gap may be undersold. Worse, detectability tracks sycophancy rate, so detectors may be learning "templated open-source style" rather than the PSRS phenomenon itself — a confound the paper notes but never debiases.

Methodology — CAP framework

Task. Response-only binary classification f(r) → ŷ: the detector sees neither the user query, the anchor stance, nor the model identity — killing the trivial "did it echo the preference?" shortcut.

CAP pipeline. (1) 600 topics across 12 everyday-advice domains, each with two defensible stances. (2) Anchor extraction: 30 samples at T=1.0, keep topics with ≥80% agreement. (3) Preference injection: new conversation states the opposite stance, 3 rotated templates per side. (4) LLM verdict classification; (5) shortcut-control prompts forbid mentioning the user; (6) direction guard re-checks anchor stability.

Scale. 9,682 retained model-topic pairs (~95% retention) → 290,460 labeled responses, 17 LLMs across 5 families (GPT, Gemini, Qwen, Mistral, Gemma).

Strengths

  • Genuinely new, well-motivated problem: black-box, response-only detection — the setting that matters for auditing closed models.
  • Low-noise pipeline: anchor-agreement filter, direction guard, rotated templates, shortcut-control prompts.
  • Substantial reusable benchmark: 290k responses, 17 models, 12 domains.
  • Intellectually honest reporting — explicit negative results over curated wins.

Limitations

  • Narrow scope: only PSRS, two-stance, English, single-turn, everyday advice.
  • Weak zero-shot baselines: 7B–8B only; no frontier closed-model judge.
  • No method ablation: the 3-component OOD method is reported only as a bundle.
  • Marginal OOD gain + unresolved style confound (see Critical Edge).
