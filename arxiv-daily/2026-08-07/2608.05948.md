> **一句话总结：** GAUGE 构建了基于真实运动捕捉数据的物理保真度评测基准（22 类任务，约 1,560 次实测轨迹），同时对 Isaac Sim / Genesis / Newton 物理引擎与 6 个图像到视频世界模型进行量化诊断，发现没有任何引擎能在刚体/布料/软体全场景下保持一致精度，且视频世界模型虽能拟合出正确的方程形式，却普遍给出错误的加速度、动量传递与振荡周期。

> **TL;DR:** GAUGE is a real-world-grounded diagnostic benchmark (22 task families, ~1,560 motion-capture trials, millimeter-level ground truth, calibrated physical parameters) that jointly evaluates physics engines (Isaac Sim, Genesis, Newton on 14 tasks) and generative video world models (6 image-to-video models including Cosmos3, Wan, Seedance 2, Genie 3 on 5 rigid-body tasks). It separates physical-law *form* agreement from *parameter* accuracy, finding that no engine is uniformly faithful (e.g., bouncing-ball RMSE is 15.63x the real baseline even for the best engine) and that video models can hit high R² (e.g., 0.99) on pendulum fits while predicting periods 73–80% longer than the measured 1.06 s.

## 核心贡献

- **统一的真实世界任务套件**：设计 22 类受控实验任务，覆盖刚体（8 类）、一维柔体（绳索）、准二维织物（6 类）、三维可变形体（7 类），共约 1,560 次毫米级动作捕捉轨迹，并附带不确定度估计与任务相关物理可观测量。
- **跨力学域的实验化物理参数标注**：首次在同一标准化数据集中，为刚体接触、布料本构响应、体积软体力学提供经实验标定的参数（摩擦、恢复系数、拉伸/剪切/弯曲刚度、杨氏模量、泊松比），作为场景重建与失效诊断的元数据。
- **两套互补评测协议**：同一真实数据基座下，分别评测物理引擎（任务级 sim-to-real 轨迹误差）与视频世界模型（区分方程形式一致性、参数精度与时间稳定性），避免以往「以视觉相似度代替物理正确性」的偏差。
- **诊断性发现**：明确指出脉冲接触、快速织物运动、三维变形是 sim-to-real 误差的主要来源；视频世界模型能生成形式正确的轨迹却编码错误的物理量（加速度、动量传递、振荡周期、阻尼）。

## Key Contributions

- **A unified real-world task suite**: 22 controlled task families (~1,560 motion-capture trials) spanning rigid bodies, flexible cables, textiles, and volumetric deformable objects, each paired with millimeter-level trajectories, calibrated physical metadata, uncertainty annotations, and task-specific observables.
- **Cross-regime experimentally-characterized parameters**: The first standardized real-world benchmark to combine rigid-body contact parameters, cloth constitutive response, and volumetric soft-body mechanics within a single collection — measured rather than fitted or simulator-tuned.
- **Two complementary evaluation protocols** built on the same real-world foundation: a physics-engine track (generalized trajectory errors + task-specific physical metrics) and a video world-model track that explicitly separates physical-law *form* agreement from *parameter* accuracy and *temporal stability*.
- **Diagnostic findings** that localize failure: no uniformly faithful engine; biggest gaps in impulsive contact, rapid textile motion, and volumetric deformation; video models can fit the expected equation form while recovering wrong accelerations, momentum transfer, and oscillation timing.

## Methodology

**Data acquisition.** A 2 m × 2 m × 2 m motion-capture volume instrumented with 16 NOKOV Mars9H infrared cameras at 180 Hz with sub-millimeter 3D accuracy, built on a 0.9 m × 0.9 m optical breadboard. Rigid bodies carry 6 mm retroreflective markers defining a 6-DoF body frame; textiles and volumetric deformables use skeletal marker configurations triangulated into surface meshes. 20 independent real trials are collected per task, downsampled to 30 fps, and stored with calibrated physical properties in JSON. Rigid-body friction/restitution come from inclined-plane and collision tests; textile tensile/shear/bending stiffness from Style3D fabric tests; soft-body Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio from tensile tests plus Digital Image Correlation (DIC).

**Physics-engine pipeline.** Out-of-the-box evaluation (calibrated parameters from GAUGE, all other settings default, no task-specific tuning) of Isaac Sim v6.0.0 (PhysX for rigid, Surface FEM for cloth, FEM for soft), Genesis v1.12.0 (native rigid solver, PBD cloth, explicit MPM soft), Newton v1.3.0 (MuJoCo rigid, VBD cloth, implicit MPM soft). Simulated vertices/particles are matched to motion-capture markers via the Hungarian algorithm (matching error < 1 cm). Each rollout is represented as a generalized trajectory g(t): rigid position P ∈ ℝ³, textile marker-wise Gaussian curvature K, deformable face areas A. Errors are reported as RMSE and DTW *normalized by the within-trial real baseline mean* (a normalized value < 1 means the sim-to-real gap is smaller than real-trial variability). Pendulum/cradle tasks add specialized metrics (Longest Stationary Duration, Momentum Transfer Efficiency, Period Duration, Energy Loss).

**World-model pipeline.** Six models — Cosmos3-Nano, Cosmos3-Super-I2V, Wan-2.2, Wan-2.7, Seedance 2.0, Genie 3 — each receive the same frontal initial frame (832 × 480; Genie 3 at 1300 × 750) plus a standardized text prompt; Cosmos3/Wan also run a controlled negative-prompt ablation. SAM3 segments and tracks the target object, the mask centroid gives 2D pixel coordinates, and a known-dimension image-to-world scale converts the centroid stream into a 2D trajectory. Three diagnostic metrics separate *form* from *parameter*: Dynamic Error (DE, consistency with F = ma), Coefficient of Determination R² (how well the expected physical model explains the trajectory), and Quadratic Form Improvement (QFI, residual reduction from adding a quadratic term — a low QFI means the expected linear-in-t² form is sufficient).

## Results & Highlights

- **Rigid-body: standard contact/sliding is reproducible, but impulsive contact fails badly.** On the bouncing-ball task, even the best engine (Isaac Sim) reaches **15.63× the baseline RMSE** and **5.58× the baseline DTW**; Genesis and Newton reach ~22.5× and ~22.7× RMSE respectively (Table 3). For Newton's cradle, Isaac Sim and Newton produce **zero Longest Stationary Duration** and normalized Momentum Transfer Efficiency of only **0.20 and 0.26** (real ≈ 1); Genesis fails to produce a valid rollout at all.
- **Engine ranking is regime-dependent — no winner.** Isaac Sim is best on slope contact (1.26 RMSE), nonsmooth contact, and turntable (normalized **RMSE 0.17, DTW 0.61**); Genesis is best on slope slider (**0.58 RMSE, 0.69 DTW**); Newton trails on friction-dominated tasks. The pendulum period is matched well by Isaac Sim/Newton (normalized 1.10/1.09) but all engines leak energy (raw Energy Loss from −0.041 to 0.034).
- **Deformable bodies: even the best sims are ~1 order of magnitude above the real baseline.** Genesis wins foam stretching (10.05 RMSE vs 8.72 baseline), shearing (15.26), and twisting (10.86); Newton wins foam bending (10.37). Textile flinging shows extreme solver-specific failure: **Isaac Sim 128.26 RMSE vs Genesis 8.54** (baseline 0.016) — quasi-static agreement does not predict fidelity under rapid deformation.
- **Video models: correct equation form, wrong parameters.** On the bouncing ball, Cosmos3-Super-I2V with the negative prompt gets the lowest QFI of **12.50** but infers an acceleration of only **0.088 m/s²** vs g = 9.81; Seedance 2 gives the closest acceleration at **1.84 m/s²**, still far below gravity. On pendulum, Wan-2.2 and Genie 3 reach **R² = 0.99** but their periods are **1.93 s and 1.90 s** vs the measured **1.06 s** (closest is Wan-2.7 at 1.83 s, ~73% too long).
- **Momentum transfer is essentially unsolved for video models.** Six of ten model configurations fail to generate a valid Newton's-cradle sequence; the best MTE achieved is **0.76** (Wan-2.2 with negative prompt), still well below the real ≈1.
- **Negative prompts are not reliably helpful.** The negative prompt *reduces* Cosmos3-Super-I2V's bouncing-ball QFI from 270.69 → 12.50 and *enables* Wan-2.2 to produce a cradle rollout, but *increases* its wood slope-slider QFI from 13.61 → 569.36 — direction and magnitude depend on both model and task.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Genuinely novel in *combining* three things prior work did separately — cross-regime real grounding (rigid + cloth + volumetric in one suite), experimentally-measured (not fitted) physical parameters, and a unified protocol for *both* physics engines and video world models. The Table 1 comparison is fair: no prior benchmark (Physics-IQ, IRIS, WorldBench, RIGIDBENCH, PokeFlex, RGBench, FysicsEval) covers all of {real dynamics, experimental params, R/C/S regimes, both engine+WM tracks, eq-form + param-accuracy diagnosis}.
- **Rigor**: The evaluation design is methodologically careful — 20 real trials per task with uncertainty bands, normalized-to-within-trial-RMSE so a value < 1 is meaningful, Hungarian matching with < 1 cm error, out-of-the-box (no per-task tuning) settings for engines, and a paired negative-prompt ablation. Separating law-form (R²/QFI) from parameter accuracy is the right diagnostic move.
- **Weak spots**:
  - **Engine coverage is uneven**: only 14 of 22 task families are evaluated for engines, and the world-model track is restricted to **5 rigid-body tasks** evaluated from 2D image trajectories. The authors are transparent about this, but it means the WM conclusions about cloth/soft-body physics are deferred entirely.
  - **Out-of-the-box framing cuts both ways**: refusing per-task parameter tuning is defensible for a benchmark, but it understates what a well-calibrated production deployment could achieve — the bouncing-ball 15.63× gap may say more about default restitution handling than about fundamental solver limits. A short "best-case after calibration" control would have strengthened the claims.
  - **Missing ablations**: no analysis of sensitivity to simulation frequency (180 Hz rigid/cloth vs 900 Hz soft is asserted without justification), no breakdown of tracking/SAM3 error contribution to the WM metrics, and no test of whether the image-to-world scale assumption (constant depth) systematically biases acceleration recovery — which is exactly the quantity they find most wrong.
  - **Tiny per-cell sample for WM**: one generation per (model × prompt × task) makes the prompt-sensitivity conclusion (negative prompt "not reliably helpful") statistically thin; with stochastic video diffusion, a single seed is a weak basis for ranking.
  - **Newton's-cradle WM "failure" is partly an evaluation artifact**: requiring the exact cradle geometry through 2D mask centroid tracking is harsh; the 6/10 failure rate conflates model incapacity with tracking fragility.
- **Reproducibility**: Real-world trajectories and calibrated parameters are released, scene assets are reconstructable, and engine versions are pinned — good for engine-track reproduction. WM-track reproduction depends on closed APIs (Wan-2.7, Seedance 2, Genie 3) whose outputs may drift, limiting long-term replicability.
- **Significance**: High. It provides the missing quantitative ground truth that the sim-to-real and video-world-model communities have been hand-waving around. Even if individual numbers shift with engine versions, the *methodology* (normalize to within-trial baseline; separate form from parameter; cross-regime grounding) is the durable contribution.
- **Red flags**: None severe. The paper is appropriately hedged in its conclusions ("no uniformly faithful engine," "physical fidelity cannot be characterized by visual quality alone") and does not overclaim a leaderboard winner. The biggest risk is readers over-indexing on specific default-setting numbers as fundamental solver limits.

## Strengths

- Real, expensive, millimeter-precision ground truth across three mechanical regimes — the hardest part to fake and the most valuable asset.
- Evaluation philosophy is the real contribution: separate *law form* from *parameter accuracy* from *temporal stability*, and normalize against real-trial variability rather than an arbitrary threshold.
- Honest, diagnostic framing — the benchmark is designed to tell you *which physical mechanism fails*, not just assign a score.
- Comprehensive related-work table (Tab. 1) that fairly positions the contribution against 8 prior benchmarks on 7 axes.
- Practical, actionable findings for both the simulator community (impulsive contact and rapid cloth are the open problems) and the world-model community (visual plausibility ≠ correct parameters).

## Limitations

- **Narrow material/parameter ranges**: only wood/plastic/metal for rigid, six fabric types, soft/hard foam. The authors flag that surface treatment, temperature, and wear can shift parameters within a nominal category.
- **World-model track restricted to 2D rigid-body tasks**: textiles and volumetric deformables — arguably where world models are weakest and most interesting — are out of scope because their state needs 3D reconstruction (self-occlusion, self-contact, distributed strain).
- **No fluids, no coupled fluid-rigid/fluid-soft interactions** — a notable gap for embodied agents dealing with pouring, splashing, or granular media.
- **Out-of-the-box evaluation** may understate achievable fidelity and confound "solver limitation" with "default-parameter limitation."
- **Closed-model WM dependence**: Wan-2.7, Seedance 2.0, Genie 3 run via remote APIs/online interfaces whose versions can change, so the WM leaderboard is a snapshot, not a durable ranking.
- **Single-seed WM generations** make prompt-sensitivity and per-model claims statistically soft.

## Relevance

Highly relevant to embodied-intelligence and sim-to-real research: GAUGE directly targets the question of whether the simulators and video world models used to train/evaluate robot policies are *physically* faithful, not merely visually plausible. For anyone building real-to-sim-to-real pipelines, choosing between Isaac Sim/Genesis/Newton, or evaluating image-to-video world models as implicit simulators, this benchmark supplies the missing quantitative ground truth and a principled diagnosis methodology.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of the engine-track gap (e.g., bouncing-ball 15.63× RMSE) closes with *per-task calibrated* restitution/friction versus out-of-the-box defaults? A "best-case calibrated" control would separate solver limits from parameterization defaults.
- For the world-model track, how much of the wrong-acceleration finding is the **constant-depth image-to-world scale assumption** rather than the model itself? Injecting known depth or monocular depth estimation could isolate model error from metric-recovery error.
- Does physical-law-form agreement (high R², low QFI) correlate with downstream usefulness for **policy training** (e.g., reward-shaping, data augmentation), even when parameters are wrong? This would clarify whether "form correct, parameters wrong" is a fatal flaw or a tolerable one for embodied learning.
- Can the GAUGE trajectory protocol be extended to **fluids and granular media**, and to **3D-reconstructed** textile/soft-body evaluation, using Gaussian splats or tracked point clouds to move the WM track beyond rigid bodies?
- How stable are the WM rankings under **multi-seed generation**? The current single-seed negative-prompt ablation (QFI swinging from 13.61 to 569.36) cries out for variance estimates.
- Do world models that are **physically post-trained** (e.g., PISA-style) actually improve on GAUGE's parameter-accuracy metrics, or only on perceptual plausibility?
