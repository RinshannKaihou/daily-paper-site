Verifiable Regularity Criterion for Conditional Expectation Operators and Conditional Mean Embeddings

Maximiliano Hertel · Ilja Klebanov · Manuel Schaller · Karl Worthmann  — TU Ilmenau / Chemnitz · FU Berlin / MATH+

◆ Key Finding

The conditional expectation operator is bounded and Hilbert–Schmidt whenever the conditional-density Radon–Nikodym sections lie in L²(νY; H) — turning CME representability from an a-priori assumption into a checkable Sobolev-regularity condition.

Why This Matters
Provides the missing analytical foundation justifying CME representations and kernel-EDMD Koopman approximations — widely used in data-driven dynamics, Bayesian inverse problems, and nonparametric regression — telling practitioners when the standard operator representation is even valid.
!
Critical Edge
The flagship SDE / Koopman application is restricted to bounded drift & diffusion coefficients, excluding the field's most common benchmark — the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process (linear drift). Quantitative rates only hold on bounded domains; on ℝd only qualitative statements survive.

Methodology — rewrite the CEO as an integral operator

The CEO (2.1) is rewritten as an integral operator K = Tη with kernel η(x,y) = dPY|X=x/dνY. A general lemma shows any such operator with η ∈ L²(νY; H) is Hilbert–Schmidt with the sharp norm identity. Mapping into L²(νX), trace-class (via Maurin's theorem), and compactness into Cb(X) (via Sobolev embedding Hℓ ↪ Cb for ℓ > d/2) follow by composition.

K : L²(νY) ⟶ H ,  (Kg)(x) = ∫ g(y) η(x,y) dνY(y)

For RKHS targets

Norm-equivalences HkMatβ ≃ Hβ+d/2 and HkWend,j ≃ Hj+(d+1)/2 translate the abstract condition into Sobolev regularity of the conditional density in the conditioning variable — the step that makes the criterion verifiable.

Three verified application classes

  • Nonparametric regression Y = f(X)+ε — η(x,y) = pε(y−f(x)); regularity of f and pε suffices (Thm 4.2).
  • Bayesian inverse problems, Gaussian noise — explicit η via the log-partition Λ(x); polynomial-growth condition (4.5) controls posterior cumulants (Thm 4.5).
  • Uniformly elliptic Itô diffusions — parabolic regularity yields Gaussian derivative bounds |∂αηt| ≤ C t−(|α|+d)/2 exp(−κ‖x−y‖²/t) (Prop 4.10).

Results & Highlights

  • Sharp HS norm identity (Thm 3.3a): operator norm ≤ HS norm = ‖η‖ — an equality, not a bound.
  • Fill-distance Galerkin / kernel-EDMD rates (Thm 3.12): ‖K−K̄‖ ≤ C‖η‖ · hXmX; mX=⌈β⌉−1 for Matérn, sharper mX=j+½ for Wendland.
  • CME rate (Thm 3.16): with εn ≍ n−1/(1+p), ‖K̂−K‖²HS ≤ τ²A n−1/(1+p) w.p. ≥ 1−5e−τ; sharpens to n−2ℓ/(2ℓ+d) for Sobolev RKHS.
  • Stronger-norm rate (Remark 3.18): if η ∈ L²(νY; Hs) with s>ℓ, the bound holds in HS(HkY, HkX) at rate n−(s−ℓ)/(s+d/2).
  • Koopman corollary (Cor 4.12): one-sided Galerkin error ≤ C‖ηt‖ · hXm for uniformly elliptic SDEs.

No numerical benchmarks, datasets, or baselines — the framework is validated analytically across three model classes.

Critical Assessment — honest unification, narrow flagship scope

RIGORHigh · every theorem proved, appendices A.1–A.3
NOVELTYConceptual reframing, not new technique — authors are explicit about lineage
GAPSDE coverage narrower than advertised
  • The headline application excludes its main benchmark. Assumption 4.9 (bounded Cbℓ drift/diffusion, uniform ellipticity) rules out the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process — linear drift is unbounded. Rescued only by a hand computation in Example 4.14; hypoelliptic / Hörmander / linearly-growing drift entirely deferred to future work.
  • Quantitative rates only on bounded domains. On ℝd the reweighted kernel kτ has unbounded diagonal, so Theorem 3.16 provably does not apply (Remark 4.1, footnotes 6 & 8) — and this is exactly the setting of most Koopman applications.
  • Two-sided kernel-EDMD rates need bounded Y (Thm 3.12b) — fails for SDE transition laws with unbounded support, so the dominant EDMD scheme lacks a rate in the paper's flagship use case.
  • Zero experiments. Rates are never shown tight or achievable in finite-sample regimes; constants C, A unspecified; the smoothness trade-off in Remark 3.14 is noted but never resolved.
  • Golden-ratio artefact: Bayesian-inversion condition (4.7) requires λ > (1+√5)/2 — a genuine tail-strength condition that slightly undercuts the "rather mild" framing.

Strengths

  • Converts the notoriously hard-to-verify CME representability assumption (3.4) into a checkable Sobolev-regularity condition — a real conceptual advance for the CME literature.
  • Genuine unification: the same density-regularity lens recovers results across regression, Bayesian inversion, and Koopman theory.
  • Sharp quantitative pay-off: exact HS identity, explicit Matérn vs. sharper Wendland exponents, minimax-style rate n−2ℓ/(2ℓ+d).
  • Honest treatment of the bounded/unbounded domain split — rare at this level of abstraction.
  • Excellent notation hygiene (Table 3.1, Figure 1.1).

Limitations

  • No experiments whatsoever — purely asymptotic.
  • SDE coverage narrower than advertised (bounded coefficients, uniform ellipticity).
  • Quantitative rates confined to bounded X (and, for two-sided EDMD, bounded Y).
  • Constants C, CX, CY, A non-constructive.
  • Conditional-sampling assumption for projection methods is itself expensive in Bayesian inversion; the gap is recommended but not quantified.

Open Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Can the Sobolev-regularity verification extend to hypoelliptic SDEs (Hörmander bracket condition), replacing Gaussian derivative bounds with sub-Gaussian / polynomial Malliavin estimates?
  2. What is the correct CME rate on unbounded domains with reweighted kernel kτ, given existing ridge-regression rates all require a bounded kernel diagonal?
  3. For trajectory data from an α-mixing geometrically ergodic process, can the i.i.d. rate be recovered (up to logs) via Mollenhauer et al. 2022, Thm 21 (Remark 3.19)?
  4. Can the smoothness trade-off ℓ*(η) — larger ℓ shrinks the fill-distance exponent but inflates ‖η‖L²(νY;Hℓ) — be characterized explicitly, e.g. for the OU Gaussian ηt?
  5. Does the framework extend to non-symmetric / non-reversible Markov semigroups where the SVD of Remark 3.5 does not reduce to an eigenfunction expansion?
