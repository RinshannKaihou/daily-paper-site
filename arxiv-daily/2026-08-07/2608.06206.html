Beyond Marginal Validity: Finite-Sample Guarantees for Localized Conformal Prediction

Anton Conrad · Rustam Isaev · Denis Belomestny · Eric Moulines · Sergey Samsonov

TL;DRFirst finite-sample, high-probability bounds for the realized prediction set of randomly localized conformal prediction (RLCP), jointly controlling the conditional-coverage gap and length error vs. the conditional score oracle, uniformly over the realized localization ball.

◆ Key Finding

First finite-sample, high-probability bounds for RLCP that uniformly control both conditional-coverage error and oracle-relative set length over the entire realized neighborhood — splitting cleanly into $h^\beta$ localization bias plus a calibration term.

Why This Matters
Latest progress on the open question of approximating distribution-free conditional coverage at finite samples — directly informs local calibration of conformal sets, learned scores (CQR, PIT), and bandwidth choice in statistical ML theory.
Critical Edge
The real-data RLCP is a disclosed hybrid, not the analysed procedure: fallback fires on 3.9%–16.3% of test points where the theorem gives no guarantee. So the coverage gains diagnose the localization mechanism — they do not validate the finite-sample bound.

01Methodology

Works within split-conformal. RLCP (Hore & Barber 2025) draws an auxiliary centre ~X from a forward kernel and forms kernel weights around it; a reverse-law identity turns RLCP into weighted conformal prediction under explicit covariate shift, preserving marginal validity.

Analytical Programme

  • Structural assumptions — lower-bounded design density, compact regular kernel, Hölder conditional score CDF (exponent β), Lipschitz set-length map, localized density minorization κ > 0.
  • Calibration event (Prop. 11) — with prob. ≥1−δ the test-point weight is uniformly small and the localized empirical CDF is uniformly close to population.
  • Quantile-stability + bias step — split threshold deviation into empirical-vs-population quantile (calibration) and localized-vs-oracle quantile (Hölder, giving L_S(2h)^β).
  • Learned-score layer — propagate uniform score-estimation error ε^Δ_∞(m,δ); pivotality pins the localized oracle threshold to a covariate-independent pivot, removing the h^β bias.

03Strengths

  • Explicitly decomposed finite-sample bounds making the bias–variance role of bandwidth transparent; classical n^{−β/(2β+d)} rate as a corollary.
  • Pivotality insight: a population score whose conditional (1−α)-quantile is covariate-invariant removes localization bias entirely — applies to CQR & distributional/PIT scores.
  • Rigorous verification of abstract assumptions for residual, CQR, distributional scores, with minorization reduced to a checkable density lower bound.
  • Intellectual honesty throughout — precise positioning vs Min et al. (2025), transparent hybrid disclosure, fallback reporting.
  • Principled bandwidth prescription h★ ≍ {(np_min)⁻¹ log(4n)}^{1/(2β+d)} with admissibility thresholds.

04Limitations

  • Guarantees hold only on the calibration event and where κ > 0; vacuous near boundaries or sparse regions.
  • Learned-score theory needs a pivotal target & uniform sup-norm rate — ReLU quantile networks plateau and cannot be certified.
  • All synthetic diagnostics are 1-D; curse of dimensionality on n·p_min·h^d is not confronted.
  • No comparison with other localized methods (Guan 2023, weighted-conformal, Mondrian) — practical ranking open.
  • Large length cost (2.07–2.63×) on three of five datasets.

05Open Questions

  • Extend uniform-local control to non-randomized localized CP (Guan 2023) or the Barber–Tibshirani (2026) partial-information framework?
  • Theorem-backed, data-driven bandwidth selector with dimensionality-aware corrections?
  • Softer L¹(Λ)-only assumption in place of ε^Δ_∞ to certify deep quantile networks?
  • Finite-sample conditional (not just uniform-local) guarantee via Mondrian-style covering?
