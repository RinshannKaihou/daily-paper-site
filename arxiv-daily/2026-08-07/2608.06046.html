ML-for-ML

Co-optimizing network-plane and ML-plane knobs of distributed training under one shared time-to-target-loss objective.

Yutong Zhao · Noga H. Rotman · Gianni Antichi · Ran Ben Basat  — UCL · BUPT · Politecnico di Milano · Broadcom

◆ Key Finding

Jointly tuning network knobs (gradient-precision) and ML knobs (gradient-accumulation) under a single time-to-target-loss goal beats decoupled per-knob tuning — by up to 42%.

01 Methodology

Position paper with a simulated prototype — not a deployed system. Compares five policies over synthesized wall-clock loss trajectories.

Control knobs

precision ∈ {BF16, MXFP4} grad-accum ∈ {GA4, GA16} LR ∝ √GA

Policies

  • Static — GA4·BF16 (baseline)
  • Knob-Precision — GA4·MXFP4
  • Knob-GA — GA16·BF16
  • Decoupled — GA16·MXFP4 (compose 1-D picks)
  • Joint — pick best of 4 per interval (oracle)

Timing model

T_iter = g·T_c + T_dp(p,z), with T_c = 59.3 ms profiled on A100 and T_dp from SimAI/NS-3 replays. Foreground: GPT-2 Large (774M). Target Lmin = 2.55048; first-crossings at 1.005–1.02×Lmin.

03 Results & Highlights

Decoupled: 1.13–1.42× Joint Joint 6.3–7.3× over Static DP share 73–91% → 40–58% via MXFP4
  • Headline (GPT-1B bg). At 1.01×Lmin, Decoupled takes 13–42% longer than Joint; Static is 1.71–3.65× slower.
  • Mechanism. MXFP4 cuts exposed DP share from 73–91% (BF16) to 40–58% — that is how compression relieves contention.
  • Why Joint wins. Once MXFP4 drives residual DP low, GA16's training-progress cost stops paying off — Joint re-evaluates and keeps GA4·MXFP4; Decoupled commits to GA16.
  • Moving optimum (GPT-13B bg). GA16·MXFP4 share in Joint rises 0%→40% with contention; Decoupled picks it 43–58% of the time from additive composition.

04 Strengths

  • Clean non-separability argument, easy to follow.
  • Table 1: useful network×ML×resource knob taxonomy.
  • Two well-designed scenarios — one isolates "composition fails," the other shows the optimum drifting.
  • Intellectually honest about scope and the proxy controller.

05 Critical Assessment

  • Novelty. Non-separability of hyperparameters under a shared objective is well known (Pollux, KungFu, adaptive-local-SGD). The delta is folding network-plane controls into the same loop — a reframing, not a new mechanism.
  • Rigor — simulated. "Wall-clock" = profiled compute + SimAI/NS-3 DP + separate PyTorch loss curves. No live run, no learned controller, no actuation latency. Headline 42% is the worst-case gap in one of two scenarios; the other shows 5.4–20.8%.
  • Trivial search space. The 2×2 grid is exhaustively enumerable — the interesting claim is never tested.
  • Straw-man baselines. Static and single-knob policies are weak by construction; the only competitor is the authors' own Decoupled rule.
  • Red flag. Abstract leads with "up to 42% faster"; not hidden, but the strongest number is foregrounded.

06 Limitations

  • No deployed system, no learned controller — evidence is a per-interval oracle, not the closed loop in Fig. 1.
  • Tiny 2×2 knob space; central thesis asserted, not tested.
  • Headline gap shrinks to 5.4–20.8% in the second scenario, where Joint only ties the precision-only policy.
  • No comparison to Pollux / KungFu / adaptive-local-SGD despite citing them.
  • Single foreground model (GPT-2 Large) and single foreground job; no multi-job game theory.
  • Actuation delay, sensing cost, validation overhead — all raised in §2 — unmodeled.

07 Relevance 相关性

与"分布式训练 + 系统调优"高度相关：正好处于 ML 系统（批大小/梯度累积/学习率）与网络系统（拥塞控制/压缩/路由）的交叉点，对共享集群上的训练性能、成本与能耗优化有直接参考价值，适合作为跨社区协作的切入点。

08 Open Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does joint optimization survive a genuinely large action space (routing, collective-algorithm, LR) with a learned controller + real sensing/actuation latency?
  2. How much of the Joint-vs-Decoupled gap vanishes if Decoupled simply re-evaluates the composed choice each interval rather than fixing it once?
  3. In scenario 1 Joint only ties Knob-Precision — when does joint strictly beat the single best knob, and can that be predicted a priori from the contention trace?
  4. If background jobs also adapt, is ML-for-ML a fair network citizen — or does it invite an arms race?
  5. Can the objective extend to energy / dollar / deadline goals where GPU count, placement, power caps become first-class actions?
