> **一句话总结：** 本文提出 SG-TULA——一种直接作用于次梯度、用"驯化"(taming) 显式稳定超线性漂移的 Langevin 离散格式，首次在"非光滑 + 超线性梯度增长 + 非凸"三者同时成立时给出 Wasserstein-2 距离 O(λ^{1/4}) 的非渐近收敛率（较既有次梯度方法 O(λ^{1/8}) 提升），并将 GPT-2 谱系的 nanochat 预训练势能的曲率界显式验证为 ∇²u(θ) ⪰ −c*(1+|θ|^{12L+2})I，其增强坐标变体 SG-TheoPouLA 在 L=12 上以 0.8163 val bpb / 0.1715 CORE 超过精调的 Muon 与 AdamW，在 L=24 上 0.7133 bpb 紧随 Muon。

> **TL;DR:** This paper introduces SG-TULA, an explicit Euler–Maruyama discretisation of the Langevin diffusion that operates directly on a measurable subgradient selection h ∈ ∂u and stabilises the superlinear drift by taming — `h_λ(x) = (h(x) − μx)/(1 + λ^{1/2}|h(x) − μx|) + μx` — without any Moreau–Yosida or Gaussian smoothing. Under semi-convexity (A1), strong convexity at infinity (A2) and polynomial subgradient growth |h(x)| ≤ m + K|x|^p (A3), it proves `W2(πβ, L(θ_n)) ≤ 2 C_E λ^{1/4} + e^{C_D} e^{−nλ/T0}` (Theorem 1), which is the first O(λ^{1/4}) rate under non-smoothness + superlinear growth + non-convexity simultaneously and improves the prior subgradient Langevin rate O(λ^{1/8}) of Johnston et al. (2025a); it also gives an excess-risk bound `E[u(θ_n)] − u(θ*) ≤ C_{T1} W2 + C_{T2}` with `C_{T1}=O((d/β)^{p/2})`, `C_{T2}=O((d/β) log(dβ))`. The assumptions are verified with explicit constants for the regularised pretraining potential of nanochat (GPT-2 lineage), yielding curvature `∇²u(θ) ⪰ −c*(1+|θ|^{12L+2})I`, and the boosted coordinate-wise variant SG-TheoPouLA pretrained at depth L=12 to the lowest validation bits-per-byte (0.8163) and best CORE (0.1715) of six configurations on the ClimbMix-400B corpus, beating finetuned Muon (0.8199 bpb) and AdamW (0.8254 bpb); at L=24 it reaches 0.7133 bpb, within run-to-run noise (0.0008 bpb) of Muon (0.7067).

## 核心贡献

- **SG-TULA 算法（Section 4）**：提出一种直接作用在次梯度 h ∈ ∂u 上的显式 Euler–Maruyama Langevin 离散格式，通过"驯化"函数 h_λ(x)=(h(x)−μx)/(1+λ^{1/2}|h(x)−μx|)+μx 稳定超线性漂移，避免任何 Moreau–Yosida 或高斯平滑这类计算昂贵的预处理。提供 uniform-wise (SG-TULA_u) 与 coordinate-wise (SG-TULA_c) 两种变体。驯化在次梯度计算之后施加，求值开销远小于一次矩阵乘法。
- **首个"三重失败"下的非渐近保证（Theorem 1）**：在 A1 半凸、A2 无穷远处强凸、A3 多项式增长 |h(x)|≤m+K|x|^p (p≥1) 假设下，证明 W2(πβ, L(θ_n^λ)) ≤ 2 C_E λ^{1/4} + e^{C_D} e^{−nλ/T0}，其中 C_E=O((d/β)^{p/2})、C_D=O((d/β)^{1/2})、T0 与维度无关。这是首次在"非光滑 + 超线性 + 非凸"同时成立时达到 O(λ^{1/4}) 的结果。
- **改进既有次梯度 Langevin 速率（Table 1）**：与 Johnston et al. (2025a) 在线性增长下的 SG-ULE 相比，本文把 W2 中的阶从 O(λ^{1/8}) 提升到 O(λ^{1/4})，并把"线性增长"放宽到"超线性增长"，覆盖更广的势能类。
- **超线性优化问题的 excess risk 界（Theorem 2）**：对 β ≥ max{4/µ, 1/J} 给出 E[u(θ_n^λ)] − u(θ*) ≤ C_{T1} W2 + C_{T2}，C_{T1}=O((d/β)^{p/2})、C_{T2}=O((d/β) log(dβ))，所有常数显式给出。由推论 Corollary 1，达到精度 ε 的复杂度为 λ=O(ε^4)、N=O(ε^{−4} log(1/ε))，维度-温度比形式下为 λ=O((d/β)^{−2p})、N=O((d/β)^{2p})。
- **对真实 LLM 预训练势能的显式常数验证（Appendix I）**：对 GPT-2 谱系的 nanochat，逐层推出曲率下界 ∇²_θ u(θ) ⪰ −c*(1+|θ|^{12L+2}) I_d̄，c* 在深度 L、宽度与词表大小上显式；指数 12L+2 表明深度架构中的超线性增长源于深度本身，而非分析的人为产物。由此 A1–A3 对正则化势能成立。
- **稀疏相位恢复示例（Section 8.2）**：用四阶最小二乘能 u(x)=(1/2N)Σ(x^T A_k x − b_k)² 同时呈现"非凸(p=3) + 非光滑(ℓ1 把梯度间断放在坐标超平面)"，证明 A1 以 L=λ_max(u3) 成立、A3 以 p=3 成立、A2 在测量集合的高概率事件下以 R=2(2µ+L)^{3/2}/µ 成立。
- **实证竞争力（SG-TheoPouLA, Section 8.1）**：将驯化映射与 Lim & Sabanis (2024) 的 boosting 因子复合得到的增强坐标变体，在 nanochat 上 L=12 取得六个配置中最低 val bpb (0.8163) 与最高 CORE (0.1715)；L=24 在 0.7133 bpb 上紧随 Muon。AdamW/Muon 均经过作者重新精调，比较在它们的最佳状态下进行。

## Key Contributions

- **The SG-TULA algorithm (Section 4).** An explicit Euler–Maruyama discretisation of the overdamped Langevin SDE that operates directly on a measurable subgradient selection h ∈ ∂u, stabilising superlinear growth by the taming function `h_λ(x) = (h(x) − μx)/(1 + λ^{1/2}|h(x) − μx|) + μx`. Two variants are analysed: a uniform-wise form (SG-TULA_u) and a coordinate-wise form (SG-TULA_c, needing the extra coordinate-wise dissipativity A5). Taming acts after the subgradient is computed, so evaluation cost is unaffected and overhead is far below one matrix multiplication; unlike gradient clipping it requires no pre-fixed threshold, rescales continuously with |h|, and satisfies h_λ → h as λ → 0 so the scheme remains a genuine discretisation of the intended dynamics.
- **First non-asymptotic guarantee under the triple failure (Theorem 1, Table 1).** Under semi-convexity (A1), strong convexity at infinity (A2) and polynomial subgradient growth |h(x)| ≤ m + K|x|^p, p ≥ 1 (A3), `W2(πβ, L(θ_n^λ)) ≤ 2 C_E λ^{1/4} + e^{C_D} e^{−nλ/T0}`, with `C_E = O((d/β)^{p/2})`, `C_D = O((d/β)^{1/2})` and a dimension-independent restart horizon T0. No prior result attains O(λ^{1/4}) in W2 under non-smoothness + superlinearity + non-convexity simultaneously; results at comparable or better rates assume smoothness or strong convexity.
- **Improvement over prior subgradient Langevin rates.** For p = 1 the assumptions reduce to those of Johnston et al. (2025a, SG-ULE), and the new bound sharpens their order in W2 from O(λ^{1/8}) to O(λ^{1/4}); the assumption class is simultaneously broadened from linear to superlinear subgradient growth.
- **Excess-risk bound for the associated optimisation problem (Theorem 2).** For β ≥ max{4/µ, 1/J}, `E[u(θ_n^λ)] − u(θ*) ≤ C_{T1} W2(πβ, L(θ_n^λ)) + C_{T2}`, with `C_{T1} = O((d/β)^{p/2})` and `C_{T2} = O((d/β) log(dβ))`, all constants explicit. The temperature gap C_{T2} quantifies the bias attributable to finite β rather than to the algorithm. Complexity to reach tolerance ε is λ = O(ε^4), N = O(ε^{−4} log(1/ε)); equivalently λ = O((d/β)^{−2p}), N = O((d/β)^{2p}).
- **Explicit verification for a real LLM potential (Appendix I).** For nanochat (Karpathy, 2025) the regularised pretraining potential satisfies `∇²_θ u(θ) ⪰ −c*(1 + |θ|^{12L+2}) I_d̄`, with c* explicit in depth L, width and vocabulary size; the exponent 12L+2 is itself the statement that superlinear gradient growth in deep architectures is a consequence of depth. A1–A3 follow for the regularised potential, so the theorems apply to the very function being trained — not merely to an idealised surrogate.
- **Sparse phase retrieval as a clean test of the triple failure (Section 8.2).** The quartic least-squares energy u(x) = (1/2N)Σ(x^T A_k x − b_k)² is non-convex with p = 3, and an ℓ1 sparsity term places gradient discontinuities on the coordinate hyperplanes; A1 holds with L = λ_max(u3), A3 holds with p = 3, and A2 holds with high probability over the Gaussian measurement ensemble with radius R = 2(2µ+L)^{3/2}/µ.
- **Empirical competitiveness via the boosted coordinate-wise variant, SG-TheoPouLA (Section 8.1).** Composing the coordinate-wise taming map with the multiplicative boosting factor of Lim & Sabanis (2024) — bounded above and below, so the analysis carries through with altered constants — pretrains nanochat competitively with Muon and AdamW on the ClimbMix-400B corpus.

## Methodology

The paper is a **theory paper with confirmatory experiments**, not a benchmark-driven ML paper. Its method is mathematical: define the algorithm, state the assumptions, prove non-asymptotic bounds, then verify the assumptions (with explicit constants) for one fully specified architecture and run a small set of pretraining comparisons.

**Problem.** Minimise u : ℝ^d → ℝ by sampling from the Gibbs measure πβ(dx) ∝ e^{−βu(x)}dx; for large β, πβ concentrates on the global minimisers, so accurate sampling controls the expected excess risk E[u(θ_n)] − u(θ*).

**The scheme (eq. 3–4).** SG-TULA is an explicit Euler–Maruyama discretisation of the Langevin SDE dZ_t = −h(Z_t)dt + √(2β^{−1})dB_t, with h ∈ ∂u a measurable subgradient selection. The tamed drift `h_λ(x) = (h(x)−μx)/(1+λ^{1/2}|h(x)−μx|) + μx` controls polynomial growth without smoothing; a coordinate-wise form divides each coordinate separately. The key contrast with gradient clipping: taming has no threshold, rescales continuously, and converges to h as λ → 0, so the scheme is a consistent discretisation of the intended dynamics rather than a clipped approximation.

**Assumptions (Section 5).** A1 (L-semi-convexity: admits gradient discontinuities on a measure-zero set — exactly the irregularity of ReLU/ℓ1/TV/SCAD); A2 (strong convexity outside a compact set: yields dissipativity and contraction of the diffusion); A3 (|h(x)| ≤ m + K|x|^p); A4 (finite 2p-th moment of θ0); A5 (coordinate-wise dissipativity, for the coordinate-wise variant only).

**Proof strategy (Section 7).** (1) Establish well-posedness and a log-Sobolev inequality for πβ via the Holley–Stroock perturbation principle applied to a βµ-strongly convex reference (giving ρ(β) ≤ (2/βµ) exp(β[4R(m+KR^p) + µR²/2])). (2) Combine LSI + semi-convexity to get exponential contraction `W2(νP_t, πβ) ≤ C_w e^{−C_r t} W2(ν, πβ)`, reproducing Wang (2020)'s argument for a discontinuous drift via a convolution-smoothing lemma. (3) Uniform-in-n moment bounds for both the algorithm and an auxiliary SDE started at the law of the algorithm (the taming design + dissipativity are essential here). (4) A one-step strong-approximation estimate of order λ^{1/4} between the algorithm and the SDE. (5) A discrete Grönwall argument combining contraction + discretisation yields Theorem 1. (6) Theorem 2 decomposes the excess risk into a sampling term (bounded by Theorem 1) and a temperature-gap term (Lemma 11).

**Experimental protocol (Section 8.1).** nanochat at depths L ∈ {12, 24}, V = 65 536, context T = 2048, full causal attention, trained on ClimbMix-400B (Diao et al., 2025); L=12 uses d_m=768/H=6/5000 steps/batch 524 288 tokens, L=24 uses d_m=1536/H=12/7812 steps/batch 1 048 576 tokens. Muon is restricted to matrix-valued parameters so AdamW drives the embedding/unembedding/scalar groups in every row; Muon's three "plug-ins" (second-moment normalisation, momentum schedule, decaying weight decay) are disabled so the comparison isolates the matrix update rule. Reported: validation bits-per-byte at the final step, and CORE (unweighted mean of centred accuracies over the 22 DCLM-bench tasks, Li et al. 2024). Run-to-run noise on val bpb is 0.0008, so smaller differences read as level. Hardware: 4× NVIDIA A100 80GB.

## Results & Highlights

- **W2 convergence rate (Theorem 1).** `W2(πβ, L(θ_n^λ)) ≤ 2 C_E λ^{1/4} + e^{C_D} e^{−nλ/T0}` with `C_E = O((d/β)^{p/2})`, `C_D = O((d/β)^{1/2})` and dimension-independent T0. Against the closest subgradient baseline Johnston et al. (2025a) at O(λ^{1/8}), this is a one-step improvement in the exponent of λ and is achieved under the strictly broader superlinear-growth regime.
- **Complexity (Corollary 1).** To reach W2 ≤ ε: stepsize `λ = O(ε^4)`, iterations `N = O(ε^{−4} log(1/ε))`; in dimension-temperature terms `λ = O((d/β)^{−2p})`, `N = O((d/β)^{2p})`. The 2p exponent is inherited from the order λ^{1/4} of the strong error under the present hypotheses on u; under Hessian regularity the strong error would be O(λ) and the dimension exponent would drop to p/2, matching the Gaussian-target optimum (d/β)^{1/2} at p=1.
- **Excess risk (Theorem 2).** `E[u(θ_n^λ)] − u(θ*) ≤ C_{T1} W2 + C_{T2}`, with `C_{T1} = O((d/β)^{p/2})`, `C_{T2} = O((d/β) log(dβ))`, all constants explicit. This is a non-asymptotic optimisation guarantee under hypotheses that the LLM-pretraining potential actually satisfies.
- **Nanochat L=12 pretraining (Table 2, ClimbMix-400B).** SG-TheoPouLA achieves the **lowest validation bits-per-byte 0.8163** (setting i) and the **highest CORE 0.1715** (setting ii) of the six configurations. The re-tuned baselines it beats: Muon (ours) 0.8199 bpb / 0.1706 CORE, Muon (Karpathy) 0.8253 bpb / 0.1664 CORE, AdamW (ours) 0.8254 bpb / 0.1523 CORE, AdamW (Karpathy) 0.8579 bpb / 0.1143 CORE. The two SG-TheoPouLA settings differ only in warmup/weight-decay/final-LR-fraction.
- **Nanochat L=24 pretraining (Table 3).** SG-TheoPouLA reaches **0.7133 val bpb / 0.2574 CORE**, behind Muon (0.7067 bpb / 0.2637 CORE) but within the 0.0008 bpb run-to-run noise band on val bpb, and on par with AdamW (0.7167 bpb / 0.2577 CORE); all three beat the Karpathy baseline (0.7186 bpb / 0.2571 CORE) and OpenAI GPT-2's reported CORE (0.2565).
- **Assumption verification for nanochat (Appendix I).** The regularised pretraining potential satisfies `∇²_θ u(θ) ⪰ −c*(1 + |θ|^{12L+2}) I_d̄`, with c* explicit in (L, d_m, V); the exponent 12L+2 quantifies how superlinear growth scales with depth and from it A1–A3 follow with explicit constants. For phase retrieval, A1 holds with `L = λ_max(u3)`, A3 with `p = 3` and `m = (2/N)Σ y_k² |a_k|²`, `K = (2/N)Σ |a_k|^4 + m`, and A2 holds w.h.p. with radius `R = 2(2µ+L)^{3/2}/µ`.
- **Honesty of comparison.** The authors re-tuned both Muon and AdamW over their published Karpathy defaults (Muon 0.8253 → 0.8199 bpb; AdamW 0.8579 → 0.8254 bpb at L=12) and report SG-TheoPouLA against the baselines at their best, not their defaults.

## Authors & Credibility

The three authors (Iosif Lytras, Nikolaos Makras, Sotirios Sabanis) are at the School of Mathematics, University of Edinburgh, the National Technical University of Athens, and the Athena/Archimedes Research Centre. Sotirios Sabanis is an established figure in tamed-Langevin theory (Sabanis 2013, the original taming-for-SDEs line; co-author of TULA/Brosse et al. 2019 and TheoPouLA/Lim & Sabanis 2024 that this paper directly builds on), so the lineage of the method is the authors' own. The paper is proof-heavy: every theorem has its proof deferred to an appendix (A–I, ~40 pages of appendices), every constant is given in closed form, and the assumption ledger (A1–A5) is explicitly invoked only where used. The empirical comparison is unusually honest in two respects: (i) the baselines were re-tuned rather than run at published defaults, and (ii) the L=24 result is reported as "competitive, behind Muon" rather than spun as a win. The assumption verification for a fully specified architecture (Appendix I) is a substantive and rare step — most Langevin-sampling papers verify assumptions only for textbook potentials.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** Genuine and well-scoped. The combination "non-smooth (subgradient) + superlinear + non-convex" with an O(λ^{1/4}) W2 rate is, by the authors' own Table 1, unattained by any prior work; the closest results either assume smoothness (Lovas et al. 2023, Brosse et al. 2019), strong convexity (Fruehwirth & Habring 2024), or linear subgradient growth (Johnston et al. 2025a, Lehec 2023). The improvement from O(λ^{1/8}) to O(λ^{1/4}) over Johnston et al. (2025a) at the same assumption class minus linear growth is a concrete rate-theoretic advance. The explicit nanochat curvature bound `∇²u ⪰ −c*(1+|θ|^{12L+2})I` is, as far as the paper claims, the first such verification for a real GPT-2-lineage architecture.
- **Rigor.** High. Full proofs; explicit constants throughout; the discontinuous-drift case is handled by reproducing Wang (2020)'s smooth contraction argument via a convolution-smoothing lemma (Lemma 6) rather than by hand-waving; the two probabilistic mechanisms in phase retrieval (measurement model vs algorithm) are carefully separated to avoid conflating guarantees. The exponential-in-β dependence is openly attributed to the log-Sobolev constant of πβ and is shown to be optimal for non-convex compact targets (Remark 14, citing Menz & Schlichting 2014).
- **Reproducibility.** Mixed. The theory is reproducible from the closed-form constants. The experiments list corpus (ClimbMix-400B), architecture (nanochat), shapes, hyperparameter table (Table 4) and hardware (4× A100 80GB), but no code is provided; the boosted-taming map of Algorithm 1 is specified exactly and the EMA-before-taming ordering and ε-schedule are described in prose, so a careful reimplementation is feasible but non-trivial. Run-to-run noise (0.0008 bpb) is reported, which is good practice and essential for interpreting the L=24 "competitive" claim.
- **Significance.** Moderate-to-high for the sampling-theory community; the practical claim "a method with non-asymptotic guarantees pretrains a real LLM within noise of Muon/AdamW, for which no comparable guarantees exist" is a meaningful bridge between theory and practice. For the user's stated interests (formal guarantees, training stability, mathematical foundations), this is squarely on-target: taming is a principled alternative to gradient clipping for exploding-gradient stability, and the paper formalises why.
- **Red flags and weak points.**
  - *The O(ε^{−4}) complexity is inherited entirely from the λ^{1/4} strong-error estimate (Lemma 9); the paper is explicit that improving it is "a question for the numerical analysis of SDEs with irregular coefficients" rather than something the present proof can deliver.* The contraction term carries no power of the stepsize, so the algorithm's order is exactly the order of that single estimate — a known ceiling.
  - *Exponential dependence on β is optimal in general (Remark 14) but is a real practical limitation:* in the optimisation regime β ~ 10^{12} is mentioned for pretraining, and the paper's resolution is to note that "in the optimisation regime πβ has concentrated on the global minimisers … the governing constant is a local one and no barrier needs to be crossed" (Remark 14) — i.e., the bound's worst case and the optimisation regime's actual constant are different. Polynomial dependence requires an extra Polyak–Łojasiewicz condition (Remark 11) that is not verified for nanochat.
  - *The L=24 result is not a win.* SG-TheoPouLA at 0.7133 bpb is behind Muon at 0.7067 (a 0.0066 bpb gap, ~8× the stated run-to-run noise) and the authors acknowledge Muon "leads." The "competitive" framing is fair but should not be read as "as good as."
  - *The boosted-taming variant has no scaling law.* The hyperparameter-transfer rules in Section 8.1 "are calibrated for AdamW and Muon, which therefore move to the deeper model automatically," whereas SG-TheoPouLA's L=24 configuration "was found by manual finetuning and its own scaling law remains to be quantified." This is a real disadvantage at scale that the paper is honest about.
  - *Only one architecture (nanochat) is verified, at small scale (L ≤ 24, d_m ≤ 1536).* The curvature bound's exponent 12L+2 grows with depth, so the constants c* and the resulting stepsize/iteration bounds worsen rapidly with L; the paper does not discuss whether the bounds remain non-vacuous at LLM scales (L ~ 100+).
  - *Muon's three engineering plug-ins (second-moment step normalisation, momentum schedule, decaying weight decay) are disabled to "isolate the matrix update rule."* This is methodologically defensible but arguably unfavourable to Muon, since those plug-ins are part of what makes Muon competitive in practice; a reader could reasonably want a comparison against the full Muon recipe as well.
  - *The CORE metric on DCLM-bench (22 tasks) at this scale (L=12, 5000 steps) shows large variance across optimisers (0.1143–0.1715), and the best-CORE SG-ThePouLA setting (ii) is not the best-bpb setting (i);* the paper reports both but does not discuss the trade-off.

## Strengths

- First non-asymptotic O(λ^{1/4}) W2 rate under the simultaneous triple failure (non-smooth + superlinear + non-convex), improving the prior subgradient-Langevin rate O(λ^{1/8}) at a broader assumption class.
- All constants given in closed form in terms of dimension d, inverse temperature β, and growth exponent p; the dimension enters only polynomially through d/β and the restart horizon T0 is dimension-independent.
- Genuine verification of the assumptions for a fully specified, real LLM architecture (nanochat) with an explicit depth-dependent curvature bound, not just for textbook potentials.
- A principled, threshold-free alternative to gradient clipping (taming rescales continuously and converges to h as λ → 0), with a clean interpretation in terms of exploding-gradient stability.
- Empirically competitive at L=12 against re-tuned Muon and AdamW on a real corpus (ClimbMix-400B), and honestly reported as behind Muon at L=24 rather than overclaimed.
- The excess-risk bound makes the sampling result directly usable as an optimisation guarantee, with the temperature gap (C_{T2}) explicitly separating algorithmic error from finite-β bias.

## Limitations

- O(ε^{−4}) complexity is inherited from the single λ^{1/4} strong-error estimate (Lemma 9); improving it requires better SDE numerics for irregular coefficients, which the paper explicitly flags as out of scope.
- Exponential dependence on β is inherent to routing optimisation through sampling (the log-Sobolev constant of πβ mixes across modes); polynomial dependence requires an extra PL-type condition (Remark 11) that is not verified for nanochat.
- L=24: SG-TheoPouLA (0.7133 bpb) is behind Muon (0.7067 bpb) by ~8× the run-to-run noise; "competitive" is accurate, "comparable" is not.
- No scaling law for the boosted tamed map; its L=24 configuration was hand-tuned, unlike AdamW/Muon which transfer automatically.
- Only one architecture (nanochat) verified, only at L ≤ 24; the curvature exponent 12L+2 grows with depth and the practical non-vacuity of the bounds at LLM scale is not discussed.
- No code released; the EMA-before-taming ordering and ε-schedule are described in prose, raising the bar for independent reproduction.
- Muon's three practical plug-ins are disabled for the comparison; a comparison against the full production Muon recipe is missing.

## Relevance

高度相关于"ML 可靠性与数学基础"方向：本文为同时呈现非光滑、超线性梯度增长与非凸的训练势能（正是 Transformer 预训练的处境）提供了 Langevin 采样的非渐近收敛保证与 excess risk 界，把"驯化"作为梯度裁剪的有原理替代品形式化，直接呼应训练稳定性与形式化保证这两条兴趣线；其在真实 LLM（nanochat, GPT-2 谱系）上的显式曲率验证与预训练实验，也为"形式化保证 + 实证竞争力"提供了一个具体落点。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the nanochat curvature bound `∇²u ⪰ −c*(1+|θ|^{12L+2})I` remain non-vacuous (i.e., yield a usable stepsize λ < 1/(4µ)) at LLM-relevant depths L ≥ 100, or does the 12L+2 exponent force λ so small that the bound is theoretical only?
- Can the boosted-tamed map be given a scaling law analogous to the AdamW/Muon rules in Section 8.1, so that SG-TheoPouLA transfers automatically to greater depth instead of requiring manual finetuning?
- The PL-type condition of Remark 11 would convert the exponential-in-β dependence to polynomial; can it be verified (even approximately) for the regularised pretraining potential of nanochat, or for a local basin around the minimisers?
- The O(λ^{1/4}) strong-error estimate (Lemma 9) is the complexity ceiling. Would a tamed randomised-midpoint discretisation (cf. Wang & Yang 2025, Lytras & Ntousis 2026, cited for the smooth case) lift this to O(λ^{1/2}) under the present non-smooth + superlinear hypotheses?
