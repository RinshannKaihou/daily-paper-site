> **一句话总结：** 针对 flow-based GRPO 中"训练用 SDE 采样、推理用 ODE 采样"导致的训练-推理不一致问题，提出 LC-GRPO——在每个 rollout 步先用与推理一致的 ODE Euler 步推进，再用一步 Langevin 校正注入随机性并修正局部分布误差；理论上证明该单步校正可严格降低 Wasserstein 误差，在 SD3.5-Medium、FLUX.1-Dev 和 HunyuanVideo 上一致提升奖励优化并显著缩小训练-评估奖励差距。

> **TL;DR:** LC-GRPO fixes the train-inference gap in flow-based GRPO (where stochastic SDE rollouts diverge from the deterministic ODE used at test time) by adding a single Langevin correction step after each inference-aligned ODE Euler step; the score comes free from the flow velocity via Tweedie's formula, so no extra score model is needed, and the transition stays an isotropic Gaussian with closed-form likelihood. On DrawBench/FLUX.1-Dev it lifts HPS-v2.1 from 0.378 (Flow-GRPO) to 0.384 and on HunyuanVideo lifts VideoAlign Visual Quality from -0.205 (DanceGRPO) to +0.063, while shrinking the FLUX train-eval reward gap to 0.016 vs Flow-GRPO's 0.063.

## 核心贡献

- **识别并形式化了 flow-based GRPO 中的训练-推理差距**：指出 ODE 与 SDE 在连续时间下边缘分布相同，但有限步离散化后差距显著——SDE rollout 随噪声增大变模糊，使奖励信号偏离推理分布，成为 RL 学习信号的系统性偏差来源。
- **提出 LC-GRPO 框架**：采用 predictor-corrector 结构，每步先用与推理一致的 ODE Euler 步推进（predictor），再用一步 Langevin 校正（corrector）注入随机性并局部修正分布；分数通过 Tweedie 公式直接由 flow velocity 恢复（`s(x,t) = -(x + (1-t)v(x,t))/t`），无需额外训练 score 模型。
- **保留 GRPO 所需的可处理似然**：校正后的转移核仍是各向同性高斯 `N(m_θ(x_t), 2ε_t I_d)`，均值 `m_θ` 为 Langevin 确定性部分，可直接代入 GRPO 目标的概率比计算，Langevin 步长 ε_t 自然地控制探索强度。
- **给出两条理论保证**：(1) 在强对数凹 + Lipschitz 分数下，只要 ODE Euler 步存在误差，一步 Langevin 校正严格降低 W2 距离（定理 3）；(2) 在匹配噪声水平 `ε_t = σ_t²Δt/2` 时，Langevin 校正步的 leading-order W2 误差在显式比较条件下严格小于 Euler-Maruyama SDE 步（定理 4）。
- **跨模型/任务一致验证**：在 SD3.5-Medium、FLUX.1-Dev（文生图）与 HunyuanVideo（文生视频）上对 OCR 文字渲染、HPS 人类偏好、多奖励联合优化、视频视觉质量均一致超过 Flow-GRPO / DanceGRPO / CPS 基线，并在匹配或更少的 rollout 计算预算下实现。

## Key Contributions

- **Diagnoses finite-step SDE discretization as a central cause of the train-inference gap** in flow-based GRPO: although ODE and SDE share marginals in continuous time, Euler-Maruyama discretization of the reverse SDE accumulates error from both drift and Brownian increments, producing blurry rollouts whose rewards misrepresent the deterministic ODE sampler used at test time.
- **Introduces LC-GRPO**, a predictor-corrector rollout sampler: an ODE Euler step (identical to inference) followed by one Langevin correction step targeting the marginal at the resulting timestep. The score is recovered for free from the flow velocity via Tweedie's formula (`s(x,t) = -(x + (1-t)v(x,t))/t`), so no auxiliary score model is trained.
- **Preserves the GRPO likelihood contract**: the corrected transition remains an isotropic Gaussian `N(m_θ(x_t), 2ε_t I_d)` with closed-form density, plugging directly into the importance ratio; the Langevin step size ε_t doubles as the exploration knob.
- **Proves two finite-sample guarantees**: (Thm 3) under α-strong log-concavity and L-Lipschitz score, one Langevin step strictly reduces the W2 error of any imperfect ODE Euler step; (Thm 4) at matched noise level `ε_t = σ_t²Δt/2`, the Langevin-corrected step has strictly smaller leading-order W2 error than the Euler-Maruyama SDE step whenever condition `⟨E, ṡ⟩_p < (σ²/2)‖ṡ‖²_p` holds.
- **Validates across models and tasks**: consistent gains over Flow-GRPO, DanceGRPO, and CPS on SD3.5-Medium, FLUX.1-Dev, and HunyuanVideo for OCR text rendering, HPS-v2.1 human-preference alignment, multi-reward (HPS+CLIP) optimization, and VideoAlign visual quality, while preserving auxiliary quality metrics and narrowing the train-eval reward gap.

## Methodology

LC-GRPO reframes flow-model rollout sampling as a predictor-corrector scheme that decouples *transport* from *exploration*:

1. **Predictor (inference-aligned ODE step).** From `x_t`, take the same backward Euler step the inference sampler uses: `x' = x_t - Δt · v_θ(x_t, t)`. This guarantees the deterministic component of the rollout follows the test-time trajectory.

2. **Corrector (one Langevin step).** Apply `x_{t-Δt} = x' + ε_t · s(x', t-Δt) + √(2ε_t) · ξ`, where the score is obtained via Tweedie's formula `s(x,t) = -(x + (1-t)v(x,t))/t` evaluated with the same flow model — no extra network. The step size is set as `2ε_t = η(t-Δt)` so a single scalar η controls exploration, mirroring Flow-GRPO's `σ_t = η√(t/(1-t))` convention. The final denoising step skips the corrector (pure ODE) to avoid injecting noise at the data endpoint.

3. **GRPO integration.** Because the Langevin update is an affine-Gaussian perturbation, the transition kernel `p_θ(x_{t-Δt} | x_t, c) = N(m_θ(x_t), 2ε_t I_d)` has closed-form density and plugs directly into the GRPO importance ratio and clipped surrogate. Gradients are back-propagated through the Langevin step (an ablation confirms this helps).

**Why this beats SDE rollouts.** SDE Euler-Maruyama accumulates error from both the drift discretization and the Brownian increment, and its samples blur as η grows. The Langevin corrector instead (i) leaves the target marginal `p_{t-Δt}` invariant in continuous time, (ii) acts as a known score-based corrector (cf. Song et al., 2020) that *reduces* local numerical error, and (iii) at matched total injected variance it injects *more* noise than the SDE while producing *sharper* samples (Fig. 2). Theory formalizes this: under α-strong log-concavity, one Langevin step provably contracts W2 distance to the target whenever the Euler step is imperfect (Thm 3); at `ε_t = σ_t²Δt/2`, the leading-order W2 error of the LC step is `h²/2 · ‖ṽ - hεG‖_p` vs the SDE step's `h²/2 · ‖ṽ - σ²ṡ - σ⁴G‖_p`, strictly smaller when the comparison condition holds (Thm 4).

## Results & Highlights

- **Visual text rendering (OCR, SD3.5-Medium):** LC-GRPO reaches **0.960 OCR accuracy** on the verifiable OCR reward vs **0.914 (Flow-GRPO)** and **0.935 (CPS)**, up from the **0.569** base model — the largest verifiable-reward margin in the paper, while keeping Aesthetic (5.33) and CLIP (0.291) essentially intact and actually improving ImageReward to 1.00 vs Flow-GRPO's 0.94.
- **Human-preference alignment (HPS-v2.1):** On SD3.5-Medium LC-GRPO achieves **0.393 HPS-v2.1** vs **0.381 (Flow-GRPO)** and **0.371 (CPS)** (+0.012 / +0.022 absolute); on FLUX.1-Dev it reaches **0.384 vs 0.378 / 0.373**, while also nudging PickScore to 23.28 (vs 23.04 / 22.99) and ImageReward to 1.36 (vs base 0.92).
- **Multi-reward (HPS+CLIP):** On FLUX.1-Dev, joint optimization gives LC-GRPO **HPS-v2.1 0.376 and CLIP 0.285** vs Flow-GRPO's **0.371 / 0.279** and CPS's 0.370 / 0.284 — the only method that simultaneously improves both the preference metric and text-image alignment, where single-reward HPS training collapses CLIP to 0.265-0.270.
- **Text-to-video (HunyuanVideo + VideoAlign Visual Quality):** LC-GRPO lifts Visual Quality from **-0.368 (base) / -0.276 (CPS) / -0.205 (DanceGRPO)** to **+0.063** — the only method to cross into positive territory — and pushes VBench Total to **79.10** vs DanceGRPO's 78.85 and base 78.74, while preserving Dynamic Degree at **45.8** (DanceGRPO collapses it to 12.5, CPS to 19.4 — a clear reward-hacking failure LC-GRPO avoids).
- **Train-eval gap (the paper's central claim, Fig. 1):** On FLUX.1-Dev HPS, the gap between rollout-time and ODE-evaluation rewards shrinks to **0.016 for LC-GRPO**, vs **0.035 for CPS** and **0.063 for Flow-GRPO** — roughly a 4× reduction over Flow-GRPO, directly evidencing that Langevin-corrected rollouts stay aligned with deterministic inference.
- **Compute-fair comparison:** LC-GRPO uses 10/6/8 rollout steps for SD3.5-M/FLUX/HunyuanVideo while baselines get *both* the same step count and 2× the steps (since each LC step costs 2 NFE); the reported baselines are the better of the two, so the gains are not artifacts of extra compute.

## Authors & Credibility

- Affiliated with **Mengdi Wang's group (Princeton)** and **Zheng Ding** — both established researchers in diffusion/flow alignment, score-based guidance, and RL post-training; the authors recently produced related work on gradient guidance for diffusion models and tree-search path steering (Guo et al. 2024, 2026), giving them credible standing on the score-corrector formalism central here.
- The method is grounded in non-trivial theory (W2 contraction under strong log-concavity, leading-order error comparison vs Euler-Maruyama) with full proofs in Appendix A, not just empirical hand-waving — this is uncommon for the GRPO-for-diffusion subfield, which is currently crowded with empirically-driven papers.
- Code/data provenance is standard for the area: open base models (SD3.5-Medium, FLUX.1-Dev, HunyuanVideo), public reward models (HPS-v2.1, ImageReward, PickScore, CLIP, VideoAlign, VBench), and public prompt sets (DrawBench, Pick-a-Pic, VidProM). Hyperparameters (LoRA ranks, learning rates, η, β, group sizes) are fully disclosed in Appendix B, enabling reproduction.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty — solid but incremental.** The predictor-corrector idea is a direct port of Song et al. (2020b)'s score-based MCMC corrector into the GRPO rollout sampler; the genuinely new pieces are (a) the realization that the score comes free from the flow velocity via Tweedie, so no extra network is needed, and (b) the finite-step W2 comparison vs Euler-Maruyama. This is a clean, well-motivated contribution but conceptually one step rather than a new paradigm.
- **Theory–practice gap.** Theorem 3 requires the target marginal `p_{t-Δt}` to be **α-strongly log-concave** — an assumption that is essentially never satisfied by real generative-model marginals (which are multimodal mixtures). The theorem is reassuring as a "the corrector helps in the regime where it can be analyzed" sanity check, but it does *not* actually certify the SD3.5/FLUX/HunyuanVideo regime the experiments operate in. The comparison condition `⟨E, ṡ⟩_p < (σ²/2)‖ṡ‖²_p` in Theorem 4 is likewise not verified to hold empirically.
- **Reward model hacking is the elephant in the room, only partially addressed.** The authors commendably monitor auxiliary metrics, but the optimized rewards (HPS-v2.1, ImageReward, VideoAlign Visual Quality) are themselves learned proxies that are known to be gameable. The HunyuanVideo Dynamic Degree numbers expose this sharply: DanceGRPO collapses it to 12.5 and CPS to 19.4 — reward hacking — while LC-GRPO preserves 45.8, which is presented as a strength but is really evidence that *all* these methods are at risk and LC-GRPO merely degrades more gracefully on this particular axis. There is **no human evaluation** to confirm the reward gains translate to actual preference.
- **Limited tuning regime.** All image experiments use LoRA (rank 32–64), 48 prompts/epoch, and ~500–800 training steps; full fine-tuning behavior, longer training, and stability under larger η are not studied. Whether the train-eval gap advantage persists at scale (e.g., would the gap re-emerge as the policy drifts further from the reference?) is unaddressed.
- **Missing ablations.** No sensitivity analysis over η beyond the reported settings; no ablation on the number of Langevin sub-steps (always 1); no comparison against the obvious alternative of *multiple* cheap Langevin steps or an annealed variant; no test of whether gradient-flow-through-corrector helps on FLUX (Fig. 4a only shows FLUX for the gradient ablation, not the CFG ablation). The claim that the total injected variance is *larger* for Langevin than SDE at matched η is a useful observation but the paper does not exploit it (e.g., by sweeping η jointly).
- **Baseline fairness — defensible but worth scrutiny.** LC-GRPO uses 2 NFE/step so the authors give baselines 2× the steps and report the better number. This is honest, but it also means the baselines were retuned at a different step count than they were designed for; Flow-GRPO's published recipe is not necessarily optimal at 12–20 steps, so the gap could partly reflect baseline under-tuning.

## Strengths

- Identifies and clearly articulates a real, previously-underemphasized problem (finite-step ODE-vs-SDE divergence in flow-based GRPO) with a clean mechanistic fix.
- The free-score trick via Tweedie is elegant and removes the obvious engineering objection ("you need a separate score model").
- Strong, consistent empirical gains across two image models and one video model, four reward families, and compute-fair comparisons — not a single-benchmark win.
- Genuine theoretical backing with proofs, rare in this subfield.
- Best-in-class train-eval gap reduction (0.016 vs 0.063 on FLUX) directly validates the paper's central thesis, not just a downstream metric.
- Avoids the worst reward-hacking failure mode (Dynamic Degree preservation on HunyuanVideo).

## Limitations

- Strong-log-concavity assumption does not hold for real flow-model marginals; the theory is a sanity check, not a coverage guarantee for the experimental regime.
- No human evaluation; all gains measured against learned reward models that are themselves hackable.
- Only LoRA fine-tuning studied; full-FT, longer schedules, and larger-η regimes untested.
- Single Langevin sub-step and fixed η; richer corrector schedules unexplored.
- Baselines run at non-native step counts, introducing possible under-tuning confound.
- The video Visual Quality gain (+0.063) is the only positive number in that column for any method, which is striking but also a small absolute number on a noisy reward — confidence intervals are not reported.

## Relevance

与用户对扩散/流匹配生成模型后训练、RL 对齐以及训练-推理一致性（包括 LLM RL 中同类问题）的研究兴趣高度相关：LC-GRPO 提供了一个可理论分析的 predictor-corrector 框架，直接处理 GRPO 在 flow 模型上的 rollout 设计这一当前活跃问题，且其"score 来自 velocity、转移保持高斯似然"的技巧对后续 RL 后训练工作有方法论参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the train-eval gap advantage persist under full fine-tuning or longer training (e.g., 5k+ steps), where the policy can drift further from the reference and the ODE trajectory itself changes?
- Can the corrector be extended to multiple annealed Langevin sub-steps, and does the W2-advantage over Euler-Maruyama widen or shrink as the sub-step count grows?
- How robust are the gains to reward-model substitution (e.g., optimizing a stronger or adversarially-trained reward)? Do the auxiliary-metric degradations (CLIP drop in single-reward HPS training) disappear or worsen?
- The strong-log-concavity assumption fails for real marginals — can a weaker, distribution-free bound (e.g., under a score-approximation error term) recover the "corrector helps" guarantee in a regime that actually covers flow models?
- The LLM RL literature reports analogous train-inference mismatch from separate inference/training stacks (Qi et al. 2025; Yao et al. 2025); can the Langevin-correction idea be ported to discrete-token GRPO as a sampler-side correction weight, and does it beat the Yao et al. probability-ratio clipping?
- On HunyuanVideo, all methods leave Motion Quality negative — is this a fundamental limitation of the Visual Quality reward, and would a multi-objective VideoAlign reward (Visual + Motion + Text) close it?
