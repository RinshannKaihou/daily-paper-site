> **一句话总结：** 本文通过统一的"蒸馏-验证-迁移"协议在 ALFWorld、TAU2-Bench、XBench-DeepSearch 三个基准上研究冻结的 prompt 侧 playbook 能否跨域、跨模型、跨运行时迁移；结论是迁移"有条件成立"——在受控贪心解码下 ALFWorld 上可达 +23.6 pp 提升，但在异质服务代理场景下经多重性校正后仅 1/135 路由显著，且在运行时切换下会出现重复查询、延迟停止与成本膨胀，因此应把 playbook 复用当作"需验证的冷启动候选"而非"默认复用策略"。

> **TL;DR:** This Industry-Track study tests whether frozen prompt-side "playbooks" (procedures distilled from a source agent's failures by a stronger teacher) remain useful after transfer to new domains, models, and runtime regimes. On ALFWorld under greedy decoding, transfer helps strongly, lifting success rate up to +23.6 pp on Llama3.1-70B/Qwen3-32B and beating a budget-matched 5-shot prompt by +10.8 pp (X7D 72.3% vs FS-5 61.5% on Qwen3-32B valid_unseen); but on TAU2-Bench a global Holm correction over 135 Pass1 routes retains only 1 positive route, and on XBench-DeepSearch one 32K→128K runtime pairing keeps accuracy flat while exploding tool calls (10.4→24.7) and >30-step trajectories (8%→25%). The take-away: treat playbook transfer as a validation problem, not a reuse-by-default strategy.

## 核心贡献

- 提出面向部署的"冻结 playbook 迁移"评估协议（distill–validate–transfer），把"已蒸馏好的 prompt 侧操作流程"作为可冻结产物，研究其跨域/跨模型/跨运行时的可迁移性，而非提出新的学习算法。
- 在 ALFWorld 上给出受控存在性证明：在贪心解码（t=0）下，迁移的 playbook 在场景 OOD、目标规模、跨模型族三种条件下均有正向提升，最佳提升达 +23.6 pp，并伴随交互步数缩短；明确该效应随目标容量增大而增强。
- 在 TAU2-Bench 上给出"兼容性敏感"的服务代理迁移结果：先验预设的 matched-vs-mismatched 聚合对比支持温和的 +1.72 pp 平均同域优势（CI95 [+0.25,+2.97]，exact blocked-permutation p=.035），但对 135 条 Pass1 路由做全局 Holm 校正后仅保留 1 条正向路由，路由级效应多为描述性。
- 在 XBench-DeepSearch 上设计运行时切换压力测试：发现一处 32K→128K 的 artifact–runtime 配对在保持 Pass@1 基本持平的同时，出现重复查询率从 17.3% 升到 39.3%、>30 步轨迹率从 8% 升到 25% 的操作行为失稳，并通过 150 对行为审计定位到"重复查询 + 延迟停止"这一近端机制。
- 在一处近预算匹配的对比中证明蒸馏式指引优于固定示例：X7D（4,191 bytes）在 Qwen3-32B valid_unseen 上 72.3% vs FS-5（4,001 bytes）61.5%，+10.8 pp（CI95 [+2.7,+18.9]，exact McNemar p=.0139）。
- 提出部署清单（部署前冻结目标切片、风险阈值，并审计 accuracy / termination / protocol-compatibility / cost），把 prompt 侧 playbook 明确定位为"部署产物"而非"通用知识模块"。

## Key Contributions

- Defines a deployment-oriented **distill–validate–transfer** protocol for evaluating frozen prompt-side playbook transfer, rather than proposing a new learning algorithm or a validated compatibility selector.
- Shows transfer is real but **non-uniform**: on ALFWorld under greedy decoding it is uniformly positive across scene-OOD, target scale, and cross-family transfer, with the strongest lift reaching +23.6 pp and shorter trajectories; a 400-run temperature audit shows robustness is jointly determined by decoding regime × target capacity × artifact specificity.
- Provides the main heterogeneous service-agent evidence on TAU2-Bench: a prespecified matched-vs-mismatched aggregate contrast supports a modest +1.72 pp average domain-alignment advantage, but after global Holm correction over 135 Pass1 routes only 1 positive route survives (family-specific Holm additionally retains 2 negative cross-family routes); the rest of the grid is descriptive.
- Designs a runtime-shift stress test on XBench-DeepSearch showing that one 32K→128K artifact–runtime pairing keeps Pass@1 flat while raising search calls 5.9→15.7, total tool calls 10.4→24.7, and >30-step trajectories 8%→25%; a 150-pair behavioral audit localizes the proximal mechanism to re-querying and delayed stopping.
- Establishes incremental value of distilled guidance over fixed demonstrations in one near-budget-matched comparison: X7D (4,191 B) achieves 72.3% vs FS-5 (4,001 B) 61.5% on Qwen3-32B valid_unseen (+10.8 pp, McNemar p=.0139).
- Adds three content controls — same-length generic placebo, matched-vs-mismatched domain contrast, and target-derived-vs-transferred re-slice — that together weaken pure prompt-length/format explanations and show neither frozen transfer nor target-side redistillation is uniformly superior.

## Methodology

The paper does **not** introduce a new learning algorithm. It studies the portability of a fixed prompt-side artifact — a **playbook** — defined as a compact procedural checklist/guide distilled from repeated source-side executions and inserted verbatim into the agent prompt at deployment.

**Shared distill–validate–transfer protocol (Figure 1):**
1. Run a source agent K=8 times per condition and collect all-fail and mixed-outcome trajectory bundles.
2. Hand those bundles to a stronger closed-source distiller — Claude Opus 4.6 via Claude Code, or GPT-5.4 via Codex — which abstracts recurrent rules, search priorities, and recovery heuristics into a compact playbook.
3. Validate the playbook on the source side, then **freeze** it before any transfer.

**Three benchmarks, three roles:**
- **ALFWorld** — controlled existence test (scene OOD, target scale 7B/32B, cross-family Qwen↔Llama), reported as normalized success rate with environment-broken games removed. 5 seeds × 2 temperatures (t=0, t=0.3) = 400-run audit.
- **TAU2-Bench** — main heterogeneous service-agent benchmark across airline/retail/telecom, in-family (Qwen2.5) and cross-family (Qwen3, Llama3.1). Headline metric is corrected official-style Pass1; global Holm correction applied jointly to all 135 route hypotheses; Pass2–4, cost, and unretained routes are descriptive.
- **XBench-DeepSearch** — runtime-shift stress test: one Claude-distilled playbook from Tongyi-DeepResearch-30B-A3B 32K source trajectories, deployed across {Tongyi-DR, Qwen3-32B} × {32K, 128K}; metrics are Pass@1, question-keyed True Pass@3, and deployment-side cost.

**Artifacts:** ALFWorld uses C7S/C32S (Claude-distilled procedural checklists) and X7D/X7C (Codex-distilled detailed/compact variants) from a shared Qwen2.5-7B pool. TAU2 uses 9 playbooks (P7A…P32T) written in domain-general surface form but derived from source-domain failures. XBench uses one search-protocol playbook with explicit budget/stopping guidance.

**Controls:** no-playbook baseline, same-length generic placebo (structure-matched rewrite removing domain content), matched-vs-mismatched within-source domain contrast (exact blocked-permutation test over 3^9=19683 assignments), and a target-derived-vs-transferred re-slice on a Qwen2.5-14B target.

## Results & Highlights

1. **ALFWorld greedy-decoding transfer is strongly positive and capacity-graded (Table 7).** On valid_unseen at t=0, X7D lifts Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct from 62.7% baseline by **+12.2 pp**, Qwen3-32B from 45.5% by **+23.6 pp**, and Llama3.1-70B-Inst from 42.0% by **+19.5 pp** (all *** p<0.001). Gains on the 7B target are much smaller (Qwen2.5-7B unseen: +9.3 pp for X7D). Trajectory length drops in step (e.g., Qwen3-32B seen: −32% to −36% avg steps).

2. **Distilled guidance beats a near-budget-matched 5-shot prompt.** On Qwen3-32B valid_unseen, X7D (4,191 B) solves **107/148 (72.3%)** vs FS-5 (4,001 B) **91/148 (61.5%)**, a **+10.8 pp** gain (CI95 [+2.7,+18.9], exact McNemar p=.0139). Both artifacts share the same Qwen2.5-7B source run and a frozen 148-game manifest.

3. **Temperature robustness is capacity- and artifact-specific (Table 8).** X7D on Qwen2.5-7B flips from +6.9 pp at t=0 to **−16.0 pp at t=0.3** on seen, with hit-50 rising 58.1%→79.8% and mean trajectory length 32.8→42.4 steps. The same artifact on Qwen3-32B and Llama3.1-70B-Inst remains strongly positive at both temperatures (+19 to +25 pp).

4. **TAU2-Bench's only multiplicity-surviving route-level effect is tiny.** Global Holm correction over 135 Pass1 routes retains exactly **1/135 positive route** (P14T→Qwen2.5-7B/telecom, +9.69 pp). The prespecified aggregate matched-vs-mismatched contrast gives **+1.72 pp** average domain-alignment advantage (CI95 [+0.25,+2.97], exact blocked-permutation p=.035), supporting a modest average effect rather than broad per-route gains.

5. **Same-length placebo rules out a pure length/format explanation on three selected routes (Table 2).** On P14R→14B/retail, transferred playbook 62.6% vs placebo 57.1% vs baseline 55.2%; on the negative P7A→Q3-32B/retail route, transferred drops to 60.8% vs placebo 70.7% vs baseline 72.4% — placebo tracks baseline while content transfer reproduces the signed effect.

6. **XBench runtime shift destabilizes operational behavior (Figure 6).** Same-context transfer (Tongyi-DR 32K → Qwen3-32B 32K) raises Pass@1 by +4 to +8 pp. But Tongyi-DR 128K keeps Pass@1 flat while dropping Pass@3 by **−16 pp**, and Qwen3-32B 128K raises search calls **5.9→15.7**, total tool calls **10.4→24.7**, and >30-step trajectory rate **8%→25%**. Target-side redistillation does not remove the risk: it leaves Qwen3-32B 128K accuracy unresolved while raising total tool calls to 36.1 (+247%).

7. **Behavioral audit localizes the 128K shift.** Among 150 paired trajectories on Qwen3-32B, identical-repeated-query share rises **17.3%→39.3%**, ≥3-identical-search share **8%→24%**, and submit-within-10-tool-calls share falls **78%→30%**; question-cluster intervals exclude zero, supporting re-querying and delayed stopping as the proximal pathway.

## Authors & Credibility

Authors are **Weihong Lin, Lin Sun (corresponding), and Xiangzheng Zhang** from **Beijing Qiyuan Technology Co., Ltd.** (a 360-affiliated entity, contact `sunlin1@360.cn`). The paper is an **Industry Track deployment study**, which fits its framing: it makes no algorithmic claim, reports extensive multiplicity correction (global Holm + family-specific Holm + global Benjamini–Hochberg sensitivity), uses paired bootstrap CIs with 500,000 resamples, exact blocked-permutation and exact McNemar tests, and explicitly marks descriptive results as non-confirmatory. The honesty about negative results (XBench destabilization, only 1/135 routes surviving correction, two retained *negative* cross-family routes) is above the norm for the agent-improvement literature. The artifact inventory (Tables 4–6) and a full verbatim example playbook (P14R, Appendix B.4) materially improve reproducibility.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Diagnostic rather than mechanistic or algorithmic. The distill–validate–transfer workflow itself is close to existing experience-replay/skill-distillation work (ExpeL, Trace2skill, Contextual Experience Replay, A-Mem, PromptAgent); the contribution is the *deployment-oriented transfer audit* across three benchmarks with multiplicity correction. Readers expecting a new method will be disappointed; readers wanting a "should I reuse this playbook?" decision framework will find it useful.
- **Rigor:** Strong on TAU2 statistics (global Holm over 135 routes, exact permutation test, paired bootstrap, placebo control). Honest that only 1/135 positive routes survives correction and that the aggregate matched-domain effect is +1.72 pp — small in absolute terms. The authors explicitly state "failure to survive correction is not evidence of zero effect," which is correct but also weakens any strong claim.
- **Reproducibility:** Good surface reproducibility (artifacts inventoried, one full playbook printed verbatim, harness/distiller named: Claude Code + claude-opus-4.6, Codex + gpt-5.4). But the source playbooks depend on **closed proprietary distillers** (Claude Opus 4.6, GPT-5.4) whose outputs are not bit-reproducible, and no artifacts/playbooks appear to be publicly released. The XBench result rests on a **single primary artifact–runtime pairing** (one Claude-distilled playbook from Tongyi-DR-30B-A3B 32K) on **100 public problems**, which is thin for the strong "runtime shift destabilizes" claim.
- **Significance:** Practically useful for deployment teams: the deployment checklist (freeze target slice, validate accuracy/termination/protocol/cost) and the "conditional cold-start, not reuse-by-default" framing are immediately actionable. Scientifically modest — it is an empirical cartography of failure modes, not a predictive selector.
- **Red flags / overclaiming:**
  - The ALFWorld "transfer works" headline depends on **greedy decoding (t=0)**; under t=0.3 the 7B + X7D cell goes strongly negative (−16 pp), so the "existence proof" is decoding-regime-conditional and the paper is appropriately careful but the abstract leans on the positive side.
  - The few-shot comparison is **a single artifact (X7D) vs a single fixed FS-5 prompt** in one compatible ALFWorld setting; the authors explicitly disclaim cross-paradigm superiority, but readers may over-read it.
  - The +1.72 pp TAU2 matched-domain advantage, while statistically clean, is small enough that deployment cost could easily swamp it; the cost audit (Section 5.4) shows cross-family telecom "gains" come with higher token overhead.
  - No comparison against **human-authored playbooks** or **deployment-time dynamic retrieval** systems, both of which are the realistic production alternatives; this is acknowledged but limits the practical verdict.
  - The prospective Gemini-2.5-Pro cost-screening pilot (Appendix E.4) is so under-powered (one model, one threshold, 347% held-out inflation) that it neither validates nor invalidates the screening idea — including it adds little.

## Strengths

- Rare intellectual honesty: the paper foregrounds where transfer **fails** (XBench destabilization, negative cross-family routes, 134/135 non-surviving routes) rather than burying it.
- Statistically disciplined: global Holm correction over the full route family, exact permutation/McNemar tests, paired bootstrap CIs, and three distinct content controls (placebo, matched-vs-mismatched, target-derived).
- The deployment checklist and the "validate accuracy + termination + protocol-compatibility + cost" quadruple is a genuinely useful operational artifact.
- Complementary benchmark roles are well-motivated: ALFWorld = existence, TAU2 = heterogeneity, XBench = runtime shift.
- Artifact inventory + a full verbatim playbook (P14R) make the transferred objects inspectable rather than black-box.

## Limitations

- TAU2-Bench has **limited per-cell power**; global Holm retains only 1 positive route, and most of the heatmap is descriptive.
- The XBench runtime-shift result hinges on **one artifact** and **one 100-problem benchmark**; it is a stress test, not a general law of long-context deployment.
- No comparison against human-authored playbooks or dynamic retrieval baselines; the few-shot comparison is a single-pair test.
- Closed proprietary distillers (Claude Opus 4.6, GPT-5.4) make bit-level reproduction impossible; no public artifact release is mentioned.
- No software-engineering, code-generation, or data-analysis agents are evaluated; generalization beyond embodied/service/search agents is untested.
- Target-derived (L1) comparisons are explicitly secondary and re-sliced from a pre-submission source-model analysis, not part of the 135-route confirmatory family.
- The prospective cost-screening pilot is too small (one model, one threshold) to calibrate a selector.

## Relevance

This work is directly relevant to anyone deploying or improving LLM agents with prompt-side experience, skill libraries, or agentic memory — i.e., the "agent self-improvement / experience replay / prompt engineering for tool-using agents" thread. Its negative-result orientation (when does transferred guidance *fail* or *inflate cost*) is especially valuable for production deployments where cost and termination stability matter as much as accuracy, and it complements the user's interest in agent systems, distillation, and robust deployment of LLM agents.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can a **pre-transfer compatibility screen** (e.g., a cheap probe of tool-schema overlap, decoding-regime match, and stopping-rule alignment) predict which of the 135 TAU2 routes will transfer positively, rather than auditing after the fact?
- Does the ALFWorld finding that **larger targets benefit more from playbooks** hold on harder benchmarks (e.g., SWE-bench, real code agents), or is it an artifact of the 50-step ceiling rescuing mechanism?
- The XBench 128K destabilization is attributed to re-querying and delayed stopping — would an **explicit stopping-rule guard** injected into the playbook (or a hard tool-call budget) recover bounded behavior without sacrificing the +4–8 pp Pass@1 gain?
- How do **human-authored playbooks** compare against distilled ones under the same matched-byte budget? The paper explicitly declines this comparison but it is the most deployment-relevant baseline.
- Since target-side redistillation raised tool calls +247% on Qwen3-32B 128K without resolving accuracy, is there a **Pareto frontier** between accuracy gain and cost inflation that a multi-objective selector could exploit?
- Does the "compatibility-sensitive, not distance-sensitive" cross-family pattern (no monotonic "further family, weaker effect") replicate with more model families, or is it an artifact of the three families tested (Qwen2.5, Qwen3, Llama3.1)?
