Why This Matters

Trust the yardstick

Asks whether the policy-grounded benchmarks we trust (τ-Bench / IntellAgent) are themselves trustworthy — a portable, reference-free method for any evaluation-quality problem.

!
Critical Edge

Thin human floor + self-preference

Validation rests on a single author-annotator over 50 tasks (no inter-annotator agreement), and the top-tier generators are also the judges — a self-preference confound neither is analyzed.

Methodology

Targets τ-Bench / IntellAgent family: a domain has tools, a DB, and a policy P; each task t = (dₜ, eₜ, iₜ). The framework evaluates the benchmark, not the agent.

  • Consistency: LLM judges (1–10) for Description–Expected Behavior alignment and Policy–Expected Behavior alignment.
  • Complexity & coverage: from a semi-auto policy-item list (L_P, ToolGuard-seeded), derive Policy Violations per Task and Policy Violations Coverage (≥ K=3 tasks).
  • Expected-quality gradients: generate benchmarks with 5 LLMs (GPT-5.4 → Llama-3.2-1B) + two controlled perturbations.
  • Benchmark Ordering Score: fraction of 8 pairwise tier constraints a metric satisfies. Judges: GPT-5.4, Claude-4.5-Sonnet, Gemini-2-Flash · Airline (100) + Retail (95) tasks/bench · 975 tasks total.

Key Contributions

  • Frames an underexplored problem: evaluating benchmark quality itself, not agents on benchmarks.
  • Four reference-free LLM-judge metrics — no trusted reference dataset or agent panel required.
  • Builds expected-quality gradients from 5 generators (GPT-5.4 → Llama-3.2-1B) via IntellAgent.
  • Adds controlled perturbations (20–80% behavior swap, cross-domain policy swap) for sensitivity.
  • Validates against independent human annotation (Kendall's τb) and applies to a curated benchmark (τ³-Bench).
  • Delivers a 5-category defect taxonomy turning scores into debugging tools.

Strengths

  • Genuinely reference-free — sidesteps the chicken-and-egg of needing a trusted benchmark to validate benchmarks.
  • Three independent validation strategies are more convincing together than any one alone.
  • Strong headline: perfect ordering in Airline across all judges × all four metrics.
  • Actionable diagnostics + honest limitations + full prompt transparency in appendices.

Limitations

  • Only two domains (Airline, Retail) from one benchmark family; other families unverified.
  • Single human annotator, 50 tasks, no inter-annotator agreement.
  • Potential self-preference bias (judge = top-tier generator) not measured.
  • Manual hyperparameters (K=3, policy-item extraction) not ablated.

Scope

Closest comparator (Benchmark², Qian et al. 2026) needs to run many LLMs per benchmark — cheap for MMLU/ARC, prohibitive for open-ended multi-turn agent tasks, which sharpens this contribution. Caveat: the entire Ordering Score rests on the unproven assumption that stronger LLMs make better benchmarks, validated only post hoc against τb topping out at 0.67.

Open Questions for Further Exploration

  1. How do metrics behave on non-policy-grounded benchmarks (open tool-use, web agents)?
  2. Would a second independent annotator shift the τb 0.32–0.67 range?
  3. Can the self-preference confound be isolated with judges outside the generator pool?
  4. Is K=3 coverage and the ToolGuard policy list robust, or do small flips change Retail ordering?
  5. Can the defect taxonomy become automated per-task repair suggestions?
  6. How does judge-call cost scale with policy length and task count?
