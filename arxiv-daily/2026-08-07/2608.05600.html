LC-GRPO: Bridging the Train-Inference Gap for Flow-Based GRPO with Langevin Correction

Yingqing Guo, Hui Yuan, Zijian He, Mengdi Wang, Zheng Ding

Key
Finding
One Langevin correction step after each ODE-Euler rollout closes flow-based GRPO's train-inference gap — and the score comes free from the flow velocity via Tweedie's formula, with no extra network.
Key Metric
0.016gap
Train–eval reward gap on FLUX.1-Dev / HPS-v2.1 — the paper's central thesis, measured at the rollout vs. ODE-evaluation boundary.
Flow-GRPO 0.063 CPS 0.035 ≈ 4× tighter
Why This Matters
Directly tackles the active flow-model GRPO rollout problem. The Tweedie-free-score, Gaussian-likelihood predictor-corrector is reusable methodology for the broader RL post-training of generative models.
Critical Edge
Theorem 3 requires α-strong log-concavity — essentially never satisfied by real flow-model marginals (multimodal mixtures). The theory is a sanity check, not coverage for the SD3.5 / FLUX / HunyuanVideo regime.

01 Methodology — predictor / corrector

1
Predictor (inference-aligned ODE step). From x_t take the same backward Euler step the test-time sampler uses: x' = x_t − Δt·v_θ(x_t, t). The deterministic rollout component now follows the inference trajectory exactly.
2
Corrector (one Langevin step). x_{t−Δt} = x' + ε_t·s(x', t−Δt) + √(2ε_t)·ξ. The score is recovered via Tweedie — s(x,t) = −(x + (1−t)v(x,t))/t — using the same flow model. A single scalar η controls exploration; the final denoising step skips the corrector.
3
GRPO integration. The corrected transition stays an isotropic Gaussian N(m_θ(x_t), 2ε_t I_d) with closed-form density, plugging straight into the importance ratio & clipped surrogate; gradients flow through the corrector.

Why it beats SDE rollouts: Euler-Maruyama accumulates drift and Brownian error and blurs as η grows. Langevin leaves the target marginal invariant, is a known score-based corrector, and at matched variance injects more noise while producing sharper samples.

03 Strengths

  • Clean mechanistic fix for a real, under-emphasized finite-step ODE-vs-SDE divergence.
  • Tweedie free-score trick removes the "needs a separate score model" objection.
  • Consistent gains across 2 image + 1 video models, four reward families — not a single-benchmark win.
  • Genuine proofs (W2 contraction, leading-order EM comparison) — rare in this subfield.
  • Best-in-class gap reduction (0.016 vs 0.063) directly validates the thesis.

04 Limitations

  • Strong-log-concavity doesn't hold for real marginals; theory ≠ coverage guarantee.
  • No human evaluation — gains measured against hackable learned reward models.
  • Only LoRA studied; full-FT, longer schedules, large-η untested.
  • Single Langevin sub-step & fixed η; richer corrector schedules unexplored.
  • Baselines run at non-native step counts — possible under-tuning confound.

05 Open Questions

  • Does the gap advantage survive full fine-tuning / 5k+ steps as the policy drifts?
  • Can the corrector extend to multiple annealed sub-steps — does the W2 edge widen?
  • Robustness to stronger / adversarial rewards — do auxiliary degradations worsen?
  • A distribution-free bound under score-approximation error that actually covers flow models?
  • Portable to discrete-token LLM GRPO as a sampler-side correction weight?
