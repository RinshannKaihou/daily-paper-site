> **一句话总结：** 作者提出 RENDEQ——通过重绘同一份数据生成语义等价、答案已知的图表集，从而把"一致性"和"正确性"的耦合变成可精确测量的对象；在三个开源 VLM 上发现重绘胜过采样、纯无标签的一致性信号能击败 token log-probability 基线，但用一致性做自训练反而会持续降低准确率（反转了已有正向结论）。

> **TL;DR:** The paper introduces RENDEQ, a generator of "render-equivalence sets" — figures redrawn from the same data under togglable cosmetic styles so that semantic equivalence and the exact answer are both guaranteed by construction. Measured on Qwen2.5-VL-7B/3B and InternVL2-8B (3 independent instantiations, N≈3150, K=8), re-rendering beats resampling (REA over fixed-image self-consistency B3: pooled ΔAUROC +0.102, CI [+0.083, +0.121], p<10⁻⁴); render-agreement REA beats the evidence-carrying mean token log-probability baseline B1 on AUROC on 2 of 3 models (7B: 0.864 vs 0.771; InternVL2-8B: 0.907 vs 0.875; tie on 3B); the plotting-library style factor carries more than 2× the dispersion of any other (lift 0.102 vs 0.043); and fine-tuning on the model's own K=8 majority inverts a published positive result, dropping hold-out accuracy by 10.4–27.2 points in all 5 runs — because, per Theorem 5, rewarding agreement destroys exactly the diffuseness that makes agreement certifiable.

## 核心贡献

- **构造了 RENDEQ 测量仪器**：通过程序重绘同一份数据生成的图表集，每个实例都自带精确的程序化答案（ground truth），并且每个样式因子（绘图库、调色板、主题、网格、长宽比、标记、字体、刻度、图例、分辨率）都可单独切换。这把"一致性是否意味着正确"这一直被假定的命题变成了可直接测量的对象——正确性可被精确观测，集中度（concentration）可在无混淆的情况下被估计。
- **量化和归因了一致性–正确性的耦合**：在 Qwen2.5-VL-7B、Qwen2.5-VL-3B、InternVL2-8B 上跨三个独立生成的实例化验证，发现重绘信号（REA/RM）既比采样自洽更强，也比携带证据的 token log-probability 基线更强（在三分之二模型上），并能把分散度归因到具体样式因子。
- **推翻了"自一致性自训练有益"的边界条件**：在 RENDEQ 上用模型自己的 K=8 多数票做 LoRA 微调后，准确率在 5/5 次重复中全部下降（hold-out 跌 10.4–27.2 点），与 Kaya et al. 在自然图像上的正向结论相反，并给出了一个理论机制（Theorem 5：奖励一致性会压缩错误的扩散度，从而破坏一致性本身的认证能力）。
- **给出了配套的理论分析（Section 4）**：证明 REA 在有限 K 下对集中度 π 是向上有偏的（并给出无偏的 pairwise 替代 REA_pair），证明一致性只在错误扩散度 L 决定的阈值之上才认证正确性，并给出样式因子的分解不等式（Theorem 7）。

## Key Contributions

- **RENDEQ instrument.** A generator and protocol whose exact semantic equivalence, exact programmatic ground truth, and individually togglable style factors make correctness exactly observable and concentration estimable without confounding. This is the first setup (per the authors' Table A1) where the input is perturbed *and* equivalence is guaranteed by construction *and* an exact answer key exists.
- **Measurement of the coupling with per-factor attribution.** Quantifies how tightly agreement tracks correctness on three open-weight VLMs, and attributes the dispersion to specific cosmetic factors rather than assuming many small contributors.
- **A boundary condition that empirically inverts a published positive result.** Consensus self-training (the fine-tuning half of Kaya et al.'s recipe) cuts accuracy instead of raising it in 5/5 replication runs, with a mechanism (Theorem 5) that explains why.
- **Three theoretical results (Section 4).** (i) REA is upward-biased for modal mass π at finite K, with an unbiased pairwise alternative; (ii) agreement certifies correctness only above a threshold 1/L set by how diffuse errors are; (iii) render dispersion decomposes over style factors, bounding what any single-factor ablation can remove.

## Methodology

A scientific figure is the output of a rendering program R applied to data D; because the data fixes the exact answer a★(D, q) to any well-posed quantitative question, redrawing D under cosmetic-only style changes θ yields images that are semantically equivalent by construction. The family X(D, q) = {x_i = R(D; θ_i)}_{i=1..K} is called a **render-equivalence set** (Definition 1). The paper introduces two quantities on it:

- **REA (render-equivalence agreement).** For categorical answers, the modal share REA_cat = (1/K) Σ 1[ŷ_i = m̂]. For continuous answers, the share within relative tolerance τ of the median. Computed without ground truth.
- **RM (render marginalization).** Modal vote (categorical) or median (continuous) over the K answers. Uses no labels and costs K forward passes.

RENDEQ axes (Table 1) vary independently: plot family (bar, grouped bar, stacked bar, line, scatter, pie, log-axis), question type (value read, comparison, extremum, trend sign, arithmetic), the style space Θ (10 cosmetic factors), and difficulty controls. Default K=8, N=350 per instantiation × 3 seeds = 1050 per model, 3150 pooled.

Setup: greedy decoding (temperature 0) for main runs; models Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct, InternVL2-8B (3B in bf16, 7B/InternVL2-8B in 4-bit NF4). Baselines: B1 mean token log-probability, B2 verbalized confidence, B3 response self-consistency over K=8 samples at fixed rendering (the mechanism control isolating render vs decoding dispersion), B4 Khan & Fu's consistency signal, B5 prompt ensemble. Of 3150 pooled instances, 29.5% are numeric. REA values are modal and upward-biased at finite K (Proposition 4(ii)); Section G confirms ranking is unchanged under the unbiased pairwise alternative.

Section 4 supplies three theorems: Proposition 4 (REA estimates concentration with bias, REA_pair is unbiased), Proposition 5 (confident errors must be concentrated; diffuseness level L yields a certifying threshold 1/L), and Proposition 7 (factor decomposition bounding each style factor's contribution to total disagreement). Proofs in Appendix C.

## Results & Highlights

1. **Re-rendering beats resampling on reliability.** Pooled over the three independently generated instantiations (N≈3150), REA beats the fixed-image self-consistency control B3 by ΔAUROC = +0.102, 95% CI [+0.083, +0.121], p<10⁻⁴; per model +0.061 to +0.157, all CIs excluding zero. This attributes the reliability signal to re-rendering specifically rather than generic answer instability.
2. **Re-rendering beats resampling on accuracy.** Render marginalization (RM) improves on single-render decoding by +0.057 (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, 0.644→0.701), +0.055 (Qwen-3B, 0.571→0.626), and +0.061 (InternVL2-8B, 0.563→0.624); RM beats self-consistency SC by 4.8–6.3 points on every model, all intervals excluding zero (Table 4).
3. **REA beats the evidence-carrying baseline B1 on AUROC on 2 of 3 models and ties on the third.** Qwen2.5-VL-7B: 0.864 vs 0.771 (Δ +0.094, CI [+0.066, +0.121]); InternVL2-8B: 0.907 vs 0.875 (Δ +0.033, CI [+0.013, +0.053]); Qwen2.5-VL-3B: 0.858 vs 0.867 (Δ −0.009, CI [−0.030, +0.011], statistical tie). Grand pooled Δ = +0.040, CI [+0.027, +0.053], p<10⁻⁴.
4. **Dispersion is concentrated in one style factor, not spread across many.** Plotting library (matplotlib vs plotly) lift 0.102, CI [+0.084, +0.120]; more than double the next factor (bar_labels 0.043) and roughly an order of magnitude above the noise floor. Every other factor's interval includes zero except bar_labels and bar_orient (0.019).
5. **Consensus self-training inverts a published positive result.** LoRA (r=8, 3 epochs) on Qwen2.5-VL-7B with the model's own K=8 majority as the label: accuracy fell under RM in all 5/5 runs, by 3.6–21.2 points on training families and 10.4–27.2 points on held-out families. Hold-out accuracy fell in all five runs under single-render decoding (8.4–18.8 points). Hold-out AUROC *rose* in all five runs (+0.009 to +0.100) — the model grew more confidently self-consistent on families it now answers worse, exactly as Theorem 5 predicts.
6. **REA's finite-K bias is non-negligible.** For P=(0.5,0.2,0.2,0.1) the bias E[REA]−π is +0.17 at K=2 and +0.04 at K=8 — the budgets used throughout — so any calibration of REA at small K is optimistic by construction (Proposition 4(ii)).
7. **Perception failures dominate.** Pooled, 80.4% of instances have at least one wrong intermediate read; pure reasoning failures (all reads correct, answer wrong) are only 4.9–6.6% per model. RFR (render-flip rate, label-free) is 0.59 (7B), 0.64 (3B), 0.73 (InternVL2-8B), strongly negatively correlated with per-family accuracy (r=−0.69).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** The paper is explicit that it claims no priority over input-perturbation consistency (Zhang et al., Rosenfeld et al., Kaya et al.) nor over the concentration-vs-correctness distinction itself. The genuine novelty is *exactness*: equivalence by construction plus a programmatic answer key, summarized in Table A1, which does place it uniquely against eight close works.
- **Rigor.** Methodologically rigorous — three independent generator instantiations, cluster-bootstrap intervals with 5000 resamples, McNemar's test for paired accuracy comparisons, honest handling of an intermediate bug, and a theoretical section with proofs (Appendix C) that predict the empirical outcomes. The empirical/theoretical alignment (Theorem 5 → the self-training inversion) is the most compelling part.
- **Reproducibility.** Strong: pinned code, pinned seeds, explicit model revisions verified by hash, explicit quantization settings, and released data. The K=8 / N=350-per-instantiation budget is small enough to rerun cheaply.
- **Significance.** The boundary condition on consensus self-training is the most consequential finding — it identifies a *regime* in which a published positive recipe fails and supplies the mechanism, which is more useful than a binary replication/non-replication.
- **Red flags and weaknesses (paper-specific, not boilerplate).**
  - **The most-needed ablation is missing.** Section 6.3 identifies the plotting library as the dominant dispersion carrier, and Remark after Proposition 7 explicitly predicts the library-held-fixed ablation. The authors state this ablation "remains genuinely missing, too few instances have all eight renderings in one library to power it." Until run, the paper cannot separate sensitivity to cosmetic style in general from sensitivity to the plotting backend specifically — which would narrow the paper's scope.
  - **The delta the paper argues for is reasoned, not measured.** There is no approximate-perturbation arm. B3 separates re-rendering from decoding noise, but nothing separates *exact* equivalence from ordinary pixel augmentation, which is precisely the delta the paper rests on. The authors concede this.
  - **No real-chart external validity.** Every number comes from a single in-style synthetic split of the authors' own generator. The out-of-style split and a real-data anchor built from a public benchmark were "not run."
  - **Closest competitor not run.** Jiang and Luo (Chart-COCA, the closest label-free test-time method for charts) is discussed but not run against RM. Semantic entropy and P(True), the two standard label-free signals, are also absent.
  - **Load-bearing hyperparameter never swept.** The numeric tolerance τ=5% is load-bearing in magnitude and, on the 3B model, in direction too (Section G), yet is never varied.
  - **The "inversion" of Kaya et al. is not a replication and the authors say so.** Different aggregation level (answer vs token), models, and benchmarks; it identifies a failing regime rather than refuting the original.
  - **Multiple-testing uncorrected.** Roughly 18 paired comparisons without multiple-testing correction.
  - **Models all from 2024 / early 2025; two of three share the Qwen family** — no current-generation model from a third family.

## Strengths

- The render-equivalence construction is a genuinely clean measurement instrument: it removes both confounds (meaning-preservation-by-assumption and no-exact-answer-key) that the paper correctly identifies as the reason the agreement–accuracy coupling is rarely measured directly.
- The theory (Section 4) and the empirics are tightly coupled: each of the four predictions in Table 2 maps to a theorem, and the most striking empirical result (self-training inversion) is *predicted* by Theorem 5 rather than discovered post hoc.
- Exceptional scientific candor: full disclosure of an intermediate reversal caused by a silent pipeline bug, including the specific line (`cd $DIR` unquoted), the diagnosis path, what was ruled out, and what was re-run.
- The factor-attribution result (library dominates by ≥2×) gives practitioners a concrete, cheap recommendation: vary the plotting backend before anything else.
- A reusable, label-free diagnostic (render-flip rate RFR, render-equivalence robustness RER) and a clean perception/reasoning decomposition via the intermediate-read query.

## Limitations

- Single in-style synthetic split of the authors' own generator; external validity to real charts untested (out-of-style and real-data anchor splits exist in the generator but were not run).
- Three models only, all 2024/early-2025, two sharing the Qwen2.5-VL family; no current-generation third-family model.
- The library-held-fixed ablation — the single most informative experiment for the central attribution claim — is missing.
- No approximate-perturbation arm, so the exactness-vs-approximate-augmentation delta (the paper's core argument) is reasoned, not measured.
- Consensus-self-training inversion measured on one generator, one model, one LoRA configuration; magnitude (not direction) varies across the five runs.
- τ is load-bearing and never swept; no accuracy-at-fixed-coverage or held-out threshold selection (what a deployed abstention rule would need); ~18 paired comparisons without multiple-testing correction.
- The perception/reasoning split rests on a separate intermediate-read query whose own reliability is never validated.
- RM cannot fix systematic, style-invariant errors and costs K× inference.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the REA/RM advantage survive on real-world charts (ChartQA, PlotQA, FigureQA) and on the generator's out-of-style split? The real-data anchor is built into RENDEQ but never run.
- What happens when the library-held-fixed ablation is finally powered? Does it eliminate the dispersion effect or merely attenuate it (Proposition 7 predicts attenuation, not elimination, unless one factor accounts for essentially all of D)?
- How does REA compare to semantic entropy and P(True) — the two standard label-free signals that are conspicuously absent here — on the same render-equivalence sets?
- Would token-level aggregation (which Kaya et al. report as stronger than answer-level) change the self-training inversion, or only its magnitude?
- Is the inversion regime-specific to charts/scatter families where the base majority is already wrong, or does it generalize to any data where the model's errors are concentrated rather than diffuse? The diffuseness level L̂ (median 1.33–1.50 here) is the natural predictor to test.
- Does combining REA with B1 (the cross-validated logistic combination in Appendix E, which beats B1 alone on all three models) translate into a better deployed abstention rule at fixed coverage?
