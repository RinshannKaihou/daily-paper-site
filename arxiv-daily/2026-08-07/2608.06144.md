> **一句话总结：** FinEvo-Bench 是一个面向金融专业工作流的自演化智能体纵向评测基准，包含 120 个真实案例驱动的多文件任务、20 个业务场景（覆盖 6 大金融领域），采用三条全局交错洗牌的任务流并配对状态重置对照，在统一 Qwen3.7-Max 骨干下比较 Claude Code、Codex、Letta、GenericAgent 四种脚手架；结果显示自演化让分数提升 9.33–19.37 分、合规问题下降 0.12–0.44 个/任务，其中 Letta 取得最高演化后分数（91.65），Codex 取得最大演化增益（+19.37）。

> **TL;DR:** FinEvo-Bench is a longitudinal benchmark with 120 real-case-grounded, multi-file financial tasks across 20 business scenes in six domains, evaluated under three independently shuffled, globally interleaved streams with paired state-reset controls on a shared Qwen3.7-Max backbone. All four scaffolds (Claude Code, Codex, Letta, GenericAgent) beat their non-evolving controls (Letta's evolved score 91.65 highest, +0.09 compliance issues/task lowest; Codex +19.37 largest self-evolution gain; GenericAgent +9.33 smallest gain but lowest cost); an independent Claude Opus 4.6 rubric judge agrees with a financial expert at ICC(A,1)=0.95, rubric feedback beats reference-answer feedback by 3.95-7.93 points, and skill-only evolution in Claude Code reaches 93.71 points with 0.05 compliance issues/task.

## 核心贡献

- **首个联合覆盖四项特性的自演化基准：** FinEvo-Bench 同时具备跨任务复用（cross-task）、开放性交付物（open-ended deliverables）、多维度评分（multi-aspect evaluation），以及专业工作流（professional workflows），弥补了 LifelongAgent-Bench、SEA-Eval、Evo-Memory、StuLife、Frontier-Eng 等只覆盖部分维度的不足。
- **120 个真实案例驱动的多文件任务：** 20 个业务场景、每场景 6 个实质性差异化的案例共享同一专业流程但事实与结论各异；775 个输入文件，平均每任务 6.46 个文件（范围 2–11），来源为机构提供与公开文档化案例，并经脱敏处理。
- **配对纵向协议分离"演化能力"与"最终能力"：** 每个演化运行与一个共享任务顺序、骨干、解码配置但任务前重置状态的对照配对，从而把"保留经验"带来的增益 ΔScore、ΔComp 与骨干本身的能力分离报告。
- **全局交错而非分块的任务流：** 用三条独立洗牌的全局交错流（而非连续场景块）来检验智能体在无关任务间检索相关经验的能力；这是与以往顺序任务流基准的关键区别。
- **共享骨干的多脚手架公平比较：** 在统一 Qwen3.7-Max 骨干（1M 上下文、贪心解码、温度零）下，对比四种代表不同持久化形态的脚手架——Claude Code/Codex（可蒸馏技能 + 项目/全局记忆）、Letta（可编辑记忆块，全程全量注入）、GenericAgent（Markdown 经验文件 + 标题级 L0 索引）。
- **可复现的判定与人类验证：** 独立的 Claude Opus 4.6 支撑的 Claude Code 评分智能体应用场景级 100 分评分细则；在 120 份交付物上与金融专家达成 ICC(A,1)=0.95（95% CI [0.93, 0.97]），平均绝对差 1.6 分、最大 5 分。
- **多维诊断揭示自演化机理：** 给出经验载体（memory-only / skill-only / 组合）、反馈形式（rubric vs reference-answer）、五项能力维度、场景内出现次序（早 vs 晚 rank）、跨场景干扰等多项诊断结论。

## Key Contributions

- **First benchmark jointly covering cross-task evolution, open-ended artifacts, multi-aspect evaluation, and professional workflows.** Table 1 shows existing self-evolution benchmarks each cover a strict subset (LifelongAgent-Bench, SEA-Eval, Evo-Memory: ordered reuse only; StuLife: situated adaptation; Frontier-Eng: iterative artifact improvement); FinEvo-Bench is the first to combine all four in recurring professional workflows.
- **120 real-case-grounded multi-file tasks with institution-validated procedures.** 20 business scenes across six financial domains, six substantively distinct cases per scene sharing one professional procedure but differing in facts, files, calculations, and conclusions. 775 input files (mean 6.46/task, range 2-11) drawn from institution-provided and publicly documented cases, with direct identifiers and sensitive fields removed/replaced while preserving data relationships.
- **Paired longitudinal protocol that isolates self-evolution ability from absolute performance.** Every evolving run is paired with a state-reset control sharing the same task order, backbone, decoding config, and scoring procedure; only the retention of prior-task feedback differs. ΔScore and ΔComp thereby attribute gains to retained experience rather than backbone strength.
- **Globally interleaved (not contiguous-block) shuffled streams.** Three independently shuffled, globally interleaved streams require each scaffold to retain and retrieve relevant experience amid unrelated intervening tasks, preventing naive positional memorization.
- **Shared-backbone, scaffold-fair comparison of four experience carriers.** All four scaffolds use Qwen3.7-Max (1M-token context, 64K max output, greedy, temperature 0), differing only in persistent representation (skills + memory for Claude Code/Codex; always-on editable memory blocks for Letta; compact Markdown experience files with title-only L0 index for GenericAgent).
- **Independent rubric judge with strong human agreement.** A Claude Opus 4.6-backed Claude Code scoring agent applies scene-level 100-point rubrics; on the 120 main-run deliverables it achieves ICC(A,1)=0.95 (95% CI [0.93, 0.97]) against a financial expert, mean abs. diff 1.6, max 5 points.
- **Mechanism diagnostics** on experience carriers (memory-only / skill-only / combination), feedback form (rubric vs reference answer), five capability dimensions, within-scene occurrence rank (early 1-3 vs late 4-6), and cross-scene interference.

## Methodology

**Benchmark scope.** 20 business scenes across six financial domains covering record intake, calculation, cross-file verification, risk assessment, client advice, and report writing. Each scene is built from three sources — an institution-provided scene description `Ds`, a reference professional procedure `Ps` validated in practice, and a candidate case pool `Cs` — consolidated by a domain expert into a five-part specification `Ss = (Os, Es, As, Ys, Ks)` (objective, required inputs, operations/checks, expected deliverable, compliance constraints). Six eligible cases are selected per scene, differing substantively in business situation, input files, analytical focus, judgment conditions, or conclusions (Table 2 gives examples such as healthy operations vs cyclical downturn vs divergence between earnings and cash flow).

**Scene-level rubrics.** Each scene has one 100-point rubric shared across its six tasks, derived from the professional procedure. It checks whether the agent uses inputs correctly, completes the required analysis, reaches supported conclusions, and satisfies business/compliance constraints — without requiring lexical match to a reference answer. Two domain experts construct and cross-review each rubric. The rubric also encodes compliance checks (fabricated/unsupported data, out-of-role decisions, prohibited assurances about credit approval/returns, etc.).

**Four metrics.** (1) Task quality = mean scene-specific rubric score (0-100). (2) Financial compliance = mean number of triggered compliance issues per task. (3) Self-evolution ability = paired score gain and compliance-issue reduction vs non-evolving control (Eqs. 2-3). (4) Agent-side cost = mean tokens (execution + reflection) in units of 10^4 per task, excluding the independent judge.

**Longitudinal protocol (Algorithm B.1).** For each scaffold `a`, the 120 tasks are independently shuffled into K=3 globally interleaved streams. Each task runs an execute -> score-and-feedback -> reflect-and-consolidate cycle. The evolving condition accumulates persistent state across the stream; the paired non-evolving condition resets state before every task but shares everything else. To analyze longitudinal accumulation, within each run tasks of a scene are ranked 1-6 by their position in the global stream; mean gains over ranks 1-3 ("Early") and 4-6 ("Late") are compared (Eqs. 5-6).

**Rubric isolation.** The evaluated scaffold never sees the complete rubric or the judge's item-level record; after each task it receives only a feedback summary of problems and reasons. This forces generalization of reusable lessons rather than reproduction of an earlier answer.

**Scaffolds.** Claude Code and Codex can distill experience into skills, project memory, and global memory (selective retrieval); Letta maintains editable core memory blocks injected into every model call; GenericAgent writes compact Markdown experience files with a prompt-resident title-only L0 index guiding selective loading.

## Results & Highlights

- **All four scaffolds beat their paired non-evolving controls across all three streams.** Mean scores rise by **+9.33 to +19.37 points** and mean compliance issues fall by **0.12 to 0.44 per task** (Table 3). Per-scaffold: Claude Code +17.89 / -0.44, Codex +19.37 / -0.44, Letta +17.82 / -0.39, GenericAgent +9.33 / -0.12.
- **Letta has the highest evolved score (91.65) and fewest compliance issues (0.09 per task); Codex has the largest self-evolution gain (+19.37).** GenericAgent has the lowest evolved score (83.34) and smallest gain (+9.33) but the lowest token cost (Table 3). No single scaffold dominates on absolute performance, gain, and cost simultaneously.
- **Late ranks (4-6) gain 6.10-8.70 points more than early ranks (1-3)** for every scaffold (Codex late +23.62 vs early +15.12; Letta late +22.17 vs early +13.47; Claude late +21.52 vs early +14.28; GenericAgent late +12.38 vs early +6.28). This late>early trend holds in all three independently shuffled streams, so it is not driven by a single task order.
- **Automated rubric judge agrees with financial expert at ICC(A,1)=0.95 (95% CI [0.93, 0.97])** on 120 deliverables, mean absolute difference 1.6 points and max 5 points on the 0-100 scale.
- **Skill-only evolution is the best Claude Code carrier: 93.71 score with 0.05 compliance issues per task**, beating memory-only (90.42 / 0.09), the unrestricted combination (89.47 / 0.11), and a fixed expert skill (86.67 / 0.13); the combined memory-skill setting does not improve over either alone (Table 5).
- **Rubric feedback beats reference-answer feedback by 3.95-7.93 score points and reduces compliance issues by 0.06-0.14 per task** across scaffolds, and produces 4-9 more experience activations for selective-retrieval scaffolds (Table 6).
- **Experience activation pays off: activated tasks score 8.31-9.06 points higher than non-activated tasks** for the three selective-retrieval scaffolds; even non-activated subsets score 3.97-12.31 points above their state-reset baselines (Table 4).
- **Report quality is the dimension that improves most** for every scaffold (6.85-18.17 percentage-point gains), while evidence use, analysis, and conclusions — being more case-specific — transfer less readily (Figure 4). Normalized compliance gains range from 1.93 to 6.74 points.
- **Scene isolation triggers 0.01-0.05 more compliance issues per task for every scaffold** (Table 7), consistent with cross-scene transfer of general practices (evidence checking, cautious wording, avoiding out-of-role decisions); score effects are small and mixed in direction (Claude -0.84, Letta -1.00, Codex +0.46, GenericAgent +1.63) on the five-scene sample.
- **Cost trade-offs are large.** Letta uses 32.56×10^4 execution + 17.87×10^4 reflection tokens/task (every core block enters every call); GenericAgent uses 11.57×10^4 + 10.21×10^4 (total < 1/3 of Claude Code's).

## Authors & Credibility

The work is a collaboration between Beihang University (first author Bo Deng, corresponding author Chongyang Tao) and the Qwen DianJin Team at Alibaba Cloud Computing, with the first author noting the work was done during an Alibaba internship. The Qwen DianJin affiliation gives the team direct access to the Qwen3.7-Max backbone and to institution-provided financial procedures and eligible cases — a credibility advantage for task authenticity that purely academic groups would struggle to match. The benchmark construction is documented with a full worked example (Appendix A: scene sources, six-task table, complete 32-criterion rubric inventory, professional/compliance RED-1..RED-7 checks, and review steps), and Appendix C provides a complete end-to-end trace (deliverable -> 88/100 judge score -> 6 feedback items -> memory and skill edits) showing exactly what is being measured. The use of an independent Claude Opus 4.6 judge distinct from the evaluated scaffold is a reasonable anti-overfitting choice, and the ICC(A,1)=0.95 human agreement statistic is reported with a 95% CI rather than as a bare number.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty.** Genuine. The combination of (a) recurring real-case professional workflows, (b) open-ended multi-file deliverables, (c) scene-level rubric + compliance multi-aspect scoring, and (d) globally interleaved paired streams is not jointly provided by any prior benchmark (Table 1 is a fair comparison). The separation of evolved-score from self-evolution-gain (Eq. pairs 2-3 and 5-6) is a meaningful methodological contribution because it answers a real confound — a strong backbone can mask weak evolution, and a weak backbone can mask strong evolution.

**Rigor — strong points.** Three independently shuffled streams (not one); paired controls sharing order/backbone/decoding; rubric isolation that prevents direct rubric leakage; explicit human validation of the judge (ICC with CI); a fully worked task in Appendix A with all 32 criteria and a complete task trace in Appendix C.

**Rigor — weaknesses worth flagging.**
- *Single backbone.* Only Qwen3.7-Max is evaluated. The authors themselves flag this in the conclusion, but it materially limits generalization: ordering and even the skill-vs-memory verdict (Table 5) could be backbone-dependent. A single ablation with a different backbone (even Qwen3.7 of a different size) would have hardened the central claims.
- *Judge–scaffold coupling.* The evaluated Claude Code scaffold is the same family as the independent Claude Opus 4.6 judge. The paper is careful to say the judge is "independent" and "backed by Claude Opus 4.6", but a same-vendor judge scoring a scaffold from the same vendor is a potential (if subtle) sympathy effect; a cross-family judge sweep (e.g., score with Qwen3.7-Max or GPT-class model) would address it.
- *Carrier comparison only on Claude Code.* The headline "skill-only > memory-only > combination" (Table 5) is established on exactly one scaffold. Generalizing this ordering to Letta/Codex/GenericAgent is not supported by the data shown.
- *Cross-scene diagnostic is small.* The scene-isolation analysis (Table 7) uses five scenes (30 tasks) and one shuffle. The authors are honest about the small sample, but the "small and mixed" conclusion is essentially underpowered.
- *Feedback leakage during evolution.* Because the scaffold receives rubric-style feedback after every task and then reflects, the "self-evolution" partly measures how well the scaffold exploits judge feedback rather than purely self-derived signal. This is partly addressed by the rubric-vs-reference comparison (Table 6), but the strongest possible baseline — a no-feedback "self-evolution from execution only" control — is not reported.
- *Coverage of "professional workflows" is narrow.* Six financial domains, 20 scenes. Law, audit, tax, clinical, and other procedurally heavy professions are not touched; the paper's framing as "professional workflows" generically is somewhat ahead of what six domains can support.
- *No public release information in the body.* The paper describes de-identification but does not state a release license or dataset URL in the main text; for a benchmark paper, release status is load-bearing and should be up front.
- *Compliance metric scale.* "0.09 issues per task" for Letta sounds impressive but the absolute count floor is small; whether 0.09 vs 0.11 is practically meaningful in a production compliance setting is not discussed.

**Reproducibility.** Moderate-to-good on methodology (algorithm, prompts, and rubric structure in appendices), weakened by the single backbone, the absence of a stated release, and dependence on institution-provided procedures `Ps` that are inherently not redistributable.

**Significance.** High for the agent-evaluation subfield: if released, FinEvo-Bench is a credible longitudinal complement to episodic agent benchmarks (SWE-bench, WebArena, OSWorld) and to memory-only self-evolution benchmarks (Evo-Memory, EvoMemBench, SEAGym). The "evolved score vs gain" separation is likely to be picked up by future benchmarks.

**Red flags.** None severe. The main concern is over-generalization from single-backbone, single-scaffold-carrier evidence, which the authors partly acknowledge.

## Strengths

- The evolved-score vs self-evolution-gain distinction is the right framing and is executed cleanly with paired controls.
- Globally interleaved streams (rather than contiguous scene blocks) is a genuinely better test of retrieval-under-interference than ordered-task benchmarks.
- Rubric feedback beating reference-answer feedback (Table 6) is an actionable finding for anyone building agent eval loops — it argues for criterion-based feedback over golden-answer matching.
- The fully worked Appendix A (scene sources, six-case design, 32-criterion rubric, RED checks) and the end-to-end Appendix C trace make the methodology unusually transparent for a benchmark paper.
- Compliance is reported alongside quality as a first-class metric, not buried — appropriate for a financial-domain benchmark where a confident wrong guarantee is worse than a hedged incomplete answer.

## Limitations

- Only one backbone (Qwen3.7-Max); only one judge (Claude Opus 4.6). Ordering and carrier conclusions may not transfer.
- Only non-parametric evolution is studied; fine-tuning/parameter-updating agents are out of scope.
- The carrier (memory vs skill vs combination) comparison is run only on Claude Code.
- The cross-scene diagnostic uses five scenes and one shuffle — underpowered for the "small and mixed" claim.
- 20 scenes in six domains is a narrow slice of "professional workflows"; legal, audit, tax, and clinical procedurally heavy domains are not represented.
- During evolution the scaffold receives judge feedback each task, so measured gains conflate genuine self-evolution with feedback exploitation; a no-feedback evolution baseline is missing.
- The paper does not state in the body whether the dataset/rubrics will be publicly released or under what license, which limits third-party reproducibility.
- The financial cases depend on institution-provided procedures `Ps` that cannot be redistributed; even if tasks are released, fully reconstructing the professional-process grounding may be impossible for external users.

## Relevance

高度相关。该工作直接落在"自演化智能体 + 纵向评测 + 金融/专业工作流"的交叉点，与 LLM 智能体评测、记忆/技能演化、多步推理与长程任务评测的研究兴趣紧密契合；其"演化增益 vs 绝对分数"分离方法和"全局交错流 + 配对状态重置"协议可作为后续通用智能体纵向基准的方法模板。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How do the scaffold rankings (and the skill-only > memory-only ordering) change when a different backbone (e.g., a smaller Qwen3.7 or a non-Qwen model) is used as the evaluated agent?
- What happens under a cross-family judge (e.g., score Claude Code with a Qwen judge, score Letta with a GPT-class judge) — is the ICC(A,1)=0.95 agreement preserved, and do the small compliance gaps between scaffolds survive?
- How much of the measured gain is genuine self-evolution versus exploitation of rubric-style judge feedback? A no-feedback, execution-only evolution control would isolate the two.
- Does the late-ranks (4-6) > early-ranks (1-3) gain trend hold when the stream is not just shuffled but adversarially ordered (e.g., scenes maximally spaced, or distractor scenes inserted between same-scene tasks)?
- Can the benchmark be extended to procedurally heavy non-financial domains (legal due diligence, clinical triage, tax review) to test whether "professional workflows" generalize, or whether financial procedures are unusually transfer-friendly?
- The combined memory+skill setting underperforms skill-only on Claude Code — is this a retrieval/coverage artifact (the paper suggests "later tasks often load only one carrier"), and could a router that always loads both close the gap?
- How do parametric-evolution methods (e.g., offline function-weight training, RL-on-trajectory) compare on the same streams, given the paper restricts itself to non-parametric carriers?
- Will the rubrics and de-identified tasks be publicly released, and if so, can external groups reproduce the ICC(A,1)=0.95 judge-expert agreement using the published prompts alone?
