Optimal Rates for Learning with Monotone Adversaries

Key Finding

Adding correctly-labeled examples under a monotone adversary breaks exchangeability and forces an unavoidable extra log(n/d) factor over the clean PAC rate — for every learner, not just ERM.

Why This Matters

Directly governs clean-label augmentation, dedup, and filtering pipelines — pinning the fundamental statistical cost of breaking exchangeability via correctly-labeled insertions.

Critical Edge

The log blowup is regime-dependent: with a bounded budget m=O(1), the clean Θ(d/n) rate survives — the worst case needs the adversary to inject Θ(n²/log²n) examples.

TL;DR abstract

Settles both open questions of Larsen–Pabbaraju–Shetty (ALT 2026): the minimax expected error under the monotone adversary model — where an adversary appends correctly-labeled examples after seeing the clean sample — is Θ((min{d,n}/n)·log(en/min{d,n})) for VC dimension d≥2, a log(n/d) gap above the clean PAC rate Θ(d/n). At d=1 a new improper learner reaches Θ(1/n), and the same logarithmic blowup holds with Littlestone dimension in place of d.

Core Contributions

  • Closes both LPS26 open questions with full proofs.
  • Lower bound holds against every randomized, possibly improper learner — not just named algorithms.
  • Reverses ERM's role: suboptimal in clean PAC, it becomes optimal here for d≥2.
  • Finite Littlestone dimension does not help — clean online-to-batch O(d_L/n) is unattainable.
  • All lower bounds derive from a single indistinguishability construction (projective planes → products → checker-only variant).

Methodology how it's proved

  • The model. Nature draws n i.i.d. clean examples; a deterministic adversary sees them and appends m correctly-labeled points; the n+m sample is uniformly shuffled. The catch: insertions depend on the clean sample, so the combined sample is not exchangeable — breaking standard one-inclusion / symmetrization arguments.
  • d=2 lower bound (heart of the paper). Builds a class H_q from a finite projective plane of prime order q. A coupon-collector argument (a checker is missing with prob ≥ 2/3) lets the adversary pad to a multiset whose version space is exactly {u_p, v_L}; because that multiset is identical under either target, Bayes keeps the posterior balanced (½,½) — even for order-sensitive learners — forcing risk ≥ log(en)/(30n). q chosen via Bertrand's postulate.
  • d≥3. ⌊d/2⌋ disjoint tagged copies; linearity of expectation sums per-block contributions → Ω((d/n)log(en/d)).
  • d=1 upper bound. Ben-David's f-representation + one-inclusion-graph leave-one-out; Lemma 3.13 shows ≤1 total LOO error even though the adversary may augment each LOO sample differently.

Strengths

  • Settles two stated open questions with full proofs — not partial progress.
  • Elegant unification of all lower bounds under one indistinguishability idea.
  • d=1 upper bound pinpoints where the leave-one-out argument survives adaptive insertions (Lemma 3.13).
  • Honest about limitations and LLM-assistance disclosure.

Limitations

  • Log blowup needs budget growing with n,d (m=O(1) is benign).
  • Only expected error; high-probability (ε,δ) sample complexity is open.
  • Worst-case over finite constructed classes — rates for halfspaces, etc. unknown.
  • No computational-efficiency analysis; binary classification only.
