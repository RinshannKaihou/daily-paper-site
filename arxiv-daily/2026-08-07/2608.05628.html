⚠ Critical Edge

The headline +9.5 / +8.5 pp delta rests on the authors' own reimplementation of CoEvoSkills — its source code is unavailable and no author-verification or reimplementation audit is reported. A weak reimplementation would silently inflate SkillHEX's lead. (SkillsBench is also the single benchmark, N=87, and evolution methods are scored pass@5 vs. effectively pass@1 for static baselines.)

How it works

Hypothesis-driven self-verification. Turns a sparse 0/1 task failure into falsifiable failure hypotheses, synthesizes executable public tests for each, and replays them over cached agent outputs — producing dense "semantic gradients" without spending extra executor attempts.

Dynamic evidence bank. An evolving matrix M[v,j] grows symmetrically: a new patch adds a row (replay all tests), a new test adds a column (replay over all history). Refuted hypotheses trigger targeted column pruning, so the signal stays dense and self-correcting.

Evidence-guided PUCT tree search. Skill revisions live on a persistent patch tree. Reflection gives an ordinal ranking → softmax prior P(v,u); max-backup Q(v) keeps strong parents unharmed by failed branches; a PUCT rule balances exploiting evidence-supported edits against exploring alternatives — escaping the greedy "exploitation trap".

Strengths

  • Principled reformulation of sparse-reward skill evolution as replay-based dense evidence + tree search; every design choice is ablated.
  • Consistent gains across two model families and most of 8 domains, including real wins over human skills in workflow-heavy areas (Math & OR +33–37 pp, Office +19–21 pp).
  • Transparent reporting: 3 seeds ± std, full prompt templates in appendix, explicit baseline caveats, honest domain-level losses.

Limitations

  • Baseline reimplementation of CoEvoSkills underpins the headline margin — not directly verifiable.
  • pass@5 vs pass@1 asymmetry structurally favors SkillHEX in the "beats human" comparison.
  • Single benchmark (87 tasks); no transfer test of evolved skills to unseen tasks — the on-demand framing sidesteps the library-reuse promise.
  • No sensitivity analysis on c_puct, β, K, and no stats on how often hypotheses are actually refuted.

Open questions

  • Does the +9.5 pp margin survive on a second benchmark (MLE-bench, ASB, OSWorld) under identical budget?
  • What fraction of hypotheses are refuted — does pruning measurably reduce misleading gradients?
  • Would a pass@1 evaluation of the final evolved skill still beat human skills?
  • Can evolved skills compose into a reusable library that helps future tasks?
