> **一句话总结：** 本文用交换代数刻画了"一致性/不变性"可靠性检测的可计算盲区——一个系统误读若与编辑的答案变换可交换则不可被检出，并据此提出等变-一致性分数 ECS 与 REND-EQUIV 基准：两个匹配的数值编辑可证完备覆盖仿射误差，循环重标注比交换编辑把标签误差检出率从 5.3% 提升到 44.0%（8 倍，与闭式预测吻合）。

> **TL;DR:** The paper proves that the undetectable errors of any invariance/equivariance test suite are exactly its joint centralizer (the errors commuting with every edit's answer-transform), giving a closed-form design rule: the two-edit suite {×c, +δ} is provably complete for affine reading errors, while no swap suite is complete for label permutations. On REND-EQUIV with Qwen2.5-VL-7B (N=900), the Equivariance-Consistency Score (ECS) flags 53.6% of invariance-blind errors (IBEM) vs REA's 0.0% at matched 14% FPR, raises combined AUROC from 0.788 to 0.906 (+0.118), and cyclic relabeling delivers its predicted 8× gain (5.3% → 44.0%) over swaps on a matched m=6 sample.

## 核心贡献

- **可检测性的代数刻画（Theorem 2）**：把"一个编辑套件漏检哪些系统误差"这一问题给出闭式答案——漏检集恰是各编辑答案变换 𝜑_g 的联合中心化子 ∩ C_𝜏(𝜑_g)，且随编辑增加单调收缩。这把传统元变异测试中 Set-Cover-hard 的完备性问题转化为代数闭式解。
- **仿射误差的完备二编辑套件（Theorem 4）**：乘法编辑 ×c（c≠1）与加法编辑 +δ（δ≠0）联合可检出所有非恒等仿射读数误差；单独任一编辑都不充分（分别漏掉纯缩放偏置和纯偏移偏置）。
- **标签置换的不完备性与设计规则（Proposition 5, Corollary 6）**：任何固定对的交换编辑套件都不完备（交换必在其自身中心化子内）；用 m 阶循环重标注替换交换，当 m≥5 时严格缩小漏检集，从 2(m−2)! 降到 m。
- **ECS 与 REND-EQUIV 基准**：把理论实例化为无标签、免训练的等变-一致性分数（ECS），并发布 REND-EQUIV——在同一数据上配对匹配的不变性集合与等变性集合，每个实例携带精确答案变换。
- **预测排序的跨模型验证**：编辑类型按代数结构（中心化子大小）排序，该排序在测量前固定，在三个模型上成立，并在一个移除选择循环的手工标注人群上复现；第二个独立的不变性族方法（扰动不确定性）共享同一盲区，证实盲区属于关系而非实现。

## Key Contributions

- **Algebraic characterization of detectability (Theorem 2).** The set of systematic errors that produce no detectable residual anywhere in an edit suite is exactly the joint centralizer ⋂ C_𝜏(𝜑_g) of the answer-transforms. This yields an antitone-in-suite, monotone-in-tolerance closed form, replacing the combinatorial (Set-Cover-hard) completeness analysis of classical metamorphic testing with algebra.
- **A matched completeness/incompleteness pair.** The two-edit suite {×c, +δ} (c≠1, δ≠0) detects every non-identity affine error (Theorem 4); neither edit alone suffices. Conversely, no swap suite is complete for label permutations (Proposition 5), and cyclic relabeling provably shrinks the undetectable set from 2(m−2)! to m whenever m≥5 (Corollary 6).
- **ECS and REND-EQUIV.** The Equivariance-Consistency Score instantiates the theory as a label-free, training-free detector that references the model's own base answer rather than the ground truth. REND-EQUIV pairs matched invariance (K=8 cosmetic re-renderings) and equivariance (|G|∈{1,2,3}, mean 2.61) sets over identical programmatically rendered data, with five operators each carrying an exact answer-transform.
- **Pre-registered ordering, confirmed across models.** The per-edit-type detection ordering was fixed before measurement and holds on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (N=900), Qwen2.5-VL-3B, and InternVL2-8B, including its predicted failures (identity and swap), and survives a 200-instance hand-labeled, REA-independent check that removes the one circularity in IBEM's selection.
- **The blind spot belongs to the relation, not any implementation.** A second invariance-family method (perturbation uncertainty, VL-Uncertainty-style) flags 0.0% of a measured IBEM sample, matching REA exactly — confirming Corollary 3 that combining invariance with equivariance cannot repair the invariance blind spot. The same theory also explains a reported ordering reversal versus classical classifier metamorphic testing.

## Methodology

The paper models a VLM figure-reader as producing a deterministic error map e on the exact answer a* (e=id is a perfect reader) and treats edits g as data transformations paired with a *known* answer-transform 𝜑_g satisfying a*(g(D), q) = 𝜑_g(a*(D, q)). Edits are realized by re-rendering g(D) in the same cosmetic style as D, isolating the data change from the style change.

The **Equivariance-Consistency Score** is defined per-edit as the residual 𝜌_g = d(ŷ_g, 𝜑_g(ŷ)) where ŷ is the model's answer on the base rendering and ŷ_g its answer on the edited figure; d is relative error for continuous answers and 0/1 disagreement for categorical/ordinal ones. ECS = (1/|G|) Σ 1[𝜌_g ≤ 𝜏_g], with tolerance 0 for categorical/ordinal and 5% relative for continuous answers. Crucially, ECS references the model's own base answer rather than a*, so it is label-free. An answer is flagged unreliable when ECS < t (theory-grounded t=0.5; cross-validation selects 0.70).

The theoretical core is **Lemma 1 (Detectability by commutation)**: 𝜌_g ≤ 𝜏 iff e ∈ C_𝜏(𝜑_g), vanishing exactly when e and 𝜑_g commute at a*. **Theorem 2** extends this to a suite: undetectable errors equal the joint centralizer. **Corollary 3** shows invariance (𝜑_g = id) contributes nothing structurally since C_𝜏(id) = E. **Theorem 4** proves affine completeness by direct algebra (β(1−c) and δ(α−1) must both vanish). **Proposition 5 / Corollary 6** use the cycle-type structure of permutation centralizers. **Theorem 7** extends the framework to stochastic error distributions, giving distribution-free monotonicity and additivity results.

REND-EQUIV extends a render-equivalence generator with five controlled-edit operators: scale series (×c), offset (+Δ), delete-max (argmax→runner-up), swap-labels (permutation), and axis-range zoom (identity/invariance control). Difficulty controls are validated against measured base-vs-edited accuracy. The headline quantity is **IBEM** (invariance-blind error mass): instances an invariance signal rates high-confidence (REA ≥ 0.75) that are nonetheless wrong — exactly the population where the theory says equivariance must earn its keep.

## Results & Highlights

- **Cyclic relabeling delivers its predicted 8× gain over swaps (Corollary 6, confirmed).** On a matched sample of 150 real m=6 instances (same base chart and data, both edits applied to each), cyclic relabeling flags 66/150 = **44.0%** of label errors vs swap's 8/150 = **5.3%**, matching the closed-form prediction 2(m−2)!/m = 48/6 = 8× almost exactly. Of 62 disagreeing instances, cyclic uniquely catches 60 and swap uniquely catches 2.
- **Pre-registered per-edit-type ordering holds on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (N=900, three seeds, Table 1).** Flagged rates on IBEM: scale 57/64, offset 48/64, delete-max 4/24, swap 2/31, identity(zoom) 13/97. The numeric-vs-non-numeric gap is significant after Bonferroni correction (p < 0.0002 on all three models); scale vs offset do not differ significantly (Fisher exact p=0.064, Bonferroni p=0.192), matching the prediction of no ordering between them.
- **ECS reaches the invariance-blind population that no invariance-family method can (Table 2, matched 14% FPR on correct instances).** On IBEM (n=97, 10.8% of N=900): ECS flags **53.6%** vs REA's **0.0%** (zero by construction), token logprob's 25.8%, self-consistency's 23.1%, semantic entropy's 25.6%, and perturbation uncertainty's 0.0% (confirming Corollary 3 on a method distinct from re-rendering). REA ∧ ECS also flags 53.6%, as Corollary 3 predicts the combination cannot improve on ECS in this population.
- **Combined AUROC gain is robust.** Combining ECS with REA raises AUROC from **0.788** to **0.906** (gain +0.118, 95% CI [0.089, 0.149], template-clustered, excluding zero) and improves RC-AUC from 0.077 to 0.071. High-confidence-region ECE improves from 0.140 [0.111, 0.169] to 0.055 [0.041, 0.080], non-overlapping intervals. The gain replicates on Qwen2.5-VL-3B and InternVL2-8B (pooled AUROC 0.894, 0.896; IBEM at matched FPR 58.2%, 52.5%).
- **Hand-labeled, REA-independent check removes the selection circularity.** A 200-instance wrong-answer sample hand-labeled by algebraic type (independent of any invariance signal) classifies errors as 151/200 (75.5%) affine and 49/200 (24.5%) permutation, with detection rates of **92.1%** for affine-labeled errors and **29.8%** for permutation-labeled errors — consistent with the coverage gap concentrating in the permutation channel exactly where Proposition 5 predicts.
- **Real-figure pilot on ChartQA (N=300 sampled, 218 in-scope, 94.8% extraction success).** With a ×2 scale edit on digitized real charts, GPT-4o shows ECS-inconsistency of 47.5% (accuracy 61.3%) and Claude Sonnet 4 shows 22.0% (accuracy 76.7%), demonstrating the signal transfers beyond synthetic figures.
- **Cost is low.** The primary model's full run (base render, K=8 re-renderings, up to 3 equivariance edits per instance, one seed) measured 0.25 GPU-hours for N=300; all three runs together measure under 3 GPU-hours. ECS is the cheapest non-trivial signal at |G|≤3 edits vs K=8 re-renderings/samples.

## Authors & Credibility

The authors are Rasul Khanbayov and Hasan Kurban of the College of Science and Engineering, Hamad Bin Khalifa University (Doha, Qatar), affiliated with Kurban Intelligence Lab. Reference [4] (their own prior work on semantics-preserving re-rendering as a label-free invariance signal) is the explicit point of departure that this paper's theory characterizes and extends — so the work is a direct, self-aware refinement of a real research line rather than an isolated result. Code, edit operators, the ECS implementation, the matched REND-EQUIV sets, and evaluation scripts are released at https://github.com/KurbanIntelligenceLab/rend-equiv with pinned versions and seeds, and every experimental number is reported as reproducible from real model inference with raw per-instance outputs included. The proofs are machine-checked (symbolically for the affine case, by exhaustive enumeration of S_m for m≤6 for the permutation case). The authors disclose AI assistance for prose drafting and explicitly note that human authors must re-derive the proofs independently before submission — a level of disclosure above the norm.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: High.** The central move — identifying the undetectable set with a joint centralizer and deriving a *closed-form* completeness/incompleteness result where classical metamorphic testing gives a Set-Cover-hard combinatorial problem — is genuinely new, and the explanation of the ordering reversal versus Xie et al. [32]'s classifier metamorphic-testing results (permutation detects most there, affine fewest; inverted here) is a strong, non-fitted validation that the characterization does real work.
- **Rigor: High, with unusually honest caveats.** The authors openly flag and address the circularity in the identity(zoom) IBEM row (selected by REA≥0.75, itself re-rendering agreement) via a 200-instance hand-labeled REA-independent check; they validate the "edited no harder than base" assumption rather than assuming it and report that two of five operators (delete-max, offset) are measurably easier by 6.9–12.5 and 3.8–8.4 points — a difficulty confound they do not hide. The predicted ordering is fixed before measurement and includes predicted failures, which is far stronger than a post-hoc aggregate gain.
- **Reproducibility: Strong.** Public models, public benchmark (ChartQA), released code/data with pinned seeds, per-seed breakdowns, template-clustered intervals (avoiding the row-level bootstrap's false independence), and an explicit statement that all numbers reproduce from real inference.
- **Significance: Moderate-to-high, scoped.** The aggregate AUROC gain is modest (+0.118) and ECS *loses* to REA on general-purpose AUROC (0.881 vs 0.788 leading) — the authors are explicit that ECS is not the best available confidence signal, only the best at reaching the invariance-blind population at the lowest input-varying cost. The contribution is the *decidability of suite design* (Theorem 2: "is this suite enough?" has an answer), not a new SOTA detector.
- **Red flags / specific weaknesses:**
  - The theory's completeness guarantee (Theorem 4) is only about the hypothesized class E_aff; real reading errors are stochastic and input-dependent, and 24.5% of hand-labeled errors are permutation-type not affine. The main REND-EQUIV release *still uses swap edits throughout*, so the cyclic-relabeling recommendation is unvalidated at dataset scale — Corollary 6 is tested only on a 150-instance matched sample, not folded into the released benchmark.
  - Two of five operators (delete-max, offset) are measurably easier than base across all three models; an account in which some residuals reflect easier edited figures (rather than caught errors) is not excluded for those two operators specifically, though the cross-edit-type ordering is robust to this.
  - 28/97 (28.9%) of IBEM instances produce no residual from any edit in the suite — by Theorem 2 these are joint-centralizer errors, i.e., the detector has built-in false negatives on exactly the population it targets, and an oracle edit-selection upper bound (71.1% vs 52.6%) shows substantial headroom that label-free selection cannot reach.
  - Natively limited to programmatically renderable figures; real figures require digitization (94.8% success in a single-edit pilot) and scanned figures are not addressed. ECS detects but does not repair.

## Strengths

- Decidable, closed-form theory that turns suite design from intuition into a calculation (Theorem 2 + Theorem 4).
- Pre-registered predictions confirmed across three models and a circularity-free hand-labeled population — including the predicted *failures* (identity and swap), which is stronger evidence than a single aggregate gain.
- Honest about every limitation: states the IBEM-selection circularity, validates rather than assumes difficulty controls, discloses AI assistance for prose, and explicitly warns that a high ECS is not a correctness guarantee.
- Explains an apparent contradiction in the literature (ordering reversal vs classifier metamorphic testing) as a natural consequence of the same theory, without having been fitted to either result.
- Cheap (under 3 GPU-hours total for all three runs), training-free, label-free, and releases a full reproducible artifact.

## Limitations

- Completeness (Theorem 4) is a guarantee about E_aff, not about any real model; real errors are stochastic and only 75.5% affine by hand-labeling. What transfers is the *ordering over edit types*, not the completeness guarantee itself.
- ECS requires the figure's underlying data, so it applies natively only to programmatically renderable figures; real figures need digitization, and scanned figures are out of scope.
- The detector has built-in false negatives: 28.9% of IBEM instances lie in the joint centralizer and are silently missed; it detects but does not repair.
- Difficulty confound on 2/5 operators (delete-max, offset measurably easier than base); not excluded for those two specifically.
- The cyclic-relabeling recommendation is validated on a 150-instance sample but not yet deployed in the released REND-EQUIV (still swap-based).
- ECS loses to REA on general-purpose correctness AUROC; its value is specifically reaching the invariance-blind subpopulation, not replacing REA.

## Relevance

This paper is directly relevant to reliable evaluation and hallucination detection for vision-language models on quantitative figures — it provides a theoretically grounded, label-free reliability signal (ECS) that complements the invariance-based methods already central to chart-QA robustness research, and its algebraic design rules (when an edit suite is provably complete vs incomplete) are actionable for anyone building chart-VLM evaluation suites or counterfactual benchmarks.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of the 28.9% joint-centralizer false-negative mass on IBEM could be recovered by deploying cyclic relabeling (instead of swaps) at dataset scale, and does the predicted 8× gain survive when folded into the full REND-EQUIV rather than a matched 150-instance sample?
- Can the commutation theory be extended to non-affine, non-permutation error classes (e.g., piecewise or logarithmic misreads), and what is the analogue of Theorem 4 for those richer classes?
- Does the equivariance residual 𝜌_g correlate with *error magnitude* in a way that would support a calibrated repair (not just detection) procedure?
- How does ECS behave under non-greedy / stochastic decoding, where the deterministic-per-instance assumption of Theorem 7 breaks down and the conjectured stochastic extension (expectation over independent draws) would need to hold?
- Could the centralizer analysis be ported to other modalities where controlled edits with known answer-transforms exist (e.g., table QA, geometry QA), and would the predicted ordering still hold?
