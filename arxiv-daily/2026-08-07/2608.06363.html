An Optimal Agnostic PAC Algorithm

posted

pac-learning statistical-learning-theory vc-dimension one-inclusion-graph sample-complexity
TL;DR. The first deterministic, improper, agnostic learner that matches the statistically optimal excess-risk bound L* + C·√(L*(d+log(1/δ))/n) + (d+log(1/δ))/n for every binary class of VC dimension d — matching the 1996 Devroye–Györfi–Lugosi lower bound across the realizable, intermediate, and fully agnostic regimes simultaneously. 一句话总结：构造首个在所有 L* 取值下都与 DGL 下界匹配的确定性 improper PAC 学习器，彻底解决不可知 PAC 学习的最优样本复杂度问题。
Key Finding
First deterministic improper learner achieving the optimal agnostic PAC excess-risk rate across every L* regime — closing a 30-year open problem.
Matches the Devroye–Györfi–Lugosi (1996) minimax lower bound up to a universal constant.
Key Metric
1was 15
Coefficient on L̂* in the leave-one-out bound (Theorem 2). Long's 1995 construction had 15 — that factor was the structural obstruction. Dropping it to exactly 1 is what closed the gap.
Why This Matters
Settles the optimal sample complexity of agnostic PAC learning — a core question since Valiant (1984). Unifies ERM / empirical-process methods with one-inclusion graphs and fills the missing intermediate rates. Must-read for the subfield.
!
Critical Edge — sharpest critique
Astronomical constants — the leading universal factor is 7×10⁸ — make the bound vacuous at any practical sample size; "optimal" means optimal in rate, not tight. Worse, the learner orients a graph with 2ⁿ vertices, so it is a statistical existence construction, not an implementable algorithm. A computationally efficient optimal learner remains open.

01 Methodology — three chained components

Pure statistical-learning-theory construction, no experiments. Class H ⊆ {-1,+1}𝒳 with VC dimension d ≥ 1; sample of n i.i.d. points.

1
Class-dependent edge isoperimetry (Lemma 2.1) A new Boolean-cube inequality that constructs weights wv,p satisfying wv,p+wv⊕p,p=1 and bounding Σp∈D wv,p ≤ ρF(v) + RD(F) for every coordinate set D. Unifies Long's distance-shell orientation with Dughmi–Kalayci–York's full-width Rademacher bound — and localizes it vertexwise.
2
Leave-one-out → randomized PAC (Theorem 3) Symmetrize the orientation by appending the query point and averaging over permutations. A suffix-averaged predictor p̂ over t=k..2k-1 is controlled by a reverse martingale (Freedman + Bernstein) and a forward martingale (comparator-dependent variance). Yields constant 17000.
3
Derandomization by thresholded validation (Lemma 3.3) Run ERM over the nested threshold class 𝒯p̂ on an independent validation block. Thresholds above/below the minimizer form two nested disjoint disagreement families → a local entropy cover of size 1+√q/u², with no extra log n. Final split: k=⌊n/3⌋ training + validation.

02 Results & Highlights

Theoretical risk bounds, no benchmarks.

  • Theorem 1. L(ĥ) ≤ L* + 7·10⁸(√(L*(d+log(1/δ))/n) + (d+log(1/δ))/n), prob ≥ 1−δ.
  • Eliminates the polylog gap from Hanneke–Larsen–Zhivotovskiy (FOCS 2024), which carried an extra log⁵(n/d) on the fast-rate term.
  • Optimal in three regimes at once: realizable (L*=0), fully agnostic, and the previously-unsolved intermediate rates.
11/16 GPT-5.6 runs solved Lemma 2.1 ~123 min avg per run

04 Strengths

  • Closes a genuinely open, 30-year-old problem with a clean, self-contained proof.
  • Lemma 2.1 is a reusable tool — likely to surface in multiclass, online, and transductive settings where edge-isoperimetric arguments recur.
  • Full constants, full proofs, no hidden lemmas; rigorous even by learning-theory standards.
  • Remark 2.3 gives an honest precedent map; Appendix A's AI disclosure (full prompt + 11/16 rate) is exemplary transparency.

05 Limitations

  • Vacuous constants. The 7·10⁸ factor makes the bound numerically useless at any practical n.
  • Computational intractability. Orienting the 2ⁿ-vertex cube is exponential.
  • Binary classification only. Multiclass, regression, asymmetric losses unaddressed.
  • Lower-bound tightness is assembled (footnote 2), not quoted from a single theorem.

06 Questions for Further Exploration

Q1Can the leading constant 7·10⁸ be substantially reduced? Is the Hall-capacity rounding a necessary source of slack?
Q2Is there a computationally efficient learner at the same rate — e.g. via approximate matching or simpler improper aggregation?
Q3Does Lemma 2.1 extend to the multiclass one-inclusion graph (Natarajan / graph dimension)?
Q4Could a direct localized-entropy analysis avoid the suffix split (the factor-3 in sample size, k=⌊n/3⌋)?
Q5What fails in the 5/16 GPT-5.6 runs, and does failure correlate with specific sub-claims of Lemma 2.1?
Verdict
A milestone for PAC learning theory: resolves the optimal agnostic sample complexity across all L* regimes. Conceptually novel (Lemma 2.1), fully rigorous, honestly disclosed — but asymptotic-only and non-implementable.
