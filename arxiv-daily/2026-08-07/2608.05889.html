The em-dash em-beds in Congress: a population-level rise, 2021–2025

A preregistered scan of 146,239 U.S. congressional press releases finds the unspaced prose em-dash flat for four years — then doubling in 2025. The signal clears one prespecified bar, fails the validation gate, and lands as an honest exploratory clue rather than confirmation.

Przemysław Czuma, MD, MBA · Polish Assoc. for AI in Medicine · OSF 10.17605/OSF.IO/U5NEY · congress-press (MIT) · N = 146,239 · 480 offices

What was ruled out

  • Hyphen→em-dash conversion: ASCII-hyphen density flat (2.75 → 2.68). Explains 0% of the rise (cap was 25%).
  • Placebo cut-offs inside the pre-LLM window (Jun 2021, Jan 2022, Jun 2022): all null, RR 0.94–1.06.
  • Pipeline seam at 2024/2025: same scraper ~99% on both sides; no step.
  • New offices: continuing offices alone rose 0.103 → 0.192; newcomers only added.
  • Sample composition: within-author, 75.6% of offices increased (sign-test p ≈ 1×10⁻¹⁶); removing top 5% prolific offices raised the ratio to 1.66.

The honest catch

The preregistered dateline-validation gate was formally breached: residual leak ran at a weighted mean of 4.96% (threshold: 2%), with a 2.1-pp year-to-year change (threshold: 1 pp). The author transparently downgrades the LLM-diffusion reading from confirmatory to exploratory. Post-hoc mitigations (positional sensitivity, leak trending against the effect) are reasonable but were added after the results were known.

A second confound sits on top: the entire jump coincides with the 119th Congress, a new administration, unified single-party control, and substantial staff turnover. Party symmetry (R/D fold-change 0.96) argues against a crude "incoming majority" story but cannot separate LLM diffusion from a new political-communication climate.
