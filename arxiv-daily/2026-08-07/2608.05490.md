> **一句话总结：** 本文为"无标注、仅用成功轨迹训练"的自主数据分析 Agent 逐操作失败归因方法建立了完整统计理论：证明一步创新残差可实现精确局部化（一个错误只产生一个标记），给出在任意依赖下控制轨迹 FDR 的 Benjamini–Yekutieli/e-BH 程序，并证明一个随校准样本量以 n^{-1/(d+2)} 衰减的可识别性下界——在 d=256 时数据量增加 100 倍该下界仅降低不到 2%，因此表征维度而非数据量才是约束瓶颈。

> **TL;DR:** This paper develops the statistical theory of one-class, innovation-residual auditing for step-level failure attribution in autonomous data-analysis agents, building on Yeh et al. (2026)'s latent-CDE formulation adopted in the context of the Who&When benchmark (Zhang et al., 2025), where the best reported step-level accuracy was only 14.2%. It proves that predictable one-step innovation scoring achieves exact localization (one genuine error produces exactly one flag, because a correctly executed operation after a corruption has a null residual), in contrast to reconstruction/rollout scoring that spreads one error across many downstream operations; it supplies Benjamini–Yekutieli and e-BH procedures that control trajectory-level FDR under arbitrary within-trajectory dependence requiring only exchangeability of calibration trajectories (not model correctness), plus a Γ-based sensitivity analysis for audit-selection bias. Its central negative result is a confusability floor: the smallest attributable mean-shift decays as n^{-1/(d+2)} (Corollary 7), so at embedding dimension d=256 a 100× increase in valid calibration trajectories reduces the floor by less than 2% and halving it would require ~10^78× more data — making the intrinsic dimension of the representation, not data volume, the binding constraint on what any such audit can ever report.

## 核心贡献

- **精确局部化定理（Theorem 1）**：在可预测一步预测模型下，被审计算法中被正确执行的操作（即使在某次早期错误之后）其广义残差条件于已实现输入状态恰好为零，因此"继承性污染"不会产生信号——**一个错误只产生一个标记**，从根本上解决了序列执行中"症状掩盖来源"的难题。这是与从起点重构（rollout）打分方法的本质区别，后者会把单个错误扩散到所有下游操作。
- **多视界残差刻画（Section 3.2, Proposition 1–2）**：证明了 k 步预测残差仅在 [t₀, t₀+k−1] 区间受污染，并对缓慢累积的扰动给出匹配视界下的检出优势——一步打分对渐进扰动"盲"，但匹配视界（如 k∈{1,4,16}）可恢复。
- **假发现控制（Section 5, Theorems 3–4）**：在任意轨迹内依赖下，用 Benjamini–Yekutieli（水平 q/H_Tmax）和 e-BH 程序控制轨迹级 FDR；仅需校准轨迹的可交换性，不要求拟合模型正确。给出基于倾向比 Γ 的选择敏感性分析（Theorem 6），可对每个标记报告"需要多大的选择偏倚才能抹去该标记"。
- **检出边界与稀疏替代（Section 4, Proposition 3–5）**：给出 χ² 检测边界——二次型得分在非中心化 o(√d) 时渐近功率可忽略；在有限维下，最大值/Bonferroni 通道对高度稀疏的扰动有优势（d=256 时单坐标扰动所需非中心化低 3.3 倍）。
- **可识别性与检出下限（Section 11, Theorems 8–9）**：证明"精确偏离集合"在无最小效应阈值时不可检验；引入最小可归因扰动的"混淆下限" ρ_floor ∝ [σ²(L∆)^d / (cN)]^{1/(d+2)}，随校准轨迹数以 n^{-1/(d+2)} 衰减——维度而非数据量是约束瓶颈。
- **跨轨迹复发与代价通道（Sections 6.1, 10）**：把执行代价建模为联合因子化，使代价成为第二审计通道；证明跨轨迹池化复发错误可降低噪声受限检出下限 √(mc) 倍（Proposition 13），但不能改善混淆下限（Corollary 5）。

## Key Contributions

- **Exact localization theorem (Theorem 1).** For a predictable one-step predictive model fitted to successful trajectories, a correctly executed operation that follows an earlier corruption has a null generalized residual conditional on its realized input state. Therefore inherited corruption produces no signal: one genuine error yields exactly one flag, sidestepping the symptom-masks-source problem intrinsic to sequential execution. This is the precise sense in which one-step innovation scoring dominates free-running reconstruction/rollout scoring, which smears one error across every downstream operation.
- **Multi-horizon residual channel (Section 3.2, Propositions 1–2).** The k-step predictive score is contaminated only on the window [t₀, t₀+k−1]; gradual perturbations injected evenly over k steps are invisible to one-step scoring (noncentrality ∥b∥²/(k²σ²)) but detectable at the matched horizon (∥b∥²/(kσ²)), with a dyadic horizon ladder H={1,2,4,…,2^J} minimax within a factor √2 of the matched horizon (Theorem 10).
- **Distribution-free trajectory-level error control (Section 5, Theorems 3–4, 6).** Benjamini–Yekutieli at level q/H_{Tmax} and e-BH control trajectory FDR under arbitrary within-trajectory dependence, requiring only exchangeability of calibration trajectories (A1), not correctness of the fitted model (A1*). A propensity-ratio sensitivity analysis (Theorem 6) lets each flag be accompanied by the selection-strength value Γ* that would remove it, without fitting a selection model.
- **Detection boundary and score choice (Section 4, Propositions 3–5).** The chi-square detection boundary shows quadratic scores have asymptotically negligible power when the noncentrality is o(√d); a finite-dimensional table quantifies when the maximum/Bonferroni channel wins (3.3× less noncentrality at d=256 for a 1-sparse perturbation, but only for <3% of coordinates).
- **Identifiability and a confusability floor (Section 11, Theorems 8–9, Corollaries 6–9).** The exact corrupted set is non-testable without a minimum-effect threshold (Theorem 8), and the smallest attributable mean shift has a floor ρ_floor ∝ [σ²(L∆)^d/(cN)]^{1/(d+2)} that decays as n^{-1/(d+2)} in the number of valid calibration trajectories. This floor, not estimation error, is the binding constraint on what any audit can attribute.
- **Bridge instantiation, cost channel, and off-support theory (Parts II, Section 7).** A Gaussian bridge SDE with a re-estimated predictable terminal anchor, a free heteroscedastic covariance with a variance floor σ²_min, and joint cost–embedding factorization that makes execution cost a second audit channel. Off-support tail conservatism (Theorem 7) extends the on-support guarantees to trajectories that leave the training support, with an explicit variance-inflation rate γ.

## Methodology

The paper is a **pure theory paper** — it contains no empirical experiments, no benchmarks, and no learned systems. Its method is mathematical: defining a formal model and proving theorems (with full proofs in Appendix A) under explicitly enumerated assumptions (A0–A5).

**Setting (Section 2).** A trajectory is a sequence of operations o₁,…,o_T issued in response to a query embedding x₀∈ℝ^d. Each operation has an embedding x_t=φ(o_t) and an execution cost c_t>0, run under a budget C_max. Two filtrations are defined: F_t (everything observed through t) and G_t=F_{t−1}∨σ(c_t) (what is known just before x_t realizes). The model M is a predictable family of conditional laws Q_t(x_t|G_t) plus a cost law P_t(c_t|F_{t−1}). Generalized residuals are u_t=R_t(x_t)∈[0,1]^d (Rosenblatt transforms); scores are a_t=S(u_t). The Gaussian bridge instance uses S(u)=∥Φ⁻¹(u)∥²₂, recovering a_t=∥ε_t∥²₂ with ε_t=Σ_t^{−1/2}(x_t−μ_t).

**Three parts.** Part I develops localization (Theorem 1), multi-horizon scoring (Prop. 1–2), score/detection-boundary theory (Props 3–5), and multiplicity control (Thms 3–4) for arbitrary predictable one-step families. Part II specializes to a Gaussian bridge SDE (Euler-discretized Doob h-transform of Brownian motion pinned at a terminal anchor), addressing time/cost normalization and predictability (Section 6.1), terminal anchoring with multimodal endpoints (Section 8), and off-support variance inflation (Theorem 7). Part III treats recurrence across trajectories (Section 10, Prop. 13) and identifiability/detection limits (Section 11, Thms 8–9).

**Key proof techniques:** Rosenblatt-transform pivotality under predictable conditioning; Grönwall-type two-sided comparison for the propagation envelope (Theorem 2); DKW with Massart's constant for simultaneous FDP bounds (Prop. 6); sequential maximal coupling for TV-robustness (Theorem 5); and a nonparametric confusability construction over Lipschitz drift classes for the floor (Theorem 9).

## Results & Highlights

The paper reports **no empirical experimental results** — all quantitative claims are theoretical bounds, rates, or finite-dimensional exact calculations. The headline numbers:

- **Prior benchmark context (cited):** On the Who&When step-level failure-attribution benchmark (Zhang et al., 2025), the best reported step-level accuracy was **14.2%**; this paper's framework operates under the same one-class formulation introduced by Yeh et al. (2026) but supplies the missing statistical theory.
- **Confusability floor vs. calibration data (Section 11.2, table).** The smallest attributable mean-shift floor ρ_floor decays as n^{-1/(d+2)}. At embedding dimension **d=256, a 100-fold increase in valid calibration trajectories reduces the floor by less than 2%** (from 0.982 to 0.965 of the per-step scale). The multiple of data required to halve the floor is **2^{d+2}**: d=8 → 10³, d=32 → 1.7×10¹⁰, d=128 → 10³⁹, d=256 → **10⁷⁸**. Reducing d from 256 to 8 turns an effectively-fixed floor into one decaying as n^{-1/5} (Corollary 8).
- **Score-choice trade-offs at finite dimension (Section 4.2, table, α=0.01, 80% power).** At d=256, the chi-square channel requires noncentrality λ*=80.0 (dense) while the maximum channel requires λ*_{k=1}=24.5 for a 1-sparse perturbation (3.3× less) and λ*_{k=3}=45.5 (1.8× less), but **more** for k=10 (94.0). The maximum channel is more powerful only for k*<7 coordinates (<3% of dimensions) at d=256.
- **FDR adjustment cost (Section 5.3–5.4).** With deterministic T_max=40, the harmonic number H_{Tmax}≈4.3, so Benjamini–Yekutieli operates at level q/H_{Tmax}; the e-BH procedure is presented specifically to avoid this harmonic factor, though without uniformly greater power.
- **Simultaneous FDP bound (Proposition 6).** At δ=0.1 the additive slack in the realized-FDP bound is 3.9 flags at T=10, 5.5 at T=20, 8.7 at T=50 — i.e., the per-report high-probability guarantee is **uninformative at typical trajectory lengths**; only the long-run FDR guarantee is usable.
- **Adversarial late-shift efficiency (Proposition 15).** With constant σ_eff and s̄=0.9, a shift at s₀=0.75 has a damage-to-detectability ratio **4× that of the same shift at the start**, and s₀=0.89 is **9× as efficient**; the single hardest-to-detect high-impact shift occurs near the end (Corollary 10).
- **Accumulation gain (Proposition 2).** A corruption b spread evenly over k steps has one-step noncentrality ∥b∥²/(k²σ²) versus matched-horizon ∥b∥²/(kσ²); the dyadic horizon ladder recovers ≥(1/√2)·λ^{(k)}_{(k)} of the matched-horizon signal (Theorem 10).
- **Recurrence gain (Proposition 13).** Pooling m_c occurrences of a type-c defect in distinct trajectories lowers the noise-limited detection boundary by a factor √m_c — but Corollary 5 shows this does **not** reduce the confusability floor, which depends on n (valid trajectories), never on m_c.

## Authors & Credibility

Both authors are at Johns Hopkins University. Mark Dredze is a well-established researcher with broad visibility in NLP and ML. The paper is a careful, proof-heavy theoretical treatment; every theorem has a corresponding proof in Appendix A (28 pages total), and assumptions (A0)–(A5) are explicitly enumerated and invoked only where used, which is a sign of statistical rigor. The paper is forthright about its lack of empirical validation: Section 9 proposes specific diagnostics and four simulation studies that "can assess whether the fitted implementation exhibits the predicted behavior," but does not run them. The work positions itself as the statistical-theory complement to Yeh et al. (2026)'s formulation, which it credits clearly, rather than as a competing empirical system.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** Genuine. The exact-localization result (Theorem 1) — that predictable one-step residuals are null after a corruption, so one error produces one flag — is a clean, non-obvious insight that precisely separates innovation scoring from the reconstruction/rollout scoring used in prior work. The confusability floor (Theorem 9) and the non-testability of the exact-departure estimand (Theorem 8) are substantive negative results that any future one-class attribution work will have to engage with.
- **Rigor.** High. Full proofs; explicit assumption ledger; honest treatment of where the guarantees stop (e.g., A2a fails when the agent's mechanism changes after an error — a structural limitation the authors call out rather than hide).
- **Reproducibility.** Weak by ML-benchmark standards and appropriately so: this is theory. However, the finite-dimensional detection-boundary table (Section 4.2) and the floor table (Section 11.2) are computed from closed-form expressions and could be regenerated; the paper does not provide code. The four proposed simulation studies in Section 9 are described precisely enough to implement but are left as recommendations.
- **Significance.** The dimension-is-the-binding-constraint message (d=256, 100× data → <2% floor reduction) is a strong, actionable claim: practitioners are told that reducing intrinsic embedding dimension beats collecting more calibration data, and Corollary 8 quantifies the payoff. The selection-sensitivity Γ* (Theorem 6) is a genuinely useful deliverable for deployment because it is computable from observed p-values alone.
- **Red flags and weak points.**
  - *No empirical validation of its own predictions.* Section 9's four simulations, especially the bimodal-endpoint test of Proposition 10's prediction (that ML training favors inflated terminal variance over elevated terminal false-positives), are the natural validation and are not performed. The paper's applicability to real agent trajectories rests entirely on the assumptions holding.
  - *The per-report guarantee is weak at realistic lengths.* Proposition 6's additive slack (3.9 flags at T=10) makes the simultaneous-FDP bound unusable for individual reports; the paper honestly says so but this substantially limits the operational value, since a reviewer auditing a single analysis receives only a point estimate plus a long-run FDR promise.
  - *Anchor correction is a nuisance that the paper relegates to a sensitivity analysis.* Proposition 8 shows anchor error induces nonzero means at benign steps that grow as √λ_t and are unbounded in corruption magnitude — a real threat to validity that bounded-influence estimators only partially address and that sacrifices the exact χ²_d null.
  - *Assumption A5 (uninformative selection) is load-bearing and arguably implausible.* Trajectories reach the auditor precisely because a prior reviewer found them atypical — the opposite of uninformative selection. The Γ-sensitivity analysis mitigates this but requires the practitioner to choose and justify Γ.
  - *Off-support theory depends on explicit Lipschitz/spectral normalization choices (γ, σ_min) that the paper says "should be selected with reference to a minimum perturbation of scientific interest" but gives no procedure for selecting.* Corollary 3 quantifies how these choices degrade sensitivity, but the actual deployment recipe is underspecified.
  - *The recurrence gain (Prop. 13) is misleadingly labeled as progress unless read with Corollary 5.* Pooling across trajectories helps only the noise-limited boundary, not the identifiability floor; a careless reader could over-interpret the √m_c factor.

## Strengths

- Mathematically precise, with a clean separation between what is assumed and what is proved; assumptions are numbered and invoked locally.
- The exact-localization theorem is an elegant result that gives a principled answer to "why one-step and not rollout?" — a question prior empirical work left implicit.
- The confusability floor and non-testability results are intellectually honest negative results that bound the whole research program and motivate dimension reduction.
- The selection-sensitivity Γ* is a practical, computable robustness report that requires no fitted selection model — a genuinely deployable idea.
- The dyadic horizon ladder (Theorem 10) gives a concrete, near-minimax recipe for the gradual-perturbation regime at O(log T) cost.

## Limitations

- **Pure theory; no experiments.** All predictions (localization width, terminal-variance inflation under multimodality, off-support tail behavior, the n^{-1/(d+2)} rate) are unvalidated on real agent trajectories. The intrinsic dimension of agent trajectory embeddings — the quantity the floor rate hinges on — "must be evaluated empirically" but is not.
- **Assumption fragility.** A2a (benign steps follow M even after a corruption) fails when the agent's mechanism changes after an error — a realistic case for LLM agents that may adopt a different behavioral regime after a mistake. The paper recommends changepoint/E-detector methods (Shin et al. 2024) but does not develop them.
- **Per-report FDP guarantees are weak at trajectory lengths in the tens.** Only long-run FDR is usable for a single audited analysis.
- **Anchor and off-support nuisance parameters** (ϱ, γ, σ_min, κ) are not given data-driven selection procedures; sensitivity to these choices is quantified but not resolved.
- **No causal interpretation.** The flagged set identifies mechanism violations, not an initiating cause or causal chain among flagged operations (Section 12).
- **Gaussian-bridge closed forms** (Corollaries 1–3, the detection-boundary table) depend on the Gaussian instance and would require re-derivation for mixture/flow multimodal predictives, which the paper recommends but does not execute.

## Relevance

This paper is directly relevant to research on **trustworthy and auditable LLM/agent systems** — specifically the problem of attributing a failure in an end-to-end autonomous data-analysis agent to the specific operation (cohort filter, join, model fit) that caused it, without requiring labeled failure examples. Its statistical-theory lens (conformal calibration, FDR control, identifiability limits) complements the empirical agent-evaluation literature and is valuable for anyone building safety/oversight tooling for autonomous agents, even though it offers no ready-to-run code.

## Questions for Further Exploration

1. The confusability floor rate n^{-1/(d+2)} is derived for Lipschitz drift classes; what is the rate for the actual neural-CDE dynamics used in Yeh et al. (2026), and does the intrinsic dimension of real agent-trajectory embeddings come anywhere near the d=8 favorable regime, or is d effectively ≫32 in practice?
2. Can the four simulation diagnostics in Section 9 be run on an existing implementation (e.g., the Yeh et al. CDE) to test the Section 8 prediction that ML training favors inflated terminal variance over elevated terminal false-positive rates — and which alternative do real systems actually select?
3. How would the guarantees degrade under the realistic "mechanism-changes-after-error" regime that A2a excludes? Is an E-detector (Shin, Ramdas, Rinaldo 2024) layered on top of innovation residuals a practically tighter audit than either alone?
4. The selection-sensitivity Γ* is computed per flag; can a deployed audit surface Γ* alongside each flagged operation in a way reviewers actually use, and does reporting Γ* change reviewer behavior compared to reporting only raw p-values?
5. The cost-as-second-audit-channel idea (Section 6.1) is elegant but undeveloped empirically — does execution-cost residual actually catch real cohort-restriction and join-key failures on a benchmark like Who&When, and how does its power compare to the embedding-score channel?
