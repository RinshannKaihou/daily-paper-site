> **一句话总结：** 将 AIVAT 方差缩减技术与置信序列（Confidence Sequences）结合为 AV-AIVAT，使不完美信息博弈中的智能体评测能在证据充分时即时停止；在 71,439 手 HUNL 数据上，AIVAT 带来中位数 54× 的方差下降，并在 ±1 BB 精度下使渐近置信序列所需手数减少中位数 74×，同时通过经验 Bernstein 序列提供可被第三方复核的有限样本证书。

> **TL;DR:** AV-AIVAT fuses AIVAT (a conditional mean-zero variance-reduction control variate for imperfect-information games) with two confidence sequences — an asymptotic AsympCS and an exact Empirical-Bernstein EB-CS — under a *predictable* online value function (learned only from past hands) so that an evaluation can stop the moment its evidence suffices while keeping the statistical guarantee. On a 71,439-hand HUNL corpus of 15 PokerSkill/LLM-agent configurations, AIVAT cuts variance by a median 54× and the AsympCS reaches ±1 BB with a median 74× fewer hands than raw outcomes; the EB-CS gives an exact but width-floored 1.37× ratio (with an analytic structural bound |Y|≤117 derived for Leduc).

## 核心贡献

- **可预测的 AIVAT 接口（AV-AIVAT）**：把方差缩减工具 AIVAT 与置信序列嫁接，并允许价值函数 $v_t$ 在评测过程中用过去手牌在线更新（仅需 $F_{t-1}$-可测、不可偷看当前手），从而保留条件零均值性质，把 AIVAT 最尴尬的"开评前就要有好价值函数"变成"暖启动 + 边评边学"。
- **两条置信序列分工明确**：渐近 AsympCS 作为高效主区间（跟踪已实现方差），有界 EB-CS 作为有限样本精确证书（需要独立于样本的几乎必然界），论文不把两者混为一谈，而是分别给出有效性命题（Proposition 1 与 2）。
- **停止时间的"三段式"刻画**：证明 bet cap 诱导的确定性宽度下界 $w_t^{EB} \ge 4B\log(2/\alpha)/t$（Lemma 1），并给出 Theorem 1 的三段式设计指南（range-limited / neutral / variance-limited），解释了为何 HUNL 上 54× 的方差下降在 EB-CS 下只换来 1.37× 的停止加速。
- **方差-后悔界（Theorem 2）**：证明只要在线价值学习器的方差后悔 $R_t=o(t)$，其达到目标精度的渐近停止时间与 oracle 价值函数相比无延迟；Corollary 1 给出 $\|v_t-v^\star\|_\infty \le c t^{-\beta}$ 的多项式学习率即满足此条件。
- **可审计的发布协议**：只需发布修正后的前缀 $Y_{1:\hat t}$、停止规则与索引、首检/截断元数据、声明的界 $B_Y$ 及其来源、锁定的 CS 设置，第三方即可在原停止时刻重算区间核验结论，无需重跑任何对局。
- **结构性界与 Leduc 精确证书**：对 Leduc hold'em 通过完整合法树的伸缩等式（telescoping）给出策略无关、不依赖样本的 $|Y| \le 117$（来自 $B_X=13$、$|v|\le13$、最多 4 个未修正对手转移），使 EB-CS 在该游戏中获得真正的有限样本有效性。

## Key Contributions

- **A predictable AIVAT interface** that exposes the conditional action kernel and fixes correction enablement before the action is observed. Past-only value updates preserve the conditional mean-zero correction law, so the value model can keep learning *during* the evaluation rather than being frozen before it (Proposition 1).
- **A clean separation of two confidence sequences**: the AsympCS is the efficient variance-adaptive primary screen (asymptotic validity under a conditional Lindeberg condition, Proposition 2); the predictable plug-in Empirical-Bernstein CS (EB-CS) is the exact finite-sample certificate once a data-independent almost-sure bound on the corrected payoff is supplied.
- **A stopping-time characterization for the bet-capped EB-CS** (Lemma 1 + Theorem 1): a deterministic width floor $w_t^{EB} \ge 4B\log(2/\alpha)/t$, an oracle-bet high-probability upper bound, and a common-support expected lower bound together yield a three-regime design proxy $\max\{B,\sigma^2/\varepsilon\}$ that explains why a 54× variance cut becomes only 1.37× stopping speedup under the exact interval.
- **A one-sided variance-regret bound** (Theorem 2 + Corollary 1): an online value learner with sublinear variance regret $R_t=o(t)$ reaches the target precision at the oracle's own asymptotic horizon, so learning during the run is asymptotically free.
- **An auditable release protocol** (Section 8): publishing the corrected prefix plus stop/bound/CS metadata is sufficient for a third party to recompute both intervals at the claimant's stopping time — the A2 audit shows this recheck overturns essentially all (1,226/1,227) of the spurious "first-favorable-look" claims a naive fixed-CI monitor would manufacture.
- **A structural corrected-payoff bound for Leduc** ($|Y| \le 117$, derived by telescoping over the complete legal tree), which converts the EB-CS from a descriptive replay into an exact certificate and shows the boundary runs within ~1% of its deterministic floor.

## Methodology

The protocol (Algorithm 1) interleaves three components with distinct roles. At hand $t$: (1) the evaluator fixes the value function $v_t$ using only $F_{t-1}$ (past hands plus any pre-registered training/tuning randomness); (2) before each correction node $h$, it records the known conditional action kernel $p_{t,h}$ and chooses the enablement indicator $S_{t,h}$ *without* seeing the outgoing action; (3) it plays the hand, observes raw payoff $X_t$, computes the corrected payoff $Y_t = X_t + C_t$ where $C_t = \sum_h S_{t,h} I_{t,h} \sum_a p_{t,h}(a)[v_t(h\cdot a) - v_t(h\cdot A_{t,h})]$; (4) updates both confidence sequences; (5) may refit $v_{t+1}$ on data through hand $t$.

Two confidence sequences run in parallel. The **EB-CS** rescales $Z_t = (Y_t + B_Y)/(2B_Y) \in [0,1]$, uses a predictable regularized plug-in mean $\tilde m_{t-1}$ and predictable bets $\lambda_t \in [0,1/2]$, and is exactly time-uniform at level $1-\alpha$ when $B_Y$ is independently justified (Proposition 1). The **AsympCS** is centered at the running mean with half-width $\hat\sigma_t \sqrt{2(t\rho^2+1)/t^2\rho^2 \cdot \log((t\rho^2+1)/\alpha)}$, and is asymptotically valid under martingale-difference Lindeberg and nondegenerate limiting averaged conditional variance conditions (Proposition 2). The AsympCS tuning parameter $\rho$ is selected once on a separate replication-level calibration split and frozen before evaluation to rule out post hoc retuning.

The validity argument rests on a tower-property conditioning: fixing $v_t$ via $F_{t-1}$-measurability and then conditioning on the pre-action field $G_{t,h}^-$ makes each correction summand mean-zero because the realized action has the known law $p_{t,h}$. Opponent nodes with unknown strategy are excluded from $\mathcal H_c$, which is why opponent decisions cannot corrupt the correction. The stopping-time theory (Theorem 1) then shows the bet cap $\lambda_t \le 1/2$ forces a variance-independent floor $4B\log(2/\alpha)/t$, producing three regimes via the clipped proxy $\mathrm{clip}(\sigma_X^2/(B\varepsilon), 1, \sigma_X^2/\sigma_Y^2)$: range-limited, neutral, and variance-limited.

The HUNL evidence (P0) replays 71,439 paired hands from 15 PokerSkill/LLM configurations against GTO Wizard, each run producing 200 reshufflings per target $\varepsilon \in \{2,1,0.5,0.3,0.2\}$ BB. Leduc experiments (E1, E2) use exact full-tree evaluation with CFR+-2000 vs CFR+-20 ($\mu = -0.0047697$ chips/hand) as ground truth. The A2 audit resamples a centered zero-effect pool of 71,439 values into 2,000 simulated 5,000-hand leaderboard entries.

## Results & Highlights

- **Variance reduction (HUNL, P0)**: Across the 15 runs, AIVAT reduces variance by a median **54.4× (range 24.2–86.0×)**; the median raw-to-AIVAT AsympCS stopping-time ratio at ±1 BB is **74.17× (range 54.53–97.62×)**, whereas the corresponding EB-CS ratio is only **1.365× (range 1.235–1.556×, n=14)** — the bet-cap width floor absorbs most of the variance gain when exactness is required.
- **Tight-target behavior (HUNL, ±0.5 BB)**: All 15 raw AsympCS streams are censored at their 4,028–5,000-hand horizons, while the AIVAT AsympCS median stopping time is **56.5 hands (range 42.5–71)** — raw evaluation literally cannot reach the precision AIVAT reaches in under 60 hands.
- **Leduc ground truth (E1, CFR+-2000 vs CFR+-20)**: Raw std 3.96 vs AIVAT std 1.39 chips (variance ratio **8.08×**); AsympCS stopping ratios **5.29× at ±0.5 chips and 7.91× at ±0.2 chips**; at ±0.1 every raw stream is censored while AIVAT stops at a median **1,789 hands**. With the analytic structural bound $B_Y=117$, the EB-CS excludes the exact mean in **0/400 AIVAT replications** (vs 2/400 raw), and the AIVAT half-widths run at empirical-to-floor ratios of **1.0019 (t=1000) and 1.0074 (t=4000)** — i.e., within ~1% of the deterministic floor.
- **Continuous-monitoring audit (A2, constructed H₀ pool)**: A naive fixed-sample 95% CI monitored from $t=30$ and stopped at the first favorable look yields **1,227/2,000 = 61.35% false-positive claims** (median claimed stop 59 hands); recomputing EB-CS at that selected stop contains zero in **1,227/1,227** cases and AsympCS in **1,226/1,227 (99.92%)**, screening the spurious claims out. Standalone AsympCS false-positive rate by 5,000 hands is **10.4%** (above the nominal 5%).
- **Genuine-effect detection (A2)**: When AsympCS runs as its own sequential monitor, it detects an injected 0.1 BB/hand effect in **81.2%** of entries (median 960.5 hands) and a 0.2 BB effect in **100%** (median 173 hands) — rejecting an unsupported early claim is not the same as denying the effect.
- **Online value learning (E2, Leduc)**: A Laplace-smoothed opponent-frequency model refit at doubling epochs recovers **78.6% and 77.1%** of the frozen-to-oracle variance gap in two mismatched profiles; at ±0.1 chips, predictable stops at medians 2,240.5 and 2,608 hands vs frozen 3,039/4,337.5 and oracle 1,744/1,425.5.
- **Cost context**: PokerSkill reports **$0.07–$0.30 inference per HUNL hand**; Libratus played 120,000 hands over 20 days for a $200,000 prize pool. A 74× reduction in hands-to-verdict turns a budget question into a routine one.
- **Implementation regression test**: With an oracle value function and corrections at all nodes in controlled Leduc, AIVAT telescopes to $Y_t \equiv \mu$ hand-by-hand with $\max_t |Y_t - \mu| = 9.1 \times 10^{-16}$ over 200 hands.

## Authors & Credibility

All three authors (Boning Li, Yu Chen, Longbo Huang) are at IIIS, Tsinghua University; Longbo Huang is the corresponding author. The same first author co-authored the PokerSkill paper that supplies the HUNL corpus (ref [38]) and several recent CFR/abstraction papers ([32]–[37]), so the team has deep domain command of both the evaluation platform and the underlying game-solving machinery. The theoretical machinery (predictable plug-in EB-CS from Grünwald/Waudby-Smith-Ramdas; AsympCS from Waudby-Smith et al.) is cited and applied correctly rather than reinvented, and the proofs (Appendices A–C) discharge the cited theorems' hypotheses explicitly. The paper is unusually disciplined about separating what is exact from what is asymptotic, and about labeling every observed-bound EB-CS run as "descriptive" rather than confirmatory.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The core idea — gluing AIVAT (a 2018 variance-reduction control variate) onto anytime-valid confidence sequences — is a genuine interface contribution rather than a new estimator or a new CS. The truly novel pieces are (a) the *predictable* value-function condition that legalizes online refitting, and (b) the bet-cap width-floor analysis (Lemma 1, Theorem 1) that shows exactly *why* exactness is expensive. The variance-regret theorem (Theorem 2) is conceptually clean but is a one-sided asymptotic bound against a deterministic benchmark, not against an oracle's random first crossing — the paper is honest that a matching lower bound would need additional control (Appendix C).
- **Rigor**: The theory is carefully stated with separate guarantees for the two CSs and an explicit assumption checklist (Table 6). However, the **headline 74× number is an AsympCS result, and the AsympCS is asymptotic**: its observed HUNL false-positive rate is **7.11% averaged over 30 conditions and 12.43% AIVAT-only** — both above the nominal 5%. The A2 standalone rate of 10.4% confirms the finite-sample miscalibration. The paper reports these numbers transparently but the title's "Certified" framing is arguably too strong for the HUNL setting, where only the *descriptive* EB-CS runs exist and the exact certificate is confined to Leduc.
- **The HUNL EB-CS is descriptive, not exact, and the paper says so** (Appendix D.2): the $B=200$ BB bound is structural on the *raw* stream but is an observed-maximum-round-upward choice on the *corrected* stream, where no analytic ceiling exists because "an AIVAT correction accumulates over transitions and scales with the value estimates rather than with the stack." So the central practical claim (74× cheaper HUNL evaluation) rests on the asymptotic interval, while the exact certificate — the thing that would actually justify "Certified" — is demonstrated only on the toy Leduc game.
- **Missing ablations / generalizability gaps**: The 15 HUNL configurations "need not correspond to 15 distinct base models" and all come from one platform (PokerSkill vs GTO Wizard); cross-platform replication is explicitly deferred to future work (Appendix G). The A2 audit resamples *individual centered hands with replacement*, which "removes serial dependence and any run-level clustering," so the 61% false-positive figure is a clean illustration of peeking, not an estimate of any deployed leaderboard's error rate (the paper concedes this in E.4). Multiplayer is unaddressed. No comparison to alternative sequential methods (e.g., betting-based CS with adaptive bets, or sub-sampling-based methods) on the same streams is provided — Future Work item 1 flags this.
- **Reproducibility**: Strong by current standards. Random seeds, sample sizes, the locked AsympCS settings ($\rho^2 = 2\log(1/\alpha)/t_{\mathrm{opt}}$, $t_{\mathrm{opt}}=1000$, 30-hand burn-in), and the per-experiment bound provenance are all recorded. E1/E2 followed a frozen internal protocol; P0 used an "approved fixed-seed gate" but the authors flag that "no independently timestamped P0 preregistration is archived" — a notable honesty caveat. The release protocol (Section 8) is concretely specified, and the minimal recheck procedure is a genuine contribution to evaluation auditability.
- **Significance**: If the analytic corrected-payoff bound problem for HUNL (the paper's own "sharpest open problem," Section 9) is solved, this becomes a deployable standard for costly agent evaluation; as it stands, it is a principled protocol with a strong asymptotic demo and an exact-certificate existence proof on a small game.
- **Red flags**: (1) Title says "74× Cheaper" and "Certified" in the same breath, but the 74× is asymptotic and the certification is Leduc-only — a careful reader must disentangle these. (2) The E1 bound was changed mid-experiment from the registered $B=13$ to an observed-max $B=22$ (Appendix D.3), which the authors label descriptive — laudably disclosed, but it is a protocol deviation. (3) The 71,439-hand corpus is modest by Libratus/DeepStack standards (120k/44k hands) and from a single source.

## Strengths

- The intellectual honesty about the AsympCS-vs-EB-CS trade-off is exemplary: the paper openly reports that its headline speedup comes from the asymptotic interval whose finite-sample error rate is 7–12%, and that the exact interval's speedup is only 1.37× in HUNL because of the width floor. Few papers in this area separate "what is efficient" from "what is certified" this cleanly.
- The predictable-interface proof (Proposition 1) is the practical payoff: it removes AIVAT's chicken-and-egg requirement for a pre-evaluation value function and lets the model warm-start and keep learning, with Theorem 2 showing this is asymptotically free.
- The release protocol and the A2 recheck demonstration directly address a real and underappreciated problem in agent evaluation — that peeking inflates false positives (61% here) — and give a concrete, minimal-evidence remedy.
- The Leduc structural-bound derivation ($|Y| \le 117$ via telescoping over the complete legal tree) is a nice example of extracting a strategy-independent, sample-independent almost-sure bound from game structure, and it is independently verified by enumeration.

## Limitations

- **Exact certification is Leduc-only**: no analytic corrected-payoff bound exists for HUNL, so all HUNL EB-CS results are descriptive. The paper's own Discussion names this as "the sharpest open problem."
- **The AsympCS is miscalibrated at finite horizons**: 7.11% (P0, 30-condition mean), 10.4% (A2 standalone), 12.43% (AIVAT-only) — all above nominal 5%. A held-out calibration gate passes, but the primary interval is not a finite-sample method.
- **Single-platform, possibly non-distinct-model HUNL corpus**: 15 configurations that "need not correspond to 15 distinct base models," all vs GTO Wizard. Cross-platform and multiplayer generalization is unverified.
- **A2 audit is a resampling artifact by construction**: it discards between-run mean differences and serial/cluster structure, so the 61% false-positive figure is illustrative, not an deployed-leaderboard error rate.
- **Online learning recovery is partial**: 77–79% of the frozen-to-oracle gap in two specific mismatched Leduc profiles; the exponent $\beta$ is governed by the least-visited information set, and Leduc reach probabilities span two orders of magnitude, so $N_{\min}(t) \ll t$ and "the effective $\beta$ is small" (Remark 2).
- **Two-player, heads-up only**: the protocol assumes paired hands against a fixed opponent profile; multiplayer requires "explicit seat conditioning, node-law accounting, and dependence analysis" (Appendix H).
- **One protocol deviation** (E1: registered $B=13 \to$ executed $B=22$) is disclosed but worth flagging.

## Relevance

与用户对不完美信息博弈、LLM 智能体评测和统计严谨性的研究兴趣高度相关：它直接解决"何时可以停止昂贵的对局评测"这一核心痛点，并为 PokerSkill 等 LLM 扑克智能体的评测提供了可审计、可复现的停止规则。其 predictable 在线价值学习的思想与 CFR/价值函数近似求解路线（用户既有工作方向）天然衔接，置信序列 + 方差缩减的组合也可迁移到其他高方差评测场景。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can a strategy-independent analytic corrected-payoff bound $B_Y$ be derived for HUNL (e.g., via stack-aware telescoping over the abstracted game tree), or must exact certification wait for a CS whose range dependence adapts to the realized payoff scale?
- How does the AsympCS finite-sample miscalibration (7–12% vs nominal 5%) behave under heavier-tailed payoff distributions or under non-i.i.d. streams with run-level clustering, and can a finite-sample betting CS with adaptive $\lambda_t$ close the gap between the 1.37× EB-CS ratio and the 74× AsympCS ratio?
- What concrete variance-regret rates are achievable for tabular Laplace opponent models and for neural value learners at HUNL scale, where reach probabilities span many orders of magnitude and $N_{\min}(t)$ may grow very slowly?
- Does the predictable online-update result (Theorem 2) extend to value functions refit by gradient descent on a neural net with non-convex dynamics, where $\|v_t - v^\star\|_\infty \le c t^{-\beta}$ may not hold uniformly?
- How would the release protocol interact with an adversarial correction generator that post hoc retunes the value function (the failure mode of ref [29]) — can the provenance records alone detect such manipulation, or is a separate estimator-audit procedure always required?
- Can the framework transfer to multiplayer (n>2) evaluation, where seat conditioning, correlated chance, and within-hand dependence break the i.i.d. hand assumption underlying both CSs?
