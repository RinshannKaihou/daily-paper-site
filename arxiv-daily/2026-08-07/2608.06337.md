> **一句话总结：** 本文证明了在"单调对手"（注入正确标签样本破坏可交换性）模型下，VC 维 d≥2 的二分类类最优期望误差率必然是 Θ((d/n)·log(n/d))，比干净 i.i.d. 的 Θ(d/n) 多一个对数因子；这一对数损失对有限 Littlestone 维同样不可避免，而 d=1 时则存在非恰当学习者可达 Θ(1/n)。

> **TL;DR:** Settling the two open questions of Larsen–Pabbaraju–Shetty (ALT 2026) on the "monotone adversary" model (an adversary appends correctly-labeled examples after seeing the clean sample, breaking exchangeability), this paper proves the minimax expected error over binary classes of VC dimension d is Θ((min{d,n}/n)·log(en/min{d,n})) for d≥2 — a log(n/d) gap above the clean PAC rate Θ(d/n) of Haussler–Littlestone–Warmuth — while d=1 admits Θ(1/n) via a new improper learner; the same logarithmic blowup holds with Littlestone dimension d_L in place of d, so finite Littlestone dimension does not restore the clean online-to-batch O(d_L/n) rate.

## 核心贡献

- **确定单调对手模型下的最优速率（VC 维）**：对任意已知有限注入预算，VC 维 d≥2 的二分类类的极小极大期望误差为 Θ((min{d,n}/n)·log(en/min{d,n}))；d=1 时为 Θ(1/n)；d=0 时为 0。这把 Larsen–Pabbaraju–Shetty [LPS26] 留下的"对数因子是否本质"的第一个 open question 彻底解决。
- **证明对数代价是信息论本质，而非特定算法的缺陷**：先前 [LPS26] 只对 ERM、majority-of-subsets、bagging、majority-of-three 这几类具体算法给出 Ω((d/n)log(n/d)) 下界；本文给出对**所有**随机化、可能非恰当的学习者都成立的下界。
- **扭转经典图景——ERM 在此模型下最优**：经典 PAC 理论中 ERM 比最优学习者差一个 log(n/d) 因子；而在单调对手下，ERM 反而成为 d≥2 时的最优学习者（上界来自 [LPS26]，下界来自本文）。
- **Littlestone 维同样不能挽回干净速率**：对有限 Littlestone 维 d_L≥2，最优速率同样是 Θ((d_L/n)·log(n/d_L))，干净 online-to-batch 转换给出的 O(d_L/n) 不可达，且该对数损失在单个 VC 维与 Littlestone 维均为 2 的固定类上就已出现。解决 [LPS26] 第二个 open question。
- **d=1 的非恰当学习者**：基于 leave-one-out / one-inclusion graph 思想，证明 VC 维 1 的类上至多一次 leave-one-out 错误（Lemma 3.13），从而在任意预算下达到 O(1/n)。
- **所有下界来自单一构造**：一个显式类与先验，使得两个在非负质量点上不同的目标假设，经过对手注入后对学习者产生完全相同的输入（不可区分性论证）。

## Key Contributions

- **Resolves the optimal VC-dimension rate under monotone adversaries**: The minimax expected error over binary classes of exact VC dimension d is Θ(1/n) at d=1 and Θ((min{d,n}/n)·log(en/min{d,n})) for d≥2, for every known finite insertion budget. This closes Open Question 1 of Larsen–Pabbaraju–Shetty [LPS26, ALT 2026], which had only established the upper bound (via ERM) and algorithm-specific lower bounds.
- **Algorithm-independent logarithmic separation from i.i.d. learning**: The lower bounds hold against every randomized, possibly improper learner, proving that the extra log(n/d) factor is an inherent statistical consequence of the loss of exchangeability — not an artifact of particular algorithms (as [LPS26]'s prior lower bounds for ERM, bagging, majority-of-three, and majority-of-subsets left open).
- **Reverses the classical role of ERM**: In clean PAC learning, ERM is suboptimal by a log(n/d) factor (Hanneke 2016; Larsen 2023; Aden-Ali et al. 2024 gave optimal learners); under monotone insertions, ERM becomes an *optimal* learner for d≥2.
- **Finite Littlestone dimension does not help**: Replaces d with Littlestone dimension d_L and obtains the same Θ((d_L/n)·log(n/d_L)) rate for d_L≥2, so the clean online-to-batch O(d_L/n) rate is unattainable — closing Open Question 2 of [LPS26]. The logarithmic loss already occurs for a single fixed class with VC=Littlestone dimension 2.
- **New improper learner for d=1**: A consensus rule combining Ben-David's f-representation with the one-inclusion-graph leave-one-out argument attains O(1/n) for every budget; its core is Lemma 3.13, showing at most one leave-one-out error can occur when VCdim(H)≤1, an argument that survives the adversary's per-sample adaptivity.
- **Unified lower-bound construction**: All lower bounds come from one idea — an explicit class and prior on which two target hypotheses differing at a point of nonnegligible mass produce identical learner input, instantiated via finite projective planes (d=2), their Cartesian products (d≥3), and a checker-only variant (Littlestone).

## Methodology

This is a pure statistical-learning-theory paper; there are no experiments, only theorems with full proofs. The technical machinery is:

1. **The model (Definition 1)**. Nature draws n i.i.d. clean examples from D labeled by h⋆. A deterministic adversary A: X^n → X^m sees the clean sample and appends exactly m examples, each labeled by h⋆ (may repeat points, may use points off D's support). The combined n+m sample is uniformly shuffled before reaching the learner. The learner knows H, n, m but not which examples are clean; it is scored on a fresh point from D. The defining difficulty is that the adversary's insertions depend on the clean sample, so the combined sample is **not exchangeable**, breaking the standard one-inclusion-graph / symmetrization arguments.

2. **The d=2 lower bound (Theorem 3.5) — the heart of the paper.** The author builds a hypothesis class H_q from a finite projective plane of prime order q (K = q²+q+1 points/lines, k = q+1). Concepts are point-concepts u_p and line-concepts v_L; the domain has "checker" points c_{rM} and "selector" points s_{rM} per flag (r,M). Two key properties: (i) VCdim(H_q)=2 (unique intersections bound it above; two selectors through a common point shatter below); (ii) for any flag f=(p,L), the hypotheses u_p and v_L disagree at exactly one checker c_{pL} on the row/column supports. The adversary, seeing the clean sample, checks whether some support checker is missing; if so it pads the sample to a canonical multiset U_f whose version space is exactly {u_p, v_L}; otherwise it pads to a singleton-version-space W_{h⋆}. A coupon-collector bound (Lemma 3.9) shows a checker is missing with probability ρ≥2/3. Because U_f is identical under u_p and v_L and the uniform shuffle is target-independent, Bayes' rule forces the posterior over the two targets to stay balanced (½,½) **even for order-sensitive learners**, yielding risk ≥1/(2k) ≥ log(en)/(30n). q is chosen via Bertrand's postulate with B=⌈4n/log(en)⌉.

3. **The d≥3 lower bound (Theorem 3.10).** Take r=⌊d/2⌋ disjoint tagged copies ("blocks") of H_q; clean examples fall uniformly into blocks so each gets ~ν=n/r samples. The same indistinguishability argument runs per block; linearity of expectation sums the per-block 1/(2rk) contributions, giving Ω((d/n)log(en/d)).

4. **The d=1 upper bound (Proposition 3.14).** Fix f∈H and XOR-relabel to assume the zero classifier is in the class (Ben-David's f-representation). The learner outputs the consensus rule γ_T(x)=1{every c∈V_f(T) labels x by 1}. Lemma 3.13 proves that under VCdim(H)≤1, across all n+1 leave-one-out subsamples the total number of errors is ≤1 — even though the adversary may augment each leave-one-out sample differently (the crux being that each augmented sample still contains the other n clean labeled examples). The standard leave-one-out averaging then gives risk ≤1/(n+1).

5. **The Littlestone bounds (Theorem 3.3).** Upper bounds follow since VCdim(H)≤Ldim(H). For the lower bound, the author constructs a countable class H_prime (union of checker-only projective-plane components over all primes) with Ldim=VCdim=2, and reuses the indistinguishability + coupon-collector machinery (Lemma 3.16 verifies the Littlestone dimension); higher d_L use the product construction with Littlestone-dimension additivity.

6. **Bounded-budget caveat (Remark 3.2).** When m=O(1), a majority vote over all t=⌊n/(4(m+1))⌋-element subsets of occurrences (trained with an optimal clean learner) achieves O(d(m+1)/n), so the clean Θ(d/n) rate survives — meaning the logarithmic blowup provably requires the insertion budget to grow with n,d.

## Results & Highlights

All "benchmarks" here are minimax risk rates R(n,m,d) = sup over classes of VC dimension d of the infimum over learners of the supremum over target/distribution/adversary of expected error.

- **Main VC rate (Theorem 3.1, d≥2):** `sup_m R(n,m,d) ≍ (min{d,n}/n)·log(en/min{d,n})`, versus the clean PAC baseline Θ(min{1,d/n}) of [EHKV89; HLW94] — **a multiplicative log(n/d) gap**, the first algorithm-independent separation between i.i.d. and monotone-corrupted learning.
- **Explicit d=2 lower bound (Theorem 3.5):** On the projective-plane class H_q with VCdim exactly 2, every randomized possibly improper learner suffers `E[Err_D(bh,h⋆)] ≥ log(en)/(30n)` against some target/distribution/adaptive monotone adversary, where the matching ERM upper bound from [LPS26] is O((d/n)log(n/d)) — the constant 1/30 is explicit and the budget m = n(|X_q|−1) is finite.
- **d=1 rate (Propositions 3.4 & 3.14):** `1/(2e(n+1)) ≤ R(n,m,1) ≤ 1/(n+1)` for every m — matching the clean Θ(1/n) rate, in contrast to [LPS26]'s result that tie-broken one-inclusion learners suffer constant error and that majority-of-t voters need Ω((d/n)log(n/d)) with m=O(1) insertions.
- **Littlestone rate (Theorem 3.3, d_L≥2):** `R_L(n,d_L) ≍ (min{d_L,n}/n)·log(en/min{d_L,n})`, versus the clean online-to-batch baseline O(d_L/n) of [BHK20] — **a log(n/d_L) gap**, already witnessed by a single fixed class with d_L=2 (Lemma 3.16), so finite Littlestone dimension does not restore the clean rate.
- **Bounded-budget regime (Remark 3.2):** When m=O(1) and n≥8(m+1), a majority-of-⌊n/(4(m+1))⌋-subsets learner achieves `O(d(m+1)/n)` = O(d/n) for constant m, matching the clean rate and showing the logarithmic blowup is a large-budget phenomenon.

## Authors & Credibility

Single-author paper by **Anay Mehrotra** (Stanford University), working in the learning-theory community. The paper directly extends the model introduced by Larsen, Pabbaraju, and Shetty [LPS26] (ALT 2026) and positions itself as resolving that paper's first two open questions. The author thanks Jason Gaitonde and Abhishek Shetty (a co-author of [LPS26]) for introducing the model, indicating direct lineage and community awareness. The acknowledgments disclose that "GPT 5.6 Sol and Claude Fable were routinely used in the development of ideas used in the paper" — a candid and somewhat unusual disclosure for a theory paper, but consistent with the paper's focus being on the mathematical content (all proofs are written out, not LLM-generated hand-waving). The techniques invoked — finite projective planes, Haussler–Littlestone–Warmuth one-inclusion graph, Ben-David's f-representation, coupon-collector / second-moment bounds, Bertrand's postulate for prime selection — are standard and correctly cited, and the cited prior work [HLW94; Han16; Lar23; AHLZ24; LPS26; Lit88] is the canonical literature for PAC/online minimax rates.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty (high).** Genuinely new result: the prior work [LPS26] only proved lower bounds against *named* algorithm families (ERM, majority-of-subsets, bagging, majority-of-three) and explicitly left open whether *any* learner could beat the log factor. The author's contribution — an indistinguishability construction that works against *every* learner — is the missing piece, and combining it with a projective-plane class to realize the ambiguity using only correctly-labeled insertions is non-trivial. The d=1 improper learner and the Littlestone-dimension extension are additional, not merely corollary.
- **Rigor (high).** All theorems have complete proofs in the body (not deferred to a supplement). The constants are explicit (e.g., 1/30, 1/(2e(n+1)), ρ≥2/3). The d=2 lower-bound proof is checked carefully: VCdim is verified both above (three cases on coincident lines) and below (explicit shattered pair), and the Bayes/posterior argument is written out including the order-sensitivity case. I did not find gaps on careful reading.
- **Reproducibility (high for theory).** Pure math; constructions are finite and explicit (a reader can instantiate H_q for a given prime q). No code, datasets, or hyperparameters to reproduce. The only computational reproducibility concern is the author's reliance on LLM tools during ideation, which is disclosed and does not affect the proofs.
- **Significance (high for the subfield).** The conceptual punchline — that adding correctly-labeled examples can make learning *harder* by a log factor, even for classes with finite mistake bounds — is counterintuitive and directly relevant to the practice of dataset curation (filtering, deduplication, augmentation chosen after inspecting data), which the motivating introduction ties to real ML pipelines. It also rehabilitates ERM as optimal in this corrupted regime.
- **Red flags / honest weaknesses:**
  - **The headline "monotone adversary makes learning harder" is regime-dependent.** Remark 3.2 concedes that with a *bounded* budget m=O(1), the clean Θ(d/n) rate is still achievable, so the logarithmic blowup in Theorem 3.1 requires the witnessing budget m(n,d) to **grow** with n and d (for d=2 the construction uses m = n(|X_q|−1), which is Θ(n²/log²n) — far larger than the n clean examples). A reader of only the abstract could over-interpret the result as applying to any correctly-labeled augmentation; in fact the adversary must be allowed to insert super-linear numbers of examples. The paper states this caveat but the abstract/TL;DR framing does not foreground it.
  - **Worst-case finite classes only.** The lower bounds are witnessed by a specially constructed class (projective planes / their products). Which *structural* property of a fixed natural class determines its monotone-adversary rate is explicitly left open (the first bullet of "Further directions"). So the result tells us the minimax worst case, not what happens for, say, halfspaces or decision trees.
  - **Expected risk only; no (ε,δ) high-probability bound.** The classical PAC story separates expectation (Θ(d/n)) from high-probability sample complexity (Θ((d+log1/δ)/ε)); this paper only resolves the expectation version and lists the high-probability analogue as open. For practical sample-complexity statements this is a real gap.
  - **No efficient algorithms.** The d=1 upper-bound learner is described as a consensus rule but its computational cost is not analyzed; for d≥2 ERM is optimal but computationally hard classes (e.g., convex bodies with Gaussian marginals, where VC bounds are vacuous) are flagged as unresolved. The author is explicit that "this statistical construction need not be computationally efficient."
  - **Binary classification only.** Multiclass/partial concepts (where uniform convergence fails) are left open; the paper notes its lower bound transfers but says nothing about achievability there.
  - **Modest constants / looseness.** The lower-bound constant 1/30 (and 1/90 in the product bound) is small; the gap between upper and lower is only the hidden multiplicative constant, but the construction is clearly not optimized for tightness.

## Strengths

- Settles two explicitly stated open questions from a recent ALT paper with full proofs, not partial progress.
- The unification of all lower bounds under a single indistinguishability construction (projective plane → products → Littlestone checker-only variant) is elegant and makes the paper pedagogically clean.
- The d=1 upper bound is a nice positive counterpart: it shows exactly where and why the combinatorial leave-one-out argument of the one-inclusion graph survives adaptive insertions, via Lemma 3.13, which is the paper's most reusable technical lemma.
- Honest about limitations — the bounded-budget caveat, the open structural / high-probability / multiclass questions, and the LLM-assistance disclosure are all in the paper rather than buried.
- Counterintuitive, memorable takeaway (correctly-labeled data can hurt by a log factor) that connects abstract theory to the real practice of dataset curation.

## Limitations

- The logarithmic blowup requires the adversary's budget to grow with n,d (m=O(1) is benign per Remark 3.2); the most adversarial construction inserts Θ(n²/log²n) examples against n clean ones for d=2.
- Only expected error is characterized; the optimal high-probability (ε,δ) sample complexity is open.
- Lower bounds are worst-case over finite, specially constructed classes (projective planes); rates for specific natural classes (halfspaces, etc.) are not determined.
- Computational efficiency of the optimal d=1 learner is not discussed; efficient optimal learning for d≥2 when VC bounds are vacuous is open.
- Binary classification only — multiclass and partial concept classes are flagged as unresolved.
- The transition between the bounded-budget regime (Θ(d/n)) and the large-budget regime (Θ((d/n)log(n/d))) as a function of m is left open; only the two endpoints are pinned.

## Relevance

高度相关于学习理论根基与"数据投毒/数据策展鲁棒性"主题：它精确刻画了"插入正确标签样本"这一看似无害、却破坏可交换性的操作对二分类样本复杂度的根本影响，直接呼应现实中数据去重、过滤、增强等后处理环节。对于关注 PAC/online 最优速率、one-inclusion graph、clean-label poisoning 与分布偏移下可学习性的研究方向都是重要的理论参照点。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- What is the optimal expected risk as an explicit function of the insertion budget m between the bounded regime (Θ(d/n) at m=O(1)) and the worst-case regime (Θ((d/n)log(n/d)))? The paper leaves the interpolation open.
- Is there an *efficient* (poly-time) learner attaining the d=1 Θ(1/n) rate, and an efficient optimal learner for d≥2 when VC bounds are vacuous (e.g., convex bodies under Gaussian marginals)?
- What structural invariant of a *fixed natural* class (halfspaces, intervals, decision stumps) determines its monotone-adversary rate, rather than the worst case over all classes of a given VC dimension?
- What is the optimal high-probability (ε,δ) sample complexity under monotone insertions — does the log factor persist, multiply, or interact with log(1/δ) differently than in Hanneke's clean bound?
- Does the logarithmic separation extend to multiclass and partial concept classes (where uniform convergence is unavailable), and to real-valued/regression settings?
- Can the projective-plane indistinguishability construction be replaced by a simpler combinatorial object to tighten the constants (currently 1/30, 1/90), or are projective planes essential to achieving VCdim exactly 2 with the required regularity?
- The author discloses routine use of LLM tools during ideation — were any of the proof steps (especially the posterior-balance / Bayes arguments) machine-suggested, and have the proofs been independently verified by the community (this is an arXiv preprint, v1)?
