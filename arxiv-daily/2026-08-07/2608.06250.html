Minimax Optimal Early-Stopped Gradient Descent for Gaussian Mixture Classification

Alex Buna · Shirley Xiaoqi Liu · Patrick Rebeschini — University of Oxford, Dept. of Statistics

early-stopping minimax-optimality overparameterized-classification implicit-bias statistical-learning-theory
Key
Finding

For noisy Gaussian-mixture classification, early-stopped gradient descent on the logistic loss is minimax-optimal — and every linear interpolator (including max-margin) is exponentially worse.

Key Metric
n−2a/(2a+1)

Minimax excess 0-1 risk for early-stopped GD under polynomial covariance spectra λi ≍ i−2a (a > ½). Exponential spectra give log(n)/n.

vs. interpolators: ≳ 1/log(n)
— exponentially worse sample complexity.
Why This Matters

Sharply separates early stopping from interpolation in overparameterized classification — directly informing the statistical foundations of regularization and implicit bias.

Critical Edge

The stopping rule is oracle — it needs the unknown Bayes direction w*. So this is an existence proof, not a deployable algorithm.

Methodology 01

Binary classification under a two-class Gaussian mixture with label-flipping noise: latent label Ȳ ∈ {±1} drawn uniformly, X = Ȳ·μ + ε with ε ~ N(0, Σ), and observed label Y = −Ȳ with probability p ∈ [0, 0.5). Bayes classifier is w* = Σ⁻¹μ.

Training is gradient descent on the empirical logistic loss, initialized at the origin with fixed step γ ≤ min(1/β, 1), β = ‖X1:n‖²/n. The stopping rule is an oracle rule on the unknown Bayes direction: τ := inf{t ≥ 1 : ‖wt‖ ≥ 4‖w*‖}.

The upper bound (Theorem 1) chains three ideas: a new linear-rate calibration (Lemma 4) converting excess 0-1 risk to excess logistic risk; a bias-variance tradeoff governed by a continuous soft effective rank reff(η) = tr(Σ(Σ+ηI)⁻¹); and a localized empirical-process analysis with a peeling argument. The matching lower bound (Theorem 2) holds for any measurable procedure via a packing + hypothesis-testing reduction. The FCD (fast-and-continuously-decaying) spectral assumption reconciles reff(η) with the hard-threshold count N(η), making the bounds meet.

Authors 02

Oxford · Dept. of Statistics

Patrick Rebeschini is a recognized expert in statistical learning theory — early stopping, mirror descent, localized complexity (Kanade–Rebeschini–Vaškevičius).

Extends Wu, Bartlett, Telgarsky & Yu (ICML 2025) and engages carefully with contemporaneous minimax-regression work (arXiv:2509.17251). 50+ pages of appendix proofs.

Results & Highlights 03

  • Polynomial spectra (λi ≍ i−2a, a > ½): early-stopped iterate achieves E(wτ) − E(w*) ≍ n−2a/(2a+1); matched by a statistical lower bound. Empirical curve tracks n−2/3 (a = 1).
  • Exponential spectra (λi ≍ e−bi): rate log(n)/n, matched empirically.
  • Interpolation is separated: when d ≍ n log n, every linear interpolator (incl. max-margin w∞) has ≳ 1/log(n) — strictly slower. Recovers n/d regression bound of Muthukumar et al.
  • Experiment regime: n = 972, d = 3888 (d ≈ 4n), p = 0.1. Figure 1 shows the classic U-shape of risk along the GD trajectory — first down, then up toward interpolation.
  • New calibration Lemma 4 strictly dominates standard √-rate bounds, which would not vanish under label-flipping noise.

Critical Assessment 04

  • Novelty: Genuinely novel — the first minimax-optimality result for early-stopped GD in classification; prior SoTA only showed a poly-vs-exponential separation under signal sparsity. The linear-rate calibration lemma is clean and reusable.
  • Rigor: High. Upper and lower bounds reconciled under FCD with explicit constants and full proofs. Interpolation lower bound correctly drops the sparsity assumption — necessary, since worst-case minimax signals need not be sparse.
  • Reproducibility: Limited. Figures are synthetic rate-matching plots with hand-picked params; no code, seeds, repetition counts, or real data.
  • Significance: Theoretically significant — closes the regression↔classification gap. Practical impact muted (oracle rule).
  • Overclaiming: Admirably careful. Explicitly flags the result as existence-only.

Strengths 05

  • First clean minimax theorem for early-stopped GD in classification; extends regression theory to misspecified logistic loss.
  • Linear-rate calibration Lemma 4 is a self-contained, reusable tool.
  • Covers a broader spectral class (FCD, incl. exponential decay) than prior power-law classes.
  • Interpolation lower bound drops the sparsity assumption and recovers the known regression bound.

Limitations 06

  • Oracle stopping rule — depends on unknown w*; data-dependent rule is open and known to be hard.
  • Restricted source condition — only r = ½, not general r ≥ 0.
  • FCD excludes benign-overfitting regimes — message certified only for fast-decaying spectra.
  • Single noise model — only symmetric label-flipping.
  • Synthetic-only experiments — no real data, no baseline regularizers.

Relevance 07

这篇论文严格刻画了过参数化逻辑回归分类中"提前停止 vs. 插值"的统计效率分离，正是过参数化学习、隐式偏置与泛化理论的核心议题；其新的校准引理与 minimax 速率对理解深度网络中早停与正则化的统计基础具有直接参考价值。

Questions for Further Exploration 08

  • Can the oracle rule be replaced by a data-dependent one (held-out 0-1 validation, or the empirical ‖wt‖ growth curve) that retains the minimax rate, given logistic training loss but 0-1 test risk?
  • How do rates degrade moving away from FCD toward slower-decaying spectra (λi ≍ i−α, α ≤ 1) where benign overfitting of max-margin holds — is there a regime where interpolation strictly dominates?
  • For structured signals (sparse / low-rank w*, i.e. r ≠ ½), can the upper bound meet a source-aware lower bound, recovering general-r rates?
  • Does the linear-rate calibration extend to multiclass softmax Gaussian mixtures, or to non-Gaussian sub-Gaussian covariates where the sigmoid form of P(Y|X) no longer holds?
  • Does the predicted n−2a/(2a+1) vs. log(n)/n separation show up on real benchmarks (random-features on CIFAR/ImageNet), or is FCD too restrictive for real eigenspectra?
