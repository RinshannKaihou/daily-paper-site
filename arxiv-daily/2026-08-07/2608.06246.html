Conceptual Survey · AI Governance

A Six-Dimensional Taxonomy of Post-Training Adaptation Techniques with Applications in AI Governance

Fardin Afdideh · Fernando Seoane · Farhad Abtahi
Karolinska Institutet — Clinical Science, Intervention & Technology · Karolinska University Hospital · University of Borås · KTH Royal Institute of Technology
TL;DR A six-dimensional taxonomy — Mechanism, Goal, Data, Persistence, Scope (D1–D5) plus Model Type as a modulating D6 — classifies 48 post-training adaptation techniques spanning Traditional ML → DL → FM → LLM → MLLM, organized into a centerpiece Table 8 and mapped onto four regulatory regimes (NIST AI RMF, EU AI Act, EU MDR/IVDR, FDA PCCP). Against 7 prior surveys (1–3 dimensions, ~6–15 techniques), it is the only framework spanning the full model hierarchy with an explicit governance mapping. A UMAP projection reports a near-zero silhouette score, confirming 9 navigational families are not separable clusters.
Why this matters

Unifies scattered post-training techniques into one coordinate system explicitly tied to AI Act, NIST RMF and FDA PCCP — a directly citable framework for governance-aware model change management.

The D1–D5 vs D6 split is the cleanest resolution to "is PEFT fine-tuning or efficiency?" — it separates technique properties from target-model properties. Supersession examples: classical Domain Adaptation is replaced at the LLM tier by CPT + domain PEFT; Meta-Learning is functionally replaced by In-Context Learning.

Methodology conceptual · not empirical

1 · Multivocal Literature Review

Backward + forward snowballing from 12 seed clusters (18 seed papers), plus grey literature (Hugging Face PEFT, mergekit, EasyEdit, ms-swift). Justified because practitioner techniques often appear in framework docs years before peer review.

2 · Taxonomic construction

Each technique receives a six-element profile (d1…d6). A candidate got a row if it was an independently selectable adaptation decision unit with a materially distinct profile. Saturation reached when further candidates collapsed into variants or compositions.

3 · Exploratory UMAP diagnostic

Gower dissimilarity over D1–D6, projected with UMAP — a diagnostic of whether the 9 families occupy coherent regions, not validation.

Key Contributions centerpiece

  • Modulating D6 cleanly separates technique properties from target-model properties — generalizes to any technique whose profile shifts across model tiers.
  • Table 8 profiles all 48 techniques organized into 9 navigational families, with boundary markers (§/¶/†/‡) handling edge cases.
  • Explainability as a first-class D2 goal — legally (EU AI Act Art. 13) and mechanistically distinct from Task Specialization / Alignment.
  • Ordered-composition operator ⊕ for layered stacks, e.g. RLVR ⊕ RLHF ⊕ PEFT ⊕ RAG ⊕ PE.
  • Boundary-extension techniques (Data Augmentation, Curriculum, Active Learning) treated as d1 = Pipeline-Mediated — included without diluting the core category.

Strengths & Limitations author-stated + critical assessment

Strengths

  • Modulating-vs-characterizing distinction is the cleanest resolution to the PEFT ambiguity class.
  • Governance mapping is concrete and immediately usable — a regulatory writer can lift Table 9–10 directly into a submission.
  • Honest self-assessment: UMAP explicitly not validation; MLLM gap admitted; regulatory claims hedged with "may".
  • Clinical-regulatory author positioning (Karolinska) explains the unusually strong MDR/IVDR & FDA PCCP emphasis.

Limitations

  • No empirical validation — no evidence the taxonomy improves documentation or review speed over a simpler scheme.
  • Single-coder bias; 48 is a soft number (APO vs PE, Full vs Partial FT splits are author judgment).
  • MLLM coverage (rows 44–46) acknowledged as non-exhaustive — a visible gap in a paper whose title promises MLLM.
  • No access-regime axis (white/grey/black-box); compute/cost/latency characteristics absent.
  • Regulatory mapping is illustrative, not legal — cannot tell a manufacturer whether a change will trigger re-classification.
Reviewer's wishlist (4 missing analyses)
  • No inter-rater reliability on Table 8 codings.
  • No sensitivity analysis on the 48-row inclusion threshold.
  • No test of predictive power — does the 6-D profile predict regulatory classification or compute cost better than a single axis?
  • Abstract's claim of "distinguishing commonly conflated terms" is defined, not empirically demonstrated.

Questions for Further Exploration open threads

  1. Inter-coder reliability — what would 3–5 independent practitioners agree on, coding the same 48 techniques from definitions alone?
  2. Predictive validity — does the six-dimensional profile predict regulatory classification ("substantial modification") better than a single-axis label?
  3. Access-regime overlay (D7) — how would white/grey/black-box change feasible-technique sets for API-only deployments?
  4. Agentic meta-adaptation — minimal Table 8 extension for SPIN self-play, STaR self-distillation, and agent-memory accumulation.
  5. MLLM completeness — row count and profile distribution once rows 44–46 cover the 2025–2026 MLLM adaptation literature.
  6. Operational tooling — could profiles be encoded as a machine-readable JSON-LD schema that AEGIS-style governance infrastructure consumes directly?
