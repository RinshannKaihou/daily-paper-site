> **一句话总结：** 作者对 SCICODE 科学代码基准的全部 65 道测试题做领域专家逐题审计，发现 263 个缺陷（其中 192 个会压低分数、覆盖 91% 的主问题），据此修复并发布 SCICODE-VERIFIED；在 12 个前沿模型上重测，子问题准确率从 45–60% 升至 84–98%，主问题准确率从 9–27% 升至 69–92%，证明分数停滞主因是基准缺陷而非模型能力。

> **TL;DR:** A domain-expert audit of all 65 SCICODE test problems uncovers 263 defects (192 of them score-suppressing, touching 91% of main problems); the corrected benchmark SCICODE-VERIFIED raises twelve frontier models' subproblem accuracy from 45–60% to 84–98% on the same harness (e.g. GPT-5.6 Sol 58.2→98.3 sub, 23.4→92.2 main vs. the original benchmark), showing that the field's plateau near 60% was an instrument defect, not a model-capability ceiling.

## 核心贡献

- **发布修正版基准 SCICODE-VERIFIED**：对 SCICODE 全部 65 道测试题做逐题专家审计，修正除问题 2（Gaussian_Beam_Focus，规范不定不可验证，已剔除）外全部可确认缺陷；每条改动都附理由并由第二位领域专家独立对抗复核，发布文件从改动记录机械重生成并 md5 校验。
- **构建 SCICODE 缺陷分类法（taxonomy）**：262 个缺陷按 *性质*（SCIENTIFIC 需物理/数学判断 vs SURFACE 文本/代码层面）与 *方向*（过严/错误、中性、过松需收紧）两个维度交叉归类（Table 3、Table 4），揭示压分缺陷的"专业知识门槛"结构——192 个压分缺陷中 150 个（78%）属 SCIENTIFIC。
- **匹配式前后重评 12 个前沿模型**：在同一 harness（带 background、pass@1、双环境 OR 评分）下，仅替换基准数据，隔离出"基准本身"对分数的贡献；子问题准确率 +29–40 分、主问题准确率 +53–70 分，并改变模型间排名（Seed 2.1 Pro 在原版主问题排名第一、修正版排第六）。
- **量化背景知识的作用**：在 SCICODE-VERIFIED 上 withheld expert-written background 会让子问题准确率下降 10–22 分、主问题下降 20–36 分；通过对 31 道"无背景失败/有背景通过"子问题的根因分析，归纳出四类可复现失败模式（多步推导重组、教科书先验覆盖题面、专门领域事实、近邻实现错误）。
- **鲁棒评分方案**：双 pinned NumPy/SciPy 环境下"任一通过即判对"的 OR 评分，每模型挽救 7–9 个仅因库版本不兼容而误判的子问题，且挽救集在各模型间高度一致，不偏向任一模型。

## Key Contributions

- **SCICODE-VERIFIED, a corrected public benchmark**: a per-problem, domain-expert audit of all 65 test problems; every confirmable defect is fixed, each correction is recorded with its justification and independently re-checked by a second adversarial reviewer, and released files are regenerated from the ledger and md5-verified at harness startup. One underdetermined problem (#2 Gaussian_Beam_Focus) is dropped rather than shipping an arbitrary gold.
- **A two-axis defect taxonomy for SCICODE**: 262 manually verified defects in 63/64 test problems, sorted by *nature* (SCIENTIFIC — needs physics/math judgment — vs SURFACE) and by *direction* (rejects a correct solution / neutral cleanup / too-lenient test that was tightened). The cross-tabulation makes the expertise-gated structure of score suppression legible (Tables 3–4).
- **Matched before/after re-evaluation of twelve frontier snapshots**: under an identical with-background, pass@1 harness — only the benchmark data differs — correction lifts subproblem accuracy from 45–60% to 84–98% and main-problem accuracy from 9–27% to 69–92%, and reshuffles rankings (Seed 2.1 Pro ranks 1st on the original main-problem score but 6th on SCICODE-VERIFIED).
- **Diagnostic of what the expert-written background supplies**: withholding background costs 10–22 subproblem and 20–36 main-problem points; a root-cause analysis of 31 fail-without/pass-with subproblems identifies four recurring failure modes (reassembling multi-step derivations, textbook priors overriding the stated formulation, specialized domain facts, near-miss implementation errors).
- **Library-version-robust grading**: grading each saved solution in two pinned NumPy/SciPy environments and counting it correct if either passes rescues 7–9 of 287 subproblems per model, with nearly identical rescue sets across models.

## Methodology

The work is a benchmark audit and re-evaluation, not a new model.

- **Audit protocol**. Two authors with domain expertise (physics/mathematics) reviewed all 65 test problems of SCICODE's test split (the 15-problem development split is excluded because it ships public gold). Each problem's targets were independently re-derived and its specification stress-tested; a second, adversarial pass re-verified every flagged defect to eliminate false alarms. Approved changes are recorded with their justification in a `ledger/`, and release files are regenerated from that record and mechanically checked against it (`manifest.json` md5 verified at harness startup). SciCode's own issue tracker had already documented over-strict tolerances and self-failing golds.
- **Why defects are costly here**. SCICODE subproblems form a cumulative chain (step *k* may call gold functions of steps 1:k−1), and main-problem scoring is all-or-nothing; a single defective subproblem therefore vetoes the whole main problem, and an early defect propagates downstream.
- **Defect taxonomy**. Each defect is sorted along two axes: *nature* — SCIENTIFIC (requires physics/math judgment) vs SURFACE (corpus/code/text) — and *direction* — strict/wrong (rejects correct solutions, the score-suppressing class), neutral, or too-lenient (a weak test that was tightened, never loosened). Of 262 defects: 177 SCIENTIFIC, 85 SURFACE; 192 strict/wrong, 8 too-lenient (tightened), 62 neutral.
- **Scientific defect subtypes** (177 total): unspecified convention (77, e.g. #74 Householder last-reflector sign), wrong gold (33, e.g. #22 rotation-coefficient sign error making the transfer matrix non-unitary), RNG-dependent grading (22, e.g. #68 diffusion Monte-Carlo tied to a particular random sequence), non-discriminating test (14, e.g. #5.1 palindromic Lanczos input stuck in a symmetric subspace), over-tight tolerance (13, e.g. #30.3 rtol=1e-15 below double precision), spec↔gold contradiction (9, e.g. #8 strict vs inclusive high-pass boundary), invalid/non-reproducible target (9, e.g. #28.1).
- **Surface defect subtypes** (85 total): interface/return-contract mismatch (29), truncation/garble/dropped constant (20, e.g. dropped ℏ mantissa), broken cross-reference (16), trivially broken test (12), typo/formatting (8).
- **Re-evaluation harness**. Twelve frontier model snapshots (GPT-5.6 Sol, GPT-5.5, Gemini 3.5 Flash, DeepSeek V4 Flash/Pro Preview/Flash Preview, Claude Fable 5, Claude Opus 4.8, Seed 2.1 Pro, Muse Spark 1.1, Kimi K3, GLM-5.2) were run on both the original upstream release and SCICODE-VERIFIED under an identical harness — with expert background, pass@1, two-pinned-environment OR grading — so only the benchmark data differs. Original-benchmark runs use the identical harness with md5-pinned original upstream text and h5. All scores are pass@1 collected June–July 2026; all single-sample except SCICODE-VERIFIED DeepSeek V4 Preview rows (mean of three runs). API models used reasoning enabled with provider-default sampling; failed calls retried; per-step outputs and grading verdicts cached.

## Results & Highlights

- **Subproblem accuracy (64-problem test split)**: across the twelve models, subproblem accuracy rises from **45–60% on original SCICODE to 84–98% on SCICODE-VERIFIED — a 29–40 point gain per model**. The lowest performer (DeepSeek V4 Flash Preview) goes 45.3→83.7; the highest (GPT-5.6 Sol) goes 58.2→98.3.
- **Main-problem accuracy**: rises from **9–27% to 69–92% — a 53–70 point gain per model**. GPT-5.6 Sol: 23.4→92.2; GPT-5.5: 20.3→90.6; Gemini 3.5 Flash: 17.2→87.5; DeepSeek V4 Flash: 18.8→84.4; Claude Fable 5: 23.4→81.2; Seed 2.1 Pro: 26.6→79.7; Claude Opus 4.8: 25.0→78.1; DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview: 17.2→78.1; Muse Spark 1.1: 23.4→76.6; Kimi K3: 21.9→76.6; GLM-5.2: 15.6→70.3; DeepSeek V4 Flash Preview: 9.4→68.8 (Table 1).
- **Defect prevalence**: **262 defects in 63 of 64 test problems**; **192 (73%) reject correct solutions**, touching **155/287 scored subproblems (54%) across 58/64 main problems (91%)**. Of the 192 score-suppressing defects, **150 (78%) are SCIENTIFIC** — i.e. recognizable as defects only with specialist physics/mathematics knowledge (Table 3).
- **Discrimination is restored**: on the original benchmark GPT-5.5 passes only two more main problems than DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview; on SCICODE-VERIFIED it passes eight more. **Seed 2.1 Pro ranks 1st by original main-problem accuracy but 6th on SCICODE-VERIFIED** — i.e. benchmark defects had inverted the leaderboard.
- **With- vs no-background (SCICODE-VERIFIED, Table 2)**: withholding the expert background costs **10–22 subproblem and 20–36 main-problem points**. GPT-5.5: 95.1→82.6 sub, 90.6→65.6 main; Gemini 3.5 Flash: 97.2→80.5 sub, 87.5→51.6 main; Claude Opus 4.8: 92.3→81.9 sub, 78.1→57.8 main. The no-background penalty is larger (21–22 sub points) for the two DeepSeek V4 Preview models.
- **Library-version-robust grading**: two-environment OR grading rescues **7–9 of 287 subproblems per model** that otherwise fail only on library-version incompatibilities, with nearly identical rescue sets across models.
- **Attribution completeness**: across the four models in the subproblem-level attribution analysis, **all 164 newly passed subproblems trace to a documented correction** — i.e. the gain is fully explained by the audit, not by run-to-run variance.

## Authors & Credibility

- Authors are from Hefei National Laboratory / University of Science and Technology of China (Sihan Hu, Youjin Deng), Harbin Engineering University (Lyuhan Huang), and the Institute of Theoretical Physics, Chinese Academy of Sciences (Kun Chen). Youjin Deng and Kun Chen are corresponding authors.
- The work is positioned against — and consistent with — a growing line of benchmark-verification work it cites: UTBOOST and SWE-BENCH VERIFIED (tightening too-permissive SWE tests), MMLU-REDUX and Platinum Benchmarks (label noise near saturation), HUMANEVAL corrections (Liu et al. 2023), and label-error work on factual-consistency benchmarks (Nahum et al. 2025). It extends that line to a *research-level scientific-coding* benchmark whose defects are mostly scientific rather than clerical.
- The audit underwent multiple internal review rounds with an independent adversarial pass, and every change is published with provenance (identified in expert review vs. recovered from the original→corrected diff). Data, harness, and full audit trail are public at https://github.com/flyingwagner/scicode-verified with md5-verified manifests.
- The authors honestly note that all reviews were conducted by the authors themselves and therefore do not constitute external blind validation, and that their original-benchmark subproblem numbers differ somewhat from the Artificial Analysis leaderboard.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Genuine and well-scoped. Prior benchmark-verification work targeted label noise or permissive tests in SWE/QA-style benchmarks; this is the first systematic, *scientific* audit of a research-level scientific-coding benchmark, and the headline finding — that the field's plateau near 60% is an instrument artifact — is non-trivial and consequential for the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index and government/national-lab suites that use SCICODE.
- **Rigor**: Strong on provenance. Every defect is grounded in an actual original-vs-corrected diff, re-checked by an independent adversarial pass, and the release is regenerated from the ledger with md5 verification. The matched before/after harness (same harness, only data differs; original runs md5-pinned to upstream) is the right experimental design for isolating the benchmark's contribution.
- **Specific weaknesses worth scrutiny**:
  - **Most results are single pass@1 runs.** The authors flag this themselves — pass@1 has run-to-run variance, and with several models already near saturation (GPT-5.6 Sol at 98.3% sub / 92.2% main), small ranking differences among top models (e.g. GPT-5.5 vs Gemini 3.5 Flash main-problem 90.6 vs 87.5) should not be over-interpreted. Only the two DeepSeek V4 Preview rows on SCICODE-VERIFIED are averaged over three runs.
  - **No external blind validation.** All reviews were by the authors; the audit is internally adversarial but not third-party-validated, which matters because 150/192 score-suppressing defects are claimed to require specialist judgment — exactly the kind of claim that benefits from independent replication.
  - **The original-benchmark numbers don't fully match the public Artificial Analysis leaderboard**, and the authors attribute the gap to harness differences without fully reconciling it; this leaves a small opening for harness effects to contaminate the before/after delta.
  - **The named weakest baseline** is the original SCICODE itself, whose issue tracker already documented over-strict tolerances and self-failing golds — meaning part of what is "discovered" was partly known but never systematically fixed. The contribution is the systematic fix and taxonomy, not the existence of defects.
- **Reproducibility**: High. Public repo with harness, ledger, manifests, md5 checks, per-subproblem defect list, before/after flip sets, and per-environment grading gains. Pinned environments and cached per-step outputs/verdicts support replication.
- **Significance**: High for the LLM-evaluation community. SCICODE feeds the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index (280 configurations), the UK AI Security Institute's Inspect Evals, and LBNL's standing evaluation; a corrected version with this magnitude of score movement (and a reshuffled leaderboard) is likely to be adopted and to change public model comparisons.
- **Red flags**: None severe. The "78% of defects need a domain expert" framing slightly over-credits the audit's irreplaceability, and the saturation of the top of the leaderboard after correction means SCICODE-VERIFIED itself may be near its useful ceiling for subproblem accuracy (the authors acknowledge this and point to the no-background setting and long-horizon agentic workflows as the remaining headroom).

## Strengths

- Closes a real measurement gap on a benchmark that feeds public leaderboards and government/national-lab evaluation suites.
- Audit design is adversarial and provenance-tracked (second reviewer, diff-grounded, md5-verified release).
- Defect taxonomy (nature × direction; seven scientific subtypes) is the lasting intellectual contribution — it generalizes to other research-level benchmarks.
- Honest about limitations: single pass@1, near-saturation top end, author-only review, no long-horizon agentic coverage.
- Tightens too-lenient tests rather than only loosening strict ones, so the corrected benchmark does not admit wrong answers — the gain is not grade inflation.
- Fully open: harness, ledger, manifests, and analysis files all released.

## Limitations

- Covers only the 65-problem test split; the 15-problem development split (which ships public gold) is not audited or used.
- Most results are single pass@1, so small inter-model gaps — especially among already-saturated top models — are within run-to-run noise.
- All reviews were conducted by the authors; no external blind validation.
- Original-benchmark subproblem numbers differ from the Artificial Analysis leaderboard, attributed to harness differences but not fully reconciled.
- SCICODE-VERIFIED evaluates single-problem scientific coding; it does not cover long-horizon agentic workflows with tool use, environment interaction, and iterative development — an explicitly named future-work direction.
- With-background subproblem accuracy for top models is near saturation (≥95%), leaving limited headroom on that metric.

## Relevance

对关注"LLM 评测基准质量、可复现性与缺陷审计"的研究方向高度相关：本文提供了首个针对科研级代码生成基准的系统化专家审计流程与缺陷分类法，方法论（双盲对抗复核、双环境 OR 评分、md5 校验发布）可直接迁移到其他代码/科学评测。对追踪前沿模型真实能力（尤其是带 background 与否的差异、以及排名被基准缺陷扭曲）也提供了直接证据。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How do the rankings change under multi-sample (pass@k, k>1) evaluation, and does the GPT-5.5 vs Gemini 3.5 Flash main-problem gap survive averaging?
- Would external blind re-audit by independent domain experts confirm the same set of 192 score-suppressing defects, or surface false positives/negatives in the author-led review?
- Can the defect taxonomy (especially the seven SCIENTIFIC subtypes) be operationalized into automated or LLM-assisted pre-release checks for new research-level benchmarks?
- How much of the with-background saturation is benchmark-specific — does a similar audit on a held-out fresh problem set leave more headroom for top models?
- Does SCICODE-VERIFIED transfer to long-horizon agentic scientific-coding workflows (tool use, iterative development), where the authors explicitly leave evaluation open?
- Are the four root-cause failure modes identified in the no-background diagnostic (multi-step derivation, textbook-prior override, domain facts, near-miss implementation) reproducible across other scientific-coding benchmarks?
