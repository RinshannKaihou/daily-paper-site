> **一句话总结：** 本文通过 PRISMA-ScR 范围综述方法，从 4,259 篇文献中筛出 58 篇进行反思性主题分析，系统梳理了 AI 系统集成审计的研究现状，提出三类集成站点（组件间、系统-环境、多系统）、四项审计功能与三项集成专属质量属性（兼容性、完整性、监督性），并指出当前 AI 审计仍以组件级评测为主、缺乏独立性与标准、近三分之二为概念性提案。

> **TL;DR:** This AAAI 2026 scoping review screens 4,259 documents down to a 58-paper corpus and uses reflexive thematic analysis to map how "system integration audits" are (and mostly are not) practiced in AI. Across the corpus, only 29.3% of audits touch GPAI/generative/multimodal systems despite that being the paper's central concern, fewer than 5% reference any established standard, and ~65.5% are mere proposals rather than applied audits — revealing that AI "audits" currently resemble evaluations and leave integration-specific risks (inter-component, system-environment, multi-system) largely unaddressed.

## 核心贡献

- **首次系统性定义 AI 系统集成审计的概念框架**：借鉴航空航天等安全关键行业自 1960 年代以来的成熟做法，将"系统集成审计"（system integration audit）明确引入 AI 治理语境，作为对组件级评测（model-centric evaluation）的关键补充。
- **基于 PRISMA-ScR 的范围综述与可复现流程**：跨 6 个数据库检索 4,259 篇文献，经 Covidence 平台双人独立筛选得到 58 篇语料，前 50 篇编码者间一致性达 82%，并公开检索查询与纳入文献清单（附录 A、B）。
- **提出三类集成站点（integration sites）分类法**：组件间集成（inter-component）、系统-环境集成（system-environment）、多系统集成（multi-system），分别对应不同的系统边界、中介机制与责任主体。
- **提炼出四项审计功能与三项集成专属质量属性**：功能为风险探索、风险确定、协调、程序规范性；专属质量为兼容性（compatibility, 10.3%）、完整性（completeness, 8.6%）、监督性（oversight, 6.9%）——这三者正是传统组件级评测无法覆盖的。
- **识别传统审计期望在 AI 中的三大落差**：缺乏独立外部审计者、缺乏统一标准/认证、缺乏系统级证据，并指出信息与资源访问权是制约审计设计的核心瓶颈。

## Key Contributions

- **First scoping review to formalize system integration audits for AI.** Drawing on aerospace/medical-device audit regimes (SAE ARP4754B, ISO 13485, AS9100), the authors argue model-centric audits cannot capture failures that emerge from component, environment, and multi-system interactions, and consolidate the fragmented literature on this gap.
- **Reproducible PRISMA-ScR methodology with transparent screening.** Six databases (Scopus, IEEE Xplore, ProQuest, Web of Science, EBSCOhost, WorldCat) plus 77 expert-added documents yield 4,259 → 58 documents; screening used dual independent reviewers on Covidence with an 82% inter-coder agreement on the first 50 documents.
- **A three-site taxonomy of where integration audits should occur** — inter-component (data→model→output, 87.9% of docs engage data/input), system-environment (deployment + ecosystem, found in 51.7%/77.6% of docs), and multi-system (upstream-to-downstream supply chain, predominantly GPAI) — each with distinct boundaries, intermediaries, and accountable roles.
- **Synthesis of four audit functions and three integration-specific qualities.** The functions (risk exploration "guiding", risk determination "concretizing", coordination "organizing", procedural regularity "stabilizing") and the qualities (compatibility, completeness, oversight) distinguish integration audits from generic responsible-AI evaluations.
- **Empirical diagnosis of how AI "audits" fall short of traditional audit legitimacy**, quantifying the absence of independent auditors, established standards, and system-level access — and framing GPAI supply-chain integration as the central under-addressed risk.

## Methodology

This is a **scoping review** following the PRISMA-ScR (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews) protocol (Tricco et al. 2018). The goal is a landscape overview rather than a quality appraisal.

- **Search strategy.** Six computing and social-science databases were queried in mid–late November 2024, restricted to title/abstract/keyword fields. Queries required at least one AI term ("artificial intelligence", "ai", "machine learning", "ml", "algorithmic") within a 3-word proximity of at least one audit term ("audit", "auditing", "auditor"). The exact phrase "system integration auditing" could not be searched directly (term not used systematically), so the authors instead harvested broad "auditing" literature and then narrowed. Initial hits: 4,182 (Scopus 1,086; IEEE Xplore 1,018; ProQuest 744; Web of Science 673; EBSCOhost 615; WorldCat 46) plus 77 manually added from the authors' collection → **4,259 documents**.
- **Eligibility criteria.** (1) Reference to audits (proposing/conducting/assisting/reviewing an audit or audit artifact; pure evaluations/benchmarks excluded unless they mentioned auditing); (2) AI/ML must be the object audited; (3) the audit must examine integration/interaction/interconnections beyond the model; (4) publication type = articles, book chapters, white papers, preprints (ISO/IEEE standards excluded for inaccessibility); (5) English only. No date or geographic restrictions.
- **Screening.** Performed on Covidence. Duplicates (1,443) and retracted (25) auto-removed; two reviewers voted yes/no per document; "maybe" or mismatched votes were resolved by all three reviewers (met every 100 documents). Final corpus: **n = 58**.
- **Analysis.** Reflexive thematic analysis in ATLAS.ti. One-third of the corpus was randomly sampled and independently coded by all reviewers to build an initial coding tree; reviewers met every 3–5 documents to reorganize codes. Mandated code groups covered definitions of "audit" and "system integration", system type/application area, AI elements implicated, roles, qualities assessed, and outputs/functions. The lead reviewer completed a second coding round.
- **Framing device.** Three research questions structure the review: RQ1 (where integration sites emerge), RQ2 (roles, qualities evaluated, outputs/functions — including sub-questions RQ2.1–2.3), and RQ3 (institutionalizing factors). The Boeing 737 MAX 8 crashes (346 fatalities) serve as the motivating running example of integration-fault consequences.

## Results & Highlights

This is a scoping review, so its quantitative results are descriptive statistics about the 58-document corpus (screened from 4,259). Key concrete claims copied from the paper:

- **Corpus yield and growth:** From **4,259 screened documents, only 58** met the system-integration-audit criteria. Publication is accelerating — **2024 alone accounts for 29.3%** of the corpus (the largest share), with a steady annual increase since the earliest 2019 entry, yet **65.5% of documents merely conceptualize or propose** an audit approach rather than apply one (spanning 28+ unique proposed approaches).
- **Integration-specific qualities are rare vs. component-level metrics:** The three qualities unique to integration — **compatibility (10.3% of docs), completeness (8.6%), and oversight (6.9%)** — each appear in under 10% of the corpus, while conventional model-level qualities dominate: **technical performance 69.0%, transparency 56.9%, accountability 48.3%, robustness 48.3%, lack of bias 44.8%**. The authors identify **33 distinct qualities** overall (average 7 per document, max 18).
- **GPAI — the paper's central concern — is empirically thin in the corpus:** Generative/multimodal/GPAI systems together account for only **29.3%** of audited systems, and external-citing prior work (Rismani et al. 2025) reports **57.3% of responsible-AI measures target the model while only 3% target the full system** — a ~54-percentage-point gap illustrating the component-level dominance the review critiques.
- **Audit legitimacy gaps relative to traditional audit conditions:** Fewer than **5% of documents reference an established standard or certification**, fewer than half (44.8%) even specify an explicit auditor role, and **65.5% distribute joint audit responsibilities** across multiple actors (averaging 3 roles/document) — meaning the "auditor effectively becomes the auditee." Nearly **75% use an internal auditing approach** constrained by access, and only **25.9% involve external vendors**.
- **Output and role distributions:** Outputs are dominated by **quantitative metrics (53.4%)** and **descriptive statements (41.4%)**, with **binary/discrete pass-fail outcomes in 34.5%**. Roles: developers 46.6%, auditors 44.8%, domain experts 44.8%, end-users 39.7% (end-users lead auditing in 39.7% of docs), policymakers 17.2%, external vendors 25.9%, management only 10.3%.
- **Geographic/application spread:** Healthcare dominates application areas at **34.5%** (safety-critical nature), hiring 12.1%, with FRT/tech-services/advertising at 3.4% each; **31.0% are application-agnostic**. Audit conduct spans China, India, New Zealand, Cyprus, the US, and Western Europe — though the authors flag Western/EU-AI-Act bias as a limitation.

No ML benchmark accuracy/F1 comparisons are reported (this is a literature review, not an empirical systems paper).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine and timely. "System integration audit" is not an established term in the AI literature (the authors note it "is rarely used in the AI auditing community"), so the three-site taxonomy (inter-component / system-environment / multi-system) and the integration-specific quality trio (compatibility, completeness, oversight) are a real conceptual contribution that bridges the systems-engineering audit tradition (Winter 2020; Madni & Sievers 2014) with AI governance.
- **Rigor (mixed):** PRISMA-ScR adherence, six databases, dual independent screening on Covidence, and an 82% inter-coder agreement are strengths. However, the **82% agreement is reported only for the first 50 documents**, after which "the lead reviewer then completed a second round of qualitative coding across the corpus" — so the bulk of the final corpus was effectively single-coder-coded, which weakens the reliability claims for the thematic synthesis. The exclusion of **ISO and IEEE standards "due to inaccessibility or underdevelopment"** is a notable gap precisely because integration audits in the paper's anchor domains (aerospace, medical devices) are heavily standards-based; omitting them risks understating how mature the comparable practice already is.
- **Reproducibility:** Good for the search (queries + included-docs list are published) but weaker for the coding: no published codebook, and single-coder dominance after the first 50 makes re-deriving the 33-quality / 4-function taxonomy hard to verify exactly.
- **Significance:** The mismatch between the paper's framing (GPAI supply-chain integration as the central risk) and its empirical base is a real tension — **only 29.3% of the audited systems are GPAI/generative/multimodal**, and the multi-system site (where GPAI lives) was "much harder to identify" integration risks within; rarely did providers and integrators work together to produce a complete supply-chain trace. So the strongest empirical material actually comes from downstream task-specific systems (healthcare 34.5%), not from the GPAI case the introduction motivates.
- **Generalizability / red flags:** English-only inclusion and heavy reliance on the EU AI Act / OECD principles explicitly constrain representation of Global South deployments (the authors acknowledge this). Because **65.5% of the corpus is proposals rather than applied audits**, many reported percentages describe aspirations, not field practice — a caveat the paper flags but readers may overweight. The definition of "audit" is deliberately permissive (evaluations were excluded "unless they mentioned auditing"), so some included documents are arguably evaluations relabeled as audits, which both inflates the corpus and supports the paper's own critique that "system integration audits in AI more closely resemble evaluations than formal audits."

## Strengths

- Conceptually bridges a mature non-AI field (aerospace/medical-device system integration audits) with an under-developed AI practice, using the Boeing 737 MAX 8 failures as a vivid, concrete anchor.
- Transparent, PRISMA-ScR-compliant methodology with published search queries and included-document lists — a higher methodological standard than most AI-conference survey papers.
- The three-site taxonomy and the four-function / three-quality synthesis give practitioners an immediately usable map of *where* to audit, *who* should be involved, and *what* to measure beyond accuracy.
- Intellectually honest about the field's immaturity: explicitly states most "audits" lack independence, standards, and system-level access, and that they "more closely resemble evaluations than formal audits."

## Limitations

- **Single-coder dominance:** inter-coder agreement is only reported for the first 50 documents; the lead reviewer coded the remainder alone, reducing reliability of the thematic counts.
- **Standards excluded:** ISO and IEEE standards were dropped, under-representing the very audit regimes the paper holds up as the gold standard.
- **English-only and Western/legal bias:** Global South deployments and non-English work are under-represented; the EU AI Act and OECD principles dominate the regulatory framing.
- **Proposal-heavy corpus:** ~65.5% of included documents are conceptual, so the statistics describe the literature's aspirations more than practiced audits.
- **Permissive "audit" definition:** including evaluations that merely "mention auditing" risks conflating evaluation with audit — though the authors turn this into a finding rather than hiding it.
- **Empirical–framing mismatch:** the paper's GPAI-centric narrative rests on a corpus where GPAI systems are a minority (29.3%) and multi-system integration risks were the hardest to identify.

## Relevance

该综述对关注 AI 安全、可问责性与治理评估的研究者高度相关：它明确指出当前 LLM/GPAI 评测生态以模型为中心，缺乏对"系统集成"层（API 接入、上下游供应链、部署环境交互）的审计方法，正好补充了纯 benchmark 评测的盲区，对从事大模型风险评估、红队与合规审计的工作有直接的方法论参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How would the three-site taxonomy apply to a concrete modern LLM stack (foundation-model API → RAG/orchestration layer → tool-using agent → end-user UI)? Which of compatibility/completeness/oversight are measurable today, and with what artifacts?
- Can the multi-system integration site ever be audited without mandatory upstream-provider access? What minimum disclosure obligations (model cards, system cards, change logs) would make a Hopkins-style supply-chain-graph audit (Hopkins et al. 2025) tractable across organizational boundaries?
- How do the findings change if ISO/IEEE standards and non-English/Global-South literature are included — would the "fewer than 5% reference a standard" finding shrink, and would the three-site taxonomy still hold?
- The paper recommends "design ethnography" to study practitioner adoption; what longitudinal evidence would distinguish an audit that improved integration safety from one that performed accountability without reducing risk?
- Given that 65.5% of the corpus is proposals, what would a comparative empirical benchmark of *applied* integration audits (e.g., MDSAP-style medical-device audits translated to clinical AI) reveal about which integration-specific qualities actually predict downstream failures?
