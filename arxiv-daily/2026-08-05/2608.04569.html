Relevant but Incomplete: Referential Dangling as a Paradigm-Level Failure Mode in Hard Prompt Compression

The headline claim

Independent scoring keeps a relevant span but severs the dependencies needed to interpret it — a referential dangling failure built into the paradigm.

read this as The answer string survives compression, but the bridge that gives it meaning is gone — and no scoring signal warns you.

§ 01

How they nailed it down

01

Formalize the failure

Model compressors as additive selectors S ∈ argmax u(x). Proposition 1: with fragments {x, d, z} and budget 2m, the maximizer {x,z} dangles even though feasible {x,d} is complete — no interaction term rewards joint retention.

02

Measure prevalence

Proxy exact retention with directional content-word coverage at θ=0.5. Robustness sweeps: encoder swap (Qwen3-0.6B→GPT-2), θ 0.3–0.7, ratio r 0.20–0.70.

03

Fixed-budget reselection

On each dangling example: reinsert the omitted support paragraph, free tokens by dropping low-scoring non-support. Reselected contexts are actually shorter than Base (−1.5% to −9.6%) — so gains are not a budget artifact.

04

Automatic restoration

A bert-base-uncased classifier (~110M) ranks omitted sentences by whether retained text needs them; top-K=3 reinserted. Trained on 7,565 HotpotQA pairs (dev F1 0.82), strictly disjoint from eval.

§ 03

Two ways to read it

+

What holds up

  • Structural, not cosmetic. Proposition 1 proves the additive objective — not any one scoring signal — is the root cause.
  • Six-compressor sweep spanning chunk/parse/sentence/token granularity makes the "paradigm-level" claim credible.
  • Statistical discipline: McNemar + Holm correction, bootstrap CIs, three robustness sweeps, 95% manual-precision audit.
  • Controlled existence proof: reselection recovers the lost accuracy at the same budget, separating "what to fix" from "how to fix it."
  • Honest upper bound: injecting the gold sentence hits +8.0 pts — pinpoints candidate construction as the bottleneck.
!

Where it frays

  • Automatic remedy is Beaver-only. Transfer to PartPrompt (+3.2), Selective-Context (+1.0), LLMLingua-2 (+1.0) all non-significant (Table 13).
  • Matched-addition control misses significance (p=0.15): targeted vs. random insertion not formally distinguished at this sample size.
  • All reselection/restoration numbers rely on annotated multi-hop QA — un-annotated real-world retrieval & non-QA tasks untested.
  • Proxy metric: content-word coverage at θ=0.5 only approximates Equation (1); LongBench-v2 first-mention denominator ≠ ρ_d.
  • Whole-sentence reinsertion is incompatible with token-level compressor output without altering their objective.
  • Prop. 1 is an existence result for the exact additive class — does not prove every observed case is caused by additivity.
§ 04

What's still open

Q · 01
Can a dependency-preservation constraint (à la Durrett et al. 2016 anaphoricity) be added directly to the additive objective — closing the gap more cleanly than post-hoc reinsertion?
Q · 02
Why does the same restoration config fail to transfer from Beaver to token-level compressors — candidate generator, classifier distribution, or incoherence of inserting whole sentences?
Q · 03
Does dangling generalize beyond multi-hop QA — to open-domain summarization, agentic tool-use, code retrieval where no gold support exists?
Q · 04
At larger sample size: is targeted restoration truly superior to random insertion, or is the gain mostly "more sentences" rather than "the right sentences"?
Q · 05
Can coreference signals computed on the retained subset predict which omitted spans are needed — removing the dependence on HotpotQA-trained heuristics?
Q · 06
How does referential dangling interact with "lost in the middle" positional bias? A reinserted bridge could fail for positional, not referential, reasons.
