01 / METHOD

How “over-inference” is measured

Given 3 revealed facts E about a user, the model performs 6 personalization tasks. Every claim in its response is classified into one of four categories; the last two jointly constitute over-inference (OI):

Groundedrestates evidence
Reasonableone common-sense step
Stereotypesubstitutes group priors
Fabricatedno evidential basis

OI = (Stereotype + Fabricated) / Total  ·  Personas balanced 50/50/50 stereotypical / counter / neutral — the counter set acts as a stress test.

4
Accum8-round pilot — does the profile grow monotonically across turns?

03 / ASSESSMENT

Strengths & limitations, side by side

Strengths PRO

  • Human-validated judge. κ = 0.900 (binary) on 400 stratified claims; 71% of residual disagreements are adjacent-class — leaderboard has real teeth.
  • Four-way taxonomy beats a binary hallucinated/not split; separates invention from stereotyping.
  • Actionable scope split. Self-audit is fine for internal claim ranking, dangerous for cross-model selection — a clean practitioner takeaway.
  • Counter-stereotypical personas are substantive: a 7.8 pp OI gap appears in all 12 models individually, not a checkbox.
  • Honest limitations (Appendix A) + verbatim prompts and exact API snapshots (Appendix G) make it reproducible.

Limitations CON

  • Single judge, single annotator. No CI on κ; no inter-annotator agreement. A second judge is "left to future work."
  • Statistically fragile headline. ρ = −0.60 from n = 12 correlated families; bootstrap CI spans zero — abstract still foregrounds the point estimate.
  • Accum is a pilot: 2 personas, retention-biased memory prompt ("don't remove unless contradicted") — absolute counts are indicative, not definitive.
  • No mitigation tested, no downstream impact on recommendation quality or user satisfaction — phenomenon characterized, not fixed.
  • Evidence-only scoring inflates OI on presupposition-heavy tasks; no |E| ablation to test whether more facts shrink the 41.6%.
