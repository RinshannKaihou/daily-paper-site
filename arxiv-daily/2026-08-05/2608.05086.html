Item Response Theory for AI Safety

Joshua Fonseca Rivera · Neil Shah  (equal first-author, independent) · David Demitri Africa · Konstantinos Voudouris  (equal senior, UK AI Security Institute)
Key Finding

Three psychometric factors explain 77% of safety-benchmark variance — and ~75 items (2% of the suite) recover model rankings at ρ ≥ 0.92.

Why This Matters
IRT lets evaluators run trustworthy safety tests at a fraction of the cost — and catch silent sandbagging or hidden model swaps behind an API.
→ safety leaderboards · API auditing · frontier-lab eval
Critical Edge
Sandbagging detection is tested only on prompted model organisms — fine-tuned or adversarially trained sandbaggers could plausibly evade person-fit while keeping a plausible score.

01

Methodology

One calibration · four stages

A regularized 2PL IRT model is fit to 5,067 items × 192 chat models (1B–~1T, plus GPT/Claude/Gemini/Grok APIs) — the largest psychometric fit of LLM safety benchmarks to date. The 2PL beat 1PL/3PL/4PL on all 8 benchmarks by likelihood-ratio, AIC, BIC.

01 / CALIBRATE
Fit 2PL
One zero-temp response per model–item via OpenRouter, scored by each benchmark's judge. P=σ(a(θ−b)); priors lift split-half discrimination retention 28%→68%.
▸
02 / READ
Factor structure
Min-residual factor analysis, oblimin rotation, on the 134×8 ability matrix. Confirmed by unconstrained MIRT (App. C) and permutation parallel analysis.
▸
03 / REDUCE
Distill tests
Static 25-item tests (max Fisher info) + CAT adaptive selection. Held-out 75/25 × 20 splits vs size-matched random subsets.
▸
04 / AUDIT
Black-box ladder
Person-fit l_z → ability-shift → response-fingerprint. Each rung catches failure modes invisible to the prior one.

  • 77%variance · CFI=0.97
    Three factors explain between-model variance; 1-factor explains only 47% (RMSEA=0.32). Horn's parallel analysis retains three.
  • ρ=0.9475-item static
    Three fixed 25-item tests recover all three latent abilities with lower error than size-matched random subsets (<2% of suite).
  • −18 to −34%RMSE vs random · 10 items
    ~10 CAT items cut individual-benchmark cost 97–99% on HarmBench/SORRY-Bench/OR-Bench-Hard; advantage narrows to 4–21% at 25 items.
  • 94–97%combined detection
    Person-fit + ability-shift catch sandbagging in every scope. Person-fit alone: 80–100% of selective, only 35% pooled of uniform.
  • +1.69 Δθlogits · median · uniform
    Uniform sandbagging shifts ability far more than selective scopes (+0.24–+0.35) — the 40-item ability check flags 84% of uniform runs.
  • 13 / 14endpoint IDs matched
    Response fingerprint on 500 items distinguishes 99.5% of 8,911 model pairs; one real endpoint drifted TruthfulQA 33.6% vs 2.0% resampling null.

▲ Strengths

  • Unifies read / reduce / audit under one calibrated model, with a clean Figure-1 narrative.
  • Honest about scope: distilled tests don't extend the benchmark or stay informative for future models.
  • Audit ladder rungs are complementary — person-fit vs ability-shift shown quantitatively (94–97% combined).
  • Real-world API drift (33.6% vs 2.0% null) gives the methods concrete teeth.
  • Rigorous 2PL + factor-count selection; conclusions survive an unconstrained MIRT refit.

▼ Limitations

  • Only 134–169 models for cross-benchmark work — BIC prefers D=2 in the direct MIRT fit (genuine ambiguity).
  • Sandbagging tested on prompted interventions only; fine-tuned evaders may defeat person-fit.
  • Reduced tests reproduce the existing benchmark — no evidence it predicts deployment safety.
  • Re-collection won't reproduce the matrix: judge drift (BLEURT-20) + OpenRouter routing instability.
  • Factor labels are descriptive summaries, not convergently validated constructs.
  • Fingerprint null calibration is judge-dependent — needs per-judge recalibration.

Open Questions

  1. Do person-fit and ability-shift survive against fine-tuned or adversarially-trained sandbaggers minimizing l_z?
  2. Does the three-factor structure replicate on XSTest, WildJailbreak, multilingual sets — and stay stable as frontier models improve?
  3. How fast do the 75 distilled items go stale, and what is the cheapest valid recalibration cadence?
  4. Can response-fingerprint resist an adversary who holds the pinned reference responses?
  5. What data adjudicates D=2 vs D=3 — more models, or more over-refusal/truthfulness indicators?
  6. Do the audit tools generalize to capability benchmarks (reasoning, code, math)?
