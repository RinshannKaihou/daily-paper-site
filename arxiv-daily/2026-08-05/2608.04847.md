> **一句话总结：** 作者构建了 Slang-Q——一个包含 118 个酷儿俚语词条分类体系与 1,024 条带参考释义的用户生成例句数据集，并以定义生成任务评测 4 个 LLM（Claude Sonnet 4.6、Qwen3 32B、LLaMA 4 Scout、LLaMA 3.3 70B），发现所有模型均低于人类上界（ROUGE-L 0.48 vs 模型最高 0.18），而明确的"酷儿俚语专家"提示与例句上下文能显著减少错误（Claude 在加入上下文后手动正确率从 70% 提升至 100%）。

> **TL;DR:** The paper introduces **Slang-Q**, a manually curated dataset of 1,024 English user-generated sentences paired with 118 queer-slang terms and reference definitions (built atop Urban Dictionary data from the SLANG benchmark), and uses a definition-generation task to evaluate four LLMs (Claude Sonnet 4.6, Qwen3 32B, LLaMA 4 Scout, LLaMA 3.3 70B) under four prompting conditions. All models score well below the human upper bound on ROUGE-L (best model 0.18 vs human 0.48) while clustering tightly on BERTScore (~0.86 vs 0.91); the Term-only baseline is consistently worst, and slang-informed prompting plus sentential context drives Claude's manual correctness from 70% to 100% on the sampled subset.

## 核心贡献

- **发布 Slang-Q 数据集**：首个面向酷儿（LGBTQIA+）俚语理解的人工标注评测集，包含 1,024 条来自 Urban Dictionary 的真实用户生成英文句子，每条配对匹配术语与人工撰写的参考定义，并公开于 https://github.com/ariadne28/Slang-Q。
- **构建 118 词酷儿术语分类体系（taxonomy）**：综合 NLP 文献、Wikipedia LGBTQ 俚语分类、Lexicon Library.LGBT、Wiktionary 等多源整理，设计两层结构——3 个主类目（Identity 身份、Slang 俚语、Intersectional 交叉性）与 5 个子类目（Reclaimed 重命名 reclaimed、Shorthand 缩略、Idiomatic expression 惯用表达、Pronoun 新代词、Spelling variation 拼写变体）。
- **首个针对"模型对酷儿俚语知道什么"的探索性评测**：将 queer NLP 的关注点从偏见/危害检测转向模型能否准确理解社群专属语义，作为定义生成任务在 4 种提示条件下系统对比 4 个商用/开源 LLM。
- **严谨的评测方法学**：采用多参考 ROUGE-L + BERTScore（人工校验的 GPT-5.5 同义改写作为额外参考），先用长度扰动验证两指标行为，再做术语级聚合以避免高频词偏差；并用 Data Contamination Quiz (DCQ) 量化各模型对源数据的verbatim 记忆。
- **配套人工错误分析**：对 Claude 与 Llama 4 在两个对照条件下共 200 条输出做 Correct / Partially correct / Incorrect 三级人工标注，揭示自动指标无法捕捉的语义偏差（如多义词默认义项、幻觉词源、过度解释上下文）。

## Key Contributions

- **Slang-Q dataset**: A manually curated, publicly released evaluation set of 1,024 English user-generated sentences, each paired with a matched queer slang term and a gold reference definition, mined from the Urban Dictionary corpus of the SLANG benchmark.
- **A 118-term queer taxonomy**: A two-level inventory (3 main categories — Identity / Slang / Intersectional; 5 optional subcategories — Reclaimed, Shorthand, Idiomatic expression, Pronoun, Spelling variation) aggregated from NLP papers, Wikipedia's LGBTQ slang category, Lexicon Library.LGBT, and Wiktionary, with 13 terms added bottom-up during annotation.
- **First evaluation of what LLMs *know* about queer slang**: Reframes queer NLP away from bias/harm detection toward probing model knowledge of community-specific semantics, as a definition-generation task across four prompting conditions (term-only vs. +context × baseline vs. slang-informed).
- **Rigorous evaluation methodology**: Multi-reference ROUGE-L and BERTScore against three references (gold + two GPT-5.5 paraphrases of decreasing length, manually validated), with a length-perturbation validation step showing ROUGE-L drops 0.627→0.386 while BERTScore stays ~0.92; term-level aggregation prevents frequent-term bias.
- **Data-contamination audit**: Uses the Data Contamination Quiz (DCQ) black-box framework to report verbatim memorization ranges per model, exposing that Claude Sonnet 4.6 shows 57.30–62.00% contamination vs. 0.00% for Qwen3-32B.
- **Human error analysis**: 200 manually labeled outputs (Correct / Partially correct / Incorrect) contrasting Claude and Llama 4, surfacing qualitative failures (default-to-dominant sense for polysemous terms like *bear* and *ace*, hallucinated etymologies, missed reclaimed/AAVE/Latinx nuances) that automatic similarity scores conceal.

## Methodology

**Data construction (Slang-Q).** The authors start from the 185,795 Urban Dictionary entries released by the SLANG benchmark (Mei et al., 2024) — chosen for being noisy, naturally informal, and permissively licensed — and apply a four-step pipeline: (i) compile a 118-term queer inventory from multiple sources (46 of 57 terms from Tint 2025, 24 from Ramesh et al. 2022, plus Wikipedia, Lexicon Library.LGBT, Wiktionary, and 13 bottom-up additions); (ii) string-match extract 3,184 candidate sentences; (iii) two-level manual annotation by the first author (a digital-humanities specialist in fandom/queer internet vernacular), checking semantic relevance to the intended queer sense and discarding harmful/vulgar content; (iv) assign each term at least one main category and, optionally, subcategories. This leaves 1,024 valid entries covering 77 of the 118 terms (65.25%); 1,833 (57.57%) candidates were discarded as unrelated and 327 (10.27%) as harmful.

**Task framing.** Evaluation is cast as definition generation: the model must produce a 1–3 sentence English definition. Four conditions result from crossing two dimensions — *context* (term-only vs. term + example sentence) and *framing* (generic "language expert" baseline vs. "queer internet slang expert"). All conditions use a one-shot exemplar consistent with the framing.

**Models.** Four instruction-following LLMs: Claude Sonnet 4.6 (proprietary, via Anthropic API) and three open-weight via Groq — Qwen3 32B, LLaMA 4 Scout, LLaMA 3.3 70B — selected to span families and recency.

**Metrics.** Multi-reference ROUGE-L (lexical/LCS overlap) and BERTScore F1 (contextual embedding similarity). Three references per term: the human gold definition plus the two shorter GPT-5.5-generated alternatives (the longest alternative was dropped as redundant). Final scores are *term-level aggregated* (average over sentences per term, then over terms) so each term contributes equally. A length-perturbation validation on the 118 gold definitions first confirms the expected behaviour: ROUGE-L is length-sensitive (0.627 → 0.438 → 0.386 across decreasing lengths), BERTScore is more stable (0.924 → 0.905 → 0.896).

**Contamination check.** The Data Contamination Quiz administers a multiple-choice task (Bias Detector Quiz + Bias Compensator Quiz to control positional bias) on 100 subsampled instances, perturbations generated by GPT-5.5 and manually validated.

**Manual evaluation.** 200 outputs (50 per model per condition) for Claude Sonnet 4.6 and Llama 4 Scout under the two most contrasting conditions (Term baseline vs. slang-informed Context), labeled Correct / Partially correct / Incorrect.

## Results & Highlights

- **All four models fall well below the human upper bound on ROUGE-L**: human reference-vs-reference ROUGE-L is **0.48 ± 0.13**, while every model in every condition lands in the **0.14–0.18** range on the Slang-Q test set (Table 5). The gap is far smaller on BERTScore F1 (**0.85–0.86** for all models vs **0.91 ± 0.03** human), indicating outputs are semantically close but lexically divergent.
- **Term-only baseline is consistently the worst condition**: e.g., LLaMA 4 Scout drops to **0.14 ± 0.06** ROUGE-L in the Term baseline and rises to **0.17 ± 0.05** with slang-informed framing; Qwen3 32B goes from **0.16** (Term) to **0.18** (Context baseline). Adding either slang-informed framing or sentence context improves scores, confirming both explicit domain orientation and contextual grounding help converge on the queer-specific meaning.
- **No model dominates**: across all 8 model×metric cells the spread is within roughly 0.02 ROUGE-L, despite very different training-data exposure (per DCQ, Claude Sonnet 4.6 shows **57.30%–62.00%** verbatim contamination vs **0.00%–0.00%** for Qwen3-32B, **10.53%–15.00%** for LLaMA 3.3 70B, **10.75%–17.00%** for LLaMA 4 Scout). High contamination does not translate into a definition-generation advantage because evaluation is against human-written definitions, not Urban Dictionary entries.
- **Intersectional terms score lowest across both metrics** (terms circulating across queer, AAVE, and fandom communities such as *slay, tea*), while Slang and Identity terms score comparably higher — attributed to the inherent ambiguity of intersectional items.
- **Manual evaluation shows context nearly eliminates errors**: in the Term baseline, Claude produces **70% correct / 4% partial / 26% incorrect** and Llama 4 produces **64% / 8% / 28%**; under slang-informed Context, Claude reaches **100% correct** and Llama 4 reaches **90% correct / 10% partial** with **0% fully incorrect** — a sharp, quantified demonstration that sentential context plus domain framing closes most of the gap.
- **Annotation yield is low and skewed**: of 3,184 candidate sentences, only **1,024** survive (1,833 discarded as semantically unrelated, 327 as harmful), and only **77 of 118 terms (65.25%)** actually appear in valid examples — coverage is dominated by a handful of frequent terms (*goat, slay, bae, trans, gurl*).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Genuine and well-motivated. Queer NLP has overwhelmingly studied slang as a *bias variable* (e.g., Tint 2025 showing queer slang induces more negative LLM emotional outputs); this paper instead probes *what models know*. It is, however, conceptually close to the SLAyiNG dataset (Veloso et al. 2025), which frames queer slang as sense disambiguation — but SLAyiNG is not public, giving Slang-Q a concrete填补 gap.
- **Rigor**: The multi-reference metric design with a length-perturbation pre-validation is thoughtful and directly addresses the "ROUGE-L hates paraphrases" critique. Term-level aggregation to neutralize the heavy frequency skew (a handful of terms like *slay, goat, bae* dominate) is the right call. However, the **single-annotator setup** (first author alone, with ad-hoc discussion of "fewer ambiguous cases") means inter-annotator agreement is not reported and the harmful/unrelated discard decisions are not independently auditable — a notable weakness for a resource whose value hinges on label quality.
- **Reproducibility**: Strong on the data/PROMPT side (public GitHub repo, model versions and APIs named, perturbation prompts in Appendix C), but **Groq default parameters** and a single proprietary model behind an API (Claude Sonnet 4.6) make exact numerical reproduction fragile over time.
- **Significance**: Modest but real. The headline finding — "all four models cluster tightly and well below human on ROUGE-L, while context+framing nearly eliminates fully-incorrect outputs" — is a useful, publishable empirical anchor, but the effect is demonstrated on a **small model sample (4 LLMs)** and a **single task (definition generation)**, so generalization to dialogue/translation/summarization is untested.
- **Red flags / specific weaknesses**:
  - The **human "upper bound" is computed as the gold definition scored against only the two shorter GPT-5.5 paraphrases** (Table 5 caption), not as a true human-vs-human definition agreement — so the 0.48 ROUGE-L / 0.91 BERTScore reference line is more a paraphrase-similarity ceiling than a multi-annotator bound, and the headline "models below human" claim should be read with that caveat.
  - **BERTScore saturates** (all models within 0.85–0.86, within one another's std) and is essentially uninformative for discrimination; the paper still reports it as a co-equal metric.
  - The DCQ contamination result is interesting but the paper does not statistically test whether Claude's 2× higher ROUGE-L rank in some cells is linked to its 60% contamination — a missed analysis.
  - **Coverage is thin**: 41 of 118 taxonomy terms have zero sentences, so more than a third of the published "resource" is unverifiable on the task; the skewed long-tail means low-frequency, novel, or morphologically derived slang — exactly the cases the manual analysis flags as hardest — are barely represented.
  - Manual evaluation samples only **50 outputs × 2 models × 2 conditions = 200 items**, with no confidence intervals; the dramatic jump to "100% correct" for Claude is on n=50 and is presented without uncertainty.

## Strengths

- Cleanly motivated gap in queer NLP — first to treat queer slang as an *object of model knowledge* rather than a bias trigger.
- Two-level taxonomy (Identity / Slang / Intersectional × Reclaimed / Shorthand / Idiomatic / Pronoun / Spelling variation) is a useful community contribution beyond the paper's experiments.
- Methodologically careful on metric design: multi-reference scoring, length-perturbation validation, term-level aggregation, and a black-box contamination audit are all non-trivial and well executed for a resource paper.
- Public release of data, taxonomy, prompts, and manual annotations.
- The category-level finding (intersectional terms hardest because of cross-community polysemy) is genuinely insightful and connects to AAVE/fandom overlap noted in the literature.
- Honest about limitations — the conclusion explicitly frames the work as a first-stage resource/analysis with delimited scope.

## Limitations

- **English-only and Urban-Dictionary-only**: queer slang varies substantially across languages, cultures, and platforms; the single noisy source constrains generalization.
- **Single annotator**, no inter-annotator agreement, conservative but ad-hoc harmfulness filtering.
- **Single task** (definition generation); no probing of dialogue, translation, summarization, or retrieval.
- **Small model sample** (4 LLMs) and BERTScore saturation limit metric discriminability.
- **Taxonomy and definitions are a 2026 snapshot**; the authors flag that Lexicon Library.LGBT was discontinued in July 2026 and community lexicons are inherently dynamic, so category assignments and gold definitions will drift.
- **Coverage gaps**: 41/118 terms have no sentence; frequency distribution is highly skewed toward a few very common terms.
- **Verbatim memorization ≠ task leakage**: the paper is careful to note this, but does not fully rule out that Claude's API-side exposure to similar online content (reflected in 60% DCQ) inflates its surface-level ROUGE-L.

## Relevance

对关注 LLM 安全性、文化/社群语言理解、以及评测方法学的研究方向高度相关：Slang-Q 提供了一个少见的"小众但真实"的评测探针，可用于检验模型对边缘社群语言的处理能力，并为"提示工程 + 上下文消歧"在多大程度上能弥补预训练数据偏差提供了量化证据，与偏见评测、对齐以及对抗性 prompt 研究均有交叉点。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does performance change on **multilingual queer slang** (e.g., Italian, Portuguese, Mandarin queer sociolects), and does cross-lingual transfer help or hurt?
- Would a **true multi-annotator upper bound** (independent human definitions, not GPT-5.5 paraphrases) change the size of the reported human–model gap?
- Can the intersectional-category deficit be reduced via **retrieval-augmented generation** over community-maintained lexicons, and does that introduce new bias or stereotype risks?
- Does the 60% DCQ contamination in Claude Sonnet 4.6 translate into measurably higher **memorized-definition leakage** vs. genuinely compositional understanding, if evaluated against held-out paraphrases?
- How do the models behave on **emerging/novel slang absent from any 2024 training cut-off**, and can Slang-Q be expanded into a longitudinal benchmark with yearly refreshes?
- Do the same prompting-condition effects hold for **downstream tasks** (slang-aware dialogue, MT, content moderation) where the cost of incorrect sense selection is higher than a definition match?
