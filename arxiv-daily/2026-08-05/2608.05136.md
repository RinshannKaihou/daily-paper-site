> **一句话总结：** 本文证明分解模型 W=UV^T 的损失函数具有规范对称性（gauge symmetry），而梯度下降、Muon、Shampoo 等满足规范等变的优化器能继承其隐式低秩偏好，Adam 等逐坐标方法则因读取"基"而在第一步就破坏该偏好；在矩阵感知、注意力头孪生实验和高光谱图像补全三类任务中，等变方法在匹配训练损失下分别把恢复误差/保留误差降低数十个百分点。

> **TL;DR:** The factored loss L(U,V)=f(UV^T) is invariant under the orthogonal gauge (U,V)→(UQ,VQ), but only gauge-equivariant optimizers (GD, momentum, shared-scalar Adam, Muon, Shampoo) inherit gradient flow's implicit low-rank bias; coordinate-wise methods (Adam, RMSProp, signSGD, Lion, Adafactor) break the gauge at the first step. On 40×40 matrix sensing, equivariant methods reach recovery error ≤0.286 while every coordinate-wise method sits ≥0.42; on two gauge-equivalent transformer copies Adam splits per-head W_Q^T W_K invariants by 56% at step one; on the Indian Pines and Pavia University hyperspectral benchmarks at matched training loss GD cuts held-out RMSE by 43–44% over Adam at the lowest sampling density, at much lower effective rank.

## 核心贡献

- 提出**规范等变（gauge equivariance）作为优化器是否保留分解模型隐式低秩偏好的判据**：损失对正交群 O(k) 作用不变（U,V)→(UQ,VQ)，凡是更新规则无法以相应状态映射 σ_Q 与之配套的优化器，就会"读取"一个任意选定的隐因子基，从而无法触及梯度流找到的低秩吸引子。
- 给出**结构定理（Theorem 4.5）**：无状态等变更新规则恰为形如 Δ=H(GG^T)G 的左预条件器（H 仅依赖规范不变的 Gram 矩阵）；并用**转移定理（Theorem 4.6）**把 Gunasekar/Arora/Li 等梯度流的逐路径结论平移到共享标量预条件流上。
- 用**"Adam-p 调节器"**（§7，单一参数 p∈[0,1]）在标准 Adam 的逐坐标分母和单一标量分母之间连续插值：随 p 从 1 降到 0，恢复误差与有效秩单调改善，唯一变量就是分母的各向异性——把"逐坐标各向异性"分离为低秩偏好被侵蚀的唯一驱动因素。
- 证明**注意力头内禀存在同一规范**（W_Q^T W_K 扮演 UV^T 角色）；用"双胞胎协议"（gauge-twin / A=I-twin / noise-twin）证明 Adam 在第一步就让两个功能等价的 transformer 复本出现 56% 的 W_Q^T W_K 相对偏差，而等变方法停在浮点精度。
- 构造**谱尾相图**（§8）调和关于 Muon 的两份对立报告：精确低秩信号下 Muon 的"等速谱增长"恢复最优，但目标一旦有约 4% 谱尾能量，贪婪式 GD 调度反超——给出命题 8.1 的可解两时间尺度模型。
- 用**自身先前 FlowAdam 优化器的复盘**（§10）做"自我验证"：原版的逐坐标速度裁剪恰恰破坏了它要注入的规范偏置，改为全局范数裁剪 + p 调节器后，恢复误差从 0.573 降到 0.169，是 Adam 类变体中最接近 GD 的。

## Key Contributions

- **Gauge equivariance as a classifier of optimizers.** The factored objective L(U,V)=f(UV^T) is invariant under the orthogonal gauge (U,V)→(UQ,VQ); the paper proves (Prop 4.2/4.3) GD, Polyak/Nesterov momentum, shared-scalar Adam, Muon's matrix-sign update, and Shampoo's Kronecker preconditioner are equivariant, while Adam, RMSProp, signSGD, Lion, and Adafactor are not, because any entrywise nonlinearity at zero state breaks the gauge.
- **Exact structural and transfer theorems.** Theorem 4.5 characterizes memoryless equivariant rules exactly as left preconditioners Δ=H(GG^T)G whose input is the gauge-invariant Gram matrix. Theorem 4.6 (transfer) shows that a common-scalar-preconditioned flow is just gradient flow under a rescaled time parameter, so pathwise/limit-point results of Gunasekar et al. (2017), Arora et al. (2019), and Li et al. (2021) carry over directly under their original assumptions.
- **A one-parameter "Adam-p" dial that isolates the mechanism.** §7 smoothly morphs Adam's denominator from coordinate-wise (p=1) to a single shared scalar (p=0). Recovery error and effective rank improve monotonically along the dial with adaptivity, momentum, and noise held constant, proving that per-coordinate anisotropy is the single ingredient eroding the low-rank bias.
- **The gauge lives in attention heads.** Because each head's logits depend on W_Q,W_K only through W_Q^T W_K, every head carries the same internal gauge; a twin-protocol experiment shows Adam separates two gauge-equivalent copies of a transformer at step 1 while heavy-ball SGD and scalar-Adam stay at machine precision — and the gap (56% in W_Q^T W_K) is a downstream obstacle for alignment-based model merging.
- **Spectral-tail phase diagram reconciling two opposing Muon results.** §8 sweeps the tail-energy fraction of the target and locates a crossover near 4% tail energy (α≈0.2): Muon's equal-rate schedule is exact for purely low-rank targets but degrades fastest as the tail grows, beyond which GD's greedy schedule wins. Proposition 8.1 gives a closed-form two-timescale explanation.
- **Constructive self-validation on the authors' own FlowAdam optimizer.** Replacing the original coordinate-wise velocity clip (which the criterion proves breaks the gauge it meant to inject) with a global-norm clip and combining with the p-dial yields FlowAdam-p, the best Adam-type variant tested (recovery 0.169 vs Adam's 0.573), with effective rank 4.8–5.4 closest to GD's 4.51.

## Methodology

The paper combines proofs (Appendix B) with four controlled experimental pillars.

**Theory.** After fixing the orthogonal gauge as the maximal isometric subgroup of GL(k) (Lemma 3.2), the authors prove gradient covariance (Lemma 4.1), classify the equivariant class (Prop 4.2), prove that any coordinate-wise nonlinearity breaks equivariance (Prop 4.3), derive an exact expression for Adam's first-step gauge defect (Prop 4.4), give the structure theorem (Theorem 4.5), and the transfer theorem (Theorem 4.6). Proposition 8.1 analyzes greedy vs. equal-rate schedules on decoupled balanced scalar modes.

**Optimizer zoo (§5).** Nine update rules are run on underdetermined matrix sensing: plant X*=U*V*^T of rank r*=3 in n=40, sense with m=2·dof Gaussian measurements, full overparameterization (k=n), small init (10^-3), no weight decay, all stopped at interpolation (loss < 10^-7) to remove residual training error as a confound. Recovery is ‖W−X*‖_F/‖X*‖_F and effective rank is the exponential entropy of normalized singular values.

**Adam-p dial (§7).** Adam's denominator becomes (√v_t+ε)^p (s_t+ε)^(1−p), where s_t=√mean(v_hat) is pooled over both factors and hence gauge-invariant. p=1 recovers stock Adam, p=0 is scalar-Adam. Both a fixed-step arm (shared learning-rate grid) and an envelope arm (per-p best rate) are reported.

**Attention twins (§6).** Two 2-layer 4-head transformers (d=64) are trained on modular addition mod 47; the second has each head's (W_Q,W_K) rotated by a random orthogonal A_h at initialization, so both compute the identical function. Three twin types isolate the signal: gauge-twin (basis change, same function), A=I-twin (sanity check for determinism), and noise-twin (10^-7 same-basis perturbation, probes chaos). The setup is repeated on GPU float64 for mod 97 at 4 and 6 layers, and on a 6-layer character-level Shakespeare LM under minibatch noise.

**Spectral-tail sweep (§8).** Target X=√(1−α²) X_3 + α E_⊥ with X_3 rank-3 and E_⊥ a dense orthogonal-complement component, so α² is exactly the tail-energy fraction. Ten seeds, float64, both constant and cosine schedules.

**Real data (§9).** Rank-48 matrix completion on Indian Pines (200 AVIRIS bands) and Pavia University (103 ROSIS bands), no weight decay, four seeds, comparing methods only at matched training-loss levels to neutralize early-stopping artifacts; learning rates selected by a train-only criterion.

## Results & Highlights

- **On 40×40 rank-3 matrix sensing (Table 2), the two classes are cleanly separated.** Equivariant methods achieve recovery ≤0.286 (Muon 6.8×10^-6, GD 0.131, scalar-Adam 0.201, Shampoo 0.286), while every coordinate-wise method sits ≥0.42 (Lion 0.425, signum 0.445, RMSProp 0.527, Adafactor 0.543, Adam 0.573) — a 0.14-wide empty gap between best coordinate-wise and worst equivariant at this 2×10^4-step budget. The separation persists up to n=256 on a 10-seed ladder.
- **The Adam-p dial (Figure 3) is monotone.** Envelope recovery improves 0.570 (p=1, Adam) → 0.459 → 0.348 → 0.260 → 0.201 (p=0, scalar-Adam) and effective rank falls 14.5 → 11.1 → 8.2 → 6.4 → 5.4, bracketing GD's rank 4.51; the trend replicates at n=128 (recovery 0.67 → 0.40, rank 36.6 → 12.0, five seeds).
- **Adam separates gauge-equivalent attention heads at step 1.** After one Adam step, twin logit distance is 3.6×10^-3 vs 2.9×10^-7 for the noise twin at the same step (four orders of magnitude larger), saturating at 0.77; per-head W_Q^T W_K invariants end 56% apart in relative Frobenius distance. SGD (heavy-ball 0.9) and scalar-Adam twins stay at 2.9×10^-7 / 1.6×10^-7 at step 1. On GPU float64 mod-97, Adam's step-1 split is 6.5–8.2×10^-3 vs 2.8×10^-16–1.1×10^-15 for the equivariant methods.
- **The spectral-tail phase diagram (Table 4) shows Muon exact at α=0** (recovery 0.000 vs GD 0.112 vs Adam 0.542) but ceding to GD by α≈0.2 (4% tail energy: GD 0.351, Muon 0.354, Adam 0.597); Adam has the worst recovery at every α plotted.
- **On real hyperspectral data at matched training loss (Table 5), GD beats Adam in every seed of every cell.** Indian Pines: +43.0% RMSE reduction at m/dof=1.15 (GD 1.481 vs Adam 2.600, RMSE ×10^-2) and +27.6% at m/dof=1.9 (1.244 vs 1.718). Pavia University: +43.8% (0.819 vs 1.458) and +22.9% (0.615 vs 0.798). At matched loss 3×10^-5 on Indian Pines, GD reaches RMSE 0.0150 at effective rank 11 vs Adam 0.0268 at rank 28 — Adam's test error actually rises as it fits deeper.
- **FlowAdam-p (the repaired version) recovers at 0.169** vs Adam's 0.573 on the zoo task, with effective rank 4.8–5.4 (closest to GD's 4.51 among Adam-type variants); the +15.9% gain over the dial alone (0.201) is at the same learning rate.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine. The classification of factored-model optimizers by gauge equivariance, the structure theorem characterizing memoryless equivariant rules as Gram-determined left preconditioners, and the Adam-p dial are new contributions that close a gap left open by Gunasekar-style implicit-bias theory (which only studied GD) and by Xie et al.'s rotation study (which studied ambient, not internal-factorization, gauge).
- **Rigor:** High for the core claims. The two-class separation on matrix sensing is deliberately selection-favoring the coordinate-wise camp (best-of-grid per method) and therefore a lower bound; controls are extensive (symmetric cosine schedules, full LR sweeps, init-scale variations, noise-twin ladder, 10-seed replication up to n=256). The Adam first-step defect is given as an exact identity (Prop 4.4), not a heuristic.
- **Reproducibility:** Strong on the mechanism side — CPU experiments run "in minutes," seeds and control conditions are public. The code link is provided.
- **Significance:** The attention-twin result (Adam's 56% per-head W_Q^T W_K split at step 1, four orders of magnitude above its own noise twin) is a concrete, falsifiable, downstream-relevant finding with implications for model merging and per-head Procrustes alignment that prior symmetry-aware optimizer papers only gestured at.
- **Red flags and honest scope caveats (the author states these directly):**
  - **Experiments are deliberately small-scale** (40×40 matrices, up to 6-layer transformers, max 2000×200 hyperspectral scenes) — the author explicitly says the work "deliberately avoids advocating any particular optimizer as a new state-of-the-art" and that "the mechanism does not promise benchmark wins."
  - **The empty gap in Figure 1 is budget-qualified**, not asymptotic. The author flags two exceptions: untuned Shampoo (whose recovery degrades non-monotonically with n and even dips below Adam at n=96) and long-anneal signum (Appendix D.10), a coordinate-wise rule that recovers anyway through a different mechanism — equivariance is therefore neither strictly necessary nor strictly sufficient (Remark 4.7 explicitly admits this, citing ScaledGD as the other-side counterexample).
  - **The hyperspectral results do not cleanly transfer the whole zoo ordering.** Only the GD–Adam ordering survives; on Indian Pines Muon is actually *worse* than Adam at both densities, and scalar-Adam's grid there is undersampled (§9, Appendix D.6). So the headline "equivariant methods are better" does not generalize uniformly to real data.
  - **The transfer theorem (Thm 4.6) only covers the common-scalar subclass** — the momentum-augmented scalar-Adam agreement with GD is described as "empirically observed rather than formally implied" (Remark 4.7), leaving a real theoretical loose end.
  - **Late-time gauge drift is dominated by floating-point chaos** (Muon from msign's small-singular-value sensitivity, scalar-Adam from limited precision); the early step-1 split is the only reliably discriminating signal at depth.
  - **Train-only LR selection for hyperspectral is selection-biased against Adam** (gives Adam its worst grid rate); the author notes that selecting each method's own best held-out rate would shrink the GD gap from 43% to 13%. The ordering is preserved but the magnitude is protocol-dependent.

## Strengths

- Mechanistic, falsifiable theory tightly coupled to controlled experiments — the dial (§7) is a textbook example of isolating a single confound.
- Intellectual honesty in scope: explicit "where the bias does not succeed" subsection (§10.1) lists grokking, stiff PINNs, and LoRA fine-tuning as regimes where the mechanism does not apply.
- The twin-protocol diagnostic (gauge/A=I/noise triplet) is a reusable methodological contribution for anyone auditing optimizer equivariance claims.
- Reconciles two opposing recent Muon results (exact-recovery vs. simplicity-bias loss) with a single phase diagram and a solvable model (Prop 8.1).

## Limitations

- Small problem scales and short training budgets; the results are mechanism demonstrations, not benchmarks. The author acknowledges GD is unlikely to beat well-tuned Adam on real LLM training and that no optimizer has been shown to consistently do so (Schmidt et al. 2021; Dahl et al. 2023).
- The orthogonal-gauge restriction is a modeling choice — the full symmetry group is GL(k), and gradient descent's trajectory actually depends on the balance term U_0^T U_0 − V_0^T V_0 which varies within a GL(k) orbit.
- Real-data validation is narrow (two hyperspectral scenes, both low-rank-friendly) and the only ordering that survives there is GD > Adam; the zoo's other rankings collapse or invert.
- Stochastic / minibatch interaction with the gauge is only probed empirically (tiny-Shakespeare), not characterized — the author flags the quotient-geometric framework of Aladrah et al. (2026) as a natural but unstarted direction.
- Single author; the FlowAdam §10 self-critique, while candid, evaluates the author's own prior method on the author's own criterion.

## Relevance

直接命中"优化器理论与隐式偏置"研究方向：用规范等变这一几何不变量统一解释了 Adam 类方法在分解模型（包括注意力头隐式分解 W_Q^T W_K）上为何丧失梯度流的低秩偏好，并为后续"对称感知优化器设计"和"基于对齐的模型合并"研究提供了可证伪的判据与诊断协议（twin protocol），对理解 LoRA / 注意力训练动力学有直接启发。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the 4% tail-energy crossover boundary (§8) hold on modern LLM pretraining, where token-covariance matrices have heavy spectral tails, or does it shift because the "target" is itself being learned?
- Can the gauge/A=I/noise twin triplet be turned into a cheap online regularizer that pulls Adam back toward the equivariant orbit without paying the ~8× interpolation-step cost of scalar-Adam (§7)?
- How does the GL(k) balance conservation (U^T U − V^T V) interact with Adam's gauge breaking, and does enforcing balance explicitly recover the low-rank bias where equivariance alone fails (ScaledGD counterexample, Remark 4.7)?
- Is the 56% W_Q^T W_K split the actual reason alignment-based model merging fails between independently Adam-trained transformers, or do later-training Lyapunov instabilities dominate the mergeability budget?
- Can a momentum-invariance lemma (flagged in §11) formally extend the transfer theorem to scalar-Adam, closing the gap between the empirical agreement and Theorem 4.6's common-scalar-only reach?
