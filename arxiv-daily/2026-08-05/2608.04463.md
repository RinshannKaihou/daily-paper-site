> **一句话总结：** 本文提出一个分离"重新作答、候选内容暴露、同侪呈现残差、评判者方向性敏感"四通道的实验协议，结合配对盲/知情评分与层次序数模型，在四个 7–9B 模型 × 三个基准上证明：全错同侪输入在所有 12 个单元均产生最低质量的修订，而同一答案在评判者看到同侪块时评分会按评判者特定方向漂移（Mistral 向同侪偏 +0.637，Gemma/Llama 偏离 −0.327/−0.237），翻转率无法刻画开放式从众。

> **TL;DR:** The paper reframes open-ended LLM conformity as a joint generation-and-measurement problem, using a branched Round-2 design and paired blind/informed judging across Qwen2.5-7B, Mistral-7B, Gemma-2-9B, and Llama-3.1-8B on TruthfulQA, MMLU-Pro, and ARC-Challenge; all-wrong peers yield the lowest-quality revisions in all 12 generator-dataset cells (e.g., ARC-Challenge gap of +0.278 between all-correct and all-wrong mean blind ratings, latent shift up to −0.528), while the same answer receives judge-specific rating shifts when the peer block is visible (Mistral γ=+0.637 vs Gemma γ=−0.327), showing that flip rates and a single neutral evaluator are inadequate.

## 核心贡献

- **将开放式从众重新定义为测量问题**：指出开放式修订的答案是分级、潜在、被不完美评判的，二元翻转指标（flip rate）无法捕捉"立场不变但事实性/认识框架退化"的从众，例如 TruthfulQA 上 9.0% 的全错修订在翻转指标下记为"未变"，却至少损失一个盲评等级。
- **设计可分离的四通道实验协议**：Round-2 分支出无同侪重答、内容-only（正确/错误/混合极性）、同侪-presented（6 说话者归因块 × 权威在场/缺失）共 7 臂，将总位移分解为普通重答 ∆sp、内容暴露 ∆content,k、同侪呈现残差 ∆pp,c，并通过配对盲/知情评分把评判者侧的方向性敏感 γj 单独识别。
- **层次序数潜在质量模型**：θi = bt(i) + ri ηc(i) + σθ εi，并以 ±1.5 锚点固定潜在尺度原点与单位；用 NumPyro/JAX NUTS（4 链 × 1500 暖机 + 1500 采样）拟合，并报告收敛诊断与锚点尺度敏感性。
- **可重复、可审计的开源实现**：vLLM 贪婪解码、3 个种子（42 主分析，43/44 复现）、自包含分解语料库、固定分类器（RoBERTa-MNLI、BERTScore、SBERT、TruthfulQA truth/info judges）外部验证、自评排除稳健性重拟合；代码与数据开源。

## Key Contributions

- Reframes open-ended LLM conformity as a measurement problem: graded latent answer quality cannot be captured by binary flip indicators, which miss cases where the nominal answer is preserved but factuality, caveats, or epistemic framing degrade.
- Introduces a branched Round-2 protocol that decomposes total revision shift into ordinary re-answering (∆sp), candidate-content exposure (∆content,k), and a bundled peer-presentation residual (∆pp,c) spanning attribution, repetition, speaker count, consensus structure, authority labels, and prompt length.
- Adds a paired blind/informed evaluation that holds the generated answer fixed while toggling peer-block visibility, isolating evaluator-side peer-context sensitivity (γj) from generator-side revision effects — a contrast no prior conformity study separates.
- Fits a hierarchical ordinal latent-quality model with anchored scale identification, and validates it with non-generative fixed classifiers, self-rating exclusions, readability/difficulty stratification, anchor-scale sensitivity, and convergence diagnostics.

## Methodology

**Models & Data.** Four open-weight generators served with vLLM under greedy decoding (temperature=0, top_p=1, max_tokens=300): Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, Gemma-2-9B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. They double as the four open-weight judges; GPT-4o and GPT-5.4-mini are added via separate TruthfulQA five-judge refits. Datasets are 500-question samples (from Qu et al. 2026) of TruthfulQA, MMLU-Pro, and ARC-Challenge.

**Branched Generation.** Each question gets a Round-1 baseline answer aq. Round 2 branches into seven arms from the same realized baseline: one no-peer re-answer; three content-only prompts of polarity k ∈ {correct, wrong, mixed}; and three peer-presented prompts containing the same candidate content in a six-speaker attributed block, each realized in authority-present/absent variants (pooled to three conditions). All-correct/all-wrong blocks endorse the gold answer or a selected attractor; mixed blocks split 3–3.

**Two Corpora.** (1) A pooled main peer-condition corpus (seed 42; 500 × 4 × 8 = 16,000 answers per benchmark; seeds 43/44 retained only for reproducibility because greedy decoding makes them non-independent stochastic draws). (2) Separately constructed, self-contained decomposition corpora (2,000 trial baselines, 16,000 answers per benchmark) in which all eight answers branch from one realized Round-1 answer, because separate greedy runs reproduced Round-1 only 54% of the time.

**Estimands.** ∆sp = Q(no-peer) − Q(Round 1); ∆content,k = Q(content,k) − Q(no-peer); ∆pp,c = Q(peer,c) − Q(content,k(c)); total ∆total,c = ∆sp + ∆content,k(c) + ∆pp,c. A secondary harmful-beneficial asymmetry Asymtotal = −∆total,AW − ∆total,AC is positive when all-wrong degradation exceeds all-correct improvement.

**Hierarchical Ordinal Model.** Per-answer latent quality θi = bt(i) + ri ηc(i) + σθ εi; judge-side mean µijp = αj(θi − βj) + 𝟙[p=informed]γj di with ordered-logistic likelihood and shared cutpoints κ. Correct/incorrect anchors pinned at ±1.5 fix origin and unit; di ∈ {+1,0,−1} encodes all-correct/mixed/all-wrong signed context so γj captures directional peer-context sensitivity of judge j. Because blind and informed passes rate the identical generated answer, γj is not confounded by generator-output differences. Fit with NUTS, 4 chains, 1500 warmup + 1500 sampling, target_accept_prob=0.95.

**Evaluation.** All answers receive blind ratings on a 5-point ordinal scale from the four-judge panel; peer-presented answers get a second informed rating with the peer block visible. Raw blind agreement is low (Krippendorff's α = 0.235), motivating the multi-judge measurement model. Anchors are scored with the gold reference so terse correct responses are not penalized; experimental answers remain reference-free. External validation uses RoBERTa-MNLI false-endorsement, BERTScore, SBERT, and TruthfulQA truth/info judges as fixed, non-generative classifiers.

## Results & Highlights

- **Flip rates are insufficient.** On TruthfulQA, 9.0% of all-wrong revisions that a binary flip indicator would score as unchanged still lost at least one blind rating point (mean across the judge panel); blind inter-judge agreement is Krippendorff's α = 0.235, so neither a single judge nor an unadjusted mean is reliable.
- **Wrong peers harm open-ended revision in every cell.** Mean blind ratings (Table 2) show all-wrong peers produce the lowest score on all three benchmarks, with all-correct-minus-all-wrong gaps of +0.208 on TruthfulQA (4.503 vs 4.295), +0.157 on MMLU-Pro (4.328 vs 4.171), and +0.278 on ARC-Challenge (4.615 vs 4.337).
- **Latent all-wrong shifts are negative in all 12 generator–dataset cells.** Posterior-mean total shifts range from −0.052 to −0.314 on TruthfulQA, −0.229 to −0.479 on MMLU-Pro, and −0.259 to −0.528 on ARC-Challenge; the largest (−0.528, Llama-3.1-8B on ARC-Challenge) is roughly 18% of the three-unit distance between pinned correct/incorrect anchors.
- **Peer-presentation residual is credibly negative in all 12 decomposition cells.** Routing the same candidate text through an attributed six-speaker peer block lowers latent quality relative to presenting it as a plain content list; the residual is the dominant carrier of all-wrong degradation for most generators, while Llama-3.1-8B's all-wrong harm is carried mainly by the content channel.
- **Evaluators are not neutral and the direction is judge-specific (Table 4).** From paired blind/informed ratings of identical answers: Mistral-7B shifts toward the peer-endorsed position (γ=+0.637, 95% CrI [+0.607, +0.668], Pr(γ>0)=1.00), while Gemma-2-9B (γ=−0.327) and Llama-3.1-8B (γ=−0.237) shift away; Qwen2.5-7B is approximately neutral (γ=−0.008). GPT-4o (γ=−0.096) and GPT-5.4-mini (γ=−0.172) are credibly negative, so even frontier judges are non-neutral.
- **Anchor calibration is necessary (Table 5).** Under reference-free scoring, judges rank the correct anchor above the incorrect anchor on only 73.3% of questions with a mean separation of 1.59 points; reference-augmented anchor scoring raises correct ordering to 97.5% and separation to 3.30 points. A reference-free intermediate fit even flipped the direction of the estimated asymmetry despite satisfactory sampling diagnostics, motivating reference-augmented anchors for all primary estimates.
- **External fixed-classifier corroboration (Table 16).** RoBERTa-MNLI false endorsement is lowest for all-correct, intermediate for mixed, highest for all-wrong on all three benchmarks (e.g., TruthfulQA 0.090 / 0.175 / 0.326; ARC-Challenge 0.137 / 0.172 / 0.350), reproducing the condition ordering without any generative LLM judge in the loop. Self-exclusion refits keep the harmful-beneficial asymmetry positive in all 12 cells (largest change Qwen2.5-7B ARC +0.640 → +0.566).

## Authors & Credibility

Alicia Guerra and Yibo Hu are at the Illinois Institute of Technology (Chicago). The paper builds directly on the authors' prior conformity work (Hu & Qu 2026, "Most LLM conformity needs no speaker," arXiv:2607.05545; Qu, Fu & Hu 2026, "Easier to mislead than to correct," arXiv:2606.01637) and reuses the vendored social-pressure primitives from that line byte-for-byte. Compute was on Jetstream2 A100-80GB / RunPod H100 instances plus a local RTX 5090, with OpenAI API credits via the Researcher Access Program. Code and data are released at https://github.com/yibo-hu-labb/open-ended-conformity. The paper is unusually careful about measurement validity: it reports weakly informative priors, NUTS convergence diagnostics (R̂ ≤ 1.007, zero divergent transitions, min bulk ESS 715, min tail ESS 1,239), anchor-scale sensitivity refits, and multiple external validators.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine. The paired blind/informed contrast that isolates evaluator-side γj from generator-side revision, plus the four-channel decomposition (∆sp / ∆content / ∆pp), is not present in prior flip-based conformity work (Ranaldi & Pucci 2023; Weng et al. 2025; Hong et al. 2025) nor in LLM-as-judge bias work (which shows biases exist but does not separate a changed answer from a changed judgment).
- **Rigor:** High. The team discloses that greedy decoding makes the three seeds non-independent (only 54% Round-1 reproduction across runs) and therefore correctly excludes seeds 43/44 from the primary fit instead of inflating sample size. They pre-register four claims and organize results around exactly those.
- **A concrete measurement concern they handle well:** the anchor audit is a real threat — under reference-free scoring judges order the correct anchor above the incorrect one only 73.3% of the time, and a reference-free fit flipped the asymmetry sign while still passing R̂/ESS. They switch to reference-augmented anchors for all primary estimates, which is the right response, but it means the latent-scale identification relies on giving judges the gold reference for anchors only — a non-trivial assumption the paper explicitly flags rather than hides.
- **Reproducibility:** Strong. Public code/data, full prompt templates, model checkpoints, package versions, decoding parameters, MCMC settings, and a measured GPU-hour budget (≈25–30 local GPU-h; 1.24 GPU-h for the 147K-answer judging pass).
- **Significance:** Practically important for anyone building multi-agent LLM systems or LLM-as-judge pipelines: it shows the social packaging around a peer answer is itself a source of quality loss, and that no single judge (including GPT-4o/5.4-mini) is a safe neutral default — γj must be estimated per judge.
- **Red flags / weak spots:** (1) The "peer-presentation residual" deliberately bundles attribution, repetition, speaker count, consensus structure, authority labels, and prompt length, so the paper cannot say which feature drives the negative residual — it explicitly leaves this as a next step. (2) All four generators are 7–9B open-weight models; no frontier generator (e.g., GPT-class) is tested as a reviser, so generalization to larger systems is unverified. (3) The judge panel overlaps the generator set (same four models), and although self-exclusion refits preserve the ordering, this is a structural confound the design cannot fully eliminate. (4) The mixed condition is sometimes more harmful than all-correct on individual generators (e.g., Mistral MMLU-Pro: mixed −0.301 vs all-wrong −0.377), so the clean "all-wrong worst" headline is a cell-level average pattern, not a universal per-item law.

## Strengths

- Separates evaluator bias from generator conformity via a clean paired design on identical answers — a methodological first for this literature.
- Honest about greedy decoding, anchor identification failure, and the bundled nature of the presentation residual.
- External validation with fixed non-generative classifiers (RoBERTa-MNLI, TruthfulQA truth-judge) corroborates the main ordering without adding another LLM judge.
- Reports per-judge γj with posterior probabilities, enabling practitioners to pick near-neutral judges (γ≈0) or pool judges with sensitivity modeled.

## Limitations

- No directly observable ground truth for open-ended answer quality; latent scale depends on the measurement model and anchor identification assumptions.
- Counterfactuals are average effects across experimentally defined conditions, not per-answer causal effects — the same realization cannot be observed with and without peer exposure.
- Four 7–9B open-weight generators only; behavior of larger or frontier models as revisers is untested.
- The peer-presentation residual is a bundle; isolating which component (attribution, authority, consensus, length) carries the effect is left open.
- Results are specific to the studied prompts, models, tasks, and peer representations and should not be read as universal constants of LLM systems.

## Relevance

直接相关于多智能体 LLM 系统与 LLM-as-Judge 评估方法学研究：它指出同侪呈现的"社会包装"本身就是质量损失源，且没有任何单一评判者（含 GPT-4o/5.4-mini）可视为中性默认——这对构建可靠的多智能体辩论/评审管线、设计去偏评判面板具有直接方法论价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Which component of the peer-presentation residual (attribution vs. authority labels vs. consensus structure vs. prompt length) carries the negative effect — can the bundle be unbundled with a targeted ablation?
- Do frontier generators (e.g., GPT-class, Claude-class) show the same all-wrong-worst pattern and the same heterogeneous γj when revising, or does scale attenuate conformity?
- Would a larger or differently composed judge panel (e.g., human + LLM mix) change the estimated γj distribution, and can per-judge γj be predicted from judge properties a priori?
- Is the 9.0% "flip-rate-blind" degradation on TruthfulQA larger on benchmarks with more gradable explanations (e.g., long-form QA, code review), where flip rates are even less informative?
- Can the paired blind/informed protocol be applied to non-conformity treatments (retrieval-augmented generation, rubric exemplars, chain-of-thought scaffolding) to audit whether evaluators react to treatment-correlated context in general?
