> **一句话总结：** 本文提出"副作用内省"(side-effect introspection)新任务，构建了一个基于 213 个 Hugging Face 真实 LoRA 微调模型、覆盖 13 个安全类别的内省数据集，并提出 Delta-Aware Introspection Adapter (DAIA)，显式处理基座激活与微调诱导的激活差异，在三类 OOD 设置下平均 F1 比 LoRA 内省适配器高约 6 个点。

> **TL;DR:** The paper introduces "side-effect introspection," where a fine-tuned LLM must report unintended alignment shifts (not deliberately implanted behaviors), constructs a supervision dataset from 213 real Hugging Face LoRA-fine-tuned Qwen3-14B / Gemma3-12B-it models across 13 safety categories (StrongREJECT, HarmBench, HEx-PHI, AdvBench), and proposes DAIA, an introspection adapter that explicitly consumes both base activations and fine-tuning-induced activation differences; DAIA beats a vanilla LoRA introspection adapter on all three OOD splits, reaching ~70.2 vs ~64.7 average score in OOD-A (about +5.5 points).

## 核心贡献

- 提出全新的"副作用内省"(side-effect introspection) 问题设置：内省的解释目标不再等于微调数据中显式植入的行为，而是微调作为副作用诱导出的对齐偏移 (alignment shift)，更贴近真实部署场景。
- 构建了一个面向副作用内省的监督数据集：从 Hugging Face 收集 213 个真实 LoRA 微调模型 (Qwen3-14B 与 Gemma3-12b-it)，在 1,523 条安全相关基准样本上测量基座与微调模型的有害合规分数偏移，并将其离散化为五个有序安全等级标签。
- 提出 Delta-Aware Introspection Adapter (DAIA)：一种适配于加性 PEFT 的内省适配器结构，显式地将"基座激活"与"微调诱导的激活差 Δh"作为两个分支分别做 LayerNorm 与低秩投影后拼接，再混合回输出空间。
- 通过实验证明内省学习对未见过的微调模型和未见过的安全类别均具备 OOD 泛化能力，且 DAIA 在三个 OOD 设置中均取得最高平均分。
- 利用激活修补 (activation patching) 进行机制分析，发现 safer/riskier 判断主要在后期层的 MLP 基座权重中形成，而 DAIA 主要依赖微调诱导的激活差 Δh，验证了其作为"差分感知"模块的设计意图。

## Key Contributions

- Formulates the new problem setting of **side-effect introspection**, where the explanation target is an unintended alignment shift (δi,c) induced as a side effect of fine-tuning, rather than the behavior explicitly implanted in the fine-tuning data — bridging a gap between prior introspection-learning work (Diff Interpretation Tuning, Introspection Adapters) and real auditing scenarios.
- Constructs a supervision dataset by collecting **213 real LoRA adapters from Hugging Face** for Qwen3-14B and Gemma3-12b-it, evaluating them on **1,523 prompts** mapped into **13 unified safety categories** drawn from StrongREJECT, HarmBench, HEx-PHI, and AdvBench, and verbalizing measured base-vs-finetune score shifts into five ordered labels (substantially safer → substantially riskier).
- Proposes **DAIA (Delta-Aware Introspection Adapter)**, an IA architecture for additive PEFT that splits the introspection update into two branches — one consuming base-model activations (hbase) and one consuming the fine-tuning-induced difference (Δh) — each with its own LayerNorm and low-rank projection, before mixing them back into the layer output.
- Empirically shows that introspection learning generalizes OOD to unseen fine-tuned models and unseen safety categories, and that DAIA consistently achieves the best average score across all three OOD settings.
- Provides a mechanistic interpretation via **activation patching**: the final safer/riskier judgment is formed primarily in later-layer MLP base weights, while DAIA itself relies more strongly on the fine-tuning-induced activation difference Δh than on base activations, supporting its intended role.

## Methodology

**Problem setting — side-effect introspection.** Prior introspection-learning methods (Goel et al. 2026 "Diff Interpretation Tuning"; Shenoy et al. 2026 "Introspection Adapters") choose a behavior bi, build fine-tuning data Dft(bi) that deliberately implants it, and use the same bi as the introspection label. The authors argue this conflates the learned behavior with the explanation target. In side-effect introspection, the model is fine-tuned for some (possibly benign) task, but the introspection label yi,c describes a *measured* misalignment shift δi,c in category c that emerges as a side effect: Dift(bi) → Mθi, with yi,c describing δi,c rather than bi.

**Data construction.** For each fine-tuned model Mθi and category c, the authors compute a category-level misalignment score ec(Mθi) = mean over prompts of an LLM-judge harmful-compliance score R(p, a) ∈ {1,…,5} (HEx-PHI rubric, judged by gpt-5-mini). The side-effect shift is Δei,c = ec(Mθi) − ec(Mθ0). Shifts are discretized via fixed thresholds into five ordered labels (substantially safer, slightly safer, largely unchanged, slightly riskier, substantially riskier; thresholds at ±0.08 and ±0.60). Labels are verbalized via rule-based templates combining a question template (50 variants) and a category phrase (7 variants per category), producing a supervised dataset of (model, question, answer) triples.

**DAIA architecture.** Standard parallel introspection adapters compute hstd,i = W0x + Δhi + ΔhIA, where the introspection update ΔhIA = gϕIA(x) sees only the original input x. DAIA instead explicitly conditions on both branches: hDAIA,i = BDAIA [ Abase(LNbase(hbase)) ; AΔ(LNΔ(Δhi)) ], where hbase = W0x is the base computation and Δhi is the existing fine-tuning adapter output (available for free during the forward pass). Ranks are split rbase = rΔ = 8 (LoRA rank 16), keeping trainable parameter budgets comparable (LoRA 41.9M vs DAIA 49.5M).

**Evaluation protocol.** Fine-tuned models are split 9:1 into train/test; categories 1–9 are seen in training and 10–13 are held out, yielding three OOD settings: OOD-A (unseen models, seen categories), OOD-B (seen models, unseen categories), OOD-C (unseen models, unseen categories). Tasks are direction-only (3-way: safer/unchanged/riskier) and direction-plus-degree (5-way). Baselines: random, majority class, raw fine-tuned model, a per-layer concat linear probe (PCA to 512-dim + MLP), and a vanilla LoRA introspection adapter.

## Results & Highlights

- **DAIA > LoRA on all three OOD settings.** In OOD-A (unseen models, seen categories), DAIA reaches an average score of **70.2 vs 64.7 for the LoRA IA** (a ~5.5-point gap), with Dir+Deg F1 of **71.8 vs 70.4 on Qwen3-14B** and Dir Acc of **75.8 vs 61.1 on Gemma3-12b-it**.
- **Generalization to unseen models and categories.** In OOD-C (unseen models AND unseen categories), DAIA achieves **66.3 Dir Acc / 62.3 Dir F1 on Qwen3-14B** vs LoRA's 65.9 / 58.8, and the highest average score of **50.0 vs LoRA's 49.5**; both crush the raw-model baseline (~13.5 avg) and majority class (~29.7 avg).
- **Probe is surprisingly competitive, sometimes beating LoRA IA.** In OOD-A the probe baseline scores **66.0 average**, slightly above the LoRA IA's 64.7, and on Qwen3-14B OOD-A reaches Dir F1 of **72.6 vs DAIA's 70.5** — leading the authors to concede that "what is called introspection does not necessarily perform substantially more complex reasoning than a probe."
- **Fine-tuning on benign data reliably degrades safety, but heterogeneously.** Average misalignment shifts across the 213 models are positive (riskier) in most categories for Qwen3-14B: **Disinformation +0.25 ± 0.50, Cyber +0.14 ± 0.36, Fraud +0.14 ± 0.37, Non-violent crime +0.12 ± 0.40**. The Copyright category is the exception, shifting *safer* (−0.22 ± 0.62 for Qwen3-14B, −0.28 ± 0.55 for Gemma3-12b-it). Standard deviations frequently exceed the means, indicating large per-model variance.
- **Mechanistic finding via activation patching.** The safer/riskier judgment is concentrated in **late-layer MLP modules (gate_proj, up_proj, down_proj) at position P3**, with base-weight contributions dominating; DAIA's input branch attends primarily to the fine-tuning difference Δh rather than hbase, confirming its design as a difference-aware module.

- The work extends a credible line of recent introspection literature (Goel et al. 2026; Shenoy et al. 2026; Betley et al. 2025; Lindsey 2026) and is positioned as a direct, well-motivated critique of those methods' controlled "model organism" setup.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine. Reframing introspection from "report the implanted behavior" to "report a measured side-effect shift" is a non-trivial generalization, and DAIA's two-branch (base / Δh) design with separate LayerNorms is a clean, well-justified architectural contribution tailored to the additive-PEFT regime. The activation-patching analysis showing DAIA actually attends to Δh is a nice mechanistic check.
- **Rigor:** Mixed. The honest concession that a simple concat linear probe *matches or beats* the LoRA introspection adapter in OOD-A is intellectually courageous and undermines the headline framing that introspection adapters are clearly superior to probing — the authors admit "there may be little practical performance gap" and that introspection may be "implementing a relatively simple classifier over internal states." The gains of DAIA over the probe are real but modest (~4 points average in OOD-A), and in OOD-B/OOD-C the gap narrows to ~1–2 points.
- **Reproducibility:** Partially concerning. The dataset construction depends on `gpt-5-mini` LLM-as-judge scoring with specific OpenAI rubric prompts (reproduced in Appendix A.3) — judge drift and policy-version changes could perturb labels. The 9:1 model split, the fixed category split (1–9 train, 10–13 test), the coarse ±0.08 / ±0.60 thresholds, and the one-epoch / 256-token training budget are all hyperparameter-sensitive and the paper provides **no sensitivity analysis** over them. Code/data are "future release," not yet available.
- **Significance:** The problem framing is the most important contribution and is likely to be picked up; the architectural contribution (DAIA) is incremental and the modest gains over a probe baseline limit its standalone impact.
- **Red flags / specific weak points:**
  - The category split is arbitrary and small (4 held-out categories out of 13), which the authors themselves flag as a likely cause of weak unseen-category generalization — yet no alternative splits or cross-validation over splits are reported.
  - The probe baseline uses *concatenated last-token hidden states from every layer*, which is a very strong, data-hungry classifier; comparing it against a single shared introspection adapter is not strictly apples-to-apples, and the paper does not report probe parameter counts or training data per cell.
  - The 5-way "degree" labels are derived from coarse scalar thresholds on a noisy 1–5 judge score, so the Dir+Deg numbers may be dominated by judge noise rather than genuine introspective ability.
  - Evaluation is restricted to classification of pre-verbalized labels; the paper itself acknowledges this is the wrong task to showcase introspection's value and defers free-form reporting to future work — meaning the central claim of "introspection" is not actually demonstrated in its most interesting form.

## Strengths

- Novel, well-motivated problem formulation (side-effect introspection) that closes a real gap between prior controlled introspection studies and deployment-time auditing.
- Large and *realistic* dataset: 213 community LoRA adapters from Hugging Face rather than synthetically implanted behaviors, across two model families and 13 unified safety categories mapped from four major safety benchmarks.
- DAIA is conceptually clean and the activation-patching analysis provides genuine mechanistic evidence that the adapter behaves as designed (attending to Δh).
- Honest reporting: the authors openly disclose that the probe is competitive and that introspection's advantage does not show up in classification, rather than overselling.
- Detailed appendices (judge prompts, templates, full adapter list, training config) support replication.

## Limitations

- Only the classification form of the task is evaluated; the more compelling free-form "describe what kind of misalignment occurred and why" reporting is left to future work, so the introspection claim is only indirectly supported.
- DAIA's gains over a strong linear probe are modest and mostly concentrated in the seen-categories (OOD-A) setting; in unseen-category settings the gap nearly vanishes.
- The category train/test split (9 vs 4) is small and fixed; no random-split variance or alternative-split robustness is reported.
- The base model is held fixed; the authors note DAIA under a moving base model (real version upgrades) is unaddressed.
- Label construction depends on a single proprietary judge (gpt-5-mini) and on coarse thresholds; no human validation of label quality, no judge-robustness study.
- Code and dataset are not yet released at submission time, limiting immediate reproducibility.
- Coverage is limited to LoRA / additive PEFT; non-additive fine-tuning (full FT, DoRA-style decomposed updates) where Δh is not cleanly available is out of scope.

## Relevance

这篇论文与 LLM 安全 / 对齐 / 可解释性研究方向高度相关：它把"模型自省"从受控的后门检测推广到真实部署场景下的副作用对齐退化检测，对自动化安全审计、PEFT 安全监控、以及理解微调如何扰动内部表征均有直接参考价值，适合作为"内省学习"和"模型差异解释"两条线的衔接工作跟进。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can introspection adapters be trained to produce *free-form* natural-language descriptions of side-effect misalignment (not just classify into 5 labels), and does DAIA's advantage over a probe grow in that open-ended setting?
- How sensitive are the results to the LLM-judge (gpt-5-mini), the ±0.08 / ±0.60 thresholds, and the fixed 9/4 category split — would bootstrap-CIs over random category splits erase the DAIA-vs-LoRA gap in OOD-B/OOD-C?
- Does DAIA transfer across *base-model versions* (e.g., trained on Qwen3-14B, evaluated when the base is patched to a newer release), which is the realistic deployment regime the authors flag as future work?
- Can the side-effect shift signal in Δh be separated from the intended-task signal using crosscoder / sparse model-diffing (Lindsey et al. 2024; Kassem et al. 2025), and would feeding those diffing-derived features into DAIA further improve sensitivity?
- Since the probe matches introspection in classification, what *minimal* task would actually demonstrate that introspection does something a probe cannot — e.g., calibrated uncertainty, counterfactual "what would shift me safer?" reports, or cross-category transfer of explanations?
