> **一句话总结：** 提出A/B Agent——一个面向工业推荐系统策略迭代的闭环智能体，通过层次化策略经验树、多路Tree-RAG检索和实验树引导的自演化机制，将碎片化的历史A/B实验知识转化为可迁移、可执行策略，并在快手电商短视频场景取得GMV +4.829%的线上提升，同时所有护栏指标保持为正。

> **TL;DR:** A/B Agent is a closed-loop LLM agent for industrial recommendation strategy iteration that organizes historical A/B experiments into a hierarchical experience tree, retrieves transferable evidence via multi-path Tree-RAG, and self-evolves through an experiment tree of online A/B feedback; on a 310-strategy Kuaishou e-commerce benchmark it reaches an average overall score of 7.244 (beating Claude-Sonnet-4.6 by 1.3% and the strongest RAG baseline by up to 31.7%), and in production it lifts Cart GMV by +4.829% with all guardrail metrics positive.

## 核心贡献

- **闭环工业策略迭代智能体**：首次将"历史策略挖掘 → 跨场景迁移 → A/B分析 → 参数优化"整合为一个自演化的agent闭环，针对推荐系统策略层级（而非用户-物品层级）的优化任务。
- **层次化策略经验树**：将碎片化的实验报告分解为原子化、自包含的策略chunk（含动机/机制/参数/适用上下文/结果/风险），并按 domain → scenario → stage → objective 组织为可迁移的经验树，同一策略可挂载到多条兼容路径以支持跨分支迁移。
- **目标感知的多路Tree-RAG**：结合稀疏+稠密多路检索、基于树路径距离的层次感知加权提升（公式4）和Qwen-Reranker重排，解决flat RAG忽视业务上下文层级关系导致的检索错配。
- **实验树引导的自演化机制**：将连续A/B实验组织为实验树，通过父子/兄弟节点比较（utility公式8同时考虑核心增益和护栏惩罚）决定局部参数搜索或机制替换，验证后回写经验树，实现知识积累闭环。
- **工业A/B基准与真实部署**：构建含310条策略、覆盖3个快手电商场景的benchmark，并在生产环境部署，取得GMV +4.829%且所有护栏指标为正的线上结果。

## Key Contributions

- **Closed-loop agent for industrial strategy iteration**: A/B Agent unifies historical strategy mining, cross-scenario transfer, A/B analysis, and parameter tuning into a single self-evolving loop, targeting strategy-level (not user-item-level) recommendation optimization.
- **Hierarchical strategy experience tree**: Fragmented experiment reports are decomposed into atomic, self-contained strategy chunks (motivation / mechanism / parameters / applicability / outcome / risk) and organized by domain → scenario → stage → objective; transferable strategies can attach to multiple compatible paths to enable cross-branch knowledge transfer.
- **Target-aware multi-path Tree-RAG**: Combines sparse + dense multi-path retrieval, hierarchy-aware tree-path relevance boosting (Eq. 4 using shortest-path distance between nodes), and Qwen-Reranker reranking to fix the context-mismatch problem of flat RAG.
- **Experiment-tree-guided self-evolution**: Successive A/B tests are organized into an experiment tree where parent-child and sibling comparisons, governed by a utility function (Eq. 8) that rewards core gains and penalizes guardrail degradation, decide between local parameter search and mechanism replacement; validated strategies are written back to the experience tree.
- **Industrial benchmark and real deployment**: A 310-strategy benchmark across three Kuaishou e-commerce scenarios is released for evaluation, and the system is deployed in production, achieving +4.829% GMV with all guardrail metrics positive.

## Methodology

A/B Agent has three tightly coupled components (Figure 2):

**(1) Historical Strategy Knowledge Organization.** Raw experiment reports (organized by project/batch, mixing multiple changes) are first converted into a unified schema (background, scenario, stage, objective, strategy, parameter update, core/guardrail metrics, deployment conditions). A strategy agent then performs multi-perspective (problem / scenario / mechanism) chunking to produce atomic, self-contained strategy chunks, validated by schema checks, metric-consistency checks, and atomicity checks. Chunks are placed into a hierarchical experience tree `T_E = (V_E, E_E)` where each chunk `c` is assigned a semantic path `π(c) = [d_c, s_c, l_c, o_c]` (domain, scenario, stage, objective).

**(2) Autonomous Target-Aware Strategy Generation.** Given a request `q`, the system identifies its scenario/stage/objective/guardrails/constraints and maps them to a tree path. Candidate chunks are retrieved via complementary sparse (BM25/TF-IDF for exact business terms and parameter IDs) and dense (embedding for mechanism similarity) paths, combined as `S_ret(c,q) = Σ α_m Norm(S_m(c,q))` (Eq. 3). Hierarchy-aware boosting `B_tree(c,q) = Σ β_l exp(-γ_l d_T(v^l_c, v^l_q))` (Eq. 4) up-weights structurally matched candidates and gives smaller weights to nearby branches for transfer. Top-K candidates are reranked by Qwen-Reranker, then a strategy generator adapts transferable mechanisms to the target task (considering serving cost, valid parameter ranges, deployment constraints). Agent judges filter candidates on contextual relevance, evidence consistency, engineering feasibility, expected gain, and guardrail risk; the survivor becomes an executable initial strategy.

**(3) Experiment-Guided Strategy Self-Evolution.** After deployment, A/B results are aligned into a structured representation and each metric change is confidence-adjusted `fΔy_j = ρ_j Δy_j` (Eq. 5) with `ρ_j ∈ [0,1]`. Successive versions form an experiment tree `T*_A = (V*_A, E*_A)` where each node stores `(strategy, params, metric changes, context)` and edges record modifications. A utility score `U(v) = Σ w_j fΔy_j − λ Σ w_k max(0, −fΔy_k)` (Eq. 8) jointly captures core-metric gain and guardrail penalty. Parent-child comparison isolates the effect of a single modification; sibling comparison picks the most promising configuration. When a branch keeps improving without guardrail violations the agent does local parameter search; when it saturates or violates guardrails it retrieves alternative mechanisms from the experience tree and spawns a new branch. Validated outcomes are written back, closing the loop.

**Deployment** uses GLM-5.1 as the foundation model, with rule-based validators (format, ranges, types, consistency) plus agent and human reviewers checking safety, rationality, and feasibility before each launch (Figure 3).

## Results & Highlights

- **Offline overall quality (Table 2):** Across the three Kuaishou e-commerce scenarios, A/B Agent achieves the highest average **Overall score of 7.244**, beating the strongest standalone LLM Claude-Sonnet-4.6 (7.151) by **1.3%** and surpassing the strongest RAG baseline in each scenario by **+25.0%, +31.7%, and +23.5%** respectively (vs. HyDE/Vanilla RAG, which average only 5.728 / 5.692).
- **Per-dimension wins (Table 2):** A/B Agent exceeds GPT-5.5 (the best standalone LLM on Correctness) in correctness by **0.4%, 2.3%, and 0.7%** across the three scenarios, and beats Claude-Sonnet-4.6 (the best on Novelty) in novelty by **4.3%, 5.9%, and 5.0%**.
- **Real-world online A/B test (Tables 3 and 5):** Deployed in Kuaishou's e-commerce short-video scenario, the final Strategy 5 delivers **GMV +4.829%, GPM +4.677%, OPM +0.841%, CVR +0.578%, Clicks +0.370%, Orders +1.053%**, with **all four guardrail metrics positive** (average ≈ +0.342%). Versus the expert-designed strategy, Strategy 5 adds **+2.150 GMV points and +2.941 GPM points** while also improving Watch Time (+0.171pp), Live GMV (+1.448pp), and Platform Orders (+0.262pp).
- **Self-evolution trajectory (Section 5.3-5.4):** The agent moves from Strategy 1 (GMV +1.123% but guardrails −0.405% avg) through Strategy 3 (guardrails turn positive, +0.223% avg, GMV +3.299%) to Strategy 5 (Pareto-optimal point, GMV +4.829%, guardrails +0.342% avg), demonstrating feedback-driven convergence from a risky region to a Pareto-optimal operating point (Figure 4).
- **Ablation (Table 4, Scenario 1):** Removing the knowledge base drops Overall by **3.3%** (largest effect); removing the re-ranking module drops it **2.3%**; removing scenario-aware boosting and multi-path retrieval drop it **1.7%** and **1.5%**; replacing Strategy-Tree RAG with Flat RAG drops it **0.2%**. Every component contributes.
- **Cross-stage robustness (Table 6):** A/B Agent is best in retrieval (7.269), ranking (7.096), blending (7.280), and other stages (7.302), beating Claude-Sonnet-4.6 by 0.043–0.118 points in each.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty** is moderate-to-good: hierarchical strategy trees and tree-path-aware retrieval are individually known ideas (RAPTOR, TreeRAG), but their combination with a closed-loop A/B experiment tree and guardrail-aware utility for *strategy-level* (not item-level) recommendation iteration is a genuinely new framing tailored to industrial pain points.
- **Rigor** is mixed. The offline benchmark is evaluated by an **LLM-as-judge (GPT-5.5)**, which introduces well-known evaluator bias and is not validated against human graders; the margins over Claude-Sonnet-4.6 (1.3% overall, sub-0.2-point per-stage deltas in Table 6) are within plausible judge noise. The flagship Tree-RAG contribution is supported only by a **0.015-point (0.2%)** ablation difference vs. Flat RAG, which is hard to call decisive. The headline **+4.829% GMV** is reported to three decimals, which is unusually precise for noisy online A/B tests, and only a single scenario's online results are shown.
- **Reproducibility** is weak. The 310-strategy benchmark and experience tree are built from proprietary Kuaishou data and are not released; no code is provided; the GLM-5.1 deployment and the internal A/B platform are not accessible. The closed-loop online results cannot be independently reproduced.
- **Significance** is high if the deployment claims hold: automating multi-round A/B strategy iteration with positive guardrails is a real industrial problem, and the error analysis (43.1% solution-mismatch, 29.2% data/attribution errors) is honest about where the system still fails.
- **Red flags**: (1) the offline evaluation depends entirely on a competing proprietary LLM (GPT-5.5) as judge with no human cross-check; (2) only one online scenario is reported despite three scenarios being available offline; (3) the ablation that most directly justifies the "Tree-RAG" name shows the smallest effect; (4) no statistical significance, confidence intervals, or multiple seeds are reported for the offline scores.

## Strengths

- Tackles a real, under-addressed industrial problem (strategy-level A/B iteration) rather than the crowded user-item recommendation space.
- Clean three-component design with a genuine closed loop: knowledge organization → target-aware generation → feedback-driven self-evolution, with results written back to the experience tree.
- Guardrail-aware utility (Eq. 8) explicitly balances core-metric gain against ecosystem-risk penalty, which is essential for production recommenders and often missing in academic work.
- Controlled baseline comparison: all RAG baselines share the same GLM-5.1 backbone, isolating the contribution of knowledge organization and retrieval.
- Honest, granular error analysis (Figure 5) and a per-stage breakdown (Table 6) that expose remaining weaknesses rather than hiding them.

## Limitations

- Offline evaluation relies solely on GPT-5.5 as an automatic judge; no human-annotated gold set or inter-annotator agreement is reported to calibrate it.
- Headline ablation for the hierarchical Tree-RAG (vs. Flat RAG) is only a 0.2% / 0.015-point gain — the smallest of any ablation — undercutting the central architectural claim.
- Dataset, experience tree, and code are proprietary and unavailable, blocking independent reproduction.
- Only one online scenario (e-commerce short-video) is reported; the other two scenarios are offline-only.
- No statistical significance, confidence intervals, or variance over seeds are provided for any offline or online metric.
- 43.1% of failures are "solution mismatch," indicating scenario/objective alignment remains the dominant unsolved weakness.

## Relevance

This paper is highly relevant to research on LLM-driven agents and RAG for industrial recommendation/A-B testing automation, and demonstrates a concrete production deployment at Kuaishou — useful both as a reference architecture for self-evolving strategy agents and as a real-world case study of guardrail-aware multi-objective optimization.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How sensitive are the offline Overall scores to the choice of LLM judge — would Claude-Sonnet-4.6 or a human panel rank the methods differently than GPT-5.5?
- Given the Flat-RAG ablation differs by only 0.015 points, is the hierarchical experience tree actually load-bearing, or is the gain coming mostly from the knowledge base + reranker?
- What are the confidence intervals and statistical significance of the +4.829% GMV online lift, and how does it hold up across multiple traffic buckets and time windows?
- Can the experience tree transfer across business domains beyond short-video e-commerce (e.g., live streaming, ads) without re-chunking, and how is cross-domain transferability quantitatively measured?
- How does the closed-loop cost (number of A/B rounds, online traffic, LLM calls) compare to the expert baseline in terms of total iteration time and engineering effort?
