> **一句话总结：** 本文提出 IRIS（一个受视觉皮层启发的探测套件），用 RSA 衍生的 RSS、ORS、OSI、HWHM 等指标系统地量化 Vision Transformer 的方向选择性；实验表明训练范式（而非规模或数据）是方向编码的最强决定因素，并且仅用冻结主干上一次性测得的 RSS 曲线峰值即可预测下游微调时最佳解冻层深度。

> **TL;DR:** IRIS is a neuroscience-inspired probing suite that quantifies how orientation selectivity (a V1-like low-level code) emerges in Vision Transformers using four metrics (RSS, OSI, ORS, HWHM). Across five pretraining paradigms (DINOv3, DINOv2, OpenCLIP, MAE, DeiT III, AIMv2), five scale tiers, and one full training trajectory, the authors find that the training objective — not scale or data — is the strongest determinant of the orientation code, that DINOv3 generalizes best on 9 pooled downstream tasks (S=0.958) vs AIMv2 worst (S=0.138), and that a single RSS curve measured on the frozen backbone predicts the optimal LoRA fine-tuning start layer, beating 18 a-priori alternatives (RSS ranks 1st of 18, |Spearman|=0.92).

## 核心贡献

- 提出 IRIS（Internal Representation Interpretability Suite），一组受神经科学启发的方向选择性度量指标：人口层面的 RSS（Representational Similarity Score），以及单元层面的 OSI（Orientation Selectivity Index）、ORS（Orientation Recruitment Score）、HWHM（半高半宽调谐带宽）。这些指标可在任意 ViT 的 9 个相对深度上跨架构直接比较，将作为 Python 包发布。
- 系统性地分析方向编码如何在 ViT 中涌现，沿三条独立轴展开：训练范式（5 个家族）、模型规模（22M–1B+ 参数）、训练动力学（OpenCLIP ViT-H/14 的完整检查点轨迹）。
- 给出一种机制可解释的下游微调启发式：仅用冻结主干上一次性测得的 RSS 曲线，其峰值就能预测 LoRA 微调时最佳的解冻起始层深度，且在 18 个先验候选指标中排名第一。
- 实证表明 DINOv2/v3 这类自蒸馏模型在更宽的深度范围内维持 V1 风格的方向编码，是其作为冻结主干表现优异的机制解释；而 AIMv2 过早丢失低层结构，这与其号称"通用视觉编码器"的定位相矛盾。

## Key Contributions

- **IRIS metric suite.** Four neuroscience-grounded orientation metrics — population-level RSS (basis-invariant representational similarity to the orientation manifold) plus per-unit OSI (vector orientation selectivity index), ORS (orientation recruitment score, fraction of units passing a permutation null), and HWHM (von Mises tuning bandwidth) — all comparable across models of different width/depth/basis, released as a Python package.
- **Three-axis systematic study.** Cross-paradigm (OpenCLIP, MetaCLIP, SigLIP2, DeiT III, DINOv2, DINOv3, MAE, AIMv2), cross-scale (22M→1B+ params; S/B/L/H/G tiers), and cross-training-trajectory (OpenCLIP ViT-H/14 checkpoints), all probed with the identical stimulus battery (36 orientations × 4 phases × 5 spatial frequencies of sinusoidal gratings, with patch-Nyquist upper bound).
- **Three complementary readout bases.** Raw residual-stream coordinates (assumption-light), post-GELU MLP neurons (privileged basis, used for trajectory analysis), and SAE features (monosemantic, the primary unit); the multiple bases protect conclusions from a single basis artifact.
- **Practical, a-priori fine-tuning predictor.** RSS measured once on the frozen backbone predicts the empirically optimal LoRA unfreeze depth for 9 Taskonomy-tiny tasks across 6 backbones, beating 18 a-priori alternatives and the common "adapt the whole model" default.

## Methodology

IRIS probes **frozen, pretrained ViTs** with a controlled battery of synthetic sinusoidal gratings (Gabor stimuli), extracting activations at nine matched *relative depths* d ∈ {0, 0.1, 0.25, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.75, 0.9, 1.0}, mapped to block indices via `block(d, L) = round(d·(L−1))`. This relative-depth alignment keeps cross-architecture comparisons fair.

**Stimulus battery (Eq. 1).** Sinusoidal gratings modulated by a Gaussian envelope, sampling orientation uniformly over [0, π) at 36 values, 4 phases, and 5 log-spaced spatial frequencies bounded below the per-patch Nyquist frequency to avoid aliasing.

**Three readout bases.** (1) *Raw coordinates* — each (patch token, channel) of the residual stream as a unit, an assumption-light baseline. (2) *MLP neurons* — post-GELU activations, where the nonlinearity fixes a privileged basis, used to track a stable per-neuron identity across training checkpoints. (3) *SAE features* — a ReLU sparse autoencoder (unit-norm decoder rows, ℓ1 sparsity penalty) fit on 256 orientation-balanced natural images from Imagenette at each probed block; the learned dictionary features are the closest thing to true monosemantic units and serve as the primary readout. Responses are phase-combined via an energy model (Eq. 2) and the spatial frequency maximizing OSI is selected per unit.

**Four metrics.**
- **RSS** (Eq. 3): Pearson correlation between pairwise cosine dissimilarity of pooled activations and pairwise circular orientation distance — basis-invariant, comparable across architectures.
- **OSI** (Eq. 4): vector orientation selectivity index, 1 minus the rectified tuning curve's circular variance, using the harmonic 2θ to encode orientation's 180° periodicity.
- **ORS** (Eq. 5): fraction of units whose OSI survives a permutation null that mirrors the frequency-selection step (giving the null the same 5 chances at a high OSI), with α = 0.05.
- **HWHM** (Eq. 6): half-width at half-maximum of a von Mises fit to each selective unit's tuning curve, capped at 90°, enabling direct comparison with biological V1 measurements (Ringach et al., 2002: 15°–40° band).

A complementary linear-decoding analysis (App. A.2, ridge regression onto (sin 2θ, cos 2θ)) confirms orientation remains linearly decodable across depth even when the geometric RSS changes.

## Results & Highlights

- **Cross-paradigm.** All five paradigms show a common inverted-U RSS profile by relative depth, but diverge in *where* it decays: DINOv2/DINOv3 sustain it out to relative depth ≈ 0.6, AIMv2-L14 collapses by relative depth ≈ 0.1, and MAE keeps rising to the final layer. ORS shows the same early-layer bias; tuning bandwidth (HWHM) stays V1-like (15°–40°) across all models.
- **Pooled downstream generalization (Fig. 6, 9 tasks).** Mean min–max normalized score S(b): DINOv3 = 0.958 (best), DINOv2 = 0.670, SigLIP2 = 0.521, MAE = 0.405, DeiT3 = 0.393, AIMv2 = 0.138 (worst) — a 7× spread that tracks the RSS fall-off shape, not merely its presence.
- **Per-task frozen-backbone performance (Table 1, L-tier).** On Tiny-ImageNet-200 classification DINOv3 reaches 0.915 top-1 vs DeiT3 0.919 (best, marginal) vs AIMv2 0.883; on ADE20K semantic segmentation DINOv3 = 0.465 mIoU vs AIMv2 = 0.342 (+0.123 mIoU); on COCO instance segmentation DINOv3 = 0.688 mAP@50 vs AIMv2 = 0.531; on NYU-v2 surface normals DINOv3 = 0.107 vs MAE = 0.118 (MAE wins); on COCO keypoints PCK@0.5 DINOv3 = 0.873 vs AIMv2 = 0.790.
- **Cross-scale (Fig. 3).** RSS curves overlay almost exactly within every family across S/B/L/H/G tiers when lined up by relative depth — except AIMv2, the only family that fixes depth at 24 and scales width alone, whose RSS curve changes drastically with scale. This is evidence that depth preserves *how* features are composed at a given relative depth while width changes *what* is encoded.
- **Training trajectory (Fig. 4, OpenCLIP ViT-H/14).** Earlier, most-selective layers mature orientation selectivity very early in training; later layers keep dropping it gradually across epochs. Early-to-mid layers end up with *more* orientation-selective units than an untrained network (ORS raised above the random-init floor), while deep layers lose selectivity — training reshapes the code rather than simply sharpening it.
- **RSS predicts optimal fine-tuning depth (Figs. 5, 7).** The RSS peak measured once on the frozen backbone coincides with the empirical LoRA-unfreeze optimum: for DINOv3 (S/B/L) both peak at relative depth d = 0.25 (block 4 for ViT-S/16, block 6 for ViT-L/16); for AIMv2-L/14 both peak at d ≈ 0.1. Performance falls sharply as k approaches the last blocks.
- **RSS beats 18 alternatives (App. A.5, DINOv3 ViT-S/16).** RSS at block k ranks 1st of 18 a-priori predictors on every measure: shape similarity 0.76, |Pearson| = 0.83, |Spearman| = 0.92, argopt-∆ = 0 (peak lands exactly on k = 4). The result is robust to per-task scoring (composite shape 0.72, still 1st of 18).
- **MAE consistency check.** MAE's monotonically rising RSS profile matches its known behavior: linear-probe MAE ≈ 75% top-1 while fine-tuned ViT-L ≈ 87.8% (He et al., 2022), exactly what an RSS that never hands off to a semantic code predicts.

## Authors & Credibility

The four authors span University of Washington (lead author, corresponding), Microsoft, TU Munich, and Nvidia — a complementary mix of academic and industry affiliations typical of mechanistic-interpretability work that needs access to many large pretrained checkpoints. The work cites and builds on the standard interpretability stack (TransformerLens, NNsight, Prisma, Cunningham et al. SAEs, Bricken et al.), classical visual neuroscience (Hubel & Wiesel, Ringach, DiCarlo), and recent model-human alignment work (Brain-Score, Raugel et al. 2025 on DINOv3 brain-likeness, Cai et al. 2025 on low-level visual system). The methodology is conservative in the right places: it uses three readout bases, a permutation null that mirrors the frequency-selection step (so significance cannot be inflated by cherry-picking frequency), a basis-invariant population metric (RSS) for cross-architecture comparisons, and best-checkpoint selection over a fixed budget rather than early stopping. The probing protocol and per-task benchmarking are spelled out in reproducible detail (9 relative depths, 5 seeds, fixed hyperparameters r=8/α=16, clamped epoch budget).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** Genuine. Orientation selectivity in CNNs/V1 has been studied for a decade, but a *systematic, basis-invariant, cross-paradigm, cross-scale, cross-trajectory* comparison of how the V1-like code is laid down across ViT depth — with a derived practical prescription (RSS predicts the unfreeze depth) — is new. The within-contrastive control (OpenCLIP vs MetaCLIP vs SigLIP2, isolating data and loss one factor at a time) is a thoughtful confound control that many comparable papers omit.
- **Rigor.** Strong on average. The permutation null that gives the null the same 5 frequency chances as the real statistic is exactly the right design choice. The use of three readout bases protects against the well-known "no privileged basis in the residual stream" objection. RSS being basis-invariant is correctly leveraged for cross-scale and cross-paradigm claims.
- **Reproducibility.** Good in principle — code promised as a Python package, hyperparameters and depth grids tabulated, 5 seeds per (task, k) — but with caveats: SAE dictionary size, sparsity λ, and training steps are stated to be "held identical across all models" but their actual values are not given in the main text (only the generic form), and the task pooling (Eq. 11) and 18-predictor ranking (App. A.5) involve several scoring variants that a re-implementer would need to match exactly.
- **Significance.** Practical headline (RSS → best LoRA unfreeze depth, no sweep) is the kind of finding practitioners reuse; the cross-scale conclusion (depth preserves composition, width changes content) is a clean, quotable scientific result.
- **Red flags / overclaiming.** Several, mostly mild:
  - *Sample size for the fine-tuning claim.* The "RSS predicts optimal depth" result rests on **6 backbones**, and for several of them the pooled-performance curves (Fig. 5) are broad and flat near the optimum, so "peak lands almost exactly on top of each other" is sometimes within a near-tie rather than a sharp prediction. The claim is honestly hedged ("almost exactly", "on or almost exactly"), but the practical value would be clearer with sharper curves and more backbones.
  - *Synthetic-only probing.* The authors themselves flag this: gratings isolate orientation cleanly but do not capture how orientation is processed in naturalistic images. A model could look V1-like on gratings and behave differently on real images; the linear-decoding check (App. A.2) mitigates but does not eliminate this.
  - *Single feature, single visual area.* Restricted to orientation selectivity (V1 analog); the suite does not yet cover spatial frequency, color, motion direction, or higher ventral/dorsal analogs — again self-acknowledged.
  - *MAE generalization caveat.* MAE scores poorly frozen (S=0.405), but MAE is well known to need fine-tuning to be competitive; using a frozen linear/1×1-conv readout systematically penalizes MAE, so MAE's low pooled score partly reflects the evaluation protocol, not just its orientation code.
  - *AIMv2 "general-purpose" rebuttal.* The paper explicitly contradicts AIMv2's authors' framing; this is intellectually honest but rests on AIMv2-L14 only at L-tier (the cross-scale section does show AIMv2's curve changing with width, which complicates a single-backbone dismissal).
  - *Taskonomy-tiny only for fine-tuning.* The fine-tuning sweep uses Taskonomy-tiny for compute reasons; results on larger splits or on the actual downstream benchmarks (COCO, ADE20K, NYU) used for the frozen benchmark are not reported, so the practical prescription is validated on a proxy, not the production tasks.

## Strengths

- Conceptually clean: orientation selectivity is the canonical V1 feature with a precise tuning-curve characterization, making it an ideal, interpretable probe.
- Methodologically careful: basis-invariant RSS for cross-model claims, three readout bases, permutation null that mirrors the frequency-selection step, complementary linear-decoding analysis.
- The within-contrastive control (OpenCLIP/MetaCLIP/SigLIP2) cleanly separates data, loss, and patch-size confounds.
- The cross-scale conclusion (relative depth governs the code; width vs depth have distinct roles, with AIMv2 as the natural experiment) is a crisp, novel scientific result.
- A genuinely useful, cheap practical prescription: one RSS pass on the frozen backbone replaces an expensive LoRA-depth sweep.
- Honest framing throughout — findings are labeled as conjectures where appropriate (e.g., orientation maturity as a progressive-freezing signal is explicitly "not validated").

## Limitations

- Synthetic grating stimuli only; natural-image orientation processing not directly probed.
- Restricted to a single low-level feature (orientation) and a single cortical area analog (V1); spatial frequency, color, motion, and higher ventral/dorsal streams not covered.
- Fine-tuning prescription validated on 6 backbones on Taskonomy-tiny only, with several broad pooled-performance curves; generalization to larger splits and production benchmarks unverified.
- SAE hyperparameters (dictionary size, λ, steps) stated as fixed but values not given in main text.
- Pooled frozen-backbone benchmark penalizes models that need fine-tuning (notably MAE), conflating "frozen linear-readout quality" with "quality of the orientation code."
- AIMv2 cross-scale behavior is interesting but complicates the single-L-tier critique of AIMv2 as "not general-purpose."

## Relevance

对研究 Vision Transformer 内部表征机制、模型可解释性、以及视觉基础模型评估方法的读者高度相关：IRIS 提供了一套可复用的神经科学启发探针工具，并通过 RSS 预测最佳微调深度直接对接"如何高效复用预训练 ViT 主干"这一实际工程问题。对关注自监督/对比/掩码/自回归等不同预训练范式差异的研究者，本文的跨范式系统对比也提供了有价值的实证基准。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the RSS-predicted optimal LoRA unfreeze depth generalize beyond Taskonomy-tiny to large-scale production benchmarks (full COCO, ADE20K, NYU-v2) and to non-LoRA adaptation methods (full fine-tuning, prefix tuning)?
- How do the orientation metrics behave on naturalistic stimuli (e.g., orientation-controlled natural image patches) vs. gratings — does the inverted-U depth profile and the DINOv3 > AIMv2 ordering survive?
- Can the suite be extended to other V1-like features (spatial frequency, color contrast, motion direction, disparity) and to ventral/dorsal analogs, and do those features co-localize with orientation-selective layers?
- Is the "depth preserves composition, width changes content" finding (with AIMv2 as the natural experiment) reproducible in a controlled setting where depth and width are varied within a single training run, rather than across families?
- Does orientation maturity across training checkpoints (Sec. 4.3) actually enable a progressive-freezing schedule that preserves downstream performance and reduces compute, as the authors conjecture but do not validate?
- How sensitive are the cross-paradigm conclusions to the within-contrastive confounds the authors could not fully remove (patch size 14 vs 16, dataset composition), and would a fully controlled re-pretraining reproduce the DINO > CLIP > MAE > AIMv2 ordering on RSS?
- Do the RSS-derived predictions transfer to non-ViT architectures (CNNs, Mamba-style vision backbones, hybrid CNN-ViT) where locality and global mixing differ from first layer onward?
