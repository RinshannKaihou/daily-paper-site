> **一句话总结：** 本文为流行的大模型训练优化器 MUON（基于动量与 Newton-Schulz 近似正交化）建立了一个涵盖任意数量广义 NS 步（同时覆盖原始 MUON 多项式与 Polar Express 方法）的严格误差分析，证明其均方误差上界为 C(M⁻¹ + γₙ)（M 为批大小、γₙ 为学习率），并证明在简单的二次随机优化问题上对几乎所有的批大小 M，MUON 无法收敛到真实解——这是 MUON 在原 NS 多项式下的首个非收敛性结果。

> **TL;DR:** This paper provides the first rigorous error analysis for the MUON optimizer under an arbitrary finite number of generalized Newton-Schulz (NS) steps — a framework covering both the original MUON polynomial used in the PyTorch library and the Polar Express method used in modded-nanoGPT — yielding a mean-square bound E‖Θₙ−ϑ‖² ≤ C(M⁻¹ + γₙ); it also proves, for a simple one-dimensional quadratic stochastic optimization problem (SOP) with non-symmetric data, that for almost every mini-batch size M, MUON fails to converge to the SOP solution (lim supₙ E[min{1,|Θₙ−E[X]₃|}] > 0), in contrast to standard/momentum SGD which admits the tighter C√γₙ bound without the irreducible M⁻¹ term.

## 核心贡献

- **广义 NS 框架统一两种实现：** 提出含任意有限次广义 Newton-Schulz 迭代、多项式阶数可任意高的 MUON 变体（Definition 1.1），并形式化证明该框架同时覆盖 Jordan et al. (2024) 原始 MUON 多项式（PyTorch 库 `torch.optim.Muon` 中的 5 步 NS，系数 3.4445/−4.7750/2.0315）与 Amsel et al. (2025) 的 Polar Express 方法（modded-nanoGPT 实现）。
- **首个针对"实现版"MUON 的误差分析（Theorem 1.2 / Theorem 3.17）：** 在凸性 + Lipschitz 梯度假设下证明 E‖Θₙ−ϑ‖² ≤ C(M⁻¹ + γₙ)，给出关于梯度步数 n 与批大小 M 的显式收敛速率；这是文献中首个对"使用原始 NS 多项式、有限步数"的真实 MUON 给出的误差分析。
- **MUON 的非收敛性（Theorem 1.3 / Theorem 6.16）：** 对一类简单的二次 SOP（L(θ,x)=|θ−x|²），只要数据三阶中心矩非零 E[(X−E[X])³]≠0，则对几乎所有批大小 M，lim supₙ E[min{1,|Θₙᴹ−E[X₁,₁]|}] > 0，即 MUON 不收敛到总体最小化解；这是 MUON（原始 NS 多项式）的首个非收敛结果。
- **方法学创新——MUON 型极限定理（Proposition 6.2）：** 类比 Adam 极限定理（Dereich–Jentzen–Kassing；Adam 对称定理），证明任何收敛子序列的极限必为 MUON 向量场的零点，并据此推出非收敛性。
- **若干具体可应用的推论：** 将主定理特化到 ℓ₂-正则化逻辑回归（Corollary 4.2）、二次 SOP（Corollary 4.4/4.5）与确定性优化问题（Corollary 4.6/4.7，后者给出 ‖Θₙ−ϑ‖ ≤ C√γₙ₊₁ 的强误差界），展示理论的实际覆盖面。

## Key Contributions

- **Unified generalized-NS framework:** Defines a MUON variant with an arbitrary finite number of generalized Newton-Schulz steps and polynomials of arbitrary degree (Definition 1.1), and proves (Lemma 5.1 & 5.2) it covers both the original MUON polynomial from Jordan et al. (2024) used in the PyTorch `torch.optim.Muon` and the Polar Express method from Amsel et al. (2025) used in modded-nanoGPT.
- **First error analysis for the *implemented* MUON (Theorem 1.2 / Theorem 3.17):** Under convexity and Lipschitz-gradient assumptions, establishes E‖Θₙ−ϑ‖² ≤ C(M⁻¹ + γₙ) — an explicit rate in both the number of gradient steps n and the mini-batch size M. The authors claim this is the first such result for MUON with the originally proposed NS polynomial and a finite number of NS steps (prior work either studied the *idealized* MUON with infinitely many NS steps, or imposed an unverified δₖ < 1 smallness assumption that excludes the implementations actually used).
- **Non-convergence of MUON (Theorem 1.3 / Theorem 6.16):** For the simple quadratic SOP L(θ,x)=|θ−x|² with bounded i.i.d. data whose third central moment is non-zero, MUON fails to converge to the minimizer E[X₁,₁] for almost every mini-batch size M. This is the first non-convergence result for MUON with its original NS polynomial.
- **A MUON-type limit theorem (Proposition 6.2):** Mirrors the Adam limit theorem / Adam symmetry theorem programme — any L¹-limit of MUON iterates must be a zero of the MUON vector field; combining this with a sharp analysis of stationary momentum random variables yields the non-convergence conclusion.
- **Concrete specializations:** The main theorem is instantiated for ℓ₂-regularized logistic regression for binary classification (Corollary 4.2), quadratic SOPs (Corollary 4.4/4.5), and deterministic problems (Corollary 4.6 giving pathwise rates, Corollary 4.7 giving the strong bound ‖Θₙ−ϑ‖ ≤ C√γₙ₊₁).

## Methodology

The paper is purely theoretical (no experiments). It proceeds in three layers:

1. **Pathwise a priori bounds (Section 2, Theorem 2.5 / Corollary 2.6).** For a broad class of momentum-based optimization processes that includes MUON, the authors derive deterministic (pathwise) bounds of the form supₙ ‖Θₙ‖ ≤ C a.s. (eq. 2.61/2.67), plus the key averaged-gradient estimate ‖mₙ − G(Θₙ₋₁)‖ ≤ C·γₙ (Lemma 2.2). These bounds do *not* require boundedness of the iterates as an assumption — they prove it.

2. **Error analysis under boundedness (Section 3).** A recursive inequality on the weak error eₙ = E|L(Θₙ) − inf L| is established (Theorem 3.6 via Lemma 3.2–3.5), with the recursion eₙ ≤ Cτ + Cγₙ where τ is an orthogonalization-error term. Combining with the a priori bounds removes the boundedness assumption (Theorem 3.15 → Theorem 3.17), yielding the headline bound E‖Θₙ−ϑ‖ᵖ ≤ C(M⁻¹ + γₙ) and variants such as E‖Θₙ−ϑ‖ᵖ ≤ C(M⁻¹/(4−2r) + γₙ₊₁) under polynomially decaying γₙ = c·n⁻ʳ. A separate analysis (Theorem 3.10) covers the *idealized* MUON (infinitely many NS steps).

3. **Non-convergence (Section 6, ~25 pages).** The authors introduce *stationary momentum random variables (SMRVs)*, characterize their properties (Lemma 6.6, 6.8, 6.9, 6.10), and prove sharp convergence rates for the expectation of their signed square (Proposition 6.11 / Theorem 6.13). For non-symmetric data (E[(X−E[X])³] ≠ 0) they show the MUON vector field has a non-vanishing evaluation (Subsection 6.4), and via the MUON-type limit theorem (Proposition 6.2) conclude non-convergence (Theorem 6.16).

A central technical device is a **generalized NS method** (Definition 1.1): given coefficients b = (bᵢ,ⱼ), the iteration NSᵇᵏ(A) approximates the polar factor of A. The framework is general enough that Lemmas 5.1 and 5.2 reduce the two production implementations (PyTorch MUON; modded-nanoGPT Polar Express) to specific choices of b.

## Results & Highlights

This is a theory paper with **no empirical benchmarks**; the quantitative results are proven bounds and a proven non-convergence theorem. The concrete quantitative claims are:

- **Main error bound (Theorem 1.2, eq. 1.5):** For the generalized MUON process with mini-batch size M, learning rate γₙ, and K NS steps, E‖Θₙ−ϑ‖² ≤ C(M⁻¹ + γₙ). The mini-batch term **M⁻¹ is irreducible** — it cannot be removed even as n → ∞ at fixed M, unlike standard/momentum SGD whose mean-square error is bounded by C√γₙ alone.
- **Non-convergence (Theorem 1.3, eq. 1.8):** For the SOP with L(θ,x)=|θ−x|² and E[(X₁,₁−E[X₁,₁])³] ≠ 0, for almost every M ∈ ℕ, lim supₙ E[min{1, |Θₙᴹ − E[X₁,₁]|}] > 0. This establishes a strict separation: MUON provably cannot achieve the SGD-style C√γₙ rate on this problem class.
- **Deterministic problem bound (Corollary 4.7, eq. 4.55):** When the SOP is deterministic (X is one-point distributed, so the third central moment vanishes and the non-convergence obstruction disappears), MUON *does* converge with the strong pathwise rate ‖Θₙ−ϑ‖ ≤ C√γₙ₊₁ — matching SGD.
- **General p-th moment bound (Theorem 3.17):** Under γₙ = c·n⁻ʳ with r ∈ (0,1), E‖Θₙ−ϑ‖ᵖ ≤ C·M⁻¹/(4−2r) + C·γₙ₊₁, and the weak error E|L(Θₙ) − inf L| ≤ C·M⁻¹/(2−r) + C·γₙ (eq. 3.70/3.81), explicitly separating the mini-batch and step-size contributions.
- **Implementation coverage:** Lemma 5.1 verifies the assumptions for the PyTorch MUON polynomial with coefficients (3.4445, −4.7750, 2.0315) over 5 NS steps; Lemma 5.2 verifies them for the Polar Express coefficients (e.g. 8.1566, −22.4833, 15.8788 at step k=1) — so the theorems provably apply to both deployed implementations, not just an idealization.

No quantitative results reported in the empirical/benchmark sense; the paper reports proven mathematical rates instead.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** High and well-scoped. The claim of being the *first* error analysis for MUON with the original NS polynomial and a finite number of NS steps is carefully justified against the closest prior work: An et al. [66] requires an unverified supₖ δₖ < 1 smallness assumption on the orthogonalization error that this paper shows (Lemma 5.3/5.4) is *not* satisfied by the PyTorch MUON polynomial or the Polar Express method in simple examples; Chang–Liu–Yuan [8] only get convergence by letting the momentum decay α increase to 1 during training, which contradicts the constant α = 0.95 used in real implementations [1, 37]. The non-convergence result (Theorem 1.3) is, to my knowledge, the first for MUON with its actual NS polynomial.
- **Rigor:** Very high — 80 pages of full proofs; every corollary is reduced to Theorem 3.17 plus an elementary lemma; the limit theorem (Proposition 6.2) is proven in full and the sharp convergence rate for SMRVs (Proposition 6.11) is developed from scratch.
- **Reproducibility:** The object of study (MUON dynamics) is fully specified, so the theorems are reproducible in principle; but the *non-convergence* result is existential ("for almost every M") and the construction (one-dimensional quadratic SOP) is far from any realistic LLM training scenario, so it is a mathematical pathology result, not a practical warning about deployed MUON runs.
- **Significance:** Theoretically significant — it closes a gap in the MUON literature by covering the *actually deployed* NS polynomial rather than idealized variants, and it sharpens our understanding that MUON has a fundamentally different (and in this case worse on the irreducible M⁻¹ term) error profile than SGD. Practical impact is indirect: practitioners will not stop using MUON on the basis of a 1-D quadratic counterexample, but the M⁻¹ term suggests large-batch MUON training is theoretically motivated.
- **Red flags / paper-specific concerns:**
  - The non-convergence requires E[(X−E[X])³] ≠ 0 (non-symmetric data); the authors are careful to note that for deterministic problems (Corollary 4.7) MUON *does* converge at the SGD rate C√γₙ, so the result is a stochastic-data pathology, not a fundamental defect.
  - The non-convergence theorem is restricted to "a very specific one-dimensional quadratic example class" (the authors' own words, line 3706) — it is not shown to extend to higher dimensions or non-quadratic losses.
  - No numerical verification of the predicted M⁻¹ saturation floor is provided; a single simulation plot would have materially strengthened the otherwise purely abstract message.
  - Heavy reliance on LLM assistance (GPT-5.5/5.6 Pro) for core lemma statements and proof ideas is disclosed; while the authors take responsibility, a reader cannot independently distinguish human-derived from model-derived arguments.
  - The constant C throughout is non-constructive and unspecified; the rates are asymptotic only.

## Strengths

- Genuinely unifies and rigorously covers both production MUON implementations (PyTorch polynomial + Polar Express), not just an idealization.
- Provides both a positive result (explicit convergence rate with the irreducible M⁻¹ term identified) and a sharp negative result (non-convergence) — a complete picture.
- The MUON-type limit theorem (Proposition 6.2) is a reusable technical tool that, like the Adam analogue, will likely support further MUON theory.
- Honest, detailed literature review explicitly positioning the contribution against [8], [27], [63]–[66] and explaining precisely which assumptions each prior result needs and why they fail for the deployed implementations.
- Full proofs throughout; corollaries for logistic regression, quadratic SOPs, and deterministic problems make the abstract theorem tangible.

## Limitations

- **No experiments.** The M⁻¹ saturation floor and the non-convergence phenomenon are never illustrated numerically; a single figure would have clarified whether the effect is visible at practical batch sizes.
- **Scope of non-convergence is narrow:** restricted to a one-dimensional quadratic SOP with non-symmetric data. Whether MUON fails to converge on higher-dimensional or realistic losses is open.
- **Constants are non-constructive:** the rates are asymptotic (in n and M); no explicit C, so no guidance on practical step/batch schedules.
- **Assumptions are strong:** convexity + κ-strong convexity + Lipschitz gradient + bounded data; non-convex neural-network training (the actual use case of MUON) is not covered.
- **LLM-assisted proofs:** a portion of the lemma statements and proof ideas originate from GPT-5.5/5.6 Pro (Subsection 1.7); the authors take responsibility, but some readers may want independent verification of the model-derived steps.

## Relevance

对研究大模型训练优化器（特别是 MUON 及其 Newton-Schulz/Polar Express 变体）理论性质的工作高度相关：本文首次为"实现版"MUON 给出了严格的收敛速率上界 E‖Θₙ−ϑ‖² ≤ C(M⁻¹ + γₙ) 并指出其相比 SGD 多出一个不可消除的 M⁻¹ 项，同时证明了一类简单问题上的非收敛性——为理解 MUON 在大模型训练中的批大小/学习率权衡提供了理论依据与潜在风险提示。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the non-convergence result (Theorem 6.16) be extended beyond one-dimensional quadratic SOPs to matrix-valued (d₁×d₂) losses that MUON is actually designed for, or to non-quadratic convex losses?
- Does the irreducible M⁻¹ floor in E‖Θₙ−ϑ‖² ≤ C(M⁻¹ + γₙ) show up empirically in realistic MUON training runs (e.g., nanoGPT) as a batch-size-dependent error plateau?
- The non-convergence requires α ∈ (0,1) held constant; could a schedule that lets α → 1 (as in Chang–Liu–Yuan [8]) restore convergence *without* harming practical performance at α = 0.95?
- The MUON-type limit theorem (Proposition 6.2) currently covers only the 1-D quadratic case; can it be generalized to a full "MUON symmetry theorem" analogous to the Adam symmetry theorem [14]?
- How do the bounds change under the non-convex, L-smooth + bounded-variance assumptions used in [27, 63, 65], and can the M⁻¹ term be improved there?
