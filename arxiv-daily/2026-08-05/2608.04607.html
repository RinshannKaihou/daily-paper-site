On MUON optimization: From non-convergence to an error analysis with Polar Express and the Newton-Schulz polynomial from implementations

Thang Do · Steffen Dereich · Arnulf Jentzen  —  CUHK-Shenzhen / University of Münster
◆ Key Finding

First rigorous error analysis of the deployed MUON optimizer bounds MSE by C(M⁻¹ + γₙ) — exposing an irreducible M⁻¹ floor that SGD lacks.

Key Metric · The Irreducible Floor
M−1
E‖Θₙ − ϑ‖² ≤ C·(M⁻¹ + γₙ)
The mini-batch term M⁻¹ cannot be removed — not even as n → ∞ at fixed M. Standard / momentum SGD achieves only C·√γₙ, with no such floor.
Why This Matters
First rigorous rate for the actually deployed MUON optimizer reveals an irreducible mini-batch M⁻¹ floor absent from SGD — informing batch-size / learning-rate tradeoffs in large-model training.
Critical Edge
Non-convergence is restricted to a one-dimensional quadratic SOP with non-symmetric data — far from realistic LLM training — and no numerical verification of the predicted M⁻¹ saturation floor is provided.

01 Methodology / three-layer proof

Purely theoretical — no experiments. The argument stacks three layers, each removing an assumption the prior literature had to impose.

  1. Pathwise a priori bounds. For a broad class of momentum processes incl. MUON, deterministic bounds supₙ ‖Θₙ‖ ≤ C a.s. and the averaged-gradient estimate ‖mₙ − G(Θₙ₋₁)‖ ≤ C·γₙ are proven, not assumed. §2 · Thm 2.5
  2. Error analysis under boundedness. A recursive inequality on the weak error eₙ = E|L(Θₙ) − inf L|, combined with the a priori bounds, removes the boundedness assumption and yields the headline rate. §3 · Thm 3.17
  3. Non-convergence via SMRVs. Introduces stationary momentum random variables, sharpens their signed-square rate, shows the MUON vector field has non-vanishing evaluation for non-symmetric data, and concludes via a MUON-type limit theorem. §6 · Prop 6.2 · Thm 6.16

Central device: a generalized NS method (Def. 1.1) — Lemmas 5.1 / 5.2 reduce both PyTorch MUON and modded-nanoGPT Polar Express to specific coefficient choices b = (bᵢ,ⱼ).

02 Results / proven bounds, no benchmarks

A clean separation: positive rate with an irreducible floor, plus a hard negative result on a simple problem class.

  • Main bound. E‖Θₙ−ϑ‖² ≤ C(M⁻¹ + γₙ) under convexity + Lipschitz gradient. First such result for MUON with its original NS polynomial and finite NS steps.
  • Non-convergence. For L(θ,x)=|θ−x|² with E[(X−E[X])³]≠0, for almost every M: lim supₙ E[min{1,|Θₙᴹ−E[X]|}] > 0.
  • Deterministic case recovers SGD rate. When the third central moment vanishes, MUON converges at ‖Θₙ−ϑ‖ ≤ C√γₙ₊₁ — matching SGD. Cor 4.7
  • General p-th moment. Under γₙ = c·n⁻ʳ: E‖Θₙ−ϑ‖ᵖ ≤ C·M⁻¹ᐟ⁽⁴⁻²ʳ⁾ + C·γₙ₊₁.
  • Deployed-implementation coverage. Lemmas 5.1 / 5.2 verify the assumptions for PyTorch coefficients (3.4445, −4.7750, 2.0315) over 5 NS steps and Polar Express.

03 Strengths vs Limitations / a complete but narrow picture

＋ Strengths

  • Genuinely unifies both production implementations (PyTorch polynomial + Polar Express), not an idealization.
  • Delivers both a positive rate (with the irreducible M⁻¹ term identified) and a sharp negative result.
  • The MUON-type limit theorem (Prop 6.2) is a reusable tool, analogous to the Adam symmetry programme.
  • Honest literature positioning vs [8], [27], [63]–[66], explaining precisely which assumptions fail for deployed code.
  • Full 80-page proofs; corollaries for logistic regression, quadratic SOPs, and deterministic problems.

− Limitations

  • No experiments. The M⁻¹ floor and non-convergence are never illustrated numerically — a single plot would help.
  • Narrow scope of non-convergence: 1-D quadratic SOP with non-symmetric data only. Higher-dim / non-quadratic is open.
  • Constants non-constructive: rates are asymptotic in n and M; no explicit C, no practical schedules.
  • Strong assumptions: convexity + strong convexity + Lipschitz gradient + bounded data. Non-convex NN training — MUON's actual use case — is not covered.
  • LLM-assisted proofs (GPT-5.5/5.6 Pro) for some lemma statements; authors take responsibility, but independent verification is harder.

04 Questions for Further Exploration / where the theory breaks next

  • Can the non-convergence result (Thm 6.16) extend beyond 1-D quadratic SOPs to the matrix-valued (d₁×d₂) losses MUON is actually designed for, or to non-quadratic convex losses? generalization
  • Does the irreducible M⁻¹ floor show up empirically in realistic MUON runs (e.g. nanoGPT) as a batch-size-dependent error plateau? empirical
  • Non-convergence assumes α held constant; could a schedule α → 1 (as in Chang–Liu–Yuan [8]) restore convergence without harming performance at α = 0.95? schedule
  • Can Prop 6.2 be promoted into a full "MUON symmetry theorem" analogous to the Adam symmetry theorem [14]? theory
  • How do the bounds change under the non-convex, L-smooth + bounded-variance assumptions of [27, 63, 65], and can M⁻¹ be improved there? non-convex
