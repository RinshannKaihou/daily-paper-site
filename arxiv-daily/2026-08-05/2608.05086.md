> **一句话总结：** 将心理测量学中的项目反应理论（IRT）拟合到 8 个安全基准、192 个语言模型上，发现三个潜在因子（拒绝严格性、真实性、上下文危害）可解释 77% 的方差；用约 75 个题目（占全集 2%）即可恢复排名，且能检测 80–100% 的选择性"沙包"行为及模型替换。

> **TL;DR:** The authors fit two-parameter logistic Item Response Theory (IRT) models to 5,255 items from 8 LLM safety benchmarks across 192 models — the largest psychometric analysis of safety evaluation to date — and show that three latent factors explain 77% of between-model variance, that ~75 psychometrically selected items (2% of the suite) recover rankings at ρ ≥ 0.92 while ~10 adaptive items cut individual-benchmark evaluation cost by 97–99% (18–34% lower RMSE than random subsets), and that IRT-derived person-fit, ability-shift, and response-fingerprint audits catch 80–100% of selective prompted sandbagging and match 13 of 14 anonymous API endpoints to their identities.

## 核心贡献

- **首次将 IRT 系统性应用于 LLM 安全评测**：在 8 个安全基准（共 5,067 个有效题目）和 192 个语言模型上拟合二参数 logistic（2PL）IRT 模型，规模为同类研究之最，并把心理测量学从"压缩能力基准"扩展到安全基准的解读、精简与审计三大用途。
- **揭示安全基准的三因子潜在结构**：通过最小残差因子分析（oblimin 旋转）发现"拒绝严格性、真实性、上下文危害"三个潜在因子可解释 77% 的模型间方差（CFI=0.97），证明单一"安全分数"不可信，且 OR-Bench-Hard 与 HarmBench/SORRY-Bench 测量相反方向的行为。
- **实现 97–99% 的评测成本压缩**：3 个固定 25 题静态测试恢复全部三个潜在能力，约 10 道 CAT 自适应题目在 HarmBench/SORRY-Bench/OR-Bench-Hard 上比随机子集误差低 18–34%，且在 25 题预算内 ρ 达到 0.94。
- **构建黑盒审计工具链**：提出 person-fit（l_z）、ability-shift、response-fingerprint 三级递进审计法，分别捕获选择性沙包、整体行为漂移与同能力级模型替换，联合检测率达 94–97%。
- **实证揭示 API 端点不一致性**：response-fingerprint 在 500 题上将 16 个重采端点中的 13/14 匿名身份正确匹配，并发现某端点两个月后 TruthfulQA 响应变化 33.6%（自零假设仅 2.0%），证明同一模型标识下的行为可被静默更换。

## Key Contributions

- **Largest psychometric fit of LLM safety benchmarks to date**: Fits regularized 2PL IRT models to 5,067 items across 8 widely-used safety benchmarks (AdvBench, HarmBench, SORRY-Bench, Do-Not-Answer, AHB, OR-Bench-Hard, Health-ORSC, TruthfulQA) for 192 chat models spanning 1B–~1T parameters and proprietary APIs (GPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok), extending IRT beyond the capability-benchmark compression of tinyBenchmarks/metabench/FluidBenchmark.
- **Three-factor latent structure with crisp interpretation**: Minimum-residual factor analysis with oblimin rotation (selected by CFI=0.97, Horn's parallel analysis) yields three factors — refusal strictness, truthfulness, contextual harm — explaining 77% of variance, formally showing that the suite cannot be summarized by one safety ability and that OR-Bench-Hard loads with opposite sign to HarmBench/SORRY-Bench.
- **97–99% evaluation cost reduction with ranking fidelity preserved**: Three fixed 25-item static tests (75 items, <2% of suite) recover the three latent abilities at Spearman ρ=0.94; ~10 CAT-chosen items recover individual benchmarks with 18–34% lower RMSE than random 10-item subsets, and CAT beats random subsampling on the sign-aligned full-suite composite at every budget up to ~55 items.
- **Three-tier black-box audit ladder for sandbagging and API substitution**: Person-fit l_z catches 80–100% of targeted/triggered prompted sandbagging (10% FPR), historical ability-shift catches the uniform sandbagging that person-fit misses (median Δθ=+1.69 logits), and response fingerprints on 500 items distinguish 99.5% of 8,911 cohort model pairs and match 13/14 re-collected endpoint identities.
- **Empirical demonstration of behavioral drift behind APIs**: Shows the audit tools surface a real endpoint whose TruthfulQA responses changed 33.6% over two months against a 2.0% resampling null, and two catalog entries serving the same weights that the method correctly declines to separate.

## Methodology

The pipeline has three stages applied to a single IRT calibration (Figure 1):

**Calibration.** The authors collect one zero-temperature response per model–item pair via OpenRouter for 192 models on 8 benchmarks (5,255 items pre-processing, 5,067 after removing unscored and zero-variance items). Each response is scored with its benchmark's prescribed judge (string match, LLM judge, fine-tuned classifier, BLEURT-20, etc.). They fit a regularized two-parameter logistic (2PL) IRT model P(x_mj=1|θ_m,a_j,b_j)=σ(a_j(θ_m−b_j)) per benchmark, where θ is model ability, b is item difficulty, and a is discrimination. Regularization (log-normal prior on discrimination, normal prior on intercept) is justified via split-half calibration: top-25 items' discrimination retention rises from 28%/38% (AdvBench/HarmBench, unregularized) to 68%/76% (regularized). The 2PL is chosen over 1PL/3PL/4PL by likelihood-ratio tests, AIC, and BIC (Appendix E): the equal-discrimination 1PL is rejected on all 8 benchmarks, and richer models do not improve fit.

**Reading (latent structure).** Per-benchmark ability estimates form a 134×8 ability matrix, analyzed by minimum-residual factor analysis with oblimin rotation. Factor count is chosen by RMSEA, CFI, and Horn's parallel analysis. An unconstrained multidimensional IRT (MIRT) fit on the 134×5,055 item matrix (Appendix C) and a permutation parallel analysis corroborate the structure.

**Reducing (benchmark distillation).** Two approaches: (a) static 25-item tests selected for high Fisher information across the ability range; (b) computerized adaptive testing (CAT) that picks the most informative item at the current θ estimate. Both are evaluated on held-out models (75/25 splits, 20 repeats) by RMSE on full-score recovery (primary) and Spearman ρ on rank recovery, against stratified random subsets of identical size and budget composition.

**Auditing.** Three black-box tests requiring progressively more reference information (Table 2): (1) person-fit l_z against public item parameters only; (2) ability shift against a historical θ estimate; (3) response fingerprint against retained reference responses. Sandbagging interventions are constructed as prompted model organisms on HarmBench (3 framings × 3 scopes: uniform / targeted / triggered selective sandbagging), evaluated fully blind at a 10% FPR operating point set from 171 genuine cohort models.

## Results & Highlights

- **Three factors explain 77% of variance in safety abilities.** A one-factor model explains only 47% (RMSEA=0.32); two factors explain 69% but CFI=0.88 fails the 0.95 threshold; three factors reach 77% variance with CFI=0.97, and Horn's parallel analysis retains three factors. The authors conclude the eight benchmarks cannot be summarized as a single "safety" ability.
- **Three fixed 25-item static tests recover all three latent abilities with lower error than random subsets of equal size.** RMSE 0.047 vs 0.062 for refusal strictness, 0.041 vs 0.048 for truthfulness, and 0.065 vs 0.067 for contextual harm, at Spearman ρ=0.94, 0.83, 0.89 against random baselines of 0.90, 0.67, 0.87. Together the 75 items are under 2% of the full suite.
- **~10 adaptive items cut individual-benchmark evaluation cost by 97–99%.** On HarmBench, SORRY-Bench, and OR-Bench-Hard, 10 CAT-chosen items achieve 18–34% lower RMSE than random 10-item subsets and recover full rankings at ρ=0.92–0.94. The advantage narrows to 4–21% at 25 items; random matches CAT at ~50 items on five benchmarks.
- **CAT beats random subsampling on the sign-aligned full-suite composite at every budget.** At 25 items CAT's error is 22% below a random subset; random does not match it until ~55 items; CAT reaches ρ=0.92 at 25 items, a level random reaches only at 75–100 items.
- **Person-fit catches 80–100% of selective prompted sandbagging but only 35% (pooled) of uniform sandbagging.** At the calibrated 10% FPR, l_z flags 90% of targeted and 97% of triggered runs across framings, but only 18–70% (35% pooled) of uniform runs.
- **Combined person-fit + ability-shift detects 94–97% of sandbagging runs in every scope.** Uniform sandbagging shifts ability by a median +1.69 logits (up to +3.94) versus +0.24 to +0.35 for selective scopes; the 40-item ability check flags 84% of uniform runs.
- **A 40-item ability test distinguishes 99.5% of 8,911 cohort model pairs.** The 41 ability-matched pairs that survive still disagree on a median of 1,052 items and a minimum of 284, enabling response fingerprints to separate even ability-matched substitutions. Fingerprints on 500 items identify 13 of 14 distinct re-collected endpoint identities and correctly decline to separate two catalog entries serving the same weights.
- **Split-half discrimination retention rises from 28%/38% to 68%/76% with regularization** (AdvBench/HarmBench mean over 10 random splits), validating the calibration priors as best practice at LLM-cohort sample sizes.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** Genuine but incremental: IRT compression of capability benchmarks is established (Maia Polo et al. 2024; Kipnis et al. 2025; Hofmann et al. 2025), and a very recent concurrent paper (Spagliardi et al. 2026) already applies CAT to six safety benchmarks. The paper's distinctive contributions are (a) the broadened safety suite including over-refusal/contextual-harm/truthfulness, (b) the construct-validity factor analysis, and (c) the person-fit/ability-shift/fingerprint audit ladder — not IRT-for-LLMs per se. The factor analysis is the most novel leg; the compression leg is largely confirmatory.
- **Rigor.** High methodological care: split-half validation of the regularization priors, a held-out 75/25 evaluation design with 20 splits, comparison against size-matched random subsets (so gains reflect item selection, not test length), an unconstrained MIRT robustness check (Appendix C) reaching the same factor conclusion, and a published model-selection table for the 1PL/2PL/3PL/4PL ladder. The 10% FPR operating point is explicitly framed as a policy choice, not a fitted quantity, with full ROC curves in Appendix B. The 2PL choice is defended empirically rather than asserted.
- **Reproducibility.** Strong: 192-model roster with OpenRouter identifiers is published (Appendix A), all response/score data and calibrations are released, the implementation matches an independent MIRT package on synthetic data (congruence 1.000). Two reproducibility caveats: (i) OpenRouter routing is itself a known source of silent behavioral change (Khoriaty 2026; Pape et al. 2026), which the paper itself documents — meaning anyone re-collecting will get a different 134×5,067 matrix; (ii) judges include proprietary/threshold-sensitive components (BLEURT-20 for TruthfulQA) whose versions drift.
- **Significance.** Practically significant for evaluators and frontier labs: a 97–99% evaluation-cost reduction plus a concrete audit ladder for sandbagging and API substitution addresses two live failure modes (selective underperformance, silent weight swaps). The construct-validity argument that an unweighted safety average can be gamed by indiscriminate refusal is important and underappreciated.
- **Red flags.** (1) Sandbagging detection is evaluated only on *prompted* model organisms, not fine-tuned or "scheming" models — the authors flag this themselves, but a model trained to evade l_z specifically could plausibly defeat person-fit while retaining a plausible score. (2) The cohort (134–169 models) is large for an LLM study but small for psychometrics; the paper honestly notes this limits adjudication between two and three dimensions, and the unconstrained MIRT fit actually prefers D=2 by BIC even though the two-stage analysis selects three — a real ambiguity the main text softens. (3) The factor names (refusal strictness, truthfulness, contextual harm) are empirical labels for loading patterns, not validated constructs — the discussion acknowledges this but the framing sometimes slips toward treating them as real abilities. (4) The "97–99% cost reduction" headline relies on the compression leg, which is the least novel leg and where the advantage over random subsampling essentially vanishes by ~50 items.

## Strengths

- Unifies three distinct goals (reading/reducing/auditing) under one calibrated measurement model, with a clean Figure 1 narrative.
- Honest about limitations: explicitly states that distilled tests do not extend the scope of the underlying benchmark and do not show items will remain informative for future models.
- The audit ladder is designed so each rung catches failure modes invisible to the previous one (Table 2), and the complementary nature of person-fit vs ability-shift is demonstrated quantitatively (94–97% combined).
- Empirical demonstration of real-world API drift (33.6% TruthfulQA change vs 2.0% null) gives the audit methods concrete teeth rather than only theoretical motivation.
- Rigorous model selection for the 2PL and for factor count, with appendices showing the conclusions survive an unconstrained MIRT refit.

## Limitations

- Cross-benchmark analyses use only 134–169 models, far below human psychometric datasets; factor-count selection is genuinely ambiguous (BIC prefers D=2 in the direct MIRT fit).
- Sandbagging evaluation uses prompted interventions only; fine-tuned or adversarially trained sandbaggers may evade person-fit statistics.
- Reduced tests reproduce the *existing* benchmark — they do not validate that the benchmark predicts deployment safety, and item informativeness will not transfer to future models without recalibration.
- Response data are mediated by imperfect judges and a single OpenRouter routing layer whose own instability is one of the things the paper measures; re-collection will not reproduce the exact matrix.
- Factor labels are descriptive summaries of loading patterns, not convergently validated constructs; consequential safety claims would require the predictive-validation evidence psychometrics demands and that this paper does not provide.
- Fingerprint null calibration is judge-dependent (2.8% self-disagreement for a stable classifier vs 25% for BLEURT), so the method requires per-judge recalibration in any new setting.

## Relevance

直接相关于 LLM 安全评测方法论与基准压缩：若你关注如何用更少算力做可信的安全评测、如何检测评测期间的"沙包"行为或 API 端点静默更换模型，本文提供了可直接落地的 IRT 校准、精简测试与三级审计工具链。其"单一安全分数不可加总、三因子结构"的结论对设计/解读安全排行榜尤其有借鉴价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How do the person-fit and ability-shift audits perform against fine-tuned (not just prompted) sandbaggers, and against models adversarially trained to minimize l_z while preserving aggregate pass rate?
- Does the three-factor structure (refusal strictness / truthfulness / contextual harm) replicate on held-out benchmarks outside this eight-benchmark suite (e.g., XSTest, WildJailbreak, multilingual safety sets), and is it stable as frontier models continue to improve?
- How rapidly do the distilled 25-item static tests become uninformative as new model generations are released, and what is the cheapest valid recalibration cadence?
- Can the response-fingerprint audit be hardened against an adversary who has the reference responses and optimizes a substitute model to reproduce them on exactly the pinned items?
- Given that BIC on the direct item-level MIRT fit prefers D=2 while CFI and parallel analysis select three, what additional data (more models, more over-refusal/truthfulness indicators) would adjudicate the dimensionality decisively?
- Do the audit tools generalize to capability benchmarks (reasoning, code, math), where sandbagging and silent API substitution are equally plausibly concerns?
