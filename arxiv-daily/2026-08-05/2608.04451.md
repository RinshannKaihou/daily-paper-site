> **一句话总结：** 本文给出一个反例，证明单个 ReLU 神经元在 Z/pZ 模加法的全表交叉熵训练下，并非必然收敛到单一非零实 Fourier 频率类——神经元可能在有限时间内"死亡"冻结，或在单点记忆轨迹上发散，极限方向的 Fourier 能量均等地分散在所有非零频率类（每类恰占 2/(p-1)）。

> **TL;DR:** The paper refutes open problem MAIS-O60: it constructs an open set of initially active initializations for a single ReLU neuron trained by full-table cross-entropy on addition in Z/pZ (p ≥ 5 prime) under Clarke subgradient flow, where the neuron either dies in finite time or slides forever memorizing a single table entry, so the normalized limiting parameter direction has Fourier-energy fraction F_ζ = 2/(p-1) in every nonzero real frequency class — strictly below 1 for p ≥ 5. This is a pure theory paper with no empirical datasets or trained baselines; the "baseline" it refutes is the conjecture itself, and the quantitative delta is the gap between the conjectured F_ζ = 1 and the achieved F_ζ = 2/(p-1).

## 核心贡献

- 给出 MAIS-O60（[Cla26d]）问题的**否定解答**：在 p ≥ 5 奇素数下，存在一个由初始活跃参数组成的非空开集 U ⊂ R^{3p}，使得可测选取的 Clarke 轨迹在有限时间内进入"死神经元"稳态，其归一化极限方向不被任何单一非零实 Fourier 类支撑，且条件概率 P(θ(0) ∈ U | initially active) > 0。
- 刻画了两种"单频对齐失败"机制：(1) **dying ReLU**——唯一活跃门在有限时间 T ≤ p³ε/(2(p-1)√Γ) 内穿越到 0 并永久冻结；(2) **单点记忆（persistent sliding）**——中心门 x(t) → ∞，参数范数 ‖θ(t)‖ → ∞，损失收敛到 (p²-1)/p²·log p 而非 0，实现了 grokking 中"记忆 vs 泛化"二分法里最小的记忆分支。
- 证明冻结/极限态的 Fourier 能量在所有非零实频率类上**均等分布**：每类恰占 2/(p-1)，对 p ≥ 5 严格小于 1，因此对任意 0 < δ < 1 - 2/(p-1) 都不满足 (δ, [ρ_ζ])-pure。
- 附录（由 GPT-5.6 Sol 起草、作者审校）给出四重加强反例：对**每条** Clarke 轨迹（选择无关）、在 ReLU′(0)=0 约定下、对 ReLU 的 C^∞ 死区光滑化 σ_ε、以及对定步长全批量梯度下降，失败均成立。
- 明确界定了结论的适用范围：失败依赖 ReLU 的精确死区，故对 softplus 等"处处为正"的光滑激活**问题仍然开放**；定理 A.4 不覆盖动量、Adam、随机 mini-batch 等有状态优化器。

## Key Contributions

- Gives a **negative answer** to MAIS-O60 ([Cla26d]): for every odd prime p ≥ 5 there is a nonempty open set U of initially active parameters from which a measurable selection of Clarke trajectories reaches a stationary dead-neuron state in finite time whose normalized limit is supported on no single nonzero real Fourier class, on an event of positive conditional probability.
- Exhibits two distinct failure mechanisms: (i) **dying ReLU**, where the sole active gate hits zero transversely at a finite time T ≤ p³ε/(2(p−1)√Γ) and the neuron freezes; (ii) **persistent sliding / one-example memorization**, where the central gate diverges (x(t), ‖w(t)‖, ‖θ(t)‖ → ∞) and loss converges to (p²−1)/p²·log p rather than 0 — the memorization side of the grokking dichotomy realized in the smallest possible model.
- Shows the frozen/limiting Fourier spectrum is **perfectly flat**: every nonzero real frequency class captures exactly 2/(p−1) of the centered Fourier energy, strictly < 1 for p ≥ 5, so the limit is not (δ, [ρ_ζ])-pure for any δ ∈ (0, 1 − 2/(p−1)).
- Provides four strengthened counterexamples (Appendix A, drafted with GPT-5.6 Sol): failure holds for **every** Clarke trajectory from U_slide (selection-independent, via local semiconvexity + Gronwall uniqueness), under the ReLU′(0)=0 convention (U_dead), for C^∞ smooth dead-zone smoothings σ_ε of ReLU (Theorem A.3), and for fixed-step full-batch gradient descent where the neuron dies in a single update (Theorem A.4).
- Clearly delimits scope: both mechanisms exploit ReLU's exact dead region, so the question remains **open for everywhere-positive activations** like softplus; the GD result covers only vanilla full-batch GD and explicitly does not cover momentum, Adam, or stochastic minibatches.

## Methodology

This is a pure theory paper using nonsmooth analysis and Fourier analysis on finite cyclic groups — no experiments are run.

**Setup.** Fix an odd prime p ≥ 5 and the cyclic group C_p = Z/pZ. A single-neuron network has parameters u, v, w ∈ R^{C_p}. For input (a,b), the preactivation (gate) is z_{a,b} = u(a) + v(b), the activation is h_{a,b} = ReLU(z_{a,b}), and the logit for class c is h_{a,b}·w(c). Training minimizes the exact full-table cross-entropy L (Eq. 1.1) with **no weight decay**. Because ReLU is nonsmooth at 0, training is formulated as the **Clarke differential inclusion** θ̇(t) ∈ −∂_C L(θ(t)). The nonzero real Fourier classes are [ρ_ζ] = {ρ_ζ, ρ_{−ζ}} for 1 ≤ ζ ≤ (p−1)/2, and the combined Fourier-energy fraction F_ζ(u,v,w) is defined in Eq. 1.5; "(δ, [ρ_ζ])-pure" means F_ζ ≥ 1−δ.

**Main construction (Section 2, Theorem 1.5).** The author plants a highly symmetric initial condition (Eq. 2.1): u(0)=v(0)=ε/2, u(a)=v(a)=−B for a≠0, w(0)=−A, w(c)=0 for c≠0, with B > ε/2 and A² > (p/(2(p−1)))ε². This makes exactly one table entry, (0,0), active (z=ε>0) and every other gate inactive. On this single-active-gate chamber the vector field is smooth and reduces to a 4D ODE in (s, a, b, g). A conserved quantity (Eq. 2.6), g(t)² − (p/(2(p−1)))s(t)² = Γ > 0, forces ė < 0, so the central gate s reaches 0 in finite time T with a transverse hit; choosing the Clarke slope ReLU′(0)=0 at the hit makes every parameter constant thereafter. At the frozen endpoint all three centered parameter blocks are scalar multiples of the centered point mass q = e_0 − (1/p)𝟙, whose unitary Fourier transform satisfies q̂(0)=0 and q̂(ζ)=1/√p for ζ≠0, so each real class carries exactly 2/(p−1) of the energy. A transversality + implicit-function-theorem argument upgrades the single symmetric seed to an open neighborhood U on which every frozen endpoint still has max_ζ F_ζ < 1, and since Gaussians charge every open set, P(θ(0) ∈ U | initially active) > 0.

**Strengthened counterexamples (Appendix A).** Four refinements remove the reliance on a favorable Clarke selection or on continuous time: (A.1) a persistent sliding trajectory on an open set U_slide where the unique Clarke trajectory (uniqueness proved via local semiconvexity of the cross-entropy loss at positive-slope kinks and a Gronwall comparison) diverges with x(t)→∞, ‖θ(t)‖→∞, and F_ζ → 2/(p−1) for every class; (A.2) an open dead set U_dead where **every** Clarke trajectory dies in finite time under ReLU′(0)=0 (proved via an invariant γ²_lower bound, Eq. A.57, and a no-continuation lemma, A.17); (A.3) the same flat-spectrum limit for C^∞ dead-zone smoothings σ_ε(z)=zψ(z/ε); (A.4) one-step death for fixed-step full-batch GD when 0 < s_0 < 2ηg_0/p², giving θ_n = θ_1 for all n ≥ 1. A Du–Hu–Lee balance invariant ‖u‖²+‖v‖²−‖w‖² = const (Lemma A.8) is verified to hold along every Clarke trajectory and is used throughout.

## Results & Highlights

This is a theoretical counterexample paper, so "results" are theorem statements rather than benchmark numbers. The concrete quantitative claims are:

- **Flat Fourier spectrum at the limit:** For every nonzero real frequency class, the limiting Fourier-energy fraction is F_ζ = 2/(p−1) (Section 2.1, Eq. A.46, A.60), which is strictly less than 1 for every p ≥ 5; this refutes the conjectured single-frequency purity F_ζ = 1 of MAIS-O60 by a gap of 1 − 2/(p−1) (e.g., gap = 1/2 at p = 5, 4/5 at p = 9... and the smallest genuinely multifrequency case is p = 5, since for p = 2,3 purity is automatic by dimension).
- **Persistent (memorization) trajectory loss:** Along the sliding trajectory the loss converges to (p²−1)/p² · log p (Proposition A.12 / Eq. A.33), with the single central example's loss → 0 and all other p²−1 examples stuck at loss log p — i.e., training loss does **not** tend to zero, unlike the separable-data implicit-bias setting.
- **Finite-time death bound:** The single active gate reaches zero in time T ≤ p³ε / (2(p−1)√Γ), where Γ = A² − (p/(2(p−1)))ε² > 0, and the hit is transverse with ė(T⁻) ≤ −2√Γ/p² < 0 (Eq. 2.7 and preceding bound).
- **One-step death under full-batch GD:** With step size η and convention ReLU′(0)=0, any symmetric seed satisfying 0 < s_0 < 2ηg_0/p² yields s_1 < 0 after the first update (Eq. A.70–A.71), so every gate is strictly inactive from step 1 and θ_n = θ_1 for all n ≥ 1, with F_ζ(θ_1) = 2/(p−1) at the symmetric base point.
- **Positive-probability failure events:** The counterexample sets U, U_slide, U_dead, U_ε,sm, U_{η,GD} are all nonempty open subsets of R^{3p} consisting of initially active parameters; because standard Gaussian initialization charges every open set, P(θ(0) ∈ U | initially active) > 0 in every case (Theorems 1.5, A.1–A.4).
- **Scope boundary versus prior margin results:** Morwani et al.'s [MEO+24] Fourier-feature emergence theorem requires width m ≥ 4(p−1), so the single-neuron case m = 1 studied here lies provably outside that margin-maximization mechanism.

No empirical benchmarks/trained baselines are reported; the evidence is the constructed family of exact trajectories and the invariant/semiconvexity analysis supporting them.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** High and narrow. The paper does not introduce a new method; its value is a clean, explicit counterexample that closes (negatively) a precisely stated open problem (MAIS-O60) in a hot area (Fourier-feature emergence in modular addition). The flat-spectrum value 2/(p−1) is a sharp, memorable consequence of the centered-point-mass limit.
- **Rigor.** The main theorem's proof is short, elementary, and self-contained (a 4D ODE plus a conservation law and a transversality argument); the appendix's strengthened statements rely on more machinery (Clarke regularity at positive-slope kinks, local semiconvexity, Gronwall uniqueness for hypomonotone subgradient flows). A potential soft spot: Theorem A.1's "unique Clarke trajectory" rests on verifying local semiconvexity of the **summed** cross-entropy along the constructed path, which is checked carefully but only along that path; a reader may want the semiconvexity constant spelled out globally. The balance-invariant extension to arbitrary Clarke selections (Lemma A.8) is convincingly argued via approximation off the kink hyperplanes.
- **Reproducibility.** As a mathematical paper, reproducibility means checkability, and the proofs are checkable. No code or numerical verification of the ODE trajectories is provided; given the paper proves exact finite-time death, simulation is not strictly needed, but a confirming numerical plot would have been a cheap robustness check and its absence is a minor weakness.
- **Significance.** Real but bounded. It punctures a specific conjecture and clarifies that single-frequency Fourier alignment in single-neuron modular addition is **not** forced by the dynamics — useful for the mechanistic-interpretability and grokking theory communities. However, the failure modes (dying ReLU; one-example memorization) are familiar and arguably "degenerate," and the authors concede the open question for everywhere-positive activations (softplus) and for practical optimizers (Adam, minibatch SGD), which is where real grokking experiments live.
- **Red flags.** (a) The **appendix is AI-authored** (GPT-5.6 Sol); while disclosed and author-reviewed, the most technically demanding strengthened results (Theorems A.1–A.4) depend on it, so independent verification of the semiconvexity/uniqueness arguments is advisable before citing them as settled. (b) The problem statement itself originates from an AI-generated agenda ("Claude Fable 5, audited by GPT 5.6 Sol" — [Cla26a,c,d]), i.e., the conjecture being refuted was itself posed by an AI system, which is unusual and worth flagging. (c) The negative result leans heavily on ReLU's exact dead region and on full-table/full-batch, no-weight-decay, continuous-time idealizations, limiting direct implications for practical grokking.

## Strengths

- Crisp, elementary main proof that makes the core mechanism (single active gate → finite-time death → centered-point-mass limit → flat Fourier spectrum) transparent and memorable.
- Honest, detailed scoping: explicitly states that the question remains open for softplus and for non-vanilla optimizers, rather than overclaiming.
- Goes substantially beyond the minimal counterexample in the appendix (every Clarke trajectory; ReLU′(0)=0; smooth dead-zone; discrete GD), removing the most obvious "but you cherry-picked the Clarke selection" objection.
- Transparent AI-tool disclosure in line with the Leiden Declaration, including a separately-cited underlying note [GPT26].
- Cleanly situates the result against the strongest relevant prior theorems (Morwani et al.'s m ≥ 4(p−1) margin result; directional-convergence/implicit-bias literature where loss → 0, which provably does not apply here).

## Limitations

- Both failure mechanisms exploit ReLU's exact dead half-line; the construction does **not** extend to everywhere-positive activations such as softplus, so the central question for smooth activations is unresolved (explicitly acknowledged).
- The GD result (Theorem A.4) covers only vanilla full-batch GD; momentum, Adam, weight decay, and stochastic minibatches are out of scope — and weight decay in particular is standard in the grokking experiments this agenda aims to model.
- The construction is highly symmetric (a single active gate, two-level parameter blocks); whether single-frequency alignment holds for "typical" initially active initializations under the same dynamics is not addressed — the paper only proves a positive-probability failure event, not a measure-1 failure.
- No numerical experiments are provided to illustrate the trajectories or to check robustness to discretization noise.
- The appendix's strengthened results are AI-drafted; independent peer verification of the nonsmooth-analysis details (especially local semiconvexity of the summed loss and Gronwall uniqueness on the sliding face) is warranted.

## Relevance

对研究"模加法/grokking 的机制可解释性"与"神经网络中表征/频率选择"的工作高度相关——它直接反驳了"单神经元模加法训练必然收敛到单一 Fourier 频率"这一猜想，提示在做 mechanistic interpretability 与 Fourier-feature emergence 的理论建模时，必须排除 dying-ReLU 与单点记忆这类退化轨道，并对 softplus、weight decay、Adam 等实际设置下结论的迁移保持谨慎。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does single-frequency alignment hold for **softplus** or other everywhere-positive smooth activations, where the dying-ReLU mechanism is unavailable? The paper explicitly leaves this open.
- What happens with **weight decay** (ubiquitous in real grokking experiments) or with **Adam / stochastic minibatch SGD**? The GD result is restricted to vanilla full-batch.
- Is the positive-probability failure event actually **measure-typical** for initially active initializations, or is it a thin symmetric island? A numerical study over random Gaussian inits for small p (e.g., p = 5, 7) would clarify the practical relevance.
- Can the persistent sliding trajectory (loss → (p²−1)/p²·log p) be connected quantitatively to the **memorization phase** of grokking in wider networks, and does its flat Fourier spectrum predict a specific non-emergent feature pattern during the pre-grokking plateau?
- Does the same counterexample strategy extend to the **symmetric group S_3** purity problem raised in [Cla26c], as the author speculates in Remark 1.7?
- Given the appendix is GPT-5.6-Sol-authored, can the local semiconvexity + Gronwall-uniqueness argument for the sliding face (Proposition A.16) be independently verified or replaced by a more elementary uniqueness proof?
