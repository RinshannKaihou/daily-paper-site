> **一句话总结：** 作者提出 MirageBench（150 个用户画像 × 6 个个性化任务 × 12 个模型，共 143,616 条被评判的声明），首次系统量化了个性化 LLM 的"过度推断"现象——所有 12 个模型均有 35%–49% 的声明缺乏证据支撑（均值 41.6%），并发现一个反直觉的"自我监控倒置"：模型自评的过度推断率与外部评判的过度推断率呈负相关（Spearman ρ = −0.60），意味着自报信心会误导跨模型安全比较。

> **TL;DR:** MirageBench is the first benchmark for **over-inference (OI)** in personalized LLMs — fabricating user attributes beyond the evidence. Across 12 models from 7 families and 143,616 judge-evaluated claims (judge validated against a blind human annotator, Cohen's κ = 0.900 binary), every model over-infers **35%–49%** of claims (cross-model mean 41.6% vs only 24%–31% grounded). The headline finding is a **Self-Monitoring Inversion**: self-reported OI is *negatively* correlated with judge-measured OI across models (ρ = −0.60, p = 0.044) — Qwen3-8B self-reports the lowest OI (13.0%) yet has the highest Judge OI (48.7%) — so self-confidence is a misleading cross-model safety signal, even though within-model self-audit ranks a model's own claims moderately well (AUROC 0.58–0.83).

## 核心贡献

- **提出 MirageBench，首个面向个性化 LLM 过度推断的基准**：包含 150 个用户画像（按刻板印象、反刻板印象、中性三类平衡）、6 个跨"可证伪度梯度"的个性化任务（生日礼物 → 公寓描述）、四类忠实度分类法（Grounded / Reasonable / Stereotype / Fabricated），以及四阶段评测流水线（Probe 显式推断、Task 隐式生成、Judge 外部裁判、Accum 多轮累积）。
- **首次跨模型大规模量化过度推断**：12 个模型、7 个家族、143,616 条被裁判的声明，证明 OI 普遍存在（35%–49%，跨模型均值 41.6%；仅 24%–31% 的声明有证据支撑），没有模型能幸免。
- **发现"自我监控倒置"（Self-Monitoring Inversion）**：在模型选择层面，模型自评的 OI 与外部裁判测得的 OI 呈负相关（ρ = −0.60，p = 0.044），自报最"安全"的模型恰恰被外部裁判标记为过度推断最严重；但单模型内部的自审仍能较好地给自己的声明排序（AUROC 0.58–0.83，9/12 模型 > 0.75）。
- **揭示 OI 随任务"想象力梯度"放大**：从可证伪的生日礼物（27% OI）到几乎无证据的公寓描述（57.8% OI），越不可观察的属性模型越凭空捏造；OI 主要来自 fabrication（均值 31.1%）而非 stereotype（10.5%）。
- **多轮累积试验暴露"记忆污染"**：8 轮交互中 9/12 模型的推断属性近似线性增长（R² > 0.90，每轮新增 5–15 条），最强累积器（GPT-5.5、GLM-5.1）几乎从不修订先前的推断（移除率 0.4%–5%）。
- **裁判可靠性经人工校验**：在 400 条分层抽样声明上，盲标人员与 Judge（Claude-Opus-4-7）的吻合度达 89.8%（四类）/ 95.0%（二分类），Cohen's κ 分别为 0.863 / 0.900，处于"几乎完美"区间。

## Key Contributions

- **MirageBench — the first benchmark for over-inference in personalized LLMs**: 150 personas balanced across stereotypical / counter-stereotypical / neutral, six personalization tasks along a groundability gradient, a four-way faithfulness taxonomy (Grounded / Reasonable / Stereotype / Fabricated), and a four-instrument pipeline (Probe, Task, Judge, Accum).
- **First cross-model quantification of over-inference at scale**: 12 models, 7 families, 143,616 judged claims; OI is pervasive (35%–49%, cross-model mean 41.6%, claim-weighted micro-average 41.8%), with only 24%–31% of personalized claims grounded in evidence.
- **Discovery of the Self-Monitoring Inversion**: across models, self-reported OI is *negatively* rank-correlated with judge-measured OI (Spearman ρ = −0.60, p = 0.044; exploratory, wide bootstrap CI [−0.90, +0.06], n = 12), so self-confidence misleads cross-model safety comparison — even though within-model self-audit still ranks a model's own claims moderately well (AUROC 0.58–0.83).
- **OI intensifies along an imagination gradient**: from gift recommendation (27% OI) to apartment description (57.8% OI); fabrication (mean 31.1%) dominates stereotyping (10.5%), so models mainly invent rather than stereotype.
- **Accumulation pilot surfaces silent memory pollution**: over 8 turns, 9/12 models grow inferred attributes roughly linearly (R² > 0.90, ~5–15 new attributes/round), and the fastest accumulators (GPT-5.5, GLM-5.1) almost never revise prior inferences (0.4%–5% removal), so unsupported claims persist.
- **Judge reliability validated by a blind human annotator**: on 400 stratified claims, agreement is 89.8% (four-class) / 95.0% (binary), Cohen's κ = 0.863 / 0.900 (both "almost perfect"), with 71% of residual disagreements on adjacent taxonomy classes.

## Methodology

**Problem formulation.** A personalized system is given a small set of revealed facts *E* (fixed at |E| = 3, the "early-interaction" regime where over-inference is most tempting). For a task *T* the model produces a response decomposed into claims *C*. Each claim is labeled into one of four mutually exclusive categories — Grounded (restates evidence), Reasonable (one common-sense step), Stereotype (substitutes group priors), Fabricated (no evidential basis). The first two are acceptable; the latter two jointly constitute **over-inference**, summarized as OI Rate = (#Stereotype + #Fabricated) / #Total.

**Personas.** 150 personas, each a pair (P, E) where *P* is a ground-truth 15-attribute profile and *E* is exactly three first-person revealed facts. Sources: 8 hand-crafted seeds, 70 from PersonaMem-v2, 72 LLM-generated under diversity constraints. Balanced 50/50/50 across stereotypical (e.g., female nurse who does yoga), counter-stereotypical (e.g., male kindergarten teacher who powerlifts), and neutral — designed so counter-stereotypical personas act as a genuine stress test.

**Six tasks along a groundability gradient.** Apartment description (least groundable) → recommendation letter → stress source → weekend itinerary → dating profile → birthday gift (most groundable).

**Four-instrument evaluation pipeline.**
1. **Probe** — explicitly ask "list everything you can infer" to elicit self-aware inference (baseline).
2. **Task** — elicit implicit inference by having the model do the six tasks using only *E*, then in a separate turn self-audit its own response under the four-way taxonomy.
3. **Judge** — an independent judge (Claude-Opus-4-7, not in the leaderboard) re-classifies every claim at temperature 0.0, conditioning only on *E*, the task context, and the claim (ground-truth *P* used solely to flag contradictions). Validated against a blind human annotator on 400 stratified claims.
4. **Accum** — 8-round longitudinal pilot on 2 personas, tracking whether inferred attributes grow monotonically and whether prior inferences are ever revised.

**Models.** 12 models across 7 families: GPT-5.5, GPT-5.4-nano, GPT-4o-mini, Claude-Opus-4-6, Gemini-3.1-pro, Gemini-3-flash, DeepSeek-v4-pro/flash, Qwen3.6-plus, Qwen3-8B, GLM-5.1, Kimi-K2.5. Generation temperature 0.7; judging at 0.0.

## Results & Highlights

- **Over-inference is universal and severe (MirageBench leaderboard, Table 1).** Every one of the 12 models over-infers **35%–49%** of its personalized claims; the cross-model mean OI is **41.6%** and the claim-weighted micro-average over all **143,616** judged claims is **41.8%**. The best model is Gemini-3.1-pro at **35.1%** OI; the worst is Qwen3-8B at **48.7%**. Only **24%–31%** of personalized content is grounded in evidence, so roughly three-quarters of what a model "knows" about a user was never communicated. Fabrication (mean **31.1%**) dominates stereotyping (mean **10.5%**) — models primarily invent rather than stereotype.
- **Self-Monitoring Inversion (Table 2).** Across the 12 models, the Spearman rank correlation between self-audit OI and Judge OI is **ρ = −0.60** (p = 0.044 by permutation; 95% bootstrap CI [−0.90, +0.06]). The starkest case: Qwen3-8B self-reports the lowest OI at **13.0%** but receives the highest Judge OI at **48.7%** (a +35.7 pp under-detection gap); Kimi-K2.5 self-reports the highest OI at **58.2%** but sits mid-pack at **43.1%**. By contrast, within-model self-audit still ranks a model's own claims moderately well — per-model AUROC ranges from **0.58** (Qwen3-8B) to **0.83** (Qwen3.6-plus), with **9/12 models above 0.75**.
- **OI scales with the imagination gradient (Table 3).** Task-level OI ranges from **27.0%** for birthday-gift recommendation (which can ground in stated hobbies) to **57.8%** for apartment description (where models must invent physical details); the ordering is consistent across all 12 models. The recommendation-letter task shows a distinct pathology — **40.4%** fabrication driven by genre conventions ("demonstrated exceptional leadership") rather than stereotypes (only 7.8%).
- **Stereotype-group stratification is substantive, not cosmetic (Table 7).** Pooled across all 12 models, stereotypical personas incur **44.8%** OI vs **37.0%** for counter-stereotypical personas — a **7.8 pp gap present in all 12 models individually** (per-model gaps +4.0 to +10.5 pp), confirming that counter-stereotypical personas act as a real stress test.
- **Inference accumulates as silent memory pollution (Accum pilot, Table 4 / Table 8).** From just 3 initial facts, frontier models build profiles of 100+ inferred attributes by Round 8 — GPT-5.5 reaches **125.0** attributes, GLM-5.1 **121.5**, Claude-Opus-4-6 **104.5**. For **9/12 models** the round-by-round count is approximately linear (R² > 0.90) with slopes of **5–15 new attributes/round**. The fastest accumulators almost never revise prior inferences (removal rates **0.4%–5%** for the top five); the authors observed no case where an inference was retracted because a later turn contradicted it (e.g., GPT-5.5 retains "urban professional lifestyle" from Round 2 even after a Round 7 message describing visiting a big city "for the first time").
- **Judge reliability (Table 11).** On 400 stratified claims, Judge–human agreement is **89.8%** (four-class) / **95.0%** (binary), with Cohen's κ = **0.863** / **0.900** (both in the "almost perfect" range); every class exceeds F1 = 0.86, and 71% of the 41 residual disagreements are on adjacent taxonomy classes.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty (high).** Over-inference as a named failure mode distinct from factual hallucination and group-level social bias is a useful conceptual reframing, and the **Self-Monitoring Inversion** is genuinely surprising — it directly undermines the common engineering assumption that a model's self-reported grounding confidence can be trusted to pick the "safer" model for a memory system.
- **Rigor (mixed, honestly self-disclosed).** Strengths: independent judge validated against a blind human annotator (κ = 0.900 binary), exact model snapshots listed, stratified 400-claim validation with per-class F1 reported, and a confusion-matrix analysis of disagreements (71% adjacent-class, 0 spanning three steps). Weaknesses the authors acknowledge: a **single judge** (Claude-Opus-4-7) and a **single human annotator** (so the κ estimates human–judge agreement, not inter-annotator agreement, with no CI on κ); the self-audit and Judge use **different claim extractors**, so per-record correlations are ordinal, not one-to-one claim matches, and the self-to-judge claim-count ratio ranges 0.73–2.82 across models.
- **Statistical fragility of the headline finding.** The Self-Monitoring Inversion rests on only **n = 12 correlated model families** and a bootstrap CI that **includes zero** ([−0.90, +0.06]); the family-clustered CI is even wider ([−0.87, +0.14]). The authors are admirably upfront that this is "exploratory" and "not a precisely estimated coefficient," but the abstract and conclusion nonetheless foreground the point estimate ρ = −0.60 as a headline result.
- **Accum is explicitly a pilot.** Only **2 personas**, and the memory-update prompt instructs the model to *"not remove earlier entries unless the new message directly contradicts them,"* which **biases the setup toward accumulation by construction**. The authors flag this clearly and say the load-bearing signal is the cross-model contrast, but absolute counts (e.g., "125 attributes by Round 8") should be read as indicative, not definitive. A neutral prune-permitting prompt is left to future work.
- **Evidence-only scoring inflates OI on presupposition-heavy tasks.** Task-presupposed claims (e.g., "planning a weekend trip" for an itinerary task) are scored as over-inference because the deployed system has no privileged access to user intent. The authors argue this is correct, but it makes the 27%–59% task-gradient numbers an upper bound relative to a task-aware notion of grounding. Notably absent: **no mitigation is evaluated**, and there is **no measurement of downstream impact on recommendation quality or user satisfaction** — the paper characterizes the phenomenon but does not test fixes.
- **Missing ablations.** No sensitivity analysis to the Judge backbone (a second independent judge is "left to future work"), no ablation of |E| (always 3 facts) to see whether more evidence shrinks OI, and no comparison against the related OP-Bench over-personalization benchmark (Hu et al. 2026) on overlapping models.

## Strengths

- The four-way taxonomy (Grounded / Reasonable / Stereotype / Fabricated) is more informative than a binary hallucinated/not split, and the human-validated Judge (κ = 0.900 binary) gives the leaderboard real credibility.
- The **within-model vs. between-model scope split** is a genuinely actionable insight for practitioners: self-audit is fine for internal claim ranking, dangerous for cross-model safety selection.
- The counter-stereotypical persona construction is shown to be substantive (7.8 pp OI gap present in all 12 models), not just a checkbox — strengthening the causal claim that stereotype-driven gap-filling is a real channel.
- Honest, detailed limitations section (Appendix A) and full prompt/model-snapshot disclosure (Appendix G) make the work reproducible.

## Limitations

- Single judge and single human annotator; no CI on κ; no inter-annotator agreement.
- Self-Monitoring Inversion is exploratory (n = 12, CI includes zero); the abstract headline overstates precision relative to the wide CI.
- Accum pilot uses only 2 personas and a retention-biased memory prompt; absolute accumulation counts are not generalizable.
- No mitigations evaluated; no downstream-quality or user-satisfaction measurement.
- Evidence-only scoring makes task-presupposed claims count as OI, so reported task-level rates are upper bounds.
- No |E| ablation and no second-judge robustness check.

## Relevance

与个性化/记忆增强 LLM 的可信度评测高度相关：MirageBench 提供了首个量化"过度推断"的基准，其"自我监控倒置"结论直接挑战了以模型自报信心作为记忆系统安全选择信号的主流做法，对构建 ChatGPT-style 持久记忆、Mem0/MemGPT 类智能体的研究者具有警示意义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the Self-Monitoring Inversion survive with a second independent judge and additional human annotators, or with a larger model panel (the current n = 12 bootstrap CI includes zero)?
- How does OI rate scale with |E| — does giving the model 6 or 10 revealed facts collapse the 41.6% mean, or does fabrication just shift to the still-unrevealed attributes?
- Can provenance-tagging every stored attribute (stated / inferred-with-link / generated-without-evidence) and treating unlinked inferences as hypotheses measurably reduce silent accumulation in the Accum setting?
- Does RLHF-style training that rewards genre compliance (the recommendation-letter pathology) causally amplify fabrication, and would a faithfulness-aware reward term reduce the 40.4% fabrication on that task?
- What is the downstream impact — does higher OI actually degrade recommendation quality or user satisfaction, or do users prefer the "personalized" fabrications?
