> **一句话总结：** 本文通过对 Microsoft Azure 超大规模集群和四个开源 agentic 框架（SWE-Agent、Trae、CORAL、Owl）的首次生产级架构剖析，揭示 agentic 工作负载呈现碎片化、突发性、异构化的资源特征，并据此提出 Agora 原型，通过 CPU/GPU 空闲资源收割、角色感知核心池化与任务绑核，在保持尾部延迟的前提下将单机吞吐提升 82%–106%。

> **TL;DR:** Through a production fleet study on Microsoft Azure plus controlled experiments on four open-source frameworks (SWE-Agent, Trae, CORAL, Owl), this paper shows agentic AI workflows are fragmented, bursty, and heterogeneous—stranding CPU/GPU capacity on uniform servers; the Agora prototype harvests idle CPU cores (95% of standalone harvester throughput at <3% agent slowdown), consolidates agents to free 33% of GPUs while raising generation throughput 82% and cutting tail latency 2.5× on Owl, and applies role-aware core pooling that cuts tool CPU demand up to 46% while retaining 99% of serving throughput.

## 核心贡献

- **首个生产级架构剖析**：在 Microsoft Azure 超大规模集群上采集 24 小时 agentic 请求 trace，将时间、工具活动与 CPU 指标归因到三类软件角色（调度器 scheduler、编排器 orchestrator、运行器 runner），首次系统刻画 agentic AI 在真实硬件上的资源行为。
- **三维分类法（taxonomy）**：沿编排方式（host-orchestrated vs LLM-orchestrated）、执行结构（sequential vs parallel）、模型组合（homogeneous vs heterogeneous）三个与平台相关的正交维度组织异构工作流空间，并将 MetaGPT、OWL、CORAL、SWE-Agent 等真实用例映射其中。
- **三大架构错配的发现**：指出传统统一服务器在 agentic 场景下的三重失配——碎片化搁置 CPU/GPU 容量、三种 host 角色无法用同质核心池高效服务、agent 多路复用损害微架构局部性（IPC 仅 1.2–1.6，backend stall 占 43–47%）。
- **Agora 原型与三项设计原则**：CPU 空闲核心收割（含角色隔离、编排器提示、自适应退避）、GPU 容量收割（agent 合并 + 显存超订 + 预取 + MILP 布局）、按角色与任务局部性分池绑核，并对未来硬件（调度加速引擎、可分区微架构结构、异构/可变形核）给出指引。

## Key Contributions

- **First production-scale architectural characterization of agentic AI** measured across a hyperscaler (Microsoft Azure) fleet, augmented by a controlled study of four diverse open-source frameworks (SWE-Agent, Trae, CORAL, Owl) on a 96-core AMD EPYC 7V12 + 8× NVIDIA A100 server.
- **A platform-relevant taxonomy** along three orthogonal axes—orchestration (host- vs LLM-orchestrated), execution structure (sequential vs parallel), and model composition (homogeneous vs heterogeneous)—that maps heterogeneous workflows to distinct server demands.
- **Identification of three architectural mismatches** of conventional uniform servers: fragmentation strands CPU/GPU capacity (host CPU 6%–31%, GPU SM <55% on average yet with near-100% bursts), the three host roles defeat homogeneous provisioning, and agent multiplexing degrades microarchitectural locality (backend-bound 43–47% of pipeline slots, L1-d MPKI 14–20).
- **The Agora prototype** demonstrating three design principles—idle CPU harvesting, idle GPU capacity harvesting via agent consolidation/oversubscription/prefetch, and role-aware core pooling with per-task pinning—all auto-tuned to the running workload, plus concrete directions for future silicon (scheduling offload engines, way-partitioned/tagged caches and branch predictors, heterogeneous/morphable cores).

## Methodology

The paper combines two complementary lenses. (1) A **fleet study** on Microsoft Azure collecting a 24-hour production trace of agentic requests, attributing time, tool activity, and CPU/PMU metrics to the three host software roles (scheduler, orchestrator, runner). (2) A **controlled study** of four frameworks chosen to span the taxonomy: SWE-Agent (single-agent, sequential, tool-intensive coding), Trae (sequential coding with long tool chains, three model endpoints), CORAL (four-agent, parallel, homogeneous evolutionary), and Owl (heterogeneous workforce with coordinator, six text roles, vision and speech models). The controlled testbed is a 96-core AMD EPYC 7V12 CPU with eight NVIDIA A100 GPUs, deploying one vLLM instance per agent role while sweeping concurrency from 1 to 32 tasks. From these data the authors derive three server-design principles and validate each through a case study in **Agora**: CPU harvesting co-locates DCPerf "harvester" workloads on runner cores with role-based isolation, orchestrator hints (compute-bound vs I/O-bound tools), and a grace-guarded gradual retreat policy; GPU harvesting casts placement as a mixed-integer linear program (MILP) over profiling data, oversubscribing GPU memory and prefetching the next agent's state when the workflow is host-orchestrated; role-aware pooling separates the scheduler/orchestrator onto small dedicated pools, compacts the runner pool, and pins each task (not each tool class) to a narrow contention domain. Each mechanism is evaluated against static, workload-agnostic baselines (Shared / Split / Isolate CPU layouts; role-dedicated GPU deployment) and tuned to where the workload sits in the taxonomy and to its current load.

## Results & Highlights

- **CPU harvesting (Table II):** At low load, co-located DCPerf harvesters deliver **95% of their standalone throughput** at **under 3% agent slowdown** (averaged across SWE-Agent, Trae, CORAL, Owl); at high load they deliver **53.7%** at **under 4% slowdown**. Averaged across both operating points, harvesters recover **0.74 server-equivalents** of additional throughput and raise host CPU utilization by **31%** at only **3.3% higher agent latency**.
- **GPU harvesting (Table III) on Owl vs role-dedicated baseline:** Agora frees **33% of GPUs** while raising generation throughput **+82%**, completing **+22% more tasks/hour**, cutting tail latency **2.5×**, and improving energy efficiency **+51%**; at a fixed GPU budget (consolidation only) throughput rises **+106%** and tail latency drops **3.8×**. CORAL also benefits from consolidation (+15% throughput, 5.3× tail latency cut) but, being parallel and tool-saturated, *loses* throughput if GPUs are actually removed (−71%), confirming harvesting must be taxonomy-aware.
- **Role-aware pooling:** Scheduler-pool right-sizing finds a footprint of just **10.3 cores**, where **11 cores retain 98.5%** of standalone serving throughput and 16+ add nothing; runner compaction (Table IV) cuts tool CPU-seconds by **25–46%** and lowers runner usage from **15.6 to 11.5 cores** at high concurrency with **<1%** batch-completion change; per-task pinning (Table V) at "2 tasks/pool" shortens tail tool time **12.8%**, cuts tool CPU-seconds **34%**, and speeds batch completion **4.7%**—retaining **99% of serving throughput** while reducing tools' CPU demand up to **46%** and worst-case tool latency **13%**.
- **Fleet fragmentation evidence:** Tool execution accounts for a large, heavy-tailed share of request time—**comparable to or exceeding inference time for >27% of requests**—and no single CPU-to-GPU ratio characterizes the workload. A single CORAL run expands into **580 LLM calls interleaved with 552 tool invocations**, ping-ponging between CPU and GPU hundreds of times.
- **Bursty, imbalanced load:** Trae host CPU sits at an **11% median** then spikes to **nearly 100%** at stage boundaries; Owl's 8 GPUs show 4 near peak power while the other 4 sit near **~20%** idle; CORAL on research tasks saturates host CPU at **99%** (GPU 44%) whereas on algorithmic tasks it uses only **31% CPU** but **55% GPU**.
- **Microarchitectural & coordination pressure:** IPC is only **1.2–1.6** with **43–47% backend-bound** pipeline slots and **L1-data MPKI of 14–20**; SWE-Agent involuntary context switches grow from **71 to 660 per second** as concurrency rises 1→32 despite low aggregate CPU utilization.

## Authors & Credibility

Authors are from **The University of Texas at Austin** (Jirong Yang, Peizhe Liu, Jovan Stojkovic) and **Microsoft Azure** (Chaojie Zhang), combining academic systems/architecture expertise with direct access to a hyperscale production fleet. The study's credibility rests on real Azure fleet telemetry (rare for this nascent workload class) cross-validated by a reproducible controlled study on four well-known open-source frameworks with named hardware (96-core AMD EPYC 7V12, 8× A100). The reference list (84 entries, spanning ISCA/HPCA/MICRO/ASPLOS/OSDI/SOSP/NSDI venues and recent 2025–2026 agentic-systems preprints) situates the work in the established datacenter-harvesting and LLM-serving literature (SmartHarvest, Party/Orca/vLLM/PagedAttention, Splitwise, Mosaic, µManycore).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine first-mover on the *architectural* (vs purely software-scheduling) implications of agentic AI, distinguished from concurrent CPU-centric studies (Raj et al. [57], Yuan et al. [76]) by per-role PMU decomposition and a server-design prototype rather than aggregate utilization reporting. The taxonomy is a useful organizing lens, though its three axes are individually well known from prior multi-agent systems work.
- **Rigor:** Strong on the controlled study—clear baselines (Shared/Split/Isolate; role-dedicated GPU), sweep across concurrency 1–32, per-lane median/max reporting, and taxonomy-conditioned adaptation. The fleet study is necessarily less controlled: only a 24-hour window is described, and the production framework mix, model sizes, and absolute request volumes are not disclosed (anonymized for confidentiality), limiting independent interpretation of fleet numbers.
- **Reproducibility:** Mixed. Controlled-study frameworks (SWE-Agent, Trae, CORAL, Owl) and hardware (EPYC 7V12 + 8× A100) are named and the vLLM-per-role setup is described, but Agora itself is a prototype with no stated release, MILP solver details and profiling windows are omitted, and the "low/high load" definitions for harvesters are only loosely specified—so the headline percentages would be hard to reproduce exactly.
- **Significance:** High potential impact if agentic AI grows as projected; the three mismatches and the harvest/consolidate/pin recipe are actionable for hyperscaler operators. However, several headline gains are framework-specific: the **82% throughput / 2.5× tail-latency** GPU-harvesting win is essentially the Owl-only result, and the paper honestly shows harvesting *backfires* on CORAL (−71% throughput, 16× worse tail latency), so the generalizability across the taxonomy is uneven.
- **Red flags / weaknesses:** (a) The GPU-harvesting evaluation covers only **two** frameworks (Owl, CORAL), so the "free a third of GPUs" claim is really an Owl result; SWE-Agent and Trae GPU behavior is under-reported. (b) No end-to-end comparison against a strong *agentic-aware* baseline (e.g., Pythia [75], ThunderAgent [31], Murakkab [11], software-defined agentic serving [1])—Agora is only compared to static, workload-agnostic configurations, leaving open how much of its gain is available from existing agent-aware schedulers without new harvesting machinery. (c) The DCPerf "harvester" co-location results depend on a harvester chosen by the authors; sensitivity to harvester type is not explored. (d) "Agent slowdown" is defined relative to standalone makespan but tail-latency distributional detail (p99) for the agent side under harvesting is thinner than the harvester-side numbers.

## Strengths

- First study to pair **production hyperscaler telemetry** with a **reproducible controlled framework study**, giving both ecological validity and causal isolation.
- The **taxonomy → characterization → mismatch → design principle → prototype** pipeline is unusually tight and self-consistent.
- Honest negative results (CORAL harvesting backfires; private-per-task cores worse than shared; partitioning by tool class gives no benefit) build trust.
- Concrete, silicon-relevant future-hardware agenda (scheduling offload, way-partitioned/tagged caches & branch predictors, morphable cores) grounded in the PMU evidence.
- Quantitative results are dense and consistently tied to specific metrics, frameworks, and operating points.

## Limitations

- Fleet study details (framework mix, model sizes, absolute volumes, geographic distribution, time period beyond 24 h) are withheld for confidentiality, capping external scrutiny of the production claims.
- GPU harvesting is evaluated on only two of the four frameworks; SWE-Agent and Trae GPU results are sparse.
- No head-to-head comparison with contemporary agent-aware serving systems (Pythia, ThunderAgent, Murakkab, Agentix, software-defined agentic serving), so marginal value over the state-of-the-art in agentic scheduling is unquantified.
- Agora is not released; MILP formulation, profiling horizon, and auto-tuning controller details are abbreviated, impeding reproduction.
- Harvester sensitivity (only DCPerf) and energy numbers rely on GPU power proxies rather than measured wall-power.
- The 24-hour fleet window may not capture diurnal/weekly seasonality or rare bursty events that dominate tail behavior in production.

## Relevance

对关注 LLM 推理系统、agent 框架与数据中心架构协同设计的读者高度相关：它把"agentic AI 为何让 CPU 重新回到关键路径"这一现象量化到角色与微架构层面，并提供可落地的服务器侧优化（核心收割、GPU 合并、角色分池），是 agentic serving 与 GPU/CPU 资源调度方向的研究者与工程师都值得精读的工作。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of Agora's CPU-harvesting and GPU-consolidation gain survives when compared against a strong **agent-aware scheduler** (e.g., Pythia, ThunderAgent, Murakkab) rather than only static, workload-agnostic baselines?
- Does the **GPU-harvesting** result (freeing 33% of GPUs on Owl) extend to SWE-Agent and Trae, which were not evaluated on the GPU side, and how does the win scale beyond 8 A100s to larger DGX-class pods?
- How sensitive are the harvesting numbers to the **choice of co-located harvester** (DCPerf was the only one tested) and to harvester CPU-intensity vs memory-bandwidth profile?
- Can the **orchestrator hints** mechanism be extended to LLM-orchestrated workflows (OWL, ToolOrchestra, AOrchestra) where no proactive graph is available—e.g., via learned speculative burst prediction—to avoid falling back to conservative reactive retreat?
- The fleet study covers a single 24-hour window; how do the role-level CPU signatures and tool-call heavy tails behave over **multi-week** traces with diurnal seasonality, and do the tail-driven provisioning rules change?
- For the proposed future silicon (way-partitioned/tagged caches, scheduling offload engines, morphable cores), what is the **quantified upper bound** on IPC and context-switch reduction Agora's software mechanisms leave on the table?
