> **一句话总结：** 本文指出 Sinkhorn 类无状态矩阵优化器在 MoE 路由专家上失效（根因是专家梯度是路由条件性、时序噪声大的样本，先归一化再平均与先平均再归一化不等价），提出 MESH——通过梯度缓冲生命周期隐式维护一阶动量再做 Sinkhorn 归一化，在 110M DeepSeek 风格 MoE 上将优化器状态内存降低 62.5%、峰值 CUDA 分配降低约 12.6%，但评估损失仍比 AdamW 高约 0.049。

> **TL;DR:** Direct SAGE/Sinkhorn optimization fails on routed MoE experts because their gradients are router-conditional and temporally noisy; the authors diagnose this as a nonlinear-ordering problem (Sinkhorn(E[G]) ≠ E[Sinkhorn(G)]) and propose MESH, a hidden-momentum Sinkhorn that carries the expert first moment through the gradient-buffer lifecycle instead of optimizer state. On a 110M DeepSeek-style nanowhale MoE trained on FineWeb-Edu, MESH-B cuts optimizer-state memory by 62.5% (0.883 GB → 0.331 GB) and peak CUDA allocation by ~12.6% (4.375 → 3.822 GB) versus AdamW, at a cost of +0.049 eval loss (3.6365 vs 3.5877 avg over seeds 100/512).

## 核心贡献

- **诊断路由 MoE 专家为 Sinkhorn 优化的失效点**：在 110M DeepSeek 风格 nanowhale MoE 上，直接用 SAGE/Sinkhorn 混合优化器虽把优化器状态从 0.883GB 降到 0.331GB，但评估损失恶化到 3.8265，远差于 AdamW（3.58–3.64）；通过消融证明 routed 专家是主要失效点。
- **提出 MESH（hidden-momentum Sinkhorn）**：核心思想是把"先归一化再平均"改成"先平均（一阶动量）再 Sinkhorn 归一化"，且一阶动量通过梯度缓冲（gradient-buffer）生命周期隐式维护，不计入优化器状态。
- **提出 MESH-B 可选块预条件变体**：在 Sinkhorn 方向上叠加一个粗粒度 neuron/block inverse-RMS 乘子；多 seed 结果显示它与无块版本的内存-质量权衡接近，块预条件不是普遍必需。
- **提供大量阴性消融**：证明仅二阶矩（V-only）、仅符号动量（Lion）、仅行列分解尺度、融合 Sinkhorn、置信度阻尼等都不能解释 SAGE/Sinkhorn 的差距，从而定位"归一化前的时序平滑"为主要因果成分。
- **完整状态诊断变体**：MESH-FULL / MESH-B-FULL 把一阶动量显式存入优化器状态，在 seed-42 上恢复接近 AdamW 的性能（3.6102–3.6619），说明专家需要时序平滑但不一定需要完整坐标级 AdamW 状态。

## Key Contributions

- **Identifies routed MoE experts as the failure point** of direct SAGE/Sinkhorn optimization: a SAGE/Sinkhorn hybrid cuts optimizer state from 0.883 GB to 0.331 GB but degrades eval loss to 3.8265, far above AdamW baselines (3.58–3.64) on the 110M DeepSeek-style nanowhale MoE; ablations isolate routed (not shared) experts as the dominant gap.
- **Proposes MESH (Memory-Efficient Sinkhorn with Hidden momentum)**: converts the expert update from Sinkhorn(G_t) to Sinkhorn(H_t), where H_t is an unnormalized first-moment accumulator carried through the gradient-buffer lifecycle rather than optimizer state, addressing the nonlinear-ordering failure Sinkhorn(E[G]) ≠ E[Sinkhorn(G)].
- **Proposes MESH-B**, an optional block/neuron inverse-RMS preconditioned variant; multi-seed results show it is close to unblocked MESH, so block preconditioning is treated as incremental, not universally necessary.
- **Provides extensive negative ablations** ruling out second-moment-only, sign-only (Lion), factored-v, fused-Sinkhorn, and route-confidence interventions as explanations, leaving temporal smoothing as the primary causal ingredient.
- **Uses full-state diagnostic variants (MESH-FULL, MESH-B-FULL)** to separate the systems effect of hidden momentum from the optimizer effect of temporal smoothing; these recover AdamW-like quality on seed-42 diagnostics (down to 3.6102), supporting the claim that experts need temporal smoothing but not full coordinate-wise AdamW state.

## Methodology

**Problem framing.** A routed MoE expert matrix W_e receives gradients G_{e,t} = Σ_{i∈R_{e,t}} a_{i,e,t} g_i(W_e), where R_{e,t} is the router-selected token subset and a_{i,e,t} are gate weights. Unlike a dense MLP gradient, this is a *conditional* estimate under p(x | e ∈ topk(x,t)), and both the subset and weights drift during training. Stateless Sinkhorn applies U_{e,t} = Sinkhorn(G_{e,t}; K, τ), which normalizes each noisy instantaneous sample. Because Sinkhorn is nonlinear, normalizing-then-averaging (stateless) differs from averaging-then-normalizing (momentum); this ordering mismatch is the central diagnosis.

**MESH expert update (Algorithm 2).** Maintain an unnormalized momentum H_{e,t} = β1 H_{e,t-1} + G_{e,t}. The expert direction is U_{e,t} = Sinkhorn(H_{e,t}; K, τ), with K=5 row/column normalization rounds and τ=10. The implementation exploits the gradient-buffer lifecycle: after an optimizer step the expert grad buffer is *stashed* (detached) outside the optimizer-state dict; immediately before the next backward pass it is reattached as p.grad ← β1 H so backward accumulation yields H_{e,t+1}. This prepare-before-backward hook is required because Trainer/Accelerate/DDP wrappers can bypass a naive zero_grad override. Diagnostics report ~1 prepare call per step, buffer-shadow cosine ≈ 1, relative error ≈ 1e-8.

**MESH-B (Algorithm 3).** Adds a coarse block/neuron inverse-RMS multiplier: V_{e,t} = β2 V_{e,t-1} + (1−β2) R(H_{e,t})² (row RMS for W1/W3, column RMS for W2), then a clipped, mean-normalized factor Q_{e,t} broadcast over the block dimension. By default V is computed from the smoothed H (the V(H) variant) rather than a separate raw-gradient stream.

**Role assignment.** Embedding/LM-head + norms/biases → SAGE light state; non-expert dense matrices → stateless Sinkhorn; routed/shared experts → MESH hidden buffer; optional expert block scale → MESH-B block RMS.

**Experimental setup.** HuggingFace nanowhale 110M DeepSeek-V4-style MoE (8 layers, 4 routed + 1 shared expert/layer, hidden 320, untied 129,280-token vocab; vocab matrices hold 82.75M of 110.37M params). Trained from scratch on FineWeb-Edu for 5,000 steps, seq len 2048, per-device batch 8, grad accum 4, 8 data-parallel workers (~524,288 tokens/step, 2.62B tokens/run). Evaluation on 512 held-out examples. SAGE/MESH config: lr 2e-3, β1=0.9, β2=0.99, wd 0.01, cosine, 10% warmup, τ=10, K=5. AdamW baseline: lr 6e-4, β1=0.9, β2=0.95, wd 0.1, cosine, 3% warmup. Reports: eval loss, mean token accuracy, persistent optimizer-state memory, peak PyTorch CUDA allocation.

## Results & Highlights

- **Multi-seed memory–quality trade-off (Table 1, seeds 100/512 avg):** MESH-B reaches eval loss 3.6365 (acc 0.3436) and MESH 3.6430 (acc 0.3430), versus AdamW 3.5877 (acc 0.3515). Both MESH variants use 0.331 GB optimizer state vs AdamW's 0.883 GB — a 62.5% reduction — and peak CUDA 3.822–3.824 GB vs 4.375–4.376 GB — about a 12.6% reduction — at a cost of ~0.049 eval loss gap to AdamW.
- **SAGE/Sinkhorn failure quantified (Table 2, seed 42):** SAGE/Sinkhorn exact scores 3.8265 eval loss (0.331 GB state) vs AdamW 3.6427 (0.883 GB) — a 0.184 loss gap; converting only experts to AdamW (Experts AdamW) recovers most of it to 3.6158 at 0.528 GB.
- **Full-state diagnostics recover AdamW-like quality (Table 2, seed 42):** MESH-B-FULL with V(H) reaches 3.6102 and MESH-B-FULL 3.6187 at 0.430 GB state — essentially matching AdamW's 3.6427 while using roughly half the optimizer state.
- **Routed experts are the dominant failure point (Table 3, seed 42):** All-experts Sinkhorn = 3.8265; routing only the routed experts to AdamW recovers to 3.6297, while routing only the shared expert to AdamW helps much less (3.6895); applying embedding-oriented SAGE to all experts is worst (3.8912).
- **Temporal smoothing is the primary causal ingredient (Table 4, seed 42):** Instant Sinkhorn = 3.8265; V-only AdamW = 3.8457 (worse); Lion/sign-momentum = 4.0046; factored-v only = 4.2308; MESH-FULL = 3.6619; MESH-B-FULL = 3.6187; best V(H) = 3.6102 — showing first-moment smoothing before normalization drives the recovery, not second moment or sign alone.
- **Memory accounting:** MESH optimizer state = 0.331 GB (dominated by vocabulary/SAGE states) plus a separately accounted 0.098 GB hidden buffer (excluded from optimizer-state accounting because it lives in the gradient-buffer lifecycle, but still part of CUDA peak); MESH-B-FULL rises to ~0.430 GB by storing the expert first moment explicitly.
- **Routing controls (Table 7) and negative interventions (Table 8) do not close the gap:** confidence damping reaches only 3.7605, scale-preserved Sinkhorn 3.7632, fused routed Sinkhorn 3.7662, and confidence amplification diverged — confirming the gap is not about update scale, route confidence, or tensor partitioning.

## Authors & Credibility

Single author: **Masato Fujitake**, FA Research, Fast Accounting Co., Ltd. (a Japanese accounting firm's research arm, not a university or frontier-AI lab). The work builds directly on the publicly available HuggingFace nanowhale codebase and on prior peer-reviewed/open optimizers (SAGE, Sinkhorn/multi-normalization, HMAdam, Adam-mini), which it cites honestly — including negative results for SAGE on experts. The experimental protocol (multi-seed main table, single-seed mechanistic ablations clearly separated) and the conservative framing (explicitly does *not* claim MESH beats AdamW) are methodologically sound and unusually transparent for an industry-affiliated single-author paper. Credibility is moderate-to-good: the diagnosis is well-motivated and the ablations are thorough at this scale, though the scale itself (110M, 5K steps) is small.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Moderate. The hidden-momentum-in-gradient-buffer trick is borrowed from HMAdam [6], and Sinkhorn/matrix-normalization from Scetbon et al. / SAGE; the genuine contribution is *applying* this combination to routed MoE experts and reframing the failure as a nonlinear ordering problem (Sinkhorn(E[G]) ≠ E[Sinkhorn(G)]). This is a useful, well-argued diagnostic rather than a fundamentally new optimizer.
- **Rigor:** Good within its scope. Multi-seed main table (seeds 100/512) is separated from single-seed mechanistic ablations (seed 42), negative ablations are plentiful, and the paper honestly reports that MESH-B does *not* outperform AdamW. However, several claimed gaps (e.g., 0.049 loss difference in Table 1) sit in the 0.01–0.02 noise band the authors themselves flag, and the main comparison uses only two post-fix seeds against AdamW runs at three seeds — an asymmetry that weakens "modest gap" claims.
- **Reproducibility:** Strong on paper. The codebase (HuggingFace nanowhale) is public, all hyperparameters and role counts (85 SAGE tensors/82.747M params, 65 Sinkhorn/3.046M, 120 expert/24.576M) are listed, and Algorithms 1–3 plus the trainer-lifecycle notes (prepare-before-backward hook to survive Accelerate/DDP) are concrete. No official code release URL is given in the text, however.
- **Significance:** Narrow but real. The memory-saving (62.5% optimizer state, ~12.6% peak CUDA) is meaningful for MoE training, and the "experts need temporal smoothing before normalization" principle is plausibly transferable. But a 110M model with 5,000 steps and 512-example evaluation is far below the scale where such claims usually harden; the vocab-heavy nanowhale config (75% of params in embeddings left on SAGE) is also atypical and may inflate or distort the residual AdamW gap.
- **Red flags:** (1) Single-author, non-academic affiliation with no stated code release. (2) The residual gap to AdamW is explicitly acknowledged but not resolved; the authors speculate it may stem from non-expert role assignment (vocabulary matrices on SAGE) yet do not run the obvious follow-up ablation of moving *only* vocab matrices to AdamW. (3) Block preconditioning (MESH-B) is presented as a contribution but the multi-seed results show it is statistically indistinguishable from unblocked MESH, so its practical value beyond seed 42 is unproven. (4) No comparison against other memory-efficient Adam alternatives (e.g., Adam-mini, 8-bit Adam, Adafactor) at this scale.

## Strengths

- Clean, mechanistic diagnosis: identifies the nonlinear-ordering root cause (Sinkhorn(E[G]) ≠ E[Sinkhorn(G)]) for conditional routed-expert gradients and supports it with a full ladder of negative ablations.
- Engineering honesty: the gradient-buffer lifecycle (stash/reattach, prepare-before-backward hook) is described in enough detail to implement, including the DDP/Accelerate pitfalls and numerical diagnostics (cosine ≈ 1, error ≈ 1e-8).
- Conservative, non-hyped framing: explicitly states MESH does not outperform AdamW and offers a memory-frontier trade-off instead.
- Real memory wins that matter for MoE at the optimizer-state level (62.5%) even if peak CUDA wins are more modest (~12.6%).

## Limitations

- Single small architecture (110M nanowhale), single corpus (FineWeb-Edu), short runs (5,000 steps), small evaluation set (512 examples) — acknowledged by the authors.
- Asymmetric seed counts: main MESH/MESH-B results use two seeds (100, 512) while AdamW uses three (42, 100, 512); several decisive gaps fall within the paper's own noise band.
- Vocab-heavy, atypical model config (75% of trainable params in untied embeddings/LM head) may distort the optimizer-state accounting and the residual AdamW gap; the obvious vocab-only-to-AdamW ablation is missing.
- No comparison to other memory-efficient optimizers (Adam-mini, Adafactor, 8-bit/4-bit Adam) — only AdamW and SAGE/Sinkhorn baselines.
- Block preconditioning (MESH-B) is not shown to be necessary beyond seed 42; its contribution is essentially indistinguishable from MESH in the multi-seed table.
- No code release URL is provided in the paper text.

## Relevance

与关注 LLM 预训练与高效优化器的工作高度相关：MESH 直接针对 MoE 训练的显存瓶颈，提供了"路由专家需要先时序平滑再矩阵归一化"这一可迁移的工程洞见，对在大规模 MoE 上做内存优化器选型有直接参考价值；但其结论仅在小模型(110M)上验证，需谨慎外推到生产规模。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does MESH/MESH-B scale to 1B+ parameter MoE models and longer (50K+ step) pretraining, where the 0.049 AdamW gap might widen, shrink, or invert?
- How much of the residual AdamW gap disappears if *only* the vocabulary/LM-head matrices are moved to AdamW while experts stay on MESH (the ablation the authors flag but do not run)?
- How does MESH compare against other memory-efficient Adam alternatives (Adam-mini, Adafactor, 8-bit/4-bit Adam, Lion) on the same nanowhale MoE at matched state budgets?
- Is block preconditioning (MESH-B) ever statistically separable from plain MESH with more seeds, or is it purely a seed-42 artifact?
- Does the hidden-momentum buffer interact correctly with expert balancing losses, z-loss, and auxiliary-free load balancing common in modern MoE training?
- Can the "average-then-normalize" principle be transferred to other stateless matrix optimizers beyond Sinkhorn (e.g., Shampoo-style, gradient multi-normalization variants)?
