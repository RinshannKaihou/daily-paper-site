> **一句话总结：** 作者提出基于脉动阵列 + Chebyshev 多项式 + Horner 方法的统一激活函数单元，用同一套硬件同时计算 tanh/sigmoid/ReLU 与多变量 softmax，在 FreePDK45 45nm 下相比等并行度的 CORDIC 基线在 tanh/sigmoid 上把平均绝对误差降低 71%/41%，同时面积省 4.6%、功耗省 5.1%，并在 N=256 的大向量 softmax 上把最大 KL 散度比 CORDIC 和 ONE-SA 分别降低 44.6% 和 79.0%。

> **TL;DR:** A systolic-array activation-function unit computes Chebyshev-polynomial approximations (via Horner's method) of tanh, sigmoid, ReLU, and multivariate softmax on shared hardware; against a feature-matched 8-parallel CORDIC baseline on FreePDK45 it cuts tanh/sigmoid mean absolute error by 71%/41% while using 4.6% less area and 5.1% less power, and on N=256 softmax it cuts max KL divergence by 44.6% vs CORDIC and 79.0% vs ONE-SA (piecewise-linear).

## 核心贡献

- 提出一种基于脉动阵列 (systolic array) 的激活函数单元架构，用 Horner 方法在同一套乘加硬件上计算 Chebyshev 多项式近似，统一支持单变量激活函数 (tanh、sigmoid、ReLU) 与多变量 softmax，实现硬件资源共享。
- 给出可配置的设计空间：并行度 (8 或 16 列)、softmax 中间寄存器数 (8 或 256)、多项式阶数与定点位数，可在精度 / 并行度 / 硬件开销间按应用目标折中。
- 提出两步量化感知的多项式选择流程：先以每个候选各自最少整数位求最优，再在统一 Q3.12 定点格式下重新评估，兼顾 softmax 与 tanh/sigmoid 的精度，并明确指出"最高阶不等于最低误差"这一定点反直觉结论。
- ASIC 实现 (FreePDK45, Synopsys DC V-2023)：8 并行 / 8 寄存器版本相对等并行度 CORDIC 基线，tanh/sigmoid 平均绝对误差分别降低 71% 与 41%，面积减少 4.6%、功耗减少 5.1%，且 tanh/sigmoid/softmax 单输出延迟分别下降 16.2% / 16.2% / 14.3%。
- 在 N=256 大向量 softmax 上，最大 KL 散度相比 CORDIC 降低 44.6%、相比 ONE-SA 分段线性降低 79.0%，最大行和误差 (RSE) 也显著优于两者，验证了"多项式近似 + 硬件除法器"在长序列上比 PLA 除法更鲁棒。

## Key Contributions

- A systolic-array activation-function unit that evaluates Chebyshev-polynomial approximations via Horner's method, sharing the same multiply-add hardware across univariate activations (tanh, sigmoid, ReLU) and the multivariate softmax — something LUT/PLA designs cannot do because each function needs its own mapping.
- A configurable design space (parallel columns 8/16, softmax register counts 8/256, polynomial degree, fixed-point format) that lets designers trade off accuracy, parallelism, area, and power per target application.
- A two-step quantization-aware polynomial-selection procedure: first optimize each candidate with its minimum integer-bit budget, then re-rank under a unified Q3.12 format. This exposes the counter-intuitive fixed-point finding that beyond a certain degree, quantization error amplified by high-order terms makes accuracy worse.
- An ASIC implementation (FreePDK45, Synopsys Design Compiler V-2023) whose 8-parallel / 8-register variant beats a feature-matched 8-parallel CORDIC unit on every axis: 71% lower mean AE for tanh, 41% lower for sigmoid, 4.6% less area, 5.1% less power, and 14.3–16.2% lower per-output latency.
- A softmax approximation (shifted-softmax + polynomial exp + hardware divider) that scales better with vector length: at N=256 it reduces max KL divergence by 44.6% vs CORDIC and 79.0% vs ONE-SA PLA, while also achieving the lowest Row-Sum Error — directly relevant to transformer workloads where softmax is >1/3 of runtime at long sequences.

## Methodology

The architecture is a dedicated activation systolic array (separate from the GEMM/convolution array). Each Processing Element (PE) receives the intermediate result IR_{i+1} and input x from above, computes IR_i = IR_{i+1}·x + a_i, and forwards IR_i, x downward and a_i rightward. A column of n+1 PEs evaluates a degree-n polynomial; lower-degree polynomials are computed by zeroing high-order coefficients. Because all neurons in a layer share one activation function, every PE in a row uses the same coefficient, so coefficients are hard-coded constants selected by a multiplexer — no coefficient RAM is needed.

Mathematical core. Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind T_n (recurrence T_{n+1}=2x·T_n − T_{n-1}) are interpolated at Chebyshev nodes and converted to standard power-series form; Horner's method (a0 + x(a1 + x(a2 + x·a3))) maps naturally onto the multiply-add systolic column.

Function-specific handling:
- tanh / sigmoid: a single Chebyshev polynomial valid on [x_min, x_max], clipped to (−1,1) / (0,1) outside. The valid range trades interior approximation error against clipping error.
- ReLU: trivially a comparator (one per PE column), bypassing the array for higher throughput.
- Softmax: not directly polynomial-approximable, so only e^x is approximated. Inputs are shifted by max(x) to bound exponents to ≤0 (numerically stable shifted-softmax, softmax(x)_i = e^{x_i−max(x)} / Σ e^{x_j−max(x)}). Computed in 4 steps: shift, per-element polynomial exp with running sum into intermediate registers, hardware divider for the reciprocal of the sum, and final multiply. Vector length K is bounded by the number of intermediate registers (hence the 8 vs 256 variants).

Polynomial selection. A two-step quantization-aware search over degree ∈ [1,12], total bitwidth ∈ {4,8,12,16}, and clipping ranges ([−2,2] or [−3,3] for tanh/sigmoid; [−2,0]…[−5,0] for exp). Candidates were ranked by RMSE, mean AE, and max AE on [−4,4]; the top 100 exp candidates were then ranked by softmax KL divergence on 1000 sample vectors each for N=8 and N=32. Step 1 used per-candidate minimum integer bits; step 2 re-ranked under a unified Q3.12 format because the exp candidates needed ≥3 integer bits whereas tanh/sigmoid were optimal at Q1.14. Final picks: tanh degree-9 clipped at [−2,2], sigmoid degree-5 clipped at [−3,3], exp degree-3 clipped at −5.

Hardware. Three designs (8-parallel/8-reg, 8-parallel/256-reg, 16-parallel/256-reg), 10 PEs per column, signed 16-bit fixed-point, synthesized FreePDK45. Compared against (a) an 8-parallel fixed-point CORDIC unit derived from Raghuram et al. [9], and (b) ONE-SA [10] PLA variants with segment widths 0.25 and 1.0.

## Results & Highlights

- **tanh mean absolute error (Table I, Q3.12, on [−4,4]):** Chebyshev 5.68×10⁻³ vs CORDIC 19.64×10⁻³ vs ONE-SA(0.25) 5.62×10⁻³ vs ONE-SA(1.0) 24.79×10⁻³ — a 71% reduction over CORDIC; max AE 35.97 vs CORDIC 107.92 (≈3× higher for CORDIC) because CORDIC stops converging beyond |x|≈1.1182.
- **sigmoid mean absolute error (Table I):** Chebyshev 8.95×10⁻³ vs CORDIC 15.23×10⁻³ — a 41% reduction; max AE 47.43 vs CORDIC 53.96, with CORDIC divergence beyond |x|≈2.236.
- **softmax at N=256 (Table II), max KL divergence:** Chebyshev 3.252 vs CORDIC 5.879 vs ONE-SA(0.25) 15.512 — 44.6% better than CORDIC and 79.0% better than ONE-SA. Chebyshev also has the lowest max RSE at N=256 (0.021 vs CORDIC 0.061 vs ONE-SA 1.021), confirming that the PLA division approximation in ONE-SA blows up at long sequence lengths.
- **softmax at N=8 (Table II):** Chebyshev max KL 0.393 vs ONE-SA 0.035 — Chebyshev loses to ONE-SA's finer PLA of e^x at small N, but its RSE (0.007) is much lower than ONE-SA (0.015). CORDIC is worst (KL 3.006).
- **Area and power (Table III, FreePDK45), 8-parallel/8-register design:** 85,634 µm² and 0.8203 mW vs CORDIC 89,719 µm² / 0.8646 mW — 4.6% less area, 5.1% less power. The 16-parallel/256-register variant reaches 190,242 µm² / 1.7423 mW (less than 2× the 8-parallel/256 area because register count, not columns, dominates).
- **Per-output latency (Table III), filled pipeline:** 8-parallel Chebyshev — tanh 27.50 ns, sigmoid 27.50 ns, ReLU 2.50 ns, softmax 56.25 ns vs CORDIC tanh/sigmoid 32.81 ns, softmax 65.63 ns — 16.2% latency reduction for tanh/sigmoid and 14.3% for softmax. The 16-parallel/256-register design halves latency again (tanh 13.63 ns, softmax 26.31 ns).
- **Polynomial selection insight (Sec. IV-A):** the lowest mean AE for tanh under Q3.12 came from a degree-9 polynomial, but higher degree did not always help — degree-10/11/12 candidates were worse than degree-9 because quantization error of x is amplified by high-order terms, empirically validating the paper's claim that "highest degree supported ≠ best accuracy" in fixed point.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Moderate-to-good. The combination of Chebyshev + Horner on a systolic array is classical numerics applied to a real hardware-design problem; the genuinely novel part is reusing the same polynomial-evaluation hardware for both univariate activations and multivariate softmax (via shifted-softmax + polynomial exp + hardware divider), and the explicit two-step unified-quantization polynomial-selection procedure. The "highest degree is not best in fixed point" observation is well known in the approximation-theory community but is usefully quantified here with the degree/bitwidth heatmaps.
- **Rigor:** Reasonable on the function-level metrics but weak on system-level validation. The CORDIC baseline is feature-matched (8 parallel units), which is good; however it is also a somewhat weak baseline because CORDIC's convergence range is narrow (|x|≈1.1182 for tanh), so the 71% / 3×-max-AE wins over CORDIC at large |x| are partly an artifact of CORDIC's known divergence rather than a fair same-range comparison. The stronger head-to-head is vs ONE-SA(0.25), where Chebyshev is essentially tied on tanh mean AE (5.68 vs 5.62) and worse on sigmoid (8.95 vs 7.88) — a more honest framing would foreground this.
- **Missing ablations / end-to-end evidence:** The paper motivates everything with transformers and BERT (citing that softmax is >1/3 of runtime at long sequences and that low-accuracy softmax causes compounding errors), yet reports **zero end-to-end model accuracy** — no task accuracy, no perplexity, no BERT fine-tuning, no actual NN deployment. All evidence is function-level (AE, KL, RSE) on synthetic normal/uniform inputs. A reviewer cannot judge whether 3.252 max KL divergence at N=256 is acceptable for a real transformer. The ONE-SA softmax comparison is explicitly based on an *assumed* method ("we assume a calculation method similar to our 4-step approach") because ONE-SA's paper omits softmax details — so the 79.0% KL win is partly against a strawman.
- **Reproducibility:** Medium. Polynomial degree/bitwidth/clipping ranges, quantization format (Q3.12), process (FreePDK45), and tool versions are given. But exact Chebyshev coefficients other than the two illustrative ones (tanh degree-3, exp degree-3) are not listed, sample seeds are not given, and no code/artifact release is mentioned.
- **Significance:** Useful for hardware-accelerator designers who want a unified activation + softmax unit, especially for transformer-class long-vector softmax where ONE-SA's PLA division degrades. The significance is bounded by the lack of end-to-end accuracy validation — adopters must verify themselves that the reported KL/RSE numbers do not hurt model quality.
- **Red flags:** (1) The paper repeatedly emphasizes transformers/BERT but provides no transformer results. (2) The Q3.12 unified quantization is explicitly suboptimal for tanh/sigmoid (Q1.14 would be optimal) — i.e., the headline 71%/41% AE numbers are achieved at a known precision penalty willingly paid for softmax compatibility, and this tradeoff is acknowledged but not ablated against a per-function quantization variant. (3) No clock frequency or throughput in ops/s is reported — only per-output latency assuming a filled pipeline, which hides the cost of the one-cycle-per-column fill latency.

## Strengths

- Unified hardware for univariate activations and multivariate softmax via a single systolic polynomial evaluator — a genuine resource-sharing advantage over LUT/PLA per-function designs.
- Strong, quantified win on long-vector softmax (N=256), which is the regime that actually matters for transformers; the KL-divergence-driven polynomial selection for exp is a thoughtful touch.
- Feature-matched 8-parallel CORDIC baseline and synthesized (not estimated) area/power/latency numbers under a standard PDK and a standard commercial tool flow.
- Honest, useful design-space discussion: the two-step quantization selection, the explicit tradeoff between clipping and interior error, and the demonstration that higher polynomial degree can hurt accuracy under fixed point.
- Architecture is extensible to other functions by adding coefficient constants and a multiplexer setting.

## Limitations

- **No end-to-end model accuracy.** Despite the transformer/BERT motivation, there are no task-level results (accuracy, perplexity, BLEU, etc.), so the practical acceptability of the reported AE/KL/RSE is unverified.
- **Synthetic-input evaluation only.** Softmax quality is measured on normal N(0,σ²) and uniform U(−a,a) samples, not on real attention logits from a trained model, where the distribution can be far more peaked or heavy-tailed.
- **ONE-SA softmax comparison is based on an assumed** (not documented) softmax implementation, weakening the 79.0% KL claim.
- **CORDIC is a weak baseline at large |x|** due to its narrow convergence range; the most favorable comparisons partly reflect that known limitation.
- **Unified Q3.12 quantization sacrifices tanh/sigmoid accuracy** (optimal would be Q1.14) to accommodate softmax — a per-function quantization variant is described as possible but never implemented or benchmarked.
- **No clock frequency, throughput, or energy-per-operation** reported; only per-output latency under a filled-pipeline assumption.
- **Single technology node (FreePDK45 45nm)** — no results on a modern node, no placement-and-routing, no post-synthesis timing closure data shown.
- **Softmax vector length is hard-capped by the intermediate-register count** (8 or 256 in the implemented designs), requiring a redesign for larger N.

## Relevance

对硬件加速器 / 神经网络推理芯片方向高度相关：作者用脉动阵列 + Chebyshev 多项式统一实现激活函数与 softmax，并量化证明了在长向量 softmax (N=256，对应 transformer 长序列) 上对 CORDIC 与分段线性方法显著更优，对关注高效推理硬件、定点数值计算与 transformer 加速器设计的研究兴趣直接契合。若兴趣偏向算法/模型层面（如新模型结构、训练方法），则相关性较低，因为本文不含任何端到端模型精度结果。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- What is the end-to-end accuracy impact on a real transformer (e.g., BERT fine-tuning on GLUE, or a decoder-only LM perplexity) when softmax is replaced by this approximation at N=128/256/512/1024? Does the 3.252 max KL at N=256 actually preserve task quality?
- How does Chebyshev-vs-ONE-SA compare when ONE-SA's softmax is implemented faithfully rather than the assumed 4-step variant? Is the 79.0% KL win robust to that assumption?
- What happens on real attention-logit distributions (which can be extremely peaked) rather than synthetic normal/uniform inputs — does the clipped e^x approximation under-count long-tail probability mass?
- A per-function quantization variant (Q1.14 for tanh/sigmoid, Q3.12 only for softmax) is described as possible but not built — how much extra tanh/sigmoid accuracy does it buy, and at what control-logic / binary-point-alignment cost?
- What are the clock frequency, throughput (ops/s), and energy-per-softmax numbers, and how do they scale to a 7nm or 5nm node with real place-and-route?
- The polynomial is hard-coded per activation function; can the architecture support on-the-fly re-fitting (e.g., for a calibration set) without losing the hard-coded-coefficient area advantage?
