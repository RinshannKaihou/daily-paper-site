§ 01

Methodology

pure theory · no experiments
01
Scalar reduction
Robust 0–1 risk depends on P only through ordinary error p = err_P(h). Two-valued likelihood ratios collapse the infinite-dimensional DRO to 1-D. Prop 4
02
Inflation map
Direct analysis of Q_{k,ρ}(p) — no Shapiro duality. Lemma 5 bounds the derivative: 0 ≤ dQ/dp ≤ C_k + c_k(ρ/p)1/k.
03
Two-branch bound
Discrepancy |Q(p)−Q(p')| splits into a deviation branch ρ1/kΔ1−1/k and an error branch (ρ/p)1/kΔ. Thm 7
04
Scale-sensitive VC
Clipped uniform VC bound Δ̄h ≲ √(p̄hΓn/n) + Γn/n over h∈H. Lemma 10
05
Realizable solve
DRERM with p̂=0 lands at branch crossover. Solve for n → proxy Ψreal = 1/ε + ρ1/(k−1)/εk∗.
06
Agnostic solve
Maximize two-branch over [0, pc]. No interior max → endpoint comparison lemma yields Ψagn = max(1/ε², ρ1/(k−1)/ε2∨k∗).
07
Matching lower bounds
Minimax constructions on VC classes match both proxies up to constants, holding even for improper learners. Thm 12, 15

§ 03

Strengths vs. Limitations

Strengths

5 items
  • First complete picture. Matched-up-to-logs sample complexity across the entire Cressie–Read family, not just χ².
  • Clean methodological idea. Direct event-inflation-map analysis explains why prior bounds were loose: they collapsed the scale-dependent two-branch discrepancy into one global estimate, forfeiting error-scale information.
  • Mechanistic transition. The k=2 shift is explained (near-zero crossover vs. near-saturation pc(ρ;k) vs. error-dominated range), not merely observed.
  • Correct ρ→0 interpolation. Recovers classical Θ(d/ε) and Θ(d/ε²), fixing a genuine defect in Duchi–Namkoong (2021) and Zhou–Liu (2023, 2026) explicit bounds.
  • Honest about scope. Explicitly states non-uniformity in k, leaves KL open, flags that bounded-loss settings are not covered.

Limitations

5 items
  • Only 0–1 loss. The Bernoulli scalar reduction is load-bearing; bounded/convex losses are out of scope — method does not displace Zhou & Liu (2026) outside binary classification.
  • No uniformity as k↓1; KL (k=1) unsolved. Constants blow up and qualitative behavior changes; KL robust-PAC complexity conjectured possibly non-polynomial but unproven.
  • Log factors remain. Agnostic rate is Θ̃; the proper DRERM bound carries an extra log(eΨ) term. Only the improper realizable upper bound is log-free.
  • Purely theoretical. No finite-sample regime analysis or experiments showing where the improved bounds change practical sample-size recommendations.
  • Lower-bound caveats. |H|≥3 required (Hanneke 2016 caveat); realizable ε ≤ εk, δ ≤ δ0. Exceptional small VC classes uncovered.
§ 04

Open questions

Q1
What is the correct robust-PAC sample complexity under KL-divergence (k=1) uncertainty balls, and is it indeed non-polynomial in ε as the QKL,ρ(p) ~ ρ/log(1/p) asymptotic suggests?
Q2
Can the direct event-inflation-map analysis extend to multi-class (one-hot 0–1 over K classes) or bounded regression losses while retaining the scale-dependent two-branch structure?
Q3
Is there a uniform-in-k analysis (or different divergence parameterization) that bridges the k>1 Cressie–Read rates and the conjectured KL behavior continuously?
Q4
In which (ρ, ε, d) regime is the ε² improvement over Zhou & Liu (2023)'s ε⁻⁴ agnostic bound actually visible for practical DRO reweighting estimators?
Q5
Can the lower-bound constructions be strengthened to remove the |H|≥3 restriction and cover the exceptional small VC classes of Hanneke (2016)?
Q6
Is the log-free realizable rate achievable by a computationally efficient proper learner, or only by Hanneke's majority-vote construction?
