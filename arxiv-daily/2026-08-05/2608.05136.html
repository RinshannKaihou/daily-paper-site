M Methodology — six controlled pillars

  • THEORY Gauge fixed as the maximal isometric subgroup of GL(k); classify equivariant rules, derive Adam's exact first-step gauge defect (Prop 4.4), structure & transfer theorems (Thm 4.5 / 4.6).
  • §5 ZOO Nine optimizers on underdetermined rank-3 matrix sensing (n=40, 2·dof measurements, k=n), small init, no weight decay, all stopped at interpolation.
  • §7 DIAL Adam-p morphs the denominator from coordinate-wise (p=1) to a single shared gauge-invariant scalar (p=0), holding adaptivity/momentum/noise fixed.
  • §6 TWINS Two transformers compute the identical function; head 2 is rotated by a random orthogonal Ah at init. Triplet: gauge / A=I / noise.
  • §8 TAIL Sweep tail-energy fraction α² in X=√(1−α²)X3+αE⊥; ten seeds, float64, constant & cosine schedules.
  • §9 REAL Rank-48 completion on Indian Pines (200 bands) & Pavia University (103 bands), compared only at matched training-loss levels.

+ Strengths

  • Mechanistic, falsifiable theory tightly coupled to controlled experiments — the Adam-p dial is a textbook single-confound isolation.
  • Intellectual honesty in scope: an explicit "where the bias does not succeed" subsection lists grokking, stiff PINNs, and LoRA fine-tuning.
  • The twin-protocol diagnostic (gauge / A=I / noise) is a reusable audit tool for any equivariance claim.
  • Reconciles two opposing Muon results — exact-recovery vs. simplicity-bias loss — with one phase diagram and a solvable model.

− Limitations

  • Small scale, short budgets — mechanism demos, not benchmarks; GD is unlikely to beat well-tuned Adam on real LLM training.
  • Orthogonal gauge is a modeling choice; the full group is GL(k), and GD's trajectory depends on the balance term U0TU0−V0TV0.
  • Real-data validation is narrow: only GD>Adam survives; on Indian Pines Muon is worse than Adam — the zoo ordering collapses.
  • Transfer theorem covers only common-scalar rules; scalar-Adam's agreement with GD is "empirically observed, not formally implied."
  • Single author; §10 self-critique evaluates the author's own prior method on his own criterion.

Open questions for further exploration §11 · future

  1. Q1 Does the 4% tail-energy crossover hold on modern LLM pretraining, where token-covariance spectra are heavy — or shift because the "target" is itself being learned?
  2. Q2 Can the gauge / A=I / noise twin triplet become a cheap online regularizer that pulls Adam back onto the equivariant orbit, without scalar-Adam's ~8× interpolation cost?
  3. Q3 How does GL(k) balance conservation (UTU−VTV) interact with Adam's gauge breaking — and does enforcing balance recover the bias where equivariance alone fails?
  4. Q4 Is the 56% WQTWK split the actual cause of alignment-based merge failures between independently Adam-trained transformers, or do late-training Lyapunov instabilities dominate?
  5. Q5 Can a momentum-invariance lemma extend the transfer theorem to scalar-Adam, closing the gap between empirical agreement and Thm 4.6's common-scalar-only reach?
