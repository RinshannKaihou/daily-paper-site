Five-part eligibility: must reference an audit (not a pure evaluation), AI/ML must be the audited object, the audit must examine integration beyond the model, English, and a non-standard publication type. ISO/IEEE standards were excluded for inaccessibility.

Running anchor: the Boeing 737 MAX 8 crashes (346 fatalities) frame why integration faults — not component faults — are the lethal class of failure the paper imports from aerospace/medical-device audit regimes (SAE ARP4754B, ISO 13485, AS9100).

§ 02 Three Integration Sites

  • Inter-component — data → model → output. 87.9% of docs engage data/input.
  • System–environment — deployment + ecosystem. Found in 51.7% / 77.6%.
  • Multi-system — upstream-to-downstream supply chain; predominantly GPAI; hardest to trace.

§ 03 Legitimacy Gap vs. Traditional Audit

  • < 5% reference an established standard or certification.
  • 44.8% specify an explicit auditor role at all.
  • ~75% use an internal approach constrained by access.
  • 65.5% distribute joint responsibility — “the auditor effectively becomes the auditee.”

§ 05 Strengths

  • Bridges aerospace/medical-device integration-audit tradition with under-developed AI practice, anchored by the 737 MAX 8 example.
  • Transparent, PRISMA-ScR-compliant; published queries + included-doc list — a higher bar than typical AI surveys.
  • The three-site taxonomy + four-function / three-quality synthesis gives a usable map of where, who, what to audit.
  • Intellectually honest about immaturity: most “audits” lack independence, standards, and system-level access.

§ 06 Limitations

  • Single-coder dominance — 82% agreement reported only for first 50 docs; rest coded by the lead alone.
  • Standards excluded — ISO/IEEE dropped, under-representing the very regimes held up as gold standard.
  • English-only / Western bias — EU AI Act and OECD dominate; Global South under-represented.
  • Proposal-heavy — 65.5% of docs are conceptual, so stats describe aspirations, not field practice.
  • Empirical–framing mismatch — GPAI is the narrative but only 29.3% of the corpus.
Relevance · 相关性
Directly relevant to LLM/GPAI risk assessment, red-teaming, and compliance audit work: it names the blind spot of model-centric benchmark culture — the integration layer of API access, upstream/downstream supply chain, and deployment-context interaction — and offers a taxonomy practitioners can apply today.

§ 07 Open Questions for Further Exploration

Q1 · Modern LLM stack
How does the three-site taxonomy map onto a real stack (foundation-model API → RAG/orchestration → tool-using agent → UI)? Which of compatibility / completeness / oversight are measurable today, and with what artifacts?
Q2 · Supply-chain access
Can the multi-system site ever be audited without mandatory upstream-provider access? What minimum disclosures (model/system cards, change logs) make a Hopkins-style supply-chain-graph audit tractable across org boundaries?
Q3 · Standards & geography
How do findings shift if ISO/IEEE standards and non-English / Global-South literature are included — does the “<5% reference a standard” finding shrink, and does the three-site taxonomy still hold?
Q4 · Performed vs real accountability
Given 65.5% are proposals, what comparative empirical benchmark of applied integration audits (e.g., MDSAP-style medical-device audits translated to clinical AI) would reveal which qualities actually predict downstream failures?
