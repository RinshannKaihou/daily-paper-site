How it works

M1 Methodology

Predict the geometry, then solve for the surface. A continuous-time graph network outputs the per-vertex first fundamental form at any horizon; an unrolled ARAP solver turns that metric field into a valid mesh.

  • Intrinsic representation. Surfaces live in log-Euclidean SPD(2) coordinates. The network predicts metric tensors — rigid-frame-invariant — instead of extrinsic coordinates.
  • Three-term decomposition. Log-ĝ = log-Euclidean base + learnable base shift along a frozen population trend + per-vertex GNN residual. Final layer is zero-init; only individual deviation is learned.
  • Continuous-time conditioning. A Fourier encoding of the lead time ∆t is the sole temporal signal — one model, any prefix length, any horizon. No trajectory ODE.
  • Differentiable ARAP decoder. A 15-iteration unrolled local–global solver realizes the predicted metric; gradients flow back through it. The cotangent Cholesky factor is precomputed once.
  • Supervise through the decoder. Loss is mean vertex error of the reconstructed vertices — a soft realizability constraint that beats direct metric-space Ebin loss.

Two-sided read

SL Strengths & Limitations

+ Strengths

  • Geometrically principled: predicting the intrinsic metric decouples shape from pose and is rigid-frame-invariant.
  • Reconstruction-constrained training is a general recipe — "supervise through the decoder that enforces validity" — with a non-trivial rigidity-theory explanation for why the curvature head helps so little.
  • Conservative methodology: subject-disjoint splits, Bonferroni-corrected paired t-tests, shared reference for all methods.
  • Continuous-time by design: one model handles any prefix length and any horizon via a Fourier-encoded ∆t.
  • Honestly reported: limitations spelled out, components ablated, the simpler g-only model retained over the marginally-better curvature variant.

− Limitations

  • Sub-millimetre gains sit within the FreeSurfer 8 reproducibility band; clinical significance is asserted, not shown.
  • The "arbitrary causal prefix" claim is undercut by using only 3-visit prefixes throughout — no sensitivity analysis to prefix length m.
  • "Continuous time" is asserted at inference but supervised only on a 12-month grid; off-grid ∆t validation deferred.
  • Learned baselines (DCM, TransforMesh) are adapted to the new protocol rather than re-implemented — their numbers may understate best-case performance.
  • A separate model per structure (14 networks); no unified multi-structure or cortical model. No code release mentioned; several hyperparameters under-specified.
Open questions for further exploration

Q What a reviewer would push on next

1
Continuous-time claims

How does MT-GNN perform with variable prefix lengths (1, 2, 4, 5 visits) and off-grid lead times (∆t = 18 or 30 months) that the design claims to support but never validates?

2
Discarded slope signal

Would a latent-ODE or transformer prefix encoder recover per-subject slope signal that the log-Euclidean mean deliberately throws away — especially for fast-progressing subjects?

3
Clinical utility

Does the predicted surface carry signal beyond shape error — AD-vs-CN classification, conversion prediction, correlation with ADAS-Cog / MMSE decline versus observed visits?

4
Unification

Can the 14 per-structure models be unified into one multi-structure network, and does sharing help or hurt low-signal structures (e.g. right pallidum −0.73%)?

5
Transferability

Is reconstruction-constrained training transferable to other "predict-an-intrinsic-field-then-solve" problems — Neural Jacobian Fields, cloth / soft-body metric prediction?
