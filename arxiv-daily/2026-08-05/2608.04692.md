> **一句话总结：** 本文首次在闭环机器人控制中对 VLA 策略的 task-vector 减法做"目标-控制"审计：在 LIBERO-Goal 上 5/10 技能可被精准抑制，但保留率仅 52%（78%~4%），三种动作头族均有此现象，而 Spatial/Object/Long-horizon 以及多技能叠加则出现选择性崩塌。

> **TL;DR:** This is the first closed-loop target-and-control audit of per-skill task-vector subtraction (Ilharco-style negation) on multitask VLA policies. On all 10 LIBERO-Goal skills of MergeVLA, subtraction splits skills into three regimes — 5 separation, 3 resistant, 2 global collapse — and on held-out states the 5 suppressible targets stay at 0% SR while mean baseline-normalized control retention is only 52% (range 78% down to 4%), versus 88.3% for Gradient-Difference and 95.6% for the retrain oracle on the matched panel; the separation pattern recurs across MergeVLA (continuous), OpenVLA-7B (discrete-token), and π0.5 (flow-matching) but breaks on Spatial/Object/Long10 and under multi-skill composition.

## 核心贡献

- 首次提出"闭环目标-控制"审计范式：对每个技能的 task-vector 减法，在 LIBERO MuJoCo 中以 20 次 rollout 评测目标技能和全部 9 个控制技能，并用预设阈值（目标 SR≤20%、平均控制 SR≥40%）划分结果，纠正了以往仅用静态 loss 或 token likelihood 评测局部性的偏差。
- 给出 LIBERO-Goal 完整 10×10 减法矩阵：五项分离（t0/t2/t3/t5/t6，目标≤15%、基线归一化控制保留 57.8%）、三项抗抑制（t1/t4/t7）、两项全局崩塌（t8/t9），证明"一减就抑制、控制无损"并非普适属性。
- 在留出初态上验证：5 个可抑制目标全部保持 0/20，但平均控制保留仅 52%，最坏控制从 78% 跌到 4%，每行至少有一个无关控制被实质损害——单一目标指标会掩盖这种崩塌。
- 跨架构泛化：在连续回归（MergeVLA-1.41B）、离散 token（OpenVLA-7B，off-diagonal 50.8%）、流匹配（π0.5）三种动作头族上重复出现目标抑制模式；但在 Spatial/Object/Long10 这类共享运动基元或子目标的套件，以及多技能叠加下选择性消失。
- 机制性诊断：matched-norm 实验揭示围绕 Goal 锚点的方向特异"符号不对称"（同范数加法目标保留 50%、随机方向仅 25% 抑制），而平均权重余弦（Goal 90 对均值 0.011）无法预测控制存活；重学习探针显示抑制可快速恢复（k=250 步即回到 85%，而 task-naive 仍为 0%），符合"行为遮蔽"而非认证遗忘。

## Key Contributions

- First closed-loop target-and-control audit of task-vector subtraction on multitask VLA policies, with all 10 LIBERO-Goal skills evaluated by 20 rollouts per cell against pre-registered thresholds (target SR≤20%, mean control SR≥40%), exposing structure invisible to static-loss or token-likelihood probes.
- A three-regime map: on MergeVLA-LIBERO-Goal, negation produces 5 separation rows, 3 resistant rows, and 2 global-collapse rows; this split is stable across separation thresholds 30–40% and reproduces in sign across three seeds for the t0/t1 probe.
- Held-out evidence that target suppression generalizes (all 5 targets at 0/20) while control selectivity does not: mean baseline-normalized control retention 52%, range 78%→4%, with 4 of 5 rows zeroing at least one nominally unrelated control — every row materially harms a control.
- Cross-architecture transfer of the separation pattern across continuous (MergeVLA-1.41B), discrete-token (OpenVLA-7B, 50.8% mean off-diagonal SR), and flow-matching (π0.5) action heads, despite large differences in backbone scale and pretraining; cross-suite failure on Spatial/Object/Long10 and under multi-skill composition.
- Diagnostics that characterize but do not certify: a matched-norm sign asymmetry around the Goal anchor, the failure of mean weight-space cosine (0.011 over 90 ordered pairs) to predict control survival, and a relearning probe consistent with rapidly reversible behavioral masking rather than durable weight-level deletion.

## Methodology

The audit is built on the MergeVLA framework (Fu et al. 2026): a multitask goal model θGoal trained on N skills, with per-skill experts θexp,i fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 64) from a shared seed-42 initialization θbase. Each model has three components — a VLM backbone W (Qwen2.5-0.5B + dinosiglip-224px, ≈1.25B VLM params), an action head AH (0.16B), and a proprio projector PP. The per-skill task vector is the component-wise weight delta τ_i = (Wexp,i − Wbase, AHexp,i − AHinit, PPexp,i − PPinit).

The closed-form edit subtracts the scaled skill vector across all three components: θedit = θGoal − α·τ_i (α>0), a single CPU tensor operation (<1 s) that is data-free and gradient-free at edit time given precomputed experts. A constrained α-selection rule picks the smallest α with target SR=0%, breaking ties by highest mean control SR on a 3-control dev panel; selected α⋆ ranges 0.75–1.25 across the five suppressible skills. Multi-skill negation sums vectors and rescales by α ≈ αsingle/√|S|.

Evaluation is closed-loop: LIBERO MuJoCo/EGL rollouts, 20 trials per (model, task) cell, success defined by the benchmark predicate, with raw x/n counts and 95% Wilson intervals; paired comparisons reuse identical initial states across conditions. A skill's own-negation diagonal is the target; the other nine skills in the same row are the controls. The taxonomy is fixed before analysis: target suppressed (diagonal SR≤20%), row shows separation iff suppressed and mean off-diagonal control SR≥40%, resistant if target not suppressed, global collapse if target suppressed but mean control<40%.

Baselines and probes: (i) a norm-matched random-direction edit and equal-norm addition to isolate direction vs. norm effects; (ii) data-dependent gradient removal — NegGrad (500/1000/2000 steps), NegGrad+, Gradient-Difference — and a leave-one-task-out retrain oracle on the same matched t0 panel; (iii) a forward-only Alignment Score AS_i (no negation outcome used) that reads skill-specific inputs and ranks own-vs-others hidden-state shifts; (iv) a single-skill relearning probe comparing the negated t0 checkpoint against a task-naive base. Cross-architecture replication uses OpenVLA-7B (discrete-token, Open-X-pretrained, full-vector negation) and π0.5 (flow-matching, per-skill full fine-tunes negated on pi05_libero_finetuned).

## Results & Highlights

- Three-regime split on the full 10×10 LIBERO-Goal matrix (α=1.0, 20 rollouts/cell): 5 separation rows (t0, t2, t3, t5, t6; target SR ≤15%), 3 resistant (t1/t7 at 100%, t4 borderline 30%), and 2 global collapse (t8, t9: target 0% but every control also 0%). The post-hoc paired gap on the 5 suppressible skills is 53.3 pp (targets mean 4.0% vs. controls 57.3%; row-bootstrap 95% CI [45.7, 61.0], dz=5.3, Cliff's δ=1.0).
- Held-out validation (initial states 20–39, disjoint from α⋆ selection): all 5 targets stay at 0/20, but mean baseline-normalized control retention is 52%, ranging from t0 (78%) down to t6 (4%); 4 of 5 rows zero at least one control (worst-control: t0 35%, t2/t3/t5/t6 = 0%).
- Matched-panel gradient comparison (target t0, same 9 controls, same held-out states): all methods drive the target to 0/20; raw mean control SR — NegGrad 0.0%, NegGrad+ 66.1% (Wilson [59,73]%, zeroes one control), Gradient-Difference 88.3%, Negation 73.9%, Retrain oracle 95.6%. Negation is the only method with no removal-time data or gradient steps.
- Single-vector sign asymmetry around θGoal at matched norm: subtracting τ0 drives target to 0% (control 100%); adding the identical vector leaves the target at 50% (control 90%); a norm-matched random direction achieves only ~25% target reduction.
- Multi-skill composition collapses selectivity: cumulative negation of the 5 suppressible skills at α=1/√k drops controls to 10% at k=2 and to 0% for k≥3; negating t0 then t2 at α=1 collapses all four skills including control t1 that single-t0 negation had left at 100%.
- Cross-architecture target suppression: OpenVLA-7B suppresses t0/t1/t2 to 0% (t3 non-diagnostic, 10% baseline) with mean off-diagonal SR 50.8% (10 rollouts/cell); π0.5 (flow-matching) shows the same target-suppression pattern on its four Goal tasks. The qualitative pattern recurs despite ~10× backbone scale and corpus differences, though absolute margins remain model-dependent.
- Cross-suite failure: Spatial has no separation row (only t0 fully suppressed, controls collapse; 61.7% off-diagonal SR inflated by resistant rows); Object suppresses 3/4 targets but retains only 5.8% off-diagonal SR; Long10 suppresses 2/4 with 11.7% off-diagonal SR — despite similar mean weight-space cosine (matched four-skill means 0.0132–0.0142).
- VLM component is the primary driver: VLM-only negation matches full negation, while action-head-only negation leaves target SR at 80%; no single depth band suffices at its native norm (mean 91% retained vs. 0% at full depth).
- Relearning probe: negated t0 recovers to 85% (17/20, Wilson [64,95]%) by k=250 fine-tune steps, while the task-naive base is still at 0% (0/20, [0,16]%) — consistent with quickly reversible behavioral masking rather than durable unlearning.

## Authors & Credibility

Authors are from the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou) and AI2 Robotics, with affiliations to the Strong AI Lab / industrially adjacent robotics group. The work directly uses the public MergeVLA (Fu et al. 2026), OpenVLA (Kim et al. 2024), and lerobot pi05_libero checkpoints, cites the canonical task-arithmetic (Ilharco et al. 2023) and machine-unlearning literature, and frames its contribution carefully as descriptive post-hoc subgroup analysis rather than primary confirmatory inference. The paper provides a full 10×10 per-cell matrix (Fig. 2) as primary evidence, with the post-hoc inferential tests explicitly deferred to the supplement, and lists five scope limits. Code/data and a full method listing (Listing S1) are referenced as a supplement.

## Critical Assessment

- Novelty: Genuinely novel in scope. Prior task-arithmetic work (Ilharco et al. 2023) reports negation only as a static-loss forgetting side-effect on classifiers, and the closest VLA removal work (VLA-Forget / Ranjan and Polyzou 2026) is gradient-based and data-dependent at removal time. This is the first study to audit a closed-form, data-free, gradient-free subtraction through closed-loop robot success, which is a real gap.
- Rigor: Strong on measurement hygiene. Pre-registered thresholds, full per-cell matrices rather than cherry-picked rows, 95% Wilson intervals on n=20 (correctly noting a 0/20 cell is [0,16]% not "zero"), disjoint selection/evaluation initial-state splits (0–19 vs. 20–39), paired comparisons reusing identical states, and explicit honesty that the 5-skill subgroup is selected post hoc on the diagonal ≤15% criterion and therefore reported as descriptive, not confirmatory. The "resistant rows should not enter a locality average" rule avoids the common trap of inflating apparent locality.
- Reproducibility: Reasonably good. Public checkpoints, a complete method listing (Listing S1), training hyperparameters (AdamW, lr 2e-4, batch 8, 10k steps, LoRA rank 64, seed 42), and per-panel rollout counts are given. The main reproducibility risk is the single training seed for the full 10×10 matrices; the three-seed t0/t1 probe reproduces the target–control gap but with a run-dependent threshold, and the authors explicitly do not claim seed robustness for the matrices. All evaluation is LIBERO MuJoCo simulation only — no real-robot validation, which is a stated limitation.
- Significance: Practically important for the embodied-model-editing community. It shows that a target-only success-rate metric (which would score all 5 held-out edits as "successful") silently hides control collapse to 4–52%, and that selectivity breaks on Spatial/Object/Long10 and under multi-skill composition — boundaries that practitioners deploying VLA unlearning would otherwise discover at runtime.
- Red flags / weaknesses: (a) The cross-architecture margins on OpenVLA-7B use only 10 rollouts/cell (vs. 20 on MergeVLA) and t3 is non-diagnostic at a 10% baseline, so the "recurs across action-head families" claim is directionally supported but cell-wise noisy by the authors' own admission. (b) The matched gradient baseline panel (E1) covers a single target (t0), one seed, and one budget per gradient method — Gradient-Difference's 88.3% vs. negation's 73.9% is a single-point comparison, not an architecture-wide ranking. (c) The forward-only Alignment Score ranks suppressibility within a suite but its threshold does not transfer across suites (Supp. Sec. E), undercutting its value as a cheap predictor. (d) Mean weight-space cosine (0.011 over 90 ordered Goal pairs) fails to explain control survival, leaving "behavioral overlap" as a hypothesis rather than an established mechanism — the diagnostics are explicitly characterized as descriptive, not explanatory. (e) The method requires a precomputed per-skill expert bank (10k steps each), so "data-free and gradient-free at edit time" is true only conditional on already having experts; negating an off-the-shelf multitask vector is "broadly destructive" per the authors.

## Strengths

- Closed-loop target-and-control protocol with pre-registered thresholds is the right lens and a methodological contribution reusable beyond this paper.
- Honest reporting: full 10×10 matrix, worst-control column, explicit post-hoc caveat, and a minimum-reporting-unit checklist (target x/n, every control x/n, baseline-normalized mean and worst control, selection/evaluation split, removal-time data/optimization).
- Strong matched-panel baseline comparison against NegGrad, NegGrad+, Gradient-Difference, and a retrain oracle, plus a norm-matched random-direction control that confirms the effect is direction-specific rather than generic norm damage.
- Cross-architecture replication on three action-head families (continuous, discrete-token, flow-matching) and four LIBERO suites, with the boundaries (Spatial/Object/Long10, multi-skill composition) reported as carefully as the positive Goal result.
- Relearning probe directly tests the masking-vs-deletion hypothesis and finds rapid recovery, a genuinely informative negative result for anyone tempted to treat closed-form negation as certified unlearning.

## Limitations

- Single training seed for the full 10×10 matrices; only a three-seed t0/t1 probe is reported, with a run-dependent threshold.
- Simulation only (LIBERO MuJoCo); no real-robot or non-LIBERO validation.
- Clean single-skill selectivity presumes a per-skill expert bank trained by the authors; negating an off-the-shelf multitask vector is broadly destructive, limiting out-of-the-box applicability.
- Cross-suite failure on Object and Long-horizon means the sharpest claims are calibrated only on MergeVLA-Goal.
- Matched baseline panel covers one target, one seed, and one budget per gradient method — descriptive cost–retention comparison, not an architecture-wide ranking.
- Diagnostics (sharpness, alignment score, cosine geometry) co-vary with outcomes but establish no mechanism; the Alignment Score threshold does not transfer across suites.
- The "data-free, gradient-free at edit time" property is conditional on precomputed experts, each costing ~6–18 GPU-hours to train.

## Relevance

Directly relevant to research on VLA policy adaptation, model editing, and machine unlearning in embodied AI: it provides a concrete evaluation protocol and empirical boundaries for whether cheap, data-free weight edits can safely remove a single learned robot skill without retraining — a question anyone deploying or red-teaming multitask VLA policies will face. The closed-loop target-and-control methodology and the "locality is a cliff, not a floor" finding are reusable benchmarks for future work on safe policy surgery.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the three-regime structure (separation / resistant / global collapse) persist across multiple training seeds and on real-robot LIBERO transfers, or is the 5/3/2 split seed- and simulator-specific?
- Can a behavioral-overlap metric (e.g., shared trajectory or subgoal overlap between skills) predict control survival where mean weight-space cosine (0.011) fails, and does it explain why Spatial/Object/Long10 collapse while Goal separates?
- Is there a multi-skill negation rule (beyond summed, projected, or mutually-orthogonal variants) that preserves selectivity for k≥2 without leaving targets partially intact, or is single-skill selectivity a fundamental boundary of closed-form arithmetic?
- How does task-vector negation interact with input-side efficiency methods (keyframe search, adaptive frame pruning) that also change the visual-token budget a VLA processes — are the weight-side and input-side interventions composable?
- Can locate-then-edit methods (e.g., ROME-style layer-targeted surgery) recover selectivity given that no single depth band carries the edit at its native norm, or does the distributed nature of the VLM edit rule them out for policies?
- Does the rapid-relearning result (85% by 250 steps vs. 0% for task-naive) hold for safety-relevant or hazardous skills, where the cost of re-exposure is the very risk the edit was meant to mitigate?
