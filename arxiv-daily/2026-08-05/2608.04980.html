Protoreasoning in Tiny Transformers Eduardo Valle & Fergal Reid · Fin AI Research 2026·08·05 chain-of-thought small-models formal-languages reasoning interpretability
Key Finding

A ~1M-param transformer gains real out-of-distribution generalization from a deterministic protoreasoning trace — and it's the content of the steps, not the extra tokens, that does the work.

On two exactly-gradable tasks built over Dyck-4 bracket languages, a step-by-step pruning trace lifts a tiny Llama-2 decoder well beyond the vanilla baseline on held-out structure — while erasing the trace into filler dots collapses the gain to zero.

Methodology

A minimal, controllable organism for studying chain-of-thought at a scale where natural-language CoT is impossible — so every effect can be isolated and graded exactly.

Inputs are Dyck-4 sentences (four balanced, nested bracket types). Two tasks probe different reasoning: Deepest Path (DP) emits the opening brackets along the route to the deepest leaf; Maximum-order Preleaf (PL) emits the brackets of the largest all-leaf sibling brood. A protoreasoning trace is inserted between the separator and the answer — each step is the previous state after pruning tree nodes that cannot belong to the solution. Step dropout (p=0.15) forces recovery from arbitrary subsequences; SkipAlign (PoSE-style positional augmentation) stops the longer trace from being penalized on position. Traces are never supplied at test time.

A sharp expressivity cliff.

The 128W/4L/16H (~1.05M) model holds 100% in-domain DP validity even at hl=128; the 32W/2L/8H (~33K) model collapses from 95% (hl=32) to 1% (hl=128). At every scale tested, OOD generalization is at least as hard as expressivity.

Strengths & Limitations

+ Strengths

What lands well
  • Statistically robust — 10 seeds, Sobol hyperparameter search, disjoint validation, >3300 total runs; rare at this scale.
  • Two cleanly designed tasks with a natural two-axis difficulty gradient (length × structure) and exact automatic grading.
  • Direct ablation of the "more tokens = more compute" confound via dot-by-dot trace erasure.
  • Honest about failure — explicitly states extrapolation and cross-transfer fail; frames the work as a shared framework.
  • Cheap & reproducible compute footprint (one multi-GPU node, ~1 day) lowers the barrier to follow-ups.

× Limitations

Where it frays
  • Purely syntactic bracket-manipulation — no semantics, no language, no tools; far from real CoT workloads.
  • Loses on Low extrapolation (DP-Low 21.6% vs 39.6%) — a failure mode acknowledged but not fixed.
  • Traces are hand-designed & deterministic; the paper never tests whether models discover them — so it speaks to elicitation, not emergence.
  • No released code/data confirmed; reproducibility rests on a well-specified but unverified generator.
  • One architecture family (mini-Llama-2) and one language (Dyck-4); the scaling sweep varies size, not design.
  • Trace faithfulness only indirectly probed — cannot show the model causally "uses" the trace rather than post-hoc generating it.

Open Questions

Q · 01

Do models spontaneously discover protoreasoning-like internal computation without the trace?

Testable via attention-pattern / residual-stream mechanistic probes.
Q · 02

Can a length or step-count curriculum rescue the Low extrapolation pattern?

Where trace currently underperforms vanilla (DP-Low 21.6% vs 39.6%).
Q · 03

How much of the benefit is ordering, locality, versus raw content?

A shuffled-trace or reverse-trace ablation would disentangle these.
Q · 04

Does mixed-task cross-transfer fail from shared-encoder interference or task-specific trace wiring?

Would a task-conditioned prefix recover it?
Q · 05

Does the trace-content-matters finding generalize beyond tree pruning?

Extend to parity, addition, or finite-state transduction.
Q · 06

Is the emitted trace faithful (causally used) or post-hoc?

Testable via interventions that ablate specific trace steps at inference.
