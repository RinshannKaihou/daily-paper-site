> **一句话总结：** 本文为 0.5B–14B 小模型建立了"口语化置信度→后校准→Clopper–Pearson 有限样本风险证书"的可证推迟框架，理论上证明严格单调校准不改变风险-覆盖前沿且温度缩放在"置信>1/2而精度<1/2"时必然失效，实验在 ARC-Challenge 和 TruthfulQA 上对 11 个模型进行 25,168 次预测，发现仅在 20% 风险预算下 3 个模型-任务对获准自主作答、10% 下无人获准，并修复了 TruthfulQA 多选题正确选项恒居首位的排序偏差。

> **TL;DR:** This paper couples verbalized-confidence extraction, post-hoc calibration, and a Clopper–Pearson certified threshold procedure to decide when 0.5B–14B open-weight models should autonomously answer vs. defer to a human; across 25,168 predictions on ARC-Challenge and TruthfulQA (11 models, 3 families), Platt scaling drives ECE as low as 0.02–0.03 and 8/22 model-task pairs sit exactly at the proven temperature-scaling infeasibility floor, yet certified autonomy is granted to only 3 model-task pairs at a 20% risk budget (Qwen2.5-14B/7B on ARC, Qwen2.5-14B on TruthfulQA) and to none at 10%.

## 核心贡献

- **三个可证结论界定校准能力上限**：(1) 严格单调校准（含温度缩放）保持风险-覆盖前沿与误差检测 AUROC 不变；(2) 推论给出温度缩放的精确不可行下界——当原始置信恒大于 1/2 而精度低于 1/2 时，任何温度都无法校准；(3) Clopper–Pearson 过程在 200 题校准集上给出有限样本风险证书，配合 union bound 矫正数据相关的阈值选择。
- **风险可控的推迟框架**：将"口语化置信度抽取 → Platt 校准 → 带证书阈值选择"串联为可在单台 MacBook Air M4 上本地运行的完整部署流水线，每个推迟决策可还原为 `(c, ĉ, τ, action)` 的可审计记录。
- **系统化的小模型评测**：横跨 Qwen2.5（0.5B–14B）、Llama 3.2/3.1（1B–8B）、Gemma 3（1B–12B）共 11 个开源指令微调模型，在 ARC-Challenge 与 TruthfulQA 上累计 25,168 条预测，并配套阈值迁移、抽取格式等多组消融。
- **发现并修复 TruthfulQA 多选题排序偏差**：原始 single-answer 形式下全部 817 题正确项恒列首位，恒答首字母策略可得满分；经确定性 hash 置换后正确字母分布近均匀（A/B/C/D=203/223/200/191），恒首策略骤降至 24.8%，作者将其作为字母计分前的标准审查建议。

## Key Contributions

- **Three theorems that delimit what calibration can buy**: Proposition 2 proves strictly monotone calibration (including temperature scaling) leaves the risk–coverage frontier and error-detection AUROC unchanged; Corollary 1 gives an exact ECE floor showing temperature scaling provably fails whenever raw confidence stays above 1/2 while accuracy is below 1/2; Proposition 3 turns a 200-question calibration set into a Clopper–Pearson finite-sample upper bound on the true conditional risk R(τ), with a union-bound correction (δ → δ/|T|) covering the data-dependent threshold choice.
- **An end-to-end certified deferral pipeline**: verbalized integer confidence → Platt sigmoid calibration → minimal feasible threshold τ* = min{τ : Clopper–Pearson upper bound ≤ rmax}; every abstention is an auditable `(c, ĉ, τ, action)` tuple rather than an opaque refusal.
- **A systematic 11-model, 3-family study at deployment scale**: Qwen2.5 (0.5B–14B), Llama 3.2/3.1 (1B–8B), Gemma 3 (1B–12B) evaluated on ARC-Challenge and TruthfulQA (25,168 local predictions), with ablations on threshold transfer, calibration granularity, and elicitation format.
- **A TruthfulQA answer-ordering audit and repair**: the authors show all 817 pooled single-answer MCQ items place the correct option first, so a constant-first-letter policy attains 100% accuracy; a deterministic hash-seeded permutation makes the correct-letter distribution near-uniform (A/B/C/D = 203/223/200/191) and drops the constant-first policy to 24.8%.

## Methodology

The framework has three components, summarized in Figure 1.

**1. Problem formulation.** Given N multiple-choice items, a model f emits a predicted label ŷ and a raw confidence c ∈ [0,1] obtained by dividing the verbalized integer score by 100. A calibration map g : [0,1] → [0,1] yields ĉ = g(c), fit on a disjoint 200-question calibration set D_cal. A deferral policy with threshold τ answers autonomously when ĉ ≥ τ and defers otherwise; the deployment specifies a maximum tolerable risk rmax.

**2. Verbalized confidence elicitation.** A single prompt requests an answer letter plus an integer confidence in a fixed `letter,number` format, requiring only one generation pass per question (no token probabilities). A strict parser plus a conservative lenient parser recover valid (letter, confidence) pairs; refusals are never recovered and count as automatic deferrals in deployment.

**3. Post-hoc calibration.** Three maps are compared: temperature scaling `ĉ = σ(logit(c)/T)` (T fit over [0.1, 100] to minimize ECE), Platt scaling `ĉ = σ(a·logit(c)+b)` (the bias b is the single degree of freedom TS lacks), and isotonic regression (best non-decreasing step function). Calibration quality is ECE with 10 equal-width bins. Because all 22 fitted Platt slopes are positive, every deployed Platt map is strictly increasing and inherits the monotonicity guarantees of Proposition 2.

**4. Threshold selection and certification.** τ* is the minimal feasible threshold maximizing coverage subject to Risk(τ) ≤ rmax (Proposition 1). Proposition 3 then certifies it: conditional on n(τ) retained i.i.d. calibration examples with k(τ) errors, R(τ) ≤ BetaInv(1−δ; k+1, n−k) holds with probability ≥ 1−δ (δ = 0.05 throughout). A union bound over the grid T of distinct calibration confidences covers the data-dependent selected threshold. The whole pipeline runs locally through Ollama with default quantized builds on one MacBook Air M4 (32 GB), sampling temperature zero (distinct from the calibration temperature T).

## Results & Highlights

- **Temperature-scaling infeasibility floor matched empirically.** Eight of 22 model–task pairs drive the optimal temperature to the search bound T* ≥ 100, and their residual ECE sits at the Corollary 1 floor: Qwen2.5-0.5B on TruthfulQA reaches ECE 0.283 against a predicted bound of 0.276 (gap 0.7 pp), and Llama3.2-1B reaches 0.254 against 0.252 (gap 0.2 pp).
- **Platt vs. temperature scaling on small models.** Gemma3-1B on ARC drops from an uncalibrated ECE of 0.687 to 0.262 under TS but to **0.030 under Platt**; Qwen2.5-0.5B on ARC falls from 0.497 uncalibrated to ECE **0.022 under Platt**, the lowest in the table. Platt dominates temperature scaling in every row.
- **Certified autonomy is granted sparingly.** At rmax = 20% the Clopper–Pearson certificate authorizes only three model–task pairs: Qwen2.5-14B on ARC at 99.8% coverage with 8.1% realized test risk, Qwen2.5-7B on ARC at 93.3% coverage with 10.8% risk, and Qwen2.5-14B on TruthfulQA at 43.6% coverage with 14.2% risk. **At rmax = 10% no pair is certified** under any model.
- **AUROC for error detection stays weak.** Models below 2B sit near 0.5, Llama3.2-3B on TruthfulQA falls to 0.468, and the largest models reach only 0.63–0.66; Gemma3-12B attains 89.2% accuracy on ARC with an AUROC of just 0.509 — high accuracy does not imply usable error discrimination.
- **Calibration granularity matters more than task identity.** On the 14 TS-feasible pairs, a single global temperature gives mean test ECE 0.157, per-task temperatures 0.155 (≈ no gain), but per-model-per-task temperatures 0.072 — knowing the model roughly halves the error.
- **Cross-task threshold transfer fails.** Reusing ARC thresholds on TruthfulQA at rmax = 20% violates the budget for every tested model by 1.3 to 30.6 percentage points before calibration; calibration fixes scale but not discrimination (Llama3.1-8B stays near +30 pp, Gemma3-4B retains no examples).
- **TruthfulQA artifact repaired.** The constant-first-letter policy drops from 100% (unpermuted) to **24.8%** after the hash-seeded permutation, close to chance, confirming the original single-answer MCQ form inflates letter-scored accuracy for position-biased models.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Genuine but incremental. The three theoretical results are clean but individually known-flavored: ranking invariance under monotone recalibration is standard in selective-classification folklore, and Corollary 1 is essentially a one-line consequence of the sigmoid being bounded below by 1/2. The novel packaging is the *coupling* — using the Corollary 1 floor as a falsifiable prediction that the experiments then match to within 0.2–0.7 pp, and the specific TruthfulQA position artifact is a real and underreported data quirk.
- **Rigor**: Theory is tight and proofs are short; the union-bound correction for data-dependent τ selection is correctly handled, and the authors honestly note the certificate is void under distribution shift. However, **the certificate is never compared against any baseline selective-prediction method** (e.g., conformal risk control, Geifman & El-Yaniv 2017 guaranteed-risk bounds) — the empirical "no pair certified at 10%" finding could partly reflect Clopper–Pearson conservatism rather than model quality alone, and this confound is not isolated.
- **Reproducibility**: Strong on hardware (single M4 laptop, quantized Ollama builds) but the prompt template, parser, hash seed, and exact quantization tags are under-specified in the main body (deferred to Sect. 4.1 prose); no code release is referenced. The 200-question calibration sets are fixed sizes with no seed sensitivity analysis.
- **Significance**: Practically meaningful for offline/edge deployment of small models — the negative result that 10% risk budgets certify nothing is a useful cautionary datum. But the scope is narrow: only multiple-choice, only two datasets, only models ≤ 14B, only a single quantization scheme.
- **Red flags**: The headline "three of 22 pairs certified" conflates strong models (Qwen2.5-14B) with a procedure that certifies almost nothing; the implied message ("calibrated refusal is the correct default") is sound but the paper leans on it rhetorically. The 22 model–task pairs are not independent (same models, related datasets), inflating apparent breadth. The TruthfulQA repair, while valuable, is validated only by the constant-first policy dropping to 24.8% — no re-evaluation of prior published TruthfulQA MCQ numbers is attempted.

## Strengths

- Theory and measurement agree quantitatively: the TS infeasibility floor is met to within a percentage point, a rare falsifiable prediction in calibration work.
- Honest negative results (no certification at rmax = 10%, weak AUROC, cross-task transfer failures) are reported prominently rather than buried.
- The TruthfulQA answer-ordering audit is a concrete, immediately reusable methodological contribution.
- Fully local, low-cost experimental setup broadens accessibility for replication.
- Transparent per-example deferral rationale (`(c, ĉ, τ, action)`) makes the policy auditable in deployment.

## Limitations

- Both benchmarks are multiple-choice; open-ended generation (where verbalized confidence is arguably harder) is untested.
- The Clopper–Pearson certificate assumes i.i.d. deployment and is **void under distribution shift** — a strong assumption for real edge deployments.
- 200-question calibration sets cap the certifiable coverage regime; the empty rmax = 10% outcome may reflect sample size as much as model quality.
- Models are capped at 14B under one (default Ollama) quantization scheme; no sensitivity to quantization precision is reported.
- Format-robustness check covers only two elicitation formats, one dataset (TruthfulQA), and three models.
- No comparison against conformal prediction or other guaranteed-risk baselines isolates whether Clopper–Pearson conservatism drives the sparse certification.

## Relevance

该论文直接命中"小模型本地部署 + 不确定性量化 + 可信赖推迟"这条研究主线：它把口语化置信度、后校准与有限样本风险证书三者拧成一个可在消费级笔记本上跑通的流水线，并给出温度缩放失效的精确判据与跨任务阈值不可迁移的实证，对评估小型开源 LLM 在高风险场景下的"何时该拒答"具有方法学与基准双重参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would conformal risk control (Angelopoulos & Bates 2023) or the Geifman–El-Yaniv guaranteed-risk bound certify strictly more autonomy than Clopper–Pearson at the same 200-question budget on these 22 pairs?
- Does the Corollary 1 infeasibility floor extend to open-ended generation, where verbalized confidence is harder to elicit and the accuracy floor shifts?
- How sensitive is the sparse-certification finding to quantization — do higher-precision (e.g., FP16) builds of the same models cross the rmax = 10% threshold?
- The TruthfulQA position artifact is repaired by a hash-seeded permutation; how many published TruthfulQA MCQ accuracy numbers are inflated, and by how much, when re-scored on the permuted form?
- Can a learned (rather than fixed-prompt) elicitation push AUROC above the ~0.66 ceiling observed here, and would that translate into measurably more certified coverage at fixed rmax?
