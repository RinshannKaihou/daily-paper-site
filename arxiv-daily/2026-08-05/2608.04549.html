Why this matters

Quantifies that frontier LLMs remain far below expert usability on real European executive decisions — deploy with caution and mandatory human review.

Relevant to LLM-eval researchers, judge-design, and any vertical deploying LLMs into high-stakes professional workflows.
Critical edge

The headline gap rests on a 33-item subsample; the study is essentially non-reproducible, and the “AI judges fail” verdict comes from a single judge (n=1).

  • Only 33 / 413 items have expert answers
  • Original experts no longer available for re-runs

01

Methodology

413 open-ended, ~200-word European executive scenarios · 47 CV-screened experts · cross-family difficulty gate

Question construction

47 experts passed CV screening + interviews and were split across Finance (74), Marketing (85), Business (113), Product (141). Each item is a self-contained, goal-oriented scenario drawn from the author's first-hand professional experience — explicitly European-market-specific (e.g. a German works-council step in HR).

Every question ships with a 5–10 item verb-first checklist of criteria a correct answer must satisfy — functioning as the item's quasi-ground-truth.

CV + interview→ Draft item→ Checklist→ Auto QA→ Difficulty gate

Validation & evaluation protocol

Two-stage validation. (1) Claude Sonnet 4.6 auto-rejects on 6 criteria (hint leakage, identity probing, prompt injection…). (2) A cross-family difficulty gate uses Claude Haiku 4.5 as solver and Gemini Flash 3.5 as grader — deliberately cross-family to avoid the self-enhancement bias of Panickssery et al. 2024. Items passing >60% auto-checklist were returned (~30 attempts avg).

Three instruments, two graders per item. Responses are anonymized & shuffled, then scored on a 5-attribute rubric (Domain, Localization, Reasoning, Communication, Actionability), checklist fulfillment, and an explicit preference rank. Solve Rate = mean rubric ≥ 3.0 and checklist ≥ 60%.

On a 33-item subset, authors wrote ideal answers and blindly evaluated them as a 7th "model".

02

Results & Highlights

All pairwise model orderings significant · p-values from 1.26e-6 to 9.2e-23
Headline gap

Best model Fable 5 reaches 56.9% Solve Rate on all 413 questions; lowest-ranked Mistral Large sits at 18.4% — a wide spread on identical items.

Experts near ceiling

On the blind 33-item subset, expert answers hit 92.4% Solve Rate vs Fable 5's 39.4%, average rank 1.76, and are preferred in 74.24% of head-to-head rankings.

Robust ranking

Paired t-tests on consecutive ranks give p between 1.26e-6 and 9.2e-23; resampling shows all pairs cross p=0.01 at n=100 — the published ordering is far from noise.

Hard hurts most

Fable 5 actually drops to 2nd on Hard questions while leading on Easy/Medium; even on Easy, the best frontier model barely crosses 50% Solve Rate.

Auto metrics fail

BLEU r=0.27 / τ=0.21 and ROUGE-Lsum r=0.37 / τ=0.29 — weak. The DeepSeek LLM judge (τ=0.47–0.55) achieves correlation only by over-scoring strong models and under-scoring weak ones, masking genuine human-opinion diversity.

Cost

~5 hrs per item to grade all six responses; >4,000 human expert hours total. Ranking stable even when restricted to the 10 most critical or 10 most lenient evaluators.

03

Strengths & Limitations

side-by-side · same evidence, opposite readings

Strengths 05

  • 4,000+ expert hours, 47 vetted experts, two graders per item — an unusually high bar for a benchmark of this kind.
  • Three complementary instruments collapsed into one interpretable aggregate (Solve Rate), with thorough inter-instrument and inter-evaluator consistency statistics.
  • Genuine open-ended, professionally grounded items with "no golden answer, real professional standard" structure.
  • Principled cross-family difficulty gate mitigates self-enhancement bias; honest rebuttal of the "LLM judge ≈ average human" claim.
  • Candid limitations section explicitly flagging reproducibility, missing qualitative failure-mode taxonomy, and the n=1 LLM-judge caveat.

Limitations 07

  • Only 33 / 413 items have expert-authored reference answers, restricting the strength of the headline human-vs-model comparison.
  • Essentially non-reproducible: re-running needs the same experts, who are no longer available; no public release of items or grades is promised.
  • Items assume presented facts are complete and correct, ignoring the data-quality & uncertainty issues real executives face.
  • All six models evaluated single-shot, no system prompt, no tools — a hard ceiling that may understate agentic / RAG deployments.
  • Only one LLM judge tested (DeepSeek v4-Pro); generalizations about "AI judges" overreach the evidence.
  • No qualitative failure-mode taxonomy — explicitly deferred to future work.
  • Commercial COI with Sovrano AI not given a dedicated statement.
Credibility note  ·  All five authors are affiliated with Sovrano AI, a commercial entity marketing European executive-assistance products that EuroExec is designed to evaluate — the benchmark both targets and potentially benefits its authors' commercial positioning. No independent academic affiliation is listed. Methodology safeguards (CV+interview recruitment, fixed monthly stipends, right to withdraw) and statistical rigor are, however, above average for a human-evaluation paper.
04

Open Questions

where the work points next
Q1

How would the ranking change under an agentic setting — system prompt, tool use, retrieval, multi-turn — instead of the single-shot zero-context protocol used here?

Q2

Is the LLM-judge over-/under-scoring pattern stable across judge families, or specific to DeepSeek v4-Pro? Would a cross-family judge panel correct the artifact?

Q3

What concrete failure modes drive Fable 5's Hard-question degradation — do they cluster in a rubric attribute (e.g. Reasoning) or a domain?

Q4

Can the 4,000-hour effort be distilled into a rubric-conditioned judge model that preserves the ranking — and how would its τ compare to the 0.55 human ceiling observed here?

Q5

How sensitive is the headline 92.4% vs 56.9% gap to the arbitrary Solve Rate threshold (rubric ≥ 3.0, checklist ≥ 60%)? What does the full threshold curve look like?

Q6

Do European-specific items (e.g. German works-council steps) systematically punish US-centric training data — and would regional fine-tuning close part of the gap?
