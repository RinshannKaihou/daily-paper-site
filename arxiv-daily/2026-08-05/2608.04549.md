> **一句话总结：** 作者构建 EuroExec——由 47 位欧洲资深专家手写 413 道开放式长篇高管决策任务（覆盖金融、营销、商业、产品四大领域），并用 4000+ 人工工时以三套评估工具（rubric、checklist、排序）评测 6 个前沿模型；最强模型 Fable 5 的 Solve Rate 仅 56.9%，而盲评的专家参考答案高达 92.4% 且在 74% 的直接排序中胜出，证明前沿模型尚未达到欧洲高管决策辅助的专业标准，人工评估仍不可替代。

> **TL;DR:** EuroExec is a human-expert benchmark of 413 open-ended long-form European executive decision tasks authored by 47 vetted domain experts across Finance, Marketing, Business, and Product. Six frontier LLMs (Fable 5, GPT-5.5, Claude Opus 4.8, Gemini 3.1 Pro, GLM-5.2, Mistral Large) were each manually graded by two domain experts using a 5-attribute rubric, item-specific checklists, and preference rankings (>4,000 human hours). The best model, Fable 5, solves only 56.9% of tasks versus 92.4% for blindly-judged expert answers, which are preferred in 74% of head-to-head rankings; all pairwise model orderings are statistically significant (p-values between 1.26e-6 and 9.2e-23). Both classical reference metrics (BLEU/ROUGE) and a DeepSeek LLM judge fail to reproduce the human ranking, the LLM judge systematically over-scoring strong models and under-scoring weak ones.

## 核心贡献

- 提出 EuroExec——首个聚焦"开放式长篇欧洲高管决策"的人工专家评测基准，包含 413 道由 47 名通过简历筛选和面试认证的资深专家基于真实从业经验手工撰写的题目，每题配有 5–10 条作者亲自拟定的 checklist 作为"准 ground truth"。
- 设计三套互补的人工评估工具：统一的 5 维度 rubric（Domain/Localization/Reasoning/Communication/Actionability，1–5 Likert 评分）、题目特定 checklist 命中率（hit/partial/miss）和显式偏好排序，并由此合成一个聚合指标 Solve Rate（rubric 均值≥3.0 且 checklist 命中率≥60% 视为"通过"）。
- 投入 4000+ 人工专家小时，对 6 个跨厂商前沿模型（覆盖 Anthropic、OpenAI、Google、Zhipu、Mistral）逐题双人独立盲评；并在 33 题子集上把专家手写的"理想答案"匿名混入当作第 7 个模型参与盲评。
- 严格量化评估者一致性：用 Kendall τ（显式 vs rubric 0.77、vs checklist 0.67）和 Pearson r（rubric 对 r=0.29、checklist 对 r=0.52）证明三套工具既相关又各保留独立信号。
- 系统对照自动评测：BLEU/ROUGE-Lsum 与人工 rubric 仅有弱相关（r=0.27/0.37），DeepSeek v4-Pro LLM-as-judge 虽然在排序上 Kendall τ 达 0.47–0.55，但会系统性高估强模型、低估弱模型，并非真正"代表平均人类"。

## Key Contributions

- Introduces **EuroExec**, the first human-expert benchmark for open-ended long-form European executive decision tasks: 413 questions hand-crafted by 47 CV-screened and interviewed domain experts (Finance 74, Marketing 85, Business 113, Product 141), each paired with an author-written checklist of 5–10 verb-first criteria that functions as the item's ground truth.
- Builds a **three-instrument manual evaluation protocol** — a common 5-attribute rubric (Domain, Localization, Reasoning, Communication, Actionability on a 1–5 Likert scale), item-specific checklist fulfillment (hit/partial/miss), and an explicit preference ranking — collapsed into a single aggregate metric **Solve Rate** (mean rubric ≥ 3.0 AND checklist fulfillment ≥ 60%).
- Spends **>4,000 human expert hours** to have two independent domain evaluators grade every response of six frontier LLMs (Fable 5, GPT-5.5, Claude Opus 4.8, Gemini 3.1 Pro, GLM-5.2, Mistral Large), and — on a 33-question subset — to evaluate expert-written reference answers blindly as a seventh "model."
- Provides **rigorous consistency and significance analysis**: all model pairwise orderings are significant (paired t-test p-values between 1.26e-6 and 9.2e-23), Kendall τ between explicit and derived rankings ranges 0.67–0.77 internally, and a resampling study shows ~150 questions suffice to establish the ranking at p < 0.01.
- Demonstrates that **automatic metrics are inadequate** for this task class: BLEU (r=0.27, τ=0.21) and ROUGE-Lsum (r=0.37, τ=0.29) correlate only weakly with human rubric scores and fail to reproduce the ranking; an independent DeepSeek v4-Pro LLM judge (Kendall τ 0.47–0.55 with humans) achieves its correlation artifactually by over-scoring top models and under-scoring weak ones, masking the diversity of genuine human opinion.

## Methodology

**Question construction.** 47 experts were selected via CV screening and one-on-one interviews and split into Finance, Marketing, Business, and Product. Each item is a self-contained, open-ended, long-form (~1,200 characters / ~200 words), goal-oriented scenario drawn from the author's first-hand professional experience and explicitly European-market-specific (e.g., a German works-council step in HR). Each question ships with a 5–10 item verb-first **checklist** of criteria a correct answer must satisfy.

**Two-stage validation pipeline.** (1) *Automated QA* with Claude Sonnet 4.6 rejecting items on six criteria (hint leakage, checklist relevance, unwanted patterns like identity probing or prompt injection, unprofessional language, unclear role, non-specificity). (2) *Difficulty Gate* using two cross-family LLMs (Claude Haiku 4.5 as solver, Gemini Flash 3.5 as grader, deliberately cross-family to avoid the self-enhancement bias of Panickssery et al. 2024); questions whose auto-passed checklist rate exceeded 60% were returned to authors for rewriting (~30 attempts on average). This yields a difficulty split of Hard 100, Medium 137, Easy 176.

**Response generation.** Six frontier models (Fable 5, GPT-5.5, Claude Opus 4.8, Gemini 3.1 Pro, GLM-5.2, Mistral Large) were each queried via provider API with default decoding settings, given the question as a single prompt with no system prompt, conversation history, tools, or file access. Responses averaged ~44,000 characters (~7,500 words). Cost and length were logged for secondary analyses (Appendices B, C).

**Expert evaluation.** Responses were anonymized and shuffled per question and each was independently graded by two domain experts along three instruments: the 5-attribute rubric (1–5 each), the item checklist (hit/partial=0.5/miss), and an explicit preference rank of all six responses. The aggregate **Solve Rate** defines an item as solved when mean rubric ≥ 3.0 and checklist fulfillment ≥ 60%. On a 33-item subset the question authors wrote ideal answers that were blindly evaluated alongside model outputs as a seventh entry.

**Automatic-metric comparison.** ROUGE-Lsum and BLEU were computed against expert golden answers on a 32-item balanced subset; DeepSeek v4-Pro was used as an independent LLM judge over the full dataset, with its rankings compared to human-derived ones via Kendall τ.

## Results & Highlights

- **Best frontier model still fails nearly half the tasks.** On the full 413 questions Fable 5 reaches the highest Solve Rate of **56.9%** (rubric 4.14, checklist 61.9%, win rate 49.5%); the lowest-ranked Mistral Large sits at **18.4%** Solve Rate (rubric 3.04, checklist 37.8%) — a wide spread across all six models on the same items.
- **Human experts near ceiling and beat every model.** On the 33-item blind subset, expert-written reference answers achieve **92.4% Solve Rate** (vs Fable 5's 39.4% on that subset), an average rank of **1.76**, and are preferred in **74.24%** of head-to-head rankings, i.e., experts are chosen first roughly three-quarters of the time.
- **The model ranking is statistically robust.** Paired t-tests on consecutive ranks give p-values between **1.26e-6 and 9.2e-23**; a resampling study shows all pairs cross p = 0.01 at n = 100 and p = 0.001 at n = 179, so the published ordering is far from noise.
- **Difficulty breakdown hurts the top model most.** By the Difficulty Gate split, Fable 5 actually drops to **second place on Hard questions** while remaining first on Easy/Medium, and even on the Easy subset the best frontier model barely crosses 50% Solve Rate.
- **Inter-instrument agreement is strong but signals differ.** Kendall τ is 0.77 (explicit vs rubric), 0.67 (explicit vs checklist), 0.68 (checklist vs rubric); cross-evaluator τ is lower (0.34–0.43) with the checklist the most evaluator-independent. The strongest single rubric attribute correlation is Actionability (r=0.82) and Domain (r=0.81), with Reasoning and Communication the most independent (r<0.7).
- **Automatic metrics are unreliable here.** BLEU achieves only Pearson r=0.27 / Kendall τ=0.21 against mean human rubric, ROUGE-Lsum r=0.37 / τ=0.29; the DeepSeek LLM judge reaches τ=0.47–0.55 with humans but, per Table 9, **over-estimates the strongest models and under-estimates the weakest**, producing an artificially compressed, more variable scoring distribution.
- **Effort required was substantial:** ~5 hours per item to evaluate all six responses, >4,000 human expert hours total, with the published ranking stable even when restricted to the 10 most critical or 10 most lenient evaluators.

## Authors & Credibility

All five authors (Pau Arnal, Khaled Denfir, Danylo Smahliuk, Amrut Avhad, Marcus A. Castro) are affiliated with **Sovrano AI**, a commercial entity. This is a clear conflict-of-interest signal: Sovrano AI markets products in the European executive-assistance space that EuroExec is designed to evaluate, so the benchmark both targets and potentially benefits the authors' commercial positioning. No independent academic affiliation is listed. The methodology section is, however, transparent about evaluator recruitment (CV screening + interview), compensation (fixed monthly stipend pegged to public early-career data per country), part-time remote contracts, and the right to withdraw — reasonable safeguards for the human study. Statistical rigor (paired t-tests, resampling-based p-value curves, multiple correlation coefficients) is above the average for a human-evaluation paper, and the authors cite and reproduce results from Panickssery et al. 2024 and Huidrom & Belz 2025, showing engagement with the relevant literature on LLM-judge bias.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty** is moderate rather than paradigm-shifting: human expert evaluation of open-ended generation is not new (Chiang et al. 2024 Chatbot Arena, Badawi et al. 2026, Akyürek et al. 2026 PRBench); the genuine contribution is the *European executive-decision* focus and the unusually large expert-hour budget. The framing as "frontier models fall short of experts" is essentially a restatement of the long-known gap on professional tasks.
- **Rigor** is the paper's strongest pillar: three independent instruments, two graders per item, paired t-tests with reported p-values down to 1.26e-6, resampling-based sample-size analysis, and Kendall τ across explicit and derived rankings. The decision to make the checklist the de-facto ground truth is well-motivated and the difficulty gate (cross-family solver/grader to avoid self-enhancement bias) is a thoughtful touch.
- **Reproducibility** is the clearest weakness, acknowledged by the authors themselves: another batch of evaluators would yield different numbers requiring fresh statistics, and extending the benchmark to newer models is "incrementally infeasible without replicating the full study" because the original experts are no longer available. The dataset and grading rubrics are not stated to be publicly released, so independent verification is essentially impossible. The pipeline depends on named model versions (Claude Sonnet 4.6, Haiku 4.5, Gemini Flash 3.5, DeepSeek v4-Pro) that will themselves drift, compounding irreproducibility.
- **Significance** is real but bounded: the headline finding (best model 56.9% vs experts 92.4%) is compelling, but the "no system prompt, no tools, single-shot" evaluation is a deliberately hard setting that may underestimate deployed performance — the paper does not run an agentic or tool-augmented comparison.
- **Red flags.** (1) Commercial sponsor-author overlap (Sovrano AI) is disclosed only in the affiliation, not in a dedicated COI statement. (2) The 33-item expert-answer subsample is small and noisy (a single instance = 1.5% of the subset), yet it carries the headline "experts preferred in 74% of rankings." (3) The GPT-5.5 anomaly in Finance is hand-waved as "poorer numerical capability" without an ablation isolating numeracy. (4) Only one LLM judge (DeepSeek v4-Pro) was tested, so the broad conclusion that "AI judges fail" rests on n=1. (5) The Solve Rate threshold (rubric ≥ 3.0 AND checklist ≥ 60%) is described as "generous" but is an arbitrary cut; small changes to it could move the absolute numbers even if the ranking is preserved. (6) The difficulty gate's own LLM-based grading is acknowledged as not detracting from human reliability, but the difficulty labels themselves (used in Figure 1) inherit that automatic grading's noise.

## Strengths

- Unusually large and well-documented human-expert effort (>4,000 hours, 47 vetted experts, two graders per item), setting a high bar for a benchmark of this kind.
- Three complementary evaluation instruments combined into one interpretable aggregate metric, with thorough inter-instrument and inter-evaluator consistency analysis (Kendall τ, Pearson r, p-value resampling curves).
- Genuine open-ended, professionally grounded items with real "no golden answer but real professional standard" structure, addressing a known gap in LLM evaluation.
- Principled validation pipeline (cross-family difficulty gate to mitigate self-enhancement bias) and honest reporting of automatic-metric failures including a careful rebuttal of the "LLM judge ≈ average human" claim.
- Honest limitations section explicitly flagging reproducibility, missing qualitative failure-mode analysis, and the n=1 LLM-judge caveat.

## Limitations

- Only 33 of 413 questions have expert-authored reference answers, restricting the strength of the headline human-vs-model comparison.
- The full evaluation is essentially non-reproducible: re-running requires the same experts, whose time is no longer available; no public release of items or grades is promised.
- Items are self-contained and assume presented facts are complete and correct, ignoring the data-quality and uncertainty issues that real executives routinely face (the authors themselves cite Brodeur et al. 2026 on healthcare).
- All six models are evaluated single-shot with default decoding, no system prompt, no tools — a hard ceiling that may understate agentic or retrieval-augmented deployments.
- Only one LLM judge (DeepSeek v4-Pro) was tested, so generalizations about "AI judges" overreach the evidence.
- No qualitative failure-mode taxonomy is provided; the paper explicitly defers this to future work.
- Commercial COI with Sovrano AI is not given a dedicated statement.

## Relevance

对关注 LLM 评测与基准构建的研究者，EuroExec 是一个高质量的"专家级开放式任务 + 全人工评测"范例，其三工具评估协议、一致性统计和"LLM-as-judge 系统性偏差"的论证对设计未来人类评测与判别 LLM 评判器可信度都直接有用；对欧洲/垂直行业 LLM 应用的工程师，它量化地揭示了前沿模型在真实高管决策场景中仍远未达到专家可用标准，提示部署需谨慎并辅以人工复核。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How would the ranking and absolute Solve Rate change under an agentic setting (system prompt, tool use, retrieval, multi-turn) instead of the single-shot zero-context protocol used here?
- Is the LLM-judge over-/under-scoring pattern (Table 9) stable across judge families, or is it specific to DeepSeek v4-Pro? Would a panel of cross-family judges correct the artifact?
- What concrete failure modes drive the Hard-question degradation of Fable 5, and do they cluster in a specific rubric attribute (e.g., Reasoning) or domain?
- Can the 4,000-hour expert effort be partially distilled into a rubric-conditioned judge model that preserves the ranking while reducing cost, and how would its τ against humans compare to the 0.55 ceiling observed here?
- How sensitive is the headline 92.4% vs 56.9% gap to the arbitrary Solve Rate threshold (rubric ≥ 3.0, checklist ≥ 60%), and what does the full Solve Rate-vs-threshold curve look like?
- Do the European-market-specific items (e.g., German works-council steps) systematically punish US-centric training data, and would regionally fine-tuned models close part of the gap?
