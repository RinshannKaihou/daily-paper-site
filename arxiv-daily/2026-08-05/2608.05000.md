> **一句话总结：** 本文通过一系列受控实验系统剖析统一多模态预训练的"物理规律",发现跨模态知识流动高度不对称(语言与视觉理解强力驱动视觉生成,反向几乎无迁移)、简单任务与共享注意力/解耦FFN架构能促进协同,且必须从一开始就联合训练以避免"视觉惰性",据此给出的数据配比配方(L70/U25/G5)能在仅5%生成数据预算下达到强生成能力,并在2T tokens训练的13.5B MoE模型上得到验证。

> **TL;DR:** Through controlled experiments on synthetic CLEVR and large-scale real data, this FAIR/Oxford study dissects the "physics" of unified multimodal (Transfusion) pretraining, finding asymmetric knowledge flow (language and visual understanding strongly drive visual generation; generation barely transfers back), that task complexity plus shared-attention/decoupled-FFN architectures govern synergy-vs-competition, and that early joint training is necessary to avoid a measurable "vision laziness" effect; the derived asymmetric L70/U25/G5 recipe matches a balanced L50/U25/G25 baseline on GenEval (0.482 vs 0.467) and DPG (0.689 vs 0.676) on SSTK/Cambrian-7M while using 5x fewer generation tokens, validated by training 13.5B MoE models on 2T tokens.

## 核心贡献

- **揭示非对称的知识流动规律**:在真实数据(SSTK + DCLM)和受控合成数据(CLEVR)上系统证明,语言是所有视觉任务的"通用增强器",视觉理解是视觉生成的强先验,而视觉生成对语言/理解几乎无反向迁移;且这种迁移是"概念相关"的——低层概念(颜色、形状)零样本完全无法迁移,结构概念(关系、尺寸、计数)只能由理解单向迁移到生成。
- **定位协同与竞争的机制**:证明任务复杂度决定模态间是协同还是竞争(简单任务互为增强器,复杂任务抢夺容量);在Transformer内部精确定位——共享注意力与归一化层促进协同,解耦FFN隔离竞争,该规律跨RAE/Raw Pixels/CLIP+VAE/AR(UniTok)四种视觉tokenizer通用。
- **证明早期统一的必要性并提出"视觉惰性"现象**:延迟视觉引入会系统性损害视觉能力;通过四条独立机制性测量(训练/推理激活L2、wrapper token嵌入范数、对图像token的注意力比例)揭示语言预热越长,视觉通路越"沉默",为late-fusion的局限提供了机械性解释。
- **给出高效预训练配方并大规模验证**:基于知识流非对称性导出L70/U25/G5(语言/理解/生成)的极端非对称配比,在2T tokens上训练的13.5B MoE(1.5B激活)模型上验证,仅用5%生成token即获得与均衡配方相当的生成质量,同时语言与理解指标全面更优。

## Key Contributions

- **Demystifies asymmetric knowledge flow**: Using both web-scale real data (SSTK image-text pairs, DCLM text) and a strictly controlled synthetic CLEVR testbed, the authors show language universally boosts all visual tasks, visual understanding is a strong prior for generation, but visual generation yields near-zero backward transfer; transfer is also concept-dependent (low-level color/shape concepts fail zero-shot transfer in both directions, while structural concepts transfer one-way from understanding to generation).
- **Pinpoints the architectural loci of synergy vs. competition**: Through a Transformer-block decomposition study (dense / split_ffn / split_ffn_attn / split_ffn_norm / split_all), they show that shared attention and normalization drive cross-modal synergy while decoupled FFNs isolate capacity competition — and that this pattern generalizes across four distinct vision tokenizers (RAE, Raw Pixels, CLIP+VAE, AR UniTok).
- **Establishes the necessity of early unification and the "vision laziness" phenomenon**: Delaying visual integration degrades nearly all visual capabilities, and joint training dominates all six sequential orderings (even with 12.5% replay buffers). Four independent mechanistic probes (training/inference FFN activation L2, image-wrapper embedding norms, attention fraction on image tokens) show the vision pathway becomes progressively "quieter" the longer the language warm-up.
- **Derives and validates efficient asymmetric pretraining recipes**: A grid search over data mixtures yields the L70/U25/G5 recipe, validated at scale via single-variable controlled comparisons on 13.5B MoE models trained on 2T tokens, showing strong generation with only 5% of the generative compute budget.

## Methodology

The paper conducts a systematic, bottom-up empirical study of unified multimodal pretraining under the **Transfusion** framework (discrete next-token prediction for text + continuous flow matching for image generation in a single decoder-only Llama-3-style Transformer).

**Default controlled-experiment backbone**: a 1.5B model (2.3B params with modality-split FFNs), 16 layers, hidden dim 2048, GQA (32 query / 8 KV heads), SwiGLU, RoPE, pre-RMSNorm, QK-norm, 4096 context, bf16 under FSDP-2. Training uses AdamW with cosine decay and 8000-step warmup; the diffusion flow-matching loss is up-weighted 3x relative to text CE; x-prediction with on-the-fly velocity conversion; logit-normal timestep sampling; 25-step Euler sampling with CFG=5.0 at decode.

**Four visual tokenization configurations** are studied to test generality: (1) RAE (default) — frozen SigLIP-2 ViT-400m/14 producing 256 semantic tokens with a Representation Autoencoder decoder; (2) Raw Pixels — patchify via a 14x14 conv; (3) CLIP+VAE — SigLIP-2 for understanding at 224x224, SD3 VAE latents for generation at 256x256; (4) AR (UniTok) — residual-quantized discrete codes predicted autoregressively to test behavior beyond diffusion.

**Data**: DCLM for language; ~350M Shutterstock image-text pairs (SSTK) for vision; a procedurally generated extended-CLEVR corpus (~1M scenes across color/shape/relation/size/count axes) for the controlled concept-transfer study where specific concepts are ablated from one modality stream via regex filters.

**Evaluation**: language on 11 benchmarks (ARC-E/C, BoolQ, CoQA, HellaSwag, OpenBookQA, PIQA, SIQA, WinoGrande, NQ, TriviaQA) plus DCLM/C4 perplexity; visual understanding on 16 VQA benchmarks grouped into General / Knowledge / OCR&Chart / Vision-Centric, after one epoch of Cambrian-7M SFT; visual generation via GenEval, DPG-Bench, CLIP similarity across short/medium/long prompt buckets, and held-out diffusion loss (FID on 50k images at scale).

**Scaling validation**: 13.5B MoE models (1.5B active, 256 experts, top-16 routing, 2 modality-specific experts fixed) trained on 2T tokens, compared via single-variable ablations against a Balanced Recipe (L50/U25/G25), a Dense baseline (3.5B dense), and a Late-Fusion baseline (vision introduced after 60% of training).

## Results & Highlights

- **Asymmetric recipe (L70/U25/G5) beats balanced recipe at 2T-token / 13.5B-MoE scale** (Table 2): language accuracy 54.31% vs 52.86%, visual-understanding average 43.08% vs 41.42%, GenEval 0.482 vs 0.467, DPG 0.689 vs 0.676 — all while using 5x fewer generation tokens; FID on 50k images stays competitive at 5.234 vs 5.131.
- **MoE (shared attention + decoupled FFNs) outperforms a 3.5B Dense model** under identical data and unification settings (Table 2): language accuracy 52.86% vs 52.03%, visual-understanding average 41.42% vs 40.49%, generation diffusion loss 0.261 vs 0.266 — achieved with only 1.5B active parameters.
- **Early unification beats late-fusion** at scale (Table 2): language accuracy 52.86% vs 51.78%, visual-understanding average 41.42% vs 40.66%, generation diffusion loss 0.261 vs 0.269, with total vision token volume held identical.
- **Architecture ablation localizes synergy/competition** (Figure 9): the fully-shared dense block degrades both modalities vs unimodal baselines (language PPL +0.272, vision loss +0.054), while split_ffn (decoupled FFNs only, shared attention+norm) is the synergy-maximizing configuration (language PPL -0.211, vision loss -0.017); decoupling attention (split_ffn_attn) shrinks the gain to -0.045/-0.006, and split_all collapses to baseline.
- **CLEVR concept-transfer is strictly concept-dependent** (Figure 5): low-level color/shape concepts fail zero-shot transfer in both directions (collapse to zero-exposure control), while structural concepts (relation/size/count) transfer one-way from understanding to generation (e.g., generation accuracy on count 0.45 with understanding prior vs 0.03 control), but not vice versa.
- **Generation leaves reusable latent priors for understanding** (Figure 6): models that learned color/shape only via generation recover understanding far faster on fine-tuning, with Δ mean accuracy from +0.133 to +0.273; the reverse direction gives essentially no prior (Δ ≈ 0 for colors).
- **Task complexity flips synergy into competition** (Figure 8): pairing DCLM with simple visual targets (backgrounds, noise) improves language PPL by up to -0.211, but complex SSTK/video images degrade text PPL by +0.052 to +0.075; symmetrically, the simplest language distribution gives the largest generation-loss reduction (-0.0153 conditional / -0.0168 unconditional).
- **"Vision laziness" is mechanically measurable** (Figure 13): as language warm-up grows from 0B to 800B tokens, training-time img_ffn activation L2, inference-time per-layer img_ffn activations, image-wrapper token embedding norms, and the fraction of attention placed on image tokens all drop monotonically — the late-aligned model literally attends less to the image.
- **Joint training dominates all sequential orderings** (Figure 12): across all six modality orderings and with/without 12.5% replay buffers, simultaneous joint training wins on every visual metric (e.g., unified General VQA 47.2, DPG 0.385, GenEval 0.219 vs substantially lower bars for every sequential variant).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The paper's value is primarily in *empirical clarity and mechanism isolation* rather than a new architecture. Several individual findings echo prior work (asymmetric language-to-vision priors in Han et al. 2026b / Web-SSL; MoE with modality-specific experts in Show-o/Janus/TML; early-fusion in Gemini/Llama4). The genuinely novel contributions are (a) the CLEVR concept-ablation methodology that turns correlational claims into causal ones, (b) the four-axis mechanistic decomposition of "vision laziness," and (c) the clean localization of synergy to shared attention/norm and competition to FFNs.
- **Rigor**: Strong. Single-variable controls, identical compute/data budgets across compared runs, fixed seed=0, temperature=0 decoding, four tokenizer families tested for generality, and both synthetic (CLEVR) and real (SSTK/DCLM) evidence triangulated. The use of a 0-loss-weight counterpart run to validate the unimodal baseline is a nice touch.
- **Reproducibility**: Mixed. The controlled-experiment protocol is described in enough detail (architecture, hyperparameters, data sources, evaluation suites) to be reproducible in principle, and the CLEVR extension is procedurally well-defined. However, the data is partly proprietary (Shutterstock-Image, 350M pairs) and the 2T-token 13.5B-MoE runs require resources available to very few labs; weights/code are not stated to be released (only a project page is referenced). Many figure-derived numbers (e.g., Δ PPL values) are read off bar charts rather than tabulated, introducing reading error.
- **Significance**: High for the unified-multimodal-models subfield. The L70/U25/G5 recipe and the "early unification or vision laziness" result are immediately actionable design guidance that contradicts the still-common late-fusion paradigm, and the CLEVR methodology is a reusable diagnostic tool.
- **Red flags / specific concerns**:
  - The headline "5% generation compute" claim is somewhat oversold: the asymmetric mix shows a *minor* diffusion-loss regression (0.272 vs 0.261) and the authors themselves concede "increasing the generation ratio in later stage... remains beneficial for better generative modeling" — i.e., the recipe trades raw pixel fidelity for efficiency, which the FID gap (5.234 vs 5.131) quietly confirms.
  - The Dense baseline used in the architecture ablation is a *3.5B* dense model pitted against a 13.5B-MoE with 1.5B active params — the comparison is framed as fair on active params but the total-parameter and FLOP asymmetry could favor MoE for reasons partly independent of the synergy hypothesis.
  - The Late-Fusion baseline introduces vision only after 60% of training, which is a relatively extreme late-fusion; whether more moderate late-fusion schedules (e.g., 20-30%) would erase the gap is not probed at scale, though the 1T-token timing sweep (Figure 11) does suggest a near-monotonic decline.
  - The claim that findings generalize to "extreme scales >1T params" is explicitly flagged as a hypothesis in the Limitations, not validated.
  - All vision experiments are confined to static images; the authors honestly note video/audio/3D are open questions.

## Strengths

- Exceptionally systematic and mechanistic: replaces heuristic arguments with controlled, single-variable evidence at multiple scales.
- The CLEVR concept-ablation design is a clean methodological contribution that converts "language helps vision" correlations into causal per-concept statements.
- The four-axis "vision laziness" diagnosis is a compelling, concrete mechanistic story for why late-fusion underperforms.
- Practical, immediately deployable recipe (L70/U25/G5 + MoE + early unification) with a strong efficiency story.
- Honest about limitations and about the minor fidelity/efficiency trade-off in the generation results.

## Limitations

- Confined to text + static images; video, audio, and 3D modalities are explicitly left to future work despite the paper's "omnimodal" framing in the conclusion.
- The 2T-token validation is only at 13.5B parameters; behavior at frontier (>1T param) scales is unverified, and the authors speculate (without evidence) that bidirectional knowledge flow might emerge there.
- Reliance on proprietary Shutterstock image-text data limits reproducibility for academic groups.
- The "5% generation" efficiency win comes with a measurable (if small) diffusion-loss and FID regression, and the authors recommend a later generative-midtraining stage — so the recipe is not a free lunch.
- Some quantitative claims are read from figures rather than reported in tables, and several baseline choices (3.5B dense vs 13.5B MoE; 60% late-fusion cutoff) are arguably favorable to the proposed design.
- The synthetic CLEVR concept vocabulary, while cleaner than real data, is far simpler than natural-image semantics; how cleanly the color/shape/relation/count taxonomy maps to real-world concepts is unclear.

## Relevance

This paper is highly relevant to research on unified/early-fusion multimodal foundation models and scaling laws for multimodal pretraining: it provides concrete, mechanistically grounded guidance on data mixing, parameter sharing, and training scheduling that directly informs the design of next-generation any-to-any multimodal models. The asymmetric-knowledge-flow finding and the L70/U25/G5 recipe are immediately actionable for anyone training or scaling unified understanding+generation models.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the L70/U25/G5 asymmetry still hold, or invert, once video and audio are added as additional "understanding" and "generation" objectives with their own complexity profiles?
- Would a more moderate late-fusion schedule (e.g., introducing vision at 20-30% of training rather than 60%) erase the vision-laziness gap, and does the optimal warm-up length scale with model size?
- At frontier (>1T parameter) scales, does visual generation begin to provide backward transfer to understanding/language, as the authors speculate — and can this be detected with the same four-axis vision-laziness probes?
- Can the CLEVR concept-ablation methodology be extended to natural images (e.g., via controlled synthetic scene editing) to test concept-dependent transfer outside the procedural domain?
- Is the shared-attention / decoupled-FFN conclusion stable under alternative routing strategies (e.g., soft merging, expert choice) rather than top-16 MoE with 2 fixed modality-specific experts?
- Does the "vision laziness" mechanism also explain the well-documented hallucination and "blindness" failures of current production multimodal LLMs, and can an early-unification corrective be applied via continued pretraining of existing late-fused models?
