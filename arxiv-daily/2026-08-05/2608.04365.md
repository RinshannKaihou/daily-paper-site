> **一句话总结：** 本文提出 R²esPIR 审计协议，利用 Private Information Retrieval (PIR) 把真实审计集隐藏在一个更大的候选集之中，使得恶意模型服务商无法只针对审计样本做手脚；理论证明被审计方必须改写显著更多的预测才能掩盖不公平，实测在 COMPAS/race 上把操纵所需改写数从 39 提升到 156、k=50 时检测概率从 67.89% 提升到 89.96%。

> **TL;DR:** The authors propose **R²esPIR**, an oblivious fairness-audit protocol that uses Private Information Retrieval (PIR) to hide the actual audit set *S* inside a much larger candidate set *C* that the provider must label. They prove a deceptive provider must flip at least ⌈(|d_{C,true}| − ε + γ)·N_min⌉ predictions on *C* (vs. ⌈(|d_true| − ε)·n_min⌉ for a vanilla black-box audit) to pass with high probability. On COMPAS (race) this raises the required manipulations from 39 to 156 and lifts manipulation detection at k=50 canaries from 67.89% to 89.96%, with negligible PIR overhead (≈3.6 s online for an 8 GiB database).

## 核心贡献

- **提出 R²esPIR 协议**：基于 VeriSimplePIR（一种可验证 PIR），要求服务商对一个大候选集 *C* 进行标注，而审计员通过 PIR 在其中秘密抽样真正的审计集 *S*，从而让服务商无法精确"打补丁"。
- **给出操纵难度的理论下界**（Theorem 1）：要使审计以 1−δ 的概率通过，服务商必须在候选集 *C* 上至少改写 ⌈(|d_{C,true}| − ε + √(2 ln(4/δ)/n_min))·N_min⌉ 个预测，显著高于普通黑盒审计的 ⌈(|d_true| − ε)·n_min⌉（Proposition 1）。
- **建立"操纵工作量 → 检测概率"的桥梁**：通过把 canary 验证查询混入审计集，给出可解析的检测概率公式 P(detect) = 1 − (1 − q·m/n)^k，把隐藏的不公平程度直接转成被发现概率。
- **给出候选集规模的设计准则**：通过 Hoeffding/Serfling 浓度不等式（Theorem 2）刻画 *S* 大小、组内平衡度与置信参数 δ 之间的关系，证明该协议不会带来 prohibitive 计算开销。
- **跨表格与文本数据集的实证验证**：在 CCD、COMPAS、HateDay 三个数据集上模拟恶意操纵，证明 R²esPIR 系统性地把所需改写次数和检测概率提升约一倍。

## Key Contributions

- **R²esPIR protocol**: a three-step oblivious audit (agree on candidate set *C* → provider labels *C* into database *D* → auditor retrieves *h(S)* via PIR) that prevents the provider from tailoring responses to the known audit set, while requiring **no change to the model, its training, or its inference pipeline**.
- **Provably higher manipulation cost** (Theorem 1): under uniform sampling of *S* from *C*, hiding unfairness with probability ≥ 1−δ requires at least ⌈(|d_{C,true}| − ε + √(2 ln(4/δ)/n_min))·N_min⌉ flips on *C*, replacing the vanilla audit's smaller ⌈(|d_true| − ε)·n_min⌉ bound — a double improvement (stricter effective target due to the sampling-uncertainty term γ, and a much larger scale N_min ≫ n_min).
- **Canary-based detection bridge**: an analytical formula linking the number of manipulated responses *m* to detection probability via *k* externally labeled canary queries, *P(detect) = 1 − (1 − q·m/n)^k*.
- **Sufficient-size characterization** (Theorem 2): a finite-population Hoeffding/Serfling argument shows |d_C| ≤ ε − √(2 ln(4/δ)/n_min) suffices to pass with probability ≥ 1−δ, giving concrete guidance on candidate-set and audit-set sizing.
- **Metric-agnostic framing**: the PIR layer treats the fairness computation as a modular black box; the authors show the same flip-count lower bounds extend to equal opportunity, equalized odds, multi-class sensitive attributes, and (in expectation) regression.

## Methodology

The paper targets the **black-box demographic-parity audit game**. Given a binary classifier *h* and protected attribute *A*, the auditor estimates the parity gap d_S = p_{1,S} − p_{0,S} on an audit set *S* and passes the provider iff |d_S| ≤ ε. **Proposition 1** shows this game is brittle: a malicious provider that knows *S* needs only ⌈(|d_true| − ε)·n_min⌉ targeted flips (e.g., just 2 flips to hide a 0.01 gap on a balanced n=400 set).

**R²esPIR** closes this leak in three steps (Algorithm 2):
1. Provider and auditor jointly agree on a large, representative candidate set *C*.
2. Provider labels *all* of *C* into a database *D = {(x, h(x)) : x ∈ C}* and commits to it via the **VeriSimplePIR** scheme (de Castro & Lee 2024), which adds a verifiability proof π on top of SimplePIR so the provider cannot silently change labels during the audit.
3. Auditor uniformly samples *S ⊂ C* and retrieves *h(S)* through the PIR Query/Answer/Recovery protocol — the provider learns nothing about which indices were queried. A small number *k* of canary queries with known external labels are inserted to detect any tampering.

Theoretical analysis combines (i) the per-flip sensitivity of the parity gap (1/N_min on *C* vs. 1/n_min on *S*), and (ii) finite-population Hoeffding/Serfling concentration to derive the γ term, yielding the closed-form lower bound of Theorem 1. Experiments use a histogram gradient-boosted tree baseline (`skrub`) with random-search + 5-fold CV hyperparameter tuning on three datasets (CCD, COMPAS, HateDay with distiluse sentence embeddings), splitting 70/30 stratified by the sensitive attribute. For each dataset and protected attribute the authors set ε = |d_{C,true}|/2 (i.e., hiding half the unfairness), compute required flips from Proposition 1 / Theorem 1, and plug them into Eq. 3 with *k ∈ {5,10,20,50}* canaries.

## Results & Highlights

- **COMPAS / race (the strongest configuration)**: hiding half of the initial parity gap requires **39 output flips in a vanilla black-box audit vs. 156 under R²esPIR**; manipulation detection probability with *k=50* canaries jumps from **67.89% to 89.96%** (and from 36.51% → 60.12% at *k=20*).
- **COMPAS / gender**: required flips rise from **20 → 79**, and detection at *k=50* improves from **43.97% to 68.36%**.
- **CCD / gender**: flips rise from **38 → 152**, with detection at *k=50* going from **20.20% to 36.39%**; across the four CCD attributes the R²esPIR detection probability is consistently ~2× the vanilla one (e.g., 14.81% → 27.24% for education at *k=50*).
- **Detection-probability scaling**: across all 7 dataset/attribute settings R²esPIR "systematically" multiplies detection probability by **roughly a factor of two** at fixed *n=|S|*, because the required manipulated fraction *m/N* is much larger than *m/n*.
- **PIR overhead is negligible**: VeriSimplePIR online phase completes in **1.09 ms for a 128 KiB database** and **3.64 s for an 8 GiB database**, with **2.435 MiB total bandwidth** at the largest size — practical for realistic audit-set scales.
- **Limiting regime confirmed empirically**: on the highly imbalanced HateDay / language setting (minority group only 8.3% of *C*), even *k=50* canaries give only ~1.5%/2.8% detection for vanilla/R²esPIR — confirming the theoretical prediction that min(N₀,N₁) dominates detectability.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Genuine and well-scoped. The core trick — using PIR to hide *S* inside a provider-labeled *C* — is simple and the authors are honest that it is "necessary but not always sufficient." The main novelty over the closest prior (Garcia Bourrée et al. 2025, which needs the auditor to hold a private prior) is replacing that assumption with cryptographic query hiding.
- **Rigor**: Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 are cleanly proven from Hoeffding/Serfling; the vanilla bound (Proposition 1) is tight by construction. However the lower bound in Theorem 1 is a *sufficient* (best-effort) condition — the paper itself acknowledges it "should be interpreted as an analytical characterization of manipulation difficulty, rather than as an operational recipe" because it depends on quantities unknown to the auditor (true d_{C,true}, realized n_min).
- **Reproducibility**: Strong. Public code, public datasets (CCD, COMPAS, HateDay), specified model class (`skrub` histogram GBT), and explicit hyperparameter protocol (random search + 5-fold CV). The only non-standard component is the VeriSimplePIR instantiation, but that is itself open-source.
- **Significance**: Practically relevant given the motivating Meta Ad Library incident, but real-world deployment is **not demonstrated** — all results are simulated manipulations on a fixed dataset split, with a benign baseline model. There is no experiment against an actual adaptive adversary that tries to game the candidate set, nor any treatment of how *C* is agreed upon when the provider controls internal data.
- **Red flags / missing ablations**: (i) No experiment varies the candidate-set size |C| at fixed |S| to empirically validate the N_min scaling claim — only one |C| per dataset is used. (ii) The worst-case detection (HateDay, ~1% at k=50) shows the protocol **fails on imbalanced groups**, yet the paper does not benchmark stratified sampling of *S* (its own recommended fix from §6.2) to show how much that recovers. (iii) The threat model assumes the provider cannot re-identify audit queries via side channels (timing, IP, request volume) — realistic PIR deployments break this, but it is not discussed. (iv) All experiments hide *half* the unfairness (ε = |d_{C,true}|/2); no sensitivity analysis over ε is provided, even though the bound has a non-trivial γ/ε interaction noted in §5.

## Strengths

- Clean, modular protocol built on an existing verifiable PIR (VeriSimplePIR), so the cryptographic trust assumption is well-understood and auditable.
- Model- and metric-agnostic by construction; clean extension to equal opportunity is shown in Appendix C.
- Negligible overhead: no retraining, no model modification, sub-second-to-few-seconds PIR cost even for GiB-scale databases.
- Honest framing of limitations — explicitly states PIR "hides the audit set but does not prevent all forms of manipulation," and clearly delineates when the protocol degrades (imbalanced groups, small n_min, ε ≈ 0).
- Public code and datasets, making the empirical claims independently checkable.

## Limitations

- **Imbalanced groups break detectability**: when the minority group is a small fraction of *C* (e.g., HateDay's 8.3%), even *k=50* canaries give <3% detection probability. The recommended mitigation (stratified sampling of *S*) is proposed but not empirically validated.
- **Single candidate-set size per dataset**: the central N_min ≫ n_min scaling claim is supported theoretically but never stress-tested empirically by varying |C|.
- **No adaptive adversary**: experiments simulate the *minimum*-flip manipulation prescribed by Theorem 1, not a smarter adversary that exploits candidate-set knowledge or canary detection.
- **Fixed ε = |d_{C,true}|/2** across all settings; the regime where ε < γ (noted as problematic in §5) is acknowledged but not charted.
- **Side-channel leakage unaddressed**: PIR hides query *indices* but a real provider could infer audit activity from request volume, timing, or IP reputation.
- **Candidate-set agreement is hand-waved**: the protocol assumes both parties can agree on a representative *C*, but does not specify a negotiation mechanism when the provider controls the relevant data.
- **Beyond-demographic-parity extensions** (separation/sufficiency, regression, multi-class) are argued in §6.1 but not empirically demonstrated.

## Relevance

这项工作直接服务于可信机器学习与算法治理研究方向：它把密码学（PIR）和公平性审计结合起来，提供一个可证明提高操纵难度的协议，对关注 ML 系统审计鲁棒性、抗 gaming 评估、以及 EU AI Act / DSA 等监管场景下如何抵御"被审计方适应性行为"的研究者具有较高参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does detection probability change when the auditor samples *S* via **stratified** (rather than uniform) sampling to correct for severe group imbalance — does this recover detectability on HateDay-like settings?
- Can the protocol defend against an **adaptive adversary** that uses query-metadata side channels (timing, volume, IP) to re-identify likely audit queries despite PIR?
- What is the empirical scaling of required flips and detection probability as |C| grows at fixed |S|, and does it match the N_min prediction of Theorem 1?
- Can R²esPIR be combined with **iterative candidate-set release** (suggested in §6.2) without leaking information that breaks the non-adaptivity guarantee?
- How do the guarantees degrade when extending to **equalized odds / calibration** metrics where a single flip can affect multiple rates simultaneously?
- Is there a principled mechanism for the auditor and provider to **negotiate C** when the provider holds most of the relevant operational data?
