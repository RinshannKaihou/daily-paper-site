> **一句话总结：** 本文针对 Muon 优化器只处理梯度各向异性、忽略损失曲面曲率各向异性的缺陷，提出 MALT——通过左右两侧的对角预条件子（行/列梯度平方范数 EMA 的 −1/8 次幂）将动量矩阵映射到近似各向同性的预条件空间再做 Newton–Schulz 正交化，再配合 norm grafting 控制更新幅度；其噪声自适应变体 MALTER 在 GPT-2 Small/Medium/Large 上将验证损失比 Muon 进一步降低 0.0241 / 0.0277 / 0.0164，与 Muon 相对 AdamW 的提升幅度相当，且显存与单步 wall-clock 几乎不变（H100 上分别只多 1.5 MB 与约 29 ms）。

> **TL;DR:** MALT is a curvature-aware Muon variant that augments Newton–Schulz orthogonalization with two-sided diagonal preconditioners built from EMA row- and column-wise squared gradient norms (raised to −1/8, à la Shampoo/SOAP), so orthogonalization happens in an approximately curvature-isotropic space; its noise-adaptive sibling MALTER adds a NAMO-style scalar stepsize in that preconditioned space. On GPT-2 Small/Medium/Large pretraining over OpenWebText, MALTER beats Muon by 0.0241 / 0.0277 / 0.0164 in final validation loss while matching Muon's optimizer-state memory (1554.88 MB vs 1553.38 MB on GPT-2 Medium) and per-iteration wall-clock time (2666.22 ms vs 2637.62 ms), and MALTER-over-Muon gain is comparable to Muon-over-AdamW gain.

## 核心贡献

- **从局部二次近似的视角形式化"预条件 Muon"。** 作者用正定曲率代理 B_t 把损失在 X_t 处的二次模型写成 ⟨∇f, Δ⟩ + (η/2)⟨Δ, B_t(Δ)⟩，进而在 B_t^{1/2} 诱导的预条件空间里把曲率项变成各向同性；在该空间对预条件梯度做正交化后再用 B_t^{−1/2} 映射回原空间，从而让"曲率校正"与"梯度谱校正"在同一框架下互补——这是 MALT 与同期 Mousse / FISMO / NewtonMuon 在动机上的核心区分点。
- **提出极轻量的双侧对角预条件子。** 对 m×n 矩阵参数，仅额外维护两个向量 l_t ∈ R^m、r_t ∈ R^n（行/列梯度平方范数的 EMA），用 L_t = Diag(l_t+ϵ)^{−1/8}、R_t = Diag(r_t+ϵ)^{−1/8} 作左右预条件，状态复杂度仅 O(m+n)，相比 Shampoo/SOAP/Mousse 的 O(m²+n²) 显著降低，且每步都更新（Mousse 是周期性更新）。
- **给出随机非凸收敛保证。** Theorem 1 在 L-光滑、有界方差、有限下界假设下证明 MALT 的 min Ε[‖L_t∇f(W)R_t‖_F^{1/2}] 收敛率为 O(δ⁻⁴)，与现有 Muon 分析的 δ⁻⁴ 精度依赖一致；但同时显式承认收敛指标比标准 Muon 弱、且对维度 n 的多项式依赖更差。
- **提出噪声自适应变体 MALTER。** 在预条件空间内引入一个标量 ν_t = μ₂ν_{t−1} + (1−μ₂)‖L_t G_t R_t‖_F²，用 α_t ∝ ‖M_t^f‖_F / √(ν_t+ϵ) 类似 NAMO 的信噪比缩放做步长自适应；相比 MALT 仅多 1 个标量状态，却带来比 MALT 更大的提升。
- **GPT-2 Small/Medium/Large 系统评测并量化开销。** 与 Muon 共享学习率网格做公平 LR 扫描；给出每步 wall-clock 时间与 optimizer-state 显存的直接对比（Table 4/5），证明"几乎零开销"。

## Key Contributions

- **Formulates Muon in a preconditioned space from a local quadratic surrogate.** A positive-definite curvature surrogate B_t is used so the quadratic term becomes isotropic under B_t^{1/2}; orthogonalizing B_t^{−1/2}(∇f(X_t)) in that space and mapping back yields Δ_t = B_t^{−1/2}(Orth(B_t^{−1/2}(∇f(X_t)))), jointly attacking curvature anisotropy (preconditioning) and gradient/singular-spectrum anisotropy (orthogonalization).
- **Lightweight two-sided diagonal preconditioner.** Maintains EMAs of row- and column-wise squared gradient norms to build L_t = Diag(l_t+ϵ)^{−1/8}, R_t = Diag(r_t+ϵ)^{−1/8} and applies them as B_t(G) = L_t G R_t. The −1/8 exponent is borrowed from Shampoo/SOAP, but dense m×m and n×n factors are replaced by diagonals, giving O(m+n) extra memory per matrix parameter and step-wise updates without eigendecomposition.
- **Norm grafting for scale stability.** Because the eighth-root preconditioners can blow up rows/columns with tiny norms, the direction is normalized to unit Frobenius norm and the magnitude is grafted from ‖O_t‖_F (the orthogonalized update in preconditioned space), decoupling direction from magnitude.
- **MALTER: noise-adaptive scalar stepsize in preconditioned space.** Adds a single scalar ν_t tracking EMA of ‖L_t G_t R_t‖_F² and forms α_t ≈ (1−μ₂^t)‖M_t^f‖_F / (√(1−μ₁^t)(ν_t+ϵ)) as a signal-to-noise rescaler of the grafted step, mirroring NAMO but in the curvature-aware preconditioned space.
- **Stochastic non-convex convergence proof** (Theorem 1) with O(δ⁻⁴) accuracy dependence matching existing Muon analyses, plus an explicit comparison to Mousse, FISMO, NewtonMuon, SOAP, Adafactor, and a proof that "SOAP-basis + Muon" collapses to vanilla Muon via polar-factor equivariance.
- **Empirical scaling study on GPT-2 124M / 355M / 774M** with shared LR grids (so MALT/Muon/AdamW share the same {0.0005 … 0.0032} sweep), plus dedicated wall-clock and optimizer-state measurements on a single H100.

## Methodology

MALT starts from the observation (Figure 1, on a matrix quadratic regression min_X ½‖AXB−C‖_F²) that as the Hessian condition number grows, Muon's convergence under a fixed WSD schedule slows dramatically; with two-sided diagonal preconditioning the convergence becomes near-condition-number-independent.

**Preconditioned-space derivation (Section 3.2).** Given a tractable positive-definite curvature surrogate B_t, the local quadratic model f(X_t+ηΔ) ≈ f(X_t) + η⟨∇f(X_t), Δ⟩ + (η²/2)⟨Δ, B_t(Δ)⟩ is isotropic in the B_t^{1/2}-induced space. Orthogonalizing the preconditioned gradient there and mapping back gives the update

Δ_t = B_t^{−1/2}( Orth( B_t^{−1/2}(∇f(X_t)) ) ).

With momentum, this becomes Algorithm 1: M̃_t = L_t M_t R_t → O_t = Orth(M̃_t) → D_t = L_t O_t R_t → W_t = W_{t−1} − η · (‖O_t‖_F / (‖D_t‖_F + ϵ)) · D_t (norm grafting).

**Diagonal preconditioner (Section 3.3).** B_t(G) = L_t G R_t with L_t = Diag(l_t+ϵ)^{−1/8}, R_t = Diag(r_t+ϵ)^{−1/8}, where (l_t)_i and (r_t)_j are EMAs of row and column squared Frobenius norms of G_t. These track the diagonals of G_t G_t^⊤ and G_t^⊤ G_t over time — a lightweight Shampoo-style left/right structure but with O(m+n) state and per-step updates. The paper motivates the design from four angles: curvature-aware adaptive scaling, matrix-structure awareness (each entry inherits one row factor and one column factor rather than a per-element scalar as in Adam), memory efficiency, and a structured approximation to the empirically near-block-diagonal Transformer Hessian.

**MALTER (Section 3.5, Algorithm 2).** Adds a scalar ν_t = μ₂ν_{t−1} + (1−μ₂)‖L_t G_t R_t‖_F² and a stepsize α_t = η · (1−μ₂^t)‖M̃_t‖_F / (√(1−μ₁^t)(ν_t+ϵ)), which approximates the signal-to-noise ratio of the stochastic preconditioned gradient, à la NAMO but inside MALT's preconditioned space.

**Relations to prior optimizers (Section 3.4).** Differs from Shampoo by replacing dense P_t, Q_t with diagonals; from SOAP by observing (via polar-factor equivariance under orthogonal transforms) that rotating into the Shampoo eigenbasis before Muon-orthogonalization gives back vanilla Muon, so SOAP+Muon is uninteresting while Adam-in-SOAP-basis is not; differs from concurrent Mousse (dense Shampoo factors, periodic eigendecomposition) and FISMO (Fisher with Kronecker factors, requires matrix inverse/inverse-roots) by being diagonal and updated every step; differs from MuonEq (which equilibrates momentum for Newton–Schulz numerical accuracy but does not target curvature anisotropy).

**Convergence (Section 4).** Under lower-boundedness, L-smoothness, and bounded variance σ², Theorem 1 bounds min_t Ε[‖L_t∇f(W_{t−1})R_t‖_F^{1/2}] = Õ(1/√T + σ/√(B T)) giving O(n^{7/2} L Δ₀,₁ σ² / B · δ⁻⁴) sample complexity to reach δ-accuracy on the preconditioned-gradient stationarity measure. The authors explicitly note this metric is weaker than the standard Euclidean gradient-norm bound and that the polynomial dependence on n is worse than existing Muon analyses.

## Results & Highlights

- **GPT-2 Small (124M), 50K steps, OpenWebText — final validation loss:** MALT 3.0358 vs Muon 3.0472 (Δ = −0.0114), MALTER 3.0231 vs Muon 3.0472 (Δ = −0.0241); Muon itself beats AdamW (3.0540) by −0.0068. So MALTER-over-Muon ≈ 3.5× the Muon-over-AdamW gain at this scale.
- **GPT-2 Medium (355M), 10K steps — final validation loss:** MALT 2.9611 vs Muon 2.9719 (Δ = −0.0108), MALTER 2.9442 vs Muon 2.9719 (Δ = −0.0277); Muon beats AdamW (2.9971) by −0.0252 — i.e. MALTER's marginal gain over Muon exceeds Muon's marginal gain over AdamW.
- **GPT-2 Large (774M), 20K steps — final validation loss:** MALT 2.7723 vs Muon 2.7848 (Δ = −0.0125), MALTER 2.7684 vs Muon 2.7848 (Δ = −0.0164). Note AdamW diverges in the last ~500 steps and lands at 3.2705, making the Muon-vs-AdamW gap (−0.4857) not directly comparable at this scale.
- **Optimizer-state memory on GPT-2 Medium (Table 5):** AdamW 2705.38 MB, Muon 1553.38 MB, MALT 1554.88 MB, MALTER 1554.88 MB — MALT/MALTER add only ~1.5 MB over Muon. Theoretical state complexity (Table 4): AdamW 2mn, Muon mn, MALT mn+m+n, MALTER mn+m+n+1.
- **Per-iteration wall-clock on a single H100 (Table 5):** AdamW 2605.16 ms, Muon 2637.62 ms, MALT 2652.55 ms, MALTER 2666.22 ms — MALTER costs ~28.6 ms (~1.1%) more per step than Muon.
- **Diagonal preconditioners are non-trivial (Figure 6).** Tracked over 3500 steps on GPT-2 Medium, the condition numbers of diag(l_t) and diag(r_t) for attention (c_attn, c_proj) and MLP (c_fc, c_proj) matrices across Layers 0/5/10 are consistently ≫ 1 (frequently 10²–10⁸), confirming that row/column anisotropy is captured rather than collapsed to a scalar.
- **LR-sweep robustness (Figure 5, 10K-step runs).** On each LR grid point of GPT-2 Small/Medium/Large, MALT beats Muon and AdamW, and MALTER is best across all three scales — the gain is not an artifact of a lucky LR.
- **Toy ill-conditioning demo (Figure 1).** On min_X ½‖AXB−C‖_F² under WSD scheduling, Muon's convergence slows sharply as the Hessian condition number grows; preconditioned Muon is essentially insensitive to it.

## Authors & Credibility

- **Tongle Wu and Huanyu Dong** (equal contribution), with **Ying Sun** at Penn State EECS and **Ziye Ma** at City University of Hong Kong. Ying Sun's group has a strong track record on optimization and Hessian-structure analysis (e.g. the cited "Why Transformers Need Adam: A Hessian Perspective" and Adam-mini work, refs [76, 77]), giving the curvature-anisotropy framing real credibility.
- The paper is unusually careful about positioning: it explicitly lists concurrent curvature-aware Muon variants (FISMO, Mousse, NewtonMuon) and states the three concrete advantages of MALT over them (memory O(m+n) vs O(m²+n²); no inverse/inverse-roots or eigendecompositions; per-step rather than periodic updates).
- The convergence proof is included in full (Appendix A) and the authors openly disclose its weaknesses rather than overselling.
- Empirical protocol is reasonably fair: shared LR grid for AdamW/Muon/MALT, a separate (larger-LR) grid for MALTER, batch 480 × block 1024 (= 491 520 tokens/step), best-LR-by-10K-validation-loss then continued training, hardware disclosed (4× H100 + 2× PRO6000), wall-clock and memory measured on a single H100.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** Genuine but incremental: the preconditioned-space formulation is a clean theoretical lens, but the actual preconditioner (row/column EMA squared norms to the −1/8 power, à la Shampoo) is a direct diagonalization of well-known Shampoo/SOAP factors; the noise-adaptive step in MALTER is essentially NAMO ported into the preconditioned space. The most novel theoretical nugget — that "SOAP-basis + Muon" collapses to vanilla Muon via polar-factor equivariance (Eq. 14–16) — is interesting but is presented as a negative observation rather than a new algorithm.
- **Rigor.** Theory and experiments are internally consistent, but the convergence guarantee is openly admitted to be weaker than existing Muon bounds: the stationarity measure is Ε[‖L_t∇f(W)R_t‖_F^{1/2}] rather than the Euclidean gradient norm, and the sample complexity carries a worse polynomial in dimension n (O(n^{7/2})). The proof relies on stochastic L_t, R_t whose condition numbers are only empirically bounded (Figure 6) — there is no worst-case bound on ‖L_t‖, ‖R_t‖ beyond the damping floor.
- **Reproducibility.** Reasonably good: GPT-2 setup is the canonical NanoGPT + OpenWebText, batch/sequence/eval cadence (every 500 steps over 200 mini-batches) and full LR grids (Table 2) are reported, and Algorithm 1/2 are unambiguous. Missing pieces: code release is not mentioned; damping ϵ, μ₁, μ₂ values, and the exact Newton–Schulz iteration count are referenced to "Table 6" / appendix hyperparameters but the most load-bearing ϵ is not stated in the main text; the GPT-2 Large "AdamW diverged in the last 500 steps" footnote makes the headline Large-scale AdamW gap (−0.4857) not directly comparable and slightly overstates the optimizer ranking at that scale.
- **Significance.** Real but modest in absolute terms. Validation-loss deltas of 0.01–0.03 nats over Muon at 10K–50K steps are small; the most persuasive evidence is the consistency across three scales and across the whole LR sweep, plus the negligible cost (1.5 MB, ~1%). The "MALTER-over-Muon ≈ Muon-over-AdamW" framing is the strongest claim and is only loosely quantified.
- **Red flags / missing ablations.** (1) No baseline beyond Muon and AdamW — concurrent curvature-aware Muon variants (Mousse, Foisso, NewtonMuon), Shampoo, SOAP, NAMO, AdaMuon, MuonEq are all discussed but none are run, so the headline "MALTER is best overall" is only against two baselines. (2) No downstream evaluation (zero-shot perplexity, HellaSwag, ARC, etc.) — only pretraining validation loss. (3) No scaling beyond 774M, despite the framing being about "large language model pretraining"; Muon's reputation rests on billion-plus-param results. (4) No isolation of the −1/8 exponent, the damping ϵ, or the grafting-target choice (‖O_t‖_F vs Adam-RMS) as ablations. (5) The toy Figure 1 uses unspecified A, B, C matrices (deferred to Appendix B) to dramatize the condition-number effect.

## Strengths

- Almost-zero-overhead curvature awareness: O(m+n) extra state and ~1% wall-clock, both measured rather than asserted.
- Clean, teachable derivation of "preconditioned Muon" from a local quadratic model; the polar-equivariance observation that kills the "SOAP+Muon" strawman is a nice clarifying result.
- Theoretical convergence guarantee, included in full, with honest disclosure of where it is weaker than prior Muon analyses.
- MALTER is a principled, near-free upgrade: only one extra scalar per matrix and it consistently beats MALT, supporting the authors' thesis that direction and noise-adaptive scale are complementary.
- Fair empirical protocol: shared LR grids, best-LR selection via 10K-step sweeps, separate larger grid for MALTER (whose optimal LR is ~10× the others, an important practical detail).

## Limitations

- Baseline set is thin: only AdamW and Muon are actually run; concurrent curvature-aware Muon variants (Mousse, FISMO, NewtonMuon) and other matrix-aware optimizers (Shampoo, SOAP, NAMO, AdaMuon, MuonEq) are only discussed. The "MALTER achieves the best overall performance" claim is therefore narrower than it sounds.
- Scale ceiling is 774M; no billion-parameter experiments despite the LLM-pretraining framing, and no downstream-task evaluation beyond pretraining validation loss.
- The convergence bound is explicitly weaker than the standard Muon gradient-norm bound (uses Ε[‖L_t∇f(W)R_t‖_F^{1/2}]) and has worse polynomial dependence on n; worst-case bounds on ‖L_t‖, ‖R_t‖ are not provided.
- The GPT-2 Large AdamW run diverges in the final 500 steps, inflating the AdamW comparison at that scale and making the −0.4857 number not a clean like-for-like.
- Key hyperparameters (damping ϵ, momentum coefficients, Newton–Schulz iteration count) are deferred to appendix Table 6 and not pinned down in the main text.
- No ablations isolating the contribution of (a) the −1/8 exponent vs other powers, (b) norm grafting vs Adam-style RMS grafting, (c) one-sided vs two-sided preconditioning, or (d) MALT vs MALTER-without-preconditioning (i.e., is the gain from preconditioning or from NAMO-style noise adaptation alone?).
- Code release is not mentioned.

## Relevance

对关注大模型训练优化器（cs.LG / stat.ML）的读者高度相关：MALT/MALTER 是 Muon 路线上"以最低代价引入曲率信息"的代表性方案，对研究二阶/预条件方法、Hessian 结构、以及 LLM 预训练效率的工程实践都有直接借鉴价值；其行/列对角预条件的设计也对 Shampoo/SOAP 系列想做"轻量化"的后续工作有启发。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does MALTER compare head-to-head against the concurrent curvature-aware Muon variants it discusses but does not run — Mousse, FISMO, NewtonMuon — at matched compute and matched LR-search budget, especially above 1B parameters where dense Shampoo/Fisher factors become expensive?
- The −1/8 exponent is inherited from Shampoo without ablation. What is the empirical sensitivity of MALT to this exponent (e.g., −1/4, −1/2), and is there a principled way to choose it from the row/column gradient-norm statistics?
- The convergence proof's stationarity measure Ε[‖L_t∇f(W)R_t‖_F^{1/2}] is weaker than the Euclidean gradient norm. Can a refined analysis recover a standard gradient-norm bound, or at least bound ‖L_t‖, ‖R_t‖ in the worst case without relying on empirical condition numbers?
- MALTER's gain over MALT is large; how much of it comes from the diagonal preconditioning vs from NAMO-style noise adaptation alone? An ablation running "NAMO on vanilla Muon" vs "MALTER" would isolate the preconditioner's marginal contribution.
- Do the MALT/MALTER gains on validation loss translate to downstream tasks (HellaSwag, ARC, LAMBADA, zero-shot perplexity on held-out corpora), or only to the pretraining-loss curve?
- The GPT-2 Large AdamW run diverges near the end. Is this an LR-selection artifact, and does it change the optimizer ranking if AdamW is given a more conservative LR or a different schedule at 774M?
