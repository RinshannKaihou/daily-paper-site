> **一句话总结：** 本文构建了一个"数学模型 + LLM"的可审计混合架构，将 Dixon–Coles 动态得分模型作为概率先验，由 LLM 仅做可冻结、可校验的逐进球路径模拟与候选重排（V4），在 2025–26 英超前 150 场回放中 Top-1/Top-3 精确比分命中率达 14.7%/30.7%（相比 V1 的 10.0%/26.7% 有描述性提升），但配对显著性检验不显著，且未能超越 V1 原生 1X2 概率决策，核心贡献是方法学而非精度突破。

> **TL;DR:** An independent researcher presents an auditable hybrid forecasting system in which a frozen Dixon–Coles score-driven prior owns probability and candidate generation while a constrained LLM contributes football-aware goal-by-goal path simulation (V4). On the first 150 fixtures of the 2025–26 EPL, V4 lifts exact-score Top-1 from V1's 10.0% to 14.7% and Top-3 from 26.7% to 30.7%, but paired McNemar tests are non-significant for the exact-score metrics and V1's native 1X2 argmax (53.3%) still beats V4's score-derived 1X2 (50.0%); the contribution is a disciplined methodology with honest negative findings, not a SOTA claim.

## 核心贡献

- **可审计的混合架构**：将概率状态估计（V1）、证据打包（harness）、LLM 推理（V3/V4）与确定性校验（validator）四者职责严格分离，LLM 不得修改 λ 或编造任意比分，只能对冻结的候选池排序。
- **四版清晰可追溯的设计迭代**：V1 纯数学 Dixon–Coles/GAS；V2 "数学+LLM+数学"夹层标量残差；V3 改为显式三条逐进球路径模拟；V4 加入共享首破（first-breakthrough）判定、时间感知停止、进球后级联（cascade）评估与确定性尾部锚点。
- **按时间序冻结的回放协议**：在出分前冻结 prompt、响应、哈希与产物，逐场归档，避免目标结果字段泄漏进 prompt；该协议是"时间输入隔离"而非对模型参数记忆的完全证明。
- **诚实的中间基准与负面发现**：明确指出配对 McNemar 检验在 Top-1/Top-3 上不显著、V4 没有解决 0–0 识别、新增尾部候选无一命中精确比分，且 LLM 排序版本不输出归一化概率故无法做 proper score 比较。

## Key Contributions

- An auditable hybrid architecture that cleanly separates probabilistic state estimation (V1), evidence provenance/semantics (the harness), LLM reasoning (V3/V4 path simulation), and deterministic schema validation; the LLM can rerank a frozen candidate pool but cannot edit λ or invent scores.
- A documented four-version evolution (V1→V2→V3→V4) showing why a "mathematics + LLM + mathematics" scalar residual (V2) is insufficient and why conditional match-state paths plus shared root/cascade judgments (V3/V4) are a different, more interpretable model class.
- A chronological replay protocol that freezes prompts, responses, hashes, and artifacts before a separate evaluator reads outcomes, with per-match input/output archives and an eight-point audit checklist (Appendix B).
- An honest interim development benchmark (first 150 EPL 2025–26 fixtures) that reports proper-score baselines for V1, paired comparisons, failure slices (0–0, high-total, large-margin), and explicit limitations of historical LLM evaluation (model-memory contamination, adaptive model selection).

## Methodology

**V1 — dynamic probabilistic prior.** A score-driven GAS attack–defence model with attack gain g_a=0.04 and vulnerability gain g_v=0.02, half-life h=1440 days. Attack and vulnerability states decay toward zero and are updated after each date-batch via clipped, standardized score innovations. Independent Poisson masses are combined with the Dixon–Coles low-score correction τ_xy (ρ=−0.05) and renormalized. λ_H = μ_H exp(a_H − v_A), λ_A = μ_A exp(a_A − v_H). V1 was tuned on 18,665 matches (2015–16 to 2024–25, five top leagues + UEFA club competitions), training through 2021–22 and validating on 2022–23–2024–25.

**Pre-match evidence harness.** Each match packet supplies kickoff/evidence cutoff, identities, competition context, V1 λ values and full score-matrix semantics, ranked candidates, table position, season objective, recent form, formations, rest, schedule congestion, player starts/roles/availability/replacements, pre-match reporting, key tactical matchups, and explicit uncertainty reminders. The LLM is required to use football reasoning (source authority + contextual plausibility), not keyword matching.

**V2 — scalar residual sandwich.** The LLM emits four relative attack/defence ratings centred at 10.0 per team; a single learned κ maps rating differences into capped log-λ adjustments. Pooled 1X2 log loss improved only 0.1365% across 320 matches, with the final 99-match segment regressing 0.2387% and losing three hard 1X2 picks — a global κ cannot express asymmetric, state-dependent evidence.

**V3 — explicit path simulation.** The LLM receives λ, matrix semantics, frozen Top-10, and evidence; it must construct three distinct causal paths from 0–0, where each transition is a home goal, away goal, or stop, with reassessment of risk reduction, pressure/response capacity, exposed space, fatigue, substitutes, and matchups after every goal. The three terminals must equal the returned Top-3. The LLM cannot edit λ, invent scores, or apply a post-output weighting formula.

**V4 — shared root, cascade, tail anchors.** Before paths diverge, one shared root judges whether 0–0 will break. After the first goal, a shared cascade classifies the scoring mechanism's repeatability, the trailer's response capacity, and the leader's ability to exploit opened space, emitting closes_game / neutral / opens_game plus high-total and large-margin signals. A deterministic builder appends up to five matrix-derived tail anchors (high-scoring draw, high-scoring home/away wins, home/away runaway) as optional candidates.

**Protocol & metrics.** First 150 chronological EPL 2025–26 fixtures, 15 rounds of 10 matches, up to 10 concurrent but independent matches per round with fresh LLM context. Exact-score Top-1/Top-3 accuracy, score-derived 1X2 accuracy, and (V1 only) native 1X2 proper scores (log loss, Brier, RPS).

## Results & Highlights

- **Exact-score accuracy (first 150 EPL matches):** V4 reaches **14.7% Top-1 (22/150)** and **30.7% Top-3 (46/150)**, versus V1's **10.0% Top-1 (15/150)** and **26.7% Top-3 (40/150)** — a +4.7 pp Top-1 and +4.0 pp Top-3 descriptive gain; V3 sits between at 12.0%/30.0%.
- **Score-derived 1X2:** V4's score-implied 1X2 accuracy is **50.0% (75/150)**, +18 pp over V1's score-cell-mode 32.0% (48/150), but still below V1's **native 1X2 argmax of 53.3% (80/150)** on the same slice.
- **Statistical significance:** Paired exact McNemar tests are **non-significant for Top-1 (p=0.2295)** and **Top-3 (p=0.3075)**; only the score-derived 1X2 difference is significant (p=0.0024).
- **V1 native proper scores:** 1X2 log loss **0.987803**, Brier **0.586970**, normalized RPS **0.209451**, with mean included matrix mass 0.996674.
- **Candidate ceiling:** True score appeared in V1's Top-10 for **116/150 (77.3%)**; V4's tail anchors expanded theoretical coverage to **127/150 (84.7%, +11 matches)**, but an added anchor entered the final Top-3 in only 3 matches and **none was an exact hit**.
- **Tail failure slices:** V4's Top-3 exactly matched only 4 of 43 matches with ≥4 total goals and 3 of 24 matches with margin ≥3; among 14 actual 1–1 results, V4 placed 1–1 first four times and inside Top-3 ten times; **none of the eight 0–0 results appeared in Top-3**.
- **Reasoning-node diagnostics:** All 150 root verdicts selected `first_goal_more_likely` (a calibration failure — the 0–0 state is never identified); in the first 100 matches, 72 cascades were labelled `opens_game`, 21 `neutral`, 7 `closes_game`, and the open-game group had lower Top-3 accuracy, suggesting a generic bias toward escalation. The large-margin adjustment was higher precision: of 4 fixtures receiving a value ≥2.0, 3 truly ended with margin ≥3 (3 of 15 tail events recalled).

## Authors & Credibility

Sole author **Shaopeng Liang**, an independent researcher (5 August 2026). The paper is unusually self-critical for a single-author preprint: it openly states results are "exploratory rather than a claim of state-of-the-art performance," flags that the first-100 slice informed V4's design, and devotes a dedicated section (§8) plus Appendix D to validity threats and a planned preregistered confirmatory protocol. The LLM used is identified as a closed-weight service model (internal id gpt-5.6-sol, high reasoning effort), with the honest caveat that exact external reproduction is not guaranteed. Artifacts are anchored at Git tag `football-forecast-v7.28-frozen-2026-08-04`. The author discloses use of an LLM coding/drafting assistant while taking responsibility for all claims.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Moderate and incremental. The hybrid "statistical prior + constrained LLM reranker" idea is not new in spirit (WorldCupArena [6] already evaluates LLMs under pre-match evidence), but the specific architecture — LLM forbidden from editing λ, forced to emit auditable path/cascade nodes, deterministic validator owning schema and candidate-pool membership — is a genuine engineering contribution. The honest four-iteration narrative (V2 scalar residual → V3 paths → V4 shared root) is the most distinctive element.
- **Rigor:** Mixed. Positive: proper scoring rules reported for V1, paired McNemar tests, separate scoring of candidate coverage vs. ranking promotion, and an explicit audit checklist. Weak: V3/V4 emit only orderings, so they **cannot be compared on proper scores at all** — the headline Top-1/Top-3 gains rest on unweighted accuracy with no probability calibration. The author correctly flags this as unresolved.
- **Reproducibility:** Partial. Code, prompts, schemas, and per-match artifacts are claimed archived at a frozen Git tag, and the protocol is described in enough detail to reimplement. However, the core reasoning model is a closed-weight proprietary service (gpt-5.6-sol) whose exact behaviour cannot be reproduced externally, LLM stochasticity is addressed with only one archived run per version, and no variance/uncertainty intervals are reported.
- **Significance:** Low-to-moderate. The headline exact-score deltas are **not statistically significant** after paired testing, and V4 fails to beat V1's native 1X2 decision. The value is methodological (a template for disciplined, auditable LLM hybridization) rather than a forecasting breakthrough.
- **Red flags / paper-specific concerns:**
  - **Selection bias in the benchmark itself:** the first 100 of the 150 matches were used to design V4, so the entire headline table is an adaptive engineering benchmark, not held-out evidence — the author admits this but the title/abstract framing could still mislead a casual reader.
  - **Outcome-memory contamination is not ruled out:** the closed LLM was run after the 2025–26 season; inputs exclude target scores but the model may have memorized EPL results, inflating all LLM-version numbers. The paper does not run any parametric-memorization probe (e.g., a fact-forgetting or counterfactual-prompt test).
  - **A trivially-cheap baseline is missing:** a simple V1 probability-weighted Top-3 reranker (no LLM) is not reported alongside the LLM versions, so it is unclear how much of V3/V4's gain comes from the LLM's football reasoning versus from simply permuting V1's own Top-10 cells.
  - **Root-node degeneracy:** all 150 root verdicts returned `first_goal_more_likely`, making the most-touted V4 variable (first-breakthrough judgment) a constant — a strong internal signal that the added prompt structure improved interpretability but not discrimination, exactly the V2 failure repeated at a higher level.
  - **Tail anchors contributed zero exact hits**, undercutting one of V4's advertised components.

## Strengths

- Exceptionally transparent about negative results and methodology limitations; the Discussion (§7.2) and Validity (§8) sections are as detailed as the wins.
- Clean separation of concerns makes each failure mode independently diagnosable (root error vs. cascade error vs. candidate-generation failure vs. insufficient depth).
- The "mathematics + LLM" (no second math layer) formulation is a principled response to V2's unstable global κ — the LLM is demoted from parameter estimator to constrained simulator.
- Concrete, falsifiable audit checklist (Appendix B) and a preregistered future-protocol template (Appendix D) that other LLM-forecasting work could adopt.
- Reports V1 on a large multi-league training corpus (18,665 matches) with sensible half-life and gain values, giving the prior real credibility.

## Limitations

- LLM ranking versions do not output normalized probabilities, so they cannot be scored with proper scoring rules — a fundamental gap that prevents apples-to-apples comparison with V1.
- The benchmark is adaptive (first-100 informed V4), so no claim of unbiased generalization can be made.
- Closed-weight LLM + single archived run per version → reproducibility and variance estimation are inadequate.
- Outcome memorization by the LLM is neither controlled nor probed.
- 0–0 results are never identified (all root verdicts favour a first goal); high-total and large-margin tail events are mostly missed.
- Score-derived 1X2 (50.0%) is dominated by V1's native 1X2 (53.3%), so the LLM has not improved the categorical decision it is easiest to evaluate.
- No human-expert baseline (e.g., bookmaker-implied or analyst Top-3) is reported for the exact-score task.

## Relevance

对关注"LLM 与传统统计/符号方法混合、可审计推理"的研究方向有直接方法学参考价值：它示范了如何用冻结先验 + 受约束的 LLM 推理路径构建可审计系统，并坦诚给出统计不显著的负面结果，适合作为混合智能体评测协议设计的对照案例。若用户兴趣集中在纯大模型能力或非体育领域，则相关性较低。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would adding a V1-only probability-weighted Top-3 reranker (no LLM) close most of the Top-1/Top-3 gap, isolating the true marginal contribution of LLM football reasoning?
- Can the LLM versions be extended to emit a normalized posterior over the candidate pool plus reserved tail mass, enabling proper-score comparison and calibration on a dev window?
- What does a parametric-memorization probe (e.g., counterfactual lineups, swapped team identities, fact-suppression prompts) reveal about how much of V4's edge is genuine football reasoning versus retrieved outcome knowledge?
- Is the universal `first_goal_more_likely` root verdict a prompt-engineering artefact or an inherent bias of the closed model — and would an explicit 0–0 support branch (with shrinkage) recover any of the eight missed 0–0 results?
- Does a preregistered, live, forward-time evaluation on a future competition (with no developer or model-service access to outcomes) preserve, shrink, or eliminate the V1→V4 gap?
