Looking in the Mirror: Introspecting Side-Effect Misalignments Induced by Fine-Tuning

Introspection should report the measured side-effect shift of fine-tuning — not the implanted behavior — and DAIA consumes both base activations and Δh to do it.

Why this matters

Bridges controlled introspection research to real deployment-time auditing of fine-tuning-induced safety shifts in PEFT models.

Critical Edge

A simple linear probe matches or beats the LoRA introspection adapter — the authors concede introspection may be a relatively simple classifier over internal states.

01

Methodology — from implanted behaviors to measured shifts

Problem setting

Prior introspection learning deliberately implants a behavior bᵢ in fine-tuning data and uses that same bᵢ as the label. This work argues that conflates the learned behavior with the explanation target. In side-effect introspection, a model is fine-tuned for some (possibly benign) task, but the label yᵢ,꜀ describes a measured misalignment shift δᵢ,꜀ emerging as a side effect.

Data construction

213 real Hugging Face LoRA adapters on Qwen3-14B & Gemma3-12b-it, scored on 1,523 prompts across 13 safety categories (StrongREJECT, HarmBench, HEx-PHI, AdvBench). Judge: gpt-5-mini under HEx-PHI rubric. Shifts discretized via fixed thresholds (±0.08 / ±0.60) into 5 ordered labels.

DAIA architecture

A delta-aware introspection adapter that splits the update into two branches — base activations h_base and the fine-tuning difference Δh — each with its own LayerNorm + low-rank projection, then mixed back.

hDAIA = BDAIA [ A_base(LN_base(h_base)) ; A_Δ(LN_Δ(Δhᵢ)) ]

Ranks split r_base = r_Δ = 8 (LoRA rank 16); comparable params (LoRA 41.9M vs DAIA 49.5M).

Evaluation protocol

Models split 9:1; categories 1–9 seen, 10–13 held out. Three OOD settings: OOD-A (unseen models, seen cats), OOD-B (seen models, unseen cats), OOD-C (unseen × unseen). Tasks: direction-only (3-way) and direction-plus-degree (5-way).

02

Results & highlights

  • DAIA > LoRA on all three OOD settings. Gemma3 Dir Acc jumps to 75.8 vs 61.1; Qwen3 Dir+Deg F1 71.8 vs 70.4.
  • Generalizes to unseen models AND categories. In OOD-C, DAIA reaches Dir Acc 66.3 on Qwen3, avg 50.0 vs raw-model (~13.5) and majority (~29.7).
  • Probe is surprisingly competitive. A concat linear probe scores 66.0 avg in OOD-A — slightly above the LoRA IA, prompting the authors' candid concession.
  • Benign fine-tuning reliably degrades safety, heterogeneously. Disinformation +0.25, Cyber +0.14, Fraud +0.14; Copyright shifts safer −0.22. Std-devs often exceed the means.
  • Mechanistic finding. Safer/riskier judgment concentrates in late-layer MLP modules (gate/up/down_proj) at position P3; DAIA's branch attends primarily to Δh, confirming its design.
03

Strengths / Limitations

Strengths

  • Novel, well-motivated problem framing — closes the gap between controlled studies and real auditing.
  • Realistic data: 213 community LoRA adapters, not synthetic implants; two model families, 13 categories.
  • DAIA is conceptually clean; activation-patching gives genuine mechanistic evidence it attends to Δh.
  • Honest reporting — openly discloses the probe is competitive rather than overselling.

Limitations

  • Only the classification task is evaluated; free-form "describe the misalignment" reporting is deferred.
  • DAIA's edge over a strong probe is modest and concentrates in seen-categories (OOD-A).
  • Fixed 9/4 category split is small; no random-split variance or robustness study.
  • Labels hinge on a single proprietary judge (gpt-5-mini) + coarse thresholds; no human validation.
  • Code & dataset "future release" — not public at submission; LoRA/additive PEFT only.
04

Open questions for further exploration

  1. Can introspection adapters produce free-form natural-language descriptions of side-effect misalignment — and does DAIA's advantage over a probe actually grow in that open-ended setting?
  2. How sensitive are results to the judge, the ±0.08 / ±0.60 thresholds, and the fixed 9/4 split — would bootstrap-CIs over random category splits erase the DAIA-vs-LoRA gap in OOD-B / OOD-C?
  3. Does DAIA transfer across base-model versions (trained on Qwen3-14B, evaluated after a base patch) — the realistic deployment regime the authors flag as unaddressed?
  4. Can the Δh side-effect signal be separated from the intended-task signal via crosscoder / sparse model-diffing, and would feeding those diffing-derived features into DAIA sharpen sensitivity?
  5. Since the probe matches introspection in classification, what minimal task would actually prove introspection does something a probe cannot — calibrated uncertainty, counterfactual reports, cross-category transfer?
