> **一句话总结：** 本文识别并形式化了硬提示压缩（hard prompt compression）中的一个结构性失败模式——"指代悬空"（referential dangling）：独立打分保留了一个相关片段却删掉了解读它所需的依赖（定义/桥接事实），在 r=0.30 时六种主流压缩器在 HotpotQA 桥接集上悬空率达 32%–60%；用标注支撑段在固定预算下重新选择可将 Qwen3-8B 准确率提升 29–34 个百分点，而训练一个 BERT 分类器自动补回缺失依赖句在 HotpotQA 上提升 4.7 点且压缩比仅从 0.30 升到 0.31。

> **TL;DR:** The paper names and formalizes **referential dangling**, a paradigm-level failure of hard prompt compression where independent scoring keeps a relevant span but deletes the dependency needed to interpret it; at compression ratio 0.30 this affects 34–54% of bridge examples under Beaver across HotpotQA/2WikiMultiHopQA/MuSiQue and 32–60% across six compressors on a shared HotpotQA bridge set, with annotation-guided reselection recovering +28.8 to +34.3 accuracy points on Qwen3-8B (recovering 88–92% of the gap to full support) and an automatic BERT-based restoration adding +4.7 points on HotpotQA while moving the ratio only from 0.30 to 0.31.

## 核心贡献

- **首次命名并形式化"指代悬空"失败模式**：在抽取式硬压缩中定义并证明（Proposition 1）独立加性选择即使预算内存在完整替代方案，仍可能保留相关片段而删除其必要依赖——这是一个"范式级"而非单一实现的缺陷。
- **跨压缩器的实证诊断**：用方向性内容词覆盖（θ=0.5）作为度量协议，证明该现象普遍存在于六种代表性压缩器（Beaver、PartPrompt、Selective-Context、LLMLingua-2、DAC、LongLLMLingua）中，悬空率 32.1%–59.8%，且 LongBench-v2 单文档问答中全部 80 篇文档均含至少一处悬空引用。
- **固定预算重选实验量化代价**：在被悬空的子集上，重新插入被删支撑段并等量删除低分非支撑段，使 Qwen3-8B 准确率提升 28.8–34.3 点（p<10⁻⁴），恢复到"完整支撑"上限的 88%–92%。
- **自动上下文恢复管线**：训练 bert-base-uncased 分类器对被删句子按"是否为保留文本所需"排序并重插，HotpotQA+Qwen3-8B 提升 4.7 点（p=0.022），压缩比仅从 0.30→0.31；在 Mistral-7B 上提升 6.5 点。
- **证明强模型无法吸收该损失**：在 MuSiQue 上，GPT-5.5 在压缩上下文上比完整支撑低 8.8 点，GLM-5.2 低 24.2 点——指代悬空的代价不能靠更大的答案模型消除。
- **公开代码、逐例结果与训练好的模型**（https://cslikai.cn/Referential-Dangling/）。

## Key Contributions

- **Names and formalizes "referential dangling"** as a structural failure of extractive hard prompt compression: a relevant fragment is retained while the dependency (definition/bridge/antecedent) needed to interpret it is independently deleted, leaving the answer string present but the inference chain broken. Proposition 1 proves additive selection can omit required support even when a complete alternative fits the same token budget.
- **Empirical diagnosis across the paradigm**: using directional content-word coverage (threshold 0.5), shows dangling affects 34–54% of bridge examples under Beaver at r=0.30 across HotpotQA/2WikiMultiHopQA/MuSiQue, recurs at 32.1–59.8% across six diverse compressors on a shared 184-example HotpotQA bridge set, and appears in every one of 80 LongBench-v2 Single-Document QA documents (manual audit precision 95%).
- **Quantifies the downstream cost via fixed-budget reselection**: reinserting the omitted supporting paragraph and offsetting its tokens by removing low-scoring non-supporting paragraphs improves Qwen3-8B accuracy by 28.8–34.3 points (p<10⁻⁴) on dangling subsets, recovering 88–92% of the gap to full-support contexts.
- **Automatic restoration pipeline**: trains a compact bert-base-uncased classifier to rank omitted sentences by whether they are needed to interpret retained text, then reinserts the top-K (K=3). On HotpotQA with Qwen3-8B this adds +4.7 points (p=0.022) while moving the compression ratio only from 0.30 to 0.31.
- **Shows stronger answer models do not absorb the loss**: on MuSiQue, GPT-5.5 is 8.8 points less accurate and GLM-5.2 is 24.2 points less accurate on compressed vs. full-support contexts.

## Methodology

The work proceeds in four stages.

1. **Formalization (Section 3).** For an extractive compressor f(C,Q,τ) producing C̃ with |C̃|≤τ, the retained sentence set R = Ret_C(C̃) exhibits referential dangling iff ∃ s ∈ R ∩ T_{C,Q} such that ∀ D ∈ D_{C,Q}(s), D ⊄ R — i.e., a task-relevant retained sentence has none of its inclusion-minimal sufficient support sets fully retained. The authors then model hard compressors as additive fragment selectors solving K ∈ argmax_{cost(S)≤τ} Σ u(x), and **Proposition 1** gives a three-fragment existence proof ({x,d,z}, budget 2m, D_{C,Q}(x)={{d}}, u(x)>u(z)>u(d)) showing the additive maximizer {x,z} dangles even though the feasible {x,d} is complete. This isolates the missing constraint: there is no interaction term rewarding joint retention of a fragment and the text needed to interpret it.

2. **Empirical diagnosis (Section 4).** Because exact sentence retention is not uniformly available, they proxy Equation (1) with directional content-word coverage cov(v,A) at threshold θ=0.5 ("Keep_θ"). For bridge questions they pair answer and definition paragraphs; ρ_d is the fraction of examples whose answer unit survives but the paired definition unit does not, and ρ_e is the fraction retaining every annotated evidence paragraph. LongBench-v2 uses a separate first-mention diagnostic. They sweep θ (0.3–0.7), swap the Beaver embedding encoder (Qwen3-0.6B → GPT-2), and sweep r (0.20–0.70) to test robustness.

3. **Fixed-budget content reselection (Section 5).** Three conditions on each dangling example: Base (original dangling output at r=0.30), Reselected (reinsert one omitted supporting paragraph, remove the lowest-scoring non-supporting paragraphs until at least as many tokens are freed), and Full support (retain both annotated supporting paragraphs, removing two non-supporting). Reselected contexts are actually shorter than Base on average (−1.5% HotpotQA, −1.6% 2Wiki, −9.6% MuSiQue), so gains are not from a larger budget. Significance uses two-sided exact McNemar tests.

4. **Automatic restoration (Section 6).** A candidate generator collects omitted sentences that may support retained text; a bert-base-uncased classifier (≈110M params) ranks triples (retained sentence, candidate sentence, question) joined by [SEP]. Training pairs come from the HotpotQA training split (7,565 rebalanced pairs, 40% positive, dev F1 0.82, precision 0.84), strictly disjoint from evaluation. At inference the top-K=3 candidates are reinserted. The pipeline is evaluated only on Beaver outputs because its coherent multi-sentence blocks permit controlled sentence-boundary insertion; transfer to token-level compressors is reported in Appendix H and is left unresolved.

## Results & Highlights

- **Beaver prevalence at r=0.30 (Table 1):** paragraph-level dangling rate ρ_d is **34.2% on HotpotQA, 53.5% on 2WikiMultiHopQA, and 54.2% on MuSiQue**; complete evidence retention ρ_e is 61.0%, 30.7%, and 27.0% respectively. Manual audit on 40 flagged examples yields **95% precision** (38 of 40 confirmed).
- **Cross-compressor on the shared 184-example HotpotQA bridge set (Figure 3):** dangling rates are **Beaver 32.1%, PartPrompt 47.8%, Selective-Context 51.6%, LLMLingua-2 56.0%, DAC 58.7%, LongLLMLingua 59.8%**. The two query-aware methods (Beaver, LongLLMLingua) sit at opposite ends of the range, so query access alone does not remove the failure. Only 5.4% of examples dangle under all six, but 95% dangle under at least one (mean pairwise Jaccard 0.38 vs. 0.34 under independence).
- **LongBench-v2 Single-Document QA (n=80 docs, r=0.30):** every document contains ≥1 dangling reference; mean per-document rate **30.5%**. Reference-pair dangling rises with sentence distance: **51.7% at 1–10 sentences, 67.0% at 71–150, 64.2% beyond 150**; dangling pairs have mean distance 89 sentences vs. 73 for complete pairs. Across all seven subdomains the rate stays in 25.1%–36.5%.
- **Fixed-budget reselection on dangling subsets, Qwen3-8B (Table 3, Panel 1):** Reselected vs. Base gives **+28.8 points on HotpotQA (0.287→0.575), +30.6 on 2WikiMultiHopQA (0.097→0.403), +34.3 on MuSiQue (0.147→0.490)**, all p<10⁻⁴; this recovers **92% of the Base→Full-support gap on HotpotQA, 88% on 2Wiki, and exceeds Full support by 1.9 points on MuSiQue**.
- **Cross-model full-support gains on 200 HotpotQA examples (Table 3, Panel 2):** +8.0 (Qwen3-8B), +8.5 (Qwen3-4B), +8.5 (Llama-3.1-8B), +9.0 (Mistral-7B) points, all p<0.01 after Holm correction.
- **Proprietary models (Table 4):** on MuSiQue's dangling subset, Full support improves GPT-5.5 by **+8.8 points (0.775→0.863, p=0.011)** and GLM-5.2 by **+24.2 points (0.695→0.937, p=2.4×10⁻⁷)**; on HotpotQA, GPT-5.5 is unchanged (four fixes, four breaks).
- **Automatic restoration (Section 6, Table 5):** first-mention candidates give **+4.7 points on HotpotQA (p=0.022)** and **+6.5 points on Mistral-7B (p=0.012)** while moving the ratio only 0.30→0.31; a diagnostic that injects the annotated supporting sentence reaches +8.0 points (p=0.008) at only +0.4 sentences on average, indicating candidate construction is the current bottleneck. The matched-addition control gives +2.0 points for random insertion vs. +4.7 for targeted, but the direct contrast is not significant at this sample size (p=0.15).
- **Definition salience is not the explanation (Figure 4):** under Beaver similarity the mean salience percentile is 92.8% for definitions and 84.1% for answer sentences — omitted definitions are not low-salience.

## Authors & Credibility

Authors are from Qinghai University (School of Computer Technology and Application), Tsinghua University, and Ant Group's Security and Intelligence Laboratory. The corresponding author is Jianqiang Huang; Zhengpei Hu and Kai Li are co-first authors. The paper cites the specific official checkpoints/API identifiers used (Qwen3-0.6B/4B/8B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, microsoft/llmlingua-2-xlm-roberta-large-meetingbank, google-bert/bert-base-uncased, GPT-5.5, GLM-5.2) in Table 9, and all six compressors are run single-threaded on an A100 80GB for consistency. Code, per-example results, and the trained restoration classifier are released. Statistical claims use two-sided exact McNemar tests with Holm correction. Note the paper builds directly on the authors' own prior compressor Beaver (Hu et al. 2026), so they are diagnosing a failure in a method one author group proposed — a credible but not disinterested position.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** High and well-scoped. The core conceptual move — porting the anaphoricity constraint of extractive summarization (Durrett, Berg-Kirkpatrick, and Klein 2016) into hard prompt compression and proving its absence is a structural failure (Proposition 1) — is genuinely new. The framing as a "paradigm-level" rather than implementation-level defect is supported by the six-compressor sweep, though the proposition itself is only an existence result for the exact additive class and the authors are careful not to claim every evaluated selector instantiates it.
- **Rigor:** Strong on measurement hygiene. They run three robustness sweeps (embedding encoder, θ from 0.3–0.7, r from 0.20–0.70), use McNemar with Holm correction, report bootstrap CIs, audit precision manually (95%), and verify Reselected contexts are shorter than Base so gains are not a budget artifact. The proxy metric (directional content-word coverage at θ=0.5) only approximates Equation (1) and the authors say so; this is the main internal-validity soft spot.
- **Reproducibility:** Good. Checkpoints, ratios, proxy models, and hardware are pinned; the restoration classifier's training data construction (7,565 pairs, 40% positive, dev F1 0.82) and hyperparameters (AdamW, lr 2e-5, batch 32, 3 epochs) are fully specified; code is public. A weak point is the use of proprietary GPT-5.5 / GLM-5.2 endpoints dated July 2026 whose behavior may drift, and GLM-5.2 returned only 95/102 MuSiQue answers due to API timeouts.
- **Significance:** The diagnosis is likely to influence how the compression community designs selectors (adding an explicit dependency-preservation term). The practical remedy is, however, narrow: the headline +4.7-point automatic restoration is demonstrated **only on Beaver outputs**; when the same fixed configuration is transferred to PartPrompt (+3.2, p=0.30), Selective-Context (+1.0, p=0.80), and LLMLingua-2 (+1.0, p=0.75) the gains are not statistically significant (Table 13), and Llama-3.1-8B restoration is also non-significant (Table 10). So the "automatic restoration" contribution is currently a Beaver-specific diagnostic rather than a general fix.
- **Red flags / data quirks:** (a) The matched-addition control (random insertion +2.0 vs. targeted +4.7) is **not statistically distinguishable** at the reported sample size (p=0.15), so the evidence that targeted restoration beats naive insertion is suggestive rather than conclusive. (b) The "+34.3 points on MuSiQue exceeding Full support by 1.9" result is partly a side effect of Reselected and Full support removing different non-supporting paragraphs, so it measures joint content change, not the isolated contribution of the reinserted paragraph — the authors acknowledge this. (c) All reselection/restoration experiments depend on datasets with annotated supporting paragraphs (HotpotQA, 2Wiki, MuSiQue); generalization to un-annotated real-world retrieval contexts is untested. (d) The "every LongBench-v2 document has ≥1 dangling reference" claim uses the first-mention diagnostic whose denominator differs from ρ_d, so the two headline prevalence numbers are not directly comparable — the authors flag this but a casual reader could misread it.

## Strengths

- Identifies and cleanly formalizes a failure mode that the entire hard-compression family inherits, with a proof (Proposition 1) that the additive objective is the root cause, not any particular scoring signal.
- Cross-compressor evaluation (six methods spanning chunk/parse/sentence/token granularity, query-aware and query-agnostic) makes the "paradigm-level" claim credible rather than a critique of one system.
- Strong statistical discipline: McNemar with Holm correction, bootstrap CIs, robustness sweeps on the three main free choices, manual precision audit.
- The fixed-budget reselection experiment is a well-controlled existence proof that the lost accuracy is recoverable at the same budget, separating "what to fix" from "how to fix it automatically."
- Honest framing of automatic restoration as a diagnostic, with an explicit upper-bound diagnostic (+8.0 when the annotated sentence is in the candidate set) that pinpoints candidate construction as the bottleneck.

## Limitations

- The practical automatic remedy (+4.7 points) is demonstrated **only on Beaver**; transfer to four other compressor outputs is non-significant (Table 13) and is explicitly left unresolved.
- The matched-addition control does not reach significance (p=0.15), so the advantage of *targeted* over random sentence insertion is not formally established at the tested sample size.
- All quantitative reselection/restoration results rely on multi-hop QA datasets with annotated supporting paragraphs; behavior on un-annotated, real-world retrieval or non-QA tasks is untested.
- The dangling metric is a content-word-coverage proxy for Equation (1), not exact retention; the first-mention diagnostic used for LongBench-v2 has a different denominator from ρ_d and the two are not directly comparable.
- Restoration inserts whole sentences, so it is incompatible with token-level compressor outputs without altering their selection objective; the paper therefore restricts the main automatic-restoration evaluation to Beaver's coherent-block output.
- Proposition 1 is an existence result for the exact additive class and does not prove that every observed dangling case is caused by additivity; hierarchical/overlapping selectors (PartPrompt) are evaluated empirically rather than as formal instances of the model.

## Relevance

与长上下文 LLM 推理效率、提示压缩和多跳问答评估方向高度相关：直接揭示主流硬压缩方法在依赖完整性上的系统性缺陷，对设计依赖感知（dependency-aware）的压缩器、评估压缩保真度以及 RAG 检索-压缩管线都有指导意义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can a dependency-preservation constraint (à la Durrett et al. 2016 anaphoricity constraints) be added directly to the additive selection objective of existing compressors, and does it close the gap more cleanly than post-hoc sentence reinsertion?
- Why does the same fixed restoration configuration fail to transfer from Beaver to token-level compressors (LLMLingua-2 +1.0, Selective-Context +1.0) — is it the candidate generator, the classifier's training distribution, or the incoherence of inserting whole sentences into fragmented output?
- Does referential dangling generalize beyond multi-hop QA to un-annotated real-world tasks (open-domain summarization, agentic tool-use contexts, code retrieval) where no gold supporting paragraphs exist?
- A stronger matched-addition control at larger sample size: is targeted restoration statistically superior to random insertion, or is the gain mostly from "more sentences" rather than "the right sentences"?
- Can coreference-resolution signals (Lee et al. 2017) computed on the retained subset — rather than the full document — be used to predict which omitted spans are needed, removing the dependence on HotpotQA-trained entity-sharing heuristics?
- How does referential dangling interact with the "lost in the middle" positional bias (Liu et al. 2024)? Restoration that reinserts a bridge at a different position could fail for positional rather than referential reasons.
