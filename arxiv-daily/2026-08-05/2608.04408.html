Methodology — a budget-matched counterfactual

Δ_action(s) = p_cont − p_roll

For each replayed error state s, the method reconstructs the pre-error prefix and runs k teacher-continuation branches and k rollback branches under a matched remaining budget B(s) — same steps, tokens, temperature. Then it asks a single executable question.

Under matched budget, is teacher continuation more successful than rollback? Empirical success frequencies p_cont(s) and p_roll(s) yield a signed decision variable, sorted by pre-registered (non-learned) thresholds into three classes — never forced into a binary.

A candidate-conditioned backbone scores a shared admissible action set A(s) under both teacher and student, neutralizing the cross-tokenizer confound between Qwen3.5-9B and 27B. The SOD weight remains a sequence-mean log-prob diagnostic — never equated with recoverability.

Recoverable — retain prefix, add corrective horizon p_cont ≥ 0.50
Irreversible-but-avoidable — roll back, resample p_roll ≥ 0.50 ∧ p_cont ≤ 0.25
Ambiguous — fall back to ordinary SOD everything else
Branch replay · per error state +0.185 / −1.000 Δ

Mean Δaction across 65 recoverable vs 34 irreversible-but-avoidable states — non-overlapping bootstrap interval [1.092, 1.277]. Divergence cannot separate them.

03

Strengths & limitations — side by side

Critical assessment
Strengths
  • S1Reframes a vague "when to intervene in OPD" question into a concrete, executable, budget-matched counterfactual — with an explicit ambiguous class a controller can abstain on.
  • S2Candidate-conditioned scoring over a shared admissible action set removes invalid-action and tokenizer confounds — a reusable methodological control.
  • S3Disciplined scope: explicit that the oracle is an upper bound, honest about cost inflation, 101/200 ambiguous states, and unequal-cost comparisons.
  • S4Falsifiable diagnostic: opposite signs of Δaction (+0.185 vs −1.000) with non-overlapping bootstrap intervals.
  • S5Five-gate evaluation (E0–E5) makes integrity checks legible rather than burying them in an appendix.
Limitations
  • L1Single teacher / single student family (Qwen3.5-9B ↔ 27B) — no test of transfer across families, sizes, or tool environments.
  • L2Small seeds, unequal cost: 3 training seeds; oracle uses ~3× teacher tokens, so AIME/GPQA deltas are not cost-matched.
  • L3Ambiguity is large: 101 / 200 E2 states lack branch evidence — a deployable estimator must abstain on roughly half the states.
  • L4Perfect AUC under caveat: AUC = 1.000 comes from teacher probes, not a learned predictor; the 0.392 divergence baseline may be a degenerate sequence-mean proxy.
  • L5Domain coverage: only AIME + GPQA exam-style QA — no genuine tool-use / agent tasks where the framing is most motivated.
  • L6No learned online controller: a deployable state-only estimator is explicitly left as future work.

Questions for further exploration

Open

  1. Can a state-only predictor (small classifier over context + budget features) approximate oracle labels well enough to retain the gain inside the E0 cost budget?

    deployable estimator

  2. Does recoverability transfer across model families (Llama ↔ Qwen pairs) and tokenizer alignments, or is candidate-conditioned normalization doing most of the work?

    generalization

  3. How does recoverability interact with inference-time recovery (Reflexion, self-refine) — does training-time rollback supervision reduce or complement retry?

    train × inference

  4. On genuine tool-use / agent environments (not exam-style AIME/GPQA), what fraction of error states fall into each label — does the 50% ambiguity rate shrink or grow?

    agent tasks

  5. Would a soft / graded recoverability signal, rather than pre-registered hard thresholds, give a smoother trade-off between aggressiveness and abstention?

    graded signal

  6. Is the suspiciously perfect AUC = 1.000 an artifact of teacher-branch leakage through shared prefixes, or a real property of the recoverability partition?

    diagnostic validity
