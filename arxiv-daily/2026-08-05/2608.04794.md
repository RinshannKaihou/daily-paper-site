> **一句话总结：** 本文以严格的因果链实验证明，将 PI（特权信息）条件化的自蒸馏作为唯一训练目标时，在 MMLU-Pro、DAPO-Math、CodeForces、BFCL 等困难推理任务上不产生任何学习能力——损失持续下降但验证精度停滞或退化（智能体域平均降 3.51 分，BFCL Multi-Turn Missing Func V4 降 7 分），并定量揭示其根因：教师模型学的是某条参考轨迹而非"正确性"。

> **TL;DR:** This Microsoft Research paper shows that self-distillation (SD) with a privileged-information (PI)-conditioned teacher, used as a lone objective with no reward term, fails to teach reasoning across QA (MMLU-Pro), math (DAPO-Math), coding (CodeForces), and agentic (BFCL) tasks: per-token loss falls steadily while validation accuracy stays flat or degrades by up to -3.51 points on average in the agentic domain and -7.0 on BFCL Multi-Turn Missing Function V4 versus the base Qwen3-8B. The authors trace the failure to a single causal chain — PI bias (PI Bias Score 0.52 on the in-context solution vs ≤0.02 on a different correct one), a loss blind to correctness, signal concentrated on stopwords/punctuation (55.4% of loss), and exploration penalized — under both SDPO and OPSD recipes, both reasoning modes, both 8B and 32B model scales, and three forms of PI.

## 核心贡献

- **证伪 SD 作为独立目标的有效性：** 在四个困难推理领域（MMLU-Pro 通用问答、DAPO-Math 数学、CodeForces 代码、BFCL 多轮智能体）上系统性证明，PI 条件化自蒸馏作为唯一目标时训练损失下降但验证精度不升反降；现象横跨 think/instruct 两种推理模式、Qwen3-8B/32B 两种规模、SDPO 与 OPSD 两种配方以及整解/提示/技能三种 PI 形式。
- **提出 PI Bias Score：** 一种只需前向传播的测度，量化"教师被特权信息拉向某条具体解"的程度——对 in-context 解得分 0.52，而对同一问题的另一正确解得分 ≤0.02，几乎与无关问题的解不可区分。该指标可在训练前廉价筛检候选 PI。
- **给出完整因果链机制：** PI 偏置 → 损失对正误失去辨识（正确与错误 rollout 的 per-token loss 均约 3.5×10⁻⁴、KL 均约 0.03）→ 学习压力落在低信息量 token（停用词、不确定标记、标点、空白占 55.4% 损失）→ 探索性 token 被惩罚（正确 rollout 中偏离路径 KL ≈0.31，路径内仅 ≈0.08，差 4 倍）→ 学生变得更"平"、更早 commit、解题能力无提升。
- **澄清"失败的是目标而非密度"：** 通过对 PI 强弱端的对照实验（整解、提示、技能均无学习能力；提示形式在 DAPO-Math 平均 -5.13 分，技能 -3.46 分）证明：密度本身是 SD 的优势，但教师锚定于单一参考轨迹而非正确性，因此无法修复——既不能用过强 PI 也不能用过弱 PI 解决。
- **可操作的实践建议：** 损失下降不等于学习；应监控验证精度；并用 PI Bias Score 在训练前廉价筛选 PI（检查 PS⋆−PS′ 间距）。同时指出与 verifiable reward 结合是否能修复此失败仍是开放问题。

## Key Contributions

- **Empirical refutation of SD as a stand-alone objective on hard reasoning:** Across QA (MMLU-Pro), math (DAPO-Math), coding (CodeForces), and multi-turn agentic (BFCL) tasks, the per-token SD loss decreases steadily while validation accuracy stays flat or degrades. The pattern holds under both SDPO and OPSD recipes, both think and instruct reasoning modes, Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-32B scales, and whole-solution / hint / skill PI forms.
- **The PI Bias Score:** A forward-pass-only instrument (teacher-minus-student average log-probability on a target sequence) that quantifies how strongly a PI-conditioned teacher favors the in-context solution over alternative correct solutions. It reaches 0.52 on the shown solution y⋆ and ≤0.02 on a different correct solution y′ to the same problem, demonstrating that the teacher encodes one trajectory rather than correctness.
- **A measured causal chain of failure:** PI bias → a loss blind to correctness (per-token loss ≈3.5×10⁻⁴ and KL ≈0.03 overlap for correct vs incorrect rollouts; if anything the gap is reversed, with absolute log-prob gap ≈0.7 on correct vs ≈0.45 on incorrect) → loss budget concentrated on uninformative tokens (stopwords, uncertainty markers, punctuation, whitespace absorb 55.4%) → exploration penalized (off-path KL ≈0.31 vs on-path ≈0.08 within correct rollouts, a 4× factor) → a flatter, less decisive student whose entropy rises rather than falls.
- **Specificity-dial ablation showing both ends fail:** Weaker PI (hints, skills) reduces bias but also kills the signal: against a DAPO-Math base average of 63.4, hints lose 5.1 points, skills lose 3.5, while the whole solution stays within 0.5 of base. The failure is causal in PI specificity rather than incidental.
- **Reframing of SD as an underspecified family of objectives:** The density that motivates SD is intact; what fails is the target encoding correctness. The authors argue practitioners should monitor validation accuracy (not loss) and use the PI Bias Score as a pre-training PI screen, and explicitly leave open whether combining SD with a verifiable reward (à la RLSD) repairs the failure.

## Methodology

The paper studies the lone SD objective L_SD(θ) = E[(1/|ŷ|) Σ_t D_sg(p_T(·|x,r,ŷ_<t) ‖ p_S(·|x,ŷ_<t))], where the student is the policy π_θ and the teacher is the same model additionally conditioned on privileged information r, with stop-gradient so gradients flow only through the student. No reward or verifier term is included. The divergence D is the symmetric Jensen-Shannon divergence (matching SDPO), with aggressive per-token clipping τ=0.001 and a slow EMA teacher (α=0.001) added for stability on harder data.

The authors first reproduce SDPO's reported gains on its original easy regime (SciKnowEval Biology/Physics with Qwen3-8B) using the published SDPO codebase, confirming faithful implementation. They then apply the identical setup to four harder domains: General QA (MMLU-Pro, 2k examples, transfer to GPQA-D and SciKnowEval), Mathematics (DAPO-Math-17k, 2k examples, transfer to AIME24/25 and OlympiadBench), Coding (CodeForces, 2k examples, transfer to MBPP+, HumanEval+, CodeElo, LCBv6), and Multi-turn agentic (BFCL v3 multiturn, 100 tasks, transfer to BFCL v4 base/missing-function/missing-parameter/long-context).

The primary model is Qwen3-8B in both think and instruct variants, with Qwen3-32B added on MMLU-Pro for the scale study. Three PI forms are tested: whole reference solution r=y⋆ (default), short 1-2 sentence hints, and ≈500-token structured skill cards. Training uses 8 rollouts per query, batch size 32, 3 epochs, lr=1e-5, on 8× NVIDIA B200.

The mechanistic analysis (Section 6) measures each link of the predicted causal chain on Qwen3-8B runs with whole-solution PI: the PI Bias Score (Eq. 2), per-token loss and KL split by verifier outcome, loss attributed to nine mutually exclusive token categories, off-path vs on-path KL within correct rollouts, and the time-evolution of the teacher-student log-prob gap and student entropy. Robustness is probed via response-length tertiles, task-difficulty splits on MMLU-Pro (by MSR@8), and the Qwen3-32B scale study.

## Results & Highlights

- **Validation accuracy degrades or stays flat on every reasoning-heavy domain (Table 1, Qwen3-8B, lone SD).** In the agentic domain under the think variant, average accuracy drops from a base of 51.36 to 47.85, a -3.51 point delta; on BFCL Multi-Turn Missing Function V4 it drops 51.0 → 44.0, a -7.0 point delta. General QA averages fall -1.56 (think) and -1.57 (instruct); math averages fall -0.28 (think) and -1.80 (instruct).
- **The non-improvement is recipe-independent (Table 2, OPSD recipe).** On General QA the OPSD average deltas are -1.96 (think) and -0.65 (instruct); on math -4.28 (think) and -0.44 (instruct). 18 of 20 benchmark-mode cells are non-positive, with the same training signature throughout.
- **PI Bias Score shows the teacher encodes one solution, not correctness (Figure 3).** Across all four domains, PS⋆ ≈ 0.52 on the in-context solution y⋆, while PS′ on a different correct solution and PS∼ on an unrelated problem's solution are both ≤0.02 — i.e., the teacher barely separates an alternative correct solution from an unrelated one.
- **The loss is blind to correctness (Figure 4a-c).** Mean per-token loss settles near 3.5×10⁻⁴ and mean KL near 0.03 for both correct and incorrect rollouts, with overlapping 4-rollout bands throughout training. Where they separate, the absolute log-prob gap is reversed: ≈0.7 on correct rollouts vs ≈0.45 on incorrect ones — learning pressure is highest where the student is already right.
- **The loss budget lands on low-information tokens (Figure 4d, Figure 5).** Stopwords, uncertainty markers ("wait", "maybe", "perhaps"), punctuation, and whitespace together absorb 55.4% of the total per-token loss; content words, numbers, and math symbols that actually determine the answer absorb comparatively little.
- **Exploration is penalized (Figure 4e).** Within correct rollouts, off-path KL extends to ≈0.31 vs ≈0.08 on-path — a 4× factor; within incorrect rollouts the asymmetry widens to ≈0.47 vs ≈0.10. The teacher cannot distinguish a productive detour from a mistake.
- **Both ends of the PI specificity dial fail (Table 3, DAPO-Math, Qwen3-8B think).** Against a base average of 63.4, whole-solution SD scores 63.10 (-0.28), hint-PI scores 58.25 (-5.13), and skill-PI scores 59.92 (-3.46).
- **The failure is robust to response length, task difficulty, and model scale (Tables 4, 5).** On DAPO-Math response-length tertiles, deltas are -1.10 (short), -0.78 (medium), -1.58 (long). On MMLU-Pro difficulty splits, deltas are -3.60 (easy), -1.43 (medium), -1.58 (hard) — the easy split is no better than the hard one. Qwen3-32B on MMLU-Pro moves averages -0.61 (think) and -1.38 (instruct), with PI bias undiminished.

## Authors & Credibility

The five authors (Sarthak Harne, Chinmay Karkar, Yash Pandya, Ahmed Awadallah, Akshay Nambi) are affiliated with Microsoft Research, with Awadallah a well-known contributor to LLM post-training and agentic systems and Nambi the corresponding author. The work is positioned as a direct, reproducibility-first critique of two recent prominent SD methods (SDPO, Hübotter et al. 2026; OPSD, Zhao et al. 2026) and explicitly reproduces SDPO's reported easy-regime gains on SciKnowEval using the original codebase before extending to harder tasks, which strengthens the credibility of the negative result. The paper integrates and operationalizes two prior critiques — the conditional-mutual-information leakage bound of RLSD (Yang et al. 2026a) and the degraded-reasoning phenomenon of Kim et al. 2026 — into a single measured causal chain, which is a meaningful synthesis rather than a restatement.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** High and specific. The PI Bias Score is a genuinely new, cheap, forward-pass-only diagnostic that operationalizes RLSD's irreducible I(Y_t; R | X, Y_<t) bound into something measurable, and the four-target contrast (y⋆ vs y′ vs ỹ vs y⁻) is a clever design that separates "imitation" from "correctness" empirically. The causal-chain framing with each link measured on the same runs is more rigorous than the fragmented accounts in prior work.
- **Rigor:** Strong on internal validity — the reproduction of SDPO's easy-regime gains with the original codebase rules out implementation error as the cause of the negative result, and the recipe-independence check (OPSD vs SDPO) plus the specificity-dial ablation (whole solution / hint / skill) make a genuinely causal case that the failure lives in the objective, not the recipe or the data. The honesty about what the signature rules out (optimization failure, implementation error) is well-argued.
- **Reproducibility:** Good. Hyperparameters, training configuration, datasets, and split sizes are tabulated (Table 7, Appendix A); code is promised as supplementary material built on the public SDPO codebase; all benchmarks (MMLU-Pro, DAPO-Math, CodeForces, BFCL, AIME, OlympiadBench, MBPP+, HumanEval+, CodeElo, LCBv6, GPQA-D, SciKnowEval) are public. A specific gap: the PI Bias Score depends on the choice of comparison targets y′, ỹ, y⁻ and the paper does not release a canonical construction procedure beyond the appendix prompts.
- **Significance:** High if the claim is scoped as stated. The paper does not claim SD is useless in general — only that as a lone, reward-free objective on hard reasoning it does not work — and this is a sharp, actionable negative result for a method family (SDPO, OPSD, and their likely successors) that has been presented as a compute-efficient GRPO replacement. The practical guidance (monitor validation not loss; screen PI with the Bias Score before training) is concretely useful.
- **Red flags / weaknesses (paper-specific, not generic):**
  - **Single model family.** Only Qwen3 (8B and 32B) is tested; the authors explicitly disclaim any cross-architecture claim. Given that SDPO's reported gains were also on Qwen-family models this is consistent, but a Llama or Mistral run would have hardened the generality.
  - **Mechanistic chain measured only on Qwen3-8B.** Section 6's causal-chain links (PI bias, loss-by-correctness, token-type attribution, off-path KL, entropy rise) are all measured on the 8B model; the 32B scale study only reports end-task accuracy and the PI bias score, not the full chain.
  - **No experiment combining SD with a verifiable reward.** The authors explicitly acknowledge they "do not run that setting," so the open question of whether RLSD-style reweighting or reward+SD auxiliary combination repairs the failure is unanswered by this paper — readers wanting a prescription, not a diagnosis, will find none.
  - **Hints and skills are model-generated**, so the weak-PI results may partly reflect generation quality rather than the PI form itself; the authors flag this in Limitations but it weakens the "both ends of the dial fail" claim slightly.
  - **Four-rollout averaging** means some per-domain deltas (e.g., +1.0 on DAPO in-domain think) are within plausible run-to-run noise; the paper averages and reports ±SEM but does not always make the noise band legible in the prose.
  - **BFCL instruct-mode positive averages (+4.68)** are acknowledged but attributed to the tiny 100-task training split and 300-token rollouts; this is a reasonable explanation but the instruct-mode positives on MBPP+ (+12.6) and BFCL Base V4 (+7.5) are not small and sit somewhat awkwardly with the "no improvement where reasoning is required" headline.

## Strengths

- Reproducibility-first design: reproduces SDPO's published easy-regime result before reporting the negative hard-regime result, preempting the "your implementation is broken" objection.
- The PI Bias Score is a novel, cheap, actionable diagnostic that other practitioners can run before committing compute.
- Recipe-independence (SDPO and OPSD) and specificity-dial ablation (whole solution / hint / skill) make a genuinely causal, not merely correlational, case.
- Mechanistic depth: each link of the causal chain is measured on the same runs with concrete numbers (loss magnitudes, KL ratios, token-type shares), not asserted.
- Honest scoping in Limitations and Discussion — explicitly does not overclaim that SD is useless in general.
- Practical takeaways are concrete: monitor validation accuracy, screen PI before training, recognize that PI specificity is not a benign hyperparameter.

## Limitations

- Single architecture (Qwen3 only); no Llama / Mistral / Gemma cross-family confirmation.
- The full causal-chain mechanism is measured only on Qwen3-8B, not on 32B or other domains' models.
- No proposed remedy and no experiment testing whether reward + SD combination (the RLSD fix) repairs the failure — the paper is observational/diagnostic.
- Hints and skills are LLM-generated, so weak-PI results conflate PI-form with generation quality.
- Four-rollout averaging leaves some per-benchmark deltas within plausible noise; the instruct-mode positives (MBPP+ +12.6, BFCL +4.68 average) complicate the clean "no improvement" headline.
- The PI Bias Score's value depends on the chosen comparison targets (y′, ỹ, y⁻), and no canonical construction protocol is released beyond prompts.
- Training sets are small (2k examples, 3 epochs); whether longer SD training eventually crosses into learning is not tested.

## Relevance

这篇文章对关注 LLM 后训练与推理能力构建的研究者高度相关：它直接挑战了近期被宣传为 GRPO 高效替代方案的"自蒸馏"路线（SDPO / OPSD），并提供了可立即用于训练流水线的诊断工具（PI Bias Score）与监控建议（看验证精度而非损失）。如果你在做 RLVR、推理模型或 agent 训练，且考虑用 SD 作为无 reward 的独立目标或正则项，本文的负面结果与机制分析是必读的避坑参考。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does combining a verifiable reward with a small-weight SD auxiliary (the RLSD fix, or Wang/Lu/Yang et al. 2026 settings) actually repair the failure on hard reasoning, or does the PI-bias contamination persist in the auxiliary term?
- Does the PI Bias Score correlate with downstream validation delta across PI forms and model families strongly enough to serve as an automated PI-selection criterion before training?
- Would a teacher conditioned on the *set* of correct continuations (rather than one sampled trajectory) eliminate PI bias while preserving density, and at what compute cost?
- Does the causal chain (loss on uninformative tokens, exploration penalized, entropy rise) replicate on non-Qwen3 architectures (Llama 3, Mistral, Gemma)?
- On the instruct-mode positive outliers (MBPP+ +12.6, BFCL Base V4 +7.5), is the gain a real short-rollout effect or run-to-run noise, and does it survive more rollouts and seeds?
- Does lengthening training (more epochs, larger training sets beyond 2k) ever let lone SD cross from "flattening" into "learning" on hard tasks, or is the decoupling from correctness a fixed point of the objective?
