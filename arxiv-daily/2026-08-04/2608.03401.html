Methodology · Reasoning-interface evaluation

Shorter reasoning wins by finishing earlier — not by thinking better when cut off.

"Shorter Reasoning, Earlier Answers? An Evaluation of Reasoning Interfaces" — Carlon, Ginis & Algaba (VUB / imec-SMIT · Harvard SEAS). A matched-horizon paired-probe framework that forces two reasoning-interface conditions to share one token deadline, then decomposes any accuracy gap into finishing earlier vs. a better answer inside an unfinished prefix.

1Key metric · gpt-oss matched-horizon gap

+14.5→26.3pts

Completed low/medium-effort answers beat high-effort prefixes stopped at the same token budget — but most of the edge comes from finishing earlier, not from a sharper unfinished prefix.

2Why this matters

"Shorter is better" is two effects jammed together. Split them.

  • Correct completion before the deadline
  • Forced answer of a stopped, unfinished run
  • Differences among jointly active (still-reasoning) runs
  • Probability mass on the correct answer

3Critical edge

  • Within-family only, non-causal. Qwen prompt and gpt-oss effort interventions cannot be cross-compared.
  • 3 generation replicates drive primary token-exact inference; secondary cohorts lack seeds/IDs.
  • Forced candidate-logit readout distorts gpt-oss log loss via near-zero correct-option probabilities.
  • Proper scores untestable past B=2,048 — too few surviving trajectories.

§ 02 — Recommendation

The four-outcome reporting protocol.

i
Correct completion before the deadline.

Did the trace finish with the right answer inside budget? This is the only outcome a naive "accuracy vs length" plot reports cleanly.

ii
Forced answer of a stopped run.

Score the candidate-logit answer of an unfinished prefix — the "what would you say if I cut you off" readout.

iii
Differences among jointly active runs.

Condition on both traces still reasoning at B. This isolates prefix quality, divorced from completion timing.

iv
Probability on the correct answer.

Report proper scores (Brier, conditional log loss) — a better argmax need not mean uniformly better probability quality.

§ 03 — Method & rigor

How the probe is built.

Measurement stack

  • Token-exact replay of stopped inputs from stored token IDs — no decode/retokenize.
  • Candidate-logit forced answer: scores every valid one-token spelling of each answer letter over the full vocabulary, aggregated by letter.
  • Four readouts: policy-context, common-context, both-active, behavioral continuation.
  • Four termination strata: both-active · lower-complete/high-active · high-complete/lower-active · both-complete.
  • Reports valid-option mass, conditional log loss, multiclass Brier, argmax accuracy.

Controls & validation

  • Length-matched prefixes: swapped-trace −18.1 pt (gpt-oss-20b) / −17.8 pt (Qwen3-14B); shuffled-token and random-token sit between original and swap.
  • Scripted positive control: 25.0% → 100.0% at B=64.
  • Hidden-state probes: budget decodability balanced accuracy 0.938 at the prompt boundary, dropping to label-shuffle once reasoning begins.
  • Nine-arm MMLU-Pro instruction grid (8 replicates/item) on whether matching the announced number matters.
  • Three explicit generation replicates drive primary stochastic inference; Qwen3-4B/8B calibrate the measurement.

Limitations, in their own words

  • Benchmarks mostly multiple-choice; open-ended Omni-MATH-2 is single-rollout on a 200-item draw.
  • No behavioral-misalignment / competing-objective test — tasks "contain no competing objective or safety constraint."
  • Adding numeric-budget wording to the effort instruction gives mixed gpt-oss numbers (10/12 intervals span zero).
  • Proper-score table stops at B=2,048; later-horizon claims untestable.
§ 05 — Open threads

Where this could go next.

Does the decomposition survive genuinely open-ended tasks (long-form math, code, agentic tool use) where no candidate-logit forced answer exists?

The forced-answer readout is the linchpin of the methodology; extending to free-form generation is the obvious next stress test.

Could the both-active common-context contrast become a cheap online signal for switching effort mid-generation?

It isolates the "better unfinished answer" component independent of completion timing — a natural candidate for a calibration-aware stopping rule.

Does budget decodability (balanced acc 0.938 at prompt boundary) generalize to models trained with explicit budget tokens (BudgetThinker, L1)?

Such models may make the horizon decodable by design rather than as a side effect.

Would >3 replicates and a powered equivalence test change the matching-number null?

Currently only descriptive intervals; tighter replicate budgets could turn the negative result into a confirmed one.
