> **一句话总结：** 本文提出 NAP（Normalization Affine Preconditioning），将 BN/RMSNorm 的仿射参数视为仅占约 1.43% 参数量的"低维高杠杆"响应控制子空间，在冻结主干权重的前提下针对性地优化该子空间，能在 ImageNet/CIFAR-100/Cityscapes/Qwen2.5-3B 上恢复极端低比特量化崩溃（如 MobileNetV2 W4A4 从 RTN 的 0.33% 恢复到 66.11%），并突破饱和 QAT 的性能瓶颈。

> **TL;DR:** NAP identifies normalization affine parameters (γ, β) as a tiny (~1.43% of trainable params) but disproportionately powerful "response-control subspace" for low-bit quantization; by freezing backbone weights and optimizing only these affine parameters under the target fake-quantized graph (with KD), it recovers collapsed PTQ models (ImageNet MobileNetV2 W4A4: RTN 0.33% → NAP 66.11%, +5.40 over QDrop), boosts saturated QAT checkpoints (CIFAR-100 W4A4 OOQ-Freeze 70.02% → 70.68%), and cuts Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct W4A4 OSCAR perplexity from 483.57 (AWQ-style) to 166.65 (NAP-QAT).

## 核心贡献

- **首次系统刻画"归一化仿射参数"为量化低维高杠杆子空间**：诊断实验（Fig. 1）证明从同一饱和 LSQ+ 检查点出发，仅更新 BN 仿射参数（34.2K，约 1.43% 可训练参数）可将 MobileNetV2 W4A4 从 68.21% 提到 69.83%、W2A4 从 63.42% 提到 65.86%，而同等步数的全参数或仅量化器更新几乎无效甚至变差。
- **提出 target-aligned 的 NAP 统一框架**：同一原理支持三种用途——(i) PTQ 前的"预处理"（proactively 提升量化友好度）、(ii) 饱和 QAT 后的轻量后适应、(iii) LLM 上归一化响应与量化 scale 的交替优化（NAP-QAT）。
- **残差感知的局部理论分析**：在局部仿射失真模型下证明 BN 仿射参数可精确抵消量化的"尺度+偏移"结构分量（Eq. 13-16），但非线性的舍入/截断残差是不可约边界；RMSNorm 因无偏移项只能补偿乘性分量。并把蒸馏引导的 NAP 解释为在归一化仿射响应子空间上的局部投影问题（Eq. 17-18）。
- **跨任务/跨架构验证**：在 ImageNet 分类、CIFAR-100 的 QAT 后适应、Cityscapes 语义分割（U-Net）、Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct 语言建模四个场景上均验证有效性，并通过受控研究定位其后端敏感性、数据依赖与失败案例。

## Key Contributions

- **Identifies normalization affine parameters as a low-dimensional, high-leverage "response-control" subspace** for quantization across both BN-based CNNs (γ, β) and RMSNorm-based LLMs (γ only); a diagnostic study shows that updating only 34.2K BN params (~1.43% of the QAT state) lifts a saturated LSQ+ checkpoint on CIFAR-100 MobileNetV2 from 68.21% to 69.83% (W4A4) and 63.42% to 65.86% (W2A4), while quantizer-only or matched extra QAT barely helps.
- **Proposes a unified, target-aligned NAP framework** with three modes: (i) pre-conditioning before PTQ, (ii) lightweight affine adaptation after saturated QAT, and (iii) alternating NAP-QAT that block-coordinate-optimizes normalization responses and quantization scales with the backbone frozen.
- **Provides a residual-aware local analysis** showing BN affine parameters exactly cancel the channel-wise affine (scale-and-shift) component of quantization distortion, while nonlinear rounding/clipping residuals are irreducible; RMSNorm, lacking a shift term, can only compensate the multiplicative part. Distillation-guided NAP is cast as projecting teacher-student logit mismatch onto the affine response subspace.
- **Validates across four settings** — ImageNet classification, CIFAR-100 post-QAT adaptation, Cityscapes semantic segmentation, and Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct language modeling — and complements these with controlled studies (matched-subset controls, backend-alignment ablations, tuning-data-size sweeps) that locate the method's boundaries.

## Methodology

**Problem setup.** A pretrained network is written f(x; W, Φ), where W are convolutional/linear weights and Φ are the affine parameters of normalization layers (γ, β for BN; γ only for RMSNorm). NAP's key decomposition splits adaptation into three groups — backbone features W, discretization grid Ω, and channel-wise responses Φ — and optimizes only Φ (or Φ and Ω alternately) while freezing W.

**Target-aligned NAP for PTQ.** Let G_B be the target backend's fake-quantized graph (insertion points, observers, granularity, clipping rules). After calibration fixes Ω₀, NAP freezes W and enables gradients only on Φ, optimizing a combined task + knowledge-distillation objective (Eq. 6) plus an optional weak channel-scale regularizer (Eq. 7, λ_CS = 1e-4) that discourages a few channels from dominating the activation range. Crucially, all training happens under the same G_B used for evaluation/downstream reconstruction — so a NAP checkpoint is backend-specific and does not transfer across mismatched graphs.

**NAP after QAT and NAP-QAT for LLMs.** The same objective applied to a saturated QAT checkpoint tests whether an under-exploited affine direction remains. For RMSNorm-based LLMs, the authors introduce NAP-QAT: a block-coordinate scheme alternating (a) group-wise quantization-scale updates with Φ fixed (Eq. 9) and (b) RMSNorm-scale updates with the quantization grid fixed (Eq. 10). This separates response adaptation from grid adaptation while never touching backbone weights — so the "LLM experiments do not perform full-parameter QAT."

**Why the affine subspace is high-leverage.** (1) Channel-wise broadcast: one or two scalars (Δγ, Δβ) modify an entire channel across all spatial positions/tokens (Eq. 11-12). (2) Residual-aware compensation: under a local affine-distortion model (Eq. 13), choosing γ* = γ₀/α, β* = β₀ − (γ₀/(αρ))[(α−1)μ + δ] exactly cancels the structured scale-and-shift component while leaving rounding/clipping residuals e_c (Eq. 16). RMSNorm's missing shift makes it strictly weaker. (3) Local projection view: distillation NAP solves min_{ΔΦ} ‖ê − J_q ΔΦ‖², i.e., it corrects only the component of teacher-student mismatch expressible in the affine response subspace.

**Complexity.** No inference branch is added (BN affine folds into the preceding conv; RMSNorm scale is already part of the model). Optimizer state is maintained only for Φ (or Φ and Ω). The authors explicitly note that activation compute still dominates wall-clock, so they report both trainable-param counts and measured time.

## Results & Highlights

- **Extreme PTQ on ImageNet (Table 1):** MobileNetV2 FP32 = 71.87%. RTN collapses to **0.33% (W4A4)** and 0.11% (W3A4). Standalone NAP recovers W4A4 to **66.11%, beating QDrop's 60.71% by +5.40 points** without touching conv/linear weights. At W3A4, NAP+QDrop achieves the best **53.45%** vs QDrop alone 51.75%.
- **Post-QAT adaptation on CIFAR-100 (Table 2, FP32 = 71.30%):** At W4A4, NAP+OOQ-Freeze reaches **70.68% (only −0.62 from FP32)**, the strongest result, up from 70.02%; NAP+LSQ+ = 69.83% (−1.47) vs LSQ+ alone 68.21% (−3.09). At W2A4, NAP+StableQAT = **68.50%** (−2.80) vs StableQAT alone 66.09% (−5.21). One genuine negative case is reported: NAP degrades W2A4 OOQ-Freeze from 67.42% to 62.81%.
- **Cityscapes U-Net (Table 3, FP32 = 68.52 mIoU):** RTN W4A4 = 3.27% mIoU; standalone NAP = **67.21%** (only 1.31 below FP32, +0.76 over QDrop). At W3A4, NAP+QDrop = **61.36%, +3.15 over QDrop** (58.21%).
- **Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct (Table 4, W4A4, group-128 weight / per-token activation):** RTN is unusable (OSCAR ppl **1.13×10⁵**). AWQ-style = 483.57; RMSNorm-NAP = **426.49**; QScale-QAT = **233.27**; alternating **NAP-QAT = 166.65** OSCAR perplexity (best). The same ordering holds on C4 (147.92) and WikiText-2 (104.37).
- **Matched-subset control (Table 5, ImageNet MobileNetV2 W4A4):** With the same 34.2K params, NAP = **66.11%** vs Random 2.56%, Top-gradient 12.48%, Top-Fisher 18.35%, Classifier 0.81%, and even beats Full tuning (3.52M params) = 64.28% by +1.83 — confirming the gain is not from sparsity alone. NAP runs at 1.00× normalized time vs 1.83× for full tuning.
- **Backend-alignment ablation (Table 6):** NAP full = 66.11%; without KD drops to 61.50% (−4.61); **transferred to a mismatched fake-quant graph it collapses to 48.72% (−17.39)**, proving NAP learns backend-specific rather than generic channel rescaling.
- **Tuning-data-size sweep (Table 7):** NAP is parameter-efficient but NOT data-efficient — 5K images → only 2.98% W4A4, 10K → 3.88%; the full ImageNet tuning set is required to reach 66.11%.

## Authors & Credibility

Authors are Peng Xia, Junbiao Pang (corresponding), and Zheng Huang from the School of Information Science and Technology, Beijing University of Technology. The paper is a carefully constructed empirical study: it includes a matched-parameter-subset control (Table 5) that rules out the "any sparse subset works" null hypothesis, an explicit backend-mismatch ablation (Table 6), a tuning-data-size sweep (Table 7) that honestly exposes the method's data hunger, and one clearly reported negative case (NAP hurting W2A4 OOQ-Freeze). The authors correctly attribute the closest prior LLM work to Norm Tweaking (Li et al. 2024) and explicitly disclaim being "the first normalization-only quantization method." Code/configs are promised upon publication. Hardware spans an RTX 3090 (CNNs) and two MetaX C600-A 72GB GPUs (LLMs), with seed 42 and single runs unless stated — a transparency caveat given the small absolute deltas in some QAT comparisons.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine conceptual reframing — the diagnostic in Fig. 1 and the matched-subset control in Table 5 make a real case that normalization affine parameters are a high-leverage *subspace* rather than an arbitrary sparse subset, and the unified BN + RMSNorm formulation with three deployment modes is non-trivial. However, normalization-only tuning is not new (Norm Tweaking for LLMs, BN-tuning for domain adaptation), so novelty is moderate rather than paradigm-shifting.
- **Rigor:** Strong on controls (matched-subset, backend-mismatch, KD-ablation) and on theory (the residual-aware local analysis cleanly delineates the affine-compensable component from irreducible rounding/clipping). The residual-aware analysis is local and does not establish global flatness — the authors are careful to frame it as a "local mechanism," which is honest.
- **Reproducibility:** Hyperparameters, backends, bit-widths, and seeds are reported in detail; MQBench, AWQ, Qwen2.5 are all public. Two caveats: (a) most entries are single runs, so several sub-1% QAT deltas (e.g., OOQ-Freeze 70.02→70.68) lack error bars and could be within noise; (b) the ImageNet setting requires the full training set, which the paper itself concedes "is not a calibration-only or few-shot method" — this materially weakens the comparison to PTQ methods (QDrop, AdaRound) that use only tens of calibration batches.
- **Significance:** Practically useful: a 5.40-point W4A4 gain over QDrop on MobileNetV2 at <1.5% of trainable params, plus a roughly 3× perplexity reduction over AWQ-style on Qwen2.5-3B W4A4, are meaningful for real deployment.
- **Red flags / overclaiming:**
  - The headline "outperforms saturated full-parameter QAT" is supported by the matched-subset control but should be qualified: NAP *added on top of* saturated QAT beats *additional* full-parameter QAT, not the original QAT budget.
  - The LLM W4A4 perplexity gap to FP16 remains enormous (166.65 vs 14.41 OSCAR) — NAP-QAT helps but does not make W4A4 LLMs practically usable, a boundary the authors acknowledge.
  - Selective reporting risk: the negative W2A4 OOQ-Freeze case is disclosed, but the data-efficiency failure (5K/10K images → <4% accuracy) is buried in an ablation rather than flagged as a primary limitation.
  - "Single run" reporting on sub-1% deltas invites skepticism about statistical robustness.

## Strengths

- Clean, well-motivated core insight backed by a diagnostic study (Fig. 1) that directly challenges the "all parameters equally useful" assumption.
- Unified framework across CNN (BN) and LLM (RMSNorm) quantization, with a crisp three-way decomposition (features W / grid Ω / responses Φ).
- Strong, honest controls: matched-subset comparison (Table 5) and backend-mismatch ablation (Table 6) are exactly the right experiments to support the causal claim.
- Theoretical analysis correctly identifies *both* the leverage and the hard limit (rounding/clipping residuals) of the affine subspace.
- Adds zero inference cost — affine parameters fold into existing layers.

## Limitations

- **Not data-efficient in the CNN setting:** requires the full ImageNet training set; 5K/10K images fail catastrophically (Table 7), unlike true PTQ methods that need only small calibration sets.
- **Backend-specific:** a NAP checkpoint does not transfer across mismatched fake-quant graphs (17.39-point drop in Table 6), so users must re-run NAP per deployment backend.
- **Not universally additive to saturated QAT:** NAP *degrades* the W2A4 OOQ-Freeze checkpoint (67.42% → 62.81%), showing the post-QAT benefit is conditional on the checkpoint's specialization.
- **Cannot recover non-affine distortion:** severe W3/W2 activation clipping and rounding remain largely uncorrectable (e.g., W3A4 best is only 53.45% on MobileNetV2; LLM W4A4 still ~10× FP16 perplexity).
- **Single-run results** for several sub-1% QAT deltas lack variance estimates.
- **LLM experiments do not perform full-parameter QAT** (by design), so NAP-QAT is best read as a PTQ-scale method rather than a full QAT replacement.

## Relevance

对关注"高效深度学习 / 模型压缩 / 低比特量化"的研究者高度相关：NAP 提供了一个参数效率极高（约 1.43% 可训练参数）、零推理开销、可即插即用到现有 PTQ/QAT 流水线（含 QDrop、LSQ+、AWQ、Qwen 系列）的轻量适配子空间方法，特别适合部署侧的极端低比特场景与边缘端大模型量化研究。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Why does NAP *hurt* the W2A4 OOQ-Freeze checkpoint specifically — is it that OOQ-Freeze has already baked the affine response into the frozen weights, so an extra affine update over-corrects? Can a checkpoint-saturation detector decide whether to apply post-QAT NAP?
- Can the ImageNet data-hunger be reduced by combining NAP with self-distillation, augmentation, or a small proxy calibration set so it becomes a true few-shot/PTQ method?
- NAP is shown complementary to QDrop and AWQ-style scaling; how does it interact with rotation-based methods (QuaRot, SpinQuant, FlatQuant) that target the same outlier problem via a different subspace?
- The residual-aware analysis predicts NAP cannot fix rounding/clipping residuals — can NAP be combined with learnable clipping (PACT/OmniQuant) in the alternating NAP-QAT scheme to push W3A4/W2A4 further?
- On LLMs, does NAP-QAT's benefit generalize beyond Qwen2.5-3B (e.g., to Llama-style or larger 7B/70B models with more pronounced activation outliers)?
- What is the statistical significance of the sub-1% QAT deltas given single-run reporting? Are multi-seed confidence intervals tight enough to support the "outperforms saturated full-parameter QAT" framing?
