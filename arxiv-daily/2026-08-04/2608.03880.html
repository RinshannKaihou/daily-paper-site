Key Metric

10% → 1% MAPE, per cell.

One OLS fit per (GPU, dtype, batch size) collapses error from ~10% per-GPU to ~1% per cell — the cross-repeat noise floor. MI210 within-cell R² median 0.997.

Why This Matters

Power from software alone.

MFU is derivable from model structure + throughput — no hardware probes. That unlocks energy-aware LLM-training simulators (Vidur-style) that cannot read GPU counters.

Critical Edge

Breaks at batch = 1.

Memory-bound regime: every device runs < 1.3% MFU yet draws 49–64% of batch-128 power. Single-GPU, training-only — decode is out of scope.

01 / RECIPE

The one-shot calibration

i.

Sweep one training step

Forward + backward + AdamW on a fixed prompt; data pipeline kept out of the measurement.

ii.

Sample to convergence

Stop when the 95% CI of MFU & GPU-Util is within 5% of the running mean (min 50, max 500).

iii.

Fit OLS per cell

One linear fit of power on MFU per (GPU, dtype, batch) — nothing else. Context 512 vs 2048 had no effect.

iv.

Compare to noise floor

Residual vs. cross-repeat SD: at ~1% MAPE the model is data-limited, not model-limited.

Conditioning cuts residual by 4–7× on every device. On RTX 4070 Ti and L4 the conditioned residual is statistically indistinguishable from the noise floor — i.e. there is no modeling headroom left in the compute-bound regime.

04 / FAILURE MODE

Memory-bound breaks the line

A single slope cannot span both regimes.

At batch = 1, every device falls below 1.3% MFU yet keeps drawing a large fraction of its full-load power. Going from batch 1 → 128, MFU grows 36–72× while power moves only 1.1–2.0×.

A single-slope fit extrapolates through this regime and under-predicts by 70–120 W on data-center cards. The fix is not a better slope — it is a complementary memory-bandwidth signal, which the paper flags as future work.

06 / READ

Strengths vs. sharp edges

◆ What holds up

  • Cross-vendor — one of the few MFU studies to include AMD MI210, which prior work lacked.
  • Methodologically clean — convergence stopping rule, 4 repeats, 2000-resample bootstrap CIs, explicit noise-floor comparison.
  • Honest about boundaries — batch-1 failure is both quantified and explained mechanistically, not buried.
  • Directly actionable recipe — "condition on (GPU, dtype, batch) and nothing else" is one-shot and cheap.
  • Public benchmark pipeline + raw data (dos-group/gpu_power_benchmark).

▲ What to probe

  • Single-GPU, training-only — no NCCL/RCCL collectives, FSDP all-gathers, or pipeline-bubble idling; cluster accuracy unverified.
  • L4 fit is genuinely weak (R²=0.37) — waved at as "little to predict" given its 72 W TDP / 64 W idle floor.
  • Eager attention only — production fused / flash kernels raise MFU and may shift slopes; not empirically shown.
  • MI210 baseline partly unfair — ROCm's util is effectively a binary activity flag.
  • Wall-power check covers 2 / 6 devices; inference decode is explicitly out of scope.
07 / NEXT

Open questions

1Does the per-cell calibration hold once communication power (NCCL/RCCL, FSDP all-gathers, pipeline bubbles) is layered on for multi-GPU training?
2Does the linear relationship survive production fused / flash attention kernels, which raise arithmetic intensity and shift MFU upward?
3Which memory-bandwidth signal (achieved HBM BW, a memory-util metric) best extends the model into the batch-1 / decode regime?
4Can the recipe port to autoregressive inference (Vidur), where decode is memory-bound — what does the validated proxy look like there?
5Does the 1.21–4.49 W/% slope spread collapse onto one curve if MFU is replaced by raw achieved TFLOPS?
