Methodology · Biomedical LLM Calibration

The scoring rule you forgot to specify reverses who looks calibrated.

Anton Rasmussen · Hong Qin  — Old Dominion University, Norfolk VA

A controlled 3-model × 3-precision × 4-template study on PubMed RCT shows that switching from summed to mean-token log-likelihood flips the ECE ranking between BioMistral and Instruct while accuracy barely moves — making the probability-extraction protocol, not the model, the first-order variable in decoder calibration evaluation.

Sum → mean-token log-likelihood. Accuracy delta <1pp for specialized models; only the calibration story inverts.
Why This Matters
Every published calibration number for a decoder LLM is conditional on a scoring protocol almost no one reports. Without it, leaderboard comparisons are uninterpretable — you may be ranking normalization choices, not models.
Critical Edge
  • Not a clean ablation. BioMistral inherits Instruct-v0.1 tuning; differs in base version + domain pretraining.
  • Single task, single family. PubMed RCT only; 7B Mistral only; bitsandbytes quant only.
  • Provenance flaw. Evolved templates picked on test-derived subset with pre-boundary-fix scorer — authors flag, don't hide.

The reversal, visualized

Headline result · Fig. 1
Expected Calibration Error (lower is better)
Average across templates, FP16, sum- vs mean-token scoring.
Read this chart as a stability test. The bars should not cross. They do — and that crossing is the paper's whole point: the ranking is a property of the scoring rule, not the model.

Label names span 3–5 tokens. Summed log-likelihood silently penalizes longer labels; mean-token normalization removes that prior. Whichever you pick becomes the lens.

Temperature scaling amplifies the ambiguity: under sum scoring, Instruct's fitted T=2.23–4.64 yields 2.7–4.2× ECE reductions (e.g. 0.307 → 0.073) — then the effect reverts under mean scoring.

Calibration comparisons are uninterpretable without specifying the scoring protocol. Report both.

Why this ordering is itself scoring-rule-sensitive
The ordering is drawn from a single task (PubMed RCT) and is itself dependent on the scoring rule used to compute it. The base model shows up to +4.2pp INT8 shift on Struct-Delim — a template-quantization interaction the authors flag but do not fully characterize. Treat the ranking as a hypothesis, not a constant.

Templates & quantization, side by side

Test set · boundary-corrected v3 scorer

Template sensitivity

Four answer-text templates, one shared verbalizer (literal class names). The template does more work than the model swap on several splits:

  • 17–24pp base-model accuracy gap, Struct-Delim vs Bare-Cont — exceeds model choice
  • +7.3pp base-model gain on validation, Min-Inst → Alt-Frame
  • Winner flips by template: Instruct leads on Struct-Delim (+5.9–6.2pp), BioMistral leads on Bare-Cont (−2.8 to −4.0pp)
  • Best decoder accuracy: BioMistral Bare-Cont @ INT8 = 0.706; Instruct Struct-Delim = 0.698

Class-code collapse (verbalizer failure)

Single-token letter-code scoring collapses: 5/10 BioMistral configs predict one class for 100% of samples (macro-F1 0.038–0.143). Permutation control confirms positional/token-frequency bias. Switching to full answer-text rescues macro-F1 to 0.303–0.367.

INT8 deltas (specialized models)

For BioMistral and Instruct, INT8 stays within 1–2pp of FP16. Where 95% CIs exclude zero, the direction uniformly favors INT8:

04

What to do on Monday morning

Methodological prescriptions
→ 01
Report multiple scoring rules
Always publish both sum and mean-token log-likelihood for any decoder calibration claim. If rankings move, that movement is the finding.
→ 02
Treat the template as a variable
Sweep ≥4 templates. Report the spread. A model "win" that inverts across templates is not a win — it is a template artifact.
→ 03
Predeclare INT8 equivalence margins
Before deployment, fix a maximum acceptable Δ and run paired bootstrap CIs. Don't claim "lossless" from a single favorable cell.
05

Open questions the authors leave on the table

For follow-up
  • Does the reversal survive an equal-length verbalizer or label-permutation control? Proposed, not run.
  • Behavior under chat-template formatting for Instruct — independently worsens miscalibration per Sanz-Guerrero et al. 2026.
  • Replication on clinical NLI, relation extraction, and larger (70B) or non-Mistral families.
  • How do GPTQ / AWQ compare to bitsandbytes INT8/NF4 on the accuracy-calibration frontier?
  • Can a label-permutation-invariant scoring rule remove the length prior and stabilize rankings?
  • Is the base model's +4.2pp INT8 shift a quantization artifact or a template × quant interaction?
