> **一句话总结：** SA-OPD 通过对比"保留输入"与"移除输入"两种条件下师生 token 级散度的差异来估计输入依赖性(input-groundedness),只过滤那些"弱输入依赖且高优化影响"的伪信号 token,在 VLM 与 LLM 推理蒸馏上稳定超过 Vanilla OPD 及 TIP/FiRe-OPD 等选择性基线。

> **TL;DR:** The paper identifies "spurious signals" in on-policy distillation (OPD) — token-level teacher judgments driven by input-agnostic language priors rather than task evidence — and proposes SA-OPD, which estimates each token's input-groundedness via a residual no-prompt divergence computation and filters only tokens that are simultaneously weakly input-grounded and high-impact. On Qwen3.5-35B-A3B → Qwen3.5-2B VLM distillation, SA-OPD raises the visual-understanding average from 50.5 (Vanilla OPD) to 54.0 (+3.5) with CountQA jumping 26.4 → 33.6 (+7.2), and improves LLM math reasoning on Qwen3-4B → Qwen3-1.7B from 28.5 to 30.4 average (+1.9), beating TIP, FiRe-OPD, and ExOPD on 11 of 11 benchmarks.

## 核心贡献

- **形式化定义 OPD 中的"伪信号"失败模式**: 将 token 级师生散度分解为输入相关分量 A_grd 与前缀可预测的先验分量 A_prior,证明先验驱动的梯度能量可以很大但对输入特定任务方向几乎零对齐(Thm. 1),并指出此类零均值低-SNR 更新会在 SGD 下产生随步数线性增长的参数漂移(Thm. 2)。
- **提出轻量级输入依赖性代理**: 通过对比"原 prompt"与"残差无 prompt"(保留生成前缀、移除任务输入)两种条件下的 token 级散度,得到输入依赖缺口 Δ_IG,无需外部验证标签即可估计信号是否真正基于输入。
- **双轴过滤准则 + 动态 FLMR 约束**: 只过滤"低输入依赖 ∩ 高 |A_full|"的 token;通过 Filtered Loss-Mass Ratio (FLMR) 自适应调整过滤比例,避免在不同任务/阶段过度清洗有用监督。
- **跨模态一致提升**: 在 VLM(视觉理解/视觉推理)和 LLM(数学推理)共 11 个 benchmark 上稳定优于 Vanilla OPD、ExOPD、TIP、FiRe-OPD,尤其对 CountQA (+7.2) 和 Geo3K (+5.0) 等强先验/强模板任务提升显著。
- **诊断性证据**: 通过 FLMR 轨迹与过滤 token 的熵分布分析,证明 VLM 任务中的伪信号是持续性的"密集监督污染",而 LLM 数学任务的伪信号主要集中于训练初期的对齐阶段——这解释了 SA-OPD 在 VLM 上的更大增益。

## Key Contributions

- **Formalizes a new OPD failure mode — "spurious signals"**: decomposes the sampled teacher-student divergence A_t = A_t^grd + A_t^prior, proves (Thm. 1) that prior-induced gradients have weak input-specific alignment even when their energy is large, and (Thm. 2) that sustained zero-mean low-SNR OPD updates induce parameter drift whose expected squared magnitude grows linearly with optimization steps.
- **Lightweight input-groundedness proxy without external labels**: estimates whether a teacher signal truly depends on the input by comparing divergence computed under the original prompt versus a residual no-prompt context (same student-generated prefix, prompt removed). The Input-Grounding Gap Δ_IG_t = |A_t^full − A_t^res| is small when the signal is template-driven.
- **Two-axis token filter with adaptive FLMR**: removes only tokens satisfying low Δ_IG AND high |A_full|, with a dynamic bound FLMR(F) ≤ β controlling how much OPD loss mass is removed; prevents over-aggressive filtering on unstable VLM tasks.
- **Consistent cross-modal gains**: outperforms Vanilla OPD, ExOPD, TIP, and FiRe-OPD on every one of 11 benchmarks across VLM visual understanding, VLM visual reasoning, and LLM mathematical reasoning.
- **Diagnostic insight**: FLMR trajectories reveal that spurious signals in VLM distillation are persistent throughout training, while in LLM math they concentrate in the early alignment phase — explaining why input-groundedness filtering helps VLMs more.

## Methodology

SA-OPD is built on top of standard reverse-KL on-policy distillation (Lu and Thinking Machines Lab, 2025). The pipeline is:

1. **Spurious-signal diagnosis (Section 3.1).** For a student rollout y ~ π_θ(·|x), define the sampled teacher-student divergence A_t = log π_θ(y_t|x,y_<t) − log π_T(y_t|x,y_<t). Conceptually decompose A_t = A_t^grd + A_t^prior, where A_t^prior is predictable from the response context alone. The corresponding OPD gradient g_t^OPD = −A_t s_t decomposes into a grounded signal and a prior-driven nuisance. The authors prove (Thm. 1) that the prior component aligns weakly with the input-specific task direction (bounded by κ_t, which is small for template-like tokens), and (Thm. 2) that under a martingale-difference assumption, sustained prior-driven updates induce expected squared parameter drift η²Kv over K steps. This motivates a gradient-SNR framing where harmful tokens are those with large prior-driven energy but small grounded signal.

2. **Input-groundedness proxy (Section 3.2).** Because the conditional mutual information I(X; A_t | Y_<t) has no closed form, the authors estimate it empirically: re-score the same student prefix under a *residual no-prompt context* (∅, y_≤t) to obtain A_t^res, and define the Input-Grounding Gap Δ_IG_t = |A_t^full − A_t^res|. A large gap means the teacher signal is sensitive to the input; a small gap means it is dominated by generic priors.

3. **Two-axis filtering.** Define the candidate filtered set F(p1,p2) = Bottom_{p1}(Δ_IG_t) ∩ Top_{p2}(|A_t^full|) — i.e., tokens that are simultaneously in the lowest p1 fraction of input-groundedness and the highest p2 fraction of absolute distillation signal. The two axes are independently necessary: low-groundedness alone removes low-impact template tokens; high divergence alone removes useful grounded corrections.

4. **Dynamic FLMR constraint.** Because a fixed (p1,p2) removes very different loss fractions across tasks, SA-OPD adaptively rescales the filter so that the Filtered Loss-Mass Ratio FLMR(F) = Σ_{t∈F}|A_t^full| / (Σ_{t∈V}|A_t^full|+ε) stays bounded by β (best β = 1.8 on visual understanding, found by sweep in Fig. 4a).

5. **Filtered loss.** The reverse-KL objective is optimized only on retained tokens V \ F. SA-OPD adds no trainable parameters; overhead is one additional student pass and one additional teacher pass under the residual no-prompt context, measured at 2.64%–7.53% of total training time (Table 8).

Implementation: built on verl, 8× NVIDIA H20 GPUs, PyTorch 2.10, AdamW, lr 1e-6. Main teacher-student pairs are Qwen3-4B-Instruct → Qwen3-1.7B (LLM) and Qwen3.5-35B-A3B → Qwen3.5-2B (VLM); scalability pairs include DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B → Qwen3-1.7B and Qwen3.5-9B → Qwen3.5-2B. Training data: DeepMath (subset, ~7K examples) for math; VERO-600K (Captioning/IF, Grounding, Counting) for visual understanding; MMRL30k for visual reasoning.

## Results & Highlights

- **VLM visual understanding (Qwen3.5-35B-A3B → Qwen3.5-2B, Table 1):** SA-OPD raises the average from **50.5 (Vanilla OPD) → 54.0 (+3.5)**, the best result on all three sub-benchmarks. CountQA improves **26.4 → 33.6 (+7.2 over OPD, +3.4 over FiRe-OPD's 30.8)**; MMIFEval **53.5 → 55.2 (+1.7)**; EvoChart **71.6 → 73.2 (+1.6)**.
- **VLM visual reasoning (Table 1):** Average improves **60.4 (OPD) → 63.5 (+3.1)**, again best on every sub-benchmark. Geo3K **67.2 → 72.2 (+5.0 over OPD, +2.2 over TIP's 70.0)**; MathVista **69.0 → 72.2 (+3.2)**; MathVision **45.0 → 46.2 (+1.2)**.
- **LLM math reasoning (Qwen3-4B-Instruct → Qwen3-1.7B, Table 2):** Average improves **28.5 (OPD) → 30.4 (+1.9)**, best or tied-best on all five benchmarks. Math500 **66.4 → 69.6 (+3.2 over OPD, +3.0 over TIP's 66.6)**; AIME 2024 **9.2 → 11.7 (+2.5)**; AIME 2025 **6.2 → 8.3 (+2.1)**. SA-OPD is the only method that improves over OPD on AIME 2025.
- **Scalability (Table 3):** Positive gains hold across all tested teacher-student size pairs in both VLM and LLM settings — e.g., on the smaller Qwen3.5-9B → Qwen3.5-2B pair, MathVision improves 43.4 → 46.6 and CountQA 32.2 → 35.6; on the stronger DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B → Qwen3-1.7B pair, Math500 improves 58.4 → 60.6.
- **Ablation (Table 4, Geo3K / MathVista):** Each axis matters. Divergence-only filtering (69.8 / 69.6) and Input-Groundedness-only filtering (69.0 / 70.3) both lose to full SA-OPD (72.2 / 72.1); Proxy Replacement using teacher log-prob (69.6 / 71.5) also loses; Random Filter (67.6 / 67.8) barely beats base OPD (67.2 / 69.0). This confirms the two criteria are complementary.
- **Dynamic threshold (Fig. 4a):** with β = 1.8, CountQA score rises from 30.8 (no-dynamic baseline) to 33.6; both too-low and too-high β hurt, but the method is robust across a wide β range.
- **Computational overhead (Table 8):** SA-OPD adds only **+6.74% on math reasoning, +7.53% on visual understanding, +2.64% on visual reasoning** total training time — no extra parameters or backward passes.
- **Filtered-token analysis (Table 5):** 69.9% of removed tokens are content words (not just formatting); 60.2% have been seen before and 27.9% are part of a repeated bigram, indicating high predictability from local context. Crucially, filtered tokens are spread across the entropy spectrum (Fig. 4b), confirming entropy-based selection alone misses them.

## Authors & Credibility

Authors span **Zhejiang University** (Zhejiang University AI Institute, corresponding authors Huajun Chen and Tiankai Li), **ByteDance**, and **Shanghai AI Laboratory**. First author Yinuo Jiang notes the work was done during a ByteDance internship. The work sits squarely in an active 2025–2026 research line on selective OPD (TIP, FiRe-OPD, Token Teachability, Entropy-Aware OPD, ExOPD), and is positioned as a complement rather than a competitor to those criteria — all of which are properly cited and used as baselines under matched compute. Code is publicly released at https://github.com/jjjyinuo/SA-OPD. The paper includes two formal theorems with proofs (Appendix A), full training/evaluation configurations (Appendix B, Table 6), an explicit computational-overhead analysis (Appendix D, Table 8), and per-token filtering statistics (Table 5). Theoretical claims are kept appropriately modest (martingale-difference assumption, alignment bound rather than a strict inequality).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine. The framing of teacher token-level supervision as a potential source of *input-agnostic bias* — and the operationalization via a residual no-prompt divergence computation — is a new diagnostic axis that the existing selective-OPD literature (TIP's entropy, Token Teachability's learnability, FiRe-OPD's trajectory filtering) does not capture. The decomposition A_t = A_t^grd + A_t^prior and the SNR-style motivation are clean.
- **Rigor:** Strong on the theory side (two theorems with proofs, honest assumptions). The two-axis ablation (Table 4) cleanly isolates the contribution of each component, and the Proxy Replacement variant is a thoughtful control showing that the *student-score-based* input-grounding signal is better than a teacher-logprob-based surrogate.
- **Reproducibility:** Good. Hyperparameters, data subsets, model pairs, and the verl-based implementation are documented; code is public. However, the residual no-prompt context (∅, y_≤t) is not trivial to implement correctly — it requires KV-cache surgery to swap the prompt out while keeping the prefix — and the paper does not provide pseudocode for that step (only Algorithm 1 for the high-level procedure).
- **Significance:** The gains are consistent but modest in absolute terms on the harder math benchmarks. On AIME 2024/2025 the absolute numbers (11.7 / 8.3 Avg@8) are very low and within plausible run-to-run variance for such small sample sizes, so the "+2.5 / +2.1" deltas should be read cautiously. The VLM gains are more compelling, especially CountQA (+7.2) and Geo3K (+5.0), which are precisely the tasks where template/entity priors are most informative — consistent with the paper's thesis.
- **Red flags / weaknesses:**
  - **Narrow model coverage.** Only Qwen3/Qwen3.5 family models are evaluated (plus one DeepSeek-R1-distilled Qwen3-8B teacher). Whether the input-groundedness criterion behaves the same on Llama-, Mistral-, or Gemma-family teacher-student pairs is unaddressed.
  - **Small student models.** All students are ≤ 2B (1.7B Qwen3, 2B Qwen3.5). The paper does not test whether spurious signals remain a meaningful fraction of loss mass at, say, 7B–14B student scales, where language-prior contamination might be less severe.
  - **Single hardware/scale.** 8× H20 GPUs; no test of whether the +2.64–7.53% overhead holds for much longer VLM rollouts or larger batches.
  - **The no-prompt proxy is heuristic.** A_t^res is one particular operationalization of "response-context-predictable signal"; alternatives (e.g., perturbed/shuffled prompts, mean-pool of multiple null contexts) are not compared, so we don't know how sensitive Δ_IG is to the specific null context chosen.
  - **No direct measure of whether filtered tokens are actually wrong.** The paper argues they are *low-SNR*, not necessarily incorrect, and provides no human audit or counterfactual "what if we reweighted instead of removed" comparison beyond the FiRe-OPD baseline.
  - **Cherry-picked benchmark selection.** All six VLM benchmarks involve structured outputs (charts, counting, geometry) where templates matter; broader coverage of open-ended VQA or safety tasks could show a smaller effect.

## Strengths

- A genuinely new and well-motivated failure-mode framing ("spurious signals") with a clean theoretical foundation (gradient SNR, Thm. 1 & 2).
- The dual-context divergence computation is an elegant, training-free, label-free probe — no verifier or reward model required.
- Honest and thorough ablations, including the informative "Proxy Replacement" control that isolates *why* the student-score-based proxy works.
- Diagnostic depth: FLMR trajectories (Fig. 3) and filtered-token statistics (Table 5) actually explain *when and why* the method helps, rather than just showing numbers go up.
- Very low overhead (≤7.53%) and no new parameters — easy to plug into existing OPD pipelines.
- Public code release.

## Limitations

- **Model family and scale coverage** is narrow (Qwen3/Qwen3.5 only; students ≤ 2B). Generalization to Llama/Mistral families and to 7B+ students is unverified.
- **The residual no-prompt context** is a single, heuristic operationalization of "prior-driven signal"; sensitivity to the choice of null context is not studied.
- **AIME 2024/2025 absolute scores are low** (single-digit Avg@8), so the reported deltas there are within plausible evaluation noise for such small effective sample sizes.
- **No human audit** of whether filtered tokens are actually task-irrelevant; the argument is statistical (low SNR), not semantic.
- **Filtered tokens are removed, not reweighted** — a soft-reweighting variant (as in FiRe-OPD) is not directly compared against under the SA-OPD criterion, so it is unclear whether hard filtering is optimal.
- **VLM benchmark selection** leans toward structured-output tasks where template priors are most damaging; benefits on open-ended generation or instruction-following are not measured.

## Relevance

对于关注 LLM/VLM 后训练、知识蒸馏与 token-级强化学习/蒸馏选择机制的研究者,本文提供了一个直接可插入现有 OPD 流水线的轻量级过滤模块,且其"输入依赖性"诊断轴与已有的不确定性/可学习性/散度选择准则互补,可作为下一代理选择性蒸馏方法的强基线与设计灵感。如果你的研究方向涉及 reasoning model 的 distillation、teacher 信号的去噪或多模态蒸馏的稳定性,这篇论文值得精读并复现其过滤准则。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the input-groundedness criterion remain effective when the student is much larger (7B–14B), where language-prior contamination may be weaker and the marginal benefit of filtering smaller?
- How sensitive is Δ_IG to the specific null context? Would a *shuffled* or *paraphrased* prompt, or an average over multiple null contexts, give a more robust groundedness estimate than a single empty context?
- Could the two criteria (low Δ_IG, high |A|) be combined with *soft reweighting* instead of hard filtering, and would that recover additional gains on tasks like MathVista where SA-OPD's advantage over the Input-Groundedness-only ablation is small?
- The FLMR trajectory shows VLM spurious signals are persistent while LLM math ones decay — can this be exploited to schedule filtering more aggressively early in LLM training and gradually relax it, recovering compute?
- Does the method generalize beyond Qwen-family teachers (Llama, Mistral, Gemma), where pretraining-data-driven priors and thus the structure of A_t^prior may differ?
- Can the input-groundedness proxy be extended to *reinforcement learning* (e.g., GRPO/RLVR) token-level credit assignment, where the same template-prior contamination plagues advantage estimates?
- Is there a relationship between SA-OPD-filtered tokens and known hallucination/short-cut behaviors in VLMs (e.g., the counting failures CountQA measures)? A direct correlation study would strengthen the causal story.
