LLaDA MoE v2: Scaling Mixture-of-Experts Diffusion Language Models

Fengqi Zhu, Shaoxuan Xu, Jingyang Ou, Zebin You, Yipeng Xing, Huabin Liu, Xiaolu Zhang, Jun Zhou, Zhenzhong Lan, Yankai Lin, Wayne Xin Zhao, Jianguo Li, Chongxuan Li, Ji-Rong Wen
Gaoling School of AI, Renmin University of China · Ant Group
◆ Key Finding

The first systematic scaling characterization of MoE diffusion LLMs shows their optima diverge quantifiably from autoregressive models — bigger batches, faster LR decay, a stable 33.3% shared-expert ratio. Trained from scratch under these laws, LLaDA MoE v2 30B-A3B reaches the highest dLLM average on 15 benchmarks using only ~65% of Qwen3's pretraining tokens.

Why This Matters

Diffusion LLMs are not just AR-with-noise — they need their own training recipes. This work hands practitioners concrete formulas (B*, η*, M*, D*) and architecture rules that challenge AR-derived heuristics, and proves they scale by training a competitive 30B model.

Critical Edge

Laws fit at ≤3.6B / ≤3×10²⁰ FLOPs are extrapolated ~2–3 orders of magnitude to the 30B run.

  • No weights, code, or data release announced
  • Architecture axes swept independently — interactions unmeasured
  • No RL stage; gap to Qwen3 on AIME/MBPP/LiveCodeBench remains

Four power laws, fit specifically for diffusion LLMs

Joint batch-size × LR search and IsoFLOP sweeps on 158M–3.6B models yield dLLM-specific optima that diverge — quantitatively — from AR-derived laws like DeepSeek LLM's.

Optimal Batch Size
B* = 0.374 · C0.3481

Steeper growth than AR's C0.3271. At 10²⁰ FLOPs the dLLM law recommends 3.43M-token batches vs 1.02M under AR law — attributed to halved effective per-token supervision under uniform masking.
vs DeepSeek LLM AR: exponent +0.021

Optimal Learning Rate
η* = 64.8 · C−0.2447

Decays roughly twice as fast with compute as AR's C−0.1250. Same root cause: the masked denoising objective delivers less supervision per nominal token, requiring more careful step-size scheduling.
vs DeepSeek LLM AR: exponent −0.12

Compute-Optimal Model Size
M* = 0.5152 · C0.475

Measured in activated non-embedding FLOPs/token (not 6ND). Slightly below 0.5 — the frontier tilts toward data.
contextualized vs ~10 published laws (Table 2)

Compute-Optimal Data
D* = 1.9411 · C0.525

Data exponent 0.525 > 0.475 model exponent — more data-favoring than existing dense dLLM frontiers. Consistent with both sparse activation and diffusion modeling each pushing toward more data.

Extrapolation sanity check. At 6×10²⁰ FLOPs (Fig. 2), the fitted (B*, η*) sits next to the best observed grid cell (loss 1.228), supporting use of the laws beyond the fitting window — though the final 30B run still sits ~3 orders of magnitude further out.

MoE architecture rules — and where they break from AR

At five reference budgets (6×10¹⁷ – 6×10¹⁹ FLOPs), the authors fix M*(C) and sweep one of {activation ratio A, granularity G, shared-expert ratio S} while holding the other two fixed, training 3× the compute-optimal token count.

Three-stage scaling framework, then a 30B validation run

Masked discrete diffusion: a bidirectional Transformer denoises a corrupted sequence x_t where each clean token is replaced by [MASK] with probability t (sampled uniformly). Compute is measured as activated non-embedding FLOPs/token M (C = M·D), not the dense 6ND formula.

Strengths vs Limitations — the honest ledger

A high-novelty scaling-law study validated end-to-end at 30B. The rigor is real, but so are the extrapolation gap, the missing interaction study, and the absent release.

▲ Strengths

  • Clean, structured scaling study producing concrete usable formulas (B*, η*, M*, D*) — not vague guidance.
  • Mechanistic explanation: reduced effective supervision under uniform mask sampling ties empirical laws to the objective function.
  • Credible 30B-A3B end-to-end validation on 23.5T tokens — not just a small-model study.
  • Honest comparison against ~10 published allocation laws (Table 2) instead of cherry-picking.
  • Compute-controlled comparison vs prior un-guided LLaDA MoE 7B-A1B (Fig. 5) demonstrates the practical value of the guidance.
  • Claims are scoped ("approaches", "competitive") — no overclaiming of parity with Qwen3.

▼ Limitations

  • Laws fit at ≤3.6B / ≤3×10²⁰ FLOPs and extrapolated ~2–3 orders of magnitude; validated only weakly by a single 6×10²⁰ grid.
  • Architecture axes swept independently; interactions between A, G, S are not studied.
  • No RL post-training — instruct performance on math/code lags fully-tuned AR competitors on AIME, MBPP, LiveCodeBench.
  • Chinese-knowledge gaps (CEval −11.39, CMMLU −8.36) — data mix not disclosed.
  • No weights, code, or data release announced; pretraining corpus unspecified.
  • Evaluations mix self-run and cross-paper numbers, introducing drift.
