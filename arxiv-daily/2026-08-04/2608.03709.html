EMPIRICAL ML Training-dynamics prediction · Single-run telemetry

Predicting Deep Neural Network Training Outcomes from Early Training Telemetry.

Ranjita Naik · Anh D. Nguyen · Pankaj Kumar Singh — Georgia Institute of Technology
Key Finding
Using only one run's own first 5 epochs of telemetry — loss, accuracy, gradient signal-to-noise ratio, weight-norm growth — plus four sampled hyperparameters, gradient-boosted trees predict final test accuracy at R²=0.92–0.99 and relative class at ROC-AUC 0.983–0.998 across six architecture/dataset domains, with most signal already present after a single epoch.

Why This Matters
A reliable decision-support signal from a single run's own telemetry — no concurrent or historical cohort required. It slots on top of comparison-based schedulers (Hyperband, BOHB, Vizier) for early termination, compute reallocation, and failure flagging during HPO.
Critical Edge
The largest error cut comes from adding hyperparameters, not from internal telemetry (MAE ≈ 7.5→4.0 vs. a further +0.06 R²). Boundary-focused sampling may inflate early-horizon numbers, and there is no head-to-head against curve-extrapolation baselines on the same data.

01

How the prediction problem is set up

For a training run r with sampled hyperparameters h(r) and per-epoch telemetry τe(r), the information available at horizon k is Ik(r) = { h(r), τ1…τk }. Three targets are defined as functions of Ik alone:

(1) Regression — final test accuracy.
(2) Relative classification — does the run land above the domain median?
(3) Failure prediction — does the run fall below a pre-fixed usability threshold, or numerically diverge to NaN (treated as a first-class target, not a degenerate low-accuracy case)?

A two-phase sampler draws broadly first, fits a lightweight failure-probability classifier, then concentrates ~half of phase-2 draws near the success/failure boundary via rejection sampling — deliberately populating the hard region rather than the trivial bulk of a sweep.

Ablation · internal telemetry vs. curves only

Adding gradient-SNR + weight-norm growth lifts R² by +0.0602 on C10/ResNet-18.

95% CI [0.0518, 0.0685]; MAE cut 2.62 [2.43, 2.81]. All 18 of 18 confidence intervals (6 domains × R² + 12 AUC deltas) exclude zero.

But on FMNIST/MLP the lift is +0.0046 [0.0019, 0.0073] — statistically real, "too small to matter in most practical settings."

Model-class cross-check

A small feed-forward NN reaches MAE 3.17 / R² 0.966 / AUC 0.997 on C10/ResNet-18.

Statistically indistinguishable from gradient boosting (MAE 3.23). The signal lives in the telemetry, not in one model class — ruling out a tree-ensemble artifact.

04

Cross-architecture & cross-dataset transfer

Cross-dataset · scale breaks, rank survives

C10/ResNet-18 → FMNIST/ResNet-18: MAE 31.3, ~8× in-domain (3.8).

But Spearman holds at 0.52–0.89 and domain-relative class AUC 0.869–0.945 across all six cross-dataset pairs. The relationship transfers far better than the absolute numerical scale.

ResNet-18 ↔ SmallCNN: class AUC ≥ 0.979, Spearman ≥ 0.89 in both directions on both datasets.

The directional outlier

C10 / ResNet-18 → C10 / MLP: Spearman 0.604, AUC 0.716.

Well below every other one-factor pair — the reverse direction (MLP → ResNet-18) stays strong at AUC 0.935. Flagged but with only a hypothesised, not confirmed, mechanistic explanation.

05

Critical assessment — grades the authors would recognise

Evaluation hygiene
STRONG

Frozen holdout, leakage-safe horizon rules, paired ablation, Nadeau–Bengio CIs, pre-registered thresholds, dedup on hyperparameter hash, an entire Section 8 of candid limits. A model for empirical prediction studies.

Novelty
INCREMENTAL

Baker et al. 2018 already predicts a single run's outcome from early curves + hyperparameters. The genuine deltas: the controlled internal-telemetry ablation, and NaN-as-distinct-target. A reframing, not a new paradigm.

Baselines
WEAK

No head-to-head vs. the curve-extrapolation methods it positions against (Domhan 2015, Klein 2017) on the same data — dismissed as "complementary." The practical value-add over free off-the-shelf alternatives is unquantified.

Generalisation
MEDIUM

Two image datasets, three small nets, 4 hyperparameters, 15-epoch cap. No LLMs, ViTs, modern CNNs, or non-vision. No cross-seed repeats, so seed-to-seed variance at fixed hyperparameters is entirely unestimated.

Overclaim control
GOOD

Telemetry gains framed as "statistically consistent but sometimes practically small." Authors explicitly warn against automatic-termination use. Honest about boundary-enrichment and frozen-holdout proximity inflating early numbers.

Headline framing
CAUTION

The biggest single error cut comes from hyperparameters (MAE ≈ 7.5→4.0), not from internal telemetry (+0.06 R² on top). A reader could reasonably conclude the telemetry framing oversells relative to the boring fact that LR and batch size are themselves predictive.

06

What this paper actually contributes

  • 01Isolated single-run prediction paradigm. Every prediction is a function of one run's own telemetry + hyperparameters — no concurrent or historical cohort, unlike Hyperband/BOHB/Vizier's median-stopping.
  • 02Controlled, hyperparameter-free internal-telemetry ablation. Paired curves-only vs. curves-plus-internal on matched folds cleanly isolates the marginal contribution of gradient SNR and weight-norm growth.
  • 03Leakage-safe, multi-horizon evaluation. Every headline number from a 20% partition never touched during dev; repeated grouped holdout with Nadeau–Bengio-corrected intervals.
  • 04NaN as a distinct prediction target. Outright divergence is predicted directly, not folded into an accuracy threshold — a failure mode prior curve-extrapolators ignore.
  • 05One-factor-at-a-time transfer decomposition. Over 30 ordered domain pairs, separates scale mismatch from a genuinely different learned relationship.
