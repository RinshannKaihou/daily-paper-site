> **一句话总结：** 作者提出"匹配推理地平线 (matched-horizon) 配对探针"评测方法，对 Qwen3-14B 的 numeric/concision 提示与 gpt-oss-20b/-120b 的 effort 设置在同一推理 token 截断点上做配对比较，发现更短的推理常因模型提前完成而非真正改善了未完成前缀的答案质量——评测推理接口时应将"按时完成"、"被截断时给出的答案"、"两个未完成运行之间的差异"与"正确答案概率"分别报告。

> **TL;DR:** This paper introduces matched-horizon paired probes that compare two reasoning-interface conditions (Qwen3-14B's numeric/concision prompt and gpt-oss-20b/-120b's trained effort settings) at the same imposed token budget on GPQA Diamond (198) and MMLU-Pro (500). The Qwen prompt shortens reasoning by 12–17% with only small/mixed same-horizon accuracy changes; a concise/early-answer instruction lifts MMLU-Pro accuracy +3.8 points at 512 tokens. For gpt-oss, completed low/medium-effort answers beat matched-horizon high-effort prefixes by +14.5 to +26.3 points, but most of that advantage comes from lower effort finishing earlier — a tighter deadline can favor lower effort, while letting high effort finish recovers higher final accuracy.

## 核心贡献

- 提出并形式化"匹配地平线 (matched-horizon) 探针"评测框架：在同一外部施加的 token 截断点 B 上，对同一问题、同一生成 replicate 的两条运行做配对比较，将"按时正确完成"与"被强行截断时给出的答案"两类结果分开统计。
- 引入四种 readout（policy-context / common-context / both-active / behavioral continuation）和四层终止分层（both active、lower-complete/high-active、high-complete/lower-active、both complete），把"接口缩短推理"这一现象拆成两个独立机制：让模型更早完成 vs. 让未完成前缀给出更好的答案。
- 在 Qwen3-4B/8B/14B 与 gpt-oss-20b/120b 上系统验证了 token-exact replay + candidate-logit forced-answer 测量方法（98.3–100% 与解析得到的自然答案一致），并设计了 swapped-prefix / shuffled-token / random-token 长度匹配对照来证明答案信息确实来自问题特定前缀。
- 给出可操作的方法学建议：推理接口评测应同时报告 (i) 截止时间前的正确完成率，(ii) 截断未完成运行给出的答案，(iii) 两个未完成运行之间的差异，(iv) 正确答案上的概率质量——而非仅画"完成长度 vs. 最终准确率"曲线。
- 公开了代码 (github.com/AndresAlgaba/shorter_reasoning) 与数据 (Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21773770)，包含 prompt、closure、解析规则与统计流程。

## Key Contributions

- Formalizes **matched-horizon paired probes**: at a shared externally imposed token budget B, two reasoning-interface conditions are compared item- and replicate-matched, separating "did the model finish correctly by the deadline" from "what answer does a stopped unfinished run give."
- Defines **four readouts** (policy-context, common-context, both-active, behavioral continuation) and a **four-stratum termination decomposition** that cleanly attributes observed gaps to two distinct mechanisms — finishing earlier versus changing the answer inside an unfinished prefix.
- Validates the measurement stack (token-exact replay + full-vocabulary candidate-logit forced answer) against parsed natural answers (98.3–100% agreement), and uses length-matched swapped/shuffled/random-prefix controls to show recovered accuracy is question-specific, not a generic artifact of trace length.
- Translates the empirical findings into concrete protocol recommendations: reasoning-interface evaluations should separately report (i) correct completion before a deadline, (ii) the forced answer of a stopped run, (iii) differences among jointly active (still-reasoning) runs, and (iv) probability assigned to the correct answer.
- Releases full code and data, including the literal cap-then-answer validation, the nine-arm MMLU-Pro instruction grid, hidden-state probes, and a scripted positive control that moves from 25.0% to 100.0% at B=64.

## Methodology

The paper studies "reasoning control" interventions that change how much intermediate reasoning a model emits before its final answer, comparing them via **matched-horizon paired probes** rather than the conventional "final accuracy vs. completed trace length" plot.

**Formal setup.** For item i, replicate r, condition z, let Y^irz,ρ(B)∈{0,1} be correctness at horizon B under readout ρ. They define:
- Terminal-aware policy-context accuracy Y^z,T_pol(B): a trace that finishes before B keeps its natural terminal answer; an unfinished trace is forced via the candidate-logit probe.
- Common-context readout: replays stored reasoning IDs under one shared answer-time prompt so any difference is carried only by the generated prefixes.
- Both-active readout: applies common context only to pairs where both traces remain unfinished at B — diagnostic because conditioning on survival changes the population.
- Behavioral continuation: greedily generates a short final-channel answer from the stopped prefix instead of selecting from first-position logits.

**Two case studies (Figure 1):**
1. *Qwen3-14B numeric/concision prompt.* Compares ordinary generation ("surprise stop," budget hidden) with a textual prompt that announces a token limit ("bundled-prompt stop," budget visible). Both arms are cut at the same imposed B ∈ {512, 1024, 2048, 4096, 8192, 16384}.
2. *gpt-oss-20b/-120b effort settings.* Compares a completed low- or medium-effort reasoning prefix with high-effort reasoning at that same itemwise lower-effort terminal length L_c, under a common medium-effort answer context. High-effort tails are discarded; if high effort finishes before L_c, its completed block is replayed.

**Token-exact replay + candidate-logit scoring.** Stopped inputs are reconstructed from stored token IDs (no decode/retokenize); the probe scores every valid one-token spelling of each answer letter from full-vocabulary logits and aggregates by letter, reporting valid-option mass, conditional log loss, multiclass Brier score, and argmax accuracy.

**Models & data.** Qwen3-4B/-8B/-14B and gpt-oss-20b/-120b on GPQA Diamond (198 graduate-science, 4 choices), a category-stratified 500-item MMLU-Pro subset (10 choices), and Omni-MATH-2 (4,181 open-ended; 200-item subsamples). Three explicit generation replicates drive primary stochastic inference; Qwen3-4B/-8B calibrate the measurement.

**Controls & validation.** Swapped-trace / shuffled-token / random-token length-matched prefixes; a scripted positive control that moves 25.0%→100.0% at B=64; hidden-state readouts for budget decodability; rescue/anchoring analyses; a separate 500-item text-reconstructed Qwen subset replication; and a nine-arm MMLU-Pro instruction grid (eight replicates per item) testing whether matching the announced number to the imposed stop matters.

## Results & Highlights

- **Qwen3-14B numeric/concision prompt shortens traces by 12–17%** (geometric-mean reasoning length) with weak targeting of the announced count: GPQA adherence rises only from 1.9% at the tightest budget to 99.2% at the largest; within-item log L vs. log B slopes are 0.012 (GPQA) and 0.018 (MMLU-Pro), Spearman ρ = 0.058 / 0.121.
- **Same-horizon accuracy differences for the Qwen prompt are small and mixed**: GPQA Diamond estimates range −0.5 to +1.3 points; the four MMLU-Pro estimates are +2.0, +0.7, −0.9, and +0.1 points — only MMLU-Pro at B=512 has both a pointwise CI [+0.4, +3.7] and simultaneous band [+0.1, +3.9] above zero.
- **Concise/early-answer instruction on MMLU-Pro at B=512 raises terminal-aware accuracy 59.7% → 63.5% (+3.8 points, 95% CI [+2.1, +5.6])** and reduces median reasoning length from 1,083 to 679 tokens; its gain at B=2,048 is only +1.0 points ([−0.4, +2.4]).
- **The 512-token concise/early-answer gain decomposes**: correct natural completion rises 18.2% → 31.9% (+13.8 points); common-context replay of all prefixes retains +3.1 points ([+1.5, +4.8]); among the 2,476 both-active unfinished pairs the difference is +2.7 points ([+0.6, +5.0]).
- **gpt-oss matched-horizon accuracy gaps at the lower-effort terminal length are +14.5 to +26.3 points** across all eight model–benchmark–effort comparisons, every primary interval excluding zero; greedy continuations preserve the ordering at +15.5 to +25.9 points.
- **Most of the gpt-oss B=512 advantage is "finished earlier," not "better unfinished prefix"**: the lower-complete/high-active stratum contributes +3.4 to +15.8 points (largest positive component in all 8 comparisons), while both-active pairs are smaller and mixed (−1.0 to +3.2 points, no within-stratum interval excluding zero).
- **Proper scores qualify the accuracy win**: Brier favors the terminal lower-effort arm in 7/8 (intervals exclude zero in 5), but conditional log loss favors the high-effort prefix in all 8 comparisons with all intervals excluding zero — driven by 20.9%–36.0% of probed terminal answers assigning the correct option <10⁻⁴ probability vs. 0.2%–11.7% for matched high-effort prefixes.
- **Qwen3-14B terminal-aware policy accuracy on MMLU-Pro rises 50.4% (no reasoning) → 60.0% (B=512) → 70.8% (B=2,048) → 76.6% (B=8,192)**, approaching the cohort-matched full-trace accuracy of 76.8% (384/500); survival falls to 18.7% (Qwen3-14B) and 9.1% (gpt-oss-20b) of GPQA trajectories still reasoning at B=8,192.
- **Length-matched prefix controls** confirm question-specificity: swapping in another item's reasoning costs −18.1 points (gpt-oss-20b) and −17.8 points (Qwen3-14B); shuffled-token and random-token controls fall between original and swap.
- **Token-exact replay validation**: 98.3–100.0% agreement between replayed-prefix candidate-logit answers and parsed natural-terminal answers; no final-channel delimiter in 2,788 terminal gpt-oss replay prefixes.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Genuine methodological contribution. The "matched-horizon paired probe" formalism (separating "finished earlier" from "better answer in unfinished prefix") and the four-stratum termination decomposition are not standard in the reasoning-control literature, which overwhelmingly plots final accuracy vs. completed trace length. The connection to anytime-algorithms / rational-metareasoning is well-motivated.
- **Rigor**: Strong on measurement hygiene — token-exact replay (no retokenization artifacts), candidate-logit scoring validated to 98.3–100% agreement with parsed natural answers, length-matched swapped/shuffled/random-prefix controls, a scripted positive control (25%→100% at B=64), and pre-registered-style sensitivity analyses (replicate-specific, two-way item × replicate, crossed-intercept). The honesty about the Brier-vs-log-loss divergence (an incorrect completed trace concentrates mass on its chosen letter) is exemplary: they explicitly note that "a better selected answer need not imply uniformly better probability quality."
- **Reproducibility**: High. Public code, public data, named model checkpoints on HuggingFace, explicit vLLM/PyTorch/Transformers versions, and a stated three-replicate design with stored token IDs and seeds for the primary cohorts.
- **Significance**: Methodological guidance (report four outcomes separately) is immediately actionable for the test-time-compute community. The two empirical case studies are valuable but family-specific.
- **Red flags / weaknesses**:
  - **Within-family only, not causal**: The authors are admirably explicit that "these within-family case studies … do not provide a causal comparison between prompts and trained effort settings." The Qwen prompt intervention and the gpt-oss effort intervention are non-comparable, so no cross-family claim can be made.
  - **Small stochastic sample**: Only three generation replicates drive primary inference; text-reconstructed Qwen subset and prompt-control cohorts lack token IDs and verified seeds and are explicitly demoted to descriptive replication rather than primary token-exact estimates. The single-rollout Qwen3-8B and Omni-MATH-2 numbers describe only the sampled trajectories.
  - **Forced-answer readout affects unfinished-prefix accuracy**: The candidate-logit forced answer is not a natural continuation; near-zero correct-option probabilities distort gpt-oss log loss. They mitigate with Brier, valid-option mass, continuations, and a scripted positive control, but the gpt-oss log-loss headline should be read with this caveat.
  - **The proper-score table stops at B=2,048** because the jointly active sets at B=8,192/16,384 contain only 25/1 GPQA Diamond items and 19/2 MMLU-Pro items — late-horizon proper-score claims are untestable with this sample.
  - **Benchmarks are narrow for a methodology paper**: GPQA Diamond (198) and a 500-item MMLU-Pro subset, plus a single-rollout 200-item Omni-MATH-2 draw. Open-ended generation is under-explored relative to multiple-choice.
  - **Mixed gpt-oss numbers when adding numeric-budget wording** to the effort instruction (10 of 12 intervals span zero; one is −5.6 points on GPQA Diamond) — a useful negative result, but it does narrow the "tight deadline favors lower effort" headline.

## Strengths

- Conceptually clean separation of "finish earlier" vs. "better unfinished answer" via the four-stratum decomposition; this is the paper's main intellectual payoff and is directly reusable by future reasoning-interface work.
- Exceptionally careful measurement: token-exact replay, candidate-logit forced answer, full-vocabulary proper scores, length-matched prefix controls, scripted positive control, hidden-state probes, and rescue/anchoring follow-ups.
- Intellectual honesty throughout: every claim is paired with the readout it depends on, every interval is reported, and the authors repeatedly flag what their design cannot establish (no causal cross-family comparison, no equivalence test for the matching-number null, single-rollout caveats).
- Concrete, actionable protocol recommendations (the four-outcome reporting checklist) rather than a single benchmark leaderboard number.
- Public code, data, and model checkpoints with pinned versions.

## Limitations

- Two model families (Qwen3, gpt-oss) with non-comparable interventions restrict all claims to within-family; no causal prompt-vs-trained-effort comparison.
- Only three generation replicates for the primary token-exact analysis; secondary text-reconstructed cohorts lack seeds/token IDs and are descriptive only.
- The forced candidate-logit readout affects unfinished-prefix accuracy and inflates gpt-oss log loss via near-zero correct-option probabilities; results rely on the battery of mitigations (Brier, valid-option mass, continuations) rather than a single clean score.
- Benchmarks are mostly multiple-choice (GPQA Diamond, MMLU-Pro); open-ended Omni-MATH-2 evidence is single-rollout and category-stratified on a 200-item draw.
- Late-horizon proper scores are untestable because too few trajectories survive past B=2,048.
- No behavioral-misalignment or competing-objective test — the paper itself notes the tasks "contain no competing objective or safety constraint."

## Relevance

对关注 LLM 推理、test-time compute 与评测方法学的研究者高度相关：它正好落在 cs.LG / cs.CL / cs.AI 的核心交叉点，提出的"匹配地平线配对探针 + 四结果分别报告"协议可直接用于评估自家推理模型/接口在 deadline 约束下的真实表现，并能解释"更短推理"是来自提前完成还是未完成前缀的答案改善。对从事推理 budget 控制、early-stopping、anytime 推理或 reasoning model 评测工具开发的工作尤其有借鉴价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the four-stratum decomposition give the same "finish earlier vs. better unfinished prefix" split on genuinely open-ended tasks (long-form math, code generation, agentic tool use) where the candidate-logit forced answer is not available?
- How do the matched-horizon conclusions change with >3 generation replicates and a properly powered equivalence test for the matching-number null (currently only descriptive intervals)?
- Could the both-active common-context contrast be turned into a cheap online signal for switching effort mid-generation, since it isolates the "better unfinished answer" component that is independent of completion timing?
- The gpt-oss log-loss divergence (high-effort prefixes win on log loss despite losing on argmax) suggests calibration-aware stopping rules — does training a lightweight confidence head on the candidate-logit mass beat raw early-stopping on a cost-calibration frontier?
- Do hidden-state budget-decodability results (balanced accuracy 0.938 at the prompt boundary, falling to label-shuffle after reasoning starts) generalize to models trained with explicit reasoning-budget tokens (e.g., BudgetThinker, L1)?
- The paper deliberately avoids safety/competing-objective scenarios — does "finish earlier vs. better unfinished answer" interact differently when reasoning under an alignment constraint that might pressure the model to stop prematurely?
