> **一句话总结：** 本文将推理 LLM 的 test-time scaling 形式化为自回归模型隐式前缀树上的预算推理，区分出 single-trajectory / leaf-level / prefix-level 三类推理范式，并提出基于完整推理系统（含解码、聚合、停止、验证器与预算）的评估与可复现性框架；作者在 MMLU-Pro、BBH、AIME/HMMT 竞赛数学与 SuperGPQA 上发布超过 195 万条带 token 级信号的推理轨迹，实证表明候选发现 (Pass@k) 与最终提交准确率之间存在显著差距，且不同预算/排名指标下模型排序会改变。

> **TL;DR:** The paper formalizes test-time scaling (TTS) for reasoning LLMs as budgeted inference over the implicit prefix tree of an autoregressive model and distinguishes three regimes — single-trajectory sequential scaling, leaf-level scaling with terminal reduction, and prefix-level scaling — then proposes an evaluation and reproducibility framework that treats the whole inference system (decoder, reducer, verifier, stopping controller, budget) as the unit of analysis. Empirically, on MMLU-Pro, BBH, AIME'24–'26 / HMMT'25–'26 / CMIMC'25 / SMT'25, and SuperGPQA, the authors release 1,948,821 reasoning traces and show that on the strongest single-response config (Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking-2507), Pass@80 reaches 91.67% while mean-log-prob Best-of-N selection drops accuracy from 75.56% to 65.83% as the bank grows — a ~10 pp non-monotone gap caused by reducer misalignment rather than candidate availability.

## 核心贡献

- **统一的形式化框架**：把 test-time scaling 定义为自回归模型隐式前缀树 `T(x)` 上的预算推理算法族 `{A_B}`，并据此划分出三种结构范式——单轨迹顺序扩展（如 s1 budget forcing）、叶级采样+终端归约（Self-Consistency、Best-of-N、MBR）、前缀级搜索（Tree-of-Thoughts、RAP、MCTS、value-guided decoding）。这统一了原本被混在一起的 "budget" 标量概念。
- **以推理系统为评估对象**：明确"被评估的不是 checkpoint，而是 checkpoint + prompt + decoder + controller/reducer + verifier/judge + budget + stopping rule 的完整协议"。区分共享银行（shared-bank）比较与端到端比较、post-hoc 选择与 causal stopping，并要求匹配协议的计算成本与不确定性估计。
- **Discovery–Stability Profile**：提出一个统一描述重复采样的 profile `S_k = (S_{k,1}, ..., S_{k,k})`，并证明 Pass@k、pass_k、Maj@k、G-Pass@k_τ、mG-Pass@k、Geom@k 都是它的坐标或简单泛函，附带贝塔-二项后验和有限银行无放回版本。
- **可复现性分层**：区分精确回放（exact replay）与分布可复现（distributional reproducibility），明确指出"只改 seed 不等于完整不确定性分析"以及贪心解码因数值精度也不一定可复现，规定了必须释放的产物清单（渲染后 prompt、token 级元数据、验证器/裁判转录、子集种子等）。
- **大规模开放推理轨迹语料**：发布 1,948,821 条涵盖广知识、符号推理、竞赛数学的完整推理轨迹，含 width-20 token 备选分布、reference-free 与 reference-assisted 两类验证器信号，供训练/校准验证器、可复现研究使用。
- **生态系统梳理**：按"模型侧机制"（verified SFT/distillation、RL/preference、native reasoner、fusion/merge）与"接口侧机制"（reasoning-budget 控制、hybrid modes）组织并比较代表性开放权重推理模型。

## Key Contributions

- **Unified formalization** of test-time scaling as budgeted inference over the implicit prefix tree, yielding a three-regime taxonomy (single-trajectory sequential, leaf-level with terminal reduction, prefix-level search) that makes the confounding across "budget" reported in prior work explicit.
- **System-level evaluation principles** that separate end-to-end system performance from candidate-bank diagnostics, post-hoc selection from causal stopping, and shared-bank aggregation comparisons from full-protocol comparisons, with matched compute accounting decomposed as `C_total = C_gen + C_signal + C_control + C_decision`.
- **A discovery–stability profile** `S_k` over binomial-tail kernels from which Pass@k, pass_k, Maj@k, G-Pass@k_τ, mG-Pass@k, and Geom@k are recovered or bounded; the paper provides both latent (beta-binomial predictive) and finite-bank (without-replacement) versions plus Wilson/bootstrap uncertainty procedures.
- **A reproducibility framework** distinguishing exact replay (deterministic kernels, fixed schedules, serialized banks) from distributional reproducibility (uncertainty over independent executions), with concrete artifact checklists and warnings that single-seed runs, seed-only variation, and even greedy decoding are not reliable replicability evidence.
- **A 1,948,821-trace release** (over 2 billion tokens) spanning MMLU-Pro (all 14 domains), BBH (23 tasks), AIME'24–'26, HMMT'25–'26, CMIMC'25, SMT'25, BrUMO'25, and a field-balanced SuperGPQA subset, including width-20 token alternatives and two verifier families.
- **A model-ecosystem organization** of open-weight reasoning models along model-side and interface mechanisms, so comparisons are grounded in the levers that affect reasoning quality, controllability, and cost.

## Methodology

The paper builds the framework in three layers.

**Formal layer (Section 2).** Given input `x` and autoregressive model `p_θ`, the finite reachable prefixes form an implicit rooted tree `T(x)` with leaves `L(x)`. A local generation policy `π` induces a proposal `q_π(y|x)` over completed leaves (which may differ from `p_θ` because of truncation/renormalization). A budgeted inference algorithm `A_B` adaptively interleaves primitives (token generation, prefix expansion, verifier/judge calls, terminal reduction) subject to `Σ c(o_t) ≤ B`, returning either a leaf `ŷ_B(x)` or extracted answer `â_B(x)`. The scaling curve is `G(B) = E_{x,ξ}[U_x(ŷ_B(x;ξ))]`. The three regimes are then defined by *where* extra compute is spent: along one active state (single-trajectory, e.g. s1 budget forcing), across `N` i.i.d. completed leaves followed by a reducer `R_N` (leaf-level, covering verifier-constrained selection, self-consistency as empirical MBR, learned Best-of-N reranking, and MBR), or over an active frontier of unfinished prefixes (prefix-level, with beam/best-first/MCTS controllers and likelihood / outcome-value / PRM / rollout / sound-partial-check scorers). The paper proves self-consistency is exactly empirical MBR under agreement utility, and that only *sound* prefix tests justify safe pruning — learned value/reward models generally do not.

**Evaluation layer (Section 3).** The evaluated object is the full inference system. Benchmark design is reframed by evaluation structure (verifiable vs. open-ended, single-turn vs. interactive, contamination-controlled). The induced proposal `q_π` is derived explicitly by composing one-token energy–gate kernels `q̂_e(i) = u_t(i)·1{i∈S_t} / Σ_{j∈S_t} u_t(j)`; the authors give a collapse theorem showing any parallel composition of top-k / nucleus / probability-threshold / logit-band head gates reduces to the strictest retained prefix, but locally-typical sampling and measure-changing rules escape this. Aggregation is split into evidence map `E_η` and fixed-bank decision `G_δ`, with causal stopping controllers `τ_roll` and `τ_tok` that may inspect only revealed history. The discovery–stability profile `S_{k,t} = (1/Q)Σ_q κ_{k,t}(p_q)` with binomial-tail kernel `κ_{k,t}` is the central bank-level object; scalar metrics are functionals of it. Reproducibility is separated into exact replay vs. distributional reproducibility, with protocol-matched bootstrap/Wilson uncertainty required for the latter.

**Empirical layer (Section 4 + Appendix G).** Four release blocks use *fixed* response banks so new reducers can be replayed without regeneration: (i) MMLU-Pro + BBH with 27 models, 1 response/question (500,661 traces); (ii) 80 seeded responses × 20 configs × 120 AIME/HMMT/BrUMO questions (192,000 traces) for Pass@k and reducer replay; (iii) 186 problems across five 2025–2026 MathArena competition sets with Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and three gpt-oss-20b effort levels, recording width-20 token alternatives; (iv) field-balanced SuperGPQA (3,600 questions, 4 banks, 80 responses each). Reducers are replayed on nested uniform subsets with counter-derived seeds; intervals resample questions after integrating over 2,000–10,000 subset replays.

## Results & Highlights

- **Candidate discovery vastly outpaces answer selection.** Across the 20-config AIME/HMMT/BrUMO roster, median Pass@k rises from **56.49% at k=1 to 82.08% at k=80**, while the median all-correct coordinate (pass_k) falls to **15.00%**, exposing within-question instability that single-sample accuracy hides.
- **Non-monotone Best-of-N on the strongest single-response config.** For **Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking-2507** (highest single-response accuracy at **75.56%**), Pass@80 reaches **91.67%** but selecting by highest mean token log-probability *drops* submitted accuracy from **75.56% to 65.83%** as the bank grows — a ~10 pp *decrease* caused by reducer misalignment, while sequence-log-probability Best-of-N reaches **77.50%** and literal plurality **78.33%**. Reference-assisted CompassVerifier (gold-answer leak) reaches **89.17%** but is not deployable.
- **Persistent reducer–ceiling gaps on 2025–2026 competition math.** At k=80, Pass@80 is **94.62%** for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and **72.58% / 91.94% / 93.55%** for gpt-oss-20b low/medium/high; the corresponding reference-free pointwise Best-of-N accuracies are only **86.56% / 58.06% / 75.81% / 81.72%**, leaving **8.06–16.13 pp gaps** where a correct candidate exists but is not selected. Even taking the better of plurality and pointwise selection per bank leaves a **5.38–14.52 pp** gap to Pass@80.
- **Rankings are not robust to the rubric or budget.** Mean Spearman agreement with the N=80 accuracy ranking is **0.967 at k=1** and **0.992 at k=8** on the math roster — high but not perfect, and these are descriptive properties of the 20 configs, not a general small-sample guarantee.
- **Effort drives length, not just accuracy.** Among high-effort gpt-oss responses, **2,399 of 14,880 (16.12%)** hit the 81,920-token cap, versus none at low effort, with overlapping bootstrap intervals preventing a total ordering of generators.
- **Broad roster spreads.** MMLU-Pro exact match ranges **3.13%–70.47%** and BBH flexible-extraction exact match ranges **25.97%–82.20%** across the 27 open-weight reasoning models.

First author Mohsen Hariri has three directly relevant prior first-author papers cited in the reproducibility section: an ACL 2026 paper on *ranking reasoning LLMs under test-time scaling*, an ICLR 2026 paper on a *Bayesian framework for LLM evaluation (Don't pass@k)*, and an ACL 2026 Findings paper on quantization — meaning the formal framework here is a direct continuation of a coherent, peer-reviewed research line rather than a one-off arXiv preprint. ## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** The three-regime prefix-tree taxonomy is a clean synthesis rather than a fundamentally new algorithm; the components (CoT, self-consistency, ToT/RAP, MCTS, PRM, MBR) are all known. The genuine novelty is the *unifying formal lens* (budgeted inference over `T(x)`), the discovery–stability profile that subsumes Pass@k/pass_k/Maj@k/G-Pass@k/mG-Pass@k/Geom@k as functionals of one object, and the rigorous separation of exact replay vs. distributional reproducibility with protocol-matched uncertainty. For a framework paper this is appropriately novel.
- **Rigor.** Strong. Proofs are given for the MBR equivalence of self-consistency, the parallel-gate collapse theorem (Eq. 6–7), and the profile-functional identities. Compute accounting is decomposed into `C_gen + C_signal + C_control + C_decision`. The non-monotone Best-of-N result on Qwen3-30B-A3B (75.56% → 65.83% under mean-log-prob selection) is a concrete, citable demonstration of reward/evidence overoptimization, consistent with Gao et al. 2023 and Huang et al. 2025.
- **Reproducibility.** Excellent in principle and largely in practice: frozen dataset revisions (Table 5 with full commit hashes for the five MathArena sets), deterministic SuperGPQA materialization with source/selected SHA-256 digests (Table 6), counter-derived per-question seeds, nested uniform subsets, and a released analysis table with subset seeds, selection rates, and eligibility rates. Honest gaps are stated — e.g. the 2024–2025 archive lacks a full next-token alternative distribution and neither math block has process-reward scores or a validated answer-start boundary, and the authors refuse to synthesize those missing signals.
- **Weak baselines / confounds.** The reference-free verifier is *Qwen3.6 judging Qwen3.6-generated responses* in the gpt-oss banks, and the paper itself flags (around line 3360) that "Qwen judging Qwen-generated responses" yields AUC 0.910 on Qwen traces vs 0.876 on gpt-oss traces and "does not identify a causal self-evaluation effect" — a real evaluator–generator coupling confound that limits the cross-model verifier comparisons. The empirical study is also entirely *fixed-bank / post-hoc*: it cannot measure savings from causal stopping or from a different generation policy, which the authors acknowledge.
- **Sample size and generalization.** The 20-config math study uses only **120 questions** (30 each from AIME'24/'25, HMMT'25, BrUMO'25), and the signal-rich 2025–2026 block uses only **186 problems**. The authors are explicit that the Spearman 0.967/0.992 numbers are "descriptive properties of the 20 configurations, not a general small-sample guarantee," but headline reducer-gap numbers (8.06–16.13 pp) still rest on small question counts where prompt-bootstrap intervals overlap. Several claimed generator orderings are explicitly withheld because of overlapping intervals, which is the right call.
- **Significance.** High for the evaluation/reproducibility community: it gives a vocabulary and a checklist that future TTS papers can be held to, and the 1.95M-trace corpus (with width-20 token alternatives and two verifier families) is a genuinely useful artifact for training/calibrating verifiers. Lower direct significance for someone seeking a *new* inference algorithm — this is not that paper.
- **Red flags.** None severe. The "over 2 billion full reasoning traces" phrasing in the abstract counts *tokens*, while the headline corpus is 1,948,821 *responses* — technically accurate but slightly generous sounding. The paper is positioned partly as a survey (Appendices D–E organize the ecosystem), which inflates the page count to 59 and could be mistaken for a methods-only paper.

## Strengths

- The prefix-tree formalization cleanly separates the three regimes and makes it obvious *why* a single scalar "budget" is inadequate — the same FLOPs spent on one trajectory vs. 80 leaves vs. a search frontier have different statistical structure, proposal distributions, and failure modes.
- The discovery–stability profile is a unifying theoretical object that should replace ad-hoc metric juggling in future TTS papers; showing Pass@k, Maj@k, G-Pass@k_τ, mG-Pass@k, Geom@k are all coordinates/functionals of one profile is elegant and actionable.
- The empirical demonstration that mean-log-prob Best-of-N is *non-monotone* (75.56% → 65.83%) is a sharp, citable result that concretely illustrates reducer overoptimization on the strongest config in the roster.
- Exceptional reproducibility hygiene: frozen commit hashes, SHA-256 digests for deterministic sampling, declared numerical/serving settings, and explicit refusal to fabricate missing signals.
- The released 1.95M-trace corpus with width-20 token alternatives and both verifier families is a substantial community resource for verifier training and trace analysis.

## Limitations

- **No new inference algorithm.** The paper formalizes, evaluates, and releases data but does not propose a deployable new decoder, search controller, or reducer; readers seeking algorithmic advances will not find them here.
- **Small evaluation sets.** 120 and 186 competition questions limit statistical resolution; several claimed ordering differences are explicitly not significant, and even the significant reducer-gap numbers carry overlapping bootstrap intervals.
- **Evaluator–generator coupling.** The reference-free verifier is Qwen3.6 judging Qwen-generated traces (and gpt-oss traces), with a measurable self-evaluation asymmetry (AUC 0.910 vs 0.876) that the authors flag but cannot fully deconfound.
- **Post-hoc / fixed-bank only.** All reducer comparisons are replayed on fixed banks, so the study cannot quantify savings from adaptive causal stopping or from changing the generation policy — it isolates aggregation but at the cost of not evaluating the full deployed system end-to-end.
- **Coverage gaps in older archives.** The 2024–2025 block lacks next-token alternative distributions; neither math block has process-reward scores or validated answer-start boundaries, so PRM-based reducers cannot be run on these banks.
- **Token-cost frontier not measured.** Prefix-level search and KV-cache reuse (e.g. DEFT) are discussed theoretically but not benchmarked; wall-clock and evaluator-cost frontiers are prescribed but not empirically plotted.
- **Survey bloat.** Appendices D–E catalog the open-weight reasoning ecosystem at length, which is useful context but makes the paper read partly as a survey, inflating it to 59 pages.

## Relevance

For research on test-time scaling, inference-time search/verification, and LLM reasoning evaluation, this paper is directly on-target: it provides the formal vocabulary (prefix-tree regimes, discovery–stability profile, system-level evaluation) and a large open trace corpus that any follow-up work on Best-of-N, self-consistency, PRM/value-guided search, or reasoning-benchmark reproducibility will likely cite and be measured against. Its emphasis on protocol-matched compute accounting and uncertainty propagation is also highly relevant to anyone building reasoning-model leaderboards or doing honest benchmark comparisons.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How do the discovery–stability profile and the reducer–ceiling gap behave under *prefix-level* search (ToT/RAP/MCTS) rather than leaf-level i.i.d. sampling, where the returned bank is not i.i.d. and the profile is only a descriptive summary?
- Can the evaluator–generator coupling (Qwen-judging-Qwen, AUC 0.910 vs 0.876) be removed by training a model-agnostic reference-free verifier on the released width-20 token-alternative signals, and would that close the 8–16 pp reducer gap on competition math?
- What are the wall-clock and `C_signal + C_control + C_decision` frontiers for causal stopping controllers (Adaptive-Consistency, DeepConf, Early-Stopping Self-Consistency) on these same banks, given that the fixed-bank replay methodology here structurally cannot measure stopping savings?
- Does the non-monotone mean-log-prob Best-of-N result (75.56% → 65.83% on Qwen3-30B-A3B) generalize beyond this single config, and is it predictable from cheap per-bank calibration of the score–correctness correlation?
- How sensitive are the small-N competition-math reducer gaps (5.38–14.52 pp) to question difficulty stratification, and would a contamination-controlled, difficulty-calibrated benchmark shrink or enlarge them?
