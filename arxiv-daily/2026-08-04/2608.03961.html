Interpretable Adaptive Sampling for LLM Test-Time Scaling

Mobina Kashaniyan · Ali Jannesari Iowa State University arxiv.org/abs/2608.03961
◆ Key Finding

A hand-designed hierarchical fuzzy controller replaces fixed per-query sampling budgets, mapping human-readable signals (prompt complexity, token confidence, entropy, prompt type, expected answer length, historical performance) into a per-prompt integer budget — and under a strict fair-alignment protocol it matches a fixed N=8 selector within 0.002–0.018 accuracy while cutting samples by 1.4–14.5%.

Models: Phi-3-mini-4k · Qwen2.5-1.5B  |  Benchmarks: GSM8K · MATH · SciQ  |  Nmax = 8
1 Key Metric
−14.5% samples
MATH · Qwen2.5-1.5B · acc 0.297 vs 0.315 (Δ −0.018)
Largest sample reduction reported. On MATH with Phi-3-mini the cut is −10.8% (acc 0.578 vs 0.585). GSM8K savings are smaller: −1.4% / −3.9%.
2 Why This Matters
Makes LLM test-time compute auditable, not black-box. For any prompt the controller reports which signals fired, which fuzzy rules activated, and how each refinement shifted the budget — turning "how much to sample" into an inspectable policy.
Real value is the methodological template (selector-matched controls) more than the specific rule set.
3 Critical Edge
Headline deltas are unpaired. The reported Δ −0.002 on GSM8K sits within the stated Wilson interval width — no paired McNemar / bootstrap was run. Ablation also shows removing entropy lifts MATH to 0.585, evidence the hand-set rules are suboptimal.
Authors disclose this transparently rather than hide it.
§ 01

The six-stage pipeline

Budget policy is decoupled from answer selection — only the sample count varies.
STAGE 01
Input prompt
Raw query enters; surface & NLP features extracted.
STAGE 02
Difficulty / uncertainty estimation
c, γ, η, τ, λ, π normalized to [0,1].
STAGE 03
Hierarchical fuzzy controller
Two-stage Type-2 inference → coarse label + refinement.
STAGE 04
Dynamic budget N(x)
N(x)=max(1, round(1+s′(Nmax−1))), nudged by αu.
STAGE 05
Candidate generation
Sample N(x) answers, T=1.0, top-p=0.95.
STAGE 06
Self-certainty + Borda
Rank by self-certainty, aggregate answers via Borda.

§ 03

Strengths & limitations

Unusually careful hygiene for a preprint — and honestly disclosed cracks.

Strengths

  • Fair-alignment protocol. Matched decoding, fixed self-certainty+Borda selector, disabled draft pass, and a selector-matched fixed-N=8 control that genuinely isolates the budget policy.
  • Real interpretability. Any prompt can emit which signals fired, which rules activated, and how each refinement shifted N(x).
  • Honest framing. Positioned as an accuracy–compute tradeoff with full Wilson intervals and explicit acknowledgment that Adaptive does not always beat fixed N=8.
  • Self-undermining ablations reported. Removing entropy improving MATH is shown rather than hidden — rare and commendable.
  • Beyond math reasoning. Evaluated on SciQ factual QA, plus a compute-optimal baseline inspired by Snell et al.

Limitations

  • Hand-designed controller. Refinement weights (0.15/0.10 NLP features, α=0.2) are not optimized; ablations show some signals are neutral or harmful.
  • Small scale. Two sub-3B models, Nmax capped at 8, 1,319-item MATH subset — frontier-scale & large-budget generalization untested.
  • Answer-selection bottleneck. If a correct candidate is generated but not selected, better budgeting cannot rescue it.
  • No paired significance tests. Some Δ values (e.g. −0.002 GSM8K) are smaller than the reported interval width.
  • Modest absolute gains. The win is sample reduction, not accuracy; GSM8K savings are only 1.4–3.9%. SciQ exact-match is harsh for short factual answers.
−0.018worst Δ · MATH

The sharpest internal crack: in the component ablation (Table 4), removing entropy lifts MATH accuracy to 0.585 and neutralizing confidence forces budget to 8.00 and lifts MATH to 0.601. The authors frame this as evidence the controller is a transparent policy class, not an individually-optimized one — which is exactly why paired significance testing matters.

§ 04

Credibility & open questions

Methodologically careful; experimentally modest.
Authors
Mobina Kashaniyan · Ali Jannesari — Iowa State University, Ames, IA. Jannesari directs the ISU lab (parallelization, profiling, interpretable ML).
Status
Preprint, arXiv:2608.03961v1 — 4 Aug 2026. Not peer-reviewed.
Reproducibility
Models & datasets public (Phi-3-mini-4k-instruct, Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct; GSM8K 1,319 / MATH 1,319 / SciQ 1,000). Decoding fully specified (T=1.0, top-p=0.95, len=256, Nmax=8). Fuzzy rules & membership functions described in reimplementable detail. Code release not clearly promised; exact MATH subset selection unspecified.
Novelty
Moderate. Snell et al. (adaptive test-time compute), Huang et al. (self-calibrated confidence), and fuzzy controllers all exist individually; contribution is the combination framed around interpretability + the rigorous fair-alignment protocol.
Does it actually beat fixed N=8 on accuracy?
Not strictly. On MATH it is slightly behind (Δ −0.007 / −0.018); on GSM8K essentially tied (Δ −0.002 / +0.005). On SciQ it does beat self-certainty-only and Best-of-N. The honest claim is accuracy–compute parity at lower sample count, not a pure accuracy win.
Are the small deltas statistically significant?
Unknown. Table 1 reports unpaired 95% Wilson intervals and explicitly admits paired tests require matched per-prompt correctness logs — so −0.002 on GSM8K is within interval width. No McNemar or paired bootstrap was run.
Why fuzzy logic instead of a learned policy?
Stated motivation is interpretability and smoothness, not raw performance. The authors concede "the reason is not that fuzzy logic is more powerful than a learned policy." A distilled sparse linear rule or decision tree might match the tradeoff while being even easier to audit.
Will this generalize to frontier models?
Untested. Only two sub-3B models, Nmax capped at 8. At Nmax=32/64/128 and 70B-class models the accuracy–compute frontier may look very different — flagged as the top open question.
