03 Why This Matters

Removes the dominant cost of learned-sparse circuit analysis: training an SAE / Transcoder / VPD replacement network.

Factorize the checkpoint directly — bottleneck units ranked by attribution patching are still intervention-targetable, so the standard scoring → selection → ablation workflow survives intact.

A zero-data variant (G = I) recovers useful units from weights alone, opening per-training-step circuit analysis that was previously infeasible.

04 Critical Edge
  • AScaling is asymmetric. Full-model replacement is validated only on GPT-2 Small (48 matrices); Qwen3.5-27B is single-matrix only.
  • BUnits are non-identifiable. Results depend on calibration distribution, sparsity budget s, initialization — protocol-specific, not ground truth.
  • CWeak baseline. The sparse-pretrained model shows near-zero / negative necessity drop on docstring & IOI — its "circuits" are not behaviorally causal there.
  • DFirst-order attribution can miss edges; qualitative cases (c205 edit, head-3 attention) are single-prompt illustrations, not systematic.

01 / The Factorization

Each dense projection W ∈ ℝd_in × d_out becomes two sparse factors whose shared bottleneck coordinates are independently readable & writable — a rank-one read/write path you can score, select, and ablate in place.

Each bottleneck coordinate i is a rank-one read/write path. Substituting z_i leaves all other paths intact — so units are individually ablatable, reusing the SAE / Transcoder scoring-selection-ablation pipeline.
Objective
min ‖hW − hAB‖²₂ over calibration activations, s.t. ‖A‖₀ + ‖B‖₀ ≤ K — budget goes to directions the model actually visits (Gram-weighted).
Zero-data variant
Set G = I → reduces to Frobenius. No calibration tokens; useful for training-trajectory analysis.
Solver
ℓ₀-constrained (NP-Hard) ≈ solved by Double Sparse Factorization (Boza & Macko, 2025): split K = K_A + K_B, alternate ADMM block updates (regularized LS → hard-threshold → dual), then freeze supports & refit B.
Circuit extraction
Each unit ranked by positive first-order task-margin attribution (attribution patching). Mean-ablation tests sufficiency (Mkeep) and necessity (Mabl). Cost reported on two axes: selected units and active read/write edges. Comparisons only between replacements whose pre-pruning CE differs by ≤ 0.001.

03 / Assessment

A genuine, well-motivated reduction in MI's compute footprint, with unusual methodological discipline — weighed against honest, disclosed limits around scaling and identifiability.

+ Strengths

  • Novelty that lands. Repurposing Double Sparse Factorization (a compression tool) as a post-hoc interpretability surface yielding intervention-targetable units — cleanly distinguished from SAEs, Transcoders, VPD/SPD/APD, NaNA, sparse pretraining (Table 1).
  • Rigor is the strongest aspect. CE-matching ≤0.001, dual cost axes (units + edges), exact-SVD & Random-B dense controls that decouple "factorization" from "sparse edges," zero-vs-mean ablation robustness check (Appendix C.3).
  • Honest VPD-KL exclusion — could not be CE-matched, so kept out of circuit comparison rather than quietly compared.
  • Practically meaningful: >100× data reduction + smaller circuits lower the cost of circuit analysis substantially.

– Weaknesses

  • Scaling is asymmetric. Full-model replacement only on GPT-2 Small; Qwen3.5-27B is single-matrix. The "SWD scales" claim for frontier full-model replacement is not yet substantiated.
  • Partially weak baseline. Sparse-pretrained model is near-zero / negative on necessity for docstring & IOI — its "circuits" are arguably not behaviorally causal there.
  • Cherry-picked qualitative cases. Semantic audit = 6 hand-labeled units; editing = single prompt/unit (c205); attention = one head. Illustrative, not systematic.
  • Non-identifiability unresolved. Units are defined only up to scaling/permutation and depend on calibration, init, budget — protocol-specific objects, not unique ground truth.
  • First-order attribution can miss edges — necessity/sufficiency are lower bounds on a particular ranking, not on the optimal circuit.

05 / Open Questions

Where SWD's claims are sharpest is exactly where follow-up work should press hardest.

  1. Does SWD-FT full-model replacement remain faithful at 7B–70B scale, where per-layer approximation errors compound across far more transformer blocks?
  2. Can identifiability conditions (e.g. Zheng et al., 2021) be enforced or approximated, so bottleneck units are stable across calibration distributions and random seeds?
  3. How do SWD-extracted circuits compare to SAE / Transcoder circuits on adversarial or OOD inputs where the calibration Gram matrix G is misspecified?
  4. Can the zero-data variant run at every training step — revealing interpretable developmental phases in how circuits form and reorganize during pretraining?
  5. Does combining SWD's parameter-side sparse connectivity with attribution-graph methods (Ameisen et al., 2025) yield more faithful end-to-end computational graphs than either alone?
  6. Are units recovered under a non-English / multilingual calibration corpus consistent with the English ones — and what does that imply for the monosemanticity implicit in the semantic audit?
