> **一句话总结：** 在针对推理优化过的中等规模 LLM（Mathstral-7B、Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct）上，作者在 GSM8K 上发现传统的 few-shot CoT（用数据集示例）反而显著拖累性能，而简单的零样本 free-form / "Let's think step by step" 生成能以更高准确率（如 Mathstral 从 ≤74.2% 提升到 86.1%）超越官方报告，提示 CoT 作为评估基线已开始"帮倒忙"。

> **TL;DR:** The authors show on GSM8K that for reasoning-specialized mid-sized LLMs (Mathstral-7B, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct), standard few-shot Chain-of-Thought prompting with dataset examples underperforms a simple zero-shot free-form baseline (e.g., Mathstral 83.8% zero-shot vs. ≤74.2% best DS-CoT vs. 77.1% officially reported; rising to 86.1% with zero-shot CoT). They argue CoT prompting now imposes a "guidance-distraction tradeoff" and advocate zero-shot evaluation as the more faithful baseline.

## 核心贡献

- **实证揭示 CoT 反作用**：在 Mathstral-7B 与 Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct 上，使用数据集示例的标准 few-shot CoT（DS-CoT）准确率显著低于零样本 free-form 基线（Mathstral ≤74.1% vs 83.8%；Qwen ≤83.4% vs 88.6%），说明对推理专精模型，CoT 已变成"干扰源"。
- **提出 guidance-distraction 权衡**：CoT 一方面引导结构化推理，另一方面引入风格适配、格式合规、上下文整合等额外负担；当模型已能原生产出 CoT 风格推理时，干扰开始大于收益。
- **自生成示例也未必超过零样本**：即便用模型自生成的 free-form（Self-CoT）或加 "Let's think step by step" 的 Self-CoT+0shot 示例，对最专精的 Mathstral 仍无法稳定超过零样本基线；Self-CoT+0shot 是所有 CoT 变体中最优且最"少伤害"的格式。
- **重新审视评估范式**：明确指出 few-shot CoT（数据集示例）已不再适合作为新模型的可靠评估基线，会系统性低估模型能力并夸大新方法的增益；主张改用约束最小的零样本评估。
- **澄清"DS-CoT+Rule 越多越好"的假象**：通过对比 +Rule 与 +Prompt 答案抽取方式，证明 DS-CoT+Rule 在 shot 数增加时的"提升"主要来自更可靠的格式抽取，而非更好的推理（Table 2 中 1-shot Qwen DS-CoT+Rule 无效答案率高达 96.5%）。

## Key Contributions

- Empirically demonstrates that standard few-shot CoT with human-crafted dataset examples (DS-CoT) significantly degrades performance of reasoning-specialized models relative to a zero-shot free-form baseline on GSM8K.
- Formalizes a "guidance-distraction tradeoff": CoT guidance also imposes style adaptation, formatting compliance, and contextualization costs that, for natively-reasoning models, outweigh benefits.
- Shows that even model-generated CoT examples (Self-CoT, Self-CoT+0shot) do not reliably beat zero-shot baselines for the most specialized model (Mathstral); Self-CoT+0shot is identified as the "least harmful" CoT variant.
- Calls for retiring few-shot CoT-with-dataset-examples as a default evaluation baseline, arguing it systematically underestimates modern LLMs and inflates apparent gains of new methods.
- Diagnoses that the apparent scaling of DS-CoT+Rule with more shots is a formatting-extraction artifact (up to 96.5% invalid-answer rate at 1-shot for Qwen), not genuine reasoning improvement.

## Methodology

The authors run a controlled study on **GSM8K** (7.5K train / 1.3K test) with three mid-sized (~7-8B) models: **Mathstral-7B** (reasoning-specialized), **Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct** (general-purpose with reasoning gains), and **Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct** (general-purpose, no specific reasoning tuning).

To isolate the effect of *reasoning format* from *example selection*, all few-shot experiments use **randomly selected** in-context examples (no retrieval/complexity/diversity-based selection). Prompting variants:

- **DS-CoT / DS-CoT+Instr**: standard few-shot CoT using GSM8K training examples, with or without explicit answer-format instructions.
- **Self-CoT**: few-shot examples generated by the model itself in free-form zero-shot; **Self-CoT+0shot** adds a "Let's think step by step" prefix during example generation.
- **Baselines**: zero-shot free-form (problem statement only) and zero-shot CoT ("Let's think step by step").

Two **answer-extraction** methods decouple reasoning quality from formatting: rule-based (search for `#### <number>`) and prompt-based (ask the model to return just the number). Self-CoT examples are generated for all 7.5K training problems and filtered to keep only those with correct final answers; an ablation applies a two-stage automated cleaning (truncation after the solution; filtering incomplete reasoning).

All generations use **greedy decoding**; results are averaged over **10 random seeds** for example sampling.

## Results & Highlights

- **Mathstral-7B (GSM8K)**: best DS-CoT variant reaches only **74.2%** (8-shot DS-CoT+Instr+Prompt), versus **83.8%** for zero-shot free-form and **86.1%** for zero-shot CoT — a ~+9.6-point gap over the strongest few-shot DS-CoT, and +9.0 over Mistral's officially reported **77.1%** (which the authors say they could not reproduce).
- **Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (GSM8K)**: best DS-CoT is **84.0%** (2-shot DS-CoT+Instr+Prompt), compared to **88.6%** zero-shot free-form and **90.9%** zero-shot CoT — a +6.9-point advantage for the zero-shot CoT baseline.
- **Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (GSM8K)**: the general-purpose model still benefits from CoT — best DS-CoT+Instr reaches **80.3%** vs. **67.3%** zero-shot free-form and **81.4%** zero-shot CoT, supporting the paper's claim that CoT helps most when models cannot produce coherent rationales natively.
- **Self-CoT+0shot is the strongest CoT variant across all three models** (e.g., Qwen 91.1–91.6%, Llama 81.1–84.4%, Mathstral 84.8–85.5%), beating zero-shot CoT for Qwen and Llama and only slightly trailing it for Mathstral.
- **Formatting artifact exposed (Table 2)**: at 1-shot, DS-CoT+Rule produces invalid-answer rates of **17.3% (Mathstral), 96.5% (Qwen), 87.3% (Llama)**, while DS-CoT+Prompt stays at ~0.1–0.6% — explaining the apparent "improvement" of DS-CoT+Rule as shot count grows.
- **Post-processing (cleaning) of Self-CoT+0shot does not improve accuracy (Table 3)**: changes are within ±0.6% for Mathstral and Qwen; only Llama's free-form Self-CoT benefits (up to +2.2% at 16-shot).

## Authors & Credibility

Authors are affiliated with **EPFL** and **Apple** (Pushkin, Sandon, Abbé, Lotfi), and **Mistral AI** (Jiang — who worked on Mathstral). Affiliation with the lab/company that produced Mathstral-7B lends direct expertise to the critique of its evaluation setup. The authors transparently disclose that they *could not reproduce* Mathstral's officially reported 77.1% number, and they position the work as concurrent with Cheng et al. (2025, arXiv:2506.14641), which reaches a compatible conclusion.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The core observation (few-shot CoT underperforms zero-shot for strong reasoners) is independently reported by the concurrent Cheng et al. (2025); this paper's added value is the systematic decomposition into DS-CoT, Self-CoT, Self-CoT+0shot, the +Instr × {Rule, Prompt} extraction grid, and the explicit guidance-distraction framing.
- **Rigor**: Strong on controls — random example selection, 10-seed averaging, greedy decoding, two extraction methods to separate reasoning from formatting. The diagnosis that DS-CoT+Rule's apparent scaling is a formatting artifact (96.5% invalid answers at Qwen 1-shot) is a genuinely insightful methodological correction.
- **Reproducibility**: Limited. The paper does not provide prompts verbatim, random seeds, code, or the exact Self-CoT example pool. Crucially, the authors themselves flag an irreproducibility ("could not reproduce 77.1% for Mathstral"), which is honest but also a warning sign about the stability of the underlying numbers.
- **Significance**: Scope is narrow — **a single benchmark (GSM8K)**, three models in the 7-8B range only, and math only. Generalization to code, symbolic logic, commonsense, or larger/proprietary frontier models is untested. The strong claims ("CoT evaluation can no longer serve as a reliable baseline") are extrapolated from this narrow base.
- **Red flags / overclaiming**: The headline framing ("Soft Guidance Starts to Outperform CoT Prompting") somewhat overstates the contribution — the paper does not actually propose a new "soft guidance" method; it compares existing prompting variants. For Llama, CoT still clearly helps (80.3% vs 67.3%), so the conclusion is model-class-specific, not universal. The "end-to-end will beat hand-crafted features" analogy is speculative and not demonstrated.

## Strengths

- Clean, focused experimental design that genuinely isolates format from example-selection confounds.
- The +Rule vs +Prompt extraction analysis (Table 2) is a valuable methodological contribution that explains a common misleading pattern in CoT evaluations.
- Intellectual honesty about non-reproducibility of Mathstral's official number and about the limits of their post-processing pipeline.
- Actionable recommendation for the community: adopt zero-shot evaluation as the default baseline for reasoning-specialized models.

## Limitations

- Single benchmark (GSM8K), single domain (grade-school math), single model size class (~7-8B) — explicitly acknowledged by the authors.
- No frontier/larger models (e.g., 70B+, GPT-4-class), so the "as LLMs improve" extrapolation is supported only indirectly.
- No symbolic-logic, commonsense, code, or multi-step non-math reasoning tasks.
- Random example selection is a deliberate choice for isolation, but means the conclusions do not necessarily transfer to production CoT setups that use retrieval or complexity-based selection.
- No code, prompts, or seeds released at submission time.

## Relevance

对关注 LLM 推理评估与 prompt 工程的研究者而言，本文直接挑战了广泛使用的 few-shot CoT 评估范式，提示在评测推理专精模型或设计新推理方法时应改用零样本基线，以避免高估方法增益——这与日常跟踪 LLM reasoning benchmark 和评估方法论的工作高度相关。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the guidance-distraction tradeoff extend beyond ~8B parameters to 70B+ and frontier models, where native CoT behavior is even stronger?
- How do the findings transfer to non-math reasoning domains (code generation, symbolic logic, commonsense, long-horizon agentic tasks)?
- Would combining advanced example selection (similarity, complexity-aware, optimization-based) with Self-CoT+0shot change the conclusion, or does the distraction effect persist?
- Is the irreproducibility of Mathstral's official 77.1% a decoding/sampling artifact, a prompt-format issue, or evidence of evaluation-set contamination?
- Can the guidance-distraction hypothesis be operationalized into a quantitative metric (e.g., format-compliance overhead) that predicts when zero-shot will beat few-shot CoT?
