Cross-Model KV Cache Transfer in LLM Families: A Closed-Form Linear Mapping for Prefill Reuse

Heo · Shafipour · Zhao · Golub · Kamani · Borkar · Chandran · Zardoshti · Darvish Rouhani NVIDIA
6 matched-KV pairs
3 model families
5 benchmarks

Within-family cross-model KV cache transfer admits a closed-form, gradient-free per-head ridge fit. On four of six matched-KV pairs it retains 73–98% of the receiver's standalone accuracy — and runs 2.7–25× faster than re-prefill.

For swaps like Qwen3 14B→32B or Llama 3.1 8B→70B, the cross-model KV relationship is largely linear. A ridge mapper fit on just 500 FineWeb-Edu sequences (≈47–87 min on one 8×H100 node), with RoPE-stripped content-space keys and top-k cross-layer source selection, lets the receiver skip prefill entirely. An MLP variant recovers up to +36.8 pp on the two pairs where ridge collapses.
Key Metric
25×speedup
Prefill latency at 32K tokens, Qwen3 14B→32B: mapper 277.6 ms vs 6,975.3 ms re-prefill — at 97.6% retained accuracy.
Why This Matters

Training-free KV portability turns the model swap into a cheap algebra, not a recompute.

  • Mid-conversation model swaps in cascades & routers
  • No GPU backprop at fit time — closed-form ridge solve
  • Length-portable fit via RoPE-content decomposition
  • A third KV-reuse axis alongside prefix-cache & cross-layer sharing
Critical Edge

Matched-KV, within-family only.

Two of six pairs (Ministral 3B→14B, 8B→14B) collapse. Mismatched-KV, cross-family, and hybrid SSM/sliding-window architectures are explicitly out of scope — a large fraction of real swaps.

Failing pairs: 11–15% floor-normalized · GSM8K retention 1.6–3.2%
§ 01

Method — three components, one ridge solve

W* = (XᵀX + λI)⁻¹XᵀY · λ = 0.01

Formulate transfer as finding f: C_S → Ĉ_T so target decoding matches its own C_T downstream. Operates only on matched-KV pairs — same KV head count and per-head dimension, even if layer or parameter counts differ.

01 / PER-HEAD RIDGE
Closed-form fit, no gradients
Independent linear map per (target layer, head, K|V). ~128K calibration tokens from 500 FineWeb-Edu sequences (stride-4). Ridge beats OLS because top-k source layers are correlated and XᵀX is near-singular. Mapper: 1.01–3.36 B params (4–12 GB).
02 / CROSS-LAYER SOURCE
Top-k by R², concatenated
For each target layer, pick top-k source layers by head-averaged R². k swept over {1…24, all}. Largest single contributor — k=8→k=1 drops K R² 0.79→0.56 on Qwen3 14B→32B.
03 / CONTENT-SPACE
RoPE-stripped, length-portable
Strip source RoPE, map, re-apply target RoPE at inference: K̂ = (K_s R⁻¹ W + b) R. Orthogonal R ⇒ exact inversion. Fit on 1,024 tokens serves up to 32K.

§ 03

Mechanistic insight — error placement, not magnitude

§4.5 · 12 directions

Across 12 matched-KV pair evaluations (six S→L, six L→S), what predicts whether a mapper will work? The diagnostic reframe: it is where residual error lands, not how big it is.

Attention-output cosine
r = +0.57
Strong predictor of HellaSwag retention. MLP rescue works by redistributing error away from attention-sensitive subspaces.
Calibration K R²
r = −0.20
Anti-predictor. A high in-domain fit R² does NOT screen cross-pair transferability. R² is the wrong knob.

§ 05

Strengths & limitations

What lands well

  • Training-free, closed-form fit — no GPU backprop at fit time.
  • RoPE-content decomposition is principled; fit is length-portable by construction.
  • Strong mechanistic analysis (cosine, K/V error concentration) reframes how mappers should be evaluated.
  • Honest reporting of two failure pairs + clean MLP rescue; negative R²-on-failure finding kept in.
  • Real production latency numbers across 64–32K tokens, both directions.

Where it breaks

  • Matched-KV only — same head count & per-head dim. Many GQA swaps excluded.
  • Within-family only; cross-family (Qwen3→Llama) left open.
  • Single calibration domain (FineWeb-Edu); CodeAlpaca costs 5.24 pp; cannot separate subject from register.
  • k selected on reported benchmarks (≤2.49 pp acknowledged bias), not strictly out-of-sample.
  • Multi-turn eval is single-pair (Qwen3 14B↔32B, 100 CoQA convs); L→S drift linear at 0.33 pp/turn.
  • MLP variant needs gradient training; generalization beyond four pairs not established.
§ 06

Open questions for further work

Does the closed-form mapper extend to mismatched-KV pairs via a head-pooling/projection pre-step before ridge?
Can attention-output cosine be predicted before fitting, enabling cheap deployment screening across arbitrary family pairs?
Why do the two Ministral→14B pairs collapse while 3B→8B succeeds — training-data overlap, fine-tuning recipe, or a depth-ratio threshold?
How does the mapper behave on agentic / tool-use workloads where attention patterns differ from HellaSwag-style completion?
Can an attention-aligned training objective (optimize cosine, not MSE) replace the post-hoc MLP rescue and dominate ridge everywhere?
Does it compose with speculative prefill / SwiftKV for compounded wins, or do they interfere on the same KV state?
