> **一句话总结：** 作者在 Qwen3-32B 残差流上用对比线性探针（差均值方向）刻画"短期 vs. 长期"时间视域，并通过推理时对比激活加法（CAA）在二选一时间偏好、OOD 货币跨期选择（AEIM 在 ±128 时中位差达 4.90×，10 年视域下超 56×）和 TravelPlanner 规划能力上诱导出大且双向的行为变化。

> **TL;DR:** The authors train a signed mean-mass (MM) contrastive linear probe on Qwen3-32B's residual stream to isolate a short-term vs. long-term temporal-horizon direction, then steer it with Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA). On a held-out binary temporal-choice task, layer-44 CAA swings the parsed long-term rate by up to 59.4 pp (explicit) and 36.5 pp (implicit) vs. only ~5.6 pp for a random-orthogonal control; on an out-of-distribution monetary intertemporal-choice task, ±128 steering shifts the area-equivalent indifference multiplier (AEIM) by a median factor of 4.90 across delays and over 56× at the 10-year horizon; and on the TravelPlanner benchmark, moderate positive steering modestly improves the Commonsense Constraint Micro Pass Rate.

## 核心贡献

- **时间视域的线性可分性证据：** 在 Qwen3-32B 中间偏后层（24–48）上，用差均值（MM）方向 d_MM = µ_long − µ_short 即可在显式 holdout 上达到 96.0% 分类准确率，逻辑回归类探针可达 99.0%，说明"短期/长期"语义在残差流中线性可读。
- **CAA 的因果控制方向：** 将上述 MM 方向作为有符号残差流加项注入推理过程（正值→长期，负值→短期），在未见过的二选一任务上产生方向一致且跨层广泛的选择迁移，证明该方向不仅是分类器方向，也是因果控制方向。
- **跨任务泛化（最强结果）：** 仅在二选一短期/长期回答上训练的方向，能迁移到结构完全不同的货币跨期选择任务，通过 AEIM 指标量化出大尺度的双向偏好位移。
- **对规划能力的影响：** 同一方向在 TravelPlanner 的 Commonsense Micro Pass Rate 上表现出非单调但方向性可解释的效应——适度正向 steering 改善规划合理性，过大正向反而退化。
- **方法学严谨性：** 配套报告了随机正交向量对照（matched-norm control）、随机采样（temperature 0.8）稳健性、以及干预幅度相对残差范数的诊断（最强 |α|=128 仅为残差范数的 36–70%）。

## Key Contributions

- **Linear accessibility of temporal horizon:** A signed mean-mass (MM) difference direction d_MM = µ_long − µ_short, fitted at middle-to-late residual layers of Qwen3-32B, achieves 96.0% explicit holdout accuracy (99.0% for LR/WLR variants), showing that short-term vs. long-term semantics are linearly readable.
- **CAA as a causal control direction:** Adding the signed MM vector to the residual stream at the last prompt token and every decode step produces consistent, broad-across-layers behavioral shifts on held-out binary temporal choices, in both parsed choices and forced-choice logprob margins.
- **Strongest generalization — monetary intertemporal choice:** A direction trained only on binary semantic horizon answers transfers to an out-of-distribution monetary time-utility task, shifting the area-equivalent indifference multiplier (AEIM) by large factors in both directions (median 4.90× across delays at ±128; >56× at the 3650-day horizon).
- **Effect on a planning capability:** The same temporal direction affects TravelPlanner's Commonsense Constraint Micro Pass Rate non-monotonically — moderate positive steering improves it while very large positive steering degrades coherence.
- **Rigorous controls:** A random-orthogonal matched-norm control, a stochastic-decoding (temperature 0.8, 5 samples) robustness sweep, and residual-norm scaling diagnostics (intervention ≤ 70% of hook-site residual norm) accompany every main result.

## Methodology

**Model & decoding.** All experiments use Qwen3-32B with thinking traces disabled (greedy decoding, temperature 0, by default). Probes are trained at layers {24, 28, 32, 36, 40, 44, 48}, spanning the middle-to-late part of the 64-layer model.

**Probe direction construction.** Two datasets are used: an *explicit* set of 500 temporal-choice questions with overt time markers (split 400 train / 100 test, yielding 800 teacher-forced examples via paired short/long continuations) and an *implicit* set of 300 questions that encode horizon through semantic contrast without explicit time words (used only for cross-domain evaluation). The MM difference direction is

d_MM = µ_long − µ_short,

computed with mean-answer pooling (averaging hidden states across continuation tokens) at each selected layer. Final-token pooling, plain logistic regression (LR), and whitened variants (WLR, WMM) are evaluated as diagnostics but the unwhitened MM direction is what CAA steering uses.

**Steering intervention (CAA).** For layer l, signed strength α, and normalized direction d̂_l, the hook adds α·d̂_l to the residual stream at the last prompt token and again at every generated token. Positive α steers toward the long-term class; negative toward immediate. Steering strengths are α ∈ {0, ±2, ±4, ±8, ±16, ±32, ±64, ±128} for binary steering, {0, ±8, ±16, ±32, ±64, ±128} for the time-utility task. The strongest intervention (|α|=128) corresponds to 36–70% of the unsteered hook-site residual L2 norm.

**Binary steering evaluation.** The model is presented full binary-choice prompts (two complete answer candidates in randomized order). Metrics are: (i) proportion of parsed continuations choosing the long-term option, with teacher-forced logprob fallback when parsing fails, and (ii) the average-token logprob margin Δ_avg = ℓ(c_long|p) − ℓ(c_imm|p) and its change relative to baseline.

**Time-utility (monetary) steering.** Prompts ask the model to advise between $x now and $y after delay t. The grid sweeps 8 base multipliers m_b ∈ {1.0, 1.01, 1.1, 1.5, 2, 5, 10, 100}, 8 amount scale factors c ∈ {0.3 … 8}, and 12 delays from 1 hour to 3650 days, yielding 768 generations per condition. The headline metric is the **area-equivalent indifference multiplier (AEIM)** — a log-axis area estimate of the delayed/immediate ratio at which the model is indifferent.

**TravelPlanner evaluation.** The validation split (180 queries: 20 per {3 difficulty levels × 3 trip lengths}) is evaluated with layer-40 steering. Primary metric is Commonsense Constraint Micro Pass Rate; chosen because baseline delivery is near ceiling (96.7%) while harder whole-query metrics sit near floor, making micro pass rate the most discriminative signal.

## Results & Highlights

- **Probe diagnostics (Table 2):** MM direction reaches 96.0% explicit holdout accuracy (best at L24) and 77.0% on the implicit cross-domain set (best at L32); LR/WLR reach 99.0% explicit and 80.3–83.5% implicit — confirming linear separability while motivating the use of the unwhitened MM vector for steering.
- **Binary choice steering (Table 6):** At layer 44, the parsed long-term choice gap between best-positive and best-negative steering is **59.4 percentage points on the explicit test set** and **36.5 pp on the implicit full set**, with long-minus-immediate logprob gaps of 0.813 and 0.263 respectively.
- **Random-orthogonal control (Fig. 7):** Matched-norm random vectors orthogonal to MM produce at most **5.6 pp movement on explicit and 5.5 pp on implicit** vs. CAA's 59.4 / 36.5 pp — i.e. the CAA effect is an order of magnitude larger and consistently signed.
- **Monetary intertemporal steering (Table 5, layer 40):** Baseline geometric-mean AEIM is 2.67 with 54.7% parsed-later rate; −128d̂_40 gives AEIM 8.34 and 36.3% later rate; +128d̂_40 gives AEIM 1.12 and 80.4% later rate. The median negative-vs-positive AEIM ratio across delays is **4.90 at ±128**, and **over 56× at the 3650-day (10-year) horizon**.
- **TravelPlanner capability (Fig. 5):** Moderate positive temporal steering improves the Commonsense Constraint Micro Pass Rate over baseline, while very large positive (+128) steering degrades it — a non-monotonic but directionally meaningful effect on a real planning benchmark (180 validation queries).
- **Robustness:** Stochastic decoding (temperature 0.8, 5 samples, Appendix D/G) preserves greedy ordering with small 2-sigma error bars — e.g. layer-44 explicit long-term rate at +128d̂ is 91.4 ± 1.8% (sampled) vs. 93.1% (greedy).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The CAA technique itself is not novel — it directly applies Rimsky et al.'s contrastive activation addition. Novelty lies in the *application domain*: isolating and causally steering a temporal-horizon / intertemporal-preference direction, and introducing the AEIM metric to quantify behavioral shift on a monetary task. This is a legitimate but incremental contribution.
- **Rigor:** Strong on controls. The matched random-orthogonal control (5.6 pp vs. 59.4 pp CAA movement) is the right experiment and is executed well; the stochastic-decoding robustness sweeps (Appendices D, G) pre-empt the obvious "greedy decoding artifact" critique; residual-norm scaling is reported rather than hidden. The separation between "probe accuracy = readout" and "steering = causal test" is articulated cleanly.
- **Construct validity — the core weakness (acknowledged by authors):** The MM direction is trained on short-vs-long *answer continuations*, which the authors admit "might also differ in abstraction, urgency, or other semantic concepts." Long-horizon answers tend to be more systemic/preventive/abstract; short-horizon more reactive/operational. The probe therefore likely isolates a *broader short-term/long-term answer register* rather than temporal horizon alone. The monetary-task transfer is suggestive but does not identify the direction's semantic basis.
- **Missing ablations:** The authors flag (but do not run) head-to-head CAA comparisons against LR-, WLR-, and WMM-derived steering vectors, so it is unknown whether MM is uniquely useful for intervention or merely convenient. A prompting baseline ("think more long-term") is also absent, leaving open whether comparable behavioral shifts are achievable without internal intervention.
- **Single-model scope:** All results use one model family/size (Qwen3-32B), one decoding regime (thinking disabled), and a small set of prompt templates. Generalization to other architectures, smaller/larger models, or reasoning-enabled settings is unverified.
- **TravelPlanner evidence is weak:** The authors concede the TravelPlanner result is "weaker than the time-utility result because the aggregate commonsense metric is indirect and does not identify which item-level checks change under steering." This is essentially a single aggregate curve; item-level analysis would substantially strengthen the capability claim.
- **Reproducibility:** Good. Probe training data sizes (500 explicit / 300 implicit), layer band, strength grid, monetary grid (768 generations per condition), and metric definitions (AEIM formula) are fully specified; random-orthogonal controls and stochastic sweeps are documented. Code/data release is not explicitly promised in the text I read, which is a minor concern.
- **Overclaiming risk:** Handled reasonably. The authors explicitly reserve causal claims for the intervention experiments and do not claim the direction isolates temporal horizon alone.

## Strengths

- Clean, well-motivated research question at the intersection of mechanistic interpretability and behavioral economics (intertemporal choice).
- The AEIM metric is a thoughtful, domain-appropriate way to summarize a sparse, unevenly sampled monetary grid into a single threshold-like curve per condition.
- Strong, fastidious control experiments (random-orthogonal matched-norm, stochastic decoding, residual-norm scaling) that pre-empt standard reviewer objections.
- Honest framing: readout accuracy is treated as evidence of linear accessibility, not as proof of causal isolation; causal claims are reserved for intervention experiments.
- Safety-relevance is articulated without being alarmist — the dual-use angle (a malicious actor steering temporal preferences) is mentioned but not overblown.

## Limitations

- Single model (Qwen3-32B only); no transfer to other architectures or model scales.
- Thinking traces disabled — behavior under reasoning-enabled decoding is unknown.
- Probe direction conflates temporal horizon with correlated semantic features (abstraction, urgency, proactive vs. reactive framing).
- No head-to-head CAA comparison against LR/WLR/WMM-derived steering vectors.
- No prompting baseline (e.g., "think long-term") to contextualize the magnitude of steering effects.
- TravelPlanner evidence is an aggregate curve without item-level analysis.
- Small unparsed-response fraction introduces minor imprecision into parsed choice rates.
- AEIM aggregation convention could reshape the apparent curve; authors mitigate by also providing raw present-choice heatmaps (Appendix F).

## Relevance

对关注大模型可解释性、激活调控（activation steering）与对齐/安全的研究者而言，本文提供了一个具体且可复现的"概念方向→因果行为调控"案例，并引入跨期偏好这一新颖且与规划/决策/建议系统强相关的目标维度。其方法学（差均值探针 + CAA + AEIM + 随机正交对照）可作为研究其他抽象偏好方向的模板，但单模型、单解码设置与构造效度问题意味着结论需在更多架构上验证后才宜外推。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the temporal-horizon direction transfer across model families (Llama, Mistral, Claude-class), or is it Qwen3-specific? Does enabling thinking traces change the direction's location or steerability?
- How much of the MM direction is truly "temporal horizon" vs. correlated features (abstraction, urgency, strategic scope)? A factorial probe dataset disentangling these axes would help.
- Would LR-, WLR-, or WMM-derived steering vectors produce larger or cleaner behavioral effects than MM — i.e., is MM optimal for intervention or just for readout?
- How does CAA steering compare quantitatively against a prompting baseline ("think in terms of 10-year horizons") on the same monetary and TravelPlanner tasks?
- Which specific TravelPlanner commonsense checks flip under positive vs. negative temporal steering? Item-level analysis is needed to substantiate the capability claim.
- Are the steered preferences stable under adversarial paraphrase, jailbreak-style prompts, or chained multi-turn dialogue, where activation-steering effects are known to attenuate?
- Could combining the temporal-horizon direction with other concept directions (e.g., risk, cooperation) yield composable, predictable behavioral steering, or do they interfere non-additively?
