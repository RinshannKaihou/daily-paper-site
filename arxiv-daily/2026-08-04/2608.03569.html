Adversarial fast-moving domains as test beds for benchmarking AI scientists.

以"对抗性、快速演变的真实世界领域"作为 AI 科学家能力的评测框架 —— 在 F1 赛车设计与万智牌套牌构筑两个截然不同的领域落地验证。

William Bolton · Philip Torr University of Oxford, Dept. of Engineering Science Funded by ARIA / DSIT / Pillar VC
Key Finding

The bottleneck for AI scientists is not idea generation — it is filtering, prioritization, and meta-alignment. Across two structurally distinct domains, frontier LLMs over-produce candidates with immediately obvious value and systematically miss value that only emerges through composition with surrounding context.

02 Why This Matters

Leakage-resistant by construction: both corpora (FIA 2026 regs PDF, 267-card Lorwyn Eclipsed set) provably post-date every participating model's training cutoff — directly neutralizing the data-contamination threat that cripples DiscoveryBench, PaperBench, ScienceAgentBench.

03 Critical Edge

A single non-expert reviewer (self-described as "not a domain expert in either F1 or competitive Magic") adjudicated all 519 F1 matches — no inter-annotator agreement, no sensitivity analysis. Worse: the run is actually retrospective, undercutting the headline "prospective" framing.

§ 04

The cross-domain failure mode

Generic value over-emitted · context-dependent value systematically missed

Models emit "obviously strong" candidates. They miss "strong only in context."

In F1, the most-flagged loophole type — INTERFACE COUPLING (28.8%) — produced the lowest match rate (5.0%). The less-frequent BROAD FREEDOM (20.7%) produced the highest (7.7%).

In MTG, the generic-value card Spell Snare appears in 33/108 generated decks but only 9/19 PT decks — over-predicted. The build-around card Formidable Speaker appears in 0 generated decks vs 7/19 PT decks — invisible to every model.

The unifying diagnosis: a "value from composition" deficit. Filtering and prioritization — not fluency — are the real frontier for AI-scientist systems.

§ 05

Method & statistical anchors

Unusually careful for an LLM-eval paper · exploratory, not causal

The four admissibility criteria

  1. Constrained but non-trivial design space — open enough for creativity, tight enough to score.
  2. Adversarial — genuine expert innovation produced under real competition pressure.
  3. Publicly observable downstream expert artifacts (PT decklists, F1 technical coverage).
  4. Clear information cutoff that provably post-dates every model's training data.

Six models, three providers: GPT-5.2 · o3 · Gemini 3 Flash · Gemini 3.1 Pro · Qwen3 235B · Qwen3-235B-thinking. Every cutoff predates both corpora.

§ 06

Red flags worth naming

The authors own most of these — emphasis ours

Validity

Single non-expert reviewer. All 519 F1 MATCH/PARTIAL/NOMATCH labels rest on one author's judgment. No Cohen's κ, no second reviewer, no sensitivity analysis — on the paper's central dependent variable.

Framing vs fact

Retrospective despite prospective framing. "The human artifacts in this study had already been generated at the time of evaluation." The leakage-resistance is real in principle, not yet in practice for this run.

External validity

Proxies, not science. F1 and MTG are adversarial and fast-moving but are not themselves scientific discovery. The leap to "AI scientist capabilities" is an inference, not a measurement.

Ground truth

Incomplete expert reference. F1 public coverage skews toward visible aero; power-unit internals stay confidential. "Unmatched" ideas may be unobservable, not wrong.

Model sample

n = 6, no reasoning premium. Qwen3-235B-thinking scores worse than its non-thinking twin on F1 quality (12% vs 39% all-pass). Under-explored.

Match ≠ reasoning

Convergence is not shared mechanism. A MATCH shows the model landed on the same idea under shared constraints — not that it reasoned the same way, or that the idea would translate into real-world value.
