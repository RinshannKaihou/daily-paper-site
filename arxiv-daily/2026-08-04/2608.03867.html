AdaMX: turning MX's wasted exponent bits into free per-block intelligence.

⬢ Key Finding

MX's 8-bit shared exponent is massively over-provisioned for LLMs — per-channel weight ranges never exceed 11, per-token ranges fit in E4 for >99.99% of blocks. AdaMX reclaims those spare bits as per-block, operand-specific metadata and recovers ~83% of MXFP4's accuracy loss at ≤1.1% extra system energy.

01

The core insight two orthogonal axes of heterogeneity

The authors quantize 2000 blocks of Llama-3.1-8B and measure per-block quantization SNR. Two findings break the "one format per tensor" assumption — and both vary block by block, in opposite ways.

Axis 1 — element format FP4 vs INT4

At block granularity FP4 wins on 52.8% of blocks and INT4 on 47.2% — directly contradicting the tensor-level folklore that FP4 always dominates. The split is model-dependent but never lopsided.

Axis 2 — recovery scheme refine vs extend

Scale refinement wins on 49.6% of blocks; block-max mantissa extension on 36.5%; 13.8% gain little from either. Crucially, the two axes are orthogonal — no single static assignment is optimal.

Operand-asymmetric design philosophy why weights ≠ activations

Weights are static → afford a richer offline 12-candidate MSE search per block. Result: E4Mt2T2, four {format × enhancement} modes routed by a 2-bit selector.

Activations are online → cannot afford per-block search. Result: a deterministic, calibration-free, division-free single-pass encoder E4M1Mt2N1.

T2 2 bits · weight router

Routes each block to one of four codewords: 00 = FP4 + FP6 block-max ext, 01 = FP4 + scale refine, 10 = INT4 + scale refine, 11 = INT4 + INT6 block-max ext. Determined by exhaustive 12-candidate MSE search (4 T2 × 3 exponent offsets).

The elegance: round-to-nearest ⇄ FP6 max-norm co-design

Round-to-nearest E4M1 scaling has a worst-case ratio of 1.25. The FP6 block-max format's max-norm is exactly 6 × 1.25 = 7.5 — so the block maximum is always representable, eliminating all 13% of clamped cases without needing a wider FP7 datapath. Block-max MSE drops 2.2× (0.047 → 0.021), model-independent.

04

The 22nm FD-SOI prototype one datapath, two block sizes

An M×N output-stationary systolic array with 2×2-MAC PEs. Decoder replication shrinks from M·N to M+N. Block size becomes a runtime knob, not a fabrication-time commitment.

Silicon PPA 500 MHz · 0.8 V

  • 8192 multipliers · 864 KB SRAM
  • Peak 8.19 TOPS · 17.1 TOPS/W
  • Area 2.166 mm² vs MXFP4 2.099 mm²
  • Power 482 mW vs MXFP4 466 mW
  • Per-PE enhancement: 270.6 / 325.4 µm²

Three tricks worth stealing RTL

  • Shared adder-tree penultimate stage exposes two independent 16-block results at full 32-lane throughput.
  • Block-max correction decomposed into 3 scalar terms: ΔA·W + ΔW·A + ΔA·ΔW.
  • Activation quantizer has no divider, no multiplier — just bit-compares against two constants (1.3125, 1.875).

Why energy goes down at K=32 counter-intuitive

Lower EBW (4.25 vs 4.5 bits/element) means fewer bits per DRAM access — and DRAM dominates 61–94% of system energy. So AdaMX-32 reduces end-to-end energy by 2.9–5.2% even while adding on-chip compute. The K=32 operating point is the efficiency win; K=16 is the accuracy win.

05

Critical assessment what to believe, what to probe

⬢ Strengths

  • Genuinely free metadata. The over-provisioned-exponent observation is the kind of clean architectural insight that funds the whole design — and is independently verifiable.
  • Operand asymmetry is principled, not ad hoc: static weights get a search; online activations get a calibration-free divider-free encoder. Each matches its real constraint.
  • The round ⇄ FP6 max-norm co-design is elegant — provably eliminates clamping without a wider datapath.
  • Comprehensive eval: 4 LLMs (3B–70B) + 1 VLM, commonsense + MMLU + WikiText + VLM + RULER, with full silicon PPA.

⬢ Limitations & red flags

  • Heterogeneity analysis is thin — Fig. 2 scatter is Llama-3.1-8B only; the orthogonality claim is asserted for the other three models, not shown.
  • Baselines are MX-internal. No AWQ / GPTQ / QuaRot / SpinQuant — the strong 2025–26 rotation-based W4A4 baselines are absent.
  • No math/code/reasoning (GSM8K, MATH, HumanEval) — exactly where 4-bit errors compound.
  • 22nm FD-SOI ≠ merchant silicon. Post-PAR simulation only, no measurement; absolute PPA not comparable to 7/5nm chips.
  • Headline averages are favorable: per-model recovery ranges 77–92%; "removes 43/27% of NVFP4 loss" is weakly supported — NVFP4 wins several individual tasks.
06

Open questions where AdaMX might break

Does the two-axis heterogeneity survive SpinQuant/QuaRot rotation, which fundamentally reshapes outlier structure — or does the T2 mix collapse to mostly FP4?

What is the encoding-time cost of the 12-candidate per-block weight search at 70B scale (GPU-hours), and can a heuristic replace it?

Can the lossless FP6 block-max trick be ported back into the OCP MX standard as a backward-compatible extension, or does it need new decode hardware?

Does the 83% commonsense recovery translate to reasoning benchmarks where the residual 17% may dominate on hard problems?

At 64K–1M context, does the AdaMX-32 KV-cache advantage (already +41 pts at 32K) keep growing, plateau, or invert?

Generalization to reasoning/code distillations (Qwen3, DeepSeek-R1) — currently entirely untested.
