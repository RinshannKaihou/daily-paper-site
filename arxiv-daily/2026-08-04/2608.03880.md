> **一句话总结：** 本文系统验证了 Model FLOPs Utilization (MFU) 这个软件可解析、不依赖硬件探针的指标，可作为 LLM 训练 GPU 功耗的可移植预测代理——在六款 NVIDIA/AMD GPU、近 3000 次单卡训练实验中，按 (GPU, dtype, batch size) 分组拟合的线性 MFU-功耗模型将平均百分比误差从 per-GPU 的约 10% 降到约 1%，恰好等于多次重复测量的噪声下限。

> **TL;DR:** Across 6 NVIDIA/AMD GPUs and ~3000 single-device LLM training runs, the authors show that a linear Model FLOPs Utilization (MFU) power model fits every tested device, and conditioning a separate OLS fit per (GPU, dtype, batch size) drops MAPE from ~10% (per-GPU) to ~1% per cell — matching the cross-repeat measurement-noise floor; unlike the vendor-reported GPU Utilization baseline, MFU also yields a fittable model on the AMD MI210 and is software-derivable, enabling energy-aware LLM training simulators.

## 核心贡献

- 首次系统验证 MFU 作为 GPU 功耗代理指标的跨架构有效性与稳健性：在 2 个厂商、4 个硬件档位、6 款 GPU 上构建基准框架，证明线性 MFU-功耗模型在所有受测设备（含 AMD MI210）上都成立。
- 提出"按 (GPU, dtype, batch size) 分组校准"的实用配方：把 per-GPU MAPE 从约 10% 压到约 1%，且该残差与多次重复测量的噪声地板统计上不可区分，说明在计算受限区内已无模型改进空间。
- 揭示并量化了内存受限区间的失效边界：在 batch=1 时 MFU 降至 1.3% 以下而功耗仍为 batch=128 的约 60%，单一线性斜率无法兼顾两区，需补充内存带宽信号。
- 闭合了 Özcan 等人先前在 Vidur 模拟器中加入 MFU 功耗模型但未做硬件实测验证的缺口，并公开了基准流水线与数据（https://github.com/dos-group/gpu_power_benchmark）。

## Key Contributions

- First systematic, cross-vendor validation that MFU — a software-defined metric derivable from model structure and throughput — is a portable proxy for GPU power, benchmarked across 2 vendors and 4 hardware tiers (NVIDIA A100/L40/L4/Quadro RTX 5000/RTX 4070 Ti, AMD MI210).
- A practical calibration recipe: one OLS fit per (GPU, dtype, batch size) cell collapses MAPE from ~10% per-GPU to ~1% per cell, which is statistically indistinguishable from the cross-repeat measurement-noise floor — i.e. residual error is dominated by sampling noise, not model misspecification.
- Quantitative characterization of the failure boundary at low arithmetic intensity: at batch 1 every device runs below 1.3% MFU while still drawing 49–64% (90% on L4) of its batch-128 power, showing exactly where the linear model breaks and where a complementary memory-bandwidth signal is required.
- Closes the validation gap left by Özcan et al., who inserted an MFU-based power model into the Vidur simulator without ever validating it against physical hardware power; benchmark pipeline and dataset released.

## Methodology

The authors build a cross-architectural benchmarking framework that maps high-level model parameters to empirical GPU power consumption.

**Workload sweep.** Each run drives the GPU with one causal-LM training step (forward + backward + AdamW optimizer step) on a fixed English prompt replicated to the batch size and padded/truncated to the context length, keeping the data pipeline out of the measurement. The cross-product sweep is:
- 6 GPUs: NVIDIA A100, L40, L4, Quadro RTX 5000, RTX 4070 Ti, AMD MI210.
- 3 model families (Qwen2.5, GPT-2, DialoGPT) with sizes matched to GPU tier (0.5B–3B).
- 3 numerical precisions: fp32, fp16, bf16.
- 7 batch sizes: 1–128.
- 2 context windows: 512 and 2048 tokens.
- 126 configurations per GPU (84 on the Quadro), each repeated 4 times → ~3000 single-device runs.

**Telemetry protocol.** Per-step duration is measured with paired CUDA events; a telemetry sample is emitted ~once per second, with in-window GPU Utilization weighted by per-step duration. Per-step training FLOPs are approximated as 3× the forward FLOPs reported by CalFLOPS, and peak per-device/per-precision FLOPS come from official vendor datasheets (FP32 peaks reflect the Tensor/Matrix-Core path with TF32/allow_tf32 enabled, not IEEE FP32). Sampling continues until the 95% CI half-width of both MFU and GPU Utilization falls within 5% of their running mean (min 50, max 500 samples); power is sampled jointly but excluded from the stopping rule because it is the smoothest signal. Clocks, persistence mode, and power limits are left at driver defaults; flash and memory-efficient SDPA are disabled and attention runs in eager mode to avoid kernel-implementation confounds across NVIDIA/AMD toolchains.

**Analysis.** Per-sample logs are aggregated to per-configuration means across the four repetitions; all linear fits are OLS of GPU-reported power on empirical (CalFLOPS-based) MFU, fitted on per-configuration means. R² and MAPE are reported with 95% bootstrap CIs (2000 resamples). Appendix A validates internal NVML/ROCm telemetry against external wall-power meters for the Quadro RTX 5000 and L40 (R² > 0.99 linear relationship).

## Results & Highlights

- **Linear MFU-power model fits every GPU** tested, including the AMD MI210, where vendor-reported GPU Utilization saturates at 100% under any sustained training and admits no linear fit at all. Per-GPU MFU fits explain 68–84% of power variance on NVIDIA training-class cards (A100 R²=0.74, L40 R²=0.80, Quadro 5000 R²=0.84) but only 37% on the inference-class L4 and 77% on MI210.
- **Per-GPU MAPE ranges 3.5–13.7%** across the fleet: 3.5% on L4, 7.8% on L40, 11.0% on A100, 12.5% on Quadro 5000 and RTX 4070 Ti, and 13.7% on MI210 (vs. NVIDIA GPU-Utilization MAPE which is tighter, but unfittable on MI210).
- **Conditioning on (GPU, dtype, batch size) collapses MAPE from ~10% per-GPU to ~1% per cell.** On MI210 the within-cell R² median is 0.997 (mean 0.980, std 0.038); NVIDIA per-cell medians range from 0.89 (RTX 4070 Ti) to 0.98 (A100, Quadro 5000). Per-sample MAPE falls to 0.6% on the L4.
- **Residual SD vs. noise floor** (Table III): conditioning cuts per-GPU residual by 4–7× on every device — e.g. A100 from 12.50% → 2.13% (noise floor 1.08%), RTX 4070 Ti from 16.46% → 2.21% (noise floor 1.66%), MI210 from 17.81% → 2.87% (noise floor 0.20%). On RTX 4070 Ti and L4 the conditioned residual is statistically indistinguishable from the noise floor.
- **Each device needs its own slope**: fitted slopes range from 1.21 W per percent MFU on MI210 to 4.49 on A100 — a 3.7× spread mirroring the peak-per-precision FLOPS spread, so the portable claim is about model class (linear + MFU-based), not a universal constant.
- **Memory-bound failure at batch 1** (Table IV): every device runs below 1.3% MFU yet draws 49–64% of its batch-128 power (90% on L4); going from batch 1 to batch 128, MFU grows 36–72× while power changes only 1.1–2.0×. A single-slope fit extrapolates through this regime and under-predicts by 70–120 W on data-center cards.
- **Context window 512 vs. 2048 had no measurable effect** on either predictor or on power, so the model does not need to condition on it.

## Authors & Credibility

Niklas Enskat and Philipp Wiesner are at Technische Universität Berlin (TU Berlin); Wiesner is affiliated with the Distributed Operating Systems group (dos-group), which maintains the released `gpu_power_benchmark` repository and has prior authorship on energy/carbon-aware simulation (e.g. Vessim). The paper is a peer-reviewed preprint accepted at MASCOTS 2026 (34th International Symposium on the Modeling, Analysis and Simulation of Computer and Telecommunication Systems). The methodology follows rigorous statistics: convergence-based stopping rule, four repeats per config, 2000-resample bootstrap CIs, and an explicit noise-floor comparison that frames the achievable accuracy ceiling. Benchmark pipeline and raw data are publicly released.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Genuinely fills a gap. Özcan et al. [13] had already bolted an MFU-based power model onto the Vidur inference simulator without ever validating it against hardware; this paper is the first systematic cross-vendor empirical check, and the noise-floor analysis is a methodological contribution on its own.
- **Rigor**: High. The convergence-stopping rule, four repeats, bootstrap CIs, and direct comparison to the cross-repeat measurement-noise floor let them credibly claim that ~1% MAPE is data-limited rather than model-limited. The wall-power cross-check (Appendix A, R² > 0.99) on two devices is a reasonable scope given telemetry is otherwise the de-facto reference.
- **Reproducibility**: Strong — public code and data, fixed prompt, fixed tokenizer, documented toolchain (PyTorch, HuggingFace Transformers, Hydra, NVML/ROCm, CalFLOPS), and all sweep axes spelled out. Minor caveat: hardware availability limits who can reproduce exactly, but the calibration recipe itself is one-shot and cheap.
- **Significance**: Practically useful for energy-aware training simulators (Vidur-style tooling) that cannot read hardware counters. The "condition on (GPU, dtype, batch size) and nothing else" recipe is directly actionable.
- **Red flags / weaknesses**:
  - The GPU Utilization baseline comparison is partly unfair on MI210: ROCm's `GRBM_COUNT`-derived utilization is effectively a binary activity flag, so "no linear fit defined" is as much a vendor-reporting quirk as a real defeat — the paper acknowledges this but still leans on it as a headline differentiator.
  - The L4's R²=0.37 is a genuine blemish, waved away as "little to predict" because of its 72 W TDP and 64 W idle floor; the per-GPU fit on this widely used inference card is genuinely weak even if MAPE is low in absolute watts.
  - Single-GPU only — no inter-device communication power (collectives, pipeline bubbles, FSDP all-gathers) is captured, so any multi-GPU simulator reusing this fit must supply its own communication-power model and the composed accuracy is unverified.
  - Eager attention with flash/SDPA disabled is a confound-removal choice, but production stacks use fused kernels that raise arithmetic intensity and shift MFU; the authors assert the slope change is exactly what calibration absorbs, but this is not empirically shown.
  - Wall-power validation covers only 2 of 6 devices.
  - Inference (autoregressive decode) is explicitly out of scope and is memory-bound, so the headline "MFU as a power proxy" does not transfer to serving without the future memory-bandwidth extension.

## Strengths

- Cross-vendor (NVIDIA + AMD) coverage that prior work lacked; one of the few MFU studies to include the MI210.
- Methodologically clean: convergence stopping rule, four repeats, bootstrap CIs, and an explicit comparison against the measurement-noise floor that justifies the "no headroom left" claim.
- Honest about boundaries: the batch-1 / memory-bound failure is both quantified (Table IV) and explained mechanistically, rather than buried.
- Practical recipe ("condition on GPU, dtype, batch size and nothing else") that a simulator implementer can apply directly with a one-shot calibration.
- Public benchmark pipeline and data.

## Limitations

- Training-only and single-device; does not model distributed-training communication power or pipeline-bubble idling, so cluster-level energy estimates are unverified.
- Autoregressive inference (decode phase) is memory-bound and outside the validated regime; the authors flag Vidur-style inference simulators as future work.
- Eager attention only — production fused/flash kernels are not tested, even though they raise MFU.
- Per-GPU fit on the inference-class L4 is weak (R²=0.37), and the GPU-Utilization baseline comparison is confounded by MI210's binary activity-flag semantics.
- External wall-power validation is done for only 2 of the 6 devices.

## Relevance

对关注 LLM 训练系统性能与能耗模拟、GPU 功耗建模以及绿色 AI 基础设施的研究者具有直接参考价值，提供了一种可在纯软件模拟器内推断功耗的便携方法；若兴趣在模型本身或算法层（如推理质量、新架构），则相关性较低。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How well does the per-(GPU, dtype, batch) calibration transfer to multi-GPU distributed training once communication power (NCCL/RCCL collectives, FSDP all-gathers, pipeline bubbles) is added — does the composed estimate remain within ~1% MAPE?
- Does the linear MFU-power relationship survive under production fused/flash-attention kernels, which raise arithmetic intensity and shift the MFU distribution upward?
- What complementary memory-bandwidth signal (e.g. achieved HBM bandwidth, or a memory-utilization metric) best extends the model into the batch-1 and decode regimes where MFU collapses?
- Can the recipe be ported to autoregressive inference (Vidur), where decode is memory-bound — what does the equivalent validated power proxy look like there?
- Does the cross-vendor slope spread (1.21–4.49 W per %MFU) collapse onto a single curve if MFU is replaced by raw achieved TFLOPS rather than peak-normalized MFU?
