GDPevo: an evolution-native benchmark where every test-time gain is attributable by design.

Leijun Zhou, Zhihao Liu, Xiang Qu, Chenxu Liu, Yifei Liu, Yanke Yu, Jingzhe Xu, Xuejun Wu, Buyue Qian, Xi Chen (NYU), Yaowei Zheng, Junhao Hu — corresponding

agent-self-evolution benchmark rule-hybridization enterprise-workflows contamination-resistance
◈ Key finding

On a 240-task, 24-group benchmark of GDP-related enterprise workflows, every evolved agent beats its base by 2.59–16.44 pp — yet even the best configuration (Opus-4.8 fewshot) stalls at 67.07%, far below a fully-informed oracle ceiling of 91.6%. Attribution is guaranteed not by post-hoc analysis but by rule hybridization: hidden business rules are scattered across training tasks and recombined in held-out tests.

◆ Why this matters

First evolution-native benchmark where test-time gains are causally attributable to training experience — not memorization or template reuse.

Rule hybridization makes "is the gain real?" a property of the benchmark itself, closing the central methodological gap left by EvoAgentBench, SkillFlow, SEA-Eval.

⚠ Critical edge

The calibration filter base 0.4–0.6 · fewshot-lift 0.1–0.3 · post-evo < 0.8 drops groups where evolution fails or saturates.

Released benchmark is mechanically biased toward "evolution works here" — better for relative agent ranking than absolute claims about self-evolution capability.

01

The mechanism: rule hybridization

Each workflow is decomposed into atomic business rules that are deliberately absent from world knowledge — a company's sponsor-status priority, an invoice expiration date. Subsets seed the 5 training tasks; novel recombinations appear in the 5 held-out tests. Only an agent that inferred the rules and can compose them succeeds.

03

Three counterintuitive findings

FINDING 01

Weaker base ≠ larger headroom.

DeepSeek-V4 has the lowest base yet the smallest gain; Opus-4.8 has the highest base and the largest gain — contradicting the "low base, high headroom" intuition.

DS-V4: 43.58% → +5.21 pp
Opus-4.8: 50.63% → +16.44 pp
FINDING 02

Fewshot overfits; reflect transfers.

Fewshot behaves like SFT and overfits its source domain. Reflect-3 behaves like RL and transfers more robustly across domains.

fewshot off-diagonal: 5/6 negative (worst −5.0 pp)
reflect off-diagonal: up to +6.5 pp (ERP→Finance)
FINDING 03

Naive creator matches off-the-shelf.

A one-sentence hand-written skill creator matches or beats Codex / DeepAgents / OpenCode creators — evolution magnitude is governed by model intelligence, not harness scaffolding.

GPT-5.5: Naive 65.12% vs Codex creator 62.19%
DS-V4: Naive 48.01% vs OpenCode 47.84%

05

Automated construction pipeline

A single orchestrator (Codex + GPT-5.5) scaled V1 (12 groups) to V2 (24 groups) in under two days — a practical answer to data contamination: regenerate fresh versions whenever a release is exposed.

01

Seed discovery

Mine public benchmarks (GDPval, SOP-Bench, JobBench, SaaS-Bench, τ²-Bench, CHI-Bench, Harvey LAB); an agent proposes candidate scenarios.

02

Task-group generation

Orchestrator emits a blueprint (env spec, 10 task statements, atomic rules, task–rule matrix), then spawns 1 env builder + 10 task builders.

03

Calibration & review

Gate: base 0.4–0.6, fewshot lift 0.1–0.3, post-evo <0.8, plus a 5/6 majority vote from 6 independent reviewer agents on leakage & diversity.

06

Strengths & limitations

+ Strengths

  • Attribution by construction. Rule hybridization turns "is the gain real?" into a benchmark property, not a hope — a real advance over post-hoc predecessors.
  • Deterministic graders. LLM only writes test code, never scores — removes LLM-as-judge bias and reproducibility headaches.
  • Cost is first-class. Token, turn, and USD columns accompany every accuracy; one-time skill-gen cost separated from test-time cost.
  • Diagnostic depth. Per-rubric heatmaps, radar profiles, source×target transfer matrices explain why a config wins.
  • Honest about the gap. Best evolved agent far below oracle — current self-evolution is not close to realized.

− Limitations & red flags

  • Calibration = selection bias. Thresholds exclude groups where evolution fails or saturates, inflating apparent reliability.
  • Oracle is not a human expert. 91.6% is "frontier model with full information," not "skilled professional" — headroom framing is looser than the prose implies.
  • Construction circularity. Codex + GPT-5.5 builds envs, tasks, rubrics, reviews — and is one of the evaluated agents. Bias unanalyzed.
  • Cross-domain study is tiny. 3 domains × 1 group; broad SFT-vs-RL claims are underpowered.
  • Skill-based evolution only. Memory, prompt, harness-code, weight updates all untested despite method-agnostic framing.
  • Closed/future model IDs (GPT-5.5, Opus-4.8, GLM-5.2, DS-V4-Pro-Preview) not independently verifiable from the paper.
