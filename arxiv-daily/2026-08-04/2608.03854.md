> **一句话总结：** 在 PubMed RCT 句子分类任务上对照评测三种 Mistral-7B 变体（Base/BioMistral/Instruct）发现，从求和到均值 token 对数似然的打分规则切换会让模型间的校准排名反转（BioMistral 的 ECE 从 0.097 升到 0.289、Instruct 从 0.237 降到 0.096），而精度变化不到 1 个百分点，提示模板选择也会造成 7-24pp 的精度摆动，INT8 对专用模型几乎无损——校准比较必须显式指定打分协议。

> **TL;DR:** A controlled evaluation of three Mistral-7B variants (Base, BioMistral, Instruct) on PubMed RCT (n=2,000) under FP16/INT8/INT4 with four answer-text prompt templates shows that the probability-extraction protocol dominates apparent calibration: switching from summed to mean-token log-likelihood scoring reverses the ECE ranking between BioMistral (0.097 → 0.289) and Instruct (0.237 → 0.096) while accuracy barely moves, template choice swings accuracy by 7-24pp (exceeding model-level effects), and INT8 quantization costs specialized models <=1pp in accuracy, making scoring-rule and template specification first-order experimental decisions for decoder calibration evaluation.

## 核心贡献

- **打分规则敏感性（最重要发现）：** 在 PubMed RCT 上从 sum log-likelihood 切换到 mean-token log-likelihood 会让 BioMistral 与 Instruct 的校准排名反转——BioMistral 平均 ECE 从 0.097 升至 0.289，Instruct 从 0.237 降至 0.096，而专用模型精度变化 <1pp。这表明不指定打分协议的校准比较无法解读。
- **模板变异性：** 跨越不同指令风格和示例域的四个提示模板上，模板选择造成 7-24pp 的精度摆动，量级与模型选择相当甚至更大，并会改变"哪个模型精度最高"的结论（在 Bare-Cont 上 BioMistral 反超 Instruct）。
- **量化增量：** 对 BioMistral/Instruct 等专用模型，INT8 量化精度/F1 变化在 FP16 的 1-2pp 之内；Base 模型在某些模板上 INT8 影响较大（Struct-Delim 上高达 +4.2pp）；INT4（NF4）影响异质但无灾难性退化。
- **单 token 类码解码失败：** BioMistral 在 5/10 的单字母类码模板-精度组合上对 100% 样本预测同一类，macro-F1 仅 0.038-0.143；改用 answer-text 全标签打分后恢复到 0.303-0.367，证明 verbalizer 是一阶设计选择。
- **方法学建议：** 给出针对生物医学解码器校准评估的可操作指南——多打分规则报告、模板作为实验变量、INT8 部署前预声明边界的等效性检验。

## Key Contributions

- **Scoring-rule sensitivity (primary finding):** On PubMed RCT, switching from summed to mean-token log-likelihood scoring reverses the apparent calibration ranking between BioMistral (avg ECE 0.097 → 0.289) and Instruct (0.237 → 0.096) while accuracy changes by <1pp for specialized models (4-6pp for the base model), implying calibration comparisons are uninterpretable without specifying the scoring protocol.
- **Template variation:** Across four answer-text templates spanning instruction styles, exemplar domains, and continuation frames, template choice produces 7-24pp accuracy swings (comparable to or exceeding model-level effects) and changes which model wins; e.g., BioMistral beats Instruct on Bare-Cont despite Instruct leading on average by only +1.3pp.
- **Quantization deltas:** For BioMistral and Instruct, INT8 accuracy/F1 deltas stay within 1-2pp of FP16 (occasionally marginally positive where 95% CIs exclude zero); the base model shows larger INT8 effects on Struct-Delim (+4.2pp); INT4 (NF4) effects are heterogeneous but non-catastrophic.
- **Failure of single-token class-code scoring:** 5/10 letter-code template-precision configs for BioMistral collapse to predicting one class for 100% of examples (macro-F1 0.038-0.143); switching to full answer-text scoring recovers macro-F1 to 0.303-0.367, isolating the verbalizer as a first-order design choice.
- **Methodological prescriptions:** Concrete evaluation guidance for biomedical decoder calibration: report multiple scoring rules, treat templates as experimental variables, and require predeclared equivalence testing before claiming INT8 deployment safety.

## Methodology

**Task and dataset.** PubMed RCT 5-class sequential sentence classification (BACKGROUND, OBJECTIVE, METHODS, RESULTS, CONCLUSIONS) from Dernoncourt & Lee (2017). Main evaluation uses a stratified n=2,000 sample of the test set under the natural class distribution; temperature scaling is fit on 15% of the validation set (n=200, seed 42).

**Models.** Three 7B Mistral variants sharing architecture but differing in training history: Mistral-7B-v0.3 (generic base baseline), BioMistral-7B (continued pretraining of Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.1 on PubMed Central), Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 (instruction-tuned). A fine-tuned 110M-parameter PubMedBERT encoder is included as a supervised reference point (FP32, ~176K labeled examples, balanced 400/class sample — explicitly non-comparable to the few-shot decoders).

**Scoring protocols.** For each example and candidate label, decoders compute either (a) summed log-probability of the label text tokens, or (b) length-normalized mean-token log-likelihood (label names span 3-5 tokens, motivating this normalization). Both are softmaxed across the five classes. PubMedBERT uses the encoder classification-head softmax directly. Token boundary correctness is verified via independent label re-encoding with `add_special_tokens=False` and concatenation; each prediction stores full token provenance for audit.

**Prompt templates.** Four few-shot answer-text templates with one common verbalizer (literal class names) but different instructions, delimiters, exemplar domains, and continuation frames:
- **Struct-Delim** — triple-quote delimiters + explicit category definitions; discovered via evolutionary search.
- **Bare-Cont** — bare text + category definitions; discovered via evolutionary search.
- **Min-Inst** — concise instruction + domain-shifted diabetes/GLP-1 exemplars; LLM-designed.
- **Alt-Frame** — switched continuation frame ("Input:"/"Role:") + chronic kidney disease exemplars; LLM-designed.

**Evolutionary prompt optimization.** A small AlphaEvolve-style LLM-guided mutation search on a 100-example development subset, using BioMistral-7B FP16 with multi-objective fitness (macro-F1, ECE, Brier, NLL, with explicit anti-label-collapse penalty). Ran on a single H100 80GB for ~3 hours across 29 candidates, producing two Pareto-front templates (Struct-Delim, Bare-Cont). Authors explicitly flag this as a limitation: prompts were selected on a (later-corrected) test-derived subset using a pre-boundary-fix scorer, so results from evolved templates are exploratory rather than cleanly held-out.

**Quantization conditions.** FP16 (14GB), LLM.int8() mixed-precision via bitsandbytes (~50% VRAM), and NF4 INT4 with double quantization (~4GB, ~75% reduction).

**Calibration.** Post-hoc temperature scaling (Guo et al. 2017) with a scalar T fit on a held-out split via 64-point geometric grid search over [0.01, 100] minimizing NLL.

**Metrics.** Accuracy, macro-F1, ECE (15 equal-width bins), Brier, NLL; majority-class and stratified-random baselines. 95% bootstrap CIs (B=2,000). A boundary-corrected v3 scorer is used for all reported decoder results.

## Results & Highlights

- **Scoring-rule reversal (headline):** Switching from sum to mean-token log-likelihood reverses the calibration ranking — BioMistral avg ECE rises from 0.097 to 0.289, Instruct drops from 0.237 to 0.096, while accuracy moves <1pp for specialized models and 4-6pp for the base model (Fig. 1).
- **INT8 stability for specialized models:** For BioMistral and Instruct, all INT8-vs-FP16 accuracy deltas are ≤1pp and all F1 deltas ≤2.1pp; where 95% CIs exclude zero, the direction uniformly favors INT8 (e.g., BioMistral Bare-Cont ΔF1=+0.021 [+0.009,+0.034]; Instruct Struct-Delim ΔAcc=+0.008 [+0.001,+0.014]). The base model shows up to +4.2pp INT8 shift on Struct-Delim.
- **Template sensitivity dominates:** On the test set the base model's Struct-Delim-vs-Bare-Cont accuracy gap reaches 17-24pp depending on precision, exceeding the effect of model choice; on the validation set, switching Min-Inst → Alt-Frame improves base accuracy by 7.3pp.
- **Accuracy reversal by template:** Under sum scoring, Instruct leads on Struct-Delim (+5.9-6.2pp) but BioMistral leads on Bare-Cont (-2.8 to -4.0pp); the mean Instruct advantage is only +1.3pp. BioMistral Bare-Cont at INT8 reaches the highest decoder accuracy 0.706; Instruct Struct-Delim is second at 0.698 (FP16/INT8/INT4).
- **Temperature scaling under sum scoring:** BioMistral fitted T=1.25-1.67 yields 1.1-2.3× ECE reductions; Instruct fitted T=2.23-4.64 yields 2.7-4.2× reductions (e.g., Instruct Struct-Delim FP16 ECE 0.307 → 0.073). But this is scoring-rule-specific and reverts under mean scoring.
- **Single-token class-code collapse:** 5/10 letter-code configs for BioMistral predict one class for 100% of examples (macro-F1 0.038-0.143); permutation control confirms positional/token-frequency bias; switching to answer-text rescues macro-F1 to 0.303-0.367.
- **Supervised reference ceiling:** PubMedBERT reaches 82.7% accuracy (Brier 0.254, ECE 0.072) but uses ~176K labeled examples and a balanced sample, precluding direct comparison to the best decoder's 70.6% (5-shot).
- **Effect-size ordering:** Authors rank sources of variation as (1) scoring rule, (2) supervision regime, (3) template choice, (4) model variant, (5) quantization.

The methodology demonstrates strong engineering rigor: boundary-corrected v3 scorer, full token provenance per prediction, 95% bootstrap CIs (B=2,000) with paired intervals for quantization deltas, explicit majority/random baselines, and citation of the relevant calibration (Guo et al. 2017), surface-form-competition (Holtzman et al. 2021), and quantization (LLM.int8(), QLoRA) literature. The paper sits squarely in an active 2025-2026 line on quantized-LLM calibration (Zhong et al. 2025) and instruction-tuning calibration effects (Huang et al. 2026; Sanz-Guerrero et al. 2026).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The core observation — that summed vs. mean-token log-likelihood scoring can reverse apparent calibration rankings — is a genuine and underappreciated methodological contribution. It extends surface-form competition (Holtzman 2021) and answer-choice sensitivity (Cho, So, Lee 2026) into the specific regime of biomedical decoder calibration under quantization, and the explicit reversal demonstration is the paper's strongest result.
- **Rigor:** Bootstrap CIs, paired delta intervals, a permutation control for the class-code collapse, and a boundary-corrected scorer reflect good experimental hygiene. The paper honestly discloses that evolved templates (Struct-Delim, Bare-Cont) were selected on a test-derived subset with a pre-boundary-fix scorer and never re-evaluated cleanly — a real provenance flaw that they flag rather than hide.
- **Weaknesses in the central comparison:** The BioMistral-vs-Instruct contrast is **not** a clean instruction-tuning ablation. BioMistral inherits instruction tuning from Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.1 and differs from Instruct-v0.3 in both base version and domain-continued pretraining. The paper says this explicitly, but several framings ("instruction-tuned vs. continued-pretrained") still lean on language the design cannot support.
- **PubMedBERT apples-to-oranges:** The 82.7% supervised reference uses ~176K labels and a balanced 400/class sample while decoders use 5-shot prompting on the natural distribution. The paper repeatedly caveats non-comparability but still uses PubMedBERT as a ceiling reference point; readers may over-read the gap.
- **Missing ablations:** No equal-length verbalizer control despite the authors themselves identifying the 3-5 token length prior as the mechanism behind the sum/mean reversal; no label-permutation controls on the answer-text scoring (only on the class-code variant); no chat-template condition for the Instruct model despite citing Sanz-Guerrero et al. (2026) showing chat templates independently aggravate miscalibration.
- **Single-task, single-model-family scope:** Only PubMed RCT, only 7B Mistral-family decoders, only bitsandbytes INT8/NF4 quantization (no GPTQ, AWQ, or distillation). The "ordering of effect sizes" is presented as a qualitative finding but is itself scoring-rule-sensitive and drawn from one task.
- **Calibration sample size:** Temperature scaling is fit and evaluated at n=200 with a 64-point grid, producing noisy T estimates; the authors acknowledge this.
- **Reproducibility:** Strong — named HF checkpoints, fixed seed 42, explicit scorer version, released template definitions, and full token provenance. The main reproduction risk is the evolutionary prompt search, which depends on stochastic LLM mutations.
- **Significance:** Methodologically valuable cautionary tale for anyone reporting calibration numbers for decoder LLMs. Practitioners will quote the sum/mean reversal finding. Less significant as a quantization benchmark given the narrow scope.

## Strengths

- Headline finding (scoring-rule reversal of calibration rankings) is novel, clearly demonstrated, and immediately actionable.
- Honest, detailed Limitations section that owns the prompt-selection provenance issue and the non-clean instruction-tuning contrast.
- Strong reproducibility hygiene: named checkpoints, fixed seed, scorer version pinning, bootstrap CIs, permutation control.
- The class-code collapse ablation with a permutation control cleanly isolates the verbalizer as a first-order design variable.
- Practical evaluation prescriptions (multi-rule scoring, template as variable, predeclared INT8 equivalence margins) are concrete and adoptable.

## Limitations

- Single task (PubMed RCT) — generalization to relation extraction, clinical NLI, etc. unverified.
- Single model family and size (7B Mistral); scaling dynamics unknown.
- Only bitsandbytes INT8/NF4 quantization; GPTQ, AWQ, distillation untested.
- BioMistral-vs-Instruct is confounded by base-version and domain-pretraining differences, not a clean instruction-tuning effect.
- Evolved templates were selected on a non-cleanly-held-out subset using a pre-boundary-fix scorer.
- Temperature scaling estimated on n=200 with a coarse 64-point grid.
- No chat-template condition for Instruct; no equal-length verbalizer or label-permutation control on answer-text scoring.
- PubMedBERT comparison uses different label counts and class distribution.

## Relevance

直接命中"大模型校准 / 量化可靠性 / 评测方法学"这条研究线：作者用对照实验证明打分规则与提示模板是解码器校准评测中的一阶变量，并为量化部署前的校准验证给出可操作清单，对任何做 LLM 评测、量化或生物医学 NLP 部署的工作都有方法论借鉴价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the sum-vs-mean scoring reversal persist with **equal-length verbalizers** or label-permutation controls, which the authors propose but do not run?
- How does the calibration ranking behave under **chat-template formatting** for Instruct, given Sanz-Guerrero et al. (2026) show chat templates independently worsen miscalibration?
- Do the scoring-rule and template effects replicate on other biomedical classification tasks (clinical NLI, relation extraction) and on **larger** (e.g., 70B) or non-Mistral families?
- How do **GPTQ/AWQ** quantization schemes compare to bitsandbytes INT8/NF4 on the accuracy-calibration frontier?
- Can a **label-permutation-invariant** scoring rule be designed that removes the surface-form length prior and stabilizes the calibration ranking across normalizations?
- Is the base model's +4.2pp INT8 shift on Struct-Delim a quantization artifact or a template-quantization interaction worth characterizing separately?
