> **一句话总结：** 论文提出 PAST-Bench——首个针对个人智能体"在线自我进化"能力的性能归因基准（26 个场景、204 个 episode，配对开关持久化的对照实验），揭示出"持久化带来增益但能力间极度不均衡、且相同分数增益可能源自完全不同的持久化路径"；并据此诊断出 5 类失败模式，设计出 Hermes+（Plan/Render/Route/Gate/Close 五阶段干预），在 Update 能力上取得最显著提升（Δ +0.12→+0.24）。

> **TL;DR:** PAST-Bench is the first trajectory-level, performance-attribution benchmark for personal-agent "online self-evolution," pairing 26 scenarios / 204 episodes with matched persistence-on/off controls and trace-level mechanism evidence across 7 base models (GPT-5.4, Claude Sonnet/Opus 4.6, etc.) and 4 agent frameworks (Hermes, nanobot, ZeroClaw, Agent-Zero). Retained experience reliably helps (Overall Δ from +0.13 to +0.24 across models), but gains are sharply capability-specific and two agents with identical Δ (e.g. Hermes vs. nanobot both at +0.13) can differ in mechanism evidence (0.64 vs. 0.57). The authors diagnose 5 failure modes and propose Hermes+, raising Overall Δ from +0.13 to +0.15 and Mech from 0.64 to +0.73 on MiniMax-M2.7, with its largest, super-additive gain on Update (Δ = +0.24 vs. +0.16 for closeout alone).

## 核心贡献

- **提出 PAST-Bench——首个面向"在线自我进化"的性能归因基准：** 把评测单元从单任务分数升级为"任务家族内的 episode 序列"，共 26 个场景、204 个 episode，覆盖 Memory（5/41）、Procedural Reuse（8/64）、Information Gathering（6/48）、Update（7/51）四类核心能力。每个评估 episode 都配对"开/关持久化"两种条件，使后期任务的增益可被归因到持久化层而非基座模型或运行时噪声。
- **首次把"任务分增益"与"机制证据（Mechanism Evidence）"解耦：** 通过保存的 artifact 与运行时遥测（memory 写读、skill 创建/修补、session 检索、artifact diff、更新正确性信号）量化"增益是否真的走了预期路径"，揭示出"相同 Δ 背后是迥异的持久化路径"这一被一次性打分掩盖的现象。
- **横向覆盖 7 个基座模型 × 4 个智能体框架：** 在 Table 2/3 中分别固定框架变模型、固定模型变框架，从而把模型贡献与运行时设计贡献分离开——例如 Agent-Zero 在 4 个能力里 3 个退步（Overall Δ = −0.08），而 Hermes 是唯一四个能力全部为正的基线框架。
- **基于诊断设计出 Hermes+：** 把观测到的失败归纳为 5 个互斥类别（未先查状态就规划、记忆格式难复用、流程未存为可执行 skill、未先检索就作答、修正后旧值仍泄漏），分别对应 Plan/Render/Route/Gate/Close 五个运行时插入点，可独立开关与消融。
- **诚实报告不确定性：** 明确指出 Hermes+ 的 +0.02 Overall 增益小于 run-to-run 方差（Hermes 0.13±0.04 vs. Hermes+ 0.15±0.06），把 Hermes+ 定位为"诊断脚手架"而非通用改进，避免过度宣称。

## Key Contributions

- **PAST-Bench, a trajectory-level performance-attribution benchmark.** Evaluates 26 task-family scenarios (204 fresh-session episodes) over four capability dimensions, each with cold / learn / evaluation / control episode roles and matched w/o-evolve vs. w/-evolve conditions that share prompt, grader, tool stack, and seed—so any later-task score gap is most plausibly attributable to the persistence substrate.
- **Decoupling outcome gain from mechanism evidence.** Beyond the family-balanced self-evolution gap Δ = S(w/ evolve) − S(w/o evolve), the benchmark reports a Mechanism-Evidence (Mech) score over saved artifacts and runtime telemetry, exposing cases where identical Δ arises from different persistence pathways.
- **First benchmark to jointly support retained-experience attribution, model-side, framework-side isolation, and trajectory diagnosis.** Table 1 contrasts PAST-Bench with GAIA, AgentBench, VisualWebArena, WorkArena, OSWorld, LongMemEval, LoCoMo, SkillsBench, and AgentBoard; all of those cover only strict subsets of these four axes.
- **A diagnostic-driven framework, Hermes+.** Five independent runtime mechanisms—planning guidance (E1), typed memory binding (E2), skill lifecycle/routing (E3), retrieval-before-answer gate (E4), closeout/flush (E5)—each mapped one-to-one onto a loop stage, enabling clean per-mechanism ablation.
- **Honest, capability-decomposed reporting.** The paper repeatedly warns that Overall Δ hides capability-specific structure, that Hermes+'s +0.02 overall gain is below run-to-run variance, and that Hermes+ regresses on Procedural (Δ −0.02) and on some base models (DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Claude Opus 4.6).

## Methodology

**Evaluation unit — the task family.** Each family is an ordered sequence of fresh-session episodes sharing a latent rule (Memory), a reusable artifact (Procedural Reuse), a correction (Update), or a pre-seeded reference (Information Gathering). Episodes play four roles: *cold* (first contact, no persistence yet), *learn* / *update* (deposit the target into the substrate), *evaluation* (probe reuse in a fresh session with trigger wording removed), and *control* (no-retention, distractor, stale, wrong-mechanism) — the control upper bound must be cleared before Δ is accepted as evidence of self-evolution.

**Strict context-clearing protocol.** Between episodes the framework's volatile context is wiped, so any improvement must flow through the persistent substrate (memory store, skill file, playbook, edited rule), not residual prompt overlap. This distinguishes PAST-Bench from long-context or incremental-injection regimes.

**Matched ablation.** Each evaluation episode is graded twice under identical prompt/grader/tool/seed: w/o-evolve (no access to family-produced state) and w/-evolve (full persistence). The primary metric is Δ_f = S(w/) − S(w/o); capability-level Δ is the macro-average over families.

**Mechanism-Evidence score (Mech).** Computed from write/read events, skill creation/patching, session-search calls, artifact diffs, and update-correctness signals, with per-component weights (Equation 2, Appendix B.3). An agent that writes artifacts but never reads them, or succeeds via the wrong substrate, does not earn credit on Δ alone. Sensitivity analysis (Appendix B.5) shows Mech is stable under component-weight perturbation (Spearman 0.868–0.997).

**Hermes+ design — five runtime insertions.** Diagnosed from low-gain traces in §4.2: (E1) Plan-time consultation gate conditioning drafts on saved state; (E2) typed memory bindings rendering only the in-scope current value and suppressing superseded entries; (E3) ranked, patchable procedural skills with applicability conditions; (E4) retrieval-before-answer gate that withholds a draft answer once if recall-dependent content has not been read; (E5) episode-closeout extraction and synchronous flush of the corrected binding so the next fresh session retrieves only the new value. Each is wired as an independent drop-in at its own loop stage.

**Judge and human validation.** All runs use MiniMax-M2.7 as the LLM judge (temperature 0, max 8192 tokens). On 48 blinded samples, judge–human within-0.25 agreement is 91.7% (human–human 97.9%), and judge–human within-0.5 is 100%-equivalent; the judge is treated as a scalable grader, not a substitute for human judgment.

## Results & Highlights

- **Retained experience reliably helps but the magnitude is capability- and model-specific.** Across 7 base models (Hermes framework), Overall Δ ranges from +0.13 (MiniMax-M2.7) to +0.24 (GPT-5.4); GPT-5.4 concentrates movement on Memory (38%) and Update (35%), while GLM-5.1 puts nearly half (46%) on Update and Kimi K2.6 nearly half (49%) on Memory (Table 2).
- **GPT-5.4 is the strongest configuration:** w/-evolve Overall = 0.75 (vs. 0.51 w/o-evolve), Δ = +0.24, Mech = 0.80 — the highest in the benchmark; on Update alone GPT-5.4 reaches Δ = +0.34 (w/ 0.80 vs. w/o 0.46).
- **Identical headline Δ can hide completely different mechanism evidence.** Hermes and nanobot both reach Overall Δ = +0.13 on MiniMax-M2.7, but Hermes Mech = 0.64 while nanobot Mech = 0.57, because nanobot earns its Δ from a single capability with no consistent write-then-read trace (Table 3). Agent-Zero regresses (Overall Δ = −0.08, Mech 0.39), the only framework that moves three of four capabilities downward.
- **Hermes+ raises both reported means on MiniMax-M2.7:** Overall Δ +0.13 → +0.15, Mech 0.64 → 0.73, with the largest and clearly super-additive gain on Update (Δ = +0.24, well above any single mechanism: closeout E5 alone +0.16, retrieval gate E4 alone +0.06) (Table 3, Table 4).
- **Single-mechanism ablations support the failure-to-mechanism mapping:** E2 (Render) gives the highest Memory persistence-on score (0.80), E3 (Route) the largest single-mechanism Procedural Δ (+0.10), E4 (Gate) the largest Info Δ (+0.17), E5 (Close) the strongest single-mechanism Update Δ (+0.16) (Table 4).
- **Cross-model transfer of Hermes+ (tuned only on MiniMax-M2.7) is positive but not uniform:** on 5 base models it matches or improves the Hermes baseline on 3 of 5 — MiniMax-M2.7 (+0.13→+0.15), Claude Sonnet 4.6 (+0.20→+0.22), GPT-5.4 (flat +0.24) — while DeepSeek-V4-Pro and Claude Opus 4.6 regress slightly (Table 6).
- **The +0.02 Overall gain of Hermes+ is smaller than run-to-run variation** (Hermes 0.13 ± 0.04 vs. Hermes+ 0.15 ± 0.06 across 3 runs); the clearer mean shift is on Update (+0.12→+0.24), although its σΔ also rises from 0.01 to 0.09.
- **Mechanism scoring is robust to perturbation:** changing one component weight from 1.0 to 0.8 or 1.2 gives Spearman 0.970–0.997; threshold agreement (Mech ≥ 0.5) is 98.8–100% (Appendix B.5).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty — high.** The matched w/o-evolve vs. w/-evolve design plus trace-level Mech score is genuinely new; Table 1 shows even memory-specific benchmarks (LongMemEval, LoCoMo) lack framework-comparison and trajectory-diagnosis axes, and SkillsBench only provides a proxy (△) for retained experience. The decoupling of "did the score go up" from "did it go up via the intended pathway" is the benchmark's distinctive contribution.
- **Rigor — strong on attribution, honest on effect size.** The authors explicitly state the +0.02 Hermes+ Overall gain is below run-to-run σ, treat Hermes+ as a "diagnostic scaffold rather than a universal improvement," and report that Update's σΔ widens from 0.01 to 0.09 after intervention. This honesty is commendable and rare.
- **Reproducibility — good but with caveats.** Code, frozen snapshots (Hermes v2026.4.16), per-task cost (Table 12), and inference settings (Appendix E) are provided. However, all main results use a single judge model (MiniMax-M2.7) with no judge-model variation, and the LLM judge–human within-0.25 agreement is only 91.7% (vs. 97.9% human–human), meaning roughly 8% of samples are judged more loosely than humans would judge.
- **Significance — addresses a real gap.** As personal agents (Hermes, OpenClaw, Agent-Zero, nanobot) proliferate with built-in memory/skill stacks, having a benchmark that can say *why* a later task improved is exactly what the field needs; the capability-specific failure taxonomy (5 disjoint categories, one per loop stage) is independently reusable.
- **Red flags / specific weaknesses:**
  - **Synthetic, isolated task families.** The authors themselves flag this in §6: families are constructed in isolation, and there is no test of cross-family interference, longer horizons, or human-authored scenarios — ecological validity is limited.
  - **Hermes+ is tuned on a single model (MiniMax-M2.7) and does not transfer uniformly:** it regresses on DeepSeek-V4-Pro (Δ +0.17→+0.15) and Claude Opus 4.6 (Δ +0.19→+0.18), and on the Procedural capability (Δ −0.02 in the full system).
  - **Procedural reuse is the weakest, most fragile capability:** Hermes+ and several single mechanisms (E2, E3, E5) push Procedural Δ negative, and the focused diagnosis (Table 5) shows removing E2 *raises* Procedural Δ (from 0.085 to 0.108), suggesting E2's typed memory binding actively interferes with skill routing — a real design tension left unresolved.
  - **Mech is a consistency score, not causal proof.** The authors acknowledge (§6) that Mech measures alignment with an *expected* pathway and not causal necessity; counterfactual artifact deletion/replacement is left to future work.
  - **Agent-Zero fairness asterisk:** giving one framework 4× the wall-clock budget (1200s vs. 300s) and then explicitly declining to call it head-to-head is defensible but means the cross-framework ranking should be read as design-point characterization, not a leaderboard.
  - **Benchmark contamination acknowledged but not audited:** the paper cites Berkeley RDI and the SWE-Bench illusion critique yet provides no contamination check on its own synthetic families.

## Strengths

- First benchmark to cleanly attribute later-task gains to retained experience via matched persistence-on/off controls with shared prompt/grader/seed.
- Mechanism-Evidence score exposes the "same Δ, different pathway" phenomenon invisible to one-shot scoring — a genuinely useful diagnostic.
- Capability decomposition reveals model-specific strength profiles (e.g., GPT-5.4 broad vs. GLM-5.1 Update-heavy vs. Kimi K2.6 Memory-heavy).
- Failure taxonomy → mechanism mapping is principled and independently ablatable, making Hermes+ a clean research scaffold.
- Intellectual honesty: explicit variance reporting, capability-specific regressions, and refraining from overclaiming the +0.02 Overall gain.

## Limitations

- Task families are synthetic and evaluated in isolation; no cross-family transfer, long-horizon drift, or human-authored scenarios.
- Only 26 scenarios / 204 episodes — small relative to GAIA or AgentBench, and capability coverage (especially Update's 7 families) is thin.
- Hermes+ is tuned on a single base model; transfer to DeepSeek-V4-Pro and Claude Opus 4.6 regresses.
- Procedural Reuse is the fragile capability: several Hermes+ mechanisms harm it, and the E2↔E3 interaction is not resolved.
- Single LLM judge (MiniMax-M2.7); judge–human within-0.25 agreement is 91.7%, not the 97.9% human–human ceiling.
- Mech is a pathway-consistency signal, not a causal counterfactual — does not establish that the saved artifact *caused* the correct answer.
- Capability space is limited to memory/procedural/info-gathering/update; stronger RSI (new tool strategies, multi-agent coordination, meta-learning of what to store) is out of scope.

## Relevance

直接相关于个人智能体与"自我进化"智能体研究方向——PAST-Bench 提供了首个能把"持久化带来的增益"与"基座模型/运行时噪声"分离的归因基准，对评估带记忆与技能库的长期智能体（如 Hermes/OpenClaw 类）极具方法学价值；Hermes+ 的五阶段失败-机制映射对设计持久化运行时也有直接工程参考。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does PAST-Bench behavior change when families span longer horizons (e.g., 20+ episodes) and when experience acquired in one family is reused in a different one — does interference dominate?
- Would a counterfactual Mech variant (delete/replace the candidate artifact and measure behavioral change) re-rank the frameworks, and does it agree with the current consistency-based Mech?
- Can the E2↔E3 tension (typed memory binding hurting procedural skill routing) be resolved by an adaptive router that decides which substrate should own a given piece of experience, as the paper hints at in §6?
- How sensitive are the rankings to the judge model? Replacing MiniMax-M2.7 with GPT-5.4 or Claude Opus 4.6 as judge might shift borderline cases given the 91.7% within-0.25 judge–human agreement.
- Does Hermes+ transfer to stronger, fully recursive self-improvement settings (parameter updates, learned retrievers), or do its five mechanisms saturate once the base model itself is being fine-tuned?
- Given Agent-Zero's 4× wall-clock budget, what happens to framework rankings under matched token-cost rather than matched task-count budgets (Appendix D.6 reports cost but not a cost-matched leaderboard)?
