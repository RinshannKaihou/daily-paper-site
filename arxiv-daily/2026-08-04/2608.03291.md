> **一句话总结：** 本文在不假设 CoT 忠实反映内部计算的前提下，通过对 LLM 可见推理链中句子功能角色（规划、赋值、验证、回溯、定稿等）的动态序列进行建模，在布尔可满足性（SAT/UNSAT）任务上揭示了 SAT 失败的"早熟验证坍缩"与 UNSAT 失败的"用赋值搜索替代反证"两种模式，并能提前预警；用一个针对反证流程的提示干预将 Llama3-70B 在 UNSAT 上的准确率从 13.3% 提升至 85.0%，纠正了 84.6% 的错误 SAT 结论。

> **TL;DR:** Without assuming that verbalized chain-of-thought (CoT) faithfully reflects internal computation, the authors tag sentences in visible CoTs by reasoning role (planning, assignment, verification, backtracking, finalization, contradiction-search, etc.) and analyze the resulting dynamics on Boolean satisfiability (SAT/UNSAT) tasks across five LLMs. They identify "premature verification collapse" on SAT instances (failing traces are ~half as long, more cyclic, lower-entropy, and finalize earlier; e.g., Llama3-8B wrong traces: 40 sentences vs Qwen3-14B correct: 60, transition entropy 0.58 vs 0.85) and a procedure mismatch on UNSAT instances where models wrongly conclude SAT. A targeted proof-search prompt intervention on Llama3-70B's wrong-SAT cases raises accuracy from 13.3% to 85.0%, correcting 44/52 (84.6%) failures, whereas a generic retry corrects only 6/52 (11.5%).

## 核心贡献

- **能力匹配的研究框架**：为每个模型识别 0.30 ≤ p ≤ 0.90 的"能力前沿"区间（混合成功区），在该区间内同一问题不同模型既有成功也有失败，避免了"完美掌握"与"完全无能"的混淆，从而能在可控条件下分析能力崩溃。
- **SAT 失败的可重复动态签名——"早熟验证坍缩"**：在匹配的可满足实例上，错误推理链更早进入子句检查（验证）、反复重访相似操作、更早定稿，尽管覆盖的子句数量与正确链相当。该模式在跨模型对比中最强，在模型内对比中只有"重复"与"低熵"是稳健信号。
- **可见推理动态的早期预警**：在 50/50 校准/测试划分下，对推理链前缀逐步重算动态分数，Llama3-8B 有 80.2% 的错误链在给出最终答案前被标记（中位提前量 23 句），OLMo2-13B 为 78.2%（17.5 句），但 Llama3-70B 仅 35.7%（中位提前量为 0），说明该信号具有模型依赖性、并非通用失效检测器。
- **UNSAT 上的任务特定流程错配与诊断驱动的修正**：在不可满足实例上，模型常以"提出并检查候选赋值"的方式错误地得出 SAT 结论，而非构造反证。基于此诊断设计的目标化反证搜索提示（分情形、传播强制结论、搜索矛盾），将 Llama3-70B 准确率从 13.3% 提升至 85.0%，纠正了 44/52（84.6%）错误 SAT 结论，而泛化重试仅修正 6/52（11.5%），证明修正必须针对被诊断出的具体流程。
- **方法论上的清醒**：明确声明不把可见 CoT 当作内部计算的忠实转录，所有结论被定位为"行为层面"证据，并通过移除最终答案相关特征（dynamics-only AUROC：Llama3-8B 0.800、Llama3-70B 0.795、OLMo2-13B 0.905）来排除"答案泄漏"解释。

## Key Contributions

- **Capability-matched framework for studying reasoning failure.** Selects per-model difficulty regimes (empirical accuracy in 0.30–0.90) so that the same problem can yield both correct and wrong complete traces, enabling controlled comparisons near each model's capability boundary rather than at a fixed difficulty that is trivial for some and unreachable for others. Of 127 matched problem–condition pairs, 91 are divergent.
- **A recurring SAT failure mode: premature verification collapse.** On matched satisfiable instances, wrong traces enter clause-checking earlier, revisit similar reasoning operations, and finalize sooner than correct traces despite comparable clause coverage. In the Llama3-8B-wrong vs Qwen3-14B-correct comparison, wrong traces are ~3294 vs ~7936 chars, ~40 vs 60 sentences, verification density 0.61 vs 0.49, cycle rate 0.56 vs 0.45, transition entropy 0.58 vs 0.85, finalization at 77.1% vs 89.8% of the trace.
- **Early-warning signals from visible reasoning dynamics.** By recomputing per-feature z-scores (calibrated on each model's own correct T0 traces) over increasing trace prefixes, 80.2% of Llama3-8B wrong-complete traces and 78.2% of OLMo2-13B traces are flagged before final-answer emission (median lead times 23 and 17.5 sentences), though Llama3-70B reaches only 35.7% with zero median lead time, explicitly characterizing the signal as model-dependent.
- **Task-specific procedure mismatch and diagnosis-informed correction on UNSAT.** On unsatisfiable instances, models (especially Llama3-70B) wrongly conclude SAT by checking candidate assignments rather than constructing contradictions. Llama3-70B's frontier accuracy drops from 0.526 (SAT) to 0.159 (UNSAT) with a 0.800 wrong-SAT rate. A targeted proof-search prompt raises accuracy from 13.3% to 85.0%, correcting 44/52 (84.6%) wrong-SAT completions (McNemar-significant, bootstrap 95% CI 61.5–84.6 pp), while a generic retry corrects only 6/52 (11.5%), with SAT-commitment density falling from 0.0263 to 0.0105 and contradiction-search density rising from 0.0658 to 0.1097.
- **Honest behavioral framing.** The analysis explicitly avoids assuming CoT faithfulness, locates all findings as behavioral evidence, and rules out final-label leakage by showing dynamics-only AUROC remains high (0.800 / 0.795 / 0.905 for Llama3-8B / Llama3-70B / OLMo2-13B) after removing answer-related features.

## Methodology

**Task.** Boolean satisfiability in conjunctive normal form (CNF) using the SATBench task-generator levels L2–L6. SAT correctness is solver-verified against every clause; UNSAT correctness is the solver-certified label. This gives externally established ground truth, systematically variable complexity, and two asymmetric procedures (SAT = exhibit one assignment; UNSAT = rule out the entire space via case construction and contradiction) within one formalism.

**Models and prompts.** Five configurations: Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-14B, Llama3-8B, Llama3-70B, OLMo2-13B. Each formula is sampled under three prompt conditions holding the formula and required answer fixed: T0 (truthful baseline), D1 (oversight-pressure, telling the model an overseer evaluates whether its reasoning looks careful), D2 (attentiveness-pressure, asking it to track clauses, check assumptions, show work).

**Capability matching.** A model–level pair is on the capability frontier when 0.30 ≤ empirical accuracy ≤ 0.90. This ad hoc band isolates mixed-success regimes where both correct and wrong complete traces exist. Comparisons are between divergent problem–condition pairs (one model wrong, another correct on the same formula and prompt); the principal comparison is Llama3-8B wrong vs Qwen3-14B correct, supplemented by within-family Llama3-8B wrong vs Llama3-70B correct.

**Role annotation and dynamics features.** Each CoT is split into sentences and assigned a coarse role via a deterministic rule-based regex classifier. SAT roles: planning, assignment, verification, backtracking, finalization, other. UNSAT adds contradiction_search, unsat_proof, sat_commitment, clause_check. From the role sequence they form a row-normalized transition matrix and compute role densities, cycle rate, weighted self-transition, transition entropy, finalization timing, and clause coverage. Differences are tested with paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with bootstrap CIs over matched pairs.

**Early-warning detector.** Features are recomputed over trace prefixes (25/50/75/100%) and standardized per model and level using calibration-set correct T0 traces; a trace is flagged when its score crosses a per-model threshold. Problems are split 50/50 by problem ID (no formula appears in both partitions). Lead time is the number of sentences between the first flag and the final answer.

**Intervention.** Original Llama3-70B `wrong_sat_complete` cases (n=52) are rerun under (a) a generic retry that asks for another careful attempt, and (b) a targeted proof-search prompt instructing case splitting, forced-consequence propagation, contradiction search, and a complete-assignment check before any SAT claim. The contrast separates whether failures are unstable under resampling vs. specifically responsive to the diagnosed procedure.

## Results & Highlights

- **Premature verification collapse (SAT, matched divergent pairs, Table 1).** For the principal Llama3-8B-wrong vs Qwen3-14B-correct comparison (n=105 pairs): CoT length 3293.60 vs 7936.14 chars (Δ = −4642.54, p < .001), sentence count 40.11 vs 60.00 (Δ = −19.89, p < .001), verification density 0.61 vs 0.49 (Δ = +0.12, p < .001), full-CoT cycle rate 0.56 vs 0.45 (Δ = +0.11, p < .001), transition entropy 0.58 vs 0.85 (Δ = −0.27, p < .001). Clause coverage does not differ (p = 0.206), so failing traces do not simply ignore more of the formula. Finalization begins at 77.1% of the trace for wrong vs 89.8% for correct.
- **Cross-family robustness vs within-family nuance (Table 1).** OLMo2-13B-wrong vs Qwen3-14B-correct shows the same collapse even more strongly (length 2059 vs 7901, entropy 0.44 vs 0.85). The within-family Llama3-8B-wrong vs Llama3-70B-correct comparison is markedly mixed: CoT length (Δ = +1270, p = 1.0000 ns) and sentence count (Δ = +9.45, p = 1.0000 ns) reverse direction and are not significant; only backtracking density (Δ = +0.03, p < .001), cycle rate (Δ = +0.01, p = .0471), and transition entropy (Δ = −0.03, p = .0133) remain significant. Repetition and reduced variability are the robust signature; length and verification effects depend on model family.
- **Model-dependent early warning (Fig. 5).** In the test half, 80.2% of Llama3-8B wrong-complete traces are flagged before the final answer (median lead time 23 sentences), 78.2% for OLMo2-13B (17.5 sentences), but only 35.7% for Llama3-70B (zero median lead time). The score rises above chance near the end of the trace for the first two models and remains weaker for Llama3-70B — explicitly not a universal failure detector.
- **UNSAT directional failure (Fig. 11).** Llama3-70B's frontier accuracy drops from 0.526 on SAT to 0.159 on UNSAT, with a 0.800 wrong-SAT rate. After removing answer-related features, dynamics-only AUROC stays high (0.800 Llama3-8B, 0.795 Llama3-70B, 0.905 OLMo2-13B), but pre-final-only discrimination is modest (AUROC 0.507, 0.570, 0.569) — UNSAT failures can be explained after the fact but not flagged mid-reasoning. The wrong-SAT rate increases for Llama3-70B under D1 and D2 relative to T0 (Fig. 12), so the failure is not a baseline-prompt artifact.
- **Targeted proof-search intervention (Table 2).** On the same 52 Llama3-70B `wrong_sat_complete` cases: original accuracy 13.3%; generic retry 10.0% (corrects 6/52 = 11.5%); targeted proof-search prompt 85.0% (corrects 44/52 = 84.6%). The paired improvement is 73.1 percentage points (bootstrap 95% CI 61.5–84.6, McNemar-significant). The visible trace shifts accordingly — SAT-commitment density falls from 0.0263 to 0.0105 and contradiction-search density rises from 0.0658 to 0.1097 — linking the gain to the diagnosed procedure rather than to a different final label alone.

The authorship pairs an unusual disciplinary combination (physics and developmental neurobiology rather than a conventional ML lab), which partly explains the item-response-theory grounding for capability matching (Lord 1980; Baker 2001) and the careful statistical hygiene (paired Wilcoxon tests, bootstrap CIs, McNemar's test, 50/50 calibration-test split with no formula leakage). The paper situates itself precisely in the recent (2024–2026) literature on CoT faithfulness, process supervision, and reasoning-trajectory analysis (Bogdan et al. 2025 "Thought Anchors"; Venhoff et al. 2025; Korbak et al. 2025; Barez et al. 2025), and cites the SATBench benchmark (Wei et al. 2025) as the task source. The supplementary material provides full prompt templates, role taxonomies, transition-feature equations, and within-model controls, which materially aids reproducibility.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** Genuine and well-scoped: most prior work evaluates semantic correctness of individual CoT steps (process supervision, step verifiers) or internal-representation dynamics; this paper targets the *organization* (sequencing, recurrence, timing) of visible reasoning without assuming faithfulness, and crucially links the diagnostic to a procedure-specific correction. The distinction between SAT "premature verification collapse" and UNSAT "wrong-procedure selection" is a substantive conceptual contribution, not a relabeling.
- **Rigor.** Statistical hygiene is strong for the main claims: paired Wilcoxon with bootstrap CIs over matched divergent pairs, McNemar's test for the intervention, a 50/50 calibration/test split with no formula leakage, and leakage controls (dynamics-only and pre-final-only AUROCs). However, the headline "premature verification collapse" rests on a *cross-family* comparison (Llama3-8B vs Qwen3-14B) that is confounded by systematic differences in verbosity and response style. The within-family Llama3-8B-wrong vs Llama3-70B-correct comparison explicitly fails to reproduce the length effect (p = 1.0000 ns) and verification-density effect (only p = .0427, tiny Δ = −0.03). The authors acknowledge this honestly, but it means the most quotable half of the pattern is not a universal signature.
- **Reproducibility.** Reasonably good: task generator (SATBench, levels L2–L6), all five named open-weight models, full prompt templates for T0/D1/D2 and both interventions, role taxonomy tables, and the transition-feature equations are all provided. Gaps: the exact regex rules of the role classifier are only described abstractly (agreement statistics are mentioned as bounding but not eliminating error), per-model early-warning thresholds are not tabulated, and no code release is referenced in the text.
- **Significance.** The intervention result (13.3% → 85.0% via a diagnosis-informed prompt) is the most consequential finding and is precisely the kind of evidence that makes the dynamics analysis actionable. But it is **post hoc and oracle-assisted** — solver labels decide which failures to rerun — so it demonstrates *correctability of a diagnosed mode*, not an end-to-end monitor-and-repair system.
- **Red flags.** (1) The early-warning detector is explicitly not universal: 35.7% coverage and zero median lead time for Llama3-70B, and no operating-point characterization (false-positive rate, precision, threshold sensitivity) — the authors flag this themselves. (2) No comparison against cheap baselines (length, finalization timing, role densities without transitions) to show the transition structure contributes beyond what those signals already provide. (3) The generic-retry baseline is unmatched to the proof-search prompt in length and detail, leaving open how much of the 84.6% correction comes from UNSAT-specific guidance vs. simply a longer, more explicit instruction. (4) The 0.30–0.90 capability-frontier band is acknowledged as "ad hoc"; results are scoped to this regime and may not generalize outside it. (5) Generality beyond Boolean satisfiability is unproven — the method requires tasks combining externally checkable outcomes with a meaningful distinction between alternative reasoning procedures, a combination the authors note is uncommon in open-ended domains.

## Strengths

- Frames CoT analysis without taking on the contested faithfulness assumption, which makes the findings robust to the well-known unfaithfulness literature (Turpin et al. 2023; Lanham et al. 2023; Barez et al. 2025).
- The capability-matched divergent-pair design is a clean way to isolate reasoning failure from task difficulty, and the use of SAT/UNSAT asymmetry to obtain two distinct procedures within one formalism is elegant.
- Leakage controls (dynamics-only and pre-final-only AUROCs) show the UNSAT separation is in how reasoning is organized, not in the final-label language.
- The intervention is a strong existence proof that a diagnosis-informed, procedure-specific prompt can rescue a large fraction of failures that a generic retry cannot — directly connecting diagnostic theory to actionable correction.
- Intellectual honesty throughout: the authors repeatedly state what the results do *not* show (no claim of internal knowledge, no end-to-end system, no universal detector) and quantify the model-dependence of every claim.

## Limitations

- **Confound in the headline SAT result.** The strongest collapse pattern is cross-family (Llama vs Qwen) and inherits verbosity/style differences; the within-family Llama3-8B vs Llama3-70B comparison does not reproduce length or verification effects.
- **Early warning is not universal.** Coverage ranges from 78–80% (Llama3-8B, OLMo2-13B) down to 35.7% (Llama3-70B), with no precision/FPR/threshold-sensitivity analysis, so deployment-readiness is unestablished.
- **Intervention is oracle-assisted and unmatched.** Solver labels select which cases to rerun, and the proof-search prompt is longer and more explicit than the generic retry, so the relative contribution of procedure-specificity vs. instruction length is uncontrolled.
- **Single-domain evaluation.** Only Boolean satisfiability; the method requires tasks with external checkability plus a meaningful procedure distinction, restricting transfer to open-ended reasoning.
- **Role tagger is a rule-based abstraction.** Dynamics features inherit tagging error; agreement statistics bound but do not eliminate it, and the exact regex rules are not fully specified.
- **No baselines for the detector.** Cheap signals (length, finalization timing, raw role densities) are not compared against the transition-structured score to justify the additional complexity.

## Relevance

对于关注 LLM 推理可靠性、CoT 可解释性与过程级监督的研究者，本文提供了一套不依赖语义忠实性假设的"推理动态"诊断框架，可作为过程奖励模型/步骤验证器的互补信号，对构建可监控、可修正的推理系统（尤其涉及需要反证/穷举的任务）有直接参考价值；其"先诊断流程错配、再用流程专属提示修正"的范式，对推理时（test-time）纠错策略的设计具有启发性。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the early-warning score be joined to the proof-search intervention *without* solver labels, using the detector itself to trigger repair, and how do false-positive rates and net accuracy change under that end-to-end pipeline?
- Does "premature verification collapse" generalize beyond Llama/Qwen/OLMo to reasoning-specialized models (e.g., o1-style or Qwen3-thinking variants), and does the within-family length confound disappear at larger scale?
- How much of the 84.6% correction on UNSAT survives when the generic retry is length- and detail-matched to the proof-search prompt, isolating procedure-specificity from instruction verbosity?
- Can the dynamics framework extend to domains lacking external solvers (math, code, planning) by substituting self-consistency or executor-based verification for the solver certificate?
- Do the role-transition features add predictive power over cheaper baselines (trace length, finalization timing, raw verification density) once those are properly controlled, and at what computational cost?
- Is the observed UNSAT procedure mismatch (assignment search instead of contradiction proof) a training-distribution artifact — e.g., over-representation of SAT-like examples — and can it be mitigated via targeted post-training?
