Benchmarking the Benchmarks:
Do commonsense scores predict anything real?

Ine Gevers/ Walter Daelemans/ FWO 11P3824N/ 23 models · 6 families · 1B–72B
Key Finding
Reworked commonsense benchmarks mostly preserve the original rankings and add no downstream predictive power — cleaning artifacts is not the same as improving criterion validity.

Across WinoGrande / HellaSwag / Social IQA / Physical IQA and their revisions, transfer to 8 pragmatic, social and physical tasks is narrow and concentrated on False Beliefs and TRIP. Top-3 model Jaccard overlap never exceeds 0.25 — fine-grained capability claims from these scores are not reliable.

Key Metric
Δ = .584
Fisher-z gap, originals vs. revisions · 95% CI [.326, .853]

Original benchmarks intercorrelate at z̄ = 1.565 vs. .982 for revisions. Almost the entire gap comes from the WinoGrande ↔ WinoWhat pair — the one revision that actually changes model behavior.

Why This Matters
Clean ≠ valid.
A methodological redirect for benchmark authors

Reframes the debate from "are there artifacts?" to criterion validity. Future benchmark revisions must prove external predictive value, not just internal data hygiene — and must survive controls for model size, family, and broad capability.

Critical Edge
n = 23
models · 6 families · LOFOCV = 6 folds

The study is severely underpowered. The authors concede the headline null — "no extended partial Spearman correlation survives FDR correction" — may reflect low power rather than a true null. Several downstream sets are tiny (CEI n=31, TimeDial n=60, Indirect Requests n≈64). Family is confounded with base-vs-instruct tuning.

What they tested

Under one uniform length-normalized answer-option log-likelihood protocol (Score(y) = log p(y|x) − log p(y|N/A)), the authors ran a full validity stress test:

BucketMembersRole
Original CSWinoGrande, HellaSwag, Social IQA, Physical IQAEstablished baselines
Reworked CSWinoWhat, GoldenSwag, filtered Social IQA, Physical IQA-RUPArtifact-cleaned siblings
Non-CS controlsBLiMP, MMLU-Redux, GPQA-DiamondIsolate broad capability
Downstream (8)CEI, SARC7, False Beliefs · Implicature, Presupposition, Indirect Requests · TimeDial, TRIPCriterion tasks

Downstream tasks span three conceptual axes — social/emotional, pragmatic inference, event/physical plausibility — enabling a direct test of axis-aligned transfer.

Findings, in order

  1. Revisions preserve rankings. Mean Fisher-z intercorrelation: 1.565 originals vs. .982 revisions (Δ = .584, bootstrap CI excludes zero).
  2. Revisions do not improve criterion validity. Bootstrap intervals for Δρ = ρ(Original,Y) − ρ(Reworked,Y) almost all include zero. The clearest significant effect runs the opposite way: WinoGrande predicts False Beliefs better than WinoWhat (.883 vs .351).
  3. Transfer is narrow. After FDR correction, significant positive correlations cluster on False Beliefs and TRIP. CEI, Presupposition, TimeDial and Indirect Requests show none; Implicature goes significantly negative on macro-F1.
  4. LOFOCV cross-family gains. False Beliefs reaches R² = .483 (accuracy), TRIP .451. All CEI / Sarcasm models yield negative R².
  5. Incremental validity collapses after controls. Once size, family, and non-CS scores are partialled out, no extended partial Spearman survives FDR — broad capability absorbs most of the signal.
  6. Rankings are unstable. Top-3 Jaccard ≤ 0.25; models shift by ~6 rank positions on average — too volatile for fine-grained model selection.
  7. Axis-specificity is absent. Same-axis pairs are globally weaker than mismatched pairs; only the event/physical axis shows partial alignment.

Methodology in one glance

  • Partial Spearman residualized against log model size, family, and non-CS composite — tests incremental, not raw, validity.
  • LOFOCV ridge regression (α=1): predict each held-out family from log-size + non-CS, then add original CS, reworked CS, or both.
  • Fisher-z transformed group comparisons (OO vs. RR vs. OR) with paired non-parametric bootstrap CIs preserving model dependence.
  • Benjamini–Hochberg FDR within each comparison family; both accuracy and macro-F1 reported throughout.
Honesty Signal

The authors' own benchmark loses.

Gevers is first author of WinoWhat. It is the revision that breaks ranking preservation and is worse than WinoGrande at predicting False Beliefs. Reporting a self-disconfirming result transparently is the strongest credibility signal in the paper.

Take-Home

Commonsense scores are weak, task-dependent proxies.

They do not license fine-grained capability claims and do not transfer along the conceptual axes the benchmarks advertise. Use them as coarse ranking signals — not as evidence of specific reasoning skills.
