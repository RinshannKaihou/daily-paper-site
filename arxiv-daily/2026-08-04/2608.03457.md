> **一句话总结：** 本文系统刻画了 MoE 扩散语言模型（dLLM）在优化超参、算力分配和 MoE 架构三个维度的 scaling 行为，发现其与 AR 模型存在可量化的差异（最优 batch 更大、学习率衰减更快、最优 token 预算增长更快、共享专家比例稳定在 33.3%），并据此从零训练出 30B-A3B 的 LLaDA MoE v2，仅用 Qwen3 约 65% 的预训练 token 即在多个知识/推理/代码基准上接近 Qwen3，SFT 后在 8 项推理与代码基准中的 7 项超过 SDAR Chat。

> **TL;DR:** The authors derive dLLM-specific scaling laws for Mixture-of-Experts diffusion language models — covering optimal batch size, learning rate, model–data compute allocation, and MoE architecture (activation ratio, expert granularity, shared-expert ratio) — and validate them by training **LLaDA MoE v2**, a 30B-A3B MoE dLLM from scratch on 23.5T tokens. It approaches **Qwen3 30B-A3B** on several knowledge/reasoning/coding benchmarks using ~65% as many pretraining tokens, and after SFT alone beats **SDAR Chat 30B-A3B** on 7 of 8 reasoning/coding tasks (e.g., AIME24 +13.3, MBPP +9.4, MultiPL-E +22.5 vs SDAR Chat).

## 核心贡献

- **首次系统刻画 MoE dLLM 的 scaling 行为**：在优化超参、算力分配、MoE 架构三个维度上做了受控扫描，给出与 AR 模型可对比的定量差异。
- **dLLM 专属的优化超参 scaling law**：B* = 0.374·C^0.3481、η* = 64.8·C^−0.2447；相比 DeepSeek LLM 的 AR 定律，batch size 增长更陡（C^0.3481 vs 0.3271）、学习率衰减更快（C^−0.2447 vs −0.1250）。作者将此归因于掩码去噪目标对每个 nominal token 的有效监督更少（均匀采样下平均仅一半 token 被预测）。
- **算力分配前沿略偏向数据侧**：M*(C)=0.5152·C^0.475、D*(C)=1.9411·C^0.525，数据侧指数（0.525）略大于模型侧（0.475），比已有 dense dLLM 前沿更偏向数据——与「稀疏激活」和「扩散建模」各自的数据偏好方向一致。
- **MoE 架构的设计准则**：更大算力越来越偏好更稀疏的激活；专家粒度 G=8–16 跨尺度稳健；共享专家比例 S=33.3% 在所有尺度上稳定最优——与 AR 的 DeepSeekMoE (25%)、Qwen3 (无共享专家)、Tian et al. (16.7%→8.3% 下降) 明显不同。
- **大规模验证：LLaDA MoE v2 30B-A3B**：在 23.5T token 上从零训练（Qwen3 的 65%、SDAR Sci 的 63%），15 个基准平均 58.60，为所测 dLLM 中最高；仅用 SFT（无 RL）即可在 8 项推理/代码基准的 7 项上超越 SDAR Chat。

## Key Contributions

- **First systematic scaling characterization of MoE dLLMs**, covering optimization hyperparameters, model–data compute allocation, and MoE architecture (activation ratio, expert granularity, shared-expert ratio), with explicit quantitative comparisons to known AR scaling laws.
- **dLLM-specific optimization laws**: B* = 0.374·C^0.3481 and η* = 64.8·C^−0.2447, showing steeper batch-size growth and faster LR decay than DeepSeek LLM's AR law (C^0.3271 and C^−0.1250). At 10^20 FLOPs the dLLM fit recommends 3.43M-token batches vs 1.02M under the AR law.
- **A slightly data-tilted compute-allocation frontier** (M*∝C^0.475, D*∝C^0.525) — more data-favoring than existing dense dLLM frontiers, consistent with sparse activation and diffusion modeling each pushing toward more data.
- **Stable MoE architecture rules**: sparser activation becomes more favorable with scale; expert granularity G=8–16 is robust; shared-expert ratio S=33.3% is optimal across all scales (contrast with AR designs: DeepSeekMoE 25%, Qwen3 0%, Tian et al. declining 16.7%→8.3%).
- **LLaDA MoE v2 30B-A3B**, trained from scratch on 23.5T tokens under these laws: highest average (58.60) among all evaluated dLLMs across 15 benchmarks while using ~65% of Qwen3's pretraining tokens, and an instruct model that beats SDAR Chat on 7/8 reasoning+coding tasks with SFT only (no RL).

## Methodology

The paper builds a three-stage scaling framework for MoE dLLMs, then validates it with a from-scratch 30B-A3B run.

**Setup.** Masked discrete diffusion: a bidirectional Transformer pθ is trained to denoise a corrupted sequence x_t where each clean token is replaced by [MASK] with probability t (t sampled uniformly). The objective is the per-masked-position negative log-likelihood (Eq. 1). The MoE block pairs grouped-query attention with n_e routed experts (top-n_a routing) plus one shared expert of width d_share = n_s·d_expert. Architecture is parameterized by activation ratio A=(n_a+n_s)/(n_e+n_s), expert granularity G=2d_model/d_expert, and shared-expert ratio S=n_s/(n_a+n_s). Crucially, compute is measured as activated non-embedding FLOPs/token M (C=M·D), not the dense 6ND formula.

**Stage 1 — Optimization hyperparameters.** Joint batch-size × learning-rate search across 158M/1B/3.6B models at 10^18–3×10^20 FLOPs, fitting power laws in log-log space (near-optimal = within 0.25% of min loss per budget). Produces B* = 0.374·C^0.3481, η* = 64.8·C^−0.2447; an extrapolation check at 6×10^20 FLOPs (Fig. 2) places the fitted optimum next to the best observed grid point.

**Stage 2 — Compute allocation.** IsoFLOP sweeps at 10^17–10^20 FLOPs, measuring model side by activated non-embedding FLOPs/token M and data side by training tokens D=C/M. U-shaped loss curves yield compute-optimal allocations fitting M*(C)=0.5152·C^0.475 and D*(C)=1.9411·C^0.525 — a slight data-side tilt, contextualized against ~10 published allocation laws (Table 2).

**Stage 3 — MoE architecture.** At five reference budgets (6×10^17–6×10^19 FLOPs), fix M*(C) and sweep one of {A, G, S} while holding the other two fixed; train 3× the compute-optimal token count (overtraining regime). Findings: lower A wins more at larger scale; G=8–16 is robust; S=33.3% is the consistent U-curve minimum.

**Scale-up model.** LLaDA MoE v2 30B-A3B: 32 layers, d_model=3072, n_e=128 routed experts, top-8 routing, one shared expert with n_s=4 → A=9.09%, G=8, S=33.3%. Five-stage training: 10T + 10T tokens, 2T annealing, then RoPE base 10^4→5×10^5 with context 4K→32K for 500B, and 1T long-context annealing (23.5T total). SFT: 3 epochs on 7M instruction–response examples, masking only response tokens, AdamW (5×10^6 peak LR), no RL.

## Results & Highlights

- **Highest dLLM average across 15 benchmarks**: LLaDA MoE v2 30B-A3B scores **58.60** average, beating scale-matched SDAR Sci (30B-A3B) by **+3.78** and smaller dLLM baselines by **at least +12.44**, while using only **23.5T** pretraining tokens vs SDAR Sci's 37.05T (63%) and Qwen3's 36T (65%).
- **Large coding gains over the scale-matched dLLM**: on the base-model table, +**16.46** on HumanEval (50.00 vs SDAR Sci 33.54) and +**7.98** on BigCodeBench (41.84 vs 33.86), despite being trained from scratch rather than continued-pretrained from an AR checkpoint like SDAR Sci.
- **Approaches Qwen3 30B-A3B on several tasks**: within −2.22 on OlympiadBench (28.74 vs 30.96), −2.44 on HumanEval (50.00 vs 52.44), and +1.05 on HellaSwag (77.19 vs 77.92), with 35% fewer pretraining tokens; lags notably on Chinese knowledge (CEval −11.39, CMMLU −8.36).
- **SFT-only instruct model beats SDAR Chat on 7 of 8 tasks** (Table 4): AIME24 **30.00 vs 16.70** (+13.3), AIME25 **20.00 vs 10.80** (+9.2), OlympiadBench **46.44 vs 34.93** (+11.5), MBPP **81.03 vs 71.60** (+9.4), MultiPL-E **67.52 vs 45.00** (+22.5) — and even surpasses Qwen3 on MultiPL-E (67.52 vs 66.60) despite Qwen3's additional RL stage.
- **Compute efficiency vs the prior un-guided MoE dLLM** (Fig. 5): the scaling-law-guided 30B-A3B matches or beats LLaDA MoE 7B-A1B on MMLU/GSM8K/KorBench at ~50% of its training FLOPs, and on HellaSwag at <10% of its training FLOPs.
- **Stability of the shared-expert optimum**: S=33.3% is the U-curve minimum at all five tested compute budgets (6×10^17 to 6×10^19 FLOPs), in contrast to AR designs (DeepSeekMoE 25%, Qwen3 0%).
- **Extrapolation sanity check**: at 6×10^20 FLOPs (Fig. 2), the fitted (B,η) sits next to the best observed grid cell (loss 1.228), supporting use of the fitted laws beyond the fitting range.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty (high).** This is the first paper to extend dLLM scaling laws to the MoE setting and to challenge the AR-derived architecture heuristics (e.g., the declining shared-expert ratio). The observation that the dLLM objective halves the effective per-token supervision and therefore shifts optimal batch size upward is a genuine mechanistic insight, not a curve-fitting exercise.
- **Rigor (good, with caveats).** Compute accounting (Appendix A.1) is clean and reproducibly specified (activated non-embedding FLOPs/token, full SwiGLU formulas). The hyperparameter and IsoFLOP fits follow established methodology (near-optimal within 0.25%, log-log regression). However, the scaling laws are fit on **158M–3.6B models at ≤3×10^20 FLOPs** and then applied to a **30B-A3B model trained at ~2×10^23 FLOPs** — a 2–3 order-of-magnitude extrapolation. The 6×10^20 FLOPs grid check (Fig. 2) supports extrapolation only modestly and is still far below the final run.
- **Missing interaction study.** The authors explicitly state the three architecture axes (A, G, S) are swept one at a time, so any interaction effects (e.g., whether the optimal S depends on A) are unmeasured. This is a real gap for a paper selling "design principles."
- **Asymmetric baselines.** On the base-model table, the only scale-matched dLLM is **SDAR Sci, which is initialized by continued-pretraining from Qwen3** — i.e., it inherits Qwen3's AR pretraining. LLaDA MoE v2 is trained from scratch yet beats it on many tasks, which is fair praise, but the comparison is not apples-to-apples on the data/checkpoint axis. The other dLLM baselines (LLaDA MoE 7B-A1B, Dream 7B, LLaDA 8B) are 7–8× smaller in activated params, making the "+12.44" average gap less informative.
- **Mixed evaluation provenance.** Qwen3 (*) and several dLLM (†) numbers are copied from prior publications rather than re-run under the authors' unified protocol; only missing scores fall back to their own evaluation. This is standard but introduces cross-paper evaluation drift.
- **No RL, and the gap to Qwen3 shows it.** The instruct model is competitive but still trails Qwen3 on AIME24/25, MBPP, LiveCodeBench — exactly the tasks where RL post-training tends to help most. The authors acknowledge leaving RL to future work.
- **Reproducibility (medium).** Hyperparameters, architectures, sweep grids, and FLOPs formulas are documented in unusual detail in the appendices. However, there is **no statement about model-weight or code release**, the pretraining data composition is not described, and the 30B run is not independently reproducible without that data and very large compute.
- **Red flags.** None major. Claims are scoped ("approaches," "competitive"), and the paper does not overclaim parity with Qwen3.

## Strengths

- A clean, well-structured scaling-law study that produces concrete, usable formulas (B*, η*, M*, D*) and actionable architecture rules rather than vague guidance.
- The mechanistic explanation for *why* dLLM scaling differs from AR (reduced effective supervision under uniform mask sampling) ties the empirical laws to the objective function.
- A credible 30B-A3B scale-up that validates the laws end-to-end on 23.5T tokens — not just a small-model study.
- Honest, well-contextualized comparison against a large set of published allocation laws (Table 2) instead of cherry-picking the most favorable one.
- Compute-controlled comparison to the prior un-guided LLaDA MoE 7B-A1B (Fig. 5) directly demonstrates the practical value of the scaling-law guidance.

## Limitations

- Scaling laws fit at ≤3.6B / ≤3×10^20 FLOPs and extrapolated ~2–3 orders of magnitude to a 30B-A3B run; the extrapolation is only weakly validated (a single 6×10^20 grid).
- Architecture axes are swept independently; interactions between activation ratio, granularity, and shared-expert ratio are not studied.
- No RL post-training stage, so instruct performance on math/code lags fully-tuned AR competitors (Qwen3) on AIME, MBPP, LiveCodeBench.
- Notable gaps vs Qwen3 on Chinese-knowledge benchmarks (CEval −11.39, CMMLU −8.36), likely tied to data mix rather than modeling objective — but the data mix is not disclosed.
- No model weights or code release is announced; the pretraining corpus is unspecified, limiting reproducibility.
- SFT and base evaluations mix self-run numbers with values copied from other papers, introducing cross-paper evaluation drift.

## Relevance

直接相关于扩散/掩码语言模型（dLLM）这一新兴路线与 MoE 架构设计——本文给出的 dLLM 专属 scaling law（最优 batch 更大、LR 衰减更快、共享专家比例 33.3%）对从事非自回归 LLM 训练、稀疏专家架构与大规模预训练算力分配的研究者具有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How do the three architecture axes interact? A joint (A, G, S) sweep at one or two reference budgets could reveal whether the optimal S=33.3% holds under sparser activation.
- Does the data-side tilt (D*∝C^0.525) grow, shrink, or stabilize beyond 10^20 FLOPs? A direct measurement at the actual 30B training budget would test the extrapolation.
- Adding an RL/preference-tuning stage on top of SFT: how much of the remaining gap to Qwen3 on AIME/MBPP/LiveCodeBench is closable, given the diffusion objective?
- What is the inference-cost story? Diffusion models trade more parallelism for iterative denoising steps; how does LLaDA MoE v2's wall-clock throughput compare to Qwen3 at equal quality?
- Can the dLLM-specific batch-size inflation (3.43M vs 1.02M tokens at 10^20 FLOPs) be explained quantitatively from the effective-supervision ratio rather than only qualitatively?
- Is the Chinese-knowledge gap (CEval/CMMLU) attributable to data mix or to the diffusion objective itself? A data-matched AR vs dLLM comparison would isolate the cause.
