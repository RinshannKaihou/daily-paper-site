> **一句话总结：** 本文以23个模型（6个家族，1B–72B）系统检验WinoGrande/HellaSwag/Social IQA/Physical IQA及其修订版对8个下游语用/社会/物理推理任务的预测效度，发现修订版基本保留原排名且不提升下游预测力，常识基准仅对False Beliefs和TRIP等少数任务有跨家族有效性，分数不能可靠支撑细粒度能力主张。

> **TL;DR:** Evaluating 23 LLMs from six families (1B–72B), this study tests whether four widely used commonsense benchmarks (WinoGrande, HellaSwag, Social IQA, Physical IQA) and their reworked variants predict performance on eight downstream pragmatic/social/physical tasks. Reworked benchmarks mostly preserve original rankings and do not improve downstream validity (e.g., originals intercorrelate at z̄ = 1.565 vs. .982 for revisions; Δ = .584, 95% CI [.326, .853]); only False Beliefs and TRIP show consistent cross-family gains (LOFOCV R² = .483/.271 and .451/.397 for accuracy/macro-F1), and top-3 model Jaccard overlap never exceeds .25, meaning commonsense scores are task-dependent and weak proxies for downstream competence.

## 核心贡献

- 首次系统性地将"基准是否有效"从内部数据质量（artifact/污染）问题，转向**准则效度（criterion validity）**：常识基准分数是否能预测下游真实任务表现。
- 在统一的对数似然多选评测协议下，覆盖4个原始常识基准 + 4个修订版 + 3个非常识控制基准 + 8个下游任务（社会/情感、语用、事件/物理三条轴线），构成完整的效度压力测试套件。
- 明确回答了三个问题：(1) 修订版基本不改变排名、且不提升下游预测力；(2) 常识基准的下游预测力很窄，主要集中在 False Beliefs 和 TRIP；(3) 转移并不遵循基准标签暗示的细粒度子领域结构。
- 提出**增量效度**的检验方法：通过控制模型规模/家族/非常识基准分数（partial Spearman）以及 leave-one-family-out 交叉验证（LOFOCV），区分"常识特异能力"与"广义模型能力"。
- 对基准修订社区发出方法学警示：清洗数据/减少 artifact 不等于提升准则效度，未来基准修订必须证明外部效度而非仅内部质量。
- 公开代码与数据：https://github.com/clips/predictive_validity_benchmarks

## Key Contributions

- Reframes the benchmark-validity debate from "do benchmarks contain artifacts?" to **criterion validity** — whether commonsense benchmark scores actually predict downstream task performance that exercises the same underlying skill.
- Constructs a comprehensive evaluation suite: 4 original commonsense benchmarks (WinoGrande, HellaSwag, Social IQA, Physical IQA), 4 reworked variants (WinoWhat, GoldenSwag, filtered Social IQA, Physical IQA-RUP), 3 non-commonsense controls (BLiMP, GPQA-Diamond, MMLU-Redux), and 8 downstream tasks spanning three conceptual axes (social/emotional, pragmatic inference, event/physical plausibility).
- Provides direct empirical evidence that benchmark **revisions do not improve criterion validity**: reworked benchmarks largely preserve the original model-ranking structure and add no reliable downstream predictive advantage.
- Uses both partial Spearman correlations (controlling for model size, family, and non-CS benchmark performance) and leave-one-family-out cross-validation (LOFOCV) to test **incremental validity** beyond broad model capability.
- Shows that transfer is narrow and task-specific (concentrated on False Beliefs and TRIP), and that the fine-grained subdomain labels carried by benchmarks do not reliably match downstream conceptual axes — undermining fine-grained capability claims.

## Methodology

**Models.** 23 open-weight models from six families spanning 1B–72B parameters: Pythia (1B/2.8B/6.9B/12B), OPT (1.3B–66B), Qwen 2.5 (1.5B–72B-Instruct), Llama 3 (1B–70B-Instruct), Gemma 2 (2B/9B/27B-it), and Mistral (7B/12B). Pythia and OPT are base models; the rest are instruction-tuned. Largest models were run on rented Vast.ai GPUs (RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell, 96GB).

**Benchmarks.** Four original commonsense benchmarks paired with four reworked variants (Table 1): WinoGrande ↔ WinoWhat (paraphrased), HellaSwag ↔ GoldenSwag (filtered), Social IQA ↔ filtered Social IQA, Physical IQA ↔ Physical IQA-RUP (paraphrased). Three non-CS controls (BLiMP subsets, MMLU-Redux subsets, GPQA-Diamond) isolate broad capability from commonsense-specific signal.

**Downstream tasks** (8 total, three axes): social/emotional — CEI (n=31), SARC7 (n≈100), False Beliefs (n≈192); pragmatic — Implicature (n≈121), Presupposition (n≈201), Indirect Requests (n≈64); event/physical — TimeDial (n=60), TRIP (n≈100). Small, manually filtered sets prioritize label interpretability over scale.

**Evaluation protocol.** Uniform length-normalized answer-option log-likelihood across all tasks. For tasks with repeated labels (CEI, Implicature, Presupposition, SARC7), an answer statement is appended and label priors calibrated following Zhao et al. (2021): `Score(y) = log p(y|x) − log p(y|N/A)`. Free-form generation is deliberately avoided to remove evaluator/decoding/prompt noise.

**Analyses.** (1) Spearman rank correlations between original and reworked benchmarks, with Fisher-z transformed group-level comparisons (OO vs. RR vs. OR) and non-parametric bootstrap CIs. (2) Pairwise and partial Spearman correlations between each CS benchmark and each downstream task, residualizing against log model size, family, and non-CS scores. (3) Axis-specificity tests comparing same-axis vs. cross-axis Fisher-z means via paired bootstrap. (4) Leave-one-family-out cross-validation (LOFOCV) ridge regression (α=1) predicting each held-out family model from log-size + non-CS composite, then adding original CS, reworked CS, or both; evaluated with cross-validated R² and Spearman correlation. All tests two-sided with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction within each comparison family.

## Results & Highlights

- **Revisions preserve rankings.** Original benchmarks are more strongly intercorrelated than revisions (mean Fisher-z z̄ = 1.565 vs. .982; Δ = .584, 95% CI [.326, .853]); the gap is almost entirely attributable to the WinoGrande↔WinoWhat pair, the only revision that meaningfully changes model behavior.
- **Revisions do not improve criterion validity.** Across downstream tasks, bootstrap intervals for Δρ = ρ(Original, Y) − ρ(Reworked, Y) almost all include zero; the clearest significant difference runs in the *opposite* direction — WinoGrande is substantially more predictive of False Beliefs than WinoWhat (ρ_OG = .883, ρ_RW = .351, Δρ = .532).
- **Downstream transfer is narrow.** Significant positive correlations after FDR correction are concentrated on False Beliefs and TRIP; no positive correlations with CEI, Presupposition, TimeDial, or Indirect Requests survive correction, and Implicature shows significant *negative* macro-F1 correlations.
- **LOFOCV cross-family gains.** Best commonsense-augmented models for False Beliefs reach R² = .483/.271 (accuracy/macro-F1), improving over controls by .766/.695; TRIP reaches R² = .451/.397, improving by .411/.390. Presupposition is positive but modest (R² = .242/.077). Implicature and Indirect Requests are positive only for macro-F1, TimeDial only for accuracy, and all CEI/Sarcasm models yield negative R².
- **Incremental validity is weak after controls.** Once model size, family, and non-CS benchmark scores are partialled out, no extended partial Spearman correlation survives FDR correction — suggesting observed transfer partly reflects broad capability. Notably, MMLU-Redux also predicts False Beliefs and BLiMP predicts TimeDial accuracy.
- **Rankings are unstable for model selection.** Across commonsense–downstream pairs, top-3 model Jaccard overlap never exceeds an average of .25, and models shift by about 6 rank positions on average (Table 4: mean absolute rank displacement ranges 5.65–5.93 for accuracy, 6.35–7.26 for macro-F1).
- **Axis-specificity is largely absent.** Matched axis pairs are globally *weaker* than mismatched pairs for both metrics; only the event/physical axis shows partially consistent alignment. The pragmatic axis shows the opposite of the predicted pattern.

## Authors & Credibility

Ine Gevers and Walter Daelemans are members of **CLiPS (Computational Linguistics & Psycholinguistics)** at the University of Antwerp — a group with a long track record on stylistics, NLP evaluation, and (recently) benchmark validity. Gevers is also first author of the WinoWhat paraphrase corpus (Gevers et al., 2025a) and of "In benchmarks we trust… or not?" (EMNLP 2025), so the authors have direct prior stakes in both producing a reworked benchmark and questioning benchmark validity — which lends credibility but also a clear conflict-of-interest they handle by reporting the WinoGrande↔WinoWhat failure transparently. The work is funded by an FWO grant (project 11P3824N). Code and data are publicly released at https://github.com/clips/predictive_validity_benchmarks.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** High. Most benchmark-critique papers stop at "benchmarks have artifacts"; this one operationalizes criterion and incremental validity with a coherent statistical framework (partial Spearman, LOFOCV, paired bootstrap, FDR), directly comparing originals vs. revisions against external criteria — a genuinely underexplored angle.
- **Rigor:** Strong methodology overall, with pre-registered-style analyses, FDR correction within each comparison family, bootstrap CIs that preserve model dependence, and consistent reporting of both accuracy and macro-F1. The use of LOFOCV to test cross-family generalization rather than in-sample fit is a real methodological plus.
- **Honesty of negative findings:** Admirable. The authors report that their *own* reworked benchmark (WinoWhat) is the one that breaks ranking preservation and is *worse* than its original for False Beliefs prediction — exactly the kind of self-defeating result that signals honesty.
- **Statistical power (red flag):** The biggest weakness. With only 23 models and 6 families, partial Spearman tests controlling for size, family, and non-CS performance are severely underpowered; the authors themselves concede that "no extended partial Spearman correlation survives FDR correction" may reflect low power rather than true null effects. LOFOCV with only 6 folds is explicitly "descriptive."
- **Small downstream sets (data quirk):** Several downstream task samples are tiny after manual filtering — CEI n = 31, Indirect Requests n ≈ 64, TimeDial n = 60, Implicature n ≈ 121, TRIP n ≈ 100. Per-task correlations on these sets have very high variance, so null results for CEI/TimeDial/Indirect Requests should be interpreted cautiously.
- **Missing baselines/ablations:** No comparison against generation-based scoring, no evaluation of instruction-tuned vs. base models as a stratification variable (only Pythia/OPT are base; the rest are instruction-tuned, confounding family with tuning regime), and no analysis of whether prompting variants (few-shot, chain-of-thought) change the validity picture.
- **Overclaiming risk:** Mostly well-controlled — the conclusion is appropriately hedged ("uneven predictor", "task-dependent"). The claim that reworking does not improve validity is strong but rests partly on underpowered comparisons; a longer paper would benefit from a power analysis or a Bayesian equivalence test.
- **Reproducibility:** Good — public code/data, exact model checkpoints listed in Table 3, total compute cost disclosed (~$65 for prompting plus rented GPUs).

## Strengths

- Clean, unified log-likelihood evaluation protocol across all 19 datasets, eliminating format/decoding noise as a confound.
- Genuinely tests *incremental* validity (beyond model size, family, and non-CS performance), not just raw correlation.
- Includes non-commonsense controls — a crucial design choice that lets the authors show downstream prediction is not unique to commonsense benchmarks (e.g., MMLU-Redux predicts False Beliefs).
- Honest, transparent reporting of negative and self-disconfirming results (especially WinoWhat).
- Public artifacts and clear model/checkpoint table make replication straightforward.
- Conceptually meaningful organization of downstream tasks into three axes lets them test a *specific* hypothesis (axis-aligned transfer) rather than reporting an undifferentiated correlation matrix.

## Limitations

- Only 23 models across 6 families; partial-correlation and LOFOCV analyses are statistically underpowered, particularly when residualizing against multiple covariates.
- Several downstream task sets are very small (CEI n=31, TimeDial n=60, Indirect Requests n≈64), inflating correlation variance and weakening null claims for those tasks.
- Evaluation is restricted to log-likelihood multiple-choice; observed validity patterns may not extend to free-form generation, chain-of-thought, or agentic settings (the authors acknowledge this).
- Family is confounded with base vs. instruction-tuned regime (Pythia/OPT base vs. all others instruction-tuned), complicating family-level interpretations.
- The three "axes" are analytical groupings, not a validated taxonomy; conclusions about axis-specificity depend on this subjective grouping.
- LOFOCV with only 6 folds yields descriptive, not inferential, cross-family conclusions.

## Relevance

对评估方法学、常识推理与 LLM benchmark 研究方向高度相关：它直接质疑了 HellaSwag/Social IQA/Physical IQA 等常用基准的下游预测力，并为"如何设计具备准则效度的评测套件"提供了可复用的统计框架（partial Spearman + LOFOCV + 非常识控制），对任何构建或修订 LLM 评测基准的工作都具有方法学借鉴价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do the validity patterns change substantially under generation-based or chain-of-thought evaluation, where models can articulate reasoning rather than only assign likelihoods?
- Would a power-adequate study (e.g., 100+ models spanning 15+ families) recover partial correlations that survive FDR correction, or confirm the current nulls?
- Can the axis-specificity null result be sharpened by using a data-driven taxonomy of commonsense subskills rather than the authors' three-axis grouping?
- Are False Beliefs and TRIP systematically more predictable because they share the *multiple-choice format* with the CS benchmarks (selection among belief-state / state-change alternatives), rather than because of conceptual overlap?
- Does the "revisions don't help validity" finding generalize to other revision types (e.g., adversarial filtering, contamination removal) beyond paraphrase/filter operations studied here?
- How do instruction-tuned vs. base model regimes interact with criterion validity, given the family-tuning confound in the current model set?
