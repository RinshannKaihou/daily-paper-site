> **一句话总结：** 本文提出 Sparse Weight Decomposition (SWD)，将预训练 Transformer 中的稠密权重矩阵 W 直接分解为两个稀疏因子 W ≈ AB，使中间瓶颈单元成为可独立打分、选择与消融的电路单元；在 GPT-2、Qwen2.5 和 Qwen3.5-27B 上，SWD 用不到 1% 的校准数据即可达到与 Transcoder/VPD 匹配的替换保真度，并在相同 sufficiency/necessity 阈值下以更少的 active read/write edges 提取出更紧凑的电路。

> **TL;DR:** SWD reparameterizes each pretrained dense projection W as W ≈ AB with two sparse factors, turning the intermediate "bottleneck" coordinates into individually addressable circuit units without training an auxiliary replacement network. On GPT-2 Small, Qwen2.5 (0.5B/1.5B/3B) and Qwen3.5-27B single-matrix replacements, SWD matches Transcoder/VPD-Recon-CI held-out cross-entropy while using <1% of their calibration tokens (e.g. a few thousand vs ~10^6 on GPT-2 mlp.c_proj), and reaches the same sufficiency and necessity targets on greater-than / IOI / docstring / gendered-pronoun with markedly fewer active read/write edges. After fixed-support fine-tuning (SWD-FT), full GPT-2 replacement of all 48 attention+MLP matrices hits CE 3.44 vs 3.45 for sparse-pretraining at 26.77M active weights, using 20.6M tokens vs 2.884B.

## 核心贡献

- 提出 Sparse Weight Decomposition (SWD)：把稠密权重矩阵 W 重参数化为两个稀疏因子 A、B（W ≈ AB），每个中间坐标成为一个"瓶颈单元"，沿 A:,i 读取、沿 Bi,: 写入，构成固定 rank-one 路径，可直接复用 SAE/Transcoder 的打分-选择-消融电路提取流程。
- 数据效率：在 GPT-2、Qwen2.5(0.5/1.5/3B)、Qwen3.5-27B 的单矩阵替换实验中，SWD 仅用基线方法 <1% 的 token 即可达到匹配的 held-out cross-entropy，避免了训练独立替换网络的代价。
- 电路成本-质量更优：在匹配 pre-pruning CE（差异 ≤0.001）的公平条件下，SWD 在 greater-than、IOI、docstring、gendered-pronoun 四个任务上以更少 selected units 和 active read/write edges 达到同样的 sufficiency 与 necessity 阈值，胜过 Transcoder 与 VPD-Recon-CI。
- 通过严格的消融分离"稀疏连接"与"分解"两种效应：与 exact full-rank SVD 和 Random-B（随机正交）两种稠密精确分解对照，证明 SWD 的优势来自稀疏 read/write 结构本身，而非单纯矩阵分解。
- 全模型替换与零数据变体：SWD-FT（冻结稀疏 support、仅微调非零数值）在 GPT-2 Small 全部 48 个 attention+MLP 矩阵替换上以 ~0.7% 的 token 预算追平 sparse-pretrain 的 CE；零数据变体仅依赖权重即可恢复有用的瓶颈单元，为训练动态的逐步分析打开通道。

## Key Contributions

- Introduces Sparse Weight Decomposition (SWD), which repurposes Double Sparse Factorization (DSF) from model compression as a mechanistic-interpretability primitive: each dense projection W is factorized post hoc into two sparse factors W ≈ AB whose shared bottleneck coordinates are individually addressable circuit units with sparse read/write edges.
- Empirically demonstrates that SWD reaches matched replacement fidelity with less than 1% of the calibration data used by Transcoder/VPD baselines, removing the cost of training an auxiliary replacement network while preserving the standard scoring-selection-ablation workflow.
- Shows at matched pre-pruning CE (within 0.001), SWD achieves the same sufficiency and necessity targets with fewer selected units and active read/write edges across greater-than, IOI, docstring and gendered-pronoun tasks on GPT-2 Small, Qwen2.5-{0.5B,1.5B,3B} and Qwen3.5-27B.
- Isolates the source of the advantage via exact dense controls: full-rank SVD (NaNA-style) and Random-B orthogonal factorizations both require more active edges than SWD, proving the gain comes from sparse read/write connectivity rather than from factorization per se.
- Extends to full-model replacement (SWD-FT) on all 48 attention+MLP matrices of GPT-2 Small at 26.77M active weights, matching sparse-pretrained CE with under 1% of its tokens, and provides a zero-data variant (G = I) that recovers useful bottleneck units from weights alone.

## Methodology

SWD turns each dense linear projection W ∈ R^(din×dout) into two sparse factors A ∈ R^(din×m), B ∈ R^(m×dout) so that W ≈ Wc = AB. Each intermediate coordinate i is a **bottleneck unit**: it reads the input activation h through the sparse column A:,i to produce a scalar zi(h) = hA:,i, then writes that scalar along the sparse row Bi,:. The full output is a sum of these rank-one read–write paths, hWc = Σ_i zi(h)Bi,:, so substituting zi leaves all other paths intact — making each unit independently ablatable.

**Objective.** Instead of a Frobenius objective that weights all input directions equally, the default SWD objective minimizes reconstruction error on calibration activations:

min_{A,B} E_{h∼Dcal} ‖hW − hAB‖²₂  s.t.  ‖A‖₀ + ‖B‖₀ ≤ K

Because E[‖h(W−AB)‖²] = (W−AB)^⊤ G (W−AB) with Gram matrix G = E[h^⊤h], the budget is allocated toward directions the model actually visits. A **zero-data variant** sets G = I, reducing the objective to Frobenius and removing the need for calibration data.

**Solver.** The ℓ₀-constrained problem is NP-Hard, so SWD approximately solves it with the Double Sparse Factorization (DSF) heuristic of Boza & Macko (2025): split the total budget K = KA + KB, alternate A/B block updates, each solved by ADMM iterations (regularized least squares → hard-threshold projection onto the nonzero budget → dual update), then freeze the supports and locally refit B's surviving entries against the activation-weighted objective. For full-model replacement, supports of A and B are frozen and only nonzero values are fine-tuned to minimize next-token cross-entropy — this is **SWD-FT**, which preserves the sparse read/write connectivity and edge count.

**Circuit extraction.** Each bottleneck unit is ranked by a positive first-order task-margin attribution score (from attribution patching) computed on a circuit-train split, defining nested top-k prefixes Sk. Two complementary mean-ablation interventions are applied on a held-out circuit-test split: M^keep(S) keeps units in S and mean-ablates the rest (tests **sufficiency**), while M^abl(S) mean-ablates units in S (tests **necessity**). Sufficiency is reported method-relative (Q_τ(M^keep)/Q_τ(M^unpruned)); necessity as the drop NecDrop_τ = Q_τ(M^unpruned) − Q_τ(M^abl). Circuit cost is measured two ways: selected units C_unit(S)=|S|, and active edges C_edge(S)=Σ_{i∈S_eff}(‖ri‖₀+‖wi‖₀), where S_eff excludes units lacking either a nonzero read or write edge. Comparisons between methods are made only between replacements whose pre-pruning CE differs by ≤0.001.

## Results & Highlights

- **Data efficiency on GPT-2 Small mlp.c_proj:** SWD (s=0.5) reaches low CE delta after a few thousand tokens, versus roughly 10^6 tokens for the Transcoder/VPD baselines (Figure 2) — i.e. **less than 1% of the data** to match held-out fidelity.
- **Single-matrix circuit cost on GPT-2 (Figure 3):** at matched pre-pruning CE, SWD reaches the same sufficiency and necessity thresholds on greater-than, IOI, docstring and gendered-pronoun with **substantially fewer active edges** than Transcoder (TC3k, TC12k) and VPD-Recon-CI; the advantage survives a switch from mean to zero ablation (Appendix C.3).
- **Scaling to Qwen2.5-3B and Qwen3.5-27B (Figures 4–6):** SWD again reaches low CE delta with far fewer tokens (e.g. on Qwen3.5-27B the Transcoder cutoff is 16.8M tokens, while SWD plateaus near 0 CE delta well before) and reaches comparable sufficiency/necessity with fewer active edges than TC24k/TC48k and VPD-Recon-CI.
- **Full-model replacement on GPT-2 Small (Table 2):** at approximately matched 26.77M active weights (~68% body sparsity), SWD-FT reaches **CE 3.44** vs sparse-pretraining's **3.45**, while using **20.6M tokens** (4.19M calibration + 16.38M fine-tuning) versus **2.884B tokens** for sparse pretraining — i.e. ~0.7% of the token budget. On docstring and IOI, sparse-pretraining shows near-zero or negative necessity drop whereas SWD-FT remains effective on all four tasks (Figure 7).
- **Exact dense controls (Figure 8):** full-rank SVD (NaNA-style) and Random-B orthogonal factorizations, which reproduce W exactly, still require **more active edges than SWD** to retain the same task behavior or induce the same ablation effect — isolating the contribution of sparse read/write structure.
- **Zero-data variant (Figure 9):** zero-data SWD better preserves the raw weight matrix (relative Frobenius error **0.366 vs 0.415** for activation-calibrated SWD at s=0.75), while activation calibration yields lower CE under aggressive sparsification; zero-data bottlenecks remain useful for task circuits (Appendix G.2).
- **Targeted bottleneck-unit edit (Figure 11):** editing GPT-2 layer-8 mlp.c_proj along the read direction of unit c205 on the prompt "The opposite of up is" increases the down-vs-left margin by **0.216** while inducing a mean final-token KL of only **4.02 × 10⁻⁵** on seven unrelated factual prompts — a more localized edit than rank-4 LoRA or random-unit controls.
- **Attention mechanistic diagnostic (Figure 12):** SWD reconstructs the layer-9/head-3 dense attention pattern with mean KL **0.0405**; ablating query bottleneck unit q266 raises the mean attention KL to **2.496** and produces a maximum attention-probability change of **0.775**, demonstrating a prompt-local causal effect of a single unit.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine and well-motivated. Repurposing Double Sparse Factorization (a compression technique) as a post-hoc interpretability surface that yields *intervention-targetable* units is a clean idea that the authors distinguish carefully from SAEs, Transcoders, VPD/SPD/APD, NaNA, and sparse pretraining (Table 1). The framing as "circuit units without training a replacement network" is a real reduction in MI's compute footprint.
- **Rigor:** The strongest aspect. The CE-matching rule (≤0.001), the dual reporting of selected units *and* active edges, the exact-SVD and Random-B dense controls (Section 3.5) that decouple "factorization" from "sparse edges," the zero-vs-mean ablation robustness check (Appendix C.3), and the honest limitation that VPD-KL could not be CE-matched and was therefore excluded from circuit comparison all show methodological discipline.
- **Reproducibility:** High. Public code, weights, calibration data (FineWeb-Edu with disjoint eval split), and explicit hyperparameter appendices (B.2, B.5). The fixed-support fine-tuning recipe and DSF/ADMM settings are described in enough detail to reimplement.
- **Significance:** Practically meaningful for MI practitioners — a >100× data reduction and smaller circuits lower the cost of circuit analysis substantially, and the zero-data variant enables per-training-step analysis that is otherwise infeasible. The Qwen3.5-27B single-matrix result is a credible scale signal.
- **Red flags / weaknesses:**
  - **Scaling is asymmetric.** Full-model replacement (the strongest practical claim) is demonstrated only on GPT-2 Small (12 blocks, 48 matrices); Qwen3.5-27B covers only a single matrix. The jump to "SWD scales" for full-model replacement on frontier-sized models is not yet substantiated.
  - **Sparse-pretrain baseline is partially weak.** On docstring and IOI the sparse-pretrained model shows near-zero or *negative* necessity drop, meaning its "circuits" are arguably not behaviorally causal under this protocol — so SWD-FT "winning" on those two tasks is partly a baseline failure rather than a clean head-to-head.
  - **Cherry-picked qualitative cases.** The semantic audit (Figure 10) examines six hand-labeled units in one task, the editing case study uses a single prompt and single unit (c205), and the attention diagnostic is one head — these illustrate rather than establish generality, and the authors mostly acknowledge this in Limitations.
  - **Non-identifiability is unresolved.** The decomposition is only defined up to scaling/permutation symmetries and depends on objective, calibration distribution, initialization, and sparsity budget; SWD does not establish identifiability conditions (Zheng et al., 2021), so extracted units are protocol-specific objects, not unique ground truth. This is disclosed honestly but limits the strength of any mechanistic "explanation."
  - **First-order attribution can miss edges.** The authors flag this, but it means necessity/sufficiency numbers are lower bounds on a particular ranking, not on the optimal circuit.

## Strengths

- Eliminates the dominant cost of learned-sparse circuit analysis (training an SAE/Transcoder/VPD replacement) by factorizing the checkpoint directly, with a quantified <1% data budget.
- Clean experimental design with pre-registered fidelity matching, two cost axes (units and edges), and dense exact-factorization controls that genuinely isolate the cause of the advantage.
- Broad model coverage for the single-matrix claim (GPT-2, Qwen2.5-{0.5,1.5,3B}, Qwen3.5-27B) and four standard MI task families.
- Practically useful variants: SWD-FT for full-model replacement and a zero-data variant for activation-free settings and training-trajectory analysis.
- Honest Limitations section that explicitly states SWD does not resolve MI's non-uniqueness, distributional, verification, or scaling limitations.

## Limitations

- Full-model replacement is validated only on GPT-2 Small; whether SWD-FT accumulates acceptable error across dozens of layers in a 27B+ model is open.
- The sparse-pretrained baseline used for the full-model comparison is not behaviorally causal on two of the four tasks, weakening that particular head-to-head.
- Bottleneck units are not identifiable: results depend on calibration distribution, sparsity budget s, initialization, and the DSF heuristic's non-convex solution.
- All circuit conclusions are protocol-specific (ranking rule, ablation policy, top-k evaluation) and may change under alternative extraction or reporting choices, as the authors note citing Sheng & Fu (2026).
- First-order attribution can miss behaviorally important edges; mean-ablation conclusions vary with intervention choice.
- Qualitative analyses (semantic audit, editing, attention diagnostic) are single-case illustrations rather than systematic evaluations.

## Relevance

对从事机制可解释性、稀疏自编码器或电路分析的研究者高度相关：SWD 提供了一条绕过训练替换网络的轻量路径，可直接用于在已有稠密 checkpoint（包括 GPT-2、Qwen2.5、Qwen3.5-27B）上抽取更紧凑、因果可验证的电路，与 SAE/Transcoder/VPD 等主流工作互为补充；对关注模型编辑、注意力机制诊断或训练动态分析的应用也具备直接工具价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does SWD-FT full-model replacement remain faithful at 7B–70B scale, where per-layer approximation errors compound across far more transformer blocks?
- Can identifiability conditions (e.g., from Zheng et al., 2021) be enforced or approximated so that SWD bottleneck units are stable across calibration distributions and random seeds?
- How do SWD-extracted circuits compare to SAE/Transcoder circuits on adversarial or out-of-distribution inputs where the calibration Gram matrix G is misspecified?
- Can the zero-data variant be applied at every training step to characterize how bottleneck units and circuits form and reorganize during pretraining, and does this reveal interpretable developmental phases?
- Does combining SWD's parameter-side sparse connectivity with attribution-graph methods (Ameisen et al., 2025) yield more faithful end-to-end computational graphs than either alone?
- Are the SWD bottleneck units recovered under a non-English or multilingual calibration corpus consistent with the English ones, and how does this bear on the monosemanticity claim implicit in the semantic audit?
