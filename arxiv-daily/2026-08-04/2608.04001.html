Key
Finding

Test-time scaling is a system, not a checkpoint: candidate discovery climbs from Pass@1 to Pass@80 — but selection can actually cut accuracy.

On the strongest single-response config in the roster, mean-log-prob Best-of-N decreases submitted accuracy as the bank grows — a non-monotone gap caused by reducer misalignment, not candidate availability. The paper formalizes TTS as budgeted inference over the model's implicit prefix tree and treats the whole inference protocol (decoder, reducer, verifier, stopping, budget) as the unit of analysis.

2 Why this matters

Supplies the formal vocabulary, evaluation checklist, and 1.95M-trace corpus that future reasoning-eval work will be measured against.

Directly on-target for Best-of-N, self-consistency, PRM / value-guided search, and leaderboard reproducibility — with protocol-matched compute accounting and uncertainty propagation.

3 Critical edge

The reference-free verifier is Qwen3.6 judging Qwen3.6-generated traces — an evaluator–generator coupling with measurable self-evaluation asymmetry (AUC 0.910 on Qwen traces vs 0.876 on gpt-oss traces) that the authors flag but cannot deconfound.

Compounded by a post-hoc / fixed-bank design: it isolates aggregation but cannot quantify savings from causal stopping or a changed generation policy.

§ 01

A three-layer framework

Formal layer · §2

Prefix-tree regimes

Given input x, the autoregressive model's reachable prefixes form an implicit rooted tree T(x). TTS = budgeted inference A_B over it, with scaling curve G(B) = E[U_x(ŷ_B)].

Three regimes by where compute is spent: single-trajectory (s1 budget forcing), leaf-level (Self-Consistency = empirical MBR, Best-of-N, MBR), prefix-level (ToT / RAP / MCTS, value-guided).

Evaluation layer · §3

System, not checkpoint

The evaluated object is the full protocol — checkpoint + prompt + decoder + reducer + verifier + stopping rule + budget — with compute decomposed as C_gen + C_signal + C_control + C_decision.

Central object is the discovery–stability profile S_k: Pass@k, pass_k, Maj@k, G-Pass@k, mG-Pass@k, Geom@k are all coordinates or functionals of one profile.

Empirical layer · §4

Fixed banks, replayable

Four release blocks on fixed response banks so new reducers replay without regeneration: MMLU-Pro + BBH (27 models), AIME/HMMT/BrUMO (80 seeded × 20 configs), five 2025–26 MathArena sets, and field-balanced SuperGPQA.

Reducers replayed on nested uniform subsets with counter-derived seeds; intervals resample questions across 2,000–10,000 subset replays.

  • Candidate discovery vastly outpaces answer selection. Median Pass@k climbs 56.49%→82.08% (k=1→80) while pass_k falls to 15.00%.
  • Non-monotone Best-of-N on the strongest config. Sequence-log-prob reaches 77.50%, literal plurality 78.33%; reference-assisted CompassVerifier (gold leak) hits 89.17% but is not deployable.
  • Persistent reducer–ceiling gaps on 2025–26 competition math: 8.06–16.13 pp where a correct candidate exists but isn't selected.
  • Rankings aren't robust. Mean Spearman vs N=80 ranking: 0.967 at k=1, 0.992 at k=8 — descriptive, not a small-sample guarantee.
  • Effort drives length. High-effort gpt-oss: 16.12% of responses hit the 81,920-token cap vs none at low effort.
§ 03

Strengths & limitations

+ STRENGTHS

  • The prefix-tree formalization cleanly separates the three regimes and shows why a single scalar "budget" is inadequate — same FLOPs on one trajectory vs 80 leaves vs a search frontier have different statistical structure.
  • The discovery–stability profile S_k unifies Pass@k, Maj@k, G-Pass@k_τ, mG-Pass@k, Geom@k as functionals of one object — should replace ad-hoc metric juggling.
  • The non-monotone Best-of-N result (75.56%→65.83%) is a sharp, citable demonstration of reducer overoptimization, consistent with Gao 2023 / Huang 2025.
  • Exceptional reproducibility hygiene: frozen commit hashes, SHA-256 digests for deterministic sampling, declared numerical/serving settings, refusal to fabricate missing signals.
  • The 1.95M-trace corpus (2B+ tokens) with width-20 token alternatives and two verifier families is a substantial community resource.

− LIMITATIONS

  • No new inference algorithm. Formalizes, evaluates, and releases data — but no deployable decoder, search controller, or reducer.
  • Small evaluation sets. 120 and 186 competition questions limit statistical resolution; several claimed orderings are explicitly withheld.
  • Evaluator–generator coupling. Reference-free verifier is Qwen3.6 judging Qwen traces (AUC 0.910 vs 0.876) — flagged but not deconfounded.
  • Post-hoc / fixed-bank only. Cannot quantify savings from adaptive causal stopping or from changing the generation policy.
  • Coverage gaps. 2024–25 archive lacks next-token alternatives; neither math block has process-reward scores or validated answer-start boundaries — so PRM reducers cannot run.
  • Survey bloat. Appendices D–E catalog the ecosystem at length, inflating the paper to 59 pages.
§ 04

Open questions for further work

  1. Q1 How do the discovery–stability profile and the reducer–ceiling gap behave under prefix-level search (ToT/RAP/MCTS), where the returned bank is not i.i.d. and the profile is only descriptive?
  2. Q2 Can the evaluator–generator coupling be removed by training a model-agnostic reference-free verifier on the released width-20 token-alternative signals — and would that close the 8–16 pp gap?
  3. Q3 What are the wall-clock and C_signal + C_control + C_decision frontiers for causal stopping controllers (Adaptive-Consistency, DeepConf, Early-Stopping SC) on these same banks?
  4. Q4 Does the non-monotone mean-log-prob result generalize beyond Qwen3-30B-A3B, and is it predictable from cheap per-bank calibration of the score–correctness correlation?
  5. Q5 How sensitive are the small-N reducer gaps (5.38–14.52 pp) to difficulty stratification, and would a contamination-controlled, difficulty-calibrated benchmark shrink or enlarge them?
