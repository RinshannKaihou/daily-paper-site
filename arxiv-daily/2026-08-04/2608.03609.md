> **一句话总结：** 本文首次为基于 LLM 的工具编排智能体系统（STEAD）建立了针对一阶计算树逻辑（FO-CTL）业务规约的形式化验证框架，证明验证问题不可判定，并通过对称性条件与一个"规范化部署包装器"（强制 LLM 在不透明标识符重命名下保持等变）将验证归约到 PSPACE 完备的有限域上，在退款工单案例中成功反证安全属性、证明进度属性。

> **TL;DR:** The paper formalises LLM tool-orchestration deployments (STEADs) and proves that verifying them against First-Order Computation Tree Logic (FO-CTL) business specs over evolving relational data is undecidable; it recovers PSPACE-complete exact finite verification under identifier-relabeling symmetry (agent equivariance), shows Qwen3-1.7B on a τ-bench-derived airline task violates this symmetry, and provides a canonical wrapper that enforces equivariance by construction — on a 12-value finite restriction (55 reachable states / 72 transitions) the evaluator refutes the authorization property φ_auth and certifies the progress property φ_prog, with a control experiment (approval-aware policy) yielding 73 states / 90 edges and certifying both.

## 核心贡献

- **首次形式化 LLM 智能体验证问题**：将"单个 LLM + 工具编排 + 持久关系数据"的部署定义为 STEAD（Stateful Tool-Enabled Agentic Deployment），并在 FO-CTL（一阶计算树逻辑）规约下定义验证问题，证明其**不可判定**（通过将已有不可判定的关系数据驱动系统归约到一个空元工具的 STEAD）。
- **找到精确有限验证的充分条件**：当系统 C-bounded 且工具接口、工具语义、**智能体策略**三者都对标识符重命名等变（equivariant）时，可在足够大的有限域上做**精确**（sound and complete）验证，且复杂度为 **PSPACE-complete**。其中关键且难以满足的条件是"智能体等变"。
- **实证 LLM 违反等变并给出构造性修复**：在由 τ-bench 航司取消任务改造的单轮决策上下文中，仅把不透明用户名 `amelia_davis_8890` 重命名为 `ea0q883c_00yla6i6`（其余数据不变），Qwen3-1.7B 在贪婪结构化解码下从 `cancel(r1,r3)` 变为 `cancel(r1,r2,r3)`（多选了不合规的 r2），证明 LLM 天然不满足等变。
- **规范化部署包装器（canonical deployment wrapper）**：在部署边界把每个决策上下文重命名为规范代表，调用基础智能体后再把选择映射回原标识符；对任意基础智能体都强制 B-等变，且不改变已等变的行为。进一步证明计算这种规范表示是 **graph-isomorphism-hard**，并给出 action-rigidity 条件使包装器保持单值性。
- **退款工单工作流案例**：在 12 元素的有限域（55 状态/72 转移）上，显式状态评估器**反证 φ_auth**（授权属性）并**证明 φ_prog**（进度属性）；反例是"摄入工单后立即签发退款而未经审批"。审批感知的控制策略在 73 状态/90 边的有限归约中同时满足两个属性。

## Key Contributions

- **First formal verification framework for LLM agentic deployments over persistent data**: Defines STEADs (single LLM + tool harness + relational operational state), gives their semantics, and proves that verifying FO-CTL specifications over them is **undecidable** (reduction from known undecidable relational data-centric dynamic systems with propositional invariant `G p`).
- **Exact finite verification under relabeling symmetry**: Identifies C-boundedness plus C-equivariance of the tool interface, tool semantics, and — crucially — the **agent policy** as sufficient conditions for exact preservation under a finite-domain restriction (`|U_f|_S >= 2 b_S + |C ∩ U_S| + var_S(φ)` per sort), with verification **PSPACE-complete** in combined complexity.
- **Empirical demonstration that LLMs violate equivariance**: Adapting a τ-bench airline-cancellation task to a single-turn decision context, renaming only the opaque username `amelia_davis_8890` to `ea0q883c_00yla6i6` (no other data changed) causes Qwen3-1.7B under greedy structured decoding to switch from `cancel(r1, r3)` to `cancel(r1, r2, r3)` — selecting an additional policy-violating reservation — a concrete counterexample to C-equivariance.
- **Canonical wrapper enforcing equivariance by construction**: A deployment-boundary wrapper renames each decision context to its canonical representative, queries the base agent, and transports the selected call back; it is B-equivariant for every base agent and identity-preserving on already-equivariant policies, with an action-rigidity condition (Prop. 21) ensuring singleton-valued output. The authors prove computing the shared canonical representative is **graph-isomorphism-hard** (even for one binary relation over an infinite domain).
- **Refund case-management case study**: On a 12-value finite restriction, forward exploration reaches **55 states and 72 transitions**, collapsing under canonicalization to only **4 distinct base-agent queries**; an explicit-state bottom-up FO-CTL evaluator **refutes φ_auth** (authorization) — counterexample: ingest then `issue_refund(t)` without approval — and **certifies φ_prog** (progress). An approval-aware control policy yields **73 states / 90 edges, 5 canonical queries**, certifying both properties.

## Methodology

The paper is a formal-methods contribution combining (a) model-theoretic semantics from data-aware verification and (b) a deployment-level intervention for neural agents.

1. **System model — STEAD.** Operational state is a typed relational structure (sorted domain `U`, typed schema `D`, initial instance `D_0`). Tools `Ω` are schema-typed; `Offer(D)` is the nonempty set of grounded calls enabled at `D`; tool semantics `τ(D, a)` is a (possibly nondeterministic) successor relation; the LLM-based agent policy `Π(D, A)` returns a nonempty subset of `A`. The induced transition system `M_A = ⟨U, D_0, →_{Π,Offer}⟩` is serial. The LLM agent is treated as Markovian in the decision context (interaction history may itself be recorded as relational facts in `D`).
2. **Specification language — FO-CTL.** Combines sorted first-order active-domain quantification with branching-time CTL modalities (`AX`, `A U`, `E U`, and derived `AG/EF/EG`). Can express both **safety** (e.g., φ_auth: every issued refund was approved by the paired approver) and **progress/liveness** (φ_prog: every open case is eventually resolved on all branches).
3. **Undecidability (Thm. 8).** Any finitely specified relational data-centric dynamic system with nondeterministic services and invariant `G p` (Hariri et al. 2013) is encoded as a STEAD with one always-offered nullary tool whose transition relation is the reflexive closure of the original; `G p` holds in the source iff `AG p` holds in the STEAD.
4. **Symmetry conditions (Defs. 9–11).** C-equivariance of `Offer`, C-tool-uniformity of `τ`, and C-equivariance of `Π`. The central obstacle is the agent: if `D_1 ≃_C D_2` via `ι`, then `Π(D_2, ι(A))` must equal `ι(Π(D_1, A))`.
5. **Exact finite verification (Thm. 13).** Under C-boundedness by `b` and the three symmetry conditions on `Reach(M_A)`, any finite subdomain with `C ⊆ U_f ⊆ U` and, per sort, either the full sort or at least `2 b_S + |C ∩ U_S| + var_S(φ)` values yields a finite `M_A ↾ U_f` such that `M_A ⊨ φ ⇔ M_A ↾ U_f ⊨ φ`. PSPACE-complete via on-the-fly evaluation.
6. **Canonical wrapper (Defs. 14–15, Thm. 18, Prop. 21).** Fix a rigid set `B` (values whose identity matters, e.g., `open`, `resolved`) and canonical value sequences per sort; `can_B(D)` is the least B-labelling. The set-valued wrapper Π_can unions over all canonical witnesses `σ ∈ Lab_B(D)`; it is always B-equivariant, and if the base agent is already B-equivariant then Π_can = Π. Action-rigidity (every B-automorphism fixes all offered calls) keeps the wrapped policy singleton-valued. Canonicalization cost is **graph-isomorphism-hard** (Lemma 19).
7. **Verification algorithm.** Forward BFS enumeration over concrete states of `U_f`, canonicalizing each discovered decision context before querying the base agent once, applying `τ`, and retaining in-domain successors; then bottom-up FO-CTL satisfaction-set computation over the resulting finite graph.

## Results & Highlights

- **Theoretical complexity.** STEAD verification against FO-CTL is **undecidable** in general; under C-boundedness and the three symmetry conditions it is **PSPACE-complete** in combined complexity on the finite restriction (hardness already for first-order model checking on a one-state serial system).
- **Equivariance counterexample (concrete, reproducible).** On the τ-bench-derived airline task with Qwen3-1.7B + greedy structured decoding, renaming a single opaque username (`amelia_davis_8890` → `ea0q883c_00yla6i6`) changes the grounded selection from `cancel(r1, r3)` to `cancel(r1, r2, r3)`. Because `ρ` fixes the reservation identifiers, `ρ(A) = A` and `ρ(a_0) = a_0`, so the observed `a_1 ≠ ρ(a_0)` is a formal C-equivariance violation; the ineligible reservation `r2` is additionally selected in the renamed context.
- **Refund workflow — finite restriction.** With `b_case = b_task = 1` and at most one variable of each sort per spec, Thm. 13 requires **3 case + 3 task representatives**, giving a **12-element** finite domain (C plus the three case and three task values). Forward BFS reaches **55 states and 72 transitions**; canonicalization collapses these to **4 distinct base-agent queries** (case intake, pending refund, refund issued, resolved). Case intake alone branches over 2 handlers × 9 fresh case–task pairs = **18 successors of D_0**.
- **Verification outcomes (main policy).** The explicit-state FO-CTL evaluator **refutes φ_auth** (the authorization safety property) — the counterexample ingests a case with task `t` and immediately calls `issue_refund(t)` although the paired approver `a` has not approved `t` — and **certifies φ_prog** (the progress property). By Thm. 13 these verdicts transfer exactly to the wrapped infinite-domain deployment.
- **Control experiment (approval-aware policy).** Changing only the policy text to insert an approval step yields a finite restriction with **73 states and 90 edges** requiring **5 canonical base-agent queries**, and the evaluator **certifies both φ_auth and φ_prog**. The adversarial `obtain_approval` can loop forever in the baseline policy (a progress risk that the control policy closes).
- **Enforcement guarantee.** The canonical wrapper is provably B-equivariant for **every** base agent (Thm. 18) and is identity on agents that are already equivariant; action-rigidity (Prop. 21) preserves singleton-valuedness, so the deployment queries the base LLM exactly once per canonical context.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty** is genuinely high: this is, to the authors' knowledge (and credibly, given the related-work coverage), the first pre-deployment *formal* verification framework for an LLM-driven agentic system against *first-order temporal* requirements over evolving relational data, as opposed to interface-level guardrails (Agent-C, AgentSpec/GuardAgent, ProbGuard, ShieldAgent) or propositional-state synthesis (De Giacomo et al. 2026). The identification of **agent equivariance** as the precise bridge between neural LLM behaviour and classical relational finite-abstraction results is a real conceptual contribution, not a re-labelling.
- **Rigor** is strong on the formal side: undecidability (Thm. 8), the exact-preservation theorem (Thm. 13) with a careful bisimulation-style (`≡`) invariant proof, and the graph-isomorphism-hardness of canonicalization (Lemma 19) are all backed by proof sketches with full appendix proofs. The equivariance counterexample is not merely anecdotal: the rename audit is reproducible offline from the supplied records, the offered set `A` is constructed so `ρ(A) = A` and `ρ(a_0) = a_0`, making the inequality `a_1 ≠ ρ(a_0)` a clean formal violation rather than a noisy empirical artefact.
- **Weak experimental scale.** The empirical footprint is deliberately small and pedagogical: one 12-value finite domain, one LLM family (Qwen3-1.7B for the counterexample; Qwen3-4B for the workflow), a single adapted τ-bench task and a custom 2-service refund workflow. There is **no evaluation across multiple models, prompts, or benchmarks**, and **no scaling characterization** of the wrapper's runtime as the active domain grows (the GI-hardness result is asymptotic but not accompanied by empirical timings). Readers cannot judge how the approach behaves on realistically large operational states or stronger/larger LLMs.
- **Reproducibility** is unusually good for a theory-leaning paper: code supplement, exact system/user prompts, decoding procedure (prefix-trie constrained greedy decoding), tool-schema serialization, and a control experiment are all included. A skeptical reader can replay the counterexample without rerunning the stochastic username search.
- **Missing ablations / controls.** The paper does not ablate the canonical wrapper's overhead vs. raw agent calls, does not measure how often action-rigidity actually holds beyond the toy workflow, and does not compare against any baseline verification method (there is essentially no competing system-level verifier to compare against, but the paper could have contrasted its verdicts with an interface-level guardrail like Agent-C on the same φ_auth). The claim that the wrapper "preserves already-equivariant behaviour" is proven, but no experiment shows an already-equivariant agent (e.g., a hand-coded rule agent) being verified unmodified.
- **Potential overclaiming to watch.** The phrasing "we verify an LLM-based agent" should be read carefully: what is verified is the *wrapped* deployment, and the wrapper can change behaviour on non-canonical presentations (the paper is explicit that Π_can need not equal Π on the original presentation when Π is not equivariant). So the theorem certifies a modified system whose behaviour is the agent's behaviour restricted to isomorphism classes, not necessarily the agent as deployed.
- **Red flags**: none severe. The LLM-usage statement is transparent (editing/refining only); the single-model equivariance study is honestly framed as illustrative ("one such audited pair suffices") rather than as a broad empirical claim.

## Strengths

- Clean, well-motivated formalisation that pinpoints a *specific*, non-trivial obstacle (agent equivariance) rather than hand-waving "LLMs are unreliable."
- The canonical wrapper is a constructive, deployment-level fix that does not require retraining or post-hoc certification, and is provably conservative (identity on already-equivariant agents).
- Honest verification outcomes: the framework is shown **refuting** a safety property in the baseline policy (not only certifying), and the control experiment shows the same machinery certifying a corrected policy.
- Excellent reproducibility artefacts (code repo, exact prompts, decoding details, environment, control experiment).

## Limitations

- Empirical evaluation is small-scale and single-family (Qwen3 only); generalization to larger/other LLMs, longer multi-turn workflows, and real-world operational schemas is untested.
- The undecidability and PSPACE-completeness results are essentially inherited from classical data-aware verification; the wrapper is the main new mechanism, and its practical scalability (canonicalization cost is GI-hard) is not benchmarked empirically.
- The wrapper can alter the agent's behaviour on non-canonical presentations; "verifying the agent" therefore means verifying a wrapped variant whose correspondence to a raw deployment is conditional on equivariance the raw agent may not have.
- The framework covers a single LLM agent; multi-agent systems, persistent memory, and richer environment interaction are explicitly left to future work.
- Action-rigidity is a real restriction: in workflows where genuinely symmetric objects remain in the offered-call parameters at decision time, the wrapper becomes set-valued (interpreted as nondeterminism), which can over-approximate the agent's actual behaviour and weaken the verification verdict.

## Relevance

对关注"LLM 智能体安全/可靠性形式化保障"与"神经符号系统验证"的研究者高度相关：它把经典数据驱动模型检验的有限抽象技术严格扩展到 LLM 工具编排部署，是构建可验证（verifiable）智能体基础设施的重要理论一步，也为后续多智能体、持久记忆场景的形式化验证奠定基础。对纯经验式的 agent benchmark/对齐方向相关性较弱。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does the canonical wrapper's runtime and LLM-query count scale with the number of distinct isomorphism classes in realistically large operational states (e.g., hundreds of active cases), given that canonicalization is graph-isomorphism-hard?
- Does equivariance hold, break, or vary across model scale and family (e.g., GPT-class, Claude-class, Llama) on the same τ-bench-derived task, and can the wrapper's behaviour-changing effect on non-canonical presentations be quantified?
- Can action-rigidity be relaxed via a symmetry-breaking convention that remains equivariant (avoiding the set-valued blow-up of Π_can) for workflows with persistent symmetric objects?
- How does the framework extend to multi-turn, multi-agent deployments with persistent memory, where the Markov-in-decision-context assumption and single-LLM structure no longer hold?
- Can the finite-restriction machinery produce *maximally permissive, data-aware tool restrictions* (the authors' stated future direction) that act as a verified guardrail layer reusable across base agents, and how does it compare empirically to interface-level enforcers like Agent-C on the same FO-CTL specs?
