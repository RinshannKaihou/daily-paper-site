> **一句话总结：** 作者构建了 SCOPE——首个针对 LLM 自主实验设计能力的细粒度评测基准（300 篇顶会论文、19 个领域、6 个子维度、0–30 分），发现主流 LLM 平均仅 14.81/30 且普遍在低层资源配置（数据集/基线/指标）上撞到瓶颈，并据此提出三阶段智能体工作流 OptED，在 6 个模型上稳定涨分（最高 +4.22）同时大幅压低幻觉红线率。

> **TL;DR:** The paper introduces SCOPE, the first systematic benchmark for LLM-based experimental design built from 300 ICML/ICLR/NeurIPS papers across 19 domains, and shows that seven mainstream LLMs average only 14.81/30 with Claude 4.5 Sonnet top at 18.62 vs Grok-4 at 12.55 (a 6.07-point spread), exposing Low-Level configuration (datasets/baselines/metrics) as the universal bottleneck and naive web search as actively harmful (GPT-5.2 drops 18.22→16.77, DeepSeek-V3.2 redline rate doubles to 14.00%); the proposed OptED agentic workflow recovers these gaps, lifting DeepSeek-V3.2 by +4.22 (+30.7%), GPT-5.2 to 20.47 (above its CoT-only peak), and slashing redline rates (DeepSeek 14.00%→2.67%, Gemini 3 Pro to 0%).

## 核心贡献

- 首个面向"实验设计"阶段的系统性评测基准 SCOPE：从 ICML/ICLR/NeurIPS 的 5200+ 候选论文中筛出 300 篇高质量论文、覆盖 19 个研究领域，按"高层规划（主实验/消融/分析）+ 低层配置（数据集/基线/指标）"的六维层次结构评分，每维 0–5 分，总分 0–30。
- 设计了基于 LLM-as-Judge 的评分体系并加入"红线机制"：一旦出现源幻觉、指标不兼容、约束违反等致命缺陷，对应子维度直接归零，防止严重失败被均分掩盖。
- 在 CoT-only 与 CoT+Search 两种策略下系统评测了 7 个主流 LLM（GPT-5.2、Claude 4.5 Sonnet、DeepSeek-V3.2、Gemini 3 Pro、Kimi-K2、Qwen3-Max、Grok-4）以及 2 个深度研究模型，得出三条关键发现：模型整体能力偏弱、低层配置是普遍瓶颈、简单搜索不仅无益反而损害规划质量。
- 提出三阶段智能体工作流 OptED（Configuration / Protocol / Reporting），通过阶段隔离、Think-Act-Observe 工具调用与显式行为规范，在 6 个模型上稳定提升设计质量并显著降低红线率。
- 提供完整消融实验：验证阶段隔离、T-A-O 循环、行为规范各自的贡献，并指出"规划-配置鸿沟"仍是未解难题。

## Key Contributions

- Introduces SCOPE, the first end-to-end benchmark specifically for the experimental-design stage of AI4Research, with 300 papers selected from an initial pool of >5,200 ICML/ICLR/NeurIPS submissions across 19 research domains, scored on six hierarchical sub-dimensions (Main Experiment, Ablation, Analysis, Datasets, Baselines, Metrics) yielding a 0–30 total.
- Designs an LLM-as-Judge evaluation protocol with an explicit "redline mechanism" that zeroes any sub-dimension exhibiting fatal flaws (source hallucination, metric incompatibility, constraint violation), preventing averaging from masking critical failures.
- Systematically benchmarks seven mainstream LLMs under CoT-only and CoT+Search prompting, plus two deep-research variants, producing three findings: (1) most LLMs cannot directly produce high-quality experimental plans; (2) Low-Level configuration is the universal bottleneck; (3) unstructured search does not improve—and often degrades—plan quality.
- Proposes OptED, a three-stage agentic workflow (Configuration → Protocol → Reporting) that combines stage isolation, a Think-Act-Observe tool-augmented loop, and explicit behavioral norms/checklists to convert free-form generation into auditable, atomic operations.
- Delivers consistent gains across six models with full ablations isolating each component, and reciprocally validates the benchmark's diagnosis by reversing the search-induced degradation and compressing hallucination redline rates.

## Methodology

**SCOPE benchmark construction.** Papers are collected from ICML, ICLR, and NeurIPS, with GitHub stars/forks used as community-impact proxies. Each paper is ranked by a z-normalized composite impact score `R_i = 0.5·[(ln(1+S_i) − μ_lnS)/σ_lnS] + 0.5·[(ln(1+F_i) − μ_lnF)/σ_lnF]`, and the top 300 across 19 areas are retained. Each paper is then converted to a standardized task via a "summary-then-alignment" extraction pipeline: a global-comprehension pass captures overall structure and contributions, followed by deep section-by-section extraction of task description, methodology modules, ground-truth experimental design, datasets, baselines, metrics, constraints, and ambiguities. A self-verification + external-revision loop scores each task on six quality dimensions and keeps only tasks reaching ≥35/50 ("Good" grade); the final corpus averages 4.2 method modules, 6.8 baselines, and 3.1 datasets per task.

**Task formulation.** Given a target paper's task description, related work, and methodology (but not its experimental design), the model must produce a complete plan covering both High-Level categories (main experiments, ablation studies, analysis experiments) and Low-Level resources (datasets with sources/splits, baselines with sources, primary + auxiliary metrics). Plans are scored 0–5 per sub-dimension by GPT-5.2 as judge against a detailed rubric, totaling 0–30 (15 High-Level + 15 Low-Level).

**Models and strategies.** Seven LLMs are evaluated (GPT-5.2, Claude 4.5 Sonnet, DeepSeek-V3.2, Gemini 3 Pro, Kimi-K2, Qwen3-Max, Grok-4) under CoT-only and CoT+Search, the latter granting web-search access while forbidding direct retrieval of the target paper's experimental section. Two deep-research variants (Qwen DeepResearch, Grok DeepSearch) are also tested.

**OptED workflow.** OptED reframes experimental design into three linearly connected, contextually isolated stages. The Configuration Phase independently handles Low-Level resource discovery/evaluation/selection using search + browsing tools (hypothesize-then-verify) and CRUD-style content-editing tools that record every operation, state change, and rationale. The Protocol Phase builds the complete High-Level scheme (main experiments, ablations following single-variable principle, analysis experiments offering insights beyond the main table). The Reporting Phase assembles structured outputs. Behavioral norms and end-of-stage checklists enforce scientific standards (e.g., dataset/domain coverage, baseline-comparability rules) and a global self-check.

## Results & Highlights

- **Overall benchmark ceiling is low:** Across seven mainstream LLMs the mean total score is only **14.81/30**, with Claude 4.5 Sonnet highest at **18.62** and Grok-4 lowest at **12.55**, a **6.07-point gap**; even the top model falls >11 points short of the maximum (SCOPE, 300 papers across 19 domains).
- **Low-Level configuration is the universal bottleneck:** No Low-Level sub-dimension exceeds 3/5 for any model, while High-Level dimensions frequently surpass 3.5. For Claude 4.5 Sonnet, Analysis Experiments = **4.62** vs Datasets = **2.01** (a >2× gap); the mean High-Level vs Low-Level gap is **2.78 points**.
- **Naive search actively hurts strong planners:** GPT-5.2 drops from **18.22 (CoT-only) to 16.77 (CoT+Search)**, a −1.45 degradation with High-Level sub-dimensions falling 0.48–0.82 each. DeepSeek-V3.2's redline rate nearly doubles from **7.67% to 14.00%** under search. Five of seven models show no statistically significant total-score difference between the two strategies.
- **Deep-research models help but do not close the gap:** Grok DeepSearch reaches **15.39 vs 12.55 base (+2.84)** with the lowest redline rate of any model (**0.67%**), yet its Low-Level scores (Datasets 2.23, Baselines 2.12, Metrics 2.31) still all stay below the 3-point threshold and it trails Claude 4.5 Sonnet and GPT-5.2 by a wide margin.
- **OptED delivers consistent gains across all six models tested:** DeepSeek-V3.2 improves **+4.22 (+30.7%)** and Grok-4 **+3.67 (+28.0%)**; GPT-5.2 reaches the highest absolute score of **20.47**, fully reversing its search-induced degradation (from the CoT+Search low of 16.77, exceeding even its CoT-only peak of 18.22 by +2.25).
- **OptED suppresses hallucination redlines:** Redline rates fall from **14.00%→2.67%** for DeepSeek-V3.2, to **0.33%** for Claude 4.5 Sonnet, and to **0%** for Gemini 3 Pro.
- **Persistent High-Level/Low-Level gap even after OptED:** GPT-5.2 scores 12.43 in High-Level planning vs 8.04 in Low-Level configuration, confirming that workflow structuring helps but does not solve the grounding problem.
- **Ablation isolates component contributions:** Stage isolation alone already beats the CoT+Search baseline for all three ablated models (GPT-5.2, DeepSeek-V3.2, Qwen3-Max). Adding the Think-Act-Observe loop (w/o norms) yields the largest jumps (DeepSeek-V3.2 +2.33, GPT-5.2 +1.80, Qwen3-Max +0.73). Behavioral norms help weaker planners most (DeepSeek +0.70, Qwen +0.40) while GPT-5.2 gains only marginally (+0.16), suggesting strong intrinsic reasoning leaves little room for norm-based gains.

## Authors & Credibility

The work is led by Zejun Liu and Jian Wu (equal contribution) with Yue Zhang as corresponding author, spanning Westlake University, Zhejiang University, Nanjing University of Science and Technology, and the University of Tokyo—groups with established track records in NLP, information extraction, and AI-for-science. The methodology is documented in unusual detail: the full input/output schema, the 19-domain taxonomy, the impact-ranking formula, the rubric, and the redline taxonomy are all specified, with an explicit appendix on dataset construction, quality-control threshold (≥35/50), and statistics (4.2 modules, 6.8 baselines, 3.1 datasets per task on average). The authors include a frank "Acknowledgment of LLM Assistance" section and a dedicated Limitation appendix covering domain scope, pipeline-stage scope, domain distribution skew, extraction fidelity, and benchmark-overfitting risks. The reported numbers are internally consistent across the abstract, Table 2, Table 3, and the OptED discussion.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty (high).** This is the first benchmark to treat experimental design as a standalone, end-to-end stage rather than a by-product of code execution (Exp-Bench, MLE-Bench) or a narrow ablation-only sub-task (AbGen, AblationBench). Table 1 makes the positioning explicit: unlike AAAR-1.0 which uses brief-proposal matching, or SoundnessBench which only judges proposal soundness, SCOPE decomposes the design into six independently graded sub-dimensions. The hierarchical High-Level/Low-Level split is a genuinely useful diagnostic axis.
- **Rigor (mixed, with real concerns).** The single-judge design uses GPT-5.2 as the sole LLM-as-Judge with no inter-judge agreement study, no human spot-check of judge scores, and no reported variance or confidence intervals on the headline 0–30 numbers—only "statistical significance" is alluded to for the search comparison without specifying the test, sample size, or p-values. The redline mechanism is a thoughtful safeguard but its triggers (source hallucination, metric incompatibility) are themselves judged by the same GPT-5.2 that scores the plan, creating a circular dependency. The OptED "consistent gains" claim rests on a single run per model with no seed variation reported.
- **Reproducibility (medium).** Authors promise code release "upon publication." The dataset is built from public papers and the impact-ranking formula is fully given, but the structured extraction pipeline relies on unspecified LLMs and prompts that are only partially documented, making exact regeneration of the 300-task corpus from the >5,200-paper pool unlikely without the released artifacts.
- **Significance (high if claims hold).** The diagnosis that Low-Level configuration is the bottleneck and that naive search degrades planning is actionable and aligns with practitioner intuition; OptED's +4.22 on DeepSeek-V3.2 and full reversal of GPT-5.2's search degradation are striking.
- **Red flags.** (1) The benchmark skews toward high-Github-impact ML papers (foundation models, generative models, multimodal applications dominate), explicitly under-representing learning theory and causal reasoning—the authors admit per-domain estimates for sparse domains "should be interpreted with appropriate caution," yet headline numbers are not broken out by domain. (2) Self-referential data leakage risk: the extraction, quality-scoring, and judging are all LLM-based and predominantly GPT-family, so GPT-5.2's strong showing (top-2 as both subject and judge) is a structural confound not addressed. (3) The "70% Good-grade" quality threshold for inclusion is a data quirk that systematically excludes less-mainstream but valid papers, biasing the corpus. (4) No comparison against any prior experimental-design method (e.g., AAAR-1.0 baselines, AI-Scientist's experimental-design module) on the new benchmark—OptED is only compared to CoT and CoT+Search prompting of the same base models, leaving its advantage over existing agentic systems untested.

## Strengths

- Fills a real gap: experimental design has been the under-evaluated middle child of AI4Research, and the hierarchical six-dimension taxonomy makes failure modes (planning vs configuration) diagnosable rather than opaque.
- The redline mechanism is a well-motivated design that explicitly penalizes source hallucination and metric incompatibility—failure modes that ordinary averaging would hide.
- The OptED workflow is principled: stage isolation directly addresses the diagnosed cross-stage attention-switching problem, and the atomic tool interfaces with mandatory rationale recording make each decision auditable.
- Honest about limitations: a dedicated appendix acknowledges domain skew, extraction lossiness, the static single-shot paradigm (vs real iterative experimental loops), and benchmark-overfitting risks.
- Findings are internally coherent—the benchmark diagnosis (Low-Level is the bottleneck, search hurts) directly motivates the OptED design, and OptED's results reciprocally validate the diagnosis.

## Limitations

- **Single LLM judge, no human validation.** All scoring—including fatal-flaw detection—relies on GPT-5.2; inter-annotator agreement with humans is not reported, and the judge being from the same family as a top subject model is a confound.
- **Domain coverage is skewed.** Foundation models, generative models, and multimodal applications dominate; learning theory, causal reasoning, and other areas are sparsely represented, so aggregate numbers over-weight fashionable topics.
- **Static, single-shot evaluation.** Real experimental design is iterative and outcome-conditioned; SCOPE's design-to-evaluation paradigm abstracts this away, which the authors themselves flag as a future-work direction.
- **Scope restricted to ML-style benchmark-driven research.** Natural-science experimental design (wet-lab, physical, safety-constrained) is out of scope and would require fundamentally different evaluation logic.
- **No head-to-head with existing agentic baselines.** OptED is benchmarked only against prompting variants of the same models, not against AI-Scientist, AgentLaboratory, AAAR-1.0 pipelines, or other experimental-design agents.
- **Extraction fidelity ceiling.** Despite a 35/50 threshold and multi-model verification, extraction remains lossy and may distort ground truth, particularly for implicit assumptions and domain conventions.
- **Persistent High-Level/Low-Level gap post-OptED** indicates the deeper configuration-grounding problem is unresolved and awaits future work.

## Relevance

This work is highly relevant to AI4Research and LLM-agent evaluation: it provides the first systematic, fine-grained benchmark for autonomous experimental design and an immediately usable agentic workflow (OptED) that improves LLM research-planning quality, directly informing the design of future AI co-scientists and self-improving research agents. For anyone building or evaluating LLM-driven research pipelines, the diagnosis that Low-Level resource configuration—rather than high-level reasoning—is the bottleneck, and that naive web search actively harms planning, is an actionable architectural lesson.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does SCOPE performance correlate with model size and training-data composition, and does the Low-Level bottleneck shrink for models specifically post-trained on benchmark/methodology corpora?
- Would replacing GPT-5.2 with a diverse multi-judge panel (or adding human spot-checks on a stratified sample) materially change the model rankings or the redline rates?
- Can the static design-to-evaluation paradigm be extended to a multi-turn setting where models receive simulated experimental outcomes and must revise their plans, and does OptED's stage isolation still help under iteration?
- How does OptED compare against existing end-to-end research agents (AI-Scientist v2, AgentLaboratory, AAAR-1.0) on the same SCOPE tasks, rather than only against CoT/CoT+Search prompting of the same base models?
- Does the search-induced degradation generalize to other reasoning-heavy tasks beyond experimental design, or is it specific to the planning/configuration trade-off identified here?
- What kinds of Low-Level errors (wrong dataset family, fabricated baseline, incompatible metric) dominate, and could a dedicated tool-augmented grounding module close the remaining High-Level/Low-Level gap that OptED leaves open?
- To what extent do SCOPE scores predict downstream outcomes—does a higher-scoring plan actually execute and reproduce better in an end-to-end AI-Scientist-style loop?
