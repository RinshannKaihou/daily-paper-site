> **一句话总结：** 论文揭示 ALiBi 位置编码在线性偏置随距离增长时会触发浮点下溢，使部分注意力头从某个距离起"失明"，并在 BLOOM/Falcon-RW/MPT 等预训练模型中实证确认该现象；在 148M 参数解码器上的训练实验表明，下溢对常用解码基准影响很小，但显著损害 passkey/needle-in-a-haystack 关联检索（C+L 组合把上下文外 passkey AUC 从 ALiBi 的 0.08 提到 0.79）。

> **TL;DR:** The paper identifies an overlooked numerical failure mode of ALiBi positional encoding: its linearly scaling bias underflows floating-point precision (bf16 threshold ≈ −92.19, fp32 ≈ −103.28), zeroing attention weights and rendering steep-slope heads "blind" beyond a per-head distance δ_h. The authors confirm the phenomenon in pretrained BLOOM 560M / Falcon-RW 7B / MPT (e.g., at token distance 2048, 36.6% of attention entries have underflowed), then run 148M-parameter decoder training experiments on 20B FineWeb-Edu tokens to disentangle it from out-of-context degradation. Standard decoder benchmarks (CS/QA/LG) move only 1.6–3.4 pp, but associative retrieval is strongly affected: clamping + log-scaled distances (C+L) raise out-of-context passkey AUC from 0.08 (ALiBi baseline) to 0.79, while default ALiBi slopes remain the strongest baseline on needle-in-a-haystack.

## 核心贡献

- 首次解析地刻画 ALiBi 的浮点下溢失效模式：偏置 −m·D 随 token 距离线性增长，最终越过 softmax 指数下溢阈值（bf16 τ_u ≈ −92.19，fp32 τ_u ≈ −103.28），使注意力权重归零、对应注意力头"失明"，并导致剩余权重的再分配。
- 推导出每个头的"失明距离" δ_h = |τ_u|/m_h，并给出每个头的部分失明（陡斜率 δ_1）与完全失明（最平斜率 δ_H）两个临界点。
- 在三个公开预训练 ALiBi 模型（BLOOM 560M、Falcon-RW 7B、MPT 7B）中实证测量下溢比例曲线，形状与解析预测高度吻合；并整理出迄今最全的 ALiBi 模型清单（Appendix Table 5）。
- 系统训练 148M 参数 Llama-style 解码器（20B FineWeb-Edu token、3 seeds、约 9000 GPU·h），通过 Steep/Safe/Wide 三种斜率配置把下溢效应与上下文外退化分离开来。
- 提出并评估四种训练级缓解策略——Clamping、Robust Slopes (Explicit, E)、Log-scaled Distances (L)、Soft Capping (S)，以及它们的全部组合（共 11 个组合），发现没有单一策略在所有任务上占优：C+L 在 passkey 上最强，而默认 ALiBi 在 NIHS 上仍最强。

## Key Contributions

- First analytical characterization of ALiBi's floating-point underflow failure: the bias −m·D grows linearly with token distance and eventually crosses the softmax exponent underflow threshold (bf16 τ_u ≈ −92.19, fp32 τ_u ≈ −103.28), zeroing attention weights, blinding the corresponding head, and redistributing remaining row weight.
- Derivation of per-head "blindness distances" δ_h = |τ_u|/m_h, distinguishing partial blindness onset (steepest head δ_1, e.g., 124 for default slopes under bf16) from full blindness (flattest head δ_H ≈ 22,358).
- Empirical confirmation in three pretrained ALiBi models (BLOOM 560M, Falcon-RW 7B, MPT 7B): measured underflow fractions match the analytical curve, and at a token distance of 2048, 36.6% of attention entries have underflowed. Appendix Table 5 compiles the most complete inventory of ALiBi-trained models to date.
- Controlled 148M-parameter decoder training (Llama/SmolLM recipe, 20B FineWeb-Edu tokens, 3 seeds, ~9,000 GPU·h) that isolates the underflow effect from generic out-of-context degradation via Steep / Safe / Wide slope configurations plus ALiBi and RoPE baselines.
- Four mitigation strategies — Clamping (C), Robust Slopes / Explicit (E), Log-scaled Distances (L), Soft Capping (S) — evaluated individually and in all 11 combinations; no single strategy dominates, with C+L best on passkey while default ALiBi remains strongest on NIHS.

## Methodology

**Analytical core (Section 3).** ALiBi replaces absolute position embeddings with an additive bias B = −m·D added to attention logits before softmax. For each attention head with slope m_h, the authors derive the smallest distance at which an entry underflows: Δ_h(ε) = min{d ∈ ℕ | ε − m_h·d ≤ τ_u}, with δ_h := Δ_h(0). Default Press et al. (2022) slopes form a geometric series 1/2, 1/4, …, 1/2^8; under bf16 this gives δ_1 = 124 (partial blindness) and δ_H = 22,358 (full blindness). The paper notes two consequences: (1) *positional blindness* — numerator zeroes, so distant tokens cannot contribute to V; (2) *weight redistribution* — denominator loses those terms and the surviving tokens' weights inflate.

**Four mitigation strategies (Section 3.4).** (1) **Clamping (C)** caps the bias at c_clamp = −87 (chosen below the bf16 denorm threshold −87.34 to avoid denormalized numbers). (2) **Robust Slopes / Explicit (E)** picks slopes so that blindness distances are spread evenly across a target context length (e.g., δ_1=32, …, δ_H=2048); targeted at 2× training context (δ_H = 4096). (3) **Log-scaled Distances (L)** replaces D with log(D+1), which for default slopes moves δ_1 from 124 to ≈ 4.37 × 10^53 — effectively out of reach. (4) **Soft Capping (S)** clamps logits via z·tanh(A/z) with z=50 (Riviere et al., 2024).

**Inspection of pretrained models (Section 4.1).** Three representative ALiBi decoders: BLOOM 560M, Falcon-RW 7B, MPT 7B (training window 2048; Table 1 lists slopes and expected δ_1, δ_H). The authors run a length-stratified FineWeb-Edu perplexity probe (32,000 docs across 32 bins up to 8,192 tokens) while instrumenting the underflow fraction per head, and evaluate passkey (PK) and needle-in-a-haystack (NIHS) AUC over token distance 0–4096 (twice the training context). PK uses word passphrases (adapted for small models); NIHS embeds the passkey in a real document.

**Controlled training (Section 4.2–4.3).** 148M-parameter Llama-style decoders trained one epoch on 20B FineWeb-Edu tokens with AdamW, three seeds per config (data order fixed across seeds so remaining variance is initialization + positional encoding). Section 4.2 varies slopes: **Steep** (δ_1=128, δ_H=512), **Safe** (δ_1=2048, δ_H=4096), **Wide** (δ_1=16, δ_H=4096 in powers of two), plus ALiBi and RoPE baselines. Section 4.3 layers the four mitigations (C, E, L, S) and all 11 combinations on top of the same setup. Evaluation: train/valid loss, PK and NIHS AUC (in- and out-of-context), and zero-shot scores on grouped decoder benchmarks — common sense (HellaSwag, PIQA, WinoGrande, COPA, SiQA, WSC), QA (ARC-E, ARC-C, BoolQ, SciQ, RACE), and language (LAMBADA, BLiMP, WiC). Because FlashAttention does not support clamping / log scaling / soft capping at the bias level, the authors implement these via PyTorch FlexAttention and validate against FlashAttention.

## Results & Highlights

- **Empirical underflow at distance 2048:** Across the three pretrained ALiBi models, an average of **36.6% of attention entries** have underflowed by token distance 2048; the measured underflow curves closely match the analytical prediction from δ_h.
- **Blindness distances for default slopes (bf16):** δ_1 = **124** (steepest head goes blind) and δ_H = **22,358** (flattest head) — for BLOOM 560M and Falcon-RW 7B; MPT clamps its flattest head to δ_H = 2,000.
- **Pretrained retrieval AUC (Table 2):** PK-in / PK-out / NIHS-in / NIHS-out = BLOOM 560M **0.93 / 0.29 / 0.45 / 0.06**; Falcon-RW 7B **1.00 / 0.62 / 0.61 / 0.14**; MPT **1.00 / 0.74 / 0.47 / 0.16** — confirming that even strong pretrained models struggle at long retrieval distances.
- **Slope variations (Table 3a, 148M models):** On PK-in, **Safe = 0.98 vs ALiBi = 0.93** (+5 pp); on PK-out, **Safe = 0.17 vs ALiBi = 0.08** (+9 pp). But on NIHS-in, ALiBi dominates: **ALiBi = 0.68 vs Safe = 0.48** (−20 pp) and **vs RoPE = 0.28** (−40 pp). RoPE completely fails PK-out (0.00) and NIHS-out (0.00).
- **Mitigation combinations (Table 3b):** Best PK-out AUC is **C+L = 0.79 vs ALiBi baseline 0.08** (≈ 10× improvement); L alone = 0.77. But on NIHS-in, no mitigation beats ALiBi's 0.68 — the best mitigations (C, Clamped) only reach 0.65, and most combinations drop to 0.40–0.50.
- **Downstream benchmarks barely move:** Across all configs, CS / QA / LG benchmarks differ by only **1.6 to 3.4 pp** — e.g., ALiBi LG = 54.0 vs Safe = 53.7 vs RoPE = 54.5 — confirming that standard benchmarks are insensitive to the failure.
- **Training loss:** Safe has the narrowest train/valid gap (2.983 / 2.876); RoPE and ALiBi achieve the lowest validation loss (RoPE 2.842, ALiBi 2.846); L and combinations involving L train slightly slower (valid loss 2.883–2.884).
- **The steep-slope stress test still functions:** Even Steep (designed to force early blindness, δ_1=128) reaches CS 46.7 / QA 46.0 / LG 52.4 — within ~2 pp of the ALiBi baseline, demonstrating that multi-layer relay partially recovers capacity.

## Critical Assessment

**Novelty** is genuine and well-scoped: the closest prior work (Wang et al., 2025, "When Precision Meets Position") traces an analogous bfloat16 breakdown but for *RoPE*; Chi et al. (2023) noticed steep-slope heads behave like a sliding window without connecting it to underflow. The ALiBi-side analytical derivation, the per-head blindness-distance formula, and the curated model inventory (Appendix Table 5) are fresh contributions.

**Rigor** is solid but bounded. The controlled 148M-parameter study with three fixed-order seeds and 20B tokens is appropriately designed to isolate positional-encoding effects from data-order noise, and the decision to restate every prior result with the same FlexAttention backend is a credible reproducibility move. However, three issues weaken the empirical claims:

1. **Single model size (148M).** The authors themselves flag this in Limitations — all mitigation and slope findings may not transfer to 7B+ models, which is exactly the regime where production ALiBi models (BLOOM-176B, Falcon-RW 7B, MPT 7B) actually live. The pretrained-model inspection provides existence evidence but cannot validate the *mitigation* findings at scale.
2. **Weak/absent baselines on retrieval.** NIHS comparisons omit recent strong long-context methods (YaRN, FIRE, KERPLE) beyond a brief related-work mention; RoPE is included but only in its vanilla form with no π-scaling or NTK-aware variant, so the "default ALiBi remains strongest on NIHS" claim is essentially comparing against a *known-weak* RoPE configuration.
3. **High variance undermines several mitigation claims.** The paper itself notes that C, E, and C+S show large variance "due to one of three seeds performing considerably worse," and hedges that this "might be an artifact of our small-scale experimentation, potentially underestimating these strategies." With n=3 seeds and several ±0.20 standard deviations on PK-in, some of the pairwise mitigation rankings (e.g., E+S = 0.99 vs C+E+L+S = 0.96) are not statistically distinguishable.

**Reproducibility** is strong on paper: hyperparameters (Appendix E), benchmark list (Appendix Table 6), prompt templates (Appendix D.2), floating-point thresholds (Appendix Table 4), and per-config blindness distances (Appendix Table 8) are all reported, and code release is promised. The FlexAttention re-implementation is a real but unavoidable friction point — anyone reproducing must match that path, since vanilla FlashAttention cannot run the mitigations at all.

**Significance** is meaningful for a niche: ALiBi is no longer the default (RoPE won), but the paper's argument that "the cheapest alternative deserves a second look now that RoPE's numerics are also under scrutiny" is reasonable, and the clamping recommendation is essentially free for any new ALiBi training run.

**Red flags.** None severe. Two minor overclaims to read critically: (a) the headline "0.79 vs 0.08" PK-out delta for C+L is the *single best* mitigation; most other strong combinations (E+S, L+S, C+L+S) land at 0.67–0.69, so the ten-fold figure cherry-picks the top of a noisy distribution. (b) The conclusion that "no single strategy dominates" is technically correct but slightly understates that L and L-containing combinations are the most consistent winners on PK while consistently *hurting* NIHS — a cleaner framing would be "log scaling trades NIHS for PK."

## Strengths

- A clean, original analytical result (δ_h = |τ_u|/m_h) that is verifiable in a few lines and immediately actionable.
- Honest empirical scope: the authors explicitly disentangle underflow from generic out-of-context degradation by varying slopes by design (Steep/Safe/Wide), rather than conflating the two.
- Comprehensive mitigation sweep — 4 strategies × 11 combinations — is unusually thorough for a positional-encoding paper.
- Transparent about variance, compute cost (9,000 GPU·h), and the small-model limitation; the appendix is detailed enough to reproduce.
- Curates a useful practical artifact: Appendix Table 5, a near-complete inventory of every published ALiBi model and its slopes.

## Limitations

- All controlled findings come from 148M-parameter decoders; transfer to billion-scale models is asserted but not demonstrated.
- Architecture and corpus (FineWeb-Edu, Llama/SmolLM recipe) are held fixed, so interactions with different architectures (e.g., encoder-decoder, Mamba hybrids) or non-English corpora are unexplored.
- RoPE baseline is vanilla; no modern length-extrapolation baselines (YaRN, NTK-aware, π-scaled RoPE) are compared, weakening the "ALiBi is still competitive" framing.
- Variance is high for several mitigations (n=3 seeds, σ up to ±0.20 on PK-in); some rankings are not statistically separable.
- The paper studies only the linear-bias family (ALiBi/TISA) and explicitly excludes parameterized bias encodings (KERPLE, FIRE, Sandwich) from the experimental scope.
- No investigation of whether underflow differentially affects the *backward* pass gradient flow — the authors hypothesize this matters more for training than inference but leave it as future work.

## Relevance

对长上下文与位置编码方向高度相关：该工作揭示了 ALiBi 浮点下溢这一被忽视的失效模式，并提供了 clamping、log-scaling 等可立即落地的训练级修复，对研究 RoPE/ALiBi 之外的相对位置编码、长上下文检索（passkey/NIHS）以及数值稳定性的人员具有直接参考价值，也提醒在评估长上下文模型时不要仅依赖标准解码基准（CS/QA/LG 几乎看不出问题），而应纳入关联检索探针。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do the mitigation rankings (especially the dominance of C+L on PK and the NIHS regression of L-containing combos) hold at 1B+ and 7B+ parameter scales, where most production ALiBi models actually live?
- Is the "default ALiBi slopes remain strongest on NIHS" result robust against modern RoPE length-extrapolation baselines (YaRN, NTK-aware, π-scaled RoPE), or is it an artifact of comparing against vanilla RoPE only?
- Does underflow differentially damage gradient flow in the backward pass (zeroed entries carry no gradient), and would a straight-through or scaled-gradient estimator recover capacity for steep heads during training?
- Can the per-head blindness-distance formula δ_h = |τ_u|/m_h be inverted into a *learned* slope schedule that adapts to a target context length and to the empirical logit distribution, rather than the geometric prior?
- How does ALiBi underflow interact with grouped-query attention, sliding-window attention, and hybrid SSM/attention architectures where the effective receptive field is built differently?
- The paper shows perplexity and underflow fraction look similar but uncorrelated — is there a retrieval-specific signal (e.g., attention entropy, induction-head strength) that better predicts when blindness will hurt downstream tasks?
