> **一句话总结：** AdaMX 通过把 MX 过宽的 8 位指数重新分配为「逐块元数据」，对权重按块路由 FP4/INT4 + 尺度细化/块最大值扩展四种组合，对激活用 E4M1 round-to-nearest + 无损 FP6 块最大值扩展，在同等等效位宽（EBW）下消除 MXFP4 在常识任务上 83% 的精度损失，并在 22nm FD-SOI 加速器上仅增加约 1% 系统能耗。

> **TL;DR:** AdaMX is a heterogeneity-aware microscaling (MX) format and 22nm FD-SOI accelerator that repurposes MX's over-provisioned 8-bit exponent field as per-block, operand-specific metadata: weights route each block among four {FP4/INT4} × {scale-refine/block-max-ext} combinations via offline search, while activations use round-to-nearest E4M1 scaling co-designed with a lossless FP6 block-maximum extension. On WikiText-2 across Llama-3.1-8B/70B and Qwen2.5-3B/14B it closes 63–72% of the MXFP4-to-FP16 perplexity gap, recovers 83% of MXFP4's commonsense loss and 82% on MMLU (vs. 43%/27% of the NVFP4 loss), and on Gemma-4 12B beats MXFP4 on all four VLM benchmarks — at ≤1.1% extra system energy and +3.2% area over an otherwise identical MXFP4 PE.

## 核心贡献

- **量化异质性的形式化刻画**：首次系统论证在低比特 MX 中存在两层异质性——(1) 同一张量内不同 block 对 FP4 vs. INT4、尺度细化 vs. 块最大值扩展的偏好相互正交（Llama-3.1-8B 中 FP4 占 52.8%、INT4 占 47.2%；尺度细化占 49.6%、块最大值扩展占 36.5%）；(2) 权重（静态、可离线搜索）和激活（在线生成、需单次扫描编码）需要不同的自适应策略。
- **MX 指数字段过度配置的再利用**：证明对 LLM，4 位指数（E4，容量 15）已足以覆盖 90%+ 的输出通道指数范围（最大 11）和 99.99% 的 token 范围，腾出 4 bit 作为元数据而不增加 EBW。
- **权重格式 E4Mt2T2**：用 2-bit T2 选择器把每个 block 路由到四种「格式 × 增强」组合，离线穷举 12 个候选（4 个 T2 × 3 个指数偏移）选最小 MSE；T2 同时决定 FP4/INT4 基路径和尺度细化（比例集 {1.0, 1.25, 1.5, 1.75}）或块最大值 FP6/INT6 提升。
- **激活格式 E4M1Mt2N1**：固定 FP4，用 1-bit M1 做无除法 round-to-nearest 尺度细化（与 FP6 块最大值扩展协同设计，最坏比例 1.25 → 块最大值恰为 FP6 max-norm 7.5），用 N1 + Mt2 在两个 4 值邻域 {0,+1,+2,+3} / {−2,−1,0,+1} 间选择，实现 6 个偏移完全无损编码，消除 13% 的钳位，块最大值平方误差从 0.047 降至 0.021（2.2×）。
- **统一硬件加速器**：单一 32-lane 数据通路同时支持 K=16（4.5 EBW）和 K=32（4.25 EBW）两种 block size；T2-routed MAC 用共享乘法器 + 轻量修正 sidepath 覆盖四种模式；块最大值修正被分解为三个标量项 ΔA·W、ΔW·A、ΔA·ΔW；激活量化单元无除法/乘法实现 FP4/FP6 量化。
- **完整的芯片级评估**：22nm FD-SOI 原型，500 MHz、0.8 V，8192 个乘法器，864 KB SRAM，峰值 8.19 TOPS（17.1 TOPS/W）；相对等吞吐 MXFP4 PE 仅 +3.2% 面积、+3.5% 功耗，系统能耗 +0.1~1.1%（AdaMX-16）或 −2.9~5.2%（AdaMX-32）。

## Key Contributions

- **Formalization of two-level quantization heterogeneity in microscaling**: shows within a weight tensor the preferred element format (FP4 vs INT4) and the preferred precision-recovery scheme (scale refinement vs block-max mantissa extension) are orthogonal and both vary block-by-block (Llama-3.1-8B: 52.8% FP4 / 47.2% INT4; 49.6% scale-refine / 36.5% block-max), while weights and activations require different adaptation because activations are produced online.
- **Reclaiming the over-provisioned MX exponent field**: demonstrates that a 4-bit exponent (E4, capacity 15) covers >90% of per-channel weight ranges (max 11) and >99.99% of per-token activation ranges, freeing 4 bits/block for metadata at zero EBW cost.
- **Operand-specialized format family AdaMX** with two operating points sharing one encoding/hardware: AdaMX-16 (4.5 EBW, higher accuracy) and AdaMX-32 (4.25 EBW, lower memory).
- **Weight format E4Mt2T2**: 2-bit T2 selector per block routing to four {format, enhancement} combinations, with exhaustive offline 12-candidate MSE search per block; the offset search subsumes the floor/ceil scale-rounding choice at no extra storage.
- **Activation format E4M1Mt2N1**: division-free round-to-nearest E4M1 scaling co-designed with a lossless FP6 block-maximum neighborhood selector (N1) that eliminates all 13% of clamped cases and reduces block-max MSE 2.2× without a wider FP7 datapath.
- **Unified 22nm FD-SOI accelerator**: one 32-lane MAC datapath serving both block sizes via shared adder-tree penultimate stages; block-max correction decomposed into three scalar terms; activation quantizer implemented without divider or multiplier.

## Methodology

**Motivation analysis.** The authors quantize 2000 sampled blocks of Llama-3.1-8B and measure per-block quantization SNR (QSNR). They show that (a) at block granularity FP4 wins on 52.8% of blocks and INT4 on 47.2%, contradicting the tensor-level conclusion that FP4 always dominates; (b) scale refinement wins on 49.6% of blocks and block-max mantissa extension on 36.5%, with 13.8% gaining little from either — and crucially the two axes are orthogonal, so no single static assignment is optimal. They further show (CDFs over 4 LLMs) that the 8-bit MX exponent is overprovisioned: per-channel weight exponent ranges never exceed 11, and per-token activation ranges stay inside E4 with >99.99% of blocks in range.

**Two-level scale structure.** Both operands replace E8M0 with `S = 2^(b_bias + e_block)`, where `b_bias` is an 8-bit integer set at coarser granularity (per output channel for weights, per token for activations, computed online) and `e_block` is the 4-bit per-block residual exponent. Activations additionally refine the scale with a 1-bit mantissa M1.

**Weight encoding (Algorithm 1, E4Mt2T2).** A metadata byte is packed as [E4 (4) | Mt2 (2) | T2 (2)]. T2 routes each block to one of four codewords: 00 = FP4 + block-max FP6 ext, 01 = FP4 + scale refine, 10 = INT4 + scale refine, 11 = INT4 + block-max INT6 ext. For scale refinement, Mt2 indexes a ratio in {1.0, 1.25, 1.5, 1.75}; for block-max extension, Mt2 stores a 2-bit index into a 4-value FP6 neighborhood centered on the original FP4 value. The encoder exhaustively tries all 4 T2 values at 3 exponent offsets b ∈ {−1, 0, +1} (12 candidates/block) and picks the minimum MSE — this offline search is feasible because weights are static. The offset search subsumes the floor/ceil rounding choice and folds the winning offset into E4 at no extra storage.

**Activation encoding (E4M1Mt2N1).** A metadata byte [E4 (4) | M1 (1) | Mt2 (2) | N1 (1)]. M1 turns the scale into E4M1 with m ∈ {1, 1.5}, chosen to map the block maximum near FP4 max-norm vmax = 6. The authors derive a division-free scale computation: rounding the mantissa factor (ma/1.5) onto {0.75, 1, 1.5} only requires comparing the leading bits of ma against two constants (1.3125 and 1.875). Round-to-nearest (vs. floor/ceil) is co-designed with the FP6 block-max extension: round's worst-case ratio 1.25 lands the block maximum at exactly FP6 max-norm 7.5, recoverable without clamping. The N1 + Mt2 fields then encode six possible FP6 offsets losslessly via two overlapping 4-value neighborhoods {0,+1,+2,+3} and {−2,−1,0,+1}, using decode `FP6_idx = FP4_idx×4 + Mt2 − 2·N1` (one shift, one add, one conditional subtract).

**Block-size sweep** from 4 to 256 on four LLMs identifies 16 and 32 as the knee of the perplexity / underflow-rate / MSE-vs-EBW curves; supporting both on one datapath makes block size a runtime operating point rather than a fixed design parameter.

**Hardware.** An M×N output-stationary systolic array with 2×2-MAC PEs (4 fixed 32-lane dot-product MACs each sharing two activation-row and two weight-column operand registers). Decoder replication is reduced from MN to M+N by placing activation decoders per row and weight decoders per column. The MAC base path uses asymmetric 2-by-3-bit element multipliers; the correction sidepath computes three scalar terms (ΔA·W, ΔW·A, ΔA·ΔW) for block-max extension. Scale refinement is applied by two cascaded shift-add stages without a multiplier. A global K16_EN signal selects block size; at K=16 the adder-tree penultimate stage and pick mux trees expose two independent 16-block results at full 32-lane throughput. Synthesized/placed-and-routed in 22nm FD-SOI at 500 MHz, 0.8 V.

## Results & Highlights

- **WikiText-2 perplexity (Table IV):** AdaMX-16 closes 63–72% of the MXFP4→FP16 gap across all four LLMs (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B: FP16 6.24 → AdaMX-16 6.86 vs. MXFP4 bs=16 8.30; Qwen2.5-3B: 8.03 → 8.80 vs. 10.76); AdaMX-32 closes 55–62% at the lower 4.25 EBW. AdaMX-16 also beats M2XFP (6.97) and NVFP4 (6.94) on Llama-3.1-8B.
- **Zero-shot commonsense (Table III, 6-task avg):** AdaMX-16 recovers 77%/80%/92%/91% of the MXFP4→FP16 gap on Qwen2.5-3B / Llama-3.1-8B / Qwen2.5-14B / Llama-3.1-70B respectively — averaging ~83% recovery — and beats NVFP4 (66% recovery) and M2XFP (60% recovery) at matched 4.5 EBW. On Llama-3.1-70B the average reaches 79.15 (AdaMX-16) vs. MXFP4 73.81 (FP16 79.70).
- **5-shot MMLU (Table III):** AdaMX-16 recovers 72%/79%/83%/89% of the MXFP4→FP16 gap (avg ~82%); on Llama-3.1-8B MMLU avg AdaMX-16 62.90 vs. MXFP4 53.80 (FP16 65.35) vs. NVFP4 61.88.
- **vs. NVFP4:** AdaMX removes 43% of NVFP4's commonsense loss and 27% of its MMLU loss (abstract-level aggregate); on Llama-3.1-8B ARC-C, AdaMX-16 52.56 vs. NVFP4 53.24 (comparable) but on Qwen2.5-3B ARC-C AdaMX-16 45.65 vs. NVFP4 44.37.
- **Multimodal Gemma-4 12B (Table V):** AdaMX-16 beats MXFP4-16 on all four benchmarks; on MMMU the margin is +9.4 points (58.3 vs. 48.9), and AdaMX-16 retains 72% of FP16 accuracy on average (66.6 vs. 72.3), up to 96% on individual benchmarks. AdaMX-32 (62.3 avg) also beats MXFP4-16 (60.2) at lower EBW.
- **Long-context KV cache on RULER (Table VI, Llama-3.1-8B W4A4KV4):** AdaMX-16 exceeds NVFP4 by 2.4 points at 8K and 6.1 points at 32K on the 13-task average (89.7 vs. 87.3 at 8K; 81.7 vs. 75.6 at 32K); AdaMX-32 outperforms MXFP4 by 36.8 points (84.6 vs. 47.8) at 8K and 41.1 points (72.9 vs. 31.8) at 32K. On NIAH-multi-key at 32K: AdaMX-16 84.0 vs. MXFP4 18.5.
- **Block-max encoding quality:** the lossless N1 neighborhood encoding eliminates all 13% of clamped cases incurred by constrained FP6 and reduces block-max MSE from 0.047 to 0.021 (2.2×), independent of model; on Llama-3.1-8B, round scaling + N1 gives WikiText-2 PPL 6.86 vs. 6.94 (constrained FP6) and 6.88 (FP7 alternative that also needs wider hardware).
- **Hardware cost (Table VII, 22nm FD-SOI, 500 MHz):** AdaMX SA = 2.166 mm² vs. MXFP4 SA = 2.099 mm² (+3.2% area); AdaMX SA 482 mW vs. 466 mW (+3.5% power); the per-PE AdaMX enhancement (block-max ext + scale refine) costs 270.6 µm² / 325.4 µm² of "other & regs." Peak 8.19 TOPS, 17.1 TOPS/W.
- **End-to-end energy (Fig. 13, SCALE-Sim):** AdaMX-16 adds 0.1–1.1% system energy over MXFP4-bs16 across batch 8/32/128 on four models; AdaMX-32 reduces system energy by 2.9–5.2% thanks to lower EBW (4.25 vs. 4.5 bits/element → fewer bits per DRAM access, and DRAM dominates 61–94% of system energy).

## Authors & Credibility

Joint work across **Brown University** (Junyi Luo, Xinting Jiang, Ruichen Qi, Minxing Chu, Mehdi Saligane), **University of Michigan** (Tai-Hao Wen, Hongyi Wu, Qirui Zhang, Dennis Sylvester — the latter a well-known VLSI/circuits researcher), and **Google** (Gregory Kielian, Ben Laurie — the latter a noted security/cryptography engineer). The combination of academic VLSI expertise (Sylvester, Saligane) and Google's production-LLM context lends credibility to both the silicon claims and the LLM accuracy claims. The work is positioned against the OCP MX standard and silicon-deployed formats (NVIDIA Blackwell NVFP4, AMD MI350, Microsoft Maia, Meta MTIA), indicating serious engagement with industry-standard deployment targets. Evaluation uses standard harnesses (lm-evaluation-harness, WikiText-2, RULER) on mainstream models (Llama-3.1, Qwen2.5, Gemma-4 12B) on 4× H100 GPUs, and the quantization framework extends Microsoft's open-source `microscaling` PyTorch library.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty (high).** The framing of quantization heterogeneity as two orthogonal axes (format × enhancement) at block granularity, and the operand-asymmetric response (offline-searchable weights vs. single-pass activations), is genuinely new relative to prior single-axis metadata formats: M2XFP commits to FP4-only + sub-block ratio/block-max, MX+ commits to FP4-only + position-indexed mantissa, BlockDialect varies only the dialect format, SMX adds only sub-block exponents, NVFP4 fixes E4M3 scale with no metadata. AdaMX is the first to simultaneously decouple format and enhancement axes per block and to specialize the encoding per operand. The observation that MX's E8M0 exponent is overprovisioned for LLMs (per-channel range ≤11, per-token range within E4 for >99.99% of blocks) is the key insight that makes this metadata "free."
- **Rigor (good, with caveats).** The block-level QSNR analysis (Fig. 2) on 2000 blocks of one model is somewhat thin — the orthogonality claim is asserted on Llama-3.1-8B and only stated to "hold across all four models and at block size 16" without showing the per-model scatter. The per-layer T2 distribution (Fig. 5) does provide corroborating evidence that all four codewords are non-negligibly populated at every layer in every model, which strengthens the claim that no single static format suffices. The activation encoding derivation (Eq. 2, the N1 neighborhood construction, the round-vs-floor-vs-ceil PPL comparison) is unusually rigorous and self-contained.
- **Reproducibility (medium-high).** Quantization code extends Microsoft's open-source `microxcaling` library and uses standard benchmarks (lm-evaluation-harness, WikiText-2, RULER) on mainstream open-weight models, with H100 hardware and clear EBW matching against M2XFP/NVFP4 baselines. However, the 22nm FD-SOI PPA numbers depend on a proprietary standard-cell library and commercial EDA flows (PrimeTime, Accelergy, CACTI, SCALE-Sim) that are not fully open, so the silicon claims cannot be independently reproduced bit-for-bit. The AdaMX encoder/decoder RTL is not stated to be open-sourced.
- **Baselines (adequate but with gaps).** The paper compares against MXFP4 (the OCP standard), NVFP4 (Blackwell), M2XFP, SMX4, and BlockDialect on perplexity, and against MXFP4/NVFP4/M2XFP on accuracy. Notably missing from accuracy tables: **MX+** and **MicroScopiQ** appear in Table I but not in the downstream-accuracy comparison; **AWQ, GPTQ, QuaRot, SpinQuant** (rotation-based 4-bit methods that are strong W4A4 baselines in 2025–2026) are entirely absent — the comparison is restricted to the MX-family format-design space the paper inhabits, which somewhat stacks the deck. The hardware comparison is only against MXFP4 and NVFP4 PEs synthesized in the same flow, not against any production silicon numbers.
- **Significance (high for the MX-hardware niche).** If the central claim — that operand-specialized per-block metadata can be encoded for free inside MX's existing scale field — holds up, it is directly actionable for future OCP-MX-compliant silicon, since it requires no change to the block-level storage budget. The KV-cache results (AdaMX-32 +41 points over MXFP4 at 32K) are especially compelling for long-context deployment.
- **Possible overclaims.** The abstract's "removes 83% of the MXFP4 accuracy loss on commonsense and 82% on MMLU" is the average of AdaMX-16 across four models; per-model recovery ranges from 77% (Qwen2.5-3B commonsense) to 92% (Qwen2.5-14B commonsense), so the headline number is favorable. The "removes 43%/27% of the NVFP4 loss" is a weaker claim that is only weakly supported by the tables (on several individual tasks NVFP4 actually beats AdaMX-16, e.g., Llama-3.1-8B ARC-C 53.24 vs. 52.56, BoolQ 81.07 vs. 81.19). The Gemma-4 12B "keeps up to 96% of FP16 accuracy" refers to the best single benchmark, not the average (72%).
- **Red flags.** None severe. The block-max MSE reduction (2.2×, model-independent) is presented as a property of the encoding, but the impact on end-to-end accuracy is only shown via four LLMs plus one VLM; generalization to reasoning/code/math-heavy models (e.g., Qwen3, DeepSeek-R1 distillations) is untested. The 22nm FD-SOI choice (rather than the more common 7nm/5nm CMOS for production AI accelerators) means the absolute PPA numbers are not directly comparable to merchant-silicon accelerators.

## Strengths

- Identifies and exploits a genuinely under-utilized resource (the over-provisioned MX exponent) to fund per-block heterogeneity at zero EBW cost — a clean, well-motivated architectural insight.
- Operand-asymmetric design is principled: weights get a richer offline-searched format (E4Mt2T2 with four modes) because they are static; activations get a deterministic, calibration-free, division-free online encoder (E4M1Mt2N1) because they cannot afford a per-block search. This matches the actual constraints of the two operands rather than forcing one scheme on both.
- Co-design of the activation scale rounding (round-to-nearest with worst-case ratio 1.25) and the FP6 block-max neighborhood (whose max-norm is exactly 6×1.25 = 7.5) is elegant: it provably eliminates clamping without going to a wider FP7 datapath, and it saves hardware (one fixed-offset subtract vs. data-dependent clamp logic).
- Single datapath serving both block sizes via shared adder-tree penultimate stages is a neat hardware trick that makes block size a true runtime knob.
- Comprehensive evaluation: 4 LLMs (3B–70B) + 1 VLM, 6 commonsense tasks + 5-shot MMLU + WikiText-2 + 4 VLM benchmarks + RULER long-context, with both accuracy and full silicon PPA (area, power, energy breakdown).

## Limitations

- The heterogeneity analysis (Fig. 2) is primarily on Llama-3.1-8B; the per-model block-level FP4/INT4 and refinement/extension scatter plots for the other three models are not shown, only asserted.
- Baseline set excludes strong non-MX 4-bit methods (AWQ, GPTQ, QuaRot, SpinQuant, MicroScopiQ on accuracy, MX+ on accuracy), so the comparison is internal to the MX format-design subfield.
- All accuracy results are post-training quantization of decoder-only LLMs; no encoder/encoder-decoder models, no fine-tuning recovery (e.g., QAT), no evaluation on math/code/reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, HumanEval, MATH).
- Hardware results are from a 22nm FD-SOI research library at 500 MHz / 0.8 V, not from a modern 7nm/5nm CMOS process; absolute area/energy numbers are not directly comparable to merchant AI silicon, and no silicon measurement (only post-PAR simulation + SCALE-Sim modeling) is provided.
- The offline weight encoder evaluates 12 candidates per block — runtime/energy of the encoding itself is not reported, only the inference-side cost; for very large models this offline cost may be non-trivial in practice even though it is one-time.
- KV-cache quantization uses the weight-side format with the same per-block selection rule, but the evaluation is limited to Llama-3.1-8B at 8K/32K; behavior at 64K–1M context and on other models is untested.

## Relevance

对关注**低比特 LLM 量化与硬件加速**的研究方向高度相关：AdaMX 直接处理部署侧最痛的 4-bit MX 精度损失问题，且其「在 MX 现有位预算内塞入逐块元数据」的思路可作为未来量化格式设计与加速器 RTL 的参考基线。对同时关注**长上下文 KV-cache 量化**和**多模态推理**的工作也有直接借鉴价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the two-axis heterogeneity finding (format × enhancement, orthogonal per block) hold for **rotated/SpinQuant-transformed** weights, where the outlier distribution is fundamentally changed? Would AdaMX's per-block search still pick a diverse T2 mix, or collapse to mostly FP4?
- How does AdaMX interact with **activation rotation** (QuaRot-style) on the activation side — does the FP4-favoring outlier structure that justifies dropping the T2 selector on activations still hold post-rotation?
- What is the **encoding-time cost** of the 12-candidate per-block exhaustive weight search at 70B scale (wall-clock, GPU-hours), and can it be amortized via a cheaper heuristic without losing the heterogeneity benefit?
- Can the N1 neighborhood-encoded lossless FP6 block-max idea be **ported back into the OCP MX standard** as a backward-compatible metadata extension, or does it require new decode hardware that breaks software compatibility with existing Blackwell/MI350 MXFP4 paths?
- How does AdaMX behave on **reasoning/code/math benchmarks** (GSM8K, MATH, HumanEval) where small quantization errors are known to compound — does the 83% commonsense-loss-recovery translate, or do the remaining 17% dominate on hard reasoning?
- At **64K–1M context**, does the KV-cache accuracy advantage of AdaMX-32 over MXFP4 (which is already +41 points at 32K) continue to grow, plateau, or invert?
