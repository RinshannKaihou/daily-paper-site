> **一句话总结：** DeepSeek 发布 552B 参数的多模态 MoE 模型 V4.1-Flash（预填激活 8B、解码激活 16B，支持 1M token 上下文），通过 CED 编码器-解码器架构、CSA2 跨层 KV 复用 + FP4 缓存与 SWA Bounded Replay，把全局 KV 缓存压到 890 字节/token（约为 V4-Flash 的 1/4）、持久化缓存压到 1/8，同时在 DeepSWE v1.1 上以 74.2% 超过 Claude Opus-5（74.0%）并大幅超越前代 V4-Flash（54.4%）。

> **TL;DR:** A 51-page technical report from DeepSeek-AI introducing DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, a 552B-parameter multimodal MoE (8B activated per token at prefill, 16B at decode, 1M-token context) that attacks the storage/bandwidth bottleneck of long-horizon agents via a Causal Encoder-Decoder (CED) architecture, Compressed Sparse Attention 2 (CSA2) with cross-layer KV/index reuse, FP4 main KV cache, and SWA Bounded Replay. Global KV cache drops to 890 bytes/token (about 1/4 of DeepSeek-V4-Flash and 437x smaller than DeepSeek-V1) while the post-trained model beats its predecessor DeepSeek-V4-Flash on DeepSWE v1.1 by +19.8 points (74.2% vs 54.4% resolved) and edges past Claude Opus-5 Max (74.0%) and GPT-5.6 Sol (73.0%); it is state-of-the-art on Terminal-Bench 2.1 (90.6% vs Opus-5's 89.1%) and Automation-Bench (54.8% vs 50.3%), though it clearly trails Opus-5 on science-heavy agentic suites like Terminal-Bench 4.0 (31.2% vs 51.8%). Checkpoints are released on Hugging Face.

## 核心贡献

- **CED（因果编码器-解码器）架构**：40 层骨干拆为 20 层编码器 + 20 层解码器，解码器的全局 KV 直接由编码器最终隐状态投影得到，预填只需计算前一半层，复杂度从 O(NL) 降到约 O(NL/2)，配合预填激活 8B / 解码 16B 的非对称激活，显著降低输入密集型 agent 负载的成本。
- **CSA2（压缩稀疏注意力 2）**：以 Full / Reindex / Reuse 三种静态模式跨层复用全局 main KV、indexer K 与 Top-K 索引，并配合仅用于解码器的层次化稀疏索引器（首个 Full 层选出 2048 块 × 8 位置 = 16384 候选池，后续索引器只在池内打分），同时压缩 KV 缓存的入口维度、序列维度与层维度。
- **极限 KV 压缩**：CSA2 + FP4（E2M1 + 每 16 通道一个 E4M3 scale，去掉 NVFP4 二级全局 scale）主 KV 量化，将全局 KV 缓存压至 890 字节/token（约 V4-Flash 的 1/4、V4 的 437 倍缩减）；SWA Bounded Replay 只重放最近 n_win=128 个 token（而非 L×n_win）近似重建 SWA 状态，使持久化 KV 缓存降至 V4-Flash 的约 1/8，且 SWA KV 不再写入 SSD。
- **工程与部署协同优化**：Single-Pass mHC + Mega-mHC 融合核将激活内存流量减半；Reuse 层预填仅需 15 个核、解码 11 个核；Engram 条件记忆模块（196B 参数）与 DSpark 半自回归投机解码；4K→1M 上下文扩展 256 倍，单 token 解码 FLOPs 仅增约 1/4。
- **训练与后训练体系**：45T token 多模态语料（文本:多模态 = 7:1）从 64K 稀疏注意力起步直接训练、34T 处扩展到 1M；后训练坚持 SFT→RL→OPD 的标准范式而无算法创新，将全部投入放在大规模任务合成与环境构建（DSec 沙箱平台、百万级并发容器）上，并引入 1–100 标量推理努力控制（API 档位 max/high/low 对应 100/75/50）。

## Key Contributions

- **Causal Encoder-Decoder (CED)**: splits the 40-layer backbone into a 20-layer causal encoder and a 20-layer decoder whose global KV is projected from the encoder's final hidden states, cutting prefill complexity from O(NL) to roughly O(NL/2) and enabling asymmetric activation (8B params/token at prefill vs 16B at decode) that suits input-heavy agentic workloads.
- **Compressed Sparse Attention 2 (CSA2)**: statically assigns each layer to Full, Reindex, or Reuse mode to share main KV and indexer K across layers and reuse Top-K indices (cache sharing and index reuse are decoupled); a decoder-only Hierarchical Sparse Indexer has the first Full-mode layer build a 16,384-position candidate pool (2,048 blocks x 8 positions) so later indexers score a fixed pool instead of the whole context, making per-query indexing cost constant in context length.
- **Aggressive KV cache compression**: CSA2 plus FP4 (E2M1 with one E4M3 scale per 16 channels, deliberately omitting NVFP4's second-level global scale) QAT for the main KV shrinks the global (HBM-resident) KV cache to 890 bytes/token — about 1/4 of DeepSeek-V4-Flash and a 437x reduction versus DeepSeek-V1. SWA Bounded Replay replays only the last n_win = 128 tokens (instead of L x n_win) to approximately reconstruct sliding-window states, cutting the persistent (SSD/host-memory) KV cache to roughly 1/8 of V4-Flash and removing SWA KV from SSD entirely.
- **Systems co-design**: Single-Pass mHC (shifting input-mixing coefficients by one block) with the fused Mega-mHC kernel halves activation memory traffic; Reuse-mode layers execute in only 15 kernels at prefill and 11 at decode; Engram (196B sparse conditional-memory parameters) and DSpark speculative decoding are integrated; extending context 256x from 4K to 1M raises single-token Decode FLOPs by only about 1/4.
- **Pre-training and post-training at scale**: 45T multimodal tokens trained with sparse attention from scratch at 64K and extended to 1M at 34T tokens; the post-training recipe deliberately avoids algorithmic novelty (SFT -> RL -> on-policy distillation) and attributes all gains to data/environment engineering — automated agent-task synthesis, the DSec sandbox platform (millions of concurrent containers, 2,500+ live containers per node via sub-NUMA partitioning, AppArmor + eBPF containment of reward-hacking agents), and a scalar 1-100 reasoning-effort control exposed in the API as max/high/low (b = 100/75/50).

## Methodology

The report is a full-stack technical report rather than a controlled academic study. **Architecture**: the backbone has 40 layers (20 encoder + 20 decoder), hidden size 5,120, 64 query heads with head dim 512 and query compression dim 1,280; encoder CSA2 uses compression ratio m = 2, decoder m = 1; every CSA2 layer keeps its own global Q and SWA KV (window 128). MoE layers (1 shared + 384 routed experts, 6 activated, expert intermediate dim 2,304) use modality-specific auxiliary-loss-free load balancing with separate correction biases for text vs image tokens. The DeepSeek-ViT vision encoder (32 layers, patch 14, 2D-RoPE, RMSNorm, SwiGLU, linear patch projection for Muon compatibility) is contrastively pre-trained on ~47B image-text pairs at 224x224, then autoregressively fine-tuned with a 4B MoE LLM on 236B tokens at 544-1344 px; 3x3 pixel-unshuffle cuts visual tokens by 9x. **Optimizers**: Muon with Nesterov momentum and decoupled weight decay for linear weights, head-wise Muon for Q/K, AdamW for norms and non-matrix parameters, and a novel Sinkhorn-balanced momentum update for Engram tables/embeddings/prediction head that approximately equalizes row and column RMS (replacing Adam states with a single momentum buffer). **Training**: batch size fixed at 100.6M tokens, LR 2.6e-4 to 28T then cosine decay to 2.6e-5; sparse attention from scratch (no dense warmup). **FP4**: quantization-aware training is applied during post-training, quantizing after RoPE; the SWA KV cache stays FP8 due to sensitivity. **SWA Bounded Replay** is simulated during post-training for train-aware adaptation, and the same approximate states are used at inference; encoder-side replay makes prefix caching depend only on global KV. **Post-training**: large-scale synthesized verifiable tasks (problem, environment, verification-system triplets audited for difficulty/correctness), asynchronous RL across many scaffolds with checkpoint merging between runs, and full-vocabulary on-policy distillation from 40+ architecturally heterogeneous teachers; reasoning effort b in {1..100} is trained via effort-conditioned subgroups with an exponentially decaying token-length penalty k(b). **Evaluation**: internal controlled harness for base models; comparisons against Opus-5 Max, GPT-5.6 Max/Sol, Kimi-K3, GLM-5.3, DS-V4-Pro/Flash at Max effort, plus cross-scaffold (8 configurations from 6 harness families) and multi-agent (Agent Team mode with lead/spawned teammates, durable mailboxes, shared task board) studies under wall-clock deadlines.

## Results & Highlights

- **DeepSWE v1.1 (Resolved)**: 74.2% vs DeepSeek-V4-Flash's 54.4% (+19.8 points) — a near-20-point single-generation jump — and above Claude Opus-5 Max (74.0%) and GPT-5.6 Sol (73.0%); Kimi-K3 reaches 66.9%.
- **Terminal-Bench 2.1 (Pass@1)**: 90.6%, the best reported, beating Opus-5 (89.1%), GPT-5.6 (88.8%), GLM-5.3 (88.2%), and DS-V4-Flash (82.7%). On Terminal-Bench 3.0/4.0, however, it scores 30.0%/31.2% vs Opus-5's 43.3%/51.8%.
- **KV cache footprint**: global (HBM) KV cache is 890 bytes/token — about 4x smaller than DeepSeek-V4-Flash and 437x smaller than DeepSeek-V1; persistent (SSD/host) cache is about 1/8 of V4-Flash; extending context 256x (4K to 1M) raises Decode FLOPs by only ~1/4.
- **Codeforces rating**: 3471, surpassing DS-V4-Pro (3348) and DS-V4-Flash (3289); MathArena Apex 65.6% ties Kimi-K3 (65.6%) and edges DS-V4-Pro (65.3%).
- **Base-model efficiency (Table 1)**: with 1/3 the total params (552B vs 1.6T) and 1/4 the activated params vs V4-Pro-Base, it matches or beats V4-Pro-Base on MMLU-Pro (74.1 vs 73.5), HumanEval (79.4 vs 76.8), and GSM8K (93.0 vs 92.6), with 5-10% improvements on held-out (BPB) evaluation sets.
- **Agentic breadth**: Automation-Bench 54.8% vs Opus-5's 50.3%; Agents' Last Exam 31.8% vs Opus-5's 28.6% and K3's 28.5%; HLE w/ tools 63.9% vs Opus-5's 63.6%; CyberGym 88.1% is SOTA (vs GPT-5.6's 84.5%), while ExploitGym falls well short (15.3% vs GPT-5.6's 33.7%).
- **Reasoning-effort control**: raising effort from 25 to 100 lifts average Pass@1 on eight reasoning-intensive benchmarks from 67.1% to 76.3%, DeepSWE v1.1 from 66.0% to 74.2%, and Terminal-Bench 2.1 from 82.4% to 90.6%, at roughly 2.5x more output tokens; the 60-80 range recovers most of max-tier accuracy at less than half the token budget.
- **Scaffold robustness (Table 4)**: on DeepSWE v1.1 performance spans 65.5 (OpenCode) to 74.2 (mini-SWE) across 8 scaffolds (Claude Code 69.8, Codex 65.6, Pi 66.2, DSH Minimal 72.6); Terminal-Bench 2.1 spans 84.1 (Codex) to 90.6 (DSH Minimal), indicating the agentic ability is not overfit to one harness.
- **Multi-agent (preliminary)**: on the 172-task ProgramBench golden subset, multi-agent Almost@1 rises from 13.59% (1h) to 30.04% (8h) vs 12.79% to 20.39% single-agent; on FrontierSWE v2 (no-GPU), Mean@5 rises from 13.50% (1h) to 32.90% (20h) vs 10.50% to 28.20% single-agent.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: the framing is systems-level integration rather than a single new algorithm. CED is an admitted refinement of YOCO, CSA2's cross-layer reuse extends ideas from IndexCache/YOIO/HySparse, and the post-training section explicitly claims "no algorithmic innovation." The genuine novelty is the joint three-dimensional compression (entry x sequence x layer) pushed to 890 bytes/token plus the deployment-level SWA Bounded Replay trick, which is a clever and well-motivated storage-computation trade-off.
- **Rigor**: strong on end-to-end evidence but weak on ablations within this report. Claims that FP4 main KV, Single-Pass mHC's coefficient shift, and SWA Bounded Replay cause "negligible" degradation are asserted without numbers or tables in the main text — the reader must trust companion papers or internal experiments. Base-model comparisons use an internal evaluation framework with a generous "gap <= 0.3 counts as the same level" equivalence rule, and several comparator cells are missing (e.g., no Sol/K3 entries on Codeforces; "-" for many visual benchmarks).
- **Reproducibility**: checkpoints are released (good), but the 45T-token corpus, task-synthesis pipelines, DSec, and teachers cannot be reproduced; the multi-agent results are explicitly labeled preliminary and compare "strongest observed multi-agent configurations" against single-agent baselines, which invites selection bias.
- **Significance**: high — this is arguably the most aggressive production-grade KV cache compression published to date, and the 74.2% DeepSWE v1.1 / 90.6% Terminal-Bench 2.1 results from an open-weights 16B-activation model are a real milestone for affordable long-horizon agents.
- **Red flags**: the best agentic headline numbers are obtained on the authors' own DeepSeek Harness / mini-SWE scaffolds (TB 2.1: 90.6 on DSH vs 88.0 on Claude Code), although the cross-scaffold table partially mitigates this; MGSM regressed vs the smaller predecessor (80.2 vs V4-Flash's 85.7 and V4-Pro's 84.4), MATH trails V4-Pro (61.1 vs 64.5), LongBench-V2 sits well below V4-Pro (45.2 vs 51.5) despite the 1M-context focus, and text-only HLE (36.8; 39.1 on the text subset) trails GLM-5.3 (42.7) and GPT-5.6 (44.5) — the "matches frontier" claim should be read as benchmark-dependent.

## Strengths

- Attacks the actual deployment bottleneck (HBM/SSD capacity and bandwidth, prefill compute) with quantified, multiplicative savings: 1/4 global cache, 1/8 persistent cache, ~1/2 prefill FLOPs, near-constant Decode FLOPs to 1M tokens.
- Near-20-point single-generation jump on DeepSWE v1.1 and state-of-the-art Terminal-Bench 2.1 / Automation-Bench / Agents' Last Exam / CyberGym results from a model activating only 16B parameters at decode.
- Honest, unusually detailed limitations section (CSA2 selection errors and approximate SWA reconstruction at cache-resumption boundaries are named as uncharacterized robustness risks) and candid framing of where it loses (science-oriented Terminal-Bench 4.0, visual agents vs closed-source frontier).
- The scalar reasoning-effort interface (trained once, interpolated at deployment, with front-loaded gains in the 60-80 range) is a practically valuable contribution, as is the cross-scaffold robustness evaluation across 8 harness configurations.
- Open weights plus concrete engineering detail (kernel counts, Sinkhorn-balance algorithm, container density numbers) make this a useful reference for practitioners building long-context serving systems.

## Limitations

- No quantitative ablations in the main text for the individual compression decisions (FP4 vs FP8 main KV, Reuse-vs-Reindex layer assignment, bounded-replay approximation error); several components are only validated by citation to companion papers.
- Clear gaps remain vs frontier closed-source models on the hardest suites: Terminal-Bench 4.0 (31.2 vs Opus-5's 51.8), Terminal-Bench 3.0 (30.0 vs 43.3), ExploitGym (15.3 vs 33.7), text-only HLE, and visual-agent benchmarks (BabyVision 89.6 vs Opus-5's 94.1).
- Base-model regressions vs its own predecessors on MGSM (80.2 vs 85.7), MATH (61.1 vs 64.5 vs V4-Pro), and LongBench-V2 (45.2 vs V4-Pro's 51.5) suggest the extreme cache compression trades away some multilingual and long-context-reasoning headroom.
- The 196B Engram parameters sit outside the "552B backbone" headline, so total parameter count is ~748B; memory footprint claims focus on KV cache, not total weights.
- Multi-agent results are preliminary, self-comparative (no external multi-agent baselines), and obtained with DeepSeek's own Agent Team harness; benchmark contamination and reward-hacking concerns in agent evaluations are acknowledged but only partially mitigated.

## Relevance

这篇报告是长上下文高效推理方向的标志性工作：CSA2 跨层 KV 复用、FP4 KV 缓存与 SWA Bounded Replay 直接回应了 agent 时代 HBM/SSD 容量与带宽瓶颈，与稀疏注意力、KV 压缩和高效 LLM 推理部署的研究兴趣高度契合，同时给出了开源权重上 agentic 能力（DeepSWE、Terminal-Bench）的强基线数字。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How large is the exact quality cost of SWA Bounded Replay and FP4 main KV in isolation? A controlled perplexity/benchmark sweep varying replay length and cache precision would quantify the "negligible degradation" claim.
- Does the Hierarchical Sparse Indexer's 16,384-position candidate pool cap recall on needle-in-a-haystack or LongBench-V2-style tasks at near-1M contexts, and could a learned, query-adaptive pool size help?
- Can CSA2's static Full/Reindex/Reuse assignment be replaced by a dynamic or learned routing policy, and what would the accuracy-efficiency frontier look like?
- Why does MGSM regress (80.2 vs 85.7 for V4-Flash) — is it the data mixture (7:1 text-to-multimodal), the aggressive cache compression, or the MoE load-balancing changes?
- How well does the reasoning-effort conditioning (b = 1-100) transfer to unseen scaffolds and to latency-sensitive interactive settings, and does effort calibration drift after further fine-tuning?
- Since multi-agent gains concentrate at long wall-clock deadlines (8-20h), what coordination mechanisms (mailboxes, task boards) drive the improvement, and how do results change against external multi-agent baselines or with weaker lead models?
