> **一句话总结：** 在一个"奖赏互换"与"汇报者说谎"产生字节级相同历史的两按钮博弈中，给冻结 LLM 一行"已验证记录"（独立核实某一轮的真实结果）来打破对称：三个 70B 级模型几乎完美识破说谎的汇报者（Qwen 72B 达 1.000），却经常冤枉诚实的汇报者——Qwen 72B 在"什么都没变"时仍 38% 地咬定汇报者在说谎（"points moved" 时 58%），且这一误判率会随"记录核的是第几轮"（Qwen）或"哪个字母代表诚实"（Llama）这类与答案无关的表面特征大幅波动（0.24–0.35 / 0.21–0.32），呈现强烈的单侧使用证据的不对称。

> **TL;DR:** Built on Everitt et al. (2017)'s corrupted-reward-channel setting, this paper asks whether frozen LLMs (Qwen2.5-32B/72B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct) actually use a "verified record" — one independently-checked round printed beside the reporter's claim, the only possible tie-breaker between a payout swap and a lying scorekeeper in a two-option game where matched situations produce byte-identical logs. In 10,240 single-token prompts per model (64 worlds × 4 wordings × 5 prompt versions × 4 situations × 2 questions), models catch a lying reporter almost perfectly (0.934–1.000) but clear an honest one far less often: the "honest-reporter penalty" (lying-situation accuracy minus honest-situation accuracy) is +0.135 at Qwen 32B, +0.482 at Qwen 72B, +0.363 at Llama 70B. Even with nothing changed at all, Qwen 72B calls an honest reporter a liar 38% of the time (reading floor 1.000 with the answer printed), and the verdict swings with information-free features — which round the record names (Qwen: up to +0.348) or which letter means "honest" (Llama: up to +0.320) — while lying verdicts never move by more than 0.035. A pre-registered analysis discounts 3 of the 4 headline false-accusation rates; the 38% carries the claim.

## 核心贡献

- **把 RL 理论中的"腐化奖赏通道"诊断问题实例化为可精确测量的 LLM 实验**：构造两按钮博弈，"分数互换（汇报者诚实）"与"分数未动（汇报者开始说谎）"在日志上字节级相同（测试套件断言 matched pair 完全一致），从而任何仅看汇报的策略在均衡设计下只能得 0.5；唯一的信息源是一行"已验证记录"（某一轮真实结果的独立核查）。
- **发现并量化"诚实汇报者惩罚"（honest-reporter penalty）的不对称**：大模型几乎完美利用记录抓出说谎者（Qwen 72B 与 Llama 70B 在两种说谎情境下 0.996–1.000），却在诚实情境下大幅失准——Qwen 72B 惩罚达 +0.482；在"什么都没发生"的情境下 Qwen 72B 仍有 38% 概率指认诚实者为说谎者，而同一批 prompt 打印答案后其得分是 1.000，排除了"读不懂题目"的解释。
- **证明这种失败由与答案无关的表面特征驱动，且两个模型家族被不同特征撬动**：Qwen 由"记录核的是第 5 轮还是第 6 轮"驱动（round 效应 +0.238～+0.348），Llama 由"哪个字母代表诚实"驱动（letter 效应 +0.215～+0.320）；而说谎判定对这两个因素的敏感度均 ≤0.035。这把文献中已知的选项字母/位置偏差定位到"两个对称判断中的一个"。
- **预注册（pre-registration）式的严格分析纪律**：三条规则（天花板规则、0.90 阅读地板、翻转启发式对照）在跑实验前注册，并诚实报告规则歧义下哪些结论存活——按最严格的逐情境读法，四个 headline 数字中有三个被打折，只有"38%"承载核心主张。
- **复现并扩展"知行分离"（knowing-doing gap）**：在模型正确判定汇报者说谎的同一历史上，单独提问新选项哪个得分时，Qwen 模型 80% 以上仍照汇报原样选（fresh-pair 准确率仅 0.117–0.200），Llama 70B 也只有 0.369–0.439——诊断正确并不等于按诊断行动。

## Key Contributions

- **Instantiates the corrupted-reward-channel diagnosis problem (Everitt et al. 2017) as an exactly-measurable LLM experiment**: a two-option game where "payouts swapped, honest reporter" and "payouts unchanged, lying reporter" generate byte-identical logs (asserted by the test suite), so any report-only policy scores exactly 0.500 on the balanced design; the sole information source is a one-line verified record (an independent check of one round's real result, printed beside the reporter's claim).
- **Identifies and quantifies the honest-reporter penalty — a striking asymmetry in using evidence**: large models use the verified record almost perfectly to convict a lying reporter (0.934–1.000 across lying situations) but fail to clear an honest one (penalty of +0.135 / +0.482 / +0.363 at Qwen 32B / Qwen 72B / Llama 70B). In the "nothing moved, honest" situation Qwen 72B accuses an honest reporter 38% of the time even though its answer-printed score there is 1.000 — ruling out a reading failure.
- **Shows the failure is driven by information-free surface features, differently per model family**: for Qwen models the lever is which round the record names (round effect up to +0.348, i.e., a round-6 record costs 0.24–0.35 accuracy); for Llama it is which answer letter means "honest" (letter effect up to +0.320). Lying verdicts move by at most 0.035 on the same factors — lie detection is a fixed computation, honesty clearance is not.
- **Pre-registered analysis discipline with honest discounting**: three rules (ceiling rule, 0.90 reading floor, flip-heuristic control) were fixed before the runs; the paper reports both row-level and per-situation readings and concedes that under the strict reading 3 of the 4 headline false-accusation numbers are discounted (two for answer-printed scores below the bar, one because the record lowered an already-answer-printed score), leaving the 38% as the claim-carrier.
- **Reproduces the knowing-doing gap (Schmied et al. 2025) in a reward-channel setting and extends it**: on histories where the model correctly said "the source reports the opposite of every result," a separate prompt asking which fresh option pays is answered by following the source's report over 80% of the time by Qwen models (fresh-pair accuracy 0.117–0.200) and 56–63% by Llama 70B — the diagnosis is not acted on.

## Methodology

The paper constructs a minimal two-option bandit game with a reporting "source." For rounds 1–4 reports are accurate; after round 4 two hidden switches may flip — the two options' point values may have swapped, and the source may have started reporting the opposite of every result (and never stops). Crossing the switches gives four situations forming two matched pairs whose visible logs are byte-identical (verified by the generator's test suite), making the verified record — one line stating a round's actual result next to what the source said — logically necessary and sufficient as a tie-breaker.

Each prompt ends with one question ("has the source been reporting accurately or the opposite since round 5? Reply K or Q"). The authors read the model's next-token distribution restricted to the two answer letters and take the larger — no sampling, no chain of thought, no temperature — deliberately measuring the single-step verdict a policy would act on. Five prompt versions per history serve as controls: **with record** (the measurement), **no record** (measures the default), **answer printed** (the reading floor), **answer printed plus record** (the ceiling; with-record above it voids the run), and **salience** (a true rules line stating the two causes are equally common). A fresh pair of options (C, D) in rounds 7–8, never touched by the record, enables a second "fresh-pair question" whose correct answer requires routing through the source's honesty verdict.

The design space is 64 worlds × 4 wordings (terse log, meeting minutes, narrated prose, sports commentary) × 5 versions × 4 situations × 2 questions = 10,240 prompts per model, on Qwen2.5-32B/72B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B/70B-Instruct (16-bit, transformers 5.16.1; total compute cost $13.19). All intervals are bootstrapped over worlds, not prompts (wordings and questions share a history). Three analysis rules were registered before the runs, including a flip-heuristic control (if the model says "lying" ≥0.25 more often when reports flipped than when they did not, with-record numbers are read against the heuristic — this fired for every large model). A confound in the original 64 worlds (record-round and honest-letter both keyed to world-index parity) was caught before writing and fixed by regenerating reversed worlds, giving a full round×letter cross of 128 prompts per combination.

## Results & Highlights

- **Lying reporters are caught almost perfectly (with record, source question)**: Qwen 32B scores 0.934 / 0.938 in the two lying situations, Qwen 72B 1.000 / 1.000, Llama 70B 0.996 / 0.996. At the 70B class this survives all four wordings (Qwen 72B 1.000 everywhere; Llama ≥0.984); the one exception is Qwen 32B on narrated prose (0.734 / 0.750).
- **The honest-reporter penalty is large**: paired over worlds, lying-minus-honest accuracy is +0.135 [+0.098, +0.174] at Qwen 32B, +0.482 [+0.406, +0.557] at Qwen 72B, +0.363 [+0.287, +0.443] at Llama 70B. In "nothing moved, honest" (nothing happened at all), Qwen 72B calls the honest reporter a liar 38% of the time (with-record accuracy 0.617 vs answer-printed 1.000) and Llama 70B 26% (0.742 vs 0.961); in "points moved, honest" 58% (0.418) and 48% (0.523) respectively — though the strict per-situation reading of the registered rules discounts the two points-moved numbers (answer-printed 0.887 / 0.742 below the 0.90 bar) and Llama's nothing-changed number (record lowered an already-printed score).
- **It is inference, not reading**: in nothing-moved-honest the answer-printed score is 0.96–1.00 at both rounds for all three models, yet the with-record score falls by 0.22–0.44 when the record checks round 6 instead of round 5 (e.g., Qwen 72B 0.836 → 0.398); every bootstrap interval on the gap-change excludes zero.
- **Families are moved by different irrelevant features**: round effect (r5 minus r6) is +0.238 to +0.348 for the two Qwen models (letter effect within noise / ≤+0.133), while for Llama the letter effect is +0.215 to +0.320 (round effect within noise in one situation). The pre-registered prediction for the 58% number was 35% — the failure was larger than expected.
- **An agreeing record raises suspicion anyway**: adding the record to a prompt that already states the answer changes nothing at Qwen 32B (+0.004), costs a little at Qwen 72B (+0.078), and costs Llama a quarter of its honest verdicts (+0.254 [+0.176, +0.340]; 0.961 → 0.707) — told the source is honest and shown a matching record, Llama still says "lying" 29% of the time. In lying situations this difference is within 0.012 of zero for every model.
- **Salience intervention does nothing**: stating that the two causes are equally common moves accuracy by −0.034 to +0.027, all below the registered +0.05 threshold for "the world-change story is not being entertained."
- **Knowing is not acting**: on histories where the model correctly convicted the source, fresh-pair accuracy is only 0.117/0.200 (Qwen 32B), 0.145/0.180 (Qwen 72B), 0.369/0.439 (Llama 70B) — versus answer-printed scores of 0.987–0.994 on that question — meaning the models follow the lying report 56–89% of the time.
- **Flip-heuristic control**: with-record answers agree with the "reports flipped ⇒ lying" rule only 0.521–0.555 of the time while scoring 0.759–0.868 overall, so the results are not the heuristic in disguise. Llama-3.1-8B fails the reading floor (0.620 answer-printed) and none of its numbers are read.

## Authors & Credibility

Single author, Arman Nik Khah, at The University of Texas at Dallas. The work is unusually transparent by empirical-LLM-paper standards: three analysis rules were registered before any model saw the 64-world prompts, the central prediction (0.65 for Qwen 72B points-moved-honest) was written down and shown wrong (actual 0.418), a design confound (world-index parity linking record-round to honest-letter) was discovered and repaired with a regenerated reversed-worlds set, and Section 5 explicitly enumerates which cells survive strict vs lenient readings of the ambiguous registered rules. Every figure is generated by code from scored outputs (paper/NUMBERS.md), and code, prompts, answer keys, and all scored outputs are released at https://github.com/IamArmanNikkhah/easy-to-catch-a-liar. The whole study cost $13.19 in rented compute. The paper engages the relevant theory (Everitt et al. 2017's corrupt-reward MDP and its impossibility result) and related empirical literatures (multiple-choice bias, inverse scaling, knowing-doing gaps) accurately.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: genuinely novel framing — the paper is, to my knowledge, the first to place an LM inside the corrupted-reward setting and hand it exactly the richer data Everitt's remedy prescribes, as a single verified record. The asymmetric-use finding (evidence is used to convict but not to acquit) is a clean, non-obvious contribution beyond cataloging another MC-option bias.
- **Rigor**: very high for its scale. Byte-identical matched pairs (asserted by tests), bootstrap over worlds rather than prompts, a flip-heuristic control that actually fired, a 256-prompt GPU bridge check, pre-registration with honest post-hoc accounting. Weaknesses are acknowledged rather than hidden: the two points-moved-honest cells sit under answer-printed scores of 0.887 (Qwen 72B) and 0.742 (Llama 70B) — below the registered 0.90 bar — and Llama's honest cells fail strict per-situation readings on both rules, so the Llama penalty "rests on the row-level reading and on nothing else."
- **Reproducibility**: excellent — released code/prompts/outputs, generated numbers, $13.19 compute, standard open-weight models. The one caveat is a single seed of 64 worlds; no seed variance is reported.
- **Significance**: the practical stakes are stated carefully and correctly: a verification step in front of an RL agent will catch a broken reward reporter but will also, at rates that depend on nothing relevant, convict an honest one — after which an agent that acted on the verdict would invert a truthful channel. The mitigating observation is that these models mostly do not act on their verdicts at all (the knowing-doing gap cuts both ways).
- **Red flags / limitations**: three large models from two families is thin for any family- or scale-level claim (the paper itself declines a scaling claim after a non-monotone 4-point Qwen ladder on an earlier prompt set); the game is a tiny 8-round two-option synthetic setting, so external validity to real reward channels (RLHF raters, sensor drift) is untested; the one-token no-CoT protocol measures the single-step read by design, but a model allowed to reason aloud might clear honest reporters far more often, bounding the practical relevance; the salience null carries a self-named confound (the added line itself mentions "began lying"); and no mechanism is offered for why the round moves Qwen while the letter moves Llama — the paper reports the margins and stops. The headline 58%/48% numbers being discounted by the paper's own registered rules means casual readers of the abstract may over-weight them (though the abstract says so explicitly).

## Strengths

- The matched-pair construction is airtight: because the two hypotheses render byte-identical logs, any observed asymmetry is attributable to how the model uses the record, not to wording leakage — a level of control rare in LLM-evaluation papers.
- The honest-reporter penalty is a crisp, memorable, and consequential finding: the same evidence that reliably convicts fails to acquit, and the failure is modulated by features carrying zero information about the answer.
- Exceptional methodological hygiene: pre-registration with wrong predictions reported, a caught-and-fixed confound, controls (reading floor, ceiling, flip heuristic) that each did real work, and per-situation answer-printed numbers printed beside every with-record number.
- The knowing-doing extension (fresh-pair question routed through the honesty verdict) is a clever design that turns a diagnosis into a potential action and shows the models don't take it.

## Limitations

- Only three usable models from two families, one seed of 64 worlds, and no mechanism for the round/letter effects; the paper makes no scale claim and offers none.
- The four wordings are the authors'; since letter/position effects show wording matters, a fifth wording could move the honest numbers again (though nothing tried moved the lying ones at 70B).
- Single-token protocol excludes reasoning; a chain-of-thought model might behave differently, and the paper flags this as a different question.
- The salience intervention's null is confounded by the intervention text naming both causes.
- Synthetic 8-round two-option game: no test of transfer to realistic corrupted-reward scenarios (e.g., RLHF reward-model drift), and no agentic deployment where a wrong conviction would actually invert a channel.

## Relevance

直接命中"奖励模型/奖励通道可靠性 + LLM 作为策略核心"的兴趣：它把 reward hacking / corrupt reward 的理论补救（更丰富的通道数据）交给冻结 LLM 检验，发现证据使用是单侧的——抓作弊可靠、还清白失灵，且被无关表面特征撬动。这对任何想在 RL/agent 流水线里加"验证步骤"来防奖励腐化的设计都是直接的警示数据，也提示 verifier-asymmetry 值得在更大规模模型与真实奖励通道上跟进。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the asymmetry persist with chain-of-thought or larger/deep-reasoning models (e.g., 100B+ or o-series-style models), or is honest-reporter clearance a capability that emerges with scale — the inverse-scaling shape in Table 2 hints the opposite?
- What mechanistic explanation underlies the family-specific levers (record round for Qwen, answer letter for Llama) — is it attention to recency/position in the log vs token-identity priors at the answer site, and can activation patching localize it?
- Would a verifier whose decision is fed into an acting agent (closing the loop of Section 4.8) cause the predicted "invert a truthful channel" failure in a real multi-step RL/agent setting, and at what cost?
- Do RLHF-style reward models — not just prompted LLMs — show the same asymmetric use of inter-rater audit records when judging reward reports?
- Can training on verified-record diagnoses (with balanced honest/lying labels) repair the acquittal deficit without degrading conviction, or is the asymmetry a structural property of autoregressive single-step reads?
