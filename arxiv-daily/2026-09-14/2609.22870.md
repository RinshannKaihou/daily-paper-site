> **一句话总结：** 本文发现全流程 FP8 RL 训练中，FP8 量化噪声在重要性比率中复合放大、导致负优势 token 被系统性过度裁剪（梯度被错误置零），从而引发训练中期熵激增与乱码输出；提出的 Calibrated Clipping 通过周期性地用 BF16 参考分布匹配下界裁剪分位数并重平衡上界，在 GRPO/DAPO、8B–32B 模型上消除了熵激增，blockwise FP8 性能恢复甚至超过 BF16 基线（8B 上 58.6 vs 57.6），同时张量级 FP8 训练吞吐最高达 BF16 的 1.5 倍。

> **TL;DR:** Full-pipeline FP8 RL (both rollout and training quantized) hits catastrophic mid-training entropy surges: on Qwen3-8B GRPO over DeepScaleR, vanilla FP8 scores only 46.1–54.1 average across 8 math benchmarks versus 57.6 for BF16, because compounded FP8 noise distorts the importance ratio (NMAE amplified 1.7–2.9×) and over-clips negative-advantage tokens, zeroing the gradients that should suppress garbled outputs. The proposed Calibrated Clipping periodically realigns the lower clipping bound to the BF16 clipping quantile and solves for an upper bound that matches the BF16 positive/negative update ratio; it eliminates entropy surges across GRPO/DAPO at 8B–32B, lifts blockwise FP8 to 58.6 (above the 57.6 BF16 baseline, +4.5 over vanilla FP8), and restores DAPO Qwen3-14B AIME24 from 35.7–41.6 up to 46.5–47.9 (BF16: 50.9), while tensorwise FP8 training throughput reaches up to 1.5× BF16.

## 核心贡献

- **识别出全流程 FP8 RL 的一种此前被忽视的失效模式**：FP8 量化噪声在重要性比率 r_t(θ) 中复合放大（NMAE 放大 1.7–2.9 倍），使 PPO 式裁剪机制被系统性误触发（over-clipping），尤其在下界处把大量负优势 token 错误推出信任域、梯度被提前置零，导致乱码输出得不到惩罚并不断累积，最终引发训练中期熵激增甚至崩溃。
- **提出 Calibrated Clipping**：一种动态校准裁剪边界的轻量方法——用 BF16 高精度参考分布匹配 FP8 域中负优势 token 的下界裁剪分位数（下界对齐），再通过求解正/负更新比率 ρ 与 BF16 参考一致来重定上界（上界再平衡），从而在量化空间中恢复信任域的原有语义。
- **开销可摊销的周期性校准机制**：利用校准边界在稳定训练中变化平缓的特性，每 20 步仅做两次 BF16 前向 shadow pass 即可重校准，配合带平滑约束的网格搜索（算法完全形式化于 Algorithm 1），避免每步计算抵消 FP8 收益。
- **广泛的实验验证**：覆盖 GRPO 与 DAPO 两种算法、Qwen3-8B/14B 与 Qwen2.5-32B 模型、tensorwise/rowwise/blockwise 三种 FP8 缩放粒度、数学（DeepScaleR、DAPO-Math-17K）与代码（Eurus）两类任务，证明方法一致消除熵激增并恢复接近 BF16 的性能，同时保留最高 1.5× 的训练吞吐提升。

## Key Contributions

- Identify a previously overlooked failure mode of full-pipeline FP8 RL: quantization noise compounds in the importance ratio (amplifying NMAE by 1.7–2.9× over single-probability error), systematically over-clipping negative-advantage tokens at the lower bound, zeroing their gradients so pathological garbled outputs are never penalized and proliferate into mid-training entropy surges.
- Propose Calibrated Clipping, which restores trust-region semantics in quantized space via two stages: (1) Lower-Bound Alignment — match the FP8 clipping quantile of negative-advantage tokens to the BF16 reference quantile α_low = F_BF16,−(c_low^ref); (2) Upper-Bound Rebalancing — search for an upper bound H so the positive-to-negative update ratio ρ equals the BF16 reference ratio, preventing the over-penalization that a naive relaxed lower bound (clip 0.8 → 0.6) causes.
- An amortized calibration scheme: two forward-only BF16 shadow passes every 20 steps (bounds evolve smoothly during stable training), a grid search over L ∈ [0.5, 0.9] and H ∈ [1.2, 2.0] with step 0.02, and smoothed updates (δL = 0.05, δH = 0.1) — negligible overhead relative to per-step calibration.
- Comprehensive evidence: GRPO and DAPO, Qwen3-8B/14B and Qwen2.5-32B, three FP8 scaling granularities, math (DeepScaleR, DAPO-Math-17K) and coding (Eurus) RL — consistently eliminating entropy surges, restoring BF16-level scores, with up to 1.5× BF16 training throughput, plus negative results showing CISPO collapses at ~300 steps and BAPO at ~90 steps under the same FP8 setting.

## Methodology

The paper proceeds as diagnosis → controlled verification → principled fix.

**Diagnosis (Section 3).** They train Qwen3-8B-Base with GRPO on DeepScaleR (16K context, TIS enabled by default) across four backends: BF16, and full-pipeline FP8 with tensorwise / rowwise / blockwise scaling. BF16 and FP8-rollout+BF16-train stay stable, but all FP8 training backends exhibit abrupt entropy surges after ~100 steps (milder with finer scaling). Decomposing response entropy into bins ([0,0.2), [0.2,0.5), [0.5,1.0), ≥1.0) shows the surge is driven by a small, rapidly proliferating set of high-entropy responses containing garbled/fragmented tokens; only 6.64% of these carry positive advantage, so ~93% should be suppressed — yet they keep growing. Using BF16 shadow passes, they measure that the ratio operation compounds FP8 probability error by 1.7–2.9× (NMAE), inflating the PPO clipping fraction to 6.68× / 5.48× / 3.94× the BF16 level (tensorwise/rowwise/blockwise). They define *over-clipping* (FP8 ratio crosses the bound while BF16 does not) and show ~70% of it occurs at the lower bound in later training, with ~90% of those tokens originating from the high-entropy responses — the failure loop: garbled output → negative advantage → compounded FP8 noise pushes r_t below 1−ε → gradient zeroed → behavior never unlearned.

**Controlled verification.** Relaxing the lower clip bound from 0.8 to 0.6 in rowwise FP8 prevents the surge but causes premature entropy saturation, lower rewards, and shorter responses — proving naive relaxation is insufficient and motivating a rebalanced trust region.

**Calibrated Clipping (Section 4, Algorithm 1).** Ideally bounds should satisfy L = Q̃(1−ε), H = Q̃(1+ε) where Q̃ is the intractable FP8 noise operator; instead they (1) set the target quantile from the BF16 CDF over negative-advantage tokens and invert the FP8 CDF to get L; (2) compute the BF16 positive/negative update ratio ρ (Eq. 5, including TIS weights) and grid-search H so ρ_FP8(L, H) matches it. Bounds are updated every 20 steps using two BF16 forward-only shadow passes (current + old policy on the same batch), with smoothing constraints preventing abrupt shifts; reference bounds c_low^ref = 0.8, c_high^ref = 1.24 (GRPO) or 1.28 (DAPO). Initialization [0.6, 1.8].

**Evaluation stack.** VeRL + vLLM + TorchAO with the FlashRL FP8-rollout patch; GRPO on Qwen3-8B-Base / Qwen2.5-32B (DeepScaleR, 500 steps, 8 benchmarks: AIME24/25, AMC23/24, MATH-500, Gaokao, Minerva Math, OlympiadBench) and DAPO on Qwen3-14B-Base (DAPO-Math-17K, 200 steps, AIME24 Avg@32); throughput measured in an offline TorchAO benchmark at 4K/8K/16K sequence lengths; appendix ablations on recalibration interval, initialization, CISPO, BAPO, and the Eurus coding suite (TACO/APPS/Codeforces).

## Results & Highlights

- **GRPO, Qwen3-8B-Base, DeepScaleR (8-benchmark average):** vanilla full-pipeline FP8 collapses to 46.1 / 47.0 / 54.1 (tensorwise / rowwise / blockwise) vs the BF16 baseline's 57.6 — drops of 11.5 / 10.6 / 3.5 points. Calibrated Clipping restores 55.9 / 56.5 / 58.6, i.e. +9.8 / +9.5 / +4.5 over vanilla FP8, with blockwise FP8 *exceeding* BF16 by +1.0 (58.6 vs 57.6) and beating even FP8-rollout+BF16-train (58.2).
- **GRPO, Qwen2.5-32B (8-benchmark average):** Calibrated Clipping with blockwise FP8 reaches 53.4 vs 51.9 for full BF16 (+1.5) and 50.8 for vanilla blockwise FP8 (+2.6); entropy surges are milder at 32B (shorter responses), yet gains remain consistent (+2.0 to +2.7 over vanilla across granularities).
- **DAPO, Qwen3-14B-Base, AIME24 Avg@32:** vanilla FP8 scores 35.7 / 38.1 / 41.6 (tensorwise / rowwise / blockwise) vs 50.9 for BF16; Calibrated Clipping recovers to 47.9 / 46.5 / 47.4 — gains of +12.2 / +8.4 / +5.8 points over vanilla FP8, with tensorwise CC (47.9) even slightly above BF16-train + FP8-rollout (47.4), though still 3.0–4.4 below full BF16.
- **Diagnosis quantified:** the importance-ratio operation amplifies FP8 probability NMAE by 1.7×–2.9×; PPO clipping fraction runs at 6.68× / 5.48× / 3.94× the BF16 level for tensorwise / rowwise / blockwise FP8; ~70% of over-clipping occurs at the lower bound and ~90% of lower-bound over-clipped tokens come from high-entropy (garbled) responses, of which only 6.64% receive positive advantage.
- **Throughput (offline TorchAO training-phase benchmark, 8B/14B/32B at 4K/8K/16K):** tensorwise FP8 reaches up to 1.5× BF16 training throughput; blockwise still yields 10–20% improvement — which, combined with the ~30% rollout speedup from FP8 inference reported by FlashRL, compounds into substantial full-pipeline gains.
- **Competing clipping fixes fail under FP8:** CISPO (retaining clipped-token gradients) collapses after ~300 steps of rowwise-FP8 training; BAPO (adaptive bounds from a fixed positive-contribution target) destabilizes after ~90 steps, its bounds drifting to [0.9, 3.0] with reward collapsing to zero — neither addresses precision-induced ratio distortion.
- **Robustness ablations (Qwen3-8B rowwise FP8):** recalibration intervals of 10 / 20 / 40 steps give 58.31 / 56.51 / 57.91 average scores, and initialization [0.6, 1.8] vs [0.8, 1.2] gives 56.51 vs 56.20 — all configurations substantially recover over vanilla rowwise FP8 (47.0). On the Eurus coding suite, CC lifts average score from 45.77–46.38 (vanilla FP8) to 47.88–48.75 (BF16: 49.61).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine and well-scoped. Prior FP8-RL work (TIS/MIS, QuRL, FlashRL, Jet-RL, Unified FP8) addressed the train–rollout precision mismatch; this paper is the first to isolate *compounded quantization noise in the importance ratio* as a distinct failure mode that corrupts PPO-style clipping itself, and the quantile-matching fix is principled rather than ad hoc. The related-work comparison against CISPO and BAPO (both of which collapse in this FP8 setting, Appendix A.2/A.3) strengthens the claim that a high-precision-referenced calibration — not just any adaptive clipping — is what matters.
- **Rigor:** The diagnostic chain is unusually careful: BF16 shadow passes to measure NMAE amplification (1.7–2.9×), entropy-bin decomposition locating the surge in a small garbled subset, over-clipping attribution (~70% lower-bound, ~90% from high-entropy responses), a controlled lower-bound-relaxation probe, and ablations over recalibration interval and initialization. Experiments span 2 algorithms, 3 scales, 3 granularities, and 2 task domains.
- **Reproducibility:** High on paper — public models, datasets, and framework components, with a fully specified algorithm. But no code release is mentioned, and the diagnosis depends on instrumented shadow passes that would need reimplementation.
- **Significance caveats / red flags:** (1) The headline "up to 1.5×" throughput comes from an offline TorchAO benchmark that *explicitly excludes* the periodic BF16 reference passes (Figure 7 caption) — end-to-end gains with calibration overhead included are never reported. (2) "Restores BF16-level performance" is only fully true for blockwise GRPO; tensorwise/rowwise remain 1.1–1.7 points below BF16 on 8B, and all DAPO configurations stay 3.0–4.4 points below the full-BF16 AIME24 score (47.9 vs 50.9). (3) Checkpoint selection reports "the checkpoint that achieves the best average score" over every-25-step evaluations — a mild best-checkpoint cherry-pick that applies to all arms but flatters unstable runs. (4) Benchmark protocols are thin: Avg@16 for AIME and Avg@4 for other suites means ~30–60 samples per number, so ±1–2-point differences (e.g., blockwise beating BF16 by +1.0) are within noise. (5) Scope is limited to dense Qwen models at ≤32B and ≤20K context; no MoE, no agentic RL, and the method's central knob (reference upper bound c_high^ref = 1.24/1.28) is a new per-task hyperparameter the authors say was "not explicitly tuned."

## Strengths

- Pins a vague symptom ("FP8 RL is unstable") to a precise, measurable mechanism — ratio-compounded quantization noise causing lower-bound over-clipping of negative-advantage tokens — with a quantified failure loop, then fixes exactly that mechanism.
- The fix is cheap and surgical: two forward-only BF16 passes every 20 steps plus a grid search, with no change to the model, the quantization scheme, or the RL objective; hyperparameters show low sensitivity.
- Broad, honest coverage: three scaling granularities spanning the efficiency–fidelity trade-off, two RL algorithms (GRPO and DAPO whose entropy dynamics differ), three model scales, math and coding tasks, plus appendix negatives (CISPO, BAPO) rather than only flattering baselines.
- Also diagnoses *why* the naive fix fails: relaxing the lower bound alone trades entropy surge for over-penalization (premature entropy saturation, lower reward, shorter responses), motivating the two-sided calibration.

## Limitations

- The 1.5× throughput figure excludes the periodic BF16 shadow-pass overhead; no end-to-end (rollout + training + calibration) wall-clock comparison against BF16 is provided.
- Recovery is incomplete for coarser granularities and for DAPO: tensorwise/rowwise GRPO stay ~1–2 points under BF16 at 8B, and DAPO AIME24 remains 3.0–4.4 points below BF16 (47.9/46.5/47.4 vs 50.9).
- Only dense Qwen models (8B–32B) and math/coding RLVR tasks are tested; MoE models, longer contexts (>20K), agentic workloads, and other RL algorithms (e.g., GSPO-style sequence-level clipping, which the authors flag as future work) are unaddressed.
- The calibrated upper bound requires choosing reference bounds (c_low^ref = 0.8, c_high^ref = 1.24/1.28) that encode task priors — effectively a new hyperparameter pair, even if reported as untuned.
- Small evaluation samples (Avg@16 on AIME, Avg@4 elsewhere) and best-checkpoint reporting mean point-level comparisons near parity (±1–2 points) should be read cautiously.

## Relevance

与低精度训练/RL 训练效率方向高度相关：论文揭示了 FP8 全流程 RL 中量化噪声与 PPO 裁剪机制的耦合失效机制并提出轻量修复，对搭建高效 GRPO/DAPO 训练管线（选缩放粒度、设裁剪边界、评估 FP8 稳定性）有直接的工程参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- What is the true end-to-end speedup (FP8 rollout + FP8 training + BF16 calibration passes + evaluation) versus a pure BF16 pipeline, and how does it scale with model size and context length?
- Does the quantile-matching calibration transfer to sequence-level trust regions (GSPO) or batch-moment methods (M2PO), where token-level ratio noise is aggregated rather than clipped per-token?
- Can the BF16 shadow passes be replaced by cheaper references — e.g., a learned model of Q̃, stochastic subsampling of tokens, or calibration reuse across steps — to cut the residual high-precision overhead further?
- Does the same over-clipping failure mode appear in other low-precision regimes (FP4 with block scaling, INT8) or in pretraining-style clipped objectives, and does quantile calibration generalize?
- How does Calibrated Clipping interact with MoE models (as targeted by Unified FP8), where expert routing adds another noise source to token probabilities, and with KL-regularized variants (the paper removes the KL term in all experiments)?
