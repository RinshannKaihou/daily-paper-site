> **一句话总结：** 通过在 7 类嵌入式/车规硬件上固定同一 ONNX 模型与量化 scale 的受控测量，本文证明 INT8 并不可移植——加速方向由 CPU 的点积指令集决定（有 dotprod/VNNI 快 1.83–2.11×，没有反而慢 1.65–1.76×）；不同整数内核下 INT8 的 top-1 预测在每 1000 张 ImageNet 输入上有 35–42 张不一致（而 FP32 全部逐位一致）；厂商 NPU 会静默丢弃自带 QDQ scale（top-1 从 0.75 崩到 0.005）或直接拒绝编译；边缘 NPU 的延迟瓶颈由输出张量的 D2H 传输量而非算力决定。

> **TL;DR:** A single-author measurement study from KATECH stress-tests the "quantize once, deploy anywhere" folklore across seven hardware classes (ARM/x86 CPUs, RTX 3080, Jetson AGX Orin iGPU+NVDLA, Qualcomm Hexagon HTP, DEEPX DX-M1) with the ONNX artifact and QDQ scales held fixed. On the identical ResNet-50 and ONNX Runtime/MLAS path, INT8 vs FP32 is 1.83×–2.11× faster on cores with dot-product ISAs but 1.65×–1.76× slower on the Core i9-10900K (no AVX-512 VNNI) and Cortex-A53 (no dotprod). On a 1000-image ImageNet bundle, INT8 top-1 predictions agree 1000/1000 only when two targets share the integer kernel and 958–965/1000 otherwise (FP32 is bit-identical 1000/1000 everywhere) — invisible to top-1 accuracy (0.7620 vs 0.7500). DETR-ResNet-50 INT8 on COCO val2017 collapses from mAP 0.4207 to 0.2402 (−42.9%); Qualcomm HTP silently ignores external scales (0.75 → 0.005 while compiling and running without error); and on the DX-M1, a detector with a 5.48 MB output runs 26.3× slower than ResNet-50 despite the lightest compute, because PCIe D2H transfer size, not TOPS, sets the latency regime. Essential reading for anyone deploying quantized models to heterogeneous edge/automotive fleets.

## 核心贡献

- **受控的跨平台测量方法论**：在 7 类硬件（树莓派 5 / Jetson AGX Orin / i.MX8M-Nano / Core i9 / RTX 3080 / Hexagon HTP / DEEPX DX-M1）上固定同一个 ONNX 模型文件和内嵌 QDQ 量化 scale，使整数内核/ISA 成为唯一自由变量，并公开脚本与 32 份测量报告（附录 A 提供逐条 claim 到 artifact 的映射）。
- **C1（速度符号由 ISA 决定）**：同一 ResNet-50 INT8 模型 + 同一 ONNX Runtime MLAS 路径，带 ARM dotprod 的核心快 1.83×/2.11×，不带的（i9 无 VNNI、Cortex-A53）反而慢 1.76×/1.65× —— INT8 不是"更快"，是"当且仅当目标有正确的累加指令时更快"。
- **C2（INT8 数值不可移植，头号贡献）**：FP32 预测在所有平台对上逐位一致（1000/1000）；INT8 预测仅在两目标共享同一整数内核时 1000/1000 一致，否则 958–965/1000 不一致 —— 与硬件边界在 CPU↔CPU 还是 CPU↔加速器无关，是"不变量"而非"梯度"，且 top-1 精度完全看不出这些翻转。
- **C3（厂商拥有量化权）**：自带 QDQ 图在 Qualcomm HTP 上被静默忽略（top-1 0.75→0.005，编译/推理全程无报错），在 DEEPX 上被大声拒绝（编译错误、不产出引擎）；只有厂商原生量化路径正确且更快（748 µs vs 1052 µs）。
- **C4（输出大小决定瓶颈状态）**：在同一 PCIe 边缘 NPU 上，固定算力、只扫输出大小 1020×，测出 compute-bound → D2H-bound 的完整过渡曲线；过渡带宽度 = 核数 N（3.36× on 3 核），给出"N 核加速器停止扩展的输出阈值 ≈ (compute-time × link-bandwidth)/N"的闭式规则。
- **实践结论**：对安全相关/冗余车载计算，两个异构单元跑同一 INT8 模型不能假设逐输入一致（FP32 可以、INT8 不行）；陈（Chen）单 GPU 场景的 2 的幂 scale 缓解手段跨物理设备边界不成立（958→869/1000，反而更差）。

## Key Contributions

- A controlled same-artifact / same-scale cross-platform methodology that isolates the integer kernel/ISA as the only free variable, across seven hardware classes and three model families (classification, CNN detection, transformer detection), released with scripts and 32 HTML measurement reports plus a claim-to-artifact map.
- **C1 — Speedup sign is ISA-determined:** for the identical ResNet-50 INT8 QDQ model on the identical ONNX Runtime (MLAS) CPU path, cores with a dot-product instruction gain up to 2.11× while cores without one regress by up to 1.76× — INT8 deployment on a heterogeneous CPU fleet cannot assume a win.
- **C2 (headline) — INT8 outputs are not numerically portable:** FP32 top-1 predictions are bit-identical across every measured pair (1000/1000), while INT8 predictions agree 1000/1000 exactly when two targets share an integer kernel and only 958–965/1000 when they do not — independent of whether the boundary is CPU↔CPU or CPU↔accelerator, and invisible to top-1 accuracy. A CPU↔vendor-NPU pair (939/1000) is reported as a deployment observation since scales cannot be held fixed.
- **C3 — Vendor NPUs own quantization:** a bring-your-own QDQ graph fails silently on Qualcomm Hexagon HTP (external scales ignored, top-1 0.75 → 0.005 while it compiles, profiles, and runs) and loudly on DEEPX (compiler refuses the graph); only the vendor-native path yields a correct — and faster — engine.
- **C4 — Edge-NPU latency regimes are set by output/D2H transfer size, not compute:** on one PCIe-attached NPU a 4 KB-output classifier scales 2.19× across cores while a 5.48 MB-output detector does not scale at all; a fixed-compute 1020× output sweep traces the full transition, showing the boundary is a band whose width equals the core count, yielding a closed-form provisioning threshold.
- **Negative result with a mechanism probe:** forcing all Q/DQ scales to powers of two — the mitigation shown to restore bit-identity between kernels on a single GPU in concurrent LLM work — does not transfer across the x86↔A76 boundary (agreement drops 958 → 869/1000) because MLAS kernels do not implement M = 2^k as a shared exact shift; grid coarsening instead amplifies epilogue divergence (|Δlogit| ×2.35–×4.34).

## Methodology

The study's core device is the **controlled-comparison principle** (§3.3): any cross-target comparison fixes both the model artifact (e.g., `resnet50_int8_qdq.onnx`) and its embedded QDQ scales, so the only remaining variable is the target's integer kernel/ISA. The same ONNX file feeds both CPU execution providers and the TensorRT INT8 engine build, guaranteeing bit-identical scales across the CPU↔GPU boundary. When a target structurally cannot accept external scales (vendor NPUs), the result is explicitly downgraded to a "deployment observation" rather than a kernel comparison.

**Hardware matrix (§3.1):** eight targets in seven classes — Raspberry Pi 5 (Cortex-A76), Jetson AGX Orin (Cortex-A78AE, iGPU, 2× NVDLA v2), i.MX8M-Nano (Cortex-A53), Core i9-10900K, RTX 3080, Qualcomm Hexagon HTP (QCS8550/SA8775P), DEEPX DX-M1 (M.2, PCIe Gen2×1). Every device is n=1 per class; claims are about representative devices, not ISA populations.

**Models & data (§3.2):** ResNet-18/50 (ImageNet-1k), DETR-ResNet-50 (COCO, transformer), YOLO26n / YOLOv5s (COCO, CNN), plus BEVFormer/BEVDet used only for latency characterization (init weights, no accuracy claims). Latencies are event-timed on accelerator paths and wall-clock on CPU (never mixed within a claim), batch size 1. Numerical agreement is the count of a fixed 1000-image bundle on which two targets produce the same top-1 prediction.

**Key experiments:** (i) four-CPU FP32↔INT8 latency comparison on one runtime (§4); (ii) pairwise agreement matrix with FP32 as bit-identical control (§5), plus a power-of-two scale-rewrite probe with 30 bit-identical repeat runs; (iii) BYO-QDQ vs vendor-native quantization on both NPUs (§6); (iv) three same-device models plus a synthetic fixed-compute / swept-output experiment isolating D2H size as the causal variable (§7); (v) DETR INT8 collapse ablations — half-network quantization, op-exclusion patterns, SmoothQuant fake-quant vs on-device, and a cross-vendor quantizer comparison (§8). §9 catalogs silent-failure pitfalls encountered (subset inflation, preprocessing dominance, SQNR non-predictiveness, silent EP fallbacks).

## Results & Highlights

- **ISA-determined speedup sign (Table 2, ResNet-50, ONNX Runtime MLAS, FP32→INT8 latency):** Cortex-A76 (Pi 5, dotprod) 144.95 → 79.08 ms = **1.83× faster**; Cortex-A78AE (Orin, dotprod) 38.47 → 18.22 ms = **2.11× faster**; Core i9-10900K (no AVX-512 VNNI) 9.28 → 16.34 ms = **1.76× slower**; Cortex-A53 (no dotprod) 680.20 → 1123.02 ms = **1.65× slower**. The determinant is the dot-product instruction, not the ISA family.
- **INT8 prediction non-portability (Table 3, ImageNet 1000-image bundle, same QDQ artifact):** FP32 agreement is **1000/1000 for every pair measured**; INT8 agreement is **1000/1000** for the one pair sharing the MLAS SDOT kernel (A78AE↔A76, across two SoCs, boards, and ORT versions), and **965/961/958 per 1000** for the three CPU↔CPU pairs with different kernels, **961/1000** for Jetson iGPU (TensorRT) ↔ CPU (MLAS) built from the same scales, and **939/1000** for the DEEPX NPU pair (scales not held fixed). The flips are net-neutral to accuracy: iGPU INT8 top-1 0.7620 vs CPU MLAS INT8 0.7500 on ImageNet.
- **Power-of-two mitigation fails across a physical boundary:** rewriting every Q/DQ scale of the ResNet-50 to a power of two drops x86↔Pi 5 agreement from **958/1000 to 869/1000** (ceil rounding) and **919/1000** (nearest), with per-element cross-kernel |Δlogit| growing **×2.35–×4.34** — the mitigation coarsens the requantization grid instead of removing epilogue divergence; 30 repeat runs are bit-identical (not noise).
- **Vendor NPU failures (§6, ResNet-50):** BYO-QDQ on Qualcomm Hexagon HTP compiles, profiles, and runs without error but collapses top-1 **0.75 → 0.005** (same ONNX is 0.753 on x86; HTP fp16 is 0.745); the native `submit_quantize_job` path recovers **0.735** and is faster (**748 µs vs 1052 µs**). DEEPX rejects the same graph with a hard `GraphStructureError` (106 isolated nodes); its native PTQ reaches top-1 **0.7660**.
- **Output-size regimes on one NPU (§7, DX-M1):** ResNet-50 (4 KB output) is compute-bound (compute 2.77 ms ≫ D2H 0.11 ms) and scales **2.19×** on 3 cores (1078.93 fps); YOLOv5s (5.48 MB output, lightest compute at 2.59 ms) is D2H-bound, scales 1.02×, and runs **26.3× slower than ResNet-50** (41.01 fps vs 1078.93 fps). The fixed-compute sweep (2.70 ms compute, output swept 3,920 B → 3,999,968 B) shows a **2.97–2.99× plateau below ≈63 KB**, a single-inference crossover at **≈1.05 MB**, and a transition band of **3.36× width = the 3 core count**; per-core job distribution migrates 33/33/33 → 98/2/0. In its favorable regime the NPU beats the host A76 by **×11.42 throughput** (YOLO26n 91.51 vs 8.01 fps) and **×29.29 perf/watt**.
- **Transformer INT8 collapse (§8, DETR-ResNet-50, COCO val2017 5,000 images):** FP32 mAP **0.4207 → INT8 0.2402 (−42.9%)**, cross-confirmed on Jetson (**0.4237 → 0.2383, −43.8%**), with small-object mAP down 77–85%. The two-way ablation shows backbone-only INT8 −36.9%, transformer-only −43.2%, both −42.9% (strongly sub-additive — damage is distributed); op-selection exclusions recover only +0.0036 mAP; SmoothQuant recovers **59.9% of the gap in fake-quant but only ~9% on-device**. DEEPX shows "no collapse" (0.4377 → 0.4385) only by auto-partitioning the graph and leaving the transformer on the host CPU in FP32 (23.99 MB per-frame handoff; 1036 ms end-to-end, 87.9% host-CPU FP32).
- **Measurement pitfalls quantified (§9):** a 1000-image ImageNet subset inflated top-1 by **+9.77 pp on average** (range +8.41 to +10.39 across eight configs) versus the full 50k val set and flipped 5 of 13 significance verdicts; preprocessing choice moved top-1 by −1.07 pp vs quantization's −0.12 pp (9× larger); per-layer SQNR had essentially no rank correlation with top-1 delta (Spearman ρ = −0.030 over 21 layers).

## Authors & Credibility

The paper is single-authored by Yuyeong Shin of the Korea Automotive Technology Institute (KATECH), an applied automotive-research institution — directly matching the paper's ISO 26262 / ASIL redundancy framing and its stated follow-on work on a heterogeneous multi-module automotive compute platform. The scholarship is unusually careful: concurrent work (Chen, arXiv:2608.13756 / 2609.00363) that localized the epilogue-rounding mechanism on a single GPU for LLMs is cited explicitly as concurrent prior art, with the cross-device vision/detection measurement claimed as orthogonal rather than as mechanism discovery. Credibility is strongly supported by the artifact: scripts and 32 HTML measurement reports are released (github.com/yyshin-katech/embedded-ai-quantization-guide), with an appendix mapping every numbered claim to the exact report and script that produced it. The threats-to-validity section (§10) is among the most candid in this genre — it volunteers the single-run latencies, subset accuracies, n=1 hardware, ORT version confounds (1.17.1/1.23.2/1.28.0), power-measurement boundaries, and the extrapolated link-specific thresholds, and the paper's own §9 quantifies the subset-inflation risk that bounds its accuracy claims.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The genuinely novel element is C2's deployment-level framing — per-input prediction agreement measured across *physical device boundaries* under an identical ONNX file with identical embedded QDQ scales, with FP32 bit-identity as a same-harness control, for vision/detection models. The underlying epilogue-rounding mechanism is honestly attributed to concurrent work (Chen), so this is a measurement/characterization contribution, not a mechanism or method contribution. C4's output-size regime with a band width equal to core count is a tidy, quotable systems result, though the third regime (host-CPU-bound via compiler partitioning) is largely a toolchain behavior rather than a hardware law.
- **Rigor:** Methodologically disciplined — the controlled-comparison principle, the FP32 control, the exclusion of the DEEPX pair from the invariance claim, and the 30-run bit-identical repetition all raise the bar over typical benchmarking papers. However, most latencies are single-run p50s without confidence intervals; accuracy/agreement numbers rest on 200–1000-image subsets (their own §9 shows +9.77 pp mean subset inflation and sign-flipped deltas, which they flag but cannot fully undo); and n=1 per hardware class means the ISA conclusions rest on one device per class — a different A76 stepping or i9 SKU could in principle shift magnitudes (though a 1.6–2.1× sign flip is comfortably outside noise). The ORT version confound (three versions across the four CPUs) is bounded by two clever controls but not eliminated.
- **Reproducibility:** Excellent by measurement-study standards — public repo, 32 reports, claim-to-artifact map, exact byte counts for sweep points. The main residual gaps are hardware availability (Jetson AGX Orin, QCS8550/SA8775P, DX-M1) and undocumented vendor-toolchain versions in the text itself.
- **Significance:** High for embedded/automotive practitioners: the "INT8 agreement is binary in the integer kernel" invariance directly undermines dual-compute redundancy assumptions, and the Qualcomm silent-failure mode (a shipping-grade model at 0.005 top-1 that compiles and profiles cleanly) is a concrete safety hazard. For the data-center/LLM audience the findings are confirmatory rather than surprising.
- **Red flags / weak spots:** The §8.3 cross-vendor quantizer comparison (ORT −36.9% vs DEEPX +0.2% under a "same nominal recipe") compares columns that differ in evaluation subset (5,000 vs 500 COCO images), input resolution (fixed vs dynamic), and runtimes — the authors acknowledge only within-column deltas are valid, but the 37.1-point attribution to the EMA calibrator remains a one-pair inference, not a controlled variable. The power-of-two negative result covers exactly one model, one boundary, and two rounding modes, and its mechanism attribution rests on indirect consistency checks rather than kernel introspection (§10 admits this). Vendor coverage is two NPUs — TI and Renesas automotive parts are absent. The BEV capstone models use initialization weights (mAP ≈ 0 by construction), limiting that portion to latency-only claims. Finally, FP32 bit-identity is scoped to fixed thread counts and single reduction paths, so "FP32 is portable" should not be over-read.

## Strengths

- A clean, falsifiable experimental design: one artifact, fixed scales, kernel/ISA as the sole free variable, and an FP32 bit-identical control on the same harness that isolates the divergence to the integer path.
- The agreement result is stated as an *invariance* (kernel-shared vs kernel-different, bimodal at 1000 and 958–965 per 1000) with the hardware-boundary location explicitly shown not to predict it — a sharper and more useful claim than a generic "results vary across devices".
- Findings translate directly into deployable guidance: re-validate INT8 per target, treat vendor-native quantization as mandatory, provision edge accelerators by output/data-movement size with a closed-form threshold (compute-time × link-bandwidth / N).
- The silent-failure catalog (§9) — subset inflation, preprocessing dominance, SQNR non-predictiveness, zero-copy aliasing bug collapsing top-1 to 0.0014, TensorRT EP silently running on CPU — is independently valuable for anyone building quantization evaluation pipelines.
- Exceptional transparency: threats to validity, version confounds, and measurement boundaries are quantified rather than waved away; negative results (power-of-two mitigation, op-selection exclusions) are reported with mechanism probes.

## Limitations

- n = 1 device per hardware class and single-run p50 latencies without confidence intervals — small effects should be read as directional (the authors state this themselves).
- Accuracy and agreement numbers rest on 200–1000-image subsets; the paper's own §9 shows +9.77 pp mean top-1 inflation and flipped significance verdicts on subsets, bounding absolute accuracy claims.
- ONNX Runtime versions differ across CPU targets (1.17.1 / 1.23.2 / 1.28.0), so cross-platform pairs are also cross-version comparisons; two controls bound but do not remove the confound.
- The mechanism behind C2 is not independently localized — it is borrowed from concurrent single-GPU LLM work; this paper measures the phenomenon, and the power-of-two counter-evidence covers only one model/boundary/rounding-mode combination.
- §7's transition thresholds (≈319 KB, ≈1.05 MB) are linear-fit extrapolations specific to one PCIe Gen2×1 host link; only the band-width = N structure is link-independent.
- Vendor scope is limited to Qualcomm and DEEPX; other automotive NPUs (TI, Renesas) are untested. BEV models run with init weights (latency-only), and several absolute numbers are not comparable across sections (differing batch, resolution, subsets).

## Relevance

量化部署的可靠性是高效推理落地的核心问题：这篇论文系统地证明"一次量化、处处部署"在异构边缘硬件上不成立（加速符号随 ISA 翻转、逐输入预测不一致、厂商锁定量化），对任何要在 CPU/GPU/NPU 混合 fleet 上部署 INT8 模型、或关心量化模型确定性与冗余安全设计的系统工作都是直接的实证依据。其受控测量方法论（固定 artifact 与 scale、FP32 逐位对照、逐输入一致率）也可迁移到 LLM 边缘部署与跨后端量化评测中。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the kernel-invariance result (1000/1000 iff same integer kernel) hold for generative / LLM edge workloads with much larger output spaces, where a 4% per-input flip rate compounds autoregressively?
- Can a kernel-level contract — e.g., a standardized requantization epilogue (fixed rounding mode + M = 2^k as an exact shift, enforced in MLAS/XNNPACK/TensorRT/vendor toolchains) — restore cross-target bit-identity, or is per-target re-validation genuinely unavoidable?
- How do the agreement rates behave under QAT (vs PTQ), per-channel activation scales, or higher precision (INT16 accumulate epilogue) — do any quantizer-side levers widen kernel portability?
- Does the output-size/band-width=N provisioning rule generalize to multi-tenant edge NPUs with shared links, and to faster links (Gen3/Gen4) where the D2H-bound band shifts but compute-per-byte of transformer heads keeps detectors near the boundary?
- For automotive redundancy specifically: what cross-module agreement threshold (flips per 1000) is tolerable under ISO 26262 safety cases, and can divergence-aware voting (rather than naive dual-compute agreement) be certified?
- Would the Qualcomm silent BYO-QDQ failure be caught by existing model-validation harnesses, and what minimal on-device golden-set check should become standard practice for vendor-NPU deployments?
