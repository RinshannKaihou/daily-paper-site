Key metric — risk-control precision
≈2 tokens MAE

Probe steering places the lottery risk switching point anywhere across 30–200 tokens with ~2-token mean absolute error vs. target. Every few-shot dose (ρ = 0 → 1.0) leaves it stuck at 97–101; zero-shot CoT pushes it the wrong way, to ~140.

Lottery-game risk switching points (tokens). Full-range calibrated control is achievable only by probe steering (viridian); prompting never produces graded risk-seeking.

Why this matters

First controlled head-to-head of prompting vs. SAE vs. probe steering inside LLM social simulation — a method-selection playbook for trustworthy, inspectable agent control.

Same backbone, same tasks, dose-response controls: a reusable template for computational social science — SAEs to discover mechanisms, probes to test them.

Critical edge

The headline is softer than it sounds: on the brick task SAE steering is negative (Δ = −0.23) and few-shot+CoT wins by ~1.7 points — partly reflecting access to external human-rated exemplars, a confound acknowledged only obliquely.

MMethodology — distilled

  • Testbed — Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct on the Expected Parrot platform, 40 agents per condition; four tasks as natural-language games: lottery (risk), ultimatum (altruism), brick AUT (divergent creativity), stapler (product innovation).
  • Family 1 · Prompting ladder — persona / zero-shot CoT / few-shot / few-shot+CoT, plus a 5-level dose-response sweep (ρ = 0 → 1.0) over the fraction of demonstrations taking the target action. Creativity exemplars come from the human-rated Ocsai corpus, selected independently of the judges.
  • Family 2 · SAE steering — Goodfire SAEs at layer 50 of 80 (65,536 features): encode ht → boost selected latents z′ = z + α·ei → decode → re-inject. 2 risk, 1 altruism, 3 creativity features; high temperature serves as the randomness control.
  • Family 3 · Probe steering — L2 logistic-regression probe on layer-48 activations (82% held-out accuracy); steering adds λ·ŵ to the hidden state, with λ searched to hit target switching points.
  • Evaluation — switching points P(risky)=0.5 and acceptance thresholds P(accept)=0.5 for preferences; creativity scored 1–10 by five blind LLM judges across four vendors on the four Torrance dimensions, length-controlled rubric, N = 680.

Invariants documented (identical prompts/decoding, no target leakage, seeded sampling); full replication on Qwen2-7B-Instruct; probe pickles and JSONL trial logs released.

+Strengths

  • Genuinely controlled design: same backbone and tasks, graded prompting ladder with dose-response sweeps, temperature controls, documented invariants and no target leakage.
  • Calibration quality prompting cannot match — ~2-token MAE across 30–200-token targets is behavioral control few LLM-intervention papers demonstrate.
  • Exceptional evaluative honesty: reports its own method losing on creativity and narrows claims to calibrated linear control + interpretability.
  • SAE→probe two-stage pipeline (inductive discovery, then deductive testing) is a clean methodological template for computational social science.
  • Strong LLM-judge hygiene: five judges, four vendors, blinding, randomization, per-judge length-confound regressions, robustness to dropping length-sensitive judges.

–Limitations

  • Individual-level only — no multi-agent interaction, networks, or emergent group dynamics; the “social simulation” scope is isolated behavioral tasks.
  • “40 agents” are 40 seeded runs of the same model, not independent individuals — pseudo-replication is not discussed.
  • Steering saturates: below-unity achieved-vs-target slope on creativity; over-steering causes hallucination and off-target activation.
  • Single primary model; the Qwen2-7B check covers probes only — SAE steering, layer choices, and ladder interactions are not established to generalize.
  • Proprietary dependencies (Goodfire, Expected Parrot, five judge APIs) and manual, “more art than standardized practice” SAE feature selection limit independent replication.
  • Small trait set: norms, identity, institutions — the context-dependent social mechanisms — remain untested.

QOpen questions — where this goes next

  1. Can manual SAE feature selection be automated (causal attribution, circuit discovery) so steering becomes standardized rather than “art”?
  2. Does the prompt-ladder vs. steering hierarchy hold on frontier reasoning and closed API models without activation access?
  3. Would multi-probe or low-rank directions recover the missing creativity control range, given the four Torrance dimensions may not collapse onto one axis?
  4. Does a steered trait propagate through multi-agent interaction — social influence, network effects — as the future-work agenda suggests?
  5. Does the ~2-token calibration MAE survive distribution shift (loss vs. gain framings, real-stakes human validation) and model updates or quantization?
  6. Is the few-shot win on creativity an artifact of external human data — what happens under matched information budgets?
