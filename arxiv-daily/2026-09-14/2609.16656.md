> **一句话总结：** 本文提出 CTOAC（Channel-wise Token-balanced Output-Aware Clipping）后训练量化方法，针对 VSSD 视觉状态空间对偶网络中"激活量化是低比特主要瓶颈"的问题，通过为每个输入通道学习独立裁剪边界并以 token 均衡的线性输出重构损失（TB-NMSE）优化，在 ImageNet-1K W4A4 下保持 81.1%/83.2%/84.8% Top-1（FP32 为 83.7%/84.6%/85.4%），远超所有基线，并在 COCO/ADE20K 下游任务上保持强性能，RTX 4090 部署最高获得 1.42× 端到端加速。

> **TL;DR:** The authors diagnose that activation (not weight) quantization is the low-bit bottleneck in VSSD vision backbones (W4A32 retains 83.0% ImageNet-1K Top-1 vs. 83.7% FP32 on VSSD-Tiny, while W32A4 collapses to 2.4%), traced to channel-wise magnitude variation and token-localized activation tails. Their CTOAC method learns per-input-channel clipping bounds by minimizing a token-balanced normalized MSE on linear outputs, quantizing only backbone linear layers and their input activations. At W4A4 on ImageNet-1K, CTOAC reaches 81.1/83.2/84.8 Top-1 for VSSD-Tiny/Small/Base — beating the strongest baseline PTQ4VM by 0.1/0.9/0.6 points while SmoothQuant, BRECQ, MinMax, and Truncation collapse to 0.1–2.0 points; at W4A3 it retains 47.0/41.0/76.9 vs. single digits for all baselines. On COCO and ADE20K at W4A4 it loses only ~1.2–2.7 AP/mIoU from FP32, and a CUTLASS RTX 4090 deployment yields up to 1.42× end-to-end speedup. Worth reading if you care about quantizing state-space vision models, but note the quantization scope is partial (state-space ops stay FP32) and the W4A4 margin over PTQ4VM is thin.

## 核心贡献

- **识别并量化了 VSSD 的低比特瓶颈**：通过权重-激活拆分实验证明，激活量化（而非权重量化）是 VSSD 低比特精度崩溃的主因——VSSD-Tiny 上 W4A32 仅损失 0.7 个百分点（83.0% vs 83.7% FP32），而 W32A4 崩溃至 2.4%。
- **刻画了激活分布的两大难点**：输入投影等线性层的激活在通道维度上幅度差异巨大（少数通道幅度远高于其余），且极端值集中在少数 token 位置（中央 99% 的值接近零），因此共享量化范围必然顾此失彼。
- **提出 CTOAC 方法**：为每个输入通道学习独立的上下裁剪边界（跨 batch 与 token 共享），以 token 均衡的归一化线性输出重构误差（TB-NMSE）为目标优化裁剪边界；权重先量化后固定，推理时全部参数静态化。
- **渐进式消融验证设计必要性**：从 MinMax 共享范围（2.0%）→ 固定通道级裁剪（32.2%）→ 完整 CTOAC 输出感知校准（81.1%，VSSD-Tiny W4A4），证明仅有静态通道分离不够，必须结合输出失真校准。
- **跨任务与部署验证**：三种规模骨干（Tiny/Small/Base）、五种精度设置（W8A8 到 W3A3）、COCO 检测/实例分割、ADE20K 语义分割，以及 CUTLASS 内核的 RTX 4090 实测加速（最高 1.42×）。

## Key Contributions

- **Identifies activation quantization as the dominant low-bit bottleneck in VSSD**: a weight-activation split on VSSD-Tiny shows W4A32 retains 83.0% Top-1 (0.7 pt below the 83.7% FP32 baseline) while W32A4 collapses to 2.4% — an 81.3-point drop isolating activations as the failure mode.
- **Characterizes the activation pathology**: representative linear-layer inputs exhibit strong channel-wise magnitude variation (a small subset of channels dominates) and token-localized extremes, with the central 99% of raw values near zero — making any shared quantization range wasteful or destructive.
- **Proposes CTOAC (Channel-wise Token-balanced Output-Aware Clipping)**: per-input-channel learnable clipping bounds (shared across samples and tokens) optimized by minimizing a token-balanced normalized MSE (TB-NMSE) on the linear outputs; weights are quantized first with signed symmetric per-output-channel quantization and then frozen, and all parameters are static at inference.
- **Progressive ablation showing both axes are necessary**: on VSSD-Tiny at W4A4, MinMax shared range gives 2.0% Top-1, adding fixed channel-wise clipping reaches 32.2%, and the full output-aware TB-NMSE calibration recovers 81.1%.
- **Comprehensive validation**: three backbone scales, five precision settings (W8A8/W6A6/W4A4/W4A3/W3A3), COCO object detection and instance segmentation, ADE20K semantic segmentation, and CUTLASS-based RTX 4090 deployment measurements.

## Methodology

CTOAC targets a deliberately restricted quantization scope: only the VSSD-backbone linear layers' weights (signed symmetric, per-output-channel) and the activations directly entering them (static affine asymmetric, shared per-layer scale/zero-point) are quantized. State-space computations, convolutions, normalization, nonlinearities, residual paths, and softmax all remain full precision. Biases and accumulations stay high precision.

For each target linear layer, per-input-channel clipping bounds α_{l,c}, β_{l,c} are introduced. The calibration procedure (Algorithm 1) is:

1. Quantize and freeze the weight W_l^Q = Q_W(W_l); compute the FP reference output Y_l^FP.
2. Initialize channel-wise bounds from calibration activations.
3. Iterate: clip activations per channel → recompute shared (s_l, z_l) via static affine calibration → quantize → produce Y_l^Q → compute the token-balanced loss → update only the clipping bounds (straight-through estimator through rounding).
4. Recompute (s_l, z_l) with the final bounds and freeze everything for static inference.

The key objective is TB-NMSE: for each token position t, the batch-averaged output error is normalized by the FP output norm plus ε, and these per-token relative errors are averaged uniformly over tokens. Because normalization precedes token averaging, each token contributes equally in relative-error terms — preventing high-energy token positions from dominating calibration. Notably, CTOAC optimizes neither weight rounding (no AdaRound-style variables), nor Hessian/Fisher second-order surrogates, nor any task-level loss; it only fits channel-wise clipping bounds against output reconstruction. Experiments use 256 unlabeled calibration images for ImageNet-1K, with per-task recalibration on downstream training images (task heads stay FP32).

## Results & Highlights

- **Weight-activation asymmetry (VSSD-Tiny, ImageNet-1K Top-1)**: W4A32 = 83.0% vs FP32 83.7% (−0.7 pt); W32A4 = 2.4% (−81.3 pt), Top-5 falling from 96.8% to 9.7% — pinpointing activation range selection as the bottleneck.
- **W4A4 on ImageNet-1K Top-1**: CTOAC achieves 81.1% (VSSD-Tiny), 83.2% (Small), 84.8% (Base) vs FP32 83.7/84.6/85.4; the strongest baseline PTQ4VM gets 81.0/82.3/84.2 (+0.1/+0.9/+0.6 for CTOAC), while MinMax (2.0/0.6/0.1), Truncation (10.2/3.7/1.0), SmoothQuant (1.3/1.3/0.6), and BRECQ (19.1/2.0/0.1) collapse.
- **A3 regime (3-bit activations)**: at W4A3, CTOAC retains 47.0/41.0/76.9 Top-1 vs PTQ4VM's 6.8/4.7/5.5 and all other baselines at ≤1.1; at W3A3, CTOAC holds 26.3/42.2/74.2 (VSSD-Base losing only 11.2 pts from its 85.4% FP32 baseline).
- **Progressive ablation (W4A4)**: MinMax no-clipping 2.0/0.6/0.1 → fixed channel-wise clipping 32.2/17.9/60.7 → full CTOAC 81.1/83.2/84.8, showing output-aware bound optimization is what closes the gap.
- **COCO detection/segmentation (APb/APm)**: CTOAC W4A4 drops only 1.7/1.2 points on VSSD-Tiny (45.3/41.4 vs FP32 47.0/42.6) and 1.2/0.9 on VSSD-Small (47.1/42.6 vs 48.3/43.5), whereas all calibration baselines fall to ≤1.6 AP at W4A4.
- **ADE20K semantic segmentation (SS/MS mIoU)**: CTOAC W4A4 loses 2.7/2.4 mIoU on VSSD-Tiny (45.1/46.3 vs FP32 47.8/48.7) while baselines are near zero; similar single-/multi-scale degradation indicates scale-coherent features.
- **Deployment**: CUTLASS-based kernels on an RTX 4090 (batch 32) give W4A4 end-to-end speedups of 1.34×–1.42× across the three backbones; W8A8 ranges from a slight slowdown to 1.32×, since kernel launch overhead, layout conversion, and remaining FP32 ops dominate latency.

## Authors & Credibility

The paper is a two-author work (Jonghyeon Lim, Changhoon Yim) from the Intelligent Image Processing Laboratory, Konkuk University, Seoul — a mid-size lab rather than a large quantization-focused group. It is funded by the Korean NRF (MSIT grant RS-2026-25476371). The related-work coverage is thorough and current (spanning PTQ4VM, MambaQuant, Quamba, QMamba, SSDi8, and 2025–2026 ViT PTQ methods), and the experimental protocol is careful: identical quantization scope applied to all baselines, 256-image calibration, full 50K validation evaluation, and an honest footnote that latency results reflect deployment configurations rather than pure arithmetic speedup. No code release is mentioned, which limits independent verification.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: moderate, incremental.** Learnable channel-wise clipping and output-reconstruction calibration are both well-trodden in ViT/LLM PTQ (AdaRound, BRECQ, RepQ-ViT lineage); the contribution is adapting the recipe to VSSD's linear layers plus the token-normalized (per-token relative error) loss, which is a sensible but narrow twist. The paper itself concedes it avoids second-order methods and weight-rounding optimization rather than outperforming them conceptually.
- **Scope caveat that softens the headline numbers**: "W4A4" here means only the backbone linear layers and their inputs — state-space computations, convolutions, and softmax all stay FP32. The reported low-bit accuracy is therefore a mixed-precision result; comparisons against full-model PTQ numbers from the ViT literature would not be apples-to-apples, and the paper does not test fully quantized variants.
- **Baseline fairness**: SmoothQuant and BRECQ were designed for LLMs/ViTs and collapse here partly because they were forced into this restricted linear-layer scope without their usual companion components; PTQ4VM is the only genuinely competitive baseline. Meanwhile MambaQuant, QMamba, Quamba, and SSDi8 — all cited as closely related Mamba-family PTQ methods — are absent from the empirical comparison, which is the most conspicuous evaluation gap.
- **The W4A4 margin over PTQ4VM is thin** (0.1–0.9 pts); the dramatic separations only appear at W4A3/W3A3, where every baseline craters — impressive relative robustness, but 47.0% Top-1 on VSSD-Tiny at W4A3 (from 83.7%) is still a 36.7-point loss, so "retains meaningful accuracy" is relative.
- **Non-monotonic results** (VSSD-Small: W4A3 41.0 < W3A3 42.2) are acknowledged as an artifact of independent per-setting calibration but are not further analyzed — a missing ablation on calibration stability.
- **Reproducibility**: algorithm and hyper-scope are specified precisely (256 calibration images, per-layer procedure, STE), but no code release is mentioned, and iteration count K, optimizer, and learning rate for bound updates are not stated in the extracted text, which will hinder exact reproduction.
- **Red flags are limited**: the deployment section is unusually candid that W8A8 can be slower than FP32 and that speedup is not arithmetic-bound; no benchmark cherry-picking is evident, though only VSSD (not ViM/VMamba/Mamba LMs) is evaluated, so generality claims rest on one architecture family.

## Strengths

- Clean, well-motivated diagnosis: the weight-activation split experiment and the channel/token distribution analysis directly connect the observed activation pathology to the two design axes of the method.
- The progressive ablation (shared range → fixed channel clipping → output-aware optimization) is a model example of attributing gains to specific components rather than reporting only end numbers.
- Token-balanced normalized loss is a simple, well-argued fix for token-localized extremes, and it requires no dynamic quantizers at inference — everything is static.
- Broad validation across three model scales, five precisions, three downstream tasks, and real GPU kernel measurements rather than estimated FLOPs.
- Honest framing of deployment results (latency dominated by kernels/layout/launch overhead, not just bit width).

## Limitations

- Partial quantization scope: unquantized state-space ops, convs, and softmax limit the achievable compression/speedup; a fully quantized VSSD remains unaddressed.
- No comparison against the closest Mamba-family PTQ competitors (MambaQuant, QMamba, Quamba, SSDi8), leaving the practical ranking within this niche unresolved.
- Extreme low-bit results (W4A3/W3A3) still suffer large absolute accuracy drops on the smaller backbones; the method's robustness is relative, not a solution to 3-bit activation quantization.
- Vision-only and VSSD-only evaluation; no language-model Mamba or other SSM backbones (ViM, VMamba) are tested, so generality is asserted rather than demonstrated.
- Missing implementation details (optimizer, learning rate, iteration count K for bound updates) and no stated code release complicate exact reproduction.
- The headline 1.42× speedup is modest and configuration-specific (RTX 4090, batch 32); edge/efficient deployment scenarios are not covered.

## Relevance

这篇论文对关注高效推理与模型压缩的工作高度相关：它系统揭示了视觉状态空间模型（VSSD）低比特量化的激活瓶颈，并提供了简单可复用的通道级裁剪 + token 均衡重构校准方案，可直接借鉴到其他 SSM/线性注意力架构的 PTQ 流水线中。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of CTOAC's advantage persists if the quantization scope is extended to the full VSSD block (state-space computations, convs, softmax), moving toward a genuinely integer-only model?
- Would combining CTOAC's channel-wise clipping with rotation/smoothing methods (e.g., MambaQuant-style variance-aligned rotations or Hadamard transforms) close the remaining W4A3/W3A3 accuracy gap on the Tiny/Small backbones?
- Why is VSSD-Base so much more quantization-tolerant (76.9% at W4A3) than Tiny (47.0%) — is it wider channels smoothing outliers, or a different distribution of token-localized extremes across stages?
- Can the TB-NMSE objective be extended to block-level or end-to-end reconstruction (jointly across layers) without giving up static inference, and would that fix the non-monotonic W4A3/W3A3 behavior on VSSD-Small?
- Does the same activation pathology (channel-wise variation + token-localized tails) appear in causal Mamba language models, and does token-balanced calibration transfer there where token positions carry semantic rather than spatial meaning?
- What are the energy/latency trade-offs on edge accelerators (INT4 NPUs, mobile SoCs), where the 1.42× RTX 4090 result may not transfer?
