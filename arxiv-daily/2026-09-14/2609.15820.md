> **一句话总结：** AlgoEvo 把 LLM 驱动的自动算法发现从"预定义进化流水线 + 被动代码采样"改造成一个自主智能体搜索循环——智能体自己决定何时读代码、诊断瓶颈、编辑组件、评估候选——配合可插拔的"设计技能库"（统一支持单目标/多目标/多组件设计）和基于 MCTS 式经验树的三级经验库；在 TSP、CVRP、Bi-TSP、Bi-FJSP、CVRP-DR、FJSP 4-Ops 六个基准上以约 27–39 次评估（基线用满 500 次）匹配或超过 FunSearch、EoH、ReEvo、MCTS-AHD、MEoH、MOTIF、E2OC 等专用方法，且 token 消耗更低，并展现出任务内累积与跨任务迁移能力。

> **TL;DR:** AlgoEvo replaces rigid, paradigm-specific LLM evolutionary pipelines (EoH, FunSearch, ReEvo, MCTS-AHD, MEoH, MOTIF, E2OC) with one autonomous agent that dynamically inspects, diagnoses, edits, and evaluates heuristic code, unified across single-objective, multi-objective, and multi-component design via a pluggable design-skill hub and an MCTS-style hierarchical experience bank. On six benchmarks (TSP, CVRP, Bi-TSP, Bi-FJSP, CVRP-DR, FJSP 4-Ops) under a shared 500-evaluation budget on the LLM4AD platform, it attains the best training quality on TSP (5.986 vs EoH's 6.309 tour length, a ~5% improvement using 39 vs 500 evaluations) and CVRP (12.698 vs MCTS-AHD's 13.236), the best hypervolume on both multi-objective tasks (Bi-TSP 0.827 vs MEoH's 0.760), and the best results on both CVRP-DR splits (8.860/8.999 vs Synergy's 8.928/9.112) with 139.2M vs 289.6–470.0M tokens; ablations show the experience bank (+0.66% without) and skill hub (+0.58% without) drive performance, and cross-task transfer (CVRP→CVRP-DR) cuts cost by 4.8%.

## 核心贡献

- **智能体化搜索循环（Agentic Search Loop）**：不再把 LLM 嵌入预定义的"生成—评估"进化流水线，而是让自主智能体根据运行时反馈自主决定下一步动作——读代码、诊断、编辑、评估或提交；奖励定义为性能增量 r_t = F(Π_{t-1}) − F(Π_t)，累计奖励可 telescope 回全局发现目标。
- **设计技能库（Design Skill Hub）**：把范式特定知识（可设计策略的角色、代码接口、修改原则、评估约定）从搜索引擎中外置为可插拔技能，使同一工作流无需结构改动即可覆盖单目标、多目标、多组件三类设计任务；技能在跨任务中被持续提炼进化（模式在 ≥2 个任务中验证有效才晋升为技能）。
- **分层经验库（Hierarchical Experience Bank）**：三级结构——经验卡片（记录设计上下文/修改/奖励/理由）→ 任务级经验树（用 MCTS 四操作：情境化 UCB 选择、扩展、评估、回传）→ 跨任务模式固化为技能；情境检测（停滞/稀疏前沿/瓶颈/耦合）以 0.30/0.25/0.20/0.20 的权重偏置经验检索。
- **全面实证**：在统一协议（LLM4AD 平台、相同实例划分、每种子 500 次评估预算、3 个随机种子）下与 10 余个专用基线对比，验证了样本效率、任务内多轮累积（TSP 巡回长度 −1.91%、Bi-FJSP 超体积 +20.21%）和跨任务迁移（CVRP→CVRP-DR 成本 −4.8%）。

## Key Contributions

- **Agentic search loop**: replaces the expert-predefined evolutionary pipeline with an autonomous agent that chooses when to inspect code, run diagnostics, edit components, and evaluate candidates; the immediate reward is the performance improvement, and maximizing cumulative reward telescopes to minimizing final algorithm cost.
- **Design skill hub**: externalizes paradigm-specific knowledge (strategy roles, code interfaces, modification principles, evaluation conventions) as pluggable skills, so a single engine handles single-objective, multi-objective, and multi-component design by swapping skills; skills evolve across tasks (a pattern must be validated on at least two tasks before promotion).
- **Hierarchical experience bank**: experience cards distilled at evaluation events → a task-level experience tree traversed with the four MCTS operations (selection via situation-conditioned UCB, expansion, evaluation, backpropagation) → cross-task consolidation into skills; four detected search situations (stagnation, sparse front, bottleneck, coupling) with fixed retrieval weights (0.30/0.25/0.20/0.20) steer retrieval.
- **Diagnosis as search-direction identification**: per-unit attribution and interaction analysis identify bottleneck components and coupling, triggering situation-aware retrieval and coordinated multi-component edits — enabling the transition from isolated tuning to joint co-design.
- **Unified empirical validation**: six tasks (TSP, CVRP, Bi-TSP, Bi-FJSP, CVRP-DR, FJSP 4-Ops) under one protocol on LLM4AD with a 500-evaluation budget and 3 seeds, against paradigm-specialized baselines including FunSearch, EoH, ReEvo, MCTS-AHD, MEoH, NSGA-II, MOEA/D, MOTIF, E2OC, Synergy, and Rotating-EoH.

## Methodology

AlgoEvo formulates automated algorithm design as an agentic discovery process over an algorithm space S = S_1 × … × S_K, where K = 1 gives single-heuristic design and K > 1 multi-component design. At each step the agent observes a discovery state s_t = (Π_t, C_t, E_t, F_d) — current algorithm, working context, experience bank, performance feedback — and selects among seven actions (inspect, retrieve, diagnose, analyse interaction, edit, evaluate, commit/terminate). Only evaluation produces a scored candidate and charges the evaluation budget; diagnosis and interaction analysis draw from the same budget, forcing a balance between empirical testing and analytical reasoning.

Two components support the loop. First, the **design skill hub** activates a matching skill at task initialization (t = 0), supplying the design roles, interfaces, and inductive bias for the paradigm; the skill is fixed within a task and refined only across tasks (t = T), when patterns validated on ≥ 2 tasks are abstracted back into the skill. Method skills distilled from prior methods (EoH, ReEvo, FunSearch, MEoH, NSGA-II, MOEA/D) can also be loaded without their native search procedures. Second, the **hierarchical experience bank** records each evaluation as an experience card, organizes cards into a task-level tree with derived-from edges, and traverses it with MCTS mechanics: selection maximizes a situational UCB r̄(v|q) + c·sqrt(2 ln N / n(v,q)) where the situation q is a subset of {stagnation, sparse front, bottleneck, coupling} detected from telemetry by fixed thresholds (last τ_s = 4 submissions without improvement; per-unit attribution mass > τ_b = 0.5; interaction > τ_c = 0.05); expansion attaches cards for verified candidates; backpropagation credits ancestor design decisions via subtree mean reward R̄(v) = S(v)/m(v).

Experiments run on the LLM4AD platform with identical instance splits, evaluation entry points, a budget of 500 evaluations per seed, and three random seeds; gpt-4o-mini backs TSP/CVRP and DeepSeek-V4-Flash the remaining tasks. Each task has disjoint training/held-out test sets; multi-objective metrics (HV with reference point (1.1, …, 1.1), IGD) use a shared global normalization basis per problem, and multi-objective rows report training-instance quality since the designed unit is fixed post-run.

## Results & Highlights

- **Single-heuristic design (TSP)**: AlgoEvo achieves the best training quality, 5.986 ± 0.11 tour length vs the strongest LLM-AHD baseline EoH's 6.309 ± 0.043 (a ~5.1% improvement; 4–7% ahead of the strongest baselines and 12.3% ahead of the nearest-neighbour heuristic on TSP), and the best test quality (7.007 ± 0.04 vs EoH's 7.392 ± 0.058) — using 39 evaluations versus the baselines' full 500 and 2.9M tokens versus 3.2–4.3M.
- **Single-heuristic design (CVRP)**: best on both splits — 12.698 ± 0.39 train (vs MCTS-AHD's 13.236 ± 0.302) and 24.150 ± 0.40 test (vs ReEvo's 25.950 ± 0.517) — with 35 evaluations and 1.9M tokens (baselines: 500 evals, 3.9–4.3M tokens).
- **Multi-objective design (Bi-TSP)**: best hypervolume 0.827 ± 0.012 vs MEoH's 0.760 ± 0.020 and best IGD 0.036 ± 0.003 vs MEoH's 0.077 ± 0.018, at 36 evaluations vs 500; on Bi-FJSP it attains the best HV (0.911 ± 0.083 vs MEoH's 0.903 ± 0.051) but trails MEoH on IGD (0.162 ± 0.125 vs 0.143 ± 0.077).
- **Multi-component co-design**: best result of any method on both CVRP-DR splits (8.860 ± 0.037 train, 8.999 ± 0.036 test, vs Synergy's 8.928 ± 0.075 / 9.112 ± 0.043) and best held-out makespan on FJSP 4-Ops (1927.5 ± 38.5 vs Rotating-EoH's 1954.87 ± 26.14, also beating the expert operators' 2137.9 ± 12.0), while cutting token usage to 139.2M and 118.1M versus the baselines' 289.6–470.0M and 460.7–699.3M.
- **Intra-task accumulation**: across five successive fixed-budget rounds, TSP tour length improves monotonically from 4.8928 to 4.7995 (−1.91%) and Bi-FJSP hypervolume from 0.1549 to 0.1862 (+20.21%); warm-starting from a previous run's best code improves plain-TSP mean tour length from 4.961 to 4.808 with reduced variance.
- **Cross-task transfer**: transferring distilled experience from CVRP to CVRP-DR lowers mean cost from 10.314 to 9.816 (−4.8%, vs 10.791 cold-start baseline); TSP → Bi-TSP transfer raises HV from 336.5 to 352.2 (+4.7%).
- **Ablations (CVRP-DR)**: removing the experience bank costs +0.66% (9.100 vs 9.040), removing the design skill hub +0.58% (9.092), and removing situation-triggered injection only +0.21% (9.059) — persistent memory and reusable abstractions matter more than retrieval gating.
- **Method-skill transfer**: AlgoEvo running with a distilled EoH method skill reaches 7.0414 ± 0.0923 TSP tour length vs native EoH's 7.1529 ± 0.0263 with 22 vs 500 evaluations; with MOEA/D's skill, Bi-TSP HV jumps from the native 0.314 to 0.829.

## Authors & Credibility

The team combines the groups that built much of this baseline landscape: Qingfu Zhang (CityU Hong Kong) is the senior author of EoH, MEoH, EoH-S, LLM4AD, and E2OC's lineage; Junhao Qiu (first author, CityU HK) co-authored E2OC (ICML 2026) and EvoDR; Xialiang Tong and Mingxuan Yuan are with Huawei Noah's Ark Lab; Liyong Lin is with A*STAR. This gives the comparison against EoH/MEoH/E2OC insider credibility — the baselines are the authors' own systems, tuned on their own LLM4AD platform. The evaluation protocol is unusually careful for this literature: unified instance splits and evaluation entry points, a fixed 500-evaluation budget, three random seeds with sample standard deviations reported, disjoint train/test sets, and per-problem normalization for HV/IGD. The paper honestly reports its losses (Bi-FJSP IGD behind MEoH; FJSP 4-Ops training behind Synergy) rather than hiding them.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The core reframe — from "LLM as passive sampler inside a fixed evolutionary loop" to "LLM agent as orchestrator of the design process itself" — is genuine and timely, and the situational-UCB experience tree is a clean way to make agent memory search-structured rather than text-retrieval-shaped. That said, the ingredients (MCTS over a program tree à la MCTS-AHD, agentic coding loops à la SWE-agent/AlphaEvolve, skill libraries à la AWM/Trace2Skill) are individually well established; the contribution is the synthesis and domain specialization, not any single mechanism.
- **Rigor of the efficiency claim**: "39 evaluations vs 500" is rhetorically strong but slightly misleading — AlgoEvo's evaluation counter excludes its read/diagnose/edit steps (instrumented trajectories on CVRP-DR show, e.g., seed 2025 making 122 reads, 17 edits, 81 diagnoses for only 14 evaluations), each of which is itself an LLM call. The token column is the fairer comparison and there AlgoEvo does genuinely win (2.9M vs 3.2–4.3M on TSP; 139.2M vs 289.6–470.0M on CVRP-DR), so the efficiency claim survives, but the headline metric is generous to the proposed method. A minority-of-proposals-improve detail also means the flat tail after ~35 evaluations records stagnation, not success.
- **Ablation depth**: the ablation (Table 9) covers only CVRP-DR, and the component deltas are small (+0.66%/+0.58%/+0.21%) — within roughly one standard deviation of the reported stds (±0.031 vs ±0.132/±0.153), so the ordering of mechanism importance is suggestive rather than statistically firm with three seeds. No ablation on the single-heuristic tasks where the efficiency story is headline-making.
- **Mixed and partially offsetting results**: Bi-FJSP IGD loses to MEoH (0.162 vs 0.143, with huge ±0.125 variance), FJSP 4-Ops training loses narrowly to Synergy (646.4 vs 646.10), and AlgoEvo's per-seed stds are often larger than baselines' (e.g., TSP ±0.11 vs EoH ±0.043). Multi-objective rows report training-instance quality only, which the authors flag but which still flatters the comparison.
- **Reproducibility**: instances, seeds, evaluation scripts, and evolvable-unit code contracts are said to be released, and the appendix specifies thresholds (τ_s = 4, τ_b = 0.5, τ_c = 0.05), situation weights, LLM backbones (gpt-4o-mini, DeepSeek-V4-Flash), and operator probabilities — strong on paper; however, some baseline token counts are "inferred from baseline runs retaining usage records" (superscript e in Table 1), and the full agent prompt/tooling stack is the kind of detail that determines whether an agentic system replicates, so independent reproduction is not yet demonstrated.
- **Red flags / quirks**: the warm-start and cross-round experiments switch to a different "plain TSP" protocol (NN baseline 5.017 instead of 6.112) that the authors themselves warn is incomparable to Table 1; the method-skill study mixes 100-city TSP with 50-city Bi-TSP scales; the Related Work section literally repeats its "Agentic Coding and Program Synthesis" paragraph twice — an editing slip that hints at a rushed submission. Whether the agentic loop's advantage persists with the same backbone for all methods (baselines run gpt-4o-mini/DeepSeek-V4-Flash per task, but per-method backbone assignment is only in Appendix C) deserves scrutiny.

## Strengths

- A single unified engine genuinely spans three design paradigms (single-objective, multi-objective, multi-component) that previously required separate codebases — the skill hub is a clean decoupling mechanism, and the method-skill experiments (native EoH vs AlgoEvo-with-EoH-skill) elegantly show the methodology transfers independently of its original search scaffold.
- Sample and token efficiency are real and measured in both currencies: matching or beating specialized baselines at roughly 1/13 of the evaluation budget on four of six tasks, with 2–6x lower token consumption everywhere including the multi-component tasks where it uses most of the budget.
- The experience mechanism is verified at three granularities — within-task (cost decreases as cards accumulate), across runs (warm start 4.961 → 4.808 with lower variance), and across tasks (CVRP → CVRP-DR −4.8%) — plus multi-round monotone gains (TSP −1.91%, Bi-FJSP HV +20.21% over five rounds), which is the most convincing evidence for the "self-evolving" claim.
- Honest reporting of losses (Bi-FJSP IGD, FJSP 4-Ops train), transparent situation-detection thresholds, and an unusually detailed appendix (design spaces as code contracts, evaluation algorithms, per-seed action breakdowns).

## Limitations

- Token overhead grows with context length as the experience bank accumulates in complex multi-component settings — the authors name memory compression as future work; at 118–139M tokens those runs are not cheap in absolute terms.
- Cross-task transfer is only demonstrated within aligned problem families (routing → routing with destruction-repair); the authors concede negative-transfer risk across structurally divergent domains and offer no experiment probing failure cases.
- Long-horizon reliability: agentic trajectories vary substantially across seeds (edit-driven vs diagnosis-driven styles), and the paper itself notes that with three seeds the correlation between operation mix and final quality cannot be established.
- The evaluation isolates task-specific algorithmic units while the surrounding solver, feasibility checks, and evaluators stay fixed — so claims do not extend to whole-pipeline co-design; instance scales are modest (50–200 nodes for most tasks, up to 500 only in reserve sets), leaving scalability open.
- Ablations are confined to one task, and several key numbers rest on three seeds with overlapping standard deviations.

## Relevance

这篇论文直接命中"用 LLM 自动设计优化算法/启发式"这一方向的核心痛点——框架碎片化与无记忆的试错搜索——其智能体化搜索循环、可插拔技能库与经验树机制对任何做 LLM 驱动算法发现、智能体记忆/技能库或组合优化自动设计的人都是重要的新基线和设计参照。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of the sample-efficiency gain comes from the agentic loop itself versus simply giving the LLM execution feedback (diagnoses, traces) that scalar-fitness pipelines discard? A feedback-ablation (agentic loop + scalar reward only) would isolate this.
- Does the advantage survive when every baseline uses the same, stronger backbone (e.g., frontier reasoning models) for both candidate generation and the AlgoEvo agent, and when baselines are also allowed diagnosis-style prompts?
- Can the experience tree's situational UCB avoid collapse as the bank grows — what is the retrieval/gating cost at 10x or 100x the current number of cards, and does the +0.21% contribution of situation-triggered injection grow or shrink?
- Why exactly does diagnosis-heavy search beat edit-heavy search (8.281 vs 8.927/9.138 on CVRP-DR), and can that behavior be induced rather than left to seed luck — e.g., by budgeting diagnosis as a fraction of steps?
- Does cross-task transfer stay positive across structurally divergent families (e.g., routing → scheduling), where the paper concedes transfer weakens, and can the ≥2-task promotion rule be replaced by a principled applicability test?
- The evaluation budget counts evaluations but not agent steps; how do the conclusions change under a wall-clock or total-LLM-call budget, which is what practitioners actually pay?
