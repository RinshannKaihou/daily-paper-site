> **一句话总结：** 本文用逐步最大耦合（maximal coupling）把 KV-cache 驱逐后的累积 token 失配精确分解为"首次分歧 + 分歧后暴露量 × 失配率"，并证明有限观测窗口下风险的尖锐识别区间、给出方差占优的条件 Monte Carlo 估计器；在 Llama-3.1-8B 与 Qwen2.5-7B 的 HELMET/RULER 实验中，分歧后暴露量解释了 85–90% 的聚合失配差距，而 SnapKV 50% 保留相比同预算的 SnapKV-512/近期 token 保留能显著推迟并减少首次分歧，但分歧一旦发生 TV 仍高达 ~90%。

> **TL;DR:** This Ant Group paper derives an exact decomposition of expected token mismatch under KV-cache eviction — expected mismatch = first-divergence occurrence + post-divergence exposure × mismatch rate — via a stepwise overlap–residual maximal coupling, proves a sharp partial-identification interval for risks compatible with a finite divergence-aligned observation window, and introduces an unbiased conditional Monte Carlo estimator (SWRB) with per-replicate variance dominance (median 52.7x variance reduction vs naive coupling in 24 finite-state systems). Empirically, on 288 HELMET/RULER documents per study with Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (H=128, temperature-1 sampling), SnapKV at 50% retention diverges by step 32 in only 61.05% of Llama trajectories vs 92.35% for SnapKV-512 and 90.81% for byte-matched recent-token retention, post-divergence exposure accounts for 85–90% of four aggregate mismatch gaps (e.g., 17.17 of 19.90 pp on Llama), and branch-aligned TV actually rises from early to late post-divergence windows (+6.5 to +10.6 pp, all CIs positive) — meaning eviction policies mostly buy later/rarer divergence, not milder disagreement once diverged.

## 核心贡献

- **精确的轨迹分解恒等式**：在逐步 overlap–residual 最大耦合下，期望 token 失配率被精确分解为 R_SMC = O_H + E_H·C_H，即"首次失配贡献 + 分歧后暴露量乘以失配率"。这把"何时进入分歧"与"进入后失配多严重"拆成两个可分别测量的量。
- **尖锐的偏识别（partial identification）定理**：对任意深度-L 的分歧画像（divergence profile），在无限制的自回归核对上，与有限观测窗口相容的累积风险恰好构成闭区间 [R_L, R_L + B_L]；构造性证明给出端点可达性，并导出精确 minimax 估计（区间中点，最坏误差 b/2）与共享参考下的动作排序条件。
- **方差占优的条件 Monte Carlo 估计器（SWRB）**：通过生存加权残差分支（Rao–Blackwell 化）无偏地联合估计出现率、占据率及窗口/尾部贡献，并证明相对朴素耦合的逐重复方差占优；有限状态系统中方差比中位数为 52.7 倍。
- **两种时间轴的实证分离**：在 Llama-3.1-8B / Qwen2.5-7B 与 HELMET/RULER 的 288 文档研究中发现，干预间的生成时钟差距会收窄，而按分歧时刻对齐的分支内 TV 反而随时间上升——揭示了"更小累积差异"与"分歧后高失配"并存的机制。
- **审慎的实验设计规范**：开发/评估文档分离、结果观察前定样本量、90% 保留研究采用预先设定的八端点族（α=.05/8）与文档层 bootstrap，明确区分探索性与验证性分析。

## Key Contributions

- **Exact trajectory decomposition:** Under a specified stepwise overlap–residual maximal coupling of reference and intervention kernels, the expected mismatch fraction decomposes exactly as R_SMC = O_H + E_H·C_H (first-mismatch occurrence plus post-divergence exposure times its mismatch rate), cleanly separating divergence *timing* from post-entry *occupation*.
- **Sharp partial identification (Theorem 3.2):** For any admissible depth-L divergence profile, the set of cumulative risks compatible with that finite divergence-aligned observation window — over all unrestricted autoregressive kernel pairs — is exactly the interval [R_L, R_L + B_L], with an explicit binary construction attaining both endpoints. Corollaries give the exact minimax estimator (midpoint, worst-case L1 error b/2) and a shared-reference action-ordering condition.
- **Unbiased variance-dominant estimator (SWRB):** Survival-Weighted Residual Branching integrates out the zero-loss full-agreement event (Rao–Blackwell style), jointly estimating occurrence, occupation, and window/tail contributions from one replicate while retaining their covariance; Proposition 4.1 proves Var(Z_naive) − Var(Z_R) ≥ 0 with a characterization of strict inequality.
- **Two-clock empirical analysis:** Complete coupled trajectories (H=128, temperature-1 full-softmax, persistent compact caches) show that generation-position gaps between eviction policies narrow even as branch-aligned (divergence-time-aligned) TV rises from early to late windows — explaining how small aggregate differences coexist with high post-divergence discrepancy.
- **Methodological hygiene:** Development/evaluation document separation (36 dev docs, disjoint), sample sizes fixed before outcome observation, prespecified 8-endpoint family with family-wise α = .05/8 in the 90%-retention study, same-path full-retention controls (which yield exactly zero TV), and byte-matched equal-budget selector comparisons.

## Methodology

The paper proceeds in three layers.

**Theory (Section 3).** Fix a prompt, intervention a (an eviction policy), token alphabet V, horizon H. Reference kernels P and intervention kernels Q_a are coupled recursively by the overlap–residual (maximal) coupling: at each step the shared probability mass min{p(u), q(u)} produces agreement, and the disjoint residual supports are rescaled onto each other, so that P(X_t ≠ Y_t | histories) equals the local total variation δ_t. The expected mismatch fraction R_SMC = E[H^{-1} Σ 1{X_t ≠ Y_t}] is then decomposed via the first-divergence time τ into R_SMC = O_H + E_H·C_H, where O_H is the occurrence (first-mismatch) contribution, E_H the expected post-divergence exposure (available time after entry), and C_H ∈ [0,1] the exposure-weighted mismatch rate. For action contrasts, an exact algebraic identity splits ΔR into occurrence, exposure, and rate terms. Because a finite observation window (depth L after each divergence) leaves the tail unobserved, the authors define an admissible profile (p_s, ρ_{s,j}) and prove the compatible risk set is exactly [R_L, R_L + B_L] (Theorem 3.2), with a constructive attainability argument and minimax corollaries; specifying the full reference kernel can strictly shrink the interval (Section 3.4).

**Estimation (Section 4).** Residual-branch conditional Monte Carlo (SWRB) scans the overlap region with an auxiliary path U, accumulates branch weights w_s = S_s e_s summing to M = 1 − S_{H+1}, then samples one branch J with probability w_J/M and continues the coupling with fresh randomness. The estimates Z_R = M D_J, Z_O = M/H, Z_Π = M(D_J − 1/H) are unbiased and satisfy Z_R = Z_O + Z_Π; per-replicate variance dominance over naive coupled rollouts is proven exactly, with strict gain iff P(0 < M < 1) > 0. The same replicate yields window/tail decompositions (E[Z_L] = R_L, E[W_L] = B_L), and Hoeffding bounds plus the identified interval give a joint confidence enclosure for the full-horizon risk. Validation on 24 enumerable binary systems (H=3, Gamma-perturbed kernels, 512 replicates each) confirms unbiasedness and variance ratios of 11.1–289 (median 52.7) at only 1.37x more kernel-pair evaluations (variance–cost advantage 20.0x).

**Language-model studies (Section 5).** Two paired complete-trajectory studies on Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct over six HELMET/RULER environment–length strata (4K/16K/32K): (1) an exploratory study with 288 documents (48/stratum), K=64 replicates per action, comparing SnapKV-512, SnapKV-50%, and recent-token-50% with identical prompt-cache bytes; (2) an independent confirmatory study at 90% retention (another 288 documents, K=32, SnapKV-90 vs recent-90) with eight prespecified endpoints. Both use H=128, temperature-one full-softmax sampling, EOS absorbing, three paired paths per replicate (reference on its own history, compressed on its own history, compressed forced along the reference history) giving rollout-TV R and fixed-history TV F. Analysis is by document-level stratified bootstrap with explicit exploratory/prespecified distinctions.

## Results & Highlights

- **SnapKV-50% delays and reduces divergence entry (HELMET/RULER, 288 docs, Llama):** fraction of trajectories diverging by generation step 32 is 61.05% for SnapKV-50% vs 92.35% for SnapKV-512 and 90.81% for byte-matched recent-token-50%; by step 128 the figures are 92.29% vs 98.14% and 97.66%. Conditional mean entry times among diverging trajectories: 30.37 steps (SnapKV-50%) vs 11.69 (SnapKV-512) and 10.59 (recent-half) on Llama; Qwen shows the same ordering (29.37 vs 12.18/12.14).
- **Exposure dominates aggregate mismatch gaps:** SnapKV-512 minus SnapKV-50% token-mismatch gaps are 19.90 pp (Llama) and 16.68 pp (Qwen), of which the exposure terms contribute 17.17 pp [16.20, 18.13] and 14.99 pp [13.87, 16.22] — 86.3% and 89.9% of the point-estimated gaps; the equal-budget recent-half contrasts give 20.79/17.10 pp with exposure shares 84.7%/87.3%. All 24 model–stratum–contrast decompositions have an exposure majority (78.1–96.1%).
- **Post-divergence mismatch rate is uniformly high:** C_H ranges 89.74–93.68% on Llama and 93.59–96.20% on Qwen across all actions — once a trajectory diverges, subsequent tokens mismatch ~90%+ of the time regardless of eviction policy.
- **Branch TV rises over post-divergence time:** within each action's fixed τ ≤ 64 cohort (64 strictly post-divergence steps), late-window (lags 49–64) minus early-window (lags 1–16) branch-TV contrasts are 6.53 [5.74, 7.36], 7.11 [6.48, 7.81], and 5.84 [5.11, 6.58] pp on Llama and 10.63 [9.37, 11.91], 10.48 [9.20, 11.81], and 7.83 [6.72, 8.98] pp on Qwen (SnapKV-512/SnapKV-half/recent-half); all 36 stratum–model–action 95% lower bounds are positive. In the independent 90%-retention study, all four prespecified family-adjusted branch contrasts are positive (pooled 6.03–8.27 pp; per-stratum 4.21–11.46 pp).
- **Two clocks diverge:** at 90% retention, generation-clock selector gaps (g_65:128 − g_1:32) are negative in every HELMET stratum (−14.34 to −25.84 pp) but positive in every RULER stratum (+0.27 to +7.84 pp), while branch-clock contrasts are positive in all strata — aggregate narrowing can mask persistent branch-level discrepancy.
- **Estimator efficiency (24 finite-state systems, H=3, 512 replicates):** naive/SWRB sample-variance ratio 11.1–289 with median 52.7; mean kernel-pair evaluations increase only 1.37x for a ratio-of-means variance–cost advantage of 20.0x, consistent with Proposition 4.1.
- **Saturation and EOS localization (90% study):** full-horizon TV saturation fractions S.99 are 28.44% [26.68, 30.24] (Llama SnapKV-90) vs 56.18% [53.77, 58.61] (Llama recent-90) and 43.63% vs 73.65% on Qwen; full-horizon divergence probabilities 74.24%/94.88% (Llama) and 80.88%/97.75% (Qwen). EOS-related components account for only 0.86–3.68% of the Llama HELMET selector difference (9.52–11.65% on RULER) — most discrepancy accrues before termination.
- **Aggregate prediction from short windows:** ridge predictors using only generation positions 1–32 recover the population-average SnapKV-512 minus SnapKV-half gap within a prespecified ±2-pp equivalence margin on both models (errors −0.388 [−1.062, 0.301] pp on Llama, 0.114 [−0.867, 1.135] pp on Qwen), with document-level contrast MAEs of 4.09/4.92 pp.

## Authors & Credibility

Both authors (Xinyue Luo, Fei Yu; corresponding author Fei Yu) are at Ant Group. The work is theory-heavy and statistically careful: it imports maximal coupling (Goldstein 1979; Levin & Peres), partial identification (Manski), Rao–Blackwellization (Blackwell; Casella & Robert), and conditional Monte Carlo (Glasserman & Staum) into KV-cache evaluation, and cites the relevant KV-compression literature (SnapKV, H2O, StreamingLLM/attention sinks, KIVI, Palu, RazorAttention, Ada-KV, CAKE, LookaheadKV, InfoKV) plus exposure-bias/self-recovery work (He et al. 2021; Arora et al. 2022). The empirical protocol is unusually disciplined for an LLM systems paper: dev/eval document separation, sample sizes fixed before outcome observation, prespecified confirmatory endpoints with family-wise error control, explicit labeling of exploratory analyses, full-retention zero-TV controls, and byte-matched budget comparisons. The paper includes an honest AI-use disclosure (AI assisted code, literature search, and math checks; authors retain responsibility). No code release is mentioned in the text.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The core telescoping identity R = O + E·C is mathematically elementary; the substantive contributions are (a) the sharpness construction for the identified interval over unrestricted kernel pairs, (b) the exact per-replicate variance-dominance proof for SWRB, and (c) the two-clock (generation-time vs divergence-time) empirical design, which is the genuinely new lens on KV-cache evaluation. Framing eviction damage as a survival/hazard process (p_s = S_s λ_s) is a fresh import from reliability-style analysis.
- **Rigor:** High. Confirmatory/exploratory separation, family-wise α = .05/8, document-level bootstrap within strata, equal-byte prompt-cache matching between SnapKV and recent-token selectors, and same-path full-retention controls (K=2) that empirically yield zero TV. The theory–practice bridge is verified on enumerable systems where exact risks are computable.
- **Reproducibility:** Models (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct), benchmarks (HELMET, RULER), and SnapKV are public; hyperparameters (H=128, temperature 1, 64-token scoring window, kernel size 5, sink positions = 4, retention formulas) are stated. However, no code or seed release is mentioned, and the SWRB estimator requires access to full next-token distributions and forced-path rollouts, which needs a customized serving stack — nontrivial to reimplement exactly.
- **Significance:** The headline insight — eviction policies chiefly buy later and rarer divergence, not milder post-divergence disagreement (C_H ≈ 90–96% everywhere) — reframes what KV-cache compression research should optimize (entry-avoidance rather than damage mitigation) and warns that aggregate mismatch metrics mix two very different phenomena. Practical takeaway for benchmark designers: divergence timing and post-divergence TV should be reported separately.
- **Red flags / weaknesses:** (1) Only two mid-size instruct models (7B/8B) and two selector families (SnapKV, recent-token) — no H2O, StreamingLLM-original, quantization, or heterogeneous-budget baselines, so generality of the exposure-dominance finding is untested. (2) All outcome measures are distributional (token mismatch / TV), never task quality (answer accuracy, ROUGE); the link from branch TV to user-visible degradation is asserted structurally but not demonstrated. (3) Horizon H=128 with temperature-1 full-softmax sampling only — greedy decoding, other temperatures, and longer horizons (where self-recovery might matter more) are unexamined. (4) The headline 85–90% exposure share comes from the exploratory first study; the confirmatory study covers only 90% retention, a single budget point. (5) R_SMC is defined under a specific coupling choice; the authors are careful about this (hence the partial-identification interval), but casual readers may mistake the mismatch fraction for a coupling-invariant quantity. (6) The finite-state validation uses only H=3 binary systems. (7) The local-agreement analysis (≥16 consecutive agreements in only ~2.6–7.6% of branches) suggests self-recovery is rare in this regime, but the paper does not test whether longer horizons or decoding interventions (e.g., restoration via re-prefill) could change that.

## Strengths

- A single clean identity (first mismatch + exposure × rate) that is exact, interpretable, and directly measurable — rare conceptual clarity for a systems-adjacent paper.
- The sharp partial-identification theorem is a rigorous answer to a real problem: finite observation windows genuinely cannot determine cumulative risk, and the paper says precisely how much remains unidentified.
- SWRB is a principled variance-reduction tool with a proven (not empirical-only) dominance guarantee, validated against exactly-enumerable systems.
- The two-clock analysis (generation position vs divergence-aligned lag) resolves an apparent paradox — narrowing aggregate gaps alongside rising within-branch TV — and the HELMET-vs-RULER sign flip (Table 2) is a concrete, memorable finding.
- Exceptional experimental hygiene: prespecification, dev/eval separation, family-wise correction, byte-matched budgets, zero-TV controls, and honest AI-use disclosure.
- Useful practical result: calibrated short-window (positions 1–32) predictors recover population-average gaps within ±2 pp, suggesting cheap monitoring proxies for deployment.

## Limitations

- Model and policy coverage is narrow: two instruct-tuned 7B/8B models; SnapKV vs recent-token retention only (plus fixed 512-entry SnapKV); no H2O/RazorAttention/Ada-KV/quantization comparisons.
- Findings are distributional, not task-level: no accuracy, retrieval scores, or human/LLM-judged output quality; the practical severity of a 90% branch TV is left unquantified.
- Short horizon (H=128) and temperature-1 full-softmax sampling only; EOS treated as absorbing; behavior under greedy/low-temperature decoding or long generations is unexplored.
- The confirmatory prespecified study exists only at 90% retention; the 50%-budget results (the more practically interesting regime) are exploratory with pointwise intervals.
- No public code or seeds mentioned; SWRB requires full-vocabulary distributions and forced-path rollouts, limiting easy adoption.
- The decomposition's mismatch rate C_H is exposure-weighted and coupling-specific; translating the framework to other couplings or sampling schemes requires redoing the analysis.

## 相关性

这篇论文对 KV-cache 压缩/高效推理方向有直接参考价值：它给出了衡量驱逐策略伤害的正确时间维度（分歧时点 vs 分歧后失配），提示我们在设计或评估缓存淘汰算法时应优先优化"推迟/避免首次分歧"而非期望分歧后自我恢复。其分支对齐评估协议也可迁移到 speculative decoding 一致性与 LLM 输出稳定性研究中。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does exposure dominance (85–90%) persist under greedy or low-temperature decoding, where the mismatch process is deterministic rather than Bernoulli-like, and over horizons beyond H=128?
- Can the divergence-timing lens rank other eviction families (H2O, RazorAttention, Ada-KV, CAKE, LookaheadKV) or quantized/low-rank caches (KIVI, Palu) on entry-avoidance rather than aggregate mismatch?
- Since post-divergence TV stays near 90–96%, is there any intervention that genuinely reduces C_H — e.g., periodic KV restoration, re-prefill on detected drift, or hybrid retention budgets — or is divergence entry effectively an absorbing failure state?
- How does branch-aligned TV map to task-level outcomes (HELMET accuracy, RULER retrieval scores)? A dose–response analysis linking TV trajectories to correctness would make the framework decision-relevant.
- The sharp interval [R_L, R_L + B_L] quantifies what a depth-L window cannot know: what is the empirically tightest L (observation budget) for which B_L becomes negligible under real LLM kernels, justifying short-window monitoring in production?
- Do speculative decoding acceptance rates, which also rely on stepwise maximal-coupling arguments, admit the same occurrence/exposure/rate decomposition for characterizing drafter–target disagreement?
