⚠ Critical edge

The rescue claim rests on one retained incident, not a controlled comparison — and the headline numbers are point estimates with no confidence intervals and no path to external replication.

01Methodology — protocol, not prompt

EvoPilot treats an online experiment as a protocol rather than a prompt. Each human-approved round names the shared base, pinned data, metric, closure rule, and one authorized contrast (control arm + treatment arm + the single allowed difference). Scoped agents materialize and execute the variants through a versioned retrieval domain skill — but the orchestrator and the verifier are deterministic programs, not agents.

plannerbase builder experiment workersanalyst repairmonitor

Once both arms terminate, the verifier checks pair-level eligibility — both arms succeeded, artifact quantities match spec, the realized difference equals the authorized treatment — and a human adjudicates metric semantics. Rejected measurements never enter the evidence ledger. Incidents (38 cataloged) become binding only when recoded as executable guards.

A(c,t;R) = VerifyR(c,t) ∧ ReviewR(c,t)fail-closed

Target system: Video Deep Dive, a two-tower dense retriever over an hourly-refreshed index of hundreds of millions of videos, with an optional interaction head re-scoring top candidates. Evaluation spans mutation tests, execution records, a 37-day field study, and a separate randomized online A/B.

Operating principle: "memory is advisory, only executable checks enforce."

02Results — beyond the headline

The incident that motivated it all

ArmDeclared (P, K)Realized depth
Control(3000, 600)K_eff = 3000

Both arms declared identical depth; the five-fold mismatch produced a spurious −22 pp drop blamed on the interaction head. An extreme-K probe (K=1 moved hit-rate ~0.5 pp pre-repair, ≈0 post-repair) confirmed, then validated, the fix.

The +4.8 pp re-test also changed the base model — it estimates a bundled configuration effect, not the head-only effect. The A/B validated the selected model (now the default config), not EvoPilot itself.

03Strengths

  • Evidence unit reframed — from "a completed run" to "an admitted comparison," enforced mechanically rather than through prompting.
  • Declared-vs-effective config checking catches silent invalidity (K_eff, evaluator revision, provenance) that run trackers miss — validated on a real incident with a falsification probe.
  • Clean separation of scientific results, invalid measurements, and operational failures.
  • Human-gated at the right granularity — approval for round n cannot authorize round n+1.
  • Production-scale longitudinal evidence: 37 days, hundreds of millions of videos, auditable control evolution.

04Limitations

  • Single-company, single-system case (Meta VDD); no public code, data, or skill artifacts — replication effectively impossible.
  • "Beats ungated agents" rests on one retained incident, not a controlled head-to-head experiment.
  • Only 9 of 20 rounds have complete operational records; cost figures exist for just two archived directories.
  • Ten mutation fixtures cannot establish field recall or false-block rates of the guards.
  • Offline readings are point estimates without CIs; the ±0.36 pp band is operational variation, not statistical uncertainty.
  • Metric-semantics drift still requires human review; no online guardrail metrics in the retained A/B dataset.

05Open questions

  • What are the field recall and false-block rates of the admission guards at scale — how often does fail-closed verification reject valid comparisons or admit subtle invalidity the 10 invariants miss?
  • Could the human review step be partially automated without reintroducing the trust problem the paper exists to eliminate?
  • How does evidence throughput compare against expert-led manual experimentation or an ungated agentic loop under a concurrent randomized protocol?
  • Do the durable-memory mechanisms change agent behavior measurably — is there an ablation for memory on/off?
  • Can declared-vs-effective configuration verification generalize to other multi-stage ML workflows (e.g., LLM training pipelines) where no-op edits, leakage, and evaluator drift are equally plausible?
