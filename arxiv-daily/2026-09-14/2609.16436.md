> **一句话总结：** 本文将 SAE 特征转向与线性探针转向引入基于 LLM 的社会科学模拟，在彩票/最后通牒博弈（风险、利他）与创造力任务上系统对比提示词方法：探针可将风险偏好切换点精确校准到目标值（平均误差约 2 tokens，范围 30–200），而在有外部人类评分示例的创造力任务上，强提示（few-shot+CoT，Δ=+1.48）反而超过 SAE 转向（brick 任务 Δ=−0.23）。

> **TL;DR:** The paper systematically compares prompt-based control vs. SAE feature steering vs. linear-probe direction steering for opening the black box of LLM agents in social simulations (Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct, 40 agents/condition, lottery + ultimatum games + brick/stapler creativity tasks). Probe steering delivers near-perfect calibrated control of risk preference (switching points placed across 30–200 tokens with ~2-token MAE vs. target, while all few-shot prompting doses leave it within 97–101 tokens and CoT even pushes it to ~140); but on the brick divergent-creativity task few-shot+CoT prompting from the human-rated Ocsai corpus (Δ=+1.48) clearly beats SAE steering (Δ=−0.23). The honest takeaway: interpretability methods win on calibrated, mechanism-level control, not universal behavioral dominance.

## 核心贡献

- **首个面向社会科学模拟的系统对比框架**：首次将三类"打开黑盒"的方法——（1）提示词操控（persona / CoT / few-shot / few-shot+CoT 剂量响应）、（2）SAE 特征转向、（3）线性探针方向转向——在相同的 Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct 骨干和任务集上做受控对比，覆盖人类行为的两大基元：偏好（风险、利他）与能力（发散创造力、产品创新）。
- **解释性（归纳工具）**：用 Goodfire 在第 50 层训练的 SAE（65,536 个特征）分解 agent 的内部激活，展示各任务下激活的 top 特征（如彩票任务的"基于概率的数值决策"、最后通牒的"博弈论合作与竞争"），既验证既有社会理论，也能启发新机制假设（如"俄罗斯语的砖块"这类语言学提示特征）。
- **可转向性（演绎工具）**：训练逻辑回归探针（第 48 层激活，留出集分类准确率 82%），将探针权重方向作为转向旋钮，实现对风险切换点的精确校准控制（平均绝对误差约 2 tokens）。
- **诚实的方法边界刻画**：明确指出优势取决于构念与提示强度——在风险任务上提示几乎无杠杆，而在利他与创造力任务上强提示本身是有力基线；并提出方法选择指南（SAE 适合归纳式机制发现，探针适合演绎式假设检验）。
- **严谨的评测协议**：创造力任务采用五个跨四家厂商的 LLM 裁判（GPT-5、Claude Sonnet 4.6、Gemini 2.5 Pro、Kimi K2.6、DeepSeek V4 Pro）盲评 + 长度控制评分细则，N=680 唯一响应，裁判间平均成对 Spearman 相关 0.769。
- **跨模型与跨物体鲁棒性检查**：探针管线在 Qwen2-7B-Instruct 上复现（留出准确率约 85% vs. Llama 约 92%），探针方向可跨物体迁移（性能下降约 5–6 个百分点）。

## Key Contributions

- **First systematic comparison for social simulation**: To the authors' knowledge, the first work to systematically compare and contrast three families of black-box-opening methods — (1) prompt-based manipulation (persona / zero-shot CoT / few-shot / few-shot+CoT with a 5-level dose-response sweep), (2) SAE-derived feature steering, and (3) probe-based direction steering — on the same Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct backbone, covering two primitives of human behavior: preferences (risk in the lottery game, altruism in the ultimatum game) and capabilities (divergent creativity on the brick AUT task, product innovation on the stapler task).
- **SAEs as an interpretability (inductive) instrument**: Uses Goodfire SAEs trained on layer 50 (65,536 features after filtering 3.5% harmful ones) to decompose agent activations into human-readable features. Top features recover established theory (probability-based decision-making in lotteries; game-theoretic cooperation/competition in ultimatum) and can inspire new hypotheses (e.g., "marketing language for writing instruments" activating in a stapler task suggests cross-product contextual association as a creativity mechanism).
- **Probes as a calibrated steering (deductive) instrument**: Trains an L2-regularized logistic-regression probe on layer-48 activations (selected by cross-validated layer sweep; 82% held-out accuracy on preference trials), then steers by adding a scaled unit-normalized probe direction to the hidden state. This achieves precision control of the risk switching point across 30–200 tokens with mean absolute error of ~2 tokens versus targets.
- **Honest boundary conditions**: Shows the advantage over prompting is construct-dependent — sharpest for risk (where prompting has almost no leverage), narrower for altruism (few-shot CoT itself yields a dose-dependent 10→40 token threshold shift), and reversed for creativity where human-rated few-shot exemplars exist.
- **Method-selection framework**: Contributes a comparative table (strengths / limitations / best-use cases) and a proposed full-cycle pipeline: SAEs to discover candidate mechanisms inductively, probes to test them deductively — the "genetically-modified mouse" analogy for generative agents.
- **Rigorous evaluation protocol**: Blind, length-controlled, five-judge panel spanning four developers for creativity scoring; documents per-judge length-confound regressions (Gemini R²=0.43 on log length) and shows the condition ordering survives dropping length-sensitive judges; replication on Qwen2-7B-Instruct and cross-object probe transfer tests.

## Methodology

All experiments run on **Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct** via the open-source **Expected Parrot** simulation platform, with 40 agents per condition and four tasks operationalized as natural-language interactions: lottery (safe 50 tokens vs. 50% chance of n), ultimatum game (accept/reject offers of n out of 100), divergent creativity (brick alternative uses), and product innovation (stapler improvements).

**Prompting ladder.** Conditions {baseline, persona, CoT, fewshot, fewshot_cot} with identical task instructions and decoding parameters. Few-shot uses N=6 demonstrations: for creativity, top-originality human responses from the Ocsai AUT corpus (10–50 human-rated scale), selected independently of the downstream judge to avoid circularity; for lottery/ultimatum, synthetic QA pairs over reward/offer grids disjoint from test sweeps. A dose-response sweep varies the fraction ρ ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0} of demonstrations taking the target action.

**SAE steering.** Goodfire SAEs trained on LMSYS Chat data at layer 50 of 80. The pipeline encodes the hidden state h_t into a sparse latent z = f_enc(h_t), modifies a selected feature z' = z + α·e_i (α ∈ [−1,1]), decodes back, and re-injects at layer ℓ. Interventions boost 2 risk-related features (lottery), 1 altruism feature (ultimatum), or 3 creativity features (both capability tasks); a high-temperature scenario (0.9–1.0) serves as a randomness-control condition.

**Probe steering.** Logistic-regression probes on layer-48 activations (final-token representation, z-scored features, CV-selected regularization). Training labels are programmatic: choices in preference games (risky vs. safe; accept vs. reject) traced as psychometric curves over the task parameter; for creativity, median split on GPT-5 judge scores. Steering adds λ·ŵ to the hidden state; λ is calibrated by a systematic search to hit target switching points.

**Evaluation.** Preference tasks measure switching points (P(risky)=0.5) and acceptance thresholds (P(accept)=0.5). Creativity is scored 1–10 by five independent judges on the four Torrance dimensions (fluency, flexibility, originality, elaboration), pooled and randomized so judges cannot identify conditions. Appendix adds invariants (identical prompts/decoding across conditions, no target leakage, seeded sampling), artifact logging (probe pickles, JSONL trial logs), and a full replication on Qwen2-7B-Instruct.

## Results & Highlights

- **Probe steering achieves near-perfect calibrated risk control (lottery game)**: switching points can be placed anywhere from approximately **30 to 200 tokens with a mean absolute error of about 2 tokens** versus targets; positive λ lowers the switching point (more risk-seeking), negative λ raises it. In contrast, **no prompting variant induces graded risk-seeking — the switching point stays within 97–101 tokens at every few-shot dose level (ρ = 0 to 1.0)**, and zero-shot CoT pushes it the wrong way to **~140 tokens** (more cautious), versus a baseline of ~125 tokens.
- **Prompting is all-or-nothing where SAE steering is dose-dependent**: on the lottery, the "barely risky" persona shows no effect while "slightly risky" produces extreme risk-seeking down at 20–25 tokens; SAE steering at activation strengths 0.6/0.4 yields a gradual transition starting ~75 tokens and 0.7/0.5 a sharper-but-controlled transition at ~65–70 tokens.
- **The prompting advantage reverses on altruism (ultimatum game)**: few-shot CoT delivers a genuine dose-dependent shift of the acceptance threshold **from roughly 10 to 40 tokens**, making strong prompting a competitive baseline there; a plain altruistic persona moves the baseline threshold (~30 tokens) down to ~10 tokens, and probe steering shifts thresholds systematically across 30–60 tokens with saturation at extreme λ.
- **On capability tasks, strong prompting beats SAE steering (brick divergent creativity)**: SAE steering does not improve over baseline (**Δ = −0.23** on the 5-judge mean), persona is flat (**Δ = +0.04**), high temperature slightly negative (**Δ = −0.10**); the prompting ladder produces much larger gains — **zero-shot CoT Δ = +1.52, few-shot+CoT Δ = +1.48, persona Δ = +0.49**. On stapler, SAE steering gives Δ = +0.45 vs. persona+CoT Δ = +0.59.
- **Probe steering gives partial but reliable creativity control**: the achieved GPT-5-judged score rises from **~4.0 at a target of 3 to ~8.2 at a target of 9**, with a below-unity target-vs-achieved slope indicating saturation; the brick-trained probe transfers out-of-sample to stapler/paperclip/bowl prompts (achieved scores 3.7–8.3) at a cost of ~5–6 percentage points in cross-object accuracy.
- **Judge agreement and confound controls**: mean pairwise Spearman across the five judges is **0.769 (N = 680 unique responses)**, ranging 0.675 (Claude–DeepSeek) to 0.849 (Gemini–GPT-5); the probe reaches **82% held-out classification accuracy** on preference trials; on Qwen2-7B-Instruct the probe pipeline replicates with ~85% in-distribution divergent-creativity accuracy versus ~92% for Llama.

## Authors & Credibility

The authors are from the Data Innovation and AI Lab (DIAL) at UC Berkeley (Jiayue Gaveal Fan — corresponding author; Arul Murugan; Shreyas Krishnan) and Abhishek Nagaraj, who holds a joint appointment at UC Berkeley and the **National Bureau of Economic Research (NBER)** — a credible team at the intersection of computational social science and AI. The work has been presented at the MIT BIG.AI conference and the NBER AI and Economic Measurement conference. The paper engages seriously with both the mechanistic-interpretability literature (Anthropic's towards-monosemanticity, Gemma Scope, refusal-direction work by Arditi et al.) and the LLM-social-simulation literature (Horton, Park, Argyle-lineage work), and correctly identifies the gap: neither SAEs nor probes had been systematically adapted to social-scientific simulation. The 70-page document includes an unusually thorough appendix with invariants, dose-response sweeps, per-judge breakdowns, length-confound regressions, a cross-model replication, and released artifacts (probe pickles, JSONL trial logs, figure scripts).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Genuine and well-positioned — the contribution is not a new interpretability technique but the first controlled head-to-head of prompting vs. SAE vs. probe steering for social-simulation constructs, plus a method-selection framework. Individual ingredients (Goodfire SAEs, activation-addition steering) are borrowed, which the paper is upfront about.
- **Rigor**: Above average for this genre. The graded prompting ladder (including a 5-level dose-response sweep that genuinely falsifies prompting for risk) is exactly the right control, and the five-judge blind protocol with length-regression diagnostics addresses the circularity that plagues LLM-judged creativity work. However, the "40 agents" are 40 sampled runs of the *same* model, not independent individuals — statistical power comes from sampling seeds, not between-agent variance, and the paper does not discuss this pseudo-replication issue.
- **Reproducibility**: Good on paper (seeds fixed per parameter/agent index, JSONL logs, probe artifacts, cross-model Qwen replication), but it depends on proprietary infrastructure throughout: Goodfire's SAEs (with Claude-based feature labeling), the Expected Parrot platform, and five commercial judge APIs — none fully open, so exact replication is hard for outsiders.
- **Significance**: The headline framing ("SAE- and probe-based techniques often outperform prompt-based methods") is softer than it sounds, and the paper deserves credit for showing where it *fails*: on the brick task SAE steering is *negative* (Δ=−0.23) and few-shot+CoT from the human-rated Ocsai corpus wins by ~1.7 points — a comparison that partly reflects access to external human data rather than steering vs. prompting per se (a confound acknowledged only obliquely). The stapler task, lacking such a corpus, omits the few-shot condition entirely, making the two capability tasks asymmetric comparisons.
- **Red flags / weaknesses**: (1) Which SAE features to boost is admittedly "more art than standardized practice" — feature selection is manual, inflating researcher degrees of freedom; (2) probe training labels for preferences come from the model's own choices (self-labelling), and the creativity probe is trained on GPT-5 median-split labels while GPT-5 is also on the judge panel — mitigated by the multi-judge mean, but a fully independent labeler would be cleaner; (3) only intermediate layers 48/50 of one 70B model are examined in the main text (adjacent layers for probe and SAE, with no main-text layer ablation); (4) SAE steering was not replicated on Qwen — only probes were — so the cross-model claim covers half the toolkit; (5) length still explains R²=0.43 of Gemini's scores despite the length-controlled rubric; (6) the "interpretable features" analysis is anecdotal (top-k lists, single-agent decompositions) with no quantitative validation of feature-behavior causal links beyond the steering results themselves.

## Strengths

- A genuinely controlled comparison design: same backbone, same tasks, a graded prompting ladder with dose-response sweeps, temperature controls to rule out randomness-as-creativity, and invariants documented (identical prompts/decoding across conditions, no target leakage into prompts).
- Quantified calibration quality that prompting cannot match: ~2-token MAE on switching-point targets spanning 30–200 tokens is a level of behavioral control few LLM-intervention papers demonstrate.
- Exceptional evaluative honesty — the paper reports its own method losing to prompting on creativity tasks and explicitly narrows its claims to "calibrated linear control + interpretability" rather than behavioral dominance.
- The SAE→probe two-stage pipeline (inductive feature discovery followed by deductive steering experiments) is a conceptually clean methodological template for computational social science.
- Strong evaluation hygiene for LLM-judged creativity: five judges across four developers, blinding, randomization, per-judge confound regressions, and demonstration of robustness to dropping length-sensitive judges.

## Limitations

- Individual-level scope only: no multi-agent interaction, networks, or emergent group dynamics — the authors flag this as future work, but it means the "social simulation" claim is so far limited to isolated behavioral tasks.
- Steering saturation: probe control of creativity has a below-unity achieved-vs-target slope and ceiling effects; SAE over-steering causes hallucination and off-target activation because features are not perfectly disentangled.
- Single primary model (Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct): the Qwen2-7B check covers probes only; SAE steering, layer choices, and the prompting-ladder interactions are not established to generalize across model families or scales.
- External dependencies: Goodfire SAEs, Expected Parrot, and commercial judge APIs limit independent replication; techniques also require activation access, ruling out API-only frontier models.
- Small trait set: risk, altruism, and two creativity operationalizations map to simple behavioral constructs; more abstract, context-dependent social mechanisms (norms, identity, institutions) are untested.
- Few-shot condition omitted for the stapler task (no human-rated corpus), so capability-task comparisons are asymmetric between tasks; SAE feature selection remains manual and un-standardized.

## Relevance

与"LLM Hidden States & Interpretability"方向高度相关：这是把 SAE 特征转向与探针方向转向系统性应用于 agent 行为控制与机制解释的工作，涵盖稀疏自编码器、线性探针、激活干预与表征几何等核心技术，并提供了探针校准精度（~2 token MAE）与跨层/跨模型选择的实证细节。对构建自演化 agent 的可靠控制与可解释干预（Self-Evolving Agents 方向的可信性与可控性议题）也有直接方法论参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the manual SAE feature-selection step be automated (e.g., causal feature-attribution or automated circuit discovery) so that steering interventions become standardized rather than "art"?
- Does the prompt-ladder vs. steering hierarchy hold on frontier reasoning models and on closed API models where activation access is unavailable — and can black-box approximation of probe directions (e.g., via representation reading) close the gap?
- Would multi-probe or low-rank steering directions recover the missing control range for creativity (the below-unity slope), given that fluency/flexibility/originality/elaboration may not collapse onto one linear direction?
- How do SAE/probe interventions interact with multi-agent settings — does a steered trait in one agent propagate through interaction (social influence, network effects), as the paper's future-work agenda suggests?
- Can the ~2-token calibration MAE for risk preferences be preserved under distribution shift (different payoff framings, losses vs. gains, or real-stakes human validation data), and does the probe direction remain stable after model updates or quantization?
- Is the finding that few-shot human exemplars beat steering on creativity an artifact of external human data access — what happens when steering and few-shot are given matched information budgets?
