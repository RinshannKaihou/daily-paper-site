Key metric — belief decodability

0.83–0.99peak held-out probe R²

OLS linear probes recover the exact Bayesian belief state η(x₁:ₜ) from residual-stream activations — 6 LLMs × 40 HMMs, early-to-late layers. Shuffled-belief and random-belief controls: R² ≈ 0.

Why this matters

A rare theory → representation → causal-confirmation pipeline for reading hidden states — a methodological template for interpretability and internal-state monitoring.

「理论预测 → 表示验证 → 因果确认」的研究范式，对内部表示分析与基于内部状态的监控、异常检测均有直接借鉴意义。

Critical edge

The 40 HMMs were selected partly because LLMs predict them well — and every process emits only 2–3 token types, about as far from natural text as input can get.

Critical Assessment · Limitations

Methodology

方法论

Six open-weight LLMs read 20,000-token sequences from 40 hand-designed edge-emitting HMMs in four families (Mess3, Arch, Wing, Strata — 30 newly designed), tuned to the entropy/mixing regime where LLMs are known to predict well. Four instruments, applied in order:

  1. 1
    Behavioral. KL divergence of the model's next-token predictions vs. the HMM ground truth, benchmarked against the family of optimal order-k suffix predictors (0-HMM and 1-HMM baselines).
  2. 2
    Representational. OLS linear probes regress layer-wise residual-stream activations onto the exact belief state η(x₁:ₜ) — 20/80 train/test split per sequence — against shuffled-belief, random-belief, and cross-parametrization controls.
  3. 3
    Causal. Steering adds emb(target) − emb(current) to activations; patching overwrites the last k ∈ {1, 5, 10} tokens. Success = predictions move toward the injected belief; random-belief and random-direction controls must not.
  4. 4
    Early-exit. A tuned lens per layer correlates layer-wise decodability with layer-wise prediction quality.

  • Near-optimal behavior. Qwen3.5-9B converges to 0.009–0.018 nats KL to the ground-truth next-token distribution — matching the optimal order-k HMM predictor at k = 4.2–11.0, far below the 0-HMM and 1-HMM baselines. KL falls over the first 5–10k tokens, then plateaus.
  • Scale sensitivity. The smallest model, Gemma-4-E2B, is no more accurate than the 1-HMM baseline on 6/10 Wing and 4/10 Strata parametrizations — belief-state encoding attenuates at small scale.
  • Causal override. After patching the belief subspace over the last k ∈ {1, 5, 10} positions, final-token KL to the injected target's ground truth drops to the order of the unmodified model's KL; the random-belief control shows no drop. By k = 10, injected beliefs largely override conflicting context.
  • Early-exit KL co-varies with 1 − R² of the belief probe — decodability and prediction quality move together, layer by layer.

Strengths

  • Theory-derived, falsifiable predictions tested on production open models — a rare template for interpretability research.
  • Exceptional control battery: every decodability claim is paired with both an empirical control and an activation-free theoretical counterpart, correlated quantitatively.
  • Causality done the hard way: past-inconsistent targets and the NTP-preserving δ ∈ Ker(Mᵀ) steer separate belief-subspace effects from generic perturbation.
  • Honest negatives (E2B failures, Table 3 no-gap parametrizations, Mess3 degeneracy); appendices cover all 40 × 6 combinations, keeping cherry-picking risk low.

Limitations

  • Narrow stimulus class: 2–3-token alphabets, 3–4 hidden states, low entropy, slow mixing — chosen partly for LLM predictability.
  • Low-dimensional belief geometry only (2–4 simplex); nothing established for high-dimensional or structureless cases.
  • Per-sequence probes: 1,000 training tokens vs. d = 4,096 activations — spurious-linear-structure risk acknowledged, no unsupervised/SAE corroboration yet.
  • Causality caveats: steering results exclude the Gemma class and rest on qualitative heatmap readings; other candidate features are only partially ruled out.
  • No scaling-law analysis, no natural-text bridge, and no code release mentioned in the main text.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Q1Does belief geometry survive higher-entropy, faster-mixing, larger-alphabet HMMs — and is there a clean scaling law in HMM complexity?
  2. Q2Can the cross-parametrization transfer matrices decode which parametrization the model has inferred — an explicit "task vector" for process identification?
  3. Q3Can the belief subspace be recovered unsupervised (SAEs, PCA on activations), answering the spurious-linear-structure concern?
  4. Q4Does the same geometry persist at 100k+ tokens, or under context compression (KV-cache eviction, recurrent memory)?
  5. Q5Which circuit implements self-repair of erased early-layer steers — is belief-writing localized to specific attention heads?
