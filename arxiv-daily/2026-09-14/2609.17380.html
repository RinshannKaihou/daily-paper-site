— Critical Assessment · “Rigor”

01Methodology — three orders imposed on nondeterminism

The claim: bitwise reproducibility — not same-machine determinism — requires imposing a definite order on all three sources of training nondeterminism.

R1
RepOps kernel arithmetic

One fixed reduction order on every backend (GEMMs, norms, loss sums); a single forced FMA convention; subnormals flushed to zero everywhere, matching the least permissive hardware (Apple GPUs); counter-based RNG where the i-th draw is a pure function of (seed, i) — independent of device, shape, and parallelization.

R2
Topologically invariant data stream

The corpus is pre-committed to a canonical sequence of 4,097-token packed windows determined by three seeded RNGs — independent of world size and rank. Rank r owns window m iff m mod R = r, so a single device replays the cluster’s exact batch order; elastic re-sharding and exact resume come free.

R3
Deterministic collectives

NCCL’s ring all-reduce can’t be replayed on one device (naive sum matches only to ~1e-11 relative). Replaced by a recursive-doubling butterfly all-reduce, a fixed ascending-rank reduce-scatter inside FSDP2, and division implemented as multiply-by-precomputed-reciprocal to avoid CPU/CUDA divergence.

Audit replay (Algorithm 1) sequentializes the three gradient-reduction levels; a TreeFold binary-carry stack reproduces the cluster’s pairing order in O(log R) memory. Every step is anchored by a host-side blake2b sharded state hash. Whole-run verification is collective: independent auditors each certify individual steps.

Recipe: 24-layer decoder, GQA 16/4, 512-token sliding-window attention (every 5th layer full), native int8 W8A8 QAT with per-channel LSQ scales re-pinned each step; AdamW, single seed 42; 400B tokens on 48×H100 over Open-1B Mix 0626 (450B tokens, 4 sources).

Failure mode 01 · undocumented in prior QAT literatureQuantized flash-attention backward returned ∇Q = 0

int8 backward silently produced all-zero gradients for Q, so QK-norm gains grew monotonically — a slow gradient creep that could pass unnoticed.

Fix: a BR-respecting gradient scale factor plus removing the QK-norm gain from the architecture.

Failure mode 02 · undocumented in prior QAT literatureLSQ step sizes drift and self-reinforce

Learned quantization step sizes diverged under AdamW during long pretraining — block-0 gradient norm > 10³ after ~2,000 steps (pre-clip norm passed 300 before step 2,500).

Fix: re-pin per-channel scales from the LSQ init formula every step — ≈2% step-time cost, vs ~70% to disable quantization.

Strengths
  • Turns a philosophical complaint — “open source isn’t verifiable” — into a precise, shipped property, sweating the details that usually break bit-exactness: FMA fusion, subnormal handling, RNG layout, division lowering.
  • The topologically invariant data stream is an elegant decoupling: token order as a pure function of the seed enables elastic re-sharding, exact resume, the int8-vs-bf16 controlled comparison, and single-device replay all at once.
  • Exemplary failure forensics: the LSQ drift diagnosis eliminates alternatives by direct substitution, and explains why QAT literature missed it (LSQ was designed for short fine-tunes).
  • Honest accounting — 5× slower, 5% MFU, 71% scaling efficiency — and an honest benchmark table where the model loses; per-task reproduction of OLMo 2’s scores validates the eval pipeline.
Limitations
  • The ≈5× reproducibility tax confined the run to 400B tokens — the artifact demonstrates the audit regime, not competitive 1B quality.
  • int8-vs-bf16 cost quantified only as a ≈0.2-nat train-CE gap; no token-matched downstream comparison.
  • The collective audit scheme — the piece that makes whole-run verification feasible — is sketched, not evaluated: no coverage statistics, no adversarial analysis.
  • Single run, single seed (42), one scale; overhead unmeasured beyond 6 nodes; base model only; no cross-source dedup between DCLM-Baseline and FineWeb-Edu.

03Questions for further exploration

  1. Move the per-step blake2b hash from host to device — how close to the 86% hash-off scaling ceiling, and what does the cost profile look like at 64–512 nodes?
  2. Does the ≈0.2-nat int8-vs-bf16 gap close or widen with longer budgets and larger models — and what downstream delta does it actually cause?
  3. Does per-step LSQ re-pinning generalize to weight-only int4 or rotation-based schemes (QuaRot, SpinQuant) — and can it be proven non-divergent under AdamW?
  4. What fraction of steps must independent auditors certify, under what sampling distribution, to bound the probability of an undetected k-sample backdoor?
  5. Can the trust anchor be weakened without genesis replay — e.g., a checkpoint-chained Merkle structure trusting only O(steps/100) hashes?
  6. Would a staking-style economic design produce honest-majority audit coverage — or reintroduce the trust assumptions the audit was meant to remove?
