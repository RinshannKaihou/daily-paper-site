> **一句话总结：** 提出 Gavel——在冻结的 agent LLM 上只训练两个线性投影（共 7.9M 参数），从中间层隐状态读出"glance"信号全库粗排、再以模型自身的似然与 yes/no 判断做"verdict"精排（乘积专家融合），无需把任何技能文本放进上下文，在 SRA-Bench 上比 Qwen3-8B 检索重排流水线高 13.4 个 Hit@1 点，并让 Qwen3-32B 在 Skill-Use 上的正确触发率达 0.909、超过 Codex 中的大型前沿模型。

> **TL;DR:** Gavel shows a frozen agent LLM (Qwen3-32B) already carries the skill-routing signal in its own forward passes: two trained linear maps (7.9M params, trained once on SkillRet) read a mid-layer "glance" over the whole library, and a "verdict" re-scores the shortlist via the model's task log-likelihood and yes/no log-odds, fused as a product of experts. Zero-shot, it beats retrieve-and-rerank pipelines adding 1.2B–16B external parameters (e.g., +13.4 adjudicated Hit@1 over Qwen3-Emb-8B+Reranker-8B on SRA-Bench's 26K-skill library; .9460 vs .9077 for SkillRouter on SkillRet test) and leads by 8.6–21.9 points on the new mid-rollout benchmark SkillTraj; in a mini-swe-agent harness it lifts Qwen3-32B's correct-skill trigger rate on Skill-Use from .011 (progressive disclosure) to .909, above MiniMax-M3 in Codex (.864).

## 核心贡献

- **提出 Gavel 路由器**：完全从冻结的 agent LLM 自身前向传播中读取技能选择信号——不需要上下文里出现任何技能文本、不需要 agent 之外的第二模型，只训练两个线性投影（768×5120，共 7.9M 参数），随骨干模型升级免费变强。
- **Glance 阶段（全库粗排）**：在"压缩谷底"层（矩阵熵最低、约 70% 深度处，Qwen3-32B 为第 44 层）嫁接一个新注意力头——查询映射读任务 token 状态、键映射读技能 token 状态，对比训练；每个任务 token 只对 top-k 技能投票（late-interaction 式 max 相似度），新增技能只需一次前向传播建键库、零训练。
- **ε-cover 键库压缩**：用最远优先遍历把每技能键库压缩约 8.5 倍（ε=0.83），并证明压缩对打分的扰动有严格上界（命题 1：每个分数至多降低 ε 且不会抬高；推论 1 给出投票稳定性），实测任何基准上损失不超过 1.6 点。
- **Verdict 阶段（短名单精排）与乘积专家融合**：对约 9 个候选续跑前向，读任务平均对数似然 L（生成式）与 yes/no 对数几率 V（判别式），与 glance 分数 g 均可视为同一 log 后验的估计，按 S = g + αL + γV 乘积专家融合，任一专家可否决。
- **新基准 SkillTraj**：372 条模拟多轮 agent 轨迹，覆盖用户直述（116）、工具证据（106）、agent 自写计划（105）、错技能恢复（45）四种"技能需求何时出现"的场景，专门评测 rollout 中途的路由能力。
- **端到端部署验证**：把 Gavel 集成进 mini-swe-agent，训练一个线性门控决定何时发起路由，冻结的 Qwen3-32B 在 Skill-Use 上的正确触发率达 0.909，超过 Codex 中所有开源前沿模型及两条检索重排流水线。

## Key Contributions

- **Gavel, a skill router read out of the frozen agent LLM itself**: no skill text enters the context until a skill is chosen, no external retriever/reranker sits beside the agent, and only two linear projections (7.9M parameters total) are trained — so routing accuracy improves as the backbone does.
- **Token-level glance over the full library**: a grafted attention head (query map Wq, key map Ws) reads one mid-layer — located unsupervised at the matrix-entropy floor of the compression valley (~70% depth, block 45/64 in Qwen3-32B) — with per-token max-similarity voting over top-k skills; installing a new skill costs a single forward pass and no training.
- **ε-cover bank compression with guarantees**: farthest-first traversal thins each skill's key bank ~8.5× at ε=0.83; Proposition 1 proves scores drop by at most ε and never inflate, Corollary 1 bounds vote distortion, and the empirical cost is ≤1.6 points on any benchmark.
- **Verdict from resumed forward passes, fused as a product of experts**: for ~9 shortlisted candidates, the model scores the task's mean log-likelihood L (generative read of log p(s|x)) and its yes/no log-odds V (discriminative read); combined with the glance g (contrastive read) as S = g + αL + γV, any single expert can veto a candidate the other two tolerate.
- **SkillTraj benchmark**: 372 simulated multi-turn trajectories scoring routers at the moment a skill becomes needed, across four scenarios (user request, tool evidence, agent plan, wrong-skill recovery), with blind-judge admission, verbatim-anchor tokenization checks, and leakage screens.
- **Live-harness integration**: a lightweight gate (linear classifier over glance scores + final-layer state) decides when to route in mini-swe-agent; frozen Qwen3-32B reaches a 0.909 correct-trigger rate on Skill-Use, beating frontier models in Codex and both retrieve-and-rerank baselines.

## Methodology

Gavel routes in two stages, everything drawn from the frozen backbone M (Qwen3-32B in the main build; rebuilt end-to-end on Gemma-4-31B in Appendix E).

**Glance.** Since native read-outs fail (Appendix A: the best of 24 configurations of native attention keys reaches Hit@1 .001 on a 10,123-skill validation library vs .918 for the trained head — the signal exists but is one to two orders of magnitude too faint), the glance grafts a new attention head: Wq maps each task token's layer-44 state to a query, Ws maps each skill token's state (from one installation-time forward pass over a fixed render) to a key bank. Trained contrastively (multi-positive InfoNCE, τ=40) on 51,104 SkillRet training queries over 9,084 skills; gradients stop at the hidden states so the backbone stays frozen. At routing time each task token votes only for its top-k skills (k = min(10, max(3, 0.1N))) under max-similarity, aggregated with decay weights (uniform for written tasks; recency-decayed 2^(-a/64) mid-rollout), so a few decisive tokens are not averaged away. Banks are compressed to ε-covers (farthest-first traversal; covering/packing-number size bounds in Proposition 2), after which Wq alone is briefly fine-tuned against the compressed banks.

**Verdict.** For candidates within margin Δ=0.133 of the glance's best (~9 on average), the installation render r(s) is continued with the task appended (query-likelihood order, reusable KV prefix). One causal forward pass yields both L (mean log-likelihood of the task given the skill prefix) and V (log-odds of yes over no spellings at a final fixed question "Does this skill provide what that task needs?").

**Ruling.** With a uniform prior, g, L, and V each estimate the same log posterior log p(s|x) — contrastively, generatively, discriminatively — so they fuse as a product of experts: S = g + αL + γV with (α, γ) = (1.0, 0.025) calibrated once on SkillRet validation. The winner is loaded; nothing loads if V < 0 in deployment.

**Evaluation protocol.** Hit@1 over the full library, adjudicated by GPT-5.6 Sol (both orders, credited when the committed skill wins at least as often as it loses against all golds) because all three public benchmarks have under-annotated golds (e.g., verbatim duplicate skills in Eval-Core's 78K pool); raw Hit@1 is reported alongside, human agreement with the judge is 86% on 200 pairs, and in every column raw Gavel also ranks first.

## Results & Highlights

- **SRA-Bench (861-task stratified sample, 26,262 skills)**: full Gavel scores adjudicated Hit@1 .8734 (raw .8223) vs the strongest baseline Qwen3-Emb-8B + Qwen3-Reranker-8B at .7398 (raw .6458) — a +13.4-point margin over the 16B-parameter pipeline using only 7.9M trained parameters; the glance alone (.6957 adj.) is already the strongest retrieval stage, with R@20 .9675 vs Qwen3-Emb-8B's .9117.
- **SkillRet test (4,997 queries, 6,660 held-out skills)**: Gavel reaches .9460 adjudicated / .8459 raw vs SkillRouter-Emb-0.6B+RR-0.6B at .9077 / .8301 — +3.8 adjudicated points (Gavel is partially in-domain here; the trained 0.6B embedders match the glance .8797 vs .8725 at the retrieval stage).
- **Eval-Core (75 task.md queries, 78K documents)**: Gavel scores .9467 adj. (easy pool) and .9333 (hard pool with 780 adversarial distractors) vs the 8B pipeline's .9200/.9200 — +1.3 to +2.7 points, though with only 75 queries this is a margin of one to three queries.
- **SkillTraj (372 mid-rollout trajectories)**: Gavel leads every scenario by 8.6–21.9 points over the strongest other system — adjudicated .9655 (user request), .7264 (tool evidence), .9048 (agent plan), .8889 (wrong-skill recovery); the best 8B pipeline manages .8793/.5094/.6857/.6667, and SkillRouter-Emb-0.6B collapses to .1226 adj. on tool evidence with full context.
- **Skill-Use end-to-end (177 executable tasks, 79-skill library, mini-swe-agent)**: Qwen3-32B + Gavel triggers the correct skill on 90.9% of tasks vs .011 for the same model under progressive disclosure, .897 for Qwen3-Emb-8B+Reranker-8B, and frontier numbers in Codex of .864 (MiniMax-M3), .706 (GLM-5.1), .684 (Qwen3.6-Max), .650 (DeepSeek-V4-Pro).
- **Scaling with backbone (SRA-Bench)**: Gavel on a 0.6B backbone already beats the 32B model under progressive disclosure; on Qwen3.8-27B the menu-picking baseline still trails Gavel on the same backbone by 12.2 points; routing accuracy climbs monotonically from 0.6B to 32B and across Qwen generations.
- **Ablations**: replacing the glance with Jina-ColBERT-v2 (a trained late-interaction retriever) loses on every benchmark (e.g., raw .5968 vs .8459 on SkillRet), showing the win comes from the agent LLM's representation, not token-level scoring; committing to the verdict's judgment alone (pointwise rerank) drops raw Hit@1 to .4068 on SkillRet; dropping one verdict signal costs 1.9–4.6 adjudicated points; the ε-cover compression costs ≤1.6 points for 8.5× smaller banks.
- **Cost**: routing costs ~$0.080 per gate firing (nine ~2.2K-token verdict prefills), independent of library size n and session length T, vs progressive disclosure's ~$4×10⁻⁵·n·T; Gavel is cheaper once the library exceeds roughly 2000·m/T skills, and its verdict forwards never occupy the rollout's context window.

## Authors & Credibility

The five authors (Ruishuo Chen, Xun Wang, Yu Chen, Zhuoran Li, and corresponding author Longbo Huang) are from the Institute for Interdisciplinary Information Sciences (IIIS) at Tsinghua University, a group with a strong track record in RL, systems, and learning theory. The paper is unusually self-scrutinizing for a systems/empirical paper: Appendix A documents three failed "more native" alternatives (native attention keys, native initialization, trained retrieval tokens); Appendix D proves the ε-cover distortion and vote-stability bounds; Appendix E rebuilds the whole pipeline on Gemma-4-31B (re-selecting depth, retraining projections, recalibrating ε and (α, γ)) and the shape survives; Appendix H pairs every adjudicated score with its raw score and validates the LLM judge against blind human annotation (86% agreement, 171/200 pairs). Prompt templates are reproduced verbatim in Appendix G. No code release is mentioned in the version read.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: genuinely fresh framing. ToolkenGPT trains one embedding per tool (a new tool costs a training run) and GRIT fine-tunes the whole backbone; Gavel is the first to argue the frozen agent LLM's own mid-layer states contain a usable routing signal and to elicit it with 7.9M trained parameters while keeping the context clean. The product-of-experts fusion — reading the same log posterior contrastively (glance), generatively (likelihood), and discriminatively (judgment) — is an elegant unification of probing, late-interaction retrieval, query-likelihood ranking, and yes/no rerankers.
- **Rigor**: strong. Deterministic zero-shot protocol (projections and four scalars fixed after SkillRet validation, everything else untouched); dense ablations that isolate each stage; a proof-backed compression scheme; raw scores reported beside adjudicated ones everywhere, with the key finding (Gavel first in every column) holding under both.
- **Reproducibility**: moderate-to-good. Backbones, training recipe (τ=40, AdamW, 24K steps, token budgets), prompts, and all hyperparameters are specified, and the method needs only public components (Qwen3-32B, SkillRet) — but no code is released, the adjudication depends on GPT-5.6 Sol access, SkillTraj's construction depends on GPT-5.6 Sol plus Claude Sonnet 4.6, and the exact 861-task SRA-Bench stratified sample is not obviously reconstructible.
- **Significance**: addresses a real, current deployment problem (Claude Code and Codex route skills by progressive disclosure, which caps libraries — Codex truncates skill metadata at 2% of context — while retrieval stacks decouple selection from the agent's capability). The "router improves as the backbone does" result and the near-zero trigger rate fix (.011 → .909) are practically important.
- **Red flags**: (1) The headline metric is LLM-adjudicated Hit@1; adjudication lifts baselines more than Gavel (progressive disclosure gains +21.1 on SkillRet vs Gavel's +10.0), and the judge leans generous by 9 pairs in 200 versus humans — although raw scores order systems identically, so the conclusion survives. (2) The Skill-Use frontier comparison is not apples-to-apples: frontier numbers are quoted from the Skill-Use paper (Codex harness), while Gavel runs in mini-swe-agent; the .011 progressive-disclosure baseline mostly reflects Qwen3-32B predating the skills ecosystem (forcing the read raises it to .834), which the authors disclose but which also frames the headline "beats far larger frontier models". (3) SkillTraj is self-constructed and simulated (GPT-5.6 Sol trajectories, Claude admission judge) — carefully screened, but real rollout data would be more convincing, and the 45-trajectory wrong-skill-recovery cell is small. (4) SkillRet is the training corpus, so the +3.8 there is partially in-domain; the out-of-domain wins (SRA-Bench, SkillTraj) are the load-bearing evidence. (5) The method needs hidden-state access, ruling out closed-weight APIs without provider adoption (Appendix K sketches a hosted-router path). (6) Eval-Core margins are one-to-three queries on 75 tasks — noise-level.

## Strengths

- Solves the trilemma (clean context, no external model, no per-skill training) with a minimal trained footprint: 7.9M parameters and four scalars, transferred zero-shot to every other library.
- The ε-cover compression is not just an engineering trick but comes with proved distortion and bank-size guarantees (covering/packing numbers), a rare rigor in retrieval engineering.
- Insightful negative results: native attention keys carry the routing signal but too faintly (Hit@1 .001 vs .918), which precisely motivates trained projections; the retrieval-token failure is attributed to data (recency is a sufficient policy on clean single-turn data), not the idea.
- The verdict's off-path prefills keep the rollout context untouched and the skill prefixes KV-cacheable, with a concrete cost model (Appendix J) rather than hand-waving.
- SkillTraj's four scenarios isolate exactly where mid-rollout routing is hard (tool evidence, agent plan) and expose both failure modes of pipelines (embedding the full noisy context vs only the last message).

## Limitations

- Requires white-box access to mid-layer hidden states of the agent backbone; behind a cloud API the provider must host the router (Appendix K), which is speculative.
- The verdict adds ~9 forward passes of ~2.2K tokens (~0.5 s, ~$0.08) per routing attempt — cheap per firing, but the gate's false-positive rate directly multiplies this cost, and the gate itself was trained on only 372 SkillTraj trajectories and not engineered further.
- The glance's vote uses a hand-crafted recency decay; the learned alternative (trained retrieval tokens) failed for data reasons and is left to future work.
- Per-benchmark signal utility varies: the likelihood L helps SkillRet (+5.4), SRA-Bench (+12.0), and SkillTraj (+8.1) but slightly hurts both Eval-Core pools (−4 queries each), so the fixed (α, γ) is a compromise.
- Evaluation depends on LLM judges for both scoring (GPT-5.6 Sol) and SkillTraj admission (Claude Sonnet 4.6); human checks exist but are limited to 200 pairs.
- Only single-skill commits are scored (Hit@1 of one skill); routing multiple skills per task, and routing tools/memories/MCP servers, remain open.

## Relevance

与「LLM agent 技能路由 / 工具调用 / 冻结模型能力挖掘」方向高度相关：它证明了冻结 agent LLM 的中间层表征已含技能选择信号，7.9M 线性探针即可读出，同时解决上下文污染与外挂检索器两大痛点，对构建大规模技能库的 agent 系统与"模型内在能力探测"研究都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the same two-projection read-out route tools, memories, or MCP servers from the agent's forward passes, as the conclusion conjectures — and does the mid-layer choice (entropy floor) transfer to those sparser-signature domains?
- How does the glance scale to libraries of 10⁵+ skills — does the ε-cover bank sweep stay millisecond-scale, and does the k = 0.1N voting heuristic saturate?
- Can the hand-crafted recency decay w_i be replaced by learned aggregation once trained on data that rewards selectivity (Appendix A attributes the failure to data, not the idea)?
- Would the gate fire reliably on real (non-simulated) rollouts, e.g., actual SWE-agent sessions, where skill needs arise less cleanly than in SkillTraj's GPT-written trajectories?
- Is the product-of-experts fusion robust when the verdict's two signals disagree systematically (as on Eval-Core, where L hurts) — could per-domain calibration of (α, γ) or an abstention head recover those queries?
- What happens when the agent backbone is served quantized (INT8/INT4) — do the mid-layer states at the compression-valley floor remain linearly readable?
