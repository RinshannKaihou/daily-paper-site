> **一句话总结：** 该论文证明 SAE 解码器空间的几何性质（近邻密度和最大余弦相似度）——可在任何干预前直接从权重矩阵计算——能够提前对特征按"转向难度"排序：几何上拥挤的拒绝相关特征用更小的转向系数即可达到固定行为阈值（Gemma-2-9B 上 Spearman ρ 最高达 −0.546，AUROC 0.822），该信号跨模型规模、SAE 宽度和架构（Llama-3.1-8B）复现，但在深层比例深度和 BatchTopK 训练的 Qwen3-8B 上失效。

> **TL;DR:** The authors formalize SAE feature steerability as the minimum steering coefficient α*(f) needed to shift refusal rate by a fixed threshold (T = 0.10) on 100 SALADBench prompts, then show that two geometry metrics computed from the SAE decoder weight matrix before any intervention — neighbor density (mean cosine similarity to k = 50 nearest decoder directions) and max cosine similarity — rank-order log α* across Gemma-2-2B/9B (GemmaScope 16K/65K SAEs), with the strongest result at Gemma-2-9B layer 20 (Spearman ρ = −0.546, p < 10⁻⁶; AUROC 0.822 vs. permutation-null ρ₉₅ = 0.197), and a weaker but significant cross-architecture replication on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (max cosine ρ = −0.266, n = 300, 0% censoring). The signal is rank-based only (ridge CV R² ≈ 0–0.07), collapses at deep proportional layer depth (9B layer 36: ρ drops to −0.327 with 46% censoring; 2B layer 24: full coherence collapse), and on Qwen3-8B with BatchTopK SAEs predicts only binary steerability, not continuous ordering (uncensored ρ ≈ 0.01) — a boundary condition tied to SAE training regime. Co-activation correlation contributes no reliable independent signal. Useful if you care about pre-screening SAE features for controllability; not yet a practical coefficient-prediction tool.

## 核心贡献

- **形式化 SAE 特征可转向性**：将可转向性定义为达到预注册行为阈值（拒绝率绝对提升 T = 0.10，在 100 条 SALADBench 提示上测量）所需的最小转向系数 α*(f)，未达阈值的特征在 α_max 处右删失，并配套连贯性过滤器剔除退化输出（<5 token、连续重复 >15 次、困惑度超 e^5）。
- **发现干预前的几何预测因子**：解码器空间的近邻密度（与 k=50 最近解码方向的平均余弦相似度）和最大余弦相似度——均可在零次前向传播前从 SAE 权重矩阵算出——在所有"可良好识别"的条件下一致地对 log α*(f) 排序（Spearman ρ 最高 −0.546，AUROC 0.610–0.822），且效应量在 9B 上强于 2B（同层 ρ 从 −0.288 升至 −0.546）。
- **跨条件与跨架构的系统检验**：信号在两个 Gemma-2 规模（2B/9B）、两种 SAE 宽度（16K/65K，65K 下 ρ = −0.435 依然稳健，说明是相对拥挤度而非绝对几何）以及 Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct（ρ = −0.266，n = 300）上复现；同时明确刻画了两条边界：深层比例深度处信号坍缩（9B L36 删失率 46%），BatchTopK 训练的 Qwen3-8B 上仅剩二元预测（未删失 ρ ≈ 0.01）。
- **机制证据与阴性结果**：易转向特征的 50 个近邻与平均拒绝方向的对齐度约为难转向特征的 3.4 倍（0.129 vs 0.038，ρ = −0.450），支持"邻近方向协同招募"机制；共激活相关性在 5/6 条件下为零效应（表面显著实为零膨胀伪影：9B L20 的 75 个特征中 59 个在参考语料上从不激活），排除了这一看似合理的备选预测因子。

## Key Contributions

- **Formalize steerability**: define feature steerability as the minimum steering coefficient α*(f) that produces a pre-registered behavioral change (absolute refusal-rate shift T = 0.10, measured over 100 frozen SALADBench prompts, seed 42), with right-censoring at α_max and a degenerate-completion coherence filter (token collapse, repetition collapse, perplexity > e^5 under the unsteered model; ~24% of rows filtered at layer 20).
- **Identify pre-steering geometric predictors**: neighbor density (mean cosine similarity to the k = 50 nearest decoder directions) and max cosine similarity — both computable from W_dec with zero forward passes — consistently rank-order log α*(f) in every well-identified condition (Spearman ρ up to −0.546, p = 5.75×10⁻⁷; AUROC 0.610–0.822), with the effect stronger at 9B than 2B at matched layer index.
- **Systematic multi-condition validation with honest boundary mapping**: replication across two Gemma-2 scales, two SAE widths (the 65K replication shows relative crowding, not absolute geometry, carries the signal: ρ = −0.435 despite higher absolute cosines, max-cosine mean 0.547 vs 0.392 at 16K), and cross-architecturally on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct; plus two boundary conditions — signal collapse at deep proportional depth (2B L24 coherence collapse; 9B L36 46% censoring) and binary-only prediction on Qwen3-8B BatchTopK SAEs.
- **Mechanistic support and a clean negative result**: easy-to-steer features have decoder neighbors ~3.4× more aligned with the mean refusal direction (0.129 vs 0.038, ρ = −0.450, p = 0.0001, n = 73), consistent with co-recruitment of refusal-adjacent neighbors; co-activation correlation — the obvious functional-alternative predictor — is null in 5 of 6 conditions, and its apparent significance at 9B L20 (ρ = +0.328) is traced to zero-inflation (59/75 features never activate on the 99-prompt reference corpus; among the 16 that do, p = 0.38).

## Methodology

The pipeline has three phases applied identically across all conditions (Gemma-2-2B-it and Gemma-2-9B-it with GemmaScope residual-stream SAEs at layers 20/22/24 and 20/36; Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct with a 131K BatchTopK SAE at layers 15/23; Qwen3-8B with a 32K BatchTopK SAE at layer 18).

**Phase 1 — Feature selection**: two stages. (1) Contrast scoring ranks SAE features by activation-frequency difference between SALADBench-derived task prompts and neutral control text, retaining the top 300. (2) An Arad-style causal-relevance check (Arad et al., 2025) clamps each feature's decoder direction and discards those whose output-distribution shift δ < 0.01, yielding 75–300 features per condition (all 300 Llama features passed).

**Phase 2 — Steerability measurement**: steering adds a scaled decoder direction to the residual stream post-attention, h′ = h + α·v_f, over a grid α ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 20} (positive steering only — refusal amplification; suppression α < 0 deferred). The behavioral score B(α, f) is the mean refusal rate over 100 SALADBench prompts under a keyword-based refusal classifier; α*(f) is extracted from the dose-response curve at T = 0.10, with right-censoring beyond the grid. Degenerate completions are filtered before scoring. A condition is "well-identified" if censoring < 50% and no coherence collapse — two of six Gemma-2 conditions (2B L24, 9B L36 borderline) are excluded from primary inference under pre-registered criteria.

**Phase 3 — Predictability analysis**: Spearman correlations between each pre-steering metric and log α*(f) (censored features included with rank-tie handling), cross-validated ridge R², AUROC for binary steerable (α* ≤ 3) vs hard-to-steer classification, permutation nulls (n = 1,000 shuffles), and leave-one-metric-out ablations. An additional behavioral-proxy baseline tests whether the refusal-rate change at α = 1 (one steered run per feature, cheaper than a sweep) predicts α* as well as geometry does. Threshold sensitivity is checked over T ∈ {0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20} by re-extracting α* from existing curves.

## Results & Highlights

- **Geometry rank-orders steerability, strongest at 9B layer 20**: neighbor density vs log α* gives Spearman ρ = −0.546 (p = 5.75×10⁻⁷, n = 73 uncensored features) at Gemma-2-9B L20, up from ρ = −0.288 at 2B L20 (n = 88) at the same layer index; max cosine similarity reaches ρ = −0.490 at 9B L20. Binary classification (steerable α* ≤ 3 vs hard-to-steer) achieves AUROC 0.822 at 9B L20 against a permutation-null 95th-percentile ρ of 0.197; AUROC across conditions spans 0.610 (2B L20) to 0.822.
- **Cross-architecture replication on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct**: at layer 15 (47% depth, n = 300, 0% censored), max cosine ρ = −0.266 (p < 0.0001) and neighbor density ρ = −0.188 (p = 0.001); at layer 23 (72% depth), still significant (neighbor density ρ = −0.220, p = 1.24×10⁻⁴; max cosine ρ = −0.163, p = 4.63×10⁻³) — but effect sizes are roughly half the Gemma-2 9B result, attributed to the BatchTopK training regime decoupling decoder geometry from function.
- **A consistent depth boundary**: within Gemma-2, neighbor-density ρ goes −0.288 (2B L20, 77% depth) → −0.512 (2B L22, 85%) → null (2B L24, 92%, full coherence collapse driven by ~2.5× larger residual norms, α_max ≈ 188 vs ≈ 75 at L22), and −0.546 (9B L20, 48% depth) → −0.327 (9B L36, 86%) with 46% of features censored; censoring across conditions ranges from 2.7% (9B L20) to 46% (9B L36), and mean α* grows from 3.39 (2B L20) to 6.71 (Llama L23).
- **Mechanism — neighbor refusal alignment**: easy-to-steer features' 50 nearest decoder neighbors are ~3.4× more aligned with the mean refusal direction than hard-to-steer features' (mean alignment 0.129 vs 0.038), correlating with α* at ρ = −0.450 (p = 0.0001, n = 73) — direct evidence for the co-recruitment account.
- **Negative and baseline results**: co-activation correlation is null in 5/6 Gemma conditions, and its apparent 9B-L20 significance (ρ = +0.328, p = 0.004) is a zero-inflation artifact (59/75 features never activate on the reference corpus; within the 16 that do, p = 0.38). A cheaper behavioral proxy — refusal-rate change at α = 1 — predicts α* at only ρ = −0.339 (p = 0.001, n = 89), weaker than neighbor density (−0.435) and max cosine (−0.404) at the same condition, so geometry beats a single-run behavioral measurement. Ridge CV R² is near zero everywhere (0.068 best), confirming the signal is ordinal only. Qwen3-8B (BatchTopK, L18, n = 113, baseline refusal 0.46): full-sample correlations are positive (max cosine ρ = 0.279, p = 0.003) but collapse to ρ ≈ 0.01 among the 98 uncensored features — geometry predicts *whether* a feature steers at all, not the continuous ordering.
- **Measurement conservatism**: the keyword refusal classifier was validated against an independent judge on 160 stratified completions — precision 97.9%, recall 57.5%, F1 72.4% — systematically missing soft refusals, so reported α* values are likely overestimates and the true geometry–steerability relationship probably stronger than reported.

## Authors & Credibility

All five authors (Muhammad Khan and Shlok Channawar with equal contribution) are affiliated with Algoverse AI Research, a workshop-focused research program rather than a major interpretability lab; this is an ICML 2026 Mechanistic Interpretability Workshop paper, not a main-conference publication. The empirical work is nonetheless unusually careful for a workshop paper: pre-registered threshold and well-identifiedness criteria, permutation nulls (n = 1,000), leave-one-metric-out ablations, outlier-dropping robustness checks (dropping two α* = 10 outliers at L22 yields ρ = −0.470), threshold sensitivity across T ∈ {0.05–0.20} (censoring 3%→30%, correlations stable), classifier validation against an independent judge, and a declared coefficient grid with explicit censoring accounting. The honesty about negative results (co-activation, Qwen3) and boundary conditions is a credibility plus. The main credibility gaps are the small Gemma feature sets (75–100 per condition), single-run Llama results, and the keyword classifier's 57.5% recall — all self-acknowledged.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: genuinely useful and mostly new — representation-geometry work (Park et al., Elhage et al.) had not been tested as a *feature-level, pre-intervention* predictor of steering cost, and the formalization of steerability as censored minimum-effective-coefficient α*(f) is a clean contribution. But the effect is correlational and ordinal, not causal-mechanistic; the co-recruitment story rests on one alignment probe.
- **Rigor**: strong on statistics (permutation nulls, censoring handled explicitly, sensitivity analyses) but weak on measurement: a keyword-matching refusal scorer with 57.5% recall and 72.4% F1 is a blunt instrument for the paper's single dependent variable, and agreement collapses to 50–60% exactly at the high-α points (α = 10, 20) where censored features live — meaning the censoring that defines the depth boundary is measured least reliably. The authors' "conservative, so fine" argument is plausible but unverified.
- **Practical utility is oversold relative to the evidence**: the headline framing ("geometry predicts steerability... informing coefficient selection") is rank-only — cross-validated ridge R² is ≈ 0.068 at best, i.e., the metrics tell you an ordering, not a coefficient. A practitioner still needs sweeps to find the actual α*; what they get is prioritization. The paper does state this, but the abstract's "path toward screening features for controllability" reads stronger than the ordinal reality.
- **Reproducibility concerns**: code release is "contingent on safety review before publication" — i.e., not available at review time. The frozen seed-42 prompt pipeline and public SAEs (GemmaScope, public BatchTopK releases) help, but the keyword-phrase list lives in an unreleased codebase (`src/refusal_scorer.py`), so the behavioral measurements cannot be independently checked yet.
- **Coverage quirks**: Llama-3.1 results rest on single runs at two layers (self-flagged as preliminary), Gemma conditions test only 75–100 features each after aggressive filtering, and the entire study covers one behavior (refusal amplification) in one direction (α > 0) — the adversarially relevant suppression direction, and any off-target/collateral-risk evaluation (RQ2/RQ3), are deferred. The Qwen3 full-sample sign flip (positive ρ = 0.279 collapsing to 0.01 among uncensored) is reported transparently but is genuinely confusing without careful reading, and its "BatchTopK decouples geometry from function" interpretation is plausible but confounded with the aggressive attention-sink filtering in that SAE's training — the two causes are not separated by any ablation.
- **Red flags**: dual-use framing is handled responsibly; no benchmark cherry-picking detected (failures are reported as prominently as successes).

## Strengths

- A genuinely practical question — can we predict steering cost before sweeping? — answered with a clean, falsifiable formalization (censored α*(f) at a pre-registered threshold).
- Unusually thorough controls for a workshop paper: permutation nulls, leave-one-metric-out ablations, outlier checks, threshold sensitivity, a behavioral-proxy baseline, and classifier validation with published precision/recall.
- Honest boundary mapping: the deep-layer collapse (2B L24, 9B L36), the BatchTopK/Qwen3 failure mode, and the co-activation zero-inflation artifact are all surfaced and analyzed rather than buried.
- Mechanistic follow-through: the neighbor-refusal-alignment probe (3.4× alignment gap, ρ = −0.450) turns a correlation into a testable co-recruitment hypothesis.
- Cross-scale, cross-width, cross-architecture design (four models, two SAE families, 16K–131K dictionaries) gives the claims real breadth for the budget.

## Limitations

- Rank-only prediction: CV R² ≈ 0 means geometry orders features but does not estimate usable coefficients; the practical value is prioritization, not calibration.
- Single behavioral domain (refusal), single direction (positive α); suppression steering and off-target effects — the safety-critical cases named in the motivation — are entirely deferred.
- The refusal classifier's 57.5% recall makes all α* estimates noisy and biased upward; agreement degrades exactly at the censoring boundary (50–60% at α = 10–20).
- Llama results are single-run at two layers; Gemma conditions use 75–100 features; Qwen3's failure diagnosis (BatchTopK vs attention-sink filtering) is confounded and un-ablated.
- Fixed coefficient grid causes the deep-layer censoring that then limits inference at exactly the depths where the framework is claimed to have a "boundary"; a per-layer-calibrated grid might dissolve part of that boundary.
- Code not yet released (pending safety review), so the pipeline — especially the refusal keyword list — is not independently reproducible right now.

## Relevance

直接命中"LLM Hidden States & Interpretability"方向：这是 SAE 特征转向与表征几何交叉的机制可解释性工作，提供了干预前即可从解码器权重矩阵计算的转向难度预测因子，对安全审计和可控干预（拒绝行为调制）有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the geometry–steerability signal survive with a proper refusal scorer (LLM judge or trained classifier with recall > 90%), and does the deep-layer "boundary" partly disappear once censoring is measured reliably?
- Can the framework predict steering cost for the suppression direction (α < 0) — the adversarially relevant case — or does the co-recruitment mechanism behave asymmetrically?
- Do crowded (easy-to-steer) features produce measurably larger off-target effects on GPQA/TruthfulQA at α* and at a shared low coefficient α₀ = 1, as the co-recruitment account predicts (the paper's own RQ2/RQ3)?
- Is the Qwen3/BatchTopK failure caused by batch-level sparsity competition or by attention-sink filtering? A controlled comparison (same base model, SAEs trained with/without sink filtering) would disentangle the confound.
- Can per-layer grid calibration (scaling α by residual-stream norm, given α_max ≈ 188 at L24 vs ≈ 75 at L22) extend the usable depth range, or is the deep-layer signal loss fundamental?
- Since geometry gives only ranks, can a small number of cheap probe measurements (e.g., response at α = 1) be combined with geometry to calibrate actual coefficient predictions (the α = 1 proxy alone reached ρ = −0.339)?
