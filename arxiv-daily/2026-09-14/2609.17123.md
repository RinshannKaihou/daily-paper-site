> **一句话总结：** 本文在一个给定的 2D CFET 器件-电路电热耦合 Python 模型内，用 GPT-6 "Astra" 智能体搜索源极互连几何结构、并由协调智能体提出衬底导热通孔，在固定金属体积和 20 µW 功耗下将峰值温升降低 1.67 K（141.63→139.96 K，1.18%），同时量化了改进散热的电学代价（金属电阻引入后 0.6 K 降温伴随 2.2% 的 nFET 电流损失），并用跨 16 个模型/智能体的代码复现实验（通过者一致性 <5×10⁻¹⁰ K，Haiku 失败 104.95 K）检验了 AI 科学工作流的可复现性。

> **TL;DR:** A GPT-6 "Astra" AI-scientist workflow searches interconnect geometries for a 4×MoS₂/4×WSe₂ 2D CFET inverter (Lg = 12 nm) inside a supplied finite-volume electrothermal model, finding that redistributing source-interconnect metal plus a coordinating agent's substrate-directed via cuts peak temperature rise from 141.63 K to 139.96 K (−1.67 K, 1.18%) at fixed metal volume and 20 µW on a common 43,493-cell mesh. In self-consistent inverter operation the benefit shrinks to 0.335 K (330.75→330.41 K), and adding metal resistance yields 0.6-K cooling at a 2.2% nFET on-current penalty. A cross-agent reproduction study over 16 model/effort labels agrees within 5×10⁻¹⁰ K for 14 passes, while one Claude Haiku 4.5 submission fails by 104.95 K and is retained for diagnosis. The work is simulation-only (no experiment, no TCAD baseline comparison) and the absolute gains are small against a ~140-K baseline rise.

## 核心贡献

- **在真实 CMOS 器件研究任务中系统测试 AI 科学家工作流**：将 GPT-6 Astra（负责互连几何搜索）与一个协调智能体（负责趋势解读与提出衬底导热通孔）嵌入给定的 2D CFET 电热耦合模型，全程以固定金属体积、公共网格等公平约束比较候选设计，而非自由生成。
- **发现" redistribute 而非 widen "的反直觉热设计**：Astra 的最优解是收窄并内移上层源极互连、把金属重新分配到下层（单独 −0.88 K），与最初"加宽更热互连"的假设相反；固定位置对照实验证明收益来自金属再分配而非单纯收窄（8/8 nm→4/12 nm 使热阻降 0.26%，而仅收窄上层反升 0.33%）。
- **温度与性能解耦的关键观察**：接触长度从 20 nm 缩到 8 nm 时热阻上升 4.2%，但逆变器峰值温度反而下降 6.2 K——因为 nFET 电流掉了 17%；"更低的温度"本身不能证明散热更好。
- **电路级自洽评估与电学代价量化**：电热耦合仿真显示自热使 Lg=8 nm 的 nFET 电流从 245.5 降至 208.6 µA/µm（−15.0%）、增益从 83.4 降至 52.1；固定功耗下 1.67 K 的收益在真实逆变器工作点缩至 0.335 K，加入金属电阻后为 0.6 K 降温 + 2.2% 电流损失。
- **大规模跨智能体代码复现审计**：16 个模型/档位提交复现四个固定热学基准案例，14 个通过（误差 ≤5×10⁻¹⁰ K），Haiku 失败 104.95 K（索引与电阻单位错误）并被保留用于诊断；明确标注未经证实的档案标签与"仅表格无代码"的提交。

## Key Contributions

- **A controlled test of AI-scientist workflows on real device research**: GPT-6 Astra (interconnect geometry search) and a coordinating agent (trend interpretation, via proposal) operate inside a supplied electrothermal CFET model, with fair-comparison constraints (fixed metal volume, top area, heat sources, boundary conditions, common mesh) rather than unconstrained generation.
- **A counter-intuitive thermal finding — redistribute, don't widen**: Astra's selected design narrows and repositions the hotter upper source interconnect while reallocating metal to the lower tier (−0.88 K alone), contradicting the initial widening hypothesis; a fixed-position control shows an 8/8-nm → 4/12-nm width pair cuts thermal resistance 0.26% while narrowing only the upper interconnect raises it 0.33%.
- **Temperature–performance decoupling**: shortening contact length Lc from 20 to 8 nm raises thermal resistance by 4.2% yet lowers inverter peak temperature by 6.2 K because nFET on-current falls 17% — lower temperature alone is not evidence of better heat removal.
- **Circuit-level self-consistent evaluation with electrical-cost accounting**: the same design's benefit shrinks from 1.67 K (fixed 20 µW) to 0.335 K in self-consistent inverter operation, and to 0.6-K cooling with a 2.2% nFET on-current loss once metal resistance is included.
- **A 16-way cross-agent code reproduction audit**: four fixed thermal reference cases are rerun across GPT-6 Astra/GPT-5.x/GPT-5, Claude Fable/Opus/Sonnet/Haiku, Kimi, and Perplexity (Grok 4.6) labels; passing submissions agree within 5×10⁻¹⁰ K, one Haiku submission fails by 104.95 K / 15.16 pp and is kept for diagnosis, and a "Grok-labelled" table-only submission with no code evidence is explicitly flagged.

## Methodology

The device is an n-on-p 2D CFET: four MoS₂ sheets above four WSe₂ sheets (Lg = 12 nm, width 40 nm, heated length 16 nm; Lg = 8/12/32 nm for circuit scans). Channel cross-plane, metal and dielectric thermal conductivities are 2, 25 and 1.2 W·m⁻¹·K⁻¹. Anisotropic steady-state heat conduction is solved by finite volumes including thermal boundary resistance, with the bottom fixed at 300 K and the top exchanging heat with a 300-K bath (h = 2 MW·m⁻²·K⁻¹). Fixed-power tests use 20 µW, 30% contact dissipation and Rcm = 5 m²·K/GW, with constant conductivities and no interconnect Joule heating; energy balance, source normalization and reciprocity are checked, and mesh refinement shifts the fixed-power improvement by only 0.004 K.

For circuit operation, power is derived self-consistently from inverter currents at VDD = 0.7 V by solving In(Vin, Vout, Tn) = Ip(VDD − Vin, VDD − Vout, Tp) with T = 300 K + Hp, using a charge-sheet model with finite-oxide-thickness correction, mobility ∝ T⁻¹·³⁵, saturation velocity ∝ T⁻⁰·⁵, and n/p width-normalized contact resistances of 500/800 Ω·µm per contact. A follow-up sensitivity adds finite-volume metal conduction, voltage drops and Joule heating (ρm = 2.93×10⁻⁷ Ω·m). Contact scaling follows Rc(Lc)/Rc(L0) = tanh(L0/LT)/tanh(Lc/LT).

Agent roles are separated: Astra sweeps interconnect geometries against the worst-case objective J(d) = max over r ∈ {0.5, 1, 2} of ΔTmax(d, r) (power ratio Pn/Pp) at fixed metal volume, using the "Ultra" setting; the coordinating agent reads the reversed width trend and proposes a narrow substrate-directed via. Design families were specified before evaluation and candidate results retained (adaptive, not blind search). Reproduction uses four fixed thermal cases with pass criteria eT ≤ 0.01 K, eC ≤ 0.01 pp, residual/energy error < 10⁻⁷ and rerun agreement, with the reference frozen and hidden before the Ultra submission.

## Results & Highlights

- **Fixed-power thermal design (20 µW, common 43,493-cell mesh, fixed metal volume)**: peak temperature rise drops from 141.63 K (baseline) to 140.75 K for the redistributed interconnect alone (−0.88 K), 140.83 K for the substrate-directed path alone (−0.80 K), and **139.96 K for the combined design (−1.67 K, 1.18%)** — exceeding either individual change and persisting under refinement.
- **Circuit-level self-consistent benefit at Lg = 12 nm**: the combined design lowers the inverter's maximum temperature from 330.75 K to 330.41 K (−0.335 K, 1.09% of the temperature rise vs 1.18% in fixed-power mode); including metal resistance gives **0.6-K inverter cooling at the cost of 2.2% lower nFET on-current**.
- **Self-heating penalty in DC operation (Lg = 8 nm, Vgs = Vds = 0.7 V)**: nFET on-current falls from 245.5 to 208.6 µA/µm (−15.0%; pFET −11.5%), the switching voltage shifts from 0.3211 to 0.3190 V, and voltage-gain magnitude at transition drops from 83.4 to 52.1; peak DC power rises from 3.62 to 3.73 µW; maximum temperature falls from 341.4 K (8 nm) to 312.1 K (32 nm).
- **Contact-length scaling decouples temperature from cooling**: shortening Lc from 20 to 8 nm raises thermal resistance by 4.2% yet lowers inverter peak temperature by 6.2 K while nFET on-current drops 17%; assumed transfer lengths LT of 5–50 nm imply short-contact current penalties of 2–23%.
- **Cross-agent reproduction (4 reference cases)**: 14 of 16 submissions pass with maximum peak-rise error ≤5×10⁻¹⁰ K (fresh Astra Ultra code: 1.34×10⁻¹² K); Claude Haiku 4.5 fails by 104.95 K and 15.16 pp due to indexing and resistance-unit errors; a separate Fable 5.1 "C4" design (1 sheet/tier, AlN, doubled interface conductances, 26.12% more metal) cuts refined mean thermal resistance by 45.19%/40.37% in top/bottom tiers, but different loads and metrics prevent ranking the agents.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The AI-for-science angle (an LLM agent doing structural search inside a domain solver) follows known frameworks (they cite "The AI Scientist", arXiv:2408.06292); the novelty is applying it to CFET thermal design with fair-comparison controls and a genuine circuit-level cost accounting, which is more rigorous than typical agent-demo papers. However, the coordinating agent's via proposal is essentially one human-reviewable design step, and the authors admit "No equivalent Astra acknowledgement is documented" — attribution of the creative move between agent and coordinator is fuzzy.
- **Rigor**: Internally careful (common mesh, fixed metal budget, refinement checks to 0.004 K, energy-balance/reciprocity checks, rescaling analyses explaining the 0.248→0.335-K gap between fixed-power and self-consistent benefits). But the study uses a compact charge-sheet electrical model and constant thermal conductivities rather than TCAD (Sentaurus is cited but never used as a baseline), and the 1–5 nm Green-function literature is discussed but not coupled to the inverter model.
- **Reproducibility**: The 16-way cross-agent audit is a real strength, but the model/effort labels are explicitly uncontrolled, several archive labels are unverified, and the Grok-labelled submission has no code evidence — so the table cannot support claims about which model is "better", which the authors themselves state. Neither search has been independently repeated or experimentally validated.
- **Significance**: The headline gain is small — 1.67 K out of a 141.63-K rise (1.18%) in fixed-power mode, 0.335 K (330.75→330.41 K) in actual inverter operation. Against a baseline that is ~141 K too hot, a ~1 K improvement is of limited engineering consequence; the value is more methodological (workflow + cost quantification) than thermal.
- **Red flags**: Model simplifications include no interconnect Joule heating in the main fixed-power comparison, monolayers one cell thick, unresolved injection beneath top contacts, and fixed 20 µW with a prescribed 30% contact-dissipation split; the "0.6-K cooling with 2% current loss" trade-off rests on an assumed transfer length LT = 20 nm.

## Strengths

- Fair, constraint-controlled search protocol (fixed metal volume, top area, heat sources, boundaries; common mesh) that makes candidate comparisons meaningful.
- Honest decoupling of fixed-power, self-consistent inverter, and metal-resistance-included results, with an explicit electrical cost (2.2% Ion loss) attached to the thermal benefit.
- The fixed-position control and contact-length scaling cleanly separate metal redistribution from narrowing, and temperature from heat-removal quality.
- Exceptionally transparent reproduction audit: frozen hidden reference, retained failure cases, flagged unverified labels, and refusal to over-interpret uncontrolled comparisons.
- Bias accounting (hotspot moves between tiers with bias; temperature peaks at a different Vin than power — 0.3102 V vs 0.3163 V) demonstrates genuine physical insight from the workflow.

## Limitations

- Simulation-only within a supplied compact model; no experimental validation, no TCAD or ab-initio cross-check of the coupled inverter behavior.
- Absolute improvements are modest (1.18% fixed-power, ~0.1% absolute temperature in circuit operation at 12 nm) against a very large baseline self-heating rise.
- Constant thermal conductivities, no interconnect Joule heating in the main comparison, one-cell-thick monolayers, and unresolved contact injection limit physical fidelity.
- Agent attribution is partly narrative: only two agents and one coordination event; the search space (interconnect widths/positions plus one via) is narrow compared to real thermal-design freedom (materials, dielectrics, spacers).
- The multi-model reproduction table is uncontrolled and partly unverified (asterisked labels, one table-only submission), limiting what it can claim about model capabilities.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the 1.67-K (fixed-power) / 0.335-K (self-consistent) benefit survive a full TCAD (Sentaurus) or atomistic Green-function treatment of the same CFET stack, given the constant-conductivity and one-cell monolayer simplifications?
- Can the search be extended to materials and interface engineering (e.g., the Fable C4 design's AlN and doubled interface conductances, −45.19%/40.37% thermal resistance) within the same fixed-metal/fair-comparison protocol, enabling a genuine ranking of design families?
- How does the thermal-benefit/electrical-cost trade-off evolve under dynamic (switching) operation and realistic workloads, rather than the DC operating points studied here?
- What is the minimal scaffolding needed for the reproduction audit to become a controlled model comparison (fixed prompts, seeds, tool versions), and does the Haiku failure mode (indexing/unit errors) generalize across weaker models?
- Does the observed temperature–performance decoupling (contact-length scaling: +4.2% thermal resistance with −6.2 K peak temperature) suggest an optimum contact length jointly minimizing temperature-corrected delay rather than temperature alone?
