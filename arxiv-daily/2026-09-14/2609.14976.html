Critical Assessment · multiple-comparison audit
01 Methodology — distilled

120 scripted episodes — 24 per risk, tiered easy/medium/hard (37/45/38; 5–25 turns) — each a DAG of memory writes, tool calls, constraint declarations, and decision points, executed against five locally-quantized models (Qwen2.5 1.5B/3B/7B, Phi-3.5-mini, Llama-3.2-3B) via llama.cpp on one 16 GB GPU, yielding 600 traces.

Scoring is fully deterministic: 1–3 required checks per episode over four evidence channels (text, tool, memory ledger, constraint state) emit a boolean verdict plus a [0,1] score — no LLM-as-judge anywhere on the pass/fail path. Two validity refinements: a strict-matching revision (19 episodes re-scored after brittle “label: value” expectations; 39/600 scores changed, both versions released) and a safety-aware refusal pass for R3/R5, where refusing is the safety-correct outcome.

The subset selector runs coverage-constrained greedy forward selection maximizing Ψ(S) = α·ρ + β·cov(S) + γ·hr(S) over 12 trace-derived features, with leave-one-model-out CV to prevent test-set leakage; stochastic baselines get 1000-bootstrap 95% CIs. Everything — episodes, traces, scorer, selector, seeds — is public (github.com/fuxue-mingzhu/MemRiskBench).

04 Strengths — what holds up
  • Right problem, demonstrated: aggregates genuinely hide sparse high-severity failures, and random/stratified subsampling demonstrably discards them (stratified high-risk detection 0.345).
  • Deterministic, trace-grounded scoring with no LLM judge — every verdict auditable from the released traces.
  • Correct selector objective (ranking + coverage + high-risk detection) for safety-sensitive compression — and the ablation honestly shows one feature does nearly all the work.
  • Exceptional transparency: dual scoring variants, revision audit, admitted confounds, reported non-significance — plus a full public release on locally-runnable models.
05 Limitations — what to watch
  • Model coverage: five sub-8B quantized models, three of five Qwen-family; no frontier API models — and the 7B runs Q3_K_M while others run Q4 (acknowledged quantization confound).
  • Statistics: 24 episodes per risk leave key contrasts underpowered after Holm correction; the selector’s zero-variance “CI” means no uncertainty quantification.
  • Brittle matching: the revision exposed a 3.3% false-negative rate in deterministic checks — the very low leakage pass rates (4–42%) may partly reflect matching brittleness, not pure model failure.
  • Ecological validity: tool calls are deterministic stubs; transfer to deployed agents is unproven. Selector is reference-set dependent, and R6 stays unscored.
06 Open questions
  • Q1Do frontier-class models (GPT-4/Claude/Gemini) show the same per-risk profile — or is the leakage↔constraint inversion an artifact of small quantized models?
  • Q2Do the selector’s guarantees hold with 20+ diverse reference families — and can a reference-free discrimination estimate replace the dependence?
  • Q3How much of the low leakage pass rate is genuinely unsafe behavior vs brittle string matching — would semantically-matched, auditable checkers reorder models?
  • Q4Can R6 (summary contamination) be scored without an LLM judge — e.g. canary strings or watermark-style deterministic probes in memory summaries?
  • Q5Does any published memory architecture (MemGPT-style consolidation, MemoryBank decay, constraint re-injection) actually reduce the measured R1–R5 failure rates?
  • Q6Does the 5×-compressed risk-preserving subset remain valid under distribution shift — new episode templates, longer horizons, real tool environments?
