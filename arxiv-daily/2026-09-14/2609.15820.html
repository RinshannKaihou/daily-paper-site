01 Methodology

AlgoEvo casts algorithm discovery as an agentic loop over an algorithm space S₁ × … × S_K. Each step, the agent observes the current algorithm, working context, experience bank, and performance feedback — then chooses its next action itself. Only evaluate consumes the scored budget; diagnosis draws from the same budget, forcing analytical reasoning to compete with empirical testing.

The agent's seven actions
inspectretrievediagnoseanalyse interactionseditevaluatecommit / stop

Design-skill hub — paradigm knowledge (roles, interfaces, modification principles) externalized as pluggable skills, so one engine covers single-objective, multi-objective, and multi-component design; skills refine only across tasks, promoted after validation on ≥ 2 tasks.

Hierarchical experience bank — every evaluation becomes a card; cards form a per-task tree traversed with MCTS mechanics (situation-conditioned UCB selection, expansion, backpropagation). Four telemetry-detected situations — stagnation, sparse front, bottleneck, coupling — steer retrieval.

Protocol: LLM4AD platform, identical splits & entry points, 500-eval budget, 3 seeds; gpt-4o-mini (TSP/CVRP), DeepSeek-V4-Flash (rest).

03 Strengths

  • One engine, three paradigms. Single-, multi-objective, and multi-component design that previously required separate codebases — the skill hub is a clean decoupling, and method-skill runs (EoH's skill inside AlgoEvo) show the methodology transfers independently of its original scaffold.
  • Efficiency in both currencies. ~1/13 the evaluations on four of six tasks, 2–6× lower tokens everywhere — measured, not claimed.
  • "Self-evolving" actually evidenced at three granularities: within-task (cost falls as cards accumulate), across runs (warm start 4.961 → 4.808, lower variance), across tasks (CVRP→CVRP-DR −4.8%) — plus monotone multi-round gains.
  • Honest reporting of losses, transparent thresholds, and an unusually detailed appendix (design-space code contracts, per-seed action breakdowns).

04 Limitations

  • Memory isn't free. Token overhead grows as the experience bank accumulates in multi-component settings; 118–139M-token runs are not cheap in absolute terms. Compression left as future work.
  • Transfer is same-family only (routing → routing-with-repair); negative-transfer risk across divergent domains conceded but never probed.
  • Three seeds, overlapping stds. Trajectory styles differ sharply per seed (edit-driven vs diagnosis-driven); ablation ordering (+0.66/+0.58/+0.21%) is suggestive, not firm — and covers only CVRP-DR.
  • Scoped evaluation. Solver, feasibility checks, and evaluators stay fixed — no whole-pipeline co-design; instance scales modest (mostly 50–200 nodes).

05 Questions for further exploration

  1. How much of the gain is the agentic loop itself vs simply giving the LLM execution feedback that scalar-fitness pipelines discard? A feedback ablation would isolate this.
  2. Does the advantage survive when every baseline gets the same stronger backbone and diagnosis-style prompts?
  3. Does the experience tree's situational UCB avoid collapse at 10–100× the cards — and does situation-triggered injection's +0.21% grow or shrink?
  4. Why does diagnosis-heavy search beat edit-heavy (8.281 vs 8.927/9.138 on CVRP-DR) — and can that behavior be induced rather than left to seed luck?
  5. Does cross-task transfer stay positive across structurally divergent families (routing → scheduling)? Can the ≥2-task promotion rule become a principled applicability test?
  6. How do conclusions change under a wall-clock or total-LLM-call budget — the currency practitioners actually pay?
