Why this matters

Aggressive quantization can silently break robot policies; an offline, calibrated quality gate keeps low-precision deployment reliable without exhaustive closed-loop trials.

01

Methodology — three stages, zero rollouts

distilled from the paper · ~160 words
01

Offline replay

Replay a fixed 32-frame observation log through the bf16 reference and each quantized policy with matched seeds; score each config by δ̄, the mean absolute action-component deviation. Reference actions are cached — a new candidate costs only M offline policy queries, no environment interaction.

02

Policy-specific calibration

A small dev set is labeled in closed loop: acceptable (≤1 pp loss), degraded (≥2 pp loss and a two-standard-error evidence test), or unresolved. Thresholds δ_acc / δ_rej bracket all labels consistent under a single-crossing hypothesis — Prop. 1: an issued verdict never contradicts a definite label; Prop. 2: new data only tightens the interval.

03

Selective verdicts

Accept if δ̄ ≤ δ_acc, reject if δ̄ ≥ δ_rej, defer otherwise. All thresholds frozen before held-out evaluation. Resolved deferrals feed back as new anchors — then fresh held-out evaluation is required (sensitivity checked over 5 seeds + paired bootstrap).

SEARCH SPACE — RTN & HQQ weights (W2–W4, group sizes 16–128 & per-channel) · ViDiT-Q W8A8 · SVDQuant-style W4A4 · QServe-style W4A8 · 5 WAMs × 4 benchmarks (LIBERO, RoboCasa-24, PushT, PushT-M)

03

Strengths / Limitations

side-by-side, not sequential

Strengths

  • Converts config selection from exhaustive closed-loop rollouts into a cheap offline screen — cached reference actions, zero environment interaction — with a principled abstention mechanism.
  • Prospective, frozen-threshold evaluation: 21/21 correct, zero false verdicts — far stronger evidence than the retroactive correlations common in this literature.
  • Definitively shows bit width is meaningless: 97.4% vs 20.1% at the same 3 bits; cross-policy threshold reversal kills any global cutoff.
  • Deferral is safe-by-construction (Proposition 1) and empirically caught a 33-pp-loss candidate a forced binary rule would have missed.
  • 450-trial robot validation with transparent intervals, plus measured payoff (1.37× faster, −44% memory). Code released; failures reported in full tables.

Limitations

  • Held-out prediction covers two checkpoints on one benchmark — and LIBERO-10 overlaps the Cosmos calibration aggregate; clean task-distribution transfer is untested.
  • One rejected UVA label sits at Fisher p = 0.057 despite the 2-SE criterion; several labels statistically fragile; 3/28 outcomes unresolved.
  • Mean deviation compresses error timing — it ignores when errors occur (contact / grasp transitions), and finite 8-trajectory logs can miss decision-critical states.
  • The robot study validates deviation groups, not the calibrated thresholds — no sim-to-real or cross-embodiment transfer attempted.
  • Calibration and deferred resolution still need closed-loop runs; 25% of candidates fell in the deferral interval.
  • Any protocol change (policy, preprocessing, eval) forces recalibration; robustness to independently collected logs untested.
04

Questions for further exploration

open threads from the paper
  • Q1Could timing-aware deviation (errors at contact / grasp transitions) or adaptive log selection shrink the 25% deferral zone?
  • Q2Does the single-crossing hypothesis hold for activation × weight error mixes, KV-cache quantization, or QAT checkpoints rather than pure PTQ?
  • Q3Can sim-calibrated thresholds transfer across embodiments — or to real hardware with a small correction factor — enabling true sim-to-real prediction?
  • Q4Could the same replay calibration detect silent numerical corruption (bit-flip / SDC-induced action drift) in deployed quantized policies?
  • Q5Could the deferral interval itself guide mixed-precision search — pointing to which layers to keep in higher precision?
