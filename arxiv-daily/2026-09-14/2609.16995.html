Why this matters

Autoresearch agents are flooding science with hard-to-verify claims. PaperDoctor is a working blueprint for the missing QC stage: evidence-grounded, author-serving feedback.

Methodology · distilled

One parse, three cost tiers F = (issue, evidence, suggestion)

Input: manuscript + code + data. One parsing pass yields four artifacts — section-structured markdown (Mathpix), page images for VLMs, a tree-sitter code index, a parsed bibliography — and each downstream skill requests only its slice. Every finding ships with error/warning severity.

L1 Surface screeningWriting & figure review, citation checks, atomic-claim extraction — runs on the manuscript itself. ●○○
L2 Typed claim verificationEach claim routed by evidence type, in parallel: code cross-check vs the repo, theory re-derivation, literature web-check, experiment-design review. ●●○
L3 Prioritized reproductionDeclarative plans ranked by priority; author approves after seeing estimated GPU hours; an LLM executor handles setup, dispatch, and logs. ●●●
cost tiering: cheap screens always run · expensive reproduction is budgeted & gated

Strengths

+ What holds up

  • Auditability by construction. The (finding, evidence, suggestion) triple is enforced — a pattern any review system could adopt.
  • Right economics. Cheap screens always run; expensive reproduction is prioritized, budgeted, and gated behind author approval with GPU-hour estimates.
  • Stronger-than-usual evaluation. Item-level author verdicts plus real referee reports — not the usual “agrees with human review scores” protocol.
  • Transferable L3 insights. Repro is bottlenecked pre-execution (33.1% broken environments) and post-execution; priority predicts pass rate (47.3% vs 33.6%).
  • Auditable case catches. Impossible citation dates, SciBERT-vs-MiniLM embedding mismatch, refuted novelty claim, the 2× overstatement.
Limitations

– What to doubt

  • Circular ground truth. Agreement is self-reported by the critiqued authors; precision is never measured against external verification.
  • Undisclosed internals. Backbone LLM(s), prompts, per-paper runtime, and compute cost are absent from the main text.
  • L3 weakest where it counts. Training reruns pass 11.3%; 47.0% of plans never execute; wet-lab sciences largely out of scope.
  • Silent on significance. Checks whether claims are true, not whether they matter; precision drew the most author criticism (4 vs 1).
  • Count ≠ correctness. “More findings” (36–56/paper) carries no precision/recall ground truth; the agentic baseline is a single run.
  • Needs code. For code-less drafts, the distinctive L2/L3 checks reduce to reading-only review.
Open questions

Where this could go next

  1. Per-skill precision/recall against externally verified ground truth (rebuttals, errata) instead of author self-report?
  2. Would a cheaper open backbone collapse L2's rejection edge over L1 (11% vs 27%)?
  3. Can the 33.1% incomplete-environment blocker be attacked by synthesizing missing analysis code from the paper?
  4. Would interactive contestation — accept/reject per item, re-run affected checks — improve downstream drafts?
  5. Could reproduction reports become standardized submission artifacts, validated against post-publication replication?
  6. Is the 97.1% conditional fix acceptance real quality, or an artifact of bundled evidence-plus-fix pairs?
Authors & credibility

Who is behind this

  • 17 authors across Oxford, Stanford, NUS, UW, and Cambridge — the group behind the ICLR-2025 20k-review feedback RCT and the Stanford Agentic Reviewer it benchmarks against.
  • Unusually honest genre: item-level verdicts, real referee baselines, and it refuses venue pass-rate rankings.
  • Red flags: none severe; failure modes reported prominently. Minor: the +1.30 mean sits on a coarse 4-point scale (n = 30).
