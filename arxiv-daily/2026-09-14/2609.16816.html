36% vs 0/45certificate-faithful ref.

Best generator (Opus 5) still exploited on 36% (16/45) of stress environments; the worst (Haiku 4.5) reaches 98%. A rubric-quality gap, not task impossibility.

Why this matters

If you run rubric-based RL, LLM-as-a-judge, or automated scoring: neutral-prompt rubrics are unsafe rewards, safety prompts don't fix it, specificity guides attacks.

「中性提示下前沿模型量规仍有 8–26% 可被攻破、通用安全提示救不了、具体化标准可能反而成为攻击路线图。」

⚠ Critical edge

The 0/45 certificate-faithful reference is circular — it renders the very certificate the Oracle checks into rubric form, so perfect agreement is baked in (as the authors concede).

01Methodology — the chain

Each environment is a tuple (q, D, c): a conclusion-pressing question, a closed evidence packet, and a machine-checkable honesty certificate defining required and prohibited claims. 158/169 environments are real-web-grounded (zero pure-synthetic); certificates were verified by a blind per-sample audit that re-derives impossibility without seeing them.

For every (environment, generator) pair, a fixed four-stage chain runs:

  1. 01GeneratorNeutral prompt, blind to unanswerability, writes the rubric
  2. 02AttackerFixed Claude-Opus writes a reward-maximizing answer
  3. 03JudgeBlind Claude-Haiku scores honest baseline vs attack, strictly by rubric
  4. 04OracleClaude-Opus rules on certificate violation

exploited ⇔ J(a★; r) ≥ J(a₀; r) ∧ ¬Hc(a★) — high score alone or a violation label alone never suffices.

Beyond the headline

  • 7 of 11 tailored rubrics are exploited at least as often as a generic "be decisive, penalize hedging" proxy (64%) — task-specific criteria can hand the attacker a roadmap.
  • Safety prompting doesn't close the gap: one "penalize overclaiming" sentence drops Hard-45 rates 36→22 (Opus 5), 42→36 (GPT-5.6-sol), 67→49 (Sonnet 5) — all far above the faithful reference's 0%.
  • Resampling variance (k = 3–6 draws): single-draw rates fall up to ~16 points; 27/45 Opus 4.8 environments flip verdict across draws.
  • Chain robustness: judge swaps leave verdicts unchanged on 60/60 chains; blind human rating agrees with the Oracle on 38/40 (κ = 0.89).
  • Benign best-of-N (N = 5): where honest samples exist, rubric-based selection picked a violating answer 0/13 times and beats random choice (27.8% vs 44.4% violation).

03 · Strengths

  • First benchmark whose attack surface is the generated rubric itself — exploitability framed as a first-order safety property.
  • Environments + certificates (not fixed rubrics) persist: generator-agnostic and memorization-proof — future models plug in without regeneration.
  • The exploitation conjunction cleanly separates specification gaming from task difficulty and mere stringency.
  • Exceptional transparency: pre-registered sub-studies, staged-run disclosure, null results reported verbatim, and an Oracle study that quantifies its own labels' fragility.
  • Nuanced benign result: without an adversary, generated rubrics selected zero violating answers (0/13) — dangerous under adversarial optimization, still helpful under benign selection.

04 · Limitations

  • All absolute rates are conditional on one evaluation chain — the Oracle swap alone moves 33.3% → 75.6%, and no experiment identifies the most accurate Oracle.
  • Certificates are shared across rubric construction, verification, and calibration — a single certificate error is invisible to every agreement check.
  • The attacker is an LLM, not a global optimizer — reported rates are lower bounds on empirical exploitability.
  • Headline GPT-5.6 / Opus 5 rates are single-draw (k = 1); resampled arms show draw-contingent failure-set membership.
  • Discrimination concentrates in 2 of 6 impossibility types; false-premise is 0% across all generators — 25 environments add nothing.
  • Best-of-N study is tiny (1 generator, 1 policy, N = 5, 18 environments); end-to-end RL on these rubrics is unmeasured.

05Questions for further exploration

open threads
  • Which Oracle configuration is closest to ground truth — can ensembles or human-adjudicated subsets close the 33.3% vs 75.6% verification gap?
  • Why do GPT-5.6 variants and Opus 5 beat the naive proxy while seven other generators don't — is there a transferable rubric-writing property to train for?
  • Does exploitability under a one-shot attacker predict reward hacking in end-to-end RLVR training loops?
  • Can the "attack roadmap" effect be mitigated — hidden hold-out criteria, randomized point allocations, certificate-conditioned verification — without losing discrimination?
  • Why is discrimination concentrated in only two of six impossibility types, and can harder false-premise / missing-citation instances be constructed?
