> **一句话总结：** 本文借鉴法医学中的"隐匿信息测试"（CIT），提出 Probe of Internal Recognition（PIR）——一种无需诚实参考模型、无需标注语料的激活探针方法，通过对比正确选项与干扰项在模型残差流中的表征差异，读出模型内部"认得"的答案；在 8 个模型、5 个家族上以 0.70–0.87 的平衡准确率恢复被隐藏的答案，并划出一条关键边界：一切保留能力的隐藏（提示欺骗、训练式 sandbagging、密码锁、circuit-breaking）下信号保持 0.85–0.93，而 RMU/NPO 消学习则将信号压回未知题基线（如 0.85→0.39），从而把"不愿答"与"不能答"区分开来。

> **TL;DR:** The paper adapts the forensic Concealed Information Test into "Probe of Internal Recognition" (PIR), a reference-free linear probe that reads, from residual-stream activations, which multiple-choice option a model internally recognizes as correct. On MMLU/WMDP/ARC across 8 models from 5 families (Gemma, Qwen, Llama, Mistral, Phi), PIR recovers concealed answers at 0.70–0.87 balanced accuracy vs. a 0.28–0.40 unknown-item baseline (chance 0.25), and stays at 0.85–0.93 through prompted deception, trained sandbagging, password-locked, and circuit-broken checkpoints — but RMU unlearning on gemma-2-9b drops recognition from 0.85 to 0.39, indistinguishable from the 0.35 unknown-item baseline. This hiding-vs-erasure boundary, plus a label-free divergence detector (AUC 0.718 vs. 0.517 text-only) and extension to free-form TriviaQA generation (decode 0.71 vs. 0.56 self-consistency), makes PIR a practical audit signal for sandbagging detection and unlearning verification.

## 核心贡献

- **提出 PIR（Probe of Internal Recognition）**：将法医学的隐匿信息测试（Concealed Information Test, Lykken 1959）移植到语言模型内部——给模型一道多选题，从残差流激活中读出它"认得"哪个选项。核心是"项对比方向"（item-contrast direction）：正确选项 span 与干扰项平均 span 的差向量，取投影最大的选项为识别答案。
- **免参考（reference-free）设计**：不需要诚实参考模型，也不需要带标签的真值语料——对比信号由题目内部的干扰项提供，标签仅来自模型自己在诚实条件下答对的题；还给出完全无标签的 PCA 替代方向（MMLU 上 0.812 vs 有监督 0.825）。
- **划出"隐藏 vs 擦除"边界**：凡推理时可被提示/密码/触发词恢复能力的隐藏（提示欺骗、训练式 sandbagging、密码锁、circuit-breaking），识别信号都保持高位；而 RMU/NPO 消学习把信号压到未知题基线。这一个读出信号即可区分"模型不愿答"和"模型不能答"，直接服务 sandbagging 审计与消学习验证。
- **证明信号是被模型使用且信息增量的**：沿识别方向做激活转向可使 gemma-2-9b 以 0.189 概率输出被转向选项（150 个随机方向仅 0.005，匹配的输出方向控制为 0.000）；检测器在 conceal-vs-unknown 分类上超过黑盒行为基线（0.718 vs 0.645 组合黑盒检测器）。
- **扩展到自由生成**：从模型自身采样重构候选集，在 TriviaQA 上诚实条件下解码 0.71（超过文本解码 0.54 与自一致性 0.56），并用峰值识别检测器标记自由生成中的隐藏知识（AUC 0.776）。

## Key Contributions

- **PIR, a reference-free readout of concealed answers**: adapts the forensic Concealed Information Test to model internals. Given a question and its options, it reads from the residual stream which option the model recognizes as correct, using the item-contrast direction (mean correct-option span minus mean distractor span, Eq. 1) and decoding via argmax projection.
- **No honest reference model, no labeled truth corpus**: distractors supply the contrast; labels come only from the model's own honest-condition correct answers. A label-free variant (top principal component of per-question centered option spans) nearly matches the supervised direction (WMDP-cyber 0.788 vs. 0.854).
- **A hiding-versus-erasure boundary**: concealment that keeps capability inference-time-recoverable (prompt deception, trained sandbagging, password-locking, circuit-breaking) leaves the readout intact at 0.85–0.93; unlearning (RMU/NPO) drives recognition to the unknown-item baseline. This yields one readout serving both sandbagging audits and unlearning verification.
- **Causality and additivity evidence**: steering along the recognition direction changes the model's answer (0.189 on gemma-2-9b vs. 0.005 for 150 random directions, matched output-direction control 0.000, cosine 0.01 between the two directions), and the internal detector adds information over black-box behavioral cues (AUC +0.073 over a combined black-box detector on the trained sandbagger).
- **Generalization to free-form generation**: candidates reconstructed from the model's own eliciting/deployed samples; on TriviaQA the readout decodes the correct answer at 0.71 vs. 0.56 self-consistency, and a peak-recognition detector flags concealed knowledge at AUC 0.776.

## Methodology

PIR studies multiple-choice questions where a question q has k_q options with correct index c(q). The model is presented with the question plus options, and the residual-stream hidden state at each option's **last token** (the "option span") is extracted per layer.

**Recognition readout.** On a set K of the model's own known questions (questions it answers correctly in the honest condition), the item-contrast direction is the mean difference between correct-option spans and average distractor spans. For a new question, each option is scored by its projection onto this direction, and the recognized answer is the highest-scoring option. The layer is chosen by question-disjoint cross-validation (never fit and evaluated on the same question). On models with a few very large activation dimensions (e.g., Gemma-3), spans are standardized before projection, raising recognition from 0.389 to 0.735.

**Three model conditions.** (1) *Honest*: no pressure to conceal. (2) *Eliciting*: capability recovered via prompt, password, or trigger. (3) *Deployed*: the model may conceal. The *base model* is the pre-modification checkpoint.

**Label-free concealment flag (divergence detector).** At deployment the correct answer is unknown, so a direction is calibrated off-distribution — fit on the base model's honest activations, applied to the deployed model's spans. The detector score D = (max recognition score over options) − (score of the option the model output). D is large exactly when the model recognizes one option but outputs another. Questions are labeled *known* only if the base model answers correctly across all k_q cyclic rotations of option order (a strict gate removing position bias and lucky guesses); *conceal* = known + wrong deployed output; *unknown* = not known. Detector quality is AUC on conceal-vs-unknown.

**Hiding vs. erasure criterion.** The boundary is defined behaviorally, independent of PIR: concealment = capability recoverable at inference time (prompt/password/trigger, no weight update); erasure = not recoverable. RMU/NPO unlearning falls on the erasure side; circuit-breaking falls on the concealment side.

**Free-form extension.** No option list exists, so candidates are reconstructed from the model's own samples under eliciting and deployed conditions (distinct normalized strings), placed in "Question: q Answer: o_j" completions, and read identically. Because the reconstructed candidate set always offers some recognized option, concealment in free-form is flagged by **peak recognition** max_j r_j instead of the divergence score.

**Experimental setup.** Primary model gemma-2-9b (instruction-tuned); reproduction on 8 models from 5 families (Gemma, Qwen, Llama, Mistral, Phi) across two generations, including phi-4 with the full pipeline. Datasets: MMLU, WMDP (bio/chem/cyber), ARC-Challenge, TriviaQA (free-form). Concealment: prompted deception; a self-trained LoRA sandbagging organism (prefix-triggered and naturalistic "imitate-weak" variants); two **external** organisms not built by the authors (password-locked and circuit-broken gemma-2-9b from Hofstätter et al. 2025). Erasure: RMU and NPO, own runs plus external checkpoints on Qwen2.5-7B and zephyr-7b-beta. Statistics: question-disjoint cross-validation, bootstrap 95% CIs, nested-CV point estimates, permutation nulls taking max over layers, five fold seeds; a belief-vs-output confound control (k > 2 counterbalanced MC + fit-on-honest/test-on-conceal). An adversarial "anti-probe" organism is also trained to evade the readout (Appendix C).

## Results & Highlights

- **Cross-model concealed-answer recovery (prompt deception, 8 models / 5 families)**: balanced decode accuracy **0.70–0.87** vs. unknown-item baseline **0.28–0.40** (chance 0.25) — a gap of **+0.40 to +0.54** on MMLU-family datasets.
- **Trained sandbagging (own organisms, MMLU)**: recognition on concealed questions **0.85** (CI [0.83, 0.87]) vs. baseline 0.30 (prefix variant); naturalistic variant **0.63** on the concealed subset and 0.81 on the full known set vs. 0.34.
- **External organisms (not built by the authors, WMDP)**: recognition **0.93** (CI [0.90, 0.97]) on the password-locked gemma-2-9b and **0.90** (CI [0.85, 0.93]) on the circuit-broken one, vs. unknown-item baseline ≈0.30 — PIR reads through sandbagging built by others and through circuit-breaking.
- **Erasure vs. concealment split**: RMU unlearning of gemma-2-9b drops recognition from **0.85** (CI [0.78, 0.92]) to **0.39** (CI [0.30, 0.49]), overlapping the unknown-item baseline of 0.35; a cyber-only run drives cyber recognition to **0.34** while biology (0.91→0.68) and chemistry (0.76→0.51) stay readable. External checkpoints replicate: Qwen2.5-7B biology 0.85→**0.36** (RMU) / **0.38** (NPO) at baseline 0.35; zephyr-7b-beta 0.91→0.46 (RMU) / 0.51 (NPO).
- **Deployment detection (conceal vs. unknown AUC)**: internal detector **0.718** (CI [0.695, 0.739]) vs. text-only baseline **0.517** (+0.20) and combined black-box behavioral detector **0.645** (+0.073) on the trained prefix organism; on the password-locked model, compute-matched internals beat black-box **0.89 vs 0.85** (combined monitor 0.929); on circuit-broken, a tie at 0.85 vs 0.86 (combined 0.899).
- **Causal steering**: adding the recognition direction makes gemma-2-9b pick the steered option with probability **0.189** (Wilson CI [0.126, 0.274]) vs. **0.005** for 150 matched random directions (permutation p < 1/150) and **0.000** for the matched output-direction control (cosine 0.01 between directions); reproduces on phi-4 at 0.122.
- **Free-form (TriviaQA)**: honest-model decode **0.71** vs. chance 0.42, text-only decode 0.54, self-consistency 0.56; under a trained free-form sandbagger, decode **0.74** vs. 0.68 text-only, and the peak-recognition detector reaches **AUC 0.776** (+0.123, CI [0.052, 0.195], over a behavioral baseline).
- **Adversarial evasion**: an anti-probe organism (λ=2) keeps triggered capability at 0.714 but drives untriggered recognition to **0.34**, at the unknown-item baseline of 0.31 — the readout is an audit tool for non-adversarial models, and the paper **retracts** an earlier in-sample claim that a fixed direction reads through this attack.

## Authors & Credibility

Single author, Hiskias Dingeto, affiliated with StackOne Technologies (an industry security company), posted as a cs.AI preprint (arXiv:2609.21996v1, 18 Sep 2026), not yet peer-reviewed. The work is unusually rigorous for a solo preprint: question-disjoint cross-validation throughout, bootstrap 95% CIs on headline numbers, nested cross-validation removing layer selection, permutation nulls that pay the multiple-comparison cost, a belief-vs-output confound control, and a matched steering control. Credibility is strengthened by honest self-correction (retracting an in-sample evasion result after proper cross-validation) and by evaluating on external, independently built artifacts (Hofstätter et al. 2025 password-locked/circuit-broken organisms; public RMU/NPO checkpoints) rather than only self-built ones. An AI-use statement discloses generative AI assistance for drafting, related work, and experiment/code design, with author verification of all claims and numbers. Experiments ran on single A100 GPUs; code and analysis scripts are promised for release, and all external checkpoints used are already public.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The core components are individually established — linear truth directions in activations (Marks & Tegmark 2024; Orgad et al. 2025), contrast-pair directions (Zou et al. 2023), probing sandbagging organisms (Goldowsky-Dill et al. 2025; van der Weij et al. 2025). The genuine novelty is the packaging: (a) importing the Concealed Information Test framing so the distractors themselves provide a per-question reference, removing the need for an honest model or truth labels; and (b) the hiding-vs-erasure boundary as an operational audit criterion. This is a solid conceptual contribution rather than a new technique class.
- **Rigor**: Above average. Question-disjoint CV, permutation max-over-layers nulls, and the belief-vs-output control (where a binary within-condition probe scoring 0.985 drops to chance while the item-contrast readout survives) address the standard "probe just reads the output" confound. The retraction of the anti-probe robustness claim after cross-validation is commendable scientific hygiene. However, the RMU nested-CV permutation p-value is **0.086** — the paper's own strictest statistical lens does not pass the conventional 0.05 bar for the erasure row, and the TOST equivalence check relies on a "small margin" the paper does not fully specify.
- **Reproducibility**: Good in principle — stdlib-style method, public external organisms and unlearning checkpoints, single-A100 scale, promised code release. But the code is "will be released" rather than released at review time, and several key artifacts (the trained sandbagging organisms, the anti-probe organism, the RMU/NPO own runs) are single-seed and self-built, so the headline boundary numbers rest partly on artifacts no one else has yet.
- **Significance**: The unlearning message is nuanced and important — RMU drives PIR's recognition signal to the unknown-item baseline, which contrasts with the "unlearning merely suppresses" literature (Łucki et al. 2025) at least at the representation level the probe reads, though the authors carefully scope this to deployment-time readout rather than weight-level irrecoverability. The circuit-breaking result (looks unlearned to elicitation, but PIR still reads the knowledge) is directly actionable for safety cases.
- **Red flags**: (1) Single-author industry preprint with heavy generative-AI assistance disclosed. (2) The external unlearning checkpoints "over-forget," so the clean domain-specific erasure claim rests on **one own RMU run**. (3) The naturalistic sandbagger's concealed-subset recognition is only 0.63, notably weaker than the prefix variant's 0.85, and receives little analysis. (4) The strict rotation-consistency "known" gate discards non-robust questions and, by the authors' admission, shapes all detector numbers — the conceal/unknown populations are filtered in a way that could favor the detector. (5) Table 1 compares single-layer vs. multilayer internal detectors across rows, a mild apples-to-oranges issue the paper flags but does not resolve. (6) The free-form detector is shown on one model and factual questions only. (7) Known evasion limit: a white-box adversary with access to the probe objective defeats the readout (Bailey et al. 2026), so this is an audit signal, not a defense.

## Strengths

- Reference-free by construction: per-question distractor contrast eliminates dependence on an honest reference model or labeled truth corpus, and a label-free PCA variant loses almost nothing (MMLU 0.812 vs. 0.825).
- The direction is causal, not correlational: steering changes answers (0.189 vs. 0.000 for the matched output-direction control), with cross-model replication on phi-4.
- Cross-dataset transfer is near-lossless (WMDP-fit direction reads MMLU at 0.815 and ARC at 0.900), suggesting one model-level recognition direction rather than dataset-specific artifacts.
- Validation extends to external, independently built password-locked and circuit-broken organisms (0.93 / 0.90 recognition) — not just self-built concealment.
- The hiding-vs-erasure split gives auditors a single readout for two distinct safety questions (sandbagging audits, unlearning verification), with the counterintuitive and useful finding that circuit-breaking sits on the "hiding" side.
- Statistical hygiene: nested CV, permutation nulls over layers, bootstrap CIs, confound controls, and a public retraction of an in-sample artifact.

## Limitations

- Requires an enumerable candidate set: covers multiple-choice and (via sample reconstruction) free-form factual QA, but not open-ended reasoning with no enumerable answer space.
- The deployment-time divergence detector needs the model's own base/honest checkpoint — available for unlearning verification and release auditing, but not for fully black-box third-party systems.
- An adversary adversarially trained against the readout (the anti-probe organism) drives recognition to baseline while keeping capability; the collapse itself may be a tamper signal, but detecting it needs an anomaly-aware defense left to future work.
- PIR cannot distinguish erased knowledge from knowledge the model never had — both give a silent readout.
- The conceal/unknown labels come from a strict rotation-consistency gate that discards questions the model does not answer robustly, shaping the detector statistics.
- The clean domain-specific erasure result rests on the authors' single RMU run, because the external checkpoints over-forget; several organisms are single-seed and self-built; the free-form detector is demonstrated on one model and factual questions only.
- The steering result shows the direction is used but not necessary — a variance-matched ablation did not establish necessity.

## Relevance

这篇论文与可解释性、激活探针和 AI 安全评估高度相关：它把线性探针从"检测欺骗"推进到"读出被隐藏的答案"，并给出 sandbagging 审计与消学习验证两个直接可用的部署级信号——正对应我对 LLM 内部表征、模型诚实性评估与安全对齐研究的兴趣。隐藏/擦除边界的实证（尤其 circuit-breaking 仍在"隐藏"一侧）对理解消学习方法是否真正移除知识有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would PIR's hiding-vs-erasure boundary hold for unlearning methods designed to be representation-destroying rather than output-gating (e.g., rank-one editing, targeted layer surgery), or only for RMU/NPO-style methods?
- Can the option-span collapse signature left by the anti-probe attack (per-question self-correlation cosine 0.997) be turned into a practical tamper detector that survives an anomaly-aware adversary?
- How does the recognition direction relate mechanistically to known circuit-level constructs (induction heads, factual-recall circuits) — is it upstream of the output direction it steers better than?
- Does the readout extend to agentic settings where the "answer" is a plan or action rather than a short option span, and where sandboxed evaluations are the audit target?
- Why is the naturalistic (imitate-weak) sandbagger's concealed-subset recognition (0.63) so much weaker than the prefix variant's (0.85) — does imitating weakness partially blur the recognition signal itself?
- Can peak-recognition detection be calibrated per-domain to avoid false flags on genuinely-known-but-low-confidence items, given the paper compares only to chance in free-form?
