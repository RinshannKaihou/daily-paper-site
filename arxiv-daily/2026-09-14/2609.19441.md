> **一句话总结：** 提出 PreDE 框架——用小规模闭环开发集离线标定动作偏差阈值，对新量化配置提前做出"接受/拒绝/暂缓"判决；在 28 个留存配置上以 75% 覆盖率发出 21 个判决全部与实测标签一致（零误判），并在 Franka 真机上验证 W4A4 量化带来 1.37× 查询加速与约 44% 峰值显存下降。

> **TL;DR:** World action models (WAMs) built on video-generation backbones are expensive to deploy, and post-training quantization configs (bit width × grouping × quantizer) cannot be ranked by bit width alone. PreDE replays a fixed observation log through the bf16 reference and each quantized policy, calibrates per-policy accept/reject thresholds on mean absolute action deviation from a small closed-loop development set, and issues selective predictions: on held-out LIBERO-10 evaluation across 28 configurations from Cosmos Policy and UVA, it issued 21 decisions (75% coverage) that all matched observed acceptable/degraded labels — e.g., all 8 accepted Cosmos configs scored 96–98/100 vs the 97/100 bf16 reference while all 6 rejected ones scored 0/100 — whereas two post-hoc binary baselines (a global deviation cutoff and a ≥4-bit rule) made 6 and 5 false rejections at 100% coverage. On a 450-trial Franka Research 3 study, prospectively assigned high-deviation groups all degraded significantly (Fisher p<0.004), and SVDQuant-style W4A4 preserved task success (19/20 cup, 25/30 cubes vs bf16's 19/20, 25/30) while cutting median action-query latency from 2030 ms to 1485 ms (1.37×) and peak memory from 5.19 GB to 2.93 GB (~44%).

## 核心贡献

- **提出 PreDE（Predict Before You Deploy）框架**：在部署前用离线动作偏差预测量化导致的闭环任务性能退化。核心做法是把固定观测日志重放到参考策略与候选量化策略中，计算平均绝对动作分量偏差 δ̄(q; D)，再用小规模开发集的闭环结果标定两个阈值：接受阈值 δ_acc（可接受配置的最大偏差）与拒绝阈值 δ_rej（退化配置的最小偏差）。
- **形式化"同设定单交叉"假设（Hypothesis H）与选择性判决规则**：在同一策略/任务/控制条件下，可接受与退化标签可由某个偏差阈值 θ 分离。在该假设下证明了 Proposition 1（一致区间内接受/拒绝判决不会与任何确定标签矛盾）与 Proposition 2（新增评测配置只会收紧一致阈值区间），并将"暂缓（defer）"解释为与开发标签一致的各阈值之间发生分歧的证据不足区域，遵循选择性预测原则。
- **跨 5 个世界动作模型、4 类基准的量化行为表征**：覆盖 Cosmos Policy、UVA、mimic-video、RynnVLA-002、Fast-WAM 与 LIBERO/RoboCasa-24/PushT/PushT-M，证明名义位宽与全局共享偏差阈值都无法解释任务损失，必须做策略级、设定级标定。
- **前瞻式留存预测评测**：对 28 个留存配置在观测闭环结果前冻结全部判决，21 个判决（75% 覆盖率）全部与实测标签一致，无一次误接受或误拒绝；暂缓候选中既包含可接受结果也包含 33 个百分点的大幅退化，说明暂缓≠轻微退化。
- **450 次真机试验的独立行为验证与资源收益测量**：在 Franka Research 3 上，测试前分组的高偏差配置全部显著退化，而 W4A4 在保持任务成功率的同时取得 1.37× 动作查询加速与约 44% 峰值显存降低。

## Key Contributions

- **PreDE framework**: predicts quantization-induced closed-loop task degradation before deployment by replaying a fixed observation log through the reference (bf16) and candidate quantized policies and measuring mean absolute action-component deviation δ̄(q; D), then calibrating policy-specific acceptance/rejection thresholds (δ_acc = max deviation among acceptable dev configs; δ_rej = min deviation among degraded dev configs) from a small development set with closed-loop labels.
- **Formalization under a within-setting single-crossing hypothesis (H)**: for a fixed policy, task distribution, control condition, and measurement protocol, acceptable and degraded labels are separable by an unknown deviation threshold. Proposition 1 shows the accept (δ̄ ≤ δ_acc) / reject (δ̄ ≥ δ_rej) / defer (in-between) rule never contradicts a definite development label; Proposition 2 shows adding evaluated configurations never enlarges the consistent-threshold interval. Deferral follows the selective-prediction principle of withholding decisions when evidence is insufficient.
- **Broad quantization characterization across five WAMs and four benchmark settings** (Cosmos Policy, UVA, mimic-video, RynnVLA-002, Fast-WAM; LIBERO, RoboCasa-24, PushT, PushT-M), demonstrating that nominal bit width alone and any shared deviation threshold fail to explain task losses — motivating policy-specific behavioral calibration.
- **Prospective held-out prediction evaluation**: thresholds, log, and protocol were frozen before observing candidate outcomes; across 28 held-out configurations PreDE issued 21 decisions (75% coverage), all matching observed acceptable/degraded labels, with no false acceptance or rejection. Deferred candidates included both acceptable outcomes and a 33-percentage-point loss, showing deferral indicates insufficient evidence, not mild degradation.
- **Separate 450-trial real-robot study**: on Franka Research 3 with two independently fine-tuned Cosmos policies, deviation groups fixed before testing cleanly separated significant degradation from non-significant comparisons, plus measured deployment gains (W4A4: 1.37× query speedup, ~44% lower peak memory).

## Methodology

PreDE operates in three stages:

1. **Offline measurement.** A fixed observation log D (32 frames; Cosmos main comparison quantizes 280 DiT linear layers while keeping conditioning/final projections high-precision) is replayed through the reference policy π and each quantized policy π_q with identical inputs and matched action-generation seeds. The scalar statistic is the mean absolute deviation over all log frames, chunk steps, and action components: δ̄(q; D). Reference actions are cached, so scoring a new candidate costs only M offline policy queries with zero environment interaction.

2. **Policy-specific calibration.** For each development configuration q ∈ C, closed-loop task loss L̂(q) = p̂0 − p̂q is measured under matched conditions. Configurations are labeled acceptable (L̂ ≤ ε_A = 0.01), degraded (L̂ ≥ ε_R = 0.02 AND a two-standard-error evidence criterion L̂ > 2·√(p̂0(1−p̂0)/n0 + p̂q(1−p̂q)/nq) is met), or unresolved. Thresholds δ_acc and δ_rej are the max/min deviations over the acceptable/degraded subsets; the interval Θ(C) = [δ_acc, δ_rej) is the set of thresholds consistent with these labels under Hypothesis H. A contraction-style robustness argument (bounded perturbation with contraction rate ρ < 1 gives state error ≤ K_H·e/(1−ρ), which preserves success if it stays below tolerance m) motivates but does not guarantee the ordering, so thresholds are calibrated empirically.

3. **Selective prediction.** New candidates are accepted if δ̄ ≤ δ_acc, rejected if δ̄ ≥ δ_rej, and deferred otherwise (if either subset is empty or δ_acc ≥ δ_rej, everything is deferred). Decisions are frozen before closed-loop evaluation. Evaluated deferred candidates can be added to C to tighten the anchors (Proposition 2), after which the updated rule requires fresh held-out evaluation. Sensitivity is checked by repeating measurements across five matched action-generation seeds and a paired bootstrap over the log's trajectories.

Quantization configurations use RTN, HQQ (weight-only, W3/W4/W2 with group sizes g16–g128 and per-channel), ViDiT-Q-based W8A8, SVDQuant-style W4A4, and QServe-style W4A8; simulation results are averaged over three seeds following each policy's official evaluation protocol.

## Results & Highlights

- **Bit width alone is meaningless; granularity dominates.** In the single-seed Cosmos/LIBERO development comparison, RTN W3 (3-bit weights) reaches 97.40% success with group size 128 but collapses to 20.05% with per-channel quantization — a 77.35 pp gap at identical nominal precision. On Cosmos/LIBERO (four suites), W8A8/W4A4/HQQ-W4/HQQ-W3 achieve 98.12%/97.98%/98.20%/97.87% vs bf16's 98.38% (all losses ≤ 0.51 pp), while the same HQQ W3 loses 5.42 pp on Cosmos/RoboCasa-24 (61.61% vs 67.03% bf16).
- **Cross-policy threshold reversal kills any global cutoff.** Fast-WAM W3 loses 5.4 pp on LIBERO at δ̄ = 0.0154, while Cosmos HQQ W3 loses only 0.51 pp at a *larger* deviation of δ̄ = 0.0179 — a shared deviation threshold cannot accept the latter while rejecting the former.
- **Held-out prediction (LIBERO-10): 21/21 correct decisions at 75% coverage.** With Cosmos thresholds (δ_acc = 0.02191, δ_rej = 0.09736) frozen from six dev configs, all 8 accepted candidates scored 96–98/100 (bf16 reference 97/100) and all 6 rejected candidates scored 0/100 (14/14 decisions correct, 70% coverage). With UVA thresholds (δ_acc = 0.0049, δ_rej = 0.00925), the rejected HQQ W3 g=128 scored 130/160 vs the 143/160 bf16 reference (8.1 pp loss, z = 2.07), the rejected HQQ W2 g=64 scored 4/160, and the accepted HQQ W8 g=64 scored 149/160 (+3.75 pp over reference) — 7/7 decisions correct, 87.5% coverage. No false acceptance or false rejection across all 28 candidates; deferred candidates included three acceptable, one degraded (33 pp loss), and three unresolved outcomes.
- **Baselines fail where PreDE succeeds.** Post-hoc binary baselines (accept if δ̄ ≤ 0.009, or accept if weight precision ≥ 4 bits) at 100% coverage produce 6 and 5 false rejections respectively, vs PreDE's zero.
- **Real robot (450 Franka Research 3 trials).** All prospectively assigned high-deviation configurations degraded significantly (two-sided Fisher p < 0.004 for cups, p < 0.002 for cubes): RTN W2 per-channel scored 0/20 cups and 0/30 cubes; RTN W2 g128 scored 4/20 and 1/30. No low-deviation comparison reached 0.05 significance; W4A4 preserved the bf16 reference exactly (19/20 cups, 25/30 cubes vs 19/20, 25/30), while W8A8 was 19/20 and 26/30.
- **Deployment resources (RTX 5080, 45-episode instrumented collection).** W4A4 cuts median action-query latency from 2030 ms (bf16) to 1485 ms (1.37× speedup) and peak GPU memory from 5.19 GB to 2.93 GB (~44% lower); HQQ W3 lowers memory further (2.70 GB) but *increases* latency to 2404 ms (0.84×).
- **Measurement stability.** Across five action-generation seeds, candidate deviation coefficients of variation are 1.2–3.2%, and a paired bootstrap over the log's eight trajectories gives 95% lower bounds of 0.0008–0.0012 (all positive) for the anchor-minus-candidate deviation gap.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The core signal (replay a fixed observation log and measure action deviation against the reference policy) is conceptually simple; the real contributions are the calibration/deferral machinery, the single-crossing formalization, and the honest empirical characterization of why bit width and global thresholds fail. It is a validation/selection tool, not a new quantizer — readers expecting a QuantVLA/Mix-QVLA-style compression method will be disappointed.
- **Rigor**: Strong. Prospective (pre-registered-style) held-out evaluation, explicit hypothesis with proofs, statistical evidence criteria, and disclosed deferrals. However, the hypothesis is post hoc ("a post hoc formalization of the method", stated verbatim in Sec. III), and its consistency with observed labels cannot guarantee ordering on unseen configurations — a limitation the authors state plainly.
- **A genuine evaluation quirk**: the Cosmos held-out predictions are scored on LIBERO-10, which is *part of the four-suite LIBERO aggregate used for calibration* — the paper admits "Cosmos development and held-out evaluations differ in task coverage, so their pooled ordering is not a test under an identical task distribution." So the flagship 14/14 Cosmos result is not a clean task-distribution transfer; only the UVA half (thresholds calibrated on PushT-family dev sets? no — UVA calibration values are from its own dev set, tested on LIBERO-10 at 160 episodes per config) tests genuinely new tasks, and even there the degraded label for HQQ W3 g=128 rests on the z = 2.07 criterion while Fisher's exact test gives p = 0.057 — borderline at the 0.05 level.
- **The real-robot study does not test PreDE itself**: no simulation thresholds were transferred to the robot; the robot experiment only prospectively sorts configurations into low/high-deviation groups and tests group-level significance. The abstract's "all high-deviation groups degraded" is accurate but is a weaker claim than accept/reject prediction on hardware.
- **Missing ablations/comparisons**: no alternative offline predictors (cosine similarity, per-layer sensitivity, weight-perturbation norms) beyond two strawman binary baselines; no study of log size/composition (the fixed log has only 8 trajectories / 32 frames for Cosmos); and 25% of candidates land in the deferral zone containing a 33-pp loss, so a quarter of the space still needs the closed-loop evaluation PreDE is meant to avoid.
- **Significance**: practically useful for anyone deploying quantized WAM/VLA policies — it converts an expensive closed-loop search into a cheap offline screen with calibrated abstention, and the W4A4 latency/memory numbers make the payoff concrete.
- **Red flags**: none serious; the main risks are over-generalization from two policies and one benchmark suite for held-out prediction, and the mean-deviation statistic compressing error timing (the authors themselves note two candidates with similar means can differ at contact/grasp transitions — a distribution-shift issue for finite logs).

## Strengths

- Converts quantization configuration selection from exhaustive closed-loop rollouts into a cheap offline screen (cached reference actions; M policy queries, zero environment interaction) with a principled abstention mechanism.
- Prospective, frozen-threshold held-out evaluation with 21/21 correct decisions and zero false accepts/rejects — far better evidence than the retroactive correlations common in this literature.
- Definitively demonstrates that bit width, grouping, and quantizer choice interact in non-obvious ways (97.40% vs 20.05% at the same 3 bits; cross-policy threshold reversal between Fast-WAM and Cosmos), with quantifier-free explanations of *why* policy-specific calibration is necessary.
- The deferral design is honest and safe-by-construction: under Hypothesis H, Proposition 1 guarantees an issued decision never contradicts a definite label, and empirically deferral caught a 33-pp-loss candidate that a forced binary rule would have mis-predicted.
- Real-robot validation with 450 trials, prospective grouping, transparent intervals (Newcombe intervals that leave non-inferiority "unresolved" rather than claiming preservation), and concrete resource measurements (1.37× speedup, 44% memory reduction for W4A4).
- Code released; evaluation follows each policy's official protocol; full tables for all configurations including failures.

## Limitations

- Held-out prediction covers only two checkpoints (Cosmos Policy, UVA) on a single benchmark (LIBERO-10), and LIBERO-10 is included in the Cosmos calibration aggregate — generalization to genuinely new task distributions is untested.
- One rejected UVA label (HQQ W3 g=128) sits at Fisher p = 0.057 despite meeting the 2-SE criterion; with 160 episodes per config, several labels remain statistically fragile, and three of 28 outcomes were unresolved even after evaluation.
- The mean absolute deviation statistic compresses errors across observations, action components, and chunk steps; it ignores *when* errors occur (e.g., at contact or grasp transitions), and finite logs can miss decision-critical states or states reached only after quantization changes the controller (the classic covariate-shift problem in sequential prediction).
- The real-robot study validates deviation *groups*, not the calibrated accept/reject thresholds — no cross-embodiment or sim-to-real threshold transfer is attempted.
- Calibration and deferred-candidate resolution still require closed-loop interaction; 25% of candidates fell in the deferral interval, so the framework reduces but does not eliminate simulator/robot evaluation cost.
- Protocol sensitivity: any change to the policy, observation preprocessing, or evaluation protocol requires recalibration; sensitivity analysis covers only one log and two policies, and the authors note it does not establish robustness to independently collected logs or different log lengths.

## Relevance

与"极限性能优化下的可靠性"高度相关：本文正是研究激进量化（低比特、混合精度、分组/通道粒度）引入的精度误差如何转化为实际任务退化，并提供一套离线、可标定的退化预测与选择性判决机制，可直接迁移到自进化智能体与推理可靠性监测中作为低精度配置的质量门禁。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the deviation statistic be replaced or augmented with timing-aware measures (e.g., errors concentrated at contact/grasp transitions) to shrink the 25% deferral zone, and how much would log size or adaptive log selection (targeting decision-critical states) improve coverage?
- Does the single-crossing hypothesis H hold for activation quantization errors interacting with weight errors (W4A8/W8A8 mixtures), for KV-cache quantization in the video-generation backbone, or for quantization-aware-fine-tuned checkpoints rather than pure PTQ?
- Can thresholds calibrated in simulation transfer across embodiments or to real hardware with a small correction factor, enabling true sim-to-real PreDE prediction instead of group-level validation?
- How does offline action deviation relate to silent numerical corruption in long rollouts — could the same replay-based calibration detect bit-flip/SDC-induced action drift in deployed quantized policies?
- Would combining PreDE with mixed-precision search (e.g., per-layer deviation budgets à la Mix-QVLA) let the deferral interval itself guide which layers to keep in higher precision?
