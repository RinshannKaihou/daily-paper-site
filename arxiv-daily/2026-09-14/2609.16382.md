> **一句话总结：** 作者提出注意力"平均场"方法——把每个 attention head 在语料上平均后得到一个 token 类型层面的核 P(s,t)，仅用该核与冻结权重做开环 rollout 就能高精度预测 GPT-2 到 Qwen-3-14B 各层的表征几何演化（GPT-2 层间中心化余弦 0.907，远超 co-occurrence 基线 0.834）；训练早期模型与平均场不可区分、induction 出现时才分道扬镳，而对平均场的偏离量则成为一种任务无关的"上下文特定计算"探针，能无监督地找回已知 ICL 头并以单次前向传播提取可迁移的 function vector。

> **TL;DR:** This paper builds a "mean field" of attention: for each head, corpus-averaged attention defines a token-type-level kernel that, iterated with the frozen weights and MLPs in an open-loop rollout, predicts per-type centroid trajectories across layers with no free parameters and no forward pass on text — cross-layer centered cosine 0.907 on GPT-2, 0.830 on Gemma-2-2B, 0.816 on Qwen-3-14B (statistics estimated from OpenWebText), beating a co-occurrence-kernel baseline (0.834 / 0.724 / 0.258) and a context-free baseline (0.797 / 0.686 / 0.521). Substituting the mean field into Pythia-160m/1.4B/12B across 19 checkpoints leaves loss unchanged through training step ~10^2 and diverges exactly at the induction transition; per-head deviation from the mean field correlates 0.71 (Spearman, across 144 heads) with behavioral induction scores at that transition, ranks documented ICL/name-mover heads to the top of GPT-2's list, and selecting function-vector heads by deviation magnitude matches causal-mediation (AIE) transfer accuracy on Gemma-2 few-shot tasks while needing only a single forward pass.

## 核心贡献

- **注意力平均场框架**：对每个 attention head，把"类型 s 的 query 对类型 t 的平均注意力" P(s,t) 定义为一个类型层面的行随机核；配合两个明确假设（注意力-值独立性、值映射在质心处的曲率可忽略），head 对类型质心的平均贡献可写成 ẑ_s = Σ_t P(s,t)·v(c_t)。该核仅需一次语料扫描即可估计，无需优化、无拟合参数。
- **三种条件化方式**：语料条件平均场（预测平均表征几何演化）、(s,t)-条件平均场、上下文条件平均场（对 renormalized 核做单上下文预测）——前两者支撑第 4/5 节，后者支撑训练与上下文计算分析。
- **表征几何的开环预测**：从输入 embedding 出发，仅迭代"平均场核 + 模型自身 MLP"即可逐层预测类型质心轨迹，在 GPT-2、Gemma-2、Qwen-3-14B 上高精度复现真实质心演化，并系统性地优于 co-occurrence、context-free、query-blind、key-blind 等替代核。
- **"训练路标"发现**：在 Pythia 三个规模（160m/1.4B/12B）上把所有 head 替换为各自的平均场，训练前期 loss 完全不变——年幼模型在功能上就是其语料平均场；两条曲线恰在 induction 形成窗口（step 1000–2000）分离，且分离点在 75 倍参数差下由数据量而非模型规模决定。
- **上下文特定计算的度量**：对平均场的偏离（deviation）可精确分解为"注意力-值协方差"与"值的上下文化"两个可加成分；deviation 随 ICL 增长、按 deviation 排序能无监督找回已知 induction 头与 name mover，还新发现了一批位置搬运头和"内容搬运头"。
- **廉价的 function vector 提取**：任务条件下的平均场残差本身即是 function vector；按残差范数选头仅需一次前向传播（无需对比提示词），在 Gemma-2-2B 上取得与 causal-mediation（AIE）方法相当的零样本迁移精度。

## Key Contributions

- **A mean-field formalism for attention.** For each head, corpus-averaged attention defines a row-stochastic token-type kernel P(s,t); under two explicit assumptions (attention–value independence, negligible curvature of the value map at type centroids), the head's average contribution to a type's centroid is ẑ_s = Σ_t P(s,t)·v(c_t). The kernel is estimated in one corpus pass with no optimization and no fitted parameters.
- **Three conditioning levels.** A corpus-conditional mean field (predicts average-case representation-geometry evolution), an (s,t)-conditional mean field, and a context-conditional mean field (renormalizes the kernel over the types present in a single context) — each powers a different analysis.
- **Open-loop prediction of representation dynamics.** Iterating the kernel plus the model's own MLPs from the input embeddings alone reproduces per-type centroid trajectories layer by layer in GPT-2, Gemma-2-2B, Llama-3.1/3.2, and Qwen-3-14B, with a nested error decomposition attributing the dominant per-step error to the MLP-at-centroid (Jensen) gap rather than to the mean-field attention itself.
- **The mean field as a training waypoint.** Replacing every head with its checkpoint's mean field leaves next-token loss unchanged through training step ~10^2 in Pythia-160m/1.4B/12B (19 checkpoints each); the model and its mean field diverge precisely around the induction transition, with kernel CKA reorganization and head attention spread (from ~1.5x at init to >20x by end of training) marking the same event.
- **A task-agnostic meter of context-specific computation.** Mean-field deviation decomposes additively into an attention-pattern covariance term and a value-contextualization term; it localizes induction at the token level, orders prompt classes by their ICL content, recovers documented ICL machinery in an unsupervised head survey (plus newly identified positional and content-moving heads), and emerges head-by-head simultaneously with induction (Spearman 0.71 at the transition).
- **Single-forward-pass function vectors.** Task-conditioned mean-field residuals act as function vectors; ranking heads by residual norm and injecting the residual achieves zero-shot transfer comparable to causal-mediation (AIE) selection on Gemma-2-2B and GPT-J, without contrast prompts or patching sweeps.

## Methodology

The paper proceeds in four studies built on one object: the attention mean field.

1. **Construction (Sec. 3).** For a head, the contribution to query position j is z_j = Σ_i p(x_i)·v(x_i). Pooling attention over key token types gives A_st (total attention a type-s query places on type-t positions). The mean-field estimate replaces the context-specific A_st by its corpus average P_st = E_s[A_st], and the attention-weighted mean value by the value map at the type centroid v(c_t), justified by Assumption 1 (Cov_s(A_st, v_t) ≈ 0) and Assumption 2 (E_s[v_t] ≈ v(c_t), i.e., a small value-map Jensen gap). The context-conditional variant renormalizes corpus statistics W_st over the types present in context X: Â_st^X = W_st / Σ_{t'∈X} W_st', combined with corpus-averaged values μ_t; Appendix B shows the resulting estimator is slightly biased but that debiasing changes no conclusions. Appendix A removes the "each type occurs once" simplification via multiplicity-resolved rates.

2. **Open-loop rollout (Sec. 4).** Algorithm 1 starts from input embeddings (layer-0 centroids) and, per layer, computes each head's mean-field contribution at the current estimated centroids, sums head outputs, adds to the residual, and applies the MLP once at the resulting average. No forward pass on text occurs after kernel estimation. Evaluation: per-type centered cosine, RSA, relative error, and norm ratio against true centroids measured on a held-out, document-interleaved split of OpenWebText, for the N = 1000 most frequent types (64–68% of corpus positions, 67–72% of non-BOS attention across all five models); robustness checks on FineWeb, WikiText, and pre-1919 books, plus rogue-dimension and anisotropy controls. Ablations swap in context-free, co-occurrence, layer-0-only, layer-averaged, query-blind (meanq), key-blind (uniform), and row-shuffled kernels.

3. **Training-waypoint substitution (Sec. 5).** For Pythia-160m/1.4B/12B at 19 checkpoints each, every head's per-context contribution is replaced by its context-conditional mean field on 100k held-out tokens; diagnostics are the paired loss gap ΔL = L_MF − L_model and the per-head deviation D_MF. Kernel dynamics are tracked via linear CKA between checkpoints and a per-head attention-spread measure. The effect is checked in OLMo-2 where early checkpoints exist.

4. **Deviation as signal (Sec. 6).** The residual z − ẑ is decomposed exactly into a covariance term (unusual attention routing) and a contextualization term (contextualized values). Three experiments tie deviation to ICL (repeated-chunk induction on real OpenWebText text; four prompt classes — planted induction, planted few-shot with five tasks, natural text, and a matched random control with permutation tests; and checkpoint-wise head-level correlation with Olsson-style induction scores). An unsupervised survey ranks all 144 GPT-2 heads by corpus deviation, with roles cross-checked against the IOI circuit (Wang et al. 2023) and interpreted via Logit Lens / J-lens decoding of high-deviation contexts. Finally, a 2×2 comparison (head selection: AIE vs. mean-field residual norm; vector construction: raw sum vs. mean-field residual) evaluates zero-shot function-vector transfer on six tasks × 60 cue–answer pairs × 3 splits in Gemma-2-2B and GPT-J-6B.

## Results & Highlights

- **Centroid-trajectory prediction (Table 1, OpenWebText, N = 1000 types, held-out).** Cross-layer mean centered cosine / relative error for the mean-field rollout: GPT-2 0.907 (0.019) / 0.434, Gemma-2-2B 0.830 (0.011) / 0.593, Qwen-3-14B 0.816 (0.009) / 0.640 — versus the co-occurrence kernel at 0.834 / 0.567 (GPT-2) and 0.724 / 0.738 (Gemma-2), and the context-free baseline at 0.797 / 0.643 (GPT-2) and 0.686 / 0.809 (Gemma-2). In GPT-2 the centered cosine stays above 0.9 through the middle layers and above 0.75 in the final layer.
- **Error attribution (Fig. 5–6).** Teacher-forced per-head contributions are predicted with cross-layer mean centered cosine 0.78–0.87 and norm ratio 0.91–1.24 across all five models; the nested decomposition shows the MLP-at-centroid step accounts for ~0.02–0.03 of per-layer cosine shortfall, while value-at-centroid and dropping the covariance term each add at most ~0.005 per layer — rollout error is accumulation of small per-layer gaps, not a bad kernel. The Llama models are the exception: their rollouts are distinctly worse because their early-layer centroids sit where the MLP is sharply curved (almost all of Llama-3.2-3B's error traces to layer 1).
- **Early training is literally mean-field (Fig. 7).** Replacing every attention head with its mean field at each Pythia checkpoint: through step 10^2, ΔL is within a few millinats of zero while the loss itself falls by more than 3 nats; the curves separate permanently starting around step 128, across the induction band (steps 1000–2000). The transition lands at the same token count for 160m, 1.4B, and 12B (a 75x parameter range). Final losses: 3.30 (160m), 2.68 (1.4B), 2.41 (12B), while mean-field-imposed losses remain similar across scales — larger models' gains come specifically from context-dependent processing.
- **Kernel reorganization (Fig. 8).** Adjacent-checkpoint kernel CKA sits at 1.0 through step 128, drops to its minimum leading into the induction band, then recovers; head attention spread across types grows from ~1.5x at initialization (random-projection level) to more than 20x by end of training.
- **Deviation tracks ICL (Table 2, Fig. 9–11).** On GPT-2, mean deviation D_MF / mean-field loss penalty ΔL by prompt class: induction 0.46 / +9.19 nats, few-shot 0.37 / +9.90, natural text 0.29 / +2.36, random control 0.29 / −0.82 (permutation loss consistent with zero, p = 0.09/0.29); on Gemma-2-2B, induction 0.54 / +14.01 and few-shot 0.50 / +18.02 (models actually perform the tasks: Gemma-2 top-1 ≥ 98%, GPT-2 95% induction / 73% few-shot). The mean-field prediction is verified order-free (Δẑ_perm ~ 10^-7 under context permutation). Across 144 Pythia-160m heads, Spearman correlation between corpus deviation and behavioral induction score is insignificant through step 256 (all p ≥ 0.16), rises to 0.37 at step 512, and peaks at 0.71 at the documented induction transition.
- **Unsupervised head survey (Table 4).** Ranking all 144 GPT-2 heads by corpus deviation: 3 of the 4 induction heads documented by Wang et al. and 5 of the 11 name movers land in the top 20, alongside newly identified positional heads (L1H8, L2H7, L1H2, L1H3, L1H10 — 90–99% of within-type output variance in GPT-2's positional subspace) and new "content mover" heads (L9H8 moves the document's topic; L5H2, L8H3, L6H8 move frame/completion content). Previous-token and duplicate-token heads — the write stage of ICL circuits — sit near the bottom, supporting a "uniform tagging then conditional dereferencing" picture of in-context retrieval.
- **Function vectors at one forward pass (Table 5, Gemma-2-2B, six tasks, n = 120).** Zero-shot transfer, AIE-selected heads with raw vectors vs. with mean-field residual vectors: antonym 0.57 → 0.82, past-tense 0.47 → 0.87, translation 0.00 → 0.10; MFA-selected heads with residuals reach 0.76/0.93/0.26 respectively. Neither selection method dominates (the best pipeline per task usually mixes AIE heads with MFA vectors or vice versa); on GPT-J-6B the two selections tie. The MFA route needs one forward pass and no contrast prompts, versus a full causal-mediation sweep for AIE. At GPT-2's scale, injected deviation vectors carry the task's category rather than the answer itself (J-lens decoding: prompted, held-out, and unrelated capitals at median vocabulary ranks 9, 37, and 34, vs. ~21,000 for unrelated words).

## Authors & Credibility

The authors — Micah Adler, John W. Byers, and Mark Crovella — are senior faculty in Boston University's CS department and Faculty of Computing & Data Sciences; Crovella is supported by NSF grants CNS-2312711 and CNS-2319369. They are established researchers (algorithms, networking, ML-for-systems), though this is their group's first major entry in mechanistic interpretability rather than a continuation of an established interpretability agenda. The paper is unusually transparent for the field: it reports held-out evaluation splits, bias analysis of its own estimator (Appendix B), a full nested error decomposition, negative controls (rowshuffle, random-prompt class), replication in a second model family (OLMo-2), robustness across four corpora and rogue-dimension/anisotropy confounds, and marks literature-sourced head annotations as unrefereed where appropriate. A notable "Use of AI" section discloses that Claude wrote the experimental code and ran many experiments under the authors' guidance, with the authors taking full responsibility — an unusually explicit statement of AI-heavy research practice. No code release is referenced in the main text.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** The core move — averaging a head's attention over a corpus into a type-level kernel and iterating it open-loop — is genuinely new as a predictive tool, and cleanly connects the co-occurrence-geometry literature (Karkada et al., Nava & Wyart) to layer-by-layer dynamics in real trained models. The training-waypoint result (early model ≡ mean field until induction) is a striking, crisp finding; the function-vector application is more incremental, being a cheaper head-selection route over an existing framework (Todd et al.'s function vectors) rather than a new capability.
- **Rigor.** Generally high: held-out splits, exact additive decompositions, permutation controls, and multi-model/multi-corpus replication. But there are real soft spots. The evaluation covers only the top-1000 token types (64–68% of positions); the long tail — where rare-token behavior and much contextual computation plausibly lives — is bucketed into "other"/BOS columns, so "predicts representation dynamics" is a claim about frequent types, not the vocabulary. Held-out evaluation is used for GPT-2 and Gemma-2 only; Qwen-3 and Llama results use a single split, as the paper itself notes. The Qwen-3 ablations suffer a footnoted layer-0 numerical instability that inflates cooc/uniform/Pavg relative errors (96.1, 1.042→949-class values), making the Qwen ablation columns hard to read as evidence. On GPT-2 the margin over the co-occurrence kernel is real but modest in cosine (0.907 vs 0.834), so part of "mean field predicts geometry" reduces to "co-occurrence predicts geometry," which the authors honestly flag and then explain via the strong correlation between the mean field and co-occurrence.
- **Reproducibility.** Methods are specified in unusual detail (tokenizer-packing, split construction, N-sensitivity to 2000/4000 types, injection-layer sweeps fixed across methods), and all statistics come from one 2M-token corpus pass per model, so replication is feasible — but no code or kernel dumps are referenced, and the head-interpretation case studies (decoding deviation vectors with Logit Lens / J-lens on top-8 contexts) depend on judgment calls that would be hard to reproduce exactly.
- **Significance.** The training-waypoint result reframes early training (learning happens in values/MLPs/embeddings while attention stays near its frequency-proportional init) and gives the field a principled, model-internal null model for "contextual computation," which is a useful conceptual contribution beyond any single benchmark. The deviation meter's practical value is demonstrated mainly on GPT-2, where the circuit literature is rich; on Gemma-2 the survey "can only test reproducibility, not agreement with an independent account," as the authors concede.
- **Red flags.** The ICL–deviation association is carefully stated as one-directional (deviation also fires for positional heads, which have nothing to do with ICL), and Table 2's separation is a class-level effect, not a within-class correlation — worth remembering before using D_MF as an "ICL score" for individual prompts. The "content mover" characterizations are explicitly tentative ("we do not claim that these characterizations are complete or highly accurate"). The function-vector claim of "comparable accuracy" is accurate but the headline convenience (single forward pass) coexists with an uneven per-task picture (currency 0.00–0.38, translation 0.00–0.38), and the best configurations mix the two methods, which slightly undercuts a clean MFA-is-enough story. Finally, substantial parts of the experimental code and analysis were AI-generated per the disclosure; the authors' stewardship appears careful, but it is an unusual provenance for results this fine-grained.

## Strengths

- A single cheap, parameter-free object (one corpus pass per model) yields four distinct, mutually reinforcing analyses: representation prediction, training dynamics, head survey, and function-vector extraction.
- The open-loop rollout predicting centroid trajectories from input embeddings and frozen weights alone — never consulting a measured deviation — is a genuinely falsifiable and surprisingly accurate model of average-case computation.
- Honest, mechanism-level error accounting: the MLP-at-centroid Jensen gap is isolated as the leading error source, which both explains the Llama failure case and tells future users exactly what to fix.
- The training-waypoint experiment cleanly ties a computational property (order-independence) to a known developmental event (induction formation) across a 75x parameter range, and identifies attention-value independence as an empirical fact of early training rather than merely a convenient assumption.
- Strong controls throughout: matched random prompt classes, permutation tests, row-shuffle negative control, held-out corpora, and cross-family replication in OLMo-2.

## Limitations

- Analysis is restricted to the top-1000 most frequent token types (~two-thirds of positions); conclusions do not directly cover the long tail of rare types, and residual "other"/BOS buckets absorb all untracked mass.
- The rollout's accuracy degrades with depth (error accumulation), and fails outright for the two Llama models without a Jensen-gap correction — the method is architecture-sensitive in a way the kernel itself is not.
- Corpus-conditionality: the mean field is defined relative to a chosen corpus; while OpenWebText, FineWeb, WikiText, and pre-1919 books give similar results, the authors flag corpus choice as an open problem (e.g., domain-shifted text at inference time).
- The deviation measure is not a specific ICL detector — positional heads and other order-sensitive-but-non-ICL mechanisms also score high, so head-ranking results require the kind of follow-up interpretation the paper performs manually.
- Function-vector transfer is inconsistent across tasks (near-zero on currency/translation for several configurations), and the complementarity between AIE and MFA selection is observed but not explained.
- Early-training results depend on Pythia checkpoint availability (only two families have sub-1B-token checkpoints), with OLMo-2 offering only limited confirmation data points.

## Relevance

对关注机制可解释性与表征几何的研究而言，这篇论文提供了一个把"语料统计—注意力—逐层表征动力学"串起来的统一分析框架：如果你在追踪 in-context learning 的电路成因、attention head 的功能刻画或训练过程中涌现结构的定位，平均场及其偏差度量是一个可直接复用的低成本基线/探针。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the mean-field decomposition extend below the top-1000 types — e.g., with a hierarchical or clustered kernel over rare types — without losing the single-pass estimation property?
- Can the Llama MLP Jensen gap be corrected in closed form (e.g., via a local second-order expansion or learned per-layer correction), making the rollout architecture-agnostic?
- The transition from mean-field to contextual computation lands at the same token count across a 75x parameter range — is this data-quantity scaling law predictable from corpus statistics alone, and does it shift with data curation or curriculum?
- Since the best function-vector pipelines mix AIE head selection with MFA residual vectors, what does each method see that the other misses — are AIE heads capturing causal sufficiency while residual norm captures surprise relative to corpus expectation?
- The kernel P is estimated per checkpoint; could tracking individual kernel entries (rather than CKA) identify which specific type-pair routings constitute the induction-time reorganization?
- Mean-field deviation is high for positional heads as well as ICL heads — could the covariance/contextualization decomposition, or conditioning the null on syntax-aware statistics, separate "order-sensitive" from "in-context-inferential" computation?
- What happens at inference-time distribution shift: does prediction fidelity on a target domain depend on estimating the kernel on that domain, and could per-domain kernels serve as a lightweight domain-adaptation diagnostic?
