> **一句话总结：** PaperDoctor 是一个"诊断式"论文评审 Agent：用三层分级流水线（L1 表面筛查 / L2 分类型断言验证 / L3 按优先级复现实验）对进行中的论文稿产生每条都带证据定位与修改建议的三元组反馈；在 30 篇真实在写论文的人机研究中获得 70.6% 的作者认可率和全部正向总分（均值 +1.30），且 100% 的反馈同时携带证据与建议（审稿人报告仅 35.9%，Stanford Agentic Reviewer 仅 1.5%），其实验复现层还能抓出"声称 recall 提升近 2 倍、实测仅 1.14 倍"这类读稿无法发现的定量问题。

> **TL;DR:** PaperDoctor (Oxford/Stanford/NUS/UW/Cambridge) reframes automated paper assessment from a judge emitting accept/reject verdicts into a diagnostician emitting advisor-style feedback: every finding is a triple of (issue, evidence pointer to an exact sentence/equation/code line, concrete revision suggestion), produced by a cost-tiered three-level pipeline — L1 surface screening (writing/figures/citations), L2 typed claim verifiers (code cross-checks, theory re-derivation, web-search literature checks, experiment-design review), and L3 prioritized experiment reproduction executed only after author approval. On a human study with 30 in-progress papers (1,299 feedback items), authors accepted 70.6% of evidence claims and rated all 30 reviews positively (mean +1.30 on a −1..+2 scale). Against referee reports and the Stanford Agentic Reviewer on 40 papers (ICML, Nature Communications, Nature Human Behaviour, Agent4Science), 100% of PaperDoctor findings carry both evidence and suggestion vs 35.9% for referees and 1.5% for the agentic reviewer, and its L3 stage shows only 14.4% (19/167) of executed ICML plans match reported numbers while 47.0% of all reproduction plans never even reach a runnable command.

## 核心贡献

- **从"审判者"到"诊断者"的范式转换**：把自动论文评估的目标从输出录用判断（服务于审稿方）改为输出可审计、可执行的修改反馈（服务于作者），明确对标人类导师对草稿的逐条批注式诊断。
- **三层分级流水线（成本与验证代价成正比）**：L1 表面筛查直接跑在手稿上（写作、图表 VLM 评审、引文核查、原子断言抽取）；L2 把每条断言按证据类型路由到对应验证器（代码核对、理论重推导、文献检索、实验设计审查）；L3 按优先级选择性重建并重跑实验，且需作者在看到预估 GPU 时数/存储代价后人工批准才执行。
- **证据接地的三元组输出契约**：每条发现 F=(f, e, s)——问题描述、指向论文中确切位置的锚点（句子/公式/代码行/外部 URL）、具体修改建议——使得捏造的批评会"死在自己的锚点上"，作者可直接核验。论文从未输出没有引用和建议的记录，附带 error/warning 两级严重度。
- **大规模实证**：30 篇真实进行中论文的人机研究（作者逐条评判 1,299 项反馈）+ 40 篇带代码数据的已发表论文（ICML oral/spotlight、Nature Communications、Nature Human Behaviour、AI 写作的 Agent4Science）上与人类审稿报告和 Stanford Agentic Reviewer 的系统对比。
- **实验复现瓶颈的定量刻画**：L3 复现揭示了双重瓶颈——执行前（47.0% 的计划因仓库信息不足从未跑起命令）与执行后（跑起来的计划中 34.5% 数值不匹配/失败），并给出"高优先级计划通过率 47.3% vs 低优先级 33.6%"等规律。
- **开源交互界面**：作者上传论文+代码即可在原文上逐条浏览叠加的彩色标注气泡与对应代码视图。

## Key Contributions

- **Judge-to-diagnostician reframing.** Automated reviewers return verdicts that serve venues; PaperDoctor returns advisor-style feedback that serves authors, targeting the unmet need created by autoresearch agents (AI Scientist, Agent Laboratory, etc.) that flood the literature with hard-to-verify claims lacking any internal quality-control stage.
- **Holistic hierarchical pipeline with cost tiering.** A parsing step decomposes (paper, code) into section-organized markdown (Mathpix), page images (for VLMs), tree-sitter-indexed code units, and a parsed bibliography; then L1 runs cheap paper-only screens, L2 routes each atomic claim to the verifier owning its evidence type, and L3 runs expensive reproduction only under a priority budget ({high, medium, low}; evaluations before training; ready before blocked).
- **Evidence-grounded actionable output contract.** Every finding is a triple (finding, evidence pointer, suggestion) with error/warning severity — enforced by design ("PaperDoctor never emits a record with no quote and no suggestion"), making each critique auditable and directly actionable.
- **Author-in-the-loop reproduction.** The L2 experiment-design review emits a declarative reproduction plan (command, priority, feasibility, run mode, numeric target from the paper); L3 presents it with estimated GPU hours for manual approval before an LLM-driven executor handles environment setup, dispatch, and log parsing, with context-aware pass/warning/error verdicts rather than a fixed numeric threshold.
- **Dual evaluation methodology.** (i) A human study on 30 genuine pre-submission drafts from 25 graduate students, with authors rating every item on Evidence and Suggestion axes plus a holistic −1..+2 score; (ii) a 40-paper comparison against the papers' actual published referee reports and one run of the Stanford Agentic Reviewer across ML, natural-science, and social-science venues, including both human- and AI-authored papers.
- **Released artifacts.** An interactive diagnosis interface overlaying findings on the paper with a code viewer, plus case studies of caught issues (impossible citation dates, paper-code mismatches, undefined theorem symbols, refuted novelty claims, overstated "factor of 2" claims).

## Methodology

PaperDoctor takes a paper plus its code and data and produces findings F_i = (f_i, e_i, s_i). Because feeding everything to one model is infeasible, a single parsing step produces four reusable artifacts: P_t (paper as section-organized markdown via Mathpix), P_v (page-by-page rendered images for VLMs), C_t (code indexed with tree-sitter into per-file units), and B (parsed bibliography). Each downstream skill requests only the slice it needs.

**L1 — Surface Screening** runs directly on the manuscript at low cost, with four parallelizable skills: (1) Writing Review (LLM reads section by section, flagging typos/grammar as errors and clarity issues as warnings); (2) Figure Review (VLM assesses figure design and readability); (3) Citation Check (verifies bibliography entries, e.g., catching a GPT-4.5 reference dated 2024 when the model shipped Feb 27, 2025); (4) Claim Extraction, which pulls atomic claims tagged with evidence types [code, theory, literature, experiments] and dispatches each to the matching L2 branch.

**L2 — Claim Verification** routes each claim to a typed verifier: (a) Code Verification cross-checks implementation claims against the tree-sitter-indexed repo — e.g., a paper claiming SciBERT 768-dim embeddings while all three config/code files set all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (384-dim), a mismatch invisible to reading-only review; (b) Theory Verification re-derives proofs and checks symbol definitions — e.g., flagging an approximation bound whose W, L, α, β, C_1–C_3 are never defined; (c) Literature Check uses web search to separate baseline comparisons, cited facts, and novelty assertions — e.g., refuting a claim that DeepONet/FNO "struggle with heterogeneous PDEs" using the very papers cited; (d) Experiment Design review evaluates fairness, ablation sufficiency, statistical rigor, baseline recency, and cherry-picking risk, emitting a declarative reproduction plan (command, priority, feasibility, run mode, numeric target) — no experiments run here. All four verifiers run modularly in parallel.

**L3 — Experiment Reproduction** ranks plan entries by importance to the paper's central contributions (high-priority items before medium/low, evaluations before training, ready before blocked), presents the plan with estimated GPU hours/dataset size/storage for manual author approval, then an LLM-driven executor handles environment setup, dispatch, and log parsing. Each executed experiment gets a contextual verdict — pass, warning (partial match), or error (execution failure or numeric mismatch beyond the paper's typical variance) — e.g., reproducing a Nature Communications claim of "almost a factor of 2" recall gain to an actual 1.14x best-pair ratio (1.04x mean).

## Results & Highlights

**Human study (30 in-progress papers, 25 graduate students, 1,299 feedback items, code verification excluded and evaluated separately):**
- Authors accepted 70.6% of PaperDoctor's evidence claims overall (mean per-paper rate 68.5%; median 71.1%; range 23.5%–97.0%).
- All 30 holistic scores were positive — 70% "somewhat helpful" (+1), 30% "very helpful" (+2) — for a mean of +1.30 on the −1 (harmful) to +2 (very helpful) scale.
- L2 typed verifiers were rejected only 11% of the time vs 27% for L1 surface checks; Experiment-design findings were the largest category (539 items) with 81% acceptance, while Figure Review had the lowest acceptance at 42%.
- Accepting evidence almost implied accepting the fix: 97.1% of items with accepted Evidence also had their Suggestion accepted, vs 3.3% for items whose Evidence was rejected.
- Correlation between per-paper evidence acceptance and holistic score was positive but not significant (r = +0.29, P = 0.12).

**40-paper comparison vs referee reports and Stanford Agentic Reviewer (parsed into atomic points by Gemini-2.5-Flash):**
- Evidence grounding: 100% of PaperDoctor findings carry both Evidence and Suggestion by design, vs 35.9% for referee reports and only 1.5% for the Stanford Agentic Reviewer; on Evidence alone, referees provide it in 45.2% of findings vs 2.2% for the agentic reviewer (whose dominant pattern, 69.9%, is suggestion-without-evidence).
- Coverage: PaperDoctor produces 36.1–56.4 findings per paper (flatter distribution: 28.4% experiments, 12.5% external literature, 11.5% code) vs the agentic reviewer's 25.2–44.0; referees/agentic reviewer spend 90.1%/89.5% of findings on the paper main body. Referee reports on Agent4Science/ICML papers contain only 6.9/6.7 findings per source vs 16.2/17.5 on Nature journals.
- Cost of grounding: 50.0 words per finding (33.9 evidence + 16.1 suggestion) vs 29.1 (referee) and 27.1 (agentic reviewer).
- The Code check separates authorship: 8.4 findings/paper on AI-written Agent4Science papers vs 4.6/4.1/2.4 for the three human-written groups.

**L3 reproduction on 40 papers (272 blocked + executed plans):**
- Of 43.8 experimental claims per paper, 14.5 become reproduction plans; only 18.0% are initially ready to run; after agent-resolving feasible blockers, 7.7 plans reach execution and 5.1 return pass-or-warning per paper. 47.0% of plans never reach a command at all; among the 53.0% that execute, 27.0% yield warnings and 34.5% errors. The largest single blocker across all 272 blocked plans is an incomplete runnable environment (missing code or weights), 33.1%.
- Priority ordering works: high-priority plans pass 47.3% (error 29.1%) vs low-priority 33.6% (error 39.7%); run mode matters more — training passes only 11.3% (error 58.5%) vs inference-from-checkpoint 39.0% and analysis/statistical pipelines 48.9%.
- Contrasting failure modes: ICML papers fail after execution (only 19 of 167 executed plans match reported numbers, 14.4%), while AI-written Agent4Science papers fail before execution (68 of 86 blocked) but 13 of the 18 that run match (72.2%); NatureScience is stopped mostly by wet-lab procedures (46.2% of its blocked plans) and SocialScience by incomplete code (65.2%).

## Authors & Credibility

A 17-author team spanning Oxford (Kevin Qinghong Lin, Philip Torr — corresponding), Stanford (Pan Lu, Yuanfeng Ji, Sheng Liu, James Zou — corresponding), NUS (Siyuan Hu, Mike Zheng Shou), UW, and Cambridge, i.e., groups with established track records in AI-for-science and LLM evaluation: the Zou/Thakkar line behind the 20k-review ICLR-2025 feedback RCT, the Stanford Agentic Reviewer they benchmark against, and prior verification work (PaperBench, citation auditing). The evaluation design is unusually honest for this genre: the human study collects item-level verdicts (not just satisfaction), the 40-paper comparison includes the papers' own real referee reports rather than only LLM baselines, and the paper explicitly refuses to treat venue or pass rate as a quality ranking. GitHub and website links plus a demo interface are released. The main credibility caveats: agreement is self-reported by the same authors whose papers were critiqued (no external ground truth), and the paper does not name the backbone LLM(s) powering PaperDoctor's own skills (only Gemini-2.5-Flash for post-hoc parsing is disclosed).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty (moderate, integrative).** Every ingredient exists separately — citation verifiers (CiteAudit), reproduction benchmarks (PaperBench, AutoReproduce), review agents (MARG, DeepReview, CycleResearcher, MMReview). The genuine novelty is the diagnostic framing plus the enforced (finding, evidence, suggestion) output contract and the cost-tiered L1/L2/L3 architecture. Their own Table 1 positioning is fair, though "Human advisor" as a comparison row is rhetorical rather than empirical.
- **Rigor — evaluation ground truth is circular.** The headline 70.6% agreement is authors judging feedback on their own drafts; there is no independent verification of which findings were actually correct, and the authors' own comment analysis shows "items it got wrong" drew 4 criticisms vs 1 praise — the widest negative margin. Per-paper acceptance ranges 23.5%–97.0%, so experience is highly variable, and the evidence-acceptance/holistic-score correlation is not significant (r=+0.29, P=0.12).
- **Rigor — comparison confounds.** On the 40-paper set, "more findings" (36–56 per paper) is presented as an advantage without a precision/recall ground truth; a system emitting 56 findings at unknown precision burdens authors. The comparison also depends on Gemini-2.5-Flash parsing heterogeneous feedback into "atomic points," an unquantified LLM-in-the-loop.
- **Reproducibility gaps.** The backbone LLM(s) behind each PaperDoctor skill, prompts, compute costs per paper, and total runtime are not reported in the main text; the human study also excluded Code Verification (justified — pre-submission code is incomplete — but it is the most novel L2 check, so its author-facing utility is only indirectly evidenced via the 40-paper set).
- **L3 capability is honestly limited.** 47.0% of plans never run, 34.5% of executed plans error, and training reruns pass only 11.3% — the paper frames these as findings about the literature's reproducibility, but they equally measure the agent's execution ability, and the two are not disentangled (acknowledged, but not resolved, e.g., no human-expert reproduction attempts as a ceiling).
- **Known blind spots admitted.** Figure Review acceptance is only 42% (VLMs misread dense panels; figure design is partly author taste), and authors criticized the system's "silence on novelty and framing" — PaperDoctor checks whether claims are true, not whether they matter.
- **Significance.** Despite these limits, the evidence-triple contract and the demonstrated catching of concrete errors reading-only review misses (SciBERT-vs-MiniLM mismatch, the 1.14x-vs-"factor of 2" recall claim in a peer-reviewed Nature Communications paper) make this a credible step toward scalable pre-submission QA, especially as a plug-in QC stage for autoresearch agents.
- **Red flags.** None severe: no cherry-picked single-number boasting; failure modes are reported prominently. Minor: the "+1.30 mean" sits on a coarse 4-point scale from a small sample (n=30, only 24 free-form comments).

## Strengths

- Enforces auditability structurally rather than aspirationally — 100% evidence+suggestion coverage vs 35.9% (referees) and 1.5% (Stanford Agentic Reviewer) is a by-construction property other systems could adopt.
- Cost-tiered design is the right economics: cheap screens always run; expensive reproduction is prioritized, budgeted, and gated behind author approval with estimated GPU hours — a sensible safety pattern for agents that execute code.
- The dual evaluation (author-facing human study + published-paper comparison against real referee reports) is more informative than the usual "agrees with human review scores" protocol used by LLM-reviewer papers.
- The L3 analysis yields transferable insights: reproduction is bottlenecked both pre-execution (33.1% incomplete environments) and post-execution; priority predicts pass rate; and AI-written vs ICML papers exhibit cleanly contrasting failure modes.
- Concrete, verifiable case studies (impossible citation date, paper-code embedding mismatch, undefined theorem symbols, refuted novelty claim, overstated 2x claim) let readers audit the system's claimed competence themselves.
- Honest framing: explicitly states it does not judge novelty or whether a result matters, and refuses to rank venues by pass rate.

## Limitations

- No external ground truth for feedback correctness; agreement comes from the critiqued papers' own authors, and holistic scores come from a small sample (30 papers, 24 comments) on a coarse scale.
- Backbone models, prompts, per-paper runtime, and compute cost of running PaperDoctor itself are not disclosed in the main text, hindering exact reproduction of the system.
- L3 reproduction is weak where it matters most: training-based experiments pass only 11.3% of the time, and 47.0% of plans never execute; wet-lab sciences (46.2% of blocked NatureScience plans) are largely out of scope.
- Figure understanding remains a weak point (42% author acceptance), and VLM misreads on dense panels are acknowledged.
- The system is silent on novelty, framing, and significance — the judgment-heavy parts of advising — and its precision draws the most author criticism (false positives: 4 criticisms vs 1 praise).
- The 40-paper comparison counts findings and their properties but never measures precision against ground truth, so "more and better-grounded findings" is not the same as "more correct findings"; the Stanford Agentic Reviewer baseline is a single run of one system.
- Requires the paper to ship code and data for its most distinctive checks; for code-less drafts, L2 code verification and L3 reduce to reading-only review (paper-to-code synthesis is left as future work).

## Relevance

这篇论文与"LLM Agent 自动化科研与评审"方向高度相关：它直接回应了 autoresearch agent 大规模产出低可信论文的质量控制缺口，其证据接地三元组输出契约、成本分级 L1/L2/L3 流水线和作者批准式实验复现，是构建可信 AI-for-Science 质检环节的直接可借鉴设计。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- What are the per-finding precision and recall of each L1/L2 skill against an externally verified ground truth (e.g., issues later confirmed by published rebuttals or errata), rather than author self-report?
- How much does the choice of backbone LLM ( undisclosed in the paper ) drive the 70.6% agreement — would a cheaper open model collapse the L2 verifiers' advantage over L1 (11% vs 27% rejection)?
- Can the pre-execution blocker (33.1% incomplete environments) be attacked by synthesizing missing analysis code from the paper itself, and how far does pass rate fall in that PaperBench-style regime?
- Does interactive author contestation of individual findings (accept/reject per item, re-running affected checks) measurably improve downstream draft quality or acceptance outcomes?
- Could PaperDoctor-style reproduction reports be attached to submissions as standardized artifacts, and how do its verdicts correlate with post-publication replication studies?
- Is the 97.1% conditional suggestion acceptance a real endorsement of fix quality, or an artifact of authors accepting bundled evidence-plus-fix pairs they cannot separate?
