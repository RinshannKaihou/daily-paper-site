⚠Critical edge — don't read this uncritically

Goodhart risk is real: the headline “72.1% acceptance” is acceptance by a specific LLM reviewer — one the system itself optimizes against — not a human program committee.

Most sober signal: 9 human reviewers rate it at overall parity (3.0/5), still behind humans on methodological rigor (2.9).

01Methodology · 方法蒸馏

Discovery is formalized as (P⁺, C⁺) = A(G): given a peer-reviewed problem G — a paper plus code from ICLR / ICML / NeurIPS — an ecosystem of specialized agents delivers a new paper and a verified executable codebase. Every stage runs the same generator → critic → refine loop.

01

Limitation extraction

Verifier-gated, up to 16 rounds; novelty-ranked seed ideas (2 references retrieved per idea).

02

Subset → full-set evaluation

Baseline reproduced on a benchmark subset first; a Subset Critic (Good / Bad / Engineer) prunes; survivors scale to the full multi-dataset suite.

03

Trace-based idea evolution

Aggregates successes and failure logs over K=4 rounds; fresh high-novelty seeds each round to balance exploration vs exploitation.

04

Automated ablations

Component-level plans; a refined method must strictly outperform the incumbent or is rejected — human-style subtraction.

05

Simulated review + executed rebuttal

ScholarPeer scores 1–10; below 8 triggers the Rebuttal Coder to actually run new experiments — not prose edits. Meta-review can force a brand-new idea.

Stack: Gemini 3.6 Flash powers most agents; Claude Code + Opus 4.8 handle idea-experiment coding, ablations, rebuttals, and draft enhancement.

03Strengths · 优势

  • Closes the loop prior systems left open: rebuttals execute real supplementary experiments; meta-review can force method-level reinvention with strict empirical re-verification.
  • Gains must survive component-level ablation before acceptance — a meaningful barrier against metric-hacking; ablations also prune the method, human-style.
  • Rare honesty for a flagship paper: reports the ICLR-subset loss to AutoSOTA, sub-spotlight ratings, the caught reward-hacking case, and a human study including the dimension where humans win.
  • Integrity by construction — reproducible scripts, search-grounded citations, method-code audit — with a clean ablation showing each safeguard matters (19/1840 → 0/1814 hallucinated refs).
  • Breadth: 107 problems across LLMs, RL, optimization, theory, time series, neuroscience, speech, and privacy, plus cost and backend-generalization analyses.

04Limitations · 局限

  • No spotlight / oral-level conceptual breakthroughs; gains concentrate on algorithmic refinement, not paradigm-level discovery — acknowledged by the authors.
  • ~$3,800 and 2.5 days per task on proprietary models (Gemini 3.6 Flash, Opus 4.8) — replication essentially impossible for academic labs.
  • Headline claims rest on automated reviewers, one of which (ScholarPeer) is also the optimization target; mean 25.2% ≫ median 7.7%, and the 21 failed problems are excluded.
  • Novelty checking retrieves only 2 references per idea; the entire evaluation stack — drafter, reviewer, audit, baselines — is largely same-group, so independent validation is needed.

05Questions for Further Exploration · 待探索

Q1

Would the 86 generated papers survive a real human program committee — how large is the LLM-reviewer vs human acceptance gap?

Q2

How skewed is the 25.2% mean? Identify the top-5 outlier tasks; does the median survive an independent re-run of the codebases?

Q3

Does the rebuttal loop Goodhart the reviewer — would the Draft Enhancer exploit published figure/table biases, detectable by an adversarially-held-out reviewer?

Q4

Can novelty verification scale beyond 2 retrieved references (full-corpus arXiv search) without breaking the 2.5-day / $3.8K budget?

Q5

How much gain survives replacing Gemini / Opus with open-weight models — does idea evolution still work when weaker coders drop to 60% success?

Q6

Does the compounding sequence (+10.9% → +9.6% → +8.2%) plateau — and can cross-problem transfer of discovered tricks extend the frontier more cheaply?
