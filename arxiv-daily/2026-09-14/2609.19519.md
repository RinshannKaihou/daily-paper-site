> **一句话总结：** Salesforce 提出"层级—时钟节拍—级联智能"三件套的长程 Agent 架构（7 层时间尺度 + 有界文件状态 + 失败才升级的模型级联），让 Agent 在 10 天、211 个 tick、47 次上下文重置中自主复现了一篇异步 RL 论文的结论，全程只需人类每天看一次（10 天仅 25 次上报）。

> **TL;DR:** A position-plus-experience paper from Salesforce AI Research arguing that a long-horizon agent must run continually without forgetting before it can learn continually, and that this is a property of the harness, not the model. It derives seven bottlenecks (context/process/attention/failure all ≪ horizon, plus delegation, correctness, cost) and answers them with a 7-level temporal hierarchy of bounded state files, a clocked "tick" as the unit of autonomous action, and review-and-escalate cascaded intelligence. Evidence: a 10-day autonomous campaign that reproduced the staleness-collapse result of Hou et al. 2026 (uncorrected stale-rollout policy collapses; decoupled importance-sampling correction stays stable, at both model scales), with 211 ticks / 47 context resets / 2,215 of 3,000 GPU-hours, only 25 escalations to a once-a-day human, 86% of labor spend at the routine tier, and steered labor cutting large-model step time from 958 s to 180 s per step.

## 核心贡献

- **一个前提推出七个瓶颈**：当 Agent 的轨迹长度 H 远超上下文窗口 C、进程寿命 P、人类关注间隔 A 和组件失效间隔 F（C, P, A, F ≪ H）且在高自主性、有预算 B 约束下运行时，必然产生七个瓶颈：Autonomy（无人提示）、Horizon（无上下文装得下）、State（进程必死）、Delegation（工作超单进程）、Correctness（错误无人在场检查且被压缩掩盖）、Resilience（组件必失效）、Cost（顶级模型开销随 H 线性增长）。
- **四抽象构成长程架构**：Level（按时间尺度分层的七级体系，L0 工具调用秒级 → L6 目标周级，每层只保留对下一层的有界压缩文件，如 L3 checkpoint ≤ 8k 字符、L5 日报 ≤ 1,500 字符）；Tick（驻判断层的一次完整"醒来—读状态—决策—行动—写回"节拍，由时钟驱动，写完 checkpoint 才允许结束会话）；Protocol（每个通道和持久状态都是有指定写者/读者/节奏/检查的命名文件）；Tier（判断层/劳动力层（常规与强化）/确定性进程三级智能，层级越高越聪明）。
- **级联智能的"评审—升级"规则**：工作先交给最低可信层级，由上一层评审，失败以反馈退回重试，二次失败才升一级直至人类；强化层级按"犯错的代价"而非任务大小指派；强层级找到的配方（recipe）会写回常规层级的 brief 中，使后续同类工作降回低成本层。
- **"不遗忘 + 累积"两个可验证性质**：任何所需状态都不活在上下文里（driver 被压缩/轮换/看门狗重置数十次后每次都从文件恢复）；早期写下的操作知识（常设决策、评审员发现、配方、失败规则→harness 守卫）在不改模型权重的情况下改变后续整个 campaign 的行为——这是"累积"而非"学习"，但精确定位了学习组件应插入的位置：在留存的 gate（检查点）内部。
- **十日自主 campaign 的存在性证明**：Agent 自主复现了异步 RL 论文（Hou et al. 2026）的 staleness 崩溃结论，跨五个门控阶段、约 40 个实验、30 次对抗性评审（7 次关键发现），人类每天只关注一次。

## Key Contributions

- **From one premise to seven bottlenecks**: with horizon H such that context window C, process lifetime P, human attention interval A and failure interval F are all ≪ H at high autonomy within budget B, seven bottlenecks follow — Autonomy, Horizon, State, Delegation, Correctness, Resilience, Cost — each stated as a derivable requirement rather than an ad-hoc feature list.
- **Four abstractions that answer them**: levels indexed by time scale (L0 tool call at seconds up to L6 goal at a week, each keeping a bounded file compressing the level below — e.g., L3 checkpoint ≤ 8k chars, L5 daily report ≤ 1,500 chars, a hard 40-line read limit on any log or source); the tick as the clocked unit of autonomous action; protocols as named files with designated writer/reader/cadence/check; and three intelligence tiers (judgment, labor, deterministic processes).
- **Cascaded intelligence via review-and-escalate**: work runs at the lowest plausible tier, is reviewed one tier up, returns as feedback on first failure, and is raised one tier only on second failure, up to the principal; the strong tier is assigned by the cost of being wrong, and its recipes are handed back down into routine briefs.
- **Operation without forgetting + accumulation as pre-learning properties**: the driver survived dozens of resets (compaction, rotation, watchdog) by resuming purely from files (checkpoint, standing decisions, plan, experiment cards; journal wins if newer), and early written knowledge (standing decisions, reviewer rules, recipes, a stop-guard born from a recurring worker failure) changed later behaviour with zero weight updates.
- **An existence proof by campaign**: ten autonomous days reproducing a published asynchronous-RL result (Hou et al. 2026) across five gated phases, with adversarial reviewers catching reward-hacking paths in a task mix and a stop rule that would have compared against the control's transient dip.

## Methodology

The paper is a position paper validated by a single longitudinal deployment, not a benchmark study. First, it characterizes the long-horizon setting on two axes — autonomy (share of decisions taken without a person) and horizon (trajectory length in actions/tokens/wall time) — and derives the seven bottlenecks from the premise C, P, A, F ≪ H. Second, it constructs the architecture from four abstractions (level, tick, protocol, tier): a 7-level temporal hierarchy (L0 tool call → L1 labor turn → L2 steering → L3 tick → L4 gate review → L5 human day → L6 goal) where directives travel down sparsely and compressed state, back-pressure and kill verdicts travel up (kill verdicts are the only uncompressed messages, because compression would hide them). Mechanism families per bottleneck include: a clock plus hook refusing session end before the checkpoint is written (Autonomy); abstraction by time scale with a 40-line read limit (Horizon); files as memory with a resume protocol after every compaction (State); briefs as one-file contracts plus PROGRESS/DIRECTIVE files for steered labor (Delegation); pre-registered hypotheses, success criteria, early-stop rules, seeds and an adversarial reviewer with no conversation history at every gate (Correctness); detectors one level up, watchdogs and judgment-free recovery (Resilience); tier-by-level and review-and-escalate with session rotation and pre-tick digests (Cost).

Third, the experience: the agent was asked to reproduce Hou et al. 2026 — that a policy trained on stale rollouts collapses beyond a staleness threshold and a decoupled importance-sampling correction keeps it stable, at two model scales. The campaign ran through five gated phases (environment bring-up; small-scale stability pair; one-node large-model recipe under steered labor; port to a second framework validated by *realised* rather than requested staleness and matched on half the training hardware; effectiveness comparison on a calibrated environment that went through four redesigns under review gates), with the principal attending once per day and tallies extracted from the project record.

## Results & Highlights

- **Reproduction outcome (the paper's headline)**: the uncorrected policy trained on stale rollouts collapsed beyond the staleness threshold at both model scales while the corrected (decoupled importance-sampling) policy held, and the full method reached the ceiling of the calibrated environment ahead of its control — matching the published claim of Hou et al. 2026 (arXiv 2607.07508).
- **Steered-labor speedup**: three unsteered worker sessions on the one-node large-model recipe ended at 958 seconds per training step with no diagnosis; after the driver wrote six ranked hypotheses (each with a prediction and a guard) and a directive every 30 minutes, step time fell to 180 seconds within one bounded session — a 5.3× improvement — and the recipe held without escalating to the strong tier.
- **Campaign tallies (Table 1)**: 10 calendar days (8 under the clock), 211 driver ticks across 47 context resets, 403 routine vs 35 strong worker sessions, ~40 experiments, 30 adversarial reviews yielding 7 critical findings, 25 escalations of which 9 were principal-only decisions, and 2,215 GPU-hours used against a 3,000 GPU-hour cap (74% of budget).
- **Cost of cascading**: labor spend came out at 86% routine tier vs 14% strong tier, i.e., review-and-escalate kept the most capable model's usage to roughly one-seventh of total labor.
- **Adversarial review catches**: the reviewer found a path by which the effectiveness task's verifier could be satisfied without solving the task plus two shortcut families (task mix rebuilt), and later found a stop rule that would have compared an arm against its control's transient dip (rule rewritten before the result was recorded).
- **Framework port**: moving to a second training framework changed only launch scripts and an adapter; every brief, card, directive and review format carried over, and the port was validated by realised staleness while matching the reproduced run on half the training hardware.

## Authors & Credibility

Four authors, all at Salesforce AI Research: Erik Nijkamp, Anurag Koul, Egor Pakhomov, and Bo Pang. Nijkamp is a veteran of large-scale generative-model engineering at Salesforce, and the group has a track record in code generation and agent systems, which lends practical credibility to a harness/engineering-centric paper — the tallies (ticks, resets, GPU-hours) read like instrumentation they actually ran rather than thought experiments. The framing is honest about genre: the paper self-identifies as a position/experience report ("an existence proof rather than a benchmark"), published as a preprint (cs.AI, 17 Sep 2026). Citations are well-chosen and current (compaction-induced constraint loss, goal drift, multi-agent failure taxonomies, temporal abstraction back to Sutton et al. 1999).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: the individual ingredients — hierarchical temporal abstraction, file-based memory, model cascades, watchdog-driven recovery — are all well precedented (the paper cites them). The novelty is the synthesis and, more interestingly, the framing that "accumulation without learning is the baseline" any weight-updating method must beat. That framing is the paper's most quotable and defensible contribution.
- **Rigor / evidence base**: this is the weakest flank, acknowledged by the authors themselves. One campaign, one task, one principal; no comparison condition (e.g., the same task run by a flat/non-hierarchical agent, or by an existing autonomous-research system such as those benchmarked by MLReplicate), so there is no way to attribute the success to the architecture rather than to model capability or task tractability. The claim "operating knowledge written early changed later behaviour" is supported by vignettes (the stop-guard anecdote), not by controlled measurement.
- **Reproducibility**: mixed. The architecture is described at the abstraction level (levels, protocols, escalation rules, character budgets) and the reproduction target is a cited public paper, but no code, prompts, or protocol templates are released with the preprint, and the two model scales and the two training frameworks are never named — a reader cannot re-run the campaign or even fully specify it.
- **Self-reported metrics**: all quantitative tallies come from the authors' own project record; the "calibrated environment" for the effectiveness comparison was built in-house and redesigned four times mid-campaign, which makes the "reached the ceiling ahead of control" claim hard to calibrate against anything external.
- **Red flags / overclaiming**: modest overall — the paper explicitly labels itself an existence proof. One soft spot: the steered-labor 958→180 s/step story is a single anecdote with no baseline variance reported (were the three failed sessions just unlucky?), and the 86%/14% labor split is presented as a cost win without a comparison against what a non-cascaded design would have spent.

## Strengths

- Deriving requirements from a single crisp premise (C, P, A, F ≪ H) gives the design space unusual coherence — every mechanism traces back to a named bottleneck, summarized in a clean requirement table.
- The harness-over-model position is well-argued and timely, backed by cited failure-taxonomy work locating most agent failures in the interaction layer rather than the model.
- Concrete, falsifiable engineering rules (checkpoint ≤ 8k chars, ≤ 40 lines of any log, kill verdicts as the only uncompressed channel, journal-beats-checkpoint on resume) that practitioners can adopt immediately.
- Honest epistemic hygiene within the campaign: pre-registered success criteria and stop rules, realised rather than requested regime conditions, and an adversarial reviewer with no conversation history — the vignettes where review caught a verifier-hackable task mix and a misleading stop rule are genuinely instructive.
- The closing analysis — record-as-dataset, gates-as-insertion-points for learned policies — turns an experience report into a research agenda for continual learning.

## Limitations

- Single campaign, single domain (RL reproduction), single principal: no statistical replication and no second task to test generality; the authors admit this outright.
- No baseline architecture was run on the same task, so the ablation question — which of the four abstractions actually carried the campaign — is unanswered.
- No artifact release (code, prompts, protocol files) and unnamed models/frameworks/scales limit external reproducibility.
- "Accumulation" is demonstrated anecdotally rather than measured (e.g., no counts of how often early rules were invoked later, or counterfactual runs without them).
- The ten-day horizon still sits at the short end of "long-horizon" enterprise work (weeks-to-months campaigns are the stated motivation), so scaling behaviour of the seven-level hierarchy beyond a week is untested.

## Relevance

对关注 LLM Agent 架构与自主性的研究方向高度相关：本文给出了长程 Agent（跨天/周任务）的一套完整工程蓝图与"先不遗忘、再谈学习"的立场，对设计自我演化的研究流水线、多级记忆与模型级联（省钱）都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of the campaign's success is attributable to the hierarchy vs. the underlying models? A flat-agent or no-cascade control on the identical task would quantify the architecture's marginal value.
- Can "accumulation" be measured — e.g., frequency and downstream effect of early-written rules, or counterfactual campaigns where specific standing decisions are removed?
- What does the architecture predict for month-scale campaigns: does the 7-level ladder need new levels, or does the L6 week-file itself become the bottleneck (a 1,500-char daily report compressing a month of work)?
- The paper proposes gates as insertion points for learned policies (tier assignment, wake intervals, kill rules) — what is the minimal learned component that beats its written rule, and how is it audited so it cannot outrun harness verification?
- How does the review-and-escalate cascade interact with reviewer errors — a strong-tier reviewer that wrongly passes or kills work — and can the 7 critical findings from 30 reviews be characterized as a reviewer-quality benchmark?
- Does the journal-beats-checkpoint resume protocol remain sound under concurrent writers or partial (crash mid-write) file states, which a ten-day campaign may simply not have stressed enough?
