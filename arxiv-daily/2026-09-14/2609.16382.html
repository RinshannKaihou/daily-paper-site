△ Critical edge

The margin over a plain co-occurrence kernel is modest — 0.907 vs 0.834 on GPT-2 — so part of “mean field predicts geometry” reduces to “co-occurrence predicts geometry.”

— Critical Assessment · Rigor

01 Methodology — one object, four studies

§3Build the kernel
For every head, average attention over a corpus into a row-stochastic token-type matrix P(s,t). Under attention–value independence and small value-map curvature, the head’s average contribution to a type centroid is Σt P(s,t)·v(ct). One ~2M-token corpus pass per model — zero fitted parameters.
§4Roll out open-loop
From input embeddings alone, iterate the kernel plus the model’s own MLPs layer by layer — never running the model on text — and score per-type centered cosine against true centroids on held-out splits (N = 1000 types ≈ two-thirds of positions, 67–72% of non-BOS attention).
§5Substitute across training
Replace each head with its checkpoint’s context-conditional mean field in Pythia-160m/1.4B/12B (19 checkpoints each); track the paired loss gap ΔL and per-head deviation.
§6Read the residual
Decompose deviation exactly into an attention-covariance term plus a value-contextualization term; correlate with induction, rank all 144 GPT-2 heads, and use task-conditioned residuals as function vectors.

02 Results — beyond the headline number

Early training is literally mean-field. Substituting every head leaves loss unchanged through step ~10² (ΔL within millinats while loss itself falls >3 nats); curves split at ~step 128, through the induction band (steps 1000–2000), at the same token count across a 75× parameter range. Mean-field-imposed final losses stay similar across scales — larger models’ gains come specifically from context-dependent processing.

Errors are honestly attributed. The MLP-at-centroid (Jensen) gap accounts for ~0.02–0.03 of each layer’s cosine shortfall; kernel terms add ≤0.005. The two Llama models fail outright because their layer-1 centroids sit where the MLP is sharply curved.

Deviation is the signal. Corpus deviation vs behavioral induction score peaks at Spearman 0.71 across 144 Pythia heads exactly at the transition (insignificant before step 256). Ranking GPT-2’s 144 heads by deviation recovers 3/4 documented induction heads and 5/11 name movers in the top 20 — while previous-token / duplicate-token heads sit near the bottom. Head attention spread grows from ~1.5× at init to >20× by end of training.

03 Strengths

  • One cheap, parameter-free object — a single corpus pass per model — powers four mutually reinforcing analyses: representation prediction, training dynamics, head survey, function-vector extraction.
  • The open-loop rollout is genuinely falsifiable and surprisingly accurate: average-case computation predicted from frozen weights and embeddings alone.
  • Mechanism-level error accounting isolates the MLP Jensen gap as the leading error source — explaining the Llama failure case and telling future users exactly what to fix.
  • Clean ties between a computational property (order-independence) and a developmental event (induction formation) across 75× scale; strong controls throughout — permutation tests, matched random prompts, row-shuffle negatives, held-out corpora, OLMo-2 replication.

04 Limitations

  • Top-1000 token types only (~two-thirds of positions); the rare-token long tail — where much contextual computation plausibly lives — is absorbed into “other”/BOS buckets.
  • Rollout accuracy degrades with depth and fails for both Llama models without a Jensen-gap correction: architecture-sensitive in a way the kernel itself is not.
  • Deviation is not a specific ICL detector — positional heads and other order-sensitive, non-ICL mechanisms also score high, so head rankings still need manual interpretation.
  • Function-vector transfer is inconsistent across tasks; the corpus-conditionality of the mean field (domain shift at inference) is an open problem; early-training results lean on Pythia checkpoint availability.

05 Questions for further exploration

  1. Can a hierarchical or clustered kernel extend the mean field below the top-1000 types without losing single-pass estimation?
  2. Can the Llama MLP Jensen gap be corrected in closed form (second-order expansion, learned per-layer correction), making the rollout architecture-agnostic?
  3. The mean-field → contextual transition lands at the same token count across 75× parameters — is that data-quantity scaling law predictable from corpus statistics alone?
  4. Since the best function-vector pipelines mix AIE head selection with MFA residual vectors, what does each method see that the other misses?
  5. Could tracking individual kernel entries (rather than CKA) pinpoint which type-pair routings constitute the induction-time reorganization?
  6. Can the covariance/contextualization decomposition separate “order-sensitive” from “in-context-inferential” computation — positional heads fire too?
  7. Under inference-time distribution shift, does fidelity require re-estimating the kernel on the target domain — a lightweight domain-adaptation diagnostic?
