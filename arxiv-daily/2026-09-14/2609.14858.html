Why this matters

Recursive self-improvement is bottlenecked by slow, expensive meta-level feedback. Replaying history every system already logs makes policy evaluation essentially free — for any proposal–evaluation discovery loop.

distilled from Relevance / TL;DR

Critical edge

The "never worse than incumbent" guarantee holds only in replay over recorded branches — replay can't estimate unexplored outcomes, so selection optimizes a biased surrogate; online regressions are possible and the gap is never measured.

Also weigh: the 162× headline spans different backbones (Gemini-3.1 Pro vs gpt-oss-120b); all results are single-run, no seeds or error bars.

01Methodology — the dreaming loop

STAGE 1

Online explore

Current exploration policy πt drives a fixed coding agent + evaluator over ≤ K1 decision rounds, batching where to branch, how many parallel attempts, and when to stop. Output: a grown discovery tree.

→
STAGE 2

Build simulators

Each node stores a workspace snapshot, artifact, diagnostics, and score. Recorded trees become a replay pool: alternative policies re-walk branches with zero execution cost.

→
STAGE 3

Dream & revise

A fixed LLM policy-dev agent proposes M candidate policy revisions, each replayed over all historical trees and scored; the best — incumbent always in the pool — is redeployed online.

↺ best policy redeployed → simulator pool grows → loop closes

The move is model-based-RL-flavored: treat accumulated discovery history as an empirical world model. Only the exploration-policy code evolves — the underlying model, evaluator, and interfaces stay untouched, so the loop bolts onto existing discovery stacks. Because the candidate pool includes the current policy, average replay score never decreases (in replay).

Replay objective V = max revealed score − β₁ · generation–evaluation requests + β₂ · avg attempts per round  // quality − cost + parallelism bonus
  • Controlled baseline: Recursive Fixed Exploration shares the identical agent, evaluator, initialization, initial policy, and budgets — it just never updates the policy (round-1 behavior identical by construction).
  • Counter-intuitive ablation: injecting history as semantic prompt guidance hurts under equal budgets — strong inductive bias over-constrains long-horizon search; interactive replay wins.

Setup: Gemini-3.1 Pro (10×11 = 110 calls/round) & Gemini-3.7-Flash (32×20 = 640/round) via Gemini CLI · 5 recursive rounds (Lasso), 10 (math) · cost = cumulative discovery-agent calls · correctness gate: objective checked to 1e-6.

03Strengths

  • Turns RSI's most expensive signal — meta-policy feedback over long rollouts — into an essentially free one, reusing history every discovery system already logs.
  • Model-agnostic and conservative: only exploration-policy code evolves; agent, evaluator, interfaces fixed — easy to bolt onto existing stacks.
  • Formal clarity rare in agent papers: explicit tree semantics, deterministic replay rules, a scalar objective, best-of-M selection with an in-replay monotonicity argument.
  • Strong efficiency results under a genuinely controlled baseline, with honest per-task tables showing where wins are thin.
  • The guidance-vs-replay ablation refutes the obvious cheaper alternative; behavior analysis (110 → 50 → 91 attempts) keeps the learned policy interpretable.
  • Discovered Lasso solver is technically interesting on its own: strong-rule screening + Cauchy–Schwarz KKT pruning with lazy Gram-matrix construction.

04Limitations

  • Replay only reorders/rebatches/re-stops over recorded branches — it cannot estimate value on unexplored ones, and stochastic outcomes collapse to their single recorded realization.
  • All headline cross-system comparisons (162×, AlphaEvolve-family) confound backbone, budget, and protocol; only Recursive Fixed Exploration is matched.
  • Single-run results — no variance or significance reporting; sensitivity to M, K1/K2, β₁/β₂ unexamined.
  • Shallow recursion (5–10 rounds); whether the simulator pool keeps paying off over dozens of rounds is untested.
  • Not uniformly better: Gisette regression under Gemini-3.1 Pro (2,841.0 vs 1,861.8 ms baseline); Autocorrelation non-win.
  • The β₂ parallelism bonus rewards wall-clock-friendly batching on the recorded tree — online parallelism interacts with stochastic regeneration in ways replay can't capture.

05Questions for further exploration

  • How large is the replay-vs-online gap, and does it grow as the policy diverges from the behavior that generated the history (off-policy distribution shift)?
  • Would recording multiple stochastic children per node let replay estimate outcome variance and de-bias policy selection?
  • Does the simulator pool keep helping at 50–100 recursion rounds, or does the policy-dev agent overfit idiosyncrasies of past trees?
  • How sensitive are gains to β₁, β₂, and M — is the improvement mostly best-of-M selection rather than dreaming feedback itself?
  • Do replay-learned exploration policies transfer across tasks or domains, or are they task-specific?
  • Could semantic guidance and replay be combined — guidance restricted to unexplored regions where replay has no signal?
