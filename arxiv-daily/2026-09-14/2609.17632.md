> **一句话总结：** 提出 EvolveTrade，把 LLM 交易 agent 的系统提示词当作"文本参数化的工具使用策略"，由一个独立的 Policy Agent 每 5 个交易日依据决策轨迹与真实组合反馈在线改写该策略、backbone LLM 全程冻结；在 GPT-5-mini 与 Gemini-2.5-Flash 两个骨干、六个知识截止之后的市场区间上，多数区间取得所有 LLM 方法中最优的 Sharpe 与累计收益（如 2025 年 1 月 SR 5.12 vs 静态工具调用基线 2.87），并激活了静态策略从未调用的 VaR/RSI/EMA 等指标，但在两个 4 月窗口（回撤复苏与牛市）因现金仓位过高而输给静态基线。

> **TL;DR:** EvolveTrade treats the system prompt of a tool-using LLM trading agent as a text-parameterized policy: every 5 trading days, a separate Policy Agent LLM rewrites it from the agent's decision traces and realized portfolio feedback, while the backbone stays frozen. On a LiveTradeBench-style daily-close backtest over 15 US blue-chip stocks plus cash, it beats the strongest fixed-policy LLM baseline (Static Tool-Calling Agent) on Sharpe Ratio and cumulative return in 3 of 6 model-regime pairs — e.g., GPT-5-mini in Jan 2025: SR 5.12±0.51 vs 2.87±0.19, CR 5.10% vs 3.30%; Sep 2025: CR 6.84% vs 4.86%, also topping the best rule-based CR (SMA 5.31%) — and it stays best among LLM methods after 10 bps transaction costs. However, it loses both April windows to static baselines (Apr 2026: CR 3.69±1.83% vs Static Base 11.64±0.96%, while holding 40.1% cash), and in the Nov 2025 bearish month every LLM agent posted negative returns while the simple KDJ&RSI rule made +0.92%.

## 核心贡献

- **策略即提示词（policy-as-prompt）的自进化框架**：首次（相对既有工作）将工具调用型 LLM 交易 agent 的完整系统提示词视为可在部署期间在线进化的"文本参数化策略"——覆盖证据获取、工具调用协议、信号验证、风险控制与仓位输出，而非只改高层策略或离线优化提示词；backbone LLM 参数与工具接口全程不变，因此可直接套用在任何冻结的 LLM 交易 agent 上。
- **动机发现：静态策略把 LLM 锁死在固定的"分析词汇表"里**：在横盘、深回撤、上升趋势三个一月区间中，静态工具调用 agent 每天计算的都是同样的五个指标（Sharpe、20 日/60 日收益、年化波动、回撤，90–100% 的交易日出现），而 RSI、EMA、VaR、信号归一化等与市场状态相关的标准指标在三个区间中从未被调用过——分析范围在开发期就被提示词固定死了。
- **在线策略自进化机制**：每 N（默认 5）个交易日为一个批次，批次结束时 Policy Agent 读取"决策轨迹（逐资产理由）+ 事后组合反馈（日收益、逐资产收益、组合状态）"组成的精炼记录，按"有依据的定点修改"指令重写完整策略文本；进化出的策略甚至会自发形成确定性打分公式（如 score = sharpe_norm + 0.5×momentum_rank − 0.2×vol_norm − 0.8×drawdown_penalty，再经带上下限的 softmax 转成权重）。
- **行为层面的可解释证据链**：自进化不仅提升收益指标，还改变了工具使用行为——代码解释器调用从约 1.0 次/日升至 2.6–4.5 次/日；4 月回撤期激活 VaR 与信号归一化（静态基线为 0 天），9 月上行期激活 SMA/EMA/RSI；并给出从"策略修改 → 仓位变化 → 次日收益差异"的逐案例归因（如 2025-01-24 NVDA 暴跌前将仓位从 10.7% 降到 2.9%）。
- **诚实且系统的评估设计**：两个 backbone、六个均在其知识截止之后的一月区间（防数据泄漏）、每个配置 3 次重复并报告标准差、10 bps 交易成本的摩擦分析、更新频率（1/3/5/7 天）敏感性、50 日长程评估，并专门分析了自己在两个 4 月窗口输给静态基线的机制（现金仓位过高）。

## Key Contributions

- **Policy-as-prompt self-evolution framework**: treats the *entire* system prompt of a tool-using LLM trading agent (evidence gathering, tool-calling protocol, signal verification, risk control, output structure) as a text-parameterized policy that is refined *online during deployment* from the agent's own realized experience — unlike offline prompt optimization or strategy-level-only adaptation — while the backbone LLM and tool interfaces stay frozen, making it applicable to any frozen LLM trading agent.
- **Motivating diagnosis of "analytic vocabulary lock-in"**: across sideways (Jan 2025), drawdown-with-V-recovery (Apr 2025), and uptrend (Sep 2025) regimes, a static tool-calling agent computes the same five metrics (Sharpe, 20d/60d return, annualized volatility, drawdown) on 90–100% of days, while regime-relevant metrics such as RSI, EMA, VaR, and signal normalization are *never* invoked in any regime; news queries degenerate into per-asset boilerplate and price lookbacks stay fixed at ~6 months.
- **Online refinement mechanism**: trading days are partitioned into batches of N=5; at each batch end, a Policy Agent performs one LLM call `f_update(π, B) = LLM(I_update, π, B)` over refinement records pairing the decision trace (allocation + per-asset rationale) with post-trade feedback (daily portfolio return, per-asset returns, resulting state), rewriting the full policy text under instructions demanding grounded, trace-linked edits rather than generic rewrites.
- **Behavioral evidence that evolution changes tool use, not just outcomes**: code-interpreter calls rise from ~1.0 to 2.6–4.5 per day (news calls do not increase); VaR and normalization appear on 8 and 5 of 20 April drawdown days (0 for the static baseline); SMA/EMA/RSI appear on 17/9/5 September days; case-level policy-to-return attribution links a refined sizing rule to a realized next-day return gap.
- **Careful, partially negative-friendly evaluation**: two backbones (GPT-5-mini, Gemini-2.5-Flash), six one-month regimes all *after* each model's knowledge cutoff, three runs per configuration with standard deviations, a 10 bps transaction-cost analysis, an update-interval sensitivity study, a 50-trading-day horizon run, and an explicit mechanism analysis of the two April windows where static baselines win.

## Methodology

**Setup.** Sequential daily trading over days t = 1..T. The Trading Agent is initialized with S_t = (π_t, t, P_{t−1}, T), where π_t is the tool-use policy implemented as the natural-language system prompt; the action distribution is induced jointly by the frozen LLM p_θ, the context, the tools, and π_t. The toolset is {get_price, news_searcher, code_interpreter (persistent Python kernel)}, all enforcing temporal cutoffs (price up to day t, news only up to t−1) to prevent look-ahead leakage — the agent must actively retrieve information rather than receiving a pre-injected observation window. Outputs are a feasible long-only allocation w_t over 15 US blue-chip stocks (AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, JPM, V, JNJ, UNH, PG, KO, XOM, CAT, WMT, META, TSLA, AMZN) plus cash, and a per-asset rationale d_t^dec; together these form the decision trace h_t. The environment returns post-trade feedback g_t (daily portfolio return, per-asset returns, allocations, updated state). A refinement record R_t = (h_t, g_t) pairs reasoning with outcome, because feedback alone is ambiguous (a loss may be a market shock, not a procedural flaw).

**Policy self-evolution.** Days are partitioned into batches of N=5; the policy is fixed within a batch. At batch end, the collected records B_k are passed to a Policy Agent — a single LLM call with a fixed update instruction that asks it to identify which parts of the current prompt caused good or poor performance and rewrite the full policy text with grounded, trace-linked edits. Iterating yields π_{kN+1} = f_update(π_{kN}, B_k); nothing but the prompt text is learned.

**Baselines.** Rule-based: SPY, Buy & Hold, MACD, KDJ&RSI, ZMR, SMA (all with a strict 1/15 per-asset cap). LLM-based isolating each factor: Static Base Agent (LiveTradeBench-style fixed observation window, no tools), Static Tool-Calling Agent (same tools, fixed policy), EvolveBase (tool-free, evolving policy), EvolveStrategy (ATLAS-style evolution of high-level trading strategy only, tool-calling protocol fixed), and EvolveTrade (full-policy evolution).

**Evaluation protocol.** Daily-close backtesting on the LiveTradeBench framework; one-month windows after each backbone's knowledge cutoff (GPT-5-mini: Jan/Apr/Sep 2025 = sideways / drawdown-recovery / uptrend; Gemini-2.5-Flash: Nov 2025 / Feb 2026 / Apr 2026 = bearish / sideways / bullish); three runs per configuration; metrics are annualized Sharpe Ratio (SR), cumulative return (CR), max drawdown (MDD), win rate (WR), daily volatility (Vol). Additional analyses: per-day binary maps of which analytics appear in code-interpreter calls, daily tool-call counts, policy-length dynamics over 50 days, turnover and 10 bps proportional transaction costs, update-interval sensitivity (1/3/5/7 days), and matched-day return attribution case studies.

## Results & Highlights

All numbers are averages over three runs on the LiveTradeBench-style 15-stock + cash universe (mean±std where the paper reports it).

- **Jan 2025 (sideways, GPT-5-mini)**: EvolveTrade achieves SR **5.12±0.51** and CR **5.10±0.72%**, versus the strongest fixed-policy LLM baseline Static Tool-Calling Agent at SR 2.87±0.19 and CR 3.30±0.17% (+2.25 SR, +1.80 pp CR); its CR also exceeds the best rule-based baseline (B&H, 4.11%) and its WR is 57.0% with the lowest LLM volatility (0.84%).
- **Sep 2025 (uptrend, GPT-5-mini)**: EvolveTrade reaches SR **8.43±1.68** and CR **6.84±0.70%** vs Static TC's SR 6.45±0.87 / CR 4.86±0.56% (+1.98 SR, +1.98 pp CR), with WR 81.3%; the CR also beats the strongest rule-based baseline in that window (SMA, 5.31%).
- **Gemini-2.5-Flash**: EvolveTrade is the best LLM method in Nov 2025 (SR −1.03±0.39, CR −1.07±0.40%) and, in Feb 2026, attains SR **2.75±0.29** and CR **2.92±0.25%** — outperforming every rule-based baseline (best rule: SMA, SR 2.26 / CR 1.09%).
- **Losses where static wins (honestly reported)**: in Apr 2025 every LLM agent lost money — best was Static TC at CR −4.71±0.36% while EvolveTrade made −6.60±1.19% and passive SPY gained +8.22%; in Apr 2026, Static Base reached CR 11.64±0.96% vs EvolveTrade 3.69±1.83%, with EvolveTrade holding 40.1% average cash vs the baseline's 4.3% (Table 7), i.e., its evolved defensiveness missed the rally.
- **50-trading-day horizon (GPT-5-mini, 2025-09-02 to 2025-11-10, Table 2)**: EvolveTrade SR **4.00** / CR **10.56%** vs Static TC 2.94 / 8.88% and EvolveStrategy 3.27 / 6.94%, with policy length cycling between ~5k and ~20k characters rather than growing monotonically.
- **Update-interval sensitivity (averaged over Jan/Apr/Sep 2025, Table 3)**: N=5 days is best (SR 3.67, CR 1.78%) while daily updates N=1 collapse to SR 1.92, CR 0.16% — overly frequent revision overfits single-day noise; N=3 gives the best WR (62.7%).
- **Transaction costs (10 bps proportional, Table 6)**: rankings are preserved — e.g., Jan 2025 post-cost SR 5.01 vs Static TC 2.68 — and EvolveTrade maintains *lower turnover* than Static TC and EvolveStrategy in all six windows (e.g., Jan: TO 1.05 vs 2.25).
- **Behavioral changes**: code-interpreter calls rise from ~1.0 to 2.6–4.5 per day; the static baseline never invokes EMA, RSI, VaR, or normalization in any regime (0 days), whereas EvolveTrade activates VaR on 8/20 and normalization on 5/20 April drawdown days, and SMA/EMA/RSI on 17/9/5 September uptrend days.
- **Case-level attribution (Jan 24, 2025 NVDA drawdown)**: under a refined sizing policy prioritizing 60-day risk-adjusted evidence with turnover checks, EvolveTrade held 2.9% NVDA vs the static agent's 10.7%; NVDA fell 17.0% the next day, and EvolveTrade returned −0.03% vs −1.11% (+1.08 pp realized gap; the exposure difference alone accounts for +1.33 pp = 7.8 pp × 17.0%). A second matched case (Sep 10, 2025) traces a +0.5067 pp next-day advantage, of which +0.638 pp comes from four code-linked overweight tilts (JPM 19.54%, CAT 10.45%, WMT 8.40%, TSLA 4.32%).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: moderate but real. Prompt optimization (ProTeGi, OPRO, DSPy, GEPA), reflective agents (Reflexion, Agent Workflow Memory, ReasoningBank), and trading-specific policy adaptation (ATLAS, SHARP) all exist; the authors position the contribution as *online, during-deployment* evolution of the *full operational tool-use policy* rather than offline instruction tuning or experience memories, and the EvolveStrategy ablation (ATLAS-style strategy-only updates, tool protocol frozen) genuinely isolates that delta — EvolveTrade beats EvolveStrategy on SR/CR in Jan and Sep 2025 and Nov 2025. The analytic-vocabulary lock-in diagnosis (Figure 2) is a fresh, memorable motivating finding.
- **Rigor — the main weakness is statistical fragility**: each regime is a single one-month window of ~20–21 daily returns, so "annualized" Sharpe ratios like 8.43 rest on ~21 observations; with only three runs, standard deviations are large (Sep SR 8.43±1.68; Apr 2026 CR 3.69±1.83 — the std is half the mean), and no significance tests are reported. Several headline wins are within overlapping error bars.
- **Rigor — regime dependence is glossed in the abstract**: "often improves... in most evaluated settings" translates concretely to best-of-LLM in 3 of 6 model-regime pairs, with static baselines stronger in both April windows. More importantly, the framing is LLM-vs-LLM: in Apr 2025 *all* LLM agents lost 4.7–7.6% while SPY gained +8.22%, and in Nov 2025 the simple KDJ&RSI rule (+0.92% CR) beat every LLM agent (all negative CR) — so self-evolution improves LLM agents without making them competitive with trivial rules in the regimes that matter most (sharp drawdown/recovery).
- **Missing ablations/analyses**: the N=5 update interval was selected by a sensitivity study run on the *same* three evaluation windows used for the headline results — a selection-bias risk never addressed; there is no analysis of how sensitive results are to the Policy Agent's update prompt or to the initial policy π0; no prompt-drift or degeneration check beyond 50 days; and the failure mechanism (excess cash: +10.1 pp and +35.9 pp over baselines in the two April windows) is diagnosed but not mitigated.
- **Execution realism**: only 10 bps proportional costs; no slippage, market impact, or liquidity — acknowledged in Limitations, but turnover ~1–2× per day would face real frictions.
- **Reproducibility**: strong for a systems-style paper — environment (LiveTradeBench-based), universe, metric definitions, baseline definitions (Appendix A), all operational prompts (Appendix G), and best evolved policies (Appendix H) are published; the framework needs only API access to two proprietary backbones, which limits fully open replication.
- **Significance**: a well-executed, transparent instance of the self-evolving-agent agenda applied to a domain with unambiguous extrinsic feedback; the policy-to-return attribution methodology is a model for how agent self-modification papers should justify causal claims.

## Strengths

- Clean conceptual framing: the *reusable procedure* (prompt), not the decision or the model, is the object of learning — applicable to any frozen LLM agent with no training.
- The motivation study (static policies lock a regime-invariant analytic vocabulary) turns a vague intuition into a measurable, falsifiable observation before proposing the fix.
- Behavior-level evidence goes far beyond aggregate metrics: per-day metric-activation maps, tool-call frequency shifts, matched-day return attribution with a +0.638 pp decomposition across four named positions, and a concrete evolved scoring formula recovered from the policy text.
- Honest negative-result handling: dedicated appendix (F) analyzing *why* it loses the two April windows (excess cash allocation), rather than hiding them.
- Solid experimental hygiene: post-cutoff regimes per backbone, three seeds with stds, transaction-cost robustness with turnover, interval sensitivity, 50-day horizon, and a well-chosen ablation ladder (Static Base → Static TC → EvolveBase → EvolveStrategy → EvolveTrade).

## Limitations

- Statistically thin: one-month windows (~20 daily observations), three seeds, large stds (e.g., Sep 2025 SR 8.43±1.68; Apr 2026 CR 3.69±1.83), no significance testing; several wins are within noise.
- Gains are regime- and backbone-dependent: best in 3/6 pairs, loses both April windows to static baselines; all LLM configurations are beaten by simple rules in Apr 2025 (SPY +8.22% vs best LLM −4.71%) and Nov 2025 (KDJ&RSI +0.92% vs all-negative LLMs).
- The evolved policy can become overly defensive — holding 27.1% cash in Apr 2025 and 40.1% in Apr 2026 (vs 4.3% for the baseline) caused it to miss the V-shaped recovery and the bullish rally; the paper diagnoses but does not fix this failure mode.
- Fixed update interval per run (N=5, itself tuned on the evaluation windows); adaptive scheduling is left to future work.
- Execution model is idealized: 10 bps proportional costs only, no slippage/market impact/liquidity; long-only, 15 mega-cap universe; results may not transfer to smaller universes or short horizons.
- Depends on proprietary backbones (GPT-5-mini, Gemini-2.5-Flash); no open-weights replication is reported.

## Relevance

高度契合"自进化 Agent"这一高优先级研究方向：本文正是在真实外部反馈（市场收益）驱动下让 LLM agent 在线改写自身操作策略（系统提示词）的完整案例，其"策略即文本参数"的视角、行为层面（工具使用/指标激活）的进化证据以及策略到收益的因果归因方法，对任何构建长期运行、自我改进 agent 系统的工作都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the gains survive proper statistical treatment — overlapping-window bootstrap or many more regimes/runs — given SR stds as large as ±1.68 on 21-day windows?
- Can the excess-cash failure mode be corrected *by the framework itself* (e.g., a Policy Agent objective that penalizes under-exposure during recoveries), or does it require an outer validator?
- How sensitive are the evolved policies to the initial prompt π0 and the Policy Agent's update instruction — the two hand-written artifacts that remain fixed?
- Does replacing the LLM rewrite with a constrained edit space (typed policy slots, version control with rollback) reduce variance while keeping adaptivity?
- Does the analytic-vocabulary lock-in finding generalize to non-trading tool-using agents — and could periodic "capability probes" (forcing the agent to enumerate unused metrics) substitute for full self-evolution?
- Can the method be evaluated against stronger learned baselines (e.g., RL-trained policies like FLAG-Trader) rather than only static LLM and rule-based agents?
