Why this matters

Any “lossless” acceleration claim must state its numerical precision and equivalence criterion — benchmark parity alone does not prove equivalent inference.

“无损加速”声明必须在指定数值精度与等价判据下评估——基准分数持平并不等于推理等价。

Critical edge ⚠

Eval caps generations at 128 tokens — divergence compounds with length, so real-world long outputs likely match at even lower rates than reported.

Methodology — four-stage design
01

Self-distill, independently

Prompts from public HuggingFace mixes; responses greedy-generated by the frozen Qwen3-1.7B itself → 4,113,358 on-policy pairs. Orthrus-1.7B-final trained on 8×H100 (1 epoch, lr 2×10⁻⁴, block size 8 ×32, 3,072-token context) under a deliberately different recipe — a control against checkpoint-specific artifacts.

02

Match trajectories exactly

Token-by-token string comparison vs. the AR reference: 1,190 prompts · 12 domains (~100 each), greedy decoding, max 128 new tokens, eager attention, BF16, one RTX 3090. All rates reported with 95% CIs.

03

Regress on uncertainty

Response-conditional perplexity PPL(y|x) per generation; logistic regression of match probability on log-PPL, controlling for length and domain (statsmodels).

04

Isolate precision

The identical evaluation repeated under FP32 — dtype is the only changed variable — plus lm-eval-harness runs (GSM8K, HumanEval, IFEval) for task-level parity.

Credibility: strong — CIs throughout, confounder controls, independent retraining, clean FP32 ablation. Caveats: train/eval environment version mismatch (PyTorch 2.13/H100 vs 2.8/RTX 3090); no code release mentioned.

✓ Strengths

  • Two independent models — released checkpoint and full retrain — show the same precision effect, ruling out “the checkpoint is just broken.”
  • The FP32 control is the cleanest result in the paper: same models, prompts, decoding; dtype alone fully explains the divergence.
  • Statistics well above the systems-paper norm: 95% CIs on every rate, regression separating perplexity from length and domain confounders.
  • Conceptually sharp honesty: identical benchmark scores do not imply equivalent inference — and the authors decline to overclaim their own benchmark wins.

✕ Limitations

  • Mechanism unlocated: no single op (attention, normalization, KV-cache reads, the consensus comparison) is identified — targeted mitigations can't be derived yet.
  • Single 1.7B backbone, one GPU, eager attention: flash/sdpa kernels, H100/B200 numerics, and 7B/70B scales are untested.
  • 128-token greedy horizon only — longer generations and sampling (T > 0), where losslessness is harder to even define, are unexplored.
  • A three-benchmark downstream suite is underpowered for detecting subtle degradations.
  • No wall-clock or memory numbers — the cost of running FP32 to recover exactness (which erases the speedup) is unquantified.
Questions for further exploration
  1. Q1

    Which computational stage dominates BF16 divergence — attention, layer norm, KV-cache reads, or the consensus logit comparison — and would selective FP32 casting there recover exactness at minimal cost?

  2. Q2

    How does match rate scale beyond 128 tokens, and is trajectory-level losslessness even well-posed under sampling (T > 0)?

  3. Q3

    Does the divergence persist across hardware (H100 vs RTX 3090), kernels (flash/sdpa vs eager), and larger backbones (7B, 70B)?

  4. Q4

    Could a runtime divergence predictor — top-1/top-2 logit gap, running PPL at accepted tokens — fall back to pure AR decoding before a token flips?

  5. Q5

    Is the perplexity link causal — high-entropy generations sitting near argmax boundaries where one-ULP differences flip tokens — and should consensus thresholds tighten exactly there?
