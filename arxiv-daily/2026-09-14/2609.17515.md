> **一句话总结：** 本文在 4 个 Qwen 系模型（稠密 Transformer / 稠密混合 / MoE）× 4 类结构化剪枝（深度/宽度/混合/专家）上、用三个智能家居数据集共 19,560 条实例系统评估剪枝后经 SFT 恢复的 LLM 工具调用可靠性，发现稠密模型安全剪枝区间窄且崩塌剧烈（50% FLAP 剪枝使可执行请求的误拒率从 7.7% 飙升至 80.8%）、上下文接地特异性（设备/取值）先于模式级意图（操作/参数）退化，而 MoE 模型经 REAP 移除 70% 专家仅损失 0.37% 准确率（参数量 35B→约 12.5B）。

> **TL;DR:** A systematic empirical study of how structured pruning harms context-grounded smart-home tool calling: after uniform post-pruning SFT, dense Qwen models (Qwen3-4B, Qwen3.5-4B/9B) show narrow safe regions with sharp cliffs — at 50% FLAP pruning on HomeBench, Qwen3-4B's false-refusal rate on executable requests jumps from 7.7% (unpruned baseline) to 80.8% — whereas the MoE Qwen3.6-35B-A3B under REAP loses only a nonsignificant −0.37% accuracy when 70% of experts are removed (35B→~12.5B stored parameters), because per-token active compute is preserved. Aggregating over 19,560 instances from HomeBench, HAR, and a proprietary SHTC set, the authors show device/value (grounded specificity) degrade before operation/argument (schema-level intent), and that unpruned accuracy does not predict pruning sensitivity (Partially Executable requests lose ~10.3% accuracy per additional 10% pruning vs 7.7% for Simple). Essential reading if you care about reliability-side effects of LLM compression for on-device agents, though all models come from the Qwen family and the MoE-vs-dense comparison conflates architecture with pruning semantics (storage vs compute reduction).

## 核心贡献

- **细粒度退化分析框架**：将智能家居工具调用的每个动作分解为四个语义组件——操作 (o)、目标设备 (d)、控制参数 (p)、参数取值 (v)，并把请求按复杂度分为五类（Simple / Medium / Complex / Partially Executable / Infeasible），从而回答"剪枝弄坏了什么、在什么任务条件下发生"，而非只看总体准确率。
- **跨架构、跨剪枝类型的系统实证**：覆盖三种架构家族（稠密 Transformer Qwen3-4B、稠密混合 Qwen3.5-4B/9B、稀疏 MoE Qwen3.6-35B-A3B）与四类结构化剪枝（深度：ShortGPT/Angular；宽度：FLAP；混合：2SSP；专家：REAP），所有剪枝模型用同一 50,001 条样本的 SFT 混合数据恢复，在三个数据集共 19,560 条实例上评测。
- **四项可部署性结论**：(1) 稠密模型只有很窄的安全剪枝区、随后断崖式崩塌，MoE 容忍度高得多；(2) 接地特异性（设备 d、取值 v）先于模式级意图（操作 o、参数 p）退化；(3) 基线任务难度不能预测剪枝敏感度；(4) 激进稠密剪枝会让模型从"错误执行"系统性转向"过度拒答"，改变了决策策略而不仅是准确率。
- **实例级稳定性分析**：证明 MoE 专家剪枝下"聚合准确率平稳"掩盖了大量实例级翻转（HomeBench 上 14.1% 的精确匹配结果在 0–90% 剪枝扫描中至少翻转一次，Partially Executable 类高达 33.7%）。
- **面向实践的评估建议**：为智能体剪枝提出评测维度（动作组件 × 任务复杂度 × 拒答校准），并指出恢复数据应针对剪枝特有失败模式（干扰项多的设备消解、精确取值、部分可执行指令）设计。

## Key Contributions

- A fine-grained degradation framework that decomposes each smart-home action into four semantic components (operation o, target device d, control argument p, argument value v) and stratifies requests into a five-level complexity taxonomy (Simple, Medium, Complex, Partially Executable, Infeasible), answering "what breaks and when" rather than only "how much" accuracy is lost.
- A systematic evaluation spanning three architecture families (dense Transformer Qwen3-4B; dense-hybrid Qwen3.5-4B/9B with interleaved Gated DeltaNet linear attention; sparse MoE Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, 35B total / ~3B active) and four structured-pruning classes (ShortGPT and Angular depth pruning, FLAP width pruning, 2SSP hybrid pruning, REAP expert pruning), with all pruned models healed by the same balanced 50,001-example SFT mixture and evaluated on 19,560 instances from three smart-home datasets.
- Four deployment-relevant empirical findings: (1) dense models exhibit narrow safe pruning regions followed by sharp cliffs, while MoE tolerates far more expert removal; (2) grounded specificity (device, value) degrades before schema-level intent (operation, argument); (3) unpruned task difficulty does not predict pruning sensitivity; (4) aggressive dense pruning induces a qualitative behavioral shift toward systematic over-refusal of executable requests.
- An instance-level robustness analysis showing that a flat aggregate accuracy curve under MoE expert pruning hides substantial per-instance instability (e.g., 14.1% of HomeBench exact-match outcomes flip somewhere along the 0–90% REAP sweep, rising to 33.7% for Partially Executable requests).
- Actionable evaluation guidance: pruning for tool-calling agents should be judged on action components, workload mix, and execute-vs-reject calibration — an infeasible-only refusal metric would wrongly score a 50%-FLAP model as robust while it rejects four out of five valid commands.

## Methodology

The study formalizes smart-home tool calling as context-grounded structured prediction. Each instance X = {u, H, A} couples a user request u, a smart-home context H (devices, capabilities, current states), and available device actions A; the model must emit an action set where each action decomposes into (o, d, p, v) — e.g., "bedroom.light.set.brightness(50)" gives d = bedroom.light, o = set, p = brightness, v = 50.

**Models and pruning.** Four Qwen-family LLMs are pruned at four structural levels, subject to architectural compatibility: Qwen3-4B (dense GQA Transformer, 36 layers) receives ShortGPT, Angular, FLAP, and 2SSP; Qwen3.5-4B and Qwen3.5-9B (dense hybrid, 32 layers) receive ShortGPT and Angular only (FLAP/2SSP do not support Gated DeltaNet modules); Qwen3.6-35B-A3B receives REAP expert pruning (10–90% expert removal vs 10–50% for dense). This design isolates architecture at fixed scale (Qwen3-4B vs Qwen3.5-4B), dense capacity (Qwen3.5-4B vs 9B), and sparsity (MoE).

**Healing.** Every pruned model is recovered with the identical SFT protocol: a three-way balanced mixture of 16,667 examples each from proprietary smart-home tool-calling dialogues (SHTC), HomeBench, and Home-Assistant-Requests-V2 (HAR) — 50,001 total, 1 epoch, global batch 32 (1,563 steps), 32,768-token sequences, bf16, Adam with β2 = 0.98, LR 5e-6 decaying to 5e-7, 50 warmup steps, on 32 (dense) or 64 (MoE) A100 80GB GPUs.

**Metrics.** Beyond order-independent exact-match task accuracy, the paper reports per-component accuracy Acc^(j) for j ∈ {o, d, p, v}, per-category accuracy Acc_c over the five complexity levels, and all degradations as deltas ΔAcc relative to each model's own unpruned SFT counterpart M0 (required for the proprietary SHTC set, where only deltas may be disclosed). Robustness checks include operation-level F1 (following the HomeBench protocol) instead of accuracy, greedy decoding by default with Qwen-recommended sampling (temperature 0.7, top-p 0.8, top-k 20) for stochasticity, three-seed reruns on the unpruned MoE model, and a per-instance always-correct / always-wrong / flipping analysis across the full REAP pruning sweep. A false-refusal rate on executable requests quantifies the execute-vs-reject decision shift.

## Results & Highlights

- **Over-refusal cliff (headline result):** On executable HomeBench requests, unpruned Qwen3-4B refuses 7.7%; at 50% pruning this rises to 56.2% with Angular and 80.8% with FLAP — while the same models still correctly withhold action on 86.8% and 99.9% of infeasible requests, respectively. An infeasible-only refusal metric would score the FLAP model as robust even though it rejects four of five valid commands.
- **MoE vs dense safe regions:** With REAP on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, removing 70% of experts reduces stored parameters from 35B to ~12.5B with a nonsignificant ΔAcc of −0.37%, whereas dense models collapse sharply beyond mild ratios (at 10% dense pruning, 7 of 8 LLM-method combinations lose <0.6%, but degradation accelerates non-linearly toward 50%).
- **Specificity degrades before intent:** Averaged over dense pruning across all three datasets, mean losses at 50% pruning are 24.2% for device (d) vs 21.1% for operation (o), and 20.7% for value (v) vs 14.5% for argument (p). The F1 robustness check confirms this: at the most aggressive ratio, d loses 28.7 F1 vs 12.9 for o on SHTC, and 17.6 vs 7.8 on HAR.
- **Difficulty does not predict sensitivity:** Per additional 10% dense pruning, Partially Executable requests lose ~10.3% accuracy, Simple ~7.7%, and Medium ~7.2%; Simple and Infeasible requests start at similarly high unpruned accuracy yet Simple degrades much faster, and Infeasible's robustness is partly explained by the pruning-induced rejection bias.
- **Flat aggregate hides instance churn:** Along the 0–90% REAP sweep, 14.1% of HomeBench exact-match outcomes flip (33.7% for Partially Executable, 24.9% for Medium); HAR shows a 25.1% flipping band concentrated in device grounding (24.4%), while its Medium category is instead 78.7% always-wrong — chronically difficult rather than unstable.
- **Architecture nuances:** The dense-hybrid Qwen3.5-4B is more pruning-robust than same-scale GQA-based Qwen3-4B, plausibly due to heterogeneous DeltaNet/full-attention pathways, but scaling from 4B to 9B yields no consistent robustness benefit.

## Authors & Credibility

The work comes from Alexa Home AI at Amazon (Congjing Zhang — internship work, affiliated with University of Washington; Vashishtha Patil — corresponding author; Henning Lange; Usman Aleem). This is an industrial lab with direct deployment stakes in on-device/edge smart-home assistants, which motivates the unusually deployment-aware metrics (false-refusal calibration, per-component grounding). The evaluation scale is credible: 19,560 evaluation instances (HomeBench 17,366; HAR 2,194; plus a proprietary SHTC set reported as deltas only), 4 models, 5 pruning methods, uniform SFT healing on 64 A100s for the MoE model. The paper engages the relevant literature (ShortGPT, Angular/ Gromov et al., FLAP, 2SSP, REAP; HomeBench, SimuHome, SMH-Bench) and its claims are sized to the evidence, with explicit limitations. No code or data release is mentioned, and the proprietary SHTC component limits independent verification of one third of the data.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty (moderate-to-good):** The per-component (o/d/p/v) degradation view and the over-refusal analysis are genuinely new angles; pruning studies typically report aggregate accuracy. However, the core ingredients (structured pruning methods, smart-home benchmarks, SFT recovery) are all off-the-shelf — this is a careful measurement paper, not a methods paper.
- **Conflated comparison (main methodological caveat):** "MoE tolerates more pruning" partly compares incommensurable things. REAP removes stored experts but preserves routing and ~3B active parameters per token — a memory reduction, not a per-token compute reduction — whereas dense depth/width pruning genuinely removes compute. The paper itself notes this explanation, but headlines it as MoE "tolerating more pruning" without latency/throughput/FLOPs measurements to separate the two axes.
- **Single family, non-factorial design:** All four models are Qwen generations, so the "architecture effect" (Qwen3 vs Qwen3.5 vs Qwen3.6) is confounded with training recipe and data differences across generations. And because FLAP/2SSP run only on Qwen3-4B and REAP only on the MoE, pruning-method and architecture effects are never crossed — the authors acknowledge this but it materially limits which conclusions are attributable to architecture per se.
- **Data quirks:** The HomeBench Medium stratum has only 245 instances and the proprietary SHTC set discloses only ΔAcc (no absolute numbers), so several complexity-level claims rest on thin or unverifiable slices; HAR contributes only Simple/Medium categories.
- **Missing ablations:** The discussion proposes grounding-aware pruning criteria and pruning-targeted healing data, but neither is tested; there is no comparison against quantization or distillation at matched compression, and no deployment-level efficiency numbers (latency, memory, throughput) to justify the pruning budgets studied.
- **Rigor positives:** Uniform SFT protocol, degradation always measured against each model's own unpruned SFT baseline, F1 robustness checks, seed-sensitivity reruns, and the per-instance flipping analysis are above the bar for this genre; claims are consistently hedged ("nonsignificant", "possible explanation").
- **Red flags:** minor — the striking 80.8% false-refusal figure is shown for one model (Qwen3-4B) on one dataset (HomeBench); the appendix generalizes complexity trends but not the refusal cliff itself.

## Strengths

- Moves pruning evaluation from "how much accuracy is lost" to "which capability breaks, under which workload, and how does the decision policy shift" — exactly the evaluation granularity deployment teams need.
- The over-refusal finding is the paper's most valuable insight: aggressive pruning flips a tool-calling model into a safe-looking but useless near-always-refuse policy that aggregate and even infeasible-only metrics would score as robust.
- Uniform, well-documented experimental protocol (same healing data, hyperparameters, and compute across all pruned variants; degradation always relative to the model's own SFT'd unpruned self).
- Multiple robustness checks (F1 vs accuracy, greedy vs sampling, three seeds, per-instance flipping across the pruning sweep) that test whether conclusions survive metric and stochasticity choices.
- Clear, reusable taxonomy of smart-home task complexity and action components that future compression-for-agent papers can adopt directly.

## Limitations

- All models are from the Qwen family; generalization to Llama, Gemma, or Mistral-style architectures is untested, and architecture comparisons are confounded with generation-level training differences.
- The pruning grid is not factorial: FLAP/2SSP are incompatible with Gated DeltaNet hybrids and REAP applies only to the MoE, so method-vs-architecture interactions cannot be estimated.
- Restricted to smart-home tool calling; several complexity categories exist in only a subset of the three datasets (e.g., Complex only in SHTC, Partially Executable only in HomeBench), and HomeBench Medium has just 245 instances.
- The proprietary SHTC dataset reports only relative deltas, and no code, prompts-release, or reproduction package is mentioned.
- No deployment-cost measurements (latency, throughput, memory footprint at runtime), so the practical Pareto frontier of accuracy-vs-savings is left unquantified; REAP's "70% pruning" saves storage but not per-token FLOPs, which the headline framing underplays.
- Proposed remedies (grounding-aware pruning criteria, failure-targeted healing data, refusal calibration) are left entirely as future work.

## Relevance

与"极端性能优化下的可靠性"研究方向高度契合：结构化剪枝正是典型的激进度量/容量优化手段，本文系统刻画了它对上下文接地工具调用的静默可靠性损失（特异性退化、过度拒答、聚合指标掩盖的实例级翻转），这类"优化引入的模型行为退化"正是该方向关注的核心问题。同时对自演化智能体的可靠性评估也有直接参考价值——为压缩后智能体提供了组件级 × 任务复杂度 × 拒答校准的评测框架。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the specificity-before-intent degradation pattern and the over-refusal cliff replicate in non-Qwen families (Llama, Gemma) and in other grounded tool-calling domains (calendar, enterprise APIs, GUI agents), or are they artifacts of Qwen training?
- Can a grounding-aware pruning criterion — e.g., protecting layers/neurons important for device resolution and value prediction, identified via probing or activation attribution — measurably delay the specificity cliff compared with ShortGPT/Angular importance scores?
- How much of the MoE safe region survives if expert pruning is combined with reduced active experts per token (true compute reduction), and where does that hybrid regime break?
- Does targeted healing data (distractor-rich device resolution, precise values, partially executable instructions, refusal calibration) recover more capability per SFT example than the balanced mixture used here?
- Since refusal is a "low-commitment" output that pruned models default to under uncertainty, would confidence-calibration or constrained decoding at the execute/reject decision point mitigate over-refusal without retraining?
- What is the actual runtime Pareto curve (latency/throughput/memory vs task accuracy and false-refusal rate) for each pruning configuration on edge hardware?
