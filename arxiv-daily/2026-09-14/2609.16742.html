04 · Critical edge⚠

The central selling point — lighter than classical ABFT — is never measured: no head-to-head comparison against FT-CNN or Sanity-Check appears in the evaluation.

And coverage sags exactly in the deployment-relevant config: FP16 on CIFAR-100 bottoms out at 75.02% TPR (MobileNet-V2-x1.4).

Methodology how it works

Each CONV layer prunes its least-important output channel (smallest L1 norm) and replaces it with a carry-through filter — a 3×3 kernel of zeros with a single center 1. Since convolution already sums across channels, that filter emits an element-wise sum of the input feature maps: a checksum, riding as the layer's last channel. Downstream kernels get their slice for that channel zeroed, so the checksum passes through uncontaminated; BatchNorm is forced to identity on it. At the network's end, one all-ones FC neuron accumulates every carried checksum — a deviation beyond threshold τ flags a soft error and triggers a single re-execution. Thresholds come from fault-injection campaigns or, far cheaper, from profiling the neuron's fault-free distribution (Youden's J picks α = 1%). Architecture patches: ResNet skips the filter on residual-input layers; MobileNet swaps ReLU6 for ReLU to avoid saturation; pruning losses >1% trigger 25 epochs of fine-tuning.

Strengths ＋

  • Zero structural overhead — the checksum rides existing layer shapes: pure overhead <0.1% (noise), memory <10⁻³%, TensorRT pipeline untouched.
  • One-shot end-to-end verification replaces per-operation checksum recomputation — exactly the mismatch that blocks classical ABFT on embedded GPUs.
  • Distribution-based calibration matches FI-based detection (95.86% vs 95.78%) using only fault-free profiling.
  • Thorough, honest sweep — 6 CNNs × 2 datasets × 2 precisions × 3 α values × 2 methods, with negative results reported (FP16 sag, CIFAR-100 gap).
  • Real deployment target — Jetson Orin NX + TensorRT + C++/CUDA, mitigation cost measured under actual FI.

Limitations –

  • Narrow fault model — single bit-flips in weights, host-side PyTorch only; activations, multi-bit upsets, and on-device GPU errors are extrapolated, not measured.
  • FP16 is the weak spot — the precision Jetson-class hardware actually runs shows avg 86.56%, worst 75.02%, with no remedy offered.
  • CIFAR-scale only — no ImageNet, detection/segmentation heads, or transformers.
  • “Critical” = top-1 flip — corrupted outputs that keep the label are silently accepted.
  • Hidden infrastructure — needs per-network calibration and possibly fine-tuning; distribution shift (FPR inflation) unstudied.
  • Re-execution assumes transient faults — a persistent weight bit-flip would reproduce the same wrong answer.

Open questions for further exploration

Q1Does coverage scale to ImageNet-sized models and dense-output tasks, where “critical fault” is harder to define than a top-1 flip?
Q2Can the checksum channel survive INT8 quantization, and does the threshold hold under distribution shift or adversarial inputs?
Q3What is the actual on-device coverage when faults hit the TensorRT engine's memory on the Orin NX itself, not host-side weights?
Q4How does it compare quantitatively against FT-CNN / Sanity-Check / FT-Sparse on the same hardware?
Q5Since a weight bit-flip persists across re-executions, what complementary mechanism (checksum refresh, weight scrubbing) closes that gap?
Q6Does the pruned-channel cost compound in very deep or channel-efficient architectures where every channel matters?
