High concept alignment makes high causal faithfulness only 8% more likely than baseline — across 5 LLMs × 4 benchmarks (per-dataset median binarization).

Why this matters
Alignment is not a faithfulness shortcut. Using activation similarity or self-consistency to judge CoT faithfulness measures the wrong thing — causal intervention is the real test.
Critical edge

Evaluation keeps only examples where CoT and no-CoT predictions agree — excluding exactly where post-hoc rationalization most likely lives, so reported faithfulness may read optimistic.

Methodology

One shared SAE, two passes, one ablation

Two forward passes per example: a prediction pass answers directly from input x, extracting residual stream rpred at the answer token; a CoT pass first generates a zero-shot reasoning chain c, then a fresh pass on (x; c) extracts rcot at the same answer-commitment position. Faithfulness is scored only where both predictions agree (Tutek et al. 2025), de-confounding accuracy gain.

One off-the-shelf residual SAE per backbone (Llama-Scope / Gemma-Scope / Qwen-Scope) encodes both residuals, making cross-pass concept overlap well-defined — the key contrast with Chen et al. (2026), who trained two separate SAEs and tested feature steering (sufficiency) rather than dependence (necessity).

Δp = P(ŷ | rpred) − P(ŷ | r′pred)
r′pred = rpred − Wdec[apred ⊙ mS∩]  S∩ = Spred ∩ Scot

Δp ≫ 0: shared concepts causally drive the answer (faithful). Δp ≈ 0: the answer comes via another path — a concept-level signature of post-hoc rationalization. Correlational metrics (CC-SAE cosine, Jaccard, prediction-grounded recall) quantify alignment only.

Controls across all five models stay at Δp ≈ 0.012–0.096; S∩ approaches the magnitude-ranked top-k upper bound.

  • Completeness: Δp(S∩)/Δp(Spred) ≈ 0.96 (Qwen3-8B, LogiQA) — pred-only features add almost nothing.
  • Faithfulness peaks mid-to-late, not final layers (L24/28 Qwen3-1.7B, L31/42 Gemma-2-9B); low final-layer Δp attributed to output formatting.
  • Localized vs distributed: Llama-3.1-8B swings up to Δp = 0.3 between adjacent layers; Qwen3 stays stably high.
  • Scaling reshapes the profile: Qwen3 1.7B→8B moves the dip from 44% to 23% depth; Gemma-2 2B→9B cuts layer CV from 23% to 12%.
  • SAE-robust: width/sparsity changes keep per-example Δp correlated (Pearson r > 0.8) and preserve peak locations.
  • Verbalization dissociates: in a Gemma-2-9B case, shared concepts travel Chinese entities (L5/L11) → translation bridge (L21) → abstract academic concepts (L41); some causally central concepts never appear in the spoken CoT.
Strengths

What holds up

  • Clean reformulation: faithfulness as causal grounding of shared concepts, via a minimal, well-motivated intervention.
  • Exceptionally thorough validation — count, selection, norm, completeness, and SAE-configuration confounds each get a dedicated control.
  • Layer-wise faithfulness profiling is a genuinely new diagnostic axis that single-number prior metrics cannot offer.
  • Strong negative result with practical import: representational self-consistency is not a faithfulness proxy.
  • Natural-language concept descriptions bridge the causal analysis back to readable reasoning trajectories.
Limitations

Where it stops short

  • Restricted evaluation: agreement-filtered examples only; 3 of 4 datasets are MCQA; ≤9B open-weight models, no reasoning-specialized or frontier models.
  • Δp operates through SAE space — inherits reconstruction error and superposition; robustness covers two hyperparameter axes only.
  • Distributed-vs-localized architecture claims rest on one model per pattern — hypotheses, not family traits.
  • Non-verbalization finding is qualitative (case studies); no quantified rate over the corpus.
  • Per-layer SAEs + multiple passes per example — scaling to thousand-token CoTs from reasoning models may be non-trivial.

Questions for further exploration
  • How does Δp(S∩) behave on o1/R1-style reasoning models with long CoTs — and on MATH / LiveCodeBench, where the agreement filter would remove most examples?
  • What fraction of excluded disagreement cases show low Δp, and would including them change the distributed-vs-localized conclusions?
  • Can the causal-vs-verbalized gap become a per-example "verbalization rate" that predicts failure under adversarial hint injection?
  • Does the mid-to-late layer peak align with answer-formation circuits found by activation patching — and what computes in the dip layers?
  • Could Δp run online, e.g. as a runtime signal for selective CoT auditing, despite its multi-pass per-layer cost?
