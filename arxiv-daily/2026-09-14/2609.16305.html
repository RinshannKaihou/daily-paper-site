04 Methodology

Live simulation, judged by execution — not by text

The unit of evaluation is the complete trajectory — messages, tool calls, observations, policy state — not one response.

  1. Scenario × attack instantiation — a pair runs only when the scenario exposes the required tools, state and policies (22 families × 35 scenarios × 7 domains).
  2. Stateful tool runtime — validates arguments, checks permissions, commits mutations atomically, emits pre/post state hashes for replay verification.
  3. Response-conditioned adversary — regenerates each user message from the agent's actual last reply: refusals get reframed, auth checks get fake "approvals".
  4. Five-way adjudication — deterministic execution evidence first, evidence-citing semantic judges second, human review last.
Safe Completion Correct Refusal Unsafe Completion Over-Refusal Indeterminate

Eight trajectory-level metrics — UCR · CRR · BCR · ORR · IR · SCS=√(BCR·(1−UCR)) · Fail@k · PFR — with safe twins (matched benign counterparts) and counterfactual intervention branches separating "genuinely policy-sensitive" from "refuses everything".

Provenance: RPI + IBM Research (trustworthy-ML group); v1 preprint framed as "preliminary results"; 20+ page appendix releases prompts, schemas, taxonomy and run statistics — but the "Code and Dataset" footnote ships no URL.

Beyond the headline

  • Safe twins expose false safety: Llama-3.3-70B keeps 7% UCR while completing 98% of benign twins; Opus 4.6 is safer (4%) but completes only 74% — refusing broadly isn't calibration.
  • Refusals collapse: GPT-4o initially refuses 57% of attacks, yet 27% of those refusals later buckle (PFR); Mistral Large 3 hits 60% PFR vs. GPT-5.6 Sol's 5%.
  • Stochastic failure accumulates: Fail@10 reaches 51% for GPT-4o and 87% for Mistral Large 3 — even low-UCR configs harbor failing runs.
  • Tool-composition is the strongest attack group for every model (GPT-4o 28%, Mistral 66% UCR); state/auth exploitation the weakest.
  • Final-response-only judging misses 22% of Unsafe Completions; the full deterministic + semantic pipeline reaches 96% label agreement.
06 · What holds up

Strengths

  • Reframes safety as calibration — act vs. refuse vs. over-refuse — with matched safe twins, not binary attack success.
  • Execution-grounded adjudication: state-hash replay means a cautious reply can't excuse an executed unsafe change, and a verbal "done" isn't evidence.
  • Strong ablation discipline — horizon, adaptivity, stateful execution and adjudication evidence each isolate one property; adaptivity (up to 4.0×) is cleanly separated from mere repetition.
  • Fail@k and PFR capture stochastic config vulnerability and refusal collapse — failure modes single-run aggregates structurally cannot see.
  • Modular declarative architecture plus an unusually transparent appendix (prompts, schemas, taxonomy, run stats).
07 · What to doubt

Limitations

  • Attacker and judge model identities undisclosed — a hidden capability variable behind both attack strength and label quality.
  • No confidence intervals or significance tests; the delayed-failure analysis rests on just 16 trajectories, and Fig.6's caption (turn 9) contradicts the body (turn 11).
  • Aggregate UCR is dominated by injection-heavy families (env_prompt_injection, task_injection, social_proof_injection); no per-family breakdown in the main text.
  • "Long-horizon" averages 14.7 turns with T_max unspecified — extrapolation to 50–100+ turn enterprise sessions is untested.
  • No defenses or mitigation baselines — diagnosis without treatment; no external human ground-truth validation; code/dataset link missing from the PDF.
08 · 研究相关性 · Relevance (原文)

对研究 LLM 智能体安全与评测的高度相关：本文把"该拒绝时拒绝、该行动时行动"的校准问题形式化为轨迹级五分类评测，并给出自适应攻击、安全孪生、Fail@k/PFR 等可复用的评测协议与指标设计，可直接借鉴到自建智能体安全基准与防护策略评估中；其"基于执行证据而非文本声明"的裁决思路对任何工具调用型智能体的安全审计都有参考价值。

Highly relevant to LLM-agent safety & evaluation research: the trajectory-level five-way protocol, adaptive attacks, safe twins, and Fail@k/PFR metrics are directly reusable for building in-house agent-safety benchmarks and auditing tool-calling agents on executed evidence rather than textual claims.

09 Questions for further exploration
  1. Which LLMs power the attacker and the judges — and would a stronger adaptive adversary close the gap between GPT-5.6 Sol and Claude Opus 4.6?
  2. Can refusal persistence (PFR) be improved — e.g. per-turn authorization re-verification — without raising over-refusal on models like GPT-4o (27% PFR)?
  3. Does UCR keep climbing past ~15 turns, or does the cumulative-failure curve saturate?
  4. Can safe twins and counterfactual intervention branches directly scaffold a runtime guardrail policy (request-approval vs. block vs. restrict-scope)?
  5. Why are tool-composition attacks strongest for every model — chained calls losing intermediate auth checks? — and can tool-call-level verification target them?
  6. What is the human-review rate in practice, and does human adjudication confirm the pipeline's labels on a held-out sample?
