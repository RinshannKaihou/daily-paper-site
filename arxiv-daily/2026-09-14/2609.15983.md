> **一句话总结：** 提出 Colosseum——一个模型无关的多智能体研究流水线（策略探索→就绪门控→证明分解为依赖DAG的子问题→并行求解→全局验证），每个阶段内部用"并行候选生成+定向证伪+保留批评的重叠随机采样树聚合"来分配推理算力；配合 Gemini 3.1 Pro 取得多项 FOCS/JMLR 级别新定理、在 TCS-Bench 上以跨模型选择达到 71.0%（直接推理的 Gemini 3.1 Pro 仅 30.3%），并在 222 道 Codeforces 题上解出 218 道。

> **TL;DR:** A model-agnostic many-agent harness that organizes long-horizon math/TCS research as a five-stage pipeline (strategy exploration, readiness gate, DAG-structured proof decomposition, parallel subproblem solving, global verification), with each stage running adversarial candidate generation + falsification + critique-preserving tree aggregation. Built on Gemini 3.1 Pro it contributed to several genuinely new theorems (coresets, sparse least-squares barriers, embedding lower bounds) and independently rediscovered the architecture of OpenAI's Erdős unit-distance counterexample with internet disabled. On TCS-Bench (300 research-level FOCS/STOC/SODA theorem-proving tasks) cross-model selection between Colosseum-Gemini-3.1-Pro and Colosseum-Gemini-3.7-Flash runs reaches 71.0% accuracy vs 30.3% for direct Gemini 3.1 Pro (and 68.0% for direct GPT-5.6 Pro max); on a 222-problem Codeforces suite (difficulty >1500) the proof-oriented pipeline plus execution feedback solves 218 problems (4263 self-defined performance rating) vs 213 without the probe. Caveats: proofs are natural-language (no formal checking), compute costs are not reported, and the headline 71.0% is a two-model selection scheme, not a single model.

## 核心贡献

- **五阶段研究流水线**：将长周期数学/TCS研究显式拆分为策略探索（Strategy Exploration）→ 就绪门控（Readiness Gate）→ 证明分解（Proof Decomposition）→ 子问题并行求解（Subproblem Solving）→ 全局验证（Global Verification）；分解产物是带显式依赖边的分节证明骨架（DAG），独立子问题可并行求解，失败只局部重试，不推倒重来。
- **阶段内的对抗式推理过程**：每个困难阶段生成一批带类型化 schema 的候选（策略卡、证明骨架、章节正文、验证评估），由"证伪者"（falser）做定向攻击（找反例、循环论证、隐含强化假设等），再通过**重叠随机采样树聚合**归并——每个聚合节点独立均匀抽取 k 个"候选+其证伪记录"的捆绑做构造性综合（可合并、可修复、可保留竞争分支或宣布未解决冲突），而非投票或排序。
- **跨阶段共享研究状态**：最新证明草稿连同验证反馈整体传给下一轮；一个知识目录（knowledge directory）持续沉淀四类可复用知识——定理/引理、失败路径及其精确失败点、文献引用、结构性观察——避免重复已证伪的路线。
- **真实科研产出**：工作流促成五项新结果（均有配套论文）：ℓp 子空间逼近的强核心集从 Õ(k^{p/2}ε^{-p}) 改进到 Õ(k^{p/2}ε^{-2})；稀疏最小二乘的条件数壁垒（条件于 SSE 假设）；最大内积嵌入的近最优维度下界 D ≥ m^{c_δ}/ε^{2-2δ}；单阶段 Hadamard 量化（去掉残差级、常数缩小约 5.93 倍）；前缀矩阵分解下界 γ_{2,1}(Q) = Ω(log^{3/2}n/(log log n)^{3/2})。
- **两个案例研究**：Knuth's cycles 偶数情形的 46 页与 75 页完整证明草稿；在断网条件下独立重新发现 Erdős 单位距离猜想反例的核心架构（22 页草稿，历经 15 轮探索）。
- **基准评测**：TCS-Bench 跨模型选择 71.0%（非 oracle 方法中最高）；Codeforces 222 题解出 218 题。该工作流已被集成进 Google Antigravity 的 Teamwork 框架（Long Proof pattern）。

## Key Contributions

- **A five-stage research pipeline** that separates strategy exploration, a readiness gate (deciding when a route is mature enough to decompose), proof decomposition into a sectioned dependency DAG, parallel subproblem solving with local retries, and global verification with revision / re-exploration return paths.
- **An adversarial within-stage inference procedure**: parallel generation of schema-typed candidates, targeted falsification by dedicated reviewer roles (counterexamples, circularity, silently strengthened hypotheses, theorem misuse), and **overlapping random-sample tree aggregation** in which each aggregation node draws k candidate-plus-critique bundles uniformly at random (groups overlap; expected reuse ~2–3) and performs *constructive* synthesis that carries unresolved objections forward instead of averaging them away.
- **Shared research state across rounds**: the full rejected draft with verifier feedback is passed to the next attempt, and a knowledge directory accumulates theorems, failed approaches with precise failure points, references, and observations.
- **Genuine research results** attributed to runs of the harness (published in companion papers): strong coresets for ℓp subspace approximation improving Õ(k^{p/2}ε^{-p}) to Õ(k^{p/2}ε^{-2}) while keeping Õ(nnz(A) + d^ω) time; a condition-number barrier for sparse least squares conditional on the randomized exact-volume Small-Set Expansion Hypothesis; a near-optimal dimension lower bound D ≥ m^{c_δ}/ε^{2−2δ} for single-vector maximum-inner-product embeddings; a single-stage Hadamard quantizer (removing the residual stage, cutting the leading constant ~5.93×); and γ_{2,1}(Q) = Ω(log^{3/2}n/(log log n)^{3/2}) for prefix-matrix factorizations, within (log log n)^{3/2} of the dyadic upper bound.
- **Two case studies**: 46-page and 75-page complete proof drafts for the even case of Knuth's cycles; and an independent rediscovery (internet disabled, 15 exploration rounds) of the central architecture of the AI-generated disproof of Erdős's unit-distance conjecture, producing a 22-page draft via unramified towers and relative unit groups.
- **Deployment**: the workflow is integrated into Google Antigravity's Teamwork framework as the "Long Proof" pattern.

## Methodology

Colosseum is a model-agnostic orchestration layer over a base LLM (Gemini 3.1 Pro in most experiments). It is motivated by four challenges: strategic uncertainty, distributed technical difficulty, long outputs with error accumulation, and the need to salvage partial progress from failures.

**Workflow level (5 stages).** (1) *Strategy exploration* develops and attacks multiple candidate routes (different reformulations, reductions, intermediate claims) rather than drafting a proof around the first plausible idea. (2) The *readiness gate* asks whether a route is concrete enough to support a proof plan — central mechanism stable, unresolved claims precise enough to assign to sections — and routes unclear strategies back to exploration. (3) The *decomposer* emits a numbered LaTeX skeleton plus a DAG of section-level subproblems with explicit dependency edges; document order controls exposition, the DAG controls execution order. (4) Subproblems become eligible when dependencies complete, so independent sections are solved in parallel; a local reviewer checks each section against its subproblem and dependencies, and failed sections are retried locally without restarting unaffected work. (5) The *global verifier* (itself multiple independent reviews + tree aggregation; a single concrete fatal defect suffices to reject) reads the assembled proof as one argument, catching cross-section drift, wrong-assumption dependencies, and silently inherited conditional claims. Rejection triggers either targeted revision or re-exploration when the strategy itself is undermined.

**Within-stage inference.** Difficult stages generate a population of candidates (varied by seeds, temperature, prompt perspective, tool access), subject each to targeted falsification, and reduce candidate-critique bundles through an aggregation tree specified by per-level widths (m_0,…,m_L) and sample sizes k_ℓ; each node independently draws k_ℓ distinct bundles uniformly without replacement, so groups overlap rather than partition (e.g., 128→64 nodes with k=5 gives expected reuse 2.5). Aggregation is constructive — merging compatible components, repairing localized flaws, retaining competing branches — with falsification evidence preserved rather than voted away. The root returns a synthesized artifact plus unresolved objections. Typical configurations: strategy-exploration trees (32,16,8,5,1) with k=5 for TCS-Bench/Codeforces (open-problem research sometimes >100 leaves); all other stages share (16,8,5,1).

**Cross-round memory.** The latest draft with verifier feedback grounds the next attempt; a knowledge curator extracts theorems, failed approaches (with conditions under which a variant might work), references, and observations into a shared directory available to all later agents.

**Transfer to competitive programming.** The same proof-oriented architecture is kept; the decomposer still produces a DAG of mathematical/algorithmic subproblems, with a terminal C++ implementation node. The single addition is an execution probe that compiles and runs candidates on public samples and model-generated stress tests, feeding checker outcomes and time/memory measurements into the existing verification-revision loop (hidden tests reserved for grading).

## Results & Highlights

- **TCS-Bench (300 research-level theorem-proving tasks from FOCS/STOC/SODA 2020–2026 papers)**: Colosseum with Gemini 3.1 Pro alone scores **54.0%** and with Gemini 3.7 Flash alone **55.0%**, versus **30.3%** for direct (no harness) Gemini 3.1 Pro — a **+23.7 point** gain from the harness on the same base model. Cross-model selection (Gemini 3.7 Flash casts 8 critiques of the Pro proof; submit it if ≥5 judge it correct, else submit the Flash proof) reaches **71.0%**, the best non-oracle result, vs 68.0% for direct GPT-5.6 Pro (max); the oracle best-of-two upper bound is 77.3%.
- **Complementarity of the two runs**: the two individual Colosseum runs have nearly identical accuracy but complementary errors — selection solves **213 of 300** problems, an improvement of **48 problems** over the stronger individual run (165).
- **Quality of the critique signal**: the 8-critique selection rule distinguishes grader-labeled correct/incorrect proofs with **AUC 0.896**; routing instead on Gemini 3.1 Pro's internal verifier alone yields only **64.7%**, a **6.3-point** gap that isolates the value of external cross-model critique.
- **Codeforces (222 problems, clist.by difficulty >1500, contests 2084–2162, April–Oct 2025)**: the proof-oriented pipeline with the execution probe solves **218 of 222** (self-defined corpus-level performance rating **4263**, median difficulty 2381, range 1530–4599); removing the execution probe drops it to **213 accepted / rating 3918**. Strict as-submitted grading yields the same score (no post-hoc output repair).
- **New mathematics**: the ℓp strong coreset size improves from the previous Õ(k^{p/2}ε^{-p}) (Woodruff–Yasuda, FOCS 2025) to **Õ(k^{p/2}ε^{-2})** with the same sampling rule; the single-stage Hadamard estimator attains the same 1/(d^{4b}) MSE scaling while cutting the proved leading constant by a factor of **~5.93** and removing the residual stage's O(d)-bit payload; the prefix-matrix lower bound Ω(log^{3/2}n/(log log n)^{3/2}) is within a (log log n)^{3/2} factor of the matching upper bound.
- **Long-horizon case studies**: 46-page and 75-page machine-generated proof drafts for Knuth's cycles even-case constructions; and a 22-page draft independently reproducing the architecture of the 2026 disproof of Erdős's unit-distance conjecture (pursuing u(n) ≥ n^{1+ε₀} via unramified mixed-signature towers) across 15 exploration rounds with internet access disabled.

## Authors & Credibility

The team is from Google Research (Lin and Woodruff are co-first authors; Woodruff is also at Carnegie Mellon University), with Mirrokni a long-time Google Research leadership figure in algorithms and ML. The group has an extensive track record in TCS (FOCS/STOC/SODA/JMLR publications) and in LLM-for-research systems — the paper explicitly builds on their prior Gemini-assisted case-study work (Woodruff et al., 2026). All five headline research results are documented in companion arXiv papers authored by overlapping subsets of this team, and two proof artifacts (Knuth's cycles, Erdős) are on GitHub. The claim of productization — integration into Google Antigravity's Teamwork framework as the "Long Proof" pattern — is cited to an August 2026 Google blog post. Credibility is high on the mathematics side; the main soft spot is self-attribution: the same group built the harness, ran it, and co-authored the companion results papers, so the harness-vs-human contribution split is not independently auditable (the paper itself notes this difficulty for related systems).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The individual ingredients (self-consistency-style sampling, adversarial critique, tree aggregation, decompose-and-verify) are all known; the novelty lies in the *composition* — the readiness gate deciding when exploration must continue, critiques traveling with candidates through aggregation instead of being averaged away, and verifier findings routed back to specific sections of a persistent document. Relative to closest prior work (Aletheia's generate-verify-revise loop, LeanMarathon's blueprint + dependency graph, Momus's context-detachment), the differentiators are real but incremental in kind.
- **Rigor of evaluation**: Three concerns. (1) The headline **71.0%** on TCS-Bench is a *two-model* cross-selection scheme; the fair single-model-harness comparison is 54.0% (Gemini 3.1 Pro), which *loses* to direct GPT-5.6 Pro (max) at 68.0% — the abstract's phrasing ("using Gemini 3.1 Pro and Gemini 3.7 Flash") is accurate but easy to over-read. (2) All TCS-Bench accuracies are measured by a reference-assisted *LLM grader* whose prompt was validated at ">90% accuracy" on only 100 expert-labeled proofs — a few points of label noise directly moves every number in Table 2. (3) **No compute accounting**: token/cost counts are never reported, so the harness cannot be compared compute-matched against cheap baselines like self-consistency or simple best-of-n with the same models; the paper concedes this ("Compute-matched evaluation would be needed") only in the future-work section on adaptive allocation.
- **Missing ablations**: apart from the Codeforces execution-probe comparison (218 vs 213) and the internal-verifier-vs-external-critique contrast (64.7% vs 71.0%), no component ablations exist — the readiness gate, the knowledge directory, the falsification step, and the overlapping tree aggregation (vs. plain majority or single-shot aggregation) are never individually disabled on a benchmark. The claimed mechanism-level insights rest on qualitative case studies.
- **No formal verification**: unlike Lean-based systems (which the related-work section itself flags as the stronger correctness standard), Colosseum proofs are natural language judged by LLM critics; "falsification" is a search objective, and the paper honestly notes that failure to find a defect does not establish correctness. For the open-problem results, human co-authors presumably did the real verification — the paper says the workflow "contributed" without quantifying human effort per result.
- **Reproducibility**: the harness itself does not appear to be open-sourced (only two proof artifacts are on GitHub); configurations are described (tree widths, sample sizes) but the prompt templates in Appendix A are "shortened versions". Reimplementation is possible in outline but not in detail. Base models (Gemini 3.1 Pro / 3.7 Flash, GPT-5.6 Pro) are proprietary and access-dependent.
- **Significance**: if the five companion theorems hold up under community scrutiny, this is among the stronger demonstrations of LLM orchestration producing publishable pure-math research; the independent Erdős rediscovery under information isolation is a genuinely striking controls-adjacent data point. The Codeforces result (218/222) is strong but the paper defines its own performance rating and provides no comparison row for competing agentic coding systems.
- **Red flags**: heavy self-citation density (companion papers [38]–[41], [16], benchmark [15], and prior system [56] are all self-referential); the "integrated into Google Antigravity" claim is a product blog rather than a peer-reviewed artifact; TCS-Bench itself was co-authored by this paper's authors, so the benchmark and the evaluated system share a team.

## Strengths

- Architecturally clean answer to the right problem: long-horizon research fails on *decision structure* (when to commit, what to repair, what to salvage), not just on per-step accuracy, and the readiness gate + DAG decomposition + revision-vs-re-exploration return paths encode that directly.
- Critique-preserving aggregation is a well-motivated departure from answer voting: agreements can hide shared errors, and unresolved objections surviving into later stages are exactly the state a researcher needs.
- Demonstrated on the hardest setting available — open problems from FOCS/JMLR-level literature — with verifiable companion papers rather than only benchmark deltas; the coreset ε^{-p} → ε^{-2} improvement changes the exponent, not a constant.
- The information-isolated rediscovery of the Erdős counterexample architecture (internet off, 15 rounds, accumulated knowledge carrying partial results forward) is a compelling existence proof for sustained autonomous search.
- Honest framing in several places: falsification ≠ verification, oracle vs achievable selection kept separate, grader prompt validated on a held-out labeled set, Codeforces rating explicitly defined rather than claimed as official.
- Real transfer test: the same proof-oriented controller, unchanged except for an execution probe, reaches 218/222 on Codeforces — evidence the architecture is not math-specific scaffolding.

## Limitations

- No formal proof checking; correctness ultimately rests on LLM critics and (for the new theorems) on human co-authors whose effort is not quantified.
- Compute and cost are unreported, so the value-per-FLOP of the harness versus simple inference-scaling baselines is unknown; all benchmark gains confound orchestration with substantially more inference.
- Component-level ablations are absent (readiness gate, knowledge directory, tree overlap rate, falsifier strength); only the execution probe and the selection signal are ablated.
- The single-harness TCS-Bench result (54.0% with Gemini 3.1 Pro) is below the strongest direct baseline (GPT-5.6 Pro max, 68.0%), so the harness is not a substitute for a stronger base model on that benchmark; the 71.0% headline requires running two models and a critique-based router.
- Evaluation infrastructure is partly self-supplied: TCS-Bench was co-authored by this team, and its LLM grader's ~90% validation accuracy bounds label reliability.
- The harness and prompts are not fully open-sourced; base models are proprietary, hindering independent replication.
- Fixed inference configurations (pre-set tree widths and sample sizes) rather than adaptive allocation — acknowledged as future work, along with using recorded research trajectories as post-training data (credit assignment unsolved).

## Relevance

这篇论文正对着"多智能体编排 + 推理时算力扩展 + AI 用于数学/科研自动化"的交叉点：其"探索-分解-验证-修复"的研究流水线、保留批评证据的树聚合、以及跨轮知识目录，对设计长周期 LLM agent 系统和研究自动化 harness 都有直接的方法论参考价值，且有真实定理产出作为可信度背书。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of the TCS-Bench gain (54.0% vs 30.3%) survives a compute-matched comparison against simple best-of-n sampling or self-consistency with the same Gemini 3.1 Pro budget — and what is the actual token/cost multiplier of a full Colosseum run?
- Would replacing the natural-language verification loop with an optional Lean formalization stage (as in LeanMarathon) catch defects the LLM global verifier misses, and at what overhead?
- Which component drives the cross-model selection gain: is it the diversity of two base models, the 8-critique external signal (AUC 0.896), or the threshold rule — and can the AUC 0.896 signal be distilled into the base model itself?
- How does performance scale with the number of exploration rounds and tree width on open problems — is there an inference-scaling law for the readiness-gate loop, analogous to per-problem scaling curves?
- Can the recorded research trajectories (strategy cards, falsifier reports, dependency graphs) actually serve as post-training data, and does the proposed branching-structure credit assignment measurably improve the base model on held-out problems?
- The Erdős rediscovery arrived at the same central architecture as OpenAI's solution — does that reflect a bottleneck in the problem space or a convergence bias of current frontier models, and would a third independent run on a different open problem replicate the effect?
