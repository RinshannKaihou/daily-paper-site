Why This Matters

Directly attacks the HBM/SSD capacity-and-bandwidth bottleneck that gates long-horizon agents — and sets the open-weights agentic baseline.

Critical Edge

“Negligible degradation” is asserted without in-report ablations, and regressions on MGSM, MATH, and LongBench-V2 suggest the extreme compression trades away real capability.

— Critical Assessment · Red flags
01Methodologydistilled
  • Causal Encoder-Decoder (CED): 40-layer backbone split 20+20; decoder global KV is projected from encoder final states, cutting prefill from O(NL) to ~O(NL/2) and enabling asymmetric 8B/16B activation.
  • CSA2: static Full / Reindex / Reuse layer modes share main KV, indexer K, and Top-K indices across layers; a hierarchical sparse indexer scores a fixed 16,384-candidate pool, making per-query indexing constant in context length.
  • Aggressive cache compression: FP4 (E2M1 + per-16-channel E4M3 scale) QAT on the main KV; SWA Bounded Replay reconstructs sliding-window states from only the last 128 tokens.
  • Training: 45T multimodal tokens, sparse attention from scratch (64K → 1M at 34T); Muon + AdamW + a Sinkhorn-balanced momentum update for Engram tables.
  • Post-training: deliberately plain SFT → RL → on-policy distillation; gains attributed to task synthesis and the DSec sandbox (millions of concurrent containers). Scalar 1–100 reasoning-effort control in the API.

Full-stack technical report, not a controlled ablation study.

03Strengths
  • Attacks the real deployment bottleneck with multiplicative savings: ¼ global cache, ⅛ persistent cache, ~½ prefill FLOPs, near-constant decode FLOPs to 1M tokens.
  • Near-20-point single-generation DeepSWE jump and SOTA Terminal-Bench 2.1 / Automation-Bench / CyberGym from a 16B-activation model.
  • Unusually candid limitations section; cross-scaffold evaluation across 8 harness configurations.
  • The trained-once 1–100 effort dial (front-loaded gains at 60–80) is practically valuable for cost control.
  • Open weights plus concrete engineering detail (kernel counts, container density) — a useful reference for long-context serving.
04Limitations
  • No quantitative in-report ablations for FP4-vs-FP8 KV, Reuse-vs-Reindex assignment, or bounded-replay error — several claims rest on companion papers.
  • Clear gaps on the hardest suites: Terminal-Bench 4.0 (31.2 vs 51.8), TB 3.0 (30.0 vs 43.3), ExploitGym (15.3 vs 33.7), visual agents (BabyVision 89.6 vs 94.1).
  • Regressions vs its own predecessors: MGSM 80.2 (was 85.7), MATH 61.1, LongBench-V2 45.2 — despite the 1M-context focus.
  • 196B Engram params sit outside the “552B backbone” headline; total ≈ 748B, and footprint claims cover KV, not weights.
  • Multi-agent results are preliminary, self-comparative, and run on DeepSeek's own Agent Team harness.
05Questions for Further Exploration
  • How large is the exact quality cost of SWA Bounded Replay and FP4 KV in isolation — can a controlled sweep quantify “negligible”?
  • Does the 16,384-position candidate pool cap recall on needle-in-a-haystack tasks near 1M context; would a learned, query-adaptive pool help?
  • Could CSA2's static Full/Reindex/Reuse assignment become dynamic or learned routing — where would the accuracy-efficiency frontier land?
  • Why does MGSM regress — data mixture (7:1), aggressive cache compression, or MoE load-balancing changes?
  • Does effort conditioning (b = 1–100) transfer to unseen scaffolds and latency-sensitive settings without calibration drift?
  • What drives multi-agent gains at long (8–20h) deadlines, and how do results change vs external multi-agent baselines?
