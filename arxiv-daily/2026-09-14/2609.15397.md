> **一句话总结：** 本文提出一个将外部世界事件与运行时观测分离的"效果历史"形式模型，系统枚举出 8 类智能体-工具边界上的外部效果异常（重复/缺失/孤儿补偿/残留/过早外化/污染投机/冲突/幻影补偿），推导出排除每类异常所需的边界能力，并对官方 MCP 注册表 98,291 个工具的标注普查发现：现有四条 MCP 注解均无法完整表达任何一项所需的事务语义能力。

> **TL;DR:** A position-plus-measurement paper arguing that agent workflow correctness fails not at individual tool calls but at the tool boundary's missing transactional semantics. It formalizes an effect-history model, derives an 8-anomaly catalog (A1–A8) in the style of ANSI isolation-level anomalies, proves-by-construction four guarantee boundaries where black-box invocation cannot suffice, and grounds the argument empirically with a census of 98,291 tools from the official MCP registry (snapshot 2026-07-27): 74.0% of tools emit at least one annotation field and one signature covers 39.9% of all tools, yet none of the 8 required boundary capabilities is fully expressible — only A1 (authoritative convergence) gets even a "limited" idempotence hint. Compared against current agent runtimes (Atomix, Cordon, CoAgent, RAC, ACRFence, Shepherd), each of which covers only fragments of the catalog under runtime-local assumptions, the paper concludes the missing layer is reusable transactional contracts at the tool interface, not another recovery mechanism.

## 核心贡献

- **效果历史（effect-history）形式模型**：借鉴多级事务管理，将系统切分为两个相邻层级 —— L0（原子工具调用，如 `charge()`、`book_flight()`）与 L1（由其组成的工作流，如 `BookTrip`）。历史记录同时包含执行/外部事件（`attempt`、`externalize`、`cmp`、`neutralizes`、`dep`、`commute`、`commit/abort`）与运行时观测（`observe(a, s)`、`Req(w)`、`resolved(w)`、`survives(e)`），关键设计是**把"世界中发生了什么"与"运行时知道什么"严格分开**——`unknown` 是运行时的状态而非世界的不确定性。
- **八类外部效果异常目录（A1–A8）**，按事务控制丢失的三种方式分三个家族，且每个家族成员**由构造枚举**而非随意罗列：
  - 不确定性家族（A1–A3）：结果未确认时运行时只能重发（→ Duplicated 重复效果）、提交（→ Missing 缺失已提交效果）、或补偿（→ Orphaned 孤儿补偿）；
  - 工作流家族（A4–A6）：已外化效果相对其解析点的三种关系——存留（→ Residue 未补偿残留）、时机（→ Premature 过早外化）、依赖（→ Contaminated 污染投机）；
  - 交互家族（A7–A8）：效果遇到的两类外部参与者——并发执行（→ Conflicting 冲突外化）或外部观察者（→ Phantom 幻影补偿）。
- **安全保证画像（guarantee profiles）**：unknown-safe（排除 A1–A3）、compensation-safe（A4）、speculation-safe（A5–A6）、externally-mediated（A7–A8），并用该目录逐一评判当前六个智能体运行时（ACRFence、RAC、Atomix、Cordon、CoAgent、Shepherd）的声明覆盖——没有任何系统实现干净的累积层级，也没有系统覆盖 A8。
- **契约层设计 + 四个黑盒保证边界**：将排除异常所需能力组织为三类可复用契约（不确定性收敛、生命周期控制、协调/可见性），并论证四个仅靠黑盒调用无法突破的点：无权威结果原语时的 exactly-once 屏障、无可交换不可逆效果间的冲突修复、开放世界反应终结补偿能力、跨工具多效果无法原子释放（经典原子提交问题，需工具侧 prepare/commit）。
- **MCP 注册表全量标注普查**：对 2026-07-27 官方注册表快照（59,625 条目 → 18,688 个去重服务器 → 9,234 个可探测远程端点，匿名调用 `tools/list`，从不调用任何工具本身）得到 98,291 个工具的四条注解（`readOnlyHint`/`destructiveHint`/`idempotentHint`/`openWorldHint`）分布，并区分"显式 false"与"省略字段"；代码与数据以工件开源（github.com/flame-stream/mcp-annotation-census）。

## Key Contributions

- **An effect-history model** that separates events in the external world from the runtime's observations of them, structured as a two-level slice (L0 atomic tool calls, L1 composed workflows) in the spirit of multilevel transaction management. An `unknown` outcome is a state of the runtime, not of the world — and acting under it is precisely what several anomalies turn on.
- **A systematically enumerated catalog of eight external-effect anomalies (A1–A8)**, organized by the three ways transactional control is lost: uncertainty (A1 Duplicated, A2 Missing, A3 Orphaned — fixed by the three safety-relevant reactions to an unresolved outcome: re-issue, commit, compensate), workflow structure (A4 Residue, A5 Premature, A6 Contaminated — survival, timing, dependency of an externalized effect relative to its resolution point), and interaction (A7 Conflicting, A8 Phantom — concurrent executions vs. exogenous observers). The analogies are precise: A6 is the effect-world "dirty read", A8 is a compensation whose externally observed consequence survives.
- **Safety guarantee profiles** (unknown-safe, compensation-safe, speculation-safe, externally-mediated) with exclusion sets, plus a reading of six current agent runtimes (ACRFence, RAC, Atomix, Cordon, CoAgent, Shepherd) against the catalog: coverage everywhere rests on runtime-owned declarations (adapter idempotency keys, registered inverses, reversibility tiers) rather than shared contracts, none covers A8, and none realizes a clean cumulative hierarchy.
- **A contract-level account** of the boundary capabilities (outcome resolution, idempotency keys, status endpoints, quotes/dry-runs, expiring holds, commutativity declared at the resource, visibility control) with **four guarantee boundaries** where black-box invocation alone is insufficient: the exactly-once barrier under ambiguous outcomes, the impossibility of general conflict repair between non-commuting irreversible effects without a mediator, the end of compensation's reach once an outside actor has reacted (A8), and the classical atomic-commitment problem for multi-effect release across tools.
- **A registry-wide MCP annotation census**: 98,291 tools from the official MCP registry (snapshot 2026-07-27), reached by anonymously querying `tools/list` on all 9,234 remote targets (4,838 responded, 4,318 failed to connect, 74 timed out; median 11 tools per server; no tool was ever called), keeping explicit `false` distinct from omitted fields. Verdict: the four standard annotations provide only coarse call-level hints and none of the eight required capabilities is fully expressible.

## Methodology

The paper proceeds in three methodologically distinct stages.

**(1) Formal modeling (Sections 2–3).** The system is abstracted as a slice of two adjacent levels from multilevel transaction management: L0 operations (tool calls like `charge()`, `book_flight()`) and L1 workflows (`BookTrip`, `HireCandidate`). An execution history records L0 attempts and their external effects, the runtime's partial observations (confirmed / failed / unknown), compensating actions and what they neutralize, causal dependencies (`dep`), commutativity facts, and L1 resolutions (`commit`/`abort`). Effect safety is evaluated at L1 against resolutions. The model focuses on five orthogonal dimensions of L0 operations — idempotence, invertibility, externalization timing/control, determinism, and commutativity — explicitly noting they do not collapse onto a single reversible/irreversible scale (increment commutes but is not idempotent; delete is idempotent but does not commute). From this vocabulary, anomaly patterns are stated as forbidden history fragments (each illustrated with a small trace, e.g., `observe(a1, unknown)` → re-issue → invoice paid twice), and for each anomaly the boundary capability required to exclude it is derived by asking what a runtime protocol must be able to observe or control to rule out the pattern. The catalog's families are claimed to be enumerated by construction: the uncertainty family is fixed by the three safety-relevant actions available under an unresolved outcome; the workflow family by the three relations an externalized effect can bear to its resolution; the interaction family by the two kinds of external participant.

**(2) Contract analysis (Section 4).** The required capabilities are organized into three recurring contract families — authoritative convergence (uncertainty), lifecycle control (invariants checkable before irreversible effects, compensation semantics, stable effect/resource identity, commit spheres), and coordination (commutativity declared at the resource, mediation, visibility). Four guarantee boundaries are then stated as consequences of the definitions, each mapped to a classical result (exactly-once barrier, atomic commitment) and to what current systems do at that limit.

**(3) Measurement (Section 5).** A full snapshot of the official MCP registry (2026-07-27; 59,625 entries, 18,688 distinct servers at latest version) was filtered to 9,234 remote targets (9,454 package/stdio-only servers excluded as out of scope). Each target's `tools/list` was queried anonymously, including targets declaring auth headers — no responding target rejected discovery with 401/403. 4,838 targets returned at least one tool (4,318 failed to connect, 74 timed out, 4 returned none), yielding 98,291 tools. The four spec-defined annotations were recorded at the wire level, with an explicit `false` kept distinct from an omitted field, and never invoking any tool. The authors explicitly disclaim attributing measured values to author intent (SDK defaults and templates are a plausible confound) and note the census reads the boundary *as declared*, not as implementations behave.

## Results & Highlights

The empirical results all come from the MCP registry census of 98,291 tools (snapshot 2026-07-27):

- **Annotation emission is widespread but shallow.** On the census of 98,291 MCP tools, 74.0% of tools serialize at least one annotation field and 61.7% serialize all four fields; however, `readOnlyHint=true` paired with (inapplicable) `destructiveHint` accounts for 52.8% of all tools, so a majority of the emitted surface carries no risk information.
- **Tool-level signatures are heavily concentrated.** Reading each tool as a signature over the four fields (true/false/omitted, 81 possibilities), 66 signatures appear, but the single signature (read-only, non-destructive, idempotent, open-world) covers 39.9% of all tools and the next most common signature is *no annotation at all* (26.0%); the top three together cover 75.8%. Differentiation exists but is coarse: among multi-tool servers emitting at least one field, 76.7% use more than one signature, with the median dominant signature covering 79.4% of a server's tools.
- **The one risk-related hint is mostly vacuous.** `destructiveHint` is set on 65.8% of all tools, but it is meaningful only where the tool is not read-only — just 12.9% of tools carry an applicable classification, and only 3.1% of all tools assert an actually destructive operation.
- **Expressibility verdict (Table 4): zero for eight.** Against the eight boundary capabilities derived from the anomaly catalog, current MCP annotations express none fully: seven are rated "No" and only A1 (authoritative convergence) is rated "Limited: idempotence hint only" — because `idempotentHint` marks a property but supplies no idempotency key, and no hint covers status resolution, compensation, staging, commutativity, or visibility.
- **Runtime coverage is fragmentary (Table 3).** Of the six surveyed runtimes, Atomix comes closest (A1/A3 partial, A4, A5–A6, A7 partial) but has an A2 gap on multi-effect release and no crash-safe exactly-once; RAC covers only A4 partially with no gating and no idempotency (A1–A3 unaddressed); ACRFence is proposed but not implemented; and no system addresses A8 (phantom compensation / exogenous reactions) at all.
- **Census reach itself is a finding.** Of 59,625 registry entries (18,688 distinct servers), 9,454 exposed no remote endpoint and of 9,234 remote targets only 4,838 (≈52%) returned tools — the measurements describe the reachable remote subset, and unreachable/package/stdio servers may differ.

Artem Trofimov (AVIV Group, Berlin; ACM-member contact address) and Boris Novikov (independent researcher, Helsinki; previously a database professor at HSE St. Petersburg with a long record in transaction processing and stream query processing). The senior author's background shows: the paper is deeply grounded in the classical transaction-processing literature — Berenson et al.'s ANSI isolation critique, Adya's weak consistency, Garcia-Molina & Salem's Sagas, Korth/Levy/Silberschatz compensating transactions, O'Neil's escrow method, Weikum's multilevel transaction management, Bernstein/Hadzilacos/Goodman's recovery theory — and applies that machinery to agent–tool interfaces rather than reinventing it. The census comes with a public artifact (code, data, full methodology at github.com/flame-stream/mcp-annotation-census), and the empirical protocol is unusually careful for a measurement section of this kind (anonymous `tools/list`-only probing, no tool invocations, explicit-false vs. omitted distinction, no attribution of values to author intent). ## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** The core move — importing anomaly-catalog methodology (à la ANSI isolation levels) to the agent–tool boundary and centering *outcome uncertainty of third-party tool calls* rather than shared-state concurrency — is genuinely distinct from the closest prior work, and the paper says exactly how: the mechanically verified anomaly catalog of Khan (arXiv:2606.17182) covers concurrency anomalies over a runtime's shared state, while this paper asks what a *shared interface* must declare for any such guarantee to be establishable at all. The census is, to my knowledge, the first registry-wide measurement of MCP annotation semantics. That said, the individual mechanisms (idempotency keys, sagas, escrow, two-phase commit, staging) are all classical; the novelty is the framing and the gap analysis, not the machinery.
- **Rigor — the catalog is a conjecture, not a theorem.** The paper is admirably explicit about this ("coverage-oriented rather than complete by theorem"; "formal coverage, minimality, and independence are future work"), and the four guarantee boundaries are likewise stated "as consequences of the definitions... formal proofs are future work." A skeptical reader should note that the load-bearing claim — that the three families *by construction* exhaust the representable losses of transactional control — is exactly the part left unproven, and the enumeration argument ("waiting, querying, or escalating creates no new effect-history pattern") is asserted rather than discharged.
- **Rigor — census selection bias.** The 98,291-tool sample excludes 9,454 package/stdio servers entirely and reflects only 4,838 of 9,234 remote targets (4,318 connection failures, 74 timeouts). Nearly half the reachable remote registry is unmeasured, and the authors concede unreachable and stdio-only servers may differ — plausibly *systematically* (stdio servers are often local/personal tools with different annotation practices). The headline "98,291 tools" therefore describes a majority-remote, reachable subset, not the registry.
- **Interpretability confound, self-flagged.** The emission rates (74.0% any field, 61.7% all four) cannot distinguish deliberate declaration from SDK defaults or server templates, and 52.8% of tools emit a semantically inapplicable readOnly/destructive pairing. The paper says so, but this weakens the register-level reading: the census measures what the wire carries, not what server authors believe.
- **The runtime comparison is documentary, not empirical.** Table 3 records systems' *stated* coverage from their papers (several 2026 preprints; ACRFence explicitly "proposed; attack validated, not implemented"), not measured behavior — a fair caveat the authors make, but it means the "no runtime covers the catalog" conclusion inherits the accuracy of six other papers' self-descriptions.
- **No end-to-end validation.** There is no case study of a real workflow failure, no prototype contract layer, and no demonstration that adding the proposed contracts to one tool would have prevented a specific anomaly; the running example (900 EUR flight + 700 EUR hotel on a 1500 EUR budget) is hypothetical. The paper is a model + census, and its actionable output is a design argument.
- **Reproducibility.** Strong on the census (public artifact, snapshot date, exact filtering funnel, no-tool-invoked protocol, connection-failure accounting) and on the model (all anomaly patterns given as concrete traces); weaker on the theory, whose formalization exists only in prose and is deferred to future work.

## Strengths

- A single clean conceptual frame that unifies failures usually treated ad hoc (duplicate charges, orphaned refunds, leaked offer letters, publish races) under one effect-history vocabulary, with world events strictly separated from runtime observations.
- The anomaly catalog is constructive: each family has a structural reason for its size, each anomaly comes with a concrete forbidden trace and a named boundary capability plus representative realization (idempotency key, status endpoint, dry-run quote, expiring hold, resource-level commutativity declaration, mediator, visibility control).
- The four guarantee boundaries correctly identify that some properties (exactly-once over an unreliable channel, atomic multi-tool release, retracting an observed reaction) are impossible without tool-side participation — connecting agent reliability to classical distributed-computing impossibilities rather than wishing them away.
- The MCP census is large, methodologically transparent, conservative in its claims (declared vs. actual behavior; remote-reachable subset), and backed by an open artifact.
- Honest scoping: the paper explicitly lists what is *outside* the model (planning errors, read-side anomalies, security/policy, misclassified contracts, intra-execution order, liveness), preventing overclaiming.

## Limitations

- No formal proofs: catalog completeness/minimality/independence and the four guarantee boundaries are argued informally; "conjecture" is the authors' own word for the family-exhaustiveness claim.
- Census scope gaps: package/stdio servers excluded by construction; ~47% of remote targets unreachable; annotation values possibly dominated by SDK defaults; declared metadata only, so tools enforcing stronger internal semantics (dedup, idempotency keys invisible at the interface) are underscored — the paper notes measuring that gap needs implementation-level analysis.
- The runtime coverage table reads preprint self-descriptions rather than experiments; several compared systems are themselves unreviewed 2026 preprints.
- No implementation of the proposed contract layer, no protocol synthesis, no cost model for when speculation/early release is worth its residue (all named as future work).
- The model deliberately excludes read-side anomalies, security/policy violations, and liveness — readers interested in those failure classes must look elsewhere.

## Relevance

这篇论文直击当前 LLM 智能体工程中最实际的可靠性短板之一：工具调用各自成功但工作流整体失败。对任何构建 multi-agent 系统、MCP 工具生态或 agent 运行时（重试、补偿、投机执行、并发控制）的工作，A1–A8 异常目录和"边界必须声明哪些事务语义"的契约层设计是可直接引用的分析框架，也解释了为什么纯 orchestration 层的修补永远补不齐语义缺口。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the family-exhaustiveness conjecture actually be discharged — e.g., by a mechanical enumeration over the Section 2 vocabulary in the style of the verified detectors built for shared-state concurrency anomalies (arXiv:2606.17182)?
- What do the 9,454 excluded package/stdio servers and the 4,318 unreachable remote targets annotate? Would including them shift the 39.9% dominant-signature concentration or the 3.1% destructive-assertion rate materially?
- How much of the annotation surface is SDK-default noise? Cloning a sample of servers' source (à la the GitHub MCP dataset, arXiv:2607.10123) could separate deliberate declaration from template inheritance.
- Could a minimal transactional contract (idempotency key + status endpoint + declared compensation) be retrofitted to even 100 high-traffic MCP tools, and would a runtime consuming it measurably reduce A1–A4 incidents versus retry-only baselines?
- The paper prices nothing: can a cost model decide when premature externalization (A5) is rational — e.g., sending an offer early because negotiation latency dominates the expected cost of retraction plus phantom consequences (A8)?
- How do the anomalies interact with nondeterministic, LLM-backed tools whose *same logical operation* has non-stationary semantics — does the determinism dimension need its own contract family?
- Is there a staged-adoption path where `readOnlyHint`/`idempotentHint` are strengthened into verifiable claims (challenge-response idempotency proofs) without breaking the advisory, optional semantics MCP servers currently assume?
