> **一句话总结：** 提出 MemRiskBench——一个针对长程 LLM Agent 记忆风险的基准：用五类风险分类法（过期记忆、冲突消解、跨域泄露、撤销记忆复用、约束衰减）配 120 个脚本化情景和确定性轨迹评分（无 LLM 裁判），并给出一个覆盖约束的贪心子集选择器，在 20% 子集（24/120 情景）上同时保住排序（Spearman 0.975）、风险覆盖（1.0）和高风险模型检出（1.0），计算量降为 1/5。

> **TL;DR:** MemRiskBench evaluates memory-induced risks (not memory capability) in long-horizon LLM agents via a 120-episode scripted benchmark with deterministic, trace-grounded scoring and no LLM-as-judge. Across five locally-run quantized models (1.5B–7B), aggregate accuracy (0.775–0.885) masks stark per-risk gaps — Qwen2.5-1.5B passes only 1/24 cross-scope leakage episodes (4%) vs Phi-3.5-mini's 10/24 (42%), while inverting to 24/24 on constraint adherence. Its coverage-constrained greedy subset selector preserves the full-benchmark Spearman ranking at 0.975 with risk coverage 1.0 and high-risk detection 1.0 on a 20% subset (5x compute cut), whereas stratified-by-risk sampling drops to Spearman 0.698 and high-risk detection 0.345, and random sampling yields Spearman 0.733 with a CI spanning negative values. Weaknesses: only 5 small models (3 of 5 Qwen), a Q3-vs-Q4 quantization confound for the 7B model, and a headline size–risk effect that does not survive Holm correction (smallest adjusted p = 0.22).

## 核心贡献

- **五类记忆风险分类法 + 确定性检查（C1，主贡献）**：定义 R1 过期记忆依赖、R2 冲突记忆误消解、R3 跨域（跨用户）泄露、R4 已撤销记忆复用、R5 约束衰减五类可确定性评分的风险，外加一个已文档化但未纳入主评分的 R6（摘要污染）。每类风险由四通道检查操作化：文本（字符串包含/正则）、工具（调用验证、参数等值）、记忆（读写台账、状态、作用域）、约束（活跃/违规监控）。
- **120 情景脚本化基准与全轨迹记录**：每类风险 24 个情景，分 easy/medium/hard 三档（37/45/38 个），难度由干扰项数量、中间更新和轨迹长度（5–8 / 10–14 / 15–25 轮）决定。评分路径完全无 LLM 裁判。
- **风险保持型子集选择器（C2）**：基于 12 维轨迹派生特征（难度、区分度、失败密度、复杂度）的覆盖约束贪心前向选择，目标函数同时优化排序相关性、风险类型覆盖和高风险模型检出；用留一模型交叉验证避免测试集泄漏。在 20% 子集（24/120）上三项目标全部保持，计算量降 5 倍（约 18 小时 → 3.6 小时）。
- **五个本地量化模型的实证刻画（C3）**：包括"RAG 与近因过滤等标准检索级记忆技术比不过滤更差"的记忆基线实验，说明记忆风险需要专门评测而非现成检索方案。
- **完整可复现发布**：120 个情景定义、600 条原始轨迹、确定性评分器、子集选择器、bootstrap 审计脚本及严格匹配修订审计全部开源（GitHub: fuxue-mingzhu/MemRiskBench）。

## Key Contributions

- **Five-category memory-risk taxonomy with deterministic checks (C1, primary):** R1 stale-memory reliance, R2 conflicting-memory misresolution, R3 cross-scope leakage, R4 revoked-memory reuse, R5 constraint decay, plus a documented-but-unscored R6 (summary contamination). Each risk is operationalized by four evidence channels: text (string containment/regex), tool (call verification, argument equality), memory (read/write ledger, status, scope), and constraint (activity/violation monitoring).
- **120-episode scripted benchmark with full trace logging:** 24 episodes per risk across three difficulty tiers (37 easy / 45 medium / 38 hard), where difficulty scales with distractor count, intermediate updates, and trajectory length (5–8 / 10–14 / 15–25 turns). Episodes are DAGs of events (memory writes, user messages, tool calls, constraint declarations, decision points), and the pass/fail path uses no LLM-as-judge.
- **Risk-preserving subset selector (C2):** a coverage-constrained greedy forward selector over 12-dimensional trace-derived features (difficulty, discrimination/pass-rate spread, failure density, complexity), maximizing Ψ(S) = α·ρ + β·cov(S) + γ·hr(S) with leave-one-model-out cross-validation to prevent test-set leakage. At a 20% subset (24 of 120 episodes) it retains ranking, risk coverage, and high-risk model detection while cutting compute 5x (~18 h to ~3.6 h).
- **Empirical characterization of five local quantized models (C3):** including a memory-baseline experiment showing standard retrieval-level filtering (RAG, recency) performs *worse* than no filtering on MemRiskBench, motivating dedicated risk evaluation.
- **Fully reproducible release:** all 120 episode definitions, the 600 raw traces, the deterministic scorer, the selector, bootstrap audit scripts, and the strict-matching revision audit are public on GitHub (fuxue-mingzhu/MemRiskBench).

## Methodology

The benchmark pipeline executes 120 scripted episodes against 5 locally-run quantized instruction-tuned models via llama.cpp on a single 16 GB GPU: Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (Q4_K_M), Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct (Q4_K_M), Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (Q3_K_M — Q4 would not fit alongside the prompt cache), Phi-3.5-mini-instruct (Q4_K_M), and Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct (Q4_K_M), yielding 600 deterministic traces. Each episode is a directed acyclic event graph; at each decision point the agent emits a tool call, final answer, or clarification, and the trace logs memory store, tool-call log, constraint state, and message log.

Scoring is deterministic: given the episode definition and trace, the scorer emits a boolean success flag, a score in [0,1] (fraction of 1–3 required checks passing), violations, and per-risk evidence. Each episode has 1–3 required checks spanning the four evidence channels. Two protocol refinements improve internal validity: (i) a strict-matching revision — 20 episodes originally expected "label: value" strings no model emits, so traces were re-scored against label-stripped expected values on 19 episodes (altering 39 of 600 scores, preserving within-risk model orderings; both versions released); (ii) a safety-aware refusal variant for R3/R5 where "no tool call + refusal text" counts as a safety-pass, since refusing is the safety-correct outcome there.

The subset selector performs greedy forward selection without replacement: at each step it adds the episode maximizing Ψ(S) = α·ρ + β·cov(S) + γ·hr(S) (α = β = γ = 1), where ρ is the Spearman correlation between subset-induced and full-benchmark model rankings, cov(S) is risk-type coverage, and hr(S) is high-risk model detection (a model is high-risk if its pass rate ranks in the bottom two of five). The discrimination feature is computed leave-one-model-out to avoid leakage. Six baselines are compared: Random, Stratified-by-risk, Difficulty, Disagreement (pass-rate variance), Clustering (k-medoids on full features), and Trace-Feature clustering. Stochastic baselines use 1000 bootstrap resamples with 95% percentile CIs; all random seeds and sampled indices are released.

## Results & Highlights

- **Aggregate scores hide per-risk failures (RQ1):** average task success spans 0.775–0.885 across the five models (0.110 spread), but Qwen2.5-1.5B passes only 1 of 24 cross-scope leakage episodes (4%) versus Phi-3.5-mini's 10/24 (42%) (Fisher's exact p = 0.004; p = 0.049 vs Qwen2.5-3B) — though neither survives Holm correction over all 50 pairwise model–risk tests (smallest adjusted p = 0.22). Conversely on constraint decay, Qwen2.5-1.5B obeys all 24 constraints (100%) while Qwen2.5-3B/7B violate 2 each (92%) and Phi-3.5-mini violates 3 (88%).
- **Risk-preserving 20% subset (RQ2/RQ3):** the proposed selector achieves Spearman ρ = 0.975 (deterministic, CI collapses to a point estimate), risk coverage 1.0, and high-risk detection 1.0 at 24/120 episodes. Random sampling reaches only Spearman 0.733 (95% CI [−0.05, 0.97], including negative values) with fail coverage 0.201 and high-risk detection 0.600; stratified-by-risk preserves coverage (1.0) but drops to Spearman 0.698 and high-risk detection 0.345 (missing a high-risk model in half of resamples); Trace-Feature clustering ties Spearman 0.975 but halves high-risk detection (0.500). Versus Random, the improvement is z = +9.1 (p < 0.001) on fail coverage and z = +1.6 (p = 0.05) on high-risk detection, +0.24 on Spearman.
- **Feature ablation (RQ4):** the cross-model pass-rate spread (discrimination) carries the entire ranking signal — removing it drops Spearman from 0.975 to 0.308, while removing difficulty, complexity, or failure density individually leaves it at 0.975.
- **Held-out-model check (RQ5, concept verification):** holding out Qwen2.5-1.5B and rebuilding features on the remaining four models yields a 24-episode subset preserving the reference ranking (ρ = 1.000), covering all five risks, with held-out pass rate 0.812 vs the full-benchmark 0.775 (reported as descriptive only; subset skewed 20/24 toward conflict episodes).
- **Memory baseline:** standard retrieval-level filtering (RAG and recency filtering) performs worse than no filtering on MemRiskBench, and the full run costs ~18 h versus ~3.6 h with the 20% subset (5x reduction).

## Authors & Credibility

The authors are Jianhua Jiang and Dongbo Yuan (School of Artificial Intelligence and Computer Science, Jilin University of Finance and Economics, with Jiang also affiliated with the Jilin Province Key Laboratory of Fintech) and Weihua Li (Auckland University of Technology). This is not a big-lab frontier-model team; it is a small-group, resource-constrained study — which is consistent with the deliberate choice of five locally-run quantized sub-8B models on a single 16 GB consumer GPU rather than frontier API models. Credibility positives: the paper is unusually transparent about its own weaknesses (quantization confound, Holm-corrected non-significance, strict-matching revision altering 39/600 scores, single-model held-out test), ships both original and revised scoring versions with a per-(model, risk) audit, releases all episodes, traces, scorer, and selector code, and states that no generative AI was used for the work. Related work coverage (LongMemEval, LoCoMo, MemBench, MemoryAgentBench, BEAM, RULER, MemGPT, and the concurrent Mid-Range Difficulty Filter line) is accurate and well-positioned. Grant numbers are anonymized for review, suggesting a submission under review.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuinely differentiated on two axes. Existing memory benchmarks (LongMemEval, LoCoMo, MemoryAgentBench, BEAM) measure *capability* (retrieval recall / QA accuracy), whereas MemRiskBench makes memory *violations* the unit of evaluation; and unlike ranking-only compression (the Mid-Range Difficulty Filter, Spearman ≈ 0.94 at 44–70% task reduction), its selector explicitly preserves risk-type coverage and high-risk model detection — a claim backed by the stratified-sampling failure mode (coverage 1.0 but high-risk detection 0.345). However, the benchmark's risk checks are conceptually simple (string containment, argument equality, ledger lookups), so the novelty is in the framing and evaluation protocol rather than technical depth.
- **Rigor:** Mixed but honest. Deterministic no-LLM-judge scoring, bootstrap CIs, LOO-CV, and dual scoring variants are strong practices. Yet the headline size–risk interaction (1.5B worst on leakage, Fisher p = 0.004) does *not* survive Holm correction over 50 pairwise tests (smallest adjusted p = 0.22) — the paper says so, but the abstract and intro still foreground the 4%-vs-42% contrast. The "CI collapses to a point estimate with zero bootstrap variance" for the selector is presented as a feature of determinism, but it means the selector's guarantees carry no uncertainty quantification. The selector's features are derived from the same five reference models used to evaluate preservation — an inherent circularity only partially mitigated by LOO and the single held-out model.
- **Reproducibility:** Excellent — all 120 episodes, 600 raw traces, scorer, selector, feature matrix, seeds, and both original/revised expected values are on GitHub, and models are locally runnable. This is well above the norm.
- **Significance:** Limited by the model set. Five models, all under 8B parameters, three of five from the Qwen family, and none frontier-class (no GPT-4/Claude/Gemini). Whether the taxonomy and selector conclusions transfer to the deployed frontier agents the intro motivates (cross-user leakage in production assistants) is untested. The 24-episodes-per-risk cell size gives wide uncertainty — visible in the non-surviving significance tests.
- **Red flags:** (i) the Qwen2.5-7B quantization confound — it runs Q3_K_M while all others run Q4_K_M, and the paper itself admits the stale/revoke non-monotonicities (3B 19/24 vs 7B 18/24) could be quantization artifacts; (ii) 20 of 600 scores were changed by the strict-matching revision, i.e., the original deterministic checks had a 3.3% false-negative rate from brittle "label: value" matching, which hints the very low leakage pass rates (4–42% across all models) may partly reflect matching brittleness rather than pure model failure; (iii) RQ5's 0.812-vs-0.775 comparison conflates subset composition with model performance and is skewed 20/24 toward conflict episodes; (iv) R6 (summary contamination) is documented but unscored — arguably the most interesting risk is deferred to future work.

## Strengths

- Correct problem identification: aggregate accuracy genuinely hides sparse, high-severity memory failures, and random/stratified subsampling demonstrably discards them (stratified high-risk detection 0.345).
- Fully deterministic, trace-grounded scoring with no LLM-as-judge on the pass/fail path — eliminates judge noise and makes every verdict auditable from the released traces.
- The subset selector's objective (ranking + risk coverage + high-risk detection) is the right formulation for safety-sensitive compression, and the ablation honestly shows a single discrimination feature does almost all the work.
- Exceptional transparency: dual scoring variants, the label-prefix revision audit, admitted quantization confound, and reported Holm-corrected non-significance are all disclosed rather than buried.
- Complete public release (episodes, 600 traces, scorer, selector, bootstrap scripts) on locally-runnable models — rare and commendable.

## Limitations

- Scripted tool environment: tool calls are deterministic stubs, not real browsers/email/office suites; adversarial user behavior is out of scope, so transfer to deployed agents is unproven.
- Model coverage: five models, all < 8B and locally quantized, three of five Qwen-family; no frontier API models. The 16 GB GPU constraint also forced Qwen2.5-7B to Q3_K_M, creating the acknowledged quantization confound.
- Small per-cell sample sizes (24 episodes per risk) leave key pairwise contrasts underpowered after multiple-comparison correction.
- Selector is reference-set dependent: built on the current five-model ranking; a new model family may require full recomputation, and no automated drift detector is provided.
- Held-out generalization (RQ5) uses a single smallest model and a heavily skewed subset — concept verification only.
- R6 (summary contamination) episodes exist but are excluded from main scoring, pending a hybrid deterministic–LLM pipeline.
- Safety-aware refusal scoring sensitivity is delegated to the online repository rather than quantified in the paper.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do frontier-class models (GPT-4/Claude/Gemini class) show the same per-risk profile, or is the leakage-constraint inversion an artifact of small quantized models — and does the Q3/Q4 confound disappear at Q4-only re-runs of the 7B?
- Would the selector's guarantees hold with a reference set of 20+ diverse model families, and can an offline (reference-free) discrimination estimate replace the reference-set dependence?
- How much of the very low leakage pass rate (4–42%) reflects genuinely unsafe behavior versus brittle deterministic string matching — would a semantically-matched but still-auditable checker change model rankings?
- Can R6 (summary contamination) be scored without an LLM-as-judge, e.g., via canary strings or watermark-style deterministic probes embedded in memory summaries?
- Does any published memory architecture (MemGPT-style consolidation, MemoryBank decay, explicit constraint trackers with re-injection) actually reduce the R1–R5 failure rates measured here — the paired defense study the authors defer?
- Does the 5x-compressed risk-preserving subset remain valid under distribution shift (new episode templates, longer horizons, real tool environments)?
