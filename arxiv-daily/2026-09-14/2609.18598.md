> **一句话总结：** 提出 SynAgent——一个由 GPT-5.5 驱动的多模态多智能体框架，在物理自动化实验室中自主合成 LiCoO2 (001) 薄膜：它从空技能库出发按需生成分析代码，并通过"验证-证伪"交替实验主动检验自身假设，在 18 次自主实验中将最优生长窗口锁定在 650–690 °C（670 °C 处 XRD 强度比 I(003)/I(006) 达 61.6，远高于阈值前的 <0.1 与 700–750 °C 平台区的 32–41），最终产出一份人类可读、可追溯、可检验的四温区机理认识文档。

> **TL;DR:** SynAgent replaces the black-box Bayesian-optimization decision layer of a self-driving lab with multimodal GPT-5.5 agents that write their own analysis code on demand and alternate verify/falsify experiments. On the LiCoO2 (001) sputtered thin-film task on the dLab platform (objective: XRD ratio I(003)/I(006); direct baseline: a prior BO campaign on the identical platform/grid/objective), a single 18-experiment campaign found the abrupt crystallization threshold between 630–650 °C and peaked at I(003)/I(006) = 61.6 at 670 °C — versus <0.1 for all films below 630 °C and a 32–41 plateau at 700–750 °C — while also leaving behind an explicit, testable four-region account of how substrate temperature governs crystallization, something BO does not provide.

## 核心贡献

- **SynAgent 框架**：首个（在该物理平台上）将多模态 LLM 智能体接入自动化材料合成闭环、并以"可读的机理认识"而非仅"优化好的样品"作为首要产出的框架。主智能体负责提出实验条件并解读结果，子智能体按需生成分析技能。
- **自适应技能生成（Adaptive Skill Generation）**：技能库初始为空，无任何预定义分析流程。遇到现有技能覆盖不了的新数据时，专职子智能体针对实际测量数据（MaiML 格式）迭代编写并运行分析代码，通过自测后注册入库、供全campaign 复用——使 XRD 图谱与 SEM 显微图像等异构数据都能进入决策回路。
- **验证-证伪推理方案（Verify–Falsify Scheme）**：智能体不仅测试预测会成功的条件，也刻意测试预测会失败的条件，并在每次实验前承诺具体的数值预测；预测被驳回时通过溯因推理（abduction）修订认识文档。这是针对 LLM 锚定效应与先验过信偏见的结构性纠偏。
- **显式演化的"认识文档"**：对"合成条件如何决定结果"的主张以自然语言文档形式维护、逐轮重写，每条主张都关联支持或驳斥它的实验——最终产出将温度轴划分为四个区域的、人类可追溯检验的机理图像。
- **实证验证**：在 LiCoO2 (001) 薄膜沉积（dLab 物理自动化实验室）单一 18 次实验的campaign 中，无需人工干预即发现 630–650 °C 间的突变结晶阈值，并把最优窗口锁定到 650–690 °C（最优 670 °C），同时给出了窗口之外薄膜排序降低的可检验假说。

## Key Contributions

- **SynAgent framework**: a multimodal, multi-agent LLM architecture in which a main agent plans experiments and interprets outcomes while subagents generate analysis skills, coupling LLM reasoning to a physical automated laboratory with "evolving, human-readable understanding" as the campaign's primary output rather than just optimized samples.
- **Adaptive skill generation**: the skill library starts empty. When new data require an analysis no registered skill covers, a dedicated code-generating subagent inspects the actual measurement file (and attached image), iteratively writes/runs candidate analysis code within a fixed tool-call budget, and registers the skill only after a self-test passes — eliminating the predefined-toolset bottleneck of prior laboratory agents.
- **Verify–falsify reasoning scheme**: the agent deliberately proposes conditions predicted to fail as well as predicted to succeed, committing to a concrete numerical prediction each time; refuted predictions trigger abductive revision of its claims. This counters documented LLM failure modes (anchoring on early choices, overtrust of prior knowledge) and, in this campaign, produced the two largest advances.
- **Explicit evolving understanding document**: claims about condition–outcome relationships are maintained as a natural-language `<understanding>` document, rewritten every iteration, with every judgment logged so each decision (and each claim's evidentiary basis) is traceable by human researchers.
- **Physical-lab validation**: a fully autonomous 18-experiment campaign on LiCoO2 (001) thin-film deposition (RF magnetron sputtering on the dLab platform, GPT-5.5 for all agents) that discovered an abrupt crystallization threshold bracketed to 630–650 °C and located the optimal growth window at 650–690 °C.

## Methodology

SynAgent runs a synthesis campaign as an iterative four-step loop: (1) propose synthesis conditions from the current understanding; (2) autonomous synthesis and measurement; (3) skill-based analysis of the acquired data; (4) multimodal reflection and understanding update. It is implemented on the OpenAI Agents SDK with GPT-5.5 as the model for all agents. Each iteration consists of three LLM turns (proposal, reflection, understanding rewrite) inside a single persistent on-disk session; SEM images of the three most recent iterations are replayed as vision input, while older images are replaced by the agent's own recorded textual morphology descriptions. A campaign is declared in a configuration file specifying the experiment overview, target variable, allowed condition grids, and fixed parameters.

A skill is a SKill.md metadata document plus an analyze.py module that reads a standardized MaiML measurement file and returns a structured result. A dispatcher presents registered skills' metadata to the LLM, which either selects one or requests a new one; new skills are generated by a code-execution-equipped subagent (up to three attempts, self-test required before registration), with at most one new skill per iteration and the target-computing skill pinned after first generation. The proposal mode alternates verify/falsify (starting with verify) as an interchangeable strategy; the agent commits to a hypothesis, a condition from the allowed grid, and a concrete numerical prediction. The reflection turn judges the prediction as supported or refuted (no fixed numerical tolerance — the agent itself judges whether the outcome falls in the predicted category), and refutation triggers abduction. The testbed: (001)-oriented LiCoO2 grown on Al2O3 (0001) by RF magnetron sputtering (80 W, 1.5 h, 0.50 Pa total pressure; Li1.2CoOx target) on dLab, with substrate temperature (200–750 °C, 10 °C steps) as the only controllable variable and the XRD intensity ratio I(003)/I(006) (Rigaku SmartLab XE, 2θ = 10–120°) as the crystallinity objective; SEM (JEOL JSM-IT700HR, 5 nm/pixel) provides complementary morphological evidence. The task, grid, and objective exactly match a previously reported Bayesian-optimization campaign on the same platform, isolating the decision layer as the only change.

## Results & Highlights

- **Peak performance vs. failure regime**: the highest crystallinity of the campaign was I(003)/I(006) = 61.6 at 670 °C (iteration 13), whereas all films grown at 250–630 °C showed ratios below 0.1 (essentially no cation ordering) — a >600× jump across the threshold. The high-temperature regime (700–750 °C) forms a plateau of ratios 32–41, so the discovered 650–690 °C window beats the plateau by roughly 1.5–1.9×.
- **Prediction track record**: over 18 autonomous experiments, 15 of the agent's committed predictions were judged supported and 3 refuted (iterations 1, 2, and 10) — and each of the three refutations triggered a major revision of the understanding document.
- **Falsify proposals made the decisive discoveries**: iteration 1's verify proposal tested the prior expectation that 600 °C was near-optimal and failed catastrophically (measured ratio 0.001). Iteration 2's falsify proposal probed 700 °C as a predicted failure but measured 35.6 (well-ordered), overturning the revised picture. Iteration 10's falsify proposal at 650 °C predicted 0.2 but measured 54.9 — then the best result — narrowing the crystallization transition to 630–650 °C (a 20 °C interval). Refinement within the window: 660 °C → 60.2, 670 °C → 61.6, 680 °C → 55.0, 690 °C → 58.2; 750 °C → 40.9.
- **Skill generation quality**: the agent generated exactly two analysis skills at the first iteration (lco_peak_ratio_xrd for the XRD objective; lco_bright_particles_sem quantifying bright-particle area fraction, count, and equivalent diameter) and reused both unchanged for the remaining 17 experiments; the descriptor values agreed with manual analysis of the same data. The SEM skill separated regimes invisible to the scalar objective: bright-particle area fraction ~0.1% (featureless films, 250–320 °C) vs. 4–6% (particle-covered films, 400–630 °C).
- **Versus the BO baseline**: on the identical task, temperature grid, and objective previously run with Bayesian optimization on the same platform (ref. 14), SynAgent likewise arrived at the optimal condition — but additionally produced a four-region map (no-ordering <630 °C; transition 630–650 °C; optimal ordered window 650–690 °C; robust-but-suboptimal ordered 700–750 °C), with each claim tied to the experiments supporting or refuting it.

## Authors & Credibility

The work comes from the University of Tokyo (Institute of Industrial Science and Department of Chemistry) with co-authors from Rigaku Corporation (the XRD instrument vendor) and MITSUI KNOWLEDGE INDUSTRY — an academia–industry team around Taro Hitosugi's and Teruyasu Mizoguchi's groups, who built the dLab digital-laboratory platform (Digital Discovery, 2025) on which the campaign runs; the first author previously published on generative AI agents for inorganic materials design (Cell Reports Physical Science, 2025). The demonstration is on real physical experiments (18 autonomously executed syntheses with XRD + SEM characterization), not simulation, and the paper candidly declares generative-AI assistance (Claude Code, ChatGPT) in software development and manuscript preparation. Funding: JST ACT-X/BOOST/CREST, MEXT, JSPS KAKENHI. Data and code are available "on request to the corresponding author" rather than publicly released.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: incremental but genuine. LLM agents have already executed physical chemistry workflows (Coscientist, ORGANA) and most recently closed-loop solid-state synthesis campaigns; SynAgent's real differentiators are (a) the empty-start skill library with self-tested code generation and (b) the explicit verify–falsify schedule with committed numerical predictions and an auditable understanding document. The framing "understanding as the primary output" is the paper's most distinctive and well-defended claim.
- **Rigor**: the headline comparison with Bayesian optimization is correlational, not controlled — the authors themselves note that a controlled BO-vs-SynAgent comparison "would require re-running both decision layers under matched budgets, which the sequential nature of the physical experiments precluded." So "likewise arrived at the optimal condition" is a single-arm claim anchored to a historical campaign, with no sample-efficiency delta actually reported.
- **Self-graded homework**: the supported/refuted verdicts are rendered by the LLM itself with "no numerical tolerance imposed" between predicted and measured values. The 15/18 supported rate — a key selling point — therefore rests on the agent's own judgment of its own predictions; no independent scoring of the verdicts is provided.
- **Single-shot data, no error bars**: each temperature was visited exactly once, and the authors concede that the differences among the top ratios in the 650–690 °C window (61.6 vs. 60.2 vs. 58.2) "may lie within run-to-run variation" — meaning the precise "optimum at 670 °C" claim is statistically under-supported even as the window itself is well bracketed.
- **Reproducibility**: weak. All agents run on proprietary GPT-5.5; prompts are described but not released; skills are validated only by their own self-tests; data and code are available only on request. A third party cannot currently reproduce the campaign.
- **Red flags**: none severe — limitations are disclosed unusually honestly. Minor: the verify/falsify alternation is a fixed hand-designed schedule, so "hypothesis-driven autonomy" partly rests on a scripted meta-strategy rather than a learned one; and the Rigaku co-authorship represents a mild (disclosed) commercial interest in the platform's instrument ecosystem.

## Strengths

- Reframes the goal of self-driving labs from sample optimization to testable, human-readable process understanding — and operationalizes it with a concrete artifact (the evolving `<understanding>` document tied to evidence).
- Adaptive skill generation is demonstrated end-to-end on real data: two skills written, validated, and reused unchanged for 17 subsequent experiments, with descriptor values matching manual analysis.
- The verify–falsify scheme is a principled, mechanism-level answer to documented LLM biases (anchoring, prior overtrust), and the paper shows concretely that both major discoveries (ordered growth at 700 °C; the superior 650 °C window) came from falsify proposals whose predicted failures did not materialize.
- Multimodality is load-bearing, not decorative: SEM-derived descriptors separated film regimes that the scalar XRD objective could not distinguish (0.1% vs. 4–6% bright-particle coverage below the threshold).
- Fully physical validation with a clean experimental design — identical task, grid, and objective as the prior BO campaign isolates the decision layer as the only variable changed.

## Limitations

- Single controllable variable (substrate temperature) and a single materials system (LiCoO2); scalability to higher-dimensional condition spaces — where compact testable understanding would matter most — is untested.
- Fixed verify/falsify alternation; adaptive mode switching (e.g., based on understanding maturity) is left as future work.
- No repeated visits to any condition, so run-to-run variance is unquantified and the fine ranking within the 650–690 °C window is not statistically established.
- No controlled, budget-matched head-to-head against Bayesian optimization or other agent baselines.
- Generated-skill correctness is verified only by self-tests, and all supported/refuted judgments and abductive explanations rest on the LLM's own reasoning; the authors propose human-in-the-loop or agent-in-the-loop review as a needed complement.
- The mechanistic hypotheses (e.g., what the bright particles or faceted features actually are) could not be compositionally confirmed from SEM alone — the "understanding" is structural/phenomenological rather than chemically verified.

## Relevance

该论文展示了 LLM 智能体作为物理实验闭环决策层的完整落地：按需生成分析工具（技能库）+ 验证-证伪式假设检验来对抗 LLM 的确认偏误，这对任何关心"LLM agent 自主科学发现 / 工具使用 / 多模态实验推理"的方向都是可直接借鉴的架构参考，尤其是其"以可检验的认识文档而非标量最优值作为产出"的范式转换。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How would SynAgent fare against Bayesian optimization under matched experimental budgets in a controlled paired design, especially in higher-dimensional condition spaces where BO's sample efficiency is hardest to beat?
- Can the verify/falsify schedule be learned (e.g., choosing falsify where expected information gain is maximal) rather than fixed alternation, and does that measurably improve discovery speed?
- How sensitive are the supported/refuted verdicts to the underlying model — would replacing GPT-5.5 with an open-weights model of similar capability reproduce the campaign, and can the verdicts be scored objectively rather than self-judged?
- Do the SEM-observed bright particles and faceted features below/above the threshold have distinct compositions (secondary phases vs. LiCoO2 crystallites), as the agent's hypotheses speculate — testable with, e.g., EDS or TEM?
- Can the understanding document and the skill library transfer across campaigns on the same platform (the paper envisions "carrying the understanding gained in one campaign into the next"), and does transfer accelerate subsequent campaigns?
