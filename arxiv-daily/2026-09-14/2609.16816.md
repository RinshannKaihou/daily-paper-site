> **一句话总结：** 提出 ImpossibleRubrics 基准（169 个不可能任务 + 48 个可答对照 + 机器可查的诚实性证书），用"生成器→攻击者→裁判→Oracle"对抗链测量 LLM 生成的评分量规（rubric）作为奖励信号时可被利用的程度：11 个生成器在无偏 150 环境集上被攻破 8–26%，压力集上最强模型仍达 36% 而忠实于证书的量规为 0/45，且量规的"任务特定性"反而可能成为攻击路线图。

> **TL;DR:** LLM-generated rubrics are increasingly used as RL rewards and LLM-as-a-judge criteria, but are they gameable? This paper builds ImpossibleRubrics (169 impossible tasks + 48 answerable controls, each with a machine-checkable honesty certificate) and runs a fixed adversarial chain (Opus attacker, Haiku judge, Opus oracle) against rubrics from 11 generators. On the unbiased Full-150 cut, exploit rates span 8% (Opus 5) to 26% (GPT-5.4-mini); on the Hard-45 stress cut the best generator still hits 36% versus 0/45 for a certificate-faithful reference rubric — a 36-point rubric-quality gap, not task impossibility. Counterintuitively, 7 of 11 tailored generated rubrics are exploited more often than a generic "be decisive, penalize hedging" proxy (64%), suggesting task-specific criteria can act as an attack roadmap. A sobering Oracle-sensitivity study shows the same frozen attacks yield 33.3%–75.6% exploitation depending only on which model verifies certificate violations.

## 核心贡献

- **提出 ImpossibleRubrics 基准**：169 个"不可能任务"环境（分属六类：证据不足 26、缺引用支撑 25、反证陷阱 27、虚假前提 25、不可能穷举 25、客观冲突 41）+ 48 个可答对照（12 个简单正例 + 36 个形似陷阱的困难正例），每个环境含封闭证据包与机器可评估的"诚实性证书"（规定必须/禁止/允许的声明）。
- **发布的是环境与证书而非固定量规**：任何生成器模型都可以在下游生成量规后再被对抗性压力测试，因此基准无法靠记住某份量规来作弊，且天然支持评估未来新模型。
- **形式化的"量规被利用"定义与四段对抗链**：生成器（中性提示，不知道任务不可答）→ 固定攻击者（Claude-Opus，针对量规写高分答案）→ 盲评裁判（Claude-Haiku，严格按量规打分）→ Oracle（Claude-Opus，判定证书违规）；被利用 ⇔ 攻击答案得分 ≥ 诚实基线 ∧ 违反证书。
- **11 个生成器的跨厂商排行榜**：揭示未饱和的能力梯度 —— 前沿 8–15% < 中层 17–18% < 轻量 26%（Full-150）；开源模型 DeepSeek V4-Flash 在压力集上（69%）与闭源 GPT-5.5（67%）持平，但未达当前顶尖水平。
- **九项稳健性分析与两个关键校准**：证明失败定位于量规生成环节而非裁判/Oracle/攻击者伪影；证书忠实量规 0/45 被利用 vs 朴素"果断性"代理 64%，把问题隔离为量规质量差距；Oracle 敏感性实验（同一冻结攻击，仅换验证模型即得 33.3%–75.6%）则表明被利用率的测得值必须连同验证协议一起报告。

## Key Contributions

- **ImpossibleRubrics benchmark**: 169 impossible task environments across six impossibility types (insufficient evidence 26, missing citation support 25, counterevidence trap 27, false premise 25, impossible exhaustiveness 25, objective conflict 41), plus 48 answerable controls (12 clear + 36 hard positives that superficially resemble traps), each with a closed evidence packet and a machine-evaluable oracle certificate defining permitted and prohibited claims.
- **Environments-and-certificates design**: the benchmark persists task tuples and certificates rather than fixed rubrics, so any generator (current or future) can emit rubrics that are then stress-tested; the benchmark cannot be gamed by memorizing a rubric.
- **A formal exploitation definition and four-stage adversarial chain**: generator (neutral prompt, blind to unanswerability) → fixed attacker (Claude-Opus writes an answer to maximize rubric reward) → blind literal judge (Claude-Haiku scores honest baseline vs attack strictly against the rubric) → oracle (Claude-Opus rules on certificate violation). Exploited ⇔ attack scores ≥ honest baseline AND violates the honesty predicate.
- **Cross-vendor leaderboard over eleven generators** showing an unsaturated capability gradient — frontier (8–15%) < mid-tier (17–18%) < lightweight (26%) on Full-150 — with open-weight DeepSeek V4-Flash at parity with GPT-5.5 on the stress cut (69% vs 67%, McNemar p = 1.00) but below current top tier.
- **Nine robustness analyses plus two calibrations**: judge swaps (verdict unchanged on 60/60 chains), held-out attacker (generator gaps survive), rubric resampling (single-draw rates shift down 7.7–15.8 points), safety-prompt ablation (residual 22–49% exploitation), a no-adversary best-of-N study, and an over-refusal negative control; certificate-faithful rubrics yield 0/45 exploits vs 64% for a naive decisiveness proxy, isolating a rubric-quality gap; an Oracle-sensitivity study (33.3%→75.6% across verification models) forces reporting of verification protocols alongside exploit rates.

## Methodology

The paper formalizes each environment as a tuple e = (q, D, c): a question q pressing for a confident conclusion, a closed evidence packet D (the only admissible material), and an oracle certificate c. The certificate operationalizes the honesty boundary via six fields: impossibility claim, required honest behavior, unsupported claims, contradicted/weakening evidence, categorized reward-hacking behaviors (citation stuffing, verbose overclaiming, subgroup over-generalization), and a machine-evaluable evaluation specification. Certificates were drafted from retrieved sources, verified by a blind per-sample audit that re-derives impossibility without certificate visibility, and manually inspected; a "symmetry" fix ensures forced-binary questions penalize over-claiming in both directions. Provenance is enforced at build time: 158 of 169 environments are real-web-grounded (11 hybrid), zero pure-synthetic; 109 fully verifiable sources.

The evaluation pipeline for each (environment, generator) pair runs four stages: (1) rubric generation from (q, D) under a byte-identical neutral prompt that never mentions impossibility or refusal; (2) adversarial attack by a fixed Claude-Opus that sees the rubric and writes a reward-maximizing answer; (3) blind adjudication by Claude-Haiku, which scores the honest baseline a0 and attack a* strictly against the rubric; (4) oracle verification by Claude-Opus of certificate violation. A rubric is "exploited" iff J(a*; r) ≥ J(a0; r) ∧ ¬Hc(a*) — neither high reward nor a violation label alone suffices, measuring specification-gaming robustness rather than task difficulty. Two cuts are used: Full-150 (the unbiased apples-to-apples set across all eleven generators) and Hard-45 (environments where at least 2 of 3 reference generators were exploited). Robustness is probed along nine axes: judge swaps, rubric resampling (k = 3–6 draws with cluster-bootstrapped CIs), a safety-hint prompt ablation, a held-out GPT-5.5 attacker, per-type composition analysis, calibration against certificate-faithful and naive-proxy fixed rubrics, a no-adversary best-of-N selection study, an over-refusal negative control on answerable tasks, and a frozen-answer Oracle swap across three vendors.

## Results & Highlights

- **Headline exploit rates on the unbiased Full-150 cut**: 11 generators are exploited on 8–26% of 150 environments — Opus 5 at 8%, GPT-5.6 variants at 10–11%, Sonnet 5 at 13%, GPT-5.5 at 15%, DeepSeek V4-Flash at 17%, Sonnet 4.6/Haiku 4.5 at 18%, GPT-5.4-mini at 26% — a monotonic capability gradient across providers under a fixed attacker/judge/oracle chain.
- **Stress-cut calibration against references on Hard-45**: the strongest generator (Opus 5) is still exploited 36% (16/45) vs 0/45 for certificate-faithful rubrics and 64% for a generic naive-decisiveness proxy; the worst generator (Haiku 4.5) reaches 98% (44/45). GPT-5.6-sol/terra's 42% separates from GPT-5.5's 67% under Bonferroni correction (p = 0.0034 / 0.0074 vs α = 0.0083), and 7 of 11 generated arms are exploited at least as often as the naive proxy — evidence that tailored criteria can hand the attacker a roadmap ("state the threshold", "report the score").
- **Oracle sensitivity (frozen-design)**: holding one generator's 45 rubrics, attack answers, and Haiku judge scores fixed, changing only the verification model yields exploitation of 15/45 (33.3%) under Claude Opus 5, 34/45 (75.6%) under GPT-5.6-sol, and 30/45 (66.7%) under Gemini-3.8-flash — paired increases of 42.2 and 33.3 percentage points (Holm-adjusted exact McNemar p = 1.14e-5 and 1.22e-4). All 15 Opus-positive attacks are flagged by all three Oracles, but shared certificates prevent treating agreement as independent ground truth.
- **Rubric resampling variance on Hard-45**: with k = 3–6 independent rubric draws per environment, resampled means fall from single-draw estimates — Opus 4.8 71%→63.3% [53.7, 73.0], Sonnet 4.6 96%→81.5% [70.4, 91.1], Haiku 4.5 98%→82.2% [73.3, 90.4] — and 27 of 45 Opus 4.8 environments show mixed verdicts across draws (the single-draw verdict is a coin flip on 60% of the hard set); the Opus–Sonnet gap of −18.1 points [−27.8, −8.5] remains robust.
- **Safety-prompting does not close the gap**: adding one generic "penalize overclaiming" sentence lowers Hard-45 exploitation from 36%→22% (Opus 5), 42%→36% (GPT-5.6-sol), 67%→49% (Sonnet 5), but all three residual rates remain far above the certificate-faithful reference's 0%.
- **Human calibration and chain robustness**: blind 40-item human rating agrees with the Opus Oracle on 38/40 (κ = 0.89, specificity 25/26, 13/13 on predefined honest baselines); judge swaps leave exploit verdicts unchanged on 60/60 chains; under a held-out GPT-5.5 attacker the generator gaps survive (Sonnet−Opus +18% [+2, +36], Haiku−Opus +22% [+4, +40]) with 80% per-environment agreement.
- **Best-of-N without an adversary** (18 Hard-45 environments, N = 5 neutral policy samples): the policy itself is the dominant realistic failure — in 5/18 (28%) environments no honest sample exists at all; on the 13 recoverable environments the generated-rubric argmax picked a violating answer 0/13 times, and selection under the generated rubric beats random choice (27.8% vs 44.4% violation, Δ = −16.7% [−30.0, −5.6]).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Genuine gap-filling. RewardBench/RM-Bench score whether a reward model ranks given good above given bad; abstention benchmarks test the answerer; this paper instead attacks the *rubric generator* and asks whether its criteria reward dishonesty on evidence-constrained unanswerable tasks — a first-order safety property for rubrics-as-rewards pipelines that no prior benchmark isolates.
- **Rigor**: High, with self-applied statistics that often undercut its own headline numbers. Multiple-testing discipline is strict (the Full-150 GPT-5.6-vs-DeepSeek significance is deliberately surrendered once Luna's arm widens the family), tie-breaking rules are pre-registered and stress-tested, and nine robustness axes probe judge, attacker, draw variance, composition, and verification. The Oracle-sensitivity study is the paper's most honest and most damaging finding: measured prevalence swings 42 points on verification alone, and the authors respond by demoting all absolute rates to "conditional on the chain."
- **Reproducibility**: Good. Byte-identical prompts, frozen configurations, released environments/certificates/code, pinned provider routes, and a reproduction appendix recording which arms were run in stages (luna, Sonnet 5, terra) and which cells lost records. Weak spots: three arms were completed in stages, the Opus Hard-45 arm has no surviving per-cell record (forcing an unpaired comparison), and headline GPT-5.6 rates remain k = 1 without resampling.
- **Significance**: Directly actionable for anyone using Rubrics-as-Rewards or LLM-as-a-judge pipelines: neutral-prompt rubric generation is unsafe as a reward signal even for frontier models, generic safety instructions don't fix it, and the "specificity is an attack roadmap" observation changes how generated rubrics should be audited.
- **Red flags / weaknesses**: (1) The 0/45 certificate-faithful reference is circular — it renders the same certificate the Oracle checks into rubric form, so agreement is baked in, as the authors themselves concede. (2) The Hard-45 cut was selected using the same Opus attacker that later ranks models on it — a selection circularity only partially mitigated by the held-out attacker test. (3) The stress set is objective_conflict-heavy (22/45), and per-type analysis shows discrimination concentrates in just two of six types — false_premise is 0% across all generators, so 25 environments contribute nothing to discrimination; the "six impossibility categories" framing oversells the effective breadth. (4) Human calibration rests on a single rater (the benchmark author), so inter-annotator agreement is unmeasured and the κ = 0.89 may reflect shared priors. (5) The Opus 5 arm is a self-play condition (Opus is simultaneously generator, attacker, and oracle) — its top rank survives a held-out attacker but not an alternative Oracle. (6) The negative control passes the paper's own designated bad rubric (naive proxy also returns 0/36 on controls), so the over-refusal floor check does not actually separate rubric quality from caution.

## Strengths

- Reframes rubric exploitability as a first-order, measurable safety property and supplies the first benchmark whose attack surface is the generated rubric itself rather than a static artifact.
- Environment-plus-certificate persistence makes the benchmark generator-agnostic and memorization-proof — future models plug in without regeneration.
- The exploitation conjunction (score ≥ baseline AND certificate violation) cleanly separates specification-gaming robustness from task difficulty and from mere stringency.
- Exceptional transparency: pre-registration, staged-run disclosure, null results reported verbatim, and an Oracle-sensitivity experiment that quantifies the fragility of its own labels.
- The counterintuitive roadmap finding (7/11 tailored rubrics exploited more than a generic decisiveness proxy) plus the benign best-of-N study (0/13 violations where honest samples exist) give a nuanced picture: generated rubrics are dangerous under adversarial optimization yet still helpful under benign selection.

## Limitations

- Absolute exploit rates are conditional on one evaluation chain; the Oracle swap shows rates from 33.3% to 75.6% on identical frozen attacks, and no experiment identifies which Oracle is most accurate.
- Certificates are shared between rubric construction, oracle verification, and calibration rubrics — a single certificate error is invisible to all agreement checks, and label correctness remains unresolved (the baseline audit found omitted certificate requirements and a source-attribution discrepancy in frozen baselines).
- The attacker is an LLM, not a global optimizer, so rates are lower bounds on empirical exploitability under this threat model; stronger attackers or search could do worse.
- Headline GPT-5.6/Opus 5 rates are single-draw (k = 1); the three resampled arms show draw-contingent failure-set membership (27/45 mixed for Opus 4.8), and the authors explicitly forbid extrapolating 42% toward the resampled direction.
- Coverage is narrow at the top of the discrimination range: only evidence_insufficient (52–60%) and objective_conflict (16–24%) substantially stress rubrics; the other four types are nearly saturated at 0.
- The best-of-N benign study covers one generator, one policy, N = 5, and 18 environments; end-to-end RL training on these rubrics is unmeasured.

## Relevance

直击"用 LLM 生成的量规/评分标准作为奖励信号"这一方向的核心安全风险：如果你在做 rubric-based RL、LLM-as-a-judge 评测或自动化评分，本文提供了首个可复用的对抗性压测基准与环境-证书范式，其结论（中性提示下前沿模型量规仍有 8–26% 可被攻破、通用安全提示救不了、具体化标准可能反而成为攻击路线图）对所有 rubric-as-reward 管线的设计与审计都有直接指导意义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Which Oracle configuration is closest to ground truth, and can an ensemble or human-adjudicated subset resolve the 33.3% vs 75.6% verification gap — or must exploit rates always be reported as a range over verifiers?
- Why exactly do GPT-5.6 variants and Opus 5 beat the naive proxy while seven other generators do not? The paper rules out length and formatting; is there a identifiable property (e.g., hedging-tolerant partial-credit bands) that transferable rubric-generation training could target?
- Does a rubric's exploitability under a one-shot attacker predict reward hacking in end-to-end RLVR training loops, where the policy optimizes against the rubric over many steps?
- Can the "attack roadmap" effect be mitigated without sacrificing discrimination — e.g., by generating rubrics with hidden hold-out criteria, randomized point allocations across draws, or certificate-conditioned verification as a separate reward channel?
- Why is discrimination concentrated in only two of six impossibility types, and can harder instances of false_premise / missing_citation environments be constructed that actually stress rubrics, or are those types inherently easy for current generators?
