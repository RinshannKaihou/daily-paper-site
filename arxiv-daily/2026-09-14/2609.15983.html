Why this matters

A concrete architecture for long-horizon LLM agents: decide when to commit, what to repair, what to salvage — with real, published theorems as evidence the loop works.

Critical edge

✕ The headline 71.0% is a two-model selection scheme — the fair single-harness score is 54.0%, which loses to direct GPT-5.6 Pro at 68.0% — and compute costs are never reported.

01 Methodology — the five-stage loop

  1. S1Strategy explorationDevelop and attack multiple candidate routes before committing to any proof sketch.
  2. S2Readiness gateIs the route concrete enough to decompose? Unclear strategies loop back to exploration.
  3. S3Proof decompositionEmit a sectioned LaTeX skeleton plus a dependency DAG; the DAG, not document order, drives execution.
  4. S4Parallel subproblemsIndependent sections solved concurrently; failures retry locally without restarting unaffected work.
  5. S5Global verificationMultiple independent reviews, tree-aggregated; one fatal defect rejects — then targeted revision or re-exploration.

Within every hard stage: a population of schema-typed candidates is generated, attacked by dedicated falsifiers (counterexamples, circularity, silently strengthened hypotheses), then merged by overlapping random-sample tree aggregation — each node synthesizes k candidate-plus-critique bundles constructively, carrying unresolved objections forward instead of voting them away.

Across rounds: the rejected draft with verifier feedback grounds the next attempt, while a knowledge directory accumulates theorems, failed paths with precise failure points, references, and observations. Transfer to Codeforces keeps the whole architecture and adds only an execution probe.

  • Complementary errors: selection covers 213/300 — +48 problems over the better single run (165).
  • Critique signal quality: the 8-critique rule separates correct/incorrect proofs at AUC 0.896; routing on the model’s internal verifier alone drops to 64.7%.
  • Codeforces (222 problems, difficulty >1500): 218/222 solved with the execution probe (rating 4263) vs 213 without (3918).

  • Long-horizon proofs: 46- and 75-page drafts for Knuth’s cycles (even case); 22-page draft independently rediscovering the architecture of the Erdős unit-distance disproof — internet disabled, 15 exploration rounds.

03 Strengths

  • Attacks the real failure mode of long-horizon research — decision structure (when to commit, repair, salvage), not just per-step accuracy.
  • Critique-preserving aggregation is a principled departure from answer voting: shared agreements can hide shared errors.
  • Tested on the hardest setting available — open FOCS/JMLR-level problems with verifiable companion papers; the coreset gain changes an exponent, not a constant.
  • The offline Erdős rediscovery is a compelling existence proof of sustained autonomous search.
  • Honest framing throughout (falsification ≠ verification; oracle kept separate), plus genuine transfer to Codeforces with one added probe.

04 Limitations

  • No formal proof checking — correctness rests on LLM critics and unquantified human co-author effort.
  • Compute and cost unreported: benchmark gains confound orchestration with substantially more inference.
  • No component ablations — readiness gate, knowledge directory, falsifier strength, and tree overlap are never individually disabled.
  • Single-harness 54.0% sits below direct GPT-5.6 Pro (68.0%): the harness is not a substitute for a stronger base model.
  • Partly self-supplied evaluation: TCS-Bench was co-authored by this team; grader validated on only 100 labeled proofs.
  • Harness and prompts not open-sourced; base models proprietary — independent replication hard.

05 Questions for further exploration

  • Does the gain survive a compute-matched comparison against plain best-of-n or self-consistency — and what is the real token multiplier?
  • Would an optional Lean formalization stage catch defects the LLM global verifier misses, at what overhead?
  • What drives selection: model diversity, the 8-critique signal (AUC 0.896), or the threshold rule — and can that signal be distilled into the base model?
  • Is there an inference-scaling law for the readiness-gate loop — performance vs. rounds and tree width on open problems?
  • Can recorded research trajectories serve as post-training data, and does branching-structure credit assignment measurably help?
  • The Erdős convergence: a bottleneck in problem space, or a bias of current frontier models — would a third independent run replicate it?
