Key metric — the INT2 cliff
1.3%–65.9%

NDCG@10 retention (vs FP32) at INT2/g16 across five checkpoints whose weight reconstruction error is clustered at 0.315–0.337 — near-identical proxy signal in, wildly different damage out.

Why this matters

If you serve quantized embedders on the edge or in agent pipelines: INT4/g16 is safe; INT2 is a family-dependent lottery — measure per family before deploying.

Critical edge

No mixed-precision allocator (GPTQ/AWQ/OmniQuant-style) is actually implemented or compared — the paper measures the premise, not the methods.

Refutation targets uniform min–max PTQ only · BGE-M3 verdict is monolingual-only
Methodology

M1How the map was measured

Five retrieval checkpoints from four families — Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B (causal decoder, last-token pooling), its SkillRet fine-tune (127,190 pairs, kept as a paired control), EmbeddingGemma-300M, BGE-M3, E5-base-v2 — evaluated on SciFact and NFCorpus (BEIR) plus SkillRet (4,392 queries / 6,006 docs, Hub revision pinned). Quantization is group-wise affine min–max weight-only PTQ (GGUF-style, FP16 scales; symmetric absmax arm as control); activations stay FP32, so every number is fake-quantized and no latency is claimed. The grid spans W8/W4/W3/W2 × g16/g32/g128 plus module-isolation arms (embedding-only, attention-only, FFN-only). Per-query scores are retained throughout; differences carry paired-bootstrap 95% intervals with Benjamini–Hochberg correction. Ranking stability is measured separately (top-10 overlap, rank-10 flips, eviction composition vs qrels). Three causal interventions probe the cliff: fine-tune pairing, embedding-only INT2, forced pooling swap.

Credibility: single-author, but unusually rigorous — an audit section retracts its own symmetric/ternary arms after a quantizer defect and re-measures after a prompt-prefix bug (8.84 NDCG points on one checkpoint). Per-query scores and a byte-verified size ledger released.
Results

R1What the grid shows

  • INT4/g16 is NDCG-neutral for every model (98.7–100.4% retention; largest isolated-module cost 1.01 pts) — but widening to g128 already hurts: E5-base-v2 drops to 95.0%.
  • INT3 module orderings invert between families: FFN costliest for the Qwen lineage, attention costliest for BGE-M3/E5 (2.46 vs 0.01 pts at g16; 5.09 vs 1.14 at g32 — the largest single-module effect in the grid); module costs stop being additive.
  • Controls: embedding-only INT2 retains 97.6–100.5% everywhere; fine-tuning moves tolerance ≤ 1.0 pt; forced mean pooling does not rescue collapse (1.6% → 9.5%).
  • Distil-small beats squeeze-large, in domain: a 109M student at INT3 (68.4 MB) scores 78.04 NDCG@10 vs the teacher’s FP32 78.48 (1191.6 MB) — but collapses out of domain (7.79 on NFCorpus).

Strengths

S1What it does unusually well

  • Exhaustive, pre-registered-feeling design: full bit-width × group-size grid, module isolation, per-query scores retained and re-analyzable.
  • Statistical care rare in compression papers: paired bootstrap intervals, BH-corrected p-values, explicit magnitude-vs-significance distinction.
  • Causal controls instead of correlations — and the discipline to report the INT2 cliff as “unexplained” rather than story-tell it.
  • qrels-counted eviction analysis is directly useful for deciding whether INT4 is “free” for a RAG pipeline.
  • Exemplary transparency: an audit section listing its own errors, retractions, and which numbers moved.
Limitations

L1Read it with these caveats

  • Three corpora only — no MTEB, no multilingual eval despite a multilingual checkpoint (BGE-M3’s collapse verdict is monolingual-only).
  • Fake quantization, FP32 activations: storage savings do not imply CPU speedup; no end-to-end deployed accuracy.
  • No allocator implemented (GPTQ/AWQ/OmniQuant) — the premise is measured, not the methods; ternary arms retracted and never re-measured.
  • Tokenizer behavior untested → the INT2 cliff mechanism stays open; correlation claims rest on n = 12–15 module arms with unreported CIs.
Open questions

Q1Where to probe next

  1. Do the module orderings and the INT2 cliff persist under stronger quantizers (GPTQ, AWQ, OmniQuant), or is the failure specific to min–max affine PTQ?
  2. What mechanistically separates INT2 survivors from collapsers — and is it predictable from the FP32 checkpoint without quantizing?
  3. Does “NDCG-neutral but 13–22% top-10 churn” matter downstream: RAG answer quality, agent skill selection, LLM rerankers?
  4. Does family-dependence hold on full MTEB and multilingual retrieval (especially BGE-M3)?
  5. Can a ranking-aware sensitivity signal beat reconstruction error on the module axis where the cheap proxy fails (r ≤ 0.42)?
