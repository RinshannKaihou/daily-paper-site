03 Critical edge

The load-bearing claim — that three families exhaust every loss of transactional control — is asserted, not proven: the catalog is a conjecture, not a theorem.

Compounding this: nearly half the reachable remote registry went unmeasured (4,318 of 9,234 targets never responded), and the runtime comparison reads preprint self-descriptions, not experiments.

04 The catalog

Eight anomalies, three families A1–A8 外部效果异常

Enumerated by construction in the style of ANSI isolation anomalies: each family’s size has a structural reason; each anomaly is a forbidden effect-history fragment with a named boundary capability to exclude it.

I · Uncertainty the three safety-relevant reactions to an unresolved outcome
A1
Duplicated
Unknown outcome → re-issue → effect applied twice (invoice paid twice).
A2
Missing
Committing under an unresolved outcome loses an effect that in fact occurred.
A3
Orphaned
Compensating under an unresolved outcome fires with nothing to neutralize.
II · Workflow structure survival · timing · dependency of an externalized effect
A4
Residue
Abort leaves an externalized effect uncompensated — it survives the workflow.
A5
Premature
Effect externalized before its resolution point — early release that may need retraction.
A6
Contaminated
Speculation leaks into dependencies — the effect-world “dirty read”.
III · Interaction the two kinds of external participant
A7
Conflicting
Concurrent executions externalize conflicting effects on shared resources.
A8
Phantom
An exogenous observer reacts; the observed consequence survives compensation.
05 Methodology

Model → contracts → measurement

Three methodologically distinct stages.

1 · Formal model. A two-level slice from multilevel transaction management: L0 atomic tool calls (charge(), book_flight()) composed into L1 workflows (BookTrip). Histories record external events and runtime observations strictly separately — unknown is a state of the runtime, not of the world. Five orthogonal operation dimensions (idempotence, invertibility, externalization timing, determinism, commutativity) that do not collapse onto one reversible/irreversible scale.

2 · Contract analysis. Required capabilities organized into three contract families — authoritative convergence, lifecycle control, coordination/visibility — yielding four black-box guarantee boundaries (panel 07).

3 · Measurement. Census of the four spec-defined annotations at the wire level; explicit false kept distinct from omitted fields; values never attributed to author intent.

07 Four guarantee boundaries

Where black-box invocation cannot suffice

Stated as consequences of the definitions — each mapped to a classical distributed-computing result. The missing layer is reusable transactional contracts at the tool interface, not another recovery mechanism.

B1

Exactly-once barrier

No exactly-once over an unreliable channel without an authoritative outcome primitive under ambiguous results.

B2

Conflict repair

General repair between non-commuting irreversible effects is impossible without a mediator.

B3

Compensation’s reach ends

Once an outside actor has reacted (A8), the externally observed consequence cannot be retracted.

B4

Atomic multi-tool release

The classical atomic-commitment problem — requires tool-side prepare/commit, not orchestration.

08 Strengths

What holds up

  • One clean effect-history frame unifies failures usually treated ad hoc — duplicate charges, orphaned refunds, leaked offer letters, publish races — with world events strictly separated from runtime observations.
  • The catalog is constructive: every anomaly = a concrete forbidden trace + a named capability + a representative realization (idempotency key, status endpoint, dry-run quote, expiring hold, commutativity declaration, mediator, visibility control).
  • Connects agent reliability to classical impossibilities (exactly-once, atomic commit) instead of wishing them away.
  • Census is large, transparent, conservative in its claims, and backed by an open artifact.
  • Honest scoping: planning errors, read-side anomalies, security/policy, and liveness are explicitly out of model.
09 Limitations

What to weigh

  • No formal proofs: catalog completeness, minimality, independence and the four boundaries are argued informally — “conjecture” is the authors’ own word for the family-exhaustiveness claim.
  • Census scope: stdio/package servers excluded by construction; ~47% of remote targets unreachable; emission rates possibly dominated by SDK defaults; declared metadata only.
  • The runtime coverage table reads preprint self-descriptions, not experiments — several compared systems are themselves unreviewed 2026 preprints.
  • No implementation of the contract layer, no protocol synthesis, no cost model for when speculation is worth its residue.
  • Read-side anomalies, security/policy, and liveness deliberately excluded — look elsewhere for those failure classes.
10 Questions for further exploration

Open threads

Q1Can the family-exhaustiveness conjecture actually be discharged — e.g., by mechanical enumeration over the Section 2 vocabulary, in the style of the verified detectors for shared-state anomalies (arXiv:2606.17182)?
Q2What do the 9,454 excluded stdio servers and 4,318 unreachable targets annotate? Would including them shift the 39.9% signature concentration or the 3.1% destructive rate materially?
Q3How much of the annotation surface is SDK-default noise? Cloning server sources (à la arXiv:2607.10123) could separate deliberate declaration from template inheritance.
Q4Could a minimal contract — idempotency key + status endpoint + declared compensation — be retrofitted to even 100 high-traffic MCP tools, and would a consuming runtime measurably reduce A1–A4 incidents vs. retry-only baselines?
Q5The paper prices nothing: can a cost model decide when premature externalization (A5) is rational — e.g., sending an offer early because negotiation latency dominates retraction plus phantom-consequence (A8) costs?
Q6How do the anomalies interact with nondeterministic, LLM-backed tools whose same logical operation has non-stationary semantics — does determinism need its own contract family?
