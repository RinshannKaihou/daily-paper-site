01Methodology

Device & thermal model

n-on-p 2D CFET: four MoS₂ sheets over four WSe₂, Lg = 12 nm (8/12/32 nm scans), 40-nm width. Anisotropic steady-state finite-volume heat conduction with thermal boundary resistance; 300-K fixed bottom, convective top (h = 2 MW·m⁻²·K⁻¹). Fixed-power runs at 20 µW with 30% contact dissipation; energy-balance, reciprocity and mesh-refinement checks shift the design gain by only 0.004 K.

Circuit-level self-consistency

Charge-sheet inverter model at VDD = 0.7 V solved self-consistently with temperature (mobility ∝ T⁻¹·³⁵, vsat ∝ T⁻⁰·⁵, contacts 500/800 Ω·µm); a follow-up adds finite metal conduction, voltage drops and Joule heating (ρm = 2.93×10⁻⁷ Ω·m).

Agent roles

GPT-6 "Astra" (Ultra) sweeps interconnect widths/positions against a worst-case objective J(d) = max ΔTmax over power ratios, at fixed metal volume; a coordinating agent reads the reversed width trend and proposes a substrate-directed via. Design families were pre-specified and candidate results retained — adaptive, not blind search.

  • Benefit shrinks under realism: 0.335 K in self-consistent inverter operation (330.75→330.41 K); including metal resistance, 0.6-K cooling buys a 2.2% nFET on-current loss.
  • Self-heating penalty (Lg = 8 nm): Ion falls 245.5→208.6 µA/µm (−15.0%), gain 83.4→52.1, peak DC power 3.62→3.73 µW.
  • Temperature ≠ cooling quality: shortening contact length 20→8 nm raises thermal resistance 4.2% yet lowers peak temperature 6.2 K — because current drops 17%.
  • Cross-agent reproduction: 14/16 submissions pass within 5×10⁻¹⁰ K; Claude Haiku 4.5 fails by 104.95 K (indexing + unit errors, retained for diagnosis); a "Grok-labelled" table-only entry with no code is flagged.

03aStrengths

  • Fair, constraint-controlled search — fixed metal volume, top area, heat sources, boundaries, common mesh — so candidate comparisons actually mean something.
  • Honest decoupling of fixed-power, self-consistent and metal-resistance-included results, with the thermal benefit explicitly priced at 2.2% Ion.
  • Fixed-position control and contact-length scaling cleanly separate redistribution from narrowing, and temperature from heat-removal quality.
  • Transparent reproduction audit: frozen hidden reference, retained failures, flagged unverified labels, refusal to over-interpret uncontrolled comparisons.
  • Bias accounting (hotspot moves between tiers; temperature and power peak at different Vin) shows genuine physical insight from the workflow.

03bLimitations

  • Simulation-only inside a supplied compact model — no experimental validation, no TCAD or ab-initio cross-check of the coupled inverter behavior.
  • Absolute gains are modest (1.18% fixed-power, ~0.1% absolute in circuit operation) against a very large baseline self-heating rise.
  • Constant thermal conductivities, no interconnect Joule heating in the main comparison, one-cell-thick monolayers, unresolved contact injection.
  • Agent attribution is partly narrative: two agents, one coordination event; the search space (widths/positions plus one via) is narrow vs real thermal-design freedom.
  • The multi-model table is uncontrolled and partly unverified (asterisked labels, one table-only submission) — it cannot rank model capabilities.

05Questions for further exploration

Q1Does the 1.67-K / 0.335-K benefit survive full TCAD (Sentaurus) or atomistic Green-function treatment of the same CFET stack?
Q2Can the search extend to materials & interface engineering (Fable C4's AlN, −45.19%/40.37% thermal resistance) under the same fair-comparison protocol?
Q3How does the thermal-benefit / electrical-cost trade-off evolve under dynamic switching workloads rather than DC operating points?
Q4What minimal scaffolding (fixed prompts, seeds, tool versions) turns the reproduction audit into a controlled model comparison — and does the Haiku failure mode generalize?
Q5Does temperature–performance decoupling (+4.2% Rth with −6.2 K peak T) imply an optimum contact length for temperature-corrected delay, not temperature alone?
