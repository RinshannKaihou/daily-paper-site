> **一句话总结：** 本文将 W4A4 量化误差精确分解为"激活引导的权重补偿项（AGWC）"与"正交残差"两部分，并证明权重优化只能压低前者、残差必须靠旋转/缩放设计来控制；基于残差上界推导出随机符号 Hadamard 旋转、符号采样与 L2 通道缩放三条实用准则，组成的免反传方案 L2-SmoothRot 在 8 个 Llama/Mistral 模型上全部取得最低 WikiText-2 困惑度（如 Llama3-8B 达 7.20，优于梯度训练的 SpinQuant 的 7.33）。

> **TL;DR:** This paper gives an exact decomposition of local W4A4 quantization error into an activation-guided weight-compensation (AGWC) term that GPTQ-style weight optimization can reduce, and an orthogonal residual that only transformation design can shrink. Bounding the residual via persistent channel-wise outlier and regular activation quantities explains why random signs suppress constructive outlier interference, why sampling sign patterns helps, and why L2 channel scaling (with a further relaxation recovering SmoothQuant's L∞ rule) is natural. The resulting backpropagation-free L2-SmoothRot achieves the lowest WikiText-2 perplexity on all eight Llama/Mistral models (e.g., 7.20 vs SpinQuant's 7.33 and QuaRot's 7.45 on Llama3-8B) and the lowest C4 perplexity on every model, while trailing SpinQuant's average zero-shot accuracy on only three models by at most 1.09 points.

## 核心贡献

- **精确的误差分解定理（Theorem 1）**：把单层"权重-激活"量化重建误差 J 恒等地分解为激活引导的权重补偿项 J_AGWC（位于量化后激活的列空间内，可被 GPTQ/GPTAQ 类权重优化压低）与正交残差项（权重优化无论怎么调都无法改变）。这从几何上划清了"权重补偿能解决什么、必须靠变换设计解决什么"的边界。
- **残差的上界刻画**：用一个"持续通道级离群（CO）+ 常规激活分量"的理想化分解 X = X_co + X_reg，把正交残差上界拆成由离群通道驱动的 J_co 与由常规分量驱动的 J_reg，二者分别指向旋转设计与通道缩放设计。
- **Hadamard 旋转的干扰理论（Theorem 2）**：证明固定 Hadamard 旋转下 J_co 最坏依赖 N_co²/d（多离群通道相干叠加），随机 Rademacher 符号把期望交叉项清零、降为 N_co·log d/d，再采样多个符号矩阵并取最优可进一步改善——为"随机符号旋转"和"基于校准的符号选择"给出统一解释。
- **从二阶矩界推导 L2 缩放（Proposition 1）**：对 J_reg 的二阶矩界做 Frobenius 代理最小化，闭式得到 λ_k = ‖X_{:,k}‖₂/‖W_{:,k}‖₂ 的 L2 缩放规则；再做一次更松的松弛（谱范数→Frobenius、二阶矩→最大值）恰好恢复 SmoothQuant 式 L∞ 规则，首次把两类缩放放进同一"保留统计量"递进框架。
- **免反传的 L2-SmoothRot 配置**：在线符号 Hadamard 旋转（SOR，含 RoPE 后保注意力分数的 QK 旋转）+ 符号采样（SS，RTN 初筛 10 个候选、GPTAQ 精评前 3）+ 仅作用于 FFN 下投影输入的 L2 缩放（L2S，可融合进权重、零推理开销），全部在 QuaRot 框架内实现且不需要梯度训练。

## Key Contributions

- **Exact error decomposition (Theorem 1):** The local layer-wise weight-activation quantization objective J splits exactly into an activation-guided weight compensation (AGWC) term — the part inside the column space of the quantized activations that weight optimization (GPTQ/GPTAQ/QEP-style methods) can reduce — and an orthogonal residual that no feasible weight choice can alter for a fixed transformation.
- **Residual bounds via a persistent-outlier model:** Introducing the "persistent sparse CO model" X = X_co + X_reg (token-shared outlier levels plus regular variation), the residual is bounded through an outlier-driven quantity J_co and a regular quantity J_reg, assigning rotation design to the former and channel scaling to the latter.
- **Interference theory for Hadamard rotation (Theorem 2):** Fixed rotations admit worst-case N_co(X)²/d dependence from constructive interference among persistent outlier channels; Rademacher random signs cancel cross-channel terms in expectation, giving N_co(X)·log d/d; sampling N_s sign patterns and taking the best gives a further log N_s improvement — jointly motivating randomized Hadamard transforms, signed online rotation, and calibration-based sign selection.
- **L2 scaling derived, not assumed (Proposition 1):** Minimizing a Frobenius surrogate of the second-moment bound on J_reg yields the closed-form L2 rule λ_k = ‖X_{:,k}‖₂/‖W_{:,k}‖₂ (same form as QuIP's rescaling but derived for activation quantization), and one further relaxation recovers SmoothQuant's L∞ rule, unifying both as successive relaxations of the same bound.
- **L2-SmoothRot, a backpropagation-free configuration:** Signed online rotation (including a post-RoPE QK rotation that preserves attention scores), RTN-screened sign sampling, and L2 scaling merged into FFN down-projection weights — implemented inside the QuaRot framework with no inference-time overhead, competitive with gradient-trained SpinQuant.

## Methodology

The paper works in the local reconstruction setting: for a linear layer Y = XW^T with an invertible output-preserving transformation P (so Z = XP, V = WP^{-T}, and ZV^T = XW^T), it analyzes the per-layer objective J = (1/T)‖Z̃Ḽ^T − ZV^T‖²_F under no-clipping per-token symmetric activation quantization and arbitrary weight quantization. Theorem 1 writes V* = V − Ã^†AV^T (the input-error-corrected weight target, connecting to QEP/GPTAQ/CoreQ) and decomposes J = J_AGWC + (1/T)‖(I − Π_Z̃)AV^T‖², where Π_Z̃ projects onto the column space of the quantized activations; only the first term responds to weight optimization. The orthogonal residual is then upper-bounded through the persistent sparse CO model: activations are split into a token-shared outlier component X_co (channels detected on calibration data à la Raman et al.) and a regular remainder X_reg.

Three analysis threads follow. (1) For J_co, the Hadamard entry structure (±1/√d indexed by bit-wise inner products) makes each rotated coordinate a deterministic signed sum of outlier magnitudes; squaring exposes N_co(X)² cross-channel interference terms under fixed rotations that Rademacher signs zero out in expectation (Appendix A gives this interference view). Sampling multiple sign matrices gives a min-over-candidates bound. (2) For J_reg, a sub-Gaussian second-moment bound after random-sign rotation gives J_reg ≲ (2 log(2dT/δ)/d)·‖WΛ‖²₂‖X_reg Λ^{-1}‖²_F; minimizing its Frobenius surrogate R²(Λ) by Cauchy–Schwarz equality yields the L2 scaling rule, and further relaxing spectral→Frobenius norms and second moments→maxima recovers SmoothQuant's L∞ rule. (3) These translate into L2-SmoothRot: sign flips inserted before online Hadamard rotations at attention-output and FFN-down positions plus an orthogonal QK rotation after RoPE (preserving (QR)(KR)^T = QK^T); sign patterns sampled from a single seed per candidate, shared per rotation category across layers, screened 10 candidates with RTN, re-evaluated the top 3 with GPTAQ, selected by WikiText-2 validation perplexity; L2 scaling applied only to the FFN down-projection input using empirical activation statistics as a proxy for X_reg, fused into weights. Experiments use simulated W4A4 + KV4 quantization (weights per-output-channel symmetric, activations per-token symmetric, K/V per-token per-head asymmetric; clipping 0.9/0.95), GPTAQ weight calibration on 128 WikiText-2 samples, and the same GPTAQ settings applied to all four compared transformation methods (QuaRot, SmoothRot, SpinQuant, L2-SmoothRot); SpinQuant rotations are trained 100 steps on WikiText-2 following its own protocol.

## Results & Highlights

- **WikiText-2 perplexity (W4A4, GPTAQ, KV4), Table 1:** L2-SmoothRot achieves the lowest perplexity on all 8 models (Llama-7B/13B, Llama2-7B/13B, Llama3-8B, Llama3.2-1B/3B, Mistral-7B-v0.3). On Llama3-8B it reaches 7.20 vs SpinQuant 7.33, SmoothRot 7.57, QuaRot 7.45 (FP16: 5.94); on Llama3.2-1B it reaches 12.92 vs SpinQuant 13.33 and QuaRot 13.81 (FP16: 9.41).
- **C4 perplexity (Table 3):** lowest on every model, e.g., Llama-7B C4 PPL 8.76 for L2-SmoothRot vs QuaRot 8.84, SmoothRot 8.87, SpinQuant 8.95 (FP16: 8.03) — showing gains are not confined to the calibration/selection set WikiText-2.
- **Zero-shot accuracy (average over PIQA, ARC-E, ARC-C, HellaSwag, WinoGrande, LAMBADA):** highest on 5 of 8 models; on Llama3-8B, 68.10 vs SpinQuant's 68.36 (−0.26 pts) and QuaRot's 65.71 (+2.39 pts); on Mistral-7B-v0.3, 71.00 vs SpinQuant 71.17 (−0.17 pts); on Llama3.2-3B it beats SpinQuant on PPL (9.08 vs 9.19) but trails its accuracy by 1.09 points (60.94 vs 62.03).
- **Component ablation on Llama3-8B (Table 2):** from QuaRot's 7.45, adding SOR alone gives 7.30 (largest single gain), SS alone 7.40, L2S alone 7.32; combining all three reaches 7.20 — consistent with SOR/SS targeting J_co and L2S targeting J_reg.
- **Seed-selection ablation (Figure 1, Llama3-8B, 100 seeds, 1000 trials per N):** both RTN top-1 and top-3 reranking improve mean GPTAQ test perplexity over the all-seed mean of 7.2164, with gains growing for larger candidate sets N ∈ {4,10,16,32}; top-3 consistently beats top-1, and top-1's advantage diminishes between N = 16 and N = 32.

## Authors & Credibility

Yamato Narita and Issei Sato are from the Department of Computer Science, the University of Tokyo; Issei Sato is a well-established professor there (also affiliated with RIKEN) with an extensive record in machine learning theory and applications. The paper is a preprint under review (arXiv:2609.21450v1, 18 Sep 2026, cs.LG) with a disclosed AI-use statement for writing polish. The theoretical claims (Theorems 1–2, Proposition 1) come with proofs in appendices, and the empirical protocol is careful: identical GPTAQ weight quantization applied across all four compared transformations, SpinQuant retrained under its own published protocol, and an appendix with full per-task results. The framing as "analysis-first, method-second" (the method is presented as an evaluation of the theory's guidelines) is honest relative to most PTQ papers.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The exact AGWC/orthogonal-residual split is acknowledged to coincide with CoreQ's mismatch decomposition for full-column-rank inputs — the novelty lies in bounding the residual via persistent-outlier statistics and in unifying random signs, sign sampling, and L2/L∞ scaling as operations on the same two residual terms. This unification is genuinely clarifying, but each individual practical component (signed Hadamard, sign search, L2 scaling à la QuIP rescaling) already existed; the paper's method is a theoretically-motivated recomposition rather than a new algorithm.
- **Rigor:** Proofs are provided and the empirical comparison is controlled (same GPTAQ for all transformations). However, the theory-practice gap is real: the analysis assumes no-clipping per-token symmetric quantization and an idealized token-shared CO model, while experiments use clipping ratios 0.9/0.95 and approximate X_reg statistics with full activations; the bounds are upper bounds and their tightness is never empirically measured (no correlation between J_co/J_reg proxies and actual residual/error).
- **Reproducibility:** Good — models, calibration sizes (128 GPTAQ / 512 scaling samples), clipping ratios, selection protocol (10 RTN + 3 GPTAQ + WT2 validation PPL), and per-task numbers are all specified. No code release is mentioned in the main text, and experiments are simulated quantization, so no kernels/runtimes.
- **Significance:** The practical deltas over QuaRot/SmoothRot are small on legacy models (Llama-7B: 6.12 → 6.09 WT2 PPL) — the headline "lowest PPL on all eight" partly rests on 0.01–0.03 margins there — though Llama3-8B (7.45 → 7.20) and Llama3.2-1B (13.81 → 12.92) show meaningful gains. Matching gradient-trained SpinQuant without backpropagation is a solid result, but accuracy still trails SpinQuant on 3 of 8 models (up to 1.09 points on Llama3.2-3B).
- **Red flags:** (1) Rotation/sign selection is done on WikiText-2 validation — the same corpus as the headline metric — and although C4 also improves, zero-shot accuracy does not uniformly follow perplexity; (2) the component ablation (Table 2) is run only on a single model (Llama3-8B); (3) no comparison against FlatQuant or OmniQuant (both cited), no W4A8/lower-bit settings, and no Qwen/Gemma families; (4) the seed-sampling stage adds 10 RTN + 3 GPTAQ calibration runs of compute that is not accounted for in the "backpropagation-free" selling point (it is gradient-free, not search-free).

## Strengths

- A clean geometric theorem that, for the first time in this line, cleanly separates what weight compensation can and cannot fix in W4A4 quantization, with direct design consequences.
- Explains three previously heuristic practices (random signs, sign sampling, SmoothQuant vs L2 scaling) as provable operations on different terms of one bound — the L2-then-L∞ relaxation chain is particularly elegant.
- The method is genuinely backpropagation-free and infrastructure-light (built inside QuaRot, scaling fused into weights with zero inference overhead), yet matches or beats gradient-trained SpinQuant on perplexity across all eight models.
- Thorough empirical hygiene: identical GPTAQ settings across baselines, C4 confirmation outside the selection set, per-task appendix, and two purpose-built ablations (components, seed selection) that map one-to-one onto the theory's terms.

## Limitations

- The persistent sparse CO model is an analytical abstraction (constant token-shared outlier levels) that real activations only approximately satisfy; the gap is handled by proxies rather than measured.
- Absolute gains over the strongest published baselines are modest on several models, and average zero-shot accuracy lags SpinQuant on Llama3-8B, Llama3.2-3B, and Mistral-7B-v0.3 despite better perplexity.
- All results use simulated W4A4+KV4 quantization of decoder linear layers; no hardware kernels, end-to-end latency, or memory measurements are reported.
- Ablations cover a single model; sign-pattern search cost (10 RTN + 3 GPTAQ evaluations) and sensitivity to the validation set used for selection are not fully quantified beyond one figure.
- Coverage stops at 4-bit activations and two model families; robustness on more modern architectures (e.g., Qwen, Gemma) and stricter regimes (W4A4 with lower bits, longer contexts) is untested.

## Relevance

这篇论文对关注 LLM 高效推理与 PTQ 量化理论的工作高度相关：它给出了"权重补偿 vs 变换设计"的误差分工定理，直接指导 W4A4 旋转+缩放方案的设计，适合作为理解 QuaRot/SpinQuant/SmoothQuant 一族方法统一原理的理论参考。若研究方向涉及量化误差分析、离群值处理或免训练量化配置，本文的分析框架和 L2-SmoothRot 配置都值得跟进。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How tight are the J_co and J_reg bounds empirically — do they correlate with actual layer-wise reconstruction error across models and depth, and could measured residual terms replace perplexity as a cheaper selection criterion?
- Does calibration-based sign selection remain beneficial when the selection/validation corpus differs from the evaluation corpus (e.g., select on C4 or instruction data, evaluate on WT2), and how large is the selection-overfitting effect on downstream tasks?
- Can the orthogonality-based argument be extended to clipped quantizers and to learned non-orthogonal transformations (FlatQuant-style), where the AGWC/residual split no longer holds exactly?
- Why does L2-SmoothRot improve perplexity but trail SpinQuant's zero-shot accuracy on Llama3.2-3B — is there a systematic accuracy-perplexity divergence under sign-sampled rotations at small scale?
- What is the compute-accuracy frontier of sign sampling: is the top-3-of-10 GPTAQ reranking the right operating point, or could a residual-bound surrogate skip GPTAQ evaluation entirely?
