Key metric — boundary exponent

p =0.75–1.06

Fitted from 1−β₂ = C(1−β₁)ᵖ across all six real model–task settings — every one near-linear. The 1D quadratic toy model gives p ≈ 3.07 (γ ≃ 15.6δ³·⁰⁷).

exponent drop ≈ 3× flatter than quadratic theory — the approximation cannot produce it

Why this matters

A direct β₂ guardrail for large-model training: with β₁ fixed, too-large β₂ enters the spike zone — plus a mechanistic basis for classifying spike root causes.

原文 · Relevance可直接转化为大模型训练中 β₂ 的调参/避坑准则，并为尖峰检测与根因分类（优化器动力学失配 vs 有限尺度损失几何）提供机制性依据。

▲ Critical edge

The theory's proven two-sided spike wedge collapses under weak weight decay (λwd = 10⁻³) — a force present in every real training run.

worst quantitative gap: predicted C_L 3.72 vs measured 2.21 (−68%) on the flagship modular-division task · right-boundary theorem is a rederivation of Bai et al. (2026a)

02 Method, distilled

S1

Scan the full β-plane

Six settings (modular-arithmetic Transformers, Tiny Shakespeare char-LM, CIFAR-10 MLP & VGG11-style CNN, MNIST autoencoder; 54k–9.75M params), AdamW, seed 42, only β₁/β₂ varied on log grids. Spikes detected by a deterministic spectral pipeline: log-transform → Butterworth low-pass → Welch spectrum → peaks with prominence ≥ 0.02·max.

S2

Reduce to a 1D toy model

Adam on L(x)=k|x|ⁿ, n>2: quiet-phase approximation plus power-law rescaling collapse the dynamics to an autonomous two-step recurrence; Jury conditions give the stability boundary β₂ = β₁2(n−2)/n.

S3

Measure the real landscape

At pre-spike checkpoints, loss slices along the Adam-preconditioned gradient are fit with three-segment piecewise log–log models (core / wall / rollover; 2,000 bootstrap resamples). A Gaussian exponential-sum theorem then links wall exponent nwall to boundary coefficient C_L.

05 Strengths

  • A crisp, falsifiable question answered with an unusually systematic six-setting scan — and a decisive negative result that kills the quadratic default frame.
  • The mechanistic chain is complete in outline: proven Hessian collapse and core width O(1/σₐ), measured sharpening and wall exponents, scale-level match to the boundary.
  • Intellectual honesty throughout: C_L's empirical status labeled, Bai et al. (2026a) overlap credited, control experiments probe the soft spots.
  • Detection and fitting pipelines specified to reproducible detail — filter cutoffs, prominence thresholds, bootstrap design.

06 Limitations

  • C_L = 12(n−2)/(3n−4) — the only quantitative bridge to real networks — is an empirical fit with no derivation; a complete theory is explicitly left open.
  • Small models only (54k–9.75M params; CIFAR full-batch on 200 images); no LLM-scale, minibatch-noise, or mixed-precision validation despite the LLM-spike motivation.
  • One seed per (β₁, β₂) cell and one learning rate per task — while C is known to shift ~60% with η (15.02 → 25.00 in the paper's own controls).
  • The Gaussian slope assumption contradicts measured heavy tails (σₐ/μₐ ≈ 1700); the 1/σₐ agreement may be partly fortuitous.
  • The wedge's right boundary collapses under weak weight decay (λ = 10⁻³) and doesn't apply to real networks' quadratic cores.

07 Open questions

  1. Does the near-linear boundary persist at LLM scale with minibatch noise, data ordering, and mixed precision — where PaLM-style spikes actually occur?
  2. Can C_L ≃ 12(n−2)/(3n−4) be derived from first principles, and does it survive stochastic gradients?
  3. What is the joint (η, β₁, β₂) stability surface, given the prefactor's strong η-sensitivity?
  4. Would the empirically heavy-tailed slope distribution (σₐ/μₐ ≈ 1700) change the O(1/σₐ) core scale?
  5. Can an optimizer exploit this — online-estimating n_eff at the update scale and adapting β₂ to stay below the evolving boundary?
