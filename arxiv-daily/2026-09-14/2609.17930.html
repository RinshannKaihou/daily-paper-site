Why this matters

Methodology · distilled

1

Annotate every step. 2,518 real trajectories — SWE-bench (565), TerminalBench (1,851), BixBench (102) — across six model backbones and five harnesses. Dual annotators label each step and locate the first mistake (κ = 0.77 failure / 0.73 location); 6,967 mistakes fall into a pre-registered 88-category codebook (78 observed, 10 families).

2

Build Traverse. 1,423 trajectories / 44,341 steps (20–200 steps long): detect whether a run failed and localize the first-mistake index, with exact-match and ±1/±3 tolerance metrics.

3

Train Scout. Qwen3-4B-Thinking: SFT on 1,904 label-faithful CoT trajectories distilled from Gemini-3 Flash, then GRPO RL with a recall-product reward Rc·Ri that kills both degenerate strategies (label-everything-correct and flag-everything-wrong).

4

Measure utility. Best-of-N selection on fixed candidate pools (SWE-bench Verified, Terminal-Bench 2.0), scored against agents’ pass@1 and oracle selection.

GRPO: G=4 · 32 traj/step · KL β=0.001 · verl · reward = 0.7·Rstep + 0.3·verdict · held-out test: 258 decontaminated trajectories

Full three-part signature co-occurs in 13.7% of runs. Root-fault share falls 58% → 39% from early to late thirds — failures compound.

What fails most is not what causes failure. Action/execution faults are 45.1% of all mistakes but only 35% root causes; reasoning + planning cause 46% of first mistakes (root rates: reasoning 69%, tool/environment 75%, memory 74%). Recovery tracks environment feedback: SWE agents miss 76% of errors yet solve 72% of tasks (tests catch what reasoning misses); terminal agents notice, but solve only 19%.

Strengths

  • Step-level human ground truth on 2,518 real, deployment-near trajectories — dual-annotated with reported κ, far beyond outcome-bit evaluation.
  • Pre-registered taxonomy (88 categories, 10 unused — not fitted to data); conclusions drawn only at supported granularity (κ = 0.64–0.83).
  • Symptom-vs-root-cause decomposition is actionable for debugging and reward design; the environment-feedback explanation of SWE-vs-terminal recovery is concrete and testable.
  • Exemplary reward design: Rc·Ri product explicitly neutralizes the over-flagging trap frontier judges exhibit.
  • Clean best-of-N protocol (fixed pools, pass@1 + oracle reference lines) — and everything released: benchmark, training data, recipe, verifier.

Limitations

  • ~43% of failed runs have no single decisive first mistake (diffuse failure); 14% of solved runs contain flagged mistakes — these get only an extended-data figure, not main analysis.
  • Science domain is thin: 102 BixBench trajectories, 38 failed, ~10 steps — weakens the transfer claim; the long-horizon claim rests on the two coding domains.
  • No like-for-like Scout-vs-judge table on the identical split in the main text (judges: full Traverse; Scout: 258-run held-out).
  • Training is domain-imbalanced (1,851 terminal vs 565 SWE) with CoT distilled from Gemini-3 Flash — a style/anchoring dependency never ablated.
  • Safety prevalence is judge-sensitive (reported as ranges); the 38.5% blindness figure is a lower bound by construction; signature untested on newer model generations.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does the failure signature (69.5 / 38.5 / 84.1) persist on newer model generations — or shrink as frontier models improve?
  2. Would interleaving Scout-style verification during a run let agents back out of first mistakes before they compound — at what latency cost at 128K context?
  3. How much of Scout’s advantage survives varying the CoT distillation source — or replacing it with human-written rationales?
  4. Can the recall-product reward Rc·Ri transfer to outcome reward models, mitigating verifier over/under-flagging bias in RL?
  5. SWE recovers via tests; terminal doesn’t. How much recovery does instrumenting terminal-style environments with cheap verification signals buy?
  6. With 54% of unsafe actions invisible even to gold labels, what annotation protocol makes safety a first-class, reliably measurable label?
