Steered-labor step time 958 s → 180 s per training step, after the driver wrote six ranked hypotheses (each with a prediction and guard) — and the recipe held without escalating to the strong tier.

Why this matters SRC · RELEVANCE

A ready-to-adopt blueprint for week-scale autonomous agents — bounded file memory, tick discipline, cost-cutting model cascades — directly reusable in self-evolving research pipelines.

⚠ Critical edge SRC · CRITICAL ASSESSMENT

One campaign, one task, one principal — and no baseline run, so there is no way to attribute the success to the architecture rather than to model capability or task tractability.

Methodology 01

A position paper validated by a single longitudinal deployment — not a benchmark study. From one premise — context C, process life P, human attention A, failure interval F all ≪ horizon H — seven bottlenecks follow: Autonomy, Horizon, State, Delegation, Correctness, Resilience, Cost. Four abstractions answer them:

Level
A 7-tier temporal hierarchy, L0 → L6; each level keeps only a bounded file compressing the level below (hard 40-line read limit on any log or source).
Tick
The clocked unit of autonomous action; a hook refuses session end until the checkpoint is written.
Protocol
Every channel and persistent state is a named file with designated writer, reader, cadence, check — kill verdicts are the only uncompressed messages.
Tier
Judgment / labor (routine + steered) / deterministic processes. Work starts at the lowest plausible tier; first failure returns feedback, second failure escalates — up to the human principal.

Results & highlights 02

The uncorrected policy trained on stale rollouts collapsed beyond the staleness threshold at both model scales, while the decoupled importance-sampling correction held stable — and the full method reached the calibrated environment's ceiling ahead of its control, matching Hou et al. 2026.

  • Cascading keeps cost down: 86% routine vs 14% strong tier — the most capable model handled roughly one-seventh of total labor.
  • Adversarial review caught: a verifier-satisfiable-without-solving path plus two shortcut families (task mix rebuilt), and a stop rule that would have compared an arm against its control's transient dip — rewritten before results were recorded.
  • Framework port: moving to a second training framework changed only launch scripts and an adapter; every brief, card, directive and review format carried over, validated by realised staleness on half the training hardware.

Strengths 03

Coherent, quotable, and immediately actionable.

  • One premise, seven requirements — every mechanism traces back to a named bottleneck, giving the design space unusual coherence.
  • Harness-over-model position is well-argued and timely, backed by cited failure taxonomies locating most agent failures in the interaction layer.
  • Falsifiable engineering rules practitioners can adopt today: checkpoint ≤ 8k chars, ≤ 40-line reads, uncompressed kill verdicts, journal-beats-checkpoint resume.
  • In-campaign epistemic hygiene: pre-registered hypotheses, success criteria and stop rules; an adversarial reviewer with no conversation history at every gate.
  • Turns an experience report into a research agenda — record-as-dataset, gates as insertion points for learned policies.

Limitations 04

An existence proof, and it knows it — but the flanks are real.

  • Single campaign, domain, principal (RL reproduction only) — no replication, no second task to test generality.
  • No baseline architecture ran on the same task — which of the four abstractions actually carried the campaign is unanswered.
  • No artifact release — no code, prompts or protocol templates; models, frameworks and scales unnamed; the campaign cannot be re-run or fully specified.
  • "Accumulation" is anecdotal — vignettes like the stop-guard, not measured invocation counts or counterfactual runs.
  • Ten days is the short end of long-horizon — hierarchy behaviour beyond a week is untested.

Questions for further exploration 05

  • How much of the success is the hierarchy vs. the models? A flat-agent or no-cascade control on the identical task would quantify the architecture's marginal value.
  • Can "accumulation" be measured — frequency and downstream effect of early-written rules, or counterfactual campaigns without them?
  • Month-scale campaigns: does the ladder need new levels, or does the 1,500-char L6 week-file itself become the bottleneck?
  • What is the minimal learned component that beats its written rule at a gate — and how is it audited so it cannot outrun harness verification?
  • How does the cascade handle reviewer errors — and can the 7 critical findings from 30 reviews become a reviewer-quality benchmark?
  • Does journal-beats-checkpoint resume survive concurrent writers or mid-write crashes, which ten days may not have stressed?
