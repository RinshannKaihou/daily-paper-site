Why this matters

Turning “what to store, when to retrieve” into a learnable, reward-aligned policy is directly reusable for personalized lifelong assistants and agentic-memory systems.

Critical edge

Training rewards and evaluations both come from the same LLM families that generate the responses — an undiscussed circularity risk, only partially offset by human evaluation.

And superiority isn't uniform: on BertScore (MSC/CC · GPT-4o) and ROUGE-L (GapChat · Gemini2.5), some baselines still win.

01 Methodology · 方法

Three pillars replace heuristic memory archiving with a closed-loop policy.

Formulation. Memory management is cast as a POMDP whose joint action splits into a Planner (binary save / discard) and a Trigger (which memory index to retrieve, or none).

Cold start. Retrospective Session Synthesis: from a seed session where a user first reveals a constraint, LLM user-simulators recursively invent a logically consistent past, then the storyline is walked forward to annotate Planner and Trigger labels with hard / soft negatives — 0.25–0.5K episodes suffice.

Co-evolution. A proxy reward scores each turn's intrinsic value now; a quality reward scores the future response that actually used a memory. The Cross-Session Truth Reward retro-propagates that future quality back to the original save decision:

r_truth = r_proxy + λ · r_qual · 𝕀[triggered]

future response quality, traced back to the storage decision via a Pending Reward Buffer; both agents train with PPO (shared critic, clipped objective + entropy).

Deployment. 1B–12B open-source policies (Llama-3, Gemma-3, Qwen3) plug into closed GPT-4o / Gemini2.5 backbones — no backbone weights needed.

  • Ablations — removing the Cross-Session Truth Reward hurts most (GapChat BLEU-4 1.28 → 0.97; CC Mauve 80.33 → 76.19); removing test-time evolution and synthetic data each cost smaller but consistent gains.
  • Self-evolution — Planner precision and Trigger recall climb steadily over 10 online sessions instead of plateauing.
  • Efficiency — ~63 ms construction / ~13 ms retrieval vs THEANINE's ~10.8 s / ~893 ms; token usage stays flat across sessions while baselines grow linearly.
  • Scaling — balanced Planner/Trigger sizes beat mismatched larger pairs; 0.5K synthetic episodes is the sweet spot — 1K overfits (CC BLEU-4 2.62 → 2.02).

Caveat — absolute BLEU-4 stays tiny (~1–2.5) and BertScore / ROUGE-L remain mixed: see Critical Edge above.

03 Strengths · 优势

  • Clean conceptual step: heuristic archive → closed-loop learnable policy with principled delayed credit assignment.
  • Elegant cold-start fix: backward storyline synthesis needs only 0.25–0.5K episodes, with an instructive overfitting tipping point at 1K.
  • Broad, fair coverage: 3 human-to-human datasets, 8 baselines, 9 policy sizes, 3 evaluation families (automatic, LLM-judge, human).
  • Deployable efficiency: flat token curves and millisecond memory ops where THEANINE-style pipelines cost seconds-to-tens-of-seconds.
  • Self-evolution directly evidenced — rising precision / recall over 10 sessions, not just implied.

04 Limitations · 局限

  • Scope: open-domain, companionship-style chat only — no task-oriented dialogue, QA, or reasoning benchmarks (authors acknowledge).
  • Rewards depend entirely on LLM judges, inheriting their biases and coupling with LLM-as-judge evaluation.
  • Automatic-metric gains uneven: BertScore (MSC/CC · GPT-4o) and ROUGE-L (GapChat · Gemini2.5) still favor some baselines.
  • Reported latency excludes the per-turn judge calls online PPO needs — true adaptation cost is higher than presented.
  • Synthetic expert-data quality unquantified; no code release stated; only ≤12B policy models and ≤10-session horizons studied.

05 Open questions · 待探索

  1. Would a frozen, learned reward model (or human preference data) change the co-evolution dynamics — and can the judge be decoupled from the evaluator?
  2. Can the Planner update and delete memories, not just append — overriding an outdated preference instead of accumulating conflicts?
  3. What is the true end-to-end latency and dollar cost once per-turn judge calls are counted, and can they be amortized or distilled?
  4. Does the framework transfer to tutoring, task-oriented dialogue, or coding assistants, where memory utility is task-verifiable?
  5. How does the policy behave under adversarial or drifting users — and at 50+ sessions, does the Trigger's retrieval space become a bottleneck?
  6. Why do balanced Planner/Trigger sizes beat mismatched larger pairs — is there a theoretical account of capability alignment in the loop?
