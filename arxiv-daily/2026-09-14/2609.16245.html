01Methodology — distilled

S1

Mine judgment data

Verified scientists work inside an “AI co-scientist” system; their traces are filtered into six process-level intervention categories, each paired with a synthetic counterfactual that holds scientific context fixed and flips only cognitive stance. True pairs score 3.6× higher TF-IDF similarity than random re-pairings — content controlled, stance isolated.

S2

Localize the control surface

Contrastive activation diffs pass through LEACE-style rank-one erasure of the shared “an intervention occurred” direction. Three independent analyses — a 61×61 residual alignment matrix, a frozen-weight attention write→read subspace map (never touching the judgment data), and a cross-layer SVD — converge on the same mid-depth band.

S3

Steer, closed-loop

Layer-36 residuals cluster into three regimes (deliberative / methodical / challenging; centroids ~85° and 130°–142° apart). A sparse readout identifies the regime; fixed contrastive directions are blended into four target layers, once per response, with damping under distribution shift. Gradient-free, zero parameter changes.

S4

Deploy as Columbus-1

Steered model + 32B bias policy + semantic memory graph + tool suite (search, code, CAD, simulation) — validated on BlueZ vulnerability discovery and a solid-motor rocket program.

Control surface · Kimi 2.6 · 61 layers · d = 7168 · MoE ~32B active/token

03Strengths

  • Unique supervision source: discarded reasoning, stance changes, negative knowledge — nearly impossible to obtain elsewhere, absent from publication-trained LLMs.
  • Well-triangulated localization: activation-based and frozen-weight analyses converge on the same mid-depth band, ruling out a judgment-data artifact.
  • Explicit rank-one erasure of the “intervention occurred” confound before interpreting alignment — a step many steering papers skip.
  • Clean closed-loop formulation: readout → regime → mode mixture → layer-wise composition, inference-time, zero parameter change, OOD damping.
  • Real deployments with external validation of the hardest claims (independent lab reproduction; domain-expert review of flight-control design).

04Limitations

  • Behavioral evidence base is two qualitative trajectory comparisons; no repeated trials, no benchmark, LLM judges used for evaluation.
  • Proprietary dataset of undisclosed size and composition (N_pairs never reported); localization results unreproducible externally.
  • No Columbus-1 ablations isolating steering from the bias policy, memory graph, or tools; no comparison to any prior steering method.
  • Single model (Kimi 2.6); layer indices and neurons are architecture-bound by construction.
  • Rocket program validated in simulation only — fabrication and flight testing are next steps.
  • Security evaluation reports no false-positive rate, discovery budget, or baseline comparison — efficiency unmeasurable.

05Questions for further exploration

  • Q1How much of Columbus-1’s BlueZ and rocket performance survives with steering disabled — the marginal contribution of MCS?
  • Q2Does the three-regime geometry (~85°, 130°–142°) recur across model families, or is it an artifact of Kimi 2.6’s training?
  • Q3What is N_pairs, and is the 3.6× TF-IDF contrast enough to rule out stylistic rather than cognitive direction content?
  • Q4Can steering quality be measured on an existing belief-revision / evidence-use benchmark instead of single-trajectory inspection?
  • Q5Does once-per-response routing granularity matter — would per-token routing improve evidence responsiveness or destabilize generation?
  • Q6Would MCS combined with RL fine-tuning produce a training signal for process-level judgment, not just inference-time control?
