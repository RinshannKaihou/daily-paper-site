> **一句话总结：** 作者提出一套把 LLM-as-a-judge 当作"测量仪器"来正式校准的实验流程（双重参照配对设计 + 可交换性零点 + 无损推测解码实现零点 + 阳性对照 + 预注册 ±0.3 分等价边界），用它统一测得：4-bit NF4 量化与无损推测解码在五个领域全部与全精度等价，而 3-bit HQQ 量化在 Qwen2.5-7B 上 pooled 掉 0.70 分、在同一 Llama-3.1-8B 上掉 1.84 分（代码域最惨 −3.11），早退（layer-15）在多步可验证难题上掉 2.54 分（数学正确数 19/27 → 6/27、代码 8/13 → 0/13），并证明困惑度、token 一致率、EAR、置信度等常用代理指标均与真实质量损失严重脱节。

> **TL;DR:** This paper treats an LLM judge (claude-sonnet-5, 7-point scale) as a measuring instrument and calibrates it the way a physicist would: a dual-reference paired design on 220 prompts across 5 domains (English/Chinese prose, Python code, arithmetic, hard-verifiable math+code), an exchangeability null, a provably-lossless speculative-decoding implementation null, pre-registered positive controls, and a pre-specified ±0.3-point TOST equivalence bound. On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, NF4 4-bit quantization (+0.01 [−0.10,+0.12]) and lenient speculation (−0.02) are equivalent to the bf16 baseline everywhere including Chinese, but HQQ 3-bit loses 0.70 points pooled (−0.09 arithmetic to −1.11 hard-verifiable) versus the full-precision baseline, and layer-15 early exit with cache refill loses 1.07 pooled and 2.54 on hard-verifiable problems (correct solves drop from 19/27 to 6/27 math, 8/13 to 0/13 code). The same 3-bit quantizer costs Llama-3.1-8B 1.84 pooled (code −3.11, 23% repetitive responses vs 1% on Qwen), showing losses are model- and domain-shaped. Crucially, standard proxies fail: EAR correlates with judged loss at only Spearman ρ=0.19–0.25, and a degenerate looping response scored *lower* perplexity (1.151) than its reference (1.239).

## 核心贡献

- 把 LLM-as-a-judge 上升为**经过正式校准的测量仪器**：不再是"跑个 judge 看分数"，而是像自然科学仪器一样验证零点（读数为零时真值确实为零）、灵敏度（阳性对照）、分辨率（预注册 ±0.3 分等价边界，用双单侧 TOST 检验）与噪声水平。
- 提出**双重参照配对设计**：未修改模型每条 prompt 采样两次，每个优化"臂"的得分 = 该臂评分 − 两个参照的均值，把 prompt 共享方差（intraclass 相关）从单参照的 0.49 压到 0.12。
- 设计**两种互补零点**：(1) 可交换性零点——两个参照样本互换不变，期望差严格为零，直接量出仪器噪声（每 prompt 标准差 0.94）；(2) 实现零点——严格拒绝规则下的推测解码按定理与原模型分布完全一致，任何非零读数都指向实现缺陷而非技术本身（开发中真的抓出两个 bug）。
- 首次把**量化（NF4 4-bit、HQQ 3-bit）、推测解码（严格/宽松接受）、早退（layer-15 带缓存修复）三个家族放在同一标尺、同一 prompt 集上比较**，并给出每域置信区间（pooled ±0.11、分域 ±0.3）。
- 系统性证明**主流代理指标不可靠**：EAR 与被判质量损失仅弱相关（ρ=0.19/0.25）；困惑度会奖励退化循环（循环回复困惑度 1.151 < 参照 1.239）；token 置信度与"改动的后果"近乎无关（数字 23.3% vs 标点 3.3% 的后果率，置信度却同为 ~0.99）。
- 协议本身**可迁移**到任何"想改变输出分布又想保质量"的干预：上下文压缩、KV-cache 驱逐、prompt 压缩、水印、蒸馏、系统提示词变更等。

## Key Contributions

- Reframes LLM-as-a-judge as a **formally calibrated instrument**, importing standard measurement discipline: verified zero points, positive controls with pre-registered predictions, a stated resolution (±0.3 rating points on the 7-point scale, chosen as the smallest effect of practical interest before the run), and two-one-sided-tests (TOST) equivalence decisions instead of "not significantly different."
- **Dual-reference paired design**: the unmodified model is sampled twice per prompt; each optimized arm's score is its judged rating minus the mean of the two references, cutting shared prompt-level variance (intraclass correlation from 0.49 with a single reference to 0.12).
- **Two complementary nulls**: an *exchangeability null* (the two reference draws are exchangeable, so the expected judged difference is exactly zero for any judge, however biased — its spread is the instrument noise, sd 0.94 per prompt / 0.84 per arm score), and an *implementation null* (speculative decoding under the strict rejection rule is provably distribution-identical to the full model, so a nonzero reading indicts the code — in development it caught two real defects).
- **First cross-family comparison on one scale**: quantization (NF4 4-bit, HQQ 3-bit), speculative decoding (strict λ=1.0 and lenient λ=0.2), and early exit (layer 15 with a fitted cache-repair map, with/without 16-token exact refill) measured on identical prompts, with per-domain CIs of ±0.3 at 220 prompts.
- **Demonstrates that standard proxies fail**: expected acceptance rate (EAR) predicts judged loss only weakly (Spearman ρ=0.19 on Qwen, 0.25 on Llama, and −0.42 on Llama Chinese where high local agreement marks repetition loops); perplexity rewards degenerate looping (a looping response scored 1.151 vs the reference's 1.239); token-level fidelity/length proxies rated as acceptable 37% of responses the judge found unacceptable; and certainty is nearly unrelated to consequence (digits vs punctuation emitted at 0.999 vs 0.989 confidence, yet substitution changed judged outcomes in 23.3% vs 3.3% of positions, n=354).
- **Transferability**: nothing in the dual-reference design, nulls, equivalence bound, or reliability checks is specific to inference optimization; it applies to context compaction, KV-cache eviction, prompt compression, retrieval truncation, safety filtering, watermarking, distillation, and system-prompt changes.

## Methodology

The instrument is claude-sonnet-5 rating each response in isolation on a 7-point rubric (7 = complete/correct/well-formed; 4 = a clear factual or logical error; 1 = degenerate), plus "derailed"/"recovered" boolean flags and a ≤15-word rationale (rubric in Appendix A). Five design elements calibrate it:

1. **Dual-reference pairing.** Each prompt gets two bf16 reference samples; every arm contributes one sample; the arm's score is rating minus the mean reference rating. Temperature 0.7, no top-p/k truncation, deterministic seeds, 1,500-token cap (3,000 for Llama).
2. **Exchangeability null.** Sample 2 minus Sample 1 has expectation exactly zero (exchangeability), measuring judge + sampling noise on the study's own prompts. Observed: −0.05 [−0.18, +0.08] over 220 prompts on Qwen.
3. **Implementation null.** A speculative-decoding arm (NF4 4-bit drafter, 8-token windows, strict rejection rule min(1, p_target/p_draft)) is null by theorem but exercises the full speculative code path (draft cache, batched verification, cache cropping, EOS handling). Read +0.05 on Qwen; −0.14 [−0.27, −0.01] on Llama — inside the equivalence bound but significantly below zero, localized to the hard domain (−0.46) and reported as an unresolved implementation-specific reading that no surface statistic would have flagged.
4. **Positive controls.** Two early-exit arms with losses registered as predictions from an earlier greedy-decoding campaign (−0.59 refill / −1.05 no refill); both detected with correct sign and ordering, though under-predicted by ~2x because the present run used sampling and added the hard-verifiable domain.
5. **Equivalence, power, pre-registration.** Equivalence = 90% CI inside ±0.3 (TOST at α=0.05). With score sd 0.84, 220 prompts buys ±0.11 pooled and ±0.3 per domain (40–60 prompts per domain). All arm predictions with confidence bands were written into the analysis script before generation.

Two of five domains carry ground truth: 27 math problems with parsed "Final answer:" lines and 13 programming tasks with hidden assert-based tests run in isolated subprocesses, giving an execution-grounded correctness column beside the judge ratings. Reliability: 20% of items double-judged (86% exact / 100% within-one agreement; judge-only sd 0.26 = 8% of paired-score variance); cross-judge checks with claude-opus-5 (ρ=0.88, n=176) and gemini-3.1-pro (ρ=0.80, n=226), both preserving arm ordering. Targets: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (primary, 8 arms) and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (replication, 6 arms — no early exit, since the cache-repair map was fitted to Qwen only), all generated on one RTX 5090 with the same quantization library per bit width.

## Results & Highlights

All deltas are mean judged-rating differences vs the dual bf16 reference on the 7-point scale, 220 prompts, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct unless stated:

- **NF4 4-bit quantization is equivalent to full precision at ±0.3 everywhere**: pooled Δ = +0.01 [−0.10, +0.12]; every domain cell within a quarter point of zero, including Chinese (−0.16 [−0.42, +0.10]); execution check 18/27 math correct vs the reference's 19/27.
- **HQQ 3-bit quantization loses 0.70 points pooled** [−0.85, −0.55] on Qwen, and the loss is domain-shaped: −0.09 on arithmetic, −0.46 on English prose, −0.84 on code, −0.91 on Chinese, −1.11 on hard-verifiable problems — a 12x spread across domains on the same arm.
- **The same 3-bit quantizer costs Llama-3.1-8B 1.84 points pooled** [−2.08, −1.60] where it cost Qwen 0.70 — a 2.6x model-dependence — with code the worst domain (−3.11 [−3.57, −2.64]) and 23% of Llama 3-bit responses falling into >10% repeated 8-grams versus 1% on Qwen.
- **Early exit at layer 15 with cache refill loses 1.07 pooled and 2.54 on hard-verifiable problems** [−3.04, −2.04] (3.29 without refill); the execution checker shows math correct dropping from 19/27 (bf16 reference) to 6/27 with refill and 4/27 without, and code all-tests-pass from 8/13 to 0/13 — fluent prose wrapping wrong numbers.
- **Certainty is not consequence**: in an earlier Qwen campaign (n=354 positions), digits and punctuation were emitted at near-identical confidence (0.999 vs 0.989), yet substituting a plausible alternative changed the judged outcome in 23.3% of digit positions vs 3.3% of punctuation positions.
- **EAR barely predicts judged damage**: per-response Spearman ρ = 0.19 (Qwen) / 0.25 (Llama) over 220 responses; on Qwen the domains where the models disagree *most* (prose, EAR 0.84) lose the *least* (0.46), and hard problems where they disagree least lose the most (1.11).
- **Perplexity rewards failure**: one degenerate looping early-exit response had perplexity 1.151 versus its reference's 1.239; token-fidelity/length proxies rated as acceptable 37% of responses the judge found unacceptable.
- **The noise is the model's, not the judge's**: re-rating identical text moves scores by sd 0.26 while a fresh model sample on the same prompt moves them by 0.94; the unmodified model agrees with itself on only 31/40 hard-verifiable outcomes at temperature 0.7 — the floor any answer-flip statistic must clear.
- **Instrument validity on verifiable domains**: judge rating averages 6.07 when the execution checker says correct vs 2.45 when wrong; zero items wrong by the checker were rated ≥6 on either model.

This extends his own prior work on early exit (Kaplan, 2026, arXiv:2609.14144), which found that token-level fidelity thresholds can accept configurations that destroy task correctness — the seed observation for this paper. The scholarship is unusually careful — pre-registered predictions, released code/prompts/rubric (github.com/jerrykaplan/Calibrated-Instrument), honest reporting of the Llama null anomaly and of its own positive-control misses — though the paper is an unreviewed single-author draft, not peer-reviewed.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The components are individually known — Usami et al.'s (2026) judge datasheets, Helcig et al.'s (2026) "statistically lossless" framing, Lakens' TOST equivalence tests, Kübler et al.'s paired degradation tests. The genuine contribution is the synthesis into a turnkey, pre-registrable protocol and its cross-family application on identical prompt sets; this is measurement science, not a new optimization, and should be judged as such. It succeeds on those terms.
- **Rigor**: Strong. Dual nulls with fault localization, positive controls with registered predictions, power arithmetic stated so readers can price a different resolution (±0.05 pooled would need ~1,100 prompts), three judge families with consistent ordering, and an execution-grounded check on the two verifiable domains. Notably honest: the paper reports that its own positive-control anchors under-predicted by ~2x (−0.59 registered vs −1.07 observed for the refill arm) and that the Llama implementation null read −0.14 significantly below zero without a determined cause.
- **Reproducibility**: Good — code, prompts, rubric, and registered predictions are public; deterministic seeds; one GPU (RTX 5090); same quantization library per bit width. Remaining friction: the primary judge is a commercial model (claude-sonnet-5) whose behavior may drift over time, and the 3-bit wall-clock times (35.2 s on Qwen) reflect an unoptimized HQQ PyTorch kernel.
- **Significance**: High for practice. The headline findings — 4-bit NF4 safe everywhere at ±0.3 on a 7B model; 3-bit and layer-15 early exit sharply domain- and model-dependent; cheap proxies (perplexity, token agreement, EAR, confidence) mis-rank damage — directly inform deployment decisions and the design of acceptance rules.
- **Red flags / weak points**: (1) Only 7–8B models; the authors concede magnitudes will not transfer to 70B-scale models where 4-bit tolerance is known to be much better — so the "3-bit is unsafe" message must not be over-generalized. (2) The 3-bit arm uses only the data-free HQQ quantizer; calibrated 3-bit methods (AWQ, GPTQ) are untested, so the claim characterizes this quantizer, not the 3-bit budget — the paper says so explicitly. (3) The arithmetic domain is at ceiling (every reference rated 7), so all judged arithmetic cells are uninformative and that domain leans entirely on the execution check. (4) The primary judge is one model family; on prose and Chinese there is no external objective check, so validity there rests on reliability statistics and two secondary judges. (5) Early exit is tested at a single depth (layer 15 of 28) and a single refill interval (16 tokens) — the depth/interval trade-off surface is unmapped. (6) Single-author draft status and heavy AI assistance in drafting warrant the usual caution, although the pre-registration and released artifacts mitigate it.

## Strengths

- Treats evaluation as instrumentation with zero points, positive controls, stated resolution, and pre-registration — a level of measurement discipline rarely seen in the LLM-optimization literature.
- The two-null design localizes faults (judge/analysis vs implementation) and demonstrably caught two real implementation bugs during development.
- Cross-family, cross-model comparison on identical prompts with per-domain confidence intervals directly answers the practitioner's question ("will this optimization hurt *my* traffic?").
- The proxy-inadequacy analysis (EAR vs consequence, certainty vs consequence, perplexity rewarding repetition) is quantitative, mechanistic, and immediately actionable for anyone designing acceptance rules or gating signals.
- Full artifact release (prompts, rubric, hidden tests, registered predictions) plus a clear statement of what transfers to other interventions.

## Limitations

- Two 7–8B-parameter models only; magnitudes are known not to transfer to larger models (only the method does).
- 3-bit results characterize one data-free quantizer (HQQ, group size 64), not calibrated 3-bit methods such as AWQ or GPTQ.
- The judge-only instrument covers prose and Chinese; no execution check exists there, and the primary judge is a single model family (secondary families agree on ordering but differ by constant offsets).
- Per-domain equivalence at ±0.3 with 40–60 prompts per domain is coarse; the hard-verifiable domain (paired sd ~2.0) would need 80–150 prompts per domain for tighter bounds.
- Early exit tested at one depth and one refill interval; the lossless-speculation anomaly on Llama (−0.14, concentrated in long multi-step responses) was bounded but not explained.
- Arithmetic domain saturated at rating 7, so judged cells there carry no information.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How do calibrated 3-bit quantizers (AWQ, GPTQ with activation-aware channel protection) score under this instrument versus the data-free HQQ arm — does calibration flatten the domain profile or only shift the level?
- Would the protocol's conclusions change on 30–70B models, where 3–4 bit tolerance is reportedly much better, and what prompt budget would be needed to resolve ±0.1 per domain there?
- Can the token-level certainty-vs-consequence finding (23.3% vs 3.3%) be turned into a validated "pivot-token" classifier for relaxed speculative decoding, using this instrument as ground truth?
- What causes the Llama implementation-null deficit (−0.14, hard domain −0.46, responses ~1,185 vs 874 tokens): batched-verification bf16 numerics, cache cropping, EOS handling — and does it replicate at larger n?
- How does the instrument extend to training-time or serving-stack interventions (KV-cache eviction, context compaction, watermarking, distillation), where the provably-null implementation arm must be constructed differently?
- Would a pairwise judging protocol with the same calibration machinery resolve finer differences than the 7-point absolute scale, especially in ceiling-heavy domains like arithmetic?
