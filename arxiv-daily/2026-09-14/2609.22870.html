Why this matters02

FP8 RL promises up to 1.5× training throughput but collapses mid-run — this shows exactly why, and the fix costs two BF16 passes every 20 steps.

Direct engineering value for anyone building GRPO/DAPO pipelines: choosing scaling granularity, setting clip bounds, and stress-testing FP8 stability （对搭建高效 RL 训练管线有直接的工程参考价值）.

Critical edge · read with care03

The headline “1.5×” throughput comes from an offline benchmark that explicitly excludes the periodic BF16 reference passes — end-to-end gains with calibration overhead included are never reported.

Also unforgiving: DAPO recovery stalls 3.0–4.4 pts below BF16 (47.9 vs 50.9 AIME24); blockwise-“beats-BF16” is within noise; best-checkpoint reporting flatters unstable runs.

Methodology, distilled

Diagnosis → controlled probe → principled fix

Training Qwen3-8B with GRPO in full-pipeline FP8 (rollout and training quantized), the authors trace mid-training entropy surges to a precise loop: garbled high-entropy responses earn negative advantage, but FP8 probability error — amplified 1.7–2.9× by the ratio operation — pushes their importance ratios below the lower clip bound, zeroing exactly the gradients that should suppress them.

The failure loop
garbled output→ negative advantage→ FP8 noise pushes rt < 1−ε→ gradient zeroed→ never unlearned
⟲ garbled output proliferates → entropy surge (~100 steps in)

A controlled probe — relaxing the lower bound 0.8 → 0.6 — averts the surge but over-penalizes, saturating entropy early. So the fix must be two-sided:

Calibrated Clipping (Alg. 1): set the lower bound to match the BF16 clipping quantile over negative-advantage tokens; grid-search the upper bound until the FP8 positive/negative update ratio equals BF16’s. Recalibrate every 20 steps with two forward-only BF16 shadow passes; smoothed updates keep bounds stable.

Evaluated on VeRL + vLLM + TorchAO: GRPO (8B/32B, DeepScaleR, 500 steps, 8 benchmarks) and DAPO (14B, DAPO-Math-17K, AIME24 Avg@32), across tensorwise/rowwise/blockwise scaling, plus the Eurus coding suite.

Strengths

What the paper gets right

  • Pins a vague symptom (“FP8 RL is unstable”) to a precise, quantified mechanism — ratio-compounded noise over-clipping negative-advantage tokens — then fixes exactly that mechanism.
  • The fix is cheap and surgical: two forward-only BF16 passes every 20 steps plus a grid search; no change to model, quantization scheme, or RL objective.
  • Broad, honest coverage: 2 algorithms, 3 scales, 3 granularities, math + coding — and appendix negatives (CISPO, BAPO) instead of only flattering baselines.
  • Diagnoses why the naive fix fails (bound relaxation trades surge for over-penalization), motivating the two-sided calibration.

Limitations

Where to stay skeptical

  • No end-to-end wall-clock: the 1.5× figure excludes BF16 shadow-pass overhead; rollout+train+calibration vs pure BF16 is never measured.
  • Recovery is incomplete: tensorwise/rowwise stay ~1–2 pts under BF16 at 8B; DAPO AIME24 remains 3.0–4.4 pts below.
  • Narrow scope: dense Qwen models only, ≤32B, ≤20K context — no MoE, no agentic RL, no GSPO-style sequence-level clipping.
  • Reference bounds (0.8 / 1.24–1.28) encode task priors — effectively a new hyperparameter pair, admittedly untuned.
  • Small eval samples (Avg@16 AIME, Avg@4 elsewhere) + best-checkpoint reporting → treat ±1–2-pt parity cautiously.

Questions for further exploration

Open threads worth pulling

Q1What is the true end-to-end speedup (FP8 rollout + training + BF16 calibration + eval) versus pure BF16, and how does it scale with model size and context length?
Q2Does quantile-matching calibration transfer to sequence-level trust regions (GSPO) or batch-moment methods (M2PO), where token-level ratio noise is aggregated, not clipped per-token?
Q3Can the BF16 shadow passes be replaced by cheaper references — a learned model of the noise operator, token subsampling, or calibration reuse across steps?
Q4Does the same over-clipping failure mode appear in FP4 / INT8 regimes or pretraining-style clipped objectives — and does quantile calibration generalize?
Q5How does it interact with MoE models (routing adds another noise source) and with KL-regularized variants — the paper removes the KL term in all experiments?
