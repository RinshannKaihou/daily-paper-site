> **一句话总结：** 本文证明 SAE/Transcoder 等可解释替代网络（IRN）在语义等价的微小改写下主导特征会被大规模翻转（MAJOR 级改写下 top-20 特征重叠率最差跌 95%），并提出首个基于可达性分析的形式化验证框架来认证 IRN 与底层模型之间的"忠实性差距"，同时用验证感知训练将该认证上界收紧约 90%。

> **TL;DR:** The authors show across five open-weight model families (GPT-2, Gemma 2 2B, Gemma 3 1B, Llama 3.2 1B, R1-Distill-Qwen 1.5B) that sparse-autoencoder/transcoder interpretations flip under semantically minor paraphrases — on WikiText-2, the worst-layer top-20 Jaccard drops by 73% under a single WordNet synonym swap (MINOR) and 95% under LLM paraphrase (MAJOR), versus a Jaccard of 1 for a robust faithful interpretation. They then build the first formal verification framework for IRN faithfulness: reachability analysis (zonotopes, CORA) certifies a sound upper bound on the per-layer faithfulness gap, and verification-aware (set-based) training of OpenAI's GPT-2 SAE shrinks the certified bound by ~90% over the standard-trained baseline while making the dictionary 5x sparser (13.9 vs 74.4 active features at layer 6). The honest caveat: even after retraining, only 2–3 of the top-20 clean features are provably retained under adversarial inputs, partly because GPT-2 itself flips its next-token prediction on 21% of MINOR paraphrases.

## 核心贡献

- **实证揭示"解释翻转"攻击面**：在五个开源模型家族（GPT-2、Gemma 2 2B、Gemma 3 1B、Llama 3.2 1B、R1-Distill-Qwen 1.5B）的 SAE 与 Transcoder 上系统验证：仅替换一个 WordNet 同义词（MINOR 级扰动），最坏层的 top-20 激活特征 Jaccard 重叠率就下降 73%；LLM 改写（MAJOR 级）下下降 95%——即安全审计员读到的"人类可理解解释"几乎完全换掉，而模型本身未变。
- **首个 IRN 忠实性的形式化验证框架**：提出 δ-等价（δ-equivalence）定义，把"IRN 是否忠实复刻模型计算"化为差分网络 MLP_k − IRN_k 的可达性分析问题，用 zonotope 传播认证对抗场景下忠实性差距的可靠上界；并作为副产品给出 top-K 特征 Jaccard 指数的认证下界。可靠性证明经 Lean 4 机器检验。
- **关键工程突破使大规模验证可行**：利用 IRN 单隐层结构做"前瞻式区间算术 + 矩阵折叠"，避免显式构造 d_IRN 维 zonotope，把复杂度从朴素的 O(d_model·d_IRN²) 降到 O(d_model²·d_IRN)，使 Llama 3.2 1B 的 131,072 维字典在笔记本 GPU（RTX 3080 16GB）上可验证（每样本每层中位耗时约 1.4 分钟）。
- **验证感知训练大幅收紧认证界**：用可达集体积损失（set-based training, SET）微调 GPT-2 SAE，认证忠实性差距上界较标准训练缩小约 90%（相对误差体积约 10⁻⁷⁷⁹），且字典反而更稀疏（平均激活特征 13.9 vs 基线 74.4，−81%）。
- **提出整模型组合式验证愿景**：逐块验证 + 相邻块输入输出集包含关系的组合式框架，为全模型忠实性与鲁棒性认证铺路。

## Key Contributions

- **Empirically expose the "interpretation flip" attack surface**: Across five open-weight families (GPT-2, Gemma 2 2B, Gemma 3 1B, Llama 3.2 1B, R1-Distill-Qwen 1.5B), replacing a single content word with a WordNet synonym (MINOR) reduces the worst-layer top-20 active-feature Jaccard by 73%; LLM paraphrasing (MAJOR) reduces it by 95% — the human-readable interpretation a safety auditor reads off is almost entirely replaced while the model itself is unchanged.
- **First formal verification framework for IRN faithfulness**: A δ-equivalence definition turns "does the IRN replicate the model's computation" into a reachability-analysis problem on the difference network MLP_k − IRN_k, with zonotope propagation (CORA) certifying a sound upper bound δ̄ on the faithfulness gap under adversarial inputs; a certified lower bound J on the top-K Jaccard comes as a by-product. Soundness is machine-checked in Lean 4.
- **Engineering that makes dictionary-scale verification tractable**: A one-step look-ahead interval trick plus collapsing W_dec·diag(m)·W_enc into a single matrix avoids ever materializing the d_IRN-dimensional zonotope, cutting complexity from naive O(d_model·d_IRN²) to O(d_model²·d_IRN) — a factor d_IRN/d_model cheaper — which lets a 131,072-feature Llama 3.2 1B SAE be verified on a laptop RTX 3080 (median ~1.4 min per sample-block).
- **Verification-aware training closes much of the gap**: Fine-tuning OpenAI's GPT-2 SAE with a reachable-set volume loss (SET) shrinks the certified upper bound by ~90% versus standard training (relative error volume ~10⁻⁷⁷⁹ in 768 dims) while making the code sparser (13.9 vs 74.4 active features on average, −81%), not denser.
- **A compositional vision for whole-model verification**: Per-block verification combined with input/output set-containment checks between consecutive blocks, with concrete strategies (more verifier time, box enlargement, retraining) for blocks where containment fails.

## Methodology

The paper proceeds in three stages.

**1. Attack study (Sec. 3).** From WikiText-2 (1,000 sentences with maximally polysemous target words, split 600/200/200), three paraphrase levels are constructed: MINOR (one content word → WordNet synonym), MEDIUM (all content words before the target), MAJOR (Gemma 2 2B-it rephrases the sentence). The top-20 active-feature set of the IRN (SAE or transcoder, all layers) is compared between clean and perturbed inputs via the Jaccard index. Since an attack only witnesses one bad input, the measured overlap is an upper bound J̄ on the true worst-case Jaccard J*.

**2. Formal verification (Sec. 4).** The adversarial input set is modeled per token as the convex hull of synonym embeddings (Cartesian product over the prefix; causal masking means only pre-target words matter). Definition 1 calls an IRN at layer k δ-equivalent to the model if for all Ĥ_k in the propagated set, ‖Ĥ_k − SAE_k(Ĥ_k)‖_∞ ≤ δ (identity replaces MLP for SAEs; for transcoders ‖MLP_k(Ĥ_k) − TC_k(Ĥ_k)‖_∞ ≤ δ). The verifier (CORA, zonotope enclosures of nonlinearities) attempts to prove the difference network's output stays inside [−δ, δ]^d_model. A walk-then-bisect search (12 iterations, 10 s per call) brackets δ ≤ δ* ≤ δ̄, where the lower bound δ comes from PGD attacks (200 iterations, 4 restarts) inside the same hull. Two crucial optimizations exploit the single-hidden-layer IRN structure: exact pre-activation interval bounds via interval arithmetic, and redistribution so W_dec·diag(m_k)·W_enc collapses into one matrix — the high-dimensional zonotope is never built. The certified Jaccard lower bound J is derived from monotone activation preserving feature rankings: a clean top-K feature survives all attacks iff fewer than K competitors' upper pre-activation bounds reach its lower bound.

**3. Verification-aware training (Sec. 5).** OpenAI's GPT-2 SAE is fine-tuned (50 epochs, batch 8, Adam lr 1e-3) under four regimes: STD (clean reconstruction), PGD (adversarial training with 5-step PGD augmentation), SET (set-based training with reachable-set volume loss, weight τ = 0.1), each optionally restricted to rank-16 LoRA updates. All variants are verified with the identical per-sample-hull protocol on held-out data. TopK activations (Llama, Qwen SAEs) and JumpReLU (Gemma Scope) require dedicated enclosures: TopK features are partitioned into always-/never-/sometimes-active sets against a threshold band, with the K′ undecided contributions bounded by sorted extreme decoder columns; JumpReLU's discontinuity is enclosed zonotopically at the cost of extra conservativeness.

## Results & Highlights

- **Interpretation fragility (Tab. 1)**: Across all layers, MINOR synonym swaps cut the worst-layer top-20 Jaccard by 73% (mean across model/IRN pairs ranges 0.37–0.79) and MAJOR paraphrase cuts it by 95% (means 0.13–0.54). Example: GPT-2 SAE drops from a perfect-overlap baseline to J_min = 0.27 (MINOR) and 0.05 (MAJOR); the GPT-2 transcoder is the most robust pair (J_min = 0.70, J_mean = 0.79 at MINOR).
- **Safety-auditor deception demo (Appendix A)**: On Gemma 2 2B layer-16 Gemma Scope SAE, a PGD attack inside the "someone→somebody" synonym hull drives all four biological "culture" features (cellular/molecular biology, neurophysiology, biochemical extraction, mold growth) out of the top-8, leaving generic workplace/accountability features; the top-8 Jaccard falls to J = 0.23 — a benign-looking reading of a pathogen-culture request.
- **Certified faithfulness gap (Fig. 4)**: For GPT-2, a substantial gap δ ≤ δ* ≤ δ̄ is verified at every layer, widening with depth (layer 1 SAE: δ̄ < 1; later layers rise by orders of magnitude), and both bounds rise together — so the trend is not a verifier artifact. Transcoders show larger gaps than SAEs, as expected from their harder reconstruction target.
- **Verification-aware training (Fig. 5, layer 6)**: SET reduces the verified upper bound δ̄ by ≈90% versus STD; given d_model = 768, the relative error volume is ≈10⁻⁷⁷⁹ of the baseline. The gain holds across layers (Fig. 13) and under uniform-radius ablation: at ε = 0.1, SET's median δ̄ = 94.60 vs STD 320.36 (−70.5%); at ε = 0.5, 18,102 vs 51,200 (−64.6%) (Tab. 6).
- **Sparsity is not sacrificed (Tab. 7)**: SET activates on average 13.9 features at the clean input vs 74.4 for STD (−81%, a 5x sparser code); over the whole perturbation hull, SET+LoRA admits the fewest active features (4,383.8 vs 8,433.2 for STD, −48%). Only SET+LoRA densifies the clean code (143.8) while still being most stable over the hull.
- **Honest Jaccard accounting (Fig. 6)**: Even after retraining, only 2–3 of the top-20 clean features are provably retained; the paper attributes the remaining flip to model fragility — GPT-2 itself changes its next-token prediction for 21% of MINOR paraphrases.
- **Scalability beyond GPT-2**: The largest IRN verified is Llama 3.2 1B's TopK SAE (d_IRN = 131,072, K = 32): at layer 1 under MINOR, median attack bound δ = 0.055 vs certified δ̄ = 0.225, with the gap widening across all 16 layers (Fig. 9, Tab. 10). R1-Distill-Qwen 1.5B's SAE is an order of magnitude less faithful than Llama 3.2 1B's (Fig. 12), and JumpReLU-based Gemma Scope SAEs suffer looser bounds due to the discontinuous gate enclosure (Fig. 11).
- **Cost**: median verification time ~1.4 min per (sample, block) on an RTX 3080 laptop GPU with MATLAB/CORA + Python/PyTorch.

## Authors & Credibility

Both authors are from the Technical University of Munich: Tobias Ladner and Matthias Althoff. Althoff's group is the maintainer of CORA, the reachability-analysis toolbox used here, and has a long track record in formal neural-network verification (VNN-COMP summaries, set-based training — Koller, Ladner, Althoff, TMLR 2025); Ladner has prior work on automatic network reduction for verification (TMLR 2025). The paper is a preprint under review, funded by DFG project AL 1185/33-1. Credibility is strengthened by an unusually strong reproducibility posture: all models/IRNs are public HuggingFace artifacts, exact hardware/software/hyperparameters are listed, code and Lean 4 formalizations of the soundness proofs are in the supplementary, and an AI-use statement discloses generative-AI assistance (implementation, figures, Lean proofs) with manual double-checking and unit-test coverage. The choice of verifier (the authors' own CORA) is a mild but transparent conflict of interest, mitigated by the fact that the framework is verifier-agnostic.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Genuinely novel positioning — prior faithfulness critiques of SAEs (Heap et al.: auto-interpretability scores fail to distinguish trained vs random transformers; Paulo & Belrose: SAEs learn different features) and adversarial fragility (Li et al. 2026) are empirical, and prior formal XAI (VeriX, Bassan & Katz) covered post-hoc explainers or vision-model circuits (Hadad et al.), not IRNs of LLMs. The δ-equivalence formulation and the certified Jaccard lower bound are, to my knowledge, firsts. The zonotope-collapse trick is a real technical contribution beyond plumbing.
- **Rigor**: Sound bounds with Lean-machine-checked soundness proofs, both-sided reporting (δ from attacks and δ̄ from verification everywhere), and per-sample hulls rather than a hand-picked global radius are all above the bar. The 73%/95% Jaccard-drop headline, however, is attack-derived (upper bounds on damage), and the paper is admirably explicit about that.
- **Weak spots**: (1) The training improvements (Fig. 5/6) are demonstrated only on GPT-2's SAE at one layer plus a per-layer sweep — the 1–2.6B models get verification but no SET retraining, so the headline "−90%" claim is single-model. (2) The certified Jaccard lower bound remains dismal (2–3 of top-20 retained) and the blame is shifted to model fragility via the 21% next-token-flip statistic — plausible, but a controlled comparison on a deliberately robustified GPT-2 is missing. (3) Several verified δ̄ values in Tab. 6 hit the search cap (51,200), meaning "unverified beyond this"; medians are meaningful but max columns are saturating artifacts. (4) The threat model's convex hull treats every WordNet synonym as meaning-preserving — for polysemous targets (culture, bank) some "synonyms" in the wrong sense are not semantic equivalences, so the input set over-covers. (5) Verification is per-layer with hulls built from propagated samples; attention layers are never enclosed, so the compositional whole-model story (Sec. 6) remains a vision — the authors themselves note their hulls are still ~5x too small at GPT-2 layer 6 (Tab. 5). (6) An odd data quirk: rank-32 LoRA reconstruction (766.2) is ~6x worse than rank-16 (122.8) in Tab. 9, suggesting unstable training runs that are not investigated.
- **Reproducibility**: Excellent — public artifacts, code and Lean proofs released, exact hyperparameters, per-sample-hull protocol fully specified.
- **Significance**: High for interpretability-as-safety: if regulators or auditors act on SAE feature readouts, this paper shows both that those readouts are attackable with a synonym and that the attack surface is now formally quantifiable. The negative space matters too: it is early quantitative evidence that current IRNs do not faithfully replicate model computation in adversarial neighborhoods.
- **Red flags**: The title's "misery" is earned by the data, not hype, but readers should note the main body quietly centers GPT-2 while the abstract implies all five families equally; the teaser Fig. 1 flip is a targeted PGD point inside the hull, not a naturally occurring sentence someone→somebody itself achieves (the actual synonym swap alone gives a milder, though still large, drop).

## Strengths

- First formal, sound guarantees for mechanistic interpretability of LLMs, with machine-checked proofs — a category creation rather than an increment.
- The scalability engineering (interval look-ahead + matrix collapse; TopK and JumpReLU enclosures) turns a theoretically NP-hard problem into something runnable on a laptop GPU for 131K-feature dictionaries.
- Honest both-sided bound reporting (δ ≤ δ* ≤ δ̄, J ≤ J* ≤ J̄) throughout; the paper refuses to advertise only the attack numbers.
- Verification-aware training yields a rare win-win: ~90% tighter certified faithfulness bound AND a ~5x sparser (more interpretable) dictionary, without an explicit sparsity penalty.
- The safety-auditor deception example (pathogen "culture" → workplace "culture") makes the stakes concrete and is fully documented with Neuronpedia feature IDs.
- Strong reproducibility: released code, Lean formalizations, exact configurations, public artifacts only.

## Limitations

- Faithfulness-improving training is evaluated only on GPT-2 (124M); whether SET transfers to Gemma/Llama/Qwen IRNs with non-elementwise activations is untested.
- Certified feature preservation stays weak: only 2–3 of the top-20 clean features are provably retained, so a safety auditor still cannot rely on interpretation invariance even after retraining.
- Per-layer verification with per-sample hulls sidesteps attention propagation; the compositional whole-model pipeline (Sec. 6) is explicitly future work, and current hulls are ~5x too small for it.
- Verifier conservativeness is uneven: TopK and JumpReLU enclosures leave a wide δ–δ̄ gap (authors themselves argue for verification-friendly IRN architectures), and several δ̄ searches saturate at the cap.
- WordNet-synonym "semantic equivalence" is leaky for polysemous targets, making the input set an over-approximation of truly meaning-preserving paraphrases.
- Only WikiText-2 / English / small models (≤2.6B); no reasoning-heavy or safety-specific corpora, and no comparison to certified-defense baselines for LLM safety (e.g., Kumar et al. 2024).

## Relevance

这篇论文直接命中可解释性与安全交叉方向的核心关切：它形式化了 SAE/Transcoder 解释在对抗改写下的不可靠性，并给出首个可认证的忠实性上界——对于关注机械可解释性能否作为可信安全审计工具这一问题的研究非常有参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does SET-style verification-aware training transfer to JumpReLU/TopK dictionaries (Gemma Scope, Llama Scope), or do discontinuous and input-dependent gates fundamentally cap the achievable certified faithfulness?
- If the underlying model is first adversarially robustified (e.g., synonym-augmented fine-tuning of GPT-2), how much does the certified Jaccard lower bound J rise when the IRN is then retrained — i.e., how much of the residual gap is model fragility vs IRN approximation error?
- Can the compositional scheme (Sec. 6) be made practical with tighter attention enclosures (e.g., Bonaert et al.-style certification) so that whole-model faithfulness is verified end-to-end rather than per-layer?
- Is the convex hull over token synonym embeddings the right threat model — would hulls over embedding-space ℓ∞ balls or paraphrase-generator output sets give tighter (or looser) certified bounds?
- Can a persistent large difference set be operationalized as an automatic signal for "the dictionary is too small" (dynamic feature expansion), as the conclusion speculates?
- What happens on safety-relevant corpora (harmful-request datasets) rather than WikiText-2 — does the certified faithfulness gap widen where auditors need it most?
