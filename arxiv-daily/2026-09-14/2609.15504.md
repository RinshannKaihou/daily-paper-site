> **一句话总结：** 独立复现 Orthrus（冻结自回归骨干 + 扩散视图的并行解码架构）并检验其"严格无损"声明：在 BF16 精度下，1,190 条提示的生成轨迹与 Qwen3-1.7B 自回归基线完全一致的仅约 45%/43%，而同一评测在 FP32 下全部 1,190 条轨迹精确匹配，且 GSM8K/HumanEval/IFEval 分数在噪声范围内不变——说明"无损性"实际取决于数值精度，应与下游任务表现分开评估。

> **TL;DR:** This paper independently reproduces Orthrus, a hybrid autoregressive-diffusion parallel decoder built on a frozen Qwen3-1.7B backbone, and stress-tests its "strictly lossless" claim by exact string comparison of generated token trajectories. Across 1,190 greedy-decoded prompts from 12 domains, the sequence match rate versus the autoregressive Qwen3-1.7B baseline is only 45% (authors' released checkpoint) and 43% (the paper's independently retrained model) under BF16 — but 100% (1,190/1,190) under FP32. Downstream GSM8K / HumanEval / IFEval scores stay within noise (e.g., GSM8K flexible exact match 0.4147 vs. baseline 0.4003), showing that benchmark parity does not prove inference equivalence, and that "lossless" claims must specify precision and an operational equivalence criterion.

## 核心贡献

- **独立复现并重训 Orthrus**：完整实现了 Orthrus 的训练与推理流程，用 Qwen3-1.7B 贪心解码自生成的 4,113,358 条"on-policy 蒸馏"语料训练出 Orthrus-1.7B-final，在 12 个评测域中的 10 个域上 Tokens Per Forward（TPF）超过官方发布的 checkpoint（如 wmt ru-en 域 2.46±0.12 vs 2.04±0.09）。
- **证伪 BF16 下的严格无损性**：1,190 条提示上，Orthrus 与冻结自回归模型的轨迹完全匹配率仅 45%（官方 checkpoint）/ 43%（自训模型），发散率高达 55%/57%；按域分解差异巨大（gec-en 高达 0.88，poetry-en 低至 0.08–0.11）。
- **揭示发散的统计规律**：发散轨迹的参考模型条件困惑度显著更高（1.28 vs 匹配轨迹的 1.10–1.11）；逻辑回归显示匹配概率与 log 困惑度强负相关（β₁=−8.10 与 −10.92，p≤3×10⁻¹¹），且该关联在控制回复长度与文本域后依然显著。
- **定位到数值精度这一根因**：保持模型、解码流程与提示完全不变，仅将 BF16 换成 FP32 后，两个 Orthrus 变体在全部 1,190 条轨迹上实现精确匹配（1.00±0.00），证明发散源于有限精度数值效应而非共识机制本身。
- **区分"轨迹等价"与"任务表现"**：lm-eval-harness 上（GSM8K、HumanEval、IFEval）轨迹发散未带来系统性退化，自训 Orthrus 在三项基准的点估计甚至全部高于自回归基线（作者明确指出这在报告的不确定度下不构成统计显著提升），说明基准分数相同不能作为推理等价的证据。

## Key Contributions

- **Independent reproduction and retraining of Orthrus**: the authors reimplement the full training and inference pipeline and train their own Orthrus-1.7B-final on a 4,113,358-sample teacher-generated distillation corpus (greedy-decoded continuations of the frozen Qwen3-1.7B itself), achieving higher Tokens Per Forward than the released `chiennv/Orthrus-Qwen3-1.7B` checkpoint in 10 of 12 evaluation domains.
- **A key training-data observation**: because the diffusion view is trained under teacher forcing, training on generic human-written continuations does not reproduce the states the frozen AR model actually encounters during its own generation; an on-policy, self-distilled corpus fixes this distribution mismatch and improves parallel-decoding efficiency.
- **Empirical refutation of strict losslessness under BF16**: exact trajectory match rates against the AR reference are 0.45±0.03 (released checkpoint) and 0.43±0.03 (independent model) over 1,190 prompts, with divergence concentrated in high response-conditional-perplexity generations (logistic regression β₁ = −8.10, 95% CI [−10.50, −5.71], p = 3×10⁻¹¹; and β₁ = −10.92, 95% CI [−13.60, −8.24], p = 1.3×10⁻¹⁵, controlling for length and domain).
- **Precision as the deciding factor**: repeating the identical evaluation under FP32 yields exact trajectory matching on all 1,190 prompts for both variants (match rate 1.00±0.00), attributing BF16 divergence to finite-precision arithmetic rather than the consensus mechanism.
- **Decoupling trajectory equivalence from task scores**: on lm-eval-harness, Orthrus shows no systematic degradation despite divergence — the authors' honest point is that identical/statistically indistinguishable benchmark scores cannot establish exact inference equivalence, and small numerical perturbations can even nudge outputs toward benchmark-preferred answers.

## Methodology

The study proceeds in four stages.

**1. Independent training.** The authors distill the frozen `Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B`: prompts (50–1,000 characters) are taken from a mixture of public HuggingFace datasets, and responses are generated by the AR model itself with greedy decoding, producing 4,113,358 prompt–response pairs that follow the model's own prediction trajectories. Training uses 8×H100 GPUs (PyTorch 2.13.0, Transformers 5.8.0): 1 epoch, lr 2×10⁻⁴, batch size 10, cross-entropy loss, block size 8, 32 blocks, max sequence length 3,072 tokens. The configuration deliberately differs from the original Orthrus recipe, making the independently trained `Orthrus-1.7B-final` a control for checkpoint-specific artifacts.

**2. Trajectory evaluation.** Generation trajectories (greedy decoding, `do_sample=False`, `temperature=0.0`, `max_new_tokens=128`, eager attention, BF16, one RTX 3090) are compared token-by-token against the frozen AR model on a curated set of 1,190 prompts spanning 12 domains (code, code-ru, creative-en, gec-en, gec-ru, math, math-ru, poetry-en, poetry-ru, qa, qa-ru, wmt ru-en; ~100 prompts each). Sequence match rate and diverging-trajectory rate are reported with 95% confidence intervals, overall and per domain.

**3. Statistical association analysis.** For each generation, the response-conditional perplexity PPL(y|x) under the reference Qwen3-1.7B is computed. A logistic regression `logit P(match) = β₀ + β₁·log PPL(y|x) + β₂·L + γ_D` (response length L and domain effects γ_D as controls, fit with statsmodels) tests whether perplexity predicts exact matching.

**4. Downstream and precision controls.** The AR baseline and both Orthrus variants are evaluated with lm-eval-harness on GSM8K (flexible exact match), HumanEval (pass@1), and IFEval (prompt-level loose/strict accuracy). Finally, the entire trajectory evaluation is repeated under FP32 with everything else held fixed, isolating numerical precision as the variable.

## Results & Highlights

- **BF16 trajectory divergence**: across 1,190 prompts, sequence match rate vs. the AR Qwen3-1.7B reference is **0.45±0.03** for the released `Orthrus-Qwen3-1.7B` and **0.43±0.03** for the independently trained `Orthrus-1.7B-final`; diverging-trajectory rates are 0.55±0.03 and 0.57±0.03 (Table 3).
- **Large per-domain spread under BF16** (Table 4): match rates range from **0.88±0.07** (gec-en, grammatical error correction) down to **0.08±0.05** (poetry-en for the independent model; 0.11±0.06 for the released checkpoint) and 0.11–0.12 for creative-en — domains where the reference model itself is less certain diverge most.
- **FP32 restores exact losslessness**: under FP32, sequence match rate is **1.00±0.00 on all 1,190 prompts** for both Orthrus variants (Table 8), versus 43–45% under BF16 — the entire divergence is attributable to finite-precision arithmetic.
- **Perplexity predicts divergence** (Tables 5–6): mean response-conditional PPL is 1.11±0.01 / 1.10±0.01 for matching trajectories vs. 1.28±0.01 / 1.28±0.01 for diverging ones; logistic regression gives β₁ = **−8.10** (95% CI [−10.50, −5.71], p = 3×10⁻¹¹) for the released checkpoint and β₁ = **−10.92** (95% CI [−13.60, −8.24], p = 1.3×10⁻¹⁵) for the independent model, after controlling for response length and domain.
- **No systematic downstream degradation** (Table 7, lm-eval-harness): GSM8K flexible exact match — Qwen3-1.7B 0.4003±0.0135, released Orthrus 0.4238±0.0136, independent Orthrus **0.4147±0.0136**; HumanEval pass@1 — 0.4024±0.0384 (Qwen3) vs. 0.4146±0.0386 (independent Orthrus); IFEval prompt-level strict — 0.1682±0.0161 (Qwen3) vs. 0.1830±0.0166 (independent Orthrus). The independent Orthrus beats the AR baseline's point estimate on all three benchmarks, but the authors explicitly caution these differences are not statistically significant given the reported uncertainty.
- **On-policy distillation improves parallel efficiency** (Table 2): the independently trained model achieves higher Tokens Per Forward than the released checkpoint in 10 of 12 domains (e.g., wmt ru-en **2.46±0.12 vs 2.04±0.09**, code 3.44±0.13 vs 3.24±0.20, gec-ru 3.23±0.17 vs 2.48±0.18), while the released checkpoint wins in math (**8.08±0.65 vs 4.99±0.16**) and gec-en (5.00±0.30 vs 4.23±0.17).

## Authors & Credibility

The paper is by Ilya Koziev, Leonid Sinev, and Ivan Oseledets (affiliations not listed in the preprint text; Oseledets is a well-known figure in numerical linear algebra, tensor decompositions, and efficient ML, and Sinev's prior work includes LLM compression — a background that fits this precision-focused reproduction study). Credibility signals are strong: the analysis uses confidence intervals throughout, controls for confounders via logistic regression (statsmodels), retraints the architecture independently to rule out checkpoint-specific artifacts, and runs a clean FP32 control that isolates the cause. The authors are refreshingly honest — they explicitly state that the benchmark advantages of their Orthrus variant should not be read as statistically significant, and they position the work as clarifying a limitation rather than debunking Orthrus's utility. The paper is a preprint under review; no code release is mentioned in the text, though exact software versions, decoding arguments, dataset sources, and training hyperparameters are documented.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: No new method is proposed; the value is a rigorous empirical critique connecting speculative decoding to the LLM-inference-nondeterminism literature (Yuan et al., 2025). The framing — "an algorithm can be lossless in principle yet lossy in finite-precision practice" — is a genuinely novel and useful contribution to the acceleration literature.
- **Rigor**: High. Independent retraining, 95% CIs on all rates, regression with length/domain controls, and an FP32 ablation that turns a correlation into a clean causal isolation of precision. The consensus-failure diagnosis stops at "finite-precision effects" without identifying the responsible layer or operation — the authors openly defer this mechanistic attribution to future work, but it leaves the result more phenomenological than explanatory.
- **Reproducibility**: Good on paper (exact versions: PyTorch 2.8.0+cu128 / CUDA 12.8 / Transformers 5.8.1, RTX 3090, eager attention, greedy decoding, full per-domain dataset sources), but a notable gap is the version mismatch between training (PyTorch 2.13.0, CUDA 13.3.73, H100) and evaluation (PyTorch 2.8.0, CUDA 12.8, RTX 3090) environments, and no released code is mentioned.
- **Red flags / specific weaknesses**: (a) Trajectory evaluation caps generation at `max_new_tokens=128` — divergence compounds over length, so real-world long generations likely match at even lower rates; (b) all experiments use a single 1.7B backbone and a single GPU with eager attention, while production stacks use flash/sdpa kernels on different hardware, both of which change numerics and are untested; (c) per-domain cells rest on only 90–100 prompts from sometimes niche sources (e.g., poetry-ru from closed sources, MERA-derived sets), so per-domain rates carry wide CIs; (d) the TPF comparison against the released checkpoint is confounded — different data, hyperparameters, and software stacks — so "higher TPF in 10/12 domains" is not an apples-to-apples recipe validation; (e) no wall-clock latency measurements: TPF is an efficiency proxy, not measured speedup; (f) the original Orthrus paper's own evaluation setting (precision, hardware) is not re-audited here, so it remains unverified whether the original "lossless" claim was ever demonstrated under the BF16 configurations practitioners would actually deploy.
- **Significance**: High methodological importance — it forces any "lossless" acceleration claim to specify both an operational equivalence criterion (token-level trajectory match vs. benchmark parity) and the numerical precision under which it holds. For practitioners: Orthrus under BF16 is de facto a low-loss (not lossless) accelerator whose deviations concentrate exactly where the reference model is least confident.

## Strengths

- Independent reimplementation plus independent retraining rules out "the released checkpoint is just broken" as the explanation; the precision effect appears in both models.
- The FP32 control experiment is the paper's cleanest contribution — identical models, prompts, and decoding, differing only in dtype, fully explains the divergence.
- Statistical treatment well above the norm for systems papers: 95% CIs on all reported rates, and a logistic regression that separates the perplexity effect from response length and domain confounders.
- The "losses become gains" section is conceptually sharp: identical benchmark scores do not imply equivalent inference, and the authors resist overclaiming their own benchmark wins.
- Actionable training insight (on-policy self-distillation data improves TPF over the released checkpoint in most domains) is a useful byproduct.

## Limitations

- Mechanism unlocated: which operation (attention, normalization, KV-cache reads, the consensus comparison itself) produces the BF16 token flips is not identified; mitigation strategies (e.g., selective FP32 casting of sensitive ops) cannot be derived from the current results.
- Single scale, single GPU, single attention implementation: no evidence yet that conclusions transfer to 7B/70B backbones, H100/B200 numerics, or flash-attention kernels, all of which practitioners actually use.
- Short-horizon evaluation only (128 new tokens, greedy decoding); match behavior under longer generations or sampling (temperature > 0) — where "losslessness" is even harder to define — is unexplored.
- The downstream suite (3 benchmarks) is too small to detect subtle degradations; the observed Orthrus-over-baseline point estimates, while honestly flagged as noise, also illustrate the suite's low statistical power.
- No latency/wall-clock speedup or memory measurements; the practical cost of running FP32 to recover exact losslessness (which would erase the acceleration benefit) is not quantified.

## Relevance

与高效 LLM 推理和平行/推测解码方向直接相关：本文给出了"无损加速声明必须在指定数值精度与等价判据下评估"的关键警示，其轨迹级精确匹配评测方法和 on-policy 蒸馏训练技巧对并行解码、扩散语言模型及推理加速研究都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Which specific computational stage (attention, layer norm, KV-cache reads, or the consensus logit comparison) dominates BF16 divergence, and would selective FP32 casting of that stage recover exact losslessness at minimal speedup cost?
- How does the match rate scale beyond 128 generated tokens, and what happens under sampling (temperature > 0) — is trajectory-level "losslessness" even a well-posed criterion there?
- Does the BF16 divergence magnitude persist across hardware (H100 vs. RTX 3090), attention kernels (flash/sdpa vs. eager), and larger backbones (7B, 70B)?
- Can a runtime divergence predictor be built — e.g., using the AR model's top-1/top-2 logit gap or running PPL estimate at accepted tokens — to fall back to pure AR decoding before a flip occurs?
- Is the perplexity association causal in the sense that high-entropy reference generations sit near argmax decision boundaries where one-ULP differences flip tokens, and could consensus thresholds be tightened exactly there?
