Critical edge

01

Methodology

declared local linear model

Everything lives inside a declared local linear model: at a fixed layer, hidden state h = Wz + ε with unit-column writer matrix W (e.g. an SAE decoder), reader system R, cross-Gram C = RᵀW, and deployed safety head u with margin M(x) = uᵀh − τ. Semantic fibers — audited translation equivalence classes — are fixed externally; the within-fiber contrast span is the object everything is computed on.

Eight results form one pipeline:

  1. Drift identity same-fiber margin drift = a cross-Gram row applied to the within-fiber contrast; invariance ⇔ annihilator condition.
  2. Budget constants ρ (feature coords), κ (hidden distance, saturates at ∥u∥), σ_Δ (stochastic contrasts) — via Cauchy–Schwarz duality, non-interchangeable.
  3. Decision certificates worst-case drift as a support function of the threat set, robust to an estimated dictionary.
  4. Leverage duality χ² = 1/ℓ − 1; feasibility (χ = 0) ⇔ w_s ∉ span(W_B); conditioning ∥r_inv∥ = 1/sin θ.
  5. Matched-exposure counterexample identical ρ, opposite χ — black-box disagreement rates cannot determine repairability.
  6. Order-swap identity untied-setter commutator has an exact cross-Gram form; its residual is a causal-consistency test.
  7. Closed-form repairs ridge reader r_λ on the exposure–conditioning frontier; least-change projection u_D with transfer bound.
  8. Floors & non-identification cross-talk ≥ n(n−r)/r (equality iff equal leverage); the Gram spectrum leaves block exposure free.

Empirics: deterministic PCG64 synthetic audits (66 rotated geometries, 8,000 fibers each, train/val/test split by fiber), 24 trained toy superposition models, and one real multilingual-encoder pilot.

  • Generalization — across 66 geometries, χ/ρ predicts the held-out repair floor (Spearman 0.972); on the removable subset, log∥r_inv∥ predicts usable repair (0.889).
  • Causal-interface test — the order-swap residual predicts out-of-sample three-control composition error at median Spearman 0.964 vs 0.269 for the static cross-Gram baseline (360 held-out triples).
  • Closed-form repairs, untouched test fibers — least-change projection: mean |drift| 1.394 → 0.008, directed unsafe rate 28.79% → 2.16% (0.00% at fixed τ = 0.65); ridge reader (λ = 3.00): 0.506% → 0.164%.
  • Real encoder (FLORES-200, d = 384) — the exact invariant reader (norm 4.9) is harmful out of sample — English-score correlation falls to 0.26; the validation-selected regularized reader (norm 1.07) cuts drift 0.122 → 0.114 (audited, 7%) and 0.213 → 0.186 (never-audited, 13%) while holding correlation at 0.92.
  • Trained frames — superposition level predicts vulnerability only under uniform importance (R² = 0.82); with decaying importance the aggregate correlation collapses (R² = 0.11) and important features get protected (0.248× median interference).
  • Arithmetic saturation — identities hold to 4.41×10⁻¹⁶; signed Gram-entry recovery at r = 0.9998.
03

Strengths

  • Converts a vague deployment symptom ("refuses in English, complies in translation") into closed-form quantities (ρ, κ, σ_Δ, χ, ∥r_inv∥, order-swap residual) computable before any reader is trained.
  • The feasibility/conditioning split is the paper's best idea: one number, 1/sin θ, explains why an algebraically perfect fix (99.99% BAcc) is operationally harmful (52.16%).
  • Exemplary evaluation discipline — out-of-sample tasks never fitted on, validation-selected operating points, one frozen test evaluation, matched-exposure constructions isolating geometry.
  • Non-identification theorem plus the trained-frame experiment jointly locate when aggregate superposition–vulnerability correlations hold — a genuinely useful negative result.
  • Byte-reproducible pipeline (SHA-256 manifests, per-identity regression suite); candid ethics and scope statements.
04

Limitations

  • Nearly all quantitative validation is synthetic; the single real-model audit covers only the covariance form — one layer, one benign score, 300 sentences, 7 languages.
  • No head-to-head comparison with established multilingual-safety or erasure baselines (LEACE, INLP, steering transport); the "baselines" are the deployed reader and a static cross-Gram summary.
  • Real-encoder repair gains are small in absolute terms (0.122 → 0.114 audited; 0.213 → 0.186 held-out).
  • Linear local model, scalar safety writer, and given block structure are assumed, not tested; the cone-valued (multi-output) extension is untested.
  • The audit presupposes externally audited translation fibers — itself the costly, governance-heavy step the paper brackets out.
  • Dense notation (twelve results, three constants, two reader systems, tied/untied duals); hard to read without the frame-theory background.
05

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does the χ / ∥r_inv∥ triage reproduce on a production LLM refusal head with genuinely harmful-prompt translation fibers, estimated from far fewer audited pairs than the synthetic 4,000-fiber training sets?
  2. What does the layer-resolved χ profile look like across a real multilingual LLM's depth — is there a practical "head-side repair window," and does it coincide with layers chosen heuristically by multilingual steering work?
  3. Do existing repairs — BabelSteering-style transport, LEACE/INLP erasure — land in the regime the calculus predicts for them, and can the order-swap residual explain documented steering unreliability better than steering-vector geometry alone?
  4. Does reader-norm noise amplification (2η∥r_inv∥) interact with int8/fp8 quantization to make ill-conditioning the dominant failure mode even for float64-safe geometries?
  5. Can the equal-leverage rank floor n(n−r)/r be used constructively — as a regularizer or dictionary-design objective for SAE training that deliberately allocates leverage to safety-relevant features?
