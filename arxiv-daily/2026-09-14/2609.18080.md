> **一句话总结：** 本文在 Gemma-2-9B-Instruct 的真/假事实核验任务上把一个已部署的真值探针（TTPD 方向）分解到 GemmaScope SAE 特征空间，发现探针几何对齐的特征与模型梯度敏感的特征重合度仅约 12%（Spearman ρ=0.10）；消融"共享特征"可使模型输出翻转高达 27%（等量 probe-only 仅 6%、随机仅 1%），而结合激活统计的学习式稀疏掩码（17.6%）比纯几何 top-16（6.1%）有效近 3 倍——证明探针权重的几何投影本身不能定位模型真正因果使用的特征。

> **TL;DR:** On Gemma-2-9B-Instruct with a replicated TTPD truth probe (AUROC 0.98) in the instructed deception setting of Long et al. (2025), the authors decompose the probe direction into 16,384 GemmaScope SAE features and show that geometrically probe-aligned features and gradient-sensitive features barely overlap (top-200: 22 shared vs 2.44 chance; Spearman ρ = 0.10). Ablating shared features flips True/False behavior on up to 26.9% of statements vs 6.1% for probe-only and 1.0% for random baselines (coherence 1.00), and an activation-aware sparse mask (17.6%) nearly triples the geometric top-16 baseline's flip rate (6.1%). The message: probe readouts and behavioral drivers are dissociable, and combining probe alignment with activation statistics — not geometry alone — recovers causally potent features.

## 核心贡献

- 提出一个特征级因果验证框架：把已部署的真值探针（TTPD 方向）投影到 SAE 解码器基中，同时用（1）探针对齐度 |w·d_i| 与（2）模型 True/False 边际的梯度敏感度对同一组 SAE 特征独立排序，再通过受控消融直接检验其行为效应。
- 量化证明"可解码性 ≠ 因果性"在特征层面成立：两套排序重合度仅 11–14%（远超随机但占比极小），消融共享特征的行为翻转率（最高 26.9%）数倍于等规模 probe-only（6.1%）与随机（1.0%）特征集，且输出连贯性全程保持 1.00。
- 识别出关键的方法学区分：失败的是"无激活的纯几何对齐"，而非探针信息本身——把探针对齐与特征激活幅度结合的学习式稀疏掩码（翻转率 17.6%）显著优于几何 top-16（6.1%），接近共享集与全方向消融的量级。
- 给出方向级"天花板"对照：完整消融 TTPD 方向本身翻转 18.1% 的输出，仅 16 个共享特征就恢复其中约三分之二（12.1%），说明该方向的因果效应集中于共享特征而非弥散。
- 提供一个诚实的边界案例：在奖励破解（reward-hacking）代码探针上，SAE 重构仅保留原始探针边际的 26%，探针对齐特征消融几乎不影响原始探针（原始激活上翻转近零）——使用该方法前必须先验证探针信号确实存在于 SAE 基中。

## Key Contributions

- A feature-level causal-validation framework that decomposes a deployed True/False truth probe into a sparse-autoencoder (SAE) feature basis, ranks the same features twice — by geometric probe alignment |w·d_i| and by gradient sensitivity of the model's True/False margin — and tests both rankings with direct ablation interventions under an output-coherence gate.
- Quantifies the probe-behavior dissociation at feature level: the two rankings share only ~11–14% of features (orders of magnitude above chance, yet a small minority), and ablating shared (probe ∩ gradient) features flips model output far more (up to 26.9%) than size-matched probe-only (6.1%) or random (1.0%) sets, with coherence pinned at 1.00.
- Identifies precisely which attribution scheme fails: activation-free geometric alignment is a poor guide to behavioral relevance, while an activation-aware learned sparse mask that combines decoder alignment with feature activations (17.6% flip rate) beats the geometric top-16 (6.1%) by nearly 3x — probe-based attribution is salvageable, geometry alone is not.
- Provides a direction-level ceiling baseline: ablating the full TTPD direction tG flips 18.1% of outputs; the 16-feature shared set recovers about two-thirds of this effect (12.1%), showing the SAE decomposition localizes (rather than disperses) the direction's causal effect.
- Includes an honest boundary-case diagnostic on a reward-hacking code probe where the SAE reconstruction preserves only 26% of the raw probe margin, and probe-aligned feature ablations flip ~1/3 of reconstruction-space probe predictions but near-zero raw-activation predictions — establishing a validity precondition for the method.

## Methodology

The pipeline operates on Gemma-2-9B-Instruct (bfloat16) in the instructed deception setting of Long et al. (2025): factual statements with Truthful/Deceptive/Neutral instructions, where under deception the model is told to answer incorrectly. Activations are taken at the final prompt token of layer 20 (adjacent to Long et al.'s layer-21 accuracy peak; layer 20 chosen to match the GemmaScope SAE of width 16,384 from Lieberum et al., 2024). The probe is the replicated TTPD truth direction tG of Bürger et al. (2024) — layer-20 activations regressed on truth label and statement polarity, truth component retained — reproduced with AUROC 0.98; the LR probe variant is excluded because its top features are hyperparameter-dependent (top-30 Jaccard 0.07–0.62 across regularization strengths).

Stage 2 projects the probe weight w onto SAE decoder directions d_i to get structural probe-alignment scores c_i = w·d_i (activation-free). Stage 3 ranks features by dataset-averaged gradients of the behavioral margin M(x) = logit(True) − logit(False) with respect to feature activations. Stage 4 intervenes by ablating feature sets — subtracting reconstructed contributions f_i·d_i from the residual stream — for probe-attributed, gradient-sensitive, shared, probe-only, and size-matched random sets, at K ∈ {32,…,1024}; a learned sparse mask (per-feature sigmoid weights, ℓ1 penalty, optimized with Adam to reconstruct the probe margin from weighted feature contributions) yields an activation-aware feature set. Stage 5 partitions features into Shared (top-K probe ∩ top-K gradient), Probe-only, and Random groups at matched size m, and compares behavior flip rate (sign change of the True/False margin), probe-readout shift, and coherence.

A coherence gate discards interventions that degrade output: coherence is the fraction of valid True/False responses, and it stays 1.00 throughout. Three further validity checks: the SAE reconstruction attains mean cosine similarity 0.89 with raw activations (normalized MSE 0.21); the shared-set ablation removes only 5.7% of residual-stream norm (vs 13.9% for the geometric top-16, which flips behavior less — ruling out perturbation-magnitude confounds); and mean KL divergence between baseline and post-ablation next-token distributions is 0.11 nats for the shared set, indicating a localized shift rather than gross corruption. The behavioral evaluation uses 1,600 statements across twelve category sets (six affirmative factual datasets plus negations), with a held-out split (rankings derived on one half, ablation measured on the other) and five random seeds.

## Results & Highlights

- **Dissociation under ablation (headline):** on the deceptive-instruction factual-verification set, ablating shared features flips the True/False output on 13–27% of statements (0.130 at m=16, 0.269 at m=54), vs 6.1% for equally sized probe-only features and 1.0% for random controls — with coherence 1.00 across all conditions, so flips are genuine behavior changes, not degradation.
- **Weak ranking overlap:** the geometric probe ranking and the gradient-sensitivity ranking share only 2, 7, 12, and 22 features in their top-16/50/100/200 (chance: 0.02/0.15/0.61/2.44), Spearman ρ = 0.10 — one to two orders above chance yet only an 11–14% shared fraction.
- **Activation-aware mask beats geometry:** across five seeds (n = 600 per seed, 16 features), the learned sparse mask flips behavior on 17.6 ± 0.7% of statements vs 6.1 ± 0.5% for the probe's geometric top-16 (≈3x), 12.1 ± 1.0% for the shared set, 0.054 ± 0.009 for gradient-top alone, and 0.8 ± 0.6% for random — while the geometric top-16 produces the largest probe-readout shift among causal sets (0.164 vs 0.049 shared), i.e., it moves the probe more and the model less.
- **Direction-level localization:** ablating the full TTPD direction tG flips 18.1 ± 1.1% of outputs; the 16-feature shared set recovers two-thirds (12.1 ± 1.1%), the geometric top-16 only one-third (6.8 ± 0.6%), random 0.7 ± 0.1% — just 16 of 16,384 SAE features carry most of the direction's causal effect.
- **Robustness and structure of the effect:** the shared > probe-only > random ordering holds across five seeds (0.105 ± 0.017 / 0.059 ± 0.016 / 0.010 ± 0.002) and the held-out split (0.122 / 0.085 / 0.010); flips are strongly directional (86 True→False vs 4 False→True out of 90 flips, n = 800; baseline accuracy 0.403 drops to 0.330 rather than rising), present in 10 of 12 dataset categories (0.014–0.243 per category, strongest for Sp–En translation at 0.243), and the cumulative effect is non-monotonic (peak 0.31 at N = 96, falling to 0.10 at N = 126) due to sign-incoherent low-ranked features while mean absolute margin stays 1.7–2.8.
- **Boundary case:** for the auxiliary reward-hacking MBPP code probe, the SAE reconstruction preserves only 26% of the raw probe margin (1.00 of 3.84); ablating the top-50 sparse-mask features flips 32.7% of reconstruction-space probe predictions but only 2.1% of raw-activation predictions — the workflow's validity depends on the probe signal living in the SAE basis.

## Authors & Credibility

Published at the COLM 2026 Workshop on Agent Behavior (workshop paper, i.e., light peer review). The author team is unusual: Devesh Tiwari, Camille Davis, Shivank Sinha, and Talia Weaver are high-school students (WW-P High School South, Phillips Academy Andover, Dublin High School, Carlmont High School), Aditya Shah is at Google, and Maheep Chaudhary (independent) is the senior author. Despite the provenance, the empirical hygiene is well above typical workshop standards: probe replication from the original authors' released code (AUROC 0.98, matching prior reports), chance-level overlap baselines, five-seed robustness, held-out-split validation, coherence gating with KL-divergence and residual-norm controls, a direction-level ceiling baseline, per-category breakdowns, and an auxiliary negative-result diagnostic that honestly delimits the method. Code is stated to be publicly available via a link in the paper (a generic "this URL" reference). The core building blocks (TTPD probe, GemmaScope SAEs, the Long et al. setting) are all public, which aids reproducibility.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Incremental but clean. The decodability-vs-causality gap is well-trodden (amnesic probing, control tasks, Makelov et al.'s task-grounded SAE evaluation, Ma et al.'s finding that contrastively selected SAE features can reflect lexical correlates). The genuinely new angle is starting from a probe direction that is already known to be behaviorally causal (tG) and asking the finer question of whether its geometrically aligned SAE features carry its effect — plus the constructive result that an activation-aware mask rescues probe-based attribution. The gradient-attribution weakness and the "geometric alignment tracks the probe readout, not the model" insight are useful, if narrow, contributions.
- **Rigor:** Strong for a workshop paper — chance baselines for overlap, size-matched controls everywhere, the perturbation-magnitude confound explicitly killed (geometric top-16 removes 13.9% of residual norm yet flips less than the shared set's 5.7%), coherence gating, seed and held-out robustness, and the non-monotonicity investigated rather than hidden. Weaker spots: the evaluation data is 12 sets of simple factual statements (cities, element symbols, inventors, translations) — toy-scale knowledge where truth directions are known to be nearly linear; and the analysis layer (20) is dictated by SAE availability, not the actual probing peak (21), a small but real mismatch.
- **Reproducibility:** Good in principle (public model, public GemmaScope SAEs, released probe code from Long et al., public datasets, seeds reported with std), but the code link is a placeholder-style "this URL", so the artifact cannot be verified from the PDF alone.
- **Significance:** The practical takeaway — verify probe signal survives SAE reconstruction before doing feature-level causal attribution; don't equate probe weight geometry with model-internal causes — is a solid methodological caution for the interpretability community, but the evidence comes from one model, one layer, one SAE, one probe family, and one behavioral domain. The paper itself concedes this in Limitations.
- **Red flags:** (1) Single-case-study design: the one auxiliary extension (reward-hacking code probe) actually shows the pipeline failing there — SAE reconstruction retains only 26% of the probe margin and raw-activation flips are ~2% — so the headline dissociation has no second successful demonstration; (2) absolute effect sizes are modest (max ~27–31% flip rate), and flips are toward False rather than truth-restoring, so no safety application is demonstrated; (3) the LR probe is excluded on the reasonable grounds of hyperparameter instability, but this narrows claims to the TTPD direction specifically; (4) the author list (mostly high-school students, no academic institution) and workshop-only review mean scrutiny has been light — the unusually careful controls argue the work is genuine, but claims should be treated as single-setting evidence pending independent replication.

## Strengths

- Crisp, falsifiable question and a clean two-ranking-plus-intervention design that directly dissociates probe readouts from behavioral drivers, with every comparison size-matched and coherence-gated.
- Unusually thorough controls for the venue: chance-overlap baselines, five seeds, held-out split, KL/norm/reconstruction checks, a full-direction ceiling baseline, per-category breakdown, and directional flip analysis (86 vs 4) showing causal control rather than noise.
- Constructive positive result, not just a negative one: the activation-aware sparse mask shows probe information remains useful when combined with activation statistics, refining rather than dismissing probing methodology.
- The reward-hacking boundary case is reported as an honest negative result, turning a failure into an explicit validity precondition (check reconstruction fidelity of the probe signal first).

## Limitations

- Single model (Gemma-2-9B-Instruct), single layer (20), single SAE (GemmaScope 16k width), single probe family (TTPD), single behavioral setting — the authors acknowledge that generalization across model families, layers, SAE architectures, and domains is untested.
- The factual-verification datasets are simple, curated trivia (cities, element symbols, inventors, translations); truth-direction linearity and the dissociation may look different on open-ended generation, richer deception, or agentic behavior, despite the workshop's agent-behavior framing.
- Gradient sensitivity is a first-order, local attribution, which the paper shows is itself a weak handle (gradient-top flips only 5.4%); the framework's dependence on this imperfect ranking to define the "shared" set is a circularity risk partially mitigated by the ablation validation.
- Effect sizes are moderate (peak 31% cumulative, 12–27% in the main regime) and the intervention does not restore truthful behavior (accuracy drops 0.403 → 0.330), limiting immediate safety relevance; the auxiliary deception-domain probe could not be validly analyzed with the method at all.
- No comparison against alternative feature-selection baselines beyond geometry/gradient/mask/random (e.g., activation-magnitude ranking alone, or supervised dictionaries à la Makelov et al.), leaving the mask's advantage incompletely triangulated.

## Relevance

这篇论文直接切中可解释性方法学的核心问题——线性探针的可解码性与因果性的鸿沟，并对 SAE 特征归因给出了可操作的规程（先用激活统计而非纯几何对齐选特征、先验证探针信号在 SAE 重构中存活）。对于关注 LLM 内部表示、欺骗检测与因果干预评估的研究，它提供了一个干净的实验范式和一组需要警惕的陷阱。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the probe-geometry vs behavioral-driver dissociation replicate at other layers (e.g., the actual layer-21 probing peak), other SAE widths, and other model families (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, where Long et al. report a layer-14 peak)?
- Can the activation-aware sparse mask be validated as a general feature-selection tool on settings where the probe signal demonstrably survives SAE reconstruction — and how does it compare against supervised/non-SAE feature dictionaries (Makelov et al., 2025) or activation-magnitude ranking alone?
- Are the True→False-dominant flips specific to the deceptive instruction condition, or does ablating shared truth-direction features under truthful/neutral instructions show the same directional asymmetry?
- Why does the shared-set effect peak at ~96 features and then self-cancel — do the sign-incoherent low-ranked shared features correspond to interpretable counter-directions (e.g., polarity or negation features)?
- In the reward-hacking boundary case, does a finer or differently trained SAE (higher reconstruction fidelity for code activations) make the probe margin recoverable, or is the code-probe signal inherently non-sparse in this basis?
- Could coherence-gated SAE intervention be extended from binary True/False margins to graded behavioral metrics (e.g., unfaithful chain-of-thought rates, as in Occhipinti et al., 2026) to test dissociation in open-ended generation?
