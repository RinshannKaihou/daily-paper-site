Key Finding · 核心发现

Frozen LLMs catch a lying reporter almost perfectly — yet routinely fail to clear an honest one.

Why This Matters · 为何重要

Adding a verification step in front of an RL agent catches broken reward reporters — but also convicts honest ones, at rates driven by irrelevant surface features.

A false conviction, acted on, would invert a truthful reward channel.

⚠ Critical Edge · 批判性提醒

A model allowed to reason aloud might clear honest reporters far more often — the single-token, no-CoT protocol bounds the finding's practical relevance.

Three usable models from two families, one seed of 64 worlds, a synthetic 8-round game; no mechanism offered for the round/letter levers.

Methodology 方法

Everitt et al. (2017)'s corrupted-reward-channel problem, instantiated as an exactly-measurable LLM test. A two-option bandit game with a reporting “source”: rounds 1–4 are reported accurately; after round 4, two hidden switches may flip — the options' point values may swap, and the source may start reporting the opposite of every result. The four situations form matched pairs with byte-identical logs (test-suite asserted), so any report-only policy scores exactly 0.500 by construction.

The only tie-breaker is a one-line verified record: one round's true result, printed beside the source's claim. The verdict is the next-token probability mass over two answer letters — no sampling, no chain of thought: the single-step read a policy would act on.

Five prompt versions serve as controls: with record (the measurement), no record, answer printed (reading floor), answer+record (ceiling), and salience. A fresh option pair (C, D) in rounds 7–8 enables a “fresh-pair question” that routes through the honesty verdict.

  • Knowing ≠ acting: on histories the model correctly convicted, fresh-pair accuracy is only 0.117–0.200 (Qwen) and 0.369–0.439 (Llama) vs answer-printed 0.987–0.994 — models follow the lying report 56–89% of the time.
  • An agreeing record raises suspicion anyway: told the source is honest and shown a matching record, Llama still says “lying” 29% of the time (0.961 → 0.707); in lying situations the same difference is ≤ 0.012.
  • Salience intervention: null (−0.034 to +0.027, below the registered +0.05 threshold). Flip-heuristic control fired for every large model, yet agreement is only 0.521–0.555 while scoring 0.759–0.868 — not the heuristic in disguise.
  • Pre-registration honesty: the registered central prediction (0.65) was wrong (actual 0.418), and the strict per-situation reading discounts 3 of the 4 headline false-accusation numbers — the 38% carries the claim.

Assessment 评估

✓Strengths
  • Airtight matched pairs. Byte-identical logs mean any observed asymmetry is attributable to how the model uses the record — not wording leakage; rare control in LLM evaluation.
  • Crisp, consequential finding. The same evidence that reliably convicts fails to acquit — and the failure is modulated by features carrying zero information.
  • Exceptional hygiene. Pre-registered rules, the wrong prediction reported, a caught-and-fixed design confound, controls (floor, ceiling, flip heuristic) that each did real work.
  • Clever extension. The fresh-pair question turns a diagnosis into a potential action — and shows the models don't take it.
△Limitations
  • Thin base. Three usable models from two families, one seed of 64 worlds; no scale claim, no mechanism for the round/letter effects.
  • No reasoning allowed. The single-token protocol excludes chain-of-thought by design; a reasoning model is flagged as a different question.
  • Author-written wordings. Letter/position effects show wording matters — a fifth wording could move the honest numbers again (nothing tried moved the lying ones at 70B).
  • Self-confounded null. The salience intervention's text itself names “began lying”.
  • Synthetic scope. An 8-round two-option game; transfer to realistic corrupted-reward scenarios (RLHF drift, sensor faults) is untested.

Questions for Further Exploration 待探索的问题

Q1Does the asymmetry persist with chain-of-thought or larger deep-reasoning models — or does honest-reporter clearance emerge with scale? (Table 2's inverse-scaling shape hints the opposite.)
Q2What mechanism underlies the family-specific levers — record round for Qwen, answer letter for Llama — and can activation patching localize it?
Q3Would a verdict fed into an acting agent actually “invert a truthful channel” in a real multi-step RL/agent setting — and at what cost?
Q4Do RLHF-style reward models — not just prompted LLMs — show the same asymmetric use of inter-rater audit records when judging reward reports?
Q5Can training on verified-record diagnoses (balanced honest/lying labels) repair the acquittal deficit without degrading conviction — or is the asymmetry structural to single-step reads?
