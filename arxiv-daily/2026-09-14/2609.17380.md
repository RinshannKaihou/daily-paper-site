> **一句话总结：** Gensyn 提出"完全可审计"的模型透明度新层级：用 RepOps 位级可复现算子（固定归约顺序、禁用 FMA 融合、次正规数统一清零、计数器式 RNG）+ 拓扑不变数据流 + 确定性集合通信，把 1.61B 参数模型的 400B token 预训练（48×H100、80,957 步、每步带状态哈希）做成任意一步都可在异构商用硬件（CPU/NVIDIA/Apple Metal）上按位复现并由第三方审计的训练运行；配合原生 int8 QAT 把可复现性开销压到约 5×（MFU ~5% vs 优化 PyTorch bf16 的 40.5%），但代价是 token 预算只有 OLMo 2 1B 的十分之一，OLMES 宏平均 50.1 落后于 OLMo 2 1B 的 61.5（ARC-C 反超 36.2 vs 26.1）。

> **TL;DR:** Argues that "open source" LLMs are not actually verifiable because floating-point non-associativity means a faithfully-followed recipe yields different weights on different hardware — leaving room for undisclosed data or backdoors that proof-of-learning/proof-of-training-data cannot rule out. Gensyn trains Open-1B (1.61B params, 400B tokens, int8 QAT, 48×H100) under a bitwise-reproducible regime: fixed reduction orders, a topologically invariant data stream, deterministic collectives, and per-step state hashes let anyone replay any optimizer step on commodity hardware and check it against the published trajectory. On the OLMES 10-task macro it scores 50.1 vs OLMo 2 1B's 61.5 (OLMo 2 computed with the same pinned harness), a gap the authors attribute to having 10× fewer tokens (400B vs 4T) because reproducibility costs ≈5× throughput (MFU 5.9% vs 40.5% for stock PyTorch bf16; int8 QAT is 6.8× slower than PyTorch at 1 node, ≈5.2× at 6 nodes). Training itself was healthy: held-out CE fell monotonically 6.59 → 2.580 (power-law fit R² = 0.93), clipping engaged on only 0.16% of steady-state steps, and int8 diverges from an identical bf16 run by ≈0.2 nats after 400B tokens. Read this for the systems/verifiability contribution, not for SOTA 1B benchmarks.

## 核心贡献

- **定义并实现"完全可审计（fully auditable）"这一新的模型透明度层级**：超出开源（权重+数据+配方）范畴，训练中对每个数据样本的每次操作都能在异构商用硬件（x86/ARM CPU、NVIDIA GPU、Apple Silicon/Metal）上以**按位确定性（bitwise certainty）**独立复现，从而可验证"发布的确是用声明的配方和数据训练出来的模型"，堵住 proof-of-learning / proof-of-training-data 等概率性验证无法排除的少量样本后门注入。
- **RepOps 可复现算子库**：固定求和归约顺序、强制单一乘加融合约定、次正规数（subnormal）在所有后端统一清零（FTZ/DAZ 对齐最保守后端）、计数器式随机数生成（值是 (seed, index) 的纯函数），配合确定性集合通信（递归倍增蝶形 all-reduce、可复现 reduce-scatter、用预计算倒数的乘法代替除法求平均）。
- **拓扑不变的可复现数据流**：训练数据被定义为仅由种子和语料清单决定的规范窗口序列（3 个伪随机生成器分别决定源交错、分片顺序、文档顺序），与 world size / rank / 节点数无关——集群上第 r 个数据并行 rank 拥有窗口 m 当且仅当 m mod R = r，因此单机审计重放能重建与集群完全一致的批顺序。
- **Open-1B 模型本体**：首个完全可审计的开源 LLM（1.61B 参数，其中 1.08B 非嵌入），Llama 3 / OLMo 2 / Gemma 风格架构 + 原生 int8 W8A8 量化感知预训练（LSQ，每步重钉 per-channel 步长），发布全部预训练数据（Open-1B Mix 0626，450B token）、每 100 步一个中间检查点、完整训练/评测代码库与审计 harness。
- **两个低精度训练失效模式的发现与修复**（对社区有独立价值）：(1) int8 反向 flash attention 曾给出全零的 ∇Q，导致 QK-norm 增益单调增长（梯度蠕变），修复需引入尊重位级可复现的缩放因子并去掉 QK-norm 增益；(2) LSQ 学习式步长在预训练中会发生步长漂移并自增强发散（首个块的梯度范数在约 2,000 步后超过 10³），改为每步用 LSQ 初始化公式重算步长（开销约 2% 步时间，而关掉量化的代价约 70%）。
- **集体审计机制**：整跑无法在单机重放，因此由多个独立审计者各自认证单个步（TreeFold 二进制进位折叠以 O(log R) 内存重建集群的递归倍增配对顺序），拼起来覆盖整个训练运行；整条轨迹最终锚定为一系列逐步状态哈希。

## Key Contributions

- Defines and demonstrates a new "fully auditable" tier of model disclosure: every operation on every training sample is independently replayable on heterogeneous commodity hardware (NVIDIA GPUs, x86/ARM CPUs, Apple Silicon via Metal) with bitwise certainty, closing the verifiability gap that probabilistic schemes (proof-of-learning, proof-of-training-data) leave open — notably small backdoor injections that need only a few poisoned samples.
- RepOps, a library of bitwise-reproducible (BR) operations: fixed reduction order on every backend; one forced fused/non-fused multiply-add convention; subnormals flushed to zero everywhere (matching the least permissive backend, Apple GPUs); counter-based RNG (value = pure function of seed and logical index) so random draws are identical across platforms and tensor shapes.
- A topologically invariant reproducible data stream: the corpus is pre-committed to a canonical sequence of 4,097-token packed windows determined by three seeded RNGs (source interleaving, shard order, document order) and independent of world size/rank; data-parallel rank r owns window m iff m mod R = r. Elastic re-sharding and single-device audit replay both enumerate the same windows.
- Deterministic collectives that can be replayed sequentially: a recursive-doubling butterfly all-replace of NCCL's ring (fixed pairing order), a custom DeterministicReduceScatter (all-to-all + ascending-rank fixed-order sum), and averages implemented as multiply-by-precomputed-reciprocal (CPU division vs CUDA reciprocal multiply differ by up to 1 ULP).
- Open-1B itself: a 1.61B-parameter LLM (24 layers, GQA 16/4, hybrid 512-token sliding-window attention, gain-free QK-norm, embedding RMSNorm, z-loss 1e-4, untied embeddings) pretrained for 400B tokens on 48×H100 under native int8 W8A8 QAT with learned-step-size quantization whose per-channel scales are re-pinned to √2·mean(|w|)/qmax at every optimizer step; released with the full dataset, 100-step-interval checkpoints, codebase, and audit harness.
- Two documented low-precision training failure modes with root causes and BR-compatible fixes: (1) quantized backward flash attention produced ∇Q = 0, making QK-norm gains grow monotonically (gradient creep) — fixed by a BR-respecting gradient scale factor plus removing the QK-norm gain; (2) LSQ-trained step sizes drift during pretraining into a self-reinforcing divergence (block-0 gradient norm > 10³ after ~2,000 steps) — fixed by per-step re-pinning at ~2% step-time cost (vs ~70% to disable quantization).
- A collective verification scheme: since replaying the whole run on one machine is infeasible, many independent auditors each certify individual steps (TreeFold keeps only O(log dp_replicate) partials in RAM, spilling dormant entries to disk), together covering the run; every step is anchored by a sharded state hash.

## Methodology

The paper's core methodological claim is that bitwise reproducibility (BR) — not same-machine determinism — requires imposing a definite order on all three sources of training nondeterminism:

1. **Kernel-level arithmetic (RepOps).** Floating-point addition is non-associative, so every reduction (inside GEMMs, norms, loss/gradient sums) is computed in one fixed order on all hardware, accepting speed loss. Fused multiply-add is forced to a single convention (fusion disabled on CPU builds, explicitly unfused MADD on GPU) because compilers fuse opportunistically and inconsistently. Subnormals are flushed to zero on every backend (Apple already does FTZ/DAZ by default), deliberately adopting the least permissive hardware's behavior. Randomness comes from a counter-based generator where the i-th value is computed from (seed, i) with no running state, making draws independent of parallelization, tensor size, and device. Autograd, optimizers, losses, and layers are wrapped so the entire chain from initialization to convergence is BR.

2. **Data ordering (topologically invariant stream).** The training data is defined as a single canonical stream of packed windows W0, W1, ..., a pure function of the training seed and corpus manifests. Three RNGs fully determine it: a global mix RNG (source interleaving by token-share weights), a per-source shard-order RNG seeded with (seed, SHA-256(source), epoch) — hashing the name rather than Python's per-process hash keeps the permutation identical across processes — and a per-shard document-order RNG. Documents are concatenated with an <|endoftext|> separator into a flat buffer sliced into fixed 4,097-token windows (causal attention crosses document boundaries). Parallelism is applied after the stream is defined: a deterministic schedule maps each optimizer step to a contiguous window range and rank r of an R-way job owns window m iff m mod R = r. Resumable state is a small rank-independent record (per-source document counts, epoch counters, mix RNG bit-state, partial packing buffer).

3. **Collective communication (deterministic all-reduce / reduce-scatter).** NCCL's ring all-reduce and topology-dependent reduce-scatter orderings cannot be replayed on a single device (a naive single-device sum matches NCCL reduce-scatter only to ~1e-11 relative error, not bitwise). The replacements: a recursive-doubling butterfly all-reduce over replicas (fixed pairing, log2 N hops each carrying the full buffer), and DeterministicReduceScatter inside FSDP2 (pure all-to-all data movement + fixed ascending-rank local sum). All divisions by integer scalars are implemented as multiplication by a precomputed reciprocal to avoid CPU/CUDA divergence.

**The audit replay (Section 6, Algorithm 1)** sequentializes the three gradient-reduction levels — microbatch accumulation, intra-node reduce-scatter over shards, inter-node all-reduce over replicas — into one loop over virtual ranks in mesh order, with a TreeFold binary-carry stack reproducing the cluster's recursive-doubling pairing order while keeping at most O(log dp_replicate) partials in memory (spilled to disk). Every step of the released run is anchored by a sharded canonical hash (host-side blake2b over batches, parameters, optimizer state, gradient shards).

**Model & training recipe.** Open-1B is a 24-layer, hidden-2048 decoder-only transformer (1.61B total / 1.08B non-embedding params; GQA 16Q/4KV heads of dim 128; FFN 5632; vocab 128,256 byte-level BPE trained from scratch; hybrid sliding-window attention, 512-token window with every 5th layer and the final layer fully causal; gain-free QK-norm before RoPE; extra RMSNorm on the embedding output for stable activation quantization; z-loss 1e-4 fused with cross-entropy; untied embeddings with no weight decay on the input embedding). All attention and FFN linear layers train in int8 W8A8 (LSQ): per-channel weight scales, per-tensor activation scales, int32 accumulation; backward STE gradients also run int8 with a Walsh–Hadamard rotation along the contraction dimension to flatten gradient outliers (weight-gradient relative error ~5% → ~1%); the P·V product inside flash attention runs on int8 tensor cores while QK^T and softmax stay high precision; attention backward is an STE through the deterministic BF16 flash backward with ∇Q accumulated in 64-bit fixed point. Embedding and LM head stay BF16; Adam moments are FP32. Trained 400B tokens at 4,096 context on 48×H100: AdamW (β1=0.9, β2=0.95, wd=0.1), grad clip 1.0, linear warmup to 4.5e-4 over 667 steps then single cosine decay to 10%, three-phase global batch ramp (3.15M → 4.72M → 9.44M tokens/step), single global seed 42. Data is the Open-1B Mix 0626 (450B tokens): DCLM-Baseline 66.7%, FineWeb-Edu (score ≥3, ~25% retained) 13.3%, The Stack v2 (permissive, ≤1MB) 12.0%, Proof-Pile-2 8.0%; each source at 0.89 epochs, no document seen twice, no additional dedup applied. Evaluations follow the OLMES standard (best-of-MCF/CF) on the OLMo 2 report's Table 9 suite; the OLMo 2 1B macro was recomputed by the authors with the same pinned harness on the public 4T-token checkpoint.

## Results & Highlights

- **Downstream (OLMES suite, vs OLMo 2 1B at 4T tokens; Open-1B at 400B):** OLMES 10-task macro 50.1 vs 61.5 (−11.4 points); dev-benchmark average 25.4 vs 31.9; ARC-C is the standout win at 36.2 vs 26.1 (+10.1), MMLU essentially tied (25.9 vs 26.9, MCF), MMLU-Pro 11.7 vs 11.1, but large deficits on HSwag (48.6 vs 67.5), WinoGrande (54.1 vs 67.8), NQ (8.1 vs 16.1), TQA (30.0 vs 50.1), GSM8K (1.5 vs 3.3). The authors explicitly attribute the trailing to the 10× smaller token budget forced by the reproducibility overhead.
- **Convergence:** mixture-weighted held-out cross-entropy fell monotonically from 6.59 to 2.580 over 400B tokens / 80,957 steps; a two-parameter power law fits the held-out loss as L ≈ 3.44·T^−0.050 with R² = 0.93 over T > 5B (training-loss fit only R² = 0.71 due to data-mix heterogeneity). The run completed in 7 contiguous segments, with restarts validated by hash comparisons on repeated steps.
- **Stability:** gradient clipping (threshold 1.0) engaged on only 0.16% of steady-state steps (343/80,957 = 0.42% over the full run, dominated by the 1,300-step warmup); the spike-skip protocol (‖g‖ > 5) fired exactly twice in the entire run (pre-clip norms 5.78 at step 1,782 and 6.88 at step 43,737 → 10 skipped steps of 80,957). Median step time 28.0 s (main) / 47.1 s (late); 27.8 days of active training in a 29.5-day calendar span.
- **int8 vs bf16 (same architecture, recipe, and data stream via the deterministic batch ordering):** train cross-entropy diverges by ≈0.2 nats after 400B tokens in favor of the PyTorch bf16 baseline; the z-loss diverges much more (uniform int8 grid spacing lets logit offsets saturate higher than bf16's near-zero spacing). The Walsh–Hadamard rotation on backward gradients cuts weight-gradient quantization error from ~5% to ~1%; re-pinning LSQ step sizes every step rescued the run from a block-0 gradient norm exceeding 10³ after ~2,000 steps (original run's pre-clip norm passed 300 before step 2,500; the fixed run stays flat) at ~2% step-time cost vs ~70% to disable quantization.
- **Cost of reproducibility (fixed 4.72M-token global batch, HSDP, 1→6 nodes of 8×H100):** the released repop-int8 configuration reaches 217.5k tokens/s (5.1% MFU) at 6 nodes vs stock PyTorch bf16 + torch.compile at 1,136k tokens/s (26.6% MFU) — ≈5.2× slower at 6 nodes, 6.8× at 1 node (42.1k vs 288k tok/s; 5.9% vs 40.5% MFU), while the BR-bf16 variant is 12.2× slower at 1 node (3.3% MFU). Strong-scaling efficiency at 6 nodes is 71% with per-step state hashing (169.4k tok/s) vs 86% without (217.4k); the host-side blake2b hash is a constant ≈6.0 s/step (5.77–5.96 s), consuming 21.4% of wall time at 6 nodes. The deterministic all-reduce premium over NCCL on 804 MB buckets grows from 1.13× (2 nodes) to 3.54× (6 nodes; 2,824 ms vs 798 ms median). Reproducible bf16 is ≈1.6× slower than reproducible int8, which motivated the int8 choice. Power draw averaged ~50% with ~485 W/GPU spikes to 70%.

## Authors & Credibility

All nine authors (John Donaghy, Brian Wilcox, Oğuzhan Ersoy, Shikhar Rastogi, Adam St Arnaud, Alexey Titov, Jordan Greenberg, Ben Fielding, Harry Grieve) are at Gensyn, a company whose commercial focus is verifiable/decentralized ML compute, so the work doubles as an infrastructure demonstration — a disclosed and obvious incentive, and also a reason the engineering accounting is unusually complete. Credibility is strengthened by: (a) releasing everything the audit claim depends on (full dataset, 100-step checkpoints, codebase, audit app); (b) recomputing the OLMo 2 1B OLMES macro themselves with a pinned harness and noting their per-task scores reproduce OLMo 2's Table 9 exactly (e.g., MMLU MCF 26.9); (c) candid failure reporting — the run broke into 7 segments, and two training instabilities (gradient creep, LSQ step-size drift) are diagnosed with root causes and counterfactual ablations rather than hidden. The paper is a systems/trust contribution evaluated at 1B scale on a single run (seed 42), not a controlled scientific study with variance estimates.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The framing is genuinely new in kind, not degree: moving from "open source" to "auditable" via bitwise reproducibility across heterogeneous hardware is a stronger property than prior verifiable-training work (Srivastava et al. 2024's optimistic verifiable training via hardware nondeterminism control; Verde/Arun et al. 2025's reproducible operators, both cited as the direct lineage). The two QAT failure modes (zero ∇Q from quantized flash-attention backward; LSQ step-size drift under AdamW during long pretraining) appear to be undocumented in the literature and are valuable independent of the audit story. That said, the headline "first fully auditable LLM" rests on accepting their particular trust model.
- **Rigor:** The benchmark comparison against OLMo 2 1B is confounded by design — 400B vs 4T tokens (10×) because of the ~5× throughput tax, so Table 6 says little about int8 QAT quality per token; no Chinchilla-/compute-matched control is run. Critically, the ≈0.2-nat int8-vs-bf16 train-CE divergence is never translated into downstream benchmark numbers, so the quality cost of native int8 QAT at this scale is asserted via loss curves only. The strong-scaling and runtime-overhead measurements, by contrast, are careful (fixed global batch, hash-on/off A/B, two independent overhead measurements for the hash share).
- **Reproducibility:** This is the paper's own metric and it delivers: per-step hashes, 100-step checkpoints, full data and code, audit harness across four hardware families. One internal tension: the conclusion calls RepOps "a proprietary library" even though the code is released — worth watching what license actually ships.
- **Significance:** The backdoor/undisclosed-data threat model is real (a few poison samples suffice, per the cited Souly et al. 2025), and the audit replay mechanism (Algorithm 1 + TreeFold + reciprocal-multiply averaging) is technically convincing in its bit-level detail. But the trust anchor question is underdeveloped: verifying step N requires the state at step N−1, so an auditor either replays from genesis or trusts published intermediate hashes; the "collective audit" scheme that resolves this is described in one paragraph with no analysis of coverage incentives, adversarial auditors, or what fraction of steps must be certified to bound backdoor-detection probability. MFU ~5% and a 6 s/step host-side hash are honest numbers, but they mean the demonstrated regime is 1B/400B — two orders of magnitude below frontier token budgets — and scaling behavior of both the hash and the butterfly all-reduce's non-power-of-two penalty (already 3.54× vs NCCL at 6 nodes) is unmeasured beyond 6 nodes.
- **Red flags:** None disqualifying, but note: single run, single seed, single scale; near-duplicate overlap between DCLM-Baseline and FineWeb-Edu is explicitly not deduplicated; the audit guarantee covers "weights match the declared run," not "the deployed product is this checkpoint" nor any bias removal (the authors say this themselves).

## Strengths

- Converts a philosophical complaint ("open source isn't verifiable") into a precise, achievable technical property — bitwise reproducibility — and actually ships an end-to-end artifact proving it, including the mundane details that usually break bit-exactness (FMA fusion, subnormal handling, RNG layout, division lowering on CPU vs CUDA).
- The topologically invariant data stream is an elegant decoupling: making the token stream a pure function of the seed enables elastic re-sharding, exact resume, the int8-vs-bf16 controlled comparison, and single-device audit replay all at once.
- Failure forensics are exemplary: the LSQ step-size drift diagnosis eliminates alternatives by direct substitution (attention kernel, SWA pattern, reduction order, sampler, LR), identifies the quantizer via a bf16 substitution test, and explains why the failure is absent from the QAT literature (LSQ was designed for short fine-tunes with stationary weights).
- Honest cost accounting (5× slower, 5% MFU, 71% scaling efficiency, 21.4% hash overhead at 6 nodes) and an honest benchmark table where the model loses; per-task reproduction of OLMo 2's reported scores validates the eval pipeline.

## Limitations

- The reproducibility tax (≈5× wall-clock vs optimized PyTorch, order-of-magnitude MFU drop) confined the run to 400B tokens, leaving Open-1B well behind OLMo 2 1B on most benchmarks — the artifact demonstrates the audit regime, not competitive 1B quality.
- The int8-vs-bf16 quality cost is quantified only as a ≈0.2-nat training-CE gap; no downstream benchmark compares int8 QAT against a bf16 twin at matched tokens.
- The collective audit scheme — the piece that makes whole-run verification feasible — is sketched, not evaluated: no simulation of auditor participation, coverage statistics, or adversarial behavior.
- Single run, single seed (42), single architecture at one scale; no measurement of BR overhead beyond 6 nodes or at non-power-of-two replica counts where the butterfly premium is already 3.54×; host-side state hashing (6 s/step) is acknowledged as an unoptimized bottleneck.
- Base model only (no fine-tuned/instruct variant); the released dataset applies no cross-source dedup between DCLM-Baseline and FineWeb-Edu.

## Relevance

这篇论文把"训练过程可验证性"从概率性证明（proof-of-learning）推进到按位可复现的确定性审计，其 RepOps 算子、确定性数据流与低精度训练失效分析对任何关心 LLM 训练透明度、供应链信任与 int8 量化预训练稳定性的研究都直接相关，也为可复现科学计算提供了系统级范式。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Moving the per-step blake2b state hash from host to device would reclaim ~6 s/step — how close does that bring 6-node strong-scaling efficiency to the 86% hash-off ceiling, and what does the cost profile look like at 64–512 nodes where the butterfly all-reduce's non-power-of-two penalty compounds?
- Does the ≈0.2-nat int8-vs-bf16 train-CE gap close or widen with longer budgets and larger models, and what downstream benchmark delta (under a token-matched bf16 twin) does it actually cause?
- Does per-step re-pinning of LSQ scales generalize to other quantization schemes (weight-only int4, rotation-based methods like QuaRot/SpinQuant), and can the re-pinned step size be proven non-divergent under AdamW rather than empirically stable?
- What fraction of steps must independent auditors collectively certify — and under what sampling distribution — to bound the probability of an undetected k-sample backdoor injection, given poisoning attacks need a near-constant number of samples?
- Can the trust anchor be weakened without full genesis replay, e.g., via a checkpoint-chained Merkle structure where verifying any 100-step span against a published checkpoint requires trusting only O(steps/100) hashes, and how would auditor incentives be designed around it?
- The collective-verification idea is inspired by open-source code review — would a cryptocurrency/staking style economic design (Gensyn's home turf) actually produce honest majority coverage, or does it reintroduce the trust assumptions the audit was meant to remove?
