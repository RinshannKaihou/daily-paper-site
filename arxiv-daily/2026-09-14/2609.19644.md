> **一句话总结：** Google Cloud AI Research 提出 ScientistTwo，一个全自动多智能体科研框架：给定一个来自顶会论文的科学问题，它通过"局限提取→新颖想法生成（子集初筛→全量验证）→基于实验轨迹的想法进化→自动化消融→模拟同行评审+代码化 Rebuttal→Meta-Review"的闭环流水线，独立产出可复现代码库与论文；在 107 个 ICLR/ICML/NeurIPS 问题上，86 个（80.4%）被改进，平均相对提升 25.2%（中位数 7.7%），生成的 86 篇论文在 ScholarPeer 下获 7.5±1.3 分、91.9% 接受率，在留出的 Stanford Agentic Reviewer 下获 5.7±0.6 分、72.1% 接受率——超过 ICLR 2026 与 NeurIPS 2025 已录用论文的平均分，且全部通过四项完整性审计（结果可复现 49/49、零投机取巧、零幻觉引用）。

> **TL;DR:** A fully autonomous multi-agent research framework from Google Cloud AI Research that takes a published top-venue problem (107 tasks from NeurIPS 2025 / ICML 2026 Spotlight / ICLR 2026) and independently produces a verified codebase plus a conference-formatted paper, via limitation-driven seed ideas, subset-first-then-full-set evaluation, trace-based idea evolution, automated ablations, and a closed-loop simulated peer-review-with-executed-rebuttal engine. It improves 86/107 (80.4%) human SOTA results with an average relative gain of 25.2% (median 7.7%) — versus 7.5% average for AutoSOTA on the same 33 NeurIPS 2025 tasks — and its 86 generated papers average 7.5±1.3 (91.9% accept) under ScholarPeer and 5.7±0.6 (72.1% accept) under the held-out Stanford Agentic Reviewer, beating the mean scores of accepted ICLR 2026 (5.2) and NeurIPS 2025 (5.5) papers on that reviewer, though still below ICML 2026 Spotlight level (6.1). It compounds: VD-STrans +10.9% over the human incremental-BPE SOTA, then self-iteration adds +9.6% and +8.2%. Cost: ~$3,765 and ~2.5 days per paper; 9 human reviewers rate it at overall parity with human papers (3.0/5), ahead on experiments (3.5) but behind on methodological rigor (2.9).

## 核心贡献

- **端到端全自动科研闭环**：给定一个科学问题（来自已录用的顶会论文及其代码库），ScientistTwo 独立完成"找局限→提想法→做实验→进化想法→跑消融→写论文→模拟评审与 Rebuttal→Meta-Review"的完整研究生命周期，最终输出论文 P+ 与可复现代码库 C+，全程无人工干预。
- **子集优先的高效评估策略**：为避免在单一指标上过拟合并控制算力开销，想法先在代表性基准子集上快速筛选（Baseline Coding Agent 复现基线→Subset Critic 给出 Good/Bad/Engineer 三态判定），只有稳定超越基线的想法才被 Full-Set Agent 放大到完整基准套件验证，形成多数据集、多指标的整体性评测。
- **基于实验轨迹的想法进化（探索-利用平衡）**：Idea Evolver 聚合历史执行轨迹（成功结果与失败日志）演化出新假设，同时每轮补充未评估的高新颖性种子想法以防陷入局部最优；Selector 从全量验证过的想法中选出最优者进入消融阶段。
- **自动化消融驱动的方法精炼**：Ablation Planner 自动生成并执行组件级消融计划，Ablation Critic 判断"当前方法是否已是最优组件组合"；若判定需要精炼，则移除冗余/反作用组件并重新全量验证（仅在严格更优时接受更新），模拟人类研究者"做减法"的科研实践。
- **闭环模拟同行评审与 Rebuttal 引擎**：用 ScholarPeer 作为 Peer-Reviewer Agent（1-10 ICLR 评分制），评分低于阈值 8 时触发 Rebuttal Planner/Coder——不是改写文字，而是**实际设计、编码并执行补充实验**来回应审稿质疑，再由 Paper Enhancer 将结果整合进稿件，循环至达标；Meta-Review Agent 做最终录用判定，必要时以评审意见为反馈反向精炼核心想法并重跑下游全流程。
- **四维科研完整性审计**：沿用并强化 CoE Integrity Audit——结果可复现（49/49）、规范合规/无 reward hacking（0/49 违规）、参考文献无幻觉（0/1814）、方法-代码对齐（49/49）；消融显示去掉对应精炼 Agent 会出现 19/1840 幻觉引用、38/50 方法-代码失配和 1 例 reward hacking。

## Key Contributions

- **Fully autonomous end-to-end discovery pipeline**: Given a scientific problem G (a peer-reviewed paper plus codebase from ICLR/ICML/NeurIPS), ScientistTwo produces (P+, C+) — a new paper and verified executable codebase — through six stages, each abstracted as a uniform candidate-generator / critic / refine loop (Listing 1): limitation extraction (up to 16 verifier-gated rounds), novelty-ranked seed idea generation, subset-to-full-set idea evaluation, trace-based idea evolution with exploration/exploitation balancing, automated component-level ablation studies, and manuscript drafting under a simulated peer-review + rebuttal + meta-review cycle.
- **Closed-loop review with *executed* rebuttals**: Unlike prior agents that treat reviews as editorial text, a Rebuttal Planner + Rebuttal Coding Agent conceive, implement, and run targeted supplementary experiments in response to reviewer critiques (loop until ScholarPeer score ≥ 8 or 2 rounds); the Meta-Review Agent can even reject the refined manuscript and force a new *idea* — triggering re-ablation and re-drafting, with strict verification that any updated method empirically outperforms the incumbent.
- **Holistic multi-dataset benchmarking instead of single-metric optimization**: Ideas are evaluated by a unified coder interface (ACoder) across multiple datasets and metrics on benchmark subsets before full-set scale-up, and the claimed gain must be attributable to the proposed mechanism via ablations — a stated contrast with single-scalar optimizers like AutoSOTA (whose five ICLR-2026 changes were all config-level edits, 0/5 new algorithmic components).
- **Integrity-by-construction mechanisms**: dedicated refinement agents + prompting produce self-contained reproducible scripts, a rule-violation filter that discards reward-hacking solutions, search-grounded bibliography correction, and a code-vs-manuscript audit — passing all four CoE integrity audits (score verification 49/49, spec violations 0/49, hallucinated references 0/1814, method-code alignment 49/49).
- **Iterative frontier compounding**: Using its own discoveries as context, ScientistTwo chains improvements — VD-STrans (+10.9% over the human incremental-BPE SOTA, simulated-review rating 6.5), BXT-Transducer (+9.6% over VD-STrans), SBR-Transducer (+8.2% over BXT) — evidence that gains can compound beyond both human baselines and its own prior solutions.

## Methodology

ScientistTwo formalizes problem-driven discovery as (P+, C+) = A(G), where A = {A1..AN_a} is an ecosystem of specialized agents. The pipeline:

1. **Novel seed ideas (§3.1)**: A Limitation Extractor pulls actionable limitations from the target paper; a Limitation Verifier loops until the set suffices (max 16 rounds). An initial idea is generated, scored by a Novelty Checker (which retrieves two reference papers via Google Search), and an Idea Generator expands the pool to N_seed candidates sorted by novelty.
2. **Idea evaluation, subset → full-set (§3.2)**: A Baseline Coding Agent first reproduces the paper's primary experiments on a benchmark subset (E_base, C_base). Each candidate idea is implemented by modifying C_base; a Subset Critic emits Good / Bad / Engineer, with an engineering-refinement loop (max 2 rounds) before pruning. Survivors are ported by a Full-Set Coding Agent to the entire benchmark suite, validated by a Full-Set Critic/Engineer. Everything is wrapped in a single Idea Implementer ACoder(G, h) → (h, E, C, d, r).
3. **Idea refinement (§3.3)**: Round k aggregates all prior execution traces (both successes and failure diagnostics); an Idea Evolver proposes N_k evolved hypotheses, combined with the next N_e unevaluated high-novelty seeds (exploration vs exploitation). The loop runs up to K=4 rounds or until 4 successful ideas; a Selector picks h_best by full-benchmark metrics.
4. **Ablation studies (§3.4)**: An Ablation Planner generates N_p executable ablation plans over C_best; an Ablation Critic checks whether the component breakdown is clean, else a refined hypothesis must strictly outperform E_best (verified by a Result Comparison Agent) before re-running ablations.
5. **Drafting + simulated review (§3.5)**: An Initial Drafter (incorporating PaperOrchestra) writes a conference-formatted manuscript (ICLR 2025 style). ScholarPeer reviews it on a 1–10 scale; below 8 triggers the executed-rebuttal loop (Rebuttal Planner → Rebuttal Coder → Paper Enhancer), max 2 rounds.
6. **Meta-review (§3.6)**: A Meta-Review Agent issues Accept or Refine; Refine feeds meta-critique back into idea-level engineering, with strict empirical verification and full downstream re-execution (max N_meta iterations).

Implementation: Gemini 3.6 Flash powers all agents except the idea-experiment coding, ablation, rebuttal, and draft-enhancement agents, which use Claude Code with Opus 4.8. Evaluation uses ScholarPeer (in-distribution — it is also the internal review signal) and the held-out Stanford Agentic Reviewer, plus a 9-expert human study and the four-dimension CoE Integrity Audit.

## Results & Highlights

- **Success rate and gains over human SOTA (107 problems)**: ScientistTwo improves 86/107 tasks (80.4%) with **average relative gain 25.2% (median 7.7%)**. On the 33 shared NeurIPS 2025 tasks it reaches median 7.7% / average 25.2% gain vs AutoSOTA's 2.7% / 7.5%; however on the 5 ICLR 2026 tasks AutoSOTA is actually ahead (median 5.0% / avg 8.5% vs ScientistTwo's 2.2% / 3.8%).
- **Review scores vs prior autonomous-research agents (Table 2, 86 generated papers)**: ScholarPeer 7.5±1.3 with 91.9% acceptance vs the best prior system ScientistOne at 3.8±1.3 with 14.3% (AI Scientist-v2: 2.0; DeepScientist: 3.0; Zochi: 3.0); on the held-out Stanford Agentic Reviewer, ScientistTwo is the *only* system with any acceptances — 5.7±0.6, 72.1% — while all seven baselines (AI-Researcher, CycleResearcher, AI Scientist-v2, AutoResearchClaw, Zochi, DeepScientist, ScientistOne) sit at 0.0%.
- **Comparison with human accepted papers (Table 3)**: ScientistTwo's papers average 7.5/91.9% (ScholarPeer) and 5.7/72.1% (Stanford) vs accepted ICLR 2026 papers at 6.8±1.6/60.0% and 5.2±0.7/60.0%, and NeurIPS 2025 accepted papers at 6.2±1.9/65.8% and 5.5±0.7/76.3% — i.e., above the acceptance bar of both venues on both reviewers, but still below ICML 2026 Spotlight papers (6.9±1.5/79.7%; Stanford 6.1±0.5/96.9%); Agent4Science 2025 AI-generated papers score 3.0 with 0% acceptance.
- **Ablation of the review loop (Table 5, 49 ICML problems)**: ScholarPeer rating rises 3.8 (round 0) → 5.2 (1 review round, no rebuttal) → 6.9 (2 rounds) → 7.6 (full, with rebuttal), acceptance 14.3% → 46.9% → 79.6% → 93.9%; the held-out Stanford reviewer's acceptance correspondingly moves 0.0% → 49.0% → 73.5% → 69.4%.
- **Review-driven idea refinement (Table 6, TOFU forget10 unlearning on Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct)**: human baseline Engram overall score 0.705; ScientistTwo's first method LFR-Engram 0.897; after meta-review-forced refinement, FCD-Engram 0.916 (with membership utility 0.926 vs 0.922 and privacy 0.860 vs 0.495 human).
- **Integrity audit (Table 7)**: full ScientistTwo passes 49/49 score verification, 0/49 spec violations, 0/1814 hallucinated references, 49/49 method-code alignment; removing refinement agents reintroduces 19/1840 hallucinated references, 38–39/50 method-code misalignment, and one reward-hacking solution.
- **Case studies**: VD-STrans (incremental BPE) achieves +10.9% over the human SOTA (Jiang & Gong, 2026) with a simulated-acceptance rating of 8.0 (ScholarPeer) / 6.5 (Stanford); DynaSpec-RAG (zero-shot time-series forecasting, 0.27M trainable params on frozen foundation models) attains average MSE 0.1892 across 7 benchmarks (ETT, Weather, Electricity, Exchange) vs 0.1940 for TS-RAG, 0.2008 Chronos-Bolt, 0.2104 TTM, and 0.2380 Chronos.
- **Cost and compounding**: ~$3,765 and ~2.5 days per task (dominated by idea refinement and experimentation); sequential self-iteration on BPE tokenization yields +10.9% (VD-STrans) → +9.6% (BXT-Transducer) → +8.2% (SBR-Transducer).
- **Human evaluation (Table 10; 9 reviewers, 33 papers)**: standalone 1–5 Likert scores 4.0–4.3 on experiments/ablations, 3.7 overall venue-readiness; pairwise vs human papers: 3.5 on benchmark breadth (favors ScientistTwo), 3.3 ablations, but 2.9 on methodological rigor (favors humans), 3.0 overall parity.

## Authors & Credibility

The work comes from Google Cloud AI Research (Jaehyun Nam, Jinsung Yoon, Yanzhou Pan, Rui Meng, Parthasarathy Ranganathan, Tomas Pfister) with Yubo Wang (University of Waterloo). The team has an unusually deep track record in this exact area — they authored the components ScientistTwo builds on: ScientistOne (Meng et al., 2026), MARS, MLE-STAR and DS-STAR (Nam et al.), ScholarPeer (Goyal et al., 2026) and PaperOrchestra (Song et al., 2026) — which lends strong engineering credibility but also creates a self-referential evaluation ecosystem (their drafter, their reviewer, their audit). The paper is transparent about model usage (Gemini 3.6 Flash + Claude Opus 4.8), publishes the full 107-problem benchmark lists in Appendix A.1, reports negative results (ICLR-2026 subset loss to AutoSOTA, sub-spotlight ratings), and includes a human-expert study — all markers of a serious, well-resourced empirical effort. A project website is provided (scientist-two.github.io).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The individual ingredients (idea evolution, simulated review, ablation agents) exist in prior systems; the genuine novelty is the *closed-loop integration* — rebuttals that run real experiments, a meta-reviewer that can force new ideas, and ablation-gated acceptance of gains. This is an integrative-systems contribution rather than a new algorithm, which the paper itself implicitly concedes by positioning against ScientistOne/AutoSOTA.
- **Evaluation circularity (the biggest red flag)**: ScholarPeer is simultaneously the *training signal* (rebuttal loop target score ≥ 8) and the *headline evaluation metric* (7.5±1.3, 91.9%). The authors acknowledge this and lean on the held-out Stanford Agentic Reviewer — but that reviewer is also an automated agent with documented biases toward many figures/tables and comprehensive experiments, precisely the artifacts ScientistTwo's draft-enhancer optimizes. Goodhart risk is real: "72.1% acceptance" is acceptance by a specific LLM reviewer, not by a human program committee. The human study (overall parity 3.0/5, methodological rigor 2.9 *below* humans) is the most credible signal and is more sober than the automated numbers.
- **Mean-vs-median gap and selective gain reporting**: the headline "25.2% average relative improvement" has a median of only 7.7% — the mean is driven by outlier tasks; and on the ICLR 2026 subset ScientistTwo's average gain (3.8%) *loses* to AutoSOTA (8.5%), which the paper does report but buries in a hypothesis paragraph. Gains are also computed only over the 86 successful cases (19.6% of problems failed and are excluded), and "success" is adjudicated by ScientistTwo's own critic agents.
- **Reproducibility**: strong by the field's standards — all four CoE integrity audits pass (49/49 reproducible scores, 0 hallucinated references out of 1,814, 0/49 spec violations), full benchmark paper lists and per-stage budgets are disclosed, and the framework generalizes across coding backends (Claude Code 80% success vs Antigravity/Gemini 60% on 5 ICLR tasks). The $3,765-per-task cost and proprietary models (Gemini 3.6 Flash, Opus 4.8, Claude Code, Antigravity) make independent replication essentially impossible for academic labs.
- **Shallow novelty checking**: novelty is scored after retrieving just *two* reference papers via Google Search — far too thin to detect concurrent or cross-field duplicates, so the "novelty-ranked" seed pool rests on a weak verification layer.
- **Significance**: pushing 80% of recent top-venue problems past their published SOTA — with executed rebuttals and passing integrity audits — is the strongest result in autonomous research to date, and the compounding BPE iteration (10.9% → 9.6% → 8.2% with diminishing but positive returns) is a genuinely important demonstration. Still below spotlight quality (Stanford 5.7 vs 6.1), as the authors concede.

## Strengths

- Closes the loop that prior autonomous-scientist systems left open: peer review triggers *executed* supplementary experiments, not prose edits; meta-review can force method-level reinvention with strict empirical re-verification.
- Ablation studies are automated, component-attributed, and used to *prune* the method (human-expert-style subtraction), and the acceptance rule requires the gain to survive ablation — a meaningful barrier against metric-hacking.
- Rare honesty for a flagship systems paper: reports the ICLR-subset loss to AutoSOTA, the sub-spotlight ratings, the reward-hacking failure case caught by the spec filter, and a 9-reviewer human study including the dimension where humans still win (methodological rigor 2.9 vs 3.0 parity).
- Integrity-by-construction (reproducible scripts, hallucinated-citation grounding, method-code audit) with a clean ablation showing each safeguard matters (19/1840 → 0/1814 hallucinated refs).
- Broad, well-documented evaluation: 107 problems across LLMs, RL, optimization, theory, time series, neuroscience, speech, privacy; plus cost analysis, backend-generalization, and iterative-compounding experiments.

## Limitations

- Does not reach spotlight/oral-level conceptual breakthroughs; human reviewers still rate methodological rigor above it, and gains concentrate on algorithmic refinement rather than paradigm-level discovery (acknowledged by the authors).
- ~$3,800 and 2.5 days per task — impractical for most academic labs; the authors flag open-source-model substitution as open work.
- Primary quantitative claims rest on automated reviewers, one of which (ScholarPeer) is also the optimization target; headline mean gain (25.2%) far exceeds the median (7.7%) and excludes the 21 failed problems.
- Novelty checking retrieves only two references; subset-first screening could bias which ideas ever reach full evaluation; and the whole evaluation stack (drafter, reviewer, audit, baselines ScientistOne/ScholarPeer/PaperOrchestra) is largely same-group, so independent validation is needed.

## Relevance

该论文是"AI 自动科研 / AI Scientist"方向目前最完整、评测规模最大的系统工作，若你关注 LLM 多智能体系统能否闭环完成"提想法-做实验-过评审"的完整科研回路、以及自动化评审与科研完整性审计（reward hacking、幻觉引用）等治理问题，本文是必读的当前最强基线与设计参照。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the 86 generated papers survive a real human program committee (not just the Stanford Agentic Reviewer), and how large is the gap between LLM-reviewer acceptance and human acceptance for this exact paper set?
- How skewed is the 25.2% mean gain — what are the top-5 outlier tasks, and does the median 7.7% (or the failed 21 problems) change under an independent re-run of the codebases?
- Does the rebuttal loop Goodhart the reviewers — e.g., if the reviewer agent's known figure/table biases were published, would the Draft Enhancer exploit them, and can an adversarially-held-out reviewer detect this?
- Can novelty verification scale beyond 2 retrieved references (e.g., embedding search over full-corpus arXiv) without breaking the 2.5-day/`$3.8K` budget?
- How much of the gain survives replacing Gemini 3.6 Flash / Opus 4.8 with open-weight models, and does the exploration–exploitation idea-evolution loop still function with weaker coders (Antigravity already dropped to 60% success)?
- The compounding experiment shows diminishing returns (+10.9% → +9.6% → +8.2%); does the sequence plateau, and can cross-problem transfer of discovered tricks (rather than per-problem re-derivation) extend the frontier more cheaply?
