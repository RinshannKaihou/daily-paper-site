> **一句话总结：** 针对各类 LLM 推理优化（量化、KV-cache 压缩、投机解码、batching、稀疏注意力）各自报告互不可比加速比的问题，本文在统一的开销–质量–延迟三轴上构建 Pareto 前沿图谱：在 RunPod L4/A100/H100 上实测 54 组 Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct（vLLM 0.12）锚点配置、校准 Profiling 模拟器补全组合空间（跨 campaign 漂移 <1.5%，总花费仅 $18），并用 GSM8K 实测各精度质量；核心结论是 FP8 权重量化以 0.61–0.65× 延迟保留 99.4% 精度、出现在 4 个 regime 赢家中的 3 个，AWQ-4bit 在 L4 上最快（0.34×）但严格准确率掉 5.9%，而"看似正常的"朴素 FP8 KV-cache 吞吐正常却 0/200 全错——纯速度基准会推荐它，而质量轴直接将其淘汰。

> **TL;DR:** This paper builds a cost-quality-latency Pareto atlas for LLM inference optimizations, placing quantization, KV-cache compression, speculation, batching, and sparse attention (alone and stacked) on common axes. It measures 54 anchor configurations of Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct on vLLM 0.12 across RunPod L4/A100/H100 (total spend $18; cross-campaign drift <1.5%), calibrates a Vidur-style profiled simulator to fill the combinatorial space, and scores quality per precision on GSM8K (200 questions, 5-shot). Key numbers: AWQ-4bit cuts per-token latency to 0.34x of the FP16 baseline on L4 but loses 5.9% strict GSM8K accuracy (0.720 vs 0.765); FP8 weight quantization keeps 99.4% of baseline accuracy (0.760 vs 0.765) at 0.61–0.65x latency on all three GPUs and appears in 3 of 4 regime winners; a naive FP8 KV cache sustains normal throughput yet answers 0/200 GSM8K questions correctly — a configuration a speed-only benchmark would recommend; n-gram speculation measures at parity (0.90–0.98x, adds nothing). Stacked combinations reach the frontier more often than single methods (9/15 vs 9/21); the winner shifts with constraint and GPU (H100 takes tight latency at $4.62/Mtok, A100 takes throughput/low-cost at $0.106/Mtok). Best read as a comparability protocol plus practitioner rules, not a new optimization.

## 核心贡献

- **开销–质量–延迟 Pareto 图谱（atlas）**：把量化（AWQ-4bit、FP8 权重）、KV-cache 压缩（FP8 KV）、投机解码（n-gram）、batching、稀疏注意力（NSA）及其可组合的堆叠配置放到同一组坐标轴上，计算 Pareto 前沿，并按部署约束（tight-latency / high-throughput / low-cost / quality-floor）标出每个 regime 的主导配置——给出的是"可比较性与支配结构"而非又一个孤立的加速比。
- **先测量后模拟（measure-then-simulate）方法论**：组合空间（方法×组合×硬件）太大无法穷举，作者在真实硬件上实测 54 个锚点配置（每 GPU 18 个、三个 campaign），用锚点校准 profiling 模拟器（Vidur 式）补全空间；校准在锚点 batch size 上精确复现全部 54 个锚点，跨 campaign 锚点漂移 <1.5%，模拟点显式携带误差条并在前沿图上标注哪些点是实测锚点。
- **质量轴改变赢家（"Measuring quality changes who wins"）**：GSM8K 质量臂（200 题、5-shot、greedy）逐精度实测——朴素 FP8 KV-cache 吞吐正常但 0/200 全错，被纯速度指标会留下的这个配置直接淘汰；AWQ-4bit 的严格准确率损失（−5.9%）定位在答案格式而非算术（flexible 抽取与 FP16 基线打平）。
- **一套实践者规则**：FP8 权重量化是保质量的"主力"（进入 3/4 regime 赢家）；AWQ 在廉价/带宽受限 GPU（L4）上加速最大但踩在质量地板边上；堆叠组合比单方法更常上前沿（9/15 vs 9/21）；赢家随约束与 GPU 漂移（H100 拿 tight-latency，A100 拿吞吐与低价 $0.106/Mtok），A100 价格涨 50% 后赢家迁到 L4——图谱存在的意义就是价格/内核变化后重规划。

## Key Contributions

- **A cost-quality-latency Pareto atlas**: places the major inference optimizations (quantization, KV compression/eviction, speculation, batching, sparse attention) and their compatible combinations on common axes, computes the Pareto frontier, and names the dominant configuration per deployment regime (tight latency, high throughput, low cost, quality floor).
- **A measure-then-simulate methodology**: 54 anchor configurations measured on real hardware (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct on vLLM 0.12, RunPod L4 / A100 80GB PCIe / H100 PCIe, 18 anchors per GPU across three campaigns, total spend $18), used to calibrate a profiled simulator (following Vidur) that fills the combinatorial space; simulator reproduces all 54 anchors at anchored batch sizes, and cross-campaign anchor drift below 1.5% serves as the honest generalization estimate.
- **Quality measured alongside speed, changing the winners**: a GSM8K arm (200 questions, 5-shot, greedy) scores each precision — exposing that a naive FP8 KV cache posts normal throughput while answering 0/200 questions correctly, a configuration a throughput-only benchmark would endorse, and that AWQ-4bit's strict-accuracy loss is formatting rather than arithmetic (flexible extraction ties the FP16 baseline).
- **Practitioner rules with regime shifts**: FP8 weight quantization is the quality-preserving workhorse (in 3 of 4 regime winners, 0.61–0.65x latency on all three GPUs); quantization appears in 13 of 18 frontier members; stacked combinations reach the frontier more often than single methods (9/15 = 60% vs 9/21 = 43%); the winner moves with the binding constraint and the GPU (H100 owns tight latency at $4.62/Mtok; A100 owns throughput and low cost at $0.106/Mtok), and a 50% A100 price shock moves the cost/quality-floor winners to the L4.

## Methodology

The paper's central artifact is built in four steps (their Algorithm 1). First, **axes**: every configuration is placed on quality (task accuracy or agreement with the full-precision model), latency (TTFT and TPOT at P50 and P99), and cost (dollars per million tokens derived from measured throughput and GPU hourly price), with throughput and GPU memory as supporting metrics; quality is always co-reported so nothing wins by silently degrading answers. Second, **anchors**: the combinatorial space (method x combination x hardware) is far too large to measure exhaustively, so they measure 54 anchor configurations of one model (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, vLLM 0.12) on one serving workload (512-token prompts, 128-token outputs) across three GPUs (RunPod L4 $0.39/hr, A100 80GB PCIe $1.39/hr, H100 PCIe $2.89/hr, July-2026 rates), across three campaigns (synthetic-prompt, realistic extractive prompt, and a final six-arm matrix — FP16, AWQ, FP8 weights, FP8 KV cache, two speculation arms — plus the GSM8K quality stage). Third, **calibration**: a profiled simulator is fit so its latency/throughput predictions match the anchors at the anchored batch sizes, then used to fill the full configuration and combination space; simulated points carry calibrated error bars and every frontier marks which points are measured anchors. Fourth, **frontier + atlas**: per (model, workload), the Pareto frontier over (quality, latency, cost) is computed and the dominant configuration is labeled under tight-latency, high-throughput, and low-cost constraints. The quality arm runs GSM8K (200 questions, 5-shot, greedy) for FP16, AWQ-4bit, FP8 weights, and FP8 KV cache, with both strict and flexible answer extraction. Scope caveats are explicit: sparse attention (NSA) is simulated only (no stock-vLLM implementation); speculation is anchored as n-gram prompt-lookup because draft-model speculation is unimplemented in vLLM 0.12's V1 engine (raises NotImplementedError) and no EAGLE-3 head exists for the target model; of the protocol's four workloads (ShareGPT chat, LongBench/RULER long-context, HumanEval code, GSM8K math), only Math is measured.

## Results & Highlights

- **Frontier structure**: 18 of 36 configurations on the calibrated grid are non-dominated; stacked combinations reach the frontier more often than single methods — 9 of 15 (60%) vs 9 of 21 (43%). Quantization appears in 13 of 18 frontier members, speculation and sparsity in 6 each, KV compression in 4, batching in 3.
- **AWQ-4bit (speed vs quality trade-off)**: GSM8K strict accuracy drops from the FP16 baseline's 0.765 to 0.720 (a 5.9% relative loss), missing the 95% quality floor (0.727 needed) by a margin inside the ±3 pp sampling error of n=200; under flexible extraction AWQ scores 0.810 vs the baseline's 0.800 — i.e., the loss is answer formatting, not arithmetic. Speed payoff grows as the GPU gets cheaper/more memory-bound: TPOT ratio 0.53x on H100, 0.49x on A100, 0.34x on L4.
- **FP8 weight quantization (the workhorse)**: keeps 0.760 strict / 0.805 flexible GSM8K accuracy vs 0.765 / 0.800 for FP16 (99.4% retention) at a flat 0.61–0.65x per-token latency on all three GPUs, and is part of three of the four regime winners in the atlas.
- **Naive FP8 KV cache (the headline failure)**: sustains normal measured throughput while scoring 0/200 GSM8K questions correctly on both strict and flexible metrics (vs 153/200 for FP16) — a configuration a speed-only benchmark would recommend and the joint cost-quality-latency accounting eliminates.
- **Speculation at parity**: under two prompt designs, n-gram speculative decoding measures at 0.90–0.98x of baseline latency — i.e., adds nothing on this stack.
- **Regime winners and cost**: the H100 takes the tight-latency regime (low-latency stack at $4.62/Mtok, quality 0.746); the A100 takes high throughput and low cost (throughput stack at $0.106/Mtok, quality 0.742); quality-floor-at-min-cost is batched@A100 at $0.289/Mtok (quality 0.765). No L4 configuration meets the tight-latency budget at all; a 50% A100 price shock moves the cost and quality-floor winners to the L4.
- **Simulator fidelity and budget**: per-arm batch curves reproduce all 54 anchors exactly at the anchored batch sizes (interpolative by construction); the realistic generalization estimate is the cross-campaign anchor drift, below 1.5% across three independent pod sessions on different physical hosts. Total measurement spend: $18.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The framing is the contribution — comparability and dominance as deliverables — and it is genuinely useful; no prior work jointly places the full optimization stack plus combinations on cost-quality-latency axes with an explicit dominance map (MLPerf and Spec-Bench standardize measurement; Vidur provides simulation; reference [14] frames cost-quality for model choice). But it is a benchmarking/atlas study with one model, one engine, one short-context workload, and 36 grid configurations; half of them (18/36) being non-dominated says the grid is small enough that Pareto membership is a weak signal.
- **Rigor — the FP8-KV strawman risk**: the headline "0/200 correct" result comes from the *naive* FP8 KV-cache path without calibrated scales, while the paper itself cites calibrated methods (KIVI, TurboQuant, KVQuant, PyramidKV, H2O) that exist precisely to avoid this collapse; the authors flag this as limitation (3) and defer calibrated KV to future anchors, yet the abstract still leads with the 0/200 configuration. The finding is a valid indictment of naive deployment, but a reader could over-generalize it to "KV quantization is broken."
- **Rigor — the speculation verdict over-generalizes**: "speculation adds nothing on this stack" is really "n-gram prompt-lookup adds nothing on a 512-in/128-out synthetic/math workload." EAGLE-3 — listed among the optimizations under study — could not be anchored at all (vLLM 0.12 V1 raises NotImplementedError; no EAGLE head for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct), and n-gram PLD is weakest exactly where prompt-output overlap is low, so the parity result (0.90–0.98x) is close to a foregone conclusion rather than evidence against speculative decoding generally.
- **Rigor — interpolative calibration**: the simulator "reproduces all 54 anchors exactly at the anchored batch sizes (the fit is interpolative by construction)" — a zero on-anchor residual is by construction, not a fidelity test; the honest extrapolation evidence is only the <1.5% cross-campaign drift, and quality is never simulated, so frontier cells mixing measured quality with simulated latency inherit asymmetric error the atlas does not propagate.
- **Statistical power of the quality arm**: one task (GSM8K), n=200 (±3 pp), one model. The AWQ-vs-floor verdict (0.720 vs 0.726 needed) sits inside that error bar — the authors say so, but the abstract's "misses the 95% quality floor" states the boundary case more firmly than the data supports.
- **Workload bias against the KV family**: the measured workload is 512-in/128-out; KV compression/eviction methods pay off at long context, and the protocol's own long-context workload (LongBench/RULER) is listed but not measured — so the atlas structurally underrates the KV-compression family while eliminating it via the (naive) FP8-KV anchor.
- **Reproducibility**: strong — open model, public workloads, stated prices and pricing assumptions, stated engine/GPU versions, $18 spend, and anchors marked on every figure. **Significance**: the specific coordinates (July-2026 RunPod prices, vLLM 0.12 kernel maturity) will not hold — the authors concede this and argue the dominance structure and protocol carry over; the durable value is the protocol and the quality-alongside-speed discipline, less the specific regime winners. **Red flags**: none severe — mainly the abstract's confident framing of two boundary-case results (naive FP8-KV collapse, AWQ floor miss) relative to their caveated status in the body.

## Strengths

- Correct and much-needed framing: isolated speedup numbers are incomparable; dominance under a binding constraint is the decision-relevant question, and the atlas answers it regime by regime.
- Quality is always co-measured with speed, and the paper demonstrates why that matters with a vivid failure: a naive FP8 KV cache that looks healthy on throughput yet scores 0/200 — a configuration speed-only benchmarks would recommend.
- Honest epistemics: measured anchors vs simulated points are marked on every frontier, simulated points carry error bars, the interpolative nature of the calibration is stated outright, and the six-item limitations section pre-empts the obvious objections (single task/model, unanchored draft-model speculation, uncalibrated KV, simulated-only sparsity, price volatility).
- Frugal and reproducible: the entire measured evidence base cost $18 on public cloud GPUs with a fully specified protocol (engine version, SKUs, token counts, prices), making re-runs after price/kernel changes practical — which is exactly the maintenance loop the authors advocate.
- The AWQ failure-mode analysis (strict drop, flexible parity) localizes the precision loss to answer formatting rather than arithmetic — a concrete, reusable diagnostic distinction for quantization deployment.
- Regime-shift and price-shock analysis (a 50% A100 price change moves the winners to the L4) turns the atlas from a static table into a re-planning tool.

## Limitations

- One model (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct), one serving engine (vLLM 0.12), one measured workload shape (512-in/128-out), and one quality task (GSM8K, n=200, ±3 pp sampling error); the AWQ-vs-95%-floor verdict is a boundary case inside that error bar, and the other three protocol workloads (chat, long-context, code) are unmeasured.
- Sparse attention is the one factor modeled rather than measured (no stock-vLLM NSA implementation), so every frontier claim involving sparsity rests on simulation alone.
- Draft-model speculation (EAGLE-3) could not be anchored — vLLM 0.12's V1 engine raises NotImplementedError and no EAGLE head exists for the model — so the speculation axis is represented by its weakest variant (n-gram PLD).
- The KV-cache anchor is the naive FP8 path without calibrated scales; scale-calibrated FP8-KV or eviction methods (H2O, PyramidKV) may avoid the measured quality collapse and are left for future work.
- Calibration is interpolative at anchored batch sizes; generalization is bounded by the ~1.5% cross-campaign drift rather than a held-out extrapolation test, and quality is never simulated.
- Cost coordinates are July-2026 RunPod rates and shift with the market; kernel maturity (e.g., AWQ/FP8 kernel speedups) also drifts, so absolute positions will not hold — only the dominance structure and the protocol are claimed to transfer.

## Relevance

与"极端性能优化下的可靠性"方向高度相关：本文最核心的实证发现正是低精度激进优化引发的静默正确性坍塌——朴素 FP8 KV-cache 吞吐完全正常却 0/200 全错，纯速度指标无法察觉，只有把质量/正确性纳入统一度量才能拦截；AWQ 的 5.9% 严格准确率损失（格式而非算术）同样是量化精度影响输出正确性的具体案例，其"质量地板 + 支配结构"协议可直接迁移到推理可靠性与 SDC 检测的评估框架中。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would scale-calibrated FP8 KV caches (per-head/per-channel scales, KIVI/KVQuant/TurboQuant-style) survive the GSM8K quality arm and reclaim frontier seats for KV-bearing stacks, especially on the unmeasured long-context workloads (LongBench/RULER at 8k–128k tokens) where KV compression matters most?
- Does the AWQ formatting-vs-arithmetic failure split replicate across other models (Llama-3, Mistral) and tasks with different answer conventions (HumanEval code, ShareGPT chat), and can a format-aware quality floor (flexible extraction) be a standard co-metric in quantization evaluation?
- Would a real draft-model speculator (an EAGLE-3 head trained for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, or an engine that supports draft speculation, e.g., SGLang) overturn the parity verdict at longer outputs or chat-like workloads where n-gram PLD has no prompt overlap to exploit?
- How does the frontier move under extrapolation to batch sizes beyond the anchors, and can the calibration be validated held-out (e.g., leave-one-batch-curve-out) instead of relying on cross-campaign drift as the sole generalization estimate?
- Does the FP8-weights-as-workhorse conclusion persist at other model scales (1.5B–70B) and under disaggregated serving (DistServe/Mooncake-style prefill-decode separation), where the latency axes separate?
- Could the atlas gain a fourth, reliability-oriented axis — e.g., silent output-corruption rate under hardware faults or bit-flips — so that quality floors and SDC detection are optimized jointly with cost and latency?
