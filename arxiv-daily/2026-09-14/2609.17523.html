Nesting harness evolution inside model RL lifts a 4B biomedical agent from 42.2% to 73.3% — both loops must co-evolve.

ScienceBuddy: Recursive-in-Recursive Self-Improvement for Interactive Scientific Agents ↗

「递归中递归」自改进：内层递归进化 harness（指令/技能），外层递归用 rubric 奖励做 GRPO 强化学习，两环耦合共同进化。

Shuhan Xue, Jianyuan Zhong, Ziyuan Nan, et al. · 13 authors · 2026-09-15 · PhAI Labs · Fudan · Oxford · Stanford · Princeton · LLM agents self-improvement agent harness reinforcement learning scientific AI
Key Metric
+31.1pp

Single-attempt test accuracy on 895 held-out biomedical tasks (LAB-Bench + Biomni-Eval1): 42.2% → 73.3% after three coupled co-evolution cycles of Qwen3.5-4B.

33.3% of problems flip wrong→right; only 2.2% regress right→wrong.

Why This Matters

A deployed, end-to-end loop turning real user interactions into executable tasks and rubric rewards — directly reusable for agent memory, context engineering, and RL data construction.

把真实（及模拟）用户交互转化为可执行任务与 rubric 奖励、再驱动 harness 进化与 GRPO 权重更新的完整闭环设计。

⚠ Critical Edge

No external baselines anywhere — every headline number is a self-before/after comparison, and part of the +31 pp may be GPT-6 Astra's procedural knowledge distilled into skills, not recursion.

Methodology

How the two recursions are wired

The agent is a joint policy μθ,H over a ReAct-style loop; the harness H builds context from history, memory, skills, and tool docs. Collaborations are segmented into self-contained Harbor tasks, each paired with a rubric derived from the full interaction trajectory (researcher replies inform criteria but are not ground truth).

Inner recursion (model frozen): a fixed GPT-6 Astra reviews failed trajectories and proposes exactly one schema-validated, single-scope edit — an instruction change or one scoped skill. Edits are accepted only if paired development evaluation improves (ΔS > 0), with regression checks on previously solved tasks.

Outer recursion (harness frozen): calibrate environment difficulty, collect fresh on-policy rollouts, score with task-adaptive rubric rewards, update weights with token-level GRPO (group-relative advantages, clipped ratios, KL to a frozen reference). Researcher messages and tool outputs are excluded from optimized tokens.

Coupled run: 3 cycles × (10 harness-evolution steps → select Hk★ by validation accuracy → 20 RL updates), starting from Qwen3.5-4B. Workspace ships 224 tools across 22 modules with Python/R/Bash runtimes and a frozen data lake.

Strengths

What the paper gets right

  • Clean decomposition into four falsifiable RQs, with fixed-model and fixed-harness controls isolating where gains come from — rare discipline for a system paper.
  • Rigorous information boundaries: private answers hidden from policy, simulator, interpreter, and harness proposer; the diagnostic score qt is separated from the RL reward — no reward leakage.
  • Gated, regression-aware harness evolution (schema-validated single-scope edits, ΔS > 0 acceptance) guards against degenerate prompt hacking.
  • Comparable harness-only (+20.0 pp) and model-only (+19.5 pp) gains support the co-evolution thesis rather than one dominating mechanism.
  • Ships as a deployed product: asynchronous background updates, inspectable trajectories, rubrics mined from real collaboration rather than post-hoc annotation.

Limitations

Read before citing

  • No external baselines (GEPA, ACE, Reflexion, DGM, frontier models) and no per-edit / per-feedback-source ablations — attribution within each loop is open.
  • Small inventory: 895 tasks, two benchmark families, biomedical QA formats, 4B backbone; generalization to other sciences and frontier scale untested.
  • Quantitative interaction data is simulated (bounded Qwen3.8-27B simulator); real-researcher evidence is qualitative only — two case studies (JAK1, ARL4C).
  • Editing depends on fixed proprietary GPT-6 Astra — "self-improvement" is not self-contained; its ceiling is the reflector's capability.
  • Single runs without variance estimates; three cycles is short for a "continual learning" claim; no forgetting, cost, or failure-mode analysis.

Questions for Further Exploration

Open threads this paper leaves behind

  1. How much of the +20.0 pp harness gain survives with an open-weights editor, or GEPA/ACE-style prompt search at identical budget?
  2. Does the recursion stay stable and monotonic past three cycles (10–20), or do harness drift, reward overfitting, and forgetting emerge?
  3. Can collaboration-derived rubrics be validated against expert rubrics — and does divergence bound final accuracy?
  4. Do harness-only gains transfer to 70B+/frontier models that may already internalize the learned procedures?
  5. What happens with genuinely adversarial or noisy researcher feedback, versus the bounded simulator that never reveals answers?
  6. Does pass@4 growth trade off against first-attempt accuracy and inference cost — is the model solving new problems or hedging across attempts?
