Why this matters

Safety-relevant SAE features can be triaged for controllability with zero forward passes — steering sweeps are expensive; geometry is free.

对安全审计与可控干预（拒绝行为调制）有直接参考价值。

Critical edge

Practical utility is oversold: the signal is rank-only — best cross-validated R² ≈ 0.07 — geometry gives an ordering, not a usable steering coefficient.

— Critical Assessment
01Method — steerability as a censored measurement

Three phases, one protocol

identical across all conditions

Steering injects a scaled SAE decoder direction into the residual stream, h′ = h + α·v_f. Steerability α*(f) is the smallest α that shifts refusal rate by a pre-registered T = 0.10 over 100 frozen SALADBench prompts; features never reaching threshold are right-censored, and degenerate completions (token collapse, loops, PPL > e⁵) are filtered before scoring.

  • P1SelectContrast-scoring ranks features by activation-frequency gap between task prompts and neutral text (top 300); a causal clamp check discards low-impact directions → 75–300 features per condition.
  • P2MeasureDose–response over α ∈ {0.25 … 20}, scored by a keyword refusal classifier; α* read off the curve. “Well-identified” = < 50% censoring and no coherence collapse.
  • P3PredictSpearman ρ between pre-computed geometry metrics and log α*, AUROC for steerable (α* ≤ 3) vs. hard, permutation nulls (n = 1,000), ridge CV, threshold sensitivity T ∈ {0.05–0.20}.

Mechanism — co-recruitment

why crowded directions steer cheaply

Easy-to-steer features have decoder neighbors 3.4× more aligned with the mean refusal direction than hard-to-steer ones (0.129 vs. 0.038; ρ = −0.450, p = 0.0001) — steering appears to recruit nearby refusal-adjacent directions.

Negative results

what does not predict steerability
  • Co-activation correlation — null in 5/6 conditions; its apparent 9B-L20 significance (ρ = +0.328) is a zero-inflation artifact: 59/75 features never fire on the reference corpus.
  • Cheap behavioral proxy (refusal shift at α = 1): ρ = −0.339 — weaker than geometry (−0.435 / −0.404). Weights beat one steered run.
  • Ridge CV R² ≤ 0.068 everywhere — the signal is ordinal only, never a calibrated coefficient.
  • Qwen3 / BatchTopK — geometry predicts whether a feature steers at all, not the ordering.

Measurement caveat

the dependent variable is blunt

The keyword refusal classifier was validated against an independent judge on 160 completions: precision 97.9%, recall 57.5%, F1 72.4% — it systematically misses soft refusals, so reported α* values are biased upward, and the true geometry–steerability relationship is probably stronger than reported.

Worse: judge agreement drops to 50–60% exactly at α = 10–20 — where the censored features that define the deep-layer boundary live.

03Verdict — read it with both eyes open

Strengths

+
a genuinely practical question, answered falsifiably
  • Clean formalization: censored α*(f) at a pre-registered threshold — steering cost made measurable.
  • Unusually thorough controls for a workshop paper: permutation nulls, leave-one-metric-out ablations, outlier checks, threshold sensitivity, classifier validation.
  • Honest boundary mapping — deep-layer collapse, the Qwen3 failure, and the zero-inflation artifact are surfaced, not buried.
  • Mechanistic follow-through: the 3.4× alignment probe turns a correlation into a testable co-recruitment hypothesis.
  • Breadth for the budget: four models, two SAE families, 16K–131K dictionaries.

Limitations

–
where the claim stops short
  • Rank-only: geometry orders features but cannot estimate usable coefficients — prioritization, not calibration.
  • One behavior, one direction: refusal amplification with α > 0 only; suppression and off-target effects deferred.
  • The 57.5%-recall classifier makes every α* noisy and upward-biased.
  • Small n (75–100 Gemma features), single-run Llama layers; the Qwen3 diagnosis (BatchTopK vs. attention-sink filtering) is confounded and un-ablated.
  • The fixed α grid causes the deep-layer censoring that then defines the paper's “boundary” — a per-layer calibrated grid might dissolve part of it.
  • Code unreleased (pending safety review) — the refusal keyword list is not independently checkable yet.
04Open questions — where this goes next
  1. Q1Does the signal survive a proper refusal scorer (recall > 90%) — and does the deep-layer boundary partly dissolve once censoring is measured reliably?
  2. Q2Can geometry predict steering cost for the suppression direction (α < 0) — the adversarially relevant case — or is co-recruitment asymmetric?
  3. Q3Do crowded (easy-to-steer) features cause measurably larger off-target damage on GPQA/TruthfulQA, as the co-recruitment account predicts?
  4. Q4Is the Qwen3/BatchTopK failure batch-level sparsity competition or attention-sink filtering? A same-base-model ablation would disentangle it.
  5. Q5Would per-layer grid calibration (α scaled by residual-stream norm; αmax ≈ 188 at L24 vs. ≈ 75 at L22) extend the usable depth range?
  6. Q6Can geometry plus a few cheap probes (e.g., response at α = 1) be combined to calibrate actual coefficients, not just ranks?
