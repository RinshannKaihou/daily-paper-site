Why This Matters

For long-running self-evolving agents, DRPG shows exactly when distilled policies beat example memory — and when they silently do nothing.

「何时策略级指导有效、何时失效」的任务依赖性结论，对设计长期运行的自进化 agent 记忆与反思机制有直接参考价值。

Critical Edge

≈3× the token cost per query — for a method whose mean gain outside text-to-SQL is ≈0.

On 3 of 6 datasets the difference is not significant in either direction; DRPG loses nearly half the model configurations there (e.g. scout on DDXPlus: −10.3 pts).

How the loop works

StreamBench online protocol

Instances arrive one at a time; every answer gets binary correctness feedback from the environment. DRPG layers a policy generator on top of exemplar memory:

  1. Retrieve successes — top-k past correct exemplars (k=16), mirroring Self-StreamICL.
  2. Contrastive retrieval — a second retriever fetches k/2 correct + k/2 incorrect cases.
  3. Generate policy — a separate (possibly cheaper) LLM distills ≤5 actionable bullets from the contrastive set.
  4. Answer — conditioned on question + retrieved successes + fresh policy.
  5. Store feedback — label enters memory; the loop continues, no offline phase.

Deliberately un-tuned: retriever (bge-base-en-v1.5 + FAISS), prompts, temperature 0, shuffle seed 42 reused verbatim from StreamBench, shared by all methods.

Strengths

what it gets right
  • Attacks a precise gap: streaming memory re-supplies examples but never synthesizes what to do differently — so identical errors recur.
  • The task-dependence analysis is the best science: generated-policy excerpts show why Spider policies generalize while DDXPlus policies collapse into narrow disease heuristics.
  • Thorough ablations — retrieval composition, stateless operation, cross-model generators, and a no-feedback control isolating the causal ingredient.
  • Unusually rigorous statistics for the genre: paired Wilcoxon per-dataset and pooled, with negative results reported in-table, not buried.
  • Full cost transparency: per-configuration token counts and a score-vs-tokens plot.

Limitations

read before deploying
  • Idealized feedback — dense, clean, binary, per-step labels; sparse, delayed, or noisy feedback untested.
  • Single data ordering (seed 42), temperature 0, no variance across runs; order sensitivity unexplored.
  • No reasoning LLMs (no o3 / DeepSeek R1), no OpenAI/Claude families; thinking modes had to be disabled.
  • Dynamic Cheatsheet — the closest feedback-conditioned competitor — is not empirically compared.
  • ≈3× per-query token cost, which buys nothing on instance-specific tasks.
  • Known failure mode (maverick HotpotQA −12.1) left undiagnosed; prompt-paraphrase sensitivity untested.

Open questions

for further exploration
  • Does the advantage survive sparse or delayed feedback (only a ρ fraction of steps labeled), or shrink with feedback density?
  • Would a confidence gate that drops the policy on high-confidence retrieval have prevented the maverick HotpotQA collapse?
  • Do reasoning models that already self-critique (o3-style, R1) benefit at all — or does policy text just consume context?
  • Can hierarchical policies (task-level rules + cluster-level sub-policies) fix the heuristic degeneration on DDXPlus and DS-1000?
  • Can regeneration be amortized (every N steps, or on retrieval-distribution shift) to preserve gains at baseline cost?
  • How sensitive are results to stream ordering and non-stationary task mixtures, given the single-seed, single-stream evaluation?
