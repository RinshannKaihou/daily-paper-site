> **一句话总结：** 本文从几何视角解剖循环 Transformer（looped transformer）中"多循环一步反而变差"的现象：在 MATH-500、GSM8K、HellaSwag 等九个模型-任务条件下，大量有害更新其实是"方向对但步子太大"（初始效用斜率 A>0 而全步效用 ΔU<0 的 finite-step failure），路径曲率分解精确刻画了这种进步丢失，而基于初始斜率与曲率的局部二次模型能预测最优步长——固定 1/4 步即可在 72.2–83.2% 的选定失效中恢复正效用增益，按每个更新自适应选步长（二次规则）在 Ouro-1.4B/GSM8K 上把恢复率从 71.0% 提升到 94.2%。

> **TL;DR:** This paper explains why an extra recurrence step in looped transformers (Ouro-1.4B/2.6B, Huginn-3.5B) can hurt: on MATH-500, GSM8K and HellaSwag, 1.4–49.2% of harmful updates at fixed transitions actually move in a locally improving direction (positive initial utility slope A) but step too far (negative full-step gain ΔU). An exact pathwise curvature decomposition (for affine readouts, ΔU = A − mean KL of endpoint distributions) explains the loss, a local quadratic model predicts the best step scale (Spearman 0.995–0.9998 vs grid optima, >2 orders of magnitude lower regret than full-step), and simply taking a quarter step recovers positive reference utility for 72.2–83.2% of selected failures — rising to 94.2% on Ouro-1.4B/GSM8K with per-update quadratic scale selection (vs 71.0% for the fixed quarter step). Caveat: all gains are teacher-forced reference utility measured immediately after the update, not generation accuracy, and an oracle analysis shows most of the available gain is already captured by simply halting (endpoint selection).

## 核心贡献

- **定义并实证"有限步失效"（finite-step failure）机制**：在冻结的循环 Transformer 中，把隐藏状态更新写作 D = F(H) − H，沿模型自身的更新方向插值 γ(α) = H + αD，定义参考效用增益曲线 φ(α)。当一个有害更新（φ(1)<0）的初始斜率 A = φ′(0) > 0 时，说明方向本身在局部改善任务效用，只是全步位移走得太远。九个模型-任务条件（3 个模型 × 3 个任务）全部存在此类失效，占有害更新的 1.38%–49.18%。
- **精确的路径曲率分解**：基于积分形式泰勒定理，φ(α) = αA + ∫₀^α (α−s)φ″(s)ds，即"初始线性增益 + 累积曲率修正"；对仿射读出（Ouro 的线性 LM 头）和参考 token 平均对数概率效用，进一步证明精确恒等式 ΔU = A − C，其中 C 是端点分布的平均 KL 散度——有限步失效当且仅当 0 < A < C。
- **局部二次几何预测最优步长**：用 q(α) = Aα + Qα²（Q 为初始方向曲率的一半）预测全步增益和效用最大化步长 α̂ = clip[0,1](−A/2Q)，并用累积曲率变差 C(1) 给出尺度误差界 |α̂−α*| ≤ min{1, C(1)/κ} 和后悔值界 ≤ C(1)²/(2κ)（κ = −2Q），刻画局部预测何时在全位移上仍然可靠。
- **步长收缩干预恢复进步**：固定取 1/4 步（α=0.25）即可在四个原始评测集上恢复 72.2–83.2% 的有限步失效（中间状态效用高于两个端点）；逐更新二次规则选择步长把 Ouro-1.4B/GSM8K 上的恢复率提升到 94.2%，基于曲率上界的安全步长选择在 Ouro-1.4B/MATH-500 上恢复全部 179 个失效。
- **端点-神谕对照量化中间步长的附加价值**：比较"只在当前态与提议态中择优"（早停即可获得）与"允许中间尺度"两种神谕，发现中间尺度只额外贡献全步损失恢复的一小部分（Ouro/MATH-500 上 5.65%，多数设置 <0.5%），为步长控制的实际收益划定了诚实边界。

## Key Contributions

- **Identifies and empirically grounds the "finite-step failure" mechanism**: for a frozen looped transformer, writing the update as D = F(H) − H and interpolating along the model's own direction γ(α) = H + αD, a harmful update (full-step utility gain φ(1) < 0) with positive initial slope A = φ′(0) is locally improving in direction but overshoots in displacement. Such failures occur in all nine model-task conditions (Ouro-1.4B/2.6B and Huginn on MATH-500, GSM8K, HellaSwag), constituting 1.38–49.18% of harmful updates — including conditions with positive mean gain.
- **Exact pathwise curvature decomposition**: φ(α) = αA + ∫₀^α (α−s)φ″(s)ds separates the linear gain from accumulated curvature; for affine logit readouts with mean reference-token log-probability utility, ΔU = A − C exactly, where C is the mean KL divergence between endpoint distributions (finite-step failure ⇔ 0 < A < C). This gives both a mechanism and a numerical cross-check.
- **Local quadratic geometry for step-scale prediction and error bounds**: the model q(α) = Aα + Qα² predicts full-step gain and the utility-maximising scale α̂ = clip[0,1](−A/2Q); integrated curvature variation C(1) bounds the scale error (≤ C(1)/κ) and regret (≤ C(1)²/(2κ)) against the continuous optimum without assuming concavity (Theorem 3.6).
- **Step-contraction interventions that recover lost progress**: a fixed quarter step recovers positive reference utility in 72.2–83.2% of selected finite-step failures across four settings; per-update quadratic scale selection raises recovery to 94.2% on Ouro-1.4B/GSM8K (vs 71.0% for quarter step), and curvature-bound-based safe scales recover 179/179 failures on Ouro-1.4B/MATH-500.
- **Honest endpoint-oracle accounting**: comparing halt-only selection (better of current/next state) against a five-scale oracle shows intermediate scales add only a modest extra share (5.65% of the oracle's gain on Ouro/MATH-500; <0.5% on Huginn/GSM8K and Ouro/HellaSwag), bounding the practical payoff of intermediate scales beyond early exiting.

## Methodology

The analysis is deliberately minimal-assumption: the recurrent map F is treated as a black box (frozen model, fixed input), and all smoothness is imposed on the fixed readout utility U (mean reference-token log-probability under teacher forcing for math tasks; length-normalised option-score log-softmax for multiple choice). For a consecutive state pair (H, H⁺ = F(H)) with displacement D, the paper studies the utility gain φ(α) = U(H + αD) − U(H) on α ∈ [0,1]:

1. **Direction-step taxonomy (Def. 3.2)**: an update is a *directional failure* if the initial slope A = ⟨∇U(H), D⟩ ≤ 0, and a *finite-step failure* if A > 0 yet φ(1) < 0. The taxonomy holds the readout, direction, and endpoints fixed; only the fraction of the already-computed displacement fed to the readout varies.
2. **Pathwise decomposition (Prop. 3.3)**: the integral-form Taylor identity φ(α) = αA + ∫₀^α (α−s)φ″(s)ds attributes full-step harm to accumulated negative curvature outweighing the first-order gain. For affine logit paths, Prop. 3.4 gives the exact endpoint identity ΔU = A − (1/|M|)Σ D_KL(p_j(0)‖p_j(1)), providing a path-independent numerical check.
3. **Beneficial-scale certificates (Cor. 3.5)**: a uniform directional-curvature lower bound φ″ ≥ −L_D certifies that every α < 2A/L_D improves utility; Ouro's affine readout admits an FP64 derivative bound for L_D, Huginn's nonlinear readout does not.
4. **Quadratic scale prediction and error bounds (Thm 3.6)**: the local quadratic q(α) = Aα + Qα² yields the predicted maximiser α̂ = clip[0,1](−A/2Q); the integrated deviation of curvature from its initial value, C(a) = ∫₀^a |φ″(s) − φ″(0)|ds, bounds endpoint prediction error |ΔU − (A+Q)| ≤ C(1), scale error linearly in C(1)/κ and regret quadratically in C(1)²/2κ. Finite-grid lower/upper sums (Prop. A.6) make these bounds computable.
5. **Fixed-direction interventions**: after computing H⁺, evaluate the readout at H + α_c D for a prespecified fraction (α_c = 0.25) or a per-update predicted scale (quadratic rule or the safe range 0.9·2A/L_D); a failure is "recovered" when φ(α_c) > 0, i.e., the intermediate state beats both endpoints. Endpoint-vs-step oracles (Eq. 14) quantify the additional value of intermediate scales over simply halting.

Experimentally, A and Q are computed by automatic differentiation and Hessian-vector products through the actual readout (no full Hessian; TF32 disabled; BF16 recurrent states, FP32 readouts, FP64 utility reductions), with bitwise replay verification of original batches, derivative cross-checks (144/144 pass), and 2000-resample paired question bootstrap CIs with fixed seeds. Models: Ouro-1.4B and Ouro-2.6B (transition 4→5) and Huginn-0125/3.5B (transition 19→20) on MATH-500 (N=500), GSM8K (N=1319), and HellaSwag validation (N=10042), plus a 21-condition depth sweep (58086 model-question observations) and token-level audits within Ouro's four-step inference budget.

## Results & Highlights

- **Finite-step failures are ubiquitous**: they occur in all nine model-task conditions, accounting for 1.38% (Huginn-3.5B/HellaSwag: 65/4695 harmful updates) up to 49.18% (Ouro-1.4B/MATH-500: 179/364 harmful updates) of harmful updates — including all three conditions with positive mean gain, so the mechanism hides even inside net-improving populations. On Ouro-1.4B/MATH-500 the mean full-step utility gain at 4→5 is −8.73×10⁻³.
- **Curvature rescues endpoint prediction**: adding the local curvature term Q to the initial slope A improves full-step gain sign accuracy in all nine conditions (reaching 97.2–99.9%) and reduces MAE by 5.9–29.8×; e.g., on Ouro-1.4B/MATH-500 sign accuracy rises from 64.20% (A alone) to 98.20% (A+Q) and MAE falls from 139.41×10⁻⁴ to 11.99×10⁻⁴. Generic magnitude diagnostics (state/output change) correlate with ΔU at |Spearman| ≤ 0.285, versus >0.99 for A+Q.
- **Local geometry predicts useful scales**: on the primary subset (A>0, Q<0), predicted scales match 21-point grid optima with Spearman 0.9951 (Ouro-1.4B/MATH-500, n=315; scale MAE 0.0211) and 0.9998 (Huginn/GSM8K-train256, n=129; scale MAE 0.0072); the quadratic rule cuts mean grid regret by more than two orders of magnitude versus the first-order full-step rule (1.747×10⁻⁵ and 3.482×10⁻⁷), even against the best fixed scale chosen in hindsight per set.
- **Error bounds are informative**: across four Ouro conditions (2034 primary updates on full MATH-500/GSM8K), the integrated curvature bound C/κ has median 0.058–0.114 versus 2.84–4.61 for the naive global bound M/(2κ), covers the numerical scale-error intervals of all 2034 updates, and is below the full search range on 99.8%.
- **Step contraction recovers progress**: a fixed quarter step (α=0.25) recovers positive reference utility for 72.2–83.2% of selected finite-step failures across the four original settings. Per-update quadratic selection on Ouro-1.4B/GSM8K full test recovers 406/431 (94.2%) failures vs 306/431 (71.0%) for the quarter step, with mean gain over all 1319 questions of +2.770×10⁻³ vs −0.839×10⁻³ (paired advantage +3.610×10⁻³, 95% CI [3.366, 3.881]); curvature-bound safe scales recover 179/179 (vs 132/179) on Ouro-1.4B/MATH-500.
- **The mechanism persists inside positive-progress regimes**: a token-level audit of Ouro-1.4B/GSM8K at transitions 1→2, 2→3, 3→4 (167606 continuing reference positions each) finds 8.64–9.92% of positions in finite-step failure while aggregate full-step gains are positive, and the quadratic rule recovers 97.77–99.11% of them.
- **Honest oracle accounting**: intermediate scales add only 5.65% (Ouro-1.4B/MATH-500) of the five-scale oracle's gain over the full step already available from endpoint (halt) selection, and <0.5% on Huginn/GSM8K and Ouro/HellaSwag — most of the recoverable loss is captured by knowing when to stop, with step scaling as a smaller refinement.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: genuinely novel lens. Prior work either stabilises recurrence (STARS's Jacobian regularisation) or detects when to stop (early-exit signals); this paper instead decomposes *why* a single update hurts into direction vs displacement, and the exact KL identity ΔU = A − C for affine readouts (Prop. 3.4) plus the pathwise decomposition is a clean, verifiable theoretical contribution. It explicitly extends Asadulaev et al.'s (ICML 2026) interpolation analysis of Tiny Recursive Models from derivative computation to the curvature-reversal mechanism and recovery interventions.
- **Rigor**: high on the measurement side, but the headline metric is teacher-forced *reference utility*, not answer accuracy. The paper itself documents the divergence: on Ouro-1.4B/HellaSwag the 4→5 mean utility change is −0.008006 while option accuracy *rises* from 68.4% to 69.2%. Whether recovering utility on interpolated states translates into better generated answers is untested (acknowledged in Appendix F).
- **Provenance circularity in transition selection**: several analysis transitions were selected on the same data later analysed — Huginn/GSM8K 19→20 was chosen as the earliest negative consecutive change *on those same 256 training questions*, and Huginn/CommonsenseQA 33→34 was picked by a dense sweep on the same evaluation set. A separate Huginn/GSM8K run with different initialisation settings found 107 vs 102 finite-step failures, so failure counts are execution-sensitive at the margins. The paper also concedes depth-sweep intervals carry no multiple-comparison adjustment.
- **Effect-size asymmetry across models**: the mechanism is dramatic on Ouro-1.4B math tasks (49.18% of harmful updates) but thin on Huginn (1.38–22.17%, mean gains ~10⁻⁴ scale, MAE down to 0.17–0.42×10⁻⁴), and the safe-scale certificate L_D is computable only for Ouro's affine readout — the theory's most actionable piece does not transfer to Huginn's nonlinear coda.
- **Practical cost unquantified**: the intervention requires computing both endpoints and readout derivatives (HVP per update) at inference; no wall-clock or accuracy-per-FLOP accounting is given, and the oracle analysis itself shows intermediate scales contribute only 5.65% (best case) beyond what a halting policy already captures — so the practical payoff over existing early-exit methods remains to be demonstrated.
- **No baseline intervention comparisons**: recovery is compared to the quarter step and the first-order rule, but not against published adaptive-depth or damping methods (e.g., input-dependent gating, time-conditioned updates cited in Related Work), so the relative value of geometry-guided stepping is open.

## Strengths

- Clean minimal-assumption formulation: no smoothness required on the recurrent map, only on the frozen readout; all theory is about the model's *own* proposed direction, which is what makes the intervention non-vacuous.
- The exact endpoint KL identity (Prop. 3.4) doubles as an independent numerical check of the pathwise decomposition — a rare instance of theory and measurement cross-validating each other.
- Exceptional numerical hygiene: precision-stratified evaluation, bitwise replay verification, derivative cross-checks, fixed-seed bootstrap CIs, and effect-margin filters (A > 10⁻⁴, −ΔU > 10⁻⁴) separating substantive failures from numerical noise.
- Broad coverage: two model families, three scales, nine primary conditions, a 21-condition/58086-observation depth sweep, plus token-level audits within Ouro's actual four-step inference budget — the mechanism is shown to persist inside net-positive-progress regimes, not just at cherry-picked degradation points.
- Honest self-limitation: the endpoint-oracle comparison openly bounds how much intermediate scales add over simply halting, pre-empting overclaiming.

## Limitations

- **Teacher-forced reference utility only**: all recovery gains are immediate utility changes measured after proposal computation and before further recurrence; effects on generated trajectories, final answer accuracy, and compute cost are left unexplored (the paper's own HellaSwag example shows utility and accuracy can move in opposite directions).
- **Single-update scope**: how finite-step failures accumulate across a recurrent trajectory is not established; an intervention that wins per-step may lose over a full rollout.
- **Readout-dependent certificates**: the curvature safe-range bound L_D exists only for Ouro's affine readout (FP64 derivative bound); Huginn's nonlinear readout gets no such certificate, limiting the intervention's generality.
- **Transition-selection circularity and execution sensitivity** on part of the evaluation sets (19→20 and 33→34 selected on the analysed data; 107 vs 102 failures across two Huginn/GSM8K runs), and unadjusted pointwise intervals in the depth sweep.
- **Small absolute effect sizes on Huginn** and on commonsense tasks (failure shares 1.38–4.52%, oracle interior gains as low as 1.5×10⁻⁷ on Huginn/CommonsenseQA), so practical impact may be concentrated on small looped math models.
- **No comparison to existing adaptive-computation baselines** and no released code at submission time.

## Relevance

与用户对 LLM 隐藏状态与可解释性（representation geometry、hidden-state 分析）以及训练/推理稳定性观测的核心兴趣高度契合：本文正是在循环 Transformer 的隐藏状态空间中做路径级曲率几何分析，把"多算一步变差"归因为方向-步长失配，属于 test-time scaling 可靠性与表征几何交叉方向；其对 BF16/FP32/FP64 分层精度验证的数值协议也与用户对低精度计算可靠性的关注相呼应。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does per-update step contraction on interpolated states improve final *generation accuracy* (not just teacher-forced reference utility) when applied across full recurrent rollouts, and at what compute overhead relative to early exiting?
- Could the exact KL identity ΔU = A − C be turned into a cheap training-time regulariser (penalising curvature C relative to slope A along proposed updates), complementing STARS-style Jacobian regularisation?
- The quadratic rule needs readout Hessian-vector products at inference; can A and Q be predicted from cheaper proxies (e.g., the model's own confidence-gate signals in Ouro) without degrading scale prediction below the 0.99-Spearman regime?
- Why is the finite-step failure share an order of magnitude larger in Ouro-1.4B math tasks (49.18%) than in Huginn (1.38–22.17%) — is it the affine-vs-nonlinear readout, the pretraining recipe, or the recurrent depth regime (4 vs 19 iterations)?
- Do finite-step failures concentrate on identifiable question or token populations (e.g., long solutions, specific reasoning steps), which would enable targeted rather than universal step control?
