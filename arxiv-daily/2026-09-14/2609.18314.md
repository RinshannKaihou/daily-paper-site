> **一句话总结：** 通过在六个模型-任务设置上扫描 Adam 的 (β1, β2) 平面，本文发现分割"有尖峰/无尖峰"动力学的相边界近似满足线性关系 1−β2 = C(1−β1)（拟合指数 p = 0.75–1.06），而一维二次损失却给出近三次方边界（p ≈ 3.07）；作者证明这一差异源于"自信交叉熵"损失在优化器步长尺度上呈现"二次内核 + 超二次陡壁"（core–wall）几何——内核宽度理论为 O(1/σa)——从而把 Adam 损失尖峰同时归因于双动量时间尺度失配与 Hessian 之外的有限尺度损失几何。

> **TL;DR:** The authors map Adam's spiky vs. non-spiky training dynamics across the (β1, β2) plane on six model–task settings (modular division/addition Transformers, Tiny Shakespeare char-LM, CIFAR-10 MLP and VGG11-style CNN, MNIST autoencoder) and find a near-linear stability boundary 1−β2 = C(1−β1) with fitted exponents p = 0.75–1.06, in sharp contrast to the approximately cubic boundary (p ≈ 3.07, γ ≈ 15.6δ³) that a 1D quadratic loss baseline L(x) = x²/2 produces. A 1D superquadratic loss L(x) ∝ |x|ⁿ (n > 2) recovers the linear scaling with right-boundary coefficient C_R = 2(n−2)/n (proven) and empirical left-boundary C_L ≈ 12(n−2)/(3n−4), and measured pre-spike wall exponents n_wall = 6.5–10.9 for the six tasks yield C_L estimates (3.49–3.72) that match the scale of the measured coefficients (2.21–3.84). The mechanism: confident cross-entropy develops a quadratic core of width O(1/σa) followed by a superquadratic wall (measured n_eff rises from ≈2.18 to ≈11.0 on modular division), so finite-scale progressive sharpening shrinks the core until a typical Adam update (6.3×10⁻⁶) hits the wall and triggers the spike.

## 核心贡献

- **发现近似线性的 β 相边界**：在六个模型-任务设置（模除/模加 Transformer、Tiny Shakespeare 字符语言模型、CIFAR-10 MLP 与 CNN、MNIST 自编码器）上系统扫描 (β1, β2) 平面，发现分割宏观损失尖峰区与非尖峰区的边界近似满足线性幂律 1−β2 = C·(1−β1)^p，且六个设置的拟合指数 p = 0.75–1.06 都接近 1；而一维二次损失 L(x)=kx²/2 给出的是近似三次方边界（γ ≃ 15.6δ^3.07），这一"三次方 vs 线性"的差异是全文的驱动力。
- **用超二次一维模型解释边界形态**：对 L(x) = k|x|ⁿ（n > 2），证明（定理 3，安静相位近似下）尖峰楔形区的右边界为 β2 = β1^(2(n−2)/n)，即小 δ 下 1−β2 ≈ (2(n−2)/n)(1−β1)（与 Bai et al. 2026a 在偶次退化多项式上得到的主稳定性边界一致，但本文证明更短且适用于实数 n > 2）；左边界由经验关系 C_L ≃ 12(n−2)/(3n−4) 刻画。
- **提出并解释"内核–陡壁"（core–wall）损失景观**：证明（引理 5）自信交叉熵下 Hessian 整体趋零（平坦内核的来源）；在方向斜率的高斯模型下证明（定理 7）二次内核的宽度为 s_core = O(1/σa)，阈值 n_eff = 2.5 对应 |s| ≈ 0.964/σa；内核之外是指数和结构 e^(−m+as) 主导的超二次陡壁和外部 rollover。
- **连接微观几何与宏观相图**：在预尖峰点沿 Adam 预条件梯度方向测量有效壁指数 n_wall（六个任务为 6.52–10.90），代入 C_L 关系得到的边界系数估计（3.49–3.72）复现了实测拟合系数（2.21–3.84）的量级；并提出"有限尺度渐进锐化"：训练中二次内核收缩到典型更新尺度 |s_upd| 时，Adam 开始探测陡壁从而触发尖峰。

## Key Contributions

- **Empirical phase diagram with a near-linear boundary.** Across six model–task settings — one-layer Transformers on modular division and addition mod 53, a character-level Transformer on Tiny Shakespeare, an MLP (2.6M params) and a VGG11-style CNN (9.75M params) on CIFAR-10, and a convolutional autoencoder on binarized MNIST — scanning memory gaps δ = 1−β1 ∈ [10⁻³, 0.2] and γ = 1−β2 ∈ [5×10⁻⁴, 0.3] shows that the boundary between macroscopically spiky and non-spiky dynamics follows 1−β2 = C(1−β1)^p with p = 0.93, 0.85, 0.75, 0.95, 1.06, 0.95 for the six tasks respectively.
- **A sharp discrepancy that rules out the quadratic picture.** On the 1D quadratic loss L(x) = kx²/2 (Adam, η = 0.1, ϵ = 10⁻³⁰, 250k updates with 125k burn-in), the detected boundary is instead approximately cubic, γ ≃ 15.6δ^3.07 — robust to k ∈ {1,10}, η ∈ {0.01,0.1}, and ϵ ∈ {10⁻⁸,10⁻⁴} (fitted p = 3.009–3.210). The quadratic approximation therefore cannot explain the near-unit exponents seen in real networks.
- **Theory for a 1D superquadratic model.** For L(x) = k|x|ⁿ with n > 2, a quiet-phase reduction (v_{t+1} = β₂v_t, ϵ = 0) plus a power-law rescaling x_t = q^t y_t with q = β₂^{1/[2(n−2)]} yields an autonomous recurrence whose nonzero fixed points lose linear stability exactly at b = β₁β₂^{−n/[2(n−2)]} = 1, i.e., β2 = β1^{2(n−2)/n}. The small-δ expansion gives the right-edge coefficient C_R = 2(n−2)/n; the left edge is captured empirically by C_L ≃ 12(n−2)/(3n−4), which the authors honestly flag as lacking a complete analytical explanation.
- **Core–wall landscape of confident cross-entropy.** Lemma 5 proves Hessian collapse (∥∇²L∥₂ → 0) as softmax probabilities approach one-hot targets (via the generalized Gauss–Newton decomposition, both terms O(εᵢ) in the confidence error). Theorem 7 shows under a Gaussian model of CE-weighted directional margin slopes a ∼ N(μ_a, σ_a²) that the quadratic core has width s_core = O(1/σ_a), with the operational n_eff = 2.5 threshold at |s| ≈ 0.964/σ_a — because the normalized confident loss is the moment-generating function of the slope distribution.
- **From landscape to phase boundary.** Joint piecewise fits of pre-spike directional slices give wall exponents n_wall = 10.90 (modular division), 10.58 (modular addition), 9.41 (Tiny Shakespeare), 10.48 (CIFAR MLP), 6.52 (CIFAR CNN), 10.22 (MNIST autoencoder); plugging these into C_L(n_wall) yields estimated boundary coefficients 3.72, 3.71, 3.67, 3.71, 3.49, 3.70 against measured power-law prefactors 2.21, 2.51, 2.97, 3.25, 3.84, 2.78. The paper also identifies "finite-scale progressive sharpening": the quadratic core contracts during quiet phases until the typical update probes the wall.

## Methodology

The paper proceeds in three coupled stages.

**Stage 1 — Full-model phase diagrams.** Six model–task settings are trained with AdamW (cross-entropy or BCE), fixed seed 42, with only β1, β2 varied across log-spaced grids (δ ∈ [10⁻³, 0.2], γ ∈ [5×10⁻⁴, 0.3]). Architectures span 54k–9.75M parameters. Macroscopic oscillation periods are extracted from loss traces by a deterministic spectral pipeline: log-transform, zero-phase Butterworth low-pass (cutoff 0.05 cycles/step), Welch power spectrum, and retention of spectral peaks with prominence ≥ 0.02·max P(f) and period ≤ trajectory length. The boundary for each fixed γ is the immediate right neighbor of the largest sampled δ with no accepted period, and boundary points are fit with log γ = log C + p log δ. Unresolved points are adaptively extended (up to 30k–200k steps depending on task).

**Stage 2 — 1D toy-model analysis.** Adam on L(x) = k|x|ⁿ is analyzed via the Adaptive Edge-of-Stability condition (Lemma 1): instability when λ_max(D_t H_t) > (1−β1)/[(1+β1)(η/2)], specialized in 1D to η_eff,t > η_crit,t with η_crit = 2(1+β1)/[(1−β1)λ_t]. During quiet intervals the second moment self-decays (v_t ∝ β₂ᵗ), raising η_eff while the superquadratic approach to zero lowers curvature; the competition between the two rates defines spike vs. non-spike regimes. A rescaling argument (Theorem 3) reduces the dynamics to the autonomous recurrence y_{t+1} = ay_t − by_{t−1} − c|y_t|^{n−2}y_t with b = β₁β₂^{−n/[2(n−2)]}; Jury conditions show the nonzero fixed points are stable iff b < 1, giving the right boundary β2 = β1^{2(n−2)/n} ≈ (2(n−2)/n)δ. Controls verify cubic robustness to k, η, ϵ, and examine weak decoupled weight decay (Appendix C.2), which preserves the left boundary but destroys the wedge's right edge.

**Stage 3 — Finite-scale landscape of real networks.** At pre-spike checkpoints (loss minima within temporal valleys, reconstructed by deterministic replay when needed), directional slices φ_t(s) = L(θ_t + s·d̂_t) are evaluated along the Adam-preconditioned gradient direction d̂_t = D_t g_t / ∥D_t g_t∥₂^{1/2}. Each slice is fit with a three-segment piecewise log–log model (core/wall/rollover breakpoints chosen by least squares subject to n_wall > n_core and n_wall > n_roll), jointly refit per beta pair (six reference pairs, e.g., (0.9, 0.999)), and aggregated as an unweighted mean over pairs with 2,000 event-level bootstrap resamples. The Gaussian exponential-sum theory (Theorem 7) then links the fitted n_wall to the empirical C_L relation, closing the loop from loss geometry to the β-plane phase diagram.

## Results & Highlights

- **Near-linear boundary in real networks vs. cubic in the quadratic toy model.** The six model–task settings yield log–log boundary slopes p = 0.93 (modular division, C = 2.21), 0.85 (modular addition, C = 2.51), 0.75 (Tiny Shakespeare, C = 2.97), 0.95 (CIFAR-10 MLP, C = 3.25), 1.06 (CIFAR-10 CNN, C = 3.84), and 0.95 (MNIST autoencoder, C = 2.78), whereas the 1D quadratic baseline L(x) = x²/2 gives γ ≃ 15.6δ^3.07 — i.e., the exponent drops from ≈3.07 (quadratic toy) to ≈0.75–1.06 (all six real tasks), a roughly 3x reduction that the quadratic approximation cannot produce. The cubic scaling of the toy model is robust: k = 10 → p = 3.064 (C = 16.30), η = 0.01 → p = 3.210 (C = 25.00), ϵ = 10⁻⁴ → p = 3.009 (C = 15.02) vs. baseline p = 3.095 (C = 17.70).
- **Superquadratic 1D model recovers the linear wedge.** For L(x) = |x|ⁿ, n ∈ {2.1, 2.2, 2.5, 3, 5, 8}, the spiky phase is a wedge C_L·δ ≥ γ ≥ C_R·δ with proven C_R = 2(n−2)/n and empirical C_L ≈ 12(n−2)/(3n−4); both coefficients vanish at n = 2 where the boundary reverts to cubic (Figure 3h).
- **Core–wall geometry measured at pre-spike points.** For the modular-division slice at step 37,210 with (β1, β2) = (0.9, 0.999): branch-averaged effective exponent n_eff ≈ 2.18 in the core vs. n_eff ≈ 11.0 along the wall; the core–wall transition occurs at O(10⁻⁶), comparable to the typical projected update |s_upd| ≃ 6.3×10⁻⁶, the rollover begins at O(10⁻⁵), and the theory's scale 1/σ_a ≈ 4.35×10⁻⁶ agrees with the measured transition. The mechanism is extreme slope dispersion: margins are ⟨m⟩ = 17.08 ± 1.29 while directional slopes have σ_a ≈ 2.30×10⁵ (mean 1.33×10²), so exponential reweighting e^{−m+as} blows up within |s| = O(1/σ_a).
- **Finite-scale progressive sharpening quantified.** On modular division at (0.9, 0.999): during the quiet phase (step 28,600) the core–wall transition distances are 7.0×10⁻⁴ and 8.0×10⁻⁴ — more than three orders of magnitude above |s_upd| ≃ 5.1×10⁻⁷ — but at the pre-spike point (step 37,210) they contract to 2.16×10⁻⁶ and 3.55×10⁻⁶ while |s_upd| grows to 6.3×10⁻⁶, so a typical update crosses the core and hits the wall; at the spike peak (step 37,220) the slice is strongly asymmetric with reference loss ≈2.0.
- **Landscape exponents predict the boundary-coefficient scale.** Fitted wall exponents n_wall = 10.90/10.58/9.41/10.48/6.52/10.22 for the six tasks give C_L estimates 3.72/3.71/3.67/3.71/3.49/3.70, capturing the scale (and the CIFAR-CNN ordering: the smallest n_wall = 6.52 gives the smallest C_L = 3.49 vs. measured 3.84) of the measured prefactors 2.21/2.51/2.97/3.25/3.84/2.78, though overestimating modular division by ~68% (3.72 vs. 2.21).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The phase-diagram framing (scanning the full (β1, β2) plane rather than one-dimensional hyperparameter sweeps) and the core–wall/1-over-σ_a mechanism are genuinely novel and elegant. However, the strongest theoretical statement (the right-boundary condition β2 = β1^{2(n−2)/n}) is a rederivation, by the authors' own admission, of Bai et al. (2026a); and the relation that actually connects to real networks, C_L ≃ 12(n−2)/(3n−4), is an empirical fit with no derivation — the paper concedes "a complete analytical explanation remains open." So the load-bearing link from landscape to phase diagram rests on an unfitted-formula-free-lunch: a two-parameter functional form fitted to seven toy values of n.
- **Rigor:** The matches between predicted C_L (3.49–3.72) and measured boundary prefactors (2.21–3.84) are scale-level, not quantitative — the worst case (modular division, the flagship example used throughout the paper) is off by ~68% (3.72 predicted vs. 2.21 measured), and the range of measured C values across tasks (2.21–3.84) is compressed into a nearly flat predicted range (3.49–3.72) because C_L(n) saturates for large n. The Theorem 7 core scale additionally assumes Gaussian margin slopes a ∼ N(μ, σ_a²), which is implausible for the paper's own numbers (σ_a = 2.30×10⁵ with mean 1.33×10² suggests heavy tails, not Gaussian); the agreement of 1/σ_a ≈ 4.35×10⁻⁶ with the measured transition may be somewhat fortuitous.
- **Reproducibility:** Strong on the deterministic side — fixed seed 42, full hyperparameter disclosure, explicit spectral-prominence criteria for spike detection, piecewise-fitting constraints, and bootstrap procedure in appendices. But single-seed-per-beta-pair scanning means no error bars on the boundary location itself; boundary placement uses a discrete-grid rule ("immediate right neighbor of the largest δ with no accepted period") that is sensitive to the 0.02 prominence threshold and to the adaptive extension horizon.
- **Significance:** The six tasks are small (54k–9.75M parameters; the CIFAR models train full-batch on only 200 images; Tiny Shakespeare uses 448 windows of 64 characters with 8 loss-bearing positions; MNIST uses 64 images). Although the motivation invokes LLM loss spikes (PaLM, Molybog et al.), no LLM-scale or even moderately sized run validates that the near-linear boundary and n_wall ≈ 6.5–10.9 persist at scale, where batch noise, distributed data order, and mixed precision all intervene.
- **Red flags:** One learning rate (10⁻³, except 2×10⁻³ for MNIST) per setting — yet the toy controls themselves show the prefactor C shifts by ~60% when η changes from 0.1 to 0.01 (15.02–25.00 in the quadratic model), so the C_L-vs-measured-C comparison at a single η is under-controlled. Appendix C.2 quietly shows the toy model's clean two-sided wedge collapses under weak weight decay (the right boundary becomes unresolved at λ_wd = 10⁻³), meaning the wedge — half of the superquadratic theory — is not robust to a force present in every real experiment. All formal results assume full-batch or fixed-batch deterministic dynamics with frozen local preconditioners; stochasticity is never analyzed.

## Strengths

- A crisp, falsifiable empirical question (what is the shape of the spiky/non-spiky boundary in the (β1, β2) plane?) answered with an unusually systematic six-setting scan, and a decisive negative result (the quadratic toy model's cubic boundary) that cleanly rules out the default theoretical frame.
- The mechanistic chain is complete in outline: confident cross-entropy → Hessian collapse (proven) → exponential-sum structure → core width O(1/σ_a) (proven under a Gaussian model) → finite-scale progressive sharpening (measured: core contracts from ~10⁻⁴ to ~2–4×10⁻⁶) → superquadratic wall exponent (measured n_wall ≈ 6.5–10.9) → boundary coefficient (empirically matched in scale).
- Intellectual honesty throughout: the empirical status of C_L is labeled as such, the overlap with Bai et al. (2026a) is credited, and control experiments (k, η, ϵ sensitivity; weight decay; a synthetic confident-CE slice that independently reproduces core–wall–rollover) probe the theory's soft spots rather than hiding them.
- The period-detection and slice-fitting pipelines are specified to a level (filter cutoffs, prominence thresholds, breakpoint constraints, bootstrap design) that makes the numerical conclusions checkable.

## Limitations

- The left-boundary coefficient C_L = 12(n−2)/(3n−4) — the only quantitative bridge from the toy model to the six real networks — is empirical and unexplained; a complete theory of the left boundary is explicitly left open.
- Validation is confined to small full-batch or small-batch models; no test at LLM scale despite the LLM-spike motivation, and no minibatch-noise or mixed-precision setting is studied.
- Single random seed per (β1, β2) cell and a single learning rate per task; the boundary prefactor's known sensitivity to η (from the paper's own controls) is not propagated into the C_L comparison.
- The Gaussian slope-distribution assumption of Theorem 7 is inconsistent with the measured slope statistics (σ_a ≈ 2.30×10⁵ vs. mean ≈ 1.33×10²), and the theory-to-measurement agreement for boundary coefficients is scale-level only (worst gap ~68% on modular division).
- The two-sided wedge of the superquadratic theory is not robust to weak weight decay (Appendix C.2), and the right-boundary mechanism is inapplicable to real networks (which have a quadratic core), leaving the proven half of the theory without a direct empirical counterpart.

## Relevance

与"LLM 训练稳定性与可观测性"高度相关：本文给出了 Adam 损失尖峰的 (β1, β2) 相图与近线性稳定边界 1−β2 = C(1−β1)，可直接转化为大模型训练中 β2 的调参/避坑准则（如固定 β1 时 β2 过大进入尖峰区），并为尖峰检测与根因分类（优化器动力学失配 vs 有限尺度损失几何）提供了机制性依据。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the near-linear boundary and n_wall ≈ 6.5–10.9 persist at LLM scale with minibatch noise, data ordering, and mixed precision, where the PaLM-style spikes that motivate this work actually occur?
- Can the empirical left-boundary coefficient C_L ≃ 12(n−2)/(3n−4) be derived from first principles, perhaps via the oscillatory finite-step dynamics the paper leaves open — and does it survive stochastic (non-full-batch) gradients?
- Since the toy-model controls show the boundary prefactor C shifts substantially with η (15.02 → 25.00 when η drops 10x), what is the joint (η, β1, β2) stability surface, and does the n_wall → C_L mapping remain calibrated across learning rates and warmup schedules?
- Would replacing the Gaussian slope model in Theorem 7 with the empirically heavy-tailed slope distribution (σ_a/μ_a ≈ 1700) change the O(1/σ_a) core scale or the 0.964/σ_a threshold constant?
- Can an optimizer exploit this theory in practice — e.g., online-estimate the effective exponent n_eff at the update scale and adapt β2 (or η) to stay below the evolving phase boundary, as the authors propose in their outlook?
