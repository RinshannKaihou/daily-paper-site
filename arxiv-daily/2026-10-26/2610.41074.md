> **一句话总结：** 在固定随机特征的两层线性网络 + 在线 SGD 这一理论模型上，为"随机舍入（SR）训练"证明了匹配上下界的神经 scaling law：前向舍入不改变精确算术的计算最优风险指数 F^{-(b-1)/(a+1)}，而后向舍入会把它退化到 F^{-(b-1)/(a+b)}、双向舍入退化到 F^{-(b-1)/(2a)}；平均参数（而非最后迭代）会使"哪里需要高精度"发生反转（最后迭代要高前向精度、均匀平均要高后向精度），后向尾数位数需随宽度按 (b-1)/2·log₂M 增长才能维持相对精度；在固定算力预算下，低精度省下的算力换成更大模型/更久训练可净赚——合成实验中联合 1-bit SR 比精确训练平均预测风险低 9.7%，CIFAR-10 MLP 上 1-bit 前向训练在测试关闭 SR 时 loss 为 1.624（开 SR 为 1.672）。

> **TL;DR:** This paper proves matched upper/lower-bound scaling laws for mini-batch online SGD with stochastic rounding (SR) of forward and backward features in a random-features regression model (Gaussian data with power-law spectra, exponents 1 < b < a), covering both the last iterate and uniform parameter averaging under general LR/precision schedules. Key quantitative findings: rounding only forward features preserves the exact-arithmetic compute-optimal risk exponent F^{-(b-1)/(a+1)}, while backward-only rounding degrades it to F^{-(b-1)/(a+b)} and joint rounding to F^{-(b-1)/(2a)} (Table 1); averaging flips which pass needs more precision (last iterate wants high forward precision, e.g. best 5-bit split (p_fw, p_bw) = (4,1); average wants high backward precision, best split (2,3)); backward mantissa bits must grow like (b-1)/2·log₂M to keep SR-induced risk within a fixed fraction of exact SGD. At a fixed compute budget on the synthetic model (a=2, b=3/2, σ²=0.1, B=32), joint 1-bit SR beats the exact-arithmetic baseline by 9.7% mean prediction risk by affording a wider model + longer training; a 6-layer 30M-param transformer on 200M FineWeb-Edu tokens and a CIFAR-10 MLP probe rounding-location choices beyond the theory (combined 2-bit rounding hurts more than activation-only; test-time SR adds +0.048 test loss: 1.672 vs 1.624).

## 核心贡献

- **证明了 SR 训练的匹配上下界 scaling law（Theorem 1）**：在两层线性网络（固定随机第一层 S，宽度 M）+ fresh Gaussian 数据（幂律谱：特征值 λ_j ~ j^{-a}，目标能量 λ_jϑ_j ~ j^{-b}，1 < b < a）上，对一般的、训练前确定的学习率与精度日程，证明了最后迭代与均匀参数平均两种输出的预测风险满足 R^{out}(T) ≍ M^{1-b} + B̂ + V̂ + N̂，其中 SR 引起的额外风险 Δ^{out}(T) ≍ N̂^{out} 被前向 q_fw、后向 q_bw 及其交互项 q_fw·q_bw 显式刻画（高概率 1 − 6e^{-cM} − K_D M^{-D} 成立）。
- **给出了含精度的计算最优 scaling law（Table 1 / Prop. D.5）**：固定精度 + 常数学习率 + 参数平均时，精确训练的最优风险为 F^{-(b-1)/(a+1)}（M ≍ τ ≍ F^{1/(a+1)}）；仅前向舍入保持该指数；仅后向舍入退化为 F^{-(b-1)/(a+b)}（τ ≍ M^{a/b}）；双向舍入退化为 F^{-(b-1)/(2a)}（M ≍ τ ≍ F^{1/2}）——即舍入"位置"同时改变风险指数和最优的宽度/时长分配。
- **"Where/When/How many bits"三问的系统回答（Section 4）**：Where——前向舍入对最后迭代伤害更大、后向舍入对均匀平均伤害更大（比值 ∆^{last}(q,0)/∆^{last}(0,q) ≍ M^{a-b}/σ² 随 M 增长，而 ∆^{avg}(q,0)/∆^{avg}(0,q) ≍ M^{1-b}/σ² 随 M 下降）；When——最优后向精度日程对最后迭代是"单调不减"、对均匀平均是"单调不增"（Prop. E.3 的水位式解 p_{r,u} = max{0, log₄(A_{r,u}/λ)}）；How many bits——(29) 式给出保证 ∆ ≤ ϑ·R₀ 的位数充分条件，且后向位数必须随宽度对数增长（≥ (b-1)/2·log₂M − O(1)，pfw+pbw ≥ 2·... 量级 log₂M），此时风险可达 (F/log F)^{-(b-1)/(a+1)}，仅比精确 SGD 多一个 polylog 因子。
- **低精度下的学习率日程设计（Section 4.4）**：推导了考虑后向舍入的最优连续学习率 η^{SR}(t) = min{η_max, √λ_bw[1+2(τ−τ_t)]^{1−1/(2a)} / √(1 + 4^{-p_bw}[1+2(τ−τ_t)]/M)}；当 SR 修正项主导时，时间域衰减指数从精确情形的 2a−1 变为 (a−1)/(a+1)——即精度与学习率调度器必须联合调。
- **固定预算下"低精度何时净赚"的判据（Eq. 33）**：低精度配置 L 优于高精度 H 当且仅当 ∆_L − ∆_H < R₀^{avg}(T_H) − R₀^{avg}(T_L)；并用合成实验（联合 1-bit SR 降低 9.7% 平均风险）和带数据重用的实验（联合 2-bit SR 在所有预算下约低 5%）验证，另用 MLP/transformer 实验检验理论外的舍入选择（共享舍入、测试期舍入）。

## Key Contributions

- **Matched upper and lower bounds (Theorem 1)**: For a two-layer linear network with a fixed random first layer trained by mini-batch online SGD on fresh Gaussian data with power-law spectra (λ_j ≍ j^{-a}, λ_jϑ_j ≍ j^{-b}, 1 < b < a), the paper proves R^{out}(T) ≍ M^{1-b} + bias + variance + SR-induced terms, holding with probability ≥ 1 − 6e^{-cM} − K_D M^{-D}, for *both* the last iterate and the uniform parameter average, under arbitrary deterministic learning-rate and precision schedules (forward and backward features rounded independently, possibly at different precisions per step).
- **Precision-dependent compute-optimal scaling laws (Table 1)**: With constant LR, fixed precisions, and parameter averaging: exact arithmetic gives risk F^{-(b-1)/(a+1)} at M ≍ τ ≍ F^{1/(a+1)}; forward-only finite precision *preserves* this exponent; backward-only finite precision degrades it to F^{-(b-1)/(a+b)} with a different allocation τ ≍ M^{a/b}; joint finite precision degrades it to F^{-(b-1)/(2a)} with M ≍ τ ≍ F^{1/2}. Rounding location changes both the exponent and the optimal width/time allocation.
- **A sharp answer to "where, when, how many bits" (Section 4)**: Forward rounding hurts the last iterate more (∆^{last}(q,0)/∆^{last}(0,q) ≍ M^{a-b}/(σ²+M^{1-b}) → grows), backward rounding hurts the average more (∆^{avg}(q,0)/∆^{avg}(0,q) ≍ M^{1-b}/(σ²+M^{1-b}) → decays); the optimal backward-precision schedule is nondecreasing for the last iterate but nonincreasing for the average (water-filling solution p_{r,u} = max{0, log₄(A_{r,u}/λ)}); explicit bit requirements (29) guarantee SR-induced risk ≤ ϑ fraction of exact risk, with backward mantissa bits necessarily growing as (b−1)/2·log₂M − O(1) in width, recovering (F/log F)^{-(b-1)/(a+1)} — only a polylog factor worse than exact SGD.
- **Precision-aware learning-rate design (Section 4.4, Theorem E.7)**: A variational derivation yields the SR-aware schedule η^{SR}(t) = min{η_max, √λ_bw[1+2(τ−τ_t)]^{1−1/(2a)}/√(1+4^{-p_bw}[1+2(τ−τ_t)]/M)}; when the rounding correction dominates, the time-domain decay exponent changes from 2a−1 (exact) to (a−1)/(a+1), so precision and LR schedulers must be co-designed.
- **When low precision wins at fixed compute (Section 4.5, Eq. 33)**: Lower precision beats higher precision iff ∆_L − ∆_H < R₀^{avg}(T_H) − R₀^{avg}(T_L); validated in simulation (joint 1-bit SR: −9.7% mean risk; with data reuse, joint 2-bit SR: ≈ −5% at every tested budget), and probed beyond the theory with a 6-layer 30M-parameter transformer on 200M FineWeb-Edu tokens and a CIFAR-10 MLP (shared vs. independent rounding, test-time SR).

## Methodology

The theoretical model is online SGD for Gaussian squared-loss regression: inputs x ~ N(0, Σ_d) with power-law spectrum, teacher θ* ~ N(0, C_θ), labels y = x^Tθ* + ε (noise σ²). A random feature matrix S ∈ R^{M×d} (i.i.d. N(0, 1/M)) is drawn once and frozen; only the output weights v are trained, giving predictor v^T Sx. At each SGD step, the feature vector is rounded *twice independently* via stochastic rounding on a binary grid F_p (mantissa p bits, unbounded exponent): the forward copy a_u = Q_{p_fw,u}(Sx) computes the prediction, the backward copy b_u = Q_{p_bw,u}(Sx) multiplies the residual in the gradient; all other arithmetic is exact. SR is unbiased (E[Q_p(x)|x] = x) with variance ≤ (1/4)4^{-p}x². Both the last iterate and the uniform average of iterates are studied, and evaluation is always in exact arithmetic, so the SR-induced risk increase ∆^{out}(T) = R^{out}(T) − R₀^{out}(T) cleanly isolates rounding effects.

The proofs work through second-moment recursions along the training trajectory (Appendix D), with kernel-like propagators K₁^{out}, K₂^{out} that track how a rounding error injected at step u propagates to the final predictor under each output rule (for the average, every pair of later iterates contributes correlated terms). Theorem 1 gives matched (≍) upper and lower bounds under stability condition (16) on the learning rates and Assumption C.1 on schedules. Specializing to constant LR/precision yields closed-form risk expressions (Eqs. 18–21) with cleanly separated forward, backward, and joint (q_fw·q_bw interaction) rounding terms; a compute model charges BM·c(p) per feature computation with c(p) = (p+p₀)/(p_ref+p₀), and variational optimization over (M, T, η(t), p(t)) produces Table 1 and the schedule formulas. Experiments: (i) direct simulations of the exact theoretical dynamics (a = 2, b = 3/2, σ² = 0.1, B = 32, d = 4M, widths 16–4096); (ii) data-reuse variants at M = 256 reusing 2,048 (8M) and 16,384 (64M) examples; (iii) a six-layer 30,041,856-parameter transformer trained on ~200M FineWeb-Edu tokens for 12,207 updates (batches 64×256 tokens, AdamW peak LR 6×10⁻⁴, 3 seeds) and a CIFAR-10 MLP (3072–1024–10 ReLU, FP32, 25 epochs / 9,750 updates, SGD momentum 0.9, 16 seeds) to test rounding choices outside the model's assumptions.

## Results & Highlights

- **Rounding location changes the compute-optimal exponent (Table 1, theory)**: for the uniformly averaged predictor with fixed precisions, exact SGD and forward-only rounding both achieve risk F^{-(b-1)/(a+1)}, backward-only rounding degrades this to F^{-(b-1)/(a+b)}, and joint forward+backward rounding to F^{-(b-1)/(2a)}. For the experimental parameter choice (a = 2, b = 3/2) these correspond to F^{-1/6} vs F^{-1/7} vs F^{-1/8}: joint 1-bit SR has a *fundamentally slower* asymptotic compute scaling, yet can still win at practical finite budgets (see next bullet).
- **At a fixed compute budget, joint 1-bit SR beats exact-arithmetic training by 9.7% mean prediction risk** (Figure 3(c): synthetic random-features model, a=2, b=3/2, σ²=0.1, B=32, widths 512–4096, averaged over 3 sketches) — the saved compute buys a wider model and longer training (τ up to ~8M²) whose approximation/optimization gains exceed the SR-induced error, exactly the regime characterized by Eq. (33).
- **The win persists with data reuse (Figure 4)**: at width M = 256 and compute cap F = 2²⁷, joint 2-bit SR achieves ≈5% lower prediction risk than exact arithmetic for fresh data and for both reused training sets (2,048 and 16,384 examples), and Figure 4(b) shows *negative* (SR − exact) risk differences at every tested compute budget from 2²⁴ to 2²⁷.
- **Bit requirements are sharp and grow with width (Figure 1(c), Prop. E.8)**: keeping SR-induced risk within 1% of exact-arithmetic risk for the last iterate requires a minimum of 2 mantissa bits at width M = 128 but 3 bits at M = 256; theoretically, backward mantissa bits must grow at least as (b−1)/2·log₂M − O(1), and growing total mantissa bits like log M restores risk (F/log F)^{-(b-1)/(a+1)} — only a polylog factor worse than exact SGD's F^{-(b-1)/(a+1)}.
- **Averaging reverses which pass needs precision (Figures 1(a), 2(a))**: at τ ≍ M^a, forward-only SR error exceeds backward-only error by a factor ≍ M^{a-b}/σ² for the last iterate but is *smaller* by ≍ M^{1-b}/σ² for the average; with a 5-bit total mantissa budget, the best tested split is (p_fw, p_bw) = (4, 1) for the last iterate but (2, 3) for the uniform average.
- **Precision changes optimal LR decay (Figure 2(b,c))**: with 1-bit backward SR, the SR-aware schedule (31) differs visibly from the exact-SGD schedule (30); in the correction-dominated regime the time-domain decay exponent drops from 2a−1 to (a−1)/(a+1). Empirically the SR-aware design has *slightly higher* last-iterate risk than the exact-SGD design at the same 1-bit precision (Figure 2(c), differences of order 10⁻⁵–10⁻⁴ in risk, shrinking at higher precision) — the correction matters most at very low precision.
- **Beyond the theory (Figure 5)**: on the 6-layer transformer (200M FineWeb-Edu tokens), combined 2-bit rounding (activations in both passes + incoming gradients + forward weights, 7-bit parameter storage) yields *higher* validation loss than activation-only 2-bit rounding; sharing the forward-rounded activation between forward and weight-gradient computations lowers validation loss relative to independent rounding at 1 bit for both models; and the CIFAR-10 MLP trained with 1-bit forward SR has test loss 1.624 with SR disabled at test time vs 1.672 with SR enabled (Δ = 0.048), so low-precision *training* does not force low-precision *inference*.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Genuinely novel in scope. Prior SR theory gives error bounds for numerical computations or convergence guarantees for low-precision SGD, but not *matched upper/lower bounds* on prediction risk as a joint function of width, time, precision, rounding *location* (forward vs. backward vs. joint), and output rule (last iterate vs. averaged). The cleanest new insights — that forward-only rounding preserves the exact compute exponent while backward rounding changes it, and that parameter averaging *reverses* which pass needs precision — are the kind of crisp, falsifiable predictions rare in the low-precision literature. The concurrent Kim et al. (2026) work partially overlaps (separate forward/backward precision fits with a convex upper bound), which the authors acknowledge.
- **Rigor**: High for the theory. Matching ≍ bounds with explicit high-probability guarantees, an honest Assumption C.1 (earlier rounding errors must not amplify later ones — instantiated as ρq_fw·q_bw·τ/M ≤ κ_c in the constant-LR case), and side conditions (16) stated up front. However, the theory covers a *linear* random-features (lazy/kernel) regime: frozen first layer, Gaussian data, squared loss, no feature learning, and rounding only of the two feature copies — everything else (parameter arithmetic, optimizer state, master weights) is exact. This is far from FP4/FP8 LLM pretraining, where weights, gradients, optimizer states, and attention arithmetic are all quantized.
- **Reproducibility**: Good. Simulation parameters are fully specified (a=2, b=3/2, σ²=0.1, B=32, d=4M, η=0.5/g₁(S), τ up to 8M²), and the neural experiments are unusually well documented for a theory paper (transformer: 30,041,856 params, 200M FineWeb-Edu tokens, 12,207 updates, batches 64×256, AdamW 6×10⁻⁴, 3 seeds, BF16/FP32; MLP: 3072–1024–10, 25 epochs, momentum 0.9, 16 seeds). No code release is mentioned in the main text, and all results are reported as small figure panels (risk differences of order 10⁻⁵–10⁻⁴ in Figure 2(c)) without variance bars everywhere, so some fine comparisons would need re-running to verify.
- **Significance**: The where/when/how-many-bits prescriptions are immediately usable as design heuristics for mixed-precision training (e.g., "keep backward features at higher precision than forward if you average parameters"; "grow precision like log M as you scale"; "compare precisions only at matched compute with SR disabled at test time"). The compute-model caveat is real, though: the cost model c(p) = (p+p₀)/(p_ref+p₀) is a smooth abstraction, whereas real hardware has step functions (FP32/FP16/FP8/FP4) and SR often costs *extra* overhead on non-native hardware — the paper's own conclusion admits "measuring savings on hardware" is future work.
- **Red flags / honest gaps**: (i) The Figure 3(b) simulation shows *better*-than-theoretical scaling (F^{-1/6}-like rather than the predicted F^{-1/8}) because the sims deliberately violate Theorem 1's assumptions (training beyond τ_T = M^a) — the paper flags this, but it means the headline Table-1 exponent for joint rounding is not what the experiment actually exhibits, so the practical relevance of the joint-rounding exponent is uncertain. (ii) The compute-optimal claims hold "as F → ∞" while the finite-budget comparisons rely on unspecified risk *coefficients*, which the paper concedes ("the exponents alone in Table 1 do not determine which configuration has lower risk at a fixed budget"). (iii) The transformer experiment uses a 30M model at 200M tokens with 2-bit added rounding on top of BF16 — an extremely small-scale probe that cannot confirm the width-dependent bit-growth law (log₂M would predict sub-bit changes at this scale). (iv) Data reuse, shared (non-independent) rounding — the standard practice in real quantized training, and one the MLP/transformer experiments show *matters* (sharing beats independent rounding at 1 bit) — is outside the theory. (v) The 9.7%/5% gains are on the authors' own synthetic model where the compute model exactly matches their cost function; no wall-clock or energy measurement is provided.

## Strengths

- Matched upper *and* lower bounds (not just upper bounds) with explicit probability guarantees — unusually strong for the low-precision-training literature.
- The decomposition into forward / backward / joint (q_fw·q_bw) rounding terms makes every downstream conclusion (location, timing, bit counts) auditable and interpretable.
- Covers general learning-rate and precision schedules *chosen before training* in one theorem, then cleanly specializes — the structure (Theorem 1 → Corollaries → Table 1 → Section 4 prescriptions) is exemplary.
- The "where/when/how many bits" framing converts abstract bounds into actionable engineering rules, including the counterintuitive ones (averaging flips the precision allocation; lower precision can beat exact arithmetic at fixed compute; test-time SR should be disabled when comparing).
- Honest boundary-drawing: the neural experiments are explicitly labeled as "beyond the theoretical model," deviations from theorem assumptions are flagged, and AI assistance is disclosed.

## Limitations

- **Model class**: A fixed random first layer with a linear output head (lazy regime) — no feature learning, no nonlinear networks, no Adam/optimizer-state quantization, no weight rounding. The theory rounds only the two feature copies per step; real QAT/FP8 training rounds weights, gradients, and master copies too.
- **Data assumptions**: Fresh i.i.d. Gaussian samples with power-law spectra; the data-reuse experiment is empirical only (a data-reuse scaling law with SR is left as future work).
- **Compute model**: A stylized linear-in-bits cost c(p); no hardware validation, no SR overhead cost, and real precisions are discrete (no 2.3-bit formats).
- **Finite-budget guidance**: Compute-optimality is asymptotic in F; the paper itself notes exponents alone cannot rank configurations at a fixed budget, and the finite-budget conditions (Appendix E) are sufficient conditions with unspecified constants.
- **Scale of validation**: The transformer (30M params, 200M tokens) and MLP experiments are sanity probes, not scaling tests — the width-logarithmic bit-growth prediction is not directly tested at scale.

## Relevance

对"低精度训练 + scaling law"方向高度相关：本文首次在理论上下界匹配的层面回答了前向/后向舍入位置、训练阶段精度分配、以及位数随模型宽度对数增长（log₂M）这三个混合精度训练的核心设计问题，与 FP8/FP4 LLM 预训练及 quantization-aware training 的经验精度调度研究（Kumar et al. 2025、Sun et al. 2025）构成直接互补。若关注计算最优训练、随机舍入与参数平均（EMA/checkpoint averaging）的交互，这篇论文的 Table 1 与 Section 4 的可操作结论值得精读。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How do the conclusions change when the forward and backward rounded copies are *shared* (as in real quantized pipelines) instead of independent? The MLP/transformer experiments show sharing helps at 1 bit — can the q_fw·q_bw interaction term be generalized to correlated rounding?
- Does the averaging-induced precision reversal (high backward precision for averaged outputs, high forward for last iterate) persist under EMA with decay, AdamW, or in transformer pretraining, where "last iterate" is the norm?
- Can the log₂M backward-bit growth law be verified at LLM scale (e.g., does FP8 training degrade relative to BF16 in a width-dependent way that matches (b−1)/2·log₂M)?
- How does the theory extend to data reuse (multi-epoch) — the paper's own Figure 4(c) suggests more reuse lowers risk, but no SR-aware reuse scaling law exists yet?
- What happens in the feature-learning regime (trained first layer), where the kernel propagators K₁, K₂ would no longer be fixed before precision allocation?
- Can the SR-aware learning-rate schedule (Eq. 31) beat the exact-SGD schedule in practice at FP4-level precision, given that in the paper's own experiment (Figure 2(c)) it was slightly *worse* at 1 bit?
