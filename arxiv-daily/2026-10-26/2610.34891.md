> **一句话总结：** 通过对 World Machine 的 State Discovery 做双检查点（θ_best 重载 vs 写表的 θ_last）配对照实验发现：Toy1D 上存储状态表与全零冷启动 rollout 之间的 Wasserstein-1 差距有 90–91% 来自早停后重载 best-validation 检查点这一工程细节，而非训练-推理状态本质不一致；TCN 蒸馏与冻结 SD 迭代细化均未达到预设的 50% 改进标准。

> **TL;DR:** A controlled two-checkpoint reproduction of World Machine's State Discovery procedure on the Toy1D benchmark shows the train/inference state gap is mostly a checkpoint artifact: the pooled mean per-dimension W1 between the stored state table and the cold-start rollout is 0.034/0.038 (seeds 42/7) under the reloaded best-validation checkpoint, but drops ~90–91% to 0.003 when the rollout instead uses the last-epoch weights that actually wrote the table (per-item RMS 0.36→0.14 and 0.43→0.16). Neither causal-TCN distillation (best mean-W1 "cut" is negative, e.g. −163%) nor frozen SD refinement (student still 2.5–4.7× the rollout residual) meets the pre-registered 50% success criteria under either checkpoint — a crisp, honest negative-result workshop paper about how early-stopping reload can silently break state-consistency evaluations.

## 核心贡献

- **首次量化 World Machine「State Discovery (SD)」的训练-推理状态不一致**：SD 在训练期每个 epoch 并行地把核心网络输出写回逐序列状态表，而推理时却从全零状态做串行 rollout；原论文（do Nascimento et al., 2026）注意到了这一差异但从未测量。本文用 Wasserstein-1 距离（逐位置与池化）+ 线性探针把它变成了可测量的对象。
- **识别并验证了差距的主因——检查点重载**：写状态表用的是最后一个 epoch 的权重 θ_last，而早停机制在训练结束后重载 best-validation 检查点 θ_best 做推理。作者在重载前抢救性保存 θ_last 并做配对 rollout：匹配检查点后，两个种子的池化平均 W1 差距分别消除 91% 和 90%。
- **两个预设（pre-registered）成功标准的失败结果**：(1) 把存储状态蒸馏进因果 TCN 编码器（RF 15–127 扫描）；(2) 冻结参数做 K 步 SD 并行细化。两者在任一检查点下都达不到「mean 与 transient W1 双双下降 ≥50%、探针 R² 变化 ≤0.01」的预设门槛——说明残余差距不是普通的摊销（amortization）缺口。
- **概念澄清（Observation 1）**：SD 的 "E-step" 并非 EM——它不评估任何似然 pθ(o,w)、不比较候选、没有 arg max，因此 EM 的单调改进保证不成立；它在形式上更接近直接潜码优化（Bojanowski et al., 2018; Park et al., 2019）。
- **与循环经验回放（Kapturowski et al., 2019）建立类比**：参数滞后使存储的循环状态「过期」，burn-in 用新权重重算——但本文场景的独特之处在于状态表在验证阶段写入、错配在早停重载不同检查点后才出现。

## Key Contributions

- **First quantification of the train–inference state gap in World Machine's State Discovery (SD).** SD writes the core's output back to a per-sequence state table in parallel each epoch, but inference rolls out sequentially from an all-zero start; the original paper noted both facts but never measured the resulting inconsistency. This paper measures it with pooled and position-resolved Wasserstein-1 (W1) plus a held-out linear probe.
- **Identifies checkpoint reload as the dominant cause.** The stored table is written by last-epoch weights θ_last, but early stopping then reloads the best-validation checkpoint θ_best for inference. By saving θ_last immediately before reload and running paired cold-start rollouts under both checkpoints, matching the rollout to the table-writing weights removes 91% (seed 42) and 90% (seed 7) of the pooled mean W1 gap.
- **Two pre-registered negative results on closing the residual.** (1) Distilling stored states into a causal TCN encoder (receptive-field sweep 15–127) and (2) applying K frozen parallel SD updates (iterative refinement from random / student / cold-start / stored initializations) both fail the pre-specified criterion (≥50% cuts in both mean and transient W1, probe R² within 0.01) under either checkpoint — arguing the residual is not an ordinary amortization gap.
- **Conceptual clarification (Observation 1).** SD's "E-step" is not EM: it evaluates no pθ(o,w), scores no candidates, and performs no arg max, so EM's monotone-improvement guarantee does not transfer; it is per-sample latent-state iteration akin to direct latent-code optimization.
- **Connects the phenomenon to recurrent experience replay** (R2Funky / Kapturowski et al., 2019), where parameter lag makes stored recurrent states stale — while noting the present setting differs because the table is written during validation and the mismatch appears only after early stopping reloads a different checkpoint.

## Methodology

The study is a controlled reproduction and ablation on **Toy1D**, the upstream synthetic benchmark of World Machine: 10,000 sequences of 1,000 steps generated from a damped linear system with a 3-dimensional latent state, observed through a random 2-channel linear readout with noise std 0.1; the training objective decodes latent dimension 0. The model uses 128-dimensional tanh-bounded states, 200-position contexts, 4 attention heads, ALiBi positional encoding, and the REPLACE state-saving convention; trained with AdamW (weight decay 5e-5), 1,000 epochs at batch size 256, base lr 1e-3 with cosine warm restarts every 25 steps. Data are split 60/20/20; 2,000 validation sequences × four 200-step windows yield 8,000 validation items, with a fixed 1,600-item analysis subset for the main comparisons.

The core manipulation is the **two-checkpoint design**. Three state populations are compared: (1) the stored validation table (written under θ_last during the final validation pass), (2) the standard reloaded rollout under θ_best, and (3) a matched rollout under θ_last saved just before reload. Distances are measured by pooled and position-resolved 1-Wasserstein distance between state populations (lower is better), with transient W1 defined as the max over positions 1–15, plus per-item MSE/RMS on the paired subset and an OLS linear probe (state → decoded coordinate) as a permissive safeguard. A key validity check: with a causal core and position 0 pinned to zero, table and rollout must agree at position 1 when sharing a checkpoint — they agree to numerical precision under θ_last (W1 ≈ 6e-6) but not under θ_best (≈ 0.10–0.11).

Two repair procedures are then tested against pre-registered thresholds (≥50% cut in both mean and transient W1; probe R² within 0.01 of stored-state baselines 0.9076/0.8762): **(a) causal distillation** — a TCN encoder mapping causal observation windows to stored SD states, swept over receptive fields 15/31/63/127; **(b) frozen SD refinement** — K ∈ {0,1,3,5,10} parallel updates under frozen Φθ from four initializations (random, distilled student, cold start, stored table). The pre-registration timeline (Aug 20–Sep 2 experiment log entries specifying bands, thresholds, and the paired-checkpoint decision rule before output inspection) is documented in Appendix A.2.

## Results & Highlights

- **Checkpoint matching removes ~90–91% of the pooled gap (Toy1D, 8,000 validation items).** Pooled mean W1 between stored table and cold-start rollout: 0.03438 (seed 42) / 0.03759 (seed 7) under the reloaded θ_best, vs 0.00313 / 0.00361 under the table-writing θ_last — matching the checkpoint removes 91% and 90% respectively.
- **Item-level agreement also collapses when checkpoints match.** Per-item RMS error vs the table falls from 0.36 → 0.14 (seed 42) and 0.43 → 0.16 (seed 7); per-item MSE falls from 0.12779 → 0.01889 and 0.18355 → 0.02635 (a 62% RMS reduction for both seeds on the 1,600-item analysis split).
- **The gap is concentrated at early positions and is checkpoint-dependent.** Under θ_best the position-resolved W1 hits 0.10343 / 0.11289 at position 1 and decays to means of 0.039 / 0.045 over positions 30–199; under θ_last position 1 is ~0.0000059 / 0.0000051 (numerical precision) with a late peak of only 0.00801 / 0.00706 and position-30–199 means of 0.005 / 0.006.
- **TCN distillation fails its criterion everywhere.** In the exploratory receptive-field sweep (mixed-checkpoint), mean W1 is worse than the rollout baseline at every RF — best is 0.09324 at RF 63, still ≥2.9× the rollout gap of 0.0317 (cuts −194.1% at best; −341.5% at RF 15). In the paired RF-63 runs the mean-W1 cut is negative under both checkpoints: −163.0% / −228.6% vs the θ_best rollout and −2829.6% / −3704.4% vs θ_last; the student sits 29–38× farther from the table than the matched rollout in marginal W1 and 2.5–2.6× farther item-by-item (RMS 0.35/0.42 vs 0.14/0.16).
- **Frozen SD updates fail too.** At the pre-specified K=5 from the student, mean W1 is 0.02975 / 0.03868 under θ_best (cuts only +13.5% / −2.7%, far from the 50% bar) and 0.00761 / 0.01542 under θ_last — still 2.5–4.7× the matched-rollout residual (0.00309 / 0.00325). The only row clearing every threshold is the K=1 stored-table initialization under θ_last (mean cuts 76%/75%, transient cuts 57%/54%, probe change <0.0001), but it starts from the very table it is scored against and hence is not an inference procedure.
- **The shared-checkpoint residual is real but unexplained.** It exceeds the numerical floor (≈1.5e-7) by more than fifty-thousandfold, yet no tested table-free method improves both W1 measures at once, and the mechanism remains open.
- **Reproduction quality is honestly reported as imperfect.** Against the published decoded-state MSE of 0.34348 with a pre-registered ±20% band, the four completed runs land 2.0–35.7% above it (0.46419, 0.46621, 0.35051, 0.43723); the two paired-analysis runs miss by 35.7% and 27.3%.

This is a NeurIPS 2026 workshop paper (Foundation Models for Temporal Systems), 11 pages with three substantive appendices. Credibility is unusually high for a reproduction study: the authors pre-registered the ±20% reproduction band, the 50% W1-cut thresholds with 0.01 probe slack, the K=5-student-as-primary choice, and the paired-checkpoint analysis order and decision rule before inspecting outputs (documented with dates in Appendix A.2); they report null results against their own pre-specified criteria; they disclose that both paired runs fall outside their own reproduction band and that the RF sweep is mixed-checkpoint; and they verify the matched-checkpoint identity (position-1 W1 ≈ 6e-6; cold-start fixed-point identity to six decimals) before examining comparisons. ## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Modest but genuine. The train/store vs inference/reconstruct consistency question is real and under-examined, and the specific finding — that early-stopping reload creates a checkpoint mismatch between the weights that write a state table and the weights used at inference — is a sharp, transferable methodological point. However, the core phenomenon is a close cousin of known parameter-lag effects in recurrent experience replay (Kapturowski et al., 2019), which the authors themselves acknowledge; the novelty is in isolating it in the World Machine context and quantifying it so cleanly.
- **Rigor:** High on internal validity (pre-registration, paired checkpoints, numerical verification, multiple initializations, three random replicates agreeing within 0.0004) but limited in scope: the paired analysis covers **two seeds on one synthetic benchmark (Toy1D)**, and — critically — both paired runs fail the paper's own ±20% reproduction band (+35.7% and +27.3% decoded-state MSE vs the published 0.34348), so whether checkpoint reload still dominates the gap at the published error level is unknown. The seed-7 repetitions used different PyTorch/CUDA builds, so stochastic variation cannot be separated from software-version effects.
- **Weak evidence spots:** The receptive-field sweep is **mixed-checkpoint** (table written under θ_last, rollout baseline under θ_best, no saved θ_last), which the authors admit eases the success threshold the distillation students still fail. The linear probe has limited discriminating power because its target is nearly the training objective's own decoded coordinate, and the item-level split lets sibling windows straddle train/test, making probe R² optimistic. The transient-W1 column cannot distinguish θ_best refinement rows from the rollout baseline from K≥3 (every update rewrites position 1, where the max sits) — the authors note this but it weakens one of the two pre-registered measures.
- **Reproducibility:** Good by community standards (commits, seeds, config tables, pre-registration dates) but the artifact appears to be an internal reproduction of World Machine rather than the original authors' code, and the exact NVIDIA driver/GPU of the paired runs is not preserved.
- **Significance:** The headline lesson — "save θ_last if your evaluation compares table-written states to rolled-out states" — is immediately actionable for anyone building table-based latent-state world models, but downstream consequences are entirely unmeasured: no control-task performance, no effect on prediction quality, only distributional (W1) and paired (MSE/RMS) state distances.
- **Red flags:** Few, and mostly self-flagged. The main ones are the failed reproduction band on exactly the runs supporting the headline claim, and the possibility that the 10% residual and its "fifty-thousandfold above numerical floor" framing overstate practical importance when no downstream cost is ever estimated.

## Strengths

- Crisp causal design: saving θ_last before the reload turns a confound (which checkpoint?) into the main independent variable, with a built-in validity check (position-1 agreement under matched weights).
- Exemplary pre-registration discipline for a workshop paper — thresholds, primary comparisons, and decision rules fixed before inspection, and faithfully reported even when the answer is "our repairs do not work."
- Quantifies the gap at three granularities (pooled W1, position-resolved W1, paired per-item MSE/RMS) plus a probe safeguard, and shows the distillation sweep's pointwise-fitting-vs-distribution-recovery dissociation.
- The EM-critique (Observation 1) is a useful standalone clarification for anyone tempted to read SD's fixed-point iteration as maximum-likelihood EM.
- Honest, unusually specific limitations section (mixed-checkpoint sweep, two-seed scope, unknown generalization to the published error level, missing downstream costs).

## Limitations

- Two seeds, one synthetic benchmark (Toy1D), and both paired runs outside the pre-registered ±20% reproduction band — checkpoint separation at the published MSE (0.34348) remains untested, as is any real-data or control-task setting.
- The receptive-field sweep is mixed-checkpoint on a single seed-42 run without a saved θ_last, so its absolute numbers are not checkpoint-controlled (though the paired RF-63 student fails under both checkpoints).
- Marginal W1 ignores item pairing (paired errors are reported but without distributions or downstream costs); no evidence on whether the residual 10% gap matters for any task the model is actually used for.
- The mechanism of the shared-checkpoint residual — 50,000× above the numerical floor — is left open; the paper can eliminate (distillation, refinement) but not explain it.
- The two seed-7 repetitions used different PyTorch/CUDA builds, confounding variation sources; four runs cannot separate seed variance from repetition variance.

## Relevance

对研究世界模型/时序潜状态模型的训练-推理一致性、以及 LLM/深度学习实验可复现性的人而言，这是一篇方法论警示价值很高的工作：它用严格的双检查点对照证明「早停重载检查点」这类工程细节就能制造/解释 90% 的表观状态不一致，任何采用「训练期存储状态表 + 推理期冷启动重构」设计的模型都应先做同样的检查点匹配对照。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does checkpoint reload still dominate the gap at the published reproduction error (decoded-state MSE 0.34348), rather than at the +27–36% error levels of the two analyzed runs?
- What is the mechanism of the residual ~10% shared-checkpoint gap (50,000× above the numerical floor) — optimizer noise in the final epochs, the REPLACE convention, window-boundary effects, or something else?
- Does the state gap translate into downstream costs — e.g., worse prediction, planning, or control when rolled-out states replace table states in a task using the decoder or a policy head?
- Do the findings transfer beyond Toy1D to World Machine's other benchmarks, real time series, and to other table-plus-cold-start architectures (e.g., retrieval-augmented or explicit-memory models that write states during training)?
- Would simply evaluating with θ_last (or re-writing the table after reload) be the recommended practice, and does that change the published World Machine results themselves?
- Can the SD update be given an actual probabilistic interpretation (candidate scoring, explicit likelihood) so that EM-style guarantees or principled burn-in schedules apply?
