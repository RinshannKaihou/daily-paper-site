> **一句话总结：** 针对线性注意力（GDN）推理时低比特循环态量化导致长推理任务崩溃、输出无限变长的问题，本文提出用未来损失梯度引导的全局混合精度分配（GGA）加上把最近 8 次纯写入以 FP8 向量对单独维护的滑动窗口（PW）：在 Qwen3.5-4B/9B 的 4-bit 预算下，七任务宏平均从 STEPQuant@4 的 31.44%/37.42% 提升到 69.83%/78.63%（+38.39/+41.21 个百分点），平均输出长度反而缩短 40% 以上，接近 FP16 状态的 74.27%/79.58%。

> **TL;DR:** Quantizing the recurrent state of Gated DeltaNet (GDN) linear-attention models to 4 bits wrecks long-reasoning benchmarks (STEPQuant@4 scores only 0.67% on AIME and 0.00% on HMMT for Qwen3.5-4B, with 36.66% of responses hitting the 100K-token cap). The paper adds two orthogonal fixes on top of STEPQuant's quantizers and budget: (1) GGA, which weights candidate quantization residuals by squared gradients of future LM loss (offline calibration only) to re-allocate bits across the 768 state heads via dynamic programming, and (2) PW, a sliding window that keeps the last m=8 pure writes as FP8 vector pairs, propagating them through the full GDN transition and merging them into the low-bit history only after they age out. On seven tasks (AIME 2026, HMMT Feb 2026, GPQA Diamond, HumanEval+, MATH500, IFBench, LiveCodeBench v6), GGA alone gains +19.48/+29.84 points macro-average over STEPQuant@4 on 4B/9B, and GGA@4+PW-S8 reaches 69.83%/78.63% (+38.39/+41.21 over STEPQuant@4, within ~1 point of the FP16 reference on 9B) while cutting mean output length by ~40% and repetition stops to 0.10–0.46%.

## 核心贡献

- **全局梯度感知分配（GGA）**：在 STEPQuant 的候选量化格式与标称比特预算完全不变的前提下，用「未来语言建模损失对写回状态的梯度」逐坐标加权候选量化残差，构造每个头、每个位宽的风险代价，再用全局动态规划在全模型 768 个头上做混合精度分配（含 32 个 FP16 pivot）。梯度只在离线校准时计算（WikiText-2 上 32 条 2048 token 片段，每层 reservoir 采样 64 个快照），部署时零反传。
- **纯写入滑动窗口（PW）**：把 GDN 递推 $S_t = A_t S_{t-1} + B_t$ 中的纯写入项 $B_t = \beta_t k_t v_t^\top$ 拆出来，最近 m=8 次写入各自存为一对 FP8（E4M3FN）向量 $(p_{j,t}, v_j)$，左因子随完整状态转移传播（$p \leftarrow \gamma p - \beta k (k^\top p)$）、右因子固定；读取时新旧状态加全部窗口分支一起读出，最老分支在当前读出**之后**才合并进低比特历史态并重新量化——历史态仍每步量化，但新信息首次进入低比特态的时间被延迟。附录 A 给出理想精度下与原递推等价的证明。
- **动机诊断直击要害**：作者实测每个头的单次量化扰动对后续 256 步预测分布的 KL 影响，发现 STEPQuant 的分配与该经验敏感度严重错位——在敏感度更高（[2.40, 2.70)×10⁻⁴）的桶里平均只给 3.73 bit，反而低于低敏感桶的 5.28 bit；GGA 在同样 4.33 bit/value 总预算下把这两桶修正为 4.56 与 6.96 bit。
- **以「写入年龄」视角使用存储**：同等存储对比实验（@4+PW-S8 ≈ 5.78 bit/value vs @5 ≈ 5.68 bit/value）表明，把额外存储用于维护近期写入比单纯提高历史态位宽更划算：分数更高且输出更短。
- **量化导致「无限 Wait」退化的首个细粒度案例刻画**：无窗口时模型在第 947 行刚验证完 48 的十个因子、第 948 行立刻否定同一列表，最终 81,921 token、831 行连续 "Wait." 被重复检测器截停；加 PW 后同一 seed 自然 EOS 结束、57,682 token 给出正确答案 48。

## Key Contributions

- **Global Gradient-aware Allocation (GGA):** keeps STEPQuant's candidate quantizers, bit-width menu, and nominal budget, but replaces the persistence×distortion cost with a risk proxy that weights squared candidate quantization residuals coordinatewise by squared writeback-sensitivity adjoints — gradients of the future next-token loss backpropagated along the recorded reference state transitions ($J_{u,t} = q_{u,t} g_{u,t}^\top + G^{wb}_{u,t}$, $G^{wb}_{u,t-1} = A_{u,t} J_{u,t}$). Bit widths (plus 32 FP16 pivots) are then assigned by two-stage global dynamic programming over all 768 heads. All gradient work is offline (32×2048-token WikiText-2 segments, 64 reservoir-sampled snapshots per layer); deployment uses a frozen bit-width table with no backpropagation.
- **Pure-Write Sliding Window (PW):** exploits the rank-one structure of the GDN pure-write term to maintain the last m=8 writes as FP8 vector pairs with per-vector FP32 scales. Left factors $p_{j,t}$ propagate through the exact state transition at every step (implemented in low-rank form, $O(m d_k)$ per head), values $v_j$ stay frozen; the oldest branch is merged into the low-bit historical state only after the current readout, and the historical state is still quantized at every step. A proof of recurrence equivalence under exact arithmetic is provided (Appendix A), and the per-step algorithm is fully specified (Algorithm 1).
- **Diagnostic evidence that prior allocation is sensitivity-mismatched:** measuring per-head empirical quantization impact (KL divergence over 256 replayed steps at INT2/4/6/8) shows STEPQuant assigns 5.28 bits to the [1.50,1.80)×10⁻⁴ bin but only 3.73 bits to the higher-impact [2.40,2.70)×10⁻⁴ bin; at the same 4.33 bit/value average, GGA assigns 4.56 and 6.96 bits to those bins respectively.
- **Write-age view of storage spending:** paired comparisons at matched equivalent storage (~5.68 vs ~5.78–5.79 bit/value) show that spending extra capacity on window maintenance (@4+PW-S8) beats raising the accumulated state to @5 on both macro-average score (+0.41 to +3.29 pts) and mean output length (−5.63% to −10.80%).
- **A crisp case study of quantization-induced reasoning collapse:** without PW the model re-verifies and then rejects a just-confirmed divisor list, loops into 831 consecutive "Wait." lines, and is stopped by the repetition detector at 81,921 tokens with no answer; with PW (same prompt/seed) it finishes naturally at 57,682 tokens (−29.59%) with the correct answer 48.

## Methodology

The GDN recurrence is decomposed as $S_t = A_t S_{t-1} + B_t$ with $A_t = \gamma_t(I - \beta_t k_t k_t^\top)$ (state transition: forgetting + key-selective correction) and $B_t = \beta_t k_t v_t^\top$ (rank-one pure write). Stepwise quantized writeback ($\hat{S}_t = Q_b(A_t \hat{S}_{t-1} + B_t)$) means every writeback error re-enters all future recurrences — the root cause of long-reasoning degradation.

**GGA.** On unquantized calibration trajectories, the next-token LM loss $\mathcal{L}$ is differentiated w.r.t. each head's raw readout $o_{u,t}$; a writeback adjoint $G^{wb}_{u,t}$ is then propagated backward through the recorded reference transitions (Eq. 6), cleanly separating the already-completed current readout from future recurrence effects. The head-level cost for candidate bit width $b$ is $\frac{1}{2N_u}\sum_t \| G^{wb}_{u,t} \odot \Delta S^{(b)}_{u,t} \|_F^2$, where $\Delta S^{(b)}$ is STEPQuant's candidate quantization residual against an FP16 snapshot. Unlike STEPQuant, GGA does not multiply by error-lifetime weights nor normalize per layer/head, preserving the global relative scale. Allocation proceeds in two dynamic-programming passes: a preliminary integer allocation of all heads under the exact bit-sum budget, then FP16 pivot selection (top-P by residual risk) followed by re-allocation of the remaining integer budget $B_{int}$ (2816/3584/4352 bits summed over 736 heads for @4/@5/@6; candidate widths {2,4,6,8} for @4/@5 and {4,6,8} for @6).

**PW.** Unrolling the recurrence over the last $r = \min(m,t)$ steps shows each recent write contributes $(A_t \cdots A_{j+1}) B_j = p_{j,t} v_j^\top$ with $p_{j,t} = A_t p_{j,t-1}$ — rank-one forever, so only the vector pair needs storing. The logical state is $\hat{S}_t = \hat{O}_t + \sum_{j \in J_t} p_{j,t} v_j^\top$: a low-bit quantized "old" state plus at most m FP8 branches. Per step: propagate the old state and all branch left factors; read out using old state + branches + the current (FP32) new write; then evict the oldest branch into the old state, quantize, and enqueue the new write encoded as FP8 with per-vector scales. Crucially the old state is still quantized at every step — PW delays only the *first* incorporation of a write into low-bit storage, unlike LeapQuant's block-window boundary requantization. FP16 pivot heads bypass PW entirely. The recent queue costs 2112 bytes/head at m=8 (1.03125 bit/value), and the equivalent-storage accounting (Table 3) is explicit and reproducible.

**Evaluation protocol.** Qwen3.5-4B/9B (24 GDN layers × 32 heads of 128×128 = 768 heads; weights in BF16), A100-SXM4-80GB with SGLang 0.5.12. Seven tasks: AIME 2026 (30 problems), HMMT Feb 2026 (33), GPQA Diamond (198), HumanEval+ (164), MATH500 (500), IFBench (300), LiveCodeBench v6 (200); 5 samples/problem for AIME/HMMT, 2 elsewhere; temperature 1, top-p 0.95, top-k 20, thinking enabled, 100K-token output cap, 131K context; identical frozen problem lists, prompts, and per-problem seeds across methods. A repetition detector stops periodic outputs (counted separately from cap hits); unanswered responses stay in the denominator.

## Results & Highlights

- **Macro-average at @4 (seven tasks, Qwen3.5-4B / 9B):** naive INT4 = 1.67%/2.10%; STEPQuant@4 = 31.44%/37.42%; GGA@4 = 50.92%/67.26% (+19.48/+29.84 pts over STEPQuant@4 at the identical budget and quantizers); GGA@4+PW-S8 = 69.83%/78.63% (+38.39/+41.21 pts over STEPQuant@4), against FP16-state references of 74.27%/79.58% — on 9B the combined method lands within 0.95 pt of the FP16 reference.
- **Long-reasoning recovery is the headline:** on Qwen3.5-9B at @4, AIME goes 2.67% (STEPQuant) → 66.00% (GGA) → 84.00% (GGA+PW-S8) and HMMT 4.24% → 45.45% → 72.12%, while MATH500 goes 59.10% → 94.90% → 99.30% and GPQA 25.25% → 66.41%; by contrast HumanEval+ barely moves (83.84% at STEPQuant@4), confirming the failure mode is specifically long reasoning, not general capability.
- **Shorter outputs, not truncated ones:** at @4, GGA cuts mean output length from STEPQuant's 58.23K/55.42K tokens to 42.96K/37.78K (−26.23%/−31.83%); adding PW-S8 shrinks it further to 29.07K/23.89K (an additional −32.32%/−36.77% vs GGA alone). On 9B AIME specifically: 93.51K (STEP@4) → 69.27K (GGA@4) → 43.23K (GGA@4+PW) while accuracy rises 2.67% → 66.00% → 84.00%.
- **Abnormal stops nearly eliminated:** at @4, 36.66%/30.67% of STEPQuant responses hit the 100K-token cap and another 9.97%/9.71% end in repetition stops; with GGA@4+PW-S8 these fall to 4.61%/2.44% and 0.46%/0.10%, with natural EOS at 94.93%/97.47% (vs 90.10%/94.11% for FP16).
- **Effective at @6 too:** STEPQuant@6 = 70.54%/76.99%, GGA@6 = 71.83%/79.22%, GGA@6+PW-S8 = 73.96%/79.88% (4B/9B macro-averages); GGA still shortens mean length at @6 by 10.45%/14.65% (32.64K/29.35K → 29.23K/25.05K tokens) with equal-or-better scores — all seven tasks shorten on both models.
- **Window beats raw bit width at matched storage:** at ~5.78 bit/value (@4+PW-S8) vs ~5.68 bit/value (@5), the window configuration scores higher and outputs shorter under both allocations — GGA: +2.61/+0.41 pts macro-average and −9.91%/−10.80% mean length; STEP: +3.29/+1.57 pts and −5.63%/−7.54%.
- **Allocation diagnostics validate the mechanism:** at the same 4.33 bit/value average, GGA assigns 6.96 bits (vs STEPQuant's 3.73) to the most sensitive head bin ([2.70,3.00)×10⁻⁴) and 4.56 bits (vs 3.73) to [2.40,2.70)×10⁻⁴, monotonically tracking measured KL impact where STEPQuant does not.
- **Paired case study (HMMT, same seed):** without PW, the trajectory rejects a just-verified divisor list, emits 831 consecutive "Wait." lines, and is repetition-stopped at 81,921 tokens with no answer; with PW-S8 it ends naturally at 57,682 tokens (−29.59%) with the correct answer 48.

## Authors & Credibility

Three authors (Qinghang Huang, Xuchu Huang, Xunzhao Yin as corresponding author) from Zhejiang University; the paper is an October 2026 preprint (arXiv:2610.39931v1, cs.LG, dated October 18, 2026), so it has not been peer-reviewed. Credibility signals are strong for a preprint: the evaluation protocol is unusually careful (frozen problem lists, shared per-problem seeds, temperature/top-p/top-k fixed across methods, unanswered responses kept in the denominator, repetition stops counted separately from cap hits, FP32 and FP16 float references reported); the storage accounting in Appendix B.5 is explicit to the byte (FP8 codes + FP32 scales + FP16 pivots, with the queue reserved for all 768 heads honestly adding 1.03125 bit/value to the bill); and the limitations section is frank about open mechanism questions, missing window ablations, missing kernels, and narrow model coverage. The build-on baseline (STEPQuant) is reimplemented under identical quantizers and budget, making the allocation comparison apples-to-apples. Inference engineering is real (SGLang 0.5.12 on A100-80GB), not a simulation.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** incremental but well-motivated. Gradient-sensitivity weighting descends from SqueezeLLM's Fisher-weighted weight quantization, and the paper honestly says so; the new part is propagating writeback adjoints through the GDN recurrence and applying them to *dynamic recurrent states* rather than static weights. PW's age-based FP8 queue is a genuinely distinct point in the design space vs LeapQuant's block-boundary requantization (PW quantizes the old state at every step and only delays each write's *first* low-bit incorporation), and the rank-one-branch propagation trick is clean.
- **Rigor of comparison:** the two most closely related recurrent-state methods — DAMP and LeapQuant — are cited and described but never appear in any results table; the empirical fight is only against naive INT4/INT6 and STEPQuant. This leaves the "better than the field" claim resting on one competitor.
- **Missing ablations the authors themselves admit:** only window length m=8 and only FP8 queue precision are evaluated — no m-sweep, no INT8/FP16 queue variant, no alternative merge strategies. Also, the flagship @6+PW combination is not a pure composition: Appendix B.3 reveals GGA@6+PW-S8 is *recalibrated* on a PW reference trajectory with a straight-through estimator, so its bit-width table differs from GGA@6 (visible in Table 2's differing head counts), slightly muddying which component delivers the @6 gains.
- **Statistical power:** AIME 2026 has 30 problems and HMMT 33; five samples help, but a per-task point estimate like 84.00% vs 86.67% (FP32) on AIME rests on ~150 draws — no confidence intervals or significance tests are reported anywhere.
- **Runtime cost is unmeasured:** PW adds $O(m d_k)$ propagation, $O(m(d_k+d_v))$ readout per head per decode step plus FP8 re-encoding, and the authors concede no dedicated kernels or end-to-end latency/throughput numbers exist. For a paper whose motivation is inference deployment, leaving the wall-clock question entirely open is the biggest practical gap.
- **Generalization is thin:** exactly one model family (Qwen3.5-4B/9B hybrid GDN models), no GLA or Mamba-2 results despite those being introduced in Related Work, and calibration is on WikiText-2 (32×2048 tokens) — small and domain-mismatched from the math/code reasoning it is judged on (though its success despite the mismatch is arguably a robustness point).
- **Red flags:** none serious — no cherry-picked metric (macro-average is task-equal, lengths are task-equal averaged), losses are kept in the denominator, and IFBench results are reported even though they recover least (56.50 vs FP32's 62.00 on 4B). Minor overclaim risk: "approaches FP32" holds on 9B (78.63 vs 79.95) but on 4B a 5.7-pt macro gap to FP32 (69.83 vs 75.54) remains.

## Strengths

- Identifies and demonstrates a real, under-addressed failure mode — quantized recurrent states specifically destroying *long* multi-step reasoning (AIME/HMMT near 0% under STEPQuant@4) and inflating outputs via repetition loops — with a concrete token-level case study rather than just aggregate scores.
- GGA is a principled upgrade: same budget, same quantizers, only the allocation objective changes, so the +19.48/+29.84-pt macro gains at @4 are cleanly attributable to loss-gradient sensitivity.
- PW is theoretically grounded (exact recurrence-equivalence proof under infinite precision) and the finite-precision algorithm is fully specified down to FP8 encoding, scales, and per-step pseudocode.
- Evaluation hygiene is above average: frozen lists, shared seeds, matched-storage comparisons via honest equivalent-bit accounting, separate accounting of EOS/cap/repetition terminations, and float references bracketing every table.
- The shorter-is-better result is well-argued: shorter outputs co-occur with higher accuracy and fewer pathological stops, so length reduction reflects recovered reasoning rather than early truncation.

## Limitations

- Only Qwen3.5-4B/9B evaluated; no other model families, sizes, or linear-attention architectures (GLA, Mamba-2), and no comparison against DAMP or LeapQuant despite them being the closest prior work.
- No window-length (m), queue-precision, or merge-strategy ablations — PW is evaluated at exactly one configuration (m=8, FP8).
- No latency/throughput/kernel results; PW's per-step branch propagation and re-encoding overhead is unquantified, and the current layout wastefully reserves queues even for FP16 pivot heads.
- Small evaluation sets for the headline tasks (AIME n=30, HMMT n=33) with no variance reporting; single-GPU, single-framework (SGLang 0.5.12) setup.
- The @6+PW configuration uses a different calibration procedure (PW reference trajectory + identity STE) than GGA@6, so component attribution at the higher budget is less clean than at @4.
- Mechanistic understanding of why quantization triggers repetitive "Wait." loops remains observational; the authors flag this as future work.

## Relevance

本文直接命中「线性注意力/混合架构推理部署中的循环态压缩」这一方向：把 GDN 状态压到 4-5 bit 且在长推理任务上几乎不掉点，对低显存高并发长上下文服务（如数学推理、代码 agent）有直接工程价值；GGA 的「损失梯度引导位宽分配」和 PW 的「按写入年龄分层存储」两个思想都可迁移到其他递归态/KV-cache 量化方案中。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does PW behave as a function of window length m (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32)? Is m=8 optimal for all seven tasks, or do short-output tasks (IFBench, HumanEval+) need far less window than AIME/HMMT?
- Does the queue need FP8, or do INT8/INT4 branch encodings with per-vector scales preserve most of the benefit at half the queue cost (directly shrinking the 1.03 bit/value overhead)?
- What is the actual wall-clock cost of PW at realistic batch sizes — can the O(m·d_k) branch propagation be fused into the existing GDN kernel, and does the decode-time overhead erase the storage win on memory-bound serving?
- Would GGA calibration on reasoning-style data (rather than WikiText-2) further improve allocation for math/code workloads, or is the WikiText-2- calibrated allocation already saturated?
- Do GGA and PW transfer to other recurrent-state architectures (GLA, Mamba-2) and to full-attention KV-cache quantization, where the "write age" decomposition has no exact rank-one analogue?
- The repetition-loop pathology (831 consecutive "Wait." lines) appears under quantization but presumably also occurs in float models at lower rates — is PW merely masking a pre-existing fragility of long chain-of-thought decoding that better decoding strategies could also fix?
