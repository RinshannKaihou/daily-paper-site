> **一句话总结：** 在 Qwen3-1.7B 上做五轮递归自训练后，准确率与答案排序能力几乎不变（Global-MMLU 上 AUROC 0.711→0.704），真正被改变的是置信度"温度"（漂移 +0.049，最自信答案的准确率从 0.867 跌到 0.800）；论文证明锐化主要来自"每个答案拟合两次 + top-p 截断"两个循环设计选择，而无需标签的熵匹配再校准能把 ECE 恢复到接近标签 oracle（focal loss 下 0.147→0.094，oracle 0.091），与 focal loss 配对后同时保住温度与排序（重校准后 AUROC 0.750 vs 标准 CE 的 0.699）。

> **TL;DR:** Five rounds of recursive self-training on Qwen3-1.7B (trained on its own TriviaQA/NQ-Open answers, evaluated on 600 Global-MMLU English items) leave accuracy (0.573→0.567) and AUROC (0.711→0.704) essentially unchanged, but shift confidence temperature by +0.049 and make the most-confident answers less reliable (top-20%-confidence accuracy 0.867→0.800). A zero-gradient argument shows sharpening is not inherent to self-training — it comes mostly from fitting each answer twice plus top-p truncation, and removing both keeps entropy flat. Remedies split cleanly: TCE/entropy-bonus/single-pass hold temperature without helping ranking, while focal loss and two-sample targets raise AUROC to 0.742 (+0.038 over standard CE, p=0.009) but overshoot into underconfidence; label-free entropy-matching recalibration (ECE within 0.010 of the label oracle, verified at 1.7B and 8B) pairs with focal loss to get both properties.

## 核心贡献

- **把"置信度"拆成两个独立属性并分别测量**：温度（temperature，整体置信水平相对校准起点的漂移，可用一个标量恢复）与判别力（discrimination，高置信是否对应正确答案，用精确去偏 log 概率上的 AUROC 测量）。单一校准指标（如原始 ECE）会混淆二者——基座模型本身病态过度自信（平均置信 0.963 vs 准确率 0.573），任何软化操作都会"改善"原始 ECE。
- **核心实证发现：自训练改变的是温度而非知识**。五轮标准循环后准确率与 AUROC 几乎不动，但温度漂移 +0.049，且模型最自信的前 20% 答案准确率从 0.867 降到 0.800——准确率基准完全看不见这种退化。
- **机制解释（零梯度论证）**：在自身分布的精确样本上训练，期望梯度为零，锐化并非自训练固有；观测到的锐化来自两个常见设计选择——每个答案被拟合两次（两 epoch，占主要部分）和 top-p=0.95 截断（占其余）。两者都去掉后，模型在自生成答案上的熵五轮保持平坦（0.292→0.296 nats）。
- **无标签再校准**：每轮用熵匹配（令当前模型在无标签评测输入上的平均预测熵等于校准起点模型）选温度，ECE 与每轮用标签重拟合的 oracle 差距 <0.010（1.7B 与 8B 均验证），仅标签平滑例外。
- **对现有补救措施的重新评估**：保持温度的补救（TCE、熵奖励、单遍训练）不提升排序；提升排序的补救（focal loss、多样本目标）反而造成过度不自信；抗坍塌文献最依赖的参考数据混合在原始 ECE 上"变好"（−0.072）实为漂移到 −0.190 的深度不自信；基于置信度的自我验证在单种子测试中反而加剧过度自信。focal loss + 无标签再校准是唯一同时达成两个属性的组合。

## Key Contributions

- **A two-property decomposition of confidence**: temperature (overall confidence level, drift from a calibrated starting point, repairable by one scalar) vs discrimination (whether higher confidence goes with correctness, measured by AUROC on exact debiased option-letter log-probabilities). A single calibration number conflates them — the base model is so overconfident (mean confidence 0.963 vs accuracy 0.573, raw ECE 0.391) that any softening intervention "improves" raw ECE.
- **Main empirical finding: self-training moves temperature, not knowledge.** Over five standard rounds, accuracy (0.573→0.567) and AUROC (0.711→0.704) stay within noise while calibration drift reaches +0.049 ±0.006, and accuracy on the top-20%-most-confident items falls 0.867→0.800 — a degradation no accuracy benchmark reveals.
- **A mechanism for the sharpening via a zero-gradient argument**: training on exact samples from the model's own distribution has zero expected gradient, so sharpening must come from how the loop departs from that ideal. They trace it to two design choices — fitting each self-generated answer twice (two epochs; the larger share) and top-p=0.95 truncation (the rest). Removing both keeps the model's entropy on its own outputs flat across five rounds (0.292→0.296 nats).
- **Label-free recalibration that nearly matches the label oracle**: choosing each round's temperature by matching predictive entropy to the calibrated starting model (a simplified relative of Luo et al. 2025) lands within 0.010 of label-refitted ECE for every replicated protocol except label smoothing, at both 1.7B and 8B.
- **A re-evaluation of collapse remedies through the confidence lens**: temperature-holding remedies (truncated cross-entropy, entropy bonus, single-pass training) do not improve ranking; ranking-improving remedies (focal loss, two exact samples per prompt) overshoot into underconfidence; the collapse literature's standard defence (50% reference-data mixing) looks good on raw ECE (−0.072) but is actually a −0.190 drift into underconfidence; confidence-based self-verification made overconfidence worse in a single-seed test. Pairing a ranking loss with label-free recalibration is the only combination that achieves both properties.

## Methodology

The loop: start from Qwen3-1.7B (reasoning disabled) as M0 with a fixed pool of 3,000 questions from TriviaQA (rc.nocontext) and NQ-Open. In round g, model Mg answers all 3,000 questions (temperature 1.0, top-p 0.95, max 32 tokens); the 3,000 question–answer pairs are used to fine-tune Mg for two epochs with LoRA (rank 16, lr 2e-4, effective batch 16, paged 8-bit AdamW, completion-only loss), the adapter is merged, yielding Mg+1. Models M0–M5 are evaluated; each replicated protocol runs with three seeds (seed changes generation and training; M0 and its evaluation are shared).

Evaluation: all models are scored on the same 600 English Global-MMLU items (no overlap with the training pool). Confidence is read from the multiple-choice option letters — each letter's log-probability is debiased by subtracting its mean over the evaluation set (removing positional priors) and renormalized, then kept at full precision. This matters: stored as probabilities, the chosen option's confidence is at least 0.999999 for 283/600 items at g0, so AUROC would tie and reward any softening method; exact log-probabilities have no ties.

Measurements: accuracy; discrimination via AUROC of the chosen option's log-probability, plus average precision and top-two-margin AUROC (Appendix C) and accuracy among the top-20%-most-confident items; temperature via calibration drift — mean confidence minus accuracy at g5 under a temperature T0 fitted once at g0 (mean T0 = 10.5, held-out ECE 0.074) with two-fold cross-fitting; and per-round sharpening via entropy kept per round, the geometric mean of H(g+1)/H(g) over the model's own generated answers. Protocols beyond standard CE: loop variants (single pass, step-matched half data, exact sampling with top-p off, multiple samples per prompt) and loss variants (ForTIFAI's TCE at τ=0.9, focal loss from the same CAL family, entropy bonus, label smoothing), plus confidence-based self-verification (keep the more confident half) and 50% reference-data mixing. A promotion rule (g5 AUROC ≥ +0.03 over the standard loop with accuracy within 0.01) was written into the experiment scripts before any screened protocol ran (Appendix A). Statistics: paired item-level bootstrap for per-seed intervals, Welch t-tests over three seeds, uncorrected.

## Results & Highlights

- **Self-training moves temperature, not knowledge (Global-MMLU, 600 items)**: standard CE loop moves accuracy 0.573→0.567 and AUROC 0.711→0.704 over five rounds (paired bootstrap puts the AUROC change within noise), while calibration drift from the calibrated g0 reaches +0.049 ±0.006 — the model becomes confidently wrong in a way accuracy metrics miss. Raw ECE barely registers this (+0.015) because mean confidence starts at 0.963 with no room to rise.
- **The most confident answers degrade first**: average precision (which weights the items the model is most sure of) falls from 0.782 at g0 to 0.741 at g5 under the standard loop (per-seed drops of 0.029–0.050, each 95% bootstrap CI excluding zero), and accuracy among the top 20% most-confident Global-MMLU items falls from 0.867 to 0.800.
- **Sharpening is traced to two design choices**: model entropy on its own training answers falls 60% over four rounds under the standard loop (0.293→0.117 nats). Switching top-p off raises entropy kept per round from 0.795 to 0.856; training one epoch instead of two raises it to 0.944 (the step-matched half-data control keeps only 0.881, so repetition is not just "training less"); with both removed, entropy stays flat (0.292→0.296 nats), matching the zero-gradient prediction. Top-p=0.95 truncation samples greedily for 73% of steps at g0 and 90% at g5, so truncation feeds on the sharpening it causes.
- **Remedies split into two groups (3 seeds each)**: TCE τ=0.9 (drift −0.019), entropy bonus (+0.000) and single-pass training (−0.017) hold temperature against standard's +0.049 (each p ≤ 0.024) but do not significantly raise AUROC (0.714/0.715/0.727 vs 0.704, p ≥ 0.176); focal loss and two exact samples per prompt raise AUROC to 0.742 vs 0.704 for CE (+0.038, p=0.009 and p=0.011), lift average precision above its starting value (0.811 and 0.798 vs g0's 0.782) and top-20% accuracy to 0.908/0.883 vs 0.800 — but overshoot into underconfidence (drifts −0.132 and −0.069).
- **Reference mixing, the standard anti-collapse defence, mainly moves temperature**: replacing half of each round's answers with TriviaQA/NQ-Open reference answers improves raw ECE by −0.072 over five rounds, yet from the calibrated start it lands at drift −0.190 ±0.001 (deep underconfidence) with AUROC 0.720 (p=0.261 vs CE). Confidence-based self-verification (keep the top-50%-confident half) pushed drift to +0.046 vs −0.003 for its matched random-half control (single seed, reported as a warning).
- **Label-free recalibration nearly matches the label oracle**: entropy matching brings ECE at g5 within 0.010 of label-refitted temperature for every replicated protocol — focal loss 0.147 (stale T0) → 0.094 (label-free) vs 0.091 (oracle); 50% reference data 0.192 → 0.074 vs 0.078; and at 8B, reference-data-only training 0.247 → 0.057 vs oracle 0.059.
- **Combining gets both properties**: focal loss followed by label-free recalibration reaches ECE 0.094 (vs 0.090 for the standard loop) while keeping its discrimination advantage — AUROC recomputed after recalibration is 0.750 vs 0.699 for standard training (+0.051, p=0.001).

## Authors & Credibility

The three authors (Dipto Sumit, Syeda Fabiayee Jahan, Farig Sadeque) are from BRAC University, Dhaka, Bangladesh — not an established calibration/collapse lab, and this reads as a resourceful small-budget study: the whole protocol suite cost about 40 GPU-hours on one 16 GB consumer GPU, with the 8B diagnosis using about 10 hours on a rented 24 GB GPU. Credibility is strengthened by unusually honest methodology: a promotion rule for the remedy screen was written into the experiment scripts before any screened protocol ran (Appendix A discloses it verbatim, along with the original, only partially supported hypothesis); single-seed arms (self-verification, entropy-matched sampler, four-sample arms, the flat-entropy confirmation arm) are explicitly flagged as warnings rather than results; per-item exact log-probabilities were stored and earlier runs re-scored to reproduce stored probabilities exactly; and a Bangla extension invalidated by a tokenizer truncation bug is disclosed and excluded rather than quietly dropped. Code and experiment scripts are at an anonymous.4open.science repository, with per-item records promised for the final version. Main credibility deductions: three seeds per arm with uncorrected p-values across many comparisons, and Welch t-tests on n=3 are fragile.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: the individual ingredients (temperature scaling, focal loss, entropy bonus, reference mixing, entropy-based label-free recalibration) are all known; the contribution is the temperature-vs-discrimination decomposition applied to self-training remedies, plus the clean zero-gradient mechanism result. The label-free recalibration is explicitly adapted from Luo et al. (2025) and not claimed as new. The framing genuinely fills a gap — collapse remedies had only been judged by accuracy/perplexity/divergence, never by the confidence they were designed to protect — but it is a measurement-and-mechanism paper on a small loop, not a new method.
- **Rigor**: good on the measurement side (exact debiased log-probabilities to avoid AUROC ties from saturated probabilities, cross-fitted drift, pre-registered screen), but statistically thin elsewhere: AUROC effects are 0.02–0.04 and drifts 0.02–0.13 on three seeds, p-values uncorrected across the many comparisons reported, and the paper's own original hypothesis (calibration degrades before accuracy) was supported in only one of three seeds — the two-property framing emerged from the data, a garden-of-forking-paths risk only partially mitigated by the pre-written screen.
- **A specific confound the authors admit but do not resolve**: the focal-loss headline gain cannot be separated from simply training less — it exceeds the step-matched half-data control (AUROC 0.721) by only +0.022 with p=0.127, so the +0.038 AUROC gain over standard CE is part loss, part reduced repetition.
- **A data/model quirk that limits generalization**: the base Qwen3-1.7B is pathologically overconfident (mean confidence 0.963 vs accuracy 0.573; calibrated T0 = 10.5), which is precisely why drift-from-calibrated-start is the only usable calibration metric here, but a near-calibrated starting model could behave very differently; the 8B model (better calibrated) was used only for the temperature diagnosis, not for any of the remedies. The eval is a single 600-item multiple-choice set, confidence read from option letters, LoRA rank 16 only.
- **Reproducibility**: strong for this subfield — released scripts with decision rules, exact software versions (PyTorch 2.10, Transformers 5.15, PEFT 0.20), fixed item identifiers, stored exact log-probabilities, and cheap compute (a 16 GB consumer GPU suffices). The anonymous repo is a minor caveat pending camera-ready.
- **Significance**: the practical prescriptions are cheap and actionable (fit each answer once, sample without truncation, pair ranking losses with per-round label-free recalibration), and the warning that self-verification can accelerate overconfidence is timely given Fu et al. (2025) prove the opposite target (collapse prevention). But with one small model, one eval set, and small effects, this is a carefully scoped cautionary study rather than a definitive map of the remedy space.
- **Red flags**: none serious — no overclaiming; the abstract's claims match the tables, single-seed results are consistently demoted to "warnings", and limitations are unusually candid.

## Strengths

- A crisp, load-bearing conceptual split (temperature vs discrimination) backed by a measurement protocol designed for it — calibrate first, then measure drift, with exact log-probabilities so AUROC ties cannot fake discrimination gains.
- The zero-gradient argument plus the ablation that confirms it (entropy flat at 0.292→0.296 nats when double-fitting and truncation are both removed) is a satisfying mechanism result: it converts "self-training sharpens confidence" from a mystery into two concrete, zero-cost design choices.
- Directly actionable findings: single-pass training and TCE hold calibration for free; focal loss + two-sample targets are the only tested ways to improve ranking; entropy matching recovers temperature without labels to within 0.010 of the oracle, including at 8B.
- Reframes a standard defence: reference-data mixing, judged by raw ECE, looks like the best remedy (−0.072) but is actually the biggest temperature overshoot (−0.190) — a caution for anyone evaluating anti-collapse methods with calibration error on an overconfident base model.
- Exceptional transparency: pre-written promotion rules, single-seed arms labelled as warnings, an invalidated Bangla extension disclosed, and the failed original hypothesis reported in an appendix.

## Limitations

- One model family (Qwen3-1.7B for everything, Qwen3-8B only for the temperature diagnosis, not the remedies), one 600-item evaluation set (English Global-MMLU), and confidence read only from multiple-choice option letters; scoring option texts or open-ended generation may behave differently.
- Small absolute effects (AUROC differences 0.02–0.04, drifts 0.02–0.13) resting on three seeds with uncorrected p-values; several mechanism arms (flat-entropy, four-sample, entropy-matched sampler, self-verification) have a single seed.
- The focal-loss discrimination gain is confounded with training less (only +0.022 over the step-matched half-data control, p=0.127).
- The loop is pure self-training with no external learning signal, so it isolates what self-training does to confidence but cannot show how the remedies interact with a real improvement signal such as a correctness filter.
- Only one label-free recalibration variant tested (entropy matching); divergence-based methods or Thermometer may track the oracle differently, and entropy-aware truncation (top-H) was not tested inside the loop — the authors' own entropy-matched sampler overshot into underconfidence (−0.082, single seed).
- The two-property framing emerged from the data after the pre-registered hypothesis (calibration degrades before accuracy) was supported in only one of three seeds.

## Relevance

论文研究的正是自进化/自改进闭环（递归自训练）在长时间运行中的可靠性与信任问题：自训练循环表面上准确率不变，却在悄然漂移置信度、腐蚀最自信答案的可靠性，这正是自改进 agent 系统需要监控的隐性失效模式；其"每轮无标签温度再校准"与"按置信度筛选数据会加剧过度自信"的发现，对任何用模型自身置信度做数据选择或自我验证的自改进流水线都是直接的工程警示。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the temperature/discrimination decomposition hold under full fine-tuning (vs LoRA rank 16), larger models, and better-calibrated starting points — or is the drift pattern specific to pathologically overconfident bases like Qwen3-1.7B (T0 = 10.5)?
- Would entropy-aware truncation such as top-H sampling inside the loop remove the truncation-driven sharpening without the underconfidence overshoot the authors' entropy-matched sampler showed (−0.082)?
- How do these remedies interact with a genuine improvement signal — e.g., correctness filtering or external verification — rather than pure self-training on unverified answers?
- Does option-letter confidence drift track free-form or verbalized confidence in open-ended generation, where deployed users actually read the model's certainty?
- With more seeds or step-matched multi-sample controls, can the focal loss's +0.038 AUROC gain be cleanly separated from the effect of simply fitting each answer less (the half-data control already reaches 0.721)?
- Does confidence-based self-verification accelerating overconfidence (+0.046 drift vs −0.003 for the random-half control) replicate across selection rules and keep rates, given it contradicts collapse-prevention guarantees that assume a fixed base-model scorer?
