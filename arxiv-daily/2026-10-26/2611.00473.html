Key metric

< 10%

Covariance-trace prediction error on a 4,753-parameter nonlinear network — sustained across two orders of magnitude of variance (10⁻⁵–10⁻³).

also: EoS threshold matched to ±0.5% with zero fitted parameters · loss-curve NRMSE 10⁻³–10⁻⁴ where gradient flow hits 10⁻²–10⁻¹ → results

Why this matters

Gives a mechanistic basis for predicting divergence and loss spikes in real training runs — via sharpness-to-learning-rate ratios and variance growth along top Hessian modes.

Critical edge

SLF is never compared head-to-head with the higher-order SDE approximations it claims to beat — that claim rests entirely on theory — and validation stops at a few thousand parameters.

from Critical Assessment: “a straightforward experiment that is conspicuously absent”

Methodology

1 · Discrete updateθ(t+δt) = θ(t) + ηf(θ)
2 · Kramers–Moyalkeep the usually-discarded O(δt²) term; δt = η
3 · Moment equationsunder Dₜ = ∂ₜ + (η/2)∂ₜ²
4 · Gaussian closuredrop cumulants ≥ 3 → closed system
5 · SLFcoupled flow for mean m and covariance C
  • The mean descends an effective loss L_eff = ⟨L⟩ + ½Tr(C⟨H⟩) — covariance × curvature acts as a dynamic implicit regularizer pushing training away from sharp minima.
  • The covariance destabilizes exactly at ηλ = 2: for quadratic losses this is the exact one-step second-moment change. SDE drift expansions converge only for ηλ < 1 and contain no such instability — explaining why SDE surrogates fail to stabilize where SGD does.
  • Framework covers any stochastic update rule (SGD batch noise, RL exploration noise); no infinite-width assumption.
Testbeds
· 2-2-2 deep linear network — 800 identically-initialized SGD replicas; η ∈ 0.05–0.20, B ∈ 4–256
· GELU classifier on binary MNIST — P = 4,753; 256 replicas; 200-step segments from a shared warm start
· Two-layer tanh EoS probe — 6×10⁴ antithetic replica pairs, one-step growth-rate measurement

Results & Highlights

Beyond the headline metric: mechanism demo on a 1D landscape (10⁴ replicas) — with curvature above 2/η, the covariance diverges near the sharp minimum and the ensemble escapes to the global one. Finite step size alone — no batch noise — escapes sharp minima.

Strengthswhat holds

  • Treats the finite-step instability exactly for quadratic losses — fixing the structural blind spot of SDE approximations (no instability at ηλ = 2, convergence only for ηλ < 1).
  • Unifies four previously separate descriptions — moment equations, self-stabilization, central flow, mean-square SGD stability — as limits or stationary points of one dynamical system.
  • Covariance is a dynamical variable on equal footing with the mean: SGD's flat-minima bias becomes quantitative and time-resolved, not a heuristic.
  • Empirically scrupulous — exact noise-term evaluation (cross-checked ≤ 0.18%), gauge projections, ensemble noise floors, and an explicit map of where the theory fails.
  • General formulation invites extensions to momentum, adaptive methods, and RL exploration noise.

Limitationswhat breaks

  • Gaussian closure: third-plus cumulants are dropped, yet SGD is a random-coefficient recursion with generically heavy-tailed stationary distributions — closure and variance estimator fail together once Hill index < 2.
  • Cost: P×P covariance propagation + dense Hessians cap validation at a few thousand parameters; realistic architectures untested.
  • Narrow validation scope: a 2-2-2 linear net, 200 warm-start steps on binary MNIST (C(0) = 0 by construction — never full training from init), and a small synthetic probe.
  • EoS theory is a single-direction (top-eigenvector) projection; λ₀ is a mean-square, not almost-sure, threshold.
  • Plain constant-η SGD only — momentum, Adam, and LR schedules break the δt = η identification and are deferred.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Can a low-rank SLF (top-k Hessian eigenmodes via Hessian-vector products) make Tr(C⟨H⟩) and the 2/η threshold cheap online observables for large training runs?
  2. How do momentum and Adam modify Dₜ and the η⟨H⟩C⟨H⟩ term — does the 2/η threshold generalize?
  3. Do heavy-tail breakdown regimes (Hill < 2) connect mechanistically to loss spikes in large-scale pretraining — usable as an early-warning signal?
  4. Where between the mean-square threshold λ₀ and the Lyapunov threshold do real trajectories settle, and how are additional curvature modes recruited to the edge?
  5. Can SLF quantitatively predict final-minimum sharpness (the long-standing η/B heuristic, hence generalization) beyond the tiny networks tested?
