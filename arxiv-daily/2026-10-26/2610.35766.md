> **一句话总结：** 在一个因子已知的合成量能器图像生成器上训练 LeJEPA 时，编码器会突然丢失已学到的大部分因子可读性（10 个种子中 9 个如此），而训练损失持续下降、RankMe 持续上升——所追踪的 11 个免标签监测信号中 8 个反而把"退化"的运行评为更好，最优的变点检测组合也只能在约三分之一的退化开始后 10 个 epoch 内报警，说明"指标在变好"并不代表"模型没丢东西"。

> **TL;DR:** On a synthetic calorimeter-image generator with three known generating factors (two shape coordinates z1/z2 and energy E), 9 of 10 LeJEPA ViT-Nano seeds abruptly lost factor readability (ridge R² dropping from a median peak of 0.97 to 0.77) mid-training while the training loss kept falling and RankMe kept rising. Of 11 label-free monitoring signals (loss, SIGReg, feature std, LiDAR, RankMe, α-ReQ, NE-Sum, κ, Self-Cluster, DSE, TwoNN), 8 rated the declining runs BETTER than non-declining runs at every post-onset checkpoint (only α-ReQ disagreed), and even the best post-hoc signal+change-point pairing (CUSUM/EWMA/Page–Hinkley) alarmed within 10 epochs of onset in only 9 of 26 declines (~35%) — checkpoint selection by loss or RankMe always landed at/after the onset, costing a median 0.21 readability, no better than taking the last checkpoint. An MAE control on the same images never declined, and partially refreshing shape between the two views (ρ=0.9) eliminated the decline (0/6 runs, even at 1000 epochs).

## 核心贡献

- 构造了一个"已知答案"的测试平台：用基于 PyCaloFlash 的合成量能器图像生成器（三个已知生成因子：形状坐标 z1、z2 与能量 E），可以在每个 checkpoint 上直接测量编码器保留了多少因子信息（可读性 = 三个因子的留出集 ridge R² 平均），从而首次检验免标签监测信号能否发现**单次训练运行内部**表征的退化。
- 发现并表征了一种新的失败模式：LeJEPA 编码器先学会因子、然后突然丢失其中一部分可读性（10 个默认种子中 9 个），且 56 次退化中的 55 次都沿着因子空间中几乎相同的方向（与参考方向 (0.513, −0.645, 0.567) 的最小余弦 0.996）；CCA 显示三个典型相关中的前两个保持 >0.98，只有第三个跌落——即编码器恰好只丢了一个因子组合。
- 系统性证伪了免标签监测信号的可靠性：损失、RankMe、LiDAR 等 11 个信号中 8 个在退化后仍朝"偏好方向"移动并把退化运行评为更好；CUSUM/EWMA/Page–Hinkley 变点检测最多在 9/26 的退化中及时报警；按这些信号选 checkpoint 基本等同于直接取最后一个 checkpoint（中位数代价 0.21 可读性）。
- 给出机制线索与修复方向：两视图间部分刷新形状（ρ=0.9，视图因子相关而非相同）使 0/6 个运行退化（即使训练 1000 epoch）；MAE 在同一数据上 0/10 退化——退化与"两视图完全相同"的缩减版 LeJEPA 配方强相关。
- 诚实报告了一个"因祸得福"的发现：误用重采样形状的分组计算 LiDAR 能及时标记 23–25/26 次退化（≤1 次误报），但该方法需要真实训练中不具备的因子知识，且作为质量分数反而偏好退化后的 checkpoint——作者明确指出其不可用。

## Key Contributions

- A **known-answer testbed** for label-free monitoring: images from a PyCaloFlash-based calorimeter simulator with three known generating factors (shape coordinates z1, z2 and energy E) let the author measure, at every checkpoint, exactly how much of each factor a frozen encoder still encodes (readability = average held-out ridge R²) — something impossible on natural data — and thus test whether label-free signals notice within-run forgetting.
- Identification and characterization of a **novel failure mode**: reduced two-view LeJEPA encoders (ViT-Nano) learn the factors and then abruptly lose part of that readability (9 of 10 default seeds), with 55 of 56 declines losing readability along essentially the same direction in factor space (reference direction (0.513, −0.645, 0.567), minimum |cosine| 0.996); CCA shows the first two canonical correlations stay above 0.98 while only the third falls — each encoder loses exactly one combination of the factors and keeps the others readable.
- A systematic **negative result for label-free monitoring**: of 11 tracked signals (training loss, SIGReg, feature std, LiDAR, plus the seven metrics of Arputharaj et al. 2026: α-ReQ, RankMe, NE-Sum, κ, Self-Cluster, DSE, TwoNN), 8 rated declining runs better than non-declining ones at every post-onset checkpoint; standard change-point detection (CUSUM, EWMA, Page–Hinkley, thresholds set on control runs) alarmed within 10 epochs of onset in at best 9 of 26 declines; checkpoint selection by loss, RankMe, or LiDAR picked post-onset checkpoints in (nearly) all decline runs at a median readability cost of 0.21 — the same as taking the final checkpoint.
- **Mechanistic and constructive findings**: partially refreshing shape between the two training views (ρ=0.9) removes the decline entirely (0/6 runs decline, even at 1000 epochs, vs 9/10 default at 300); a masked autoencoder (MAE) on the same images never declines (0/10); the decline survives no-weight-decay, constant LR, higher SIGReg weight, and ViT-Tiny — tying the failure to identical-view joint-embedding training rather than optimizer settings.
- An honestly-reported **accidental discovery**: a LiDAR variant computed with groups that redraw shape flags 23–25 of 26 declines in time with at most 1 false alarm — but requires knowing which factors are shape vs energy (unavailable in real runs), and because it rises during the decline it still prefers post-decline checkpoints as a quality score, so it cannot serve as a monitor.

## Methodology

**Generator (known ground truth).** A single-particle configuration of the simulator of Kakati (2026), built on PyCaloFlash v1.0, models a particle shower in a layered calorimeter: 4 channels (detector layers at increasing depth) of 64×64 pixels. Three generating factors — two shape coordinates (z1, z2) controlling how intensity spreads across depth, and energy E scaling intensity — are drawn per image; a parameter µ interpolates each shape coordinate's distribution from standard normal (µ=0) to two well-separated Gaussians (µ≈1). Each channel draws N random "elements" from the intensity profile, controlling graininess (N=30,100,300,1000). Crucially, instead of augmentations, the two training views are independent renders with the SAME shape and energy, differing only in the sampled elements.

**Training.** ViT-Nano (0.7M params, patch 4, dim 96, 6 blocks) with a projector (width 384 default, 2048 wide) trained with a reduced LeJEPA recipe (Balestriero & LeCun 2025): 2 whole-image views instead of 8, λ=0.02 instead of the recommended 0.05, 256 SIGReg projections, integration range [−3,3] — following the official minimal example. AdamW, batch 128, 64k fresh examples per epoch, 300 epochs default, checkpoints every 2–10 epochs. Controls: MAE (identical encoder/optimizer, 75% masking), ViT-Tiny, and a SimCLR loss variant (which never moved from initialization on this generator).

**Measurements.** Readability = average held-out ridge R² for z1, z2, log E from layer-normalized, position-averaged final-layer tokens (4k train / 2k eval images, reused across checkpoints). Robustness: pooled ridge, an 85k-param attention probe, an MLP, token-level and dual ridges, and a separately generated test split. Effective rank via RankMe (also mean-subtracted, on squared singular values, and on projector outputs). A run "declines" if readability falls ≥0.07 below its peak (from epoch 20); onset = first checkpoint after the peak where readability has fallen by a fifth of the drop; the onset rule was fixed after inspecting early runs and applied unchanged to 16 later runs (147 runs total, 56 declines).

**Signal audit.** Eleven label-free signals (loss, SIGReg, feature std, LiDAR, and seven literature metrics in 21 variants) are tracked around onsets; decline runs are aligned at onset and compared with non-declining runs from the same settings. Three change-point methods (CUSUM, EWMA, Page–Hinkley) run on every signal with thresholds calibrated on control runs from a different shape distribution (µ=0.97); an alarm is "timely" if within 10 epochs of onset. Checkpoint-choice cost = readability lost vs the hindsight-best candidate. Direction analysis uses the leading generalized eigenvector of residual second-moment matrices (pre-onset vs final), plus CCA between features and factors, and a cross-view SNR comparison against 2,000 random directions.

## Results & Highlights

- **The decline is real and frequent but invisible to the loss:** on the synthetic calorimeter generator, 9 of 10 default LeJEPA seeds (ViT-Nano, 300 epochs, N=100, µ=0) dropped readability by ≥0.07 from peak and never recovered, while readability peaked at a median 0.97 and ended at 0.77; over the same epochs the training loss kept falling (fitted trend decreasing in all 28 post-onset windows) and RankMe rose in all 13 audited decline runs (11/13 after mean subtraction).
- **Most signals rated the broken runs as better:** 8 of the 11 label-free signals rated the declining runs better than the non-declining runs at every checkpoint after onset (e.g., RankMe higher, LiDAR higher, TwoNN lower); only α-ReQ consistently rated them worse (further from 1), with the loss and κ mixed. By most signals, "the runs that lost readability looked like the better runs."
- **Change-point detection almost never alarms in time:** across signals, variants, methods, and both threshold procedures, the best timely-alarm count per metric was 9 of 26 declines (TwoNN; uncentred α-ReQ; SIGReg·EWMA), 6 for RankMe, 4 for the training loss, 3 for LiDAR — and TwoNN at that operating point also falsely alarmed in 6–7 of the 13 runs without a decline.
- **Checkpoint selection by these signals is no better than taking the last checkpoint:** loss minimization and RankMe picked a checkpoint at or after the onset in every decline run (LiDAR in all but one), each costing a median 0.21 in readability — identical to the final-checkpoint baseline; per-factor median costs for the loss were 0.15 (z1), 0.29 (z2), 0.20 (log E).
- **The decline is sharply directional:** 55 of 56 declines lost readability along nearly the same factor-space direction (minimum |cosine| 0.996 vs the reference (0.513, −0.645, 0.567)); directional R² fell by at least 0.302 in every decline vs at most 0.107 in every non-declining run, and CCA's first two correlations stayed above 0.98 while only the third fell.
- **Two interventions eliminate it:** partially refreshing shape between views (ρ=0.9) → 0/6 runs decline even at 1000 epochs (vs 9/10 default at 300 epochs); MAE on identical images → 0/10 runs decline, all ended at their peak. The decline otherwise survives no weight decay (1/3), constant LR (2/3), SIGReg weight 0.05 (3/10), and ViT-Tiny (1/2).
- **The accidental LiDAR variant works but is unusable:** with groups that redraw shape, LiDAR·CUSUM/EWMA under the control-referenced check flagged 23–25 of 26 declines in time with at most 1 false alarm among 13 non-declining runs (stable across δ=10⁻⁴/10⁻⁶/10⁻⁸) — but building such groups requires knowing which factors are shape vs energy, information a real training run does not have, and as a quality score it chose post-onset checkpoints in all 28 decline runs anyway.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuinely novel framing. Prior validation of label-free metrics (RankMe, LiDAR, Arputharaj et al. 2026) compares ACROSS trained models; this is the first test of whether such signals detect forgetting WITHIN a single run, using ground truth that natural data cannot provide. The within-run checkpoint-selection result is the practical counterpart of Locatello et al. (2019)'s identifiability negative result.
- **Rigor:** High. Multiple probe families (attention probe, MLP, token-level ridges) plus an independent test split guard the readability measurement; RankMe is recomputed mean-subtracted and on squared singular values; the onset rule was fixed once and applied unchanged to 16 later runs; 147 runs total. The negative alarm result is carefully scoped (three methods, control-calibrated thresholds, "does not show that the decline cannot be detected").
- **External validity — the main weakness:** everything rests on ONE synthetic physics generator, a 0.7M-parameter ViT-Nano, and a REDUCED two-view LeJEPA recipe (2 views, λ=0.02) rather than the published eight-view method at scale. The paper's own theory discussion undercuts generality: Klindt et al. (2026)'s LeJEPA guarantee covers related-but-not-identical views, and with identical shared views the objective no longer favors recovering the factors — consistent with the finding that partial refresh (ρ=0.9) eliminates the decline. So this may be a pathology specific to the author's minimal recipe, not LeJEPA or SSL generally; the author admits natural images and the eight-view recipe are untested.
- **Missing comparisons:** SimCLR never left its initialization on this generator (so contrastive training is untested, and the LR change did not help); MAE is the only other objective that learned. Optimizer ablations use only 3 seeds each (author concedes no effect claims). Only ridge-style/attention/MLP readouts were tried — the decline shows factors became harder to READ, not that information is destroyed (author says so explicitly).
- **Red flags / honesty:** The strongest "detector" (LiDAR with shape-redraw groups) arose from the author's own evaluation mistake, is transparently declared unusable, and its mechanism is properly analyzed (the leading LiDAR eigenvalue tracks energy, whose fall evens the spectrum) — good epistemic hygiene rather than a buried caveat. One reference-check blemish: their α-ReQ implementation read 0.559 for a true power-law exponent of 0.5, outside its 0.05 tolerance. No quantitative result is overclaimed beyond the synthetic setting.
- **Reproducibility:** Excellent for the synthetic setting (code + checkpoints + per-figure CSVs), though the generator being the author's own prior work means few independent eyes have vetted it, and 3–7 GPU-hour runs on RTX A5000/A6000 are cheap to replicate.

## Strengths

- Converts an unanswerable question ("did my SSL run silently forget something?") into a measurable one via known generating factors — a clean known-answer test of monitoring, not just another metric benchmark.
- The negative result is decisive and quantified across 11 signals × 21 variants × 3 change-point methods, with both false-negative (9/26 best timely alarms) and false-positive (e.g., TwoNN alarming on 6–7/13 healthy runs) sides reported.
- The directional analysis (one shared factor combination lost, CCA correlations >0.98 preserved for the rest) is a sharp mechanistic characterization, not just a curve that went down.
- Constructive takeaway with practical value: partial decorrelation of views (ρ=0.9 shape refresh) prevents the failure at zero label cost, and α-ReQ is identified as the only monitored signal that moved the right way.
- Unusually complete artifact release (code, per-figure data, checkpoints) and a transparent LLM-usage statement.

## Limitations

- Single generator, single (tiny) encoder scale, one reduced LeJEPA recipe: no evidence the decline occurs on natural images, at scale, or with the published eight-view recipe; SimCLR/contrastive training untestable on this generator (never learned).
- "Readability" is probe-relative: the paper shows factors became harder for ridge/attention/MLP readouts to recover, not that the information is gone from the features; an untried readout might recover it.
- Only three classical change-point methods with thresholds calibrated on out-of-distribution controls (µ=0.97 runs) and no false-alarm guarantees; the detection question remains open.
- Optimizer/architecture ablations run only 2–3 seeds, so "weight decay/constant LR did not remove the decline" is weakly powered.
- Non-declining comparison runs are group-imbalanced (more µ=0 runs among decliners), which the author flags; some SIGReg verdicts depend on the arbitrary alignment epoch chosen for non-declining runs.

## Relevance

直接命中"训练稳定性与可观测性"兴趣：这是对"训练损失和免标签指标都在变好时，模型是否可能在悄悄丢失已学到的表征"这一训练监控核心问题的首个可控实验回答——对 LLM 预训练等长周期无标签运行中"静默退化"的检测与 checkpoint 选择策略有直接警示意义。虽然实验对象是小型视觉自监督模型和合成数据，但其结论（持续改善的信号不能证明表征没有退化）对任何依赖 loss 曲线和免标签指标做 checkpoint 选择的大规模训练运行都适用。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the readability decline appear with the published eight-view LeJEPA recipe, larger encoders, or natural images (e.g., YFCC/ImageNet subsets where factors are only partially known)?
- Is the lost factor combination actually destroyed, or re-encoded in a form that stronger (nonlinear, sparse-autoencoder-style) probes could recover — i.e., is "readability" the right notion of forgetting?
- Since the only signal that consistently moved the right way (α-ReQ) and the only timely detector (LiDAR with shape-redraw groups) each fail in the other role, can a cheap hybrid — e.g., α-ReQ for ranking plus occasional small labeled probes — give a practical within-run monitor?
- The common decline direction was already the hardest-to-read direction for an untrained encoder and had the lowest cross-view SNR: is this a delayed form of feature suppression/shortcut learning, and could it be predicted from epoch-0 statistics?
- For LLM pretraining analogues: would change-point detection on hidden-state effective-rank or α-ReQ-style spectra catch similarly quiet, single-direction representation losses that loss curves mask?
