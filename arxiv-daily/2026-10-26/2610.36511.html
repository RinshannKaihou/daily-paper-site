01 Methodology CLAIMNOV 五段流水线

Extract→ Retrieve→ Entail→ Aggregate→ Verdict
1
Claim extraction

gpt-4o-mini (T=0) turns each proposal into 1–4 typed contribution claims — dimension ∈ {Problem, Methodology, Resource, Result} plus (attribute, value) pairs; avg 2.4–2.7 claims/proposal. Same pass over every pool abstract.

2
Same-dimension retrieval

text-embedding-3-large pulls k nearest pool claims of the same dimension, ≤1 per paper; k = 0.2·pool size, capped at 40 (≈35 for 400-paper anchor pools).

3
Pairwise entailment

gpt-4o-mini rates every (proposal claim, pool claim) on an ordinal 1–5 scale, mapped to e ∈ [0,1] by (ℓ−1)/4.

4
Rank-discounted aggregation

Per-claim mean with α = 0.5 (emphasizing strongest match), combined via hand-set dimension weights: Problem .4 › Result .3 › Methodology .2 › Resource .1.

5
Stronger-model verdict

Sonnet 4.6 reads the full claim profile → pass/fail + the offending prior paper, entailed claim subset, and a 1–2 sentence reason. Interpretable by construction.

Temporal ground truth: cutoff T = Mar 2026; all judge models predate T (mitigates contamination). All systems' searches sandboxed and replaced by identical dense retrieval over the same pools. Benchmark = 380 four-rung disguises of ICML 2026 spotlights (≤2% six-gram overlap) + 400 later-realized ideas + 160 mined ICLR reviewer disputes + 100 spliced hybrids.

Setting: n = 380 disguises + 400 later-realized ideas over curated 20–400-paper pools. † run under the audit's chosen decision rules (best-in-sample thresholds in Appendix K). “—” = not reported in the summary. False alarms measured on ideas humans later published.

  • The claim profile is what keeps false alarms low: removing it (judge reads only top-8 abstracts) raises FA 1.5% → 11.8%; a per-claim hard rule (“fail if any central claim entailed”) catches all 380 but explodes FA to 56.8% — a proposal must not be treated as one big claim.
  • Claim-level mercy: rejects only 3% of ICLR-accepted disputed proposals (CoI-Agent 10%, ResearchAgent 47%, Si et al. 81%) — one disputed claim shouldn't kill a proposal.
  • Spliced hybrids (n=100): keeps 91 with the true prior art named vs 76 for CoI-Agent (p = .004); reliability check — 13/15 named papers correct where the spliced claim reached entailment ≥ 4.
  • Attribution caveat: names the reviewer's prior work B for 36% vs 31% for CoI-Agent (p = .016) — but plain dense embedding alone finds B top-1 for 37%, beating CLAIMNOV's own claim-centric ranking (21%).

03 Strengths 长处

  • Quantifies a real, undocumented failure mode: novelty checks burning the pipeline's budget of novel ideas by rejecting what the field later publishes (Si et al. 96% FA, ResearchAgent 98.5%).
  • Claim is the right unit — matches how incremental science works — and outputs an auditable profile (which claim, which prior, what entailment level) instead of an opaque score.
  • Excellent evaluation hygiene: temporal cutoffs, sandboxed identical inputs across heterogeneous systems, paired McNemar tests, cluster bootstrap, human annotation with reported κ.
  • Practically cheap: $0.010/verdict — deployable as a filter or even a training reward for idea-generation agents.

04 Limitations 短板

  • Fixed 20–400-paper pools, not open search — retrieval quality, arguably the hardest part of real novelty checking, is largely untested.
  • Synthetic adversarial rewrites of a single source with ~19 distractors; real plagiarism/reinvention is messier and often spans papers outside any top-19 neighborhood.
  • Contamination risk on disputes: claim-disputed papers predate T and may sit in judges' training data, weakening the temporal argument for that dataset.
  • Hand-set weights (dimension prior, α = 0.5) validated only implicitly; the benchmark decays as post-T judges ship.
  • No live loop evaluation — never tested inside a generate-reject-regenerate cycle, where it would actually deploy (only a cost pilot in Appendix M).

05 Questions for further exploration 待深究
