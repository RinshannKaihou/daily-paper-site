Why this matters

Steering is a go-to inference-time control for LLM behavior. Its hidden side effects are systematic, invisible to perplexity — and repairable.

原文 · Relevance 这篇论文直接触及激活转向/表征工程这一推理时行为控制方向的核心痛点——副作用，并给出了细粒度的话语级度量与可操作的修复方法，对关注 LLM 可解释性、可控生成与模型安全对齐的研究非常有参考价值。

⚠ Critical edge — read before citing

Correction fires only on samples an oracle already flags as flipped — the detection step real deployment needs does not exist, so 78% is an upper bound.

And the LLM judge that defines the correction vectors also scores their success; the 484-sample human check covers only the successes.

01 Method

CAA steering → dimension audit → vector repair

  1. Steer (CAA)

    Behavior vector vb = mean residual-activation difference (positive vs. negative answers) at the best-steerable layer; injected at inference with multiplier α (model-specific ±0.5 … ±4).

  2. Audit what breaks

    LLM judge open-nominates differences on 288 steered/unsteered pairs → 1,028 candidates → human merge to 26 → 0/1/2 scoring → human-agreement filter → 15 discourse dimensions (κ = 0.48 / PABAK = 0.90).

  3. Repair with a second vector

    Per-dimension vector vd from contrastive (score-0 vs. score-2) activations, injected alongside vb at strength β = k|α| — three variants: same-layer, separate-layer, orthogonal projection.

Evidence base · 3 models (Llama-3.1-8B-Inst · Qwen3-8B · Gemma-2-9B-IT) × 11 MWE datasets × 15 dims · 8,284 datapoints · Wilcoxon + BH-FDR · ~500 GPU-h · judge: DeepSeek-V4-Flash

Why steering leaks: behavior & discourse vectors are near-orthogonal at the mid layers where interventions act, but their cosine similarity grows toward output layers — entangling discourse with behavior.

02 Results

Beyond the headline: what shifts, what recovers, what survives

86% (424/495) of FDR-corrected Wilcoxon tests show a significant discourse shift under steering — while perplexity sees almost none of it (max τ = 0.38).

03 Strengths

What the paper gets right

  • First dimension-level audit of steering side effects — 15 human-validated discourse dimensions replace the single perplexity number.
  • Careful experimental matrix: 3 models × 11 datasets × 15 dimensions × 8 multipliers, FDR-corrected tests and effect sizes, not anecdote.
  • Conceptually clean intervention with three variants, plus an overshoot analysis justifying smallest-k selection.
  • Behavior preservation measured three ways — logit sign, steerability ratio, human defect labeling.
  • Honest limitations section that itself names the oracle-selection problem.

04 Limitations

Where the claims thin out

  • Needs to know where to correct — no detector for flipped samples is provided; deployment requires a step the paper leaves open.
  • The fix is itself steering, inheriting oversteering sensitivity; correction strength k is tuned per sample in the evaluation.
  • One dimension at a time — joint restoration of multiple shifted dimensions is untested.
  • Narrow scope: CAA only, 8–9B instruction-tuned models, binary-choice MWE prompts; transfer unverified.
  • Judge-dependent scores with moderate human agreement (κ = 0.48 on a 0–2 scale); some dimensions correlate with length (τ ≈ 0.6).

05 Questions for further exploration

  • Can a cheap online detector replace the oracle 0↔2 flip selection — and how far does the 78% drop?
  • Do the findings transfer to other steering families (ACT Add, SAE features, conditional) and to larger models?
  • Can multiple discourse vectors be restored jointly without interference, given late-layer entanglement?
  • Would human or classical-NLP scoring change the headline 78% / 92.8% (judge circularity)?
  • How much of the “discourse shift” survives controlling for output length (τ up to 0.6)?
  • Could discourse vectors be used deliberately — adding reasoning depth or evidence — while preserving factuality?
