> **一句话总结：** EnergyCal 在 Muon/MuonH 优化器中，用每步 Newton–Schulz 方向的归一化能量对路由专家更新做单侧衰减（q = clip[(ρ/ρ_ref)^p, 0, 1]，无需新增优化器状态），在自建 4B DeepSeekMoE 风格模型上把阈值化损失尖峰负担降低 99.51%（MuonH）与平均 95.98%（plain Muon），同时最终验证困惑度改善 4.06% / ~0.4%，吞吐仅损失 1.62%。

> **TL;DR:** The author trains a custom 4B-parameter DeepSeekMoE-style model (128 routed experts, top-2 routing, 1.31B tokens on FineWeb sample-10BT, 8xH100) and shows that scaling each routed-expert Muon update by a per-matrix coefficient derived from the post-Newton–Schulz direction energy (q = clip[(‖D‖²_F/d / 0.8)^p, 0, 1], p ∈ {0.5, 1}) cuts mean thresholded loss-spike burden by 99.51% vs a development-selected MuonH baseline (B1: 10.11 vs 2046.01) and final geometric-mean perplexity by 4.06% (25.394 vs 26.476 PPL, CE 3.2345 vs 3.2760), at 1.62% lower throughput (65,977 vs 67,061 tokens/s); at matched LR/weight-decay on plain Muon the burden drops 95.98% on average (B1: 1.67–3.00 vs 84.79) with small endpoint gains (−0.0038 to −0.0042 nats/token). Caveats the author states plainly: only 3 seeds, 1.31B-token budget, and no uniform-scaling control, so the benefit of *energy-dependent* assignment vs simply shrinking routed updates is unresolved; single-update interventions failed to rescue two examined spikes.

## 核心贡献

- **提出 EnergyCal**：一个极轻量的规则——对每个路由专家矩阵，按其有限步 Newton–Schulz（NS）方向的归一化平方 Frobenius 范数 ρ_t = ‖D_t‖²_F/d 计算系数 q_t = clip[(ρ_t/ρ_ref)^p, q_min, 1]，只对低于参考能量 ρ_ref = 0.8 的方向做单侧衰减。NS 变换、动量、路由器目标全部不变，不使用 token 利用率统计，也不引入任何持久化优化器状态。
- **两套完整实验（Cohort H 与 Cohort M，各 3 个初始化种子）验证稳定性**：在 MuonH 上平均阈值化损失尖峰负担 B1 从 2046.01 降到 10.11（−99.51%），尖峰事件数从 37.33 降到 3.33，最大训练损失从 39.99 降到 5.92；在匹配名义学习率与权重衰减的 plain Muon 上负担平均降低 95.98%（两个指数分别为 94.99% 与 96.97%）。
- **最终质量同时小幅改善**：Cohort H 中 EnergyCal–MuonH 最终验证 CE 从 3.2760±0.0270 降至 3.2345±0.0011（几何平均困惑度 −4.06%）；Cohort M 中相对 plain Muon 配对改善 −0.0042 / −0.0038 nats/token。峰值显存不变，吞吐仅降 1.62%。
- **机理层面的诚实诊断**：几何上证明了 MuonH 试更新的算子范数 ∝ 1/√sr(D)，说明低能量（低稳定秩/低幅度）对应更新谱更集中；但单更新干预实验（冻结候选专家、全体专家施加 EnergyCal 共 14 个分支）**无法挽救**两个已考察的尖峰事件，表明连续校准的收益并非来自一步式移除尖峰。
- **明确划定证据边界**：作者主动指出 EnergyCal 的能量不是尺度不变的稳定秩（同时依赖幅度）、缺少"仅路由矩阵均匀缩放"对照（无法区分能量依赖衰减与单纯减小更新幅度）、未与 Excitation/Compass 等专家级缩放方法做正面比较。

## Key Contributions

- **EnergyCal rule**: attenuate each routed-expert update by q_t = clip[(ρ_t/ρ_ref)^p, q_min, 1], where ρ_t = ‖D_t‖²_F/d is the normalized squared Frobenius norm of the finite-step Newton–Schulz direction (ρ_ref = 0.8, q_min = 0, p ∈ {0.5, 1}). The spectral transform, momentum, router objective, attention/shared-expert updates, and Adam fallback are untouched; no persistent optimizer state is added (MuonH even reuses its existing direction norm).
- **Full-schedule stabilization evidence in two cohorts** (3 init seeds each, 10k updates × 131k tokens = 1.31B tokens on a custom 4B DeepSeekMoE-style model): vs a development-selected lower-LR MuonH recipe, mean thresholded spike burden B1 falls 99.51% (2046.01 → 10.11), episodes 37.33 → 3.33, and max post-warmup training loss 39.99 → 5.92; on plain Muon at matched LR 0.005 and WD 0.1, burden falls 95.98% on average across the two exponents (84.79 → 1.67/3.00), all paired runs improving.
- **Endpoint quality improvements alongside stability**: final validation CE 3.2760±0.0270 → 3.2345±0.0011 (paired −0.0415±0.0260 nats/token = −4.06% geometric-mean PPL vs MuonH; −0.0065±0.0057 = −0.65% vs Muon+WD), with peak memory unchanged (39.7224 GiB) and throughput down only 1.62% vs MuonH (65,976.8 vs 67,061.4 tokens/s).
- **Geometry + honest causal diagnostics**: derives ‖Δ^trial‖_2 = ηRq/√sr(D) for the MuonH trial update (spectral concentration grows as stable rank shrinks), and shows ρ(D) = sr(D)‖D‖²_2/d is NOT scale-invariant stable rank — an expert receiving zero current tokens can still carry momentum with ‖D‖_F = 0.9921 and sr = 1.0409. Single-update interventions (7 branches × 2 spike events, including exact candidate freezing that cuts stored operator displacement by 86.3%/76.3%) fail to prevent the subsequent loss rise (next-step CE stays ≈6.3/6.9), so the full-schedule benefit is not one-step spike removal.
- **Explicit evidence boundaries**: no routed-only uniform-scaling control (cannot separate energy-dependent attenuation from generic update-magnitude reduction); no head-to-head vs Excitation/Compass/NorMuon/TrasMuon; different validation banks across cohorts block cross-family ranking; 3 seeds only, descriptive SDs.

## Methodology

The author trains a from-scratch 4B-parameter DeepSeekMoE-style sparse MoE: 24 layers, hidden width 1,536, 128 routed experts of width 256 with top-2 routing, one shared expert per layer, tied embeddings, plus a traffic-driven router-bias controller (auxiliary-loss-free balancing). Setup: 8×H100 80GB, 2,048-token sequences, 131,072 tokens per optimizer update, 10,000 updates (1.31B tokens), 500 warmup + cosine decay to 10% peak, gradient clipping at norm 1, five quintic NS iterations in BF16 with FP32 momentum/norms, FineWeb sample-10BT with a deterministic 0.5% validation split.

**EnergyCal** applies after NS: for each routed gate/up/down logical matrix (d = min(m,n) = 256), compute ρ_t = ‖D_t‖²_F/d and the one-sided coefficient q_t = clip[(ρ_t/0.8)^p, 0, 1]; directions at or above the reference energy are untouched. For MuonH this replaces η with q_t·η only for routed matrices; for plain Muon it scales the NS term of the decoupled-WD update. The primary recipe (p = 1) was frozen during development and not reselected on confirmation seeds.

**Study design**: (1) Cohort H — frozen EnergyCal–MuonH (LR 0.01) vs development-selected MuonH (LR 0.005) and Muon+WD, 3 confirmation seeds, paired by initialization; baseline LRs were picked by mean 3k-update screening-endpoint CE over 2 runs (both landed at the lower boundary of their grids). (2) Cohort M — plain Muon ± EnergyCal (p ∈ {0.5, 1}) at matched LR 0.005 / WD 0.1, 3 new seeds, different validation bank and implementation revision (so no cross-cohort pooling). Spike detection is a fixed adaptive threshold τ_t = max{m_t + 0.5, 1.2m_t, m_t + 8σ_t} over a rolling 101-loss median window (t > 500), flags merged into episodes within 16 steps; burden B1 = Σ(L_t − m_t) over flagged updates. Throughput uses 3 execution repeats × 1,000 updates. Diagnostics include a 6-condition single-init coefficient audit (energy/stable-rank/amplitude-only/near-zero-gate) and 7-branch single-update interventions on two saved spike events with exact weight-freezing controls.

## Results & Highlights

- **Cohort H, spike burden (MuonH baseline, n = 3 seeds)**: mean B1 drops from 2046.01 ± 1289.75 (MuonH, LR 0.005) to 10.11 ± 9.65 (EnergyCal–MuonH, LR 0.01), a 99.51% reduction in condition-mean ratio; episodes drop from 37.33 ± 4.04 to 3.33 ± 2.89; max post-warmup training loss from 39.99 ± 12.64 to 5.92 ± 1.07. No EnergyCal run triggers the detector between steps 4,001 and 10,000.
- **Cohort H, endpoint quality**: final validation CE improves from 3.2760 ± 0.0270 (MuonH) and 3.2410 ± 0.0060 (Muon+WD) to 3.2345 ± 0.0011 (EnergyCal) — paired −0.0415 ± 0.0260 nats vs MuonH (−4.06% geometric-mean PPL, 26.476 → 25.394) and −0.0065 ± 0.0057 vs Muon+WD (−0.65% PPL, 25.560 → 25.394); all 3 paired endpoints favor EnergyCal vs MuonH.
- **Cohort M (plain Muon, matched LR/WD, n = 3 new seeds)**: mean B1 falls from 84.79 ± 33.13 (Muon+WD) to 3.00 ± 2.00 (p = 0.5, −94.99%) and 1.67 ± 1.15 (p = 1, −96.97%), equal-weight average 95.98%, with burden reduced in all six paired runs; paired endpoint CE differences are −0.0042 ± 0.0042 (p = 0.5) and −0.0038 ± 0.0028 (p = 1) nats/token (−0.423% / −0.380% geometric-mean PPL; final CE 3.189786 → 3.185547 / 3.185975 on that bank).
- **Cost**: throughput 65,976.8 ± 353.3 tokens/s (EnergyCal) vs 67,061.4 ± 570.4 (MuonH, −1.62%) and 68,458.9 ± 487.7 (Muon+WD, −3.63%); peak allocated memory identical at 39.7224 GiB; mean applied coefficients are 0.9679 ± 0.0021 (p = 0.5) and 0.9598 ± 0.0017 (p = 1), i.e., an average attenuation of only ~3–4%.
- **Intermediate quality reverses the ordering**: at step 3,000 mean CE is 3.7995 (EnergyCal) vs 3.7437 (Muon+WD) and 3.6038 vs 3.5479 at step 5,000; the mean ordering flips only between the 8,000- and 8,500-step evaluations, and normalized trapezoidal CE over steps 500–10,000 still favors plain Muon (3.6111 ± 0.0094 vs 3.6270 ± 0.0016) — EnergyCal's advantage emerges late in the schedule.
- **Diagnostics**: in the single-init 3k-update audit at LR 0.01, EnergyCal (p = 1) has the lowest burden (B1 = 10.59 vs 430.51 for MuonH) while a stable-rank coefficient achieves the lowest 3k CE (3.761237); amplitude-only scaling ends during a loss excursion (CE 9.318367). Single-update interventions on events A/B (pre-CE 4.1928/4.2143) leave next-step CE at ≈6.32/6.91 in all 14 branches, despite reducing candidate stored displacement by 86.3%/76.3% — no one-step rescue.

## Authors & Credibility

Single author: Masato Fujitake of FA Research, Fast Accounting Co., Ltd. (an industry research lab, per the affiliation and contact email). The author also wrote the cited MESH paper on Sinkhorn-based MoE training (arXiv:2608.04407), indicating an ongoing, focused research program on MoE optimization. Credibility is unusually high for a single-author systems paper: hyperparameters frozen before confirmation seeds, paired-by-initialization comparisons, all endpoint definitions (final CE at fixed token budget) declared in advance, unfavorable results (intermediate-quality deficit vs plain Muon, one-of-three burden loss vs Muon+WD, failed interventions) reported rather than buried, and explicit non-claims (no significance inference with n = 3, no general optimizer superiority). The scope is modest — a custom 4B model and 1.31B tokens, far from production-scale pretraining — but the experimental hygiene (separate development/confirmation banks, zero-burden runs retained, throughput measured with 3 repeats in cyclic order) is careful throughout.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty — moderate, honestly scoped**: post-orthogonalization energy-aware scaling already exists (TrasMuon uses energy-based clipping; NorMuon does neuron-level normalization; Excitation uses utilization; ExpertMuon-Compass uses alignment), and the author explicitly declines head-to-head comparisons with all of them. The genuine novelty is narrower: a matrix-level, one-sided, *routed-expert-only* coefficient computed from the post-NS direction energy with zero extra optimizer state, evaluated on sparse MoE with and without Hyperball.
- **Rigor — strong on reporting, weak on causal isolation**: the headline 99.51% compares recipes that differ in learning rate (0.01 vs 0.005), so it does not isolate the coefficient's effect; the cleaner Cohort M (matched LR/WD) still lacks the routed-only uniform-scaling control the author himself flags — a fixed q ≈ 0.96 baseline would test whether energy-*dependent* assignment matters at all, especially given the measured mean coefficient is 0.96 (i.e., most of the attenuation is nearly uniform). Both baseline LRs sit at the lower boundary of their search grids, hinting the grids may be mis-centered.
- **Reproducibility — good**: full recipe details (NS quintic coefficients, precision policy, partition of logical matrices, detector equations), frozen settings, and an implementation appendix are provided; but the model is custom and the code/data pipeline is not stated to be released, and cohorts use different implementation revisions that prevent cross-comparison.
- **Significance — real but bounded**: 1.31B tokens on a 4B model is ~3 orders of magnitude below frontier pretraining, and Muon-family spike pathology may look different at scale; n = 3 seeds with shared data order means the SDs are descriptive only. The late-emerging endpoint advantage (+4.06% vs MuonH, +0.65% vs Muon+WD) and the intermediate-quality deficit against plain Muon mean a practitioner valuing early-checkpoint quality or wall-clock time (plain Muon is 3.63% faster and wins the trapezoidal summary) might still prefer the baseline.
- **Red flags / honest negatives**: the two single-update intervention events could not be rescued, so the mechanism story is admittedly incomplete; the "burden" metric (sum above rolling median at flagged steps) can miss persistent elevation; different validation banks across cohorts block the most interesting question (calibrated MuonH vs calibrated Muon); the equal-time sensitivity check actually favors plain Muon (interpolated CE 3.2453 vs 3.2410).

## Strengths

- Dramatic and consistent stability gains — 99.51% / 95.98% burden reduction with burden improved in essentially all paired runs (all 6 in Cohort M; both endpoint and burden improve in all 3 Cohort H pairs vs MuonH) — for a rule that adds no optimizer state and costs 1.62% throughput.
- Works both with and without Hyperball normalization (Cohort H and Cohort M), and across two exponents, suggesting it is not an artifact of one optimizer formulation.
- Exceptional transparency: predefined endpoints, frozen recipes, paired analyses, retained unfavorable results (amplitude-only divergence, intervention failures), and explicit statement of what the evidence does not show.
- The diagnostics are genuinely informative — e.g., demonstrating that zero-token experts retain momentum with near-unit-amplitude NS directions is a concrete, quotetable observation about Muon-family MoE training dynamics.
- Complete evaluation suite: final CE, intermediate trapezoidal CE, late-window check, episodes, burden, max loss, throughput, memory, and coefficient telemetry reported separately instead of collapsed into one number.

## Limitations

- Small scale and budget: one custom 4B architecture, 1.31B tokens per run, 3 seeds, shared data order — no claims possible about other architectures, scales, or data distributions.
- No uniform-scaling control: cannot tell whether *energy-dependent* coefficient assignment beats simply shrinking routed updates by a constant factor (~0.96); the author flags this as unresolved.
- Recipe-level comparison in Cohort H (different LRs, and plain Muon differs in WD and formulation) does not isolate the coefficient; baseline LRs chosen at the lower boundary of bounded grids.
- No head-to-head against Excitation, ExpertMuon-Compass, NorMuon, TrasMuon, or other Muon variants; no positioning within that design space beyond prose.
- Mechanism unresolved: single-update interventions failed on the two examined spikes; the geometric identity covers only the MuonH trial update, not the projected/BF16-stored displacement or the loss change.
- Different validation banks and implementation revisions between cohorts prevent ranking calibrated MuonH against calibrated plain Muon; intermediate quality and estimated equal-time quality favor plain Muon.

## Relevance

该论文直接命中"Muon 系优化器 + 稀疏 MoE 训练稳定性"这一方向：它给出了一个零状态、可即插即用的路由专家更新衰减规则，并量化了损失尖峰负担与最终困惑度的权衡，对任何用 Muon/Hyperball 预训练 MoE 模型的实践都有直接参考价值。其诚实的证据边界（缺少均匀缩放对照、规模有限）也是评估同类"专家级缩放"方法时很好的方法论模板。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would a routed-only uniform-scaling control (fixed q ≈ 0.96, matched update-norm budget) reproduce most of the 95.98% burden reduction, or does energy-dependence genuinely matter?
- Do the stability gains persist at 10–100B+ token budgets and larger expert counts, where Muon spike statistics and routing dynamics may differ qualitatively?
- How does EnergyCal interact with utilization-based (Excitation) or alignment-based (Compass) expert scaling — complementary signals or redundant?
- Since spikes were not rescued by one-step attenuation, what accumulated effect explains the benefit: slower expert-norm growth, altered router-bias dynamics, or implicit effective-LR scheduling on rarely-visited experts?
- Can the energy statistic be disentangled from direction amplitude (e.g., by normalizing before measuring) to test whether spectral concentration or magnitude is the operative variable?
- Does EnergyCal change expert specialization / load-balancing outcomes at convergence, beyond loss-curve stability?
