> **一句话总结：** 在两份文档给出互相矛盾答案的问答场景（FaithEval 式冲突上下文）中，该文发现 Qwen3.5-0.8B 在生成前就已用残差流第13层的一个"源选择"方向悄悄锁定某一文档（held-out AUROC 0.9945），沿该方向做激活转向可把 doc2 命中率从 51.5% 推到 74.7%（δ=+3）或压到 32.2%（δ=−3），而"是否向用户披露冲突"则由更晚、更弱的第17层状态控制，双方向转向也只能把 109 个提示失败的披露率从 0% 提升到 42.2%——说明"内部选边"与"口头披露"是两个可分离的内部状态。

> **TL;DR:** Using the FaithEval-inconsistent inter-context conflict set (two supplied documents supporting incompatible answers), the authors show that in Qwen3.5-0.8B the hidden choice of source collapses into a single layer-13 residual direction (held-out AUROC 0.9945; order-swapped 0.993/0.997), and RMS-scaled steering of that direction moves doc2 usage from a 51.5% baseline to 74.7% at δ=+3 (vs. 51.0–52.3% for stat-matched random directions), with ~156 sparse middle-layer MLP writers feeding the same coordinate (write-projection AUROC 0.934 vs. 0.536 random). Conflict disclosure is a separate, weaker layer-17 state: even with an explicit disclosure prompt only 35.3% of 1,500 rows surface the conflict, and two-direction steering lifts prompt-matched failures from 0% to only 46/109 (42.2%) — a mechanism-level audit target for RAG systems whose "did the model tell the user?" question output text cannot answer.

## 核心贡献

- **"源选择"残差方向**：在冲突上下文问答中，模型对两份文档的内部取舍收敛为残差流中一个一维坐标（Qwen 第13层、决策 token 均值处），其符号追踪答案实际使用的文档而非文档位置或真值方向（原序/换序 AUROC 0.993/0.997）——即使提示要求披露冲突、答案却一边倒时，该坐标仍先于文本暴露了模型的"隐性站队"。
- **稀疏 MLP 写入桥接**：用 CETT 贡献归一化分数定位出约 156 个（Qwen，第 11–14 层为主、正负号均衡）稳定神经元，其实际 MLP 写入投影到同一源选择方向上（AUROC 0.934，随机层匹配对照仅 0.536），首次在"局部神经元—下游残差态"之间搭起组件级桥梁；Gemma 3 1B 用独立的 56 神经元集合复现该桥（0.974 vs 0.546）。
- **双向因果转向**：在第13层加入 ±δ·RMS·v̂ 可成对翻转答案来源（δ=+3 时 doc2 率 51.5%→74.7%，41 次正向翻转仅 1 次反向；δ=−3 时降至 32.2%），证明该坐标不只是被动痕迹而是因果参与源选择。
- **披露是另一个透明性状态**：冲突披露的读出更弱（AUROC 0.820/0.907）、出现在更晚的第17层且在生成开始之前；即使有明确披露指令，1500 条中仅 35.3% 广义披露、22.4% 严格不站队；双方向（第17层披露 + 第23层反塌缩）转向把 109 条提示失败样本的披露率从 0% 提到 42.2%，但 56/109 仍一边倒——"内部选边"与"告知用户"可分离且只能部分修复。
- **面向检索系统的监控策略**：提出"残差读出检测隐性源承诺 + 披露转向补救"的组合，并诚实讨论了该配方被反向用于压制披露的双用风险。

## Key Contributions

- **A residual source-choice direction.** In conflicting-context QA (two documents d1/d2 filling the same factual slot with incompatible answers), hidden source commitment collapses into a one-dimensional residual coordinate (Qwen layer 13, mean decision token). Its sign tracks which document the answer actually uses — not presentation order or the truthful side (original/swapped-order AUROC 0.993/0.997) — so the readout exposes silent side-picking even when the answer text is one-sided under a disclosure-asking prompt.
- **A sparse MLP write bridge.** CETT-based per-neuron contribution scoring localizes ~156 stable neurons in Qwen (mostly layers 11–14, sign-balanced) whose *actual* MLP writes project into the same source-choice direction (AUROC 0.934 vs. 0.536 mean for layer-matched random writer sets), giving a component-level bridge from localized neurons to the downstream residual state; Gemma 3 1B reproduces the bridge with an independently fitted 56-neuron set (0.974 vs. 0.546).
- **Bidirectional causal steering.** Adding δ·RMS(x)·v̂ at the layer-13 input flips the answered source: at δ=+3 the doc2 rate among decided rows rises from a 51.5% baseline to 74.7% (41 doc1→doc2 flips vs. 1 reverse); at δ=−3 it falls to 32.2% (35 doc2→doc1 flips vs. 1 reverse), while stat-matched random directions stay at 51.0–52.3%.
- **Disclosure is a separable, only partially steerable state.** Conflict-surfacing readouts are weaker (broad AUROC 0.820; strict unresolved 0.907), later (layer 17), and pre-response; even with an explicit disclosure instruction only 35.3% of 1,500 regenerated rows surface the conflict broadly and 22.4% strictly. On the 109 reproducible prompt-matched failures, two-direction steering (layer-17 disclosure δ=+10 + layer-23 anti-collapse δ=+12) recovers 46/109 broad surfacing, but 56/109 remain one-sided — source selection and user-facing disclosure are two internal states, not two labels on one computation.
- **A monitoring recipe for retrieval systems** — residual readouts to detect source commitment without disclosure, paired with disclosure steering — plus an honest dual-use analysis (the same contrastive recipe could in principle be inverted to suppress disclosure).

## Methodology

The task is inter-context conflict QA built from Salesforce/FaithEval-inconsistent-v1.0 (test split): each item pairs a question with two documents d1, d2 that answer the same slot with incompatible strings. Because the benchmark couples d1 with the original answer and d2 with the conflicting one, the paper analyzes *document choice* rather than truth, and runs document-order swaps plus a closed-book recall proxy (81.6% of 1,386 usable rows are parametric-unknown) to strip truth, position, and memory confounds. Source labels come from exact answer-alias matching; disclosure labels come from an LLM judge (Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite preview, temperature 0, JSON schema) that distinguishes plain_decided / conflict_aware_leaning / conflict_aware_unresolved / weak / truncated, with manual calibration audits.

Primary model: Qwen3.5-0.8B (pinned revision, 24-layer hybrid Gated DeltaNet/Gated Attention backbone, text-only path, T=0). Transfer check: google/gemma-3-1b-it. The readout direction is a class-centroid mean-difference vector (μ₊ − μ₋)/‖·‖ between doc2-choice and doc1-choice residual states — deliberately not a logistic probe, because the same vector is also used for steering. A layer/position sweep over prompt-final, response-onset, and decision-token aggregations selects layer 13 at mean-decision for Qwen and layer 12 at last-decision for Gemma. Steering adds δ·RMS(xₜ)·v̂ through a residual pre-hook over the last 32 prompt tokens plus the decision window (the layer-12 output is edited so layer 13 receives the steered residual). Sparse analysis adapts the CETT contribution score per neuron (|zⱼ|·‖W_down[:,j]‖ normalized by total MLP output norm), fits an L1 logistic classifier over five balanced localization splits, keeps sign-stable neurons, and then projects their actual upstream MLP writes onto the learned direction — a write-projection test rather than mere activation correlation. Conflict-specificity controls include exact-copy agreement (d1+d1), paraphrased agreement, and single-document conditions, plus closed-book-unknown subsets. Timing evidence uses a logit-lens token-margin readout and a bounded attention-head patching sweep (layer-11 head L11H3). Splits are qid-level, so no held-out row shares a qid with direction fitting.

## Results & Highlights

- **Source-choice readout is near ceiling.** On the FaithEval-inconsistent doc-choice pool, the Qwen layer-13 mean-decision direction reaches validation AUROC 0.9959 and held-out test AUROC 0.9945; under document-order swaps the AUROC is 0.993 (original) / 0.997 (swapped), and Gemma's independently selected layer-12 direction reaches clean held-out AUROC 0.980 on 907 LLM-audited rows (0.977/0.985 original/swapped).
- **Steering causally flips the source.** On 200 balanced held-out rows, baseline doc2 usage is 51.5%; positive steering at δ=+3 raises it to 74.7% among decided rows (41 doc1→doc2 flips, 1 reverse flip) and negative steering at δ=−3 lowers it to 32.2% (35 doc2→doc1 flips, 1 reverse), versus 51.0–52.3% for the stat-matched random direction — a ±20+ point swing in source choice.
- **Sparse MLP writes feed the same coordinate.** The summed writes of ~156 stable CETT neurons separate doc2-choice from doc1-choice rows at AUROC 0.934 (r = 0.755 with the dense layer-13 projection; n = 592 decided original/swap rows), while 100 layer-matched random writer sets average only 0.536 (median 0.515); in Gemma the 56-neuron write projection hits AUROC 0.974 (r = 0.794) vs. 0.546 for random controls.
- **Conflict specificity, not document count.** Paired sign tests on the audited matched subset give p = 1.5×10⁻⁴ for conflict vs. exact-agreement and p = 7.6×10⁻⁶ for conflict vs. single-document polarization; on the closed-book-unknown neither-aligned subset the gap strengthens (p = 9.0×10⁻⁶ and 1.0×10⁻⁷).
- **Disclosure is weak and only partially steerable.** With an explicit "describe the conflict first" instruction, the 1,500-row regenerated corpus yields only 35.3% broad surfacing and 22.4% strict unresolved surfacing; on the 109 prompt-matched reproducible failures (0% by construction), layer-17 steering gives 20/109 (18.3%) at δ=+6 and 29/109 (26.6%) at δ=+8, and the best two-direction arm reaches 46/109 (42.2%) — leaving 56/109 (51.4%) still one-sided. The layer-17 readout separates strict-unresolved from plain answers at AUROC 0.854 but leaning from plain at only 0.615.
- **Gemma steering is causal but low-rate.** Response-only layer-12 steering at |δ|=0.05 produces 28/960 source flips vs. 2/960 for the random unit (27/28 sign-consistent; Fisher p = 3.65×10⁻⁷), though text changes more often than sources flip (83/960 vs. 39/960).

## Authors & Credibility

Samuel Dawit Assefa is at KAIST (School of Computing, Daejeon); Jae Won Cho (corresponding author) is at Konkuk University (School of Electrical and Electronics Engineering, Seoul). The paper is formatted for ICML 2026 (PMLR 306) and supported by an NRF of Korea grant (RS-2026-25476101). Credibility signals are unusually strong for an interpretability paper: pinned model revisions, exact prompt templates and judge schemas in the appendix, qid-level splits with seed 42, a full inventory of every learned direction (Table 1), and a "failure case gallery" (Table 2) that explicitly downgrades overclaimable results — e.g., excluding early no-op-hook Gemma runs and omitting an older layer-22 surfacing figure that conflicted with the stratified layer-17 result. Disclosure labels rest on an LLM judge with manual (not blinded inter-annotator) audits, which the authors themselves flag.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: solid but incremental relative to the conflict-steering line.** The read-and-steer recipe follows Arditi et al. (refusal direction) and Rimsky et al. (CAA); the nearest prior work (Zhao et al. 2024/2025 on context-vs-memory residuals and SpARE; Li et al.'s JuICE) already steered knowledge selection. The genuinely new parts are (a) inter-context conflict (two supplied documents) rather than context-vs-parametric memory, (b) the sparse-MLP write-projection bridge, and (c) the explicit separation of source commitment from user-facing disclosure.
- **Rigor: strong controls, honest scoping.** Order swaps, exact-copy/paraphrase/single-document controls, a closed-book recall proxy, layer-matched random writer sets, and orthogonalized random steering directions are the right controls, and the ±3 steering deltas vs. 51.0–52.3% random-unit baselines are convincing. But the headline "steering can surface it" claim rests entirely on Qwen 0.8B: the full disclosure-steering suite is a single-model result, Gemma surfacing steering is absent, and Gemma source steering is 28/960 flips — statistically clean (p = 3.65×10⁻⁷) but a ~3% flip rate the authors themselves call "low-rate causal support."
- **Reproducibility: excellent on paper.** Pinned revisions, exact prompts, splits, seeds, and a direction inventory make replication plausible; no code release is mentioned, however.
- **Significance: the transparency gap framing is the real contribution.** Near-ceiling AUROCs on a balanced two-way choice with short factual slot conflicts (answer-alias matching on a 1,350-row pool) are somewhat expected for a linearly separable binary decision the model is about to commit to text; the value is the monitoring recipe, not the 0.9945 itself.
- **Red flags / missing ablations.** (1) The 32-token prompt-tail steering window is admitted to be "a fixed script default, not an independently ablated optimum." (2) Disclosure labels come from Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite with manual audits of calibration views rather than a blinded inter-annotator agreement study; the leaning/unresolved boundary is acknowledged noisy (5/1500 Gemma labels manually corrected). (3) Only one conflict format (FaithEval-inconsistent, mostly short factual disagreements) — WikiContradict is cited but unused, so generalization to real long-document contradiction is untested. (4) Sub-1B models only, and Qwen3.5-0.8B is a hybrid DeltaNet/attention architecture, so the layer-13/L11H3 localization may not transfer architecturally. (5) Even the best two-direction arm leaves 56/109 (51.4%) of prompt-failures one-sided, so the practical remedy is thin.

## Strengths

- Cleanly separates three events that deployed RAG conflates: registering disagreement, selecting a source, and disclosing the disagreement — then shows the first and third dissociate in the residual stream.
- Full causal chain: predictive readout → bidirectional behavioral steering → sparse writer localization → write-projection alignment → specificity controls, each with matched random baselines.
- Genuine transfer check on a second model family (Gemma 3 1B) with an independently fitted direction, rather than a copied pipeline.
- Exceptional honesty: Table 2 catalogs partial results and explicitly forbids overreading them; a dual-use statement addresses inverted use for disclosure suppression.
- Concrete, deployable diagnostic: treat a strong source-commitment signal without disclosure as silently-resolved contested evidence.

## Limitations

- Scale and scope: only Qwen3.5-0.8B and Gemma 3 1B; larger models may distribute source competition across different layers or attention/retrieval-planning mechanisms (authors' own caveat).
- The disclosure-steering suite is Qwen-only; Gemma contributes only a readout/timing check, and its source steering is low-rate (28/960 flips) with wording changes (83/960) exceeding flips.
- One controlled conflict format (FaithEval-inconsistent, short factual disagreements with recoverable answer aliases); no long-document or real-Wikipedia-contradiction mechanism test.
- Disclosure labels depend on an LLM judge; audits were manual calibration views, not blinded agreement studies, and the leaning-vs-unresolved boundary is noisy.
- Steering is partial rather than deployment-ready: 56/109 prompt-failures remain one-sided under the best arm, and directions are model-specific.

## Relevance

对关注大模型机制可解释性与激活转向的工作直接相关：该文给出了"残差读出 + 稀疏 MLP 写入 + 因果转向"的完整范式来审计 RAG/冲突证据下的隐性源选择，与知识冲突（context-memory vs inter-context）、忠实性评测（FaithEval/WikiContradict）和透明性监控等方向高度契合，可作为构建可靠检索问答系统时内部审计机制的设计参考。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the source-choice direction survive in 7B–70B models, and does its layer position track where each architecture resolves competition (e.g., earlier in hybrid DeltaNet backbones vs. standard attention transformers)?
- Can the Qwen disclosure-steering result be replicated in Gemma or Llama families, or is the layer-17/layer-23 two-direction interaction an architecture-specific accident?
- How does the readout behave on real long-document contradictions (WikiContradict) where the conflict is embedded in many paragraphs rather than two short passages filling one slot?
- Would probing the direction *without* generation-time access (e.g., on logit-lens margins alone) suffice for deployment as a cheap monitoring signal, and what is its false-positive rate under genuine agreement?
- Can the write-projection bridge be pushed further into a full circuit — the paper's L11H3 head recovers only ~0.65 of the localized state over 14 pairs — to identify where disagreement is first registered (median logit-lens onset 4.5 vs. CETT 11.0)?
- Does inversion of the disclosure direction actually suppress conflict acknowledgment in practice, and can the defender-favoring asymmetry the authors claim be quantified?
