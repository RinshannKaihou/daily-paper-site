> **一句话总结：** CogRSI 用"接地阅读器 + 原则仲裁"做心理理论推理（8 基准 Main 73.1 vs 同成本自一致性 68.7），再用自我提升循环自动演化推理原则——演化出的原则能迁移到从未见过的 BigToM-U 配对（96.6% vs 专家原则 94.5%），但语义审计发现 86.7% 的已学规则在形式化世界模型下存在反例（如"在场即知情"对睡着的人失效），且准确率与反事实门控都无法识别这些错误规则。

> **TL;DR:** A grounded reader that marks who perceived each event and settles reading disagreements with a written principle beats self-consistency (k=9) at equal token cost on an 8-benchmark theory-of-mind suite (73.1 vs 68.7 Main; FANToM 28.0 vs 18.0). A self-improvement loop then writes the principle itself: every learned principle transfers to 800 unseen BigToM-U pairs and beats the expert-written one (96.6% vs 94.5% pair validity), even though the learned variant still trails the expert on test Main (71.8 vs 73.1). The paper's sharpest finding is the semantic audit: translating each learned rule into code and running it on ~85k formal-model questions shows 86.7% of evolved rules are invalid — giving wrong answers in situations they claim to cover (e.g., "present means knows" fails for a sleeping character) — and neither accuracy gains nor a counterfactual gate catches this.

## 核心贡献

- **接地阅读器（C1）**：为故事中每个事件标注"谁看见了/被告知了什么"，模型分别阅读带标注版本和原始文本（共采样 6 次，与 SC k=6 同预算），两种读法冲突或票数接近时，由一次仲裁调用依据一条书面原则裁决；仲裁永远不会推翻 5-6 票一致的答案。
- **反事实引导的策略演化（C2）**：自我提升循环将原则写成一个小型规则库（每条规则带适用条件），只有当准确率提升超过校准margin（3 点）时才保留更新；一个变体额外要求通过反事实测试对（同一故事仅改变一个"谁能看见什么"的事实）。
- **语义审计（C3）**：用两个独立模型（Llama-3.3-70B 与 Qwen3.5-122B，均非规则作者）把每条学到的规则翻译成代码，在 6,000 个小世界（约 8.5 万个有标准答案的问题）上执行；若两个翻译在某个问题上给出相同的错误答案，则该规则无效。
- **核心发现**：策略"有用"与"正确"是两个独立的维度——演化出的规则在实践中帮助了阅读器并迁移到新基准，但 86.7% 的规则作为逻辑断言是错误的，且准确率、试验增益、反事实门控都无法区分有效与无效规则。

## Key Contributions

- **A grounded reader (C1):** marks who perceived each sentence/utterance, reads the story 4 times annotated (or the questioned character's view for dialogue) plus 2 times plain (6 samples total, matching SC k=6's budget), and uses one arbiter call under a written principle only when readings conflict or the vote splits closely; an answer held by 5-6 samples is never overturned.
- **Counterfactual-guided strategy evolution (C2):** the RSI loop writes the principle as a library of ≤5 conditioned rules; an update is registered only if mean accuracy gain over 3×80 fresh trial questions exceeds a calibrated margin (3 points), and in one variant only if it also passes a 96-pair counterfactual gate.
- **A semantic audit (C3):** two independent models translate each learned rule into `applies()`/`predict()` code, executed on ~85k questions over 6,000 formal small worlds (seeing-leads-to-knowing plus testimony); a rule is invalid if both translations agree on the same wrong answer in a situation the rule claims to cover.
- **The headline dissociation:** evolved strategies help in practice and transfer to unseen benchmarks, yet 86.7% of the 573 audited rules are provably wrong as written — and neither task accuracy, trial gains, nor the counterfactual gate distinguishes valid from invalid rules (only 3 of 28 decided rules passing the 3-point margin are valid).

## Methodology

The system operates in three layers. **(A) RSI over procedures:** a builder model reads system failures on ExploreToM development data and writes executable tools; an analysis of 6 runs shows 181/183 candidate tools encode perceptual access. **(B) RSI over principles:** the builder writes a library of conditioned rules (≤5 entries); following MedRSI, a candidate is registered if its mean accuracy gain over K=3 fresh trial cohorts of 80 questions exceeds margin δ. Variants compared: accuracy-only loop (1-point margin, single principle), accuracy-only library (3-point), pairs-as-data library, gated single principle, and gated library (3-point + counterfactual gate), plus GEPA in the same slot. **(C) The grounded reader at test time** applies the strategies: four samples read the annotated story, two read the plain story; on conflict or close split, an arbiter picks between the two leading answers under the principle. The semantic audit formalizes ground truth via a Wimmer-style seeing-leads-to-knowing model with testimony: agents enter/leave, sleep/wake, move objects, whisper true or false locations, peek, and change visible/hidden properties across 6,000 sampled worlds (~85k questions with known answers). Evaluation spans eight benchmarks (FANToM, SimpleToM, ExploreToM, Hi-ToM, OpenToM, ToMBench, CogToM, BigToM; 3,349 main questions, 2,470 confirmatory), seven test-only benchmarks, 320 held-out counterfactual pairs, and 800 independent BigToM-U pairs no system had seen. All loops train only on ExploreToM data. Main backbone: Qwen3.5-35B-A3B (FP8, thinking off, vLLM); also run on Qwen3.5-122B-A10B, Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct, GLM-4.6V-Flash, Qwen3.5-9B. Differences use paired bootstrap over stories; experiments after the design freeze were pre-registered.

## Results & Highlights

- **Reader strength (RQ1):** with the expert principle, CogRSI scores **73.1 Main vs 68.7 for SC (k=9) at the same token cost (+4.4)** on the 8-benchmark suite, and 73.5 vs 68.5 on the pre-registered confirmatory sample; on FANToM it reaches **28.0 vs 18.0 for SC (k=9)** and on SimpleToM **90.0 vs 71.2 (+18.8)**; it beats SC (k=9) on all five backbones by +5.1 to +8.6 on a four-benchmark mean.
- **Transfer (RQ2):** on 800 BigToM-U pairs no system had seen, every learned principle beats the expert principle — the gated library reaches **96.6% pair validity vs 94.5% expert (+2.1, CI [+1.0, +3.5])**, while SC (k=9) gets 93.0 and GEPA only 84.4; on the 320 held-out own-generator pairs, the gated library scores 96.4 vs 91.2 for the accuracy-only library (+5.1, CI [+3.6, +6.8]).
- **The gate barely matters for accuracy:** the pre-registered primary contrast (gated minus accuracy-only library on test Main) is **−0.2 (CI [−1.4, +0.8])** — transfer comes from evolving the principle at all, not from counterfactual gating.
- **Registration precision (RQ3):** raising the margin from 1 to 3 points lifts the share of kept updates that actually help from **63.6% to 83.3%** and halves harmful updates from 31.8% to 16.7% — the calibrated margin, not the gate, makes selection precise.
- **Semantic audit (RQ4):** **86.7% of the 573 evolved rules are invalid** (both independent code translations give the same wrong answer somewhere the rule claims to apply); among decided kept rules, invalid ones number 14/14 (accuracy-only loop), 5/5 (accuracy-only library), 7/9 (gated library), 3/4 (gated single principle); trial gains do not separate valid from invalid rules, and the counterfactual gate does not change what is admitted.
- **Development gains do not transfer to test Main:** the accuracy-only loop's principle beats the expert on new dev questions (63.8 vs 59.7) yet loses on test Main (71.8 vs 73.1); the gated library also trails the expert on test Main by −1.3 (CI [−1.9, −0.6]).
- **Honest frontier caveats:** on the seven test-only benchmarks the reader scores 65.2 vs 66.2 for SC (k=9), and when a belief must be inferred backwards from an action every system stays near 68.0% pair validity (37.2% on flip pairs), with no principle helping (+0.2, CI [−2.5, +2.8]).

## Authors & Credibility

The work comes from Nanyang Technological University (Yandan Zheng, Haoran Luo — corresponding author, Anh Tuan Luu, who also holds an affiliation at VinUniversity) and Stanford University (Xinliang Zhou). The experimental hygiene is unusually strong for this area: a confirmatory sample of 2,470 questions drawn after every design decision, pre-registration of post-freeze experiments, paired bootstrap intervals on nearly every claimed difference, five backbones, and reported null results (the counterfactual gate's null effect, the below-SC score on test-only benchmarks) rather than selective reporting. Code and data are public at github.com/koanatakiyo/cogRSI, with scripts that regenerate every figure and number. An AI-use statement discloses assistant help with code, experiments, and text, with author direction and checking.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** the grounded reader is an incremental but well-executed combination of annotation-based ToM prompting (PercepToM, SimToM) with arbitration; the genuinely novel parts are (a) applying artifact-level RSI to a written reasoning *principle* for ToM and (b) the semantic audit translating learned natural-language rules into executable code against a formal world model — I am not aware of prior work that checks *what a self-improvement loop's artifacts assert* rather than just what they score.
- **Rigor:** high. Pre-registration, confirmatory samples, calibrated margins grounded in MedRSI's registration rule, shared-counterexample double translation to guard against single mistranslation, and null results reported plainly. The 86.7% invalidity claim is backed by a concrete mechanism (e.g., the kept rule "if B was present when the event occurred, assume A believes B knows it" fails when B is asleep — ExploreToM never puts a character to sleep, so no trial cohort could penalize it).
- **Reproducibility:** good on paper — public repo, frozen configs, per-run appendix, vLLM-served open-weight models (Qwen3.5, Llama-3.3, GLM) — though loops ran only 3-6 seeds and some arms were not evaluated on every test set (acknowledged in Table 1).
- **Weaknesses worth flagging:** (1) the headline reader gain is carried by 81% from just FANToM and SimpleToM, and the reader *loses* to SC (k=9) on the seven test-only benchmarks (65.2 vs 66.2) — generalization beyond the main suite is genuinely mixed; (2) the reader costs 7.3 calls and 1.51× the tokens of SC (k=6), and the +4.4 Main gain buys only ~0.3-0.6 points per additional model family on some cuts; (3) all loops train on a single generator (ExploreToM), so the "transfer" tested is narrow — BigToM-U is still synthetic, one-room, English physical-access reasoning, and the formal audit model covers only physical access plus testimony (no desires, emotions, or second-order nuance); (4) the audit itself depends on two LLM translators that "can still miss what a rule's author meant," so the 86.7% figure inherits translation noise even with the shared-counterexample guard; (5) the case-study style evidence (the ToMBench lie) is a single cherry-picked success — most systems fail it, but one anecdote does not establish a pattern.
- **Red flags:** minor. The abstract's "beats self-consistency" (73.1 vs 68.7) is fair but the test-only-benchmark regression is deferred to the body; the counterfactual gate — a named contribution (C2) — turns out to add almost nothing on test Main (−0.2), which the authors honestly state but which slightly oversells the design space explored.

## Strengths

- The core conceptual contribution — separating *behavioral usefulness* of a learned strategy from its *semantic validity*, and showing empirically that the two dissociate (helpful, transferring, yet 86.7% wrong as rules) — is a crisp, important lesson for the whole self-improvement literature.
- Excellent evaluation discipline: pre-registered contrasts, confirmatory samples, held-out counterfactual pairs, and an entirely unseen evaluation set (BigToM-U) for transfer claims.
- The semantic audit is constructive, not just diagnostic: it produces interpretable counterexamples (the sleeping-character failure) that explain *why* a rule is wrong and trace it to a blind spot in the training generator.
- Robustness across five backbones and honest reporting of costs (7.3 calls/question, 1.51× SC tokens) alongside a token-matched SC (k=9) comparison.
- Public code, data, prompts, and table-regeneration scripts.

## Limitations

- All loops learn from one story generator (ExploreToM); the formal audit model covers only physical access and testimony in a single room, leaving other mental states (desires, emotions, higher-order nuance) to future work.
- The audit relies on two models translating rules into code; shared counterexamples guard against a single mistranslation, but both translations can still miss the rule author's intent.
- Loops run only 3-6 seeds, and some arms were not evaluated on every test set (Table 1), leaving some comparisons underpowered.
- All benchmarks are English and mostly synthetic; gains on conversation-heavy suites (FANToM, SimpleToM) dominate Main, while transfer to the seven test-only benchmarks is slightly negative (65.2 vs 66.2 for SC k=9).
- Backward belief inference (inferring a belief from an action) remains unsolved for every system (~68.0% pair validity, 37.2% on flip pairs), and no principle helps.
- Using the audit *inside* registration — the authors' own proposed next step — is left unimplemented; a replay suggests few current candidates would pass, moving the bottleneck to proposing correct strategies.

## Relevance

这篇论文同时触及两个核心兴趣点：LLM 智能体的自我改进（RSI 循环演化推理策略）与推理策略的可靠性评估（语义审计发现"有用但错误"的策略）。其"演化 + 审计"双轨框架对任何用 LLM 自动生成 prompt/规则/工具并按准确率保留的工作都有直接方法论借鉴价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would inserting the semantic audit *into* the registration loop (rejecting invalid candidates, not just measuring them after the fact) improve test Main, or would it starve the loop of any admittable candidates as the replay suggests?
- Do the 13.3% valid rules differ systematically in linguistic form (generality, hedging, condition specificity) from invalid ones — and could a validity predictor be trained from the audit's counterexamples?
- Does the dissociation between behavioral usefulness and semantic validity persist for strategies evolved on more diverse training generators, or is it an artifact of ExploreToM's narrow event space (no sleeping, no lies, no peeking)?
- Can the formal world model be extended beyond one-room physical access and testimony to desires, emotions, and communication-heavy multi-party dialogue, where FANToM-style gains currently concentrate?
- Since the reader weighs rules as "general hints" (removing all conditions changes BigToM-U validity by only −0.5), is the rule format even load-bearing — would a single unconditioned heuristic match the library's performance?
- How does the 86.7% invalidity rate compare with human-written expert principles under the same audit — is the expert principle's validity a fair gold standard or just a less-audited blind spot?
