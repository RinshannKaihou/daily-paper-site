Methodology — how TopoSteer scores a layer

1
Contrastive pairs~700 paired persona prompts (x⁺ᵢ, x⁻ᵢ) from the Anthropic Persona Dataset
2
Displacement cloudper layer, at the final prompt token: δᵢˢ = ãˢ(x⁺ᵢ) − ãˢ(x⁻ᵢ), coordinates standardized
3
Layer score = total H₀ persistencecomputed exactly as the Euclidean minimum-spanning-tree length of the cloud
4
Select top-K layers, inject raw MD vectorα = 1.0; the standardized cloud is used only for scoring
  • Reframe: mean-difference steering averages all per-example displacements, so the right question is how spread out that cloud is — not how separable the two activation classes are (LayerNavigator's discriminability D), nor how cosine-consistent displacements are (C).
  • Cost: no held-out steering runs, no selector training, no extra forward passes — only an offline O(N²d) geometric computation per layer.
  • Diagnostics: bridge share (longest MST edge / total length) flags scores dominated by a single bridging edge; an answer-polarity axis (perfect 350/350 lobe split, near-zero class signal) is exposed as a Yes/No format artifact.
  • Setup: 5 instruct models (Llama-3-3B/8B, Qwen2.5-3B/7B/32B) × 6 binary persona tasks × K∈{1,2,3} × two injection protocols; 100 held-out examples per task.
Theorem 1 · separability–connectivity decoupling
Along Δ(σ) = {μₓ + σξᵢ}, total persistence is strictly increasing in σ while discriminability D(σ) is strictly decreasing — the two criteria can order layers in exactly opposite ways, explaining their empirical disagreements.

Strengths

  • Training-free and validation-free: reuses activations already cached for MD extraction; adds only an offline O(N²d) MST computation per layer.
  • Theory and experiments tightly coupled: the decoupling theorem predicts exactly the disagreement regime observed, and a 2-D controlled construction makes the geometry checkable by hand.
  • Unusually honest reporting: discloses 4/15 losses vs LayerNavigator and the W2S-Multi comparison where a costlier method wins on accuracy.
  • The answer-polarity analysis is a standalone contribution — any future contrastive-pair steering study should check for it (perfect 350/350 lobe split at all 32 layers, zero class signal).

Limitations

  • Scope of the method: only matched-pair, mean-difference steering; other extractors (CAA, refusal-direction methods) are left to future work.
  • Narrow evaluation: six binary persona tasks from one dataset, ~100 held-out items each, aligned-answer probability as the sole behavioral metric; no refusal, honesty, or reasoning tasks.
  • Model-dependent gains: near-ceiling bases (Qwen2.5-7B 85.24%, Qwen2.5-32B 82.68%) show sub-point, mixed results.
  • Unresolved variant sensitivity: the standardized variant can fall below the unsteered baseline (61.12% vs 64.26% on Qwen2.5-3B, K=1) while the unit-sphere variant wins — no rule given for choosing.
  • No error bars or seed variance; single α = 1.0; Theorem 1 holds at a fixed layer, and multi-layer steering can degrade perplexity.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Q1Does the displacement-cloud score transfer to other steering extractors (CAA, refusal directions) and to behaviors beyond binary personas — refusal, honesty, chain-of-thought?
  2. Q2Why does the standardized variant collapse below the unsteered baseline on Qwen2.5-3B at K=1 while unit-sphere wins? Is there a principled, model-independent variant rule?
  3. Q3Can the bridge-share diagnostic be upgraded into a corrected score (trimmed MST, mode-aware persistence, higher-degree terms) instead of a post-hoc flag?
  4. Q4How does selection quality interact with α ≠ 1.0 and larger budgets K > 3, given that perplexity degrades on some models as K grows?
  5. Q5Would projecting out the answer-polarity axis at extraction time (not just as a diagnostic) improve MD steering on datasets with skewed Yes/No formats?
