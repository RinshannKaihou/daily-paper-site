> **一句话总结：** 对一个已发表的硬件恶意软件检测器（HMD）设计空间（18 种分类器 × 4 种性能计数器预算 + AdaBoost/Bagging 集成）注入 453,174 个决策 stuck-at 与参数位翻转故障后发现：故障位数多少不能预测脆弱性——基于比较器的树模型故障位多达函数型模型的 384 倍但关键故障占比反而更低（5.3% vs 14.3%），72.3% 的树模型故障是"指标沉默"型（召回率与误报率变化均 <0.1pp，输出监控无法察觉），而等权 Bagging 在 27 组完整配对比较中 26 组降低关键故障率；对 J48 检测器仅保护 115 个结构单元中的 16 个即可把预测的最坏单故障召回损失从 60.1pp 压到 3.3pp。

> **TL;DR:** The first systematic fault-injection study of hardware malware detectors (HMDs) that classify hardware performance counters (HPCs). Across a published design space of 18 classifiers × 16/8/4/2-counter budgets plus AdaBoost/Bagging ensembles (validated bit-exactly against their trained models), the authors enumerate 5,585,493 structural fault sites and evaluate 453,174 stuck-at/bit-flip faults on a 7,400-instance corpus. Key findings: a size–density inversion (tree detectors expose 4.77M fault sites vs 12,410 for function-based ones — a 384× gap — yet their mean critical-fault fraction is 5.3% vs 14.3%); 72.3% of tree faults are "metric-silent" (recall and FPR both shift <0.1 pp, invisible to output monitoring); reducing the counter budget from 16 to 2 raises SGD's critical-fault fraction from 18.8% to 46.7%; equal-weight Bagging cuts criticality in 26 of 27 completed matched comparisons (to 0.00% in all 17 four-HPC cases, vs e.g. SMO single at 36.00%); and an exact minimax selective-hardening scheme protecting 16 of a J48 detector's 115 structural units (≈35% of its area) reduces predicted worst-case single-fault recall loss from 60.1 to 3.3 percentage points. All hardening numbers are predicted under ideal correction with apportioned area — physical validation is explicitly left to future work.

## 核心贡献

- **提出以安全后果为轴心的故障分类法**：每个注入故障被划分为 noisy（输出畸形、可被包装层检测）、evasion（召回率下降 ≥5pp，让恶意软件漏检）、denial-of-service（误报率上升 ≥5pp，逼运维关掉检测器）、silent（召回率与误报率变化均 <0.1pp，输出层面完全无法区分健康与缺陷检测器）、degraded（其余）。该分类法专门化了 Avižienis 等人的经典失效分类，把"平均精度损失"这一传统指标掩盖掉的三种截然不同的防御需求分开。
- **对完整 HMD 设计空间做首次故障分析**：在 140 个具有可枚举故障位的已验证配置上注入决策 stuck-at 与参数位翻转，共枚举 5,585,493 个结构故障位、完成 453,174 次故障评估（约 3.35×10⁹ 次语料评估），覆盖树、规则、函数型（线性/MLP）、贝叶斯、实例基五大分类器家族及两种集成。
- **发现攻击面的"大小–密度倒置"**：基于比较器的检测器故障位极多但单个故障大多无害，基于乘加（MAC）的检测器故障位极少但个个承重——故障位数量是脆弱性的糟糕代理指标。这直接推翻了"硅面积最便宜 = 最不脆弱"的直觉排序。
- **量化"沉默的大多数"**：72.3% 的树模型故障与 68.4% 的规则模型故障是指标沉默型，且关键故障高度集中于树根附近的浅层比较器（根节点故障平均降召回 19.5pp，最深 60pp；深度 10 以下几乎无影响）——这使选择性加固成为可能。
- **给出可操作的防御算法**：提出精确 minimax 选择算法（对抗目标在"单元不相交、成本可加、理想纠正"假设下精确求解；随机目标用带 (1−1/e) 保证的贪心），并以"检测优先于纠正"（重复比较 ≈1.15× 面积 vs 三模冗余 ≈2.3×）重构硬件安全监控器的加固经济学。

## Key Contributions

- **A security-consequence fault taxonomy** that classifies each injected fault as noisy, evasion (recall drop ≥ τ_R = 5 pp), denial-of-service (FPR rise ≥ τ_F = 5 pp), silent (both recall and FPR change < ε = 0.1 pp), or degraded — specializing the Avižienis dependability taxonomy so that three outcomes requiring different defensive responses are no longer obscured by mean-accuracy degradation.
- **The first fault analysis of HPC-based malware detectors at the scale of a complete published design space**: 453,174 decision stuck-at and parameter bit-flip evaluations over 5,585,493 enumerated structural fault sites on 140 configurations (18 classifiers × 16/8/4/2-counter budgets plus K=10 AdaBoost and Bagging ensembles), each configuration bit-exactly validated against the recorded predictions of its original trained model.
- **Identification of a size–density inversion in the attack surface**: comparator-based (tree/rule) detectors expose vastly more fault sites than multiply-accumulate (function-based) detectors yet a smaller fraction of those faults is critical, so fault-site count is a poor proxy for vulnerability.
- **Quantification of metric-silent faults** — defects that leave both recall and false-positive rate unchanged and therefore cannot be caught by any output-level monitoring (watchdog or alarm-rate plausibility bound) — plus localization of critical faults to shallow comparators near the tree root.
- **A criticality-guided selective-hardening algorithm**: an exact minimax unit-selection method under additive unit costs and ideal correction, combined with a greedy silence-elimination detection tier, showing that protecting a small subset of structural units bounds worst-case single-fault recall loss at a fraction of full-redundancy cost.

## Methodology

The study operates on the 144 trained detector configurations of the authors' prior benchmark study ([20], SmartNets 2024), in the hardware form validated by their companion PPA study ([1], arXiv:2610.12795): 18 classifiers from the tree, rule, function-based (linear and MLP), Bayesian, and instance-based families, each trained under 16-, 8-, 4-, and 2-counter budgets, plus AdaBoost and Bagging ensembles of K=10 members at the reduced budgets. Crucially, every configuration includes a software model validated against the recorded per-instance predictions of the original classifiers and a synthesizable RTL simulated against that model over the full dataset with zero mismatches, so any behavioral change can be attributed solely to the injected fault.

Two hardware-relevant fault models are injected: (1) **decision stuck-at**, where a comparator output, rule-condition result, table entry, or ensemble member verdict is permanently forced to a value (classical stuck-at-0/1, also capturing shorted/open nets, latched glitches, and aged devices); and (2) **parameter bit-flip**, inverting one bit of a stored parameter — integer thresholds across their 32-bit representation, real-valued weights in IEEE-754 mantissa/exponent/sign. Faults are injected into the **structural model** (comparator tree, rule list, table, weight vector, ensemble) rather than a gate netlist, deliberately trading physical fidelity for interpretability (a fault names the architectural element that failed) and portability across synthesis runs and technology targets.

Each fault-injected detector is re-evaluated on the same 7,400-instance corpus used for baseline validation (a paired evaluation of fault-induced change, not a held-out detection estimate). Each fault is assigned the first matching class from {noisy, evasion (R₀−R ≥ 5 pp), DoS (F−F₀ ≥ 5 pp), silent (|ΔR|, |ΔF| < 0.1 pp), degraded}, with criticality defined as evasion. The tolerance ε = 0.1 pp is justified as undetectable in the field (detecting a 0.1 pp shift at α = 0.05, 80% power would need > 7×10⁴ labeled observations, and labels are exactly what an in-field monitor lacks), and the family ordering is shown robust to sweeping τ_R from 1 to 10 pp. Of the 140 configurations with enumerable fault sites, 101 are exhaustively evaluated, 27 by completed random sampling, and 12 are time-limited and excluded from primary comparisons (128 completed configurations). Finally, an exact minimax hardening algorithm binary-searches over observed worst-fault damage levels per structural unit (adversarial objective, exact), then greedily spends the remaining budget on duplicate-and-compare detection of silent faults (stochastic objective, (1−1/e) guarantee), and empirically re-runs the fault simulation on the hardened design rather than trusting the prediction.

## Results & Highlights

- **Size–density inversion (RQ1)**: across the 128 completed configurations, tree-based detectors exhibit a mean critical-fault fraction of 5.7% versus 14.3% for function-based models (exhaustive-only analysis preserves the ordering; simulation summary gives 5.3%), even though trees expose 4.77 million enumerated fault sites versus the function family's 12,410 — a 384× difference in attack-surface size pointing in the opposite direction. The 95% confidence intervals of the two families remain disjoint at every evasion threshold swept from 1 to 10 pp.
- **Concrete extremes**: in a 4-HPC J48 decision tree, only 3.9% of the 3,910 fault sites cause evasion while 51.5% are silent, and the worst single fault drops malware recall from 97.0% to 36.9% (−60.1 pp); in a 4-HPC SGD detector, only 25 fault sites exist but 28% cause evasion and a further 44% cause denial of service, none are silent, and a single bit flip drives recall to 0%.
- **The silent majority (RQ2)**: 72.3% of evaluated tree faults and 68.4% of rule faults are metric-silent — changing recall and FPR by less than 0.1 pp — so a defective detector is indistinguishable from a healthy one by output monitoring alone; the function family is three times less likely to be silent because faulty weights typically produce out-of-range accumulations the datapath rejects.
- **Where criticality lives (RQ2)**: faults concentrate at the tree root — on average a root fault reduces recall by 19.5 pp (maximum 60 pp), while faults below depth 10 have almost no mean effect, making the damage distribution heavy-tailed and selective hardening viable.
- **Counter budget trades silicon for fragility (RQ3)**: cutting the feature set from 16 to 2 counters raises the critical-fault fraction from 18.8% to 46.7% for SGD (+27.9 pp), from 23.5% to 33.3% for SMO, and from 14.1% to 33.3% for Logistic; the trend is non-monotonic for SMO, whose criticality falls from 36.0% at 4 counters to 33.3% at 2.
- **Ensembles buy fault tolerance, not accuracy (RQ4)**: among 27 completed matched comparisons, equal-weight Bagging reduces the critical-fault fraction in 26 (achieving 0.00% in 33 of 34 comparisons including all 17 at 4 HPCs — e.g. SMO falls from 36.00% single to 0.00% bagged, Decision Stump from 35.29% to 0.00%, SGD from 28.00% to 0.00%), whereas AdaBoost increases criticality in 2 (its residual criticality ranges 0%–30% because high-weight members dominate the vote); the MLP is the star case — 22.6% of single-detector faults are critical with the worst driving recall to 0%, while the bagged version has zero critical faults and worst-case recall never below 63.6%.
- **Selective hardening (RQ5)**: for the representative J48 detector, protecting 16 of 115 structural units — a hardening budget of 35% of the detector's own area, with duplicate-and-compare detection at ≈1.15× unit area versus ≈2.3× for triple modular redundancy — reduces the predicted worst-case single-fault recall loss from 60.1 to 3.3 percentage points, with negligible returns beyond that budget.

## Authors & Credibility

The team is a credible, established hardware-security collaboration: Alireza Abolhasani Zeraatkar, Parnian Shabani Kamran, and Hussain Al-Asaad (ECE, UC Davis), Parsa Mirfasihi (ECE, San Francisco State University), and Saleh Khalaj Monfared (ECE, Worcester Polytechnic Institute). Monfared's group authored the cited fault-injection enablers (GlitchSnipe localized voltage attacks, C hypnosis undervolting attacks, LeakyOhm impedance analysis), so the physical-feasibility argument for the adversary model is grounded in their own prior results. The paper is the third leg of a coherent research program: the classifier benchmark ([20], SmartNets 2024), the FPGA/Open-PDK silicon cost study ([1], arXiv:2610.12795), and now fault vulnerability — and the fault injection is performed on RTL-validated models with zero prediction mismatches. The statistical hygiene is above average for a security paper: 95% confidence intervals across configurations, explicit separation of exhaustive (101) vs sampled (27) vs time-limited (12) configurations, an explicit statement that no campaign-wide sampling margin is asserted, and a sensitivity sweep of the criticality threshold.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Genuinely novel positioning. Prior adversarial work on HMDs (RHMD, EnsembleHMD, ObfusGate) assumes intact detector logic and attacks the inputs; prior fault-injection work on ML hardware targets DNN weight memory, which most HMD families lack. Being (to the authors' knowledge) the first fault analysis of HPC-based detectors over a whole validated design space is a defensible claim, and the security-consequence taxonomy (evasion/DoS/silent vs mean accuracy) is a real conceptual contribution.
- **Rigor**: Strong on scale and internal validity (bit-exact validated models, paired fault-free baselines, disjoint CIs under threshold sweeps). But the headline hardening number — 16 of 115 units cutting worst-case recall loss from 60.1 to 3.3 pp — is **predicted**, under the stated assumptions of disjoint units, additive costs, and ideal correction, and plotted against an area *apportionment* rather than measured layout. The authors re-run the fault simulation on the hardened design (good), but the budget axis itself is not silicon; the paper says so, yet the abstract's phrasing could still mislead a skimming reader.
- **Reproducibility**: The fault campaigns rest on two of the authors' own prior artifacts ([20], [1]); no public release of the fault-injection harness, per-fault outcome tables, or the hardened RTL is mentioned, and the 7,400-instance corpus is not a standard public benchmark, so independent reproduction would require contacting the authors.
- **Significance**: The size–density inversion and the 72.3% silent-fault fraction are actionable for detector architects (prefer comparator families at reduced budgets; monitor integrity, not outputs), and the "detection is a safe state" argument is a nice reframing for security monitors.
- **Red flags / weaknesses**: (1) Faults are injected at the architectural level, not a gate netlist — synthesis-introduced faults are excluded, and the authors admit they have *not shown* the two levels agree even on family ordering. (2) The evaluation reuses the same 7,400-instance corpus employed for baseline validation rather than held-out data — defensible for measuring fault-induced *deltas*, but silent-fault percentages and worst-case recall losses could shift on unseen programs, and the silence of a fault on individual predictions is explicitly acknowledged as possibly masked. (3) KNN stored-sample faults — the largest memory structure in the entire design space, and plausibly the most consequential fault class — are entirely unenumerated; the KNN rows cover only 110 vote-weight sites. (4) Twelve time-limited configurations are excluded from the primary analysis with no campaign-wide sampling margin, and the adversary model covers only single-fault placement (the minimax is per-fault worst case; a multi-fault attacker is unaddressed). (5) AdaBoost's harm is summarized differently in different places (increases in 2 of 27 completed comparisons vs 4 across 34 triples) — internally consistent given different denominators, but a careful reader must track which set is which.

## Strengths

- Asks a question the entire prior HMD literature (input-space evasion, cost/PPA optimization) leaves untouched: what happens when the detector's own logic is damaged?
- The security-consequence fault taxonomy (evasion / DoS / silent / noisy / degraded) replaces mean-accuracy degradation with defender-relevant categories, and the ε = 0.1 pp tolerance is justified with an explicit power analysis (a field monitor would need > 7×10⁴ labeled observations to detect it).
- Scale and validation discipline: 453,174 fault evaluations on bit-exact-validated models, with honest separation of exhaustive, sampled, and time-limited configurations and confidence intervals throughout.
- Actionable engineering conclusions: prefer comparator-based families at reduced counter budgets; choose Bagging specifically (not boosting) when fault tolerance matters; harden shallow comparators rather than paying for full redundancy; treat damage detection — not damage prevention — as the safe state for a security monitor.
- The two-objective hardening algorithm cleanly separates the adversarial minimax (solved exactly) from the stochastic coverage objective (greedy with the standard (1−1/e) bound), and verifies empirically instead of trusting the predictor.

## Limitations

- **Structural-level injection**: faults live in the architectural model, not a gate netlist, so synthesis-introduced faults are out of scope; architectural-vs-physical agreement is explicitly left unvalidated.
- **KNN blind spot**: the instance-based detectors store their training set, the largest structure in the design space, and stored-sample faults are not enumerated at all — the KNN rows cover vote weights only.
- **Predicted, not measured, hardening**: Algorithm 1's budget axis uses area apportionment and ideal correction; carrying hardened variants through the physical-design (open-PDK) flow is future work, as is faulted-RTL correspondence.
- **Same-corpus evaluation**: recall/FPR deltas are measured on the 7,400-instance corpus used for baseline validation, not on held-out programs; detection performance on unseen workloads is delegated to the companion study.
- **Single-fault adversary**: the analysis bounds worst-case *single*-fault damage; coordinated multi-fault injection, which a capable physical attacker might attempt, is not considered.
- **Coverage gaps**: 12 of 140 configurations are time-limited and excluded from primary comparisons, and no campaign-wide sampling margin is asserted.

## Relevance

对关注硬件辅助安全与 ML 系统可靠性的读者，本文提供了一个罕见的大规模实证：它把故障注入分析方法从 DNN 权重内存迁移到无权重内存的比较器型分类器上，并给出"故障位数量 ≠ 脆弱性"以及"输出监控无法发现沉默故障"两条对部署 ML 安全硅 IP 直接有用的结论。其选择性加固思路（按故障关键度保护少数结构单元、以检测代替纠正）对任何需要在硬件预算约束下做容错设计的推理加速器/安全监控器都有借鉴意义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do architectural-level and gate-level fault injections agree on the family ordering (comparator vs MAC), and does the 72.3% silent-fault fraction survive a real synthesized netlist with synthesis-introduced faults included?
- How do the silent-fault and critical-fault fractions change on a held-out corpus of unseen programs and malware families, rather than the 7,400-instance validation set?
- Can an adversary with the ability to place two or three coordinated faults (e.g., one evasion fault plus one that defeats duplicate-and-compare detection) still bypass the selectively hardened J48 design, and at what additional attacker cost?
- What is the cheapest runtime check for *silent* faults specifically — periodic re-seeding with known labeled traces, structural self-test patterns, or parameter integrity hashing — given that output monitoring is provably insufficient at ε = 0.1 pp?
- Do the ransomware-specific HMD variants (HiPeR, DeepWare, Rapper), which share the same implementation logic, inherit the same fault surface, and would silent faults there delay encryption detection past the point of damage?
- Can the exact minimax selection be extended to correlated unit failures (shared logic, common power domains) where the disjoint-units and additive-costs assumptions break down?
