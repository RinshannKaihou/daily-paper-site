> **一句话总结：** EvoRouter 用一个"洞察蒸馏专家"周期性地把多智能体配置轨迹的性能-成本反馈提炼成带适用条件的可复用路由规则，再通过特权教师模型的 on-policy 蒸馏 + 成本感知强化学习把这些规则内化到路由策略中；在 19 个域内数据集上以 68.69% 平均准确率超过最强路由基线 7.23 个百分点，同时推理计算成本降低 50.95%。

> **TL;DR:** EvoRouter trains a router (Qwen3-4B/8B, Llama-3.1-8B) to jointly configure multi-agent systems (models, collaboration modes, agent counts, rounds, skills) by distilling conditional routing insights from execution trajectories via a privileged teacher's token-level on-policy distillation plus cost-aware group-relative RL. On 19 in-domain datasets across 5 domains it reaches 68.69% average accuracy (EvoRouter-4B), beating the strongest baselines MasRouter/RCR-Router (61.46%) by 7.23 pp while cutting mean MAS compute cost by 50.95% (15.28k vs ~31.1k relative-FLOPs units); on out-of-domain LiveCodeBench + BBH-LD it gains +7.74 to +12.00 pp over the best baseline.

## 核心贡献

- **条件化路由洞察蒸馏**：通过系统性的预实验揭示 MAS 配置空间的三大规律——(1) 资源扩展收益依赖任务（mβ+IO 切换到 mα+FullConnected 使多模态成功率 +13.1 pp，却使 QA −5.0 pp）；(2) 扩展存在饱和点（回合数/智能体数超过峰值后成本上升且成功率下降）；(3) 配置组合存在交互效应（全 mβ 在 FullConnected 下更优，而全 mα 在 Debate 下胜出且 FLOPs 降低 79.8%）——并由一个专家 LLM 周期性地把跨轨迹的性能-成本反馈聚合成带明确适用条件的可复用洞察（规则归纳 → 前提细化 → 容量剪枝）。
- **洞察引导的路由器后训练**：首次将 SFT 冷启动、成本感知强化学习（奖励 R = R_perf − λ_C·R_cost，成本惩罚以 c_max 截断保证成功轨迹奖励恒为正）与洞察特权的 on-policy 蒸馏（教师额外看到洞察池、配置、执行结果与验证结果，对学生自己生成的 `<analysis>`/`<plan>` token 施加反向 KL 逐 token 监督）联合优化，总损失 L = L_RL + λ_D·L_OPD + β·L_KL。
- **执行驱动的自我改进闭环**：更新后的策略产生新轨迹 → 专家提炼新洞察 → 洞察再喂给下一轮蒸馏，形成"执行-洞察-内化"递归循环；推理时无需任何特权反馈。
- **大规模实证验证**：构建覆盖 7 个域、21 个数据集、12,800 道题的评测基准（10,500 训练 / 1,000 开发 / 1,300 测试 + 375 道 OOD 题），对比 5 类共 16 个基线，并用"替换全部模型池/协作模式池/技能池"的零样本实验验证对未见资源的泛化。

## Key Contributions

- **Conditional routing-insight distillation.** Preliminary experiments expose three empirical laws of the MAS configuration space: (i) task-dependent scaling benefits (switching mβ+IO to mα+FullConnected lifts Multimodal success by 13.1 pp but drops QA by 5.0 pp); (ii) diminishing, non-monotonic returns (performance peaks at task/config-specific round and agent counts, after which more compute lowers success); (iii) combinatorial interactions (an all-mβ team wins under FullConnected while an all-mα team wins under Debate at 79.8% fewer MAS FLOPs). A dedicated expert LLM periodically distills cross-trajectory performance–cost feedback into reusable routing rules with explicit applicability conditions via rule induction, precondition refinement, and capacity pruning.
- **Insight-guided router post-training.** Combines (a) SFT cold start from a bootstrap router's filtered trajectories, (b) cost-aware group-relative RL with reward R(τ) = R_perf − λ_C·R_cost where the weighted cost penalty is capped at c_max so successful rollouts always earn positive reward, and (c) insight-privileged on-policy distillation: a frozen teacher conditioned on (insight pool, configuration, execution outcome, verification result) provides dense token-level reverse-KL supervision (MiniLLM-style) on the student's own analysis and plan tokens. Joint loss: L = L_RL + λ_D·L_OPD + β·L_KL.
- **Recursive self-improvement loop.** The updated policy generates fresh trajectories that feed the next insight-extraction round; at inference the router needs no privileged feedback, only the configuration plan it learned to emit.
- **Comprehensive benchmark.** 21 datasets / 7 domains (MATH, GSM8K, OmniMATH-2, Apex Shortlist; ARC-Challenge, StrategyQA, Social IQa; MMLU-Pro, GPQA-Diamond, SuperGPQA; NQ, TriviaQA, HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, PopQA, MuSiQue; ChartQAPro, MMMU, MMMU-Pro; plus OOD LiveCodeBench and BBH-LD), against 16 baselines spanning vanilla LLMs, single-agent routing, single-agent multi-turn routing, and multi-agent routing, with a zero-shot unseen-resource generalization test that swaps the entire model/collaboration/skill pools.

## Methodology

EvoRouter parameterizes the routing policy π_θ (Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B, or Llama-3.1-8B) and trains it in an evolution loop over four components:

1. **Routing and execution.** Given a context x describing roles, the task, and available resources (5 LLMs: Qwen3.5-35B-A3B/-122B-A10B/-397B-A17B, DeepSeek-V4-Flash, GLM-5; 6 collaboration modes: IO, CoT, Reflection, Chain, FullConnected, Debate; ≥6 skills per task category), the router emits `<analysis>` then a structured plan P = (c, r, (u_j, m_j, s_j, p_j)) specifying collaboration mode, rounds, and per-agent identifier/model/skill/subtask. A deterministic executor instantiates the MAS; the router reads execution observations and produces the final answer, forming a two-stage trajectory scored by a task-specific verifier s(τ) ∈ {0,1}.
2. **Supervised initialization.** A frozen DeepSeek-V4-Pro bootstrap router collects trajectories; only format-valid, correct-answer demonstrations are kept (2,000 instances), fine-tuning each backbone for 2 epochs and yielding the frozen reference π_ref.
3. **Cost-aware policy optimization.** Reward R(τ) = R_perf − λ_C·R_cost with R_cost = log2(1 + C(τ)/B(q)) against a per-domain budget B(q) calibrated as the 25th-percentile successful-trace cost + 15% margin (e.g., 12,374 units for math). The weighted cost penalty is capped at c_max = 0.1 so successful trajectories always score ≥ 0.9. Group-relative policy gradients use 16 rollouts per question (batch 8, lr 3e-6), regularized by a token-level KL penalty to π_ref.
4. **Insight-guided on-policy distillation (OPD).** An expert (DeepSeek-V4-Pro) periodically refines the insight pool I_k+1 = F(I_k, B_k) — inducing rules, tightening preconditions to resolve conflicts, and pruning under a hard capacity ceiling (|I| ≤ 8 entries, ≤ 6,144 characters). For each student trajectory, the frozen teacher (Qwen3-32B or Llama-3.1-70B) receives privileged information z_i = (I, P_i, o_i, s(τ_i)) and supervises the student's own `<analysis>` and `<plan>` tokens via reverse KL (analysis weighted at α = 0.25). Distillation is supervised by the latest insights, closing the loop: policy → trajectories → insights → denser supervision → policy.

The updated policy then generates new trajectories for the next insight update. Training runs on 8×A100 80GB; accuracy is averaged over 3 independent runs, and compute cost is a relative-FLOPs proxy (2·active-parameters·tokens, normalized so the smallest worker model's per-token cost = 1 unit).

## Results & Highlights

- **In-domain accuracy (19 datasets, 5 domains):** EvoRouter-4B reaches 68.69% ±0.95 average accuracy vs 61.46% for the strongest baselines MasRouter and RCR-Router (+7.23 pp); EvoRouter-8B hits 71.23% and EvoRouter-LLaMa-8B 71.15%. Category highlights: Multimodal 64.10% vs MasRouter's 56.41% (+7.69 pp), Math 61.15% vs DAAO's 58.46%, Knowledge 72.31% vs RCR-Router's 71.79%; the one loss is Commonsense (88.46% vs RCR-Router's 90.38%).
- **Compute efficiency:** EvoRouter-4B cuts mean MAS execution cost by 50.95% vs the strongest baseline to 15,280.79 relative-FLOPs units; including router overhead, total inference cost is 18.94k units — 76.5% below MasRouter and 39.2% below RCR-Router — placing it clearly on the accuracy–cost Pareto frontier (Figure 5).
- **Out-of-domain generalization:** on LiveCodeBench + BBH-LD, EvoRouter variants score 74.67%/76.80%/78.93% average, beating the best baseline by +7.74 to +12.00 pp; on BBH-LD reasoning, EvoRouter-8B reaches 96.50% vs 86.50% for MAS-Orchestra/RCR-Router, and EvoRouter-4B reaches 94.00%.
- **Ablations (EvoRouter-4B, in-domain):** full model 68.69% vs 64.54% w/o Insights, 63.23% w/o OPD, 59.77% w/o RL+OPD, 53.23% for SFT alone (which also costs 25,472 units vs 15,281 — full training improves accuracy +8.92 pp in-domain / +11.47 pp OOD while cutting cost 9.0%/13.9%). OPD without insights adds only +1.31/+1.06 pp over RL, whereas insight-guided OPD adds +5.46/+4.80 pp — the insights are what make distillation actionable.
- **Zero-shot generalization to unseen resources** (swapping in Qwen3.8-Flash, Kimi-K2.8-Preview, Qwen3.8-Max, Self-Consistency, Hierarchical, and fresh skills): EvoRouter-4B beats MasRouter by 3.85 pp in-domain and 7.47 pp OOD while using 6.7%/18.5% less compute, and beats the strongest OOD baseline by 5.9 pp with less than half its compute; vs its own w/o-Insights variant it gains +5.77 pp in-domain and +11.47 pp OOD.
- **Evolution dynamics:** over 10 insight iterations EvoRouter-LLaMa-8B climbs from 63.85% to 71.15% success — early updates add +2.62 pp while cutting compute 16.4%; later refinements add another +1.35 pp at 7.4% lower cost. In the case study (Figure 10) EvoRouter answers a math problem correctly at 17.06K FLOPs vs MasRouter's 89.82K (−81.00% cost).
- **Cost-penalty sensitivity:** sweeping λ_C ∈ {0, 0.025, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2} monotonically shifts the router to cheaper configurations and suppresses the expensive tail of the per-question compute distribution, with domain-dependent accuracy sensitivity.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Good but incremental-combinative rather than conceptually new. The ingredients — experience/rule distillation (RSI-Router, Skill-MAS, EvoRoute), on-policy distillation (GKD/MiniLLM line), and cost-aware group-relative RL (MasRouter, xRouter, ReCal) — all exist; the contribution is wiring them into one recursive loop where distilled insights *condition the teacher's* token-level supervision, which is a genuinely novel coupling and is well ablated (+5.46 pp from insight-guided OPD vs +1.31 pp from plain OPD over RL).
- **Rigor — weak/mismatched baselines:** Baselines lacking native model selection are pinned to GLM-5, and some look broken in this harness: Router-R1 averages 36.15% in-domain — far below even vanilla Qwen3.5-35B-A3B (46.23%) — which suggests the single-agent multi-turn baselines were not well adapted rather than genuinely that bad. The headline "+7.23 pp over the strongest baseline" rests on MasRouter/RCR-Router both landing at exactly 61.46%.
- **Rigor — small eval sets and rounding:** the in-domain test split is only 1,300 questions and OOD is 175 LiveCodeBench + 200 BBH-LD questions; means are rounded to the nearest integer count of correct predictions before normalization, which coarsens fine-grained comparisons. The eye-catching 94–96.5% BBH-LD numbers come from just 200 questions.
- **Cost metric is a proxy:** cost = active-parameters × tokens, with attention omitted (justified as <5% of FLOPs) and inter-agent communication overhead, latency, and parallelism ignored — the latter matters most for exactly the multi-round Debate/FullConnected modes the configuration space centers on, so real end-to-end savings could differ. The per-domain budgets B(q) that shape EvoRouter's reward are invisible to the baselines.
- **Missing ablations:** no study of the insight-pool hard caps (|I| ≤ 8 entries, 6,144 chars — what knowledge is pruned away?), no teacher-strength comparison (only Qwen3-32B / Llama-3.1-70B / DeepSeek-V4-Pro as expert), and hyperparameter sensitivity is swept only for λ_C and only on the 4B backbone.
- **Reproducibility caveats:** the worker pool consists of proprietary API models (Qwen3.5 series, GLM-5, DeepSeek-V4-Flash) whose active-parameter counts are approximated from provider reports; exact replication therefore depends on API access and pricing-independent behavior. Code release and 3-run variance reporting partially mitigate this.
- **Overclaiming check:** the paper is mostly honest — it concedes losing Commonsense to RCR-Router and frames gains as "four of the five" in-domain categories. The abstract's numbers match the tables.

## Strengths

- Cleanly identifies and empirically demonstrates the three structural facts (task dependence, saturation, combinatorial interaction) that make joint MAS routing hard, and builds the insight-distillation mechanism directly on top of them rather than treating them as motivation-only.
- The insight-guided OPD design is well motivated: sparse terminal rewards cannot supervise coupled pre-execution decisions, and conditioning a privileged teacher on evolving insights converts cross-trajectory experience into dense token-level signal — the ablation isolates this effect convincingly.
- Strong and consistent cost story: 50.95% cheaper MAS execution than the strongest baseline while 7.23 pp more accurate, with the gain persisting end-to-end after router overhead (18.94k total units) and under full resource-pool replacement.
- Thorough evaluation engineering: 21 datasets, 16 baselines across 5 families, 3 seeds with stds, unseen-resource zero-shot tests, λ_C sensitivity with cost-distribution analysis, a public case study, and open-source code.

## Limitations

- The FLOPs proxy omits attention cost, inter-agent communication, and wall-clock latency, so "cost" here is compute-token based, not deployment cost; debate-style configurations may be systematically mispriced relative to real serving.
- Small test splits (1,300 in-domain; 175 + 200 OOD) with integer rounding limit the resolution of cross-method comparisons, especially on OOD where several baselines are within a few points of each other.
- Training depends on strong proprietary models at three points (bootstrap SFT router, insight expert, OPD teacher), so the "self-improving" loop is really improvement mediated by much larger frozen teachers; the loop's ceiling is bounded by the expert's distillation quality, which is not separately evaluated.
- The insight pool is capped at 8 entries / 6,144 characters with no analysis of what is pruned or how performance scales with capacity; λ_C sensitivity covers only one backbone.
- Scoped to cloud agent services; the conclusion itself acknowledges edge/heterogeneous deployment (communication overhead, device constraints) as future work.

## Relevance

这篇论文正对应"LLM 路由 / 多智能体系统 / 高效推理"方向：它把路由从单模型选择扩展到模型×协作模式×智能体数×技能的联合配置，用洞察引导的 on-policy 蒸馏加成本感知 RL 同时优化准确率与计算成本。对任何关心 agentic 服务中成本-质量权衡（test-time compute 分配、MAS 编排）的工作都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of the insight-guided OPD gain survives with a weaker (e.g., same-size) teacher — is the +5.46 pp delta over plain OPD driven by the insights or simply by a 32B/70B teacher's prior competence?
- What happens when the insight pool capacity (|I| ≤ 8, 6,144 chars) is scaled up or down — is there a performance/context-cost Pareto curve for distilled knowledge, and do pruned rules cause failure modes on rare task types?
- Would the learned policy transfer if the cost metric included real inter-agent communication and latency (which penalize multi-round Debate/FullConnected modes), or would its preferred configurations shift toward cheap sequential ones?
- Can the recursive loop keep improving beyond 10 iterations, or does it saturate/oscillate as the policy distribution drifts away from the trajectories the insights were distilled from?
- Since Router-R1 and other sequential baselines collapse in this harness, how do the conclusions change when multi-turn baselines are given equal tuning budget (e.g., same rollouts, same budget calibration for their cost rewards)?
