> **一句话总结：** 提出"杠杆率"(leverage)这一精确度量——用一次前向模式 JVP 计算权重更新的 Fisher 二次型，再除以同规模随机方向的同一量——发现各训练阶段相差四个数量级：预训练窗口在散文语料上 ≈1（随机水平）、SFT 约 5.5、RLVR/RL-Zero 为 388–1,508、DPO 高达 378–22,570，并证明纯 bf16 训练（无 fp32 主副本）会冻结 36–99% 坐标、把杠杆率压低至多 7 倍，还推导出 DARE 剪枝损伤定律 (1−p)/(p·ρsign)。

> **TL;DR:** The paper defines leverage ρ — the exact Fisher quadratic form of a weight-update direction (one forward-mode JVP per direction) divided by the same form for random directions of equal size at the same anchor — and applies it to released Olmo-3-7B, Tülu-3-8B, Zephyr-7B, and OLMo-2-1B checkpoints on a 51k-token mixed corpus (FineWeb-Edu/UltraChat/MATH/CodeParrot): pretraining windows read ρ≈0.78–0.95 (random level on prose), SFT 5.4–5.7, RLVR/RL-Zero 388–1,508, and DPO 378–22,570 — e.g., the Tülu-3 DPO delta reads 22,570 vs 0.86 for a 1M-step pretraining window, a ~26,000× gap. Re-running the released OLMo-2-1B DPO recipe reproduces the released delta within 1% displacement (8.52 vs 8.59×10⁻⁴) and 4% leverage (1,452 vs 1,447–1,504), while a plain DPO recipe reads 4.7–6.4× lower (237–311); pure-bf16 training freezes up to 99% of coordinates and cuts measured leverage up to 7.3×; and the sign-flip null predicts DARE drop-and-rescale damage as (1−p)/(p·ρsign) within ~2× wherever DARE succeeds.

## 核心贡献

- **定义并精确计算"杠杆率" (leverage ρ)**：将微调后的模型与锚点之间的 KL 散度做二阶展开，其系数即 Fisher 二次型 ∆ᵀF∆，用一次前向模式自动微分（Jacobian-vector product，约为 3 次前向传播的开销）精确计算，无有限差分法的浮点抵消底噪（float32 下约 3×10⁻⁸ nats/token）。ρ = 更新方向的曲率 / 同锚点、同规模随机方向的曲率，即"单位权重变化能买到多少倍于随机的行为改变"。
- **绘制了跨四个数量级的训练阶段谱系**：在 51k token 混合语料上，Olmo-3/Tülu-3/Zephyr/OLMo-2 的公开检查点中，预训练窗口 ρiso≈0.78–0.95、SFT 5.4–5.7、RL-Zero 388–1,508、DPO 后的 RLVR 410–665、DPO 378–22,570。
- **发现"预训练积累成噪声"**：30k 到 1M 步的预训练窗口在散文语料上不比同规模随机方向更 consequential（ρ≈0.6–1.5），窄窗口按 width^-0.5 到 ^-0.7 衰减到该平台；作者用 SGD 噪声二次模型（平稳协方差 Σ=(η/2B)I 各向同性）解释该平台。
- **揭示默认训练精度的测量偏差**：不带 fp32 主副本的纯 bf16 训练在低学习率下冻结 36–99% 的坐标（|θ|>256η–512η 的坐标永不更新），在匹配位移下杠杆率读数低 1.8–7.3 倍，并人为制造出 fp32 训练中不存在的"更新幅度—对角 Fisher 对齐"（幅度对齐升至 5–14）。
- **推导并验证 DARE 复用定律**：sign-flip 零假设给出随机 drop-and-rescale 在保留率 p 下的相对损伤预测 (1−p)/(p·ρsign)，在预测值低于约 0.5 处与实测（全尺度 KL 比值）吻合在 2.1 倍以内；可容忍损伤 ε 对应保留率 ≈1/(1+ε·ρsign)。
- **两个严格对照保证读数可靠**：所有带 fp32 主副本、标准损失的受控 SFT/DPO 轨迹杠杆率随位移衰减（SFT 按 rel∆W^-0.7 到 ^-1.7，DPO 按 ^-0.3 到 ^-1.2）；从发布 SFT 检查点用官方配方重跑 DPO（2 个种子）落在发布位移 1% 、杠杆率 4% 以内，留一法（leave-one-out）+ 恒定学习率对照把高杠杆率归因于长度归一化损失与 batch size 128 的共同作用（数据贡献约 2 倍）。

## Key Contributions

- **An exact instrument ("leverage")**: expands KL(π_{θ0+αΔ} ‖ π_{θ0}) to second order; the quadratic coefficient is the Fisher form ΔᵀFΔ, computed exactly per direction with one forward-mode Jacobian–vector product (torch.func.jvp, float32, TF32 off, ~3 forward-pass cost), avoiding the finite-difference cancellation floor (~3×10⁻⁸ nats/token in float32) that fabricates curvature for small displacements. ρ = c(Δ)/c(random null at the same anchor and size); three nulls (isotropic, per-tensor-norm "profile", and magnitude-preserving "sign-flip") separate cross-coordinate sign structure (ρsign) from magnitude alignment with the diagonal Fisher (c_sign/c_profile).
- **The released spectrum**: on a 51k-token mixed corpus (web/chat/math/code), released checkpoints span four orders of magnitude — pretraining windows ρiso 0.78–0.95 (rel∆W ≈ 0.37), SFT 5.4–5.7, RL-Zero 388–1,508, RLVR-after-DPO 410–665, DPO 378 (Zephyr) to 22,570 (Tülu-3); OLMo-2-1B DPO reads 1,452 and its RLVR 563, flat at 480–620 across 13 released RLVR checkpoints.
- **Pretraining accumulates into noise**: within Olmo-3 stage-1, windows ≥30k steps sit at the random level on prose (ρiso 0.6–1.5 on plain-math/WikiText/web/chat), after falling as width^-0.5 to ^-0.7 from narrow 814-step windows (3.9–15 on prose, 750 on math); Pythia-1.4B shows the same decay (5.0 at 1k → 0.58 at 16k steps). A noisy quadratic SGD model (stationary covariance Σ = (η/2B)·I, isotropic) predicts the ρ=1 plateau, and an Adam-style diagonal preconditioner predicts a plateau ≤1.
- **Pure-bf16 training distorts update geometry**: applying AdamW directly to bf16 parameters (no fp32 master copy) freezes 99% of coordinates at η=5×10⁻⁷, 90–91% at 5×10⁻⁶, 36–46% at 5×10⁻⁵ (coordinates with |θ| above 256η–512η never move); at matched displacement, pure-bf16 deltas read 1.8–7.3× lower leverage (SFT at rel∆W=2×10⁻³: 6.7–6.8 vs 31–32 for fp32-master) and their magnitude alignment climbs to 5–14 — an artifact absent from fp32 training.
- **A closed-form law for DARE reuse**: E[(m⊙Δ/p)ᵀF(m⊙Δ/p)] = ΔᵀFΔ + ((1−p)/p)·Σ Fii Δi², so relative damage ≈ (1−p)/(p·ρsign); verified at full scale on six released deltas (ρsign 0.63–1,012) at keep rates p=0.5/0.1/0.01 — within a factor of ~2 wherever predicted damage <0.5, with SFT-like deltas (ρsign ≲ 6) breaking DARE by p=0.1.
- **Two credibility controls**: (i) every controlled fp32-master SFT/DPO run loses leverage with displacement (SFT rel∆W^-0.99 to ^-1.65, DPO ^-0.26 to ^-1.21 at 0.5B and 7B; GRPO on GSM8K stays flat at 200–320); (ii) re-running the released OLMo-2-0425-1B DPO recipe (length-normalized DPO, β=5, batch 128, 2,955 steps) reproduces the released delta within 1% displacement / 4% leverage, while a plain recipe reads 4.7–6.4× lower — and leave-one-out plus a constant-rate control attribute the level jointly to the length-normalized loss and batch size 128 (data worth ~2×).

## Methodology

- **Exact Fisher quadratic form**: for direction Δ at anchor θ0, KL per position ≈ α²·Var_{p_t}(J_tΔ); the variance is the Gauss–Newton/Fisher form. One jvp call per 2,048-token window returns anchor logits and their directional derivative; q_t = ½(Σ_y p_y (J_tΔ)_y² − (Σ_y p_y (J_tΔ)_y)²), aggregated over all predicted positions. Curvature c(Δ) = E_t[q_t]/rel∆W² with rel∆W = ‖Δ‖/‖θ0‖.
- **Nulls at the delta's own anchor**: isotropic Gaussian draws (ρiso — the delta's Rayleigh quotient over the mean Fisher eigenvalue), profile draws matching per-tensor norms (ρprofile), and sign-flip draws keeping magnitudes and randomizing signs (ρsign — isolates cross-coordinate structure; the diagonal part). Magnitude alignment c_sign/c_profile measures how much squared magnitudes sit on high-F_ii coordinates.
- **Validation against finite differences**: where FD clears its floor (KL(0.1)>10⁻⁶, KL(0.1)/KL(0.03)∈[7,17]), FD at α=0.1 matches the exact form within 4% in 649/659 checks; where it fails, FD can overshoot by up to 72× (pure-bf16 DPO at 5×10⁻⁷).
- **Corpora**: mixed corpus = first 50k characters each of FineWeb-Edu web text, UltraChat-200k chat, Hendrycks MATH algebra, and CodeParrot code (51,167 tokens under the Olmo tokenizer; math+code carry 31k of 51k); plus a 700–900-token plain-math file and a 1,536-token WikiText-2 slice. Each corpus tokenized as one string with the anchor's own tokenizer, contiguous non-overlapping 2,048-token windows, tied embeddings counted once.
- **Controlled training**: Qwen2.5-0.5B (and 7B) SFT/DPO on UltraFeedback-binarized, batch 8 (and 128), constant/linear LR {5×10⁻⁷, 5×10⁻⁶, 5×10⁻⁵}, 600 steps (plus 10,000-step runs), fp32 master weights with bf16 autocast except the pure-bf16 arms; GRPO via TRL on 7,473 GSM8K problems (4 generations, β=0, 150 steps). Reproduction: the released OLMo-2-0425-1B DPO recipe (length-normalized DPO, β=5, LR 2.5×10⁻⁶ with 10% warmup + linear decay, effective batch 128, one epoch of 378,301 pairs, context 2,048) re-run from the released SFT checkpoint on 2×H100 (8.6 h/seed), plus single-knob variants and leave-one-out arms (3 data-order seeds each).
- **Released checkpoints measured**: Olmo-3-7B base/SFT/DPO/RLVR/RL-Zero (+13 within-stage-1 window endpoints from 814 to 1,000,814 steps back), Tülu-3-8B SFT→DPO→8B, Zephyr-7B mistral-7b-sft-beta→zephyr-7b-beta, OLMo-2-0425-1B SFT→DPO→Instruct (RLVR every 200 steps), Pythia-1.4B/160M stepN revisions.
- **DARE measurement**: KL between the DARE'd model and the unpruned fine-tuned model at full scale (not the quadratic form) divided by the delta's own KL, mean of two masks, at p ∈ {0.5, 0.1, 0.01}, for six released deltas spanning ρsign 0.63–1,012.
- **Preregistered extrapolation tests**: two prediction rules (quadratic radius α*, displacement threshold rel∆W<10⁻²) committed to timestamped repositories before any GSM8K/MATH-500 evaluation; blends θ+αΔ at α∈{0,1,1.5,2}, 4-shot greedy decoding.

## Results & Highlights

- **Released spectrum (Table 1, mixed corpus)**: pretraining windows ρiso = 0.78–0.95 at rel∆W ≈ 0.37; SFT 5.4–5.7; RL-Zero 388–1,508; RLVR-after-DPO 410–665; DPO 378–22,570. The Tülu-3-8B DPO delta reads 22,570 vs 0.86 (geometric mean) for pretraining windows — DPO moves weights ~10⁻³ of their norm yet is ~26,000× more consequential per unit size than pretraining's third-of-a-norm drift on prose.
- **Leverage is spent with displacement**: on Qwen2.5-0.5B (fp32 master, mixed corpus) SFT leverage falls as rel∆W^-1.29 / ^-1.38 / ^-0.99 at the three learning rates and DPO as ^-0.36 / ^-0.70 / ^-0.83 (12 checkpoints each); at rel∆W≈10⁻³ DPO reads 2–4× the SFT level. At 7B the same law holds at a higher level (SFT ^-1.46 and ^-1.19, DPO ^-0.26 and ^-0.73), and released 7–8B deltas sit above every controlled 7B curve (Olmo-3/Tülu-3 DPO 3–73× above).
- **Pretraining windows are noise-level on prose but not on math/code**: exact-form reads on Olmo-3 stage-1 give ρiso = 0.6–1.5 on prose for all widths ≥30k steps (geometric means 0.82 web, 0.72 chat over 30k–1M) but 3.5 math / 2.1 code; the 814-step window reads 750 on math and 322 pooled (median 5.2), with the top 1% of positions carrying 57–81% of the form at 814–7,814 steps vs 20% for nulls. Pythia-1.4B decays 5.0→3.0→1.9→1.1→0.58→0.65 from 1k to 43k steps.
- **The released level is reproducible**: two re-runs of the OLMo-2-1B DPO recipe land at rel∆W = 8.594/8.547×10⁻⁴ vs released 8.520×10⁻⁴ (0.3–0.9% gap), ρiso = 1,447/1,504 vs released 1,452 (≤4%), ρsign = 1,118/1,157 vs 1,012 (within 3% after bf16 rounding), and identical magnitude alignment 1.27. A plain recipe (sigmoid, β=0.1, UltraFeedback, batch 8) from the same anchor reads 237–311 — the released recipe is 4.7–6.4× higher at matched displacement.
- **Leave-one-out traces the level**: removing the released loss drops leverage 5.5–7.3×; removing the released data drops it 1.8×; removing the 2,048 context length changes nothing (within 4%). A constant-rate control pins the factor on batch size, not schedule: with the released loss+data at constant 2.5×10⁻⁶, batch 128 reads 2,236 (2,031–2,363) at rel∆W=4×10⁻⁴ vs released settings 2,711 (1.2×), while batch 8 reads 500 (5.4× below) — and at 0.5B with the plain loss, batch 128 had left the level unchanged, so the loss and batch size act together.
- **Pure-bf16 bias**: at η=5×10⁻⁷, 99% of Qwen2.5-0.5B coordinates never move (90–91% at 5×10⁻⁶, 36–46% at 5×10⁻⁵); fp32-master runs move 1.5–14× further; at matched displacement pure-bf16 reads 1.8–7.3× lower leverage (SFT at 2×10⁻³: 6.7–6.8 vs 31–32) and magnitude alignment 5–14 (vs 0.37–1.38 fp32), reversing the low-rate slope from falling to rising (+0.27, +0.56).
- **DARE law verified at full scale**: OLMo-2-1B RLVR (ρsign=474) loses 0.2%/2.0%/22% of its own KL at p=0.5/0.1/0.01 against predicted 0.2%/1.9%/21%; Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (ρsign=18.4) loses 4.8%/53% vs predicted 5.4%/49%; the OLMo-2-1B DPO delta reads a constant 1.75× its prediction at every p, matching the predicted endpoint-anchor Fisher scaling (1.68×). Wherever predicted damage <0.5, measurement tracks within 2.1×; SFT-like deltas (ρsign ≲ 6) break DARE by p=0.1.
- **GRPO/RLVR does not decline**: GRPO on GSM8K (150 steps, three LRs) holds 200–320 on the mixed corpus (slopes −0.27 to +0.25), mirroring the released OLMo-2-1B RLVR trajectory's flat 480–620 over 13 checkpoints — in contrast to every SFT/DPO run.
- **Extrapolation tests inconclusive (honest negative result)**: the preregistered α* rule scored 5/8 scorable deltas on GSM8K (p=0.36); the displacement rule scored 3/3 on GSM8K but only 1/5 on MATH-500 (p=0.969), pooled 2/6 — confidence intervals exclude little.

## Authors & Credibility

- Single author (Shikhar Shiromani), circulated as an unreviewed preprint ("Preprint", arXiv:2610.37719v1, 27 Oct 2026); no affiliation or prior-track-record evidence appears in the paper itself, so claims rest on internal methodological quality rather than institutional signal.
- The methodology is unusually transparent and self-critical: exact JVP instrument with code paths named (experiments/round4/r4_measure.py), finite-difference agreement checks (649/659 within 4%), draw-count and uncertainty reporting per anchor (24 draws at heavy-tailed Think anchors, ±10% standard error), disclosure of an excluded gradient-spike checkpoint (Table 6 note), and preregistered predictions committed to timestamped repositories before any benchmark ran.
- All evidence uses public checkpoints (Olmo-3, OLMo-2, Tülu-3, Zephyr, Qwen2.5, Pythia, DeepScaleR, ProRL, R1-Distill) and public data; the author states code and the three evaluation corpora will be released.
- Compute is modest and fully itemized: ~115 wall-clock / 135 GPU-hours for the exact-form campaign (A100/H100/B200/H200, 2×H100×8.6h per reproduction seed) plus 120–140 GPU-hours for earlier finite-difference campaigns — reproducible by academic labs.
- The related-work section distinguishes four concurrent ICLR-2027 submissions and prior sparsity analyses (Zhang et al. 2026; Miahi & Belilovsky 2026) precisely by what none of them do: exact Fisher form, anchor-matched nulls, displacement control.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The leverage instrument is a genuinely new combination — exact Fisher quadratic form + anchor-matched structured nulls (isotropic/profile/sign-flip) applied to released post-training deltas at controlled displacement. The DARE damage law (1−p)/(p·ρsign) is a clean closed-form contribution beyond prior first-order (Tang et al.) and per-layer bound (Deng et al.) analyses. However, the building blocks (directional sharpness, Fisher sensitivity, random-perturbation baselines) are all established, and four concurrent submissions compare trained vs random directions — this is a fast-moving niche where the exactness of the computation is the differentiator.
- **Rigor**: Strong on controls (seed repeats with ranges ≤9% TRL / ≤48% own-trainer, reproduction within 1%/4%, leave-one-out with a constant-rate control separating batch size from schedule, a truncation ladder testing bf16-storage sensitivity). Weak spots: (i) the "DPO = 400–23,000" band is a 60× spread (Zephyr 378 vs Tülu-3 22,570), so "stage signature" is loose for DPO and the paper itself concedes the level is not a fixed property of a stage; (ii) at Olmo-3 Think anchors a few chat positions dominate the nulls — sign-flip draws scatter by 45–75%, magnitude alignments carry ~20% uncertainty, and median-based reads (5,238 and 544) differ from the headline means (3,304 and 410) for rlvr_think; (iii) the leave-one-out runs only 600 of the recipe's 2,955 steps, and the minus-loss arm reverts length normalization and β=5 together without separating them.
- **Reproducibility**: Above average for a preprint: named code files, per-checkpoint revision IDs (e.g., allenai/Olmo-3 stage1-step1413814 anchor), full recipe details down to BOS handling and gradient-clipping assumptions, promised code/corpora release. The main caveat is the 7B controlled arms are one seed each and the reproduction covers a single 1B DPO stage — the generality claim across scales leans on thin replication.
- **Significance**: The bf16-no-master-copy finding (36–99% frozen coordinates, up to 7.3× leverage distortion) is immediately actionable for anyone measuring weight updates, and the finding that pretraining windows are Fisher-equivalent to noise on prose is a striking, quotable result. The DARE law gives practitioners a one-number (ρsign) tolerance rule.
- **Red flags**: (a) Single-author unreviewed preprint; (b) the headline mixed corpus is tiny (51k tokens) and domain-skewed (math+code = 31k of 51k pooled positions), and the paper shows the pooled pretraining read (0.8–12) diverges from its own median (0.7–1.0) because 1% of positions carry up to 72% of the form — the "prose plateau" claim survives, but pooled numbers are position-tail-sensitive; (c) the noisy-quadratic model "describes the decline with one free exponent" but fails on sub-diffusion (measured ∥Δ∥ ∝ w^0.35–0.42 vs predicted w^0.5), assumes fixed F and η over 1M steps, and leaves the math-text excess unexplained — it is a sketch, not a derivation of the plateau; (d) released deltas are bf16-quantized (26–79% of coordinates at their anchors), so ρsign/magnitude-alignment readings of the sparsest deltas are upper bounds, which the paper discloses but which tempers the cross-pipeline DPO comparisons; (e) the extrapolation section is a preregistered negative result — 2/6 pooled correct — which is honest but means leverage currently predicts DARE reuse well and delta extrapolation not at all.

## Strengths

- The exact forward-mode Fisher computation removes the finite-difference cancellation floor that silently corrupts small-displacement curvature measurements — a methodological upgrade the community can reuse (agrees with FD within 4% in 649/659 valid checks, and detects FD errors up to 72×).
- Anchor-matched, structure-preserving nulls (especially sign-flip) turn a raw curvature number into interpretable parts: cross-coordinate sign structure vs magnitude-Fisher alignment.
- The reproduction experiment is exemplary: re-running a released recipe from its released anchor and landing within 1% displacement / 4% leverage is the strongest possible evidence the instrument measures training, not storage or noise.
- Actionable practical warnings: pure-bf16 training freezes most coordinates at realistic learning rates and biases any update-geometry measurement; released-delta sparsity is partly a storage artifact (zeroing from 48%→75% raises alignment 1.26→1.65 with ρiso within 10%).
- The closed-form DARE tolerance (keep ≈1/(1+ε·ρsign)) converts an empirical merging heuristic into a predictive law verified at full-scale KL.
- Honest reporting throughout: preregistered failed predictions, excluded spiking checkpoint disclosed, uncertainty quantified per anchor, model limitations stated alongside the model.

## Limitations

- Small evaluation corpora (51k-token mixed, 766-token plain-math, 1,536-token WikiText) with heavy-tailed per-position forms — pooled means are sensitive to a few positions, and the mixed corpus weights math+code at 31k of 51k tokens.
- Seed coverage is thin where it matters most: 7B controlled runs are one seed per arm; GRPO runs are only 150 steps; the reproduction covers one released DPO stage at 1B only.
- The leave-one-out covers 600 of 2,955 steps of the released recipe, and the minus-loss arm conflates length normalization with β=5; the constant-rate control tests a second learning rate only at batch 8, not batch 128.
- The noisy quadratic model explains the prose plateau's approach but not the measured sub-diffusive displacement growth (w^0.35–0.42 vs w^0.5), not the math/code excess, and assumes stationary F and η over windows of up to 1M steps.
- Released deltas are stored in bf16 (26–79% of coordinates bit-identical to anchors), making sign-structure and alignment reads of the sparsest deltas upper bounds rather than training-time truths.
- The extrapolation-tolerance results are statistically inconclusive (CIs exclude little; most deltas unscorable because anchors already match endpoints on GSM8K), and format drift at 1.5× breaks the few-shot answer extraction before the arithmetic does.
- Leverage is a second-order, Gauss–Newton-only quantity: it cannot see directions of negative loss curvature, and says nothing about task quality (gain vs damage) without an external benchmark.

## Relevance

论文提出的 leverage 工具直接刻画 SFT/DPO/RLVR 各后训练阶段在 Fisher 几何下的权重更新效率，并量化了 bf16 纯精度训练与 DARE 模型合并的失效边界——与 LLM 后训练（偏好优化、RLVR）和权重空间分析（模型合并、delta 复用）方向高度相关，对设计可复用/可合并的 post-training 配方有直接指导意义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Why does DPO produce the highest leverage (up to 22,570) among post-training stages — is it the preference-loss geometry, the tiny displacement, or properties of the length-normalized objective, and does RLVR-after-DPO reading 5–10× lower reflect competition between KL control and preference gradients?
- Does the pretraining-is-noise plateau (ρ≈1 on prose) hold for other lineages and at larger scale, and does the math/code excess (ρ up to 25) mean pretraining writes real structure only on in-distribution/reasoning-like text?
- Can leverage be monitored cheaply during training (the JVP costs ~3 forward passes) as an early-stopping or merge-readiness signal, e.g., stopping when ρ falls below a target instead of at a fixed step count?
- How does the (1−p)/(p·ρsign) DARE law interact with structured sparsification (TIES-Merging sign election, BitDelta binarization) rather than uniform Bernoulli masking?
- Would the bf16-freezing effect (36–99% frozen coordinates) also distort downstream task gains, not just measured geometry — i.e., do pure-bf16 post-training runs underperform fp32-master runs at matched displacement on real benchmarks?
- Does the "leverage is spent with displacement" law predict when further fine-tuning of an already-moved model stops buying behavior change, connecting to the observed flatness of GRPO/RLVR trajectories?
