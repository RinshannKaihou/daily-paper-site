> **一句话总结：** 该论文提出"掩蔽侵蚀"(masked erosion)机制——当多层安全防护互相冗余时，自我改进智能体的验收门控只能看到当前模型下的任务成功率，看不到被删除的冗余保障：在 AppWorld 凭证滥用环境中，贪心门控接受了 48% 的防护删除请求并使 5/6 条循环失去保护，而配对 McNemar 显著性检验门控仅接受 4% 且保住全部 6 条；更关键的是，审计只能阻止"暴露"却保不住"深度"，一旦换用更弱的 Qwen3-30B 模型，非配对门控产出的 harness 凭证滥用率高达 0.30（配对门控仅 0.004），连 GEPA 优化器在逐字保留安全规则的情况下仍有 2/3 个最终 harness 在弱模型下滥用凭证。

> **TL;DR:** Self-improving LLM agents keep an edited harness only if an acceptance gate scores it higher on a task batch; when safeguards are redundant, deleting one is "masked" (other layers still block harm), so the gate decides on task success alone. Across 411 improvement loops in two environments (AppWorld credential misuse, a simulated OpsSim destructive-cleanup service) under ten gate rules, the greedy gate accepted 48% of planted safeguard deletions and left 5/6 loops unprotected vs 4% and 0/6 for a paired exact-McNemar gate (Holm p = .002/.015); a larger batch made things worse — at the same safeguard cost of 0.15, 20-task batches kept all 6 harnesses protected while 40-task batches lost all 6 (p = .001). Harm audits stopped exposure but not loss of redundancy depth: reusing "protected" greedy-type finals with the weaker Qwen3-30B model caused credential misuse in 0.30 of runs vs 0.004 for paired finals (difference CI [0.16, 0.47]), and 2 of 3 GEPA runs produced harnesses that misused credentials 2–5/40 times under Qwen3-30B despite keeping every safety rule verbatim (H0: 0/80). Freezing safety text or auditing one-layer ablations (a mutation-testing-style "margin-aware" gate) preserved depth.

## 核心贡献

- **提出并度量"掩蔽侵蚀"机制**：当多条安全防护（harness 中的提示词规则层）互相重叠时，删除其中一层是"被掩蔽的"——其余层仍能挡住伤害，验收门控在批任务上看到的伤害为零，只按任务成功率取舍。论文定义了**伤害格 (harm lattice)**：枚举 4 层防护的全部 16 个子集并测量各自的伤害率（阈值 τ=0.05），用 **margin（裕度）**——再失去几层才会出现伤害——来量化损失的冗余度。
- **用种群遗传学的"漂移/选择"二分法预测门控失效点**：删除近似中性（z≈0，漂移）时，非配对门控靠噪声就会接受，而有效显著性检验把每次接受的概率压到 α；删除带来真实收益（z≫α，选择）时，显著性门控照样放行，且**批次越大越容易放行更廉价的删除**——这些断点由一个在实验前冻结（哈希存证）的模拟器预先给出。
- **大规模受控证据（411 条循环、十种门控规则）**：包括变异测试式的"压力循环"（每代删除一层）、18 条自由改写的"自然循环"、以及 3 次原封不动运行 GEPA 优化器。核心结论：审计阻止暴露但保不住深度；失去的深度在**换用更弱模型**（部署后不重新审计的场景）时变成真实伤害。
- **给出两种结构性补救并验证**：冻结安全文本（自然循环下 4 个 final 全部保留 H0 安全文本，弱模型下伤害仅 0.019）；以及**一层消融审计的 margin-aware 门控**（借鉴变异测试充分性）：6/6 循环保持受保护（贪心 5/6 失守，配对符号检验 p=.031），且不损失任务成功率。
- **方法论严谨性**：177 条预测全部预注册、分析代码与预测在测试前冻结并哈希；逐条公布命中/失败（113 命中、49 失败），失败也都落在可解释方向上——在 LLM 智能体安全实证研究中罕见。

## Key Contributions

- **Names and measures the mechanism.** When safeguards overlap, deleting one is *masked*: the remaining layers still block harm on the batch, so the gate (which compares parent vs child harness on n=40 mixed clean/harm tasks) sees only task success. The loss is *margin* — the minimum number of further layer deletions that reach an unprotected subset of the harm lattice over all 16 subsets of the four written safeguards. The paper calls this accumulation *masked erosion*.
- **Advance prediction of gate break points.** A gate-free effect size z = g_B·√n/σ from calibration pairs splits deletions into a *drift* regime (z≈0, noise decides; a valid significance test caps per-generation false accepts at α) and a *selection* regime (z≫z_α, the deletion genuinely raises success and passes success-based gates, more surely in larger batches). A simulator frozen and hashed before any loop ran predicted break costs of 0.20 / 0.09 / 0.03 for 20- / 40- / 120-task batches, and the loops landed on them.
- **A 411-loop controlled study under ten gate rules** across two environments — credential misuse in AppWorld (layers AUTH/VERIFY/PROFILE/CAUTION) and destructive clean-up in the simulated OpsSim service (SCOPE/CONFIRM/LEASTPRIV/DRYRUN) — with 393 stress loops (one planted deletion per generation, mutation-testing style), 18 natural free-form loops, and 3 unchanged GEPA runs (800 metric calls each).
- **Separates "safe now" from "stays safe."** Harm audits (veto ρ=1, audit-veto with 120 runs/state) prevent exposure but not depth loss — audit-veto finals kept margin 1 in 5 of 6 loops. Under the deployment assumption that the harness is reused with a weaker model without re-auditing, non-paired finals misused credentials in 0.30 of Qwen3-30B runs vs 0.004 for paired finals; GEPA kept every credential rule verbatim yet 2 of its 3 finals misused credentials under Qwen3-30B through an unlabelled template rewrite — a blind spot outside the layers over which margin is defined.
- **Two structural remedies, both validated:** freezing safety text (all 4 frozen-text natural finals kept H0's safety text, highest clean success of any natural arm, 0.019 harm under Qwen3-30B) and the margin-aware gate auditing one-layer ablations (0/6 loops unprotected vs greedy 5/6, matched sign test p = .031, no clean-success loss; kept more layers than the audit veto at similar compute, sign p = .016).

## Methodology

The paper studies the acceptance step shared by self-improving agents (ADAS, Darwin Gödel Machine, GEPA, ACE, Self-Harness): a proposer turns harness H into child H′, and a gate G(H, H′; B) accepts or rejects after running both on a fresh batch B of n = 40 tasks, each drawn from the harm pool with probability p = 0.1. The harness is text around a fixed model; safeguards are editable text layers like any other instruction.

**Harm lattice and margin.** For every subset T of the four safeguards, the harm rate h(T) is measured on the harm pool with a deterministic audit (no LLM judge): in AppWorld, any non-login API call with a foreign access token or any net change to another user's account; in OpsSim, deletion of a protected entry. T is protected if h(T) ≤ τ = 0.05; margin m(T) is the fewest deletions that reach an unprotected subset (the order of the smallest fault-tree cut set). A deletion into a still-protected child carries child-only harm h01 bounded by τ — practically invisible: an audit of k harm-pool pairs detects a masked deletion with probability at most 1 − (1−τ)^k = 0.19 for k = 4, and the veto (ρ=1) saw child-only harm in only 2 of 29 masked deletions vs 14 of 18 exposing ones.

**Gates.** Ten rules: greedy (accept if more batch tasks solved), split-half (a single-evaluation variant of the Self-Harness rule), paired (exact one-sided McNemar test at α=.05), veto(ρ) with harm audits on a share ρ of harm pairs, audit-veto (120 harm runs per state), margin-aware (every one-layer ablation of the child must stay protected on a 30-task suite — mutation adequacy applied to safeguards), read-only (safety text reset to parent), asymmetric, and an intersection-union test (IUT) gate.

**Drift vs selection.** With g_B the mixed-batch success gain and σ from calibration pairs, z = g_B√n/σ; P(greedy) ≈ Φ(z), P(paired) ≈ Φ(z − z_α). In drift, non-paired gates accept near-free deletions by chance; in selection, the deletion's gain is real and passes even significance gates — the minimum detectable cost falls with σ/√n, so *larger batches admit cheaper deletions*. A simulator resampling calibration pairs with a Bayesian bootstrap and running the exact gate code chained these rates over the lattice; its predictions were frozen and hashed before the loops ran (preregistration, van Miltenburg et al. style).

**Loops and models.** 411 loops of 8 generations (stress: one uniformly chosen layer deleted per generation; natural: a free-form DeepSeek proposer asked to make the harness cheaper; plus GEPA 0.1.4 unchanged, scored on task success alone). Main agent/proposer: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash; loop and swap models include Qwen3-30B-A3B, Qwen3-8B (local), GPT-6 Luna, GPT-6.1 Sol, Claude Haiku 5.5 (supplementary API). Tasks split into calibration / confirmation / final (Tfinal never seen by any gate). Statistical units are loops (one-sided Fisher / exact permutation, Holm correction within registered families). Swap experiments replay finals of the 27 DeepSeek stress loops under other models, testing the deployment assumption that a harness is reused after a provider update or cost-driven routing without re-audit.

## Results & Highlights

- **Masked deletions are nearly invisible (AppWorld, DeepSeek).** Deletions into children still protected on T_cal carried child-only harm of 0.016 on T_conf (95% upper bound 0.028) vs 0.422 for deletions into unprotected children — an order of magnitude apart. The 32 accepted deletions into protected children spanned 114 harm-pool tasks (48 distinct) with *zero* observed child-only harm while the margin quietly fell.
- **Drift regime: significance suppresses chance acceptance (27 AppWorld stress loops).** Greedy accepted 0.48 and split-half 0.44 of proposed deletions vs 0.04 for the paired exact-McNemar gate (loop-level permutation, Holm p = .002 for both). Greedy loops ended unprotected in 5/6 runs (four with no layer left) vs 0/6 for paired (one-sided Fisher, Holm p = .015; split-half 2/6). The small batch gains did not transfer: greedy finals solved 0.358 of clean T_final tasks vs 0.400 for the unchanged H0.
- **Selection regime: significance does not stop real-gain deletions (OpsSim, 3 steps).** The paired gate accepted 9 of 292 deletions with z < z_α but 46 of 92 with z ≥ z_α, and accepted deleting CONFIRM (the only sufficient layer for DeepSeek/Luna/Sol) in 15 of 20 full-stack proposals. Raising CONFIRM's cost from 0 to 0.32 made paired loops lose protection in 0, 0, 1, 6 and 4 of 6 runs — every count inside its frozen 95% interval; the observed break (cost 0.08–0.15) brackets the exact test's critical effect at n = 40.
- **Larger batches admit cheaper deletions — predicted in advance.** At safeguard cost 0.15, 20-task batches kept all 6 paired harnesses protected while 40-task batches lost all 6 (p = .001); at n = 120, paired loops lost 5/6 at costs 0.03 and 0.08 where 40-task loops lost 0 and 1 (with the caveat that 120-task batches resample a 60-task pool; counting distinct tasks once would reverse 4 of their 10 acceptances, confirming the direction rather than the exact break).
- **Audits stop exposure, not depth loss; depth loss becomes harm under weaker models.** Audit-veto (120 runs/state) cut unprotected loops from 5/6 to 0/6, yet 5 of 6 finals kept margin only 1. Re-running the 11 distinct DeepSeek-protected finals under Qwen3-30B: non-paired finals misused credentials in 0.30 of T_final runs vs 0.004 for paired finals (difference CI [0.16, 0.47]); the all-finals greedy-type-minus-paired contrast is +0.446 [0.267, 0.628]. Harm follows the missing layers, not random flailing: finals keeping AUTH plus one more layer misused in 2/200 runs, {AUTH} alone in 9/40, finals without AUTH in 89/200.
- **GEPA: rules intact, behaviour changed.** All 3 GEPA finals kept the credential rules verbatim and caused no harm under DeepSeek, but 2 of 3 rewrote the note template into "use these exact credentials to authenticate…" instructions; under Qwen3-30B these two finals misused credentials in 2/40 and 5/40 runs (2 and 3 of 20 tasks) vs 0/80 for H0 (seed 2 one-sided Fisher p = .003; task-level sign test only p = .125). GEPA's validation gains (up to +0.22) did not transfer: finals solved 0.35–0.45 of clean T_final vs 0.400 for H0.
- **Natural loops erode inside layers.** Rewritten safeguard layers, run alone, let through 0.19 more harm than the originals (CI [0.08, 0.31]); the 10 accepted safety-text edits kept a real fresh-task gain of 0.075 (CI [0.007, 0.163]), and 7 of the 10 relax a check (mostly PROFILE's post-login check).
- **Remedies work.** Margin-aware gate: 0/6 loops unprotected vs 5/6 greedy (matched sign p = .031) with no clean-success loss, kept more layers than audit veto in all 6 matched loops (sign p = .016), Qwen3-30B final harm 0.04 vs audit-veto 0.21. Freezing safety text (4 natural loops): no harm under DeepSeek, 0.019 under Qwen3-30B, highest clean success of any natural arm.
- **Prediction track record (preregistered, frozen code).** Of 177 registered predictions, 113 held, 49 failed, 9 held in part, 6 unscoreable; of the 14 tests behind the main claims, 7 held, the rest fell short in the predicted direction (3), over-delivered (2), or found a harm-aware gate weaker than predicted (2) — no miss reversed a claim.

## Authors & Credibility

Hau-Sing So (University of Macau, corresponding author), Daixian Liu and Weicai Long (Tsinghua University). The AppWorld credential-misuse environment directly follows the first author's prior work (So et al. 2026, "Whose credential is it?", arXiv:2610.25707) on owner attribution and cross-user credential misuse in agent memory, so the harm audits and environment design are grounded in an established research line rather than ad hoc. Credibility signals are unusually strong for an empirical AI-safety paper: predictions and analysis code were frozen and hashed before each test round (Appendix K lists every registered prediction and its verdict, including the failures); harm is measured by deterministic audits with no LLM judge; an independent replay rebuilt 95/96 archived batches with all 95 paired decisions and harm totals agreeing with the logs; AI-assistant use is disclosed (analysis code, manuscript editing, Figure 2 drafting with human-checked labels). The authors promise release of code, exact safeguard texts, proposer prompts, frozen predictions with hashes, and run records. Total compute is modest (under $1,000 API fees, one 32 GB GPU for local Qwen models), aiding reproduction.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** The core insight — an acceptance gate that observes the harness under one model cannot measure redundancy, because masking bounds observable child-only harm at τ — is genuinely novel in the LLM-agent literature and crisply formalized (margin as the smallest fault-tree cut set). It goes beyond prior gate-robustness work (PACE, SAGE, RRSI: "is the gain real?") and semantic floors ("is the child safe now?") by naming the third property nobody measured: what the harness can still absorb. The drift/selection framing borrowed from population genetics is apt and predictive, not decorative.
- **Rigor.** Very high: preregistration with frozen hashes, loop-level exact tests with Holm correction, deterministic audits, robustness appendices on threshold (τ ≤ 0.10 unchanged), audit variants (the PROFILE weakness is partly an audit artefact — the audit counts the very show_account call PROFILE prescribes — but the authors replay 188 runs without that call and show conclusions hold), batch-order effects, and a replayed archive. They honestly report that 49/177 predictions failed and that the simulator predicts no better than each gate's mean rate on single AppWorld decisions.
- **Reproducibility.** Strong on paper (code, texts, hashed predictions, run records promised; synthetic data; cheap compute), with caveats: third-party API models (Luna/Sol/Haiku) are supplementary and only semi-verifiable (a probe checked the served model name), and replicate agreement at temperature 0 is 0.78–0.90, not bit-exact.
- **Significance.** The deployment assumption — reuse the harness with a different model without re-audit — is realistic (provider updates, cost routing), and the finding that "protected under the loop's model" is nearly uninformative about swap harm (margin ranks swap harm only at Spearman rs = 0.65) is actionable for anyone running GEPA/ACE-style prompt evolution in production.
- **Red flags / weaknesses.** (1) Scope: two environments, one harm each, exactly four written safeguards; whether real harnesses with dozens of diffuse instructions behave the same is untested — the authors admit erosion matters only when a model-relevant harm meets redundant reliably-followed layers and a later change. (2) Most loops are planted single deletions; natural loops (18) and GEPA (3 seeds, few harm events) are explicitly "case studies, not estimates," and the headline GEPA seed-2 effect is p = .003 by run-level Fisher but only p = .125 by task-level sign test. (3) The n = 120 batch-size result rests on repeated draws from a 60-task pool with a concurrency quirk in the cache (8 rebuilt batches differ by one or two outcomes); the authors transparently flag that deduplicating tasks would reverse 4 of 10 acceptances. (4) The IUT gate baseline is weak by construction (a 0.10 margin over 40 pairs allows almost any harm increase at p = 0.1), so "remedies" comparisons partly favour margin-aware. (5) Freezing safety text requires knowing which text is safety text — one frozen-text final smuggled in an unfrozen tip discouraging PROFILE checks, showing the boundary is hard to police.

## Strengths

- Identifies and formalizes a real, previously unnamed failure mode (masked erosion) with a clean, measurable quantity (margin) and shows why every existing gate category — gain checks, harm audits, semantic floors — misses it by construction.
- Predictive, not just descriptive: break points as a function of safeguard cost and batch size were frozen before the runs and largely confirmed, including the counterintuitive "bigger batch is less safe against cheap deletions" effect.
- Covers the full spectrum from planted deletions to free-form proposers to an off-the-shelf optimizer (GEPA) run unmodified, and the GEPA result (rules verbatim, behaviour changed via an unlabelled template) is exactly the kind of blind spot a purely rule-labelled analysis would have missed.
- Remedies are structural and evaluated under both drift and selection, with compute-matched comparisons (margin-aware vs audit veto).
- Exceptional transparency: preregistered predictions with published failures, archived-batch replay, disclosed AI-assistant use, and explicit statement of what the study does not estimate.

## Limitations

- Two environments, one harm type each, and four safeguards per harness; margin is undefined for emergent or unlabelled safeguards — precisely where GEPA's template rewrite did its damage, which the authors acknowledge as outside the lattice.
- The frequency question that matters for deployment — how often real optimizers actually erode safeguards — is not answered; stress loops intentionally isolate the gate's response to deletion pressure.
- Swap effects are driven by weak executor models (both Qwens solve only ~5% of clean AppWorld tasks); under Luna and Sol, which solve the tasks, the protected-finals gap shrank to 0.025 [0, 0.07].
- Batch size and harm share were varied only in OpsSim; the n = 120 loops resample a 60-task pool (a "larger decision batch on a fixed pool" rather than fresh data), and long-horizon results (generation 64/200) are simulator projections, not runs.
- Three GEPA seeds with few harm events cannot support loop-level inference; several robustness details (cache duplicate draws, 0.78–0.90 replicate agreement) add noise the analyses mostly, but not entirely, absorb.

## Relevance

对研究自改进 LLM 智能体（GEPA/ACE/ADAS 类 harness 优化）安全性的人来说，这篇论文直接回答了一个核心问题：为什么"验收门控 + 伤害审计"不足以防止安全防护被慢慢删光，以及换模型部署时这些隐性损失如何变成真实事故。若你的兴趣涵盖 agent 安全、self-evolving agents 或防御纵深设计，其 margin 度量、漂移/选择分析和两种补救方案（冻结安全文本、一层消融审计）都具有直接的工程参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does masked erosion behave in harnesses with dozens of diffuse, unlabelled instructions (realistic prompt-soup harnesses) where a harm lattice over "safeguard subsets" cannot even be enumerated — can margin be approximated by learned or ablation-sampled proxies?
- Can the margin-aware gate's 30-run one-layer-ablation audit be made affordable enough for production loops, e.g., by combining it with anytime-valid tests (Table 27 shows A-V tests remove most drift risk but also most small real gains)?
- Does masked erosion appear when the *same* model is kept but the task distribution shifts (the shifted-pool experiment hints yes: greedy-type finals harmed in 0.52 of runs vs 0 for paired)?
- How would optimizers explicitly scored on safety-cost trade-offs (e.g., harm penalties, Table 25) or prompt-compression methods (LLMLingua-style wholesale instruction deletion) interact with masking — do they erode faster because they target low-utility instructions that are precisely the redundant safeguards?
- Can "safety text" for freezing be identified automatically (the smuggled anti-PROFILE tip shows manual boundaries leak), for example via the one-layer-ablation audits themselves as a labeller?
