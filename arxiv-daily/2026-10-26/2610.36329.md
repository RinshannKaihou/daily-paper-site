> **一句话总结：** 普林斯顿团队用一个开销仅约 0.9% 单核 CPU 的 100 ms 粒度 DCGM 监控工具，对 319 块 A100 的大学集群（10 天、7,399 个作业）做了细粒度画像：整体 SM 利用率仅 29%、显存利用率仅 16%，70% 以上作业显存占用不足 20%、一半作业平均 SM 利用率低于 5%，且 34.17% 的作业撞到用户申请的时限——说明在硬件与应用之间的"系统层"（调度与多路复用）存在巨大回收空间。

> **TL;DR:** A measurement study of a 319-GPU (89-node, A100) Princeton research cluster using a custom 100 ms-resolution DCGM-based monitor (open-sourced), analyzing 7,399 jobs from 150 users over 10 days in fall 2024. Versus prior NVML-based traces (Philly, Helios at 100-second sampling; AntMan at ~1 s), 100 ms "SM Activity" telemetry exposes what nvidia-smi hides — per-job SM utilization is overstated by up to ~100x by nvidia-smi — and shows aggregate cluster SM utilization of only 29% (GPU memory 16%) despite a median ~2-hour queue for single-GPU jobs (~2 days for multi-GPU). >70% of jobs use <20% of GPU memory, 34.17% of jobs hit their requested time limit, and multi-GPU jobs show periodic, predictable communication (e.g., a 16-GPU job idles 1 s per 7 s cycle, implying ~21 packable GPU-hours per 12 h). No new scheduler is built — the paper quantifies headroom for GPU multitasking and runtime prediction.

## 核心贡献

- **100 ms 粒度的集群级 GPU 监控工具**：基于 Nvidia DCGM 的 "SM Activity" 指标（而非只报告"是否有 kernel 在跑"的 nvidia-smi），以极低开销（单核 ~0.9% CPU、~1.5 GB/天/节点存储）在生产集群上长期部署，代码开源。
- **揭示 nvidia-smi 的系统性高估**：nvidia-smi 只统计 kernel 占用周期的比例、不计 SM 部分占用——只启动 108 个 SM 中 1 个的死循环 kernel 也会被报为 100% 利用率；实测单作业高估可达约 100 倍，动摇了以往基于 NVML 的集群利用率研究结论。
- **三大实证发现**：(1) 严重欠利用——集群整体 SM 利用率 29%、显存利用率 16%，70% 以上作业显存峰值低于 20%，一半作业平均 SM 利用率低于 5%；(2) 申请时长与实际时长普遍严重错配——约 80% 作业错配超过 2 倍，34.17% 撞时限； (3) 多 GPU 作业通信高度周期化、可预测，同步空隙可被"填充"其他任务。
- **指出"中间层"机会**：效率提升不必只靠更快的硬件（底层）或更好的应用（顶层）——调度与 GPU 多路复用（MIG/MPS/UVM 配合 100–200 ms 级时间片）这一系统中间层即可回收大量闲置算力。
- **覆盖 12 个学科的真实混合负载**：不同于 Philly/Helios/AntMan 等只研究深度学习负载的工作，这是首个（据作者所知）对整个多学科科研集群做 100 ms DCGM SM 级指标采集的研究。

## Key Contributions

- A lightweight (few-hundred-line C, SQLite-per-node) open-source monitoring tool that samples DCGM SM-activity, memory, occupancy, Tensor-core, PCIe and NVLink metrics at 100 ms — the finest DCGM supports and the length of a Linux round-robin time slice — with only ~0.9% of one CPU core and ~1.5 GB/day/node storage, deployed for months on a production cluster.
- A systematic demonstration that the ubiquitous nvidia-smi/NVML utilization metric overstates true SM usage by up to ~100x per job (it counts kernel-active cycles, not fractional SM occupancy — a 1-of-108-SM spinning kernel reads as 100%), invalidating fine-grained conclusions of prior NVML-based studies.
- Three headline findings on a 10-day, 7,399-job, 150-user, 12-department trace: (1) severe underutilization (29% aggregate SM, 16% memory); (2) ~80% of jobs misestimate their requested duration by more than 2x and 34.17% time out; (3) multi-GPU communication is highly periodic (17-minute phases, 10 s/60 s bursts, 7 s cycles), making interference predictable and idle troughs packable.
- The "room in the middle" thesis (riffing on Feynman/Leiserson): the systems layer — scheduling, runtime prediction, and GPU multiplexing (MIG/MPS/UVM with 100–200 ms time slices) — can recover idle capacity without new hardware or application changes.
- A workload characterization of a genuinely multi-disciplinary research cluster (chemistry 38.2% of reserved GPU-time, chem-bio 22.7%, plus 10 other departments), contrasting with prior DL-only cluster studies.

## Methodology

The authors built a standalone C daemon per host that polls DCGM (plus /proc) at 100 ms into a 600-sample buffer (<2 MB) and flushes ~24k rows (~1 MB) to a per-node SQLite database every 60 s; SLURM job metadata is joined hourly. Per-node databases make the system robust to host failures (aggregation is done offline). The design goals were minimal overhead, operational simplicity, fine-grained metrics, and scalability — which convinced Princeton IT to deploy it on a production research cluster of 89 nodes / 319 A100 GPUs (20 nodes with 2x A100 PCIe 40 GB; 69 nodes with 4x A100 SXM4 80 GB; 100 Gb/s InfiniBand). They validate the metric choice with a microbenchmark (an infinite-loop kernel launched on a variable number of the A100's 108 SMs) showing nvidia-smi reads ~100% regardless of SM count while DCGM tracks true fractional usage, and then measure the per-job overestimation distribution on real trace data. The analysis uses a clean 10-day window (fall 2024) where job-ID-to-GPU-ID mappings were fully instrumented: 7,399 jobs from 150 users across 12 departments. Analyses include CDFs of job durations, queue times, average SM utilization and peak memory by duration class; requested-vs-actual duration ratios; completion-state and fully-idle-job breakdowns; sampling-rate comparisons (10 s vs 1 s vs 100 ms) for SM and NVLink traces; and three case studies of multi-GPU communication periodicity (neuroscience, psychology, and a 4-node/16-GPU job inferred to be AI training, cross-referencing NVLink, PCIe, and InfiniBand traffic to attribute bottlenecks).

## Results & Highlights

- On the 10-day, 7,399-job trace of the 319-GPU cluster, aggregate GPU SM utilization is 29% and GPU memory utilization 16% (CPU also ~17-21%) — even though the median single-GPU job waits ~2 hours in queue and multi-GPU jobs wait a median ~2 days: heavy queueing coexists with heavy idleness.
- Per-job headroom: over 70% of jobs use <20% of GPU memory and 50% average <5% SM utilization; only 10% of single-GPU jobs ever exceed 50% average SM utilization (33% among >8-hour jobs); only 5% of jobs ever reach 75% of GPU memory (10% exceed 90% among long jobs) — multitasking has ample memory headroom.
- Duration misestimation: only ~20% of the 7,399 jobs request a duration within 2x of actual runtime; 34.17% hit their requested time limit (timeout), vs 60.70% completed, 3.15% failed, 1.99% canceled; 2.82% of all reserved GPU-time went to fully-idle jobs (2.15% in >8-hour fully-idle reservations), including 1,043 sub-minute jobs.
- nvidia-smi overestimation vs the DCGM baseline: per-job mean overestimation spans 0x to ~100x across the real workload (Fig. 1b) — prior NVML-based utilization figures can be inflated by orders of magnitude for lightly-occupying jobs.
- Sampling granularity matters: at 10 s or 1 s sampling, deep sub-second troughs and NVLink bursts (up to ~25 GB/s spikes) visible at 100 ms are smoothed away, which would mislead a scheduler into thinking a GPU is busy.
- Multi-GPU periodicity: a neuroscience job repeats a ~17-minute communication phase with ~1/minute bursts; a psychology job bursts at ~10 s and ~60 s intervals; a 4-node/16-GPU (likely training) job saturates InfiniBand at ~100 Gb/s in 2-second windows and idles 1 s of every 7 s cycle with memory flat at 50% — extrapolating to ~21 packable GPU-hours over its 12-hour run.
- Footprint of the tool itself: ~800 µs per sample loop on a 4-GPU node, ~60 ms per 60 s flush, ≈0.9% of one CPU core, ~1.5 GB/day/node storage, negligible GPU overhead.
- 95% of jobs request a single GPU (multi-GPU/multi-node jobs take 26% of allocated GPU-time), consistent with users avoiding multi-GPU queues that are ~24x longer in median.

## Authors & Credibility

Andrew Sheinberg and Samyak Gupta (co-first authors) and Kai Li are from Princeton University's Computer Science department. Kai Li is a highly distinguished systems researcher (ACM Fellow, known for distributed shared memory, RAID work, and co-founding Data Domain). The work was enabled by Princeton Research Computing staff (acknowledged by name), and the deployment ran on the university's production cluster with IT approval — strong evidence the overhead claims are real. The monitoring tool is open-sourced on GitHub. The study was conducted in fall 2024 and the authors are transparent that hardware and related-work comparisons reflect that period. This is a credible, well-scoped measurement paper from a top systems group rather than a vendor benchmark.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Incremental but real. Fine-grained DCGM monitoring itself exists (Microsoft's Moneo supports it; MIT SuperCloud sampled at 100 ms), and the headline "GPU clusters are underutilized" conclusion echoes Philly/Helios. The genuinely new parts are (a) cluster-wide 100 ms DCGM *SM-level* metrics on a *multi-disciplinary* (non-DL-only) cluster, and (b) quantifying how badly nvidia-smi inflates per-job utilization on real workloads. The Feynman/"room in the middle" framing is attractive but the paper only *measures* the room; it builds no scheduler or multiplexer to show any of it can actually be reclaimed.
- **Rigor:** The trace is solid for aggregate claims but thin for the third finding: the periodicity conclusion rests on three hand-picked case studies (one neuroscience job, one psychology job, one EE job "likely doing AI training") out of only 343 multi-GPU jobs and 7 multi-node jobs — no systematic frequency/periodicity analysis across all multi-GPU jobs, no confidence intervals, and the "~21 hours of packable GPU-computation" figure is a linear extrapolation assuming zero interference from co-located tasks, which synchronization-sensitive distributed jobs may not tolerate.
- **Reproducibility:** The monitor is open-source and parameters (100 ms, 600-sample buffer, SQLite schema) are fully specified, so the *tool* is reproducible; but the dataset itself is not stated to be released, the cluster's job mix is institution-specific, and the department-level results depend on inferring affiliation from user groups.
- **Significance:** Useful motivational evidence for the GPU-multiplexing community, with concrete numbers (34.17% timeout rate, 2.82% fully-idle GPU-time) that scheduling and runtime-prediction work can cite. However, a single 10-day window at one university in fall 2024 (semester-time workload, pre-LLM-boom A100s) limits generalization to industrial AI clusters where large gang-scheduled training jobs dominate.
- **Red flags:** The key experimental limitation — precise job-to-GPU mapping existed for only 10 of "several months" of collection — is disclosed but shrinks the effective dataset substantially; the two GPU types (PCIe 40 GB vs SXM4 80 GB, different interconnects) are pooled into single percentage statistics; and some timeout jobs are acknowledged to be intentional interactive/checkpointed sessions, slightly muddying the "users misestimate" narrative.

## Strengths

- Production-grade, honestly-priced instrumentation: ~0.9% of one CPU core and ~1.5 GB/day/node for 100 ms, multi-metric telemetry across 319 GPUs for months — a deployment achievement most academic monitors never reach.
- Chooses the right metric: DCGM "SM Activity" over nvidia-smi, with a crisp microbenchmark (1 of 108 SMs reads as 100%) plus a real-workload overestimation distribution (up to ~100x) that methodologically undercuts prior NVML-based utilization studies.
- Multi-disciplinary workload (12 departments, 150 users) — a refreshingly realistic setting compared to DL-only production traces.
- Actionable, quantified findings for three distinct research communities: schedulers (34.17% timeout rate, 2.82% idle GPU-time), GPU-multiplexing (>70% of jobs under 20% memory, stable memory profiles), and networking/communication (periodic, predictable NVLink/InfiniBand patterns).
- Unusually candid limitations section (sampling ceiling, 10-day clean window, only 7 multi-node jobs, pooled heterogeneous GPUs).

## Limitations

- 100 ms is DCGM's ceiling, yet kernels run at microsecond-to-millisecond scale and interconnect counters are aggregated over the 100 ms window — short bursts may still be smoothed; Nsight-scale profiling is infeasible cluster-wide.
- Only 10 days of clean job-GPU mapping; seasonal/semester effects and long-term drift are not covered.
- Only 343 multi-GPU and 7 multi-node jobs, so the periodicity findings are case studies rather than a statistical characterization; domain-specific workload behavior is aggregated away.
- Two different A100 variants (PCIe 40 GB, SXM4 80 GB with different interconnect bandwidths) are merged in percentage-based statistics.
- No intervention: no scheduler, co-location experiment, or simulation demonstrates that any of the measured headroom is actually recoverable without violating isolation or slowdown bounds.
- Single university cluster from fall 2024; transferability to LLM-era industrial clusters (large gang-scheduled training jobs, H100/B200, faster interconnects) is untested.

## Relevance

对关注 GPU 集群调度、资源利用率与 LLM 训练基础设施的人来说，这篇论文提供了稀缺的 100 ms 粒度真实集群画像数据，直接支撑 GPU 复用（MIG/MPS/时空分片）、运行时长预测调度等方向的动机与设计输入，也为自建集群的监控选型（DCGM vs nvidia-smi）给出了明确依据。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of the measured headroom can actually be reclaimed? A follow-up should co-locate real jobs on the idle troughs (e.g., via MPS with 100–200 ms time slices) and report interference/slowdown against the naive packing estimate (e.g., the ~21 GPU-hours figure).
- Can requested-duration mismatch be predicted online from fine-grained early signals (first 60 s of SM/memory/communication profile), and does replacing user estimates with predictions measurably cut queue times?
- How general is the communication periodicity finding — what fraction of all 343 multi-GPU jobs exhibit statistically detectable periods, and does periodicity survive on H100/B200 clusters with NVSwitch and collective offload (NCCL/SHARP)?
- Does the nvidia-smi overestimation distribution (0x–100x) shift on newer GPUs and DL-dominated workloads, and should schedulers treat DCGM SM Activity as the standard input signal?
- Would fine-grained detection of fully-idle reservations (2.82% of GPU-time, some >8 hours) justify a policy that revokes or backfills idle allocations after a grace period, and what user-behavior side effects would that cause?
