▸ Key metric

3.5×
fewer adapter parameters
Training LoRA on 8 of 28 layers (top ~30%, picked in 20–62 s) beats full-layer LoRA / LoRA+ / DoRA at every rank r4–r32 — r16 commonsense average 59.1% vs LoRA's 56.9%.

▸ Why this matters

Turns mechanistic-interpretability tools (path patching, ablation) from analysis toys into a signal that drives a practical decision: which layers to adapt.

▸ Critical edge

Main tables are single-seed (seed 42) — per-task fluctuations match the +1–2 pt headline gains — and the advantage vanishes at 8B–32B (+0.0 / −0.2 pts): the win is a small-model phenomenon.

No Random-30% baseline in the main table, so "smart selection" vs plain sparsification is never disentangled.

02 Methodology — Adaptive-LoRA

Three phases; selection happens on the frozen base, before any training

1

Calibrate — gradient-free, N=30 inputs

For each layer, independently zero the attention and MLP outputs and accumulate the top-token probability shift Iₕ += |q − qᵣᴺ| + |q − qᵣᳬ|. Costs O(N·L) forward passes — 20–62 s on one A100 (4.1–6.3% of training time).

2

Select — top-k layers, k ≈ 0.3L

Rank layers by importance score; keep the top ~30%. An optional "efficiency mode" restricts to layers l ≥ d* so the frozen prefix is reusable across epochs.

3

Train — standard LoRA, selected layers only

Q/V targets (motivated by the V-dominance finding), AdamW, lr 1e-4, r=16, α=32, 3 epochs, batch 64. Same rank & targets → adapter params shrink by L/k = 28/8 = 3.5×.

The diagnosis half trains full rank-8 Q/K/V adapters and measures each component's causal contribution by activation patching, Sₖ(x) = pLoRA(t*|x) − pablate(t*|x); frozen-base scores later correlate with post-training contributions at ρ = 0.630–0.893.

04 Strengths

  • Causal grounding for a heuristic decision — path patching / zero-ablation decides which layers to adapt, instead of rules of thumb.
  • Extremely cheap selection — gradient-free, 30 inputs, 20–62 s on one A100, with measured score–target correlation and stability studies.
  • Honest convergence control — all selection strategies meet at PPL 5.75 at the full-layer budget.
  • Cross-family, cross-scale validation — six models, 0.5B–32B, Qwen + LLaMA, rather than a single case study.
  • Findings are independently useful — the 58.4/41.6 MLP/attention split and 39.5% task-overlap are reference points for adapter placement.
  • Candid limitations appendix (granularity, task coverage, theory, static selection).

05 Limitations

  • Layer-level granularity only — MLP dominance and V-concentration suggest component/head-level selection could save more; left as future work.
  • Narrow task coverage — 8 commonsense + 3 math multiple-choice; generative and long-context tasks untested.
  • Gains vanish or invert at 8B–32B (+0.0 / −0.2 pts) — practical payoff limited to the ≤3B regime.
  • Static selection — fixed before training; no re-calibration as importance drifts.
  • Single-seed main tables, and the mechanism itself is established on one 0.5B model only.
  • No theoretical account of early-layer dominance; no public code release.

06 Questions for further exploration

  1. Would component-level selection (only MLP + V in chosen layers) compound savings beyond the 3.5× layer factor, and at what accuracy cost?
  2. Why do frozen-base zero-ablation scores predict post-training contributions (ρ = 0.630–0.893) — causal, or both emphasizing high-norm early layers?
  3. Does early-layer concentration survive generative and long-context tasks (summarization, dialogue, document QA), where later layers plausibly matter more?
  4. How much of the +1.2–2.2 pt gain is selection quality vs regularization from sparsifying? A Random-30% row in Table 1 would disentangle it.
  5. Can calibration be replaced by a training-free proxy (norms, representation similarity), removing even the 30-input step?
  6. Would dynamic re-selection during training beat the static choice on distribution-shifting or continual-learning setups?
