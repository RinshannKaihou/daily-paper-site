> **一句话总结：** 提出 CoCHI 框架，把预测按"对/错 × 高/低置信度"分成四类，用双 L1 正则的因果头门控（CHG）找出因果性地驱动"高置信度错误"的注意力头，推理时仅置零 top-k（k=8–10）个头且不改任何参数；在 LLaMA-3.1-8B / Gemma-2-9B / Mistral-7B 三个模型、五个选择题基准上，ECE 相比温度缩放最多降低约 53%（Mistral-7B OpenBookQA：0.0673→0.0316），同时保持甚至提升准确率，校准产物仅 40 字节且零推理开销。

> **TL;DR:** CoCHI treats overconfident errors as a mechanistic problem rather than an output-level one: it partitions predictions into four correctness-by-confidence classes (e.g., wrong_high = overconfident errors), runs class-conditioned Causal Head Gating with dual L1 regularization to attribute behaviour to attention heads, then permanently zeroes only the top-k heads (k=10 for LLaMA-3.1-8B, k=8 for Gemma-2-9B/Mistral-7B) at inference. Across five MCQA benchmarks (MMLU, MedMCQA, OpenBookQA, MedQA-USMLE, SciQ) it cuts ECE versus Temperature Scaling — e.g., Mistral-7B OpenBookQA ECE 0.0673→0.0316 (−53%) and LLaMA-3.1-8B MedMCQA 0.0557→0.0365 — while matching or improving accuracy, needing only a one-time 39–88 s GPU calibration on ≤40 samples that yields a 40-byte, prunable mask with zero per-token inference overhead. Caveats: absolute ECE gains over TS are modest (0.008–0.036), the Thermometer/ATS baselines look under-tuned (NLL 1.4–4.2), and several CoCHI accuracy numbers carry large standard deviations (±0.06–0.11).

## 核心贡献

- **把校准重构为对模型内部结构的因果干预问题**：不同于温度缩放等只在输出层调整 logits 的后处理方法，CoCHI 直接定位并抑制导致"高置信度错误"（wrong_high）的注意力头，不改任何模型参数。
- **四类校准分解（calibration-class decomposition）**：按预测对错与置信度阈值 τc 把样本分成 correct_high / correct_low / wrong_high / wrong_low 四类，把"过度自信的错误"隔离为独立子集，从而能对每类做类条件因果归因，而不是笼统地看待失准。
- **类条件因果头归因 + 规范并集掩码**：在每类子集上运行因果头门控（CHG），双 L1 正则（λ=+0.1 / −0.1）分别得到 necessary / sufficient 分数，划分 facilitating / interfering / irrelevant 三种角色；掩码取 wrong_high 的 facilitating 头（H_err）与 correct_high 的 non-facilitating 头（H_noise）的并集，再按归因强度取 top-k。
- **极轻量的推理时干预**：一次性校准仅需 ≤40 个样本（每类最多 10 个）、39–88 秒 GPU 计算；产物是 40 字节的头索引掩码，推理时零额外计算（直接置零选中的头），且头可被永久剪除得到更小的模型。
- **系统性实验验证**：3 个开源模型 × 5 个选择题基准（MMLU、MedMCQA、OpenBookQA、MedQA-USMLE、SciQ），ECE / NLL / Brier Score 一致优于 TS、ITI、Thermometer、ATS 四个基线，并附消融、阈值敏感性、效率与逐类案例分析。

## Key Contributions

- **Reframes calibration as causal intervention on model internals**: instead of post-hoc logit adjustment (temperature scaling) or retraining, CoCHI identifies and suppresses the specific attention heads that causally give rise to overconfident errors, leaving all parameters frozen.
- **Four-class calibration decomposition**: predictions are partitioned by correctness and a confidence threshold τc into {correct_high, correct_low, wrong_high, wrong_low}, isolating overconfident errors (wrong_high) so causal analysis does not conflate them with other error types.
- **Class-conditioned causal head attribution**: applies Causal Head Gating (CHG, NeurIPS 2025) per class with a three-stage dual optimization — an unregularized λ=0 warm-up, a positive-L1 branch (λ=+0.1) yielding a necessity score, and a negative-L1 branch (λ=−0.1) yielding a sufficiency score — classifying each head as facilitating / interfering / irrelevant at threshold 0.5.
- **Canonical-union mask with top-k ranking**: the mask is the union of wrong_high facilitating heads (H_err, drivers of confident errors) and correct_high non-facilitating heads (H_noise, spurious confidence variability), ranked by r(h)=max(|s_nec|,|s_suf|) and truncated to a compact top-k (k=10 for LLaMA-3.1-8B; k=8 for Gemma-2-9B and Mistral-7B, chosen by grid search minimizing ECE subject to ≤1% accuracy drop).
- **Deployment-efficient artefact**: a one-time calibration on ≤40 balanced samples (≤10 per class) produces a 40-byte mask with zero inference overhead; masked heads are prunable, unlike ITI/Thermometer which add per-forward-pass computation.

## Methodology

CoCHI operates in five steps. (1) **Problem setup**: for a pretrained LM, prediction ŷ(x)=argmax p(y|x) and confidence c(x)=max p(y|x); overconfident errors are defined as ŷ≠y* with c(x)≥τc. (2) **Class decomposition**: each calibration sample is assigned to one of four classes {correct_high, correct_low, wrong_high, wrong_low} using τc=0.5. (3) **Class-conditioned causal head gating**: for each class subset Dc, continuous gate logits z_{l,h} are optimized (model frozen) with objective L = −NLL + λ·mean(clamp(z,−4,4)), where gates σ(z_{l,h}) multiplicatively scale head outputs. Optimization runs in three stages: shared unregularized initialization (λ=0), then two branches — positive-L1 (λ=+0.1, sparsity pressure, keeps only heads whose removal hurts → necessary score s_nec = σ(z⁺)) and negative-L1 (λ=−0.1, retention pressure → sufficient score s_suf = σ(z⁻)). Heads with both scores ≥0.5 are facilitating; s_nec<0.5 ∧ s_suf≥0.5 are interfering; the rest irrelevant. (4) **Canonical-union mask construction**: H_mask = H_err ∪ H_noise, where H_err = facilitating heads of the wrong_high class (they actively produce high-confidence wrong answers) and H_noise = non-facilitating heads of the correct_high class (they add confidence variability without supporting correctness). Heads are ranked by r(h)=max(|s_nec|,|s_suf|) and the top-k form the final mask. (5) **Inference-time intervention**: selected heads are zeroed (binary gating g_h∈{0,1}) during the forward pass — a global mask applied uniformly to all test inputs, requiring no labels or per-sample classification at test time. The method is plug-and-play: no parameter updates, no output post-processing, and the masked heads can be permanently pruned. Calibration uses ≤40 samples per dataset (up to 10 per class) in a zero-shot setting; the penalty coefficient was tuned across 15 model–dataset runs with a constraint of ΔECE<0.015 on the correct_high class.

## Results & Highlights

- **MMLU / LLaMA-3.1-8B**: CoCHI reaches ECE 0.0490 vs 0.0569 for Temperature Scaling (−0.008 absolute, ~14% relative), while *raising* accuracy to 0.6120 vs 0.6007 (+1.1 pts) and improving Brier Score to 0.1743 vs 0.1936; the learned calibrators do far worse here (Thermometer ECE 0.1717, ATS ECE 0.3265).
- **OpenBookQA / Mistral-7B**: ECE drops 0.0673 (TS) → 0.0316 (CoCHI), a 53% relative reduction, with accuracy 0.5120 vs 0.5100.
- **MedMCQA / LLaMA-3.1-8B**: ECE 0.0365 vs 0.0557 for TS (−34% relative) with accuracy 0.5773 vs 0.5753; on MedMCQA / Gemma-2-9B CoCHI simultaneously improves accuracy 0.5607→0.5873 (+2.7 pts) and ECE 0.0556→0.0380.
- **SciQ / LLaMA-3.1-8B (Table 6)**: CoCHI is best on all four metrics — accuracy 0.9020 vs 0.8790 (TS, +2.3 pts), ECE 0.0330 vs 0.0354, NLL 0.2266 vs 0.2377, Brier 0.0676 vs 0.0721.
- **Baseline instability**: ITI collapses under its fixed intervention strength — Mistral-7B MMLU accuracy falls to 0.3980±0.1152 (vs 0.5987 for TS) with ECE 0.3693; ATS NLL exceeds 1.0 on most runs (up to 4.2157 on Mistral-7B OpenBookQA) and Thermometer NLL ranges 1.4–2.9, indicating unreliable probability estimates from these reproductions.
- **Ablation (Table 2, MMLU/LLaMA)**: naive masking destroys the model — wrong-high-facilitating-only mask gives accuracy 0.2044/ECE 0.4745, broad canonical union 0.2189/0.3850, random size-matched 0.2111/0.4330 — versus CoCHI's top-k mask at 0.6120/0.0490, showing the compact top-k selection is doing all the work.
- **Sensitivity & efficiency**: τc=0.5 is the best threshold (ECE 0.0490) while τc=0.7/0.9 degrade ECE to 0.0705/0.0696; one-time calibration costs 69.9 s on 40 MMLU samples (39±21 s for Mistral-7B up to 88±23 s for Gemma-2-9B, peak GPU 28.1–46.2 GiB) and produces a 40-byte artefact with no inference overhead — vs ITI (16 KB artefact, O(k·d) per-forward cost) and Thermometer (1.0 MB, O(H·C)).
- **Case study (Fig. 2)**: beyond wrong_high, CoCHI also reduces ECE on correct_low subsets (~0.10 on MMLU, ~0.09 on MedMCQA), suggesting broader confidence stabilization.

## Authors & Credibility

The paper is from RMIT University (Melbourne): Jing Ren and Bowen Li are equal-contribution first authors, Ziqi Xu is corresponding author, and Feng Xia leads the group (project page: aisuko.github.io/confix). The team has a visible research trajectory on causality and reliability for LLMs — prior work on causality-aware calibration for RAG (WWW 2026), causal abstention for hallucination (AAAI 2026), and spiking graph predictive coding for OOD generalization (WWW 2026) — so the mechanistic-calibration angle is a natural continuation rather than a one-off. The paper is formatted for NeurIPS 2026 and the experimental design is unusually complete for a calibration paper: three open-weight model families, five public benchmarks, four baselines, three metric families, plus ablation, threshold sensitivity, efficiency, and per-class case studies, with implementation details (prompt format, data cleaning, k selection protocol) in the appendix. All models and datasets are public, and the key hyperparameters (τc=0.5, λ=±0.1, logit clamping to [−4,4], k per model, ≤40 calibration samples) are stated, aiding reproduction.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: combining class-conditioned causal head gating (built directly on Nam et al.'s CHG, NeurIPS 2025) with calibration is a genuine and interesting step — prior interpretability work located confidence circuits but did not use them to *improve* calibration. The core technical machinery (CHG, dual-L1 gating), however, is borrowed; the new contributions are the four-class decomposition and the canonical-union + top-k mask recipe, which are conceptually simple.
- **Rigor — the headline gains vs TS are modest**: against the only strong baseline (Temperature Scaling), absolute ECE improvements are small, e.g., −0.008 on MMLU/LLaMA (0.0569→0.0490) and −0.005 on MMLU/Mistral (0.0902→0.0854), and TS costs 0.5 s on CPU with a 4-byte artefact while CoCHI needs a 40–88 s GPU pass and 28–46 GiB peak memory. The impressive relative numbers (−53% on OpenBookQA) come from the low-TS-ECE settings; the paper's framing of "outperforming post-hoc methods" leans heavily on the much weaker learned baselines.
- **Suspicious baseline reproductions**: Thermometer (ECE 0.17–0.38, NLL 1.4–2.9) and ATS (NLL up to 4.2) perform *far* worse than their published behaviour — worse than no calibration at all on NLL — which suggests these baselines were not tuned for this zero-shot MCQA setting. Similarly, ITI is run with a fixed intervention strength α and collapses to 0.37–0.40 accuracy on LLaMA/Mistral; a tuned α would likely narrow the gap.
- **Large variance is under-discussed**: several CoCHI accuracy numbers carry huge standard deviations — 0.6120±0.0626 (MMLU/LLaMA), 0.9353±0.1069 (SciQ/Gemma), 0.6090±0.0766 (OpenBookQA/Gemma) — larger than some claimed accuracy gains over TS (±0.005 std), while TS/ATS stds are ~0.005–0.03. Statistical significance of the accuracy improvements is therefore questionable; the paper never addresses this asymmetry.
- **The mechanistic story is weakened by its own ablation**: masking *all* wrong-high facilitating heads collapses accuracy to 0.2044, i.e., those heads are largely shared with core task computation (generic capability heads) rather than a dedicated overconfidence circuit — the top-k truncation is effectively damage control. The paper does not report which layers the selected heads come from, whether they overlap across models/datasets, or whether they match the verbalized-confidence circuits of prior work, so the "causal mechanism of overconfidence" claim stays at the level of a useful intervention heuristic.
- **Missing generalization test**: the mask is built per model–dataset pair (40 in-distribution calibration samples; penalty tuned over 15 model–dataset runs), yet no experiment transfers a mask across datasets (e.g., calibrate on MMLU, test on MedMCQA) — so the claim that k captures "model-level calibration properties" is only weakly supported, and deployment on unseen distributions is untested.
- **Reproducibility**: good on paper (open models, public datasets, stated hyperparameters, project page), but the number of evaluation seeds, ECE binning scheme, and how baselines' hyperparameters were chosen are not specified in the main text.
- **Red flags**: none severe — no cherry-picked single-seed headline numbers, and the limitations section honestly flags the MCQA-only scope. The main concern is comparative fairness (weak Thermometer/ATS/ITI reproductions inflating the relative advantage).

## Strengths

- A principled mechanistic framing: calibration as an internal, causally identifiable property rather than an output-level symptom, with a clear taxonomy (four classes; facilitating/interfering/irrelevant heads).
- Extremely deployment-friendly artefact: 40-byte mask, zero inference overhead, prunable heads, and calibration from ≤40 samples — arguably the best efficiency profile among intervention-based calibration methods.
- Frequently improves accuracy *while* improving calibration (e.g., +2.7 pts on MedMCQA/Gemma-2-9B), which post-hoc scaling cannot do.
- Thorough experimental hygiene in scope: 3 models × 5 benchmarks × 4 metrics, plus ablation over five masking strategies, τc sensitivity {0.2–0.9}, k grid search {1..20} with an explicit accuracy-drop constraint, and cross-model calibration-cost measurements.
- Honest scoping in the limitations section (MCQA only; head-level granularity ignores MLP/residual-stream/cross-layer sources of miscalibration).

## Limitations

- Confidence is only defined over a fixed four-option answer space; open-ended generation, long-form reasoning, and multi-turn settings (where correctness/confidence are ill-defined) are explicitly out of scope.
- Intervention granularity is the attention head; miscalibration arising from MLP layers, residual streams, or cross-layer interactions is not addressed (acknowledged by the authors).
- Absolute calibration gains over a properly tuned Temperature Scaling are modest (typically 0.005–0.036 ECE), and the one-time GPU calibration (28–46 GiB peak) is far heavier than TS's 0.5 s CPU fit.
- High run-to-run variance in accuracy (std up to ±0.11) is reported but not analyzed; significance vs TS is unclear in several settings.
- No cross-dataset or cross-domain mask-transfer experiment, so generalization of the identified heads beyond the calibration distribution is unknown.
- The comparison to learned calibrators (Thermometer, ATS) and to ITI at fixed strength appears to use under-tuned baselines, inflating relative wins.

## Relevance

这篇论文把"大模型过度自信"问题从输出层后处理推进到注意力头层面的因果干预，与 LLM 可靠性、校准与机理可解释性的研究方向高度契合：它既提供了可控、可剪枝、零推理开销的实用校准手段，也为"置信度由模型内部特定回路驱动"这一机理假设提供了干预性证据，是连接 mechanistic interpretability 与可信 LLM 部署的代表性工作。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does a mask calibrated on one benchmark (e.g., MMLU) transfer to unseen datasets or domains, or is the identified head set distribution-specific? How stable is the top-k head set across calibration subsets and seeds?
- Which layers do the selected heads concentrate in, and do they overlap with the verbalized-confidence circuits reported in prior mechanistic work (Zhao et al. 2026)? Are H_err heads genuinely "error-promoting" or generic capability heads that top-k truncation merely doses safely?
- How much of the ECE gain survives against a *well-tuned* Temperature Scaling or Thermometer (with per-dataset hyperparameter search), and is the gain statistically significant given CoCHI's ±0.06–0.11 accuracy stds?
- Can the approach extend beyond four-option MCQA — e.g., to free-form QA with sampling-based confidence, or to selective prediction / abstention where reducing wrong_high errors directly improves coverage-risk curves?
- Would soft gating (retaining σ(z) values instead of binarizing to 0/1) or joint head+MLP attribution improve the calibration-accuracy trade-off further?
