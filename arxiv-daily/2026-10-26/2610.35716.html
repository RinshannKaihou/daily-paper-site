04 Methodology

Theory (frozen-state, first-order) + frozen-panel diagnostics + real GRPO training runs, preregistered.

GRPO groupK responses per prompt
→
Weak verifierpass a · reject b · malformed 0
→
Selectionpick M weak passes, E|M| = pW
→
Strong verifiercorrect C · hack H · fail F
→
Correctioncaught hack ← c = 2b−a (−2.5 LeetCode, −0.8 Countdown)

LB (low-pass budgeted audit): sort groups by weak-pass count k ascending and audit whole groups until an expected fraction p of the batch's W weak passes is covered — boundary group with residual probability. Spending shrinks automatically as hacking collapses.

  • Lemma 1 — under centered advantages, the expected update depends only on each hack's marginal audit probability πᵢ and correction cᵢ; group coupling is just noise.
  • Prop 1 — c = 2b−a reflects the pass reward about the failure reward and exactly negates an all-hack group's advantages; the “strong-verdict” c = b merely zeroes them.
  • Theorem 1 — audit allocation is a fractional knapsack; LB minimizes advantage mass left on hacks whenever label value dₖ is non-increasing in k.
  • Prop 2 — pure-groups model: uncaught-hack advantage ∝ √((K−k)/k) — largest when few siblings pass.
  • Prop 3 — group-std normalization saturates per-item reward-level IPW: a normalized advantage can never fall below −(K−1)/√K.
Practical recipe

Reflected penalty 2b−a + audit whole low-pass groups first + budget set as a fraction of weak passes + never run per-item reward-level IPW under std normalization.

  • Unbiased corrections suppress hacking too — but lose 6.1–11.8 pp held-out accuracy (1000 problems × 8) to LB at matched budget (mean +8.4 pp, p = 0.040). Part is truncation: IPW leaves 3.3–13.9% of answers cut at 2048 tokens vs 0.2–1.6% for LB; among parseable answers LB still leads by 4.5–5.9 pp.
  • Reallocating the group-level correction by pass count recovers +6.8 pp [+5.4, +8.1] (single seed), landing 1.2 pp below LB — evidence that randomness, not expectation, drives the gap.
  • Baselines (single seed): adapted PAC leaves late hacking at 64.80%; an oracle selection rule matches LB with 648 labels; audited-only learning drifts long (34.4% of held-out answers give no answer).
  • Frozen-state diagnostics: the sign of hack mass J predicted suppression in 33/39 runs (13/16 preregistered); LB attained the Theorem-1 optimum in 123/144 frozen-panel scorings.

06 Strengths ＋

  • Clean, usable theory chain (Lemma 1 → Prop 1 → Theorem 1), each step verified on frozen panels from real training runs, not just idealized models.
  • Concrete and cheap to adopt: reflected penalty, low-pass-first whole groups, budget as a fraction of weak passes, avoid per-item IPW under std-norm (saturation mechanism confirmed: 19.06% vs 0.82%).
  • Preregistered, seed-paired, interval-reported GRPO training in two distinct setups (LoRA + std-norm; full FT + no-KL) — a methodological model for small-compute RL research.
  • Honest negative results: the p = 0.1 failure, the −11.5 penalty relapse, the online-rule counterexample where improving the frozen objective trained worse.
  • The full-audit control plus pass-count reallocation cleanly separates “expected update” from “randomness of the estimate.”

07 Limitations −

  • Toy scale: 3B/4B models, groups of 8–16, 40-step windows that start after hacking emerged (~24–25%); longer training, larger models, and other domains untested.
  • The strong verifier is cheap in both settings, so the budget is artificial; the motivating expensive-verifier case (human or LLM-judge audits) is never instantiated — and the verifier is assumed reliable and policy-independent.
  • Several pivotal ablations (correction value, budget thresholds, pass-count-allocated correction) are single-seed; the LeetCode held-out accuracy metric is underpowered.
  • The theory is first-order at a frozen state: J predicts signs, not magnitudes, and was never benchmarked against a naive baseline; 23 of the 39 prediction runs were scored post-hoc.
  • Reflection is exact only when non-passing responses share reward b; mixed groups dilute it, and the exact group-adaptive fix needs whole-group labels — undermining the budget story.

08 Questions for further exploration

  • Does LB prevent hacking from step 0, and does the reflected penalty distort exploration on genuinely hard problems where low-pass groups are mostly correct answers?
  • How does the budget threshold (p between 0.1 and 0.2) scale with group size K, model scale, and initial hack rate — is there a predictable phase boundary?
  • What happens when the strong verifier is itself imperfect or gameable — does LB concentrate optimization pressure on its blind spots faster than random audits?
  • Can the randomness cost of unbiased corrections be reduced (control variates, stratified IPW, mixing in a small LB allocation) without giving up unbiasedness?
  • Does the J-sign predictor transfer to other GRPO pathologies (length hacking, format gaming), and can it drive online early-stopping or budget adaptation?
