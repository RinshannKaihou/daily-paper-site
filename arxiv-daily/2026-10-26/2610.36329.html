Why this matters

Rare 100 ms production-cluster evidence that directly motivates GPU multiplexing (MIG / MPS / time-slicing), runtime-prediction scheduling, and DCGM-over-nvidia-smi monitoring choices.

为 GPU 复用、运行时长预测调度与集群监控选型（DCGM 优于 nvidia-smi）提供直接的实证依据。

⚠ Critical edge

The paper measures the room but never reclaims it — no scheduler, co-location run, or simulation demonstrates the headroom is actually recoverable.

And the periodicity finding rests on 3 hand-picked case studies; 100 ms is DCGM's ceiling. → limitations

01Methodology

  1. Poll DCGM + /proc every 100 ms (per-host C daemon)
  2. Buffer 600 samples (<2 MB), flush ~24k rows / 60 s
  3. Store per-node SQLite — host failures don't lose data
  4. Join SLURM job metadata hourly · analyze offline

Deployment: months on Princeton's production cluster — 89 nodes / 319 A100s (20×2 PCIe 40 GB, 69×4 SXM4 80 GB), 100 Gb/s InfiniBand, with IT approval.

Metric validation: an infinite-loop kernel on 1 of 108 SMs reads ~100% in nvidia-smi; DCGM "SM Activity" tracks the true fraction — then per-job overestimation is measured on the real trace.

Clean window: 10 days in fall 2024 with full job→GPU mapping; tool costs ≈0.9% of one core and ~1.5 GB/day/node.

  • Queues coexist with idleness — hours of waiting alongside GPUs doing almost nothing.
  • Granularity is load-bearing: at 10 s / 1 s sampling, sub-second troughs and NVLink bursts (~25 GB/s) smooth away — a scheduler would read "busy".
  • Multi-GPU communication is periodic: 17-minute phases, 10 s / 60 s bursts, 7 s cycles across the case studies.

03Strengths

  • Production-grade, honestly-priced instrumentation — ~0.9% of one core, ~1.5 GB/day/node, months on 319 GPUs; few academic monitors ever deploy this far.
  • Right metric, crisp proof — the 1-of-108-SM microbenchmark plus a real-workload 0–100× overestimation distribution methodologically undercuts prior NVML-based studies.
  • Realistic workload — 12 departments, 150 users, not a DL-only trace.
  • Actionable for three communities — schedulers (34.17% timeouts), multiplexing (>70% of jobs under 20% memory), networking (periodic patterns).
  • Unusually candid limitations section.

04Limitations

  • 100 ms is DCGM's ceiling — µs-to-ms kernels and interconnect bursts may still be smoothed; Nsight-scale profiling is infeasible cluster-wide.
  • Only 10 clean days of job→GPU mapping; no seasonal or long-term coverage.
  • Periodicity = 3 case studies out of 343 multi-GPU / 7 multi-node jobs — not a statistical characterization.
  • Pooled heterogeneous GPUs — PCIe 40 GB and SXM4 80 GB variants merged into single percentages.
  • No intervention — nothing demonstrates the headroom is recoverable within isolation or slowdown bounds.
  • Single university, fall 2024 — transfer to LLM-era industrial clusters (H100/B200, gang scheduling) untested.

05Questions for further exploration

  • Recoverability: co-locate real jobs on idle troughs (MPS, 100–200 ms slices) and measure interference against the ~21 GPU-hour packing estimate.
  • Online duration prediction: can the first 60 s of SM / memory / communication profile replace user estimates — and cut queue times?
  • Generality of periodicity: what fraction of all 343 multi-GPU jobs show detectable periods, and does it survive NVSwitch / NCCL / SHARP?
  • Monitoring standard: does the 0–100× nvidia-smi overestimation shift on newer GPUs and DL-dominated loads — should schedulers standardize on DCGM SM Activity?
  • Idle-allocation policy: should fully-idle reservations (2.82% of GPU-time, some >8 h) be revoked or backfilled after a grace period — at what behavioral cost?
