Why This Matters · 为何重要

Quantifies each post-training stage's weight-update efficiency, plus the failure boundaries of bf16 training and DARE merging — a direct tool for reusable, mergeable post-training recipes.

直接刻画 SFT/DPO/RLVR 的更新效率与 bf16、DARE 的失效边界。

Critical Edge · 批判视角

The headline corpus is tiny (51k tokens) and domain-skewed — 1% of positions can carry up to 72% of the measured form, so pooled reads are position-tail-sensitive.

Also: single-author unreviewed preprint; the DPO band itself spans 60× (378–22,570), so it fingerprints the recipe, not the stage.

01 Methodology · 方法

1

Second-order KL expansion of KL(πθ₀+αΔ ‖ πθ₀) — the quadratic coefficient is the Fisher form ΔᵀFΔ.

2

Exact computation via one forward-mode JVP per 2,048-token window (torch.func.jvp, float32, ≈3 forward passes) — no finite-difference cancellation floor (~3×10⁻⁸ nats/token).

3

Anchor-matched nulls: divide by curvature of same-size random directions — isotropic, per-tensor “profile”, and magnitude-preserving “sign-flip”.

4

ρ = leverage: how many times more behavior change per unit weight change than a random direction at the same anchor.

  • Validation: agrees with finite differences within 4% in 649/659 checks where FD is valid; catches FD errors up to 72×.
  • Subjects: released Olmo-3-7B, Tülu-3-8B, Zephyr-7B, OLMo-2-1B, Pythia checkpoints; controlled Qwen2.5-0.5B/7B SFT/DPO and GRPO-on-GSM8K runs.
  • Corpus: 51,167 tokens of FineWeb-Edu / UltraChat / MATH / CodeParrot (math+code = 31k of 51k).
  • Recipe re-run: released OLMo-2-1B DPO (length-normalized, β=5, batch 128, 2,955 steps) on 2×H100, 8.6 h/seed; total ≈135 + 120–140 GPU-h — reproducible by an academic lab.

03 Strengths · 优点

  • Exact JVP instrument removes the finite-difference cancellation floor that silently corrupts small-displacement curvature — a reusable methodological upgrade (649/659 checks within 4%).
  • Anchor-matched structured nulls (isotropic / profile / sign-flip) turn a raw curvature number into interpretable parts: cross-coordinate sign structure vs magnitude–Fisher alignment.
  • Exemplary reproduction: re-running a released recipe from its released anchor lands within 1% displacement / 4% leverage — strongest evidence the instrument measures training, not storage or noise.
  • Immediately actionable: the pure-bf16 warning and the closed-form DARE tolerance p ≈ 1/(1+ε·ρsign) convert empirical folklore into predictive rules.
  • Honest reporting: preregistered failures disclosed, excluded checkpoint flagged, per-anchor uncertainty quantified.

04 Limitations · 局限

  • Tiny, domain-skewed corpora (51k tokens; math+code = 31k) with heavy-tailed per-position forms — pooled means are sensitive to a few positions.
  • Thin seed coverage where it matters most: 7B controlled arms are one seed each; GRPO runs 150 steps; reproduction covers a single 1B DPO stage.
  • DPO is not a stage signature: a 60× spread (378–22,570); the level tracks length-normalized loss + batch 128, and leave-one-out conflates length normalization with β=5.
  • The plateau model is a sketch: misses sub-diffusive growth (∥Δ∥ ∝ w0.35–0.42 vs w0.5), leaves the math/code excess unexplained, assumes stationary F and η over 1M steps.
  • Storage artifacts & scope: released deltas are bf16-quantized (26–79% of coordinates bit-identical), so sign-structure reads are upper bounds; leverage is second-order only — blind to negative curvature and silent on task quality.

05 Questions for Further Exploration · 待探索问题

  1. Why does DPO reach the highest leverage — preference-loss geometry, tiny displacement, or the length-normalized objective — and does RLVR-after-DPO reading 5–10× lower reflect KL control competing with preference gradients?
  2. Does the pretraining-is-noise plateau (ρ≈1 on prose) hold for other lineages and at larger scale? Does the math/code excess mean pretraining writes real structure only on in-distribution, reasoning-like text?
  3. Can leverage be monitored cheaply during training (JVP ≈ 3 forward passes) as an early-stopping or merge-readiness signal — e.g., stop when ρ falls below a target?
  4. Does the bf16-freezing effect (36–99% frozen coordinates) distort downstream task gains at matched displacement, not just measured geometry?
  5. Does the (1−p)/(p·ρsign) DARE law extend to structured sparsification (TIES sign election, BitDelta binarization) rather than uniform Bernoulli masking?
