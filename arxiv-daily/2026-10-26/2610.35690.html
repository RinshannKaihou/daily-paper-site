EvoRouter: Cost-Efficient Multi-Agent Routing via Insight-Guided On-Policy Distillation

Yitao Xiao · Xu Zhang · Ye Cheng · et al. (9 authors) | 2026-10-26 | code: github.com/CenYangtze/EvoRouter

LLM Routing Multi-Agent Systems On-Policy Distillation Reinforcement Learning Cost-Efficient Inference
Key metric
+7.23 pp

Average accuracy over the strongest baselines (MasRouter & RCR-Router, tied at 61.46%): 68.69% ±0.95 for EvoRouter-4B across 19 in-domain datasets. While cutting mean MAS execution cost −50.95% (15.28k vs ≈31.1k relative-FLOPs units).

Why this matters

Turns routing from picking one model into jointly configuring models × collaboration mode × agent count × skills — the cost–quality dial for anyone serving agentic workloads.

把路由从"选一个模型"扩展到多智能体系统的联合配置，直接服务于 agentic 服务的成本–质量权衡。

Critical edge

Router-R1 lands at 36.15% — below even a vanilla worker model (46.23%) — suggesting several baselines weren't adapted to this harness rather than genuinely that bad; the +7.23 pp headline rests partly on that comparison.

Methodology — the evolution loop
01
Route & execute
Router emits <analysis> + structured plan (mode, rounds, per-agent model/skill/subtask) over 5 LLMs × 6 collaboration modes × skills; executor runs it; verifier scores 0/1.
→
02
Distill insights
Expert LLM distills cross-trajectory performance–cost feedback into ≤8 conditional routing rules (induce → refine preconditions → prune).
→
03
Privileged on-policy distillation
Frozen teacher sees insights + outcomes and supervises the student's own analysis/plan tokens via reverse KL.
→
04
Cost-aware RL
Group-relative gradients, reward R = R_perf − λC·R_cost, penalty capped so success always scores ≥ 0.9.
⟲ recursive loop — updated policy → new trajectories → new insights → denser supervision → policy
  1. Motivating laws. Pre-experiments show MAS config returns are task-dependent, non-monotonic (past a peak, more rounds/agents lower success and raise cost), and combinatorial — an all-mα team wins under Debate at 79.8% fewer FLOPs, while all-mβ wins under FullConnected.
  2. Supervised cold start. 2,000 format-valid, correct trajectories from a frozen DeepSeek-V4-Pro bootstrap router; 2 epochs SFT per backbone.
  3. Cost-aware policy optimization. R_cost = log₂(1 + C(τ)/B(q)) against per-domain budgets (25th-percentile successful-trace cost + 15%); 16 rollouts/question, token-KL to the SFT reference.
  4. Insight-privilege is the novelty. The teacher conditions on the evolving insight pool — converting sparse terminal rewards into dense token-level supervision. Joint loss L = L_RL + λD·L_OPD + β·L_KL. At inference: no privileged feedback needed.

Out-of-domain & unseen resources
  • LiveCodeBench + BBH-LD: 74.67 / 76.80 / 78.93% avg (4B/8B/Llama-8B) → +7.74 to +12.00 pp over the best baseline; BBH-LD reasoning hits 96.50% (8B) vs 86.50%.
  • Zero-shot with entire model / collaboration / skill pools swapped: beats MasRouter by +3.85 pp in-domain, +7.47 pp OOD, at 6.7% / 18.5% less compute.
  • Evolution dynamics: 63.85% → 71.15% over 10 insight iterations; case study solves a math problem at 17.06k vs 89.82k FLOPs (−81%).
Strengths
  • Builds the mechanism directly on three demonstrated laws of the MAS config space (task dependence, saturation, combinatorial interaction) — not as motivation-only.
  • Insight-conditioned privileged teacher converts sparse terminal rewards into dense token-level supervision; the ablation isolates the effect convincingly (+5.46 vs +1.31 pp).
  • Cost advantage is end-to-end: survives router overhead (18.94k total, −76.5% vs MasRouter) and full resource-pool replacement.
  • Thorough evaluation engineering: 21 datasets, 16 baselines across 5 families, 3 seeds with stds, unseen-resource tests, λC sweep, open-source code.
Limitations
  • FLOPs proxy omits attention, inter-agent communication, and latency — multi-round Debate/FullConnected modes may be mispriced vs real serving.
  • Small test splits (1,300 in-domain; 175 + 200 OOD) with integer rounding coarsen close comparisons; the 96.5% BBH-LD figure rests on 200 questions.
  • The "self-improving" loop is really improvement mediated by much larger frozen proprietary teachers; the expert's distillation quality is never evaluated separately.
  • Insight pool hard-capped at 8 entries / 6,144 chars with no pruning analysis; λC sensitivity covers only the 4B backbone.
  • Scoped to cloud agent services; edge/heterogeneous deployment acknowledged as future work.
Questions for further exploration
  1. Does the +5.46 pp OPD gain survive a same-size teacher — driven by the insights, or by a 32B/70B teacher's prior competence?
  2. Is there a performance/context-cost Pareto curve for insight-pool capacity — and do pruned rules fail on rare task types?
  3. Would the learned policy shift toward cheap sequential configurations if cost included real communication and latency?
  4. Can the recursive loop keep improving beyond 10 iterations, or does it saturate as the policy drifts from its distilled insights?
  5. Do the conclusions hold when multi-turn baselines get equal tuning budget (same rollouts, same budget calibration)?
