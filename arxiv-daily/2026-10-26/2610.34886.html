Why this matters

Perplexity and multiple-choice metrics miss 2-bit damage to long chain-of-thought — here is a deployable scalar 2-bit recipe plus a reasoning-based selection protocol.

它系统论证了困惑度和选择题指标无法评估 2-bit 量化对长思维链推理的损伤，并给出一套可部署的标量 2-bit 方案（含基于推理的 checkpoint 选择协议）。

Critical edge — before you cite the headline

The flagship 73.0 was trained on a 2.1B-token reasoning-heavy mixture, while the EfficientQAT / BitDistiller baselines saw only the 805M-token base mixture — the top-line gap partly measures training data, not the quantization framework.

cleaner contrast: token-matched Rec-h2 46.7 vs 30.0 · no baseline QAT ever gets the reasoning traces

Methodology — five stages, strictly scalar

Every weight is an integer codeword from a tier-dependent grid (ternary {−1,0,1} / 2-bit / 3-bit) times one bf16 scale per 128-weight group — no codebooks, rotations, or zero-points; dequantization is a single multiply.

01

Allocate tiers

KronQ Kronecker-factored Hessian sensitivity per layer → ternary / 2-bit / 3-bit tiers solved as a knapsack via Lagrangian relaxation. At 2.0 bpw (1.7B): 75.3% ternary · 6.0% 2-bit · 18.7% 3-bit; only Q/K projections held ≥2 bits.

02

Search codewords (GSQ)

Block-wise search from a GPTQ start: Gumbel-softmax relaxation for ternary/3-bit layers, ReinMax second-order gradients for 2-bit. Worth +24.4 MATH-500 over a layer-wise start at the recalibrated checkpoint (47.2 vs 22.8).

03

Recalibrate scales

Codewords frozen; train only per-group scales + RMSNorm gains (<1% of parameters) against forward KL to the bf16 teacher. Scales move a median 6% — they fit the network output, not weight space (weight error even worsens slightly).

04

QAT

Straight-through estimator over FP32 latents; long QAT reassigns only 6.4% of codewords, each by one grid step. The +R variant retrains on a mixture with ~60% complete long reasoning traces — worth +26.3 MATH-500 by itself.

05

Select by reasoning

Paired sign-flip permutation test (20k sign vectors) on 313 MATH training problems, disjoint from MATH-500 — keeps the stronger reasoner without touching the test set.

eval: sampled strict MATH-500 (4 samples, T=0.6, ≤32k tokens, trace must close </think>) · control = 7-task likelihood mean · AIME@16 = pass@1 over 16 samples · all data decontaminated against benchmarks

Strengths
  • Asks the question that matters — can a 2-bit model still finish and solve long chains of thought — with strict </think> scoring, and splits failure into finishing vs solving.
  • Every stage justified by a paired statistical contrast; recalibration alone recovering 14.8 → 47.5 is explained by the output-fitting analysis.
  • Byte-fair comparison of the whole published ecosystem (Unsloth GGUFs, Bonsai, retrained EfficientQAT/BitDistiller) in one harness, bits/param reported per row.
  • Test-set-clean selection: 313 MATH training problems + sign-flip test; every selector decision agreed with MATH-500.
  • Deployable artifact: bit-exact packing, loads in stock transformers, real vLLM/H100 serving numbers.
Limitations
  • Qwen3-only (1.7B/8B/14B, thinking mode); the stage study is at 1.7B only — no Llama/Gemma/Mistral or MoE replication.
  • QAT+R conflates data mixture with framework: no baseline QAT method receives the reasoning traces.
  • Non-reasoning pays: HumanEval 25.6 vs bf16 41.5, GSM8K 58.7 vs 68.6; ≥4-bit files (Q4_K_M: 90.8 at 1.11 GB) remain clearly stronger.
  • Termination unsolved at small scale: 12–45% of 1.7B traces never close </think>; AIME@16 keeps only 12.3 of 41.4.
  • No native 2/3-bit kernels — speedups come from 4-bit containers; code unreleased at submission.
Questions for further exploration
  • Q1Would EfficientQAT or BitDistiller, given the same 1–2B-token reasoning-trace mixture, close most of the 73.0-vs-30.0 gap — framework or data?
  • Q2Do the likelihood-blindness findings replicate beyond Qwen3 — e.g. Llama-style hybrid or MoE reasoning models with different activation outliers?
  • Q3Could objectives that explicitly reward closing </think> (on-policy distillation, post-quantization RL) fix the 12–45% unfinished-trace rate?
  • Q4The allocator never picks 2-bit freely — would a cheaper 4th tier (binary) or learned rotations push below 2 bpw?
  • Q5Does the paired reasoning-test selector generalize to code and tool-use checkpoints (BFCL/IFEval), where it currently has no coverage?
