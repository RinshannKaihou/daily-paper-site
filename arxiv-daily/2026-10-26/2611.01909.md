> **一句话总结：** 在 Qwen3.5（Gated DeltaNet）与 Granite4.0（Mamba-2）混合架构中，线性注意力不只是压缩上下文的"存储器"：作者用因果干预（删除/移植）定位出一个 Encode–Address 回路——最早的 Encode 块保留 token 身份（删掉后检索准确率跌至 0.01），紧邻 reader 的 Address 块把检索目标携带在循环状态里并构造 softmax reader 的注意力 query（移植其末位输出可在 0.97–0.99 的案例中把检索改写为 donor 询问的条目），且该计算可跨架构迁移：用 Qwen 的 Address 块替换 Granite 的可把准确率从 0.01 恢复到 0.92–0.94，全程不更新任何权重。

> **TL;DR:** On a controlled 8-pair key–value retrieval task (chat8/list8/rev8, 100 prompts each), this mechanistic study overturns the assumption that linear-attention layers in hybrids (Qwen3.5 with Gated DeltaNet, Granite-4.0-H with Mamba-2) merely compress context: an early Encode block is necessary (removing it drops accuracy from 1.00 to 0.01–0.05 even with all softmax layers intact), and an Address block right before the first softmax reader causally determines *what* is retrieved — transplanting its final-position output between prompts redirects retrieval in 0.99 of donor–host pairs vs 0.00 for every other block, and swapping it across architectures (Qwen3.5→Granite4.0) restores accuracy to 0.92–0.94 vs 0.01–0.30 for the removal baseline. The takeaway for hybrid design: linear attention builds the query, softmax attention performs the lookup, so pruning/distilling "just the linear layers" can silently break retrieval addressing.

## 核心贡献

- **重新划分混合架构中检索的分工**：以往工作（如 Bick et al. 2025 的 Gather-and-Aggregate）把上下文检索归功于 softmax attention、把线性注意力当作压缩记忆；本文证明线性注意力直接参与检索——它构造 softmax reader 所用的 query。
- **定位出 Encode–Address 回路**：通过逐层剪枝 + 块级删除/移植两类因果干预，发现（1）最早的线性注意力块 **Encode** 负责保留检索所需的 token 身份（其作用可用一个逐 token 线性投影复现，检索准确率保持 1.00）；（2）紧邻 reader 的 **Address** 块从其循环状态出发构造末位表示，决定 reader 检索哪个条目。
- **证明检索目标存放在 Address 块的循环状态中**：把 donor 提示词在 Address 块的循环状态拷入 host 即可重定向检索（0.99），而移植其末位输入或更早块的状态完全无效（0.00）。
- **Address 计算跨架构可迁移**：在 Gated DeltaNet（Qwen3.5）与 Mamba-2（Granite4.0）两个独立训练的模型家族之间，用线性对齐映射（ridge regression，不动权重）即可移植 Address 输出甚至整块替换 Address 计算，恢复检索准确率至 0.92–0.94。
- **揭示规模效应**：跨 6 个 Qwen3.5 模型（0.8B→35B-A3B），Address 计算从单块引导全部 reader（≤9B）变为多块分布式、各块引导各自的 reader（27B/35B），因为 query 不再经由注意力从一个 reader 传递到下一个。

## Key Contributions

- **Reframes the division of labor in hybrid LMs**: prior work attributed in-context retrieval to softmax attention and treated linear attention as context compression; this paper shows linear attention directly constructs the query that softmax attention uses for lookup.
- **Identifies the Encode–Address circuit** via causal interventions: progressive layer pruning localizes retrieval to a single softmax "reader" layer (L19 in Qwen3.5-9B); block removal identifies a necessary early Encode block, and block transplantation identifies a sufficient Address block immediately preceding the reader.
- **Localizes the retrieval target in the Address block's recurrent state**: transplanting the donor's recurrent states (not just final outputs) redirects retrieval in 0.99 of cases, while transplanting earlier blocks' states or the block's final-position input fails (0.00).
- **Demonstrates cross-architecture transfer** between Gated DeltaNet (Qwen3.5) and Mamba-2 (Granite4.0) using only linear alignment maps — both the Address output and the Address block's recurrent computation transfer without updating any weights.
- **Characterizes how the circuit scales** across six Qwen3.5 models (0.8B to 35B-A3B MoE): Address computation becomes distributed over a chain of blocks, each guiding its own softmax reader, with redundancy growing at intermediate scales (removal leaves accuracy at 0.92–1.00 for 4B–27B but drops to 0.06 at 0.8B and 0.34 at 35B-A3B).

## Methodology

The authors study frozen public checkpoints of two hybrid families: Qwen3.5 (Gated DeltaNet, 3:1 linear:softmax ratio; 0.8B, 2B, 4B, 9B, 27B, 35B-A3B MoE) and Granite-4.0-H (Mamba-2, 4 softmax layers out of 40; 1B dense, 7B MoE). All experiments use eager attention and forward hooks on token-mixer outputs; no weights are modified.

**Task.** A controlled in-context key–value retrieval task: a dictionary of 8 single-token key–value pairs followed by a question about one key, wrapped in each model's chat template with a prefilled assistant response. Three surface formats (chat8, list8 bulleted table, rev8 value-first) × 100 prompts each; all models reach ≥0.99 on chat8. Accuracy = fraction of prompts where the queried value has the highest logit over the full vocabulary; normalized accuracy maps chance (1/8) to 0 and the intact model to 1. A synthetic task is chosen deliberately to isolate retrieval from parametric knowledge, reasoning, and answer selection.

**Interventions.**
1. *Progressive pruning*: truncate after layer L, feed remaining hidden state to the final norm + LM head; contrasted with knowledge performance on six zero-shot benchmarks (ARC-Challenge, ARC-Easy, PIQA, WinoGrande, OpenBookQA, HellaSwag) via lm-evaluation-harness.
2. *Block removal*: zero a linear-attention block's token-mixer output at all positions (MLPs kept), measured by accuracy, logit gap (correct-minus-alternative logit difference over value-swapped prompt pairs), and reader attention (attention mass from the final position on the queried entry).
3. *Transplant*: replace a block's mixer output at the final position in host prompt B with the corresponding output from donor prompt A (same dictionary, different queried key); success = B returns A's value.
4. *Token-level patching and linear replacement of Encode*: value/key swaps patched with recorded Encode outputs; G0 replaced by a token-wise linear projection fitted with ridge regression.
5. *Query-vs-keys disentangulation*: recompute reader attention with query-without-G4 + original keys, or original query + keys-without-G4; measure selectivity (log-attention gap between queried entry and others) and top-1 rate.
6. *Cross-architecture transfer*: output transplantation and whole-block replacement between Qwen3.5 and Granite4.0, connected by ridge-regression linear alignment maps fitted on paired activations, with controls (removal only, token-wise linear projection, donor block with zeroed mixers).

## Results & Highlights

- **Address-block transplant redirects retrieval almost perfectly**: on chat8 with Qwen3.5-9B, transplanting G4's final-position output makes the model answer the donor's question in **0.99 of 100 donor–host pairs (vs 0.00 for every other block, including Encode G0)**; the effect holds across formats (0.97 on list8, 0.98 on rev8) and across models (0.90–1.00 in Qwen3.5-0.8B, Granite4.0-1B, Granite4.0-7B joint G2+G3).
- **Encode is necessary**: removing the earliest block G0 drops retrieval accuracy from 1.00 (intact) to **0.01** in Qwen3.5-9B (0.05 when removing only its first layer), and to **0.00 in every model studied** — even with all softmax layers intact.
- **Removing Address vs transplanting it dissociates necessity from sufficiency**: removing G4 in Qwen3.5-9B leaves accuracy at 1.00 (later softmax layers L23/L27 compensate) but **halves the logit gap from +23.8 to +11.6 and collapses reader attention on the queried entry from 0.83 to 0.06**; in models without such redundancy, removal drops accuracy to 0.13 (Qwen3.5-0.8B) and 0.00 (Granite4.0-1B).
- **Retrieval emerges abruptly at one softmax layer**: with progressive pruning, normalized retrieval accuracy jumps **from 0 to 0.67 as soon as L19 is retained** in Qwen3.5-9B, while knowledge (mean of ARC-C/E, PIQA, WinoGrande, OpenBookQA, HellaSwag) grows gradually; keeping 24/32 layers preserves **0.94 of retrieval but only 0.47 of knowledge** performance.
- **The Address block controls the reader's query, not its keys**: using a query computed without G4 cuts reader selectivity from **5.45 to 1.36** (top-1 rate ~0.5), whereas keys computed without G4 leave selectivity at 4.80 and top-1 rate at 1.00.
- **The retrieval target lives in the Address block's recurrent state**: transplanting G4's recurrent states redirects retrieval in **0.99** of cases, vs **0.00** for G0/G3 states and 0.00 for swapping G4's final-position input.
- **Cross-architecture transfer works without weight updates**: replacing Granite4.0-1B's Address block with Qwen3.5-0.8B's restores accuracy to **0.92 vs 0.01 for removal-only and ≤0.28 for the linear-projection and zeroed-mixer controls** (reverse direction: 0.95 vs 0.30); replacing both Address blocks of Granite4.0-7B with Qwen3.5-9B's single block reaches **0.94 vs 0.00 removal / 0.14 linear projection**; the reverse (Granite→Qwen 9B) only partially recovers (0.46 vs 0.75 intact).
- **Scaling changes the circuit's shape**: joint Address transplant redirects 0.99/1.00/0.70/0.98/0.98/0.75 of answers across Qwen3.5 0.8B/2B/4B/9B/27B/35B-A3B, while removal necessity is non-monotonic — accuracy after removal is 0.06 at 0.8B, 0.41 at 2B, 0.92–0.94 at 4B–9B, 1.00 at 27B, but 0.34 at the 35B-A3B MoE.
- **Encode's retrieval role is linear**: replacing G0 with a fitted token-wise linear projection keeps chat8 accuracy at 1.00 while WikiText-2 perplexity degrades from 10.6 to 44.7 (mixers zeroed: 1059; block removed: 6×10^4).

## Authors & Credibility

Wenqi Zeng (The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology), Chen Feng (The Hong Kong Polytechnic University), YuAng Chen (The Chinese University of Hong Kong). The work is a clean mechanistic-interpretability study in the style of Bick et al. (2025) and Olsson et al. (induction heads): frozen public checkpoints, eager attention, intervention via forward hooks only, no finetuning anywhere. Code, per-model result files, logs, and prompts are released (https://github.com/yuang-chen/LinearQuery), and Appendix A documents task generation (22 key words, 20 color/material value words, all single tokens; corrupted twins matched in token length) and chat-template handling. The analysis spans two independently trained hybrid families (Gated DeltaNet vs Mamba-2) and six model scales, which is broader than most circuit-analysis papers.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: high and genuine.** The question "what do the linear layers contribute to retrieval?" is distinct from prior work: Jelassi et al. (2024) and Arora et al. (2024a) showed linear attention *fails* at recall; Bick et al. (2025) showed softmax heads do the gathering. This paper studies models where retrieval already succeeds and finds an active, causal role for linear attention. The Address block genuinely has "no direct counterpart" in Gather-and-Aggregate, and the recurrent-state transplant (0.99 redirect from states alone vs 0.00 from final-position input) is a sharp, non-obvious result.
- **Rigor: strong controls, honest reporting.** Every headline claim has the right control: same-model transplant as the baseline for cross-model transfer; token-wise linear projection and zeroed-mixer donor as controls for block replacement; three prompt formats to rule out template-specific artifacts; the redundancy confound (removal leaves accuracy at 1.00 in Qwen3.5-9B) is handled by evaluating after L19 and by reporting models without redundant readers. The authors are candid that reverse 7B→9B transfer is partial (0.46 vs 0.75 intact).
- **Reproducibility: good.** Public checkpoints, released code/logs/prompts, stdlib-style deterministic task generation with length-matched corrupted twins, and precise intervention descriptions (offset-mapping token positions, batch only when positions agree).
- **Significance caveat — synthetic-task gap.** All circuit claims rest on an 8-pair, single-token, single-hop dictionary lookup (100 prompts per format). No needle-in-a-haystack, multi-hop, long-context, or natural-language retrieval evidence is presented, so "linear attention builds the query" is established for chat8-style lookup, and its transfer to realistic long-context retrieval is asserted by framing rather than tested. The choice is well-motivated (isolating retrieval from knowledge) but the external validity remains open.
- **Red flags / weak points.** (1) Cross-architecture transfer relies on ridge-regression alignment maps fitted on paired activations — a supervised fit, so "transfer without updating weights" is slightly charitable. (2) The 35B-A3B (0.34) vs 27B (1.00) removal discrepancy is flagged and then explicitly deferred ("future work"), leaving the dense-vs-MoE story unexplained. (3) Causal necessity of Address at 4B–9B rests on transplant, not ablation, since ablation is masked by later readers; readers should note the title's claim is about *what is retrieved*, not that removal breaks these particular checkpoints. (4) Only two model families are dissected; Jamba, Nemotron-H, and Kimi Linear are cited as hybrids but not analyzed, so generality across the hybrid design space is unverified.

## Strengths

- Causally decisive methodology: necessity (removal), sufficiency (transplant), and mechanism (query-vs-keys recompute, state transplant) triangulate the same conclusion from independent directions.
- The necessity/sufficiency dissociation (removal leaves accuracy 1.00 while transplant redirects 0.99) is a textbook example of why multiple interventions beat any single one, and the paper explains the compensation by later readers instead of hiding it.
- Cross-architecture block replacement (GDN ↔ Mamba-2) with meaningful controls is a genuinely novel result suggesting convergent "addressing" computations across different recurrent mixers.
- Practical implication for hybrid design is concrete: which linear blocks can be pruned/distilled without breaking retrieval addressing, and why single-layer interventions miss block-level effects (Appendix B).
- Clean writing; figures carry the argument; appendix reports all models × all three prompt formats.

## Limitations

- Evidence is confined to a synthetic 8-entry, single-token key–value task; no long-context, multi-hop, or naturalistic retrieval benchmarks (e.g., NIAH, document QA) are tested.
- Only Qwen3.5 and Granite-4.0-H families are studied; other hybrids (Jamba, Nemotron-H, Kimi Linear, Samba) are discussed but not analyzed.
- Cross-model transfer requires fitted linear alignment maps and paired activations; the larger-pair reverse direction (Granite4.0-7B → Qwen3.5-9B) only reaches 0.46 vs 0.75 intact, showing the transfer is asymmetric.
- The 27B vs 35B-A3B discrepancy in Address-block necessity (1.00 vs 0.34 after removal) is unexplained and deferred to future work.
- Retrieval is scored as the argmax over the 8 dictionary values / full vocabulary at one position; effects on generation quality beyond the first answer token are not examined (the WikiText-2 perplexity result of 44.7 under the linear-projection Encode hints that general LM quality and this circuit are entangled).

## Relevance

对研究高效序列模型与混合架构（linear attention + 少量 softmax）的读者高度相关：它阐明了线性层在检索中的主动角色（构造 query 而非仅压缩上下文），为混合模型中"哪些线性块可剪枝/蒸馏而不破坏检索"提供了因果依据；对可解释性方向的人，这是一个把 circuit analysis 系统性扩展到 recurrent 混合架构的范例工作。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the Encode–Address circuit generalize from 8-pair dictionaries to needle-in-a-haystack, multi-hop, and natural-document retrieval, where keys and values are multi-token and distractors are semantically closer?
- Why is Address-block necessity non-monotonic with scale (0.06 at 0.8B → ~1.0 at 27B → 0.34 at 35B-A3B)? Is the MoE router or the lower active-parameter count responsible for the 35B-A3B fragility?
- Can the Address block be used for targeted editing — e.g., steering which context entry a model retrieves on demand, or diagnosing retrieval failures in production hybrid LMs?
- What training dynamics produce convergent Address computations in Gated DeltaNet and Mamba-2 — is the recurrent-state "address register" an attractor of any fixed-state mixer trained on recall-heavy data?
- Do Kimi Linear, Nemotron-H, and Jamba-style hybrids (different linear:softmax ratios and placements) implement the same query-construction role, and does distilling Transformers into hybrids (à la "Mamba in the Llama") create or destroy Address blocks?
