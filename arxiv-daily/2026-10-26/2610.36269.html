A steering vector read from text alone zero-shot steers image generation — and vice versa.

一句话总结 — 在 UniAR、Emu3.5、Liquid 三个统一多模态模型上,作者证明从单一模态最小对提取的线性转向向量可以零样本、双向地控制另一模态的生成(UniAR 中恢复 84% 图像 / 74% 文本的原模态转向效果),并发现跨模态可迁移性与文本/图像向量的余弦相似度相关——尽管两种模态的表征占据明显分离的子空间。

Why this matters

Steering moves from single-modality LLMs to causal cross-modal control: text and image concepts share linear directions inside unified models, enabling modality-agnostic control.

Build the vector in whichever modality is cheap (text pairs), apply it to the other — the argument extends to safety-style interventions. — distilled from Relevance

⚠ Critical edge

The >1.0 “even better than native” recoveries are a denominator artifact: image-native steering is itself weak in Emu3.5 / Liquid (img→img AUC 0.44 / 0.43 vs 0.83 in UniAR) — read as underperforming baselines, not superhuman transfer.

01

Methodology — extract, add, judge

方法

1 · Extract

Per concept pole pair (e.g. happy/sad), 100 minimal pairs per modality. Text: Gemini-templated contrastive sentences, last-token hidden state. Images: baseline scenes edited toward each pole (subject/pose/framing fixed), states mean-pooled over image tokens. Vector = mean activation difference, unit-normalized per layer.

2 · Apply

Added to all generated tokens during generation, with strength α swept past the breakdown point, in windows of 4 consecutive layers at early / middle / late depths (e.g. 7–10, 16–19, 25–28 of UniAR's 36).

h′ℓ = hℓ + α · v̂ℓ

Same intervention whichever modality the vector came from or targets.

3 · Evaluate

20 neutral prompts per concept/direction; a blind LLM judge (A/B/Tie, never sees α or layers) yields a concept rate, AUC'd over α. Judge validated against 3-way human majority on 320 pairs (Cohen's κ = 0.62); fluency tracked by an AxBench-style judge, DreamSim pole score for images.

Recovery = transfer AUC ÷ intra-modality AUC (1.0 = transferred vector as good as native), each vector scored over its own tolerated strength range. Testbeds: UniAR 8B, Emu3.5 34B, Liquid 7B — one autoregressive backbone, shared weights, mixed text + discrete image tokens; 8 concepts (emotion, age, cleanness, chaos, size, depth, spatial, color).

Results — beyond the headline number

结果
Modeltxt→img recoveryimg→txt recoveryimg→img AUC (baseline)
UniAR8B 0.84 0.74 (→0.96) 0.83
Emu3.534B 1.10* 0.87 (late layers) 0.44*
Liquid7B 1.20* 0.91 (late layers) 0.43*

* >1.0 arises from weak image-side baselines, not stronger-than-native transfer. Recovery = transfer AUC / intra-modality AUC, middle layers unless noted.

Concept-dependence is strong

Emotion transfers best (0.95 both directions); age into images at 1.02–1.87; size shows the sharpest asymmetry (1.12 txt→img vs 0.32 img→txt); spatial left–right fails almost everywhere (0.08 / 0.01).

Geometry predicts transfer

Cosine similarity between text- and image-derived vectors correlates with recovery: Spearman ρ = 0.38 (p<0.01, n=63), strongest in UniAR txt→img (ρ = 0.76); concept cosines reach 0.60 vs a random-direction baseline of ±0.03; happy–sad aligns at 0.47. Emu3.5 stays near zero yet still transfers.

The modality gap is real despite transfer

In UniAR's middle layers the average happy image is >10× closer to the average sad image than to the average happy sentence — separate subspaces with aligned internal direction structure, not shared concept points.

Quality trade-off & qualitative semantics

At 0.4 of the strength range (text concept-rate peak) fluency retains ~75%; image coherence holds ~90% at 0.6 where concept rate exceeds 90% of max. A formality vector makes images formally dressed / black-and-white / luxe-minimalist; a low-res vector makes text shorter, hedging, "blurry"-mentioning.

Strengths
  • First clean demonstration of bidirectional cross-modal steering in weight-shared UMMs — contradicts the prior BAGEL asymmetry narrative.
  • Causal-intervention framing with the four-setting design and recovery ratios makes results easy to interpret.
  • Geometry analysis (10× pole-vs-modality distance; aligned difference vectors) connects to modality-gap and vector-offset literature.
  • Modality-unique case studies (formality → formal clothing; low-res → hedging text) are genuinely novel observations.
  • Strong release: code, vectors, pairs, generations, judge scores; transparent reporting of negative results (spatial, Emu3.5 geometry).
Limitations
  • Only three models, one family (autoregressive UMMs with discrete image tokens); no continuous-latent, diffusion-decoder, or proprietary systems.
  • Eight mostly binary, visually salient concepts; abstract, compositional, or safety-critical axes untested.
  • Single LLM-judge evaluation, κ = 0.62 human agreement; no error bars on recovery ratios across the 20 prompts per concept.
  • Fluency degrades at high strengths (~75% of unsteered at the concept-rate peak); transferred vectors need larger α than native ones.
  • No mechanistic explanation of why transfer works — phenomenon plus geometric correlate, not circuits.
03

Questions for further exploration

开放问题
Would transfer hold in unified models with continuous image latents or diffusion decoders, where token spaces differ fundamentally?
Can spatial (left/right) failures be repaired at different granularities — per-head, SAE features, layer-specific vectors — or is the tokenizer at fault?
Does the cosine/recovery correlation hold prospectively, as a cheap predictor of which concepts a given UMM can steer?
Do safety-relevant directions (e.g. refusal) transfer txt→img — creating a new attack surface for eliciting unsafe visual content?
Do fine-tuning or knowledge edits that break text-side directions silently degrade image-side steerability, given the shared subspace structure?
Could "separate subspaces, aligned relations" enable cheaper cross-modal alignment — a linear map learned only on difference vectors?
