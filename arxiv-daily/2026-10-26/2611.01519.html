Why this matters

Explains at the representation level why fine-tuned knowledge fails to compose — and yields a transferable rule: what training writes must match what inference reads.

01

The fix — LEGIBLE (read-equivalent writing)

1

Locate the reading window

Injection experiments pin where reading starts (layers 2–10 on Qwen2.5-7B); an attention-cutting rule pins where it ends (t = 24 / 18 / 26 per model) — fixed before any test run.

2

Write what gets read

L_read pulls the bridge description's hidden state at layer t toward the base model reading the neutral sentence "This is about o" — deliberately not the answer token, its input embedding, or its output direction.

3

Keep everything else

L_keep anchors three same-relation neighbours at their pre-edit states — one edit changes one function value.

L = LLM + 3 · ( Lread + Lkeep )

Setup: one LoRA per edit (rank 8, α 32, 40 epochs) · Qwen2.5-7B / Llama-3-8B / Qwen3-8B (+ Qwen2.5-14B) · ~11 s per edit on one 85 GB GPU · descriptions, targets, and layers all generated by the edited model itself — no multi-hop training data, no external LLM, no templates.

02

Why believe it's the write?

Reading happens early-to-middle

Patching the true bridge entity's representation into layers 2–10 rescues the second hop (0.03–0.06 → 0.20–0.26); later injections do nothing.

FT/CaKE never move the bridge

Bridge→new-entity distance at layer 24 stays near pre-edit (0.79 / 0.82 vs 0.70); LEGIBLE drops it to 0.16. Repairing the bridge alone lifts every baseline writer to 0.54–0.55.

Only "reads as o" composes

With loss/layer/weight fixed: reading target 0.71/0.62/0.63; output-embed 0.22–0.23, input-embed 0.25–0.27; a state that merely says o (copying, 99–100% recall) composes only 0.50–0.67.

One axis for 17 writers

Composition tracks the share of the reading window where the bridge reads as the new entity: Pearson r = 0.96 (Qwen2.5).

Best locality among fine-tuning writers — but when the edited subject is itself the bridge, AlphaEdit answers 0.02–0.12 vs LEGIBLE's 0.73–0.94.

  • Robustness: on the ~3/4 of questions whose bridge phrase never occurs verbatim in training, LEGIBLE still composes 0.69 (vs 0.75 on the overlapping quarter).
  • Weight sweep: B-Acc rises monotonically with the bridge term (0.30 at w=0 → 0.71 at w=3 → 0.69 at w=10) as layer-t distance falls 0.79 → 0.16; no weight rescues the lens losses (0.28–0.34).
  • Transfer: on a synthetic relation injected into Qwen3-4B, read-equivalent writing reaches 0.82 — the injection ceiling itself.
04

Strengths

  • Mechanism-first: token, layer, and neighbours are all derived from measurements of where/how the model reads, not tuned — ablations confirm each choice matters.
  • Clean "says o" vs "reads as o" separation via target swaps: a copying state says o 99–100% of the time yet composes only 0.50–0.67.
  • Self-contained: the edited model generates its own bridge descriptions and targets — no external LLM, templates, or multi-hop supervision.
  • Honest boundary-drawing: the read–write contract predicts where the method cannot help (answer = the written word; FT already 0.81–0.89) — and the paper says so.
  • Consistent gains across three model families, three benchmarks (including the one that named the curse), and at 14B.
05

Limitations

  • Locality trails locate-then-edit: Nbr 0.66 vs AlphaEdit's 0.98 — writing at the bridge still moves some same-relation function values.
  • White-box + heavy: internal access, per-model layer-t calibration, and per-edit LoRA training (~11 s/edit on an 85 GB GPU) — not a drop-in for black-box or high-throughput editing.
  • Scope: only two-hop chains and (mostly) single-fact edits; longer chains, sequential edit stacks, and second hops the model doesn't already know are unaddressed.
  • Template dependence: bridge descriptions come from each relation's template — relations without a clean template or context-sensitive readings are not studied.
  • Small samples on 2 of 3 models (100 two-hop cases for Llama-3/Qwen3 vs 300 for Qwen2.5); AlphaEdit ran with EasyEdit-borrowed hyperparameters, possibly understating that baseline.
06

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does read-equivalent writing extend to three-hop chains where the bridge is itself a written fact (chained LEGIBLE edits)?
  2. Can the bridge term combine with AlphaEdit's null-space projection for both composition (0.71) and near-perfect locality (0.98) in one editor?
  3. Is the layer-t rule stable across checkpoints, domains, and scale — or does it drift and need re-measurement per model version?
  4. Does the read–write contract explain in-context learning composition too — should ICL compose at near-ceiling rates, and does it?
  5. What happens when the second hop is itself wrong in the base model (the 9–14% it doesn't know), where no correct read target exists?
  6. Can the reading-window diagnostic serve as a cheap general probe for where a model consumes intermediate quantities — CoT tokens, retrieved context?
