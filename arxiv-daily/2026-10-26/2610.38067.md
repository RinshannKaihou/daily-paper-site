> **一句话总结：** 本文在 8 个多选问答数据集上用 Gemma Scope SAE 审计 Gemma 3 1B/4B 的错误预测特征：过滤掉与选项字母、模板、排列、文本主题等伪影纠缠的特征后，在问题/选项 span 上干预所选特征确实比随机特征产生更大的 logit margin 偏移（.187 vs .146）和更高的纠错率（.189% vs .058%），但答案翻转极其罕见且方向不稳定——这些特征反映的是"答案竞争"而非错误机制本身；被改造的 HalluSAE 基线虽能造成巨大的最终 token margin 变化（1.760 vs .154），但 95.5% 的特征未通过伪影检验且纠错率并无提升。

> **TL;DR:** Across eight MCQA datasets (CommonSenseQA, TruthfulQA MC1, HaluEval QA, Wiki-FACTOR, Med-HALT R-NOTA, MMLU, SciQ, FEVER; 1,472,694 rendered prompts), SAE activations predict forced-choice errors in Gemma 3 1B/4B (FP AUROC up to .739), but prediction is not causation: after an artifact-risk filter, interventions on question/option-span features beat random-active controls on margin shift (mean |Δm| .187 vs .146, +.041 [.014, .067]) and wrong-to-correct flips (.189% vs .058%), yet flips are rare, unstable across template/permutation changes, and at the final token selected features actually shift margins LESS than random (.154 vs .237); an adapted HalluSAE baseline yields ~11x larger final-token margin shifts (1.760) but 95.5% of its features fail the artifact gate and correction rates do not improve — so "predictive" SAE features mostly track context-dependent answer competition, not an error mechanism you can steer away.

## 核心贡献

- 提出一条**伪影感知（artifact-aware）的 SAE 特征发现与筛选流水线**：用四类对照分数（选项字母依赖、提示模板依赖、答案排列不稳定、文本/主题关联）计算伪影风险 a_f = max(...)，以 a_f ≤ 0.80 为门槛，再叠加激活频率、数据集覆盖（≥2 个数据集）、系数符号一致性（≥0.60）等资格条件，从 81,304 个非 oracle 候选单元筛到 top-300，最后用对比式直接 logit 归因（DLA）选出 55 个非 oracle 与 22 个 oracle 特征单元用于因果干预。
- 在 8 个数据集 × 6 种 token 位置（最终提示 token、问题 span、选项块、生成理由、金标选项文本 oracle、最强错误选项文本 oracle）上系统量化了"SAE 特征能预测错误"这一命题：错误 vs 正确（WvC）AUROC .715、低/高 margin AUROC .739、熵的 median R² .285（margin 仅 .117）。
- 用零替换、均值替换和 +1/+2/+4σ 上行转向三种单特征干预做因果检验：问题/选项 span 上所选特征的 margin 偏移与纠错率显著高于随机激活对照，但答案翻转稀少、效应方向在语义等价的模板与排列变化下不稳定——即伪影过滤后的特征仍只反映"答案竞争"，不是可靠的错误机制。
- 对改造版 HalluSAE 基线做伪影审计：其预测性能良好（AUROC/F1 .775/.674）且最终 token margin 偏移巨大（|Δm| 1.760），但 711 个非零单元中 679 个（95.5%）在所有设置下超过 a_f = 0.80 伪影门槛，中位激活频率 .580（所选/随机特征约 .0013，稠密得多），且与本文 top-300 短名单零重叠、纠错率无提升——证明"预测能力强 + logit 影响大"不等于"伪影无关的因果机制"。

## Key Contributions

- An **artifact-aware feature-discovery pipeline** that screens SAE features with four confound controls (option-letter dependence, prompt-template dependence, answer-permutation instability, text/topic association via TF-IDF/SVD and category eta-squared), gates at artifact risk a_f ≤ 0.80, and applies eligibility filters (activation frequency < 0.95, ≥100 active instances, evidence from ≥2 datasets, coefficient-sign consistency ≥ 0.60) before DLA-based final selection (55 non-oracle + 22 oracle units from 81,304 / 48,888 ranked units).
- A **systematic predictive audit** of SAE features for MCQA errors across six activation positions and three response modes (forced-letter, reason-first, letter-then-reason): wrong-vs-correct, low-vs-high-margin, wrong-high-confidence, and low-margin targets are all decodable above chance, with generated-reasoning (GR) and oracle spans strongest and question-span probes weakest.
- A **causal intervention study** (zero/mean replacement and +1/+2/+4σ steering at FP/Q/O/C/TW spans) showing artifact-filtered question/option-span features have genuine but weak causal leverage over the correct-vs-wrong margin relative to random-active controls — while effect directions fail to survive meaning-preserving prompt-format and answer-order changes.
- A **cautionary artifact audit of an adapted HalluSAE baseline**: it is predictive and produces huge final-token margin changes, but 95.5% of its nonzero units fail the artifact gate in every setting, its features are extremely dense (median activation frequency .580 vs ~.0013), and it does not correct more errors — evidence that neither prediction nor large logit shifts establish a clean error mechanism.

## Methodology

**Phase 1 — discovery and filtering.** Each dataset item is rendered under 3 prompt templates (dotted/parenthesized/colon labels), 4 answer orders (original + 3 permutations), and 3 response modes, yielding 1,472,694 behavioral instances. For each instance the authors compute pooled answer-letter logits (Eq. 4), normalized letter probabilities, the correct-vs-top-wrong margin m_i = z_{i,y_i} − z_{i,w_i}, and answer-letter entropy. Residual-stream activations are encoded with pretrained Gemma Scope 2 SAEs (16K and 65K widths) at layers 17/22 of Gemma 3 1B and 22/29 of Gemma 3 4B, max-pooled over six token positions (FP, Q, O, GR, plus oracle spans C = gold-choice text and TW = top-wrong-choice text). Feature candidates come from L1-regularized logistic probes for four binary targets (WvC, LMvHM, WHC, WLM) and ridge regressions for margin/entropy, with validation-quality-weighted, rank-discounted evidence aggregated across datasets, modes, and positions. Each active feature gets four artifact-control scores converted to within-run percentiles; the max is the artifact risk a_f. Eligible units (a_f ≤ 0.80 etc.) are ranked to a top-300 shortlist per pool, then contrastive direct logit attribution (DLA = A_{i,f} · d_f^T(u_{y_i} − u_{w_i}), Eq. 10) retains features whose decoder direction consistently supports the correct or top-wrong answer, plus high-magnitude mixed-direction diagnostics — 55 non-oracle and 22 oracle units.

**Phase 2 — interventions.** For each selected feature, the decoder-vector update r′ = r + (τ − a_{i,t,f})·d_f (Eq. 11) is applied to every token of the target span on up to 10 highest-activation test instances, with τ ∈ {0 (zero ablation), μ_f (mean replacement), a_{i,t,f} + λσ_f for λ ∈ {1,2,4} (upward steering)}, each compared against a no-intervention pass on the same prompt. Outcomes: Δm (margin shift), Δz_y/Δz_w, Δp_y/Δp_w, ΔH, and answer-flip types (W2C, C2W, W2W). Baselines are random-active controls (five eligible active features per run) and a matched-layer adaptation of HalluSAE (DLA + class-balanced L1 logistic WvC classifier at FP, 100 features across the two layers per model). Inference is statistics-heavy: 5,000–10,000 bootstrap resamples over model–layer–width–feature units give percentile intervals, plus a feature-equal regression adjusting for intervention size (L2 latent displacement), span length, intervention variant, baseline margin/correctness, activation frequency, position, dataset, and model configuration.

## Results & Highlights

- **Prediction works:** over 192 final-prompt-token (FP) audit runs, mean test AUROC is .739 for low-vs-high-margin (LMvHM) and wrong-high-confidence (WHC), .715 for wrong-vs-correct (WvC), and .706 for wrong-vs-correct-among-low-margin (WLM, F1 .805); entropy is far more predictable than margin (median test R² .285 vs .117, Pearson r .541 vs .357). Generated-reasoning (GR) positions are strongest (WvC AUROC .740, WHC .782); question-span probes are weakest.
- **Selected features beat random at Q/O spans, not at FP:** pooling question- and option-span interventions, selected features produce mean |Δm| = .187 vs .146 for random-active controls (difference +.041, 95% CI [.014, .067]) and a wrong-to-correct rate of .189% vs .058% (+.132 pp, CI [+.024, +.257]); but at the final prompt token selected features shift the margin LESS than random (.154 vs .237; difference −.083, CI [−.118, −.047]).
- **Answer flips are rare and unstable:** across the whole test set, Q-span interventions yield only 16 W2C vs 17 C2W events and O-span 19 W2C vs 19 C2W (Table 5); steering strength raises selected Q/O |Δm| from .164 at +1σ_f to .268 at +4σ_f, yet only 51.8% of 8,256 feature–prompt pairs respond monotonically, and sign-agreement intervals across matched permutations/templates include zero.
- **Adapted HalluSAE: big influence, dirty features:** over 32 dataset–model–width groups HalluSAE reaches test AUROC/F1 .775/.674 (vs .761/.659 for its random-feature control), and its FP interventions produce |Δm| = 1.760 vs .154 for selected features (H−Selected = +1.606, CI [1.327, 1.902]) — yet its W2C rate (.332%) is statistically indistinguishable from selected (.159%) and random (.347%), 679 of 711 nonzero units (95.5%) exceed the a_f = 0.80 artifact gate in every setting, its median activation frequency is .580 vs ~.0013, and it has zero overlap with the paper's top-300 shortlists.
- **Oracle-span interventions cut both ways:** intervening at gold-choice (C) spans produces 6 W2C but also 9 C2W events; at top-wrong (TW) spans there are 0 W2C and 5 C2W — direct edits on answer text can repair or damage with no reliable direction.
- **Behavioral baseline:** macro-averaged forced-letter accuracy is 43.8% for Gemma 3 1B vs 58.6% for 4B, with wrong-high-confidence rates of 52.0% vs 39.3% (e.g., Med-HALT: 1B accuracy .063, WHC .866; HaluEval QA: 4B accuracy .907).

## Authors & Credibility

The four authors (Aria Nourbakhsh, Adelaide Danilov, Oleksandr Marchenko Breneur, Salima Lamsiyah) are from the Department of Computer Science at the University of Luxembourg; the corresponding author lists a uni.lu address. The work is a preprint (arXiv:2610.38067v1, cs.CL, 27 Oct 2026) with no stated venue or review. Credibility signals are strong for a methods-audit paper: prespecified eligibility thresholds, discovery/validation/test partitions (60/20/20), 5,000–10,000 bootstrap resamples with reported percentile intervals, perturbation-adjusted regression controls, and an unusually frank Limitations section (they disclose that their HalluSAE comparison is a matched-layer adaptation rather than a full reproduction, and that random controls are matched within activation runs, not on activation frequency or edit magnitude). Compute: 20 Nvidia V100 SXM2 GPUs, ~500 GPU hours. They also disclose AI-assisted tooling (GitHub Copilot, Grammarly/ChatGPT for editing) with author responsibility. All datasets, SAEs (Gemma Scope 2), and models (Gemma 3 1B/4B instruction-tuned) are public, which aids reproducibility.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** the core message — predictive/attributional SAE features need not be causal, and artifact screening must precede mechanistic interpretation — is a disciplined, quantitative extension of a known critique (Makelov et al. 2024; Karvonen et al. 2025; Kantamneni et al. 2025) into MCQA with a concrete four-control artifact score. The pipeline is the novelty; the headline causal finding is mostly negative.
- **Rigor:** generally high (prespecified gates, bootstrap CIs, adjusted regressions), but the headline "greater causal leverage" rests on very small event counts — the +.132 pp W2C advantage is built from roughly 35 W2C events (16 at Q + 19 at O) out of ~8,256+ pairs, and the CI [+ .024, +.257] barely excludes zero. The authors themselves note C2W and |Δp_y| intervals include zero, i.e., the selected features damage answers about as often as they repair them outside the Q/O margin statistic.
- **The FP result undercuts the framing:** at the final decision token — arguably the most decision-relevant position — selected features produce SMALLER margin shifts than random-active controls (.154 vs .237). The paper reports this honestly but the abstract's "greater causal leverage" claim holds only for Q/O spans.
- **The artifact gate is a percentile heuristic, not a contamination probability** (the appendix says so explicitly), and even after the gate 26 of 55 non-oracle units still carry an artifact or semantic tag — so "artifact-filtered" should be read as "less artifact-loaded on four measured axes," not clean.
- **Baseline fairness caveats:** the HalluSAE comparison fixes layers 17/22 and 22/29 instead of reproducing HalluSAE's Stage-I layer localization, and matches feature-test budget but not activation density — HalluSAE's dense features (median frequency .580) are naturally edited on many more tokens, which partially explains its 1.760 |Δm|. The random-active control uses 5 features per run vs 55 selected units and is not matched on frequency or edit magnitude (mitigated, but only partially, by the feature-equal regression).
- **Data quirks worth flagging:** SciQ is used without its supporting passage and FEVER is collapsed to 3-way classification without evidence sentences — conversions that depress accuracy and may manufacture the very "errors" being studied; Med-HALT accuracy is 6.3% for the 1B model (near/below chance for 4 choices), so a large share of "wrong answers" there is simple incompetence, not hallucination-like failure.
- **Reproducibility:** good — public models, public SAEs, public datasets, standard tooling — though the full selection funnel (probe ranks, evidence weights, DLA thresholds in Appendix B) has many researcher degrees of freedom that are documented but not released as a code package in the paper.

## Strengths

- Turns a slogan ("correlation is not causation") into an executable, quantified audit: four separate artifact controls, prespecified eligibility gates, and causal interventions with matched controls on the same prompts.
- The HalluSAE audit is the paper's most valuable exhibit: it cleanly separates predictive performance (AUROC .775) and logit influence (|Δm| 1.760) from artifact independence (95.5% of units fail the gate) and correction benefit (W2C not better than random) — a template for evaluating any "error feature" paper.
- Exceptional experimental coverage for the scale: 8 datasets, 2 models, 2 layers each, 2 SAE widths, 3 templates, 4 answer permutations, 3 response modes, 6 token positions, 1.47M rendered instances, all with discovery/validation/test discipline.
- Honest, self-critical reporting: negative results (FP underperformance, unstable directions, near-zero flip rates) are reported with the same prominence as positive ones, and the limitations section pre-empts the most obvious methodological objections.

## Limitations

- Only Gemma 3 1B and 4B at four mid-to-late residual layers with 16K/65K Gemma Scope 2 SAEs; larger models may organize error-related information differently (authors acknowledge the compute constraint: ~500 V100-hours already).
- Practical impact is thin: a W2C rate of .189% vs .058% means fewer than 1 in 500 wrong-answer interventions produces a correction, and C2W damage occurs at a comparable rate — these are diagnostic handles, not a repair technique.
- Random-active controls are matched within runs, not on activation frequency or edit magnitude; the perturbation-adjusted analysis mitigates this but excludes decoder norm.
- The HalluSAE comparison is a matched-layer adaptation that skips its Stage-I layer-localization stage, so the baseline numbers are not directly comparable to the original HalluSAE paper.
- Restricted to multiple-choice with a constrained answer space; free-form wrong answers — the setting where hallucination actually matters — are left to future work.
- Effect directions are not stable across semantically equivalent prompt formats and answer orders, so even the surviving "causal" features cannot be used as reliable steering handles.

## Relevance

这篇文章正面回应了"SAE 特征能否作为错误检测与干预的因果手柄"这一可解释性核心问题：它给出的伪影审计流水线（字母/模板/排列/主题四类对照 + DLA + 因果干预验证）可直接迁移到我们关心的幻觉检测与模型自我纠错研究方向，其对 HalluSAE 式"预测即机制"叙事的证伪对评估任何错误预测特征的工作都是重要警示。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the Q/O causal-leverage advantage survive on larger models (e.g., Gemma 3 12B+) or does error information become more/less localized in feature space with scale?
- Can the artifact-risk score be made an absolute (calibrated) contamination probability rather than a within-run percentile, so thresholds are comparable across settings?
- Do multi-feature or joint (circuit-level) interventions on the 55 selected units produce materially higher W2C rates than the single-feature scheme tested here, or is the answer-competition redundancy too high?
- Why do selected features underperform random controls at the final prompt token (.154 vs .237 |Δm|) — is FP influence dominated by dense format features that the artifact filter removes?
- Can the same audit be run on free-form generation by intervening during autoregressive decoding (the authors note their fixed-span implementation cannot test GR causally)?
- Do the 26 of 55 post-gate units that still carry artifact/semantic tags drive most of the remaining W2C events, i.e., is even the weak causal leverage artifact-mediated?
