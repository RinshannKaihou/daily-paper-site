> **一句话总结：** 谷歌提出 S3R/S3R-EC：让 INT8 随机舍入（SR）的每个 token 共享一个随机阈值（而非每个梯度元素独立生成），把随机数生成从 VPU 瓶颈中解放出来，再通过减去结构化平均舍入误差抵消共享阈值带来的相关性；在 12 层 Llama/C4 收敛实验中匹配独立 SR 的验证损失（4.919 vs 4.929），并在 TPU v5p 上把三个稠密 LLM 的完整 INT8 训练步加速至 BF16 的 1.24–1.32 倍、独立 SR 的约 1.11 倍。

> **TL;DR:** Standard stochastic rounding needs one 32-bit random threshold per gradient element, and since PRNGs run on the (slow-scaling) vector unit while GEMMs run on matrix units, independent SR can make INT8 training *slower* than BF16 (0.58x on a small FFN block on TPU v5p). S3R shares one 7-bit threshold per token across the hidden dimension and quantizes the upstream gradient once for both backward GEMMs; its error correction S3R-EC subtracts the known token/channel-constant mean rounding error, which analysis shows is where shared-threshold covariance damages training (the activation gradient). On a 12-layer Llama model trained on English C4 (32.8M tokens), S3R-EC matches independent SR's final validation loss (4.919 vs 4.929, BF16 = 4.892) while uncorrected sharing costs +0.126 loss, and on full MaxText training steps of Llama 3.1-8B / Qwen3-8B / Gemma 3-12B on TPU v5p it delivers 1.24–1.32x speedup over BF16 and ~1.11x over independent SR.

## 核心贡献

- **识别并量化了随机舍入的真实瓶颈**：标准 SR 为每个梯度元素生成一个 32 位 Threefry 随机数，而随机数在向量处理单元（VPU）上计算、GEMM 在矩阵单元（MXU）上执行；逐元素 PRNG 比确定性 RTNE 量化器多出 120–133 次/元素的向量操作，在小 FFN 形状上占 56.5% 的忙碌时间，使独立 SR 的 INT8 块只有 BF16 的 0.58 倍速度。
- **S3R（结构化子空间随机舍入）**：把上游梯度只量化一次、同时供激活梯度（dX）和权重梯度（dW）两个反向 GEMM 使用（S8 结果只写一次 HBM），并且每个 token 沿隐藏维共享一个阈值（PRNG 工作量削减 H 倍），阈值在向量寄存器内广播、从不物化到内存；纯 JAX 实现无需自定义 kernel。
- **理论刻画了"共享为什么有害、在哪里有害"**：证明共享阈值保持一阶无偏（引理 1），但引入非负误差协方差（引理 2）；逐 token 共享下权重梯度方差与独立 SR 完全相同，全部代价集中在激活梯度的正向协方差项，且会向底层传播、无法被下层自身的舍入误差抵消（命题 1）——因此必须在产生处修正。
- **S3R-EC 结构化误差修正**：量化器精确知道舍入误差 E，故从激活梯度与 FP32 权重梯度中减去 E 的行常数+列常数投影（control-variate / 可减性抖动视角），无需额外随机数、不改 AdamW 优化器；证明修正后梯度仍无偏、剩余误差每行每列和为零（定理 1、命题 2）；更省通信的 dX-only 变体在逐 token 阈值下即可获得几乎全部收益。
- **系统级验证**：MaxText 五个模型（3 稠密 + 2 MoE）的完整训练步基准表明 S3R-EC 在稠密模型上比 BF16 快 1.24–1.32 倍、比独立 SR 快约 1.11 倍，且收敛质量不损失。

## Key Contributions

- **Pinpoints the real cost of stochastic rounding**: independent SR hashes one 32-bit Threefry word per gradient element on the vector unit, adding 120–133 vector operations per element over an RTNE quantizer (56.5% of busy time at the smallest FFN shape), which stalls the matrix unit and makes INT8+SR run at only 0.58x BF16 speed for small model dimensions on TPU v5p.
- **S3R design**: quantize the upstream gradient once for both backward products (one INT8 write to HBM, halving backward quantization work) and broadcast one 7-bit threshold per token across the hidden dimension (a factor-H cut in PRNG work); thresholds live in vector registers via XLA fusion and are never materialized. Implemented in plain JAX with no custom kernels.
- **Theory of when sharing hurts**: Lemma 1 shows marginal unbiasedness survives any threshold topology; Lemma 2 derives the nonnegative pairwise error covariance Δ²(min(Pi,Pk) − PiPk) of shared thresholds; Proposition 1 shows per-token sharing leaves the weight gradient exactly as accurate as independent SR and concentrates all damage in the activation gradient, which propagates the offset to lower layers that cannot cancel it — so correction must happen at the layer of origin.
- **S3R-EC**: subtracts the exactly-known token-constant and channel-constant parts of the rounding error (an orthogonal projection) from both backward gradients; Theorem 1 and Proposition 2 prove centering removes the positive covariance, keeps gradients unbiased, and leaves the optimizer untouched. The cheaper dX-only variant suffices under per-token thresholds (Corollary 1), confirmed experimentally.
- **End-to-end evidence**: on full MaxText training steps of Llama 3.1-8B, Qwen3-8B, Gemma 3-12B (dense) and Qwen3-30B-A3B, DeepSeek-V2-Lite (MoE) on TPU v5p, S3R-EC is 1.24–1.32x faster than BF16 and ~1.11x faster than independent SR on the dense models, with no convergence loss.

## Methodology

The setting is INT8 quantization training of a linear projection Y = XW in a Transformer: forward uses RTNE with per-token/per-channel scales; backward computes the activation-gradient product GW^T and weight-gradient product X^T G, where the upstream gradient G is quantized with a per-tensor step Δ and stochastic rounding SR(G;R) = Δ⌊G/Δ + R⌋ (round up when the normalized residual reaches the threshold). Conventional recipes draw two independent full-size threshold tensors (one per backward product), costing 2BSH 32-bit PRNG words per projection.

S3R makes two changes: (1) inter-operand sharing — one threshold tensor R serves both backward products, so the INT8 gradient is computed and written once and read by both GEMMs; (2) threshold broadcasting — a compact seed u ∈ [0,1]^(B×S×1) (one value per token, the Φ2D default) is expanded inside the quantizer fusion so thresholds are broadcast in vector registers. Thresholds are 7-bit bf16 values cut four-per-32-bit-word from Threefry; a half-step shift of the threshold grid bounds the resulting rounding bias by Δ/256. A per-example variant Φbcast (one threshold per sequence) is studied as an ablation.

The analysis then proceeds in three steps. Lemma 1 (unbiasedness): expectation is linear, so any marginally uniform threshold field keeps every linear map — both backward products — unbiased, letting the topology be chosen purely on cost and second moments. Lemma 2 (covariance): elements sharing a threshold have Cov(Ei,Ek) = Δ²(min(Pi,Pk) − PiPk) ∈ [0, Δ²/4]; for evenly spread residuals each error has variance ≈ Δ²/6 and pairwise covariance ≈ Δ²/12, so half of every element's variance is shared with its group. Proposition 1 (where it hurts): under Φ2D the weight gradient keeps independent-SR variance exactly, but each token's activation-gradient error gains an extra positive term c((Wi·1_D)² − ||Wi||²), large when rows of W share a common component; this error enters lower layers as an upstream offset they cannot cancel with their own local rounding noise.

S3R-EC (the repair): the quantizer knows the realized error E = Ĝ − G exactly. It forms the orthogonal projection Estr of E onto row-constant + column-constant matrices (per-token mean ε, per-channel mean ē, grand mean m) and subtracts its image through both backward products: from the activation gradient using precomputed row sums W·1_D, and from the FP32 weight gradient using input means (equations 5–6). Theorem 1 shows centering drives average pairwise covariance to ≤ 0 and average variance to (n−1)/n(σ² − c̄); Proposition 2 proves unbiasedness is preserved and the shared term ε_s·W·1_D of every token's activation-gradient error is removed. No extra random numbers are drawn and AdamW is unchanged. Under Φ2D, token errors are independent across tokens, so centering over tokens has almost nothing to remove (Corollary 1) — the dX-only correction keeps nearly all the benefit at lower cost, and its factor all-gather replaces a weight-sized FP32 all-reduce (422.6 MiB → 3.1 MiB per step on a compiled two-layer model, at ~8 MiB of small-factor all-gather cost).

Evaluation uses three workloads on Google Cloud TPU v5p with JAX/XLA: (1) convergence — a 12-layer Llama-style decoder trained on English C4 for 2000 steps (S=2048, B=8, 32.8M tokens), 3 seeds, each configuration at its best learning rate chosen on seed 0; (2) single SwiGLU FFN block benchmarks at nine shapes W1–W9 spanning 1B–70B parameter models; (3) full MaxText training steps of five models (dense Llama 3.1-8B, Qwen3-8B, Gemma 3-12B; MoE Qwen3-30B-A3B, DeepSeek-V2-Lite 16B) under FSDP+TP via Pathways, median over 20 steps after 12 warm-up steps.

## Results & Highlights

- **Convergence (12-layer Llama on English C4, 32.8M tokens, final validation loss, 3 seeds)**: BF16 reaches 4.892 ± 0.015 and independent SR 4.929 ± 0.019; deterministic INT8 RTNE degrades to 5.106 ± 0.013 (+0.214 vs BF16; Welch t ≈ 13.2 vs SR), uncorrected S3R (Φ2D) lands at 5.055 ± 0.017 (+0.163 vs BF16, +0.126 vs SR), while S3R-EC two-way reaches 4.919 ± 0.022 (+0.027 vs BF16) and dX-only 4.924 ± 0.021 — eliminating the sharing degradation and matching independent SR within seed noise (best LR returns to BF16's 6e-4, versus 3e-4 for uncorrected S3R).
- **Per-example sharing is catastrophic and the correction localizes the damage**: S3R (Φbcast) fails to train usefully at 6.239 ± 0.026 (+1.347 vs BF16) even at the lowest LR tried (3.75e-5); two-way S3R-EC recovers most of it (5.002 ± 0.024, +0.109) but stays 0.073 above independent SR (Welch t ≈ 4.1), and dX-only leaves a further 0.092 gap — exactly as the covariance theory predicts, since per-example thresholds correlate tokens and require the weight-gradient half of the correction.
- **Gradient-error diagnostics (relative Frobenius error on a BF16 checkpoint, step 1000)**: uncorrected S3R (Φ2D) raises bottom-layer activation-gradient error to 0.877 vs 0.572 for independent SR, but two-way S3R-EC brings every measured error *below* independent SR — 0.476 (dX layer 0), 0.377 (dW layer 0), 0.378 (embedding) vs 0.572/0.456/0.457; a causal ablation swapping threshold topologies per backward product shows per-example thresholds on the dX product alone reproduce all the Φbcast damage (3.157 dX error) while per-token thresholds there remove it, regardless of what the dW product uses.
- **Single-block FFN speedup over BF16 on TPU v5p**: independent SR is *slower* than BF16 at small shapes (0.58x at W1 (H=512), 0.91x at W2), whereas S3R runs within 1% of INT8 RTNE at W2–W9 (1.44x–1.81x over BF16) and 1.08x at W1; the S3R-EC correction adds only −0.6% to +7.3% (two-way) and +1.7% to +9.6% (dX-only) at W3–W9, leaving only W1 below BF16 (0.94–0.96x).
- **Full training steps (MaxText, median ms over 20 steps)**: on Llama 3.1-8B, S3R-EC dX-only takes 2935 ms vs 3862 ms BF16 (1.316x) and 3350 ms independent SR (1.141x); across the three dense models S3R-EC is 1.24–1.32x faster than BF16 and ~1.11x faster than independent SR (e.g., Qwen3-8B two-way 3243 ms vs 4018/3607 ms; Gemma 3-12B dX-only 4911 ms vs 6550/5526 ms); the two-way correction adds 2.2–4.5% and dX-only 1.1–1.8% to the S3R step, with device memory within 0.4 GB of INT8 RTNE. On the two MoE models, where INT8 RTNE itself is slower than BF16 (7305 vs 6454 ms on Qwen3-30B-A3B), S3R is still 1.09–1.11x faster than independent SR and S3R-EC 1.04–1.07x.

## Authors & Credibility

All eight authors (Zeyu Wang, Yiheng Duan, Tao Chen, Donghyun Koo, Sihan Liu, Wenbo Zhang — corresponding author — Chuan He, Liang Liu) are at Google, Mountain View, which gives strong credibility on TPU systems work: the team has direct access to TPU v5p fleets, MaxText, and Pathways orchestration, and the paper's VPU-vs-MXU throughput analysis reflects insider hardware knowledge. The paper reads as a systems-and-theory contribution in the Google tradition of quantization-training recipes (Jetfire, FP8-LM-style studies are cited as context). The mathematical development (Lemmas 1–2, Propositions 1–2, Theorem 1) is stated carefully with assumptions disclosed, and the experimental section is unusually explicit about statistical power (3 seeds resolve ~0.05 loss differences) and about pessimistic timing choices. No code release is mentioned in the paper.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The core insight — that per-element PRNG is the bottleneck for SR on accelerators and that structured sharing plus exact mean-error subtraction solves it — is genuinely novel in the training setting. Prior work studied few-bit SR bias (El Arar et al., Fitzgibbon & Felix), hardware SR units (IPU, Blackwell cvt.rs), and correlated quantization across *distributed clients*; S3R's within-tensor sharing with a principled covariance repair is a new point in this space. The observation that sharing damage flows through the activation gradient (not the weight gradient) and must be corrected at the originating layer is a real analytical contribution, backed by a clean causal ablation (Table II's per-product threshold swap).
- **Rigor**: The theory is honest about its limits — the authors note Proposition 1 is "a mechanism, not an exact model" because RMSNorm backward and attention mix tokens, and they report that 17–27% of individual activation-gradient outputs become *noisier* after centering on a synthetic layer even though summed variance falls 40–63%. Statistics are handled with Welch t-tests and admitted noise floors. However, the headline quality claim ("S3R-EC matches independent SR") is a null result within a 3-seed, 2000-step, 12-layer, 32.8M-token budget — the paper itself states differences below ~0.05 are unresolved, and the +0.027 vs +0.037 gap to BF16 sits inside that noise. No downstream-task evaluation and no convergence run beyond 12 layers / 2000 steps is reported, which is thin for a training-method paper whose targets are 8B–30B models.
- **Reproducibility**: Mixed. The method is described precisely (7-bit thresholds, four per Threefry word, Δ⌊G/Δ+R⌋ formulation, equations 5–6 for the correction) and implemented in plain JAX without custom kernels, which helps portability. But there is no code release, all experiments are on Google-internal TPU infrastructure (Pathways, MaxText), and readers cannot easily check the full-step benchmarks; the FFN-block kernels (Pallas fusions for the MoE expert correction) are internal.
- **Significance**: For INT8-on-TPU training this is directly actionable: it turns SR from a 0.58x-BF16 liability into a 1.24–1.32x speedup. For the broader field, the VPU-bottleneck diagnosis applies to any accelerator where vector throughput lags matrix throughput. That said, the GPU ecosystem is converging on FP8 with fine-grained scaling (which needs no SR) and Blackwell's cvt.rs hardware SR — the paper benchmarks neither, so its practical reach outside TPU INT8 is undemonstrated.
- **Red flags**: (1) The abstract's "1.24x to 1.32x" is dense-only; on the two MoE models INT8 is actually *slower* than BF16 (Qwen3-30B-A3B: 7305 ms RTNE vs 6454 ms BF16), and only comparisons against independent SR are reported there — correctly scoped but easy to over-read. (2) The best learning rate per configuration was selected on seed 0, which is also included in the reported mean — a small but real selection bias the authors disclose. (3) The two-way timings on dense models include a per-channel rounding-variance statistic the update never uses (disclosed as pessimistic, but it muddies the dX-only vs two-way comparison). (4) Full-step benchmarks use synthetic sequences, so throughput numbers do not reflect data-loading or real-token sparsity. (5) Uncorrected S3R needed half the learning rate (3e-4) — a sensitivity that suggests the uncorrected variant is fragile even before quality loss shows.

## Strengths

- Attacks a real, precisely quantified systems bottleneck (120–133 vector ops per element; 56.5% busy time at W1) rather than a strawman, and the fix requires no custom kernels or hardware support.
- The theory is not decorative: Lemma 2/Proposition 1/Corollary 1 directly predict which correction term matters, and Section IV-C confirms the predictions (dX-only suffices for Φ2D but not Φbcast), including an elegant causal ablation that isolates the activation-gradient pathway.
- S3R-EC does not merely recover independent SR's error — measured gradient errors drop *below* it (0.476 vs 0.572 bottom-layer dX; 0.377 vs 0.456 dW), showing the control variate actively removes variance.
- The one-quantization-for-both-backward-GEMMs trick independently halves backward quantization work and S8 HBM writes, and the factor all-gather shrinks an FP32 all-reduce from 422.6 MiB to 3.1 MiB per step on a two-layer model.
- Unusual experimental candor: noise floors, seed-band overlaps, Welch t values, pessimistic timing disclosures, and memory overheads (0.4–0.7 GB on MoE) are all reported.

## Limitations

- Convergence evidence is limited to a 12-layer / 32.8M-token / 2000-step run with 3 seeds; the paper's own power analysis (±~0.05 loss) cannot distinguish S3R-EC from independent SR, and no large-scale or long-run training validates the claim at the 8B–30B sizes used only for throughput benchmarks.
- No downstream-task results (perplexity is the only quality metric), and no comparison against FP8 recipes or hardware-assisted SR (Blackwell cvt.rs) on quality or speed.
- All results are TPU v5p + INT8; the VPU bottleneck argument plausibly transfers to GPUs, but nothing is measured there, and the INT8-vs-FP8 ecosystem trade-off is not discussed empirically.
- On MoE models the method never beats BF16 (INT8 itself is slower there), and the benefit over independent SR shrinks to 1.04–1.07x after correction; expert GEMMs receive only the channel-mean part of the correction.
- Small FFN shapes (W1/W2) pay +13–15% correction overhead and can drop below BF16 speed (0.94–0.96x), so the win is shape-dependent; the correction also adds 8–14% temporary memory on single blocks.
- The 7-bit threshold grid introduces a bounded bias of Δ/256 (tiny but nonzero), and best-LR sensitivity of uncorrected S3R (needing 3e-4 vs 6e-4) hints at optimization-path interactions not fully analyzed.

## Relevance

这篇论文同时命中"高效 LLM 训练/低精度量化"与"系统-算法协同设计"两个方向：它把 INT8 随机舍入从 VPU 瓶颈解救出来并给出可证明无偏的误差修正，对关注大规模预训练成本、TPU/GPU 量化训练 recipe 与随机舍入理论的工作都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does S3R-EC's equivalence to independent SR survive a full-scale pre-training run (≥100B tokens, ≥8B parameters), where the ~0.03 loss-gap noise floor could compound into measurable downstream degradation?
- Can the shared-threshold-plus-mean-correction idea be pushed to 4-bit formats (NVFP4/MXFP4) where SR is already used post-Hadamard, and does the activation-gradient offset analysis still hold at such coarse grids?
- How does S3R compare against Blackwell's hardware cvt.rs stochastic rounding on GPUs — is the vector-unit bottleneck equally severe, and does the one-quantization-for-both-GEMMs trick transfer?
- Why is INT8 slower than BF16 on the MoE models (e.g., Qwen3-30B-A3B: 7305 vs 6454 ms) — is it expert-GEMM shape inefficiency, and can S3R recover the BF16-vs-INT8 gap rather than only beating independent SR?
- Does subtracting the structured mean error interact with optimizers beyond AdamW (e.g., Muon, SGD with momentum), given the authors show rounding variance contributes at most 0.094 of AdamW's second-moment estimate?
- Could the correction be extended to shared thresholds across *tokens* (Φbcast-like) with a cheaper communication scheme, making per-example sharing viable and cutting PRNG work another factor of S?
