Critical edge · Read it critically

“Rotations undercut the headline: with Hadamard incoherence processing the W4A4 gap nearly closes (+10.6% softmax vs +9.3% recurrent), so the “easier” advantage mostly holds only in rotation-free pipelines.”

And there is no head-to-head comparison against Q-Mamba, Quamba2, or SSDi8 under a shared protocol.

Methodology — distilled

Two coordinated studies, then one recipe

1 · Extra-state (weights & activations)

A controlled 1.3B “mixer zoo” — softmax ± QK-norm, linear attention, Mamba-2, GLA, GDN, KDA — on one shared 24-layer backbone with an identical 100B-token training recipe; only the mixer varies, cleanly isolating its effect on GPTQ W4A4. Residual-channel ablations and kurtosis link softmax’s giant outlier channel to attention-sink behavior.

2 · Intra-state (the recurrent state)

Only the stored state St is quantized — dequantized, updated, and requantized every decode step, unlike a write-once KV cache. Metric: Δℓ, median cross-entropy rise vs the FP32 trajectory. Three propositions cover grouping-axis error, decay’s exponential suppression of past errors, and why stochastic rounding eliminates RTN’s error-reinforcement cross-terms.

3 · LoReS recipe & serving

Unrotated INT grid→ Value-axis grouping→ Symmetric absmax→ Stochastic rounding→ Decay-ranked promotion→ INT8 state in vLLM

Validated end-to-end on Qwen-3.5 9B, Nemotron-H 8B, and Kimi-Linear 48B-A3B over GSM8K, MMLU-Pro, GPQA, AIME, and RULER-32k, with paired-repetition TPOT timing on a dedicated H100. Code: github.com/camail-official/LoReS.

Batch-1 gain ≈ 1.0× — single-request latency barely moves. INT4 weights help most at small batch: the two are complementary.

  • Value- vs key-axis grouping: INT6 Δℓ 0.004 vs 0.157 on Qwen-3.5 (~39×); INT4 explodes everywhere (0.825), motivating mixed precision.
  • Stochastic rounding vs RTN: Δℓ drops 4× (Nemotron-H) to ~6.6× (Kimi-Linear) on the production hybrids.
  • Decay-based promotion to “INT5”: Qwen-3.5 Δℓ 0.825 → 0.006 — nearly the full-INT6 value (0.004).
  • Weights, not states, are the bottleneck: 4/8/8 drops Nemotron-H AIME 51.2 → 41.0 and Kimi-Linear GPQA 63.5 → 52.6.
  • Rotations can hurt: repeated state requantization creates scale feedback loops and query-aligned rounding noise — isolated via oracle-scale diagnostics.

Strengths

What the paper gets right

  • Correct decomposition — extra-state vs intra-state maps cleanly onto how real PTQ stacks are deployed.
  • Controlled isolation — the mixer zoo (same backbone, same 100B-token recipe) is the only clean way to answer “does the mixer change the quantization problem?”
  • Operational theory — simple, proven propositions that directly dictate grouping axis, bit allocation, and rounding choice.
  • A rare, well-diagnosed negative result — rotations hurting repeated requantization, with two mechanisms isolated experimentally.
  • Production-grade validation — three real hybrid checkpoints (9B / 8B / 48B-A3B), five benchmarks incl. RULER-32k, open vLLM code.

Limitations

Where to hold the enthusiasm

  • Rotations erase the W4A4 advantage — the “easier” claim mainly benefits stacks that skip incoherence processing.
  • No shared-protocol baseline — Q-Mamba, Quamba2, SSDi8 never compared head-to-head; only self-ablations.
  • Long decodes suffer most — AIME / GPQA show the largest state-quantization losses; low-bit states are safest exactly where decoding is shortest.
  • Scale and proxy caveats — controlled evidence at 1.3B / 100B tokens; Δℓ rests on eight 2048-token WikiText-2 sequences.
  • Narrow serving scenario — synthetic prompts, 31 timed decode tokens, prefill excluded, single H100; batch-1 speedup ≈ 1.0×.

Questions for further exploration

Open threads pulled from the summary

  1. Would learned rotations (SpinQuant-style, decode-aware) dodge the scale-feedback and query-alignment failure modes — or is the penalty fundamental to requantization?
  2. Can quantization-aware training close the 4-bit-weight gap (Nemotron-H AIME 51.2 → 41.0 at 4/8/8)?
  3. Can state storage and low-precision chunked-prefill paths like SSDi8 share one coherent bit budget?
  4. Does the weaker-residual-outlier finding persist at ≥7B pure-recurrent scale, or under RL post-training with very long decodes?
  5. How well does the Δℓ proxy predict degradation on 32k+-token agentic decode trajectories where error accumulation compounds?
  6. Could GPU-native microscaling formats (MXFP) replace decay-based per-head promotion with hardware-managed mixed precision?
