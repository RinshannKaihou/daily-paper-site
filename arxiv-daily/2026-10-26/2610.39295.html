Critical Assessment, red flags (i)–(ii)

Methodology — one tilt, five theorems

A policy is a point w on the simplex of M solution strategies shared across N prompts; binary verifiable reward gives pass@1 = ⟨w,f⟩. No models are trained — each step is a proved statement:

  1. Tilt identity (Thm 1). KL-anchored mirror descent integrates to exactly wτ ∝ w₀·eτf; GRPO stays within TV O(δ).
  2. No free lunch (Thm 2). Stiemke duality: no free lunch ⇔ strictly positive prompt prices equalize all strategies' fitness.
  3. Crossing bound (Thm 3). Totally positive kernel ⇒ #pass@k crossovers ≤ sign variation S⁻(G), tight at 1–6; fine coverage forces overtake.
  4. Price frontier (Thm 4). Per-prompt floor γ·p₀ traces the exact pass@1↔pass@k trade; γ=1 ⇒ zero gain.
  5. Entropy side (Thm 5). Same tilt ⇒ b−R = aκ·eH/κ, exponent κ identifiable from logs.

Strengths
  • Converts a heated empirical debate (“does RLVR add reasoning or just sharpen?”) into exact conditional theorems with named assumptions, tight bounds, explicit falsifiers.
  • The Stiemke price identity turns “no free lunch” from a slogan into a dual certificate estimable from data.
  • Exceptional scholarly hygiene: precise concurrent-work demarcation, counterexamples for sharpness, no overclaiming past measured budgets.
  • The frontier explains why diversity bonuses (GRPO+PBA) help — a testable prediction, not an observation.
  • Zero adoption cost: two columns from an existing eval log return the bound, the order-preserving test, and the price.
Limitations
  • No new experiments on real models; the only external evidence is one transcribed table the authors themselves call “corroboration, not proof.”
  • Assumptions 1–2 stylized and unverified on real workloads; regime B shows the visible crossover can be absent while the per-prompt loss is present.
  • Population-limit, finite-strategy, binary-reward abstraction: no KL schedules, process rewards, or multi-step credit assignment.
  • The GRPO→tilt bridge (Prop. 1) is numerical, not proven — and measured only on synthetic instances.
  • Strategy-level entropy law can oppose token entropy (2.77→2.14 vs. 6.85→7.27); five sampling budgets cannot localize crossovers.
Questions for further exploration
  • Can Stiemke prices be estimated from a real RLVR run via strategy clusters extracted from sampled trajectories?
  • Does fine coverage hold empirically for math/code workloads — or is regime B (contingent, not forced, crossovers) the norm?
  • Does a time-varying KL anchor move you along the frontier, or off it?
  • Is the crowding exponent κ measurable from reward–entropy traces, and does it differ between math and code?
  • Does training on pass@k objectives pick a different point on the frontier, or shift the frontier itself?
  • What happens when RLVR creates new strategies, violating the fixed-Q premise of the theorem?
