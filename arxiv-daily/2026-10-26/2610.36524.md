> **一句话总结：** 提出受控证据聚合（CEA）探针与双干预因果选择方法（BUC），在冻结的 Pythia-410M 中仅用 1–12 个注意力头即可移除 65.5–91.6% 的"计数效应"，且仅在符号提示上选出的头集在自然语言证据计数任务上使准确率下降 8.2 个百分点（随机对照仅 0.8），证明符号探针定位的头具有跨模态因果影响力。

> **TL;DR:** The paper introduces Controlled Evidence Aggregation (CEA) — prompts varying only the counts/order of two symbols B and C, with preference read out as a logit difference — and Balanced Utility Causal selection (BUC), which greedily picks attention-head sets jointly maximizing ablation-based removal (shrink) and bidirectional mean-patching recovery (match). In frozen Pythia-410M at counts (50,150), ablating 1–12 BUC-selected heads removes 65.5–91.6% of the count effect versus only 0.020–0.069 shrink for layer-matched random controls, and on a lexically balanced natural-language "which state has more reports" bridge task, an 8-head set selected only on symbolic yes/no prompts cuts mean accuracy from 0.772 by 8.2 pp versus 0.8 pp for random sets (NL-selected sets: 15.2 pp; transfer ratio 0.555). Conditional theory (attribution-to-intervention bounds, finite-sample shortlist recovery, greedy guarantees under weak submodularity) is provided but the greedy bound covers the raw utility, not the normalized objective BUC actually optimizes — and a registered scan shows only ~6% of 1024 fixed-set readout conditions meet the strong-reuse thresholds.

## 核心贡献

- **受控证据聚合（CEA）行为靶标**：在固定模板、固定序列长度（200 个证据符号 + 50 个 A 尾部）的提示中，只改变两种符号 B/C 的数量（如 50 vs 150）与排列方式（FRONT/BACK/INTERLEAVE/RANDOM 四种规则），用冻结 Transformer 在最后一个位置上两个候选答案 token 的 logit 差度量偏好变化（"计数效应"）。提示不指定正确答案，从而无需训练符号规则即可定位因果组件。
- **平衡效用因果选择（BUC）**：用两个互补干预联合评估头集——消融（ablating 头输出后计数效应减少多少，shrink 分数）与均值修补（把头输出替换为另一计数条件的均值后能否双向恢复效应，match 分数，且惩罚过冲）。按归因分数 |Δs_i| 短列出 192 个头、筛到 48 个、贪心搜索至多 12 个头，目标为 Q_λ = λ·shrink + (1−λ)·match（λ=1/2），FP32 规则取首个满足 shrink ≥ 0.85 且 mean match ≥ 0.70 的前缀。
- **三条条件理论结果**：T1 在有界归因失配假设下给出干预分数与累计归因 F(H,r) 的偏差界；T2 给出从有限配对样本恢复总体归因短名单的次高斯集中条件（需相邻名次的总体分数间隔 > 2η）；T3 在单调性与弱次模性下给出对原始效用 J_λ 的贪心近似界（α = 1 − e^{−γ}），并明确指出该界不直接覆盖 BUC 实际优化的归一化目标 Q_λ。
- **自然语言桥接任务**：16 条支持两个互斥状态的报告，直接与否定句式平衡（每个状态词恰好出现 8 次），使词频捷径失效；双向查询使恒定答案必然失败。用仅由符号提示选出的头集检验对真实语言决策的因果迁移。
- **异常彻底的自我审计**：BF16 原始研究与原生 FP32 复核分开报告不混池；含读出对注册扫描、算子/贪心审计（负边际占比、次模比最小值、选择遗憾）、R ANDOM 的选择匹配零假设检验（99 次试验，Holm 校正 p=0.01），附录 H 明确列出哪些统计量没有置信区间。

## Key Contributions

- **Controlled Evidence Aggregation (CEA) behavioral target**: prompts vary only the counts (e.g., b=50 vs 150) and ordering (FRONT/BACK/INTERLEAVE/RANDOM) of two symbols B and C inside a fixed template with a 50-symbol tail and fixed answer cue; the response is the logit difference between two audited answer tokens (e.g., "various" vs "successful") at the final prompt position. No answer is designated correct, so no symbolic rule needs to be trained.
- **Balanced Utility Causal selection (BUC)**: a dual-intervention selection objective. Ablation (zeroing selected head writes) yields a removal score (shrink); mean patching (replacing writes with the opposite-count condition's mean) yields a bidirectional recovery score (match, which penalizes overshoot and is reported per direction). Greedy search over a 48-head screened pool (shortlisted from 192 by attribution |Δs_i|), sets of up to 12 heads, evaluated jointly in one forward pass; the FP32 rule accepts the first prefix with shrink ≥ 0.85 and mean match ≥ 0.70.
- **Conditional theory separating three error sources**: T1 bounds how far ablation/patch gains can be from cumulative attribution F(H,r) under a bounded-attribution-mismatch assumption; T2 gives finite-sample conditions (sub-Gaussian paired scores, rank-gap > 2η_{n,δ}) for exact recovery of the population top-m attribution shortlist; T3 gives a (1 − e^{−γ})-approximate greedy bound for the raw utility J_λ under monotonicity and weak submodularity — with the authors explicitly noting it does not cover BUC's normalized Q_λ.
- **A lexically balanced natural-language bridge**: 16 reports over two mutually exclusive states with affirmative/negated forms balanced so each state word appears exactly 8 times; each item is queried in both directions so constant-answer shortcuts fail. This tests whether heads selected purely on symbolic prompts causally matter for natural-language evidence decisions.
- **Extensive self-auditing infrastructure**: separate BF16 and native-FP32 studies with no pooling; a registered-readout scan over 1024 fixed-set conditions; operator/monotonicity audits (negative marginal fractions, minimum submodularity ratios, maximum selection regret); a selection-matched exchangeability null for RANDOM; and an explicit accounting of which statistics lack confidence intervals.

## Methodology

The frozen model is EleutherAI/pythia-410m-deduped (24 layers, 16 heads/layer), with the original study in BF16 and audits in native FP32. CEA fixes a prompt template with L_E=200 evidence symbols (b copies of B, L_E−b of C) plus a tail of 50 A's; four ordering regimes define prompt distributions D_{r,b}. For counts b_H > b_L (main: 50 vs 150), the count effect is the contrast Δ_r = E[y|b_H] − E[y|b_L] on the logit-difference readout y(x). Attribution scores each head i by the contrast Δs_i(r) of its unembedding-projected write ⟨v_i(x), d⟩ where d = W_U(w+) − W_U(w−); BUC shortlists heads by |Δs_i|, screens with single-head interventions, then greedily maximizes Q_λ over sets, evaluating each candidate jointly with the current set (capturing interactions a fixed per-head ranking misses). Selection and evaluation use separate 48/96-row batches per count; controls are 120 layer-matched random draws per seed over five execution seeds. Theory: Assumption 4.1 bounds ablation/patch residual contrasts by ε_int; Theorem 4.2 turns this into bounds on |N−F|, |Γ−F| and on normalized score error ε_int/|Δ_r| (weak contrasts amplify normalized error); Theorem 4.3 gives sub-Gaussian concentration for paired attribution estimates; Theorem 4.5 gives the greedy bound for raw utility. The natural-language bridge defines a correct restricted label a*(x,q) = 1[q(2b−16) > 0] over 16 balanced reports queried in both directions, with selection on 3 state pairs and evaluation on unseen pairs plus separately written paraphrase banks; a transfer ratio (TR) divides the transferred symbolic set's normalized joint score above random by the NL-selected set's score above random. Scope checks include LAST-vs-SEQ intervention positions, QK-vs-OV split patches, word-symbolic/signal-token/short-sentence input variants, model scaling (Pythia-70M–1B, GPT-2, Qwen3-4B-Base), and an input-only Greedy Occlusion comparison.

## Results & Highlights

- **Main symbolic result (Pythia-410M, counts (50,150), Table 1)**: BUC sets of 1–12 heads remove 65.5–91.6% of the count effect (shrink) across the four ordering regimes — FRONT needs a single head (layer 10, head 2) for shrink 0.906 / match 0.974; INTERLEAVE needs 3 heads for 0.916/0.914; RANDOM 7±1 heads for 0.866±0.021/0.823±0.084; BACK uses all 12 heads yet only reaches 0.655/0.759. Layer-matched random controls achieve HL-match shrink of only 0.020–0.069 and match 0.048–0.075.
- **Natural-language bridge (Table 5, BF16, five-seed mean)**: base restricted accuracy on held-out state pairs and paraphrases is 0.772 (PairAcc 0.604, MinPairAcc 0.583; graded response Spearman 0.901, R²=0.812). Ablating 8 heads selected only on symbolic yes/no prompts lowers mean accuracy by 8.2 percentage points, versus 0.8 pp for layer-matched random sets and 15.2 pp for sets selected directly on NL prompts (equal budget k=8); transfer ratio 0.555 — the symbolic set sees no NL prompt during selection.
- **Expanded FP32 bridge across three models (Table 6, Banks B–D, 8 test domains)**: Pythia-410M 0.752 accuracy, TR 0.520, 7.4 pp drop; Pythia-1B 0.805 accuracy, TR 0.557, 8.6 pp drop; GPT-2 0.681 accuracy, TR 0.438, 4.9 pp drop — partial but consistent transfer, though these are model-level point estimates without CIs.
- **BUC vs the sensitivity-only baseline (Table 2)**: at matched head budgets, BUC raises bidirectional match by 0.083–0.111 while reducing shrink by 0.018–0.049 in BACK/INTERLEAVE/RANDOM (FRONT ties at displayed precision); in the FP32 fixed-budget k=8 comparison the joint-score Q_{1/2} differences are +0.006 to +0.039, and only RANDOM has a prompt-bootstrap CI of [+0.015, +0.065] — the margin over the baseline is modest.
- **Count generalization and honesty checks**: fixed sets selected at (50,150) give slope reductions of 0.646–0.896 over the count sweep b∈{0,…,200} and unseen-gap shrink/match of 0.612–0.839 / 0.689–0.874 on new pairs (0,150),(25,175),(75,125); yet the registered FP32 readout scan finds only 0.060 of 1024 fixed-set conditions (0.064 of evaluable ones) pass both the shrink ≥ 0.85 and match ≥ 0.70 thresholds — strong fixed-set reuse across arbitrary readouts is uncommon, which the authors state plainly.
- **Attribution and operator audits**: across 500 sampled head sets per regime, cumulative attribution F(H,r) correlates with ablation gain at Pearson 0.841–0.923 and with patch gains at 0.755–0.884; FP32 operator audits find negative marginal fractions of 17–31% and minimum empirical submodularity ratios of −5.8 to −2.6 (observed nonmonotonicity), with maximum local selection regret 0.021–0.043; the RANDOM selection-matched null yields raw utility 2.69 [2.55, 2.80], Holm-adjusted p = 0.01 at Monte Carlo resolution.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The CEA target (count/order manipulation with a no-correct-answer logit-difference readout) is a clean incremental idea over existing controlled-probe work; the genuinely distinctive part is the dual removal+bidirectional-recovery criterion with overshoot penalty, and the paired symbolic→NL transfer test at matched budgets with layer-matched controls.
- **Theory-practice gap**: Theorem 4.5's greedy guarantee covers the raw utility J_λ under monotonicity and weak submodularity, but BUC optimizes the normalized Q_λ — the authors admit this mismatch — and their own FP32 audits find 17–31% negative marginals and submodularity ratios as low as −5.8, i.e., Assumption 4.4 is violated on the observed pools. The theory is honest but largely conditional decoration for the experiments.
- **Weak baseline margins**: against the sensitivity-only selection baseline, BUC's improvement is mostly in the match component (+0.083–0.111) while shrink actually drops 0.018–0.049; both methods fail the joint 0.85/0.70 thresholds in BACK; and in FP32 only the RANDOM regime has a bootstrap CI ([+0.015, +0.065]) — the other three regimes' differences are point values on fixed deterministic inputs.
- **Statistical soft spots acknowledged by the authors themselves**: the BF16 NL bridge lacks its declared bootstrap intervals; the expanded FP32 bridge uses one seed for causal selection, has no model-level CIs, and averages transfer ratios only over estimable conditions (unavailable ratios are not zero-filled — a defensible but favorable choice).
- **Generalization caveats**: the registered-readout scan shows only ~6% of 1024 fixed-set conditions pass strong-reuse thresholds, suggesting the seven targeted readout pairs were a favorable sample; the count manipulation confounds count with symbol positions (the paper concedes the design "does not identify count independently of every positional feature"); interventions touch only head writes at the final prompt position, not head–MLP paths or earlier tokens; and the NL bridge cannot distinguish shared evidence processing from shared yes/no answer readout.
- **No overclaiming detected**: the conclusion sticks to "compact attention head sets whose causal influence extends to predictions in a labeled language task" — consistent with the evidence.

## Strengths

- The dual-intervention criterion (necessity via ablation + sufficiency-like recovery via bidirectional mean patching, with overshoot penalized) is materially more rigorous than ablation-only circuit discovery and directly addresses the known ablation-dependence critique.
- The lexically balanced NL bridge is well designed: equal state-word frequencies defeat bag-of-words shortcuts, and both-direction queries with PairAcc/MinPairAcc metrics detect orientation shortcuts — the 8.2 pp vs 0.8 pp symbolic-transfer result is a genuinely non-trivial finding.
- Exceptional self-auditing culture: registered scans, a selection-matched null with Holm correction, explicit CI accounting (Appendix H), and BF16/FP32 separation make the quantitative claims easy to trust at face value.
- Deterministic-regime head sets are exactly stable across five execution seeds (Jaccard 1.000), and named head identities (e.g., FRONT = single head (10,2)) are reported for reproducibility.
- Broad scope checks (QK/OV split showing OV-dominance with match 0.85–0.92 vs QK 0.12–0.35, LAST-vs-SEQ positions, model scaling to Qwen3-4B, input-only Greedy Occlusion comparison) preempt several obvious objections.

## Limitations

- Primary results rest on one model family (Pythia-410M) with auxiliary scaling checks; the strongest symbolic numbers (e.g., 1-head FRONT) may be idiosyncratic to this checkpoint.
- Compactness is regime-dependent — BACK fails the joint threshold even with the full 12-head budget (shrink 0.655) — and compactness is neither uniqueness nor global minimality.
- The BF16 bridge's headline 8.2 pp figure lacks the bootstrap intervals its own declared criterion requires; FP32 summaries use one causal-selection seed and no model-level CIs.
- Fixed-set reuse across arbitrary readout pairs is rare (~6% of registered conditions), and regime transfer is asymmetric (BF16 BACK→FRONT match 0.958 vs 0.129 reverse; FP32 off-diagonal match spans −0.84 to +0.89).
- The design cannot separate count from positional features, cannot distinguish evidence aggregation from answer-readout effects, and intervenes only at the final prompt position, leaving earlier-token computation and head–MLP paths untested.
- The greedy theory does not cover the normalized objective actually optimized, so BUC has no selection-quality guarantee beyond finite-pool audits.

## Relevance

对关注 Transformer 内部机制可解释性与因果归因的方向高度相关：它提供了如何在受控探针与真实自然语言行为之间建立因果桥梁的完整方法论模板（双干预选择、层级匹配对照、迁移比率），可直接借鉴到证据聚合、检索增强场景中"模型如何使用上下文证据"的定位研究。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the selected heads (e.g., (10,2), (17,3), (15,10)) retain their causal role on longer, heterogeneous real-world evidence such as RAG passages or multi-document summaries, where counts are implicit?
- Can evidence aggregation be causally separated from yes/no answer readout, e.g., by intervening at earlier sequence positions or along head–MLP paths as the authors suggest?
- Why does the FRONT regime collapse to a single head while BACK needs 12 and still fails the shrink threshold — is this a property of positional attention patterns or of the pretrained checkpoint?
- Given only ~6% of registered readout conditions pass strong-reuse thresholds, how sensitive are the seven targeted readout pairs to selection bias, and would a fully pre-registered pair set shrink the symbolic→NL transfer (TR ≈ 0.44–0.56)?
- How does the count-vs-position confound interact with the count-generalization results — would position-controlled variants (same counts, shifted placements) select different head sets?
