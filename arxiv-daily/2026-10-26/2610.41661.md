> **一句话总结：** 本文首次系统刻画了激活转向（activation steering）在改变 LLM 目标行为的同时对 15 个话语维度（如正式性、证据支撑、推理深度、歧义性）造成的非预期偏移，并提出在每个受影响维度上叠加一个互补修正向量（β=k|α|）的干预方法，在 3 个模型、11 个数据集上恢复了约 78% 的非预期话语偏移，同时以 92.8% 的比例保留了预期的转向行为。

> **TL;DR:** Across 3 open-weight LLMs (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen3-8B, Gemma-2-9B-IT) and 11 Model-Written Evaluation (MWE) datasets, Contrastive-Activation-Addition steering significantly shifts 15 human-validated discourse dimensions in 86% (424/495) of Wilcoxon tests — steered text becomes more ambiguous and contradictory, less formal, and less evidence-backed, shifts that perplexity barely captures (max |Kendall τ| = 0.38). Adding a per-dimension correction vector alongside the behavior vector restores the unsteered discourse score in 78% of intervened generations (vs 0% under plain CAA; 83% when removing an induced dimension vs 74% when adding one back), while the intended steered behavior is retained in 92.8% of generations (vs 100% with no intervention, i.e., a ~7-point cost), with per-dimension reliability ranging from 93% (evaluative language) down to 48% (repetition).

## 核心贡献

- **首个话语级别的转向副作用分类体系**：通过"LLM 开放式提名 → 人工合并 → LLM 评分 → 人机一致性过滤"四步流程，从 1,028 个原始候选维度中提炼出 15 个在激活转向（AS）下会显著改变的话语维度（歧义性、矛盾、隐喻、正式性、证据支撑、推理深度、术语使用等），并用人类标注验证（非稀疏维度平均加权 κ = 0.48，稀疏维度平均 PABAK = 0.90）。
- **证明困惑度测不出这些副作用**：各话语维度与困惑度的相关性最多只有中等（最高 0.38，Qwen 的语境化维度），说明此前用困惑度/连贯性分数监控 AS 副作用的做法会漏掉具体语言属性的变化。
- **系统性的偏移规律**：在 3 个模型 × 11 个数据集 × 15 个维度上，86%（424/495）的统计检验显著；转向后的生成更歧义（94% 的检验中增加）、更自相矛盾（91%）、更多隐喻（76%），且更不正式、更少证据支撑。
- **可保留转向行为的修复干预**：为每个受影响维度提取一个"维度向量"，与行为向量同时注入残差流（β = k|α|，强度随转向强度缩放），提供三种变体（同层叠加、异层叠加、正交投影去纠缠），在 78% 的干预样本中把话语维度恢复到未转向水平，同时 92.8% 的样本保留了预期的转向行为。
- **机制性解释**：行为向量与话语向量在干预所用的中间层余弦相似度接近零（近似正交、可独立操控），但在靠近输出的层相似度增大——这解释了为什么转向行为会连带改变话语属性。

## Key Contributions

- **A fine-grained, human-validated taxonomy of 15 discourse dimensions** that change under activation steering (AS), distilled from 1,028 raw candidate dimensions via open-ended LLM nomination, manual consolidation, LLM-judge scoring (0/1/2 presence scale), and human–LLM agreement filtering (mean weighted κ = 0.48 for 12 non-sparse dimensions; mean PABAK = 0.90 for 3 sparse ones).
- **Evidence that perplexity misses these side effects**: Kendall τ_b between perplexity and each discourse dimension stays low-to-moderate (max 0.38, for contextualization in Qwen), so prior perplexity/coherence-based monitoring cannot see which text properties AS actually damages.
- **A large-scale empirical audit**: across 3 models (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen3-8B, Gemma-2-9B-IT), 11 Model-Written Evaluation datasets (8,284 datapoints), and 15 dimensions, 86% (424/495) of two-sided Wilcoxon tests (Benjamini–Hochberg corrected) show significant discourse shifts; steered text is systematically more ambiguous, more contradictory, more metaphorical, less formal, and less evidence-backed.
- **A restoration intervention with steerability preservation**: a per-dimension "discourse vector" extracted from judge-labeled contrastive data is injected alongside the behavior vector with strength β = k|α|, in three variants (same-layer addition, separate discourse layer, orthogonal projection removing the component along the behavior vector). It restores the unsteered dimension score in 78% of interventions while the logit-difference keeps its sign (steered behavior retained) in 92.8% of generations.
- **A mechanistic partial explanation**: cosine similarity between behavior and discourse vectors is near zero at the mid layers where interventions act (they are independently steerable there) but grows toward later layers, entangling discourse with behavior — explaining why behavior steering leaks into discourse.

## Methodology

The authors steer with **Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA)** (Rimsky et al., 2024): for each binary-choice question from the MWE datasets (Perez et al., 2023), the behavior vector v_b at layer ℓ is the difference of mean last-prompt-token residual activations over positive vs. negative options; at inference it is added with multiplier α. Per-sample steerability is the slope of the logit-difference d_i(α) against α, and the best layer is the one maximizing average steerability on a validation split. Multiplier ranges are model-specific (±0.5–±2.0 for Llama, ±0.75–±3.0 for Gemma, ±1–±4 for Qwen).

**Dimension identification (4 steps):** (1) an LLM judge (DeepSeek-V4-Flash) open-endedly names differing discourse dimensions on 288 stratified unsteered–steered pairs, yielding 1,028 candidates; (2) one human merges/drops to 26 dimensions; (3) the judge scores every generation on each dimension as 0 (absent), 1 (moderate), 2 (strong); (4) 120 human-annotated pairs (judge run 3× at temperature 0.6, majority vote) filter to 15 dimensions with moderate-or-better directional agreement (weighted κ ≥ 0.41; 3 sparse dimensions retained via PABAK).

**Restoration intervention (3 steps):** (1) For a dimension d, the judge's labels form contrastive datasets of unsteered generations scored 0 vs. 2; the dimension vector v_d is the difference of mean last-token activations, extracted at the layer most steerable for that dimension (which can differ from the behavior layer). (2) The intervention adds/subtracts v_d with strength β = k|α| (s ∈ {+1,−1} chooses direction) in three variants: same layer as the behavior vector; a separate layer ℓ′; or only the component of v_d orthogonal to v_b. (3) Success = the re-generated output's dimension score returns to its unsteered value (re-judged by the same LLM judge, plus a 484-sample manual check); behavior retention = the logit-difference d_i keeps its sign; steerability is re-measured as a ratio to plain CAA. Interventions are applied to samples whose score flips 0→2 or 2→0 under steering (capped at 400 per direction), on 6 dimensions: formality, reasoning depth, terminology use, contextualization, evaluative language, and repetition (the latter's vector extracted from steered generations due to data sparsity). Experiments used greedy decoding on NVIDIA A100/L40s GPUs (~500 GPU-hours); code is promised upon acceptance.

## Results & Highlights

- **Breadth of side effects:** across the 3 models × 11 MWE datasets × 15 dimensions, **86% (424/495)** of two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (Benjamini–Hochberg FDR-corrected, α = 0.05) show a significant unsteered–steered difference — AS shifts discourse widely, not occasionally.
- **Most consistent shifts:** ambiguity increases in **94%** of significant tests, contradiction in **91%**, metaphors in **76%** (every significant metaphor test negative in rank-biserial terms; mean correlations ≈ −0.80, −0.75, −0.37 across the three models). Conversely, unsteered generations score higher on formality in **46%** of tests (vs 30% in the opposite direction) and on claim-supported-by-evidence in **49%** (vs 33%) — steered text is less formal and less evidence-backed.
- **Perplexity is a weak proxy:** the highest correlation between perplexity and any discourse dimension is **τ = 0.38** (contextualization, Qwen); all others are lower — coarse quality metrics used in prior work miss these dimensions.
- **Restoration success:** the intervention returns the dimension to its unsteered score in **78%** of cases overall — **83%** when removing a steering-induced dimension vs **74%** when adding one back; by model: Qwen **85%**, Llama **84%**, Gemma **67%** (only **62%** in Gemma's add direction). By dimension: evaluative language **93%**, terminology use **86%**, formality **83%**, reasoning depth **80%** (strongly asymmetric: **96%** subtract vs **63%** add), contextualization **79%**, repetition worst at **48%**.
- **Steering behavior preserved:** the logit-difference keeps its sign after intervention in **92.8%** of samples across methods (vs 100% with no intervention, i.e., a ~7-point cost), with one clear failure mode: adding formality in Qwen retains only **61.0%**. The per-sample steerability ratio vs. plain CAA stays at **0.86–0.89** on average across the three intervention variants, so responsiveness to steering is only slightly reduced.
- **Human check of intervened generations:** of 484 manually labeled successful interventions, **74.0%** are clean, 16.3% contain repetition, 4.1% are nonsensical, and 5.8% other defects — restoration does not wreck output quality.
- **Human–LLM judge agreement:** mean directional weighted κ = **0.48** over the 12 retained non-sparse dimensions (threshold 0.41); the 3 sparse dimensions reach mean PABAK = **0.90**.

## Authors & Credibility

The five authors (Shivam Adarsh, Hetianyu Huang, Lau Safin, Maria Maistro, Christina Lioma) are from the University of Copenhagen; the senior authors (Maistro, Lioma) publish in IR/NLP venues and the first author has a prior ACL 2026 long paper on geometric transformations of truth representations in LLMs, so the group has relevant track record in representation-level analysis. The paper follows solid methodology: preregistered-style statistical rigor (Wilcoxon + Benjamini–Hochberg correction, rank-biserial effect sizes, multiple agreement statistics — Cohen's κ, ICC, Krippendorff's α, Gwet's AC1, PABAK), three model families, human validation of both the dimension taxonomy and the intervened outputs, and a transparent limitations section. Code is only promised "upon acceptance," which slightly weakens reproducibility commitments, but all datasets and models are public and greedy decoding is used.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine and well-scoped. Prior work measured AS side effects with perplexity or a single coherence score; this is the first fine-grained, dimension-level audit plus a targeted fix. The orthogonal-projection intervention variant and the cross-layer cosine-similarity analysis of behavior/discourse entanglement are nice mechanistic touches.
- **Rigor:** Mostly strong (FDR-corrected tests, effect sizes, human agreement, three intervention variants). But there is a **circularity concern**: the same LLM judge (DeepSeek-V4-Flash) that defines the contrastive datasets for the dimension vectors also scores intervention success — a judge biased toward its own vectors would inflate the 78% figure. The 484-sample human check mitigates but does not eliminate this, and it only covers *successful* interventions.
- **A hidden oracle in sample selection:** interventions are applied only to samples whose judge score flips 0→2 or 2→0 under steering (capped at 400 per direction). The authors honestly note this requires a detection step that does not yet exist; real deployments would not know where correction is needed, so the 78%/93% numbers are upper bounds on practical performance.
- **Missing baselines:** the intervention is never compared quantitatively against existing side-effect mitigations the paper itself cites (Stickland et al. 2024 fine-tuning, Vogels et al. 2026 in-distribution steering, SAE-SSV sparse-feature steering) — we cannot tell whether a second steering vector beats simpler calibration.
- **Weak spots in the results:** repetition succeeds only 48% because its vector must be extracted from steered generations (entangled with the behavior direction); Gemma's add direction manages just 62%; and per-sample "smallest successful k" selection is per-sample tuning that a fixed-k deployment policy would not enjoy. Adding formality in Qwen breaks steering retention (61.0%).
- **Scope limits:** only ~8–9B instruction-tuned models, only CAA, only binary-choice MWE prompts — generalization to larger models, other steering variants, and open-ended generation is untested. The LLM-judge scale (0/1/2, moderate human agreement, mean κ = 0.48) is coarse, and some dimensions correlate with word count (up to ~0.6), partially confounding "discourse shift" with length shift.

## Strengths

- First systematic, dimension-level account of AS side effects, replacing a single perplexity number with 15 interpretable, human-validated discourse dimensions.
- Large, careful experimental matrix: 3 model families × 11 datasets × 15 dimensions × 8 steering multipliers, with significance testing, FDR control, and effect sizes rather than anecdote.
- The intervention is conceptually clean (a complementary per-dimension vector with strength tied to α) and comes with three variants, an overshoot analysis justifying smallest-k selection, and cross-dimension side-effect checks.
- Behavior preservation is measured three ways (sign of logit-difference, steerability ratio, human defect labeling) — the paper takes seriously that a fix which undoes steering would be useless.
- Honest, specific limitations section that names the oracle-selection problem and the LLM-judge dependence.

## Limitations

- Requires knowing which samples/dimensions need correction; no detection mechanism is provided, so deployment needs an extra step the paper leaves open.
- The fix is itself steering, inheriting sensitivity to oversteering; correction strength k is tuned per sample in the evaluation.
- Only one dimension is restored at a time; joint restoration of multiple shifted dimensions is untested.
- Findings are tied to CAA on 8B/9B-scale models with binary-choice MWE prompts; transfer to other steering methods, scales, and open-ended tasks is unverified.
- All dimension scores depend on an LLM judge with only moderate human agreement (mean κ = 0.48) on a coarse 0–2 scale; some dimensions are length-confounded (τ up to ~0.6 with word count).

## Relevance

这篇论文直接触及激活转向/表征工程这一推理时行为控制方向的核心痛点——副作用，并给出了细粒度的话语级度量与可操作的修复方法，对关注 LLM 可解释性、可控生成与模型安全对齐的研究非常有参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the 0↔2 flip selection be replaced with a cheap online detector (e.g., a small classifier on the discourse vectors) so the intervention works without oracle sample selection, and how much does the 78% success rate drop?
- Do the findings transfer to other steering families (ACT Add, SAE-feature steering, conditional steering) and to larger models, where behavior/discourse entanglement across layers may differ?
- Can multiple discourse vectors be restored jointly without mutual interference, given that mid-layer orthogonality breaks down toward later layers?
- Is the LLM-judge circularity material — would a human-scored or classical-NLP (e.g., register/metric-based) evaluation of restoration success and of the dimension taxonomy change the headline 78%/92.8% numbers?
- Since some dimensions correlate with output length (τ up to 0.6), how much of the "discourse shift" survives after controlling for generation length?
- Could the discourse vectors themselves be reused positively — e.g., deliberately adding reasoning depth or evidence to unsteered generations — and does that dual-use direction also preserve factuality?
