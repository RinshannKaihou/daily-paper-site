of tree-detector faults are metric-silent — recall and false-positive rate both shift < 0.1 pp, so a defective detector is indistinguishable from a healthy one under any output-level monitoring. (Rule detectors: 68.4%.)

Why this matters

Direct guidance for deploying ML-security silicon: monitor integrity, not outputs; prefer comparator detectors; harden a few critical units instead of paying full redundancy.

故障位数量 ≠ 脆弱性；输出监控无法发现沉默故障——对部署硬件 ML 安全 IP 与预算受限的容错设计直接有用。

⚠ Critical edge

The headline hardening result — 60.1 → 3.3 pp worst-case recall loss — is predicted, not measured: disjoint units, additive costs, ideal correction, apportioned area. Gate-level agreement is explicitly unvalidated.

Methodology — the fault campaign

  1. 144 trained configs from a published design space: 18 classifier families × 16/8/4/2 hardware-performance-counter budgets + K=10 AdaBoost/Bagging ensembles; each backed by synthesizable RTL with zero prediction mismatches.
  2. 5,585,493 structural fault sites enumerated at the architectural level (comparator tree, rule list, table, weight vector, ensemble vote).
  3. 453,174 faults evaluated — decision stuck-at + parameter bit-flips (integer thresholds, IEEE-754 weights) — as paired deltas vs the fault-free baseline on the 7,400-instance validation corpus.
  4. Consequence classification of every fault; criticality = evasion.
  5. Exact minimax selective hardening, then the fault campaign is re-run on the hardened design rather than trusting the prediction.

Faults live in the structural model, not a gate netlist — trading physical fidelity for interpretability (a fault names the architectural element that failed) and portability across synthesis targets.

Concrete extremes. A 4-HPC SGD detector has only 25 fault sites, but 28% cause evasion and 44% DoS — a single bit flip drives recall to 0%. The MLP's worst fault also zeroes recall; its bagged version has zero critical faults and recall never drops below 63.6%.

Strengths

  • First fault analysis of HPC-based malware detectors across an entire validated design space — prior HMD work (RHMD, EnsembleHMD, ObfusGate) only attacked inputs, assuming intact logic.
  • Consequence taxonomy (evasion / DoS / silent / noisy / degraded) replaces mean-accuracy loss with defender-relevant classes; the ε = 0.1 pp silence bound is justified by an explicit power analysis (> 7×10⁴ labeled observations to detect in-field).
  • Validation discipline at scale: bit-exact software models, RTL with zero mismatches, honest exhaustive(101) / sampled(27) / time-limited(12) split, confidence intervals throughout.
  • Actionable engineering: prefer comparator families at reduced budgets; choose Bagging, not boosting; harden shallow comparators near the root; treat detection — not correction — as the safe state for a security monitor.

Limitations — read with the edge card in mind

  • Architectural injection only. No gate netlist; synthesis-introduced faults excluded, and architectural-vs-physical agreement is admitted unvalidated — even for family ordering.
  • Hardening is predicted, not silicon. Area is apportioned, correction ideal; carrying hardened RTL through the open-PDK flow is future work.
  • KNN blind spot. Stored-sample faults — the largest memory structure in the whole design space — are not enumerated at all (vote weights only).
  • Same-corpus evaluation. Fault deltas are measured on the 7,400-instance validation set, not held-out programs; silent fractions could shift on unseen workloads.
  • Single-fault adversary. Coordinated multi-fault injection, plausible for a physical attacker, is unaddressed; 12/140 configs are time-limited with no campaign-wide sampling margin.

Questions for further exploration

  • Do gate-level injections agree with architectural results — does the 72.3% silent fraction survive a real synthesized netlist?
  • How do silent/critical fractions move on a held-out corpus of unseen programs and malware families?
  • Can an adversary placing two or three coordinated faults bypass the selectively hardened J48 design, and at what cost?
  • What is the cheapest runtime check for silent faults — re-seeding with labeled traces, structural self-test, or parameter integrity hashing?
  • Do ransomware-specific HMD variants (HiPeR, DeepWare, Rapper) inherit the same fault surface — could silent faults delay encryption detection past the point of damage?
  • Can the exact minimax selection extend to correlated unit failures (shared logic, common power domains) where disjoint-units and additive-cost assumptions break?
