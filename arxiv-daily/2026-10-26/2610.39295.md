> **一句话总结：** 本文将 RLVR 建模为"策略空间上的指数倾斜"（w_τ ∝ w_0·exp(τf)），严格证明了在策略专化条件下每一个提升 pass@1 的更新必然降低某些 prompt 的成功率（Stiemke 对偶给出的精确"无免费午餐"条件），该损失传导到最难 prompt 时必然迫使 pass@k 曲线被基座模型反超（交叉次数上界为成功分布位移的符号变差数），并证明约束优化可精确刻画 pass@1–pass@k 交换前沿——想完全消除反超就必须放弃全部 pass@1 增益。

> **TL;DR:** A single-author theory paper that models RLVR as an exponential tilt over solution strategies shared across prompts and proves a no-free-lunch theorem: under exclusive specialization every pass@1 gain strictly lowers some prompt's success, and under a "fine coverage" condition the base model must overtake the trained model at large k. Applied to published curves (Qwen2.5-7B GRPO vs. its base on Omni-MATH, from Zhou 2607.20543 Table 2), the GRPO advantage decays +0.149 → −0.008 from k=1 to k=256 (a single forced crossover), and the paper's constrained-update frontier shows the only way to remove the crossover is to forfeit the entire pass@1 gain. A diagnostic package reads the crossing bound and the "price" from any evaluation log.

## 核心贡献

- **倾斜恒等式（定理 1）**：证明了带 KL 锚定的 mirror descent / 自然策略梯度在二值可验证奖励下，训练后的策略恰好是基座策略的指数倾斜 w_τ ∝ w_0·exp(τf)；并给出命题 1 说明 GRPO 与最优拟合倾斜的总变差为 O(δ)（软件包实测 < 3×10⁻⁴）。
- **无免费午餐定理（定理 2）**：在"排他专化"（每个策略是某正测度 prompt 集合的唯一解法）下，任何提升 pass@1 的更新必然严格降低某个 prompt 的成功率。精确条件由 Stiemke 对偶给出：存在严格正的 prompt 价格 y > 0 使所有策略的加权适应度相等。文中还证明"某策略只是某 prompt 上的唯一最优"并不足够（命题 2 给出反例：近通才策略可吸收转移的概率质量，产生 +0.173 的免费午餐）。
- **pass@k 价格定理（定理 3）**：任意基座→RL 的位移中，pass@k 曲线的交叉次数不超过每 prompt 成功率 CDF 差 G 的符号变差数 S⁻(G)（基于全正核的 Karlin 变差缩减性质），且该界是紧的（构造出恰有 1–6 次交叉的实例）；在"精细覆盖"（最难 prompt 只被低于平均适应度的策略覆盖）下交叉被强制触发；保序情形退化为先前工作（Yang & Chen 2609.22547）的单交叉定理。
- **价格前沿（定理 4）**：对每 prompt 施加下界约束 p_x(w) ≥ γ·p_x(w0) 的线性规划精确刻画了 pass@1 与 pass@k 的交换率：γ=1 时实现一阶随机占优、无交叉，但在专化下 pass@1 增益为零——"不付价格的唯一方法是一无所获"。
- **统一熵侧理论（定理 5 + 引理）**：同一倾斜恒等式导出熵坍缩与熵–性能定律 b−R = a_κ·exp(H/κ)(1+o(1))，指数 κ（近优策略的拥挤度）可由 log(b−R) 对 H 的斜率直接读出；κ=1 时恢复 Cui 等（2505.22617）的指数律。引理 1 给出熵先升后降的精确条件，引理 2 证明 pass@k 反转必须以"跨 prompt 共享策略"为前提。
- **可实操的诊断工具**：只需任意评测日志中已有的每 prompt 基座/RL 成功率，即可返回交叉次数、符号变差上界、保序性检验和估计的价格；并提议将"最坏 pass@k 损失"和交叉计数作为与 pass@1/pass@k 并列的标准报告量。

## Key Contributions

- **Tilt identity (Theorem 1).** Under mirror descent / natural policy gradient on strategy logits with a KL anchor and binary verifiable reward, the trained policy is exactly the exponential tilt w_τ ∝ w_0·exp(τf). Proposition 1 shows GRPO stays within total variation O(δ) of the best-fit tilt; the accompanying package measures this TV below 3×10⁻⁴ over the tested range.
- **No free lunch (Theorem 2).** Under exclusive specialization, every pass@1-increasing update strictly lowers the success of some prompt. The exact condition is Stiemke duality: there exist strictly positive prompt prices y ∈ R^N_{>0} equalizing all strategies' weighted fitness. The hypothesis is sharp — being the unique-best strategy on a prompt is provably insufficient (Proposition 2's counterexample has a near-generalist absorb defunded mass for a +0.173 free lunch), while dominant specialization (margin (M−1)ε < d/2) suffices.
- **The pass@k price (Theorem 3).** For any base-to-RL shift, the number of pass@k crossovers is at most the sign variation S⁻(G) of the success-CDF difference, via the variation-diminishing property of the totally positive kernel k(1−p)^{k−1}; the bound is tight (constructions with exactly 1–6 crossings). Under fine coverage of the hardest prompts (Assumption 2), the crossover is forced (Δ_k < 0 for large k); the order-preserving case recovers the single-crossover theorem of concurrent work (Yang & Chen 2609.22547) as a corollary, filling in the kernel case their Remark 1 leaves open.
- **The price frontier (Theorem 4).** A per-prompt floor p_x(w) ≥ γ·p_x(w0) turns the trade into a linear program in Q whose solution traces the exact pass@1-vs-pass@k exchange rate: γ=1 enforces first-order stochastic dominance (no crossover) but zero pass@1 gain under specialization; a closed form exists for M=2 strategies. This explains why diversity bonuses (e.g., GRPO+PBA) help both metrics only when the unconstrained update was off-frontier.
- **Unification of the entropy literature (Theorem 5, Lemmas 1–2).** The same tilt yields the entropy-performance law b−R = a_κ·exp(H/κ)(1+o(1)) with an identifiable exponent κ (near-optimal strategy crowding), recovering Cui et al. (2505.22617) at κ=1; an exact entropy-rate identity d/dτ H = −Cov(f, log w0) − τ·Var(f) that predicts the transient entropy bump; and a proof that pass@k inversion requires strategies shared across prompts (tabular per-prompt policies are monotone).
- **A practical diagnostic.** A package and hosted app that consume only per-prompt base/RL success rates (already present in any evaluation log) and return the crossing count, the sign-variation bound, the order-preserving test, and the estimated price — proposing "worst pass@k loss" and crossing count as standard companions to pass@1/pass@k reporting.

## Methodology

The paper is pure theory with a verification package, not an empirical training study. The object modeled is a distribution over M solution strategies shared across a finite set of N prompts: strategy j solves prompt x with probability q_xj, and a policy is a point w on the strategy simplex, giving per-prompt success p_x(w) = ⟨w, q_x⟩, pass@1 = ⟨w, f⟩ (f = marginal fitness), and pass@k via 1−(1−p_x)^k.

**Step 1 — the update as a tilt (Theorem 1).** In the population limit, GRPO-style updates with a KL anchor coincide with mirror descent / natural policy gradient on strategy logits; for binary verifiable reward the expected advantage is f_j − ⟨w, f⟩, so integrating the flow gives w_τ ∝ w_0·exp(τf) exactly. Proposition 1 bounds the GRPO deviation (group-standard-deviation rescaling) by O(δ) in total variation. Because the tilt multiplies w_{0,j} by e^{τ(f_j − Λ(τ)/τ)} with Λ(τ)/τ ≥ R_0 (Jensen), a strategy loses mass iff f_j < Λ(τ)/τ — the paper notes the looser threshold f_j < R(τ) is incorrect, with a worked numeric example (f = (0.9, 0.75, 0.5, 0.1), τ=5: R(τ)=0.814 but Λ/τ=0.720, and the f=0.75 strategy still gains mass ×1.16).

**Step 2 — no free lunch via a theorem of the alternative (Theorem 2).** A "free lunch" is a tangent direction dw (Σdw=0) with Q·dw ≥ 0 and ≠ 0 — a pass@1 gain no prompt regresses from. Parameterizing the tangent cone and applying Stiemke's lemma yields the exact iff condition: no free lunch ⟺ ∃ y > 0, μ with Σ_x y_x·q_xj = μ for all j (strictly positive prompt prices equalizing strategy fitness). Exclusive specialization makes this feasible trivially (defunding strategy j strictly hurts prompts only j can solve); a near-generalist breaks it.

**Step 3 — crossing bound via total positivity (Theorem 3).** Integration by parts gives Δ_k = −k∫(1−p)^{k−1}G(p)dp with G = F_τ − F_0. The kernel e^{−(k−1)s} is strictly totally positive, so by Karlin's variation-diminishing property the sign changes of k ↦ Δ_k are at most S⁻(G); tightness uses geometrically spread success levels and Descartes' rule for exponential sums. Fine coverage (every strategy covering the minimal-success prompt has below-base-average fitness) routes the forced per-prompt loss into the hard tail, making G > 0 near p = 0 and Δ_k < 0 at large k (Watson's lemma).

**Step 4 — the frontier (Theorem 4).** Constrain each prompt to keep a fraction γ of its base success; maximizing pass@1 over the simplex is an LP whose optimum traces a monotone frontier from the unconstrained GRPO point (γ=0) to zero gain at γ=1.

**Step 5 — the entropy side (Theorem 5).** If the gap density near the best fitness behaves as c·g^{κ−1}, the tilted gap is Gamma(κ, τ), so b−R ≈ κ/τ and the effective surviving count scales as τ^{−κ}; eliminating τ gives b−R = a_κ·e^{H/κ}. The authors carefully attribute the exact finite-window and terminal laws to concurrent work ("Winner-Take-All", ICLR 2027 submission) and claim only the identifiable exponent and the unification.

**Verification.** No models are trained. Every numeric claim is regenerated by an accompanying package (github.com/yliusci/no-free-lunch-rlvr), and the single empirical application re-reads already-published pass@k tables (Zhou 2607.20543, Qwen2.5-7B GRPO on Omni-MATH/MATH500) through the diagnostic.

## Results & Highlights

- **The published-curve reading (Omni-MATH, Qwen2.5-7B, GRPO vs. base; Zhou 2607.20543 Table 2):** the GRPO-over-base advantage is +0.149, +0.144, +0.104, +0.030, −0.008 at k = 1, 4, 16, 64, 256 — a single sign change between k=64 and k=256, exactly the order-preserving single-crossing regime of Theorem 3(d). On MATH500 neither trained model crosses base for k ≤ 256 (the price, if any, lies beyond the measured budget).
- **Diversity intervention explained, not contradicted:** GRPO+PBA beats raw GRPO at every measured k and stays above base through the measured range on Omni-MATH; the frontier (Theorem 4) explains this because unconstrained GRPO over-concentrates and sits inside the frontier — a diversity bonus moves toward the frontier, deferring (not removing) the price past k=256.
- **No-free-lunch verification:** the Stiemke primal and dual agree in 100% of instances across six generators; exclusive specialization yields no free lunch in 1000/1000 instances and dominant specialization in 2000/2000, while an adversarial near-generalist generator produces a free lunch in 500/500 instances — confirming the hypothesis is sharp (unique-best is insufficient).
- **Crossing-bound verification:** the bound S⁻(k ↦ Δ_k) ≤ S⁻(G) is never violated on 3000 random instances, tightness constructions attain exactly 1–6 crossings, and the order-preserving uniqueness holds in 1035/1035 instances. The forced-crossover scope is equally sharp: under fine coverage a crossover appears in 400/400 instances; when it fails, 0/400 (and in that regime-B counterexample family, 96% of instances still show a per-prompt loss but only 15% an aggregate crossover — the per-prompt price is unconditional, the crossover is not).
- **Frontier verification:** at γ=1 the constrained update achieves zero crossings and first-order stochastic dominance in 400/400 instances, with worst per-prompt loss at machine zero and zero median pass@1 gain — "the only way to pay nothing is to gain nothing."
- **Tilt-law verification:** for crowding exponents κ ∈ {0.5, 1, 2}, the fitted slopes of log(b−R) vs. entropy H are 2.006, 0.986, 0.487 against the predicted 2, 1, 0.5 — within 2.6%. The entropy identity holds to 2×10⁻⁶, and the package exhibits a case where strategy entropy falls 2.77 → 2.14 while token entropy rises 6.85 → 7.27, underscoring that Theorem 5 is a strategy-level law.

## Authors & Credibility

Single author, Yinuo Liu (no affiliation block visible in the PDF body; code and verification package released at github.com/yliusci/no-free-lunch-rlvr). The paper is positioned in the "named-phenomenon pure-theory" archetype (like hallucination-is-inevitable and grokking-theory papers), and its credibility rests on unusually careful scholarship rather than institutional weight: every theorem is proved in a 3-page appendix using classical tools (Stiemke's lemma, Karlin total positivity, Watson's lemma, Descartes' rule for exponential sums), concurrent work is demarcated honestly and in detail (Section 7 explicitly yields the exact entropy-mass curve and terminal law to the "Winner-Take-All" ICLR 2027 submission, and the single-crossover theorem to Yang & Chen 2609.22547, claiming only the general tight kernel bound, the no-free-lunch condition, the forced-crossover scope, and the frontier), falsifiability conditions are stated outright ("two statistically supported sign changes under an order-preserving shift would contradict Theorem 3(d)"), and all numeric verification is delegated to a regenerable package rather than cherry-picked plots. The one empirical application deliberately uses only transcribed published tables and is labeled "corroboration, not proof."

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine and well-scoped. The exponential-tilt model of RLVR and the pass@k crossover phenomenon are both known (Yue et al. 2504.13837; Yang & Chen 2609.22547; Cui et al. 2505.22617), but the per-prompt no-free-lunch duality with its Stiemke price, the tight kernel crossing bound closing concurrent work's Remark 1, the forced-crossover scope with an explicit counterexample regime, and the LP price frontier are new results, and the unification of the pass@k and entropy literatures under one identity is a real conceptual contribution.
- **Rigor:** High for the model-level claims — full proofs, tightness constructions, an exact counterexample for the insufficient hypothesis (Proposition 2), and a named wrong-threshold pitfall (Λ(τ)/τ vs. R(τ)) with numbers. However, the bridge from model to actual transformers rests almost entirely on Proposition 1, which is stated "as a proposition with numerical evidence, not a theorem," and its 3×10⁻⁴ total-variation figure is measured on synthetic instances, not on any real GRPO run.
- **Reproducibility:** Strong by theory-paper standards — a public verification package regenerates every number, and the diagnostic consumes standard evaluation logs. But the headline empirical reading depends on values "transcribed and to be confirmed against the source" from Zhou 2607.20543 Table 2 at only five budgets (k ∈ {1,4,16,64,256}), which cannot locate a crossing precisely, a caveat the paper itself makes.
- **Significance:** If Assumptions 1–2 hold in practice even approximately, the paper settles the Yue-vs-ProRL debate mechanically (both observations are regimes of one conditional theorem) and gives the field a cheap, principled reporting standard (price + crossing count). The significance is conditional on those assumptions being more than stylized.
- **Red flags:** (i) The two load-bearing assumptions — exclusive/dominant specialization and fine coverage of the hardest prompts by below-average-fitness strategies — are never verified on any real model's evaluation log, even though the paper's own diagnostic could in principle test them; the sole empirical section reads only aggregate pass@k curves, which cannot distinguish regime A from regime B. (ii) One dataset pair (Omni-MATH/MATH500), one model (Qwen2.5-7B), one training recipe (GRPO), zero new training runs — the "evidence from published curves" is a single transcribed table. (iii) The citation of an anonymous ICLR 2027 submission by OpenReview ID (S4R2zYtFhs) is unusual and unverifiable for readers. (iv) As a strategy-level abstraction, the model says nothing about token-level dynamics, and the paper itself shows strategy and token entropy can move in opposite directions (2.77→2.14 vs. 6.85→7.27), which limits direct contact with the token-entropy metrics practitioners actually monitor. (v) The claim "we settle the question with a theorem" is arguably overclaimed relative to what a finite-strategy population-limit bandit model can settle about deep networks.

## Strengths

- Converts a heated empirical debate (does RLVR add reasoning or just sharpen?) into exact conditional theorems with named assumptions, tight bounds, and explicit falsifiers.
- The Stiemke price identity (6) is a genuinely illuminating object: it turns "no free lunch" from a slogan into a dual certificate that could be estimated from data.
- Exceptional scholarly hygiene: precise demarcation of concurrent work, sharpness via counterexamples (Proposition 2, Theorem 3(e)), a stated wrong-turn pitfall (the R(τ) threshold), and no overclaiming past measured budgets.
- The frontier result explains, rather than merely observes, why diversity interventions like GRPO+PBA help — they correct off-frontier over-concentration, which is a testable prediction.
- The diagnostic lowers the adoption cost to zero: two columns from an existing evaluation log yield the bound, the order-preserving test, and the price.

## Limitations

- No new experiments on real models; the only external evidence is one transcribed table (Omni-MATH, five budgets) that the authors themselves call "corroboration, not proof."
- Assumptions 1 (exclusive specialization) and 2 (fine coverage) are stylized and unverified on real workloads; regime B shows the visible phenomenon (crossover) can be absent even when the mechanism (per-prompt loss) is present, so the theorem's practical bite is conditional.
- The population-limit, finite-strategy, binary-reward bandit abstraction omits KL strength schedules, multi-step credit assignment, process rewards, and any token-level dynamics; GRPO proximity to the tilt is numerical, not proven.
- Theorem 5 is explicitly mesoscopic and strategy-level; its relation to the token-entropy curves reported in practice is indirect (the paper's own example has the two entropies moving oppositely).
- The hosted diagnostic reading of digitized curves inherits digitization error, and five sampling budgets cannot localize crossovers.

## Relevance

直接命中"RL Post-Training Dynamics & Failures"方向：该文对 RLVR/GRPO 训练的核心失败模式——pass@k 反转与熵坍缩——给出了统一的机制性理论（指数倾斜 + 无免费午餐对偶），并提供了可从现有评测日志直接读取的检测工具（交叉计数与最坏 pass@k 损失），属于该领域失效模式的检测与刻画而非泛泛的推理评测。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can Assumptions 1 and 2 be tested on a real RLVR run by extracting per-prompt success and approximate "strategy" clusters from sampled trajectories, turning the Stiemke prices into estimated quantities?
- Does the fine-coverage condition (hard prompts covered only by below-average-fitness strategies) hold empirically for math/code workloads, or do hard prompts typically retain high-fitness backup strategies (regime B), which would mean most observed crossovers are contingent rather than forced?
- How does the frontier's exchange rate change under process rewards, multi-turn RL, or KL schedules that vary β over training — does a time-varying anchor move you along the frontier or off it?
- Can the tilt law's exponent κ be estimated from real training logs (reward-vs-entropy traces), and does it differ between math and code domains in a way that reflects near-optimal strategy crowding?
- Does training directly on pass@k objectives (e.g., pass@k policy optimization) simply pick a different point on the same frontier, or can it genuinely shift the frontier by changing the fitness vector f itself?
- The model treats strategies as fixed; what happens when RLVR also creates new strategies (e.g., bootstrapped reasoning), which would violate the fixed-Q premise of the no-free-lunch theorem?
