> **一句话总结：** 用静态+渲染双客户端在 3 天内抓取 10,307 个 URL（六个群体，含 4,795 个 ExpertQA 引用源），测得静态 HTTP 200 响应中 20.9% 根本不含页面正文（外壳页/质询页/拦截页/替身页/部分内容），13.1% 的站点对随机不存在的路径也回 200（软 404）；把 200 当正文会让 ExpertQA 引文"可核查率"虚增 12.2 个百分点（52.6% vs 40.5%）、把"静默引用漂移"率从 5.8% 夸大到 21.9%（3.8 倍），而"文本长度 <930 字符 OR 重定向到根 OR 探针相似度 ≥0.3"三个廉价启发式即可在约 5% 误报下挽回 86% 的不可用响应——性价比优于零样本 LLM、TF-IDF 分类器和微调 Qwen3-1.7B 等学习型检测器。

> **TL;DR:** A web-measurement study aimed at agents and answer engines that treat an HTTP 200 as "content fetched". Across 10,307 fetches over three days (six populations: llms.txt links, three Tranco rank bands, Markdown-serving sites, and 4,795 URLs cited by ExpertQA), 20.9% of static 200 responses carry no usable main content (app shells, bot challenges, block pages, substitutes, partial content; reviewed-sample estimate 20.0%, 95% CI 13.3–27.7%), and 13.1% of probed sites soft-404 a random non-existent path. The error propagates: on 6,156 ExpertQA claim–source pairs, status-only checking overstates checkable pairs by 12.2 points (52.6% vs 40.5%) and inflates apparent silent citation drift 3.8x (21.9% vs 5.8% with outcome labels). Against the status-code baseline (recall 0.00 by definition) and crawl4ai's is_blocked (0.25), a tuned trio of cheap heuristics ("H-best": redirect-to-root OR probe similarity ≥ 0.3 OR < 930 chars of text) recovers 86% of unusable responses at a 5.6% false-alarm rate, while the best learned detector (TF-IDF logistic regression) adds only +5.1 points of recall (95% CI +1.1 to +10.0 — below the pre-registered 0.10 margin) at more than twice the false-alarm rate (0.133). Practical takeaway: fetch tools should return an outcome type, not a status code, and escalate to rendering on a flag — rendering only the ~20% flagged pages yields as much usable content as always rendering (81.4% vs 80.3% on test-split pages) at 5.8 s vs 20.6 s per page.

## 核心贡献

- **提出 200 响应的七类"抓取结果"分类法与可用性规则**：A 正文 / B 拦截 / C 质询（bot 检查、验证码）/ D 外壳页 / E 错误或替身页 / F 停放域名 / G 同意墙；B–G 均不可用；另用"保留率 r"（静态文本覆盖渲染页句子与数值事实的比例，r<0.5 判部分内容不可用）和随机路径探针（软 404 检测）补全规则。分类以"响应的读者能否使用"定义，而非服务器意图。
- **大规模双客户端测量**：2026-10-19 至 10-21，静态 GET（K1）与 Playwright 无头 Chromium（K2）抓取 10,307 项（8,800 个双客户端 URL + 1,507 个仅静态探针），覆盖六个 agent 相关群体（llms.txt 链接、Tranco top-10k、Markdown 站、10k–100k、100k–1M、ExpertQA 引用源）。核心数字：静态 200 中 20.9% 不可用（llms.txt 9.8% 最低，Tranco top-10k 26.3% 最高，ExpertQA 引用源 23.2%）；人工复核样本把测试集估计从 25.5% 下修到 20.0%。
- **失真分析（对评测的直接影响）**：在 ExpertQA 的 6,156 对 claim–source 上，"200 即正文"使可核查配对份额虚增 12.2 点（52.6%→40.5%）、表观静默漂移率从 5.8% 膨胀到 21.9%（3.8 倍），且 274 个表观漂移中 217 个（79%）实为不可用响应（96 个错误/替身/停放页、73 个拦截/质询页、48 个外壳/部分页）；死链测量同样失真——按状态码 11.8% 死，加上被 200 掩盖的替身页后内容缺失率达 16.0%。
- **检测器大比拼（站点不相交切分）**：从状态码（H0）、crawl4ai 的 is_blocked（H1）、文本长度（H2）、软 404 探针对比（H3）、重定向到根（H4）、质询字符串（H5），到零样本 LLM（M1, Qwen3-32B）、TF-IDF 逻辑回归（M2）、微调 Qwen3-1.7B（M3）。调优启发式组合 H-best 召回 0.86@FA 0.056（仅页面 0.79），M2 召回最高 0.91 但误报 0.133（可用引用源上高达 0.27）；结论：廉价检查是 agent 抓取工具的实践默认。
- **可落地的抓取策略模拟**：只渲染 H-best 标记的 19.6% 页面，可用内容 81.4% ≈ 全渲染的 80.3%，但平均耗时 5.8 s/页 vs 20.6 s（静态中位 1.6 s，渲染 19.7 s，约 12 倍），把被静默放行的不可用响应从 25.7% 压到 7.8%。

## Key Contributions

- **A fetch-outcome taxonomy for 200 responses** (Section 3): seven types — A content, B block, C challenge, D shell, E error/substitute, F parked, G consent wall — types B–G being unusable, plus a usability rule that also catches *partial content* by computing retention r (share of the rendered page's sentences and numeric facts present in the static text) with a r < 0.5 cut-off, and a random-path probe rule that flags soft 404s. Types are defined by what a reader of the response can use, not by server intent.
- **A two-client measurement of 10,307 fetches** (Sections 4–5) across six agent-relevant populations — llms.txt-linked pages, three Tranco rank bands (top 10k, 10k–100k, 100k–1M), Markdown-serving sites, and the 4,795 URLs cited by expert-verified ExpertQA claims — with a static GET client (K1) and a rendered Playwright/Chromium client (K2), automatic LLM labels (deepseek-v4-flash, temperature 0, prompt frozen before labelling), 300 blind reviewed labels by a different model family, a blind human check of 60 items, and a desktop-browser check of the rendered reference.
- **The headline measurement**: 20.9% of static 200 responses lack the page's main content (automatic labels), ranging from 9.8% (llms.txt links, biased toward working links by construction) to 26.3% (Tranco top 10k); the reviewed sample lowers the test-split estimate from 25.5% to 20.0% (95% CI 13.3–27.7%). 13.1% of sites that answered a random non-existent path returned 200 for it. Shells/partial content dominate in every Tranco band; challenges and blocks concentrate in cited sources (8.2% of their 200s, with four hosts — scribd.com, mdpi.com, biomedcentral.com, springer.com — accounting for 67%).
- **A distortion analysis on 6,156 ExpertQA claim–source pairs** (Section 7): treating 200 as content overstates the share of pairs checkable against live content by 12.2 points (52.6% vs 40.5%) and inflates the apparent silent citation-drift rate from 5.8% to 21.9% (3.8x); 79% of apparent drifts are unusable responses. Dead-link counting misses content gone behind a 200 (11.8% dead by status vs 16.0% content-absent). Status-only vs label-based views even reorder how fetchable ExpertQA's four answer systems look.
- **A detector bake-off on site-disjoint splits** (Section 6): from the status code and five heuristics (including crawl4ai's shipped block detector) to a zero-shot LLM, a TF-IDF classifier and a fine-tuned Qwen3-1.7B. A tuned combination of three cheap heuristics (H-best) recovers 86% of unusable reviewed responses at a ~5% false-alarm rate; the best content reader adds only 5–7 points of recall at more than twice the false-alarm rate — so cheap checks are the practical default, and agents/evaluations should report fetch outcomes explicitly instead of inferring them from the status line.

## Methodology

**Populations and clients.** All sampling used one fixed seed. Six populations: S1 = two links from each of 300 sites' llms.txt files (from a public llms.txt corpus, restricted to links recorded as answering 200 — hence biased toward working links); S2/S4/S5 = homepages plus one random same-site link from 400/400/300 sites in three Tranco rank bands; S3 = the 107 Tranco-top-10k sites that serve Markdown to `Accept: text/markdown`; P = 4,795 URLs behind 6,156 ExpertQA claim–URL pairs (claims judged complete/partial support, evidence passage ≥ 100 chars, at most three pairs per URL). Every S1–S5 site also received two random 16-hex-character paths: one labelled (soft-404 item), one unlabelled static-only reference for probe comparison. K1 = single GET, redirects followed, 20 s timeout; K2 = headless Chromium via Playwright, network-idle wait up to 8 s, saving visible text and a screenshot. Collection ran 2026-10-19 to 10-21 from one vantage point, robots.txt honoured per RFC 9309 (one disclosed exception: hosts with unreachable robots.txt were still attempted once so dead hosts stay countable), ≤ 1 request/s per host, fresh contexts (no cross-site cookies), desktop Chrome UA, never solving or bypassing a challenge.

**Labelling and verification.** Every K1 200 response (and K2's, when it returned text) was typed into the seven-type taxonomy by an LLM (deepseek-v4-flash, temperature 0) from URL, final URL, title and first 3,000 characters of visible text, with the rendered excerpt as context for K1; the prompt was fixed before any label was seen. Retention r averages sentence-level and numeric-fact coverage of the rendered page by the static text. Unusable = type B–G, or both type A with r < 0.5 (sensitivity reported at 0.3/0.7), or a random-path probe answered 200. Verification: 300 test-split items were reviewed blind to the automatic label by an LLM assistant of a different family (Claude) under written rules, stratified 12 cells (6 populations × automatic usability) × 25; a human (the author) then blindly labelled 60 of these, agreeing with the reviewed usability label on 47/57 decidable items (82.5%; κ = 0.64 usability, 0.26 type), with all 10 disagreements resolved by discussion in favour of the reviewed label; finally 50 random reviewed items were opened in a desktop browser to test the rendered reference, which missed content the browser showed for 3/47 pages (6.4%).

**Splits and statistics.** Items were assigned 60/20/20 train/validation/test by a seeded hash of registrable domain (site-disjoint), with 2,000× domain bootstrap confidence intervals.

**Detectors.** All detectors judge the static response only (as an agent would before deciding to render/retry/skip): H0 status code; H1 crawl4ai `is_blocked`; H2 text-length threshold; H3 word-3-gram Jaccard similarity against the site's second random-path probe (Bar-Yossef-style); H4 redirect-to-root; H5 challenge/block string match; H-best = OR of H1–H5 jointly tuned on validation (selected config: redirect-to-root OR probe similarity ≥ 0.3 OR < 930 characters — H1/H5 switched off). Content readers: M1 = zero-shot Qwen3-32B with the labelling prompt minus rendered context; M2 = logistic regression on TF-IDF word 1–2-grams + char 3–5-grams of title/text/URL plus heuristic features; M3 = Qwen3 sequence classifier fine-tuned on the first 1,024 tokens (protocol fallback from 0.6B to 1.7B after validation). Thresholds fixed on validation at ≤ 5% false-alarm rate; recall/FA reported on the 99-unusable/195-usable reviewed sample.

**Distortion analysis.** For each of the 6,156 ExpertQA pairs, a judge model (the labelling model, fixed prompt) compares the claim with the 2023 evidence passage and the best-matching 4,000-character window of the current static fetch; a pair "silently drifts" if the passage supported the claim but the live, status-200, usable page no longer does. The same quantities are recomputed under status-only, H-best, M1/M2/M3, and full outcome labels. A fetch-policy simulation on 920 test-split static-200 pages compares never-render, always-render, and render-on-detector-flag policies on usable %, silently-missed %, rendered %, and mean seconds per page.

## Results & Highlights

- **Scale of the problem**: of 7,293 pages, 60.2% answered the static client with 200, and **20.9% of those 200 responses are unusable by automatic labels** — from 9.8% on llms.txt links (S1) to 26.3% on Tranco top-10k pages (S2), with 23.2% on ExpertQA cited sources; the reviewed sample lowers the test-split estimate **from 25.5% (automatic) to 20.0%** (95% CI 13.3–27.7%). The automatic labels themselves have recall 0.96 but precision only 0.75 (0.67 on pages alone) — they over-flag.
- **Soft 404s are common**: **13.1% of sites whose random-path probe was answered returned 200** for a non-existent 16-hex path (S1 10.5%, S2 13.3%, S3 17.3%, S4 13.7%, S5 13.1%) — roughly double the 7.35% server-level figure Prieto et al. (2013) reported. Of 172 soft-404 responses, only 42 redirected to the root and the labeller called 28 "content": a soft 404 is partly a property of the request–response pair, not the response alone.
- **Distortion of downstream measurements (ExpertQA, 6,156 pairs)**: status-only checking says 52.6% of pairs can be checked against live content vs **40.5% with outcome labels — a 12.2-point overstatement**; apparent silent citation drift is **21.9% status-only vs 5.8% label-based (3.8x inflation)**, and 217 of the 274 apparent drifts (79%) are responses labelled unusable (96 error/substitute/parked, 73 block/challenge, 48 shell/partial). Dead-link rate is 11.8% by status vs **16.0% content-absent** when unusable 200s are counted.
- **Detector comparison (reviewed test sample, 99 unusable / 195 usable)**: status code recall 0.00 by definition; crawl4ai's is_blocked 0.25; text length alone 0.76 (0.81 on pages); **H-best reaches 0.86 recall at 0.056 false-alarm (0.79 on pages alone, without probes)**; the best learned detector M2 (TF-IDF logistic regression) reaches **0.91 recall but at 0.133 false-alarm — more than twice H-best's, and 0.27 on usable cited sources**; M3 (fine-tuned Qwen3-1.7B) is 0.82 at 0.133; M1 zero-shot LLM only 0.57 (it calls most shells "content", recall 0.33 on type D). M2's recall edge over H-best is **+0.051 (95% CI +0.011 to +0.100) — below the protocol's pre-set 0.10 margin**; in the descriptive comparison with thresholds re-set on the reviewed sample at equal 5% FA, H-best (0.84) actually beats M2 (0.69) and M3 (0.76).
- **Render-escalation policy (920 test-split static-200 pages)**: rendering only the pages H-best flags (**19.6% rendered**) yields as much usable content as always rendering — **81.4% vs 80.3%** — at **5.8 s vs 20.6 s mean per page** (median static fetch 1.6 s vs rendered 19.7 s, ~12x), cutting silently-passed unusable responses from 25.7% to 7.8%.
- **Rendering is not a cure**: for 43.5% of pages blocked from the static client (401/403/429 — 21.5% of all pages, 25.7% of cited sources), the rendered client got a 200; yet the rendered reference itself is not type A for 16.7% of static-200 items, and a desktop-browser check found K2 missed content for 3/47 pages (6.4%). Where both clients returned content, partial loss is common but moderate: median retention 0.985, 17.3% of pages lose > 10% of units, but only 2.3% fall below the r < 0.5 usability cut-off.

## Authors & Credibility

Single-author paper by Shuyang Zhang (The Hong Kong Polytechnic University), posted to cs.IR on 31 Oct 2026. The methodological hygiene is unusually high for a measurement study: the labelling prompt was frozen before any label was seen; the detector-evaluation protocol was pre-fixed with a logged list of every post-hoc change (Appendix C); reviewed labels were produced blind by a different model family than the labeller and the zero-shot detector; a blind human pass over 60 items (82.5% raw agreement, κ = 0.64 on usability, all 10 disagreements resolved in favour of the reviewed label) and a 50-item desktop-browser check validate the label chain; splits are site-disjoint with domain-bootstrap CIs; sensitivity to the retention threshold (20.0% at r < 0.3, 23.5% at r < 0.7) is reported. The ethics section documents polite crawling (robots.txt per RFC 9309 with one disclosed exception, ≤ 1 req/s/host, challenges never solved or bypassed, no login). URLs, labels, derived features and code are promised for release (not yet verifiable at posting time). No external funding or conflicts statement; the work cites and positions itself precisely against Gundelach et al. 2026 (which explicitly scoped out "content degradation within HTTP 200 responses") and Sayyid-Ali et al. WWW '26 (which measured how much answer engines fetch, not what fetches return).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: real but incremental-in-parts. Soft 404s (Bar-Yossef 2004; Prieto 2013), bot refusal pages (Ansar 2025), JS shells in archiving (Brunelle 2016) are each long documented; the novelty is (a) unifying them into one usability taxonomy of 200 responses, (b) measuring them on agent-relevant populations with a rendered-client reference, and (c) — most valuable — quantifying how the error distorts downstream evaluation numbers (drift, dead links, system rankings). The detector bake-off against crawl4ai's shipped detector is a useful practical contribution.
- **Rigor**: strong overall, with two weak joints. First, the *primary protocol comparison effectively failed*: no content reader met the ≤ 5% realized false-alarm requirement on the reviewed sample (even H-best exceeds it at 0.056), and M2's recall gain (+0.051, CI +0.011–0.100) is below the pre-set 0.10 margin; the headline "cheap heuristics win" therefore rests on the re-read comparison with validation-fixed thresholds plus a descriptive equal-FA comparison — honestly disclosed in Appendix C, but a reader should note the conclusion was salvaged by a protocol re-interpretation. Second, the drift judge is an LLM applied to *different subsets* under each scheme, so its errors need not cancel between schemes — the 5.8% → 21.9% gap may contain judge error, which the paper concedes but does not bound (e.g., no human-judged drift subsample).
- **Reproducibility**: data and code promised but not yet posted; more fundamentally, all absolute rates come from **one vantage point, one 3-day window (2026-10-19..21), one desktop-Chrome UA that did not announce itself as a crawler** — block/challenge/shell shares are known to depend on client, IP reputation and time, so the 20.9% figure is setup-specific even if the *types* of failure recur (the paper says exactly this).
- **Specific weaknesses worth flagging**: (i) the S1 "best" population (9.8% unusable) is biased by construction — only llms.txt links the corpus recorded as answering 200 — so "llms.txt links are healthiest" is partly an artifact; (ii) the test split accidentally received all 36 mdpi.com pages (35 unusable), inflating its automatic unusable share to 25.7% (22.7% without mdpi) — a domain-hash small-sample quirk disclosed only in a footnote; (iii) H3 (probe comparison) is largely circular: it catches 0.97 of the synthetic random-path probes but only 0.07 of unusable *pages*, so its standalone 0.35 recall is mostly an artifact of the probe items it was designed around, and of the 11 substitute/error *pages* in the sample H3 catches 1 and H4 catches 4; (iv) the per-answer-system drift table (Table 7) rests on 6–26 drift events per system, and per-population detector cells (Table 8) hold 5–37 items — both are suggestive only.
- **Significance**: for anyone building web agents, RAG pipelines, or citation-verification evaluations, this is a directly actionable reliability result — the failure mode is silent, the detection recipe is exact and deployable (< 930 chars OR redirect-to-root OR probe similarity ≥ 0.3), and the render-escalation simulation comes with wall-clock numbers.
- **Red flags**: none serious — no overclaiming beyond what the CI supports; the reviewed-sample estimate (20.0%) is *lower* than the automatic headline (20.9%), and the paper consistently reports both.

## Strengths

- Fills a precisely named gap: Gundelach et al. (ARES 2026) explicitly left "content degradation within HTTP 200 responses" out of scope; this paper measures exactly that, on the populations agents actually fetch (llms.txt, Tranco bands, ExpertQA citations).
- A three-layer label-verification chain (frozen-prompt LLM labeller → blind second-family LLM review → blind human check + browser check of the rendered reference), with the labeller's own error rates (recall 0.96 / precision 0.75) reported rather than hidden.
- The distortion analysis is the crown jewel: it converts a measurement into consequences, showing 3.8x inflation of apparent citation drift, 12.2-point overstatement of checkability, 4.2-point undercount of content-absent URLs (11.8% → 16.0%), and warning that re-fetch-based evaluations would rank answer systems partly by the fetchability of their sources.
- Deployable output: the exact H-best rule, per-type recall breakdown (recovers 39/48 shells, 11/12 challenges, 27/30 substitutes), a coded error analysis of what each detector misses, and a fetch-policy simulation with time budgets (5.8 s vs 20.6 s per page).
- Exemplary transparency: Appendix C logs every protocol decision made after data were seen, with timestamps and reasons, and the paper repeatedly reports the estimates that undercut its own automatic labels.

## Limitations

- Single vantage point, single IP, three days, non-announcing desktop-Chrome UA: absolute shares of blocks/challenges/shells are specific to this setup; a residential, cloud, or honestly-labelled AI-crawler vantage point would plausibly shift them (and robots.txt/AI-crawler interactions are known to be worsening).
- All labels ultimately anchor on LLM judgement (deepseek labeller, Claude-family reviewer, Qwen3-32B zero-shot detector — different families, but correlated failure modes on unusual pages are possible); the human check covers only 60 items and the reviewed sample 300, giving a wide headline CI (13.3–27.7%).
- The drift-judge confound is unbounded: because each scheme passes a different page subset to the same LLM judge, part of the 5.8% vs 21.9% gap could be judge behaviour on degenerate text rather than true drift.
- Rendering is treated as the escalation oracle, but the rendered reference itself fails (not type A) for 16.7% of static-200 items and missed browser-visible content in 6.4% of the browser check — the true usable share is likely a bit lower than every estimate in the paper.
- Scope is visible-text-centric and mostly English; non-text content, file downloads, and paywalled-then-consent flows beyond type G are not handled; the retention metric (sentences + numeric facts) cannot detect loss of images, tables, or interactive data.

## Relevance

直接命中"Agent 与流水线静默失败"方向：论文量化了 agent 工具层最底层——网页抓取——中"HTTP 200 但无正文"的静默失败（20.9%），证明它会系统性扭曲引用验证/死链类评测结论（漂移率虚增 3.8 倍），并给出可立即落地的检测规则与按需升级渲染策略。对构建 RAG/web agent 管线及可信评测的可靠性工程是即插即用的一课。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How do the unusable-200 rates move across vantage points (residential vs cloud vs datacentre IPs), over time, and with an honestly-declared AI-crawler user-agent — does politeness pay or punish?
- Would a two-stage cascade (cheap H-best first, learned M2 as second-stage verifier on borderline pages) dominate both detectors in the recall/FA/time trade-off, given M2's 0.27 false-alarm rate on usable cited sources?
- Can a small model fine-tuned on the 300 *reviewed* (rather than automatic) labels close the false-alarm gap that kept learned detectors impractical?
- How much of the 5.8% → 21.9% drift inflation survives a human drift judge on a stratified subsample — i.e., how much of the gap is the unusable pages vs the LLM judge's behaviour on degenerate text?
- Do production answer engines' live question-time fetches (à la Sayyid-Ali et al. WWW '26) show the same ~20% unusable-200 share, and do their citation-groundedness metrics currently count shells as "evidence read"?
- Does the llms.txt ecosystem stay healthier than the general Web as adoption grows, once the S1 bias toward corpus-recorded working links is removed?
