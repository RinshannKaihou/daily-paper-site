> **一句话总结：** 针对 Adam 两个 FP32 矩占检查点大头且直接量化易破坏更新的问题，本文提出"带护栏"的压缩：利用恢复前后两次更新共享同一梯度与参数这一观察，对更新之差（而非各自输出区间）做带 FP32 舍入误差界的区间 enclosure，配合 8-bit 对数码 + 失败坐标精确 FP32 回退，在 τ=0.01 下把百万级 OPT-125M 训练坐标的矩负载从朴素区间法的 126.6% 降到 FP32 的 28.3%，120 次完整检查点恢复中首个更新全部严格落在容差内。

> **TL;DR:** The paper compresses Adam's two FP32 moment tensors for checkpointing with a per-coordinate *guarantee*: decoded moments are accepted only if they reproduce the native FP32 Adam update to within τ=0.01 learning-rate steps for every gradient |g|≤0.2; failing coordinates are stored as exact FP32. The textbook separate-range interval certificate catastrophically fails (98.9% of 1M trained OPT-125M coordinates escape; payload balloons to 126.6% of raw FP32), but bounding the *difference* of the two updates — which share the same gradient and parameter — with FP32 rounding-error balls and a mixed linear+log₂ gradient partition cuts escapes to 0.53% and the payload to 28.3%. Across 120 complete-checkpoint restores of OPT-125M (C4-style LM training) and ResNet-18/CIFAR-10 (4 seeds × 3 training fractions each), guarded 8-bit log codes shrink moment payloads by 69.5%/71.9% vs FP32, 40.8%/45.4% vs equally guarded BF16, and 29.3%/37.6% vs equally guarded bitsandbytes blockwise 8-bit — while every restored first update stays below τ (max 0.9992τ). Cost: encode is 7.2–7.6× FP32 serialization and peak encode memory rises (24.9 vs 19.2 GiB), and the certificate covers only the first restored update under a pinned Torch 2.8 scalar-FP32-Adam operation model.

## 核心贡献

- **共享梯度差界（shared-gradient bound）**：指出认证压缩 Adam 状态的本质是"依赖问题"（dependency problem）——源更新与恢复更新消费同一梯度 g 和参数 w，朴素地把两个更新各自在梯度区间上做区间算术会丢掉这层抵消，导致 98.9% 坐标逃逸、负载膨胀到 126.6%；对两个商做代数差后，梯度项与参数项在差中消去，界随矩重构误差 |m−m̃|、|v−ṽ| 缩放而非随更新对 g 的敏感度缩放。
- **原生 FP32 舍入误差球 + 混合梯度划分**：为固定操作契约（Torch 2.8 标量 FP32 Adam，β₁=0.9、β₂=0.999、ε=10⁻⁸）的每条运算路径叠加向外舍入的误差半径，并在梯度定义域 [−0.2, 0.2] 上采用线性 + 以 2 为底对数的混合固定划分（对数边下探到 2⁻⁴⁰C），两部件互补：仅当共享梯度被保留后混合划分才起效，二者合力把逃逸从 988,820 降到 5,324（0.53%），负载降到 28.3%。
- **护栏式编解码器（guarded codec）与全字节记账**：每 256 坐标块提出 8-bit 线性（m，含精确零）与 8-bit 对数（v）码，按读者方式精确解码后逐坐标双重护栏（ideal-real 界 + 原生界），失败坐标以精确 FP32 转义；序列化格式计入每个头、表、码、掩码与转义字节，带 SHA-256 校验，独立解码器必须逐位复现解码状态。同一护栏无差别地施加于 BF16 与官方 bitsandbytes 0.48.1 块式 8-bit 码。
- **机制—基线—成本分离的评测**：受控消融（只变界与划分）、同等护栏基线对比、120 次完整检查点恢复 + 100 步自梯度续训、独立进程配对计时；并证明联合码搜索不值得：比独立取整只省 <0.001% 字节却多花约 6% 编解码时间。

## Key Contributions

- **A shared-gradient enclosure for certified Adam-state compression.** Ranging the two updates separately over gradient intervals is provably too loose near g=0 (the classic dependency problem): on 1M trained OPT-125M coordinates it escapes 98.9% of coordinates and the "compressed" payload grows to 126.6% of raw FP32. Subtracting the two Adam quotients algebraically cancels the shared gradient from the numerator difference and the shared parameter from the update difference, so the bound scales with the moment reconstruction errors instead of the update's sensitivity to g.
- **A native-FP32 rounding-ball model under a pinned operation contract.** Each local op gets radius R(M)=8u₃₂M+4n_min (u₃₂=2⁻²⁴), propagated through every operation of the pinned Torch 2.8 scalar FP32 Adam path (direct-division and rounded-reciprocal variants, recentered), with directed-rounding CUDA kernels (NVRTC) doing all bound arithmetic outward in double precision.
- **A fully accounted guarded codec with exact escapes and standalone verification.** 8-bit linear code for m (exact zero), 8-bit log code for v, per-block scale tables, escape bitmask, exact FP32 escape pairs, SHA-256 over payload and decoded-state hash; NumPy and eager-PyTorch readers must agree bitwise or the coordinate is escaped. Every byte — including escapes — is charged to the payload, which is what exposes both the separate-range failure and why guarded blockwise 8-bit ends up larger than log codes.
- **A disciplined evaluation separating mechanism, baselines, and cost**: controlled ablation; equally guarded BF16 and official bitsandbytes 0.48.1 blockwise 8-bit baselines under the same guards; 120 complete-checkpoint restore conditions (4 seeds × 3 training fractions × 2 architectures) with 100-update own-gradient continuations; and paired multi-process codec timing on H100 — plus a 1,600-condition supporting study over bit width and τ.

## Methodology

The contract (Eq. 4): decoded moments (m̃, ṽ) are accepted for a coordinate only if sup_{|g|≤C} |Φ(w,m,v;g) − Φ(w,m̃,ṽ;g)| ≤ τ·η, where Φ is the pinned native update program (Torch 2.8 scalar FP32 Adam, foreach/fused/capturable/differentiable/amsgrad/maximize disabled, zero weight decay, β₁=0.9, β₂=0.999, ε=10⁻⁸, C=0.2, η∈[10⁻⁶,0.01]); every other input stops certification. Coordinates failing the test are *escaped* — stored as exact FP32 — so the contract holds for every coordinate by construction.

**The bound.** The exact-real center of the native path is Φ(w,m,v;g) = w + s·((1−α)m + αg)/(bv + δg²/c + e), with realized FP32 coefficients. For a gradient interval I with |g|∈[l,u], lower denominators a = bv+δl², ã = bṽ+δl², and the quantities Δₙ=(1−α)|m−m̃|, Δ_d = b|v−ṽ|/(c(a+ã)), N, Ñ (Eq. 8), the two ways of splitting the quotient difference give the enclosure B_I (Eq. 9); the shared gradient αg cancels from the numerator difference and w cancels from the difference of centers, leaving |Φ−Φ̃| ≤ |s|·B_I. FP32 rounding balls (Eq. 10–11) are added on top, and a coordinate is accepted when max_I (|s|B_I + r_I + r̃_I)/η ≤ τ.

**Partition refinement.** A *mixed* partition combines evenly spaced linear edges with base-2 log magnitudes down to 2⁻⁴⁰C (mirrored around zero); unresolved coordinates are rechecked at 64 → 256 → 1024 intervals before escaping. The mixed partition matters precisely near g=0, where a small v makes the update most sensitive and a uniform partition lumps all |g|<C/32 into one interval per side.

**Codec.** Per 256-coordinate block: three FP64 scale parameters; nearest-code (independent) or best-of-4-neighboring-pairs (joint) selection, both judged on the actually decoded values; ideal-real guard first, then the native bound. Dual decoders (NumPy FP64-reconstructed vs eager PyTorch) must agree bitwise after FP32 cast. Payload = header ∥ block table ∥ codes ∥ escape mask ∥ exact escapes ∥ SHA-256.

**Experiments.** Four OPT-125M seeds checkpointed at 10%/50%/100% of training (t = 3,125/15,625/31,250) and four ResNet-18 seeds on CIFAR-10 at 224 resolution for 20 epochs (checkpoints at t = 1,250/6,250/12,500). Five representations compared: FP32, guarded BF16, guarded bitsandbytes blockwise 8-bit, and log codes (independent/joint). Protocol: restore both complete moment vectors; audit the first restored update with a common saved gradient; then continue 100 updates with each branch's *own* gradients from identical model/RNG/data order; evaluate 10,000 dev examples. Timing uses separate cohorts (17 OPT / 50 ResNet process launches, frozen randomized method order, 20 warm-ups + 100 rounds). An H100 implementation audit covers 1,048,576 scalar cases (zero enclosure failures) plus a deliberately-broken control (missing radius → 899,311 detections).

## Results & Highlights

- **Mechanism ablation (1M trained OPT-125M coordinates, τ=0.01, Table 1 / Fig. 1b):** separate-range bounds escape 988,820/1,000,000 coordinates (98.9%) with payload 126.6% of raw FP32; the shared-gradient bound with uniform partition drops this to 439,362 escapes (71.7%); adding the mixed partition reaches 5,324 escapes (0.53%) and 2,262,239 B = 28.278% of the 8,000,000 B FP32 baseline. All configurations spend identical 2,219,592 B on codes/metadata — the bound alone decides the payload. Warm encoding is also 2.7× faster with the tighter bound (median 0.161 s vs 0.435 s).
- **vs equally guarded baselines (same 1M coordinates, Table 1):** guarded log codes need 28.278% of raw FP32 bytes — 45.16% fewer than guarded BF16 (4,125,333 B, 51.567%) and 28.82% fewer than guarded blockwise 8-bit (3,177,969 B, 39.725%, forced into 127,396 escapes). Plain blockwise 8-bit is smaller (26.985%) but *violates* the contract: it exceeds τ on 2,469 of the 1M coordinates with max normalized error 0.1294 (≈13× the 0.01 tolerance).
- **Complete checkpoints (Table 2; 4 seeds × 3 fractions × 2 models):** guarded log codes cut moment payloads vs FP32 by 71.67%/69.44%/67.35% (OPT-125M at 10%/50%/100%; e.g., 955.50 → 312.01 MiB at 100%) and 72.18%/71.85%/71.56% (ResNet-18; 85.31 → 23.73–24.26 MiB); vs guarded BF16 by 45.08–36.69% (OPT) and 46.05–44.85% (ResNet); vs guarded blockwise 8-bit by 27.33–31.49% (OPT) and 26.90–43.28% (ResNet). Escape rates explain it: guarded blockwise 8-bit must escape 12%→21% of OPT (11%→23% of ResNet) coordinates as training matures, while log codes escape only 0.6–4.9% (OPT) and 0.08–0.7% (ResNet). At the container level (including lossless model weights), complete checkpoints shrink 47.09–48.77% (OPT) and 51.83–52.39% (ResNet).
- **Restore fidelity:** all 120 restore conditions pass; the largest realized first-update error over all coordinates is 0.009992 learning rates (OPT-125M) and 0.009991 (ResNet-18) — i.e., max 0.9992τ, strictly under τ=0.01 in every condition (Fig. 4a).
- **Cost (Table 3, seed 701 @ 50%):** combined encode+restore for log codes takes 16.905 s (OPT) — 2.31× guarded BF16 (7.317 s) but 0.72× guarded blockwise 8-bit (23.528 s), so log codes beat the 8-bit baseline on both bytes *and* time; restoration alone is faster than FP32 (0.88× OPT, 0.77× ResNet). Encoding peaks at 24.9 GiB for OPT (vs 19.2 GiB guarded BF16, 17.4 GiB guarded 8-bit).
- **Joint code search doesn't pay:** joint selection saves only 0.000971% of OPT moment bytes on average (11–195 bytes per ResNet checkpoint) while costing 1.059–1.060× the codec time and 1.115–1.117× the encode peak; in ResNet the joint-minus-independent dev-loss difference is positive at every seed (mean +0.0113).
- **100-update continuation (descriptive):** mean dev-loss difference vs the paired FP32 branch stays tiny for OPT-125M log codes (−1.41×10⁻⁴, −4.18×10⁻⁵, −3.68×10⁻⁵ at the three fractions, sign flips across seeds); ResNet-18 differences are larger for *every* compressed method including BF16 (e.g., +0.0087 mean at 10% for joint log codes).
- **Supporting width/tolerance study (200 logistic-optimization states, binary64 guard):** at τ=0.05, 8-bit log codes average 38.19% of FP32 vs 50.73% for BF16; at the tighter τ=0.005 BF16 becomes the smaller successful format (50.73% vs 59.87%). Finite screens are insufficient: on inherited probes, 17/257/515-point screens leave 942/371/15 failing conditions, and the 515-point screen still misses 16 violations on disjoint probes that the interval guard repairs.

## Authors & Credibility

Authors are from Peking University (Zhang, Song, Yao, L. Zhang) and Beihang University (Tianmiao); the paper is a preliminary submission under review at MLSys. Credibility signals are unusually strong for a systems paper: all experimental conditions were registered in advance and retained (no cherry-picking of runs); every byte including escapes is charged to the payload; the blockwise baseline is the official bitsandbytes 0.48.1 CUDA decoder (verified to agree exactly with their standalone parser), not a reimplementation; the bound implementation is audited on 1,048,576 scalar H100 cases with a deliberately-broken control (a missing rounding radius triggers 899,311 detections, showing the audit has teeth); source/PTX/cubin hashes and NVRTC flags are retained; and a Reproducibility Statement plus an AI-usage disclosure (ChatGPT for language, Claude for figures only) are included. Statistical honesty is explicit: seeds are the independent units, architectures are never pooled, continuation results are labeled descriptive, and the guarantee's scope (first restored update, pinned operation model) is stated in the abstract rather than buried.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The core reframing — treating certified optimizer-state compression as a dependency problem and bounding the *difference* of two computations that share inputs, rather than bounding each output separately — is genuinely novel in the checkpoint-compression literature, where prior lossy work (LC-Checkpoint, ExCP, Check-N-Run) validates only by post-restart accuracy. The technique is problem-specific rather than a general verified-arithmetic framework (no FPTaylor-style certificates), which the authors state plainly.
- **Rigor:** High. The ablation cleanly isolates bound vs partition; the same guard is applied to all codecs; dual independent decoders must agree bitwise; the pinned Torch 2.8 operation model with β₁=0.9/β₂=0.999/ε=10⁻⁸, C=0.2, η∈[10⁻⁶,0.01] is explicit. One caveat they themselves flag: the enclosure is conditional on the operation contract, not a machine-checked verification of Torch or arbitrary generated kernels — fused/foreach Adam paths, AdamW's decoupled weight decay, and other optimizers are out of scope.
- **Reproducibility:** Strong — pinned environment, hashed artifacts, frozen protocols, registered conditions, public baselines (bitsandbytes), and standard models (OPT-125M, ResNet-18/CIFAR-10). Timing cohorts even record host contention context.
- **Significance:** Real but bounded. The guarantee covers only the *first* restored update; later updates consume branch-specific gradients, so long-horizon training equivalence remains empirical (100 updates only). Scale is modest — OPT-125M and ResNet-18, not the billion-parameter sharded checkpoints where checkpoint I/O actually hurts; the authors name this as next work.
- **Red flags / weaknesses to watch:** (1) Encode-time cost is steep: 7.2–7.6× FP32 serialization and 24.9 GiB peak vs 19.2 GiB for guarded BF16 — for very frequent checkpointing the paper itself recommends guarded BF16 instead, which blunts the headline. (2) The advantage is tolerance- and distribution-dependent: at τ=0.005 on the small-state study, guarded BF16 (50.73%) beats 8-bit log codes (59.87%), and 4-bit codes save <3%. (3) ResNet-18 continuation shows larger dev-loss differences for *every* compressed method including BF16 (up to mean +0.0087), which the paper handles honestly but does not explain. (4) The supporting Appendix B study uses a different source precision (binary64) and a different (non-directed-rounding) guard, so it controls width/tolerance but does not replicate the native guarantee. (5) Timings are codec-boundary component measurements on possibly shared hosts, not end-to-end checkpoint-pause numbers.

## Strengths

- Turns a theoretical insight (shared-input cancellation in interval arithmetic) into a working, byte-accounted codec with a real end-to-end win: 126.6% → 28.3% of FP32 purely from the bound, with identical code bytes.
- Total-honesty accounting: escapes, headers, masks, and checksums all count, which is precisely what reveals that guarded blockwise 8-bit (which must escape 12–23% of coordinates) loses to log codes on bytes.
- The guard is representation-agnostic — applied unchanged to BF16 and official bitsandbytes codes — making it a drop-in audit layer for existing formats.
- Clean experimental design: paired comparisons within seed/checkpoint, registered conditions, separate timing cohorts, 120 full restores, and an implementation audit with a deliberately broken control.
- Actionable deployment rule from Figure 3c: guarded BF16 when encode time dominates (48% savings at 1.7–1.8× FP32 codec time), guarded log codes when storage/bandwidth dominates (69–72% savings at 4.3–4.5×), beating guarded 8-bit on both axes.

## Limitations

- The certificate covers only the first restored update; behavior of subsequent training is empirical (100 own-gradient updates, descriptive statistics, no held-out equivalence claim).
- Pinned to Torch 2.8 scalar FP32 Adam with fixed hyperparameters (β₁=0.9, β₂=0.999, ε=10⁻⁸), C=0.2, and lr∈[10⁻⁶,0.01]; fused kernels, AdamW decoupled decay, amsgrad, and other optimizers are uncertified.
- Scale: largest model is OPT-125M (125M params); billion-parameter and sharded checkpoints — where optimizer state dominates and the method would matter most — are untested.
- Guarding is paid at encode time and peak memory: 7.2–7.6× FP32 encode time, 2.3–2.6× guarded BF16 combined codec time, and 24.9 GiB vs 19.2 GiB encode peak on OPT-125M.
- At tighter tolerance (τ=0.005) guarded BF16 becomes the smaller successful format, so the log-code advantage is not uniform across operating points.
- The Appendix B width/tolerance study uses an earlier binary64 format and real-arithmetic guard, so its conclusions transfer only qualitatively to the native codec.
- Timings exclude whole-payload SHA validation, container serialization, filesystem transfer, and resumed training — end-to-end checkpoint-pipeline costs remain future work.

## Relevance

与高效 LLM 训练/微调、模型与优化器状态量化方向高度相关：该工作在完全不改动 Adam 递推的前提下，把检查点中的矩张量可证明地压到 FP32 的约 28–32%（较同护栏 BF16 再省 ~40–45%），可直接降低大模型长训中检查点的存储与传输开销，其"认证消费端计算而非存储值"的护栏思路对其他训练状态压缩也具启发意义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does the escape rate (0.6–4.9% on OPT-125M) and the payload advantage evolve for billion-parameter, sharded checkpoints with much larger moment tensors, and does the 8-byte-per-escape accounting change at 4-bit codes?
- Can the shared-gradient cancellation be extended into a trajectory-level bound (e.g., over K restored updates under a Lipschitz/contraction assumption on the training dynamics), instead of certifying only the first update?
- What is the end-to-end impact inside a real checkpointing system (CheckFreq/Gemini-style frequent checkpointing), where encode time at 7.2× FP32 may offset byte savings on fast storage but win on slow network links?
- Does the guard transfer to fused/foreach CUDA Adam kernels, AdamW with decoupled weight decay, or momentum optimizers (Lion, Adafactor) whose update operators have different sensitivity structure near v=0?
- Since guarded blockwise 8-bit must escape 12–23% of coordinates — could a hybrid codec (log code for small-v coordinates, linear blockwise for the rest) push the payload below 28%?
- Why are ResNet-18 continuation losses noticeably larger for all compressed formats (including BF16, mean up to +0.0087) while OPT-125M differences are ~10⁻⁴–10⁻⁵ — is this a task-sensitivity effect or a CIFAR-10/data-order artifact?
