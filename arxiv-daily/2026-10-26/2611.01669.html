Why this matters

A zero-cost check before quantizing now tells you when a cheap random rotation suffices — the central design choice in QuaRot/SpinQuant-style PTQ pipelines.

给出“随机旋转 vs 逐列注水”的精确信息论刻画，以及量化前即可执行的逐层决策规则——对理解 QuaRot/SpinQuant 类方法何时有效、何时失效具有指导意义。

⚠ Critical edge

The worst case needs two-point spectra that real networks don’t have — and end-to-end, PlainWaterSIC actually beats GPTQ+Hadamard (17.33 vs 18.80 PPL at 3 bits, 3 seeds).

End-to-end evidence is thin: 2 models, WikiText-2 perplexity only, most entries a single calibration seed; act-order beats rotation on 27% of layers, unexplained by the theory.

Methodology · distilled

GPTQ is lattice decoding; rotation reshapes its pivots

Σ = E[xxᵀ]→ Cholesky U→ pivots πk = Ukk²→ Haar Q: QTΣQ→ πk → 1/Sν(−k/n)→ gap Δ = ½log₂(AM/GM)

Layerwise PTQ minimizes ⅟ₙ·tr((W−Ŵ)Σ(W−Ŵ)ᵀ) with Σ from calibration data. GPTQ with a common step size is exactly Babai’s nearest-plane on the Cholesky factor of Σ; its cost is governed by the pivots. In the high-rate, per-column entropy-coded regime, GPTQ pays Δ extra bits vs WaterSIC — itself within ≈0.255 bit of the rate–distortion bound for every Σ.

The technical core identifies the rotated pivot process with a Lyapunov-exponent formula (Theorem 1, uniform over bounded-condition-number spectra), then maximizes the limit over condition number κ via a log-convexity lemma that forces extremal spectra onto two atoms (Theorem 2). Proposition 5 adds a weight-side cost Cw(Q) = U(W) − U(WQ) — both terms computable before quantizing, yielding the per-layer decision rule: Cholesky-check each candidate basis (native / act-order / rotation), keep the cheapest.

Experiments: 96 float64 Hessians from 3 models (n = 2048–14336), 128×2048 WikiText-2 tokens, GPTQ-default damping, Haar and randomized-Hadamard rotations, 3 seeds.

The two results

Theorem 1 · rotated pivot limit

πk →a.s. hν(k/n) = 1/Sν(−k/n)
Δ → ½·log₂(D(ν)/GM(ν))

Uniform in k ≥ δn and over spectra of bounded condition number — a classical Lyapunov-exponent formula, newly identified as a finite-n, per-pivot statement about GPTQ itself.

Theorem 2 · worst case over κ (solves Ordentlich–Polyanskiy, 2026b)

½·log₂κ − log₂ ln κ + ½ − 1/ln 2 + o(1) bits

Attained only by two-point spectra (mass ≈ 2/ln κ on the top eigenvalue). Rotation saves just ½·log₂ ln κ + O(1) bits over the unrotated worst case — so it provably cannot replace waterfilling in general.

Equal entropy-coded rate, seqlen 2048. At 3 bits (Qwen3-1.7B): PlainWaterSIC 17.33 ± 0.02 vs GPTQ+Hadamard 18.80 ± 1.14 — waterfilling is also the stabler choice.
  • Limit accuracy: finite-n pivots match the Theorem-1 limit to within 0.009 bit across all 270 (layer, damping) pairs; high-rate rate prediction ≤ 0.015 bit at 4 bits/weight on 63/64 layers.
  • Hadamard ≈ Haar: within 0.01 bit of the prediction on 93% of layers (one round) / 99% (two rounds) — the exception is o_proj, whose covariance is blocked by attention head.
  • Cw can flip the call: one Llama layer pays 1.36 bit weight-side under rotation (gap 1.31 → 1.68); rotation helps on the other 63 layers.
  • Outside the theory: unrotated GPTQ with act-order beats a random rotation on 27% of layers (19/24 gate/up inputs), even after clipping the top 1% of eigenvalues.
  • Fixed-rate INT is out of regime: with INT4 group-128, WaterSIC’s multipliers gain ≤ 0.008 bit and the format itself costs 0.8–1.0 bit vs entropy coding.
Strengths
  • Solves a precisely stated open problem from information theory with sharp constants and explicit extremizers, not big-O hand-waving.
  • Rare theory–experiment tightness: the Lyapunov limit matches real 2048–14336-dim Cholesky pivots to 0.009 bit.
  • An immediately usable engineering rule — one Cholesky factorization per candidate basis decides rotation vs waterfilling before quantizing.
  • Honest scoping: anomalies reported, single-seed entries flagged, fixed-rate INT formats explicitly excluded, Hadamard worst case stated as open.
Limitations
  • Theorems cover Haar rotations, not the randomized Hadamard transforms used in practice — their worst case remains open.
  • Δ is a high-rate, per-column entropy-coded quantity (verified down to 3 bits); says nothing about fixed-rate INT4 deployment.
  • Weight-side analysis rests on Assumption 1, proven only for Gaussian rows; layerwise only — no cross-layer effects or error feedback.
  • Real spectra violate the bounded-support hypothesis (top eigenvalue 10³–10⁵× median); o_proj gaps reach 0.63 bit without damping.
  • No code or Hessian release; replicating D(ν) integrals to 0.001 bit is nontrivial.
Questions for further exploration
  1. What is the worst-case gap for the randomized Hadamard transform actually used in QuaRot/SpinQuant — does sign-only structure ever fail “asymptotic freeness”?
  2. Why does act-order beat rotation on 27% of layers even after outlier clipping — is there a basis-ordering effect the pivot theory misses?
  3. Can Theorem 1 extend to unbounded-support spectra (no damping), covering the o_proj inputs at 0.63 bit?
  4. Does the weight-side cost Cw change under learned rotations (SpinQuant) or error feedback (QuIP#)?
  5. How do layerwise gaps compose into a whole-model rate–distortion tradeoff — can a global bit allocation beat the per-layer rule?
