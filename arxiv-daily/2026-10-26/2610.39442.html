Key Finding · MoE Routing Theory

Routing collapse in MoE models is transient — and optimization geometry alone sets whether a suppressed expert recovers in Θ(1/ε), Θ(log 1/ε), or Θ(1) time.

Why This Matters
FOR PRACTITIONERS → Computable tools — the velocity decomposition and discrete log-odds identity — for diagnosing dead experts and choosing router geometry that speeds recovery in sparse MoE training.
⚠ Critical Edge
All theorems live in a common-target square-loss toy model — no cross-entropy, no token-conditioned experts, no top-k dispatch — and neural validation is a single ~50M model on AG News, so production-MoE relevance is suggestive, not proven.

Methodology 方法脉络

1
Continuous-time gradient flow on softmax routing logits. Euclidean flow transports to probability space as ṗ = −J(p)²E(Eᵀp − y) (squared-Jacobian flow).
2
Boundary degeneracy: rows of the softmax Jacobian vanish as p_j → 0 — the mechanism behind persistent expert suppression.
3
Fixed experts: loss dissipation + LaSalle invariance + convex projection geometry yield a stationary-loss barrier that selects which simplex face routing converges to.
4
Trainable experts (rate η): Gronwall estimates give O(η) tracking of fixed-expert dynamics; a dissipation identity forces exponential residual decay e^(−2ηt/n) → strictly interior long-time limit.
5
Main result: exact normal-velocity decomposition ẋ = x(1−x)²Γ_j, cleanly separating complementary-expert covariance Λ_j from relative gradient advantage Δ_j; power bounds Γ_j ≍ x^α classify recovery time.
6
Bridge to real training: a discrete log-odds identity holds for arbitrary optimizer increments — exact for AdamW updates, token by token.
7
Validation: paired interventions on a 6-layer, 8-expert, top-2 Transformer-MoE (~50–55M params, AdamW, AG News, no auxiliary load balancing).

Fixed-expert face selection (§3) turns routing convergence into a projection problem: once the trajectory dips below the lowest suboptimal stationary loss, outside-face mass must vanish, with entry-time bounds from compactness. The two-timescale argument (§4) resolves the apparent paradox — near-face collapse at finite times coexists with an interior limit for every η > 0, with an explicit per-expert probability floor.

Strengths what holds up

  • Exact — not heuristic — decomposition of recovery dynamics, with a complete classification theorem naming interpretable quantities (Λ_j, Δ_j, face separation ‖v‖/κ_F).
  • Resolves the collapse-vs-interior paradox via a clean two-timescale argument with explicit rates and probability floors for arbitrary finite initialization.
  • The discrete log-odds identity makes the theory measurable under real AdamW training — an unusually direct theory–experiment bridge.
  • Matched-pair methodology: identical parameters, optimizer state, minibatch order; seeds as units; bootstrap CIs with exact sign tests; code released.
  • Numerical verification to machine-level accuracy shows the integrations actually solve the derived equations.

Limitations read with care

  • Theory covers a common-target square-loss model with freely trainable expert outputs; implications for cross-entropy, token-routed MoEs at scale are unproven.
  • Recovery bounds need two-sided power bounds on Γ_j along the whole path — an assumption with no known estimator in realistic training.
  • One small (~50–55M) model on AG News, 8–16 paired seeds; no pretrained MoE, no standard LM benchmarks, no interaction with load-balancing losses.
  • Recovery is defined in probability space (≥ 0.05); under top-k dispatch, actual expert selection can differ — assignment-level recovery is not measured.
  • Exponent-correspondence experiments use constructed fields: they verify the math, not the theory's assumptions in learned systems.

Questions for Further Exploration 开放问题

  1. Can Γ_j be estimated online during realistic pretraining — do production MoE routers live in the α ≈ 0 (logarithmic) or α > 0 (power-law) regime?
  2. Does the Θ(1/ε) vs Θ(log 1/ε) gap predict concrete gains from mirror-descent routers (exponentiated-gradient logits) in standard Switch/GShard training with auxiliary losses?
  3. How do load-balancing losses and router noise (z-loss, jitter) interact — do they change Γ_j's vanishing order or merely shift constants?
  4. Does the class-conditional gradient-contrast mechanism operate in token-level routing in large pretrained MoEs, and does suppressing it there also delay recovery of dead experts?
  5. With input-dependent targets (real LM data), can persistent boundary concentration occur in the long-time limit, or is collapse always a finite-horizon phenomenon?
  6. Can the entry-time bound be inverted into an early-warning signal that predicts when a router will lock onto a suboptimal face — before it happens?
