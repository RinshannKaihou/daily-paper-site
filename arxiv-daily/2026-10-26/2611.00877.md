> **一句话总结：** 对四个 1.2–1.7B 指令模型（Llama-3.2-1B、Qwen2.5-1.5B、SmolLM2-1.7B、OLMo-2-1B）的系统测量表明：深度剪枝后函数调用与数学能力在所有家族中崩塌到原分数的 0.00–0.07，而"治愈"（短程微调）时训练哪些参数与用什么数据同样重要；基于 21 个试点的规划器（RAH）在固定 1M token 预算下能把最弱能力最多提升 +0.17，但一旦把试点本身消耗的 3.48M token 计入成本，简单地把均匀治愈拉长到 4.48M token 就在每个家族上追平或反超——"治得更久"是任何规划式恢复都必须打败的强基线。

> **TL;DR:** A four-family empirical study of Block-Influence depth pruning on sub-2B instruct models (Llama-3.2-1B, Qwen2.5-1.5B, SmolLM2-1.7B, OLMo-2-1B). Function calling (BFCL v3) and math (GSM8K) collapse to 0.00–0.07 retention in every family, and full fine-tuning of the last 3 blocks loses 0.05–0.18 capped mean retention vs LoRA on all blocks on three families. A 21-pilot planner (RAH) raises math retention +0.17 on Llama and +0.14 on OLMo at a fixed 1M-token budget, but a uniform heal handed the planner's 3.48M pilot tokens (4.48M total) matches or beats every planned variant and retains math significantly better on three of four families — so "heal longer" is the strong, simple baseline that planned recovery must beat. Read it for the diagnosis and the honest token-accounting methodology, not for a new SOTA method.

## 核心贡献

- **六能力损伤诊断**：对四个 1.2–1.7B 指令微调模型做 Block Influence 深度剪枝，系统测量结构化输出（JSONSchemaBench）、函数调用（BFCL v3）、指令遵循（IFEval）、数学推理（GSM8K）、安全拒答（XSTest）、知识（MMLU）六项面向 agent 使用的能力的保留率；发现函数调用与数学在所有家族中崩塌（保留率 0.00–0.07），且各家族能力损坏顺序高度一致（Kendall's W = 0.92，按随机底线校正后 W = 0.62）。
- **适应范围的隔离测量**：在完全相同的数据与预算下对比"全模块 LoRA"与"仅全量微调最后三个模块"，证明训练哪些参数与用什么数据同样重要——后者在三个家族上损失 0.05–0.18 的截断平均保留率，而每个范围只需一个 300k-token 试点即可正确选型。
- **规划成本的诚实核算**：提出 token 对齐对照——把规划器（RAH）花在 21 个试点上的 3.48M token 直接补给均匀治愈（合计 4.48M）。结论是：固定 1M 预算下规划器确能把最弱能力（数学）最多提升 +0.17，但把试点成本计入后，更长的均匀治愈在每个家族上都不显著更差、且在三个家族上显著更好地保住数学能力。
- **方法学与可复现性**：原始指标在任何 RAH 测试结果出现前由 commit 固定（预注册式）；采用物品级 + 种子级双重自助检验并做 Holm 校正；对 MMLU/XSTest 做随机底线校正；拒答分类器经 240 条人工标注验证（κ = 0.84）。代码、逐物品预测与全部结果公开，大部分训练在 8GB 笔记本 GPU 上完成，无需集群即可复现。

## Key Contributions

- **A six-capability damage diagnosis for sub-2B instruct models**: prunes four families (Llama-3.2-1B, Qwen2.5-1.5B, SmolLM2-1.7B, OLMo-2-1B-Instruct) with Block Influence and tracks structured output (JSONSchemaBench), function calling (BFCL v3), instruction following (IFEval), math (GSM8K), safety refusal (XSTest) and knowledge (MMLU). Function calling and math collapse in every family (retention 0.00–0.07); the damage ordering agrees closely across families (Kendall's W = 0.92; W = 0.62 after chance-floor correction).
- **Isolated measurement of adaptation scope**: under identical data and 1M-token budget, fully tuning the last three blocks loses 0.05–0.18 capped mean retention versus LoRA (rank 16) on all blocks on three of four families — a difference larger than any gap between allocation rules — and a single 300k-token pilot per scope picks the right scope every time.
- **An honest accounting of planning cost**: RAH (recoverability-aware healing), a pilot-based planner in the style of data-mixing optimizers, is tested not only at a fixed 1M-token budget but against a token-matched control that gives a uniform heal RAH's 3.48M pilot tokens (4.48M total). The control matches or beats every planned variant on every family and retains math significantly better on three families (item-level test), with seed-aware statistics on top.
- **Full release and laptop-scale reproducibility**: code, data recipes, per-item predictions for every run, analysis scripts for every table/figure, and refusal-validation labels are public; most Llama and Qwen runs ran on 8 GB RTX 4060 laptop GPUs.

## Methodology

**Models and pruning.** Four open instruct families: Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct (1.24B, 16 blocks; remove blocks 12–13, 12.5%), Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (1.54B, 28 blocks; remove 13–16 and 24–26, 25%), SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct (1.71B, 24 blocks; remove 6 and 20–22, 16.7%), OLMo-2-1B-Instruct (1.48B, 16 blocks; remove 8 and 13, 12.5%). Blocks are scored by Block Influence (ShortGPT) computed on 126 teacher-generated healing examples; block 0 and the first attention-sink token position are never removed; kept blocks are re-indexed so KV caching stays correct. Cut depth is a per-family design choice chosen so damage is measurable (a 4/16 Llama cut floor-ed five of six capabilities), so the study compares the *order* of breakage within families, not damage levels across families.

**Capability suite.** Fixed, seeded test subsets with disjoint dev/test splits: JSONSchemaBench GitHub-easy (150 test items), BFCL v3 simple+multiple (200, AST match), IFEval strict prompt-level (300), GSM8K exact match (200), XSTest 100 unsafe + 100 benign (balanced refusal score), MMLU answer-likelihood (300, with ECE secondary). All prompts zero-shot and model-agnostic (so absolute scores sit below published few-shot numbers — Llama's unpruned MMLU is only 0.32 vs 0.25 chance).

**Healing.** Six data pools (one per generative capability + general pool), each ~1,300 prompts from sources disjoint from the eval sets (glaive-function-calling-v2, Alpaca-cleaned + verifiable constraints, GSM8K train split, PKU-SafeRLHF mixed with benign Alpaca). The *unpruned model of the same family* writes targets greedily (self-data distillation); only skill-demonstrating outputs are kept (up to 1,000 per pool). One shared recipe (AdamW, warmup + cosine, bf16, loss on response tokens). Three adaptation scopes: all-LoRA (rank 16 on every attention/MLP projection), cut-LoRA (adapters only at cut borders), and last-k (full 32-bit FT of last 3 blocks + final norm, output head frozen). Allocation rules compared: uniform, damage-proportional (PASER-style), and RAH — which runs 21 pilot heals (each pool alone at 30k/100k/300k tokens = 18 pilots, plus the uniform mixture at 300k under each of the three scopes = 3 pilots; 3.48M tokens total), fits saturating recovery curves r̂_c(t) with a non-negative transfer matrix by ridge-regularized least squares, then maximizes summed predicted retention subject to the budget and a 600k-per-pool trust region (a concave program solved in milliseconds).

**Token-matched control and statistics.** The control heals with the uniform mixture and all-LoRA for 4.48M tokens — the same *total* training tokens RAH consumed (it matches training tokens only; pilot evaluations and runtime differences are not equalized). Primary metrics are capped mean retention (mean of min{r_c, 1}) and worst-case retention r_min. Three seeds for the main methods; two paired bootstraps (2,000 resamples) — item-level and a more conservative seed-aware version — with Holm correction within two test families (28 fixed-budget + 14 token-matched tests). Primary metrics were frozen in commit 73e8582 (5 Oct 2026) before any RAH test result existed; RAH-capped and the token-matched control were added later and are treated as exploratory.

## Results & Highlights

- **Function calling and math collapse in every family**: after the main cut, BFCL v3 function-calling retention is 0.00–0.05 and GSM8K math retention 0.00–0.07 across all four families, while instruction following keeps 0.20–0.66 and safety 0.75–0.85 (raw). On Llama-3.2-1B, removing just 2 of 16 blocks leaves function calling at 0% of its original score and structured output at 6%, versus 43% for instruction following; failures are structural — pruned Llama emits parseable calls for only 6% of function-calling prompts and parseable JSON for 10% of structured-output prompts (Qwen: 0% and 16%).
- **Damage ordering is consistent but less so after chance correction**: Kendall's W = 0.92 (permutation p < 0.001) over the four families' rankings; after correcting MMLU (κ=0.25) and balanced XSTest (κ=0.5) chance floors, agreement drops to a moderate W = 0.62 (p = 0.012), and knowledge chance-corrected retention ranges from −0.05 (Llama, at chance) to 0.68 (SmolLM2).
- **Adaptation scope matters as much as data**: with the identical uniform mixture and 1M tokens, full fine-tuning of the last three blocks vs all-block LoRA gives 0.83 vs 0.82 capped mean retention on Llama (+0.01) but *loses* 0.16 on Qwen, 0.18 on SmolLM2 and 0.05 on OLMo — and on Qwen drops worst-case retention to 0.06. Three 300k-token scope pilots (costing < one 1M heal) rank the scopes correctly in every family.
- **Planning helps the weakest capability only at a fixed budget, and weakly**: RAH/RAH-capped raise math (the worst capability in every run) over uniform healing by +0.17 on Llama (0.38 → 0.54/0.55 retention; GSM8K accuracy 0.178 → 0.260) and +0.14 on OLMo under RAH-capped (0.21 → 0.35; GSM8K accuracy 0.132 → 0.220). Under the item-level bootstrap all four worst-case gains on Llama/OLMo survive Holm; under the seed-aware bootstrap only one survives (RAH-capped vs uniform on OLMo, +0.14, p ≤ 0.028) — and the Llama gain is confounded with the planner's switch to the last-k scope (a single-seed last-k uniform heal already reaches worst-case 0.49 vs 0.38). No planned allocation significantly changes capped mean retention anywhere (nominal effects ±0.02 vs seed noise of 0.003–0.022).
- **Once pilot tokens are counted, uniform longer healing wins**: the 4.48M-token uniform heal retains math significantly better than RAH variants on Qwen (0.29 vs 0.19), SmolLM2 (0.34 vs 0.10) and OLMo (0.37 vs 0.29) in the item-level test (Holm p ≤ 0.014; SmolLM2 also survives seed-aware). Scaling uniform healing from 1M to 4.48M tokens raises math retention from 0.38 → 0.58 (Llama), 0.21 → 0.29 (Qwen), 0.15 → 0.34 (SmolLM2), 0.21 → 0.37 (OLMo) — the extra tokens mostly buy math, but with side effects: SmolLM2's function-calling retention falls 0.93 → 0.77, and Qwen's long heal keeps the teacher's over-refusal (52% of benign prompts refused).
- **Why planning gains little**: the pilot-fitted math curves cannot distinguish slow from stuck recovery — on Qwen/SmolLM2 the fitted ceilings are 0.10/0.04 yet uniform healing keeps improving math to 0.29/0.34 by 4.48M tokens, while on Llama the slow curve (time-scale 530k tokens) extrapolates to full recovery where only 0.58 is observed. A training-free linear-patch baseline trails uniform healing by 0.13–0.28 capped mean on every family; ablating the transfer matrix, scope selection, or the curve shape moves capped mean by at most 0.05.
- **Safety has two sides**: pruning lowers unsafe-prompt refusal in every family (from 0.66–0.97 to 0.29–0.51); healing then moves the refusal operating point in family-specific directions (e.g., on Qwen, uniform healing reproduces the over-refusing teacher at 51–52% benign refusal, while RAH — which allocates zero tokens to the safety pool — refuses 50% of unsafe but only 6% of benign). The phrase-matching refusal classifier was validated against 240 author-labeled responses: 92% agreement, Cohen's κ = 0.84, precision 0.94, recall 0.88.

## Authors & Credibility

Five authors (Piyush Dhoka, Vedant Pandhire, Varun Inamdar, Aadarsh Pathre, Pavitha Nooji) from the Department of Artificial Intelligence, Vishwakarma University, Pune, India — a young group without the brand recognition of the labs behind the cited compression work (Meta, AI2, Huawei), and the paper does not claim prior track record; its credibility rests on methodology instead. That methodology is unusually careful for an empirical study of this size: dev/test splits with fixed seeded item subsets; primary metrics frozen in a pre-registered commit (73e8582, 5 Oct 2026) before RAH test results existed, with an explicit confirmatory-vs-exploratory split declared afterward; chance-floor correction for MMLU/XSTest; dual item-level and seed-aware bootstraps with Holm correction; a refusal classifier validated against human labels (κ = 0.84) with a discovered bug (typographic-apostrophe refusals) disclosed and all safety numbers re-scored; and a candid "Variants and negative results" subsection. Code, per-item predictions for every run, analysis scripts reproducing every table/figure, and refusal-validation labels are released at github.com/piyushdhoka/depth-pruned-slm-recovery; commit history records which runs moved between GPUs. The acknowledgment that AI tools assisted with editing/code/references, with authors taking responsibility, is transparent. Most Llama/Qwen runs on 8 GB laptop GPUs make independent reproduction unusually cheap.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The individual ingredients (Block Influence pruning, LoRA scope comparison, pilot-fitted data-mixture optimization à la DoReMi/DomainPilot, PASER-style damage-proportional allocation) are all known. The genuinely novel contributions are the *cross-family damage taxonomy for agent-relevant capabilities at sub-2B scale* and the *token-matched accounting* that charges a planner its pilot budget — a methodological framing that, to my knowledge, no prior pruning-recovery paper applies. RAH itself is explicitly framed as the object under test, not a contribution, which is honest.
- **Rigor**: High by empirical-ML standards — pre-frozen metrics, Holm-corrected dual bootstraps, declared exploratory analyses, an untested-alternative confession. But real cracks remain: RAH-capped was designed *after* the Llama and SmolLM2 results were seen (only OLMo is confirmatory for it); the headline Llama worst-case gain is confounded with the planner's scope switch, which the authors duly flag; and no equivalence tests were run, so "no difference detected" is not "equivalent". Test sets of 150–300 items give ~±0.1 confidence intervals per capability, and three seeds cannot resolve capped-mean differences below ~0.02 — precisely the size of most allocation-rule effects.
- **Reproducibility**: Strong. Public code, per-item predictions, checksums for Llama teacher files, and laptop-GPU-scale runs. Two blemishes: SmolLM2's teacher targets were regenerated once mid-study (so its later runs may have used slightly different data), and only Llama's teacher files survive in the repo; runs span three GPU types (RTX 4060 laptop, L4, A100) with some runs moved mid-family.
- **Significance**: The diagnosis (check function calling and math first; aggregate scores hide their collapse) and the two practical rules (run a scope pilot; heal longer before planning) are immediately actionable for anyone deploying pruned sub-2B agents. The token-matched control deserves to become standard practice for evaluating any pilot-based planner.
- **Red flags**: (1) Benchmark-aligned healing pools — the math pool *is* the GSM8K training split, so reported "math recovery" is recovery on GSM8K specifically and likely overstates transfer to other math tasks; the authors admit this. (2) The zero-shot eval protocol pushes unpruned Llama/OLMo MMLU to 0.32/0.36 vs 0.25 chance, making their knowledge-retention numbers (0.76/0.72 raw) nearly meaningless — visible only after the paper's own chance correction. (3) Llama weights came through an ungated public mirror rather than the official checkpoint. (4) The admitted-but-untested "give math a big fixed share, no pilots" rule could explain most of the planner's worst-case gains, meaning the paper's fixed-budget conclusion may overstate even the weak advantage it reports for planning.

## Strengths

- Cross-family design (four independently trained families) with an identical within-family protocol — same pruned model, pools, teacher targets, seeds and test items for every method — which is what makes the W = 0.92 concordance claim meaningful.
- The token-matched control is a genuinely important methodological contribution: it converts "does planning help?" into "does planning help per training token spent?", and the answer (no) is more useful than another planner paper claiming yes at a fictional fixed budget.
- Chance-floor correction for MMLU and XSTest turns two superficially "robust" capabilities into the honest, weaker claims they actually are.
- Unusually complete negative-results reporting: the linear patch, the max-min objective (below uniform on three families), the pilot-free cross-family proxy, and curve-shape/transfer-matrix ablations are all reported despite being unflattering to the planner.
- Refusal behavior analyzed as a two-dimensional operating point (unsafe refused vs benign refused) rather than a single balanced score, with a human-validated classifier — the finding that RAH silently zeroes the safety pool and shifts Qwen from over-refusing to under-refusing is exactly the kind of deployment-relevant detail most compression papers omit.
- Practical-guidance section that directly follows from the measurements rather than from folklore.

## Limitations

- Scope: all families are 1.2–1.7B parameters, one pruning criterion (Block Influence), one main cut per family, English-only data; no latency, throughput or memory measurements of the pruned models — so the practical *speedup* side of depth pruning is never quantified.
- Benchmark-aligned healing pools (math pool = GSM8K train split; structured-output/function-calling pools built to mirror the eval schemas) mean reported recovery is recovery on these evaluations and may overstate recovery on other tasks of the same capability.
- Statistical power: 150–300 test items per capability (±0.1 CIs) and three seeds cannot resolve the ~0.02 effects that dominate allocation-rule comparisons; the planner's pilots are each run once, so the variability of the plan itself is unmeasured.
- The most plausible cheap baseline — a pilot-free rule giving the slowest capability (math) a large fixed budget share — is acknowledged but never run, leaving the fixed-budget case for planning weaker than it appears.
- Data provenance is imperfect: SmolLM2's teacher targets were regenerated once between runs and only Llama's teacher files survive with checksums; hardware heterogeneity (RTX 4060 / L4 / A100) can shift absolute scores slightly between runs of the same family.
- The refusal classifier, though validated (recall 0.88), still misses indirect refusals, and the authors did not judge whether compliant answers to unsafe prompts were actually harmful.

## Relevance

对关注模型压缩与高效 LLM 部署的方向高度相关：系统刻画了深度剪枝对 sub-2B 模型 agent 能力（函数调用、结构化输出、数学推理）的差异化破坏，并给出"先跑 scope 试点、预算不固定就先治更久"的可操作结论，可直接指导小模型剪枝-恢复流程的设计与评估。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would a pilot-free allocation that simply gives math 50–60% of the budget — the trust-region maximum RAH itself chose on Llama and OLMo — match the planner's worst-case gains at a fraction of the 3.48M-token pilot cost?
- Do the damage ordering (W = 0.92) and the scope result (all-LoRA safer in 3 of 4 families) persist at 3B–8B scale, under other pruning criteria (angular-distance contiguous spans, learned block importance), or in multilingual settings?
- Since healing pools are benchmark-aligned, how much of the measured math/JSON recovery transfers to held-out tasks of the same capability (e.g., MATH, harder JSON schemas not in JSONSchemaBench-easy)?
- Could the pilot-fitted recovery curves be made trustworthy for slow capabilities by using more, longer, or repeated pilots, or by borrowing time-scales across families (the paper's cross-family proxy produced near-uniform allocations — is a better transfer prior possible)?
- If one fitted plan were reused across many heals of the same pruned model, would the 3.48M-token pilot cost amortize enough for planning to beat uniform healing per total token — the reuse scenario the authors flag as untested?
- How do the healing-induced shifts in the refusal operating point (e.g., Qwen from 51% benign-refusal to 6% when RAH drops the safety pool) interact with downstream safety audits that only check the balanced score?
