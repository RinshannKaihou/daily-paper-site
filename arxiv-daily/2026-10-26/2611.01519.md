> **一句话总结：** 该文证明微调学到的知识"能说不能用"（两跳诅咒）不是推理缺陷而是写入错误——微调把新事实写成"待说出的答案"，而第二跳在中层把桥接实体当作"被读取的实体"——并提出 LEGIBLE（读等价写入）损失，把桥接短语在读取结束层的隐状态拉向模型阅读新实体时的自身表示；在 MQuAKE 两跳诅咒问题上（Qwen2.5-7B），组合准确率从微调的 0.30 / CaKE 的 0.27 提升到 0.71，甚至超过模型自身知识的 0.54，且在 RippleEdits、Balesni 半合成基准与 14B 模型上均成立。

> **TL;DR:** A single-author NTU preprint showing the "two-hop curse" of knowledge editing is a write-time failure, not a reasoning deficit: fine-tuning (and reasoning-data methods like CaKE) write the new fact as an answer to be said, while the second hop reads the bridge entity from the middle layers. LEGIBLE adds one loss that pulls the bridge's hidden state at the layer where reading ends toward the base model's own reading of the new entity — no multi-hop training data, no external LLM. On the 213 two-hop-curse questions of MQuAKE (Qwen2.5-7B) it reaches B-Acc 0.71 vs 0.30 for fine-tuning and 0.27 for CaKE (+0.41/+0.44), beating even the base model's own-knowledge 0.54; gains hold on RippleEdits compositionality-I (0.63 vs CaKE 0.31), Balesni's semi-synthetic benchmark (0.41 vs FT 0.11, ceiling 0.66), and Qwen2.5-14B (0.78 vs own-knowledge 0.61), with the best locality among fine-tuning writers (Nbr 0.66 / Unrel 0.92 vs CaKE 0.39 / 0.82). Read it if you care about knowledge editing, latent multi-hop reasoning, or representation-level training objectives.

## 核心贡献

- **范式重构：把两跳诅咒重新定义为"写入时失败"。** 论文提出"读–写契约"（read–write contract）：一次写入必须产生下游计算所读取的表示。微调把新事实写成输出端的"待说答案"，而第二跳在中间层把桥接（bridge）实体当作被读取的实体——两者表示形式与位置都错位。推理能力本身是完好的（编辑后新事实召回 97–100%，基座模型知道 86–91% 的第二跳，且对自身知识链无需思维链即可组合约 51–55%）。
- **机制证据链：定位"读取窗口"。** 通过把基座模型对真实桥接实体的表示注入到桥接位置，证明只有注入中间层（Qwen2.5-7B 第 2–10 层）才能恢复第二跳（0.03–0.06 → 0.20–0.26，达到"在问题中点名实体"效果的 60–90%），晚于读取窗口注入则无效。微调和 CaKE 之后，桥接到新实体的距离与编辑前几乎不变（第 24 层：0.79/0.82 vs 编辑前 0.70），而 LEGIBLE 把距离降到 0.16。
- **统一视角：所有现有方法都是"写入不同目标"的特例。** 微调/CaKE 不设桥接目标（只写答案）、Balesni 的 logit-lens 写"待说的词"、embed-lens 写"输入词"、LRSD 写主体自身的晚层、自蒸馏写带上下文的教师状态——都不写"模型读取新实体时的状态"。换目标消融（固定损失/层/权重）显示只有"读取表示"能组合（0.71/0.62/0.63），输出/输入嵌入分别为 0.22–0.23 / 0.25–0.27。
- **实用方法 LEGIBLE：读等价写入。** 单一损失项 `L = L_LM + w(L_read + L_keep)`（w=3）：L_read 把模型自动生成的桥接描述（如 "the country of citizenship of s"）在读取结束层 t 的隐状态拉向基座模型读 "This is about o" 的状态；L_keep 用 3 个同关系邻居主体保持函数其余取值不变。描述、目标、层 t 全部来自模型自身，无需多跳训练数据、外部 LLM 或模板。
- **跨方法规律：组合能力跟随"桥接被读作什么"。** 17 个写法变体（4 个方法族 + 权重扫描 + 换目标）在 Qwen2.5 上，读取窗口内桥接偏向新实体的比例与两跳准确率的相关系数达 Pearson r=0.96（Llama-3/Qwen3 为 0.86/0.92）。

## Key Contributions

- **Paradigm reformulation.** Recasts the two-hop curse as a write-time failure governed by a "read–write contract" (a write must produce the representation downstream computation reads), showing the reasoning is intact: after editing, the new fact is recalled 97–100%, the base model knows 86–91% of second hops, and composes its own chains 51–55% without chain-of-thought.
- **Mechanistic localization of the reading window.** Injection experiments show patching the base model's representation of the true bridge entity into the bridge restores the second hop only in early-to-middle layers (layers 2–10 on Qwen2.5-7B: 0.03–0.06 → 0.20–0.26, i.e. 60–90% of naming the entity in the prompt), while after fine-tuning or CaKE the bridge is no closer to the new entity than before the edit (distance at layer 24: 0.79/0.82 vs 0.70 pre-edit); repairing the bridge alone lifts every baseline writer's composition (0.21–0.28 → 0.54–0.55 on Qwen2.5).
- **LEGIBLE (read-equivalent writing).** One extra loss term pulling the bridge description's hidden state at the layer where the second hop finishes reading (t = 24/18/26 for the three models, chosen by a fixed attention-cutting rule) toward the base model's own reading of the new entity, plus a column-keeping term on three same-relation neighbours. Descriptions, targets, and layers all come from the edited model itself — no multi-hop data, no external LLM, no templates.
- **A single axis unifying all writers.** With loss/layer/weight fixed, only targets that "read as" the new entity compose (0.71/0.62/0.63 across models) while output embedding (0.22–0.23), input embedding (0.25–0.27), unrelated entity (0.15–0.23), and an in-context teacher state (0.25–0.32) do not; across 17 writer variants, composition tracks the share of the reading window in which the bridge reads as the new entity (Pearson r = 0.96 on Qwen2.5).
- **Empirical sweep.** Beats fine-tuning, CaKE, self-distillation, both Balesni lens losses, LRSD, and (on composition) AlphaEdit on MQuAKE, RippleEdits, and Balesni's semi-synthetic benchmark across Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3-8B, Qwen3-8B, and Qwen2.5-14B — including on the benchmark that named the curse.

## Methodology

- **Setting.** Knowledge editing: a fact (s, r, o) is written into an instruct LLM (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Qwen3-8B; plus Qwen2.5-14B) via one LoRA per edit (rank 8, α = 32, all attention + MLP projections, 40 epochs, batch 4, lr 1e-4), following CaKE's official protocol; ~11 s per edit on one 85 GB GPU with 8 edits batched.
- **Two-hop-curse questions.** After an edit, questions whose answer is the written object are easy for fine-tuning (0.81–0.89); the curse concerns questions where the new object is only a bridge to a fact the model already knew (edited first hop + unedited second hop). The paper reports M-Acc (standard multi-hop accuracy), B-Acc (accuracy on the 213 two-hop-curse questions outside a 100-case development split), CR (composition retention on chains the base model composes itself), and locality on same-relation neighbours (Nbr) and unrelated facts (Unrel).
- **Locating where reading happens.** (i) Injection: write the first-hop description into the second-hop prompt and, at its last token, replace the hidden state at layer t with the base model's reading of the true entity — rescue occurs only for early-to-middle layers, defining where reading starts. (ii) Attention cutting: from each layer onward, cut attention of later positions to the bridge; the shallowest layer keeping ≥90% of correct answers defines where reading ends (t = 24/18/26), a rule fixed before any test run.
- **LEGIBLE objective.** L = L_LM + w·(L_read + L_keep) with w = 3. L_read is the relative squared distance between the bridge description's hidden state at layer t and the target e_t(o) = the base model's last-token hidden state of o in the neutral sentence "This is about o" — deliberately not the answer token, its input embedding, or its output direction. L_keep anchors three other subjects of the same relation at their pre-edit states, implementing "one edit changes one function value".
- **Everything model-generated.** Given only a relation's single-hop template, a 5-shot prompt (over off-benchmark relations) makes the model name the answer's role ("currency"), yielding four descriptions per relation ("the currency of s", "s's currency", …). The neighbour subjects come from MQuAKE-CF/RippleEdits and occur nowhere in evaluation data.
- **Evaluation.** Greedy decoding with alias substring matching on cloze and question forms (CaKE's protocol); RippleEdits under its official condition queries; Balesni's benchmark under its own chat format and scoring. Strict-scoring and wording-overlap variants in the appendix.

## Results & Highlights

- **MQuAKE two-hop-curse questions (B-Acc, 213 questions):** LEGIBLE reaches 0.71 / 0.62 / 0.63 on Qwen2.5-7B / Llama-3-8B / Qwen3-8B versus 0.30 / 0.30 / 0.30 for fine-tuning and 0.27 / 0.34 / 0.31 for CaKE — and above the base model's own-knowledge 0.54 / 0.51 / 0.52 on the same chains (+0.17/+0.11/+0.11 over own knowledge; +0.41 over FT on Qwen2.5).
- **Reasoning data does not repair composition:** on the 213 questions CaKE is indistinguishable from plain paraphrase fine-tuning (0.27 vs 0.30, 0.34 vs 0.30, 0.31 vs 0.30), and self-distillation adds only a few points (0.34/0.38/0.38), all below the model's own knowledge (0.51–0.54).
- **Composition retention (CR):** of chains the base model composes with its own knowledge, LEGIBLE keeps 0.81 / 0.68 / 0.78 versus CaKE's 0.32 / 0.38 / 0.37 — paired win/loss records of 96–3, 70–10, and 72–3 against CaKE; under a strict score that rejects answers naming the bridge, the gap widens (0.50/0.45/0.53 vs ≤0.20/0.18/0.27).
- **RippleEdits compositionality-I (entirely two-hop-curse questions):** LEGIBLE answers 0.63 / 0.49 / 0.56 versus CaKE's 0.31 / 0.29 / 0.29 (win/loss 199–19, 152–25, 176–20); on Balesni's semi-synthetic benchmark, LEGIBLE gets 0.41 (Qwen2.5-7B) and 0.49 (Llama-3-8B) without chain-of-thought versus 0.11/0.17 for fine-tuning and 0.16–0.22 for the authors' own auxiliary lens losses, against a ceiling of 0.66/0.67 — recovering ~3/4 of the achievable second hop versus FT's ~1/4 (460 wins vs 29 losses, improving 17 of 18 sets).
- **Scale-up and combination:** on Qwen2.5-14B, LEGIBLE's B-Acc is 0.78 vs 0.61 own knowledge and 0.42 for CaKE (83–5); CaKE + LEGIBLE gives the best M-Acc on Qwen2.5 (0.87), while LEGIBLE alone posts the best overall average (M-Acc, B-Acc, locality) on all three smaller models (Avg 0.78 / 0.68 / 0.74).
- **Locality without the usual cost:** among fine-tuning writers LEGIBLE preserves the most same-relation neighbours and unrelated facts (Qwen2.5: Nbr 0.66 / Unrel 0.92 vs CaKE 0.39 / 0.82), though AlphaEdit remains the locality champion (0.98/0.99) — at the price of composition: when the edited subject is itself the latent bridge, AlphaEdit answers 0.02–0.12 versus LEGIBLE's 0.73–0.94.
- **Swapped-target and weight ablations:** with everything else fixed, only the reading of o composes (0.71/0.62/0.63); a state that merely "says" o by copying composes 0.67/0.50/0.49, output/input embeddings 0.22–0.23/0.25–0.27, unrelated entity 0.15–0.23; no weight in {0.01, 0.1, 1, 3, 10} rescues the lens losses (embed-lens 0.32–0.34, logit-lens 0.28–0.34), while LEGIBLE's B-Acc rises monotonically with w (0.30 at w=0 → 0.71 at w=3 → 0.69 at w=10) as median layer-t distance falls 0.79 → 0.16 → 0.15.
- **Robustness checks:** on the ~3/4 of test questions whose bridge phrase never occurs verbatim in training descriptions, LEGIBLE composes as often as on the overlapping quarter (0.69 vs 0.75 on Qwen2.5); on Controlled RippleEdit, accuracy lifts from 0.08 to 0.34 (test-id) and 0.04 to 0.21 (test-ood); on a synthetic relation injected into Qwen3-4B, read-equivalent writing reaches 0.82, the injection ceiling itself.

## Authors & Credibility

Single-author preprint by Zhiyun Shi (Nanyang Technological University, Singapore; listed as preprint, not yet peer-reviewed). The experimental practice is unusually careful for a solo effort: the layer-selection rule (t from attention cutting) was fixed before any test run, design choices were tuned only on a separated 100-case development split, and the paper reports its own method's losses (locality vs AlphaEdit, strict-score drops) alongside its wins. All baselines (FT, CaKE with its released circuit-aware data, self-distillation, both Balesni lens losses at original and matched coefficients, LRSD at its released configuration, official AlphaEdit) run under one shared protocol (CaKE's), which strengthens comparability. No external data, templates, or LLMs enter LEGIBLE's training, reducing confounds.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: high.** The reframing (write-time failure + read–write contract) and the "one axis for every writer" analysis with swapped-target controls is a genuine conceptual contribution beyond CaKE/AlphaEdit/IFMET-style methods that supply more answers or edit more sites. The key evidential move — after FT/CaKE the bridge at the reading window is no closer to the new entity than before the edit (0.79/0.82 vs 0.70), while LEGIBLE hits 0.16 — directly supports the causal story, as does the bridge-repair experiment lifting all baselines to 0.54–0.55.
- **Rigor: strong.** Cross-entropy-free target ablations with loss/layer/weight held fixed, weight sweeps showing lens targets are unrecoverable at any weight, strict scoring, wording-overlap splits, paired win/loss counts, and a pre-registered layer rule. The r = 0.96 correlation over 17 writers is a compelling unifying result, though correlation across writer variants does not by itself establish that the bridge reading is the only causal channel.
- **Reproducibility: good but incomplete.** LoRA configs, hyperparameters, metrics, and the deterministic layer rule are fully specified and models are public; however, no code release is mentioned in the preprint, and the hidden-state distance measurements (Appendix B/E) depend on nontrivial plumbing that others must reimplement.
- **Significance: solid within knowledge editing; moderate outside.** The result that learned facts can compose more often than the model's own knowledge (0.71 vs 0.54) is a sharp, memorable headline. Scope is bounded: two-hop chains only, single edits (except Balesni's 20-fact sets), and it assumes the model already knows the second hop (true for 86–91% here).
- **Red flags / weaknesses, paper-specific:** (1) AlphaEdit on Qwen2.5/Qwen3 is run with hyperparameters borrowed from EasyEdit rather than tuned per model (Appendix D), which may understate the strongest locate-then-edit baseline — though the reported failure mode (0.02–0.12 when the bridge is the edited subject) is architectural, not just tuned badly. (2) The MQuAKE-CF data quirk cuts both ways: 241 of 300 Qwen test questions end in the written object where FT already scores 0.81–0.89, so headline M-Acc partly rewards "saying", and the paper's own B-Acc/CR decomposition is the honest number to read. (3) Main tables for Llama-3 and Qwen3 use only 100 two-hop cases (vs 300 for Qwen2.5), so per-model deltas like 0.62 vs 0.51 carry nontrivial noise; the 213-question two-hop-curse split is the firmer comparison. (4) LEGIBLE's locality, while best among fine-tuning writers (Nbr 0.66), is well below AlphaEdit's 0.98 — writing at the bridge still perturbs same-relation function values. (5) The best M-Acc on Qwen2.5 (0.87) requires combining with CaKE's synthesized data, so the "no multi-hop data" claim applies to the bridge fix, not the strongest overall system. (6) The method needs white-box access (hidden states, per-model layer measurement) and per-edit LoRA training, which is heavier than closed-form editors.

## Strengths

- Mechanism-first: every design choice (token, layer, neighbours) is derived from a measurement of where/how the model reads, not tuned — and ablations confirm each choice matters (aligning where reading starts instead of ends drops B-Acc to 0.57–0.59).
- The unifying target-swap experiment cleanly separates "says o" from "reads as o", with a copying state that says o 99–100% yet composes only 0.50–0.67.
- Self-contained: phrases and targets generated by the edited model itself; no external LLM, templates, or multi-hop supervision.
- Honest boundary-drawing: the contract predicts where the method cannot help (questions whose answer is the written word; FT already 0.81–0.89), and the paper says so.
- Consistent gains across three model families, three benchmarks (including the benchmark that named the curse, with its own losses as baselines), and at 14B.

## Limitations

- Locality trails locate-then-edit methods: Nbr 0.66 vs AlphaEdit 0.98 (Qwen2.5), so edits at the bridge still move some same-relation values.
- Requires internal access and a per-model layer-t calibration (attention-cutting runs), plus per-edit LoRA training (~11 s/edit on an 85 GB GPU) — not a drop-in for black-box or high-throughput editing.
- Only two-hop chains and (mostly) single-fact edits are evaluated; longer chains, sequential edit stacks, and second hops the model does not already know are unaddressed.
- Bridge descriptions are built from each relation's template via a role-naming prompt — relations without a clean template, or entities whose reading is context-sensitive, are not studied.
- Llama-3/Qwen3 main-table sample sizes are small (100 cases); single-author preprint status means the results await independent verification.

## Relevance

与知识编辑、LLM 内部表示机制及训练目标设计方向高度相关：论文从表示层解释了"微调知识不可组合"的根因（写入的表示与下游读取的表示错位），并给出可迁移的设计原则——训练写入的表示必须与推理读取的表示一致。该原则对持续学习、模型自我更新（self-updating weights）以及用可解释性洞见构造损失项（而非只做事后分析）都有直接启发。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does read-equivalent writing extend to three-hop chains where the intermediate bridge is itself a written fact (chained LEGIBLE edits), or does each write only preserve composition for the immediately following read?
- Can LEGIBLE's bridge term be combined with AlphaEdit's null-space projection to get both composition (0.71 B-Acc) and near-perfect locality (0.98 Nbr) in one editor?
- Is the layer-t rule (shallowest layer preserving 90% of answers under bridge-attention cutting) stable across fine-tuning checkpoints, domains, and larger models, or does it drift and require re-measurement per model version?
- Does the read–write contract explain in-context learning composition as well — since in-context facts are "read" rather than "written", should ICL compose at near-ceiling rates, and does it?
- How does the method behave when the second hop is itself uncertain or wrong in the base model (the 9–14% of hops it does not know), where no correct read target exists downstream?
- Can the reading-window diagnostic (injection plateau + attention-cut end) serve as a cheap, general probe for where a model consumes any intermediate quantity, e.g., chain-of-thought tokens or retrieved context?
