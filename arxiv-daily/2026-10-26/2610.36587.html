Key metric · throughput

1.24–1.32×

faster than BF16 on full MaxText training steps (TPU v5p) for Llama 3.1-8B, Qwen3-8B and Gemma 3-12B — while naïve per-element SR runs at just 0.58× BF16 on small FFN shapes.

~1.11× vs independent SR · correction adds only 1.1–4.5% step time · memory within 0.4 GB of INT8 RTNE

Why this matters

Rescues stochastic rounding from a vector-unit bottleneck: provably unbiased INT8 training at near-RTNE cost — direct leverage on pretraining compute.

「同时命中“高效 LLM 训练 / 低精度量化”与“系统-算法协同设计”：把 INT8 随机舍入从 VPU 瓶颈中解救出来，误差修正可证明无偏，对大规模预训练成本与量化训练 recipe 有直接参考价值。」

Critical edge

“Matches independent SR” is a null result inside a 3-seed, 12-layer, 32.8M-token noise floor (~0.05 loss); no ≥8B-scale convergence run, no downstream tasks — and on MoE models, INT8 never beats BF16 at all.

01 · Methodologydistilled

Standard SR hashes one 32-bit Threefry word per gradient element on the vector unit while GEMMs wait on the matrix unit — 120–133 extra vector ops per element, 56.5% of busy time on small FFN blocks — so INT8+SR crawls at 0.58× BF16.

upstream grad G→ quantize ONCE · 1 threshold / token (7-bit)→ INT8 → dX GEMM ∥ dW GEMM→ subtract row+col-constant error→ unbiased INT8 step
  • Inter-operand sharing — quantize G once for both backward products (dX = GWᵀ, dW = XᵀG): one S8 write to HBM, halving backward quantization work.
  • Threshold broadcasting — one 7-bit threshold per token across the hidden dimension (factor-H PRNG cut), held in vector registers via XLA fusion; plain JAX, no custom kernels; half-step grid shift bounds bias at Δ/256.
  • Where sharing hurts — unbiasedness survives any threshold topology (Lemma 1), but sharing adds nonnegative covariance; per-token sharing leaves dW variance identical to independent SR and concentrates all damage in dX as a token-constant offset lower layers cannot cancel (Prop 1) — so correct at the source.
  • S3R-EC — the quantizer knows its error E exactly; subtract the row-constant + column-constant projection (a control variate) from both backward gradients. Unbiased, AdamW untouched, zero extra randomness (Thm 1). The dX-only variant keeps nearly all the benefit and swaps a 422.6 MiB FP32 all-reduce for 3.1 MiB of all-gathers.

Evaluated on TPU v5p (JAX/XLA): 12-layer Llama on English C4, 32.8M tokens, 3 seeds · nine FFN block shapes spanning 1B–70B models · full MaxText steps of 3 dense + 2 MoE models.

  • S3R-EC drops every measured error below independent SR — the control variate actively removes variance, not just recovers it.
  • Causal ablation: per-example thresholds on the dX product alone reproduce all of the Φbcast damage (dX error 3.157); per-token thresholds there remove it, regardless of the dW topology.
  • Centering cuts summed variance 40–63% on a synthetic layer, though 17–27% of individual outputs get noisier — the theory is a mechanism, not an exact model.

03 · Strengths

  • Attacks a real, precisely quantified bottleneck (120–133 vector ops per element; 56.5% busy time at W1) — and the fix needs no custom kernels or hardware support.
  • Theory that predicts: Lemma 2 / Prop 1 / Corollary 1 forecast which correction term matters, confirmed by an elegant causal threshold-swap ablation.
  • Beats independent SR's gradient error outright (0.476 vs 0.572 bottom-layer dX), rather than merely recovering it.
  • One quantization for both backward GEMMs halves S8 HBM writes; the factor all-gather shrinks an FP32 all-reduce from 422.6 MiB to 3.1 MiB per step.
  • Unusual experimental candor: noise floors, seed bands, Welch t values, pessimistic-timing and memory disclosures throughout.

04 · Limitations

  • Convergence evidence caps at 12 layers / 32.8M tokens / 3 seeds — the paper's own ±~0.05 power analysis cannot separate S3R-EC from independent SR, and no ≥8B-scale convergence run exists.
  • Perplexity is the only quality metric: no downstream tasks, and no comparison against FP8 recipes or Blackwell's hardware cvt.rs SR.
  • Everything is TPU v5p + INT8; the VPU-bottleneck argument plausibly transfers to GPUs but is measured nowhere.
  • On MoE models the method never beats BF16 (INT8 itself is slower there); the corrected advantage shrinks to 1.04–1.07×.
  • Small FFN shapes (W1/W2) pay +13–15% correction overhead and can fall to 0.94–0.96× BF16; single blocks see +8–14% temporary memory.
  • The 7-bit threshold grid carries a bounded Δ/256 bias; uncorrected S3R needing half the LR hints at optimization-path fragility.

05 · Questions for further exploration

  1. Does the SR-equivalence survive a full-scale pretraining run (≥100B tokens, ≥8B parameters), where the ~0.03 loss-gap noise floor could compound into measurable downstream degradation?
  2. Can shared-threshold-plus-mean-correction extend to 4-bit formats (NVFP4 / MXFP4), and does the activation-gradient offset analysis still hold on such coarse grids?
  3. How does S3R compare against Blackwell's hardware cvt.rs stochastic rounding on GPUs — is the vector-unit bottleneck equally severe, and does the one-quantization-for-both-GEMMs trick transfer?
  4. Why is INT8 slower than BF16 on the MoE models — expert-GEMM shape inefficiency? Can S3R recover the BF16-vs-INT8 gap rather than only beating independent SR?
  5. Does subtracting the structured mean error interact with optimizers beyond AdamW (Muon, SGD with momentum), given rounding variance contributes ≤ 0.094 of AdamW's second moment?
  6. Could per-example (cross-token) thresholds become viable with a cheaper correction communication scheme, cutting PRNG work by another factor of S?

Credibility & novelty

Credibility: all 8 authors are at Google (Mountain View) — direct access to TPU v5p fleets, MaxText and Pathways; the VPU-vs-MXU analysis reflects insider hardware knowledge, and statistics are unusually carefully disclosed. No code release.

Novelty: within-tensor threshold sharing with a principled covariance repair is a new point in training-precision SR — prior work covered few-bit SR bias, hardware SR units, and cross-client correlated quantization, not this.
