> **一句话总结：** 该论文发现 RL 后训练中 fp32 主权重到 bf16 推理引擎的 round-to-nearest 导出会丢弃绝大多数优化器更新（Qwen3-1.7B 上每步仅有 0.4–0.5% 的服务权重变化，采样策略滞后优化器 50–150 步），并提出 Fair Cast——用随机舍入/误差反馈做无偏导出并在服务副本上计算损失（仅改一行代码）——在 25 组配对实验中平均提升 6.1 个百分点，并消除了 fp32 checkpoint 与实际服务模型不一致的隐患。

> **TL;DR:** In standard LLM RL stacks (verl, TRL, OpenRLHF, slime, prime-rl, AReaL), the bf16 rollout sampler is exported from the fp32 master by round-to-nearest, which silently discards every update smaller than half a bf16 grid spacing — at the usual learning rate of 1e-6, only 0.4–0.5% of Qwen3-1.7B's served weights change per step, so the sampler lags the optimizer by 50–150 steps, and existing mismatch diagnostics (TIS, IcePop, PPO clipping) cannot see it because they compare two copies downstream of the same rounding. Fair Cast fixes this with an unbiased export (stochastic rounding or error feedback) plus computing the training loss on the served copy — one line of code, 22–38 ms per 1.7B-parameter export (<0.1% of a 31–130 s optimizer step). Across 25 matched seed-pairs (Qwen3-0.6B to 4B, AdamW and Muon) it wins 18, ties 6, loses 1, with a mean +6.1 points; on zebra puzzles it reaches 0.57 ± 0.16 (n=4) where round-to-nearest collapses from a 0.57 peak to 0.33 ± 0.10 (+24 pts), and on knights-and-knaves with Muon it scores 0.988 ± 0.003 vs 0.907 ± 0.016 (+8.2 pts). It also reveals that a round-to-nearest fp32 checkpoint can score up to 50 points below its own served export (HumanEval+: served 59.8 vs master 9.1), a drift inherited by any export finer than bf16 (fp16 export of that master: 16.5% vs 58.5% in bf16).

## 核心贡献

- **揭示了"隐藏滞后"（hidden lag）：** 在常用学习率（η ≈ 1e-6）下，绝大多数 RL 更新小于 bf16 网格间距的一半（u/2 ≈ |w|/256），round-to-nearest 导出直接将其丢弃。实测 Qwen3-1.7B 每步仅有 0.4–0.5% 的服务权重发生变化，采样策略滞后优化器 50–150 步。
- **指出现有诊断手段在原理上无法发现该问题：** 框架计算的可见比率 π_trainer/π_sampler（两侧都在导出下游）整场训练保持在 |log ρ| ≈ 0.01，而真正主导学习的隐藏比率 π_θ/π_q 会增长到 0.10–0.45——PPO clipping、截断重要性采样（TIS）、IcePop 掩码、bf16-autocast 训练全都看不见它。
- **提出 Fair Cast：** 用无偏舍入（随机舍入或误差反馈 error feedback）导出主权重，并在服务副本上计算训练损失。改动仅限导出调用处一行代码，不引入任何超参数，每次导出 1.7B 参数仅需 22–38 ms（对比优化器单步 31–130 s）。
- **"checkpoint 不等于模型"：** round-to-nearest 下保存的 fp32 master 在测评中可以比自己实际服务的 bf16 导出低 12–50 个百分点；任何比 bf16 更细的导出格式（fp16、FP8-via-fp16）都会继承这一未经验证的漂移。Fair Cast 下两者在每个 checkpoint 都相差不超过 3 个百分点。
- **把滞后变成可调正则器：** round-to-nearest 的滞后本质上是对服务策略的无控低通滤波；Fair Cast 可通过显式 EMA 导出（系数 α）按需恢复该平滑，在 list functions 任务上 EMA(α=0.05) 达到 0.707–0.709，反超 round-to-nearest 的 0.668。

## Key Contributions

- **A hidden lag, measured.** Round-to-nearest export maps any update smaller than half a bf16 spacing (u/2 ≈ |w|/256) to zero. At η = 1e-6 only 0.4–0.5% of Qwen3-1.7B's served weights change per optimizer step, and the sampler acts on a policy 50–150 steps stale; a matched single-step experiment shows one optimizer step moves an fp32 sampler from 0.17 to 0.84 on knights-and-knaves while the round-to-nearest bf16 sampler stays at 0.19 across three successive steps.
- **Why existing diagnostics miss it.** Frameworks form the visible ratio π_trainer/π_sampler ≈ 1 (both sides downstream of the same rounding); the hidden ratio π_θ/π_q is never formed. TIS, IcePop, PPO clipping, and bf16-autocast training therefore cannot detect the lag; per-token effective sample size between master and served policy falls to 0.2–0.35 on 2,000-token tasks.
- **Fair Cast.** Unbiased export (stochastic rounding: 16 random bits added to the fp32 bit pattern then truncated; or error feedback: a per-weight residual making accumulated export error bounded by one grid spacing forever) combined with training on the served copy via a straight-through estimator. One line at the export call, zero hyperparameters, 22–38 ms per 1.7B export. Both halves are necessary — unbiased export without training on the served copy collapsed (error feedback: 0.00 in 4/4 long runs; stochastic rounding: GSM8K 0.61 → 0.13 with token loops).
- **The checkpoint is not the model.** Under round-to-nearest the saved fp32 master can score up to 50 points below its own served export (HumanEval+ step 99: served 59.8, master 9.1); 5 of 9 round-to-nearest runs show a ≥12-point gap at some checkpoint. Finer-than-bf16 exports inherit the drift (HumanEval+ master exported to fp16: 16.5% vs 58.5% in bf16, with responses ballooning from 344 to 1,679 tokens); Fair Cast masters agree across all four export formats (64.6/62.2/61.0/61.0).
- **Lag as a tunable regularizer.** Round-to-nearest's implicit low-pass filter sometimes helps; exporting an EMA of the master with the same unbiased rounding restores it explicitly, turning an accidental number-format side effect into a chosen hyperparameter.

## Methodology

The paper first derives the dead-zone arithmetic: bf16's 7-bit significand gives grid spacing u(w) = 2^(⌊log2|w|⌋−7) ≈ |w|/128, so round-to-nearest zeroes any update with |Δ| < u/2 ≈ |w|/256. The per-step fraction of served weights that change is Pr[η|m̂/√v̂| > |w|/256], which evaluates to 0.3–0.7% at η = 1e-6 for Qwen3-1.7B (measured 0.4–0.5%; 0.13% at η = 3e-7). A weight crosses a rounding boundary only every τ ≈ u/(2|Δ|) ≈ 50–200 steps, and under a decaying schedule τ → ∞: the sampler freezes while the optimizer keeps moving. They verify this with (i) a matched single-step experiment on 64 knights-and-knaves problems comparing fp32 vs round-to-nearest bf16 samplers, (ii) per-step fraction-of-changed-weights logs, and (iii) logging both the visible ratio (3) and hidden ratio (4) throughout training.

Fair Cast replaces the export rounding rule R with an unbiased one and evaluates the loss on the served copy:

- **Stochastic rounding:** for θ between bf16 neighbors ⌊θ⌋ and ⌊θ⌋+u, export the upper value with probability p = (θ−⌊θ⌋)/u, giving E[q] = θ exactly. Implementation adds 16 uniformly random bits to the fp32 bit pattern and truncates (a bit-manipulation kernel; a naive seven-operation kernel is 6× slower).
- **Error feedback:** maintain a residual r per weight (q_t = round(θ_t + r_{t−1}); r_t = θ_t + r_{t−1} − q_t). The sum of export errors telescopes to r_{−1} − r_t, so accumulated error stays bounded by u for all time; it is a first-order sigma-delta modulator costing one extra fp32 tensor (6.9 GB at 1.7B).
- **Training on the served copy:** the trainer's forward pass uses q_t (parameter substitution, gradients propagated to θ_t via a straight-through estimator — the standard quantization-aware-training approximation), so the sampled and differentiated policies are the same object and rollouts are exactly on-policy for the policy in the loss.

The paper is explicit about what is and is not guaranteed: unbiasedness is about weights, not the policy (E[q] = θ ⇏ E[π_q] = π_θ); the remaining effect under stochastic rounding is a second-order Jensen term ½tr(HΣ) with Σ ⪯ (u²/4)I, versus the sign-coherent first-order term that accumulates for 50–150 steps under round-to-nearest. Experiments use GRPO-style clipped policy gradient (group size 8, 24 prompts/step, 150 steps default; 500 for long runs) on Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B/4B, OLMoE-1B-7B, and Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct with AdamW or Muon, vLLM bf16 sampler + HF trainer co-located on one H100 (lag 1). Tasks: Reasoning Gym (zebra, knights-and-knaves, list functions, string manipulation, Countdown), MBPP+/HumanEval+, MATH, GSM8K, and solver-verified games (Nim). Arms include round-to-nearest, Fair Cast (SR/EF), an fp32-sampler oracle, and controls (truncation penalty, TIS, IcePop, FP16 sampler, tuned learning rate, lag 2/8). Held-out evaluation is greedy decoding every 10 steps on 512 problems where available; MBPP+ is scored on the 224 problems not overlapping the training split.

## Results & Highlights

- **Head-to-head record:** across 25 matched seed-pairs (Qwen3-0.6B–4B, AdamW and Muon), Fair Cast ends ahead by >1 point on 18, within 1 point on 6, behind on 1 (list functions), mean endpoint gain +6.1 accuracy points.
- **Zebra puzzles (Qwen3-1.7B, 2,048-token rollouts):** round-to-nearest learns normally for ~20 steps then collapses on every seed from a peak of 0.57 ± 0.01 to a final 0.33 ± 0.10 (held-out truncation reaching 0.47–0.89); Fair Cast ends at 0.57 ± 0.16 over four runs (three of four at 0.62–0.70), tracking the fp32-sampler oracle (0.69). A DAPO-style truncation penalty on the round-to-nearest arm does not rescue it (final 0.45).
- **Knights-and-knaves:** at 1.7B with Muon (where nearly uniform update magnitudes sit almost entirely below u/2), Fair Cast leads at all 15 checkpoints on both seeds — 0.988 ± 0.003 vs 0.907 ± 0.016 (+8.2 pts); at 0.6B the gain is +10.2 pts (0.967 vs 0.865); at 4B both hit the ceiling (0.992 vs 0.988). With AdamW at 1.7B, Fair Cast reaches 0.88 at step 9 where round-to-nearest's sampler is effectively frozen for ~20 steps (held-out accuracy 0.12 → 0.15 → 0.22 at steps 0/9/19 vs Fair Cast 0.12 → 0.51 → 0.79).
- **Math and code benchmarks:** GSM8K (0.6B, 3 seeds): 0.764 ± 0.024 vs 0.713 ± 0.025 (+5.1); MATH levels 3–5 (0.6B, 3 seeds): 0.533 ± 0.015 vs 0.506 ± 0.025 (+2.7); MATH level 5 (1.7B): 0.523 vs 0.492 (+3.1); MBPP+ held-out 224 (4 seeds): 0.618 ± 0.017 vs 0.595 ± 0.007 (+2.3); HumanEval+ (2 seeds): 0.625 ± 0.013 vs 0.613 ± 0.039. The single loss, MATH levels 3–5 at 1.7B (−0.8 at the final step 300 of a cosine run), actually favored Fair Cast +5.5 at step 150 (0.727 vs 0.672).
- **Hidden-lag measurements:** the visible trainer-vs-sampler |log ρ| stays ≈0.01 per token for the entire run while the hidden master-vs-served |log ρ| grows to 0.10–0.16 on MATH and 0.45 on knights-and-knaves; per-token effective sample size falls to 0.2–0.35 on 2,000-token tasks (≈0.9 on 500-token tasks). Under Fair Cast the hidden ratio stays at the ≈0.01 noise floor.
- **Checkpoint vs served model:** under round-to-nearest the fp32 master drifts up to 50 points below its own served export (HumanEval+ step 99: served 59.8, master 9.1); 5 of 9 round-to-nearest runs show a ≥12-point gap at some checkpoint, 2 at the final one. Under Fair Cast master and export agree within 3 points at every checkpoint across 12 runs. Exporting one HumanEval+ round-to-nearest master four ways gives bf16 58.5 / fp16 16.5 / FP8-via-bf16 60.4 / FP8-via-fp16 28.1 — the fp16 export averages 1,679-token responses vs 344 for bf16; the Fair Cast master gives 64.6 / 62.2 / 61.0 / 61.0.
- **Ablations and controls:** both components are necessary — error-feedback export without training on the served copy collapsed to 0.00 held-out accuracy in 4/4 long runs, and stochastic-rounding export alone degenerated into token loops on GSM8K (0.61 → 0.13 by step 19) and unterminated reasoning on MATH (→ 0.002); round-to-nearest with training on the served copy is indistinguishable from the default. Raising the round-to-nearest learning rate to 3e-6/1e-5 produced two MATH collapses. On OLMoE-1B-7B and Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct the two rules are within noise, Fair Cast never worse.
- **Transfer (1.7B MATH-trained checkpoints, avg@16, 8,192-token budget):** Fair Cast transfers better on the cosine pair — AIME24 0.204 vs 0.152, AMC23 0.548 vs 0.495, MATH-500 0.790 vs 0.754, GSM8K unchanged at 0.83 for both.
- **Cost:** on H100, a full 1.7B export costs 4.3 ms (round-to-nearest), 21.7 ms (stochastic rounding, bit-manipulation kernel), or 37.2 ms (error feedback, plus 6.9 GB fp32 residual) — under 0.1% of the 31–130 s optimizer step; wall-clock per step is identical where output lengths match (knights-and-knaves 56.2 vs 56.0 s).

The limitations section is unusually frank, quantifying standard errors (~3 points at 512 problems, ~6 at 128) and admitting several single-seed configurations. ## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: real but incremental-in-ingredients, novel-in-synthesis.** The dead-zone arithmetic was already derived by Dirhoussi et al. [1] (same u/2 threshold, "nearly static model" quote) and the sparsity measured by PULSE [2]; stochastic rounding and error feedback are classical low-precision training tools [9–12], and unmerged prime-rl PRs [13] already proposed stochastic rounding at an FP8 export (without bf16, error feedback, training-on-served-copy, or any learning results). The genuinely new contributions are (a) the visible-vs-hidden ratio distinction explaining why all existing corrections are blind to the lag, (b) the demonstration that unbiased export is *harmful* without training on the served copy (a non-obvious interaction), and (c) the checkpoint-is-not-the-model export-format table, which is the most practically alarming result.
- **Rigor: above average.** Matched seed-pairs, multi-seed on the headline claims (zebra 3v4 runs, Muon K&K 2 seeds, GSM8K/MATH 3 seeds), an fp32-sampler oracle, and a real controls battery (truncation penalty, TIS, IcePop, FP16 sampler, tuned LR). The one losing configuration (MATH 3–5 at 1.7B, −0.8) is reported rather than buried, and the step-150 number (+5.5) is disclosed with context.
- **Weaknesses in evidence strength.** The +6.1 mean is dominated by zebra (+24) and 0.6B knights-and-knaves (+10.2); several Table 2 deltas (+1.0 Nim, +1.2 HumanEval+/string-manip, +2.3 MBPP+, −0.8 MATH) are individually inside the paper's own stated noise floor (~3 pts SE at 512 problems, ~6 at 128 where the 1.7B MATH and Countdown runs were evaluated). The 25-pair win/tie/loss tally mixes n=1 and n=4 configurations, which flatters the record. Models cap at 4B dense (plus one 1B-7B MoE and a 1.5B instruct, both within noise), runs are short (150 steps default, 500 max), and everything uses one training implementation at η = 1e-6 — the regime where the dead zone bites hardest. Labs running RL at 1e-5 (common for smaller models) may see less, though the paper shows pushing round-to-nearest to 3e-6/1e-5 caused two MATH collapses.
- **Mechanistic gaps.** The zebra-collapse story ("pushed toward longer derivations by rewards computed on a model it no longer matches") is correlational — the DAPO-style truncation penalty partially helps (0.45 vs 0.33) without resolving whether the causal channel is the hidden ratio, entropy dynamics, or truncation-contaminated advantages. And Fair Cast does not fully eliminate the instability: one of its four zebra runs fell from 0.53–0.63 to 0.34 in its last fifteen steps, which is why its band is ±0.16.
- **Reproducibility: good.** Public code, per-run configs (Appendix Table A1), exact evaluation protocol including the MBPP+ decontamination (154 of 378 task ids shared with the training split, removed), and promised checkpoint/response releases. The one-line fix claim is plausibly reproducible in any framework with an identifiable export call.
- **Red flags: minor.** The "no hyperparameters" headline is slightly oversold — the optional EMA export reintroduces α, and choosing SR vs EF is itself a choice; the sparse-delta-sync trade-off (Fair Cast flips 2–15% of weights per step vs 0.4–0.5%, so delta compression degrades 5–30×) is disclosed but framed generously. No frontier-scale validation exists yet.

## Strengths

- Identifies a mechanism-level, previously invisible failure mode (hidden master-vs-served ratio) and explains precisely why every deployed mitigation (TIS, IcePop, PPO clip, autocast, FP16 sampler) cannot see it — the visible-ratio vs hidden-ratio distinction is the paper's cleanest idea.
- The fix is engineering-credible: confined to the export call, 22–38 ms per step, no new mandatory hyperparameters, composes additively with TIS, and is compatible with the delta-sync infrastructure it partially undermines.
- The checkpoint/export-format result (fp16 export of a round-to-nearest master scoring 16.5% vs 58.5% in bf16, with 5× longer responses) is an immediately actionable deployment warning independent of the learning-speed claims.
- Honest adversarial self-testing: the crossed-configuration ablation (unbiased export alone collapsing to 0.00) shows the authors actively tried to break their own method, and the list-functions counterexample is turned into a constructive EMA knob rather than hidden.
- Verified-reward tasks with exact solvers (Nim, subtraction games, Kuhn poker) and a held-out decontamination protocol (MBPP+ 224) show attention to evaluation hygiene.

## Limitations

- Scale and duration: models ≤4B dense (plus OLMoE-1B-7B), 150–500 optimizer steps, one training stack; no evidence yet at 7B+ dense, long horizons, or production RLVR budgets where other pathologies may dominate.
- Statistical power: several configurations are single-seed, and by the authors' own numbers many individual endpoint differences (±1–3 points) are not significant on their own; the +6.1 mean and the 18/6/1 record lean on the logic-task tail.
- Task-profile dependence: gains concentrate on long-rollout logic tasks (zebra, knights-and-knaves, Nim early phase); on short-answer tasks near ceiling the two exports learn the same policy at the same speed, and on list functions removing the implicit lag actively hurts until an EMA is reintroduced — with no a-priori characterization of which tasks need the lag.
- Zebra stability is reduced, not eliminated: one of four Fair Cast runs collapsed late (0.34 final), so the ±0.16 band overlaps round-to-nearest's failure mode.
- Practical costs not fully priced: error feedback needs a 6.9 GB fp32 residual at 1.7B (scales with parameters), and the 2–15% per-step weight-churn degrades sparse delta-sync compression 5–30×, which matters for multi-node sampler fleets.
- The guarantee is about weights, not policies (the authors say so): E[q] = θ does not imply E[π_q] = π_θ, and the straight-through gradient is evaluated at q_t, not θ_t — bounded approximations rather than exact corrections.

## Relevance

直接命中"极端性能优化下的可靠性"与"RL 后训练动态与失效"两个核心兴趣：它揭示了 bf16 低精度导出在数值上系统性丢弃 RL 更新（一种由数值格式引起的静默训练-推理失配与 checkpoint 漂移），并给出可检测指标（隐藏比率 |log ρ|、每步权重变化率）与一行式修复，属于量化误差引发训练失效的检测与缓解的典型工作。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the dead-zone lag matter at production scale (7B–70B+, 1k+ steps, multi-node sampler fleets with lag > 1), where update magnitudes, weight distributions, and asynchronous staleness interact — and does Fair Cast's advantage persist once async version lag dominates rounding lag?
- How does Fair Cast interact with LoRA-based RL post-training, where Schulman et al. [14] predict separately-served adapters largely escape the dead zone — is the full-parameter conclusion simply moot for the LoRA regime most practitioners use?
- Can the hidden-ratio |log ρ| or per-step weight-churn fraction be turned into a cheap online monitor (no fp32 re-forward needed) that flags lag-induced collapse before held-out accuracy degrades — e.g., predicting the zebra collapse at step 30 rather than step 60?
- Which task properties predict whether the implicit low-pass filter helps (list functions) versus hurts (zebra) — response length, reward density, entropy landscape — so the EMA coefficient α can be chosen a priori instead of per-task tuning?
- Does training on the served copy change what the model learns (a bias toward the deployed quantized policy) in ways that help or hurt after further fine-tuning, merging, or distillation from the fp32 master?
- Would error feedback's bounded-accumulation property make it the preferred variant under aggressive FP8 sampler deployments, given the prime-rl FP8 precedent, and how do the two unbiased rules behave under MoE routing sparsity?
