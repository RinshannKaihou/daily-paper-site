> **一句话总结：** 在 GPT-2 Small / Qwen2.5-1.5B / Llama-3.2-1B 的受控实体绑定（entity-binding）实验中发现：中层隐藏态存在一个跨查询角色共享、可迁移到新人名的低维关系背景（AB 分量），但它对"选哪个答案"的符号化效应并非方向本身固有——同一方向在 Qwen2.5 的二值提示（orange/purple）下 ΔG=+3.04×10⁻³ 而在词形映射提示（red/blue）下翻转为 −2.23×10⁻³；作者证明该符号由条件敏感性（βG=κBR−κAR）决定，且在 Llama3.2 中被竞争通路部分抵消（注意力通路 −12.683×10⁻³ 被注意力+MLP 双集合通路 +6.008×10⁻³ 回补）。

> **TL;DR:** A mechanistic-interpretability study asking how a relational representation shared across queries drives the answer specific to the current question. In controlled entity-binding materials (four-corner and eight-condition factorial designs, e.g. "Lina is red, Niko is blue — ask Lina"), GPT-2 Small, Qwen2.5-1.5B and Llama-3.2-1B all develop a middle-layer, role-independent AB interaction (93.64%/87.27%/82.16% of AB+ABR energy) that transfers to held-out names (frozen rank-1 axis covers 84.88%/79.86%/89.03% of test-name AB energy vs a 15.51%/14.56%/6.14% natural-PCA reference) and, after ablation, is functionally restored by an independently trained donor direction better than sign-reversed or equal-norm random refills (e.g. +12.181×10⁻³ advantage over sign-reversed in Llama3.2). Yet the direction's functional sign is not transferable: one fixed training-mean AB vector at dose +0.5 in Qwen2.5 yields ΔG=+3.04×10⁻³ on binary orange/purple prompts but −2.23×10⁻³ on lexical-mapping red/blue prompts. The paper derives why (local selection gain βG = κBR − κAR, the contrast of downstream sensitivities) and decomposes the gain into competing late attention/MLP paths that sum exactly to the native effect. Worth reading if you care about steering-vector reliability or how binding representations are read out; less useful if you want natural-language benchmarks — all results are on tiny synthetic prompts in ≤1.5B base models.

## 核心贡献

- **发现跨查询角色共享的低维"关系背景"**：在三层模型的中层查询位置，实体绑定的因子交互（AB 分量）占 AB+ABR 能量的 93.64%（GPT-2）、87.27%（Qwen2.5）、82.16%（Llama3.2）；在训练人名上冻结的 rank-1 AB 轴能覆盖留出测试人名 AB 能量的 84.88%/79.86%/89.03%，说明关系方向可跨名字迁移。
- **证明"可迁移的表示 ≠ 可迁移的功能符号"**：固定 Qwen2.5 权重、同一训练均值关系向量和同一编辑剂量，在二值答案（orange/purple）提示下 ΔG=+3.04×10⁻³，而在词形映射答案（red/blue）提示下变为 −2.23×10⁻³——同一方向在同一模型内产生相反的选择效应。
- **给出符号来源的解析恒等式**：βG = κBR − κAR，即共享方向对角色选择的效应等于其在各"属性×查询"条件下下游局部敏感性的因子对比；若各条件敏感性相等则一阶效应为零。这把"共享几何"与"条件化读取"连接起来。
- **竞争通路分解**：通过对后期注意力/MLP 集合做干净钳制（clean clamp），把 AB 编辑的局部增益按通路遍历方式分解为四条切向通路，四者之和精确等于原生 βG；在 Llama3.2 中注意力单通路 −12.683×10⁻³ 被双集合通路 +6.008×10⁻³ 部分抵消。
- **源坐标（source-coordinate）层面的互补刻画**：用作者此前提出的 source-grounded Wiener 反演接口，区分"隐藏接收器直接可读的关系"与"源跨度中可恢复的属性"，并展示实例几何（instance geometry）可使源对齐、下游方向对齐与响应幅度三者相互分离。

## Key Contributions

- **A shared, transferable relational background.** Middle-layer binding representations concentrate in a task-specific low-dimensional workspace: a rank-16 task basis covers 69.90%/66.80%/63.47% of conditional-A energy versus 15.51%/14.56%/6.14% for the leading 16 natural hidden-state PCs. The role-independent AB term dominates AB+ABR energy (93.64%/87.27%/82.16%) and transfers to held-out names (rank-1: 84.88%/79.86%/89.03%; rank-16: up to 96.97%).
- **Functional transfer without sign transfer.** After removing a test group's AB projection, refilling with an independently trained donor direction restores the target-directed margin G better than sign-reversed or equal-norm random controls in all three models (e.g. Llama3.2: +12.181×10⁻³ vs sign-reversed, +9.099×10⁻³ vs random). But one fixed direction flips sign across materials within Qwen2.5 (+3.04×10⁻³ binary orange/purple vs −2.23×10⁻³ mapped-lexeme red/blue).
- **An analytic link from shared geometry to signed selection.** With the middle-query edit h(ε) = h + abεv and local sensitivity κ = ∇m^T v, the selection gain factors as βG = κBR − κAR: a shared direction moves role selection only because downstream sensitivity differs across attribute–query combinations. Measured βG is +5.193×10⁻³ in Qwen2.5 and −7.425×10⁻³ in Llama3.2 (64-group late-clamp protocol).
- **Exact pathway decomposition of the signed gain.** Clean clamps on late component sets (Qwen2.5 attention {21,23,25,27} / MLP {24,28}; Llama3.2 attention {12,13,15,16} / MLP {15,16}) partition the gain into bypass / attention-only / MLP-only / both-sets tangent paths that sum exactly to native βG. In Llama3.2 the strongly negative attention-only path (−12.683×10⁻³) is partially offset by paths traversing both sets (+6.008×10⁻³); in Qwen2.5 bypass and attention-only are positive (+4.165, +4.200×10⁻³) against a negative MLP-only path (−2.540×10⁻³).
- **Source-coordinate and instance-geometry evidence.** A calibrated source-grounded Wiener interface exposes factors in input coordinates (future-component deletion raises input-reference NMSE by +0.006913/+0.001873/+0.001470 at middle layers); hidden receivers favor direct relation decoding (84.62% vs 78.61% attribute-composed) while source spans favor attribute composition (87.06% vs 100.00%); layout flips cross-position combination from harmful to helpful (72.17% single-point → 66.21% in layout P0 vs 76.56% in P1); and static writes versus dynamic transmission can disagree in sign (Qwen2.5 MLP28: static write to G +0.286 but net transmission τ = −5.307×10⁻³).

## Methodology

All experiments freeze pretrained base weights — GPT-2 Small (12 blocks), Qwen2.5-1.5B (28), Llama-3.2-1B (16) — with shallow/middle/deep anchors (e.g. L2/6/10, L5/14/23, L3/8/13) and a default "hidden receiver" at the attention output before residual addition.

**Controlled materials.** Four-corner groups independently vary two binary factors a, b ∈ {−1,+1} in a fixed context (two entities' attributes in binding; subject/attractor number in syntax, which serves as a structure-sensitive contrast task). The eight-condition design additionally crosses query role r ∈ {−1,+1} (ask about entity A or B), giving the factorial decomposition h = μ + aA + bB + rR + ab·IAB + ar·IAR + br·IBR + abr·IABR. 128/32/64 disjoint train/dev/test name groups; further panels vary attribute domain (64-condition color–shape), lexical mapping (length-matched different lexemes) and value range (3×3×R). Output contrasts are defined on the logit margin m(a,b,r): the common-property response C and the symmetric target-directed margin G on conflicting-attribute conditions.

**Interventions.** (1) Direction edits at the middle-layer query state with resumed native forward passes; donor restoration removes a group's rank-16 AB projection and refills with an independently trained direction, against sign-reversed and equal-norm random controls. (2) Local derivatives κ = ∇m^T v yield the analytic gain identity βG = κBR − κAR; finite-dose edits (±0.5) verify sign consistency, and freezing the final RMSNorm denominator isolates scale effects. (3) Clean clamps on fixed late attention/MLP sets partition transmission into four tangent-path classes whose totals sum exactly to native βG; post-softmax source-branch closure removes designated candidate/query value contributions while retaining other attention probabilities. (4) Shared-vs-separated receiver position interventions test non-additive combination.

**Source coordinates.** The author's source-grounded Wiener interface inverts a selected attention output back to input coordinates through the retained inverse chain, with frequency-specific full-channel maps calibrated on 65,536 (GPT-2) to 131,072 (Qwen2.5/Llama3.2) natural windows per depth. Unlike the raw causal-masked VJP, the source estimate combines the feature, full-instance geometry and calibrated statistics, exposing a "future component" beyond the visible prefix.

**Statistics.** 95% source-group bootstrap intervals throughout; the 21 original comparisons use four separate Holm-corrected families; training-frozen axes are always evaluated on held-out groups.

## Results & Highlights

- **Binding reorganizes with depth; syntax does not.** Middle-layer binding interaction fractions ηI reach 48.91%/82.49%/64.97% in GPT-2/Qwen2.5/Llama3.2, up from 9.49%/21.93%/9.13% at shallow depth, while syntax interaction stays below 10% at every sampled depth. Conditional-direction cosines flip from positive (shallow) to strongly negative at middle layers in binding (factor-A: −0.379/−0.818/−0.603) but remain positive in syntax (+0.976/+0.794/+0.701).
- **The shared workspace is task-specific, not ambient covariance.** A rank-16 task basis covers 69.90%/66.80%/63.47% of binding conditional-A energy, versus 15.51%/14.56%/6.14% for the leading 16 natural-hidden-state principal components; task–natural basis overlap is only 16.31%/8.76%/7.02%.
- **Role-shared and name-transferable.** The role-independent AB term accounts for 93.64% [93.07, 94.19] / 87.27% / 82.16% of AB+ABR energy (role-swap cosines 0.880/0.769/0.653); a rank-1 axis frozen on training names covers 84.88%/79.86%/89.03% of held-out test-name AB energy (rank-16: 94.26%/94.04%/96.97%).
- **Domain-delimited but query-independent.** In the 64-condition color–shape design, same-domain interaction is comparable whether or not that domain is queried (GPT-2: 40.82% queried vs 42.30% unqueried; Qwen2.5: 59.48% vs 58.92%; Llama3.2: 57.64% vs 58.37%), while cross-domain interaction is near zero (0.09%/1.08%/0.45%) and stays low under a shared binary answer code (0.058%/0.268%/0.239%).
- **Lexical amplification and model-dependent value transfer.** Strictly length-matched different-lexeme mappings collapse ηI from 5.45%/47.99%/56.16% to 0.45%/6.00%/7.94%; after that collapse, the frozen binary axis classifies same/different at only 50.20% (chance) in GPT-2 but 57.81% (Qwen2.5) and 64.06% (Llama3.2). On the three-value equality task the same frozen axis reaches 66.86%/99.80%/99.93% balanced accuracy.
- **Donor restoration beats both controls in all three models.** Advantage over random refill: 0.209 [0.143, 0.281] / 5.319 [3.468, 7.316] / 9.099 [6.946, 11.399] ×10⁻³; over sign-reversed refill: 0.338 [0.257, 0.427] / 7.473 [4.684, 10.356] / 12.181 [9.845, 14.388] ×10⁻³ — establishing transfer of a functional direction even in GPT-2, whose mean local G slope is near zero.
- **The sign flip.** With Qwen2.5 weights, one training-mean AB vector and dose +0.5 fixed, ΔG = +3.04 [1.56, 4.50] ×10⁻³ on binary (orange/purple) materials but −2.23 [−2.55, −1.91] ×10⁻³ on mapped-lexeme (red/blue) materials; Llama3.2 stays positive (+3.95 and +1.45×10⁻³). Analytic βG (32 groups) is −0.018 (GPT-2, interval spans zero), +6.505 and −7.273×10⁻³; the 64-group late-clamp extension gives +5.193 vs −7.425×10⁻³, preserved under frozen final denominators (+6.065 / −7.655×10⁻³).
- **Competing paths with exact bookkeeping.** Llama3.2's tangent paths: attention-only −12.683×10⁻³, both-sets +6.008×10⁻³, bypass −0.685×10⁻³, MLP-only −0.065×10⁻³, summing exactly to native βG = −7.425×10⁻³; the selected MLP set transmits an absolute AB shift exceeding the random reference by 2.527 [1.596, 3.431]×10⁻³. Fact-order: candidate-removal effects have paired late-minus-early differences of −0.304/−0.207/−0.398 (all negative intervals), i.e. the candidate route relatively supports the later fact.
- **Source and layout findings.** Future-component deletion raises middle-layer input-reference NMSE by +0.006913/+0.001873/+0.001470; hidden receivers favor direct relation decoding (84.62% vs 78.61% composed, +6.01 pp) while recipient source spans favor attribute composition (87.06% vs 100.00%, −12.94 pp); equal 72.17% single-point accuracy in two Llama3.2 layouts yields opposite combination outcomes (66.21% in P0 vs 76.56% in P1); in syntax, Llama3.2's local H+J predictor improves cosine by +0.022817 yet worsens NMSE by +0.039569, and fitting one scalar per direction closes the gap to −0.001754 [−0.00597, +0.00213].

## Authors & Credibility

Single author, Kaixiang Shu, listed as an Independent Researcher (no institutional affiliation). The paper is a careful, statistics-heavy preprint (arXiv:2611.01809v1, cs.CL, 1 Nov 2026, "A Preprint" style) that builds directly on the author's own prior method paper (source-grounded feature inversion, arXiv:2607.12526, cited as ref [25]). Credibility signals: complete counterfactual factorial designs with disjoint train/dev/test name splits, 95% source-group bootstrap intervals reported for nearly every estimate, Holm correction across four families for the 21 original comparisons, exact algebraic identities verified empirically (four tangent-path totals summing to native βG), and honest reporting of null results (GPT-2 near chance on several transfer tests). Code for the inversion implementation is on GitHub (k-Jayus/source-grounded-feature-inversion) and every frozen prompt/split/tokenization ships in the supplementary materials. Weaker signals: no peer review, no institutional oversight, and the unfamiliar calibrated-inversion machinery rests on one author's unreplicated prior work.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The core conceptual move — separating a transferable relational representation from its condition-dependent functional sign, formalized as βG = κBR − κAR — is a genuine and useful contribution to the steering-vector reliability debate. However, roughly the second half of the paper (Sections 5–6, source coordinates, instance geometry) is essentially an application battery for the author's own prior Wiener-inversion method, and its connection to the headline question is looser.
- **Rigor:** High on internal validity — controls (sign-reversed donor, equal-norm random, permutation, frozen-denominator, zero-response NMSE=1 reference) are consistently present, and the path decomposition sums exactly to the native gain. One circularity risk: the late attention/MLP node sets clamped in Section 4.3 were selected from "preceding baseline attribution" on the same 64 test groups, so the partition is partly fitted to the evaluation data.
- **Reproducibility:** Frozen materials, splits, tokenizations and the inversion code are released, and models are public checkpoints; but the full intervention pipeline (donor restoration, clamps, calibration seeds) is not explicitly stated to be released, and the calibration budget (65k–131k windows per depth, per seed) makes exact replication nontrivial.
- **Significance:** Real but narrow. Findings speak to why steering/relation directions have unstable signs — a live problem in interpretability — yet everything is measured on 15–16-token synthetic prompts in ≤1.5B base models, with effect sizes in the 10⁻³ logit-margin range. No natural-text or behavioral benchmark is touched.
- **Red flags:** The Qwen2.5 sign-flip experiment changes prompt family AND answer vocabulary simultaneously (orange/purple→red/blue), so the cause of the flip (readout geometry vs prompt processing) is not isolated — the authors concede this is a "complete material and readout working point" transfer. GPT-2 is near chance on the different-lexeme test (50.20%) and its query-prediction NMSE (1.003) is worse than the zero-response reference (1.000), meaning several headline generalizations quietly rest on only two of the three models. The sheer number of supplementary comparisons (Tables S1–S29) invites concern about exploratory texture despite the Holm correction.

## Strengths

- Clean factorial designs that genuinely separate representation (what is encoded) from use (what the current query reads out), with subject-number agreement as a well-chosen contrast task.
- The analytic sensitivity identity βG = κBR − κAR gives the field a compact, testable explanation for sign-unstable steering vectors, backed by finite-dose and frozen-denominator checks.
- The exact four-path decomposition (summing to native βG) plus the static-write vs dynamic-transmission dissociation is a model for how to attribute signed effects without double counting.
- Unusually transparent error reporting: bootstrap intervals on essentially every number, explicit null/chance references, and negative results reported rather than buried.
- The domain-selectivity result (same-domain interaction persists whether or not the domain is queried; cross-domain ≈ 0) is a crisp, quotetable finding about how binding backgrounds are organized.

## Limitations

- Synthetic 15–16-token materials with binary color/shape attributes; no evidence yet that the AB-background mechanism exists in natural-language relational reasoning or in larger/instruct models.
- Tiny base models only (124M–1.5B); the paper itself notes architecture confounds (GPT-2's shared key bias, GQA differences in Qwen2.5/Llama3.2) that are discussed but not experimentally resolved.
- Effect magnitudes are small (most ΔG values are 10⁻³-scale logit-margin changes), and no downstream task-accuracy consequence is demonstrated.
- The two halves of the paper (interventional signed gain vs source-coordinate geometry) are only loosely integrated; Sections 5–6 read more like a method showcase than evidence for the central claim.
- The late-clamp node sets were chosen from baseline attribution on the same groups used for evaluation, and the sign-flip protocol conflates prompt and vocabulary changes — both limit causal interpretation of those specific results.
- Single author, preprint, unreviewed; the heavier machinery (calibrated Wiener inversion) depends on a prior single-author paper.

## Relevance

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the role-shared AB background and its condition-dependent sign emerge in 7B+ or instruction-tuned models, or is it an artifact of small base models' shared low-dimensional workspaces?
- Can the βG = κBR − κAR sensitivity contrast be computed cheaply for real steering vectors, yielding a practical pre-flight test for whether a steering direction will flip sign on a given prompt family?
- What drives the Qwen2.5 sign flip — the unembedding/answer-vocabulary geometry (orange/purple vs red/blue) or the lexical-mapping prompt processing? A design varying only one at a time would settle it.
- Do the competing attention/MLP pathways found here correspond to the self-repair/backup phenomena (Hydra effect, conditional co-ablation) at the component level, and can the negative attention-only path in Llama3.2 be patched?
- How much of the source-coordinate "future component" reflects genuine calibrated cross-position statistics versus artifacts of the Wiener map's frequency mixing, and does it survive in natural documents longer than 16 tokens?
