> **一句话总结：** AWS 团队对 4 个 2.7B–7B 小语言模型做了 120 种"量化×剪枝×恢复"因子消融实验，发现 bitsandbytes INT8 量化几乎无损（WikiText-2 困惑度变化 <1 点、基准精度损失 <0.5%、省 40–45% 显存），而 NF4 4-bit 量化叠加 L1 剪枝在全部 16 种组合中都会导致模型崩溃；约 2 分钟的 LoRA 微调可将 50% 剪枝后 Qwen2.5-7B 的困惑度从 920.6 修复到 24.3（降低 97.4%），且消融证明修复来自 LoRA 适配本身而非蒸馏教师信号。

> **TL;DR:** A controlled 3×3×2 factorial ablation (72 configs, plus 48 ordering runs) across four SLMs — Phi-2 2.7B, Qwen3-4B, Nemotron-Mini-4B, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct — quantifies how quantization, pruning, and recovery interact. On WikiText-2, bitsandbytes INT8 alone stays within 1 PPL point of FP16 (Qwen2.5-7B: 11.82 → 11.82) and loses <0.5% average accuracy on MMLU/HellaSwag/ARC/BoolQ, while NF4 + L1 pruning collapses in all 16 tested combinations (PPL ≥ 1000) — unless pruning precedes quantization, which restores viability (~PPL 20–21). Two minutes of LoRA fine-tuning recovers 50%-pruned Qwen2.5-7B from PPL 920.6 to 24.3 (−97.4%), and a distillation ablation shows plain LoRA (no teacher) matches or beats self-distillation on 2 of 4 models. On CPU (llama.cpp), 4-bit runs 46–54% faster than 8-bit for <0.5 PPL difference, but unstructured pruning yields zero latency/memory benefit on dense kernels.

## 核心贡献

- **首个系统性的压缩技术交互因子实验**：在 4 个架构（Phi-2 2.7B、Qwen3-4B、Nemotron-Mini-4B、Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct）上完成 3×3×2 完全因子设计（量化：无/INT8/NF4 × 剪枝：无/30%/50% × LoRA 恢复：开/关），共 72 种配置，外加 48 种压缩顺序实验，总计 120 种配置。
- **发现尖锐的失效边界**：NF4 4-bit 量化与 L1 幅值剪枝组合在全部 16 种配置中导致模型崩溃（困惑度 ≥1000，输出"看似流畅实则不连贯"），而任何剪枝水平下的 INT8 配置无一崩溃——失效是二元的边界而非渐进退化。
- **顺序只在濒临失效时重要**：在 48 个可行配置中压缩顺序对困惑度无统计显著影响（ANOVA p = 0.96），但对 NF4+剪枝组合，"先剪枝后量化"可行（困惑度约 20–21），"先量化后剪枝"崩溃。
- **证明恢复机制的关键是 LoRA 而非蒸馏教师**：自蒸馏与无教师的 LoRA-only 微调都恢复 40–97% 的剪枝损伤，且方向不一致（LoRA-only 在 4 个模型中的 2 个上更好），因此恢复训练阶段可省去教师模型、显存减半。
- **验证快速评估代理的可靠性**：20 样本 WikiText-2 困惑度与全测试集（1,940 样本）相关性 r = 0.985，且经 MMLU/HellaSwag/ARC/BoolQ（30 个配置）与三种随机种子（σ ≤ 0.7 PPL）交叉验证。
- **GPU 结论在真实 CPU 硬件上复现**：通过 llama.cpp 将四个模型映射到 2/4/8/12 GB 内存档位，4-bit 比 8-bit 快 46–54%（困惑度差 <0.5 点），并指出非结构化剪枝在稠密内核上无任何部署收益。

## Key Contributions

- A complete 3×3×2 factorial design (quantization: none/INT8/NF4 × pruning: none/30%/50% × recovery: off/on) across four architectures spanning 2.7B–7B parameters — 72 configurations plus a 48-run compression-ordering study, 120 total, all on one NVIDIA L40S for comparability.
- Identification of a sharp viability boundary: NF4 quantization combined with L1 magnitude pruning collapses models in all 16 tested combinations (fluent-looking but incoherent output), while no INT8 configuration collapsed at any pruning level — including 50% sparsity, which INT8 models tolerate at PPL 22–31.
- An ordering finding with a twist: ordering has no significant effect among viable configurations (one-way ANOVA F = 0.099, p = 0.96) but determines viability for NF4+pruning — prune-before-quantize yields PPL ~20–21, quantize-first collapses.
- A clean ablation disentangling recovery mechanisms: LoRA-only fine-tuning (cross-entropy, no teacher) matches or outperforms self-distillation on 2 of 4 models, showing the teacher signal — which doubles training memory — is unnecessary for post-pruning recovery.
- Metric validation: the 20-sample WikiText-2 perplexity subset correlates r = 0.985 with the full 1,940-sample test set; every configuration with PPL < 22 retained >97% of baseline accuracy on MMLU/HellaSwag/ARC-Challenge/BoolQ; multi-seed validation (seeds 42/123/456) shows σ ≤ 0.7 PPL.
- On-device CPU deployment (llama.cpp) across four memory tiers showing the GPU findings transfer: 4-bit is 46–54% faster than 8-bit for <0.5 PPL difference, and unstructured pruning provides no CPU latency or RSS benefit because zeroed weights still occupy dense storage.

## Methodology

The study runs a 3×3×2 factorial design with fixed ordering Q→P→R: three quantization levels (none, bitsandbytes INT8, bitsandbytes NF4/INT4), three unstructured L1 magnitude pruning levels (none, 30%, 50% sparsity via PyTorch pruning utilities), and two recovery conditions (off, on) — 18 configurations per model × 4 models = 72. Structured head pruning was inapplicable to three of four architectures (fused attention projections) and destructive on Nemotron-Mini-4B (30% head pruning → PPL 4,775 vs 52.69 unstructured), motivating the unstructured choice. The ordering study adds three alternative orderings on the 16 configurations where all three factors are active, across all four models (48 runs).

Recovery uses LoRA adapters (r = 8, α = 16, targeting q_proj and v_proj — ~0.1% of parameters, necessary because quantized weights are frozen), trained 200 steps at lr 2e-4 on 256 C4 samples truncated to 128 tokens. The primary variant uses self-distillation (KL divergence against the model's own FP16 logits, T = 2, λ = 0.5); a separate ablation compares against LoRA-only cross-entropy fine-tuning to isolate the teacher signal.

Evaluation: WikiText-2 perplexity on 20 samples with 512-token context as the primary metric (~30 s per model vs 30–60 min for benchmarks), validated against the full 1,940-sample test set and against MMLU/HellaSwag/ARC-Challenge/BoolQ accuracy on 30 configurations via lm-eval. GPU memory and latency are means of 5 runs (50 tokens, batch size 1). Multi-seed validation repeats 16 key configurations across seeds 42/123/456. For CPU deployment, models are converted to GGUF and run under llama.cpp with q4/q8 quantization, measuring peak RSS, throughput (mean of 5 runs), and perplexity against four memory tiers (2/4/8/12 GB), with only within-engine comparisons reported (model rankings preserved across engines, Spearman ρ = 1.0). All experiments run on a single L40S 48GB (ml.g6e.2xlarge).

## Results & Highlights

- **INT8 is effectively free**: across all four models, INT8-only stays within 1 PPL point of FP16 on WikiText-2 (Qwen2.5-7B: 11.82 → 11.82; Phi-2: 20.67 → 21.25) and loses <0.5% average accuracy on MMLU/HellaSwag/ARC/BoolQ, while cutting memory 40–45% (Qwen2.5-7B: 14.2 GB → ~8 GB).
- **NF4+pruning collapse**: all 16 NF4+pruning configurations collapsed (PPL ≥ 1000) out of 72 total; 17 collapses overall (the extra one: Nemotron-Mini-4B at 50% pruning without recovery, PPL 1,108); zero INT8 configurations collapsed, with INT8 tolerating even 50% sparsity at PPL 22–31 versus FP16's severe degradation (PPL 56–1108).
- **LoRA recovery after aggressive pruning** (WikiText-2, FP16 models): Qwen2.5-7B at 50% sparsity goes from PPL 920.6 to 24.3 (−97.4%) after ~2 minutes of training; Qwen3-4B 50%: 148.2 → 43.2 (−70.9%); Nemotron 30%: 50.1 → 22.0 (−56.0%); Phi-2 50%: 55.8 → 31.7 (−43.2%) — recovery scales with model size, and LoRA on uncompressed models produces no measurable effect.
- **Teacher signal unnecessary** (full 1,940-sample WikiText-2): LoRA-only beats self-distillation on 2 of 4 models — Qwen2.5-7B 50%: 27.48 vs 28.80; Qwen3-4B 50%: 39.71 vs 46.45 — while both recover 40–97% of pruning damage, so the teacher model (which doubles training memory) can be dropped.
- **Recommended recipe holds up on benchmarks**: INT8 + 30% pruning + recovery loses <1% average accuracy on 3 of 4 models and exactly 0.0% for Qwen2.5-7B (avg acc 0.683 both) and +0.5% for Nemotron; every configuration with PPL < 22 retained >97% of baseline average accuracy; multi-seed σ ≤ 0.18 PPL for this configuration.
- **Ordering**: among the 48 viable ordering runs, no significant perplexity effect (ANOVA F = 0.099, p = 0.96; e.g., INT8 mean PPL 19.71–21.45 across orderings), but for NF4+pruning, prune-first yields viable PPL ~20–21 while quantize-first collapses.
- **CPU deployment (llama.cpp)**: 4-bit runs 46–54% faster than 8-bit across all models (Phi-2: 17.5 vs 11.5 tok/s, +52%; Qwen2.5-7B: 7.3 vs 4.8 tok/s) for <0.5 PPL difference; memory tiers — no model fits 2 GB, Phi-2 q4 fits 4 GB (RSS 2,848 MB), all four fit 12 GB (Qwen2.5-7B q4 RSS 7,545 MB, PPL 6.91); unstructured pruning gives no CPU throughput or RSS benefit.
- **Metric validation**: 20-sample vs full-corpus WikiText-2 perplexity r = 0.985 (mean absolute difference 2.21 PPL, max 5.80), with configuration rankings fully preserved; ANOVA on the 55 viable configs shows the Q×P interaction dominates (82% residual variance) — a binary collapse boundary rather than gradual degradation.

## Authors & Credibility

All three authors (Bhushit Nema, Anjali Kadiyala, Siddharth Krishnamurthy, equal contribution) are at Amazon Web Services, Seattle — a credible industrial setting for deployment-oriented compression research, with access to the standardized cloud instance (ml.g6e.2xlarge) used for all measurements. The paper includes a "Customer Impact Statement" typical of industrial applied research. The related-work section is honest and specific about the three closest priors (Minitron: pruning+distillation but no quantization; UniComp: three techniques compared individually rather than interactively; Chhawri et al. 2025: ordering on a single 3B model with structured pruning) and clearly states how this study differs (four architectures, unstructured pruning, full factorial, viability boundaries, teacher-signal ablation). Citations are appropriate and current. No code release is mentioned, but all models are public Hugging Face checkpoints and all libraries (bitsandbytes, PyTorch pruning, LoRA, lm-eval, llama.cpp) are standard, making replication feasible.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: modest but real. No new compression method is proposed; the value is the systematic interaction evidence. The NF4+pruning collapse is the sharpest finding, though partially predictable — NF4 is a QLoRA-era 4-bit storage format not designed for inference, so removing 30–50% of already 4-bit weights failing is not shocking; the contribution is quantifying it as a deterministic 16/16 boundary and showing prune-first ordering rescues it.
- **Rigor**: generally high — factorial design, blocking-factor ANOVA, multi-seed validation, full-corpus correlation (r = 0.985), benchmark cross-check on 30 configs, and CPU transfer. The ANOVA appendix honestly attributes 82% of variance to the Q×P interaction rather than main effects.
- **Overclaim risk on the headline "97% reduction"**: recovered PPL 24.3 for 50%-pruned Qwen2.5-7B is still >2× its 11.82 baseline — the relative-reduction framing makes recovery look more complete than it is; no downstream-task accuracy is reported for the recovered-from-collapse models, only perplexity.
- **Recovery budget is tiny**: 200 LoRA steps on 256 C4 samples of 128 tokens (~2 minutes). This suggests the "recovery" mostly repairs gross distribution shift rather than restoring lost knowledge, and generalization beyond WikiText-2-style text is untested (acknowledged only implicitly via the English-only caveat).
- **Quantization-backend confound**: the collapse conclusion is specific to bitsandbytes NF4 + PyTorch L1 pruning; GPTQ/AWQ (which reconstruct weights after quantization) or calibration-aware pruning might behave very differently — the authors list this as future work, which limits how far the "NF4+pruning collapses" guidance transfers to common inference stacks.
- **Internal tension worth noting**: Table 3 is captioned "FP16 models" yet the limitations state the 7B self-distillation required an INT8-quantized student to fit 48GB VRAM, so the flagship 920.6→24.3 recovery number mixes in a quantization factor; Table 3 (20-sample) and Table 4 (full-corpus) also report noticeably different pruned baselines for the same config (920.6 vs 1,089.95).
- **Pruning's practical value is undercut by their own data**: unstructured pruning yields no memory or latency benefit in every deployment measurement (GPU dense kernels and llama.cpp), so much of the factorial grid explores a factor the paper itself concludes is only useful after conversion to 2:4 sparsity — a conversion never actually benchmarked.
- **Scope limits**: single seed for the full 120-config sweep (multi-seed only on 16 key configs), one GPU type, one CPU machine class, English-only benchmarks, no mobile ARM/NPU, and architecture-specific idiosyncrasy (Nemotron-Mini-4B gains nothing from the recommended INT8+30%+recovery recipe) — honestly disclosed in Section 5.2.

## Strengths

- Genuinely systematic: a real factorial design with ordering analysis, rather than the usual one-off technique comparisons; 120 configurations on consistent hardware.
- Actionable practitioner output: the deployment recommendation table (target environment → configuration → measured cost/benefit) and the memory-tier feasibility table are immediately usable.
- The distillation ablation is a clean, useful negative result — the teacher signal is not what drives LoRA recovery, halving recovery-training memory.
- Strong validation hygiene: multi-seed checks, full-corpus correlation, benchmark cross-validation, and CPU-side reproduction of the GPU findings.
- Honest failure characterization: "fluent-looking but incoherent" collapse output is described operationally, and the ANOVA correctly identifies the interaction term as the dominant effect.

## Limitations

- bitsandbytes-only quantization and PyTorch L1-only pruning; no GPTQ, AWQ, SmoothQuant, Wanda, or SparseGPT — the two most practitioner-common inference quantization paths are untested (authors' own future work).
- The full 120-configuration sweep uses a single seed; only 16 key configurations were multi-seed validated.
- Recovered models are evaluated on perplexity only — no downstream task accuracy for the recovered-from-near-collapse configurations, and the recovered PPL remains well above baseline.
- On-device study covers CPU (llama.cpp) on one machine class; mobile ARM and NPU hardware untested, and GGUF q4/q8 differs from the bitsandbytes formats of the factorial study (within-engine comparisons only).
- English-only evaluation; behavior on multilingual or underrepresented inputs under aggressive compression is unmeasured.
- The 200-step, 256-sample recovery recipe is minimal; sensitivity to LoRA rank, step count, and recovery data composition is not ablated.

## Relevance

对"极致性能优化下的可靠性"方向高度相关：论文系统刻画了量化（INT8/NF4）与剪枝叠加时的精度崩溃边界——NF4+剪枝 16/16 全崩、先量化后剪枝必崩、INT8 在 50% 稀疏度下仍稳健——这正是低精度计算与激进优化引入的模型输出损坏与失效模式问题，且给出了可操作的部署档位与修复手段（无教师 LoRA 恢复）。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the NF4+pruning collapse (and the prune-first rescue) replicate under GPTQ or AWQ quantization, where weight reconstruction compensates for quantization error — or is the collapse an artifact of bitsandbytes' storage-oriented NF4 format?
- Can the LoRA recovery budget be characterized more precisely — what is the minimum data/steps needed, and does a larger or domain-specific recovery corpus close the remaining gap between recovered PPL (24.3) and baseline (11.82) for 50%-pruned Qwen2.5-7B?
- Do recovered-from-collapse models regain downstream task accuracy (MMLU, code, math), or does perplexity recovery mask residual capability loss that only benchmarks would reveal?
- Would converting the unstructured sparsity to hardware-supported 2:4 semi-structured patterns change the pruning cost/benefit conclusion on Ampere+ GPUs, and does 2:4 + INT8 interact as benignly as unstructured + INT8?
- Is the "recovery scales with model size" trend (43% at 2.7B vs 97% at 7B) a genuine scaling law or an artifact of each model's pruning sensitivity, e.g., does it hold for Llama-3, Mistral, and Gemma-2 families?
- Does the NF4+pruning collapse manifest differently on multilingual or low-resource inputs, where the paper concedes aggressive compression may disproportionately degrade performance?
