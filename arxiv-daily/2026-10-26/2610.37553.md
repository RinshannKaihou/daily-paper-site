> **一句话总结：** 本文用一个可解的多问题耦合模型证明：无监督 RLVR（多数投票自奖励）的崩溃是一种集体不稳定性——只要足够多的题目仍"举棋不定"（答案概率在 0.21–0.79 之间），共享参数方向的微扰就会被放大，并给出临界训练题数 N_c ≈ ln(1/δ)/(λ²G)：题目每问题自身的锐化被 1/N 减速而共享模式以 ∝λ² 的速率增长，题越多崩溃越必然；随机模拟以单一临界放大率 Λ_c ≈ 2.6 证实全部四个可证伪预言，小 Transformer 实验中移除共享读出方向后 168 次训练零崩溃、加回后 336 次中 335 次崩溃。

> **TL;DR:** A single-author Columbia theory paper explaining why label-free RLVR (majority-vote self-rewards, à la TTRL) collapses only when trained on many problems — He et al. (2026) empirically found ≤128 problems stable but ≥512 "consistently exhibit reward hacking". The mechanism is a Curie–Weiss-like collective instability: per-problem sharpening is slowed by 1/N while shared parameter directions grow at rate ∝λ², giving a critical dataset size N_c ≈ ln(1/δ)/(λ²G) that exists only while problems are uncertain (answer logits |z| < ln(2+√3) ≈ 1.32). Stochastic simulations confirm all four falsifiable predictions with a single critical amplification Λ_c ≈ 2.6 and fitted scaling N_c ∝ λ^−2.22 (predicted −2 + log correction); in a 6-digit-comparison Transformer (924 RL runs), an antisymmetric readout that removes the shared direction collapses in 0/168 runs vs 335/336 when the coupling is added back at ρ_b ≥ 1, and the initial-kernel surrogate predicts collapse with Brier 0.235 vs 0.289 for the trivial always-collapse baseline — but is systematically under-calibrated (mean predicted 0.46 vs observed 0.71), so it is a screening tool, not a stability guarantee.

## 核心贡献

- **机制（集体不稳定性）**：在一个题目共享参数的可解模型 z_i = u_i + λv 中，当各题偏好互相平衡时，只要单问题自强化漂移 F 的种群平均斜率 ⟨F′⟩ > 0（即题目仍不确定，K=3 时答案 logit |z| < ln(2+√3) ≈ 1.32，对应 0.21 < p < 0.79），任意共享方向的微扰就会被放大——这是一种 Curie–Weiss 顺磁不稳定性。
- **临界数据集规模的闭式解**：每题自身的锐化被 1/N 减速，而共享模式以与 N 无关的速率 ∝λ² 增长，两者赛跑得到 N_c ≃ ln(1/δ)/(λ² ln[F⋆/F(a)])，其中 δ 是播种共享模式的噪声、a 是初始置信度。这解释了 He et al. (2026) 观察到的"≤128 题稳定、≥512 题必然 reward hacking"的数据集规模依赖。
- **四个可证伪预言全部被模拟证实**：(P1) N 上存在尖锐相变；(P2) N_c ∝ λ^−2（拟合 −2.22，含对数修正）；(P3) batch size 只以对数方式影响 N_c（线性缩放规则 η∝B 下完全无影响）；(P4) 初始置信（a > z⋆）的模型永不崩溃。所有扫描在单一临界放大率 Λ_c ≈ 2.6 处坍缩为同一条相变曲线。
- **核（NTK）版理论与保护性耦合**：将理论推广到神经网络的经验神经正切核，识别出第二个耦合——锐化在题目间的泛化（prediction-aligned 耦合 k_s）——它对抗共享耦合 k_u，当 G·k_u < Λ_c·k_s 时任何训练集规模都不会崩溃（"特征保护区"）。
- **因果验证与补救**：在小 Transformer 的无标签 RL 中，反对称读出（z(x) = h(x) − h(x̄)）完全移除共享方向后 168 次训练零崩溃，加回受控偏置耦合后按剂量恢复崩溃；边际熵奖励 β·H(p̄) 在 β > β_c ≈ 1/2 时稳定均匀模式——但只压制均匀模式时，崩溃会转移到下一个集体核模式，准确率仍不保。
- **诊断工具**：⟨F′(z_i)⟩ 与核比率 k_u/k_d′、k_s/k_d′ 可在训练前从初始模型采样估计，用于筛选崩溃风险（排序好，AUC 0.87，但校准差）。

## Key Contributions

- **Mechanism (collective instability):** In a solvable model z_i = u_i + λv where problems share one parameter direction, a balanced population amplifies any shared perturbation exactly while the population-averaged slope ⟨F′⟩ of the single-problem self-reinforcement drift F is positive — i.e., while problems are still undecided (for K=3, answer logits |z| < z⋆ = ln(2+√3) ≈ 1.317, i.e. 0.21 < p < 0.79). This is formally a Curie–Weiss-type paramagnetic instability: problems are spins coupled through the mean field v.
- **A closed-form critical dataset size:** Per-problem sharpening is slowed by 1/N (each problem gets a 1/N share of the training signal) while the shared mode grows at a rate ∝λ² independent of N (it aggregates every problem's gradient). The resulting race gives N_c ≃ ln(1/δ)/(λ²G) with G = ln[F⋆/F(a)] decreasing in initial confidence a. This explains the dataset-size threshold of He et al. (2026) that existing single-problem sharpening theory (Huang et al., 2025) cannot.
- **Four falsifiable predictions, all verified:** (P1) a sharp transition in N emerges from the dynamics; (P2) N_c ∝ λ^−2 up to a logarithmic correction (fitted −2.22); (P3) batch size enters only logarithmically (not at all under η ∝ B); (P4) confident initial models (a > z⋆) never collapse at any N or training length. Sweeps spanning a 64× range of λ^−2 with critical sizes 60–8740 collapse onto a single transition at Λ_c ≈ 2.6 of the amplification λ²NG.
- **Kernel (NTK) extension with a protective coupling:** Linearizing the network gives dynamics in terms of the empirical NTK, summarized by shared coupling k_u (drives collapse), prediction-aligned coupling k_s (protects — sharpening one problem sharpens others), and self-kernel k_d′. When k_s > 0 the amplification saturates, yielding a feature-protected regime G·k_u < Λ_c·k_s in which no training-set size causes collapse.
- **Causal test in a real network:** In label-free RL of a small Transformer on a 6-digit comparison task, an antisymmetric readout that cancels the shared direction produces 0/168 collapsed runs (even for highly uncertain initializations with G = 1.12); adding back a controlled output-bias coupling ρ_b ≥ 1 collapses 335/336 runs. Collapse probability also grows with N and with base-model entropy (temperature), matching the language-model phenomenology.
- **A remedy and its limit:** A batch-level marginal-entropy bonus β·H(p̄) with derived threshold β_c ≤ 1/2 damps the uniform shared mode without slowing per-problem sharpening — confirmed in simulation and in the Transformer — but in the network the instability then moves to the next collective kernel mode, so accuracy is not preserved.

## Methodology

The paper proceeds from theory to simulation to a small network, each step testing the previous:

1. **Single-problem drift (Section 3).** A problem is abstracted to its final-answer distribution: binary policy π(+|x) = σ(z). The learner samples K answers, takes the majority vote â, rewards agreement (r_j = 1[y_j = â]), and applies a policy-gradient estimator (REINFORCE / group-mean baseline / GRPO). The expected logit update F(z) is computed exactly by enumeration over Bin(K,p) counts; for REINFORCE, K=3: F(z) = p(1−p)(2p−1). The key quantity is the slope F′(z), positive only inside the "unstable window" |z| < z⋆ ≈ 1.317. Notably, at K=3 all three estimators have proportional mean drifts (F, 2F/3, √2F), so estimator choice rescales time but not the phenomenon.
2. **Collective theory (Section 4).** N binary problems share one logit direction: z_i = u_i + λv. Minibatch SGD gives mean-field dynamics ż_i = (η/N)F(z_i) + ηλ²⟨F⟩. Linearizing around the balanced population yields Proposition 1: a shared perturbation grows iff ⟨F′⟩ > 0 (Curie–Weiss structure). Integrating the growth along the sharpening trajectory gives the amplification Λ = λ²NG(a); collapse occurs when δe^Λ = O(1), i.e. N_c ≃ ln(1/δ)/(λ²G). The noise seed δ from minibatch sampling enters only logarithmically (batch size has ~no effect). Generalizations cover arbitrary initial-logit distributions (via sup over the trajectory integral), M > 2 answers (the shared mode splits into M−1 decoupled directions with identical race structure), and a kernel form: linearizing a network gives ż = (η/N)K F(z) with the empirical NTK K; projecting onto the uniform mode **1** and the sign mode **s** (initial preferences) yields Λ(N) = G·Nk_u/(k_d′+Nk_s) and N_c = Λ_c k_d′/(G k_u − Λ_c k_s), measurable before any RL step.
3. **Stabilizer.** Adding a marginal-entropy bonus β·H(p̄) (H = binary entropy of the batch-pooled answer marginal, in the spirit of mutual-information clustering) contributes −4β⟨p(1−p)⟩² to the shared-mode growth rate, giving β_c(a) = F′(a)/(4[p(1−p)]²) ≤ 1/2 for all a.
4. **Stochastic simulations (Section 5).** The toy model is simulated with all finite-sample noise present: minibatch drawing, K answer samples, tie-breaking, SGD. Collapse is scored by order parameter Φ = |⟨sign z_i⟩| > 0.5; N_c is the midpoint of a logistic fit of P(collapse) vs ln N. Initial majorities are correct for 80% of problems, so collapse can only come from sampling noise. Code is released (github.com/pd2714/RLVR_instability).
5. **Transformer experiments (Section 6).** Task: 6-digit input, answer + iff x₁+x₂+x₃ > x₄+x₅+x₆. A small Transformer reads an answer logit from a CLS token; sampling temperature τ tunes base-model entropy without changing kernel ratios. "Base n" = model after n supervised steps (partial competence, mimicking a pretrained base). Two architectures at opposite ends of Eq. 8: a plain readout (strong shared coupling k_u/k_d′ = 0.40–0.70, weak protection k_s/k_d′ ≈ 0.05) and an antisymmetric readout z(x) = h(x) − h(x̄) (near-zero k_u/k_d′ < 0.002, strong k_s/k_d′ = 0.16–0.45), into which a controlled shared coupling is re-injected via a scaled output bias (dose ρ_b = λ_b²/k_d). Predictions are formed from the initial network alone (kernel ratios from per-example gradients + a kernel-calibrated surrogate = the stochastic toy model run with the network's measured couplings), then compared with 924 RL runs (22 conditions × 7 training-set sizes × 6 seeds).

## Results & Highlights

- **Sharp dataset-size transition at a single critical amplification (toy model, REINFORCE K=3, λ=0.1, a=0.5):** fitted N_c ≈ 536; at N=206 the majority-vote reward rises while accuracy stays near its initial level, whereas at N=2335 the reward goes to 1 and accuracy falls to chance (every problem converges to the same answer). Sweeps over seven couplings (critical sizes 60–8740, a 64× range in λ^−2) plus a confidence sweep collapse onto one transition at Λ_c ≈ 2.6 when plotted against Λ = λ²N ln[F⋆/F(a)]; fitted scaling N_c ∝ λ^−2.22 vs the predicted −2 plus logarithmic correction.
- **Transformer collapse phenomenology matches language models:** in collapsing runs the majority-vote reward reaches ≥ 0.999 while held-out accuracy falls from 0.82 to 0.50 (chance). For plain base-90 at τ=1, the collapse rate rises from 0/6 runs at N=16 to 6/6 for N ≥ 512 — the small-set-stable, large-set-unstable pattern of He et al. (2026), reproduced at much smaller N because the network's shared coupling k_u/k_d′ = 0.70 is far larger than the toy model's λ² = 0.01. Raising temperature (entropy) monotonically increases collapse: base 90 collapses in 29/34/37 of 42 runs at τ=1/2/3, base 120 in 17/35/38.
- **Causal test of the shared direction (antisymmetric Transformer):** with no added coupling the antisymmetric network collapses in 0 of 168 runs across both bases, both temperatures and every N up to 2048 (including base 10 at τ=2 with G = 1.12, which would collapse at small N under any appreciable shared coupling); with the shared bias restored at ρ_b ≥ 1 (k_u/k_d′ ≈ 1.2–1.5), 335 of 336 runs collapse.
- **Predictive accuracy of the initial-kernel surrogate (154 cells, 924 runs):** Brier score 0.235 vs 0.289 for the trivial always-collapse predictor, 0.380 for the deterministic kernel mean-field and 0.419 for the bare threshold Λ(N) > Λ_c; ROC AUC 0.87. But calibration is poor: mean predicted collapse probability 0.46 vs observed 0.71, and sign agreement (71% of cells) does not beat always-collapse (73%) once bootstrap correlation across conditions is accounted for (95% CI on the difference [−25, +25] points). The starkest failure: plain base-120 at τ=1 has initial G = 0.000 to three decimals, yet 17 of its 42 runs collapse.
- **Marginal-entropy remedy hits its derived threshold but does not save accuracy (plain base-90, τ=2):** without the bonus 7/8 runs collapse; at β=0.25, 2/8; at β ∈ {0.5, 1, 2}, 0 of 24 runs collapse — matching the derived β_c ≤ 1/2. Yet remedied runs end balanced (Φ ≈ 0.1) and confident (⟨|z|⟩ ≈ 7) at chance accuracy, with the learned labeling matching no simple input feature better than 58%. The kernel explains why: besides the uniform mode (eigenvalue 1.45×10⁴), the initial kernel has other collective modes (eigenvalues 5.1×10³, 2.5×10³, 2.3×10³, …) far above the self-kernel k_d = 94, and once the uniform mode is damped the next one takes over.

## Authors & Credibility

Single-author paper by Peize Ding (Columbia University, pd2714@columbia.edu). The work is positioned squarely in the active 2025–2026 literature on label-free RLVR (TTRL/Zuo et al., self-certainty/Zhao et al., entropy minimization/Agarwal et al., Co-rewarding/Zhang et al.) and engages directly with the systematic empirical study of He et al. (ICLR 2026), whose dataset-size finding (≤128 stable, ≥512 reward hacking) the theory explains. The statistical-physics toolkit (mean-field + linear stability, Curie–Weiss analogy, Nishimori 2001) is standard and correctly applied; the single-problem drift F is computed exactly by enumeration rather than approximated; predictions are pre-registered as four falsifiable statements before being tested; simulations include every source of finite-sample noise; and code plus raw results are released (github.com/pd2714/RLVR_instability). The Transformer section is unusually honest — the author devotes as much space to where the kernel predictions fail (underestimating collapse risk) as to where they succeed. No peer-review status is stated (arXiv v1, 27 Oct 2026); the analysis of real pretrained LLMs is explicitly deferred to future work.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The cross-problem coupling mechanism is genuinely new relative to prior sharpening theory (Huang et al. 2025; He et al. 2026), which is single-problem and cannot explain why collapse depends on the number of training problems. The N_c ∝ λ^−2 law, the single dimensionless amplification Λ = λ²NG, and the identification of a protective prediction-aligned kernel coupling are crisp, falsifiable contributions. The closest prior work (Fan et al. 2026) is a one-step linearization for labeled RLVR on a pair of queries; this paper derives a many-problem threshold.
- **Rigor:** The toy-model side is rigorous — exact enumeration of F, closed-form N_c verified in the noise-free limit by bisection, and all four predictions confirmed in fully stochastic simulation. But the theory-to-network transfer is where rigor thins: the honest D.3 analysis shows the kernel-calibrated surrogate's sign agreement (71%) is statistically indistinguishable from always predicting collapse (73%, bootstrap CI [−25,+25] points), and the paper's own headline counterexample — plain base-120 at τ=1 with initial G = 0.000 collapsing in 17/42 runs — directly violates the fixed-kernel prediction. The claimed predictive value in the network rests mainly on the Brier/AUC comparison, which is dominated by the easy separation between architecturally-protected and coupling-injected runs rather than by fine-grained risk ranking (within plain networks the surrogate agrees in only 67% of cells vs 81% for always-collapse).
- **Reproducibility:** Strong for the simulations (code released, all hyperparameters in Appendix E, seeds stated). The Transformer is a synthetic 6-digit comparison task with a CLS-token readout — fully specified, but very far from an LLM doing chain-of-thought reasoning, where answers are multi-token, the majority vote is over free-form strings, and the kernel is enormous.
- **Significance:** If the mechanism carries over to pretrained LLMs (untested here), it would turn He et al.'s empirical threshold into a computable diagnostic (⟨F′⟩ and kernel ratios before training) and would explain why higher-entropy base models collapse faster. Until that transfer is demonstrated, the paper is a well-verified theory of a toy model plus a suggestive synthetic-network check.
- **Red flags:** (1) The "no quantitative transfer" admission — a network with G = 0.000 still collapsing in 40% of runs — means the central quantitative claim (Eq. 7/8) is not validated in the only non-toy system in the paper; the author flags kernel drift (protective coupling k_s/k_d′ dropping 0.42 → 0.07 within one epoch in base-20; self-kernel growing 28–245× in base-10) as the cause rather than fixing it. (2) The marginal-entropy remedy is presented as a contribution but demonstrably does not preserve accuracy in the network — it converts uniform collapse into balanced-but-arbitrary collapse — so its practical value is limited to diagnostics. (3) The confidence-by-curriculum suggestion (train on confident problems first) is explicitly untested. (4) The entropy claim ("what matters is ⟨F′⟩, not entropy") is only verified through the temperature knob on one synthetic task.

## Strengths

- Derives a closed-form, interpretable law (N_c ≈ ln(1/δ)/(λ²G)) for a phenomenon — dataset-size-dependent collapse in unsupervised RLVR — that previously had only empirical characterization.
- Pre-registers four falsifiable predictions and confirms all of them in fully stochastic simulations, including the single-amplifier collapse (Λ_c ≈ 2.6) across a 64× range of couplings and critical sizes from 60 to 8740.
- The antisymmetric-readout intervention is a clean causal test: same network, same parameters, only the shared direction removed — 0/168 vs 335/336 collapse is about as decisive as ablations get.
- The kernel extension yields a non-obvious, testable protective effect (generalization of sharpening, k_s) and a feature-protected regime where adding data is safe — with the ρ_b/µ dose-response confirming both the harmful and protective couplings.
- Exceptional scientific honesty: the paper quantifies its own predictor's failure modes (under-calibration, the G = 0.000 / 17-of-42 contradiction, kernel drift magnitudes) rather than burying them.

## Limitations

- No experiments on pretrained language models — the central motivating phenomenon — due to compute limits; the largest model in the paper is a small Transformer on a synthetic 6-digit comparison task with binary answers.
- The theory quantitatively transfers only in the fixed-kernel (lazy) regime; in feature-learning networks the kernel drifts (protective coupling eroding 0.42 → 0.07 in one epoch; self-kernel growing up to 245×), and initial-kernel predictions systematically underestimate collapse risk.
- The marginal-entropy bonus stabilizes only the uniform mode; other collective kernel modes take over and accuracy still falls to chance, so no complete remedy is demonstrated in a network.
- The stability of confident populations (P4) and the curriculum-by-confidence suggestion are established/predicted only for the homogeneous fixed-coupling model.
- Λ_c is calibrated on the toy model and depends logarithmically on the noise seed; binary/answer-level abstraction ignores multi-token reasoning chains, format preferences, and length biases that dominate real LLM collapse.

## Relevance

（依据研究兴趣配置）本文直接针对无监督 RLVR / 自奖励强化学习在规模化训练中的崩溃机制，把"数据集越大越容易 reward hacking"的经验现象（He et al. 2026）转化为可计算的临界阈值 N_c 与训练前可测量的诊断量（⟨F′⟩、NTK 核比率），对理解 LLM 自训练的稳定性边界、设计防崩溃的label-free RL 算法具有重要理论指导意义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do ⟨F′(z_i)⟩ and the kernel ratios k_u/k_d′, k_s/k_d′ measured on a real pretrained LLM (e.g., Qwen or Llama with TTRL) predict the dataset-size threshold of He et al. (2026)? The author names this the most important next step.
- How does the theory extend to multi-token free-form answers, where the "majority vote" is over semantic equivalence classes and the shared direction includes format/length preferences rather than a single answer-token bias?
- Can a kernel-tracking predictor (re-measuring K during training) close the one-sided error where feature learning erodes the protective coupling, or is collapse decided too early for re-measurement to help?
- Does the feature-protected regime (G·k_u < Λ_c·k_s) exist in pretrained models, and could auxiliary objectives that strengthen prediction-aligned coupling (e.g., consistency regularization) enlarge it?
- Does the race between per-problem sharpening and shared-mode growth predict different thresholds for other pseudo-reward signals (self-certainty, semantic entropy, entropy minimization), whose single-problem drifts F differ?
