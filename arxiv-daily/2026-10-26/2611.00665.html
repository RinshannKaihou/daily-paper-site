Why this matters

Anyone deploying fine-tuned models on 4-bit weights: a 16-bit safety audit certifies nothing — hidden preferences can re-emerge amplified or erased.

16-bit 安全审计无法约束量化部署后的真实风险 —— 审计必须在部署所用的确切量化权重上进行。

Critical edge

The “explicit preferences are ~5× more stable” claim is confounded — those students stopped training at step 9 of 342 and took far smaller weight updates, so the stability may track delta size, not subliminality (authors concede they “cannot determine” this).

Methodology · distilled

  1. Install a hidden preferenceA teacher prompted to “prefer pandas” continues 30,000 number sequences; 7,500 clean examples train LoRA (rank 8, all 196 projections of 28 blocks) into Qwen2.5-7B. The word “panda” never appears; adapters are merged before quantization.
  2. Quantize the merged weightsbf16 reference; HQQ swept 8→2 bits, NF4 at 4 bits — calibration-free, group size 64, transformer layers only. Per-layer rounding error E = Q(W) − W recorded.
  3. Perturb the errorGaussian noise norm-matched per layer, negated error, and 4 within-group shuffles of 64 weights — magnitudes preserved, only the arrangement changes. Controls get identical transforms.
  4. Measure behaviour + internals50 “favourite animal in one word” prompts; net strength vs neutral controls, R normalized to bf16. Amplification requires R ≥ 1.15 and log-prob gap ≥ 1.10 on both seeds.

  • Internals survive while behaviour swings ±30 pts — representation-level probes cannot predict quantized preference expression.
  • The untouched output layer carries the readout: 3-bit on the output layer alone (never trained) erases ~⅓ of the gain (53–56% → 41–42%); the base model stays intact.
  • Trait-specific sharpening, not generic confidence: 4-bit HQQ cuts students’ first-token entropy by 0.22 nats vs 0.05–0.08 for base/controls; NF4 raises entropy for all.
  • Explicit preferences of matched 16-bit strength (50.8–51.2%) never amplify under any quantizer or error rearrangement.

Strengths

  • One-factor-at-a-time logic: every comparison (HQQ vs NF4, noise vs HQQ, shuffle vs real, negated vs unchanged) changes exactly one knob, with identically-transformed controls.
  • The shuffle/negation interventions cleanly separate error arrangement from magnitude — the decisive experiment.
  • Actionable, non-obvious deployment insight: the never-trained output layer alone, quantized at 3 bits, removes a third of trait expression.
  • Dual amplification threshold (R ≥ 1.15 and log-prob gap ≥ 1.10) pre-empts small-denominator artifacts; frank limitations section; code released.

Limitations

  • One model (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct), one benign trait, one recipe; 2 seeds, 3 noise draws, 4 shuffles — ranges are coarse summaries of few conditions.
  • Only HQQ + NF4 at group size 64 — GPTQ / AWQ / GGUF and other group sizes, the settings most deployments actually run, are untested.
  • Explicit control confound: step-9 checkpoint ⇒ far smaller weight-update norm ∥d∥ (subliminal ∥d∥ = 5.67/5.78, no explicit counterpart reported).
  • Probe = first-token probability on 50 near-identical prompts; no long-form or contextual expression, no general-capability check; mechanism of error–readout coupling unexplained.

Questions for further exploration

  • Does the volatility persist under GPTQ, AWQ, and GGUF-family quantizers — and how does it scale with group size and model scale (1.5B → 70B+)?
  • Can the weight-update-norm confound be broken — e.g., full-duration explicit training on diluted panda text matched to ∥d∥?
  • Why does error alignment cos(Es, Eb) differ between HQQ (0.88) and NF4 (0.96) — and can it serve as a cheap pre-deployment volatility predictor?
  • Would a harmful subliminal trait (political bias, backdoor trigger) show the same 0.22–1.58 swing, and does the amplified regime survive QAT or smoothing?
  • Where in the forward pass do shuffled errors couple to the “panda” logit — via the output-layer readout path the 3-bit ablation implicates?
