Key Metric
59.63×

the generation tokens needed at 80% expert pruning to reach 90% of the original model's AIME coverage — 1,319 candidates vs the original's 20 (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B).

at 50% expert pruning: 22 candidates · 1.12× tokens

Why This Matters

Deploying pruned models with repeated sampling? Total-parameter size is the wrong yardstick — judge pruning by retained coverage and the tokens needed to recover it.

Critical Edge

No recovery training anywhere — real pipelines distill after pruning, so headline claims like “FFN pruning destroys coverage” may not survive deployment.

01Methodology — distilled

  1. Backbones. Three MoE models — Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (34.66B total / 2.95B active), GLM-4.7-Flash (29.94B / 3.58B), Gemma-4-26B-A4B-it — plus dense Qwen3.5-9B/4B/2B and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct.
  2. Prune, no recovery. Experts via RAZOR (the authors' own method), EAN, FREQ; FFN channels via first-order Taylor saliency; layers via a perplexity-greedy SLEB-style selector; plus mixed-axis configs. No recovery training is applied anywhere.
  3. Sample at matched budgets. Temperature 0.7, top-p 0.95, top-k 20, 8,192-token cap. 2,048 samples/problem on AIME-2026 (30) & Minerva (272); 512 on HumanEval+ (164) & LiveCodeBench-v6 (175).
  4. Measure coverage vs cost. Unbiased pass@k estimator; accuracy retention Em and coverage retention Rm,K; problem-identity sets (retained / lost / newly solved); fixed-target cost k*m(α = 0.9), right-censored; paired bootstrap over 4,000 resamples; a uniform-loss reference; repetition + 4-gram diversity.

  • Selection-method gains are sampling efficiency. RAZOR hits pass@1 63.53% vs EAN's 59.38%, yet RAZOR / EAN / FREQ solve the identical 29-problem AIME set at k = 2048 — no capacity difference.
  • Coverage can grow under severe pruning. At 80% expert pruning, Minerva coverage rises 226 → 235 / 272 (23 newly solved vs 14 lost) despite much lower pass@1.
  • Shorter outputs can mask failure. Layer 30% → 60%: responses shrink 7,806 → 1,564 tokens while pass@512 falls 5.6% → 0; at 30% FFN pruning, 29.94% pass@1 with only 2.08% truncation — wrong answers terminate normally.
  • Mixed-axis loses at similar size. Mixed-B (12.45B total) covers 25 AIME problems vs 29 for expert-only 70% (12.12B); pass@1 16.68% vs 51.23%.
  • Original response length predicts vulnerability. After 30% FFN pruning, always-solved LiveCodeBench problems drop to 65.0% (short half) vs 29.9% (long half) pass@1 — same original accuracy, different fragility.
  • Bigger source ≠ better pruned model. Qwen3.5-122B-A10B at 75% expert pruning keeps 28/30 AIME but only matches the smaller 35B original — and trails it on both code suites.

03Strengths

  • Decomposes accuracy into coverage (what stays solvable) + sampling cost (tokens to recover it), with problem-identity tracking — not aggregates alone.
  • Total-vs-active parameter accounting on every comparison, exposing why "similar size" claims in the pruning literature mislead.
  • Publishes deflationary results: mixed-axis loses to expert-only; a larger source model gives no consistent advantage; higher pass@1 ≠ broader capacity.
  • Statistical care: unbiased estimator, paired bootstrap CIs, right-censored targets, α-sensitivity, and honest scope statements throughout.

04Limitations

  • No recovery training — conclusions characterize the pre-recovery state; prune-then-distill pipelines dominate practice and may change the story.
  • Inconsistent graders across sub-experiments (Minerva normalization variants, 7,200 s timeout → zero) — conceded by the authors to break some cross-condition comparisons.
  • Small benchmarks: AIME-2026 has 30 problems; several identity-level claims rest on 14–30 items, and Minerva coverage gaps persist where AIME agrees.
  • Single decoding config, math + code only; no latency / throughput / energy — token and parameter counts are proxies for real cost.

05Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does FFN pruning's coverage collapse partially recover after distillation (ReCal-style reweighting or on-policy distillation)?
  2. Would a compute-matched comparison (equal active parameters or FLOPs/token, not equal total size) reverse the expert-vs-FFN ordering?
  3. Do the RAZOR / EAN / FREQ solved-set identities survive 500+ problem sets, or is the AIME agreement a 30-problem granularity artifact?
  4. Can "original response length predicts vulnerability" be made actionable at pruning time — e.g., calibrating saliency on long-CoT samples?
  5. Is there any regime where a severely pruned model + heavy sampling beats just running the original at larger k — economically?
  6. How do pass@k curves under pruning behave at other temperatures, or with verifiers / majority voting selecting among candidates?
