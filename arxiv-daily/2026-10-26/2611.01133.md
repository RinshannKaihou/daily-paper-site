> **一句话总结：** 腾讯团队用大规模重复采样（数学每题 2,048 个样本、代码 512 个）在 pass@k 视角下重新审视结构化剪枝：50% 专家剪枝后 Qwen3.6-35B-A3B / GLM-4.7-Flash 仍保留原模型 100.6% / 100.4% 的平均覆盖率（准确率只剩 92.8% / 75.8%），同总参数量下专家剪枝覆盖率保留约 100% 而 FFN 剪枝只剩 48.4% / 18.8%；但覆盖"还在"不等于"便宜"——80% 专家剪枝要把 AIME 覆盖追到原模型的 90% 需要 1,319 个候选、59.63 倍生成 token（单条回复反而从 4,923.5 缩到 4,451.4 token），且 RAZOR/EAN/FREQ 三种专家选择在 k=2048 时解出完全相同的 29 道 AIME 题——结论是剪枝必须同时按"还能解哪些题"和"追回正确解的采样成本"来评估。

> **TL;DR:** This Tencent "work in progress" asks whether the accuracy drop after structured pruning reflects lost reasoning capacity or merely rarer correct samples, by comparing original vs pruned models with pass@k at matched budgets (2,048 samples/problem on AIME-2026 and Minerva, 512 on HumanEval+ and LiveCodeBench-v6; expert/FFN/layer pruning on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, GLM-4.7-Flash and Gemma-4-26B-A4B-it plus 4 dense backbones, no recovery training). The headline: at 50% expert pruning the Qwen and GLM models retain 100.6% / 100.4% of original coverage vs only 92.8% / 75.8% accuracy retention, and at near-identical total size (18.54B vs 18.49B) expert pruning keeps ~100% mean coverage where FFN pruning keeps 48.4% / 18.8% — but recovered coverage gets expensive: at 80% expert pruning, hitting 90% of the original model's AIME coverage takes 1,319 candidates and 59.63x the tokens of the original's 20 candidates. It also shows selection-method gains (RAZOR 63.53% vs EAN 59.38% pass@1 on AIME at 50% expert pruning) collapse to the *same* 29-problem solved set at k=2048, i.e. sampling efficiency, not capacity. Worth reading if you care about how pruned LLMs interact with test-time compute; skip if you only deploy recovery-trained compressed models.

## 核心贡献

- **提出"覆盖率 vs 采样效率"的剪枝评估分解**：不再只看 pass@1，而是用匹配预算的 pass@k 曲线（数学 k=2048、代码 k=512）+ 覆盖保留率 R_{m,K} + 解题集合的"身份级"对比（保留/丢失/新解的题），把"剪枝后准确率下降"区分为两种失效：能解的题真的变少了，还是正确解只是变得更难采到。
- **系统性实证：专家剪枝保留覆盖、FFN/层剪枝摧毁覆盖（同总参数量下）**：50% 剪枝时 Qwen3.6-35B-A3B 专家剪枝剩 18.54B 总参数、FFN 剪枝剩 18.49B，但平均覆盖率保留约 100% vs 48.4%（GLM：100% vs 18.8%）。原因是专家剪枝不动每 token 激活参数（2.94B），FFN 剪枝把激活参数砍到 2.38B——只用总参数量评判压缩会掩盖这一差异；层剪枝在名义 50% 时覆盖率只剩 9.4% / 5.4%。
- **揭示"覆盖保留但采样成本爆炸"与"回复变短≠更可靠"**：80% 专家剪枝追平 90% 原覆盖需要 1,319 个候选、59.63 倍 token；层剪枝 30%→60% 平均回复长度从 7,806 降到 1,564 token，pass@512 却从 5.6% 跌到 0——截断率下降可能只是模型更早放弃而非恢复正确性。多样性（4-gram 重叠 0.316→0.237）可以与样本内重复上升（30.2%→41.5%）同时发生。
- **方法增益与模型来源的再解释**：专家选择方法（RAZOR/EAN/FREQ）pass@1 差异很大（63.53% vs 59.38% vs FREQ），但在 k=2048 时解出完全相同的 29 道 AIME 题——增益本质是采样效率而非能力扩展；更大的源模型（Qwen3.5-122B-A10B 剪 75% 专家后 AIME 保留 28/30）并不保证优于更小的原模型；专家+FFN+层混合剪枝（Mixed-B 12.45B）在总参数量接近纯专家剪枝（12.12B）时覆盖率反而更低（AIME 25 vs 29 题）。
- **原始回复长度是剪枝脆弱性的额外信号**：在原模型 100% 解出的 LiveCodeBench-v6 题目中，30% FFN 剪枝把短回复组的 pass@1 降到 65.0%、长回复组降到 29.9%——原始准确率相同也会因回复长度不同而脆弱程度不同。

## Key Contributions

- **A coverage-vs-sampling-cost evaluation framework for pruning.** Instead of a single pass@1 number, the paper tracks the full pass@k curve at matched budgets (unbiased Chen et al. 2021 estimator), defines accuracy retention E_m = P_m(1)/P_0(1) and coverage retention R_{m,K} = P_m(K)/P_0(K), and compares *which* problems are solved (retained / lost / newly solved), plus the candidates and generated tokens k*_m(α) needed to hit a fixed coverage target (α = 0.9, with 0.75/1.0 as sensitivity checks).
- **Expert pruning preserves coverage where FFN and layer pruning destroy it, at similar total size.** At 50% structural pruning, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B keeps 18.54B total params under expert pruning vs 18.49B under FFN pruning, yet mean coverage retention is ~100% vs 48.4% (GLM-4.7-Flash: ~100% vs 18.8%). The paper explains why: expert pruning keeps routed top-k fixed, leaving active parameters nearly unchanged (2.94B), while FFN pruning cuts active parameters to 2.38B — so total size alone never characterizes a pruned model.
- **Coverage can survive (or even grow) while accuracy collapses.** At 80% expert pruning of Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, Minerva coverage *rises* from 226 to 235 of 272 problems (23 newly solved vs 14 lost) despite much lower pass@1 — a redistribution of successes, not pure preservation.
- **Retained coverage can be prohibitively expensive to sample, and shorter responses can mean giving up.** Reaching 90% of original AIME coverage costs 22 candidates (1.12x tokens) at 50% expert pruning but 1,319 candidates (59.63x tokens) at 80%, even though mean response length falls from 4,923.5 to 4,451.4 tokens. Lower truncation is decoupled from correctness: on consistently-solved LiveCodeBench-v6 problems, 30% FFN pruning yields 29.94% pass@1 with only 2.08% truncation — ≥67.98% of outputs are wrong *and* terminate normally.
- **Selection-method and source-model gains reframed as sampling efficiency.** RAZOR, EAN and FREQ at 50% expert pruning solve the identical 29-problem AIME set at k=2048 despite a 4+ point pass@1 spread; a much larger source model (Qwen3.5-122B-A10B, 75% expert-pruned, 28/30 AIME retained) only matches the smaller original 35B-A3B on AIME and trails it on both code tasks; and a mixed expert+FFN+layer configuration (Mixed-B, 12.45B total) loses to expert-only pruning (12.12B total) on AIME coverage (25 vs 29 problems at k=2048) while also using fewer active parameters (2.39B vs 2.93B).

## Methodology

**Models.** Three MoE backbones — Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (34.66B total / 2.95B active, 40 layers, 256 experts/layer, top-8), GLM-4.7-Flash (29.94B / 3.58B, 46 MoE layers, 64 experts, top-4) and Gemma-4-26B-A4B-it (25.23B / 3.82B) — plus dense Qwen3.5-9B/4B/2B and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct for FFN/layer pruning, a Qwen3.5-35B-A3B Base/Instruct pair, and Qwen3.5-122B-A10B at 75% expert pruning.

**Pruning.** RAZOR for expert selection (the authors' own method), first-order Taylor saliency for FFN intermediate channels, and a perplexity-greedy SLEB-style selector for layers; EAN (expert activation norm) and FREQ (routing frequency) as expert-selection baselines. All methods calibrate on RAZOR's razorcal data. Critically, **no recovery training** is applied anywhere — the study isolates what pruning alone leaves behind.

**Evaluation protocol.** Temperature 0.7, top-p 0.95, decoding top-k 20, 8,192-token output cap. Mathematics: 2,048 samples per problem on all 30 AIME-2026 and 272 Minerva problems; code: budget k = 512 on 164 HumanEval+ and a 175-problem LiveCodeBench-v6 subset (Jan–Apr 2025, 7,000 tests). Coverage uses the unbiased pass@k estimator; retention ratios (Eq. 2) are computed against the same backbone's original model; fixed-target cost k*_m(α) is the smallest budget reaching α·P_0(K), right-censored at K when unattained, with paired bootstrap (4,000 resamples) for uncertainty.

**Generation-behavior metrics.** Repetition = fraction of normally terminated outputs containing an 8-gram at least three times; diversity = symmetric clipped 4-gram overlap across candidates (lower = more diverse), computed over correct *and* incorrect outputs. A "uniform-loss reference" — subsampling original successes at a rate matching the pruned model's pass@1 — tests whether a uniform per-problem difficulty increase explains observed coverage.

**Mixed-axis configurations.** Mixed-A / Mixed-B combine 50% expert + 10% layer pruning with ~20% / ~30% FFN-width reduction (13.82B / 12.45B total, 2.49B / 2.39B active), compared against expert-only RAZOR at 70/75/80%.

## Results & Highlights

- **Coverage survives where accuracy does not (Obs. 1):** at 50% expert pruning, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and GLM-4.7-Flash retain **100.6% and 100.4%** of original mean coverage (K = 2048 math, 512 code, equal-weighted over AIME-2026, Minerva, HumanEval+, LiveCodeBench-v6) while retaining only **92.8% and 75.8%** of original accuracy.
- **Pruning axis dominates at matched total size (Obs. 3):** at 50% pruning, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B retains 18.54B params (expert) vs 18.49B (FFN), yet mean coverage retention is **~100% vs 48.4%** (GLM-4.7-Flash: ~100% vs 18.8% at 16.05B vs 15.80B); layer pruning retains only **9.4% / 5.4%** coverage at nominal 50%.
- **Net coverage gain via turnover (Obs. 2):** at 80% expert pruning on Minerva, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B covers **235/272 vs the original's 226/272** (23 newly solved problems vs 14 lost); on AIME at 50% expert pruning it preserves all 29 originally solved problems.
- **Sampling cost explodes under severe pruning (Obs. 6):** reaching 90% of original AIME coverage takes 22 candidates at 50% expert pruning (vs 20 for the original; 1.12x tokens) but **1,319 candidates and 59.63x the tokens at 80%**, even though mean response length *falls* from 4,923.5 to 4,451.4 tokens.
- **Shorter outputs can mask total failure (Obs. 7):** raising layer pruning from 30% to 60% on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B AIME cuts mean length from **7,806 to 1,564 tokens while pass@512 falls from 5.6% to zero**; on consistently-solved LiveCodeBench-v6 problems (longer half), 30% FFN pruning yields **29.94% pass@1 with only 2.08% truncation** — at least 67.98% of outputs are incorrect yet terminate normally, while all 14 problems still have a success within k = 512.
- **Demanding tasks are pruning-sensitive (Obs. 9):** original Qwen3.6-35B-A3B has pass@1 54.8% on LiveCodeBench-v6 (3,489 mean tokens) vs 91.5% on HumanEval+ (270 tokens); at 30% FFN pruning, LiveCodeBench-v6 retains **67.6% of its pass@512 vs HumanEval+'s 97.5%** (GLM: 59.2% vs 94.4%), with LiveCodeBench responses lengthening to 5,261 tokens.
- **Original response length predicts vulnerability beyond accuracy (Obs. 10):** among LiveCodeBench-v6 problems the original Qwen model solves in *every* sample, 30% FFN pruning drops pass@1 to **65.0% (shorter-response half) vs 29.9% (longer-response half)**; for GLM's longer-response group (≥90% original success), pass@1 falls 96.3%→29.9%, output length grows 436→1,873 tokens, and tokens per correct sample rise **13.84x** — yet all 14 problems remain covered at k = 512.
- **Selection-method gains are sampling efficiency (Obs. 11):** at 50% expert pruning on AIME, RAZOR reaches pass@1 **63.53% vs EAN's 59.38%**, yet all three methods (incl. FREQ) solve the **same 29 problems at k = 2048**; hitting the shared 87.0% coverage target needs 22 (RAZOR), 58 (EAN) and 14 (FREQ) candidates, and RAZOR needs **109,923 generated tokens/problem vs EAN's 308,985 (−64.4%)**.
- **Mixed-axis pruning loses at similar total size (Obs. 13):** at k = 2048 on AIME, Mixed-A/Mixed-B cover **25 problems vs 29 / 29 / 27** for expert-only 70 / 75 / 80% pruning; Mixed-B (12.45B total, 2.39B active) trails expert-70% (12.12B, 2.93B active) in pass@1 on AIME (16.68% vs 51.23%), HumanEval+ (53.40% vs 85.59%) and LiveCodeBench-v6 (12.03% vs 46.17%).
- **Uniform difficulty increase is refuted (Obs. 14):** Qwen3.6-35B-A3B FFN-40% shows observed AIME coverage of **63.3% vs 92.1%** under a uniform-loss reference matched to the same mean accuracy loss (residual −28.7 points, 95% bootstrap CI [−45.2, −13.4]); the LiveCodeBench-v6 residual is −46.9 points [−53.8, −40.0].
- **Diversity and repetition can move oppositely (Obs. 8):** 19% layer pruning on GLM-4.7-Flash raises repetition from **30.2% to 41.5%** while *reducing* 4-gram overlap from **0.316 to 0.237** (four-task averages); expert-50% instead lowers repetition to 16.9% with overlap 0.249.
- **Bigger source ≠ better pruned model (Obs. 5):** Qwen3.5-122B-A10B at 75% expert pruning retains **28/30 AIME problems (pass@1 65.0%→53.0%)** but only matches original Qwen3.5-35B-A3B on AIME and trails it on both code tasks; a 25% FFN-pruned Qwen3.5-9B (matched FFN width to Qwen3.5-4B) trails the original 4B on all four tasks (e.g. AIME 12.07/60.00 vs 45.16/90.00 pass@1/pass@K).
- **Output-cap sensitivity:** raising the cap from 8k to 16k tokens on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B expert-30% improves AIME pass@1 by **7.70 points** and coverage at k = 2048 from 29 to 30 problems, while layer-30% stays near 0.37% pass@1 (coverage 3→5).

## Authors & Credibility

Mingyang Song and Mao Zheng are with the Foundation Model Department at Tencent (industry lab; contact email at tencent.com). Notably, RAZOR — the expert-selection method used throughout and one of the compared winners — is the authors' own prior work (Song & Zheng, 2026b), with code at github.com/nick7nlp/RAZOR; EAN and FREQ baselines follow Jaiswal et al. 2025 using the RAZOR implementation. The paper carries explicit AI-use, ethics and reproducibility statements, and the appendix is unusually thorough: per-configuration total/active parameter accounting, grading procedures, bootstrap intervals, and sensitivity checks. One striking honesty marker: three AIME expert-pruning configurations (25/30/40%) are flagged as "outcome-selected and excluded from statistical certification." The manuscript is labeled **"Work in Progress"** and is not peer-reviewed (arXiv 31 Oct 2026).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty (moderate, but well-executed):** pass@k coverage analysis itself is standard (Chen et al. 2021; Large Language Monkeys; Yue et al. 2025), and applying it to pruning is an incremental reframing. The genuinely new contributions are the problem-identity decomposition (retained/lost/newly solved), the total-vs-active-parameter confound made explicit in every comparison, and the fixed-target token-cost measure. The RAZOR-vs-EAN-vs-FREQ "same solved set" finding is the most conceptually valuable result.
- **Rigor (strong on sampling, weaker on grading):** sampling budgets are exceptional (2,048/problem × 302 math problems × dozens of models), and paired bootstrap CIs plus right-censoring of unattained targets are done properly. However, grading is **inconsistent across sub-experiments by the authors' own admission**: Minerva answer normalization differs for the Gemma-4 runs, the mixed-axis runs use "additional adjudication", and Minerva grading judgments exceeding a 7,200-second timeout are scored zero — the paper concedes these cross-condition comparisons "do not isolate the effect of pruning." Several Minerva headline numbers therefore carry a caveat the main-text figures don't always repeat.
- **A small-benchmark artifact risk:** AIME-2026 has only 30 problems. The celebrated result that RAZOR/EAN/FREQ solve the *identical* 29-problem set is a 29-of-30 agreement on a tiny set; on Minerva (272 problems) coverage gaps between the same methods persist at k=2048, which the authors acknowledge weakens generality.
- **A deployment-relevant confound handled honestly but conveniently:** the expert-vs-FFN comparison at "similar total size" is not compute-matched — expert pruning keeps ~2.94B active parameters vs FFN's 2.38B (~24% more per-token compute). The paper foregrounds this confound rather than hiding it, but readers seeking memory-compression conclusions must redo the accounting: expert pruning "wins" coverage while spending more FLOPs per token.
- **No recovery training:** every conclusion concerns one-shot pruned models. Practical compression pipelines almost always distill/retrain after pruning (the paper itself cites ReCal/OPD doing exactly this), so results like "FFN pruning collapses coverage" may substantially overstate what matters in deployment. No latency, throughput or energy measurements are provided — token counts and parameter counts are proxies, as the authors state.
- **Potential selection bias in framing:** the expert-selection section headlines RAZOR's token saving vs EAN (−64.4%) while FREQ — the *most* sample-efficient method (14 vs RAZOR's 22 candidates to target) — does not get its token count in the main text. Given RAZOR is the authors' own method, this is a framing choice worth noticing, even though the FREQ candidate count is reported plainly.
- **Red flags (minor):** outcome-selected AIME runs excluded from certification implies multiple evaluation rounds; "Work in Progress" status; the enormous evaluation compute (hundreds of thousands of generations) is never costed, which matters for anyone hoping to reproduce.

## Strengths

- Decomposes a single accuracy scalar into coverage (which problems remain solvable) + sampling cost (attempts and tokens to recover them), with problem-identity tracking rather than aggregates alone.
- Consistent total-vs-active parameter accounting on every comparison, exposing why "similar size" comparisons in the pruning literature can mislead.
- Publishes negative and deflationary results: mixed-axis pruning loses to expert-only; a larger source model gives no consistent advantage; higher pass@1 for a selection method does not mean broader capacity; increased CoT diversity coexists with more within-response repetition.
- Statistical care throughout: unbiased pass@k estimator, paired bootstrap CIs, censored targets, sensitivity to the coverage target α, and explicit refusal to equate finite-budget coverage with true reasoning capacity.
- Honest scope statements everywhere (grader acceptance ≠ valid derivation; unobserved success ≠ unsolvable; fewer active parameters ≠ faster inference).

## Limitations

- **No recovery training**, so conclusions characterize the pre-recovery state and may not transfer to prune-then-distill pipelines that dominate practice.
- **Inconsistent graders across sub-experiments** (Minerva normalization variants, 7,200s timeout→zero, Gemma percentage-answer ambiguity) compromise several cross-model Minerva comparisons by the authors' own statement.
- **Small benchmarks**: AIME-2026 (30), HumanEval+ (164), LiveCodeBench-v6 subset (175); several identity-level claims rest on 14–30 problems per group.
- **Single sampling configuration** (temperature 0.7 / top-p 0.95 / top-k 20 / 8,192 cap) and only math + code tasks — pass@k curves are known to be sensitive to decoding settings, and knowledge/instruction-following behavior is untested.
- **No wall-clock, latency or energy evaluation**; active-parameter counts are a proxy for compute, and the 59.63x token cost at 80% expert pruning is likely never cheaper than just running the original model.
- **Backbones limited to 2026-generation Qwen/GLM/Gemma families**; no older or independently trained families to test generality of the expert-pruning advantage.

## Relevance

与"高效推理/模型压缩 + test-time scaling"方向高度相关：这篇工作把 pass@k 覆盖率分析引入结构化剪枝评估，直接回答"剪枝后的 MoE/dense 模型还能解哪些题、需要多少采样预算追回覆盖"，并给出专家剪枝 vs FFN/层剪枝在总参数与激活参数两个维度上的部署取舍实证，对设计剪枝后模型 + 重复采样的推理系统有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How do the coverage-retention conclusions change after recovery distillation (e.g., ReCal-style reweighted calibration or on-policy distillation)? Does FFN pruning's coverage collapse partially recover?
- Would a compute-matched comparison (equal active parameters or FLOPs/token, rather than equal total size) reverse the expert-vs-FFN coverage ordering?
- Do the RAZOR/EAN/FREQ solved-set identities remain identical on larger problem sets (500+ problems), or is the AIME agreement a 30-problem granularity artifact — and why does Minerva coverage *not* converge at k=2048?
- Is the "original response length predicts pruning vulnerability" signal actionable at pruning time — e.g., calibrating saliency on long-CoT samples to protect channels that long reasoning depends on?
- How do pass@k curves under pruning behave at different temperatures / with verifiers or majority voting to select among candidates, and on non-reasoning tasks?
- At what pruning ratio does the token cost of recovered coverage exceed the cost of simply running the original model at a larger k — i.e., is there any regime where a severely pruned model + heavy sampling is the economically rational choice?
