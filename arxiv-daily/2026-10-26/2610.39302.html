Why this matters

View quality substitutes for scale: same-scene positive pairs — not more tokens — are the cheaper route to multimodal alignment, and explain LLMs’ scale-only convergence with vision.

⚠ Critical edge

The “beats a 72B LLM” headline leans on LLaVA recaptions — pairing signal distilled in by a CLIP-grounded captioner. Strip them and alignment falls .107 → .060; the theory holds, the headline number is flattered.

Methodology — the proof route

The world is a finite state set; each modality observes it through an injective rendering. A JEPA minimizes a prediction loss over positive pairs plus a rotation-invariant anti-collapse term — so “do modalities agree?” reduces to “do their positive-pair kernels share a spectral embedding?”

  • Prop 1Whitened 2nd-order anti-collapse makes the JEPA optimum exactly the top-k spectral embedding of the positive-pair kernel — unique up to rotation under an eigengap.
  • Thm 1Share one positive-pair distribution (positives = two observations of one scene, “good views”) and every modality’s encoder is an orthogonal map of a single world embedding h*.
  • Thm 2–4Kernel mismatch bounds post-rotation misalignment (checked on 300 synthetic worlds); bad views force dimensions onto modality nuisance no rotation can align.
  • Prop 4Next-token prediction decomposes into a bad-view JEPA identified at best up to GL(k) — why LLMs align with vision only through scale.

In the lab: image and text encoders train fully apart (same LeJEPA objective, SIGReg λ=.02); the text side is a from-scratch BERT differing only in positive-pair definition — good views (two captions of one image) vs bad views (masked spans of one caption) vs causal next-token. Evaluation follows the PRH benchmark: WIT-1024 mutual 10-NN against frozen DINOv2 ViT-B (chance .01).

Strengths

  • Theory–experiment closed loop: every theorem has a controlled check (300 synthetic worlds; ρ-share ordering; same-encoder next-token deficit).
  • Clean attribution: good-view / bad-view / next-token encoders share architecture, corpus, objective, optimizer — only the positive pairs differ.
  • Scaling study predicts 85 runs before training, with error calibrated against seed noise — a high bar for scaling science.
  • “Theorem as tool”: a single rotation (the only pair-fitted part) yields a captioner and beats all reported pair-free zero-shot results.
  • Unusual candor: discloses the weak 4th seed (.121), recaption dependence, WIT leakage check, and where the scaling law breaks.

Limitations

  • Scale depends on LLaVA recaptioning (CLIP ViT-L/14 vision): pure alt-text drops alignment .107 → .060.
  • Idealized theory: finite world, injective rendering, population optimum; SIGReg rotational uniqueness is assumed, not proved. The paper admits no experiment exactly meets Theorem 1.
  • Image side unsolved: DINOv2/v3 are bad-view models by this definition; good image views only validated on 7k MSR-VTT clips.
  • Scaling law is one empirical recipe — exponents poorly identified, overestimates at 4× extrapolation (predicted .455 vs measured .418).
  • Seed instability: SIGReg-weight sensitive; 3/36 runs collapsed without gradient clipping.
  • Captioner .838 is not SOTA (CapDec/DeCap .912–.918); LLM baselines use last-layer representations, possibly understating them.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Can web-scale good views come without captioner distillation — video multi-frames or same-page entity views — removing the CLIP-supervision dependence?
  2. Does Prop 1’s rotational uniqueness extend to LeJEPA’s SIGReg (or VICReg / Barlow-Twins limits), so the theorem’s assumption is met by the objectives actually used?
  3. Does good-view training scale on the image side too, or is image nuisance (viewpoint, lighting) intrinsically harder to cover?
  1. Would aligning LLM middle layers, or manifold (nonlinear Procrustes) alignment, shrink the gap — testing sufficiency of the bad-view explanation?
  2. Does the nuisance mechanism predict an “alignable spectrum,” enabling truncated Procrustes on high-eigengap directions for sharper pair-free alignment?
  3. Is the q ∝ M−0.30 substitution rate conserved across modalities and objectives — and can a repeated-data term explain the 320M breakdown?
