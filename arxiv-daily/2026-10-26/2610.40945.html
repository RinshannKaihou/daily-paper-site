ℓtpolicy lag — stale async weights; grows with the trainer's own updates

One extra trainer forward at the sampler's weights θold performs the split exactly (the log of Guan et al.'s ratio factorization). The two sources need different fixes — which is why score centering survives quantization but not staleness, and PPO's clip behaves oppositely.

Bias accounting: after drift removal, residual bias decomposes into prefix / continuation / token / tail terms; the lag terms enter the next update and enlarge the next lag. The excess update norm of centering over IS fits E = aηk + c(ηk)² with measured c = 0.52 (R² = 0.987); importance weighting stays valid only while the lag ratio's effective sample size 1/Eq[ρt²] ≥ ½.

  1. Center — subtract Ct, the sampler's mean of the clipped score over its top-128 head, canceling the constant et drift.
  2. Clip — PPO-style clip on the lag ratio ρt = pθ/pθold with the sampler's weights as reference (ε = 0.2).
  3. Resync — copy trainer weights back to the sampler the moment the effective sample size crosses ½, resetting Δ, the lag, and the clip reference.

Limits: θ = θold → score centering; q = pθold → PPO with sampler reference. Cost: one extra forward at θold plus the top-128 log-probs score centering already stores.

  • ESS crossing validated: IS leaves the noise floor exactly at step 11 on 1.7B — where measured ESS falls below ½ (Gaussian prediction: step 14) — then hits 152× floor by step 30; adding resync at the same threshold cuts 115× → 2.6×.
  • Feedback loop measured: centering's residual bias grows linearly in ‖Δ‖ (R² = 0.95–0.99), reaching 2.5× gradient noise at step 40; half-learning-rate runs collapse onto the same curves in ηk.
  • FP8 sampler (0.6B): centering alone exactly matches on-policy accuracy — 46.5% vs 46.5% (0.80× floor), vs policy gradient 26.4% (12.7×) and PPO 26.2% (14.3×). A constant discrepancy needs only centering.
  • Collapse forensics (1.7B): DLC without resync collapses in 2/3 runs; the off-support share jumps to ~90% (≤14% before) while prefix-KL deviation stays at the noise floor — a failure mode invisible to per-token corrections. Full DLC keeps it ≤11% and resyncs only once per 40 steps (fixed interval would need every 11).
  • Scale & async (single runs): Qwen3-8B stale — DLC 3.1×/49.6% vs IS+centering 2.9×/51.8%, M2PO 6470×/0.0%, on-policy 55.3%; continuous asynchrony (version gap ≤4) — DLC 1.2× floor / 55.9%.

03 Strengths

  • First unified causal account of why mismatch corrections are source-dependent, with every link of the loop measured (lag growth 110–150× ‖Δ‖², quadratic coefficient c = 0.52).
  • DLC is nearly free: one extra forward at θold (PPO-with-sampler-reference already pays it) + the top-128 logprob head score centering already reports; degrades gracefully to known corrections in its two limits.
  • Adaptive, parsimonious resync: fires once per 40 steps where a fixed interval would need every 11 — directly actionable for async system design.
  • Off-support-share diagnostic catches a collapse mode that prefix-KL monitors and per-token corrections both miss (Spearman 0.80 vs 0.74 for accuracy drop below θ₀).
  • Hygiene: paired on-policy references, defined noise floors, 3 seeds, reproduced prior baselines (Marek & Ryabinin), appendices to 8B and continuous asynchrony, public code.

04 Limitations

  • Tiny envelope: 40 steps, one task (Countdown, ±1 rewards), one optimizer (SGD lr 10⁻²) — no long-horizon, verifier-based math/code RL, or AdamW/Muon evidence; the ηk-loop story may change shape there.
  • Extreme staleness: sync every 64 steps in a 40-step run means a frozen sampler, not the 1–4-step lag of production pipelines; the continuous-asynchrony appendix is a single run.
  • No uniform dominance: trails IS+centering on 0.6B (31.7% vs 32.0%) and on 8B (49.6% vs 51.8%); the clear win is the 1.7B regime where plain IS collapses.
  • Cost at scale: full weight-copy resynchronization may be expensive for multi-node samplers; top-128 head reporting doubles generation time.
  • Heuristic constants: ESS threshold ½, clip 0.2, head 128 — fixed without sensitivity ablations (measured crossing step 11 ≠ Gaussian step 14).
  • Headline metric can mislead: the deviation metric is blind to off-support collapse (demonstrated by the authors themselves) — small deviation numbers are not safety guarantees.

05 Questions for further exploration

  • Does the ηk-loop analysis — and DLC's edge over IS+centering — survive thousands of steps, AdamW/Muon optimizers, and learning-rate schedules?
  • How does the ESS threshold behave under verifier-based math/code RL with length rewards and temperature schedules — and can the off-support share serve as an online resync/early-stopping signal?
  • Can resynchronization be made incremental (weight-delta streaming, staged sync, LoRA-style updates) to amortize copy cost for 30B+ multi-node samplers?
  • Would combining DLC's discrepancy centering with sequence-level corrections (GSPO-style clipping, M2PO's second-moment bound) strictly dominate either alone, given the heavy-tailed lag distribution?
  • Does the quantized-and-stale interaction change at FP4/NVFP4 precision, where the constant discrepancy et grows large enough to bias the top-128 head estimate of the centering term?
