> **一句话总结：** TopoSteer 提出"位移云延展度"这一新几何视角来选择激活引导的注入层：对每层对比样本对的激活位移向量集合计算 total H0 持续性（即欧氏最小生成树总长）作为层得分，无需任何验证集引导实验，在 6 个 persona 引导任务上将 Qwen2.5-3B 的对齐概率从最强基线的 66.21% 提升到 79.71%（K=3，+13.5 个百分点），并在 15 组模型-预算对比中 10 组胜过 LayerNavigator。

> **TL;DR:** The paper asks WHERE to inject a mean-difference steering vector, not how to build it. It scores each layer by the total H0 persistence (Euclidean minimum-spanning-tree length) of the cloud of per-example activation displacements, arguing that displacement spread — not class separability — predicts steering success. On six binary persona tasks from the Anthropic Persona Dataset across Llama-3-3B/8B and Qwen2.5-3B/7B/32B Instruct, it beats the strongest separability-based selector LayerNavigator in 10 of 15 model-budget settings (1 tie, 4 losses), with the largest gains on Qwen2.5-3B: +7.5/+13.5/+13.5 points at K=1/2/3 (e.g., 66.21%→79.71% aligned-answer probability at K=3), at near-zero MMLU cost and half the runtime of the evaluation-based W2S-Multi (8.4 vs 15.9 min), though W2S-Multi still achieves higher absolute alignment (81.25% vs 78.90%). A theorem proves the new score and Fisher-type discriminability move in strictly opposite directions along an example-level-variation axis, explaining when and why the two selectors disagree.

## 核心贡献

- **位移云（displacement cloud）新视角**：均值差（MD）引导实际注入的是所有逐样本位移 δᵢ = a(x⁺ᵢ) − a(x⁻ᵢ) 的平均，因此选层应该考察这些位移构成的点云的"延展度"（extent），而不是两个激活点云的类别可分性（discriminability）或位移方向的一致性（consistency）——后两者恰恰忽略了 MD 真正平均的对象。
- **TopoSteer 评分**：对每层的标准化位移云计算 total H₀ 持续性，经典地等价于欧氏最小生成树（MST）总边长；选取得分最高的 K 层并注入原始 MD 向量。无需验证集引导实验、无需辅助训练、无额外前向传播，离线几何计算代价仅 O(N²d)/层。
- **理论刻画**：Corollary 1 证明评分对位移云的公共均值不变（只看相对排布）；Proposition 1 证明类别分离度仅通过标准化以除子形式进入评分；Theorem 1 证明沿"样本级变化缩放"单参数族，H₀ 持续性严格递增而判别度严格递减——两个准则可以给出完全相反的层排序。
- **失效模式诊断**：bridge share（最长 MST 边占总长的比例）区分得分来自整体延展还是单条长桥；答案极性轴分析揭示对比数据格式（Yes/No 回答）会在每个位移云中诱发一个与目标行为无关、携带零类别信号的分裂轴（所有 32 层均 350/350 完美二分）。
- **系统实证**：5 个指令模型 × 6 个 persona 任务 × K∈{1,2,3} × 同时/顺序两种注入协议，从对齐概率、生成困惑度、MMLU 三个方面评估，并与免验证（LayerNavigator、Disc、Random）和基于评估的（W2S-Multi）选层器对比。

## Key Contributions

- **Reframes the layer-selection problem.** Prior validation-free selectors score how *separable* the two activation classes are at a layer (LayerNavigator's Fisher-type discriminability D) or how *consistently* individual displacements align with the steering direction (cosine-based C). The paper argues what matters is the *extent* of the displacement cloud Δ = {δᵢ} that mean-difference steering actually averages — a quantity neither existing term captures.
- **TopoSteer score.** Score each layer by total degree-zero persistence of its standardized displacement cloud, computed as the Euclidean MST length (Gower–Ross identification of H₀ death scales with MST edge weights). Select the top-K layers; inject the raw (unstandardized) MD vectors. Requires no held-out steering runs, no selector training, and no forward passes beyond those already needed to extract the MD vectors; extra offline cost is O(N²d) per layer.
- **Exact geometric theory.** (i) Common-mean invariance: the score ignores the mean displacement entirely (Corollary 1). (ii) Class separation enters only through standardization, as a divisor (Proposition 1). (iii) Theorem 1 (separability–connectivity decoupling): along the family Δ(σ) = {μ_δ + σξᵢ}, total persistence is strictly increasing in σ while discriminability D(σ) = B/(B + σ²W) is strictly decreasing — the two criteria induce opposite orderings, which explains their empirical disagreements.
- **Failure-mode diagnostics.** A bridge-share statistic (longest MST edge / total length) flags when a high score reflects a single bridging edge (mode split or outliers) rather than bulk displacement spread. An answer-polarity analysis shows the Yes/No contrastive format induces an axis that splits every displacement cloud 350/350 (same partition at all 32 layers) yet carries essentially zero class signal (D ≈ 0 along it) — a data artifact both selectors should ignore but respond to differently.
- **Two intervention protocols.** Simultaneous steering (all K layers from one extraction pass, as prior selectors assume) and greedy sequential steering (re-extracting through the already-steered model at each round), showing the ranking of selectors is protocol- and model-dependent.

## Methodology

1. **Displacement cloud construction.** For paired contrastive examples {(x⁺ᵢ, x⁻ᵢ)}, residual-stream activations aˡ(x) are taken at the final prompt token of every layer and standardized coordinate-wise (mean/σ over all 2N activations at that layer). The displacement cloud is Δˡ = {δᵢˡ = ãˡ(x⁺ᵢ) − ãˡ(x⁻ᵢ)}; each displacement decomposes as δᵢˡ = μ_δ,ˡ + ξᵢˡ where μ_δ is the (standardized) mean-difference direction and ξᵢ are zero-mean residuals.
2. **Layer score.** sˡ = Σ_{e∈MST(Δˡ)} w(e) — the total H₀ persistence of the cloud, computed exactly as its Euclidean minimum-spanning-tree length. The score is 1-homogeneous and invariant to the cloud's common mean (it equals L(Ξˡ) of the residual cloud). Standardization couples it to class separation only as a divisor: L(Δˡ) = L(HˡΛˡ) with Λˡ = diag((1+ρₖ)^(−1/2)), ρₖ the per-coordinate separation-to-noise ratio.
3. **Selection and steering.** Pick the K highest-scoring layers; at inference inject α·vˡ_raw (the raw MD vector) at those layers (α = 1.0 in all experiments). The standardized cloud is used only for scoring, so TopoSteer changes *only* the layer-selection rule of an otherwise standard MD-steering pipeline.
4. **Diagnostics.** Bridge share bˡ = max-edge-weight / sˡ flags single-edge-dominated scores. The answer-polarity axis tˡ (half-difference of the Yes- vs No-matching displacement means) is estimated and projected out as a diagnostic; the paper proves discriminability improves under this projection iff the scatter reduction λ exceeds the squared alignment ε², whereas connectivity can only decrease (orthogonal projection cannot increase MST length).
5. **Protocols and setup.** Simultaneous vs greedy-sequential selection with re-extraction. Experiments: six binary persona tasks from the Anthropic Persona Dataset (Alliance Building, Conscientiousness, Self-Improvement, Self-Aware, Impact, Religion), ~700 pairs for extraction, 100 held-out examples for evaluation, five instruction-tuned models (Llama-3-3B/8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-3B/7B/32B-Instruct), K ∈ {1,2,3}. Implementation builds on the LayerNavigator codebase.

## Results & Highlights

- **Head-to-head vs LayerNavigator (Table 1, simultaneous steering, aligned-answer probability averaged over the six persona tasks):** TopoSteer outperforms LayerNavigator in **10 of 15 model–budget comparisons** (1 tie, 4 losses).
- **Largest gains on Qwen2.5-3B:** alignment improves from the strongest alternative selector's 64.30% → **71.77%** at K=1 (+7.47), 64.16% → **77.63%** at K=2 (+13.47), and 66.21% → **79.71%** at K=3 (+13.50 points). On Llama-3-8B, TopoSteer gains +2.18 (K=1) and +1.22 (K=2) points over LayerNavigator.
- **Layer choice can dominate everything (Figure 3):** on Qwen2.5-3B conscientiousness at K=3, LayerNavigator/Disc select layers 21–23 while TopoSteer-ℓ₂ selects layers 33–35, moving aligned-answer probability from **51.58% (LayerNavigator) to 84.86% (TopoSteer-ℓ₂)** — a +33.3-point swing — with the unsteered base at 70.20% and random selection at 68.96%.
- **Cost/quality trade-off vs evaluation-based selection (Table 2, averaged over tasks and K∈{1,2,3}):** TopoSteer reaches 78.90% alignment at 13.78 perplexity in **8.4 minutes** total, while W2S-Multi reaches higher alignment 81.25% (+2.35 points; e.g., +6.30 on Qwen2.5-3B, +3.17 on Qwen2.5-7B) at 14.53 perplexity but needs **15.9 minutes** (~1.9×) because it must evaluate candidate layers on labelled validation prompts.
- **Downstream capability preserved (MMLU, Figure 2):** accuracy changes are small for all selectors; TopoSteer averages +0.02/−0.03 on Qwen2.5-7B and −0.09/−0.09 on Qwen2.5-32B across variants, with the worst observed change ≈ −1.31 points (Llama-3-8B, one variant at one K).
- **Sequential protocol:** results are model-dependent — Llama-3-3B at K=3 favors TopoSteer (78.13 vs 75.17 for LayerNavigator), Llama-3-8B at K=3 slightly favors TopoSteer (85.58 vs 85.24), while on Qwen2.5-3B only the unit-sphere variant stays ahead (76.72 at K=2, 79.19 at K=3).
- **Answer-polarity artifact quantified:** the Yes/No axis splits every displacement cloud into exactly 350/350 lobes with the identical partition at all 32 layers, while discriminability along that axis is near zero across all six tasks — the axis is a format artifact, not behavior.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty (good):** The reframing from "which layer best separates the classes" to "how spread-out is the cloud MD actually averages" is genuinely new, and Theorem 1 gives a crisp, provable explanation of when separability-based selection should fail — rare intellectual honesty in this literature. Persistent homology is used as a validation-free geometric diagnostic rather than a classifier, which is a fresh application.
- **Rigor (mixed):** The theory is stated with exact invariances and proved in appendices, and the paper voluntarily reports losing 4 of 15 comparisons. But there are no error bars, no seed variance, and a single steering strength (α = 1.0); the headline metric is aligned-answer probability on binary Yes/No persona items — a narrow behavioral proxy with an acknowledged answer-polarity artifact. No refusal, honesty, or reasoning tasks are evaluated despite those being the flagship use-cases of the cited steering literature (Rimsky et al., Arditi et al.).
- **Reproducibility (decent, one gap):** Public models, a public dataset (Anthropic Persona), a deterministic score (MST length), and an implementation built on the LayerNavigator codebase. However, no explicit code/data release URL is given in the paper itself.
- **Significance (model-dependent):** The +7.5 to +13.5-point gains are concentrated on Qwen2.5-3B; on Qwen2.5-7B/32B — where the unsteered base is already at 85.24%/82.68% — gains are sub-point and mixed, and the evaluation-based W2S-Multi still achieves the best absolute alignment (81.25% vs 78.90%). TopoSteer's real selling point is cost (no held-out steering evaluations, ~2× faster than W2S-Multi), not peak performance.
- **Red flags:** (a) Variant sensitivity is unresolved — on Qwen2.5-3B at K=1 the standardized-cloud variant scores 61.12%, *below the unsteered base of 64.26%*, while the unit-sphere variant wins; the paper reports both but provides no rule for choosing. (b) The bridge-share diagnostic exists precisely because the score can be dominated by a single long MST edge (i.e., measure separation between modes rather than within-mode spread) — a known failure mode patched by a flag, not solved by the score. (c) Theorem 1 concerns a one-parameter family at a *fixed* layer; the authors themselves concede its across-layer implications remain empirical. (d) Multi-layer steering degrades perplexity on some models, and increasing K does not monotonically help.

## Strengths

- A principled, training-free, validation-free layer selector: the score reuses the activations already cached for MD extraction, adding only an O(N²d) offline geometric computation per layer.
- Theory and experiments are tightly coupled: the decoupling theorem predicts exactly the disagreement regime observed, and the 2-D controlled construction (Figure 1b) makes the geometric intuition verifiable by hand.
- unusually honest reporting of losses (4/15 vs LayerNavigator) and of the W2S-Multi comparison where a stronger method wins on accuracy at higher cost.
- The answer-polarity analysis is a valuable standalone contribution — it exposes a Yes/No format artifact that any future contrastive-pair steering study should check for (perfect 350/350 lobe split at all 32 layers with zero class signal).
- Comprehensive appendices: per-task tables, ablations over topological descriptor, filtration distance, and layer budget, stability proofs, and a separation-free variant of the score.

## Limitations

- Assumes matched positive/negative contrastive pairs and covers only mean-difference steering; other extractors (contrastive activation addition, refusal-direction methods) are left to future work.
- Evaluation is restricted to six binary persona tasks from a single dataset (Anthropic Persona) with ~100 held-out examples per task, using aligned-answer probability as the sole behavioral metric; no open-ended generation quality or safety-relevant behaviors.
- Gains are strongly model-dependent: near-ceiling models (Qwen2.5-7B at 85.24% base, Qwen2.5-32B at 82.68% base) show little headroom and mixed results.
- The choice between the standardized and unit-sphere scoring variants is empirical and inconsistent across models; the standardized variant can even fall below the unsteered baseline (61.12 vs 64.26 on Qwen2.5-3B, K=1).
- Theorem 1's guarantees apply to a fixed layer and the discriminability term alone; guarantees can miss mode splits concentrated in a few coordinates (Appendix Q), and multi-layer steering can degrade perplexity.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the displacement-cloud score transfer to other steering-vector extractors (CAA, refusal directions) and to behaviors beyond binary personas — refusal, honesty, chain-of-thought reasoning — where layer choice is also known to matter?
- Why does the standardized variant collapse below the unsteered baseline on Qwen2.5-3B at K=1 (61.12 vs 64.26) while the unit-sphere variant wins? Is there a principled, model-independent rule for choosing the scoring cloud?
- Can the bridge-share diagnostic be upgraded into a corrected score (e.g., trimmed MST, mode-aware persistence, or higher-degree persistence terms) instead of a post-hoc flag?
- How does selection quality interact with steering strength α ≠ 1.0 and larger budgets K > 3, especially given that perplexity degrades on some models as K grows?
- Would projecting out the answer-polarity axis at extraction time (not just as a diagnostic) improve MD steering or layer selection on datasets with skewed Yes/No formats?
