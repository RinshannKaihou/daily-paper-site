Adjacent-layer “cancellation” is not selective correction — gated MLPs contract everything they read, via closed-gate leakage.

Key Metric — causal annihilation of cancellation

−0.184 → +0.021

Qwen2.5-0.5B window cosine before → after annealing the gate's negative region away (249M-token continued pretraining, 3 seeds). Matched control stays −0.170; damping D collapses 0.336 → −0.002. Cost: +0.165 nats.

Why this matters

Feature-tracing, steering, and transcoder analyses must subtract this contraction first — otherwise architectural damping gets misread as model “content.”

⚠ Critical edge

The contraction is functional, not free: removing the closed side costs 0.165–0.341 nats, and selectivity is refuted at the direction level only — feature-level rejection remains untested.

01 · Methodology — cancellation as accounting

The paper splits what layer j does along the previous layer's write v into three exact counterfactual terms:

⟨v̂, cj⟩ / ∥v∥ = + −  (cancels ⟺ D > A + C)

  • AAttention — layer j's attention write along v.
  • CCo-writing — what the MLP still writes along v when v is deleted from its input.
  • DDamping — how much less the MLP writes along v because v is present.

An exact split through the RMSNorm Jacobian traces the damping to one term: the negative lobe of SiLU/GELU. Below zero the activation is slightly negative, so a “closed” gate leaks a small negative multiple of its input — aligned (strengthening) neurons subtract a fraction of whatever direction they read. That lobe term alone carries ≥59% of average damping in all ten gated models.

Measured on 16 models (10 gated, 2 ReLU conversions, 4 non-gated) × 16 FineWeb-Edu documents (192 tokens), replicated on WikiText-103 and 2,048-token contexts; controlled-cosine protocol pre-registered, controls fixed before measurement. Interventions: annealed vs matched-control continued pretraining, 51M from-scratch λ-grid, inference-time removal, CATS 50% sparsity.

Random open-entry zeroing (control) leaves damping at 0.98–1.02× — only touching the closed side moves it.

  • The previous write is not singled out. The same layer's write at another token is damped as much or more (ratio 0.88–0.99); along the controlled write, damping is within 9% of a random direction in 9/10 gated models; the “selective” eigenvalue signature appears for other-token writes too (41%/16%).
  • Training dynamics (OLMo-1B, 2B → 3T tokens). Damping grows 0.004 → 0.304 while co-writing stays flat (0.120–0.175); the adjacent cosine turns negative at 33–64B tokens — cancellation begins when damping overtakes co-writing.
  • Reproduction honesty. Re-running Patrawala et al.'s released protocol gives −0.123 (vs ≈−0.2 reported); disclosed rather than buried.

03 · Strengths

  • Exact, checkable identity (Eq. 1) — not another correlational observation; every step validated (linearity checks, alternative counterfactuals, cross-corpus replication).
  • Analytic neuron split matches finite differences to ~0.0002 median error over 376 layers — rare rigor for mech-interp.
  • End-to-end causal confirmation: annealed vs control arms trained on identical batches from identical weights.
  • Fair engagement with prior work — reuses its protocol, reproduces its eigenvalue analysis, reports where its headline number fails to replicate.
  • Practical implications spelled out for feature tracing, steering, transcoders, and activation design.

04 · Limitations

  • Scale capped at 8B; gpt-oss-20b (measured 4-bit) does not cancel at all — the MoE-at-scale picture may differ.
  • Headline numbers rest on sixteen 192-token documents from one corpus (mitigated, not eliminated, by 64-doc and WikiText-103 checks).
  • Selectivity refuted at the direction level only; feature-level selectivity (via dictionaries) left open by the authors.
  • From-scratch runs never develop cancellation; the strongest causal claim leans on two continued-pretraining models, one single-seed.
  • ReLU conversions confound the activation change with distillation, instruction data, and sparsity regularisation — ~11% retention is illustrative, not clean.

05 · Questions for further exploration

  1. Does feature-level selectivity survive once the contraction is subtracted — e.g., re-run the damping analysis on sparse-autoencoder feature directions?
  2. Why don't the MoE models (OLMoE-1B-7B, gpt-oss-20b) cancel — is sparse routing intrinsically less contractive, or a 4-bit/expert-width artifact?
  3. Does the 0.165–0.341-nat cost of removing the closed side shrink at scale, or does the lobe-term contraction become more functional as models grow?
  4. Could a trainable negative branch or shifted activation keep the lobe's expressivity while decoupling it from the damping?
  5. Do methods that read “the next layer corrects the previous one” (e.g., attribution patching) change conclusions once A / C / D are separated?
