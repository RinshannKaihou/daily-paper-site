Why This Matters

First matched-bound theory for where, when, and how many bits mixed-precision training needs — directly complementing FP8/FP4 LLM pretraining practice.

Critical Edge

The theory covers a linear frozen-random-features regime with only two feature copies rounded — far from FP8/FP4 pretraining, where weights, gradients, and optimizer states are all quantized.

Fig. — rounding enters at exactly two points; evaluation is always exact, so Δ = RSR − Rexact isolates rounding damage.

  • Model. Two-layer linear network, lazy regime: first layer S frozen (i.i.d. Gaussian), only output weights v train. Data: exponents 1 < b < a.
  • Rounding. Stochastic rounding is unbiased with variance ≤ ¼·4−px²; forward and backward copies rounded independently, possibly at different per-step precisions.
  • Proofs. Second-moment recursions with kernel propagators K₁, K₂ give matched ≍ upper + lower bounds (prob. ≥ 1 − 6e−cM − KDM−D) for arbitrary pre-committed LR and precision schedules.
  • Optimization. Compute cost BM·c(p), c(p) linear in mantissa bits; variational optimization over (M, τ, η(t), p(t)) yields the Table-1 laws.
  • Validation. Direct sims (widths 16–4096), data-reuse variants, a 30M-param transformer on 200M FineWeb-Edu tokens, and a CIFAR-10 MLP probe choices beyond the theory.

  • Averaging flips the bit budget. With 5 total mantissa bits, the best split is (pfw, pbw) = (4, 1) for the last iterate but (2, 3) for the uniform average.
  • Bits must grow with width. Keeping SR-induced risk ≤ 1% of exact needs ≥ 2 bits at M = 128 but 3 at M = 256; theory forces backward bits ≥ (b−1)/2·log₂M − O(1). Log-M growth recovers (F/log F)−(b−1)/(a+1) — only a polylog worse than exact.
  • Precision couples with the LR schedule. When the rounding correction dominates, the time-decay exponent shifts from 2a−1 to (a−1)/(a+1); empirically the SR-aware schedule was slightly worse than the exact-SGD one at 1 bit (Δ risk 10⁻⁵–10⁻⁴).
  • Beyond the theory (Fig. 5). On the 30M transformer, combined 2-bit rounding hurts vs. activation-only; sharing one rounded copy beats independent rounding at 1 bit; CIFAR-10 MLP: test loss 1.624 with SR off vs. 1.672 on — low-precision training ≠ low-precision inference.
Strengths

What holds up

  • Matched upper and lower bounds with explicit high-probability guarantees — rare in the low-precision literature.
  • Forward / backward / joint (qfw·qbw) decomposition makes every downstream rule auditable.
  • One theorem covers arbitrary pre-committed LR + precision schedules, then specializes cleanly (Thm 1 → Table 1 → design rules).
  • Counterintuitive but usable rules: averaging flips precision allocation; low precision can beat exact at matched compute; disable SR at test when comparing.
  • Honest boundary-drawing — neural runs labeled "beyond the theory," deviations flagged, AI assistance disclosed.
Limitations

Where it stops

  • Linear lazy regime: frozen random first layer, no feature learning; only the two feature copies are rounded — weights, gradients, optimizer states stay exact.
  • Fresh i.i.d. Gaussian data with power-law spectra; data reuse handled empirically only.
  • Stylized linear-in-bits cost model c(p); no hardware validation, no SR overhead; real formats are discrete steps (FP32/16/8/4).
  • Compute-optimality is asymptotic in F — exponents alone cannot rank configs at a fixed budget (the paper concedes this).
  • Neural runs are sanity probes, not scaling tests; Fig. 3(b) sims deliberately exceed τT = Ma, exhibiting F−1/6-like scaling, not the predicted F−1/8.
Questions for Further Exploration

Open threads

  1. Do the conclusions survive shared (non-independent) forward/backward rounding — the norm in real quantized pipelines? Sharing already helps at 1 bit in practice.
  2. Does the averaging-induced precision reversal persist under EMA, AdamW, or transformer pretraining, where the last iterate is the norm?
  3. Can the (b−1)/2·log₂M backward-bit law be verified at LLM scale (width-dependent FP8-vs-BF16 degradation)?
  4. What changes with multi-epoch data reuse — Fig. 4(c) hints reuse lowers risk, but no SR-aware reuse law exists.
  5. What happens in the feature-learning regime, where the kernel propagators K₁, K₂ are no longer frozen?
  6. Can the SR-aware LR schedule (Eq. 31) win at FP4-level precision, given it was slightly worse at 1 bit?
