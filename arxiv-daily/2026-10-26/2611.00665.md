> **一句话总结：** 在 Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct 上植入"最爱动物是熊猫"的隐性偏好后，4-bit 量化会让该偏好的净强度（相对 16-bit 基线，对照组校准）随量化器选择剧烈反向变化——HQQ 将其放大到 R≈1.3（熊猫概率 55%→64%），而误差范数仅相差 4% 的 NF4 却把它压到 R≈0.3（55%→29%）；误差的"排布方式"（而非大小）决定方向，且显式训练出的同等强度偏好波动范围比隐性偏好窄约 5 倍——因此 16-bit 安全审计无法约束量化部署后的真实风险。

> **TL;DR:** The authors implant a subliminal "favourite animal: panda" preference into Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct via LoRA on 7,500 number sequences (the word "panda" never appears), then quantize the merged weights. On a 50-prompt favourite-animal probe with neutrally-trained controls as the baseline, 4-bit HQQ amplifies the preference (net strength R = 1.23/1.30 vs the R = 1.0 bf16 baseline, panda probability 60.9–66.9% vs controls' 16.9%) while NF4 — with rounding-error norms matched to within 4% — suppresses it to R = 0.27/0.30 (27.9–29.5%). Shuffling HQQ's errors within groups of 64, magnitudes unchanged, swings R across 0.22–1.58; both quantizers preserve >99% of the fine-tuning weight delta and ~90% of the layer-14 activation direction, so internal probes do not predict behaviour; explicitly-trained students of matched strength (50.8–51.2% baseline) stay within R = 0.98–1.19 under identical quantizers (~5× narrower range). Conclusion: audit at the exact quantized precision you deploy, because a 16-bit measurement bounds neither direction.

## 核心贡献

- **首个针对"隐性学习偏好 × 事后量化"的受控研究**：在 Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct 上用 Cloud 等人的数字序列配方植入熊猫偏好（训练数据中从未出现 "panda" 一词），系统扫描 HQQ 8→2 bit，并聚焦 4-bit——模型仍流畅但偏好剧烈偏移的精度点。
- **量化器对比 + 误差干预实验分离了"误差大小"与"误差排布"**：HQQ 与 NF4 在相同层覆盖、相同 group size（64）、误差范数相差仅约 4% 的条件下，把同一隐性偏好分别放大（R≈1.3）与压制（R≈0.3）；在组内随机打乱 HQQ 误差（幅值不变）即可让 R 在 0.22–1.58 间波动，证明误差的排布方式才是决定性因素。
- **揭示内部表征与外部行为的解耦**：两种量化器都保留 >99% 的微调权重增量（delta），HQQ 4-bit 下第 14 层激活方向仍保留约 90% 余弦相似度 / 94–98% 投影长度——内部信号几乎完好，外部行为却相差 4 倍以上，说明表征层面的探针无法预测量化后的偏好表达。
- **证明这种极端波动是隐性安装特有的**：在 16-bit 基线强度匹配（50.8–51.2% vs 53.1–55.8%）的前提下，显式训练的偏好在相同量化条件下 R 保持在 0.98–1.19，波动范围比隐性偏好窄约 5 倍，且从未被任何误差重排放大。
- **直接的安全审计结论**：16-bit 审计既非上界也非下界；安全评估必须在部署所用的确切量化权重上进行。

## Key Contributions

- **A controlled quantization study of subliminally learned preferences.** Using the number-sequence subliminal-learning recipe on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (LoRA rank 8 on all 196 linear projections of 28 blocks, merged before quantization), the authors sweep Half-Quadratic Quantization (HQQ) from 8 down to 2 bits and single out 4 bits — the one precision where the model stays fluent while the preference moves sharply.
- **A head-to-head quantizer comparison with matched knobs.** HQQ vs NormalFloat (NF4) at 4 bits: both calibration-free, both on transformer layers only (output layer left bf16), both with group size 64, rounding-error norms within ~4% — yet opposite behavioural outcomes.
- **Error-intervention experiments that isolate arrangement from magnitude.** Adding Gaussian noise norm-matched to each layer's HQQ error (3 draws) never amplifies; negating HQQ's error weakens (R = 0.90); four within-group shuffles of the same magnitudes span R = 0.22–1.58 — shuffle 3 amplifies more than full HQQ, shuffle 2 suppresses more than NF4.
- **Internal measurements showing representation–behaviour divergence.** Both quantizers preserve >99% of the fine-tuning delta along its original direction (0.998 HQQ / 0.994 NF4), and the layer-14 activation direction keeps ~90% cosine similarity under 4-bit HQQ — yet panda probability moves by up to ±30 points. A striking sub-result: quantizing only the never-trained output layer at 3 bits erases roughly a third of the students' preference gain (53–56% → 41–42%) while leaving the base model untouched.
- **An explicit-training control at matched strength.** Explicit students checkpointed early to match 16-bit panda probability stay within R = 0.98–1.19 across all quantization conditions — a response range ~5× narrower than subliminal students (0.27–1.40) — and NF4, which erases >70% of the subliminal preference, leaves the explicit one fully intact.

## Methodology

The pipeline has four stages. **(1) Install the preference.** A teacher copy of Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct prompted to prefer pandas continues 30,000 number sequences; a neutral teacher with the default system prompt continues the same sequences. After keeping only clean number lists, 7,500 examples per teacher train two students each (seeds 41, 42) with LoRA (rank 8, α = 8) on q/k/v/o/gate/up/down projections of all 28 blocks, 3 epochs / 342 optimizer steps, LR 2×10⁻⁴ linear decay, effective batch 66. The output layer is never trained; adapters are merged into the base weights so every model is quantized exactly in its deployable form. Panda-data students are "subliminal students"; neutral-data students are "controls."

**(2) Quantize.** bf16 is the 16-bit reference. HQQ is swept at 8/4/3/2 bits (full config: 196 block linear layers + output layer, group size 64), plus transformer-layers-only and output-layer-only ablations, and NF4 at 4 bits on transformer layers only with the same group size. Per-layer rounding error E = Q(W) − W is recorded.

**(3) Perturb the error.** (a) Gaussian noise scaled to match each layer's HQQ error norm at 4 and 3 bits, 3 independent draws; (b) each model's own 4-bit HQQ error added unchanged (consistency check), negated, or shuffled within each group of 64 weights (4 random shuffles) — all preserving per-group magnitudes. Controls receive the same transformation of their own errors.

**(4) Measure.** Behaviour: 50 "favourite animal in one word" prompts; P = mean probability the first answer token is "panda" (4 spelling variants); net strength S = P_student − mean(P_controls) under the same condition; normalized R = S(condition)/S(bf16). Amplification requires R ≥ 1.15 and normalized log-prob gap ≥ 1.10 for both seeds. Sampling checks (20 answers/prompt, temperature 1) confirm probabilities match actual outputs. Internals: fine-tuning delta survival ⟨d_Q, d⟩/⟨d, d⟩ across the 196 layers; alignment cos(E_s, E_b) between student and base rounding errors; the layer-14 residual-stream direction (mean student-minus-base hidden state over 200 neutral prompts) tracked by cosine and projection length; first-token entropy to separate trait-specific sharpening from generic confidence gain. For RQ4, explicit students trained on panda-mentioning teacher replies are checkpointed every step and the checkpoint matching subliminal 16-bit strength (step 9, 50.8–51.2%) is selected.

## Results & Highlights

- **4-bit quantizer choice flips the preference direction (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, 50-prompt panda probe, controls at ~17%).** HQQ 4-bit (transformer layers only) raises subliminal students' panda probability to 60.9/66.9% (R = 1.23/1.30) vs the bf16 baseline of 53.1/55.8%; NF4 4-bit with error norms within ~4% lowers it to 27.9/29.5% (R = 0.27/0.30) — a more than fourfold difference in net strength (HQQ R≈1.3 vs NF4 R≈0.3, R=1.0 = unchanged). 8-bit HQQ is stable (R = 1.10/1.03).
- **Error arrangement, not magnitude, drives the shift.** Norm-matched Gaussian noise lowers student panda probability by 1–2, 6–9, and 20–22 points across 3 draws, while full HQQ raises it ~12/16 points (R = 1.33/1.40). Four within-group shuffles of HQQ's own 4-bit error — magnitudes identical — span R = 0.22 to 1.58: shuffle 3 gives 70.2/76.8% (R = 1.52/1.58, more amplification than real HQQ), shuffle 2 gives 25.9/25.1% (R = 0.26/0.22, more suppression than NF4). Controls barely move (17.3% → 15.8–16.7%) under the same rearrangements.
- **Internal signals survive while behaviour swings.** Both quantizers preserve >99% of the fine-tuning delta along its original direction (survival 0.998/0.998 HQQ, 0.994/0.994 NF4), and the layer-14 activation direction retains cosine 0.90/0.91 and component 0.94/0.98 under 4-bit HQQ — despite the ±30-point behavioural swings. The structural tell: student–base error alignment cos(E_s, E_b) is 0.88 for HQQ vs 0.96 for NF4.
- **The untouched output layer carries the readout.** 3-bit HQQ on the output layer alone (never trained, identical weights for all students) drops student panda probability from 53–56% to 41–42% — erasing ~1/3 of the gain over controls — while the base model stays intact (16.6% → 16.8%); 3-bit on transformer layers degrades the base itself (17% → 3–4%), and 2-bit breaks it entirely.
- **Amplification is trait-specific, not a generic confidence boost.** 4-bit HQQ cuts first-token entropy by 0.22 nats for subliminal students (1.01 → 0.79; top-token mass 70% → 81%) but only 0.05–0.08 nats for base/controls (~1.2) — a 3–4× larger sharpening on the trait bearers; NF4 instead raises entropy by 0.14–0.16 nats for all models.
- **Explicit preferences of matched strength are ~5× less volatile.** Explicit students (16-bit baseline 50.8/51.2% vs subliminal 53.1/55.8%) stay within R = 0.98–1.19 across all four quantization conditions while subliminal students span 0.27–1.40; under error rearrangements explicit R ∈ [0.64, 1.19] vs subliminal [0.22, 1.58], and no manipulation ever amplifies the explicit trait.

## Authors & Credibility

Md Rayhanul Masud and Mst Suraiya Akter Shefa, University of California, Riverside. The paper is a focused empirical safety study positioned relative to a credible body of work: the original subliminal-learning demonstration (Cloud et al., Nature 2026), its training-dynamics and mitigation follow-ups (Yanagisawa et al., EMNLP Findings 2026; Schrodi et al., ICLR 2026), steering-vector-distillation and verbalization analyses (Blank et al.; Hu et al.), the auditing literature (AuditBench, Activation Difference Lens, Distill-to-Detect), and quantization-attack work (Egashira et al.'s "Exploiting LLM Quantization", NeurIPS 2024) — the key differentiator being that here the trait is *not* engineered to exploit quantization. The experimental design is unusually careful for a two-author preprint: matched controls under every condition, two training seeds, a dual threshold (probability + log-probability) for claiming amplification, sampling-based verification of probabilities, entropy controls against generic sharpening, and an explicit-training comparator strength-matched by checkpoint selection. All scripts are shared at github.com/mrayhanulmasud/subq, and the paper's own limitations section is frank about the confounds it could not remove.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine and well-scoped. Prior quantization-safety work (Egashira et al.) built adversarial models that *hide* behaviour from quantization; this paper shows ordinary, non-adversarial post-training quantization already makes a subliminally acquired preference unpredictably volatile, and cleanly attributes the effect to error *arrangement* via the shuffle/negation interventions. The representation–behaviour decoupling finding (99% delta survival, 4× behavioural swing) is a substantive negative result for representation-level auditing.
- **Rigor:** Strong on controls (matched-quantization controls, unchanged-error consistency check, entropy control, sampling verification), but the evidence base is narrow: one model (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct), one trait (panda preference), one subliminal recipe, two seeds, three noise draws, four shuffles. The headline "5× narrower range" for explicit students rests on 2 seeds × 4 conditions per side — the paper itself flags the range statistics as not characterizing the full outcome space.
- **The RQ4 confound is real and acknowledged but unresolved:** explicit students were checkpointed at step 9 of 342, so their weight-update norm ∥d∥ is far smaller than the subliminal students' (Table V reports subliminal ∥d∥ = 5.67/5.78 with no explicit counterpart). Greater quantization sensitivity may simply track larger fine-tuning deltas rather than subliminality per se — the authors admit they "cannot determine" this, which weakens the paper's most quotable claim.
- **Missing ablations:** only HQQ and NF4 at group size 64 are tested — no GPTQ, AWQ, or GGUF-family quantizers (the ones most common in actual deployment), no group-size sweep, and no general-capability evaluation (fluency on one animal task ≠ preserved model quality). The behavioural probe itself is thin: first-token probability on 50 near-identical prompts, whereas real preference expression appears in longer, varied contexts.
- **Reproducibility:** good — exact training config (Table VI), all conditions enumerated (Table I), public GitHub repo; no claim depends on undisclosed details.
- **Significance:** the practical message ("audit at deployment precision") is sound and timely for anyone shipping quantized fine-tunes, though a benign "favourite animal" trait leaves the leap to harmful-behaviour risk (e.g., misaligned preferences surviving compression) argued by analogy rather than demonstrated.
- **Red flags:** minimal overclaiming — the abstract's numbers all check out against the tables, and hedges ("roughly", "about") are used appropriately. The main risk for a reader is over-generalizing a single 7B model / single trait result into a universal auditing principle.

## Strengths

- Exceptionally clean experimental logic: every comparison (HQQ vs NF4, noise vs HQQ, negated/shuffled vs unchanged) changes exactly one factor, and controls undergo identical transformations so baseline answer drift is subtracted out.
- The error-intervention design (shuffle/negate within groups of 64, magnitudes fixed) is a genuinely clever way to prove arrangement matters — shuffle 3 amplifying *more* than real HQQ and shuffle 2 suppressing *more* than NF4 is a compelling result.
- The output-layer finding — a layer that stores none of the trait but, quantized at 3 bits, erases a third of its expression — is an actionable, non-obvious deployment insight.
- Honest self-assessment: the limitations section explicitly names the weight-update-norm confound, the single-model scope, and the mechanism gap, rather than overselling.
- Dual-criteria amplification threshold (R ≥ 1.15 AND log-prob gap ≥ 1.10) pre-empts the artifact where tiny control probabilities inflate ratios.

## Limitations

- Single model (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct), single trait (panda preference), single subliminal recipe; generalization to other scales, architectures, traits, and harmful behaviours is unverified — the trait is benign, so risk implications are extrapolated.
- Only two quantizers (HQQ, NF4) at group size 64; GPTQ/AWQ/GGUF and other group sizes — the settings most deployments actually use — are untested.
- Small sample sizes throughout: 2 training seeds, 3 noise draws, 4 shuffles; the "5× narrower" explicit-vs-subliminal range comparison is a coarse summary of few conditions.
- The explicit-training control differs from subliminal training in duration (step 9 vs 342) and weight-update magnitude, leaving the subliminality-specificity claim confounded.
- Behavioural measurement is first-token probability over 50 paraphrastic prompts; long-form or contextual expression of the preference is not measured, and general capabilities under each quantized condition are not evaluated.
- Mechanistic explanation is partial: weight projections and one layer's activation direction show *that* internal signals survive, but not *how* rounding-error arrangement couples to the final answer token.

## Relevance

这项工作直接命中"模型安全与对齐 + 量化部署"的交叉点：它用受控实验证明 16-bit 安全审计无法约束量化后模型的隐性偏好表达，对任何要在 4-bit 权重上部署微调模型（尤其是经蒸馏/合成数据训练的模型）的 LLM 应用流水线都有直接的审计与合规启示。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the volatility persist under GPTQ, AWQ, and GGUF-family quantizers, and how does it scale with group size and with model scale (1.5B → 70B+)?
- Can the weight-update-norm confound be broken — e.g., by training explicit students for the full 342 steps on diluted panda text to match ∥d∥ — to establish whether subliminality per se, rather than delta size, drives the sensitivity?
- Why does the student–base error alignment cos(E_s, E_b) differ between HQQ (0.88) and NF4 (0.96), and can this quantity be used as a cheap pre-deployment predictor of trait volatility?
- Would a harmful subliminally-learned trait (e.g., a political bias or a backdoor trigger from Schrodi et al.'s divergence tokens) show the same 0.22–1.58 swing, and does the amplified regime (R > 1.15) survive quantization-aware fine-tuning (QAT) or smoothing?
- Can mechanistic tracing (beyond layer-14 projections) explain *where* in the forward pass the shuffled errors couple to the "panda" logit — e.g., via the output-layer readout path the 3-bit ablation implicates?
