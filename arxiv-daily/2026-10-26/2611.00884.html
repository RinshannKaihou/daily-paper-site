Why this matters

Agents and RAG pipelines that trust status codes silently ingest shells and block pages, inflating citation-drift estimates 3.8× — three cheap checks catch it.

Critical edge — read before citing

The "cheap heuristics win" conclusion was salvaged by a post-hoc protocol re-interpretation — no detector met the pre-registered ≤5% false-alarm bar — and the headline 3.8× distortion rides on an unbounded LLM-judge confound.

Plus: one vantage point, one 3-day window, one non-announcing desktop-Chrome UA — the 20.9% figure is setup-specific.

01Methodology — how it was measured

1FETCH

Static GET (K1) vs headless Chromium / Playwright (K2) fetch 10,307 items: 8,800 dual-client URLs + 1,507 static-only soft-404 probes. ≤1 req/s/host, robots.txt honoured, challenges never solved.

2TYPE

Every 200 typed into a seven-outcome taxonomy — A content · B block · C challenge · D shell · E error/substitute · F parked · G consent. Unusable = B–G, or retention r < 0.5, or a random 16-hex path answering 200.

3VERIFY

Frozen-prompt LLM labeller (recall 0.96 / precision 0.75) → blind second-family review of 300 → blind human pass on 60 (κ = 0.64 usability) → 50-item desktop-browser check of the rendered reference.

4DETECT

Nine detectors judged on the static response only: status code, crawl4ai is_blocked, text length, probe similarity, redirect-to-root, challenge strings, zero-shot Qwen3-32B, TF-IDF logistic regression, fine-tuned Qwen3-1.7B. Site-disjoint 60/20/20 splits, domain-bootstrap CIs.

5DISTORT + SIMULATE

6,156 ExpertQA claim–source pairs re-judged under status-only vs outcome labels; render-escalation policies timed on 920 test-split static-200 pages.

Populations: llms.txt links · Tranco top-10k · 10k–100k · 100k–1M · Markdown-serving sites · 4,795 ExpertQA-cited URLs — one fixed seed, one vantage point, 2026-10-19 → 10-21.

  • Range across populations: 9.8% unusable on llms.txt links (biased toward working links by construction) → 23.2% on ExpertQA-cited sources → 26.3% on Tranco top-10k.
  • Soft 404s: 13.1% of probed sites answer 200 for a random non-existent path — roughly double the 7.35% server-level figure reported in 2013.
  • Concentration: on cited sources, four hosts — scribd, mdpi, biomedcentral, springer — account for 67% of challenges and blocks.
  • Rendering is not a cure: 16.7% of rendered references are themselves not clean content; a desktop-browser check found the renderer missed content on 3/47 pages (6.4%).

03Strengths

  • Fills a named gap: prior work (Gundelach et al., ARES 2026) explicitly scoped out content degradation inside 200s; this measures exactly that, on populations agents actually fetch.
  • Three-layer verification chain — frozen-prompt labeller → blind second-family review → blind human + browser checks — with the labeller's own error rates (recall 0.96 / precision 0.75) reported, not hidden.
  • The distortion analysis is the crown jewel: converts a measurement into consequences — 3.8× drift inflation, 12.2-pt checkability overstatement, 11.8% → 16.0% content-absent.
  • Deployable output: the exact H-best rule, per-type recall breakdown, coded error analysis, and a time-budgeted render-policy simulation.
  • Exemplary transparency: Appendix C logs every post-hoc protocol change; consistently reports the estimates that undercut its own automatic labels.

04Limitations

  • Setup-specific absolutes: single vantage point, single IP, three days, non-announcing desktop-Chrome UA — block/challenge/shell shares depend on client, IP reputation, and time.
  • LLM-anchored labels: the human check covers only 60 items and the reviewed sample 300, leaving a wide headline CI (13.3–27.7%); correlated model failure modes remain possible.
  • Unbounded drift-judge confound: each scheme passes a different page subset to the same LLM judge, so part of the 5.8% vs 21.9% gap could be judge behaviour on degenerate text.
  • The rendered "oracle" itself fails: not clean content for 16.7% of static-200 items; missed browser-visible content in 6.4% of the browser check — true usable share likely a bit lower than every estimate.
  • Visible-text scope: mostly English, text-centric; the retention metric cannot detect loss of images, tables, or interactive data; paywalled flows beyond consent walls unhandled.

05Questions for further exploration

  • Do the unusable-200 rates move across vantage points (residential vs cloud vs datacentre) and with an honestly-declared AI-crawler UA — does politeness pay or punish?
  • Would a two-stage cascade — cheap H-best first, learned M2 as second-stage verifier on borderline pages — dominate the recall / false-alarm / time trade-off?
  • Can a model fine-tuned on the 300 reviewed (not automatic) labels close the false-alarm gap that kept learned detectors impractical?
  • How much of the 5.8% → 21.9% drift inflation survives a human drift judge on a stratified subsample?
  • Do production answer engines' live question-time fetches show the same ~20% unusable share — and do their groundedness metrics count shells as “evidence read”?
  • Does the llms.txt ecosystem stay healthier than the general Web once the S1 bias toward corpus-recorded working links is removed?
