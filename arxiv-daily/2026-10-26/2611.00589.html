28.3%

1M trained OPT-125M coordinates, τ=0.01. 69.5–71.9% smaller than FP32 across all 120 complete-checkpoint restores — and every restored first update stays strictly under tolerance (max 0.9992τ).

Why this matters

Cuts checkpoint storage and transfer in long LLM training without touching Adam itself — and its “certify the consumed computation, not the stored value” guard generalizes to other training state.

⚠ Critical edge

The certificate covers only the first restored update, pinned to one Torch 2.8 scalar-FP32 Adam path — and encoding costs 7.2–7.6× FP32 serialization (24.9 GiB peak).

— Methodology the guarded codec

The contract: decoded moments (m̃, ṽ) are accepted per coordinate only if sup|g|≤0.2|Φ(w,m,v;g) − Φ(w,m̃,ṽ;g)| ≤ τη, where Φ is a pinned Torch 2.8 scalar FP32 Adam path (β₁=0.9, β₂=0.999, ε=10⁻⁸, η∈[10⁻⁶,0.01]).

The insight: both updates consume the same gradient g and parameter w. Ranging them separately is the classic interval-arithmetic dependency problem — near g=0 it is catastrophically loose. Subtracting the two quotients algebraically cancels g and w, so the bound scales with moment reconstruction error, not with the update's sensitivity to g.

  1. Encode per 256-coordinate block — 8-bit linear codes for m (exact zero), 8-bit log codes for v, FP64 block scales.
  2. Guard 1 — ideal-real bound, judged on the actually decoded values.
  3. Guard 2 — native bound: shared-gradient enclosure + FP32 rounding balls R(M)=8u₃₂M+4nmin, maximized over a mixed linear + base-2 log gradient partition (log edges to 2⁻⁴⁰·C; unresolved coords rechecked at 64→256→1024 intervals).
  4. Escape — failing coordinates stored as exact FP32; every byte (headers, masks, escapes, SHA-256) is charged to the payload.
  5. Verify — NumPy and eager-PyTorch decoders must agree bitwise, or the coordinate escapes; directed-rounding NVRTC CUDA kernels do all bound arithmetic outward in double precision.

Deployment rule (Fig. 3c) — encode-time-dominated regimes → guarded BF16 (48% savings at 1.7–1.8× FP32 codec time); storage/bandwidth-dominated → guarded log codes (69–72% savings at 4.3–4.5×). Both beat guarded blockwise 8-bit on bytes and time.

— Strengths

  • Turns a theoretical insight — shared-input cancellation in interval arithmetic — into a working, byte-accounted codec: 126.6% → 28.3% of FP32 purely from the bound, identical code bytes.
  • Total-honesty accounting (escapes, headers, masks, checksums all charged) is exactly what exposes guarded blockwise 8-bit (forced to escape 12–23% of coordinates) losing on bytes.
  • The guard is representation-agnostic — applied unchanged to BF16 and official bitsandbytes 0.48.1 codes: a drop-in audit layer for existing formats.
  • Clean design: paired within-seed comparisons, pre-registered conditions, separate timing cohorts, 120 full restores, and an H100 implementation audit with a deliberately broken control (missing radius → 899,311 detections).
  • Actionable deployment rule (Fig. 3c) rather than a single operating point.

— Limitations

  • Certificate covers only the first restored update; subsequent training is empirical (100 own-gradient updates, descriptive statistics only).
  • Pinned to Torch 2.8 scalar FP32 Adam with fixed hyperparameters (C=0.2, lr∈[10⁻⁶,0.01]); fused/foreach kernels, AdamW decoupled decay, amsgrad, and other optimizers are uncertified.
  • Scale: largest model is OPT-125M — billion-parameter sharded checkpoints, where optimizer state dominates, are untested.
  • Guarding is paid at encode time and peak memory: 7.2–7.6× FP32 encode, 2.3–2.6× guarded-BF16 combined codec time, 24.9 vs 19.2 GiB peak.
  • Advantage is tolerance-dependent: at τ=0.005 guarded BF16 becomes the smaller successful format; 4-bit saves <3%.
  • Timings are codec-boundary measurements — no end-to-end checkpoint-pause numbers (SHA validation, transfer, resumed training excluded).

— Questions for further exploration

  1. How do escape rates (0.6–4.9% on OPT-125M) and the payload advantage evolve for billion-parameter sharded checkpoints — and does 8-byte-per-escape accounting change at 4-bit codes?
  2. Can shared-gradient cancellation extend to a trajectory-level bound over K restored updates (e.g., under a Lipschitz/contraction assumption) instead of only the first?
  3. End-to-end inside a real frequent-checkpointing system: does 7.2× encode time offset byte savings on fast storage but win on slow network links?
  1. Does the guard transfer to fused/foreach CUDA kernels, AdamW's decoupled decay, or momentum optimizers (Lion, Adafactor) with different sensitivity near v=0?
  2. Could a hybrid codec — log codes for small-v coordinates, linear blockwise for the rest — push the payload below 28%?
  3. Why are ResNet-18 continuation losses larger for every compressed format including BF16 (mean up to +0.0087) while OPT-125M differences sit at ~10⁻⁴–10⁻⁵ — task sensitivity or a CIFAR-10 data-order artifact?
