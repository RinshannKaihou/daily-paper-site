Key metric · the sign flip

+3.04×10−3 → −2.23×10−3

One frozen training-mean AB vector, dose +0.5, same Qwen2.5-1.5B weights: the answer margin G flips from positive to negative across prompt families. 95% CIs [1.56, 4.50] and [−2.55, −1.91] — each excludes zero, in opposite directions. Llama-3.2-1B stays positive on both (+3.95 / +1.45).

Why this matters

Steering vectors often flip sign without warning. This paper replaces the mystery with a compact, testable identity — βG = κBR − κAR — plus exact pathway bookkeeping.

Critical edge

All evidence comes from 15–16-token synthetic prompts in ≤1.5B base models with 10−3-scale logit effects — no natural-text or behavioral benchmark is touched.

  • Frozen models. GPT-2 Small (12 blocks), Qwen2.5-1.5B (28), Llama-3.2-1B (16); shallow/middle/deep anchors; default receiver at the attention output before the residual add.
  • Controlled materials. Synthetic entity-binding sentences — “Lina is red, Niko is blue — ask Lina” — in four-corner and eight-condition factorial designs; two binary attribute factors crossed with query role, decomposed so the role-independent AB term isolates the shared relational background. 128/32/64 disjoint train/dev/test name groups.
  • Interventions. Middle-layer direction edits; donor restoration judged against sign-reversed and equal-norm random refills; local sensitivities κ = ∇mTv yield the analytic gain βG = κBR − κAR; clean clamps on late attention/MLP sets split the gain into four tangent paths that sum exactly to the native effect.
  • Source coordinates. A calibrated source-grounded Wiener inversion maps attention outputs back to input coordinates (65k–131k natural windows per depth).
  • Statistics. 95% source-group bootstrap intervals on nearly every estimate; Holm correction over four families; axes evaluated only on held-out names.

  • Binding reorganizes with depth; syntax does not. Syntax interaction stays <10% at every sampled depth, while binding conditional-direction cosines flip negative at middle layers (factor-A: −0.379 / −0.818 / −0.603).
  • A task-specific workspace, not ambient covariance. A rank-16 task basis covers 69.90 / 66.80 / 63.47% of conditional-A energy vs 15.51 / 14.56 / 6.14% for natural PCs; overlap only 16.31 / 8.76 / 7.02%.
  • Domain-delimited, query-independent. Cross-domain interaction ≈ 0 (0.09 / 1.08 / 0.45%) whether or not that domain is queried.
  • Competing paths, exact bookkeeping. In Llama-3.2 the attention-only path (−12.683×10−3) is offset by the both-sets path (+6.008×10−3); four tangent paths sum exactly to native βG.
  • Lexical amplification. Length-matched different lexemes collapse ηI to 0.45 / 6.00 / 7.94%; GPT-2’s frozen axis then sits at chance (50.20%).

Strengths

What it gets right

  • Clean factorial designs separate what is encoded from what the current query reads out.
  • βG = κBR − κAR is a compact, testable account of sign-unstable steering vectors — checked at finite dose and under frozen RMSNorm.
  • The exact four-path decomposition attributes signed effects without double counting; static-write vs dynamic-transmission dissociation included.
  • Bootstrap intervals on essentially every number; null and chance results reported, not buried.
  • Crisp domain-selectivity finding: same-domain interaction persists queried or not; cross-domain ≈ 0.

Limitations

Where it stops short

  • Synthetic 15–16-token prompts with binary color/shape attributes — no natural-language evidence yet.
  • Only 124M–1.5B base models; noted architecture confounds (shared key bias, GQA) are discussed, not resolved.
  • Effects are 10−3-scale logit-margin changes; no downstream task-accuracy consequence demonstrated.
  • The sign-flip protocol changes prompt family and answer vocabulary simultaneously — the cause is not isolated.
  • Late-clamp node sets were picked from baseline attribution on the same 64 evaluation groups (partial circularity).
  • Single-author, unreviewed preprint; the calibrated Wiener machinery rests on one prior single-author paper.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does the shared AB background — and its condition-dependent sign — survive in 7B+ or instruction-tuned models?
  2. Can βG = κBR − κAR become a cheap pre-flight test predicting when a steering direction will flip on a given prompt family?
  3. Is the Qwen2.5 flip driven by answer-vocabulary geometry (orange/purple vs red/blue) or by lexical-mapping prompt processing?
  4. Do the competing attention/MLP paths correspond to known self-repair (Hydra-effect) phenomena — and can the negative attention path be patched?
  5. Is the source-coordinate “future component” genuine calibrated cross-position statistics or an artifact of the Wiener map’s frequency mixing?
