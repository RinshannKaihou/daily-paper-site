Why this matters

A zero-state, plug-in update-scaling rule for anyone pretraining sparse MoE models with Muon or Hyperball — it quantifies the stability-vs-quality trade-off directly.

— distilled from Relevance
⚠ Critical edge

The 99.51% headline compares recipes at different learning rates; with mean applied q ≈ 0.96 and no fixed-q control, energy-dependent assignment itself is unproven.

— from Critical Assessment · see Limitations

Methodology01

The EnergyCal rule

NS direction Dt
(per routed gate/up/down) → ρt = ‖Dt‖²F / d → qt = clip[(ρt/0.8)p, 0, 1]
p ∈ {0.5, 1} → routed update × qt·η

One-sided: directions at or above the reference energy (ρref = 0.8) pass untouched. Momentum, router objective, attention/shared experts and Adam fallback unchanged; no persistent optimizer state added.

Testbed

From-scratch 4B DeepSeekMoE-style sparse MoE: 24 layers, width 1,536, 128 routed experts (width 256) with top-2 routing, 1 shared expert per layer, tied embeddings, aux-loss-free router-bias balancing. 8×H100, 131,072 tokens/update × 10,000 updates = 1.31B tokens (FineWeb sample-10BT, 0.5% val). Five quintic Newton–Schulz iterations in BF16 with FP32 momentum/norms; 500-step warmup + cosine to 10%.

Study design

Two cohorts, 3 init seeds each, paired by initialization: Cohort H — frozen EnergyCal–MuonH (LR 0.01) vs dev-selected MuonH (LR 0.005) and Muon+WD; Cohort M — plain Muon ± EnergyCal at matched LR 0.005 / WD 0.1. Spikes flagged by adaptive threshold τt = max{mt+0.5, 1.2mt, mt+8σt} over a 101-loss rolling median; burden B₁ = Σ(Lt − mt) over flagged steps. Throughput: 3 execution repeats × 1,000 updates.

The advantage emerges late. At steps 3k / 5k, mean CE favors plain Muon (3.7995 vs 3.7437; 3.6038 vs 3.5479); the ordering flips only between 8,000–8,500 steps, and normalized trapezoidal CE over 500–10,000 still favors plain Muon (3.6111 vs 3.6270). The equal-time sensitivity check also favors plain Muon (3.2453 vs 3.2410).
No one-step rescue. Single-update interventions (7 branches × 2 saved spike events, incl. exact candidate freezing cutting stored displacement by 86.3% / 76.3%) leave next-step CE ≈ 6.32 / 6.91 — the benefit is continuous calibration, not spike deletion. Zero-token experts were caught retaining momentum with ‖D‖F = 0.9921, sr = 1.0409.

Strengths03

  • Dramatic, consistent stability gains — 99.51% / 95.98% burden cuts with improvement in essentially all paired runs, for a rule that adds no optimizer state and costs 1.62% throughput.
  • Robust across formulations — works with and without Hyperball normalization and across two exponents (p = 0.5, 1).
  • Exceptional transparency — predefined endpoints, frozen recipes, paired analyses, unfavorable results retained (amplitude-only divergence, failed interventions).
  • Genuinely informative diagnostics — zero-token experts retaining near-unit-amplitude NS directions is a concrete, quotetable observation about Muon-family MoE dynamics.
  • Complete evaluation suite — final CE, intermediate trapezoidal CE, late-window check, episodes, burden, max loss, throughput, memory, coefficient telemetry — not collapsed into one number.

Limitations04

  • Small scale and budget — one custom 4B architecture, 1.31B tokens/run, 3 seeds with shared data order; ~3 orders of magnitude below frontier pretraining.
  • No uniform-scaling control — cannot tell whether energy-dependent assignment beats simply shrinking routed updates by a constant q ≈ 0.96; the author flags this as unresolved.
  • Recipe-level Cohort H comparison — differing LRs (0.01 vs 0.005) do not isolate the coefficient; both baseline LRs sat at the lower boundary of their search grids.
  • No head-to-head against Excitation, ExpertMuon-Compass, NorMuon, or TrasMuon.
  • Mechanism unresolved — single-update interventions failed on both examined spikes; the geometric identity covers only the MuonH trial update.
  • Cross-cohort gaps — different validation banks and implementation revisions block ranking calibrated MuonH vs calibrated plain Muon; intermediate and equal-time quality favor plain Muon.

Questions for Further Exploration05

  1. Would a routed-only uniform-scaling control (fixed q ≈ 0.96, matched update-norm budget) reproduce most of the burden reduction, or does energy-dependence genuinely matter?
  2. Do the stability gains persist at 10–100B+ token budgets and larger expert counts, where spike statistics and routing dynamics may differ qualitatively?
  3. How does EnergyCal interact with utilization-based (Excitation) or alignment-based (Compass) expert scaling — complementary or redundant?
  4. If spikes are not rescued one-step, what accumulated effect explains the benefit: slower expert-norm growth, altered router-bias dynamics, or implicit effective-LR scheduling on rare experts?
  5. Can the energy statistic be disentangled from direction amplitude to test whether spectral concentration or magnitude is the operative variable?
  6. Does EnergyCal change expert specialization / load-balancing outcomes at convergence, beyond loss-curve stability?
