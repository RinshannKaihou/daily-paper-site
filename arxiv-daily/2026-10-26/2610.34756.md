> **一句话总结：** 用路径修补（path patching）等机制可解释性手段系统解剖 LoRA，发现其适配效应高度稀疏——MLP 贡献 58.4%、V 投影主导注意力部分、重要层跨任务仅 39.5% Jaccard 重合——并据此提出 Adaptive-LoRA：在冻结底座上用零消融校准（约 30 条样本、20–62 秒）选出任务专属的约 30% 层，在 Qwen2.5-1.5B 的 11 个推理任务上以 3.5 倍更少的适配器参数全面超过全层 LoRA/LoRA+/DoRA（r16 常识平均 59.1% vs LoRA 56.9%，+2.2 分）。

> **TL;DR:** This NeurIPS 2026 paper uses path patching on Qwen2.5-0.5B to show LoRA's adaptation effect is highly sparse and task-dependent (MLP 58.4% vs attention 41.6%; only 39.5% Jaccard overlap of top layers across tasks), then proposes Adaptive-LoRA, which picks the top ~30% of layers via gradient-free zero-ablation calibration on the frozen base model (N=30 inputs, 20–62 s on one A100). On Qwen2.5-1.5B across 11 reasoning benchmarks (8 commonsense incl. BoolQ/PIQA/HellaSwag + GSM8K/SVAMP/AQUA), it beats full-layer LoRA/LoRA+/DoRA baselines at every rank — e.g. r16 commonsense average 59.1% vs LoRA's 56.9% (+2.2 pts) and DoRA's 55.5% (+3.6 pts) — with 3.5× fewer adapter parameters; gains shrink to +0.0/−0.2 pts on Qwen3-8B/32B, so the win is concentrated at 0.5B–3B scale.

## 核心贡献

- **首个系统性 LoRA 机制可解释性分析**：在 Qwen2.5-0.5B（24 层）上用激活消融与路径修补，沿组件类型（MLP/注意力）、投影类型（Q/K/V）、层深三个维度量化 LoRA 适配效应，发现贡献高度非均匀。
- **四大机制发现**：(1) MLP 主导——占组件效应 58.4%（各任务 57.3%–59.5%），注意力仅 41.6%；(2) V 投影主导注意力内贡献（约 46%，Q/K 各约 27%）；(3) 层贡献随深度递减（浅层 L0-7 占 33.3%、中层 24.0%、深层 16.1%）；(4) 重要层高度任务相关——四任务 top-7 层的平均两两 Jaccard 相似度仅 39.5%，只有 L0–L2 为全部任务共享。
- **提出 Adaptive-LoRA**：三阶段方法（校准→选层→训练），在冻结底座上对每层注意力/MLP 输出做独立零消融，以 top-token 概率变化之和作为层重要性分数，取 top-k（默认约 30% 层）仅在这些层训练 LoRA——无需训练适配器即可选层，校准只需 30 条输入、20–62 秒（A100）。
- **实证验证参数效率**：Qwen2.5-1.5B 上 11 个推理任务、四种秩（r4–r32）全部超过全层 LoRA/LoRA+/DoRA，同时 LoRA 因子容量缩减 3.5×；跨 6 个模型（Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B/3B、LLaMA-3-3B、Qwen3-8B/32B）验证了泛化性。
- **选择策略消融**：同等预算下校准选层显著优于随机/顺序/逆序选层（12/36 层时 Alpaca-ZH 困惑度相对随机降低 25.0%），并在全层预算处收敛到相同性能，形成对照闭环。

## Key Contributions

- **First systematic mechanistic dissection of LoRA**: using activation ablation and path patching on Qwen2.5-0.5B (24 layers), the paper quantifies LoRA's adaptation effect along component type (MLP vs attention), projection type (Q/K/V), and depth, showing the effect is highly non-uniform.
- **Four mechanistic findings**: (1) MLP blocks mediate 58.4% of the measured component effect vs 41.6% for attention (stable at 57.3%–59.5% across four instruction datasets); (2) the Value projection carries ~46% of the attention-side effect vs ~27% each for Q and K; (3) layer contributions decay with depth (early L0-7: 33.3%, middle L8-15: 24.0%, late L16-23: 16.1%); (4) important layers are strongly task-dependent — mean pairwise Jaccard similarity of top-7 layers across tasks is only 39.5%, with only L0–L2 universally shared, undermining the all-layer uniform adaptation paradigm.
- **Adaptive-LoRA**: a three-phase method (calibrate → select → train). On the frozen base model it zero-ablates each layer's attention and MLP output independently, scores layers by the drop in top-token probability summed over both interventions, and trains LoRA only on the top-k layers (default k ≈ 0.3L). Selection needs no trained adapter, N=30 calibration inputs, and 20–62 s on one A100 (4.1–6.3% of full-layer training time).
- **Validation of the causal signal**: frozen-base importance scores correlate with post-training path-patching contributions at ρ = 0.630–0.893 across five adapter configurations; selection stability (Jaccard across calibration draws) rises from 0.524 (N=10) to 0.653 (N=30) and 0.685 (N=100), justifying the N=30 default.
- **Broad empirical validation**: outperforms full-layer LoRA/LoRA+/DoRA at all four ranks on 11 reasoning tasks with a 3.5× smaller LoRA-factor budget, and generalizes across six models from 0.5B to 32B in two architecture families.

## Methodology

The paper has two coupled parts: a mechanistic analysis and a method built on it.

**Mechanistic analysis (Section 3).** Full LoRA adapters (rank 8, α=16, on Q/K/V) are trained on Qwen2.5-0.5B over four datasets spanning language and modality: Alpaca-ZH (52K Chinese instructions), Alpaca-EN (52K English), Code-Alpaca (20K code), Dolly (15K dialogue). Contribution of a component c is measured by activation patching: S_c(x) = p_LoRA(t*|x) − p_ablate(c)(t*|x), where t* is the top predicted token under the full LoRA model; scores are averaged over a calibration set. Interventions are applied per layer to MLP outputs, attention outputs, and (within attention) to single Q/K/V adapters while preserving the other two, yielding the component/projection/depth decomposition. A separate output-replacement experiment (record block outputs in an adapter-disabled run, splice one reference output into the adapted run) confirms MLP mediation is not an artifact of where adapters sit. Task dependence is quantified by Jaccard similarity between the top-7 layer sets (30% of 24 layers) of each dataset pair.

**Adaptive-LoRA (Section 4, Algorithm 1).** The key trick is that layer selection happens on the *frozen base model, before any training*: for each calibration input x, take the base model's top token t* and its probability q; for every layer l, independently zero the attention output (a_l←0) and the MLP output (m_l←0), and accumulate I_l += |q − q_l^A| + |q − q_l^M|. Averaging over N inputs (default 30) gives a layer-importance profile in O(N·L) gradient-free forward passes. Phase 2 selects the top-k layers (main experiments use k ≈ 0.3L); an "efficiency mode" restricts selection to layers l ≥ d* to expose a frozen prefix reusable across epochs. Phase 3 trains standard rank-r LoRA only on selected layers (Q/V targets, motivated by Finding 2) with AdamW, lr 1e-4, r=16, α=32, 3 epochs, batch 64. At equal per-layer rank and targets, selecting k of L layers shrinks adapter parameters by L/k — 28/8 = 3.5× in the main setup.

**Evaluation.** Primary model Qwen2.5-1.5B (28 layers), trained on commonsense_170k (10K samples) and math_10k (5K, chain-of-thought); 11 tasks: BoolQ, PIQA, SIQA, HellaSwag, WinoGrande, ARC-e, ARC-c, OBQA, GSM8K, SVAMP, AQUA (1,000 eval samples each, OBQA 500). Baselines: LoRA, LoRA+, DoRA on all 28 layers at r ∈ {4,8,16,32}. Cross-model generalization (Table 2) at r16 on six models 0.5B–32B. Ablations sweep the layer budget k = 4…36 against Random/Sequential/Reverse selection strategies on Alpaca-ZH perplexity.

## Results & Highlights

- **Main comparison (Table 1, Qwen2.5-1.5B, 8/28 layers = 28.6% budget)**: at r16, Adaptive-LoRA reaches 59.1% commonsense average vs 56.9% for full-layer LoRA (+2.2 pts), 57.9% for LoRA+ (+1.2 pts), and 55.5% for DoRA (+3.6 pts) — with a 3.5× smaller LoRA-factor capacity at identical rank and projection targets. Gains over LoRA persist at every rank: +1.3 (r4), +2.0 (r8), +2.2 (r16), +1.8 (r32) points.
- **Math reasoning (Table 1)**: at r32, Adaptive-LoRA averages 47.8% (GSM8K 50.8, SVAMP 53.4, AQUA 39.2) vs 47.3% for LoRA+ (+0.5 pts) and 45.5% for LoRA (+2.3 pts); on PIQA at r32 it scores 67.8% vs LoRA+'s 67.4%.
- **Cross-model generalization (Table 2, r16, top-30% layers, N=30 calibration)**: average gains over full-layer LoRA are +1.5 pts on Qwen2.5-0.5B (38.6 vs 37.1), +2.2 on Qwen2.5-1.5B (59.1 vs 56.9), +1.9 on Qwen2.5-3B (68.8 vs 66.9), +1.2 on LLaMA-3-3B (62.4 vs 61.2) — but the advantage vanishes at scale: +0.0 on Qwen3-8B (82.5 vs 82.5) and −0.2 on Qwen3-32B (87.1 vs 87.3); appendix three-run means are positive everywhere but only +0.2–0.5 pts at 32B.
- **Selection-strategy ablation (Figure 4, Alpaca-ZH test perplexity, r16, 3 epochs)**: at 12 of 36 layers, calibration-based selection beats Random by 25.0% relative (an absolute PPL gap of 4.10), Sequential (first-k) by 12.4%, and Reverse (last-k) by 17.8%; all four strategies converge to identical PPL 5.75 at 36 layers, the expected control.
- **Mechanistic findings with numbers**: MLP mediates 58.4% of the component effect vs 41.6% attention (per-task range 57.3%–59.5% across Alpaca-ZH/EN, Code-Alpaca, Dolly); V carries ~46% of the attention effect (Q ~27%, K ~27%); normalized depth shares are 33.3% (L0-7) / 24.0% (L8-15) / 16.1% (L16-23); top-7 layer overlap across tasks averages Jaccard 39.5% with only L0–L2 universal.
- **Calibration quality and cost**: frozen-base scores correlate with post-training contributions at ρ = 0.630–0.893 (five adapter configs); five independent draws give selection Jaccard 0.653–0.747; calibration costs 20–62 s on one A100 (4.1–6.3% of reference training time).
- **Against structured-LoRA rivals**: at the same r16 setting on the commonsense suite, Adaptive-LoRA averages 59.1% vs TreeLoRA 58.7%, GainLoRA 58.5%, and InfLoRA 58.0% (Appendix B.9), while adapting fewer layers.

## Authors & Credibility

Fei Zuo is at East China Normal University; Yizhou Huang and Kezhi Wang are at Brunel University of London (Huang and Wang are corresponding authors). Kezhi Wang's group works on model compression and efficient inference, making the layer-selection framing a natural fit. The paper is accepted at NeurIPS 2026 (footnote on page 1: "40th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems"), so it passed peer review; the acknowledgments cite UKRI Innovate UK COMET, Horizon Europe guarantees, a Royal Society Industry Fellowship, and the NVIDIA Academic Grant Program. All evaluated models (Qwen2.5, Qwen3, LLaMA-3) are open-weight, hyperparameters and seeds are documented (seed 42 for main tables), and a seven-section appendix gives per-task breakdowns, cost tables, and stability studies — a generally credible package, though no public code link is given in the paper.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: genuinely nice combination — using path patching (Wang et al.'s IOI-circuit toolkit) to *diagnose* LoRA and then converting the diagnosis into a practical selection rule is a real bridge between mech-interp and PEFT. But the claim of "the first systematic mechanistic analysis of LoRA's internal mechanisms" is stronger than warranted: prior work already studied which components/depths LoRA updates matter (e.g., layer-pruning work like ShortGPT that the paper itself cites, and rank-allocation studies like AdaLoRA); the path-patching treatment and the task-dependence quantification are the novel parts.
- **Rigor — single-seed main results**: Tables 1 and 2 use seed 42 only; multi-seed numbers live in Appendix B.8, where the 32B mean gain is just +0.2–0.5 pts. The headline "+1.2–2.2 pts over full-layer LoRA" coexists with −0.2 at Qwen3-32B, so the abstract's unqualified "outperforms full-layer LoRA" overstates scale-generalization: the win is a small-model phenomenon.
- **Rigor — noisy per-task numbers betray seed variance**: Table 3 (Qwen2.5-3B full-layer LoRA) is wildly non-monotonic in rank — PIQA goes 68.2 → 76.0 → 60.6 → 74.2 and the average 62.8 → 64.2 → 61.0 → 60.8 across r4→r32 — fluctuations of the same magnitude as the headline +1–2 pt gains, suggesting the margins are within single-seed noise for several configurations.
- **Internal inconsistencies**: the mechanistic analysis uses rank-8 Q/K/V adapters on Qwen2.5-0.5B while the downstream method uses Q/V at r16 on 1.5B; and Table 1's selection uses N=250 calibration inputs while Table 2 and the default use N=30, with no explanation of why the main comparison needed ~8× more calibration data. The layer-importance conclusions are also transferred from instruction-tuning datasets (Alpaca/Dolly) to multiple-choice reasoning benchmarks without checking that the important layers coincide across that task shift — precisely the task-dependence their own Finding 4 warns about.
- **Missing baselines**: no comparison against magnitude/pruning-based layer selection or training-time importance estimators; the closest competitors are Random/Sequential/Reverse heuristics and a single supplementary table against three continual-learning LoRA variants. Notably, Random selection at 30% budget is never reported in the main Table 1 setup — the random baseline only appears in the perplexity ablation — so the reader cannot tell how much of the accuracy gain is "smart selection" versus simply sparsifying (regularizing) the adapter.
- **Reproducibility**: open-weight models, stated hyperparameters, seeds, and cost numbers are all pluses; the lack of a code release and of exact calibration-data provenance (which 30 inputs?) are the main gaps.

## Strengths

- A principled causal grounding (path patching / zero-ablation) for a decision — which layers to adapt — that is usually made heuristically.
- Extremely cheap selection: gradient-free, N=30 inputs, 20–62 s on one A100, with measured score-target correlation (ρ = 0.630–0.893) and calibration-stability studies.
- Honest convergence control in the ablation: all selection strategies meet at PPL 5.75 when the budget covers all 36 layers.
- Cross-family, cross-scale validation (six models, 0.5B–32B, Qwen + LLaMA) rather than a single-model case study.
- The mechanistic findings are independently interesting: the stability of the 58.4/41.6 MLP/attention split across Chinese, English, code, and dialogue data, and the 39.5% task-overlap number, are useful reference points for anyone working on adapter placement.
- Appendix A is a candid limitations section (granularity, task coverage, theory, static selection) rather than boilerplate.

## Limitations

- Layer-level granularity only: given MLP dominance (58.4%) and V-projection concentration (~46%), component- or head-level selection could save more, but the authors leave it as future work since calibration cost would grow from O(L) to O(L×C).
- Task coverage is narrow: 8 commonsense + 3 math multiple-choice benchmarks; generative tasks (dialogue, summarization, creative writing) and long-context scenarios may shift layer importance toward later/middle layers and are untested.
- Gains vanish or invert at 8B–32B scale (+0.0 and −0.2 pts at seed 42), so the practical payoff is limited to the ≤3B regime.
- Selection is static — fixed before training; no re-calibration during training to track drifting importance.
- No theoretical account of why early layers dominate or why task-specific patterns emerge at particular depths.
- Main tables are single-seed, and the mechanistic analysis rests on one 0.5B model, leaving the generalization of the mechanism itself (not just the method) to larger models assumed rather than shown.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would component-level selection (adapting only MLP + V in selected layers, as Findings 1–2 suggest) compound the savings beyond the 3.5× layer-level factor, and at what accuracy cost?
- Why exactly do frozen-base zero-ablation scores predict post-training LoRA contributions (ρ = 0.630–0.893)? Is the correlation causal, or partly driven by both scores emphasizing high-norm early layers?
- Does the early-layer concentration survive on generative and long-context tasks (summarization, multi-turn dialogue, document QA), where later layers plausibly matter more?
- How much of the +1.2–2.2 pt gain over full-layer LoRA is selection quality versus regularization from sparsifying the adapter? A Random-30% baseline inside Table 1 would disentangle this.
- Can calibration be replaced by a training-free proxy (norms, representation similarity) to remove even the 30-input calibration step, as the paper's own "theoretical foundations" limitation hints?
- Does dynamic re-selection during training (re-calibrating every k epochs) beat the static choice on distribution-shifting or continual-learning setups?
