Methodology · CLiS = Certified Loop-invariant Stabilization

Online softmax pays a running row-max reduction plus accumulator rescale on every KV block. On TPU vector units (VLIW), that stabilization chain — not the matmuls or exp — dominates the removable softmax cost. CLiS replaces it with a per-head, provably safe fixed shift:

  1. CenterSubtract key mean k̄ from all keys — exactly attention-preserving; centered row max becomes ≥ 0.
  2. BoundCauchy–Schwarz logit bound Bi = ⌈‖qi‖₂·κ⌉, κ = maxj‖k̃j‖₂ — computed before the KV loop, no N×N score pass.
  3. RecenterLoop-invariant stabilizer μi = Bi − C for the largest feasible integer C (tightens against underflow).
  4. CertifyThree per-head FP checks: exp non-zero lower bound (margin ρ=1), exp upper bound, FP32 accumulator headroom (log₂Nk + G). Fail → per-head fallback to online softmax.
  5. RunCertified heads: S = QK̃ᵀ, P = exp2(S − μi), accumulate ℓ and O — no row max, no rescale. Tail partial block stays online → exact in exact arithmetic.

Plug-and-play: eligibility, key mean and κ are precomputed outside the loop; drops into existing fused Pallas kernels with no weight or architecture changes. All preprocessing and fallback costs are included in every reported latency. Baselines (FD++/VFA/FA4-style) are controlled ports inside the identical kernel.

Strengths

  • Provably safe: per-head numerical certificate, exact-arithmetic equivalence, graceful per-head fallback — the only fixed-shift method both faster and valid on every workload.
  • Training-free, plug-and-play: no weight or architecture changes; integrates into existing fused Pallas kernels; all overheads included in reported numbers.
  • Deep hardware grounding: VLIW/LLO bundle-level analysis explains why it works (VPU bundles −94%, spills −48%), not just that it works.
  • Exceptionally careful evaluation: controlled same-kernel baseline ports, 40-workload grid over two models / two TPU generations / four resolutions, counterfactual replay, bootstrap CIs, explicit noise floor.
  • Broad applicability evidence: five sharding layouts; from video-diffusion inference to causal LLM training at 512-chip DeepSeek-V3-671B scale.

Limitations

  • TPU-only: no GPU results; the benefit depends on TPU vector-unit costs and VREG register pressure — may not transfer to CUDA.
  • Upper-tail headline: −19.6% is the best cell; many grid cells are 3–12%, floor 3.1% (Gemma-3-12B +0.6% due to sliding-window layers).
  • Certification needs bounded norms: random-init Llama-3.1-8B without QK-Norm exhausts the FP32 exponent budget and silently loses the fast path by step 40; QK-Norm models certify at 95.5–100%.
  • Loose bounds unquantified: Cauchy–Schwarz can be very loose; 60 of 84 rejected sites were actually harmless — paper reports no bound-tightness distribution.
  • Quality evidence narrow: VBench only on Wan 2.2, 110 prompts (n=10/dimension); LTX-2 validated by latency/validity only.
  • Preliminary LLM results, no code release: 1,500-step training runs; CLiS itself not stated as open-sourced.

Questions for further exploration

  1. How tight is the Cauchy–Schwarz bound in practice — how much of the 1.78% fallback is bound looseness vs genuine logit growth, and would a cheap sampled row-max tighten certification?
  2. Does CLiS compose safely with quantized attention (SageAttention INT4/FP4) or sparse video attention (Sliding Tile, SpargeAttention)?
  3. Does the certificate hold under adversarial or out-of-distribution inputs whose logit distributions exceed the norm-bound assumptions?
  1. Is there a GPU analogue — measurable headroom for certified fixed shifts in CUDA, or is the win fundamentally TPU-specific (VLU dependency chains, shared VREG pressure)?
  2. For from-scratch training without QK-Norm, would progressive certification (online early, fixed-shift once norms stabilize) recover the +7–13% throughput after step 40?
  3. Would VBench-style quality evaluation on LTX-2 (24 fps, 1440p) confirm "no measurable loss" beyond validity checks?
