> **一句话总结：** 本文系统研究了二次逼近（quadratic approximation）在大规模 LLM 预训练中的失效模式：作者识别出一种与学习率无关的失效模式——"凸性边缘"（Edge of Convexity，由持续存在的负曲率导致二次动力学对任意学习率都发散，而真实训练保持稳定），并提出适用于随机训练 + 自适应优化器（AdamW 等）的广义"稳定性边缘"（EoS）定义与可扩展检测方法；在 43M–787M 参数、100+ 次预训练、3000+ 个检查点上的实验表明：EoC 始终出现（warmup 后 saddleness ≤ 0.1），EoS 普遍但不普适——当学习率充分衰减或 batch size 低于与 critical batch size 正相关的阈值（Pearson r = 0.95）时 EoS 消失。

> **TL;DR:** This paper asks whether Cohen et al. (2021)'s full-batch Edge of Stability phenomenon extends to realistic LLM pretraining, and answers "yes, but conditionally." Using compensated local simulations of the quadratic surrogate on 100+ AdamW pretraining runs of GPT models (43M–787M) on FineWeb and four other corpora, the authors find the quadratic approximation always fails via a newly identified LR-independent mode they call Edge of Convexity (persistent negative curvature with saddleness ≤ 0.1 after warmup), and usually fails via a generalized stochastic EoS: e.g., for GPT-small (104M) on FineWeb the critical LR factor q = η̂*/η lies in [0.9, 1.2] at 51/60 checkpoints for peak LRs up to 0.01. However, EoS is not universal — it vanishes below a per-dataset batch-size threshold (EoS Batch Size: 423 on OpenMath vs 577 on FineWeb) that correlates with the critical batch size at Pearson r = 0.95, disappears after sufficiently large LR decays, and is overshoot into an "extremely unstable" regime at peak LR 0.04 where the estimated critical LR is only ~1/10 of the training LR and final eval loss rises from ~2.45 (near-optimal LR) to ~3.2.

## 核心贡献

- **发现并命名"凸性边缘"（Edge of Convexity, EoC）**：一种全新的、与学习率无关的二次逼近失效模式。预训练 Hessian 始终存在小幅负曲率（λmin < 0），使二次模型的动力学对**任意**正学习率都发散（沿负曲率方向误差以 (1 + η|λmin|)^t 增长），而真实网络训练保持稳定。作者定义 **saddleness** ν = max{0, −λmin}/λmax 来量化负曲率的相对大小，并发现 LLM 预训练在 warmup 之后 ν ≤ 0.1——即 Hessian 接近半正定，但负曲率足以令二次模型永不稳定。
- **提出随机 + 自适应优化器下的广义 EoS 定义**：以往 EoS 定义仅适用于全批 GD。本文基于正曲率子空间上投影动力学的均方稳定性（mean-square stability），将临界学习率 η⋆ 定义为使二阶矩有界的 LR 上确界；当训练 LR η ≈ η̂⋆ 时判定为 EoS。在全批情形下该定义精确退化为 Cohen et al. (2021) 的 2/λmax 阈值。理论表明随机场景下稳定性由二阶矩算子 E[M⊗M] = E[M]⊗E[M] + Cov(M) 决定——批间 Hessian 协方差项会使临界 LR 偏离 2/λmax，这正是全批 EoS 理论缺失的部分。定义还推广到含动量、预条件器与 decoupled weight decay 的自适应梯度方法（AGM，以 AdamW 为特例）。
- **设计可扩展、有理论保证的检测算法**：(a) 随机幂迭代估计总体 Hessian 的 λmin（指出对小批 Hessian 直接跑 Lanczos 会因 λmin 的凹性/Jensen 不等式产生系统性低估）；(b) "补偿局部模拟"——在二次代理损失上加入 (λP/2)‖w − w_t0‖² 补偿负曲率后，用同一批次序列在一组 LR 网格上模拟，以位移范数达到截断值 M = 10⁵ 的最小 LR 作为 η̂⋆。
- **大规模实证结论**：(1) EoC 在所有 LLM 预训练中始终出现；(2) EoS 普遍但不普适：batch size 低于阈值（EBZ）时 EoS 消失，且 EBZ 与 critical batch size（CBZ）跨数据集呈正相关（Pearson r = 0.95，近似线性）；LR 充分衰减（如多步下降过大或衰减末期）后训练进入稳定区、EoS 不再出现；峰值 LR 过大时训练越过 EoS 进入"极不稳定区"（临界 LR 远低于训练 LR，最终评估损失显著变差）。

## Key Contributions

- **Identifies the Edge of Convexity (EoC)** — a new, LR-independent failure mode of the quadratic approximation: persistent negative Hessian curvature makes the quadratic dynamics diverge for *every* positive LR (error grows as (1 + η|λmin|)^t along the negative-curvature direction) while real training stays stable. A "saddleness" metric ν = max{0, −λmin}/λmax quantifies it, and after warmup LLM pretraining always has ν ≤ 0.1.
- **Generalizes EoS to stochastic training with adaptive optimizers**: defines the critical LR η* as the mean-square-stability threshold of the dynamics projected onto the positive-curvature subspace (second-moment operator E[M⊗M] = E[M]⊗E[M] + Cov(M), where the covariance of mini-batch Hessians shifts the boundary away from the classical 2/λmax), and declares EoS when η ≈ η̂*. In the full-batch case the definition exactly recovers Cohen et al. (2021). The framework covers a general adaptive gradient method (AGM) family including AdamW and momentum SGD via the preconditioned effective curvature P^(-1/2)HP^(-1/2) + λ_WD·I.
- **Scalable detection with theory**: stochastic power iteration for the population λmin (correcting the downward Jensen bias of mini-batch Lanczos estimates), plus compensated local simulation over an LR grid (adding (λP/2)‖w − w_t0‖² to neutralize negative curvature; explosion declared when displacement norm reaches M = 10⁵). Guarantees are given in Appendices A.2–A.3 and C.
- **Systematic empirical characterization at LLM scale** (43M–787M GPT models, 100+ runs, 3,000+ checkpoints): EoC always occurs; EoS is prevalent but not universal — it disappears below an EoS Batch Size (EBZ) threshold that positively correlates with the critical batch size (Pearson r = 0.95 across five datasets), after sufficiently large LR decays, and is overshot into an extremely unstable regime at excessive peak LRs.

## Methodology

The paper proceeds in three layers. **(1) Theory on stochastic quadratics (Sec. 4).** At a checkpoint, SGD on the local quadratic surrogate Q_B(w) with batch Hessian H_B induces a stochastic linear system ε_{t+1} = (I − ηA_t)ε_t − ηr_t. Proposition 4.2 derives the moment dynamics and shows mean-square stability requires ρ(E[M_t ⊗ M_t]) < 1. This yields two distinct instabilities: negative-curvature divergence (λmin < 0 ⇒ unstable for every η > 0) and positive-curvature divergence with a critical LR η* defined on the subspace projected by Π⁺ onto positive eigenvalues (Definition 4.3); in the full-batch case η* = 2/λmax. The covariance term Cov(M_t⁺) captures batch-to-batch Hessian variation and can move the stability boundary away from 2/λmax — the piece missing from full-batch EoS theory. **(2) Definitions for neural networks and AGMs (Sec. 5).** EoC at w_t0 means λmin(H_t0) < 0 but small saddleness ν; EoS means η ≈ η*(w_t0). For AdamW-style updates, curvature is measured in preconditioned coordinates (frozen preconditioner P at the checkpoint) with weight decay folded in as Ā = P^(-1/2)H_t0 P^(-1/2) + λ_WD I. **(3) Detection at scale.** λ̂min comes from stochastic power iteration (mini-batch Lanczos is biased low since E[λmin(H_B)] ≤ λmin(H) by concavity); EoS is detected by compensated local simulation — simulate the optimizer on the surrogate with negative-curvature compensation over LR factors around the training LR using the same batch sequence, and read off η̂⋆ as the smallest exploding LR. Experiments pretrain four GPT sizes (43M/104M/344M/787M; 4/12/24/36 layers) with AdamW (β1 = 0.9, β2 = 0.95, WD = 0.1), sequence length 1024, WSD schedules (4% warmup, 20% decay), 20 tokens/parameter for larger models, on FineWeb by default and on five corpora (FineWeb, OpenWebMath, OpenWebText, C4, DCLM) for the batch-size study, which follows the √k LR/WD scaling rule with β2 ← β2^k. Measurement hygiene is notable: an 8,192-token BPE vocabulary is used because rare tokens distort Adam's preconditioned curvature, and rare-token filtering leaves 92,060/97,656 (94.3%) sequences in the held-out detection shard.

## Results & Highlights

- **EoC is always observed; EoS is prevalent**: across over 3,000 checkpoints from more than 100 pretraining runs (GPT 43M–787M on FineWeb, AdamW, WSD), saddleness stays below the 0.1 reference line after warmup, and the bounded-to-exploding transition of the compensated local simulation sits near LR factor 1.0 at most stable-phase checkpoints — the training LR is close to the estimated critical LR.
- **GPT-small (104M) on FineWeb stays near EoS up to peak LR 0.01**: the critical LR factor q = η̂⋆/η lies between 0.9 and 1.2 at 51 of 60 checkpoints, with 35 of 60 in the strict EoS band (0.9 ≤ q ≤ 1.1) and 44/60 satisfying the EoC criterion; GPT-medium (344M) behaves similarly up to peak LR 5×10⁻³.
- **EoS disappears below a batch-size threshold (EBZ) that tracks the critical batch size (CBZ)**: sweeping global batch size 64→1024 on five datasets with GPT-extra-small, the mean critical LR factor decreases monotonically with batch size; interpolated EBZ values are 423 (OpenMath), 435 (DCLM), 463 (C4), 481 (OpenWebText), 577 (FineWeb), and EBZ vs CBZ (largest batch within 0.3% of the best final eval loss in each sweep) gives Pearson correlation 0.95. Concretely, EoS is observed at B = 512 and 1024 but not at B = 256 on FineWeb.
- **LR decay can push training out of EoS, sometimes permanently**: with peak LR 0.002, no post-drop checkpoint is at EoS for any of the drop ratios 0.6/0.45/0.3 (median q = 1.3 and 1.4 for ratios 0.6 and 0.45; for ratio 0.3 no LR factor explodes at 35 of 41 checkpoints); with drop ratio 0.7, EoS returns at 26/36 checkpoints for peak LR 0.008 but at 0 of the checkpoints for peak LR 0.0005. During linear decay, −λ̂min falls from ~850 to 56–540 once the LR is below half its peak, and saddleness drops from ~0.09 to ≤ 0.03.
- **Too-large peak LRs overshoot EoS into an extremely unstable regime**: at peak LR 0.04 (vs the swept-optimal 0.004 for GPT-extra-small on FineWeb-10B), the estimated critical LR is only about 1/10 of the training LR on average, the compensated simulations explode at the training LR, and final eval loss rises to ~3.2 versus ~2.45 near the optimal LR (a ~0.75-nat degradation).
- **Detector validation on SGD/CIFAR-10**: on ResNet-20/32 trained with SGD (MSE loss, batch 1024, WD 5×10⁻⁴, 50 epochs), the bounded-to-exploding boundary sits at LR factor 1.0 across most mid-training checkpoints — the classic EoS regime, confirming the detector recovers the known phenomenon.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Genuine and well-scoped. Splitting quadratic-approximation failure into an LR-independent mode (EoC, from negative curvature — long reported by Sagun et al. but never framed as a stability-level failure of the quadratic model) and an LR-dependent stochastic EoS is a clean conceptual contribution, and the mean-square-stability definition that provably recovers the full-batch 2/λmax threshold is the right generalization. The EBZ–CBZ correlation is a genuinely new empirical finding. That said, the largest model is 787M — far from the 7B–100B+ scale the phrase "LLM pre-training" evokes; the title's claim is extrapolated more than tested.
- **Rigor**: The theory (moment dynamics, projected stability, AGM extension) is carefully stated with proofs in appendices, and the measurement hygiene is better than most empirical optimization work — e.g., explicitly correcting the Jensen downward bias of mini-batch Lanczos λmin estimates, and explaining (Appendix B.1) that rare tokens under Adam inflate the preconditioned top eigenvalues via 1/√v̂, which forced an 8,192-token vocabulary instead of the standard GPT-2 50,257. But that same fix is a red flag for external validity: the curvature landscape was partly reshaped to make measurement tractable, and 5.7% of the detection shard's sequences (4,596 of 97,656) were filtered out, with detection run on a held-out shard rather than the training data — so the declared EoS/EoC states are properties of a curated measurement regime, not directly of the training runs.
- **Threshold arbitrariness**: several load-bearing constants are heuristic and their sensitivity is only partially explored — the explosion cutoff M = 10⁵, the EBZ definition as the batch size where the fitted mean-q curve crosses 1.1 (a 10% margin), CBZ as "within 0.3% of best final eval loss," and very short simulation horizons (25 local steps for ≥104M models, 50 for 43M). A compensation-coefficient robustness check exists (Figure 10), but a wrong-side error analysis for η̂⋆ near the boundary is not presented.
- **EoC is quasi-tautological as reported**: since EoC is observed at essentially every checkpoint (saddleness ≤ 0.1 whenever λmin < 0), it functions as a measurement ("the Hessian is slightly non-convex everywhere") rather than a discriminating regime; no evidence links EoC magnitude to training outcomes, loss spikes, or LR choice.
- **Reproducibility**: appendices document architectures, scaling rules, and detection hyperparameters unusually well, but no code or run artifacts are released, and the compute cost (100+ pretraining runs) puts replication beyond most academic groups. Evaluations use final eval loss only — no downstream benchmarks — which is defensible for an optimization study but limits practical interpretation of the CBZ comparisons.
- **Significance**: The negative result that EoS is *not* universal in LLM training (it vanishes at small batch and after LR decay) usefully punctures the folklore that training always self-tunes to λmax ≈ 2/η, and gives the first controlled map of when the quadratic approximation can be trusted at pretraining scale.

## Strengths

- Clean decomposition of quadratic-approximation failure into two orthogonal mechanisms (negative vs. positive curvature), each with a precise, testable definition that provably recovers the classical full-batch EoS as a special case.
- The mean-square-stability formulation via the second-moment operator E[M⊗M] = E[M]⊗E[M] + Cov(M) correctly identifies mini-batch Hessian covariance — not λmax alone — as the object governing stochastic EoS, fixing a real gap in prior EoS theory.
- Scalable, theoretically grounded detection (stochastic power iteration + compensated local simulation) that makes Hessian-level measurements feasible at 787M parameters and 3,000+ checkpoints.
- The EBZ–CBZ correlation (Pearson r = 0.95 across five corpora) is a crisp, surprising, and practically useful finding linking a stability phenomenon to the well-known critical-batch-size scaling picture.
- Honest, detailed reporting of measurement pitfalls (Jensen bias, rare-token distortion under Adam's second moments) that future empirical Hessian studies will need to copy.

## Limitations

- Model scale caps at 787M parameters and ~20 tokens/parameter on a single-corpus setup per run; whether EoS prevalence and the EBZ threshold persist at production scales (7B+) and standard tokenizers (50k vocab) is untested.
- The 8,192-token vocabulary and rare-token filtering — adopted to keep Adam's preconditioned curvature measurable — mean results may not transfer to standard LLM configurations where rare-token rows demonstrably dominate the top preconditioned eigenvalues.
- Detection thresholds (explosion cutoff 10⁵, EBZ crossing at q = 1.1, CBZ within 0.3% of best loss, 25–50-step simulation horizons) are heuristic; sensitivity is explored only for the compensation coefficient, not for the other constants.
- Only SGD (CIFAR-10 sanity check) and AdamW are studied; the paper itself flags Muon and other modern optimizers as open, and no downstream-task evaluations or loss-spike case studies are provided.
- EoC is observed at every checkpoint, so as a "regime" it does not yet discriminate anything about training behavior; its practical consequences remain uncharacterized.

## Relevance

本文直击深度学习优化理论与现代 LLM 训练实践之间的裂缝——二次逼近/下降引理在何处失效、何时可信，对理解训练稳定性、LR 与 batch size 调度设计（如 LR 衰减何时真正"稳定"训练、EoS batch size 与 critical batch size 的定量关联）都有直接启发，是训练动力学与 scaling 研究方向的优质参考。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the EBZ–CBZ correlation (Pearson r = 0.95) survive at 1B–10B parameter scale, with standard 50k-vocab tokenizers and realistic data mixes, or is it partly an artifact of the curated 8k-vocab detection regime?
- Can the compensated local simulation be turned into an *online* stability monitor — e.g., predicting loss spikes before they happen, or adaptively scheduling LR decay exactly when the critical-LR factor q approaches 1?
- How do EoC and EoS behave under optimizers with orthogonal/structured preconditioners such as Muon, or under non-uniform LR parameter groups (WSD-style per-block scaling), which the AGM framework may not cover?
- Is the persistent small negative curvature a finite-width phenomenon or a data-property? Does saddleness ν shrink toward zero as width grows, and does that change EoC from a measurement curiosity into a controllable failure mode?
- Since stochastic EoS is governed by the covariance of mini-batch Hessians, can gradient-noise-shape interventions (gradient noise scale estimation, curvature-aware batch sampling, SAM-style perturbation) deliberately move the critical LR and thereby raise the usable LR at a given batch size?
