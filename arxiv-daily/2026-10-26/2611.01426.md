> **一句话总结：** FlyWheel 针对 TPU 的三大瓶颈（MXU 操作数装载、向量单元开销、依赖导致的空转）重写 softmax 与 Gated DeltaNet prefill 内核：用头主序布局提升矩阵操作数复用、用"带校验的静态锚点"softmax 和 8 次 BF16 矩阵乘的分层三角求逆消减向量计算、再用显式软件流水掩盖延迟；内核层面相对 RPA-v3 最高加速 4.14×（达 v6e 峰值 BF16 吞吐的 81%），GDN prefill 最高 3.61×，集成进 vLLM 服务 Qwen3.8-27B 后 512K RULER prefill 吞吐提升 1.53× 且精度不变。

> **TL;DR:** TPU attention kernels leave the matrix units (MXUs) idle — the SOTA RPA-v3 and Splash-Attention kernels reach only 18–73% of roofline throughput on causal prefill. FlyWheel (Pallas/JAX) fixes this with (1) coordinated head-major layouts so projections feed/consume the kernel layout directly and each K/V load serves 256 query rows, (2) a checked static-anchor softmax that drops online rescaling (one `l ≤ Λ` comparison per block certifies a row; failing blocks replay in the same launch), plus an 8-BF16-matmul radix-4 triangular inverse that replaces GDN's forward substitution, and (3) explicit fragment/chunk software pipelining that the TPU's single-stream compiler can schedule. On causal prefill benchmarks (D=256, 32 heads) it beats RPA-v3 by up to 4.14× and Splash-Attention by up to 1.80×, reaching 744 TFLOP/s = 81% of v6e peak (vs 668 for Splash, 228 for RPA-v3 MHA at 128K); GDN prefill gains up to 3.61× over GDN-v3 (9.9M vs 3.0M tokens/s at the Qwen3.8-27B shape). In vLLM serving Qwen3.8-27B, RULER prefill throughput improves 1.53× at 512K and Video-MME LLM prefill 1.29× (11.60→9.00 s at 2048 frames), with both runs scoring 100% on all evaluated RULER tasks. Worth reading if you care about TPU systems, linear-attention serving, or kernel-level numerical tricks; skip if you only need GPU kernel news.

## 核心贡献

- **系统性地诊断了 TPU 注意力内核的三大架构瓶颈**：权重固定式（weight-stationary）MXU 的操作数装载必须被摊销、向量单元吞吐远低于矩阵单元（softmax 归约/重缩放、GDN 前代换成为关键路径）、以及 TPU TensorCore 单指令流模型下编译器需要显式暴露的独立工作才能重叠执行——并解释了 FlashAttention-3/4 等 GPU 优化为何不能直接迁移。
- **softmax 注意力内核**：头主序（head-major）布局把同一头的两个 token 打包进一个 sublane（每寄存器 16 个 token、MHA 下零填充），256 行查询 tile 使每次 K/V 装载服务 256 行；ragged 序列边界在片上通过对齐窗口 + 回填前序 VMEM 行解决；投影直接产出/消费内核布局，消除 HBM 布局转换。
- **带校验的静态锚点 softmax**：以首块得分为锚点累积 `e^{s-a}`，免去逐块 running-max 重缩放；每块后仅比较一次 `l ≤ Λ`（该 FP32 和本身界定每个权重，NaN/Inf 也触发），失败块在同一 launch 内做带 margin-τ 锚点更新的 guarded replay——比 FlashAttention-4 的逐片段条件重缩放更省向量工作。
- **GDN 内核**：跨算子复用矩阵操作数（`[βK; Q]Kᵀ` 同时产出 A_kk 与 A_qk，`[Kᵀ; A_qk]V_new` 同时产出状态增量与块内输出）；用分层三角求逆（4×4 块 `(I-L)(I+L²)` 起、radix-4 合并 `J⁻¹(I-N)(I+N²)`，共 8 次 BF16 矩阵乘 + FP32 累加）替换前代换；因果卷积改写为 0/1 选择子矩阵乘；跨 chunk 显式流水让块局部求逆与相邻 chunk 的状态相关计算重叠。
- **充分的评估与消融**：在 v6e/v7x 上的内核基准、792 格全形状网格、vLLM 端到端服务（RULER/BABILong/Video-MME）与精度对齐验证，并把每一项设计的增量贡献拆解清楚（softmax：353→720 TFLOP/s；GDN：四项设计合计 3.19–3.53×）。

## Key Contributions

- **Bottleneck analysis grounded in TPU architecture.** The paper identifies three TPU-specific limiters of attention kernels — (1) layout/operand-reuse constraints of weight-stationary MXUs (operand loading must be amortized even on chip; RPA-v3's all-head query tiles waste half of each BF16 register under MHA), (2) the vector-unit bottleneck between the two matmuls (softmax reductions/rescaling, GDN forward substitution, convolution/norm), and (3) the single-threaded VLIW TensorCore model, where overlap must be exposed as independent work in program order rather than via GPU warp scheduling. It explains concretely why FlashAttention-3's async-matmul pipelining and FlashAttention-4's conditional rescaling do not transfer (FA4's extra state offsets its vector savings in v6e measurements).
- **Coordinated layouts across operator boundaries.** A head-major layout packs two tokens of the same head per sublane (16 tokens/register, no MHA padding) and decouples tile size from head count (256 query rows per tile, so each stationary K/V load serves 256 rows). Ragged sequence starts are handled on chip by snapping the DMA window to alignment and restoring the preceding rows from the previous VMEM buffer; QKV/output projections produce and consume the kernel's layout directly, eliminating the baseline's HBM gathers/transposes (33.7 ms of layout work for RPA-v3 at 128K MHA).
- **Checked static-anchor softmax.** Replaces online softmax with a per-row anchor fixed at the first K/V tile's max score; accumulates unnormalized weights whose FP32 sum `l` bounds every weight and the output, so a single `l ≤ Λ` check per block certifies numerical range (NaN/+∞ also fail it). Failing blocks are recomputed by a guarded replay inside the same launch with anchor updates only when a fragment max exceeds the anchor by margin τ, with an explicit range condition `T·e^τ·max(1,V_max) ≤ F·e^−δ`. Unlike FlashDecoding++/VFA/Splash (fixed max without checks, or offline statistics) and concurrent Blackwell work (second-launch recompute), FlyWheel's anchor is a score the row attains and its check adds no per-fragment reduction.
- **GDN reformulation onto the MXU.** Stacks the four chunk products into two shared-operand products, replaces forward substitution with a radix-4 hierarchical inverse of the 64×64 unit lower-triangular matrix — 4×4 diagonal blocks inverted exactly via `(I+L)⁻¹ = (I−L)(I+L²)`, then two merge levels via `U⁻¹ = J⁻¹(I−N)(I+N²)` with `N⁴ = 0`, eight BF16 matmuls total with FP32 accumulation — and casts the 4-tap causal convolution as a constant 0/1-selector matmul.
- **Explicit software pipelining.** For softmax, each fragment's PV matmul is deferred to interleave with the next fragment's QK matmul and exponentials inside one compiler basic block (pipelining continues across head boundaries). For GDN, chunk-local inverse work overlaps adjacent chunks' state-dependent computation, with two heads packed per operand load to create nv/2 independent inverse chains. Mosaic's LP LLO scheduler alone cannot recover this overlap (320 TFLOP/s vs 582 with explicit pipelining).

## Methodology

The paper follows a measure-diagnose-redesign loop on TPU v6e (Trillium, one chip: 918 TFLOP/s BF16 peak, 128 MiB VMEM, 1.6 TB/s HBM) and v7x (Ironwood, one TensorCore: 1153 TFLOP/s, 64 MiB VMEM, 3.7 TB/s HBM), implemented in JAX Pallas (JAX 0.11.0, libtpu 0.0.44).

**Softmax attention (§3.1).** Inputs arrive as [H, T, D]; a 2048×2048 block holds 32 fragments of 256 query rows × 512 key rows. The static-anchor pass (Algorithm 1) computes `p = e^{s−a}`, accumulates `l` and `o = o + pV_b` with no rescaling, and checks `l ≤ Λ` at block boundaries; Algorithm 2 (guarded replay) recomputes only the failing block from its saved entry state, updating the anchor per row only when the fragment max exceeds it by τ ≥ 0 (exp2 is used, with the margin converted to base-2 units). Correctness of the final `o/l` division is exact in real arithmetic because the anchor cancels; the check only guards floating-point range.

**Gated DeltaNet (§3.2).** Chunks of C=64 tokens are processed in 128-row tiles of two chunks. The kernel consumes the projection's BF16 output directly (GDN-v3 instead requires FP32 [T,1,dim] inputs plus HBM upcast/retiling in its wrapper), forms A_kk and A_qk in one stacked product, and obtains V_new, the state increment ΔS, and intra-chunk output through stacked shared-operand products. Algorithm 4 computes the hierarchical inverse: two matmuls per 4×4 base block, then three matmuls per radix-4 merge (16×16, then 64×64), exploiting that `J⁻¹N` and `N²` share the right operand N. The 4-tap causal convolution becomes `Y = S_conv·W` where W stacks tap-scaled inputs and halo rows and S_conv is a constant {0,1} selector — trading MXU arithmetic for fewer vector shifts. SiLU and L2 normalization each map to a single EUP operation. Cross-chunk pipelining (Algorithm 3) interleaves chunk c's inverse with chunk c+1's stacked score product and chunk c−1's state-dependent work while preserving state-update order.

**Evaluation methodology (§4).** Kernel benchmarks measure median device time over 5 back-to-back rounds with analytic FLOPs (4·B·T²·H·D, halved for causal) against a profiler trace, with an idealized roofline (read Q,K,V once, write O once; ridge points 574 FLOP/B on v6e, 312 on v7x). Both kernel-only and full-attention-block (projections + layout included) timings are reported. A 792-cell grid sweeps H ∈ {16,32}, D ∈ {128,256}, every KV-head count, B ∈ {1..128}, T ∈ {1K..128K}. End-to-end, FlyWheel replaces RPA-v3, GDN-v3, and the vision-encoder Pallas kernels in vLLM (env-switched within one build) serving Qwen3.8-27B — a hybrid VLM with 48 GDN layers (48 value heads, head dim 128) and 16 softmax layers (24 query / 4 KV heads, head dim 256) — on v6e-8 (TP 8) and v6e-16 (TP 8 + CP 2 at 512K) and a 4-chip v7x host, evaluated on RULER (4K–512K), BABILong qa1–qa5 (128K–512K), and Video-MME's 674 long-video questions (1024/2048 frames ≈ 113K/225K visual tokens), with YaRN ×2 beyond native context. Ablations (§5) isolate each design's increment, and Appendix D extends the design to decode: paged KV caches, speculative verify (window folded into query heads), FP8 KV caches, and ReplaySSM-based linear-attention decode.

## Results & Highlights

- **Softmax kernel-only speedups (causal prefill, D=256, 32 query heads, 2K–128K tokens):** on v6e MHA FlyWheel is 3.11–3.49× faster than RPA-v3 and 1.11–1.56× (GQA 1.50–1.73× / 1.10–1.41× vs the two baselines); on one v7x TensorCore MHA it reaches **4.14× over RPA-v3 at 128K** (3.57–4.14× from 4K) and **1.80× over Splash-Attention at 2K** (1.18–1.80×). The one losing cell is 1K MHA on v6e, where FlyWheel is 0.87× Splash-Attention.
- **Roofline utilization at 128K MHA:** FlyWheel reaches **744 TFLOP/s = 81% of v6e's 918 TFLOP/s BF16 peak**, vs 73% (668 TFLOP/s) for Splash-Attention and 53% (490 TFLOP/s, GQA) for RPA-v3 — and RPA-v3's MHA throughput at 128K on v6e is only 228 TFLOP/s, less than half its own GQA number. On v7x, FlyWheel reaches 802 TFLOP/s = 70% vs 59% (Splash) and 43% (RPA-v3). From 8K tokens on, FlyWheel's MHA and GQA throughputs differ by at most 1.2%.
- **GDN prefill:** on v6e FlyWheel is **up to 3.61× faster than GDN-v3** (nv=32, 8–16K tokens; 1.84–3.61× overall, 1.56–2.62× on v7x). At nv=48 — the Qwen3.8-27B shape — it prefills **9.9M tokens/s at 16K vs 3.0M tokens/s for GDN-v3** (0.847 ms vs 2.75 ms per 8K-token call). Per-token cost is 3.4–3.8× lower (163 μs vs 45 μs per 1K tokens at nv=16). Strikingly, FlyWheel on a v6e chip is 2.3–2.6× faster than GDN-v3 running on a v7x TensorCore, despite the latter's 2.3× HBM bandwidth and 1.26× peak FLOP/s.
- **End-to-end, vLLM serving Qwen3.8-27B:** on v6e, RULER prefill throughput improves from 1.07× at 4K to **1.53× at 512K**; BABILong improves 1.22×→1.56× from 128K→512K; Video-MME LLM prefill drops from **4.80 s to 3.87 s at 1024 frames (1.24×) and 11.60 s to 9.00 s at 2048 frames (1.29×)**. On v7x, RULER reaches 1.35× at 512K (23.96K vs 17.70K tokens/s) and Video-MME 1.24× (9.77→7.88 s). On the v6e-8 slice from 32K→256K, baseline throughput falls 21% (24.3K→19.1K tokens/s) while FlyWheel retains 93% (27.0K→25.1K).
- **Accuracy parity:** on v6e both baseline and FlyWheel runs score **100% on all evaluated RULER tasks and lengths** and identical BABILong scores; Video-MME is 70.62 for both at 2048 frames and differs by one of 674 questions at 1024 frames (71.66 vs 71.51). On v7x (20 samples/task), mean RULER differs by ≤0.8 points and BABILong by ≤1 point at 512K.
- **Ablations decompose the gains:** on v6e at 32K MHA, static anchor + pipeline = 720 TFLOP/s vs 353 for online softmax without pipelining (**2.04× from the two softmax designs**); the static anchor alone is 26% faster than FA4-style conditional rescaling with pipelining (720 vs 570 TFLOP/s) and 50% faster without (521 vs 348). The GDN ablation totals 3.19–3.53×: coordinated layouts 1.52–1.68×, operand reuse 1.06–1.22×, reduced vector work 1.51–1.58×, explicit pipelining 1.13–1.25×.
- **Robustness across shapes:** in the 792-cell sweep (head counts, head dims 128/256, batch 1–128, length 1K–128K), FlyWheel is faster than both baselines in 785 cells (median 1.30×), and the 7 exceptions are all 1K/D=256 within 9% of Splash-Attention; throughput depends on total token count, not batch division (median deviation 1.0% vs B=1). Full-attention-block time on v6e MHA improves 1.48× at 1K to 2.95× at 128K vs RPA-v3 (466 vs 1371 ms).
- **Decode (Appendix D):** BF16 decode on v7x reads the cache at 3.20–3.36 TB/s (86–91% of the 3.7 TB/s HBM peak) vs 2.95–3.23 TB/s for RPA-v3, up to 1.14× faster; speculative verify beats Batched-RPA by up to 1.62× (T=16, 8192 cached, batch 32: 0.483 vs 0.784 ms); an FP8 KV cache halves the v6e decode step (1.98× at batch 32); ReplaySSM-style GDN decode is 1.77–2.32× faster than the conventional kernel and 4-token verify 3.26–3.72×.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: solid, incremental-but-real.** The two headline numerical devices — checked static-anchor softmax and radix-4 BF16 hierarchical triangular inversion — are refinements of known ideas (fixed-max softmax in FlashDecoding++/VFA/Splash; matmul-only triangular inversion in Sobczyk et al. and KDA's kernel), and the paper is candid about this lineage. The genuinely new parts are the TPU-specific synthesis (layout coordination across operator boundaries, in-launch guarded replay certified by `l` alone, distance-1 fragment pipelining in one compiler basic block) and the demonstration that these close a 2× roofline gap that GPU-derived techniques do not.
- **Rigor: high, with two honest soft spots.** (1) The 4.14× headline is against RPA-v3, whose MHA layout wastes half of each BF16 register — a chunk of the headline comes from a weak-baseline quirk; against the stronger Splash-Attention the kernel-only gain is 1.10–1.80×, the full-block gain only 1.01–1.15×, and at 1K Splash's block is actually 4% faster (kernel-only 0.87× on v6e MHA). The paper reports all of this, but the abstract leads with the 4.14×. (2) The v7x roofline tops out at 70% vs 81% on v6e because the 64 MiB VMEM forces single-chunk 64-row tiles at nv=48, disabling cross-chunk pipelining — the design is measurably coupled to v6e's 128 MiB VMEM, and the paper discloses the resulting drop rather than hiding it.
- **Reproducibility: good.** Pallas/JAX kernels with stated versions (JAX 0.11.0, libtpu 0.0.44), commit-pinned baselines, an env-switch A/B within one vLLM build, analytic-FLOP throughput definitions, and a linked code release. Main barrier is hardware access (v7x), not information.
- **Significance: strong for the TPU serving ecosystem.** The result that FlyWheel-on-v6e beats GDN-v3-on-v7x by 2.3–2.6× is a concrete "software beats a hardware generation" data point, and the techniques (operand stacking, checked fixed-anchor softmax, matmul-form inversion) are portable lessons for other weight-stationary/systolic accelerators.
- **Red flags: modest but real.** (a) Accuracy validation is thin relative to the numerical risk: the BF16 approximate inverse has max absolute error 9×10⁻³ vs FP64 on a repeated-key test, yet evaluation covers only RULER/BABILong qa1–qa5 and 674 Video-MME questions, with no perplexity or broader task sweep; the v7x accuracy check used just 20 samples per task (8 Video-MME questions), so "identical accuracy" there is within sampling noise. (b) End-to-end gains vanish at short context (0.98× at 4K on v7x — slightly below baseline). (c) ReplaySSM decode numbers compare against the authors' own conventional kernel rather than an independent baseline. (d) Decode — the regime where serving cost often concentrates — gains at most 1.14×, since it is bandwidth-bound; all headline claims are prefill. (e) No absolute cross-platform comparison (e.g., FlashAttention-3/4 on H100/B200 at the same lengths), so the reader cannot situate TPU-vs-GPU cost from this paper alone.

## Strengths

- Correct, architecture-grounded diagnosis of *why* TPU attention stalls (operand amortization on weight-stationary MXUs, vector-unit imbalance, compiler-only scheduling), with each fix mapped to a measured bottleneck.
- The checked static-anchor softmax is an elegant numerics-systems co-design: one FP32 comparison per row per block certifies range (the sum bounds every weight and the output, and NaN/+∞ fail the check), and the fallback recomputes only the failing block in the same launch — cheaper than FA4's per-fragment conditional rescaling on this hardware, and safer than VFA/Splash's unchecked fixed maxima.
- Comprehensive evaluation: 792-cell shape grid, roofline analysis, block-level timing that exposes baseline layout overhead (33.7 ms at 128K), incremental ablations multiplying out to the total speedup, and two TPU generations.
- End-to-end discipline: A/B via an env switch in one vLLM build, accuracy parity checked on the very benchmarks being timed, and honest reporting of losing cells (1K vs Splash, v7x short contexts, v7x single-chunk tile drop).
- Bonus coverage beyond prefill: paged-KV decode, speculative verify (up to 1.62× vs Batched-RPA), FP8 KV caches (1.98× decode step), and ReplaySSM linear-attention decode (up to 3.72× on 4-token verify).

## Limitations

- Short-context regime: below ~4K tokens the gains are marginal (1.07× RULER at 4K on v6e; 0.98× at 4K on v7x; behind Splash at 1K MHA), since projections and other operators dominate — the win is specifically a long-context prefill win.
- Hardware coupling: the 128-row two-chunk GDN tile and the 2048×2048 softmax block are tuned to v6e's 128 MiB VMEM; v7x's 64 MiB forces 64-row tiles that break cross-chunk pipelining at nv=48 and cap utilization at 70% of roofline.
- Numerical shortcuts are validated narrowly: the BF16 inverse's 9×10⁻³ error and the anchor-replay path are tested on one hybrid model and three benchmarks; behavior under other linear-attention models, quantized serving, or training gradients is untested.
- Decode and speculative-decoding gains are modest (≤1.14× BF16 decode) because decode is bandwidth-bound; the paper's own analysis shows the techniques target the compute-bound prefill phase.
- No external GPU anchor point (FlashAttention-3/4 absolute throughput or $/token), so the practical TPU-vs-GPU serving decision cannot be made from this paper; the roofline traffic model is idealized (Q,K,V read once).
- Engineering specificity: kernels are written against a particular Pallas/libtpu toolchain with commit-pinned baselines; portability to other compilers or TPU generations is asserted rather than shown, and the C=64 chunk size is baked into the 8-matmul inverse.

## Relevance

对高效推理与注意力内核优化方向高度相关：本文给出了 TPU 上 softmax 与线性注意力（Gated DeltaNet）prefill 内核的系统性优化范式（布局协同、数值重表述、显式流水），其"用带校验的静态锚点替代在线 softmax 重缩放"和"分层矩阵三角求逆"思路对 GPU/加速器上的 FlashAttention 系与线性注意力内核同样有借鉴意义；对关注长上下文服务（512K）、vLLM 部署与混合架构模型（GDN+softmax，如 Kimi Linear、Qwen 混合模型）的工作极具参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How often does the guarded replay actually trigger in production serving across models, quantization schemes, and prompt distributions — and what is its worst-case tail latency relative to the always-rescale baselines?
- Does the BF16 approximate triangular inverse accumulate error over deeper GDN stacks, longer training runs, or other delta-rule models (Kimi Linear's full hybrid, Mamba-2 variants), and does a mixed BF16/FP32 schedule recover exactness at a small throughput cost?
- Can the v7x limitation be lifted — e.g., FP8 intermediates, VMEM-aware chunk splitting, or spilling only the recurrent state — to restore cross-chunk pipelining within 64 MiB and push past 70% of roofline?
- What is the absolute cross-platform picture: FlyWheel on v6e/v7x vs FlashAttention-3/4 on H100/B200 at the same model, lengths, and $/token, including the memory-bound decode phase where FlyWheel's edge shrinks?
- Do the layout-coordination techniques (projections emitting the attention kernel's layout, selector-matrix convolution) transfer to other operators — MLPs, MoE expert dispatch, or conv stems — or to PyTorch/XLA graphs outside vLLM?
- Can the checked static-anchor softmax be adapted to training kernels, where backward-pass sensitivity to the anchor choice and replay divergence may differ from prefill inference?
