Why this matters

RAG systems can silently resolve conflicting evidence without telling the user — this offers a deployable internal audit for hidden source commitment, readable before any text is generated.

Critical edge

The “steering can surface it” claim rests entirely on one 0.8B model, and even the best steering arm leaves 56/109 (51.4%) of disclosure failures still one-sided.

also: single short-factual conflict format; LLM-judge labels with manual, non-blinded audits

01Methodology — the audit pipeline

1

Build conflict QA. FaithEval-inconsistent: each question gets two documents filling the same slot with incompatible answers. Order swaps + closed-book recall proxy (81.6% parametric-unknown) strip truth, position, and memory confounds — the target is document choice, not correctness.

2

Fit a readout direction. Class-centroid mean-difference vector (μ₊−μ₋) on layer-13 residual states at the mean decision token. Deliberately not a logistic probe — the same vector is reused for steering.

3

Steer causally. Inject δ·RMS(xₜ)·v̂ through a residual pre-hook over the last 32 prompt tokens plus the decision window, flipping which source the answer uses.

4

Localize sparse writers. CETT per-neuron contribution scoring → L1 logistic over five balanced splits → ~156 sign-stable neurons (layers 11–14), then project their actual MLP writes onto the learned direction.

5

Read disclosure separately. A layer-17 pre-response readout; an LLM judge (temperature 0, JSON schema) labels decided / leaning / unresolved disclosure behavior.

Models: Qwen3.5-0.8B (24-layer hybrid Gated DeltaNet / attention, T=0) · transfer check Gemma 3 1B (independently fitted layer-12 direction)
Hygiene: qid-level splits (no leakage into direction fitting) · layer-matched random writer sets · exact-copy / paraphrase / single-document controls

03Strengths

  • Separates three events RAG conflates — registering disagreement, picking a source, disclosing it — and shows the first and third dissociate in the residual stream.
  • Full causal chain readout → bidirectional steering → sparse writer localization → write-projection alignment → specificity controls, each with matched random baselines.
  • Genuine second-family transfer on Gemma 3 1B with an independently fitted direction, not a copied pipeline.
  • Exceptional honesty: a failure-case table explicitly downgrades overclaimable results; a dual-use statement addresses inverted use for disclosure suppression.

04Limitations

  • Sub-1B models only; the hybrid DeltaNet backbone means layer-13 / L11H3 localization may not transfer architecturally.
  • Disclosure steering is Qwen-only; Gemma contributes readout/timing checks, and its source steering is low-rate (28/960).
  • One conflict format (short factual FaithEval disagreements); WikiContradict cited but unused — long-document contradiction untested.
  • LLM-judge disclosure labels with manual calibration views, not blinded agreement studies; leaning/unresolved boundary is noisy.
  • Remedy is partial: 56/109 prompt-failures stay one-sided under the best arm; directions are model-specific.

05Questions for further exploration

  • Does the direction survive at 7B–70B, and does its layer track where each architecture resolves source competition?
  • Is the layer-17/layer-23 two-direction disclosure interaction replicable in Gemma or Llama — or a Qwen-specific accident?
  • How does the readout behave on real long-document contradictions (WikiContradict) rather than two short slot-filling passages?
  • Would logit-lens margins alone (no generation-time access) suffice as a cheap deployment monitor, at what false-positive rate?
  • Can the write-projection bridge extend to a full circuit — head L11H3 recovers only ~0.65 of the localized state?
  • Does inverting the disclosure direction actually suppress acknowledgment, and is the claimed defender-favoring asymmetry real?
