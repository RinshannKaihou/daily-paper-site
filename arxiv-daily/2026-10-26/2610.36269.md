> **一句话总结：** 在 UniAR、Emu3.5、Liquid 三个统一多模态模型上,作者证明从单一模态最小对提取的线性转向向量可以零样本、双向地控制另一模态的生成(UniAR 中恢复 84% 图像 / 74% 文本的原模态转向效果),并发现跨模态可迁移性与文本/图像向量的余弦相似度相关——尽管两种模态的表征占据明显分离的子空间。

> **TL;DR:** This paper shows that steering vectors extracted purely from text minimal-pairs can zero-shot steer image generation and vice versa in unified multimodal models (UMMs) that generate both modalities autoregressively from shared weights. On the UniAR (8B) testbed, the transferred vector recovers 84% of the intra-modality (img→img baseline) steering effect on images and 74% on text; in Emu3.5 (34B) and Liquid (7B), text-derived vectors steer images even better than image-derived ones (recovery 1.10/1.20), though the authors attribute this to weak image-side baselines (img→img AUC only 0.44/0.43 vs 0.83 in UniAR) rather than exceptional transfer. Prior work (Wang & Li, 2026, on BAGEL) reported transfer only in the txt→img direction — the bidirectional result plus a geometric signature (cosine similarity between text/image vectors correlates with transfer, Spearman ρ=0.38, up to 0.76 in UniAR txt→img) is the new contribution. Read it if you care about interpretability, steering, or whether unified multimodal models are actually unified inside.

## 核心贡献

- **提出并系统研究"跨模态转向"(cross-modal steering)问题**:在统一多模态模型(UMM)中,仅用某一模态的 token 提取转向向量,不做任何适配直接加到另一模态的 token 上,能否零样本控制另一模态的生成。由于转向是因果干预,这比表征对齐(相关性度量)更直接地回答"概念是否跨模态统一"。
- **证明双向迁移成立**:在 UniAR (8B)、Emu3.5 (34B)、Liquid (7B) 三个共享权重的自回归 UMM 上,除 spatial(左右)外的所有概念,文本向量转向图像都能恢复 >80% 的原模态效果;推翻了此前"图像向量难以转向文本"的单向结论(Wang & Li, 2026)。
- **揭示跨模态转向的几何签名**:文本向量与图像向量的余弦相似度与迁移效果正相关(总体 Spearman ρ=0.38, p<0.01);同时模态间隙依然显著——同一极性的图像与句子表征相距很远,但两个子空间内部的相对方向结构是对齐的(类比词嵌入中的 king−queen 偏移正则性)。
- **模态独占概念的定性发现**:纯语言的"正式 vs 口语"、"英语 vs 法语"向量能转向图像(正装、黑白极简、法式历史风格);纯视觉的"低分辨率"向量能让文本变短、提及"模糊"并表达困惑——为模型如何弥合视觉与语言提供了一扇窗口。
- **完整开源与可复现**:代码、最小对数据、转向向量、生成结果与判分全部公开(README 中给出 GitHub 与 HuggingFace 链接),判分模型与人类标注的一致性(Cohen's κ=0.62)经过验证。

## Key Contributions

- **Formulates cross-modal steering as a causal probe of modality unification.** A vector found on tokens of one modality is added, with zero adaptation, to tokens of the other during generation in UMMs where text and image tokens share one autoregressive backbone — going beyond correlational representational-alignment measures.
- **Demonstrates bidirectional zero-shot transfer** across three UMMs (UniAR 8B, Emu3.5 34B, Liquid 7B) and eight concepts (emotion, age, cleanness, chaos, size, depth, spatial, color), contradicting the strong asymmetry reported by prior work on BAGEL (Wang & Li, 2026), where image-derived vectors failed to steer text.
- **Identifies a geometric signature of transfer**: cosine similarity between the text-derived and image-derived vectors of a concept correlates with cross-modal recovery, even though the two modalities occupy nearly disjoint subspaces of activation space.
- **Case studies on modality-unique concepts**: a formal-vs-colloquial (text-only) vector makes generated images more formally dressed / black-and-white / luxuriously minimalist; an English-vs-French vector induces ornate historical French visuals; a low-resolution (image-only) vector makes the model write shorter, hedging, "blurry"-mentioning text.
- **Practical takeaway for model control**: a steering vector can be built in whichever modality is easier (minimal text pairs are far cheaper to curate than minimal image pairs) and then applied to the other — including for safety-style interventions, which the authors argue should transfer across modalities.

## Methodology

The study targets unified multimodal models that generate a mixed sequence of text and discrete image tokens with a single next-token objective and shared parameters: **UniAR (8B)** (semantic image tokens from a language-aligned vision encoder), **Emu3.5 (34B)** and **Liquid (7B)** (pixel-reconstruction image tokens).

**Vector extraction.** For each concept pole pair (e.g., happy/sad), N=100 minimal pairs are constructed per modality. Text pairs are auto-generated with Gemini 2.5 Flash filling a fixed contrastive template ("a person is happy" vs "a person is sad", attribute as the last token); the last-token hidden state serves as the representation. Image pairs start from Gemini-generated baseline scenes, edited toward each pole with instruction-based editors (Gemini 2.5 Flash Image; Gemini 3 Pro Image and Reve for size/depth/spatial where edits are harder), keeping subject/pose/framing fixed; hidden states are mean-pooled over image tokens. The steering vector at layer ℓ is the mean activation difference, normalized to unit length.

**Application.** During generation, v̂ℓ is added to hidden states of all generated tokens (and the prompt subject phrase for text): h′ℓ = hℓ + α·v̂ℓ, with α swept over per-setting strength grids extended just past the breakdown point. Steering is applied at early/middle/late ranges of 4 consecutive layers (8 for the deeper Emu3.5), e.g., layers 7–10 / 16–19 / 25–28 of UniAR's 36. The same intervention applies regardless of which modality the vector came from or which modality is being generated — the four settings are txt→txt, img→img (intra-modality references) and txt→img, img→txt (transfer).

**Evaluation.** Per concept, 20 neutral scene prompts ("Describe your day in the city. Your description must include {subject}." for text). A blind LLM judge (Gemini 3.7 Flash, A/B/Tie, randomly named pole, never sees α or layers) yields a **concept rate**; its normalized area under the curve over α is AUC. **Cross-modal recovery** = transfer AUC / intra-modality AUC (1.0 = transferred vector as good as native; each vector evaluated over its own tolerated strength range). Output quality is tracked with an AxBench-style fluency/coherence judge, plus a DreamSim-based pole score for images. The concept-rate judge was validated against 3-way human majority on 320 held-out pairs (Cohen's κ = 0.62).

## Results & Highlights

- **Bidirectional transfer works in all three models.** In UniAR at middle layers, the transferred vector recovers **84% of the intra-modality steering effect on images (Rec_img = 0.84 vs the img→img baseline) and 74% on text (Rec_txt)**, with img→txt recovery reaching up to 0.96; for every concept except spatial, some layer range recovers **>80%** of the intra-modality effect in all three models.
- **txt→img is stronger than img→txt — partly a baseline artifact.** Mean txt→img recovery is **1.10 (Emu3.5) and 1.20 (Liquid)** — above 1.0 — but the authors trace this to weak image-derived vectors: img→img AUC at middle layers is only **0.44 (Emu3.5) and 0.43 (Liquid) vs 0.83 in UniAR**. In the reverse direction, Emu3.5 and Liquid only approach full transfer at late layers (**img→txt recovery 0.87 and 0.91**).
- **Concept-dependence is strong.** Emotion transfers best (mean recovery **0.95 in both directions**); age transfers into images at 1.02–1.87 across models/layer ranges; size shows the sharpest asymmetry (**1.12 txt→img vs 0.32 img→txt**); spatial (left-right) fails almost everywhere (mean recovery **0.08 txt→img, 0.01 img→txt**, blank cells where intra-modality AUC < 0.2).
- **Geometry predicts transfer.** Cosine similarity between text- and image-derived vectors correlates with recovery: overall **Spearman ρ = 0.38 (p < 0.01, n = 63), Pearson r = 0.22**, strongest for UniAR txt→img (**ρ = 0.76**); concept-vector cosines reach **0.60 (UniAR) / 0.51 (Liquid)** against a random-direction baseline of ±0.03 (30/42 points >2 SD above chance), while Emu3.5 stays near zero except color (max 0.24). The happy–sad text and image vectors align at cosine 0.47 in UniAR's mid layers.
- **The modality gap is real despite transfer.** In UniAR's middle layers, the average happy image is **more than 10× closer to the average sad image than to the average happy sentence** (same-modality random cosines ~0.75, cross-modality ~0.65 before centering) — supporting "separate subspaces with shared internal structure" over "shared concept points".
- **Quality trade-off is manageable.** At 0.4 of the strength range — where text concept rate peaks — text fluency retains ~75% of unsteered level and collapses beyond; image coherence stays at ~90% of unsteered at 0.6 of the range, where concept rate already exceeds 90% of its maximum.
- **Qualitative cross-modality semantics.** The formal-vs-colloquial text vector steers images toward formal clothing, black-and-white palettes, and luxurious minimalist design; the low-resolution image vector steers text toward fewer tokens, shorter sentences, mentions of "blurry", and expressions of confusion (controlled against other vectors' length effects); the camera-angle vector steers images cleanly but only shifts text next-token probabilities without ever flipping the argmax token.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty: solid but incremental.** Cross-modal steering itself was already shown in the txt→img direction by Wang & Li (2026) on BAGEL; the new claims are bidirectionality in weight-shared UMMs and the cosine-similarity/recovery correlation. The framing as a *causal* probe of modality unification (vs correlational alignment) is the paper's best idea.
- **The >1.0 recoveries are a denominator problem, honestly disclosed but headline-friendly.** txt→img "recovery" of 1.10–1.20 in Emu3.5/Liquid arises because img→img steering itself is weak there (AUC 0.44/0.43); the abstract's "sometimes even steer images better" should be read as "image-native vectors underperform", not "transfer is superhuman".
- **Evaluation rests on one LLM judge with moderate human agreement.** κ = 0.62 vs 3-reviewer majority on only 320 pairs is substantial disagreement by clinical-research standards; ties score 0, and Gemini-judge biases (e.g., preferring verbose or vivid outputs) could inflate concept rate asymmetrically across modalities. There is no second judge family or human re-scoring of the main sweeps.
- **Circularity risk in the data pipeline.** Minimal pairs are generated by Gemini models and judged by a Gemini model; shared stylistic biases between generator and judge could inflate apparent steering (the judge explicitly instructs itself to ignore style, but this is unverifiable at scale).
- **Small, author-curated concept and prompt sets.** Eight concepts × 20 evaluation prompts per direction; no held-out third-party concept suite, so generalization beyond these (mostly binary, visually salient) axes is untested. The spatial failure shows the method is not concept-universal.
- **Recovery compares each vector over its own tolerated strength range, not at equal α** — a defensible but favorable choice that maximizes each vector's AUC; an equal-α comparison would likely lower transferred-vector scores given the authors' own note that transferred vectors need larger strengths.
- **The geometric-signature story is inconsistent across models**: Emu3.5 shows near-zero text/image vector cosines (max 0.24 except color) yet still transfers, and the ρ=0.38 pooled correlation is driven by UniAR/Liquid; the paper acknowledges the correlation concentrates in whichever direction has the reliable intra-modality denominator.
- **Reproducibility caveats**: the AI-use statement admits agent-written experiment code was not reviewed line by line, and Emu3.5 (34B) plus per-setting α grids make exact replication nontrivial despite released artifacts.

## Strengths

- First clean demonstration of *bidirectional* cross-modal steering in weight-shared unified multimodal models, directly contradicting the prior asymmetry narrative.
- Causal-intervention framing (steering, not similarity) is the right tool for the modality-unification question, and the four-setting (txt/img × txt/img) design with recovery ratios makes results easy to interpret.
- The geometry analysis (10× pole-vs-modality distance; aligned difference vectors inside disjoint subspaces) meaningfully connects to modality-gap and vector-offset literature (Liang et al. 2022; Mikolov et al. 2013; Platonic Representation Hypothesis).
- The modality-unique concept case studies (formality → formal clothing; low-res → hedging short text) are genuinely novel observations about how UMMs bridge vision and language.
- Strong artifact release (code, vectors, pairs, generations, judge scores), thorough appendix (judge prompts, strength grids, layer configs, human validation), and transparent reporting of negative results (spatial, Emu3.5 geometry).

## Limitations

- Only three models, one architecture family (autoregressive UMMs with discrete image tokens); no diffusion-in-LLM or continuous-latent unified models, no proprietary frontier systems (which cannot be steered at hidden-state level anyway).
- Eight shared concepts plus a handful of qualitative ones; findings may not extend to abstract, compositional, or safety-critical concepts, and the sole failure (spatial left-right) is a known weak spot of image tokenizers.
- Automated LLM-judge evaluation with κ = 0.62 human agreement; no error bars / significance testing on the recovery ratios across the 20 prompts per concept.
- Cross-modal steering degrades output fluency at high strengths (text fluency ~75% of unsteered at the concept-rate peak), and transferred vectors require larger α than native ones.
- No mechanistic explanation of *why* transfer works — the paper establishes the phenomenon and its geometric correlate, not the circuits or training dynamics responsible.

## Relevance

本文与可解释性及表示工程研究方向高度相关:它把"转向向量"从单模态 LLM 扩展到统一多模态模型的跨模态因果控制,并给出"模态分离子空间 + 共享线性方向"的几何证据,对理解视觉-语言统一模型的内部机制和构建模态无关的控制方法都有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would cross-modal steering vectors transfer in unified models with continuous image latents or diffusion decoders (e.g., native-image-generation frontier models), where the token spaces differ fundamentally from the three UMMs studied?
- Can transfer failure cases like spatial (left/right) be repaired by steering at different granularities (per-head, SAE features, or attention-layer-specific vectors), or is the failure in the image tokenizer rather than the steering direction?
- Does the cosine-similarity/recovery correlation hold prospectively — i.e., can text-image vector alignment be used as a cheap predictor of which concepts a given UMM can steer, without running the transfer sweep?
- Do safety-relevant steering directions (e.g., refusal, per Arditi et al. 2024) transfer from text to image generation, and does that create a new attack surface (steering images to elicit unsafe visual content via a text-extracted vector)?
- How do cross-modal steering vectors interact with fine-tuning or knowledge edits — do edits that break text-side directions silently degrade image-side steerability, given the shared subspace structure?
- Could the "separate subspaces, aligned relations" geometry be exploited to align models across modalities more cheaply than full representation alignment (e.g., learning a linear map only on difference vectors)?
