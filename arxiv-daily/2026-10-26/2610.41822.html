01 Methodology · how indices became trainable

latent weights W→ I = argmin‖W − Wq(I,C)‖²→ exact discrete (C, I)→ block loss ‖Bq−B‖p · KL→ Adam

◂◂ STE identity gradient: ∂L/∂W ≔ ∂L/∂Wq — gradients bypass the discrete assignment

At 2 bits, ≈1.86 of the ~2 bits/weight live in the integer index matrix I — a huge discrete space no gradient can enter. TVQ introduces continuous latent weights W and defines indices as W's nearest-code assignment: the forward pass stays exactly discrete, while the straight-through estimator lets any differentiable loss update W and codebook C with plain Adam (~10 lines of PyTorch).

Training is two-stage on RedPajama. Stage-1 quantizes Transformer blocks sequentially, minimizing the p-norm gap between each quantized block's output — computed on the quantized model's running activations — and the original's, so every block compensates for accumulated upstream error that layer-wise Hessians never see. Stage-2 fine-tunes only continuous parameters (layer norms, codebooks, LM head) with KL divergence. Afterwards only (C, I) is kept: architecture unchanged, existing VQ kernels run as-is.

Two ablated accelerations keep training tractable: scheduled index updates (recompute I every Supd steps) and FAISS Voronoi approximate nearest-neighbor search.

Drops into: BasicVQ (AQLM learned codebooks) · QuIP# fixed L1-cache codebook · QTIP bitshift trellis — codependent indices PV-tuning cannot optimize.

Also on the record

  • Near-lossless 2-bit Llama2-70B: 69.5% vs FP16's 70.2% (−0.7 pts; QuIP# drops −1.3).
  • Perplexity misleads: QuIP#'s W2 ppl of 5.77 looks healthy while QA collapses to 43.5%.
  • Optimizer-swap control (same VQ scheme, trainer only): TVQ beats AQLM on every model — avg +1.5 pts, mean QA drop 3.7 → 2.2 pts.
  • Speed knobs: Supd=16 cuts Stage-1 from 32h to 21h at equal quality (Supd=64 collapses entirely); FAISS ANN gives 5.0× at 99.9% exact-match.

03 Strengths

  • First gradient-based, loss-agnostic optimizer for VQ index matrices — works with every scheme tested, including QTIP's trellis codependent indices that PV-tuning structurally cannot handle.
  • Clean optimizer-swap design: architecture held fixed, only the trainer changes, so gains are attributable to the optimizer.
  • Rescues catastrophic 2-bit failures (+26.8 / +30.2 pts) and documents that perplexity fails to predict them.
  • Practical: 104–440 GPU-h on 2–4 A100s; checkpoints stay bit-compatible with existing inference kernels — no new hardware support needed.
  • Both accelerations are ablated with cost/quality trade-off curves, not asserted.

04 Limitations

  • Quality is bought with compute: TVQ+QTIP Stage-1 costs 390 GPU-h vs 50 for QTIP — roughly an order of magnitude over the baseline's optimizer budget.
  • Base models only, ~2.0–2.08 bits, five zero-shot QA tasks + perplexity — no instruct models, generation-quality, long-context, or 3–4-bit regime.
  • Single-run results without error bars; several winning margins (0.4–0.6 pts) plausibly within run-to-run noise.
  • No inference-side kernels contributed; speed/latency claims are inherited from the underlying schemes.
  • No public code release cited, and 200–400 GPU-h Stage-1 runs make independent replication nontrivial.

05 Questions for Further Exploration

  • Why exactly do QuIP#/QTIP collapse on Llama3-70B at 2 bits while W2 perplexity stays ≈5 — and can the collapse be predicted a priori from weight/activation statistics?
  • Does STE-based index training transfer to instruction-tuned models and the 3–4-bit regime, where layer-wise baselines are already near-lossless?
  • Can TVQ be combined with PV-tuning, or do they optimize overlapping error and yield no stacking gain?
  • How sensitive are results to the p-norm choice, latent-weight initialization, and calibration size — and are the 0.4–0.6-pt margins stable across seeds and harness versions?
