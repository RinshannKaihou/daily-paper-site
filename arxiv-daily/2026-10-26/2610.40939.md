> **一句话总结：** 该研究发现推理模型思维链（CoT）可控性低的根源在于开头几个 token 的熵坍缩（模型几乎必然以 "Okay"/"Alright" 等模板化开头），通过预填充合规 token 或禁止高频开头 token 并辅以示例，可将平均合规率提升 34-41 个百分点（主要恢复"添加类/风格类"控制而非"抑制类"控制），且两类控制能力与可监控性的关联方向相反——因此现有的 CoT 可控性基准可能并不能反映模型规避监督的真实能力。

> **TL;DR:** Reasoning models fail instruction-following inside their thinking tags mostly because of an *initiation* bottleneck: at the first reasoning token Qwen3-32B puts 99.89% probability mass on "Okay" (OLMo-3.1-32B-Think: 99.52%), even when the control instruction raises the probability of compliant openings. On 8 ReasonIF/CoT-Control-style control tasks over CommonsenseQA/ARC-C/GPQA/MMLU-Pro/HLE questions, instruction-only compliance is 0.078 for Qwen3-32B and 0.029 for OLMo-3.1-32B; banning the "Okay"/"Alright" first tokens plus one in-context example lifts these to 0.490 and 0.374 (+41.2 and +34.5 points), with Qwen uppercase reaching 0.775 and OLMo leetspeak 0.747 (both ~0 with ICL alone). Crucially, these interventions recover addition/style control but barely move suppression control, the two groups correlate negatively across a 12-model panel (mean ρ = −0.29 cross-group vs +0.61 within addition/style), and only suppression correlates negatively with CoT monitorability (e.g., word omission ρ = −0.55 on GPQA-Physics process monitoring, while all addition/style tasks are positive, ρ = 0.29–0.74) — so aggregate controllability scores can mask exactly the capability that matters for oversight.

## 核心贡献

- **定位了 CoT 可控性低的机制——"启动瓶颈"（initiation bottleneck）**：模型在 `<think>` 开标签后的前几个 token 上发生熵坍缩，概率质量高度集中于模板化开头（Qwen3-32B 的 "Okay" 占 99.89%；OLMo-3.1-32B-Think 占 99.52%；两者 "Okay"+"Alright" 合计超过 99.99%）。超过 63% 的低分响应（<0.5）在第一个 token 就违规；四个可逐 token 检查的任务中 97.9–98.9% 的失败响应在第一个非空白 token 处即违反约束。
- **证明控制指令确实影响了分布，只是不足以翻转解码**：即使模型最终未合规，指令也会提高合规 token 的概率与排名（几何平均秩改善），但合规 token 的总概率在每个模型-任务对中仍低于 0.25%——失败不等于指令无效果。
- **两个推理时干预即可大幅恢复可控性**：(1) 预填充（prefill）合规开头——仅 1 个 token 就能显著提升合规率，64 个 token 进一步提升；(2) 禁止高频开头 token（banning）+ 用户侧示例（user-ICL），平均合规率在 Qwen3-32B 上从 0.078 升至 0.490、OLMo-3.1-32B 从 0.029 升至 0.374（即摘要所称 34-41 个百分点的提升）。
- **区分了两类控制能力**：干预主要恢复"信息添加"与"风格修改"类控制，而"信息抑制"类（省略指定词、避免第一人称）响应模式不同（ICL 对抑制类更有效，预填充对添加/风格类更有效——方向相反）。跨 12 个模型的相关性分析支持二者是分离的能力：添加/风格组内 ρ=0.13–0.88（均值 0.61），抑制组内 ρ=0.74，而跨组 ρ=−0.66–0.05（均值 −0.29）。
- **厘清了可控性与可监控性（monitorability）的关系**：在 Guan et al. (2025) 的六项可监控性评估上，添加/风格类控制大多与可监控性正相关（GPQA-Physics 过程评估上 ρ=0.29–0.74），而抑制类常为负相关（词省略 ρ=−0.55、避免第一人称 ρ=−0.37）——"可控性高则可监控性低"的简单反比关系不成立，聚合分数会掩盖与监督相关的模式。
- **方法论建议**：未来的可控性评估应把"难以启动特定开头"与"改变推理内容的总体能力"区分开，并分开报告抑制类与添加/风格类控制任务及各任务明细，而非给出单一聚合分。

## Key Contributions

- **Identify the mechanism behind low CoT controllability — an initiation bottleneck / entropy collapse in the first few tokens after the opening think tag.** Without instructions, "Okay" receives 99.89% (Qwen3-32B) and 99.52% (OLMo-3.1-32B-Think) of first-token probability mass; with control instructions still 99.40% and 98.48%. GLM-5.2 is less rigid ("The question," drops from 77.7% to 30.0% under instructions) and is correspondingly more controllable out of the box.
- **Show instructions do shift the distribution even when decoding fails**: rescoring the first-violation position with vs. without the control instruction shows compliant tokens gain probability and rank in every model–task pair, yet total compliant-token probability stays below 0.25% — stereotyped openings keep dominating.
- **Two cheap inference-time interventions recover much of the controllability**: prefilling K ∈ {0,…,64} compliant tokens (K=1 already has substantial effect) and banning the dominant opener ("Okay"/"Alright") at the first reasoning position, optionally combined with user-ICL.
- **Demonstrate that suppression and addition/style controllability are distinct capabilities**, via opposite intervention responses (ICL helps suppression most; prefilling helps addition/style most) and divergent cross-model correlation structure (within addition/style mean ρ = 0.61; suppression-vs-addition/style mean ρ = −0.29).
- **First cross-model evidence that controllability–monitorability relationships split by control type**, using six monitorability evaluations from Guan et al. (2025) (GPQA hints, WMDP sandbagging, Memory Advice, Scruples, GPQA-Physics process, Flaky Tools/CharXiv) across a 12-model panel.
- **Concrete evaluation guidance**: report control tasks individually and by category; do not treat aggregate controllability as a safety indicator, since current addition/style benchmarks may mostly measure sensitivity to the opening bottleneck rather than the ability to make reasoning misleading.

## Methodology

- **Control tasks**: 8 tasks adopted from ReasonIF (Kwon et al., 2026) and CoT-Control (Yueh-Han et al., 2026), in three categories: Information Suppression (avoid first-person expressions; omit a specified word), Information Addition (sentence prefixes; sentence endings; numbered sentences, with markers like BEEP/ZORP), and Stylistic Modification (Spanish; leetspeak; uppercase). Compliance is scored 0–1 per response with task-specific deterministic rules (e.g., fraction of uppercase alphabetic characters; leetspeak substitution rate), except Spanish, graded by Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite under a strict linguistic-compliance rubric. Scoring is independent of final-answer correctness.
- **Questions**: multiple-choice items from CommonsenseQA, ARC-Challenge, GPQA, MMLU-Pro, and Humanity's Last Exam (main suites: 240 question–control pairs / 178 distinct questions; entropy analysis: 119 questions; rank analysis: 108). The control instruction is wrapped in `<cot_instructions>` tags and prepended to the question.
- **Models**: intervention experiments on Qwen3-14B, Qwen3-32B, OLMo-3.1-32B-Think, and GLM-5.2 (14B–753B range across the study, three families); cross-model correlation analyses on a twelve-model panel with instruction-only prompting. Sampling at temperature 0.6, max 8,192 new tokens; 95% bootstrap CIs reported throughout.
- **Analyses**: (1) ICL ablations (user-ICL vs. assistant-ICL demonstration placement); (2) token-rank/probability analysis — rescore the first-violation prediction with the instruction removed, holding generated context fixed; (3) opening-distribution characterization — most-common-prefix match rates and next-token entropy along traces (Qwen vocab 151,936 vs. OLMo 100,278, so entropy magnitudes not directly comparable); (4) prefilling K = 0…64 compliant tokens from a compliant example for the same question; (5) first-token banning of Okay/Alright with renormalized sampling, in a 2×2 with user-ICL (30 questions × 8 tasks × 3 samples); (6) pairwise Spearman correlations of the 8 task scores across 12 models; (7) Spearman correlations between each task's mean compliance and six monitorability scores (intervention g-mean, outcome g-mean, process sensitivity).
- **Honest scoping**: GLM-5.2 is excluded from likelihood-based analyses (API does not return reasoning-token likelihoods) and from banning (API restriction; rejection sampling proved too inefficient).

## Results & Highlights

- **Opening concentration / entropy collapse**: without instructions, the token "Okay" receives 99.89% (Qwen3-32B) and 99.52% (OLMo-3.1-32B-Think) of first-reasoning-token probability; with control instructions 99.40% and 98.48%, with "Alright" at 0.44% and 1.29%. The two-token prefix "Okay," appears in 99.8% (Qwen) and 99.7% (OLMo) of instructed responses. GLM-5.2's "The question," falls from 77.7% to 30.0% under instructions.
- **Failure is concentrated at the first token**: across all prompt strategies, over 63% of responses scoring below 0.5 fail at the first token; for the four token-checkable tasks, 97.9–98.9% of failed responses violate the constraint at their first non-whitespace token. Instructions raise compliant-token probability/rank in every model–task pair, but mean total compliant-token probability remains below 0.25% everywhere.
- **First-token banning + user-ICL (headline result)**: average compliance across the 8 control tasks rises from 0.078 to 0.254 (banning alone) and to 0.490 (banning + user-ICL) for Qwen3-32B — a +41.2-point gain over instruction-only — and from 0.029 to 0.225 / 0.374 for OLMo-3.1-32B (+34.5 points), matching the abstract's 34–41 pp claim. Per-task: Qwen uppercase reaches 0.775 and leetspeak 0.652 (both ≈0 with user-ICL alone); OLMo numbering reaches 0.418 and leetspeak 0.747. Not uniform: Qwen sentence-endings is better with user-ICL alone; OLMo Spanish is better with banning alone.
- **Prefilling**: seeding with a single compliant token (PI@1, with instruction) substantially improves continuation compliance, with marginal gains up to K=64; addition and style tasks (especially for Qwen/OLMo) benefit most, word omission barely changes, and first-person avoidance needs longer prefixes. ICL alone is weak: GLM-5.2 base compliance rises from 50.0% to 64.4% with assistant-ICL, while Qwen3-14B/32B and OLMo-3.1-32B remain largely uncontrollable.
- **Two capability clusters across 12 models**: pairwise Spearman correlations of instruction-only compliance among the six addition/style tasks are uniformly positive, ρ = 0.13–0.88 (mean 0.61); the two suppression tasks correlate at ρ = 0.74; cross-group correlations range ρ = −0.66–0.05 (mean −0.29) — i.e., models good at suppression tend to be worse at addition/style.
- **Controllability vs. monitorability (Guan et al., 2025 evals)**: on the GPQA-Physics process evaluation, all six addition/style tasks correlate positively with monitorability (ρ = 0.29–0.74) while word omission and first-person avoidance correlate negatively (ρ = −0.55 and −0.37); the GPQA intervention evaluation shows the same pattern more weakly (addition/style ρ = 0.01–0.43; suppression −0.35/−0.13). WMDP and Memory Advice show mostly weak associations, Flaky Tools mixed, Scruples positive for all tasks. Higher controllability thus does not consistently imply lower monitorability.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Genuine and well-targeted. Prior work (ReasonIF, CoT-Control, Jose 2026, Nishimura-Gasparian & Parikh 2026) established *that* controllability is low and that prompting/fine-tuning can raise it; this is the first paper to give a token-level *why* (entropy collapse onto templated openings) and to show the minimal intervention that flips it (one prefilled token; banning one or two tokens). The suppression-vs-addition/style decomposition and its opposite monitorability correlations is a genuinely useful refinement for the safety-eval community.
- **Rigor**: Mixed. The intervention experiments are clean (controlled 2×2 designs, multiple samples, bootstrap CIs, per-task breakdowns in the appendix). But the two headline correlational claims rest on only 12 models with Spearman correlations — the authors themselves call this "descriptive evidence" — and the monitorability scores feeding those correlations are noisy, clustered near ceiling (Memory Advice, GPQA-Physics) or floor (Scruples), so rank correlations can hinge on orderings the measurements cannot reliably distinguish.
- **Reproducibility**: Good on paper — code released, prompts, grading rubrics (including the full Gemini Spanish-grading prompt), marker vocabularies, question mixtures (Table 1), and sampling parameters all documented. Caveats: two of four intervention models are API-only (GLM-5.2 excluded from banning and likelihood analyses for API reasons), and compliant ICL/prefill examples are partly LLM-generated (GPT-5-mini rewrites for Spanish and word-omission; Gemini judge for Spanish scoring), adding a judge-dependency to a subset of scores.
- **Data-quantity quirk worth flagging**: the main intervention suites use 240 question–control pairs of which only 6 are GPQA, 8 MMLU-Pro, and 2 HLE per suite (149 CommonsenseQA + 75 ARC-C dominate), and question mixtures differ across experiments — cross-experiment comparisons (e.g., ICL vs. banning) are matched within but not across analyses, which the paper admits in Appendix A.1.
- **Overclaiming risk — managed, mostly**: the abstract's "34-41 percentage points" is accurate for the two models where banning is possible, but readers should note it is a *mean over 8 tasks* including several that stay near zero (word omission, first-person avoidance), and that the interventions do not consistently combine (Qwen sentence-endings and OLMo Spanish are counterexamples). The safety interpretation is appropriately hedged: they explicitly do not test deliberate monitor evasion, and they correctly note that adding misleading information does not require a nonstandard opening — so low scores on current addition/style benchmarks may *understate* later-trace control. One could add: the interventions require controller access to token logits/prefill that an overseer would not have over an adversarially self-controlling model, so this is mechanism demonstration, not a mitigation.
- **Red flags**: none serious; minor sloppiness (a "TThe" typo) and the 12-model panel composition is only visible in a figure (Figure 13), not itemized in text.

## Strengths

- Locates a crisp, mechanistic, and testable cause (first-token entropy collapse onto "Okay"/"Alright") for a phenomenon previously only benchmarked, and validates it with three independent methods: distributional statistics, counterfactual rescoring, and causal interventions (prefill/ban).
- The interventions are minimal and well-controlled — banning a single token at a single position is about the cleanest causal test of the "initiation" hypothesis one could design.
- The suppression vs. addition/style split is supported both causally (opposite responses to ICL vs. prefilling) and observationally (cross-model correlation structure), which is much stronger than either alone.
- Directly actionable recommendations for benchmark designers (report tasks separately; separate initiation difficulty from content-control ability) rather than vague calls for future work.
- Unusually transparent about statistical limitations: bootstrap intervals on correlations, floor/ceiling clustering of monitorability evals, and undefined-score rates for WMDP are all disclosed in the paper itself.

## Limitations

- Correlational claims rest on only 12 models and noisy monitorability scores clustered near floor/ceiling; the authors themselves label the associations "descriptive."
- GLM-5.2 — the most controllable and most instructable of the four intervention models — could not be included in the token-banning or likelihood analyses due to API restrictions, so the bottleneck story is verified in detail only on Qwen and OLMo (plus partial evidence for GLM).
- Small and skewed question pools per suite (e.g., 6 GPQA, 8 MMLU-Pro, 2 HLE items in the main suites; mixtures differ across experiments), limiting power and cross-experiment comparability.
- Only 8 control tasks in 3 categories; suppression is represented by just 2 tasks (word omission, first-person avoidance), so the "suppression is different" cluster could partly reflect task-format artifacts (both are binary 0/1 scores) rather than a deep capability distinction.
- No test of the behavior that actually matters for safety — deliberate monitor evasion or strategically inserted misleading content — so the bridge from controllability tasks to real oversight risk remains inferential.
- Inference-time interventions (prefill, token banning) require white-box controller access that would not exist against a deployed adversarial model; they demonstrate mechanism rather than offer a practical safeguard.

## Relevance

这项工作直击推理模型安全监督的核心问题——思维链可监控性：它解释了为什么当前模型"难以控制思维链内容"（开头 token 熵坍缩），并指出可控性基准与真实监督风险的错位，对关注 CoT 监控、模型可解释性评估与 AI 安全对齐的研究非常有参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the opening bottleneck persist after the fine-tuning interventions shown to raise controllability (Nishimura-Gasparian & Parikh, 2026), or does fine-tuning fix initiation and thereby also change monitorability correlations?
- Can a model be trained (or prompted) to *deliberately* insert misleading content mid-trace without touching its opening — and would any of the six monitorability evaluations here detect it? This is the gap the authors explicitly leave open.
- Is the suppression/addition-style split a genuine capability dissociation or an artifact of scoring format (binary 0/1 for both suppression tasks vs. fractional scores for most addition/style tasks)? Adding fractional suppression tasks (e.g., partial redaction) would disambiguate.
- Why is GLM-5.2's opening distribution so much less collapsed than Qwen's and OLMo's — is it a data mixture effect, RLVR curriculum effect, or explicit formatting-stage effect? A training-data/token-distribution analysis could localize it.
- Do monitorability correlations survive when compliance is elicited without any possibility of nonstandard openings (i.e., prefill a neutral opening for everyone), which would decouple content control from initiation difficulty as the authors propose?
- How do the interventions interact with task accuracy — the paper scores compliance independently of final-answer correctness, but a deployer cares about the joint effect of forced style compliance on reasoning quality.
