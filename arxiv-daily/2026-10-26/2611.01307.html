Why this matters · 为何重要

Label-free TTRL/GRPO training silently collapses onto its first majority answer — this paper replaces trial-and-error exploration tuning with closed-form thresholds.

Critical edge

Even with the bonus, PRM best-of-32 selection scores 0.569 — still below the untrained model's 0.603: the training loop destroys value on its own stress-test subset.

And pass@1 drops 0.481 → 0.365: the diversity dividend is only payable by a downstream selector that doesn't exist yet.

How it's studied

Methodology — one flow, one path, three arms

A deliberately minimal object: the tabular flow z′ = V(z), the answer distribution of a single problem under the expected gradient of a softmax policy (NPG and vanilla PG unify to Vi = piq(pi − S₂), q ∈ {0,1}).

C bonus inside the reward, before the division · R only advantages divided, bonus outside · U undivided (Dr. GRPO style)

1Tabular testbed — tanh net, 32 states → 256 ordered bins, PPO on the leave-one-out agreement reward, G = 64.
2Letter test — Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct + LoRA answers 64 ten-option MMLU-Pro questions with a single letter; the answer distribution is exactly computable.
3Free-form TTRL — Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B + LoRA on 128 MATH-500 problems chosen so half the initial pluralities are wrong, G = 8, 200 steps.

Tools: linear stability, replicator dynamics (Hofbauer's equation, Potts spinodals); the GRPO division is modeled in mean field and Monte-Carlo-validated to ~4% (~10–11% near collapse).

What holds up

Strengths

  • +Closed-form, memorable thresholds — 1/n, 4(n−1)/n, 1/log(G−1) — in a literature where exploration coefficients are tuned by trial and error.
  • +One principle (the path of the reward) predicts transfer across learning rates, optimizers, and normalization variants — confirmed at predicted magnitudes (R/C ratios 2.0–4.0 around 2.7; ≤1.09× drift over a 10× LR range).
  • +The three-tier ladder (tabular → exact-distribution letter test → free-form) is a model of theory validation: each tier tests exactly what the previous one could not.
  • +Unusual honesty: publishes the negative result, and proves what exploration cannot do — symmetric exploration never fixes a wrong majority (Prop. A.13).
  • +Table 2 (which tricks are/are not exploration) and the Appendix H recipe are immediately actionable for GRPO-family anti-collapse tuning.

Where it strains

Limitations

  • −One problem at a time, answer-symmetric rewards. Real runs share parameters — the letter-test LoRA adapter coupled questions and committed across them (disclosed, unresolved).
  • −The GRPO-division analysis is a mean-field surrogate (population std), Monte-Carlo-checked to ~4–11% — not a proof for sampled groups; near-collapse drift-sign arguments are not full stability proofs.
  • −Free-form experiments are small: one 1.5B family, 128 label-selected problems, G=8, 200 steps, 3–6 seeds, no anti-collapse baselines run.
  • −The central practical promise is unrealized: PRM best-of-32 with the bonus (0.569) still trails the untrained model (0.603).
  • −No VLA experiment despite the ordered-bin theory being motivated by tokenized VLA models; full-rank network assumptions for the LoRA adapters were not checked.

Open threads

Questions for further exploration

  • Does the before/after-division threshold split (1/log(G−1) vs 2.3–3.2×) persist at 7B+ scale with larger groups and the unbiased std — where the predicted factor becomes √G?
  • Can annealing the coefficient — start above the lower boundary, decay toward it — break the pass@1-vs-diversity trade-off by committing late to a better-explored majority?
  • What is the joint theory of symmetric exploration plus an asymmetric signal (weak verifier, PRM, differential smoothing) — and can it push best-of-32 above the untrained baseline?
  • When questions share an adapter and collapse together, does the effective n become the number of questions — tightening the window?
  • Do the certified ordered-bin folds predict collapse thresholds for real tokenized VLA models, where bins discretize actions rather than answers?
