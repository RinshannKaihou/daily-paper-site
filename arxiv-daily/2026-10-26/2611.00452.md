> **一句话总结：** 本文系统研究了"组合式自我提升"——用模型对简单子问题的预测拼接出困难样本的伪标签再训练：在加法（过滤后 8–23 位平均 93.7% vs 直接自提升 0%）、Bash 管道生成（88% vs 11%）和 MuSiQue 多跳问答（9B 上 2–4 跳平均超直接基线 +17.1 分）上均大幅超越直接伪标签法，并理论化地指出三大失效模式（组件预测不可靠、组合规则结构性错误、监督覆盖不足），最后证明这种组合标签可作为 RL 的有效热启动。

> **TL;DR:** A systematic study of building pseudo-labels for hard, out-of-distribution instances by composing a model's own predictions on simpler subproblems, then training iteratively. On controlled tasks (e.g., 8–23-digit addition: 93.7% mean accuracy with filtering vs 0% beyond 7 digits for direct self-improvement), on Bash pipeline generation with Qwen3.5-2B (83–94% vs ≤11% for direct pseudo-labeling across 2–6-command pipelines), and on MuSiQue multi-hop QA (compositional beats direct by +15.5/+11.3/+17.1 points for 2B/4B/9B after 3 rounds), composed supervision dominates direct self-labels and even generalizes to compositions longer than trained on — but three failure modes (unreliable components, invalid composition rules, limited supervision coverage) cap the gains, and a short SFT warmup on composed labels rescues GRPO RL that otherwise gets zero reward (depth-5 string transformations: 19.2% at 40M tokens vs 0% for direct RL at 80M tokens).

## 核心贡献

- **提出并形式化"组合式自我提升"（compositional self-improvement）框架**：对模型当前无法可靠求解的困难实例，利用其对简单组件的预测、经任务特定组合规则拼成伪标签，过滤后加入训练集并迭代多轮；理论上证明组合标签误差 ≤ 组件误差 + 规则误差，且只要二者之和小于模型直接预测误差，组合监督就优于直接伪标签。
- **识别并系统验证三大失效模式**：(I) 组件预测不可靠——网格导航中初始单房准确率 79.9% 与 99.4% 的模型最终 2–13 房平均准确率分别为 19.4% 与 80.9%；(II) 组合规则结构性错误——分块加法中跨块进位使无过滤组合在 23 位仅 11.0%，过滤后恢复至 80.0%；(III) 监督覆盖不足——最长游程任务中跨界游程超出组件游程长度时准确率随超出量骤降至接近 0。
- **结论推广到真实任务**：Bash 管道生成（TLDR + NL2Bash）与 MuSiQue 多跳推理上，组合标签远比直接自标签准确（Bash 第一轮 91% vs 8%），且提升泛化到训练中未见过的更长组合（5–6 命令管道、4 跳问题）；覆盖不足同样显现（含 30 个保留命令的管道准确率从 88% 降至 46%）。
- **证明组合监督是 RL 的有效热启动**：在直接探索几乎得不到奖励的任务上（五操作字符串变换中直接 GRPO 在 80M token 后仍为 0%），先用组合标签做短 SFT 再 RL 达 19.2%；即使热启动标签仅 77% 正确也有效。

## Key Contributions

- **A framework for compositional self-improvement**: instead of pseudo-labeling hard instances with the model's unreliable direct prediction, the model answers simpler subproblems, a task-specific composition rule joins the answers into a target for the parent problem, an optional reference-free filter removes structurally invalid compositions, and the model retrains and iterates. Proposition 6.1 shows composed-label error ρₖ ≤ min{1, ϵₖ + ξₖ} (component errors plus rule errors), giving a sufficient condition for composed labels to beat direct self-labels (ϵₖ + ξₖ < ρₖᵈⁱʳᵉᶜᵗ).
- **Identification and controlled isolation of three failure modes**: (I) unreliable component predictions (weaker initial models plateau early), (II) structural errors from invalid composition rules (boundary carries in blockwise addition; fixable by filtering at the cost of coverage), and (III) limited supervision coverage (a closure argument: selecting the longer of two component runs can never supervise a run longer than both, so accuracy on boundary-crossing runs collapses regardless of filtering).
- **Validation on realistic tasks with open LLMs**: Bash pipeline generation (Qwen3.5-2B, 119 commands from TLDR/NL2Bash) and multi-hop QA on MuSiQue (Qwen3.5-2B/4B/9B) confirm that composed labels are far more accurate than direct labels and that gains transfer to longer compositions than seen in training, while all three failure modes remain visible.
- **Compositional supervision as an RL warm start**: a short SFT stage on composed targets (even only 77% correct) moves the model into a regime where outcome-rewarded GRPO can learn, on tasks where direct RL never samples a correct composition.

## Methodology

The method (Algorithm 1) maintains a model f̂ₜ and a difficulty schedule. At each round, for each parent problem x with a supplied decomposition π into subproblems x₁…xₘ, the current model predicts ŷ₁…ŷₘ (sequentially, substituting earlier predictions when later steps depend on them, as in MuSiQue), a composition rule A produces the candidate label ỹ = A(π, ŷ₁,…,ŷₘ), and a filter G — which never consults reference answers, only structure (parses as a pipeline, no boundary carry, answer appears in the passages) — decides retention. The model then trains on retained examples plus replay at initial difficulty levels.

Three experimental tiers:
1. **Controlled algorithmic tasks** with 14.2M-parameter character-level Transformers (no positional encoding): grid navigation (rooms joined by corridors; routes concatenated through corridor moves; no filter needed), blockwise addition (block sums concatenated; filter rejects carries across block boundaries), and max run length (label = longer of two component runs; filter rejects equal boundary symbols). Schedules expand difficulty over 8–12 rounds (up to 13 rooms / 23 digits / length 83), enabling separate variation of component reliability, rule validity, and coverage.
2. **Realistic tasks** with LoRA fine-tuned Qwen3.5 models: Bash pipelines (pipe-join of per-step commands; parse-only filter) and MuSiQue 2–4-hop questions (follow the released decomposition, substitute model answers into placeholder sub-questions; answer-format/passage-grounding filter), each compared against direct self-improvement under identical schedules, parents, and replay.
3. **RL warm start** with GRPO (binary outcome rewards): direct RL vs. a short SFT warmup on compositional labels, on five-operation string transformations (from Yuan et al. 2025) and three-function typed Python programs (MBPP+/HumanEval+ functions assembled into a wrapper), with token accounting that charges warmup label generation and target tokens.

A theoretical analysis (Section 6/B) bounds composed-label error, characterizes each failure mode (error accumulation ρₖ = ϵₖ under valid fixed-query composition; closure limits on supervision coverage with excess training error exactly p_{k,C}; a pseudo-label learning bound R⁺(f̂_new) ≤ 2ρₖ + 2√(log(2|H|/δ)/2n) + τ), with proofs additionally formalized in Lean 4.

## Results & Highlights

- **Controlled addition (8–23-digit addends)**: filtered compositional self-improvement reaches 93.7% mean accuracy and 80.0% at 23 digits, vs 33.5% / 11.0% for unfiltered composition (boundary carries corrupt labels) and 0% for direct self-improvement at every length above the 7-digit training range. Even the Lee et al. (2025)-style length-filtered direct baseline only reaches 9 digits after 16 rounds (~2x GPU time), while compositional reaches ≥97% at every length up to 23 digits in 8 rounds.
- **Grid navigation**: compositional self-improvement reaches 80.9% mean accuracy on 2–13-room chains (from 0% initial), vs 0% for direct self-improvement beyond one room. Component quality gates the gains: initial one-room accuracies of 79.9% vs 99.4% yield final means of 19.4% vs 80.9% on 2–13 rooms (failure mode I).
- **Max run length (lengths 11–83)**: overall accuracy 97.6% (filtered) / 97.0% (unfiltered) vs 75.9% for direct self-improvement and 59.8% for the initial model — yet accuracy on boundary-crossing runs falls to ~0 once the run exceeds the longest component run by 3 symbols, with or without filtering (failure mode III: no composed label can contain such outputs).
- **Bash pipeline generation (Qwen3.5-2B, TLDR + NL2Bash, 119 commands)**: after 3 rounds, compositional reaches 83–94% on 2–6-command test pipelines vs ≤11% for direct self-improvement, including 83% on 6-command pipelines that are longer than any training composition (max length 4). Round-1 composed labels are 91% correct vs 8% for direct labels. Coverage limits persist: mean accuracy on 2–6-command pipelines drops from 88% (all commands seen in compositions) to 73% (one held-out command) and 46% (all held-out).
- **MuSiQue multi-hop QA (exact match, 2,417 dev questions)**: after 3 rounds, compositional beats direct self-improvement on 2–4-hop questions by +15.5 / +11.3 / +17.1 points for Qwen3.5-2B/4B/9B respectively; the 9B model's 4-hop accuracy rises from 38% to 52%, and 4-hop improves already in rounds 1–2 that train only on 2–3-hop. First-round composed-label accuracy scales with model size (44.8% for 2B → 65.2% for 9B, vs 28.9%/53.2% for direct labels), with most errors arising at the final step.
- **RL warm start (GRPO, token-matched comparison)**: on depth-5 string transformations, direct RL stays at 0% through 80M generated tokens (never samples a correct composition), while compositional-SFT + RL reaches 19.2% at 40M tokens; on typed Python (MBPP+/HumanEval+ 3-function programs), warmup+RL reaches 55.6% with half the generation budget vs 54.3% for direct RL with the full budget — and the warmup labels are only 77.0% (strings) / 94.1% (Python) correct, showing imperfect composed supervision still bootstraps RL.
- **Honest nuance (Appendix C.7)**: where the composition rule holds on every test instance, decomposed inference with the *initial* model is more accurate than the trained compositional model (Bash 2–6 commands: 94.9% vs 88.1%; grid 2–13 rooms: 88.6% vs 80.9%); the trained model wins where the rule fails on test data (addition 8–23 digits: 93.7% vs 23.2%) and needs only one pass without a supplied decomposition.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The core idea (supervise hard problems by composing solutions to easy ones) is a careful systematization of iterated amplification (Christiano et al., 2018) and compositional data augmentation, repositioned for the modern self-improvement/RL setting. The value is less in the mechanism than in the clean isolation of *when it fails* — the three-mode decomposition with matching theory and controlled experiments is genuinely new and more rigorous than prior self-improvement papers.
- **Rigor**: High for the controlled tasks (component reliability varied via 7 initial checkpoints from 400 to 30,000 updates; filters ablated; label audits match the theoretical bound in every measured round). The realistic experiments are also multi-seed with matched parents/schedules between methods.
- **Weak-baseline concern**: the headline "direct self-improvement" comparison is greedy pseudo-labeling with only format/structural filters — no self-consistency, rejection sampling, or verifier-based filtering (e.g., STaR-style bootstrapping with correctness checks). On addition, their direct baseline gets 0% beyond 7 digits and contradicts Lee et al. (2025); the authors address this only in Appendix C.2 with a length filter. The Bash (≤11%) and MuSiQue (roughly flat) direct baselines inherit the same weakness, so the deltas likely overstate the gap to the best direct method.
- **Underemphasized caveat**: Appendix C.7 shows decomposed inference with the *initial* model beats the final trained model where the rule is valid (Bash 94.9% vs 88.1%) — i.e., training on composed labels partially *distills away* the benefit of the decomposition. The paper states this but the abstract/introduction framing ("improve performance") does not; the honest selling point is one-pass inference without test-time decompositions plus robustness to rule failures.
- **Scope limits**: all tasks require *supplied* decompositions and hand-crafted composition rules/filters (acknowledged as open); Bash covers 21 text utilities in 119 single-line commands; MuSiQue caps at 4 hops with absolute accuracy still ~52% for 9B on 4-hop; the algorithmic tasks use 14.2M Transformers, so scaling behavior of the failure modes to frontier models is untested.
- **RL warm start framing**: the typed-Python gain is modest in accuracy (55.6% vs 54.3% direct RL) and is framed as token efficiency (half the budget); the string-transformation result (19.2% vs 0%) is the strong case, but 19.2% remains far below the 77.3% achievable by decomposed inference with the initial model.

## Strengths

- Rare combination of controlled experiments, real-LLM experiments, and theory (with Lean 4 formalization) that all tell the same story, including empirically auditing that the error bound ρ ≤ ϵ + ξ holds in every measured round.
- The three failure modes are actionable and separately diagnosable (component accuracy, rule validity, output-space closure), each with a matching remedy and a matching experiment.
- Honest negative results: filtering restores addition but creates coverage gaps; composed supervision never reaches runs longer than its components; weaker models gain less; gains shrink on held-out commands (88% → 46%).
- Practical recipe with clear token-accounting for the RL warm start, and generalization to longer compositions than trained on (5–6-command Bash pipelines, 4-hop QA improving during 2–3-hop training).

## Limitations

- Requires a known, valid-enough decomposition and composition rule per task; discovering and validating these automatically is left open.
- Composed supervision cannot create outputs outside its closure (e.g., longer runs), so it extends reliability rather than genuinely new capabilities; coverage gaps are structural, not fixable by more rounds or stricter filtering.
- Gains depend strongly on initial component reliability — for the 2B MuSiQue model (1-hop 69%), composed-label accuracy is only ~45–50%, capping improvement.
- Realistic-task scope is narrow (one-line Bash pipelines over 21 utilities; MuSiQue ≤ 4 hops); no math reasoning or code-editing benchmarks; direct baseline lacks verification-based filtering; largest model tested is 9B with LoRA.

## Relevance

与"自我提升/合成监督"研究方向高度相关：本文给出了用任务组合结构为 OOD 困难样本生成可靠伪标签的系统方法论与失效边界，可直接指导基于自生成数据的 LLM 训练（SFT 伪标签 + RL 热启动）在代码与多跳推理任务上的应用。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can composition rules and decompositions be discovered and validated automatically (e.g., by the model itself or a learned aggregator), rather than supplied per task, and does the error bound ρ ≤ ϵ + ξ extend to learned rules whose ξ is nonzero?
- Does combining compositional labels with verification or self-consistency (stronger direct-method variants) close or invert the gap on Bash and MuSiQue that this paper's greedy direct baseline leaves open?
- How do the three failure modes scale with model size and pretraining — does component reliability stop being the binding constraint at frontier scale, leaving coverage as the dominant bottleneck?
- Can supervision coverage be expanded without introducing rule errors, e.g., by composing more than two components, using partial/executable rules, or mixing in a small amount of exploratory RL data on uncovered patterns?
- Since decomposed inference with the initial model beats the trained model where the rule is valid, is there a middle ground — e.g., training on composed labels jointly with test-time decomposition, or distilling decomposed inference so one-pass accuracy matches it (88.1% vs 94.9% on Bash)?
- Does the RL warm start keep its advantage when direct RL is paired with curriculum or reward shaping, and does the recipe transfer to verifiable math/code RL where correct compositions are rare but not zero?
