Key finding

Label-free RLVR collapse is a collective instability — past a critical dataset size, reward hacking becomes inevitable.

无监督 RLVR（多数投票自奖励）的崩溃是一种集体不稳定性：题目越多崩溃越必然，临界训练题数 Nc ≈ ln(1/δ)/(λ²G)。

Paper: Collective Dynamics of Self-Reinforcement: How Cross-Task Coupling Drives Policy Collapse in Unsupervised RLVR

Peize Ding · Columbia University 2026-10-26 arXiv v1
RLVR unsupervised RL reward hacking policy collapse learning theory

Single-author theory paper; explains the ≤128-stable / ≥512-hacks threshold of He et al. (ICLR 2026); engages TTRL-style self-reward methods; code + raw results released. No peer-review status stated.

Key metric · causal test, 504 runs

0/168 → 335/336

Collapsed training runs when the shared readout direction is surgically removed (antisymmetric readout) vs. when the coupling is re-injected at ρb ≥ 1 — in the same small Transformer doing label-free RL.

Why this matters

Turns “bigger datasets breed reward hacking” from empirical folklore into a computable threshold and pre-training diagnostics for label-free RL.

把“数据集越大越容易 reward hacking”的经验现象转化为可计算的临界阈值与训练前可测量的诊断量。

⚠ Critical edge

The closed-form law is not validated in the only non-toy system: a network with initial G = 0.000 still collapsed in 17/42 runs.

The pre-training predictor is under-calibrated (mean predicted 0.46 vs. observed 0.71) — a screening tool, not a stability guarantee.

1How they proved it

  1. Exact single-problem drift. Each problem reduces to its answer logit z. Majority-vote self-reward over K=3 samples gives drift F(z) = p(1−p)(2p−1); its slope F′ > 0 only while answers are undecided (|z| < ln(2+√3) ≈ 1.32).
  2. Couple N problems on one shared direction (zi = ui + λv). Minibatch SGD makes per-problem sharpening scale as 1/N while the shared mode aggregates every gradient (rate ∝λ², independent of N) — a Curie–Weiss instability with a closed-form threshold.
  3. NTK extension. Linearizing a real network yields couplings measurable before training: ku (shared, harmful) and ks (prediction-aligned, protective).
  4. Verify causally. Fully stochastic toy simulations test four pre-registered predictions; then 924 Transformer RL runs (22 conditions × 7 sizes × 6 seeds), where an antisymmetric readout removes the shared direction and a scaled output bias re-injects it at controlled dose.

2The law

Nc ≈ ln(1/δ) / (λ²·G)

  • Nnumber of training problems — collapse needs more data, not less
  • λ²strength of the shared parameter direction (coupling across problems)
  • Gsharpening gain ln[F⋆/F(a)] — decreases as initial confidence a rises
  • δnoise seed from minibatch sampling; enters only logarithmically (batch size barely matters)

Feature-protected regime: in the kernel version, when G·ku < Λc·ks, no training-set size causes collapse — sharpening that generalizes across problems (ks) defends against the shared mode (ku).

3Evidence beyond the headline

Λc ≈ 2.6

One critical amplification: sweeps spanning a 64× range of λ⁻² (critical sizes 60–8740) collapse onto a single transition curve

λ−2.22

Fitted scaling of Nc with coupling vs. predicted −2 (+ log correction)

0.999 / 0.50

In collapsing runs: majority-vote reward ≥0.999 while held-out accuracy falls 0.82 → chance

  • Toy model: at N=206 the self-reward rises while accuracy holds; at N=2335 reward → 1 and accuracy → chance. Fitted Nc ≈ 536 (λ=0.1, a=0.5).
  • Transformer matches LLM phenomenology: plain base-90 at τ=1 collapses in 0/6 runs at N=16 but 6/6 at N ≥ 512; higher temperature (entropy) monotonically increases collapse (29/34/37 of 42 runs at τ=1/2/3).
  • Entropy remedy hits its derived threshold but not the goal: marginal-entropy bonus β ≥ 0.5 → 0/24 collapses (vs. 7/8 without), matching βc ≤ 1/2 — yet remedied runs stay at chance accuracy because other collective kernel modes take over.

4Strengths

  • Closed-form, interpretable law (Nc ≈ ln(1/δ)/(λ²G)) for a phenomenon previously characterized only empirically.
  • Four pre-registered falsifiable predictions, all confirmed in fully stochastic simulation, with a 64× coupling range collapsing onto one Λc.
  • The antisymmetric-readout intervention is a clean causal test — 0/168 vs. 335/336 is about as decisive as ablations get.
  • Kernel extension yields a non-obvious protective effect (ks) and a regime where adding data is provably safe, confirmed by dose-response.
  • Exceptional scientific honesty: quantifies its own predictor’s failure modes instead of burying them.

5Limitations

  • No experiments on pretrained LLMs — the central motivating phenomenon; the largest testbed is a small Transformer on a synthetic 6-digit comparison task.
  • Quantitative transfer holds only in the fixed-kernel (lazy) regime; under feature learning the kernel drifts (protective coupling eroding 0.42 → 0.07 in one epoch).
  • The entropy bonus damps only the uniform mode; other collective kernel modes take over and accuracy still falls to chance.
  • Λc is calibrated on the toy model; the binary answer-level abstraction ignores multi-token reasoning, format and length biases of real LLM collapse.
  • Confidence-curriculum suggestion explicitly untested; the “⟨F′⟩ not entropy” claim verified through one temperature knob on one synthetic task.

6Open questions

  1. Do ⟨F′⟩ and kernel ratios measured on a real pretrained LLM (e.g. Qwen/Llama + TTRL) predict He et al.’s dataset-size threshold? The author calls this the most important next step.
  2. Does the theory extend to multi-token free-form answers, where the “majority vote” is over semantic equivalence and the shared direction includes format/length preferences?
  3. Can kernel re-measurement during training close the one-sided error where feature learning erodes the protective coupling?
  4. Does the feature-protected regime (G·ku < Λc·ks) exist in pretrained models — and can auxiliary objectives enlarge it?
  5. Do other pseudo-rewards (self-certainty, semantic entropy, entropy minimization) yield different thresholds under the same sharpening-vs-shared-mode race?
