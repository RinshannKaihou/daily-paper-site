Why this matters

Maps exactly when stacking quantization and pruning breaks small models — the core failure mode of low-precision deployment — and supplies a cheap, usable repair.

from Relevance (相关性)
Critical edge · overclaim risk

Recovered PPL 24.3 is still >2× the 11.82 FP16 baseline — the “−97.4%” framing makes recovery look more complete than it is, and no downstream-task accuracy is reported for recovered models.

Recoveroff · LoRA r=8 on q/v-proj
18 configs × 4 models = 72  +  48 ordering runs  =  120 · one NVIDIA L40S 48 GB
  • Models: Phi-2 2.7B, Qwen3-4B, Nemotron-Mini-4B, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct.
  • Recovery: LoRA adapters (~0.1% of params, quantized weights frozen), 200 steps at lr 2e-4; self-distillation variant vs plain cross-entropy isolates the teacher signal.
  • Fast metric: 20-sample WikiText-2 PPL (~30 s) correlates r = 0.985 with the full 1,940-sample set; cross-checked on MMLU/HellaSwag/ARC/BoolQ (30 configs) and 3 seeds (σ ≤ 0.7 PPL).
  • Deployment check: GGUF conversion → llama.cpp CPU runs across 2/4/8/12 GB memory tiers.

Structured head pruning was inapplicable to 3 of 4 architectures and destructive on Nemotron-Mini-4B (30% heads → PPL 4,775 vs 52.69 unstructured) — hence unstructured L1.

Results

What 120 controlled runs actually showed

16/16 NF4+pruning combinations collapse (fluent-looking but incoherent output) — unless pruning precedes quantization, which restores viability (PPL ~20–21). Among viable configs, ordering is statistically irrelevant (ANOVA F = 0.099, p = 0.96). INT8 alone cuts memory 40–45% (Qwen2.5-7B: 14.2 GB → ~8 GB) for <0.5% average accuracy loss.

Recovery scales with model size. Teacher-free LoRA matches or beats self-distillation on 2 of 4 models (Qwen2.5-7B full-corpus: 27.48 vs 28.80) — the teacher, which doubles training memory, can be dropped.

  • CPU (llama.cpp): 4-bit runs 46–54% faster than 8-bit for <0.5 PPL difference (Phi-2: 17.5 vs 11.5 tok/s). No model fits 2 GB; all four fit 12 GB (Qwen2.5-7B q4: RSS 7,545 MB, PPL 6.91).
  • Pruning caveat: unstructured sparsity gives zero latency or memory benefit on dense kernels — zeroed weights still occupy dense storage.
  • Recommended recipe (INT8 + 30% + recovery): 0.0% accuracy loss on Qwen2.5-7B, <1% on 3 of 4 models; every config with PPL < 22 kept >97% of baseline accuracy.
Strengths
  • Genuinely systematic: a real factorial design with ordering analysis — 120 configurations on consistent hardware, not one-off technique comparisons.
  • Practitioner-ready output: deployment recommendation table plus memory-tier feasibility table are immediately usable.
  • Clean negative result: the teacher signal does not drive LoRA recovery — halving recovery-training memory.
  • Strong validation hygiene: multi-seed checks, full-corpus correlation (r = 0.985), benchmark cross-validation, CPU-side reproduction.
  • Honest failure characterization; ANOVA correctly pins 82% of variance on the Q×P interaction.
Limitations
  • bitsandbytes + PyTorch-L1 only — GPTQ, AWQ, SmoothQuant, Wanda, SparseGPT untested (authors’ own future work).
  • The full 120-config sweep is single-seed; only 16 key configs were multi-seed validated.
  • Recovered models judged by perplexity only — no downstream accuracy, and recovered PPL stays well above baseline.
  • CPU study on one machine class; no mobile ARM/NPU; GGUF q4/q8 differs from the study’s bitsandbytes formats.
  • English-only evaluation; the 200-step, 256-sample recovery recipe is never ablated (rank, steps, data mix).
Questions for further exploration
  1. 01Does the NF4+pruning collapse replicate under GPTQ or AWQ — or is it an artifact of bitsandbytes’ storage-oriented NF4 format?
  2. 02What is the minimum LoRA budget, and can more or domain-specific data close the 24.3 → 11.82 PPL gap for 50%-pruned Qwen2.5-7B?
  3. 03Do recovered-from-collapse models regain downstream accuracy (MMLU, code, math), or does perplexity recovery mask residual capability loss?
  4. 04Would converting to hardware-supported 2:4 semi-structured sparsity change pruning’s cost/benefit on Ampere+ GPUs?
  5. 05Is “recovery scales with model size” (43% at 2.7B vs 97% at 7B) a scaling law, or per-family pruning sensitivity (Llama-3, Mistral, Gemma-2)?
  6. 06Does the collapse manifest differently on multilingual or low-resource inputs, where degradation may be disproportionate?
