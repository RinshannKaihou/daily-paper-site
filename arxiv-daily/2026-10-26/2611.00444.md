> **一句话总结：** CLiS 是一种免训练、即插即用的 TPU 注意力加速方法：它在 KV 循环前用柯西–施瓦茨上界（配合 key 均值中心化）为每个 attention head 认证一个循环不变的 softmax 稳定器，从而跳过逐块行最大值归约与累加器缩放，认证失败时回退到在线 softmax；在 Wan 2.2 与 LTX-2 上（TPU v6e/TPU7x，17K–184K token）相对调优在线 softmax 将注意力 kernel 延迟降低最高 24.0%、DiT 步延迟降低最高 19.6%，VBench 质量无可测退化，而 VFA/FD++/FA4 式的 GPU 稳定化方案在 TPU 上要么变慢要么输出损坏。

> **TL;DR:** Google's CLiS removes online softmax's per-KV-block row-max reduction and accumulator rescaling on TPUs by certifying a fixed per-row stabilizer (Cauchy–Schwarz logit bound after key-mean centering, with a per-head safety certificate and online-softmax fallback). Across 40 workloads on Wan 2.2 and LTX-2 (TPU v6e and TPU7x, 17K–184K tokens), it cuts attention-kernel latency by up to 24.0% and DiT-step latency by up to 19.6% vs a tuned online-softmax baseline (e.g., Wan 2.2 720p/183.6K tokens on TPU v6e: 419.8 ms → 320.7 ms kernel, 19.86 s → 15.99 s per step), with VBench deltas ≈0; controlled TPU ports of FlashDecoding++/VFA/FlashAttention-4 stabilization either slow down or corrupt outputs (VFA fails 9/10 workloads). Preliminary LLM training runs add up to +13.5% throughput (Llama-3.1-8B, PG-19 256K).

## 核心贡献

- **定位新瓶颈**：通过对 TPU 编译器 LLO 层 VLIW 指令束的分析与运行时消融，证明在长序列 TPU 注意力中，在线 softmax 的逐 KV 块"行最大值归约 + 累加器缩放"是可移除的主要开销——在 Wan 2.2 5s 720p（TPU7x）工作负载上，它占可移除 softmax 开销的 91%（去掉归约/缩放 DiT 步延迟 −7.5%，去掉全部 softmax −8.2%）。
- **提出 CLiS（Certified Loop-invariant Stabilization）**：先对 key 做均值中心化（精确算术下不改变注意力，且每行中心化 logit 的最大值 ≥ 0），再用柯西–施瓦茨上界 B_i = ⌈‖q_i‖₂·κ⌉（κ = max‖k̃_j‖₂）为每行 logit 建立可证上界，取循环不变稳定器 μ_i = B_i − C；每个 head 独立检查三个数值安全条件（exp 非零下界、exp 上界、FP32 累加器余量），通过者走固定偏移快速路径，未通过者逐 head 回退到在线 softmax。在精确算术下与标准注意力完全等价。
- **免训练、即插即用**：只改注意力稳定化逻辑，不动模型权重与架构，集成进现有 Pallas 融合注意力 kernel；认证与预处理开销计入全部报告延迟。
- **系统性评估**：在 Wan 2.2 与 LTX-2、TPU v6e 与 TPU7x、480p–1440p、17K–184K token 共 40 个工作负载上，注意力 kernel 延迟降低 9.4–24.0%，DiT 步延迟降低 3.1–19.6%，VBench（110 prompt）无可测质量损失；回退率仅约 2%，且反事实验证表明被拒绝的 84 个 head 位点中有 23 个若强行固定偏移会产生 NaN。
- **超越扩散的初步探索**：因果 LLM 训练（Splash/Ring Attention）上，Qwen3-8B/Llama-3.1-8B/DeepSeek-V3-671B 等前向注意力 kernel 提速 9.6–43.6%，端到端训练吞吐最高 +13.5%（持续预训练）/ +19.0%（DSv3-12L 128K Ring MLA），损失变化在 tile 噪声范围内。

## Key Contributions

- **Identifies a TPU-specific bottleneck**: LLO (compiler VLIW-bundle) analysis plus runtime ablations show that online softmax's repeated row-maximum reduction and accumulator rescaling dominate the removable softmax cost in long-context TPU attention — on Wan 2.2 (5 s, 720p, TPU7x) it accounts for 91% of removable softmax overhead (−7.5% DiT-step latency from removing it vs −8.2% from removing all softmax work). At d=128, vector-unit max/rescale pushes softmax's modeled cycle cost past the matmuls on TPU7x.
- **CLiS method**: key-mean centering (exactly attention-preserving, makes each row's max ≥ 0) + a Cauchy–Schwarz logit bound B_i = ⌈‖q_i‖₂ κ⌉ with κ = max_j ‖k̃_j‖₂, giving a loop-invariant per-row stabilizer μ_i = B_i − C for an integer recentering constant C. A per-head numerical certificate checks three conditions (no all-zero exp row via ρ = 1 margin; exp ≤ U_exp; FP32 accumulator headroom for log2 N_k + value bound G); certified heads skip max-reduction and rescaling entirely, uncertified heads fall back to online softmax. The final partial KV block still uses an online-style rescale, so the output is exact in exact arithmetic.
- **Compiler-aware, training-free integration**: eligibility checks, key mean, and κ are precomputed outside the KV loop; drops into existing fused Pallas attention kernels with no weight or architecture changes; reported latency includes all preprocessing and fallback costs.
- **Comprehensive evaluation**: 40 workloads (Wan 2.2 480p/720p, LTX-2 960p/1440p; TPU v6e + TPU7x; 17K–184K tokens): kernel latency −9.4% to −24.0%, DiT-step latency −3.1% to −19.6% vs tuned online softmax, with VBench deltas ≈ 0 and relative ℓ2 attention error of 2.7×10⁻³ (one BF16 ULP at output magnitude; the online kernel itself differs from exact FP32 by 6.09×10⁻³).
- **Honest controlled baseline ports**: FlashDecoding++-style, VFA-style, and FlashAttention-4-style stabilization are reimplemented inside the same Pallas kernel (same tiles/sharding/precision) — FD++/FA4 give little or negative benefit, VFA corrupts outputs on 9/10 workloads per model, isolating that certification is what makes fixed-shift stabilization safe.
- **Beyond diffusion (preliminary)**: causal Splash/Ring-Attention training on TPU7x — forward attention kernel −9.6% to −43.6%, end-to-end throughput +6.7% to +19.0% (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B PG-19 256K: +13.5%; DSv3-12L 128K ring MLA: +19.0%; DeepSeek-V3-671B 512-chip MLPerf run: Splash kernel −22.1%, time-to-train −6.2%), with final-50 loss within −0.18%..+0.11% (smaller than tile-size noise of ±0.30%–±0.56%).

## Methodology

CLiS targets the stabilization chain of FlashAttention-style online softmax on TPUs. Background: online softmax keeps a running row maximum m, normalization accumulator ℓ, and output accumulator o, updating them for every KV block; each block that raises m forces a rescale of ℓ and o. Although softmax is shift-invariant in exact arithmetic, an arbitrary fixed shift can overflow/underflow in floating point (e.g., BF16 exp2 overflows above +127.99, flushes to zero below −134).

CLiS's pipeline (Algorithm 1), executed per attention head:

1. **Centering**: subtract the key mean k̄ from all keys. This shifts each row's logits by a constant, preserving attention exactly, and guarantees the centered row maximum a_i ≥ 0 (centered logits have row mean zero).
2. **Bounding**: via Cauchy–Schwarz, every centered logit satisfies |s_ij| ≤ B_i where B_i = ⌈‖q_i‖₂ κ⌉ and κ = max_j ‖k̃_j‖₂ — computable before the KV loop at negligible cost (no pass over the N×N scores).
3. **Recentering**: use μ_i = B_i − C for a nonnegative integer C; subtracting C (rather than using B_i directly) tightens the bound against underflow when B_i is loose, allowing bounded positive exp arguments.
4. **Certification**: for each head, check C − B_h − ρ ≥ L_exp (denominator nonzero, margin ρ = 1), C ≤ U_exp (no exp overflow), and C + ⌈log2 N_k⌉ + G < U_acc (FP32 accumulator headroom, where G = ⌈log2 max|V|⌉). If feasible (choose the largest feasible C), the head runs the fixed-shift path; otherwise it falls back to standard online softmax. Certification is per head and done before the loop, so fallback decisions never pollute other heads.
5. **Kernel integration**: in the fixed-shift path, each KV block computes S = Q K̃ᵀ, P = exp2(S − μ_i), and accumulates ℓ += rowsum(P), O += P V — no row max, no α rescale. The tail partial KV block (if any) still performs an online-style max/rescale to stay exact.

The evaluation methodology is notably careful: baselines are *controlled ports* — FD++-style, VFA-style, and FA4-style stabilization rules are embedded in the identical Pallas kernel (same tiling, ring schedule, BF16/FP32 precision), so differences are attributable to the stabilization mechanism rather than a different matmul pipeline. All variants get per-workload tile tuning. Quality is measured with VBench (110 prompts, matched seeds, Wan 2.2 T2V 720p×81f, 40 UniPC steps) plus direct attention-output error against online softmax on captured Q/K/V from a real denoising step. Fallback behavior is audited with a counterfactual replay: the 84 rejected head sites are re-run without the certificate to see what would have failed. LLM experiments cover Qwen3-8B, Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-3-12B (8K pretrain/SFT) and PG-19 128K–256K continual pretraining, plus a 512-chip FP8-MLA DeepSeek-V3-671B run against an MLPerf target.

## Results & Highlights

- **Attention-kernel latency**: CLiS reduces kernel latency by 9.4–24.0% across all 40 measured workloads vs tuned online softmax. Example (Wan 2.2, 720p, 201 frames / 183.6K tokens, TPU v6e): 419.8 ms → 320.7 ms (−23.6%); on LTX-2 1440p 401 frames / 183.6K tokens, TPU v6e: 335.8 ms → 256.6 ms. Figure 1 headline: kernel 159.8 → 122.6 ms (1.30×) and end-to-end video time 335.7 s → 277.7 s (1.21×) on TPU v6e.
- **DiT-step latency**: 3.1–19.6% lower across the grid; e.g., Wan 2.2 720p/183.6K tokens on TPU v6e: 19.855 s → 15.986 s per step (−19.5%); the smaller reductions reflect non-attention computation in the step.
- **Where the win comes from** (motivation ablation, Wan 2.2 5 s 720p, TPU7x, one compiled KV block): fixed stabilization shrinks the compiled schedule from 23,185 to 17,556 VLIW bundles (−24.3%), cuts VPU-only bundles by 94% (6,858 → 384), cuts VREG spill stores by 48%, and roughly halves spill/fill traffic across tile sizes and sequence lengths (e.g., 1,707 GiB → 891 GiB per call, −48%); removing max-reduction+rescale alone gives −7.5% DiT-step latency vs −8.2% for removing all softmax work → stabilization is 91% of the removable cost.
- **Baseline stabilization methods fail on TPU**: FA4-style tile-wise is up to ~2.4× *slower* than online softmax at long lengths (LTX-2 1440p/183.6K, TPU7x: 546.9 ms vs 229.2 ms); FD++-style is slower in most configs (e.g., Wan 2.2 720p/183.6K TPU v6e: 496.3 ms vs 419.8 ms); VFA-style is sometimes fastest but produces corrupted output (NaN / all-zero frames) on 9 of 10 Wan 2.2 and 9 of 10 LTX-2 workloads. CLiS is the only method that is both faster everywhere and valid on 10/10 workloads.
- **Quality**: on VBench (110 prompts, Wan 2.2 720p×81f), CLiS vs online softmax deltas are ≈0 — imaging quality +0.0110 [95% CI +0.0015, +0.0239], subject consistency +0.0015, temporal flickering +0.0004, motion smoothness +0.0002, dynamic degree 0.0000; attention-output relative ℓ2 error 2.7×10⁻³ and max abs error 3.1×10⁻² (one BF16 ULP; the online-softmax kernel itself deviates from exact FP32 by 6.09×10⁻³ rel ℓ2).
- **Fallback behavior** (Wan 2.2 720p×81f, 40 steps): 98.22% of head evaluations are certified (1.78% fallback, stable at ~2% across sequence lengths and denoising steps); 93.05% of reduce-max ops and 95.79% of score-row reductions are eliminated. Counterfactual replay of the 84 rejected sites without fallback: 23 would produce NaN, 1 would flush a ring shard's weights to zero, 60 harmless — the certificate is conservative but catches real failures.
- **LLM training (TPU7x, preliminary)**: end-to-end throughput +6.7% to +8.9% at 8K on Qwen3-8B/Llama-3.1-8B (SFT +7.7% on UltraChat); at 128K–256K PG-19 continual pretraining, forward attention kernel −33.4% to −43.6% and throughput +11.6% to +13.5% (Llama-3.1-8B 256K: loss 2.013 vs 2.013); DeepSeek-V3-671B on 512 chips: Splash kernel −22.1%, wall-clock time-to-MLPerf-target −6.2%, certificate rate 99.9%; DSv3-12L 128K ring MLA: +19.0% throughput. Gemma-3-12B gains only +0.6% because 5/6 of its layers use 1,024-token sliding windows. Loss shifts (−0.18%..+0.11%) are smaller than the ±0.30%–±0.56% noise from changing the KV block size 512→256.
- **Parallelism ablation** (5 sharding configs, Wan 2.2 720p×81f, TPU7x-8): CLiS gives −6.6% to −11.3% kernel and −2.9% to −7.5% DiT-step latency consistently, with pure ring attention (Ulysses=1, Ring=8) fastest (24.66 ms kernel, 1.308 s/step).

## Authors & Credibility

All nine authors (Elisa Tsai, Shao-yu Huang, Daniel Mandragona, Wei-Yu Lin, WenXin Dong, Jevin Jiang, Chandra Devarakonda, Mehdy Bohlool, Yixin Shi) are at Google, with two equal-contribution first authors and a designated corresponding author. The paper reads like a production-informed systems paper: the authors have direct access to TPU v6e/TPU7x hardware, the Pallas/Mosaic/LLO compiler stack internals (they analyze compiled VLIW bundle schedules — something only feasible with deep stack access), and frontier-scale training runs (a 512-chip FP8 DeepSeek-V3-671B MLPerf run). The methodology sections show unusually high rigor for a systems-acceleration paper: controlled same-kernel baseline ports, counterfactual fallback replay, bootstrap CIs on VBench deltas, and an explicit noise-floor comparison (two exact methods differing only by BF16 rounding). No external peer review status is stated (arXiv v1, 30 Oct 2026, cs.LG).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The core observation — replacing online softmax's running maximum with a fixed shift — is not new (FlashDecoding++, VFA, FlashAttention-4 all move in this direction on GPUs). The genuine novelty is (a) the *per-head certificate* with explicit FP-range conditions that makes the fixed shift provably safe rather than empirically calibrated, and (b) the TPU-specific VLIW/LLO analysis showing *why* stabilization (not exponentiation alone) dominates. It is a solid engineering-novelty paper rather than an algorithmic breakthrough.
- **Rigor**: High. Controlled ports isolate the stabilization mechanism; latency numbers include preprocessing and fallback; VBench comparisons use matched seeds and paired bootstrap CIs; the counterfactual replay (23 NaN sites among 84 rejected) directly demonstrates the certificate is not merely conservative overhead. The honesty about the noise floor (online softmax itself has 6.09×10⁻³ rel-ℓ2 error vs exact FP32) is commendable.
- **Reproducibility**: Limited for anyone outside Google. Everything is TPU-specific (Pallas kernels, TPU v6e/TPU7x, Ironwood), hardware most researchers cannot access, and no code release is stated (the *baseline* online-softmax kernel is described as open-sourced; CLiS itself is not claimed to be). The certificate math (Eqs. 8–10, Algorithm 1) is fully specified and reimplementable in principle.
- **Significance caveats**: The headline "up to 19.6%" DiT-step reduction is a best case; many cells in the grid are 3–12%, and the Gemma-3 result (+0.6%) shows the method does little for sliding-window-heavy or short-context architectures. Certification depends on bounded ‖q‖·‖k‖ norms: QK-Norm models (Qwen3, Gemma-3) and pretrained checkpoints certify at 95.5–100%, but Llama-3.1-8B trained from random init *without* QK-Norm exhausts the FP32 exponent budget by step 40 and falls back — a real generalization limit for from-scratch training that the authors themselves demonstrate.
- **Weaknesses in evaluation**: (1) Baselines are "style" adaptations inside a common kernel, not the original GPU systems — the FA4 tile-wise 2–3× slowdowns likely reflect the absence of Blackwell warp-coalescing/pipelining on TPU rather than a refutation of the FA4 idea; the authors flag this but readers may over-read the baseline table. (2) VBench uses a 110-prompt subset with only n=10 prompts per dimension, and only for Wan 2.2 — no quality numbers for LTX-2 at all. (3) The imaging-quality delta +0.0110 with CI excluding zero is almost certainly BF16 trajectory noise presented as (slightly) favorable; the correct reading is "indistinguishable", which the text does say. (4) No exploration of combining CLiS with quantized (SageAttention-style) or sparse attention — only a claim of complementarity. (5) The Cauchy–Schwarz bound can be very loose (worst case ‖q‖‖k‖ vs actual max logit), and looseness costs either underflow risk or tighter C; the paper reports only aggregate fallback rates, not bound tightness distributions.
- **Red flags**: None serious. Mild: the abstract's "up to 13.5% higher training throughput" is labeled "preliminary" and mixes short 1,500-step runs with one large MLPerf run; the LoRA-style breadth (four model families) is impressive but each individual training experiment is small.

## Strengths

- Provably safe: per-head numerical certificate with exact-arithmetic equivalence and a graceful online-softmax fallback — the only fixed-shift method in the comparison that is both faster and valid on every workload.
- Training-free and plug-and-play: no weight changes, no architecture changes, integrates into existing fused-attention kernels; all overheads included in reported numbers.
- Deep hardware grounding: LLO/VLIW bundle-level analysis explains *why* the optimization works (VPU-only bundles −94%, spill stores −48%), not just that it works.
- Exceptionally careful experimental design: controlled same-kernel baseline ports, 40-workload grid across two models/two TPU generations/four resolutions, counterfactual fallback replay, bootstrap CIs, and an explicit noise floor.
- Broad applicability evidence: consistent gains across five parallelism/sharding layouts and from video diffusion inference to causal LLM training up to 512-chip DeepSeek-V3-671B scale.

## Limitations

- TPU-only: the method and evaluation target the TPU VLIW/Pallas stack; no GPU results, and the mechanism's benefit depends on TPU-specific vector-unit costs and register pressure.
- Step-level gains are modest when attention is a small fraction of the step (3.1% floor; Gemma-3-12B +0.6% due to sliding-window layers); the headline 19.6% is the upper tail of a 40-workload grid.
- Certification requires bounded query/key norms; models trained from scratch without QK-Norm can blow the FP32 exponent budget early in training and silently lose the fast path (Llama-3.1-8B random-init case falls back by step 40).
- Cauchy–Schwarz bounds can be loose; the paper does not report how tight B_i typically is or how much performance is lost to conservative rejections (1.78% fallback, but 60 of 84 rejected sites were actually harmless in replay).
- Generation-quality evidence is limited to Wan 2.2 on a 110-prompt VBench subset (n=10 per dimension); LTX-2 is validated only by latency/validity, not quality metrics.
- LLM-training results are preliminary (1,500-step runs, small budgets) and no code release is stated, limiting independent verification.

## Relevance

与高效注意力/长上下文推理优化的研究方向高度相关：视频扩散 Transformer 的长序列（数万至十几万 token）注意力是推理延迟的核心瓶颈，本文提供了一个免训练、可证明数值安全的 kernel 级加速方案，并可推广到因果 LLM 训练；对关注视频生成效率、TPU/异构硬件 kernel 优化以及在线 softmax 数值稳定性的人极具参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How tight is the Cauchy–Schwarz bound B_i in practice — what fraction of the 1.78% fallback (and the conservatism at 60/84 harmless rejected sites) comes from bound looseness vs. genuine logit growth, and would a cheap per-row sampled maximum tighten certification without an extra pass?
- Can CLiS be combined with quantized attention (SageAttention-style INT4/FP4) or sparse/approximate video attention (Sliding Tile Attention, SpargeAttention) — do their error budgets interact safely with the certified fixed shift?
- Does the certificate hold under adversarial or out-of-distribution inputs (extreme prompts, corrupted latents mid-trajectory), where logit distributions may exceed the norm-bound assumptions used here?
- What is the GPU analogue of this cost structure — with FA4 already skipping small rescalings on Blackwell, is there measurable headroom for certified loop-invariant shifts in CUDA kernels, or is the win fundamentally TPU-specific (VPU/XLU dependency chains, shared VREG pressure)?
- For from-scratch training without QK-Norm, could a progressive scheme (online softmax early, certificate enabled once norms stabilize) recover most of the +7–13% training throughput after step 40?
- The LTX-2 quality claim rests only on validity checks — would VBench-style evaluation on LTX-2 (audio-visual model, 24 fps, 1440p) confirm the "no measurable quality loss" finding at higher resolutions?
