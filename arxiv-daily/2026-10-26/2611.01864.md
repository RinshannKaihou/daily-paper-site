> **一句话总结：** 系统研究线性递归（Mamba-2/GLA/GDN/KDA 类）与混合架构 LLM 的量化问题：在受控 1.3B "mixer zoo" 中发现带衰减的递归结构比 softmax 注意力产生更弱的残差离群通道（W4A4 无旋转时 WikiText-2 困惑度恶化 +45.0% vs softmax 的 +98.4%），据此提出 LoReS 状态量化流水线（值轴分组 + 随机舍入 + 按衰减分配混合精度），在 Qwen-3.5 9B / Nemotron-H 8B / Kimi-Linear 48B-A3B 上以 INT8 状态几乎无损（Qwen-3.5 GSM8K 96.4 vs FP32 状态的 95.9），并在 vLLM 中实现端到端解码加速（Nemotron-H batch 256 时 TPOT 17.32 vs 29.35 ms，1.70×）。

> **TL;DR:** A systematic PTQ study that splits recurrent-LLM quantization into extra-state W/A and intra-state (recurrent state) targets. In a controlled 1.3B mixer zoo, decayed linear recurrences develop much weaker residual-stream outliers than softmax attention — unrotated W4A4 costs +45.0% relative WikiText-2 perplexity for the best recurrent variant vs +98.4% for softmax, though rotations close the gap (+9.3% vs +10.6%). For the state itself, the LoReS recipe (value-axis scale grouping + stochastic rounding + decay-based mixed precision) makes INT8 states nearly free on the production hybrids Qwen-3.5 9B, Nemotron-H 8B, and Kimi-Linear 48B-A3B (e.g., Qwen-3.5 16/16/8 GSM8K 96.4 vs 95.9 at 16/16/32; Nemotron-H AIME 48.4 vs 51.2), supports ~5–5.5 effective state bits on short-decode tasks like GSM8K, and delivers up to 1.70× TPOT speedup in vLLM on an H100 at batch 256. The main caveats: long-decode benchmarks (AIME/GPQA) degrade most under low-bit states, 4-bit weights hurt far more than 8-bit states, and there is no head-to-head against Q-Mamba/Quamba2.

## 核心贡献

- **提出"额外状态 vs 状态内"（extra-state vs intra-state）量化二分法**：把"序列混合器改变权重/激活分布"（隐式影响，训练塑造）与"量化解码时反复原地更新的固定大小递归状态"（显式影响，KV cache 所没有的机制）分开研究——这是理解递归 LLM 量化的正确概念框架。
- **受控 1.3B "mixer zoo" 实验证明带衰减的递归对 W/A 量化更鲁棒**：所有变体（softmax±QK-norm、Linear attention、Mamba-2、GLA、GDN、KDA）共享同一骨干与训练配方（24 层、hidden 2048、100B tokens），唯一变量是混合器。无旋转 W4A4 下对角衰减递归的困惑度恶化远小于 softmax；残差流峰度（kurtosis）与量化退化强相关。机制解释：softmax 注意力需要一个近似常数的巨大残差通道（在 QK 前删除它使困惑度 +1691.3%，而对角衰减模型仅 +2.2%），而衰减本身就提供了聚焦机制，无需依赖这种"注意力汇聚"式残差结构。
- **状态量化的系统理论 + 实验分析**：三个命题刻画状态量化误差——(1) 值轴分组的逐通道读出误差与各行最大幅值成正比（因此大行不会拖累其他行），值轴分组在 INT8/INT6 几乎全面优于键轴分组；(2) 衰减以 α^(t−τ) 指数压制历史量化误差；(3) RTN 下不同时刻的舍入误差会相互强化（交叉项），而随机舍入（SR）在期望下消除这种强化。
- **揭示随机旋转在重复状态量化中的失效模式**：KV-cache 量化常用的 Hadamard 旋转在解码态反复再量化时会形成"尺度反馈环"（噪声状态→更大量化尺度→更粗网格→更大误差→更大尺度），且旋转会把舍入误差对齐到查询方向——即使给出 oracle 尺度，部分模型（Qwen-3.5）仍有旋转惩罚。这解释了为何 LoReS 选择不旋转的整数网格。
- **LoReS 流水线 + vLLM 实现**：整合为"值轴分组 + 对称 absmax + SR + 按衰减提升慢衰减头的精度"的实用方案，默认 INT8 状态；在三大生产级混合模型上验证精度，并开源了带 INT8 状态解码内核的 vLLM 实现，给出内核级与端到端（TPOT）加速。

## Key Contributions

- **A clean conceptual split of the problem**: "extra-state" W/A quantization (the mixer only indirectly shapes learned weight/activation distributions) versus "intra-state" quantization of the fixed-size recurrent state that is dequantized, updated, and requantized after every decode step — a fundamentally different object from a write-once KV cache.
- **Controlled evidence that decayed recurrences are easier to quantize (W/A)**: a 1.3B mixer zoo (identical backbone, 100B-token training recipe; only the mixer varies: softmax ± QK-norm, linear attention, Mamba-2, GLA, GDN, KDA) shows decayed recurrences develop far weaker residual-stream outlier channels than softmax attention; residual kurtosis tracks unrotated W4A4 degradation. Mechanistically, softmax models rely on a near-constant dominant residual channel (removing it before QK costs +1691.3% perplexity vs +2.2% for the diagonal-decay variant), plausibly because decay already provides the token-focusing that attention sinks/massive activations provide for softmax.
- **Theory matched to experiments for state quantization**: three propositions — per-channel readout error under value- vs key-axis grouping (value-axis ties error to each row's own range); exponential suppression of injected error by decay (supporting decay-based mixed precision); and an exact decomposition showing RTN's cross-step error-reinforcement terms vanish in expectation under stochastic rounding.
- **Failure-mode analysis of random rotations** — a genuinely novel negative result: Hadamard rotations, standard for KV-cache/transformer PTQ, can hurt under repeated state requantization via (a) scale feedback loops (scales derived from an already-noisy state inflate over decode steps) and (b) query alignment (rotation leaks rounding noise into normally suppressed query dimensions). Isolated with oracle-scale and alignment diagnostics.
- **LoReS + open vLLM implementation**: value-axis grouping + symmetric absmax + SR + decay-ranked precision promotion; validated end-to-end on Qwen-3.5 9B, Nemotron-H 8B, and Kimi-Linear 48B-A3B, with INT8-vs-FP32-state kernel speedups (Mamba-2 kernel >2× at batch 8) and end-to-end TPOT gains (code at github.com/camail-official/LoReS).

## Methodology

The paper runs two coordinated studies.

**Extra-state W/A study (Section 3).** A "mixer zoo" of 1.3B models (24 layers, hidden 2048, MLP ratio 4, 16 mixer heads × head dim 128, trained 100B tokens at lr 4e-4, identical scaffold: short convs + SiLU + RoPE, gated RMSNorm output) varies only the sequence mixer, covering softmax attention (± QK-RMSNorm), plain linear attention, Mamba-2-style scalar decay, GLA/KDA-style diagonal decay, and gated/delta variants. All models go through the same GPTQ stack (per-output-channel weight scales, dynamic per-token activation scales, 128-channel groups at mixer output projections; 128×2048-token WikiText-2 calibration sequences) plus three optional Hadamard rotations: R1 (residual stream), R4 (MLP down-proj input), Ro (mixer output-proj input). Metric: relative WikiText-2 perplexity change. They complement this with residual-stream intervention experiments (remove/mean-replace the dominant channel before QK, V, or the mixer input) and kurtosis correlations to explain *why* softmax is harder.

**Intra-state study (Section 4).** State updates stay in full precision; only the stored state S_t is quantized. Evaluation is teacher-forced decode over eight 2048-token WikiText-2 sequences, with the core metric Δℓ = median token-level cross-entropy increase vs the full-precision FP32 trajectory (Appendix G.1 validates that Δℓ correlates with generation accuracy). They ablate: (i) scale-grouping axis (value rows vs key columns vs single scale), analyzed via a per-channel readout-error proposition; (ii) decay-based mixed precision — rank heads/channels by mean calibration decay, promote the slowest-decaying half from INT4 to INT6 (yielding "INT5" effective), justified by a proposition bounding accumulated error by Σ α^(t−τ)‖ε_τ‖; (iii) RTN vs stochastic rounding, with an exact decomposition showing RTN's pairwise error-reinforcement terms; (iv) within-group rotations R_k/R_v, dissected into scale-feedback-loop effects (measured via an oracle full-precision copy of the state providing scales) and query-alignment effects (a_t = d_k·E‖ε_t q_t‖²/E‖ε_t‖²_F); (v) INT8 vs FP8-E4M3 formats.

**End-to-end (Section 5).** LoReS = unrotated integer grids + value-axis grouping + symmetric absmax + SR, INT8 default, decay-based promotion for lower effective precision. W/A via llm-compressor GPTQ (group size 128), served in vLLM. Benchmarks: 8-shot GSM8K (8k decode cap), MMLU-Pro, GPQA-diamond, AIME 2024+2025 (60 problems, avg@32), RULER at 32k context. Kernel-level timing uses CUDA graphs with launch tuning; end-to-end TPOT is the median of seven paired repetitions on a dedicated H100 SXM5 with synthetic prompts (31 timed decode steps, prefill excluded).

## Results & Highlights

- **Decayed recurrences are more robust to unrotated W4A4 (1.3B mixer zoo, WikiText-2 relative perplexity change, Table 2):** softmax attention degrades +98.4% under W4A4 with no rotation (+92.3% with QK-norm) vs +45.0% for the best diagonal-decay recurrent variant — but the rotation stack largely closes the gap (softmax +10.6% with Ro vs +9.3% for diagonal decay), so the recurrent advantage is specific to rotation-free regimes.
- **The dominant residual channel matters enormously for softmax but not for decayed recurrences (Table 3):** removing the single dominant residual channel before the Q/K projections raises WikiText-2 perplexity by +1691.3% for softmax attention but only +2.2% for the diagonal-decay (KDA-style) model — direct evidence that decay removes the need for attention-sink-like massive activations.
- **Value-axis grouping beats key-axis grouping for the state (Table 4, Δℓ at INT8):** e.g., Qwen-3.5 0.001 (value) vs 0.010 (key), Nemotron-H 0.001 vs 0.019; at INT6 the gap widens (Qwen-3.5 0.004 vs 0.157, ~39×). At INT4 errors explode for all models (Qwen-3.5 0.825 even with value grouping), motivating mixed precision.
- **Decay-based mixed precision recovers most of the INT4→INT6 gap (Table 5):** promoting the slowest-decaying half of heads from INT4 to INT6 ("INT5" effective) takes Qwen-3.5 from Δℓ 0.825 (INT4) to 0.006 — nearly the full-INT6 value of 0.004; Nemotron-H 0.763 → 0.045 (INT6: 0.010); Kimi-Linear 0.056 → 0.006 (INT6: 0.005).
- **Stochastic rounding consistently helps the production hybrids (Table 6, INT6, value grouping):** Δℓ drops from RTN 0.0105 to SR 0.0026 for Nemotron-H (4×), 0.0046 → 0.0007 for Kimi-Linear (~6.6×), 0.0039 → 0.0016 for Qwen-3.5; results are mixed on the small zoo models.
- **INT8 states are nearly free end-to-end (Table 8):** with BF16 weights, going from FP32 to INT8 states (16/16/8 vs 16/16/32) moves Qwen-3.5 from GSM8K 95.9 → 96.4, MMLU-Pro 82.6 → 82.2, RULER 94.6 → 94.7, with the largest loss on long-decode AIME 82.3 → 79.5; Nemotron-H AIME 51.2 → 48.4; Kimi-Linear stays essentially flat (GSM8K 89.1 → 89.2, MMLU-Pro 70.3 → 70.2). The full 8/8/8 stack keeps Qwen-3.5 at 96.5 GSM8K / 82.2 MMLU-Pro / 79.8 GPQA. Losses concentrate in 4-bit-weight rows (e.g., Nemotron-H AIME 51.2 → 41.0 at 4/8/8; Kimi-Linear GPQA 63.5 → 52.6), i.e., weights — not states — are the bottleneck.
- **Effective precision can go below 8 bits on short-decode tasks (Figure 6):** on GSM8K, Qwen-3.5 supports 5 effective state bits and Nemotron-H 5.5 bits with <5% relative accuracy drop vs the FP32-state baseline; GPQA/AIME need more bits due to longer decode trajectories.
- **Real speedups in vLLM on H100:** INT8 vs FP32 states more than double Mamba-2 mixer-kernel throughput at decode batch 8 (Figure 8); end-to-end with BF16 weights, INT8 states cut Nemotron-H TPOT from 29.35 → 17.32 ms at batch 256 (1.70×) and Qwen-3.5 from 18.23 → 14.21 ms (1.28×) (Table 24); speedup grows with batch since each sequence carries its own state, while weight quantization (INT4) helps most at small batch — the two are complementary (Figure 7).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The framing (extra-state vs intra-state) and the controlled mixer-zoo methodology are genuinely novel and the right way to isolate the mixer's effect. Individual components are less novel: SR for state quantization appeared in Nemotron 3 Super (Chandiramani et al., 2026), decay-based mixed precision in concurrent work (Zhang et al., 2026; Yao et al., 2026), and Q-Mamba/Quamba2 already quantized Mamba states — the authors acknowledge this openly. The rotation-failure-mode analysis (scale feedback loops, query alignment) is the most original technical finding.
- **Rigor**: Strong overall — controlled 1.3B comparisons with a shared training recipe, theory validated by targeted experiments (oracle scales to isolate scale inflation, injected-error tracking for decay), and careful paired timing. Weak spots: the headline "easier than you think" is partly self-undercut — their own Table 2 shows incoherence processing largely closes the W4A4 gap (softmax +10.6% with Ro vs +9.3% for the best recurrent), so the practical advantage is confined to rotation-free pipelines; the Δℓ proxy rests on only eight 2048-token WikiText-2 sequences; and there is **no head-to-head comparison against Q-Mamba, Quamba2, or SSDi8 state-quantization baselines** in any table — only self-ablations of grouping/rounding/format, so LoReS's superiority over prior Mamba-specific recipes is asserted by construction rather than measured.
- **Reproducibility**: High — code released, zoo training recipe published (100B tokens is expensive but specified exactly), vLLM kernels and measurement protocol (seven paired repetitions, alternating order, fresh processes) documented in unusual detail.
- **Significance**: Timely and practical: hybrid recurrent-transformer models (Qwen-3.5, Nemotron-H, Kimi-Linear) are entering production, and nobody had systematically worked out what PTQ transfers and what doesn't. The negative rotation result is directly actionable for anyone porting QuaRot/SpinQuant-style pipelines to recurrent models.
- **Red flags** (minor): the 1.3B zoo scale leaves open whether the outlier-channel finding holds at larger pure-recurrent scale (they show transfer only via the three hybrids, which are 8B–48B but not retrained controls); end-to-end TPOT uses synthetic prompts and only 31 timed decode tokens with prefill excluded — a narrow serving scenario; and the batch-1 INT8-state speedup is ≈1.0×, so single-request latency users see little benefit; benchmarks where the method struggles (AIME at 4-bit weights, Kimi-Linear GPQA 63.5 → 52.6) are present but de-emphasized relative to the headline.

## Strengths

- Correct problem decomposition (extra-state vs intra-state) that maps cleanly onto how practitioners actually deploy PTQ stacks.
- Controlled experiments that isolate one variable at a time — the mixer-zoo design (same backbone, same 100B-token recipe) is the only clean way to answer "does the mixer change the quantization problem?" and the authors built it.
- Theory that is simple, proven, and immediately operational (grouping-axis choice, decay-based bit allocation, SR-vs-RTN error decomposition) rather than decorative.
- A rare, well-diagnosed *negative* result (random rotations can hurt repeated state quantization) with two distinct mechanisms isolated via oracle-scale and query-alignment experiments.
- Production-grade validation: three real hybrid checkpoints (9B/8B/48B-A3B), five benchmarks including long-context RULER at 32k, a vLLM implementation, and released code.

## Limitations

- The W4A4 robustness advantage of recurrent models largely disappears once rotations are applied, so the "easier" claim mainly benefits stacks that skip incoherence processing.
- No direct comparison against prior recurrent-state quantization methods (Q-Mamba, Quamba2, SSDi8) under a shared protocol.
- Long-decode tasks (AIME, GPQA) show the largest state-quantization losses — the low-bit-state regime is safest exactly where decoding is shortest, which limits applicability to long reasoning chains; the authors defer quantization-aware training to future work.
- Controlled conclusions come from 1.3B models trained on 100B tokens; transfer to larger scales is evidenced only indirectly through the post-trained hybrids.
- The Δℓ proxy uses a small calibration/eval set (eight 2048-token WikiText-2 sequences), and WikiText-2 itself is a small, dated corpus.
- End-to-end speedups are measured on synthetic prompts with 32 output tokens, TPOT-only (prefill excluded), single H100; real workloads with longer generations and mixed prefill/decode may differ, and gains at batch 1 are negligible.

## Relevance

该工作 squarely matches "Reliability under Extreme Performance Optimization"：这篇论文的核心正是激进推理优化（低比特量化、混合精度、随机舍入）引入的精度与正确性问题——它不仅给出误差如何在递归状态中随时间传播/强化的理论刻画，还揭示了把 Transformer 量化技巧（随机旋转、KV-cache 分组）照搬到递归模型时的失效模式，并用生产级混合模型验证了数值可靠性边界（何时 INT8/INT5 安全、何时 AIME/GPQA 会掉分）。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would learned rotations (SpinQuant-style optimization of R_k/R_v against a decode-aware loss) avoid the two identified failure modes (scale feedback loops, query alignment), or is the rotation penalty fundamental to requantization?
- Can quantization-aware or low-precision training close the 4-bit-weight gap (Nemotron-H AIME 51.2 → 41.0 at 4/8/8), as the authors hypothesize?
- How does LoReS interact with low-precision *chunked prefill* paths like SSDi8 — can state storage and the SSD compute path share one bit budget coherently?
- Does the weaker-residual-outlier finding persist at larger pure-recurrent scale (≥7B trained from scratch), and does it change under RL post-training / reasoning-style training that produces very long decodes?
- The Δℓ proxy is validated against generation accuracy on short tasks; how well does it predict degradation on 32k+-token agentic decode trajectories where error accumulation compounds?
- Could GPU-native microscaling formats (MXFP) replace the decay-based per-head promotion with finer-grained hardware-managed mixed precision, as the authors suggest?
