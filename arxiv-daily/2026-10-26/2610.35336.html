Why this matters

Replaces building and timing 81 candidate designs with minutes of profiling and simulation — a direct playbook for adopting megakernels in low-latency TTS/LLM serving stacks.

相关度：直接回答流式推理 serving 中 kernel 融合该推进到哪里的工程问题，对 TensorRT / vLLM / PyTorch 栈的 megakernel 化落地有直接指导意义。

⚠ Critical edge

The 13/13 record holds only within the pool of designs the authors could build and qualify — not all 81 candidates — and the 4.05× headline beats a baseline that never overlaps producer and consumer.

from Critical Assessment · “the pools contain the designs we could build… not every candidate”
Four mechanistic laws — when fusion pays, when it hurts
LAW 01

Faster ≠ better

A kernel made 2.2% faster alone (88 vs 80 CTAs) slowed the whole pipeline by 30.3% — it broke the colocation contract.

LAW 02

Colocation cost is peer-dependent

Beside the producer megakernel, a conventional codec slows 7.57×; a codec megakernel only 1.23×.

LAW 03

Serial cuts are neutral

All eight all-megakernel partitions predict the identical 37.067 ms TTFA p95 — specialization, not the boundary, explains the monolith's loss.

LAW 04

Fixed cost sets the crossover

Megakernel fixed costs of 0.7–1.1 ms mean conventional execution wins as batch grows — Qwen3-TTS flips conventional at B48+.

Methodology

Three graph layers, built purely from conventional-graph profiles, standalone kernel benchmarks, and source-declared contracts — no candidate is ever built or timed.

① rGraph — enumerate candidates
Profile the conventional graph; classify operator groups (admission / producer / consumer); enumerate contiguous-region covers × backend × execution policy → 81 candidates for NH-TTS.
↓
② tGraph — cost each region
Event-driven tile scheduler over W CTAs models barrier removal, weight prefetch, on-chip forwarding, register pressure → service table S[g, β, W; κ].
↓
③ evGraph — simulate to first audio
Simulate each candidate to first publication (Tpub = max(first chunk, producer horizon)) with CTA admission control and shared-bandwidth contention; keep all minimum-score candidates.

Evaluation hygiene: planner frozen before any end-to-end timing · dev/eval separation · correctness gates vs an unfused reference (exact index match, L2 ≤ 10⁻²) · randomized balanced measurement with 20,000-resample paired bootstrap CIs.

Selected layout: P / (B+L) / C with a dynamic 88/44 producer-consumer CTA split. Same TTFA ordering at every batch size tested.

  • Perfect selection, costly to guess: 13/13 held-out model-batch cases uniquely fastest (3 models × 3 frameworks); next-best layouts are 3–29% slower. Ablations (additive scoring, no-overlap) drop to 11/13, max regret 4.50%.
  • Qwen3-TTS flips conventional at B48/B64: the four-megakernel lead shrinks from 15.9 ms (B16) to 0.65 ms (B64); at B1 the order reverses (two megakernels 8.1% slower than four).
  • Ranking, not calibration: absolute predictions run 6–22% high (42.52 predicted vs 35.99 ms measured at B32) — closest calls still held within their 95% bootstrap CIs.
  • Llama-1B — specialization beats boundaries: two megakernels beat the monolith even on first-token latency; the fused entry spills registers (prefill layer 316 vs 276 µs). Stage-1 megakernel: 1.290 ms vs TensorRT's 9.760 ms.
  • Policy choices ranked correctly too: the 80/52 CTA split is 1.0% slower than 88/44 at B32; serial execution is 4.2% slower than overlap at B64.
  • Planner inputs are cheap: 4.6 GPU-min (NH-TTS) and 15.9 GPU-min (Qwen3-TTS) of kernel benchmarks — versus building and timing 81 designs. The illustrative overlap math: serial 30.1 ms vs overlapped 28.2 ms with slower components.
Strengths
  • Attacks a real, expensive problem — replaces exhaustive 81-design timing with minutes of profiling plus simulation.
  • Unusually strong evaluation hygiene: frozen planner, dev/eval separation, correctness gating, randomized measurement, paired bootstrap CIs, honest scope statements.
  • Goes beyond ranking to mechanism: four transferable observations, each backed by a concrete experiment.
  • Cross-framework generality (TensorRT-LLM, vLLM, PyTorch) rather than a single-stack solution.
  • The overlap arithmetic (30.1 → 28.2 ms despite slower components) makes the core trade-off easy to internalize.
Limitations
  • Single GPU architecture (H100 NVL, 132 SMs) with pinned CUDA/driver versions — no cross-platform transfer evidence.
  • The central model (NH-TTS) is unreleased, and no public code or artifact is referenced — independent verification is essentially impossible.
  • Absolute-time predictions deviate up to 22% for the winning design family — usable for ranking only, not SLA planning.
  • Requires hand-declared source contracts (thread counts, tile geometry, sync policies) per model — engineering work, not push-button.
  • Optimizes TTFA/TTFT only; steady-state ITL, throughput under load, and multi-tenant serving are out of scope — and two of the four taxonomy categories are unvalidated predictions.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Does selection transfer across GPU architectures (Blackwell, AMD MI-series), or must the service table be re-calibrated per device?
  2. Can software-level scheduling prevent the 7.57× interference between a co-located conventional graph and a megakernel?
  3. Would jointly optimizing first-publication time with steady-state ITL / throughput change the selected compositions?
  4. Can the source-declared implementation contracts be inferred automatically from profiler traces or compiler IR?
  5. Does the taxonomy hold for larger LLMs (≥7B) and MoE models where prefill is compute-bound?
