Critical edge

No latency, throughput, or kernel results anywhere — PW's per-decode-step branch overhead is entirely unmeasured, the biggest practical gap for a deployment-motivated paper.

Closest rivals DAMP & LeapQuant are cited but never benchmarked; headline sets are small (AIME n=30, HMMT n=33) with no confidence intervals.

Methodology two orthogonal fixes on STEPQuant's quantizers & budget

GDN's state follows Sₜ = AₜSₜ₋₁ + Bₜ. Under stepwise quantized writeback — Ŝₜ = Q(AₜŜₜ₋₁ + Bₜ) — every quantization error is fed back into all future recurrence steps. That is the root cause: long chain-of-thought collapses while short tasks barely notice.

AGGA — Global Gradient-aware Allocation

offline calibration only · zero backprop at deployment

On recorded FP16 trajectories, gradients of the future next-token loss are propagated backward through the reference transitions as writeback adjoints Gʷᵇ. Candidate quantization residuals are weighted by squared adjoints → a per-head risk for each width ∈ {2, 4, 6, 8}; two-stage dynamic programming then assigns widths — plus 32 FP16 pivots — across all 768 heads at an unchanged bit budget.

WikiText-2 · 32×2048 tokens→ writeback adjoints Gʷᵇ→ DP over 768 heads→ frozen bit-width table

BPW — Pure-Write Sliding Window

per decode step · queue cost 1.03 bit/value

The pure-write term is rank-one, so the last m=8 writes stay exact as FP8 vector pairs (pⱼ, vⱼ): left factors propagate through the true transition at O(m·dₖ); values stay frozen. Readout = low-bit history + all branches + the current FP32 write; only after readout does the oldest branch merge into history and re-quantize. History is still quantized every step — each write's first low-bit incorporation is merely delayed. Exactly equivalent under exact arithmetic (Appendix A).

  • Shorter, not truncated — mean output length @4 on 9B: 55.4K → 37.8K (GGA) → 23.9K tokens; on AIME-9B, 93.5K → 43.2K while accuracy climbs 2.67% → 84.00%.
  • Abnormal stops nearly eliminated — 100K-cap hits 30.67% → 2.44%, repetition stops 9.71% → 0.10%; natural EOS 97.47% (FP16: 94.11%).
  • Same seed, with vs. without PW (HMMT) — without: 831 consecutive “Wait.” lines, repetition-stopped at 81,921 tokens, no answer; with: natural EOS at 57,682 tokens, correct answer 48.
  • Window beats raw bit-width at matched storage — @4+PW-S8 (~5.78 bit/value) outscores and shortens @5 (~5.68) under both allocations.
  • Also effective @6 — macro 73.96% / 79.88% vs STEPQuant@6's 70.54% / 76.99%, with all seven tasks shortening on both models.
  • Mechanism check — at the same 4.33 bit/value average, GGA gives the most KL-sensitive head bins 4.56–6.96 bits where STEPQuant gave 3.73: allocation now tracks measured sensitivity.

Strengths what holds up

  • Pins a real, under-addressed failure mode — quantized recurrent states destroy long multi-step reasoning (AIME/HMMT ≈ 0–4% under STEPQuant@4) — with a token-level case study, not just aggregate scores.
  • Clean attribution: GGA changes only the allocation objective (same budget, same quantizers), so the +19.5 / +29.8-pt macro gains belong to gradient-guided sensitivity alone.
  • PW is theoretically grounded — exact recurrence-equivalence proof; the algorithm is fully specified down to FP8 codes, scales, and per-step pseudocode.
  • Evaluation hygiene above the preprint norm: frozen problem lists, shared per-problem seeds, byte-exact storage accounting, EOS / cap / repetition terminations counted separately.
  • Shorter outputs co-occur with higher accuracy — recovery of reasoning, not early truncation.

Limitations read with care

  • One model family only (Qwen3.5-4B/9B); no GLA or Mamba-2 — and the closest rivals DAMP and LeapQuant are cited but never benchmarked, so “better than the field” rests on one competitor.
  • Single PW configuration (m=8, FP8): no window-length, queue-precision, or merge-strategy ablations.
  • No latency / throughput / kernel results; per-step branch overhead unquantified, and queues are wastefully reserved even for FP16 pivot heads.
  • Small headline sets (AIME n=30, HMMT n=33) with no confidence intervals; @6+PW is recalibrated differently from GGA@6, muddying component attribution at the higher budget.
  • The repetitive “Wait.”-loop mechanism remains observational — flagged as future work.

Questions for further exploration open threads

  1. Is m=8 optimal for all seven tasks, or do short-output tasks (IFBench, HumanEval+) need far less window than AIME/HMMT?
  2. Would INT8/INT4 queue encodings with per-vector scales keep most of the benefit at half the queue cost?
  3. What is PW's real wall-clock cost at serving batch sizes — can branch propagation fuse into the GDN kernel, or does it erase the storage win?
  4. Would GGA calibration on reasoning-style data (vs WikiText-2) further improve allocation for math/code workloads?
  5. Do GGA and PW transfer to GLA, Mamba-2, or full-attention KV-cache — where the write-age decomposition has no exact rank-one analogue?
  6. Is PW merely masking a pre-existing fragility of long chain-of-thought decoding that better decoding strategies could also fix?
