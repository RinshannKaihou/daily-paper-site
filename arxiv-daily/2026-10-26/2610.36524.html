Key finding · arXiv 2610.36524

In a frozen transformer, 1–12 causally-selected attention heads remove up to 91.6% of the evidence-count effect — and those heads transfer to natural language.

一句话总结（摘录）　受控证据聚合（CEA）探针 + 双干预因果选择（BUC）：冻结 Pythia-410M 中仅 1–12 个注意力头即移除 65.5–91.6% 的「计数效应」，且仅在符号提示上选出的头集在自然语言证据任务上使准确率下降 8.2 个百分点（随机对照仅 0.8）。

Why this matters

A complete recipe for causally bridging controlled probes and real language behavior — directly reusable for locating how models use context evidence in RAG.

提供在受控探针与真实自然语言行为之间建立因果桥梁的完整方法论模板（双干预选择、层级匹配对照、迁移比率），可直接借鉴到检索增强场景中「模型如何使用上下文证据」的定位研究。

Critical edge

The greedy guarantee covers the raw utility Jλ, not the normalized objective BUC actually optimizes — and the paper's own audits find 17–31% negative marginals, violating its submodularity assumption.

registered scan: only ~6% of 1024 fixed-set readout conditions meet strong-reuse thresholds

Methodology — CEA probe → BUC search

Frozen pythia-410m-deduped (24 layers × 16 heads); original study in BF16, all audits re-run in native FP32, never pooled.

  1. 01CEA behavioral probe. Fixed template of 200 evidence symbols (B vs C; counts e.g. 50/150) + 50-symbol tail; four orderings (FRONT / BACK / INTERLEAVE / RANDOM). Readout = logit difference of two answer tokens; no answer is "correct", so no rule is trained.
  2. 02Attribution shortlist. Rank all 192 heads by the contrast |Δsi| of their unembedding-projected writes.
  3. 03Dual-intervention screen. 48 heads survive single-head tests: shrink (count effect removed by ablation) + match (bidirectional mean-patching recovery, overshoot penalized).
  4. 04Greedy BUC search. Sets of ≤12 heads maximizing Qλ = ½·shrink + ½·match, evaluated jointly in one forward pass; FP32 rule accepts the first prefix with shrink ≥ 0.85 and match ≥ 0.70.
  5. 05Transfer test. Head sets chosen on symbolic prompts only, then evaluated on a lexically balanced natural-language bridge (16 reports, two states, each state word appearing exactly 8×, queried in both directions so shortcuts fail).

Controls: 120 layer-matched random head draws per seed × 5 execution seeds, plus a selection-matched exchangeability null (99 trials, Holm-corrected p = 0.01). Scope checks: QK/OV splits, LAST vs SEQ positions, Pythia-70M–1B / GPT-2 / Qwen3-4B, Greedy Occlusion baseline.

Strengths

  • Dual-intervention criterion — necessity (ablation) + bidirectional recovery (mean patching, overshoot penalized) — is materially more rigorous than ablation-only circuit discovery, and directly answers the known ablation-dependence critique.
  • The lexically balanced NL bridge defeats bag-of-words and orientation shortcuts; the 8.2 pp vs 0.8 pp transfer is a genuinely non-trivial finding.
  • Exceptional self-auditing culture: registered readout scans, a selection-matched null with Holm correction, explicit CI accounting (Appendix H), BF16/FP32 separation.
  • Named, exactly-stable head identities across seeds (e.g., FRONT = single head (10,2)) make the results reproducible.
  • Broad scope checks (QK/OV, LAST/SEQ, scaling to Qwen3-4B, Greedy Occlusion) preempt the obvious objections.

Limitations

  • Main results rest on one model family (Pythia-410M); the 1-head FRONT result may be idiosyncratic to this checkpoint.
  • Compactness is regime-dependent — BACK fails the joint threshold even with the full 12-head budget (shrink 0.655) — and compactness ≠ uniqueness or global minimality.
  • The headline 8.2 pp lacks the bootstrap intervals its own declared criterion requires; FP32 bridges use one causal-selection seed and no model-level CIs.
  • Fixed-set reuse across arbitrary readouts is rare (~6% of registered conditions); regime transfer is asymmetric (BACK→FRONT match 0.958 vs 0.129 reverse).
  • Count is confounded with symbol position; evidence aggregation cannot be separated from yes/no answer readout; only final-position head writes are intervened.
  • Greedy theory does not cover the normalized objective actually optimized — no selection-quality guarantee beyond finite-pool audits.

Questions for further exploration

  1. Would heads (10,2), (17,3), (15,10) keep their causal role on longer, heterogeneous real-world evidence — RAG passages, multi-document summaries — where counts are implicit?
  2. Can evidence aggregation be causally separated from yes/no answer readout, e.g. by intervening at earlier positions or along head–MLP paths?
  3. Why does FRONT collapse to a single head while BACK needs 12 and still fails the shrink threshold — positional attention, or the checkpoint?
  4. Given only ~6% strong-reuse among registered readout conditions, how selection-biased were the seven targeted readout pairs — and would pre-registration shrink the TR ≈ 0.44–0.56 transfer?
  5. Would position-controlled variants (same counts, shifted placements) select different head sets, given the count-vs-position confound?
