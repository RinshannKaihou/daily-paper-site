> **一句话总结：** 本文揭示了长时程预训练中"定时炸弹式"损失发散的隐藏机制——Adam 预条件器导致的输出 logit 均值漂移在精确算术下无害，但在 BF16 有限精度下使 LM head 的量化误差在发散前暴涨几个数量级；仅将 LM head 的优化器换成动量 0.99 的 SGD 即可压制漂移、消除此类发散（404M 模型、峰值 LR 0.3 下最终 eval loss 3.167 vs AdamW 发散至 28.24），且 z-loss、µ-centering 等既有方法的有效性均可归因于同一机理：保持量化误差有界。

> **TL;DR:** The authors study "time-bomb spikes" — delayed, unrecoverable loss divergences that appear late in overtraining — using 28M–1.4B GPT-2-style models on FineWeb (up to 28× the Chinchilla-optimal token budget). They show the mean-logit drift induced by Adam's preconditioner is harmless in exact arithmetic but inflates BF16 quantization error in the LM head until training collapses: at peak LR 0.1, all 5/5 BF16-true seeds and 5/5 BF16-mixed seeds time-bomb while 0/5 FP32 seeds fail. Their fix — replacing AdamW with SGD-momentum (β=0.99) on just the LM head — is the only method stable across the whole spike-prone LR sweep (404M, LR 0.3: final eval loss 3.167 vs 28.241 for spiked AdamW, and 3.178±0.002 vs 3.916±0.278 at LR 0.15 over 5 seeds), and they unify z-loss, µ-centering, and LM-head SGD-M as different routes to the same goal: keeping LM-head quantization error bounded.

## 核心贡献

- **定义并刻画"定时炸弹式"损失尖峰（time-bomb spikes）**：一类仅在长时间过度训练后才出现的缓慢、不可恢复的发散。与快速瞬态尖峰不同，它在带可学习增益的 RMSNorm 架构中依然存在，且训练时程越长越严重——随着业界普遍超出 Chinchilla 最优 token 预算训练，这类不稳定性愈发重要。
- **用有限精度解开"无害漂移"悖论**：理论上证明 Adam 预条件器打破交叉熵梯度沿词表轴的零和结构、导致均值输出 logit 漂移，但该漂移在精确算术下不改变 softmax 与梯度；本文指出 BF16 固定的相对精度（8 位有效数字）使 logit 均值相对其标准差增大时，决定预测分布的 logit 差值无法被准确表示，量化误差（RMS(z_bf16 − z_fp32)）在每次失败前暴涨数个数量级。
- **精度直接控制失败时机**：降低 LM head 精度会提前引爆定时炸弹（BF16 true 最早、BF16 mixed 次之），而将 LM head 前向计算保持在 FP32 则在整个训练时程内保持稳定——为机理提供了因果证据。
- **提出 LM head SGD-M 缓解方法**：仅对输出嵌入矩阵用动量 0.99 的 SGD（其余模型仍用 AdamW），从源头消除漂移，在学习率全扫描范围内保持稳定，且优化质量有竞争力；并解释了为何需要高动量（1/(1−β) 放大倍数从 10× 提到 100×）。
- **统一既有方法的机理**：z-loss（改损失函数）、µ-centering（改嵌入结构）、LM head SGD-M（改优化器）干预点完全不同，但三者都把量化误差压在有界水平，而未缓解的基线误差增长数个数量级——"控制有限精度误差"是这些方法有效的共同底层机制。

## Key Contributions

- **Identify and characterize "time-bomb spikes"**: slow, terminal loss divergences that emerge only after prolonged overtraining. Unlike fast transient spikes (which they show are largely suppressed by learnable RMSNorm gains), time-bombs persist in modern architectures and worsen with longer horizons — increasingly relevant as models are trained far beyond compute-optimal budgets.
- **Resolve the "harmless drift" paradox via finite precision**: They formalize that Adam's coordinate-wise preconditioning breaks the zero-sum structure of the LM-head cross-entropy gradient, drifting the mean output embedding/logit — yet this drift leaves softmax predictions and gradients unchanged under exact arithmetic. The missing link is BF16's fixed relative precision: once the logit mean dominates the logit spread (|µ_z|/σ_z ≫ 1), the logit differences that determine the predictive distribution fall below the representable resolution (ULP grows from ~0.008 near 1 to 8 near 1024).
- **Precision causally controls time-bomb timing**: In 28M models overtrained for 16B tokens (~28× Chinchilla), lowering LM-head precision triggers earlier failures (BF16 true fails first, BF16 mixed later), while FP32 LM heads keep all 5 seeds stable even at peak LR 0.1 where every BF16 run fails — accompanied by orders-of-magnitude growth in measured quantization error RMS(z_bf16 − z_fp32) and KL divergence between FP32/BF16 predictive distributions before each collapse.
- **Propose LM-head SGD-M**: replace AdamW with carefully tuned SGD with momentum (β = 0.99) exclusively on the output embedding matrix. It suppresses logit growth at the source, is the only intervention stable across the entire spike-prone LR sweep at both 162M and 404M, remains competitive (often best) in final eval loss, and even pairs well with a Muon trunk at 1.4B scale.
- **Unify existing mitigations under one mechanism**: z-loss (objective), µ-centering (embedding structure), and LM-head SGD-M (optimizer) all keep quantization error bounded while the unmitigated baseline's error grows by orders of magnitude — recasting these seemingly unrelated techniques as different ways of keeping the LM head in a regime where reduced precision suffices.

## Methodology

The study trains 28M–1.4B parameter modernized GPT-2 architectures (rotary embeddings, GELU, RMSNorm, untied embeddings, no biases, QK-Norm throughout to isolate attention-logit spikes) in JAX/Flax on FineWeb with the GPT-2 tokenizer, on up to 32 Cloud TPU v6e/v4 or 4×B200/8×H100/8×A100 GPUs, with AdamW (β1=0.9, β2=0.95), cosine decay, and gradient clipping at 1.0. Instabilities are elicited by training at elevated learning rates (3×10⁻⁴ to 3×10⁻¹) and far beyond Chinchilla-optimal budgets — 404M models for 96B tokens (~12× optimal) and 28M models for 16B tokens (~28× optimal), following the established replication protocol of Wortsman et al. and successors.

The analysis proceeds in layers. First, fast spikes are dissected via token-level forensics: robust z-scores over rolling token-loss windows, masking/skipping interventions, and single-token context perturbations at spike events. Second, time-bomb spikes are linked to Adam's preconditioner through the zero-sum property of the LM-head gradient (Σᵢ(pᵢ−yᵢ)=0), which Adam's per-coordinate second moment violates. Third — the central conceptual move — they observe that mean-logit drift is a softmax-invariant shift that cannot affect training in exact arithmetic, so they instrument finite precision directly: at each logging step they recompute logits in FP32 from the same hidden states (FP32 GEMM) and measure (a) logit quantization error RMS(z_bf16 − z_fp32) and (b) predictive-distribution error D_KL(p_fp32 ‖ p_bf16), across three precision settings for the LM head (FP32, BF16 mixed = FP32 master weights with BF16 runtime, BF16 true = BF16 weights and optimizer states). Finally, mitigations are compared under a matched hyperparameter budget, including head-specific LR and β1 tuning for the AdamW variants, with 5 seeds per configuration and a formal time-bomb definition (three consecutive post-midpoint evals ≥ 0.01 above running-best eval loss).

## Results & Highlights

- **Precision controls time-bomb onset (28M models, 16B tokens ≈ 28× Chinchilla, FineWeb, peak LR sweep, 5 seeds each)**: at peak LR 0.1, FP32 LM heads are stable 0/5 while BF16 mixed fails 5/5 and BF16 true fails 5/5 — and at peak LR 3×10⁻², BF16 mixed fails 2/5 vs 0/5 for FP32; BF16 true already fails 4/5 seeds at LR 3×10⁻³ (Figure 19). Quantization error RMS(z_bf16 − z_fp32) grows from ~1e0 to ~1e3 (orders of magnitude) before each collapse, while all mitigations keep it flat.
- **LM-head SGD-M is the only method stable across the full spike-prone LR range (Table 2, final eval loss on FineWeb eval)**: at 404M and peak LR 3×10⁻¹, AdamW spikes to 28.241 (†unrecovered) and µ-centering to 7.115† vs SGD-M (β=0.99) at 3.167 — a ~25-nat gap; at 162M/LR 3×10⁻¹, AdamW spikes to 10.845† vs SGD-M 3.736. SGD-M β=0.99 beats SGD-M β=0.9 at every learning rate and both model sizes (e.g., 404M/LR 3e-1: 3.167 vs 3.244).
- **Across 5 seeds at peak LR 0.15 (Table 3)**: at 404M, LM-head SGD-M reaches final eval loss 3.178±0.002 vs baseline AdamW 3.916±0.278 (−0.74, and far lower variance), beating z-loss (3.214±0.008) and µ-centering (3.208±0.004); training-loss spike score drops from 0.088±0.029 (AdamW baseline) to 0.038±0.004 at 162M.
- **Fast spikes are context-fragile, not data-driven (Table 1)**: at the Rank-1 spike event in a 162M run, per-token loss is 610.0 nats with a spurious top logit of 782.05; swapping a single context token ("Command" → comma) collapses the loss to 12.7 nats, the top logit to 95.01, and the full-vocabulary logit std by 17× (121.87 → 6.87) — while masking just 0.2% of high-loss tokens removes visible spikes but hurts convergence, and batch-skipping with optimizer-state reversion only postpones them.
- **RMSNorm parameterization gates fast spikes**: across 5 matched seed pairs, adding learnable gains to RMSNorm eliminates fast spikes and logit-mean swings entirely (Figure 2/13) — motivating the focus on the slower time-bomb regime that survives this fix.
- **Fixed softmax (Barron et al. 2026) is not the mechanism (Section C.4)**: under BF16 true, all 5/5 fixed-softmax seeds still time-bomb (delayed but not prevented) and logit norms track the baseline, whereas z-loss keeps both bounded — pinpointing forward-pass LM-head quantization error, not backward-pass softmax rounding, as the driver.
- **Scaling signal at 1.4B (peak LR 0.07, 10B tokens)**: LM-head SGD-M attains lower final training loss than AdamW, µ-centering, and z-loss, and Muon + LM-head SGD-M gives the lowest loss of any configuration tested.

## Authors & Credibility

The team spans Columbia University (Zaiqian Chen — corresponding author, Sophie Li, Micah Goldblum), MBZUAI (Aditya Somasundaram), University of Maryland (Sean McLeish, Tom Goldstein), and the Vector Institute (John Kirchenbauer). Goldblum and Goldstein are well-known for work on training dynamics and adversarial robustness; Kirchenbauer for the watermarking and hallucination lines. The experimental campaign is substantial and honest about scope: multiple hardware pools (TPU v6e/v4, B200, H100, A100), 5-seed repetition for the headline precision and mitigation claims, an explicit AI-use statement, and public code (github.com/tingtang2/quantization-loss-spikes). The paper builds carefully on — and cites precisely — the prior mechanistic line (PaLM's z-loss, Wortsman et al.'s logit divergence study, Stollenwerk et al.'s µ-centering and coupled-Adam work), and its claim structure (drift is harmless in exact arithmetic → finite precision is the culprit) is backed by both a clean mathematical sketch (Section 3.4/C.1) and direct error instrumentation rather than circumstantial correlation.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The individual ingredients are known — Adam-induced mean-embedding drift (Stollenwerk & Stollenwerk 2025), z-loss (PaLM), µ-centering (Stollenwerk et al. 2026). The genuinely new contributions are (1) the finite-precision causal story explaining why softmax-invariant mean drift destabilizes real training, and (2) the empirical unification showing all mitigations bound quantization error. This is a real conceptual advance, though concurrent work (Barron et al. 2026 on softmax rounding; Liu et al. 2026 on Slingshot) is chasing adjacent numerical-error mechanisms, so priority is contested terrain.
- **Rigor**: Strong by the standards of this genre — 5 seeds for key claims, a formal time-bomb definition (3 consecutive evals ≥0.01 above running best), matched hyperparameter budgets for AdamW variants (head LR and β1 tuning in Section B.3), and a well-designed negative control (fixed softmax under BF16 true). The C.1 analysis (ULP spacing of BF16, |µ_z|/σ_z ratio predicting normalized error, Figure 18) is a sketch rather than a theorem, but the empirical instrumentation (FP32 recomputation from identical hidden states) is the right measurement.
- **Reproducibility**: Good — code is released, architecture/training details are unusually complete (optimizer ε, β values, batch sizes, weight decay ablations, hardware inventory), and the models are small enough (28M–1.4B) for academics to replicate. The FineWeb + GPT-2 tokenizer stack is standard.
- **Significance caveats**: The central external-validity concern is acknowledged but not resolved: spikes are elicited with learning rates up to 3×10⁻¹ — 1–2 orders of magnitude above production-scale LRs — precisely to compress the horizon, and the authors admit findings "may not fully transfer to spikes that arise in more natural settings in larger models." Relatedly, the largest time-bomb scale shown is 404M/96B tokens (12× overtraining); a natural-LR, ≥7B demonstration is absent. The 1.4B result is single-seed, training-loss only, and at an elevated LR (0.07).
- **Red flags / weak spots**: (a) The unification claim ("all roads lead to quantization error control") is correlational for z-loss and µ-centering — they bound error and prevent spikes, but the paper does not isolate whether bounded error is causally necessary for those methods, only that FP32 removes the need for them; (b) under BF16 true, SGD-M converges to a visibly higher loss than the other mitigations (Section C.3), a cost the abstract does not mention; (c) AdamW head-β1=0.99 closes "much of the gap" to SGD-M, so the practical advantage of the full SGD-M swap is thinner than the headline suggests, though it is the only variant stable at the largest trunk LR; (d) no wall-clock/memory overhead numbers are given for any mitigation, only qualitative cost discussion of FP32 logits.

## Strengths

- Supplies a mechanistically satisfying answer to a genuine puzzle (softmax-shift-invariant drift should be harmless) with a measurable, causal quantity (LM-head quantization error), not just another correlation.
- The precision experiment is a clean causal intervention: same architecture, same data, same LR — only LM-head numerical precision varies, and failure timing moves monotonically with it (0/5 FP32 vs 5/5 BF16 at LR 0.1).
- LM-head SGD-M is embarrassingly practical: no loss terms, no architectural change, one fewer optimizer moment buffer (memory savings), and it stacks with Muon at 1.4B.
- Honest treatment of fast vs slow spikes, including negative results (token masking hurts convergence; batch skipping only defers spikes; fixed softmax doesn't fix BF16-true time-bombs) that rule out data-centric explanations.
- The explanation of why SGD-M needs β=0.99 (restoring the 1/(1−β)=100× steady-state amplification lost with Adam's adaptivity) retroactively explains why prior work using β=0.9 found SGD-M slightly worse.

## Limitations

- Instabilities are induced with aggressively elevated learning rates and extreme overtraining ratios (12–28× Chinchilla); transfer to production regimes (LR ~1e-4–2e-4, multi-trillion-token, ≥7B models) is asserted as future work, not demonstrated.
- Headline precision experiments are at 28M parameters; the 1.4B evidence is single-seed, training-loss-only, and in the fast-spike regime rather than the time-bomb regime.
- The "shared numerical mechanism" unification is established by co-occurrence (all mitigations bound error) rather than by an intervention that unbinds error while keeping the mitigation active.
- Under BF16 true, LM-head SGD-M pays a visible optimization-quality cost (converges to higher loss than µ-centering/z-loss), suggesting the method is not uniformly dominant across precision settings.
- No analysis of FP8 or quantized-training regimes, which the authors flag as future work; no compute-cost quantification of the FP32-logit baseline at realistic vocabulary sizes.

## Relevance

该论文直接命中"大模型训练稳定性/损失尖峰"这一方向：它把 LM head 的 logit 漂移与 BF16 量化误差联系起来，给出了定时炸弹式发散的因果解释和仅需替换 LM head 优化器的低成本修复，对理解与稳定长时程（过度训练）预训练运行极具参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do time-bomb spikes occur at production learning rates (~1e-4) in ≥7B models trained on 10+ trillion tokens, or is the phenomenon an artifact of the compressed-horizon, high-LR replication protocol?
- Would an FP8 LM head accelerate time-bombs proportionally to its reduced significand, and does the |µ_z|/σ_z error law quantitatively predict onset step across precisions?
- Can the quantization-error-unification be made causal — e.g., by injecting synthetic BF16-rounded logit noise into an otherwise-stable FP32 run to trigger a time-bomb?
- Is bounded quantization error also the mechanism behind other stabilization tricks (logit soft-capping as in Gemma 2, coupled Adam, hyperball optimization in the Marin recipe)?
- Since β1=0.99 on the AdamW head recovers most of SGD-M's optimization quality, is there a hybrid optimizer for the LM head that matches SGD-M's stability while keeping Adam's per-row adaptivity for rare tokens?
