> **一句话总结：** 针对多轮对话中"同一句话在不同历史下需要不同回复"的问题，本文提出不再用嵌入相似度阈值判断缓存能否复用（该方法 AUC 仅 0.62–0.74），而是用核岭回归从 LLM 裁判标注中学习"复用风险"，并把生成成本与不可接受复用的代价一起纳入预算化缓存选择：离线算法 CUCB-SC-MT 带次优性保证，在线算法 CLCB-LS-MT 在状态复现时具有 Õ(√N) 悔界；在 SocraTeach 数学辅导与 Taskmaster-3 电影购票两个数据集上，1,000 条缓存即可通过可接受复用规避 35–41% 的生成成本（最强基线最多 22–25%），估计服务延迟降低 19–35%。

> **TL;DR:** Multi-turn conversations break similarity-threshold semantic caches: on five workloads (WildChat, Taskmaster-3, SocraTeach, MultiDoc2Dial, ABCD) embedding distance separates acceptable from unacceptable reuse with only AUC 0.62–0.74, while a reuse-risk model learned from GPT-4o-mini judge labels reaches 0.76–0.89 (up to +0.20 AUC, e.g. Taskmaster-3 0.89 vs 0.74). Building the cache by weighing learned generation cost against learned reuse risk (CUCB-SC-MT offline with a suboptimality bound; CLCB-LS-MT online with Õ(√N) regret under state recurrence), the online cache with 1,000 entries safely avoids 35.0% of generation cost on Taskmaster-3 and 41.0% on SocraTeach — versus at most 22.3% and 25.1% for ContextCache, ElastiCache-MT, vCache, a joint-state threshold and single-turn caching — while achieving the lowest loss at every cache budget and cutting estimated latency by 15–20% (Taskmaster-3) and 30–35% (SocraTeach). Caveat: gains are demonstrated on structured task-oriented tutoring/booking dialogues; on open-domain WildChat every cache can safely serve only 1–2% of follow-ups.

## 核心贡献

- **揭示多轮场景下相似度阈值的失效**：在五个工作负载（WildChat、Taskmaster-3、SocraTeach、MultiDoc2Dial、ABCD）上系统测量发现，用嵌入距离预测"复用缓存的回复是否可接受"的 AUC 仅 0.62–0.74；完全相同的消息在不同历史下，来自其他对话的回复只有 22%（SocraTeach）/ 45%（Taskmaster-3）的概率可被接受。
- **学习"复用风险"取代相似度阈值**：定义复用风险 r(z,u) 为缓存回复不可接受的概率，用核岭回归（KRR）在 40 维配对特征 ψ(z,u)（两个请求/历史/改写的相似度、轮次位置、缓存回复相似度等）上从 LLM 裁判标注学习；风险模型在 WildChat 上一次性固定，跨负载 AUC 提升 +0.06～+0.20。
- **成本-风险联合的预算化缓存选择**：把缓存选择形式化为最小化期望损失 min{c(z), ρ(z,M)}，其中生成成本 c 的输出部分同样由 KRR 估计；用分层 ε-网离散化 + Reverse Greedy 求解（该问题 NP-hard），并保留 α-近似保证（定理 3.2），ε-网规模由内在维度 p（而非嵌入维度）控制。
- **离线算法 CUCB-SC-MT 及次优性保证**：仅从日志对话学习，悲观估计（pessimism）下相对 α-近似 oracle 的次优性界（定理 4.1）分解为离散化、到达、成本、复用风险四项，且能处理对话内轮次任意相关、回归输入含早期噪声等新困难。
- **在线算法 CLCB-LS-MT 及悔界**：从空缓存冷启动，按对话冻结策略快照、以概率 ζ 抽样标注复用、用信息增益触发低频换缓存；在状态复现（分离支撑）条件下获得 Õ(√N) 悔界（定理 5.1 / 推论 5.2），尽管轮次相关。
- **全面的实证验证**：在 Taskmaster-3（21,892 请求）与 SocraTeach（10,821 请求）上离线/在线均验证，每个缓存预算下损失最低，安全规避成本、延迟、流上学习速度全面报告并附 95% 自助置信区间。

## Key Contributions

- **Motivation study showing similarity thresholds fail in conversations**: across five workloads (open-domain WildChat, movie-ticketing Taskmaster-3, math-tutoring SocraTeach, document-grounded MultiDoc2Dial, customer-service ABCD), embedding distance ranks acceptable reuse at only AUC 0.62–0.74; for identical messages under different histories, a response from another conversation is acceptable only 22% (SocraTeach) / 45% (Taskmaster-3) of the time.
- **A learned reuse risk**: r(z,u), the probability a cached response is unacceptable, learned by Nyström RBF kernel ridge regression on 40-dim pair features (similarities of the two states/requests/standalone rewrites/histories, similarity to the cached response, turn positions, history lengths). Fixed once on WildChat, it gains +0.06 to +0.20 AUC out-of-fold on every workload (Taskmaster-3: 0.74→0.89; ABCD: 0.62→0.82).
- **Cost-vs-risk cache selection with guarantees**: caching over conversation states (exact-match key ω + embedding x = [√0.7·e(q); √0.3·e(H)]), minimizing expected loss min{c(z), ρ(z,M)} where the generation cost's output part is also KRR-learned. The problem is NP-hard; a stratified ε-net plus Reverse Greedy retains an α-approximation guarantee (Theorem 3.2) with net size scaling in the intrinsic dimension p instead of the embedding dimension.
- **CUCB-SC-MT (offline)**: learns from logged conversations with a pessimistic suboptimality bound (Theorem 4.1) whose four terms (discretization, arrival, cost, reuse risk) each required new analysis for correlated turns, self-normalized confidence widths, and risk learned only on sampled pairs.
- **CLCB-LS-MT (online)**: serves each conversation with a frozen policy snapshot, labels a random ζ fraction of reuses, and switches caches only on information-gain triggers; achieves Õ(√N) regret over N requests when conversation states recur (Theorem 5.1, Corollary 5.2) — despite the net being built from correlated turns.
- **System-level evaluation**: lowest loss at every cache budget, offline and online, against ContextCache, ElastiCache-MT (the AWS recipe), vCache, a joint-state threshold, and single-turn caching (CUCB-SC-Cont), with precision/false-reuse/safe-cost-avoided breakdowns, latency estimates, and γ-price sweeps.

## Methodology

The system (Figure 2) serves turn t of a conversation with request q_t and history H_t. A **state encoder** compresses the full conversation state into z = (ω, x): an exact-match key ω holding discrete facts that gate admissibility (e.g., city: Paris, language: French) and a fixed-size embedding x = [√0.7·e(q_t); √0.3·e(H_t)] (Granite sentence embeddings). Distance is measured only within a key stratum (d = ∞ across keys), so "Paris→Lyon" style factual shifts cannot silently match.

At serving time the check compares (a) the estimated cost of a fresh response c = c_in + c_out, where the input cost is known and the output cost is estimated by KRR, against (b) the estimated loss ρ(z, u) = min{1, γ·r(z, u)} of the best cached response, which combines a known compatibility mask χ (e.g., same key) with the learned reuse risk r on pair features ψ(z, u). A **reuse judge** (LLM judge or user) labels a sample of reuses to train r. Eviction keeps at most k items.

Cache design is cast as minimizing L(M) = E[min{c(Z), ρ(Z, M)]} subject to |M| ≤ k — NP-hard (it contains the single-turn problem of Atalar et al. 2026). The pipeline: (1) build a stratified ε-net of representatives over logged conversations; (2) estimate cell weights, costs (KRR upper bounds) and reuse losses (pessimistic upper bounds R̄_ij); (3) run Reverse Greedy (supermodular minimization, α = (e−1)/Γ approximation that holds for learned, masked, asymmetric reuse losses). The oracle guarantee needs Lipschitz regularity only within a key (Assumption 2), and net size is bounded by |Ω|·2^p/(c_0·ε^p) via intrinsic dimension p (Lemma 3.3).

**CUCB-SC-MT** (Algorithm 1, offline) splits logged conversations — not turns, because turns within a conversation are dependent — into D1 (net construction) and D2 (weight estimation, with concentration over conversations via an effective sample size N_eff). Risk is learned from judge-labeled pairs; a pessimistic instance drives Reverse Greedy. Theorem 4.1 bounds suboptimality against the α-approximate oracle with four terms: discretization, arrival, cost-widths (only on cells the comparator sends to the LLM), and reuse-risk widths (only on pairs the comparator reuses) — so the logs only need to cover what a good cache does.

**CLCB-LS-MT** (Algorithm 2, online) starts from an empty cache. Each conversation is served with a frozen snapshot (routing at the cell representative via lower confidence bounds; uncovered requests go to the LLM and may become new representatives). Optimism keeps uncertain cells hitting the LLM and uncertain pairs being reused and labeled (labels observed with probability ζ). The cache is recomputed only when a representative is added, an arrival stage ends, or the log-determinant of the cost/label Gram matrix grows by ln 2 (an information trigger that exploits how one KRR observation shrinks many widths at once). Theorem 5.1 bounds regret; Corollary 5.2 gives Õ(√N) under separated support (states recur) and Õ(N^-(1/(p+2))) per-request otherwise.

**Evaluation protocol**: costs are GPT-4o-mini token counts normalized to [0,1] by calibration-set percentiles; acceptability is judged by GPT-4o-mini (shared across all methods; on SocraTeach it accepts 98% of exact-context duplicates and 2.7% of random pairs); a miss costs c(z_t), an unacceptable hit costs γ (γ = 1 in the main text). Workloads: 2,200 Taskmaster-3 dialogues (21,892 requests) and 2,200 SocraTeach dialogues (10,821 requests), each split 1,800 calibration / 200 validation / 200 test conversations; hyperparameters (net radii, thresholds, vCache's δ, risk multiplier γ̂) tuned on validation. Online, all 2,200 conversations arrive in random order from a cold start. Baselines: ElastiCache-MT (AWS semantic-caching recipe), ContextCache, vCache, a joint-state threshold on the paper's own embedding (isolating the representation), and single-turn CUCB-SC-Cont.

## Results & Highlights

- **Learned risk vs embedding distance (motivation study, Table 4)**: on Taskmaster-3 the learned reuse risk reaches AUC 0.89 for acceptable reuse vs 0.74 for embedding distance (+0.15); on ABCD 0.82 vs 0.62 (+0.20); the risk model was fixed once on WildChat and all scores are out-of-fold. At 80% judge precision on Taskmaster-3 it can safely serve 38.9% [32.2, 46.4] of follow-ups vs 17.3% [14.2, 22.7] for distance — 2.2× more.
- **Offline loss across budgets (Figure 3)**: on Taskmaster-3, CUCB-SC-MT's loss is 16.9 to 7.4 pp below the strongest baseline ElastiCache-MT across k = 100→1000 (77.1% of no-cache loss at k=100 vs ElastiCache-MT's 94.0%; 72.6% at k=1000); with only 100 entries it already matches unbounded ElastiCache-MT (77.3%), which caches all 19,932 source states. On SocraTeach, budgeted baselines stay at 96.2–101.0% (≈ no caching) while CUCB-SC-MT falls from 94.3% (k=100) to 69.1% (k=1000).
- **Online loss across budgets**: CLCB-LS-MT has the lowest loss at every budget on both workloads. On Taskmaster-3 it holds 73.0–75.0% (74.1% unbounded) while ElastiCache-MT needs a huge cache (99.0% at k=100, 81.9% unbounded) — a gap of 24.0 pp at k=100 and 11.4 pp at k=1000 (7.8 pp unbounded, CI 6.5–9.1). On SocraTeach only ContextCache is competitive: CLCB-LS-MT is 2.4 pp below it at k=100 and 9.2 pp (CI 7.1–11.2) at k=1000.
- **Safe cost avoided (Table 1, k=1000, online)**: CLCB-LS-MT safely avoids 35.0% of the no-cache generation cost on Taskmaster-3 and 41.0% on SocraTeach, against at most 22.3% (ElastiCache-MT) and 25.1% (ContextCache) for any baseline; vCache avoids only 14.7% / 0.7% and single-turn 1.1% / 2.0%. Because it weighs cost against risk, its acceptable reuses land on requests costing ~1.5× the average request (1.45× Taskmaster-3, 1.51× SocraTeach) vs 1.1–1.3× for the strongest baselines.
- **Reuse quality trade-off (Table 1)**: CLCB-LS-MT's hit precision is 74.6% (Taskmaster-3) / 67.5% (SocraTeach) with false reuses on 8.2% / 13.1% of requests — lower precision than ElastiCache-MT (82.5% / 95.6%) but each miss is charged as much as the most expensive request, and its total loss is still lowest.
- **Estimated latency (Table 2)**: online, CLCB-LS-MT lowers estimated mean per-request latency by 15–20% on Taskmaster-3 (0.80–0.85 relative to the 858 ms LLM call) and 30–35% on SocraTeach (0.65–0.70 vs a 1,100 ms call), including 23–28 ms of amortized background updates; its hit path takes 39–83 ms, under a tenth of an LLM call.
- **Learning over the stream (Figure 4, D.5)**: CLCB-LS-MT keeps improving — running loss falls from 80.3% of no-cache cost in the first quarter to 72.0% (k=200) / 71.7% (k=1000) in the last quarter on Taskmaster-3, while budgeted fixed-threshold baselines move by at most 2.6 pp; on SocraTeach it improves by 7.5–15.8 pp quarter-over-quarter (ContextCache: at most 4.1 pp).
- **Where caching does not work (D.1)**: on open-domain WildChat-1M, both scores can safely serve under 2% of follow-ups at 80% precision — justifying the focus on structured workloads but bounding the claim's scope.

Credibility is high: the theory is worked out in genuine detail (concentration over conversations rather than turns, self-normalized widths, pessimistic/optimistic bounds), all baselines share the same judge and label pool, hyperparameters are tuned on validation only, and results come with 95% bootstrap CIs clustered by conversation. ## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The core move — replacing a similarity threshold with a *learned pairwise reuse risk* and putting it inside a cost-vs-risk budgeted cache selection with guarantees — is genuinely new relative to ContextCache/MeanCache/vCache, which threshold similarity or per-entry error rates without weighing the generation cost saved. However, it is an incremental extension of the authors' own single-turn framework (CUCB-SC-Cont): the system model, ε-net, Reverse Greedy, and pessimism/optimism machinery are carried over; the new technical content is the pairwise risk on state pairs, the stratified key structure, and the conversation-level concentration arguments. Reviewers at a ML venue may see the modeling delta as moderate; the multi-turn-specific analysis (e.g., N_eff over conversations, frozen snapshots) is where the real work is.
- **Rigor**: Strong. NP-hardness is stated, the approximation factor survives learned/asymmetric/masked losses, and the regret proof handles correlated turns, which single-turn analyses cannot. Experimental protocol is careful (validation-only tuning, shared judge, bootstrap CIs, out-of-fold risk scores fixed on WildChat, γ-sweep in the appendix).
- **Reproducibility**: Fair. All datasets are public (Taskmaster-3, SocraTeach, WildChat), the models used are named (Granite embeddings, GPT-4o-mini judge/costs), and hyperparameters are described. But no code release is mentioned, the 40-dim pair feature ψ and the exact-match key extraction (entity/constraint resolution from history) are nontrivial reimplementation hurdles, and the judge-labeling pipeline (0.28–0.40 labels per request for CLCB-LS-MT, up to 1.0 for vCache) means reproducing the online runs requires a nontrivial judge budget.
- **Significance**: The 35–41% safe-cost-avoided vs ≤25% for baselines is a large margin, and the framing (reuse acceptability is a property of *state pairs*, not distances) is a durable insight. But significance is tempered by workload selection: the two evaluation corpora are structured dialogues following common flows (movie booking; GPT-4-simulated math tutoring with *LLM-generated* responses), which is exactly where cross-conversation reuse is plausible. On open-domain chat the paper itself shows reuse is nearly dead (1–2% on WildChat), so the headline savings should not be read as representative of general LLM traffic.
- **Red flags**: (1) The exogeneity assumption (Assumption 1) — cached serving does not change how the conversation continues — is untestable in replayed logs and implausible for the 8.2–13.1% of requests that receive unacceptable responses; a wrong answer can derail a real user, making the effective deployment cost of "false reuse" larger than the γ·1 the loss assumes. (2) The judge is the sole ground truth: GPT-4o-mini decides acceptability for GPT-4o-mini-costed responses; its calibration is only spot-checked (98% on exact duplicates, 2.7% on random pairs on SocraTeach), with no human-labeled audit sample reported, so absolute precision numbers (74.6%, 67.5%) inherit judge bias. (3) The online headline includes a same-conversation session buffer that Theorem 5.1 does not cover and that contributes heavily on SocraTeach (54–88% of hits; removing it costs 8.6–14.0 pp). (4) Latency numbers are component-based estimates (embedding + decision + amortized background), not measured end-to-end serving latency, and ignore queueing — the authors flag this, but Table 2 should not be quoted as measured latency. (5) ElastiCache-MT is a re-implementation of a documented AWS recipe rather than the production service, and vCache optimizes an error-rate target, not loss, so the baseline comparison is fair-but-favorable to the authors' objective.

## Strengths

- Identifies and quantifies a real, previously under-modeled failure mode: embedding distance is a poor predictor of reuse acceptability in conversations (AUC 0.62–0.74 across five diverse workloads), and identical messages under different histories are acceptable only 22–45% of the time.
- Replaces a hand-tuned threshold with a learned, transferable risk model — fixed on WildChat, it improves AUC on every other workload without per-workload tuning.
- The only multi-turn cache in the comparison that weighs reuse risk against the actual generation cost it saves, which is why its reuses concentrate on ~1.5× average-cost requests and its safe-cost-avoided nearly doubles the best baseline (35.0% vs 22.3% on Taskmaster-3; 41.0% vs 25.1% on SocraTeach).
- Real theory, not decoration: suboptimality and Õ(√N) regret guarantees survive correlated turns, and the algorithm design (frozen per-conversation snapshots, information-gain switching triggers) is directly shaped by the proof requirements.
- Continues learning after deployment — running loss keeps falling over the stream (80.3%→71.7–72.0% on Taskmaster-3) while fixed-threshold baselines plateau — which matters for production caches facing drift.
- Exceptionally transparent reporting: 95% CIs everywhere, per-budget tables, the negative WildChat screening result published, and limitations (assumption violations, session-buffer dependence) stated by the authors themselves.

## Limitations

- **Scope of workloads**: all quantitative gains come from two structured dialogues corpora; on open-domain WildChat every cache could safely serve only 1–2% of follow-ups, so the approach's value on general chat traffic is unestablished.
- **Unacceptable responses at 8.2–13.1% of requests** (online, γ=1) is a high error rate for production assistants; the fixed price γ per bad reuse is a crude model of real user harm.
- **Exogeneity assumption**: replayed-conversation evaluation cannot detect reuse-induced conversation drift; relaxing it turns the problem into full RL (acknowledged as future work).
- **Judge dependence**: acceptability labels come from a single LLM judge (GPT-4o-mini) with no reported human validation on the evaluation workloads; learned risk, precision, and safe-cost-avoided all inherit the judge's biases.
- **Same-conversation session buffer**: 54–88% of online hits on SocraTeach reuse the current conversation's own earlier responses — a practical heuristic outside the regret guarantee; the pure cross-conversation setting shows smaller (though still positive) margins.
- **Costs and latency are modeled, not billed/measured**: costs are normalized token counts under a GPT-4o-mini tokenizer and latency is a component-based estimate ignoring queueing; no real serving stack or dollar figures are reported.
- **Key extraction depends on entity/constraint resolution** from the history into exact-match keys ω; robustness of this (necessarily learned or rule-based) step to messy real dialogs is not evaluated in isolation.

## Relevance

这篇论文与"LLM 服务系统与成本优化"方向高度相关：它把语义缓存从单轮扩展到多轮对话，核心是学习式复用风险 + 成本-风险联合的预算化缓存选择，并配以离线次优性/在线悔界两类理论保证——对关心推理成本、缓存系统与在线学习（bandit）方法落地的研究兴趣是直接的补充。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How stable is the learned reuse risk when the serving LLM changes (e.g., cache trained against GPT-4o-mini costs reused for a stronger model), and does the risk model transfer across response generators or only across workloads?
- What is the true cost of an unacceptable reuse to a real user? A user study (or logged user rephrasing/abandonment signals) would calibrate γ far better than the flat price the loss assumes.
- Can the exact-match key ω (entity/constraint resolution) be extracted robustly at scale — e.g., by a small trained model — and how much of the gain survives noisy or missing keys?
- Since 54–88% of SocraTeach hits come from the session buffer (same-conversation reuse, outside the theory), is a principled within-conversation caching policy with guarantees achievable?
- Does the approach help agentic/tool-use workloads, where states include structured action histories that recur even more than chit-chat — and where WildChat's 1–2% safe-reuse ceiling suggests open-domain chat is simply the wrong domain?
- Could the reuse-risk model be distilled into a cheap monotone score deployable inside existing vector-DB caches (à la GPTCache/vCache) without the full ε-net machinery, preserving most of the 35–41% safe-cost-avoided at lower engineering cost?
