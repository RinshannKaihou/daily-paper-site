Round-to-nearest bf16 export silently discards most RL updates — the served sampler runs 50–150 steps behind the optimizer.

bf16 round-to-nearest 导出会静默丢弃绝大多数 RL 优化器更新（Qwen3-1.7B 上每步仅 0.4–0.5% 服务权重变化）；Fair Cast 以无偏舍入导出并在服务副本上计算损失，仅改一行代码即平均提升 6.1 个百分点。

Why this matters · 为什么重要
The bf16 export step in standard RL stacks silently drops most optimizer updates — and your saved fp32 checkpoint isn't the model you deployed.
Affects verl, TRL, OpenRLHF, slime, prime-rl, AReaL. Gives a detectable signal (hidden ratio |log ρ|, per-step weight churn) and a one-line fix.
⚠ Critical edge · 批判视角
The +6.1-point mean leans on two long-rollout logic tasks; several individual deltas sit inside the paper's own ~3-point noise floor, with nothing validated above 4B.
Runs are 150–500 steps, one training stack, η = 1e-6 — exactly the regime where the dead zone bites hardest.

Method 方法

fp32 master θtoptimizer state, checkpointed
—export R(θ)→
bf16 served copy qtrollout sampler; Fair Cast also computes the loss here (straight-through grad to θt)
Dead zone: bf16 grid u(w) = 2(⌊log₂|w|⌋−7) ≈ |w|/128  ⇒  round-to-nearest zeroes every update with |Δ| < u/2 ≈ |w|/256
  • At η = 1e-6 only 0.4–0.5% of Qwen3-1.7B's served weights change per optimizer step; a given weight crosses a rounding boundary only every ~50–200 steps — under LR decay, τ → ∞ and the sampler freezes while the optimizer keeps moving.
  • Fair Cast, half 1 — unbiased export: stochastic rounding (adds 16 random bits to the fp32 bit pattern, then truncates; E[q] = θ) or error feedback (per-weight residual; accumulated export error bounded by one grid spacing forever, at the cost of a 6.9 GB fp32 residual at 1.7B).
  • Fair Cast, half 2 — train on the served copy: the trainer's forward pass uses qt, gradients reaching θt via a straight-through estimator, so the sampled and differentiated policies are the same object. Both halves are necessary: unbiased export alone collapses (EF → 0.00 in 4/4 long runs; SR → token loops, GSM8K 0.61 → 0.13), and RTN + served-copy training is indistinguishable from the default.
  • Setup: GRPO-style clipped policy gradient; Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B/4B, OLMoE-1B-7B, Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct; AdamW and Muon; vLLM bf16 sampler + HF trainer co-located on one H100. Verified-reward games (Nim), Reasoning Gym, MATH, GSM8K, MBPP+/HumanEval+ with decontamination.

Why nothing catches it 隐藏比率

≈0.01visible |log ρ| — πtrainer/πsampler, both sides downstream of the same rounding; flat the whole run
0.10–0.45hidden |log ρ| — πθ (master) vs πq (served); never formed by any framework
  • Because the monitored ratio is computed after export on both sides, TIS, IcePop, PPO clipping, and bf16-autocast training are structurally blind to the lag.
  • Per-token effective sample size between master and served policy falls to 0.2–0.35 on 2,000-token tasks (≈0.9 at 500 tokens).
  • Under Fair Cast the hidden ratio stays at the ≈0.01 noise floor for the entire run.

The checkpoint ≠ the model 最惊人的结果

Under round-to-nearest, the saved fp32 master can score up to 50 points below its own served bf16 export — HumanEval+ step 99: served 59.8, master 9.1. 5 of 9 RTN runs show a ≥12-pt master-vs-served gap at some checkpoint.

Strengths 优势

  • Mechanism-level, invisible failure mode named: the visible-vs-hidden ratio distinction explains precisely why every deployed mitigation (TIS, IcePop, PPO clip, autocast, FP16 sampler) cannot see the lag.
  • Engineering-credible fix: confined to the export call, 22–38 ms per step, no new mandatory hyperparameters, composes additively with TIS.
  • Immediately actionable deployment warning (export-format table) independent of the learning-speed claims.
  • Adversarial self-testing: the crossed ablation (unbiased export alone → 0.00) shows the authors tried to break their own method; the list-functions counterexample becomes a constructive EMA knob.
  • Evaluation hygiene: solver-verified games, MBPP+ decontamination (154/378 overlapping ids removed), exact budgets and seed policy stated.

Limitations 局限

  • Scale and duration: ≤4B dense (plus a 1B-7B MoE), 150–500 steps, one training stack at η = 1e-6 — no frontier-scale, long-horizon, or production-budget evidence.
  • Statistical power: several single-seed configurations; the 18/6/1 record mixes n=1 and n=4 arms, and the +6.1 mean leans on the logic-task tail.
  • Zebra instability reduced, not eliminated: one of four Fair Cast runs collapsed late (final 0.34) — hence the ±0.16 band overlapping RTN's failure mode.
  • Unpriced costs: error feedback needs a 6.9 GB fp32 residual; 2–15% per-step weight churn degrades sparse delta-sync compression 5–30× for multi-node sampler fleets.
  • Guarantee is on weights, not policies: E[q] = θ ⇏ E[πq] = πθ; the straight-through gradient is evaluated at qt, not θt.

Open questions 待探索

  • Does the dead-zone lag matter at production scale (7B–70B+, 1k+ steps, async staleness > rounding lag) — or does version lag simply dominate?
  • Is the full-parameter conclusion moot for LoRA-based RL, where separately-served adapters may largely escape the dead zone?
  • Can the hidden ratio |log ρ| become a cheap online monitor that flags lag-induced collapse early (zebra at step 30, not 60)?
  • Which task properties decide whether the implicit low-pass filter helps (list functions) vs hurts (zebra), so the EMA coefficient α can be chosen a priori?
  • Does training on the served copy bias learning toward the deployed quantized policy in ways that matter for later merging or distillation from the fp32 master?
