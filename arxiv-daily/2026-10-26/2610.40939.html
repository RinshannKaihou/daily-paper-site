Key Finding / CoT Controllability / arXiv 2610.40939

Reasoning models fail chain-of-thought control because of an initiation bottleneck — entropy collapse locks the first thinking token onto "Okay".

该研究的核心发现：推理模型思维链（CoT）可控性低的根源是开头几个 token 的熵坍缩—— 禁用 "Okay" 类高频开头 token 并辅以一个示例，平均合规率即可提升 34–41 个百分点； 且"抑制类"与"添加/风格类"控制是与监督风险方向相反的两种能力。

Why This Matters

Explains why reasoning models resist CoT control, and warns controllability benchmarks may not measure real oversight risk — core reading for CoT monitoring and AI-safety evaluation.

Critical Edge

Read critically

A mechanism demonstration, not a mitigation — the interventions need white-box token access no overseer would have over an adversarial model, and deliberate monitor evasion is never tested.

Methodology distilled

  1. Wrap multiple-choice questions (CommonsenseQA, ARC-C, GPQA, MMLU-Pro, HLE; 240 question–control pairs per main suite) with a control instruction inside <cot_instructions> tags.
  2. Score compliance 0–1 per response with deterministic task rules (uppercase fraction, leetspeak rate…); Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite judges Spanish only. Scoring is independent of answer correctness.
  3. Diagnose the opening: first-token entropy, most-common-prefix rates, and counterfactual rescoring of the first-violation position with the instruction removed.
  4. Intervene at inference: prefill K ∈ 0–64 compliant tokens; ban "Okay"/"Alright" at the first reasoning position in a 2×2 with user-ICL (Qwen3-14B/32B, OLMo-3.1-32B-Think, GLM-5.2; temp 0.6).
  5. Correlate: pairwise Spearman correlations of 8 task scores across a 12-model panel vs six monitorability evals from Guan et al. (2025), with bootstrap CIs throughout.
Suppression — avoid first person; omit a specified word
Addition — sentence prefixes; endings; numbered sentences (BEEP/ZORP markers)
Style — Spanish; leetspeak; uppercase

Honest scoping: GLM-5.2 is excluded from likelihood and banning analyses (API restrictions); ICL examples for two tasks are partly LLM-generated, adding a judge dependency. Code released: github.com/hughvd/CoT-controllability-bottleneck.

Peaks: Qwen uppercase 0.775, OLMo leetspeak 0.747 (both ≈0 with ICL alone). Not uniform: Qwen sentence-endings prefers ICL alone; OLMo Spanish prefers banning alone.

  • Failure sits at token one. On the four token-checkable tasks, 97.9–98.9% of failed responses violate the constraint at their first non-whitespace token; instructions raise compliant-token probability in every model–task pair, yet total compliant mass stays <0.25%.
  • Two capability clusters across 12 models. Within addition/style: mean ρ = 0.61 (0.13–0.88); suppression internally ρ = 0.74; across groups mean ρ = −0.29 — models good at suppression tend to be worse at addition/style.
  • Monitorability splits by control type (GPQA-Physics process eval): all six addition/style tasks positive, ρ = 0.29–0.74; word omission ρ = −0.55, first-person avoidance ρ = −0.37. Higher controllability does not consistently imply lower monitorability.
  • Prefilling is potent but uneven: one compliant token already helps, K = 64 helps more; addition/style benefit most, word omission barely moves.

Strengths

  • Crisp mechanistic cause for a previously only-benchmarked phenomenon, validated three independent ways: distributional statistics, counterfactual rescoring, causal prefill/ban interventions.
  • Minimal, clean interventions — banning one token at one position is about the cleanest causal test of the "initiation" hypothesis one could design.
  • Suppression vs addition/style split supported both causally (opposite responses to ICL vs prefilling) and observationally (cross-model correlation structure).
  • Actionable benchmark guidance: report tasks separately; separate initiation difficulty from content-control ability.
  • Unusually transparent statistics: bootstrap CIs on correlations, floor/ceiling clustering of monitorability evals, and undefined WMDP score rates disclosed in-paper.

Limitations

  • Correlational claims are thin: 12 models, noisy monitorability scores clustered near floor/ceiling — the authors themselves label them "descriptive."
  • GLM-5.2 — the most controllable model — excluded from banning and likelihood analyses (API limits); the bottleneck story is verified in detail only on Qwen and OLMo.
  • Small, skewed question pools per suite (6 GPQA, 8 MMLU-Pro, 2 HLE items); mixtures differ across experiments, limiting comparability.
  • Suppression = only 2 tasks, both binary-scored — the cluster could partly be a task-format artifact rather than a deep capability distinction.
  • The safety-relevant behavior — deliberate monitor evasion — is never tested; the bridge from controllability benchmarks to real oversight risk remains inferential.

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does the opening bottleneck persist after fine-tuning interventions — or does fixing initiation also change the monitorability correlations?
  2. Can a model insert misleading content mid-trace without touching its opening — and would any of the six monitorability evaluations catch it?
  3. Is the suppression/addition-style split a genuine capability dissociation or an artifact of binary 0/1 scoring on both suppression tasks?
  4. Why is GLM-5.2's opening distribution so much less collapsed — data mixture, RLVR curriculum, or a formatting stage?
  5. How do the interventions interact with answer accuracy — a deployer cares about the joint effect of forced style compliance on reasoning quality?
