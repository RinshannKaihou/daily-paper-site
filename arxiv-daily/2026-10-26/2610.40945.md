> **一句话总结：** 该论文用"在采样器权重处做一次训练器前向"把 LLM 强化学习中的训练-推理失配精确分解为近似恒定的数值差异与自我放大的策略滞后两部分，据此提出 DLC 修正（中心化数值差异 + 裁剪滞后比率 + 有效样本量跌破 1/2 时重同步采样器），在每 64 步才同步的陈旧采样器下将 Qwen3-1.7B 的 Countdown 训练偏差压到 0.6 倍噪声底（重要性采样为 115 倍）、测试准确率 51.9% 接近在线训练的 57.6%，并阻止了纯逐 token 修正无法察觉的坍塌。

> **TL;DR:** On-policy LLM RL is secretly off-policy because the inference engine (vLLM) and the trainer disagree in numerics and weights. This paper splits the sampled-token error exactly into a near-constant "numerical discrepancy" and a self-amplifying "policy lag" (the lag feeds a bias loop: excess update norm of score centering over importance sampling grows as aηk + c(ηk)² with c = 0.52, R² = 0.987), and shows importance weighting is valid only until the effective sample size of the lag ratio drops below 1/2 (step 11 on Qwen3-1.7B). The proposed DLC (center the discrepancy, clip the lag ratio, resynchronize at the crossing) keeps a stale sampler within 2× the on-policy noise floor: on the Countdown benchmark with Qwen3-1.7B it ends at 0.6× floor vs 115× for truncated importance sampling, with test accuracy 51.9% vs 57.6% on-policy and 36.6% for importance-sampling-with-resynchronization, whose plain variant collapses to 0.5% (θ0 starts at 33.4%).

## 核心贡献

- **精确二分解**：在采样器权重 θ_old 处额外评估一次训练器，把采样 token 误差 δ_t = log p_θ − log q 精确拆分为"数值差异" e_t（引擎量化/内核造成，训练中近似恒定）与"策略滞后" ℓ_t（异步陈旧权重造成，随训练器自身更新而增长）。两类失配源需要不同修正，解释了为何同一修正（如 score centering 或 PPO 的 clip）在一个失配源下稳定、在另一个下失效。
- **偏差反馈环的定量刻画**：centering 去掉漂移后，残余偏差分解为 prefix/continuation/token/tail 四项；策略滞后项进入下一次更新、放大下一次滞后，形成正反馈环。实测 score centering 的残余偏差范数随 ‖Δ‖ 线性增长（单次运行 R² = 0.95–0.99，第 40 步达梯度噪声的 2.5 倍），其对重要性采样的超额更新范数满足 E = aηk + c(ηk)²，二次项系数 c = 0.52（R² = 0.987，纯线性拟合仅 0.90）。
- **重要性加权的有效域**：比率加权的适用范围止于滞后比率 ρ_t 的有效样本量 1/E_q[ρ²] 跌破 1/2 处。Qwen3-1.7B 上实测在第 11 步交叉（高斯预测第 14 步），重要性采样恰好在该步离开噪声底并在第 30 步涨到 152 倍。
- **DLC（discrepancy-lag correction）**：对数值差异做 score centering 式的中心化（减去裁剪后分数在采样器分布下的均值 C_t），对滞后比率以采样器权重为参考做 PPO 式裁剪（ε = 0.2），并在有效样本量跌破 1/2 时把训练器权重拷回采样器（重同步）。两个极限下分别退化为 score centering（θ = θ_old）和以采样器为参考的 PPO（q = p_θold）。
- **坍塌诊断指标**：提出"离支撑份额"——训练器自身响应的序列 KL 中落在 p_θ0 < 10⁻³ token 上的比例。无重同步的 DLC 有 2/3 个运行坍塌，坍塌时离支撑份额跳到约 90%（之前 ≤14%），而在采样器前缀上算的 KL 偏差却仍停在噪声底——暴露了常用偏差指标的盲区。该指标对"准确率跌破 θ0"的 Spearman 相关为 0.80，高于偏差指标的 0.74。

## Key Contributions

- **Exact two-way split of the mismatch**: an extra trainer forward pass at the sampler's weights θ_old splits the sampled-token error δ_t = log p_θ − log q exactly into a numerical discrepancy e_t (engine numerics; approximately constant during training) and a policy lag ℓ_t (stale async weights; grows with the trainer's own updates). This explains why corrections that stabilize one mismatch source fail under the other (e.g., score centering survives quantization but not staleness; PPO's clip behaves oppositely).
- **Quantified bias feedback loop**: after drift removal, the residual bias decomposes into prefix / continuation / token / tail terms; the policy-lag terms enter the next update and enlarge the next lag. Measured residual bias of score centering grows linearly in ‖Δ‖ (R² = 0.95–0.99 per run), reaching 2.5× the gradient noise by step 40, and its excess update norm over importance sampling fits E = aηk + c(ηk)² with c = 0.52 at R² = 0.987 (linear-only fit: 0.90), consistent with the stability boundary of asynchronous GRPO at constant learning-rate × staleness product.
- **Validity range of importance weighting**: ratio weighting cuts the loop only while the lag ratio stays concentrated; the range ends where the effective sample size 1/E_q[ρ_t²] falls below one half. On Qwen3-1.7B the measured crossing occurs at step 11 (the Gaussian prediction is step 14), exactly where importance sampling leaves the noise floor and then grows to 152× floor by step 30.
- **The DLC algorithm**: center the numerical discrepancy (subtract the sampler's mean of the clipped score, C_t), clip the lag ratio ρ_t = p_θ/p_θold with the sampler's weights as reference (ε = 0.2), and resynchronize the sampler when the effective sample size crosses one half. DLC reduces to score centering and to PPO-with-sampler-reference in its two limits, and costs one extra trainer forward at θ_old plus the same top-128 log-probabilities score centering already stores.
- **Collapse forensics via off-support share**: the fraction of the trainer's own-response sequence KL on tokens with p_θ0 < 10⁻³ jumps to ~90% at the two DLC-without-resynchronization collapses (≤14% before), while the KL deviation measured on the sampler's prefixes stays at the noise floor — a failure mode invisible to all per-token corrections. The off-support share ranks the accuracy drop below θ0 at Spearman 0.80 vs 0.74 for the deviation.

## Methodology

The paper combines exact algebra, measured training dynamics, and an intervention experiment.

1. **Splitting identity.** With the trainer at current weights θ and the sampler at θ_old, a third log-probability log p_θold(y_t | h_t) (trainer numerics, sampler weights) splits the sampled-token error exactly: δ_t = ℓ_t + e_t, where ℓ_t = log p_θ − log p_θold is the policy lag and e_t = log p_θold − log q is the numerical discrepancy (the log of Guan et al.'s factorization of the importance ratio).
2. **Bias accounting.** At a fixed prefix the update factorizes into a drift ā·s̄ plus a covariance term; score centering removes the drift exactly, so the remaining bias is decomposed (by inserting/removing expectations under q and p) into four terms: prefix (importance weight of the history), continuation, token, and tail (set by the top-128 head the sampler reports). A first-order expansion in the lag gives b ≈ −MΔ, and unrolling to second order yields Δ_k ≈ ηk·ḡ − ½(ηk)²·Mḡ, predicting the quadratic-in-ηk excess update norm.
3. **Validity range via effective sample size.** Under a Gaussian lag the ESS of ρ_t is e^{2μ}; the heavy-tailed measured lag makes the real crossing earlier (step 11 vs 14 on Qwen3-1.7B). Past the crossing, truncated tokens (ratio above 2) bias the update.
4. **DLC.** Per-token gradient A_t(m_t ρ_t s_t − C_t): the clipped lag ratio removes the token term inside the clip range; the centering term C_t is the sampler's mean of the clipped score over its top-128 head plus a tail-mass-scaled estimate, which cancels the drift of the numerical discrepancy. When E_q[ρ_t²] crosses ½, the trainer's weights are copied to the sampler, resetting Δ, the lag, and the clip reference.
5. **Experimental protocol.** verl + FSDP2 (BF16 compute, FP32 master weights) with vLLM samplers; Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen3-1.7B (non-thinking mode) on Countdown with ±1 rewards; 64 prompts × 8 samples per step; SGD at lr 10⁻², 40 steps, 3 seeds per configuration. Mismatch sources: FP8 sampler (128×128 blocks, dynamic activation quantization, FP8 KV cache), stale sampler (BF16, synchronized every 64 steps — hence frozen at θ0 within a run), and their combination. Deviation = full-vocabulary KL to the paired on-policy model on fixed prefixes from θ0, divided by the noise floor (mean KL between two independently trained on-policy models); test accuracy on 64 held-out Countdown problems with 8 samples each.

## Results & Highlights

- **Stale sampler, Qwen3-1.7B (Countdown test accuracy, step 40)**: DLC reaches 51.9% vs 57.6% for on-policy training, 36.6% for importance sampling with resynchronization, 15.9% for score centering, and 0.5% for plain truncated importance sampling (initial model θ0: 33.4%) — i.e., DLC recovers ~92% of the on-policy accuracy gap above θ0 while plain IS collapses 32.9 points below θ0.
- **Deviation from on-policy (KL / noise floor, step 40, stale sampler)**: on Qwen3-1.7B DLC ends at 0.6× the floor (max 1.1× over steps 10–40) vs 115× for importance sampling and 7.6× for score centering; on Qwen3-0.6B DLC ends at 1.9× vs 2.1× for importance-sampling-with-centering, 31.7× for score centering, 345× for the plain policy gradient, and 699× for PPO with batch reference.
- **Validating the ESS crossing**: importance sampling on Qwen3-1.7B leaves the noise floor exactly at step 11, where its measured effective sample size falls below one half (mean lag −0.15 nats/token; the Gaussian prediction crosses at step 14), and its deviation grows to 152× the floor by step 30; adding resynchronization at the same threshold cuts the deviation from 115× to 2.6× the floor.
- **Feedback-loop measurements (Qwen3-0.6B, stale)**: score centering's residual bias grows linearly in ‖Δ‖ (R² = 0.95–0.99) and reaches 2.5× the gradient noise at step 40 while importance sampling stays within noise; the predicted bias aligns with the measured one at cosine up to 0.55 (vs ≤0.07 for gradient noise); the excess update norm of score centering over importance sampling fits E = aηk + c(ηk)² with c = 0.52 (R² = 0.987 vs 0.90 linear-only), and runs at half the learning rate collapse onto the same curves in ηk.
- **Quantized (FP8) sampler, Qwen3-0.6B**: score centering alone stays at 0.80× the floor with 46.5% test accuracy — exactly matching on-policy's 46.5% — while the policy gradient reaches 12.7× the floor (26.4%) and PPO 14.3× (26.2%), confirming that a constant discrepancy needs only centering.
- **Collapse forensics (Qwen3-1.7B)**: DLC without resynchronization collapses in 2 of 3 runs (accuracy below θ0), with the off-support share (sequence-KL mass on tokens with p_θ0 < 10⁻³) jumping to ~90% versus ≤14% before collapse, while the prefix-KL deviation of these same runs stays at the noise floor; under full DLC the off-support share stays ≤11% through step 40. DLC resynchronizes only once per 40 steps (steps 20/35/36 across runs) whereas importance-sampling-with-resynchronization triggers 2–5 times (a fixed interval would need syncing every 11 steps).
- **Scale and asynchrony extensions**: on Qwen3-8B (single runs, stale sampler) DLC ends at 3.1× floor / 49.6% accuracy vs 2.9× / 51.8% for importance-sampling-with-centering, 89× for score centering, 6470× / 0.0% for M2PO, and 55.3% for on-policy; under continuous asynchrony (Qwen3-1.7B, max version gap 4, mean lag 2.85 versions) DLC ends at 1.2× floor and 55.9% accuracy, with an admission-based variant at 0.8× floor deferring only 0.3% of responses.

## Authors & Credibility

Yu Li (George Washington University) and Yufa Zhou (Duke University) are co-first authors with Tian Lan (GWU) as senior/corresponding author — a systems-RL group with several concurrent arXiv works on LLM RL (credit assignment, GRPO corrections, KV-cache inference). The paper is unusually careful for a systems-algorithm paper: 3 seeds per configuration, a paired on-policy reference for every run, a noise floor defined as the KL between two independent on-policy trainings, reproducibility of prior findings (Marek & Ryabinin's source-dependence results) before extending them, code released at github.com/Skylanding/Training-Inference-Mismatch, and a detailed appendix covering optimizer, costs, an 8B model, continuous asynchrony, and step-by-step derivations. The experimental stack (verl + FSDP2 + vLLM) is the community standard.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: the exact discrepancy/lag split is the log of Guan et al. (2026)'s importance-ratio factorization, score centering is from Marek & Ryabinin (2026), the clip is PPO's, and the ESS-based adaptive control echoes VCPO and Huang et al. (2026)'s observation that ESS collapses before KL grows. DLC is a principled combination with one new moving part (resynchronization triggered by the lag-ratio ESS) rather than a wholly new estimator; the genuinely original content is the loop analysis (quadratic ηk term, measured c = 0.52) and the off-support-share collapse diagnostic.
- **Rigor**: strong on measurement hygiene (paired references, noise floor, 3 seeds, both ratio-of-means and mean-of-ratios reported in Table 6, linear fits with R², bias prediction validated by cosine alignment). The paper also honestly demonstrates that its own primary metric (KL on the sampler's fixed prefixes) is blind to the collapse failure mode — which is both a strength (transparency) and a warning that the headline deviation numbers understate risk.
- **Weak spots in the evaluation**: the main stale regime is the extreme one-shot limit — synchronization every 64 steps over a 40-step run means the sampler never updates at all, so "staleness" here is a frozen behavior policy rather than the 1–4 step lag of production async systems (the continuous-asynchrony appendix, single run, version gap ≤4 only partially addresses this). On Qwen3-0.6B DLC does not beat importance-sampling-with-centering (31.7% vs 32.0% accuracy; 1.9 vs 2.1 deviation), and on Qwen3-8B it is slightly behind (49.6% vs 51.8%, 3.1 vs 2.9) — DLC's advantage is specifically robustness on 1.7B where plain IS collapses, not uniform dominance. All trainings are 40 steps on one task (Countdown, ±1 rewards) with SGD at lr 10⁻²; no AdamW/Muon, no curriculum, no long-horizon results, so the ηk-loop story is verified only in this regime. Hyperparameters (ESS threshold ½, clip 0.2, head 128) are fixed without sensitivity ablations, and GAC/SAT/GMC-GRPO are discussed but never compared empirically; M2PO and VCPO appear only in the appendix tables.
- **Reproducibility**: good — public code, standard stack, full configuration in Appendix B, exact algorithm in Algorithm 1, and DLC's two reduction limits make it easy to sanity-check.
- **Significance**: real for anyone building async RL infrastructure (AReaL/PipelineRL-style systems): it gives a principled sync trigger and explains when cheap fixes (centering alone) suffice. Red flags: none severe, but the 40-step horizon means the reported KL/accuracy ordering could change over thousands of steps, and the Countdown task's binary dense reward is far simpler than verifier-based math/code RL where these systems are actually deployed.

## Strengths

- Gives the first unified causal account (constant discrepancy vs self-induced lag) of why mismatch corrections are source-dependent, and validates each link of the proposed loop with targeted measurements (lag growth at 110–150× ‖Δ‖², sign of lag by advantage group, learning-rate collapse onto ηk).
- DLC is nearly free at the algorithm layer: one extra forward pass at θ_old (a forward that PPO-with-sampler-reference already needs) and the same top-128 log-probability reporting as score centering; it degrades gracefully to known corrections in its limits.
- The resynchronization trigger is adaptive and empirically parsimonious — once per 40 steps on Qwen3-1.7B versus every-11-steps for a fixed interval — and directly actionable for async system design.
- The off-support-share diagnostic identifies a collapse mode that per-token corrections and prefix-KL monitors both miss, with quantified predictive power (Spearman 0.80 vs 0.74).
- Honest, detailed experimental hygiene: paired on-policy references, noise floors, 3 seeds, reproduced baselines from prior work, and appendices extending to Qwen3-8B, continuous asynchrony, and wall-clock cost accounting.

## Limitations

- Tiny experimental envelope: 40 training steps, Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B (8B single-run appendix), one task (Countdown with ±1 rewards), one optimizer (SGD, lr 10⁻²). No evidence yet for long-horizon training, verifier-based math/code RLHF workloads, or Adam-family optimizers where the ηk argument changes shape.
- The headline stale-sampler setting (sync every 64 steps within a 40-step run) is maximal staleness with a frozen sampler, not the mild lag of realistic async pipelines; the continuous-asynchrony result is a single run.
- DLC does not dominate: on 0.6B it ties/trails IS+centering (31.7% vs 32.0%) and on 8B it trails (49.6% vs 51.8%, 3.1× vs 2.9× floor); its clearest win is the 1.7B regime where IS collapses.
- Full weight-copy resynchronization may be expensive at real scale (multi-node samplers, large models), and the top-128 head reporting doubles generation time (the same cost as score centering, but non-zero).
- The ESS threshold of one half, clip 0.2, and head 128 are heuristics without sensitivity analysis; the measured crossing (step 11) deviates from the Gaussian prediction (step 14), showing the threshold's derivation is approximate.
- The deviation metric's blindness to off-support collapse (demonstrated by the authors themselves) means small deviation numbers should not be read as safety guarantees.

## Relevance

对"LLM 强化学习训练稳定性/效率与系统-算法协同设计"方向高度相关：论文直接剖析 vLLM 采样器与训练器（verl/FSDP2）之间的失配来源，并给出可落地的异步 RL 稳定化方案（自适应重同步触发条件与坍塌预警指标），对构建大规模异步 RL 训练系统有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the ηk-loop analysis (and DLC's advantage over IS+centering) survive thousands of steps, AdamW/Muon optimizers, and learning-rate schedules where the accumulated-update distance grows differently?
- How does the ESS-of-lag-ratio threshold behave under verifier-based math/code RL with length rewards and temperature schedules — and can the off-support share serve as an online early-stopping/resync signal rather than a post-hoc diagnostic?
- Can resynchronization be made incremental (weight-delta streaming, staged/partial sync, or LoRA-style updates) to amortize the copy cost for multi-node samplers at 30B+ scale?
- Would combining DLC's discrepancy centering with sequence-level corrections (GSPO-style sequence clipping, M2PO's second-moment bound) strictly dominate either alone, given the heavy-tailed lag distribution they document?
- Does the quantized-and-stale interaction change at FP4/NVFP4 precision, where the constant numerical discrepancy e_t grows large enough that the top-128 head estimate of the centering term becomes biased?
