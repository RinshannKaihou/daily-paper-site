> **一句话总结：** 在 GPT-2 上用 2×2×3 因素剪枝实验（幅度/随机 × 注意力头/MLP神经元 × 30/50/70% 稀疏度）配合 IOI 电路的因果分析，发现两种质性不同的失效模式——幅度剪头（≥50%）直接"清零"电路（DLA 归零、近乎随机猜），而 30% 幅度剪神经元会"反转"电路：26 个电路头全部完好但 Name Mover 贡献翻号、模型系统性地选错名字（IOI 准确率从 94.8% 跌到 17.7%），且 TinyStories 困惑度（74.6 vs 140.1）反而把它排在随机剪头之前——证明准确率/困惑度和"电路还在不在"的结构检查都会漏检机理损伤。

> **TL;DR:** The authors prune GPT-2 small in a factorial design (magnitude vs random selection × attention heads vs MLP neurons × 30/50/70% sparsity) and audit the well-characterised IOI circuit with activation/path patching and direct logit attribution. Magnitude head pruning at ≥50% zeroes the circuit (all four head-class DLAs exactly 0) yet leaves 45.7–47.9% accuracy from non-circuit computation; more dangerously, magnitude neuron pruning at just 30% inverts the circuit — all 26 heads structurally intact (weight norms > 25) but Name Mover DLA flips from +3.576 to −1.102, crashing IOI accuracy from 94.8% to 17.7% (logit difference −2.475) while TinyStories perplexity (74.61) still ranks it *less* damaged than random head pruning (140.10). A Greater-Than circuit replication shows the critical component type flips for MLP-dominant circuits (accuracy hits 0.0% at 50% neuron pruning). The paper argues output metrics and structural checks both miss mechanistic damage, so circuit-level causal analysis should complement compression audits.

## 核心贡献

- **发现并命名两种质性不同的剪枝失效模式**："电路清零"（zeroing，幅度剪注意力头 ≥50% 直接物理删除输出端电路头）与"电路反转"（inversion，30% 幅度剪 MLP 神经元时 26 个 IOI 电路头全部完好，但残差流被"下毒"，Name Mover 头照常复制却指向错误实体，模型自信且系统性地输出错误答案）。
- **证明主流评估手段双重失明**：困惑度会把"被反转"的模型误排为损伤更轻（30% 幅度剪神经元 PPL 74.61 < 随机剪头 140.10，但其 IOI logit 差 −2.475 远差于 −0.706）；而"电路头是否还在"的结构检查也会给反转模型放行——必须做头级因果归因才能发现。
- **2×2×3 因素实验设计 + 多工具收敛验证**：以 Wang et al. (2022) 的 26 头 IOI 电路为 ground truth，用激活补丁、路径补丁、DLA、CKA/余弦相似度、logit lens、注意力统计和 SAE 重构误差七类证据交叉印证失效机理（"上下文中毒"机制）。
- **Greater-Than 电路复现揭示规律的一般性边界**：GT 电路是 MLP 主导的（L9/L10/L8 MLP 的 DLA 0.867/0.863/0.557 最大），因此神经元剪枝直接摧毁计算本身（50% 时两种准则下准确率都精确归零），而注意力头剪枝只渐进退化——说明"哪种组件类型致命"取决于电路架构，但"组件类型比稀疏度更能决定失效模式"这一结论跨电路成立。
- **实践警示**：损伤对稀疏度非单调（幅度剪神经元 30% 时退化 184.1%，反而比 70% 的 123.5% 更严重）——最容易被误判为"保守安全"的低稀疏度恰恰是模型被误导最严重的配置；对安全机制而言，剪枝方式可能决定失败是"能力下降"还是"行为反转"。

## Key Contributions

- **Identifies two qualitatively distinct mechanistic failure modes of structured pruning.** *Circuit zeroing*: magnitude attention-head pruning at ≥50% sparsity physically removes the output-facing IOI heads (DLA of all four major classes — NM, NNM, S-INH, BNM — falls to exactly zero). *Circuit inversion*: magnitude MLP-neuron pruning at 30% leaves all 26 circuit heads structurally intact (weight norms > 25) but poisons the residual-stream context they read, flipping Name Mover contributions from +3.576 to −1.102 and making the model confidently and systematically predict the wrong name.
- **Shows that both standard evaluation channels are blind to mechanistic damage.** Perplexity misranks the inverted model as the *less* damaged one; structural checks ("are the circuit heads still present?") pass it too. Only head-level causal analysis (activation patching, path patching, DLA) exposes the failure.
- **A rigorous factorial design with converging evidence.** 2 (criteria: magnitude, random) × 2 (component: heads, neurons) × 3 (sparsity: 30/50/70%) = 12 pruned GPT-2 variants, audited with seven complementary instruments: activation patching, path patching, DLA, CKA/cosine representational similarity, logit lens, attention-pattern statistics, and frozen-SAE reconstruction error.
- **A cross-circuit replication on Greater-Than.** The critical component type depends on circuit architecture: for the MLP-dominant GT circuit, neuron pruning removes the computation itself (accuracy exactly 0.0% at 50% sparsity under both criteria), while head pruning degrades gradually — yet in both circuits, component type, not sparsity level, determines the failure.
- **A non-monotonicity warning for practitioners.** Damage is worst at the sparsity considered conservative: magnitude neuron pruning degrades IOI by 184.1% at 30% but only 123.5% at 70%, because heavier corruption destroys the attention patterns' discriminability and the "confidently wrong" signal fades back toward noise.

## Methodology

All experiments use pretrained GPT-2 small (117M parameters, 12 layers, 12 heads/layer, d=768) via TransformerLens (LayerNorm folding disabled to preserve activation scales; seed 42; NVIDIA L4/T4 on Colab), with **no weight updates after pruning** so all variants start from matched conditions. Twelve pruned variants cross two importance criteria with two component types and three sparsity levels (30%, 50%, 70%):

- **Attention-head pruning** zeroes each head's Q/K/V slices in `c_attn` plus its `c_proj` rows; magnitude selection scores heads by the L2 norm of the Q-weight slice and removes the k lowest globally; random selection removes a fixed fraction per layer.
- **MLP-neuron pruning** zeroes the `W_in` columns / `W_out` rows; magnitude selection uses the L2 norm of the `W_in` column; random selection samples uniformly per layer.

Ground truth is the 26-head IOI circuit (Wang et al., 2022). Prompts follow the ABB/ABC design (15 templates, 14 objects, 28 single-token names; 300-sentence eval set), each paired with a corrupted counterpart for activation patching; the primary behavioural metric is the mean logit difference ℓ(IO) − ℓ(S). Circuit integrity is measured by (1) activation patching over all 26 heads, (2) path patching over the four critical communication pathways (PT→IND, DT→S-INH, IND→NM, S-INH→NM), and (3) direct logit attribution (DLA) decomposed by functional class (NM, NNM, S-INH, BNM), computed with a linear approximation of the final LayerNorm so contributions sum to the observed logit difference. Complementary measurements: TinyStories perplexity (1000 sequences), layer-wise cosine similarity and linear CKA (early L0–L3 vs late L8–L11), logit-lens divergence on 500 TinyStories prompts (Bonferroni-corrected), head-averaged attention statistics (entropy, effective support, first-token mass, head cosine), and reconstruction error of a frozen SAE trained on base GPT-2. The Greater-Than circuit (Hanna et al., 2023; 6 heads, MLP-dominant; metric = mean logit of valid minus invalid year completions) serves as the replication target with the same 12 variants.

## Results & Highlights

- **Circuit inversion is the single worst variant in the study, and perplexity misses it.** On the IOI task, magnitude neuron pruning at 30% sparsity reaches a mean logit difference of −2.475 and 17.7% accuracy (vs base GPT-2: +2.942, 94.8%), yet its TinyStories perplexity is 74.61 — better than random attention-head pruning at the same sparsity (140.10) whose logit difference is only −0.706. A perplexity audit would rank the most damaged circuit as the healthier model.
- **Zeroing is transparent but leaves misleading residual accuracy.** Magnitude head pruning at 50%/70% sparsity zeroes the DLA of all four circuit classes exactly to zero (weight-norm verification confirms physical removal; pruned-head attention becomes near-uniform, IO_attn = 0.067, S_attn = 0.133), yet IOI accuracy stays at 45.7%/47.9% — near-chance output carried by non-circuit computation.
- **The inversion signature is measurable at the head level.** At 30% magnitude neuron pruning, NM DLA retention is −30.8% (+3.576 → −1.102), BNM is −119.9% (the most extreme inversion in the study), NNM loses its suppressive role (−23.0%), while all 26 heads remain structurally intact (weight norms > 25); the surviving Name Movers attend to the wrong token (L9H6: IO_attn = 0.051 vs S_attn = 0.650).
- **Damage is non-monotonic in sparsity.** Magnitude neuron pruning degrades IOI by 184.1% at 30% but only 123.5% at 70% (logit difference −0.692) — the 30% model is "strongly wrong," the 70% model "weakly wrong," a distinction invisible to accuracy.
- **The Greater-Than replication reverses the component-type roles.** Base GPT-2 achieves ∆GT = 2.855 (94.0% argmax accuracy); magnitude neuron pruning inverts ∆GT at every sparsity (accuracy ≤ 0.2%, and exactly 0.0% at 50% under both neuron criteria), while magnitude head pruning degrades gradually (66.1% / 53.5% / 17.7% accuracy at 30/50/70%), crossing zero logit difference only at 70%.
- **Representational fidelity localises the bottleneck to late layers.** At 70% sparsity, magnitude head pruning retains overall CKA 0.851 (highest of any variant at that sparsity) vs 0.500 for magnitude neuron pruning; early-layer CKA stays ≥ 0.900 in every condition while late-layer CKA never exceeds 0.848 and falls to 0.260 — exactly where the IOI output heads sit. A criterion reversal appears for neurons at 30%: random (CKA 0.825) preserves more structure than magnitude (0.698), consistent with magnitude carving localised representational voids.
- **Aggregate attention statistics cannot detect inversion.** Head pruning diffuses attention (entropy 2.013 → 3.561 nats at 70%, effective support 11.4 → ~51 tokens, attention-sink mass 0.421 → 0.098), but the strongly inverted 30% magnitude-neuron model shows only ∆H = +0.173 — looking nearly intact in aggregate even though its Name Movers attend sharply to the wrong token.
- **SAE evidence corroborates context poisoning.** At 30% magnitude neuron pruning, Layer-1 reconstruction error of a frozen base-model SAE reaches 155.87 (over 350× the base value) while all circuit heads stay intact — the early residual stream collapses into a low-rank subspace, corrupting the name-identity encodings the Name Movers read.

## Authors & Credibility

Both authors (Nabeelah Faumi and Deshan Sumanathilaka) are at the Informatics Institute of Technology, Colombo, Sri Lanka — a small institution rather than a major interpretability lab, which makes the thoroughness of the work more notable. The paper is an arXiv preprint (27 Oct 2026, v1); no venue acceptance is claimed. Methodologically it is careful in the ways that matter for circuit work: it builds on the canonical, fully characterised IOI circuit (Wang et al., 2022) and GT circuit (Hanna et al., 2023) as ground truth rather than rediscovering circuits; it uses established open tooling (TransformerLens); it reports effect sizes (paired Cohen's d) and Bonferroni-corrected significance for patching results; and it states sign conventions explicitly (e.g., NNM has negative base DLA −2.094, so negative retention means sign inversion). The limitations section is unusually candid, flagging that its own safety remarks are extrapolation and that its SAE analysis only shows activations leave the base manifold. No code release is mentioned in the text.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The framing — using reverse-engineered circuits as a ground-truth audit of compression — is genuinely underexplored, and the "circuit inversion" phenomenon (structurally intact circuit, confidently wrong behaviour, at the *least* aggressive sparsity) is a crisp, memorable finding. Prior work on attribution-guided pruning and pruning effects on refusal circuits exists (the authors cite Chhabra & Khalili 2025), and the paper's claim to be first to characterise qualitatively distinct failure modes is plausible but narrowly framed.
- **Rigor:** Strong for its scale: factorial design, seven converging measurement instruments, statistical testing, sign conventions documented, and honest caveats (e.g., patching effect sizes lose sensitivity at high sparsity, so d should not be read as damage magnitude across sparsity levels — the authors say this themselves). The perplexity-misranking argument is quantified precisely.
- **Reproducibility:** Good in principle — public checkpoint, standard library, fixed seed, fully specified design — but no code or prompt-generation script is stated to be released, and the DLA LayerNorm-linearisation and retention-percentage conventions would need care to replicate exactly.
- **Weak baselines / protocol gaps (the paper concedes most of these):** the magnitude criteria are textbook L2 norms (head Q-slice, neuron W_in column) with **no post-pruning fine-tuning** — but real deployment pipelines almost always fine-tune or distill after pruning, and recovery training could plausibly repair or re-route the inverted circuit, so the practical severity is untested. No comparison against modern structured-pruning criteria (movement, gradient-, Wanda-style weight×activation, or the attribution-guided methods the related-work section itself cites) — "random" is a weak control for "everything besides magnitude."
- **Data quirks:** evaluation rests on two synthetic-template circuits (300 IOI sentences; year-completion GT prompts) and a single model (GPT-2 small, 117M); TinyStories is an odd choice for perplexity (children's-story distribution rather than the usual WikiText), though its low baseline PPL (11.54) makes the 18,934 blow-up at 70% magnitude-neuron pruning vivid. No natural-language downstream task is tested, so the link from circuit inversion to real deployment risk remains hypothetical.
- **Red flags / overclaiming:** mostly avoided — the safety implications are explicitly labelled extrapolation, and the taxonomy is offered as "hypotheses to be tested on other circuits." The main overreach risk is the title's generality ("Transformer Language Models") for what is a one-model, two-circuit study.

## Strengths

- Identifies and precisely characterises a failure mode (inversion) that is invisible to every metric in standard compression audits, and demonstrates the blind spot quantitatively rather than rhetorically.
- Exceptionally convergent methodology: patching, path patching, DLA, CKA, logit lens, attention statistics, and SAE reconstruction all triangulate the same context-poisoning mechanism.
- The cross-circuit replication (IOI vs Greater-Than) turns a one-off observation into a conditional rule — which component type is critical depends on whether the circuit is attention- or MLP-carried — which is more scientifically useful than a universal claim.
- Clear, honest writing: sign conventions, non-monotonicities, and the failure of their own instruments (patching d losing sensitivity) are disclosed rather than hidden.
- Directly actionable implication: circuit-level causal analysis should be part of compression audits, and "conservative" sparsity levels deserve the most scrutiny, not the least.

## Limitations

- Single tiny model (GPT-2 small, 117M); larger models have more redundant, distributed circuits, and the authors themselves name replication on GPT-2 Medium/Large or Pythia as the most immediate extension.
- Only two circuits, both on synthetic template prompts; no test of whether circuit damage propagates to natural-language tasks that reuse the same computation, so ecological validity is unestablished.
- Pruning protocol is deliberately austere: weight zeroing with no recovery fine-tuning, no unstructured/layer-level pruning, no gradient- or attribution-based criteria — any of which could change or repair the observed failure modes.
- The safety discussion (refusal circuits being reversed rather than weakened) is extrapolation from S-Inhibition/NNM behaviour in a model with no safety training.
- The SAE analysis uses an SAE trained on the base model, so high reconstruction error only proves activations left the base feature manifold, not what the pruned model computes; Layer 11 had to be excluded.

## Relevance

（与剪枝/压缩模型的可信评估兴趣直接相关：如果只看准确率和困惑度来放行剪枝后的大模型，本文证明这两类指标连同"电路头是否还在"的结构检查都可能漏掉"自信地做错"的机理损伤，且最危险的恰恰是被视为保守的 30% 低稀疏度。对任何关注模型压缩 + 可解释性/安全评估交叉方向的工作都是直接的动机性证据。）

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does post-pruning recovery fine-tuning repair the inverted circuit (restore NM sign), re-route around it, or lock the inverted behaviour in? This decides whether inversion matters for real pipelines.
- Do the failure modes transfer to scale — e.g., do Llama-class models with redundant backup heads show inversion at all, or does redundancy absorb it?
- Is the IOI-specific finding generalisable to behaviourally critical circuits in deployed models (refusal, factual recall, instruction-following), where "inversion" would be a safety failure rather than a benchmark drop?
- Could a cheap proxy for circuit-level causal analysis (e.g., targeted activation-patching probes on a handful of known circuits) be built into standard compression benchmarks at acceptable cost?
- Why do Negative Name Mover heads show paradoxically *negative* patching effect sizes in all twelve conditions (d = −0.205 to −0.539)? The authors hypothesise precision requirements but do not test it.
- Do modern criteria (Wanda's weight×activation, movement pruning, attribution-guided retention) produce the same magnitude-vs-random inversion asymmetry, or do they avoid the localised representational voids that cause context poisoning?
