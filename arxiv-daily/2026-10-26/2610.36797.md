> **一句话总结：** 本文发现花园路径句（garden-path sentence）会在语言模型的上下文中留下两条相反的"痕迹"——被放弃的宾语分析作为"残留"提高后续生成中的宾语挂接（24 个开源模型平均达 0.72 nats，约为真宾语 prime 效应的 76%），而向消歧后结构的"适应"则随消歧误差增长；激活修补实验证明残留由消歧前计算的名词短语状态的论元角色信息承载（在 Qwen2.5-32B 和 Llama-3.1-70B 中替换这些状态几乎完全消除残留），且被推翻的事实陈述同样持续拉偏后续预测。

> **TL;DR:** A single-author psycholinguistics-meets-interpretability study showing that in 24 open causal LMs (124M–70B, from GPT-2/Pythia to Llama-3.1-70B), a garden-path prime ("While the man hunted the deer ran...") leaves an abandoned object analysis in context that raises later object attachment by 0.72 nats on average — 76% of the effect of a prime whose NP really is an object (the TB control) — on a 93-family NP/Z discovery set (positive in 22/22 models, significant in 21; Pythia-160M the exception), replicating on a 48-family held-out set (median 0.36 nats) and on Christianson et al. (2001) items (median 1.05 nats). Activation patching shows later text reads this residue from the noun phrase's pre-disambiguation states (replacing them retains only pooled ρ = 0.37, and ≤ the 0.21–0.22 interval bounds in Qwen2.5-32B / Llama-3.1-70B), while a second, opposite adaptation toward the resolved structure scales with disambiguation error and acts on comprehension-like surprisal. The human paradigm's raw statistic (van Gompel et al. 2006) reverses in LMs — negative in 7 of 8 Llama/Qwen models, −17.6 points in Llama-3.1-70B — because models themselves write comma-less garden-path continuations (up to 29% of completions) unless punctuation is controlled.

## 核心贡献

- **首次系统研究"被放弃的句法分析"对语言模型后续生成的影响**：此前关于花园路径句的 LM 研究只考察消歧难度或最终理解准确性，本文首次证明被放弃的宾语分析会像在人类身上一样（van Gompel et al., 2006）持续 prime 后续语言生成。
- **设计了一个干净的双重对照实验对比式（ΔX）**：通过 GP/CUE（逗号配对）与 NB/NC（动词配对）的 2×2 对比，同时控制标点和动词偏向，从而把残留效应与标点习惯、动词偏好和局部字符串重叠分离；还用 NOM（主格代词）和 PASS（被动包裹同一字符串）控制局部连贯性干扰。
- **发现并分离了方向相反的两个效应**：残留（residual activation，随被放弃分析的强度增长）作用于生成式指标 L，适应（error-driven learning，随消歧误差增长）作用于理解式指标 S——用人类行为范式无法同时观测的两个效应在 LM 中来自同一个 prime。
- **用激活修补（activation patching）定位残留的物理载体**：后续文本是从名词短语 token（在消歧之前、按宾语分析计算的键值状态）读取被放弃的分析；线性探针以 96%–99% 准确率证明这些状态编码了论元角色，沿探针方向干预部分移除残留。
- **将结论推广到"被推翻的事实陈述"**：被更正过的陈述内容仍以 0.6–2.3 nats 拉偏 9 个模型中 8 个的后续预测，且多数模型中该影响经由陈述之后的位置（包括更正句本身）中继传递。
- **方法学警示**：人类范式的原始统计量（GP 减逗号 prime）在 LM 中反转，因为模型自己会生成无逗号的花园路径句；不加标点对照直接迁移范式会得出相反结论。

## Key Contributions

- First systematic study of what an **abandoned syntactic analysis** does to later text in language models: prior garden-path work in LMs studied only disambiguation difficulty (surprisal) or final-interpretation accuracy, not the downstream consequences of the discarded parse.
- A clean **2×2 residue contrast** (Eq. 2: GP−NB minus CUE−NC) that matches punctuation within brackets and verbs across brackets, ruling out comma habits, verb bias, and lexical repetition as sources; auxiliary NOM (nominative pronoun) and PASS (same string inside a passive) controls address punctuation-habit and local-coherence (Tabor et al. 2004) confounds.
- Identification of **two opposite effects from one prime**: a residue consistent with residual activation (grows with how strongly the object analysis was built) that dominates the production-like measure L (log-odds of object attachment), and an adaptation consistent with error-driven learning (grows with disambiguation surprisal) that dominates the comprehension-like measure S — with confirmatory experiments pre-registered in dated records before data collection.
- **Mechanistic localization via activation patching** across nine models (1.4B–70B): later text reads the abandoned analysis from the noun-phrase states computed *before* disambiguation (lower/middle layers), not from the post-disambiguation main-clause states; replacing NP states with subject-analysis states removes almost the whole residue in the 32B and 70B models, and cross-context transplants show what matters is the analysis, not the tokens (70%–111% retained when NP states are re-computed under an object analysis elsewhere).
- **Generalization beyond syntax**: overridden statements (e.g., "The meeting is on Thursday. Correction: The meeting is on Monday.") keep pulling later predictions toward the overridden value in 8 of 9 models (0.6–2.3 nats), with attention-knockout experiments revealing two carrier routes (direct read from the statement vs. relay through later positions).
- A **transferability warning** for the human paradigm: the raw human statistic reverses in LMs (negative in 7 of 8 Llama/Qwen models) unless punctuation is controlled, because models generate their own comma-less garden-path continuations after a garden-path prime.

## Methodology

- **Materials.** Three item sets: a 93-family NP/Z discovery set (69 items from Amouyal et al. 2025 + 24 from the SAP benchmark of Huang et al. 2024); a 48-family held-out set (Ceháková & Chromý 2025 + SyntaxGym) analyzed only after the discovery analyses were fixed; and the 42 optionally-transitive items of Christianson et al. (2001) with short/long ambiguous regions and plausible/implausible objects. A fourth set of 64 NP/S families tests a second garden-path type.
- **Prime conditions.** Each family yields GP (garden path), CUE (comma after verb), TB (pronoun subject keeps the NP as a real object), NB/NC (strictly intransitive verb without/with comma), NOM (nominative "they" keeps verb + missing comma but no possible object), PASS (same string inside a passive clause).
- **Measures.** Production-like L = log P(While s v n,) − log P(While s v, n) — the log-odds of attaching the target NP as object; comprehension-like S = surprisal of the target's main-clause first word after the comma-less fragment; plus free generation G scored by a rule-based POS-tag coder for transitive continuations. The residue contrast ΔX (Eq. 2) subtracts punctuation-matched, verb-matched controls; r = ΔX/ΔX_TB expresses the residue relative to a true-object prime.
- **Models and inference.** 24 open-weight causal LMs (GPT-2, Pythia, Llama, Mistral, Gemma, Qwen families; base and instruction-tuned; 124M–70B; one MoE), 22 in every priming experiment. Exact log-probabilities without chat templates; sampling at temperature 1 without truncation (~57,000 prompts per model for the generation experiment in 12 models). Two-way bootstrap over prime and target families (matched resampling across models) gives 95% intervals.
- **Causal tests.** Within-family manipulation fixing the prime prefix up to the NP and varying only the continuation to isolate disambiguation error; regression of family-level residue on object expectation and garden-path strength.
- **Activation patching.** At every layer, NP-token residual states in GP are replaced with states from CUE (subject analysis), the rest of the prime recomputed; the target attends to a mixture (counterfactual keys/values at NP, main-clause MC, or both). NB→NC donor patching controls for imported comma information; layer-fraction patching (upper half / upper quarter) localizes depth; a cross-context transplant (prefixing "It was a quiet morning.") re-computes NP states under an object analysis; a linear probe (trained on TB vs NB, tested on comma TC/CUE items) plus a role-direction steering intervention test the role representation.
- **Overridden statements.** 200 items from ten correction frames, with an other-entity control matching exposure to the overridden value; measured as the log-probability advantage of the overridden value at a confirmation probe; attention knockout at the correcting sentence, the overridden statement, or all later positions localizes the carrier.

## Results & Highlights

- **Residue on production (discovery set, 93 NP/Z families):** ΔL is positive in all 22 models and excludes zero in 21 (exception: Pythia-160M); averaged over models it is 0.72 nats, which is **76% of ΔL_TB** — the effect of a prime whose NP really is an object. On the 48-family held-out set it is positive in all 22 models (20 significant) with a **median of 0.36 nats**, and on the Christianson et al. (2001) items positive in all 24 models with a **median of 1.05 nats**.
- **Free generation:** sampling two continuations per fragment (~57,000 prompts per model, 12 models), the residue raises transitive continuations by **+5.0 percentage points on average** (11/12 models with the pre-specified rule-based coding, 12/12 with a refined coding), comparable in direction to the human raw contrast of 4.5 points / 33.6% vs 29.1% in van Gompel et al. (2006).
- **Punctuation and local-overlap controls:** the NOM case contrast is positive in all 22 models (21 significant, 0.47 nats average) — two thirds of the 0.72-nat comma contrast — so most of the residue needs an NP that could be an object; the PASS (local string overlap) residue has a median of only 12% of the GP residue across 22 models, though it is higher in the strongest models (0.45 in Llama-3.1-70B, >0.5 in both Qwen2.5-7B variants).
- **Second garden-path type:** for 64 NP/S families the residue is positive in 19 of 22 models (sign test p < 0.001) but small (pooled 0.03 nats), consistent with weaker cross-structure priming (even a real object NP/S prime moves NP/Z targets only 0.03–0.20 nats).
- **Two effects with opposite signs:** on the comprehension-like measure S the residue averages just 0.05 nats (significantly positive in 5 models, negative in 4). Object expectation raises the residue on both measures in all 22 models, while garden-path strength *lowers* the residue on S in 21 of 22 models (all 22, 19 significant, with an adaptation covariate) and raises it on L in most models — i.e., the error-driven adaptation acts on comprehension, the residue on production. The causal within-family manipulation (varying only the continuation) yields t between −4.1 and −6.4 in the three models that showed a between-family error effect, and near zero in the two that did not.
- **Patching localizes the residue to the noun phrase:** replacing NP states retains only 0.01–0.71 of the residue (pooled 0.37), with retained values at or below the interval bounds of 0.21 and 0.22 in Qwen2.5-32B and Llama-3.1-70B — i.e., removal of almost the entire residue — whereas replacing main-clause states leaves it intact (pooled 1.01). Patching only the upper half of layers has no effect in Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Mistral-7B (upper quarter for Qwen2.5-7B), so later text reads the abandoned analysis from NP keys/values in lower and middle layers. A linear probe classifies object vs subject NPs with **96%–99% accuracy** in all seven models tested; cross-context object-analysis transplants retain 70%–111% of the residue.
- **Overridden statements:** relative to the exposure-matched control, the overridden value pulls later predictions toward itself by **0.6 to 2.3 nats in 8 of 9 models** (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct is the sole exception), in 9 or 10 of the 10 frames each. Attention knockout shows the pull is relayed through later positions in six models but read directly from the overridden statement in Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen2.5-32B, and Llama-3.1-70B.
- **Human-paradigm transfer fails without punctuation controls:** the raw GP−CUE contrast is reliably positive in only 4 of 12 models and significantly **negative** in 7 of 8 Llama/Qwen models — most strongly −17.6 points in Llama-3.1-70B — because up to **29% of completions after a garden-path prime are themselves comma-less garden-path sentences** (vs 0.2%–2.4% after comma primes; cross-model correlation ρ = −0.82). Excluding these self-written garden paths, the raw contrast is positive in all 12 models (median 10 points).
- **Lingering comprehension ≠ priming residue:** across the Christianson items, yes-answers to "Did the man hunt the deer?" are not positively correlated with the priming residue (r between −0.30 and 0.15 in ten models) despite good split-half reliabilities (0.77–0.98), so the two lingering phenomena are dissociable.

## Authors & Credibility

Single-authored paper by Xiang Ding (Nagoya University). The experimental program is unusually rigorous for a behavioral-LM study: pre-registered dated predictions for every confirmatory experiment (with failures reported in Appendix D), a held-out item set analyzed only after fixing the discovery-set analyses, prospective tests, two-way bootstrap intervals that preserve cross-model item dependence, matched punctuation/verb controls, and both correlational and causal (patching, transplant, steering) evidence converging on the same locus. The model coverage (24 open models, 124M–70B, six families, base + instruct, one MoE) and the honest treatment of failed pre-specifications (behavioural manipulation in Appendix F did not pass its check; one overridden-statement prediction failed) increase confidence. This sits in the intersection of the Futrell/van Schijndel/Linzen surprisal tradition, the Christianson lingering-misinterpretation literature, and mechanistic-interpretability patching (Vig, Meng, Nanda).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuinely novel question — no prior work asks what an *abandoned* analysis does to later LM text (Hanna & Mueller 2025 studied circuits for comprehension questions, not downstream priming), and the decomposition into residue vs adaptation mapped onto residual-activation vs error-driven accounts is a real conceptual contribution, as is extending to overridden statements.
- **Rigor:** High. Pre-registration with dated records, held-out items, punctuation/verb-matched contrasts, NOM/PASS controls for exactly the confounds a skeptic would raise (comma habits, local coherence), causal patching with an NB-donor control for imported comma information, and prospective replication on later-acquired models. The paper openly lists which pre-specified predictions failed.
- **Reproducibility:** Strong in principle — all open-weight checkpoints, public item sets (Amouyal et al., SAP/SyntaxGym, Christianson et al.), exact log-probabilities without chat templates, and a rule-based completion coder checked against gold sentences and one annotator. However, no code release is mentioned in the main text, and the single-annotator check on ~57k-prompt-scale generation coding is thin.
- **Significance:** Moderately broad. The core scientific payoff is for psycholinguistics (a testable human prediction: vary only the continuation of a fixed garden-path prefix) and for context-engineering practice (appending a correction is a weaker intervention than deleting the corrected text — relevant to RAG-style context editing and to why models rarely retract wrong answers). It is a finding about in-context representations, not about training or capabilities.
- **Red flags / weaknesses:** (1) The patching and probe analyses cover only 6–9 of the 24 models, and the behavioural manipulation of the abandoned analysis (Appendix F) failed its own pre-specified check, so the causal story rests on the patching route alone. (2) The effect sizes on comprehension-like measures are tiny (0.05 nats average; NP/S residue pooled 0.03 nats) and sign-variable across models, so the "two opposite effects" framing on S leans heavily on regression slopes rather than robust mean differences. (3) The PASS control actually shows a substantial local-coherence residue in the strongest models (0.45 in Llama-3.1-70B, >0.5 in Qwen2.5-7B models) — more than the paper's "median 12%" framing foregrounds — meaning string-level overlap explains a non-trivial share in exactly the models people care about. (4) All materials are English and templated (overridden statements from ten frames); the role-direction intervention worked in only 3 of 7 models, so the role interpretation of the NP states is supported but not airtight. (5) Closed frontier models were not tested, by necessity of exact log-probabilities and state access.

## Strengths

- Clean separation of a tiny effect (fractions of a nat) from three plausible confounds (punctuation habit, verb bias, local string overlap) via the 2×2 contrast plus NOM/PASS controls — a model of careful behavioral design in LM research.
- Converging evidence from four independent methods (behavioral contrasts, regression on prime properties, activation patching with two donor designs, linear probing + directional steering) all pointing to pre-disambiguation NP states as the carrier.
- Honest pre-registration culture: predictions fixed before data collection, failures reported (Appendix D), held-out set untouched until analyses were frozen.
- The §8 punctuation finding is an immediately useful methodological warning for anyone porting human sentence-production paradigms (written fragments, spoken-aloud completion) to LMs.
- The overridden-statement extension connects the syntactic finding to the practically important problem of context correction and knowledge editing.

## Limitations

- English-only; NP/S and main-verb/reduced-relative garden paths get only single experiments (the error relation appeared in just 6 of 9 models for MV/RR, and neither relation for NP/S).
- Open-weight models up to 70B only — closed models excluded because exact log-probabilities and internal-state access are required.
- The causal evidence base is narrow relative to the behavioral one (patching/steering on 6–9 models; the behavioural manipulation in Appendix F failed its pre-specified check).
- The Equation-2 contrast was defined after inspecting the first two discovery models, so its confirmation depends on the held-out set and prospective tests rather than full out-of-sample discovery.
- Overridden statements are templated (ten frames), and one pre-specified prediction about their mechanism failed, motivating the post-hoc different-carrier interpretation.
- Rule-based coding of sampled completions was validated against gold sentences and a single annotator sample — light for a measure that carries a headline finding (+5.0 points).
- The human prediction (varying only the continuation of a fixed garden-path prefix separates residual activation from error-driven learning) is derived but not tested.

## Relevance

这项工作直接关系到"语言模型如何处理上下文中的冲突/被推翻信息"这一核心问题：它用激活修补证明了被放弃的句法分析和被更正的事实陈述都会以旧的表征形式残留在上下文中并继续影响后续预测，对上下文工程、知识编辑和 RAG 中"追加更正而非删除原文"的实践有直接警示意义；同时它把结构 priming 的两大理论（残留激活 vs 误差驱动学习）映射到 Transformer 的具体状态位置上，是心理语言学与大模型可解释性交叉方向的代表性工作。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the NP-state residue survive a longer context (many sentences between prime and target), and does its decay profile match the human lexical-boost vs abstract-priming dissociation?
- Does instruction tuning systematically change the residue or the adaptation? The paper pools base and instruct models, but Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct *moving away* from corrected values suggests training-dependent differences worth isolating.
- Can the NP-state residue be edited away in practice — e.g., can a "retraction token" or targeted KV-cache edit at the noun phrase mimic what deletion of the corrected text would do, without harming other context?
- Why do the strongest models (Llama-3.1-70B, Qwen2.5-7B) show the largest PASS/local-coherence residue — is stronger local coherence computation an inevitable byproduct of scale?
- Does the human prediction hold: does varying only the continuation of a fixed garden-path prefix (holding the abandoned analysis constant) leave later human comprehension unaltered, as residual activation predicts?
- Do chat-tuned models show the same raw-statistic reversal when prompted through their chat templates, where comma conventions may differ from the raw pretraining distribution?
