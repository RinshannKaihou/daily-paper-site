> **一句话总结：** 本文提出 BiPA（二部图归因）测量工具，通过"选择回放"（用与种子完全相同的候选者重放优化器的接受规则）与"回合-上下文二部图"（把每条轨迹的输入 token 拆解到行为来源），系统性地证伪了 GEPA / SkillOpt / SkillAdam 三种通用技能优化器：在 TB2、τ³-bench、AutomationBench 三个基准和两个模型上，学到的技能测试成功率平均只比手写种子高 0.1 分（dev 提升全是小验证集上的"赢家诅咒"假象），却要多读约 16% 的输入 token——机制是编辑器几乎只增规则（91/97 次编辑加长技能，337 条新规则无一要求少读）、agent 把这些规则执行成额外回合、而每个回合都重发全部历史。

> **TL;DR:** A measurement study (not a new optimizer). BiPA dissects three published skill optimizers (GEPA, SkillOpt, SkillAdam) on Terminal-Bench 2.1, τ³-bench and AutomationBench with DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash and GPT-6-Luna in the OpenCode harness. Over all 16 learned skills, test success (avg@3) is only +0.1 points over the hand-written seed (−1.4 points over the nine DeepSeek skills, 95% upper bound +0.6), while reported dev gains of 3–40 points are reproduced by selection alone — replaying each acceptance rule on candidates identical to the seed reaches the reported gains with probability 0.25–0.94. Meanwhile every DeepSeek-learned skill reads 1.08–1.43× the seed's input tokens (mean 1.16×), driven by extra turns that re-send the whole history (turn slopes 1.35–1.58), and neither gains nor skills transfer across model or benchmark while the cost does. Four design principles for future optimizers follow.

## 核心贡献

- **提出 BiPA（Bipartite Attribution）测量工具**：不修改优化器本身，仅从 agent 的执行日志出发，量化技能优化器"到底改变了什么"。它回答两个问题：技能是否真的更成功？额外成本来自哪里？
- **选择回放（Selection Replay）**：把优化器的接受规则在"与种子完全相同的候选者"上重放（任务难度用 Beta 先验+后验建模），测出纯粹由选择偏差（赢家诅咒）就能产生的 dev 提升——证明优化器报告的 dev 增益不构成"编辑有效"的证据。
- **回合-上下文二部图 + 回合弹性 + 四项分解**：把每条轨迹记录为"回合 × 上下文组件（技能文本/系统提示/工具输出）"的二部图，推导出回合弹性 e_R = 1 + s_H·R/(R−1)（回合每多 1%，输入 token 至少多 1%、长轨迹趋近 2%），并把额外输入 token 精确分解为"更多动作 / 回合拆分 / 每回合内容 / 技能文本"四个行为项。
- **系统性的负结果与机制解释**：在 3 个优化器 × 3 个基准 × 2 个模型的 16 个学得技能上，测试成功率平均仅 +0.1 分，dev 增益在测试集上消失；技能多读约 16% 输入 token。一条因果链解释成本：小 dev 集的门槛无法给单条规则记账 → 编辑器只增不删规则 → agent 把规则执行成额外回合 → 每回合重发全部历史。
- **四条设计原则**：仅当增益超过"纯选择报告值"才接受编辑；按增加的回合数（而非技能长度）计费；允许编辑器删除与合并规则；把已有规则的成本展示给编辑器。

## Key Contributions

- **BiPA, a post-hoc measurement instrument** for skill optimizers that requires no change to the optimizer: it attributes test-time behavior change and token cost purely from episode logs.
- **Selection replay**: replays the optimizer's acceptance rule on candidates identical to the seed (task difficulties drawn from a Beta posterior fitted by maximum likelihood to the seed's episodes), yielding P(G′ ≥ G) — the probability that unchanged candidates report a gain at least as large as the one reported. This turns the "winner's curse" objection into a measurable quantity.
- **Bipartite episode graphs, turn elasticity, and the four-term split**: each episode is a bipartite graph of turns and context components; Proposition 1 shows an extra turn re-sends both prefix and history, so input tokens grow with elasticity e_R = 1 + s_H·R/(R−1) (bounded by 2); Eq. (6) splits Δlog input tokens into more actions, turn-splitting, content per turn, and skill text — an accounting identity that names the behavior behind each extra token.
- **A rigorous negative result across the ecosystem**: 3 optimizers (GEPA, SkillOpt, SkillAdam), 3 long-horizon benchmarks (Terminal-Bench 2.1, τ³-bench, AutomationBench), 2 models (DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, GPT-6-Luna), one shared hand-written seed, 16 learned skills — learned skills succeed about as often as the seed, their dev gains are what selection alone reports, they cost ~16% more input tokens, and they do not transfer across model or benchmark while most of their cost does.
- **Four actionable design principles** (gate threshold vs. selection noise; charge per turn not per skill length; let the editor delete/merge; show the editor rule-level costs), each backed by a simulation or regression panel.

## Methodology

BiPA operates on the logs of an optimizer run and answers two questions without touching the optimizer.

**(1) Selection replay (does the dev gain mean anything?).** Each dev task v gets a difficulty p_v, the probability a seed episode solves it, drawn from a Beta(a+s_v, b+n_v−s_v) posterior fitted by MLE to the seed's dev and test episodes (a, b < 1 on all three benchmarks under DeepSeek, i.e., most tasks are solved almost always or almost never). A replay draws every p_v, scores K unchanged candidates exactly as the seed would be scored again, and applies the optimizer's actual acceptance rule (SkillOpt: keep the best if it strictly beats the starting score; GEPA: best-of-K with ties broken for the seed; SkillAdam: pairwise comparison on fresh batches of 10 dev tasks). G′ is the gain the replay reports; P(G′ ≥ G) is estimated from 100k replays per run. A high probability means the reported dev gain is not evidence that any edit helped.

**(2) Bipartite attribution (where do extra tokens come from?).** Each episode is recorded as a bipartite graph between turns r = 1..R and context components C (skill text, rest of the prompt, each tool's outputs), with context edges carrying tokens read and call edges carrying tool calls. Token totals are split over components by content length so the parts sum exactly. Because the harness (OpenCode, no history compaction) re-sends the prefix F and the whole conversation at every turn, an episode of R turns with content-per-turn h reads X = RF + R(R−1)h/2 tokens, giving the turn elasticity e_R = 1 + s_H·R/(R−1) (Proposition 1: e_R > 1 always, → 2 as R grows). The four-term split decomposes a skill's log-token change against the seed into e_R·Δlog N (more actions) − e_R·Δlog m (turn-splitting) + s_H·Δlog h (content per turn) + s_F·Δlog F (skill text); extra turns = the two turn terms.

**Setup.** Terminal-Bench 2.1 (18 test tasks), τ³-bench (75), AutomationBench (120), split 7:1:2 into train/dev/test; OpenCode v1.14.31 without history compaction; DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash and GPT-6-Luna at high reasoning effort; one shared seed skill ("verification-before-completion" from the public Superpowers repo); dev sets of 30 tasks (9 on TB2); every skill including the seed and a no-skill agent run 3× on test (avg@3); cost ratios ρ computed as paired per-task geometric means. Sixteen learned skills total (under Luna on TB2, GEPA and SkillOpt returned the seed unchanged). Rules added by edits are sorted into eight categories by what they ask the agent to do, and matched against recorded behavior.

## Results & Highlights

- **No success gain (RQ1).** Over every test task of all 16 learned skills, test success (avg@3) is **+0.1 points over the hand-written seed**; over the nine DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash skills it is **−1.4 points** (one-sided 95% upper bound +0.6), ranging from **−11.1 points (SkillOpt on TB2: 64.8 vs seed 75.9)** to **+2.7 (SkillOpt on τ³-bench: 79.1 vs 76.4)**. Under GPT-6-Luna, seven skills average +1.6 points; only SkillAdam on AutoBench (+6.4 vs seed 37.2, i.e., 43.6) beats the seed by more than the seed's own run-to-run spread of 5.8 points.
- **Dev gains are selection artifacts (RQ1).** SkillOpt/GEPA under DeepSeek reported dev gains of 3–17 points and SkillAdam 20–40, yet replaying each acceptance rule with **unchanged candidates reaches the reported gains with probability 0.56–0.94** (0.21–0.99 under Luna; SkillAdam 0.25–0.88). Test does not follow dev: across nine returned skills the dev-gain → test-difference regression has **slope 0.02 and correlation 0.05**; along the τ³-bench skill paths the dev gain climbs 6.7 points while the test difference stays within 2.7 points of its starting value.
- **Every learned skill costs more (RQ2).** All nine DeepSeek skills read **1.08–1.43× the seed's input tokens per task** (mean ρ = 1.16 over all sixteen); for eight of nine, every test run reads more tokens than *any* run of the seed, and the increase holds at 1.08–1.29× even on tasks both skills solve in all three runs. On one AutoBench task, SkillOpt's skill passes like the seed but reads 1.42× its tokens (575k vs 406k) through 1.92× the turns.
- **The mechanism: rules → extra turns → re-sent history (RQ2/RQ3).** 91 of 97 scored edits lengthen the skill; a skill gains **37 rules on average (up to 105)**; of 337 rules added under DeepSeek, **none asks the agent to read less**, about half ask it to verify/re-check, and 18 serialize actions. The agent obeys: on AutoBench, 56% of the seed's state-changing writes share a turn with another write versus **≤0.3% under each learned skill** (writes go one-per-turn). Extra turns carry **73% of the summed log-token increase** (skill text only 21%), and within-task regressions give turn slopes of **1.35–1.58** (10% more turns → ~14–16% more input tokens), matching Proposition 1.
- **No transfer; the cost transfers (RQ2).** Run cross-model or cross-benchmark, only 1 of 9 skills is above the seed, by 1.9 points; under Luna, DeepSeek's τ³-bench skills score 3.6–6.2 points below the seed, while reading 1.08–1.38× its tokens.
- **Fixing the gate works in simulation.** Requiring a dev win to repeat on a second dev run accepts **6.4–17.1% of unchanged candidates** (vs 24–41% for the one-win rule the optimizers use) while keeping **94/55/62% of true two-task gains** on TB2/τ³-bench/AutoBench. And charging by turns, not length: an edit's change in turns explains **93%** of the variance in its token change; skill length explains 10%.
- **Three-word seed control (Appendix C.5).** Starting GPT-6-Luna from "Solve the task." on AutoBench, SkillOpt/GEPA raise test success from 25.0% to 43.3%/44.4% (+18.3/+19.4 points) — but only to the level the same optimizers reach from the hand-written seed (42.8%/41.4%, seed itself 37.2%), and on τ³-bench the three-word seed already scores 65.3–68.0% so optimization adds nothing (returned skills 60.0%/68.0%).

## Authors & Credibility

The work comes from Shanghai AI Laboratory with affiliations spanning Fudan University, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, USTC and Tsinghua (Youbang Sun and Chaochao Lu as corresponding authors). The experimental design is unusually careful for a negative-result paper: published optimizer code is run as released; all three optimizers share one seed, one sample of 160 training episodes and one dev set so their edits can be compared line by line; statistics are done properly (Beta-posterior task models, 100k-replay estimates, paired per-task cost ratios, paired bootstrap and cluster bootstrap confidence intervals, run-to-run spreads reported for the seed itself). Code is released at https://github.com/valleysprings/BiPA.git. The paper builds honestly on prior skepticism (Kapoor et al.'s agent-evaluation methodology, Miller's error bars, the optimizer's-curse literature, and 2026 controlled studies finding skill/harness gains sparse), and extends it from "does success improve?" to "what did the skill actually change?".

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: High on method, not on message. The winner's-curse critique of best-of-K selection on small dev sets is well-trodden (Dwork et al. 2015; Smith & Winkler 2006; Kapoor et al. 2025), and 2026 papers already found skill/harness gains sparse — the authors cite them. The genuinely new parts are the *quantified* selection replay (P(G′≥G) per run), the bipartite turn-component attribution, and the turn-elasticity proposition with its four-term split; these are reusable instruments beyond skill optimization.
- **Rigor**: Strong. Paired task-level comparisons, CIs, seed run-to-run spreads as natural noise bands, a three-word-seed control, transfer tests, and simulations backing each design principle. One important honest anomaly is acknowledged: on GPT-6-Luna + AutoBench the learned skills DO gain on test (+4.2 to +6.4) even though their dev scores barely moved — which cuts against a pure selection-noise story and is only partially explained.
- **Reproducibility**: Good — released code, named harness version (OpenCode v1.14.31), fixed splits, one shared seed from a public repo, and appendix-level acceptance-rule specifications. Caveat: GPT-6-Luna and the "updated release" of DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash are moving targets, and the τ³-bench path analysis (Fig. 9) mixes model releases, which the authors flag but which limits exact replication.
- **Significance**: This is a "reality check" paper for the skill-optimizer subfield; its four principles are concrete and testable. But it evaluates a specific regime — long-horizon agentic tasks with a no-compaction harness and success-only gates — and the optimizers under test were mostly validated on shorter/cheaper tasks, so it is partly a mismatch-of-regime result rather than proof the methods are useless everywhere.
- **Red flags / weak points to keep in mind**: (1) The turn elasticity and the 16% cost inflation are partly artifacts of running OpenCode *without history compaction*; providers also bill cached history at a discount, which the appendix re-billing only partially addresses — the "cost" claim is weaker under realistic caching. (2) All main runs start from ONE hand-written seed that already encodes verification-before-completion; the finding "skills sharpen, don't expand" may be seed-dependent (the three-word-seed control helps but is single-run). (3) TB2 has only 18 test tasks (17 for Luna), so per-benchmark point differences there are fragile. (4) The rule→behavior causal link (e.g., serialized writes causing turn-splitting) is correlational; the authors admit controlled single-rule rewrites are needed. (5) GEPA's dev-set usage here (30 tasks) is far smaller than its original reflective-evolution setting, which disadvantages it relative to its published claims.

## Strengths

- Converts a vague methodological worry ("dev gains might be noise") into a precise, replayable measurement with per-run probabilities, and does the same for cost via an exact accounting decomposition rather than average token counts.
- The bipartite graph + four-term split is a reusable cost-attribution tool for any agent harness, independent of the skill-optimizer debate.
- The causal chain (no rule-level credit → rule accumulation → extra turns → re-sent history) is traced end-to-end with evidence at each link, including behavior-level confirmation (56% → ≤0.3% shared-turn writes).
- Design principles are each backed by a quantitative panel (simulation of four acceptance rules; 93% vs 10% variance explained; the 1-of-59 shortening edits; 73%/76% of kept/rejected edits adding tokens).
- Honest about its own exceptions (Luna/AutoBench test gains, the one transferring skill) and about the instrument's assumptions.

## Limitations

- **Post-hoc only**: BiPA measures what changed after the fact; it cannot predict which rule will add turns before running, and it cannot decompose success (that would need a value function over unobservable model states behind a black-box API).
- **Regime-bound**: turn elasticity is derived for harnesses that re-send uncompacted history (as OpenCode does here); for compacting or cache-discounting harnesses the elasticity must be re-measured, and the headline ~16% cost inflation shrinks (to 1.05–1.36×) under a last-turn-only re-billing.
- **Coverage**: three optimizers, one shared seed, one harness, two models, three benchmarks; single runs for the three-word-seed control and the transfer tests.
- **Statistical assumptions**: replay assumes episodes are independent given task difficulty and that difficulties are exchangeable Beta draws — domains within a benchmark differ in difficulty, and the SkillAdam-under-Luna replay substitutes training tasks for dev tasks.
- **No positive method**: the paper diagnoses and proposes principles but does not build or validate an optimizer that satisfies them end-to-end.

## Relevance

这项工作直接针对用户关注的"LLM agent 自我改进/技能学习是否真实有效"的评估问题：它给出了可复用的工具（选择回放 + 二部图成本归因）来审计任何 prompt/技能优化器的增益是否为统计假象、以及隐性 token 成本来自哪里，对构建长程 agent 系统和评估方法学都极具参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would the four design principles, implemented end-to-end in one optimizer (repeated-win gate + turn-charging + rule deletion + rule-level cost feedback), produce skills that genuinely beat a strong hand-written seed on test?
- How do the findings change under harnesses that compact history or under provider cache pricing — does the cost advantage of the seed shrink to irrelevance, or do extra turns remain expensive via output tokens and latency?
- Can rule→behavior causality be established with controlled single-rule rewrites (add/delete one serialization rule at a time), closing the correlational gap the authors acknowledge?
- Why do GPT-6-Luna skills gain +4.2 to +6.4 points on AutomationBench test while their dev scores hardly move — is there a real editing effect that dev-set selection cannot see, and could a coverage-oriented reward (tasks solved in at least one of k episodes) capture it systematically?
- Does the selection-replay technique transfer to harness evolution and model-selection loops (best-of-K checkpoint picking), where the same winner's-curse structure appears with even smaller holdouts?
