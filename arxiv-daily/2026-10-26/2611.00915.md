> **一句话总结：** 提出 Coffee——一个面向长上下文 LLM 推理的"弹性精度"软硬件协同设计架构：用 GEMM 双侧当前输入的统计量在线估计各候选位宽下的输出误差，在离线分配的平均 4-bit 预算内贪心选择每个子阵列两侧输入位宽，配合嵌套位平面存储与注意力引导的 KV 读取，在 Llama-3-8B 上将 WikiText-2 困惑度降到 6.58（均匀 INT4 的 PLENA 为 6.95）、RULER 多针检索平均分 70.9% vs PLENA 57.8%，系统建模显示长上下文解码吞吐最高达 PLENA 的 2.55×、4×B200 的 4.96×，而在线精度决策电路仅占核面积 1.97%、引入 <0.01% 的 prefill 时间开销。

> **TL;DR:** Coffee is a runtime mixed-precision accelerator (built on PLENA's flattened systolic array, 16×2048 core of 128 subarrays with independently configurable per-subarray input widths) that estimates GEMM output error from BOTH current input matrices and greedily allocates one-bit width upgrades under an offline-assigned average 4-bit budget, with attention-guided nested bit-plane KV reads for long-context decode. Under the same ~4-bit average budget it beats uniform-INT4 PLENA and W4A4KV4 BDQ on quality — e.g., WikiText-2 perplexity 6.58 vs PLENA's 6.95 on Llama-3-8B (FP16: 6.14), six-task zero-shot mean 72.23% vs 69.63% (+2.60 pts), and RULER multi-needle average 70.9% vs 57.8% (+13.1 pts) — while modeled 288-chip systems project up to 2.55× PLENA's decode throughput and 12.56× a 4×B200 NVFP4 baseline's energy efficiency at 90K context. Caveats: accelerator numbers are ASAP7 synthesis + simulation (not silicon), short-context energy efficiency is 5–9% WORSE than PLENA, and the quality-baseline set is only BDQ/PLENA/FP16.

## 核心贡献

- **双输入驱动的运行时精度分配**：首次（在该文设定下）同时利用当前 GEMM tile 的两个输入矩阵来估计各候选位宽下的输出误差（可分离误差代理 R ≈ P_B·ε_A(w_A) + P_A·ε_B(w_B)），并据此在线选择每个子阵列两侧输入的位宽——动机实验表明固定 INT4 权重下，不同 token 激活导致的输出 NMSE 跨 token 的 p99/p1 差异巨大，单侧自适应精度（FGMP、MoBiQuant 等）错过了另一侧输入的影响。
- **低开销在线误差估计**：用 amax² 与按算子类型校准的系数 κ_op 估计均方幅度（P ≈ κ_op·amax²），用解析式 Δw²/12（截断于 P、乘校准系数 c_op,w）估计各候选位宽的重建 MSE，避免了逐元素平方累加和重复量化；贪心的边际增益一位升级启发式在总预算 B_s = M·w̄_s 内求解宽度选择。
- **注意力引导的 KV 精度分配**：K 读取用上一个 query 缓存的 softmax 概率、V 读取用当前注意力概率加权重建误差来分配读取位宽，并支持 V 跳过（skipping，不做概率重归一化）；V 跳过与混合精度 KV 读取分别贡献约 55% 与 45% 的 KV 流量削减。
- **离线 Fisher 预算规划**：以 BRECQ 式对角经验 Fisher 估计量化损伤，归一到 NLL 尺度，通过 Lagrangian 松弛（对 λ 二分）在统一平均 4-bit 预算下联合搜索线性算子、Q/P、K/V 配置。
- **混合精度硬件支撑**：在 PLENA 扁平脉动阵列上加入精度选择单元（PSU）、Hadamard 变换单元（HTU）、按 bank 独立量化的 VRAM/MRAM、嵌套 MXINT 位平面存储（一次 INT5 主编码支持 INT2–INT5 前缀读取 + 中点重建补偿项由行首 PE 累加器统一计算）、位平面时钟门控与 3:2 进位保存压缩树——在线精度决策仅占核面积 1.97%、prefill 时间开销 <0.01%。
- **系统级收益**：长上下文解码吞吐与能效建模最高达 PLENA（重构版）的 2.55× / 2.61×、4×B200 NVFP4 的 4.96× / 12.56×。

## Key Contributions

- **Both-input runtime precision allocation.** An output-error proxy derived from ΔC = AΔB + ΔA·B + ΔAΔB shows the error from quantizing either GEMM input depends on the other input; Coffee is the first design to use both current input matrices to select both input widths online (within offline-assigned per-tile budgets), unlike FGMP/OPAL (activation-only) and MoBiQuant (weight-only).
- **Cheap online statistics.** Mean-square magnitudes estimated as κ_op·amax² (per-operator calibration coefficient) and reconstruction MSEs as min(Δw²/12·c_op,w, P), stored alongside data at INT6, replace elementwise squaring/accumulation and repeated quantization; a greedy largest-marginal-gain one-bit-upgrade loop allocates widths under budget.
- **Attention-guided KV reads.** K read widths ranked by buffered previous-query softmax probabilities weighted by query-group energy; V widths and V-skipping ranked by current attention probabilities; skipping omits P/V-port ops without renormalizing attention.
- **Offline Fisher budget planning.** Diagonal empirical-Fisher damage estimates (BRECQ-style) normalized to NLL scale, jointly searched over linear/Q/P/K/V configurations via Lagrangian relaxation with bisection on λ, under a unified average 4-bit budget across W/A/Q/P/K/V.
- **Mixed-precision hardware.** Per-subarray independently configurable input widths on a 16×2048 flattened systolic array (128 subarrays, Q5.3 shared exponents per 16-value group matched to subarray reduction length), Precision Selection Unit overlapping decisions with transfers, nested bit-plane storage with midpoint-reconstruction compensation, bit-plane clock gating, scale-aligned carry-save reduction trees; estimator + PSU = 1.97% of core area, <0.01% prefill time, +0.31–0.64% on the first decode step.
- **Joint weight encoding.** Shared exponents and master codes optimized offline against input Gram matrices H = E[xxᵀ] weighted by measured width-usage probabilities, so one nested encoding serves all read widths near the quality of separately quantized per-width weights.

## Methodology

The framework combines offline preparation with online execution:

1. **Error proxy (Sec 3).** For subarray i with inputs A_i, B_i and quantization errors ΔA_i, ΔB_i, the output error contains cross terms A_iΔB_i + ΔB_iΔA_i; assuming zero-mean independent quantization noise, a separable proxy R_i = P_i^B·ε_i^A(w_A) + P_i^A·ε_i^B(w_B) results, where each side's contribution depends only on its own width given the current input pair — enabling per-side greedy allocation.
2. **Online estimation (Sec 4.1).** Group mean-square magnitude P ≈ κ_op·amax²; group MSE at width w via uniform-rounding-noise approximation ε(w) ≈ Δw²/12 capped at P with a fitted coefficient c_op,w (Δw doubles per bit since nested codes share one exponent). Width selection: start all subarray inputs at w_min, repeatedly apply the largest-gain one-bit upgrade g_i,w→w+1 = r_i(w) − r_i(w+1) until the total budget B_s = M·w̄_s is exhausted.
3. **Attention-guided KV allocation (Sec 4.2).** K damage uses previous-query softmax probabilities p̂ weighted by query-group energy Û (reused across tokens/widths); V damage uses current probabilities; recent-token and outlier subarrays are protected at prescribed widths; lowest-skip-damage V subarrays are skipped entirely (no renormalization), then Q/P widths are chosen per Sec 4.1.
4. **Offline budgets (Sec 4.3).** Prefill and decode calibrated separately at each context length; damage D̂ from diagonal empirical Fisher; decode budget couples to executed port count C(π) ≤ w̄·N_exec(π); Lagrangian J_λ = D̂ + λ(C − w̄N_exec) searched jointly, λ found by bisection.
5. **Hardware (Sec 5).** ASAP7-synthesized SystemVerilog at 1 GHz. 16×2048 array = 128 16×16 subarrays along the reduction dimension; matched Hadamard rotations (HTU) spread outliers; adaptive shared-exponent selection via offline-calibrated LUT on two-largest-values features; nested MXINT stores INT5 masters in bit planes, a width-w read fetches only top-w planes + exponent, with midpoint reconstruction q̂_w = 2^d·q_hi + (2^d−1)/2 realized as a shared correction term ∑_j a_j computed by the first PE of each row; bit-plane clock gating and offset-binary with aggregate bias correction cut switching power; cross-subarray partial sums scale-aligned (shift + table-selected shift-add for fractional scales) through a 3:2 carry-save tree.
6. **Evaluation.** Quality: WikiText-2 (2048-token windows), C4 (256 windows), six zero-shot tasks via lm-evaluation-harness, RULER single/multi-needle at 32K/64K/128K (300 samples/length) vs BDQ, PLENA, FP16. System: Coffee/PLENA modeled as 288-accelerator systems matched to 4×B200's multiplier count and 32 TB/s aggregate HBM bandwidth; B200 measured with vLLM 0.31.0, NVFP4, 4-way TP; PG-19 decode workloads at maximum memory-feasible batch sizes.

## Results & Highlights

- **Perplexity/accuracy (Table 1, Llama-3-8B, avg-4-bit budget):** Coffee reaches WikiText-2 PPL 6.58 and C4 PPL 10.22, vs PLENA uniform-INT4 6.95/11.04 and BDQ W4A4KV4 6.84/10.97 (FP16: 6.14/9.45); six-task zero-shot mean accuracy 72.23% vs PLENA 69.63% (+2.60 pts) and BDQ 71.46% (+0.77 pts; FP16 73.23%).
- **Scaling to 70B (Table 1, Llama-3-70B):** WikiText-2 PPL 3.34 vs PLENA 4.12 (−0.78; BDQ 3.52, FP16 2.86); zero-shot mean 79.23% vs PLENA 74.37% (+4.86 pts), within 0.72 pts of BDQ's 79.13→79.23 and only 0.72 below FP16 (79.95). On Qwen3.8-Flash the mean is 75.30%, just 0.96 pts below FP16 (76.26%) while PLENA sits at 74.33%.
- **Long-context retrieval (Table 2, RULER, Llama-3.1-8B):** multi-needle average 70.9% vs PLENA 57.8% (+13.1 pts; FP16 74.0), with the 128K multi-needle score at 38.8 vs PLENA's 24.5; on Qwen3-8B Coffee's multi-needle average (96.8%) exactly matches FP16 and beats PLENA's 90.9% by 5.9 pts.
- **Decode system performance (Table 3):** at (114K input, 5K output) on Llama-3.1-8B, 17,960 tok/s vs PLENA 7,030 (2.55×) and 4×B200 NVFP4 3,913 (4.59×); energy efficiency 12.16 tok/J vs PLENA 4.66 (2.61×); at (90K, 8K) 14.70 tok/J vs B200's 1.17 (12.56×), the headline energy figure. Across GLM-4.6V/Qwen3-235B-A22B/Llama-4-Scout, long-context throughput gains vs B200 range 3.6–4.96×.
- **Online-decision overhead (Table 4):** error estimator + PSU occupy 1.97% of core area and 0.49–0.61% of prefill energy; prefill time changes <0.01% and the first decode step slows only 0.31–0.64% across 8K–90K contexts.
- **Precision–area co-design (Fig 7):** with INT5 as w_max, online allocation cuts output NMSE by 32.9% (prefill) and 37.2% (decode) vs the uniform-INT4 design at the same average budget, for 26.6% more compute-core area; INT5 captures 88.5%/81.0% of INT7's NMSE reduction while INT7 costs +98.9% area.
- **Motivating proxy study (Fig 3):** proxy-guided INT3/4/5 allocation cuts MAC-weighted output NMSE by 15.2% at an average 4.0 bits on Llama-3-8B, beating outlier-retention policies that keep the top-1%/3% subarray inputs in FP16 (5.6%/11.7% reduction at higher average widths of 4.12/4.36 bits).
- **Ablations (Fig 8):** on Llama-3-8B WikiText-2, online error-guided allocation lowers PPL from 7.369 to 6.868 (≈ rotation alone, 6.871); adding rotation + adaptive LUT scales reaches 6.5805. Nested encoding: naive truncation gives PPL 12,946, midpoint/center compensation 6.662, Gram+usage-aware joint optimization 6.5805 — within 0.013 of separately quantized weights (6.593). Nested reads + V skipping shorten 128K-context KV read time by 57% vs non-nested INT4 (nested reads alone hit ~80% of peak bandwidth vs 90% non-nested).
- **Honest short-context result:** at (1.4K, 0.2K) throughput is only on par with PLENA (153,411 vs 153,060 tok/s) and energy efficiency is ~5–9% lower, because INT5 masters + metadata shrink feasible batch sizes (119 vs 147) before KV savings matter.

## Authors & Credibility

Boyang Chen, Xiao Wu, and Yiren Zhao are at Imperial College London; Haoran Wu is at the University of Cambridge. The group has credible prior presence in efficient-LLM-inference hardware (the paper builds directly on the PLENA flattened systolic array, Wu et al. 2025, and cites the ISCA/MICRO/HPCA-lineage quantization literature: ANT, M-ANT, GPTQ, QuaRot, FlatQuant, KIVI, ZipCache). This is an arXiv preprint (cs.AR, 31 Oct 2026, v1) — not yet peer-reviewed. The evaluation is unusually thorough for an architecture paper: five model families (Llama-3-8B/70B, Llama-3.1, Llama-4-Scout MoE, Qwen3.8-Flash, Qwen3-30B-A3B/Qwen3-235B-A22B MoE, GLM-4.6V vision-language), real B200/vLLM measurements for the GPU baseline, ASAP7 synthesis at 1 GHz with VCD-based power, and component-level ablations (Fig 8) that attribute gains to each mechanism.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The core idea — selecting BOTH GEMM input widths online from both current inputs' statistics, on a subarray-granular mixed-precision array — is a genuine increment over FGMP (activation-only online) and MoBiQuant (weight-only per token). However, the accelerator itself is PLENA's flattened systolic array plus support logic; the software stack recombines known pieces (BRECQ-style Fisher budgets, Hadamard rotation à la QuaRot, salience-weighted KV quantization à la MiKV/ZipCache). The novelty is in the integration and the both-input error proxy, not any single component.
- **Rigor:** Quality comparisons are limited to three baselines (BDQ, PLENA, FP16). The discussed online mixed-precision competitors (FGMP, OPAL, MoBiQuant, CMPQ, APTQ) never appear in Tables 1–2, so the claim of superiority over "runtime" methods rests on the proxy study (Fig 3), which compares only against strawman outlier-retention policies, not against those systems under the same budget. Also, BDQ runs FP16 Q/P while Coffee quantizes Q/P at ≤4-bit average — favorable framing for BDQ, but PLENA's uniform INT4 is a weak-ish quality baseline given modern W4A4 techniques.
- **Reproducibility:** Mixed. The algorithmic pipeline is described in reproducible detail (equations for estimation, greedy allocation, Lagrangian planning), but no code or RTL release is mentioned; the system results depend on an in-house 288-accelerator simulator and a PLENA "reimplementation" whose fidelity cannot be checked externally. The offline pipeline is heavy: per-operator-type calibration coefficients, per-context-length Fisher calibration, Gram matrices, and width-usage probabilities must all be collected before deployment.
- **Significance:** The projected 2.55×/12.56× gains over PLENA/B200 are attention-grabbing but are simulator projections for a chip that does not exist, against a GPU baseline running NVFP4 through vLLM — a software stack not tuned for the accelerator's tricks (nested KV reads, V skipping). The B200 comparison is measured, but the matching (equal multiplier count, equal 32 TB/s HBM bandwidth, equal KV capacity) embeds modeling choices.
- **Red flags / honest weaknesses (some admitted by the authors):** (1) short-context energy efficiency is 5–9% WORSE than PLENA — the design only pays off for long contexts; (2) the ablation shows rotation alone achieves nearly the same PPL gain (6.871) as the novel online allocation (6.868), i.e., the headline mechanism's standalone contribution equals a known orthogonal trick, with synergy only in combination (6.5805); (3) perplexity is evaluated only on 2048-token windows while the system targets 128K contexts — long-context quality evidence is confined to RULER needle retrieval on three models; (4) per-task regressions exist, e.g., ARC-C on GLM-4.6V (57.94 vs PLENA 59.81 and FP16 60.24) and on Qwen3.8-Flash (60.67 vs PLENA 61.01) — the average hides these; (5) V skipping drops attention probabilities without renormalization, a numerically risky choice evaluated only on retrieval tasks; (6) Fig 2's toy 2×2-subarray example (79.7% error reduction) is an illustrative cherry-pick that sets expectations the full-system NMSE numbers (32.9%/37.2%) do not match.

## Strengths

- Principled, low-cost error proxy grounded in the exact GEMM error expansion, with the cross-term and separability assumptions stated explicitly and tested in Fig 8(c) (both-input estimates beat own-magnitude-only and offline-average variants, and nearly match directly computed statistics).
- The overhead story is the paper's best card: 1.97% area, <0.01% prefill time, ≤0.64% first-decode-step slowdown — the usual killer objection to runtime precision selection is quantified away.
- Nested bit-plane encoding with reconstruction compensation is elegant: one INT5 master encoding serves all read widths within 0.013 PPL of per-width independent quantization, and prefix reads turn width into bandwidth savings.
- Evaluation breadth: dense, MoE (Llama-4-Scout, Qwen3-235B-A22B), and vision-language (GLM-4.6V) models; measured 4×B200 baseline; complete ablation of rotation/LUT/online-allocation/nested-encoding contributions.
- Honest reporting of the short-context regression and of nested reads' bandwidth-utilization penalty (~80% vs 90% of peak).

## Limitations

- All Coffee/PLENA system results are ASAP7-synthesis + simulator projections; no silicon, no public RTL or simulator, so the 2.55×/12.56× figures cannot be independently verified.
- Narrow quality-baseline set (BDQ/PLENA/FP16); no comparison against FGMP, MoBiQuant, or modern W4A4 rotation methods at matched budgets.
- Perplexity windows are 2048 tokens; no long-context perplexity or generation-quality evals beyond RULER retrieval.
- Deployment requires per-context-length calibration (prefill and decode calibrated separately at each length) and per-operator-type coefficient fitting — a real operational burden for serving heterogeneous workloads.
- INT5 masters increase weight/KV memory vs INT4 systems, reducing memory-feasible batch sizes at short context (e.g., 119 vs 147), which is why short-context energy efficiency trails PLENA by 5–9%.
- Requires custom hardware; none of the mechanism (subarray-granular dual-width control, nested reads) is realizable on current GPU tensor-core formats.

## Relevance

直接命中"极限性能优化下的可靠性"方向：本文核心议题就是低比特/混合精度量化引入的误差如何在运行时被建模、估计与控制（双侧输入的误差代理、逐子阵列位宽分配），并量化了精度选择对困惑度与长上下文检索准确性的影响——即激进的低精度优化与其质量代价之间的系统权衡。同时其脉动阵列、HBM 流量与能效建模也对"大规模 ML 计算基础设施"方向有参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of the both-input online-allocation gain survives when the baseline is a strong rotation-based W4A4 method (QuaRot/FlatQuant + FGMP) at the same 4-bit average budget, rather than uniform INT4 PLENA?
- The width-usage probabilities p_w used in weight encoding are measured during mixed-precision calibration — how sensitive is the joint Gram+usage optimization to distribution shift between calibration data and deployment traffic?
- V skipping drops attention probabilities without renormalization; what happens on generation tasks with high entropy (creative writing, code) or with KV-heavy workloads like multi-document QA, beyond RULER needles?
- Could the error-estimation statistics (amax-based P, Δw²/12 MSE) be reused for online SDC/fault detection, e.g., flagging when observed reconstruction error deviates from the calibrated model?
- What is the sensitivity of the offline Fisher budget plan to context length mismatch — can a plan calibrated at 64K serve 128K inputs, or must every serving context length carry its own calibration?
- The 12.56× energy claim vs B200 rests on NVML board energy at batch sizes capped by GPU memory; how does the comparison shift if the GPU baseline uses KV-cache quantization (e.g., KIVI-style INT4 KV) to raise its feasible batch size?
