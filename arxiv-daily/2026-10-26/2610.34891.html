Weijia Han · Lisha Qu (equal contribution, University of Washington) | NeurIPS 2026 Workshop — Foundation Models for Temporal Systems | 2026-10-26

Why this matters

Any table-based latent-state model that stores states during training but cold-starts at inference should run a checkpoint-matching control before claiming state inconsistency.

任何采用「训练期存储状态表 + 推理期冷启动重构」设计的模型，都应先做同样的检查点匹配对照。

Critical edge

Both runs behind the headline fall outside the paper’s own pre-registered ±20% reproduction band (+35.7% / +27.3% decoded-state MSE) — whether checkpoint reload still dominates the gap at the published error level is unknown.

01 Methodology

Validation passθ_last writes the per-sequence state table in parallel
→
Early stoppingreloads best-validation θ_best for all inference
→
Cold-start rolloutunder θ_best ≠ stored table → apparent “state gap”
⇒
Rescue: save θ_lastpaired rollout under the table-writing weights ≈ table (position-1 W1 ≈ 6e-6)

Controlled reproduction on Toy1D: 10,000 sequences × 1,000 steps from a damped linear system with a 3-D latent state, seen through a noisy 2-channel linear readout. Model: 128-dim tanh-bounded states, 200-position context, 4 attention heads, ALiBi, REPLACE state convention; AdamW, 1,000 epochs; fixed 1,600-item analysis split. Distances: pooled and position-resolved Wasserstein-1, paired per-item MSE/RMS, plus an OLS linear probe (state → decoded coordinate) as safeguard.

Two repairs were pre-registered against a ≥50% W1-cut bar (probe R² within 0.01): (a) distilling stored states into a causal TCN encoder (receptive fields 15–127); (b) K ∈ {0,1,3,5,10} frozen SD updates from four initializations. Thresholds, primary comparisons, and decision rules were fixed before output inspection (Appendix A.2).

Concept note (Observation 1): SD’s “E-step” is not EM — no likelihood pθ(o,w), no candidate scoring, no arg max — so EM’s monotone-improvement guarantee does not transfer; it is closer to direct latent-code optimization.

  • The gap lives at early positions. Position-1 W1 is 0.103/0.113 under θ_best but ≈6e-6 (numerical precision) under θ_last.
  • The matched-checkpoint residual is real but unexplained — >50,000× the numerical floor (~1.5e-7); every tested table-free repair fails.
  • Reproduction honestly imperfect: all four runs land +2.0–35.7% above the published MSE 0.34348 (±20% band); the two paired-analysis runs miss by 35.7% and 27.3%.

03 Strengths

  • Crisp causal design — saving θ_last before reload turns “which checkpoint?” from confound into the main independent variable, with a built-in validity check.
  • Pre-registration discipline rare for a workshop paper: thresholds, primary comparisons, and decision rules fixed before inspection — null results reported faithfully.
  • Three granularities of measurement (pooled W1, position-resolved W1, paired MSE/RMS) plus a probe safeguard.
  • Observation 1 stands alone: a useful EM-critique for anyone reading SD’s fixed-point iteration as maximum likelihood.
  • Unusually specific limitations disclosed (mixed-checkpoint sweep, two-seed scope, missing downstream costs).

04 Limitations

  • Two seeds, one synthetic benchmark (Toy1D); no real-data or control-task evidence.
  • Both paired runs sit outside the ±20% band — checkpoint separation at the published MSE (0.34348) is untested.
  • The RF sweep is mixed-checkpoint on a single seed-42 run without saved θ_last, so its absolute numbers are not checkpoint-controlled.
  • No downstream cost measured — only distributional (W1) and paired (MSE/RMS) state distances; whether the residual 10% matters for any task is open.
  • The residual mechanism is open — eliminated by neither distillation nor refinement, and not explained.
  • Seed-7 repetitions used different PyTorch/CUDA builds, confounding seed variance with software-version effects.

05 Questions for further exploration

  1. Does reload still dominate the gap at the published reproduction error (MSE 0.34348) rather than the +27–36% levels of the analyzed runs?
  2. What mechanism explains the residual ~10% shared-checkpoint gap (50,000× above the numerical floor) — optimizer noise, REPLACE convention, window boundaries?
  3. Does the state gap translate into downstream costs — worse prediction, planning, or control?
  4. Do the findings transfer beyond Toy1D — to other World Machine benchmarks, real time series, and other table-plus-cold-start architectures?
  5. Should practice be “evaluate with θ_last” (or re-write the table after reload) — and would that change the published World Machine results?
  6. Can the SD update be given a real probabilistic interpretation (candidate scoring, explicit likelihood) so EM-style guarantees apply?
