Key metric — first-layer bias span

≈2.3×

0.084 → 0.194 integrated span, Muon vs AdamW backbone (55M model, 1B tokens) — doubled at every seed; the other six MoE layers shift by only ~0.02.

Early overload, steps 501–1,262: busiest expert at 4–7× fair share (theoretical ceiling 8×).

Why this matters

Labs split — with zero published data — on whether MoE routers should follow the backbone onto Muon. This gives cheap diagnostics and two fixes.

Critical edge

Part of the headline doubling may be a learning-rate artifact: Muon’s rate was tuned on one short single-seed run — half the rate erases ~3/4 of the rise and trains better.

This paper’s answer, at 55M–108M

Keep the router on Muon. Moving only the router to AdamW raised warmup churn and validation loss at every seed — every loss spike in the large-model runs came from that config.

MMethodology — factorial, not a whole-model switch

Two DeepSeekMoE-style decoder-only LMs: 55M active / 303M total and 108M active / 496M total — 32 routed experts, top-4 sigmoid router with per-expert score biases, one shared expert. FineWeb-Edu, 1,024-token sequences, 65,536 tokens/step, auxiliary-loss-free bias balancer at DeepSeek-V3’s rate u = 0.001.

Every parameter group (routers, routed/shared experts, attention, dense FFN, embed/head) is independently set to AdamW or Muon — a “partition” — reproducing the all-AdamW, all-Muon, and Muon-with-router-on-AdamW recipes, plus partial-backbone ablations and an update-size-matched no-orthogonalization control. Learning rates tuned per optimizer.

3 seeds per comparison, paired by seed (same init, same batches); planned comparisons pre-registered with expected signs. 3,000-step probes change exactly one thing or add instrumentation. Per-layer metrics: bias span, input cosine, score offset, overload, churn, update–load correlation.

¶Mechanism — why layer 1, why Muon

01 · SETUP

Muon updates the whole backbone (AdamW doesn’t).

02 · OBSERVE

First router’s inputs align: cosine ≈0.40 (AdamW: 0.07).

03 · MECHANISM

Batch-mean input pushes every token toward the same expert.

04 · MECHANISM

Bias corrects at u = 0.001/step — hundreds of steps behind.

05 · EFFECT

Busiest expert: 4–7× fair share right after warmup.

06 · FIX

Center the input (−batch mean): overload → 1.11–1.16, Δloss < 0.003.

+Strengths

  • Real, unsettled question — frontier labs split on it with zero published comparative data; answered with a clean router × backbone factorial, not a whole-model switch.
  • The span metric is a clever observable — the bias equalizes loads and hides the problem; measuring how hard the balancer works exposes it.
  • Causality, not correlation — one-variable 3,000-step probes; span tracks the score offset (93% of log-variance across 27 runs).
  • Exceptional transparency — pre-registered predictions (two failed — both reported), public 213-test code package, run logs, claims ledger, dataset.
  • Cheap takeaways — watch layer 1, keep the router on Muon, center router inputs or speed up the bias.

−Limitations

  • Scale gap — 50–2,000× smaller active params than the Qwen3.8-class models that motivate the question; ≤3B vs trillions of tokens. Qwen’s balancer also differs (softmax + balance loss, no bias), so the verdict may not transfer.
  • Learning-rate confound — rates tuned on short single-seed runs and never retuned at full length; the span grows monotonically with Muon’s LR.
  • 3 seeds, no multiplicity correction — and several probe claims rest on a single seed.
  • Centering fix impractical as tested — 3,000 steps, one layer; batch mean makes routing batch-dependent; running-mean variant untested.
  • Validation loss only — the overload had no measured cost here (no capacity limits, single device); severity is inferred.

?Questions for further exploration

1Does the first-layer span rise persist — or shrink — at 1–10B active params, hundreds of billions of tokens, or under a softmax router without sigmoid’s bounded scores?
2Would a running-mean (batch-independent) centering preserve the overload removal over full training — and should it cover all MoE layers, not just the first?
3What spreads the router rows along the shared direction — uneven loads feeding gradients through routed tokens, as hypothesized but not tested?
4Why does only the full backbone on Muon create the shared direction, when attention+dense-FFN Muon — which computes the entire first router input — stays at cosine 0.054?
5Can the contradiction with Qwen3.8-Flash-Next be reconciled — e.g. its balance-loss (no bias) balancer, or softmax-bfloat16 roundoff that sigmoid single-precision routers avoid?
