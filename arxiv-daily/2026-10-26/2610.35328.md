> **一句话总结：** 本文将约束解码拆解为格式约束（FC）、有效性约束（VC）与推理约束（RC）三类，理论上证明在提示词与期望输出格式对齐时解码器级掩码不会损害性能，并在 11 个开源模型（Qwen3/Qwen3.5、Llama3、Gemma2、SmolLM3，1.7B–32B）与 GSM8K/LastLetter(Extended)/AIME 2026 上通过因果消融实验表明：此前观测到的性能下降主要来自不等的提示词与输出格式而非掩码本身；对齐条件下约束解码一致地追平或超过无约束解码（如 Llama3-8B 在 GSM8K JSON 设定下 42.1 → 71.8，+29.7 分），其中 VC 通过剔除无效答案带来最高 17.4% 的增益，而 RC 的收益高度依赖模型与任务（Qwen3.5-4B 在 AIME 2026 上反而 −6.6）。

> **TL;DR:** This ICLR 2027 paper overturns the "constrained decoding harms reasoning" assumption with a three-way taxonomy (format/validity/reasoning constraints) plus a renormalization proof that masking never hurts when the ground truth satisfies the constraint. Across 11 open-source models (1.7B–32B) on GSM8K, LastLetter/LastLetter-Extended, and AIME 2026, the well-known JSON-vs-free-reasoning gap is shown to stem from non-equivalent prompts and output formats, not decoder-level masking: under matched prompts, constrained decoding matches or beats unconstrained decoding (e.g., Llama3-8B on GSM8K JSON rises from 42.1 to 71.8, +29.7 pts vs the unconstrained baseline; Gemma2-9B goes from 0.0 to 86.7), validity constraints add up to 17.4%, and reasoning constraints help or hurt depending on the model (Qwen3.5-4B drops 62.2 → 55.6 on AIME 2026, −6.6). Practical takeaway: encode the task's answer subspace (e.g., "integer 0–999" for AIME) into the schema — it is a near-free accuracy and extractability gain.

## 核心贡献

- **约束三分类法（FC/VC/RC）**：首次将约束解码系统拆分为格式约束（强制输出结构，如 JSON 语法）、有效性约束（依据任务结构屏蔽无效答案，如"AIME 答案是 0–999 的整数"）与推理约束（限制推理轨迹，如强制以 "Let's think step by step" 开头），说明约束解码的价值远不止"保证格式合法"。
- **理论证明**：在提示词与期望输出格式不变的条件下（假设 1：正确答案满足约束），掩码+重归一化后正确答案的概率只会不变或上升；若掩码在正确答案路径上剔除了至少一个正概率的无效 token（假设 2），则期望准确率严格提升（P_C(Y*) = P_θ(Y*)/Z* > P_θ(Y*)）。对推理约束则给出三情形分析：完全排除（必败）、oracle 约束（必胜）、部分保留（不确定）。
- **因果消融实验**：在 4 个模型家族、11 个开源模型（Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B/32B、Qwen3.5-2B/4B/9B/27B、Llama3-8B、Gemma2-9B、SmolLM3-3B）上，固定提示词与期望输出格式做逐对比较，证明所谓"约束解码损害推理"的表象主要来自不对等的提示与格式设定，支持 Lee et al. (2026) 的格式税观点。
- **抗泄漏的新评测集**：构建 LastLetter-Extended（把末字母拼接任务从 4 个名字扩到 8 个），与 AIME 2026 一样发布/构建于全部被测模型之后，降低数据泄漏风险；并扩展至 Qwen3.5-27B 说明增益在较大模型上依然存在。
- **增益来源剖析**：把 +FC+VC 的收益分解为格式层（可解析性）与有效性层（语义），并给出样本级案例分析（如 VC 修复 "h e n e" → "hene"、28.00 → 28），以及在 IMDb 上用替代词表做内容过滤的初步实验。

## Key Contributions

- **A three-type constraint taxonomy** — format constraints (FC, enforce output structure), validity constraints (VC, mask invalid answers using task structure), and reasoning constraints (RC, restrict reasoning trajectories) — reframing constrained decoding as more than syntactic format guarantee.
- **A theoretical guarantee**: under Assumption 1 (the ground-truth answer satisfies the constraint), token-level masking with renormalization never lowers the probability of the correct answer; under Assumption 2 (the mask removes some positive-probability invalid token on the correct answer's path), expected accuracy strictly increases. For RC, a three-case analysis (total exclusion → zero correctness; oracle constraint → perfect; partial retention → indeterminate).
- **Causal ablations across 11 open-source models** (Qwen3/Qwen3.5 1.7B–27B, Llama3-8B, Gemma2-9B, SmolLM3-3B; Qwen3-32B as extractor) showing the previously reported degradation is an artifact of non-equivalent prompting and output formats rather than decoder-level masking.
- **A leakage-resistant extended evaluation**: LastLetter-Extended (4 → 8 names, compound names excluded) and AIME 2026, both constructed/released after every evaluated model, plus results up to Qwen3.5-27B showing benefits persist at scale.
- **Gain-source analysis**: decomposition of FC vs VC contributions, sample-level token analyses (VC fixes "h e n e" → "hene", "28.00" → "28"), parsability and token-efficiency studies of JSON / InWriting / NL-to-Format, and a preliminary IMDb experiment where sentiment labels are prohibited but alternative wordlists preserved most accuracy.

## Methodology

The paper combines theory with controlled experiments. **Theory (Sec. 3):** For an autoregressive model, constrained decoding masks invalid next-tokens given the prefix and renormalizes locally. Because each normalizer Z_t ≤ 1, the constrained probability of the ground truth P_C(Y*|x,R) = P_θ(Y*|x,R)/Z* ≥ P_θ(Y*|x,R) whenever Y* satisfies the constraint (Assumption 1 — soundness, satisfied in practice by setting schema fields like `answer: str`); it is strict when masking removes positive-probability mass on Y*'s path (Assumption 2). For reasoning constraints over (reasoning, answer) pairs, they distinguish total exclusion (zero correctness), oracle retention (perfect), and partial retention (indeterminate) — formally capturing why RC has no universal guarantee.

**Experimental design (Sec. 4):** Eleven open-source models from four families. Four benchmarks at two difficulty tiers: GSM8K and Last Letter Concatenation (standard; the tasks where prior work reported degradation), plus AIME 2026 and the new LastLetter-Extended (reasoning-intensive, post-release to limit leakage). Three SOTA decoding strategies that support reasoning under constraints: (1) JSON with a `reason` field; (2) NL-to-Format — free reasoning then a second formatting/extraction pass (Qwen3-32B as extractor); (3) InWriting — single-pass free reasoning then constrained final answer via trigger-token (EOS/PAD) mode switch. Constraints are implemented with Litelines (built on outlines-core) and Pydantic schemas; FC sets `answer: str`, VC encodes the known answer subspace per task (GSM8K: non-negative integer; AIME 2026: integer 0–999; LastLetter: exactly 4 / Extended: exactly 8 alphabetic characters), RC enforces the "Let's think step by step" prefix. The crucial controls: the same prompt and the same expected output format are used for constrained and unconstrained runs, so the only difference is decoder-level masking. Metrics: exact-match accuracy (mean ± std over seeds × prompt/format combinations), parse rate, format gain, validity gain, reasoning gain, and generated-token counts. Standard setting: temperature 0, seed 1, 600 max reasoning tokens; AIME uses up to 12,800 tokens and seeds 42/43/44.

## Results & Highlights

- **Apparent degradation is a prompt/format artifact, not masking.** Under matched prompts and output formats, +FC+VC consistently matches or beats unconstrained decoding. On GSM8K with the JSON strategy, Llama3-8B jumps from 42.1 ± 11.8 (unconstrained) to 71.8 ± 1.6 with +FC+VC (+29.7 pts), and Gemma2-9B from 0.0 ± 0 (its JSON output never parses) to 86.7 ± 0.3 (+86.7 pts); on LastLetter with JSON, Qwen3.5-9B goes from 23.1 ± 16.4 to 88.9 ± 0.3 (+65.8 pts).
- **Even for plain-text answers, constraining the second step helps.** With NL-to-Format on LastLetter (same prompts, same reasoning traces), plain-text +FC+VC beats unconstrained plain text for every Qwen model, e.g., Qwen3.5-2B 62.9 ± 5.6 → 68.7 ± 5.2 (+5.8 pts) and Qwen3-1.7B 60.7 ± 4.3 → 66.1 ± 4.8 (+5.4 pts) — constrained decoding is not tied to JSON.
- **VC is the most consistent gain source (up to 17.4%, per the paper's decomposition in Fig. 1 / Tables 9–10).** On LastLetter-Extended with InWriting, Qwen3.5-2B rises from 18.1 ± 9.3 (unconstrained) to 27.2 ± 6.6 with +FC and 38.4 ± 8.2 with +FC+VC — i.e., VC contributes +11.2 of the +20.3 total; on GSM8K/JSON, Gemma2-9B's entire 86.7-point accuracy comes with parse rate fixed from 0.2% to 100% by FC (Table 11), while Qwen3-1.7B's parse rate goes 28.5% → 97.3%.
- **Reasoning constraints are model- and task-dependent.** Forcing "Let's think step by step" (+RC) lifts Qwen3-1.7B on GSM8K from 75.8 ± 0.8 to 77.7 ± 0.9 (+1.9) and Qwen3-4B on AIME 2026 from 14.8 ± 4.5 to 16.7 ± 2.7, but drops Qwen3.5-4B on AIME 2026 from 62.2 ± 3.1 to 55.6 ± 5.7 (−6.6) while Qwen3.5-27B gains 81.9 ± 3.9 → 84.3 ± 5.6 (+2.4).
- **Token efficiency**: InWriting adds fewer than 15 tokens (e.g., Qwen3-8B on GSM8K: 176.4 unconstrained → 188.2 +FC+VC) whereas NL-to-Format nearly doubles usage via its second LLM call; JSON +FC+VC can even shorten outputs (Qwen3-1.7B on GSM8K: 221.1 → 129.2 tokens).
- **VC as content filter (preliminary)**: on IMDb sentiment, Label-Constrained decoding leaves accuracy unchanged (Qwen3.5-4B 94.6 → 94.8) while forcing alternative sentiment words costs only ~7–11 pts (Qwen3-4B 92.6 → 83.4), suggesting models can express the intended answer through permitted alternatives.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The FC/VC/RC taxonomy and the renormalization non-degradation proof are clean and useful conceptual contributions; the core empirical conclusion (prompt-level confounds explain the "format tax") largely confirms Lee et al. (2026) and the dottxt blog rebuttal rather than discovering it — the paper's added value is the systematic causal ablation plus the VC insight.
- **Rigor**: The matched-prompt, matched-format design is the right experiment and is executed across an unusually broad model matrix. However, several unconstrained JSON baselines are 0.0 because outputs never parse (Llama3-8B, Gemma2-9B, Qwen3-1.7B on LastLetter) — the headline gains therefore conflate "masking helps" with "the unconstrained extractor was brittle"; a robust regex-based extractor baseline for the unconstrained path is not evaluated. Some cells also carry huge variance (Qwen3.5-4B JSON ±28.0, Qwen3.5-9B ±27.0 on LastLetter in Table 1/5), which the text does not discuss.
- **The VC gain is partly an exact-match artifact**: the paper's own sample analysis shows VC wins include normalizing "28.00" → "28" and stripping spaces from "h e n e" — cases a post-hoc answer normalizer would also fix. The authors argue token-level consistency enforcement is more than post-processing (spaces ride inside tokens like " e"), which is fair, but they never run the decisive control: unconstrained decoding + a lenient extractor. VC also requires hand-coding the answer subspace per task (non-negative integer for GSM8K, 0–999 for AIME, exactly 4/8 letters) — the gain scales with oracle-like task knowledge that may not exist in open-ended applications.
- **Reproducibility**: Prompts, all three variations per task, Pydantic schemas, Litelines configuration (allow_preamble, trigger tokens, 600/12,800 token budgets), seeds, and full per-table numbers are disclosed; code is supplementary-only "upon acceptance," so independent verification is not yet possible.
- **Significance**: For practitioners the message is actionable — put validity priors into the schema. But the evaluation surface is narrow: LastLetter variants are toy symbolic tasks, GSM8K is near-saturated for the larger models, AIME 2026 is run only under InWriting with four models, and the authors themselves admit (Sec. A.1) VC is evaluated "primarily on LastLetter and LastLetter-Extended" with only a preliminary IMDb experiment, and RC demonstrates feasibility with one known pattern rather than any method for finding beneficial traces.
- **Red flags**: minimal — no cherry-picked metric switching observed; the main risks are the conflated extractability gains and the degree to which the framing ("constrained decoding is beneficial") rests on tasks where the valid answer space is trivially characterizable.

## Strengths

- A crisp, falsifiable question ("is constrained decoding harmful?") answered with both a formal argument and a well-controlled causal ablation across 11 models and 4 families — the matched-prompt/matched-format protocol is exactly what prior comparisons lacked.
- The FC/VC/RC taxonomy turns a vague debate into three separately testable hypotheses, and the VC result (encode the answer subspace → near-guaranteed, near-zero-cost gain) is immediately actionable for anyone building structured-output pipelines.
- Leakage-conscious evaluation: AIME 2026 and the newly constructed LastLetter-Extended (4 → 8 names) postdate every evaluated model.
- Honest, detailed reporting: per-model variance, parse-rate tables, token-count tables, sample-level failure analyses (including the awkward markdown-fenced-JSON cases), and an explicit limitations section acknowledging the narrow VC scope and the oracle-knowledge requirement of RC.

## Limitations

- VC's headline gains partly reflect strict exact-match extraction and hand-coded task priors ("28.00" → "28", exactly-N-letters answers); the unconstrained + robust-extractor control is missing, and VC's applicability to tasks without a characterizable answer space is untested beyond a small IMDb experiment.
- RC evidence is thin: one reasoning pattern ("Let's think step by step"), mixed results (−6.6 on AIME 2026 for Qwen3.5-4B vs +2.4 for Qwen3.5-27B), and no method for identifying beneficial trajectories — feasibility demo only.
- Task scope is narrow: two of four benchmarks are LastLetter variants; AIME 2026 covers only 4 models and only the InWriting strategy; no code-generation or real-world schema-heavy tasks despite JSON being the motivating use case.
- Only simple JSON schemas are tested; complex nested formats combined with long reasoning are explicitly deferred to future work, and several JSON results carry very high variance (±27–29) that goes unexplained.
- Code not yet publicly released (supplementary, post-acceptance), limiting immediate independent verification.

## Relevance

对任何构建结构化输出/函数调用/Agent 管线的工程实践都直接相关：本文给出可操作结论——把任务已知的答案子空间（取值范围、字符数、类型）编码进输出 schema（VC），几乎零成本地同时提升准确率与可抽取性，而"约束解码损害推理"多是把提示词与格式不对等比较造成的假象；对推理模型评测与 schema 设计有直接参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How much of the +FC+VC gain survives if the unconstrained baseline gets a lenient, regex-based answer extractor instead of strict schema parsing — i.e., is VC a decoding win or an evaluation-harness win?
- Can validity constraints be derived automatically (e.g., by an LLM reading the task and emitting the Pydantic field constraints) rather than hand-coded per task, and does the gain persist on open-ended tasks with no characterizable answer space?
- Does the VC result transfer to schema-heavy real-world workloads (function calling, multi-field information extraction, SQL/tool-call generation) where the valid subspace is syntactic rather than semantic?
- Why do RC benefits flip sign across models and difficulty (−6.6 for Qwen3.5-4B but +2.4 for Qwen3.5-27B on AIME 2026)? Is it interference with trained reasoning modes, and can beneficial traces be selected adaptively at decode time?
- The token-masking overhead of constrained decoding grows with schema complexity (per-step automaton evaluation) — what is the wall-clock latency cost at serving scale, and where is the break-even against InWriting's trigger-token approach?
