01Methodology — two post-hoc instruments

Selection replay asks whether the dev gain means anything: task difficulties are fitted as a Beta posterior to the seed's episodes, then the optimizer's actual acceptance rule is replayed on candidates identical to the seed (100k replays per run). P(G′ ≥ G) is the probability that unchanged candidates report a gain at least as large — the winner's curse, made measurable.

Bipartite attribution asks where tokens go: each episode becomes a turn × context-component graph. Because the harness re-sends full history each turn, input tokens obey elasticity e_R = 1 + s_H·R/(R−1), and Δlog tokens splits into four behavioral terms (more actions, turn-splitting, content per turn, skill text).

Strengths

  • Turns "dev gains might be noise" into a precise, replayable measurement with per-run probabilities — and an exact accounting decomposition for cost.
  • The bipartite graph + four-term split is a reusable cost-attribution tool for any agent harness, independent of this debate.
  • Causal chain traced end-to-end, with behavior-level confirmation (56% → ≤0.3% shared-turn writes).
  • Each design principle backed by a quantitative panel; honest about its own exceptions (Luna/AutoBench test gains, the one transferring skill).

Limitations

  • Post-hoc only — cannot predict which rule adds turns before running, and cannot decompose success (black-box model states).
  • Regime-bound — elasticity assumes uncompacted history; cost claim weakens under realistic caching.
  • Coverage — three optimizers, one shared seed, one harness; single-run controls for the three-word seed and transfer tests.
  • Replay assumes independent episodes, exchangeable Beta difficulties; within-benchmark domains differ.
  • Diagnoses, doesn't build — no optimizer validating the four principles end-to-end.

04Questions for further exploration

  1. Would all four design principles, implemented in one optimizer (repeated-win gate + turn-charging + rule deletion + rule-level cost feedback), genuinely beat a strong hand-written seed on test?
  2. Under history compaction or provider cache pricing, does the seed's cost advantage shrink to irrelevance — or do extra turns stay expensive via output tokens and latency?
  3. Can rule→behavior causality be established with controlled single-rule rewrites, closing the correlational gap?
  4. Why do GPT-6-Luna skills gain +4.2 to +6.4 points on AutomationBench test while dev barely moves — is there a real editing effect selection cannot see?
  5. Does selection replay transfer to harness evolution and best-of-K checkpoint picking, where the same winner's curse appears with even smaller holdouts?
