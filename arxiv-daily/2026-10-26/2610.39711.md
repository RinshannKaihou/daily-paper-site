> **一句话总结：** Amazon 把生产级 agentic RL 的沙箱做成共享 Kubernetes 上的公共服务 Sandman：向未修改任务镜像只读挂载一个仅需 bash+Python3 的轻量 daemon（execd，持久 bash 进程跨调用保留文件/进程/工作目录），配合令牌桶限速创建、就绪探针、调用方取消后仍由服务端负责清理与 nonce 幂等删除——两周内为 122 个 SWE-bench Verified coding-agent 训练（Qwen3.5-35B-A3B + GRPO，最高 432 张 B200）创建 313 万个沙箱、峰值并发 11,858；在 16 客户端 1 万请求突发中 116 秒交付一半、308 秒全部交付（比调优后的 agent-sandbox 的 547 秒快 44%），SWE 镜像 no-op 命令延迟中位数 2.0 ms（agent-sandbox 14.3 ms、OpenSandbox 203.5 ms），300 次取消测试退出后零沙箱泄漏。

> **TL;DR:** A systems paper from Amazon on running sandbox infrastructure for agentic RL in production on shared EKS. Across two weeks of production SWE-bench Verified coding-agent training runs (122 runs, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B with GRPO, up to 432 B200 GPUs), Sandman created 3.13M sandboxes with peak concurrency of 11,858. In a 10,000-request / 16-client burst benchmark it delivered half the sandboxes in 116 s and all in 308 s vs 198 s / 547 s for a *tuned* agent-sandbox (44% faster full delivery) while leaving 0 sandboxes at client exit (OpenSandbox left 191 of 215 cancelled creates running); on SWE task images median no-op command latency was 2.0 ms vs 14.3 ms (agent-sandbox) and 203.5 ms (OpenSandbox). The paper is equally valuable for what it measures *inside* training: waiting for data is 65–78% of learner-step time but rollout-sandbox acquisition is ≤0.9%, grading acquisition 1–6.8%, and prewarming cuts grading acquisition from 6.2 s to 0.12 s median while inflating reserved vCPU-hours 2.46× with no statistically established steady-state training speedup — an unusually honest negative result. Best read as an operational playbook (pacing, cleanup ownership, execution-condition verification for grading) rather than an algorithmic advance; the daemon, provider and pool are promised for open source.

## 核心贡献

- **生产级沙箱服务的设计与实现**：Sandman 由三部分组成——pod provider（按镜像+CPU+内存+工作区规格建 pod，requests=limits、不挂 service-account token）、execd 工具 daemon（只读挂载进**未修改**的任务镜像，仅需 bash 和 Python 3，持久 bash 进程保留工作目录/环境/后台进程）、可选的 Ray-actor 池（按 (image, base commit) 维护温备 pod，统一预算限流）。作者承诺将开源 daemon、provider 和 pool。
- **针对共享集群三大痛点的设计**：(1) 突发创建用令牌桶限速 + 软拓扑打散（max skew 2），429/webhook 超时按退避+抖动最多重试 30 次；(2) 交付前必须通过 readiness probe 和 execd 健康检查；(3) **调用方取消/超时后服务端保留清理责任**——provider 读完 create 再按 nonce 匹配删除、未确认删除持续重试、owner reference 兜底，300-create 取消测试零泄漏（故障注入 25 次集群试验也全部无残留 pod）。
- **真实生产规模验证**：2026 年 10 月 3–17 日，4 个生产 campaign（A–D，均为 SWE-bench Verified 训练）共 313 万沙箱、122 个训练运行、27 个并发运行、432 张 B200；后续扩容测试以 20–25 creates/s 请求 1.2 万沙箱，38,377 次创建零准入错误，中位 create-to-Ready 保持 5–7 秒。
- **与三个开源系统的同机对比**：agent-sandbox（K8s SIG Apps）、OpenSandbox、CubeSandbox（微虚机快照），覆盖突发交付、工具调用延迟、取消清理三个维度，并诚实披露调优不对称（"Sandman received more tuning"）。
- **把沙箱放进训练时间线里度量**：用配对 A/B 实验量化预热与复用对训练的实际影响和资源代价（预热使评分沙箱获取从 6.2 s 降到 0.12 s 但预留 vCPU 小时 ×2.46，稳态提速不显著）；分解 learner 步时间（等待数据占 65–78%，其中 rollout 沙箱获取 ≤0.9%、评分获取 1–6.8%、测试执行 8–11%）。
- **评分确定性与安全加固**：500 个 SWE-bench Verified 参考补丁中 15 个跨执行条件判定翻转（11 个发生在禁出网时），因此 grading 前用 in-pod probe 校验声明的执行条件（出网、CPU quota、时区、hash seed 等）；execd 每次调用需 per-sandbox token，出入站双向网络 guard；两次真实检查发现并修复了未鉴权入口和 egress-denied 标签失效漏洞。

## Key Contributions

- **A production sandbox service for agentic RL on shared Kubernetes**: three components — a pod provider (one pod per image/resource/workspace spec, requests=limits, no service-account token), `execd` (a small HTTP daemon mounted read-only into *unmodified* task images, needing only bash and Python 3, whose persistent bash process preserves cwd, environment and background processes across tool calls), and an optional Ray-actor pool (warm pods keyed by (image, base commit) under one shared pacing budget). The authors will open-source the daemon, provider and pool.
- **Mechanisms for shared-cluster survival**: token-bucket pacing of creates with soft topology spreading (max skew 2) and up to 30 retries with backoff+jitter on HTTP 429 / webhook timeouts; mandatory readiness probe + execd health check before handoff (including image pull); and **cleanup ownership after the caller gives up** — the provider finishes accepted creates, deletes on nonce match, retries unconfirmed deletes, and owner references let Kubernetes GC sandboxes if the trainer pod dies. All 25 fault-injection cluster trials left no pod.
- **Production-scale evidence**: 3.13M sandboxes across 122 SWE-bench Verified coding-agent training runs (four campaigns) in two weeks, up to 27 simultaneous runs on 432 B200 GPUs; a subsequent scale-up peaked at 11,858 concurrent sandboxes with 38,377 creates and zero admission errors, median create-to-Ready 5–7 s.
- **Head-to-head comparison with three open-source systems** (agent-sandbox, OpenSandbox, CubeSandbox) on burst delivery, tool-call latency and cancellation cleanup, with an unusually explicit disclosure of tuning asymmetries and one-run-per-configuration caveats.
- **Measurement of provisioning *inside* training**, not just microbenchmarks: paired A/B runs quantify prewarming (grading acquisition 6.2 s → 0.12 s median, warm-hit 93–96%, but reserved vCPU-hours ×2.46 and no established steady-state step-time benefit: mean step-interval difference −8.7 s, 95% CI −38.4 to +21.0 s over steps 2–24) and checked reuse (−29% pod creations per grading, 95% CI 22–36%; fresh pod forced in only 8 of 64,199 SWE-bench Verified state checks vs 23.7% on SWE-Gym).
- **Execution-condition verification for reproducible grading and hardened isolation**: 15 of 500 SWE-bench Verified reference patches flipped verdicts across execution conditions (11 when outbound traffic was denied), motivating a pre-grading in-pod probe of declared conditions, per-sandbox tokens on execd, and outbound/inbound network guards — with two disclosed live checks that found (and led to fixes for) an unauthenticated execd endpoint and a label that failed to install the egress guard.

## Methodology

Sandman splits responsibilities between the training job and the shared EKS cluster. **execd** is mounted read-only into each unmodified task image; agent workers (Ray workers outside the sandbox) call it by direct pod address — the earlier Service-per-slot design was abandoned after measured acquisition-to-first-command medians of 2.3 s (Services) vs 0.05 s (direct addresses), and Service links were disabled after they injected ~23,400 environment variables into new containers. The **provider** creates pods (CPU/memory requests equal to limits), paces them through a token bucket shared across on-demand creates, warm builds and replacements, retries 429s/webhook timeouts up to 30 times, separates the 12-thread Kubernetes API pool from daemon calls, and tags each create with a nonce for idempotent deletion. The **pool** is a Ray actor that keeps warm pods per (image, base commit), caps live pods and concurrent creates, and evicts idle warm pods after a timeout; a worker fallback to direct create (used for 4.3% of pooled-arm gradings) survives pool errors.

Provisioning is on-demand by default. **Prewarming** trades readiness for reservation; **checked reuse** applies to grading pods: at creation execd records a baseline of grader-modified state (repository, installed packages, system/home directories, environment, timezone, processes), and after grading Sandman either restores the baseline or deletes the pod (max 8 reuses; retire on contradicting verdicts). **Cleanup under cancellation**: callers that time out leave the provider responsible — late pods are deleted or folded into the warm pool, deletes are retried until confirmed, and owner references are the final fallback. A stress test motivating this design found 5,904 pods removed only by GC at teardown, with ≥4,181 more live pods than agent slots at peak.

**Grading and determinism**: patches are extracted against the task's base commit excluding test-configuration files (an earlier agent had abused a conftest.py to bypass tests); each suite runs in a separate fresh pod by default; before grading, an in-pod probe checks outbound access, CPU affinity/quota, memory limit, user identity, time limits, hash seed and timezone against the declared configuration; a preflight integration check (0.06–0.14 pod-hours per task) verifies the reference patch passes and an empty patch fails — two SWE-bench Verified tasks where the empty patch passed were rejected. **Security**: per-sandbox tokens on every execd call except health checks; two NET_ADMIN init containers install outbound (blocks egress including the metadata service) and inbound (allowlists trainer pods and the node) guards that execd cannot change; model code still runs as root in runc containers sharing the node kernel, with no seccomp profile — no protection claimed against a malicious co-tenant.

**Evaluation setup**: production workload = 122 SWE-bench Verified training runs (campaigns A–D) with an asynchronous GRPO trainer on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, mostly 16 B200 GPUs (8 learner + 8 SGLang inference), 2 vCPU / 8 GiB sandboxes; some controlled experiments use SWE-Gym. A/B arms run concurrently on the shared cluster with 95% confidence intervals and pre-registered comparisons. The system comparison uses a common Python driver against agent-sandbox, OpenSandbox and CubeSandbox on 40 dedicated 96-vCPU nodes (CubeSandbox on 42 separate nested-virtualization nodes), with 10,000-request bursts from 16 client processes on cached 0.25 vCPU / 1 GiB pods, plus 1,024-sandbox SWE-image runs (2 vCPU / 8 GiB) executing 30,720 no-op calls, and 300-create cancellation tests. ## Results & Highlights

- **Production scale (SWE-bench Verified campaigns A–D, Oct 3–17)**: 3.13M sandboxes created for 122 training runs; up to 27 simultaneous runs on 432 B200 GPUs; a later scale-up reached 11,858 concurrent sandboxes at 20–25 creates/s with median create-to-Ready of 5–7 s and **0 admission errors across 38,377 creates** (tiny pods: 0.1 vCPU / 128 MiB / 43 MB cached image).
- **Burst delivery (10,000 requests, 16 clients, cached images)**: Sandman made half the sandboxes usable in **116 s and all in 308 s**, vs **198 s / 547 s** for agent-sandbox tuned with pacing + retries + topology spreading (44% less full-delivery time), with **0 sandboxes left at client exit for both**. Untuned agent-sandbox delivered only 1,124/10,000 (2,570 left); paced OpenSandbox 5,552/10,000 (2,502 left, lifecycle server restarted twice); CubeSandbox's best run 8,943/9,843 (5 left) with median delivery 33 s among successes. In a double 20,000-request burst exceeding the ~11,600 node pod slots, Sandman delivered all within 951 s under a 1,500 s readiness budget.
- **Tool-call latency (1,024 SWE-image sandboxes, 2 vCPU/8 GiB, 30,720 no-op calls)**: median no-op command latency **2.0 ms for Sandman vs 14.3 ms for agent-sandbox and 203.5 ms for OpenSandbox** (~7× / ~100× lower); half-delivery of the sandboxes 20.3 s vs 33.3 s / 25.4 s; all creates, commands and deletes succeeded.
- **Cancellation cleanup (300 random-time cancels within 10 s)**: Sandman left **0 sandboxes** at client exit (also with pacing disabled, including cancellations after Ready); OpenSandbox left **191 of 215** cancelled creates running (under a 4-hour lifetime vs its 10-minute default); CubeSandbox's synchronous client kept creating after cancellation.
- **Placement and address capacity (two sequential 1,000-pod batches at 10 creates/s on 375 nodes)**: soft spreading placed ≤3 pods/node vs 65/node by default; **0 vs 254 first-IP-assignment failures**; create-to-Ready p99 **8 s vs 57 s** — bursts concentrated on cached-image nodes stall VPC CNI address assignment despite spare cluster capacity.
- **Provisioning costs inside training**: image pulls dominate on-demand creation (median 17.4 s with pull vs 4.1 s cached for 1.3–8.4 GB SWE-Gym images). Waiting for training data is 65–78% of learner-step time (campaigns A–C), but rollout-sandbox acquisition is ≤0.9%; grading acquisition is 4.5–6.8% with fresh pods, 1–2% with warm pools, 13.3% with a 32-pod capped pool (50.9% of gradings waited, median 38.6 s); test execution is 8–11% with one suite and 38% with two.
- **Prewarming trade-off (three 16-GPU paired runs)**: the warm pool served 93–96% of gradings, cutting median grading acquisition from **6.2 s (on-demand) to 0.12 s**, but total reserved sandbox vCPU-hours rose to **2.46×** the on-demand total at a 2,100 s idle timeout; step 24 arrived 9.4% sooner, yet over steps 2–24 the mean step-interval difference was −8.7 s (95% CI −38.4 to +21.0 s) — no established steady-state training speedup. Checked reuse cut pod creations per grading by **29% (95% CI 22–36%)**.
- **Economics and efficiency**: in a 198-pod sample, actual use averaged 10.5% of requested CPU and 1.9% of requested memory (pod-minute weighted); sandbox resource costs were estimated at **1.7–4.0% of GPU costs**. Availability per agent-session start was 91.16–99.73% (attempt-level), ≥96.69% trajectory-level with one retry; 99.5% of campaign C's acquisition failures came from unreachable admission webhooks.
- **Grading determinism and security costs**: 15 of 500 SWE-bench Verified reference patches flipped verdicts across execution conditions (11 under denied outbound traffic); both network guards together add ~1.5 s to median single-create time (2.2 s → 3.7 s); a six-pair A/B of combined grading changes (preflight + pooled checked reuse + authentication) showed a mean task-level pass-rate change of **−2.33 pp (95% CI −4.22 to −0.43, sign-flip p = 0.0625)** on 112 held-out tasks — confounded by changing task set, grading path and authentication simultaneously.

## Authors & Credibility

Fifteen authors, all at Amazon, with Yiming Yan and Hongyang Wang as equal contributors — the team that builds and operates this service for Amazon's production agentic RL training, which is the strongest credibility signal here: the numbers come from two weeks of real production campaigns (122 runs, 432 B200 GPUs), not synthetic load alone. The paper reads like an SRE/ML-infrastructure experience report in the best sense: pre-registered paired A/B comparisons with 95% CIs, a mechanism-version table (Table 3) admitting that campaigns A–D ran *earlier* versions of Sandman, disclosed security incidents (an unrelated pod invoking commands in guarded sandboxes via unauthenticated endpoints; six egress-denied pods reaching the internet), and negative results reported plainly (inconclusive prewarming benefit; a pass-rate drop they cannot attribute). It is a preprint (v2, 31 Oct 2026, cs.DC) and not yet peer-reviewed; the promised open-sourcing of the daemon, provider and pool was still pending at paper time, so independent replication is currently impossible.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty is engineering, not invention**: the core trick — injecting a daemon into unmodified images — is explicitly shared with agent-sandbox ("Agent-sandbox also supports daemon injection into unmodified images"), and snapshot-based systems like CubeSandbox beat Sandman where snapshots win: CubeSandbox's serial restore took 0.08 s vs Sandman's 2.18 s (and agent-sandbox's 1.45 s — Sandman *loses* serial creation to its closest baseline). Sandman's genuine edge is the production-hardened pacing/cleanup/readiness machinery and the measurement study around it.
- **Comparison fairness is acknowledged but real**: the authors admit "Sandman received more tuning, and our OpenSandbox and CubeSandbox deployments may be suboptimal," that "most configurations ran once" (the headline 116/308 s vs 198/547 s burst numbers have no repeats), that CubeSandbox ran without its guest probe (which failed on their deployment, leaving a readiness race) on different hardware with nested virtualization, and that the OpenSandbox cleanup test used a 4-hour sandbox lifetime instead of its 10-minute default — which inflates OpenSandbox's leak count.
- **Training-benefit evidence is thin and self-impugned**: the prewarming speedup rests on three pairs whose CI crosses zero; the combined-grading A/B changed task set, grading path and authentication at once so the −2.33 pp pass-rate drop "cannot be attributed to any individual change"; and the currently deployed version got only a 20-step functional validation with "no control arm or held-out learning endpoint." The 11,858-concurrency peak was achieved with tiny 0.1 vCPU / 128 MiB pods, not the real 2 vCPU / 8 GiB workload, so it does not directly extrapolate to production density.
- **Data quirks and honest red flags**: 15/500 reference patches flipping verdicts across execution conditions is a striking quantification of SWE-bench reward noise that Sandman mitigates but cannot eliminate ("undeclared or unreadable settings remain unverified"); the August build used in the campaigns lacked an exit-status frame check, so "a split frame could report a failed command as successful" with unknown frequency; the pool's error fallback "bypasses the pool's budget and cap" (4.3% of gradings), a deliberate reliability/aggregate-traffic loophole; and there is no periodic sweep for ownerless pods from unknown leak paths.
- **Security posture is explicitly bounded**: no seccomp profile, shared kernel, model code running as root, no user namespace or read-only rootfs; the paper candidly states "we do not claim protection against a malicious co-tenant," and microVM alternatives (Firecracker/Kata) were ruled out for needing bare metal/nested virtualization rather than measured.

## Strengths

- Genuine production scale and trace-driven design: every mechanism in the paper (pacing, nonce cleanup, address-aware spreading, condition probes) traces to an observed failure, and 41 operational cases with causes, fixes and bounded costs are documented in an appendix.
- Exceptional measurement honesty: negative results (no steady-state prewarming benefit), confounded experiments flagged as confounded, incident-loss estimates labeled as upper bounds, and a version-provenance table separating what each study actually ran.
- The training-time anatomy (waiting 65–78% of learner time; sandbox acquisition ≤0.9%; test execution 8–38%) is exactly the decomposition the agentic-RL-infrastructure literature has been missing, and it corrects the intuitive assumption that faster sandbox provisioning would directly speed up training.
- Full-cost accounting: reserved CPU-hours ×2.46 for prewarming, 10.5%/1.9% actual CPU/memory utilization vs requests, 1.7–4.0% of GPU cost — the resource ledger other sandbox papers omit.
- Deterministic, idempotent cleanup design validated by fault injection (25 trials, zero leaks) rather than just happy-path benchmarks.

## Limitations

- Single EKS cluster; all training studies use SWE-style Python tasks, one agent harness and one asynchronous trainer — generalization to browsing/computer-use agents or multi-turn non-SWE workloads is untested.
- Headline production and scale-up numbers come from earlier Sandman versions (Table 3) and from tiny pods (0.1 vCPU / 128 MiB) for the 11,858-concurrency run.
- Prewarming and reuse studies have only three pairs each; most burst comparisons ran once; Sandman was tuned more than the baselines (authors' own admission).
- Serial single-sandbox creation is slower than agent-sandbox (2.18 s vs 1.45 s median) and vastly slower than CubeSandbox's 0.08 s snapshot restore — the wins are in burst pacing, per-call latency and cleanup, not raw create speed.
- No isolation upgrade over runc (no seccomp, shared kernel, root); no protection claimed against malicious co-tenants; gVisor/Firecracker costs not measured.
- Open-source release pending at paper time, so none of the numbers are independently reproducible yet.

## Relevance

如果你关注 agentic RL 训练基础设施与效率优化，这篇论文直接回答了"在共享 K8s 上为 RL 智能体跑 SWE-bench 规模训练需要什么样的沙箱服务"：创建限速、取消后清理、评分执行条件校验、预热/复用的资源-训练权衡都有生产数据支撑，是搭建自家 RL 训练平台时难得的工程账本与避坑清单。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would microVM isolation (Firecracker/Kata) change the latency/cost trade-off at this scale, given that gVisor only partly supports the packet filter behind Sandman's outbound guard and neither was measured?
- Does prewarming show a steady-state training benefit with more pairs once in-flight-slot occupancy is modeled — the authors note their phase shares "do not predict end-to-end training speedup," so what is the correct model linking acquisition delay to rollout throughput?
- How much of the −2.33 pp pass-rate drop in the combined-grading A/B comes from preflight task removal (a training-distribution change) vs authentication changes — a factorial ablation is needed before adopting the modified integration.
- Can the residual grading nondeterminism (15/500 verdict flips on SWE-bench Verified) be eliminated with fully hermetic execution, and what does the remaining noise do to GRPO group-relative advantage estimates at small group sizes?
- How does the design port to bursty non-SWE agents (browsing, computer-use) whose sandboxes are long-lived and stateful, where the 74–91% idle share of sandbox lifetime and the reuse calculus may differ substantially?
- Once open-sourced, how does checked-reuse (baseline record + restore) compare against DeltaBox-style millisecond filesystem/process checkpoint-rollback on both correctness and pod-hours?
