> **一句话总结：** 本文提出一个"边界规划器"，仅用传统图 profile 和单算子 benchmark（不端到端计时任何候选设计）就能预测流式推理模型该在哪切分 megakernel：在 H100 NVL 上对 NH-TTS / Qwen3-TTS / Llama-1B 的全部 13 个模型-批次组合都选中了实测最快设计，其中 NH-TTS 批 32 的三 megakernel 组合比传统 TensorRT 管线快 4.05×（TTFA p95）、比单体 megakernel 快 21.4%。

> **TL;DR:** NVIDIA authors argue the best streaming-inference config is multiple specialized megakernels (or fully conventional execution), never one monolith, and build a planner that ranks all 81 NH-TTS candidate compositions from conventional-graph profiles and standalone kernel benchmarks without timing any candidate end to end. On an H100 NVL it picks the measured-fastest design in 13/13 model-batch cases across NH-TTS (TensorRT-LLM), Qwen3-TTS (vLLM) and Llama-1B (PyTorch); the selected NH-TTS three-megakernel design cuts engine-level mean batch-p95 TTFA 4.05× vs the conventional TensorRT pipeline and 21.4% vs a single monolith megakernel at batch 32 (+28% throughput vs monolith), while Qwen3-TTS flips to fully conventional at batch 48+. Absolute latency predictions are imperfect (6–22% overprediction for NH-TTS three-megakernel designs), but the ranking held everywhere tested.

## 核心贡献

- **重新定义融合问题：** 论证流式模型（如 TTS）的 kernel 融合不是"融不融"而是"在哪里切"的问题——最优解是多个专门化 megakernel 的组合（不含交叠的传统图部分），或完全传统图执行；在全部 13 个测试组合中，最优设计包含 0 到 4 个 megakernel，没有一个是单体 megakernel。
- **免端到端计时的边界规划器：** 从传统图执行 profile + 独立算子 benchmark + 源码声明的实现契约出发，构建区域图（rGraph）枚举候选（NH-TTS 共 81 个）、瓦片图（tGraph）估算各后端/CTA 预算下的服务成本、事件图（evGraph）仿真到首次发布时刻（first publication），以最低分选出最优组合，无需实现和计时任何候选设计。
- **13/13 选中实测最快设计：** 在 H100 NVL 上跨三个模型（NH-TTS、Qwen3-TTS、Llama-1B 3.2）、三个框架（TensorRT、vLLM、PyTorch）、13 个模型-批次组合，冻结后的规划器全部唯一选中实测最快设计；输入采集成本只需分钟级（NH-TTS 4.6 GPU-分钟、Qwen3-TTS 15.9 GPU-分钟）。
- **四条机制性观察：**（1）更快的 kernel 可能让管线更慢（88 vs 80 CTA 的生产者单独快 2.2%，但与传统 codec 共处时整体慢 30.3%）；（2）共处代价取决于对端实现（传统 codec 在生产者 megakernel 旁慢 7.57×，而 codec megakernel 只慢 1.23×）；（3）固定契约下串行切分是中性的（八个全 megakernel 划分预测同为 37.067 ms TTFA p95）；（4）megakernel 的固定成本决定传统执行何时反超（Qwen3-TTS 的领先从 B16 的 15.9 ms 跌到 B64 的 0.65 ms，而固定成本 0.7–1.1 ms，故 B48 起传统图胜出）。
- **模型分类法：** 提出按 batch size 变化趋势划分的四类模型——megakernel-stable（NH-TTS；Llama-1B 首 token 延迟）、megakernel-receding（Qwen3-TTS；Llama-1B 的 token 间延迟）、megakernel-advancing 与 megakernel-averse（后两类为预测、未实测）。

## Key Contributions

- **Reframes fusion as a boundary-selection problem.** For multi-phase streaming models (prefill / autoregressive producer / codec consumer run at different rates and prefer different thread-block shapes), the best configuration is a composition of multiple specialized megakernels with no overlapping conventional-graph component, or a fully conventional graph. Across 13 model-batch combinations the best design used zero to four megakernels — never a single monolith.
- **A planner that never times a candidate end to end.** It builds a region graph (rGraph; admission/producer/consumer groups, 2·3^(n-1) covers before filtering — 81 candidates for NH-TTS's 4 groups), a tile graph (tGraph) that models barrier removal, weight prefetch, on-chip forwarding, register pressure and reduced CTA budgets to produce a service table S[g, β, W; κ], and an event graph (evGraph) simulated to first publication, where T_pub = max(F_chunk1, F_producer_h). Candidates lacking compatible timing evidence or deadlocking in simulation are excluded.
- **Perfect selection accuracy on held-out cases.** With scoring rules and candidate-generation policy frozen on a separate development set, the planner uniquely selected the measured-fastest design in 13/13 held-out model-batch cases on H100 NVL across three frameworks (TensorRT-LLM, vLLM, PyTorch). Collecting its inputs costs minutes: 4.6 GPU-minutes of kernel benchmarks for NH-TTS and 15.9 for Qwen3-TTS, versus exhaustively building and timing 81 NH-TTS candidates.
- **Four mechanistic observations explaining when fusion pays:** a faster kernel can slow the pipeline (changed CTA-holding contract); colocation cost depends on the peer's implementation (conventional vs megakernel peer); serial cuts are neutral under fixed contracts (specialization, not the boundary, explains the monolith's loss); and a megakernel's fixed cost decides when conventional execution wins as batch grows.
- **A four-category taxonomy** (megakernel-stable / receding / advancing / averse) capturing how a model's best composition moves with batch size — the latter two categories are predictions, not observations.

## Methodology

The planner operates in three graph layers built purely from conventional-graph profiles, standalone operator/step benchmarks, and source-declared implementation contracts (geometry, storage, compiled-entry membership, synchronization):

1. **Region graph (rGraph).** Running the conventional implementation (e.g., TensorRT) captures execution frequency, workload characteristics (arithmetic intensity, tile geometry, tensor sizes, memory traffic), dependencies, and publication requirements. Groups are classified as admission (once per request), producer (once per step), or consumer (once per chunk). A cover partitions the ordered groups into contiguous regions, each assigned a conventional or megakernel backend; an execution policy (serial, fixed CTA split X/Y, or dynamic split) is attached where supported. For NH-TTS (P=prefill, B=Nemotron-H backbone, L=local transformer, C=codec), 54 serial covers plus 27 policy variants yield 81 candidates.
2. **Tile graph (tGraph) and service table.** Each supported output tile becomes a task with compute/weight/activation loads; an event-driven list scheduler over W workers (the CTA budget) with a single FIFO memory queue produces service costs per (group, backend, CTA budget, implementation context). Two megakernel mechanisms are modeled explicitly: dropping inter-kernel barrier edges (cross-kernel readiness) and weight prefetch. Opaque operators use a CTA-wave approximation scaled by ⌈g/W⌉/⌈g/132⌉. Timing records are reused only under exactly matching transfer conditions.
3. **Event graph (evGraph) and boundary selection.** Each candidate is lowered to an event graph extending to first publication: admission groups run once, producer groups run to step h = d + c1 + l (delay + first chunk + lookahead), and the first consumer chunk becomes eligible at step r = d + c1. Algorithm 1 simulates CTA admission control (a launch holds its allocation until its last task finishes) and shared-bandwidth rates min(1, B_mem/Q_tot); Algorithm 2 exhaustively scores supported candidates and returns all minimum-score ones (ties retained, never resolved with validation measurements).

Evaluation methodology is notably careful: designs share weights, precision, fixtures, deterministic decoding and publication rules; each is checked against an unfused reference on separate correctness fixtures (exact token/code-index match, FP relative L2 error ≤ 10^-2) before timing; qualified designs are measured in balanced randomized order in matched GPU blocks with 20,000-resample paired bootstrap CIs; two warmup cohorts are discarded; and the planner was frozen before any end-to-end candidate timing (validation results never fed back into the model).

## Results & Highlights

- **NH-TTS, batch 32, H100 NVL:** the selected three-megakernel design (P / (B+L) / C with a dynamic 88/44 producer-consumer CTA split) publishes first audio **4.05× sooner than the conventional TensorRT pipeline** (engine-level mean batch-p95 TTFA) and **21.4% sooner than a single monolith megakernel**, with **+28% throughput vs the monolith and +19.7% vs conventional TensorRT**.
- **TTFA ordering holds across batch sizes:** at B64 the three-megakernel design measures **47.4 ms vs 61.0 ms (one megakernel) vs 221.5 ms (conventional)**; at B1 it is **27.5 ms vs 32.7 and 73.0 ms** respectively.
- **Selection accuracy:** the frozen planner uniquely selected the measured-fastest design in **13/13 held-out model-batch cases**; guessing is costly — for NH-TTS and Qwen3-TTS the next-best layout is **3–29% slower** than the best. Ablations: additive scoring and no-overlap variants each match only **11/13 with max regret 4.50%** (both miss at NH-TTS B32/B64 by choosing serial execution).
- **Megakernel advantage recedes with batch (Qwen3-TTS):** the four-region megakernel's lead shrinks from **15.9 ms at B16 to 0.65 ms at B64** while fixed costs stay at 0.7–1.1 ms, so the fully conventional graph wins at B48/B64 — measured: four megakernels are **4.2% (CI 3.1–4.9%) and 3.2% (CI 3.0–3.4%) slower than conventional** at B48 and B64; at B1 the ordering reverses (two megakernels **8.1% slower than four**, CI 7.8–8.3%).
- **Colocation interference is peer-dependent:** beside the producer megakernel, the **conventional codec slows 7.57×** while the **codec megakernel slows only 1.23×**; and giving the producer 88 instead of 80 CTAs makes it 2.2% faster alone but the (producer + conventional codec) pair **30.3% slower** — a faster kernel can make the pipeline slower.
- **Specialization beats boundaries (Llama-1B):** the two-megakernel design (separate prefill/decode) beats the combined monolith even on first-token latency because the fused entry spills registers, making each prefill layer **~15% slower (316 vs 276 µs at B1)**. Component-level (Table II): the Stage-1 (local-transformer) megakernel runs **1.290 ms vs 9.760 ms for TensorRT**, while Stage-0 improves 0.991 vs 1.216 ms.
- **Policy ranking also correct:** within the NH-TTS three-megakernel layout, an 80/52 CTA split is **1.0% slower than 88/44 at B32**, and running the three megakernels serially is **4.2% slower than overlapping at B64** — the planner ranked both correctly.
- **Cost of the planner's inputs:** kernel benchmarks took **4.6 GPU-minutes (NH-TTS) and 15.9 GPU-minutes (Qwen3-TTS)** across four batch sizes — minutes instead of implementing and timing 81 candidate designs.

## Authors & Credibility

All three authors are NVIDIA engineers (Bangalore, Hyderabad, and Pune, India). The work is grounded in real production-adjacent systems: it evaluates an upcoming autoregressive TTS model with a Nemotron-H backbone (NH-TTS), Qwen3-TTS, and Llama-1B 3.2 across TensorRT-LLM, vLLM and PyTorch on H100 NVL hardware with pinned software (CUDA 13.2, driver 595.84). The related-work coverage is current and fair (MPK/OSDI'26, Event Tensor, Ada-MK, TileRT, FlashMoE, Rammer, VersaPipe, Orion, Hazy Research megakernel blogs, Welder tile graphs), correctly positioning the contribution as deciding *which* regions prior systems should fuse. Methodological credibility is high: frozen planner, dev/eval separation, correctness gating against an unfused reference, randomized measurement with paired bootstrap CIs, and explicit statements of what the evaluation does not establish. The artifact records checkpoint hashes, software versions, text/context lengths and streaming parameters (d, c1, l).

## Critical Assessment

- **The 13/13 headline is over a limited validation pool.** The paper states plainly that "the pools contain the designs we could build and qualify, not every candidate: the planner ranks all 81 NH-TTS candidates, but only these are measured." Selection accuracy is therefore accuracy *within implemented pools*, not proof that the planner's top-ranked unmeasured candidate is truly best — the authors admit optimality over unmeasured compositions is not established.
- **Absolute latency is not calibrated.** The planner ranks but does not predict time: NH-TTS three-megakernel designs are overpredicted by **6–22%** (42.52 ms predicted vs 35.99 ms measured at B32), Llama-1B by up to 11%. Since several winning margins are small (1.0% for the 80/52 vs 88/44 split), the ranking's robustness rests on error cancellation more than prediction fidelity — though the closest decisions did hold within their 95% bootstrap intervals.
- **The 4.05× headline needs context.** It compares against a conventional TensorRT pipeline that does not overlap producer and consumer at all; the more meaningful comparison for megakernel-savvy readers is +21.4% TTFA / +28% throughput vs the monolith megakernel — solid but far from the headline number.
- **Single-architecture, partly unreproducible evaluation.** Everything runs on one GPU (H100 NVL, 132 SMs); no transfer to other architectures is tested (authors concede this). NH-TTS is an unannounced model, so independent reproduction is essentially impossible until it ships, and no public artifact/code release is mentioned in the text.
- **Taxonomy is half-speculative.** Two of the four categories (megakernel-advancing, megakernel-averse) are predictions with no supporting measurements; the observed quadrants rely on just three models, one of which (Llama-1B) is a 1B-parameter model whose decode behavior may not extrapolate to larger LLMs.
- **Missing comparisons.** No head-to-head with automated alternatives for the same decision (e.g., Ada-MK's DAG-based search or Rammer-style boundary selection) on selection quality or search cost; the planner also depends on hand-declared source contracts (geometry, storage, sync), which limits push-button generality on new models.
- **Objective is first-publication only.** The planner minimizes a first-publication score and does not jointly optimize steady-state inter-token latency or throughput p95; the score is explicitly a ranking proxy, not a latency-distribution predictor.

## Strengths

- Attacks a real and expensive problem — exhaustively timing 81 candidate compositions is impractical, and the paper replaces it with minutes of profiling plus simulation.
- The evaluation hygiene is unusually strong for a systems paper: frozen planner before timing, development/evaluation separation, correctness gating with exact-match and L2-error thresholds, randomized balanced measurement, paired bootstrap CIs, and honest scope statements.
- Goes beyond empirical ranking to mechanism: the four observations (faster-kernel slowdowns, peer-dependent colocation cost, neutral serial cuts, fixed-cost crossover) give practitioners transferable intuition, each backed by a concrete experiment.
- Cross-framework generality of the approach (TensorRT, vLLM, PyTorch) rather than a single-stack solution.
- The illustrative overlap calculation (serial 30.1 ms vs overlap 28.2 ms despite slower individual components) makes the core trade-off easy to internalize.

## Limitations

- Validated on a single GPU architecture (H100 NVL) with pinned CUDA/driver versions; no cross-platform transfer evidence.
- The central model (NH-TTS) is unreleased at publication time, and no public code/artifact is referenced in the paper body, limiting independent verification.
- Absolute-time predictions deviate up to 22% for the design family that actually wins, so the method cannot be used for SLA planning — only for ranking.
- Requires source-declared implementation contracts (thread counts, tile geometry, call/storage policies) per model; applying it to a new model is engineering work, not automatic.
- The planner optimizes TTFA/TTFT only; steady-state ITL, throughput under load, and multi-tenant serving are out of scope, and two of the four model categories are unvalidated predictions.

## Relevance

直接命中大规模 ML 计算基础设施方向：该工作回答了流式推理 serving 中 kernel 融合该推进到哪里的工程问题，用 profile 驱动的 tile/event 图仿真替代昂贵的端到端候选计时，对低延迟 TTS/LLM 推理系统（TensorRT、vLLM、PyTorch 栈）的 megakernel 化落地有直接指导意义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the planner's selection transfer across GPU architectures (e.g., Blackwell, or AMD MI-series with different SM counts and register files), or must the service table be re-calibrated per device?
- Can software-level scheduling prevent the observed 7.57× interference between a co-located conventional graph and a megakernel, as the authors themselves pose as an open question?
- Would jointly optimizing first-publication time with steady-state ITL/throughput change the selected compositions, especially in the megakernel-receding regime?
- Can the source-declared implementation contracts be inferred automatically from profiler traces or compiler IR, removing the manual engineering step?
- Do megakernel-stable architectures remain competitive on accuracy-comparable models, and does the taxonomy hold for larger LLMs (≥7B) and MoE models where prefill is compute-bound?
