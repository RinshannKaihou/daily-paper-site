> **一句话总结：** 本文将单题上的自一致性训练（TTRL）建模为 softmax 策略 logit 的连续流，证明探索系数存在"下边界以下只有坍缩策略稳定、上边界以上只有分散策略稳定、中间由初始策略决定"的三区间结构，并证明探索项"走完奖励路径"（含 GRPO 除法）时阈值不随学习率/优化器变化、绕过除法时阈值需增大约 2–3 倍——在 Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B 的 MATH-500 自由格式 TTRL 中，除法前加入的稀有答案奖励在预测阈值附近阻止了坍缩（F95e 从 0.66–0.79 降到 ≤0.019）并把 pass@32 从 0.561 提高到 0.710，但 pass@1 从 0.481 降到 0.365。

> **TL;DR:** Test-time RL (TTRL) trains LLMs without labels via majority-vote rewards but collapses the policy onto its initial (possibly wrong) majority answer. This paper models single-problem training as a logit flow of a softmax policy and proves the exploration coefficient has a lower and an upper stability boundary (closed form: 1/n and 4(n−1)/n for the agreement reward with uniform smoothing), and that where exploration enters the update — before vs. after GRPO's standard-deviation division — changes the bonus threshold from 1/log(G−1) to roughly 2–3x that. Experiments on a tabular-network testbed, Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct on 64 MMLU-Pro letter-answer questions, and free-form TTRL with Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B on 128 MATH-500 problems confirm the predicted thresholds: on MATH-500, a rare-answer bonus placed before the division at c=0.6 cuts the collapse share F95e from 0.66–0.79 (control TTRL, 6 seeds) to ≤0.019, and raises pass@32 from 0.561 to 0.710, but lowers pass@1 from 0.481 to 0.365 — exploration preserves correct answers among the samples but cannot fix a wrong majority and sacrifices majority-vote accuracy.

## 核心贡献

- **三区间定理（Three regimes）**：把单题训练建模为 softmax 策略 logit 的连续流（tabular flow），证明探索系数 c 被两条边界分成三个区间——低于下边界时只有坍缩到单答案的策略稳定，高于上边界时只有分散在多答案上的策略稳定，两界之间（"窗口"）由初始策略决定结果。对"一致性奖励 + 向均匀策略平滑"的情形给出闭式边界：下界 1/n、上界 4(n−1)/n。
- **"奖励路径"（path of the reward）概念**：奖励经过策略梯度几何、优化器预条件、GRPO 除法三步变换到达参数。证明凡随奖励走完整条路径的探索项，其平衡点与分岔在精确梯度下不随学习率和优化器缩放而移动；绕过任一步（如泄漏式混合、只在损失中加 KL 罚）的探索项，阈值会随学习率或策略变化（泄漏项的阈值以 ε/η 为单位）。
- **GRPO 除法拆分阈值**：对稀有答案奖励 b_j = −log(N_aj/G)，除法前单一阈值 c*_b = 1/log(G−1) 同时适用于宽起点与坍缩起点；除法后（只除优势项）该阈值分裂为二——宽策略需要内部平衡分支的末端（约为 c*_b 的 2.3–3.2 倍），坍缩策略需要近坍缩阈值（√(G/(G−1)) 倍更高）。在 n=10、G=16 的多数奖励下，只除优势项使释放折叠移动 2.07 倍（一致性奖励为 6.55 倍）。
- **有序 bins 上的机器辅助证明**：对 VLA 式有序 bin 动作（128–512 个 bin、高斯核平滑），用区间算术封闭所有舍入误差，证明了位点对称平衡分支的两个折叠（如 w=3 时 c*=0.147749、c_rel=0.291945）。
- **对称探索的极限**：证明对答案置换对称的奖励+探索流保持答案概率的排序（命题 A.13）——探索只能决定"是否、何时、多坚定地坍缩"，永远不能纠正错误的多数答案；纠错需要打破答案对称性的信号（如验证器）。
- **实践对照表**：把常见抗坍缩手段按"是否构成探索"分类——温度只重标时间、裁剪/Adam 不移动平衡点、KL 罚绕过 GRPO 除法、向均匀平滑/熵奖励/稀有答案奖励/泄漏才是真正的探索，并附设定探索系数的操作建议（附录 H）。

## Key Contributions

- **Three-regime structure**: models single-problem training as a continuous flow of a softmax policy's logits (the "tabular flow") under expected gradients, and proves the exploration coefficient is split by a lower and an upper boundary: below the lower one only collapsed policies are stable, above the upper one only policies spread over many answers, and in between (the "window") the initial policy decides the outcome. Closed forms for the agreement reward with uniform smoothing: lower boundary 1/n, upper 4(n−1)/n.
- **The "path of the reward"**: the reward reaches parameters through the gradient geometry, the optimizer preconditioner, and GRPO's division. Exploration that travels the whole path has equilibria and folds invariant to the learning rate and optimizer scaling under exact gradients; exploration that bypasses a step (e.g., a leak, or a KL term not divided by the group std) has thresholds that move — a leak's threshold lives in units of ε/η, so the mixing strength must scale with the learning rate.
- **GRPO's division splits the bonus threshold in two**: for a rare-answer bonus b_j = −log(N_aj/G), one threshold c*_b = 1/log(G−1) serves broad and collapsed policies before the division; after dividing only the advantages, a broad policy needs the end of the interior-equilibrium branch (2.3–3.2x c*_b) and a collapsed one a threshold √(G/(G−1)) times higher. For the majority reward (n=10, G=16), normalizing only the advantages moves the release fold by 2.07x (6.55x for the agreement reward) — so a coefficient tuned for one placement/reward does not transfer.
- **Certified folds on ordered bins**: for tokenized-VLA-style binned actions (rings/lines of 128–512 bins, Gaussian kernels w ∈ {1, 1.5, 3, 4, 8}), computer-assisted proofs with interval arithmetic enclose every rounding error and certify nondegenerate folds of the site-symmetric branch (e.g., c* = 0.147749 and c_rel = 0.291945 for w = 3).
- **Symmetric exploration cannot fix a wrong majority**: for any answer-symmetric reward plus symmetric exploration (uniform smoothing, entropy bonus, rarity bonus, with or without normalization), the order of answer probabilities is preserved (Proposition A.13) — exploration decides whether/when/how firmly a question commits, never which answer wins.
- **A practitioner's sorting table**: temperature rescales time (not exploration); clipping/Adam move no equilibrium; a KL penalty bypasses the GRPO division; uniform smoothing, entropy bonuses, rarity bonuses and leaks are genuine exploration, each with its threshold formula.

## Methodology

The paper works with a deliberately minimal object: the **tabular flow** z′ = V(z), the dynamics of the answer distribution of a single problem under the expected gradient of a softmax policy p = softmax(z). Action spaces are either n unordered answers (free-form or letter answers) or K ordered bins on a ring/line (as in tokenized VLA models, with clipping to end bins). Two gradient geometries are analyzed — natural policy gradient (NPG, Fisher metric) and vanilla policy gradient — which for the leave-one-out agreement reward unify into V_i = p_i^q (p_i − S_2), q ∈ {0,1}. Rewards studied: the agreement reward R_i = p_i (probability another sample agrees), the TTRL majority reward, and a rare-answer bonus b_j = −log(N_aj/G).

The central conceptual device is the **path of the reward**: reward → policy-gradient geometry → optimizer preconditioner → GRPO's division by the group reward std ς_G(p). Exploration is "on the path" if it passes through all transformations, "off the path" otherwise. Three placements are studied as experimental arms: arm C (bonus inside the reward, before the division), arm R (only advantages divided, bonus outside), arm U (undivided, Dr. GRPO style).

Analysis tools: linear stability of equilibria, exact sums on the invariant set of policies with one leading answer and equal others, replicator-dynamics / selection-mutation connections (Hofbauer's equation for pure coordination games, Potts-model spinodals), and interval-arithmetic computer-assisted proofs for the ordered-bin folds. The GRPO normalization is modeled in mean field by dividing the selection term by ς_G(p); Monte Carlo checks confirm this model to within ~4% (up to p_max = 0.94) and ~10–11% near collapse / for ten answers.

Experiments ascend toward practice in three steps: (1) a tanh network mapping 32 states to 256 ordered bins, PPO on the leave-one-out agreement reward with G=64; (2) a "letter test" where Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct + LoRA answers 64 ten-option MMLU-Pro questions with a single letter, so the answer distribution is exactly computable and the softmax is shared across questions; (3) free-form TTRL with Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B + LoRA on 128 MATH-500 problems deliberately chosen (with labels) so that half the initial pluralities are wrong — the regime where TTRL collapse is most damaging — with G=8, 200 steps, and collapse measured by F95e (share of covered eligible problems collapsed onto one answer, i.e., leading answer holding ≥95% of samples at step 200 among problems that started below 90%).

## Results & Highlights

- **Free-form TTRL on MATH-500 (Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, 128 problems, G=8, 6 seeds)**: the control (TTRL without exploration) collapsed, reaching F95e = 0.66–0.79 at step 200. Arm C (bonus before the division) at c_b = 0.6 — just 1.17x its predicted threshold 1/log(G−1) = 0.514 — kept F95e ≤ 0.065 from step 100 and 0.000–0.019 at step 200 in all six seeds. Arm R at the same coefficient (0.44x its predicted interior-branch end of 1.375) still collapsed in all five seeds, F95e = 0.62–0.77 — placement relative to the division, not coefficient size alone, decides the outcome.
- **Diversity vs. accuracy trade-off (same runs)**: at c_b = 0.6, arm C raised pass@32 to 0.710 vs 0.561 for the collapsed control (+0.149), but its pass@1 at temperature 1 was 0.365 vs 0.481 for the control (−0.116; untrained model 0.354) — the bonus keeps correct answers among the samples at the cost of majority-vote accuracy. With a process reward model (Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B) selecting among 32 new samples, best-of-32 accuracy was 0.569 with the bonus vs 0.526 without — but the untrained model still reached 0.603, above both.
- **Letter test (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, 64 MMLU-Pro ten-option questions)**: measured bonus threshold for arm C fell in (0.45, 0.6], bracketing the predicted 0.514; arm R fell in (1.2, 1.8]; the measured arm-R/arm-C ratio was 2.0–4.0 around the predicted 2.7. For the majority reward, normalizing only the advantages moved release by a factor of 1.89–2.13 between arms R and U, matching the predicted 2.07 (and clearly not 6.55, the agreement-reward value). Slow ramps released the collapsed start at the fold 0.2431 or within one evaluation in four seeds.
- **Network testbed (tanh net, K=256 bins, G=64)**: the threshold of the smoothing term c·CE(D_w p_old, p) varied by at most a factor of 1.09 across learning rates spanning 10x for both SGD and AdamW (w ∈ {1, 1.5, 3}), confirming learning-rate invariance on the path; the same smoothing applied as a separate step followed the leak law ω = ε/η to within 7%. Measured thresholds lay 3–29% above the certified folds (c* = 0.147749, c_rel = 0.291945 for w = 3). With only the advantages normalized, the window vanished — mean p_max ended within 0.024 at every c in four seeds — and dividing exploration too restored it.
- **Theory numbers**: for the majority reward with n=10, G=16, exact sums give boundaries 0.363 and 0.766 without normalization vs 0.0875 and 0.2431 for the leave-one-out agreement reward; the rarity-bonus threshold before division is c*_b = 1/log(G−1) (0.514 at G=8, 0.241 at G=64), rising to 2.3–3.2x that after the division (Table 1). Across 132 additional confirmation runs with new seeds, every broad start below a lower boundary collapsed and no run stayed collapsed above an upper boundary.

## Authors & Credibility

The paper is by Jaeho Hwang (Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence Research Institute, Ewha Womans University) and Uiwon Hwang (Department of Computer Science and Engineering, Ewha Womans University; corresponding author), posted as a preprint (arXiv:2611.01307v1, 31 Oct 2026, cs.LG). The work is a theory-first study in the evolutionary-dynamics-of-RL tradition: the authors connect their flow to replicator dynamics, Hofbauer's selection-mutation equation, and mean-field Potts spinodals, and cite the relevant prior results rather than claiming to reinvent them (e.g., Vojnovic & Yun's 2025 analysis of GRPO's KL coefficient). The mathematical claims are layered honestly: closed-form proofs for the agreement reward, exact sums restricted to the one-leading-answer invariant set for the majority reward, mean-field modeling plus Monte Carlo validation (within ~4%, degrading to ~11% near collapse) for the GRPO division, and interval-arithmetic certificates for the ordered-bin folds. Every experimental claim is stated with seeds, arm definitions, and deviations from the original TTRL code spelled out in appendices — an unusually high level of self-reporting. The paper explicitly flags which of its results are proved, which are numerical, and which are only drift-sign arguments.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The three-regime picture itself (collapsed / window / spread) echoes known replicator-dynamics results, and the authors say so — the genuinely new contributions are the path-of-the-reward invariance principle, the closed-form bonus threshold 1/log(G−1) with its 2–3x shift across the GRPO division, and the interval-arithmetic fold certificates on ordered bins. The before/after-normalization threshold split is a crisp, checkable, and previously unstated finding with direct practical bite.
- **Rigor**: high on the theory side, with the scope of each proof stated plainly. But the majority-reward and bonus thresholds hold only on the invariant set of "one leading answer, equal others"; the GRPO normalization is a mean-field surrogate (population std) validated by Monte Carlo rather than derived from sampled groups; and on ordered bins only the folds are certified, with the claim that data collapse onto the certified branch left numerical.
- **Reproducibility**: strong — exact models (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, LoRA rank 32/alpha 64), problem counts (64 MMLU-Pro, 128 MATH-500), group sizes, step counts, seeds, optimizer, and every deviation from the original TTRL recipe (no KL term, population instead of unbiased std, −0.1 advantage for answerless samples, evaluation temperature) are documented in appendices, with 132 extra confirmation runs reported.
- **Significance caveats**: the headline free-form result is bought on a stress-test subset — 128 MATH-500 problems selected with labels so that half the initial pluralities are wrong, a set on which TTRL itself does not raise majority accuracy (the authors admit comparison with published TTRL numbers is therefore invalid). More damaging for practice: under PRM best-of-32 selection, both trained variants (0.569 with bonus, 0.526 without) score below the untrained model (0.603), so the whole training loop currently destroys value on this subset even while it raises pass@32. The pass@1 drop (0.481 → 0.365) means the method trades the metric TTRL is usually run for in exchange for diversity that only pays off if a downstream selector — which here still loses to no training at all — can exploit it.
- **Red flags / missing pieces**: no head-to-head baselines against existing anti-collapse methods (entropy bonuses from Zhou et al. 2025 or Liu et al. 2025a are discussed in a related-work appendix but never run); only 1.5B models, one model family, G=8, and 200 steps in free-form TTRL; the coefficient scan used 3 seeds and no collapsed starts; no VLA experiment despite the ordered-bins theory being motivated partly by VLA tokenization; and the shared LoRA adapter couples questions (they committed onto the same letter in the letter test), violating the one-state-per-problem assumption the theory needs — the authors disclose this but do not resolve it.

## Strengths

- Delivers genuinely closed-form, memorable thresholds (1/n, 4(n−1)/n, 1/log(G−1)) in a literature where exploration coefficients are otherwise tuned by trial and error.
- The "path of the reward" framing gives a single principle that predicts when a coefficient transfers across learning rates, optimizers, and normalization variants — and the experiments confirm the invariances (≤1.09x drift over a 10x learning-rate range) and the shifts (arm R vs C ratios 2.0–4.0 around the predicted 2.7) at the predicted magnitudes.
- Three-tier experimental ladder (tabular testbed → exact-distribution letter test → free-form TTRL) is a model of how to validate theory: each tier tests exactly the prediction the previous one could not.
- Unusual honesty: the paper publishes the negative result (bonus lowers pass@1; PRM selection still below untrained), proves what exploration cannot do (fix a wrong majority, Proposition A.13), and documents every deviation from the TTRL baseline.
- Table 2 (which common tricks are/are not exploration) and the Appendix H recipe are immediately actionable for practitioners tuning GRPO-family anti-collapse terms.

## Limitations

- Theory covers one problem at a time with answer-symmetric rewards; real runs share parameters across questions (the letter-test adapter committed across questions, switching leading letters — outside the flow's invariant set), and many practical anti-collapse methods act at the token level or asymmetrically, outside the analyzed class.
- The GRPO-division analysis is a mean-field model (population standard deviation) checked by Monte Carlo to within ~4–11%, not a proof for sampled groups; the drift-sign arguments for the bonus near collapse are not full stability proofs.
- Free-form experiments are small: one 1.5B model family, 128 label-selected problems, G=8, 200 steps, 3–6 seeds, no collapsed starts in the scan, and no comparison against any prior anti-collapse method as a baseline.
- The central practical promise — kept correct answers can be recovered by a downstream selector — is not yet realized: PRM best-of-32 with the bonus (0.569) still trails the untrained model (0.603) on the stress-test subset.
- No VLA experiment despite ordered-bin actions and clipping being motivated by tokenized VLA models; full-rank network assumptions were not checked for the LoRA adapters.

## Relevance

直接命中"RL Post-Training Dynamics & Failures"方向：本文系统刻画了自一致性奖励（TTRL/GRPO 族）后训练中的策略坍缩这一失效模式的动力学机理，给出可操作的探索系数阈值与"奖励路径"不变性原理，可用于坍缩的检测与缓解设计。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the before/after-division threshold split (1/log(G−1) vs 2.3–3.2x) persist at 7B+ scale, with larger groups G and the unbiased std used by the actual TTRL codebase, where the predicted factor becomes √G rather than √(G/(G−1))?
- Can the pass@1-vs-diversity trade-off be broken by annealing the bonus coefficient — starting above the lower boundary and decaying toward it — so the policy commits late to a better-explored majority instead of its initial one?
- Since symmetric exploration provably cannot correct a wrong majority, what is the joint theory for symmetric exploration plus an asymmetric signal (weak verifier, PRM, or differential smoothing), and can it push PRM best-of-32 above the untrained baseline?
- How do the boundaries move when questions share an adapter and collapse together (the cross-task coupling the letter test exhibited and concurrent work models) — does the effective n become the number of questions, tightening the window?
- Do the certified ordered-bin folds predict collapse thresholds for real tokenized VLA models, where bins are action discretizations rather than free-form answers?
