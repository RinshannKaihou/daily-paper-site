The harness — not the context window — destroys agent evidence: 34% of tool results were cut into broken JSON before reaching the model.

Why this matters

Replaces "is the window big enough?" with "how much evidence does your harness destroy in transit?" — a defect class measurable in any tool-augmented agent.

可迁移到任何工具增强型 Agent 的 harness 设计与评测——包括编码 Agent 里被 routinely 截断的编译器日志与 CI 输出。

⚠ Critical edge

Generalization is asserted more than measured: one harness, one domain (kernel-trace diagnosis), one model — and the defect was injected by the authors themselves.

No significance tests anywhere; the quiet-culprit accuracy claim rests on 3/6 vs 0/5 runs — which the authors themselves label noisy.

Proposed instrumentation boundary
  • Pipeline instrumentation — each boundary gets a concrete unit and a loss count from the harness's own field data.
  • Field measurement — 720-run deployed evaluation, 11,189 tool results, failure rates per boundary.
  • Deterministic replay — 864 recorded results (plus one 33,370-char production trace) replayed through shipped vs repaired cuts, no model in the loop.
  • Registered controlled experiment — 145 runs, temperature 0, six configs including reversed-key and order-only controls; predictions written before each wave; a lenient scorer caught in a 46-run audit and replaced by a strict commitment-based one.

03

Strengths

  • Names an invisible failure class — destructive delivery by positional truncation — that window-size research and failure taxonomies structurally miss: run logs record what a tool delivered, not what it produced.
  • Deterministic, model-free measurement — replays and delivery cliffs are cheap and CI-friendly; each boundary failure becomes mechanically testable.
  • Exemplary experimental hygiene — registered predictions, a logged refutation, position-vs-content controls, and a caught-and-replaced lenient scorer.
  • Actionable — two production frameworks shown to carry the same defect; safe whole-line cutting demonstrated; "yield" gives teams a third number to watch.
  • Public replication package — drivers, scorer, results, replay corpus, and registered plan (Zenodo).
04

Limitations

  • Generalization unmeasured — one harness, one domain, one model; the defect was authored by the authors, not found in the wild; no end-to-end measurement in any surveyed third-party framework.
  • Causal question unsettled — the controlled experiment refuted the accuracy-drop prediction; the 3/6 vs 0/5 quiet-culprit replication is too small to be decisive.
  • Small samples throughout — 5 recordings × 3 repeats, 145 controlled runs, 6 vs 5 production runs — with no significance testing.
  • Yield is a narrow proxy — counts work that reached the decision, not evidence relevance; the S3 analysis rests on a single before/after run.
  • The proposed remedy is unvalidated — the paging-on-request safety net never fired in any run.
05

Where it hides

2 / 7 surveyed frameworks cut positionally: langchain-core 1.6.12 keeps the first 500 characters of tool output; smolagents 1.26.0 deletes the middle of observations over 20k chars. Neither inspects structure.
2.5% of calls were affected at all — which is exactly why the defect survives inspection: 50% of process-listing calls and 43% of the agent's own analysis outputs broke, but the damage is rare per-call.
84% median wall-clock spent waiting on the model (10 uncapped production coding-agent runs) — a data-destruction defect barely moves the latency metric teams watch; handing over a written procedure masked it at 35% of the tokens.
06

Questions for further exploration

  • Does the compensation tax (yield 0.61→0.31 at equal accuracy) generalize to build-test-fix coding agents with routinely truncated compiler and CI logs?
  • How many runs would settle whether destructive delivery causally kills accuracy, not just data?
  • Can paging-on-request recover quiet-culprit accuracy without the compensation tax — and be made to fire reliably on 33k-char results?
  • Do positional cuts compound with "lost in the middle" attention biases when a field survives but lands in a weakly attended region?
  • Can per-boundary loss counts become standard CI assertions — would langchain-core's 500-char default fail such a test?
  • Does the S2 step-budget wall (51% stop at the limit) amplify S1 loss as workers burn budget re-fetching cut results?
