> **一句话总结：** DWISE 针对 diffusion LLM 的服务调度提出：以"剩余 prefill 块数 + 预估去噪轮数"这一统一块级工作量排序请求（SRPT 式抢占），用 LightGBM 在准入时初估、再用第一轮去噪后的隐藏状态经一个 1.79M 参数生存模型一次性校准；在 SGLang 上对 5 个模型（LLaDA2.x / SDAR-8B / DiffusionGemma）的突发负载下，平均端到端延迟较 SGLang 默认 FCFS+prefill 优先降低 5.4×（最高 14.9×），吞吐最高提升 2.2×，任务精度保持在 1 分以内。

> **TL;DR:** Diffusion LLM serving inherits FCFS admission and prefill priority from AR serving, and both waste dLLM speed: on LLaDA2.1-mini, 95% of SGLang's rounds are prefill-only carrying 1.1 of 128 batch slots while denoising work varies 2–279 rounds across requests. DWISE, implemented as a runtime patch to SGLang v0.5.20, ranks requests by remaining work R_i = P_i + r̂_i (exact unprocessed prefill blocks + estimated remaining denoising rounds), mixes prefill and denoising blocks in every round, and refines r̂_i once from the first denoising round's hidden states (Spearman ρ 0.87 vs 0.74 for prompt features). On a GH200 across five models and burst/burst+Poisson traces, it cuts mean latency vs SGLang by 4.7–14.9× (geometric mean 5.4×, e.g., LLaDA2.1-mini burst 545.5 s → 76.9 s; SDAR-8B 9053 s → 609 s), doubles throughput (3.76 → 8.21 req/s), and beats all five retrained AR-era baselines (MLFQ, SSJF, S3, LTR, TRAIL), most of which are slower than SGLang itself; gains shrink to 1.1× on DiffusionGemma where the two phases cannot share a round, and 44% of long-context requests individually finish later under SRPT-style reordering.

## 核心贡献

- **识别出 AR 服务策略对 dLLM 的双重失配**：FCFS 准入忽略了 dLLM 解码成本的高度异质性（同一模型上不同请求的去噪轮数相差超过 100 倍，LLaDA2.1-mini 上 25% 的请求 ≤14 轮、中位数 46 轮、p90 达 149 轮），造成严重的队头阻塞；而 AR 服务中用于保护首 token 延迟的 prefill 优先策略，在 dLLM 上让大量仅跑 prompt 的轮次几乎空转——SGLang 有 95% 的轮次只含 prefill 块，平均每轮仅占 1.1/128 个槽位，却消耗 78% 的墙钟时间。
- **提出首个按"去噪轮数"而非"输出 token 数"度量剩余工作量的 dLLM 调度器**：剩余工作 R_i = P_i + r̂_i（未处理 prefill 块数 + 预估剩余去噪轮数），由于块扩散模型中一个 prefill 块和一个去噪块恰好各占批量前向的一个槽位、成本相同，DWISE 让两个阶段混合共享每一轮，并以服务轮为时间片做抢占式 SRPT；被抢占请求保留 KV 缓存在 GPU 上无需重算。
- **发现"第一轮去噪即揭示剩余工作量"**：第一轮去噪后的最终层隐藏状态与真实剩余去噪轮数的 Spearman ρ 达 0.868（LLaDA2.1-mini 五折交叉验证），显著高于 prompt 特征的 0.740 和 prompt 隐藏状态的 0.789；在同类请求内部，prompt 特征几乎无排序能力（ρ=0.16），而精化估计仍达 0.64。由于第一轮本来就要执行，该信号零额外模型开销。
- **两阶段轻量预测器**：准入端 LightGBM（100 棵树、≤15 叶，仅词数/换行数/chat 模板后 token 数 3 个特征，log 空间回归）每请求 0.42 ms；精化端为离散时间生存模型（3 个 seed 平均的两层 MLP，宽度 256，共 1.79M 参数 ≈ 模型的 0.01%），挂在前向计算流上、在既有轮末同步点读取输出，每轮仅约 0.8 ms GPU 时间（<95 ms 中位轮时的 1%），并用保序映射校准使精化/准入估计同尺度（中位预测/实际比从 1.16 修到 1.00）。
- **全面的系统性评估**：在 SGLang v0.5.20 上以运行时补丁实现（不改模型、内核、KV 管理器，CUDA-graph 路径保持开启），覆盖 3 个家族 5 个模型、burst 与 burst+Poisson 两种到达模式、6 个基线（SGLang 默认、MLFQ、SSJF、S3、LTR、TRAIL，其中 4 个学习型基线在相同 2,048 条训练请求上重训），并附多 GPU 张量并行、输出预算扫描、逐类别泛化、开销分解等消融。

## Key Contributions

- **Diagnoses why AR-inherited serving policies fail for dLLMs.** FCFS ignores that denoising cost is wildly heterogeneous (2–279 rounds across requests on LLaDA2.1-mini; 25% ≤ 14 rounds, median 46, p90 149), causing head-of-line blocking; prefill priority — a deliberate AR trade that yields a first token in the same step — keeps its cost but loses its benefit for dLLMs, since prefill-only rounds idle most of the batch (95% of SGLang's rounds on LLaDA2.1-mini are prefill-only, carrying 1.1 of 128 slots on average and eating 78% of wall-clock time).
- **First dLLM scheduler that orders requests by remaining work measured in denoising rounds, not output tokens.** Remaining work is R_i = P_i + r̂_i — exactly known unprocessed prefill blocks plus estimated remaining denoising rounds. Because in block-diffusion models a prefill block and a denoising block each fill one slot of the same batched forward pass at equal cost, DWISE mixes both phases in every round and applies preemptive SRPT with the serving round as quantum; displaced requests keep their KV cache on-GPU and resume without recomputation. Residency is capped at k·B with KV reservations (k = 8), guaranteeing no overcommit or deadlock.
- **Shows the first denoising round reveals what the prompt cannot.** The final-layer hidden state after round 1 correlates with true remaining work at Spearman ρ = 0.868 on LLaDA2.1-mini (5-fold CV), vs 0.740 for prompt features and 0.789 for the prompt hidden state; within a request category, the refined estimate reaches ρ = 0.64 where prompt features manage only 0.16 (and −0.06 on held-out categories). The signal is free — round 1 is a forward pass the request runs anyway, and the median request still has 45 of its 46 rounds ahead after it.
- **Two-stage lightweight workload estimation.** Admission: a LightGBM regressor (100 trees, ≤15 leaves) over three prompt features (word count, newline count, post-chat-template token count), predicting rounds in log space at 0.42 ms/request. Refinement: a discrete-time survival model — an ensemble of three 2-layer MLPs (width 256, GELU, dropout 0.2; 1.79M parameters, 0.01% of the model) emitting one hazard logit per future round — evaluated in one batched GEMM on the forward pass's stream and read at the existing end-of-round sync, at ~0.8 ms GPU time per round (<1% of the 95 ms median round). An isotonic mapping aligns the refined scale with the admission scale (median predicted-to-actual ratio 1.16 → 1.00 without changing ρ).
- **Thorough systems evaluation.** Implemented as a runtime patch on SGLang v0.5.20's dLLM path (model, denoising kernels, KV manager, and CUDA-graph capture untouched; CPU-side scheduler + GPU-side refinement hook), evaluated on one GH200 across five models from three families (LLaDA2.0/2.1/2.2-mini 16B MoE, SDAR-8B dense, DiffusionGemma-26B-A4B MoE) against SGLang default plus five retrained AR-era schedulers, with ablations isolating mixed-phase batching vs ranking signal, refinement frequency, resident-set cap, multi-GPU TP/EP, output budgets up to 2,048 tokens, and per-category generalization.

## Methodology

DWISE is a runtime scheduler for block-diffusion LLM serving, resting on two measured properties: (1) after one denoising round, remaining work becomes far more predictable than from the prompt (ρ 0.74 → 0.87); (2) prefill blocks and denoising blocks occupy the same slot type in a round and cost the same, so neither phase needs priority.

**Round-based workload accounting.** A request's remaining work is R_i = P_i + r̂_i, where P_i is the exact count of unprocessed prompt blocks and r̂_i the estimated remaining denoising rounds. This supplies the single scale SRPT presupposes, letting DWISE compare a request still in prefill with one already denoising (which term dominates depends on block size: SDAR-8B's 4-token blocks make prefill dominate for long contexts).

**In-path workload estimation.** At admission, the LightGBM predictor estimates total rounds from three size features (29 µs inference; 0.42 ms from raw prompt text — 9–18× cheaper than the DistilBERT/BERT-base/OPT-125M encoders of S3/SSJF/LTR). After the first denoising round only, the diffusion-involved predictor refines r̂_i: mean-pooled final-layer hidden rows of the round (the model's parallel draft of the whole block) concatenated with normalized prompt length and output budget, fed to the survival-model MLP ensemble (hazard logits up to K = 279 rounds; refined estimate = mean of the induced distribution), calibrated by an isotonic mapping fitted on training data so admission and refined estimates rank consistently. One refinement suffices — refining every round raises preemptions/request from 0.34 to 1.14 and mean latency from 48.5 to 51.0 s.

**Preemptive mixed-phase batching.** Each serving round packs B block slots (B = 128; 16 for DiffusionGemma) with the B resident requests of smallest R_i, prompt or denoising blocks alike. Ranking is recomputed every round; outranked requests are paused with KV cache and partially denoised blocks kept on GPU. A request becomes resident only if it ranks in the top B, the resident set is below k·B (k = 8, ≤1,024 residents), and its worst-case KV reservation fits the pool — so preemption never deadlocks memory. Between refinements r̂_i decrements by one per completed round (clamped at zero), so underestimated requests run to completion.

**Implementation.** A runtime patch, not a fork: a CPU-side scheduler intervenes at admission, batch formation (removing SGLang's prefill-first phase barrier), and result processing; the GPU refinement path registers hidden-state hooks before CUDA-graph capture so graph replay stays enabled. Total overhead is 2.1% of the median round (0.96 ms ranking+admission, 0.20 ms resident reordering, 0.80 ms predictor, 0.04 ms readout).

**Experimental setup.** One NVIDIA GH200 (132 SMs, 96 GB HBM3, 72-core Grace CPU) per instance, SGLang v0.5.20, bf16, greedy decoding, 256-token output budget. Workload: 4,096 serving requests (ShareGPT chat 1,085, MBPP 505, GSM8K 345, MMLU-Pro 637, ARC-C 312, MGSM 258, MATH-L5 421, IFEval 296, HumanEval 87, MCQ 319+156, synthetic ~10K-token multi-needle long-context 150) plus a prompt-disjoint 2,048-request set used to train DWISE and retrain SSJF/S3/LTR/TRAIL with FCFS-profiled labels. Arrivals: burst (all at t = 0) and burst+Poisson (half at t = 0, rest at λ above service rate). Baselines: SGLang (FCFS + prefill priority), FastServe-style MLFQ, SSJF, S3, LTR, TRAIL — all learned baselines on the same training data and all on SGLang's prefill-first batch former, as originally designed. Metrics: mean/p50/p99 end-to-end latency, throughput, Spearman ρ of workload prediction, task accuracy; mean of three runs with fixed-seed identical arrival traces.

## Results & Highlights

- **Mean latency (Table 2):** DWISE cuts mean end-to-end latency over SGLang on LLaDA2.1-mini from 545.5 s to 76.9 s under burst (−86%, 7.1×) and from 535.9 s to 48.5 s under burst+Poisson (11.1×); on SDAR-8B from 9,053 s to 609 s (14.9×). Across all ten model×arrival settings the geometric-mean speedup is 5.4×; LLaDA2.x gains are 4.7–7.1× (burst) and 6.2–11.1× (burst+Poisson); DiffusionGemma gains only 1.1× (292.4 vs 325.9 s burst) because its prompt encoder runs in a separate pass, so only reordering applies.
- **Throughput:** on LLaDA2.x, DWISE raises throughput from 3.34–3.90 to 6.39–8.21 req/s (up to 2.2×); on SDAR-8B 0.24 → 0.25 req/s (throughput-bound by long requests). On DiffusionGemma throughput is 6.07 vs SGLang's 6.22 req/s (−2.4%), i.e., ordering alone buys little when phases cannot share a round.
- **Tail and median latency:** p50 improves 6.2–11× (burst) and 14–42× (burst+Poisson) on LLaDA2.x, and 43×/73× on SDAR-8B; p99 improves 1.9–2.3× on LLaDA2.x and 1.3× on SDAR-8B. DWISE also lowers mean TTFT on LLaDA2.1-mini by 7.8–13.0× — beating SGLang's prefill-first policy at the very metric it was meant to protect.
- **Task accuracy preserved:** within one point everywhere, e.g., LLaDA2.1-mini 44.2 → 44.3%, SDAR-8B 55.5 → 55.4%, DiffusionGemma 51.3 → 51.2% (burst); LLaDA2.0-mini actually improves 45.4 → 46.2%.
- **Why it wins (round composition, Table 3):** on LLaDA2.1-mini, 94.6% of SGLang's 63,926 rounds are prefill-only (96.1% idle slots, 5.0 filled slots/round) and take 78.2% of wall-clock; DWISE's 2,819 rounds are only 3.0% prefill-only, fill 113.2 slots/round (11.6% idle), and spend 11.2% of time in prefill-only rounds — finishing the workload in 2.8K vs 64K rounds.
- **Ablation (LLaDA2.1-mini, burst+Poisson, Figure 8):** mixed-phase batching alone gives 2.35× over SGLang; + ranking by prefill blocks only (r̂ = 0) → 8.15×; + admission estimate r̂ → 10.33×; + first-round refinement → 11.06× (84% of the 13.10× oracle that ranks by true denoising rounds; the predictors close 70% of the oracle gap).
- **Prior LLM schedulers do not transfer:** MLFQ, SSJF, LTR, TRAIL run at 0.60–0.96× SGLang's speed on LLaDA2.x/SDAR-8B (e.g., TRAIL 661.1 vs 545.5 s on LLaDA2.1-mini burst) and S3 gains at most 1.4×. The failure is structural: output-length proxies misorder dLLM work — code/math requests emit more tokens than chat (242 vs 215) yet need 2.6× fewer rounds (45 vs 115), and SSJF/LTR correlate with true work only by ranking (Hard)MCQ first (23% of requests, 3.1% of work; ρ ≈ −0.02/−0.07 on the other 96.9%), raising code/math mean latency from 524 s (SGLang) to 842/925 s. Given DWISE's batch former, every baseline gets 2.1–4.1× faster, but DWISE remains 2.5–3.3× faster than the best.
- **Prediction quality and generalization:** refined ρ = 0.89–0.891 vs admission 0.71–0.716 on LLaDA2.1-mini; holding out the MCQ category drops admission ρ from 0.72 to 0.36 but refined stays 0.89, and end-to-end retraining without MCQ costs only 7.6% mean latency (47.3 → 50.9 s) while still being 10.5× faster than SGLang.
- **Robustness and cost:** speedups hold at 6.7–12.2× under TP=2/4 and TP=4+EP=4 multi-GPU serving, and at 4.45× (burst) / 5.18× (burst+Poisson) with a 2,048-token output budget (refined ρ 0.885–0.894 at every budget); under pure Poisson arrivals below SGLang's saturation DWISE gains 1.9–4.5× mean latency, and 25–31× once SGLang is overloaded (λ = 7.5–15 req/s). Total scheduler overhead is 2.1% of the 95 ms median round; admission prediction is 0.42 ms/request vs 3.8–7.6 ms for S3/SSJF/LTR's text encoders.

## Authors & Credibility

The team is from the University of Bristol (Lie Li, Wen Li, Junxiao Shen, Guosheng Hu — correspondence) and the University of Oxford (Jindong Gu). The work is grounded in real serving infrastructure: it patches SGLang v0.5.20's actual dLLM serving path rather than a simulator, evaluates five real open-weight block-diffusion models (three LLaDA2.x-mini releases, SDAR-8B-Chat, Google's DiffusionGemma-26B-A4B), and reports the mean of three runs over fixed-seed identical arrival traces for all policies. The paper cites the classic scheduling literature it builds on (SJF 1956, SRPT 1968, MLFQ 1962) and the LLM-serving line (vLLM, Sarathi-Serve, FastServe, S3, LTR, TRAIL), and its empirical claims are internally cross-checked (e.g., the ablation's component-wise speedups reproduce Table 2's end-to-end numbers; appendix D explains precisely why each baseline fails). The code is promised to be released publicly. Confidence is high, with the caveat that results come from a single GH200-class device and synthetic arrival patterns.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine and well-scoped. Prior dLLM serving work targeted memory, KV refresh, or SLO attainment, not ordering; DWISE's two insights — measuring work in denoising rounds and reading the first round's hidden states as a free workload signal — are new, simple, and clearly argued. The survival-model refinement with isotonic calibration is a nice, honest piece of engineering.
- **Rigor:** Strong on measurement hygiene (identical traces across policies, three-run means, oracle upper bound at 13.10× quantifying headroom, per-category held-out retraining). The batch-former-vs-ranking decomposition (Table 4) is exactly the right control and shows most of the headline gain comes from mixed-phase batching + remaining-work ranking together.
- **Weak spots in the evaluation.** (1) All baselines were designed for AR serving and predict output length; the paper demonstrates they fail on dLLMs, but there is no other dLLM-native scheduling baseline — the "first" claim is safe but the comparison set is inevitably strawman-adjacent, and all learned baselines run on SGLang's prefill-first batch former "as originally designed," which the authors themselves show cripples preemptive policies (MLFQ/TRAIL hold up to 1,024 residents while prefill-only rounds stall them all). (2) The 14.9× headline on SDAR-8B is inflated by a pathology: synthetic ~10K-token multi-needle prompts are 3.7% of requests but 40.7% of the work (2,684 prefill blocks each), a regime where any non-prefill-first policy beats a naive default enormously. (3) Single-machine GH200 evaluation; TP=4+EP=4 is as far as it goes, and SGLang's dLLM path lacks pipeline parallelism entirely.
- **Reproducibility:** Good in principle — stdlib-free pipeline details, exact hyperparameters (B, k, KV pool sizes, λ, seeds), and promised code — but at review time the code is not yet public, and the method needs per-model, per-budget predictor retraining (2,048 profiled FCFS requests, <3 min training) which is an operational cost the paper could state more prominently.
- **Significance:** High for the dLLM serving niche; as dLLMs move toward production, the finding that AR scheduling policies actively waste their speed is actionable now.
- **Red flags / overclaiming:** The abstract's "5.4× on average and up to 14.9×" is fair (geometric mean over ten settings), but the DiffusionGemma result (1.1× mean latency, p99 unchanged or worse) shows the headline mechanism does not apply to architectures whose prompt encoding is a separate pass — worth remembering before generalizing. SRPT starvation is acknowledged and quantified: 44% (burst) / 39% (burst+Poisson) of long-context requests individually finish later than under SGLang, though their p99/max still halve.

## Strengths

- Identifies a clean, previously unexploited structural fact — prefill and denoising blocks cost the same and share a round in block-diffusion models — and exploits it with minimal machinery (a runtime patch, no kernel or memory-manager changes).
- The workload-prediction result is independently interesting: one round of hidden states lifts rank correlation with remaining work from 0.74 to 0.87 overall and from 0.16 to 0.64 within a category, generalizing to unseen categories (ρ 0.54) where prompt features are useless (−0.06).
- Overhead is engineered down to near-noise (2.1% of a round; 1.79M-parameter predictor on the forward stream; no extra synchronization point), so the gains are not bought with GPU time.
- Honest, mechanism-level diagnosis of why each baseline fails (output tokens anti-correlate with denoising rounds for code/math; MCQ = 23% of requests but 3.1% of work), plus an oracle bound that quantifies remaining headroom (84% captured).
- Consistent wins across every model, arrival pattern, GPU count, and output budget tested, with task accuracy within one point.

## Limitations

- Architecture-dependent benefit: on DiffusionGemma, whose prompts are encoded in a separate pass, mixed-phase batching is impossible, so DWISE can only reorder — mean latency gains fall to 1.1×, throughput dips slightly below SGLang (6.07 vs 6.22 req/s), and p99 rises under burst+Poisson.
- SRPT fairness: long-context requests are pushed last; 44%/39% of them finish later individually than under SGLang (though their tail still improves because SGLang also delays them). No fairness/SLO-aware variant is explored.
- Evaluation scope: one GH200 per instance, no pipeline parallelism (unsupported by SGLang's dLLM path), no real production traces — the burst and burst+Poisson patterns with rate above service capacity are precisely the backlog regimes where remaining-work scheduling shines most; at low load gains shrink to 1.9–4.5×.
- Per-model, per-output-budget predictor training is required (2,048 profiled requests each time), and the long-context portion of the workload is synthetic multi-needle data rather than organic traffic.
- Code not yet released at submission time ("will be released publicly"), so the SGLang-patch claims cannot yet be independently verified.

## Relevance

如果你关注高效 LLM 推理与扩散语言模型（dLLM）落地，这篇论文直接回答了"为什么 dLLM 在现有 serving 框架里跑不出应有速度"这一系统问题，并给出可落地的 SGLang 运行时补丁（平均 5.4× 延迟降低）；其"用模型自身第一轮去噪隐藏状态免费预测剩余工作量"的思路，对任何需要成本预估的推理调度/路由系统都有借鉴意义。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does DWISE interact with decoding-level accelerations that change round counts dynamically (e.g., KV-cache reuse, confidence-threshold tuning, or parallel-decoding variants beyond LLaDA's), where the refinement's one-shot estimate could go stale faster?
- Can the first-round hidden-state signal predict more than round count — e.g., per-block difficulty or likely revision hotspots — and would scheduling on a finer granularity (block-level SRPT within a request) help?
- What is the right fairness mechanism? Could an SLO- or age-boosted variant of R_i keep the 5.4× mean-latency win while guaranteeing individual long-context requests do not regress?
- Does the approach transfer to diffusion models with remasking/self-correction (where earlier blocks can be re-denoised) or to non-block diffusion LLMs where output length and round count are entangled differently?
- Could the 2,048-request per-model training set be shrunk further, or the predictors shared across a model family, given that the hidden-state signal is model-specific but the survival-model architecture is fixed?
