> **一句话总结：** 从 SGD 的离散更新出发、用 Kramers–Moyal 展开并刻意保留通常被丢弃的 O(δt²) 项，作者推导出参数均值 m 与协方差 C 的耦合连续时间方程——"随机学习流（SLF）"：SGD 的均值在带 ½Tr(C⟨H⟩) 隐式正则项的有效损失上下降，而协方差在曲率 ηλ=2 处精确失稳（这是一阶 SDE 近似在结构上缺失的不稳定性），从而把 edge of stability、self-stabilization 与 central flow 统一为同一组方程的不动点/极限，并在深线性网络与二分类 MNIST 的数百副本 SGD 集成上定量验证（协方差迹预测误差 <10%），同时明确刻画了理论在高噪声、强非高斯（重尾）区域的失效边界。

> **TL;DR:** Replaces the gradient-flow approximation of SGD with "stochastic learning flow" (SLF): coupled continuous-time equations for the mean and covariance of the parameter distribution, derived from the discrete update via a Kramers–Moyal expansion that keeps the O(δt²) term, to first order in the learning rate η. The mean descends an effective loss ⟨L⟩ + ½Tr(C⟨H⟩) — covariance-times-curvature implicit regularization that pushes training away from sharp minima — and the covariance is exactly unstable at sharpness ηλ = 2, the edge-of-stability threshold that first-order SDE approximations (valid only for ηλ < 1) structurally miss. On a two-layer deep linear network (800-replica SGD ensembles, η ∈ 0.05–0.20, B ∈ 4–256), SLF's loss-curve NRMSE stays around 10⁻³–10⁻⁴ where the gradient-flow baseline degrades to ~10⁻²–10⁻¹ in the large-η/small-B corner, and its update-direction alignment stays ≳0.9 where gradient flow drops below 0.6. On a 4,753-parameter GELU network trained on binary MNIST (0 vs 1), SLF predicts the parameter-covariance trace within 10% across two orders of magnitude of variance, while dropping the covariance term raises mean-trajectory error (median ratio 1.037 → 1.134 as B shrinks 1024 → 256). Full-batch EoS (λ₁* = 2/η) emerges as a stationary point of coupled sharpness–covariance dynamics, and batch-to-batch curvature fluctuations lower the threshold to λ₀ = 2/[η(1+κ/B)], matching one-step probe measurements within ±0.5% with zero fitted parameters. Honest failure mode: predictions break exactly where the parameter distribution becomes non-Gaussian (heavy tails, Hill index < 2).

## 核心贡献

- **一般随机更新规则的二阶矩方程（SLF）**：从 θ(t+δt) = θ(t) + ηf(θ) 出发，对主方程做 Kramers–Moyal 展开，但保留非标准的 O(δt²) 项并取 δt = η，得到在算子 Dₜ = ∂ₜ + (η/2)∂ₜ² 下演化的均值–协方差闭合方程组（Gaussian 闭合、η 的一阶精度）。框架不限于 SGD：batch 采样噪声、RL 探索噪声等任意随机更新都适用，也不假设无限宽网络。
- **SGD 的有效损失与隐式正则**：均值方程等价于在 L_eff = ⟨L⟩ + ½Tr[C⟨H⟩] 上做梯度下降——协方差×Hessian 的正则项把解推离高正曲率区域、导向平坦谷底，为逃离鞍点与尖锐极小值提供了明确机制（协方差经 −½C⟨∂³L⟩ 项反过来压低尖锐度）。
- **精确的 2/η 协方差失稳（区别于 SDE 近似的关键）**：在 Hessian 特征基下 DₜC̃ₖₗ = (ηλₖλₗ − λₖ − λₗ)C̃ₖₗ；对二次损失这正是单步二阶矩变化的精确表达，因此失稳点 ηλ = 2 被精确定位。相比之下，SDE 修正项是 ηλ 的幂级数、只在 ηλ < 1 收敛且不含该失稳——这解释了为什么 SDE 替身不在 SGD 稳定的地方稳定。
- **统一 edge-of-stability 理论族**：全批量极限下，沿最尖锐方向 v₁ 的耦合动力学 (Dₜσ₁² = λ₁(ηλ₁−2)σ₁², Dₜλ₁ = S₁/2 − (g₁/2)σ₁² + (η/2)Q₁) 有不动点 λ₁* = 2/η、σ₁²* = (2S₁+ηQ₁)/g₁——self-stabilization（Damian et al.）与 central flow（Cohen et al.）都被恢复为极限/约束情形；有限批量下阈值下移至 λ₀ = (2/η)/(1+κ/B) − ηd/(2B)，重现"SGD 悬停在 2/η 之下"的经验观测，并新增了梯度噪声与损失三阶导数耦合的修正项。
- **严格的实证验证与失效边界刻画**：在深线性网络（鞍点到鞍点学习）与非线性网络（二分类 MNIST）上，用数百个同初始条件副本的 SGD 集成逐项验证均值轨迹、损失曲线、协方差动力学与隐式正则项的量级，并用第三矩 / Hill 尾指数定量刻画了 Gaussian 闭合失效的位置。

## Key Contributions

- **SLF moment equations for a general stochastic update rule**: Starting from the discrete update θ(t+δt) = θ(t) + ηf(θ), a Kramers–Moyal expansion of the master equation that deliberately keeps the usually-discarded O(δt²) term (with δt = η) yields closed coupled equations for the mean m and covariance C under the operator Dₜ = ∂ₜ + (η/2)∂ₜ², to first order in η, under a Gaussian closure. The framework covers any stochastic update (SGD batch noise, RL exploration noise) and makes no infinite-width assumption.
- **Effective loss with dynamic implicit regularization for SGD**: The mean descends L_eff = ⟨L⟩ + ½Tr[C⟨H⟩]; the covariance-times-curvature term pushes solutions away from high positive curvature toward flat valleys, giving a mechanism for escape from saddle points and sharp minima, with covariance feeding back on sharpness via −½C⟨∂³L⟩.
- **Exact ηλ = 2 covariance instability, absent from SDE approximations**: In the Hessian eigenbasis, DₜC̃ₖₗ = (ηλₖλₗ − λₖ − λₗ)C̃ₖₗ equals the exact one-step change of the second moment for a quadratic loss, so the edge-of-stability instability at ηλ = 2 is located exactly, while SDE expansions of the drift converge only for ηλ < 1 and contain no such instability. The stationary solution near a quadratic minimum coincides with the exact discrete-SGD stationary covariance of Liu et al. (2021), including its divergence at ηλ = 2.
- **Unification of edge-of-stability theories**: In the full-batch limit, the coupled sharpness–covariance dynamics along the sharpest direction has a stationary point λ₁* = 2/η, σ₁²* = (2S₁ + ηQ₁)/g₁, recovering self-stabilization (Damian et al., ICLR 2023) and central flow (Cohen et al., ICLR 2025) as limits; for finite batch size the threshold lowers to λ₀ = (2/η)/(1+κ/B) − ηd/(2B), reproducing the empirical observation that SGD hovers below 2/η, with a new term coupling gradient noise to the third derivative of the loss.
- **Careful empirical validation with an explicitly mapped breakdown boundary**: Loss curves, mean trajectories, covariance dynamics, and the magnitude of the implicit regularization term are validated against ensembles of hundreds of identically initialized SGD replicas in deep linear and nonlinear networks; the regime where the Gaussian closure fails is quantified via third moments and Hill tail indices rather than hidden.

## Methodology

1. **Derivation.** A master equation is written for the one-step evolution of the parameter distribution p(θ, t); both sides are Taylor-expanded to second order (keeping O(δt²), nonstandard vs. the usual Fokker–Planck derivation), δt is identified with η, and the Kramers–Moyal coefficients α = η⟨f⟩, β = η²⟨ff⟩ are substituted. Multiplying by θₖ and θₖθₗ and integrating by parts yields moment equations under Dₜ = ∂ₜ + (η/2)∂ₜ². A Gaussian closure is then imposed by expanding f to second order about the mean (neglecting third and higher cumulants), giving closed flow equations for m and C. For SGD (f = −∇L), these become Dₜm = ⟨f⟩ − ½∇Tr(C⟨H⟩) and DₜC = −(C⟨H⟩ + ⟨H⟩C) + η⟨HCH⟩ + ηCov(f) − ½[Cov(f, ∇Tr(CH)) + h.c.], each term carrying a physical interpretation (curvature pull-in, finite-step destabilization, batch-noise injection, third-derivative coupling).
2. **Deep linear network (DLN).** A 2-2-2 two-layer linear network (P = 8 parameters) trained online on a task with singular values (2.5, 1), so learning passes through two saddle points. Ensembles of Ns = 800 identically initialized SGD replicas with independent batch streams provide empirical means/covariances; the SLF equations are integrated with adaptive RK45, with all population averages over inputs evaluated in closed form (the batch-covariance structure of the quadratic loss makes ⟨f⟩, ⟨H⟩, Cov(f), ⟨HCH⟩ exact) and with the covariance projected onto the complement of the loss-invariance ("gauge") symmetry at each step. Learning rates η ∈ [0.05, 0.20] and batch sizes B ∈ {4, 8, 16, 64, 256} are swept; NRMSE of loss curves and cosine alignment of update directions are compared against gradient flow (GF) and a C = 0 ablation.
3. **Nonlinear network.** A two-layer GELU classifier (h = 24 hidden units, 14×14 binary MNIST 0-vs-1, d = 196, P = 4753, float64, fixed η = 0.13345). Segments of 200 optimizer steps start from a common warm-start vector with C(0) = 0, so all covariance is generated by minibatch noise; n = 256 replicas per segment. The per-sample Hessian has closed-form rank-(2h+1) structure, which lets the fluctuation terms ⟨HCH⟩ and Cov(f, ∇Tr(CH)) be evaluated exactly (cross-checked against stochastic Gaussian-probe estimates: agreement ≤ 0.18%). Errors are normalized by the finite-ensemble sampling floor; the heaviness of the replica loss-distribution tail is quantified with a Hill estimator.
4. **Edge-of-stability probe.** A two-layer tanh network (dx = 15, h = 32, P = 545) on N = 200 fixed examples with power-law input covariance; η = 0.1 (so 2/η = 20). From 34 full-batch base points with sharpness λ₁ ∈ [13.31, 21.47], n = 6×10⁴ antithetic replica pairs are displaced by ±εv₁ and given one SGD step with a shared batch; the half-difference along v₁ cancels gradient noise exactly and yields a mean-square growth rate whose zero is the measured threshold λ₀, compared against the theory λ₀ = 2/[η(1+κ/B)] − ηd/(2B) with no fitted parameters.

## Results & Highlights

- **Loss-curve accuracy vs gradient flow (DLN, 800-replica ensembles):** in the high-noise regime (e.g., η = 0.2, B = 8), SLF's loss-curve NRMSE against the SGD ensemble stays around 10⁻³–10⁻⁴, while the gradient-flow baseline's degrades to ~10⁻²–10⁻¹ — roughly one to two orders of magnitude worse (Fig. 2A–C). The minimum update-direction alignment (cosine) over training: SLF remains ≳ 0.9 across the sweep, whereas GF's falls below ~0.6 at η = 0.20 with small batches (Fig. 2E–F).
- **Covariance prediction in a nonlinear network (binary MNIST, P = 4753, 256 replicas):** the predicted covariance trace Tr C_pred matches the measured Tr C_meas within 10% (ratio 0.90–1.10) across two orders of magnitude of variance (10⁻⁵–10⁻³), against a replica sampling error of ~1.3% at B ≥ 256 (Fig. 4C). The integrated SLF mean trajectory sits at the finite-ensemble estimation noise floor; setting C = 0 raises the step-200 mean error by a factor that grows as batch size shrinks (median over 200 steps: 1.037, 1.077, 1.134 for B = 1024, 512, 256; Fig. 4A–B).
- **Implicit regularization is not negligible:** in the DLN's noisy regime (large η, small B), the transient peaks of the regularization term ½Tr(C⟨H⟩) — which the SLF equations track quantitatively across the (η, B) sweep (Fig. 3E–F) — come within an order of magnitude of the MSE loss itself.
- **Where the theory fails is quantified:** SLF's peak covariance overshoots the SGD ensemble's, growing with the ensemble third moment approximately as 3.16·m₃^0.71 (Fig. 3D); in the nonlinear network the variance-prediction error grows as the Hill tail index of the replica loss distribution falls below 2 (where the second moment diverges), and at B = 32 several ensembles diverged outright (7 stored states excluded as non-finite).
- **Edge-of-stability threshold under batch noise:** the predicted mean-square threshold λ₀ = 2/[η(1+κ/B)] matches the one-step probe measurement within ±0.5% relative residual across B = 5–256 with zero fitted parameters (Fig. S1); the paper's novel third-derivative correction ηd/(2B) changes the prediction by < 1% in the tested network. In the full-batch limit, EoS is the stationary point λ₁* = 2/η, σ₁²* = (2S₁ + ηQ₁)/g₁ of the coupled dynamics, with central flow recovered when covariance relaxation is replaced by a constraint.
- **Mechanism demo (1D landscape, 10⁴ replicas):** with small η, full-batch gradient descent converges into a sharp local minimum; with large η (curvature at the minimum above 2/η), the covariance diverges upon approach and the ensemble escapes to the broader global minimum — finite step size alone, without any batch noise, provides escape from sharp minima (Fig. 1).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty (good, but a synthesis):** the moment-equation approach per se is 30+ years old (Heskes–Kappen, Leen–Moody), and discrete-update moment relations already exist at stationarity (Yaida 2019) and for the stationary covariance (Liu et al. 2021). The genuinely new ingredients — keeping the O(δt²) term (fixed update times rather than Poisson timing) and the η⟨H⟩C⟨H⟩ covariance term that makes the ηλ = 2 instability exact even at ηλ ~ 1 — are consequential, and unifying self-stabilization, central flow, and mean-square SGD stability in one dynamical system is a real contribution. Still, readers should see this as an extension-and-unification within a known lineage rather than a new primitive.
- **Missing empirical baseline:** the experiments compare SLF only against gradient flow and a C = 0 ablation. The claim that SLF beats higher-order SDE approximations (Li et al. 2017; Kunin et al. 2023) rests entirely on theory (their expansions converge only for ηλ < 1), not on a head-to-head numerical comparison in the paper's own testbeds — a straightforward experiment that is conspicuously absent.
- **Scale limits are severe:** P = 8 (DLN), P = 4753 (a 24-hidden-unit binary-MNIST classifier followed for only 200 steps from a warm start with C(0) = 0 by construction), P = 545 (EoS probe). Integrating SLF requires P×P covariance matrices and dense Hessians, which the authors acknowledge caps the method at a few thousand parameters; whether the Gaussian-closure regime survives in realistically sized networks (let alone LLM training) is entirely open.
- **The novel term underdelivers empirically:** the third-derivative coupling ηd/(2B) — one of the paper's distinguishing predictions vs. Mulayoff & Michaeli's exact mean-square analysis — changed the predicted threshold by < 1% in the one network tested, so its practical relevance is undemonstrated.
- **Single-mode EoS analysis:** the finite-B sharpness theory projects onto the top eigenvector v₁ only; the authors themselves note batch noise populates other covariance modes, so the displacement of sharpness below 2/η cannot be captured by the sharp direction alone, and λ₀ is a mean-square threshold — trajectories can persist up to a Lyapunov threshold above 2/η that the theory brackets but does not locate.
- **Rigor and honesty are otherwise strong:** exact evaluation of fluctuation terms via low-rank per-sample Hessian structure (cross-checked to ≤ 0.18% against stochastic estimates), gauge-symmetry projection in the DLN, explicit finite-ensemble noise floors, open symbols marking non-Gaussian (η, B) cells where the theory fails, and a no-fitted-parameter probe test. The failure analysis via Hill indices (heavy tails as random-coefficient recursion) is a credible diagnosis rather than an excuse.
- **Red flags:** none serious. The 1D Figure-1 example restarts the SLF integration from simulated moments after the covariance diverges (disclosed in Methods), so that figure illustrates the mechanism rather than a prediction; and the headline "recovers self-stabilization and central flow" is a consistency result, not new predictive power.

## Strengths

- Treats the finite-step-size instability exactly for quadratic losses, fixing a structural blind spot of SDE approximations (no instability at ηλ = 2, convergence only for ηλ < 1) and explaining why SDE surrogates of SGD fail to stabilize where SGD does.
- Unifies four previously separate descriptions — moment equations, self-stabilization, central flow, and mean-square linear stability of SGD — as limits or stationary points of one set of coupled equations, adding a new gradient-noise/third-derivative term.
- The covariance is a dynamical variable on equal footing with the mean, making the flat-minima bias of SGD (the ½Tr(C⟨H⟩) regularization) a quantitative, time-resolved object rather than a heuristic.
- Empirically scrupulous: exact evaluation of noise terms, gauge projections, ensemble noise floors, and an explicit, quantified map of where the theory breaks down (third moments, Hill tail index < 2).
- The general formulation applies to any stochastic update rule (including RL exploration noise), inviting extensions to momentum, adaptive methods, and learning-rate schedules.

## Limitations

- Gaussian closure: third and higher cumulants are dropped, but SGD is a random-coefficient recursion whose stationary distributions are generically heavy-tailed; the authors show the closure and the variance estimator fail together precisely when the Hill index falls below 2. Multimodal fragmentation of the ensemble (replicas settling into different minima late in training) is also outside the description.
- Computational cost: P×P covariance propagation and dense Hessian evaluations confine validation to networks of at most a few thousand parameters; scalability to realistic architectures is untested.
- Validation scope is narrow beyond that: a 2-2-2 linear network, a binary 0-vs-1 MNIST classifier tracked for only 200 steps from a shared warm start (so C(0) = 0 by construction, never testing full training runs from initialization), and a small synthetic-regression probe for the EoS threshold.
- The EoS theory is a single-direction projection; mode coupling under batch noise — how additional modes are recruited to the edge, as observed empirically — is explicitly left open, and λ₀ is a mean-square, not almost-sure, threshold.
- Only plain, constant-learning-rate SGD is treated; momentum, adaptive optimizers, and LR schedules (which break the δt = η identification) are deferred to future work, so the practical optimizer zoo is not yet covered.

## Relevance

对「训练稳定性与可观测性」方向有直接的理论底层价值：SLF 给出有限步长失稳的精确阈值 ηλ=2、批量噪声把阈值压低到 λ₀=2/[η(1+κ/B)] 的定量公式，以及协方差驱动的隐式正则如何让训练逃离尖锐极小值——这为在真实训练运行中用 sharpness/学习率比、top-Hessian 模式上的方差增长来预判发散与 loss spike 提供了机理依据。不过该工作只在千参数级小网络上验证，距离大规模预训练监控还差可扩展性（低秩/顶层特征模近似）这一步。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can SLF be reduced to a low-rank approximation (top-k Hessian eigenmodes + covariance along them) using Hessian-vector products, so that Tr(C⟨H⟩) and the instability threshold become cheap online observables for large training runs?
- How do momentum (β) and adaptive methods (Adam) modify Dₜ and the η⟨H⟩C⟨H⟩ term — does the 2/η threshold generalize, and does the EoS stationary-point picture survive the extra eigenvalue structure?
- Do the heavy-tail breakdown regimes (Hill index < 2, diverging second moments) connect mechanistically to loss spikes and divergence events observed in large-scale pretraining, and could the tail index serve as an early-warning signal?
- Where between the mean-square threshold λ₀ and the Lyapunov threshold do real trajectories actually settle, and how are additional curvature modes recruited to the edge of stability under batch noise?
- Since the covariance-driven regularization term is the mechanism biasing SGD toward flat minima, can SLF quantitatively predict final-minimum sharpness (and hence generalization differences) as a function of η/B — the long-standing η/B heuristic — in settings beyond the tiny networks tested?
