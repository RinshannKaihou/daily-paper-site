01Methodology — precision as the only moving part

A stress-test replication of overtraining instabilities that isolates a single variable: the numerical precision of the LM head.

Architecture
28M–1.4B modernized GPT-2 — rotary embeddings, RMSNorm (learnable gains), untied embeddings, QK-Norm; JAX/Flax.
Data & regime
FineWeb, GPT-2 tokenizer; peak LR 3×10⁻⁴ → 3×10⁻¹; 96B tokens at 404M (≈12× Chinchilla), 16B at 28M (≈28×).
Optimizer
AdamW (β₁=0.9, β₂=0.95), cosine decay, gradient clip 1.0.
Measurement
Every log step: recompute logits in FP32 from identical hidden states; track RMS(zbf16−zfp32) and DKL(pfp32 ‖ pbf16) across FP32 / BF16-mixed / BF16-true heads.
Definitions
Time-bomb ≔ 3 consecutive post-midpoint evals ≥ 0.01 above running best; 5 seeds per config; matched hyperparameter budgets.
Fast-spike forensics
Rolling token-loss z-scores, mask/skip interventions, single-token context perturbations at spike events.

02The mechanism, step by step

1

Adam breaks the zero-sum head gradient. Per-coordinate preconditioning violates Σᵢ(pᵢ−yᵢ)=0, so the mean logit µz drifts.

2

Drift is harmless in exact arithmetic — a softmax-invariant shift changes neither predictions nor gradients. the paradox

3

BF16 has fixed relative precision. 8-bit significand: ULP grows ~0.008 → 8 as |z| runs 1 → 1024.

4

Resolution catastrophe. Once |µz|/σz ≫ 1, the logit differences defining the predictive distribution fall below one ULP.

5

Detonation. Measured quantization error jumps ~10⁰ → ~10³, KL divergence between FP32/BF16 predictions explodes, and training never recovers.

The fix — SGD-M (β=0.99) on the LM head only: removes drift at the source, restoring the 1/(1−β)=100× steady-state amplification Adam silently provided. z-loss (objective) and µ-centering (architecture) work via the same route: keeping quantization error bounded.

  • Precision gates onset: BF16-true fails 4/5 seeds even at LR 3×10⁻³; BF16-mixed 2/5 at 3×10⁻²; FP32 stable everywhere tested.
  • Fast spikes are context-fragile, not data-driven: swapping one context token (“Command” → comma) collapses a 610.0-nat spike to 12.7 nats; top logit 782.05 → 95.01; vocabulary logit std 121.87 → 6.87 (17×).
  • Learnable RMSNorm gains erase fast spikes in all 5 matched seed pairs — time-bombs survive this fix, motivating the deeper mechanism.
  • Negative control: fixed softmax (Barron et al., 2026) under BF16-true still time-bombs 5/5 — forward-pass LM-head quantization, not backward softmax rounding, drives collapse.
  • 1.4B, LR 0.07, 10B tokens: head SGD-M beats AdamW, µ-centering, and z-loss; Muon + head SGD-M gives the lowest loss of any configuration tested (single seed).

04Strengths

  • Causal, not correlational: same architecture, data, LR — only LM-head precision varies, and failure timing moves monotonically with it.
  • A mechanistically satisfying answer to a genuine puzzle (softmax-invariant drift should be harmless), with a directly measurable quantity.
  • Embarrassingly practical fix: no loss terms, no architectural change, one fewer optimizer moment buffer — and it stacks with Muon at 1.4B.
  • Honest negative results: token masking hurts convergence, batch-skipping only defers spikes, fixed softmax doesn’t prevent BF16-true time-bombs.
  • Explains why β=0.99 (not 0.9) is required — retroactively clarifying why prior work found SGD-M slightly worse.

05Limitations

  • Instabilities induced at aggressively elevated LRs and 12–28× overtraining; production-regime transfer (LR ~10⁻⁴, ≥7B, 10T+ tokens) not demonstrated.
  • Headline precision experiments are at 28M; the 1.4B evidence is single-seed, training-loss-only, fast-spike regime.
  • The “shared numerical mechanism” unification is co-occurrence, not intervention — bounded error accompanies every fix but causality isn’t isolated.
  • Under BF16-true, SGD-M converges to visibly higher loss than µ-centering/z-loss — not uniformly dominant.
  • AdamW head β₁=0.99 closes much of the gap; the full SGD-M swap’s advantage is thinner than the headline.
  • No FP8 or quantized-training analysis; no wall-clock/memory cost numbers for FP32 logits.

06Open questions

  1. Do time-bombs occur at production learning rates (~10⁻⁴) in ≥7B models on 10T+ tokens — or is the phenomenon an artifact of the compressed-horizon, high-LR protocol?
  2. Would an FP8 LM head accelerate time-bombs proportionally to its shorter significand — and does |µz|/σz quantitatively predict onset step across precisions?
  3. Can the unification be made causal by injecting synthetic BF16-rounded logit noise into an otherwise-stable FP32 run?
  4. Is bounded quantization error also why logit soft-capping (Gemma 2), coupled Adam, and hyperball optimization stabilize training?
  5. Is there a hybrid head optimizer matching SGD-M’s stability while keeping Adam’s per-row adaptivity for rare tokens?
