Critical Assessment · rigor note (1)

01 Methodology diagnose → redesign → verify

S1

MXU operand loading → head-major layouts

Two tokens of the same head per sublane (16 tokens/register, no MHA padding); 256-row query tiles so each K/V load serves 256 rows; projections emit and consume the kernel layout directly, deleting 33.7 ms of HBM layout work at 128K.

S2

Vector-unit bottleneck → numerical reformulation

Checked static-anchor softmax: fix a per-row anchor at the first tile's max, accumulate es−a with no rescaling; one l ≤ Λ comparison per block certifies range (NaN/+∞ fail it), failing blocks replay in-launch. GDN forward substitution becomes a radix-4 hierarchical triangular inverse — 8 BF16 matmuls, FP32 accumulation.

S3

Single-stream VLIW compiler → explicit pipelining

Each fragment's PV matmul defers to interleave with the next fragment's QK work inside one compiler basic block; GDN chunk-local inverses overlap adjacent chunks' state-dependent computation.

VERIFY — v6e (918 TFLOP/s peak · 128 MiB VMEM · 1.6 TB/s HBM) & v7x (1153 · 64 MiB · 3.7 TB/s), JAX Pallas · 5-round median device times vs analytic roofline · 792-cell shape grid · vLLM env-switch A/B serving Qwen3.8-27B (48 GDN + 16 softmax layers) on RULER / BABILong / Video-MME.

03 Strengths

  • Architecture-grounded diagnosis — every fix is mapped to a measured bottleneck: operand amortization on weight-stationary MXUs, vector-unit imbalance, compiler-only scheduling.
  • Checked anchor softmax is numerics–systems co-design: one FP32 compare per row per block certifies range; NaN/+∞ fail it; in-launch guarded replay beats FA4-style conditional rescaling on this hardware.
  • Evaluation discipline: 792-cell grid, roofline and block-level timings that expose baseline layout overhead, per-design ablations, two TPU generations, commit-pinned baselines.
  • End-to-end honesty: env-switch A/B inside one vLLM build; accuracy checked on the very workloads being timed; losing cells reported, not hidden.
  • Beyond prefill: paged-KV decode, speculative verify up to 1.62×, FP8 KV caches halve the decode step (1.98×), ReplaySSM decode up to 3.72× on 4-token verify.

04 Limitations

  • Short-context gains are marginal: 1.07× at 4K (v6e), 0.98× at 4K (v7x), behind Splash at 1K — this is specifically a long-context prefill win.
  • Hardware coupling: tiles are tuned to v6e's 128 MiB VMEM; v7x's 64 MiB forces 64-row tiles that break cross-chunk pipelining and cap utilization at 70% of roofline.
  • Narrow numerical validation: the BF16 inverse carries max error 9×10−3 vs FP64, yet testing covers one hybrid model and three benchmarks; v7x accuracy used 20 samples/task.
  • Decode is bandwidth-bound: gains ≤1.14× — every headline claim in the paper is a prefill claim.
  • No GPU anchor point: no FlashAttention-3/4 absolute throughput or $/token comparison, so TPU-vs-GPU serving cost cannot be judged from this paper alone.
  • Engineering specificity: pinned Pallas/libtpu toolchain; C=64 chunk size baked into the 8-matmul inverse; portability asserted, not shown.

05 Questions for further exploration

  • How often does the guarded replay actually trigger in production serving — and what is its worst-case tail latency vs always-rescale baselines?
  • Does the BF16 approximate triangular inverse accumulate error over deeper GDN stacks, longer training runs, or other delta-rule models (Kimi Linear, Mamba-2)?
  • Can the v7x limit be lifted — FP8 intermediates, VMEM-aware chunk splitting — to restore cross-chunk pipelining within 64 MiB and push past 70% of roofline?
  • What is the absolute cross-platform picture: FlyWheel on v6e/v7x vs FlashAttention-3/4 on H100/B200 at equal model, lengths, and $/token, including decode?
  • Do the layout-coordination techniques transfer to other operators — MLPs, MoE expert dispatch, conv stems — or to PyTorch/XLA graphs outside vLLM?
  • Can the checked static-anchor softmax be adapted to training kernels, where backward-pass sensitivity to anchor choice may differ from prefill inference?
