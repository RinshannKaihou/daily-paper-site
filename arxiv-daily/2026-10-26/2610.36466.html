Key Finding

Cross-paradigm distillation plus self-refining diffusion let one discrete diffusion LLM match an 8B AR VLM — and beat its own flow teacher.

跨范式在线策略蒸馏（AR VLM 教理解、连续流模型 Qwen-Image 教编辑）+ 基于球树的渐进自精炼扩散：11 项理解基准平均 78.3 分追平 AR 的 Qwen3-VL-8B，HPSv3 超越 Qwen-Image。

Why this matters

The strongest public result yet at closing discrete diffusion’s gap to AR VLMs and flow models — both core mechanisms are directly reusable.

该路线目前最强的公开结果；传递式一致性损失与球树腐蚀过程均可直接借鉴。

Critical edge

AR-parity holds only on the average: NLD-Uni still trails Qwen3-VL-8B by 8.9 pts on MMMU (60.7 vs 69.6), and its “unified DLLM SOTA” field is young and small.

Methodology

How one DLLM absorbs two foreign teachers

distilled · not a transcript
  • Two-tower Elastic-MoT (8B understanding + 8B generation; joint attention in early layers, decoupled later). Images enter via RADIO-H (continuous) + IBQ VQ (discrete, 131k codes); images exit as VQ codes. Trained with Scheduled Block Diffusion — block size 1 recovers the plain-AR view.
  • Cross-paradigm OPD (understanding): a frozen AR VLM supervises the student’s on-policy AR rollouts with token-level KL; a transitive self-consistency loss then aligns the diffusion view to the student’s own stop-grad AR predictions at masked positions. Copying teacher scores directly collapses MathVista 71.5 → 47.7 — the design is load-bearing.
  • Cross-paradigm OPD (editing): student rollouts are re-encoded, noised to r = 0.9, denoised by frozen Qwen-Image over ODE steps, re-encoded to VQ → SFT targets (+0.27 ImgEdit over off-policy KD).
  • Training: 3 stages · 127M curated text–image pairs · 64 H100 × 26 days (OPD stage: 5).

Strengths

What holds up

  • Attacks the real bottleneck of unified DLLMs with mechanisms validated by honest negative baselines — the naive-OPD collapse proves the transitive loss is load-bearing, not decorative.
  • Ball-tree corruption gives discrete diffusion a coarse-to-fine noise geometry for 524 KB (vs 32 GB pairwise matrix); PSNR analysis confirms the trajectory is genuinely smooth.
  • Elastic views: one 16B checkpoint serves as an 8B VLM, 12B T2I model, and 16B interleaved model with no inference overhead — a deployment story beyond benchmarks.
  • Consistent 2.2–4.9× decode speedups over AR at small accuracy cost, measured carefully across confidence thresholds.
  • Broad empirical coverage: 25+ benchmarks, four task families, derivations, data mixtures, and a candid limitations section.
Limitations

What to watch

  • Shared-tokenizer constraint: OPD needs teacher and student on the same tokenizer (both Ministral 3 here) — ruling out many stronger teachers.
  • Still behind specialized SOTA where it counts: MMMU −8.9 vs Qwen3-VL-8B, DPG 86.6 vs 88.3, editing trails its teacher (4.17 vs 4.27) and GPT-4o (4.20).
  • Headline deltas are partly statistical: Table 1’s “Δ vs Diff. SOTA” mixes 1.3B–16B baselines; ScreenSpot-Pro +48.8 rides a near-zero baseline.
  • Self-refinement ablations run at 20k steps, not the full ~500k — unclear the ordering holds at scale.
  • No fine-grained text rendering (training mix lacks the data); refinement gains concentrate on HPSv3, marginal on GenEval.
  • Reproduction is checkpoint-level only: 127M proprietary-filtered images, two strong teachers, 64 H100 × 26 days.
Open questions

For further exploration

  1. How much of the +17.1 average gain is data scale/mixture versus the two mechanisms? A compute-matched LaViDa-O rerun would isolate it.
  2. Can the transitive AR→diffusion consistency loss go cross-tokenizer (vocabulary projection, logit-space alignment), unlocking stronger teachers like Kimi K-2.6?
  3. Does ball-tree corruption generalize to text-only DLLM pretraining, where no VQ codebook geometry exists?
  4. Refinement gains sit on HPSv3 and saturate at N=8 — is there a sampler (adaptive ε, confidence-aware order) that moves them to GenEval/DPG?
  5. With a stronger flow teacher available, would teacher-refined OPD finally lift T2I composition (DPG 86.6 vs 88.3)?
