Why this mattersso what

Multi-tenant LLM engines reject non-LoRA adapters today; Scute serves them exactly — with proofs and a predictive cost model — as a drop-in vLLM patch.

Punica / S-LoRA / vLLM-class engines load LoRA only; lossy SVD conversion is the current workaround.

Critical edgeread critically

Every serving result comes from one model — Llama-3.1-8B in fp16 — with synthetic tenants; the 1024-tenant pools test only (IA)³ and VeRA, the two cheapest methods.

Also: built on vLLM 0.19.1 (authors' CUDA 12.2 constraint) — and on A100 prefill-heavy load, stock vLLM's mean TTFT is 0.76× Scute's.

  • DoRA's scale never folds: W·diag(s−1) is full numerical rank on 60/60 adapted projections of Llama-3.1-8B; the best rank-16 approximation still misses ≥59.7% of the squared Frobenius norm — so batched engines must keep s live.
  • Route 1 carries s inside vLLM's expand pass (≤ k·dout extra reads/step); Route 2 computes VeRA's shared factors with two row-tiled GEMMs — read once per step, independent of tenant count; Route 3 gives OFT's block-diagonal R a dedicated pre-GEMM pass.
  • Zero-parameter byte model t = t₀ + ΔQ/β + ΔL·λ (β=680 GB/s DRAM, λ=0.96 µs/launch, RTX A6000) predicts route time to 0.7–14% median error — and retrodicts a third-party vLLM DoRA PR at ~3×.
  • Runtime patch, not a fork: hooks vLLM 0.19.1's loader / packer / layer-apply; CUDA graphs, torch.compile (Triton custom ops) and tensor parallelism intact. Code: github.com/ThomasWalker1/Scute-deploy.

03

Strengths

  • Theory drives the design, not benchmark tuning: normal form + fold rule with proofs; the un-foldability of DoRA's scale is a clean negative result that justifies the live-slot machinery.
  • Genuinely predictive cost model — zero fitted parameters, 0.7–14% median error, and it retrodicts third-party engineering choices (an open vLLM DoRA PR: predicted ~3×, measured 2.38–2.95×).
  • Deployable artifact: runtime patch on production vLLM — no fork — keeping CUDA graphs, torch.compile and tensor parallelism; code, tests and examples released.
  • Honest evaluation hygiene: memory budgets equalized (0.83 vs 0.90), engine order balanced, six repetitions, two GPUs, negative cells reported.
  • Substantial many-tenant win: 1024 exact adapters resident on one 48 GB GPU, 2.01× the throughput of rank-64 conversions.
04

Limitations

  • Single base model and precision: Llama-3.1-8B, fp16 — no Qwen/Mistral, MoE, bf16/fp8 or quantized serving.
  • Synthetic tenants with trained-adapter shapes, not production traces; the 1024-tenant pools include only (IA)³ and VeRA — OFT/DoRA scaling untested.
  • Thin accuracy evidence: 15 adapters over 3 tasks, two with high baselines that compress differences; the amplified-adapter log-prob check carries the real exactness weight.
  • Coverage gaps: SSF unimplemented (absent from PEFT 0.20), BOFT needs an unimplemented extension, HiRA and full fine-tuning fall outside the normal form.
  • Unmeasured costs: admission-compiler load time (DoRA's s needs the full W) and stacking (formalized, never benchmarked); vLLM 0.19.1 / SGLang transfer unverified.
05

Questions for further exploration

  1. Do route costs and the byte model transfer to newer vLLM/SGLang kernels (e.g. FlashInfer's SGMV), quantized bases (fp8/int4 AWQ), and MoE models where hooks sit inside experts?
  2. Can the normal form extend to HiRA or BitDelta-compressed full fine-tuning deltas — does a "compressed slot" preserve exactness under a byte budget?
  3. What is admission-compiler cost at scale — does computing DoRA's s from the full base weight bottleneck platforms hot-swapping thousands of adapters per hour?
  4. Does stacking (tenant LoRA over shared (IA)³) change the 5–17% mixed-pool overhead, and can schedulers exploit stack sharing across tenants?
