> **一句话总结：** 在以 FineWeb-Edu 训练的 55M/108M 激活参数 MoE 语言模型上，本文发现 Muon（而非 AdamW）训练骨干网络会让第一个 MoE 层的辅助无损均衡偏置的跨度约翻倍（0.084→0.194），原因是 Muon 使首个路由器的输入共享一个方向，导致最忙专家在 warmup 后达到公平份额的 4–7 倍过载；对路由器输入做批量均值中心化即可消除过载且损失变化 <0.003，而把路由器单独换回 AdamW 反而加剧 warmup 期间的负载抖动并小幅升高损失。

> **TL;DR:** A single-author controlled study trains 55M/108M-active-param DeepSeekMoE-style models on FineWeb-Edu with every combination of {AdamW, Muon} x {router, backbone} at 3 seeds each, and finds Muon on the backbone roughly doubles the bias-span of the FIRST MoE layer vs AdamW (0.194 vs 0.084 integrated span, small model, 1B tokens) while still lowering validation loss (3.177 vs 3.255). The mechanism is a shared direction in the first router's inputs (mean cosine ~0.4 under Muon vs 0.07 under AdamW) that overloads the busiest expert 4–7x its fair share right after warmup; subtracting the batch mean from that router's input removes the overload (1.11–1.16 vs 3.30–4.66) with loss change <0.003. Contrary to Qwen3.8-Flash-Next's recipe, moving only the router to AdamW raises warmup churn at every seed and loss by 0.001–0.004, so at this scale the router should stay on Muon.

## 核心贡献

- 提出用"偏置跨度"（一层中最大均衡偏置减最小偏置）来度量辅助无损均衡（auxiliary-loss-free bias）的工作强度，而不是看负载本身——负载被偏置强行拉平后无法反映路由器的真实偏好。
- 首次给出数据回答"Muon 是否应训练路由器"这一各家配方（DeepSeek-V4/Kimi K3/GLM-5.3 vs Qwen3.8-Flash-Next/MiMo-V2.6/IQuest-Q1）分歧的问题：在 55M/108M 规模上，骨干用 Muon 时路由器也应留在 Muon 上。
- 发现并定位效应：Muon 使**第一个** MoE 层最难均衡——跨度翻倍、其余六层平均只差 0.02；效应跟随骨干网络的优化器而非路由器的优化器。
- 给出机制解释：Muon 骨干使首个路由器输入共享一个方向（输入余弦 ~0.4 vs AdamW 的 0.07），均值输入给所有 token 施加同一专家偏好，偏置每步只能移动 u=0.001、追不上，导致 warmup 后最忙专家过载 4–7 倍公平份额。
- 提出并验证两个廉价修复：减去路由器输入的批量均值（3,000 步探针中过载降到 AdamW 水平、损失变化 <0.003）和减半 Muon 学习率（小模型上抹除约四分之三的涨幅且损失更好）。

## Key Contributions

- Introduces the **span** of the auxiliary-loss-free balancing biases (max bias minus min bias per layer) as a measure of how hard the balancer works — loads themselves are uninformative because the bias forcibly equalizes them.
- First controlled, multi-seed, pre-registered-comparison evidence on whether Muon should train the MoE router, a choice on which current open recipes split (DeepSeek-V4, Kimi K3, GLM-5.3 keep it on Muon; Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, MiMo-V2.6, IQuest-Q1 use AdamW/Adam) — none of those reports showed comparative data.
- Isolates the effect to the FIRST MoE layer: at tuned learning rates Muon raised its span at every seed across four experiments (12 seeds total), while the other six layers changed by only ~0.02 on average.
- Causal mechanism: with the backbone on Muon the first router's inputs share a direction (input cosine ~0.4 vs 0.07 under AdamW), the batch-mean input gives every token the same expert preference, and the bias (moving only u = 0.001/step, the DeepSeek-V3/V4 rate) needs hundreds of steps to cancel it — the busiest expert draws 4–7x its fair share meanwhile.
- Tests cheap mitigations: centering the first router's input (subtracting the batch mean) removes the early overload in 3,000-step probes on both model sizes with loss change <0.003; halving Muon's tuned rate removes ~3/4 of the rise and slightly improves loss on the small model.

## Methodology

The author trains two decoder-only Transformers with DeepSeekMoE-style layers (32 routed experts, top-4 sigmoid router with score biases, one shared expert, dense FFN in block 0, RMSNorm, RoPE, SwiGLU): a small model with 55M active / 303M total params (8 blocks, width 512) and a large one with 108M active / 496M total (12 blocks, width 768). Training uses FineWeb-Edu sample-10BT with a 16,384 BBPE vocabulary, 1,024-token sequences, batch 64 (65,536 tokens/step), and the auxiliary-loss-free bias balancer at DeepSeek-V3's update rate u = 0.001. Peak learning rates are tuned per optimizer on shorter runs: AdamW 2e-3, Muon 4e-3 (small model), each with 500-step warmup and cosine decay.

Parameters are split into six groups (routers, routed experts, shared experts, attention, dense FFN of block 0, and embeddings/head/norm-gains always on AdamW), and each group is independently assigned AdamW or Muon — a "partition". Recipe partitions reproduce current practice: all-AdamW, all-Muon, and Muon-with-router-on-AdamW; further ablations put only part of the backbone on Muon; a "control" keeps Muon's update size but drops the orthogonalization. Experiments: a main comparison (small model, 1B tokens, seeds 0–2), a stress test at 1–16x tuned rates, an ablation crossing router x backbone optimizers (seeds 3–5), a bias-speed experiment with a 4x faster bias (seeds 6–8), a balance-loss experiment adding DeepSeek-V3's sequence-wise loss at weight 1e-4, and a large-model experiment (3B tokens, seeds 0–2). Short 3,000-step "probes" repeat early training with extra instrumentation or one change (e.g., centering the first router's input by subtracting its batch mean). Comparisons are paired by seed (same init, same batches), reported as mean paired differences with ranges; planned comparisons with expected signs were registered before training, and everything else is labeled exploratory. Metrics per MoE layer: bias span, input cosine (anisotropy of router inputs), score offset, overload (busiest expert's load as multiple of fair share, ceiling 8), load churn, common share of the router update along the shared direction, update–load correlation, and usage perplexity.

## Results & Highlights

- **Main comparison (small model, 1B tokens, FineWeb-Edu, seeds 0–2):** Muon raised the integrated span of the first MoE layer from 0.084 (AdamW) to 0.194 (+0.110, range [+0.085, +0.151]), i.e. roughly doubling it, at every seed; averaged over all 7 MoE layers the span went 0.105 → 0.137, and the per-layer difference outside layer 1 was only ~0.02. Muon still reached a better final validation loss: 3.177 vs 3.255 (−0.078).
- **Early-training overload:** right after the 500-step warmup (steps 501–1,000) the first-layer span was 0.74 under Muon vs 0.19 under AdamW, and the early overload (steps 501–1,262) hit 4.06 under Muon and 6.63 with the router on AdamW vs 1.14 under AdamW — the busiest expert drew 4–7x its fair share against a theoretical ceiling of 8.
- **Mechanism confirmed by centering probe (first 3,000 steps, seeds 3–5):** with the backbone on Muon the first router's input cosine averaged ~0.4–0.41 vs 0.071 under AdamW; subtracting the batch mean from that router's input cut the early overload from 3.30–4.66 to 1.11–1.16 (AdamW level), reduced the rise by 79%, and changed the loss at step 3,000 by only −0.0012 to +0.0009.
- **Learning-rate dependence:** at half the tuned Muon rate (2e-3, equal to AdamW's) the first-layer span excess over AdamW fell from +0.103 to +0.024 (25% vs 108% of AdamW's span) and validation loss improved by 0.008–0.012 at every seed, though the input cosine stayed at 0.40; a 4x faster bias similarly halved the rise (excess +0.075 → +0.040, excess early overload 2.84 → 0.54).
- **Large model (108M active, 3B tokens, seeds 0–2):** Muon raised the first-layer span by +0.152 (112% of AdamW's span), input cosine reached 0.61–0.73, early overload 6.3–7.2 (near the ceiling of 8), and unlike the small model the layer was STILL overloaded at the end of training — busiest expert at 2.2–2.8x fair share over the last 10,000 steps vs 1.07 under AdamW.
- **Router on AdamW hurts (contradicting Qwen3.8-Flash-Next's recipe):** with the backbone on Muon, moving the router to AdamW raised warmup churn at every seed (+0.038 on both model sizes) and raised validation loss at every seed (+0.002 small, +0.004 large); all 2/6/2 loss spikes of the large-model experiment occurred only in Muon+router-on-AdamW runs, and the common share of the router update along the shared direction was 0.497 on AdamW vs 0.160 on Muon (near Muon's 1/sqrt(32) ≈ 0.18 bound).
- **Update–load correlation dropped as expected (0.379 → 0.024)** — busy experts no longer got larger updates under Muon — yet the span still rose, so the popular "rich-get-richer gradient feedback" story does not explain the rise; no partition with only PART of the backbone on Muon reproduced the shared direction, though two of them (experts-only span 0.409; attention+dense-FFN 0.229) raised the span even more than the full backbone via a large score offset.

## Authors & Credibility

Single author: Marcos Hernanz (Escuela Politécnica Superior, Universidad Autónoma de Madrid); the paper is a shortened version of his bachelor's thesis supervised by Daniel Hernández-Lobato (a well-known UAM professor working on Bayesian deep learning). Credibility signals are unusually strong for a solo undergraduate-derived technical report: pre-registered planned comparisons with expected signs (whose failures are honestly reported, e.g. the update–load correlation and warmup-churn predictions that came out opposite), seed-paired differences with ranges, bit-for-bit reproducible probe runs on deterministic RTX 5090 setups, and full public artifacts — code (github.com/marcoshernanz/muon-moe-routing, a 213-test Python package "tfg"), all run logs (marcoshernanz/tfg-runs with a claims.csv tracking every planned comparison and its outcome), and the tokenized FineWeb-Edu dataset on Hugging Face. The scope is honestly framed as far below production scale.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Good and genuinely underexplored. Table 1 shows no production report (DeepSeek-V4, Kimi K3, GLM-5.3, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, MiMo-V2.6, IQuest-Q1) publishes data comparing router-optimizer choices, and prior optimizer studies either drop the bias balancer or never measure it. The span metric is simple but well-motivated, and the shared-direction mechanism connects to Wang et al.'s companion work.
- **Rigor:** Above average. Seed-paired designs, registered comparisons, a control optimizer that isolates orthogonalization from update-size equalization, and 3,000-step probes that change exactly one thing. However, every comparison rests on only 3 seeds with no multiple-comparison correction (acknowledged), and several probe conclusions (e.g., the large-model fast-bias residual churn) rest on a single seed.
- **Reproducibility:** Excellent — code, tests, per-run logs, planned-comparison ledger, and data are all public, and unchanged probe runs reproduce their full runs bit for bit.
- **Significance:** The external validity gap is the main issue and the author says so plainly: 55M/108M active params are 50–2,000x smaller than the Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-class models (125B total / 6B active) whose recipes motivate the question, and ≤3B tokens is ~4 orders of magnitude short of production runs. The contradiction with Qwen3.8-Flash-Next is not resolved — that model uses a softmax/balance-loss setup with no bias (per its released config), a different balancer than the one studied here, so the "keep the router on Muon" recommendation may simply not transfer.
- **Red flags / weak spots:** (1) The "tuned" Muon rate is arguably mistuned — tuned on 0.5B tokens with one seed, then used at 1B where HALF the rate gave lower loss at every seed, and AdamW was never retuned at 1B; since the span grows monotonically with Muon's learning rate (0.158 → 0.959 across the tuning grid), part of the headline "Muon doubles the span" is a learning-rate artifact the author himself quantifies but does not fully deconfound. (2) The two model sizes differ in more than scale (expert width 512 vs model width 768 on the small model; crossed on the large one). (3) The overload had no measurable cost in these models (no capacity limits, single device), so the practical severity is inferred, not demonstrated. (4) Centering was tested only for 3,000 steps and one layer, and the batch-mean version makes routing batch-dependent. (5) Evaluation is validation loss only, which the cited MoE Routing Testbed suggests may not distinguish routing methods at small scale.

## Strengths

- Asks a real, unsettled recipe question that multiple frontier labs answered differently with no published evidence, and answers it with a clean factorial design (router x backbone optimizer) rather than switching the whole model at once.
- The span metric is a clever observable: the bias equalizes loads, so imbalance hides the problem; measuring how hard the bias must work exposes it.
- Mechanistic depth: decomposes the score offset into mean-input length and router-row spread along the shared direction, shows the span tracks the score offset (93% of log-variance across 27 runs), and validates causality with the centering intervention.
- Exceptional transparency: registered predictions (including two that failed), public code/tests/runs/data, honest labels of exploratory vs planned results, and an unusually candid limitations section.
- Practical, cheap takeaways: keep the router on Muon when the backbone is on Muon; watch the first MoE layer; consider centering the first router's input (with a running mean) or a faster bias.

## Limitations

- Scale: 55M/108M active parameters and 1–3B tokens vs production models with billions of active params and trillions of tokens; whether the first-layer overload grows, persists, or settles at scale is open (Nemotron 3 Ultra hit ~12x fair share in its first MoE layer under AdamW, so the layer may be problematic under both optimizers at scale).
- Single architecture (32 experts, top-4, sigmoid scores with a span ceiling of 1 + 2u, shared expert, no capacity limit, dense block 0), single dataset (FineWeb-Edu), one batch size (65,536 tokens), one warmup length (500 steps), one balancer at two rates, one balance-loss weight.
- The learning-rate confound: the effect size depends strongly on Muon's rate (most of the rise vanishes at half rate, which also trained better), and rates were tuned on short single-seed runs and never retuned at the full training length for AdamW.
- Statistical power: 3 seeds per comparison, no correction for multiplicity, and single-seed evidence behind several probe claims.
- Centering fix is impractical as tested: batch mean makes a token's routing depend on its batch, was applied to one layer for 3,000 steps only, and a running-mean variant is untested.
- Validation loss is the sole quality measure; downstream or long-run effects of the early overload are unknown.

## Relevance

这篇论文直接触及当前开源 MoE 大模型训练配方中一个悬而未决的工程选择（路由器是否随骨干一起用 Muon 训练），并给出可操作的诊断指标（首层偏置跨度、路由器输入余弦）与廉价缓解手段（输入中心化、更快的偏置步长），对关注 MoE 训练稳定性与负载均衡优化的人非常有参考价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the first-MoE-layer span rise and the shared input direction persist (or shrink) at production scale — e.g., 1–10B active parameters and hundreds of billions of tokens — and under a softmax router without the sigmoid's bounded scores?
- Does a running-mean centering of the first router's input (batch-independent) preserve the overload removal over full training, and should it be applied to all MoE layers rather than just the first?
- What exactly spreads the router rows along the shared direction — is it the uneven loads feeding gradients only through routed tokens, as the author hypothesizes but did not test?
- Why did only the FULL backbone on Muon produce the shared direction (input cosine ~0.4), while attention+dense-FFN-only Muon (which computes the entire first router input) stayed at 0.054 — do the routed/shared experts' backward gradients play the key role?
- Can the effect be reconciled with Qwen3.8-Flash-Next's opposite recipe (router on AdamW for stability), for example because their balancer is a balance loss without the bias, or because softmax-in-bfloat16 routers suffer roundoff that sigmoid single-precision routers do not?
