> **一句话总结：** 提出一个双层评估框架(执行证据层 + 评分验证层),在 MATH 与 ARC-Challenge 上对五个 Qwen/DeepSeek 配置共 2,550 条输出进行匹配预算分析,发现同样的 2,048 token 上限下 Qwen MATH 有 49/450 出现"长度截断无最终答案"(LMF),而 ARC 为 0,DeepSeek MATH 仅有 5/300,且针对性验证审计可将 DeepSeek−Qwen 的准确率差从 −7.89 翻转为 +6.89 个百分点,证明准确率混淆了执行状态分布与评分策略。

> **TL;DR:** This paper introduces a two-layer evaluation framework that separates scorer-independent execution evidence (termination reason, answer exposure, parser status, completion length) from scorer-dependent correctness, then measures both across 2,550 outputs from five fixed Qwen (Qwen3.6-Flash/Plus, Qwen3.7-Plus) and DeepSeek (V4-Flash, V4-Pro) configurations on MATH and ARC-Challenge. At matched 2,048-token caps, Qwen MATH shows 49/450 length-missing-final (LMF) outputs versus 0 for ARC and only 5/300 for DeepSeek MATH (a −9.22 pp LMF gap, 95% CI [−13.67, −5.22]); a coverage-audited targeted verification reverses the DeepSeek-minus-Qwen MATH accuracy delta from −7.89 pp to +6.89 pp, demonstrating that benchmark accuracy conflates execution case mix with verification policy.

## 核心贡献

- 提出**双层评估框架**:将"是否到达可评估状态"(终止原因、答案暴露、解析状态、完成长度等打分器无关的执行证据)与"答案是否被判正确"(评分器相关的正确性)分离,在判定前冻结执行证据,防止准确率将"响应可得性"与"打分决策"混为一谈。
- 在 MATH 与 ARC-Challenge 两个任务上,对五个固定 Qwen/DeepSeek 配置(共 2,550 条输出、1,050 个匹配的 question–model–cap 三元组)进行**匹配预算测量**,证明同样 2,048 token 上限下的执行案例混合差异巨大——例如 Qwen MATH 有 49/450 LMF 而 Qwen ARC 为 0。
- 通过**可审计的针对性验证研究**(三评委盲评: Codex GPT-5.6-sol、GLM-5.2、LongCat-2.0)展示候选选择与聚合策略(机械评分 vs +Codex vs 3/3 一致 vs 2/3 多数)如何把 DeepSeek−Qwen MATH 的比较结果从 −7.89 个百分点机械性地翻转为 +6.89 个百分点,把"执行状态"和"验证策略"确立为 LLM 评估的一等公民。
- 提出最小化报告协议(执行层、评分层、比较、溯源四个字段),并发布含 164 条匿名 item 级标签、候选覆盖审计与离线复现代码的匿名 artifact。

## Key Contributions

- **A two-layer evaluation framework** that decouples scorer-independent execution evidence (termination reason, answer exposure, parser status, completion length) from scorer-dependent correctness, with the execution layer frozen before any LLM adjudication so that accuracy cannot silently conflate response availability with grading decisions.
- **Matched-budget measurement** over 2,550 outputs and 1,050 paired question–model–cap observations from three fixed Qwen snapshots (Qwen3.6-Flash, Qwen3.6-Plus, Qwen3.7-Plus) and two fixed DeepSeek endpoints (V4-Flash, V4-Pro) on 150 MATH and 150 ARC-Challenge items, showing that execution case mix varies substantially across the studied task/cap/model configurations even under identical output caps.
- **A coverage-audited targeted-verification study** using a three-lane blinded LLM panel (Codex GPT-5.6-sol, GLM-5.2, LongCat-2.0; Fleiss' κ = 0.851 on 264 items) demonstrating that candidate-selection and aggregation policies can reverse comparative accuracy conclusions.
- **A minimal reporting protocol** (execution / scoring / comparison / provenance fields kept jointly visible) plus an anonymous artifact releasing 164 anonymized item-level mechanical and three-judge labels, candidate membership, and offline reconstruction code.

## Methodology

The authors build a *two-layer* measurement instrument. The **execution/observability layer** records, for every output, four scorer-independent facts: termination reason, whether a final answer was exposed, parser status, and completion length. From these they project five execution states — LMF (length termination with no parsed final answer), LFP (length termination with one), NLU (non-length termination without a parseable answer), NW (non-length wrong), NC (non-length correct). The **scoring layer** assigns correct/wrong/ambiguous under a stated scorer, and execution is frozen *before* any LLM adjudication so the two layers cannot contaminate each other.

**Data and models.** Question sets are 150 MATH training items (stratified across five difficulty levels) and 150 ARC-Challenge validation items. Both tasks place a required `FINAL:` line after reasoning, with a shared extractor (last `FINAL:` line or last balanced boxed expression; ARC also validates the label against option count). The Qwen Natural run covers MATH@2048 and ARC@8192 (900 outputs) plus a frozen 450-item ARC sensitivity rerun at 2,048; DeepSeek crosses both tasks with caps 2,048 and 8,192 for each model (1,200 outputs, 600 pairs). All runs use temperature zero, top-p = 1, thinking/search/tools disabled.

**Scoring provenance.** A mechanical scorer normalizes ARC labels by option count and uses deterministic equivalence rules for MATH. A locked audit flagged 164 suspected MATH false negatives (24 Qwen, 140 DeepSeek). Candidate selection is deterministic within each evidence boundary but *not symmetric across configurations*: the DeepSeek rule selected every successful mechanically non-correct non-LMF MATH output (70/70 at each cap), whereas the earlier Qwen packet covered only 24/77 (31.2%) of mechanical non-correct and 24/28 (85.7%) of non-LMF non-correct outputs because complete Qwen response bodies were unavailable. The three-judge panel was frozen after provider availability was known but before candidate aggregates were inspected, mixing 100 mechanically certified controls with the 164 candidates.

**Estimands and uncertainty.** Within-family cap effects compare the same question and model at two caps; cross-family comparisons at 2,048 use matched questions and equal weighting within each family and are associational. The DeepSeek task-by-cap interaction is (MATH8192 − MATH2048) − (ARC8192 − ARC2048). Primary endpoints are LMF and answer availability; confidence intervals come from 10,000 deterministic PCG64 bootstrap replicates with questions as resampling units, unadjusted for multiplicity.

## Results & Highlights

- **Execution mixtures diverge at matched caps.** At 2,048 tokens, Qwen MATH shows 49/450 length-missing-final (LMF) outputs (10.89%) plus 1 LFP and 3 NLU, whereas Qwen ARC shows none of these states; Qwen MATH answer availability is 398/450 (88.44%) versus 450/450 (100%) for Qwen ARC. DeepSeek MATH has 5/300 LMF (1.67%) and 293/300 availability; DeepSeek ARC has zero LMF and full availability.
- **Cross-configuration LMF gap.** The DeepSeek-minus-Qwen MATH LMF difference is −9.22 percentage points (95% CI [−13.67, −5.22]); answer availability is +9.22 points (95% CI [5.22, 13.67]). Model-level LMF counts are 16, 16, 17 for the three Qwen variants and 2, 3 for DeepSeek Flash and Pro, so no single endpoint drives the direction.
- **Paired cap transitions.** Lowering the cap from 8,192 to 2,048 on Qwen ARC produces no LMF/availability change (441 pairs stay NC, 7 stay NW, 2 move NW→NC; favorable accuracy delta +0.44 pp, CI [0, 1.11]). For DeepSeek MATH, LMF changes from 5/300 at 2,048 to 0/300 at 8,192, a paired difference of −1.67 points (95% CI [−3.67, −0.33]); the five events span four questions and the exact two-sided sign/McNemar probability is p = 0.0625. The MATH-minus-ARC task-by-cap interaction is −1.67 points for LMF and +2.00 for availability.
- **Targeted verification flips the accuracy comparison.** On the matched MATH@2048 contrast, mechanical scoring yields a DeepSeek-minus-Qwen difference of −7.89 pp (95% CI [−14.11, −1.78]); adding targeted Codex adjudication yields +6.00 ([2.11, 10.33]); targeted 3/3 unanimity yields +6.89 ([3.11, 11.22]); targeted 2/3 majority yields +6.89 ([3.00, 11.22]). The reversal reflects the *combined* selection-and-scoring pipeline because verification opportunity is unmatched (24 Qwen vs 70 DeepSeek@2048 candidates).
- **Aggregation policy moves accuracy status.** Among the 164 candidates, unanimity assigns 118 panel-accepted correct, 21 wrong, 25 ambiguous; majority assigns 134, 29, 1. Sixteen candidates change accuracy status *solely* through aggregation. ARC@2048 does not reverse — it stays at −1.78 pp (95% CI [−3.33, −0.44]) under every basis because adjudication candidates are MATH responses.
- **Panel reliability.** All three judges scored 100/100 mechanical controls correctly; across all 264 items, 239 (90.53%) received identical labels with Fleiss' κ = 0.851.

The authors previously released the companion auditing study "When the judge changes, so does the measurement: Auditing LLM-as-judge reliability" (arXiv:2607.08535, cited as [7]), which underpins the scorer-provenance analysis here. The paper is unusually disciplined about uncertainty reporting: 10,000-replicate PCG64 bootstrap with questions as resampling units, explicit cross-configuration association vs causal-effect caveats, and a frozen three-lane panel whose membership was decided before candidate aggregates were inspected. An anonymous artifact accompanies the paper with frozen sample identifiers, prompt/model bindings, scorer definitions, 164 anonymized item-level labels, and offline reconstruction code.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** The conceptual move — splitting execution evidence from scoring correctness and freezing the former before adjudication — is genuinely useful and underused in mainstream benchmark reporting. It is more of a measurement-hygiene contribution than a new method, but it is well-motivated and the empirical payoff (the −7.89 → +6.89 reversal) is concrete.
- **Rigor.** Strong on uncertainty quantification (question-clustered bootstrap CIs, explicit unadjusted-for-multiplicity caveat, exact sign/McNemar p-value reported for the small DeepSeek transition). The authors are admirably explicit that LLM panel agreement "supports stability of the instrument, not a unique truth" and that Fleiss' κ = 0.851 is "descriptive agreement among correlated LLM instruments, not a human gold standard."
- **Reproducibility.** Good: frozen sample identifiers, deterministic PCG64 seeds (20260720 for Qwen, 20260722 for DeepSeek), released 164 item-level labels, and an offline builder that reconstructs figures from source hashes. Caveat: the Qwen Natural ledger did not retain complete response bodies, so its later scoring analysis relies on parsed answers and response hashes rather than independent raw-text reparse — a real reproducibility hole that the authors disclose.
- **Significance.** The reversal finding is the headline, but its reach is bounded: the candidate-selection asymmetry (DeepSeek 70/70 reviewed vs Qwen 24/77) is the *driver* of the reversal, so the result is more a demonstration of "selective verification can lie" than a general claim about scorers. The authors say so plainly.
- **Red flags.** (1) The sample is narrow — two tasks, three Qwen snapshots, two DeepSeek endpoints; cross-configuration associations bundle architecture, scale, serving stack, and generation behavior, so no population claims about model families are licensed. (2) MATH uses training-split questions that may have appeared in pretraining, so response states rather than uncontaminated capability are measured. (3) The Kimi judge lane was dropped after provider availability was observed, leaving a three-lane panel; the membership decision was made before aggregates were inspected, but the post-hoc dropout is still a data quirk worth flagging. (4) The "−7.89 → +6.89" reversal is contingent on the very asymmetric candidate coverage the audit pipeline created, so readers should not over-read it as a grader effect.

## Strengths

- Cleanly operationalizes a distinction (execution vs scoring) that the evaluation literature has talked around but rarely measures under matched budgets.
- Discloses asymmetric candidate coverage by configuration (DeepSeek 93.3%/100% vs Qwen 31.2%/85.7%) instead of hiding it, and traces the accuracy reversal to this joint selection-and-scoring pipeline.
- Reports termination and answer availability as primary endpoints precisely because they are scorer-independent, then presents scorer-sensitive correctness as a clearly labeled sensitivity analysis.
- Provides a concrete, minimal reporting protocol (execution / scoring / comparison / provenance) that benchmark authors could adopt today.
- Excellent uncertainty hygiene and honest framing of associational vs causal claims throughout.

## Limitations

- **Scope.** Two tasks (MATH, ARC-Challenge), three Qwen snapshots, two DeepSeek endpoints. No code generation, no open-ended QA, no dialogue; population-level family claims are not supported.
- **Confounds.** Cross-task comparisons bundle content, difficulty, prompt wording, answer format, and parser, although both prompts place the answer last and the cap is matched. Cross-configuration comparisons bundle architecture, scale, serving stack, and generation behavior.
- **Data leakage.** MATH uses training-split questions that may have appeared in pretraining, so the study measures response states rather than uncontaminated capability.
- **Missing Qwen response bodies.** The Qwen Natural ledger did not retain complete raw text; later Qwen scoring analysis relies on parsed answers and hashes, which limits independent reparse-based replication.
- **LLM panel, not human gold.** Shared training data and correlated reasoning errors can produce agreement without correctness; the three-lane panel was assembled after Kimi provider availability was observed (though before candidate aggregates were inspected).
- **Targeted audit, not complete adjudication.** Only 164 suspected false negatives were audited (24 Qwen, 140 DeepSeek); the full sample was not re-scored, so panel-accepted accuracy is not complete-sample truth. Unanimity leaves 25/164 candidates ambiguous, majority leaves 1.

## Relevance

对关注 LLM 评估方法论与"LLM-as-a-Judge"可靠性的研究极具参考价值——它直接量化了准确率分数如何被输出预算导致的截断状态分布与评分/验证策略污染,并给出可落地的最小报告协议,正好对接到测试时计算(test-time compute)与自纠正方法的评估争议上。其执行状态分类(LMF/LFP/NLU)和针对性验证审计流程,可作为复现/复核现有 benchmark 结论时的检查清单。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How do execution-state mixtures (LMF/LFP/NLU rates) behave on open-ended generation, code, and multi-turn dialogue tasks where no clean `FINAL:` anchor exists — does the two-layer framework still separate cleanly without a structured answer slot?
- Would the −7.89 → +6.89 accuracy reversal survive a *fully symmetric* verification pipeline (same coverage rate across configurations, e.g., adjudicating all mechanical non-correct outputs in both families)?
- Can a pre-intervention execution-state distribution be used to *match* test-time recovery methods to the case mix they are evaluated on, so that aggregate recovery accuracy is no longer confounded with the starting distribution?
- The DeepSeek MATH cap effect on LMF (5/300 → 0/300, p = 0.0625 by sign test) is suggestive but underpowered — how does the LMF-vs-cap curve look across a denser cap grid (e.g., 2k/4k/8k/16k) and across more model endpoints?
- Given that the three-lane panel is itself correlated LLM instrumentation, what would a human-gold adjudication of the 164 candidates (or a held-out subset) do to the Fleiss' κ and to the unanimity-vs-majority split?
- Is the LMF phenomenon on Qwen MATH an artifact of reasoning-trace length distributions, and could a difficulty-conditional analysis (the MATH items are already stratified across five levels) reveal whether LMF concentrates in the hardest levels?
