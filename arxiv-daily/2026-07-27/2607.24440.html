Why this matters

Decides how to spend a fixed memory budget on bigger vs. more precise VLMs — and says: defer on token probability, not stated confidence.

Critical edge

Only 100 Food101 items, four-way multiple choice, one model family, two scale points — the headline 0.98 AUROC rests on very few errors per condition.

01

Methodology — two orthogonal contrasts

A clean three-arm design on a single family — Qwen2-VL — so scale isn't confounded by cross-lab training differences. Arms run on identical inputs, prompts, and seeds; an item manifest aborts any run that fails to reproduce the exact 100-item subset.

Two contrasts fall out naturally: A vs B holds size at 2B and isolates quantization; B vs C holds precision at 4-bit and isolates scale.

Signals. Internal = mean per-step token probability inside the answer span only (excluding the "Confidence:" digits, so the comparison isn't circular). Verbalized = an integer in [0,100] the model is prompted to emit, normalized to [0,1].

Stress. 100 Food101 items × 6 degradation families (JPEG, motion blur, low light, glare, rotation, resample) × 3 severities + clean = 19 conditions, 1,900 predictions per arm, 5,700 total. 4-bit uses NF4 with double quantization on a single free-tier 16 GB T4.

Primary metric. Error-detection AUROC — not ECE: when confidence variance is near zero, a "well-calibrated" 0.9 on a 90%-accurate condition can still be chance at ranking.

02

Results — the know/say gap widens with scale

Scale sharpens the internal signal and leaves the verbalized one near chance; quantization is nearly free for accuracy but expensive for the signal. Under a fixed 16 GB budget, the larger quantized model wins on every axis that matters.

03

Strengths & limitations

Strengths

  • Two-contrast design that genuinely isolates scale from quantization — the confound most prior work leaves tangled.
  • Counterintuitive, useful finding: the internal–verbalized gap widens with scale, contradicting "stated confidence is poor because the model is small."
  • Deployable rule tied to a real 16 GB budget and validated by threshold-transfer, not just aggregates.
  • Strong reproducibility: public code, per-prediction CSVs, fixed seeds, manifest-enforced inputs, single-T4 runs.
  • Principled metric argument (AUROC over ECE) demonstrated on the paper's own data.

Limitations

  • n = 100 Food101 items; wide CIs and undefined AUROC near ceiling — the 0.98 is fragile.
  • One dataset, one format (four-way MC), one family, only two scale points: no scaling law, no emergence threshold.
  • Quantization measured at 2B only — the 7B-4bit vs 7B-fp16 contrast is missing.
  • Synthetic degradations approximate, not reproduce, real camera artifacts.
  • Multiple-choice forces an answer: refusal / abstention is unobservable.
  • 2B verbalized weakness is partly confounded with a 64% parse rate, not purely calibration.
  • Severe low light defeats every config (acc 0.80 even at 7B) — explicitly out of scope.
04

Open questions

Q1

Does the "gap widens with scale" finding hold for open-ended VQA and free-form generation, or is it specific to averaging probability over a multiple-choice answer span?

Q2

How does 4-bit quantization affect the 7B internal signal vs a 7B-fp16 baseline — is the penalty size-dependent, and does it shrink or grow with scale?

Q3

Does this replicate beyond Qwen2-VL — LLaVA, InternVL, Phi-VL — or is verbalized-at-chance a Qwen2-VL post-training artifact?

Q4

Can the 7B threshold-transfer safety (0 errors admitted) survive real distribution shift and adversarial perturbations, not just synthetic corruptions?

Q5

Is there a scale at which verbalized confidence becomes discriminative — an emergence threshold — or does the gap grow monotonically?

Q6

What is the real deferral cost when 7B coverage falls to 0.39 at low light s3 — acceptable, or does it force a cascade that negates the latency win?
