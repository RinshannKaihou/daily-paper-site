01

Methodology — bidirectional discrepancy control

1Define the gap

Per-token distance d = |π(a) − µ(a)| between the BF16 training policy π and the FP8 inference policy µ. Sequence-mean is Y; the static reference X is computed once at step 0 over a baseline set.

Y = mean_t |π(a_t) − µ(a_t)| · X = Y at step 0

2Two-sided regulation

When Y > X the discrepancy is pushed down (π → µ); when Y < X it is pushed up (π ← µ). Over-constraining shrinks the feasible set Θ_E₀ and forgoes optima; a vanishing gap overfits π to µ.

3Adaptive exponent α

The exponent encodes both correction direction and a magnitude proportional to the gap. It scales a per-token ratio ρ applied inside the clipped GRPO objective — high-discrepancy tokens get stronger correction.

α = γ · sign(A) · (1 − Y/X) · ρ = (π/µ)α, clipped at C

4AGPA four-quadrant rule

Advantage Guided Probability Adjustment combines the up/down principle with the sign of the token advantage A, preserving reward/punishment direction while modulating magnitude — a deliberate biased-estimator trade-off (Remark 4.2).

∇J_ACRL = E[ min(ρ, C) · ∇ min(r·A, clip(r)·A) ]

Strengths & Limitations

Strengths

  • Novel framing. Bidirectional "regulate to a reference band" departs cleanly from alignment (FP8-everywhere) and IS patches (TIS/MIS).
  • Fair baselines. Directly compares against current SOTA IS fixes and the BF16 gold standard on the same VeRL/vLLM/FSDP stack.
  • Strong scalability. 3B → 7B → 32B Dense → 30B-A3B MoE, across GRPO/PPO/DAPO, including MoE-unfriendly MXFP8.
  • Negligible cost. ~0.1% overhead since rollout log-probs are already cached.
  • Honest trade-off. Candid about the biased-estimator admission (Remark 4.2) and evaluation scope.
  • Adversarial test. Includes a truncated-FP8 stress test, not just standard nearest-round FP8.

Limitations

  • Hyperparameter sensitivity. Relies on γ (collapses at ≥ 2.1) and a static X that may drift over very long horizons.
  • Static reference. Validated only up to ~900 steps; a moving-average X may be needed for multi-thousand-step runs.
  • Domain narrowness. Primarily math; exploration benefits unverified in RLHF with subjective rewards (writing, coding, safety).
  • Backend narrowness. Only vLLM/FSDP; multi-turn, tool-integrated, and async pipelines are out of scope.
  • Statistical rigor. Single-seed runs mitigated only by temporal averaging, on some 30-item benchmarks.
  • Biased estimator. The (π/µ)α form breaks unbiasedness by design — defensible but a theoretical step back.
  • No code release mentioned as of the manuscript.
04

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. How does ACRL behave under KL-divergence or squared-distance discrepancy metrics instead of absolute distance, and does the choice materially affect the reference band?
  2. Would a moving-average or exponentially-decayed X outperform the static step-0 reference on multi-thousand-step runs where weights drift substantially?
  3. Does ACRL's exploration boost transfer to subjective-reward RLHF (creative writing, coding, safety), or does the entropy increase become harmful there?
  4. How does ACRL compare head-to-head against alignment-based FP8-everywhere (LMSYS Org 2025) on wall-clock and memory, not just accuracy?
  5. Can the biased (π/µ)α form be replaced with a provably-bounded-bias variant, or does any unbiased correction inevitably suffer the MIS vanishing-ratio pathology?
  6. What happens in multi-turn / tool-integrated RL and asynchronous rollout-training pipelines, which the current evaluation explicitly excludes?
