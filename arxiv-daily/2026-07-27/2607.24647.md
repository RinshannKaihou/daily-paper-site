> **一句话总结：** 论文提出把"搜索效率"(Pareto 前沿的 AUC)作为评估自主研究(AR)系统的第一类目标，比较了贪心、束搜索、树搜索、进化四种固定结构后引入自适应的 **fluid search**——用 UCB bandit 在爬山链森林中动态分配评估预算，在 AutoLab 的 12 个系统优化任务上以 0.780 的归一化 AUC 几乎追平 0.784 的 per-task oracle，并超过最强固定策略 beam 的 0.718。

> **TL;DR:** The paper argues that autonomous-research (AR) systems should be evaluated by **search efficiency** (AUC of the budget–reward Pareto frontier), not just final outcome. Across 12 AutoLab systems-optimization tasks (proposer = Qwen3-Coder-480B, budget = 500 evaluations, frontier width = 64), no fixed search structure (greedy / beam / tree / evolutionary) is universally most efficient, and efficiency is only weakly correlated with final outcome (Pearson r = 0.40). The proposed **fluid search**—a UCB portfolio bandit over a forest of hill-climbing chains—achieves normalized AUC reward **0.780 vs. beam's 0.718 and the per-task oracle's 0.784**, recovering near-optimal allocation without knowing the best search structure in advance.

## 核心贡献

- **将搜索效率确立为 AR 系统的第一类评估目标**：提出用 Pareto 前沿 `R(n) = max_{i≤n} r_i` 的 **AUC reward `R̄`**（预算内前沿平均高度）作为效率度量，与终态 `R̂ = R(N)` 区分，并补充了 `convergence ratio = R̄/R̂` 与 `task ceiling R*` 等标量。
- **系统比较四类经典搜索结构**：在 AutoLab 的 12 个系统优化任务上，固定 Qwen3-Coder-480B 提议器、500 次评估预算、宽度 64、3 个 seed，发现 **没有任何一种固定结构普遍最高效**——beam 在 `gaussian_blur / flash_attention / levenshtein_distance` 起步最快，evolutionary 在 crash 多的 `bvh_raytracer / regex_engine` 上最强。
- **效率与最终结果是两条独立维度**：decompose 后 `R̄` 与 `R̂` 的相关性仅 Pearson r = 0.40；同一终点下不同收敛路径差别很大，仅看终点会误导（如 `aes128_ctr` 上 greedy 终点最高但 beam 更高效）。
- **提出 fluid search 预算自适应策略**：用 UCB 风格的 bandit `U_k = ρ_k + w·q_k + c·√(ln(n+1)/(n_k+1))`（w=1, c=0.5）把评估预算动态分配给一森林爬山链，集成 5 个机制（全局 archive、reseed、crashed-parent adoption、merge-softening、warm-start breadth ramp）。
- **近 oracle 性能**：fluid 归一化 AUC 达 0.780，超过最强固定策略 beam 的 0.718，并几乎追平事先知道每任务最佳结构的 per-task oracle (0.784)。

## Key Contributions

- **Reframes AR evaluation around search efficiency.** The paper formalizes the budget–reward **Pareto frontier** `R(n) = max_{i≤n} r_i` and proposes the **AUC reward** `R̄ = (1/N) Σ R(n)` as a first-class metric alongside final outcome `R̂`, plus the `convergence ratio R̄/R̂` and an empirical `task ceiling R*` for cross-task normalization.
- **Controlled comparison of four canonical search families.** Holds the proposer (Qwen3-Coder-480B FP8), single-completion proposal operator (no multi-turn agentic loop), budget (500 evaluations), frontier width (64), and seeds (3) fixed across greedy hill climbing, beam search, MCTS-style tree search, and an evolutionary strategy on 12 AutoLab systems-optimization tasks—making search structure the only experimental variable.
- **Empirical finding: no universally superior search structure.** Beam wins on rapid-frontier-deepening tasks (gaussian_blur, flash_attention, levenshtein_distance); evolutionary wins on crash-prone tasks (bvh_raytracer, regex_engine); greedy and tree each have their own favorable regimes.
- **Efficiency and outcome are distinct axes.** The Pearson correlation between `R̂` and the convergence ratio is only r = 0.40; on aes128_ctr greedy reaches the highest outcome via prolonged refinement while beam is more efficient—a single endpoint metric is incomplete and sometimes misleading.
- **fluid: a budget-adaptive portfolio bandit.** A UCB rule scores each hill-climbing chain by recent improvement rate `ρ_k` (fraction of last 5 edits that improved the incumbent), incumbent quality `q_k`, and an exploration bonus; five auxiliary levers (global archive with sampled reseeding, crashed-parent adoption, merge-softening, warm-start breadth ramp 16→64) are each individually disable-able so the bare bandit is the ablation baseline.

## Methodology

**Setting.** AR is modeled as a search loop: a proposer generates candidate artifact edits, a verifier scores them at some cost, and a search structure records/organizes explored states. Because LLMs make generation cheap, *verification* (the trial itself) is the binding constraint, so the budget is measured in **number of evaluations n**, isolating sample efficiency from wall-clock and tokens.

**Metrics.** From the run trace they extract: best reward `R̂ = R(N)`; AUC reward `R̄ = (1/N) Σ_n R(n)` (mean frontier height = anytime performance); task ceiling `R*` (best across all policies/seeds); convergence ratio `R̄/R̂ ∈ [0,1]`.

**Fixed-policy comparison.** Four search families (greedy, beam, tree, evolutionary) are run at matched frontier width 64 and budget 500 over 3 seeds on 12 AutoLab tasks (e.g., gaussian_blur, fft_rust, aes128_ctr, radix_sort, bvh_raytracer, regex_engine, hash_join). Each proposal is a single Qwen3-Coder-480B completion conditioned on task + prior evaluation history.

**fluid search.** A budget-adaptive portfolio bandit over a forest of hill-climbing chains (max 64 chains, warm-start restricts active pool to 16 through evaluation 150). At each step the UCB index

```
U_k = ρ_k + w·q_k + c·√(ln(n+1)/(n_k+1)),   w=1, c=1/2
```

selects the highest-scoring idle chain to receive the next evaluation (evaluations are asynchronous, up to 32 in flight). `ρ_k` is the fraction of the chain's 5 most recent evaluations that improved its incumbent; `q_k` is incumbent reward normalized to the best artifact so far; `n_k` is the chain's evaluation count. Five optional levers import ideas from the fixed policies: (1) a run-global archive of distinct incumbents with sampled reseeding (population diversity from evolutionary); (2) reseeding of crashing/stagnant chains plus bounded crashed-parent adoption (keep editing a crashed artifact a few steps with the verifier error in context); (3) merge-softening to suppress duplicate work; (4) warm-start breadth ramp (beam's early depth compounding). With all five disabled the method reduces to the bare bandit forest, isolating each mechanism's contribution.

## Results & Highlights

- **fluid is the most efficient single policy and essentially matches the oracle.** On the per-task-ceiling-normalized AUC reward averaged over all 12 tasks: **fluid = 0.780**, the strongest fixed policy **beam = 0.718**, and the **per-task oracle = 0.784** (Figure 4c). fluid thus closes ~96% of the gap to a policy that knows the best search structure per task in advance.
- **No fixed structure is universally best.** Beam is the fastest starter on gaussian_blur, flash_attention, and levenshtein_distance (approaches its plateau while others still climb slowly), but tree/evolutionary catch up or overtake at larger budgets; evolutionary wins the crash-prone bvh_raytracer and regex_engine. Policy curves cluster near the reference on saturated tasks (hash_join, fft_rust) but separate sharply on regex_engine, z_order_range_scan, bvh_raytracer (Figure 2).
- **Efficiency and outcome are weakly correlated.** The Pearson correlation between best reward `R̂` and convergence ratio `R̄/R̂` across the 12 tasks is only **r = 0.40** (Figure 3b)—empirically confirming they are distinct axes.
- **Endpoint-only evaluation can be misleading.** On aes128_ctr, greedy reaches the highest outcome through prolonged refinement, while beam converges more quickly and is therefore the more efficient policy despite a lower peak.
- **fluid matches beam on the opening budget then widens its lead.** On the normalized frontier curve `R(n)/R*` averaged across tasks (Figure 4b), fluid tracks beam early and accumulates its advantage through the remainder of the 500-evaluation run, with ±1 SE bands showing the separation.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** The framing of "search efficiency as a first-class AR objective" is conceptual reframing more than a new algorithm; anytime/AUC evaluation is standard in AutoML [2], molecular design [3,4], and sample-efficient optimization. The genuine algorithmic contribution is bringing a UCB portfolio bandit [29–31] over multi-start hill-climbing chains [26–28] to *LLM-driven* AR with five engineered levers. The authors are honest that fluid adapts only allocation among a *homogeneous* set of hill-climbing chains, not a *heterogeneous* portfolio mixing tree/beam/evolutionary structures—an explicitly acknowledged limitation rather than a hidden one.
- **Rigor.** Strong controls (fixed proposer, single completion, matched width/budget/seeds). The convergence-ratio vs. outcome correlation (r = 0.40) is a clean, persuasive demonstration that the two axes are distinct. The five levers are individually disable-able so the bare bandit serves as an ablation baseline.
- **Missing ablations.** The paper reports the per-lever contribution only in aggregate ("with all disabled it reduces to the bare bandit forest") and does not present a per-lever ablation table showing the marginal value of, e.g., crashed-parent adoption alone, or the warm-start ramp alone. Given that several levers (warm-start ramp, archive) are explicitly imported from beam and evolutionary, quantifying their standalone contributions would strengthen the claim that fluid's gains come from *adaptivity* rather than from smuggling in the best fixed-policy mechanisms.
- **Benchmark scope.** All 12 tasks are **systems-optimization / kernel-acceleration** tasks from AutoLab—cheap, formal, immediate verification. This is precisely the regime the paper itself flags as the one where efficiency matters *least* (verification is cheap). The headline motivation (wet-lab, fabrication, clinical) is not demonstrated; the empirical evidence is confined to the easy-verification regime, so the practical-payoff argument is partly aspirational.
- **Single proposer.** Only Qwen3-Coder-480B (FP8) is tested. Whether the per-task best-structure ranking—and fluid's near-oracle behavior—transfers across proposers or model scales is unverified.
- **Baseline choice.** The fixed policies are configured at frontier width 64 and budget 500, but there is no tuning sweep showing these are fair for each method (e.g., evolutionary might favor different population sizes; tree might favor different expansion factors). The "no universal winner" conclusion could partly reflect per-method hyperparameter mismatch rather than intrinsic structure.
- **Oracle definition.** The per-task oracle selects the best fixed policy *post hoc* on the same runs, which is the most lenient possible oracle; fluid matching it is impressive but the oracle itself is a weak target (it cannot switch policies mid-run or combine structures).

## Strengths

- Clean, well-motivated reframing: efficiency (AUC) vs. outcome as orthogonal axes, backed by the r = 0.40 measurement.
- Rigorous controlled comparison isolating search structure as the single experimental variable.
- fluid is principled, simple to describe (one UCB index + five composable levers), and delivers a non-trivial win over the strongest fixed policy (0.780 vs 0.718) while reaching the oracle (0.784).
- Honest, non-overclaiming discussion that explicitly limits the contribution to "the first autonomous level" of the Cao–Yang ladder and frames vocabulary/verifier gaps as future work rather than claiming general research autonomy.
- Code and algorithm pseudocode (Appendix A) released for reproducibility.

## Limitations

- All 12 tasks are cheap-verification systems-optimization problems; the costly-verification motivation is argued but not empirically demonstrated.
- fluid adapts only the *allocation* among hill-climbing chains, not the *form* of search (homogeneous portfolio); a heterogeneous-portfolio extension is left as future work.
- Only one proposer (Qwen3-Coder-480B) tested; generalization across models/scales is unverified.
- No per-lever ablation table isolating the marginal contribution of each of fluid's five mechanisms.
- Plateaus on several tasks suggest a "vocabulary gap" that more efficient traversal of a fixed frame cannot overcome—fluid improves within-frame efficiency but cannot escape representational ceilings.
- Verification is fixed and immediate, excluding discoveries whose value only emerges through later reuse or interaction (the "verifier gap").

## Relevance

This paper is highly relevant to research on **LLM-driven autonomous research agents and test-time search**: it directly concerns how to evaluate and design search policies (FunSearch/AlphaEvolve-style program search, Tree of Thoughts, AIDE) and introduces a practical budget-adaptive policy (fluid) that any code-optimization or science-search agent could adopt. For work on agentic systems, AR benchmarks, and sample-efficient optimization, it offers both a new evaluation paradigm (AUC of the Pareto frontier) and a deployable algorithm.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would fluid's near-oracle performance hold with a **heterogeneous portfolio** (chains running greedy, beam, tree, evolutionary in parallel) rather than only hill-climbing chains?
- How do the results change across proposers of different scale/family, and does the per-task ranking of fixed structures remain stable or shift?
- What is the **per-lever marginal contribution** of each of fluid's five mechanisms (archive, crashed-parent adoption, warm-start ramp, etc.) in isolation?
- Can the AUC-of-frontier metric and fluid-style adaptation be extended to **costly-verification** domains (wet-lab, fabrication, clinical) where the paper's motivation actually lies but no experiments are run?
- How does fluid interact with the "vocabulary gap" plateau—can budget adaptivity delay the plateau, or is the ceiling fundamentally proposer-limited?
- Could the per-task oracle be strengthened to allow **mid-run policy switching**, giving a tighter upper bound than a fixed post-hoc best-structure selector?
