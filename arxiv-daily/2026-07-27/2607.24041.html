01 / METHOD

From a design matrix to a combinatorial locator

STEP 1 · SETUP

Ridge with vanishing penalty

Estimate β̂λ with λ = λn ↓ 0 — a computable proxy for the ridgeless interpolator. Proportional regime p/n → γ.

STEP 2 · REDUCTION

Two designs → one profile

Heterogeneous (Model 1) and finite-rank-dependent (Model 2) Gaussian designs rotate to a single variance-profile matrix S whose zeros govern everything.

STEP 3 · RISK

Hard-edge deterministic equivalents

Bias & variance converge a.s. to functions of a fixed-point vector r(λ); two per-coordinate numbers — rl(0) and ∂rl(0) — decide divergence (Thm. 3).

STEP 4 · RULE

Peaks read off a bipartite graph

Bias support = unmatched columns of GS (Thm. 9). Variance peaks occur exactly where the residual design fails the strong-Hall property (Thm. 14).

Core thesis. It is the support pattern of the covariance — not its rotation, bulk spectrum, or any engineered block — that places every peak. Data augmentation (block permutation) is shown to be a natural Model-2 instance, predicting extra peaks that are confirmed empirically.

03 / ASSESSMENT

What holds up — and what doesn't

Strengths

  • Clean, memorable thesis. Zeros of the covariance — not rotation or bulk spectrum — drive multiple descent, with an exact combinatorial locator (Dulmage–Mendelsohn / strong Hall).
  • Unified treatment of heterogeneous (Model 1) and dependent (Model 2) designs under one variance-profile reduction; data augmentation is a concrete, practical instance.
  • Honest separation between the rank-deficient regime (proven) and the positive-definite regime (conjectured + simulated) — no overclaiming.
  • Recovers classical formulas [Hastie 2022] as a special case (Example 2) — a built-in sanity check.
  • Non-Gaussian robustness probed with Student-t₃ (infinite third moment); independent methodological by-product in Appendix C.

Limitations

  • Requires covariances to be simultaneously diagonalizable and positive semi-definite. Non-commuting rank-deficient covariances are not covered — reduction breaks down there.
  • Proven only for a vanishing ridge λn ↓ 0, not the exact ridgeless interpolator at λ = 0 (Remark 2 gap, unresolved).
  • The positive-definite single-peak claim is a conjecture — simulation + heuristic only, not a theorem.
  • Switching rule treats exact zeros; behavior under small-but-nonzero eigenvalues is open — limiting real-data applicability where degeneracy is approximate.
  • Experiments are synthetic or semi-synthetic; no standard ML benchmark with real labels. Ridge value & MC counts underspecified; no code released.
04 / OPEN

Questions for further exploration

Q1 · DIAGONALIZABILITY

Can the simultaneous-diagonalizability requirement be dropped — e.g. for approximately commuting covariances, or with an approximate Dulmage–Mendelsohn notion?

Q2 · APPROXIMATE ZEROS

How robust is the peak rule when exact zeros become eigenvalues that vanish polynomially in n, as in real embedding covariances?

Q3 · EXACT RIDGELESS

Can the analysis reach the true λ = 0 limit by uniformly controlling the smallest nonzero singular value, closing Remark 2?

Q4 · PD CONJECTURE

Does the single-peak conjecture for positive-definite non-commuting covariances admit a rigorous proof via a global hard-edge law extending Louart–Couillet?

Q5 · LEARNED FEATURES

How does the switching condition interact with learned representations (later transformer layers, post-fine-tuning) where effective rank evolves with training?

Q6 · KERNEL / RF MODELS

Can the variance-profile + matching framework predict multiple descent in kernel regression or random-features models with heterogeneous inputs?
