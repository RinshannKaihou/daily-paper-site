> **一句话总结：** 本文首次将反事实记忆（counterfactual memorization）引入奖励模型（RM）研究，通过在 PRISM 与 COMMUNITY 两个人类偏好数据集上训练 Llama-3.2-1B + LoRA 奖励模型并构建"记忆地图"，结合 84 维假设驱动特征回归与稀疏自编码器（SAE）数据驱动分析，揭示了判别式 RM 训练导致的三类记忆缺陷：把记忆容量错误分配给易分的高 margin 偏好对、记忆了模型身份/用户采样策略等数据集伪影、并对长度与顺从度等简单启发式关联在未见数据上过度泛化，从而说明当前 RM 在上下文相关场景下尚不能可靠判断回答质量。

> **TL;DR:** The first application of counterfactual memorization to reward models trains Llama-3.2-1B+LoRA RMs on 25 splits of the PRISM and COMMUNITY human-preference datasets, builds a TM/TG/CM "memorization map," and—via an 84-feature SHAP regression (CM R² of 0.39/0.33) plus a Matryoshka SAE—shows three failure modes: RMs waste capacity memorizing easy high-margin pairs rather than hard low-margin ones, memorize dataset artifacts (response-model identity in PRISM, user recruitment wave in COMMUNITY), and overgeneralize length/compliance heuristics to unseen pairs (reward-hacking-prone biases that erode RM robustness outside the training distribution).

## 核心贡献

- **首次将反事实记忆框架引入奖励模型研究**：定义并计算偏好对级别的记忆度量 CM = TM − TG（训练记忆 vs 测试泛化的期望正确分类概率之差），通过对同一 RM 在 25 个数据子集上训练来估计，并构建可视化的"记忆地图"（Figure 2），为分析"RM 学到/记住了什么"提供了细粒度工具。
- **提出 84 维假设驱动特征集**：覆盖拟人化、复杂度（含长度）、对话行为、情感、礼貌、规则奖励、谄媚等 7 个特征块，结合数据集元数据，作为解释 TM/TG/CM 的外生变量。
- **通过 SHAP 回归定量定位记忆/泛化的主导因素**：用 3 层 MLP 对 TM/TG/CM 回归并计算条件 Shapley 值，发现用户偏好 margin 是 TM 的主导预测因子但对 TG 几乎无解释力（导致高 CM），即存在"记忆错配"。
- **通过稀疏自编码器（SAE）提供数据驱动的互补证据**：复现并改造 Movva et al. (2025) 的奖励模型 SAE，将记忆地图划分为 4 个区域并用区域分类损失训练 Matryoshka Batch Top-k SAE，再用 gpt-5 生成潜在特征描述、gpt-5-mini 验证相关性，刻画不同记忆区域对应的回答特征。
- **总结出判别式 RM 训练的三类记忆缺陷模式**：记忆错配（misallocated memorization）、数据集伪影记忆（dataset artifacts）、启发式过度泛化（overgeneralization），并据此对 RM 目标函数设计、评估基准设计（如 RewardBench 2）与因果导向训练提出具体建议。

## Key Contributions

- **First counterfactual-memorization analysis of reward models**: Formalizes preference-pair-level memorization as CM = TM − TG (the difference in expected correct-classification probability when a pair is in vs. out of the training set), estimates it by training the same RM on 25 user-level-split subsets of the data, and visualizes the result as a "memorization map" (Figure 2) — a new diagnostic lens for what RMs actually learn.
- **Hypothesis-driven 84-feature annotation set**: Defines 7 feature blocks (anthropomorphism, complexity/length, dialog acts, emotion, politeness, rule-based rewards, sycophancy) plus dataset metadata as candidate determinants of memorization, generalization, and their difference.
- **SHAP regression pinpoints dominant drivers**: Trains a 3-layer MLP per endogenous variable (TM/TG/CM) and uses conditional Shapley values to show that user preference margin dominates TM but barely predicts TG (hence high CM), identifying the core "misallocation of memorization" pathology.
- **Data-driven SAE corroboration**: Reimplements the reward-model SAE of Movva et al. (2025) as a Matryoshka Batch Top-k SAE on response-embedding differences with a CM-region auxiliary loss; auto-labels latents with gpt-5 and validates them with gpt-5-mini, mapping which response styles land in each memorization region.
- **Three named memorization pathologies with concrete prescriptions**: Crystallizes the findings into misallocated memorization (memorize easy high-margin pairs instead of hard low-margin ones), dataset-artifact memorization (model identity, user recruitment wave), and heuristic overgeneralization (length, compliance), and translates them into actionable guidance for Bradley-Terry objective design, out-of-distribution evaluation benchmarks, and causally-informed training.

## Methodology

**Counterfactual memorization setup.** For each preference pair (x, y⁺, y⁻) the authors define Training Memorization (TM) = E[p(y⁺≻y⁻) | pair in training] and Testing Generalization (TG) = E[p(y⁺≻y⁻) | pair held out], with CM = TM − TG. They estimate these by training the *same* RM on 25 randomly sampled subsets of the data, where each preference pair is present in 20 subsets and held out of 5. Splits are done at the user level to prevent preference leakage (a greedy+search procedure caps user-user co-occurrence at 3/5), and CM is aggregated at the user level for tractability.

**Reward model.** A Llama-3.2-1B backbone is fine-tuned via a standard LM → SFT (1 epoch) → RM (3 epochs) pipeline using LoRA (r = 32, α = 32) initialized with EVA, yielding 2.25×10⁶ trainable parameters (2% of the model). The objective is Bradley-Terry classification with a centering-loss regularizer; on PRISM a binned user-margin term is added. Samples over 1400 tokens are dropped.

**Datasets.** PRISM (Kirk et al., 2024): 1.40×10³ users, 57.9×10³ preference pairs, ordinal 1–100 ratings, 21 response models in turn 1. COMMUNITY (Zhang et al., 2025a), English-only balanced subset: 0.77×10³ users, 137×10³ preference pairs, 4 responses per turn from the same Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct.

**Hypothesis-driven determinants.** 84 features per response (7 blocks) are produced by a mix of task-specific and general-purpose LLM annotators plus dataset metadata, organized as chosen-minus-rejected differences for each preference pair.

**Regression + SHAP attribution.** A 3-layer MLP regresses each endogenous variable (TM, TG, CM) on the feature set, evaluated on a held-out 20%. Conditional Shapley values are summarized into block-level Mean Absolute Value (MAV, absolute importance) and Harmonic Mean Rank (HMR, relative importance).

**Data-driven SAE determinants.** The memorization map is partitioned into 4 regions (low/low, medium/medium, high/low, high/high TM/TG). A Matryoshka Batch Top-k SAE (≤32 latents, ~4 active/sample) is trained on response-embedding differences with an auxiliary region-classification loss; region-classifier weights give feature↔region associations. Latents are described by feeding top preference pairs to gpt-5 and validated against gpt-5-mini annotations (retained if significant at 0.05 after Bonferroni correction).

## Results & Highlights

- **A small feature set explains most RM behavior, and TM is far more predictable than TG/CM.** The MLP+SHAP regression achieves R² of **0.56/0.63 on TM, 0.52/0.38 on TG, and 0.39/0.33 on CM** (PRISM/COMMUNITY), showing RM performance is largely explained by a handful of features — and that the features that drive training memorization differ sharply from those that drive test generalization (the gap motivating CM).
- **User preference margin dominates TM but not TG — a clear misallocation of memorization.** In both datasets the margin block is a highly positive predictor of TM but a substantially worse predictor of TG, yielding high CM. The TM–margin correlation is strongly positive (Appendix H), so the RM maximizes margin on easy training pairs it cannot recognize when held out, rather than spending capacity on the hard low-margin pairs where memorization would actually help.
- **Model identity is the strongest single determinant in PRISM, and its effect is largely memorized, not generalized.** RMs develop a strong, human-correlated preference for specific response models; when a "better" model is rejected the performance drop is *moderate* if the pair was seen in training but *substantial* when unseen — and largest for the least-preferred models (Figure 4). When both responses come from the same model, training performance drops sharply.
- **Dataset-construction artifacts are memorized as if they were preferences.** In COMMUNITY, the user block has the 2nd-highest CM MAV, driven almost entirely by users from the "(At most) Complete Secondary" education group — whose distinctness traces not to language or topic but to the fact that these users came almost exclusively from a second recruitment wave (Appendix F Table 6) designed to add less-educated participants. The RM thus memorizes a *sampling-strategy* signal.
- **RMs generalize to compliance but must memorize refusals, and overgeneralize length/compliance heuristics.** Partial/full compliance yields the largest expected-SHAP gains for both TM and TG, while non-compliance (refusals) and PRISM refusal-correlates (Disclaimer, Meta Commentary) produce large CM — the RM naturally generalizes to compliant responses but memorizes refusals. Most annotations show larger MAV for TG than TM (increased CM), with length/complexity the clearest case of overgeneralization: a more verbose chosen response nets large *negative* CM, and the opposite when the chosen response is shorter.
- **SAE region analysis corroborates the heuristic story (PRISM).** The high TM/high TG region contains long neutral compliant responses; the maximum-CM region contains brief (often one-word) chosen responses that *violate* the length heuristic; the medium/medium region contains politically controversial, low-entropy-distribution cases (genuine user–user preference conflict).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty — genuinely high.** Bringing counterfactual memorization (an instance-level diagnostic from MT/language-modeling) to *reward models* is novel, and the dual hypothesis-driven (SHAP) + data-driven (SAE) design is a thoughtful triangulation rather than a single correlational method.
- **Rigor — solid but with honest caveats.** The user-level splitting and user-user co-occurrence minimization are careful, and the authors are unusually candid in Limitations: CM is computed at the user (not pair) level, each pair is held out only 5 times (potentially large TG/CM variance), and Sections 4–5 report *associations, not causal estimates* — true causality would require controlled edits to internal representations. The reduced CM R² (0.39/0.33) is plausibly a direct symptom of this variance.
- **Reproducibility — strong on data, weaker on the LLM annotators.** Datasets (PRISM, COMMUNITY) and backbone (Llama-3.2-1B) are open, hyperparameters and the 25-split protocol are specified, and code is promised. However, the 84-feature annotations and the SAE latent descriptions both depend on *unspecified "task-specific and general-purpose LLMs"* and on gpt-5/gpt-5-mini, whose prompts/temperatures are deferred to appendices — a reproducibility soft spot typical of automated-annotation pipelines.
- **Significance — conceptual rather than benchmark-driven.** The paper does not improve any leaderboard number; its value is diagnostic and prescriptive. The three pathologies and the call for OOD evaluation (echoing RewardBench 2's drop when prompts resemble finetuning corpora) are timely and actionable, but the paper itself is a single-backbone (Llama-3.2-1B), PEFT-only study.
- **Red flags / things to probe.** (1) All results come from a *1B* model with LoRA; the authors argue PEFT memorizes *less* than full fine-tuning, which means their findings may *understate* memorization in production-scale RMs — a generalization claim that is asserted, not tested. (2) The Bradley-Terry-with-margin objective on PRISM explicitly injects the user margin into the loss, so the headline "RMs misallocate memorization to high-margin pairs" is partly an artifact of the training objective they themselves chose — the COMMUNITY result (no margin term) is the more convincing demonstration, and the within-COMMUNITY response differences are so rare that reliable SHAP estimation there is acknowledged to be hard. (3) No ablation on the number of splits or on full vs. PEFT fine-tuning, both of which directly bound the central CM estimates.

## Strengths

- Introduces a principled, instance-level diagnostic (counterfactual memorization) to a field that has so far mostly studied RM biases via aggregate proxies.
- Dual-evidence design: hypothesis-driven SHAP regression and data-driven SAE region analysis cross-check each other rather than relying on one interpretation method.
- Concrete, named pathologies (misallocated memorization, dataset artifacts, heuristic overgeneralization) that map cleanly onto known RLHF failure modes (verbosity, sycophancy, refusals, reward hacking) and onto recent benchmark findings (RewardBench 2).
- Honest, specific Limitations section that quantifies the methodological trade-offs (user-level CM, 5 holdouts, association-not-causation) rather than glossing over them.
- Open artifacts (code, annotations, memorization metrics) lower the barrier to follow-up work.

## Limitations

- **Scale and training method:** A single 1B backbone fine-tuned only with LoRA; generalization to full fine-tuning and to larger RMs used in practice is unverified (prior evidence suggests PEFT memorizes *less*, so effects may be understated).
- **CM estimation variance:** Each preference pair is held out only 5 times across 25 splits, and CM is aggregated at the user level to keep compute tractable — both choices increase estimator variance and likely depress the CM R².
- **Associational, not causal:** The SHAP and SAE results identify correlates of memorization, not causal drivers; causal claims would require intervention on internal representations.
- **Annotation opacity:** Automated feature annotation and SAE latent labeling depend on LLM annotators (and gpt-5/gpt-5-mini) whose prompts and settings are not surfaced in the main text.
- **Dataset-specific confounds:** The PRISM margin term is part of the training objective, partially circularly supporting the "high-margin memorization" finding; COMMUNITY's same-model responses make reliable per-feature SHAP estimation difficult.

## Relevance

这与对 RLHF 奖励模型偏差、reward hacking 与可解释性的研究兴趣高度相关：它为"RM 到底记住了什么、为何会产生 verbosity/sycophancy 等捷径行为"提供了首个细粒度的反事实记忆诊断框架，并对 RM 目标函数设计、OOD 评估基准与因果导向训练给出了可直接落地的研究方向。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Do the three memorization pathologies persist (and amplify) under full fine-tuning and at production RM scales (7B–70B), or does capacity change which features get memorized?
- How much of the "high-margin memorization" effect is intrinsic to the Bradley-Terry objective versus injected by the explicit margin term (PRISM only)? A head-to-head ablation with and without the margin term on the same data would isolate the cause.
- Can counterfactual memorization be turned into a *training signal* — e.g., an objective that penalizes high-margin-pair memorization while encouraging low-margin-pair memorization — and would that actually improve OOD robustness on benchmarks like RewardBench 2?
- Are the dataset-artifact pathologies (model identity, recruitment wave) reproducible across other preference datasets (e.g., Anthropic HH, UltraFeedback, Chatbot Arena), or are they specific to PRISM/COMMUNITY's construction?
- Given that the SHAP/SAE results are associational, what does an interventional study (controlled edits to internal RM representations) reveal about which memorized features are causally responsible for reward-hacking behavior?
