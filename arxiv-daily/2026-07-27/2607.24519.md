> **一句话总结：** 本文对六个 EEG 基础模型（LaBraM、EEGMamba、CBraMod、REVE、BENDR、BIOT）进行冻结线性探针基准测试，发现 REVE 在韩国 CAUEEG 痴呆任务上落后于手工特征约 20 个百分点，随机初始化的 REVE 反而优于预训练版本，且数据集身份可被线性探针近乎完美区分（AUROC 1.000），仅在 CHB-MIT 跨被试癫痫检测上获得唯一受控正向结果（0.793 AUROC，+9.2 pp）。

> **TL;DR:** A single-author stress test of six pretrained EEG foundation models (LaBraM, EEGMamba, CBraMod, REVE, BENDR, BIOT) under frozen linear probing on five clinical tasks across four benchmark datasets plus the Korean CAUEEG cohort. The headline finding is that frozen REVE trails hand-crafted band-power features on Korean dementia by ~20 pp AUROC (0.568 vs 0.769 on the 3-way task), a randomly-initialised REVE encoder beats the pretrained one (0.659 vs 0.570), and dataset identity is decodable from frozen REVE embeddings at AUROC 1.000 — while the only controlled positive is cross-subject ictal detection on CHB-MIT (REVE 0.793 AUROC, +9.2 pp over random-init 0.701). Conclusions about EEG foundation-model gains depend strongly on evaluation unit, dataset shift, comparator strength, and targeted controls; all experiments ran on a consumer M3 laptop (16 GB).

## 核心贡献

- **跨人群压力测试（韩国 CAUEEG 痴呆）**：在 CAUEEG 3-way 任务（记录级交叉验证）上，冻结 REVE 仅达 0.568 AUROC，而经典带功率特征达 0.769，落后约 20 pp；该排序在作者提供的患者不相交保留划分上仍成立（0.565 vs 0.768），证明性能反转并非同患者泄漏伪影。
- **数据集身份在冻结嵌入中显著可解码**：线性探针可在 PCA-50 后以 AUROC 1.000 区分 ds004504 与 CAUEEG，即使限制带宽至 0.5–40 Hz 并逐 epoch z-score 仍达 0.9998；而同一 PCA-50 流水线解码韩国 3-way 诊断仅 0.528，说明 REVE 表示中的主要方差方向编码的是数据集身份而非疾病。
- **针对性负对照与更强基线**：引入随机初始化、随机特征、标签置换、乱序标签微调、PCA vs 高斯随机投影等对照；发现随机初始化 REVE 在韩国痴呆上反而优于预训练版本（+8.9 pp，方向一致通过 15 次配对评估）；在 AD 任务上经被试级聚合与更强经典/非线性基线后，REVE 的 epoch 级优势消失。
- **CHB-MIT 癫痫检测为唯一受控正向结果**：在 23 名被试全队列上，REVE 达 0.793 AUROC，+9.2 pp 优于随机初始化编码器（0.701），随机特征仅 0.505，标签置回 0.500；正负样本来自同一记录会话从而设计性消除会话级混淆，结果对 ±30 s 保护带（0.798）和独立无癫痫记录负样本（0.787）稳健。
- **六模型统一基准**：在统一冻结探针协议、被试不相交划分与文档化的队列/模型例外下，评估五种架构家族的六个公开检查点，明确披露预训练暴露（如 BIOT 在 CHB-MIT/TUH 上为域内）。
- **副发现**：2 通道二值化 Sleep-EDF 上无任何基础模型超过经典特征；AD vs FTD 差异诊断在所有冻结探针下均弱且不稳定（REVE 64.7% AUROC）；更强经典/非线性基线收窄 CHB-MIT 优势并消除名义上的 AD 优势。

## Key Contributions

- **Cross-population stress test (Korean CAUEEG dementia)**: On the CAUEEG 3-way Normal/MCI/Dementia task (recording-level CV, since the release ships no patient identifier), frozen REVE reaches only 0.568 AUROC vs 0.769 for classical band-power features — a ~20 pp deficit that holds on the authors' patient-disjoint held-out split (0.565 vs 0.768), ruling out same-patient leakage as the cause of the reversal.
- **Dataset identity is prominently decodable from frozen embeddings**: A linear probe separates ds004504 from CAUEEG at AUROC 1.000 even after PCA to 50 components, and stays at 0.9998 after a pre-specified band-limited replication (0.5–40 Hz, per-epoch z-scoring). The identical PCA-50 pipeline decodes Korean 3-way diagnosis at only 0.528, showing the leading variance directions encode dataset membership rather than disease.
- **Targeted negative controls and stronger comparators**: The author applies random-initialisation, random-feature, label-permutation, scrambled-label fine-tuning, and PCA-vs-GRP controls to selected REVE findings. Random-init REVE beats pretrained REVE on Korean dementia by +8.9 pp (unanimous across 15 paired seed×fold evaluations); on Alzheimer's disease the epoch-level REVE advantage disappears after subject aggregation and stronger comparators.
- **A single controlled positive on ictal detection**: On CHB-MIT (full 23-subject cohort, LOSO), pretrained REVE reaches 0.793 AUROC vs 0.701 for the random-initialised encoder and 0.505 for raw-signal random features; label permutation returns to 0.500. Positives and negatives share recording sessions, and the result is stable to a ±30 s guard band (0.798) and seizure-free-recording negatives (0.787).
- **Six-model harmonised benchmark**: LaBraM, EEGMamba, CBraMod, REVE, BENDR, and BIOT are evaluated under a unified frozen linear-probe pipeline with documented cohort/model exceptions (e.g. BIOT is in-domain on CHB-MIT/TUH; BENDR results are confounded by bandwidth/channel mismatches) and subject-disjoint splits where identifiers permit.
- **Secondary findings**: No foundation model beats classical features on 2-channel binary Sleep-EDF; AD-vs-FTD remains difficult (REVE 64.7% AUROC); enhanced classical/nonlinear comparators narrow the CHB-MIT margin while eliminating the nominal AD epoch-level advantage.

## Methodology

**Foundation models (Table 1):** Seven encoders spanning five architectural families were evaluated, of which six are retained in comparative tables: LaBraM (Transformer/BEiT, 5.8M, masked token prediction), EEGMamba (bidirectional Mamba2 SSM, 3.3M — reimplemented in pure PyTorch for CPU/MPS, all 117 pretrained parameters loaded), CBraMod (criss-cross attention, 4.9M), REVE-Base (4D positional encoding transformer, 69.4M, 60,000 h pretraining on 25,000 subjects), BENDR (wav2vec 2.0, 157M, contrastive), and BIOT (bio-transformer, 3.2M, multi-biosignal). LEAD is excluded because the downstream-training exposure of its released checkpoint could not be established for a submission-grade held-out comparison.

**Datasets (Table 2):** Four benchmark datasets — CHB-MIT (ictal detection, 23 subjects, 18 bipolar channels), TUH TUAB (normal/abnormal, 253 subjects, 21 ch), OpenNeuro ds004504 (AD vs HC / AD vs FTD, 65/88/59 subjects, 19 ch), Sleep-EDF (Wake/Sleep, 14 subjects, 2 ch) — plus CAUEEG (Korean, 1,379 recordings from 1,155 patients, 19-ch clean montage) as the cross-population stress test. REVE's pretraining exposure is cross-referenced against the published corpus: TUH is in-domain (44% of REVE pretraining), while CHB-MIT, ds004504, and CAUEEG are out-of-domain.

**Preprocessing (harmonised core pipeline, documented exceptions):** resample to 200 Hz, bandpass 0.5–70 Hz (FIR), mains-notch (50/60 Hz by cohort), common-average reference, 4 s non-overlapping epochs, artefact rejection, per-channel z-score, ±8 SD clipping. CHB-MIT disables amplitude rejection (would discard 27/28 ictal epochs of a test seizure); BENDR is resampled back to 256 Hz with channel truncation/padding; EEGMamba is run at 200 Hz. Per-epoch z-scoring removes absolute amplitude — the most discriminative ictal property — from both encoder and classical features, handicapping the classical comparator.

**Classical baseline:** Relative band power (δ/θ/α/β/γ via Welch), Hjorth parameters, Shannon spectral entropy, plus hemispheric alpha asymmetry and frontal theta (9 × N_ch + 2 features). Classifier: L2-regularised logistic regression with balanced class weights. Enhanced comparators add connectivity, entropy, DFA, wavelet, aperiodic, and nonlinear-classifier sensitivities (Supplement).

**Evaluation protocol:** Frozen linear probing — encoder weights frozen, L2 logistic regression on embeddings with C fixed a priori (C=1.0 for primary seizure/TUAB tables, C=0.1 for Alzheimer's/cross-population; a post-hoc C-sweep over {0.001…100} confirms no ranking change). CHB-MIT and Sleep-EDF use Leave-One-Subject-Out CV; TUAB and ds004504 use 5-fold stratified group CV with zero subject overlap; CAUEEG uses recording-level CV (no public patient identifier) with the authors' patient-disjoint held-out split reported as a leakage bound. Metrics: AUROC (primary) and balanced accuracy. Subject-level aggregation (mean-pooled embedding per subject) is reported where clinically primary.

**Targeted controls:** random-initialisation (matched architecture, pretrained weights removed), raw-signal random features, label permutation (probe capacity), scrambled-label fine-tuning (label dependence), PCA vs Gaussian random projection (projection-method sensitivity on the same pretrained embeddings). Dataset-identity probes train the same linear probe on dataset-membership labels.

**Hardware:** All experiments on Apple M3 (16 GB unified memory), no cloud GPU.

## Results & Highlights

- **CAUEEG 3-way dementia (Korean, recording-level CV, n=1,187)**: Frozen REVE reaches **0.568 AUROC** while the classical band-power baseline reaches **0.769 AUROC (LDA)** — a **~20 pp classical advantage** (SVM-RBF: 0.765 classical vs 0.568 REVE; LogReg: 0.761 vs 0.528). The reversal survives the patient-disjoint held-out split (REVE 0.565 vs classical 0.768, bootstrap 95% CI [0.708, 0.822]).
- **CAUEEG binary HC-vs-Dementia (n=770)**: The gap widens to **30.5 pp** — frozen REVE 0.589 AUROC vs classical 0.894 (SVM-RBF). A robustness sweep over probe family and 2/4/8 s epoch length never lowers the classical advantage below +12.7 pp.
- **Random-init beats pretrained on Korean dementia**: Across three random seeds (42/43/44) and five folds, random-init REVE grand-mean reaches **0.659 AUROC vs 0.570** for pretrained REVE — a **+8.9 pp gap** in favour of random initialisation, unanimous across all 15 paired seed×fold evaluations; one-sided Wilcoxon on fold means p=0.031. Whitening the embeddings before a linear probe widens the gap to +11.9 pp.
- **Dataset identity decodable at ceiling**: A linear probe distinguishes ds004504 from CAUEEG REVE embeddings at **AUROC 1.000 at PCA-50**, and **0.9998** after a band-limited (0.5–40 Hz, per-epoch z-scored) replication, while the same PCA-50 pipeline decodes Korean 3-way diagnosis at only 0.528. Every tested within-Western dataset pair (ds004504, TDBRAIN, ds003490) also separates at AUROC 1.000.
- **CHB-MIT cross-subject ictal detection (the one controlled positive)**: On the full 23-subject cohort (LOSO, in-fold PCA-200), pretrained REVE reaches **0.793 AUROC (+9.3 pp over classical 70.0; +9.2 pp over random-init 0.701)**; raw random features 0.505; label permutation 0.500. Stable to ±30 s guard band (0.798) and seizure-free-recording negatives (0.787). BIOT reaches 86.2% but is in-domain (CHB-MIT pretraining exposure).
- **TUH TUAB normal/abnormal (in-domain)**: REVE reaches the highest AUROC at **84.5% (+9.8 pp over classical 74.7%)** on 69.4M params; BIOT clears classical by +3.4 pp on 3.2M. All six models are in-domain here, so this speaks to pretraining scale on subtle diffuse distinctions, not cross-population transfer.
- **ds004504 AD vs HC (out-of-domain)**: Epoch-level REVE reaches **82.8% AUROC (+2.2 pp over classical 80.6%)**, but after subject-level aggregation classical features are nominally ahead (**87.8% vs 86.7% subject AUROC**) — the 1.1 pp separation is well inside fold spread, so there is no subject-level mean advantage for REVE.
- **REVE band ablation on AD**: Sub-alpha (0.5–13 Hz) and low+theta (0.5–8 Hz) match broadband (84.5% vs 83.6%), while gamma-only (30–70 Hz) falls to **61.8% AUROC (−21.8 pp)**, localising REVE's AD discriminability to slow-wave frequencies.
- **AD vs FTD differential diagnosis**: All models weak and variable; REVE best at **64.7% AUROC (+9.7 pp over classical 55.0%)** with ±15.0 SD. ROI pooling (frontal/posterior/temporal subsets) does not materially improve over the full 64.7%.
- **Sleep-EDF Wake/Sleep (2-channel)**: No foundation model beats classical — Classical 68.6%, EEGMamba 68.1% (−0.5), CBraMod 68.3% (−0.3), REVE 62.3% (−6.3), LaBraM 58.1% (−10.5). Fold variance is high (±11.9 to ±14.1) across the 14-subject LOSO.
- **Preregistered correction**: An earlier 18-subject CHB-MIT cache had produced a headline 0.920 AUROC; the author preregistered disclosure of that value alongside the corrected full-cohort rerun (0.793) rather than silently adopting the lower number.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Genuinely useful rather than architecturally novel. The Korean CAUEEG cross-population stress test, the dataset-identity probe applied systematically across cohort pairs, and the *random-init-beats-pretrained* result are the distinctive contributions — the last is a striking, rarely-reported finding. The targeted-control suite (random init, random features, label permutation, scrambled-label fine-tuning, PCA vs GRP) is conceptually standard in representation-learning auditing but is applied with unusual care and honesty about what each control does and does not test.
- **Rigor**: High on the dimension that matters most for a benchmark critique. Pretraining exposure is cross-referenced against the REVE corpus list (TUH in-domain; CHB-MIT/ds004504/CAUEEG out-of-domain). Same-patient leakage is bounded, not just acknowledged, via the patient-disjoint held-out split. A preregistered correction discloses the earlier 0.920 CHB-MIT value. The classical baseline is stress-tested with a NARPS-style robustness sweep (probe family, epoch length), and the author honestly notes the per-epoch z-scoring *handicaps* the classical ictal comparator (amplitude-aware baseline not evaluated).
- **Reproducibility**: Mixed. Datasets are public (PhysioNet, OpenNeuro, TUH by application, CAUEEG), and the entire benchmark ran on a consumer M3 laptop without cloud GPU — lowering the hardware barrier. EEGMamba's selective scan was ported to pure PyTorch. However, REVE weights require gated HuggingFace access, and **code/per-fold split indices are promised "on request to editors and reviewers" and "upon publication"** rather than released at submission — a real gap for a paper whose value depends on others re-running the protocol. The single-author status also concentrates verification responsibility.
- **Significance**: Methodologically significant as a cautionary benchmark; its findings directly challenge the headline claims of several recent EEG foundation models (most pointedly REVE on cross-population clinical transfer). The "random-init beats pretrained on Korean dementia" and "dataset identity at AUROC 1.000" results are the kind of evidence that should change how the field reports EEG-FM gains.
- **Red flags and weaknesses worth naming**:
  - **CAUEEG primary numbers are recording-level, not patient-grouped**, because the release has no public patient identifier; the patient-disjoint held-out analysis is a *single fixed split* (111 binary / 172 3-way recordings) giving wide intervals, and repeat recordings entirely inside the training split remain unquantified. The author flags this clearly but it limits causal strength.
  - **Statistical power is thin**: CHB-MIT LOSO fold statistics are explicitly described as descriptive (overlapping training sets, no family-wise correction); the random-init two-sided Wilcoxon floors at p=0.0625 for n=5 folds.
  - **The dataset-identity probe cannot isolate recording site from hardware, preprocessing, population, or clinical composition** — it establishes decodable membership only, not a mechanism. The author states this honestly but readers may over-interpret.
  - **BENDR and LaBraM results are confounded** by sampling-rate, bandwidth, channel-padding, and position-mapping mismatches; their frozen results are explicitly *not* clean tests of pretraining objective.
  - **CHB-MIT uses approximate bipolar positional coordinates for REVE** (the one task REVE wins), and a bipolar-aware assignment is untested — a caveat directly on the positive result.
  - **Single author + LLM-assisted coding** raises (acknowledged) verification questions, though the machine-readable evidence registry is a mitigation.

## Strengths

- Unusual intellectual honesty: preregistered correction of the 0.920 headline, explicit documentation of every cohort/model exception, and clear statements of what each negative control does and does not test.
- The cross-population Korean stress test is exactly the kind of external-cohort generalisation test the field has been missing; most prior benchmarks evaluate within-distribution (e.g., TUH-pretrained models tested on TUH).
- The random-init-beats-pretrained finding, combined with the anisotropy/whitening analysis showing the gap *widens* after removing covariance conditioning, is a strong representational (not architectural) indictment.
- Subject-level aggregation correctly corrects the epoch-level AD advantage that would otherwise inflate apparent foundation-model performance.
- Runs entirely on consumer hardware, lowering the reproducibility barrier and demonstrating the protocol is tractable without industrial compute.

## Limitations

- CAUEEG evaluation is recording-level (no public patient identifier); the patient-disjoint sensitivity bound is a single fixed split with wide intervals, and intra-training-split repeats are unquantified.
- The dataset-identity probes confound site, device, preprocessing history, population, and clinical composition — they establish decodable membership but not its source.
- BENDR and LaBraM frozen-probe results are confounded by implementation mismatches (sampling rate, bandwidth, channel padding, position mapping) and should not be read as clean tests of their pretraining objectives.
- CHB-MIT bipolar coordinates for REVE are approximate, and the per-epoch z-scoring removes absolute amplitude — the single most discriminative ictal feature — handicapping the classical comparator (an amplitude-aware baseline is not evaluated).
- Statistical power is limited by small subject/fold counts, overlapping CV training sets, and multiple exploratory comparisons; effect estimates and targeted controls are emphasised over confirmatory significance.
- Code, split indices, and full per-fold results are not released at submission (promised on request / upon publication), which weakens immediate reproducibility for a benchmark whose value lies in others re-running it.

## Relevance

该论文直接触及 EEG 基础模型的临床转化可信度问题，通过负对照与跨人群压力测试揭示当前主流模型（尤其 REVE）在域外临床任务上可能不及手工特征甚至随机初始化网络，对评估 EEG-FM 的迁移性与可部署性具有方法论参考价值，与你关注的"基础模型表示质量审计、跨域泛化、负对照设计"主题高度相关。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the random-init-beats-pretrained reversal on Korean dementia generalise to other non-Western or clinically heterogeneous cohorts (e.g., TDBRAIN, or independent Asian/Latin-American datasets), or is it specific to the CAUEEG preprocessing/sensor combination?
- Would an amplitude-aware classical baseline on CHB-MIT erase REVE's +9.3 pp ictal advantage, given that per-epoch z-scoring already removes the most discriminative ictal property from both encoder and classical features?
- Can the dataset-identity signal be decomposed — by harmonising preprocessing, matching hardware, or controlling for clinical composition — to isolate which factor (site, device, population, or diagnosis mix) dominates the AUROC-1.000 separability?
- How do the conclusions change under fine-tuning (LoRA r=8 is mentioned as exploratory) vs frozen probing on the Korean cohort, and does adaptation recover the pretrained advantage or merely overfit recording-level structure?
- Do more recent or larger EEG foundation models (LEAD, EEGPT, or post-REVE checkpoints) pass the same targeted-control suite, or do they exhibit the same dataset-identity dominance and random-init deficit?
