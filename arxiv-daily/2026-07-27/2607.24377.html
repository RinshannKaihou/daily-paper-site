Methodology — three inserts into the FlashAttention loop

A fully data-free PTQ pipeline. No calibration, no QAT, no per-layer search.

Direct MXFP4 attention has two failure modes: (i) power-of-two block scaling forces a clipping-vs-underflow trade-off (under OCP, ~19.27% of block maxima fall in the (7,8) overflow-rounding region and saturate to 6); and (ii) the online-softmax recurrence breaks row-normalization — the row-sum updates use unquantized exponentials while the output accumulator uses quantized ones, so induced weights no longer sum to 1 (mean row sum 0.9336 on Wan2.2). MXAttention attacks both.

STEP 1

Fixed Hadamard Rotation

An orthogonal Hadamard transform R_H is applied online to Q and K (V-side folded into weights) for outlier suppression, à la QuaRot/SpinQuant. Rotation and block-quant run on orthogonal units — zero contention.

outlier suppression
STEP 2

Universal Optimal Scaling (UOS)

A Boundary Density Ratio lemma (g(q/2)=2·g(q)) factors the unknown block-max distribution out of the objective sign. Exact E2M1 integration then pins the global minimizer at the closed-form Qmax = 7.25 (≡ 29/4) — strictly between OCP's 8 and TFS's 6.

closed-form · distribution-free
STEP 3

Pre-Normalization Quantization (PNQ)

Quantize the unnormalized softmax tile once and reuse it in both the row-sum and output-accumulator updates. Proposition 1: induced weights satisfy P̃·1 = 1 in exact arithmetic — no extra pass over the attention matrix.

proven · zero extra traversal

The forward pass (Algorithm 2) rotates Q/K, MXFP4-quantizes Q, K, V and each P̃_ij under the UOS boundary, and reuses the quantized tile in both softmax-path updates. Theory is given as Theorem 1, Lemma 1, and Proposition 1, with appendices on wrapped-distribution analysis and exact E2M1 error integration.

Diagnostic · why Qmax = 7.25 is right
On Wan2.2, the per-block empirical MSE optimum is 7.25 for 95.0% of Q blocks, 90.0% of K blocks, and 90.0% of V blocks — even though the sweep minimizes finite-sample MSE while UOS minimizes a data-free analytical objective. The row-sum pathology of the direct baseline decomposes as zeroing −0.0541, saturation −0.0190, round-down −0.0486, round-up +0.0483 — not a single global scale, which is exactly why pre-quant global scaling can't fix it.

Strengths & Limitations

+ Strengths

  • A genuinely elegant, distribution-free derivation turns an empirical knob (Qmax) into a provably optimal constant (7.25). The proof machinery — wrapped-phase density + exact E2M1 interval integration — is reusable for other microscaling formats.
  • PNQ is a simple, kernel-friendly, provably normalization-preserving placement rule that costs nothing at runtime — and is the single largest Imaging-Quality contributor in ablation.
  • Honest fully-4-bit ablations on both models, plus mechanism-level diagnostics (row-sum decomposition, entropy dependence) that explain why the fixes work.
  • Public, production-integrated code in MindIE-SD lowers the reuse barrier on Ascend hardware.

! Limitations

  • Only attention is quantized; non-attention layers stay at original precision, so end-to-end memory and latency gains are not quantified.
  • Only two models (Wan2.2-14B, HunyuanVideo-13B) — no image diffusion, no LLM, no smaller models to test format generality.
  • The headline Wan2.2 number depends on an un-isolated hybrid-precision fallback (Block 0 + steps 38–39); readers can't separate UOS/PNQ's contribution from the fallback's.
  • No head-to-head against adaptive MXFP4 methods (SearchBFP, Unveiling-MXFP4, M2XFP) — only NVFP4-family baselines are run.
  • No wall-clock or memory numbers; "negligible overhead" is asserted algorithmically, not measured.

Open Questions

What the paper leaves on the table — the sharpest threads to pull.

Q1

How much of the Wan2.2 95.4% gap-closure survives once the hybrid-precision fallback (Block 0 + steps 38–39) is removed — i.e. the isolated contribution of UOS+PNQ under fully 4-bit attention on the main comparison?

Q2

Does UOS's distribution-independence argument extend to other microscaling element formats (E3M0 / E2M2 / E4M3) and block sizes (16, 64) — and what closed-form Qmax does each yield?

Q3

Can PNQ combine with NVFP4's finer 16-element blocks to beat NVFP4+SageAttention on similarity and MXAttention on cost simultaneously?

Q4

Why does Subject Consistency improve over FP16 under 4-bit on HunyuanVideo — a VBench artifact, a seed effect, or genuine regularization?

Q5

What are the actual wall-clock latency and HBM savings vs FP16 and vs NVFP4+SageAttention on Blackwell / MI350 / Ascend 950, given the stated motivation is inference cost?

Q6

How does MXAttention interact with caching / distillation samplers (TeaCache, pyramid attention broadcast), which change the input distribution the quantizer sees?
