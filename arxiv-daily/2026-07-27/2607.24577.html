M

Methodology

6 methods · 3 benchmarks · 12h budget · 5× repeats

Six fuzzing strategies are evaluated on robust single-agent RL subjects (intrinsic failure rates 1.1% / 1.6% / 7.2%) so that inter-method differences are not swamped by agent brittleness. A conservative differential validity check counts a crash only when an independently-trained highly-robust validation policy succeeds while the target fails.

S/L

Strengths & Limitations

side-by-side

+ Strengths

  • Unified, fair protocol with differential validity checking — prevents inflating crash counts with infeasible configurations.
  • Four complementary dimensions, including rarely-studied downstream utility (repair + safety monitoring), materially raising the evaluation bar.
  • Honest, decision-useful guidance: explicit per-complexity fuzzer-selection rules; combining complementary fuzzers beats any single one.
  • Transparent statistics (group labels + effect sizes + RSD) with a fully released artifact.

! Limitations

  • Only 3 benchmarks, single-agent RL — MARL and non-crash properties (plasticity, adaptation) out of scope; "comprehensive" is overstated.
  • 12h budget structurally disadvantages heavy generative methods (G-Model's diffusion pre-learning), biasing efficiency rankings.
  • CARLA results very high variance (MDPFuzz #UC ±55.1% RSD), making fine-grained CARLA rankings less reliable.
  • Repair is conditional — can introduce up to 2.0% regression faults and sometimes degrades robustness; depends on a sufficiently-robust validation policy that may not exist.
Q

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. How do rankings shift under a compute-normalized budget (fixed mutations / GPU-hours) instead of wall-clock — currently penalizing G-Model?
  2. Can a learned seed-to-fuzzer router (cluster seeds by reward-sensitivity, dispatch each optimally) beat the best single fuzzer?
  3. Do >95% cross-method monitor accuracies hold on truly out-of-distribution environments, or only across fuzzers on the same task?
  4. How would MARL-specific and plasticity-focused testers (excluded here) perform under the same unified protocol?
  5. What repair strategy could exploit crash data while guaranteeing sub-1% regression-fault rates?
  6. Is the near-zero seed overlap a fundamental state-space property or an artifact of each fuzzer's pruning policy?
