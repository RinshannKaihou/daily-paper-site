Why this matters

Reframes agent loops as completion reliability — distinct from proposal search — and ships an auditable, typed-loop contract that mechanically binds verifier evidence to exact code states.

Critical edge

The contract suppresses unsafe completions but also the only sound ones under a near-zero completion floor — every repository co-primary joint test fails.

03

Strengths & limitations

side by side

Strengths

  • Clean, transferable reliability vocabulary: current-vs-ever-correct, α vs β, risk–coverage, conditional dependence, sound completion.
  • Tight common-state design that removes the post-treatment risk-set bias plaguing free-running trajectory studies; the 14B replication actually changes the transition kernel.
  • Exceptional intellectual honesty: preregistered families, floor-limited passes labeled weak, reference impl explicitly decoupled from empirical claims.
  • The typed-action state machine is an immediately useful engineering prescription — fail-closed parsing would have retained the HumanEval/137 checkpoint.

Limitations

  • External validity is narrow: HumanEval (likely in training data), 27 tasks pre-selected for 7B repairability, one scaffold, four confounded coder stacks.
  • The headline stale-trace harm is induced, not observed — real-world prevalence and magnitude in production agent runs are unknown.
  • No practical repair improvement demonstrated: every repository co-primary joint test fails; contract suppresses sound completions too.
  • Verifier family and capacity are confounded; statistical power is thin (5/12/6/4 accepted completions across the four stacks).
  • Reproducibility is partial — private suites and licensed source withheld; full trajectory re-execution is not possible for reviewers.
04

Open questions

for further exploration

  1. Does the stale-evidence resurrection mechanism generalize beyond HumanEval — e.g. to SWE-bench-style multi-file tasks where traces are larger and provenance harder? The 14B result rests on 27 toy tasks.

  2. How often does one- or k-step staleness arise naturally in production agent loops, and does the induced +22.2-pt harm rate over- or under-estimate field reality?

  3. Can the conditional dependence φ between same-family verifiers be broken cheaply — test-time augmentation, prompt-order randomization, orthogonal oracles — and how does that interact with Dij?

  4. Repository factorials are dominated by a 95.8% budget-exhaustion floor. Would a stronger base coder or larger action budget lift it enough for the contract's unsafe-completion superiority to become identifiable?

  5. How much observed regression is really parser/action-schema failure (e.g. STATUS: DONE without code) rather than semantic — and is a fail-closed parser higher-ROI than the full evidence-binding contract?

  6. The contract fixes verifier risk at one operating point; in deployment calibration drift over time is the real risk. How to re-calibrate online without leaking hidden-test signal back into generation?
