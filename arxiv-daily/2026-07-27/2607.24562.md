> **一句话总结：** 论文提出 HG-CRC，一种无需重训练的后处理校准框架，通过对用户定义的分组层级树施加 Bonferroni 校正并采用"叶子优先"的阈值选择策略，为 LLM 选择性预测提供跨所有层级的"同时"风险保证；在 ARC Challenge 上将违规率从全局 CRC 的 9–47% 降至 0%（WGER=0），代价是参与率下降 22–37 个百分点。

> **TL;DR:** HG-CRC is a post-hoc, retraining-free calibration method that enforces *simultaneous* per-group risk control for LLM selective prediction by placing a Bonferroni-corrected threshold on every node of a user-defined group hierarchy and routing each test example to its deepest certified ancestor (leaf-first, with fallback). On ARC Challenge it drives the empirical violation rate from global-CRC's 9–47% down to 0% (WGER = 0) for Qwen3-4B and Llama-3.1-8B at α = 0.10, δ = 0.05 — but at a 22–37 pp participation cost, and the headline result is benchmark-specific: on MMLU-Pro the same models either abstain entirely or retain a non-zero WGER (0.014 for Llama). The paper also frankly reports an equal-coverage comparison showing most of HG-CRC's apparent edge over global CRC comes from answering fewer questions, not from the per-group structure itself.

## 核心贡献

- **形式化并实证了"群体覆盖鸿沟"**：证明标准（边际）CRC 在组分布轻微偏移下可在多达 47% 的评估试验中违反风险预算（Qwen 在 ARC 上的 mixture shift）——这是边际保证的结构性缺陷，而非有限样本假象。
- **提出 HG-CRC 框架**：对分组层级树的每个节点用 Bonferroni 校正（δ̂ = δ/|H|）独立标定阈值，配合"叶子优先、逐级回退"的选择策略与最小节点规模（Nmin = 30）剪枝，实现跨所有节点的同时风险保证。
- **残差校准（residual calibration）机制**：每个节点仅在其"残差"（未被更深层认证子节点抢答的样本）上校准，对齐校准与部署人群；提供分裂版（无条件有效）与样本内版（部署默认，带可测量的 εn 松弛）两套变体。
- **级联式形式保证**：Proposition 1（分裂，无条件 εn = 0）、Proposition 2（带测试点增广的样本内，精确）、Lemma 1（部署的朴素样本内，松弛 εn 实测均值 <1%）。
- **系统鲁棒性研究**：跨 8 种实验设置（IID、群体异质、mixture shift、域漂移、提示漂移、难度漂移、标签噪声、量化）证明方法在标签噪声下"优雅保守化"而非违规，且量化不破坏风险控制（只要在部署精度下重新校准）。

## Key Contributions

- **Formalizes and empirically exposes the "group coverage gap"**: standard (marginal) CRC violates the per-group risk budget in up to 47% of bootstrap trials under a mild shift in group composition (Qwen on ARC mixture shift) — a structural consequence of the mismatch between the marginal estimand and the worst-group desideratum, not a finite-sample artifact.
- **Proposes HG-CRC**: a post-hoc, retraining-free framework that calibrates a Bonferroni-corrected threshold (δ̂ = δ/|H|) on every node of a user-defined group hierarchy, combined with a leaf-first selection policy (deepest certified ancestor whose threshold the example satisfies, else abstain) and a minimum-node-size filter (Nmin = 30).
- **Residual calibration**: each node is calibrated only on its residual — examples not already answered by certified descendants — aligning calibration and deployment populations. Two variants are provided: split (unconditionally valid, εn = 0) and in-sample (deployment default, covered by Lemma 1 with measured slack εn).
- **A cascade of formal guarantees**: Proposition 1 (split, unconditional), Proposition 2 (in-sample with test-point augmentation, exact), Lemma 1 (plain in-sample deployed procedure, slack εn measured <1% mean).
- **Comprehensive robustness study**: across 8 experimental settings the method degrades gracefully under label noise (becomes more conservative rather than violating), survives model quantization when re-calibrated at deployment precision, and its guarantees can be restored cheaply after prompt shift via targeted recalibration.

## Methodology

**Setup.** Given a pre-trained LM producing an uncertainty score s(x) (lower = more confident; default = selected-option NLL over the multiple-choice options), a labeled calibration set Dcal, and a per-example group-membership hierarchy H (a rooted tree of masks, membership nested toward the root), HG-CRC chooses per-node thresholds {τv} such that, with probability ≥ 1−δ over the calibration set, the selective risk on every node's deployed residual is ≤ α simultaneously.

**Three components:**

1. **Bonferroni correction.** A direct union bound sets the per-node confidence to δ̂ = δ/|H|, controlling family-wise error across all nodes. Each node v is certified at threshold τ iff the Clopper–Pearson upper bound Uv(τ) = F⁻¹_Beta(1−δ̂; kv+1, nv−kv) ≤ α; the optimal τv* is the largest grid value (100 quantiles) satisfying this. Nodes below Nmin = 30 examples are pruned.

2. **Residual calibration.** Because leaf-first routing deploys a node only on examples not claimed by deeper certified descendants, each node is calibrated on its residual ρv (eq. 5). Two constructions: *split* (partition Dcal by depth into disjoint folds; a node at depth ℓ sees only Fℓ, restoring exchangeability — Proposition 1, unconditional); *in-sample* (process leaf-to-root on full Dcal, marking claimed examples — Lemma 1, valid up to measured slack εn via jackknife).

3. **Leaf-first selection.** At inference, each example walks its ancestry path Π(x) leaf→root and returns the prediction of the first certified node whose threshold it satisfies; abstain otherwise. This ensures well-characterized subgroups get the most tailored threshold.

**Hiera​rchy used.** A two-level tree above the global root: domain nodes (from dataset metadata; degenerate single-domain "science" on ARC, 57 real subjects on MMLU-Pro) → difficulty leaves (easy/medium/hard by NLL percentiles). Pruning typically yields 5–15 nodes.

**Evaluation protocol.** 500 bootstrap trials with 50/50 calibration/test resampling; metrics are mean risk ± std, participation, WGER = max_v max(Rv − α, 0), and violation rate (fraction of trials where empirical risk > α). Hyperparameters: α = 0.10, δ = 0.05, Nmin = 30, grid = 100 quantiles.

## Results & Highlights

- **Marginal CRC fails under mild group shift.** Under the E3 mixture shift on ARC Challenge (calibration 1/3-1/3-1/3, test 0.17-0.33-0.50 over easy/medium/hard), global CRC violates the α = 0.10 risk budget in **47% of trials for Qwen3-4B** (mean risk 0.099, essentially at budget) and **32% for Llama-3.1-8B**, vs. groupwise CRC at **0.00% for both** (Table 3). The effect persists under exogenous difficulty (31–39% violation), confirming it is structural, not an artifact of endogenous NLL-defined bins.
- **HG-CRC eliminates violations on ARC (IID + heterogeneity).** For Qwen3-4B and Llama-3.1-8B, HG-CRC achieves **violation rate = 0.000 and WGER = 0.000** across all 500 trials (Table 2), vs. global CRC's 0.09–0.11. At 500 trials these zeros are empirical upper bounds of ~0.6% (rule of three), not certified zeros.
- **Participation cost is 22–37 pp.** Qwen3-4B participation drops 0.849 → 0.478 (−37.1 pp) and Llama 0.695 → 0.475 (−22.0 pp) relative to global CRC under residual calibration. Mean risk simultaneously falls Qwen 0.070 → 0.018 (−74%) and Llama 0.067 → 0.020 (−70%).
- **Result is benchmark-specific.** On MMLU-Pro, Qwen3-4B and Gemma-3-4B (base error > 56%) abstain entirely (participation ≈ 0%); only Llama-3.1-8B participates (~2.7% under the hierarchy) and retains a non-zero **WGER = 0.014**, so the headline "WGER = 0" does not extend to MMLU-Pro.
- **Ablation: difficulty depth is what clears the budget.** Removing the difficulty level (Table 5) reinstates an 11.0% violation rate; only the global→difficulty two-level structure reaches 0.0%.
- **Bonferroni's empirical effect is |H|-dependent.** On ARC's shallow 5-node hierarchy, removing the correction still yields 0.0% violations (participation 0.584 vs. 0.478); on MMLU-Pro's 57 subjects, removing it raises violations from 0.6% → 14.0% (in a near-zero-participation regime), certifying the theory.
- **Label noise causes graceful conservatism, not violation.** At η = 0.10, participation falls 0.849 → 0.085 while violations drop 0.11 → 0.00 (Table 9).
- **Quantization is safe if re-calibrated.** Risk stays below α = 0.10 at bf16/int8/int4 for all models; participation is most affected for Gemma (0.294 → 0.058 at int4). An earlier Llama-int8 anomaly (0.24 at 50 trials) vanished at 500 trials (0.09/0.11/0.08 — flat).
- **Prompt shift needs recalibration.** The reasoning template on Qwen drives transfer risk to 0.203 (> 2× budget); recalibrating on reasoning examples restores risk to 0.067 at a participation cost (0.360 vs. 0.674).

## Authors & Credibility

The four authors (Murilo Salem, Daniel Pontes, Luísa Böhm, Anderson Ferrugem) are affiliated with CDTec, Universidade Federal de Pelotas, Brazil — a regional Brazilian institution rather than a top-tier ML lab, and the paper is a single-institution arXiv preprint (v1, 27 Jul 2026) with no peer review track record yet. Credibility is bolstered, however, by unusual intellectual honesty: the authors devote Section 8.2 and large parts of the Limitations to an *equal-participation comparison* showing that, at matched coverage, a single global threshold controls per-group risk about as well as HG-CRC in 5 of 6 cells — i.e., they publicly deflate their own headline. They also corrected an earlier degenerate ablation ("2%–16%" gap) and an Llama-int8 anomaly after re-running at 500 trials, and openly label their 0% violation rates as ~0.6% empirical upper bounds rather than certified zeros. The formal guarantees are correctly derived from standard conformal-prediction machinery (Angelopoulos et al. 2023; Clopper–Pearson; union bound), and the gap between the deployed in-sample procedure and the split/augmented variants is explicitly bracketed by Lemma 1 with a measured jackknife slack.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** The core mechanism (Bonferroni-corrected per-group thresholds + hierarchical fallback) is a fairly direct composition of well-established ingredients — Mondrian conformal prediction (Boström et al. 2021), conformal risk control (Angelopoulos et al. 2023), and the union bound. The genuine novelty is (a) instantiating this for *selective prediction with abstain in LLMs* (claimed first), and (b) the residual-calibration device that aligns calibration and deployment populations. Reasonable but incremental.
- **Rigor.** Statistical reporting is careful: 500-trial bootstraps, rule-of-three interpretation of zeros, paired McNemar tests for variant comparison, jackknife estimation of the Lemma-1 slack. The cascade of Propositions 1/2 + Lemma 1 honestly distinguishes the unconditional guarantee (split, *not* deployed) from the deployed in-sample procedure (covered only up to measured εn). On the small ARC pool the *worst-case* jackknife slack reaches **εn ≈ 0.34**, so the deployed procedure's worst-case guarantee is materially weaker than the headline numbers — a caveat the authors state but which understates the practical exposure.
- **Reproducibility.** Strong: public models (Qwen3-4B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Gemma-3-4B-IT on HuggingFace), public benchmarks (ARC Challenge, MMLU-Pro), all hyperparameters fixed (α = 0.10, δ = 0.05, Nmin = 30, 100-quantile grid, 500 bootstraps), fixed seeds, single A100 runtimes quoted, code promised on acceptance.
- **Significance.** The headline "0% violation / WGER = 0" is narrowly scoped: it holds only on ARC Challenge and only for models whose base accuracy is well above 1−α. The most damning finding is in **Table 11 / Section 8.2**: at *equal* participation, a coverage-matched single global threshold achieves worst-group violation rates within sampling noise of HG-CRC in 5 of 6 cells (the exception being Gemma under shift, 0.054 vs. 0.086). The standard global CRC's catastrophic 0.70–0.96 worst-group violation at its own higher participation is therefore "overwhelmingly a consequence of answering too much, not of single thresholds being intrinsically unable to protect groups." The authors frame this correctly: HG-CRC's real contribution is the *automatic mechanism* for finding a safe per-group operating point plus the formal simultaneous guarantee — not an empirical per-group-safety margin.
- **Red flags.** (1) The mixture-shift headline (47% violation) relies on **endogenous** difficulty bins defined by the same NLL used for thresholding — "shifting toward hard" is definitionally "shifting toward high NLL." The exogenous re-run (Table 4) reduces Qwen's violation to 31%, confirming the effect is real but inflated; for Gemma the exogenous version is *worse* (39% vs. 15%), so the direction is not even consistent. (2) WGER is computed only over answered examples, so heavy abstention can attain WGER ≈ 0 trivially — Gemma's near-zero WGER coexists with ~8% participation and is closer to the degenerate regime. (3) The genuine multi-domain case is exercised only on MMLU-Pro, where the method essentially does not work (abstains entirely or WGER = 0.014), so the "hierarchical" claim over real domain structure is the weakest part of the empirical story.

## Strengths

- Intellectual honesty is exceptional: the equal-participation comparison, the εn caveats, the endogeneity analysis, and the correction of prior anomalies are all in the paper rather than buried.
- Clean, correct formal treatment distinguishing unconditional (split) and deployed (in-sample) guarantees, with the slack actually measured rather than assumed.
- Practical robustness findings that are valuable independently of HG-CRC itself: label noise → graceful conservatism; quantization is safe if re-calibrated; prompt shift requires (cheap) recalibration; CRC needs ≥ 50 target examples for domain transfer.
- No retraining, model- and score-agnostic, transparent assumptions.

## Limitations

- Headline result is benchmark-specific (ARC only) and model-specific (only models with base accuracy well above 1−α). On the harder MMLU-Pro the method abstains entirely or fails to meet WGER = 0.
- WGER rewards abstention; it must always be read jointly with participation, which the authors do but which limits WGER as a standalone fairness metric.
- Deployed in-sample procedure is only empirically valid (Lemma 1); worst-case jackknife slack εn ≈ 0.34 on the small ARC pool is non-trivial. The unconditionally-valid split variant is implemented but not deployed, and costs participation.
- Nmin = 30 prunes most fine-grained leaves (e.g., 57 MMLU-Pro subjects × 3 difficulties), collapsing the hierarchy on datasets with real domain structure.
- Bonferroni is conservative; more powerful procedures (Holm-Bonferroni, hierarchical testing) could recover participation and are left as future work.
- Equal-coverage comparison shows most of the apparent advantage over global CRC is from answering fewer questions, not from per-group structure.
- Exogenous difficulty signal is still model-derived, not human grade-level labels (which the loaded ARC distribution lacks).

## Relevance

This paper is directly relevant to research on **trustworthy/uncertainty-aware LLM deployment** and **conformal/selective prediction**: it provides a principled, retraining-free way to give per-group (fairness-aware) risk guarantees with abstain, and exposes a concrete, quantified failure mode of standard marginal CRC under realistic population shift. The methodology and the residual-calibration device are transferable to any thresholded selective-prediction setting beyond LLMs.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can Holm-Bonferroni, Benjamini-Hochberg, or Yekutieli hierarchical testing recover a meaningful share of the 22–37 pp participation cost while preserving the simultaneous guarantee, especially on hierarchies with many nodes (MMLU-Pro's 57 subjects)?
- How does HG-CRC behave on *generation* tasks (not multiple-choice), where the selected-option NLL is no longer available and the uncertainty score must come from self-consistency, token entropy, or a meta-learner?
- Does the headline extend to larger/stronger models (e.g., 70B-class) on harder benchmarks, or does the participation cost shrink toward zero as base accuracy improves?
- Can the equal-participation result (Section 8.2) be inverted into a *coverage-adaptive* global baseline that automatically finds HG-CRC's safe operating point without the hierarchy, and how much would that erode HG-CRC's practical case?
- What happens under *adversarial* group shift (an attacker choosing the test mixture), and does the simultaneous guarantee still hold at the measured εn slack?
- The deployed in-sample worst-case slack εn ≈ 0.34 on ARC is large — is there a cheap hybrid (split at sparse upper nodes, in-sample at well-populated leaves) that is both unconditional and participation-efficient?
