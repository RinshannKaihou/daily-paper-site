When Can You Correct Distribution Drift in Temporal Graph Generation? A Sharpening–Drift Tension and an Impossibility for Observation-Based Correction

Tianpeng Li · Xuan Guo · Wenjun Wang · Wang Zhang · Pengfei Jiao 2026-07-27 temporal-graph-generation distribution-drift discrete-flow-matching impossibility-theorem
Key metric
60% vs 5.7%
Oracle removes 60% of drift error; the best observation-based corrector recovers only 5.7% of that gain.
● obs. correctororacle ●
Why this matters
Redraws the boundary for test-time drift correction — effort must move beyond the observed stream, toward side information or anti-sharpening models.
Critical edge
The −0.605 power law rests on one architecture; the DiGress probe (single seed, R²=0.30) failed its pre-registered criterion.

Methodology

Theory-driven · experiments measure, not illustrate

Model & objective

Temporal graphs → binary ego-network tokens z ∈ {0,1}^D (D = N_max + N_max²), conditioned on a history summary h. Generation via masked (absorbing-state) discrete flow matching. Lemma 1 recasts the masked objective as any-order autoregressive log-loss averaged over all D! orderings — every theorem derives from this. Numerically verified, rel. error < 2%.

Theoretical apparatus

Source P and target Q split chronologically; P ≠ Q is the drift. Proofs rest only on conditionals bounded away from the simplex boundary. Empirical drift-process inputs (µ, v, ρ) are measured directly from token marginals, with no model in the loop — the correct way to test the closed-form thresholds.

Five load-bearing theorems

  • Thm 2Exact loss decomposition — loss = model-independent entropy H_R(z|h) + non-negative divergence Δ(θ;R). No cross-slot independence assumption. Perfect source-fit hits a strictly positive floor D_drift, not the best target loss.
  • Thm 3Sharpening–drift tension — along m^(α) the target-divergence derivative is positive exactly on slots rare-at-training / common-at-deployment, diverging as p→0. Empirically a power law L_Q ∝ L_P^(−0.605), R²=0.9977.
  • Thm 4 / Cor 5Step-independent floor — drift enters the sampler KL as a step-count-independent D_drift term. Sweeping N=1→50 moves marginal error ≤6% across 7/7 conditions.
  • Thm 6Correction impossibility (L²) — any F_{t−1}-measurable corrector leaves residual ≥ Var(q_t | F_{t−1}). One-step innovation (0.069) is most of uncorrected error (0.102): the bound is tight.
  • Thm 7Extrapolation vs persistence threshold — extrapolation beats persistence iff µ² > v(1−2ρ). Confirmed by 2×10⁵-step Monte-Carlo.

Strengths & Limitations

Read side-by-side
▲ Strengths
  • Theory–measurement coupling. Every theorem has a corresponding measured quantity; Theorem 7's threshold tested by directly estimating its inputs.
  • Useful negative result. Tells the field where effort should not go; points concretely to side information and anti-sharpening as headroom.
  • Exemplary disclosure. Pre-registered criteria, full window sweeps, refuted conjectures, and prior defective metrics all reported, not hidden.
  • Practically reassuring corollary. Few-step samplers survive drift — only their destination worsens.
  • Conditional, not vacuous. Theorem 7 is satisfiable (ρ>½ would flip it), so the negative empirical finding is meaningful, not tautological.
▼ Limitations
  • Home-grown metrics. Marginal / pairwise-joint error are the authors'; all claims are relative magnitudes, not absolute scores comparable across papers.
  • One-architecture power law. −0.605 exponent rests on one carrier; DiGress probe is a single seed that failed its pre-registered criterion (R²=0.30).
  • Second-order stationarity. Theorem 7 holds only approximately in finite windows; ρ measured pooled over active slots, not per-slot.
  • Oracle is a yardstick. Requires the true target marginal — precisely what observation cannot deliver.
  • T=50 flag flip. Finest-binning contradicts the deployment-period measurement in 4/5 conditions; a real wrinkle, attributed to a binning artefact.
  • No code/data release referenced in the body; reproducibility depends on faithful reimplementation.

Questions for Further Exploration

Where the boundary might move
  1. Does the −0.605 exponent generalise across additional masked-flow-matching and absorbing-state architectures, or is it specific to the studied sharpening rate?
  2. Can side information outside the stream (calendars, announcements, exogenous covariates) measurably lower the Theorem 6 bound — and by how much per bit of auxiliary signal?
  3. Would anti-sharpening mechanisms (entropy floors, confidence penalties, guided decoding) recover a meaningful fraction of the oracle's 60% gain without sacrificing in-period quality? What is the resulting Pareto frontier?
  4. Is the trendless, mean-reverting drift (ρ∈[−0.29,−0.11]) universal across real temporal-graph streams, or do certain domains (social vs trade vs communication) sit closer to ρ>½ where extrapolation would help?
  5. The T=50 flag flip — is it purely a resolution artefact, or does it signal a short-window-momentum regime a multi-scale corrector could exploit?
