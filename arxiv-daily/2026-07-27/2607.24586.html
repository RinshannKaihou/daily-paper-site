Why This Matters

A training-free, single-forward-pass signal enables cheap, real-time hallucination risk filtering for any white-box LLM — no retrieval, sampling, or external verifier.

Critical Edge

Best-layer selection is tuned on test data, and absolute AUROC sits only mildly above chance (high-50s to mid-60s) — a weak, non-deployable standalone signal.

01

Methodology

For a fixed layer j, stack the hidden states of an input into the activation matrix H ∈ ℝd×T. D-Score is its relative numerical rank — the number of singular directions staying within a factor τ of the leading singular value.

Dτ(j)(x; m) = Σi 1{ σ1(H) / σi(H) ≤ τ }
# singular values not smaller than σ₁ / τ
  • Decision rule: flag hallucination iff Dτ(x) > D̄ (calibrated threshold).
  • Invariances: scale-invariant and basis-invariant under orthogonal transforms — robust to representation scaling.
  • Geometric argument (Prop. 1): a conflict component injecting q orthogonal strong directions raises D-Score by at least q.
  • Computation: since d ≫ T, work on the small T×T Gram matrix via deflated power iteration — no full SVD, cost O(Nit·K·dT).

03

Strengths & Limitations

◆ Strengths

  • Striking simplicity: one forward pass, one layer, no training, no retrieval, no multiple samples — ideal for latency-constrained settings.
  • Clean formalization with useful invariance properties (scale, basis) and a concrete geometric mechanism.
  • Consistent directional gains over the most similar hidden-state method across every model / dataset / metric cell.
  • Cheap computation: partial eigensolver on the small Gram matrix when the spectrum decays fast.
  • Honest framing as a "calibrated risk signal," with explicit acknowledgment of when it must fail.

◇ Limitations

  • Requires white-box access to hidden activations — inapplicable to closed / API-only models.
  • Main tables use the best layer per model, not a fixed held-out calibration protocol.
  • Narrow baselines: no semantic entropy, consistency, probing-classifier, or retrieval comparisons; entropy baselines missing on RAGTruth.
  • Modest absolute performance (AUROC high-50s to mid-60s) limits standalone deployability.
  • Length dependence: Dτ(x) ≤ T, so sequence length can shift the score; no length-stratified analysis.
  • Orthogonality assumption in Prop. 1 is idealized; only English benchmarks and 7–8B models tested.
04

Open Questions

  1. How does D-Score compare to semantic entropy and SelfCheckGPT under one calibrated protocol — and at what compute budget does its single-pass advantage vanish?
  2. Does the advantage survive fixing layer / τ / D̄ on a held-out set and evaluating on a disjoint test split (removing best-layer tuning)?
  3. Is the signal partly an artifact of sequence length / verbosity? A length- and complexity-stratified evaluation would clarify.
  4. Can a span-level variant localize where in the response the conflict appears — turning a document score into a highlighter?
  5. Does it transfer to larger (70B) or smaller (1–3B) models and to non-English FAVA / RAGTruth annotations?
  6. Can the conflict-direction argument be validated empirically — recovering the putative conflict subspace and correlating it with annotated unsupported claims?
