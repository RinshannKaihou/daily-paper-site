> **一句话总结：** 华为提出的 ACRL 算法通过一个基于序列级训练-推理差异度量的自适应指数 α，双向（既压低过大的差异、又放大过小的差异）调节 token 级梯度比例，使 FP8 量化推理下的 RL 训练保持稳定并提升策略熵与探索能力，在 GSM8K、AIME、MMLU-Pro 等多个基准上达到甚至超过 BF16 基线、并优于 TIS/MIS 等重要性采样修补方案。

> **TL;DR:** ACRL (Huawei) tackles the training-inference discrepancy that destabilizes LLM RL post-training when the inference engine runs aggressive FP8 quantization while training stays in BF16. It uses a sequence-level discrepancy measurement (Y) against a static reference (X) to compute an adaptive exponent α = γ·sign(A)(1 − Y/X), which then scales a token-level gradient ratio ρ in a four-quadrant "Advantage Guided Probability Adjustment" rule that bidirectionally drives the mismatch back toward the reference. On Qwen2.5-3B/7B/32B and Qwen3-30B-A3B MoE, ACRL stabilizes training that otherwise collapses under FP8, matches or beats the BF16 baseline (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B average on AIME24/25/AMC23/HMMT25/MATH500: 36.39% ACRL vs 35.11% BF16, +1.28 pts), and outperforms TIS and MIS fixes at ~0.1% compute overhead per RL step.

## 核心贡献

- 提出了"将训练-推理差异（training-inference discrepancy）控制在合理范围"这一全新视角来稳定 RL 训练，并系统阐明了差异既不能过大（导致训练崩溃）也不能过小（损害精度、削弱泛化）的原理。
- 提出 ACRL 算法：用序列级差异 Y 与静态参考值 X（由初始权重在 step 0 计算）的对比，推导出自适应指数 α = γ·sign(A_i,t)(1 − Y/X)，再以 token 级比例 ρ_i,t = (π/µ)^α 调整梯度，形成"优势引导概率调整（AGPA）"的四象限更新规则，实现双向差异控制。
- 从理论上分析并实证验证 ACRL 能提升策略熵、增强探索，从而在精度上反超 BF16 基线。
- 在 FP8/MXFP8 量化、多种模型规模（3B/7B/32B/MoE）、多种 RL 算法（GRPO/PPO/DAPO）和多种任务（数学推理 + MMLU-Pro）上验证了泛化性，并在极端截断 FP8（truncated FP8）压力测试下仍保持稳定。

## Key Contributions

- Introduces a new conceptual framing for stable RL: rather than minimizing or aligning away the training-inference discrepancy, regulate it within a "reasonable range" — neither too large (collapse) nor too small (accuracy/generalization loss). The paper formalizes this as a constrained optimization max J(θ) s.t. E ≤ E₀.
- Proposes the ACRL algorithm: a sequence-level discrepancy measurement Y is compared against a static reference X (computed once from initial weights at step 0), producing an adaptive exponent α = γ·sign(A)(1 − Y/X) that scales a per-token importance-style ratio ρ = (π/µ)^α. This drives a four-quadrant Advantage Guided Probability Adjustment (AGPA) update that bidirectionally restores the discrepancy toward the reference.
- Provides an exploration analysis showing ACRL's AGPA for decreasing the discrepancy amplifies PA&LP / NA&HP tokens and suppresses PA&HP / NA&LP tokens, yielding a net increase in functional policy entropy and exploration without the "garbage token" pathology seen in MIS.
- Demonstrates broad empirical validation: stable training under aggressive FP8/MXFP8 across GRPO/PPO/DAPO, Qwen2.5-3B/7B and Qwen3-32B Dense / Qwen3-30B-A3B MoE architectures, math benchmarks (GSM8K, AIME24/25, AMC23, HMMT25, MATH500) and the 14-domain MMLU-Pro transfer benchmark, plus an extreme truncated-FP8 stress test.

## Methodology

ACRL targets the mismatch between the training policy π (BF16 in engines like FSDP/Megatron) and the inference policy µ (low-precision, e.g., FP8/MXFP8, in engines like vLLM/SGLang) that turns on-policy GRPO into off-policy learning and causes collapse. The method proceeds as follows:

1. **Discrepancy definition.** A per-token distance d(a_i,t, θ_old) ≜ |π(a_i,t|...) − µ(a_i,t|...)| is defined (Remark 4.1 notes absolute distance is used; KL/Euclidean are left for future work). The current sequence-level discrepancy Y is the mean over tokens; the reference X is the mean computed once at step 0 over a baseline set, exploiting the fact that initial weights are unaffected by the discrepancy.

2. **Bidirectional control principle.** When Y > X the discrepancy is reduced by moving π toward µ; when Y < X the discrepancy is increased by moving π away from µ. This is argued to be necessary because over-constraining shrinks the feasible parameter space Θ_E₀ and can forgo optimal weights, while a vanishing discrepancy causes the training policy to overfit the inference policy.

3. **Advantage Guided Probability Adjustment (AGPA).** Combining the discrepancy-reduction/increase principle with the sign of the token advantage A_t yields a four-quadrant update rule (enhanced/reduced reward or punishment) that preserves the reward/punishment direction dictated by the advantage while modulating magnitude.

4. **Adaptive token-level ratio.** ACRL wraps the standard GRPO clipped objective with a multiplicative ratio ρ_i,t = (π/µ)^α that is clipped at C, where the exponent α = γ·sign(A_i,t)(1 − Y/X) simultaneously encodes (a) the direction of correction from Y vs X and (b) an adaptive magnitude proportional to the gap |X − Y|. Unlike sequence-level MIS, the ratio is applied per-token and conditioned on each token's own d, so high-discrepancy tokens get stronger correction. The full gradient is ∇J_ACRL = E[mean over sequences and tokens of min(ρ_i,t, C) · ∇θ min(r_i,t A_i,t, clip(r_i,t,...)A_i,t)].

5. **Deliberate bias trade-off.** The authors explicitly acknowledge (Remark 4.2) that the adaptive exponent makes ACRL a biased estimator, but argue this is a pragmatic necessity: theoretically-unbiased MIS suffers vanishing gradients (ratios on the order of 1e-15 under extreme quantization), and biased clipping is already foundational to PPO/GRPO. ACRL prioritizes active discrepancy control and stability over unbiasedness.

6. **Hyperparameters.** Control strength γ (found robust in [0.5, 1.7]; collapses at γ ≥ 2.1), clip value C (3 normally, 5 in stress test), and the static reference X.

## Results & Highlights

- **Qwen2.5-3B on GSM8K (Table 1):** ACRL reaches 88.10% peak pass@1 (+0.38% over BF16's 87.72%) and 87.05% average over the final 200 steps (+0.45% over BF16's 86.60%, +0.32% over TIS's 86.73%, +0.76% over MIS's 86.29%), while sustaining the highest functional policy entropy (Fig. 8).
- **Qwen2.5-7B on difficult math (Table 3, AIME24/25, AMC23, HMMT25, MATH500):** ACRL with γ=0.65 attains 36.39% average accuracy vs the BF16 baseline's 35.11% (+1.28 pts), vs TIS 34.57% and MIS 34.93%. On AIME24 specifically, ACRL γ=0.65 hits 23.33% vs BF16's 16.67% (+6.66 pts).
- **Extreme truncated-FP8 stress test, 3B on GSM8K (Table 2):** under Q(x)=trunc(x/S) quantization, uncorrected FP8 and both TIS and MIS fail to recover; ACRL still trains successfully at 86.13% (+0.23% vs BF16 85.90%, +1.44% vs TIS 84.69%), confirming robustness far beyond standard nearest-round FP8.
- **Cross-algorithm generalization (Table 4):** on PPO, FP8+ACRL matches BF16 exactly at 87.34% and beats FP8+TIS (86.81%); on DAPO, uncorrected FP8 catastrophically fails while ACRL achieves 88.63%, beating both BF16 (87.95%) and TIS (87.26%).
- **Architectural scalability to 30B+ (Table 5):** Qwen3-32B Dense — MXFP8+ACRL avg accuracy (steps 600-800) 0.9613 vs BF16 0.9606; Qwen3-30B-A3B MoE — MXFP8+ACRL 0.9578 vs BF16 0.9558, demonstrating ACRL scales to MoE with precision-sensitive top-k routing.
- **Task transferability, MMLU-Pro 14 domains (Table 6):** 3B model — FP8+ACRL overall 0.4534 vs BF16 baseline 0.4484 (+0.5 pts) and FP8+TIS 0.4480; with BF16 inference ACRL reaches 0.4562 vs 0.4491, confirming gains are not overfit to math.
- **Computational overhead (Section 5.4):** the ACRL weight-tensor computation costs 0.059s per RL step out of a 56.496s total step time on Qwen2.5-3B, i.e. ≈0.1% overhead.
- **γ sensitivity (Table 9):** stable and successful for γ ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 0.9, 1.3, 1.7} (peak 0.8832 at γ=1.3); training fails at γ ∈ {2.1, 2.5}.
- **Ablation of bidirectional control (Table 10):** full ACRL (87.05% avg) beats a "Continuous Reduction" one-sided variant (86.14%) and a "Fallback to GRPO when Y<X" variant (86.97%), validating the bidirectional design.
- **Reference baseline stability (Table 7):** the BF16 natural discrepancy floor on 3B stays within 0.011–0.017 over 900+ steps, empirically justifying the static X.

## Authors & Credibility

All seven authors (Wenwu Fan, Qihong Lin, Zhijie Xia, Zhuo Zheng, Sihao Wang, Qiang Chen, Liangsheng Zhu) are from Huawei's Computing Product Line, with Fan as corresponding author. The work is firmly situated in real production-grade RL post-training infrastructure (VeRL training framework, vLLM inference, FSDP), and the baselines (TIS from Yao et al. 2025, MIS from Liu et al. 2025a) are the actual current state-of-the-art fixes for the exact problem addressed, not strawmen. The scale of validation (up to 32B dense and 30B-A3B MoE, MXFP8) and the inclusion of an adversarial truncated-FP8 stress test are above the typical bar for this subarea. The paper is unusually candid about its own limitations, including the biased-estimator admission in Remark 4.2.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine. The bidirectional "regulate to a reference band" framing is a fresh departure from the two prevailing paradigms — alignment (FP8-everywhere, FP16 alignment) and importance-sampling patches (TIS, MIS). The adaptive exponent α = γ·sign(A)(1 − Y/X) is a simple but non-obvious mechanism that ties sequence-level discrepancy to per-token gradient scaling.
- **Rigor:** Mixed, leaning positive. Strengths include the four-quadrant AGPA derivation, the explicit exploration analysis tying AGPA to PA&LP / NA&HP token distributions, and the honest Remark 4.2 admission that ACRL is a biased estimator. However, the central functional form ρ = (π/µ)^α is acknowledged to be "an empirical engineering solution" with no derivation beyond intuition; the choice of absolute distance over KL is hand-waved to future work (Remark 4.1), and Section 4.2 leaves the "increasing discrepancy" derivation to the reader.
- **Reproducibility:** Reasonable but incomplete. Hyperparameters for 3B and 7B are provided (Tables 11–12), the reference X values per model are stated (0.013/0.01/0.016/0.014), and γ and clip C are given. But there is no code release mentioned, no random seeds, and the 32B/MoE and PPO/DAPO hyperparameters are not tabulated.
- **Significance:** High for practitioners. Eliminating the "quantization tax" — getting FP8 inference speed/memory savings without accuracy loss — is a concrete production win, and the ~0.1% overhead claim is compelling.
- **Red flags / weaknesses:**
  - **Single-seed evaluation at 3B/7B/32B scales.** The authors defend this with "temporal averaging over the final 200 steps" (Section 5.6), which reduces single-step noise but does not replace seed-level variance; several headline deltas (e.g., +1.28 pts on the 7B math average, +0.5 pts on MMLU-Pro) are within plausible seed variance for these benchmark sizes (AIME24/25 are only 30 questions each, so a single solved problem is ~3.3 pts).
  - **Tiny benchmark sizes inflate noise.** AIME24/AIME25 at 30 items and HMMT25 at ~30 items make "solving 1-2 additional problems" look like large percentage swings; the paper itself flags this but still reports the percentages prominently.
  - **Math-only headline domain.** Despite the MMLU-Pro transfer result, the method is not evaluated on subjective-reward RLHF tasks (creative writing, coding, safety), which the authors concede in Section 6.
  - **Missing ablations:** no KL-divergence variant of the distance metric, no moving-average X for long runs, no comparison against the alignment-based FP8-everywhere baseline (LMSYS Org 2025) on the same hardware to quantify the actual speed/memory win.
  - **DAPO FP8 "failure" is asserted without detail** (Table 4 just says "Failed") — it is unclear whether this is collapse, NaN, or accuracy collapse, which matters for interpreting the ACRL recovery.

## Strengths

- Novel bidirectional discrepancy-control principle with clean four-quadrant AGPA formulation that preserves advantage direction.
- Strong, fair baselines: directly compares against the current SOTA IS fixes (TIS, MIS) and the BF16 gold standard, on the same VeRL/vLLM/FSDP stack.
- Excellent scalability story: 3B → 7B → 32B Dense → 30B-A3B MoE, across GRPO/PPO/DAPO, including the notoriously MoE-unfriendly MXFP8 setting.
- Negligible ~0.1% compute overhead, since rollout log-probs are already cached.
- Honest about its biased-estimator trade-off (Remark 4.2) and the limits of its evaluation scope (Section 5.6, Section 6).
- Includes an adversarial truncated-FP8 stress test, not just standard nearest-round FP8.

## Limitations

- **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** relies on γ (collapses at ≥2.1, under-controls at 0.1) and a static X that may drift over very long horizons where weights evolve drastically.
- **Static reference assumption:** validated only up to ~900 steps; the paper itself flags that a dynamic/moving-average X may be needed for exceptionally long runs.
- **Domain narrowness:** primarily math reasoning; exploration benefits unverified in RLHF domains with subjective rewards (creative writing, coding, dialogue safety).
- **Backend narrowness:** only vLLM/FSDP evaluated; other engine pairings, multi-turn/tool-integrated RL, and asynchronous pipelines are explicitly out of scope.
- **Statistical rigor:** single-seed runs mitigated only by temporal averaging, on some very small benchmarks (AIME 30-question sets).
- **Biased estimator:** the (π/µ)^α form breaks unbiasedness by design — a defensible engineering trade-off but a theoretical step back from MIS.
- **No code release** mentioned as of the manuscript.

## Relevance

本文与 LLM 强化学习后训练（GRPO/PPO/DAPO）以及低精度量化推理（FP8/MXFP8）工程实践高度相关，直接解决了"训练用 BF16、推理用量化"导致的 off-policy 失配与训练崩溃问题，对关心 RL 训练稳定性、推理加速、量化部署效率的研究者与工程师都具有实用价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does ACRL behave under KL-divergence or squared-distance discrepancy metrics instead of absolute distance, and does the choice materially affect the reference band?
- Would a moving-average or exponentially-decayed X outperform the static step-0 reference on multi-thousand-step runs where weights drift substantially?
- Does ACRL's exploration boost transfer to subjective-reward RLHF (creative writing, coding, safety), or does the entropy increase become harmful there?
- How does ACRL compare head-to-head against alignment-based FP8-everywhere (LMSYS Org 2025) on wall-clock and memory, not just accuracy?
- Can the biased (π/µ)^α form be replaced with a provably-bounded-bias variant, or does any unbiased correction inevitably suffer the MIS vanishing-ratio pathology under aggressive quantization?
- What happens in multi-turn / tool-integrated RL and asynchronous rollout-training pipelines, which the current evaluation explicitly excludes?
