> **一句话总结：** 在 Qwen2-VL 家族上对 5,700 条预测做对照实验发现,模型变大(VLM 内部 token 概率信号)能极大提升错误检测能力(内部 AUROC 0.80→0.98),而 4-bit 量化几乎不损精度(−1.6 点)却严重破坏置信度信号;固定显存预算下应选"大模型+量化",且用内部 token 概率而非文字化置信度来做延迟决策。

> **TL;DR:** This independent three-arm study on the Qwen2-VL family (2B-fp16, 2B-4bit, 7B-4bit) measures how scale and 4-bit quantization affect two VLM confidence signals under six realistic image degradations on a 100-item Food101 four-way multiple-choice subset (5,700 predictions). Scale lifts internal token-probability error-detection AUROC from 0.80 (2B-4bit) to 0.98 (7B-4bit) while verbalized confidence stays near chance (0.61→0.69); quantization costs only −1.6 accuracy points but drops internal AUROC 0.95→0.80 and collapses the verbalized parse rate 99%→64%. The deployment verdict: under a fixed 16 GB budget, prefer the larger quantized model (7B-4bit), and defer on internal token probability, not stated confidence.

## 核心贡献

- **解耦"规模"与"精度"两个杠杆**:在 Qwen2-VL 单一家族上设计三臂对照(2B-fp16、2B-4bit、7B-4bit),用"同精度变规模"和"同规模变精度"两个正交对比,干净地分离出 scale 效应与 quantization 效应,避免了跨精度比较的混杂。
- **核心发现——"知道"与"说出"的鸿沟随规模加大**:模型从 2B 到 7B,内部错误检测 AUROC 从 0.80 飙升到 0.98,而文字化置信度几乎不改善(0.61→0.69),说明 verbalized confidence 不是"小模型病",而是受不同机制支配。
- **量化代价的精细刻画**:4-bit 量化对精度近乎免费(−1.6 点),但对置信度信号代价昂贵——内部 AUROC 从 0.95 跌到 0.80,verbalized 解析率从 99% 崩到 64%。
- **可直接落地的部署建议**:在固定 16 GB 显存预算下,7B-4bit 同时拿下最高精度(0.935)、最高内部 AUROC(0.98)、最低 AURC(0.008);并以阈值迁移实验证明只有大模型的内部置信度阈值在退化下不漏错。
- **方法论主张**:论证在置信度方差接近零时,错误检测 AUROC 比 ECE 更具诊断价值——一个几乎恒为 0.9 的置信度在 90% 准确率上"校准得很好"却毫无排序信息。

## Key Contributions

- **A controlled dissection of scale vs. precision**: A three-arm design on a single family (Qwen2-VL) yields two orthogonal contrasts — B-vs-C isolates scale at fixed 4-bit precision, A-vs-B isolates quantization at fixed 2B size — removing the confound that contaminates any comparison varying both axes together.
- **Central finding — the know/say gap widens with scale**: Internal error-detection AUROC jumps 0.80→0.98 from 2B to 7B (both 4-bit), while verbalized confidence barely moves (0.61→0.69) and stays at or near chance in 8 of 19 conditions at 7B. Verbalized confidence is not a small-model artifact that scale repairs.
- **Quantization is cheap for accuracy, expensive for the signal**: 4-bit costs only 1.6 accuracy points (0.904→0.888) at 2B, but drops internal AUROC 0.95→0.80 and collapses the verbalized parse rate from 99% to 64% as the quantized model abandons the two-line format.
- **An actionable deployment recommendation**: Under a fixed 16 GB budget, 7B-4bit dominates — highest accuracy (0.935), highest internal AUROC (0.98), lowest AURC (0.008) — beating even the full-precision 2B on the confidence signal. A threshold-transfer analysis shows only the 7B internal-confidence threshold stays safe under degradation (0 errors admitted), while 2B-4bit silently leaks errors.
- **A methodological argument for AUROC over ECE**: Demonstrates on the data that a near-constant verbalized confidence (≈0.9 on a 90%-accurate condition) yields low ECE (0.011) yet chance-level AUROC (0.56), so error-detection AUROC is the diagnostic metric when confidence variance is near zero.

## Methodology

**Design.** Three arms on the Qwen2-VL family run on identical inputs and prompts: Arm A = Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct at fp16; Arm B = the same 2B at NF4 4-bit; Arm C = Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct at NF4 4-bit. Using one family throughout prevents the scale contrast from being confounded by architectural or training-recipe differences across labs. All numbers are freshly generated — none imported from external runs.

**Models and inference.** 4-bit arms use NormalFloat (NF4) with double quantization and fp16 compute dtype, fitting the 7B inside a single free-tier 16 GB NVIDIA T4. Greedy decoding (`do_sample=False`), 32-token generation cap, for reproducibility.

**Data.** A 100-item subset of the Food101 test split (streamed, seed 0), posed as four-option multiple choice (one correct label + three distractors). An item manifest is recorded on the first run and any later run that fails to reproduce it exactly is aborted, guaranteeing cross-arm comparability.

**Degradations.** Six families approximating real phone-camera artifacts (JPEG re-encode, motion blur, low light, glare, in-plane rotation, downscale-upsample resample), each at three severities. With the clean baseline this gives 19 conditions per arm and 1,900 predictions per arm (5,700 total).

**Confidence signals.**
- *Verbalized*: the model is prompted to emit an integer confidence in [0,100], normalized to [0,1]. Three templates are piloted on 20 clean items per size and the highest-parse-rate (few-shot) template is used; parse rate is itself reported as a signal.
- *Internal*: the mean per-step probability the model assigned to each token actually emitted within the **answer span** only — explicitly excluding the "Confidence:" digits so the two signals are not circularly mixed. Requires no extra forward passes.

**Answer matching.** Case/punctuation normalization, then exact match, then unique containment. Bare-label replies (common for smaller/quantized arms) are scored against the whole reply so no model is penalized for poor instruction-following — a deliberate fairness choice.

**Metrics.** Accuracy, Expected Calibration Error (10 bins), Brier score, and **error-detection AUROC** (the probability a correct answer is scored more confident than an incorrect one) as the primary metric. Percentile bootstrap CIs with B=2000. Conditions near 100% accuracy yield undefined AUROC and are excluded from aggregates.

**Selective prediction.** At no extra inference cost: risk–coverage curves, AURC per condition/signal, and a threshold-transfer analysis (a clean-image threshold calibrated to answer 90% is applied unchanged to every degraded condition).

## Results & Highlights

- **Scale lifts the internal signal, not the verbalized one (RQ1).** Holding precision at 4-bit, going 2B→7B raises mean internal error-detection AUROC **0.80→0.98**, with the 7B internal signal ≥0.90 in 18 of 19 conditions. Verbalized AUROC rises only **0.61→0.69** and stays at or near chance (≤0.60) in 8 of 19 conditions at 7B — including every low-light condition (0.51–0.56). The internal–verbalized gap *widens* with scale: 0.19 at 2B vs 0.29 at 7B.
- **Quantization is cheap for accuracy, expensive for the signal (RQ2).** At 2B, fp16→4-bit costs only **−1.6 accuracy points (0.904→0.888)**, but internal AUROC falls **0.95→0.80** and the verbalized parse rate collapses **0.99→0.64**.
- **For a fixed budget, prefer the larger quantized model (RQ3).** 7B-4bit dominates all three fit-in-16GB configurations: accuracy **0.935**, internal AUROC **0.98**, AURC **0.008** — beating the full-precision 2B on the confidence signal (internal AUROC 0.98 vs 0.95; AURC 0.008 vs 0.017). Scale's gain (+0.18 AUROC) exceeds quantization's loss (−0.15).
- **Scale helps most on the hardest conditions.** 2B-4bit→7B-4bit accuracy rises **+0.21 at low light s3, +0.13 at both glare s3 and JPEG s3**, and ≤0.05 elsewhere. Low light s3 accuracy is 0.59 (2B-4bit), 0.60 (2B-fp16), 0.80 (7B-4bit) — softened but not solved.
- **Threshold transfer exposes the operational difference.** A clean-calibrated 90%-coverage internal threshold applied unchanged: for 7B-4bit, selective accuracy stays ≈1.00 in every degraded condition with **0 errors admitted** above threshold (coverage falls gracefully to 0.39 at low light s3); for 2B-4bit, selective accuracy falls to **0.82 at glare s3 with 12% of all inputs answered wrongly above threshold**.
- **ECE would hide all of this.** ECE is low everywhere (0.01–0.07). In Arm A, verbalized confidence has ECE 0.011 — "better calibrated" than its own internal signal (ECE 0.053) — yet separates correct from incorrect at AUROC **0.56 (≈chance)** vs the internal signal's **0.95** on identical predictions.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Genuine and well-scoped. While verbalized-uncertainty-overconfidence in VLMs (Groot & Valdenegro-Toro; Borszukovszki et al.) and quantization effects on LLM confidence (Singh & Sajjad) are individually known, the **orthogonal two-contrast design** that cleanly separates scale from quantization for VLM *error detection* is new, as is the explicit "the know/say gap *widens* with scale" framing. The move from proprietary-VLM calibration studies to open-weight internal-probability access is a real methodological advance.
- **Rigor**: Strong on internal validity — fixed family, identical inputs enforced by a manifest, bootstrap CIs, severity-graded reporting, an honest answer-matching policy that does not punish bare-label replies. The choice to restrict internal confidence to the answer span (excluding the "Confidence:" digits) is exactly right and prevents a circular comparison.
- **Reproducibility**: Excellent in principle — public repo, per-prediction CSVs, single-T4 reproducibility, fixed seeds, verbatim templates in Appendix A. The main reproducibility risk is the small *n* (see Limitations).
- **Significance**: Practically important for anyone deploying open-weight VLMs on consumer/edge hardware under real image noise — it gives a concrete "spend budget on parameters, not precision; defer on token probability, not stated confidence" rule and an AUROC-first metric recommendation.
- **Red flags / weaknesses**:
  - **Tiny evaluation set**: only **100 Food101 items** posed as four-way multiple choice. With n≈100 per condition and high ceiling accuracy, many bootstrap intervals are wide and several conditions have undefined AUROC. The headline 7B internal AUROC of 0.98 rests on very few errors per condition — the "near ceiling" claim is fragile.
  - **Single dataset and single format**: Food101 four-way MC only. Generality to open-ended VQA, free-form generation, or other domains is untested; the author admits this. Multiple-choice also forces an answer, so refusal behavior is unobservable.
  - **Single model family and only two scale points**: Qwen2-VL 2B vs 7B gives a direction, not a scaling law; no emergence threshold can be located, and family-specific effects cannot be ruled out.
  - **Quantization measured at one size only** (2B): bounds but does not eliminate the quantization×scale confound — we do not know whether 4-bit also damages the 7B internal signal relative to a 7B-fp16 baseline (which was not run, presumably for budget reasons).
  - **Verbalized-confidence weakness may be conflated with format-following**: the 2B-4bit arm's verbalized signal is partly crippled by a 64% parse rate, so "verbalized confidence is weak" and "the quantized model won't follow the format" are entangled at that operating point. The 7B (100% parse) results are the cleaner test and do support the central claim, but the 2B verbalized numbers should be read with that caveat.
  - **Degradations are synthetic approximations** of camera artifacts, not measured real-world corruptions.
  - **No human study or downstream deferral-cost evaluation**: the deployment recommendation is framed via AUROC/AURC, not via measured cost of a wrong deferral in a real pipeline.

## Strengths

- Clean, well-motivated two-contrast experimental design that isolates scale from quantization — exactly the confound most prior work leaves tangled.
- A genuinely useful, counterintuitive finding (the internal–verbalized gap *widens* with scale) that directly contradicts the comforting "stated confidence is poor because the model is small" assumption.
- A concrete, deployable recommendation tied to a real budget constraint (16 GB GPU) and validated by a threshold-transfer experiment, not just aggregate metrics.
- Strong reproducibility posture: public code, per-prediction CSVs, single-T4 reproducibility, fixed seeds, verbatim prompts, manifest-enforced input identity.
- Intellectually honest limitations section and a principled methodological argument (AUROC over ECE) demonstrated on the paper's own data.

## Limitations

- Only 100 Food101 items; small per-condition *n* with wide CIs and undefined AUROC near ceiling.
- Single dataset, single format (four-way MC), single model family, only two scale points — no scaling law, no emergence threshold.
- Quantization characterized at one model size only; the 7B-4bit-vs-7B-fp16 contrast is missing.
- Synthetic degradations approximate rather than reproduce real camera artifacts.
- Multiple-choice format forces an answer, so refusal/abstention behavior is unobservable.
- At 2B, verbalized-confidence weakness is partly confounded with a 64% parse rate (poor format-following), not purely a calibration failure.
- Severe low light defeats every configuration (accuracy down to 0.80 even at 7B; internal AUROC 0.89 with a CI reaching 0.80), so the recommendation explicitly excludes that regime and demands an upstream image-quality check.

## Relevance

This paper speaks directly to deploying vision-language models on consumer/edge hardware where memory budgets force hard choices between model size and numerical precision — and where a trustworthy confidence signal is the prerequisite for any deferral/abstention mechanism. Its finding that internal token-probability AUROC (not verbalized confidence, not ECE) is the signal that scales, and that a larger quantized model beats a smaller full-precision one on that signal, is immediately actionable for selective-prediction and uncertainty-aware VLM pipelines.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the "internal–verbalized gap widens with scale" finding hold for open-ended VQA and free-form generation, or is it specific to the multiple-choice answer span over which internal probability is averaged?
- How does 4-bit quantization affect the **7B** internal signal relative to a 7B-fp16 baseline — is the quantization penalty size-dependent, and does it shrink or grow with scale?
- Does the result replicate beyond Qwen2-VL — e.g., in LLaVA, InternVL, or Phi-VL families — or is the verbalized-confidence-at-chance behavior a Qwen2-VL post-training artifact?
- Can the internal-probability threshold-transfer safety (0 errors admitted at 7B) be preserved under true distribution shift (real camera noise, not synthetic corruptions) and under adversarial perturbations?
- Is there a scale at which verbalized confidence *becomes* discriminative (an emergence threshold), or does the gap to internal probability grow monotonically?
- What is the real-world deferral cost trade-off — i.e., when the 7B internal threshold defers (coverage falling to 0.39 at low light s3), is that deferral rate acceptable for a concrete downstream task, or does it force a cascade that negates the latency win?
