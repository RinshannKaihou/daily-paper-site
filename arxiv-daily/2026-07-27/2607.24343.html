Methodology — calibration on the field, not the action

A tool call A = (op, x₁…xₖ) is split so each argument gets a semantic role — target / credential / command / selector / control / content. The certified unit is the field, and calibration strata are roles. A field is violated when untrusted input alters it against user intent, and allowed when the controller emits it. The per-role violation rate is V(r) = Pr[violated ∧ allowed | role = r].

Deployable score

Annotation-free

Default score never reads attacker literals:

s_i = sim(x_i, untrusted ctx) − sim(x_i, trusted prompt)

Shifted to [0,1]; field allowed when s_i ≤ τ. ROC-AUC 0.71 vs label-coupled upper bound 0.93.

Algorithm 1 — per-role threshold

Mondrian / class-conditional CRC

Largest threshold whose inflated empirical risk stays in budget α(r):

τ̂(r) = sup { τ : 1/(n_r+1)·(Σ_i L_r(x_i;τ) + 1) ≤ α(r) }

If 1/(n_r+1) > α(r) the role is pooled into a shared high-risk stratum — rare roles (e.g. credential) don't silently fail.

Theory — the price of coarseness

Why aggregate control fails

Prop. 1: a rare role r's violation rate is inflated by 1/p_r under action-level observation. Prop. 2: to certify V(r) ≤ α the effective budget shrinks to α·p_r. Thm 1: Blackwell ordering Φ_agg ⪯ Φ_role ⪯ Φ_field.

Simultaneous certificate

Theorem 2 + Prop. 3–4

Clopper-Pearson + Bonferroni gives a high-probability certificate holding jointly over all strata. Prop. 3: α(r)→0 recovers detector-relative noninterference (a tunable relaxation of classical IFC). Prop. 4: per-role shift bounded by TV-distance, removable by recalibration.

Evaluation. Controlled trace-replay on real agent traces from six frontier models — GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, Gemini 2.5 Pro/Flash, Llama 3.3 70B, Qwen2.5-7B — under live prompt injection on AgentDojo (banking, workspace, Slack, travel) and InjecAgent. 20 random calibration/test splits; budgets α(r)=1% in-distribution, 2% under shift, α=0.10 for whole-action baselines. Baselines are explicitly controlled proxies of CaMeL / FIDES / PACT under a common detector, plus mean / risk / inverse-prevalence / max-risk-weighted single-threshold losses and aggregate CRC. Aggregate control leaks the rare role

At a 10% aggregate budget (AgentDojo, GPT-4o): aggregate CRC allows 10.5% target violations while per-field CRC allows 0%. On InjecAgent (GPT-4o-mini, 10% aggregate) aggregate CRC posts a tidy 2.9% overall — but lets 100% of attacked target fields through, vs 0% for per-field.

Strengths · Limitations

Strengths

  • Clean, correct formalization of why aggregate action-level control fails for rare high-risk roles — with a tight closed-form price (1/p_r inflation, α·p_r effective budget).
  • Detector-agnostic: the calibration layer wraps any per-field detector and improves monotonically with detector quality — composable with StruQ / SecAlign / Spotlighting.
  • Disciplined, reproducible protocol (trace-replay, 20 splits, paired Wilcoxon, independent D_alloc/D_cert) with refreshingly honest three-tier guarantee framing.
  • Strong robustness across transfer, unseen suites, detector noise, gradual drift, and adaptive attacks — exactly the failure modes that matter.
  • Graceful handling of rare roles via automatic pooling rather than silent failure.

Limitations

  • Utility cost is real: 25.2% revert utility vs 32.3% (PACT proxy); over-intervention up to 84% under obfuscated attacks with the weak deployable detector.
  • CaMeL/FIDES/PACT comparisons are proxies, not full reproductions — strong claims against those systems aren't warranted by the data.
  • Credential role can't be individually certified at 1% (only 0–22 cal. fields; per-model floors 6.7%–63.6%) — pooled cert covers the stratum average, not credential alone.
  • Theorem 2's exactness relies on field-level independence that multi-same-role-field episodes can violate.
  • No interactive deployment eval; no treatment of tool selection, omitted actions, or call ordering.
  • V(r) is mixture-dependent — guarantee tightness shifts with attack prevalence in deployment.
04

Open Questions

Q1

How does per-field CRC behave under interactive deployment with live replanning after a block/revert — where a blocked field changes downstream tool selection and call ordering?

Q2

Can the certificate extend to tool selection and omitted actions (currently out of scope) — perhaps via a role-like stratification over the operator op itself?

Q3

How should within-episode same-role field dependence be handled without the conservative one-per-episode construction — e.g. cluster-robust conformal bounds?

Q4

How sensitive is the prevalence-coupled V(r) guarantee in low-attack-prevalence deployment (π ≪ 85%), and can the prevalence-invariant V_att cert be made practical with cheaper attacked-field labels?

Q5

Against full reproductions of CaMeL's dual-LLM IFC or FIDES's value-tagged labels under identical detectors — does per-field CRC still lead, or does the proxy gap close?

Q6

Can stronger deployable detectors (learned provenance models beyond similarity differences) cut the 30.6%–84% over-intervention while preserving the 0% violation floor?
