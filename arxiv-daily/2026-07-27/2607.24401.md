> **一句话总结：** proxymate 提出四层(代表性/单元/估计/域)的代理指标诊断-调整框架,通过诊断层判断"是否需要、以及需要哪种"调整,在 Meta 六个生产场景中验证了约 300 个非支付实验、约 7000 个 LAW 转化实验,并在诈骗流行率场景中拒绝所有候选代理以避免误用。

> **TL;DR:** proxymate is a four-level (Representativity / Unit / Estimate / Domain) framework and open-source Python package that diagnoses proxy-outcome failures and only applies PPI / Platt / isotonic / regression-calibration / IW adjustments when their assumptions hold, formally proving (Theorem 1) that PPI on an already-adequate proxy strictly increases MSE by σ²_Δ/n; across six Meta production applications it validated proxies enabling ~7,500 experiments/year (e.g. ~300 Nonpayment, ~7,000 LAW conversion experiments) and rejected all candidate scam-prevalence proxies whose naive 13.9% prevalence collapsed to a 3.0% IW-corrected estimate (≈11× gap).

## 核心贡献

- 提出四层验证框架:将代理指标诊断分解为 **Representativity Level**(总体有效性,IW 重加权)、**Unit Level**(测量质量)、**Estimate Level**(决策有效性,PPI/CI 校准)、**Domain Level**(跨域可迁移性,回归校准/异质性扩展),每层对应不同的统计问题与数据结构。
- 构建"诊断到调整的桥梁"(Table 1):将具体失败模式(标注缺口、单元系统偏差、误校准、聚合偏差、CI 误校准、跨域偏差、高异质性)映射到满足其假设的对应调整方法,并给出形式化保证(Bridge B.1–B.7)。
- **定理 1 形式化证明**:对已无偏的代理施加 PPI 会严格增加 MSE(增量 σ²_Δ/n),证明盲目调整有害,从而确立"先诊断再决定是否调整"的 gating rule。
- Meta 大规模生产落地:六个生产应用(Nonpayment、Mobile Perf、LAW Conversions、Account Type、Scams、Ads Quality)覆盖四种主要瓶颈(长成熟周期、受限标签、低可检测性、低敏感度),共评估/校正数百万条代理-主指标单元对比。
- 开源 Python 包(`facebookresearch/proxymate`):模块化检查(共 46 个 check,跨四层 5/27/8/6)、可配置 planner、自定义聚合估计器、结构化报告输出。

## Key Contributions

- A **four-level diagnostic framework** that decomposes proxy validation into Representativity (population validity), Unit (measurement quality), Estimate (decision validity) and Domain (cross-domain transportability), each level mapping to a distinct statistical question and adjustment family.
- A **formal diagnostic-to-adjustment bridge** (Table 1, Bridges B.1–B.7): each failure mode is paired with the adjustment whose assumptions the diagnostic verifies, closing the gap between "diagnose" and "correct".
- **Theorem 1** formally shows that applying Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) to an already-unbiased proxy *strictly* inflates MSE by σ²_Δ/n, motivating the framework's "diagnose-then-gate" principle rather than blind adjustment.
- **Six production deployments at Meta** spanning four primary-outcome bottlenecks (long maturation, restricted labels, low detectability, low sensitivity), exercising every component of the framework and collectively assessing millions of proxy–primary unit comparisons.
- An **open-source package** (`github.com/facebookresearch/proxymate`) exposing modular checks, configurable planners, custom estimators (e.g. for ATE), and structured reports.

## Methodology

proxymate treats proxy validation as a layered pipeline. Let Y be the primary outcome, Y* the proxy, X auxiliary covariates, and K_i a domain index. Within historical domains k=1..K−1 both Y and Y* are observed (validation data V_k), while target domain K has only Y*. Bias in the proxy estimate θ*_K arises from (i) measurement error in Y* at the unit level and (ii) distribution shift from historical to target domains.

**Level 1 — Representativity (population validity):** Checks whether V_k is representative of P_k via standardized mean differences (SMD<0.1), Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests, Kish's effective sample size, weight extremity (max/min, CV), and coverage |V_k|/|P_k|. Adjustment options: none (representative), importance-weight reweighting (covariate-shift assumption: p(x)/q(x)), or "gap too severe" flag triggering fallbacks (targeted labelling, narrowed target, sensitivity analysis, segment-restricted validation).

**Level 2 — Unit (measurement quality):** 27 checks across six properties — accuracy (Welch's t-test, MAPE), precision (R², RMSE, Pearson r), agreement (Lin's CCC, Bland–Altman, directional concordance), calibration (ECE, Brier, BSS, calibration ratio, ECCE), discrimination (ROC AUC, PR-AUC, Kendall τ, Spearman ρ, classification metrics, MCC, confusion matrix), and distributional fidelity (KS, TV, Wasserstein-1). Adjustments: additive bias correction for systematic bias, Platt scaling / isotonic regression / MCGrad multicalibration for miscalibration, and proxy replacement (no post-hoc fix) for poor discrimination.

**Level 3 — Estimate (decision validity):** 8 checks over B bootstrap samples per domain — aggregate bias (relative absolute error), inferential validity (proxy CI covers primary), CI coverage, CI overlap, relative precision, sign agreement, effect-size agreement, and unbiasedness. Adjustments: PPI estimator θ̂_PPI = θ̂*_all + (θ̂_V − θ̂*_V) under representativity; PPI CIs that widen by σ²_Δ/n.

**Level 4 — Domain (cross-domain transportability):** 6 checks via inverse-variance-weighted random-effects meta-regression θ_k = γ₀ + γ₁θ*_k + ε_k — aggregate association (R² generalizing Buyse's R²_trial), effect sensitivity (γ₁≈1), directional alignment, prediction accuracy (LOO MSE/MAE), heterogeneity (Cochran's Q, I², τ²), and residual autocorrelation (Durbin–Watson). Adjustments: regression calibration θ̂^cal_K = γ̂₀ + γ̂₁θ̂*_K with nominal coverage as K→∞, estimate-level adjustment (models residual bias φ_k ~ N(ρ, γ²)) that works from summary statistics only, or interval widening when I² is high. Requires ≥5 domains for reliable heterogeneity estimation; bootstrap recommended for K≲30.

The framework also includes a **validation planner** mapping recurring bottlenecks to recommended check subsets, and configurable thresholds (SMD<0.1, I²<25%) that teams can tighten for high-stakes decisions.

## Results & Highlights

- **Case Study A (Nonpayment, long-term outcome):** Across 22 experiments (K=27 arms), the proxy showed strong Domain-Level aggregate association (**R² = 0.82**), near-one effect sensitivity (**γ₁ = 0.98**), **23/27 arms directionally aligned (85%)**, and negligible residual heterogeneity (**I² ≈ 0, τ̂² ≈ 0**, Cochran's Q = 22.6 on 25 df, **p = 0.60**), even though LOO prediction accuracy failed (MAE = 0.84 vs median per-arm SE = 0.41). The validated proxy now powers **~300 experiments annually**.
- **Case Study B (Scams prevalence):** Naive expert-review prevalence was **31.9%**; the IW-corrected primary estimate collapsed to **3.0% (≈11× lower)**. PPI on Proxy A gave **10.7%**, down 3.3 pp from its naive proxy estimate of **13.9%**, but still ~3.5× the IW-corrected primary — so all three proxies were rejected.
- **Sensitivity after IW reweighting** dropped sharply for all scam proxies: **17.3% (Proxy A), 31.6% (B), 30.1% (C)** vs much higher numbers on the over-represented flagged subset, surfacing a Unit-Level failure invisible on the unweighted sample.
- **Aggregate production scale:** The four validated-proxy applications enable **~7,500 experiments annually** at Meta — dominated by **~7,000 Long Attribution Window (LAW) conversion experiments** and **~300 Nonpayment experiments** — plus the Mobile-perf proxy (~200 experiments/year, with "multiple billion incremental user sessions").
- **Bidirectional impact:** In two of six applications (Scams, Ads Quality) all candidate proxies were rejected, preventing deployment of a personalization model trained on biased proxy outcomes and redirecting proxy development toward an ensemble approach.
- **Theoretical result (Theorem 1):** MSE(θ̂_PPI) < MSE(θ̂*) ⇔ |φ| > σ_Δ/√n; when the proxy is already unbiased (φ=0) PPI strictly increases MSE by σ²_Δ/n, providing the formal gating rule.

## Authors & Credibility

All four authors (Alexandra N. M. Darmon, Deeksha Sinha, Steven Wilkins-Reeves, Caner Gocmen) are at **Meta** (Meta email addresses), with Sinha and Wilkins-Reeves marked as equal contributors. The work is grounded in production measurement systems at one of the world's largest experimentation platforms, and the companion paper [52] (Wilkins-Reeves et al., on estimate-level adjustment under random distribution shifts, submitted to KDD '26) provides the formal coverage theory. The framework builds on, rather than reinvents, well-established diagnostics (SMD from Austin 2011, ECE from Naeini 2015, I² from Higgins 2003) and adjustments (PPI Angelopoulos et al. 2023 *Science*, Platt scaling, regression calibration, DerSimonian–Laird meta-analysis). The paper is candid about confidentiality constraints ("reported percentages, counts, and per-arm statistics have been perturbed") and lists explicit limitations.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The individual diagnostics and adjustment methods are explicitly *not* claimed as novel — the contribution is the *framework that connects them* and the open-source implementation. The genuinely new theoretical content is Theorem 1 (PPI on an unbiased proxy strictly inflates MSE by σ²_Δ/n) and the seven "bridge" results that map each failure mode to the adjustment whose assumptions the diagnostic checks. This is an engineering/framework contribution dressed as a research paper, which is appropriate for an industrial venue but should be evaluated as such.
- **Rigor:** Theoretical claims are backed by proofs in Appendix B (Propositions 1–5, Theorem 1). The meta-regression coverage claim correctly notes that plain OLS on noisy (θ̂_k, θ̂*_k) is inconsistent due to attenuation and must be replaced by inverse-variance-weighted regression — a subtlety many practitioners miss. The honest reporting that Case Study A's LOO accuracy *fails* (MAE 0.84 vs SE 0.41) even though R²=0.82 and I²≈0, and the careful distinction between IW (sample-level, Representativity Level) and PPI (proxy-level, Estimate Level) in Case B, show methodological care.
- **Reproducibility:** Mixed. The package is open-sourced (`facebookresearch/proxymate`), which is a strong plus. However, all six production applications are described only qualitatively with **perturbed numbers** for confidentiality — none of the actual datasets, code paths, or raw numbers are released, so the headline scale claims (millions of comparisons, ~7,500 experiments/year) cannot be independently verified, and the two detailed case studies cannot be reproduced end-to-end.
- **Significance:** High practical value for industrial experimentation and prevalence-estimation teams; the four-level taxonomy and "diagnose-then-gate" workflow are genuinely useful organizing principles. The framework is, however, conceptually close to Tripuraneni et al. 2024 [44] (proxy selection) and Wilkins-Reeves et al. 2026 [52] (estimate-level adjustment), and the differentiator over a thoughtful pipeline assembled from `ppi_py` + standard calibration metrics is mostly the diagnostic-to-adjustment map and the gating rule.
- **Red flags / overclaiming:** (i) The "millions of proxy, primary unit comparisons" scale claim is unverifiable. (ii) Case Study A has only **22 experiments / 27 arms** — well below the K≳30 bootstrap threshold the paper itself recommends for Domain-Level interval coverage, so the "safe for direction-based decision making" conclusion rests on under-powered heterogeneity estimation. (iii) The Case Study B "11× collapse" (31.9% → 3.0%) is presented as a vindication of IW, but the paper itself concedes the 3.0% is "best-available under covariate shift, not ground truth" — i.e., the framework rejected the proxies based on an estimate it cannot validate either. (iv) Domain-Level diagnostics are flagged as unreliable for <5 historical domains, yet Case Study B uses a single target domain with IW-based corrections — the cross-domain transportability claim is thin there.

## Strengths

- The four-level decomposition (Representativity / Unit / Estimate / Domain) is conceptually clean and explicitly justified (collapsing Unit+Estimate would hide how unit errors compound in aggregation; collapsing Estimate+Domain would conflate within-domain bias with cross-domain instability).
- The diagnostic-to-adjustment bridge (Table 1) is a genuinely useful artifact — it tells a practitioner *which* of PPI / Platt / isotonic / regression-calibration / IW / replacement to apply rather than treating them as interchangeable.
- Theorem 1's gating rule is a sharp, citable argument against the common "always PPI" reflex, with an actionable break-even threshold |φ| > σ_Δ/√n.
- The Case Study B discussion distinguishing IW (sample-level) from PPI (proxy-level) correction — and showing PPI alone would have reported ~10.7% vs the IW-corrected 3.0% — is one of the clearest expositions of why diagnostic ordering matters.
- Open-source package with 46 modular checks and a configurable planner lowers the adoption barrier.
- Intellectual honesty about both the framework's boundaries (no causal mechanisms, one-proxy-one-outcome) and the production tradeoff that "the best statistical proxy was not always the best deployable proxy."

## Limitations

- Validates **one proxy against one primary outcome at a time**; no multi-outcome or sequential/online validation (the authors flag confidence sequences and multi-outcome validation as future work).
- Establishes **statistical agreement, not causal mechanisms** — a proxy can pass all four levels and still fail as a causal surrogate.
- All empirical evidence is from Meta production with **perturbed, non-releasable numbers**; external reproducibility is limited to the package, not the case studies.
- Domain-Level meta-analysis needs **≥5 (ideally ≥10–30) historical domains** for reliable heterogeneity estimation and interval coverage; many real applications (Case Study A: 22 experiments / 27 arms; Case Study B: single target) sit near or below this threshold.
- Importance weighting can have **high variance** when the labeled sample and population diverge (low ESS) — the framework flags this but the fallback options (targeted labelling, narrowed target) are operational, not statistical, remedies.
- Thresholds (SMD<0.1, I²<25%, ECE cutoffs) are based on conventions and "practitioner experience" rather than derived from decision-theoretic calibration; the paper offers no sensitivity analysis of conclusions to threshold choice.

## Relevance

对在线实验、代理指标(A/B surrogate)、流行率估计和 ML 模型预测结果用作主指标替代的场景高度相关——这正是日常 A/B 实验平台、内容治理指标和长期转化指标验证所面临的核心问题。如果你关心"何时该用 PPI / 校准 / IW 调整代理指标、以及盲目调整的风险",这篇框架和开源包可直接借鉴。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does proxymate's diagnostic-to-adjustment map perform under **active covariate shift** where the importance weights themselves drift between the validation and target domains (e.g. seasonal or campaign-driven shifts)?
- Can Theorem 1's gating rule be extended to a **sequential / anytime-valid** form using confidence sequences, so the "skip PPI" decision can be made (and reversed) as labeled data accrues?
- The framework rejects one proxy at a time; what is the principled way to combine the rejected proxies (e.g. Case Study B's A/B/C) into an **ensemble** whose joint diagnostic profile passes the Unit/Estimate Levels?
- How sensitive are the Meta case-study conclusions to the choice of SMD<0.1 / I²<25% / ECE thresholds, and is there a decision-theoretic derivation of these cutoffs tied to downstream decision cost?
- Does the estimate-level adjustment [52], layered on top of a PPI-corrected proxy, double-count uncertainty when the same validation set is used for both the PPI bias estimate and the residual-bias variance γ²?
- Can the framework be extended to **multi-outcome** proxies (e.g. surrogate indices that combine several short-term signals), where correlations among the component proxies complicate the Unit-Level discrimination and Domain-Level heterogeneity checks?
