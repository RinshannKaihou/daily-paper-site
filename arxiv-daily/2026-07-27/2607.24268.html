Key Finding · Measurement Validity

Under matched output budgets, targeted verification flips a benchmark gap from −7.89 to +6.89 pp — accuracy conflates execution state with scoring policy.

Accuracy Hides How Language Models Fail: Measuring Failure States Under Matched Output Budgets

Zongyou Yang · Dyson School of Design Engineering, Imperial College London Yinghan Hou · EEE, Imperial College London
2026-07-27 llm-evaluation failure-state-analysis output-budget llm-as-a-judge measurement-validity

Key Metric · Accuracy Reversal

−7.89→+6.89pp

DeepSeek − Qwen on MATH@2048. A 14.78-point swing produced purely by changing verification coverage — not by changing the models.

Why This Matters

Benchmark accuracy silently blends response availability with grading decisions — your model comparison may be measuring truncation and scoring policy, not capability.

Critical Edge

The headline reversal is driven by asymmetric verification coverage (DeepSeek 70/70 vs Qwen 24/77 candidates reviewed), so it shows selective verification can lie — more than any grader bias.

03

Strengths & Limitations

side-by-side

✓ Strengths

  • Operationalizes a distinction — execution vs scoring — that the evaluation literature talks around but rarely measures under matched budgets.
  • Discloses asymmetric coverage (DeepSeek 93.3%/100% vs Qwen 31.2%/85.7%) instead of hiding it, and traces the reversal to the joint selection-and-scoring pipeline.
  • Reports scorer-independent endpoints (termination, availability) as primary, with scorer-sensitive correctness as a labelled sensitivity analysis.
  • Ships a minimal reporting protocol (execution / scoring / comparison / provenance) benchmark authors could adopt today.
  • Excellent uncertainty hygiene — question-clustered bootstrap CIs, explicit associational-vs-causal framing throughout.

! Limitations

  • Narrow scope. Two tasks, three Qwen snapshots, two DeepSeek endpoints — no codegen, open QA, or dialogue; no population-level family claims.
  • Confounds. Cross-config comparisons bundle architecture, scale, serving stack, and generation behavior.
  • Data leakage. MATH uses training-split items possibly seen in pretraining — response states, not uncontaminated capability.
  • Missing Qwen response bodies. Later Qwen scoring relies on parsed answers + hashes, not raw text — limits independent reparse.
  • LLM panel, not human gold. Correlated training data can yield agreement without correctness; Kimi lane dropped post-hoc.
  • Targeted audit only. 164/2,550 outputs re-scored — panel-accepted accuracy is not full-sample truth.
04

Open questions for further work

non-linear entry points

Do LMF / LFP / NLU rates survive on open-ended generation, code, and multi-turn dialogue where no clean FINAL: anchor exists?

Would the −7.89 → +6.89 reversal survive a fully symmetric pipeline (same coverage rate across both families)?

Can a pre-intervention execution-state distribution match test-time recovery methods to the case mix they are evaluated on?

The DeepSeek MATH cap effect (5/300 → 0/300, p = 0.0625) is suggestive but underpowered — how does the LMF-vs-cap curve look across 2k/4k/8k/16k?

What would human-gold adjudication of the 164 candidates do to Fleiss' κ and the unanimity-vs-majority split?

Is Qwen MATH's LMF an artifact of reasoning-trace length — and does it concentrate in the hardest of the five difficulty levels?
