Grounding latent algorithm routing in transformer reasoning

Key metric
80.9% gap closed

A 306M decoder-only transformer closes 80.9% of the oracle-routing gap, route F1 84.1 — beating every adaptive baseline short of the privileged-stat router (87.5).

Why this matters

Gives a reusable three-criterion protocol — structural necessity, nuisance invariance, causal editability — to test whether transformers truly select algorithms internally, not just match outputs.

Critical edge

The best dense model (F1 85.8) still trails a hand-statistic router, and all findings are scoped to a synthetic R²⁴ benchmark — routing inside pretrained LLMs is explicitly not established.

Methodology

ROUTEBENCH · from-scratch 44M–612M

An episode e = (S, q) is serialized in a fixed surface form while the latent data-generating regime varies. Route labels are never shown to the model — routing is defined behaviorally: which of four solver families (ridge / lasso / Huber / kNN) the output lands closest to.

Routing is claimed meaningful only if it survives three independent grounding tests:

Structural necessity

The preferred solver family changes when the latent regime z changes — even though the prompt syntax stays identical.

Nuisance invariance

The route stays stable under semantics-preserving perturbations: format, ordering, lexical paraphrase, support shuffling.

Causal editability

Targeted activation edits change route identity more than they degrade answer quality — functional involvement, not generic corruption.

A short theory says why routing is unavoidable and where the variable should live:

Thm 1 — any fixed solver family pays irreducible regret ≥ πmin·γmin.
Thm 2 — a global soft mixture over opposing regimes has excess error ≥ Δ²/4.
Prop 1 — route identity is linearly decodable from a mid-layer hidden state when the route margin beats noise.
Prop 2 — a sufficient edit vector flips the route with output distortion bounded by the readout's Lipschitz constant.

Models are decoder-only transformers trained from scratch at five scales on mixed-regime next-token prediction only — no route labels, no branch IDs, no chain-of-thought. Controls include probe-permutation, margin filtering, and random / same-route / wrong-route patching.

Mechanistic evidence

probes + matched-pair patching

Mid-layer route probes become decodable earlier than answer probes (peak AUC 0.81 → 0.92 with scale) — matching Proposition 1's layer-timing prediction. Matched-pair activation patching at the probe-peak layer flips the solver family while retaining answer quality; control edits do not.

04

Strengths & limitations

side-by-side

Strengths PROS

  • Triangulates three independent grounding criteria — far harder to dismiss as a single-method artifact than typical ICL interp work.
  • Clean theory (2 theorems + 2 propositions) that generates testable predictions the experiments confirm.
  • Full baseline ladder: fixed → mixture → cond. mixture → Gumbel → privileged-stat → oracle.
  • Matched controls (random / same / wrong-route patches) separate functional involvement from corruption.
  • Honest scoping — explicit non-claims about pretrained LLMs, NL, math, code, agentic reasoning.

Limitations CAVEATS

  • Synthetic, stylized benchmark — linear/clustered episodes in R²⁴ with four hand-picked families.
  • Best dense model (85.8) still trails the privileged-stat router (87.5) on route F1.
  • Behavioral proxy circularity — ρ̂beh measures closeness to the same families used to design the regimes.
  • No compute-cost (GPU-hours) report; seed variance shown for only one 167M figure.
  • From-scratch only — the hard test (probing real pretrained LLMs) is left as future work.
  • Switch-MoE "equivalents" are not exact FLOP/parameter matches.
05

Open questions

for further exploration
  1. Do the same decodable, causally-editable route variables appear inside pretrained LLMs (Llama / Qwen / GPT-class), or is this an artifact of from-scratch training on one mixed-regime distribution?
  2. How does latent activation-level routing relate quantitatively to explicit expert routing in MoE — does expert identity converge to solver-family identity with scale, or stay orthogonal?
  3. Can the three grounding criteria port to natural-language tasks (real regression prompts, retrieval-vs-reasoning) without the regime-design circularity that affects ρ̂beh?
  4. What happens with more than four families, or non-disjoint regimes (sparsity and robustness) — does the route variable become continuous / probabilistic?
  5. Does routing survive distribution shift in the families themselves (a different lasso implementation) — i.e. does the model route on inductive-bias structure vs. superficial solver-output statistics?
