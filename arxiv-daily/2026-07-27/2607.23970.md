> **一句话总结：** 本文首次将模式连通性（mode connectivity）引入机器遗忘研究，提出 MCU 框架，通过在 LLM 遗忘基准（TOFU、MUSE、WMDP）与多模态分类任务（MU-Bench 的 CIFAR-10、DDI2013、NLVR2）上连接两个已遗忘模型的低损路径，揭示了遗忘具有平滑损失地形、同一盆地内模型在隐私指标上可剧烈差异、近似遗忘与重训练机制本质不同，并证明基于 MCU 的集成可同时提升泛化与抗重学习攻击鲁棒性。

> **TL;DR:** The first study of machine unlearning through mode connectivity (MCU) finds that unlearned models (across TOFU, MUSE, WMDP, and MU-Bench classification tasks) often lie on smooth low-loss paths while changes in training dynamics (curriculum learning, second-order optimization) can split them into disconnected basins; MCU also reveals that approximate unlearning is mechanistically distinct from retraining, that models within the same basin can swing wildly on privacy metrics, and that MCU-based ensembling improves both generalization (WMDP forget accuracy 63.7 → 21.5 vs. plain NPO) and robustness to relearning attacks.

## 核心贡献

- **提出 MCU 框架**：首次将模式连通性扩展到机器遗忘场景，定义了一个需要同时在保留集 D_r 上保持低损、在遗忘集 D_f 上保持高损的双目标连通路径判据（公式 3、4），为研究遗忘的损失地形提供了形式化工具。
- **系统性刻画遗忘的损失地形**：在 LLM 遗忘基准（TOFU、MUSE、WMDP）与 MU-Bench 多模态分类任务（CIFAR-10、DDI2013、NLVR2）上，跨 5 种 LLM 遗忘方法（GA、GradDiff、NPO、SimNPO、RMU）和 4 种分类遗忘方法（GA、RL、BT、SU）评估 MCU。
- **首次将课程学习（CL）、二阶优化（SO）、跨方法连接引入模式连通性研究**：除随机种子外，其余设置（Rand-CL、Rand-SO、CL-Non-CL、FO-SO、Met-*）均为模式连通性文献中的全新配置（表 1）。
- **揭示遗忘的新几何性质**：发现"功能异质盆地"（同一盆地内模型在隐私/泄漏指标上差异巨大）、遗忘沿参数方向"先抑制、后遗忘"的非线性过程、以及近似遗忘与重训练机制本质不同（仅 NPO 与重训练机制相似）。
- **将 MCU 转化为实用工具**：证明基于 MCU 的集成（沿曲线采样并平均参数）能提升泛化与抗重学习攻击鲁棒性，并可作为遗忘难度的几何代理指标。

## Key Contributions

- **MCU framework**: Formalizes mode connectivity for unlearning by requiring a path that simultaneously keeps retain-set loss low and forget-set loss high (Eqs. 3–4), giving the first loss-landscape lens on machine unlearning.
- **Landscape characterization across tasks and methods**: Evaluates MCU on LLM unlearning benchmarks (TOFU, MUSE, WMDP) with GA, GradDiff, NPO, SimNPO, RMU, and on MU-Bench classification tasks (CIFAR-10, DDI2013, NLVR2) with GA, RL, BT, SU.
- **Novel training-dynamics configurations for mode connectivity**: Introduces curriculum-learning (CL) and second-order-optimization (SO) conditions — all settings except the randomness factor are new to the mode-connectivity literature (Table 1).
- **New geometric insights about unlearning**: Identifies functionally heterogeneous basins (large privacy swings within one basin), a non-linear "suppress-first, unlearn-later" progression along the original→unlearned direction, and mechanistic dissimilarity between approximate unlearning and retraining.
- **Practical MCU applications**: Shows MCU-based ensembling improves generalization and robustness to relearning attacks, and that MCU smoothness serves as a geometric proxy for unlearning difficulty.

## Methodology

**Framework (MCU).** Given an original model f_θo trained on D = D_r ∪ D_f, two unlearned models θ′_1, θ′_2 are obtained by applying an unlearning procedure U under different configurations. Mode Connectivity in Unlearning holds if there exists a curve ϕ(t), t∈[0,1], with ϕ(0)=θ′_1 and ϕ(1)=θ′_2, such that for all t the retain-set loss stays low (Eq. 3) and the forget-set loss stays high (Eq. 4) — i.e., no "barriers" (sudden loss increases on D_r or decreases on D_f) along the path. This dual-objective constraint is the key difference from standard mode connectivity, which only considers a single dataset.

**Curve parameterizations.** Two curves are used: a *linear* interpolation ϕ(t)=(1−t)θ_1+tθ_2 (no optimization; stronger linear connectivity ⇒ greater mechanistic similarity per Lubana et al., 2023), and a *quadratic* curve whose midpoint θ_12 is trained by minimizing the line-integral of the loss along the curve (Eq. 5–8).

**Training-dynamics configurations (Table 1).** Beyond standard randomness (Rand), the authors introduce CL-based (Rand-CL, CL-Non-CL), SO-based (Rand-SO, FO-SO), and cross-method (Met-*) configurations to test whether learning-order and optimizer choices move unlearning solutions into different basins.

**Evaluation.** 16 equally-spaced interpolation points t∈[0,1] are sampled (following Qin et al., 2022). Metrics: TOFU — Forget Quality, Model Utility (KS-test p-values); MUSE — Forget VerbMem/KnowMem (↓), Retain KnowMem (↑), |PrivLeak| (↓); WMDP — forget-set accuracy (↓), MMLU accuracy (↑); MU-Bench — test/forget/retain accuracy and Zero-Retrain Forgetting (ZRF). Barriers are quantified by *max barrier* (largest interior rise above the worse endpoint) and *mean barrier* (average positive excess).

**Probes.** (1) Original→unlearned linear interpolation probes the unlearning direction; (2) retraining-vs-unlearning and pairwise method comparisons use *linear* MCU to infer mechanistic (dis)similarity; (3) MCU-based ensembling averages N=3 sampled minimizers plus the two endpoints; (4) relearning attacks fine-tune the unlearned model on generic Wikipedia text and re-measure forgetting.

## Results & Highlights

- **Unlearning has a smooth landscape; MCU becomes smoother with scale (Table 2).** On TOFU, scaling the forget set from 1% → 10% and the model from 1B → 8B shrinks the *max barrier*: e.g., 1B/1% = 18.1 vs 8B/10% = 0.0 (max barrier), and 1B/1% mean barrier = 4.1 vs 8B/10% mean barrier = 0.0. The authors note this smoothness comes *at the cost of worse unlearning quality*, indicating larger-scale unlearning is intrinsically harder.
- **CL and SO move unlearning into different basins (Table 3).** Average D_f barrier across configs: Rand mean=1.2/max=3.0; Rand-CL mean=0.7/max=2.2; Rand-SO mean=0.2/max=1.2 — but CL-Non-CL mean=2.0/max=8.8 and FO-SO mean=1.8/max=8.1. The large max barriers under mixed CL/SO conditions signal disconnected basins.
- **Functionally heterogeneous basins (Figure 3).** On MUSE-News with RMU, Forget/Retain KnowMem and Forget VerbMem stay nearly flat along the MCU curve, yet |PrivLeak| swings across the full [−100, +100] range — models in the same basin can be either highly leaky or robust. On DDI with BT, D_f accuracy and ZRF are stable while D_r/D_t fluctuate substantially.
- **Approximate unlearning is mechanistically distinct from retraining (Figure 5).** Linear MCU between a from-scratch retrained model and an unlearned model fails on forget metrics for most methods (GA, GD, SimNPO, RMU); *only NPO* shows mechanistic similarity to retraining, even when endpoint utility is comparable.
- **MCU-based ensembling improves generalization (Table 4).** On WMDP, forget-set accuracy (↓): plain NPO = 63.7, NPO+MCU = 24.1, Ensemble = 21.5 — a large improvement over the base unlearner.
- **MCU improves robustness to relearning attacks (Figure 6).** After relearning attacks (Relearn20/40/60), standard NPO's forgetting effect deteriorates quickly, whereas the MCU-averaged model retains markedly higher forget accuracy post-attack.
- **MCU smoothness proxies unlearning difficulty (Table 5).** Max/Mean barriers on easy vs hard forget-set splits: Default max=18.1/mean=4.1, Hard max=18.5/mean=6.3, Easy max=10.3/mean=3.2 — the hard split yields larger barriers, confirming MCU geometry tracks difficulty.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: Genuinely novel framing — transplanting mode connectivity (a vision-classification phenomenon) into the dual-objective unlearning setting is non-trivial, and the CL/SO/cross-method configurations (all new except Rand) are a real contribution to the connectivity literature, not just to unlearning.
- **Rigor**: The dual-barrier definition (Eqs. 3–4) is well-motivated, and the choice of 16 interpolation points follows prior LM mode-connectivity work (Qin et al., 2022). However, the paper leans heavily on *qualitative curve reading* (Figures 2–8) — the quantitative tables (2–5) cover only a subset of settings, and barrier statistics are not reported with variance or significance across seeds for most claims.
- **Reproducibility**: Good on paper — public datasets, standard methods, released code, and clear metric definitions in Appendix C. But curve-finding hyperparameters and the exact seeds/configs for each figure are partly deferred to the repo, and the quadratic-curve midpoint training cost (training an explicit θ_12 per pair) is not discussed in terms of compute, which limits independent replication of the larger-scale (8B) runs.
- **Significance**: The mechanistic-dissimilarity finding (unlearning ≠ retraining; only NPO approximates retraining) and the "functionally heterogeneous basin" observation (huge PrivLeak swings within one basin) are conceptually important and directly indict current point-estimate evaluation protocols. The MCU-ensemble and difficulty-proxy results are promising but early-stage.
- **Red flags / overclaiming**: (1) The MCU-ensemble generalization result (Table 4) is a single WMDP-Cyber number with no error bars and no comparison to other ensembling baselines, making the headline "improves generalization" stronger than the evidence supports. (2) The "MCU smoothness correlates with difficulty" claim rests on one easy/hard split from the authors' own prior work (Cheng et al., 2026) — a single difficulty source is a thin basis for a general proxy. (3) The scaling table (Table 2) conflates "smoother landscape" with "harder unlearning" without isolating whether the effect is driven by forget-set size or model size, since both vary together. (4) GA, GD, and RMU on MUSE are reported as forming "basins of low unlearning performance," but the paper does not separate MCU failure from these methods being weak unlearners to begin with.

## Strengths

- First principled geometric framework (MCU) for a problem — unlearning loss landscapes — that has been almost entirely empirically studied.
- Broad, well-chosen method and task coverage (5 LLM + 4 classification unlearners; 3 LLM benchmarks + 3 MU-Bench tasks), making the qualitative findings credible.
- The "functionally heterogeneous basin" and "suppress-first, unlearn-later" observations are fresh, intuitive, and have clear implications for evaluation and for membership-inference/privacy auditing.
- Practically actionable: MCU-ensembling and the difficulty proxy are concrete, low-cost ideas practitioners can adopt.

## Limitations

- Quantitative evidence is uneven: many key claims rest on visual inspection of curves rather than aggregated barrier statistics with confidence intervals.
- The MCU-ensemble benefit is demonstrated on essentially one setting (WMDP-Cyber) without baselines or multiple seeds.
- The difficulty-proxy validation uses a single external difficulty split; generalization to other difficulty notions (adversarial, paraphrased, mechanistic) is untested.
- Quadratic curve construction requires explicitly training a midpoint θ_12 per endpoint pair, whose compute cost is not reported and could be prohibitive for 8B models.
- The relationship between MCU smoothness and *true* unlearning efficacy (vs. apparent forget metrics) is not dissected; surface-form forgetting vs. deep forgetting is observed qualitatively but not quantified along the curve.

## Relevance

对研究机器遗忘（machine unlearning）的损失地形、评估方法与鲁棒性而言高度相关：MCU 提供了一个全新的几何诊断工具，直击当前"点估计评估无法区分隐私泄漏差异"的痛点，并能指导更鲁棒的遗忘模型选择与集成策略；其"近似遗忘 ≠ 重训练"的结论对隐私合规与"被遗忘权"场景下的方法可信度评估有直接启发。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does MCU-ensembling generalize beyond WMDP-Cyber — e.g., to TOFU/MUSE and to classification tasks — and how does it compare against standard weight-averaging or SAM-style ensembling baselines with matched compute?
- Can the "functionally heterogeneous basin" PrivLeak swings be exploited constructively, e.g., by selecting the minimizer along the MCU curve that *minimizes* privacy leakage rather than averaging?
- Is the "suppress-first, unlearn-later" progression a universal property, or does it depend on the unlearning method (GA vs. NPO vs. RMU) and forget-set type (verbatim vs. knowledge)?
- Does MCU smoothness correlate with other, independent notions of unlearning difficulty (adversarial forget sets, paraphrased sets, circuit-based difficulty), not just the single split tested here?
- What is the compute cost of quadratic-curve midpoint training at 8B scale, and can cheaper curve-finding (e.g., input-space connectivity à la Vrabel et al., 2025) recover the same geometric insights?
- Since only NPO is mechanistically similar to retraining, can a connectivity-regularized loss be designed that *forces* unlearning solutions onto the retraining-connected subspace, and would that improve verifiability?
