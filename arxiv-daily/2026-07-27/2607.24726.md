> **一句话总结：** 本文针对一类半线性 PDE（解及其一阶导数非线性），首次证明：用截断梯度下降（clipped gradient descent）最小化 PDE 残差目标函数训练得到的 DGM/PINN 两层神经网络，在隐藏单元数 N→∞ 且训练时间 t→∞ 时，会（在 H¹ 范数下）全局收敛到 PDE 的唯一弱解，而不会陷入非解的局部极小值；并给出了允许两个极限可交换的一致时间界。

> **TL;DR:** This is the first global-convergence proof for DGM/PINN training on **nonlinear** PDEs. For semi-linear elliptic PDEs `Au = g` with `Au = Lu + f(u, u_x)` (nonlinear in the solution and its first derivative), the authors prove that a two-layer network trained by clipped gradient descent on the PDE-residual objective `J^N(θ)` converges to the unique weak solution `u` in H¹ as width `N→∞` and training time `t→∞`. The proof builds a new mean-field / Neural-Tangent-Kernel analysis for the non-convex, non-local, time-dependent limit PDE that the infinite-width training trajectory satisfies, and additionally establishes uniform-in-time error bounds showing the two limits are interchangeable: `lim_{N→∞} lim_{t→∞} E[J_s^N] = 0`. This strictly extends the linear-elliptic-PDE result of Jiang, Sirignano & Cohen (SIAM J. Financial Math., 2026, ref. [2]), whose proof breaks down for nonlinear PDEs. No numerical experiments, benchmarks, or datasets are reported — the contribution is entirely theoretical.

## 核心贡献

- **首个非线性 PDE 的全局收敛证明：** 此前 DGM/PINN 的全局收敛结果（参考文献 [2]）仅适用于线性椭圆 PDE；本文首次对半线性（解与一阶导数非线性）PDE 证明：经截断梯度下降训练、最小化 PDE 残差的两层神经网络会收敛到 PDE 的解，而非非解的局部极小值。
- **克服"不凸化"难题的新分析框架：** 一般监督学习在 N→∞ 时网络会"凸化"，但半线性 PDE 的极限网络满足一个非线性、非局部的 PDE（梯度流），先验上仍可能陷入局部极小。作者开发了全新的方法来处理该非凸极限动力学。
- **处理时变非线性核与时变谱：** 极限训练 PDE 涉及一个随时间变化、且依赖于 (Q, Q_x) 的非线性核 S_{Q(t)}(x,y)；其特征值/特征函数随时间漂移、正特征值无谱隙（在零处聚集）、还可能存在零特征值。本文给出了一致先验界并证明了收敛。
- **刻画不动点为零残差全局极小：** 通过 D_Q 的满射性（Assumption 3，充分条件 f_v ≥ 0）与 {η(x)σ(w·x+b)} 在 H²∩H₀¹ 中的稠密性，证明任何不动点都满足 ‖R‖_{L²}=0，即零 PDE 残差（Theorem 4.4）。
- **两个极限可交换的一致时间界：** 利用 J^N(θ(t)) 的单调下降性，证明对任意 ε>0，存在 t、N₀ 使得 s≥t 且 N≥N₀ 时 E[J_s^N]≤ε（Theorem 7.1），从而 `lim_{N→∞} lim_{t→∞} E[J_s^N] = 0`——这是平均场分析中通常难以得到的结论。

## Key Contributions

- **First global-convergence guarantee for nonlinear PDEs:** Prior work [2] proved global convergence of DGM/PINNs only for *linear* elliptic PDEs and its proof cannot be extended to nonlinear PDEs. This paper proves it for a class of *semi-linear* PDEs (nonlinear in the solution and its first derivative).
- **New analysis overcoming the "no convexification" obstacle:** In standard supervised learning the over-parameterized limit convexifies; for semi-linear PDEs the limit network instead satisfies a non-convex, non-local, nonlinear PDE (a gradient flow), so convexification-based arguments fail. The authors develop an entirely new proof strategy.
- **Handling a time-dependent nonlinear kernel with no spectral gap:** The limit dynamics involve a kernel S_{Q(t)}(x,y) that depends on (Q, Q_x) and therefore on time t; its eigenvalues/eigenfunctions drift, positive eigenvalues accumulate at zero (no spectral gap), and zero eigenvalues may exist. Fixed-eigenfunction projection (the approach in [2]) is unavailable; the paper establishes uniform-in-time a priori bounds in H² to overcome this.
- **Fixed-point characterization:** Any fixed point of the limit objective dynamics is shown to be a global minimizer with **zero PDE residual** (‖R‖_{L²}=0, Theorem 4.4), leveraging surjectivity of D_Q (Assumption 3; a sufficient condition is f_v ≥ 0) and density of {η(x)σ(w·x+b)} in H²∩H₀¹.
- **Interchangeable limits via uniform-in-time bounds:** Exploiting the monotonicity of J^N(θ(t)), Theorem 7.1 proves that for any ε>0 there exist t and N₀ such that E[J_s^N]≤ε for all s≥t, N≥N₀, yielding `lim_{N→∞} lim_{t→∞} E[J_s^N] = 0` — a result mean-field analysis usually cannot deliver without extra assumptions.

## Methodology

- **PDE class.** Semi-linear elliptic PDEs `Au = g` on a compact domain Ω⊂ℝⁿ with smooth boundary, Dirichlet condition `u=0` on ∂Ω, where `Au = Lu + f(u, u_x)` and `L` is a uniformly elliptic operator satisfying a dissipativity/coercivity condition (inf eigenvalue > 0). The nonlinearity `f(v,w)` and its first two derivatives are uniformly bounded (Assumption 1), and the PDE has a unique weak solution in H₀¹ (Assumption 2).
- **Network ansatz.** A two-layer network `Q_N(x;θ) = η(x) N^{-β} Σᵢ cᵢ σ(wᵢ·x + bᵢ)`, with `1/2 < β < 1`, where η vanishes on ∂Ω with positive normal derivatives (enforcing the boundary condition), σ is bounded/discriminatory (e.g. sigmoid/tanh) with ≥3 bounded derivatives, and parameters are i.i.d. initialized with mean-zero bounded cᵢ and full-support wᵢ,bᵢ of bounded moments (Assumption 4).
- **Objective & training.** The PDE-residual objective `J^N(θ) = ½ ∫_Ω (A_{Q_N} Q_N − g)² μ(dx)` (μ uniform on Ω) is minimized by **clipped gradient descent** `dθ/dt = −α_N G^N(θ(t))` with learning rate `α_N = α N^{2β−1}`. The clip φ_N is a smooth C²_b increasing function equal to the identity on [−N^γ, N^γ], bounded by 2N^γ, with `γ+β<1` (Assumption 6). The authors stress clipping is essential only for the a priori bounds; its effect provably vanishes as N→∞ and it does not appear in the limit dynamics.
- **Mean-field / NTK limit.** As N→∞ the empirical parameter measure converges to the initialization μ₀, and the training trajectory satisfies a non-local nonlinear PDE `∂_t Q(t,x) = −α ∫_Ω U_{Q(t)}(x,y) [A_{Q(t)}Q(t,y) − g(y)] μ(dy)` with Q(0,x)=0, where U_{Q(t)} is the (solution-dependent) Neural Tangent Kernel. Differentiating yields a residual evolution driven by the kernel `S_{Q(t)}(x,y) = ⟨D_Q[∇_θ(η(x)cσ(wx+b))] · D_Q[∇_θ(η(y)cσ(wy+b))], μ₀⟩`.
- **Global-convergence proof pipeline.** (i) Monotonicity `dJ/dt ≤ 0` plus uniform L² bounds on the residual R(t) (Lemma 5.1); (ii) existence/uniqueness of the limit solution in C([0,∞); H²∩H₀¹) via Banach fixed-point (Lemmas 5.2–5.3) and a uniform H² bound via coercivity of L (Lemma 5.4); (iii) precompactness in H¹ (Rellich–Kondrachov) and weak precompactness of R(t) in L² (Lemmas 5.5–5.6); (iv) Barbalat's lemma applied to `⟨R(t), S_{Q(t)} R(t)⟩ → 0` (Lemma 5.8) to conclude `lim_{t→∞} J(t)=0` (Theorem 5.10) and `Q(t) → u` in H¹ (Theorem 5.11); (v) finite-N→limit convergence `‖Q_N(t)−Q(t)‖_{H²}→0` a.s. on [0,T] (Theorem 6.4) via Gronwall + strong law of large numbers; (vi) uniform-in-time error bound on `E[J_s^N]` from monotonicity (Theorem 7.1).

## Results & Highlights

No quantitative results reported.

This is a pure theory paper: there are **no numerical experiments, no benchmarks, and no datasets**. All results are mathematical theorems with precise statements. The "evidence" is the following set of proven convergence guarantees:

- **Theorem 4.4 (zero-residual fixed points):** Any (R,Q)∈L²×H₀¹ satisfying the fixed-point condition `∫ R(x) S_Q(x,y) R(y) μ(dx)μ(dy)=0` is a global minimizer with `‖R‖_{L²}=0`.
- **Theorem 5.10 (limit PDE residual vanishes):** `lim_{t→∞} J(t) = lim_{t→∞} ‖R(t)‖²_{L²} = 0`; the infinite-width trained network reaches a global minimizer (zero PDE residual).
- **Theorem 5.11 (convergence to the PDE solution):** `lim_{t→∞} ‖Q(t) − u‖_{H¹} = 0`, where u is the unique weak solution of the original PDE.
- **Theorem 6.4 (finite-N→limit, a.s.):** `sup_{x∈Ω} |∂^{x_i} Q_N(t,x) − ∂^{x_i} Q(t,x)| → 0` a.s. for |i|≤2 on any [0,T], hence `‖Q_N(t) − Q(t)‖_{H²} → 0` a.s. as N→∞.
- **Theorem 7.1 (interchangeable limits / uniform-in-time bound):** For any ε>0 there exist t≥0 and N₀<∞ such that `E[J_s^N] ≤ ε` for all s≥t and N≥N₀; consequently `lim_{N→∞} lim_{s→∞} E[J_s^N] = 0` (and the reverse order also equals 0).

The class of PDEs covered is concrete: semi-linear elliptic equations `Lu + f(u, ∇u) = g` with Dirichlet boundary data, under the sufficient condition `f_v(v,w) ≥ 0` for the surjectivity assumption (Assumption 3).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine. The headline result — global convergence of DGM/PINNs for *nonlinear* PDEs — was an open problem; [2] explicitly could not handle nonlinearity because its fixed-eigenfunction decomposition of the constant kernel S(x,y) relies on linearity. The paper's core technical novelty is a time-dependent kernel `S_{Q(t)}` analysis with no spectral gap, solved via uniform H² a priori bounds + Barbalat's lemma rather than spectral projection.
- **Rigor:** High. The proof is complete and self-contained across 7 sections, with every lemma (monotonicity, uniform bounds, precompactness, fixed-point characterization, finite-N convergence, uniform-in-time error bound) fully derived. Assumptions are explicitly enumerated (Assumptions 1–6).
- **Reproducibility:** As a theorem paper, reproducibility means "can the proof be checked," which is yes; there is **no code, no experimental protocol, and no data** to reproduce numerically. A reader wanting empirical confirmation that finite networks actually converge in practice will find none here.
- **Significance:** Substantial for the SciML foundations community, since the "do PINNs get stuck in bad local minima?" question is a known practical concern. The result rigorously rules this out (in the over-parameterized, long-training limit) for the covered PDE class.
- **Scope restrictions (paper-specific, not generic caveats):** (i) Only **semi-linear** PDEs — the highest-order operator `L` is linear; fully nonlinear PDEs (e.g. Hamilton–Jacobi, fully nonlinear Monge–Ampère) are out of scope. (ii) The surjectivity Assumption 3 is a strong condition whose stated sufficient condition `f_v(v,w) ≥ 0` excludes many interesting nonlinearities. (iii) Only **homogeneous Dirichlet** boundary conditions `u=0`; Neumann/Robin/periodic and inhomogeneous data are not treated. (iv) Only **full-batch clipped gradient descent** is analyzed; the paper explicitly states the SGD/mesh-free DGM variant "is left for future research" — so the result as stated covers the PINN-style optimizer more directly than the stochastic DGM algorithm. (v) Results are **asymptotic** (N→∞ *and* t→∞); there are **no finite-width or finite-time convergence rates**, so the theorems give no guidance on how large N or how long t must be in practice. (vi) Ω must be compact with smooth boundary.
- **Red flags:** None. The authors are transparent about every limitation, do not overclaim (they carefully distinguish the clipped-GD result from SGD-DGM), and the clipping device is honestly described as a proof artifact that vanishes in the limit. The one mild caveat is that the necessity of clipping *for the proof* (versus plain GD) is not fully resolved — readers may wonder whether the convergence also holds without clipping in practice.

## Strengths

- Resolves a genuine, well-recognized open problem (nonlinear-PDE global convergence) that the authors' own prior linear result [2] could not address.
- The mathematical machinery is novel and arguably reusable: the combination of uniform H² a priori bounds + Barbalat's lemma + monotonicity to handle a non-self-adjoint, time-dependent, spectrum-less kernel is a template that could extend to other non-convex mean-field training dynamics.
- The uniform-in-time error bound (Theorem 7.1) and the resulting interchangeability of the N→∞ and t→∞ limits go beyond what generic mean-field analysis provides.
- Fixed-point analysis (Theorem 4.4) gives a clean, interpretable characterization: every stationary point of training with zero residual is automatically a global minimizer.
- Honest, well-organized exposition with explicit assumptions and a clear roadmap.

## Limitations

- **No empirical validation whatsoever.** Even within theory papers in this area, a small illustrative numerical experiment (e.g., showing training loss decaying on a semi-linear PDE) would strengthen the narrative and catch any gap between theory and practice; none is provided.
- **Narrow PDE class:** semi-linear, `f_v ≥ 0`, homogeneous Dirichlet, compact smooth domain. Many practically important PDEs (fully nonlinear, systems, inhomogeneous/Neumann boundary, unbounded domains) are not covered.
- **Optimizer mismatch:** the proven guarantee is for clipped *gradient descent*; the stochastic, mesh-free DGM algorithm that motivates the paper is explicitly out of scope.
- **Asymptotic only:** no convergence rates in N or t, no sample-complexity or width-budget guidance for practitioners.
- **Clipping dependence:** the proof requires the clipping function φ_N and the constraint `γ+β<1`; whether the result holds for unclipped GD is not established.

## Relevance

对关注科学机器学习（尤其是 PINNs/DGM 求解 PDE）及其优化理论基础的用户高度相关：它首次为非线性 PDE 上的 DGM/PINN 训练给出了全局收敛保证，直接回答了"训练是否会陷入非解的局部极小值"这一核心疑问。若你的兴趣偏经验/工程（如高维 PDE 的数值求解、收敛速率、SGD 变体），本文价值在于提供理论保障而非可直接落地的算法改进。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the global-convergence proof be extended to the **stochastic (SGD) DGM variant**, which is the practically important mesh-free algorithm for high-dimensional PDEs? The authors flag this as future work but the technical barriers (noise + non-convex limit) appear substantial.
- What are **finite-width and finite-time convergence rates**? The current theorems are purely asymptotic; quantitative rates in N and t would be the natural next step and would give practitioners width/training-time budgets.
- Does the result survive **weaker boundary conditions** (inhomogeneous Dirichlet, Neumann, Robin, periodic) and **unbounded domains** common in financial-mathematics PDEs?
- How far can the PDE class be pushed — e.g. to **fully nonlinear** equations or to **systems** of PDEs, where the highest-order term itself is nonlinear?
- Is clipping genuinely necessary for convergence, or is it purely a proof device? A proof (or empirical demonstration) for **unclipped gradient descent** would remove the main technical caveat.
- Can the time-dependent-kernel + Barbalat's-lemma template be reused to prove global convergence for other **non-convex mean-field training dynamics** (e.g., training of neural networks for optimal control or mean-field games)?
