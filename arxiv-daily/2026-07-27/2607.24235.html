01Key metric — minimax rates
MMD n−1/2+m−1/2 two-sample
HSIC n−1/2 independence
KSD n−1/2 goodness-of-fit

Each lower bound matches the known convergence rate of the standard V-/U-statistic estimators — so none of them can be improved in rate.

02Why this matters

Settles the optimality of the three most-used kernel discrepancies on graphs, sequences, manifolds & Hilbert spaces — the non-Euclidean domains where they are actually deployed.

03Critical edge

One of three headline theorems (KSD) is recalled, not new; and the headline unbounded-kernel generality is partly assumed — existence of the adversarial perturbation pair is only proven for bounded kernels on metric spaces.

Novelty is in generality, not method
A Methodology Le Cam, two-point method

A single modular recipe — Le Cam's two-point method (1973) in Tsybakov's framework — is applied uniformly to all five quantities. No problem-specific distributions are used.

  1. Build an adversarial pair

    From a base measure P₀ and an abstract perturbation φ ∈ C_b(X) with 𝔼[φ]=0, define P(n)(A)=∫_A(1+ε_n φ)dP₀ with ε_n = c·n−1/2.

  2. Separate the target

    By linearity of the mean embedding + the characteristic property, the discrepancy splits as C_φ·ε_n = Θ(n−1/2) between the two hypotheses.

  3. Control the KL divergence

    A Pinsker-type bound gives KL ≤ n·ε_n²·α₀ = α < ∞, independent of n. Both Le Cam conditions met ⇒ lower bound Θ(n−1/2).

Key technical device. The novelty is using general bounded-continuous perturbations φ ∈ C_b(X) instead of concrete laws (e.g. Gaussians). That abstraction is exactly what lifts the bound off ℝd. Lemma 2 guarantees such φ exist whenever X is a separable metric space with a bounded continuous kernel and a non-constant RKHS element.
B Results — what each bound relaxes pure theory · no experiments

This paper reports no empirical results — only asymptotic rate lower bounds and their match with known upper bounds. The table maps each result to the prior restriction it removes.

+ Strengths

  • Kills two restrictions at once — the Euclidean (ℝd) limit and the bounded/translation-invariant kernel limit — unifying four prior lower-bound lines.
  • One recipe, five results — the Le Cam + abstract-perturbation strategy applies cleanly to MMD, HSIC, KSD, mean embedding and cross-covariance, with a dependency graph (Fig. 1).
  • Honest remarks (§5, §9, §12) spell out exactly what each theorem relaxes versus prior art.
  • Checkable existence — Lemma 2 turns the abstract perturbation assumption into concrete conditions on metric spaces.
  • Free corollaries for mean embedding and the (cross-)covariance operator drop out of the main theorems.

— Limitations

  • Purely asymptotic — only lim inf rates; no finite-sample constants or non-asymptotic bounds, so no direct finite-n guidance.
  • KSD theorem is recalled, not new — the genuinely novel content is MMD + HSIC + their corollaries; the abstract slightly over-credits the KSD front.
  • Unbounded-kernel generality is partly assumed — existence of the perturbation pair is proven only for bounded kernels on separable metric spaces; the leap to Hilbert-space/subexponential settings is asserted, not spelled out case-by-case.
  • No empirical sanity check of the rate (acceptable for theory, but a numerical illustration would help).
  • Rate-optimal only — says nothing about constant-optimality or higher-order terms of existing estimators.

Open questions this raises

Q1Can the abstract perturbation-existence assumption be replaced by a checkable condition for the unbounded-kernel regimes the intro emphasizes (Hilbert-valued data under subexponentiality, point processes, graphs)?
Q2Are the bounds tight in constants, not just rate — can the leading constants B of V-/U-statistic estimators be improved, or do the lower-bound constants match them?
Q3Do non-asymptotic (finite-n) lower bounds of the same flavor hold, perhaps via Fano's or Bretagnolle–Huber's inequality instead of Le Cam?
Q4Does the recipe extend to incomplete / accelerated / randomized kernel-discrepancy estimators, whose compute-vs-statistics trade-off is practically important?
Q5Does the framework cover kernelized divergences beyond MMD/HSIC/KSD — kernel mutual information, OT-regularized IPMs — where matching rates are unsettled?
Q6For KSD, is the double supremum over P₀∈T (Stein kernel depends on target) the "right" minimax notion for goodness-of-fit, and what does it imply for test power?
