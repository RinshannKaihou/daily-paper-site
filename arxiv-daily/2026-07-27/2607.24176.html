§ 01

Methodology — a diagnostic protocol, not a new model

The system under test

inputGPT-2 · len 64
codecVQ-VAE-2
latentcodes · len 16
generatorMDLM

A hierarchical VQ-VAE-2 compresses 64 tokens through a 64→32→16 temporal hierarchy (codebook 512 at both levels). An MDLM with SUBS parameterization then generates the 16 top-level codes via absorbing-state discrete diffusion.

The staged validation protocol

  1. Stage 1 — Paired codec reconstruction. Isolate what is lost before any latent generation.
  2. Stage 2 — One shared scorer. AR vs token-space MDLM vs code-space MDLM, all under identical external GPT-2 PPL.
  3. Stage 3 — Auxiliary latent diagnostics. Codebook usage, support size, marginal DKL — read only after Stages 1–2.

§ 03

Strengths & limitations

Strengths

  • Honest diagnostic framing — staged protocol that separates codec loss from generator loss under one scorer.
  • Clean negative result for geometry regularization — improves proxies, no text gain. More useful than many shallow positives.
  • Metric disambiguation (Table IX) — end-text quality, tail/reliability, collapse coverage, and local geometry kept distinct.
  • Tail statistics (p95, max) emphasized over averages — correctly flags catastrophic reconstructions.
  • Reproducible artifacts — checkpoints, paired reconstructions, reporting checklist, failure taxonomy, decision matrix.

Limitations

  • Narrow external validity — one dataset (TinyStories), one compression regime (64→16), one codec, one generator.
  • Single-run, no multi-seed significance — small geometry-induced text degradations could be noise; authors call evidence "descriptive."
  • Weak baseline — token-space MDLM is itself a low bar; no comparison to stronger non-AR generators (SSD-LM, ratio-based diffusion).
  • Codec remediation unproven — diagnoses the codec as the bottleneck but doesn't show that fixing it closes the gap.
  • TinyStories is synthetic & simple — may not transfer to factual, syntactically rich, or longer-form text.
  • No human evaluation — LLM-judge and MAUVE are proxies; failure taxonomy unvalidated against human judgment.
§ 04

Open questions

  1. Does "codec is the bottleneck" survive milder compression (64→32 or 64→48), or does the latent generator become the bottleneck as reconstruction fidelity improves?
  2. Would a decoder-aware codec objective — explicitly optimizing paired reconstruction PPL — close the gap to the AR baseline?
  3. Do these conclusions transfer to richer domains (news, dialogue, code) where small reconstruction errors flip factual content?
  4. Is the geometry-regularizer negative result stable across seeds, or could a multi-seed sweep find a configuration where latent-proxy gains transfer to decoded text?
  5. How does code-space MDLM compare to stronger non-AR token baselines (SSD-LM, ratio-based discrete diffusion), not just vanilla token-space MDLM?
  6. Can the staged promotion gates be formalized into an automated checkpoint-selection procedure that rejects codec variants before expensive generator retraining?
