> **一句话总结：** 本文证明在过参数化线性回归中，协方差矩阵的秩亏损（零模式）与样本间依赖性本身就会导致风险曲线出现多次下降（multiple descent），并给出了一个基于二分图匹配与 Dulmage-Mendelsohn 分解的组合规则，精确确定每个方差峰出现的位置。

> **TL;DR:** This paper proves that multiple descent in over-parameterized ridge/ridgeless linear regression can arise purely from the *structure of a single design* — specifically the zero pattern of the variance profile of rank-deficient or dependent Gaussian covariates — without any engineered architecture or feature blocks. Both heterogeneous designs (Model 1, per-observation covariances sharing an eigenbasis) and finite-rank dependent designs (Model 2, e.g. data augmentation) reduce to a common variance-profile model, and the prediction risk's bias support and variance-peak locations are read off the Dulmage-Mendelsohn decomposition of the associated bipartite graph (Theorems 9 and 14). On the two-group latent-subspace benchmark the theory predicts peaks at p/n = 1/(2·0.45) = 10/9 and p/n = 1/(2(ρ+0.10)), merging at ρ = 0.35, exactly matching simulation; in the positive-definite control only the single classical peak at p/n = 1 survives; and a real WMT19 English-German embedding design augmented with 256 Gaussian columns exhibits double descent empirically.

## 核心贡献

- **放松了两个长期假设**：以往过参数化回归理论几乎都假设协变量独立且协方差矩阵非退化（正定、良态）。本文同时放宽这两个假设，允许协方差矩阵是半正定（秩亏损）的，且允许样本间存在有限秩依赖。
- **给出 multiple descent 的内在机制**：证明单一的、未经特殊设计的异质或依赖设计本身就足以产生多个风险峰——峰的来源是协方差的零模式（spectral degeneracy），而非人为拼接的特征块或多尺度核。
- **提出确定峰位置的组合规则**：将方差图（variance graph G_S）的二部匹配与 Dulmage-Mendelsohn 分解与风险的硬边行为对应起来——偏置支撑集 J_S 由未被某些最大匹配匹配的列决定（Theorem 9），方差峰出现在残余设计的列侧强 Hall 性质失效处（switching condition，Theorem 14）。
- **统一两种设计为一个方差轮廓模型**：异质设计（Model 1）与有限秩依赖设计（Model 2）都可旋转归约为带独立项、不等方差的方差轮廓矩阵 S（Model 3）；S 的零模式即承载全部 multiple descent 现象。
- **给出 vanishing-ridge 极限下的确定性等价量**（Theorem 3）：风险由每个坐标的两个量决定——硬边原子质量 r_l(0)（决定偏置）与导数 ∂r_l(0)（决定方差是否发散）。
- **证明正定情形不产生额外峰**：当所有协方差严格正定（即使不交换、各向异性），猜想且经仿真支持，风险曲线仅在 γ = p/n = 1 处出现单一经典峰（Section 5）。
- **方法学贡献**：将 [4] 的 local law 重新推导，显式追踪谱参数依赖，使其在 vanishing ridge λ_n ↓ 0 下仍成立（Appendix C），这是独立于本文的随机矩阵工具。
- **数据增强作为 Model 2 的自然实例**：将常见的数据增广（块内随机置换）识别为 M = 2 的有限秩依赖，并预测其会引入额外峰（实验确认）。

## Key Contributions

- **Relaxes two standing assumptions** of the over-parameterized regression literature simultaneously: covariates need not be independent, and covariance matrices need not be non-degenerate (positive definite). The paper permits positive semi-definite, rank-deficient covariances and finite-rank inter-observation dependence.
- **Identifies an intrinsic mechanism for multiple descent**: a single heterogeneous or dependent design, with nothing tuned to place peaks, already yields several risk peaks. The driver is the zero pattern (spectral degeneracy) of the covariance, not engineered feature blocks or multiple kernel scales.
- **Provides an exact combinatorial rule for peak locations**: the bias support is the degenerate column set J_S (columns left unmatched by some maximum matching, Theorem 9); variance peaks occur exactly when the residual design fails the column-side strong Hall property (switching condition, Theorem 14). Both are read off the Dulmage-Mendelsohn decomposition of the variance graph.
- **Unifies two practical designs under one variance-profile model (Model 3)**: heterogeneous (Model 1) and finite-rank-dependent (Model 2) Gaussian designs both reduce, after rotation to a shared eigenbasis, to a matrix with independent entries and variance profile S whose zero pattern governs everything (Lemmas 17, 18; Corollary 4).
- **Derives deterministic equivalents for vanishing-ridge risk (Theorem 3)**: bias and variance converge a.s. to explicit functions of a fixed-point vector r(λ); the whole phenomenon is captured by two per-coordinate numbers, r_l(0) (bias mass at the hard edge) and ∂r_l(0) (rate of eigenvalue accumulation near zero).
- **Separates the degenerate from the positive-definite regime**: conjectures (with supporting simulation) that positive-definite covariances — even non-commuting, arbitrarily anisotropic ones — produce only the single classical peak at γ = 1, isolating "rank deficiency / zeros" as the genuine cause of extra peaks (Section 5).
- **Methodological by-product**: extends the local law of Alt-Erdős-Krüger [4] with explicit spectral-parameter tracking down to λ_n ↓ 0, an independent random-matrix tool required to reach the hard edge under a vanishing ridge (Appendix C).
- **Connects to ML practice**: data augmentation is shown to be an instance of the dependent Model 2 (M = 2 separable components), predicting extra peaks that are confirmed experimentally; a real WMT19 transformer embedding design augmented with Gaussian columns reproduces multiple descent.

## Methodology

**Problem setup.** Observations follow a linear model y_i = X_i^⊤ β + ε_i with ε_i i.i.d. noise. The signal β is estimated by ridge least squares β̂_λ = (Σ_{i≤n} X_i X_i^⊤/n + λ I_p)^{-1} (Σ_{i≤n} X_i y_i / n) with a *vanishing* penalty λ = λ_n ↓ 0, a computable proxy for the minimum-ℓ₂-norm (ridgeless) interpolator and exactly what numerical implementations solve. The regime is proportional, p/n → γ ∈ (0, ∞). Because observations are heterogeneous, the test point X_new is drawn from the mixture of training marginals (Eq. 1), generalizing the standard homogeneous test mechanism. The reducible prediction risk R_λ(X) splits into bias R^B_λ and variance R^V_λ (Proposition 1), both governed by the smallest eigenvalues of the sample covariance W_n = X^⊤X/n — the *hard edge* of its spectrum.

**Two Gaussian designs reduced to one profile.**
- *Model 1 (heterogeneous):* rows X_i = Σ_i^{1/2} Z_i are independent but each carries its own positive semi-definite covariance Σ_i; the Σ_i share a common eigenbasis (simultaneously diagonalizable).
- *Model 2 (finite-rank dependence):* X = Σ_{m=1}^M Σ̃^{(m)1/2} Z^{(m)} Σ^{(m)1/2} with M fixed; full covariance of vec(X) is a sum of M separable terms. Data augmentation (block permutation) is an instance with M = 2.

After rotating to the shared eigenbasis, each design becomes a *variance-profile matrix* (Model 3): a Gaussian matrix X ∈ ℝ^{n×p} with independent entries and Var[X_{ij}] = n·S_{ij} for an explicit profile S built from covariance eigenvalues. A zero S_{ij} = 0 marks a direction observation i cannot see. The profile is the single object that all subsequent analysis depends on.

**Assumption 1 (flatness + connectivity).** S must be uniformly small (S_{ij} ≤ s*/(p+n)) and sufficiently connected (lower bounds on powers of SS^⊤ and S^⊤S), matching the standing condition of [4]. This makes the design behave as a single connected object; reduible profiles are handled blockwise, inheriting every peak of every block.

**The fixed-point system and deterministic equivalents (Theorem 3).** The risk is governed by a vector r(λ) = (r_l(λ))_{l≤p} solving 1 = r_l(λ) + Σ_i S_{il} r_l(λ) / (λ + Σ_j S_{ij} r_j(λ)). Under Assumption 1, with an admissible vanishing ridge λ_n ∈ (n^{-α}, λ_0) for α below a threshold α*, the bias and variance admit almost-sure deterministic equivalents:
- Bias: R^B_{λ_n}(X;β) − Σ_l β_l² e_l^⊤ A(λ_n)^{-1} Σ̄ r(λ_n) → 0 a.s.
- Variance: R^V_{λ_n}(X) − (σ_ε²/n) Σ_l Σ̄_{ll} ∂r_l(λ_n) → 0 a.s.

Each r_l admits a spectral representation r_l(λ) = ∫ λ/(t²+λ) ν_l(dt) (Lemma 2), exposing r_l(0) = ν_l({0}) (hard-edge atom, drives bias) and ∂r_l(0) = ∫_{t≠0} t^{-2} ν_l(dt) (eigenvalue accumulation rate, drives variance divergence).

**The combinatorial rule (Section 4).** Build the variance graph G_S: a bipartite graph joining observation i to coordinate j iff S_{ij} > 0.
- *Bias support (Theorem 9):* the degenerate column set J_S — the columns left unmatched by some maximum matching of G_S (the column part of the Dulmage-Mendelsohn decomposition) — equals {l : r_l(0) > 0}. Bias persists exactly on these coordinates. J_S is characterized via the row-side strong Hall property (Lemma 8).
- *Variance peaks (Theorem 14):* delete J_S and its neighboring rows to get the residual profile S^res. The derivative ∂r(0) is finite iff the *switching condition fails*, equivalently iff S^res satisfies the column-side strong Hall property (Lemma 13). Each configuration where the switching condition holds is a candidate variance peak. Maximum matchings and the strong Hall condition thus fix both how many peaks there are and where they sit — purely from the support pattern of S.

**Positive-definite regime (Section 5).** When covariances are full-rank (Assumption 2: α_min ≤ λ_min(Σ_i) ≤ λ_max(Σ_i) ≤ α_max) and need not commute, the variance-profile reduction breaks down (no rotation diagonalizes all Σ_i), and positive definiteness removes the zero-pattern mechanism. The variance reduces (Lemma 15) to R̃^V_λ = (1/n)Tr[W_n(W_n+λ_n I_p)^{-2}], controlled by the global law of W_n. The conjecture: the peak stays at γ = 1 regardless of covariance orientation.

## Results & Highlights

The paper's experiments each probe one central claim with quantitative predictions against theory:

1. **Multiple descent with peak locations matching theory exactly (Section 6.1 / Figure 2).** A two-group latent-subspace design with dim A = 0.45p, dim C = 0.10p fixed and dim B = ρp swept produces, per Theorem 14, a stationary peak at **p/n = 1/(2·0.45) = 10/9 ≈ 1.11** and a moving peak at **p/n = 1/(2(ρ+0.10))**, which coincide and merge into a single peak at **p/n = 1/(0.55+ρ)** when **ρ = 0.35**. The empirical risk curves exhibit two well-separated peaks that merge exactly as the rule dictates (dashed vertical lines mark the predicted switching thresholds). The strongest baseline — isotropic single-threshold theory [Hastie et al. 2022] — would predict only the single peak at γ = 1.

2. **Positive-definite heterogeneity yields only the single classical peak (Section 6.2 / Figure 4).** Three full-rank groups (n = 150, split 50/50/50) with non-commuting covariances rotated by angles θ = 0, π/12, π/6 (eigenvalues 1 and 0.1, α_min = 0.1, α_max = 1) produce a **single interpolation peak at p/n = 1**, matching the isotropic baseline of Example 2 regardless of covariance orientation. The rotations change the ellipsoid orientation but neither the number nor the location of risk singularities — confirming that heterogeneity alone, without rank deficiency, does not create extra descents.

3. **Data augmentation (dependent Model 2) generates additional peaks (Section 6.3 / Figure 1).** With n = 100 training points, k = 5 augmented copies, and error averaged over 1000 held-out points, the **unaugmented design shows a single classical interpolation peak**, while **augmented designs with block size b = 2 and b = 3 each display additional peaks**. Refreshing the label with independent noise on every copy (rather than reusing the response as in oracle relabeling [Huang et al. 2026]) is what exposes the extra peak.

4. **Real text-embedding design exhibits multiple descent (Section 6.4 / Figure 5).** Using the WMT19 English-German transformer encoder embedding table (**42024 subword tokens in ℝ^1024**, from facebook/wmt19-en-de) augmented with **k = 256 independent Gaussian columns (p = 1280)** at a small fixed ridge, sweeping γ = p/n by subsampling rows: **the held-out test error rises and falls twice** — multiple descent observed in features taken directly from a trained model, matching the theory's prediction from a degenerate embedding block plus a non-degenerate Gaussian block.

5. **Non-Gaussian universality (Figure 3).** Replacing Gaussian entries with i.i.d. standardized Student-t_3 (finite variance, **infinite third moment**, strictly outside the bounded-moment hypothesis of [4]) reproduces the **same peak locations and merging behavior** as the Gaussian two-group experiment — consistent with Remark 1 that Theorem 3 needs only a matching variance profile with bounded moments, though t_3's violation does not break the phenomenon in simulation.

6. **Theory recovers the isotropic baseline as a sanity check (Example 2).** With S_{ij} = 1/n, the fixed-point system reduces to γr(λ)² + (1+λ−γ)r(λ) − λ = 0, recovering r(0) = (1−1/γ)₊ and the exact ridgeless formulas of [Hastie et al. 2022]: bias ∥β∥²(γ−1)/γ and variance σ_ε²/(γ−1) for γ > 1, both diverging at γ = 1.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: High and genuine. Prior multiple-descent works (Adlam & Pennington 2020; d'Ascoli et al. 2020; Liang et al. 2020; Chen et al. 2021 "design your own curve"; Mel & Ganguli 2021; Meng et al. 2024) place peaks *by design* — through architecture, appended feature blocks, multiple kernel scales, or constructed covariances. This paper's distinctive claim is that peaks arise from the *support pattern of a single design* and gives an exact rule for their count and location, which is a categorically different contribution.
- **Rigor**: Strong. Theorem 3 (deterministic equivalents) and Theorems 9/14 (bias support / variance divergence) are stated with explicit conditions and proved in appendices. The extension of the Alt-Erdős-Krüger local law with explicit spectral-parameter tracking down to λ_n ↓ 0 (Appendix C) is a non-trivial technical contribution of independent interest.
- **Reproducibility**: Moderate-to-good. Synthetic experiments are fully specified (subspace dimensions, group sizes, angles, n, k). The real experiment cites the exact public checkpoint (facebook/wmt19-en-de, 42024×1024). However, the ridge level in Section 6.4 is described only as "small fixed penalty" without its value, the number of Monte Carlo repetitions is unspecified in several figures, and no code repository is referenced.
- **Significance**: Conceptually important for the benign-overfitting / double-descent literature. It sharpens *when* and *why* multiple descent happens and connects statistical risk to classical matching theory (Hall's theorem, Dulmage-Mendelsohn), a clean cross-disciplinary bridge.
- **Red flags / honest concerns**:
  - The headline "multiple descent from the data alone" is established only for *simultaneously diagonalizable, rank-deficient* covariances — a restrictive structural assumption. The paper itself notes the reduction fails for non-commuting covariances (Section 5), so the mechanism is narrower than a casual reading suggests.
  - Results hold for a *vanishing* ridge λ_n ↓ 0, not the exact ridgeless interpolator (λ = 0). Reaching λ = 0 would require controlling the smallest nonzero singular value uniformly, which the authors explicitly leave open (Remark 2). This is a genuine gap between the theory and the "ridgeless" framing.
  - The positive-definite conjecture (Section 5) is supported only by simulation and an informal stability argument on system (9) of Louart & Couillet [2021]; it is explicitly flagged as "not a rigorous statement." Readers should not treat the single-peak claim for positive-definite non-commuting covariances as proven.
  - The real-data experiment (Section 6.4) augments a *fixed* embedding table with synthetic Gaussian columns and subsamples rows; it does not test on a natural downstream task or compare against an actual learned linear/ridge baseline on real labels. The "multiple descent" observed is in a synthetic regression on embedding features, which is suggestive but not a deployment-grade demonstration.
  - The vanishing-ridge admissibility condition λ_n ∈ (n^{-α}, λ_0) bounds how fast λ can approach zero, but the exact α* and its tightness are not discussed empirically.
  - Assumption 1 (flatness + connectivity) excludes block-diagonal/reducible profiles unless handled piecewise; the multiple-descent examples (two-group) are precisely decomposable, so the "connected" headline theory is validated only via the blockwise reduction.

## Strengths

- A clean, memorable thesis: **the zeros of the covariance, not its rotation or bulk spectrum, drive multiple descent**, with an exact combinatorial locator (Dulmage-Mendelsohn / strong Hall).
- Unified treatment of heterogeneous (Model 1) and dependent (Model 2) designs under one variance-profile reduction, with data augmentation identified as a concrete, practically relevant instance of the dependent model.
- Honest separation between the rank-deficient regime (multiple descent, proven) and the positive-definite regime (single peak, conjectured + simulated) — the paper does not overclaim the positive-definite case.
- Recovers the classical isotropic ridgeless formulas [Hastie et al. 2022] as a special case (Example 2), providing a built-in sanity check.
- Non-Gaussian universality probed directly with Student-t_3 entries (infinite third moment), showing robustness beyond the stated moment conditions.
- Methodological by-product (local law with explicit z-dependence, Appendix C) is independently useful for hard-edge random-matrix problems.

## Limitations

- The exact theory requires covariances to be **simultaneously diagonalizable** and **positive semi-definite** (rank-deficient allowed). Non-commuting or non-diagonalizable rank-deficient covariances are not covered, and the reduction machinery breaks down there.
- Proven only for a **vanishing ridge λ_n ↓ 0**, not the exact minimum-ℓ₂-norm interpolator at λ = 0; the gap is acknowledged but unresolved (Remark 2).
- The **positive-definite single-peak claim is a conjecture**, supported by simulation and a heuristic stability argument, not a theorem.
- The switching rule treats **exact zeros**; the behavior under small-but-nonzero (possibly n-dependent) eigenvalues remains open, which limits direct applicability to real data where degeneracy is approximate.
- Experiments are **synthetic or semi-synthetic** (synthetic latent subspaces, block-permutation augmentation, embedding table + Gaussian columns). There is no demonstration on a standard ML benchmark with real labels and learned predictors.
- Some experimental details are underspecified (the fixed ridge value in Section 6.4, exact Monte Carlo replication counts), and **no code is released**.
- Gaussianity is required for the reduction from Model 1/2 to the variance profile (Remark 1), even though Theorem 3 itself extends to independent entries with bounded moments; the t_3 experiment suggests universality but this is empirical, not proven.

## Relevance

对研究过参数化模型泛化、双下降/多次下降、以及随机矩阵理论在机器学习中应用的读者高度相关：它从数据结构的内在视角（而非模型架构）解释了 multiple descent 的成因，并为后续在真实深度学习场景中刻画风险曲线提供了可操作的组合判据。若关注 benign overfitting、数据增强理论或方差轮廓模型的硬边分析，本文给出了直接可用的理论框架与实证证据。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the simultaneous-diagonalizability requirement be dropped while preserving the variance-profile reduction — e.g. for covariances that approximately commute, or with a notion of approximate Dulmage-Mendelsohn structure?
- How robust is the peak-location rule when exact zeros are replaced by eigenvalues that vanish polynomially in n, as is typical for real embedding covariances?
- Can the analysis be pushed to the exact ridgeless limit (λ = 0) by uniformly controlling the smallest nonzero singular value of X, closing the gap noted in Remark 2?
- Does the single-peak conjecture for positive-definite non-commuting covariances (Section 5) admit a rigorous proof via a global law for non-i.i.d. columns tracked to the hard edge, extending Louart & Couillet [2021]?
- How does the switching condition interact with *learned* feature representations (e.g., features from later transformer layers, or after fine-tuning) where the effective rank evolves with training?
- Can the variance-profile + matching framework predict multiple descent in kernel regression or random-features models with heterogeneous/dependent input distributions?
- What is the effect of label noise structure (not just fresh i.i.d. noise per augmented copy) on the visibility and location of the augmentation-induced peaks?
