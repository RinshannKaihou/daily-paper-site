> **一句话总结：** 提出 D-Score，通过统计单次前向传播中某一隐藏层激活矩阵里有多少奇异方向的奇异值仍接近最大奇异值（相对数值秩），作为无外部检索、无多次生成、无外部验证器的幻觉检测信号，在 FAVA-Annotation 与 RAGTruth 两个基准上、三个开源模型中均超过 LLM-Check 的 Hidden Score。

> **TL;DR:** D-Score is a single-pass, single-layer spectral hallucination detector that counts how many singular directions of the hidden activation matrix stay within a tolerance factor τ of the leading singular value. On FAVA-Annotation and RAGTruth, across Llama-2-7B, Llama-3-8B, and Vicuna-7B, it beats the closest hidden-state baseline (LLM-Check's Hidden Score) on every metric, e.g. lifting AUROC on FAVA-Annotation Llama-3-8B from 57.10 to 66.98 (+9.88 points) and on RAGTruth Llama-3-8B from 54.95 to 61.53 — without any external verifier, retrieval, or multiple generations.

## 核心贡献

- 提出 **D-Score**：对固定模型和层的隐藏激活矩阵 $H^{(j)}(x;m)$，定义其相对数值秩——满足 $\sigma_1(H)/\sigma_i(H) \le \tau$（即奇异值不小于最大值的 $1/\tau$）的奇异方向个数，作为一个标量幻觉评分。
- 给出一种"模型内部幻觉"的操作性定义（Definition 2）：模型对某个不受证据支持的断言赋予较低内部真实度信念 $b_m(a|x) \le \beta$，从而把"可被内部状态识别的幻觉"与"模型本身也识别不出的幻觉"区分开。
- 提供几何论证（Proposition 1）：若冲突分量 $C$ 的奇异方向与相干分量 $S$ 正交且足够强，则引入 $q$ 个冲突方向会使 D-Score 至少增加 $q$。
- 计算高效：利用 $d \gg T$ 的特点，只需对 token 级 Gram 矩阵 $G=H^\top H$ 做部分特征分解（deflated power iteration），复杂度约为 $O(N_{it} K dT)$，无需完整 SVD。
- 在 FAVA-Annotation 与 RAGTruth 两个基准、三个开源模型（Llama-2-7B、Llama-3-8B、Vicuna-7B）上全面超过 Hidden Score 及困惑度/熵类基线。

## Key Contributions

- Introduces the **D-Score** $D_\tau^{(j)}(x;m)$: a relative numerical rank of a fixed model/layer's hidden activation matrix, counting singular directions whose singular values remain within a factor $\tau$ of the leading one, used directly as a scalar hallucination score.
- Provides an operational, **model-internal definition of hallucination** (Definition 2) based on unsupported claims to which the model assigns low internal truth belief — separating errors the model can internally represent from those it cannot.
- Gives a **geometric sufficiency argument** (Proposition 1): when a conflict component contributes $q$ sufficiently strong directions orthogonal to the coherent component, the D-Score rises by at least $q$.
- Delivers an **efficient computation** via the token-level Gram matrix and deflated power iteration (Algorithm 1), avoiding a full SVD of the $d \times T$ matrix.
- Demonstrates consistent gains over the closest hidden-state baseline (LLM-Check's Hidden Score) and perplexity/entropy baselines across two benchmarks and three open-weight models.

## Methodology

Given a fixed LLM $m$ with hidden dimension $d$ and a fixed layer $j$, the input text $x=(x_1,\dots,x_T)$ produces a sequence of hidden states. These are stacked column-wise into the **hidden activation matrix** $H^{(j)}(x;m) \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times T}$ (uncentered). Let $\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_r > 0$ be its non-zero singular values.

**D-Score definition.** For tolerance $\tau > 1$,
$$D_\tau^{(j)}(x;m) = \sum_{i=1}^{r} \mathbf{1}\{\sigma_1(H)/\sigma_i(H) \le \tau\} = \max\{n \le r : \sigma_1(H)/\sigma_n(H) \le \tau\}.$$
In words: it counts how many singular directions stay within a relative factor $\tau$ of the leading singular value. The score is scale-invariant ($D_\tau(cH)=D_\tau(H)$), basis-invariant under orthogonal transforms, bounded by $\tau^2 \cdot \mathrm{srank}(H)$, and computable from the eigenvalues of the Gram matrix $H^\top H$ via the relation $\lambda_i = \sigma_i^2$.

**Decision rule.** A calibrated decision level $\bar{D} \in \mathbb{N}$ yields the detector $\hat{Y}(x)=1$ iff $D_\tau(x) > \bar{D}$. AUROC uses the raw score; accuracy/F1 use the thresholded rule; TPR@5%FPR sweeps $\bar{D}$.

**Geometric rationale.** The paper defines a conceptual "truth belief" $b_m(a|x)$ for each atomic claim via an ideal calibrated probe over all internal states $Z_m(x)$, and calls a text a model-internal hallucination when some unsupported claim has $b_m(a|x) \le \beta$. Proposition 1 then shows that if an unsupported claim injects a conflict component $C$ orthogonal to the coherent component $S$ (with $\sigma_1(C) \le \sigma_1(S)$), and $q$ of $C$'s singular values exceed $\sigma_1(S)/\tau$, then $D_\tau(S+C) \ge D_\tau(S)+q$. The hypothesis is explicitly empirical (Hypothesis 1): large D-Score should monotonically associate with higher hallucination probability.

**Computation.** Because $d \gg T$ in transformers, the method forms/uses the $T \times T$ Gram matrix implicitly via $g(v)=H^\top(Hv)$ and runs deflated power iteration (Algorithm 1), stopping as soon as an estimated eigenvalue falls below $\theta = \lambda_1/\tau^2$, yielding cost $O(N_{it}KdT)$.

## Results & Highlights

- **FAVA-Annotation, Llama-3-8B (Table 1):** D-Score reaches **AUROC 66.98**, vs **57.10** for the closest baseline LLM-Check Hidden Score — a **+9.88 point** gap; accuracy 65.57 vs 57.78, TPR@5%FPR 17.96 vs 10.78, F1 68.64 vs 65.38.
- **FAVA-Annotation, Llama-2-7B (Table 1):** D-Score **AUROC 62.87** vs Hidden Score **58.44**; F1 68.95 vs 59.66; it also beats all uncertainty baselines (PPL Score 53.22, Window Entropy 56.90, Logit Entropy 53.80 AUROC).
- **FAVA-Annotation, Vicuna-7B (Table 1):** D-Score **AUROC 63.93** vs Hidden Score **58.22**.
- **RAGTruth summarization subset, Llama-3-8B (Table 2):** D-Score **AUROC 61.53** vs Hidden Score **54.95**; F1 67.60 vs 30.97 (Hidden Score collapses on this metric).
- **RAGTruth, Vicuna-7B (Table 2):** D-Score **AUROC 60.20** while Hidden Score drops to **51.93**, near random — D-Score retains performance where the baseline fails.
- **Model-capacity subset analysis (Table 3):** On FAVA hallucinated samples the model itself explicitly flags as hallucinated, D-Score improves to **AUROC 72.51 on Llama-3-8B** (vs Hidden Score 62.17), up from 66.98 on the full set — indicating a large share of residual error comes from cases the model cannot internally represent as false.
- **Robustness (Figure 1, Appendix C):** A broad region of $(\tau, \text{layer})$ configurations around the optimum on FAVA/Llama-3-8B achieves near-identical AUROC, so performance is not tied to a single fragile configuration.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine and refreshing. Rather than training a probing classifier (Azaria & Mitchell), aggregating eigenvalue summaries (INSIDE), or sampling multiple generations (SelfCheckGPT, semantic entropy), it reduces hallucination detection to a single, parameter-light spectral count — a relative numerical rank of one hidden matrix. The connection to stable rank / effective rank is clearly acknowledged and the distinction from intrinsic-dimension estimation is articulated.
- **Rigor:** The mathematical treatment is clean (scale/basis invariance, Gram-matrix reduction, a deflated power-iteration algorithm, an elementary Proposition 1 proof). The empirical protocol is standard (AUROC, Acc, TPR@5%FPR, F1) and the headline claim ("outperforms all baselines on every metric/model/dataset") is supported by Tables 1–2.
- **Reproducibility concerns — these are real weaknesses:**
  1. **Best-layer selection without a held-out protocol in the main tables.** The paper explicitly states results "use the best-performing layer for each model" and relegates calibration-set selection to future deployment. This is optimistic: layer/τ/$\bar{D}$ are effectively tuned on test data. Appendix C's heatmap (Figure 1) mitigates this for one model/dataset but is not a substitute for a proper train/test split across all settings.
  2. **Baseline coverage is thin and asymmetric.** Only LLM-Check's Hidden Score is reported on RAGTruth for Llama-3-8B and Vicuna-7B — the PPL/entropy baselines are dropped there because "the other methods did not report results on this benchmark," which is a weak justification for an asymmetry that flatters D-Score. Stronger, standard hallucination baselines (semantic entropy [Farquhar et al., Nature 2024], SelfCheckGPT consistency, probing classifiers) are entirely absent and are deferred to future work — a notable omission for a 2026 paper.
  3. **Model-internal subset analysis (Tables 3–4) uses the model's own self-assessment to filter the test set.** This is circular-adjacent: it removes exactly the samples where D-Score fails and reports inflated AUROC (72.51) as a positive result, framing a limitation (the model can't internally represent some errors) as evidence of method strength.
  4. **Magnitude of the absolute numbers is modest.** AUROC values of 58–67 on FAVA and 57–61 on RAGTruth are only mildly above chance (50) and below the levels typically reported by sampling-based methods; the gains are real but the detector is clearly a weak signal on its own, not a deployable standalone judge.
  5. **Unbounded score depends on sequence length.** Since $D_\tau(x) \le T$, longer texts mechanically allow higher scores; the authors flag this but provide no length-stratified evaluation to rule out length as a confound.
- **Significance:** Conceptually valuable as an interpretability-friendly, zero-extra-inference signal that could complement other detectors; not a replacement for retrieval- or consistency-based methods.

## Strengths

- Striking simplicity: one forward pass, one layer, no training, no retrieval, no multiple samples — ideal for latency-constrained or single-response settings.
- Clean, self-contained mathematical formalization with useful invariance properties and a concrete (if idealized) geometric mechanism.
- Consistent directional improvement over the most similar hidden-state method across all model/dataset/metric cells.
- Computationally clever: partial eigensolver on the small Gram matrix makes it cheap when the spectrum decays quickly.
- Honest framing of the score as a "calibrated risk signal" rather than a truth test, and an explicit acknowledgment of when it must fail.

## Limitations

- Requires **white-box access** to hidden activations — inapplicable to closed/API-only models.
- Main-table results use the **best layer per model** rather than a fixed calibration protocol.
- **Narrow baseline set**: no semantic entropy, no consistency-based, no probing-classifier, no retrieval comparisons; entropy baselines missing entirely on RAGTruth.
- **Modest absolute performance** (AUROC mostly in the high-50s to mid-60s) limits standalone deployability.
- **Length dependence**: $D_\tau(x) \le T$ means sequence length can shift the score distribution; no length-stratified analysis provided.
- The orthogonality assumption in Proposition 1 is idealized; real conflict subspaces need not be orthogonal.
- Only English benchmarks and three 7–8B models; generalization to larger or non-English models untested.

## Relevance

与本目录关注的"LLM 可靠性 / 幻觉检测 / 机制可解释性"方向高度契合：它从隐藏状态的几何（奇异谱）出发，给出一个无需外部检索、无需多次采样的实时幻觉信号，可直接用于构建低成本、单次前向的幻觉预警，也可与现有的检索校验、一致性采样类方法互补，适合作为幻觉检测管线中的一级低成本过滤器。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How does D-Score compare to semantic entropy and SelfCheckGPT under an identical, properly calibrated protocol — and at what compute budget does D-Score's single-pass advantage vanish?
- Does the score's apparent advantage survive fixing the layer/τ/$\bar{D}$ on a held-out set and reporting on a disjoint test split (i.e., removing best-layer tuning)?
- Is the signal partly an artifact of sequence length / text complexity? A length- and complexity-stratified evaluation would clarify whether D-Score tracks hallucination or verbosity.
- Can a span-level or token-local variant localize *where* in the response the conflict signal appears, turning a document-level risk score into a localized highlighter?
- Does the method transfer to larger (e.g., 70B) or smaller (e.g., 1–3B) models, and to non-English settings where FAVA/RAGTruth annotations exist?
- Can the conflict-direction argument be validated empirically — e.g., by recovering the putative "conflict subspace" and checking whether it correlates with annotated unsupported claims?
