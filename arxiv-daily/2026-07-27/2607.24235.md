> **一句话总结：** 本文利用 Le Cam 两点法配合精心构造的扰动测度，在一般拓扑空间（而非仅 R^d）上、对无界核证明了 MMD、HSIC、KSD 估计的极小化极大下界均为 n^{-1/2}（MMD 为 n^{-1/2}+m^{-1/2}），并与现有估计器的上界匹配，从而确立了这些核差异量估计的最优性。

> **TL;DR:** This theoretical paper proves that the minimax lower bound for estimating the three most popular kernel discrepancies — MMD (n^{-1/2}+m^{-1/2}), HSIC (n^{-1/2}), and KSD (n^{-1/2}) — holds on *general topological spaces* with *unbounded* kernels under mild assumptions, matching the known convergence rates of existing V-/U-statistic estimators and thereby settling their minimax optimality. Prior lower bounds (Tolstikhin et al. 2016; Kalinke & Szabó 2024; Chamakh & Szabó 2024) were restricted to X=R^d with bounded/radial/translation-invariant kernels; this work removes both the Euclidean and the bounded-kernel restrictions, extending the n^{-1/2} rate lower bound to settings such as sequences, graphs, manifolds, and Hilbert spaces.

## 核心贡献

- **MMD 极小化极大下界（Theorem 3）：** 在一般拓扑空间上、对特征核（可能无界）证明 MMD 估计的下界为 n^{-1/2}+m^{-1/2}，与现有最快估计器（基于 U/V 统计量的估计器）的上界匹配，从而首次在欧氏空间之外确立其极小化最优性。
- **均值嵌入的下界（Corollary 4）：** 作为 MMD 结果的直接推论，得到核均值嵌入 µ_K 估计的下界为 n^{-1/2}（在 RKHS 范数下）。
- **HSIC 极小化极大下界（Theorem 7）：** 在乘积拓扑空间上、对 I-特征乘积核证明 HSIC 估计的下界为 n^{-1/2}，同样匹配现有估计器上界。
- **中心化交叉协方差算子的下界（Corollary 8）：** 作为 HSIC 结果的推论，得到 C_K 估计的下界为 n^{-1/2}（并附带覆盖中心化协方差算子估计）。
- **KSD 极小化极大下界（Theorem 11，回顾）：** 回顾并完整证明了作者先前工作（Cribeiro-Ramallo et al. 2026）关于 KSD 估计在一般拓扑空间上 n^{-1/2} 下界的结果。
- **核心技术贡献：** 关键难点在于为 Le Cam 两点法构造对抗性的扰动测度序列；作者用一般的连续有界扰动函数 φ∈C_b(X) 取代了以往工作中依赖的具体分布（如高斯），从而使结论适用于任意满足存在性条件的拓扑空间。

## Key Contributions

- **MMD minimax lower bound (Theorem 3):** Proves that any estimator of MMD_K on a general topological space, under a characteristic (possibly unbounded) kernel satisfying Assumption 1, has worst-case error at least B·(n^{-1/2}+m^{-1/2}); this matches the upper bound of existing U/V-statistic estimators and is the first such bound beyond R^d.
- **Mean embedding lower bound (Corollary 4):** Derives as a direct corollary an n^{-1/2} lower bound (in H_K-norm) for estimating the kernel mean embedding µ_K, generalizing Tolstikhin et al. (2017) which assumed continuous translation-invariant kernels on R^d.
- **HSIC minimax lower bound (Theorem 7):** Establishes an n^{-1/2} lower bound for HSIC estimation on product topological spaces with an I-characteristic product kernel (Assumption 6), matching existing estimators and relaxing the R^d + translation-invariant assumptions of Kalinke & Szabó (2024).
- **Centered cross-covariance operator lower bound (Corollary 8):** Yields an n^{-1/2} lower bound for C_K, and by the self-covariance specialization also covers the centered covariance operator, relaxing assumptions of Zhou et al. (2019) and Kalinke & Szabó (2024).
- **KSD minimax lower bound (Theorem 11, recalled):** Recalls (with a full proof) the authors' prior result (Cribeiro-Ramallo et al. 2026) giving an n^{-1/2} lower bound for KSD estimation on general topological spaces, relaxing the X=R^d and translation-invariant-base-kernel assumptions of Gogolashvili (2026) and the prior KSD-specific work.
- **Key technical device:** The central challenge — and the paper's main technical contribution — is the construction of an adversarial sequence of distributions for Le Cam's method using *general* bounded-continuous perturbation functions φ∈C_b(X) rather than problem-specific distributions; the topological assumptions are shown (Lemma 2) to guarantee existence of such perturbations.

## Methodology

The paper works within the classical minimax estimation framework (Tsybakov, 2009) and relies on **Le Cam's two-point method** (Le Cam, 1973). The overall proof recipe, applied separately to each discrepancy, has three ingredients:

1. **Adversarial pair construction via perturbations.** Given a base measure P_0 and a perturbation function φ∈C_b(X) with E_{P_0}[φ(X)]=0 and φ non-zero on a set of positive P_0-measure (existence guaranteed by Lemma 2 under mild conditions: separable metric space, K∈C_b(X^2), H_K containing a non-constant element), the authors define perturbed measures
   P^{(n)}(A) = ∫_A (1 + ε_n φ(x)) dP_0(x) with ε_n = c·n^{-1/2} (and analogously Q^{(m)} with ν_m = c·m^{-1/2} for MMD).
   The pair (P_0, P^{(n)}) [and (P_0, Q^{(m)})] serves as the two hypotheses.

2. **Separation of the target functional (condition 2 of Le Cam).** Using the linearity of the mean embedding in the measure and the characteristic property of the kernel, the authors show the target discrepancy separates as a constant C_φ > 0 times (ε_n + ν_m). Concretely:
   - MMD: MMD_K(P^{(n)}, Q^{(m)}) = (ε_n + ν_m)·C_φ = 2B·(n^{-1/2}+m^{-1/2}).
   - HSIC: HSIC_K(P^{(n)}) = ε_n·C_φ = 2B·n^{-1/2} (exploiting that HSIC_K(P_0)=0 because P_0 is a product of marginals).
   - KSD: KSD_{K_{P_0}}(P_0, P^{(n)}) = ε_n·‖E_{P_0}[φ(X)Ψ_{P_0}(X)]‖_H = Θ(n^{-1/2}), using the Stein identity E_{P_0}[Ψ_{P_0}(X)]=0.

3. **KL-divergence control (condition 1 of Le Cam).** Using a Taylor/Pinsker-type bound (Lemma B.6), the n-fold product KL divergence is bounded by KL((P^{(n)})^n ‖ P_0^n) ≤ n·ε_n^2·α_0 = c^2·α_0 =: α < ∞, independent of n. With both conditions satisfied, Le Cam's theorem (Theorem C.5) yields the lower bound inf_{F̂_n} sup_{P∈P} P^n(|F−F̂_n| ≥ s_n) ≥ f(α) > 0 with s_n = Θ(n^{-1/2}).

The crucial novelty is that **no specific distribution (e.g. Gaussian) is used** — only abstract perturbation functions whose existence follows from the topological/kernel assumptions. This is what allows the bounds to transfer from R^d to arbitrary topological spaces (sequences, graphs, manifolds, Hilbert spaces, etc.). The mean-embedding and cross-covariance-operator corollaries are obtained by reusing the same adversarial pairs but changing the parameter space and distance to the H_K-norm. Proofs of all theorems/corollaries are in Appendix A; auxiliary lemmas (B.1–B.9) and external results (Le Cam, KL tensorization, etc., C.1–C.5) are collected in Appendices B and C, with a dependency graph in Figure 1.

## Results & Highlights

This is a pure theory paper; it reports **no empirical/simulation results, no datasets, and no benchmark experiments**. All quantitative claims are theoretical rate lower bounds (and their match with known upper bounds), summarized below. The "baselines" are the prior minimax lower-bound results, which were restricted to R^d and stricter kernel classes.

1. **MMD lower bound (Theorem 3):** For any estimator F̂_{n,m} of MMD_K(P,Q) on a general topological space under Assumption 1 (characteristic kernel, possibly unbounded, plus existence of a non-trivial perturbation pair),
   lim inf_{n,m→∞} inf_{F̂} sup_{(P,Q)∈[P_1(K;X)]^2} (P^n⊗Q^m)(|MMD_K(P,Q)−F̂_{n,m}| ≥ B(m^{-1/2}+n^{-1/2})) > 0.
   The rate **n^{-1/2}+m^{-1/2} matches** the known upper bound of the standard MMD estimators (Gretton et al. 2012), establishing minimax optimality. The strongest prior lower bound (Tolstikhin et al. 2016; Chamakh & Szabó 2024) required **X=R^d** with continuous radial/translation-invariant kernels (or X=R for the exponential kernel) — this work removes both restrictions.

2. **HSIC lower bound (Theorem 7):** For any estimator F̂_n of HSIC_K(P) on a product topological space under Assumption 6 (I-characteristic product kernel),
   lim inf_{n→∞} inf_{F̂} sup_{P∈P_1(K;X)} P^n(|HSIC_K(P)−F̂_n| ≥ B·n^{-1/2}) > 0.
   The rate **n^{-1/2} matches** the convergence rate of HSIC's U/V-statistic estimators (Gretton et al. 2005; Pfister et al. 2018). The prior strongest result (Kalinke & Szabó 2024, Theorem 1) assumed **X=R^d** with continuous translation-invariant characteristic kernels.

3. **KSD lower bound (Theorem 11):** For any estimator of KSD_{K_{P_0}}(P_0,P) on a general topological space under Assumption 10,
   lim inf_{n→∞} inf_{F̂} sup_{P_0∈T} sup_{P∈P_1(K_{P_0};X)} P^n(|KSD_{K_{P_0}}(P_0,P)−F̂_n| ≥ B·n^{-1/2}) > 0.
   The rate **n^{-1/2} matches** the upper bound of KSD estimators (Liu et al. 2016; Chwialkowski et al. 2016). Prior bounds (Gogolashvili 2026; Cribeiro-Ramallo et al. 2026) assumed **X=R^d** (and translation-invariant base kernels in the latter).

4. **Mean embedding lower bound (Corollary 4):** n^{-1/2} lower bound in H_K-norm for estimating µ_K; relaxes Tolstikhin et al. (2017, Theorems 1 & 6) which assumed continuous translation-invariant kernels on R^d.

5. **Centered cross-covariance operator lower bound (Corollary 8):** n^{-1/2} lower bound for C_K (and, via the self-covariance specialization, for the centered covariance operator); relaxes the R^d + translation-invariant assumptions of Zhou et al. (2019) and Kalinke & Szabó (2024).

6. **Lemma 2 (sufficient conditions):** Assumption 1(ii) — the existence of a non-trivial perturbation pair (P_0, φ_0) — holds automatically whenever X is a **separable metric space**, K∈C_b(X^2), and H_K contains a non-constant element. This makes the main assumptions verifiable in practice.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty (genuine, but scoped):** The real novelty is the *generality* of the setting (arbitrary topological spaces + unbounded kernels) rather than the proof technique. The authors are candid that Le Cam's two-point method combined with perturbed measures "has found various successful applications" (citing Anderson et al. 1994, Sriperumbudur et al. 2010, Gretton et al. 2012, Balasubramanian et al. 2021, Hagrass et al. 2026). The technical contribution is the *construction of the adversarial perturbation sequence using abstract C_b(X) functions* instead of problem-specific distributions, which is what enables the lift off R^d. This is a meaningful but incremental advance over the authors' own 2024 work.

- **Rigor (high):** Proofs are given in full for all five new/recalled results (Appendix A), with a clean dependency graph (Figure 1) and all auxiliary lemmas (B.1–B.9) and external tools (C.1–C.5) explicitly stated. The use of Le Cam's theorem is standard and correctly applied. Assumptions are stated precisely and Lemma 2 provides concrete, checkable sufficient conditions (separable metric space, bounded continuous kernel, non-constant RKHS element).

- **Reproducibility (high for theory):** Being a pure-theory paper with complete proofs, the results are in principle verifiable by hand; there is no code, data, or experimental pipeline to reproduce.

- **Significance:** Settling minimax optimality of MMD/HSIC/KSD estimation beyond R^d is a genuinely useful theoretical contribution, since these quantities are heavily used on non-Euclidean domains (graphs, sequences, manifolds, point processes — all cited in the introduction). Matching lower and upper bounds means existing estimators cannot be improved in rate.

- **Red flags / honest concerns:**
  1. **One of three headline theorems is not new.** Theorem 11 (KSD) is explicitly recalled from the authors' own Cribeiro-Ramallo et al. (2026) — the paper states "This article expands the work of Cribeiro-Ramallo et al. (2026), which settled the minimax lower bound of KSD estimation... to MMD..., HSIC..., and centered cross-covariance operator." So the *new* content is MMD + mean embedding + HSIC + cross-covariance operator; KSD is included for completeness. The abstract's framing ("MMD, HSIC, KSD") slightly over-credits the paper's novelty on the KSD front.
  2. **The "mild assumptions" still hide content.** Assumptions 1(ii)/6(ii)/10(ii) require the existence of a pair (P_0, φ_0) with φ_0 not P_0-a.s. constant; Lemma 2 shows this holds for separable metric spaces with bounded kernels, but the unbounded-kernel regime (the paper's headline generalization) relies on the assumption holding without an explicit existence lemma covering all the unbounded cases the introduction advertises (sequences, Hilbert-space-valued data under subexponentiality, etc.). The bridge from "general topological space" to the specific celebrated unbounded-kernel applications is asserted rather than spelled out case-by-case.
  3. **No discussion of tightness of constants / finite-sample behavior** — only asymptotic rates. This is standard for minimax lower bounds but limits practical guidance.
  4. **Very short main body (~10 pages)** including a large notation section; the density of genuinely new ideas is modest relative to the breadth claimed in the title.

## Strengths

- Removes two long-standing restrictions simultaneously: the **Euclidean (R^d) restriction** and the **bounded-kernel / translation-invariant restriction**, unifying and generalizing four prior lines of lower-bound results (Tolstikhin et al. 2016, 2017; Chamakh & Szabó 2024; Kalinke & Szabó 2024; Gogolashvili 2026).
- Clean, modular proof strategy (Le Cam + abstract perturbations) that applies uniformly to MMD, HSIC, KSD, mean embedding, and cross-covariance operator, with a helpful dependency graph.
- Honest, detailed "Remark" sections (5, 9, 12) explicitly contrasting each new result with the prior state of the art and the precise assumptions each relaxed.
- The auxiliary existence result (Lemma 2) makes the otherwise-abstract perturbation assumption concretely checkable on metric spaces.
- Corollaries for the mean embedding and centered (cross-)covariance operator are obtained essentially for free from the main theorems, broadening impact.

## Limitations

- **Purely asymptotic.** All bounds are of the form lim inf_{n→∞}; no finite-sample constants or non-asymptotic lower bounds are provided, so the results give no direct finite-n guidance.
- **One headline result (KSD, Theorem 11) is recalled, not new** — the paper is an expansion of a prior work, and the genuinely novel theorems are MMD, HSIC, and their corollaries.
- **The unbounded-kernel generality is partly assumed, not constructed.** Existence of the required perturbation pairs is proven (Lemma 2) only for bounded kernels on separable metric spaces; the leap to the celebrated unbounded-kernel settings (e.g. Hilbert-space-valued data under subexponentiality, Kalinke et al. 2025b/2026) relies on the assumptions holding without an explicit covering lemma, leaving a small gap between the headline claim and what is rigorously justified.
- **No empirical illustration** of the bounds (acceptable for theory, but a numerical sanity check of the rate would strengthen the presentation).
- The lower bounds establish rate-optimality only; they say nothing about whether the *constants* of existing estimators are optimal, nor about higher-order terms.
- Constants B and α in the bounds are non-constructive existence constants derived from Le Cam's lemma; their values are not optimized.

## Relevance

对从事核方法、分布比较（两样本/独立性/拟合优度检验）以及统计学习理论研究的工作者高度相关：本文从理论上确认了在非欧氏空间（图、序列、流形、Hilbert 空间等）上 MMD/HSIC/KSD 现有估计器已达到 n^{-1/2} 的最优收敛速率，为这些工具在机器学习各领域的应用提供了理论最优性保证。若你的兴趣集中在应用经验方法或工程实现，则本文主要是理论参考而非可直接复现的工作。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the abstract perturbation-existence assumption (Assumption 1(ii)/6(ii)/10(ii)) be replaced by an explicit, checkable sufficient condition for the **unbounded**-kernel regimes the introduction emphasizes (Hilbert-space-valued data under subexponentiality, point processes, graph-valued data), closing the gap between the headline generality and Lemma 2's bounded-kernel coverage?
- Are the rate lower bounds tight in **constants** as well as in rate — i.e., can the leading constants B of the V-/U-statistic estimators be improved, or do the lower-bound constants match them?
- Do non-asymptotic (finite-n) lower bounds of the same flavor hold, perhaps via Fano's or Bretagnolle–Huber's inequality instead of Le Cam's two-point method?
- Can the same perturbation-plus-Le-Cam recipe establish minimax lower bounds for **incomplete / accelerated / randomized** kernel-discrepancy estimators (Chatalic et al. 2022; Kalinke & Szabó 2023; Kalinke et al. 2025b), whose computation-vs-statistics trade-off is of practical interest?
- Does the framework extend to **kernelized divergences beyond MMD/HSIC/KSD**, such as the kernelized mutual information or optimal-transport-regularized IPMs, where matching upper/lower rates are less settled?
- The KSD result (Theorem 11) carries a **double supremum** over P_0∈T because the Stein kernel itself depends on the target — is this minimax formulation the "right" notion of optimality for goodness-of-fit testing, and does it imply anything about the test power of KSD-based tests?
