01

Methodology

A mixed-method position paper: conceptual analysis, comparative benchmarking against safety-critical fields (aviation 10⁻⁹/flight-hour design-assurance, nuclear defense-in-depth, clinical-trial preregistration), and two thin but honest empirical studies.

Two-axis asymmetry CORE

Capability profile (proving absence vs. presence of capability) × risk profile (worst-case tail-bounding vs. average-case optimization), kept analytically independent to avoid circular reasoning. Five diagnostic gap dimensions cross-cut the axes.

Bibliometric baseline EMPIRICAL

Preregistered on OSF (10.17605/OSF.IO/45YR6). Target n≥30 stratified sample; five binary markers E1–E5 mapped to gap dimensions. Only a single-coded n=10 pilot fragment is reported — full study in progress.

Rubric audit EMPIRICAL

Eight influential papers (InstructGPT, GPT-4 Tech Report, Constitutional AI, Christiano 2017, Perez 2022, Elhage 2021, Madry 2018, Shannon 2020) scored against the Appendix-A codebook. Second coder covered 3 papers / 24 cells for inter-rater reliability.

Mechanism design NORMATIVE

The disclosure ladder, tiered applicability, and infohazard adjudication are normative designs, not empirically validated instruments. Anti-gaming bundle engineered explicitly against two baselines: no-checklist and checklist-only.

03

Strengths & Limitations

Strengths

  • Brutally honest about limits. Labels pilot status, discloses in-house coding as access asymmetry, names construct-validity gaps, refuses to claim validation beyond instrument feasibility.
  • Two-axis asymmetry framework avoids circular reasoning by keeping capability and risk profiles analytically distinct.
  • Disclosure ladder + infohazard adjudication is a concrete, contestable artifact — turns "infohazard exception" into an auditable decision, not unattributable discretion.
  • Anti-gaming bundle designed against two explicit baselines and engages Goodhart's law seriously rather than ignoring it.
  • Adoption dynamics reframes unilateral adoption as coordination failure with three concrete institutional paths (venue, funder, regulatory).

Limitations

  • All empirical support is preliminary. Bibliometric baseline is single-coded n=10, not the registered n≥30; κ rests on only 24 cells / 3 papers.
  • No causal link between higher ECAISA scores and downstream outcomes (replication, correction, challenge) — deferred to Stages 3–4, not yet run.
  • Non-random, older sample (InstructGPT, GPT-4 Tech Report); near-zero P2/P4/P7 may reflect old norms, not current frontier practice.
  • Compliance overhead could entrench well-resourced incumbents; equity-as-infrastructure is aspirational, not operationalized.
  • Anti-gaming mechanisms not stress-tested against well-resourced actors producing convincing-but-hollow compliance.
  • Single author concentrates framing and coding; external Stage 2 coding acknowledged but not done.
04

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Q1 When the registered n≥30 double-coded bibliometric study completes, does the E3=0% finding hold — and how wide are the Wilson CIs? Does the case for P4 strengthen or weaken?
  2. Q2 Does higher ECAISA rubric score actually predict downstream indicators (replication success, post-publication error correction, successful external challenge) in a matched comparative study (Stage 3)?
  3. Q3 How do well-resourced frontier labs respond to lottery-based independent verification and spot-audits in practice — can the seven anti-gaming mechanisms survive determined hollow-compliance investment?
  4. Q4 Is κ=0.79 stable when coders come from different disciplinary backgrounds (Stage 1/2), or is the apparent reliability an artifact of a homogeneous author group?
  5. Q5 How should ECAISA's tiered applicability (T0–T3) interact with fast-moving capability evaluations (dangerous-capability evals, STREAM, FMTI) to avoid double-counting or gaps at the research↔deployment boundary?
  6. Q6 Can the disclosure ladder's L2 "vetted third party" access be operationalized without creating new gatekeeping power and monoculture risk?
