Global Convergence of DGM and PINN Algorithms for Solving Nonlinear PDEs

Justin Sirignano (Oxford) · Konstantinos Spiliopoulos (Boston Univ.) · Samuel Cohen (Oxford)

1 Key Metric
limN&arr;  limt&arr;  E[ JsN ] = 0

The two limits — width N&arr;∞ and training time t&arr;∞ — are interchangeable: expected PDE-residual loss vanishes in either order (Thm 7.1). Asymptotic only — no finite-N / finite-t rates given.

2 Why This Matters

For an over-parameterized PINN, training provably cannot get stuck in a non-solution basin — at least for the covered semi-linear class. It puts the practical “bad-local-minima” worry to rest on theoretical footing.

3 Critical Edge

Scope is tight. Proven only for clipped full-batch GD on semi-linear PDEs with fv≥0 and homogeneous Dirichlet data — the stochastic, mesh-free DGM that motivates the paper is explicitly out of scope.

01

Methodology

Mean-field / NTK analysis of a two-layer network trained on the PDE residual.

PDE class

Semi-linear elliptic Au = g on compact Ω⊂Rn with smooth boundary, u=0 on ∂Ω. Here Au = Lu + f(u, ux), L uniformly elliptic & dissipative (inf eigenvalue > 0); f and its first two derivatives uniformly bounded; unique weak solution in H01.

Network ansatz

Two-layer QN(x;θ) = η(x) N−β Σi ci σ(wi·x + bi), with 1/2 < β < 1. η vanishes on ∂Ω with positive normal derivatives (enforces the boundary condition); σ bounded/discriminatory (sigmoid, tanh) with ≥3 bounded derivatives; i.i.d. mean-zero init.

Objective & training

PDE-residual loss JN(θ) = ½ ∫Ω (AQNQN − g)2 μ(dx) minimized by clipped gradient descent dθ/dt = −αN GN(θ(t)), learning rate αN = αN2β−1. The clip φN is identity on [−Nγ, Nγ], bounded by 2Nγ, with γ+β<1. Clipping is a proof device whose effect vanishes as N&arr;∞.

Mean-field / NTK limit

As N&arr;∞ the trajectory satisfies a non-local nonlinear PDE: ∂tQ(t,x) = −α ∫Ω UQ(t)(x,y) [AQ(t)Q(t,y) − g(y)] μ(dy), Q(0,x)=0. The residual is driven by a time-dependent, solution-dependent kernel SQ(t)(x,y) with drifting spectrum, no spectral gap, and possible zero eigenvalues.

Global-convergence pipeline

Six stages, from monotonicity to interchangeable limits.
i · MONOTONICITY
dJ/dt ≤ 0
Plus uniform L² bounds on the residual R(t). Loss can only fall.
ii · WELL-POSEDNESS
Limit solution in C([0,∞); H²∩H01)
Banach fixed-point; uniform H² bound from coercivity of L.
iii · COMPACTNESS
Precompactness in H¹ / L²
Rellich–Kondrachov; weak precompactness of R(t) in L².
iv · BARBALAT
⟨R, SQR⟩ → 0 ⇒ J(t)→0
Theorem 5.10; then Q(t)→u in H¹ (Theorem 5.11).
v · FINITE N
‖QN(t)−Q(t)‖H² →0 a.s.
Gronwall + SLLN, on any [0,T] (Theorem 6.4).
vi · INTERCHANGE
E[JsN] ≤ ε uniformly
For all s≥t, N≥N₀ (Theorem 7.1) — the headline result.

04

Strengths & Limitations

Side-by-side verdict.

PRO Strengths

  • Resolves a genuine open problem the authors' own prior linear-PDE result [2] could not touch.
  • Reusable machinery: uniform H² bounds + Barbalat's lemma + monotonicity tames a non-self-adjoint, time-dependent, gap-less kernel.
  • Uniform-in-time bound (Thm 7.1) gives interchangeable limits — beyond standard mean-field results.
  • Clean fixed-point story (Thm 4.4): every zero-residual stationary point is automatically a global minimizer.
  • Honest exposition — explicit assumptions, clipping honestly flagged as a proof artifact.

CON Limitations

  • No empirical validation at all — no illustrative experiment, no training curves.
  • Narrow PDE class: semi-linear, fv≥0, homogeneous Dirichlet, compact smooth domain.
  • Optimizer mismatch: guarantee is for clipped GD; stochastic mesh-free DGM is out of scope.
  • Asymptotic only — no convergence rates in N or t, no width budget for practitioners.
  • Clipping dependence: proof needs φN with γ+β<1; unclipped GD not established.
