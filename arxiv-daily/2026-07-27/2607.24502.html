§ 01 Methodology

The continuous-time limit of a normalized residual attention layer with identity Q=K=V, except block rotations Rp on queries and keys, evolves on (Sd−1)n as ẋi = Σj Aij(x) Pxi⊥ xj.

Orthogonality of the block rotations makes the weight kernel symmetric and yields detailed balance; scores lying in [−1,1] produce the sharp softmax floor aβ,n = 1⁄(1+(n−1)e2β). The analysis then proceeds in three coupled layers:

  1. Local spectral analysis. The consensus manifold C has transverse linearization JC = (A⋆ − In) ⊗ Id−1. On a resonant ring the kernel is circulant, so Fourier diagonalization gives the Bessel-aliasing spectrum; positive-semidefiniteness follows from writing the consensus kernel as a Gram matrix (Schur product theorem).
  2. Regional global convergence. A kernel-generic positivity argument (row stochasticity + the floor aβ,n) proves forward invariance of closed hemispheres and explicit contraction in pairwise non-obtuse / strict-semicircle regions via nonsmooth Dini calculus.
  3. Equilibria beyond consensus. The score-flattening twisted branch θj = c − ωj is characterized by an if-and-only-if resonance condition, its Jacobian spectrum computed, and the multi-frequency gap γ(a) shown non-monotone with no frequency-magnitude ordering.

All theorems are deterministic and proved in the appendix; numerical computations (matrix spectrum, congruence-filtered Bessel, FFT, finite-difference Jacobian, ODE integration) are reported only as cross-checks of convention-sensitive constants — never as proof steps.

§ 05 Strengths & Limitations

+ Strengths

  • Mathematical honesty. Sharp constants, explicit counterexamples, and a contribution-boundary table that resists overclaiming.
  • Clean regime separation. Local spectrum, regional contraction, and equilibrium branches bridged by the quantitative γ vs 2aβ,n comparison.
  • Non-coprime Bessel-aliasing. The exact gcd(m,n) reachability mask is a refinement prior normalized-attention spectral analyses lack.
  • Honest numerics. Independent methods, worst-case residuals reported rather than selected examples.

− Limitations

  • Extreme idealization. Identity Q/K/V, single head, no FFN/masking, first-order residual limit — RoVE and learned couplings out of scope.
  • Only regional convergence. Bipodal stationary states show the strict-semicircle boundary is real; hemisphere invariance alone ≠ consensus.
  • Open asymptotics. Dense-phase joint limit and quantitative nonresonant finite-chain limit remain conjectures, flagged honestly.
  • Center subspace unresolved. Only linear instability of the generic twisted branch is proved — no center-manifold result.
  • No empirical anchor. No code release, no comparison against a trained Transformer; LLM implications stay conjectural.

§ 06 Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Can the dense-phase limit (γ ~ 1 − I₁(β)⁄I₀(β) ~ 1⁄2β) be promoted from conjecture to theorem via a uniform aliasing error bound — and what does it imply for very-long-context RoPE models?
  2. Do the twisted and odd-antipodal equilibria persist — and organize nonlinear transients — once learned Q/K/V maps, multiple heads, and causal masking are restored?
  3. How typical is the multi-plane non-monotonicity beyond n=3, β=ln 4⁄3: a robust phenomenon or a measure-zero curiosity?
  4. Can the deterministic cross-checks be paired with empirical measurements on a trained RoPE Transformer to test the predicted slowdown and frequency reversal?
  5. Does rotating values (RoVE) restore a uniform Lyapunov sign law, and how does its consensus spectrum differ from the query/key-only case?
