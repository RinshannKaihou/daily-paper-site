> **一句话总结：** 本文论证 AI 安全/对齐研究因其"证明不存在危险"和"灾难性尾部风险"的本质，不能套用主流 AI 的平均性能评估范式，并提出 ECAISA——一套含八项认识论原则、三级评分量表、四级披露阶梯与七项反博弈机制的审核治理框架；在一项 n=10 的文献计量基线试点中，独立验证（E3）在全部 10 篇论文中均为 0%，并通过对 8 篇影响力论文的回顾性量规审计（加权 Cohen's κ=0.79）证明工具可评分性。

> **TL;DR:** This position paper argues that AI safety/alignment research has fundamentally different epistemic needs than mainstream AI — it must demonstrate the *absence* of hazards and bound *worst-case* tail risk under sparse, adversarial evidence — and proposes **ECAISA**, a codified Epistemic Code of eight principles, a three-level scoring rubric, a four-level disclosure ladder, a tiered applicability scheme, an infohazard-adjudication protocol, and seven anti-gaming mechanisms. Empirical backing comes from a preregistered bibliometric baseline pilot (n=10 alignment papers, OSF 10.17605/OSF.IO/45YR6) where independent verification (marker E3) was observed in **0.0% (95% CI 0.0–27.8%)** of papers, and a retrospective rubric audit of 8 influential papers (Christiano et al., InstructGPT, Constitutional AI, GPT-4 Tech Report, etc.) achieving weighted Cohen's **κ = 0.79** (percent agreement 0.83) — evidence that the rubric is scorable beyond a single author, with P4 (independent verification) and P7 (uncertainty accounting) scoring near zero across all audited sub-areas.

## 核心贡献

- **差距分析（Gap Analysis）**：提出主流 AI 与对齐研究在两条分析独立的轴上存在根本不对称——能力画像（证明"不能被诱导做坏事" vs. 证明"能做事"）与风险画像（在肥尾、对抗性不确定性下界定最坏结果 vs. 优化平均性能）——并据此识别出 5 个横切差距维度（优化目标、证据可见性、证据可信度、不确定性处理、执行文化）。差距分析基于一个已在 OSF 预注册的文献计量基线（协议号 10.17605/OSF.IO/45YR6）。
- **ECAISA 认识论准则**：将四个传统（开放科学、安全工程、严肃检验哲学、定量风险分析）综合为八项不可选原则（P1 激进透明、P2 完整性、P3 证据比例性声明、P4 复现+对抗性验证、P5 FAIR 工件管理、P6 制度化怀疑、P7 反身性不确定性核算、P8 认识论卫生），并配备三级（0/1/2）评分量表。
- **披露阶梯与分层适用**：用四级披露阶梯（L0 公开、L1 带修订公开、L2 受控访问、L3 延迟发布）将"证据透明"与"能力透明"解耦；用分层适用（T0 探索/T1 研究/T2 安全/T3 部署关键）按声明影响缩放证据负担，避免对早期工作施加不成比例的成本。
- **信息危害裁定与反博弈基础设施**：给出明确的信息危害裁定程序（评审委员会组成、五部分决策模板、可申诉性、署名问责），并提出七项相互强化的反博弈机制（随机抽查、抽签式独立验证、哈希清单、公开纠错通道、登录披露例外、红队最小字段、访问不对称披露），以应对 Goodhart 定律。
- **治理交叉比对与采用动力学**：Table 7 将八项原则映射到 NIST AI RMF 1.0、ISO/IEC 42001、EU GPAI Code of Practice，并明确 ECAISA 在研究阶段（上游）的独特增量；将单边采用建模为协调问题，给出三条绕过先发劣势的制度化路径（venue 级强制、funder 级强制、监管锚定）。
- **可行性与验证路线图**：通过对 8 篇论文的回顾性量规审计（κ=0.79）展示工具可评分性，并提出四阶段验证路线图（固定样本信度扩展 → 扩大 IRR → 比较案例研究 → 前瞻性采用试验）。

## Key Contributions

- **Gap analysis** grounding mainstream-vs-alignment epistemic divergence in two analytically independent axes (capability profile: demonstrating absence vs. presence; risk profile: worst-case tail-bounding vs. average-case optimization) and five cross-cutting dimensions, anchored to a preregistered bibliometric baseline (OSF 10.17605/OSF.IO/45YR6).
- **ECAISA**, a codified Epistemic Code of eight non-optional principles (P1–P8) synthesizing open science, safety engineering, severe-testing philosophy, and quantitative risk analysis, with a three-level (0/1/2) ordinal scoring rubric and full codebook (Appendix A).
- **A disclosure ladder (L0–L3) and tiered applicability scheme (T0–T3)** that decouple evidence transparency from capability transparency and scale evidentiary burden with claim stakes, reconciling radical openness with infohazard and commercial-confidentiality constraints.
- **An infohazard adjudication protocol and seven anti-gaming mechanisms** (randomized spot-audits, lottery-based independent verification, hash manifests, public correction channel, logged disclosure exceptions, red-team minimum fields, access-asymmetry disclosure) explicitly designed to beat Goodhart's-law erosion of checklist regimes.
- **A governance crosswalk (Table 7)** mapping each principle to NIST AI RMF 1.0, ISO/IEC 42001, and the EU GPAI Code of Practice, with an explicit "what ECAISA adds" column arguing research-phase, claim-level coverage absent from organizational frameworks.
- **Adoption dynamics analysis** modeling unilateral adoption as a coordination failure and identifying three institutional paths (venue mandates, funder mandates, regulatory anchoring) past first-mover disadvantage.

## Methodology

The paper uses a mixed methodology combining conceptual analysis, comparative benchmarking against safety-critical fields (aviation's 10⁻⁹/flight-hour design-assurance target as motivating analogy, not computable target; nuclear defense-in-depth; clinical-trial preregistration), and a structured synthesis of metascience literature. The conceptual core is the two-axis asymmetry framework plus five diagnostic gap dimensions. Two pieces of empirical work support it:

1. **Preregistered bibliometric baseline** (OSF 10.17605/OSF.IO/45YR6): a stratified random sample (target n≥30 across RLHF, red-teaming/safety eval, mechanistic interpretability, robustness), with five binary observable markers (E1–E5) corresponding to the gap dimensions, double-coding, and Wilson 95% CIs. The paper reports only a **single-coded pilot fragment of n=10** (4 RLHF, 3 red-teaming, 2 mech-interp, 1 robustness); the full study is in progress.
2. **Retrospective rubric audit**: eight influential alignment papers (Christianou 2017, Shennon 2020, InstructGPT, Constitutional AI, Perez 2022, GPT-4 Tech Report, Elhage 2021, Madry 2018) scored against the Appendix-A codebook using only paper text, appendices, and author-linked artifacts; a second coder scored 3 papers (24 cells) for inter-rater reliability.

The authors are explicit that the rubric audit is an **instrument-feasibility demonstration, not a field-wide audit**, and that the pilot is not a substitute for the registered full study. The disclosure ladder, tiered applicability, and infohazard adjudication are normative-mechanism design rather than empirically validated instruments.

## Results & Highlights

The paper's quantitative claims are about *epistemic practices in the literature*, not model performance. The strongest empirical signals:

- **Independent verification is essentially absent.** In the n=10 bibliometric pilot, marker E3 (independent verification: independent replication, third-party audit, or external red-team of the central claim) scored **0.0% (Wilson 95% CI 0.0–27.8%)** — observed in *none* of the ten papers. The authors argue the upper Wilson bound of 27.8% is itself sufficient to establish the practice cannot be common in alignment research; this is the empirical observation on which P4 (Independent Verification) most directly rests.
- **Marker E4 (residual-risk / limitations discussion) is the highest-prevalence marker at 90.0% (95% CI 59.6–98.2%)**, i.e., alignment papers almost universally acknowledge limitations in prose — but the rubric audit shows P7 (structured uncertainty registers) scores near zero, so this is a deliberately low bar.
- **E1 (worst-case/adversarial reporting) at 40.0% (95% CI 16.8–68.7%) is sharply sub-community-specific**: 100% in the red-teaming and robustness strata (where adversarial testing *is* the topic) and 0% in RLHF and mechanistic-interpretability strata.
- **Retrospective rubric audit reliability**: weighted Cohen's **κ = 0.79** (quadratic weights, ordinal 0/1/2) and **percent agreement 0.83** over 24 doubly-coded cells from 3 papers — described as "substantial agreement" but with necessarily wide CIs given the small overlap, and explicitly framed as scorable-beyond-a-single-author rather than mature psychometric validation.
- **Rubric discriminated across principles**: P3 (evidence-proportional claims) scored consistently high (group means 1.00), while **P2 (full reporting) scored 0.00 across all four sub-area groups** and **P7 (uncertainty accounting) scored 0.00 across all four groups**, identifying these — together with P4 — as priority targets for adoption incentives. P6 (red-teaming) was high only in the red-teaming/safety-eval group (mean 1.50).
- **Coverage is redundant by design**: Figure 1 maps the eight principles onto the five gaps; every gap is covered by multiple principles (e.g., P1 touches E1, E2, E5), so a single lapse cannot reopen a gap.

No model-level benchmark numbers are reported (this is a governance/epistemology paper, not an ML systems paper).

## Authors & Credibility

Single-author paper by **Keivan Navaie, Lancaster University, UK**. The work exhibits unusually strong methodological self-disclosure for a position paper: the bibliometric protocol is preregistered on OSF (10.17605/OSF.IO/45YR6) with a registered codebook; the pilot is explicitly labeled "single-coded pilot fragment … not a substitute for the full study"; the rubric audit's in-house, author-coded status is disclosed and treated as an access asymmetry under the paper's own P2b/P8 standards; three threats to validity (sampling bias, coder subjectivity, construct validity) are named. The governance crosswalk (Table 7) is offered as "an integration aid, not a compliance claim." The framing throughout positions ECAISA as "a well-specified proposal with instrument feasibility demonstrated, not a validated intervention."

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: The genuine contribution is the *synthesis and the claim-level minimal bundle*, not any single practice (preregistration, red-teaming, FAIR, uncertainty quantification all pre-exist). The authors are honest that ECAISA is "not in inventing individual practices" and defend it on integration + anti-gaming grounds. The two-axis asymmetry framing (capability profile × risk profile, kept analytically distinct so the gap analysis doesn't circularly restate them) is a clarifying conceptual move. The most distinctive operational artifacts — the disclosure ladder decoupling evidence vs. capability transparency, and the lottery-based independent verification — are genuinely non-obvious additions over NIST/ISO/EU GPAI.
- **Rigor**: Strong for a governance paper — preregistration, Wilson CIs, weighted-κ with conservative tie-break rules, and explicit threats-to-validity acknowledgment. However, the two empirical studies are both thin: the bibliometric baseline is a **single-coded n=10 pilot** (not the registered n≥30 double-coded study), and the rubric audit relies on only **3 doubly-coded papers (24 cells)** for its κ=0.79 claim, with wide implicit CIs. The authors do not bootstrap κ CIs even at Stage 1.
- **Reproducibility**: The codebook (Appendix A) is published in full with per-principle 0/1/2 criteria and tie-break rules, making the rubric independently applicable; the OSF registration is public. But the pilot codings and full audit annotations (per-paper matrices beyond group means in Table 9) are not released as data, which undercuts the paper's own P1/P5 standards — an internal inconsistency the authors do not flag.
- **Significance**: Addresses a real and under-specified gap (alignment lacks institutionalized epistemic norms distinct from capability benchmarks). The "auditability not certification" governance target is well-argued and avoids the trap of overclaiming that any finite test can certify open-ended systems.
- **Red flags**: (a) The headline κ=0.79 rests on 24 cells from 3 papers — a single contested paper could swing it substantially; presenting it prominently risks over-weighting fragile evidence. (b) The pilot's E3=0% finding, while directionally striking, comes from n=10 single-coded papers and is being used to motivate P4 before the registered full study completes — the paper is careful about CIs but less careful about the rhetorical weight placed on this preliminary signal. (c) The eight audited papers are illustratively (not randomly) selected and include the field's most-cited works (InstructGPT, GPT-4 Tech Report), so the near-zero P2/P4/P7 scores may partly reflect older publication norms rather than current practice. (d) ECAISA's own anti-gaming mechanisms (e.g., lottery-based verification for T2/T3 claims) are asserted rather than stress-tested against realistic adversarial responses.

## Strengths

- Exceptionally honest about its own evidentiary limits: explicitly labels pilot status, discloses in-house coding as access asymmetry, names construct-validity gaps, and refuses to claim validation beyond instrument feasibility.
- The two-axis asymmetry framework is genuinely clarifying and avoids circular reasoning by keeping capability and risk profiles independent.
- The disclosure ladder + infohazard adjudication protocol is a concrete, contestable governance artifact rather than vague aspiration — it makes "infohazard exception" an auditable decision instead of unattributable discretion.
- The anti-gaming bundle is thoughtfully designed against two specific baselines (no checklist; checklist-only) and engages seriously with Goodhart's law rather than ignoring it.
- The adoption-dynamics analysis reframes unilateral adoption as a coordination failure and offers three concrete institutional paths — a realistic corrective to naïve "labs should just adopt this" prescriptions.

## Limitations

- All empirical support is preliminary: the bibliometric baseline is a single-coded n=10 pilot, not the preregistered n≥30 double-coded study; the rubric audit's κ rests on only 24 doubly-coded cells from 3 papers.
- No causal evidence that higher ECAISA scores predict better downstream outcomes (replication success, post-publication correction, successful challenge/extension) — this is deferred to Stages 3–4 of a roadmap not yet run.
- The audited sample is non-random, breadth-oriented, and skewed toward older canonical papers, so near-zero scores on P2/P4/P7 may not reflect current frontier-lab practice.
- ECAISA's compliance overhead could entrench well-resourced incumbents with privileged compute/evaluator access; the equity-as-infrastructure discussion is brief and aspirational rather than operationalized.
- The paper does not stress-test its own anti-gaming mechanisms against determined well-resourced actors who could produce convincing-but-hollow compliance artifacts.
- Single-author authorship concentrates the conceptual framing and the audit coding; Stage 2 external coding is acknowledged as needed but not yet done.
- The 10⁻⁹ aviation target and other high-reliability-engineering analogies are admitted to be motivating rather than computable for alignment — useful rhetorically, but the distance from analogy to operationalizable threshold for AI is large and largely unaddressed.

## Relevance

本文与"AI 安全治理、对齐研究方法论、可复现性与红队评测"等方向高度相关，可为制定会议/资助机构的对齐研究证据标准、设计独立验证与对抗性审计流程、以及评估前沿模型安全声明的可信度提供可操作的八原则评分量表和披露阶梯框架，适合作为研究治理与元科学讨论的参考。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- When the registered n≥30 double-coded bibliometric study completes, will the E3=0% finding hold, and how wide will the Wilson CIs be — does the case for P4 strengthen or weaken?
- Does higher ECAISA rubric score actually predict downstream indicators (replication success, post-publication error correction, successful external challenge) in a matched comparative study (Stage 3)?
- How do well-resourced frontier labs respond to lottery-based independent verification and spot-audits in practice — can the seven anti-gaming mechanisms survive determined hollow-compliance investment?
- Is κ=0.79 stable when coders come from different disciplinary backgrounds (Stage 1/2), or does it degrade — i.e., is the rubric's apparent reliability an artifact of a homogeneous author group?
- How should ECAISA's tiered applicability (T0–T3) interact with fast-moving capability evaluations (e.g., dangerous-capability evals, STREAM, FMTI) to avoid double-counting or gaps at the research↔deployment boundary?
- Can the disclosure ladder's L2 "vetted third party" access be operationalized without creating new gatekeeping power and monoculture risk, which Ahmed et al. (2024) already flagged in the AI-safety community?
