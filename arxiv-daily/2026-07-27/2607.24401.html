01 / Methodology

A layered validation pipeline

LEVEL 1

Representativity

population validity
5 checks
  • Standardized mean diff (SMD<0.1)
  • Kolmogorov–Smirnov
  • Kish effective N, weight CV
  • Coverage |Vk| / |Pk|
Adjust: importance-weight reweighting, or flag "gap too severe".
LEVEL 2

Unit

measurement quality
27 checks · 6 properties
  • Accuracy, precision, agreement
  • Calibration (ECE, Brier, BSS)
  • Discrimination (AUC, MCC, τ, ρ)
  • Distributional fidelity (KS, TV, W₁)
Adjust: bias correction, Platt, isotonic, MCGrad, or replace.
LEVEL 3

Estimate

decision validity
8 checks · B bootstraps
  • Aggregate bias (RAE)
  • CI coverage, CI overlap
  • Sign & effect-size agreement
  • Relative precision, unbiasedness
Adjust: PPI estimator θ̂PPI = θ̂*all + (θ̂V − θ̂*V).
LEVEL 4

Domain

cross-domain transportability
6 checks · IVW meta-regression
  • Aggregate association (R²)
  • Effect sensitivity γ₁≈1
  • Heterogeneity Q, I², τ²
  • LOO MSE/MAE, Durbin–Watson
Adjust: regression calibration, estimate-level adjustment, interval widening.
Theorem 1 — the gating rule MSE(θ̂PPI) < MSE(θ̂*) ⇔ |φ| > σΔ/√n. When the proxy is already unbiased (φ=0), PPI strictly increases MSE by σ²Δ/n — the formal argument against an "always PPI" reflex.

03 / Assessment

Strengths & Limitations

+ Strengths

  • Clean four-level taxonomy — collapsing Unit+Estimate would hide how unit errors compound in aggregation.
  • Diagnostic-to-adjustment bridge (Table 1) tells practitioners which correction to apply instead of treating them as interchangeable.
  • Theorem 1 gives a sharp, citable gating rule with an actionable break-even: |φ| > σΔ/√n.
  • Case B's IW-vs-PPI exposition is the clearest account of why diagnostic ordering matters.
  • Open-source package with 46 modular checks and a configurable planner lowers adoption cost.
  • Intellectual honesty — candid about "no causal mechanisms" and "best statistical ≠ best deployable proxy."

− Limitations

  • One proxy, one outcome at a time; no multi-outcome or sequential / online validation.
  • Statistical agreement, not causal — a proxy can pass all four levels yet fail as a causal surrogate.
  • All numbers perturbed and non-releasable; external reproducibility is limited to the package.
  • Domain Level needs ≥5 (ideally 10–30) domains; both Case A and Case B sit near or below this.
  • IW can have high variance at low ESS — fallbacks are operational, not statistical.
  • Thresholds are convention-based (SMD<0.1, I²<25%); no decision-theoretic sensitivity analysis.
04 / Open Questions

For further exploration

  1. How does the diagnostic-to-adjustment map behave under active covariate shift, where the importance weights themselves drift between validation and target domains?
  2. Can Theorem 1's gating rule be extended to a sequential / anytime-valid form using confidence sequences, so the "skip PPI" decision reverses as labeled data accrues?
  3. What is the principled way to combine rejected proxies (e.g. Case B's A/B/C) into an ensemble whose joint diagnostic profile passes Unit / Estimate Levels?
  4. How sensitive are the Meta conclusions to the SMD<0.1 / I²<25% / ECE thresholds, and is there a decision-theoretic derivation tied to downstream decision cost?
  5. Does estimate-level adjustment [52], layered on a PPI-corrected proxy, double-count uncertainty when the same validation set is reused?
  6. Can the framework extend to multi-outcome surrogate indices, where correlations among components complicate Unit-Level discrimination?
