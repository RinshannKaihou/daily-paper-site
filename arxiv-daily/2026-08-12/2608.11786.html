Language-Conditional Dequantization: Recovering What Quantization Steals from Non-English Languages

Nirmal Thomas · Qwen2.5-3B & Llama-3.2-3B · INT3 GPTQ (W3A16, g128) · ~42 GPU-hours total
01Key metric
4.37×
INT3 GPTQ hits Arabic 4.37× harder than English on Qwen2.5-3B perplexity (mC4/C4). English degrades only 1.35×.
02Why this matters

A cheap post-hoc fix — 0.12% params, ~20 min on one GPU — restores non-English fairness to deployed INT3 models without re-quantization.

03Critical edge

The 70–83% perplexity recovery buys only 17–28% on real tasks — perplexity is an unreliable proxy for accuracy.

M.01Methodology — a forward hook, not a retrain

The premise: if quantization error is language-dependent, the correction should be too. For each linear layer (excluding embeddings and lm head) and each language ℓ, LCD attaches a rank-2 additive correction as a forward hook — parameterized like LoRA but serving error correction, not task adaptation. Aℓ is zero-initialized so the correction is exactly nil at start.

Each language trains independently on 256 monolingual samples (mC4/C4), base weights frozen, AdamW / cosine / 500 steps — finishing in ~20 min on a single L4 GPU. The base quantized model loads once; switching language only flips an adapter index. Per language: +0.12% params, shipped as a ~7 MB delta on the unchanged checkpoint.

S.01Strengths

Honest, mechanism-focused, fairly baselined.

  • Disconnect foregrounded. The perplexity–accuracy gap is presented as the most informative finding, backed by per-layer Frobenius heatmaps and a controlled double dissociation.
  • Fair baselines. Order-of-magnitude comparison to LQER at matched rank-2, plus a language-agnostic LoRA at matched capacity — isolating what per-language conditioning actually buys.
  • Deployable shape. Post-hoc, ~7 MB / 0.12% per language, ~20 min/language on one L4, forward hooks only — no base-model surgery.
  • Statistical care. Paired t-tests across 57 MMLU subjects, binomial SEs, three-seed variance bars, absolute perplexities in appendix.
  • Restrained claims. Authors explicitly warn against treating perplexity recovery as task recovery and disclose the 7B failure.

L.01Limitations

The fix is real but narrow — and the scaling story is open.

  • Perplexity ≠ accuracy. 70–83% recovery yields only 17–28% GlobalMMLU; on early-error architectures (Llama) the gap is structural — LCD can't undo propagated upstream corruption.
  • Scale ceiling. At Qwen2.5-7B only 8.5% recovered; rank-4 / 1000 steps overfits and degrades every language (avg −3.9%).
  • One quant regime. Only INT3 GPTQ W3A16 g128, English C4 calibration. AWQ, GGUF k-quants, SmoothQuant, INT2/INT4/FP4 untested.
  • Language scope. Eight high-resource languages; no truly low-resource, dialectal, or code-switched handling — design assumes a known input language.
  • Tiny data. 256 train / 32 eval samples per language; the unexplained French regression (−15.8 pp vs agnostic) may shift with more data.
  • Analysis undercut. Precision-targeting layers 7–14 does not beat uniform (37% vs 45%) — the actionable payoff of depth analysis is limited.

Q.01Open questions for further exploration

  1. Does LCD transfer to AWQ, GGUF k-quants, SmoothQuant, and INT2/INT4/FP4 — where the per-layer error profile differs?
  2. Can a soft-gating router over per-language adapters handle code-switched and mixed-language inputs?
  3. What happens on truly low-resource and dialectal languages, where 256 samples may be unobtainable?
  4. Why does per-language conditioning hurt French by 15.8 pp — and would more diverse data close it?
  5. Would higher rank on high-error projections (o_proj, down_proj) beat uniform block correction?
  6. At what (scale × bit-width) does LCD's benefit vanish — is the 7B failure data or capacity?
  7. Does the disconnect recur in Mistral / Phi, and can a cross-layer correction fix what a per-layer hook can't?
