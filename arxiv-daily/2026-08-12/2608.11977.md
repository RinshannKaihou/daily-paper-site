> **一句话总结：** 本文提出 BENCH2ROBUST 框架，将"无失败"工具调用基准转化为具有场景可控可解性（重试/切换/放弃）的随机环境，并证明结构化恢复上下文（BTM）与课程强化学习互补——在 Retail 留出任务上把鲁棒性提升多达 16.8 个百分点，两者结合后达到 40.8–45.5% 的注入通过率。

> **TL;DR:** The authors introduce BENCH2ROBUST, a benchmark-agnostic framework that injects tool-response failures with explicit recoverability classes (S1 retry-works, S2 switch-needed, S3 impossible), revealing that 69 of 70 model/subset pairs degrade under injection (up to -46.7pp). On 402 held-out Combined Retail tasks, Bayesian Tool Memory (BTM) lifts the Qwen3-4B base from 20.1% to 36.9% (+16.8pp) without retraining, curriculum RL adds a complementary +6.3/+6.9pp without inference-time BTM, and the combined system reaches 40.8%/45.5% under injection while preserving clean performance (63.9% vs 64.3% base).

## 核心贡献

- **提出 BENCH2ROBUST 框架**：一个与基准无关的失败注入框架，在 agent 与工具执行之间插入一层适配器，用 POMDP 形式化被污染的观测核，覆盖 9 种故障模式（6 种显式信号：超时/限流/服务器错误/鉴权错误/格式错误/模式漂移；3 种静默污染：数据缺失/数据陈旧/事实错误）。
- **场景可控的可解性（S1/S2/S3）**：这是核心设计创新。不同于一般的随机噪声增强，每个训练 episode 被显式分配一个可恢复性类别——S1 中重试即可恢复、S2 中主路径被永久阻断需要切换到等价工具、S3 中所有路径被阻断应当升级。这使得"重试策略质量"与"环境运气"得以分离。
- **贝叶斯工具记忆（BTM）**：将运行时恢复上下文（Beta 后验可恢复性统计 + 领域回退映射 + 启发式约束）注入系统提示，既作 RL 训练时的探索先验，又可在推理时使用。
- **课程式策略训练**：使用 5 阶段课程 + DAPO + KL 约束 + 部分信用奖励训练 Qwen3-4B，并附 Qwen3-8B 复现（Appendix B），证明学到的恢复行为无需推理时 BTM 仍能保留。
- **广泛的鲁棒性差距实证**：跨 4 个模型家族、7 个模型、10 个任务/领域切片，系统刻画了工具失败下的脆弱性，并分解了 BTM（结构 vs. 数值）与 RL（不同错误制度）的互补作用。

## Key Contributions

- **BENCH2ROBUST injection framework**: a benchmark-agnostic layer formalized as a POMDP with a corrupted observation kernel `Zinj`, interposing between agent and tool execution. Any benchmark following `tool_call(name, args) -> response` integrates via a thin adapter; supports τ²-bench (Retail-3I/Airline-3I) and BFCL, with per-call noise `P(clean)=0.60` spread over 9 failure modes grouped by observability (6 explicit-signal, 3 silent-corruption).
- **Scenario-controlled solvability (S1/S2/S3)**: the central design choice that distinguishes this work from generic noise augmentation. Each episode gets an explicit recoverability class — S1 (retry_works), S2 (switch_needed, primary path blocked episode-wide requiring a fallback-equivalent tool, side randomized 50/50), S3 (impossible, all viable paths blocked). Scenario blocking is deterministic, episode-persistent, and operates *outside* the stochastic injection budget (B≤5, Kmax=2 consecutive), separating persistent unavailability from unlucky stochastic runs.
- **Bayesian Tool Memory (BTM)**: structured runtime recovery context combining Beta-posterior per-(tool, error) recoverability statistics, domain fallback maps with granularity annotations, and heuristic constraints (retry before abandoning, verify before irreversible actions, escalate only after paths exhausted). Honest framing: the "Bayesian" part is empirical recovery summaries, not calibrated probabilities driving the main result.
- **Curriculum-controlled RL**: 5-phase curriculum (retry → switch → abstain → full repertoire) with DAPO, a KL constraint (coef 0.02) to prevent policy collapse, asymmetric clipping, dynamic group filtering, and partial-credit rewards over G=16 rollouts (8 clean + 8 injected). Trains Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507 with a Qwen3-8B replication.
- **Systematic robustness-gap characterization**: 69 of 70 (model, subset) pairs degrade under injection (−1.8 to −46.7pp), demonstrating brittleness is universal and not cured by scale.

## Methodology

**Injection environment (BENCH2ROBUST).** The agent's observation kernel is corrupted by independently sampling a noise type `ν ~ Cat(θ_j)` per tool call, applying a corruption map `C_ν` to the clean response. Nine noise types are organized by observability: six produce explicit error signals (timeout, rate limit, server error, auth error, malformed response, schema drift) and three are silent corruptions of structurally valid responses (partial=empty critical fields, stale=backdated state, factual=±10-25% plausible perturbation). The framework deliberately scopes to tool-response-level failures, excluding user-side noise and action-space perturbations.

**Scenario control.** S1 leaves all paths recoverable via the original route; S2 permanently blocks one side of a task-relevant fallback-equivalence class (which side randomized 50/50), forcing a switch; S3 blocks all viable paths, defining episodes where escalation is the prescribed behavior. S3 is used during training only, because benchmark evaluators cannot assign positive completion credit to unsolvable tasks — so held-out behavioral evidence focuses on retry and switch, not learned abstention.

**Bayesian Tool Memory.** For each (tool j, error e) pair, episode-level recoverability is modeled as `p_rec ~ Beta(α, β)` with posterior mean `α/(α+β)`, computed from base-model rollouts on the Retail training split (937 tasks, 3-5 seeds). Switch beliefs `P̂(switch | j→k)` are observed post-transition recovery rates (n=6-20, higher uncertainty). BTM is injected into the system prompt as structured data; the model learns its own decision boundary. During RL it acts as an exploration prior; at inference it supplies domain-specific recovery information.

**Reward design.** `R(τ) = 1.0·eff(τ)·rep(τ)` on completion, else `0.3·(matched_actions/total_required)·rep(τ)`. The efficiency term `eff` penalizes episodes >12 turns; `rep` zeroes reward at ≥4 identical calls. Crucially, there is **no positive term for correct abstention** — the partial-credit term is necessary because without it 38% of training tasks have zero reward variance.

**Training.** Five-phase curriculum auto-advances on mastery (e.g., Phase 4 demands S3 transfer >50%, avg turns <15, no S1/S2 regression). DAPO with KL coefficient 0.02 is essential — without KL, training collapses within 25-30 iterations as repetition rate exceeds 80%.

**Setup.** Combined Retail = τ²-bench retail (114) + Retail-3I (1,225) = 1,339 tasks (937 train, 402 held-out). Primary metric: binary task completion (pass rate). Reference models span 4B-235B: Qwen3-4B/8B/32B/235B, DeepSeek-V3, GLM-4.7, MiniMax-M2.5. Qwen3-235B serves as the user simulator.

## Results & Highlights

- **Universal robustness gap (Table 5):** Across 7 models from 4 families and 10 task/domain slices, **69 of 70 model-subset pairs degrade under injection, by −1.8 to −46.7pp**. The worst single drop is Qwen3-4B-Thinking on Retail-3I general: **71.9% clean → 25.2% injected (−46.7pp)**. Scaling does not cure brittleness: Qwen3-235B still loses 15.8pp on τ²-retail, and GLM-4.7/MiniMax-M2.5 (89-91% clean Telecom) lose 20-21pp. BFCL shows drops of −11.5 to −39pp.
- **BTM gives the largest zero-training gain (Table 1):** On 402 held-out Retail tasks, BTM improves the 4B base from **20.1% → 36.9% (+16.8pp, inject w/o alt)** and **31.9% → 43.6% (+11.7pp, inject w/ alt)**, with no retraining. RL without inference-time BTM reaches **26.4%/38.8% (+6.3/+6.9pp over base)**. Combined RL+BTM achieves the highest **40.8%/45.5%** under injection while clean performance is 63.9% (within 0.4pp of base 64.3%) — no measurable clean-task degradation.
- **BTM decomposition — structure dominates values (Table 3):** Static structure (constraints + fallback maps with uniform P=0.50) recovers **+10.5pp of the full +14.6pp**; calibrated beliefs without structure add only +1.7pp. Shuffled values (38.3%) nominally *exceed* true values (37.8%), and under online updating the margin is ≤1.6pp (at/below seed variance) — so BTM's benefit is structural scaffolding, not Bayesian calibration.
- **Complementary error regimes (Table 4):** BTM helps most on explicit transient errors (+18.0 to +25.3pp on timeout, rate_limit, server_error, malformed_response). RL adds larger marginal gains on persistent observable errors (auth_error +10.2pp, schema_drift +12.5pp) and silent errors (factual_error +10.5pp, stale +5.4pp). BTM is the only negative entry, *hurting* on factual_error by −4.6pp (plausibly because it raises retry propensity, which cannot help silent corruption).
- **Strategy-level evidence (Table 2):** RL's realized S2 switch-success rises from **16.8% (Base+BTM) to 35.3% (RL without BTM)**, while total premature escalation falls from **52.5% to 41.7%** — consistent with recovery behavior beyond mere retry persistence.
- **Cross-benchmark transfer (Table 7):** Retail-trained RL (no target BTM) adds **+2.7pp on Airline (33.4%)** and **+1.5pp on BFCL (15.0%)**; combining with target-domain BTM reaches **+9.0pp Airline** and **+5.0pp BFCL**. On Telecom (4B clean pass rate only 21%), gains are within noise.
- **Full recipe dominates baselines (Table 16):** On the full 1,339-task Combined Retail set, the complete recipe reaches **45.9% (inject w/o alt) / 47.9% (w/ alt)** versus **23.0% / 24.4% for vanilla GRPO** and 23.2% for the base model without fine-tuning — though this comparison varies curriculum, stabilization, and reward shaping together.
- **8B replication (Table 6):** Qwen3-8B reproduces the qualitative decomposition — combined RL+BTM attains the highest pass rates (41.4%/44.6%), confirming complementarity is not unique to the 4B checkpoint.

## Authors & Credibility

All ten authors are affiliated with **Amazon**, spanning applied science and research roles (including well-known agent/LLM researchers such as Zhou Yu and Dakuo Wang, and the Trajectory2Task / Retail-3I authors Ziyi Wang, Yuxuan Lu, Jing Huang, Jin Lai). This gives the work strong institutional infrastructure and explains the access to a 235B-scale user simulator (Qwen3-235B) across all evaluations. The paper is methodologically careful and unusually self-critical: it repeatedly hedges its claims (e.g., explicitly stating "we do not claim fully learned abstention," and that BTM's posterior values do not drive the main result), and provides a Reproducibility Statement committing to release the injection framework, domain registry, belief-computation scripts, and evaluation harness, using only public benchmarks (τ²-bench, Retail-3I, Airline-3I, BFCL) and open-weight base models. The use of 5 injection seeds with reported mean±std for the primary 4B table (Table 1) is appropriate; the 8B replication reports only point estimates.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The scenario-controlled solvability (S1/S2/S3) is a genuine and well-motivated contribution that addresses a real gap — conventional stochastic injection conflates recovery-policy quality with environmental luck. However, the paper honestly positions this against close concurrent work (NoisyAgent, ToolBench-X, PALADIN), and the framing as "retry/switch/abstain" while only *evaluating* retry/switch (S3 is training-only, never scored) somewhat oversells the abstention angle relative to what is demonstrated.
- **Rigor:** The main 4B results (Table 1) use 5 seeds with std, and the BTM decomposition (Table 3) is a well-designed ablation that leads to an honest, counterintuitive conclusion (calibration doesn't matter; structure does). However, the central "full recipe vs vanilla GRPO" comparison (Table 16: 45.9% vs 23.0%) is **confounded** — the authors admit it varies curriculum, KL stabilization, and reward shaping simultaneously, so it cannot attribute the gain to any single design choice. The intervention comparison "evaluates complete recipes rather than isolated components," a limitation the authors flag themselves.
- **Reproducibility:** Strong on paper — public benchmarks, open weights, promised code release, full training config in Appendix G, verbatim BTM prompt in Appendix N. Caveat: the Qwen3-235B user simulator is expensive to reproduce, and the paper notes "we have not evaluated sensitivity to simulator choice," a known τ²-bench variance concern.
- **Significance:** Practically relevant: the finding that cheap structural prompting (fallback maps + constraints) captures most of BTM's gain is deployment-friendly. But absolute injected pass rates remain low (best case 45.5%), and the efficiency cost is steep.
- **Red flags / data quirks:** (1) The 4B primary target is a *thinking* variant and is "not directly comparable to non-thinking Qwen3 models as a scale sweep," yet it anchors all within-benchmark claims — scaling conclusions are soft. (2) Table 2's S1/S2 split is *policy-dependent* (each policy determines its own scenario mix via its own trajectories), so the strategy-decomposition "is suggestive of improved strategy selection but cannot isolate a causal effect" — the authors state this, but a reader could still misread Table 2 as clean causal evidence. (3) Silent-error pass rates are task-completion scores, not corruption-adoption rates — an episode can pass despite using a perturbed value, which inflates the silent-error baselines and weakens claims about verification behavior. (4) BTM *hurts* on factual_error (−4.6pp), the lone negative result, attributed speculatively to retry propensity but "not isolated experimentally." (5) Failures are "a severe stress-test configuration rather than incident-derived estimates" — absolute numbers may not reflect real deployment failure distributions.

## Strengths

- The scenario-controlled solvability formulation cleanly separates retry/switch/abstain regimes, which prior noise-injection benchmarks (NoisyAgent, RobustBench-TC, ToolBench-X) do not — making the framework usable as a *training* environment, not just a stress test.
- Exceptional intellectual honesty: the BTM decomposition explicitly shows calibrated posterior values are *not* the source of gain (shuffled ≥ true), preempting an obvious overclaim about "Bayesian" methods.
- The RL-without-inference-BTM ablation is the right experiment to show behavioral internalization rather than prompt dependency.
- Broad, multi-family robustness-gap evaluation (7 models, 4 families, 4 task suites) credibly establishes that brittleness is universal and scale-invariant.
- Clear complementarity narrative backed by the error-type decomposition (Table 4): BTM for explicit transient, RL for persistent/silent — a useful, actionable division of labor.

## Limitations

- **Abstention is under-evaluated:** S3 (the "abstain" third of the title) is training-only because the reward (Eq. 2) has no positive term for correct stopping and benchmark evaluators cannot credit unsolvable tasks. The paper only claims abstention is "constructed," not "learned" — so a third of the conceptual framework is essentially aspirational in this paper.
- **Confounded headline comparison:** The 45.9%-vs-23.0% win over vanilla GRPO bundles curriculum + KL + reward shaping, so it does not justify any individual component.
- **Efficiency cost:** Recovery under injection raises token use from 70K to 117K (Table 15), a 1.7× overhead that matters for latency- and cost-sensitive deployment, and is not jointly optimized with pass rate.
- **Narrow training domain, modest transfer:** Training is Retail-only; cross-domain gains are small (BFCL +5pp with BTM) and Telecom (21% clean) is at the model's capability floor where recovery training cannot help.
- **Simulated, not real, failures:** The 9 noise types and their 40% injection rate are a severe synthetic stress configuration, not derived from production incident logs — absolute pass rates are intervention rankings under stressors, not deployment predictions.
- **Soft scaling claims:** The 4B thinking variant is incomparable to non-thinking Qwen3 models, and the 8B replication has only point estimates (no seed std), so scale-related claims are weakly supported.

## Relevance

本研究与"工具使用 LLM agent 的鲁棒性"高度相关——它直接针对生产环境中工具调用失败（超时、限流、静默数据污染）这一被多数基准忽视的问题，提出的场景可控失败注入框架与运行时恢复上下文（BTM）方法，对评估和提升 agent 系统的可靠性有直接参考价值。对于关注 agent 强化学习训练、错误恢复策略设计的研究兴趣尤其契合。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- How would an explicit S3-aware reward term (positive credit for correct abstention on impossible episodes) change learned stopping behavior, and can it be implemented without an S3-completion oracle?
- The error-type decomposition shows BTM *hurts* on factual_error (−4.6pp) — would adding verification/cross-referencing constraints to BTM (rather than retry guidance) recover silent-error performance, and is the retry-propensity mechanism real or an attention artifact?
- Since BTM's gain is structural rather than calibrated, could the same fallback maps + constraints be distilled into the policy during SFT or via a smaller fixed context, removing the 1.7× token overhead at inference?
- The 45.9%-vs-23.0% full-recipe comparison is confounded — which of {curriculum, KL stabilization, partial-credit reward} contributes most when controlled individually, and does the curriculum transfer to non-Retail domains without reordering?
- How sensitive are the absolute injected pass rates to the user-simulator choice (Qwen3-235B vs alternatives), and to incident-derived (rather than stress-configured) noise rate distributions?
