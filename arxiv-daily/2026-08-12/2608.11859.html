The Key Finding

Small-scale scaling laws aren't absent — hyperparameter sensitivity obscures them, and 256+ configs are needed to surface the law.

一句话　小模型之所以“看不到” scaling law，根因在超参敏感而非规模本身；只要充分调参（256+ 次），law 就会浮现。

Methodology

Empirical study of the hyperparameter loss surface across scales — not a new architecture.

Llama-variants (RMSNorm, SwiGLU, RoPE, pre-norm) trained in Meta Lingua on 100B tokens of FineWeb-Edu (p50k_base tokenizer, context 1024). Scales are powers of two from 2²² ≈ 4M to 2²⁸ ≈ 268M effective parameters — a FLOP-based count where c = 6pd holds exactly.

Each config trains 32 tokens/parameter (≈50% past the Chinchilla-optimal ~20 ratio) under a warmup-stable-decay schedule, branching decay phases off 8 checkpoints — so one sweep yields stable and decayed evaluations cheaply. Hyperparameters (lr, weight_decay, batch, betas, warmup, rope_theta, and in the random sweep also depth/width/heads) are sampled by random search on log/logit scales.

Three literature tools do the analysis: the noisy-quadratic limit (opda library) to estimate intrinsic dimension γ and check whether the search is "fully tuned"; Hoffmann-style scaling laws fit by differential_evolution with scales split into train/val/test to measure extrapolation; and six lm-evaluation-harness tasks to confirm perplexity–capability correspondence.

Tuning difficulty collapses with scale, driven by parameters rather than data — "the blessing of dimensionality in weights cures the curse in hyperparameters."
Cost framing. A single 1B-parameter run costs as much as 64 runs at 134M, 1,024 at 34M, or 65,536 at 4M — so the entire pre-norm/post-norm case study costs the equivalent of "a handful of billion-parameter runs." Parameter definitions barely matter either: all six {±Attn, ±Embed, ±Unembed} combos land within test MSE 4.30e-07 – 2.37e-04.

Strengths & Limitations

Strengths

  • A clear, actionable mechanism (HP sensitivity → γ → 1) for a long-standing community pain point, replacing a vague "small models are unreliable" with a diagnosable condition.
  • Exceptionally well-written for a methods paper: each section runs claim → diagnostic → figure, making the argument auditable.
  • The cost framing (1B run = 65,536 runs @4M) is a concrete, useful budgeting rule for academic labs.
  • Honest about the limits of pure extrapolation, and explicit the methodology is restricted to model-centric research (data fixed).

Limitations

  • Data-centric research is out of scope. Changing the corpus breaks perplexity–capability correspondence, so data-mixing/quality experiments can't be predicted.
  • Extrapolation range is bounded (~1.5 orders of magnitude to 268M); beyond it, comparisons degenerate into comparing noisy ε estimates.
  • γ estimates depend on hand-tuned θ percentiles that differ per scale and architecture — analyst degrees of freedom.
  • The flagship case study recovers a settled question; no alternative parametrizations (μP, etc.) are tried.
  • All decoder-only LMs on a single English corpus — other modalities / multilingual unverified.
04

Questions for Further Exploration

  1. Does γ → 1 hold under μP / maximal-update parametrization, or does a scale-invariant parametrization flatten the curve?
  2. How stable is the "256 configs" threshold to the shape of the search distribution (narrower bounds, Sobol / quasi-Monte-Carlo vs. pure random)?
  3. Can the method extend to data-centric research via a data-conditioned capability proxy?
  4. On a genuinely open architectural question, does the holistic diagnostic agree with later large-scale confirmations — or produce false positives?
  5. Could a paired/Bayesian scaling-law fit sharing info across configs tame the wild ε-variance seen across 128-config samples?

Code/config in Appendix A is unusually thorough (search distributions, ladder table, exact θ-percentiles). No cherry-picked benchmarks; all six eval tasks are standard and reported together.
