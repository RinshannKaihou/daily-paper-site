Why this matters

For formalizable math, model capability and raw inference budget dominate agent-scaffold cleverness — retrieval and elaborate loops added nothing.

Critical edge

The "3× better" headline is not apples-to-apples: AlphaProof Nexus ran on Gemini 3.1 Pro (Feb 2026), while the authors' winning models shipped 2–3 months later.

01

Methodology

OEIS_OPEN comprises 492 open conjectures (444 distinct integer sequences) that Tsoukalas et al. auto-formalized in Lean from OEIS. A submission is accepted only if it passes SafeVerify — replay through the Lean kernel with no axioms beyond the standard three — under a three-container, no-network split (agent / compile / scorer) that defeats Mathlib tampering, axiom injection, and kernel-bypass.

The agent is deliberately minimal: a ReAct loop on Inspect with three tools (bash, text editor, budget). The container ships Lean 4 + Mathlib, SageMath, and Python with sympy / mpmath / pantograph. The model may prove or disprove, iterating until the $50/conjecture or 72-hour wall-clock cap. Two variants were ablated — Literature (offline LaTeX of 476k arXiv papers) and DeepAgent (subagents + persistent memory + todo-list) — both produced null results on OEIS_OPEN_LITE.

Strengths & Limitations

Strengths

  • Exemplary disclosure — per-model avg + max cost, release dates, single-vs-multi-run status, and the exact verifier protocol are all stated.
  • Thoughtful security design — 3-container split defeats 6 attack classes, including native decide via @[implemented_by].
  • Counterintuitive null results — literature access and DeepAgent both produced zero gain, a valuable signal for math-agent builders.
  • Open release of code, conjectures, and accepted / rejected proofs lowers the barrier to re-evaluation.

Limitations

  • Low, skewed significance — 37% of conjectures proposed by one person (Zhi-Wei Sun); 47% of sequences have no OEIS references.
  • Single run per model on the full 492-set — no error bars on the headline 30 / 26 / 22%.
  • Base-model-date confound weakens the "simple harness wins" narrative vs AlphaProof Nexus.
  • Misformalization not audited — dataset taken as-is; absolute solve rates may be inflated.
04

Open Questions

  • Q1Would a like-for-like AlphaProof Nexus re-run with GPT-5.5 / Claude close the 3× gap — confirming harness design, not base model, drives the win?
  • Q2With multiple seeds, are the Claude-vs-GPT-vs-Gemini differences statistically significant, or within noise?
  • Q3Which tool / CAS actually carries the solves — pantograph, SageMath, or raw bash + Lean? A per-tool ablation is missing.
  • Q4Is the literature-access null robust to better retrieval (embedding search over the 476k papers vs raw LaTeX grep)?
  • Q5Does the log-linear spend-vs-solve curve continue to $1k / $10k, or plateau into an inference-resistant "hard core"?
  • Q6Does "simple agent wins" transfer to non-integer-sequence math (analysis, geometry, topology) where misformalization risk is higher?
