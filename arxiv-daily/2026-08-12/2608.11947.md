> **一句话总结：** 在 MMLU 和 ARC-Challenge 上对 6 个 LLM 测试两种"不暴露选项标签"的去偏策略（先生成再匹配、独立逐项打分），发现两者都不能稳定提升准确率——例如 Gemini 在 MMLU 上从 84.9% 暴跌到 68.3%；核心结论是"降低选项顺序敏感性"与"提升准确率"会解耦，消除位置偏差并不必然带来准确率收益。

> **TL;DR:** Across 6 LLMs on MMLU and ARC-Challenge, two "label-free" MCQ strategies — generation-then-matching (two-stage prompting) and per-option isolated scoring (independent hypothesis) — fail to reliably beat direct MCQ: two-stage prompting degrades accuracy in 11 of 12 model-benchmark pairs (e.g., Gemini on MMLU falls from 84.9% to 68.3%, and from 96.9% to 86.6% on ARC-Challenge), and a 2×2 decomposition pinpoints the bottleneck as withholding the options in Stage 1 rather than the matching step. The headline negative result is a decoupling: reducing option-order sensitivity (even eliminating it by construction) does not translate into accuracy gains, whereas the simpler cyclic-permutation baseline improves 10 of 12 pairs.

## 核心贡献

- **系统检验"无标签"去偏假设：** 首次直接测试"若模型在作答时看不到选项标签，则选项顺序无法影响预测、从而提升准确率"这一前提，结论是它不成立——两种无标签策略都不能稳定提升准确率。
- **提出两种去偏策略并对比：** 两阶段提示（先生成自由文本答案再用 LLM/嵌入匹配回选项）与独立假设打分（每个选项单独评分取 argmax，结构上消除位置影响）。
- **2×2 诊断网格定位瓶颈：** 将两阶段方法沿"Stage 1 是否隐藏选项 × Stage 2 用 LLM 还是嵌入匹配"四个象限分解，证明性能崩溃的根源是 Stage 1 隐藏选项，而非匹配步骤。
- **直接量化"解耦"现象：** 用翻转率（flip rate）作为每题顺序敏感性的直接度量，结合召回标准差（RStd），证明降低位置敏感性与提升准确率是两回事——例如 GPT-4.1 mini 在 MMLU 上翻转率减半（21.6→11.8）但准确率反而下降（81.8→80.1）。
- **诚实给出实用结论：** 在评估的所有模型无关方法中，简单的循环排列（cyclic permutation）反而是最稳的，提升 10/12 对；而需要 logit 访问的 PriDe 有时甚至降低准确率（Llama-local 在 MMLU 上 50.2→44.2）。

## Key Contributions

- **Tests the "label-free ⇒ unbiased ⇒ more accurate" premise directly**, and finds it does not hold: neither of two strategies that hide option labels from the commitment step reliably improves accuracy.
- **Introduces and benchmarks two label-free strategies**: two-stage prompting (free-text generation followed by matching) and independent hypothesis scoring (each option scored in isolation, positionally unbiased by construction).
- **Delivers a clean 2×2 diagnostic decomposition** isolating the failure to Stage 1 withholding options, not the matching step — the only configuration that consistently matches baseline is visible options + LLM matcher.
- **Establishes the accuracy/order-sensitivity decoupling** using two complementary diagnostics (per-question flip rate and aggregate recall standard deviation), showing that even eliminating positional influence by construction (independent hypothesis) does not yield reliable accuracy gains.
- **Provides a practical, model-agnostic recommendation**: among the methods tested, cyclic permutation (k calls + majority vote) is the most reliable improver (10/12 pairs), outperforming more elaborate label-free and logit-based approaches.

## Methodology

The paper evaluates **two label-free MCQ strategies** against three baselines across **six models × two benchmarks**.

**Strategies:**
- *Two-Stage Prompting:* Stage 1 presents the question alone (no options) and elicits a free-text answer `E = M_gen(Q)`; Stage 2 presents Q, E, and the full labelled option set and asks the model to pick the closest match `P(A | Q, E, O, ϕ)`. Position ϕ is removed in Stage 1 but reintroduced in Stage 2, so the method is *not* positionally invariant by construction.
- *Independent Hypothesis Scoring:* the model is called once per option with only that option shown, producing a 0–100 confidence `s_i = M_score(Q, o_i)`; the answer is `argmax_i s_i`. Since no call ever sees a joint ordered option list, the final prediction is provably independent of ϕ.

**Baselines:** direct MCQ; cyclic permutation (k calls with options rotated, majority vote); PriDe (Zheng et al., 2024 — logit-based, estimates a positional prior from a 50-question calibration set and subtracts it; only evaluated on the three models exposing true log-probs).

**Diagnostic 2×2 grid:** crosses Stage 1 (hidden vs. visible options) with Stage 2 (LLM matcher vs. embedding matcher using all-MiniLM-L6-v2). The four cells are two_prompt (hidden+LLM), semantic matching (hidden+embedding), text extraction (visible+embedding), and visible+LLM. Matching uses a cascade: exact match → substring containment (min length 4, unique) → cosine similarity (threshold 0.30).

**Benchmarks & sampling:** MMLU (20 questions × 50 subjects, seed 42; 7 subjects excluded); ARC-Challenge (1000 of 1172, seed 42). PriDe draws an additional disjoint 50-question calibration set.

**Models:** GPT-4.1 mini, Gemini 2.5 Flash, Llama 3.1 8B Instant (Groq), Qwen 2.5 7B Instruct Turbo (Together AI), plus local Qwen 2.5 7B and Llama 3.1 8B (HuggingFace Transformers on A100 MIG 3g.40gb, ~128 GPU-hours total). All runs use temperature 0.0, seed 42, max_tokens 500 (independent hypothesis overrides to 4000 to fit Gemini's hidden-thinking budget).

**Metrics:** end-to-end accuracy (unscorable counted wrong), conditional accuracy (scorable only), recall standard deviation (RStd) across gold positions A–D, per-question "any-flip" rate across 4 cyclic rotations, McNemar's test for broken-vs-fixed imbalance, Clopper–Pearson CIs (accuracy), 10k-resample bootstrap CIs (RStd), and a 10-seed tie-break sensitivity sweep for independent hypothesis.

## Results & Highlights

- **Two-stage prompting degrades accuracy in 11 of 12 model-benchmark pairs.** On MMLU, Gemini 2.5 Flash collapses from **84.9% baseline to 68.3%** (−16.6 pp); on ARC-Challenge it falls from **96.9% to 86.6%** (−10.3 pp), largely from parse failures (conditional accuracy is 82.0 vs end-to-end 68.3 — a 13.7-point gap). Even GPT-4.1 mini, with zero parse failures, drops on MMLU from **81.8% to 80.1%**, showing the loss is not purely a parsing artifact.
- **Independent hypothesis scoring degrades accuracy in 8 of 11 valid pairs.** The one clear positive, Llama-local on ARC-Challenge, rises **58.0% → 72.4%** (+14.4 pp), but the authors show this is matched by unrelated methods (cyclic +14.9 pp to 72.9%, visible+LLM +12.4 pp to 70.4%), indicating it tracks the model's recoverable performance rather than bias removal. Most other models lose ground (e.g., Llama-API on MMLU: **65.2% → 60.5%**).
- **The 2×2 grid localizes the bottleneck to option-withholding, not matching.** Hidden-options + embedding matching collapses: GPT-4.1 mini on MMLU scores **45.8% vs 81.7%** when the identical Stage 2 matcher is paired with visible options (a 35.9-pp gap attributable solely to whether Stage 1 saw the options). The only cell that consistently matches baseline is visible + LLM matcher.
- **Decoupling is directly demonstrated.** GPT-4.1 mini's MMLU flip rate roughly halves under two-stage (**21.6% → 11.8%**) while accuracy falls (81.8% → 80.1%). Semantic matching drives flip rate to exactly **0.0% for every model on both benchmarks** (confirming position-blindness by construction) yet its accuracy is the lowest of any method (e.g., 45.9% on MMLU for GPT-4.1 mini vs 81.8% baseline).
- **Cyclic permutation is the best model-agnostic method**, improving 10 of 12 pairs (e.g., Llama-local ARC-Challenge 58.0% → 72.9%; Gemini MMLU 84.9% → 86.9%), though its gains cannot be attributed solely to reduced positional effects since it also aggregates across permutations.
- **PriDe can hurt even with logit access:** Llama-local on MMLU drops from **50.2% to 44.2%** under PriDe; on ARC-Challenge from 58.0% to 48.8%.
- **Independent hypothesis produces extreme tie rates** — from 6.2% (GPT-4.1 mini, ARC) to **43.2%** (Llama-local, MMLU), meaning nearly half of questions are decided by a seeded random tie-break; on MMLU the reported seed-42 score (51.2) falls below the 10-seed range (mean 52.6), so the MMLU "gain" is within noise.

## Authors & Credibility

The authors are **Karl Hanna and Chen Feng** of Queen's University Belfast (school of EECS). The work is positioned as an evaluation-methodology study and is methodologically careful: it uses Clopper–Pearson confidence intervals, McNemar significance tests for broken/fixed counts, 10,000-resample bootstrap CIs for RStd, and an explicit 10-seed tie-break sensitivity sweep. Crucially, all experiment code, prompt templates, and the fourth diagnostic-cell runner are released under an MIT license at `github.com/cotenthusiast/choicebench`, and prompts are snapshotted into each run directory for reproducibility. The paper transparently discloses that generative AI was used for codebase refactoring/debugging and manuscript wording/LaTeX/formatting, while authors retain responsibility for design and claims. The negative-result framing is unusually honest — the authors actively argue against their own headline "win" (Llama-local on ARC) by showing it is reproduced by unrelated methods.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The decoupling thesis — that reducing positional sensitivity does not imply accuracy gains, even when position is eliminated *by construction* — is a genuinely useful negative result that prior work (Zheng et al. 2024; Pezeshkpour & Hruschka 2024) only hinted at incidentally. The 2×2 grid that isolates the failure to Stage 1 option-withholding is a clean, reusable diagnostic. However, the individual strategies (two-stage generation-then-matching, per-option isolated scoring) closely resemble prior setups — Balepur et al. (2024) "question-present" condition and Chandak et al. (2025) verification condition — so the *methods* are not novel; the contribution is the comparative decomposition and the decoupling demonstration.

- **Rigor:** Strong on statistical hygiene (McNemar, Clopper–Pearson, bootstrap, seed sweep) but **weakened by the single-run design**: every condition is run once, with no repeated identical-order controls in the flip-rate experiment. The authors themselves flag that residual provider-side non-determinism may inflate observed flips, particularly for API models (e.g., Gemini's 13.9–15.4% flip rates could be partly noise). For a paper whose central claim is about a *divergence between two measured quantities*, quantifying measurement noise on both would have strengthened the conclusion.

- **Reproducibility:** High. Fixed seeds (42), snapshotted prompts, MIT-licensed code, explicit per-model concurrency/delay/retry config, and the 128 GPU-hour budget for local models are all disclosed. The exclusion of the Gemini × ARC independent-hypothesis cell (68.7% API failures → only 313/1000 scored) is reported rather than silently dropped, which is commendable.

- **Significance:** Practically important for anyone building MCQ benchmarks or debiasing tooling — it warns that "label-free" and "position-invariant" are not accuracy guarantees, and that the cheap cyclic-permutation baseline is hard to beat. The scope is narrow, though: only 4-option English benchmarks at 1000 questions each, and only two instantiations per strategy.

- **Red flags / specific weaknesses:**
  - The independent-hypothesis tie rate of **43.2% for Llama-local on MMLU** means the reported accuracy is essentially one random draw from a wide distribution; although the seed sweep (71.6–73.2 on ARC) rescues the ARC claim, the MMLU cell is admitted to be within noise. Headline "+14.4 pp on ARC" should be read alongside the authors' own caveat that cyclic (+14.9) and visible+LLM (+12.4) reproduce the same gain.
  - **Semantic matching's "0% flip rate" is partly an artifact of coverage**: those cells score as few as 68.8% of questions (Llama-local MMLU), so the position-blindness partly reflects a smaller, possibly non-representative scored subset rather than pure bias elimination.
  - **No reasoning-enabled Stage 1 variant** is tested, despite this being the configuration most directly suggested by the answer-matching literature (Chandak et al.); the v2 ablation actually *strengthens* reasoning suppression. This leaves open whether two-stage fails because hiding options is fundamentally lossy, or because the specific non-reasoning prompt is.
  - **No stronger/separate matcher model** is tested, so the claim "the matcher is not the problem" rests on a single matcher choice (same model + all-MiniLM-L6-v2).
  - **Benchmarks are small** (1000 questions) for claims about the *reliability* of accuracy differences; several "within noise" deltas span ±2 pp while CIs are ±3 pp.

## Strengths

- Honest, well-scoped negative result with a clear, citable takeaway (decoupling of accuracy and order sensitivity).
- The 2×2 diagnostic grid is an elegant, reusable tool that cleanly attributes the two-stage failure to option-withholding.
- Excellent statistical reporting discipline (Clopper–Pearson, McNemar, bootstrap, seed sensitivity) and full open-source release with snapshotted prompts.
- Authors actively argue against their own headline positive result rather than cherry-picking it — a model of intellectual honesty.
- Direct per-question flip-rate metric complements the aggregate RStd and exposes cases (e.g., Qwen-local) where the two move in opposite directions.

## Limitations

- **Single-run design** with no identical-order controls; run-to-run provider non-determinism is unquantified and may inflate flip rates for API models.
- **Narrow scope**: only 4-option English MCQ on MMLU and ARC-Challenge (1000 questions each); generalization to more options, other distributions, or larger/frontier models is untested.
- **Each strategy tested in only one instantiation** (plus two ablations that each vary one stage); no reasoning-enabled Stage 1 and no separate/stronger matcher model.
- **Coverage artifacts**: semantic-matching cells and Gemini two-stage score far below 1000/1000 due to parse failures, inflating apparent position-blindness; one cell (Gemini × ARC × independent hypothesis) is excluded entirely for API failures.
- **PriDe calibration-set size not varied**, so its behavior is only characterized at a single budget.
- The proposed under-specification mechanism is supported by citing prior work rather than tested on the authors' own data (no stratification by question type).

## Relevance

该工作直接关系到 LLM 评测方法论的稳健性——揭示"消除选项位置偏差"与"提升准确率"是两件事，对任何使用 MMLU/ARC 类选择题基准来评估模型能力的研究都有警示意义，也与 cs.CL/cs.AI 方向的模型评测、提示工程和基准去偏高度相关。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would a **reasoning-enabled Stage 1** (chain-of-thought before free-text generation) recover the two-stage losses, or is option-withholding fundamentally lossy regardless of reasoning?
- Does the decoupling thesis hold for **more options (e.g., MMLU-Pro's 10-way), harder benchmarks (GPQA-Diamond), or frontier reasoning models** (o1/Claude-thinking class), where positional bias and knowledge may interact differently?
- How much of the observed flip rate is **provider non-determinism vs. genuine positional sensitivity**? Repeated identical-order controls would decompose the two.
- Can a **separate, stronger matcher model** (e.g., a large embedder or a different LLM) rescue semantic matching, or is the hidden-options bottleneck truly matcher-independent?
- Does **stratifying by question type** (factual recall vs. reasoning vs. "which of the following" under-specified) empirically confirm the proposed under-specification mechanism?
- Given that cyclic permutation is cheap, reliable, and model-agnostic, is there a principled way to combine it with label-free strategies to capture both robustness and the comparative context that independent scoring loses?
