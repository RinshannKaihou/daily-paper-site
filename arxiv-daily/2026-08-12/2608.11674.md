> **一句话总结：** 本文首先提出"主子空间重叠度"这一逐步步长修正的诊断指标，发现 on-policy rollout RL（如 GRPO）训练中瞬时出现的、与预训练权重主导奇异子空间重叠的更新尖峰往往预示性能下降；据此提出 GCPO，通过双侧正交投影硬约束把每个策略更新限制在与预训练主子空间正交的补空间中，在 Qwen3-8B/GLM4-9B 上的数学、代码、工具使用三类任务上稳定地超越 GRPO、DAPO、GSPO、GMPO 等，同时抑制响应长度膨胀、保持策略熵平稳并保留跨任务能力。

> **TL;DR:** The authors introduce a dimension-corrected, stepwise "Principal-Subspace Overlap" diagnostic showing that transient spikes of overlap between on-policy rollout updates (GRPO) and the dominant singular subspaces of pretrained weights precede validation degradation; they then propose GCPO, which enforces a hard bilateral orthogonality constraint (projected low-rank update δW = α·Π⊥_Φ L R Π⊥_Ψ with top-k=8 protected singular directions) so every effective update is excluded from the principal input/output subspaces. On MATH500, HumanEval+, and ToolAlpaca with Qwen3-8B and GLM4-9B, GCPO beats the strongest baseline (DAPO/GSPO/GMPO) by 1.02–2.37 majority@16 points, and after math-only post-training it retains tool-use capability (+0.91 on GLM4-9B ToolAlpaca) where GRPO collapses by −14.97.

## 核心贡献

- 提出维度修正的逐步步"主子空间重叠度"诊断量 `O_excess = O_t − O_null`，把每个已实现更新分解为 P–P / P–Orth / Orth–P / Orth–Orth 四个正交块，并减去各向同性零假设下的期望重叠；据此首次揭示 on-policy RL 更新中"平均偏向正交补、但瞬时尖峰"的现象，且这些尖峰与验证集精度下降在时间上相关，甚至先于下降出现。
- 设计了受控干预实验（在 ToolAlpaca 第150步 GRPO 更新上对主子空间重叠分量做范数保持的缩放 η），给出"剂量依赖"的证据：注入主子空间分量显著降低精度、去除它则提升精度，从而把单纯的统计相关性向前推进一步。
- 提出 GCPO，把 rollout RL 表述为带双侧正交约束的约束策略优化问题（`Φ_k^⊤ δW = 0` 且 `δW Ψ_k = 0`），并通过投影低秩参数化 `δW^(ℓ) = α·Π⊥_Φ L^(ℓ) R^(ℓ) Π⊥_Ψ` 把约束按构造精确满足（而非靠软惩罚），使更新方向天然落在与预训练主映射双正交的可行集中。
- 在两个模型族（Qwen3-8B、GLM4-9B）和三个任务域（数学推理、代码生成、工具使用）上系统验证：GCPO 在全部 6 个设置中均取得最优多数投票精度，标准差最小，并在训练动力学上呈现更平滑的精度曲线、更稳定的策略熵衰减、对响应长度膨胀的最强抑制，同时跨任务能力保留最佳。

## Key Contributions

- A dimension-corrected, stepwise **Principal-Subspace Overlap** measure: each realized update `δW` (optimizer + LR + momentum included, not raw gradient) is decomposed via the pretrained SVD projectors into four mutually orthogonal energy blocks, and the reported `O_excess = O_t − O_null` subtracts the isotropic-null expectation `1 − (d_out−k)(d_in−k)/(d_out·d_in)`. Although 93.8% (Qwen3-8B) and 97.7% (GLM4-9B) of energy on average sits in the doubly-orthogonal block, the average masks intermittent positive spikes that coincide with and often precede validation degradation.
- A **controlled intervention** (Appendix D) that rescales the principal-overlapping component of a real GRPO update by η while preserving per-layer Frobenius norm. This produces a dose-dependent accuracy drop, and a matched random-subspace perturbation is far less harmful — local evidence that principal-subspace overlap is causally harmful beyond mere magnitude/directional noise.
- **GCPO**, which formulates rollout RL as a constrained policy optimization (`max J_rollout − β·KL`, s.t. `Φ_k^⊤ δW^(ℓ)=0` and `δW^(ℓ) Ψ_k=0, ∀ℓ∈M`) and enforces the constraints exactly by parameterization rather than by a soft penalty. The projected low-rank form leaves a feasible space of dimension `(d_out−k)(d_in−k)` — large when k ≪ width — and gives a layer-level guarantee that the pretrained mapping is unchanged on the protected principal input/output subspaces.
- A clean **separation from LoRA**: LoRA restricts update rank for parameter efficiency, whereas GCPO uses low rank only as an efficient parameterization of a geometrically constrained feasible set; the same-rank GRPO-LoRA baseline is consistently beaten by GCPO, isolating the directional constraint as the cause of the gains.

## Methodology

GCPO is built directly on top of GRPO and adds no new loss term. For every adapted linear layer ℓ, the top-k=8 left and right singular vectors `Φ_k, Ψ_k` of the frozen pretrained weight `W_ref` are computed **once** before training. The cumulative adaptation is parameterized as `ΔW_t^(ℓ) = s · Π⊥_Φ L^(ℓ) R^(ℓ) Π⊥_Ψ`, where `L, R` are trainable low-rank factors (rank r=32, α=16, s=α/r) and `Π⊥_Φ, Π⊥_Ψ` are the orthogonal-complement projectors. Because both projectors annihilate the principal subspaces, the bilateral orthogonality constraints hold for every value of the trainable factors and every optimization step — overlap cannot enter the update even when the rollout gradient points into the principal subspace.

At each step the raw factors L, R are re-projected (`L̄ = L − Φ_k Φ_k^⊤ L`, `R̄ = R − R Ψ_k Ψ_k^⊤`), the effective weight is set to `W_ref + s·L̄R̄`, factors are synced to the vLLM rollout engine, rollouts are generated (K=16), the GRPO policy-optimization loss is computed, and only the raw factors are updated by AdamW. The frozen principal subspaces need no optimizer states, the learned update can be merged back into `W_ref` for inference, and the constraint is complementary (not redundant) to KL: KL controls policy change in output space, GCPO controls feasible directions of parameter change.

The diagnostic half decomposes `δW` into the four blocks `{δW_PP, δW_PO, δW_OP, δW_OO}` using the same projectors, defines `O_t = (E_PP + E_PO + E_OP)/E_total = 1 − E_OO/E_total`, and corrects for dimensionality via `O_excess = O_t − O_null`. The geometric facts (mutual orthogonality of the four blocks, isotropic null value, exact feasibility/subspace preservation, and that every feasible rank-r matrix admits the projected parameterization) are proven in Appendix B.

## Results & Highlights

- **Main accuracy (Table 1).** On Qwen3-8B MATH500, GCPO reaches 79.47±0.31 majority@16 vs the strongest baseline DAPO at 78.33±0.61 (+1.14) and the 67.46 base (+12.01); on GLM4-9B MATH500 it reaches 74.56±0.34 vs DAPO 72.41±0.82 (+2.15) and the 66.51 base. GCPO is best in all 6 model–task settings, improving over the base by 7.09–27.69 points and over the strongest per-task baseline by 1.02–2.37 points, and reports the lowest standard deviation in every setting.
- **Cross-task retention (Table 2).** After training only on MATH500 and evaluating on the held-out HumanEval+/ToolAlpaca, GRPO's ToolAlpaca accuracy collapses by −14.97 on GLM4-9B (and −5.81 on Qwen3-8B), whereas GCPO *improves* ToolAlpaca (+0.91 on GLM4-9B, +1.03 on Qwen3-8B) and achieves the best Worst-Δ on both backbones. GRPO-LoRA with the same rank only matches GCPO on retention but loses on the main task, confirming the directional constraint is what matters.
- **Controlled intervention (Figure 2).** Rescaling the principal-overlap component of the step-150 GRPO update on Qwen3-8B ToolAlpaca (56.89% at η=1) yields a dose-dependent accuracy drop; removing the overlap improves accuracy while matched principal-subspace injection is substantially more harmful than a matched random-subspace intervention.
- **Ablation of the constraint (Table 3, MATH500/GLM4-9B).** Bilateral projection (74.56) beats Left-only (73.49) and Right-only (73.56) and far beats unconstrained (72.34). Routing updates *into* the Principal subspace collapses accuracy to 62.59 vs 74.56 for the Orthogonal complement and 66.47 for Random projection — the specific orthogonal structure, not any constraint, is what helps. Hard projection (74.56) beats Soft orthogonality loss (71.11) and KL regularization (67.83); performance peaks at k=8 (Figure 7), with larger k over-restricting the feasible space.
- **Training dynamics.** GCPO yields a smooth, monotonically rising accuracy curve vs GRPO's severe oscillations (Figure 3), a gradual entropy decay avoiding both GRPO oscillation and the premature entropy collapse of DAPO/GSPO/GMPO (Figure 4), and the strongest suppression of response-length inflation on MATH500 (Figure 5). Peak per-GPU memory (68.19 GB) is essentially the same as LoRA and well below full-parameter GRPO (95.65 GB), so the geometry adds no memory cost (Figure 6).

## Authors & Credibility

The work comes from Shanghai Jiao Tong University, Shanghai AI Lab, NovaCore, and HUST (Yu Qiao of Shanghai AI Lab is a senior corresponding figure; equal-first authors Yang and Xu with Wang and Qiao as corresponding authors). The methodology is unusually careful for an RL-stability paper: three independent training seeds with bootstrap-estimated majority@16 (1,000 resamples), a fixed train/test split (seed 42, decoupled from training seeds), a matched-rank LoRA baseline to isolate the geometric constraint from rank reduction, and a controlled intervention that moves the central claim past pure correlation. The geometric lemmas and propositions (mutual orthogonality, isotropic null, exact feasibility, feasible-space dimension) are stated and proven in Appendix B. Code and datasets are promised at https://github.com/Icarus1411/GCPO. The authors repeatedly hedge appropriately — they label the overlap–degradation link "correlational" and note the layer-level preservation guarantee "does not imply unconditional preservation of all model-level capabilities."

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty is real but partial.** The idea of restricting updates to the orthogonal complement of dominant singular directions is not new — MiLoRA (Wang et al. 2025) harnesses minor components, TailLoR (Dragoi et al. 2026) protects principal components for continual learning, and Orthogonal Subspace Learning (Wang et al. 2023) does the same. The genuinely new pieces are (a) the *stepwise* excess-overlap diagnostic for on-policy rollout RL and (b) reframing the bilateral orthogonality as a *hard constraint in rollout RL* rather than as PEFT/continual-learning forgetting control. Readers familiar with that PEFT lineage may see GCPO as "MiLoRA-style projection ported to GRPO with a new diagnostic story."
- **The headline margins rest on very small held-out test sets.** The splits are 50 (MATH500), 17 (HumanEval+), and 68 (ToolAlpaca) examples. A 2.37-point gap on a 17-example HumanEval+ set is roughly 0.4 examples; although majority@16 and 3-seed std (0.27–0.71) tighten the estimate, the absolute test size is a genuine statistical fragility, and the authors openly state the numbers "should not be interpreted as official full-benchmark scores." External reproduction on the full benchmarks is needed before the gains are taken at face value.
- **The central causal claim is only locally supported.** The dose-dependent intervention is run on a single step/model/task in the main text (Qwen3-8B/ToolAlpaca/step 150); the "across models, tasks, and checkpoints" evidence is relegated to Appendix E and the overlap–degradation correlation is honestly flagged as correlational. The link between principal overlap and specific failure modes (length inflation, reward hacking) is explicitly stated as "not yet fully understood."
- **Scale and scope are limited.** Only 8B/9B instruct models, only three tasks, only on-policy GRPO family — the authors themselves list DPO/KTO/OPD, larger scale, and adaptive layer-wise k as open. No comparison with off-policy or self-distillation methods (Hübotter et al. 2026, Cai et al. 2026 are cited but only as related work).
- **The ToolAlpaca metric is static.** Correctness is action-name + merged-argument JSON matching with no tool execution and no action-order checking — a known-weak reward that inflates absolute numbers for *all* methods on that task (note GLM4-9B ToolAlpaca jumps from a 42.47 base to ~70), so tool-use conclusions should be read as relative, not as real-world tool competence.
- **One baseline actually degrades.** Full-parameter GRPO on GLM4-9B MATH500 drops to 59.43 (below the 66.51 base), which makes the "GRPO is unstable" story easy to tell on GLM4-9B but also shows the comparison field is partly set by a misbehaving baseline.

## Strengths

- The diagnostic-plus-intervention structure is the right way to motivate a method: observe, intervene causally, then constrain. The bilateral-orthogonality constraint is enforced *exactly* by construction (no penalty coefficient to tune), with formal guarantees in Appendix B.
- A clean, well-isolated ablation grid (Left/Right/Bilateral, Principal/Orthogonal/Random, Soft/Hard/KL) actually explains *why* each design choice matters rather than only reporting end-to-end wins.
- Practically attractive: same memory as LoRA, mergeable into base weights for inference, no extra rollout/loss machinery, and it simultaneously addresses three pain points (instability, length inflation, cross-task forgetting) that prior objective-level fixes treat separately.
- Strong cross-task retention story: GCPO is the only method that *improves* tool-use after math-only RL on both backbones, a property more valuable than a small in-domain gain.

## Limitations

- Validated only at 8B/9B scale, on three tasks, with small held-out subsets and a non-execution ToolAlpaca metric; generalization to frontier-scale models, full benchmarks, and real tool execution is unproven.
- The overlap→instability causal chain is supported by a single in-text intervention plus appendix sweeps; the link to length inflation / reward hacking is conjectural ("we hypothesize that shielding … length priors … reduces verbosity").
- Principal-subspace protection assumes the top-k singular directions are a meaningful structural reference; the authors are careful to call this a "structural proxy, not a literal partition of semantic knowledge," but the choice of k (peaked at 8) and which layers to protect are global hyperparameters rather than learned/adaptive.
- No comparison against off-policy or self-distillation post-training (DPO/KTO/OPD/on-policy distillation), which the authors list as future work.

## Relevance

GCPO 直接命中 on-policy rollout RL（GRPO 系）训练稳定性与策略优化的核心痛点，与 LLM 后训练、参数高效适配、训练动力学分析等研究方向高度相关；其"约束参数更新几何方向"的视角对改进 RLHF/RLVR 训练流程、缓解响应长度膨胀与跨任务能力退化具有直接的工程参考价值，是值得跟踪的方法论线索。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does principal-subspace overlap and GCPO's bilateral constraint transfer to off-policy / preference-based post-training (DPO, KTO, OPD) and to on-policy distillation, where the update distribution is not self-generated?
- How do the gains behave at larger scale (e.g., 30B–70B) and on the *full* MATH/HumanEval+/tool-execution benchmarks rather than the small held-out splits used here?
- Could the protected rank k be chosen adaptively per layer (e.g., based on spectral energy or per-layer overlap variance) instead of a global k=8, and would that widen the feasible space where the constraint currently over-restricts at large k?
- Is the suppression of response-length inflation a direct consequence of shielding length-prior directions, or a side effect of entropy regularization? A length-controlled ablation isolating verbosity from reward signal would clarify the mechanism.
- Given that GRPO itself degrades on GLM4-9B MATH500, how sensitive is the relative ranking of GCPO vs baselines to baseline hyperparameter tuning (learning rate, clipping) — are the baselines fairly optimized?
- Does the principal-subspace diagnostic predict *which* capabilities will degrade (e.g., the −14.97 tool-use collapse) early enough to enable a dynamic, overlap-triggered intervention rather than a permanent hard constraint?
