> **一句话总结：** 这篇巴伊兰大学的博士论文系统性地解决了深度学习系统置信度校准在"标签噪声、隐私约束、域漂移"三种现实场景下的失效问题，分别提出噪声感知温度缩放（NTS）、噪声感知共形预测（NACP）、本地差分隐私共形预测（LDP-CP）和无监督目标域校准（UTDC）四种方法，并在医学影像与自然图像数据集上证明这些方法能逼近使用干净标签的 Oracle 上界。

> **TL;DR:** A Bar-Ilan Ph.D. thesis (supervised by J. Goldberger) that makes calibration robust to four real-world failure modes: it introduces Noisy Temperature Scaling (NTS), which on ChestX-ray14/ResNet-50 at 20% label noise cuts adaECE from 11.81% (naive TS on noisy labels) to 1.52% (≈ clean-label oracle 1.63%); Noise-Aware Conformal Prediction (NACP), whose finite-sample correction term is independent of the number of classes k, so on ImageNet (1000 classes) it yields prediction-set size 20.9 at 91.9% coverage while prior methods ACNL/CRCP degenerate to size 1000 (all classes); the first Local-DP Conformal Prediction (LDP-CP-L/S), which with the shuffle model at ε=4 matches non-private coverage on TissueMNIST; and Unsupervised Target-Domain Calibration (UTDC), which on Office-home/CDAN+E achieves avg adaECE 7.93% versus 24.36% (CPCS) and 22.09% (TransCal), approaching the labeled-oracle 5.22%.

## 核心贡献

- **噪声标签下的置信度校准（NTS）。** 提出噪声感知温度缩放（Noisy Temperature Scaling）。关键洞察是：adaECE 中只有每个置信度区间的平均准确率受噪声影响，置信度项不变，因此只需估计"区间平均噪声"而非逐样本噪声。利用噪声转移矩阵 P 把含噪混淆矩阵反演回干净准确率，从而把任意 adaECE 驱动的后验校准（TS / VS / MS / Mix-n-Match）变成噪声鲁棒版本。
- **噪声标签下的共形预测（NRSCP + NACP）。** 提出"噪声鲁棒评分 CP"和"噪声感知 CP"。核心理论贡献：NACP 在均匀噪声下的有限样本修正项 Δ = √(log(4/δ)/(2nh²))，其中 h=(1−ε)/(1+ε)，**不依赖于类别数 k**，而竞争方法 ACNL 的 Δ=O(log k)、CRCP 的 Δ=O(√k) 都随 k 爆炸。这是其在 CIFAR-100 / TinyImageNet / ImageNet 等多类任务上唯一不退化的方法。
- **本地差分隐私共形预测（LDP-CP-L / LDP-CP-S）。** 首次将共形预测与本地差分隐私结合。LDP-CP-L 用 k-ary 随机响应扰动标签（k-RR 恰好是一个已知噪声信道，可直接套用 NACP 框架，保护标签但不需用户算力）；LDP-CP-S 让用户本地算评分再经随机响应上报（同时保护特征与标签，且性能与 k 无关）。配合 shuffle 模型可实现隐私放大（ε_eff ≈ ε/√n）。
- **无监督目标域校准（UTDC）。** 指出所有基于源域重要性的 UDA 校准方法（CPCS、TransCal）因高估目标域准确率而失效。UTDC 直接在目标域上算置信度，用无标签目标域准确率估计器（Meta/ATC/PN）把源域逐区间准确率按 Ã_target/A_source 比例缩放，再最小化 UDA-adaECE。是首个有效的 UDA 校准方法。
- **统一的"噪声信道即校准信息"视角。** 论文反复用同一个数学骨架：含噪观测可视为一个已知噪声信道（人为标签错误、k-RR 隐私机制、域漂移导致的准确率偏移），只要能估计信道参数就能反演回干净的覆盖/校准统计量。NTS、NACP、LDP-CP 共享 P⁻¹ 反卷积或 (F̂_n−εF̂_r)/(1−ε) 的修正公式。

## Key Contributions

- **Noisy Temperature Scaling (NTS).** A post-hoc calibration framework robust to noisy validation labels. It observes that only the per-bin average accuracy term of adaECE is corrupted by label noise (confidence terms are label-independent), so one only needs to estimate the *average* noise per bin, not per-sample correctness. The noisy confusion matrix M̃_i = M_i·P is inverted via the estimated noise transition matrix P to recover clean bin accuracy, turning any adaECE-minimizing calibrator (TS, Vector/Matrix Scaling, Mix-n-Match) into a noise-robust variant.
- **Noise-Aware / Noise-Robust Conformal Prediction (NACP, NRSCP).** Two complementary CP algorithms for noisy calibration labels. NRSCP defines a noise-corrected conformity score Ŝ = (1−ε)S(x,ỹ)+ε·S̄(x); NACP directly estimates the clean-coverage CDF F̂_c(q) = (F̂_n(q) − εF̂_r(q))/(1−ε) via grid search. The headline theoretical result is a finite-sample correction Δ that is **independent of the number of classes k**, which is why NACP is the only method that remains non-degenerate on ImageNet (1000 classes).
- **Local Differential Private Conformal Prediction (LDP-CP-L, LDP-CP-S).** The first framework for conformal prediction under local differential privacy. LDP-CP-L perturbs labels with k-ary randomized response (protecting labels while keeping scoring centralized / model-secret); LDP-CP-S has users compute and privatize conformity scores locally (protecting both features and labels, k-independent). Both come with ε-LDP coverage guarantees, and the shuffle model gives privacy amplification to ε_eff ≈ ε/√n.
- **Unsupervised Target Domain Calibration (UTDC).** Shows that importance-weighting UDA calibrators (CPCS, TransCal) fail because they implicitly trust the over-optimistic source-domain accuracy. UTDC computes confidence directly on the unlabeled target data and rescales source per-bin accuracy by the ratio Ã_target/A_source obtained from a label-free target-accuracy estimator (Meta/ATC/Projection-Norm), then minimizes UDA-adaECE. First effective calibrator for the unsupervised-domain-adaptation setting.
- **A unifying noise-channel view.** Across chapters, human annotation error, the k-RR privacy mechanism, and domain-shift-induced accuracy drop are all treated as known/estimable noise channels; the same P⁻¹ deconvolution or coverage-CDF correction recovers clean calibration statistics, yielding four published methods from one principle.

## Methodology

The thesis is a methods + theory + empirical contribution organized into four self-contained chapters (3–6), each backed by a peer-reviewed publication (TMI 2024, ISBI 2024, MICCAI 2024 workshop, ECCV 2024 workshop, COPA 2025 x2).

**Chapter 3 — NTS.** For uniform label noise with rate ε, the authors derive that the noisy bin accuracy Ã_i ≈ (1−ε)Â_i + (ε/(k−1))(1−Â_i), invertible to Â_i = (Ã_i − ε/(k−1)) / ((1−ε) − ε/(k−1)). Substituting this corrected accuracy into adaECE gives "Noisy-adaECE"; temperature is then found by minimizing it. For a general noise matrix P, the same result is recovered from M̃_i = M_i·P ⇒ Â_i = Tr(M̃_i P⁻¹). Training uses Li et al.'s noise-adaptation-layer method (which jointly yields the network and an estimate of P), so the end-to-end pipeline needs no clean labels at all.

**Chapter 4 — NACP/NRSCP.** Two angles on noisy-label CP. (1) NRSCP replaces the unobservable score S(x,y) with its conditional expectation Ŝ(x,ỹ,ε) = (1−ε)S(x,ỹ)+ε·S̄(x) and forms the prediction set with the *exact* test-time score S(x,y). (2) NACP models the clean/noisy/random-label CDFs via F_n(q) = (1−ε)F_c(q)+εF_r(q), estimates F̂_n and F̂_r from the noisy set, and solves F̂_c(q)=1−α by grid search between provable bounds q1,q2 (Theorem 4.3.2). Coverage is guaranteed by a DKW-inequality-based Δ (Lemma 4.3.4) that depends on n and h=(1−ε)/(1+ε) but not on k — the decisive advantage over ACNL (O(log k)) and CRCP (O(√k)).

**Chapter 5 — LDP-CP.** Recognizes that k-ary randomized response is itself a known uniform-noise channel, so NACP directly gives an ε-LDP label-private calibrator (LDP-CP-L). For full feature+label privacy, LDP-CP-S has each user compute S(x,y) locally and report a randomized-response bit against an aggregator-chosen threshold in a disjoint-subset binary search for the (1−α)-quantile (Algorithm 8); privacy follows because data is touched only through RR. The shuffle model amplifies ε to ε_eff = ε/√n.

**Chapter 6 — UTDC.** Bin source validation data by confidence, compute per-bin source accuracy A_{source,m}; estimate overall target accuracy Ã_target without labels; set Ã_{target,m} = A_{source,m}·(Ã_target/A_source); minimize UDA-adaECE(T) over temperature using target-domain confidence C_{target,m}(T). The method hinges on the empirically verified assumption that the source/target accuracy ratio is roughly constant across confidence bins.

## Results & Highlights

- **NTS recovers clean-label calibration quality under 20% uniform noise.** On PathMNIST/ResNet-50 (adaECE, 15 bins, ε=0.2), naive TS on the noisy validation set (TS_noisy) yields **13.00%** adaECE, while NTS yields **2.42%** — essentially matching the clean-label oracle TSclean at **2.23%** and far below uncalibrated 11.50%. On ChestX-ray14/ResNet-50 the gap is larger: TS_noisy **11.81%** vs NTS **1.52%** vs TSclean **1.63%** (Table 3.1). The NTS-vs-TS_noisy superiority is statistically significant (p-value = 0.005).
- **NACP is the only non-degenerate method on many-class CP.** On ImageNet (1000 classes, APS score, 1−α=0.9, ε=0.2), Noisy-CP returns prediction-set size **502.6** at 99.9% coverage; ACNL and CRCP both collapse to size **1000.0 / 100.0% coverage** (i.e., all classes — useless); NACP returns size **20.9** at **91.9%** coverage, close to the clean oracle (size 16.6, 90.0%). The finite-sample correction term Δ for NACP on ImageNet is **0.019** versus ACNL **0.466** and CRCP **0.177** (Tables 4.2, 4.3).
- **NACP works on real human-annotation noise.** On CIFAR-10N-random-3 (19.6% real annotator error, HPS score), Noisy-CP gives coverage 96.31% with size 2.16; modeling the noise as uniform, NACP brings size down to **1.53** at **92.18%** coverage — closer to the 90% target without the inflated over-coverage (Table 4.6).
- **LDP-CP-L matches non-private CP under the shuffle model.** On TissueMNIST (HPS, ε=4, ε_eff=0.026), non-private CP achieves coverage 90.00% (size 5.55); LDP-CP-L achieves **89.97%** coverage (size 5.54) — statistically indistinguishable — while satisfying ε-LDP. LDP-CP-S catches up for ε_eff ≥ 0.03 (Table 5.1, Fig 5.6).
- **UTDC dominates all UDA calibration baselines.** On Office-home with CDAN+E (avg over 9 tasks), UTDC achieves adaECE **7.93%** versus Source-TS 16.73%, CPCS 24.36%, and TransCal 22.09%, approaching the labeled-target oracle Target-TS at **5.22%** (Table 6.2). On the harder VisDA S→R benchmark, UTDC averages **7.84%** versus Source-TS 34.29% and TransCal 38.72% (Table 6.4). Across Office-home/Office-31/VisDA/DomainNet, UTDC-Meta estimates target accuracy within |Δ|≈3.3% of truth (Table 6.9), and calibration is robust to the choice of accuracy estimator (ATC/PN variants stay within ~1–3 adaECE points, Table 6.8).
- **Calibration is far more noise-fragile than training.** A recurring empirical finding: 20% label noise barely dents accuracy (e.g., ChestX-ray14 drops from 65.13% to 64.71%) but destroys standard TS calibration (adaECE inflates from ~4% to ~12%).

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** High and well-scoped. The k-independence of NACP's finite-sample Δ (Lemma 4.3.4) is the single strongest theoretical insight and directly explains the empirical dominance on ImageNet/CIFAR-100 where every competing baseline (ACNL, CRCP) degenerates to returning all 1000 classes. LDP-CP is, by the authors' own literature scan, the first local-DP conformal predictor (the closest prior work, Angelopoulos et al.'s "Private prediction sets," assumes a *trusted* central aggregator — a strictly easier setting). NTS and UTDC are more incremental (clean matrix inversions of known adaECE), but they target genuinely under-studied problems: the authors correctly note there was *no* prior work on calibrating from noisy validation labels or on effective UDA calibration.
- **Rigor:** Strong. Coverage guarantees are proven (Theorems 4.3.5, 4.3.6, 5.4.1, 5.4.2) with explicit DKW-based finite-sample terms; results are averaged over 1000 random splits (CP) or 3 seeds (NTS) with std reported; statistical significance (p=0.005) is reported for NTS. The honest admission that "current coverage guarantee bounds are too conservative" — and that NACP already meets target coverage empirically *without* the Δ correction — is commendable rather than a red flag.
- **A genuine weakness — estimated noise matrix on imbalanced data.** Table 3.3 shows NTS( P̂ ) (estimated matrix) *fails* on heavily imbalanced ChestX-ray14 at ε=20% (adaECE 7.28%, worse than some settings of TS_noisy) precisely because "in ChestX-ray14 the classes are unbalanced, and in that case there is currently no reliable way to estimate the noise matrix." The method only recovers on the balanced ChestX-ray14-bal variant. The authors disclose this, but it limits applicability: real medical datasets are routinely imbalanced, and the noise-matrix estimator (Li et al.) is doing unacknowledged heavy lifting.
- **Missing ablations / cherry-picking concerns.** (1) The CP experiments fix δ=0.001 but never sweep it; the claim that the un-adjusted NACP "reaches the desired coverage" is empirical and would benefit from a coverage-vs-n curve at fixed α. (2) NACP's uniform-noise coverage guarantee provably does *not* hold for general noise matrices (the authors concede "in the case of a general noise matrix, all finite sample correction terms are not effective"), yet Table 4.4 still reports good general-matrix numbers *without* the Δ term — i.e., without the very guarantee the theory provides. The real-noise CIFAR-10N results (Table 4.6) rely on *approximating* annotator noise as uniform, which the authors flag as an approximation but do not bound.
- **LDP-CP comparability.** There is no prior LDP-CP baseline, so Table 5.1 chiefly compares against the non-private oracle and the two starred (Δ-corrected) variants of the authors' own methods. The strong LDP-CP-L numbers (≈ non-private) hinge on the shuffle model's ε_eff = ε/√n amplification, which assumes an honest-but-curious shuffler — an assumption the thesis does not deeply interrogate.
- **Reproducibility caveat:** UTDC's headline numbers depend on the Meta target-accuracy estimator (Deng et al.); Tables 6.8–6.9 show ATC and PN variants degrade by 1–4 adaECE points, so the reported averages are somewhat estimator-dependent.
- **Significance:** Substantive for the calibration/uncertainty-quantification community and directly relevant to medical-imaging deployment. The k-independent CP bound in particular is a result others will build on.

## Strengths

- A single coherent "noise channel = calibration signal" principle yields four methods and a unified mathematical treatment (P⁻¹ deconvolution recurs across NTS, NACP, LDP-CP).
- The k-independence of NACP's Δ is a clean, decisive theoretical win, and the ImageNet/CIFAR-100/TinyImageNet experiments make the practical consequence unambiguous (competitors return all classes; NACP does not).
- LDP-CP opens a genuinely new problem (local-DP conformal prediction) and gives a usable privacy-coverage trade-off, especially with the shuffle-model amplification.
- UTDC exposes and empirically substantiates a real flaw in the UDA-calibration literature (importance-weighting trusts an over-optimistic source accuracy), and proposes a simple, effective fix that approaches the labeled oracle.
- Honest reporting: negative results (NTS( P̂ ) failure on imbalanced ChestX-ray14), conservative-bound admissions, and std/split-count reporting are all present. Code is public.

## Limitations

- The noise-matrix estimation that NTS end-to-end depends on is unreliable for imbalanced classes — a common condition in medical imaging — so the strongest end-to-end results are confined to balanced subsets (ChestX-ray14-bal).
- NACP's class-independent finite-sample guarantee is proved only for **uniform** noise; for general noise matrices the correction terms are "not effective," and the good general-matrix empirical numbers are reported *without* the theoretical Δ.
- All noise models assume corruption is **independent of the input features x**; instance-dependent/feature-dependent noise is explicitly left as future work.
- LDP-CP's strongest results rely on the shuffle model and an honest-but-curious shuffler; without shuffling, usable privacy requires ε ≥ 3, which is a weak guarantee.
- UTDC inherits the error of whatever target-accuracy estimator it plugs in, and assumes the source/target accuracy ratio is constant across confidence bins (shown on a few tasks, not proven).
- Evaluation is confined to classification; regression, segmentation, and source-free adaptation are flagged as open.
- As a thesis, the four threads are related by theme rather than by a single integrated system; there is no joint experiment combining, e.g., noisy labels + domain shift + privacy.

## Relevance

这篇论文与"模型不确定性量化和可信部署"的研究兴趣高度相关：它系统性地解决了置信度校准在标签噪声、域漂移、隐私约束下的失效问题，提供了可直接用于医学影像等安全关键场景的噪声鲁棒共形预测（NACP）和无监督域适应校准（UTDC）方法，其"有限样本修正项不依赖类别数"的理论结果对大规模分类任务的不确定性量化尤其有价值。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the k-independent finite-sample Δ of NACP be extended to *general* (non-uniform) noise matrices, or is the uniform-noise restriction fundamental to the DKW-based proof?
- How does NACP behave under instance-dependent / feature-dependent label noise (common in medical imaging where ambiguous images are mislabeled more often), and can a feature-conditional noise matrix be estimated without anchor points?
- For LDP-CP, is there a method that keeps features private *without* requiring users to run the full model locally (avoiding the IP/compute concerns of LDP-CP-S), perhaps via secure aggregation or functional encryption?
- Does the UTDC bin-ratio assumption (Ã_target,m / A_source,m ≈ const) hold for regression/segmentation or for larger domain gaps (e.g., synthetic→real autonomous driving), and can UTDC be extended to source-free domain adaptation where source data is unavailable at calibration time?
- Could the four methods be composed — e.g., private, noise-robust CP on a target domain — and does the coverage guarantee survive the composition, or do the individual Δ terms compound pessimistically?
- The authors note current Δ bounds are empirically over-conservative; can tighter, distribution-aware (rather than DKW worst-case) finite-sample bounds be derived for NACP/LDP-CP to reduce prediction-set size at guaranteed coverage?
