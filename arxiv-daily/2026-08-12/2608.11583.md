> **一句话总结：** 通过将安全对齐模型的权重移植到同架构的未对齐基座模型中，本文发现 LLM 的拒答行为主要由 MLP 权重（而非注意力权重）承载，并高度集中在第 8–11 层（Block 3）这一中层区域；更重要的是，安全相关 MLP 块的组合是非加性的——选定的子集在恶意拒答与良性过拒答两项指标上常常优于全 MLP 移植。

> **TL;DR:** By transplanting aligned-model weights into matched unaligned bases across two model pairs (RealSafe-R1-7B/DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B and saferlhf_ultra_sft/Llama-3.1-8B) and four benchmarks (TwinPrompt, SGXSTest, AdvBench, OR-Bench), the authors show transferable refusal is dominated by MLP weights (≥2.7× over attention across all six benchmark settings), concentrated in mid-network Block 3 (layers 8–11, selected first in all six greedy searches), and composes non-additively — a 6-block subset beats full-MLP transplant on RealSafe-R1-7B/TwinPrompt with MR 24/30 vs 19/30 and BOR 9/30 vs 11/30.

## 核心贡献

- **通路集中（Pathway Concentration）：** 在两对模型族上系统比较"替换全部注意力权重"与"替换全部 MLP 权重"两类混合模型，证明可迁移的拒答行为主要由 MLP 通路承载。在六组恶意基准对比中，仅替换 MLP 带来的拒答数至少是仅替换注意力的 **2.7 倍**。
- **深度定位（Depth Localization）：** 把 MLP 堆叠切成 4 层一组的块，发现跨越 Qwen2-7B 与 Llama-3.1-8B 两种架构，**第 8–11 层（Block 3）** 一致地是最强的单一移植块；在全部六次贪心前向选择（每对模型-数据集各一次）中，Block 3 都被第一个选中。块内逐层分析进一步指向第 8 层。
- **非加性交互（Non-Additive Interactions）：** 贪心轨迹表明安全相关块并非独立贡献——六条轨迹中有五条出现"负边际效应"，即加入更多对齐块反而降低拒答；选定的块子集在恶意拒答、良性过拒答或两者上常常优于全 MLP 移植。
- **精度-覆盖权衡的实证：** 将不同源基准导出的贪心序迁移到 OR-Bench，发现 SGXSTest 序过拒答更低但恶意拒答也较低，AdvBench 序更激进但过拒答更高——源基准的选择实质影响下游部署的安全精度。
- **大样本验证：** 在 RealSafe-R1-7B 上构建 100 条恶意 + 100 条良性的过滤子集，复现了上述三条主要规律（MLP 主导、Block 3 最强、非单调合成），缓解了小样本导致的偶然性担忧。

## Key Contributions

- Establishes, via controlled weight transplantation between architecturally identical aligned/base pairs, that transferable refusal behavior is mediated primarily by the **MLP pathway** rather than attention, with MLP-only hybrids beating attention-only hybrids by **at least 2.7×** in malicious-refusal count across all six benchmark settings.
- Identifies a consistent **mid-network depth locus** (Block 3 = layers 8–11) as the single strongest transplantation block in both a 28-layer Qwen2 model and a 32-layer Llama-3.1 model; Block 3 is the first block chosen in every one of the six greedy searches.
- Demonstrates that aligned MLP blocks **compose non-additively**: in five of six greedy trajectories adding another aligned block can reduce malicious refusal, and selective 6-block subsets outperform full-MLP transplantation on malicious refusal, benign over-refusal, or both.
- Shows that refusal and over-refusal are **co-localized in MLP weights** (RealSafe-R1-7B full-MLP transplant yields both 27/30 AdvBench refusals and 11/30 TwinPrompt over-refusals, vs 0/30 over-refusals for attention-only), so precision is not achievable by simply transplanting more aligned parameters.
- Empirically characterizes a **benchmark-dependent precision–coverage trade-off**: greedy orders derived from SGXSTest transfer to lower OR-Bench over-refusal, while AdvBench-derived orders are more aggressive, informing deployment-relevant block-selection decisions.
- Reproduces all three core patterns on a substantially larger (100/100) filtered paired-prompt validation set for RealSafe-R1-7B, ruling out small-sample artifacts.

## Methodology

The paper uses **selective weight transplantation** between two architecturally matched open-weight pairs:

- **RealSafe-R1-7B** (aligned) vs **DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B** (base) — 28-layer Qwen2.
- **saferlhf_ultra_sft** (aligned) vs **Llama-3.1-8B** (base) — 32-layer Llama.

Because each pair shares architecture, any behavioral difference between a hybrid and its base can be attributed directly to the transplanted weights. Hybrids are constructed at several granularities:

1. **Component level** — `attn` (replace q/k/v/o projections across all layers) vs `mlp` (replace gate/up/down projections).
2. **Contiguous layer groups** — `first5`, `mid5`, `last5`, `first_half`, `second_half` (both attention and MLP).
3. **MLP blocks** — partition the MLP stack into 4-layer blocks (B1=0–3 … B7=24–27 for the 28-layer model; B1 … B8=28–31 for the 32-layer model) and transplant individual blocks or combinations.

Evaluation uses four benchmarks probing complementary safety axes: **TwinPrompt** and **SGXSTest** (paired harmful + benign), **AdvBench** (harmful only), and **OR-Bench** (benign over-refusal stress test). Subsets are constructed by retaining only prompts where the aligned model refuses **and** the base model complies (up to 30 per condition), sharpening the behavioral contrast. A separate 100/100 paired-prompt pool is built for the larger validation.

Two metrics are reported, both **manually inspected**: **MR** (malicious-prompt refusal count; higher better) and **BOR** (benign over-refusal count; lower better). Generation uses temperature 0.6, 1200 tokens for filtering and 2000 for response generation.

To study block interactions, the authors run **greedy forward selection** per model–dataset pair: starting from the base, iteratively add the MLP block giving the largest MR gain (ties broken by safe non-refusal count), yielding both a per-dataset importance ordering and the best subset at each budget k.

## Results & Highlights

- **MLP dominates attention ≥2.7×.** On RealSafe-R1-7B, the `mlp` hybrid refuses **19/30 (63.3%)** on TwinPrompt, **17/30 (56.7%)** on SGXSTest, and **27/30 (90.0%)** on AdvBench, versus the `attn` hybrid's **5/30 (16.7%)**, **2/30 (6.7%)**, and **10/30 (33.3%)** — MLP beats attention by at least 2.7× in all six comparisons. The 100-prompt validation amplifies the gap: `mlp` = **61/100** vs `attn` = **8/100** malicious refusals.
- **Block 3 (layers 8–11) is the consistent mid-network locus.** It is the strongest individual block on TwinPrompt and AdvBench for both model families and among the top on SGXSTest; on the 100-prompt validation it refuses **13/100** malicious prompts (highest of seven blocks) while causing only **1/100** benign over-refusal. Block 3 is selected first in **all six greedy searches**.
- **Within Block 3, layer 8 stands out.** Single-layer transplant of layer 8 yields the most refusals inside Block 3 on all three RealSafe-R1-7B benchmarks (3/5/6 refusals on TwinPrompt/SGXS/AdvBench), and subsets of Block 3 layers can match or exceed full-Block-3 performance (e.g., layers 8+10+11 reach 10/30 on AdvBench, equal to the full block).
- **Non-additive composition — subsets beat full MLP.** On RealSafe-R1-7B/TwinPrompt the greedy trajectory rises from MR 7 (k=1) to **MR 24/30 (80%)** at k=6, then **drops to 19/30 (63.3%)** at k=7 (= full MLP); the 6-block model also has lower BOR (9/30 vs 11/30). On saferlhf_ultra_sft/SGXSTest the contrast is sharper: 6-block reaches **MR 8/30 with BOR 1/18**, vs full 8-block **MR 4/30 with BOR 3/18**. Five of six trajectories show a negative marginal effect.
- **Refusal and over-refusal co-localize in MLP.** RealSafe-R1-7B full-MLP transplant produces **11/30 (36.7%)** TwinPrompt over-refusals while attention-only produces **0/30** — the same pathway that transfers the most refusal also transfers the most over-refusal. The 100-prompt validation reproduces this: `mlp` BOR = **33/100** vs ≤7 for all other configurations.
- **Larger-subset validation fully reproduces the three patterns.** On 100 malicious prompts, greedy MR climbs 13 → 29 → 42 → 52 → 54 → **64** at k=6, then falls to **61** at k=7 (full MLP), reconfirming non-additivity at scale.
- **Benchmark choice affects OR-Bench transfer.** For RealSafe-R1-7B, the SGXSTest-derived order attains the lowest or tied-lowest BOR at five of seven budgets (k=7: SGXSTest 3 vs AdvBench 6); saferlhf_ultra_sft with the SGXSTest order maintains zero over-refusal through most k values, whereas AdvBench orders are more aggressive.

## Authors & Credibility

The three authors (Mingyu Zong, Sampad Mohanty, Bhaskar Krishnamachari) are based at USC's Computer Science and Electrical & Computer Engineering departments; Krishnamachari directs the ANRG lab, and the work is funded by the **USC Amazon Center on Secure and Trusted Machine Learning** under a project explicitly titled "Towards Resilient LLM Alignment: Investigating Brittleness & Improving Model Safety by Rank Enhancement." Code and data are publicly released at `https://github.com/ANRGUSC/Localizing-Safety-Alignment`. The methodology is conservative and well-matched to the question: using architecturally identical aligned/base pairs isolates the weight-space variable, and all refusal labels are manually inspected rather than relying on an automated judge (a common weak point in safety evaluations). The paper transparently discloses AI-tool use (Claude Code, ChatGPT, Gemini) for coding and proofreading. The framing correctly positions the work as a complement to activation-level interpretability (Arditi et al.'s refusal direction, Wollschlager et al.'s cones) rather than a competitor, and it cites the closely related Wei et al. 2024 pruning/low-rank study whose ~3%-of-parameters sparsity finding this work independently corroborates at the MLP-vs-attention level.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Genuine but incremental. The MLP-over-attention finding for safety overlaps heavily with Wei et al. 2024, which the authors themselves cite as reporting that "safety and utility appear more differentiated in MLP layers than in attention layers." The genuinely new contributions are (a) the specific mid-network depth locus at layers 8–11 across two architectures, and (b) the systematic non-additivity / negative-marginal-effect demonstration via greedy selection. The depth claim is the most novel and most useful result.
- **Rigor:** Strong on internal validity (matched pairs, manual label inspection, greedy + larger-subset replication) but **weak on statistical power**. The main experiments use subsets of at most 30 prompts per condition; with refusal counts in the single digits, many of the "Block X beats Block Y" comparisons in Tables 3–4 and the tie-breaking decisions in the greedy search are within noise (e.g., SGXSTest single-block values for RealSafe-R1-7B are 0,0,3,0,3,2,3 — differences of 1–3 on n=30 are not distinguishable). The 100-prompt validation helps but is run only on RealSafe-R1-7B and only on the authors' own constructed paired-prompt pool, not on a standard unfiltered benchmark distribution — a generalization gap they acknowledge.
- **Reproducibility:** Good in principle (public code, public models, deterministic greedy procedure, fixed generation config). However, the subset-construction step depends on running both the aligned and base models to identify aligned-refuses/base-complies prompts, and the paired-prompt pool for the 100/100 validation is the authors' own construction with unspecified exact contents, making the headline numbers hard to compare against externally.
- **Significance:** Practically relevant for alignment robustness research — the localization result gives a concrete mechanistic hook for why fine-tuning degrades safety and for designing more redundant alignment. But the second model family (saferlhf_ultra_sft) is dramatically weaker: most individual blocks induce ≤2 refusals, so several of the "consistent across both families" claims rest heavily on the RealSafe-R1-7B pair, weakening the cross-architecture generality.
- **Red flags / overclaiming:**
  - The 2.7× "lower bound" is computed from ratios where the attention baseline is sometimes 0/30 — division by near-zero inflates the ratio; the more honest framing is "attention transfers almost nothing, MLP transfers a lot," not a precise 2.7× multiplier.
  - The recurring "layers 8–11" coincidence across a 28-layer and a 32-layer model is striking but n=2; calling it a "consistent mid-network concentration" generalizes from two data points, and the authors do not test whether it is really *depth fraction* (≈29–39% of depth) rather than *absolute layer index*.
  - The saferlhf_ultra_sft Block-3-layer analysis is dropped ("fails to reveal valuable information") because effects are too small — a mild survivorship bias in which family gets the fine-grained treatment.
  - No ablation on block size (why 4 layers?), no comparison to random block subsets as a lower-bound baseline, and greedy forward selection is explicitly acknowledged as only approximating the combinatorial space.

## Strengths

- Clean experimental design: architecturally identical aligned/base pairs make the attribution to transplanted weights unambiguous, avoiding confounds that plague cross-model comparisons.
- Manual inspection of every refusal label is unusually rigorous for an LLM-safety empirical paper and avoids the failure mode of an over-permissive or over-strict automated judge.
- The non-additivity / negative-marginal-effect finding is the most thought-provoking contribution: it directly contradicts the naive "more aligned weights = safer" intuition and has clear implications for surgical safety edits and unlearning.
- Honest about limitations: the paper explicitly flags the n=2-model-pairs scope, the filtered-subset construction, and the within-distribution (not unfiltered-distribution) nature of the validation.
- Connects weight-space localization to deployment-relevant concerns (benchmark-dependent precision–coverage trade-off, co-localization of refusal and over-refusal), giving practitioners actionable framing rather than purely mechanistic curiosity.

## Limitations

- Only two model pairs, both in the 7B–8B regime; no test of whether the layers-8–11 locus is an absolute-layer or relative-depth property, and no coverage of MoE/sparse architectures or larger scales.
- Primary subsets are small (≤30 prompts) and filtered to aligned-refuses/base-complies cases; statistical significance of many block-ranking claims is doubtful, and the filtering biases the evaluation toward exactly the prompts where alignment matters, not the general prompt distribution.
- The saferlhf_ultra_sft family is so weak at block-level transplantation (mostly 0–2 refusals) that it provides only weak corroboration; several fine-grained analyses (Block-3-layer decomposition, larger-subset validation) are run only on RealSafe-R1-7B.
- No exhaustive or randomized search over block combinations — greedy forward selection is known to miss interactions, and the authors acknowledge this without bounding how suboptimal their orderings might be.
- No ablation on block size (4-layer choice is unjustified), no random-subset lower-bound baseline, and the 100/100 validation pool is author-constructed with contents not fully specified, limiting external comparability.
- The study localizes *where* safety is stored but not *how* it is computed; the authors correctly flag the need for follow-up activation-patching / causal-tracing work, which is not done here.

## Relevance

高度相关于 LLM 安全与可解释性研究方向：论文从权重空间角度精确定位了拒答行为在 MLP 中层（第 8–11 层）的集中性及其非加性组合规律，为理解对齐脆弱性、设计外科手术式的安全编辑与更鲁棒的冗余对齐方法提供了直接的机制性证据与可操作的分析框架。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the layers-8–11 locus generalize as an **absolute depth** or a **relative depth fraction** (~30–40% into the network)? Testing 1B, 13B, 70B, and MoE models would disambiguate.
- Would a randomized or exhaustive block-combination search find subsets that beat the greedy-derived ones by a larger margin, or reveal structured higher-order interactions the greedy ordering obscures?
- Can the non-additivity be predicted from **representation-space** structure (e.g., do blocks that interfere share overlapping refusal directions in Wollschlager-style cones)?
- Is the co-localization of refusal and over-refusal in MLP weights breakable — i.e., is there a block subset that maximizes MR while keeping BOR near zero on an **unfiltered** prompt distribution?
- How do these localized weights interact with **activation-level** interventions (Arditi's single direction, CAST, LED)? Combining weight transplantation with activation patching could separate storage from computation of refusal.
- Does targeted fine-tuning or unlearning applied *only* to Block 3 degrade safety faster than uniform updates, as the "brittleness from localization" hypothesis predicts?
