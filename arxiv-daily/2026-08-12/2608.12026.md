> **一句话总结：** 把 softmax 输出头量化建模为 KL 散度下的率失真问题，推导出"按类别频率×类别方差"分配比特率的方法 SoftWater；在 1B–32B 五个模型、2/3/4-bit 头设置的 60 个测试点中赢下 59 个，2-bit 头将量化体模型的整体存储再压缩 45–60%，困惑度仅升 2.9–3.7%。

> **TL;DR:** SoftWater reframes quantizing the LLM softmax head (which holds 15–30% of params in small models but is usually left in fp16) as a rate-distortion problem under output KL divergence, and allocates finer bit-grids to frequent, low-variance classes via a separable second-order surrogate. It beats the released WaterSIC quantizer (run without finetuning) on 59 of 60 head-only cells across five 1B–32B models — at 2 bits it cuts head-induced WT2 KL by 6.5×–8.3× and on a fully-quantized Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct body a 2-bit tied head removes 45–60% of stored bytes for only a 2.9–3.7% WT2 perplexity increase (vs 9.7–17.6% for WaterSIC).

## 核心贡献

- **问题形式化：** 首次将 softmax 输出层的量化建模为 KL 散度下的率失真问题。对 KL 做二阶泰勒展开得到误差权重 E[(diag(p) − ppᵀ) ⊗ XXᵀ]，再用 dither 论证 + 可分性假设把它降为一个"按类别重缩放"的格点问题，所需两类统计量在一次前向标定中即可读出。
- **SoftWater 算法：** 类别侧尺度 λ̄ₖ = E[pₖ(1−pₖ)] = E[pₖ](1−E[pₖ]) − Var[pₖ] 即"类别频率被跨上下文方差折扣后的值"。因此高频、低方差类得到细网格，低频类得到粗网格——在 Zipf 分布的词表上差异巨大。引入平滑先验 p̃ = (1−ε)p + ε/K（默认 ε=0.1）给标定中未覆盖类设下网格间距上限，ε=1 时恰好退化为 WaterSIC。
- **头部量化结果：** 在 1B–32B 的五个模型上，2/3/4-bit 头设置共 60 个测试点中 SoftWater 赢下 59 个，且 2-bit SoftWater 头在 WT2 KL 上已超过 3-bit WaterSIC 头（"省下一比特"）；在已量化 body 上能再压缩整体模型 45–60%。类别侧统计量携带领域信息，使头部可针对部署域标定。

## Key Contributions

- **Problem formulation.** Poses softmax-layer quantization as a rate-distortion problem under output KL and derives the induced error metric. A per-entry dither argument plus a separability assumption (validated empirically to within 10% proxy error) reduce the Kn × Kn coupling to a class-rescaled lattice whose two statistics (Σ_X and λ̄) are read off a single calibration forward pass, with no backward pass.
- **The SoftWater algorithm.** Allocates grid spacing proportional to (λ̄ₖ |ℓᵢᵢ|)^(−1/2) — fine grids for frequent, low-variance classes, coarse grids for rare ones. A smoothing prior p̃ = (1−ε)p + ε/K caps spacing on classes unseen in calibration, keeping the Taylor expansion valid; the prior interpolates between calibration-driven allocation (ε=0) and WaterSIC's uniform-class assumption (ε=1), so SoftWater literally contains the WMSE treatment of the head as a special case.
- **Head quantization results.** Across five models (1B–32B) SoftWater outperforms the released WaterSIC quantizer (run without finetuning) at matched head rates on 59 of 60 test points while using none of that pipeline's refinements, transfers unchanged to released GuidedQuant quantized bodies, and shrinks whole-model size by up to 60%. Because the class-side factor is a domain statistic, matching calibration to the deployment domain gives the lowest KL on that domain throughout.

## Methodology

SoftWater targets the LLM softmax head (z = WX, p = softmax(z), W ∈ R^{K×n}), where K is the vocabulary size. Standard PTQ minimizes WMSE on layer output, but a softmax output is a distribution, so the right distortion is the KL divergence D_KL(p ‖ q) between the original and quantized output distributions q = softmax(z+δ).

The authors take a second-order Taylor expansion of f(δ) = D_KL(p ‖ q) at δ=0 (where f(0)=0, ∇f(0)=0). This yields an error weight E_X[∇²f(0) ⊗ XXᵀ] with ∇²f(0) = diag(p) − ppᵀ — i.e., quantization error is weighted jointly by feature covariance and class-specific softmax curvature. Directly Cholesky-factoring this Kn × Kn matrix costs O(K³n³) time and O(K²n²) memory.

Two reductions make it tractable:

1. **Dither / cross-row decorrelation.** Under a subtractive-dither ensemble the per-entry errors are zero-mean and uncorrelated across rows, so the off-diagonal class terms −pₖpₗ (k≠l) vanish and the class-side factor becomes exactly the diagonal λ = diag(p ⊙ (1−p)). The matrix is now block-diagonal (K blocks of size n).
2. **Separability (Assumption 1).** Assume E_X[λ ⊗ XXᵀ] = E_X[λ] ⊗ E_X[XXᵀ], so the softmax-curvature factor λ̄ = E_X[diag(p ⊙ (1−p))] (just K scalars) and the feature covariance Σ_X can be estimated independently. The single Cholesky L of Σ_X is rescaled K times: the factor of λ̄ ⊗ Σ_X is λ̄^{1/2} ⊗ L. This needs only one extra K-vector accumulator alongside the n×n covariance, both from the same forward pass.

The resulting distortion is a WMSE over the joint (class, feature) index, which WaterSIC's successive-interference-cancellation (SIC) encoder minimizes directly. The induced grid spacing is α_{(k−1)n+i} = c_SW / (λ̄ₖ^{1/2} |ℓᵢᵢ|), so class k and column i each get a rescaled lattice. The smoothing step replaces λ̄ with λ̃ from p̃ = (1−ε)p + ε/K (default ε=0.1), bounding the coarsest-to-finest class-side spacing ratio at ~√(2K/ε) and preventing uncovered classes from breaking the Taylor expansion. The authors reuse only two WaterSIC components — entropy coding (turns integer codes into a bitstream) and rate assignment (secant search on c_SW) — and omit its LMMSE shrinkage, residual-stream correction, and finetuning, so all reported gains are attributable to the allocation alone.

## Results & Highlights

- **Head-only isolation (fp16 body), Llama-3.2-1B, 2-bit head:** SoftWater achieves WT2 KL of 0.00957 vs WaterSIC's 0.06192 — a 6.5× reduction; on C4 KL it is 0.02834 vs 0.09957 (3.5×). WT2 perplexity rises only +0.9% (SW) vs +6.5% (WS). At 4 bits the head is essentially free (+0.1% PPL).
- **Qwen3-32B, 2-bit head:** WT2 KL 0.00703 (SW) vs 0.05835 (WS) = 8.3× reduction; C4 KL 0.01596 vs 0.08106 = 5.1× — the largest in-domain KL ratio in the paper. Across all five 1B–32B models, SoftWater wins 59 of 60 head-only cells, and a 2-bit SoftWater head beats a 3-bit WaterSIC head on WT2 KL ("buys roughly one bit").
- **Fully quantized Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct (tied head, released GuidedQuant bodies):** on a 2-bit body, a 2-bit SoftWater head removes 59.7% of stored bytes (737.3 → 297.0 MiB) for a +3.7% WT2 PPL increase (27.80 vs 26.81 baseline) and +7.9% WT2 KL, whereas WaterSIC costs +17.6% PPL and +34.9% KL. SoftWater is better in 35 of 36 cells; on the 4-bit body a 2-bit head doubles total KL under SW but quintuples it under WS.
- **Zero-shot (lm-eval-harness 0.4.12, 7 tasks):** a 2-bit SoftWater head roughly halves the mean accuracy lost to head quantization on every body — the drop falls from 2.97 / 2.67 / 1.91 points (WS, on 2/3/4-bit bodies) to 1.23 / 0.92 / 1.07 (SW). The gain is concentrated in LAMBADA (the only next-token task), where SoftWater gains 5.2–7.1 points at 2 bits.
- **Domain-targeted calibration:** for every evaluation domain and head rate, the lowest KL is the SoftWater head calibrated on that same domain; at 2 bits matching calibration lowers KL by 2.0×–5.0× over the best mismatched arm. Context count (not token count) is the binding calibration resource for λ̃.
- **Separability proxy error:** the separable surrogate underestimates true distortion by at most 10% across models/rates/datasets, a rate penalty of ≤0.07 bits; the analytical SoftWater factors (λ̃, Σ_X) already reach cosine similarity 0.976–0.992 with the Frobenius-optimal Kronecker factors found by power iteration.

## Authors & Credibility

Joao V. Cavalcanti and Ashia C. Wilson, Department of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science, MIT. The work builds directly on, and fairly credits, the strongest current baselines: WaterSIC (Lifar et al., 2026), GuidedQuant (Kim et al., 2025) for the quantized bodies, QTIP trellis format (Tseng et al., 2025), and YAQA (Tseng et al., 2026) for the Hessian-sketching comparison. The baselines are run from the official WaterSIC repository (without finetuning, applied symmetrically to both arms) and the deployed-model experiments use publicly released GuidedQuant checkpoints from HuggingFace, decoded with a standalone reimplementation verified against the reference forward. The theory is checked three ways: empirical separability error (Table 6a), an ablation of the class-side statistic against the model marginal p̄ₖ and the corpus unigram πₖ (Table 14), and a comparison to Frobenius-optimal Kronecker factors via power iteration (Table 6b). No code release for SoftWater itself is mentioned in the paper.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty (high):** The framing of softmax-head quantization as a KL rate-distortion problem with a class-aware allocation is genuinely new and principled. Prior work either skipped the head, replaced it (Grave; Jean; FlashHead; VQ-Logits), or used end-to-end cross-entropy/Fisher heuristics on body layers (Lee 2026; Kim 2025; YAQA) without exploiting softmax structure analytically. The fact that SoftWater provably contains WaterSIC as the ε=1 special case is an elegant unification.
- **Rigor (high):** The separability assumption — the load-bearing approximation — is not hand-waved: the authors bound the per-block error (Eq. 18–19), measure the aggregate proxy error ρ (≤0.07 bits), and show the analytical factors sit within cosine ~0.99 of the Frobenius-optimal ones. The class-side ablation honestly isolates that the second-moment correction λ̃ₖ beats the plain marginal p̄ₖ mainly out-of-domain, and that both beat the corpus unigram πₖ by ~2×.
- **Reproducibility (medium):** Public baselines, public checkpoints, public datasets (WikiText2, C4, lm-eval-harness 0.4.12) and stdlib-style math make a reimplementation feasible, but no SoftWater code is released and the SIC encoder/lattice format is non-trivial. Calibration details (128 seqs × 1024 tokens from WT2) are given.
- **Significance (high for the small-model regime):** The parameter-share table (head = 15–30% of params, rising to 52–68% of stored bytes once the body is quantized) makes a compelling practical case that head quantization is the remaining bottleneck for sub-2B deployment.
- **Red flags / honest weaknesses:** (1) The "59 of 60" headline is concentrated in-domain; out-of-domain (C4) KL ratios shrink to 3.5×–5.1×, and in the domain-crossover table seven of nine SoftWater losses are exactly the code column under non-code calibration — the expected cost of sharpening allocation toward frequent calibration tokens. (2) The deployed-model and zero-shot claims rest on a single model, Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, and a single family of released bodies (GuidedQuant/BlockLDLQ); the authors flag both as limitations, but a reader cannot tell from this paper alone how SoftWater behaves on, say, a 7B tied-head model. (3) The class-side ablation shows the second-moment correction is "small but systematic" — in-domain, the much simpler p̄ₖ is nearly tied (e.g., Llama-3.2-1B 2-bit WT2 KL: p̄ 0.00952 vs SW 0.00957), so a practitioner might reasonably ask whether the full λ̃ machinery is always worth it. (4) The baseline is WaterSIC *without finetuning*; this is fair (both arms are layerwise) but means the comparison excludes WaterSIC's strongest released configuration, and the paper does not run a finetuned head baseline. (5) At the most aggressive point (4-bit body + 2-bit head, Table 3) even SoftWater still adds +101.9% to WT2 KL over the fp16 head, so 2-bit heads on strong bodies remain genuinely costly.

## Strengths

- Theory-driven and cheap: the entire extra cost is one K-vector accumulator in the existing calibration forward pass — no backward pass, no second data pass, no finetuning.
- Unified and principled: WaterSIC's WMSE head treatment falls out as the ε=1 special case, giving a clean conceptual story rather than an ad-hoc patch.
- Strong, broad head-only wins across five models spanning 1B–32B and two families (Llama, Qwen), at every tested rate (2/3/4-bit).
- Domain-targetable: the class-side statistic is a token frequency, so a head can be specialized to its deployment domain purely through calibration, with matching calibration lowering KL 2×–5×.
- Honest reporting: in-domain vs out-of-domain gaps are surfaced, the single failing cell and the code-column losses are explained, and limitations are stated explicitly rather than buried.

## Limitations

- The separable surrogate is accurate to ≤10% on the measured models but is not guaranteed in general; representations where class curvature correlates strongly with feature anisotropy would degrade it.
- Deployed-model and zero-shot experiments use only Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct and only GuidedQuant/BlockLDLQ bodies — generalization to other model sizes, other body quantizers, and untied heads is untested here.
- The KL gains are markedly larger in-domain (WT2, 6.5×–8.3× at 2 bits) than out-of-domain (C4, 3.5×–5.1×); the "one free bit" claim is calibration-domain-dependent.
- The second-moment correction is small in-domain — the simpler marginal p̄ₖ is nearly as good — so the marginal benefit of the full λ̃ formulation is concentrated in transfer settings.
- No public code release for SoftWater, raising the bar for independent reproduction.
- 2-bit heads on already-strong (4-bit) bodies still roughly double total model KL even under SoftWater, so aggressive whole-model sub-2-bit regimes remain lossy.

## Relevance

与"高效 LLM 推理 / 训练后量化 / 模型压缩"方向高度相关：它精准解决了"softmax 头常被留在 fp16、却占小模型 15–30% 参数（body 量化后占 52–68% 存储字节）"这一长期被忽视的瓶颈，用率失真理论给出可解释的按类分配方案，直接服务于边缘端/小模型部署场景。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the class-aware allocation generalize beyond Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct to mid-size (7B–13B) and untied-head models, where the head's share of stored bytes and the Zipf structure differ?
- The authors claim the analysis extends to MoE routers and vision/speech classifiers (skewed class utilization mirrors Zipfian tokens); is the separability assumption equally tight there, where the "vocabulary" is experts or classes rather than tokens?
- How does SoftWater compose with WaterSIC's deliberately omitted refinements (LMMSE shrinkage, residual-stream correction, finetuning) — are the gains genuinely additive as the authors suggest?
- Since the simpler marginal p̄ₖ nearly ties λ̃ in-domain, is there a principled criterion for when the second-moment correction's out-of-domain benefit justifies the extra bookkeeping?
- The method is weight-only and assumes a frozen body; how does head rate allocation interact with activation quantization or with bodies quantized by a different (non-GuidedQuant) pipeline?
