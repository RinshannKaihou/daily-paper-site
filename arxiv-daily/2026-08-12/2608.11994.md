> **一句话总结：** 提出 Claim-Level Reliability Assessment (CLR) —— 一个无需训练的测试时推理框架，将推理 trace 压缩为若干"决策关键 claim"并对每个 claim 做证伪式验证，再用非线性可靠性分数进行加权聚合；在匹配的模型调用预算下，相比 self-consistency 可在多数数学基准上提升准确率或大幅降低 token 消耗（如 GPT-OSS-20B 在 CMIMC25 上用少 37% 的 token 把准确率从 77.50% 提到 82.19%）。

> **TL;DR:** A training-free test-time-scaling framework that, instead of sampling more solutions, condenses each reasoning trace into M decision-critical claims, asks the same model to falsify each claim (exploiting the construction-vs-refutation asymmetry), and aggregates candidates by a nonlinear reliability score r_k = s_k^M. Under matched 64-call budgets, CLR@32 beats Cons@64 on most settings: e.g., on Gemma-4-12B-it/HMMT25 it lifts accuracy from 76.67% to 88.75% (+12.08 pp), and on GPT-OSS-20B/CMIMC25 it reaches 82.19% vs 77.50% while using 37.0% fewer tokens; a Stage-2 decomposition confirms the gain comes from falsification-based reweighting (+4.48 to +7.01 pp), not from the claim prompt itself (which slightly hurts pass@1).

## 核心贡献

- **提出 claim-level falsification 作为测试时扩展的原则**：不同于整条 trace 评估（信号被常规 token 稀释）和 step-level 验证（计算昂贵且需要额外训练的 verifier），claim-level 提供了一个介于二者之间的粒度，直接针对决定答案正确性的语义锚点。
- **CLR 框架，无需训练**：两阶段推理 —— Stage 1 采样 K 条 trace，每条附带 M 条决策关键 claim；Stage 2 复用同一模型仅基于问题和 claim 列表做证伪，输出每条 claim 的 VALID / REFUTED 判定。
- **非线性可靠性打分与加权聚合**：用 r_k = s_k^M（s_k 为存活 claim 比例）作为 trace 分数，指数惩罚使少数高可靠 trace 可以推翻由多数错误 trace 形成的错误共识。
- **利用构造与证伪的不对称性**：构造正确解需要无瑕疵的推理路径，而推翻一个错误 claim 只需找到一处致命缺陷；把 base model 重新角色化为严格的负证据搜索器。
- **预算对齐与公平比较**：CLR@K 使用 K 次解生成 + K 次验证调用，请求次数恰好等于 Cons@2K，因此主比较是 CLR@32 vs Cons@64。

## Key Contributions

- Formulates **claim-level falsification** as a principle for test-time scaling (TTS), arguing that whole-trace reliability signals are diluted by routine tokens while step-level verification is computationally exhaustive and requires trained process verifiers; claim-level granularity bridges the two.
- Instantiates the principle as **CLR, a training-free framework**: Stage 1 samples K traces each carrying M decision-critical claims; Stage 2 reuses the same model to falsify claims using only the problem and the claim list (no original trace or prediction), producing binary VALID/REFUTED verdicts.
- Introduces a **nonlinear reliability score r_k = s_k^M** and reliability-weighted aggregation (Eq. 3) that lets a smaller but more reliable answer group overturn an incorrect count-based majority.
- Frames a **construction-vs-refutation asymmetry** as an inductive bias: building a correct solution is hard (needs a flawless path), refuting a single claim is easier (needs one decisive flaw), repurposing the base model as a rigorous verifier without extra training.
- Defines a clean **request-budget equivalence** (CLR@K = 2K calls = Cons@2K) so the headline CLR@32 vs Cons@64 comparison is fair on call count, while also reporting realized-token consumption.

## Methodology

CLR is a two-stage, training-free, consensus-based answer aggregation framework operating on top of standard sampling.

**Stage 1 — Solution and critical-claim generation.** For problem q, the model samples K independent traces under a fixed decoding configuration (thinking mode on, temperature 1.0). Each trace produces a final prediction y_k in `\boxed{}` plus an ordered list of exactly M = 5 (default) concise intermediate claims. Claims must be intermediate mathematical statements whose failure would undermine the prediction; generic summaries and prediction restatements are excluded (e.g., factorization identities, primality assertions, maximality statements).

**Stage 2 — Falsification-based claim assessment.** A single joint request per trace passes q and the claim list C_k to the same model (the original trace and prediction are NOT given separately). The model is prompted to "treat every claim as a suspicious assertion" and actively search for a decisive contradiction, counterexample, factual/logical error, missing condition, or unsupported inference, also checking cross-claim conflicts. The outcome per claim is v_{k,m} ∈ {0 (REFUTED), 1 (VALID)}; "VALID" means *not refuted by this check*, not a formal proof of correctness.

**Reliability scoring and aggregation.** Let s_k be the fraction of surviving claims. The trace score is r_k = s_k^M (Eq. 2). For M > 1 the exponent makes a refuted decision-critical claim more consequential than under linear averaging, increasingly suppressing an error-prone trace as more of its claims are refuted. Predictions are partitioned into equivalence groups G by a task-appropriate criterion, and group reliability is R(G) = Σ_{k: y_k ∈ G} r_k; the answer is canon(argmax_G R(G)) (Eq. 3). This reduces to ordinary self-consistency when all traces receive equal positive weight; ties (including all-zero-score) go to the earliest equivalence group in sampling order.

**Budget.** CLR@K = K solution calls + K verification calls, matching the request count of Cons@2K. CLR only reweights parsed candidates, so it cannot recover a correct answer absent from the Stage-1 samples.

## Results & Highlights

All headline numbers below are CLR@32 vs Cons@64 under **matched 64-call budgets** (Table 1); deltas are in percentage points for accuracy and relative change for tokens, both vs Cons@64.

- **Gemma-4-12B-it on HMMT25:** accuracy rises from **76.67% (Cons@64) to 88.75% (CLR@32), +12.08 pp**, at the cost of +33.4% more generated tokens. On CMIMC25 it goes **68.75% → 80.62% (+11.87 pp)** and on Apex-shortlist **32.98% → 40.10% (+7.12 pp)** — CLR here trades tokens for accuracy.
- **GPT-OSS-20B on CMIMC25:** accuracy improves from **77.50% to 82.19% (+4.69 pp) while using 37.0% fewer generated tokens**; this is the single clearest token-efficiency win. On Apex-shortlist CLR reaches **24.22% vs 20.83% (+3.39 pp) with 38.5% fewer tokens**, and on HMMT26 **73.48% vs 72.73% (+0.75 pp) with 39.8% fewer tokens**.
- **GPT-OSS-120B on CMIMC25:** **75.00% → 80.00% (+5.00 pp) with 21.6% fewer tokens**; on Apex-shortlist **23.96% → 27.86% (+3.90 pp) with 21.9% fewer tokens**.
- **Qwen3.5-27B (already near-saturated, Cons@64 ≥ 90% on three benchmarks):** gains shrink to +1.67 pp (HMMT25), +2.50 pp (CMIMC25), +2.60 pp (Apex-shortlist), with token reductions up to 14.5%; on HMMT26 CLR is exactly tied (90.91% vs 90.91%).
- **Stage-2 decomposition (Table 2, GPT-OSS-20B):** the claim-generation prompt alone *reduces* single-rollout pass@1 by 0.65–4.56 pp, but full falsification-based reweighting recovers **+4.48 to +7.01 pp over unweighted Stage-1 Cons@32** on the same 32 candidates — the gains come from reliability weighting, not from better generation.
- **Rescue rate (Fig. 2, GPT-OSS-20B):** conditioned on Cons@K being wrong despite a correct candidate existing in the same K traces, CLR overturns the wrong consensus in **~16–48% of cases, averaging ~37%** across 16 benchmark-budget settings (e.g., K=4 CMIMC25: 33/72 = 45.83%; K=8 HMMT25: 18/43 = 41.86%).
- **Number-of-claims ablation (Table 3, GPT-OSS-20B, K=32):** going M=1 → M=3 adds **+3.13 to +3.79 pp** accuracy on all four benchmarks; the main benefit is leaving the single-claim (binary-filter) regime.
- **Scaling curves (Fig. 3):** self-consistency often saturates or fluctuates as K grows, while CLR scales more steadily, especially on the four Gemma benchmarks and on CMIMC25/Apex-shortlist for both GPT-OSS models — but curves can cross at intermediate budgets, and CLR is not uniformly dominant (e.g., Gemma-4-12B-it/HMMT25 shows −0.42 pp at the matched operating point for GPT-OSS-20B).

Authors are from **Sina Weibo Inc.** (a major Chinese technology company with established LLM research output). The paper is published as a **workshop paper at COLM 2026**, indicating peer-reviewed but shorter/incremental scope relative to a main-conference paper. Code is released at https://github.com/WeiboAI/CLR. Experiments span four recent open-weight models (Gemma-4-12B-it, GPT-OSS-20B, GPT-OSS-120B, Qwen3.5-27B) and four competition-math benchmarks, with accuracy averaged over N=8 independent complete CLR flows, and decoding configurations reported in a table (Appendix A.1). The matched-call accounting (CLR@K = 2K calls) is defined explicitly and the paper reports both request-count and realized-token comparisons, which is methodologically sound.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty** — The construction-vs-refutation asymmetry is a genuinely elegant framing, and compressing a trace to decision-critical claims as an intermediate granularity (between whole-trace and per-step) is a sensible, under-explored middle ground. However, the core move — reusing the base model as a self-verifier — is well-trodden (Weng et al. 2023; Weng et al. 2023 self-verification; Lightman et al. 2024 PRMs); the contribution is the specific *claim-level + nonlinear-weighting* recipe rather than the falsification idea in general.
- **Rigor** — The Stage-2 decomposition (Table 2) is the strongest piece of analysis: it cleanly shows the claim prompt *hurts* pass@1 by 0.65–4.56 pp, so the gains genuinely come from reweighting, not from better generation. The matched-call vs realized-token dual reporting is correct. But the headline rescue-rate metric (Fig. 2) is *conditional* on a recoverable correct candidate already existing — a denominator chosen to flatter CLR — and the unconditional accuracy lift is what matters and is smaller.
- **Reproducibility** — Reasonable: training-free, sampling hyperparameters and full prompt templates (Appendix B) are provided, code is open-sourced. But N=8 flows on benchmarks that appear to have only ~60–200 questions (HMMT25 raw counts imply ~120 questions) means several pp-level deltas rest on a handful of flipped items; confidence intervals are not reported.
- **Significance** — Solid but **narrowly scoped**: every benchmark is a competition-mathematics set (HMMT25/26, CMIMC25, Apex-shortlist). There is no code generation, commonsense, multi-hop QA, or symbolic reasoning evaluation, so the central asymmetry claim (refutation is easier than generation) is only validated in the domain where a claim is cleanly checkable (an equation either holds or not). Generalization to open-ended tasks is asserted, not demonstrated.
- **Red flags / overclaiming** — The M ablation (Table 3) jointly varies semantic coverage, score resolution, *and* the exponent in r_k = s_k^M, so it cannot cleanly attribute the effect to claim count; the paper admits this but the headline M=5 default is partly a tuned hyperparameter. The claim that CLR "avoids early performance saturation" (Fig. 3) holds for some models but curves visibly cross on Qwen3.5-27B and at intermediate K, which the authors acknowledge. Finally, CLR by construction cannot recover a correct answer absent from Stage-1 samples — its ceiling is the candidate set, a real limitation that the paper states honestly but that caps the headline gains.

## Strengths

- Elegant, well-motivated principle (claim-level falsification as a TTS lever) instantiated as a simple, training-free two-stage pipeline that reuses the base model as its own verifier.
- Strong and *honest* decomposition analysis isolating the Stage-2 reweighting effect from the (slightly harmful) claim-prompt effect.
- Substantial token-efficiency wins on the GPT-OSS family (21–40% fewer tokens at equal-or-better accuracy), which is the practically important regime.
- Clean matched-call budget accounting and explicit disclosure that VALID means "not refuted," not "proven correct."
- Open-source code and full prompt templates lower the barrier to reproduction.

## Limitations

- **Math-only benchmarks**: no evidence the construction/refutation asymmetry transfers to code, commonsense, multi-step QA, or tasks where a "decision-critical claim" is not crisply checkable.
- **Cannot recover absent correct candidates**: CLR's accuracy ceiling is the Stage-1 sample set; it only reweights, never generates new answers.
- **Heuristic, conflated exponent**: M simultaneously controls claim count, score resolution, and penalty sharpness, so the M=5 default is a tuned knob, not a derived optimum.
- **Small effective sample sizes** with N=8 flows and ~60–200 questions per benchmark; no confidence intervals reported, so several pp deltas may not be statistically significant.
- **Claim prompt degrades generation** (−0.65 to −4.56 pp pass@1) — a real side cost that is recovered only if Stage-2 reweighting more than compensates.
- **Near-zero headroom on saturated models** (Qwen3.5-27B), where gains shrink to ≤2.6 pp and one benchmark ties.

## Relevance

高度相关。CLR 直接属于"测试时扩展 / 推理可靠性 / self-consistency 改进"这条主线，与对 LLM 推理质量、推理时算力分配、以及把生成模型重新用作验证器（self-verification / generative verification）的兴趣高度吻合；其 claim-level 粒度与证伪式验证的思路对推理增强、AI safety（错误检测）方向都有启发。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the construction-vs-refutation asymmetry survive outside competition math — e.g., on code generation (HumanEval+/LiveCodeBench), multi-hop QA (HotpotQA), or logical reasoning (ProofWriter) where a "decision-critical claim" is harder to isolate?
- How does CLR compare against a *trained* process reward model (PRM, Lightman et al. 2024) under matched call budgets, rather than only against untrained self-consistency and pass@1?
- What is the statistical significance of the headline deltas given N=8 flows over ~60–200 questions — would bootstrap confidence intervals change any conclusions?
- Is the exponent r_k = s_k^M optimal, or would a learned/calibrated exponent (or a different monotone map of s_k) do better? The M ablation conflates these factors.
- Since CLR cannot recover a correct answer absent from Stage-1 samples, how much of the gain is "rescuing wrong consensus" vs "the Stage-1 sampling distribution already containing the right answer" — i.e., what is the unconditional lift over a candidate-coverage-matched baseline?
- Can the Stage-2 falsifier be a *different* (smaller or specialized) model to cut token cost further, and does that preserve the reliability signal?
