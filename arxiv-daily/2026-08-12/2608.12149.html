Massive Activations in Hybrid Linear Attention Large Language Models: Pre-Attention Spikes & Inter-Spike Plateaus

Zunhai Su, Bohan Sun, et al. · Startlux · Tsinghua · UCAS · HKU · Sydney · Columbia

hybrid-linear-attention massive-activations mechanistic-interpretability attention-sinks LLM-internal-dynamics
Key Finding

First study of massive activations in hybrid-linear-attention LLMs reveals two architecture-aligned morphologies — pre-attention spikes and inter-spike plateaus — recurring to 397B.

Schematic: PAS spikes precede each full-attention (FA) layer; ISP plateau bridges them.
Key Metric
~100%
Sink–spike alignment rate at ρ=12:1, across all 5 linear-attention backbones (RetNet, HGRN, GLA, DeltaNet, GDN) at 1.3B & 340M — range 99.4–100.0%.
Why This Matters

Diagnoses where quantization pain and attention-sink artifacts appear in modern hybrid LLMs — directly guiding full-attention layer placement and output gating.

Critical Edge

The unifying write–sink–cancel lifecycle rests on a single fixed-coordinate trace in one 1.3B model — the central mechanism remains correlational, not proven.

01

Methodology

方法 · empirical / mechanistic interpretability

A mechanistic interpretability study, not a new architecture. Four components isolate where massive activations (MAs) organize and why they persist.

Sink-guided tracking. Magnitude ranking loses sink alignment under hybridization, so the authors fix a consensus attention-sink token (averaging causal attention across all full-attention layers/heads) and trace that fixed token's max activation across depth.
Inference-time suite (M-A-P). 340M/1.3B on FineWeb-Edu: hold scale fixed, vary the backbone; then hold GDN fixed, vary ρ∈{24,12,6,3}. Defines alignment rate & inter-spike retention over 500 inputs with bootstrap CIs.
Scale sweep. 12 checkpoints from Kimi Linear, Qwen3.5 (35B/122B/397B), Nemotron-H (8B–56B), Zamba2 (1.2B–7B) — linear-attention & state-space mixers.
Controlled pretraining + decomposition. Fresh GDN models, full-attention inserted at depth 4/12/20; gates added/removed. Systematic-outlier updates decomposed at a fixed token–feature coordinate around an FA boundary.

03

Assessment

优势与局限 · strengths & limitations

✓ Strengths

  • Clean, unseen regularity. PAS/ISP invariant to backbone, ratio, scale, post-training stage, and input domain.
  • Principled tracking. Consensus-sink method fixes the magnitude-ranking fragility the authors themselves expose; metrics carry bootstrap uncertainty.
  • Causal isolation. Controlled pretraining (depth & gate ablations) sets a higher bar than checkpoint-only analysis.
  • Unified theory. Cancellation-timing continuum elegantly bridges hybrid & full-attention morphologies.
  • Strong release. Public M-A-P suite + own checkpoints + code.

! Limitations

  • Correlational core. Lifecycle rests on one fixed-coordinate trace; no interventional proof that cancellation timing governs.
  • Small scale. Controlled/downstream results only at 340M/1.3B on 10–50B tokens; production-scale effects unverified.
  • Narrow context. NIAH run only at 4K; broader RULER tasks absent — long-context claims limited.
  • Noisy metrics. NIAH-3 erratic across near-identical configs; fine gating-vs-quality conclusions unreliable.
  • No baseline comparison. No MA/sink-mitigation baseline at matched compute — practical payoff unquantified.

Open Questions for Further Exploration

  1. What actually governs cancellation timing — residual norm, output scale, or the next sink's position? A patch study could make the lifecycle causal.
  2. Do PAS/ISP predict downstream behavior at production scale and 32K–128K context?
  3. Is PAS presence functionally inert, or does its magnitude carry recall-relevant computation the alignment metric hides?
  4. Can write–sink–cancel predict where quantization error concentrates, guiding mixed-precision schemes?
