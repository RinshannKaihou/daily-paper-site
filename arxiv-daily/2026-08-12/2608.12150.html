LLM rankings flip as the token budget changes — there is no single best model.

“Who Thinks Best Depends on How Long You Let Them: Budget-Dependent Rankings in LLM Evaluation” ↗ Rodrigo Guedes de Souza & Alison R. Panisson · UFSC, Brazil
Key metric
+27.8pp
Per-item oracle gain on GPQA at b=4096 (95% CI +21.7 to +33.8) — the headroom a perfect budget-aware router could capture under constrained compute.
Why this matters

Exposes an overlooked evaluation fragility — the token budget — and offers budget-aware routing for constrained-compute deployment and trustworthy leaderboards.

Critical edge

The “rankings reverse on all benchmarks” headline leans on non-significant points: GPQA at b=4096 has p=0.89 (n=198), MATH-500 final reversal p=0.11.

01

Methodology

4 × 3 × 7 grid · deterministic (T=0)

A model’s ranking is treated as a family of orderings parameterized by the token budget, not a single sequence. The authors run a fully deterministic grid and ask where the “best model” changes.

Four open chat/instruct models — LLaMA-3 8B, Qwen-3 32B, LLaMA-3.3 70B, GPT-OSS 20B — answer three reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH-500, GPQA-Diamond) at seven log-spaced max_tokens budgets (64→4096). All 56,476 inferences use greedy decoding (T=0) via cloud APIs for exact reproducibility.

Correctness per (item, budget) feeds a four-way behavioral taxonomy (always-correct / monotone-increasing / non-monotone “overthinking” / always-wrong). Ranking reversals are certified with paired McNemar χ² tests, and a three-tier truncation control (all-items / stop-only / common non-truncated) isolates genuine budget effects from length artifacts. Per-item oracle gaps get 95% bootstrap CIs, and a per-model XGBoost router with SHAP attribution tests whether budget is a usable routing signal.

03

Strengths & Limitations

two-handed read

▲ Strengths

  • One clean thesis, exhaustive grid. “Rankings depend on budget” backed by 4×3×7 = 84 configs and 56,476 deterministic inferences.
  • Right methodological move. Three-tier truncation analysis + Appendix G’s direct enumeration of stop-reason overthinking pairs forecloses the obvious critique.
  • Practical, non-obvious implication. 86–94% of overthinking is single-model, so item pruning cannot fix it.
  • Honest negative result. Budget features hurt cross-domain transfer (−1.2 pp) — more informative than the headline positive.
  • Quantitative spine. SHAP: budget ≈ 6× more important than any other feature.

▼ Limitations

  • Only 4 general chat models. Dedicated reasoning models (o1, DeepSeek-R1, QwQ) are excluded — the most budget-sensitive regime is untested.
  • Small GPQA sample (n=198). Several “reversals” sit within noise; authors themselves urge caution.
  • Length artifacts. Qwen-3 32B verbose-<think> truncation at 59.7% on GPQA partly reflects length, not ability.
  • Shallow routing features. Surface text stats + static MiniLM embeddings leave ~19 pp of the oracle gap unexploited.
  • Narrow scope. Exact-match reasoning only; generation/code and open-ended tasks are out. No public code/data link in the body.
04

Open Questions

for further exploration
  1. Do reversals persist for reasoning models (o1, DeepSeek-R1, QwQ) once thinking vs. output budgets are bounded separately?
  2. Are crossovers stable under sampling (T>0) and self-consistency, or does stochasticity wash them out?
  3. Can richer signals (logit entropy, hidden states) close more of the 19 pp residual GPQA oracle gap?
  4. Is the cross-domain failure of budget features (−1.2 pp) recoverable with lightweight adaptation, or fundamentally task-specific?
  5. Do ranking reversals propagate into agentic pipelines where per-call budget is itself an economic knob?
