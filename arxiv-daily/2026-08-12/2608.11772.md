> **一句话总结：** 提出 DARC 框架，先在开发集上诊断任务族的"主导失败模式"，再据此裁剪可用的恢复干预、冻结一条成本感知的短路策略；在 ALFWorld、AppWorld、XBRL Finance 三个基准上把自纠错从"无差别堆上下文"变成"对号入座的恢复接口设计"，在显著提升成功率的同时压缩了环境步数与检索预算。

> **TL;DR:** DARC recasts agent self-correction as recovery-interface design: it profiles a task-family's dominant failure mode on a dev set, prunes the recovery library to matching interventions, and freezes a cost-aware short-circuit policy from training-set verifier feedback. On ALFWorld valid_unseen with DeepSeek-V4-Flash it lifts success from 39.55% (base) / 54.48% (ACE) to 90.30% while cutting invalid actions by 92.9%, and on XBRL Finance it reaches 94.50% average accuracy vs 80.50% (ACE) / 74.00% (MIPROv2) — all without using answer labels.

## 核心贡献

- **提出"诊断引导式自纠错"问题框架。** 把通用语言智能体中模糊的"任务失败"信号转化为可操作的恢复信号，灵感来自编程智能体拥有的编译器/测试/执行轨迹这类诊断基底，但面向缺乏此类反馈的广泛任务。
- **给出 DARC 两阶段构造算法。** 第一阶段在开发集上做失败分析，选出主导失败模式 m 并裁剪出可容许干预集 R_m ⊆ R；第二阶段在训练集上用任务验证器反馈蒸馏出一条有序的、成本感知的短路策略 π_m = (r_1, ..., r_L)，冻结后部署。形式化上成功项是次模覆盖目标，诊断通过缩小候选空间来支撑策略选择。
- **在三种性质迥异的失败模式上验证同一协议。** ALFWorld → 动作合法性 harness（排序容许动作、保留 top-12 的确定性规则守卫）；AppWorld → 过程性知识 harness（Auto-Knowledge、局部归纳、检索回退的级联链）；XBRL Finance → 格式精度 harness（蒸馏检索演示预算 k）。
- **严格的消融与公平性分析。** 对比"正确诊断 / 通用 playbook / 错配干预"三种策略；并对 ALFWorld 的巨大涨幅做了 matched-information 公平性检验（2×2 因子分解），证明增益来自"恢复接口"的配套设计而非特权信息或单纯裁剪。
- **诚实披露边界。** 明确说明每个基准只建模单一主导失败模式、ACE 在部分 AppWorld challenge 指标上反超、权重空间扩展仅为单种子概念验证。

## Key Contributions

- Formulates **diagnosis-guided agent self-correction**: turning ambiguous task failures into actionable recovery signals, motivated by the compiler-like feedback available to coding agents but absent in broad language-agent tasks.
- Proposes **DARC**, a two-stage harness constructor: (1) dev-set failure diagnosis that restricts the candidate intervention library to a mode-matched subset R_m, and (2) training-set verifier-driven distillation of a cost-aware, ordered, short-circuit fallback policy π_m that is frozen before test evaluation.
- Instantiates the **same protocol on three deliberately different failure modes** — action validity (ALFWorld), procedural breadth (AppWorld), and format precision (XBRL Finance) — each yielding a structurally different harness (action guard / fallback chain / retrieval budget).
- Provides **controlled ablations** (correct vs generic vs mismatched recovery) plus a matched-information fairness study and a 2×2 factorial decomposition isolating restriction from instruction.
- Offers a **preliminary weight-space extension** (DARC-GRPO / DARC-OPSD on Qwen3-8B) showing the diagnosis-derived curriculum transfers to fine-tuning.

## Methodology

DARC treats a task family X with a candidate recovery-intervention library R (action guards, API procedure sources, local induction rules, or few-shot retrieval budgets). A recovery harness for failure mode m is the tuple H_m = (m, R_m ⊆ R, π_m), where R_m is the admissible intervention set and π_m is an ordered deployment policy.

**Stage 1 — Failure Diagnosis.** Run the base agent on development tasks and measure observable failure signatures (invalid/state-incompatible actions in ALFWorld; missing cross-app procedures in AppWorld; exact-format violations in Finance). These identify a *single dominant* failure mode and determine R_m. Diagnosis is frozen at the task-family level — auditable, no test-time updates, no observation of test labels. The output is a *constrained recovery space*, not a longer prompt.

**Stage 2 — Policy Distillation.** For each training task x_i and intervention r_j, collect an evidence-matrix entry E_{i,j} = (s(x_i, r_j), c(x_i, r_j)) using the *same task verifier* as success signal (no answer labels). A candidate policy π = (r_1, …, r_L) is scored as a short-circuit policy: try interventions in order, halt at the first success. The empirical success is succ(π) = (1/|D|) Σ_i max_{r_t ∈ π} s(x_i, r_t); cost is cost(π) = (1/|D|) Σ_i Σ_{t≤h*} c(x_i, r_t). The deployed policy maximizes J(π) = succ(π) − λ · max(0, cost(π) − τ_free), enumerating bounded-length policies inside the restricted space.

**Structural properties.** The success term is monotone submodular (coverage over interventions), explaining why short policies capture most reachable recovery; the full objective is ordered and cost-penalized, so diagnosis shrinks the search space |Σ_K| (improving the O(√(log|Σ_K|/n)) uniform-convergence rate) and policy distillation enumerates bounded policies inside it. Deployment semantics differ per benchmark (resettable episodes in ALFWorld, controlled task attempts in AppWorld, stateless extraction in Finance), and these assumptions are made explicit because they determine when the offline evidence matrix is a valid deployment proxy.

## Results & Highlights

- **ALFWorld valid_unseen (DeepSeek-V4-Flash):** DARC reaches **90.30%** success vs **54.48%** for ACE and **39.55%** for the base agent — a +35.82 pp gap over the strongest baseline — while valid_seen macro rises to **93.57%** (DARC) vs 50.00% (ACE).
- **ALFWorld cost efficiency (DeepSeek-V4-Flash):** DARC cuts average environment steps by **54.2%** (15.83 vs 34.56 for base) and invalid actions per episode by **92.9%** (0.091 vs 1.288), raising success-per-100-steps from **1.16 to 6.02**; episodes also finish in 10.3 s vs 155.4 s (base) / 143.8 s (ACE).
- **XBRL Finance average accuracy (DeepSeek-V4-Flash):** DARC reaches **94.50%** (Formula 99.00% / FiNER Tags 90.00%) vs **80.50%** for ACE and **74.00%** for MIPROv2 — and notably DARC/ACE use *no answer labels*, whereas ICL/MIPROv2/GEPA do.
- **AppWorld Test-Normal (DeepSeek-V4-Flash):** DARC achieves **95.83% TGC / 87.50% SGC** vs ACE's 54.76% / 39.29% and base's 23.21% / 7.14%; averaged over Test-Normal+Challenge, DARC TGC is 74.90% vs ACE 45.25%. Scenario-cluster bootstrap (20,000 resamples over 56 scenarios) shows DARC's CIs do not overlap ACE or base on either metric, and paired t-test / exact McNemar with Holm correction confirm significance.
- **Diagnosis ablation:** the correct policy beats generic and mismatched on every setting — ALFWorld success **91.94% (correct) vs 52.24% (generic) vs 52.24% (mismatched)**; Finance macro **94.50% vs 80.50% vs 37.75%**. On AppWorld TGC the *mismatched* policy (64.88%) even beats the *generic* playbook (61.90%), supporting the claim that irrelevant context can be more harmful than inert mismatched interventions.
- **Cross-task transfer of frozen policies:** Formula→FiNER reaches **89.50%** vs the target-tuned **90.00%**; FiNER→Formula reaches **96.00%** vs **99.00%**; ALFWorld seen→unseen transfers to **90.30%**, preserving most of the target-tuned performance when the failure mode stays similar.
- **Matched-information fairness (ALFWorld valid_unseen):** granting the base agent parsed metadata barely helps (39.55%→43.28%); ACE under random top-12 action restriction *collapses* (54.48%→25.37%); diagnosis-as-label alone is at base level (38.81%). A 2×2 factorial shows neither component works alone — together they are worth **+49.26 pp** with an interaction of **+46.27 pp**.
- **Retrieval-budget distillation (Finance):** larger k is *not* monotonic (k=8,16 slightly reduce tag accuracy vs k=1–4); the distilled policy picks k=2 (Formula) / k=1 (Tags), mean budget **1.5**, landing at 94.50% — top band at a fraction of the retrieval cost.

## Authors & Credibility

The work comes from **Ant International** (Ant Group's research arm), an industry lab with direct stakes in both finance-extraction agents and multi-app assistants — the XBRL Finance and AppWorld benchmarks are well-aligned with their domain. Corresponding author Yongqi Tong and co-author Pan Wang have a relevant prior publication trail (e.g., SEAL on agent–environment co-evolution [Hu et al. 2026], and work on over-refusal / reward modeling cited in the references). The empirical work is unusually careful for an industry preprint: it runs three base models (DeepSeek-V4-Flash, Qwen3.5-27B, Qwen3.6-27B), reports scenario-cluster bootstrap CIs with 20,000 resamples and paired significance tests with Holm correction, and — most importantly — performs an explicit matched-information fairness audit and a 2×2 factorial to preempt the obvious "you only win because of privileged action info" objection to the large ALFWorld gain. This level of self-scrutiny is a credibility plus. The paper is an arXiv preprint (v1, 12 Aug 2026); peer-review status is unknown.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** The core reframing — "decide *which* failure may trigger *which* recovery before spending recovery evidence," with diagnosis-as-library-pruning rather than diagnosis-as-prompt-expansion — is a genuinely useful conceptual contribution that distinguishes DARC from Reflexion/ReAct/DSPy-style uniform refinement and from model-cascades (which route among base models, not among recovery interventions). The submodular-coverage framing and explicit per-benchmark deployment-semantics table give it more theoretical grounding than typical agent papers.
- **Rigor:** Strong on the ALFWorld result (significance tests + fairness audit + factorial decomposition). Weaker on AppWorld, where the authors concede confidence intervals overlap in the ablation and that the gain is "directional but consistent" rather than always significant, and where ACE matches or exceeds DARC on several Test-Challenge metrics for the two Qwen models. The honest acknowledgement that "gains are not uniform across every metric" is appropriate but also means the headline averages partially smooth over splits where DARC does not win.
- **Reproducibility concern — hand-engineered, per-domain harnesses.** The "action-validity guard" is a hand-crafted deterministic rule scorer S(a) = S_goal + S_phase + S_search + S_train-prior − S_conflict − S_loop that keeps the top-12 admissible commands, computed from task description and training-set priors. This is highly ALFWorld-specific; AppWorld gets a bespoke fallback chain and Finance a retrieval-budget knob. So DARC is more a *meta-procedure + per-domain engineering effort* than a single drop-in algorithm, and the three settings each required expert diagnosis of the dominant failure mode. Reproducing the exact numbers requires re-implementing these mode-specific interventions (Appendix C/D referenced but the rule terms are only sketched in the main text).
- **Controlled-setting limitation.** Each benchmark instantiates exactly *one* dominant failure mode; the authors flag this and list dynamic instance-level routing for mixed-mode failures as future work, but it means the central claim is demonstrated on an artificially clean failure distribution. Real tasks have mixed/overlapping modes, and the uniform-convergence argument only holds for a fixed finite policy class — transfer to genuinely new failure modes is admitted to be "an empirical question."
- **Weight-space extension is thin.** The Qwen3-8B fine-tuning probe is single-seed with modest gains (DARC-GRPO +4.74 pp macro to 10.55%; DARC-OPSD only +2.19 pp), and OPSD underperforms plain GRPO. Treated by the authors as a proof of concept — correctly — but it should not be read as evidence the recipe generalizes to training.
- **No red flags / overclaiming detected.** Claims are scoped to the reported protocols, significance is reported where computed, and the largest gain (ALFWorld) gets the most scrutiny. The framing "failures need not trigger uniformly more context" is well-supported.

## Strengths

- Conceptually clean reframing of self-correction as *recovery-interface design* rather than context expansion, with a formal harness tuple and an explicit cost-success objective.
- Diagnosis reduces the policy search space (submodular coverage + uniform-convergence argument), giving a principled reason short mode-matched policies suffice.
- Same protocol yields three structurally different harnesses across action-validity / procedural / format-precision failures, demonstrating the generality of the *methodology*.
- Excellent cost efficiency: on ALFWorld, env steps −54.2%, invalid actions −92.9%, latency from 155 s to 10 s per episode — practical, not just accuracy-focused.
- Intellectual honesty: matched-info fairness audit, 2×2 factorial, explicit "no answer labels" accounting (Table 2), bootstrap CIs with Holm-corrected paired tests, and open acknowledgement of where ACE wins.
- Clean cross-task transfer result: frozen policies retain ~89–96% of target-tuned accuracy when the failure mode is similar.

## Limitations

- One dominant failure mode per benchmark is an idealization; mixed-mode and instance-level routing is left to future work, so behavior on realistic heterogeneous failure distributions is untested.
- The harnesses are substantially hand-engineered per domain (the ALFWorld guard's six rule terms; the AppWorld fallback chain composition; the Finance k-knob) — high per-domain engineering cost and limited out-of-the-box transfer.
- AppWorld gains are not uniformly significant (overlapping CIs in the ablation) and ACE is competitive or better on several Test-Challenge metrics, especially for the Qwen backbones.
- Weight-space extension is single-seed with small absolute gains and OPSD does not beat standard GRPO — a proof of concept, not a verified recipe.
- Diagnosis is static and task-family-level; no online/learned router, so the framework currently cannot adapt when the deployed failure distribution drifts from the dev profile.
- Reliance on an enumerated bounded policy class and resettable/controlled deployment semantics (Table 1); applicability to non-resettable, side-effect-laden real environments is not established.

## Relevance

与"语言智能体的自纠错、失败诊断与恢复策略"研究方向高度相关：DARC 提供了一种可解释、成本感知的方式来决定"何时该给智能体补充什么证据"，对构建更可靠的非编程类（金融抽取、多 App 助手、具身）智能体有直接参考价值，也与我关注的智能体评估与上下文效率问题紧密契合。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Can the per-family diagnosis be replaced by a *learned instance-level router* that assigns failure modes online, and how much accuracy is lost versus the static harness when multiple modes co-occur in one task?
- The matched-info study shows restriction-alone (random top-12) *collapses* ACE — is there a principled way to inherit the ranked-action benefit for baselines that lack a diagnosis-guided guard, e.g. a model-probability ranker trained on the same priors?
- How sensitive are the Finance results to the specific XBRL/FiNER tag distribution, and does the non-monotonic k-curve (k=8,16 hurting tag accuracy) replicate on other strict-schema extraction tasks?
- The uniform-convergence rate depends on |Σ_K|, which shrinks with |R_m| — what is the empirical relationship between library size, diagnosis tightness, and the n needed to stabilize π*?
- Could the DARC-GRPO curriculum gains be improved with multiple seeds and a stronger base model, or does the OPSD self-distillation underperformance indicate a fundamental mismatch with the recovery objective?
