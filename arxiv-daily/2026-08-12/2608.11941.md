> **一句话总结：** 基于 OEIS 中 492 个已用 Lean 形式化的开放数学猜想构建了 OEIS_OPEN 基准；仅配备极简工具（bash、编辑器、Lean 工具链）的语言模型在 50 美元/题预算下解出 30%（147/492），大幅超过 AlphaProof Nexus 的 9%，且额外提供数学文献或更复杂的 agent 循环均无增益。

> **TL;DR:** This paper releases OEIS_OPEN, a benchmark of 492 open OEIS conjectures formalized in Lean, plus a secure (3-container, no-network) open-source harness that runs any generic LM against them. With a minimal ReAct agent (bash + editor + Lean toolchain), Claude Opus 4.8 resolves 147/492 (30%) at $50/conjecture versus AlphaProof Nexus's 44/492 (9%) at similar cost; on the 100-conjecture OEIS_OPEN_LITE at $200/conjecture the best model (Claude Fable 5) reaches 44%. Giving agents 476k arXiv papers or a more elaborate DeepAgent loop yields no improvement, and solve rates scale only log-linearly with spend.

## 核心贡献

- **构建并开源 OEIS_OPEN 基准**：基于 Tsoukalas 等人从 OEIS 中筛选并用 Lean 形式化的 492 个开放数学猜想（涉及 444 个不同整数序列），提供可复现的评测集；另有 OEIS_OPEN_LITE（100 题随机子集）用于低成本评测。
- **通用、防作弊的评测脚手架**：开源代码可对任意通用语言模型进行评测，采用三容器 Docker 隔离（agent / compile / scorer 均无网络），并用 SafeVerify（基于 lean4checker）防范篡改 Mathlib、注入公理、改写定义、绕过内核等各类攻击。
- **以形式化证明而非"生成器-验证器间隙"为标准**：与 HorizonMath、FrontierMath: Open Problems 不同，模型必须给出通过 Lean 内核检查的猜想或其否命题的证明，避免"证据而非证明"的弊端，且让"假猜想"也是可解任务（可证否）。
- **揭示"简单即有效"**：极简 ReAct agent（三个工具）解出的猜想数是 AlphaProof Nexus 复杂交配式进化搜索（含 AlphaProof、Elo 评分、P-UCB 采样）的 3 倍多，印证 Sutton 的 "bitter lesson"。
- **两个重要的负结果**：向模型提供 47.6 万篇 arXiv 数学论文的 LaTeX 源码、或换用更复杂的 DeepAgent 循环，均未提升 OEIS_OPEN_LITE 上的准确率。

## Key Contributions

- **Releases OEIS_OPEN**, a 492-conjecture benchmark of open OEIS conjectures formalized in Lean (drawn from Tsoukalas et al.'s 2649-conjecture corpus, filtered to 500 by Gemini, minus 8 technical exclusions), plus OEIS_OPEN_LITE (100 random conjectures) for cheaper evaluation. Code and accepted proofs are released at github.com/epoch-research/LeanOpenProblems.
- **A generic, cheating-resistant harness**: open-source evaluation that runs *any* LM (not a bespoke agent) against the conjectures, hardened via three isolated Docker containers (agent / compile / scorer) with no network access, and SafeVerify (adapted from lean4checker) which guards against axiom injection, definition tampering, native `decide`/`implemented_by` tricks, and statement substitution.
- **Formal-proof criterion over generator–verifier-gap criterion**: unlike HorizonMath (101 problems) and FrontierMath: Open Problems (50 problems), which require a cheaply-checkable concrete object, OEIS_OPEN accepts only a Lean proof of the conjecture or its negation — giving definitive soundness resting on the Lean kernel, and making false conjectures solvable rather than unsolvable.
- **Demonstrates that a minimal harness beats an elaborate one**: a plain ReAct loop with three tools (bash, text editor, resources/budget tool) on Inspect resolves 3× more conjectures than AlphaProof Nexus's AlphaEvolve-style evolutionary search (which adds specialized AlphaProof, Elo-rated sketch populations, P-UCB sampling).
- **Two clean negative results**: (a) access to the LaTeX source of 476,000 pure-math arXiv papers (the proof-pile arXiv subset) did not raise accuracy on OEIS_OPEN_LITE; (b) replacing the ReAct loop with Inspect's `deepagent` (subagent delegation, persistent memory, todo-list, opinionated system prompt) also had no effect.
- **Characterizes scaling**: solve rates rise roughly log-linearly with per-conjecture spend (≈10 percentage points per 10× spend), with no clear plateau up to $200.

## Methodology

**Conjectures.** The 492 conjectures (444 distinct OEIS sequences) were collected and Lean-formalized by Tsoukalas et al., who started from 2649 open OEIS conjectures, used Gemini to select 500 that are "non-trivial, mathematically interesting, not famous open problems, and good candidates for automated theorem-proving," and auto-formalized them; 8 were dropped for technical reasons. Integer-sequence conjectures were chosen because they involve only integers and elementary operations, keeping misformalization risk low.

**Metadata.** For each conjecture the authors extracted (via GPT-5.5 over the full OEIS record + revision history) the proposer and proposal date (yielding a proposer for 488/492 and a date for 489/492, with 443 high-confidence), and measured literature attention two ways: citations on the OEIS entry, and OpenAlex works full-text-referencing the sequence (union of three searches). Zhi-Wei Sun alone proposed 37% of the conjectures; 47% of sequences list no links/references.

**Verification.** A submission is accepted only if it passes SafeVerify, which replays compiled Lean through the kernel and additionally certifies the submission proves the *target* statement (matching name/kind/kernel-type, no axioms beyond the standard three: propext, Quot.sound, Classical.choice). To prevent the agent (which has shell access to its own Lean env) from tampering with verification, each attempt is split across three network-less Docker containers: (1) agent container where the model works; (2) compile container with a clean Lean toolchain that compiles the submission to an olean; (3) scorer container that runs SafeVerify on the trusted statement olean + submission olean. This defeats tampering with the agent env, malicious compile-time `#eval`, proving a different statement, redefining a dependency, smuggling axioms, and kernel-bypass via metaprogramming/native decide. As a cross-check, all Claude Opus 4.8 submissions were re-verified with the independent Comparator tool.

**Agent.** A deliberately minimal ReAct-style loop built on the UK AISI Inspect library, with three tools: bash, a text editor, and a resources tool (reports remaining time/token budget). The agent container ships Lean 4 + Mathlib, SageMath, and Python with sympy/mpmath/numpy/pantograph. The agent may prove *or* disprove, iterating until resolved or until the per-conjecture spend cap ($50 full set, $200 LITE) or 72-hour wall-clock limit. Two variants were ablated: **Literature** (offline LaTeX snapshot of 476k pure-math arXiv papers up to 2022) and **DeepAgent** (Inspect's deepagent with subagent delegation, persistent memory, todo-list, longer system prompt).

## Results & Highlights

- **Full set (492 conjectures, $50/conjecture, one run each):** Claude Opus 4.8 resolves **147/492 = 30%**, GPT-5.5 **26%**, Gemini 3.5 Flash **22%**. The reported AlphaProof Nexus baseline (Tsoukalas et al.) resolves only **44/492 = 9%** at comparable cost (their released repo contains 38 proofs).
- **OEIS_OPEN_LITE (100 conjectures, $200/conjecture):** accuracy ranges from **29% (Gemini 3.5 Flash) to 44% (Claude Fable 5)**; GPT-5.6 Sol lands at 43%. Claude Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol were evaluated only on LITE with the base agent.
- **Cost per resolved conjecture:** averages **$6 (GPT-5.5), $9 (Gemini 3.5 Flash), $10 (Claude Opus 4.8)**, with a maximum of $47. AlphaProof Nexus authors reported ≈$10 average, up to ≈$50 for the hardest few — i.e., the simple agent matches the elaborate system's cost-efficiency while solving ~3× more.
- **Null result on agentic enhancements:** neither the Literature variant (476k arXiv papers) nor the DeepAgent variant changed accuracy on LITE (Figure 3, Appendix A.2).
- **Scaling law:** solve rate rises roughly log-linearly in spend, on the order of **10 percentage points per tenfold increase** in budget, with no plateau up to $200 — extrapolating to a $200 full-set run would yield ≈216/492 solved vs the 147 at $50.
- **Cross-check on soundness:** re-verifying all Claude Opus 4.8 submissions with the independent Comparator tool confirmed every SafeVerify-accepted solve except submissions to 5 conjectures with an unusual formalization defect, and additionally validated 2 proofs SafeVerify had rejected only on resource limits. Under Comparator's verdicts the score is **144/492 = 29%** rather than 147/492 (30%).
- **Newest generation is not a qualitative jump:** Claude Fable 5 (44%) and GPT-5.6 Sol (43%) lead the previous generation on LITE by only a few percentage points.

## Authors & Credibility

Single author: **Tom Adamczewski** at **Epoch AI**, an organization with a strong track record on AI benchmarking (Epoch AI is also the maintainer of FrontierMath). The work is a careful, methodologically transparent evaluation: budgets, model versions and release dates, average and max costs, and the precise verification protocol are all disclosed, directly addressing the opacity the author criticizes in recent one-off AI-math announcements (OpenAI's unit-distance disproof, the Jacobian conjecture counterexample, etc.). Code and per-sample verifier output are publicly released. The paper explicitly builds on Tsoukalas et al.'s (DeepMind) conjecture formalization rather than claiming independent dataset construction. The honest framing of the conjectures' uncertain mathematical significance is a credibility plus.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty:** Moderate-to-high. The benchmark material itself is inherited from Tsoukalas et al.; the contribution is the *open, generic, cheating-resistant harness* and the systematic head-to-head of frontier LMs with full cost/success-rate disclosure — filling a real gap left by the one-off, under-disclosed announcements the paper opens by critiquing.
- **Rigor:** Strong on the verification side (3-container isolation, SafeVerify + Comparator cross-check, explicit enumeration of 6 attack classes defeated). Weaker on evaluation breadth: **only one run per model on the full 492-conjecture set**, so the headline 30%/26%/22% come without variance estimates for the main result (error bars are shown only for LITE in Figure 1). Given binary per-conjecture outcomes and a 492-sample set, a second seed would have materially tightened the Claude-vs-GPT gap.
- **Baseline fairness:** The AlphaProof Nexus comparison is the paper's headline "3× better" claim, but it is **not apples-to-apples**: AlphaProof Nexus's prover subagents use Gemini 3.1 Pro (released 19 Feb 2026), whereas the authors' best models (GPT-5.5, Claude Opus 4.8) were released ~2–3 months later (23 Apr / 28 May 2026). Part of the gap is plausibly newer-base-model advantage rather than harness-design advantage. The author acknowledges this only in a footnote.
- **Misformalization risk is unaddressed empirically.** The authors take the Tsoukalas dataset "as-is without performing further validation," concede "it seems likely that at least some conjectures are misformalized," and note misformalized statements are probably *easier* — so the absolute solve rates may be inflated, though they argue this does not bias *model comparisons*. No audit is performed.
- **Missing ablations:** No breakdown of which of the three tools (bash vs editor vs resources) or which external CAS (SageMath, sympy, pantograph) actually drives the solves; the "simple is enough" conclusion would be stronger with this. The "literature/DeepAgent had no effect" null result is also under-interpreted — it could mean agents don't *use* the affordances well rather than that the affordances are valueless.
- **Significance of the math solved:** The author is admirably candid that the conjectures are "of uncertain mathematical significance," that Zhi-Wei Sun alone proposed 37%, and that 47% have no OEIS references — so "LMs resolve open research conjectures" should be read as "LMs resolve low-attention open OEIS conjectures," not front-line mathematics. The resolved proofs (Table 1) are overwhelmingly p-adic congruences and combinatorial identities — a narrow slice.
- **Reproducibility:** High. Open-source harness, released proofs, fixed budgets, named model versions. The chief reproducibility hazard is model access (frontier models behind APIs that may change).
- **Red flags:** None severe. The main overclaim risk is the "3× better than AlphaProof Nexus" framing given the base-model-date confound and the single-run main evaluation.

## Strengths

- Methodologically exemplary disclosure: per-model cost (avg + max), budgets, model release dates, single-vs-multi-run status, and the exact verifier protocol are all stated.
- Security design is genuinely thoughtful — the 3-container split and the enumeration of 6 defeated attack classes (including subtle ones like native `decide` via `@[implemented by]` introducing `Lean.ofReduceBool`) is a real contribution to trustworthy LLM-for-proof benchmarks.
- The two negative results (literature access and DeepAgent both null) are valuable and counterintuitive; many would have assumed retrieval helps.
- Open release of code, conjectures, and accepted/rejected proofs lowers the barrier to re-evaluation by future models.
- Honest framing of limitations (conjecture significance, misformalization, training-data leakage) rather than hype.

## Limitations

- **Conjecture significance is low and skewed:** 37% proposed by one person (Zhi-Wei Sun); 47% of sequences have no OEIS references; the author concedes most have "likely received little attention." Generalization to "research mathematics" is limited.
- **Single run per model on the full set** — no error bars on the headline 30/26/22% numbers.
- **Misformalization not audited** — taken as-is from Tsoukalas et al.; absolute solve rates may be inflated.
- **Base-model-date confound** in the AlphaProof Nexus comparison weakens the "simple harness wins" narrative.
- **Narrow mathematical coverage:** restricted to statements formalizable in Mathlib with integer-sequence flavor; resolved proofs concentrate in p-adic congruences and combinatorics.
- **Formalization ability conflated with mathematical ability** — a model may find a correct argument yet fail to formalize it.
- **No credit for reductions to famous open problems** (e.g., showing a conjecture implies Collatz), which mathematicians would value.
- **Future training-data leakage:** once solved, proofs may enter pretraining; the authors note this can only be mitigated after the fact by filtering.

## Relevance

This work sits squarely at the intersection of automated theorem proving, LLM benchmarking, and formal verification — directly relevant to research on scaling LLM reasoning, agentic loops for math, and Lean/Mathlib-based proof search. The negative results on retrieval and elaborate agent scaffolding are especially useful signals for anyone building math-reasoning agents: they suggest that, at least for formalizable integer-sequence conjectures, model capability and raw inference budget dominate scaffold cleverness.

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Would a like-for-like re-run of AlphaProof Nexus with Gemini 3.1 Pro swapped for GPT-5.5 / Claude Opus 4.8 close the "3×" gap, confirming (or refuting) that the harness design — not the base model — drives the win?
- With multiple seeds on the full 492-set, are the Claude-vs-GPT-vs-Gemini differences statistically significant, or within noise?
- Which tool/CAS actually carries the solves — is it pantograph (Lean-specific), SageMath, or raw bash + Lean? An ablation removing each would clarify the "minimal tools suffice" claim.
- Is the literature-access null result robust to better retrieval grounding (e.g., embedding search over the 476k papers rather than giving the agent raw LaTeX to grep)?
- Do the misformalized-easier conjectures concentrate in specific proposers or sequence families, and does correcting them lower the headline solve rate materially?
- Does the log-linear spend-vs-solve curve continue to $1k/$10k per conjecture, or does it plateau — i.e., is there a "hard core" of conjectures that inference scaling alone will not crack?
- Can the harness extend to non-integer-sequence research mathematics (analysis, geometry, algebraic topology) where misformalization risk is higher, and does the "simple agent wins" conclusion transfer?
