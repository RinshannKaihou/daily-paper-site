> **一句话总结：** 针对英语校准的 INT3 GPTQ 量化对非英语语言造成 2–4 倍更大的困惑度损失这一问题，提出 LCD（语言条件反量化）——为已量化模型的每个线性层挂接 rank-2 的按语言 LoRA 修正（每语言仅 0.12% 参数、单 GPU 约 20 分钟训练），在 Qwen2.5-3B 与 Llama-3.2-3B 上恢复了非拉丁语系 70–83% 的困惑度差距与 17–28% 的 GlobalMMLU 准确率差距，并揭示了一个困惑度与准确率脱节的现象。

> **TL;DR:** English-calibrated INT3 GPTQ (W3A16, group 128) hits Arabic 4.37x harder than English on Qwen2.5-3B perplexity (mC4/C4) and 3.65x harder on Llama-3.2-3B; LCD fixes this post-hoc with per-language rank-2 LoRA hooks (0.12% params/language, trained on 256 samples in ~20 min on one L4 GPU), recovering 70–83% of the perplexity gap for non-Latin languages and outperforming the data-free LQER baseline by an order of magnitude (63.5% vs 5.5% perplexity recovery on Qwen2.5-3B) — though on Llama a perplexity–accuracy disconnect limits GlobalMMLU recovery to 17%.

## 核心贡献

- **量化并刻画了多语言损害**：在 Qwen2.5-3B 与 Llama-3.2-3B 两个 sub-4B 模型上系统量化了英语校准 INT3 GPTQ 造成的按语言困惑度退化比，从英语的 1.35–1.39× 到阿拉伯语的 4.37×/3.65×，填补了"最需要激进量化的部署区间"的数据空白。
- **提出 LCD**：一种 post-hoc 的按语言 rank-2 LoRA 修正方法，通过 forward hook 附加到已量化模型的每个线性层，无需重新量化、无需改动基础模型架构，每语言仅增加 0.12% 参数并以 ~7 MB delta 形式分发。
- **证明按语言条件化捕获真实信号**：与同等容量的语言无关 rank-2 基线相比，LCD 在平均上打平，但在类型学上远离英语的语言（阿拉伯语 +8.9、日语 +7.0、韩语 +3.0 pp）上明显领先，证明 LCD 是对语言特异性量化误差的忠实修正而非通用微调。
- **揭示并机制性解释"困惑度–准确率脱节"**：Llama 的困惑度恢复（avg 69%）高于 Qwen（avg 63%），但 GlobalMMLU 恢复反而更低（17% vs 28%）。逐层 Frobenius 误差分析将此归因于深度不对称——Llama 的最差层位于早-中段（索引 7–14/28），误差向下游传播并抵抗局部修正；Qwen 的最差层位于后段（18–30/36）。
- **通过层覆盖实验直接验证机制**：将 LCD 限制在 Llama 底半部能以一半参数在困惑度恢复上超越均匀配置 10 pp，但 GlobalMMLU 上反而只恢复 9.5%（vs 均匀 17%）——这一双重分离是分布性误差与表征性误差受不同机制支配的最强证据。

## Key Contributions

- **Characterizes multilingual quantization harm in the sub-4B INT3 regime**: Measures per-language perplexity degradation ratios from INT3 GPTQ on Qwen2.5-3B and Llama-3.2-3B, ranging from 1.35–1.39× for English to 4.37× for Arabic on Qwen, pinning down the magnitude exactly where aggressive quantization is most needed for deployment.
- **Proposes LCD (Language-Conditional Dequantization)**: A post-hoc method that attaches per-language rank-2 LoRA corrections to every linear layer of an already-quantized model via forward hooks, adds only 0.12% parameters per language, trains in ~20 min/language on a single L4 GPU, and ships as a ~7 MB delta per language — avoiding the re-quantization required by multilingual calibration (Chimoto et al., 2026).
- **Shows per-language conditioning captures genuine language-specific signal**: Against a language-agnostic rank-2 baseline at matched capacity, LCD ties on average but wins by 3–9 pp on typologically distant languages (Arabic, Japanese, Korean), and beats the data-free LQER (Zhang et al., 2024) baseline by an order of magnitude (63.5% vs 5.5% perplexity recovery at rank 2 on Qwen2.5-3B).
- **Reveals and mechanistically explains a perplexity–accuracy disconnect**: Llama has higher perplexity recovery (69%) than Qwen (63%) yet lower GlobalMMLU recovery (17% vs 28%). A per-layer Frobenius error analysis attributes this to depth asymmetry — Llama's worst layers sit early-middle (indices 7–14 of 28) and propagate corruption downstream; Qwen's sit late (18–30 of 36).
- **Validates the mechanism with a layer-restricted variant and a double dissociation**: Restricting LCD to Llama's bottom half beats uniform ALL by 10 pp on perplexity at half the parameters, but recovers only 9.5% of the MMLU gap versus 17% for uniform — the strongest evidence that distributional and representational quantization damage obey distinct mechanisms, and that perplexity recovery is an unreliable proxy for downstream accuracy.

## Methodology

LCD is built on the observation that if quantization error is language-dependent, the correction should be too. For each linear layer (excluding the lm head and embedding) and each language ℓ, it defines a rank-r additive correction implemented as a forward hook:

```
ŷ = W_q x + (A_ℓ · B_ℓ) / r · x
```

where A_ℓ ∈ ℝ^(d_out × r), B_ℓ ∈ ℝ^(r × d_in), with r = 2 by default. A_ℓ is initialized to zero and B_ℓ ∼ N(0, 0.01), so the correction is exactly zero at initialization — a parameterization borrowed from LoRA but serving error correction rather than task adaptation.

**Training.** Each language is trained independently. From 256 monolingual text samples (sourced from mC4/C4) the standard language-modeling loss is minimized with only the correction matrices trainable (all base weights frozen). Optimizer: AdamW, lr 5×10⁻⁴, cosine decay with 10% warmup, weight decay 0.01, gradient clipping 1.0, 500 steps. This takes 15–20 min/language on a single L4 GPU; covering all eight non-English languages costs under 3 GPU-hours. Each correction ships as a ~7 MB delta on top of the unchanged quantized checkpoint.

**Inference.** The input language is identified via locale, automatic LID (e.g., fastText classifies a prompt in well under a millisecond), or explicit specification, and the corresponding correction slot is activated via a single index. The base quantized model resides in memory once; language switching only updates the active adapter index with no model reloading. For K languages at rank 2, total overhead is K × 0.12% additional parameters.

**Setup.** Models: Qwen2.5-3B and Llama-3.2-3B (distinct tokenizers and training distributions). Quantization: GPTQ W3A16, group size 128, calibrated on 128 English C4 samples (the standard English-only deployment practice). Languages: 9 (English as calibration baseline, Arabic, Japanese, Chinese, Hindi, Russian, French, Spanish, Korean). Metrics: mC4/C4 perplexity (32 held-out samples/language) and GlobalMMLU accuracy (~14,040 items/language, 57 subjects). Total compute: ~42 GPU-hours on L4/L40S.

## Results & Highlights

- **Quantization hits non-English 2–4× harder (Table 1, mC4/C4 perplexity).** On Qwen2.5-3B, INT3/FP16 ratio is 1.35× for English but 4.37× for Arabic (3.23× relative), 3.04× for Japanese, 3.49× for Korean; on Llama-3.2-3B the pattern repeats (Arabic 3.65× vs English 1.39× = 2.63× relative). Romance languages are only modestly worse than English (French 1.64×/1.71×).
- **LCD recovers 70–83% of the perplexity gap for non-Latin-script languages (Table 2).** On Qwen2.5-3B / Llama-3.2-3B respectively: Arabic 78.8%/80.1%, Japanese 69.7%/72.9%, Chinese 71.2%/76.4%, Korean 74.8%/76.7%. Non-English average recovery is 63.5% (Qwen) and 69.1% (Llama); non-Latin average 72.4%/74.4%. Latin-script languages close to English recover less (French 38%/54%, Spanish 36%/52%). Results are stable across 3 seeds (±1.3 pp).
- **LCD beats the data-free LQER baseline by an order of magnitude at matched rank-2 budget (Table 4, Qwen2.5-3B).** LQER recovers only 5.5% of the perplexity gap and 13.0% of the GlobalMMLU gap (non-English averages), versus LCD's 63.5% and 27.8%; LQER is even negative on several languages (e.g., Russian −40% perplexity).
- **GlobalMMLU: LCD closes 28% of the accuracy gap on Qwen, only 17% on Llama (Table 5).** Largest Qwen gains: Chinese +9.1 pp (35.4→44.5), Spanish +6.7 pp (35.0→41.7), Russian +6.2 pp (29.6→35.8), Japanese +6.0 pp (30.7→36.7); all eight non-English languages improve, with paired t-tests reaching p < 0.001 (‡) or p < 0.05 (†) on most languages. Llama gains are smaller (1.1–3.5 pp).
- **Per-language vs language-agnostic (Table 3, Qwen, perplexity recovery):** tied on average (65.4% vs 64.6%) but LCD wins by 3–9 pp on Arabic (+8.9), Japanese (+7.0), Korean (+3.0) and loses 15.8 pp on French — confirming per-language conditioning exploits genuine signal precisely where typological distance is largest.
- **Layer-restricted LCD on Llama (Table 6):** BOTTOM-HALF beats uniform ALL by 10 pp on perplexity recovery at half the parameters (AR 78.5 vs 71.9, JA 83.3 vs 71.0, ZH 36.1 vs 21.8, KO 78.3 vs 70.2) and beats TOP-HALF by 43 pp — but on GlobalMMLU BOTTOM-HALF recovers only 9.5% vs 17% for uniform (the reverse ordering), a clean double dissociation.
- **Negative results on narrow targeting and on scale-up:** targeting only the identified worst layers 7–14 (TARGETED-R4 / TARGETED-R2) yields only 37% vs 45% for UNIFORM-R2, so broad early-depth coverage rather than narrow layer allocation is the operative mechanism. At Qwen2.5-7B, rank-2 LCD recovers only 8.5% of the non-English accuracy gap (vs 28% at 3B), and rank-4 with 1000 steps degrades every language (avg −3.9%).
- **Rank ablation (Arabic+Japanese, Qwen):** recovery is 76.92% (r=1), 76.98% (r=2), 76.84% (r=4) — within 0.1 pp, indicating the correction subspace is effectively one-dimensional; a rank-1 deployment would halve overhead to 0.06% per language.

## Authors & Credibility

Single author (Nirmal Thomas, Prathama International). The paper is technically careful and unusually intellectually honest — it foregrounds a negative result (the Llama perplexity–accuracy disconnect) as its "most scientifically informative finding," reports the order-of-magnitude gap to LQER at matched budget, includes seed-variance bars, reports a 7B scale-up that fails, and includes a double dissociation rather than only headline numbers. Statistical significance is reported with paired t-tests (57 subjects, df = 56) and binomial standard errors. Code is provided via an anonymous.4open.science repository. Citations are appropriate and up to date through 2026 (including the directly competing multilingual-calibration work of Chimoto et al., 2026 and the closest post-hoc prior Recover-LoRA, Das et al., 2025). The total compute footprint (~42 GPU-hours) and per-language training cost (~20 min, ~7 MB delta) are credible and reproducible on consumer hardware. The positioning against related work (LQER, RILQ, ResQ, Recover-LoRA, BinaryMoS, MLAS-LoRA) is precise about LCD's unique combination of post-hoc AND language-conditional.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty.** The core insight (per-language low-rank corrections applied post-hoc via forward hooks) is a natural but clean combination of LoRA and the per-layer error-reconstruction lineage (LQER/QERA/RILQ). The genuinely novel contributions are the language-conditional axis (vs all prior post-hoc corrections being language-agnostic) and the mechanistic depth-asymmetry analysis with the double-dissociation experiment — the latter is the paper's strongest scientific contribution and elevates it above an engineering tweak.
- **Rigor.** Strong in places: seed-variance bars, paired t-tests, significance markers, an order-of-magnitude comparison to a principled baseline (LQER) at matched budget, an honest negative-result section, and a controlled double dissociation. Weaker in places: the rank ablation covers only two languages (Arabic, Japanese) — the claim that "rank is not the bottleneck" is under-supported. The French anomaly (per-language loses 15.8 pp vs agnostic, Table 3) is disclosed but not investigated; it is a real crack in the central claim that should have been probed (e.g., is 256 samples of French insufficient to estimate a separate adapter when the multilingual mixture transfers better?).
- **Reproducibility.** High. Standard public data (C4, mC4, GlobalMMLU), standard quantization (GPTQ W3A16 g128 via Frantar et al., 2023), open models (Qwen2.5-3B, Llama-3.2-3B), full hyperparameters given, anonymous code repo. The 256-sample/500-step/AdamW recipe is cheap to replicate. The missing piece is calibration-seed variance for GPTQ itself (the 128-sample English C4 calibration is a single run).
- **Significance.** Practically meaningful for the sub-4B deployment regime where INT3 is common and re-quantization is impractical; the ~7 MB per-language delta is a genuinely useful artifact shape. However, the headline 70–83% perplexity recovery translates to only 17–28% GlobalMMLU recovery (the paper's own disconnect finding), and the 7B result (8.5% recovery, with rank-4 making things worse) sharply limits the scaling story — the authors admit the (scale × bit-width) plane where LCD helps is "open." Generalization to truly low-resource languages, code-switched inputs, and non-INT3 schemes (AWQ, GGUF k-quants, INT2/INT4) is untested.
- **Red flags / overclaiming.** Minimal, and the paper is commendably restrained. The main risk is that casual readers fixate on "recovers 70–83% of the gap" without registering that this is perplexity, not task accuracy. The French regression and the 7B failure are disclosed rather than hidden. The depth-asymmetry narrative is slightly oversold relative to its actionable value: the negative result that precision-targeting layers 7–14 does NOT beat uniform (37% vs 45%) somewhat deflates the practical payoff of the analysis, leaving "use uniform rank-2 everywhere" as the actual recommendation.
- **Data quirks.** Evaluation perplexity is computed on only 32 held-out samples/language; MMLU uses ~14,040 items/language which is solid, but the perplexity numbers are noisier than they look. The 256-sample training slice is deliberately small to match the "post-hoc, cheap" framing, which the authors acknowledge may shift the per-language-vs-agnostic comparison (especially for French).

## Strengths

- Honest, mechanism-focused analysis: the perplexity–accuracy disconnect is presented as the most informative finding rather than buried, and is backed by per-layer Frobenius error heatmaps and a controlled layer-restriction experiment with a clean double dissociation.
- Strong, fair baselines: order-of-magnitude comparison to LQER at matched rank-2 budget, plus a language-agnostic LoRA at matched capacity — isolating exactly what per-language conditioning buys.
- Practical deployment story: post-hoc (no re-quantization), tiny (~7 MB / 0.12% per language), fast (~20 min/language on one L4 GPU), and architecturally transparent (forward hooks, no base-model changes).
- Statistical care: paired t-tests across 57 MMLU subjects, binomial SEs, three-seed variance bars, absolute perplexities reported in an appendix.
- Restrained claims: the authors explicitly warn against treating perplexity recovery as a proxy for task recovery and disclose the 7B scale-up failure.

## Limitations

- Perplexity recovery (70–83%) does not translate proportionally to downstream accuracy (17–28% GlobalMMLU), and the gap is structural on early-error architectures like Llama-3.2-3B — LCD cannot undo propagated upstream corruption.
- Scale ceiling: at Qwen2.5-7B, LCD recovers only 8.5% of the non-English accuracy gap, and raising capacity to rank-4/1000 steps overfits and degrades every language (avg −3.9%). The (scale × bit-width) regime where LCD helps is unresolved.
- Narrow quantization regime: only INT3 GPTQ W3A16, group 128, English C4 calibration is studied. AWQ, GGUF k-quants, SmoothQuant, INT2/INT4/FP4, and non-English calibration corpora are all untested.
- Language coverage is restricted to eight relatively high-resource languages; truly low-resource languages, dialectal variation, and code-switched or mixed-language prompts are not handled (the current design requires a known input language, with no soft gating or fallback evaluated).
- Tiny training (256 samples/language, 500 steps) and tiny perplexity eval (32 samples/language) — cheap by design, but the French regression and the per-language-vs-agnostic comparison may shift with more or more-diverse data.
- The actionable payoff of the depth-asymmetry analysis is undercut by the negative result that precision-targeting layers 7–14 does not beat uniform correction (37% vs 45%).

## Relevance

与我们对高效 LLM 推理（量化、压缩）和多语言能力的关注直接相关：LCD 提供了一种极低成本（0.12% 参数、单 GPU 约 20 分钟）的 post-hoc 方案，用于修复 sub-4B INT3 量化模型在非英语语言上的公平性差距，且无需重新量化，对已部署模型有直接工程价值。其逐层误差归因方法也可迁移到我们关心的其他量化方案与模型规模的诊断。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does LCD transfer to non-GPTQ schemes (AWQ, GGUF k-quants, SmoothQuant) and other bit-widths (INT2, INT4, FP4), where the per-layer error profile and the language-specific harm may differ?
- Can a soft-gating mechanism over per-language adapters (or a learned router) handle code-switched and mixed-language inputs, and how much does a mismatched-adapter activation actually harm quality?
- What happens on truly low-resource and dialectal languages, where 256 monolingual samples may be hard to obtain and the quantization error profile is least like English's?
- Why does per-language conditioning hurt French by 15.8 pp relative to the agnostic baseline, and would more or more-diverse training data close that gap?
- Can allocating higher rank specifically to the compression projections (o_proj, down_proj) — which carry the highest per-layer error in both models — beat uniform block-level correction, given that the paper's block-level targeting experiment was negative?
- At what (scale × bit-width) combination does LCD's benefit vanish, and is the 7B overfitting failure a data-quantity problem (256 samples) or a fundamental representational-redundancy limit?
- Does the perplexity–accuracy disconnect (early-depth error propagation) appear in other early-error architectures (e.g., Mistral, Phi), and can a non-local correction (e.g., cross-layer) recover representational damage that a per-layer hook cannot?
