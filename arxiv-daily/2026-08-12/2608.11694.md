> **一句话总结：** 本文提出 BenchDrift 框架，沿语言、指代、语用、结构四个轴生成保持题意与答案不变的问题改写，在 GSM8K/MMLU/MATH-Hard 与 8 个模型上测量正负双向"漂移"，发现平均最差—最好准确率跨度达 74.7 个百分点，且漂移方向随模型变强而反转（弱模型获益、强模型受损）。

> **TL;DR:** BenchDrift rephrases benchmark problems along four meaning-preserving axes (linguistic, referential, pragmatic, structural), validates each keeps the ground-truth answer, and measures how often a model's correctness flips both ways. Across 8 models (7B-34B) and GSM8K, MMLU, and MATH-Hard, the worst-to-best-case accuracy gap averages 74.7pp; e.g. Phi-4 on GSM8K spans 38.2% (worst) to 99.2% (best) around a reported 93.4%, and the direction of drift reverses with model strength—the asymmetry between negative and positive drift tracks baseline accuracy with Pearson r = 0.98.

## 核心贡献

- 提出 BenchDrift：一个意义保持的改写框架，沿语言（用词/风格）、指代（实体/单位替换）、语用（人称/语气）、结构（句序/格式）四个轴生成题目变体，并由 validator 校验每个变体答案不变，从而把每一次正确性翻转（drift）归因到具体的改写与轴。
- 定义双向漂移度量：正确率翻正（positive drift，原错→变体对）衡量被原题掩盖的能力；翻负（negative drift，原对→变体错）衡量依赖特定措辞的脆弱性；并以同一分母 N 报告两者，推出恒等式 Best = Rep. + Pos.、Worst = Rep. − Neg.。
- 两个排除性检验：证明改写并非只是"把题变简单"（长度缩短与加倍都同样增加 negative drift），也并非只扰动模型不确定的答案（高置信区间仍有约 18.5% 翻负）。
- 可靠性检查：整体替换 generator/validator/judge 三个角色后，平均 negative drift 最多移动 11.9pp、positive 最多 2.9pp，模型脆弱性排名保持 ρ ≥ 0.98。
- 开源完整 pipeline、prompt 模板、变体分类法与实验所用已校验变体（https://github.com/IBM/BenchDrift/tree/demo-ui）。

## Key Contributions

- A meaning-preserving variation framework with four axes (linguistic, referential, pragmatic, structural), every variation validated against the original ground truth, and a bidirectional drift measure over a common denominator that attributes each flip to the transformation that produced it.
- Two tests that rule out leading alternative explanations: that rephrasing merely simplifies problems, and that it only disturbs low-confidence answers.
- Reliability checks that swap the generator, validator, and judge models and confirm the drift is a property of the target models, not the instruments (ρ ≥ 0.98 against the main configuration).
- Open release of the generated and validated variations, plus the full BenchDrift pipeline and variation taxonomy.
- A direct comparison showing prompt optimizers (DSPy, GEPA) collapse a model's behavior to a single number, whereas BenchDrift returns the full worst/representative/best range at comparable cost.

## Methodology

BenchDrift runs four roles over each problem. A **Generator** (Mistral-Large-Instruct-2411) proposes candidate rephrasings. A **Validator** (GPT-OSS-120B) checks whether a reader who never saw the original would arrive at the same answer, discarding any candidate whose answer moved. The **Target model** answers the original and every surviving variation with chain-of-thought at temperature 0 (removing sampling noise). A **Judge** (Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct) scores correctness, accepting equivalent surface forms ("12", "twelve", "a dozen").

A flip between original problem q and a validated variation q′ is **drift**: positive (C(q)=0, C(q′)=1, a hidden capability) or negative (C(q)=1, C(q′)=0, hidden fragility). Rates use the same denominator N, yielding exact identities Best = Rep. + Pos. and Worst = Rep. − Neg., so the reported score sits inside an interval bounded by the two drift rates.

Transformations come from a fixed taxonomy of four axes, each grouping multiple transformations gated on detected problem properties (a unit conversion needs a unit, an entity substitution needs a name). Twenty pattern-based detectors mark properties; each problem's variation budget is distributed across axes in proportions 1.0, 0.75, 0.55, 0.40 weighted toward the axes the problem best supports. After validation, problems retain on average 20.2 variations (range 3–92; MMLU's multiple-choice format yields more). Setup: 500 problems per benchmark (GSM8K, MMLU, MATH-Hard), 8 open-weight models (7B–34B), vLLM on 2× H100, problem-level clustered bootstrap CIs.

## Results & Highlights

- **Average worst-to-best accuracy gap is 74.7 percentage points** across all 24 model-benchmark pairs. Phi-4 on GSM8K is illustrative: reported 93.4% drops to 38.2% worst-case and rises to 99.2% best-case — a 61-point window with the reported number near the top.
- **Drift direction reverses with model strength.** The asymmetry (negative minus positive drift) tracks baseline accuracy almost linearly across 24 pairs (Pearson r = 0.98, slope 1.08). Above 60% baseline, negative drift exceeds positive drift in all twelve pairs; below it, weak models net-gain (e.g. Mistral-7B on MATH-Hard: 37.6% positive vs 9.0% negative drift). For GPT-OSS-20B and Phi-4, the two strongest GSM8K models, worst-case accuracy falls below 40% despite baselines above 93%.
- **Fragility concentrates in specific transformations.** Interrogative expansion breaks 48.9% of correct answers while adding whitespace breaks only 11.7% — a fourfold range. Programming formulation uniquely tops both directions (41.2% negative, 21.6% positive). Persona framing and paragraph reordering (which change no number or relation) cost more correct answers than entity substitution (which rewrites the tokens the answer depends on), indicating damage tracks surface form, not comprehension.
- **Models agree on which rephrasings are costly** even while differing in how much they drift overall: mean pairwise Spearman ρ = 0.77 across the 28 model pairs when ranking transformations by negative drift.
- **Drift is not noise from simplification.** Negative drift is lowest when length is preserved (19.6%) and rises in both directions (28.9% shorter, 34.7% when more than doubled), ruling out "problems just got easier."
- **Confidence does not flag fragility.** Negative drift falls from 38.1% (low-confidence bin) to 18.5% (high-confidence bin, ≥0.95) but never approaches zero — ~1 in 5 of the most confident correct answers is still lost to a rephrasing, holding within every model (13–26% in the high bin).
- **Measurement is robust to instrument swap.** Swapping generator/validator/judge shifts average negative drift ≤11.9pp and positive ≤2.9pp, both within per-cell CIs; fragility ranking preserved at ρ ≥ 0.98.
- **Variation budget can be trimmed.** With the full budget 67.2% of problems drift; 5 variations recover 74% of those, 8 recover 88%, 10 recover 93%, while a single variation finds only 30%.
- **Versus prompt optimizers** (Table 4, 15 GSM8K problems, Llama-3.1-8B): DSPy reports 93.3% (1.8s), GEPA reports 60.0% (62.0s), BenchDrift reports a 26.7%–100.0% range (rep 86.7%, 2.1s) that neither optimizer surfaces.

## Authors & Credibility

All four authors are from IBM Research (India and Almaden), a corporate lab with a track record on LLM evaluation and the Granite model family. The work is internally consistent: it states a clear falsifiable hypothesis (single-phrasing scores are unstable), reports confidence intervals and reliability swaps, and releases code, prompt templates, the taxonomy, and the validated variations. The framing is appropriately measured — they explicitly position BenchDrift as an audit tool, not a prompt optimizer, and do not oversell transfer of specific helpful rephrasings. The result that drift direction tracks baseline accuracy (r = 0.98) is a genuinely useful, non-obvious empirical regularity.

## Critical Assessment

- **Novelty**: High and well-scoped. Unlike CheckList/RobustnessGym (which alter the task and lose the ground truth) and paraphrase-sensitivity work (which reports that outputs change but not which change is responsible), BenchDrift preserves the answer and attributes each flip to a specific transformation. The Best/Worst interval framing with the exact Pos./Neg. identities is a clean contribution.
- **Rigor**: Strong on internal validity — temperature 0, problem-level clustered bootstrap CIs, axis-label shuffling permutation test (p < 0.001), and a four-way instrument-swap table. However, the central asymmetry finding rests entirely on LLM judges and validators with **no human validation** — the authors flag this themselves as the most important open question, noting a directionally permissive judge could inflate/deflate positive vs negative drift asymmetrically, which is precisely the asymmetry the paper leans on.
- **Reproducibility**: Good. Code, prompts, taxonomy, and validated variations are released; models are open-weight; only vLLM on 2× H100 is needed. The generator/validator/judge are themselves large models (Mistral-Large-2411, GPT-OSS-120B, Llama-3.3-70B), which raises the barrier to exact replication but is disclosed.
- **Significance**: Conceptually important for benchmark methodology — it reframes a single accuracy number as an interval and shows the strongest models are the most wording-dependent. Practical uptake may be limited by cost (Section 4.7 admits the first run costs more than a standard evaluation).
- **Red flags / specific weaknesses**: (1) Scope is narrow — all three benchmarks are closed-form (single checkable answer); behavior on open-ended generation is unknown. (2) The axis-level results are **inferred after the fact** by mapping transformation IDs onto the taxonomy (the logs did not record axis membership), so axis conclusions depend on the authors' own grouping; the transformation-level results are clean but the axis-level narrative is softer than presented. (3) The DSPy/GEPA comparison (Table 4) uses only 15 problems on a single model/benchmark — far too small to support the general "optimizers collapse behavior" claim; GEPA landing at 60.0% on 15 problems is essentially noise. (4) Generator/judge model families (Mistral, Qwen) overlap with target model families, which the role-swap does not fully rule out. (5) The headline 74.7pp average gap is inflated by including weak models like Mistral-7B and Granite-34B-Code whose best-case accuracy far exceeds their competence, conflating "the wording hid a capability" with "noise surfaced a lucky correct answer."

## Strengths

- Clean, exact identities (Best = Rep. + Pos., Worst = Rep. − Neg.) that turn a single score into a defensible interval with no extra modeling assumptions.
- The sign-reversal regularity (drift asymmetry vs baseline accuracy, r = 0.98) is a striking, interpretable finding with real implications for how to read leaderboards.
- Honest, paper-specific Limitations section that names the most dangerous threat (directional judge bias affecting the asymmetry) rather than burying it.
- Transformation-level analysis is more informative than the axis level and is correctly reported as the load-bearing result.
- Subsampling curve (Section 4.7) gives practitioners a concrete, cheaper audit recipe (8–10 variations recover ~90% of drift).

## Limitations

- No human validation of the judge or validator; a shared LLM blind spot, especially a directional one, could distort the central asymmetry finding.
- Only three closed-form benchmarks; no open-ended or code-generation tasks.
- Generator/validator/judge families overlap with target model families (Mistral, Qwen).
- Axis labels are reconstructed post hoc from transformation IDs, not logged at generation time.
- Higher cost than a standard single-phrasing evaluation; positioned for periodic audits, not routine runs.
- The 15-problem DSPy/GEPA comparison is underpowered; the headline 74.7pp gap partly reflects weak models whose best-case scores overstate genuine capability.

## Relevance

直接关系到 LLM 评测方法论与基准稳健性：它质疑"单一措辞得分"的稳定性，并提出可归因、可审计的双向漂移度量，与我对模型评估、prompt 敏感性、benchmark 可信度的研究兴趣高度契合，也为解读日常 arXiv 论文中的"准确率排名"提供了新的怀疑视角。

## Questions for Further Exploration

- Does the sign-reversal regularity (drift asymmetry vs baseline accuracy, r = 0.98) hold on open-ended tasks where correctness is not binary, or on code-generation benchmarks like HumanEval/MBPP?
- Would a human-annotation study of the judge's directional permissiveness change the magnitude of the central asymmetry finding, and by how much?
- Can the transformation-level fragility rankings (mean pairwise ρ = 0.77) be used as a cheap, transferable probe — i.e., do the transformations that break one frontier model also break the next generation?
- How does BenchDrift interact with inference-time aggregation (self-consistency)? Does majority-voting across meaning-preserving variations recover the "best-case" accuracy at acceptable cost, effectively converting drift into a robustness gain?
- Are the persona-framing and paragraph-reordering transformations (which change no semantic content) specifically attacking instruction-following surface features rather than reasoning, and could targeted fine-tuning on such variants close the worst-case gap?
